---
summary: Andromache, lamenting Hector, says that now by the curved ships, far from his parents, “writhing worms will eat you, when the dogs have had their fill / of your naked body” (22.508–510).
---

νῦν δὲ σὲ μὲν παρὰ νηυσὶ κορωνίσι νόσφι τοκήων / αἰόλαι εὐλαὶ ἔδονται, ἐπεί κε κύνες κορέσωνται / γυμνόν (22.508–510). αἰόλος, “writhing”, is the quick, glancing movement of a living thing (see [[flashing]]). The worms come after the dogs: Andromache foresees what Achilles has just threatened over the body, that Hector will lie unburied and naked by the ships (22.335–336, 22.348–354; see [[dogs]]). Achilles had feared the same for Patroclus, that flies would breed worms in his wounds while he fought (19.24–27). The fear is not fulfilled for either: Thetis keeps the flies from Patroclus (19.29–39), and in Book 24 Hermes tells Priam that the worms that feed on men slain in battle have not touched his son (24.411–415).
