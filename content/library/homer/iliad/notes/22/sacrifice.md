summary: The race around the walls is for no sacrificial victim: “it was no beast for sacrifice or ox-hide / they strove to win, … / but they ran for the life of Hector” (22.159–161). Zeus remembers the sacrifices Hector made him, “many thighs of oxen … / on the peaks of many-folded Ida, and at other times / on the height of the city” (22.170–172).
grc: ["μηρίʼ ἔκηεν"]
en: [thighs of oxen]

ἱερήϊον, “beast for sacrifice”, is named as a prize of the footrace that this race is not (see [[racehorses-at-the-turning-post]] and [[psyche]]). The thighs burned to Zeus are the worshiper's claim on the god, and here the god himself recalls them (see [[reciprocity-with-the-gods]], [[thigh-pieces]] and [[prayer]]).
