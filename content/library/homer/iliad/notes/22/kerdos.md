summary: Hector reckons the better course twice: it would have been “far better” to obey Polydamas (22.103), and now it would be “far better” to face Achilles and kill him or die before the city (22.108–110). Athena leads Hector to his death “by craft” (22.247).
grc: [κερδοσύνῃ]
en: [by craft]
---

κέρδιον, “better”, “more profitable”, is the word of a man counting his advantage (see [[pondering-two-ways]] and [[retreat]]). κερδοσύνη is cunning: in the Odyssey Odysseus uses it to evade Helen's questions when she knows him in disguise at Troy, and to sit down when Eumaeus' dogs rush at him (*Odyssey* 4.250–251, 14.29–31). Here it is a goddess's, in a brother's shape (22.226–247; see [[truth-and-deception]] and [[athena]]).
