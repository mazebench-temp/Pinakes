---
summary: The book's fathers are Zeus in heaven and Priam on the wall. Zeus is “the father of men and gods” (22.167), “the father” who holds out the scales (22.209), and Athena's “Father” (22.178); Priam is “his dear father” who groans over Hector (22.408), and hopes to move Achilles because “he too has a father like me, / Peleus” (22.420–421). In Andromache's lament Hector becomes the father the orphan has lost: “Your father does not feast among us” (22.498), “having lost his dear father” (22.505).
---

The father who destroys Priam is Zeus, “the father, the son of Cronus”, who will kill him on the threshold of old age (22.60; see [[zeus]] and [[geron]]). See [[fathers-and-sons]] and [[the-gods-mirror-the-mortals]].
