---
summary: Achilles comes upon Hector “the equal of Enyalius, the warrior of the shaking helmet” (22.132), and Hector, who once said that Enyalius kills the man who would kill, dies at his hands.
---

ἶσος Ἐνυαλίῳ, “the equal of Enyalius”, is found only here; elsewhere the name stands in the verse of Meriones, ἀτάλαντος Ἐνυαλίῳ ἀνδρειφόντῃ, “the equal of Enyalius, slayer of men” (2.651, 7.166, 8.264, 17.259; see [[ares]] and [[equal-of-ares]]). κορυθάϊξ, “of the shaking helmet”, is also found only here in Homer (see the translation's row on κορυθάϊξ), in a book in which Hector is again and again κορυθαίολος, “of the gleaming helmet” (22.232, 22.249, 22.337, 22.355, 22.471; see [[of-the-gleaming-helmet]]). When Achilles charges, he nods with his shining helmet and the plumes wave about it (22.314–316; see [[helmet]]). In Book 18 Hector answered Polydamas' counsel with a maxim: “Enyalius is impartial, and he kills the man who would kill” (18.309; see [[polydamas]]); Walter Leaf renders it, “The god of battle is impartial and slays him that would slay” (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 18.309). Hector, who killed Patroclus, is killed by the man who comes on like Enyalius (see [[hector]] and [[achilles]]).
