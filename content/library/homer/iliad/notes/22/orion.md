---
summary: Priam sees Achilles running over the plain, gleaming like the star of late summer, “the star that men call by the name of the dog of Orion”, the brightest of all and a sign of evil (22.25–31).
---

ὅν τε κύνʼ Ὠρίωνος ἐπίκλησιν καλέουσι, “the star that men call by the name of the dog of Orion” (22.29; see [[the-autumn-star]]). The hunter of the Shield, “the strength of Orion” whom the Bear watches (18.486–488), has a dog that follows him in the sky. The star is Sirius, the star of late summer of Book 5 (5.5; see the translation's note on 22.26–31). Walter Leaf notes that the name of the dog has since passed to the constellation, Canis Maior, of which Sirius is the brightest star, and which follows close upon Orion; he would read Ὠαρίωνος here, as at 18.486; and since ἐπίκλησιν at 18.487 means “as a second name”, he allows that it may mean so here too, if the description is enough to call up the first name, Sirius (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 22.29). ἐπίκλησιν καλέουσι is the verse-end of the Bear, “which they also call by the name of the Wagon” (18.487; see [[the-bear]]), and of Astyanax, “whom the Trojans call by the name of Astyanax” (22.506; see [[astyanax]]).
