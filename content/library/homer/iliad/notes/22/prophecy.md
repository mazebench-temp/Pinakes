summary: Hector dies prophesying, as Patroclus did. “Take care now, lest I become a cause of the gods' wrath against you, / on that day when Paris and Phoebus Apollo / destroy you, brave as you are, at the Scaean gates” (22.358–360); and Achilles answers that he will accept his doom “whenever / Zeus is willing to bring it to pass” (22.365–366).
refs: [22.356-366]
---

Achilles' answer uses the verb of fulfillment, τελέσαι, “to bring it to pass” (22.366; see [[death-of-achilles-foretold]] and [[the-short-life-of-achilles]]). Hector's first word may itself be one of foresight: προτιόσσομαι means “look at” in some places of the Odyssey and “forebode” in others, and the translation renders it “as I look at you” (22.356; see the translation's note on 22.356). The dying man's prophecy completes a chain: Patroclus foretold Hector's death at Achilles' hands (16.851–854), and Hector foretells Achilles' (see [[death-in-battle]] and [[menis]]).
