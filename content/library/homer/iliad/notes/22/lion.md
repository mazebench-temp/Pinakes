# λέουσι (22.262), the dative plural, only here in Homer.
grc: [λέουσι]
---

ὡς οὐκ ἔστι λέουσι καὶ ἀνδράσιν ὅρκια πιστά, “as there are no trusty oaths between lions and men” (22.262; see [[no-oaths-between-lions-and-men]]). ὅρκια πιστά are the words of the sworn truce of Book 3, where the two armies were to “make friendship and swear a trusty truce” (φιλότητα καὶ ὅρκια πιστὰ τάμωμεν, 3.94; 3.73), and Achilles refuses both: “so it is not possible for you and me to be friends, nor will there be / oaths between us two” (22.265–266; see [[oath-formulas]]). In the similes the lion is the hero on the attack, and Achilles has been one, rising against Aeneas like a lion that the countrymen have set out to kill (20.164–175; see [[wounded-lion]]). In his words to Hector the lion is no likeness but the beast with which men make no compact, and he speaks as the lion. In Book 24 Apollo says of him that he knows savage things, like a lion that goes out against the flocks of men to take his meal (24.41–43). See also [[wolves]].
