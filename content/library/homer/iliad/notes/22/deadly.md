summary: “Deadly fate” holds Hector before the gates (22.5); Achilles calls Apollo “most baneful of all the gods” (22.15); Priam foresees his sons' wives “dragged off by the deadly hands of the Achaeans” (22.65); and Hector remembers “this deadly night, when brilliant Achilles rose up” (22.102).

en: [most baneful]
---

ὀλοιή, ὀλοώτατε and ὀλοῇς are new to the entry. The form ὀλοιή for ὀλοή is found only here, at 1.342 and in the Hymn to Aphrodite (see the translation's note on 22.5), and the superlative of a god matches the comparative of Zeus in Menelaus' complaint, “no other god is more baneful than you” (22.15, as 3.365; see the translation's note on 22.15 and [[far-worker]]). The deadly night is the night after Patroclus' death, when Polydamas bade Hector lead the Trojans into the city (22.100–103; [[polydamas]]). See [[moira]] and [[hector]].
