---
summary: In Andromache's picture of the orphan the feast is where the fatherless boy is turned away: the boy whose parents are both alive “drives him from the feast”, saying, “Off with you! Your father does not feast among us” (22.496–498).
grc: [δαιτύος, μεταδαίνυται]
---

δαιτύς, “feast”, is found only here in Homer, and μεταδαίνυμαι, “feast among”, once more in the Iliad, when Iris goes to share the Aethiopians' sacrifices (23.207). The feast is the place of a man's share among his peers, and the boy is refused his because his father is no longer one of them (see [[the-day-of-orphanhood]], [[childhood]] and [[oneidos]]).
