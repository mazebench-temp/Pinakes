---
summary: The gods watch the chase around the walls, and when Zeus gives way, Athena “went darting down from the peaks of Olympus” to bring Hector down (22.166–187).
---

“So saying he urged on Athena, who was eager already, / and she went darting down from the peaks of Olympus” (22.186–187; see [[athena]]). The two verses are those with which Zeus sent Athena down to make the Trojans break the truce in Book 4 (4.73–74; see [[pandarus-breaks-the-truce]]), and the second tells her descent in Book 2 as well (2.167), and with one word changed in Book 7 (7.19), and Thetis' descent in Book 24 (24.121). The poem does not say where the gods sit to watch the chase (22.166); Athena's descent shows that they are on Olympus (see [[zeus]], [[watching-the-war]] and [[not-all-the-gods-approve]]).

Hector names the god of Olympus before the chase begins: “let us see to which of us the Olympian will hold out triumph” (22.130; see [[olympian]]).
