---
summary: “Brilliant” is Achilles eight times, from Hector's memory of the night “when brilliant Achilles rose up” (22.102) to Andromache's fear “that brilliant Achilles has cut off my bold Hector” (22.455); and it is Hector four times, as Athena comes upon him, as Achilles' spear point is turned against him, and in the paean and the verse of the defilement (22.226, 22.320, 22.393, 22.395).
---

**Achilles.** The narrator names him δῖος Ἀχιλλεύς at each turn of the pursuit and the duel: he shakes his head at his men so that no one else may hit Hector (22.205), drives his spear at the throat (22.326), boasts over the fallen man (22.330), answers the dying man's prophecy (22.364), and, as ποδάρκης δῖος Ἀχιλλεύς, strips him (22.376; [[podarkes]]). Zeus uses the formula as he watches the pursuit, “and now brilliant Achilles / is pursuing him with swift feet around the city of Priam” (22.172–173).

**The verses of Patroclus' death.** “And to him, though he was dead, brilliant Achilles spoke” (22.364) is the verse with which Hector spoke to the dead Patroclus, with δῖος Ἀχιλλεύς in place of φαίδιμος Ἕκτωρ (16.858; [[phaidimos]]); the three verses before it are those of Patroclus' death (22.361–363 = 16.855–857; [[the-end-of-death]], [[leaving-manhood-and-youth]]).

**Brilliant Hector.** Achilles ends his paean with the epithet, “we have killed brilliant Hector” (22.393), and the next verse gives it to the body he means to defile: “He spoke, and devised shameful deeds against brilliant Hector” (22.395). The verse returns when he lays the body face down beside Patroclus' bier in the next book (23.24). See [[achilles]] and [[hector]].
