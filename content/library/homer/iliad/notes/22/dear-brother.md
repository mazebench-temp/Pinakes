---
summary: Athena, in the likeness of Deiphobus, twice addresses Hector as “Dear brother” (22.229, 22.239).
---

The first speech begins as Paris began his apology to Hector in Book 6, ἠθεῖʼ ἦ μάλα δή σε (22.229, as 6.518). Hector answers with gratitude to the brother he loved best, “Deiphobus, truly before now you were by far the dearest to me / of my brothers” (22.233–234), and the word of kinship is the lie by which the goddess leads him to his death (22.247; [[truth-and-deception]], [[deiphobus]]).
