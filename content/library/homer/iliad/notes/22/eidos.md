---
summary: When Hector is dead the Achaeans run up and “gazed at the stature and admirable looks / of Hector”, and then stab him (22.370–371).
---

The beauty of the young man dead in battle is what Priam had set against the old man's shame, “all that is seen of him is fair, dead though he is” (22.73; see [[all-is-fitting-for-the-young]]), and the dragging fouls it: his head lies in the dust, “that once was handsome” (22.402–403; see [[the-dragging-of-hector]] and [[watching-the-war]]). Athena comes to Hector “in the likeness of Deiphobus in form” (22.227; see [[truth-and-deception]]).
