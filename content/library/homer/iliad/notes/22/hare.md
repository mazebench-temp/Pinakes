---
summary: The hare of Book 22 is the prey of the eagle to which Hector is likened in his last charge, which goes down through the clouds “to snatch a tender lamb or a cowering hare” (22.310).
---

ἁρπάξων ἢ ἄρνʼ ἀμαλὴν ἤ πτῶκα λαγωόν, “to snatch a tender lamb or a cowering hare” (22.310; see [[the-eagle-flying-high]] and [[eagle]]). Only here does Homer join the hare's two names, πτώξ, “the cowerer”, from πτήσσω, “cower”, and λαγωός. In Book 17 the eagle's sight found the swift-footed hare crouching under a leafy bush (17.674–678; see [[the-eagle-and-the-hare]]). In this book the cowering belongs also to the fawn of the chase, which hides “cowering under a bush” (καταπτήξας, 22.191; see [[the-dog-and-the-fawn]]), from the same verb.
