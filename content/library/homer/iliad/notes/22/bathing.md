**The tripod.** ἀμφὶ πυρὶ στῆσαι τρίποδα μέγαν, ὄφρα, “to set a great tripod over the fire, so that”, is the verse in which Achilles told his companions to heat water and wash the clotted blood from the dead Patroclus (22.443 ≈ 18.344; see [[tripod]] and [[patroclus]]). There the bath was for a dead man; here it is made ready for a living one who is already dead. θερμὰ λοετρά, “a hot bath”, is what Nestor promises the wounded Machaon when Hecamede has heated the water (14.6), the ease of a man back from the fighting, as Odysseus and Diomedes bathe and anoint themselves when they come back from the night raid (10.572–577).

**Home from the battle.** μάχης ἐκ νοστήσαντι, “when he came home from the battle” (22.444), are the words in which Zeus, watching Hector put on Achilles' armor, foretold that he would not come home from the battle for Andromache to take the armor from him (μάχης ἐκνοστήσαντι, 17.207–208; see [[nostos]] and [[andromache]]). The narrator's judgment follows at once: νηπίη, “the fool”, “nor did she know that far from all baths / gray-eyed Athena had brought him down at the hands of Achilles” (22.445–446; see [[nepios]] and [[athena]]). τῆλε λοετρῶν, “far from all baths”, sets the bath at home against the death on the plain (see [[the-death-of-hector]]).

**The bath he is given.** Hector's body will be washed after all, in the last book, by the slave women of his killer: Achilles has them wash and anoint it with oil, away from Priam's sight, before it is given back (24.582–588; see [[honoring-the-dead]]). Andromache's own last gift to him is a burning of his clothes (22.510–514; see [[weaving]]).
