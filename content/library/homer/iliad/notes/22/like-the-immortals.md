---
summary: Hector taunts Achilles after his miss: “So, Achilles, like the gods, you did not yet at all / know my fate from Zeus” (22.279–280).
---

θεοῖς ἐπιείκελʼ Ἀχιλλεῦ is a vocative of address to Achilles at the end of the verse: Phoenix uses it twice in his speech (9.485, 9.494), Patroclus' ghost and Priam will use it (23.80, 24.486), and Agamemnon's ghost greets Achilles with it in the Odyssey (*Odyssey* 24.36). Only Hector speaks it in mockery, to a man who said he knew Hector's fate from Zeus. See [[achilles]] and [[hector]].
