summary: The fate of the city hangs on Hector. Fate holds him before Ilios while the Trojans crowd inside; Priam begs him to come in and save them, and foresees the sack; Hector thinks of buying peace with Helen and half the city's wealth. Achilles chases him three times around the city of Priam, and when he is dead the wailing in the city is as if all Ilios were burning. Andromache laments that they were born to one fate, he in Troy and she in Thebe, and that he alone guarded the city's gates and walls.

**Before Ilios.** The Trojans who fled from Achilles are inside, but “deadly fate fettered Hector to stay where he was, / in front of Ilios and the Scaean gates” (22.5–6; see [[scaean-gates]] and [[the-walls-of-troy]]). Walter Leaf reads Ἰλίου here, with Ahrens, as Ἰλίοο (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 22.6, referring to his note on 21.104; see the translation's note on 22.6). Apollo taunts Achilles that the Trojans “have crowded into the city” while he turned aside (22.12), and Achilles answers that but for the god many more “would have bitten the earth with their teeth before they reached Ilios” (22.17; see [[apollo-deceives-achilles]]).

**The city's fate.** Priam begs his son to come inside the wall, “so that you may save / the Trojans and the Trojan women” (22.56–57), and tells what he will see if Hector dies: “my sons perishing and my daughters dragged off, / and the chambers ravaged, and little children / dashed to the ground in grim combat” (22.62–64), and at last himself torn by his own dogs at his doors (22.66–76; see [[priam]] and [[sack-of-troy-foretold]]). Hector, debating with himself, finds it better “either to face Achilles, kill him, and come home, / or to perish gloriously at his hands before the city” (22.109–110). He thinks of offering Helen and all that Alexander “brought to Troy, which was the beginning of the quarrel” (22.115–116; see [[helen]] and [[paris]]), and besides of dividing in two “the wealth that the lovely citadel holds within it” (22.117–121). The verse is that of the besieged city on the Shield of Achilles, whose besiegers could not agree whether to sack it or to take half of all it held (18.511–512; see [[the-two-cities]]). Leaf takes the terms from these lines: half of the city's movable property left to the citizens, half taken by the besiegers as ransom (on 18.511; see the translation's note on 22.117–120).

**Around the city of Priam.** The two run “three times around the city of Priam” (22.165), and the phrase returns in Zeus' words, in Athena's as Deiphobus, and in Hector's (22.173, 22.230, 22.251; see [[the-pursuit-of-hector]]). Zeus remembers the thighs of oxen that Hector burned for him on Ida and “on the height of the city” (22.170–172; see [[ida]] and [[pergamus]]).

**Ilios mourns.** Over the body Achilles would make trial of the Trojans, “whether they will leave the height of the city now that this man has fallen” (22.383), and remembers instead Patroclus, lying unwept and unburied by the ships (22.385–390; see [[achilles]] and [[patroclus]]). When Hector is dragged before the city (see [[the-dragging-of-hector]]), the people “were seized by wailing and groaning throughout the city. / And it was most like as if all / beetling Ilios were smoldering with fire from top to bottom” (22.409–411). ὀφρυόεσσα, “beetling”, set on a brow (ὀφρύς), is found only here in Homer (see [[beetling]]). The poem will not tell the sack of Troy, but the city's grief for Hector is likened to its burning (see [[as-if-ilios-were-smoldering]] and [[sack-of-troy-foretold]]).

**The laments.** Hecuba calls him “my boast throughout the city” (22.433), a son whom the Trojans and the Trojan women greeted as a god (22.434–435; see [[hecuba]]). Andromache begins her lament with the two cities of their birth: “both of us, you in Troy in the house of Priam, / and I in Thebe under wooded Placus” (22.478–479; see [[thebe]] and [[andromache]]). Their son is called Astyanax, “lord of the city”, by the Trojans, “for you alone guarded their gates and long walls” (22.506–507; see [[astyanax]] and [[the-walls-of-troy]]).
