---
summary: Priam foresees the women of his house taken: “my sons perishing and my daughters dragged off”, and “my sons' wives dragged off by the deadly hands of the Achaeans” (22.62–65). In the simile of the chase the prize of a funeral race is “a tripod or a woman” (22.164).
refs: [22.62-65, 22.164]
---

The verb is ἕλκω, “drag”, used again of Hector's body behind Achilles' chariot (22.398, 22.464–465; see [[the-city-and-the-household]] and [[in-laws]]). The prizes of the simile are those that Achilles will set out as the first prize of the chariot race for Patroclus, a woman skilled in blameless handiwork and a tripod (23.262–264; see [[racehorses-at-the-turning-post]]). Andromache, the captive Hector foresaw in Book 6, does not yet speak of her own slavery in this book; her lament is for her son (22.484–507; see [[slavery-and-freedom]]).
