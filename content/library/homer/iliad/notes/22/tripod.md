summary: A tripod is one of the prizes in the simile of the chase, “a tripod or a woman, in honor of a man who has died” (22.164); and Andromache, not knowing that Hector is dead, has her handmaids “set a great tripod over the fire” for his bath (22.443).
grc: [τρίπος]
---

τρίπος (22.164) is the only nominative of the word in Homer. The prize of the simile belongs to funeral games (see [[contests]]), and the next book holds the games for Patroclus, where tripods are among the prizes. The tripod of the bath is the vessel set over the fire to heat water, as for washing the body of Patroclus, “they set the tripod for the bath on the blazing fire” (18.346; see [[bathing]]); Andromache's is heated for a living man who will not come home (22.442–446).
