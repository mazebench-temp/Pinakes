---
summary: Athena begins her protest with the third of the epithet's addresses in the Iliad: “Father, lord of the gleaming thunderbolt and of the dark clouds, what a thing you have said!” (22.178).
---

Each time the address is spoken by a god to Zeus: by Hera in Agamemnon's story (19.121), by Poseidon at the assembly of the gods (20.16), and here by Athena, who answers Zeus' pity for Hector with the words with which Hera answered his pity for Sarpedon, “A man who is mortal, long since ordained to his portion, / do you wish to set free again from grim-sounding death?” (22.179–180 = 16.441–442; [[not-all-the-gods-approve]], [[lord-of-dark-clouds]], [[what-a-word]]).
