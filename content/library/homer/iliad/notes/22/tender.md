---
summary: Achilles' spear goes “straight through the soft neck” of Hector (22.327); and the eagle of the simile swoops “to snatch a tender lamb” (22.310).
grc: [ἀμαλὴν]
---

**The soft neck.** ἀντικρὺ δʼ ἁπαλοῖο διʼ αὐχένος ἤλυθʼ ἀκωκή is the verse of Menelaus' killing of Euphorbus (17.49), and it returns in the Odyssey when Odysseus' first arrow kills Antinous (*Odyssey* 22.16). Here the point leaves Hector able to speak (22.328–329; [[heavy-with-bronze]]).

**The tender lamb.** ἀμαλός, “tender,” is another word, found once in each poem: of the lamb here (22.310) and of a bitch's tender puppies in the Odyssey (*Odyssey* 20.14). ἀμαλήν is new to the entry. See [[high-flying]] and [[hector]].
