---
summary: Gold is the ransom of the book. Priam would ransom Lycaon and Polydorus “with bronze and gold, for we have it at home” (22.50); Hector, dying, offers Achilles “bronze and gold in plenty” for his body (22.340), and Achilles refuses even if Priam should bid them “weigh out your own body in gold” (22.351). It is also the gods' metal: Zeus holds out “his golden scales” (22.209), and Hephaestus set “the fair plumes / of gold” on Achilles' helmet (22.315–316).
---

The ransom for Laothoe's sons would come from her father's gifts, for Altes “granted much to his daughter” (22.51; see [[altes]] and [[laothoe]]). A body's weight in gold is the utmost a ransom could be (see [[apoina]]); in the golden scales Zeus weighs two fates of death (22.209–213; see [[the-scales-of-zeus]] and [[helmet]]).
