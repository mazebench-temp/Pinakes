**The web.** μυχῷ δόμου ὑψηλοῖο, “in the inner part of the high house”, is the women's part of the house (see [[house]]). The web is told in the words of Helen's in Book 3, ἱστὸν ὕφαινε … δίπλακα πορφυρέην, “a double-folded purple cloth” (3.125–126; see [[double]] and [[purple]]), and the verb of the pattern is the same, ἐν … ἔπασσε here and ἐνέπασσεν, “working into it”, of Helen (see [[helen]]). Helen's pattern was the many trials of Trojans and Achaeans suffered for her sake (3.126–128); Andromache's is θρόνα ποικίλα, “flowers of many colors”, a word found only here, flowers or figures worked into the cloth, and not θρόνος, “throne” (see the translation's table of key words). She is at the work to which Hector sent her in Book 6, “the loom and the distaff” (6.490–492), and which he had foreseen her doing as a slave in Argos “at another woman's bidding” (6.456; see [[hector-and-andromache]] and [[captive-women]]).

**The shuttle.** At the cry from the tower, “her limbs shook, and the shuttle fell from her to the ground” (22.448; see [[andromache]] and [[lament]]). κερκίς, the weaver's shuttle or rod, is named in the Iliad only here; in the Odyssey Calypso weaves at her loom “with a golden shuttle” (χρυσείῃ κερκίδʼ, *Odyssey* 5.61–62; see [[loom]]).

**The clothes in the halls.** “yet there are clothes laid up for you in the halls, / fine and lovely, made by the hands of women. / But all of these I will burn up with blazing fire” (22.510–512). The cloth that the women of a great house wove was its wealth, like Hecuba's robes, “the work of women of the Sidonians”, from which she chose a gift for Athena (6.288–295; see [[robe]]), and the robes and cloaks and tunics that Priam will take from his chests for Hector's ransom (24.228–231). λεπτά τε καὶ χαρίεντα, “fine and lovely”, is said in the Odyssey of the work of goddesses at the loom, when Circe is heard singing as she weaves (*Odyssey* 10.221–223). Burned without the body they were made for, Hector's clothes are to be his fame among the Trojans (22.513–514; see [[honoring-the-dead]] and [[kleos]]).
