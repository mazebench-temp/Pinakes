summary: Hecuba fears that she will not weep for Hector “on a bier” (22.87), and Achilles tells him that his mother will not “lay you on a bier and lament for you” (22.353); Andromache remembers the child sleeping “in a bed, in the arms of his nurse, / in a soft bed” (22.503–504).
grc: [λέκτροισιν]
---

λέχεα is the bed on which the dead are laid out for mourning, as Patroclus was laid “on a bier” (18.233, 18.352; see [[lament]] and [[honoring-the-dead]]). Hecuba's fear and Achilles' threat are the same: Hector's body will lie unburied, for the dogs (22.86–89, 22.352–354). λέκτροισιν, “in a bed” (22.503), is found only here in the Iliad; with εὐνῇ ἔνι μαλακῇ, “in a soft bed” (22.504), it belongs to the life the orphan has lost (see [[the-day-of-orphanhood]] and [[astyanax]]).
