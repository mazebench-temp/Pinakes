---
summary: The cattle of Book 22 are the oxen whose thighs Hector burned for Zeus on Ida and on the citadel of Troy, which Zeus remembers as he watches him run for his life (22.168–172).
# βοῶν ἐπὶ μηρίʼ (22.170); βοῶν alone is also the participle of βοάω "shout".
grc: ["βοῶν ἐπὶ μηρίʼ"]
---

ἐμὸν δʼ ὀλοφύρεται ἦτορ / Ἕκτορος, ὅς μοι πολλὰ βοῶν ἐπὶ μηρίʼ ἔκηεν / Ἴδης ἐν κορυφῇσι πολυπτύχου, ἄλλοτε δʼ αὖτε / ἐν πόλει ἀκροτάτῃ, “and my heart grieves / for Hector, who has burned many thighs of oxen for me / on the peaks of many-folded Ida, and at other times / on the height of the city” (22.169–172; see [[zeus]] and [[sacrifice]]). The thighs wrapped in fat are the gods' portion of the sacrifice, and a god who has had them owes the man his favor (see [[reciprocity-with-the-gods]]). Zeus remembers Hector's oxen, and asks the gods whether to save him; Athena objects, and he lets her go (22.174–185). The other oxen of the book are their hides: an ox-hide (βοείη) is the kind of prize that men win with their feet in a race, and this race is not run for one (22.159–161; see [[racehorses-at-the-turning-post]]), and the straps of oxhide by which Achilles ties Hector's feet to his chariot are βοέους … ἱμάντας (22.397; see [[the-dragging-of-hector]]).
