summary: Achilles answers Apollo, Hector's proposal and Hector's plea in the formula (22.14, 22.260, 22.344); “swift Achilles” drives Hector on and, in the false Deiphobus' words, presses him hard (22.188, 22.229); and Hector cannot escape “the swift-footed son of Peleus” (22.193).
---

**The pursuit.** Book 22 turns the epithet into the action. Apollo begins with a taunt, “Why, son of Peleus, do you pursue me with your swift feet” (22.8), Zeus and the false Deiphobus both speak of Achilles pursuing Hector “with swift feet around the city of Priam” (22.173, 22.230; [[swift]]), and the dog of the simile runs on tracking the fawn, “so Hector did not escape the notice of the swift-footed son of Peleus” (22.189–193; ποδώκεα, the compound named in the entry). Yet the race is a dream in which “the one could not catch him with his feet, nor the other escape” (22.199–201).

**Looking darkly.** The two refusals are spoken in the same verse, “Looking at him darkly, swift-footed Achilles said” (22.260, 22.344; [[looking-darkly]]): to Hector's offer that the victor give back the body, and to his dying plea for burial. See [[achilles]] and [[son-of-peleus]].
