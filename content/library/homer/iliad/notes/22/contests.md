summary: The chase around the walls is likened to a race, and the narrator names the prize: it was “no beast for sacrifice or ox-hide … such as are prizes for the feet of men”, but the life of Hector; and the two run like horses rounding the turning posts at the games “in honor of a man who has died” (22.159–166).
grc: ["ἀέθλια γίγνεται", "μέγα κεῖται ἄεθλον"]
---

The two runners are not racing for a prize, for “they ran for the life of Hector, tamer of horses” (22.161; see [[a-mans-soul-cannot-come-back]]). The second comparison is to a chariot race at a dead man's funeral games, where “a great prize is set out, / a tripod or a woman” (22.163–164; see [[tripod]] and [[racehorses-at-the-turning-post]]). The games of the simile anticipate the next book, where Achilles holds games for the dead Patroclus. ἀέθλια, “prizes”, is the word of the horses that won prizes with their feet in Agamemnon's offer (9.124, 9.266).
