---
summary: Achilles rushes toward the city “like a prize-winning horse with its chariot” (22.22); and he and Hector run around the walls as “prize-winning single-hoofed horses round the turning posts” (22.162).
grc: [ἀεθλοφόρος, ἀεθλοφόροι]
---

The forms with ἀε-, ἀεθλοφόρος and ἀεθλοφόροι, are new to the entry (see the translation's table of formulas, under ἵππος ἀθλοφόρος). In the second simile the race is for a prize “in honor of a man who has died,” and the narrator adds that the two runners strove for no such prize, “but they ran for the life of Hector, tamer of horses” (22.159–164; [[tamer-of-horses]], [[single-hoofed]]). See [[like-a-racehorse]], [[achilles]] and [[horses]].
