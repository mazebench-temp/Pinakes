summary: Deiphobus himself stays inside the wall, but his likeness comes out. Athena takes his form and voice, tells Hector that she has come out to stand by him, and leads him on to face Achilles; Hector greets him as the dearest of his brothers. When Hector's spear has failed and he calls to Deiphobus of the white shield for another, he is nowhere near, and Hector knows that Athena has deceived him (22.226–247, 22.294–299).
# The false Deiphobus' words (22.229-231, 22.239-246) and Hector's to and of him (22.235-237, 22.295, 22.299).
refs: [22.229-231, 22.235-237, 22.239-246, 22.295, 22.299]
---

**The likeness.** When Zeus' scales have sunk for Hector, Athena leaves Achilles to catch his breath and comes to Hector “in the likeness of Deiphobus in form and in tireless voice” (22.214–227; see [[athena]]). The verse brought Poseidon to the two Ajaxes in the likeness of Calchas and Athena to Menelaus in the likeness of Phoenix (13.45, 17.555), each time to help; this time the help is a lure (see [[truth-and-deception]]). The false brother greets him ἠθεῖε, “Dear brother” (22.229, 22.239), the word with which Paris greeted Hector at the Scaean gates in Book 6 (6.518; see [[dear-brother]]), and says what Zeus said to the gods, that Achilles is “pursuing you with swift feet around the city of Priam” (22.230 ≈ 22.173). Let them stand and beat him off together (22.231).

**The dearest brother.**

{{quote:a98e6209-bf77-5612-bb45-516f946e0db0}}

The poem has not spoken of this love before. In Book 13 Deiphobus fought at the ships with Aeneas, Paris and Agenor, until Meriones wounded him in the arm and Polites led him out of the battle; Hector looked for him among the champions and learned from Paris that he had gone, wounded (13.402–539, 13.758–783; see [[aeneas]], [[polites]] and [[paris]]). Here the brother to whom Hector speaks is a goddess, and she answers with a story made to match what has just happened to Hector himself. Their father and their lady mother, she says, begged her one after the other to stay inside, and their companions too, “so much do they all tremble before him”, but grief wore her heart down (22.239–242). Hector has just refused the same pleas from Priam and Hecuba (22.38–91; see [[priam]] and [[hecuba]]). She ends with the call to fight, “and let there be no sparing / of spears” (22.243–244); φειδωλή, “sparing”, occurs nowhere else in Homer, and a spear is what Hector will lack. “So saying, Athena led the way by craft” (22.247).

**Nowhere near.** Achilles' spear misses, and Athena gives it back to him unseen; Hector's glances off Achilles' shield, “and he stood downcast, for he had no other ash spear” (22.273–293).

{{quote:bba0b963-8200-58fb-b541-8f30fe88ef89}}

λευκάσπις, “of the white shield” (22.294), is used only here in Homer, the narrator's word for the brother whom Hector thinks is beside him. “For I thought that the hero Deiphobus was beside me” (22.298): the real Deiphobus is among the others who, as Hector said to the false one, “stay inside” (22.237), ἐν τείχει, “within the wall” (22.299). He does not come out again in the poem. In the last book he is one of the nine sons whom Priam abuses while he makes ready to ransom Hector (24.248–262; see [[sons-of-priam]]).
