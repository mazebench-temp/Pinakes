---
summary: Priam fears that Zeus “will destroy in a grievous fate” the old man he is (22.60–61); Athena tells Zeus that Hector is “A man who is mortal, long since ordained to his portion” (22.179); in the scales “the fated day of Hector sank” (22.212); and Andromache begins her lament, “We were both born, it seems, to one fate” (22.477).
---

Athena's verse is Hera's over Sarpedon (22.179 = 16.441; see [[not-all-the-gods-approve]] and [[gods-and-mortals]]), and the sinking of αἴσιμον ἦμαρ, “the fated day”, is the line of the scales in Book 8, where the day that sank was the Achaeans' (22.212; 8.72; see [[the-scales-of-zeus]]). Andromache's one portion, ἰῇ … αἴσῃ, joins two births in two cities, Hector's in Troy and hers in Thebe under Placus (22.477–481; see [[moira]], [[andromache]] and [[husbands-and-wives]]).
