summary: The book's children are remembered and imagined. Hecuba holds up the breast that nursed Hector (22.80–83); Priam pictures his grandchildren, “little children / dashed to the ground” in the sack (22.63–64); and Andromache sets Astyanax's infancy, when he sat “on his father's knees”, ate marrow and slept “in the arms of his nurse” after his “childish play” (22.500–504), beside his life as an orphan (22.490–499).
refs: [22.63-64, 22.80-83, 22.484-507]
---

νηπιαχεύων, “his childish play”, is found only here in Homer (22.502; see [[nepios]]). The orphan's day is told in the present tense, as something that always happens to such a child, and then turned back to the one boy, Astyanax (22.490–500; see [[astyanax]], [[the-day-of-orphanhood]] and [[chere]]). In Priam's list of the sack the little children come between the daughters dragged off and the sons' wives (22.62–65; see [[the-city-and-the-household]] and [[captive-women]]).
