---
summary: Glory is what the duel decides. Achilles complains to Apollo, “now you have taken great glory from me” (22.18); Priam begs Hector not to “hold out great glory / to the son of Peleus” (22.57–58); Achilles keeps his men from throwing, “lest someone win the glory by hitting him” (22.207); Athena promises “great glory for the Achaeans” (22.217); and over the body Achilles cries, “We have won great glory; we have killed brilliant Hector” (22.393). Hecuba's lament gives the word to Hector himself: “truly you were their very great glory / while you lived” (22.435–436).

μέγα κῦδος, “great glory”, comes five times in the book (22.18, 22.57, 22.217, 22.393, 22.435), more often than in any other book of Homer. κῦδος ὀρέξαι, “hold out glory”, has a god for its subject everywhere else in Homer, Zeus or Athena or a δαίμων (5.33, 5.260, 11.79; *Odyssey* 4.275); Priam's μὴ δὲ μέγα κῦδος ὀρέξῃς makes Hector the giver, if he dies (22.57; see [[priam]]). Hector leaves the choice to the god, “let us see to which of us the Olympian will hold out triumph” (εὖχος ὀρέξῃ, 22.130), where Sarpedon and Idomeneus had made the fighters the givers, to “hold out triumph to another, or another to us” (12.328, 13.327; see [[euchomai]]). Glory is not to be shared: Achilles will not let another man hit Hector and leave him “second” (22.205–207; see [[watching-the-war]] and [[the-pursuit-of-hector]]).

Hecuba's phrase for her son is the one that honors Odysseus and Nestor in the address μέγα κῦδος Ἀχαιῶν, “great glory of the Achaeans” (9.673, 10.87), said of a Trojan and in the past tense (22.435; see [[hecuba]] and [[lament]]).
