---
summary: Achilles answers Apollo's revelation, “You have foiled me, Far-Worker, most baneful of all the gods” (22.15); and Athena tells him that Hector cannot escape, “not even if far-working Apollo should go to great lengths, / groveling before aegis-bearing father Zeus” (22.220–221).
---

Book 21 ended with the Far-Worker drawing Achilles away from the city in Agenor's likeness (21.599–605), and Book 22 opens with the god revealing himself (22.7–13). Achilles' answer begins with the vocative and a verb that Homer uses in this sense of the work of gods, ἔβλαψας, “you have foiled me” (22.15; see the translation's note on 22.15). The second use is Athena's picture of the brother god powerless before Zeus once the scales have sunk (22.219–221; [[father-zeus]], [[aegis-bearing]]). See [[apollo]] and [[deadly]].
