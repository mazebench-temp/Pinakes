summary: The narrator calls Priam “the old man” through his plea: “the old man Priam was the first to see him” (22.25), “the old man groaned aloud” (22.33), “the old man addressed him pitifully” (22.37), “The old man spoke” (22.77). His argument is his age, “on the threshold of old age” (22.60), and the death of “an old man who is slain” (22.75); after Hector's death the people hold back “the old man” (22.412), who hopes that Achilles will pity “my old age” (22.420).
grc: [γήραος]
---

γέρων also gives Altes, Laothoe's father, his title, “the old man of famous name” (22.51; see [[altes]]), and the adjective γερούσιος the “oath of the elders” that Hector imagines taking from the Trojans (22.119), the adjective of the elders' wine in Book 4 (4.259; see [[oath]]). Priam sets the old man's death against the young man's: for the young “it is all fitting”, and the old man's body given to the dogs is “the most pitiable thing” (22.71–76; see [[all-is-fitting-for-the-young]], [[old-age-and-memory]] and [[pity]]).
