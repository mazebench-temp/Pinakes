---
summary: Hector waits like a serpent at its hole into which “dread anger has come” (22.93–95), and when his spear glances off Achilles' shield, “Hector was angry / that his swift missile had flown from his hand in vain” (22.291–292).
---

The serpent's χόλος comes from the “evil drugs” it has eaten, and it glares and coils as it waits (22.94–95; see [[the-serpent-at-its-hole]]). Hector's anger is short: he stands “downcast”, with no second spear, and calls for Deiphobus (22.293–295; see [[menos]] and [[truth-and-deception]]).
