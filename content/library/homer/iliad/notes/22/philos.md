summary: Achilles refuses Hector the word: “so it is not possible for you and me to be friends” (22.265). Elsewhere in the book φίλος is the word of the family and of the gods' favor. Priam begs “Hector, dear child” (22.38), and wishes that Achilles were as dear to the gods as he is to him (22.41–42); Zeus calls Hector “a man dear to me” (22.168); and Hector, deceived, tells the false Deiphobus that he was “by far the dearest to me / of my brothers” (22.233–234), and then, seeing his death near, that “this long ago was dearer / to Zeus” (22.300–302).
grc: [φιλήμεναι]
---

φιλήμεναι, “to be friends”, stands where Hector had asked for agreements sworn before the gods: wolves and lambs, Achilles says, do not have hearts at one (22.261–266; see [[oath]] and [[thymos]]). Both men are dear to Zeus: Athena greets Achilles with Διῒ φίλε, “dear to Zeus” (22.216), as Zeus has just called Hector φίλον ἄνδρα (22.168; see [[zeus]]). After the killing the word goes back to the dead and the mourners, “my dear companion” for Patroclus (22.390; see [[hetairos]]), “his dear father” for Priam (22.408), and the “dear father” whom Astyanax has lost (22.505; see [[fathers-and-sons]]).
