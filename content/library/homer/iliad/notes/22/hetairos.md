---
summary: Achilles fights for his companions: Hector will now “pay back all at once / the sorrows of my companions, whom you killed raging with your spear” (22.271–272), and over the body he remembers the one still unburied, “yet even there I will remember my dear companion” (22.390). In Andromache's picture the orphan goes begging to “his father's companions” (22.492).
---

The false Deiphobus invents companions too: “our companions around me” begged him to stay inside (22.240; see [[truth-and-deception]] and [[deiphobus]]). Achilles' vow to remember Patroclus in the house of Hades, where “men forget the dead”, turns him from his plan to test the city back to the ships and the paean (22.381–394; see [[patroclus]] and [[honoring-the-dead]]). The companions of the dead father, in Andromache's lament, give his son pity for a moment and a cup that only wets his lips (22.492–495; see [[childhood]] and [[astyanax]]).
