summary: When Hector calls for his brother and no one is there, he knows the trick: “but he is within the wall, and Athena has deceived me” (ἐξαπάτησεν, 22.299).
# ἐξαπάτησεν: in the Iliad only here (also Odyssey 9.414).
grc: [ἐξαπάτησεν]
---

Athena has come to Hector in the likeness of Deiphobus and led him on “by craft” to stand against Achilles (22.226–247; see [[athena]] and [[truth-and-deception]]). ἐξαπάτησεν, “has deceived”, is the strong compound of ἀπατάω. Achilles used it of Agamemnon, “if he still hopes to deceive some other of the Danaans” (ἐξαπατήσειν, 9.371), and its kin ἐξαπαφίσκω, “beguile”, is the verb of Agamemnon's words and of Hera's plan against Zeus (9.376, 14.160; see [[the-deception-of-zeus]]). Here the deceiver is a god and the deceived man learns it in time to say so, and no more: “Ah, truly the gods have called me to my death” (22.297; see [[hector]] and [[seeing-the-gods]]). In the *Odyssey* the same form is Odysseus' own boast, how his name and his cunning deceived the Cyclopes (*Odyssey* 9.413–414).
