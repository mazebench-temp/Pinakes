---
summary: Hecuba is “his mother” at each of her appearances: she mourns and holds up her breast (22.79–80), tears her hair when she sees the body (22.405–407), and Priam names her “his mother, most ill-fated, who bore him” (22.428). Both men speak of her as πότνια μήτηρ, “lady mother”: Hector offers the gifts his father and mother will give (22.341), and Achilles answers that “not even so shall your lady mother / lay you on a bier” (22.352–353).
---

The false Deiphobus invents a plea from “our father and our lady mother” (22.239; see [[truth-and-deception]]). Laothoe, the mother of Lycaon and Polydorus, shares Priam's sorrow (22.53), and Andromache's orphan goes “to his widowed mother” (22.499; see [[mothers-and-sons]] and [[chere]]).
