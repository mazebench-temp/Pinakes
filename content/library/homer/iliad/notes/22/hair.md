---
summary: Priam pulls at the gray hairs of his head as he begs Hector to come inside (22.77); Hector's dark hair spreads out in the dust as he is dragged (22.401–402); and Hecuba tears her hair when she sees it (22.406).
---

Tearing the hair is the gesture of grief, as of Agamemnon in the night (10.15) and of Achilles at the news of Patroclus' death (18.27; see the translation's note on 10.15–16). Priam's are the gray hairs of an old man (see [[old-age-and-memory]]), and Hector's hair, κυάνεαι χαῖται, “his dark hair”, lies in the dust with the head “that once was handsome” (22.401–403; see [[the-dragging-of-hector]]).
