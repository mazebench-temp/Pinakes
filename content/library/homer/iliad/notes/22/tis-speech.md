**Hector's imagined speech.** ποτέ τις εἴπῃσι is the frame of Hector's imagined speeches in Book 6, of a man who would see Andromache in slavery and of one who would praise his son (6.459–462, 6.479). Here, with μή, “lest some day another man … should say,” it is a speech he fears (22.106–107), and its close, ὣς ἐρέουσιν, “So they will say,” is new to the entry (22.108; [[aidos]], [[hector]]). In the Odyssey Nausicaa closes an imagined speech of the townspeople with the same words (*Odyssey* 6.285).

**Over the body.** ὧδε δέ τις εἴπεσκεν ἰδὼν ἐς πλησίον ἄλλον and ὣς ἄρα τις εἴπεσκε frame the jeer of the Achaeans, with the iterative verb: each man said it as he stabbed the body (22.371–375).
