---
summary: Hector, weighing an offer to Achilles, names the cause of the war: Helen and “the possessions with her, / all, as many as Alexander in his hollow ships / brought to Troy, which was the beginning of the quarrel” (22.114–116).
grc: [νείκεος]
---

νείκεος ἀρχή, “the beginning of the quarrel”, is found only here. The poem has laid the beginning on Paris before: Menelaus speaks of the sufferings borne “because Alexander began it” (3.100), and Phereclus built for Alexander the ships “that began the evil” (5.62–63; see [[paris]], [[helen]] and [[who-is-to-blame]]). Hector gives up the thought of returning them, since Achilles “will not pity me” (22.123; see [[eris]]).
