summary: Hector stays outside because of the λαός he has lost: “since I have destroyed the army by my own recklessness”, someone baser will say, “Hector trusted in his own strength and destroyed the army” (22.104–107). Priam had set the people's sorrow beside the parents' (22.54); Achilles keeps his men from throwing at Hector (22.205); and after the death the people wail around Priam and can hardly hold him back at the gates (22.408–413).
---

ὤλεσα λαόν, “I have destroyed the army”, is Agamemnon's confession in Books 2 and 9, “when I have lost so many men” (2.115, 9.22), and in the Odyssey it is said of Eurymedon, king of the Giants, who “destroyed his reckless people, and perished himself” (*Odyssey* 7.60); Hector joins it to his own recklessness (see [[atasthalie]], [[polydamas]] and [[who-is-to-blame]]). He is still “shepherd of the people” when Athena tricks him (22.277; see [[shepherd-of-the-people]]).
