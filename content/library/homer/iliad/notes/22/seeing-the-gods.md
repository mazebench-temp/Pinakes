---
summary: Achilles has chased Apollo across the plain without knowing him, and the god tells him so: “Not even yet / have you recognized that I am a god” (22.9–10). Hector takes Athena for his brother Deiphobus, does not see her give Achilles back his spear, and knows her only when his brother is not there: “And Hector understood in his heart” (22.296).
refs: [22.8-13, 22.226-237, 22.276-277, 22.294-299]
---

**Two recognitions.** ἔγνως, “have you recognized” (22.10), and ἔγνω, “understood” (22.296), are the same verb, γιγνώσκω, the word of Athena's gift to Diomedes, “so that you may clearly tell god from man” (5.128). Achilles does not know Apollo until the god tells him what he is, and the god still has Agenor's likeness when he speaks (Walter Leaf, *Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 22.7; see [[apollo-deceives-achilles]] and [[divine-epiphany]]). Hector knows Athena only by her work, when it is done (22.297–299; see [[truth-and-deception]]). In the *Odyssey* Athena, putting off a disguise, tells Odysseus that not even he recognized her (οὐδὲ σύ γʼ ἔγνως, *Odyssey* 13.299–300), and he answers that it is hard for a mortal who meets a goddess to know her, however skilled he is, since she makes herself like anyone (*Odyssey* 13.312–313).

**Form and voice.** Athena takes Deiphobus' shape and his voice, δέμας καὶ ἀτειρέα φωνήν, “in form and in tireless voice” (22.227; see [[deiphobus]] and [[tireless]]), and Hector believes his eyes: he thanks the false brother for coming out “when you saw me with your eyes” (22.236; see [[watching-the-war]]). What she does in the fight he does not see, for she gives Achilles back his spear “unseen by Hector” (λάθε δʼ Ἕκτορα, 22.277; see [[athena]]). To Achilles she comes as herself, and he obeys her (22.214–224).
