---
summary: When Andromache sees Hector dragged behind the chariot and faints, she flings from her head “the veil that golden Aphrodite had given her / on the day when Hector of the gleaming helmet took her” (22.470–471).
---

The goddess is named in the book only in this memory of a wedding (22.468–472; see [[andromache]], [[veil]] and [[golden]]). The gift fits the province Zeus gave her in Book 5, “the lovely works of marriage” (5.429). Another wedding gift of the gods is dated the same way: the armor that the gods gave Peleus “on the day when they put you into the bed of a mortal man” (18.84–85; see [[the-armor-of-peleus]]) is the armor Hector wears when he dies (22.322–323). A poem of Sappho tells the wedding itself (see [[sappho-wedding-of-hector-and-andromache]]). Achilles has told Hector that “the dogs and the birds will divide you all among them” (22.354); in the next book Aphrodite keeps the dogs from his body day and night and anoints it with oil of roses (23.185–187).
