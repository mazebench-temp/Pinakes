---
summary: Achilles answers Apollo's revelation, “Deeply troubled, swift-footed Achilles said to him” (22.14); and Hector, waiting at the gates, “deeply troubled … spoke to his own proud heart” (22.98).
---

**Achilles.** τὸν δὲ μέγʼ ὀχθήσας προσέφη πόδας ὠκὺς Ἀχιλλεύς is the verse of his answers to Patroclus' plea, to Thetis' warning and to the horse Xanthus' prophecy (16.48, 18.97, 19.419). Here it answers the god who has foiled him: “You have foiled me, Far-Worker, most baneful of all the gods” (22.15; [[far-worker]]).

**Hector.** The whole verse opens the fourth and last of the Iliad's battlefield monologues, as it opened those of Odysseus, Menelaus and Agenor (22.98 = 11.403 = 17.90 = 21.552; [[pondering-two-ways]], [[great-hearted]]). See [[achilles]] and [[hector]].
