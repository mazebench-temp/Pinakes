---
summary: Ares' name stands for war. Priam's young man lies “killed in battle” (ἄρηϊ κταμένῳ, 22.72), and Achilles refuses Hector any oath “before one or the other has fallen / and glutted Ares with blood” (22.266–267). Achilles comes on “the equal of Enyalius”, the war god under his other name (22.132).
---

**War itself.** For a young man, Priam says, all is fitting, ἄρηϊ κταμένῳ, “killed in battle” (22.72; see [[death-in-battle]]). The god's name is the battle, as when Achilles tells Lycaon that someone will take his life too “in the work of Ares” (Ἄρῃ, 21.112), and the flies feed on men ἀρηϊφάτους, “slain in battle” (19.31).

**The oath of blood.** There will be no oaths between Achilles and Hector, “before one or the other has fallen / and glutted Ares with blood, the warrior with the oxhide shield” (22.266–267; see [[no-oaths-between-lions-and-men]], [[with-the-oxhide-shield]] and [[oath]]). Diomedes said the same to Pandarus and Aeneas as they drove at him (5.288–289), and the narrator used the last verse in Book 20 of Achilles' own longing, whose heart bade him glut Ares with Hector's blood above all (20.75–78). Now he says it to Hector (see [[achilles]] and [[hector]]).

**The equal of Enyalius.** “the equal of Enyalius, the warrior of the shaking helmet” (22.132; see [[enyalius]] and [[equal-of-ares]]). The comparison was Hector's when he led the Trojans, “the equal of Ares, ruin of mortals” (11.295, 13.802), and Patroclus' when he came out of Achilles' hut, “the equal of Ares; and this was the beginning of evil for him” (11.604). Now it is said of Achilles as he comes to kill Hector.
