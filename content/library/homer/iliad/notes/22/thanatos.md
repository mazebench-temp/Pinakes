---
summary: Hector's death comes near by stages. Zeus asks whether to “save him from death” (22.175), Athena calls it “grim-sounding death” (22.180), and the narrator asks how Hector could have “escaped the fates of death” but for Apollo (22.202). Hector, deceived, sees that “the gods have called me to my death” and that “evil death is near me, and no longer far off” (22.297–300). “The end of death covered him” (22.361), and Achilles speaks to him “though he was dead”: “Lie dead” (22.364–365).

τέθναθι, “Lie dead”, the perfect imperative, “be dead”, is found only here, said to a man who has already died (22.365; see [[the-short-life-of-achilles]]). θάνατόνδε, “to my death”, with the suffix of direction, is also found only here (22.297). The dead are the subject of Achilles' vow to Patroclus: “even if in the house of Hades men forget the dead, / yet even there I will remember my dear companion” (22.389–390; see [[hades]] and [[hetairos]]).

The mourners date their grief from the death: Hecuba asks why she should live “now that you are dead” (22.431–432), and Andromache tells Hector that he will be no blessing to his son, “since you are dead” (22.486). Priam had set the young man's death against the old man's, “all that is seen of him is fair, dead though he is” (22.73; see [[death-in-battle]] and [[pity]]).
