---
summary: With Hector dead, Achilles proposes a test of the city: “come, let us make trial around the city in our armor, / so that we may know what mind the Trojans have” (22.381–382), whether they will leave the citadel or stay. He drops it in the next breath for Patroclus, who lies unburied (22.385–390).
grc: [πειρηθῶμεν]
---

The trial is an armed probe, to learn the enemy's mind without assault (22.381–384; see [[noos]] and [[the-walls-of-troy]]). It is broken off with the line of a man arguing with himself, “But why does my heart debate these things with me?” (22.385; see [[thymos]] and [[pondering-two-ways]]).
