# Unnamed: the chariot and the pair (22.398-400) and the swift horses that drag Hector (22.464-465).
refs: [22.398-400, 22.464-465]
---

Achilles ties Hector's feet to the chariot, mounts it with the armor, and drives the team himself: “he whipped them to a run, and the pair flew on not unwilling” (22.399–400; see [[the-dragging-of-hector]] and [[chariot]]). The verse is said of eager teams from Book 5 on (5.366, 8.45, 11.519). τώ, “the pair”, is a dual. Achilles' team is the two immortal horses, Xanthus and Balius, that the gods gave to Peleus. They wept for Patroclus beside the battle (17.426–440), and on the morning of this day Achilles called to them, as they were yoked, not to leave him dead on the field as they had left Patroclus (19.392–403). Now they drag Patroclus' killer. Andromache, coming to the wall, sees him “being dragged in front of the city; and the swift horses / were dragging him ruthlessly toward the hollow ships of the Achaeans” (22.464–465; see [[andromache]] and [[horses]]).
