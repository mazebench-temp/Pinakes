summary: Zeus remembers the thighs of oxen that Hector burned for him on Ida and “on the height of the city” (22.170–172), and Achilles wonders whether the Trojans “will leave the height of the city now that this man has fallen” (22.383).
refs: [22.172, 22.383]
---

Book 22 does not name Pergamus. ἐν πόλει ἀκροτάτῃ (22.172) and πόλιν ἄκρην (22.383) are the phrase used of the temple of Athena and the houses of Priam and Hector (ἐν πόλει ἄκρῃ, 6.88, 6.297, 6.317) and of the place of the Trojans' assembly (7.345); Pergamus itself is “the height of Pergamus” (Περγάμῳ ἄκρῃ, 5.460; Περγάμου ἄκρης, 6.512). The altar where Hector sacrificed to Zeus stood there (see [[thigh-pieces]]). Achilles' question is whether, with Hector dead, the Trojans will give up the citadel; he breaks it off to remember Patroclus (22.385–390; see [[troy]]).
