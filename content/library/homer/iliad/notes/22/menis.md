---
summary: The dying Hector warns Achilles that his body may bring the gods' wrath on him: “Take care now, lest I become a cause of the gods' wrath against you, / on that day when Paris and Phoebus Apollo / destroy you” (22.358–360).
grc: [μήνιμα]
en: [a cause of the gods' wrath]
---

μήνιμα, a thing that causes μῆνις, is found in Homer only here and in the same half-verse in the Odyssey, where the ghost of the unburied Elpenor asks Odysseus to burn and bury him, “lest I become a cause of the gods' wrath against you” (*Odyssey* 11.73; see the translation's note on 22.358). Both speakers ask for burial, and both warn of the gods' anger, not their own. The wrath of the poem's first word was Achilles' (1.1); here Achilles is the one who may suffer it (see [[honoring-the-dead]], [[death-of-achilles-foretold]] and [[prophecy]]).
