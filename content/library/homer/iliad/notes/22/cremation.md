---
summary: Hector, dying, asks for his body to be given back “so that when I am dead / the Trojans and the wives of the Trojans may give me my portion of fire” (22.342–343), and Achilles refuses (22.345–354). Andromache, who will not have the body, will burn the clothes laid up for him “with blazing fire” (22.510–512).
---

The request is the verse of Hector's challenge in Book 7 (7.79–80; see [[honoring-the-dead]]). In the burning of the clothes without the body Walter Leaf sees a hint of a cenotaph, which Homer never mentions (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 21.323; see [[fame-and-the-tomb]]). Hector's own pyre waits for the last book (24.782–801; see [[pyres]]).
