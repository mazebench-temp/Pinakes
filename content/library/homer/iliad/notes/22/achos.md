---
summary: Priam speaks of his grief before Hector's death and after it. If Achilles were dead and the dogs ate him, “truly then dread grief would go from my heart” (22.42–43); when Hector is dead, Priam mourns all his sons less, “grieved though I am, / as for one, for whom sharp grief will bring me down to the house of Hades” (22.424–425).
---

ἄχος ὀξύ, “sharp grief”, is what struck Zeus when Hera told him that Eurystheus was born before Heracles (19.125; see [[the-birth-of-heracles]]). Priam's grief has a cause, the man who “has made me bereft of many brave sons” (22.44; see [[bereaved-parents]], [[algos]] and [[penthos]]).
