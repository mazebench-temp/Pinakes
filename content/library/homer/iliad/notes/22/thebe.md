**Born at Thebe.** “Hector, unhappy that I am! We were both born, it seems, to one fate, / both of us, you in Troy in the house of Priam, / and I in Thebe under wooded Placus, / in the house of Eetion, who reared me when I was little” (22.477–480; see [[andromache]] and [[eetion]]). The verse joins the two cities that Achilles destroys, the one already sacked and the one whose defender he has just killed (see [[sack-of-thebe]] and [[troy]]). ὑπὸ Πλάκῳ ὑληέσσῃ, “under wooded Placus”, is the half-verse with which Andromache's father and mother were first named, Eetion “who lived beneath wooded Placus”, and her mother, “who was queen beneath wooded Placus” (6.396, 6.425; see [[placus]] and [[wooded]]).

**The plural.** Here the city's name is plural, Θήβῃσιν, the form that Boeotian Thebes takes when Diomedes says that his father Tydeus left him, still little, “when the army of the Achaeans perished at Thebes” (6.222–223; see [[thebes]] and [[tydeus-at-thebes]]); in Book 6 Eetion's city was singular, Θήβῃ Ὑποπλακίῃ, “Thebe under Placus” (6.397), and Θήβην, “high-gated Thebe” (6.416). The mountain keeps the two apart.
