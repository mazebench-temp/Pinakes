---
summary: Alone before the gates, Hector weighs his courses aloud: to go within the walls and bear Polydamas' reproach, or to lay down his arms and offer Achilles terms; he decides to fight: “Better to drive together in strife as quickly as may be” (22.98–130).
refs: [22.99-130]
---

Hector's is the last of the Iliad's four battlefield monologues, after those of Odysseus, Menelaus and Agenor, and it turns with the same verse, “But why does my heart debate these things with me?” (22.122 = 11.407, 17.97, 21.562; see [[pondering-two-ways]]). It weighs three courses: to go inside (22.99–110), to make terms (22.111–121), and to stand and fight (22.129–130). Achilles speaks the same verse once more over Hector's body, when he turns from the thought of attacking the city to Patroclus (22.385).
