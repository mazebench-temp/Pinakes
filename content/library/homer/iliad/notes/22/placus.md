---
summary: The mountain is named for the last time in Homer when Andromache, lamenting Hector, remembers that she was born “in Thebe under wooded Placus” (22.479).
---

ὑπὸ Πλάκῳ ὑληέσσῃ, “under wooded Placus” (22.479; see [[wooded]]), is the half-verse that twice closed a line on Andromache's family in Book 6, of her father, “Eetion who lived beneath wooded Placus”, and of her mother, “who was queen beneath wooded Placus” (6.396, 6.425; see [[eetion]]). Each time the mountain belongs to a parent whom Achilles' sack of Thebe took from her (6.414–428; see [[sack-of-thebe]]). Here it belongs to her own birth, set against Hector's “in Troy in the house of Priam” (22.477–481; see [[andromache]] and [[thebe]]).
