summary: Hector measures his death in the words of fame: it would be better “to perish gloriously at his hands before the city” (ἐϋκλειῶς, 22.110), and at the end, “Let me not die without a struggle and without glory” (ἀκλειῶς, 22.304). The book's last κλέος is Andromache's: she will burn the clothes laid up for him, “to be your fame before the Trojans and the Trojan women” (22.513–514).
grc: [ἀκλειῶς, ἐϋκλειῶς]
en: [gloriously]
---

ἀκλειῶς is found in the Iliad only here, and in the Odyssey of Odysseus snatched away by the Harpies without a tomb (*Odyssey* 1.241, 14.371; see [[fame-and-the-tomb]]); ἐϋκλειῶς is found only here in Homer. Hector's fame is to be for “men to come to hear of” (22.305; see [[song-and-fame]]), and Andromache's for the Trojans who are left (see [[honoring-the-dead]] and [[andromache]]). Achilles' word for what he has won is the other one, κῦδος (22.393; see [[kydos]]).
