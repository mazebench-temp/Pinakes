summary: The gods decide Hector's end and act on it. Apollo, who has drawn Achilles away, reveals himself (22.7–20) and rouses Hector's knees for the last time (22.203–204); Zeus asks the gods whether to save Hector, yields to Athena and weighs the fates, and Apollo leaves (22.166–213); Athena stops Achilles, deceives Hector in the likeness of Deiphobus and gives Achilles back his spear (22.214–247, 22.276–277). The men name the gods' part at every turn, and the narrator says that Athena brought Hector down and that Zeus gave his body to his enemies (22.403–404, 22.446).

**The ways of the gods.** The book shows most of the ways in which the gods take part. Apollo comes in a mortal's likeness and then speaks as a god (22.7–13; see [[divine-epiphany]] and [[apollo]]); he puts strength into a man, “who roused his might and his nimble knees” (22.204; see [[menos]]). Zeus takes counsel with the gods and weighs the fates in his scales (22.174–185, 22.209–213; see [[zeus]] and [[the-scales-of-zeus]]). Athena comes to Achilles as herself and to Hector in the likeness of his brother, and her hand is in the fight itself, when she snatches up the spear that has missed and gives it back to Achilles “unseen by Hector” (22.214–247, 22.276–277; see [[athena]] and [[truth-and-deception]]). No god rescues Hector, though Zeus asks whether they should (22.174–176; see [[divine-rescue]]).

**The men's part.** Each act of a god meets a man's act. Fate holds Hector outside the gates, “And deadly fate fettered Hector to stay where he was” (22.5; see [[moira]]), and he argues himself into staying (22.99–130; see [[pondering-two-ways]]); Achilles, told to stand and catch his breath, “obeyed, and rejoiced in his heart” (22.224); the deceived Hector chooses how to die, “Let me not die without a struggle and without glory” (22.304; see [[hector]]). The men speak of the gods' part as they see it. Hector bids Achilles drive his spear through his chest, “if a god has granted it to you” (22.284–285), and knows at the end that “the gods have called me to my death” (22.297; see [[olympians]]); Achilles tells the Achaeans that “the gods have granted us to bring down this man” (22.379). The narrator gives the full account: “gray-eyed Athena had brought him down at the hands of Achilles” (22.446), and “then Zeus gave him to his enemies / to defile in his own native land” (22.403–404). Achilles' spear and Athena's hand kill Hector together, as Euphorbus, Hector and Apollo killed Patroclus (16.849–850; see [[the-death-of-hector]] and [[the-death-of-patroclus]]).
