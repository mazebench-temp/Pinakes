summary: The women of Priam's house by marriage appear at both ends of the book. Priam foresees “my sons' wives dragged off by the deadly hands of the Achaeans” (22.65). Andromache, hearing “the voice of my revered mother-in-law” (22.451), runs to the wall; when she faints, “her husband's sisters and his brothers' wives in plenty” stand around her and hold her up (22.473–474).
grc: [νυοὺς, ἑκυρῆς, εἰνατέρες]
en: ["sons' wives", mother-in-law, "brothers' wives"]
---

The women who hold Andromache up are the ones Hector asked after when he looked for her in Book 6, “Has she gone to my sisters, or to my brothers' well-robed wives” (γαλόων ἢ εἰνατέρων, 6.378; see [[andromache]] and [[hector-and-andromache]]). Andromache's αἰδοίης ἑκυρῆς, “my revered mother-in-law”, is Hecuba (see [[hecuba]] and [[aidos]]); Helen will name the same relatives in her lament in the last book (24.769–770).
