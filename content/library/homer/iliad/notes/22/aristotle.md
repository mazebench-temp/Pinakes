---
summary: Aristotle's “pursuit of Hector” is the chase of this book, an irrational thing that epic can carry because the hearer does not see it, and an impossibility that is right because it astonishes; his Achilles “waving them back” is Homer's Achilles shaking his head at his men. In the Ethics Hector's fear of Polydamas' reproach is his example of the courage of citizens.
---

- **The pursuit of Hector.** Epic has more room than tragedy for the irrational, from which the marvelous chiefly comes, because we do not see the person acting: “The scenes of Hector's pursuit would seem ridiculous on the stage, with the men standing still and not pursuing, and Achilles waving them back; but in epic it goes unnoticed” (*Poetics* 24, 1460a11–17). The men who stand still are the Achaeans, and the gesture is Homer's: “And brilliant Achilles shook his head at his men, / and would not let them hurl their bitter missiles at Hector, / lest someone win the glory by hitting him, and he himself come second” (22.205–207). Aristotle's ἀνανεύων, “waving them back”, is the verb of 22.205, ἀνένευε. The chase that he would not put on the stage runs three times around the city, past the lookout, the fig tree and the springs, and in it neither man can catch or escape the other, “as in a dream” (22.145–201): [[the-pursuit-of-hector]], [[alogon]] and [[thaumaston]].
- **The impossible made right.** In the next chapter the same scene is his example of an impossibility that is correct if it achieves the end of the art, making this or another part more astonishing: “An example is the pursuit of Hector” (*Poetics* 25, 1460b23–26): [[adynaton]] and [[ekplexis]]. Right after the pursuit in chapter 24 he says that the marvelous is pleasant, and that “It is Homer above all who has taught the others how to tell lies as they should be told” (*Poetics* 24, 1460a17–19): [[homer-taught-lies]].
- The courage of citizens, illustrated by Hector's “Polydamas will be the first to heap disgrace on me” (*Nicomachean Ethics* 3.8, 1116a17–29, quoting 22.100): [[aristotle-civic-courage]].
