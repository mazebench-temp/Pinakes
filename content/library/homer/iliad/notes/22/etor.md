---
summary: Zeus watches the chase and says, “my heart grieves / for Hector” (22.169–170); Andromache, hearing her mother-in-law's wail, says, “in my own / breast my heart leaps up to my mouth” (22.451–452), and runs through the hall “with her heart leaping” (22.461).
---

The god's grief and the wife's fear are told in the same organ (see [[watching-the-war]], [[pity]] and [[kradie]]). Andromache's “my heart leaps” (πάλλεται ἦτορ) and “with her heart leaping” (παλλομένη κραδίην) use the verb with which Achilles brandished the spear that killed Hector (πάλλεν, 22.320; see [[andromache]]).
