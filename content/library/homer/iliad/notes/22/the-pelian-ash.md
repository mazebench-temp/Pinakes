---
summary: Achilles comes at Hector “brandishing over his right shoulder the Pelian ash, / the terrible spear” (22.133–134), and leans on it while Athena goes to deceive Hector (22.225). His cast misses, and Athena gives the spear back to him unseen (22.273–277); its point shines like the evening star, and it goes through Hector's throat, but the ash “heavy with bronze” does not cut the windpipe (22.317–329). Then Achilles draws it out of the body and lays it aside (22.367–368).

**Brandished.**

{{quote:b8cc1d24-1b92-5d8e-981b-2627d4da6eec}}

Πηλιάδα μελίην, the spear's full name, is said here for the fourth and last time in the poem (16.143, 19.390, 21.162, 22.133; Πηλιὰς … μελίη, 20.277; see [[achilles]] and [[enyalius]]). δεινήν, “the terrible spear”, is set at the head of the next verse; when Patroclus left it behind, it was “heavy, huge, and massive” (16.141).

**Leaning on it.** When Athena tells him to stand still and catch his breath, “he stood, leaning on his ash spear with its point of bronze” (22.225; see [[athena]]). χαλκογλώχιν, “with its point of bronze”, is found only here in Homer. ἐρεισθείς, “leaning”, is the verb of Hector's thought before the gates, to “lean my spear against the wall” and go to meet Achilles unarmed (22.112; see [[spear]]); he leaned his shining shield against a tower as he waited (22.97).

**The cast.**

{{quote:d04fbc4e-7603-5247-900c-7436e280caa3}}

The verse of the throw is the formula of a hero's cast, and Hector throws in the same words (22.273 = 22.289; 3.355, 7.244, 11.349; see [[long-shadowed]]). Hector, “looking straight at it”, sinks down and it flies over him, as the spear of Deiphobus flew over Idomeneus, who crouched beneath his shield (22.274–275; 13.404–408). In the duel with Aeneas, Poseidon drew this spear out of Aeneas' shield and set it down before Achilles' feet (20.322–324); here Athena puts it back into his hand, unseen by Hector, who taunts him for missing (22.279; see [[divine-intervention]]). Hector, whose own spear glances from the shield, has “no other ash spear”, and calls to Deiphobus for a long one, but no one is there (22.291–299; see [[ash-spear]] and [[deiphobus]]).

**Through the neck.** The point shines like the evening star as Achilles brandishes it in his right hand (see [[the-evening-star]]), “eyeing his fair skin, where it would best give way” (22.317–321). He drives it in at the throat, where the armor Hector took from Patroclus leaves him bare:

{{quote:bb3354aa-fd71-5a0b-89bd-bf0e79f28404}}

μελίη … χαλκοβάρεια, “the ash spear heavy with bronze”, is said of no other spear in the Iliad; the adjective is used of a helmet (11.96), and the *Odyssey* has the same verse-end of the suitors' spears (*Odyssey* 22.259, 22.276; see [[heavy-with-bronze]]). The spear that no other Achaean could wield, which Patroclus therefore left behind (16.140–144), kills the man who killed him (see [[patroclus]] and [[the-armor-of-peleus]]).

**Drawn out.** “He spoke, and drew the bronze spear out of the body, / and laid it aside, and began to strip the armor from his shoulders” (22.367–368; see [[stripping-the-dead]]).
