---
summary: Hector's line-end formula, Ἕκτορος ἱπποδάμοιο, comes twice in the pursuit: the two run “for the life of Hector, tamer of horses” (22.161), and Zeus sets in the scales two fates, “one of Achilles, and one of Hector, tamer of horses” (22.211).
---

In the first the race around the walls is compared with a race for a prize: “for it was no beast for sacrifice or ox-hide / they strove to win, such as are prizes for the feet of men, / but they ran for the life of Hector, tamer of horses” (22.159–161). In the second the prize is weighed (22.209–213; [[the-scales-of-zeus]]). The genitive formula returns as the last words of the poem, “So they held the funeral of Hector, tamer of horses” (24.804).
