---
summary: Hector charges with his sword, “and crouching, he swooped like an eagle flying high / that goes down to the plain through the murky clouds” (22.308–309).
grc: [ὑψιπετήεις]
---

The longer form ὑψιπετήεις is new to the entry; the verse returns in the Odyssey when Odysseus charges the suitors' kinsmen (22.308 = *Odyssey* 24.538). The eagle's prey is “a tender lamb or a cowering hare” (22.310; [[tender]], [[murky]]). See [[eagle]] and [[hector]].
