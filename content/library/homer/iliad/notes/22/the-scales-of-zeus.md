# Unnamed: the fates set in the pans, the lifting of the beam, and the sinking of Hector's day (22.210-213).

{{quote:b3ca717f-55ad-5165-89a1-d8c459771e51}}

The verses are those of the weighing in Book 8 (22.209–210 = 8.69–70; 22.212 ≈ 8.72), with two changes. The fates are no longer of two armies but of two men, “one of Achilles, and one of Hector, tamer of horses” (22.211; see [[tamer-of-horses]]), and the day that sinks is Hector's, ῥέπε δʼ Ἕκτορος αἴσιμον ἦμαρ (22.212), where in Book 8 it was the Achaeans', ῥέπε δʼ αἴσιμον ἦμαρ Ἀχαιῶν (8.72; see [[aisa]] and [[ker-doom]]). There the Achaeans' fates settled on the bountiful earth and the Trojans' were lifted toward the sky (8.73–74; see the translation's note on 8.69–74); here Hector's day goes off to the house of Hades (22.213; see [[hades]]). The weighing of Book 8 began Hector's day of victory; this one ends his life.

It comes at a fourth return. Three times the two men have run around the city (22.165), and “when for the fourth time they came to the springs” (22.208), Zeus holds out the scales (see [[three-times-and-a-fourth]]). As in Book 8, the weighing follows the decision: Zeus has asked the gods whether to save Hector and has let Athena go (22.174–187; see [[zeus]] and [[not-all-the-gods-approve]]). The gods act on it at once. Apollo, who had roused Hector's knees for the very last time (22.203–204), leaves him, and Athena comes to Achilles (22.213–214; see [[apollo]] and [[athena]]). Achilles' fate lies in the other pan and does not sink; Hector, dying, will name its day (22.359–360; see [[the-short-life-of-achilles]] and [[death-of-achilles-foretold]]).
