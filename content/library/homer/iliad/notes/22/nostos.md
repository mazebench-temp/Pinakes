summary: Hector's homecoming is from the plain into his own city, and he does not make it. He weighs whether “to face Achilles, kill him, and come home” (22.109); dying, he asks for his body “to be taken home” for the fire (22.342); and Andromache has water heated “for Hector when he came home from the battle” (22.444), not knowing that he is dead.
grc: [νοστήσαντι]
en: [came home]
# νεώμεθα (22.392) is Achilles' "let us go back to the hollow ships", a return to the camp, not a homecoming.
except: [22.392]
---

Zeus had foretold it in Book 17, when Hector put on Achilles' armor: “you will not come home from the battle / for Andromache to receive from you the glorious armor” (17.207–208), and μάχης ἐκ νοστήσαντι, “when he came home from the battle”, is the phrase of that prophecy (22.444; see [[bathing]], [[nepios]] and [[the-making-of-the-arms]]). Achilles, who has given up his own homecoming (18.101; see [[the-short-life-of-achilles]]), refuses Hector the return of his body (22.345–354; see [[honoring-the-dead]]).
