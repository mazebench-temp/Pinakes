---
summary: Both of Hector's parents call Achilles “the hard man!” as they beg their son not to face him (22.41, 22.86).
---

Priam ends his warning with the word, “since he is far the stronger, / the hard man!” (22.40–41), and Hecuba hers, “do not stand forth as champion against this man, / the hard man!” (22.85–86). In both the word stands first in the verse, closed off by a stop (see the translation's table of key words, under σχέτλιος). Walter Leaf notes that the word often has a tone of friendly but shocked remonstrance (citing 22.86, 10.164 and 18.13), and that at 22.41 it would be more natural to apply it to Hector in that tone, were not the verbs before and after both of Achilles (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 22.41).
