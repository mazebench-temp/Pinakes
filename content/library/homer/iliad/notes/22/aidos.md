summary: Respect is a mother's plea, a son's fear and a father's hope. Hecuba, her breast in her hand: “Hector, my child, respect this, and pity me” (22.82); Hector fears that Achilles “will not pity me, / nor respect me at all” if he comes to him unarmed (22.123–124); Priam hopes that Achilles “may respect my years and pity / my old age” (22.419–420). Shame keeps Hector outside the wall: “I would feel shame before the Trojans and the Trojan women with trailing robes” (22.105).
grc: [αἰδέσσεται]
---

The pair of verbs, αἰδέομαι and ἐλεέω, is the suppliant's, said by Lycaon to Achilles in Book 21 and by Priam to Achilles in Book 24 (21.74, 24.503; see [[pity]] and [[supplication]]); here a mother says it to her son. Hector's verse of shame is the one with which he answered Andromache in Book 6, when she asked him to stay on the wall (6.442 = 22.105; see [[hector-and-andromache]] and [[retreat]]).

αἰδώς also names what shame covers. Priam's old man killed at his doors has his “nakedness” disfigured by the dogs (αἰδῶ, 22.75), the sense of Odysseus' threat to strip Thersites of the clothes “which cover your nakedness” (2.262; see [[aischros]]). The adjective αἰδοῖος is Andromache's for Hecuba, “my revered mother-in-law” (22.451; see [[in-laws]]).
