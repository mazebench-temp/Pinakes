summary: Achilles speeds toward the city, “so Achilles plied his nimble feet and knees” (22.24); Hector flees before him, “and plied his nimble knees” (22.144); and Apollo comes to Hector for the last time and “roused his might and his nimble knees” (22.204).
---

The verse of Achilles' run is the verse of Hector's return to the battle in Book 15 (22.24 = 15.269), and in both it closes a simile of a horse running over the plain, there a stallion broken loose from the stall, here a prize-winning horse with its chariot (15.263–268, 22.22–23; [[like-a-racehorse]]). Walter Leaf takes λαιψηρά there either as an adverb or as an epithet drawn from the familiar λαιψηρὰ … γοῦνα (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 22.24; see the translation's note on 22.24). The half-verse of Hector's flight is the one of Dolon's (22.144, as 10.358), and Apollo's help is told in the words with which Aeneas told of Zeus' help to him (22.204, as 20.93).
