summary: Achilles' bronze shines “like the light / of a blazing fire or of the rising sun” (22.134–135); one spring of the Scamander steams “as if from a blazing fire” (22.150); the Achaeans jeer that Hector is softer to handle “than when he burned the ships with blazing fire” (22.374); and Andromache will burn his clothes “with blazing fire” (22.512).
grc: ["πυρὸς αἰθομένου"]

**Two formulas.** πυρὸς αἰθομένου (22.135) is new to the entry, beside πυρὸς αἰθομένοιο, “as if from a blazing fire,” of the warm spring (22.150). πυρὶ κηλέῳ names, as in Book 8, the fire that Hector carried to the ships (22.374, as 8.217, 8.235), and Andromache's last words take it up for the fire she will make of his clothes, “of no use to you, since you will not lie in them, / but to be your fame before the Trojans and the Trojan women” (22.512–514; see the translation's note on 22.512–514).
