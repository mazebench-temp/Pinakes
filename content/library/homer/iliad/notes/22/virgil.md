---
summary: Virgil builds the end of the Aeneid on this book, from Jupiter's scales and the chase to the plea for the body and the soul's flight, and his Aeneas sees Hector twice after his death, dragged around the walls in the pictures at Carthage and in a dream on the night Troy falls. His Andromache faints at a sight from Troy and honors Hector at an empty tomb, and his Aeneas blazes like Sirius, bringing thirst and disease.
---

- The death of Turnus: the scales, the chase in circles with the hound and the stag, the sword given back by a goddess, the dream, the plea for the body, the belt of Pallas and the soul's flight (*Aeneid* 12.725–952, on 22.131–366): [[virgil-the-death-of-turnus]], [[the-weighing-of-souls]], [[as-in-a-dream]] and [[the-death-of-hector]].
- Hector dragged three times around the walls of Ilium and sold for gold, and Hector's ghost, black with bloody dust and pierced through the feet with thongs, “how changed from that Hector” who came back in the spoils of Achilles and threw fire on the Danaan ships (*Aeneid* 1.483–487, 2.270–297, on 22.322–323, 22.371–374, 22.395–404): [[virgils-hector]] and [[the-dragging-of-hector]].
- Andromache in Epirus, who faints at the sight of Aeneas in Trojan arms, asks whether he is a true messenger, and pours offerings at an empty tomb of Hector (*Aeneid* 3.301–312, on 22.437–439, 22.466–467, 22.508–514): [[virgils-andromache]].
- **Sirius.** As Aeneas comes back to the battle by sea, his helmet blazes, flame pours from the crest and the golden boss of his shield spouts fire, as when comets glow blood-red on a clear night, or as the blaze of Sirius, “bringing thirst and diseases to sick mortals” (*sitim morbosque ferens mortalibus aegris*), rises and saddens the sky with its baleful light (*Aeneid* 10.270–275). Priam sees Achilles coming over the plain like the star that men call the dog of Orion, “and brings much fever to wretched mortals” (22.26–31); Walter Leaf compares Virgil's line (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 22.31): [[the-autumn-star]].
