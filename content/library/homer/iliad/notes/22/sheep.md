---
summary: The sheep of Book 22 are in Andromache's memory of her son's childhood: Astyanax, on his father's knees, “ate only marrow and the rich fat of sheep” (22.500–501).
---

Ἀστυάναξ, ὃς πρὶν μὲν ἑοῦ ἐπὶ γούνασι πατρὸς / μυελὸν οἶον ἔδεσκε καὶ οἰῶν πίονα δημόν, “Astyanax, who once on his father's knees / ate only marrow and the rich fat of sheep” (22.500–501; see [[astyanax]]). δημός is the fat of an animal, another word from δῆμος, “community” (see [[demos]]); the fishes and the eels tear the fat of the dead in Book 21 (21.127, 21.204). Marrow and fat are the richest food, and the boy ate them on his father's knees. Andromache sets the memory against the life of the orphan, who goes to his father's companions in his need and is given a cup for a moment, and is driven from the feast by a boy whose parents are both alive (22.490–499; see [[the-day-of-orphanhood]]).
