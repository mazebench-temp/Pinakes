# The two verses before the saying (22.179-180 = 16.441-442).

{{quote:8c9f1387-91bd-56c7-b8fa-15df30e66950}}

Only the address is new. For Hera's “Most dread son of Cronus” (16.440), Athena says “Father, lord of the gleaming thunderbolt and of the dark clouds” (22.178; see [[of-the-gleaming-thunderbolt]] and [[lord-of-dark-clouds]]). The rest is Hera's: a man “long since ordained to his portion”, whom Zeus would set free again from death (22.179–180 = 16.441–442; see [[aisa]]). Hera went on to give her reasons, the sons of other gods who fight at Troy and the burial that Sarpedon could still be given (16.444–457); Athena stops at the warning. Zeus answers her with the words he used to her in Book 8, “Take heart, Tritogeneia, dear child” (22.183–184 = 8.39–40; see [[tritogeneia]]), and tells her to do as her mind is set (22.185), as he told Hera in Book 4, “Do as you wish” (4.37). In all three scenes Zeus has the power to act and does not use it. See [[zeus]], [[athena]] and [[olympians]].
