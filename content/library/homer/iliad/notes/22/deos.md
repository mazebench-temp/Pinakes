summary: Fear moves the book's turns. Achilles reproaches Apollo, who has “no fear at all of vengeance hereafter” (22.19); Hector, seeing Achilles come, is seized by trembling and flees (22.136–137), and later says that Achilles lied “so that in fear of you I might forget my might and my courage” (22.282). Andromache, hearing the wailing, says, “I am terribly afraid / that brilliant Achilles has cut off my bold Hector” (22.454–455).
grc: [ἔδεισας, ὑποδείσας]
---

The god's freedom from fear is the mark of his difference from the mortal who threatens him (22.18–20; see [[gods-and-mortals]]). Hector's fear has a different word, τρόμος, “trembling”, and his flight the verb of rout, φοβηθείς (22.136–137; see [[phobos]] and [[retreat]]); the false Deiphobus says that all the Trojans tremble before Achilles (ὑποτρομέουσιν, 22.241).
