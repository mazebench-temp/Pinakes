---
summary: Priam foresees his own death: “And myself, last of all, at the outer doors the dogs / that eat raw flesh will tear at” (22.66–67), the dogs he reared at his own table.
---

ὠμησταί with ἐρύουσι(ν) at the head of the verse is the threat Odysseus spoke over Socus, “but the birds / that eat raw flesh will tear at you” (11.453–454). Priam turns the warrior's threat on himself, and on his own dogs, “the dogs I reared in my halls to share my table and guard my doors” (22.69; [[dogs]], [[old-age-and-memory]]). Achilles' wish later in the book is to eat Hector's flesh raw himself (22.346–347; see the translation's note on 22.346–347). See [[priam]].
