---
summary: Priam, begging Hector from the wall, names Polydorus with his brother Lycaon as the two sons he cannot see among the Trojans in the city, and hopes to ransom them if they are alive (22.46–53). Achilles killed Polydorus the same day.
---

{{quote:8bf09c64-4778-5239-99c0-5350f6862e5e}}

**The youngest.** Polydorus was the son his father “would not let … fight at all, / because he was the youngest born among his children, / and the dearest to him” (20.408–410), and he died earlier that day, showing off his speed among the champions (20.411–418; see [[the-fourth-day-of-battle]]). Priam, who kept him from the fighting, does not know that he went into it. The Trojans had fled into the city without waiting to learn who had escaped and who had died (21.606–611), and Priam can only say that he cannot see him (22.47; see [[priam]]).

**Two mothers.** Lycaon and Polydorus are the sons of Laothoe, and Priam speaks of their grief as hers and his, “it is sorrow to my heart and to their mother, we who brought them into the world” (22.53; see [[laothoe]], [[lycaon-son-of-priam]] and [[bereaved-parents]]). Hector, whom he begs in the same speech, is Hecuba's son (22.234; see [[hecuba]] and [[sons-of-priam]]). It was the sight of Polydorus dying that drew Hector against Achilles in Book 20 (20.419–423; see [[hector]]); now Priam sets the two dead brothers before Hector to keep him from the same fight.
