---
summary: Apollo taunts Achilles, “you who are mortal, me, an immortal god?” (22.9); and Athena protests to Zeus about Hector, “A man who is mortal, long since ordained to his portion” (22.179).
grc: ["θνητὸν ἐόντα"]
---

The god and the goddess both remind the other side of what a man is. Athena's verse is Hera's about Sarpedon, and the phrase θνητὸν ἐόντα also takes in that earlier line (22.179 = 16.441; [[not-all-the-gods-approve]], [[of-the-gleaming-thunderbolt]]). See [[immortals]] and [[gods-and-mortals]].
