summary: The Achaeans come up under the wall and watch the pursuit, held back by Achilles from throwing at Hector (22.3–4, 22.205–207); Athena promises Achilles “great glory for the Achaeans” (22.217); and after the killing they run up and stab the body, and Achilles leads them back to the ships singing the paean (22.369–394).
# Ἀχαιοῖσι without the final ν (22.217) is found only here in Homer.
grc: [Ἀχαιοῖσι]
---

**Under the wall.** The book opens with the Achaeans coming “nearer the wall, leaning their shields against their shoulders” (22.3–4), a formation whose use in an advance is unclear (see the translation's note on 22.4). During the pursuit Achilles shakes his head at his men and will not let them throw at Hector, “lest someone win the glory by hitting him, and he himself come second” (22.205–207; [[kydos]]).

**Glory for the Achaeans.** At the springs Athena tells Achilles that the two of them “will carry off great glory for the Achaeans to the ships” (22.216–217; [[dear-to-zeus]]). The dative Ἀχαιοῖσι, without the final ν, is found only here in Homer and is new to the entry.

**Over the body.** “and the other sons of the Achaeans ran up around him, / and gazed at the stature and admirable looks / of Hector; and not one stood beside him without stabbing him” (22.369–371; [[sons-of-the-achaeans]], [[tis-speech]]). Achilles stands among them and speaks to the “leaders and rulers of the Argives” (22.377–378), and calls the “young men of the Achaeans” to go back to the ships with the body, singing the paean (22.391–394; [[paean]], [[the-dragging-of-hector]]). See [[achilles]].
