summary: Hector proposes that the winner strip the other's armor and give back the body (22.254–259), and Achilles refuses. Hector meets Achilles in the armor he stripped from Patroclus (22.323), and Achilles, over the dying Hector, recalls him “stripping Patroclus” (22.331). When Hector is dead Achilles strips the bloody armor from his shoulders and lifts it into his chariot (22.368–369, 22.376, 22.399).
# Three forms found only here in Homer: συλήσω (22.258), ἐνάριξε (22.323), ἐξεναρίζων (22.331).
grc: [συλήσω, ἐνάριξε, ἐξεναρίζων]
# Unnamed: the terms (22.254-257, 22.259), the armor bloody (22.369) and lifted into the chariot (22.399).
refs: [22.254-257, 22.259, 22.369, 22.399]
---

**The terms.** Athena, in the likeness of Deiphobus, urges Hector to fight, “so that we may see whether Achilles / will kill us both and carry off our bloody spoils / to the hollow ships” (22.244–246; see [[bloody-spoils]]). Hector offers Achilles the terms of his challenge in Book 7 (7.76–86): “but when I have stripped you of your glorious armor, Achilles, / I will give your body back to the Achaeans; and you do the same” (22.258–259). The terms keep the armor for the victor and give the body back for burial; Achilles refuses them, as he refuses any agreement (22.261–267; see [[no-oaths-between-lions-and-men]] and [[the-death-of-hector]]).

**Stripped and stripping.** The armor Hector wears was stripped from Patroclus: ἐνάριξε, “he had stripped” (22.323), as Hector himself said, ἐνάριξα, “I stripped” (17.187; see [[the-armor-of-peleus]]). Achilles reminds the dying man of it, “Hector, you thought, I suppose, when you were stripping Patroclus, / that you would be safe” (ἐξεναρίζων, 22.331–332; see [[patroclus]]). The translation renders ἐξεναρίζω “strip” where the stripping is the point, as here and at 22.376, and “slay” where it is only the killing (see its row for ἐξεναρίζω).

**Achilles strips Hector.**

{{quote:5cb3ee3a-7cbe-51db-8cfd-100eef3a43e1}}

ἀπʼ ὤμων τεύχεʼ ἐσύλα, “began to strip the armor from his shoulders” (22.368), ends the verse as at 6.28 and 15.524. The other Achaeans do not touch the armor; they gaze at the body and stab it (22.370–375; see [[the-dragging-of-hector]]). “And when brilliant swift-footed Achilles had stripped him” (22.376), he speaks to the army, and when he mounts his chariot he lifts “the glorious armor” in with him (22.399). He had vowed to bring Hector's armor and head back to Patroclus (18.334–335).
