summary: Book 22 opens on the Trojans who have fled into the city “in panic like fawns” and now let the sweat dry from them and drink, leaning on the battlements (22.1–3). Hector alone is left outside, and before long he is the fawn.

---

ὣς οἳ μὲν κατὰ ἄστυ πεφυζότες ἠΰτε νεβροί, “So they throughout the city, in panic like fawns” (22.1). πεφυζότες, “in panic”, is the word of the rout at the end of Book 21, where the Trojans poured over the plain and came crowding to the city before Achilles (21.6, 21.528, 21.532; see the translation's note on 22.1). It belongs with φύζα, panic flight, and with the deer “quick to flee” (φυζακινῇς ἐλάφοισιν) to which Poseidon likened the Trojans of former days (13.102; see [[timid-deer]]). Agamemnon's fawns stood still because they had run themselves out on the plain, and had no courage left (4.243–245). These have run to safety, and the next verses show them as such animals after the chase: they “let the sweat dry from them, and drank, and quenched their thirst, / leaning on the fair battlements” (22.2–3; see [[battlements]] and [[the-walls-of-troy]]).

The only Trojan left outside is Hector, held there by deadly fate before the Scaean gates (22.5–6). In the chase that follows he becomes the fawn: a dog rouses it from its lair in the mountains and tracks it through the glens, and however it cowers under a bush it does not escape (22.189–193; see [[the-dog-and-the-fawn]] and [[deer]]).
