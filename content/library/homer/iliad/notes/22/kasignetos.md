summary: Athena takes the shape of Hector's brother Deiphobus, and Hector greets him as “by far the dearest to me / of my brothers, the sons whom Hecuba and Priam bore” (22.233–234). Priam fears for two other brothers, Lycaon and Polydorus, whom he cannot see in the city (22.46–48).
grc: [γνωτῶν]
---

γνωτοί, “kinsmen, brothers”, is the word of Hector's greeting; the goddess answers with the address of brothers, ἠθεῖε, “Dear brother” (22.229, 22.239; see [[dear-brother]] and [[deiphobus]]). A brother's help is what Hector counts on, and when he calls for a second spear “he was nowhere near him” (22.294–299; see [[truth-and-deception]] and [[sons-of-priam]]).
