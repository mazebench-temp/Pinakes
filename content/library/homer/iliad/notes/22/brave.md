**The brave and the better.** “There they ran past, one fleeing, the other pursuing behind; / in front a brave man fled, but a far better one pursued him” (22.157–158): the narrator grants Hector the word and Achilles the comparative of ἀγαθός, μέγʼ ἀμείνων.

**Brave as he is.** The participial phrase ἐσθλὸν ἐόντα follows each of the two men to his death. Zeus uses it of Hector, asking the gods whether they shall save him “or now at last / bring him down by Achilles, son of Peleus, brave as he is” (22.175–176), and Hector uses it of Achilles in his last words, “on that day when Paris and Phoebus Apollo / destroy you, brave as you are, at the Scaean gates” (22.359–360; [[death-of-achilles-foretold]]). The elided form ἐσθλὸν ἐόντʼ is new to the entry, and so is ἐσθλὸς ἔφευγε. See [[hector]] and [[achilles]].
