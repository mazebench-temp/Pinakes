---
summary: Priam wishes Hector had died in his arms, so that “his mother, most ill-fated, who bore him, and I myself” might have had their fill of weeping (22.427–428); and Andromache speaks of the child “whom you and I begot, both most ill-fated” (22.485).
grc: [δυσάμμορος, δυσάμμοροι]
---

The nominatives are new to the entry. The two parents and the two spouses lament in the same word, which the translation keeps apart from δύσμορος, “ill-fated,” and δύστηνος, “unhappy” (22.60, 22.477, 22.481; see the translation's table of key words, under δύστηνος). Andromache's verse returns in her lament over the body in the last book (22.485 = 24.727). See [[lament]].
