---
summary: Hector meets Achilles in Achilles' own armor, “the fine armor he had stripped from mighty Patroclus when he killed him” (22.322–323), and it covers all of him but the throat. When Hector is dead Achilles strips it from his shoulders, “bloody”, and lifts “the glorious armor” into his chariot (22.368–369, 22.399).
refs: [22.258, 22.322-325, 22.367-369, 22.376, 22.399]
---

The armor Hector put on in Book 17 was the armor the gods gave Peleus (17.192–197; see [[the-armor-of-peleus]] and [[the-making-of-the-arms]]), and Zeus said then that Hector would not come home to give it to Andromache (17.207–208; see [[nostos]]). Before the duel Hector promised to strip Achilles of “your glorious armor” and give back his body (22.258–259); it is Achilles who strips the armor, his own, and keeps the body (see [[stripping-the-dead]] and [[honoring-the-dead]]).
