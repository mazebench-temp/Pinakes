summary: The book's speeches close on deeds: Achilles strides toward the city (22.21), Zeus urges Athena on (22.186), the two warriors throw their spears and Hector draws his sword (22.273, 22.289, 22.306), Hector dies as he speaks (22.361), Achilles draws out his spear and devises the defilement (22.367, 22.395); and the laments of Priam, Hecuba and Andromache each close in weeping (22.429, 22.437, 22.515).
# ἦ ῥʼ ὃ γέρων (22.77, only here); ὣς τώ γε κλαίοντε (22.90; also 11.136, the sons of Antimachus).
grc: ["ἦ ῥʼ ὃ γέρων", "ὣς τώ γε κλαίοντε"]
en: [The old man spoke]
---

**The parents.** Priam's plea ends “The old man spoke” (ἦ ῥʼ ὃ γέρων, 22.77, found only here), and the two speeches of the parents end together, “So the two, weeping, addressed their dear son / begging him earnestly” (22.90–91), in the verse that closed the plea of Antimachus' sons to Agamemnon (11.136); both pleas fail (11.137, 22.91–92; [[huge]]). The new forms take in that earlier line.

**Words and deeds.** “So saying he urged on Athena, who was eager already” (22.186) is the verse with which Zeus sent her to break the truce and to strengthen Achilles with nectar (4.73, 19.349). ἦ ῥα καί runs the speech into the act four times: the two spear casts (22.273, 22.289; [[long-shadowed]]), the drawing of the spear from the body (22.367), and the verse of the defilement, “He spoke, and devised shameful deeds against brilliant Hector” (22.395; [[brilliant]], [[the-dragging-of-hector]]). Hector's last speech has no close of its own: “As he spoke, the end of death covered him” (22.361; [[the-end-of-death]]).

**The laments.** Each of the three laments is closed by weeping and by the groans of those who hear it: “So he spoke, weeping, and the citizens groaned with him” (22.429), “So she spoke, weeping” (22.437), and the last verse of the book, “So she spoke, weeping, and the women groaned with her” (22.515), the verse that closed Briseis' lament for Patroclus and will close Andromache's last lament for Hector (19.301, 24.746; [[lament]]).
