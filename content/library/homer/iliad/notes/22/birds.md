---
summary: The birds of Book 22 are the falcon, “the nimblest of winged birds”, which swoops on a timid dove in the first simile of the chase (22.139–140), and the birds that, with the dogs, Achilles promises will tear Hector's body and divide it among them (22.335–336, 22.354).
---

**Birds of prey.** ἠΰτε κίρκος ὄρεσφιν ἐλαφρότατος πετεηνῶν, “As a falcon in the mountains, the nimblest of winged birds” (22.139; see [[the-falcon-and-the-dove]], [[falcon]] and [[doves]]). The falcon is Achilles. At the end of the chase the bird of prey is Hector, who swoops on Achilles like an eagle flying high (22.308; see [[the-eagle-flying-high]] and [[eagle]]).

**The birds of the dead.** σὲ μὲν κύνες ἠδʼ οἰωνοὶ / ἑλκήσουσʼ ἀϊκῶς, “and you the dogs and the birds / will tear shamefully” (22.335–336), and again, ἀλλὰ κύνες τε καὶ οἰωνοὶ κατὰ πάντα δάσονται, “but the dogs and the birds will divide you all among them” (22.354). The οἰωνοί are the great birds that eat the unburied, joined with the dogs from the first verses of the poem (1.4–5; see [[dogs]]); Priam names the vultures (22.42; see [[vultures]]).
