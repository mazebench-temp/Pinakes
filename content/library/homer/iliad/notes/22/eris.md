---
summary: Hector ends his monologue by choosing the fight: “Better to drive together in strife as quickly as may be” (22.129).
---

ἔριδι ξυνελαύνειν, “drive together in strife”, is said in two earlier places of the gods: Poseidon does not wish to drive them together, and Ares accuses Athena of doing it (20.134, 21.394; see [[the-battle-of-the-gods]]). Hector uses it of himself and Achilles, after he has weighed and given up the offer of Helen and the city's wealth (22.111–128; see [[pondering-two-ways]], [[neikos]] and [[words-and-violence]]).
