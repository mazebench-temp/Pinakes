---
summary: Hector blames his own recklessness: “since I have destroyed the army by my own recklessness”, he is ashamed to go back into the city (22.104–105). Priam, after Hector's death, gives the word to Achilles: “Let me beg this reckless man, this doer of violent deeds” (22.418).
---

The noun is Hector's judgment on himself, as the entry describes (see [[who-is-to-blame]] and [[laos]]). The adjective is Priam's on the man he will supplicate, as Menelaus in Book 13 said it of all the Trojans, “whose might is always reckless” (13.634; see [[supplication]]). Beside it Priam sets ὀβριμοεργός, “doer of violent deeds”, found in Homer only here and of Heracles, “Hard man, doer of violent deeds” (5.403). He had already called Achilles σχέτλιος, “the hard man!” (22.41), and Walter Leaf compares the same line on Heracles (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 22.41; see [[hard]]).
