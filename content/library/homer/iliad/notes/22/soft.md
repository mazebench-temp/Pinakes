summary: The Achaeans stab the dead Hector, saying he is “much softer to handle now” than when he burned the ships (22.373–374); and Andromache remembers Astyanax sleeping “in a soft bed, his heart filled with good things” (22.504).
grc: [μαλακώτερος]
en: [softer]
---

The comparative μαλακώτερος is new to the entry and found only here: the soldiers' jeer at the body of the man who burned their ships (22.371–375; [[tis-speech]], [[blazing-fire]]). The soft bed is the formula of comfort, εὐνῇ ἔνι μαλακῇ, in Andromache's picture of the child's past that the orphan will lose (22.500–505). See [[hector]] and [[astyanax]].
