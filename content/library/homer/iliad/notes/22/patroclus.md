summary: Patroclus lies dead by the ships, and the last fight of the book is fought for him. Hector meets Achilles in the armor he stripped from Patroclus (22.322–323); Achilles tells him that he will pay for “the sorrows of my companions” (22.271–272), and over the dying man says that Hector, stripping Patroclus, had no thought for the helper left at the ships, and that the dogs will tear Hector while the Achaeans bury Patroclus (22.331–336). Then he remembers him, “a corpse unwept, unburied”, whom he will not forget even in the house of Hades (22.386–390).

**Avenged.** Hector dies in the armor he took from Patroclus' body, “the fine armor he had stripped from mighty Patroclus when he killed him” (22.323; see [[the-armor-of-peleus]] and [[might-of-patroclus]]). Achilles has told him that now he will pay back all at once “the sorrows of my companions, whom you killed raging with your spear” (22.272; see [[hetairos]] and [[the-death-of-hector]]).

**The boast.**

{{quote:df795f8d-d63b-526d-825c-7227d2a82ba0}}

Hector, over the dying Patroclus, had said that “not even Achilles, brave as he is, has helped you” (16.837; see [[the-death-of-patroclus]]). Achilles answers him now: the helper was left behind at the ships, far from Patroclus, and he is “a much better man” (22.333–334; see [[achilles]]). The burial rites he promises Patroclus, κτεριοῦσιν (22.336), are those he swore to withhold until he brought back Hector's armor and head (18.334–335; see [[honoring-the-dead]]). Nestor uses the verb when he bids Achilles honor his companion with games (κτερέϊζε, 23.646).

**Not forgotten.** In his speech to the Achaeans Achilles breaks off his plan to make trial of the city:

{{quote:5210dc68-2e8b-54c9-b1ba-77448c3a54d4}}

ἄκλαυτος ἄθαπτος, “unwept, unburied”, are said of Elpenor in the *Odyssey*: his companions left his body in Circe's house unwept and unburied, and his ghost begs Odysseus not to leave him so (*Odyssey* 11.53–54, 11.72; see the translation's row for ἄκλαυτος / ἄθαπτος). Who forgets in the house of Hades is left unsaid, the dead themselves or men in general (see the translation's note on 22.389 and [[hades]]); Achilles will remember there even so. He takes Hector back to the ships, and early in the next book he lays the body face down in the dust beside Patroclus' bier (23.24–26; see [[the-dragging-of-hector]]).
