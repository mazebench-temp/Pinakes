summary: Andromache's handmaids are heating water for Hector's bath when she hears the wailing from the tower; she takes two of her slave women with her, and they go with her as she runs through the hall “like a maenad” (22.442–461). In her lament she remembers the nurse in whose arms Astyanax used to sleep (22.503).
# Unnamed: the "two of you" who follow her (22.450).
refs: [22.450]
---

**The household at work.** “And she called to her handmaids with lovely tresses through the house / to set a great tripod over the fire” (22.442–443; see [[with-lovely-tresses]] and [[bathing]]). Heating water for a bath is the handmaids' work in Homer, and the women who wash Hector's body in the last book are the slave women of Achilles' hut (24.582–588). While they work, Andromache weaves in the inner room (22.440–441; see [[weaving]]), as Hector told her in Book 6 to go home to her loom and bid her handmaids go about their work (6.490–492).

**Two follow her.** At the cry from the tower “she spoke again among her slave women with lovely tresses” (22.449), and told two of them to follow her, so that she might see what had been done (22.450). ἀμφίπολοι, “handmaids”, and δμῳαί, “slave women”, are the same women here (see the translation's table of key words). A lady goes out with two of them, as Helen did when she went to the wall (3.143), and as Penelope does in the Odyssey (*Odyssey* 1.331). “and her handmaids went with her” (ἅμα δʼ ἀμφίπολοι κίον αὐτῇ, 22.461) is the half-verse of Book 6 in which a handmaid came with her to the Scaean gates, carrying the child (ἅμα δʼ ἀμφίπολος κίεν αὐτῇ, 6.399; see [[andromache]] and [[hector-and-andromache]]). Then she found Hector alive; now she sees him dragged. In Book 6 her handmaids mourned him in his house while he was still alive (6.498–502; see [[lament]]).

**The nurse.** Astyanax, in his father's lifetime, “would sleep in a bed, in the arms of his nurse” (22.503), the τιθήνη who carried him to the gates and into whose bosom he shrank from his father's helmet (6.389, 6.467; see [[astyanax]]).
