**The jutting tower.** Hector, waiting for Achilles like a serpent at its hole, “did not give ground, / leaning his shining shield against a jutting tower” (22.96–97; see [[the-serpent-at-its-hole]] and [[hector]]). πύργῳ ἔπι προὔχοντι: προὔχω is “jut out”, said in the Odyssey of shores and headlands, and of the headland by the Hellespont on which the tomb of Achilles was heaped (ἀκτῇ ἔπι προὐχούσῃ, *Odyssey* 24.82). The poem does not say whether this is the great tower by the Scaean gates, where Andromache met Hector in Book 6 (6.386, 6.392–394), but Hector waits before the same gates (22.6, 22.35; see [[scaean-gates]]).

**From the tower.** Andromache is at her loom in the inner part of the house, and has ordered a bath heated for Hector's return, when “she heard the wailing and the groaning from the tower” (22.447). The voice is her mother-in-law's (22.451; see [[hecuba]]). Her limbs shake and the shuttle falls to the ground, and she rushes “through the hall like a maenad” (22.448–460; see [[andromache]]):

{{quote:4becb2ef-1edf-503c-8078-f9529775d606}}

The scene of Book 6 returns. Then too she went “to the great tower of Ilios”, because she heard that the Trojans were hard pressed, “hurrying to the wall / like a woman out of her mind” (6.386–389; see [[like-a-woman-out-of-her-mind]]); she came running to meet Hector at the Scaean gates and begged him to “stay here on the tower” (6.431), but he went back to the war (6.392–502; see [[hector-and-andromache]]). Now she comes to the tower and the wall when he is dead (see [[the-walls-of-troy]]). In her last lament she will foresee that some Achaean will hurl her son “from the tower” (24.735; see [[astyanax]]).
