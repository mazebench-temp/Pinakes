# δειλοῖσι βροτοῖσιν (22.31, 22.76); βροτοῖσι without ν at 24.525.
grc: ["δειλοῖσι βροτοῖσι*"]
---

δειλοῖσι βροτοῖσιν, “to wretched mortals,” is a verse-end of the Odyssey, and of Achilles' words to Priam in the last book (24.525); in Book 22 it closes two pictures of evil, the star that brings sickness (22.30–31; [[the-autumn-star]]) and the old man's body mangled by dogs (22.74–76; [[old-age-and-memory]]). The forms are new to the entry. Hecuba's δειλή is the word of the mourner for herself, as in the laments of Book 18 and 19 (see [[lament]] and [[hecuba]]).
