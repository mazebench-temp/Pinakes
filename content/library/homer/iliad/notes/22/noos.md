---
summary: Zeus gives Athena leave, “do as your mind is set” (22.185), and Achilles proposes to test the city “so that we may know what mind the Trojans have” (22.382). The verb νοέω marks the moments of seeing and knowing: “when Hector saw him, trembling seized him” (22.136); Andromache has a bath made ready, “nor did she know that far from all baths” Athena had brought him down (22.445–446), until on the wall she “saw him / being dragged in front of the city” (22.463–464).
---

The narrator calls her νηπίη, “the fool”, for not knowing (22.445; see [[nepios]] and [[bathing]]). See also [[peira]] and [[phobos]].
