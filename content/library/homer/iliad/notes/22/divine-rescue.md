---
summary: No god saves Hector. Zeus asks the gods “whether we shall save him from death” (22.175), and Athena answers that not all the other gods approve. Apollo, who snatched Hector away from Achilles in Book 20, rouses his knees for the last time and leaves him when his day sinks; and Athena, instead of snatching a man out of the fight, gives Achilles back the spear that has missed.
refs: [22.174-176, 22.202-204, 22.213, 22.219-221, 22.276-277]
---

**The question.** Zeus puts the question of the type to the gods. In Book 16 he wondered whether to snatch Sarpedon alive out of the battle and set him down in Lycia (16.435–438; see [[the-death-of-sarpedon]]); now he asks “whether we shall save him from death, or now at last / bring him down by Achilles” (22.175–176; see [[zeus]]). Athena answers with Hera's verses over Sarpedon (22.179–181; see [[not-all-the-gods-approve]]), and tells Achilles that Hector cannot get clear of them now, “not even if far-working Apollo should go to great lengths, / groveling before aegis-bearing father Zeus” (22.219–221; see [[athena]]).

**The last help.** In Book 20 Apollo saved Hector from Achilles in the words of the type, “but Apollo snatched him away / very easily, as a god can, and hid him in thick mist” (20.443–444). Here the narrator's “had not” brings no rescue: “And how could Hector have escaped the fates of death, / had not Apollo come to him for the very last time, / close by, who roused his might and his nimble knees?” (22.202–204; see [[would-have-had-not]] and [[apollo]]). The god gives him strength to run, not escape, and leaves him when the scales sink (22.213; see [[the-scales-of-zeus]]). The only god's hand in the fight helps the attacker: Athena snatches up the spear that Achilles has thrown in vain and gives it back to him (22.276–277). Hector draws his own conclusion: “Ah, truly the gods have called me to my death” (22.297; see [[hector]]).
