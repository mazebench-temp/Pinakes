---
summary: Achilles pursues Hector “As a falcon in the mountains, the nimblest of winged birds, / swoops easily after a timid dove”, darting at her again and again with shrill cries (22.139–144).
---

ἠΰτε κίρκος ὄρεσφιν ἐλαφρότατος πετεηνῶν / ῥηϊδίως οἴμησε μετὰ τρήρωνα πέλειαν (22.139–140; see [[the-falcon-and-the-dove]]). The falcon has all three of its marks here. It hunts the dove (see [[doves]]); it is “the nimblest of winged birds”, as it is in the Odyssey, where not even a hawk, a falcon, could keep pace with the Phaeacian ship (*Odyssey* 13.86–87); and it kills, or means to: ἑλέειν τέ ἑ θυμὸς ἀνώγει, “and his heart bids him seize her” (22.142). Its cry is shrill, ὀξὺ λεληκώς, “with shrill cries” (22.141). In Book 17 the falcon was the bird before which a cloud of starlings and jackdaws flee screaming, as the Achaeans fled before Aeneas and Hector (17.755–757; see [[a-cloud-of-starlings]]). In Book 22 the falcon is Achilles, and the bird it hunts is Hector.
