---
summary: The serpent of Book 22 is Hector, waiting for Achilles before the gates “As a serpent of the mountains waits for a man at its hole, / having fed on evil drugs”, glaring terribly as it coils (22.92–97).
---

ὡς δὲ δράκων ἐπὶ χειῇ ὀρέστερος ἄνδρα μένῃσι, “As a serpent of the mountains waits for a man at its hole” (22.93; see [[the-serpent-at-its-hole]]). The simile gives the δράκων both of its marks. It has the glare, σμερδαλέον δὲ δέδορκεν, “and it glares terribly” (22.95), with δέρκομαι, the verb from which its name is usually derived; and it has the poison, which it has eaten, βεβρωκὼς κακὰ φάρμακʼ, “having fed on evil drugs” (22.94). χειή, its hole, is named only in this simile. In Book 3 the serpent was the danger from which Alexander recoiled, like a man who starts back from a snake in a mountain glen (3.33–37; see [[man-who-meets-a-serpent]]). In Book 22 the Trojan is the serpent, and he waits, until Achilles comes near and trembling seizes him (22.136).
