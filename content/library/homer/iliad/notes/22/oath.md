---
summary: Hector thinks of offering Achilles an oath of the Trojan elders, to divide the city's wealth (22.119–121), and before the duel he asks that the gods be “the best / witnesses and overseers of agreements” that the victor will give back the loser's body (22.254–259). Achilles refuses: “as there are no trusty oaths between lions and men”, “nor will there be / oaths between us two” (22.262–266).
---

γερούσιον ὅρκον, “an oath of the elders”, would be sworn by the Trojan elders for the city, “to hide nothing” of its wealth (22.119–121; see [[geron]] and [[polis]]). Hector's terms are those of his challenge in Book 7, declared with Zeus as witness (7.76–86), and Achilles' refusal is told under [[no-oaths-between-lions-and-men]] (see also [[oath-formulas]] and [[honoring-the-dead]]).
