---
summary: Athena hails “shining Achilles, dear to Zeus” (22.216), and “shining Hector, looking straight at it,” avoids Achilles' spear (22.274).
---

The two men of the duel each have the epithet once in the book. Hector's is his own line-end formula, φαίδιμος Ἕκτωρ, at the moment of his last success: he sinks down, the spear flies over him, and Athena gives it back to Achilles unseen (22.274–277). When Achilles speaks to Hector's body, the verse is the one with which “shining Hector” spoke to the dead Patroclus, with “brilliant Achilles” in his place (22.364, as 16.858; [[brilliant]]).
