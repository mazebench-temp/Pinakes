# The hero's shining (22.25-32, 22.131-135), the gods (22.203-225, 22.270-277) and the kill (22.312-330).
refs: [22.25-32, 22.131-135, 22.203-225, 22.270-277, 22.312-330]
---

**The shining.** At the start of his day Diomedes blazed like the star of late summer (5.4–7), and at the end of Achilles' the same star returns: Priam sees him coming over the plain like “the star that men call by the name of the dog of Orion”, the brightest of the stars and a sign of evil (22.25–31; see [[the-autumn-star]]). As he comes at Hector the bronze shines around him “like the light / of a blazing fire or of the rising sun” (22.134–135), and in the charge the point of his spear shines like the evening star (22.317–320; see [[the-evening-star]]). Achilles' shining began on the evening before, when Athena set a fire above his head at the ditch (18.203–227; see [[achilles]]).

**The opponent.** The pattern brings the hero against an enemy of rank, with the gods taking part. Apollo snatched Hector away from Achilles in a mist in Book 20 (20.443–444; see [[divine-rescue]]); now he comes to Hector “for the very last time”, and leaves him when Zeus' scales turn against him (22.202–213; see [[the-scales-of-zeus]]). Athena takes Achilles' side, stops him so that he may catch his breath, deceives Hector into standing, and gives Achilles back his spear (22.214–247, 22.276–277; see [[athena]] and [[divine-intervention]]).

**The god he chases.** In the pattern the hero at his height goes against the gods: Diomedes wounded Aphrodite and Ares, and Achilles fought the river (see the note on Book 21). The book begins with Apollo telling Achilles that he has been chasing a god, and Achilles answering that he would take vengeance on him if he had the strength (22.8–20; see [[apollo-deceives-achilles]]).

**The end.** The aristeia ends with the death of the great enemy, and the hero's own death is foretold over the body (22.355–366; see [[the-death-of-hector]] and [[death-of-achilles-foretold]]). Patroclus' aristeia ended with his own death, struck by Apollo and killed by Hector (16.787–867); Achilles' ends with Hector's.
