summary: The war is measured by the two men. Hector tells Achilles, “the war would be lighter for the Trojans / once you were dead; for you are their greatest bane” (22.287–288); Athena promises to cut down Hector, “insatiable of battle though he is” (22.218); and Andromache, who had a bath ready for him “when he came home from the battle” (22.444), fears that even if their son escapes “the tearful war of the Achaeans” he will have toil and sorrow (22.487–488).
---

Andromache's πόλεμον πολύδακρυν Ἀχαιῶν, “the tearful war of the Achaeans”, is Priam's phrase on the wall in Book 3, where he does not blame Helen for it (3.164–165; see [[tearful]] and [[the-war-in-small]]). Achilles' word for Hector's last stand is πολεμιστής, “warrior”: he must now be “a spearman and a bold warrior” (22.269), and the god to be glutted with blood is “the warrior with the oxhide shield” (22.267; see [[ares]] and [[arete]]).
