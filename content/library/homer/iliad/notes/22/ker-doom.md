---
summary: Hector could not have “escaped the fates of death” so long without Apollo (22.202); Zeus sets in the scales “two fates of death that lays men low, / one of Achilles, and one of Hector” (22.210–211); and Achilles answers the dying Hector, “I will accept my doom then, whenever / Zeus is willing to bring it to pass” (22.365–366).
---

The verse of the scales is the one of Book 8, where the two fates were those of the Trojans and the Achaeans (22.210 = 8.70; see [[the-scales-of-zeus]]). Achilles' mother had told him of two κῆρες of his own, two ways his life could end (9.411); here one of the two in the scales is his, and it does not sink. His answer is the verse with which he chose in Book 18 (22.365 = 18.115; see [[the-short-life-of-achilles]] and [[moira]]).
