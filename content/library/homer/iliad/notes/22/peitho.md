summary: Persuasion fails all through the book, except by a god's trick. Priam and Hecuba “did not persuade Hector's heart” (22.78, 22.91); Hector remembers Polydamas' advice, “But I did not listen—it would have been far better” (22.103), and fears the reproach that he “trusted in his own strength and destroyed the army” (22.107). Athena promises Achilles, “I will go to him and persuade him to fight you face to face”, and he “obeyed” (22.222–224); Hector, dying, knows that he “was not going / to persuade” Achilles (22.356–357).
grc: [πεπιθήσω, πείσειν]
---

πεπιθήσω, the reduplicated future, is found only here in Homer (22.223), and what persuades Hector is the false Deiphobus, led “by craft” (22.247; see [[truth-and-deception]]). The verb is also the trust of the two men in their own strength: Hector “trusted in his own strength” in the imagined reproach, and Achilles springs after him “trusting in his swift feet” (22.107, 22.138; see [[obedience-and-persuasion]] and [[polydamas]]).
