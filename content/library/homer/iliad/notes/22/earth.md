summary: The earth of the book is the ground of death and the land of home. Achilles says that many more Trojans would have bitten the earth with their teeth; Priam foresees little children dashed to the ground; Achilles' spear sticks fast in the ground; Zeus gives Hector to his enemies to defile “in his own native land”; and Andromache sees him go to the house of Hades “beneath the depths of the earth”, and foresees that others will take away their son's lands.
# ἀρούρας is claimed with its verb, found only here, since bare ἀρούρας is also the fields struck by the sun (7.421)
# and sprinkled with snow (10.7).
grc: ["ἀπουρίσσουσιν ἀρούρας"]
en: [lands]
---

**Biting the earth.** But for Apollo's trick, Achilles says, many more Trojans “would have bitten the earth with their teeth before they reached Ilios” (22.17). γαῖαν ὀδὰξ εἷλον is the formula of a warrior's death, as when Agamemnon prayed that Hector's companions might “fall headlong in the dust and bite the earth” (2.418), and Achilles counted the Achaeans who “would have bitten the boundless ground with their teeth” while he nursed his wrath (19.61; see [[androktasia]]).

**The ground.** In Priam's picture of the fall of the city, “little children / dashed to the ground in grim combat” (22.63–64; see [[sack-of-troy-foretold]] and [[priam]]). Achilles' spear passes over Hector “and stuck fast in the ground” (22.276), as it stood fixed in the ground over Aeneas and over Lycaon (20.279–280, 21.69–70); Athena snatches it up and gives it back to him (22.276–277; see [[athena]] and [[the-pelian-ash]]).

**The native land.** When Achilles drags Hector behind his chariot, “Zeus gave him to his enemies / to defile in his own native land” (22.403–404; see [[dear-native-land]] and [[the-dragging-of-hector]]). πατρὶς γαῖα, the land of one's fathers, is the place of every return in the poem; here it is the ground in which Hector's head is dragged.

**Beneath the earth.** Andromache laments, “But now you go to the house of Hades beneath the depths of the earth” (22.482; see [[hades]] and [[andromache]]). ὑπὸ κεύθεσι γαίης, “beneath the depths of the earth”, is found elsewhere in Homer only at the end of the Odyssey, again of the house of Hades (*Odyssey* 24.204).

**The lands.** Her son, if he escapes the war, will have toil and sorrows, “for others will take away his lands” (22.489; see [[astyanax]] and [[the-day-of-orphanhood]]). ἀπουρίσσουσιν, found only here in Homer, is made from οὖρος, “boundary”: others will move the boundaries of the orphan's fields and take them for their own (see [[boundary-stones]]).
