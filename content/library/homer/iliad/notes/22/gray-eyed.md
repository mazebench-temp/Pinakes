summary: The epithet marks each of Athena's moves in the book: she answers Zeus (22.177), comes to Achilles at the springs (22.214), answers Hector in the likeness of Deiphobus (22.238), and, as Andromache prepares the bath, “gray-eyed Athena had brought him down at the hands of Achilles” (22.446).
---

The reply verse “Then the gray-eyed goddess Athena spoke to him in turn” is used twice (22.177, 22.238; [[answering-him]]). The second time the narrator names the goddess in a speech that Hector hears as his brother's, so that the reader knows what Hector does not (22.226–247; [[truth-and-deception]]). In the last use the narrator gives the killing to her, with Achilles' hands as its instrument (22.445–446; [[nepios]]). See [[athena]].
