summary: The parents weep before the fight and the city after it. Hecuba “mourned, shedding tears”, and “So the two, weeping, addressed their dear son” (22.79–90); she tells him that if he dies, “I shall never / weep for you on a bier” (22.86–87). Priam wishes Hector had died in his arms, so that “we would have had our fill of weeping and shedding tears” (22.427), and the three laments close in tears (22.429, 22.437, 22.515). In Andromache's picture the orphan's “cheeks are wet with tears”, and he goes “in tears” to his widowed mother (22.491, 22.499).
grc: [κλαύσομαι, κλαίοντε, δεδάκρυνται, δακρυόεις]
---

The verse of the parents' plea, ὣς τώ γε κλαίοντε προσαυδήτην, is the verse of the two sons of Antimachus begging Agamemnon for their lives (22.90 = 11.136; see [[supplication]]). The laments end in the formula of Briseis' lament for Patroclus, “So she spoke, weeping, and the women groaned with her” (22.515 = 19.301), and Priam's in the variant with the citizens (22.429; see [[lament]] and [[shedding-tears]]). Neither Achilles nor Hector weeps in the book.
