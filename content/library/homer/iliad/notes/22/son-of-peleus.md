---
summary: Apollo begins the book by addressing “the son of Peleus” (22.7–8); Priam and Zeus fear Hector “brought down by the son of Peleus” (22.40, 22.58, 22.176); the narrator names Achilles so as he springs after Hector, as Athena comes to him, and as Hector's spear strikes his shield (22.138, 22.193, 22.214, 22.290); and Hector speaks to him so twice before the duel (22.250, 22.278).
---

**Priam and Zeus.** Priam begs Hector not to wait for Achilles alone, “lest you soon meet your fate, / brought down by the son of Peleus, since he is far the stronger” (22.39–40), and not to “hold out great glory / to the son of Peleus” (22.57–58). Zeus asks the gods whether they shall save Hector or “bring him down by Achilles, son of Peleus, brave as he is” (22.176; [[brave]]).

**The chase and the duel.** “And the son of Peleus sprang at him, trusting in his swift feet” (22.138; [[swift]]). Hector's first words to him use the vocative of Apollo's taunt, Πηλέος υἱέ, “I will no longer flee from you, son of Peleus, as before” (22.250, as 22.8). After Achilles' miss, “Hector spoke to the noble son of Peleus” (22.278; [[blameless]]), and his own spear “struck the middle of the shield of the son of Peleus, and did not miss” (22.290).
