---
summary: “Phoebus Apollo” reveals himself to Achilles (22.7), leaves Hector when the scales of Zeus sink (22.213), and is named by the dying Hector with Paris as Achilles' killer at the Scaean gates (22.359).
---

The three uses mark Apollo's part in the book: the god who saved the Trojans by deceiving Achilles (22.7–20), the god who abandons his favorite, “and went off to the house of Hades; and Phoebus Apollo left him” (22.213; [[the-scales-of-zeus]]), and the god who will kill Achilles, “on that day when Paris and Phoebus Apollo / destroy you, brave as you are, at the Scaean gates” (22.359–360; [[death-of-achilles-foretold]]).
