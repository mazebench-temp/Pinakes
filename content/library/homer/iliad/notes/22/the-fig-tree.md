# ἐρινεὸν ἠνεμόεντα occurs only here; ἐρινεόν alone is also the fig tree from which Lycaon cut shoots (21.37).
grc: ["ἐρινεὸν ἠνεμόεντα"]
passages:
  - 22.145-148 | Past the lookout and the fig tree
---

{{quote:db8a620c-ae35-558b-8fd6-b662e170bed5}}

The fig tree stands near the wall. Andromache told Hector to “station the army by the wild fig tree”, where the city could most easily be scaled (6.433–434; see [[andromaches-advice]]), and the Trojans routed by Agamemnon ran past the tomb of Ilus and the fig tree toward the Scaean gates and the oak (11.166–170; see [[the-tomb-of-ilus]]). Here it is named with the lookout as the first landmark of the race around the city, which runs past them close under the wall and on to the springs of the Scamander (see [[the-lookout-at-troy]], [[the-springs-of-the-scamander]], [[the-walls-of-troy]] and [[the-pursuit-of-hector]]).

ἠνεμόεις, “windy”, is said in the Iliad of Ilios and of the Arcadian Enispe, and in the Odyssey of Mimas and of mountain heights and glens; this is the only tree that Homer calls windy (see [[windy]]).
