---
summary: Achilles boasts over Hector that he, the helper left behind at the ships, is the one “who have loosened your knees” (22.333–335).
grc: ["γούνατʼ ἔλυσα"]
en: [loosened your knees]
---

The first person γούνατʼ ἔλυσα is new to the entry: the killer says of himself what the narrator says of a killing (γούνατʼ ἔλυσε, 13.412). Laertes uses the same words in the Odyssey, of the suitors he wishes he had fought beside his son (*Odyssey* 24.376–382). See [[achilles]] and [[gounata]].
