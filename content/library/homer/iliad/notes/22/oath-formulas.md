summary: Hector imagines taking from the Trojans “an oath of the elders” to divide the city's wealth (22.119); before the duel he asks Achilles to call the gods as “witnesses and overseers of agreements” (22.254–255); and Achilles refuses: there are “no trusty oaths between lions and men,” nor will there be “oaths between us two” (22.262–266).
# γερούσιον ὅρκον (22.119), μάρτυροι ἔσσονται (22.255), ὅρκια ἔσσονται (22.266): each only here.
grc: ["γερούσιον ὅρκον", "μάρτυροι ἔσσονται", "ὅρκια ἔσσονται"]
en: [an oath of the elders, witnesses, oaths]
---

**Witnesses.** Hector names the gods as witnesses, μάρτυροι, the word with which Agamemnon called on the gods to watch over the trusty oaths of Book 3 (3.280): “But come, let us call upon the gods; for they will be the best / witnesses and overseers of agreements” (22.254–255; see the translation's notes on 22.254 and 22.261). His terms are those of a sworn compact: the victor will strip the loser's armor and give back his body (22.256–259).

**No oaths.** Achilles' answer denies the oath its ground, for oaths are sworn between those who can be friends (22.261–267; [[no-oaths-between-lions-and-men]], [[with-the-oxhide-shield]]). The “oath of the elders” of Hector's monologue is the sworn undertaking of the Trojan elders to hide nothing, half of the city's goods for the besiegers (22.119–121; see the translation's note on 22.117–120 and [[pondering-two-ways]]).
