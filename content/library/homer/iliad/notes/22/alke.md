---
summary: Hector, having dodged Achilles' spear, accuses him of lying “so that in fear of you I might forget my might and my courage” (22.282).
---

The half-verse μένεος ἀλκῆς τε λάθωμαι is the one with which Hector refused his mother's wine in Book 6, “for fear you unnerve me and rob me of my might, and I forget my courage” (6.265; see [[menos]] and [[hecuba]]). To forget courage is the opposite of the leaders' call, “remember your furious courage” (6.112), and Achilles has just turned that call into a taunt, “Remember all your prowess” (22.268; see [[arete]]).
