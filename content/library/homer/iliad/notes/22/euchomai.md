---
summary: The family's four words in the book are a triumph, a boast over the fallen, a prayer and a mother's pride. Hector chooses the fight to see “to which of us the Olympian will hold out triumph” (22.130); when he falls, “brilliant Achilles boasted over him” (22.330), and says that the Trojans “prayed as to a god” to the man he has killed (22.394); and Hecuba calls her son “my boast throughout the city” (22.433).
---

The verse that introduces Achilles' boast is the one that introduced his boast over Iphition, with the dust in place of the thud (22.330; 20.388; see [[fell-with-a-thud]]). εὐχωλή, “a thing to boast of”, was in Book 2 the Helen whom the Achaeans would leave to Priam and the Trojans if they sailed home (2.160–161, 2.176, 4.173); for Hecuba the Trojans' boast was Hector (22.433; see [[kydos]] and [[hecuba]]). Of the four only εὐχετόωντο is a prayer, and prayer counts it (22.394; see [[prayer]] and [[gods-and-mortals]]).
