summary: Andromache's father is named twice over Hector's death, each time by his house: Hector took her “from the house of Eetion, after giving a countless bride-price” (22.472), and in her lament she was raised “in the house of Eetion, who reared me when I was little, / he ill-fated, and I of dread fate; would that he had never begotten me!” (22.480–481).
# Unnamed: "he ill-fated", the father who should never have begotten her (22.481).
refs: [22.481]
---

**The house of Eetion.** Both times he is named, it is by his house, ἐκ δόμου Ἠετίωνος (22.472) and ἐν δόμῳ Ἠετίωνος (22.480; see [[house]]). The first, in the narrator's words, is the house of her wedding. When she sees Hector dragged and faints, she flings from her head the veil that golden Aphrodite gave her “on the day when Hector of the gleaming helmet took her / from the house of Eetion, after giving a countless bride-price” (22.470–472; see [[veil]], [[bride-price]] and [[marriage]]). The second, in her own words, is the house of her childhood, set against the house of Priam in her lament: “you in Troy in the house of Priam, / and I in Thebe under wooded Placus, / in the house of Eetion” (22.478–480; see [[thebe]] and [[placus]]).

**Ill-fated.** δύσμορος, “ill-fated” (22.481), is the word Priam used of himself on the wall (22.60), and in the Iliad it is found only in these two places: the old king of Troy and the dead king of Thebe share it, two fathers whom Achilles has bereaved or killed (6.414–416; see [[priam]] and [[sack-of-thebe]]). Andromache is αἰνόμορος, “of dread fate”, a word the Iliad uses only here. In Book 6 she told Hector that Achilles had killed her father, and that Hector was “father to me, and lady mother, / and brother” (6.413–430; see [[hector-and-andromache]]). Now she has lost the second father too, and wishes that the first had never begotten her (22.481), as Helen wished that a storm had carried her away on the day her mother bore her (6.345–348; see [[andromache]]).
