---
summary: Priam measures sorrow. The death of Lycaon and Polydorus “is sorrow to my heart and to their mother”, but “to the rest of the people the sorrow will be shorter-lived, / unless you die too” (22.53–55); and Achilles, he says after the death, “on me above all others he has laid sorrows” (22.422).
---

μινυνθάδιος, “short-lived”, the word of Achilles' own life (1.352; see [[the-short-life-of-achilles]]), is here the measure of the people's sorrow (22.54). ἄλγεʼ ἔθηκε, “he has laid sorrows”, ends the second line of the poem, where the wrath “laid countless sorrows on the Achaeans” (1.2), and in Homer is found again only here, where the sorrows are a Trojan father's (22.422; see [[bereaved-parents]], [[achos]] and [[priam]]).
