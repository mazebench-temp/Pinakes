summary: Hector argues himself out of retreat and then runs. Going back inside the wall would bring Polydamas' reproach and the people's, so it is “far better” to face Achilles (22.99–110); but when Achilles comes, “trembling seized him; and he could no longer bear / to stay there, but left the gates behind him, and went off in flight” (22.136–137). After three circuits of the city he stands: “I will no longer flee from you, son of Peleus” (22.250), and asks for the brave man's wound, “You will not plant your spear in my back as I flee, / but drive it through my chest as I come straight on” (22.283–284).

The wound in the back is the coward's, as Idomeneus told Meriones, whose wound would never fall “on the back of your neck, nor on your back” (13.288–289; see [[death-in-battle]]). Hector's flight is no retreat into the city: each time he makes for the Dardanian gates, Achilles turns him back toward the plain (22.194–198; see [[the-pursuit-of-hector]] and [[dardanian-gates]]). Andromache remembers the opposite habit, the pride that kept him from staying “in the crowd of men” (22.457–459; see [[menos]]). The Trojans have already taken refuge, “in panic like fawns” (22.1; see [[like-fawns]] and [[phobos]]).
