---
summary: Andromache fears that Achilles “has now put an end to the grievous pride / that held him” (22.457–458).
---

ἀγηνορίη, “pride,” is the word Diomedes used of Achilles' pride (9.700); the translation keeps it apart from εὐχωλή, “boast” (see its table of key words, under ἀγηνορίη). Andromache's “grievous” pride is Hector's habit of running out in front of the ranks, “yielding to no one in his might” (22.458–459; [[bold]]). See [[andromache]] and [[hector]].
