{{quote:b77dbb45-360f-5073-b571-834339b3eed6}}

The first two verses of the answer are those of Book 8 (22.183–184 = 8.39–40). There Zeus spoke them smiling, after Athena had promised to keep out of the war as he commanded and asked only to give the Argives counsel (8.30–40); here they end his own wish to save Hector (22.168–176; see [[zeus]] and [[not-all-the-gods-approve]]). The third verse is new to this scene, “do as your mind is set, and hang back no longer” (22.185), and he sends her down in the verses of Book 4 (22.186–187 = 4.73–74; see [[athena]]). This is the last of the title's three uses in the Iliad (4.515, 8.39, 22.183); the *Odyssey* has it once (*Odyssey* 3.378).
