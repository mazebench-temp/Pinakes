---
summary: As often as Hector makes a rush for the Dardanian gates, “to dart straight in beneath the well-built towers,” Achilles turns him back (22.194–197).
grc: [ἐϋδμήτους]
---

The accusative ἐϋδμήτους is new to the entry. Hector hopes that the men on the towers may defend him with their missiles, and every time Achilles gets in front of him and drives him back toward the plain (22.194–198; [[dardanian-gates]], [[the-walls-of-troy]]). See [[hector]].
