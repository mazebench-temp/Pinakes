---
summary: After its battle with Achilles in Book 21, the river is named once in this book, at its springs near the walls of Troy, where “the two sources / of the eddying Scamander leap up”, one warm and one cold. Achilles and Hector run past them, and at the fourth coming Zeus weighs their fates (22.147–152, 22.208–213).
---

“And came to the two fair-flowing springs, where the two sources / of the eddying Scamander leap up” (22.147–148; see [[the-springs-of-the-scamander]], [[fair-flowing]] and [[eddying]]). Σκαμάνδρου δινήεντος, “of the eddying Scamander”, is found only here; the river under its gods' name is “the eddying Xanthus” in the couplet of its ford (Ξάνθου δινήεντος, 14.434 = 21.2). The god who fought Achilles in Book 21 and swore never again to save the Trojans (21.211–384; see the note on Book 21) is not seen in this book; the chase runs past his springs (see [[the-pursuit-of-hector]]).

The springs near the city are called the river's sources, though the Scamander, like the other rivers of the Troad, flows down from Ida (12.19–21; see [[ida]]). Strabo reports Demetrius of Scepsis, who placed the river's single source on Cotylus, a hill of Ida, and found no hot spring at the place (13.1.43; see [[strabo-on-the-chase-around-troy]]).
