---
summary: Strabo, after Demetrius of Scepsis, uses the chase of this book against the claim that the Ilium of his day stood on Homer's site, since only the old site could be run around, and explains why only one of Homer's two springs could still be found. He finds no Placus at deserted Thebe, and reports that Hector's son Scamandrius, whose orphaned life Andromache foresees, refounded Scepsis and reigned there.
---

- The run around the city, which fits only the old site of Ilium at the Village of the Ilians, and the wild fig tree below it (*Geography* 13.1.34–38, on 22.145, 22.165): [[strabo-on-the-chase-around-troy]], [[the-fig-tree]] and [[the-lookout-at-troy]].
- The two springs, one hot and one cold, of which only the cold could be found: the hot had failed, and the Scamander rises from a single spring on Ida (*Geography* 13.1.43, 1.3.17, quoting 22.147–152): [[strabo-on-the-chase-around-troy]] and [[the-springs-of-the-scamander]].
- **Thebe under Placus.** Andromache was born “in Thebe under wooded Placus, / in the house of Eetion” (22.479–480). In Strabo's day Thebe lay deserted in the interior, in the plain near Adramyttium (*Geography* 13.1.61), the city “which the poet calls ‘under wooded Placus’; but neither Placus nor Plax is said of anything there, nor does a forest lie above it, although it is near Ida” (*Geography* 13.1.65). He counts Eetion's realm, the home of Andromache, as the second of the Trojan dynasties of the Troad (*Geography* 13.1.7, quoting 6.395–397): [[thebe]], [[placus]] and [[eetion]].
- **Hector's son.** Andromache foresees for the orphan Astyanax a life of want, robbed of his lands and driven from the feast (22.487–499). Strabo reports a tradition in which the boy lived: Scamandrius, the son of Hector, with Ascanius, the son of Aeneas, moved the people of old Scepsis sixty stades lower down to the Scepsis of his own day, and the two families are said to have reigned there for a long time (*Geography* 13.1.52): [[astyanax]] and [[strabo-on-the-rule-of-aeneas]].
