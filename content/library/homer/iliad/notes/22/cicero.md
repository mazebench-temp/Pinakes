---
summary: Cicero quotes Hector's monologue in his letters, the shame before the Trojans and the Trojan women and the fear of Polydamas' reproach, and in the Tusculan Disputations he takes the dragging of Hector as proof that the dead feel nothing: Achilles dragged not Hector but the body that had been Hector's.
---

- “I would feel shame before the Trojans and the Trojan women with trailing robes” and “Polydamas will be the first to heap disgrace on me”, with Cato for Polydamas (*Letters to Atticus* 2.5.1, 7.1.4, quoting 22.105 and 22.100): [[cicero-trojans]].
- The dragging of Hector, the tragic heroine who saw it, and Accius' Achilles, who gave Priam back the body but took Hector away (*Tusculan Disputations* 1.105, on 22.395–404): [[cicero-the-body-of-hector]] and [[the-dragging-of-hector]].
