summary: The last clause of the forecast that Zeus gave Hera in Book 15 to fall within the poem is fulfilled: “And in anger for him brilliant Achilles will kill Hector” (15.68). Zeus watches it come, asks the gods whether to save Hector and lets it be, and his scales confirm it; Athena, whom the narrator had named in Book 15 as rousing Hector's fated day, brings it about.

---

**The forecast.** In Book 15 Zeus told Hera the course of the war: Hector would kill Patroclus before Ilios, “And in anger for him brilliant Achilles will kill Hector. / From then on I would bring about a counterattack from the ships, / always and without a break, until the Achaeans / take steep Ilios through the counsels of Athena” (15.68–71; see the note on Book 15). Book 22 tells the clause about Hector. Achilles kills him in anger for Patroclus, whom he names in his boast over the fallen man, “Hector, you thought, I suppose, when you were stripping Patroclus, / that you would be safe” (22.331–332; see [[achilles]] and [[patroclus]]). The rest of the forecast lies beyond the poem, and the book looks toward it: the city wails as if all Ilios “were smoldering with fire from top to bottom” (22.410–411; see [[the-fall-of-troy]]), and Andromache tells the dead Hector, “for you alone guarded their gates and long walls” (22.507).

**Zeus' part.** The forecast does not make Zeus indifferent. He grieves for Hector, asks the gods whether to save him, and yields to Athena (22.166–187; see [[zeus]] and [[not-all-the-gods-approve]]); then he weighs the fates, and Hector's day sinks (22.208–213; see [[the-scales-of-zeus]]). In Book 15 the narrator had said that Zeus gave Hector glory because he was to be short-lived, “for already she was rousing his fated day against him, / Pallas Athena, under the strength of the son of Peleus” (15.613–614; see [[short-lived]]). Here she does it, and the narrator says so: “gray-eyed Athena had brought him down at the hands of Achilles” (22.446; see [[athena]]). After the killing Zeus gives the body to Hector's enemies to defile (22.403–404).
