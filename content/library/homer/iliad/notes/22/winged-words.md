summary: Hecuba, “shedding tears,” addresses Hector with winged words (22.81); Athena, “standing near him,” addresses first Achilles and then Hector in the same verse (22.215, 22.228); and Achilles, having stripped Hector, “stood among the Achaeans and spoke winged words” (22.377).
---

**Athena's two speeches.** ἀγχοῦ δʼ ἱσταμένη ἔπεα πτερόεντα προσηύδα, “and standing near him she addressed him with winged words,” is the verse of a goddess's approach: Athena's to Pandarus in Laodocus' likeness, to urge him to break the truce (4.92), and to Diomedes (5.123), and Iris' to Achilles (18.169). Here it is spoken twice within thirteen verses, once to Achilles in her own form and once to Hector in the likeness of Deiphobus (22.214–215, 22.226–228; [[truth-and-deception]], [[unyielding]]).

**Hecuba and Achilles.** Hecuba's verse begins with the formula of weeping, δάκρυ χέουσʼ (22.81; [[shedding-tears]]). Achilles' has the singular ἀγόρευεν, as in Book 21, and στὰς ἐν Ἀχαιοῖσιν, “he stood among the Achaeans,” found only here (22.377). See [[hecuba]], [[athena]] and [[achilles]].
