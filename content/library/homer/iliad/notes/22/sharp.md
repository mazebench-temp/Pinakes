---
summary: Priam pictures his own death “when someone with the sharp bronze / has struck or hit me” and the young man “torn by the sharp bronze” (22.67, 22.72); Hector draws “the sharp sword” for his last charge (22.306, 22.311); the falcon of the simile cries shrilly (22.141); and Priam's grief for Hector is “sharp grief” (22.425).
---

**The sharp bronze.** Priam's speech uses the formula twice in four verses, for the old man killed at his own door and for the young man killed in battle, for whom “it is all fitting” (22.66–73; [[old-age-and-memory]]).

**The sword.** With his spear gone, Hector draws the sword that hung beneath his flank, “great and heavy,” and swoops like an eagle, “brandishing his sharp sword” (22.306–311); the half-verse φάσγανον ὀξύ ends both verses.

**Shrill and sharp.** ὀξὺ λεληκώς, “with shrill cries” (22.141), is the adverb of sound, as in Hector's shrill shout, which did not escape Menelaus (17.89). ἄχος ὀξύ, “sharp grief” (22.425), is the grief that struck Zeus when he heard of Eurystheus' birth (19.125): “for all of them I do not mourn so much, grieved though I am, / as for one, for whom sharp grief will bring me down to the house of Hades, / for Hector” (22.424–426; [[priam]]).
