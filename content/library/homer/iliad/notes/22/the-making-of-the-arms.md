---
summary: Achilles meets Hector in the armor Hephaestus made, which shines around him like a blazing fire or the rising sun (22.134–135); in the charge he covers his chest with the shield and nods with the helmet whose plumes of gold “Hephaestus had set thick about the crest” (22.313–316). Hector wears the armor the new arms replaced, which he stripped from Patroclus (22.322–323).
# Unnamed: the armor shining (22.32, 22.134-135), the shield and the helmet (22.313-316), and the old armor (22.322-323).
refs: [22.32, 22.134-135, 22.313-316, 22.322-323]
---

**Like fire or the sun.** “and the bronze shone around him like the light / of a blazing fire or of the rising sun” (22.134–135). Hephaestus made the corselet “brighter than the light of fire” (18.610), and when Achilles armed, the blaze of the shield reached the upper air, like the moon and like a fire burning in the mountains seen by sailors at sea (19.373–380; see [[the-shield-of-achilles]]). Walter Leaf takes ἀμφί, “around him”, of the whole armor, to right and left, and compares the verse with which Priam's sight of him ended, when Achilles came over the plain like the star of late summer: “so the bronze shone about his chest as he ran” (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 22.134; 22.32 = 13.245; see [[the-autumn-star]]).

The last verse repeats the arming (22.316 = 19.383). Leaf takes it in both places for an interpolation made to bring in an allusion to the making of the arms, and says that here it is out of place in the old part of the poems, where the making of the arms is unknown (on 19.382, 22.315; see the translation's note on 22.315–316 and [[helmet]]). καλὸν δαιδάλεον, “fine and intricately worked”, of the shield (22.314), is said of it in the arming and of the helmet in the making (19.380, 18.612; see [[intricately-worked]]).

**The old armor and the new.** “All the rest of his flesh the bronze armor covered, / the fine armor he had stripped from mighty Patroclus when he killed him” (22.322–323). The arms were made because Hector had the old ones: Thetis told her son that Hector glories in them on his own shoulders, but not for long, and promised to bring him new armor at dawn (18.130–137). At the Scaean gates the two meet, Achilles in the god's armor and Hector in the armor the gods gave Peleus, which covers him everywhere but at the throat (22.324–327; see [[the-armor-of-peleus]] and [[the-death-of-hector]]). When Hector is dead Achilles strips it from him and carries it to the ships (22.367–369, 22.399).
