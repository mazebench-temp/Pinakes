---
summary: Hector thinks of offering Achilles Helen “and the possessions with her, / all, as many as Alexander in his hollow ships / brought to Troy”, and half of “the wealth that the lovely citadel holds within it” (22.114–121).
---

These are the goods of Book 3, staked with Helen on the duel of Paris and Menelaus (3.282, 3.285; see [[the-duel-of-paris-and-menelaus]]), which Paris offered to give back without Helen and the Achaeans refused: the herald's words, κτήματα μὲν ὅσʼ Ἀλέξανδρος κοίλῃς ἐνὶ νηυσὶν (7.389), return in Hector's thought here (ὅσσά τʼ Ἀλέξανδρος κοίλῃς ἐνὶ νηυσὶν, 22.115), and “Let no one now accept the possessions of Alexander” was Diomedes' answer (7.400). Hector puts the thought aside (22.122; see [[pondering-two-ways]] and [[the-beginning-of-evils]]).
