---
summary: The ships are “hollow” in Hector's memory of the treasures Alexander brought to Troy “in his hollow ships” (22.115), in the false Deiphobus' fear that Achilles will carry their spoils “to the hollow ships” (22.246), in Achilles' boast and his call to go back with the body (22.334, 22.392), and as Andromache sees Hector dragged “toward the hollow ships of the Achaeans” (22.465).
---

The epithet names the goal of each side's hope and fear: the Achaean ships where the spoils are taken, where Achilles waited while Patroclus fell, “was left behind at the hollow ships, I myself” (22.333–334), and where Hector's body is dragged (22.464–465). The ships of the Achaeans are also where the dogs will devour Hector, in the warnings of his mother and of Achilles (22.89, 22.339; [[swift-dogs]], [[curved-ships]]). See [[ships]].
