summary: Priam expects to ransom his sons Lycaon and Polydorus, if they are alive: “we will ransom them with bronze and gold, for we have it at home” (22.50). Hector, dying, offers bronze and gold for his body, and Achilles refuses any ransom, “not even if ten and twenty times the ransom / they bring here and weigh out”, “not even if he should bid them weigh out your own body in gold” (22.340–352).
grc: [ἀπολυσόμεθʼ]
---

Priam does not know that both sons are dead, Polydorus in Book 20 and Lycaon, ransomed once already, in Book 21 (20.407–418, 21.34–135; see [[lycaon-son-of-priam]], [[polydorus-son-of-priam]] and [[laothoe]]). Hector calls his offer δῶρα, “gifts” (22.341; see [[doron]]); Achilles names it ἄποινα and multiplies it past counting, ten and twenty times (εἰκοσινήριτα, only here in Homer), and then the body's own weight in gold (22.349–351). In the last book he accepts a ransom from Priam himself (see [[book-1-and-book-24]] and [[supplication]]).
