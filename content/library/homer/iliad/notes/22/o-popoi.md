---
summary: Zeus begins his speech to the gods, “Ah, truly a man dear to me, pursued around the wall” (22.168); Hector, knowing he is deceived, “Ah, truly the gods have called me to my death” (22.297); and an Achaean jeers over the body, “Ah, truly much softer to handle now / is Hector” (22.373–374).
---

The three exclamations frame Hector's death: the god's pity before it, the victim's recognition, and the enemy's mockery after it. Each is followed by ἦ, “truly,” and the last two by ἦ μάλα δή. Plato's Socrates would not have the poets make the greatest of the gods so unlike himself as to say Zeus' words (*Republic* 3.388c; his text has περὶ ἄστυ for περὶ τεῖχος).
