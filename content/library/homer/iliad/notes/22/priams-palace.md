---
summary: Andromache begins her lament with the houses where she and Hector were born: “you in Troy in the house of Priam, / and I in Thebe under wooded Placus” (22.478–479).
grc: ["Πριάμου κατὰ δῶμα"]
---

“We were both born, it seems, to one fate”, she says, and names the two houses side by side, Priam's in Troy and Eetion's at Thebe (22.477–481; see [[thebe]] and [[eetion]]). The palace of fifty chambers, where Priam's sons slept beside their wives, is described when Hector comes to it in Book 6 (6.242–250). When Hector dies, Andromache is not there but in their own house, weaving “in the inner part of the high house” (22.440; see [[weaving]]).
