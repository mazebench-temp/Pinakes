summary: Vengeance is what Achilles cannot take from a god and will take from a man. Apollo has “no fear at all of vengeance hereafter”, and Achilles answers, “Truly I would take vengeance on you, if I had the strength” (22.19–20). To Hector he says, “now you will pay back all at once / the sorrows of my companions, whom you killed raging with your spear” (22.271–272).
grc: [τίσιν, τισαίμην, ἀποτίσεις]
---

Achilles' line ends as Telemachus' does in the Ithacan assembly, “Truly I would defend myself, if I had the strength” (*Odyssey* 2.62), which Walter Leaf compares, reporting from the T scholia a reading ᾗ for ἦ (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 22.20; see the translation's note on 22.20 and [[gods-and-mortals]]). The debt Hector is to pay is the deaths of Achilles' companions, and above all of Patroclus (22.271–272, 22.331–336; see [[hetairos]] and [[patroclus]]).
