summary: The book is Hector's flight. It opens on the Trojans in the city, “in panic like fawns” (22.1), whom Achilles had “put to flight” (22.11); when Hector saw Achilles coming, “trembling seized him”, and he “went off in flight” (22.136–137), as the dove in the simile “flees out from under” the falcon (22.141). When he stands at last he says, “I will no longer flee from you, son of Peleus” (22.250).
refs: [22.1, 22.11, 22.136-144, 22.250-251]

πεφυζότες, “in panic”, closes the run of the word that fills Book 21 (22.1; see the translation's note on 22.1). Hector's flight is told with φοβέομαι, the verb of rout, and with τρόμος, “trembling”, in the words that told how the Myrmidons could not face the armor Thetis brought, ἕλε τρόμος, οὐδέ … ἔτλη (22.136; 19.14). Before Ajax in Book 7 Hector's heart pounded, but he could not shrink back, since he had made the challenge (7.215–218; see [[deos]] and [[retreat]]). The god's taunt and Hector's resolve frame the pursuit in the same verb (ἐφόβησας, 22.11; φοβήσομαι, 22.250; see [[the-falcon-and-the-dove]] and [[the-pursuit-of-hector]]).
