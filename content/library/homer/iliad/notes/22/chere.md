---
summary: Andromache's first lament makes her the widow she foresaw in Book 6: Hector leaves her “a widow in the halls” with a son still a mere infant (22.483–484), and she imagines the orphan, on “The day of orphanhood”, coming back in tears “to his widowed mother” (22.490–499).
---

In Book 6 she had told him that she would soon be his widow (6.408–409), and the words of this lament return in her lament over his body in the last book (24.725–727; see [[andromache]], [[astyanax]] and [[childhood]]). ἦμαρ ὀρφανικόν, “the day of orphanhood”, is one of the poem's days that change a life, like the ἐλεύθερον ἦμαρ and δούλιον ἦμαρ, “the day of freedom” and “the day of slavery”, of Hector's farewell to her (6.455, 6.463; see [[the-day-of-orphanhood]] and [[slavery-and-freedom]]).
