---
summary: Andromache, hearing the wailing from the tower, fears “that brilliant Achilles has cut off my bold Hector / alone from the city” (22.455–456).
---

What she fears in him is the boldness itself: “and has now put an end to the grievous pride / that held him, since he never stayed in the crowd of men, / but ran far out in front, yielding to no one in his might” (22.457–459; [[grievous]]). See [[andromache]] and [[hector]].
