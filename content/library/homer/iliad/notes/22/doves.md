summary: The dove of Book 22 is Hector, after whom Achilles swoops as a falcon in the mountains swoops on a timid dove, which flees out from under him while he darts at her again and again (22.139–144).
# πέλειαν, the accusative (22.140; again 23.853, 23.855, 23.874).
grc: [πέλειαν]
---

ῥηϊδίως οἴμησε μετὰ τρήρωνα πέλειαν, “swoops easily after a timid dove” (22.140; see [[the-falcon-and-the-dove]] and [[falcon]]). τρήρων, “timid”, from τρέω, “flee in fear”, is the dove's standing epithet (see [[timid]]), and the verb returns for the man: τρέσε δʼ Ἕκτωρ, “and Hector fled” (22.143). ἣ δέ θʼ ὕπαιθα φοβεῖται, “and she flees out from under him” (22.141), is the flight of Artemis from Hera in the simile of the dove and the hawk a book before (21.493; see [[the-dove-and-the-hawk]]). That dove reached her hollow rock. This one is still in the air when the simile ends, and Hector never reaches the gates.
