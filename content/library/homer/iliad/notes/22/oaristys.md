---
summary: Waiting for Achilles, Hector knows that there is no way now, “from an oak or from a rock, / to talk intimately with him, as a maiden and a young man, / a maiden and a young man talk intimately with each other” (22.126–128).
---

ὀαριζέμεναι and ὀαρίζετον, the infinitive and the dual, occur only here in Homer; the verb's one other use is Hector's talk with Andromache in Book 6 (ὀάριζε, 6.516; see [[hector-and-andromache]]). Hector has pictured laying down his shield, helmet and spear and going to Achilles unarmed with an offer of terms (22.111–121), and he gives the picture up: Achilles would not pity him, but “will kill me naked, / just like a woman, once I have taken off my armor” (22.123–125; see [[pondering-two-ways]]). The talk of a maiden and a young man is the measure of what cannot pass between them. The sense of ἀπὸ δρυὸς οὐδʼ ἀπὸ πέτρης, “from an oak or from a rock”, is disputed (see [[words-and-violence]]).
