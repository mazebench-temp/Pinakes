**Where are the threats?** Idomeneus is going back to his hut from a wounded companion when Poseidon meets him, in the voice of Thoas, son of Andraemon: “Idomeneus, counselor of the Cretans, where have your threats gone, / the threats the sons of the Achaeans used to make against the Trojans?” (13.219–220; see [[poseidon]], [[thoas]] and [[idomeneus]]). The question is Agamemnon's from Book 8, “Where have our boasts gone, when we said that we were the best?” (8.229), put now by a god in disguise to one man. The same verse, with another name and people, is Apollo's to Aeneas in Book 20: in the likeness of Lycaon he asks “Aeneas, counselor of the Trojans, where have your threats gone”, the threats he made to the Trojan kings over the wine, that he would fight Achilles face to face (20.79–85). Both times a god takes a mortal's shape to rouse a hero with his own past words.

Idomeneus does not accept the reproach. No man now is to blame, he says, as far as he knows: all of them know how to make war, and no one shrinks from it; it must be the pleasure of Zeus that the Achaeans perish here, nameless, far from Argos (13.222–227; see [[who-is-to-blame]]). He turns the question back on the questioner, a man who was always staunch and urges on others: let him not give up now, and call on every man (13.228–230). The god's answer is a curse on whoever holds back that day, and a call to arms (13.231–238; see [[joined-prowess]]).

**Where are the men?** At the end of the book Hector, having found his leaders dead or wounded, turns the “where” of rebuke on Paris: “where are Deiphobus and mighty lord Helenus, / and Adamas, son of Asius, and Asius, son of Hyrtacus? / And where is Othryoneus?” (13.770–772; see [[hector]] and [[paris]]). The question expects an answer that shames the hearer, as Aeneas' did when he asked Pandarus where his bow and his fame had gone (5.171–172). Paris' answer is the plain fact: the companions Hector asks about have been killed, and Deiphobus and Helenus have gone away wounded (13.778–783; see [[beyond-ones-strength]]).
