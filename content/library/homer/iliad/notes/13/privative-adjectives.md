**Unbreakable.** Poseidon casts golden hobbles around his horses' feet, ἀρρήκτους ἀλύτους, “unbreakable, not to be loosed, so that they might stay there steadfast / until their lord returned” (13.37–38; see [[chariot-journey]]). The verse is the one in which Hephaestus forges the bonds that will hold Ares and Aphrodite on his bed, ὄφρʼ ἔμπεδον αὖθι μένοιεν (Od. 8.274–275). The pair returns, joined by τε … τε, for the rope of strife and war that Zeus and Poseidon stretch over both armies, ἄρρηκτόν τʼ ἄλυτόν τε, “unbreakable and not to be loosed, which loosened the knees of many” (13.360; see [[the-rope-of-war]] and [[loosened-his-limbs]]). What cannot be loosed loosens the knees of men.

**Unharmed.** Hector, going among the champions to find Deiphobus, Helenus, Adamas and Asius, “found them no longer at all unharmed or unscathed” (ἀπήμονας οὐδʼ ἀνολέθρους, 13.761; see [[hector]]): the two negatives are themselves negated, and the litotes tells that some are dead and some wounded (13.762–764; see [[litotes]]).

**Not privative.** ἄβρομοι αὐΐαχοι, said of the Trojans who follow Hector like a flame or a storm wind (13.41), both words only here, are taken with an ἀ- of intensity, “roaring and crying aloud”, as rendered. Read as privatives they would mean “without roar, without cry”, which suits neither the simile nor the clamor of the Trojans elsewhere (see the translation's note on 13.41).
