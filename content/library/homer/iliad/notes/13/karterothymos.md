---
summary: Zeus wills victory for the Trojans to give glory to Thetis “and her stout-hearted son”; the book adds the accusative καρτερόθυμον.
grc: [καρτερόθυμον]
---

The narrator explains the conflict of the two sons of Cronus: “Zeus wished for victory for the Trojans and for Hector, / to give glory to Achilles, swift of foot; but he did not at all / wish the Achaean army to perish before Ilios, / but was giving glory to Thetis and her stout-hearted son” (13.347–350; [[plan-of-zeus]], [[achilles]], [[thetis]], [[kydos]]). The epithet with which Pandarus addressed Diomedes (5.277) here names Achilles, absent from the fighting, in the verses that recall Thetis' prayer for her son's honor (1.505–510). The accusative καρτερόθυμον is new to the entry; the Odyssey gives it to Heracles (Od. 21.25).
