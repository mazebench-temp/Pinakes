---
summary: Sheep in Book 13 are the flock that follows the ram to water, the Trojans following Aeneas (13.492–495), and their wool: Agenor binds Helenus' wounded hand with a sling of well-twisted sheep's wool, and the Locrians fight with bows and slings of the same wool (13.599–600, 13.716).
grc: ["κτίλον ἕσπετο μῆλα", "οἰὸς ἀώτῳ"]
---

**The flock.** “The army followed, as sheep follow the ram from the pasture to drink, and the shepherd is glad at heart” (13.492–493). μῆλα are the flock; κτίλος is the ram that leads it (see [[ram]] and [[sheep-following-the-ram]]). In Book 4 the Trojan host was a flock of ewes in a rich man's fold, bleating without pause (4.433–438; see [[sheep-in-the-fold]]); here the flock is an army that follows its leader.

**The wool.** ἐϋστρεφεῖ οἰὸς ἀώτῳ, “with well-twisted sheep's wool” (13.599, 13.716). ἄωτος is the finest wool, the fleece, and Telemachus sleeps under a blanket of it in the Odyssey (οἰὸς ἀώτῳ, *Odyssey* 1.443). Twisted, it makes a sling. Agenor binds up Helenus' hand, pierced by Menelaus' spear, “with well-twisted sheep's wool, a sling, which an attendant carried for him” (13.598–600), and the Locrians, who have no helmets, shields or spears, follow the son of Oileus to Troy “trusting in bows and in well-twisted sheep's wool”, and shoot from behind the armed men (13.712–722; see [[sling]] and [[locrians]]). The digitized text printed οἶος, “alone”, at 13.716, a slip corrected to οἰός as at 13.599 (see the translation's note on 13.599–600 and 13.716).
