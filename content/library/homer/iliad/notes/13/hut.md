**Going back for a spear.** Meriones, his spear broken on Deiphobus' shield, goes “along the huts and ships of the Achaeans / to bring a long spear, which he had left behind in his hut” (13.167–168; see [[meriones]] and [[spear]]). κλισίηφι, “in his hut”, keeps the old case-ending -φι. The first of the two verses returns at once for Poseidon, who goes “along the huts and ships of the Achaeans” to urge on the Danaans (13.208).

**Idomeneus' hut.** Idomeneus is coming from a companion wounded behind the knee, whom he has left to the healers, and is going to his hut (13.210–214; see [[idomeneus]] and [[healing]]). Poseidon, in Thoas' likeness, tells him to take his armor and come, “and when Idomeneus came to his well-made hut” he arms (13.235–241; see [[arming-scene]]). Still near the hut he meets Meriones, and says that he himself has no desire “to sit in the huts, but to fight” (13.252–253). He has spears enough:

“standing in my hut against the gleaming walls, / Trojan spears, which I take from the men I kill” (13.261–262). ἐνώπια παμφανόωντα, “the gleaming walls”, are the walls against which the Hours lean the goddesses' chariot on Olympus (8.435) and Menelaus' attendants lean Telemachus' chariot at Sparta (Od. 4.42), and against which Odysseus leans his bow in his hall (Od. 22.121). The hut is a solid building (see the entry), and Idomeneus' is an armory: “So I have spears and bossed shields / and helmets and corselets shining brightly” (13.264–265; see [[stripping-the-dead]]). Meriones too has “many spoils of the Trojans” “beside my hut and my black ship”, but too far to fetch (13.267–268). After Idomeneus has spoken of the ambush, Meriones takes up “a bronze spear from the hut” and follows him (13.294–297; see [[ambush]]).

**Huts and ships.** The poem names the huts with the ships among which they stand. Hector threatened to go through to the sea “through the huts and ships of the Achaeans” (13.144), and the Trojans would have retreated “from the ships and the huts” to Ilios, had Polydamas not spoken (13.723–725; see [[ships]] and [[would-have-had-not]]).
