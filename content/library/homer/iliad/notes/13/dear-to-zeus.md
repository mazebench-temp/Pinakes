---
summary: Hector, “dear to Zeus,” does not know that his men are being cut down on the left of the ships.
---

“But Hector, dear to Zeus, had not heard, and did not know at all / that on the left of the ships his men were being cut down / by the Argives. And soon the glory would have been the Achaeans'; / so hard did the earth-holder, the shaker of the earth, / urge on the Argives” (13.674–678; [[hector]], [[earth-holder]], [[kydos]]). The epithet stands beside the god who is working against him. Zeus does mean to give the victory to the Trojans and to Hector (13.347–348; [[plan-of-zeus]]), but he has turned his eyes away from Troy (13.1–9), and on the left Poseidon helps the Achaeans unseen. Hector was “dear to Zeus” in Book 8 too, when he spoke to the Trojans at nightfall (8.493). See [[poseidon-rallies-the-achaeans]].
