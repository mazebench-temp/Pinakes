---
summary: Poseidon watches the battle “on the topmost peak of wooded Samos, the Thracian”; the book adds the genitive ὑληέσσης.
grc: [ὑληέσσης]
---

Poseidon “sat marveling at the war and the battle, high up on the topmost peak of wooded Samos, the Thracian; for from there all of Ida could be seen, and the city of Priam could be seen, and the ships of the Achaeans” (13.11–14; [[samothrace]], [[poseidon]], [[ida]]). The island is named by its woods and by Thrace, which keeps it apart from the Samos of Odysseus' kingdom (see the translation's note on 13.12–14). The wooded peak is a lookout, and the woods tremble under the god's feet as he strides down from it (13.17–19; [[rocky]]).
