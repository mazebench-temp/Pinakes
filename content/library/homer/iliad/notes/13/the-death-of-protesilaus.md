---
summary: Book 13 recalls the death of Protesilaus only through what he left: his ships, still on the shore under his name where Hector breaks in, and his men, whom his younger brother Podarces leads in their defense (13.681, 13.693–700).
refs: [13.681]
---

“Where the ships of Ajax and of Protesilaus were, / drawn up on the shore of the gray sea” (13.681–682): the leader who died “as he leapt from his ship, by far the first of the Achaeans” (2.702) is named by the ships he left on the beach, where the wall was built lowest and Hector broke in (13.679–684; see [[protesilaus]]). The Catalogue told how his men longed for him and how his brother Podarces marshaled them (2.703–709); here Podarces leads them, with Medon, “in front of the Phthians” (13.693; see [[podarces]] and [[phthians]]). The story returns when Hector seizes the stern of “the ship that had brought Protesilaus to Troy and did not take him home again” (15.704–706).
