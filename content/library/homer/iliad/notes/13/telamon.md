The great Ajax is “Ajax, the son of Telamon” (Αἴαντα … Τελαμώνιον υἱόν, 13.67) when the son of Oileus turns to him, “Telamonian Ajax” when he answers (13.76), when Idomeneus says that he would give way to no man (13.321) and when the son of Oileus stands beside him (Αἴαντος Τελαμωνίου, 13.702), and “the son of Telamon” whose companions take his shield when he tires (Τελαμωνιάδῃ, 13.709; see [[ajax]] and [[telamonian]]). The genitive and the dative are new forms in this book.

The other son has the title once: “And Teucer, son of Telamon, was the first to kill his man” (13.170), and “the son of Telamon” stabs Imbrius below the ear (υἱὸς Τελαμῶνος, 13.177; see [[teucer]] and [[imbrius]]). Teucer is the bastard son whom Telamon raised in his own house (8.283–284; see [[nothos]]), and Agamemnon called him “son of Telamon” when he cheered him on in Book 8 (Τελαμώνιε, 8.281). The father's name goes with whichever son is fighting. The common noun τελαμών, “strap”, of a shield or a sword belt, has the same forms, and this entry claims the name only with a capital (see [[shield]]).
