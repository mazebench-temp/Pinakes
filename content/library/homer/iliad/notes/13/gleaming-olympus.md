---
summary: Idomeneus goes out like the lightning that the son of Cronus brandishes “from gleaming Olympus,” a sign to mortals.
---

Idomeneus arms in his hut and goes back to the battle, “and went on his way like the lightning that the son of Cronus / takes in his hand and brandishes from gleaming Olympus, / showing a sign to mortals; and its flashes are plain for all to see; / so the bronze shone about his chest as he ran” (13.242–245; [[like-lightning]], [[idomeneus]]). The verse-end ἀπʼ αἰγλήεντος Ὀλύμπου is the one from which Thetis leapt into the sea in Book 1 (1.532). In the Odyssey Zeus thunders “from gleaming Olympus” as the sign that Odysseus asked for (Od. 20.97–104). Both times the bright mountain is the place from which Zeus sends a sign.
