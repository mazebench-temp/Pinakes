---
summary: Idomeneus and Meriones, and the Achaeans in front of the Locrians, fight “in their intricate armor”; the book adds δαιδαλέοισι.
grc: [δαιδαλέοισι]
---

When the Trojans see Idomeneus “like a flame in his courage, / himself and his attendant, in their intricate armor,” they all come at him (13.330–332; [[like-a-flame]], [[idomeneus]], [[meriones]]). On the left the Achaeans in front of the Locrians fight Hector: “So then the ones in front, in their intricate armor, / fought with the Trojans and with Hector armed in bronze, / and the others shot from behind, unseen” (13.719–721; [[locrians]], [[armed-in-bronze]]). The phrase σὺν ἔντεσι δαιδαλέοισι ends the verse both times; the second time it sets the armored men against the Locrian archers, who have no armor at all. Book 6 had the phrase with the movable ν, δαιδαλέοισιν, for the armor with which Achilles burned Eetion (6.418); the form without it is new to the entry.
