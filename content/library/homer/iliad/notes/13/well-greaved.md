---
summary: Poseidon, as Calchas, assures the Ajaxes that elsewhere “the well-greaved Achaeans will hold” the Trojans, and Antilochus drives Asius' horses to the well-greaved Achaeans.
---

In Poseidon's first speech the formula carries a promise: he does not fear the Trojans elsewhere, “the well-greaved Achaeans will hold them all,” only where Hector leads them (13.49–54; [[the-two-ajaxes]], [[frenzied]]). When Idomeneus has killed Asius and Antilochus has killed his charioteer, “as for the horses, Antilochus, son of great-hearted Nestor, drove them away from the Trojans to the well-greaved Achaeans” (13.400–401; [[antilochus]], [[asius]], [[great-hearted]]). The horses that Asius would not leave behind at the ditch (12.110–115) end as Achaean spoil. See [[achaeans]].
