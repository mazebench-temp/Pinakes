---
summary: Polydamas names the lyre and song among the gifts that a god gives to one man and not to another (13.731), the gifts that Hector once told Paris would be no help to him against Menelaus (3.54).
grc: [κίθαριν]
---

“For to one man a god has given the works of war, / to another dancing, to another the lyre and song” (13.730–731; see [[different-gifts]], [[dance]] and [[polydamas]]). κίθαρις, the lyre, is the instrument of Paris that Hector scorned in Book 3: “Your lyre would be no help to you then, nor the gifts of Aphrodite” (3.54; see [[paris]] and [[gifts-of-the-gods]]). In Polydamas' speech the lyre is a god's gift beside the works of war and a good mind, and the point of the list is that no man has all of them (13.729–734). In the Odyssey “the lyre and song” are what the suitors care for while they devour Odysseus' goods (κίθαρις καὶ ἀοιδή, Od. 1.159).
