**Meriones' spear.** Meriones' throw strikes Deiphobus' shield, but “long before / the long spear snapped at the socket” (13.161–162; [[meriones]], [[deiphobus]]), δολιχὸν δόρυ, in the verse that tells of Idomeneus' spear breaking on Hector's corselet in Book 17 (17.607). He goes back along the huts “to bring a long spear, which he had left behind in his hut” (13.168; [[hut]]).

**Killing and wounding.** Teucer stabs Imbrius “below the ear, with his long spear” (13.177; [[imbrius]]). When Idomeneus and Meriones reach the left, “the battle, destroyer of mortals, bristled with the spears, / long and flesh-cutting” (13.339–340; [[flesh-cutting]], [[bold-hearted]]), with μακρῇς, the adjective of the spears with which Menelaus and Paris were to fight for Helen (3.137, 3.254). Over Alcathous, Trojans and Achaeans rush at one another “with their long spears” (13.496–497), μακροῖσι ξυστοῖσι, the long pikes that lie in the ships in Book 15 (15.388). And Paris tells Hector that Deiphobus and Helenus “have gone away, both struck by long spears / in the hand” (13.781–783; [[helenus]]).

**Hector's spear.** Hector ends his answer to Ajax with a threat: “if you dare / to stand up to my long spear, which will tear your lily-white skin” (13.829–830; [[hector]], [[ajax]], [[lily-like]]).
