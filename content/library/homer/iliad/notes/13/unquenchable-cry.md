---
summary: “And the others fought on, and an unquenchable cry went up”: the same verse comes twice, each time as a man of the exchange between Meriones and Deiphobus leaves the battle.
---

The whole verse οἳ δʼ ἄλλοι μάρναντο, βοὴ δʼ ἄσβεστος ὀρώρει is used twice in the book, and each time it marks a man's leaving the fight. Meriones' spear breaks on Deiphobus' shield, and Meriones goes back to his hut for another (13.159–168): “And the others fought on, and an unquenchable cry went up” (13.169), and the narrative turns to Teucer. Later Meriones wounds Deiphobus in the arm, and Polites leads him to his horses, which carry him groaning to the city (13.528–539); the verse comes again (13.540), and Aeneas kills Aphareus. The din goes on while the two men whose exchange opened the fighting at the ships are out of it. See [[meriones]], [[deiphobus]] and [[good-at-the-war-cry]].
