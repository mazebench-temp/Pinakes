---
summary: Idomeneus, too slow on his feet to strip Oenomaus, “in close fighting … kept off the pitiless day,” in the half-verse that described Odysseus at bay in Book 11.
---

Idomeneus has killed Oenomaus but cannot take the armor, “For his legs were no longer steady when he rushed, to dart after his own spear or to avoid a missile. So in close fighting he kept off the pitiless day, but his feet no longer carried him nimbly to flee out of the war” (13.512–515; [[idomeneus]], [[oenomaus-the-trojan]]). ἀμύνετο νηλεὲς ἦμαρ, “kept off the pitiless day,” is the half-verse of Odysseus surrounded by Trojans like a stag by jackals (11.484). Here it measures age: the man who is “half gray” (13.361; [[half-gray]]) can still stand and fight, but no longer run, and he goes back step by step (13.516). See [[old-age-and-memory]].
