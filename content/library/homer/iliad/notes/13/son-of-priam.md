---
summary: The patronymic names three brothers: Hector four times, as the Trojans follow him and the Ajaxes long to meet him, Deiphobus as he advances behind his shield, and Helenus as he shoots at Menelaus.
---

**Hector.** The Trojans, like a flame or a storm wind, “followed Hector, son of Priam” (13.39–40), and Telamonian Ajax longs “even alone to fight Hector, son of Priam, raging without end” (13.79–80): the two verses begin alike, Ἕκτορι Πριαμίδῃ ἄμοτον μεμαῶτ-, with the raging said once of the Trojans and once of Hector ([[raging-without-end]]). Idomeneus tells Meriones that the two Ajaxes and Teucer will give “Hector, son of Priam” his fill of war (13.315–316), and at the end of the book Hector leads the Trojans as “the equal of Ares, ruin of mortals, the son of Priam” (13.802–803; [[hector]], [[ruin-of-mortals]]).

**Deiphobus and Helenus.** Deiphobus strides among the Trojans “in high spirits, the son of Priam” (13.156–157; [[deiphobus]], [[balanced-on-every-side]]), and Helenus is “the son of Priam” when his arrow strikes Menelaus' corselet (13.586; [[helenus]]). Both brothers leave the battle wounded in the hand (13.781–783), and Hector, looking for them, finds Paris, a fourth son of Priam (13.765–769; [[paris]]). See [[sons-of-priam]] and [[priam]].
