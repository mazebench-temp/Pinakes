summary: The Paphlagonians tend Harpalion, the son of their king Pylaemenes, when Meriones' arrow kills him, and carry him to Ilios in a chariot, grieving, with his father behind; Paris, who had been Harpalion's guest-friend “among the many Paphlagonians”, avenges him (13.656–661).
grc: [Παφλαγόνες, Παφλαγόνεσσι]

τὸν μὲν Παφλαγόνες μεγαλήτορες ἀμφεπένοντο, “the great-hearted Paphlagonians tended him” (13.656; see [[great-hearted]]): they lift him into a chariot and take him to sacred Ilios, grieving (13.657–658; see [[harpalion]]). The father who goes with them is their king, Pylaemenes, killed by Menelaus in Book 5 (see [[pylaemenes]] and [[pylaemenes-dead-and-alive]]). Paris had been a guest among them, πολέσιν μετὰ Παφλαγόνεσσι, “among the many Paphlagonians” (13.661), when he made Harpalion his guest-friend; the poem does not say when or how (see [[paris]] and [[xenia]]).
