---
summary: Olympus is named three times in Book 13: as the home of “the gods who hold Olympus”, as the height from which the son of Cronus brandishes his lightning, and as the peak where Ares sits beneath golden clouds with the other gods, shut out from the war.
passages:
  - 13.521-525 | Ares on Olympus
---

In Book 13 the gods are apart, each in his own place. Zeus sits on Ida, where he came down from heaven earlier that day and where Hera will see him (11.183–184, 14.157–158; see [[zeus]] and [[ida]]); Poseidon watches from the peak of Samothrace and then goes among the Achaeans (13.10–38; see [[poseidon]] and [[samothrace]]); the other gods stay on Olympus.

The son of Oileus says that “one of the gods who hold Olympus” has spoken to him in Calchas' likeness (13.68; see [[olympians]]). Idomeneus goes out like the lightning that the son of Cronus “takes in his hand and brandishes from gleaming Olympus” (13.243; see [[like-lightning]] and [[gleaming-olympus]]), the epithet of the mountain when Thetis leaps from it into the sea (1.532). And when Ascalaphus falls, his father Ares sits “on the peak of Olympus beneath golden clouds, / held back by the counsels of Zeus” (13.523–524; see [[ares]]). The golden clouds are found nowhere else. Thetis found Zeus sitting apart “on the topmost peak of many-ridged Olympus”, and there he called the gods to assembly on the second day of battle (1.498–499, 8.2–3; see [[many-ridged-olympus]]).
