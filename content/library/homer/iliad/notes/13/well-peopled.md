summary: Idomeneus offers the dead Othryoneus a bride if he will help sack “the well-peopled citadel of Ilios,” and Ajax tells Hector that his “well-peopled city” will be taken before the ships; the book adds the nominative εὖ ναιομένη πόλις.
grc: [εὖ ναιομένη πόλις]
---

Both uses are taunts, and both look forward to the sack of Troy. Idomeneus, over the dead Othryoneus: “we would give you the most beautiful in form of the daughters of the son of Atreus, bringing her from Argos, to marry her, if with us you would sack the well-peopled citadel of Ilios” (13.378–380; [[othryoneus]], [[son-of-atreus]]). Ajax answers Hector's hope to sack the ships with the city's own sack: “Much sooner would your well-peopled city be taken and sacked beneath our hands” (13.815–816; [[ajax]], [[hector]], [[troy]]). The formula praises a city as it is threatened, as Achilles praised the towns of the Trojans that the Achaeans sacked (1.163–164). See [[polis]].
