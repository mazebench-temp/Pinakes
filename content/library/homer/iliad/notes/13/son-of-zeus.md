---
summary: Hector “claims to be the son of mighty Zeus,” in Poseidon's words, and at the end of the book wishes he were; between the two, Idomeneus calls himself “offspring of Zeus” by a descent of three generations.
grc: [Διὸς πάϊς, Ζηνὸς γόνος]
refs: ["13.54"]
---

**Hector's claim.** Poseidon, in Calchas' likeness, warns the Ajaxes of the place “where that frenzied man leads them, like a flame, Hector, who claims to be the son of mighty Zeus” (13.53–54; [[hector]], [[frenzied]], [[eristhenes]]). The words Διὸς … πάϊς are split by the god's epithet. The claim is the god's description of him; Hector's own boast at the wall was that he trusted the promises of Zeus (12.235–243; [[one-omen-is-best]]).

**Idomeneus' descent.** Idomeneus taunts Deiphobus: “Strange one, come and stand against me yourself, so that you may see what kind of offspring of Zeus I am, who have come here” (13.448–449; [[idomeneus]], [[strange-one]]). Ζηνὸς γόνος here means a descendant, and he spells it out: Zeus fathered Minos, Minos Deucalion, and Deucalion Idomeneus (13.450–453; [[minos]], [[deucalion]], [[genealogy-in-battle]]).

**Hector's wish.** Answering Ajax, Hector wishes: “If only I were the son of aegis-bearing Zeus all my days, and queen Hera had borne me, and honored as Athena and Apollo are honored” (13.825–827; [[aegis-bearing]], [[queenly]]). Διὸς πάϊς αἰγιόχοιο turns Poseidon's charge into a wish, and the wish is impossible: it measures his certainty that the day will bring evil to the Argives (13.828; see the translation's note on 13.825–828).
