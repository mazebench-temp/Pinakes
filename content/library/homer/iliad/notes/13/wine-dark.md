---
summary: The two Ajaxes stand side by side like “two wine-dark oxen” pulling the plow; the book adds the dual οἴνοπε, the only use of the epithet for anything but the sea in the Iliad.
grc: [οἴνοπε]
---

The son of Oileus never stands apart from Telamonian Ajax, “but as in a fallow field two wine-dark oxen pull the jointed plow with equal spirit, and around them, at the roots of their horns, much sweat bubbles up” (13.703–705; [[oxen-at-the-plow]], [[the-two-ajaxes]], [[jointed]], [[cattle]]). Everywhere else in the Iliad οἶνοψ is the color of the sea. Here it is the dark gloss of a pair of oxen, and the Odyssey has nearly the same verse in its simile of the plowman who longs for his supper as Odysseus longs for the sunset (Od. 13.31–35).
