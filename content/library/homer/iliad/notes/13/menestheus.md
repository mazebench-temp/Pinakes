**The body of Amphimachus.** When Hector has killed Amphimachus and Ajax has driven him back from the dead, “Amphimachus, then, Stichius and brilliant Menestheus, / the leaders of the Athenians, carried back to the army of the Achaeans” (13.195–196; see [[amphimachus]], [[stichius]] and [[brilliant]]). The Athenian leaders bring in the dead Epeian while the two Ajaxes carry off the Trojan Imbrius (13.197–202; see [[the-two-ajaxes]]).

**The picked men.** Where Hector broke in and the wall was lowest, the poet names the leaders of the peoples who hold him off, and the Athenians come first:

{{quote:5dda8018-71dc-5b5d-9c20-0d89b8bcf3ec}}

υἱὸς Πετεῶο Μενεσθεύς, “Menestheus, son of Peteos” (13.690), is the half-verse with which the Catalogue introduced him (2.552; see [[peteos]]). The Catalogue praised him as the best of men at marshaling horses and shield-bearing men (2.553–555); here he leads men chosen out, προλελεγμένοι (13.689), with three followers: Stichius, who carried Amphimachus with him, and Pheidas and Bias, whom the poem names only here (see [[pheidas]], [[bias-the-athenian]] and [[athenians]]). The Ionians “with trailing tunics”, named in the list of peoples just before, may be his Athenians under another name (13.685; see [[ionians]]). He does nothing more in the book. In Book 15 Hector kills Stichius, “the trusted companion of great-hearted Menestheus” (15.329–331).
