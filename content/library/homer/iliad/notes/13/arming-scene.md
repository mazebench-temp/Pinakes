**Poseidon's gold.** Before he drives over the sea, Poseidon yokes his horses, “and he himself put on gold about his body, and took the whip” (13.25), the verse in which Zeus made ready for his drive to Ida (8.43; see [[chariot-journey]] and [[poseidon]]). A god's arming for a journey is gold, not armor.

**Idomeneus.** Poseidon, in Thoas' likeness, tells Idomeneus to take his armor and come with him (13.235). The arming takes one verse:

“and when Idomeneus came to his well-made hut, / he put on his fine armor about his body, and took two spears, / and went on his way like the lightning that the son of Cronus / takes in his hand and brandishes from gleaming Olympus” (13.240–243; see [[idomeneus]], [[hut]] and [[like-lightning]]). So Ajax armed in a verse and a half in Book 7 and went out “as huge Ares goes forth” (7.206–210): the short arming leads straight into a simile of a god. “So the bronze shone about his chest as he ran” (13.245; see [[bronze]]). Two spears are what Paris, Agamemnon and Patroclus take up when they arm (3.18, 11.43, 16.139; see [[spear]]). Meriones, who has only broken his spear, takes a new one from the hut and follows (13.294–297).

**Ares and Rout.** In the simile that sends Idomeneus and Meriones into the battle, Ares and his son Rout “arm themselves to go out from Thrace” (θωρήσσεσθον, a dual) against the Ephyri or the great-hearted Phlegyes (13.298–303; see [[ares-and-rout]] and [[rout]]). The two Cretans then “went into the war, armored in gleaming bronze” (13.305).
