summary: Book 13 states the limit of the plan. Zeus wills victory to the Trojans and Hector to give glory to Achilles and Thetis, but not the destruction of the Achaean army; while he looks away, Poseidon holds the Achaeans up, and the battle nearly turns against the plan.

**The limit.** Zeus has brought Hector to the ships, as he promised him through Iris (11.191–194; 13.1), and he looks away (13.1–9; see [[zeus]]). Then the narrator sets out the plan, and its limit, beside Poseidon's secret help:

{{quote:a4f934a6-484e-5c1b-ad67-7f2f322a8f1d}}

The verses answer Thetis' prayer, “give strength to the Trojans until the Achaeans / pay honor to my son” (1.509–510; see [[thetis-supplicates-zeus]]). The Trojans have the victory; κυδαίνω, “give glory”, is said of Achilles and of Thetis herself (13.348, 13.350; see [[kydos]]). In Book 2 the narrator gave the plan as honor for Achilles and death for many beside the ships (2.3–4). Here he adds what Zeus does not want, the death of the whole army, as on the second day Zeus nodded to Agamemnon “that his people would be saved and not perish” (8.246). Idomeneus has just said the opposite, that it must please Zeus that the Achaeans perish “nameless, far from Argos” (13.226–227).

**Slowed, not undone.** Poseidon does not defy the elder brother openly. He helps in secret, and the two stretch over both armies a rope of strife and war that no one can break or loose (13.351–360; see [[poseidon-rallies-the-achaeans]] and [[the-rope-of-war]]). Twice near the end of the book the plan almost fails: “the glory would have been the Achaeans'”, but Hector holds on where he first broke in (13.676–684), and the Trojans would have retreated wretchedly to Ilios, “had not Polydamas come and stood beside bold Hector” (13.723–725; see [[would-have-had-not]]). Polydamas fears that the Achaeans may “pay back yesterday's / debt”, the defeat of Book 8, since “a man insatiable of war” waits by the ships (13.745–747; see [[polydamas]]). He cannot know the term. Zeus told Hera that Hector would not cease from war until Achilles was roused beside the ships, over Patroclus dead (8.473–476), and Achilles told the embassy that he would not fight until Hector set fire to them (9.650–655).

**Resumed.** Hera puts Zeus to sleep, and Poseidon leads the Achaeans openly (14.352–401). When Zeus wakes, he tells Hera the plan's whole course, from the death of Patroclus to the fall of Ilios, and sends Iris to take Poseidon out of the war and Apollo to restore Hector (15.49–77; see [[iris]] and [[apollo]]). He waits to see the glare of a burning ship, and then he will turn the rout back from the ships (15.596–602). Meanwhile he gives Hector glory, “for he was to be short-lived” (15.612–613), with the verb that in Book 13 gives glory to Achilles.
