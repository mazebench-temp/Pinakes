---
summary: As a noun, δήϊοι are “the enemy” twice in Book 13: Asius' charioteer does not dare to turn his horses away from their hands, and Antilochus is never away from them.
---

Idomeneus kills Asius in front of his chariot, and the charioteer, struck out of his wits, “did not dare to escape from the hands of the enemy / by turning the horses back” (13.394–396; [[asius]]). Antilochus kills him and drives off the horses (13.396–401; [[antilochus]]). Later Antilochus fights on in the throng under Poseidon's guard: “For he was never away from the enemy, but among them / he kept turning” (13.554–557; [[poseidon]]). Both uses are the genitive plural δηΐων, as in Nestor's hope that a spy might catch a straggler “of the enemy” (10.206). In this book the fire the Trojans threaten is not “consuming” but “deadly” (13.629; [[deadly]]).
