---
summary: “Two trusty companions,” Mecisteus and Alastor, carry the stricken Hypsenor to the ships, in the verses that carried the wounded Teucer in Book 8.
---

Antilochus “ran and stood over him, and covered him with his shield. Then two trusty companions stooped under him, Mecisteus, son of Echius, and brilliant Alastor, and carried him, groaning heavily, to the hollow ships” (13.420–423; [[antilochus]], [[hypsenor-son-of-hippasus]], [[mecisteus-son-of-echius]], [[alastor-who-carries-teucer]], [[groaning-heavily]]). The four verses are those in which Ajax covered his fallen brother Teucer with his shield and the same two companions carried him off (8.331–334; [[teucer]]). There the groaning suits a wounded man; here it follows a verse of death (13.412), and whether Hypsenor is dying or dead is not settled (see the translation's note on 13.412–423). See [[hetairos]].
