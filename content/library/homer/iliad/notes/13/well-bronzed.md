---
summary: Peisander fights Menelaus with “a fine axe of good bronze”; the book adds the accusative εὔχαλκον.
grc: [εὔχαλκον]
en: [of good bronze]
---

Menelaus leaps at Peisander with his silver-studded sword, “but he, from under his shield, took a fine axe of good bronze, on a handle of olive wood, long and well polished” (13.611–613; [[peisander-killed-by-menelaus]], [[axe]], [[polished]], [[silver-studded]]). ἀξίνην εὔχαλκον gives the axe-head the epithet that in Book 7 was given to the rim of Eioneus' helmet (7.12). The blow of the axe falls on the ridge of Menelaus' helmet, under the crest, and does not kill; Menelaus strikes Peisander above the nose and kills him (13.614–618; [[menelaus]]). See [[bronze]].
