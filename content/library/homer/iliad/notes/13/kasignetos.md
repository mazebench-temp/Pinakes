---
summary: Brothers stand by brothers in Book 13. Polites, “his own brother”, leads the wounded Deiphobus out of the battle; Medon is “the brother of Ajax”, an exile for killing a kinsman of his stepmother; and Paris, rebuked, persuades “his brother's mind”.
grc: [γνωτὸν]
en: [kinsman]
---

**His own brother.** When Meriones has wounded Deiphobus in the arm, “Polites, / his own brother, put his arms around his waist / and led him out of grim-sounding war, until he came to his horses” (αὐτοκασίγνητος, 13.533–535; see [[polites]] and [[deiphobus]]). αὐτοκασίγνητος, “own brother”, stresses the shared blood (see the article above).

**The brother of Ajax.** Medon is “the bastard son of divine Oileus, / … the brother of Ajax” (Αἴαντος ἀδελφεός, 13.694–695; see [[medon]], [[nothos]] and [[ajax-son-of-oileus]]), born to Oileus by Rhene (2.727–728), and he lives in exile because he killed “a kinsman of his stepmother Eriopis” (γνωτὸν, 13.696–697; see [[in-laws]] and [[exile-and-refuge]]). γνωτός, “known, related”, is the kin one is born to, beside the kin one marries into; the two meet in this killing.

**His brother's mind.** After Paris has answered Hector's rebuke, “So saying, the hero persuaded his brother's mind” (ἀδελφειοῦ φρένας, 13.788; see [[paris]], [[hector]] and [[peitho]]). It is the verse with which Agamemnon persuaded Menelaus not to meet Hector in Book 7 (7.120); there one brother held the other back from the fight, and here the two go into it together (13.789).
