summary: In the simile of Idomeneus waiting for Aeneas, the boar at bay bristles along its back, “and its two eyes blaze with fire” as it whets its tusks (13.471–475).

en: [blaze with fire]
---

ὀφθαλμὼ δʼ ἄρα οἱ πυρὶ λάμπετον: “and its two eyes blaze with fire” (13.474). The eyes are a beast's this time, the boar in the mountains that waits for the din of the hunters, and the verb is a dual, λάμπετον, for the pair of eyes (see [[the-bristling-boar]] and [[boar]]). In Books 1 and 12 the fire was in the eyes of men, Agamemnon's anger and Hector's onset (1.104, 12.466); here it is the fury of the animal at bay, which Idomeneus is said to match. The boar of Parnassus, which wounded Odysseus in his youth, came out of its lair against the hunters “with fire flashing from its eyes” (πῦρ δʼ ὀφθαλμοῖσι δεδορκώς, *Odyssey* 19.446). The Hesiodic *Shield of Heracles* gives its boar the words of Agamemnon's eyes in Book 1, ὄσσε δέ οἱ πυρὶ λαμπετόωντι ἔικτον, “its two eyes were like blazing fire” (*Shield* 390; compare 1.104).
