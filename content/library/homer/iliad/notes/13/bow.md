summary: Teucer is “the best of the Achaeans at archery, and good also in close battle”; Helenus draws his bow against Menelaus and is struck through the hand that holds it; Meriones and Paris each kill a man with an arrow; and the Locrians, armed only with bows and slings, shoot from behind the line (13.313–314, 13.583–600, 13.650–672, 13.712–722).

**Teucer.** Idomeneus names the defenders of the middle of the line, the two Ajaxes and Teucer, “who is the best of the Achaeans / at archery, and good also in close battle” (13.313–314; see [[teucer]]). τοξοσύνη, “archery”, occurs only here. The praise joins what Diomedes set apart when he mocked Paris as a mere “Archer” (11.385), and in this book Teucer fights with the spear: he kills Imbrius, and Hector's spear narrowly misses him as he rushes to strip the body (13.170–184; see [[imbrius]]).

**Helenus' bow.** Helenus, Priam's son and “by far the best of the bird-seers” (6.76), fights with the bow. As Menelaus comes at him brandishing his spear, “the other drew back his bow by the grip” (13.583), in the words with which Paris drew his against Diomedes (11.375). The two let fly together. The arrow glances from Menelaus' corselet, and Menelaus' spear goes through the hand that holds “the polished bow” and into the bow itself (13.584–597; see [[helenus]], [[corselet]] and [[polished]]). Agenor draws the spear out and binds the hand with a sling (13.598–600; see [[sling]]).

**Arrows that kill.** Meriones shoots Harpalion as he draws back, “a bronze-fitted arrow” that strikes him in the right buttock and goes under the bone into the bladder (13.650–652; see [[harpalion]] and [[meriones]]). Meriones is an archer elsewhere too: he gives Odysseus a bow and a quiver for the night raid (10.260) and wins the archery contest at Patroclus' games, where Teucer is second (23.859–883). Paris, angry for his guest-friend Harpalion, shoots Euchenor under the jaw and the ear (13.660–672; see [[paris]] and [[euchenor]]). See [[arrows]].

**The Locrians.** The Locrians followed the son of Oileus to Troy “trusting in bows and in well-twisted sheep's wool”, and they shoot from behind the men in armor, unseen, until the Trojans are thrown into confusion (13.712–722; see [[locrians]] and [[sling]]). The bow is here the weapon of a whole people, as it is of Philoctetes' men in the Catalogue, “well skilled with the bow” (2.718–720).
