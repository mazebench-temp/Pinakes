---
summary: Menelaus complains that Zeus, who is said to surpass all others in wisdom, “favors” the arrogant Trojans.
grc: [χαρίζεαι]
---

Standing over Peisander, Menelaus addresses Zeus: “Father Zeus, they say that you surpass in wisdom all others, / men and gods; and all these things come from you. / How you favor men who are arrogant, / the Trojans” (οἷον δὴ ἄνδρεσσι χαρίζεαι ὑβριστῇσι, 13.631–634; see [[hybris]] and [[who-is-to-blame]]). χαρίζομαι is to do someone a χάρις, a favor that pleases, and favor creates a debt of gratitude (see the article above and [[reciprocity-with-the-gods]]). The complaint is that Zeus gives his favor where it is least deserved. Zeus' purpose in this book is told a few hundred lines earlier: he wished for victory for the Trojans and Hector “to give glory to Achilles” (13.347–348; see [[plan-of-zeus]] and [[kydos]]). The favor Menelaus sees is the means by which Zeus repays Thetis, who reminded him of the service she once did him (1.503–510; see [[thetis-supplicates-zeus]]).
