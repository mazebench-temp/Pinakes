---
summary: Flight does not seize Idomeneus “as if he were a darling child”; he stands his ground against Aeneas like a boar in the mountains.
---

Deiphobus has brought Aeneas to defend the body of Alcathous, and Aeneas comes on: “But flight did not seize Idomeneus as if he were a darling child, / but he stood his ground like a boar in the mountains, trusting in its courage” (13.470–471; [[idomeneus]], [[the-bristling-boar]]). The τηλύγετος is the cherished child of a house. Idomeneus, whom the poem has just called “half gray” (13.361; [[half-gray]]), is no such child: he stands his ground and calls his companions. He is afraid of Aeneas, who “has the flower of youth, which is the greatest power” (13.481–484; [[aeneas]]). Elsewhere in the Iliad the word names children whom a father loves: Orestes, “reared as my darling son” in Agamemnon's offer (9.143), and the only son of Phoenix's comparison (9.482).
