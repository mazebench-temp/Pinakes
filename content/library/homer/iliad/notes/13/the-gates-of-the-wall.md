summary: The broken gate is remembered. Poseidon tells the Achaeans that Hector “has broken the gates and the long bar” (13.124), and Hector holds his ground where he “had first leapt in at the gates and the wall”, by the ships of Ajax and Protesilaus (13.679–681).

Poseidon, in the likeness of Calchas, ends his rebuke of the men at the rear with the breach: “Hector, good at the war cry, is fighting beside the ships / in his strength, and he has broken the gates and the long bar” (13.123–124; see [[poseidon-rallies-the-achaeans]]). The second verse takes up the narrator's words before the breach, that the Trojans would not then “have broken the gates of the wall and the long bar” but for Sarpedon (12.291). The gates have no part in the fighting of Book 13, for the Trojans are inside. They mark a place: Hector holds on “where he had first leapt in at the gates and the wall” (13.679), with the verb of his leap through the gate, ἐσᾶλτο (12.466). There the ships of Ajax and Protesilaus were drawn up, and the wall had been built lowest (13.681–684; see [[the-achaean-wall]] and [[the-battle-at-the-ships]]). Book 12 placed the gate that Asius attacked “toward the left of the ships” (12.118) but did not say where the gate that Hector broke stood; here the poem places the breach beside these ships.
