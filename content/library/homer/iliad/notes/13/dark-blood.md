---
summary: The dark blood flows from Harpalion, shot by Meriones, as he lies “like a worm on the ground.”
---

Meriones' arrow strikes Harpalion as he draws back, and passes through to the bladder. He sits down in the arms of his companions, “breathing out his life, like a worm on the ground / he lay stretched out; and the dark blood flowed and soaked the earth” (13.650–655; [[harpalion]], [[like-a-worm]], [[meriones]]). The formula ἐκ δʼ αἷμα μέλαν ῥέε stands at the end of the death. It is the only dark blood of the book: when Meriones wounds Deiphobus, “the blood ran down from his freshly wounded arm” (13.539), with no color named. The Paphlagonians take Harpalion back to Ilios, and his father follows in tears (13.656–659; [[pylaemenes]], [[pylaemenes-dead-and-alive]]).
