---
summary: The weary Achaeans in the rear refresh their hearts “beside the swift ships,” and Idomeneus says that only Zeus himself could cast a firebrand on them.
---

The weary men in the rear, whom Poseidon rouses after the Ajaxes, “were refreshing their hearts beside the swift ships”; their limbs were loosened by weariness, and they wept as they watched the Trojans come over the wall (13.83–88; [[poseidon]], [[achaeans]], [[tears]]). Idomeneus is sure that Hector will not easily reach the ships against the Ajaxes and Teucer, “unless the son of Cronus himself casts a blazing firebrand on the swift ships” (13.319–320; [[son-of-cronus]], [[blazing-fire]]). The fire that Hector promised in Book 8 (8.180–182) and called for at the wall (12.440–441) is here a thing only Zeus could bring. In Book 15 Zeus does wait to see a ship burning, and Hector lays hold of the stern of Protesilaus' ship and calls for fire (15.596–600, 15.704–718). See [[ships]].
