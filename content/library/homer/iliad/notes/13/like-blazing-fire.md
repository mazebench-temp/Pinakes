ὣς οἳ μὲν μάρναντο δέμας πυρὸς αἰθομένοιο: “So they fought on like blazing fire” (13.673). The verse is the one that closed the fight around Ajax and Eurypylus in Book 11 (11.596), and it does the same work here. Its μέν looks to a δέ, and the δέ is Hector: Ἕκτωρ δʼ οὐκ ἐπέπυστο Διῒ φίλος, οὐδέ τι ᾔδη, “but Hector, dear to Zeus, had not heard, and did not know at all that on the left of the ships his men were being cut down by the Argives” (13.674–676). The fire is left burning on the left wing, where Menelaus has killed Peisander, Meriones Harpalion and Paris Euchenor (13.601–672), and the narrative goes to the center, where Hector holds on at the gates he broke (13.679–684; see [[hector]]).

The narrator adds what the fire would have done: “And soon the glory would have been the Achaeans'; so hard did the earth-holder, the shaker of the earth, urge on the Argives” (13.676–678; see [[poseidon-rallies-the-achaeans]] and [[kydos]]). The book is full of fire. The Trojans follow Hector “like a flame” (13.39), and he and Idomeneus are “like a flame” three times more (13.53, 13.330, 13.688; see [[like-a-flame]]); Menelaus says the Trojans are eager “to throw deadly fire” on the ships (13.628–629), and Polydamas tells Hector that “the crown of war is blazing” all around him (13.736).
