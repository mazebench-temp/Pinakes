---
summary: Idomeneus tells Meriones that any missile would strike him in front, as he pressed forward “to the intimate talk of the champions”, the first time the poem gives the word for lovers' talk to battle.
grc: [ὀαριστύν]
en: [intimate talk]
passages:
  - 13.275-291 | The brave man in ambush
---

Idomeneus ends his praise of Meriones' courage with his wounds: if he were hit as he toiled, the missile would not fall on the back of his neck or his back, “but it would meet your chest or your belly / as you pressed forward to the intimate talk of the champions” (πρόσσω ἱεμένοιο μετὰ προμάχων ὀαριστύν, 13.290–291; see [[the-brave-man-in-ambush]] and [[meriones]]). ὀαριστύς is the talk of a man and a woman (see the article above), and here it is the close exchange of blows among the πρόμαχοι, the men who fight in front (see the translation's note on 13.291). The word for the talk of lovers is given to the closest meeting of enemies, face to face, where the wounds are in front. Hector will say the same to his allies in Book 17, telling them to face the enemy and live or die, “for this is the intimate talk of war” (πολέμου ὀαριστύς, 17.228), and in Book 22, waiting for Achilles, he knows that there can be no talk between them like the talk of a maiden and a youth (22.126–128).
