---
summary: Atreus is named in Book 13 only in his sons' patronymic: twice of Agamemnon, whom Poseidon allows may be to blame and whose daughters Idomeneus offers in mockery, and five times of Menelaus, in his fights with Helenus, Peisander and Harpalion (13.112, 13.378, 13.581–646).
---

The son of Atreus is Agamemnon when Poseidon, in Calchas' likeness, grants that “the hero, son of Atreus, wide-ruling Agamemnon” may be wholly to blame (13.111–112), and when Idomeneus offers Othryoneus “the most beautiful in form of the daughters of the son of Atreus” (13.378; see [[agamemnon]]). Everywhere else in the book he is Menelaus. Grief seizes him for Deipyrus, his spear pins Helenus' hand to the bow, his spear misses Peisander and his sword kills him, and Harpalion stabs his shield (13.581, 13.593, 13.605, 13.610, 13.646; see [[menelaus]] and [[son-of-atreus]]). Agamemnon is absent, wounded, and the patronymic belongs mostly to his brother.
