---
summary: Apollo takes no part in Book 13 and is named once, in Hector's wish to be “honored as Athena and Apollo are honored” (13.827 = 8.540).
---

Answering Ajax's challenge at the end of the book, Hector wishes that he were the son of Zeus and Hera, “and honored as Athena and Apollo are honored” (13.825–828; see [[hector]], [[athena]] and [[hera]]). The verse is the one he spoke at the end of the second day of battle (8.540), when he wished to be immortal and ageless (see [[immortal-and-ageless]]).

The Trojans' god is absent from the battle at the ships while Poseidon helps the Achaeans in secret (see [[poseidon-rallies-the-achaeans]]). When Zeus wakes and orders Poseidon out of the war, he sends Apollo to Hector with the aegis to rouse him and to put the Achaeans to flight (15.220–235).
