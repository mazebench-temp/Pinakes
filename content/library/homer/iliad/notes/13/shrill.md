---
summary: The winnowing simile has “the shrill wind” that drives the chaff, and the simile of the dust storm its “shrill winds,” with λιγύς, the simple adjective, in a phrase the book adds.
grc: [λιγέων ἀνέμων]
---

Two similes of wind. When the armies meet, it is “As when storms rush on before shrill winds on a day when the dust lies thickest on the roads” (13.334–335; [[the-dust-storm]]): λιγέων ἀνέμων is the genitive plural of λιγύς, the simple adjective from which λιγυρός is formed, and the phrase returns of winds in later books (14.17, 15.620). When Helenus' arrow glances from Menelaus' corselet, it flies like beans or chickpeas from the winnowing shovel “under the shrill wind and the swing of the winnower” (13.588–590; [[beans-on-the-threshing-floor]], [[helenus]], [[menelaus]]): πνοιῇ ὕπο λιγυρῇ is the half-line of the winds that scatter the clouds in Book 5 (5.526). The shrill sound is the wind's in both, a storm in the one and the winnower's breeze in the other.
