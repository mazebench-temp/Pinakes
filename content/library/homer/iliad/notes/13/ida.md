---
summary: Ida is the first thing Poseidon sees from the peak of Samothrace: “for from there all of Ida could be seen, / and the city of Priam could be seen, and the ships of the Achaeans” (13.13–14).
---

Zeus has sat on the peaks of Ida since he came down to it with the lightning in his hands (11.182–184), and Book 13 opens with him turning his shining eyes away from Troy to the far peoples of the north (13.1–9; see [[zeus]]). Poseidon watches from the opposite height, across the sea, “high up on the topmost peak of wooded Samos, / the Thracian” (13.12–13; see [[samothrace]]). The same words, ἐπʼ ἀκροτάτης κορυφῆς, place Zeus “on the topmost peak” of Ida when Hera sees him there from Olympus in the next book (14.153–158). The two brothers, “with their minds set on different sides” (13.345), watch one battle from two mountains, and the view from Samothrace takes in mountain, city and ships in turn (13.13–14; see [[troy]] and [[watching-the-war]]).
