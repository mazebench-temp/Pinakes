grc: ["θεὸς πολεμήϊα ἔργα"]
en: [the works of war]
passages:
  - 13.723-753 | Polydamas' counsel at the ships
---

The phrase with which Zeus sent Aphrodite out of the battle, “Not to you, my child, are given the works of war” (5.428), becomes in Polydamas' mouth the description of one man's gift. Hector will not heed advice, “Because a god has given you beyond others the works of war, / you want also in counsel to know more than others” (οὕνεκά τοι περὶ δῶκε θεὸς πολεμήϊα ἔργα, 13.727–728; see [[hector]] and [[polydamas]]). Two verses later the same words open his list of the gods' gifts: “For to one man a god has given the works of war, / to another dancing, to another the lyre and song”, and to another a good mind (13.730–734; see [[different-gifts]]).

In Books 2, 5, 7 and 11 the works of war were what women, children and young men do not know (2.337–338, 5.428, 7.235–236, 11.718–719; see the notes on Books 7 and 11). Here the knowledge is granted to Hector in full, and it is the thing that limits him: a man to whom war is given is not thereby given counsel. The verb is the one Zeus used, δέδοται and δῶκε, a god's allotment (see [[gifts-of-the-gods]]).
