summary: The gold of Book 13 belongs to the gods: Poseidon's house at Aegae, his horses' golden manes, the gold he puts on, his golden whip and golden hobbles, and the golden clouds under which Ares sits on Olympus. Among men the book's one precious metal is the silver of Menelaus' sword.
grc: [χρύσεα, χρυσείας]
passages:
  - 13.17-38 | Poseidon crosses the sea
---

**The god's gold.** Poseidon's house at Aegae, in the depths of the sea, is “golden, gleaming, imperishable forever” (13.21–22; see [[aegae]]). There he yokes his horses, “with flowing manes of gold”, puts gold about his body, and takes “the whip, golden and well made” (13.23–26), in the verses with which Zeus set out for Ida (8.41–44; see [[chariot-journey]] and the note on Book 8). In the cave between Tenedos and Imbros he casts “golden hobbles” around the horses' feet, “unbreakable, not to be loosed” (13.36–37; see [[poseidon]]). On Olympus Ares sits “beneath golden clouds” with the other gods, shut out from the war (13.523; see [[ares]]). The golden clouds of Olympus are found only here; in the next book Zeus covers himself and Hera with a golden cloud on Ida (14.343–351).

**Men's silver.** On the plain there is no gold. Menelaus draws his “silver-studded sword” against Peisander (13.610), in the verse with which he drew it against Paris (3.361; see [[silver-studded]] and [[sword]]).
