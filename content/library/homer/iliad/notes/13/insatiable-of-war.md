---
summary: Polydamas warns Hector that “a man insatiable of war” waits by the ships, giving Ares' epithet to Achilles without naming him.
---

Polydamas ends his counsel with a fear: “For I / fear that the Achaeans may pay back yesterday's / debt, since beside the ships there waits a man insatiable of war, / who I think will no longer hold back altogether from battle” (13.744–747; [[polydamas]], [[achilles]]). The line-end formula ἆτος πολέμοιο is Ares' (5.388, 5.863; [[ares]]), and only here does it describe a man. Polydamas does not name him; the war god's epithet is enough. Hector takes the counsel (13.748–753). Menelaus says the like of the whole Trojan army with another word, “the Trojans are insatiable of battle” (μάχης ἀκόρητοι, 13.639; [[insatiable]]).
