---
summary: Euchenor boards his ship “knowing well his deadly fate,” and Menelaus says the Trojans are eager “to throw deadly fire” on the ships; the book adds the neuter ὀλοόν.
grc: [ὀλοὸν]
---

Euchenor's father, the seer Polyidus, had often told him that he would die of a grievous sickness at home or be killed by the Trojans among the Achaean ships, and he went on board for Troy “knowing well his deadly fate” (13.663–668; [[euchenor]], [[polyidus-the-seer]], [[ker-doom]]). The fate he knew of is the death he chose, and Paris' arrow gives it to him (13.671–672). Menelaus, over the body of Peisander, accuses the Trojans: “and now again you are eager, among the seafaring ships, / to throw deadly fire, and to kill the Achaean heroes” (13.628–629; [[menelaus]]). The neuter ὀλοόν is new to the entry. The same ὀλοὸν πῦρ, “deadly fire,” is one of the comparisons for Hector's rage at the ships in Book 15 (15.605). Compare [[destructive]], the epithet of the consuming fire.
