---
summary: The best men are the book's measure. Poseidon rebukes “the best men in the army”, the best are chosen for the close ranks and for the ambush, Teucer is “the best of the Achaeans at archery”, Ajax would yield to no man but Achilles, and on the Trojan side Alcathous was “the best man in wide Troy” and Paris is “best in looks”.
---

**The best of the army.** Poseidon's rebuke is aimed at the leaders as “the best men in the army” (πάντες ἄριστοι ἐόντες ἀνὰ στρατόν, 13.117; see [[aidos]]): a wretch he would not blame for holding back, but they are the best. When the battalions form around the two Ajaxes, “the best men, / chosen out, awaited the Trojans and brilliant Hector” (13.128–129; see [[battle-order]]), and Idomeneus imagines “all the best of us” chosen for an ambush, where courage is seen most clearly (13.276–277; see [[the-brave-man-in-ambush]]). The Trojans, coming on at the start of the book, hope to “kill all the best men there beside them” (13.42), and Polydamas' advice to Hector is to “call all the best men here” for counsel (13.740, 13.751; see [[boule]]).

**Best at what.** The superlative is often limited. Teucer is “the best of the Achaeans / at archery, and good also in close battle” (ὃς ἄριστος Ἀχαιῶν / τοξοσύνῃ, 13.313–314; see [[teucer]]), and Ajax, Idomeneus says, would give way to no mortal man, nor even to Achilles “in the standing fight at least”, though no one can contend with Achilles in speed (13.321–325; see [[ajax]] and [[best-of-the-achaeans]]). The Catalogue made Ajax the best of the men “while Achilles kept up his wrath” (2.768–769); here Idomeneus measures him against Achilles himself and divides the title between them by the kind of fighting. Paris is εἶδος ἄριστε, “best in looks” (13.769; see [[best-in-looks]]), a superlative of the wrong kind for a fighter.

**The best man in Troy.** Alcathous had married Anchises' eldest daughter, who surpassed her age-mates in beauty, handiwork and wits, “and so / the best man in wide Troy had married her” (ἀνὴρ ὤριστος ἐνὶ Τροίῃ εὐρείῃ, 13.432–433; see [[alcathous]] and [[fathers-and-daughters]]). ὤριστος, “the best man”, is the contracted form, used by Hector of Agamemnon when he leaves the battle wounded (11.288) and by Menelaus of the dead Patroclus (17.689); in the same book Hector calls Zeus “the best of the gods” (θεῶν ὤριστος, 13.154).
