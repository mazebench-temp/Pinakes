summary: The boar of Book 13 is Idomeneus: when Aeneas comes against him, he stands his ground like a boar in the mountains that bristles, its eyes blazing, and whets its tusks against the dogs and men coming at it (13.470–477).
grc: [σῦς]
passages:
  - 13.470-477 | Idomeneus like a boar
---

ἀλλʼ ἔμενʼ ὡς ὅτε τις σῦς οὔρεσιν ἀλκὶ πεποιθώς: “but he stood his ground like a boar in the mountains, trusting in its courage” (13.471). σῦς is the plain word for the boar; the Lapiths of Book 12 were σύες ἀγρότεροι, “wild boars” (12.146), and Odysseus' boar at bay a κάπριος (11.414). The boar is Idomeneus' animal: in the review of the army he stood among the champions “like a boar in courage” (4.253; see [[like-a-boar]]). Here the simile is drawn in full, with the din of the hunt, the bristling back, the blazing eyes and the whetted tusks (13.472–475; see [[the-bristling-boar]] and [[eyes-like-fire]]). The boar waits alone; Idomeneus, as soon as the simile is over, calls his companions and admits his fear of Aeneas' youth (13.477–486; see [[idomeneus]]).
