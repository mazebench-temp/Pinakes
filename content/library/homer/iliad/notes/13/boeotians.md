summary: Poseidon rouses the Boeotian leaders Leitus and Peneleos among the men in the rear, and where Hector broke in, the Boeotians head the list of the five peoples who hold him off; the Phthians fight beside them in defense of the ships (13.91–92, 13.685–700).
passages:
  - 13.685-700 | The peoples who held off Hector
---

**Roused.** Among the men in the rear whom Poseidon urges on are two of the five Boeotian leaders of the Catalogue: he comes to “Teucer and Leitus” and to “the hero Peneleos” (13.91–92; 2.494; see [[leitus]], [[peneleos]] and [[poseidon]]).

**At the ships.** Where the wall was lowest, “There the Boeotians and the Ionians with trailing tunics, / the Locrians and the Phthians and the shining Epeians, / hardly held him off from the ships as he darted at them” (13.685–687). The Boeotians, who open the Catalogue, open this list too (2.494–510; see [[catalogue-of-ships]]). The poet then names the leaders of the Athenians, the Epeians and the Phthians, but no Boeotian leader, and brings the Boeotians in once more beside the Phthians, whose leaders Medon and Podarces “fought along with the Boeotians, defending the ships” (13.699–700; see [[phthians]]). In the next two books three of their five leaders fall: Prothoenor (14.449–457), Arcesilaus (15.329–330) and Clonius (15.340) (see [[prothoenor]], [[arcesilaus]] and [[clonius]]).
