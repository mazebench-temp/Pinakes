---
summary: Twice in his talk with Idomeneus “wise Meriones” answers, in the verse-type that the Odyssey gives to Telemachus; he is the first fighting man in the Iliad to be called πεπνυμένος.
---

“And wise Meriones answered him face to face” introduces both of Meriones' replies to Idomeneus (13.254, 13.266; [[meriones]], [[idomeneus]]): first that he is going for a spear, since he has shattered his own on Deiphobus' shield (13.255–258), then that he too has spoils of the Trojans and has not forgotten his courage (13.267–273; [[where-men-win-glory]]). The verse has the shape of the Odyssey's line for Telemachus, τὸν δʼ αὖ Τηλέμαχος πεπνυμένος ἀντίον ηὔδα. Until now the Iliad has given the word to the Trojan elders Antenor and Ucalegon and to heralds (3.148, 3.203, 7.276, 9.689); Meriones is the first fighting man to have it. Antilochus has it in the same verse when he answers Menelaus in the chariot race (23.586). Meriones' other epithet in the book is [[wise-hearted]] (13.164).
