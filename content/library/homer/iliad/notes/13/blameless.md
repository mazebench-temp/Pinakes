---
summary: The epithet of praise, rendered “noble,” is given to Deucalion in Idomeneus' genealogy, to Menelaus as he strips Peisander, and to Polydamas, and once, rendered “flawless,” to dancing.
---

**Persons.** Idomeneus tells Deiphobus of his descent from Zeus: “Zeus first fathered Minos, a guardian over Crete; / and Minos in turn fathered a son, noble Deucalion, / and Deucalion fathered me, a lord over many men / in wide Crete” (13.450–453; [[deucalion]], [[minos]], [[genealogy-in-battle]]). The epithet of praise goes to Idomeneus' own father. Menelaus is “noble Menelaus” as he strips Peisander's bloody armor and gives it to his companions (13.640–641; [[peisander-killed-by-menelaus]], [[bloody]]). When Hector and Paris go back into the fighting, the battle is thickest “around Cebriones and noble Polydamas” (13.790; [[polydamas]], [[cebriones]]), who was led out with the same epithet in Book 12 (12.88).

**A thing.** In Menelaus' saying, “Of all things there is satiety, of sleep and of love, / of sweet song and of flawless dancing” (13.636–637; [[satiety-of-all-things]], [[dance]]). The translation renders ἀμύμων “flawless” of things, as it rendered the women “skilled in flawless handiwork” in Book 9 (9.128).
