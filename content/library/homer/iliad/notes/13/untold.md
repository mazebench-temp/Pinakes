---
summary: The winter torrent breaks the boulder loose “with its untold rain”; the book adds the dative ἀσπέτῳ.
grc: [ἀσπέτῳ]
---

Hector charges like a rolling boulder “which a river in winter flood thrusts down from a crest, breaking with its untold rain the props of the shameless rock” (13.138–139; [[the-rolling-boulder]], [[shameless-stone]], [[river-in-flood]], [[hector]]). ἄσπετος ὄμβρος, rain too great to be told, is the force that looses the boulder, as the cranes of Book 3 flee “the winter and its endless rain” (3.4), where the rain is ἀθέσφατος, a word of the same kind. The boulder, like Hector, runs on until it reaches level ground and stops (13.141–148).
