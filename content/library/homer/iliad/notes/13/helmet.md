summary: In the close ranks the helmets touch crest to crest, and on the left their gleam blinds the eyes. In the fighting Hector rushes to snatch Amphimachus' helmet, Deiphobus tears Ascalaphus' from the body and drops it when Meriones wounds him, Helenus' Thracian sword strikes Deipyrus' helmet from his head, and Peisander's axe falls on the ridge of Menelaus' helmet, which holds (13.131–133, 13.188–193, 13.340–342, 13.527–530, 13.576–579, 13.614–615).

**Crest on crest.** In the close ranks “helmet on helmet” presses, and “the helmets with horsehair crests touched with their bright ridges / when the men nodded, so close did they stand to one another” (13.131–133; see [[battle-order]] and [[horsehair-crested]]). The φάλοι, the ridges on the crown (see the entry), touch as the crests nod; the three verses return for the Myrmidons (16.215–217). When the armies meet on the left, the eyes are blinded “by the gleam of bronze from the shining helmets” (13.340–342; see [[bronze]] and [[eyes]]).

**Hector's.** Ajax's spear can find no skin on Hector, for “all of him was covered / in terrible bronze” (13.191–192). At the end of the book he leads the Trojans like Ares, “and around his temples his shining helmet shook” (13.805; see [[hector]] and [[of-the-gleaming-helmet]]).

**Snatched helmets.** Twice in the book a man reaches first for the helmet of the dead (see [[stripping-the-dead]]). Hector rushes “to snatch the helmet, fitted close to the temples, / from the head of great-hearted Amphimachus”, and Ajax drives him back (13.188–193; see [[amphimachus]]). Deiphobus snatches “the shining helmet” from Ascalaphus (πήληκα, 13.527); Meriones leaps on him and stabs his arm, and “the helmet with its socket for the plume fell to the ground with a clang” (αὐλῶπις τρυφάλεια … βόμβησε, 13.529–530; see [[ascalaphus]] and [[with-a-socket-for-the-plume]]). Helenus strikes Deipyrus on the temple with a great Thracian sword and strikes off his helmet; it glances away and rolls among the feet of the fighters, and one of the Achaeans picks it up, while murky night covers Deipyrus' eyes (13.576–580; see [[deipyrus]] and [[sword]]).

**Under the crest.** Peisander's axe strikes “the ridge of his horsehair-crested helmet, / up under the crest itself” (13.614–615; see [[axe]] and [[menelaus]]). κόρυθος φάλον ἱπποδασείης is the half-verse of the first killing of the poem, when Antilochus struck Echepolus on the ridge of his helmet and the spear went into his forehead (4.459 = 6.9), and Menelaus' own sword once shattered on the ridge of Paris' helmet (3.361–363). Here the helmet holds, and it is Menelaus who kills.

**Spoils and the unarmed.** Idomeneus keeps helmets taken from the enemy in his hut with the spears and the shields (13.265; see [[hut]]). The Locrians have no “bronze-fitted helmets with horsehair crests” (κόρυθας χαλκήρεας ἱπποδασείας, 13.714; see [[sling]]). When Aeneas strikes Aphareus in the throat, his shield and his helmet go down with him (13.543–544).
