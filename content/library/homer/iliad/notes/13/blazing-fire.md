---
summary: “So they fought on like blazing fire”: the verse that ends a battle scene closes the fighting of Menelaus, Meriones and Paris, before the poem turns to Hector.
---

After Paris has killed Euchenor, the narrator sums up the fighting on the left: “So they fought on like blazing fire” (13.673). The same verse ended the battle around Ajax and Eurypylus in Book 11 (11.596) and will end the fight over Patroclus' body (18.1). The next verses turn to Hector, who “had not heard, and did not know at all” that his men were being cut down on the left of the ships (13.674–676; [[dear-to-zeus]], [[hector]]).

Fire is what the Trojans bring to the ships in this book. Idomeneus says that Hector will not burn them “unless the son of Cronus himself / casts a blazing firebrand on the swift ships” (13.319–320), with the same participle, αἰθόμενος, and Menelaus says the Trojans are eager “to throw deadly fire” on them (13.628–629; [[deadly]]).
