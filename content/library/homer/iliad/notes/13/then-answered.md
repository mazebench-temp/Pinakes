grc: ["ἀμείβετο φαίδιμος Ἕκτωρ"]
en: [answered]
---

“Then Poseidon the earth-shaker answered him” (τὸν δʼ ἠμείβετʼ ἔπειτα Ποσειδάων ἐνοσίχθων, 13.231). The god keeps his disguise as Thoas, and only the narrator names him (see [[poseidon]] and [[divine-epiphany]]).

At the end of the book the reply comes in the second half of a verse that began with the army's shout: “and the army of the Achaeans shouted out, / emboldened by the omen; and shining Hector answered” (ὃ δʼ ἀμείβετο φαίδιμος Ἕκτωρ, 13.822–823; see [[divination]], [[hector]] and [[phaidimos]]). The eagle has flown on the right as Ajax finished speaking, and Hector's answer must meet the sign as well as the challenge (see [[what-a-word]]).
