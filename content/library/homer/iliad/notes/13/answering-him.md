---
summary: The talk of Idomeneus and Meriones by the huts is carried by two whole verses of reply in turn, “Then Idomeneus, leader of the Cretans, answered him face to face” and “And wise Meriones answered him face to face” (13.221, 13.254–274, 13.311).
---

τὸν δʼ αὖτʼ Ἰδομενεὺς Κρητῶν ἀγὸς ἀντίον ηὔδα, “Then Idomeneus, leader of the Cretans, answered him face to face”, introduces four of Idomeneus' replies (13.221, 13.259, 13.274, 13.311), and τὸν δʼ αὖ Μηριόνης πεπνυμένος ἀντίον ηὔδα, “And wise Meriones answered him face to face”, two of Meriones' (13.254, 13.266). The first of them answers Poseidon in the likeness of Thoas (13.221); the rest give the exchange by the huts the shape of a dialogue in which each man is brought in by a verse of his own, with his own title, the Cretans' ἀγός, “leader”, and πεπνυμένος, “wise” (13.249–327; see [[idomeneus]], [[meriones]], [[wise]] and [[ambush]]).

The other replies of the book are Telamonian Ajax's to the son of Oileus, “Answering him, Telamonian Ajax said” (13.76; see [[ajax]]), and Paris' to Hector's rebuke, “Then godlike Alexander spoke to him in turn” (13.774; see [[paris]]).
