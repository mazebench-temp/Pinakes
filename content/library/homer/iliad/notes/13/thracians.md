---
summary: The Thracians are the first of the far peoples on whom Zeus turns his eyes away from Troy, “the horse-herding Thracians” (13.4); the adjective “Thracian” marks Samothrace, Poseidon's lookout, and the great sword with which Helenus kills Deipyrus (13.12–13, 13.576–577).
---

ἐφʼ ἱπποπόλων Θρῃκῶν καθορώμενος αἶαν, “looking out far off on the land of the horse-herding Thracians” (13.4). ἱππόπολος, “horse-herding”, occurs again only in the same phrase, when Hera crosses “the snowy mountains of the horse-herding Thracians” on her way to Lemnos (14.227). These are not the Thracian allies who came to fight for Troy (2.844–845, 10.434–435), but the people of the land itself, the first of the far nations that Zeus looks on while he leaves the battle to itself (13.3–7; see [[abii]], [[mysians]] and [[thrace]]).

The adjective Θρηΐκιος serves twice. It tells Samothrace from the other islands called Samos: “wooded Samos, / the Thracian” (13.12–13; see [[samothrace]]). And Helenus kills Deipyrus “with a sword, / a great Thracian sword” (13.576–577; see [[helenus]], [[deipyrus]] and [[sword]]). A Thracian sword is one of the prizes at Patroclus' games, the one Achilles took from Asteropaeus (23.807–808; see [[asteropaeus]]), and Priam's finest cup was a gift of Thracian men (24.234–235): Thracian work was prized on both sides.
