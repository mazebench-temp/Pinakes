summary: As Peisander goes straight for Menelaus, “an evil fate was leading him toward the end of death, / to be beaten down by you, Menelaus, in grim combat”: the narrator tells the end before the fight, and turns to Menelaus to tell it.
passages:
  - 13.601-619 | Menelaus and Peisander
---

Peisander comes on against Menelaus, and before the two men meet the narrator says how it will end: “and an evil fate was leading him toward the end of death, / to be beaten down by you, Menelaus, in grim combat” (τὸν δʼ ἄγε μοῖρα κακὴ θανάτοιο τέλος δὲ / σοὶ Μενέλαε δαμῆναι, 13.602–603; see [[peisander-killed-by-menelaus]] and [[menelaus]]). Fate leads a man to his death, as the fates of black death led the sons of Merops to the war against their father's warning (κῆρες γὰρ ἄγον μέλανος θανάτοιο, 2.834 = 11.332; see [[ker-doom]]). θανάτοιο τέλος, “the end of death”, is the phrase of Priam's words as he leaves the field before the duel of Book 3: Zeus knows “for which of the two the end of death is fated” (3.308–309); and Achilles says that “two different fates are carrying me toward the end of death” (9.410–411). The fight that follows goes the way the narrator has said: Menelaus' spear misses, Peisander's breaks on his shield, and the sword meets the axe (13.604–618; see [[axe]] and [[sword]]).

The narrator speaks to Menelaus directly, as he does seven times in the Iliad; only Patroclus, in Book 16, is addressed so more often: when Athena turns Pandarus' arrow from him and his thighs are stained with blood (4.127, 4.146), when he would have died at Hector's hands had the kings not held him back (7.104), and when he searches for Antilochus over Patroclus' body (17.679, 17.702), and at the end of the chariot race, when his anger at Antilochus melts (23.600). Here the address comes with the man's victory. In Book 12 the narrator foretold Asius' death in the same way, “fate of evil name enfolded him / through the spear of Idomeneus” (12.116–117; see the note on Book 12), and the prophecy is fulfilled in this book (13.384–393; see [[asius]] and [[prophecy]]).
