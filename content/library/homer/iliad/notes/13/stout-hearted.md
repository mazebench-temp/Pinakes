---
summary: Rout, the son of Ares, “routs even a warrior of enduring heart”: ταλάφρων, a shorter form of ταλασίφρων found only here, which the book adds.
grc: [ταλάφρονά]
en: [of enduring heart]
---

Idomeneus and Meriones go into battle like Ares and his son: “and Rout, his dear son, strong and fearless, follows with him, who routs even a warrior of enduring heart” (13.299–300; [[rout]], [[ares-and-rout]], [[fearless]]). ταλάφρονά περ πολεμιστήν is built like the line of Book 4 in which the sound of Diomedes' armor would have made “even a stout-hearted man” afraid, ταλασίφρονά περ (4.421): the same root of τλῆναι, “endure,” and the same περ, “even,” for the man whom fear should not touch and does. ταλάφρων is found only here in Homer. It is not ἀταλάφρων, “of tender mind,” the word for the infant Astyanax (6.400; [[of-tender-mind]]).
