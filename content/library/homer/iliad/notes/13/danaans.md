---
summary: The Danaans are the army that Zeus expects no god to help, that Poseidon goes through the camp to urge on, and whose close ranks Hector broke where he first leapt in; to Menelaus they are “the Danaans of the swift horses” (13.9, 13.209, 13.620, 13.680).
---

Zeus turns his eyes from Troy, “for he did not expect in his heart that any of the immortals / would come to help either the Trojans or the Danaans” (13.8–9; see [[zeus]]). Poseidon comes, and after Amphimachus' death “he went along the huts and ships of the Achaeans / to urge on the Danaans” (13.208–209; see [[poseidon]]); Idomeneus leaps among the Trojans “calling on the Danaans” (13.361; see [[idomeneus]]). Imbrius had come back to Ilios “when the curving ships of the Danaans came” (13.174; see [[imbrius]]). Menelaus, over Peisander, tells the Trojans that they will leave “the ships of the Danaans of the swift horses” (13.620; see [[menelaus]] and [[of-the-swift-horses]]). Hector holds on where he first leapt in, “breaking the close ranks of the shield-bearing Danaans” (13.680; see [[shield-bearing]] and [[the-gates-of-the-wall]]), and Paris tells him that since he roused the battle by the ships “we have been here engaging the Danaans / without rest” (13.779–780; see [[paris]]).
