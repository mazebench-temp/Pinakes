summary: As Book 13 opens on the battle, the Trojans follow Hector to the ships “like a flame or a storm wind” (13.39); at its end they come on behind him like a blast of grievous winds that falls on the sea (13.795–800).
grc: ["ἠὲ θυέλλῃ"]
en: [a storm wind]
---

Τρῶες δὲ φλογὶ ἶσοι ἀολλέες ἠὲ θυέλλῃ / Ἕκτορι Πριαμίδῃ ἄμοτον μεμαῶτες ἕποντο: “And the Trojans, like a flame or a storm wind, in a body, raging without end, followed Hector, son of Priam” (13.39–40). The storm wind is θύελλα here, not the ἄελλα of Hector's simile in Book 11 and of the half-verse at the wall (11.297, 12.40), and it is no longer Hector but the whole army behind him, joined with the flame (see [[like-a-flame]] and [[trojans]]). θύελλα is also the wind that Zeus roused from Ida earlier on this same day, to carry the dust against the ships (12.252–254).

The wind returns at the end of the book in a full simile. The Trojans follow Hector again, “like a blast of grievous winds” (ἀτάλαντοι ἀέλλῃ) that comes down under the thunder of father Zeus and mingles with the sea, raising wave behind wave (13.795–801; see [[the-storm-and-the-waves]]). Between the two the Achaeans, roused by Poseidon, have held (see [[poseidon-rallies-the-achaeans]]).
