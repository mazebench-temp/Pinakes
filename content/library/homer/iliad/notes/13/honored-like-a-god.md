---
summary: Poseidon takes the voice of Thoas, who “was honored by the people like a god,” to rouse Idomeneus.
---

“And the powerful earth-shaker spoke to him, / making his voice like that of Thoas, son of Andraemon, / who in all Pleuron and steep Calydon / was lord of the Aetolians, and was honored by the people like a god” (13.215–218; [[thoas]], [[aetolians]], [[poseidon]], [[divine-epiphany]]). The verse-end that honors kings and priests here belongs to the man whose voice a god borrows: a god speaks as a man whom his people honor as a god. It is the fourth of the formula's six uses in Homer (5.78, 10.33, 11.58; 16.605, Od. 14.205), and after Agamemnon (10.33) the second given to an Achaean leader. Thoas himself is in the battle; Poseidon has urged him on with the others (13.92), and in Book 15 he will speak in his own voice when Hector comes back to the fight (15.281–299).
