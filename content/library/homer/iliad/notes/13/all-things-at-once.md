summary: Polydamas uses Nestor's maxim against Hector, who wants to excel in counsel as he does in war: “you will in no way be able to take everything for yourself at once”.
grc: ["ἅμα πάντα δυνήσεαι"]
en: [everything for yourself at once]
passages:
  - 13.723-753 | Polydamas' counsel at the ships
---

Nestor's maxim in Book 4 answered a wish for youth: “But the gods never give men all things at once” (ἀλλʼ οὔ πως ἅμα πάντα θεοὶ δόσαν ἀνθρώποισιν, 4.320), and in his case the gifts were given in turn, strength when he was young and counsel now that he is old (see the article above). Polydamas turns the same half-verse to a man in his prime. Hector has the works of war from a god and wants to know more than others in counsel too, “but you will in no way be able to take everything for yourself at once” (ἀλλʼ οὔ πως ἅμα πάντα δυνήσεαι αὐτὸς ἑλέσθαι, 13.729; see [[hector]] and [[polydamas]]). The subject has changed from the gods who give to the man who would take, ἑλέσθαι, and the time from a lifetime to a single day. The gifts are divided among men as Nestor's were divided across his life, and Polydamas goes on to name them: war, dancing, the lyre and song, and a good mind (13.730–734; see [[different-gifts]]).
