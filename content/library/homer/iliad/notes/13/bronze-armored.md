---
summary: Meriones calls Idomeneus “counselor of the bronze-armored Cretans” and says that some other of “the bronze-armored Achaeans” may not see him fight.
---

When Idomeneus asks why he has left the battle, Meriones answers: “Idomeneus, counselor of the bronze-armored Cretans, / I am going to see if a spear is left for you in the huts” (13.255–256; [[meriones]], [[idomeneus]], [[cretans]]). The address has the form of Pandarus' greeting to Aeneas, Αἰνεία Τρώων βουληφόρε χαλκοχιτώνων (5.180), with the epithet given to one contingent instead of to the army. Poseidon had called Idomeneus plain “counselor of the Cretans” (13.219; [[man-of-counsel]]). Meriones adds that he has not forgotten his courage: “Some other of the bronze-armored Achaeans, perhaps, / may not notice me fighting, but you yourself, I think, know it” (13.272–273; [[achaeans]]).
