summary: Imbrius falls like an ash tree that brings its “tender leaves” to the ground, his head is cut “from his tender neck,” and the Trojans cannot reach the “tender flesh” of Antilochus; the book adds ἁπαλῆς, from ἁπαλός, which the translation renders “tender” here.
grc: [ἁπαλῆς]
---

**Imbrius.** The word is given twice to Imbrius, Teucer's first victim. He “fell like an ash tree that on the peak of a mountain seen from afar on every side is cut by the bronze and brings its tender leaves to the ground” (13.178–180; [[imbrius]], [[the-felled-ash]]), and when the two Ajaxes have carried off his body, the son of Oileus, angry for Amphimachus, cuts off “his head from his tender neck” and sends it rolling to Hector's feet (13.202–205; [[ajax-son-of-oileus]], [[amphimachus]]). The second word is ἁπαλός, “soft, tender,” which the translation renders “soft” of Paris' throat in Book 3 (3.371) and “tender” here; it is added to the entry. In Book 18 Iris tells Achilles that Hector means to cut off Patroclus' head in the same words, ἁπαλῆς ἀπὸ δειρῆς (18.175–177).

**Antilochus.** Stripping Thoon, Antilochus is beset by Trojans who “could not get through to graze the tender flesh with the pitiless bronze of Antilochus” (13.552–554; [[antilochus]], [[pitiless-bronze]], [[panaiolos]]). The leaves of the felled tree and the neck of the dead man are cut by the bronze; from Antilochus' flesh a god keeps it off.
