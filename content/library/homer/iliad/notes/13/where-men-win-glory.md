---
summary: Meriones tells Idomeneus that he takes his stand “among the foremost, in the battle where men win glory.”
---

Meriones, who has come back for a spear, answers Idomeneus' question about his courage: he too has spoils of the Trojans by his hut, “For I tell you that I too have not forgotten my courage, but among the foremost, in the battle where men win glory, I take my stand, whenever the quarrel of war arises” (13.269–271; [[meriones]], [[alke]], [[neikos]]). It is the phrase Sarpedon used of the battle that he and Glaucus must enter because they are mortal (12.322–328; [[sarpedons-speech]]). Idomeneus answers that he knows Meriones' prowess, and would see it in an ambush, “where most of all the prowess of men is seen clearly” (13.275–277; [[ambush]], [[arete]]). See [[kydos]].
