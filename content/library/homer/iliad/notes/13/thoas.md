**Urged on.** Thoas is among the leaders whom Poseidon rouses in the rear, “Thoas and Deipyrus” (13.92; see [[deipyrus]] and [[poseidon]]).

**His voice.** When Poseidon, angry for his grandson, meets Idomeneus, he speaks “making his voice like that of Thoas, son of Andraemon, who in all Pleuron and steep Calydon was lord of the Aetolians, and was honored by the people like a god” (13.216–218; see [[andraemon]], [[pleuron]], [[calydon]], [[aetolians]] and [[honored-like-a-god]]). The last half-verse was said of Agamemnon, “who ruled mightily over all the Argives” (10.32–33). As Calchas, the god took both form and voice (13.45); here only the voice is named, φθογγή (see [[divine-epiphany]]). Idomeneus answers him as Thoas, and praises him for what the man was known for: “since even before this you were staunch against the enemy, and you urge on another man too, wherever you see him holding back” (13.228–229; see [[idomeneus]]).

Book 15 bears the praise out. There Thoas, far the best of the Aetolians, skilled with the javelin, brave in the standing fight and beaten in assembly by few, advises the chiefs to stand against Hector while the army goes back to the ships (15.281–299).
