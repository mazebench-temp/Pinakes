---
summary: Meriones strikes Adamas between the navel and the genitals, “where most of all / Ares is painful for miserable mortals”; the book adds the nominative ἀλεγεινός.
grc: [ἀλεγεινὸς]
---

Adamas, son of Asius, has stabbed at Antilochus' shield, and Poseidon has made his spear powerless; as he draws back, “Meriones followed him as he went away and struck him with his spear / between the genitals and the navel, where most of all / Ares is painful for miserable mortals” (13.567–569; [[adamas]], [[meriones]], [[ares]]). The war god's name stands for the violence of war, here for the wound itself, as at the death of Alcathous, where “mighty Ares let its might go” (13.444). The nominative ἀλεγεινός is new to the entry, and this is its only use of Ares. Adamas writhes around the spear like an ox that herdsmen have bound with ropes (13.570–573; [[the-roped-ox]]).
