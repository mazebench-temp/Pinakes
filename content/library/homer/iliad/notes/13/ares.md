summary: Ares takes no part in Book 13. His name measures the fighters and stands for war itself; in a simile he goes out from Thrace with his son Rout; and when Deiphobus kills his son Ascalaphus, he does not hear of it, for he sits on Olympus under golden clouds, held back with the other gods by the counsels of Zeus.

**The judge of battle.** The ranks that form around the two Ajaxes are so strong that “not even Ares could have made light of” them, “nor Athena who rouses armies” (13.127–128; see [[battle-order]] and [[who-rouses-armies]]). The two war gods are the judges of a fight: no Ares and no Athena would have made light of the fight over Patroclus either (17.398–399), and Achilles says that not even they could range over the whole mouth of so great a battle (20.358–359; see [[athena]]).

**Ares and Rout.** Idomeneus and Meriones go out “as Ares, ruin of mortals, goes into war, / and Rout, his dear son, strong and fearless, follows with him”; the two arm in Thrace against the Ephyri or the Phlegyes, and “do not / listen to both sides, but give glory to one or the other” (13.298–305; see [[ares-and-rout]], [[rout]] and [[ruin-of-mortals]]). Thrace is Ares' home: freed from Hephaestus' bonds, he goes there (*Odyssey* 8.361; see [[thrace]]). Strabo reports that the people of Crannon were once called Ephyri and those of Gyrton Phlegyae, so that it is hard to say whom the poet means (9.5.21; see [[strabo]]).

**The equal of Ares.** Meriones is three times “the equal of swift Ares” (13.295, 13.328, 13.528), Aeneas and Idomeneus are “the equals of Ares” as they meet over Alcathous (13.500), and Hector leads the Trojans “the equal of Ares, ruin of mortals” (13.802; see [[equal-of-ares]]). Polydamas, without naming Achilles, calls him “a man insatiable of war” (13.746), with Ares' own epithet (5.388, 5.863; see [[insatiable-of-war]]).

**Ares as war.** The name stands for the power of war in a weapon and a wound. When Alcathous' heart, still beating, has shaken the butt of the spear fixed in it, “mighty Ares let its might go” (13.442–444; see the translation's note and [[obrimos]]). Meriones strikes Adamas “where most of all / Ares is painful for miserable mortals” (13.568–569). Menelaus tells the Trojans that they will be stopped, “eager though you are for Ares” (13.630).

Ascalaphus and Ialmenus are sons of Ares by Astyoche (2.512–515; see [[the-birth-of-ascalaphus-and-ialmenus]]). The ban of Book 8 keeps the gods from the war (8.10–12; see [[olympians]]); ἐελμένος, “held back”, is the word that pens the Argives beside their ships at the start of Book 12 (12.38). βριήπυος, “loud-voiced”, is found only here; when Diomedes wounded him, Ares bellowed “as loud as nine thousand or ten thousand men shout / in war” (5.860–861). Golden clouds are found only here; Zeus covers himself and Hera with a golden cloud on Ida (14.343–351). Over the dead son Meriones, “the equal of swift Ares”, wounds Deiphobus in the arm (13.526–530). Ares hears the news from Hera in Book 15, beats his thighs and arms to avenge his son against Zeus' command, until Athena takes his helmet, shield and spear and sits him down (15.110–142).
