---
summary: Companions carry the wounded, take the spoils, and hold a leader's shield when he tires; men die stretching their hands to their companions or in their arms; and Paris tells Hector that the companions he asks about are dead.
grc: [ἕταροι]
---

**Care for the fallen.** The book's companions carry and cover. Idomeneus comes from a companion struck behind the knee: “His companions had carried him off” (13.211–214; see [[healing]]). Antilochus, grieving at Deiphobus' boast, “did not neglect his companion” Hypsenor, but stood over him and covered him with his shield, and two trusty companions carried him to the ships (13.418–423; see [[antilochus]] and [[alastor-who-carries-teucer]]). Menelaus gives the armor he strips from Peisander to his companions (13.640–641; see [[stripping-the-dead]]).

**In their arms.** The dying turn to them. Thoon falls on his back “stretching out both his hands to his dear companions” (13.548–549), in the verse of Diores' death in Book 4 (4.523), and Harpalion sits down “in the arms of his dear companions”, breathing out his life (13.653–654; see [[harpalion]]). The wounded and the threatened draw back “into the crowd of his companions” (13.165, 13.533, 13.566, 13.596, 13.648; see [[ker-doom]] and [[retreat]]).

**The shield.** With Telamonian Ajax “there followed many brave / men, his companions, who would take over his shield / whenever weariness and sweat came over his knees” (λαοὶ ἕπονθʼ ἕταροι, 13.709–711; see [[ajax]] and [[gounata]]). When his knees tire, other men hold the great shield for him.

**Lost.** Paris answers Hector, who asks after Deiphobus, Helenus, Adamas, Asius and Othryoneus: “the companions you ask about have been killed” (ἕταροι δὲ κατέκταθεν οὓς σὺ μεταλλᾷς, 13.780), all but Deiphobus and Helenus, who have gone away wounded (13.781–783; see [[paris]] and [[beyond-ones-strength]]).
