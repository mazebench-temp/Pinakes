---
summary: Meriones explains that he broke his spear “striking the shield of overweening Deiphobus”; the book adds the genitive singular ὑπερηνορέοντος.
grc: [ὑπερηνορέοντος]
---

Met by Idomeneus near the huts, Meriones says why he has come back from the fight: “I am going to see if a spear is left for you in the huts, to bring it; for the one I had before I have shattered striking the shield of overweening Deiphobus” (13.256–258; [[meriones]], [[deiphobus]], [[of-bulls-hide]]). The narrator showed Deiphobus advancing “in high spirits,” μέγα φρονέων (13.156); the Achaean who failed to pierce his shield calls the same confidence arrogance. It is the second and last use of the word in the Iliad, and both are Achaean words for Trojans: in Book 4 Agamemnon imagined one of the overweening Trojans leaping on Menelaus' tomb (4.176). See [[trojans]].
