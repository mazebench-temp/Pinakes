---
summary: The polished things of Book 13 are Helenus' bow, which Menelaus' spear pins to his hand, the yoke of the oxen to which the two Ajaxes are compared, and the handle of Peisander's axe, ἐϋξέστῳ, “well polished,” which the book adds.
grc: [ἐϋξέστῳ]
---

Menelaus strikes Helenus' hand, the hand “that held the polished bow; and into the bow the bronze spear was driven right through the hand” (13.594–595; [[helenus]], [[menelaus]], [[bow]]): the archer's weapon is nailed to the hand that holds it. Peisander comes at Menelaus with “a fine axe of good bronze, on a handle of olive wood, long and well polished” (13.611–613; [[peisander-killed-by-menelaus]], [[axe]], [[well-bronzed]]); ἐΰξεστος, “well-scraped,” is the twin of ἐΰξοος, from the same verb ξέω. In the simile of the two Ajaxes as a team of oxen at the plow, “only the polished yoke keeps the two apart as they strain down the furrow” (13.706–707; [[oxen-at-the-plow]], [[the-two-ajaxes]]).
