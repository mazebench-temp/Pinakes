---
summary: Poseidon's house at Aegae is “golden, gleaming, imperishable forever”; the book adds the neuter plural ἄφθιτα.
grc: [ἄφθιτα]
---

Poseidon reaches “Aegae, where his glorious house is built in the depths of the sea, / golden, gleaming, imperishable forever” (13.21–22; [[aegae]], [[poseidon]], [[famous]]). ἄφθιτα αἰεί is the phrase of Agamemnon's scepter, “imperishable forever” (2.46, 2.186; [[the-scepter-of-agamemnon]]), which Hephaestus made. Gold and imperishability go together in the things of the gods, as in the golden rims of Hera's wheels (5.724). The neuter plural ἄφθιτα is new to the entry; it returns in the imperishable counsels of Zeus (ἄφθιτα μήδεα, 24.88).
