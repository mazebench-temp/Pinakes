---
summary: The close ranks hold out their spears “from bold hands,” and Polydamas comes to “bold Hector” a third time, now with counsel that Hector takes.
---

In the close ranks around the two Ajaxes, “the spears from their bold hands overlapped / as they shook” (13.134–135; [[battle-order]], [[the-two-ajaxes]]). The phrase θρασειάων ἀπὸ χειρῶν, used in Book 11 of the spears flung at the lion of the simile and at Ajax (11.553, 11.571), here describes the hands of men who stand their ground against Hector's charge (13.136–148).

When the Trojans are about to fall back from the ships, the verse of Polydamas' two counsels in Book 12 returns with “had not”: “had not Polydamas come and stood beside bold Hector and said” (13.725; 12.60, 12.210; [[would-have-had-not]], [[polydamas]]). This time his “safe counsel pleased Hector,” and Hector leaps from his chariot (13.748–749), as he did at the ditch (12.80–81). See [[hector]].
