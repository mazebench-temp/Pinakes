---
summary: Hector, driven back by the close ranks, is sure that “the loud-thundering husband of Hera” has roused him.
---

Hector's charge has broken on the close ranks around the Ajaxes, and he calls on the Trojans to stand by him: the Achaeans will give ground before his spear, “if truly / the best of the gods has roused me, the loud-thundering husband of Hera” (13.153–154; [[hector]], [[loud-thundering]]). The formula usually closes a sentence in which Zeus is called on to witness or fulfill a word; here it closes Hector's claim that the god is with him. He is right in part. Zeus does will the Trojans' victory (13.347–348; [[plan-of-zeus]]), but he has turned his eyes away from Troy, and Poseidon is rousing the Achaeans against Hector (13.1–16, 13.43–58; [[poseidon-rallies-the-achaeans]]).
