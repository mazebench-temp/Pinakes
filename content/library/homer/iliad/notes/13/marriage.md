summary: The obituaries of Book 13 tell of marriages into the royal houses of Troy. Imbrius had a bastard daughter of Priam as his wife and lived beside Priam; Othryoneus was promised Cassandra for a deed, and Idomeneus mocks him with the offer of a daughter of Agamemnon; Alcathous, the best man in Troy, had married Hippodameia, Anchises' eldest daughter, and reared her brother Aeneas. Menelaus reproaches the Trojans who carried off his wedded wife (13.171–176, 13.363–382, 13.427–466, 13.626).

**Married into Priam's house.** Imbrius, son of Mentor, “had as his wife a bastard daughter of Priam, Medesicaste”, and when the Achaean ships came he moved back to Ilios, where he lived beside Priam, who honored him as he honored his own children (13.171–176; see [[imbrius]], [[medesicaste]] and [[nothos]]). The king's son-in-law has a place in the king's house, as Bellerophon had in Lycia (6.191–195; see [[bellerophon]]).

**Othryoneus.** Othryoneus asked for Cassandra “without bride-price”, promising to drive the Achaeans from Troy, and Priam promised her. Idomeneus kills him and offers him in mockery the most beautiful of Agamemnon's daughters, brought from Argos “to marry her” (ὀπυιέμεν), if he will help to sack Ilios, and invites him to the ships to come to terms “about the marriage” (ἀμφὶ γάμῳ, 13.363–382; see [[bride-price]], [[othryoneus]] and [[cassandra]]). The mockery is shaped on Agamemnon's real offer to Achilles in Book 9 (9.141–148), which Achilles refused.

**Alcathous and Hippodameia.** The obituary of Alcathous is the story of a marriage:

{{quote:eaf3d772-3d51-513a-8e7f-bed8cb15076e}}

Hippodameia surpassed the girls of her age “in beauty and handiwork and wits”, the gifts for which Agamemnon preferred Chryseis to his wedded wife, “not in form or stature, not in mind or in handiwork” (1.114–115), and two of those Achilles named when he refused Agamemnon's daughter, “not if she rivaled golden Aphrodite in beauty / and equaled gray-eyed Athena in handiwork” (9.389–390). The best of the girls married “the best man in wide Troy” (13.433; see [[alcathous]], [[hippodameia-daughter-of-anchises]], [[anchises]] and [[fathers-and-daughters]]). The marriage made him Aeneas' brother-in-law, and Deiphobus reminds Aeneas that Alcathous “reared you in his house when you were small” (13.463–466; see [[aeneas]] and [[in-laws]]). Poseidon binds his eyes and limbs, and Idomeneus kills him where he stands (13.434–444).

**Wives.** Menelaus reproaches the Trojans who carried off “my wedded wife” (κουριδίην ἄλοχον) and many possessions (13.626; see [[possessions]] and [[helen]]), and Paris is “the husband of Helen of the lovely hair” (13.766; see [[paris]]). Medon, the bastard son of Oileus, killed a kinsman of his stepmother Eriopis, “whom Oileus had as wife”, and lived in exile in Phylace (13.694–697; see [[medon]], [[eriopis]] and [[exile-and-refuge]]).
