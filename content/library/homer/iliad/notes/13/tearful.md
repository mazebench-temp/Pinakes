---
summary: Hector finds Paris “on the left of the tearful battle”; the book adds the genitive δακρυοέσσης.
grc: [δακρυοέσσης]
---

Looking for Deiphobus, Helenus, Adamas and Asius, Hector finds them dead or wounded, “But soon he found, on the left of the tearful battle, brilliant Alexander, the husband of Helen of the lovely hair” (13.765–766; [[hector]], [[paris]], [[lovely-haired]]). μάχη δακρυόεσσα is the battle that causes tears, as war is πολύδακρυς in Book 3, and the word comes at the moment Hector learns how many of his companions it has cost (13.758–764). The book shows the tears as well: the Achaeans in the rear weep as they watch the Trojans cross the wall (13.88), and Pylaemenes follows his son's body in tears (13.658; [[pylaemenes-dead-and-alive]]).
