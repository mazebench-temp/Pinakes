---
summary: Poseidon takes the likeness of Calchas “in form and in tireless voice”, and later makes his voice like that of Thoas; εἶδος, “form, looks”, names Cassandra and Agamemnon's daughter as the most beautiful daughters of their fathers, and Paris as “best in looks”.
grc: [εἰσάμενος]
en: [making his voice like]
---

**The god's likenesses.** Poseidon comes to the two Ajaxes “in the likeness of Calchas in form and in tireless voice” (εἰσάμενος Κάλχαντι δέμας καὶ ἀτειρέα φωνήν, 13.45), and the son of Oileus, who sees through him, says that a god has come “in the likeness of the seer” (μάντεϊ εἰδόμενος, 13.69; see [[seeing-the-gods]] and [[calchas]]). To Idomeneus he speaks “making his voice like that of Thoas, son of Andraemon” (εἰσάμενος φθογγήν, 13.216; see [[thoas]]), as Apollo will make his voice like Lycaon's to rouse Aeneas (20.81). The first disguise is of form and voice, the second of voice alone; the narrator sums up both as “the likeness of a man” (ἀνδρὶ ἐοικώς, 13.357; see [[divine-intervention]]).

**Looks.** The noun names beauty and its price. Othryoneus asked Priam for “the most beautiful in form of the daughters of Priam, / Cassandra” (εἶδος ἀρίστην, 13.365–366; see [[cassandra]]), and Idomeneus mocks the bargain with an offer of “the most beautiful in form of the daughters of the son of Atreus” (13.378; see [[fathers-and-daughters]]). Hector calls Paris “Evil Paris, best in looks” (εἶδος ἄριστε, 13.769 = 3.39; see [[best-in-looks]] and [[beauty-and-its-price]]).

**Seen.** The verb is also plain seeing. Ajax's spear reaches for Hector, “but none of his skin showed, and all of him was covered / in terrible bronze” (οὔ πῃ χροὸς εἴσατο, 13.191–192; see [[hector]]), and Poseidon warns the Achaeans that “now indeed the day is in sight when we will be beaten down by the Trojans” (εἴδεται, 13.98).
