summary: Challenged by Idomeneus, Deiphobus weighs whether to fight him alone or to find a companion, and resolves in the verse of many Homeric decisions, “And as he pondered, this seemed to him the better way” (13.446–459).
grc: ["δοάσσατο κέρδιον εἶναι"]
en: [this seemed to him the better way]
refs: [13.455-457]
---

The four parts of the scene are all here (see the entry). The shock is Idomeneus' boast and challenge, “come and stand against me yourself” (13.446–454; see [[genealogy-in-battle]]). The division is the formula, “Deiphobus was torn two ways in thought” (13.455; see [[pondering-two-ways]]). The alternatives are a companion or a fight alone (13.456–457), and the resolution comes in a whole verse: “And as he pondered, this seemed to him the better way: / to go for Aeneas” (ὧδε δέ οἱ φρονέοντι δοάσσατο κέρδιον εἶναι, 13.458–459; see [[deiphobus]] and [[aeneas]]).

The verse of the resolution returns in the next book, when Nestor decides to go to Agamemnon rather than into the throng (14.20–24), and in Book 16, when Zeus decides that Patroclus shall drive the Trojans to the city before he dies (16.647–655). It is used seven times in the Odyssey (Od. 5.474, 6.145, 10.153, 15.204, 18.93, 22.338, 24.239). A king of the gods, an old counselor and a young fighter weigh their choices in the same words.
