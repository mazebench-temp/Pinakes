---
summary: In Idomeneus' account of the ambush, ἄλκιμος names the brave man set against the coward: there “the coward and the valiant man are shown for what they are.”
---

Idomeneus tells Meriones that if the best men were being chosen for an ambush, “where most of all the prowess of men is seen clearly, where the coward and the valiant man are shown for what they are” (13.277–278; [[idomeneus]], [[meriones]], [[ambush]], [[arete]]), no one would fault Meriones' courage. ὅ τε δειλὸς ἀνὴρ ὅς τʼ ἄλκιμος, “the coward and the valiant man,” is the pair the speech goes on to describe: the coward's color changes and his teeth chatter, while the brave man's color does not change (13.279–286). The adjective of the strong spear and the valiant heart is here the whole man, as it is of Patroclus, “the valiant son of Menoetius” (11.605). See [[alke]].
