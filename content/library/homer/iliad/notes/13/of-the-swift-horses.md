---
summary: Menelaus, standing over the body of Peisander, tells the Trojans that they will leave “the ships of the Danaans of the swift horses.”
---

Menelaus has killed Peisander and stripped him, and he begins his speech with a threat in the form of a certainty: “So, I think, you will leave the ships of the Danaans of the swift horses, you overbearing Trojans, insatiable of the dread war cry” (13.620–621; [[menelaus]], [[peisander-killed-by-menelaus]], [[overbearing]], [[insatiable]]). His own side keeps its formula of honor, while the enemy are given two words of blame. The rest of the speech is about the wrong done to him and the wrath of Zeus, god of guests (13.622–639; [[xenia]], [[who-thunders-loud]], [[satiety-of-all-things]]). See [[danaans]].
