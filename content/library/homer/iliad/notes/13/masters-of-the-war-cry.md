summary: Meriones and Antilochus are “masters of the war cry” in two lists of Achaean leaders, those whom Poseidon urges on and those to whom Idomeneus calls for help; the book adds the accusative μήστωρας ἀϋτῆς.
grc: [μήστωρας ἀϋτῆς]
---

The phrase closes the same verse twice, “and Meriones and Antilochus, masters of the war cry” (13.93, 13.479; [[meriones]], [[antilochus]]). The first time it ends the list of leaders whom Poseidon urges on: Teucer and Leitus, Peneleos, Thoas and Deipyrus, Meriones and Antilochus (13.91–93; [[poseidon]]). The second time it ends the list of companions to whom Idomeneus calls when Aeneas comes at him: Ascalaphus, Aphareus, Deipyrus, Meriones and Antilochus (13.477–479; [[idomeneus]]). The pair receives the epithet that Book 4 gave to the Athenians (4.328). Of the three named with them in the second list, all are killed before the book ends: Ascalaphus by Deiphobus, Aphareus by Aeneas, Deipyrus by Helenus (13.518–520, 13.541–544, 13.576–580; [[ascalaphus]], [[aphareus]], [[deipyrus]]).
