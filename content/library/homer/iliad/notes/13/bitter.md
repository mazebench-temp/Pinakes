---
summary: Helenus' “bitter arrow” strikes Menelaus' corselet and flies off, and the formula ends the verse on either side of the simile of the winnowed beans.
---

Menelaus goes at Helenus with his spear while Helenus draws his bow, and the arrow comes first: “Then the son of Priam struck him in the chest with an arrow, / on the plate of his corselet, and the bitter arrow flew off” (13.586–587; [[helenus]], [[menelaus]], [[corselet]]). As beans or chickpeas leap from a winnowing shovel, “so from the corselet of glorious Menelaus / the bitter arrow glanced far away and flew off” (13.588–592; [[beans-on-the-threshing-floor]], [[kydalimos]]). The formula πικρὸς ὀϊστός ends both verses, before and after the simile. In Book 4 the bitter arrow of Pandarus went through Menelaus' belt and corselet and drew his blood (4.134–140); this one does not get through. Menelaus' spear then pins Helenus' hand to his bow (13.593–595; [[ash-spear]]). See [[arrows]].
