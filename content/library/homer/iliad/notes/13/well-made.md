---
summary: Poseidon's whip is “golden and well made,” as Zeus' was in Book 8; Idomeneus arms in his well-made hut; and Asius' charioteer falls from “the well-made chariot” in the verse of Mydon's death.
---

Three made things. At Aegae Poseidon “took the whip, golden and well made, and mounted his chariot” (13.25–26; [[swift-flying]], [[chariot-journey]]), in the verses of Zeus' drive to Ida (8.43–44). Idomeneus, leaving his wounded companion, comes “to his well-made hut” and arms (13.240–241; [[idomeneus]], [[hut]], [[arming-scene]]): κλισίην εὔτυκτον is the phrase that ends the night raid at Diomedes' hut (10.566), where the translation renders it “well-made.” And when Antilochus spears Asius' charioteer, “gasping he fell from the well-made chariot” (13.399; [[antilochus]]), in the verse of the fall of Pylaemenes' charioteer Mydon, killed by the same Antilochus; both times he drives off the horses (5.585–589, 13.400–401; [[chariot]]).
