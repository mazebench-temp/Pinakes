---
summary: The boulder to which Hector is likened is torn from “the shameless rock”; the word for a man without αἰδώς is given again to stone.
grc: [ἀναιδέος]
---

Hector leads the Trojans on “like a rolling boulder from a rock, / which a river in winter flood thrusts down from a crest, / breaking with its untold rain the props of the shameless rock” (ἀναιδέος ἔχματα πέτρης, 13.137–139; see [[the-rolling-boulder]] and [[hector]]). In Book 1 ἀναιδής was Achilles' insult to Agamemnon (1.149, 1.158); in Book 4 it was said of the stone that crushed Diores' ankle (4.521; see the note on Book 4). Here it is the rock face itself, which the torrent breaks apart. The stone respects nothing and stops for nothing, and yet the boulder it releases does stop, on level ground and against the close ranks of the Achaeans (13.141–148; see [[shameless-stone]] and [[aidos]]).
