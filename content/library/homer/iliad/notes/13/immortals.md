---
summary: Zeus expects none of the immortals to help either side, but Poseidon comes on his “immortal feet”; the other immortals are shut out from the war, and Ajax foretells that Hector will pray to them in flight.
---

The book begins with an expectation: Zeus “did not expect in his heart that any of the immortals / would come to help either the Trojans or the Danaans” (13.8–9; [[zeus]]). It is wrong at once. Poseidon comes down from Samothrace, “and the high mountains and the woods trembled / under the immortal feet of Poseidon as he went” (13.18–19; [[poseidon]]). The others keep to Zeus' ban: Ares sits on Olympus under golden clouds, “held back by the counsels of Zeus, where the other / immortal gods were also, shut out from the war” (13.523–525; [[ares]], [[olympians]]), and does not know that his son Ascalaphus has fallen. At the end of the book Ajax tells Hector that he will soon pray, in flight, “to father Zeus and the other immortals” (13.818; [[father-zeus]]).
