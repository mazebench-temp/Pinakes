φράξαντες δόρυ δουρί, σάκος σάκεϊ προθελύμνῳ· / ἀσπὶς ἄρʼ ἀσπίδʼ ἔρειδε, κόρυς κόρυν, ἀνέρα δʼ ἀνήρ: “fencing spear with spear, shield with overlapping shield; / shield pressed on shield, helmet on helmet, man on man” (13.130–131; see [[battle-order]]). Five pairs in two verses, each a noun beside itself in another case, and one verb, ἔρειδε, “pressed”, serving the three pairs of the second line. The figure is the formation: each thing is set against its like, and the last pair, man on man, gives what the weapons stand for. The second verse returns, with the two that follow, when the Myrmidons form up behind Patroclus (16.215–217). In the first battle the same figure reduced the fight over a body to “man hurled man about” (ἀνὴρ δʼ ἄνδρʼ, 4.472); here it builds a wall of men (see [[shield]], [[spear]] and [[helmet]]).
