summary: The Locrian Ajax is “swift Ajax” twice and Meriones three times “the equal of swift Ares,” and the book brings in πόδας ταχύς, “swift of foot,” for Meriones, Achilles and Aeneas; it adds the phrase to the entry.
grc: [πόδας ταχὺ, πόδας ταχύν]
---

**Ajax son of Oileus.** His formula Ὀϊλῆος ταχὺς Αἴας names him as the first to know the god, “swift Ajax, the son of Oileus” (13.66; [[ajax-son-of-oileus]], [[seeing-the-gods]]), and as the man who never stands apart from Telamonian Ajax (13.701–702; [[oxen-at-the-plow]]).

**Meriones.** Three times “Meriones, the equal of swift Ares” (13.295, 13.328, 13.528; [[equal-of-ares]], [[meriones]]): as he takes up a spear from Idomeneus' hut, as he leads Idomeneus to the left of the army, and as he leaps on Deiphobus.

**Swift of foot.** πόδας ταχύς, “swift of foot,” has the shape of Achilles' πόδας ὠκύς ([[swift-footed]]), and it first appears in this book. Idomeneus greets “Meriones, son of Molus, swift of foot, dearest of my companions” (13.249; [[molus]]); Zeus wills victory to the Trojans to give glory “to Achilles, swift of foot” (13.348; [[achilles]], [[plan-of-zeus]]); and Idomeneus fears “Aeneas, swift of foot, coming on” (13.482; [[aeneas]]), while his own feet are failing him (13.512–515; [[half-gray]]). The phrase returns for Achilles and for Antilochus in later books (17.709, 18.2).
