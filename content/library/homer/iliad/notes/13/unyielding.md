Poseidon comes up out of the sea to urge on the Argives, “in the likeness of Calchas in form and in tireless voice” (13.45; [[calchas]], [[shaker-of-the-earth]], [[divine-epiphany]]). εἰσάμενος … δέμας καὶ ἀτειρέα φωνήν is the half-verse of a god in a man's likeness: it returns when Athena takes Phoenix's shape to rouse Menelaus over Patroclus (17.555) and Deiphobus' to deceive Hector (22.227). The epithet of the bronze that does not wear out is given to the voice the god borrows. In this book the son of Oileus knows the god, not by the voice, but by his gait as he goes away (13.70–72; [[seeing-the-gods]]). See [[poseidon]].
