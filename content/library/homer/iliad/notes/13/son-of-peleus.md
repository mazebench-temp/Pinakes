---
summary: Poseidon, in Calchas' likeness, names the cause of the Achaeans' plight: Agamemnon “dishonored the swift-footed son of Peleus.”
---

Achilles is named in the book's first rebuke by his father's name: the one wholly to blame may be Agamemnon, “because he dishonored the swift-footed son of Peleus” (13.111–113; [[agamemnon]], [[swift-footed]], [[aitios]]). ἀπητίμησε, “dishonored,” recalls the beginning of the poem, where Calchas named Agamemnon's dishonoring of Apollo's priest (ἠτίμησʼ, 1.94) as the cause of the plague (see the translation's note on 13.108–114). The speaker is Poseidon in the likeness of the same Calchas (13.45). Achilles is named twice more in the book, as the measure of Ajax in the standing fight (13.324) and as the hero whom Zeus wishes to glorify (13.348); Polydamas does not name him (13.746).
