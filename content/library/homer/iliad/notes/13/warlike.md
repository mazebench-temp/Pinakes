summary: Over Alcathous' body, “two warlike men beyond the others, Aeneas and Idomeneus, the equals of Ares,” are eager to cut each other's flesh.
---

The adjective of Ares and the comparison with Ares stand in consecutive verses: “and two warlike men beyond the others, Aeneas and Idomeneus, the equals of Ares, were eager to cut each other's flesh with the pitiless bronze” (13.499–501; [[aeneas]], [[idomeneus]], [[equal-of-ares]], [[pitiless-bronze]]). The narrator has just set them against each other in two similes, Idomeneus as a boar that waits for the hunters and Aeneas as a ram that the flock follows to water (13.470–475, 13.491–495). Idomeneus feared Aeneas' youth (13.481–486); the verse makes the two of them equals. See [[ares]].
