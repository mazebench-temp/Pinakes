---
summary: Idomeneus sends Meriones for “a heavy spear,” and in one exchange Deiphobus' heavy spear kills Ascalaphus, son of Ares, and Meriones pulls his own out of Deiphobus' arm.
---

Meriones has broken his spear on Deiphobus' shield, and Idomeneus ends their talk of courage: “but go to the hut and take a heavy spear” (13.294; [[meriones]], [[idomeneus]]). The next two uses come on the two sides of one exchange. Deiphobus throws at Idomeneus and misses, and strikes Ascalaphus, “the son of Enyalius; and the heavy spear went through his shoulder” (13.518–520; [[ascalaphus]], [[enyalius]]). As Deiphobus snatches Ascalaphus' helmet, Meriones wounds him in the arm, and leaping in again like a vulture he “pulled the heavy spear out from the top of his arm” (13.527–532; [[deiphobus]], [[like-vultures]]). In the same scene ὄβριμος is the epithet of “mighty Ares,” whose son has just fallen (13.521; [[obrimos]], [[ares]]). See [[spear]].
