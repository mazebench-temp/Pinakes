---
summary: Poseidon, in Calchas' likeness, shames the Achaeans in the rear with Thersites' word: “Weaklings, soon you will make some evil greater by this slackness.”
---

In his rebuke of the men in the rear, Poseidon turns from reproach to command with the plural πέπονες: “Weaklings, soon you will make some evil greater by this slackness. But let each of you set in his mind shame and blame” (13.120–122; [[poseidon]], [[aidos]], [[nemesis]]). The address was Thersites' in Book 2, “Weaklings, wretched disgraces” (2.235), and there it called the army to sail home. The god uses it to send the army back to the fight: the scorn is the same, the purpose the opposite. Just before, Poseidon has said that he would not quarrel with a man who held back from war if he were a wretch, “but with you I am indignant with all my heart” (13.118–119), since they are the best men in the army (13.117).
