summary: Meriones and Paris each let fly “a bronze-fitted arrow” in the same half-verse, and the Locrians have no “bronze-fitted helmets”; the book adds χαλκήρεʼ and χαλκήρεας.
grc: ["χαλκήρεʼ", χαλκήρεας]
---

Two arrows, one from each side, close the fighting around Menelaus. As Harpalion draws back, “Meriones let fly a bronze-fitted arrow” and strikes him in the buttock (13.650–652; [[meriones]], [[harpalion]]); Paris, angry for his guest-friend, “let fly a bronze-fitted arrow” at Euchenor and kills him (13.660–672; [[paris]], [[euchenor]], [[arrows]]). The verse-end χαλκήρεʼ ὀϊστόν is the same both times; the arrow is fitted with a bronze head. The Locrians, on the other hand, “had no bronze-fitted helmets with horsehair crests” (13.714; [[locrians]], [[horsehair-crested]]), and κόρυθας χαλκήρεας ἱπποδασείας is the verse-end of the helmets brought from Odysseus' storeroom (Od. 22.111, 22.145). The elided χαλκήρεʼ and the accusative plural χαλκήρεας are new to the entry.
