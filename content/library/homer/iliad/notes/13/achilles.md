---
summary: Achilles stays away, but the book measures the war by him. Poseidon, in Calchas' likeness, grants that Agamemnon may be to blame for dishonoring him; Idomeneus says that Ajax would not give way even to him, except in speed; Zeus wants victory for the Trojans to give him glory; and Polydamas fears “a man insatiable of war” who waits by the ships (13.113, 13.324–325, 13.347–350, 13.746–747).
refs: [13.350, 13.746-747]
---

**Dishonored.** Poseidon, in the likeness of Calchas, grants that Agamemnon may be wholly to blame, “because he dishonored the swift-footed son of Peleus” (13.113; see [[agamemnon]] and [[swift-footed]]). ἀπητίμησε recalls the word of Thetis' plea, that Agamemnon had dishonored her son (ἠτίμησεν, 1.507; see [[time]]).

**Swift of foot.** Idomeneus, measuring Telamonian Ajax, says that he would not give ground “even to Achilles, breaker of men, / in the standing fight at least; but in speed no one can contend with him” (13.324–325; see [[ajax]] and [[breaker-of-men]]). Each time the book names Achilles it names his speed: he is ποδώκης, “swift-footed” (13.113), πόδας ταχύς, “swift of foot” (13.348), and unmatched ποσί, “in speed” (13.325; see [[son-of-peleus]]).

**The will of Zeus.**

{{quote:a4f934a6-484e-5c1b-ad67-7f2f322a8f1d}}

Thetis asked Zeus to give the Trojans the mastery until the Achaeans honored her son (1.505–510; see [[thetis-supplicates-zeus]] and [[plan-of-zeus]]). The narrator adds a limit she did not ask for, that the Achaean army is not to perish, and makes the glory hers as well as her son's (see [[thetis]], [[kydos]] and [[zeus]]). καρτερόθυμος, “stout-hearted”, is used elsewhere in Homer of Diomedes and of Heracles (5.277; Od. 21.25).

**The man by the ships.** Polydamas does not name him: “For I / fear that the Achaeans may pay back yesterday's / debt, since beside the ships there waits a man insatiable of war, / who I think will no longer hold back altogether from battle” (13.744–747; see [[polydamas]] and the translation's note on 13.745–746). ἆτος πολέμοιο, “insatiable of war”, is elsewhere the title of Ares (5.388, 5.863, 6.203; see [[insatiable-of-war]] and [[ares]]). Polydamas ends with Achilles' own words: μάχης σχήσεσθαι ὀΐω closes the verse in which Achilles told Ajax that at his hut and black ship he thought Hector, eager as he was, would be held back from battle (9.654–655; see [[the-embassy-to-achilles]]). After Achilles has shown himself at the ditch, Polydamas will name him: as long as this man was angry with Agamemnon, the Achaeans were easier to fight (18.257–258).
