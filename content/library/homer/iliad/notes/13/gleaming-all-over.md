---
summary: Idomeneus' captured spears stand in his hut “against the gleaming walls,” in the phrase of the walls of Olympus.
---

“Spears, if you want them, you will find, one or twenty, / standing in my hut against the gleaming walls, / Trojan spears, which I take from the men I kill” (13.260–262; [[idomeneus]], [[hut]], [[bossed]]). The phrase πρὸς ἐνώπια παμφανόωντα ends the verse in which the Hours leaned the goddesses' chariot “against the gleaming walls” of Olympus (8.435), and in the Odyssey the servants of Menelaus lean the chariot of Telemachus against the gleaming walls of his house (Od. 4.42). The hut of a captain at Troy has walls that shine like those of a god's house or a king's. See [[stripping-the-dead]].
