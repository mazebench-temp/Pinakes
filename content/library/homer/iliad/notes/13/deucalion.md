---
summary: Deucalion is named twice in Book 13. Meriones addresses Idomeneus as “son of Deucalion” (13.307), and Idomeneus, boasting of his descent from Zeus, makes Deucalion the son of Minos and his own father (13.451–452).
---

Δευκαλίδη, “Son of Deucalion” (13.307), is Meriones' word to Idomeneus as the two set out together (see [[meriones]] and [[idomeneus]]); the narrator gave the patronymic in Book 12 (12.117). Over the body of Alcathous, Idomeneus names his father in the line that runs from Zeus: “and Minos in turn fathered a son, noble Deucalion, and Deucalion fathered me, a lord over many men in wide Crete” (13.451–453; see [[minos]] and [[genealogy-in-battle]]). ἀμύμων, “noble” (see [[blameless]]), is the only epithet the Iliad gives him; outside the patronymic, it names him only here.
