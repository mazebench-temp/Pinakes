---
summary: The formula of the fairest daughter is given to Cassandra, whom Othryoneus asked for, and turned by Idomeneus into a mock offer of Agamemnon's daughter; Hector calls Paris “best in looks” again, in the verse of Book 3.
---

Othryoneus of Cabesus “asked for the most beautiful in form of the daughters of Priam, / Cassandra, without bride-price” (13.365–366; [[othryoneus]], [[cassandra]], [[bride-price]]), and promised to drive the Achaeans from Troy. The formula that named Laodice in Books 3 and 6 (3.124, 6.252; [[laodice]]) names her sister here, in the first mention of Cassandra in the poem. When Idomeneus has killed him, he mocks the bargain with an offer of the same kind: “we would give you the most beautiful in form of the daughters of the son of Atreus, / bringing her from Argos, to marry her, if with us / you would sack the well-peopled citadel of Ilios” (13.378–380; [[idomeneus]], [[agamemnon]]). Agamemnon had offered Achilles whichever of his three daughters he would take, “without bride-price” (9.144–148).

Hector's rebuke of Paris begins with the verse of Book 3: “Evil Paris, best in looks, mad for women, seducer” (13.769 = 3.39; [[evil-paris]], [[paris]]). In Book 3 Paris had shrunk back from Menelaus; now he has been fighting on the left, and he answers that Hector is blaming one who is not to blame (13.765–777).
