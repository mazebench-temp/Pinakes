---
summary: Idomeneus breaks off his talk of courage with Meriones: “let us no longer talk like this, like children, / standing here”, in the verse that Aeneas will speak to Achilles in Book 20.
grc: [νηπύτιοι]
en: [like children]
---

Idomeneus and Meriones have been talking at the huts of spoils and of courage, and Idomeneus stops: “But come, let us no longer talk like this, like children, / standing here, lest someone be overbearingly indignant” (ἀλλʼ ἄγε μηκέτι ταῦτα λεγώμεθα νηπύτιοι ὣς / ἑσταότες, 13.292–293; see [[the-brave-man-in-ambush]] and [[nemesis]]). νηπύτιος is a longer form of νήπιος, and it keeps the word's two senses, a child and a fool (see the article above): talk instead of action is what children do. Aeneas speaks the same verse to Achilles in Book 20, after he has recited his lineage, “standing in the middle of the battle” (20.244–245), and has already told him not to hope to frighten him with words, “like a child” (20.200–201). In the battle of the gods νηπύτιε becomes the gods' own taunt, “fool” (21.410, 21.441, 21.474, 21.585). Here the word is a rebuke of the speaker as much as of his hearer, and it ends the talk: Meriones takes a spear and the two go into the war (13.294–305).
