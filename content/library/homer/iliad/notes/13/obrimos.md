---
summary: “Mighty Ares” is named twice within a hundred lines, once as the force spent in the spear that killed Alcathous, once as the god on Olympus who has not yet heard that his son Ascalaphus is dead.
---

**The spear.** Idomeneus' spear is fixed in the heart of Alcathous, “and the heart, still quivering, shook even the butt of the spear; and then mighty Ares let its might go” (13.442–444; [[alcathous]], [[idomeneus]]). The verse returns twice, each time after a spear has stuck quivering in the ground (16.613, 17.529), so the might that Ares lets go is the spear's: its force spent, it comes to rest. Some take μένος as the dying man's strength (see the translation's note on 13.443–444).

**The god.** When Deiphobus kills Ascalaphus, the narrator turns to the father: “And loud-voiced, mighty Ares had not yet heard at all that his son had fallen in the fierce battle, but he was sitting on the peak of Olympus beneath golden clouds, held back by the counsels of Zeus” (13.521–524; [[ascalaphus]], [[briepyos]], [[boule]]). ὄβριμος, the god's weight, stands beside βριήπυος, his voice, a word found only here. In Book 15 Hera tells him, naming Ascalaphus as the son whom ὄβριμος Ἄρης claims as his own, and he arms to avenge him until Athena sits him down (15.110–142). See [[ares]], and [[heavy-spear]] for the ὄβριμον ἔγχος of 13.294, 13.519 and 13.532.
