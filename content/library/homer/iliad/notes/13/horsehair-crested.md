summary: The helmets with horsehair crests touch in the close ranks, Peisander strikes the ridge of Menelaus' horsehair-crested helmet, and the Locrians fight without such helmets; the book adds ἱππόκομοι and ἱπποδασείας.
grc: [ἱππόκομοι, ἱπποδασείας]
---

In the close ranks around the two Ajaxes the men stand so near that “the helmets with horsehair crests touched with their bright ridges / when the men nodded, so close did they stand to one another” (13.132–133 = 16.216–217; [[battle-order]], [[helmet]]). The nominative ἱππόκομοι is new to the entry, whose genitive Book 12 used of the helmets struck at Menestheus' tower (12.339).

Peisander's axe falls on Menelaus' helmet: “Peisander struck the ridge of his horsehair-crested helmet, / up under the crest itself” (13.614–615; [[peisander-killed-by-menelaus]], [[axe]], [[menelaus]]). κόρυθος φάλον ἱπποδασείης is the phrase of the first killings of Books 4 and 6, where the spear struck the ridge and went into the brow (4.459–461, 6.9–11); here Menelaus is unhurt, and Peisander dies.

The Locrians “had no bronze-fitted helmets with horsehair crests” (13.714; [[locrians]], [[bronze-fitted]]), and so they fight from behind with bows and slings. The accusative plural ἱπποδασείας is new to the entry; the same verse-end names the helmets brought from Odysseus' storeroom in the fight with the suitors (Od. 22.111, 22.145).
