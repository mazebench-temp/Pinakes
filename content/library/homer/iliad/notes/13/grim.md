---
summary: Poseidon in both his disguises uses λυγρός of men, “a wretch” and “very wretched men”; the brave man prays to close with the enemy “in grim battle,” and the sons of Cronus make “grievous sorrows” for the heroes.
grc: [λυγρῶν, λυγρῇ]
en: [wretch, wretched]
---

**Of men.** Twice Poseidon uses the word of a man without courage. As Calchas he tells the Achaeans in the rear, “I would not / quarrel with a man who held back from war / if he were a wretch; but with you I am indignant with all my heart” (13.117–119; [[nemesis]]). As Thoas he tells Idomeneus that two men together can be of use: “Even the prowess of very wretched men counts when it is joined, / and we two would know how to fight even against brave men” (13.237–238; [[joined-prowess]], [[arete]], [[brave]]). Of a man, λυγρός is the worthless fighter, set against the brave. The genitive λυγρῶν is new to the entry.

**Of battle and sorrow.** In the ambush the brave man's color does not change, “but he prays to close with them quickly in grim battle” (13.284–286; [[the-brave-man-in-ambush]]). The phrase ἐν δαῒ λυγρῇ, whose dative is new to the entry, returns in Andromache's lament, where Hector was not gentle in battle (24.739). And the two sons of Cronus, with their minds set on different sides, “were making grievous sorrows for the heroes” (13.345–346; [[zeus]], [[poseidon]]), with the neuter plural of the sorrows of Phaenops (κήδεα λυγρά, 5.156).
