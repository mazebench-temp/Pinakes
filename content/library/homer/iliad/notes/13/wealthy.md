“There was one Euchenor, son of Polyidus the seer, rich and brave, who lived in his house at Corinth” (13.663–664; [[euchenor]], [[polyidus-the-seer]], [[corinth]]). The Catalogue gives his city the same word, “wealthy Corinth” (2.570). He knew his fate from his father: to perish of a grievous sickness at home, or to be beaten down by the Trojans at the ships; and by going to Troy he avoided both the sickness and “the grievous fine of the Achaeans” (13.665–670; [[nousos]], [[ker-doom]]). A rich man could pay to stay at home: Echepolus of Sicyon, to whom Zeus had given great wealth, ἄφενος, the noun from which ἀφνειός is formed, gave Agamemnon a mare so as not to follow him to Ilios (23.296–299). Paris kills Euchenor with an arrow under the jaw (13.671–672; [[paris]]).
