---
summary: The patronymic names Agamemnon twice, in Poseidon's concession that he may be to blame and in Idomeneus' mock offer of his daughter, and Menelaus five times, in his fights with Helenus, Peisander and Harpalion.
---

**Agamemnon.** Poseidon, in Calchas' likeness, allows that “the one wholly to blame is the hero, son of Atreus, wide-ruling Agamemnon, because he dishonored the swift-footed son of Peleus” (13.111–113; [[agamemnon]], [[wide-ruling]], [[aitios]], [[son-of-peleus]]). Idomeneus offers the dead Othryoneus “the most beautiful in form of the daughters of the son of Atreus” (13.378), to mock Priam's promise of Cassandra (13.365–369; [[othryoneus]], [[cassandra]]); Agamemnon named his three daughters in his offer to Achilles (9.144–145).

**Menelaus.** From 13.581 on the patronymic belongs to Menelaus, five times in seventy lines. Grief seizes “the son of Atreus, Menelaus, good at the war cry” when Deipyrus falls (13.581; [[good-at-the-war-cry]]); “the son of Atreus, Menelaus, good at the war cry,” pins Helenus' hand to his bow (13.593); against Peisander “the son of Atreus missed” (13.605) and then drew his sword (13.610); and Harpalion stabs “the middle of the shield of the son of Atreus” (13.646; [[harpalion]]). See [[menelaus]] and [[atreidae]].
