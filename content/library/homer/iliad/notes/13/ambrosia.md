---
summary: In the cave between Tenedos and Imbros Poseidon unyokes his horses and sets ambrosial fodder before them, in the verse with which Iris fed the horses of Ares' chariot on Olympus (13.35; 5.369).
---

“unyoked them from the chariot, and set ambrosial fodder before them / to eat” (λύσας ἐξ ὀχέων, παρὰ δʼ ἀμβρόσιον βάλεν εἶδαρ, 13.35–36; see [[chariot-journey]] and [[poseidon]]). The verse is the one in which Iris, bringing the wounded Aphrodite to Olympus in Ares' chariot, fed his horses (5.369). The gods' horses eat the gods' food: the Simoeis made ambrosia spring up for Hera's horses to graze on (5.777), and the Hours tied them “at their ambrosial mangers” (8.434). Poseidon's horses eat theirs under the sea, held by golden hobbles until their lord returns (13.36–38).
