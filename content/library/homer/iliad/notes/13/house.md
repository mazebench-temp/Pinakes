summary: Poseidon's golden house stands in the depths of the sea at Aegae (13.21–22). The other houses of Book 13 are those the dead have left: the hall where Hippodameia was loved by her parents, the house where Alcathous reared Aeneas, and the house at Corinth where Euchenor was fated to die of sickness had he not sailed (13.430–431, 13.466, 13.664–667).

en: [lived in his house]

**A god's house.** At Aegae Poseidon's “glorious house is built in the depths of the sea, / golden, gleaming, imperishable forever” (13.21–22; see [[aegae]] and [[poseidon]]). There he yokes his horses for the drive to Troy (see [[chariot-journey]]). In the Odyssey his journey ends there: “and he came to Aegae, where his glorious house is” (Od. 5.381).

**Houses left behind.** The obituaries of the book remember the houses of the dead. Hippodameia's father and mother loved her “with all their hearts / in their hall” (13.430–431; see [[hippodameia-daughter-of-anchises]]), and Alcathous, her husband, reared Aeneas “in his house when you were small” (13.466; see [[alcathous]] and [[aeneas]]). Euchenor, “rich and brave, who lived in his house at Corinth”, chose death at Troy over a grievous sickness “in his own halls” (13.664–670; see [[euchenor]]). Harpalion followed his father to Troy “and did not come back again to his native land” (13.645; see [[harpalion]]).
