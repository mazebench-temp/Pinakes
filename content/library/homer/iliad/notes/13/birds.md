---
summary: Birds in Book 13 are the quarry of the hawk to which Poseidon is likened (13.64), the bird of omen that flies on the right as Ajax challenges Hector (13.821–823), and the eaters of the dead with whom Hector threatens Ajax (13.831); Calchas, whose likeness Poseidon takes, is “the prophet and augur” (13.70).
grc: [ὄρνεον]
---

**The hawk's quarry.** Poseidon darts from the two Ajaxes like a hawk that rises from a sheer rock “to chase some other bird” (διώκειν ὄρνεον ἄλλο, 13.64; see [[the-hawk-from-the-rock]] and [[hawk]]). ὄρνεον, a form of ὄρνις, occurs only here.

**The augur.** The son of Oileus knows that the speaker was a god and “not Calchas, the prophet and augur” (θεοπρόπος οἰωνιστής, 13.70; see [[calchas]] and [[divination]]). οἰωνιστής is the reader of birds, the title of the Mysian Ennomus in the Catalogue (2.858).

**The omen.** As Ajax ends his challenge to Hector, “a bird flew by on the right, an eagle flying high” (13.821–822), and the Achaeans take heart from the omen (οἰωνῷ, 13.823; see [[eagle]]).

**The eaters of the dead.** Hector answers that Ajax will “glut the dogs and the birds of the Trojans” with his fat and his flesh, fallen beside the ships (13.831–832), the fate of the unburied that the proem gives to the dead of Achilles' wrath (1.4–5; see [[dogs]]).
