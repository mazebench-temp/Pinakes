---
summary: Euchenor escapes “the hateful sickness” of his father's prophecy and dies at Troy, where “hateful darkness seized him.”
---

The seer Polyidus had told his son Euchenor that he would die of a grievous sickness at home or at the hands of the Trojans among the ships, “so he avoided at once the grievous fine of the Achaeans / and the hateful sickness, so that he would not suffer sorrows in his heart” (13.663–670; [[euchenor]], [[nousos]]). Paris' arrow strikes him under the jaw, “and quickly his spirit / went from his limbs, and hateful darkness seized him” (13.671–672; [[paris]]). The two uses frame his choice: he went to the war to escape the hateful sickness, and the hateful darkness took him there. The half-verse is the one of Phaestus' death in Book 5 (5.47).
