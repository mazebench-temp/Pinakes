---
summary: Menelaus is “glorious” three times in a few verses, as Helenus' arrow and Peisander's spear both fail against his armor.
---

The line-end formula Μενελάου κυδαλίμοιο follows Menelaus through his fight with two Trojans. Helenus' arrow glances off: “so from the corselet of glorious Menelaus / the bitter arrow glanced far away and flew off” (13.591–592; [[bitter]], [[helenus]]). Then “Peisander went straight for glorious Menelaus” (13.601), and “Peisander stabbed the shield of glorious Menelaus, / but could not drive the bronze right through” (13.606–607; [[peisander-killed-by-menelaus]]). Each time the epithet stands beside a weapon that fails against him, and each time Menelaus answers: he drives his spear through Helenus' hand into the bow, and he kills Peisander with his sword (13.593–595, 13.610–618; [[menelaus]], [[kydos]]).
