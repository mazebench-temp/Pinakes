summary: Poseidon fetters the “shining limbs” of Alcathous, and “shining Hector” answers Ajax's challenge; the Epeians are φαιδιμόεντες, a lengthened form of the word found only here, which the book adds.
grc: [φαιδιμόεντες]
---

**Limbs.** Poseidon beats Alcathous down at the hands of Idomeneus, “bewitching his bright eyes and fettering his shining limbs” (13.434–435; [[alcathous]], [[phaeinos]]). φαίδιμα γυῖα are the limbs of a warrior in his strength, and the book names them at the moment the god binds them: Alcathous can neither flee nor swerve, but stands still like a pillar or a tall tree (13.436–438; [[like-a-pillar]]).

**Hector.** The formula φαίδιμος Ἕκτωρ comes once, after Ajax's challenge and the eagle on the right: “and shining Hector answered” (13.823; [[hector]], [[high-flying]]). The answer is his wish to be the son of Zeus and Hera (13.824–832).

**The Epeians.** Among the peoples who hold Hector off at the ships are φαιδιμόεντες Ἐπειοί, “the shining Epeians” (13.686; [[epeians]]). φαιδιμόεις is φαίδιμος lengthened with the suffix -όεις, as ὀξυόεις is ὀξύς lengthened ([[sharp]]); it is found only here in Homer, and Strabo quotes the verse with it (*Geography* 9.5.7).
