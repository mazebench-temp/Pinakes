summary: Hector leads the Trojans at the end of the book “with a wondrous din,” in the verses with which he led them at the wall, and in the simile before it the winds mingle with the sea “with a wondrous din,” θεσπεσίῳ ὁμάδῳ, a form the book adds.
grc: [θεσπεσίῳ]
---

The book's last movement is loud. The Trojans go into battle like a blast of winds that comes down to the plain under the thunder of Zeus, “and with a wondrous din mingles with the sea” (13.795–797; [[loud-roaring-sea]]): θεσπεσίῳ ὁμάδῳ, the din with which the Trojans will flee when Patroclus puts out the fire on the ships (16.293–295). After Ajax's challenge and Hector's answer, “So he spoke, and led the way, and they followed with him with a wondrous din, and the army shouted behind” (13.833–834; [[hector]]), the verses with which Hector led the Trojans to the wall (12.251–252). The Argives shout in answer, “And the roar of both reached the upper air and the rays of Zeus” (13.835–837). See [[the-battle-at-the-ships]].
