summary: In the close ranks shield presses on shield, and in the single fights shields hold. Deiphobus' bull's-hide shield breaks Meriones' spear; Idomeneus crouches under his round shield of hides and bronze, fitted with two rods, and Deiphobus' spear grazes it and flies over; Antilochus covers the fallen Hypsenor with his, and Menelaus' broad shield stops Peisander and Harpalion. Hector comes on behind his shield, thick with hides and beaten over with bronze (13.130–131, 13.156–164, 13.404–420, 13.551–565, 13.606–647, 13.802–807).

**Shield on shield.** In the close ranks around the two Ajaxes the men stand “fencing spear with spear, shield with overlapping shield; / shield pressed on shield” (13.130–131; see [[battle-order]] and the translation's note on 13.130–135).

**Deiphobus' shield.** Deiphobus comes on in high spirits, holding his round shield in front of him, “stepping lightly forward and advancing under cover of his shield” (ὑπασπίδια προποδίζων, 13.156–158; see [[deiphobus]] and [[balanced-on-every-side]]). Meriones' spear strikes it and snaps at the socket before it can drive through, and Deiphobus holds the shield “of bull's hide” away from him, afraid (13.159–164; see [[of-bulls-hide]] and [[spear]]). ὑπασπίδια, “under the shield”, is said again of Hector at the end of the book (13.807) and of Meriones in Book 16 (16.609).

**Idomeneus' shield.** When Deiphobus throws at Idomeneus, the shield saves him:

{{quote:09c56d45-3e0d-5eab-ac1c-8717bff1eec5}}

It is round and made of oxhides and bronze, in the words with which the defenders of the wall “fenced the battlements with oxhides” (12.263; see [[battlements]]). The κανόνες are the rods fixed across the inside, by which the shield was held, as on Nestor's golden shield (8.193; see the translation's note on 8.192–193). The shield “gave a dry ring” as the spear grazed it (καρφαλέον … ἄϋσεν, 13.409), a harsh sound like that of the helmets struck by stones at the wall (12.160) and of Alcathous' bronze tunic torn by Idomeneus' spear (13.441; see the translation's note on 13.409, 13.441). The spear passes over and kills Hypsenor (see [[hypsenor-son-of-hippasus]]).

**Covering the fallen.** Antilochus runs and stands over Hypsenor “and covered him with his shield” (13.420), the verse with which Ajax covered the wounded Teucer in Book 8 (8.331; see [[fighting-over-the-body]]). Later Antilochus' “broad, gleaming shield” takes the Trojans' thrusts while he strips Thoon, for Poseidon guards him (13.550–555; see [[antilochus]] and [[panaiolos]]), and Adamas' spear breaks in it and half of it stays fixed there (13.560–565).

**Menelaus' shield.** Peisander stabs “the shield of glorious Menelaus, / but could not drive the bronze right through; / for the broad shield held it” (13.606–608), and Harpalion's thrust fails in the same words (13.646–647). Peisander's axe comes “from under his shield” (13.611; see [[axe]]).

**Shields on shoulders.** Idomeneus' companions stand close beside him, “leaning their shields against their shoulders” (13.488), ready for Aeneas' attack, as the Achaeans stood around Ajax in Book 11 (11.593). Telamonian Ajax has companions who take over his shield “whenever weariness and sweat came over his knees” (13.709–711; see [[the-shield-of-ajax]]). When Aeneas strikes Aphareus in the throat, “his shield fell with it, / and his helmet” (13.543–544); the verb, ἑάφθη, is obscure (see the translation's note on 13.543).

**Hector's shield.** Ajax stabs “the boss of his shield” and pushes Hector back from Amphimachus' body (13.192–193; see [[bossed]]). At the end of the book Hector leads the Trojans, “and he held in front of him his shield, balanced on every side, / thick with hides, and much bronze was beaten over it” (13.803–804), and makes trial of the battalions “as he stepped forward under cover of his shield” (13.807; see [[hector]]).

**Without shields.** The Locrians have no “well-rounded shields” (13.715; see [[well-rounded]] and [[sling]]). In Idomeneus' hut “bossed shields” stand among the spoils (13.264), and in the clash on the left the eyes are blinded by the gleam of bronze from “the bright shields” (13.342; see [[bronze]]).
