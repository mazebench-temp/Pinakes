---
summary: Poseidon comes down “from the rocky mountain” of Samothrace, and his cave lies between Tenedos and “rocky Imbros”: παιπαλόεις again, in two forms the book adds.
grc: [παιπαλόεντος, παιπαλοέσσης]
---

At the start of Poseidon's journey, “At once he came down from the rocky mountain, striding swiftly on his feet” (13.17–18; [[samothrace]], [[poseidon]]): the peak of Thracian Samos from which he has watched the battle (13.12–14; [[wooded]]). He leaves his horses in a wide cave in the depths of the sea, “between Tenedos and rocky Imbros” (13.32–33; [[tenedos]], [[imbros]]). As in Book 12 (12.168), the adjective is παιπαλόεις, not πετρήεις, and the translation renders it “rocky.” Imbros keeps the epithet when Iris plunges into the sea between Samos and Imbros in the last book (24.78). See [[chariot-journey]].
