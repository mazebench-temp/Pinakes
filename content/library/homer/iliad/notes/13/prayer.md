summary: No prayer is spoken in Book 13. The brave man in an ambush “prays to close with them quickly” (13.286), and Ajax tells Hector that he will soon pray to Zeus and the other gods for horses swifter than hawks (13.817–819); Menelaus' address to father Zeus is a complaint (13.631–639). The book's other words of the family of εὔχομαι are boasts and claims (13.54, 13.327, 13.417, 13.447, 13.619).
grc: [ἀρήσῃ]
# Hector "claims" (εὔχετʼ, 13.54); "triumph" (εὖχος, 13.327); "boasting", "boast", "in triumph" (13.417, 13.447, 13.619).
except: [13.54, 13.327, 13.417, 13.447, 13.619]
---

**Prayers named.** Idomeneus says that the brave man in an ambush, once he has taken his place, “prays to close with them quickly in grim battle” (ἀρᾶται, 13.286; see [[ambush]]): the verb of prayer for a fighter's wish. Ajax tells Hector that “the time is near when, in flight, / you will pray to father Zeus and the other immortals / that your fine-maned horses may be swifter than hawks” (13.817–819; see [[ajax]] and [[hector]]), and an eagle flies on the right as he speaks (see [[divination]]).

**A complaint to Zeus.** Menelaus, standing over Peisander, turns to the god: “Father Zeus, they say that you surpass in wisdom all others, / men and gods; and all these things come from you. / How you favor men who are arrogant” (13.631–633; see [[menelaus]] and [[zeus]]). He asks for nothing. It is a reproach, like his cry when his sword broke on Paris' helmet, “Father Zeus, no other god is more baneful than you” (3.365), and like Asius' “Father Zeus, so you too, it seems, have become a lover of lies” (12.164; see [[lover-of-lies]]).

**Boasts.** The other words of the family are boasts and claims. Hector “claims to be the son of mighty Zeus” (13.54); Idomeneus speaks of holding out “triumph” to another, or another to them (εὖχος, 13.327); the Argives grieve at Deiphobus' boasting (13.417), and Idomeneus asks him whether three for one is a fair exchange, “since you boast like this” (13.447); Menelaus speaks “in triumph” over Peisander (13.619). See [[euchomai]].
