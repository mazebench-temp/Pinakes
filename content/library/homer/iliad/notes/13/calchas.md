summary: Calchas does not appear in Book 13, but his likeness does. Poseidon takes his form and voice to rouse the two Ajaxes and the army, and the son of Oileus knows that “this is not Calchas, the prophet and augur”. It is the last time the Iliad names him.
refs: [13.69]
passages:
  - 13.43-82 | Poseidon and the two Ajaxes
---

Poseidon comes up out of the sea “in the likeness of Calchas in form and in tireless voice” (13.45; see [[poseidon]] and [[poseidon-rallies-the-achaeans]]). The son of Oileus sees through it: one of the gods, “in the likeness of the seer”, bids them fight, but “this is not Calchas, the prophet and augur” (13.69–70; see [[ajax-son-of-oileus]] and [[seeing-the-gods]]). θεοπρόπος, “prophet”, is the word of Agamemnon's taunt in Book 1, “And now among the Danaans you prophesy and declare” (θεοπροπέων, 1.109; see [[theopropion]]), and οἰωνιστής, “augur”, is said elsewhere only of the Mysian Ennomus (2.858, 17.218; see [[divination]]).

The disguise suits the speech. In Book 1 Calchas laid the plague on Agamemnon's dishonoring of Apollo's priest (1.93–96); in his likeness Poseidon grants that Agamemnon may be wholly to blame for dishonoring Achilles (13.111–113; see [[who-is-to-blame]]). The seer whose words began the quarrel is named for the last time as a mask for a god.
