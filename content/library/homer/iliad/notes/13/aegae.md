**The house.** τρὶς μὲν ὀρέξατʼ ἰών, τὸ δὲ τέτρατον ἵκετο τέκμωρ: three strides, and the fourth brings him to his goal (13.20; see [[three-times-and-a-fourth]]). The house is κλυτὰ δώματα, “glorious house”, as in the Odyssey, where Poseidon, after wrecking Odysseus' raft, drives to Aegae, “where his glorious house is” (Od. 5.381). It lies βένθεσι λίμνης, “in the depths of the sea”, the words used of the cave between Tenedos and Imbros where he leaves his horses (13.32; see [[imbros]]). ἄφθιτα αἰεί, “imperishable forever”, is said of Agamemnon's scepter, made by Hephaestus (2.46, 2.186), and Hephaestus' own house is “imperishable, starry” (18.370). At Aegae Poseidon keeps his horses, his golden armor and his golden whip, and he yokes his chariot in the verses with which Zeus yoked his for the drive to Ida (13.23–26 ≈ 8.41–44; see [[chariot-journey]]).

**From Aegae to the Achaeans.** In Book 8 Hera reproached him: “Yet they bring you gifts to Helice and Aegae, / many and pleasing; so you should will them victory” (8.203–204). He would not fight against Zeus (8.208–211; see [[helice]] and [[hera]]). Now, while Zeus looks away, he sets out from Aegae itself to help the Achaeans, though in secret (13.351–357; see [[poseidon-rallies-the-achaeans]] and [[poseidon]]).

**Which Aegae.** Strabo, who takes the Aegae of this passage for the one in Euboea, quotes its verse together with the verse of the cave, as if they ran on: “Aegae, where his glorious house is built in the depths of the sea … there Poseidon … halted his horses” (13.21, 13.34; *Geography* 8.7.4). In the poem Poseidon yokes his horses at Aegae and halts them at the cave. The Homeric *Hymn to Apollo* names an Aegae among the lands that Leto visited, after Euboea and before Peiresiae and Peparethus, in the northwestern Aegean (*Hymn to Apollo* 31–32).
