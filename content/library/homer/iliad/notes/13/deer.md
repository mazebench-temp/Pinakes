summary: The deer of Book 13 is the Trojans' past: Poseidon, in the likeness of Calchas, says that they used to be like deer quick to flee, the prey of jackals, leopards and wolves, without courage, and now they are fighting at the ships (13.101–107).
grc: [ἐλάφοισιν]
---

φυζακινῇς ἐλάφοισιν ἐοίκεσαν, “were like deer quick to flee” (13.102). The deer is the Iliad's animal of flight and cowardice: Achilles gave Agamemnon “the heart of a deer” (1.225), and Agamemnon asked the Argives who hung back why they stood “dazed, like fawns” (4.243). Here the god turns the image on the enemy, and makes it a reproach to his own side. The Trojans were once deer “which through the forest are the prey of jackals and leopards and wolves, straying aimlessly, without courage, with no joy of battle in them” (13.102–104), and they would not face the Achaeans “not even a little” (13.106). Now the deer have come to the ships, and Poseidon calls it “a great wonder” (13.99; see [[timid-deer]], [[jackal]], [[leopard]] and [[wolves]]).
