---
summary: Poseidon, as Calchas, tells the two Ajaxes to remember their courage “and not chilling rout”; the book adds the phrase κρυεροῖο φόβοιο.
grc: ["κρυεροῖο φόβοιο"]
---

Poseidon, in the likeness of Calchas, comes first to the two Ajaxes: “Ajaxes, you two will save the army of the Achaeans / if you remember your courage, and not chilling rout” (13.47–48; [[the-two-ajaxes]], [[poseidon]], [[alke]]). The adjective is κρυερός, from the same κρύος (“icy cold”) as κρυόεις, and the thought is the one of the opening of Book 9, where Panic was the “companion of chilling Rout” (φόβου κρυόεντος, 9.2; [[rout]]). Courage and rout are set side by side, as Courage and Rout are on the aegis (5.739–740; [[courage]]). The phrase κρυεροῖο φόβοιο is new to the entry; κρυερός describes lament in the last book (24.524).
