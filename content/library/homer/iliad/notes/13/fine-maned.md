---
summary: Ajax foretells that Hector will pray, in flight, for his “fine-maned horses” to be swifter than hawks.
---

Ajax answers Hector's advance with a challenge: the Achaeans have hands to defend the ships, and Troy will fall first; “And for you yourself, I say, the time is near when, in flight, / you will pray to father Zeus and the other immortals / that your fine-maned horses may be swifter than hawks, / the horses that will carry you to the city, raising the dust of the plain” (13.817–820; [[ajax]], [[hector]], [[hawk]], [[father-zeus]]). In Book 11 the fine-maned horses carried Hector's chariot into the battle (11.531); Ajax looks ahead to the day when they will carry him out of it. It comes in Book 16, when Hector's swift horses carry him back across the ditch and leave his men behind (16.367–369). See [[horses]].
