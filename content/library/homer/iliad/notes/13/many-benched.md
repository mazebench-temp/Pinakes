---
summary: Polydamas asks the Trojan leaders to consider whether they should “fall upon the many-benched ships” or come away from them unharmed.
---

With the Trojans scattered among the ships and the Achaean left holding firm, Polydamas urges Hector to call the best men together, “and then we might consider every plan, whether we should fall upon the many-benched ships, if a god is willing to give us the power, or after that come away from the ships unharmed” (13.741–744; [[polydamas]], [[boule]], [[kratos]]). ἐν νήεσσι πολυκλήϊσι πέσωμεν, “fall upon the many-benched ships,” turns a phrase of flight into one of attack. In Book 2 Athena warned Odysseus that the Achaeans would flee, “tumbling into your many-benched ships” (2.175), and in Book 15 Zeus foretells that, fleeing, they will fall among the ships of Achilles (15.63–64). It is the first time a Trojan gives the Achaean ships this epithet. Hector agrees to the council (13.748–753). See [[ships]].
