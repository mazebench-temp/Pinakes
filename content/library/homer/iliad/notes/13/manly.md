---
summary: When Hector leaves him to rally the Trojans, the leaders run to “the son of Panthous, manly Polydamas”; the book adds the accusative ἀγαπήνορα.
grc: [ἀγαπήνορα]
---

Hector accepts Polydamas' counsel, tells him to keep the best men there, and rushes off like a snowy mountain; “And they all hurried to the son of Panthous, manly Polydamas, when they heard the voice of Hector” (13.756–757; [[polydamas]], [[panthous]], [[like-a-snowy-mountain]]). It is the only time Polydamas has this epithet. In Book 8 it was given to Nestor's attendant Eurymedon (8.114), and in Book 23 it will describe Idomeneus, in the verse “Meriones, the attendant of manly Idomeneus” (23.113, 23.124). The counselor whom the narrator elsewhere calls ἀμύμων (12.88, 13.790; [[blameless]]) is given a warrior's word as the Trojans gather to him. See [[hector]].
