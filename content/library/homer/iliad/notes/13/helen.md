---
summary: Helen is named once, in the verse that calls Paris “the husband of Helen of the lovely hair” (13.766), and once without her name, when Menelaus reproaches the Trojans for carrying off his wedded wife after they had been welcomed by her (13.626–627).
refs: [13.626-627]
---

Standing over Peisander, Menelaus speaks of her as κουριδίην ἄλοχον, “my wedded wife”, carried off with many possessions “wantonly, after you had been welcomed by her” (13.626–627; see [[menelaus]], [[xenia]] and [[husband-of-helen]]). He names the god the Trojans offended, “Zeus who thunders loud, / god of guests” (13.624–625; see [[who-thunders-loud]]), and Paris is still at war for her: Hector finds him “the husband of Helen of the lovely hair” (13.766; see [[paris]] and [[lovely-haired]]).
