---
summary: Polites leads his wounded brother Deiphobus “out of grim-sounding war” to his chariot.
---

Meriones' spear has struck Deiphobus in the arm, “And Polites, / his own brother, put his arms around his waist / and led him out of grim-sounding war, until he came to his horses” (13.533–535; [[polites]], [[deiphobus]]). The chariot carries him toward the city, “groaning heavily / in his distress” (13.538–539). In Book 11 Cebriones told Hector that they were fighting “at the edge of grim-sounding war” while Ajax drove the other Trojans in confusion, and Eurypylus feared that Ajax would not escape from it (11.523–526, 11.590). Here one brother brings another out of it. See [[the-battle-at-the-ships]].
