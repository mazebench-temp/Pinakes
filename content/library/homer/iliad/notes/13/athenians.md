**Their leaders.** “Stichius and brilliant Menestheus, / the leaders of the Athenians” (13.195–196) carry the body of the Epeian Amphimachus back to the army (see [[amphimachus]], [[stichius]] and [[menestheus]]). Where the wall was lowest, the Athenians are named after the five peoples who hold Hector off: “these were the picked men of the Athenians” (13.689), led by Menestheus, with Pheidas, Stichius and Bias (13.689–691; see [[pheidas]] and [[bias-the-athenian]]). προλελεγμένοι, “picked”, from προλέγω, “choose out before others”, occurs only here in Homer.

**The Ionians.** The list of peoples just before has no Athenians, but it has “the Ionians with trailing tunics” (13.685), the only Ionians in Homer, and the list of leaders that follows names Athenians, Epeians and Phthians, as if in answer to it (see [[ionians]]). Strabo read the verse so: Attica was anciently called Ionia and Ias, and “the poet, when he says ‘There the Boeotians and Ionians’, means the Athenians” (*Geography* 9.1.5; see [[strabo]]). In the Catalogue the Athenians come under their own name, with fifty ships, and Ajax stations his men from Salamis beside their battalions (2.546–558; see [[athens]] and [[solon-and-salamis]]).
