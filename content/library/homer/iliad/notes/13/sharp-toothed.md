---
summary: The two Ajaxes carry off Imbrius' body as two lions snatch a goat “away from sharp-toothed dogs.”
---

When Teucer has killed Imbrius and Hector has been driven from the body, the two Ajaxes carry it off: “As two lions snatch a goat away from sharp-toothed dogs and carry it through the thick brushwood, high above the ground, holding it in their jaws, so the two Ajaxes, helmeted men, held him high and stripped his armor” (13.198–202; [[the-two-ajaxes]], [[imbrius]], [[helmeted]]). As in Book 10 the epithet belongs to a simile in the dual (10.360–364). There the two Achaeans were the dogs, hunting Dolon; here they are the lions that rob the dogs of their prey, and the Trojans who could not keep the body are the dogs. See [[dogs]] and [[lion]].
