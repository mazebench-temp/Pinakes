Μηριόνης δʼ ἐξ αὖτις ἐπάλμενος αἰγυπιὸς ὣς: “And Meriones, leaping in again like a vulture” (13.531). The bird is the αἰγυπιός, the large bird of prey of the similes, not the γύψ of the curses and threats (see the article). In Book 7 the gods sat like αἰγυπιοί on a high oak to watch the men (7.58–61); here the vulture swoops, takes, and is gone, as Meriones pulls his spear from the top of Deiphobus' arm and draws back among his companions (13.531–533; see [[like-vultures]], [[meriones]] and [[deiphobus]]).
