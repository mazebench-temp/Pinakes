---
summary: Poseidon gives Hector the formula as he warns the Achaeans that Hector is fighting at the ships, and Menelaus has it twice in his fight with Helenus.
---

**Hector.** Poseidon, in the likeness of Calchas, ends his rebuke of the Achaeans with the danger before them: “Hector, good at the war cry, is fighting beside the ships / in his strength, and he has broken the gates and the long bar” (13.123–124; [[hector]], [[the-gates-of-the-wall]]). The formula belongs above all to Menelaus and Diomedes; here a god on the Achaean side gives it to the enemy, to shame the Achaeans with his strength.

**Menelaus.** When Helenus kills Deipyrus, “grief seized the son of Atreus, Menelaus, good at the war cry” (13.581; [[deipyrus]], [[achos]]), and he goes at Helenus with his spear. When Helenus' arrow has glanced off his corselet, “the son of Atreus, Menelaus, good at the war cry, struck / the hand that held the polished bow” (13.593–594; [[helenus]], [[bitter]]). The two verses frame his fight with Helenus. See [[menelaus]].
