“But soon he found, on the left of the tearful battle, / brilliant Alexander, the husband of Helen of the lovely hair, / encouraging his companions and urging them on to fight” (13.765–767; [[paris]], [[lovely-haired]], [[brilliant]]). The line-end formula, which names Paris when he arms for the duel (3.329), when he refuses to give Helen back (7.355) and when he shoots with his bow (8.82, 11.369, 11.505), stands here in the accusative, as the object of Hector's search. It names him by the woman over whom the war is fought just before Hector reproaches him as “mad for women” (13.769; [[evil-paris]]). The accusative Ἑλένης πόσιν is new to the entry.
