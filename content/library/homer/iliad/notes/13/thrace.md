grc: [Θρῄκης]
passages:
  - 13.298-305 | Ares and Rout go out from Thrace
---

τὼ μὲν ἄρʼ ἐκ Θρῄκης Ἐφύρους μέτα θωρήσσεσθον, “these two arm themselves to go out from Thrace to the Ephyri” (13.301). Thrace is Ares' own country: in the Odyssey, freed from Hephaestus' net, he flies off to Thrace (Od. 8.361; see [[ares]] and [[rout]]). The simile sends the god and his son from their home to a war between two peoples, where they give glory to one side or the other (13.302–303), and like them Meriones and Idomeneus, “leaders of men”, go into the war (13.304–305; see [[ares-and-rout]], [[idomeneus]] and [[meriones]]). Strabo found it hard to say whom the poet meant, since the people of Gyrton were once called Phlegyae and those of Crannon Ephyri, both in Thessaly (*Geography* 9.5.21; see [[ephyri]] and [[phlegyes]]).

The book's first lines also look toward Thrace: Zeus turns his eyes to “the land of the horse-herding Thracians” (13.4), and Poseidon watches from the peak of Samothrace, “the Thracian” (13.12–13; see [[thracians]] and [[samothrace]]).
