---
summary: Idomeneus and Ajax both open a challenge with the vocative δαιμόνιε, “strange one”, the address of a man whose conduct seems to come from some power outside him.
grc: [δαιμόνιε]
---

δαιμόνιος, “strange one, possessed”, is the adjective of δαίμων, and as an address it marks a person whose behavior the speaker cannot account for, as if a god were driving it (see the article above). In Book 13 it opens two challenges. Idomeneus, having killed Alcathous, answers Deiphobus' boast: “Strange one, come and stand against me yourself, / so that you may see what kind of offspring of Zeus I am” (δαιμόνιʼ, 13.448–449; see [[idomeneus]] and [[genee]]). At the end of the book Ajax calls to Hector as he comes on testing the ranks: “Strange one, come near; why do you try to frighten the Argives / like this?” (δαιμόνιε, 13.810–811; see [[ajax]] and [[deos]]). Elsewhere in the Iliad the address passes between husband and wife, brothers and comrades, and once from Helen to Aphrodite (3.399); only in this book is it spoken to an enemy, and both speakers go on to say who they are (see [[strange-one]]).
