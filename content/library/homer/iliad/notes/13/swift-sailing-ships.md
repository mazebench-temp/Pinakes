---
summary: In Poseidon's two speeches as Calchas, the swift-sailing ships are what the Ajaxes must keep Hector from and what the slack army will not defend.
---

Both uses are in the speeches of Poseidon in the likeness of Calchas, and in both the ships are what is at stake. To the two Ajaxes: “then you might hold him back from the swift-sailing ships, for all his haste, even if the Olympian himself is rousing him” (13.57–58; [[the-two-ajaxes]], [[olympian]]). To the men in the rear, of the army at strife with its leader: they “are not willing to defend the swift-sailing ships, but are being killed among them” (13.109–110; [[aitios]]). At the wall the Achaeans had shot stones in defense of themselves, their huts “and the swift-sailing ships” (12.155–156); now the ships are the line itself. See [[ships]] and [[poseidon]].
