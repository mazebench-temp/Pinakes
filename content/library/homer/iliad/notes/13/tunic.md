---
summary: Alcathous' tunic is of bronze, “which before had kept destruction from his body”, and Idomeneus' spear breaks it (13.438–441); the Ionians at the ships are “the Ionians with trailing tunics” (13.685).
---

**A tunic of bronze.** Idomeneus stabs Alcathous, whom Poseidon has fettered where he stands, in the middle of the chest, “and broke the tunic around him, / of bronze, which before had kept destruction from his body; / and then it rang dry as it was torn about the spear” (13.439–441; see [[alcathous]] and [[idomeneus]]). χιτὼν χάλκεος is the corselet under another name, as the Achaeans are χαλκοχίτωνες, “bronze-armored” (13.255, 13.272; see [[corselet]] and [[bronze-armored]]). The linen tunic is worn beneath it: in the duels of Books 3 and 7 the spear goes through the shield and the corselet, and “straight on beside the flank it cut through the tunic”, but the man swerves and escapes (3.357–360 = 7.251–254). Alcathous cannot swerve.

**Trailing tunics.** Among the peoples who hold Hector off from the ships are “the Ionians with trailing tunics” (Ἰάονες ἑλκεχίτωνες, 13.685), the only Ionians in Homer, with the long dress in which the Homeric Hymn to Apollo has the Ionians gather at Delos (*Hymn to Apollo* 147; see [[ionians]] and [[with-trailing-tunics]]).
