summary: Deiphobus fears the spear of “wise-hearted Meriones,” and Deiphobus' boast stirs the heart of “wise-hearted Antilochus” most of all.
---

Both uses fall in Deiphobus' fights. He holds his shield away from him, and “feared in his heart the spear of wise-hearted Meriones” (13.163–164; [[deiphobus]], [[meriones]], [[of-bulls-hide]]). When he boasts over Hypsenor that Asius will be glad of an escort to the house of Hades, grief comes on the Argives, “and most of all he stirred the heart of wise-hearted Antilochus” (13.417–418; [[antilochus]], [[hypsenor-son-of-hippasus]]), who runs to stand over the fallen man. A little later Meriones is also “wise,” πεπνυμένος, in his talk with Idomeneus (13.254; [[wise]]).
