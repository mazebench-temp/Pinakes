summary: Book 13 has few swords. The close ranks stab at Hector with swords and double-pointed spears, Helenus kills Deipyrus with a great Thracian sword that strikes off his helmet, and Menelaus draws his silver-studded sword against Peisander in the verse with which he drew it against Paris (13.147, 13.576–580, 13.610–618).
grc: [ξίφεσιν]
---

**In the close ranks.** The Achaeans who face Hector at the close-packed battalions push him back, “stabbing at him with swords and double-pointed spears” (13.147; see [[battle-order]] and [[spear]]). The pair of weapons returns in the fighting at the ships and over Patroclus (14.26, 15.278, 15.712, 16.637, 17.731).

**The Thracian sword.** “And Helenus struck Deipyrus close at hand on the temple with a sword, / a great Thracian sword, and struck off his helmet” (13.576–577; see [[helenus]], [[deipyrus]] and [[helmet]]). The sword is the weapon of the close fight (σχεδόν, “close at hand”). Another Thracian sword is a prize at Patroclus' games: Achilles offers “this silver-studded sword, a fine Thracian one”, which he took from Asteropaeus (23.807–808; see [[asteropaeus]] and [[thrace]]). The poem does not say how Helenus came by his.

**Menelaus' sword.** “Then the son of Atreus drew his silver-studded sword / and leapt at Peisander” (13.610–611), in the verse with which he drew it against Paris in their duel (3.361; see [[silver-studded]] and [[menelaus]]). In Book 3 the sword shattered on the ridge of Paris' helmet (3.362–363). Here Peisander's axe falls on Menelaus' helmet, and Menelaus strikes Peisander on the forehead above the base of the nose, so that the bones crack (13.611–617; see [[axe]] and [[peisander-killed-by-menelaus]]). The weapon of the blow is not named, but it is the sword he has just drawn.
