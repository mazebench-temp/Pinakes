---
summary: Imbrius came back to Ilios “when the curving ships of the Danaans came”; the book adds the nominative ἀμφιέλισσαι.
grc: [ἀμφιέλισσαι]
---

Teucer's first victim, Imbrius, son of Mentor, had married a bastard daughter of Priam and lived in Pedaeum, “but when the curving ships of the Danaans came, / he came back to Ilios, and stood out among the Trojans, / and lived beside Priam” (13.170–176; [[imbrius]], [[pedaeum]], [[medesicaste]]). The coming of the Achaean fleet turns his life, and the same verses tell the story of Melanippus, son of Hicetaon, who kept his cattle in Percote until the ships came and then lived with Priam, honored like his children (15.547–551). The nominative ἀμφιέλισσαι, in the verse-end νέες ἤλυθον ἀμφιέλισσαι, is new to the entry. See [[ships]].
