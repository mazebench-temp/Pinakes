---
summary: Polydamas' counsel at the ships pleases Hector in the verse that told his first counsel at the ditch: “and his safe counsel pleased Hector”.
grc: [μῦθος]
---

When Polydamas has advised Hector to draw back and call the best men to council, “So spoke Polydamas, and his safe counsel pleased Hector” (ἅδε δʼ Ἕκτορι μῦθος ἀπήμων, 13.748; see [[polydamas]], [[hector]] and [[boule]]). The verse is the one that told how Hector took Polydamas' first advice in Book 12, to leave the chariots at the ditch (12.80), and both times Hector at once leaps down from his chariot (12.81 = 13.749). μῦθος here is a speech of counsel, and ἀπήμων, “causing no harm”, is said elsewhere of a fair wind and of men who come home unharmed, as Polydamas hopes the Trojans may “come away from the ships unharmed” (ἀπήμονες, 13.744; see the translation's note on 12.80). Hector did not like Polydamas' second advice in Book 12 and will not like his last in Book 18, where he answers, “Polydamas, what you say is no longer pleasing to me” (18.285 = 12.231; see [[different-gifts]]).
