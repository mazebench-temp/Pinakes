---
summary: Poseidon rallies the Argives in secret, in Calchas' likeness, with the cry “Shame, Argives, young men!”, and urges them on until the book ends with their answering shout as the best of the Trojans come on (13.44, 13.95, 13.351–352, 13.677–678, 13.835–836).
---

**The god's army.** Three times the name comes with Poseidon's work. He “urged on the Argives, coming up out of the deep sea” (13.44), “went among the Argives and spurred them on, / having risen secretly out of the gray sea” (13.351–352), and urged them on so hard that “himself defended them with his strength” (13.677–678; see [[poseidon]]).

**Shame.** His rebuke opens with αἰδὼς Ἀργεῖοι, κοῦροι νέοι, “Shame, Argives, young men!” (13.95; see [[aidos]]). Hera, in the likeness of Stentor, shamed them with the same two words in Book 5 (5.787), and Agamemnon when they were penned in by their ships in Book 8 (8.228); Ajax will do so again at the ships (15.502). Here a god in a seer's likeness asks them to set in their minds “shame and blame” (13.122; see [[nemesis]]).

**At the ships.** When Deiphobus boasts over Hypsenor, “grief came upon the Argives at his boasting” (13.417; see [[deiphobus]]). Hector does not know that on the left of the ships his men “were being cut down / by the Argives” (13.675–676), and when he looks for his companions, some lie dead “at the hands of the Argives” (13.763; see [[hector]]). Ajax asks him why he tries to frighten the Argives (13.810–811), and Hector answers that this day brings evil “to the Argives, / to all of them” (13.828–829; see [[ajax]]).

**The answer.** The book ends with the two armies shouting. The Trojans come on with a wondrous din, “And the Argives on the other side shouted in answer, and did not forget / their courage, but awaited the best of the Trojans as they came on” (13.835–836; see [[alke]]).
