summary: The Trojans follow Hector to the ships like a flame or a storm wind, but break against the close ranks around the two Ajaxes; on the left Idomeneus, Meriones, Antilochus and Menelaus cut them down, and at the breach the Locrians' arrows throw them into confusion. They would have fallen back to Ilios but for Polydamas, and at the end they come on again behind Hector like waves before a storm.

**Like fire.** “And the Trojans, like a flame or a storm wind, in a body, / raging without end, followed Hector, son of Priam, / roaring and crying aloud”, hoping to take the ships and kill the best men beside them (13.39–42; see [[hector]] and [[like-a-flame]]). ἄβρομοι αὐΐαχοι, both words found only here, are taken with the ἀ- that strengthens, not the one that denies (see [[roaring-and-crying-aloud]] and the translation's note on 13.41).

**Deer once.** Poseidon, speaking as Calchas, calls it a great and terrible wonder that the Trojans have come to the ships, “who before now / were like deer quick to flee”, the prey of jackals, leopards and wolves; they would not face the Achaeans even a little, and now they fight far from the city, through the fault of the Achaean leader and the slackness of his army (13.99–110; see [[timid-deer]] and [[poseidon-rallies-the-achaeans]]).

**At the close ranks.** They drive forward in a body behind Hector and stop against the battalions around the two Ajaxes; Hector calls on them, with the Lycians and Dardanians, to stand by him (13.136–155; see [[battle-order]], [[lycians]] and [[dardanians]]).

**On the left.** When they see Idomeneus “like a flame in his courage”, they all come at him, calling to one another (13.330–332), and the dead begin: Othryoneus, Asius, Alcathous, Oenomaus, Thoon, Adamas, Peisander, Harpalion (see [[the-battle-at-the-ships]]). Standing over Peisander, Menelaus speaks to them all: “you overbearing Trojans, insatiable of the dread war cry” (13.621), who stole his wife from the house that welcomed them; Zeus, he complains, favors “men who are arrogant, / the Trojans, whose might is always reckless”, and “the Trojans are insatiable of battle” (13.620–639; see [[menelaus]], [[hybris]] and [[satiety-of-all-things]]).

**At the breach.** Hector holds the place where he broke in, but there the Locrians shoot from behind the armed men, “and of the joy of battle / the Trojans remembered nothing; for the arrows threw them into confusion” (13.721–722; see [[locrians]]). “Then wretchedly from the ships and the huts / the Trojans would have retreated to windy Ilios”, had not Polydamas spoken (13.723–725; see [[polydamas]] and [[would-have-had-not]]). He describes them as they are: some stand apart with their arms, others fight, “fewer against more, scattered among the ships” (13.737–739).

**The storm.** At the end they go on “like a blast of grievous winds” that falls on the sea with a wondrous din and raises wave behind wave, “so the Trojans, some in front in close array, and others behind, / gleaming with bronze, followed with their leaders” (13.795–801; see [[the-storm-and-the-waves]]). When Hector has answered Ajax, they follow him “with a wondrous din”, and the Argives shout in answer (13.833–837).
