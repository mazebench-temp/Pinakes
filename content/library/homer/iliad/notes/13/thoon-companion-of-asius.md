summary: Antilochus watches a Thoon as he turns away, leaps on him and cuts the vein that runs up the back to the neck; he falls on his back, stretching out both hands to his companions, and Antilochus strips him under Poseidon's guard (13.545–555). The poem does not say whether this is the Thoon of 12.140.

“And Antilochus, watching for Thoon as he turned around, / stabbed him with a rush” (13.545–546; see [[antilochus]]). The wound is told exactly: the spear cuts through the whole vein “that runs right up the back and reaches the neck” (13.547), and the dying man falls on his back, ἄμφω χεῖρε φίλοις ἑτάροισι πετάσσας, “stretching out both his hands to his dear companions” (13.549). As Antilochus strips him, the Trojans stab at his shield from every side, but Poseidon keeps every point from him (13.550–555; see [[poseidon]] and [[stripping-the-dead]]). If this Thoon is Asius' companion of Book 12, he dies in the same fighting as Asius and his son Adamas (see [[asius]] and [[adamas]]).
