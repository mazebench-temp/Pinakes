---
summary: Mecisteus and Alastor carry Hypsenor, Antilochus' companion, to the ships, in the four verses with which they carried the wounded Teucer in Book 8 (13.420–423 = 8.331–334).
refs: [13.421, 13.423]
passages:
  - 13.417-423 | Antilochus covers Hypsenor
---

Deiphobus' spear, meant for Idomeneus, strikes Hypsenor, son of Hippasus, “in the liver beneath the midriff, and at once loosened his knees”, and Deiphobus boasts that he has given Asius an escort to the house of Hades (13.411–416; see [[hypsenor-son-of-hippasus]] and [[deiphobus]]). Antilochus stands over his companion and covers him with his shield, and “two trusty companions stooped under him, Mecisteus, son of Echius, and brilliant Alastor, and carried him, groaning heavily, to the hollow ships” (13.420–423; see [[antilochus]], [[echius]], [[alastor-who-carries-teucer]] and [[trusty]]). The verses are the rescue of Teucer from Book 8 (8.331–334). “Loosened his knees” is the verse of a killing, and the boast speaks of death; yet the man carried off is “groaning heavily” (see [[loosened-his-limbs]] and [[groaning-heavily]]). Either Hypsenor is dying, not dead, or the block of verses has brought its groaning with it from Book 8. He is not named again (see the translation's note on 13.412–423).
