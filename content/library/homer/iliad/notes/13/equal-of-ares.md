summary: Meriones is “the equal of swift Ares” three times, Aeneas and Idomeneus together are “the equals of Ares,” and Hector leads the Trojans “the equal of Ares, ruin of mortals”; the book adds the plural ἀτάλαντοι Ἄρηϊ.

en: [the equals of]
---

**Meriones.** The fuller form θοῷ ἀτάλαντος Ἄρηϊ, “the equal of swift Ares,” names Meriones three times. When Idomeneus sends him for a spear, “Meriones, the equal of swift Ares, / quickly took up a bronze spear from the hut” (13.295–296), and the two go out to war like Ares and his son Rout (13.298–305; [[ares-and-rout]]): the epithet comes just before the simile. It returns when he leads the way to the left (13.328), and when he leaps on Deiphobus, who has just killed Ascalaphus, son of Ares, and strikes his arm (13.526–529; [[meriones]], [[ascalaphus]]). The war god himself has not yet heard that his son has fallen (13.521–525; [[ares]]).

**Aeneas and Idomeneus.** Over the body of Alcathous, “two warlike men beyond the others, / Aeneas and Idomeneus, the equals of Ares, / were eager to cut each other's flesh with the pitiless bronze” (13.499–501; [[aeneas]], [[idomeneus]], [[warlike]]). The plural ἀτάλαντοι Ἄρηϊ is used only here, and it gives the comparison to a Trojan and an Achaean at once.

**Hector.** At the end of the book “Hector led them, the equal of Ares, ruin of mortals” (13.802), with the half-verse βροτολοιγῷ ἶσος Ἄρηϊ that named him in Book 11 (11.295; [[ruin-of-mortals]], [[hector]]).
