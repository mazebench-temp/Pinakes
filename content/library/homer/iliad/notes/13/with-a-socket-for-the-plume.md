---
summary: Ascalaphus' helmet, “with its socket for the plume,” falls to the ground with a clang when Meriones strikes the arm of Deiphobus, who has snatched it from the body.
---

Deiphobus has killed Ascalaphus, son of Ares, and “snatched the shining helmet from Ascalaphus, but Meriones, the equal of swift Ares, leapt on him and struck his arm with his spear, and from his hand the helmet with its socket for the plume fell to the ground with a clang” (13.527–530; [[deiphobus]], [[ascalaphus]], [[meriones]], [[phaeinos]], [[stripping-the-dead]]). The prize falls from the hand that has just taken it. τρυφάλεια is the helmet's name in this formula, as for Diomedes' and Hector's (5.182, 11.352–353); Deipyrus' τρυφάλεια is struck from his head a few lines later (13.576–579).
