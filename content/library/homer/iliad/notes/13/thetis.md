summary: Thetis is named in Book 13 in the narrator's account of the will of Zeus, who wants victory for the Trojans to give glory to Achilles, but not the Achaean army's destruction, “but was giving glory to Thetis and her stout-hearted son” (13.347–350).
# Θέτιν (13.350), the accusative, only here so far.
grc: [Θέτιν]
---

ἀλλὰ Θέτιν κύδαινε καὶ υἱέα καρτερόθυμον, “but was giving glory to Thetis and her stout-hearted son” (13.350; see [[zeus]], [[kydos]] and [[achilles]]). The glory is the honor she begged of Zeus in Book 1: “honor my son”, and give the Trojans the mastery until the Achaeans honor him (1.505–510; see [[thetis-supplicates-zeus]] and [[plan-of-zeus]]). The narrator gives the will of Zeus a limit she did not ask for: he does not wish “the Achaean army to perish before Ilios” (13.349). Zeus recalls her supplication himself in Book 15, when he says that he will not end his anger until the wish of the son of Peleus is fulfilled, as he promised “on that day when the goddess Thetis touched my knees” (15.72–77).
