Two mothers. Alcathous' wife Hippodameia, the eldest of Anchises' daughters, was the one “whom her father and lady mother loved with all their hearts in their hall; for she surpassed all the girls of her age in beauty and handiwork and wits” (13.430–432; [[hippodameia-daughter-of-anchises]], [[anchises]], [[alcathous]]). πατὴρ καὶ πότνια μήτηρ is a formula of the household: Andromache has neither father nor lady mother left (6.413), and Cleopatra's father and lady mother, in their halls, gave her the name Alcyone (9.561–562). Hippodameia's mother is not named. At the end of the book Hector answers Ajax with an impossible wish: “If only I were the son of aegis-bearing Zeus all my days, and queen Hera had borne me” (13.825–826; [[hector]], [[hera]], [[son-of-zeus]]). The verse has the shape of his wish in Book 8 to be “immortal and ageless all my days” (8.538–539), with Hera's motherhood in place of immortality (see the translation's note on 13.825–828).
