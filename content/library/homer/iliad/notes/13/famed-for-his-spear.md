---
summary: Idomeneus, who leads the Cretans with the epithet in the Catalogue, is “famed for his spear” three times in the book of his greatest fighting.
---

Book 13 gives the epithet only to Idomeneus, whom the Catalogue named with it (2.645, 2.650; [[idomeneus]]), and it marks the turns of his day. “And Idomeneus, famed for his spear, met him” (13.210): Poseidon meets him as he comes from a wounded companion, and rouses him in the likeness of Thoas. Deiphobus tells Aeneas that Alcathous, who reared him, has been killed: “and Idomeneus, famed for his spear, has slain him and stripped him of his armor” (13.465–467; [[alcathous]]). And when Aeneas comes on, Idomeneus waits for him like a boar in the mountains: “so Idomeneus, famed for his spear, stood his ground, and did not give way” (13.476; [[the-bristling-boar]]). Each time it is the compound δουρικλυτός, which Book 5 first gave him (5.45).
