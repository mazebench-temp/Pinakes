---
summary: In Polydamas' counsel “far-seeing Zeus” puts a good mind in one man's breast, as a god gives another the works of war.
---

Polydamas begins his last counsel at the ships with the gifts of the gods: “For to one man a god has given the works of war, / to another dancing, to another the lyre and song, / and in the breast of another far-seeing Zeus puts a mind / that is good, and many men profit from it” (13.730–733; [[different-gifts]], [[polydamas]]). The first gifts come from “a god,” and Zeus is named only for the last, the gift of mind. Hector has the first, Polydamas implies, and should listen to the man who has the last (13.726–729, 13.735–747; [[boule]]). Hector agrees (13.748). See [[zeus]].
