---
summary: The two Ajaxes stand side by side like two oxen pulling “the jointed plow” through a fallow field.
---

The son of Oileus no longer stands apart from Telamonian Ajax, “but as in a fallow field two wine-dark oxen pull the jointed plow / with equal spirit, and around them, / at the roots of their horns, much sweat bubbles up; / and only the polished yoke keeps the two apart / as they strain down the furrow, and the plow cuts to the end of the field; / so the two stood close beside each other” (13.701–708; [[oxen-at-the-plow]], [[the-two-ajaxes]], [[wine-dark]], [[polished]]). It is the second of the epithet's three uses, each with a plow in fallow land. In Book 10 the jointed plow measured a distance (10.351–353); here it gives the picture of two men bound to one task and kept apart only by the yoke between them. See [[cattle]].
