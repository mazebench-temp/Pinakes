summary: The whole-verse formula brings Peisander and Menelaus face to face: “And when they had come close, advancing against each other”, Menelaus' spear misses, Peisander's breaks on his shield, and the fight turns to sword against axe (13.604–618).
---

“And when they had come close, advancing against each other, / the son of Atreus missed, and his spear turned aside” (13.604–605; see [[menelaus]] and [[peisander-killed-by-menelaus]]). As in the duels of Book 5, the verse lifts one encounter out of the general fighting (see the entry). Menelaus throws first and misses; Peisander stabs his shield and breaks his spear at the socket; and the two close with sword and axe (13.606–618; see [[spear]], [[sword]] and [[axe]]). The end is already known: the narrator has turned to Menelaus himself to tell him that an evil fate was leading Peisander “to be beaten down by you, Menelaus, in grim combat” (13.602–603; see [[apostrophe]]).
