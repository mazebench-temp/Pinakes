---
summary: Hades is named once in Book 13, in Deiphobus' boast over Hypsenor: Asius, “even as he goes to the house of Hades, the strong gatekeeper”, will be glad, since Deiphobus has given him an escort.
---

Idomeneus has killed Asius before his horses (13.384–393; see [[asius]]). Deiphobus, throwing at Idomeneus, misses him and strikes Hypsenor, son of Hippasus, “in the liver beneath the midriff, and at once loosened his knees” (13.411–412).

{{quote:7556c548-64ea-5402-aa73-a828488da333}}

The verse-end πυλάρταο κρατεροῖο, “the strong gatekeeper”, returns in the Odyssey, when Epicaste hangs herself and goes down “to the house of Hades, the strong gatekeeper” (*Odyssey* 11.277); in Book 8 Eurystheus sent Heracles “to the house of Hades the gatekeeper” to fetch his hound (8.367; see [[gatekeeper]] and [[heracles-and-cerberus]]). The dead pass through the gates of Hades (5.646, 23.71), and the god keeps them.

πομπός, “escort”, is in the Iliad otherwise the word for Hermes, whom Zeus sends to escort Priam to Achilles' hut (24.153, 24.182, 24.461; see [[hermes]]). In the Odyssey Hermes leads the souls of the suitors down to the meadow of asphodel (*Odyssey* 24.1–14). Deiphobus' escort is a dead man sent with the dead. Yet the poem leaves Hypsenor's death in doubt: Antilochus stands over him and covers him with his shield, and two companions carry him to the ships “groaning heavily”, in the verses that carried the wounded Teucer from the field (13.420–423 = 8.331–334; see the translation's note and [[hypsenor-son-of-hippasus]]).
