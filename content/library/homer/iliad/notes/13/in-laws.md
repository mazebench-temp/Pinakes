**Son-in-law and brother-in-law.** Alcathous, whom Idomeneus kills, “was the son-in-law of Anchises, / and had married the eldest of his daughters, Hippodameia” (γαμβρὸς δʼ ἦν Ἀγχίσαο, 13.428–429; see [[alcathous]], [[anchises]] and [[hippodameia-daughter-of-anchises]]). γαμβρός names any man who has married into a family: to the bride's father he is a son-in-law, to her brothers a brother-in-law (see the article above). Deiphobus uses the word in its second sense when he finds Aeneas at the back of the throng: “now you must indeed / defend your brother-in-law, if any grief for your kin touches you. / But follow me; let us defend Alcathous, who in former days, / being your brother-in-law, reared you in his house when you were small” (γαμβρῷ ἀμυνέμεναι, εἴ πέρ τί σε κῆδος ἱκάνει … γαμβρὸς ἐὼν ἔθρεψε, 13.463–466; see [[aeneas]] and [[deiphobus]]). κῆδος, “care, grief”, is here the grief a man feels for his own people, as the translation renders it. Aeneas, nursing his anger against Priam, is moved by it at once (13.468–469; see [[menis]]).

**A stepmother's kin.** Medon, the bastard son of Oileus, lived in Phylace, “since he had killed a man, / a kinsman of his stepmother Eriopis, whom Oileus had as wife” (γνωτὸν μητρυιῆς Ἐριώπιδος, 13.696–697; see [[medon]], [[eriopis]] and [[exile-and-refuge]]). μητρυιή, “stepmother”, is found elsewhere in the Iliad only in Dione's story of Ares imprisoned by the sons of Aloeus, whose stepmother Eeriboea told Hermes where he was (5.385–391), and when these verses return at Medon's death (15.336). A killing among his father's kin by marriage drove him from his father's land.
