---
summary: Helen is “of the lovely hair” in the formula that names Paris as her husband, when Hector finds him on the left of the battle.
---

Hector looks for his brothers and allies along the line, “But soon he found, on the left of the tearful battle, / brilliant Alexander, the husband of Helen of the lovely hair, / encouraging his companions and urging them on to fight” (13.765–767; [[husband-of-helen]], [[paris]], [[helen]], [[tearful]]). The formula introduces Paris in Books 3, 7, 8 and 11; here it is in the accusative, just before Hector's rebuke (13.769; [[evil-paris]]). It is the only time Helen is named in the book. Menelaus speaks of her once, as “my wedded wife,” whom the Trojans carried off with many possessions (13.626).
