---
summary: As Antilochus strips Thoon, the Trojans stab from every side at his “broad, gleaming shield,” and Poseidon keeps their spears from his flesh.
---

When he has killed Thoon, “Antilochus sprang on him and began to strip the armor from his shoulders, looking about him; and the Trojans, standing around, one from here, one from there, stabbed at his broad, gleaming shield, but they could not get through to graze the tender flesh with the pitiless bronze of Antilochus” (13.550–554; [[antilochus]], [[thoon-companion-of-asius]], [[tender]], [[pitiless-bronze]]). It is not the shield that saves him: “for Poseidon the earth-shaker guarded the son of Nestor all around, even among many missiles” (13.554–555; [[poseidon]]). In Book 11 Paris shot Diomedes in the foot as he was taking “the gleaming corselet” from the body of Agastrophus (11.373–377); a man who strips the dead is exposed, and here a god stands between Antilochus and the spears.
