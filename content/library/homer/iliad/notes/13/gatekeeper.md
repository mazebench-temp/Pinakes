---
summary: Deiphobus boasts that Asius, going down to the house of Hades, “the strong gatekeeper,” will be glad of the escort he has sent him.
en: [gatekeeper]
---

Deiphobus' spear, thrown at Idomeneus, flies over his shield and strikes Hypsenor, and Deiphobus boasts: “Asius does not lie unavenged after all; no, I tell you, / even as he goes to the house of Hades, the strong gatekeeper, / he will be glad in his heart, since I have given him an escort” (13.414–416; [[deiphobus]], [[asius]], [[hades]]). The phrase is the one of Heracles' journey for the hound (8.367), with κρατεροῖο, “strong,” added to the gatekeeper, as in the Odyssey's verse for Epicaste (Od. 11.277; [[krateros]]). The escort, πομπός, is the man he has just struck, Hypsenor, son of Hippasus, whom two companions carry to the ships “groaning heavily” (13.411–423; [[hypsenor-son-of-hippasus]]); whether he dies of the wound the poem does not make clear. Idomeneus answers the boast by killing Alcathous and asking whether three for one is a fair exchange (13.445–447). The translation's “gatekeeper” is added to the entry's English forms.
