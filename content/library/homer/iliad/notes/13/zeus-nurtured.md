---
summary: Alcathous enters the book as “the dear son of Aesyetes, whom Zeus had nurtured”: the epithet of kings is given to his father.
---

“Then the dear son of Aesyetes, whom Zeus had nurtured, the hero Alcathous” (13.427–428; [[alcathous]], [[aesyetes]]): the genitive διοτρεφέος names the father, as in the verse that named Menestheus “the dear son of Peteos, whom Zeus has nurtured” in Book 12 (12.355; [[menestheus]], [[peteos]]). The poem says nothing more of Aesyetes, and whether he is the old man from whose tomb the Trojan lookout watched (2.793) is not said. The title opens the account of Alcathous' marriage into the house of Anchises and of his death, bound by Poseidon (13.428–444; [[hippodameia-daughter-of-anchises]]). See [[basileus]].
