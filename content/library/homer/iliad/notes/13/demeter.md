---
summary: Demeter is named once in Book 13, in a phrase for bread: Telamonian Ajax, says Idomeneus, would give way to no man who was mortal and “ate the grain of Demeter” (13.322).
grc: [Δημήτερος]
---

Idomeneus tells Meriones that the middle of the line is safe with the two Ajaxes and Teucer, and praises the son of Telamon (see [[ajax]] and [[idomeneus]]):

{{quote:8d2de3f6-1818-5c27-95c6-56ad940e1f3b}}

Δημήτερος ἀκτή, “the grain of Demeter”, is meal or bread, the goddess's gift. To eat it is to be mortal. The gods “do not eat bread, they do not drink sparkling wine, / and so they are bloodless, and are called immortals” (5.341–342; see [[ichor]] and [[gods-and-mortals]]), and men are “the mortals who eat the fruit of the field” (6.142; see [[who-eat-the-fruit-of-the-field]]). The man to whom Ajax would not give way is any man who eats bread and can be broken by bronze and stones; Idomeneus grants Achilles the advantage over Ajax only in speed (13.324–325; see [[achilles]]).

The phrase recurs once in Homer, when Lycaon begs Achilles for his life: “with you first I tasted the grain of Demeter”, on the day Achilles took him captive (21.76–77). Hesiod uses it of the harvest the earth bears (*Works and Days* 32, 466, 597, 805; *Shield* 290). The genitive Δημήτερος is found in the Iliad only in these two verses; the Catalogue has Δήμητρος (2.696).
