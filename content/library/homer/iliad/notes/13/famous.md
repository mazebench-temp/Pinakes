---
summary: Poseidon's “glorious house” stands in the depths of the sea at Aegae; the book adds the phrase κλυτὰ δώματα.
grc: ["κλυτὰ δώματα"]
---

Poseidon takes three strides from Samothrace, “and with the fourth he reached his goal, / Aegae, where his glorious house is built in the depths of the sea, / golden, gleaming, imperishable forever” (13.20–22; [[aegae]], [[poseidon]], [[imperishable]]). κλυτὰ δώματα, “glorious house,” is a phrase of both poems. The Odyssey uses it of the same house at Aegae, where Poseidon goes after raising the storm against Odysseus (Od. 5.381), and of the house of Aeolus (Od. 10.60); in the Iliad it is the house to which Priam brings Hector's body (24.717–719). The phrase is new to the entry.
