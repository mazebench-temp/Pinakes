---
summary: The name stands once in Book 13, for the father of Ascalaphus: Deiphobus' spear strikes “the son of Enyalius” (13.519), and two verses later the narrator calls the same father Ares.
grc: [Ἐνυαλίοιο]
---

Aiming at Idomeneus, Deiphobus misses again and strikes Ascalaphus, “the son of Enyalius” (υἱὸν Ἐνυαλίοιο, 13.518–519; see [[ascalaphus]] and [[deiphobus]]). In the Catalogue Ascalaphus and Ialmenus are “sons of Ares” (2.512), and two verses on the narrator says that “loud-voiced, mighty Ares” had not yet heard of his son's death (13.521–525; see [[ares]]). The two names are one god here, as when Athena faces Enyalius in the battle of the gods (20.69), the only other place where the genitive occurs.

Meriones, whom four verses call “the equal of Enyalius, slayer of men” (2.651, 7.166, 8.264, 17.259), fights over the body: he wounds Deiphobus as he snatches the dead man's helmet, and is “the equal of swift Ares” (13.526–530; see [[meriones]] and [[equal-of-ares]]).
