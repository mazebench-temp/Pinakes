**A bastard daughter.** Imbrius, son of Mentor, the first Trojan killed in the book, “had as his wife a bastard daughter of Priam, Medesicaste” (κούρην δὲ Πριάμοιο νόθην ἔχε, 13.173; see [[medesicaste]] and [[imbrius]]). νόθη is found only here; every other bastard in Homer is a son (see the article above). Priam's bastard sons fight beside their brothers and die in the war, as Democoon and Isus have died (4.499–500, 11.101–104). His bastard daughter is married to a man of Pedaeum, who comes to live beside Priam when the Achaeans land and is honored like Priam's own children (13.172–176; see [[fathers-and-daughters]]).

**A bastard son.** In front of the Phthians stand Medon and Podarces. “One was the bastard son of divine Oileus, / Medon, the brother of Ajax; but he lived / in Phylace, away from his native land, since he had killed a man, / a kinsman of his stepmother Eriopis, whom Oileus had as wife” (13.694–697; see [[medon]], [[eriopis]] and [[exile-and-refuge]]). The Catalogue named his mother, Rhene (2.727–728); here the poem names his father's wife, and the man of her family whom he killed. The four verses return when Aeneas kills him (15.332–336). His brother, Ajax son of Oileus, fights on the same part of the line (13.695, 13.701–722; see [[ajax-son-of-oileus]]).
