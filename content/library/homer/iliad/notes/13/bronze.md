summary: Bronze shines and rings through Book 13. Hector is covered in it, Idomeneus' armor shines like lightning, and the gleam of helmets, corselets and shields blinds the eyes when the armies meet; bronze rings on the chests of the men who fight over Alcathous and tears on his body; and it is the blade that cuts. Poseidon's horses are bronze-footed and his axle bronze, and Peisander's axe is “of good bronze” (13.23–30, 13.191–192, 13.245, 13.339–344, 13.439–441, 13.497–498, 13.612).

en: [of good bronze]

**The gleam.** When the armies meet on the left, the battle bristles with spears:

{{quote:992591b1-2151-575e-bf3d-4155e776080f}}

The sight is the battle (see [[the-armies-clash]] and [[eyes]]). Ajax's spear cannot find Hector, for “none of his skin showed, and all of him was covered / in terrible bronze” (13.191–192; see [[hector]]). Idomeneus goes out armed like the lightning of Zeus, “so the bronze shone about his chest as he ran” (13.245; see [[like-lightning]]); he and Meriones go into the war “armored in gleaming bronze” (13.305), and the Trojans follow their leaders at the end of the book “gleaming with bronze” (13.801).

**The ring.** Imbrius falls, and his armor rings around him, “inlaid with bronze” (13.181; see [[fell-with-a-thud]]). Over Alcathous' body “around their chests the bronze / rang terribly as they took aim at one another through the throng” (13.497–498), and Alcathous' own tunic of bronze, “which before had kept destruction from his body”, “rang dry as it was torn about the spear” (13.439–441; see [[corselet]]).

**The blade.** χαλκός is the weapon itself: “the sharp bronze” (13.212, 13.338, 13.561), “the pitiless bronze” (13.501, 13.553; see [[pitiless-bronze]]), the bronze that went through Asius' throat (13.388) and “let out” Oenomaus' bowels (13.507), the bronze that Peisander and Harpalion could not drive through Menelaus' shield (13.607, 13.647). The ash tree of a simile is “cut by the bronze” (13.180), and Idomeneus says that Telamonian Ajax would give way to no man “who was mortal and ate the grain of Demeter / and could be broken by bronze and by great stones” (13.322–323; see [[ajax]] and [[demeter]]). To be mortal is to be open to bronze.

**The gods' bronze and good bronze.** Poseidon's horses are “bronze-footed”, and as he drives over the sea “the bronze axle was not wetted beneath” (13.23, 13.30; see [[bronze-footed]] and [[chariot-journey]]). Peisander's battle-axe is “of good bronze” (εὔχαλκον, 13.612; see [[axe]]), a word used once more, of Achilles' ash spear (20.322). The Locrians have no “bronze-fitted” helmets (13.714), and Meriones and Paris shoot “bronze-fitted” arrows (13.650, 13.662; see [[bronze-fitted]]). The Achaeans and the Cretans are “bronze-armored” (13.255, 13.272; see [[bronze-armored]]), and Hector is “armed in bronze” (13.720; see [[armed-in-bronze]]).
