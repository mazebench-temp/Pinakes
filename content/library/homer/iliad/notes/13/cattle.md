---
summary: Cattle in Book 13 are the ox that writhes when herdsmen bind it with twisted ropes, as Adamas writhes around Meriones' spear (13.571–573), and the yoke of wine-dark oxen that pull the plow with equal spirit, the two Ajaxes side by side (13.703–708).
grc: [βόε]
---

**The roped ox.** Adamas, speared below the navel, “writhed, as an ox writhes that herdsmen in the mountains have bound by force with twisted ropes and lead along against its will” (13.571–572; see [[the-roped-ox]]). The βουκόλοι, the herdsmen, are named from the ox (βοῦς).

**The plow team.** The two Ajaxes stand together “as in a fallow field two wine-dark oxen pull the jointed plow with equal spirit”, with sweat bubbling up at the roots of their horns and only the yoke between them (13.703–707; see [[oxen-at-the-plow]] and [[jointed]]). βόε is a dual, a yoke of two; οἶνοψ, “wine-dark”, is the color of the sea and here of oxen, as in the Odyssey's plowman longing for his supper (*Odyssey* 13.31–32).

**Hides.** The rest of the book's cattle are leather: Idomeneus' shield is made of oxhides and bronze (13.406), Deiphobus' shield is of bull's hide (13.161, 13.163; see [[bulls]]), and Hector's is “thick with hides” (13.804; see [[shield]]). Hector's insult to Ajax at 13.824, βουγάϊε, “you braggart”, has also been explained as “big ox, lout” (see the translation's note on 13.824).
