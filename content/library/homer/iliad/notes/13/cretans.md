---
summary: In Book 13 the Cretans are named only in Idomeneus' titles. He is “counselor of the Cretans” to Poseidon and to Meriones, and “leader of the Cretans” when he answers them (13.219–311).
---

Κρητῶν βουληφόρε, “counselor of the Cretans”, is how Poseidon, in the voice of Thoas, and Meriones address him (13.219, 13.255; see [[man-of-counsel]]); Meriones adds “bronze-armored”. Κρητῶν ἀγός, “leader of the Cretans”, brings in four of his answers (13.221, 13.259, 13.274, 13.311), as it did in Book 4 (4.265; see [[idomeneus]]). The contingent does not fight as a body in this book. Of the five companions whom Idomeneus calls to his side against Aeneas, only Meriones is a Cretan: Ascalaphus leads the men of Orchomenus (2.511–512), Antilochus is Nestor's son, and the poem gives Aphareus and Deipyrus no people (13.477–479; see [[meriones]], [[ascalaphus]], [[antilochus]], [[aphareus]] and [[deipyrus]]).
