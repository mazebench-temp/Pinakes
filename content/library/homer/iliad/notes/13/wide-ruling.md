---
summary: Poseidon, in Calchas' likeness, grants that “the hero, son of Atreus, wide-ruling Agamemnon,” may be wholly to blame, in the verse with which Agamemnon rose to answer Calchas in Book 1.
---

“But even if in very truth the one wholly to blame is the hero, son of Atreus, wide-ruling Agamemnon, because he dishonored the swift-footed son of Peleus, for us at least there is no way to hold back from war” (13.111–114; [[agamemnon]], [[aitios]], [[who-is-to-blame]]). The verse ἥρως Ἀτρεΐδης εὐρὺ κρείων Ἀγαμέμνων is the one with which Agamemnon rose in anger to answer Calchas at the beginning of the poem (1.102). Here the god speaks it in Calchas' likeness, and the charge of Book 1, that the king's dishonor brought the evil on the army, is repeated without the king to answer it.
