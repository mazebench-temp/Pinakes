---
summary: Pylaemenes, whom Menelaus killed in Book 5, is alive in Book 13: his son Harpalion is killed by Meriones, and as the Paphlagonians carry the body to Ilios, “his father went with them, letting tears fall” (13.643–659).
refs: [13.644, 13.658-659]
---

He is named once, as the father: “There the son of king Pylaemenes leapt at him, / Harpalion, who had followed his dear father to make war / at Troy” (13.643–645; see [[harpalion]]). When Meriones' arrow has killed the son, the father walks behind the chariot that carries the body, weeping, “and no penalty was paid for his dead son” (13.658–659; see [[bereaved-parents]] and [[poine]]). The contradiction with his death at Menelaus' hands (5.576–579) and the ways readers have answered it are set out under [[pylaemenes-dead-and-alive]].
