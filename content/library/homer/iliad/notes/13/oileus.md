summary: Oileus is named in Book 13 as the father of both his sons, the lesser Ajax and the bastard Medon, and the poem names his wife Eriopis, whose kinsman Medon killed before he fled to Phylace (13.66, 13.694–697, 13.701).
# Ὀϊλεύς, the nominative (13.697 = 15.336). Ὀϊλῆα (11.93) is the Trojan charioteer (oileus-the-trojan).
grc: [Ὀϊλεύς]
relations:
  - wife: eriopis (13.697)
  - child: medon (13.694-695)
  - child: ajax-son-of-oileus (13.66, 13.701)

His name marks his son in every scene the lesser Ajax has in the book: “swift Ajax, the son of Oileus” is the first to know Poseidon (Ὀϊλῆος ταχὺς Αἴας, 13.66), and “Ajax, the swift son of Oileus” stands beside Telamonian Ajax (13.701); the patronymic Ὀϊλιάδης names him when he beheads Imbrius and when his Locrians do not follow him (13.203, 13.712; see [[ajax-son-of-oileus]]).

The poem also gives Oileus a household. “One was the bastard son of divine Oileus, / Medon, the brother of Ajax; but he lived / in Phylace, away from his native land, since he had killed a man, / a kinsman of his stepmother Eriopis, whom Oileus had as wife” (13.694–697; see [[medon]], [[eriopis]] and [[phylace]]). In the Catalogue Medon's mother is Rhene and his father is Oileus, “sacker of cities” (2.727–728; see [[rhene]] and [[sacker-of-cities]]); here he is θεῖος, “divine” (13.694; see [[divine]]), and he has a wife. Whether Eriopis was the mother of the lesser Ajax the poem does not say. The four verses come again when Aeneas kills Medon (15.333–336).
