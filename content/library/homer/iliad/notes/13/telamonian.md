---
summary: Telamonian Ajax answers the son of Oileus, would yield to no man, and stands beside him like an ox at the plow, and Teucer too is called Telamonian.
grc: [Τελαμωνίου, Τελαμωνιάδῃ, υἱὸς Τελαμῶνος]
---

**Ajax.** When the son of Oileus has recognized Poseidon, “at once he spoke to Ajax, the son of Telamon” (13.67), and “Telamonian Ajax” answers that his own hands quiver around his spear (13.76–78; [[ajax]], [[irresistible-hands]]). Idomeneus says that “to no man would great Telamonian Ajax give way” who eats the grain of Demeter, not even to Achilles in the standing fight (13.321–325; [[breaker-of-men]]). In the book's last scene of the two Ajaxes their names are kept apart by the epithet: the son of Oileus never stood “apart from Telamonian Ajax” (13.701–702), and “with the son of Telamon there followed many brave men, his companions,” who took over his shield when he tired (13.709–711), while the Locrians did not follow the son of Oileus (13.712; [[the-two-ajaxes]], [[locrians]]).

**Teucer.** “And Teucer, son of Telamon, was the first to kill his man” (13.170; [[teucer]]), and he is “the son of Telamon” again, υἱὸς Τελαμῶνος, when his spear strikes Imbrius below the ear (13.177; [[imbrius]]). The adjective goes to both of Telamon's sons, as it did when Agamemnon praised Teucer in Book 8 (8.281).
