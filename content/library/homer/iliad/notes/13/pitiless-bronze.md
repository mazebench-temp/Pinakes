---
summary: Aeneas and Idomeneus are eager “to cut each other's flesh with the pitiless bronze,” and the Trojans cannot graze Antilochus' tender flesh with it.
---

Both uses join the bronze to flesh, χρόα. Over the body of Alcathous, “two warlike men beyond the others, Aeneas and Idomeneus, the equals of Ares, were eager to cut each other's flesh with the pitiless bronze” (13.499–501; [[aeneas]], [[idomeneus]], [[warlike]], [[equal-of-ares]]). Neither does: Aeneas' spear goes quivering into the earth, and Idomeneus kills Oenomaus instead (13.502–508; [[oenomaus-the-trojan]]). A little later the Trojans around Antilochus “could not get through to graze the tender flesh with the pitiless bronze of Antilochus,” because Poseidon guarded him (13.552–555; [[antilochus]], [[tender]], [[panaiolos]]). Twice the pitiless weapon is held off from the flesh it is aimed at. See [[bronze]].
