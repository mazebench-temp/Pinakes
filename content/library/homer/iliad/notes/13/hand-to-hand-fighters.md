summary: Hector calls on the “Trojans and Lycians and Dardanians who fight hand to hand” when the close ranks have driven him back, and at the start of the book the Mysians “fight hand to hand” in a sister word, ἀγχέμαχος.
grc: [ἀγχεμάχων]

Hector's charge breaks on the close ranks around the two Ajaxes, and he gives ground, reeling; then he shouts: “Trojans and Lycians and Dardanians who fight hand to hand, / stand by me! The Achaeans will not hold me back for long” (13.148–151; [[hector]], [[battle-order]]). It is the third of the six times he speaks the verse (8.173, 11.286; 15.425, 15.486, 17.184).

The book opens with a sister word. Zeus turns his eyes away from Troy to the land “of the Mysians, who fight hand to hand” (Μυσῶν τʼ ἀγχεμάχων, 13.4–5; [[mysians]]). ἀγχέμαχος, “fighting near,” is formed from the same ἄγχι and μάχομαι as ἀγχιμαχητής; later in the poem it describes the Myrmidons (16.248, 16.272 = 17.165). The genitive ἀγχεμάχων is added to the entry.

## Outside Homer

Strabo quotes 13.792–793 with an extra verse after Morys' name, Μυσῶν ἀγχεμάχων ἡγήτορα, “leader of the Mysians who fight hand to hand,” giving the epithet of 13.5 to Mysians from a nearer Ascania (*Geography* 12.4.5; [[ascanius-the-mysian]], [[strabo]]).
