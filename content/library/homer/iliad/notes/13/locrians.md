The Locrians are named among the peoples who hold Hector off where the wall was lowest (13.685–687; see [[ionians]]), and they come into the story as the two Ajaxes stand side by side (13.701–711; see [[the-two-ajaxes]]):

{{quote:58fd12c7-b858-579e-a165-062a7ef588b6}}

No other contingent in the Iliad is said to fight without the arms of the line. They lack the three things that make a man fit for close battle, the helmet with its horsehair crest, the round shield and the ash spear (13.714–715; see [[helmet]], [[shield]] and [[spear]]), and they trust in bows and in “well-twisted sheep's wool” (13.716): slings of wool, like the one with which Agenor binds Helenus' wounded hand (13.599–600; see [[sling]] and [[bow]]). “Shooting thick and fast” (13.718), they stay unseen behind the armed men in front, and “of the joy of battle / the Trojans remembered nothing; for the arrows threw them into confusion” (13.721–722; see [[charme]]). Their leader in the Catalogue is small and wears a linen corselet, but he surpasses all the Achaeans with the spear (2.527–530; see [[ajax-son-of-oileus]] and [[with-linen-corselet]]). Here he fights in front, beside Telamonian Ajax, and his men do not follow him (13.712).
