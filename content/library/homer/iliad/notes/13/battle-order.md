**The close ranks.** Poseidon has rebuked the men in the rear, and the battalions take their stand around the two Ajaxes (see [[poseidon-rallies-the-achaeans]] and [[the-two-ajaxes]]):

{{quote:632c9896-e036-5d41-b9ea-ed0aaf2621a3}}

The best men are κρινθέντες, “chosen out”, and each pair of the formation repeats a noun in a second case (see [[polyptoton]]). The shields are taken as set layer on layer, and the spears as overlapping, held out by the men behind past those in front (see the translation's note on 13.130–135). The three verses of shield, helmet and nodding crest (13.131–133) return when the Myrmidons form up behind Patroclus (16.215–217). The picture has often been compared with the close-packed hoplite phalanx of later Greece; how far it reflects real tactics is disputed (see the entry).

Hector comes on like a rolling boulder until he meets “the close-packed battalions” (πυκινῇς … φάλαγξι) and stands still, pressed hard against them; they stab at him with swords and double-pointed spears, and he gives ground, reeling (13.136–148; see [[the-rolling-boulder]], [[hector]] and [[spear]]). He calls them arrayed “in close order like a tower” (πυργηδόν, 13.152), the words of the hunters who face a boar or a lion in Book 12 (12.43).

**Right, middle and left.** Meriones asks where they should enter the throng: “On the right of the whole army, or through the middle, / or on the left?” (13.308–309). Idomeneus answers that in the middle of the ships there are others to defend them, the two Ajaxes and Teucer, and Ajax would yield to no man in the standing fight; so “steer us this way, to the left of the army” (13.312–327; see [[ajax]] and [[teucer]]). When the Trojans see the two Cretans, they all come at them, and the quarrel stands even “at the sterns of the ships” (13.330–333; see [[stern]]). The left is where the book's killing happens. Hector does not know that “on the left of the ships his men were being cut down by the Argives” (13.675–676), and he finds Paris “on the left of the tearful battle” (13.765).

**Where Hector broke in.** Hector, who does not know of the slaughter on the left, holds on where he first broke in, by the ships of Ajax and Protesilaus, where the wall was built lowest (13.679–684; see [[the-achaean-wall]]). Against him stand the Boeotians and the Ionians, the Locrians, the Phthians and the Epeians, with “the picked men of the Athenians” (προλελεγμένοι) under Menestheus (13.685–700; see [[athenians]], [[epeians]], [[phthians]], [[boeotians]] and [[ionians]]). The two Ajaxes stand side by side like oxen yoked to one plow (13.701–708; see [[oxen-at-the-plow]]), and Telamonian Ajax has companions who take over his shield when he tires (13.709–711). The Locrians do not keep with their leader in the close fight: without helmets, shields or spears, they shoot from behind with bows and slings, “unseen”, while the men in armor fight in front (13.712–722; see [[locrians]] and [[sling]]). No other contingent in the Iliad is described fighting so.

**Around a man.** Idomeneus' companions form around him against Aeneas: they “stood close beside him, leaning their shields against their shoulders” (13.487–488), as the Achaeans stood around Ajax in Book 11 (11.593).

**The Trojans re-form.** Polydamas tells Hector that, now they are over the wall, some of the Trojans “stand apart with their arms, and others fight, / fewer against more, scattered among the ships” (13.738–739; see [[polydamas]]). Then, when Hector has gone “among the champions” looking for his leaders (13.760), they come on again like the waves of a storm, “some in front in close array, and others behind” (13.795–801), and Hector, behind his shield, “made trial” of the battalions everywhere, “but he did not confound the spirit in the breasts of the Achaeans” (13.806–808).
