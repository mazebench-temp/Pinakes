---
summary: The book opens with Zeus turning his “shining eyes” away from Troy, and the word then belongs to the spears that are thrown and aimed, to helmets and shields, and to the “bright eyes” of Alcathous, which Poseidon bewitches.
grc: [φαεινῶν, φαεινά]
---

**Eyes.** Having brought Hector to the ships, Zeus “turned his shining eyes away,” and to Troy “he no longer turned his shining eyes at all” (13.3, 13.7; [[zeus]]). The dual ὄσσε φαεινώ frames the look he gives instead to the Thracians, the Mysians and the Abii. The word is also given to the eyes of a dying man. Poseidon beats Alcathous down at the hands of Idomeneus, “bewitching his bright eyes and fettering his shining limbs” (13.434–435; [[alcathous]], [[phaidimos]]): the god takes from him the sight that might have saved him.

**Spears.** The formula δουρὶ φαεινῷ, “with his shining spear,” runs through the fighting. Meriones takes aim at Deiphobus (13.159), Hector throws at Teucer (13.183), Ajax reaches out at Hector (13.190), Idomeneus takes aim at Othryoneus (13.370), and Deiphobus throws at Idomeneus twice (13.403, 13.516; [[spear]]). Only Idomeneus' spear kills the man it is aimed at; Hector's and Deiphobus' kill others.

**Helmets and shields.** Deiphobus snatches “the shining helmet” from the dead Ascalaphus (13.527; [[with-a-socket-for-the-plume]]), and at the end of the book Hector leads the Trojans with “his shining helmet” shaking about his temples (13.805; [[helmet]]). In the battle that comes together like a dust storm, eyes are blinded “by the gleam of bronze from the shining helmets and the newly burnished corselets and the bright shields” (13.341–342; [[shield]]). The genitive plural φαεινῶν of the shields is added to the entry, with the neuter plural φαεινά of Alcathous' eyes.
