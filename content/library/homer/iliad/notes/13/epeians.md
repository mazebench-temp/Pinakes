**Amphimachus.** Hector's spear, thrown at Teucer, kills Amphimachus, son of Cteatus, one of the four leaders under whom the Epeians sailed (13.183–187; 2.615–621; see [[amphimachus]]). The Athenian leaders carry his body back to the army, and Poseidon, angry “for his grandson”, goes through the huts and ships to rouse the Danaans (13.195–196, 13.206–209; see [[poseidon]] and [[the-moliones]]).

**At the ships.** Where the wall was lowest, the Epeians are among the peoples who hold Hector off: “There the Boeotians and the Ionians with trailing tunics, / the Locrians and the Phthians and the shining Epeians, / hardly held him off from the ships” (13.685–687). φαιδιμόεντες, “shining”, a fuller form of φαίδιμος (see [[phaidimos]]), occurs only here in Homer; elsewhere the Epeians are “bronze-armored” (4.537, 11.694) and “great-hearted” (11.732, 11.744). Their leaders now are Meges, son of Phyleus, and two men named only here, Amphion and Dracius (13.691–692; see [[meges]], [[amphion-the-epeian]] and [[dracius]]). Of the four leaders of the Catalogue, Diores fell in Book 4 (4.517–526) and Amphimachus in this book; Thalpius and Polyxeinus are not named after the Catalogue (see [[thalpius]] and [[polyxeinus]]). Meges, who led the men of Dulichium, is an Epeian by descent (2.625–630).
