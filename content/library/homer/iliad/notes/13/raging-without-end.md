---
summary: The phrase of Strife passes to the Trojans, who follow Hector “raging without end,” and to Hector himself, whom Telamonian Ajax longs to fight.
---

The Trojans come on against the ships, “like a flame or a storm wind, in a body, / raging without end,” behind Hector, “roaring and crying aloud” (13.39–41; [[like-a-flame]], [[roaring-and-crying-aloud]], [[trojans]]). Poseidon, in the likeness of Calchas, fills the two Ajaxes with strength, and Telamonian Ajax answers that he longs “even alone / to fight Hector, son of Priam, raging without end” (13.79–80; [[ajax]], [[hector]]). The two verses begin with the same words, Ἕκτορι Πριαμίδῃ ἄμοτον μεμαῶτ-: first the Trojans rage behind their leader, then the leader himself. In Books 4 and 5 the phrase belonged to Strife; here it passes to the Trojans and to Hector, whose phrase it remains when he waits for Achilles before the gates (22.36). See [[son-of-priam]].
