---
summary: Deiphobus' shield, “balanced on every side, made of bull's hide,” stops Meriones' spear, which snaps at the socket.
---

Deiphobus comes on behind his round shield, and Meriones “hit him, and did not miss, on his shield, balanced on every side, made of bull's hide; but he did not drive through it, and long before the long spear snapped at the socket” (13.160–162; [[deiphobus]], [[meriones]], [[balanced-on-every-side]], [[spear]]). Deiphobus “held the shield of bull's hide away from him, and feared in his heart the spear of wise-hearted Meriones” (13.163–164; [[wise-hearted]]). The epithet comes twice in three lines, first as the shield stops the blow, then as its owner holds it out at arm's length, afraid of the spear that has already failed to pierce it. Meriones goes back to his hut for another spear, and so meets Idomeneus (13.165–168, 13.246–258). The only other shield of bull's hide in Homer is Hector's (16.360). See [[shield]].
