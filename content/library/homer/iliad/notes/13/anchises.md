summary: Anchises is named as the father-in-law of Alcathous, who married his eldest daughter, Hippodameia, the child whom “her father and lady mother loved with all their hearts” (13.428–433).
refs: [13.429-430]
---

γαμβρὸς δʼ ἦν Ἀγχίσαο, “he was the son-in-law of Anchises” (13.428). The house of Anchises appears here as a family: an eldest daughter, Hippodameia, praised for her beauty, handiwork and wits; her mother, the πότνια μήτηρ, “lady mother”, who is not named; and her husband Alcathous, “the best man in wide Troy”, who reared the young Aeneas in his own house (13.428–433, 13.465–466; see [[hippodameia-daughter-of-anchises]], [[alcathous]] and [[aeneas]]). Aeneas' mother is Aphrodite (2.820); Hippodameia's is a lady of Anchises' hall whom the poem does not name.
