summary: The goat of Book 13 is the prey that two lions snatch from the dogs and carry high in their jaws, the body of Imbrius in the hands of the two Ajaxes (13.198–202).
en: [goat]
---

ὥς τε δύʼ αἶγα λέοντε κυνῶν ὕπο καρχαροδόντων: “As two lions snatch a goat away from sharp-toothed dogs” (13.198). The goat is the lion's prey, as the wild goat is in the simile of Menelaus seeing Paris (3.23–24) and the bleating goats that shudder at a lion in Paris' boast (11.383; see [[goats-before-a-lion]]). Here the goat is a light burden for two lions, held “high above the ground” as the Ajaxes hold the body of Imbrius high and strip it (13.200–202; see [[two-lions-and-a-goat]]).
