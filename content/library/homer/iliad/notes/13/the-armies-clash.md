summary: Book 13 does not use the six verses of the clash, but twice tells the meeting of armies in other words: on the left, where the battle comes together like a storm of dust, bristles with spears and blinds the eyes with the gleam of bronze (13.330–344), and at the end, where the shouts of both armies reach the upper air (13.833–837).
refs: [13.333-344, 13.834-837]
passages:
  - 13.330-344 | The battle comes together on the left
  - 13.833-837 | The roar of both armies
---

**On the left.** When the Trojans see Idomeneus and Meriones, they all come at them, “And the quarrel stood even among them at the sterns of the ships” (13.330–333; see [[battle-order]]). A simile of storm winds raising the dust on the roads takes the place that the torrents meeting in a ravine had in Book 4 (13.334–336; 4.452–456; see [[the-dust-storm]]), and then the battle “came together” (ὁμόσʼ ἦλθε, 13.337). As in the six verses of Books 4 and 8 (4.446–451 = 8.60–65), no one is named. But where those verses go by sound, these go by sight. The battle “bristled with the spears, / long and flesh-cutting” (13.339–340), and “eyes were blinded / by the gleam of bronze from the shining helmets / and the newly burnished corselets and the bright shields / as they came on together” (13.340–343; see [[bronze]] and [[eyes]]). The passage ends with an imagined onlooker: “he would be very bold of heart / who could then have rejoiced at the sight of the toil, and not grieved” (13.343–344). So the first battle of the poem ended with the man who, coming into the work unwounded, could not make light of it (4.539–544; see [[apostrophe]]).

**The two shouts.** The book ends as the armies come on again after Ajax's challenge. The Trojans follow Hector “with a wondrous din, and the army shouted behind. / And the Argives on the other side shouted in answer, and did not forget / their courage, but awaited the best of the Trojans as they came on. / And the roar of both reached the upper air and the rays of Zeus” (13.834–837; see [[the-battle-at-the-ships]]). The shouts meet before the armies do, and the next book begins with the sound, which reaches Nestor in his hut (14.1).
