---
summary: Poseidon's cave lies in the depths of the sea “between Tenedos and rocky Imbros”; there he unyokes his horses, feeds them ambrosia and hobbles them with golden fetters before he goes to the Achaean army (13.32–38).
---

μεσσηγὺς Τενέδοιο καὶ Ἴμβρου παιπαλοέσσης, “between Tenedos and rocky Imbros” (13.33). Tenedos lies off the coast of the Troad, south of the mouth of the Hellespont; Strabo puts it no more than forty stades from the mainland (*Geography* 13.1.46). Imbros lies to the northwest, off the Thracian Chersonese. The cave is in the open sea between them, before the ships, and from it the god goes on foot “to the army of the Achaeans” (13.38; see [[imbros]] and [[poseidon-rallies-the-achaeans]]).

Tenedos has been named before as Apollo's island, which he rules “with might” (1.38, 1.452), and as a place Achilles sacked (11.625). In the Odyssey the Achaeans who sailed first from Troy stop at Tenedos to sacrifice on their way home (Od. 3.159).
