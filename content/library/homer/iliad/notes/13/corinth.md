---
summary: Corinth is the home of Euchenor, the rich and brave son of the seer Polyidus, “who lived in his house at Corinth” (13.664).
grc: [Κορινθόθι]
---

Κορινθόθι, “at Corinth” (13.664), has the old ending of place -θι, as in Ἰλιόθι πρό, “before Ilios” (13.349). ἀφνειός, “rich”, the first word of Euchenor's praise, is the Catalogue's epithet for his city, “wealthy Corinth” (2.570; see [[wealthy]]). An ancient note on the line holds that the poet names the city Corinth in his own voice, but Ephyra when his heroes speak, which would make Glaucus' Ephyra (6.152, 6.210) the same city (scholia on 13.664); Homer himself never joins the two names (see [[ephyra-in-argos]]). See [[euchenor]] and [[polyidus-the-seer]].
