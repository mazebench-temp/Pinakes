---
summary: Over Peisander's body Menelaus reproaches the Trojans who carried off his wedded wife “and many possessions” after they had been welcomed by her, without fear of the wrath of Zeus, god of guests (13.620–627).
---

“you who carried off my wedded wife and many possessions / wantonly, after you had been welcomed by her” (13.626–627; see [[menelaus]] and [[marriage]]). The pair of wife and possessions is the stake of the duel in Book 3 and of Antenor's proposal in Book 7 (3.70–72, 7.350–351; see the entry). In Menelaus' speech it is a wrong done to a host: the Trojans, he says, did not fear “the harsh wrath of Zeus who thunders loud, / god of guests”, who will one day destroy their city (13.624–625; see [[xenia]] and [[who-thunders-loud]]), and this is “the other insult and shame” with which they insulted him (13.622). The speech joins the old wrong to the present one: now they are eager to throw fire on the ships (13.628–629; see [[ships]] and [[satiety-of-all-things]]).
