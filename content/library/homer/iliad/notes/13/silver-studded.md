---
summary: Menelaus draws his “silver-studded sword” against Peisander's axe.
---

Peisander's spear has broken at the socket on Menelaus' shield, and the two close: “Then the son of Atreus drew his silver-studded sword and leapt at Peisander; but he, from under his shield, took a fine axe of good bronze” (13.610–612; [[menelaus]], [[peisander-killed-by-menelaus]], [[axe]], [[sword]]). The epithet belongs to the arming verse, as when Paris armed for his duel with Menelaus in Book 3 (3.334); here it is Menelaus' sword, drawn for the kill. Peisander's axe strikes the ridge of Menelaus' helmet under the crest; Menelaus strikes Peisander on the forehead above the nose, and the bones crack (13.614–617; [[horsehair-crested]]).
