---
summary: Poseidon leaves the Ajaxes as a hawk that rises “from a sheer and towering rock” to chase another bird across the plain.
---

“And he himself sped off as a swift-winged hawk flies, that rises from a sheer and towering rock, and darts across the plain to chase some other bird; so from them darted Poseidon the earth-shaker” (13.62–65; [[the-hawk-from-the-rock]], [[swift-winged]], [[poseidon]]). In Book 9 the sheer rock was the face down which a spring of dark water pours, the image of Agamemnon's tears (9.14–15). Here it is the height from which a bird of prey launches itself, and the simile gives the god's speed as he leaves. The son of Oileus knows him from behind as he goes (13.66–72; [[ajax-son-of-oileus]], [[seeing-the-gods]]). See [[hawk]].
