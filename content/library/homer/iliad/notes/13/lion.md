---
summary: The only lions of Book 13 are the pair to which the two Ajaxes are compared as they carry off the body of Imbrius, like two lions that have snatched a goat from sharp-toothed dogs and carry it high in their jaws (13.197–202).
---

ὥς τε δύʼ αἶγα λέοντε κυνῶν ὕπο καρχαροδόντων / ἁρπάξαντε φέρητον: “As two lions snatch a goat away from sharp-toothed dogs and carry it” (13.198–199). λέοντε is a dual, the number for a pair, as in the twin lions of Book 5 and the two lions of the night raid in Book 10 (5.554, 10.297; see [[two-lions]]). The lions are the Ajaxes, the goat the dead Imbrius and the dogs the Trojans who guard him (see [[two-lions-and-a-goat]] and [[the-two-ajaxes]]). After the many lions of Book 11, Book 13 has only this pair. Its fighters are likened instead to a hawk, a boulder, a boar, a ram, a vulture and a yoke of oxen.
