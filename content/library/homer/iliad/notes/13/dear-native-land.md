---
summary: Harpalion followed his father to Troy “and did not come back again to his native land,” and Medon lives in exile “away from his native land.”
grc: ["πατρίδα γαῖαν", "γαίης ἄπο πατρίδος"]
---

Two men of the book are measured by the land they have lost. Harpalion, son of king Pylaemenes, “had followed his dear father to make war / at Troy, and did not come back again to his native land” (13.643–645; [[harpalion]], [[nostos]]); the verse-end is the one the poem uses of the ship that brought Protesilaus to Troy and did not carry him home again (15.705–706; [[protesilaus]]). Medon, the bastard son of Oileus, “lived / in Phylace, away from his native land, since he had killed a man” (13.694–696 = 15.333–335; [[medon]], [[exile-and-refuge]]). Neither phrase has φίλη, “dear,” the word of the formula of return; both are new to the entry.
