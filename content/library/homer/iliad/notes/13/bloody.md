---
summary: Asius dies clutching “the bloody dust,” Peisander's eyes fall bloody in the dust, and Menelaus strips his bloody armor; the book adds the genitive αἱματοέσσης.
grc: [αἱματοέσσης]
---

Three times in the book the epithet marks a death. Idomeneus' spear goes through Asius' throat, and he falls like an oak or a tall pine felled for ship-timber: “so he lay stretched out in front of his horses and chariot, / roaring, clutching at the bloody dust” (13.389–393; [[asius]], [[the-felled-tree]]). The five verses return for Sarpedon's death (16.482–486), and the genitive αἱματοέσσης is new to the entry. Menelaus strikes Peisander on the forehead, “and the bones cracked, and his two eyes / fell bloody at his feet on the ground in the dust” (13.615–617; [[peisander-killed-by-menelaus]]). Then “he stripped the bloody armor from his body” and gave it to his companions (13.640–641; [[menelaus]], [[stripping-the-dead]]).
