---
summary: “Godlike Alexander” answers Hector's rebuke in the verse with which he answered it in Book 3, but now in his own defense.
---

Hector has met Paris on the left with the verse of his rebuke in Book 3 (13.769 = 3.39; [[evil-paris]]), and Paris replies in the verse that introduced his answer there: “Then godlike Alexander spoke to him in turn” (13.774 = 3.58, 6.332; [[paris]]). θεοειδής, “godlike in form,” is Paris' standing epithet, and Hector has just called him “best in looks” ([[best-in-looks]]). In Book 3 the answer was an admission; here it is a defense. Hector is blaming one who is not to blame, Paris says, and he and his companions have been fighting beside the ships without rest; he will follow wherever Hector leads, as far as his strength allows (13.775–787; [[beyond-ones-strength]]).
