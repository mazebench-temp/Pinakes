summary: The allies around whom the battle is thickest “had come from deep-soiled Ascania as relief / on the morning before.”
---

Hector and Paris go where the battle is thickest, around Cebriones and Polydamas and a list of allies that ends with Palmys, Ascanius and Morys, son of Hippotion, “who had come from deep-soiled Ascania as relief / on the morning before” (13.789–794; [[ascania]], [[ascanius-the-mysian]], [[trojan-allies]]). In the Catalogue Ascanius leads the Phrygians “from far-off Ascania” (τῆλʼ ἐξ Ἀσκανίης, 2.862–863); here Ascania is named for its soil, as Achilles names Phthia (1.155, 9.363; [[phthia]]). Whether the two Ascanias are one place the poem does not say.

## Outside Homer

Strabo distinguishes a far Ascania in Phrygia from a nearer one in Mysia, by Nicaea, and takes the “deep-soiled Ascania” of these verses for the nearer one (*Geography* 12.4.5; [[strabo]], [[hand-to-hand-fighters]]).
