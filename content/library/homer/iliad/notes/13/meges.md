αὐτὰρ Ἐπειῶν / Φυλεΐδης τε Μέγης Ἀμφίων τε Δρακίος τε, “and of the Epeians / Meges, son of Phyleus, and Amphion and Dracius” (13.691–692; see [[epeians]], [[amphion-the-epeian]] and [[dracius]]). In the Catalogue he leads not the Epeians of Elis but the islanders of Dulichium and the Echinae, because his father Phyleus had quarreled with his own father and left Elis for Dulichium (2.625–630; see [[phyleus]], [[dulichium]] and [[phyleus-and-augeas]]). Here he is counted among the Epeians, his father's people. Two of their four leaders in the Catalogue are dead, Diores in Book 4 and Amphimachus earlier in this book (4.517–526, 13.183–187; see [[amphimachus]]). In Book 15 he fights beside Otus of Cyllene, “the companion of the son of Phyleus, a leader of the great-hearted Epeians” (15.518–519), and his father's corselet saves his life (15.528–534).
