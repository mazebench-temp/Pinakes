---
summary: Poseidon is angry for his grandson, “fallen in grim combat,” and the narrator turns to Menelaus to foretell Peisander's death “in grim combat.”
---

When Hector kills Amphimachus, “then indeed Poseidon was angry with all his heart / for his grandson, fallen in grim combat” (13.206–207; [[poseidon]], [[amphimachus]]), and he goes along the ships to rouse the Danaans. The formula, at home in the challenge to single combat (3.20, 7.40, 7.51), here names a death in the general fighting. Its second use comes with an address of the narrator to Menelaus: Peisander went straight for him, “and an evil fate was leading him toward the end of death, / to be beaten down by you, Menelaus, in grim combat” (13.601–603; [[apostrophe]], [[peisander-killed-by-menelaus]], [[moira]]). It is the fourth of the seven times the narrator speaks to Menelaus as “you” (4.127, 4.146, 7.104; 17.679, 17.702, 23.600). See [[menelaus]].
