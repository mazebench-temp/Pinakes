summary: Poseidon names jackals first among the beasts whose prey the Trojans used to be, “like deer quick to flee, which through the forest are the prey of jackals and leopards and wolves” (13.102–103).
grc: [θώων]
---

θώων παρδαλίων τε λύκων τʼ ἤϊα πέλονται: “are the prey of jackals and leopards and wolves” (13.103). The jackals of Book 11 hunted a wounded stag in the mountains and scattered when a lion came (11.474–481; see [[the-jackals-and-the-stag]]). In Poseidon's taunt they are again hunters of deer, the first of three beasts of prey, and the deer are the Trojans as they used to be (see [[timid-deer]], [[leopard]] and [[wolves]]). The jackal is not named again in Homer.
