---
summary: Idomeneus says that Telamonian Ajax would not give way “even to Achilles, breaker of men,” in the standing fight; the book adds the dative ῥηξήνορι.
grc: [ῥηξήνορι]
---

Idomeneus sets Ajax against the measure of all fighters: “But to no man would great Telamonian Ajax give way, a man who was mortal and ate the grain of Demeter and could be broken by bronze and by great stones. He would not give ground even to Achilles, breaker of men, in the standing fight at least; but in speed no one can contend with him” (13.321–325; [[ajax]], [[telamonian]], [[achilles]], [[demeter]]). The man who breaks men, ῥηξήνωρ, comes a verse after the list of what can break Ajax, χαλκῷ … ῥηκτός, “broken by bronze” (13.323): both words are from ῥήγνυμι. Ajax himself named Achilles with this epithet in his challenge to Hector (7.228). See [[best-of-the-achaeans]].
