summary: Teucer is the first leader Poseidon rouses in the rear and the first Achaean to kill a man in the battle at the ships. With his long spear he kills Imbrius, Priam's son-in-law, and Hector's spear, thrown at him as he goes to strip the body, kills Amphimachus instead; Idomeneus calls him “the best of the Achaeans at archery, and good also in close battle” (13.91, 13.170–187, 13.313–314).

**Roused.** Poseidon goes to the men in the rear, worn out and weeping at the sight of the Trojans over the wall: “First he came to Teucer and Leitus, giving orders” (13.91), and with them Peneleos, Thoas, Deipyrus, Meriones and Antilochus, and he rebukes them, “Shame, Argives, young men!” (13.83–124; see [[poseidon]] and [[argives]]).

**Imbrius.**

{{quote:f8b2d322-1da2-5a27-880b-52296d51f810}}

The first verse is built like those that give Telamonian Ajax the first kill of a battle (Αἴας δὲ πρῶτος Τελαμώνιος, 6.5; Αἴας ῥα πρῶτος Τελαμώνιος, 14.511), with the half-brother's name in his place. Τελαμώνιος, “son of Telamon”, which usually names Ajax, is here Teucer's, as when Agamemnon addresses him in Book 8 (Τελαμώνιε, 8.281; see [[telamon]], [[telamonian]] and [[nothos]]). The archer kills with “his long spear” (13.177); his bow is not mentioned in the book. His victim is a man of Priam's house, Imbrius of Pedaeum, son of Mentor, married to Medesicaste, a bastard daughter of Priam, who honored him “as he honored his children” (13.171–176; see [[imbrius]], [[medesicaste]], [[mentor-father-of-imbrius]] and [[pedaeum]]). He falls like an ash tree on a mountain peak, cut by the bronze (13.178–181; see [[the-felled-ash]]).

**Hector's spear.** As Teucer rushes to strip the body, Hector throws at him. “But he, looking straight at it, avoided the bronze spear / narrowly” (13.184–185), and the spear strikes Amphimachus in the chest (13.185–187; see [[amphimachus]] and [[hector]]). The verse of the dodge comes twice more in the book, both times of Idomeneus (13.404, 13.503). The two men have met before. In Book 8 Teucer's arrows twice missed Hector and killed others, Gorgythion and Hector's charioteer Archeptolemus (8.300–315), and then Hector's stone broke his bowstring and laid him low (8.320–334); at the ships Zeus will snap his bowstring as he draws on Hector again (15.458–465). The two Ajaxes carry off Imbrius, and the son of Oileus sends his head to fall at Hector's feet (13.197–205; see [[the-two-ajaxes]]).

**Bow and spear.** Choosing where to fight, Idomeneus leaves the middle of the ships to “the two Ajaxes and Teucer, who is the best of the Achaeans / at archery, and good also in close battle” (13.313–314; see [[idomeneus]]). τοξοσύνη, “archery”, occurs only here in Homer; σταδίη ὑσμίνη is the standing fight, hand to hand (see the translation's note on 13.325). His one fight in the book is a fight with the spear, as at the ships in Book 15, when Zeus has broken his bowstring and Ajax tells him to take up a long spear and a shield (15.472–483; see [[bow]] and [[spear]]).
