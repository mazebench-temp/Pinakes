---
summary: δῖος honors Hector twice, Menestheus and Alastor as they carry men from the field, Priam in the verse of Aeneas' resentment, Agenor, and Alexander as Hector finds him.
---

**Hector.** The chosen men around the Ajaxes “awaited the Trojans and brilliant Hector” (13.129; [[battle-order]]), and where Hector first broke in, the Boeotians, Locrians, Phthians and Epeians “could not / push away from them brilliant Hector, like a flame” (13.685–688; [[like-a-flame]]).

**Bearers of the fallen.** Twice the epithet names a man who carries another from the field. “Stichius and brilliant Menestheus, / the leaders of the Athenians,” bring Amphimachus' body back to the army (13.195–196; [[menestheus]], [[stichius]]), and “Mecisteus, son of Echius, and brilliant Alastor” carry the stricken Hypsenor to the ships (13.421–423; [[alastor-who-carries-teucer]], [[mecisteus-son-of-echius]]), in the verses with which they carried the wounded Teucer in Book 8 (8.331–334).

**Trojans.** Aeneas stands at the back of the throng, “for he was always nursing wrath against brilliant Priam” (13.460; [[priam]], [[aeneas]]): the honorific stands in the verse of Aeneas' grievance. When he rallies his men he looks to “Deiphobus and Paris and brilliant Agenor” (13.490; [[agenor]]). Hector finds “brilliant Alexander, the husband of Helen of the lovely hair” on the left of the battle (13.765–766; [[husband-of-helen]], [[lovely-haired]]), in the formula that introduces Paris in Books 3, 7 and 8 (3.329, 7.355, 8.82); here it is in the accusative, and Hector's rebuke follows.
