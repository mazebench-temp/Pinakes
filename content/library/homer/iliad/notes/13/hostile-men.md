---
summary: Idomeneus does not mean “to fight standing far off from hostile men,” and so he has Trojan spears in his hut.
---

Idomeneus explains why his hut holds Trojan spears, one or twenty: “Trojan spears, which I take from the men I kill; for I do not mean / to fight standing far off from hostile men” (13.262–263; [[idomeneus]], [[bossed]], [[stripping-the-dead]]). The phrase ἀνδρῶν δυσμενέων opens the verse, as in Book 10, where Diomedes offers “to enter the camp of the hostile men close by” (10.220–221) and Dolon is ordered to come close to “the enemy men” (10.394–395). There the enemy are men to be spied on from the dark; here they are men a fighter seeks out, and the spears and shields in his hut are the proof that he has met them close at hand.
