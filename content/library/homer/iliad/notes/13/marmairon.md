summary: The word gleams at both ends of the book, of Poseidon's golden house at Aegae, “gleaming, imperishable forever,” and of the Trojans who follow Hector “gleaming with bronze”; the book adds the plural μαρμαίροντες.
grc: [μαρμαίροντες]
---

**Poseidon's house.** In four strides Poseidon reaches “Aegae, where his glorious house is built in the depths of the sea, golden, gleaming, imperishable forever” (13.21–22; [[aegae]], [[imperishable]], [[poseidon]]). The participle stands between two words of the gods' possessions, χρύσεα, “golden,” and ἄφθιτα, “imperishable.” The Odyssey has the god go home to Aegae, where his glorious house is, in the same words κλυτὰ δώματα (Od. 5.381).

**The Trojans in bronze.** At the end of the book the Trojans come on like a blast of winds on the sea, “so the Trojans, some in front in close array, and others behind, gleaming with bronze, followed with their leaders” (13.800–801; [[trojans]], [[hector]]). The plural μαρμαίροντες, of men, is added to the entry; Patroclus and his charioteer are seen gleaming in the same way when they lead the Myrmidons out (σὺν ἔντεσι μαρμαίροντας, 16.278–279).
