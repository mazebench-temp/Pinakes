---
summary: The northern mare-milkers on whom Zeus looks are ἀγαυοί, rendered here “proud”; the book adds the genitive plural ἀγαυῶν.
grc: [ἀγαυῶν]
en: [proud]
---

Zeus looks away from Troy to the Thracians, the Mysians, “and of the proud mare-milkers who live on milk, and of the Abii, the most just of men” (13.5–6; [[thracians]], [[mysians]], [[abii]], [[most-just]]). Elsewhere the epithet belongs to heroes, to heralds and to “the other noble Trojans” (3.268, 7.386); here it is given to a people at the edge of the earth, known only by what they drink. The translation renders it “proud” in this verse. Strabo takes the words as a description: Homer does not name the northernmost peoples, he says, but marks them by their way of life, calling them nomads and ἀγαυοὺς ἱππημολγοὺς γαλακτοφάγους ἀβίους τε (*Geography* 1.1.6; see [[strabo-on-the-mare-milkers]]).
