---
summary: Poseidon, disguised as Calchas, names his brother only by his title: the Ajaxes may stop Hector “even if the Olympian himself is rousing him.”
---

Poseidon, in the likeness of Calchas, ends his speech to the two Ajaxes with a concession: “then you might hold him back from the swift-sailing ships, for all his haste, even if the Olympian himself is rousing him” (13.57–58; [[poseidon]], [[the-two-ajaxes]], [[swift-sailing-ships]]). Ὀλύμπιος stands alone for Zeus, as when Hephaestus warned his mother that “the Olympian is hard to stand against” (1.589). The speaker is Zeus' own brother, who is helping the Achaeans in secret, against the will of Zeus (13.351–357), and the title lets him speak of Zeus as a mortal seer would. Hector, for his part, is sure that he has been roused by “the best of the gods, … the loud-thundering husband of Hera” (13.154; [[husband-of-hera]], [[loud-thundering]]).
