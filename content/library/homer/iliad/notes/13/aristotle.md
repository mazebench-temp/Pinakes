- **The vein along the back.** Antilochus, watching for Thoon as he turns, stabs him with a rush, “and cut through the whole vein / that runs right up the back and reaches the neck” (13.546–547; see [[antilochus]] and [[thoon-companion-of-asius]]). Aristotle, tracing the blood vessels in the *History of Animals*, comes to the vein that runs to the vertebra of the neck and back along the spine, “which Homer too spoke of in his verses”, and quotes the two lines (*History of Animals* 3.3). For him the poet's vein is a real vessel, and the verse a description of it.
