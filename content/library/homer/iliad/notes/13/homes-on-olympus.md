---
summary: The son of Oileus knows that “one of the gods who hold Olympus” has come to them in the likeness of Calchas.
---

When the false Calchas has struck the two Ajaxes with his staff and darted off like a hawk, the son of Oileus is the first to know him: “Ajax, since one of the gods who hold Olympus, / in the likeness of the seer, bids us fight beside the ships, / this is not Calchas, the prophet and augur; / for I easily knew the movements of his feet and legs from behind / as he went away; and gods are easy to know” (13.66–72; [[ajax-son-of-oileus]], [[seeing-the-gods]], [[theopropion]]). The short form of the formula, οἳ Ὄλυμπον ἔχουσι, is the one of Book 5 (5.404, 5.890). It names the gods as a body, and Ajax uses it because he cannot tell which god has come; the narrator has told the listener that it is Poseidon (13.43–45; [[poseidon]], [[divine-epiphany]]).
