---
summary: Idomeneus and Meriones go into battle like “Ares, ruin of mortals,” and his son Rout, and at the end of the book Hector leads the Trojans as “the equal of Ares, ruin of mortals.”
---

The god's commonest epithet frames the two sides of the battle at the ships. Idomeneus and Meriones go out together, and the narrator gives them the god and his son: “And as Ares, ruin of mortals, goes into war, and Rout, his dear son, strong and fearless, follows with him, who routs even a warrior of enduring heart” (13.298–300; [[ares-and-rout]], [[rout]], [[fearless]], [[stout-hearted]]). Meriones is “the equal of swift Ares” in the verses before and after (13.295, 13.328; [[equal-of-ares]]). At the end of the book the formula of comparison returns for the enemy: “And Hector led them, the equal of Ares, ruin of mortals, the son of Priam” (13.802–803; [[hector]], [[son-of-priam]]), as when he rushed into the battle in Book 11 (11.295).
