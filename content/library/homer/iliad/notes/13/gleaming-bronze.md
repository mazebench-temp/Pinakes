---
summary: Idomeneus and Meriones go into the war “armored in gleaming bronze,” and Idomeneus' shield is made “of oxhides and gleaming bronze.”
---

After the simile of Ares and Rout, “like them, Meriones and Idomeneus, leaders of men, / went into the war, armored in gleaming bronze” (13.304–305; [[ares-and-rout]], [[meriones]], [[idomeneus]]). The formula κεκορυθμένος αἴθοπι χαλκῷ, used in Book 5 of a single man going through the champions (5.562, 5.681), is here in the plural, of the pair. The other word of the entry, νῶροψ, describes the bronze of Idomeneus' shield, under which he hides from Deiphobus' spear: “which he carried, made of oxhides and gleaming bronze, / rounded, fitted with two rods” (13.405–407; [[balanced-on-every-side]], [[shield]]). In its earlier uses νῶροψ is the bronze a man puts on (2.578, 7.206, 11.16); here it is the plating of a shield.
