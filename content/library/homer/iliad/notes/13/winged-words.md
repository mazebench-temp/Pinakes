---
summary: Four speeches of Book 13 are introduced with winged words: twice, in the same verse, an urgent call to companions, Poseidon's to the leaders in the rear and Idomeneus' when Aeneas comes on (13.94, 13.480); Deiphobus' appeal to Aeneas (13.462); and Hector's answer to Polydamas (13.750).
---

τοὺς ὅ γʼ ἐποτρύνων ἔπεα πτερόεντα προσηύδα, “and urging them on he addressed them with winged words”, brings in Poseidon's rebuke to the leaders in the rear (13.94) and Idomeneus' call to his companions when Aeneas comes against him (13.480; see [[exhortation]]). Both times a list of names comes first, and the verse before ends the list in the same words, “and Meriones and Antilochus, masters of the war cry” (13.93 = 13.479; see [[meriones]] and [[antilochus]]). The verse of the call returns once more, for Hector rallying the allies over Patroclus (17.219).

Deiphobus goes to Aeneas at the back of the throng and speaks “standing near him” (ἀγχοῦ δʼ ἱστάμενος, 13.462; see [[deiphobus]] and [[aeneas]]), and Hector, who has leapt from his chariot at Polydamas' advice, “spoke to him, addressing him with winged words” (13.750; see [[polydamas]]). In each case the winged words carry an urgent request or order.
