---
summary: The boulder to which Hector is compared is broken loose from “the shameless rock”; the book adds the genitive ἀναιδέος.
grc: [ἀναιδέος]
---

Hector charges “like a rolling boulder from a rock, which a river in winter flood thrusts down from a crest, breaking with its untold rain the props of the shameless rock” (13.137–139; [[the-rolling-boulder]], [[untold]], [[hector]]). Here the epithet is given not to a stone thrown by a man but to the rock face from which the torrent tears the boulder: ἀναιδέος ἔχματα πέτρης, “the props of the shameless rock,” the rock that respects nothing. The word was the narrator's for the stone that crushed Diores' sinews and bones (4.521), and the Odyssey's for the stone of Sisyphus, which rolls (κυλίνδετο) back down to the plain (Od. 11.598); Hector's boulder bounds down until it reaches level ground, “and then it rolls no more” (κυλίνδεται, 13.142).
