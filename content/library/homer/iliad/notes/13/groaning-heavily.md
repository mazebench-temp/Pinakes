---
summary: In the plural, βαρέα στενάχοντα, the formula is the groaning of the wounded carried from the field: Hypsenor, borne to the ships in the verses that carried the wounded Teucer in Book 8, and Deiphobus, borne to the city in the verse that will carry Hector there (13.423, 13.538).
grc: ["βαρέα στενάχοντα"]
en: [groaning heavily]
---

“Then two trusty companions stooped under him, / Mecisteus, son of Echius, and brilliant Alastor, / and carried him, groaning heavily, to the hollow ships” (13.421–423; see [[hypsenor-son-of-hippasus]], [[mecisteus-son-of-echius]] and [[alastor-who-carries-teucer]]). The three verses carried Teucer from the field in Book 8, when Hector's stone had struck him (8.332–334; see [[teucer]]). Deiphobus' spear has just struck Hypsenor and “at once loosened his knees”, the verse of a killing (13.412), and the groaning makes it uncertain whether he is dead or dying; it may have come over with the verses from Book 8 (see the translation's note on 13.412–423).

Deiphobus, wounded in the arm by Meriones, is carried by his horses “toward the city, groaning heavily / in his distress” (13.538–539; see [[deiphobus]]). The verses that bring him to his horses and carry him away return for Hector, struck down by Ajax's stone in Book 14 (14.429–432 ≈ 13.535–538). Elsewhere βαρὺ στενάχων introduces the speech of a man in grief (see the entry); here the plural, βαρέα στενάχοντα, is the sound of the wounded.
