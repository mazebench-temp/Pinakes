---
summary: Phylace is named in Book 13 as the place of Medon's exile: the bastard son of Oileus lived there, away from his native land, after killing a kinsman of his stepmother Eriopis (13.696–697).
---

“One was the bastard son of divine Oileus, / Medon, the brother of Ajax; but he lived / in Phylace, away from his native land, since he had killed a man” (13.694–696; see [[medon]] and [[exile-and-refuge]]). The town that sheltered him is the home of Protesilaus and Podarces (2.695–706; see [[protesilaus]] and [[podarces]]), and the men of both leaders fight together here under the name of Phthians, Medon leading Philoctetes' men and Podarces those of his dead brother (13.693–700; see [[phthians]]). The Catalogue gave Medon command of Philoctetes' men from Methone and its neighbors (2.716–728), not of the men of Phylace; the poem does not say how the exile came to lead them (see [[philoctetes]]).
