---
summary: Poseidon rises secretly “out of the gray sea” to rouse the Argives, and the ships of Ajax and Protesilaus are drawn up “on the shore of the gray sea.”
---

Zeus wills victory for the Trojans, “But Poseidon went among the Argives and spurred them on, / having risen secretly out of the gray sea” (13.351–352; [[poseidon]], [[poseidon-rallies-the-achaeans]]). In Book 1 Thetis “rose from the gray sea like a mist” to her son (1.359), with the same πολιῆς ἁλός. Hector holds on where he first leapt in at the wall, “where the ships of Ajax and of Protesilaus were, / drawn up on the shore of the gray sea” (13.679–682; [[protesilaus]], [[the-achaean-wall]]), with the half-verse θῖνʼ ἔφʼ ἁλὸς πολιῆς of Achilles sitting apart “on the shore of the gray sea” (1.350). The same word for gray describes old men's hair, and the book gives Idomeneus the compound μεσαιπόλιος, “half gray” (13.361; [[half-gray]]).
