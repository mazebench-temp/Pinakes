---
summary: Crete is named twice in Idomeneus' boast of his descent: Zeus fathered Minos “a guardian over Crete”, and Idomeneus was born “a lord over many men in wide Crete” (13.450–453).
grc: [Κρήτῃ]
---

Κρήτῃ ἐπίουρον, “a guardian over Crete” (13.450), makes Minos the island's warden from his birth (see [[minos]] and [[guardian-of-the-achaeans]]). Of the two views of Minos, a stranger in Crete or a native, Strabo thought the verse favors the second (10.4.9). Κρήτῃ ἐν εὐρείῃ, “in wide Crete” (13.453), is the Iliad's only use of the epithet for the island, which the Odyssey uses in Odysseus' Cretan tales (Od. 13.256, 13.260, 14.252). The Catalogue counted “Crete of the hundred cities” (2.649); the genealogy ties Idomeneus' rule there to Minos, and so to Zeus (13.449–453; see [[idomeneus]] and [[deucalion]]).
