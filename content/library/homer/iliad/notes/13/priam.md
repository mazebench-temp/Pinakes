summary: Priam is seen only through others: the father who honored his son-in-law Imbrius like his own children and promised Cassandra to Othryoneus, and the king whom Aeneas resents for not honoring him. Idomeneus tells Deiphobus that he has come to be an evil to him and to his father.
# Δαρδανίδῃ Πριάμῳ, "Priam, descendant of Dardanus" (13.376).
grc: [Δαρδανίδῃ]
refs: [13.369, 13.454, 13.461]
---

**His daughters' husbands.** Imbrius had married “a bastard daughter of Priam, Medesicaste” and lived beside him, “and Priam honored him as he honored his children” (13.173–176; see [[imbrius]] and [[medesicaste]]). The newcomer Othryoneus asked for Cassandra without bride-price, promising to drive the Achaeans from Troy, “and the old man Priam promised him and nodded his assent / to give her” (13.365–369; see [[othryoneus]] and [[cassandra]]). Idomeneus mocks the promise made Δαρδανίδῃ Πριάμῳ, “to Priam, descendant of Dardanus” (13.376; see [[dardanus]]), and offers Agamemnon's fairest daughter on better terms (13.374–382). Each of the two is the first man killed in his part of the battle: Imbrius when Teucer opens the fighting at the close ranks (13.170), Othryoneus when Idomeneus leaps among the Trojans (13.361–363).

**His sons.** In this book Πριαμίδης, “son of Priam”, is Hector (13.40, 13.80, 13.316, 13.803), Deiphobus (13.157) and Helenus (13.586; see [[son-of-priam]]). Idomeneus tells Deiphobus that the ships have brought him to be an evil “to you and to your father and to the other Trojans” (13.454). Deiphobus and Helenus are wounded, and Polites leads Deiphobus from the battle (13.533–539, 13.781–783; see [[sons-of-priam]]).

**Aeneas' grudge.** Aeneas stands at the back of the throng, “always nursing wrath against brilliant Priam, / because, brave as he was among men, Priam did not honor him at all” (13.460–461; see [[aeneas]] and [[time]]). The poem does not say what lay behind it.
