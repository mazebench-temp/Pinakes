---
summary: Poseidon lays the Achaeans' plight on “the baseness of their leader”; Idomeneus is four times “leader of the Cretans”, and he and Meriones go out together as “leaders of men”; Aeneas calls on the other “leaders of the Trojans”, and at the end of the book the Trojans follow “with their leaders”.
grc: [ἀγοὶ]
---

**The leader's fault.** Poseidon, in Calchas' likeness, tells the Achaean leaders that the Trojans are fighting by the ships “through the baseness of their leader and the slackness of the army” (ἡγεμόνος κακότητι μεθημοσύνῃσί τε λαῶν, 13.108; see [[who-is-to-blame]], [[the-army-and-its-leaders]] and the translation's note on 13.108–114). The leader is Agamemnon, named a few lines later (13.112), and the fault of the one becomes the fault of the many.

**Leader of the Cretans.** The verse that introduces Idomeneus' answers, once to Thoas and three times to Meriones, calls him Κρητῶν ἀγός, “leader of the Cretans” (13.221, 13.259, 13.274, 13.311; see [[idomeneus]] and [[cretans]]), as “counselor of the Cretans” is the title by which others address him (13.219, 13.255; see [[boule]]). He and Meriones go into the war like Ares and Rout, “Meriones and Idomeneus, leaders of men” (ἀγοὶ ἀνδρῶν, 13.304; see [[ares-and-rout]]). Stichius and Menestheus, who carry Amphimachus back, are “the leaders of the Athenians” (ἀρχοὶ Ἀθηναίων, 13.196; see [[menestheus]]).

**Leaders and the led.** Aeneas, going to defend Alcathous, calls on Deiphobus, Paris and Agenor, “who were leaders of the Trojans with him”, and the army follows like sheep after a ram (13.489–495; see [[aeneas]] and [[sheep-following-the-ram]]). In the last movement of the book the Trojans come on like the waves of a stormy sea, “gleaming with bronze, followed with their leaders” (13.795–801; see [[the-storm-and-the-waves]]).
