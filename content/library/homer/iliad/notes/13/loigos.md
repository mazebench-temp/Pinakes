---
summary: Idomeneus, after Hypsenor is struck down, is eager always “either to cover some one of the Trojans in murky night / or himself to fall with a thud, warding off ruin from the Achaeans”, the task that Achilles was to have done and has refused.
---

When Deiphobus' spear has struck down Hypsenor and his companions have carried him to the ships, “Idomeneus did not let his great might slacken, but was eager always / either to cover some one of the Trojans in murky night / or himself to fall with a thud, warding off ruin from the Achaeans” (ἀμύνων λοιγὸν Ἀχαιοῖς, 13.424–426; see [[idomeneus]] and [[menos]]). λοιγὸν ἀμῦναι, “to ward off ruin”, is the phrase of Achilles' task. He called the heralds in Book 1 to witness, “if ever again / there comes a need of me to ward off shameful ruin / from the others” (1.338–342), and Phoenix reared him, he says, so that one day he might ward off shameful ruin from him (9.495). Patroclus will ask him, in Book 16, who of later men will have good of him if he does not ward off shameful ruin from the Argives (16.31–32). While Achilles refuses, other men take the task on themselves. Idomeneus' alternative is plain: to kill, or to die doing it. ἢ αὐτὸς δουπῆσαι, “or himself to fall with a thud”, puts his own death in the verb of a man falling in battle (see [[fell-with-a-thud]]).
