**The Phthians.** In the Catalogue Medon marshals the men of Philoctetes, from Methone, Thaumacia, Meliboea and Olizon, while Philoctetes lies wounded on Lemnos (2.716–728; see [[philoctetes]]). Here, with Podarces, who commands the men of Protesilaus, he leads “the great-hearted Phthians” (13.693, 13.699; see [[podarces]] and [[phthians]]), and they fight beside the Boeotians, defending the ships (13.700; see [[boeotians]]). Strabo quotes these verses to show that the men of Philoctetes and of Protesilaus were called Phthians, like Achilles' own, who stayed at rest by their ships in the battle at the ships (*Geography* 9.5.7; see [[strabo]]).

**The exile.** The Catalogue named his mother, Rhene (2.728; see [[rhene]]); this book tells where he lived and why. He had killed “a kinsman of his stepmother Eriopis, whom Oileus had as wife”, and he lived “in Phylace, away from his native land” (13.696–697; see [[eriopis]], [[oileus]] and [[nothos]]). Phylace is the town of Protesilaus and Podarces (2.695–706; see [[phylace]]). A killing at home and a life among strangers make a common story in the Iliad. Tlepolemus fled to Rhodes after killing his father's uncle (2.661–667), Lycophron lived with Ajax after killing a man on Cythera (15.430–432), and Patroclus came to Peleus' house as a boy after killing a playmate (23.85–90; see [[exile-and-refuge]]).

**His brother.** He is “the brother of Ajax” (13.695), the lesser Ajax, who stands with Telamonian Ajax a few verses later (13.701–708; see [[ajax-son-of-oileus]]). When Aeneas kills Medon in Book 15, the four verses of his birth and exile are told again (15.332–336).
