summary: Achilles is “the swift-footed son of Peleus” in Poseidon's rebuke, and ὠκύς describes the horses that carry the wounded Deiphobus to the city; the book adds the bare ὠκέας, which there runs over into a new verse.
grc: [ὠκέας]
---

**Achilles.** Poseidon, as Calchas, grants that Agamemnon may be to blame, “because he dishonored the swift-footed son of Peleus” (13.113; [[son-of-peleus]], [[aitios]]). The compound ποδώκης names the absent hero; elsewhere in this book his swiftness is πόδας ταχύς, “swift of foot” (13.348; [[swift]]), and Ajax, says Idomeneus, would yield to him in speed alone (13.324–325; [[breaker-of-men]]).

**Deiphobus' horses.** When Meriones has wounded Deiphobus in the arm, Polites leads him “out of grim-sounding war, until he came to his horses, his swift horses, which were standing behind the battle and the war” (13.535–536; [[deiphobus]], [[polites]], [[grim-sounding]]). The phrase ὠκέας ἵππους is split over the end of the verse, ἵππους / ὠκέας, so the bare ὠκέας is added to the entry. The same verses carry Hector out of the battle when Ajax's stone has struck him (14.429–432). See [[horses]].
