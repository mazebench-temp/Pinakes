---
summary: Imbrius falls “and around him his armor rang, inlaid with bronze,” and Deiphobus' horses wait behind the battle with “the inlaid chariot.”
---

Teucer's spear strikes Imbrius below the ear, and he falls like an ash tree on a mountain peak: “so he fell, and around him his armor rang, inlaid with bronze” (13.177–181; [[imbrius]], [[teucer]], [[the-felled-ash]]). The half-verse ἀμφὶ δέ οἱ βράχε τεύχεα ποικίλα χαλκῷ was the formula of Alcmaon's fall in Book 12 (12.396). Teucer rushes to strip the armor, and Hector throws at him (13.182–184; [[stripping-the-dead]]). Later Polites leads the wounded Deiphobus to “his swift horses, which were standing behind the battle and the war / with their charioteer and the inlaid chariot” (13.535–537; [[deiphobus]], [[chariot]]). Elsewhere chariots are ἅρματα ποικίλα χαλκῷ, “inlaid with bronze” (4.226, 10.322).
