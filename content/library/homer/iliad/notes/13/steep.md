---
summary: Calydon is “steep,” and so is Troy, in Menelaus' threat and Hector's despair, and the word is figurative in the “steep climb” before Hector and the “sheer destruction” that Hector foresees.
grc: [αἰπεινή, αἰπήν]
---

**Places.** Thoas, whose voice Poseidon takes, is the man “who in all Pleuron and steep Calydon was lord of the Aetolians” (13.217–218; [[thoas]], [[calydon]], [[pleuron]]); in the Catalogue Calydon was “rocky” (2.640; [[rocky]]). Troy is steep twice. Menelaus warns the Trojans of the wrath of Zeus, god of guests, “who will one day destroy your steep city” (13.624–625; [[xenia]], [[who-thunders-loud]]), and Hector, who cannot find his companions, reproaches Paris: “Now from top to bottom all of steep Ilios has perished; now sheer destruction is sure for you” (13.772–773; [[hector]], [[paris]], [[troy]]).

**Figures.** Idomeneus tells Meriones that Hector will find it hard to reach the ships against the Ajaxes and Teucer: “It will be a steep climb for him, for all his eagerness to fight” (13.317). αἰπύ οἱ ἐσσεῖται, “it will be steep for him,” is the uphill of the battle. Hector's αἰπὺς ὄλεθρος, “sheer destruction” (13.773), is the fall with no way back.
