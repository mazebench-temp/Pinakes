summary: Hector answers Ajax's challenge in protest, “Ajax, blundering in speech, you braggart, what a thing you have said!” (13.824), in the shorter form ποῖον ἔειπες that the Odyssey uses after an abusive address.
grc: ["ποῖον ἔειπες"]
---

“Ajax, blundering in speech, you braggart, what a thing you have said!” (13.824). The full formula is ποῖον τὸν μῦθον ἔειπες (see the entry); after two insults only ποῖον ἔειπες fits the verse. The line has the shape of Poseidon's protest to Hera in Book 8, “Hera, reckless in speech, what a thing you have said!” (Ἥρη ἀπτοεπὲς ποῖον τὸν μῦθον ἔειπες, 8.209): a name, a word for faulty speech, and the protest. ἁμαρτοεπής, “blundering in speech”, occurs only here (compare ἀφαμαρτοεπής, 3.215), and βουγάϊος, “braggart”, only here and in Antinous' abuse of the beggar Irus (Od. 18.79); both are uncertain in sense (see the translation's note on 13.824).

In the Odyssey the short form ends three abusive addresses by suitors: Antinous' twice to Telemachus, “high in speech, unrestrained in might” (Od. 2.85, 17.406), and Leocritus' to Mentor, “reckless, crazed in mind” (Od. 2.243). Hector's protest answers Ajax's prophecy that he will soon pray for swift horses to carry him in flight to the city, and the eagle on the right that followed it (13.817–823; see [[divination]]). He answers with an impossible wish, to be the son of Zeus and Hera as surely as this day brings evil to the Argives (13.825–832; see [[hector]] and [[ajax]]).
