He is named twice among those called to the defense: by Poseidon, “and Thoas and Deipyrus” (13.92), and by Idomeneus, who calls on “Ascalaphus and Aphareus and Deipyrus and Meriones and Antilochus” when Aeneas comes at him (13.477–479; see [[idomeneus]]). His death takes five verses. Helenus strikes him close at hand on the temple with “a great Thracian sword” and strikes off his helmet, which “glanced away and fell to the ground, and one of the Achaeans picked it up as it rolled among the feet of the fighters; and murky night covered his eyes” (13.576–580; see [[helenus]], [[sword]] and [[helmet]]). The helmet is saved and the man is not. The seer Helenus, “by far the best of the bird-seers” (6.76; see [[best-of-bird-seers]]), fights here with a sword; the silver-studded sword of Asteropaeus that Achilles gives as a prize is also Thracian (23.807–808). Grief seizes Menelaus, and he goes at Helenus (13.581–582; see [[menelaus]]).

Deipyrus is the last of three captains of the night watch (9.83) to die in this book, after Ascalaphus and Aphareus (13.518–520, 13.541–544; see [[night-watch]], [[ascalaphus]] and [[aphareus]]).
