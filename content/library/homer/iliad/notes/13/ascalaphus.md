**The spear meant for another.** Idomeneus has called him to his side with Aphareus, Deipyrus, Meriones and Antilochus (13.477–479; see [[idomeneus]]). The spear that kills him was thrown at Idomeneus, against whom Deiphobus “had always kept a steady grudge”, and it misses Idomeneus for the second time; the first killed Hypsenor (13.402–412, 13.516–518; see [[deiphobus]] and [[kotos]]). He falls in the words of Oenomaus' death a few lines before: “he fell in the dust and clutched the earth in his palm” (13.508, 13.520).

**His father.** He is υἱὸν Ἐνυαλίοιο, “the son of Enyalius” (13.519), the war god under his other name (see [[enyalius]] and [[ares]]); the Catalogue told how Astyoche bore him to Ares in her father's upper room (2.511–515; see [[the-birth-of-ascalaphus-and-ialmenus]]). The narrator turns at once to the father, who sits on Olympus under golden clouds with the other gods, held back from the war by the counsels of Zeus (13.521–525), the ban of Book 8 (8.5–27; see [[zeus]]). He learns of it from Hera, beats his thighs and arms to avenge his son, until Athena takes his helmet, shield and spear and sits him down again (15.110–142).

**Over the body.** The two sides rush at each other hand to hand around him. Deiphobus snatches his shining helmet, and Meriones strikes Deiphobus' arm with his spear, so that “the helmet with its socket for the plume fell to the ground with a clang” (13.526–530; see [[meriones]] and [[with-a-socket-for-the-plume]]). The poem does not say what becomes of the body. Ascalaphus is the first of three captains of the night watch to die in the book; Aphareus and Deipyrus follow (9.80–88; 13.541–544, 13.576–580; see [[night-watch]], [[aphareus]] and [[deipyrus]]).
