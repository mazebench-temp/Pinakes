---

“Two trusty companions stooped under him, Mecisteus, son of Echius, and brilliant Alastor, and carried him, groaning heavily, to the hollow ships” (13.421–423; see [[mecisteus-son-of-echius]] and [[hypsenor-son-of-hippasus]]). Here the man they carry is Antilochus' own companion (ἑοῦ … ἑταίρου, 13.419; see [[antilochus]]), and Antilochus is Nestor's son. The bearers may be Pylians, like the Alastor among Nestor's captains (4.295; see [[alastor]]), but the poem does not say so; in Book 8 the same pair carried Telamonian Ajax's brother. Whether Hypsenor is dead or dying when they lift him is not clear (see [[mecisteus-son-of-echius]] and the translation's note on 13.412–423).
