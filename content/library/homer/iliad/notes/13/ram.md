summary: The ram of Book 13 leads the flock to water: the Trojan army follows Aeneas as sheep follow the ram from the pasture to drink (13.492–495).
grc: [κτίλον]
---

λαοὶ ἕπονθʼ, ὡς εἴ τε μετὰ κτίλον ἕσπετο μῆλα / πιόμενʼ ἐκ βοτάνης: “the army followed, as sheep follow the ram from the pasture to drink” (13.492–493). κτίλος, “tame”, is the ram that leads the flock, the word of Priam's likeness of Odysseus, who “ranges like a ram through the ranks of men” (3.196; see [[ram-among-the-sheep]]). There the ram went through a flock that stayed in its place; here the flock follows it, and Aeneas, whom the Trojans follow, rejoices as the shepherd does (13.494–495; see [[sheep-following-the-ram]], [[sheep]] and [[aeneas]]).
