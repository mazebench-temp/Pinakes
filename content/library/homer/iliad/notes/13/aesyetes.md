summary: Alcathous, Anchises' son-in-law, is “the dear son of Aesyetes, whom Zeus had nurtured” (13.427). Whether this is the old Aesyetes whose tomb served the Trojans as a lookout (2.793) the poem does not say.
---

Αἰσυήταο διοτρεφέος φίλον υἱόν, “the dear son of Aesyetes, whom Zeus had nurtured” (13.427; see [[alcathous]] and [[zeus-nurtured]]). In Book 2 Polites keeps watch “on top of the tomb of old Aesyetes” (2.793), and the man is known only by his mound. The poem does not say whether Alcathous' father is the same man.
