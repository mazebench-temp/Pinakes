---
summary: The sharp bronze wounds Idomeneus' companion and kills Aphareus, Adamas' sharp point is made powerless against Antilochus' shield, and Menelaus goes at Helenus “brandishing his sharp spear.”
---

The formula ὀξέϊ χαλκῷ, “with the sharp bronze,” names the wound of the companion whom Idomeneus has just left with the healers, “struck behind the knee by the sharp bronze” (13.212; [[idomeneus]], [[healing]]); the eagerness of the armies in the dust “to slay one another through the throng with the sharp bronze” (13.337–338; [[the-dust-storm]]); and the thrust of Adamas, who “stabbed the middle of his shield with the sharp bronze” (13.561), the shield of Antilochus, before Poseidon took the force from the point (13.562–563; [[adamas]], [[antilochus]], [[dark-haired]]). With ὀξέϊ δουρί Aeneas strikes Aphareus in the throat, “with his sharp spear” (13.542; [[aphareus]]). Menelaus goes at Helenus “brandishing his sharp spear,” and the two shoot at the same moment, “the one was eager to throw his sharp spear, and the other to shoot an arrow from the string” (13.583–585; [[menelaus]], [[helenus]]). The second spear is ὀξυόεις, the lengthened adjective, as Menelaus' spear was when he killed Scamandrius (5.50).
