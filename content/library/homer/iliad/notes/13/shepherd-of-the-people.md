---
summary: Hypsenor, son of Hippasus, “shepherd of the people,” is struck by the spear Deiphobus meant for Idomeneus, and the sling with which Agenor binds Helenus' hand is carried by the attendant of “the shepherd of the people.”
---

**Hypsenor.** Deiphobus' spear flies over Idomeneus' shield, “Yet Deiphobus did not let it go in vain from his heavy hand, but struck Hypsenor, son of Hippasus, shepherd of the people, in the liver beneath the midriff, and at once loosened his knees” (13.410–412; [[hypsenor-son-of-hippasus]], [[hippasus-father-of-hypsenor]], [[deiphobus]], [[loosened-his-limbs]]). The leader's title is given to a man named only here, at the moment he falls; whether he is dead or dying when he is carried off groaning, the verses do not settle (13.420–423; see the translation's note on 13.412–423).

**Helenus' sling.** When Menelaus' spear has pinned Helenus' hand to his bow, Agenor draws it out, “and bound the hand up with well-twisted sheep's wool, a sling, which an attendant carried for him, the shepherd of the people” (13.599–600; [[agenor]], [[helenus]], [[sling]]). The Greek does not make clear whether the shepherd of the people is Agenor or Helenus.
