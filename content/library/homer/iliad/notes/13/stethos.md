---
summary: The chest is where the brave man takes his wounds, where Idomeneus' bronze shines, where Alcathous is struck as he stands still, and where Menelaus sets his heel on Peisander; it is also where the spirit and the mind live.
grc: [στήθεσι]
---

**Wounds in front.** Idomeneus tells Meriones that a missile would never strike him on the back of the neck or the back, “but it would meet your chest or your belly / as you pressed forward” (13.289–291; see [[the-brave-man-in-ambush]]). The book's blows land there. Hector strikes Amphimachus “in the chest” (13.186), Idomeneus stabs Alcathous, who stands bound by Poseidon like a pillar, “in the middle of the chest” (13.438), and Helenus' arrow strikes Menelaus' chest and glances off his corselet (13.586–587).

**Shining and trampled.** Idomeneus goes out like the lightning of Zeus, “so the bronze shone about his chest as he ran” (13.245; see [[like-lightning]]). Menelaus, having killed Peisander, “set his heel on his chest / and stripped off his armor” (λὰξ ἐν στήθεσι βαίνων, 13.618–619; see [[stripping-the-dead]] and [[menelaus]]).

**The seat of feeling.** In the breast are the heart and the mind: the son of Oileus feels “the heart in my own breast” eager for war (13.73), the heart of Aeneas rejoices in his breast to see the army follow him (13.494), Zeus puts a good mind “in the breast of another” (13.732; see [[different-gifts]]), and Hector cannot confound the spirit in the breasts of the Achaeans (13.808; see [[thymos]]).
