---
summary: Poseidon, watching from Samothrace, pities the Achaeans as they are beaten down by the Trojans, and his pity, joined with indignation against Zeus, brings him over the sea to help them.
passages:
  - 13.10-16 | Poseidon watches from Samothrace
---

Zeus has turned his eyes away from Troy, but Poseidon is watching from the peak of Thracian Samos, marveling at the war: “There he had come up out of the sea and sat, and he pitied the Achaeans / as they were beaten down by the Trojans, and he was fiercely indignant with Zeus” (ἐλέαιρε δʼ Ἀχαιοὺς / Τρωσὶν δαμναμένους, 13.15–16; see [[poseidon]], [[watching-the-war]] and [[nemesis]]). This is the pity of a god who looks on (see the article above), and like Hera's in Book 8, when she saw the Danaans perishing and called on Athena (8.350–353; see the note on Book 8), it moves him to act. At once he comes down from the mountain and drives over the sea to the ships (13.17–38; see [[poseidon-rallies-the-achaeans]]). Pity and indignation are given together: the pity for the men, the indignation against the god who lets them be beaten (the verse returns when the narrator explains the god's secret help, 13.352–353, with ἤχθετο, “he was vexed”, in place of the pity).

The imperative of the same verb was Odysseus' plea to Achilles in Book 9: “yet the rest, all the Achaeans, / worn down throughout the army, pity them; like a god / they will honor you” (ἐλέαιρε, 9.301–303; see [[achilles]]). Achilles refused. The god does for the Achaeans what their best fighter would not.

Pity of another kind protects one man. When Adamas stabs Antilochus' shield, Poseidon makes the spear-point powerless, “begrudging him the other's life” (βιότοιο μεγήρας, 13.562–563; see [[antilochus]] and [[divine-intervention]]): he will not let the Trojan have Nestor's son. In the Odyssey Athena, in the likeness of Mentor, prays to the same god at Pylos: “Hear me, Poseidon, earth-holder, and do not begrudge” the fulfillment of their prayers, and give glory first to Nestor and his sons (*Odyssey* 3.55–57).
