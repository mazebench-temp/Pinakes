---
summary: An eagle “flying high” comes on the right as Ajax foretells Hector's flight, and the Achaeans shout at the omen.
---

“As he spoke, a bird flew by on the right, / an eagle flying high; and the army of the Achaeans shouted out, / emboldened by the omen” (13.821–823; [[eagle]], [[divination]], [[ajax]]). The bird answers the eagle of Book 12, “flying high, on the left,” as the Trojans were about to cross the ditch (12.200–201; [[the-eagle-and-the-serpent]]). Then Polydamas read the sign and Hector refused it; now the Achaean army reads it for itself, and Hector again answers with defiance, wishing he were the son of Zeus (13.823–832; [[aegis-bearing]], [[hector]]). The book ends with the shouts of both armies reaching “the upper air and the rays of Zeus” (13.834–837).
