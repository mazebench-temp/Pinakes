---
summary: “Godlike Polyphetes,” named only here, is one of the men around whom the battle is thickest when Hector and Paris go back into the fighting.
---

After Hector's rebuke the two brothers go “where the battle and the din of battle were greatest, / around Cebriones and noble Polydamas, / Phalces and Orthaeus and godlike Polyphetes, / Palmys and Ascanius and Morys, son of Hippotion, / who had come from deep-soiled Ascania as relief / on the morning before” (13.789–794; [[polyphetes]], [[blameless]], [[deep-soiled]], [[ascania]]). Polyphetes is named nowhere else in Homer, and the epithet is all the poem tells of him, as it is all it tells of “godlike Lycophontes” among Teucer's victims (8.275). The honorific that Nestor gave the heroes of the past (1.264) here dignifies a name in a list of the Trojans' allies ([[trojan-allies]]).
