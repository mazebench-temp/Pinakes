summary: Menelaus calls the Trojans “insatiable of the dread war cry,” and ends his speech with the maxim that men have their fill of all things, “but the Trojans are insatiable of battle”; the book adds the plural ἀκόρητοι.
grc: [ἀκόρητοι]
---

Menelaus' speech over Peisander begins and ends with the word. It opens: “So, I think, you will leave the ships of the Danaans of the swift horses, you overbearing Trojans, insatiable of the dread war cry” (13.620–621; [[overbearing]], [[of-the-swift-horses]]). It closes with a maxim: “Of all things there is satiety, of sleep and of love, of sweet song and of flawless dancing, of which a man would rather have his fill than of war; but the Trojans are insatiable of battle” (13.636–639; [[satiety-of-all-things]], [[menelaus]]). Between the two he complains to Zeus that the Trojans “cannot have their fill of the din of battle” (13.634–635): κορέσασθαι, the verb of which ἀκόρητος is the negative. A hundred lines later Polydamas fears Achilles, “a man insatiable of war” (13.746; [[insatiable-of-war]]).
