---
summary: Athena takes no part in Book 13. She is named twice, beside Ares as a judge of battle who could not have made light of the Achaean ranks, and beside Apollo in Hector's wish to be honored as they are honored.
---

**A judge of battle.** The battalions that form around the two Ajaxes are such that “not even Ares could have made light of” them if he came among them, “nor Athena who rouses armies” (13.127–128; see [[battle-order]] and [[ares]]). λαοσσόος, “who rouses armies”, is Athena's here; in later books it is given to Ares, Strife and Apollo (17.398, 20.48, 20.79; see [[who-rouses-armies]]). The two war gods measure a fight together again over Patroclus' body and in Achilles' boast (17.398–399, 20.358–359), and in Book 4 a man led through the battle by Pallas Athena would not have made light of the work (4.539–542).

**Honored like Athena.** Answering Ajax, Hector wishes that he were the son of Zeus and Hera, “and honored as Athena and Apollo are honored” (13.827), a verse he spoke on the eve of this day (8.540; see [[hector]] and [[apollo]]). The two children of Zeus whom he names fight on opposite sides of the war.

The goddess herself keeps away, as Zeus' ban requires (8.10–12). On Olympus, in Book 15, she takes Ares' helmet, shield and spear from him when he means to avenge Ascalaphus (15.121–142), and in Book 22 she takes the form of Deiphobus, who is wounded in this book, to bring Hector to his death (22.226–247; see [[deiphobus]]).
