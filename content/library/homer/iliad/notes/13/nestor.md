---
summary: Nestor takes no part in Book 13. He is still in his hut with the wounded Machaon, and the poem names him only as the father of Antilochus, whom Poseidon guards at the ships (13.400, 13.555).
---

“Antilochus, son of great-hearted Nestor” (13.400) is the verse that has named his son since Book 5 (5.565; see [[antilochus]] and [[great-hearted]]); later in the book Poseidon “guarded the son of Nestor all around, even among many missiles” (13.554–555; see [[poseidon]]). The old man himself has been in his hut since he brought Machaon from the field (11.618–643). The din of the battle at the ships reaches him there at the beginning of the next book, as he sits drinking with Machaon, and he goes out to look (14.1–8; see [[machaon]] and [[the-battle-at-the-ships]]).
