summary: Antilochus, “steadfast in battle” (μενεχάρμης), kills Asius' charioteer, and Podarces, “steadfast in battle” (μενεπτόλεμος), leads the Phthians with Medon; the book adds the nominative μενεχάρμης.
grc: [μενεχάρμης]
---

Two compounds, one sense. When Asius falls, his charioteer does not dare to turn the horses, “and Antilochus, steadfast in battle, pierced him squarely through the middle with his spear” (13.396–397; [[antilochus]], [[asius]]): μενεχάρμης joins μένω, “stand fast,” to χάρμη, “the joy of battle” ([[charme]]). It becomes Antilochus' epithet: he has it again in Books 15 and 23 (15.582, 23.419). On the left of the line, “in front of the Phthians Medon and Podarces, steadfast in battle” (13.693; [[podarces]], [[medon]], [[phthians]]): μενεπτόλεμος, with πτόλεμος, “war.” In the Catalogue Podarces was an “offshoot of Ares” (2.704), and μενεπτόλεμος was Polypoetes' word (2.740); the translation renders both compounds alike. Compare [[staunch]] (μενεδήϊος), a third compound of μένω.
