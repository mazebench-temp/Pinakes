summary: The Catalogue's story of Ascalaphus' birth is recalled at his death. Deiphobus kills “the son of Enyalius”, and Ares, held on Olympus by Zeus' ban, has not yet heard that his son has fallen (13.518–525).
refs: [13.519, 13.521-522]
---

The union of Ares and the maiden Astyoche in her father's upper room (2.511–515) gave the leaders of Orchomenus a god for a father. Book 13 shows what the father can do for his son. Deiphobus' spear kills υἱὸν Ἐνυαλίοιο, “the son of Enyalius” (13.519; see [[ascalaphus]] and [[enyalius]]), and the narrator turns to Ares, who “had not yet heard at all that his son had fallen in the fierce battle”, but sat on Olympus under golden clouds, “held back by the counsels of Zeus” (13.521–524; see [[ares]] and [[zeus]]). When Hera tells him, he calls on the gods not to blame him if he goes to avenge his son, until Athena disarms him (15.110–142). Hera speaks of Ascalaphus as the son “whom mighty Ares says is his own” (15.112). The other son of the story, Ialmenus, is not named after the night watch (9.82; see [[ialmenus]]).
