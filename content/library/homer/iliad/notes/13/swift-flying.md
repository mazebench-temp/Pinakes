---
summary: Poseidon yokes his horses at Aegae, “swift-flying, with flowing manes of gold,” in the verses of Zeus' drive to Ida in Book 8.
---

At Aegae Poseidon “yoked to his chariot his two bronze-footed horses, swift-flying, with flowing manes of gold, and he himself put on gold about his body, and took the whip, golden and well made” (13.23–26; [[bronze-footed]], [[well-made]], [[chariot-journey]]). Apart from their first words the four verses are those of Zeus' journey to Ida (8.41–44). The horses fly over the waves, “very swiftly, and the bronze axle was not wetted beneath” (13.30), and they are ἐΰσκαρθμοι, “bounding,” as they carry him to the ships (13.31; [[bounding]]). He unyokes them in a cave between Tenedos and Imbros and hobbles them with golden hobbles (13.32–38). See [[poseidon]] and [[horses]].
