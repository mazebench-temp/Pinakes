summary: Two sons of Priam come on behind shields “balanced on every side,” Deiphobus at the start of the battle and Hector at its end, and Idomeneus hides beneath his; the book adds the dative πάντοσʼ ἐΐσῃ.
grc: ["πάντοσʼ ἐΐσῃ"]
---

One verse frames the book's fighting. Deiphobus comes on “in high spirits, / the son of Priam; and he held in front of him his shield, balanced on every side, / stepping lightly forward and advancing under cover of his shield” (13.156–158; [[deiphobus]]). Meriones' spear strikes it “on his shield, balanced on every side, / made of bull's hide,” and snaps at the socket (13.160–162; [[meriones]], [[of-bulls-hide]]). At the end of the book Hector leads the Trojans in the same words: “the son of Priam; and he held in front of him his shield, balanced on every side, / thick with hides, and much bronze was beaten over it,” as he tests the battalions “under cover of his shield” (13.803–807; [[hector]]). 13.803 repeats 13.157, and ὑπασπίδια, “under cover of his shield,” describes the advance of both brothers (13.158, 13.807).

Between them the shield saves an Achaean. Deiphobus throws at Idomeneus, “but he, looking straight at it, avoided the bronze spear, / Idomeneus; for he was hidden beneath his shield, balanced on every side, / which he carried, made of oxhides and gleaming bronze, / rounded, fitted with two rods” (13.404–407; [[idomeneus]], [[gleaming-bronze]]). The dative πάντοσʼ ἐΐσῃ is new to the entry. The spear flies over him and strikes Hypsenor (13.408–412; [[hypsenor-son-of-hippasus]]). See [[shield]].
