summary: The ash spear hangs from Helenus' pierced hand as he draws back, and the Locrians fight without “ash spears”; the book adds the plural μείλινα δοῦρα.
grc: ["μείλινα δοῦρα"]
en: [ash spears]
---

Menelaus' spear goes through the hand in which Helenus holds his bow, and Helenus draws back into the crowd of his companions “with his hand hanging at his side; and the ash spear trailed along with it” (13.593–597; [[helenus]], [[menelaus]]). Great-hearted Agenor draws it out and binds the hand with a sling (13.598–600; [[agenor]], [[sling]], [[healing]]). The spear hangs from the wound as Tlepolemus' ash spear dragged from Sarpedon's thigh, until Pelagon pushed it out (5.663–666, 5.694–695; [[tlepolemus-and-sarpedon]]).

The Locrians, who fight behind the line with bows and slings, “had no bronze-fitted helmets with horsehair crests, / nor had they well-rounded shields and ash spears” (13.714–715; [[locrians]], [[bronze-fitted]], [[horsehair-crested]], [[well-rounded]]). The plural μείλινα δοῦρα is new to the entry; the same verse-end closes the list of helmets, shields, corselets and ash spears that pour from the ships like snowflakes when the Achaeans arm in Book 19 (19.357–361). The tree itself stands in the simile of Imbrius' fall: “he fell like an ash tree” (13.178; [[the-felled-ash]]).
