**The glory.** Hector does not know that his men are being cut down on the left of the ships:

“And soon the glory would have been the Achaeans'; / so hard did the earth-holder, the shaker of the earth, / urge on the Argives, and himself defended them with his strength; / but Hector held on where he had first leapt in at the gates and the wall” (13.676–679; see [[kydos]], [[poseidon]] and [[hector]]). The second part of the turn is not “had not” (εἰ μή) but “but” (ἀλλά). The god who might have decided the battle is named in the first part, and what stops him is a man holding his ground where the wall was built lowest (13.679–684; see [[the-achaean-wall]]).

**Polydamas.** “Then wretchedly from the ships and the huts / the Trojans would have retreated to windy Ilios, / had not Polydamas come and stood beside bold Hector and said” (13.723–725). The third verse is the one that brought Polydamas to Hector's side at the ditch and at the omen of the eagle (12.60, 12.210; see [[polydamas]]). What prevents the rout is a man's counsel, as Odysseus' call to Diomedes saved the Achaeans in Book 11 (11.310–312). Hector takes the advice, as he did at the ditch, in the same verse, “his safe counsel pleased Hector” (13.748 = 12.80).
