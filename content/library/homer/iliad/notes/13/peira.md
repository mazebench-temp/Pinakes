summary: Deiphobus weighs whether to take a companion or “to try it even alone”, and at the end of the book Hector goes along the Achaean battalions and makes trial of them, to see whether they will give way.
grc: [πειρήσαιτο]
en: [made trial]
---

**Trying alone.** Challenged by Idomeneus, Deiphobus “was torn two ways in thought, / whether to take as his companion one of the great-hearted Trojans, / drawing back, or to try it even alone” (ἦ πειρήσαιτο καὶ οἶος, 13.455–457; see [[deiphobus]] and [[kerdos]]). To try (πειράομαι) is to test oneself against an enemy, and Deiphobus decides against the test and fetches Aeneas.

**Testing the ranks.** At the end of the book Hector leads the Trojans like Ares: “And everywhere along the battalions he made trial of them, advancing, / to see if they would give way to him somehow as he stepped forward under cover of his shield; / but he did not confound the spirit in the breasts of the Achaeans” (πάντῃ δʼ ἀμφὶ φάλαγγας ἐπειρᾶτο προποδίζων, 13.806–808; see [[hector]] and [[battle-order]]). The test is the one that the close ranks around the two Ajaxes met at the start of the battle, when Hector stopped against them and gave ground (13.145–148). The words of the advance, προποδίζων and ὑπασπίδια, are the words of Deiphobus' advance behind his shield earlier in the book (13.158; see [[shield]]).
