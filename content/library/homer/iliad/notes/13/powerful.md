summary: Twice the book calls Poseidon “the powerful earth-shaker,” as he keeps watch from Samothrace and as he speaks to Idomeneus in Thoas' voice; in the fuller εὐρὺ κρείων of Agamemnon (13.112) the word belongs to wide-ruling.
except: [13.112]
---

The formula κρείων ἐνοσίχθων, first used when Poseidon refused Hera's proposal in Book 8 (8.208), opens his part in this book: when Zeus has turned his eyes away, “the powerful earth-shaker kept no blind watch” (13.10; [[earth-shaker]], [[samothrace]]). It also introduces his first speech in disguise: “And the powerful earth-shaker spoke to him, making his voice like that of Thoas, son of Andraemon” (13.215–216; [[thoas]], [[idomeneus]]). The title of rule is given to the god who in this book does not act openly, but “secretly … in the likeness of a man” (13.357; [[poseidon]], [[poseidon-rallies-the-achaeans]]). Agamemnon has the fuller title once, in Poseidon's own words, “wide-ruling Agamemnon” (13.112), and that line is listed under [[wide-ruling]].
