---
summary: The battle line bristles with spears, “long and flesh-cutting”; the book adds the accusative plural ταμεσίχροας.
grc: [ταμεσίχροας]
---

When Idomeneus and Meriones reach the left, “the battle, destroyer of mortals, bristled with the spears, / long and flesh-cutting, that they held” (13.339–340; [[long-spears]], [[bold-hearted]]). It is the only time in Homer that the epithet describes spears; elsewhere it is the bronze, in Apollo's call to the Trojans (4.511) and in the armed contest of the funeral games (23.803). Here it stands in the narrator's picture of the line, with “the gleam of bronze from the shining helmets” that blinds the eyes (13.340–343). The accusative plural ταμεσίχροας is new to the entry.
