---
summary: At the end of the book Ajax, “coming on with long strides”, is the first to challenge Hector, and the two exchange threats while the armies shout (13.809–837). It is a challenge in battle, not an agreed duel, but it sets against each other the two men of the duel of Book 7, and they meet in Book 14.
---

“And Ajax was the first to challenge him, coming on with long strides” (Αἴας δὲ πρῶτος προκαλέσσατο μακρὰ βιβάσθων, 13.809; see [[ajax]] and [[hector]]). He went out to the duel of Book 7 in the same way, “with long strides, brandishing his long-shadowed spear” (μακρὰ βιβάς, 7.213; see [[the-duel-of-hector-and-ajax]]). Here no terms are proposed, no lots cast and no ground measured (see the entry). Ajax's challenge is a speech of defiance: the Achaeans know how to fight, and it was the lash of Zeus that beat them down; they have hands to defend the ships; Troy will be taken first; and Hector will soon pray for horses swifter than hawks to carry him in flight (13.810–820). An eagle flies on the right as he speaks (13.821–823; see [[divination]]). Hector answers with an impossible wish and a threat: Ajax will be killed “if you dare / to stand up to my long spear”, and will glut the dogs and the birds of the Trojans (13.824–832; see [[what-a-word]]).

In Book 7 the two men fought until the heralds parted them at nightfall. Here the challenge leads only to the shout of the two armies (13.833–837; see [[the-armies-clash]]). They meet in the next book, when Hector's spear strikes Ajax where the straps of his shield and his sword cross on his chest, and Ajax answers with a stone that fells him (14.402–420; see [[stone-throwing]]).
