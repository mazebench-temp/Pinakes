---
summary: Idomeneus breaks off his talk with Meriones about courage and the ambush: “let us no longer talk like this, like children, standing here, lest someone be overbearingly indignant” (13.292–294).
grc: ["νηπύτιοι ὣς"]
---

ἀλλʼ ἄγε μηκέτι ταῦτα λεγώμεθα νηπύτιοι ὣς / ἑσταότες, μή πού τις ὑπερφιάλως νεμεσήσῃ: “But come, let us no longer talk like this, like children, standing here, lest someone be overbearingly indignant” (13.292–293). νηπύτιος is a form of νήπιος, “childish, foolish” (see [[nepios]]). The likeness turns on the same fault as Nestor's in Book 2, where the Achaeans “talk in the assembly like children” (2.337–338): talk in place of action. Idomeneus has let his attendant speak of his spoils and of his place among the foremost, and has answered with the long picture of the brave man and the coward in ambush (13.249–291; see [[ambush]] and [[meriones]]). Now he ends it. A man who saw them standing and talking would be indignant (see [[nemesis]]), and he sends Meriones to take a spear from the hut (13.294).

The verse is said again in Book 20, when Aeneas ends his long speech of genealogy to Achilles (20.244 = 13.292). Twice in the same book a hero tells his enemy not to try to frighten him with words, “as if I were a child” (νηπύτιον ὥς, 20.200–201, 20.431–432).
