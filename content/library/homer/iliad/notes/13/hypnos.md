---
summary: Sleep comes first in Menelaus' list of the things of which men have their fill, “of sleep and of love, / of sweet song and of flawless dancing”, set against the Trojans, who are insatiable of battle.
---

Book 13 names sleep once, in Menelaus' complaint to Zeus over the body of Peisander: “Of all things there is satiety, of sleep and of love, / of sweet song and of flawless dancing, / of which a man would rather have his fill / than of war; but the Trojans are insatiable of battle” (13.636–639; see [[satiety-of-all-things]] and [[menelaus]]).

The first two of Menelaus' pleasures are the two powers of the next book. Hera borrows Aphrodite's band and wins over Sleep, the brother of Death, and Zeus lies on the peak of Gargarus “overcome by sleep and love” (ὕπνῳ καὶ φιλότητι δαμείς, 14.352–353); then Sleep runs to the ships to tell Poseidon to help the Danaans with a ready heart while Zeus sleeps (14.354–360; see [[philotes]] and [[poseidon-rallies-the-achaeans]]). Sleep and love stand together in one verse only in these two places in Homer.
