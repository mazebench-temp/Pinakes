# αὐτὰρ ὃ Ἰφίκλοιο πάϊς τοῦ Φυλακίδαο (13.698) is Podarces.

{{quote:d5190763-e1f7-5a8c-88e8-c4139123a167}}

μενεπτόλεμος, “steadfast in battle” (13.693; see [[steadfast-in-battle]]), is his epithet here; in the Catalogue he was “offshoot of Ares” (2.704). His descent is the Catalogue's, “son of Iphiclus, rich in flocks, the son of Phylacus” (2.705), now “the son of Iphiclus, the son of Phylacus” (13.698; see [[iphiclus]] and [[phylacus]]). He leads the men from Phylace whom his brother led to Troy (2.695–710), and they fight beside “the ships of Ajax and of Protesilaus” (13.681), where Hector broke in (see [[protesilaus]] and [[the-death-of-protesilaus]]). With Medon he leads them “in front of the Phthians” (13.693): Strabo took the men of Protesilaus and those of Philoctetes for Phthians, like Achilles' own (*Geography* 9.5.7; see [[phthians]] and [[medon]]). The Iliad does not name him again. His name is also the epithet ποδάρκης, “swift-footed”, of Achilles (see [[podarkes]]).
