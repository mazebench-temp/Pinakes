---
summary: Book 13 is Poseidon's book, and his commonest epithet names him seven times, from the watch he keeps on Samothrace to his guard over Antilochus.
---

Book 13 and Book 20, where the gods go to war, each use ἐνοσίχθων seven times, more than any other book of the Iliad. Here the epithet follows Poseidon's secret campaign for the Achaeans ([[poseidon]], [[poseidon-rallies-the-achaeans]]). It opens his part: “But the powerful earth-shaker kept no blind watch” (13.10), with κρείων ἐνοσίχθων, the formula of his refusal to Hera in Book 8 (8.208; [[powerful]]), as he sits on Samothrace while Zeus looks away. In the cave between Tenedos and Imbros “Poseidon the earth-shaker halted his horses” (13.34; [[ambrosial]]). When he has struck the two Ajaxes with his staff, he leaves them like a hawk: “so from them darted Poseidon the earth-shaker” (13.65; [[the-hawk-from-the-rock]]). He rouses the men in the rear: “But the earth-shaker / came easily among them and urged on the strong battalions” (13.89–90). In the likeness of Thoas, “the powerful earth-shaker spoke to him,” to Idomeneus, and “Poseidon the earth-shaker answered him” (13.215, 13.231; [[idomeneus]]). Last, he guards Antilochus among the missiles: “for Poseidon the earth-shaker / guarded the son of Nestor all around” (13.554–555; [[antilochus]]).

The book gives him his other titles as well. He is “the earth-holder, the shaker of the earth,” γαιήοχος ἐννοσίγαιος, when he comes to the Ajaxes in Calchas' likeness, when he strikes them, and when he urges on the Argives on the left (13.43, 13.59, 13.677; [[earth-holder]], [[shaker-of-the-earth]]), and “dark-haired Poseidon” when he makes Adamas' spear powerless (13.563; [[dark-haired]]). His anger at the death of his grandson Amphimachus (13.206–209; [[amphimachus]]) recalls Nestor's story of his grandson's father and uncle, whom “their father, the wide-ruling earth-shaker,” saved from Nestor's spear (11.750–752; [[the-moliones]]).
