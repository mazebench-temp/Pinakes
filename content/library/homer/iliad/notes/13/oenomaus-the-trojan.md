“But Idomeneus struck Oenomaus in the middle of the belly, / and broke the plate of his corselet” (13.506–507; see [[idomeneus]] and [[corselet]]). He falls in the dust and clutches the earth in his palm (13.508), the half-verse that ends Ascalaphus a few lines later (13.520; see [[ascalaphus]]). Idomeneus draws out his spear but cannot take the armor from the dead man's shoulders: “his legs were no longer steady when he rushed, / to dart after his own spear or to avoid a missile” (13.512–513; see [[stripping-the-dead]]). Over this body the narrator shows the age of the “half-gray” Cretan (13.361; see [[half-gray]]).
