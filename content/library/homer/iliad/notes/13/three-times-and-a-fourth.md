summary: The pattern measures a god's journey instead of a hero's attack: from the peak of Samothrace Poseidon takes three strides, “and with the fourth he reached his goal”, Aegae (13.17–21).
grc: ["τὸ δὲ τέτρατον"]
en: [with the fourth he reached his goal]
passages:
  - 13.17-22 | Poseidon's four strides
---

“Three strides he took, and with the fourth he reached his goal, / Aegae, where his glorious house is built in the depths of the sea” (13.20–21; see [[poseidon]] and [[aegae]]). τρὶς μὲν ὀρέξατʼ ἰών, τὸ δὲ τέτρατον ἵκετο τέκμωρ: the verse has the “three times” and the “fourth” of the pattern, and τέκμωρ is the goal (see the translation's note on 13.20–22). τὸ δὲ τέτρατον returns when Asteropaeus has tried three times to pull Achilles' spear from the riverbank, “and the fourth time” means to bend and break it (21.176–178; see [[asteropaeus]]).

The fourth step usually marks the limit that a god sets to a man (see the entry). Here it measures what a god can do. The mountains and the woods tremble “under the immortal feet of Poseidon as he went” (13.18–19), and four strides carry him from Samothrace, where he sat watching the battle, to his house in the sea (see [[samothrace]] and [[chariot-journey]]). So the horses of the gods leap at a bound as far as a man can see from a lookout over the sea (5.770–772).
