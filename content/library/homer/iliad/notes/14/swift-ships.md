---
summary: The Trojans fight “at the swift ships,” and the props of the swift ships lie loose among the fighters' feet; the “swift Night” of 14.261 is excepted; the book adds the genitive θοάων.
grc: [θοάων]
except: [14.261]
---

Nestor tells Agamemnon that the Trojans “keep up a relentless battle at the swift ships / without rest” (14.57–58; [[nestor]], [[relentless]]). When Ajax looks for a stone to throw at Hector, he takes one of “the props of the swift ships,” which “rolled about at the feet of the fighters” (14.410–411; [[props]], [[ajax]], [[stone-throwing]]): the battle has reached the hulls. The genitive θοάων, set apart from νηῶν in the verse, is new to the entry; the Iliad uses it only of ships. At 14.261, Νυκτὶ θοῇ, “swift Night,” is the goddess, and the line is excepted, as is νυκτὶ θοῇ at 12.463 ([[swift-night]]). See [[ships]].
