---
summary: Zeus names Peirithous, the son whom Ixion's wife bore him, “a counselor equal to the gods”; the book adds the accusative of the formula.
grc: ["θεόφιν μήστωρʼ ἀτάλαντον"]
---

The first of Zeus' loves is “the wife of Ixion, / who bore Peirithous, a counselor equal to the gods” (14.317–318; [[ixion]], [[peirithous]], [[the-loves-of-zeus]]). The formula that named Priam rising in the Trojan assembly (7.366; [[priam]]) is here the praise of a son of Zeus. The Catalogue had already named Peirithous as a son of Zeus, “whom immortal Zeus begot,” and the father of Polypoetes (2.741–742; [[polypoetes]]); Nestor counted him among the strongest men of his youth (1.263). The accusative θεόφιν μήστωρʼ ἀτάλαντον is new to the entry. See [[zeus]].
