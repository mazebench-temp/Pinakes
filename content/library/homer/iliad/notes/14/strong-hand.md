summary: Poseidon holds his dread sword “in his strong hand,” and Polydamas boasts that his javelin did not leap in vain “from his strong hand”; the book adds the phrase χειρὸς ἄπο στιβαρῆς.
grc: [χειρὸς ἄπο στιβαρῆς]
---

**Poseidon.** “Holding a dread long-edged sword in his strong hand, / like lightning” (14.385–386; [[poseidon]], [[long-edged]], [[like-lightning]]): the line-end formula χειρὶ παχείῃ, given to a god as he leads the army.

**Polydamas.** Over Prothoenor he boasts: “Truly, I do not think that from the great-hearted son of Panthous, / from his strong hand, the javelin has leapt in vain this time” (14.454–455; [[polydamas]], [[prothoenor]], [[great-hearted]]). In Book 13 Aeneas' spear went quivering into the earth, “since it had sped in vain from his strong hand” (13.505); Polydamas denies the phrase of the miss. The order χειρὸς ἄπο στιβαρῆς, with the preposition after its noun, is new to the entry; it returns when Polypoetes throws the weight in the funeral games (23.843).
