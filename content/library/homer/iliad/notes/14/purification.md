---
summary: Hera begins her adornment by cleansing “all defilements” from her body with ambrosia, the only other place in Homer where the word λύματα is used.
---

“First, with ambrosia, she cleansed from her lovely body / all defilements” (λύματα πάντα κάθηρεν, 14.170–171; see [[ambrosia]] and [[the-adornment-of-a-goddess]]). λύματα occurs twice in Homer: here, and in Book 1, where the army “cleansed themselves, and threw the defilements into the sea” before their hecatombs to Apollo (1.313–314; see the note on Book 1). There the army cleanses itself before it approaches an angry god with sacrifices; here a goddess cleanses herself to approach her husband, and the cleanser is the gods' own ambrosia. καθαίρω, “cleanse”, is the verb Zeus uses when he bids Apollo cleanse the dark blood from Sarpedon's body before he anoints it with ambrosia (16.667–670).
