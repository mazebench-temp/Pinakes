**Hyperenor.** The last of the named killers in the rout is “the son of Atreus”:

{{quote:0a85bbaf-1f77-51a2-b1b5-88b54921e5dc}}

The verse does not say which son of Atreus (see [[hyperenor]], [[psyche]] and [[darkness-covered-his-eyes]]). Book 17 does. Menelaus warns Euphorbus that Hyperenor did not enjoy his youth when he reviled and stood against him, nor did he go home on his own feet to gladden his wife and parents (17.24–28); Euphorbus answers that Menelaus killed his brother, made his wife a widow, and boasts of it (17.34–37; see the translation's note on 14.516). Hyperenor was a son of Panthous, then, like Polydamas, who has just killed Prothoenor (14.449–450; see [[panthous]] and [[polydamas]]). Agamemnon, wounded, does not fight in the book (see [[agamemnon]] and [[the-wounded-chiefs]]).

**The wound.** οὖτα κατὰ λαπάρην, “stabbed in the flank”: so the son of Oileus has just stabbed Satnius (14.447), and so Agamemnon stabbed the Trojan Adrastus whom Menelaus had been ready to spare (6.37–65; see [[adrastus-the-trojan]]). The soul rushes out κατʼ οὐταμένην ὠτειλήν, “through the stab wound” (14.518), as Patroclus, pulling his spear from Sarpedon's body, draws out the soul with the spear point (16.505). The same words return when Hector, coming up, sees Euphorbus lying dead at Menelaus' feet with the blood running from the stab wound (17.84–86): the two brothers are killed by the same man and told of in the same phrase. Menelaus' words in Book 17, of the wife and parents whom Hyperenor did not live to gladden, repeat the thought of Peneleos' taunt in this book: Ilioneus' father and mother are to wail for him, and the wife of Promachus will not be glad at her husband's coming home (14.501–505; see [[peneleos]] and [[promachus]]).
