**A death.** Menelaus stabs Hyperenor in the flank, his soul rushes out through the wound, “and darkness covered his eyes” (τὸν δὲ σκότος ὄσσε κάλυψε, 14.519; see [[hyperenor]] and [[psyche]]), the verse that ended Adamas in Book 13 (13.575; see the note on Book 13).

**A faint.** Hector, carried to the Xanthus after Ajax's stone, is lifted down and drenched with water; he gets his breath back, looks up, kneels and vomits dark blood, “then he sank back again to the ground, and his two eyes / black night covered; and the missile still overpowered his spirit” (τὼ δέ οἱ ὄσσε / νὺξ ἐκάλυψε μέλαινα, 14.435–439; see [[hector]] and [[thymos]]). The words of death are used for a man who is not dead. It has happened to him before. Struck on the helmet by Diomedes' spear in Book 11, he sank to his knees, “and black night covered his eyes”, and came to himself and drove away (11.354–360), in the verses that told how Aeneas fell under Diomedes' stone in Book 5 and was saved by Aphrodite (5.305–310). This time he does not come to himself: he lies in the dark until Apollo, sent by Zeus, raises him in the next book (15.239–262).
