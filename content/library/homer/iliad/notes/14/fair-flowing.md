---
summary: The ford where the Trojans bring the fallen Hector is “the ford of the fair-flowing river,” the eddying Xanthus.
---

“But when they came to the ford of the fair-flowing river, / the eddying Xanthus, whom immortal Zeus begot, / there they lifted him down from the chariot to the ground, and poured water / over him” (14.433–436; [[scamander]], [[eddying]], [[hector]]). The adjective is ἐϋρρεής, “well-flowing,” of the same sense as καλλίρροος. The genitive ἐϋρρεῖος ποταμοῖο ends a verse of the simile of the stalled horse, “used to bathing in the fair-flowing river” (6.508, repeated at 15.265; [[the-stallion]]); here the river's water revives a man. The whole verse returns at 21.1 and 24.692.
