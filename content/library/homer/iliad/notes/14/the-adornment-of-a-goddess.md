summary: Hera shuts herself in the chamber Hephaestus built her, cleanses her body with ambrosia, anoints it with an oil whose fragrance reaches earth and heaven, braids her hair, and puts on Athena's robe, golden pins, a girdle of a hundred tassels, earrings, a veil white as the sun and her sandals; then she goes to Aphrodite for the strap that holds love and longing.
passages:
  - 14.166-187 | Hera adorns herself
  - 14.188-223 | Aphrodite's strap
---

**The chamber.** “She went to her chamber, which her dear son built for her, / Hephaestus, and he fitted close doors to the doorposts / with a secret bolt, which no other god could open. / There she went in and closed the shining doors” (14.166–169; see [[house]] and [[hephaestus]]). The secret bolt makes the scene private: the adornment is prepared where no god can see it. Hera will name this chamber again on Ida, when she asks Zeus to take her there instead of the open peak (14.337–340).

**Ambrosia and oil.** “First, with ambrosia, she cleansed from her lovely body / all defilements” (14.170–171; see [[ambrosia]] and [[purification]]), and anointed herself with olive oil, “ambrosial and sweet, which had been perfumed for her; / and when it was stirred in the bronze-floored house of Zeus, / even so, its fragrance reached to earth and heaven” (14.171–174; see [[bathing]]).

**Hair and clothes.** She combs her locks and plaits “the shining tresses, / fair and ambrosial, from her immortal head” (14.175–177). The robe is the work of Athena, who “had smoothed and worked for her, and set many intricate designs in it”, and Hera fastens it at her breast “with golden pins” (14.178–180; see [[robe]] and [[brooch]]). Then the girdle “fitted with a hundred tassels” (14.181; see [[girdle]]), the earrings “with three drops, clustered like mulberries; and much grace shone from them” (14.182–183; see [[earrings]] and [[charis]]), the veil, “a fine one, new-made, and it was white like the sun” (14.184–185; see [[veil]]), and last the sandals, in the verse with which men dress (14.186; see [[sandals]] and [[dressing-scene]]).

**The adornment complete.** “But when she had put all her adornment about her body” (πάντα περὶ χροῒ θήκατο κόσμον, 14.187), she goes out and calls Aphrodite apart. Her own adornment is not enough: she asks for love and longing, and Aphrodite gives her the embroidered strap, which she puts in her bosom (14.188–223; see [[aphrodites-band]]). The scene's last element, the journey, follows by way of Lemnos and Sleep (14.225–291), and its effect comes on Gargarus: “And as he saw her, so desire wrapped his shrewd mind” (14.294; see [[eros]] and [[the-deception-of-zeus]]).
