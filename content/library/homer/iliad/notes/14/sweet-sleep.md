---
summary: νήδυμος, the epithet of sleep, here names Sleep the god: “sweet Sleep” answers Hera, recalls pouring himself “sweetly” around Zeus, and runs to the ships with his message.
---

The adjective that Homer gives only to sleep here names the god Sleep in the narrative: “Answering her, sweet Sleep said” (14.242; [[sleep]]), and, when Zeus sleeps, “sweet Sleep went running to the ships of the Achaeans” (14.354; [[earth-holder]]). In his own speech he uses it of himself, recalling the day when he lulled Zeus, “pouring myself sweetly around him” (νήδυμος ἀμφιχυθείς, 14.253; [[aegis-bearing]], [[hera-drives-heracles-to-cos]]): the image of sleep poured around the sleeper, as when the Dream came to Agamemnon (2.19; [[ambrosial]]). The plain γλυκύς of the entry's other formula is given in this book to longing, in Zeus' words, “sweet longing takes hold of me” (14.328; [[himeros]]). See [[hypnos]].
