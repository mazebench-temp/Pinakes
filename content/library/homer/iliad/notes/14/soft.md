summary: The grass and flowers that the earth sends up under Zeus and Hera are “thick and soft,” and Sleep has wrapped “a soft slumber” around Zeus; the book adds both phrases.
grc: ["πυκνὸν καὶ μαλακόν", "μαλακὸν περὶ κῶμʼ"]
---

Under Zeus and Hera “the shining earth sent up fresh-sprung grass, / and dewy lotus, and crocus, and hyacinth, / thick and soft, which held them up off the ground” (14.347–349; [[lotus]], [[crocus]], [[hyacinth]], [[earth]]): the bed of the gods is made of flowers. Then Sleep runs to Poseidon with his message: “for I have wrapped a soft slumber around him” (14.359; [[sleep]], [[hypnos]]). The soft bed and the soft sleep, the two chief uses of the epithet, are both Zeus' here, and both are Hera's work. κῶμα is found only here in the Iliad; in the Odyssey Penelope, waking, says that a soft slumber wrapped her (*Odyssey* 18.201). Both phrases are new to the entry.
