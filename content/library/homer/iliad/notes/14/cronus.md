---
summary: Book 14 names Cronus as Hera's father, as the god whom Zeus put beneath the earth and the sea, and as the god around whom are “the gods below”, the Titans whom Hera names in her oath to Sleep; Zeus is three times “the son of Cronus”.
grc: [Κρόνον, Κρονίονος]
relations:
  - child: hera (14.194, 14.243)
---

**Hera's father.** Aphrodite and Sleep both address Hera as “august goddess, daughter of great Cronus” (14.194, 14.243; see [[august-goddess]] and [[hera]]), and Hera's own story begins in the days of his fall: Oceanus and Tethys reared her, “taking me from Rhea, when far-seeing Zeus put Cronus / down beneath the earth and the barren sea” (14.203–204; see [[rhea]], [[oceanus]], [[tethys]] and [[fall-of-cronus]]). It is the only place where Homer says outright that Zeus put his father below. Poseidon, whom Hera sees busy in the battle, is “her own brother” (14.156; see [[poseidon]]).

**The gods around Cronus.** Sleep asks Hera to swear so that all may be witnesses, “the gods below who are around Cronus” (οἳ ἔνερθε θεοὶ Κρόνον ἀμφὶς ἐόντες, 14.274), and she names them, “all the gods / beneath Tartarus, who are called Titans” (14.278–279; see [[titans]] and [[tartarus]]). Zeus uses the same words in Book 15 (15.225), and in Book 8 he speaks of Cronus and Iapetus sitting at the lowest limits of earth and sea, with Tartarus all around them (8.478–481; see [[iapetus]]).

**The son of Cronus.** Zeus is Κρονίων in Sleep's refusal, “I would not come near Zeus, the son of Cronus” (Κρονίονος, 14.247), a genitive found elsewhere only in the Odyssey (*Odyssey* 11.620); he is Κρονίδης in Hera's “Most dread son of Cronus” (14.330; see [[most-dread]]), and Κρόνου παῖς when he takes her in his arms (14.346; see [[son-of-cronus]] and [[zeus]]).
