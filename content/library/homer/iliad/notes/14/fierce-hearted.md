---
summary: Zeus names Heracles, whom Alcmene bore him in Thebes, “a fierce-hearted son.”
---

“Nor when I loved Semele, nor Alcmene in Thebes, / who bore Heracles, a fierce-hearted son” (14.323–324; [[alcmene]], [[heracles]], [[semele]], [[the-loves-of-zeus]]). It is the only time the Iliad gives the epithet to a man; its other Iliad use is the beast that the watchdogs hear in the simile of Book 10 (10.184). Earlier in the book Sleep spoke of Heracles as “that high-hearted son of Zeus” whom Hera drove to Cos (14.250; [[high-hearted]], [[son-of-zeus]], [[hera-drives-heracles-to-cos]]). Zeus names the son without a word of that day, to the wife who persecuted him.
