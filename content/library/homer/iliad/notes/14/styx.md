**The gods' oath.** Sleep has suffered once for doing Hera's bidding, and before he does it again he asks for the oath that binds the gods (see [[sleep]] and [[hera]]). ἀάατον Στυγὸς ὕδωρ, “the inviolable water of Styx”: ἀάατος, “inviolable”, is found in the Iliad only here, and in the Odyssey twice of the contest of the bow (*Odyssey* 21.91, 22.5). With one hand on the bountiful earth and the other on the gleaming sea, Hera calls the gods below to witness, naming “all the gods / beneath Tartarus, who are called Titans” (14.272–279; see [[earth]], [[sea]], [[titans]], [[tartarus]] and [[oath]]). Only then, “when she had sworn and completed the oath” (14.280), does Sleep go with her.

**Again in Book 15.** When Zeus wakes and accuses her, Hera swears to him by Earth and Heaven and “the down-flowing water of Styx, which is the greatest and most dread oath for the blessed gods”, by his head and by their marriage bed (15.36–40); Calypso swears the same oath to Odysseus (*Odyssey* 5.184–186). In the Catalogue the Titaressus is a branch of the water of Styx, “the dread oath” (2.755), and Athena speaks of its “sheer streams” in the world below (8.369). Aristotle reports that some saw in the oath by Styx, as in Oceanus and Tethys, the parents of becoming, a sign that the earliest theologians held water to be the first principle, “for what is oldest is most honored, and the oath is the most honored thing” (*Metaphysics* 1.3, 983b30–33; see [[aristotle]]).
