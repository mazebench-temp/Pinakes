---
summary: The wounded of Book 11 are still wounded. Machaon waits for a warm bath to wash away the clotted blood; Nestor says that a man who has been hit cannot fight; Diomedes takes the kings back to the battle, but out of range, “lest someone take wound upon wound”. Hector, struck by Ajax's stone, is revived with water at the ford and faints again.
---

**The wounded kings.** Agamemnon, Odysseus and Diomedes are “all who had been hit by the bronze” (14.27–29): Coön's spear went through Agamemnon's arm, Paris' arrow pinned Diomedes' foot, and Socus' spear tore the skin from Odysseus' ribs (11.251–253, 11.376–378, 11.434–437; see [[the-wounded-chiefs]]). Machaon, shot in the right shoulder (11.505–507), stays behind until Hecamede “heats a warm bath / for you and washes away the clotted blood” (14.6–7; see [[bathing]] and [[machaon]]). Nestor will not take them into the fight, “for a man who has been hit cannot fight” (14.63). Diomedes says that they must go all the same, “stabbed though we are” (οὐτάμενοί περ, 14.128), but keep “out of range of the missiles, lest someone take wound upon wound” (ἐφʼ ἕλκεϊ ἕλκος ἄρηται, 14.130; see [[arrows]]), and in the end the kings, “stabbed though they were”, marshal the army themselves (14.379).

**Hector.** Ajax's stone strikes Hector in the chest, and his companions carry him from the battle (14.409–432; see [[stone-throwing]]). At the ford of the Xanthus “they lifted him down from the chariot to the ground, and poured water / over him; and he got his breath back and looked up with his eyes, / and kneeling he vomited dark blood” (14.435–437; see [[dark-blood]]), and then he sank back, and black night covered his eyes (14.438–439; see [[darkness-covered-his-eyes]]). No healer comes to him; in the next book Apollo, sent by Zeus, puts strength into him (15.239–262; see [[apollo]]).
