---
summary: Aphrodite and Sleep both answer Hera with the whole verse, in the vocative, the one granting her request at once and the other refusing it.
---

“Hera, august goddess, daughter of great Cronus” opens two replies in the deception of Zeus, with θύγατερ, “daughter,” in the vocative. Aphrodite goes on, “say what is in your mind; my heart bids me accomplish it” (14.194–195; [[aphrodite]]). Sleep goes on, “any other of the everlasting gods / I would easily lull to sleep,” but not Zeus, unless Zeus himself should bid him (14.243–248; [[sleep]], [[gods-who-live-forever]]). In Books 5 and 8 the verse named Hera in the narrative as she harnessed her horses (5.721, 8.383); here it is spoken to her, by two gods whom she is about to use. Both honor her as the daughter of Cronus, whose fall beneath the earth she recalls in her lie to Aphrodite (14.203–204; [[cronus]]). See [[hera]] and [[the-deception-of-zeus]].
