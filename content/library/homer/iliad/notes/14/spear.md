summary: The wounded kings come up from the ships leaning on their spears; Poseidon arms the army with the longest; Polydamas boasts that the man he has speared will use the spear as a staff on the road to Hades; and Peneleos lifts Ilioneus' head on the spear still fixed in the eye.

  - 14.493-500 | Peneleos and Ilioneus
---

**Leaning on spears.** Nestor arms in the verse of the night of Book 10, “And he took a strong spear, tipped with sharp bronze” (14.12 = 10.135; see [[nestor]]). The wounded kings come up from the ships “leaning on their spears, all together” (ἔγχει ἐρειδόμενοι, 14.38; see [[the-wounded-chiefs]]), as Diomedes and Odysseus will come to the assembly in Book 19, leaning on their spears because their wounds are still painful (ἔγχει ἐρειδομένω, 19.47–49). Diomedes says of his father Tydeus that he “surpassed all the Achaeans / with the spear” (14.124–125; see [[tydeus]]).

**The longest spears.** Poseidon's orders end with the spear: “and take in our hands the longest spears, / and go” (τὰ μακρότατʼ ἔγχεʼ, 14.373–374; see [[exchange-of-armor]]). When the armies meet, Hector's spear is the first thrown, and it is stopped by Ajax's straps (14.402–406; see [[shield]]); when the stone strikes him “the spear dropped from his hand” (14.419).

**A spear for a staff.** Polydamas spears Prothoenor through the right shoulder and boasts that the javelin has not leapt in vain from his hand: “one of the Argives has caught it in his body, and I think / he will lean on it as a staff as he goes down into the house of Hades” (αὐτῷ σκηπτόμενον, 14.456–457; see [[polydamas]], [[prothoenor]] and [[euchomai]]). σκήπτομαι, “lean on a staff”, belongs with σκῆπτρον, the staff of a king (see [[scepter]]); in the Odyssey it is said of Odysseus walking as a beggar (*Odyssey* 17.203, 17.338). The kings who leaned on their spears at the start of the book were wounded; this man leans on his in death.

**A head on a spear.** Peneleos' spear goes in under Ilioneus' brow and through the eye and the back of the head; he cuts off the head with his sword, “and the heavy spear was still / in the eye; and holding it up like a poppy head / he showed it to the Trojans” (14.493–500; see [[peneleos]], [[ilioneus]], [[eyes]] and [[the-poppy]]).
