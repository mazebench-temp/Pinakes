---
summary: Hera “of the golden throne” looks out from a peak of Olympus and sees Poseidon in the battle and Zeus on Ida, and she will promise Sleep a golden throne.
---

“And Hera of the golden throne looked out with her eyes, / standing on a peak of Olympus” (14.153–154; [[hera]], [[olympus]]). She sees Poseidon busy in the battle, “her own brother and her brother-in-law,” and rejoices; she sees Zeus sitting on the topmost peak of Ida, and he is hateful to her heart (14.155–158; [[poseidon]], [[hateful]], [[of-the-many-springs]]). The goddess of the golden throne then offers Sleep a throne for his help: “a fine throne, imperishable forever, / golden,” which Hephaestus will make (14.238–240; [[throne]], [[imperishable]]). Book 1 ended on the epithet, with Hera asleep beside Zeus (1.611); here it begins the scheme that ends with Zeus asleep beside her.
