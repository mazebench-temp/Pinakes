The Catalogue called him the son “whom immortal Zeus begot” (2.741), and here Zeus himself names his mother, though not by her own name: she is “the wife of Ixion” (see [[ixion]] and [[the-loves-of-zeus]]). Leaf notes that the two passages agree in making Peirithous a son of Zeus (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 14.318). His epithet, θεόφιν μήστωρ ἀτάλαντος, “a counselor equal to the gods”, is the one Priam bears in the Trojan assembly (7.366; see [[counselor-equal-to-the-gods]]). The other sons in the list are “preeminent”, “godlike” and “fierce-hearted” (14.320–324); Peirithous, whom Nestor named first among the mightiest men of his youth (1.262–263; see [[nestor-and-the-centaurs]]), is praised for counsel. In the Odyssey he and Theseus are θεῶν ἐρικυδέα τέκνα, “glorious children of the gods” (*Odyssey* 11.631).
