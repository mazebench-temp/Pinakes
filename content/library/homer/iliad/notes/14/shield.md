**A son's shield.** Nestor, leaving Machaon to go and look at the battle, takes “the wrought shield of his son / that lay in the hut of Thrasymedes, tamer of horses, / shining with bronze; and the son had his father's shield” (14.9–11; see [[nestor]] and [[thrasymedes]]). Nestor's own shield was the one Hector wished to take, all of gold (8.191–193; see the notes on Books 8 and 10).

**The biggest shields.** Poseidon's plan for the battle begins with shields: “the shields that are best in the army and biggest, / let us put them on”, and whoever is steadfast in battle but has a small shield on his shoulder is to give it to a worse man and put on a bigger one (14.371–377; see [[exchange-of-armor]] and [[poseidon]]).

**Two straps.** Hector throws first at Ajax, and does not miss, “where the two straps were stretched across his chest, / the one of his shield, the other of his silver-studded sword; / and these two protected his tender flesh” (τῇ ῥα δύω τελαμῶνε περὶ στήθεσσι τετάσθην, 14.404–406; see [[ajax]], [[hector]] and [[sword]]). The great shield of Ajax hangs from a strap over the shoulder, as the sword does, and the spear is held where the two straps lie across the chest. The τελαμών was the shield's strap in Book 5, where Diomedes wipes away the blood beneath it (5.796–798), and the same word is the name of Ajax's father, Telamon (see [[telamon]]).

**Over the rim.** Ajax answers with a stone, one of the props of the ships, and strikes Hector “in the chest over the shield rim, near the neck” (ὑπὲρ ἄντυγος ἀγχόθι δειρῆς, 14.412; see [[props]] and [[stone-throwing]]). Hector falls, “and his shield fell upon him, / and his helmet” (14.419–420; see the translation's note on 14.419). The other Trojan leaders hold their “well-rounded shields” before him, and his companions carry him away (14.424–429; see [[well-rounded]]).
