---
summary: Ida is “of the many springs” three times in the book, where Hera sees Zeus sitting, where she and Sleep arrive, and where, she says, her horses stand; the book adds the genitive πολυπίδακος.
grc: [πολυπίδακος]
---

The genitive πολυπίδακος Ἴδης ends the verse twice. Hera sees Zeus “on the topmost peak of Ida of the many springs” (14.157; [[zeus]], [[hateful]]), and she tells him that her horses “are at the foot of Ida of the many springs, / standing ready to carry me over dry land and sea” (14.307–308; [[horses]], [[hera]]). The second is a lie: she came without horses or chariot, as Zeus has just observed (14.298–299). Between the two, the accusative stands in the verse of a god's arrival, “And they came to Ida of the many springs, mother of wild beasts” (14.283; [[mother-of-wild-beasts]], [[sleep]]). The genitive πολυπίδακος is new to the entry. See [[ida]].
