Dionysus is the only god among the sons in Zeus' list of his loves, and the last of them. His mother is named twice, at the head of the shared verse and again in his own, so that the two Theban sons, Heracles and Dionysus, come in the reverse order of their mothers (see [[semele]], [[heracles]] and [[the-loves-of-zeus]]). χάρμα βροτοῖσιν, “a joy to mortals”, occurs only here in Homer; the other sons in the list are praised as men are praised, “preeminent”, “godlike”, “fierce-hearted” (14.320–324). Hesiod's word for him is πολυγηθής, “of many joys” (*Theogony* 941).

“In Thebes” (14.323) stands with Alcmene, but it was the Theban birth of Dionysus that one of the *Homeric Hymns* denied: some say Dracanum, or Icarus, or Naxos, or the Alpheus, and others Thebes, “but they lie”, for his father bore him far from men, hidden from Hera (*Hymn* 1.1–7; see [[thebes]]). Walter Leaf, discussing the ancient rejection of the list, called Dionysus “definitely late” in Homer, though he did not think that a reason to reject the verses (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 14.317).
