“and she fastened it at her breast with golden pins” (χρυσείῃς δʼ ἐνετῇσι κατὰ στῆθος περονᾶτο, 14.180; see [[robe]] and [[the-adornment-of-a-goddess]]). ἐνετή, “pin”, usually derived from ἐνίημι, “put in, insert”, occurs only here in Homer; the verb is that of the περόνη, the brooch, used of Nestor pinning on his crimson cloak (10.133) and of a spear pinning a man through (7.145, 13.397; see the article above). Hera's pins are gold, like all the brooches of the poems. In the Odyssey golden brooches and earrings like Hera's are among the suitors' gifts to Penelope: Antinous gives her a robe with twelve golden brooches, and Eurydamas earrings described in the verse used of Hera's (*Odyssey* 18.292–298; see [[earrings]]).
