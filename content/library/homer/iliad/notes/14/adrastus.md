# Ἀδρήστοιο (14.121), the genitive, is found only here.

Ἀδρήστοιο δʼ ἔγημε θυγατρῶν, “He married one of the daughters of Adrastus” (14.121). In Diomedes' account of his family, Tydeus came to Argos after his wanderings, married into the house of Adrastus, and grew rich there (14.119–125; see [[tydeus]], [[diomedes]] and [[argos]]). The poem does not say that Adrastus ruled Argos; the Catalogue remembers him as the king of Sicyon “at first” (2.572; see [[sicyon]]). Diomedes is tied to him twice over, for his own wife Aegialeia is “the wise daughter of Adrastus” (5.412; see [[aegialeia]] and the note on Book 5). Polynices, whom later legend made Adrastus' other son-in-law, came with Tydeus to Mycenae “gathering an army” against Thebes (4.376–378; see [[polynices]] and [[the-seven-against-thebes]]), but Homer does not say whom he married.
