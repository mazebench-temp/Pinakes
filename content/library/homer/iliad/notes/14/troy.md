summary: In Book 14 Troy is seen only from the field and from the past. Hector has vowed not to go back to Ilios before he burns the ships, Odysseus refuses to leave the city of the Trojans behind, Poseidon foretells the Trojans' flight back to it, and the stunned Hector is carried toward it; Sleep recalls an earlier sack, when Heracles sailed from Ilios.
# Ἰλιόθεν, "from Ilios" (14.251; also Od. 9.39).
grc: [Ἰλιόθεν]
refs: [14.88, 14.146, 14.432]
---

**From the field.** Agamemnon fears Hector's vow not to go back from the ships “to Ilios” before he has burned them (14.46; see [[hector]]). Odysseus refuses to flee: “Are you so eager, then, to leave the wide-streeted city of the Trojans / behind, for whose sake we have suffered so many evils?” (14.88–89; see [[odysseus]] and [[wide-streeted]]). Poseidon tells Agamemnon that he will see the Trojans “fleeing back to the city, away from the ships and huts” (14.146; see [[poseidon]]), and the first to go back is Hector, stunned by Ajax's stone, whose companions carry him “toward the city, groaning heavily” (14.432). Peneleos looks forward to the day “when / we young men of the Achaeans come back from Troy with our ships” (14.504–505; see [[peneleos]]).

**An earlier sack.** Sleep recalls the day when Heracles “was sailing from Ilios, when he had sacked the city of the Trojans”, and Hera drove him off to Cos (14.250–255; see [[heracles-sacks-troy]] and [[heracles]]).
