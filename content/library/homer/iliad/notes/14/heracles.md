**Sleep's story.** Sleep will not lull Zeus a second time, because he did it once before, on the day of Heracles' return from Troy:

{{quote:784f5a0e-f17d-5278-b45b-4dc956e7d56e}}

Sleep does not name him. He is ὑπέρθυμος Διὸς υἱός, “that high-hearted son of Zeus” (14.250; see [[high-hearted]]). Walter Leaf heard dislike in κεῖνος, “that”, as in Diomedes' κεῖνος Ἄρης at 5.604, and noted that the name comes only with Hera's answer (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 14.250). The day is dated by his sack of Troy, “when he had sacked the city of the Trojans” (ἐξαλαπάξας, 14.251), the verb of Tlepolemus' boast that his father “sacked the city of Ilios” (ἐξαλάπαξε, 5.642; see [[heracles-sacks-troy]]). Hera's winds carried him off to Cos, “far from all his friends”, and Zeus' rage when he woke fell on Sleep (14.254–261; see [[cos]], [[sleep]] and [[hera-drives-heracles-to-cos]]).

**Hera's answer.** Hera names him, and makes him the measure of Zeus' anger: “Do you think that far-seeing Zeus will help the Trojans as much / as he was enraged for Heracles, his own son?” (14.265–266). For this son Zeus flung the gods about his house (14.257); he will not rage so for Troy. In Book 15 Zeus himself recalls the same storm and the same Cos, and the pain for “divine Heracles” that would not leave him (15.24–30).

**Alcmene's son.** Zeus' list of his loves names his mother: “nor Alcmene in Thebes, / who bore Heracles, a fierce-hearted son” (14.323–324; see [[alcmene]], [[the-loves-of-zeus]] and [[fierce-hearted]]). The verse has twins in the Odyssey's catalogue of heroines, where Alcmene is the woman “who bore Heracles”, ἥ ῥʼ Ἡρακλῆα … γείνατʼ (*Odyssey* 11.267–268), and Leda the woman who bore to Tyndareus two sons, κρατερόφρονε, “fierce-hearted”, Castor and Polydeuces (*Odyssey* 11.299–300). Heracles' birth in Thebes is told at length in Book 19, where Hera holds back Alcmene's labor so that Eurystheus is born first (19.95–133; see [[eurystheus]]).
