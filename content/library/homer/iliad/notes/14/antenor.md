summary: Antenor's son Archelochus is killed by the spear Ajax throws at Polydamas, and Ajax, who knows the family likeness, calls the dead man “a brother of Antenor, tamer of horses, / or a son”; another son, Acamas, avenges him (14.463–485).
refs: [14.474]
---

Antenor himself does not appear. His son Archelochus dies in place of Polydamas (14.463–464; see [[archelochus]] and [[polydamas]]), and Ajax judges the dead man by his look: “He does not look to me like a coward, nor born of cowards, / but a brother of Antenor, tamer of horses, / or a son; for in his lineage he looked most like him” (14.472–474; see [[ajax]] and [[genee]]). The narrator adds that Ajax spoke “knowing well” (14.475). ἱππόδαμος, “tamer of horses”, the epithet of the Trojans, was given to Antenor once before, when Theano was named as his wife (6.299; see [[tamer-of-horses]] and [[theano]]). Another son, Acamas, kills the Boeotian Promachus over his brother's body (14.476–485; see [[acamas]]). In Book 11 Agamemnon killed two of his sons, Iphidamas and Coon (11.221–263; see [[iphidamas]] and [[coon]]).
