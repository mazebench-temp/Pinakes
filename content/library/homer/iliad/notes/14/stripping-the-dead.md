summary: The poet asks the Muses who was the first of the Achaeans “to win the bloody spoils of men” when Poseidon turned the battle, and answers with a list in which Antilochus “slew and stripped” two men. Earlier the Achaeans run up hoping to drag away the fallen Hector, and Promachus dies dragging a dead Trojan by the feet.
grc: [ἀνδράγριʼ]
passages:
  - 14.508-519 | The first to win the spoils
---

**The spoils of men.** The invocation of the Muses near the end of the book asks for a name: “who was the first of the Achaeans to win the bloody spoils of men, / when the famous shaker of the earth had turned the battle” (βροτόεντʼ ἀνδράγριʼ, 14.509–510; see [[invocation-of-the-muses]] and [[bloody-spoils]]). ἀνδράγρια, “spoils taken from men”, built like ζωάγρια, “reward for a life saved” (18.407), occurs only here in Homer. The turn of the battle is measured by spoils. The answer is a list, Telamonian Ajax first, then Antilochus, who “slew and stripped Phalces and Mermerus” (ἐξενάριξε, 14.513), Meriones, Teucer and Menelaus (14.511–519; see [[androktasia]]).

**Bodies dragged.** Before the rout the fight is over bodies. When Hector falls, the sons of the Achaeans run up “hoping to drag him away”, but the Trojan leaders stand over him and hold their shields before him (14.421–428; see [[fighting-over-the-body]] and [[hector]]). Promachus the Boeotian drags at the body of Archelochus by the feet, and Acamas, standing over his brother, kills him (14.476–477; see [[promachus]] and [[acamas]]). Peneleos does not strip Ilioneus but cuts off his head and holds it up on his spear (14.496–500; see [[peneleos]]).
