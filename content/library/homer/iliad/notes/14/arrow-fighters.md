---
summary: Acamas taunts the Argives over the body of Promachus, “Argives, you arrow-fighters, insatiable of threats,” the word's second and last use.
---

Acamas has killed Promachus, who was dragging away the body of Acamas' brother Archelochus, and he boasts over him: “Argives, you arrow-fighters, insatiable of threats, / surely the toil and the misery will not be ours / alone; but some day you too will be killed like this” (14.479–481; [[acamas]], [[promachus]], [[archelochus]], [[insatiable]]). In Book 4 Agamemnon used the same address to shame his own men (4.242); here an enemy uses it. The phrase that follows, ἀπειλάων ἀκόρητοι, “insatiable of threats,” comes close to the second sense proposed for the word, “braggarts” ([[obscure-iomoroi]]). Acamas boasts of paying back his brother's death (14.482–485; [[poine]]), and Peneleos answers at once by killing Ilioneus (14.486–505; [[peneleos]], [[ilioneus]]).
