---
summary: Ilioneus was the son of Phorbas, “rich in flocks”, whom Hermes loved most of the Trojans “and granted him wealth”, and his mother bore him alone.
---

“Ilioneus, / son of Phorbas, rich in flocks, whom most / of the Trojans Hermes loved, and granted him wealth; / and to him the mother bore Ilioneus, an only child” (Ἑρμείας Τρώων ἐφίλει καὶ κτῆσιν ὄπασσε, 14.489–492; see [[ilioneus]], [[phorbas-father-of-ilioneus]], [[hermes]] and [[rich-in-flocks]]). κτῆσις is what a man has acquired, his wealth; the half-verse returns in the Odyssey, when Eumaeus speaks of the master who would have loved him and given him property, as a kind lord gives his servant (*Odyssey* 14.62–63).

The poem gave the same kind of detail for the two sons of Phaenops in Book 5, whom Diomedes killed: their father had no other son to leave his possessions to, and “distant kinsmen divided his wealth among them” (5.152–158). Ilioneus is an only son, and Peneleos sends word to his father and mother to wail for him in their halls (14.501–502; see [[peneleos]] and [[the-poppy]]).
