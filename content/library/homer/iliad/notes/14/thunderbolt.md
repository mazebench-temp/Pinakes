summary: While Zeus sleeps on Gargarus his weapons appear only in comparisons: Poseidon's sword is “like lightning”, and Hector falls under Ajax's stone like an oak struck by the thunderbolt of father Zeus, with its dread smell of sulfur.
passages:
  - 14.414-418 | The oak struck by the thunderbolt
---

The thunderbolt is named in Book 14 only in a simile, and the god who throws it is asleep. Ajax's stone strikes Hector and sends him spinning, and he falls:

{{quote:380b2062-321f-52b2-9dc4-d71367e6656a}}

The smell of sulfur (θέειον) and the courage that fails the onlooker are the marks of the bolt (see the article above and [[the-oak-struck-by-the-thunderbolt]]). The simile gives Zeus' stroke to Ajax's stone at the moment when Zeus himself can strike no one (see [[hypnos]] and [[the-deception-of-zeus]]). In the same way Poseidon, leading the Achaeans openly, carries a sword “like lightning” (εἴκελον ἀστεροπῇ, 14.386; see [[like-lightning]] and [[sword]]). When Zeus has woken, his own thunder is heard again over the battle, and the Trojans take heart from it (15.377–380; see [[charme]]).
