---
summary: Ajax judges the man he has killed by his looks: “He does not look to me like a coward, nor born of cowards”, but kin of Antenor, “for in his lineage he looked most like him”.
en: [look to me like]
---

Ajax's spear, thrown at Polydamas, has killed Archelochus, son of Antenor (14.461–464). Ajax calls out: “is not this man, killed in return for Prothoenor, / worthy of him? He does not look to me like a coward, nor born of cowards, / but a brother of Antenor, tamer of horses, / or a son; for in his lineage he looked most like him” (οὐ μέν μοι κακὸς εἴδεται οὐδὲ κακῶν ἔξ, 14.471–474; see [[archelochus]], [[antenor]] and [[genee]]). The narrator confirms the guess, “He spoke, knowing well” (14.475; see [[so-he-spoke]]). A man's looks show his birth, and a son is measured by his likeness to his father: Athena taunts Diomedes that “Tydeus has begotten a son little like himself” (5.800), and in this book Diomedes proves his birth by telling it (14.113–125; see [[pater]]).
