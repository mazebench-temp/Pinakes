---
summary: Zeus' list of his loves ends with “glorious Leto,” the last before Hera herself.
---

“Nor when I loved Demeter of the lovely tresses, the queen, / nor when I loved glorious Leto, nor you yourself, / as I love you now, and sweet longing takes hold of me” (14.326–328; [[leto]], [[demeter]], [[of-the-lovely-tresses]], [[the-loves-of-zeus]]). The epithet of the gods' gifts and of a glorious feast (3.65, 24.802) is given here, for the only time in the Iliad, to a person: the mother of Apollo and Artemis, the last name in the list before Zeus turns to his wife. The Odyssey gives it to Earth as the mother of Tityus, whom Odysseus sees punished among the dead for laying hands on Leto (*Odyssey* 11.576–581).
