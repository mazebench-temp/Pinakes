---
summary: The book turns on Zeus' “shrewd mind”, on which Hera means to pour sleep and which desire wraps when he sees her. Odysseus scorns Agamemnon's mind for proposing flight, and Poseidon says that there is no sense in Achilles, “not even a little”.
passages:
  - 14.159-165 | Hera's plan
  - 14.292-296 | Desire wraps his mind
---

**A shrewd mind overcome.** Hera's plan is to pour sleep “upon his eyelids and his shrewd mind” (φρεσὶ πευκαλίμῃσι, 14.165; see [[hypnos]] and [[shrewd-mind]]). πευκάλιμος, “shrewd, sharp”, is said elsewhere of Athena's mind, in her own words, and of Hermes, who excels in it (8.366, 20.35). When Zeus sees her on Gargarus, “so desire wrapped his shrewd mind” (πυκινὰς φρένας, 14.294; see [[eros]]); in Paris' version of the verse the mind had no epithet (3.442). Between the two, the strap holds the persuasion that steals the mind “even of men who think shrewdly” (πύκα περ φρονεόντων, 14.217; see [[aphrodites-band]] and [[noos]]). The φρένες of the shrewdest of the gods are where the deception works.

**Minds scorned.** The council of the wounded kings is a judgment of minds. Odysseus tells Agamemnon that no man would let such a word pass his lips “who knew in his mind how to speak fitting words” (ᾗσι φρεσὶν ἄρτια βάζειν, 14.92), and “But now I utterly scorn your mind, from what you have said” (νῦν δέ σευ ὠνοσάμην πάγχυ φρένας, 14.95; see [[odysseus]] and [[what-a-word]]). Hector rebukes Glaucus with the same verse in Book 17 (17.173). Poseidon, in the likeness of an old man, comforts Agamemnon by blaming Achilles, who rejoices at the slaughter of the Achaeans “since there is no sense in him, not even a little” (ἐπεὶ οὔ οἱ ἔνι φρένες οὐδʼ ἠβαιαί, 14.141; see [[achilles]] and [[poseidon]]). In the Odyssey Antinous says it to the beggar who asks to try the bow (*Odyssey* 21.288).

**Other uses.** The rest are the usual ones. Nestor makes his choice “as he pondered” (φρονέοντι, 14.23; see [[decision-scene]]); Aphrodite asks Hera to say “what is in your mind” (14.195), and promises that she will not come back with nothing done, “whatever you have in your mind” (14.221); Hera asks Sleep why he has such things in his mind (14.264).
