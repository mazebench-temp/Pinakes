---
summary: Poseidon leads the Achaeans with “a dread long-edged sword” in his strong hand, “like lightning”; the book adds τανύηκες, the other form of the compound.
grc: [τανύηκες]
---

“They went; and Poseidon the earth-shaker led them, / holding a dread long-edged sword in his strong hand, / like lightning; it is not lawful to close with it / in wretched battle, but fear holds men back” (14.384–387; [[poseidon]], [[sword]], [[like-lightning]], [[strong-hand]], [[themis]]). The adjective here is τανυήκης, from τανύω, “stretch,” beside the ταναήκης of the formula “with the long-edged bronze” (7.77); the translation renders both “long-edged.” ἄορ τανύηκες returns in the verse of a sword drawn from beside the thigh, when Automedon cuts loose the fallen trace horse (16.473) and twice in the Odyssey (*Odyssey* 10.439, 11.231). The form τανύηκες is new to the entry. See [[poseidon-rallies-the-achaeans]].
