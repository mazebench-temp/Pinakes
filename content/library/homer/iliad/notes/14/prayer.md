grc: [εὐκτὰ]
# Diomedes "claims" (εὔχομαι, 14.113); Hector "boasts" (εὔχεται, 14.366); "at his boasting" (εὐξαμένοιο, 14.458, 14.486); "in triumph" (εὐχόμενος, 14.500).
except: [14.113, 14.366, 14.458, 14.486, 14.500]
---

The gods are busy with one another in Book 14, and no man asks them for anything. Prayer is named twice. Odysseus says that if the ships are dragged to the sea in the middle of the battle, “the Trojans may have what they pray for, winning as they are already” (εὐκτὰ γένηται, 14.98; see [[odysseus]]). Acamas, boasting over Promachus, gives the reason he has avenged his brother: “that is why a man prays / to leave a kinsman in his halls, to defend him against disaster” (14.484–485; see [[a-kinsman-in-his-halls]]). Both are prayers for a man's own ends: victory over the enemy, and a kinsman to pay back his death.

The other words of the family are a claim of birth and the boasts of the battle: Diomedes claims his descent from Tydeus (14.113), Poseidon says that Hector boasts of taking the ships (14.366), the Argives grieve at the boasting of Polydamas and of Acamas (14.458, 14.486), and Peneleos speaks in triumph over Ilioneus (14.500). See [[euchomai]].
