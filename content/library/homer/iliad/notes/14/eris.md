summary: Poseidon's shout is as loud as nine or ten thousand men “as they join in the strife of Ares”, and when he leads the Achaeans openly he and Hector “stretched tight the most dread strife of war”.
passages:
  - 14.388-391 | The strife stretched tight
---

**The strife of Ares.** Poseidon, leaving Agamemnon, gives a great shout, “As loud as nine thousand or ten thousand men shout / in war as they join in the strife of Ares” (ἔριδα ξυνάγοντες Ἄρηος, 14.148–149; see [[ten-thousand-men]] and [[poseidon]]). The two verses are the ones that measured Ares' own scream when Diomedes wounded him (5.859–861): the cry of a war god and of an army are the same size.

**Stretched tight.** When Poseidon has armed the Achaeans and Hector has marshaled the Trojans, “Then they stretched tight the most dread strife of war, / dark-haired Poseidon and shining Hector, / the one helping the Trojans, the other the Argives” (αἰνοτάτην ἔριδα πτολέμοιο τάνυσσαν, 14.389–391; see [[hector]]). In Book 13 Zeus and Poseidon stretched the rope of mighty strife over both armies (13.358–360; see [[the-rope-of-war]] and the note on Book 13). Now Zeus is asleep, and the two who stretch it are a god and a man. The verb returns with Zeus as its subject when the son of Cronus stretches “mighty strife” over Sarpedon's body (16.662).
