---
summary: The breast holds the heart: grief and fear in the breasts of the Achaeans, joy in Achilles', desire in Zeus'. Hera pins her robe at her breast and Aphrodite takes the strap from hers; the straps across Ajax's chest stop Hector's spear, and Ajax's stone strikes Hector in the chest over the rim of his shield.
---

**The heart in the breast.** The wounded kings come, “and grieved / was the heart in their breasts”, and the old man “made the heart cower in the breasts of the Achaeans” (θυμὸς ἐνὶ στήθεσσιν, 14.38–40; see [[thymos]]). Poseidon says that “the deadly heart of Achilles / rejoices in his breast” at their rout (14.139–140; see [[ker-heart]]), and his own voice comes “out of his breast” as loud as ten thousand men (ἐκ στήθεσφιν, 14.150–151; see [[ten-thousand-men]]). Zeus says that desire has never “so flooded round the heart in my breast” (14.316; see [[eros]]).

**The goddesses.** Hera “fastened it at her breast with golden pins” (κατὰ στῆθος περονᾶτο, 14.180; see [[brooch]]), and Aphrodite “loosed from her breast the embroidered strap” (ἀπὸ στήθεσφιν ἐλύσατο, 14.214; see [[aphrodites-band]]).

**The chest in battle.** Hector's spear hits Ajax “where the two straps were stretched across his chest, / the one of his shield, the other of his silver-studded sword; / and these two protected his tender flesh” (14.404–406; see [[shield]] and [[sword]]). Ajax's stone hits Hector “in the chest over the shield rim, near the neck” (στῆθος βεβλήκει ὑπὲρ ἄντυγος, 14.412; see [[stone-throwing]]).
