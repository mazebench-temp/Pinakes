---
summary: Hera answers Zeus' proposal with her verse of protest, “Most dread son of Cronus, what a thing you have said!”, here in pretended modesty.
---

“And queen Hera, with treachery in her heart, addressed him: / ‘Most dread son of Cronus, what a thing you have said!’” (14.329–330; [[hera]], [[with-treachery-in-her-heart]], [[son-of-cronus]]). Elsewhere the verse opens a real protest: against Zeus' refusal to share his counsels, his offer to let Troy stand, his wish to save Sarpedon (1.552, 4.25, 16.440; [[what-a-word]]). Here Zeus has proposed what Hera came for, and she objects only that they would be seen on the open peaks of Ida, and that it would be a thing for blame (14.331–336; [[nemesis]]). The formula of indignation serves the deception. See [[the-deception-of-zeus]].
