---
summary: Poseidon's shout puts great strength into each of the Achaeans, “in his heart, to go to war without ceasing and to fight”, as Eris did at the start of the day and Athena before the Catalogue.
---

Poseidon's shout goes out, “and he put great strength into each of the Achaeans, / in his heart, to go to war without ceasing and to fight” (Ἀχαιοῖσιν δὲ μέγα σθένος ἔμβαλʼ ἑκάστῳ / καρδίῃ, ἄληκτον πολεμίζειν ἠδὲ μάχεσθαι, 14.151–152; see [[poseidon]] and [[ten-thousand-men]]). The two verses are those of Eris, who stood on Odysseus' ship at dawn and shouted, at the start of the day on which Book 14 still stands (11.10–12; see [[eris]]). The second of them is also Athena's, when she went through the army with the aegis before the Catalogue and “roused strength in each man's / heart” (2.446–452; see [[athena]]). Poseidon, in the likeness of an old man, does it with a shout like that of ten thousand men (14.147–151).
