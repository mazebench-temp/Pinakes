**His death.** The Catalogue named him among the five leaders of the Boeotians (2.495; see [[boeotians]]). Book 14 gives him a father and a death:

{{quote:24f11b22-fdce-52ec-8437-0694813dfc47}}

The last two verses told the death of Ascalaphus in Book 13, with the father's name changed: Ascalaphus was “the son of Enyalius”, that is, of Ares, and Prothoenor is the son of Areilycus, whose name seems to contain Ares' own (13.519–520; see [[ascalaphus]] and [[areilycus]]). Polydamas has come to defend the body of Satnius, whom the son of Oileus has just killed (14.442–448; see [[ajax-son-of-oileus]]).

**The boast.** Polydamas “boasted terribly, shouting aloud” (14.453), with a grim joke: “one of the Argives has caught it in his body, and I think / he will lean on it as a staff as he goes down into the house of Hades” (14.456–457; see [[polydamas]] and [[hades]]). σκηπτόμενον, “lean on it as a staff”, makes the spear in the dead man's body the staff of an old man walking.

**In return for him.** The man “fell very close” to Telamonian Ajax (14.460), who throws at Polydamas and kills Archelochus instead, and asks Polydamas whether this man is not worthy, “killed in return for Prothoenor” (Προθοήνορος ἀντὶ πεφάσθαι, 14.471; see [[ajax]] and [[archelochus]]). Prothoenor's death begins a chain of killings and boasts: Archelochus for Prothoenor, Promachus for Archelochus, Ilioneus for Promachus (14.449–505; see [[acamas]], [[promachus]] and [[peneleos]]). He is the first of the five Boeotian leaders to die; Hector kills Arcesilaus and Agenor kills Clonius in the next book (15.329–330, 15.340; see [[arcesilaus]] and [[clonius]]).
