---
summary: Calydon is “steep” in Diomedes' genealogy, and αἰπὺς ὄλεθρος, “sheer destruction,” frames the book's turn: Odysseus fears it for the Achaeans, and in the rout it is the Trojans who look for a way to escape it.
---

**A place.** “For to Portheus were born three noble sons, / and they lived in Pleuron and steep Calydon” (14.115–116; [[calydon]], [[pleuron]], [[portheus]], [[blameless]]), the epithet that Calydon had in the verse about Thoas in Book 13 (13.217).

**A figure.** Odysseus warns that if the ships were dragged to the sea in the middle of the battle, the Trojans would have what they pray for, “and sheer destruction may weigh down on us” (14.98–99; [[odysseus]]). At the end of the book, after Peneleos' boast over Ilioneus' head, the same destruction faces the other side: “each man looked around for a way to escape sheer destruction” (14.507; [[peneleos]], [[trojans]]). The phrase that the Achaeans feared at the beginning is the Trojans' at the end.
