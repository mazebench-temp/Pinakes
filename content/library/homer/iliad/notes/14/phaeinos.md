---
summary: In the deception the word belongs to the doors of Hera's chamber, to her tresses and to Zeus' eyes, and in the battle to Ajax's spear; the book adds φαεινάς and φαεινούς.
grc: [φαεινάς, φαεινούς]
---

**The deception.** Hera goes into the chamber that Hephaestus built for her “and closed the shining doors” (14.169; [[closely-fitted]]), and she plaits “the shining tresses” of her immortal head (14.176; [[ambrosial]], [[dressing-scene]]). Then she asks Sleep: “Lull to sleep for me the shining eyes of Zeus beneath his brows” (14.236; [[sleep]], [[zeus]]). The dual ὄσσε φαεινώ is the phrase of the opening of Book 13, where Zeus turned his shining eyes away from Troy (13.3, 13.7); the eyes that watched the war from Ida are the eyes that Hera wants closed.

**The battle.** Ajax answers Polydamas' boast: “And quickly he threw his shining spear at him as he went away” (14.461; [[ajax]], [[polydamas]], [[spear]]), the formula δουρὶ φαεινῷ.

The forms φαεινάς and φαεινούς are new to the entry.
