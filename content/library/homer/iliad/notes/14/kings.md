διοτρεφέες βασιλῆες, “the kings whom Zeus has nurtured” (14.27; see [[zeus-nurtured]]), are the three leaders wounded earlier that day, the son of Tydeus, Odysseus and the son of Atreus, who come up from the ships to look at the battle (14.27–29; see [[the-wounded-chiefs]], [[diomedes]], [[odysseus]] and [[agamemnon]]). In their council Odysseus says what a king is: no man would let Agamemnon's proposal of flight pass his lips “who knew in his mind how to speak fitting words, / and bore a scepter, and whom the people would obey” (14.92–93; see [[scepter-bearing]]).

When they appear again, they do a king's work in battle. “And the kings themselves, stabbed though they were, marshaled them” (14.379): ἐκόσμεον, the verb of the title of the two sons of Atreus, κοσμήτορε λαῶν, “marshals of the people” (1.16; see [[agamemnon]]), and of Hector, who “marshaled the Trojans” on the other side (14.388). They go among all the men and see Poseidon's order carried out: “the brave man put on the good, and gave the worse to the worse man” (14.381–382; see [[exchange-of-armor]] and [[poseidon]]).
