---
summary: Agamemnon recalls Hector's vow not to go back to Ilios before he had burned the ships; Aphrodite tells Hera that she will not come back with nothing done; Hera pretends that she could not go back to Zeus' house from a bed on Ida; and Peneleos says that the wife of Promachus will not be glad at her husband's homecoming.
grc: [νεοίμην]
---

**Not to return.** Hector vowed “that he would not go back from the ships to Ilios / before he had set fire to the ships” (ἀπονέεσθαι, 14.46–47; see [[assembly]]). The return the Achaeans might seek is Agamemnon's plan of flight, to drag the ships down and escape by night (14.74–81; see [[no-blame-in-fleeing]]), and Odysseus refuses it (14.83–102).

**Among the gods.** Aphrodite, giving Hera the strap: “I do not think / you will come back with nothing done, whatever you have in your mind” (ἄπρηκτόν γε νέεσθαι, 14.220–221; see [[aphrodites-band]]). Hera, pretending to be shy of Zeus' bed on the open peak: “I would not go back to your house / when I rose from that bed” (οὐκ ἂν ἔγωγε τεὸν πρὸς δῶμα νεοίμην, 14.335–336; see [[bed]]).

**A homecoming denied.** Peneleos, boasting over Ilioneus, answers Acamas' boast over Promachus: “for neither will the wife of Promachus, son of Alegenor, / be glad at her dear husband's coming home, when / we young men of the Achaeans come back from Troy with our ships” (νεώμεθα, 14.503–505; see [[promachus]] and [[peneleos]]). He speaks of the Achaeans' return as a thing that will come.
