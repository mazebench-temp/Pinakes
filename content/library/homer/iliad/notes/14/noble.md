---
summary: Peneleos, holding up Ilioneus' head, tells the Trojans to bid “noble Ilioneus'” father and mother mourn.
---

“Trojans, tell noble Ilioneus' dear father for me / and his mother, to wail for him in their halls” (14.501–502; [[peneleos]], [[ilioneus]], [[phorbas-father-of-ilioneus]]). The epithet of praise comes in a boast over a severed head. Ilioneus was the only child of Phorbas, a man rich in flocks whom Hermes loved (14.490–492; [[rich-in-flocks]]), and Peneleos sets their grief against the grief that Acamas has caused: the wife of Promachus will not be glad at her husband's homecoming either (14.503–505; [[acamas]], [[promachus]]).
