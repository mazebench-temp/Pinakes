summary: Odysseus wishes that Agamemnon were giving orders to some other, unworthy army and were not lord over the Achaeans, in the two verbs of the quarrel of Book 1.
refs: [14.84-85]
en: [giving orders]
---

“Ruinous man, I wish you were giving orders to some other, unworthy army, / and were not lord over us” (σημαίνειν, μὴ δʼ ἄμμιν ἀνασσέμεν, 14.84–85; see [[odysseus]], [[agamemnon]] and [[anax]]). The two verbs stood together in the quarrel of Book 1, when Agamemnon complained that Achilles wanted “to have power over all, to be lord over all, / and to give orders to all” (πάντεσσι δʼ ἀνάσσειν, / πᾶσι δὲ σημαίνειν, 1.288–289), and Achilles answered, “Give these orders to others, but do not give them / to me” (1.295–296; see [[achilles]]). Odysseus' wish has the shape of Achilles' refusal; he says it to keep the army in the war (see [[what-a-word]]).
