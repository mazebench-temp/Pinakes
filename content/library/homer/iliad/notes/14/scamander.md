---
summary: The Trojans carry the stunned Hector to the ford of “the eddying Xanthus, whom immortal Zeus begot”, and revive him with its water (14.433–436).
refs: [14.433, 14.435]
---

“But when they came to the ford of the fair-flowing river, / the eddying Xanthus, whom immortal Zeus begot” (14.433–434; see [[fair-flowing]] and [[eddying]]): the river is named by its gods' name, Xanthus (see [[language-of-gods-and-men]]). There they lift Hector down from the chariot and pour its water over him; he gets his breath back and looks up, vomits dark blood, and sinks back into darkness (14.435–439; see [[hector]]). The two verses of the ford return when the Trojans, fleeing from Achilles, reach the river and he splits them (21.1–3), and when Priam brings Hector's body home across it and Hermes leaves him (24.692–694; see [[priam]] and [[hermes]]). ὃν ἀθάνατος τέκετο Ζεύς, “whom immortal Zeus begot”, makes the river a son of Zeus.
