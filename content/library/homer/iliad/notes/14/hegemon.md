summary: Poseidon foretells that “the leaders and rulers of the Trojans” will flee; Sarpedon, “leader of the Lycians”, stands over the fallen Hector; and Hyrtius, “leader of the stout-hearted Mysians”, is the first man killed in the rout. On the Achaean side the god leads.
grc: [ἡγήτορα]
---

Poseidon tells Agamemnon, “but surely the leaders and rulers of the Trojans will yet / fill the wide plain with dust, and you yourself will see them / fleeing back to the city” (Τρώων ἡγήτορες ἠδὲ μέδοντες, 14.144–146; see [[poseidon]]). The verse is most often an address, “Friends, leaders and rulers of the Argives” (2.79 and later); it named the Trojans once before, the leaders whom Hector called to his council by night (10.300–301; see [[trojans]]). At the end of the book the Trojans flee (14.506–522). Sarpedon, “leader of the Lycians” (ἀρχὸς Λυκίων, 14.426; see [[sarpedon]]), stands with the best of them over Hector, and the first man killed in the rout is Hyrtius, “son of Gyrtius, leader of the stout-hearted Mysians” (Μυσῶν ἡγήτορα καρτεροθύμων, 14.512; see [[hyrtius]] and [[mysians]]).

The Achaeans are led by their wounded king and by the god: “Agamemnon, lord of men, led them” to the battle (14.134), and Poseidon says “I will lead” (ἡγήσομαι, 14.374) and goes before the army with his sword (14.384–386; see [[like-lightning]]).
