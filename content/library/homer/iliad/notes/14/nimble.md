---
summary: In the simile of Nestor's indecision, the sea senses “the swift paths of the shrill winds.”
en: [swift paths]
---

“As when the great open sea heaves with a soundless swell, / sensing the swift paths of the shrill winds, / aimlessly, and does not roll forward to either side / until some settled wind comes down from Zeus” (14.16–19; [[the-heaving-sea]], [[shrill]], [[nestor]], [[pondering-two-ways]]). The translation renders λαιψηρὰ κέλευθα “swift paths”; the adjective of a runner's knees is given to the winds, whose coming the sea feels before any wind has settled. The phrase returns of the winds in Book 15 (15.620).
