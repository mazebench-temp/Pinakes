---
summary: Hector's companions carry him out of the fighting to “his swift horses,” which stood behind the battle.
---

“And his companions / lifted him in their arms and carried him out of the toil, until he came to his horses, / his swift horses, which were standing behind the battle and the war / with their charioteer and the inlaid chariot” (14.428–431; [[hector]], [[horses]], [[inlaid]], [[chariot]]). The adjective ὠκέας is run over to the start of the verse. The horses carry Hector toward the city, groaning heavily, as far as the ford of the Xanthus (14.432–434; [[scamander]], [[groaning-heavily]]).
