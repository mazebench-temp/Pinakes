---
summary: Asclepius is named only in Machaon's patronymic, when Nestor speaks to his son for the last time, “the son of Asclepius” (Ἀσκληπιάδην, 14.2).
---

Ἀσκληπιάδης, “son of Asclepius”, names Machaon three times in the poem: when Talthybius summons him to the wounded Menelaus, “Rise, son of Asclepius” (4.204), when Achilles guesses at the wounded man in Nestor's chariot (11.614), and here, when Nestor leaves him to his wine and his bath (14.2; see [[machaon]], [[nestor]] and [[hecamede]]). Elsewhere the poem gives the father's name in a longer form, “the son of Asclepius, the noble healer” (4.194, 11.518). It is the last time Asclepius is named in the Iliad.
