---
summary: Poseidon tells the Argives that Hector boasts because Achilles stays by the ships “angered at heart”, and that there will be no great longing for him if they defend one another.
---

“So he says and boasts, because Achilles / stays by the hollow ships, angered at heart; / but there will be no great longing for him, if we others / urge ourselves on to defend one another” (κεχολωμένος ἦτορ, 14.366–369; see [[achilles]], [[cholos]] and [[pothe]]). κεχολωμένος ἦτορ, “angered at heart”, occurs only here in Homer. Earlier, in the likeness of an old man, Poseidon has spoken to Agamemnon of Achilles' heart in another word: “the deadly heart of Achilles / rejoices in his breast” at the Achaeans' rout (ὀλοὸν κῆρ, 14.139–140; see [[ker-heart]]).
