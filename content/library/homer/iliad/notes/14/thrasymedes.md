**The shields.** “So saying, he took the wrought shield of his son / that lay in the hut of Thrasymedes, tamer of horses, / shining with bronze; and the son had his father's shield” (14.9–11; see [[nestor]], [[shield]] and [[tamer-of-horses]]). ἱππόδαμος, “tamer of horses”, is given to Thrasymedes only here; in the earlier books he was “shepherd of the people” (9.81) and “steadfast in battle” (10.255).

The father's shield, which the son has with him, was famous. Hector, in Book 8, urged his horses on to take “Nestor's shield, whose fame now reaches the sky, / that it is all of gold, its rods and the shield itself” (8.192–193). The poem does not say why father and son have exchanged them. In the night of Book 10 Thrasymedes gave Diomedes “a two-edged sword … and a shield” for the raid on the Trojan camp (10.255–257; see [[diomedes]]); now his own shield goes out with his father. In Poseidon's ordering of the army later in the book the best men take the biggest shields (14.371–377; see [[exchange-of-armor]]).

Thrasymedes himself has no part in the fighting of the book. He fights next beside his brother Antilochus in Book 16, when the two sons of Nestor kill two companions of Sarpedon (16.317–329; see [[antilochus]]).
