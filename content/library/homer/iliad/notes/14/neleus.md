---
summary: Neleus is named in his son's patronymic, when Agamemnon greets Nestor as “Nestor, son of Neleus, great glory of the Achaeans” (14.42).
---

ὦ Νέστορ Νηληϊάδη μέγα κῦδος Ἀχαιῶν, “Nestor, son of Neleus, great glory of the Achaeans”, is a whole verse of greeting, spoken to Nestor four times in the Iliad: Agamemnon, when he comes to Nestor's hut in the night of Book 10 (10.87); Odysseus, when he and Diomedes return from the raid (10.555); Idomeneus, when he sends Nestor to carry Machaon from the field (11.511); and Agamemnon again here, when the old man meets the wounded kings at the ships (14.42; see [[nestor]], [[son-of-neleus]] and [[great-glory-of-the-achaeans]]). In the Odyssey Telemachus greets him with it twice at Pylos (Od. 3.79, 3.202).
