---
summary: Hera finds Sleep on Lemnos, “the city of divine Thoas”, and from “the city of Lemnos and Imbros” the two cross the sea to Ida.
---

**Where Hera finds Sleep.** From Athos Hera steps onto the sea “and came to Lemnos, the city of divine Thoas. / There she met Sleep, the brother of Death” (14.230–231; see [[hera]], [[sleep]] and [[thoas-of-lemnos]]). The poem does not say why Sleep is there. The island is Hephaestus' own, where he fell when Zeus threw him from heaven (1.590–594; see [[hephaestus]]), and the throne Hera offers Sleep is to be his work (14.238–240). Lemnos is named by its king, πόλιν θείοιο Θόαντος, as Troy is “the city of lord Priam” (2.373); later writers make Thoas the father of Hypsipyle, the mother of Euneus, who sends Lemnian wine to the Achaeans (7.467–469; see [[hypsipyle]] and [[euneus]]).

**Lemnos and Imbros.** When Hera has sworn her oath, “the two set off, leaving the city of Lemnos and Imbros, / clothed in mist”, and come to Ida at Lectum (14.281–284; see [[imbros]] and [[lectum]]). The two islands lie on the way from Athos to the Troad, and Hecuba names them together, with Samos, among the places to which Achilles sold her sons (24.751–753; see [[samothrace]]).
