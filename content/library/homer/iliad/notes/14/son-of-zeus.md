---
summary: Sleep recalls the day when “that high-hearted son of Zeus,” Heracles, sailed from Troy after sacking it.
---

“On the day when that high-hearted son of Zeus / was sailing from Ilios, when he had sacked the city of the Trojans” (14.250–251; [[heracles]], [[high-hearted]], [[heracles-sacks-troy]]). Sleep names Heracles by his father, the god against whom he and Hera worked that day: he lulled Zeus to sleep while Hera drove the son off his course to Cos (14.252–256; [[hera-drives-heracles-to-cos]]). Zeus himself names the son, born of Alcmene, in the list of his loves (14.323–324; [[fierce-hearted]]).
