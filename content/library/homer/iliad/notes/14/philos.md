---
summary: In the deception φίλος is a family word: Hephaestus is Hera's “dear son”, Aphrodite her “dear child”, Zeus and Hera first lay together “without the knowledge of their dear parents”, and Hera hopes to be called “dear and respected” by Oceanus and Tethys. Agamemnon gives his reason for flight in his verse of despair, “such, it seems, must be the pleasure of Zeus”; and the book ends with a dear father and a dear husband.
---

**Family.** Hera's chamber was built by “her dear son” Hephaestus (φίλος υἱός, 14.166), and she reminds Zeus of the chamber “which your dear son built for you” (14.338; see [[hephaestus]] and [[house]]). She calls Aphrodite, the daughter of Zeus, “dear child” (φίλον τέκος, 14.190), the words with which Aphrodite's own mother Dione comforted her in Book 5 (5.373; see [[aphrodite]]). Zeus and Hera first lay together “without the knowledge of their dear parents” (φίλους λήθοντε τοκῆας, 14.296; see [[eros]] and [[plato-zeus-deceived]]), and Heracles was carried off to Cos “far from all his friends” (νόσφι φίλων πάντων, 14.256; see [[hera-drives-heracles-to-cos]]).

**Dear and respected.** Hera's pretended errand would make her “dear and respected” to Oceanus and Tethys (φίλη τε καὶ αἰδοίη, 14.210; see [[aidos]]); the same pair, reversed, greets Thetis in the house of Hephaestus (αἰδοίη τε φίλη τε, 18.386 = 18.425).

**The pleasure of Zeus.** Agamemnon's reason for flight is a verse he has spoken twice before, when he tested the army and when he proposed flight in earnest before the embassy: “such, it seems, must be the pleasure of Zeus, supreme in might” (Διὶ μέλλει ὑπερμενέϊ φίλον εἶναι, 14.69 = 2.116, 9.23; see [[supreme-in-might]] and [[agamemnon]]).

**The dead.** In Peneleos' boast, the last speech of the book, the word belongs to the families of the dead: the wife of Promachus will not be glad at “her dear husband's coming home” (ἀνδρὶ φίλῳ, 14.504; see [[nostos]]), and Ilioneus' “dear father” is to wail for his son (πατρὶ φίλῳ, 14.502).
