In Book 13 Idomeneus told his line from Zeus: “Zeus first fathered Minos, a guardian over Crete” (13.450). Here Zeus himself names Minos among the sons of his loves, with a brother the Iliad has not named before, Rhadamanthys, and a mother, “the daughter of far-famed Phoenix”, whom later writers call Europa (see [[rhadamanthus]], [[europa]], [[phoenix-father-of-europa]] and [[the-loves-of-zeus]]). Only for these sons does Zeus say ἣ τέκε μοι, “who bore me”.

Μίνων is the accusative as Aristarchus read it; Book 13 has Μίνωα (13.450), and the common text here had Μίνω, which Leaf thought hardly defensible (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 14.322).
