summary: The gods as a body stand in the background of Hera's deception. She calls Aphrodite apart from them, Sleep remembers Zeus flinging them about his house, and Hera pretends to fear that one of them might see her with Zeus on Ida and tell the rest.

**Watching and telling.** The deception is a secret from the other gods. Hera calls Aphrodite “apart from the other gods” to ask for love and longing (14.188–189; see [[aphrodite]]). Sleep remembers how Zeus, waking after the first deception, went “flinging the gods about the house” in his anger (14.257; see [[sleep]]). On Gargarus Hera pretends to fear that “one of the everlasting gods / saw the two of us sleeping, and went among all the gods / and told them” (14.333–335), and Zeus answers that no god or man will see them through his golden cloud (14.342–345; see [[the-golden-cloud]]). In Book 1 the gods were the audience of their quarrel and laughed at Hephaestus as he served them (1.595–600; see [[quarrel-on-olympus]] and [[laughter-of-the-gods]]); here the scene of Zeus and Hera is hidden from them.

**The blessed gods.** Poseidon tells Agamemnon that “the blessed gods do not yet bear you a grudge altogether” (14.143; see [[blessed-gods]] and [[poseidon]]), though Zeus has given the Trojans the day.
