---
summary: The Trojans carry the fallen Hector to the ford of “the eddying Xanthus, whom immortal Zeus begot,” the first use of the verse for the river of Troy.
---

“But when they came to the ford of the fair-flowing river, / the eddying Xanthus, whom immortal Zeus begot” (14.433–434; [[scamander]], [[fair-flowing]], [[immortals]], [[hector]]). In Books 2 and 5 “the eddying Xanthus” was the river of Lycia (2.877, 5.479; [[xanthus-of-lycia]]); here it is the river of Troy, called by the name the gods give it, whereas men call it Scamander (20.74). There the Trojans lift Hector from the chariot and pour water over him (14.435–436). The two verses return when Achilles drives the Trojans to the same ford (21.1–2) and when Priam brings Hector's body back across it (24.692–693). The book adds the Trojan river to the entry's bearers.
