---
summary: Poseidon calls on the Argives not to yield the victory to Hector again, and leads them; when Hector is carried off they spring on the Trojans, and the Trojan boasts over their dead bring “grief … upon the Argives” twice, and twice an Argive's revenge (14.364, 14.391–393, 14.440, 14.456–458, 14.479–486).
---

**Poseidon's army.** The god opens his speech with the name: “Argives, are we once again to yield the victory to Hector, / son of Priam, so that he may take the ships and win glory?” (14.364–365; see [[poseidon]] and [[hector]]). In the battle that follows the two leaders stretch tight the strife of war, “the one helping the Trojans, the other the Argives”, and the sea surges up to “the huts and ships / of the Argives” (14.389–393; see [[poseidon-rallies-the-achaeans]]). Odysseus had used the name for Agamemnon's kingdom, the army “you are lord over among the Argives” (14.94; see [[odysseus]]).

**The boasts.** “But when the Argives saw Hector going away, / they sprang on the Trojans more fiercely, and remembered the joy of battle” (14.440–441), the verse with which they turned on the Trojans after the eagle omen in Book 8 (8.252; see [[charme]]). The Trojans answer with boasts addressed to them. Polydamas tells them that “one of the Argives” has caught his spear in his body and will lean on it as a staff on his way to Hades (14.456–457; see [[polydamas]] and [[prothoenor]]). Acamas calls them “Argives, you arrow-fighters, insatiable of threats” (14.479; see [[acamas]] and [[promachus]]). ἰόμωροι, “arrow-fighters”, is found only once more, in Agamemnon's mouth, when he shamed his own men in Book 4 (4.242; see [[arrow-fighters]]): the king's reproach has become the enemy's taunt. After each boast “grief came upon the Argives at his boasting” (14.458 = 14.486), and one man is stirred most of all, Telamonian Ajax the first time and Peneleos the second, as Antilochus was when Deiphobus boasted in Book 13 (13.417–418; see [[ajax]], [[peneleos]] and [[achos]]).
