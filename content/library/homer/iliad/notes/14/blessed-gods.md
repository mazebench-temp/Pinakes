---
summary: Agamemnon complains that Zeus makes the Trojans “like the blessed gods / in glory,” and Poseidon answers that “the blessed gods do not yet bear you a grudge altogether”; the book adds μακάρεσσι θεοῖσι.
grc: [μακάρεσσι θεοῖσι]
---

Agamemnon uses the title in his despair: Zeus once defended the Danaans, “and I know it now, when he makes those men like the blessed gods / in glory, and has bound our might and our hands” (14.72–73; [[agamemnon]], [[kydos]], [[zeus]]). Poseidon, in the likeness of an old man, takes his hand and answers him: “The blessed gods do not yet bear you a grudge altogether” (14.143; [[poseidon]], [[divine-epiphany]]). The king believes that the gods have left him, and a god has come to stand beside him. The dative μακάρεσσι θεοῖσι, without movable ν, is new to the entry; the form returns at 15.38, in the oath by the water of Styx, the greatest and most dread oath for the blessed gods. See [[olympians]].
