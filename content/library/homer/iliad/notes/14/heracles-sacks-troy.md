---
summary: Sleep dates his first deception of Zeus by the first sack of Troy, the day when Heracles “was sailing from Ilios, when he had sacked the city of the Trojans” (14.250–251); on the voyage home Hera drove him to Cos.
refs: [14.250-251]
---

ἤματι τῷ ὅτε κεῖνος ὑπέρθυμος Διὸς υἱὸς / ἔπλεεν Ἰλιόθεν Τρώων πόλιν ἐξαλαπάξας, “on the day when that high-hearted son of Zeus / was sailing from Ilios, when he had sacked the city of the Trojans” (14.250–251). The sack itself is not told again; it is the date of another story, Hera's storm and Zeus' anger, which Sleep tells to refuse her (see [[hera-drives-heracles-to-cos]] and [[sleep]]). The verb is the one Tlepolemus used of his father in Book 5, “and sacked the city of Ilios” (ἐξαλάπαξε, 5.642). Where Tlepolemus said that Heracles came “with only six ships and fewer men” (5.641), Sleep has him sailing home with his friends, until Hera's winds carried him off to Cos “far from all his friends” (14.256; see [[heracles]] and [[cos]]).

The first fall of Troy is recalled here to Hera, who now wants the second. She was the enemy of Heracles and is the enemy of Troy, and her answer to Sleep turns on the difference: Zeus raged for his son, but he will not rage so for the Trojans (14.264–266).
