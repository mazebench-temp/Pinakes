summary: Machaon stays in Nestor's hut. The three wounded kings come up from the ships to look at the battle; Nestor says that a wounded man cannot fight, and Diomedes persuades them to go into the battle all the same, out of range, to urge the others on. Later, “stabbed though they were”, they marshal the army and share out the armor (14.1–8, 14.27–134, 14.379–382).

**At the ships.** Machaon is still drinking in Nestor's hut, waiting for the bath that Hecamede is to heat (14.1–8; see [[machaon]] and [[hecamede]]). The three kings have stayed by the ships, which are drawn up far from the fighting (14.27–36; see [[ships]]). They come up together, “all who had been hit by the bronze” (14.28), “eager to look on the war cry and the war, / leaning on their spears, all together; and grieved / was the heart in their breasts” (14.37–39). Nestor, meeting them, will not let them fight: “a man who has been hit cannot fight” (14.63; see [[nestor]]). Eurypylus, the fifth of the wounded, is in his hut with Patroclus, who is tending his wound (11.842–848, 15.390–394; see [[eurypylus]]).

**Back to the battle.** Diomedes finds the way between Nestor's caution and the need of the army:

{{quote:264a9dfc-7e6d-52d9-8877-d4343a9dedd8}}

ἐφʼ ἕλκεϊ ἕλκος, “wound upon wound” (14.130), is found only here. οὐτάμενοί περ, “stabbed though we are” (14.128), is said of all three, and the narrator repeats it when they marshal the army, “stabbed though they were” (14.379). Nestor had kept the kinds of wound apart: Diomedes “has been shot”, Odysseus and Agamemnon “stabbed” (11.660–661); here the word for the spear's wound stands for all of them (see [[diomedes]]). They obey, and Agamemnon leads them (14.133–134; see [[agamemnon]]).

**Marshaling the army.** When Poseidon has told the army to give the best shields and the longest spears to the best men, the wounded kings carry out the order: “going among them all, they exchanged the armor of war: / the brave man put on the good, and gave the worse to the worse man” (14.381–382; see [[exchange-of-armor]], [[kings]] and [[poseidon]]). They do not fight. Their part is the one Diomedes proposed, to urge the others on, and the book does not name them again.
