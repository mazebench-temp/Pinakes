summary: The gold of Book 14 belongs to the gods and to the deception: Hera of the golden throne, her golden pins, the golden throne she offers Sleep, and the golden cloud under which Zeus sleeps. Among men there is only the silver of Ajax's sword.
grc: [χρυσείῃς]
passages:
  - 14.342-351 | The golden cloud
---

**The gods' gold.** Hera looks out from Olympus as “Hera of the golden throne” (χρυσόθρονος, 14.153; see [[golden-throned]]), fastens her robe “with golden pins” (χρυσείῃς … ἐνετῇσι, 14.180; see [[brooch]]), and offers Sleep “a fine throne, imperishable forever, / golden”, the work of Hephaestus (14.238–239; see [[throne]]). Zeus answers her fear of being seen with a golden cloud, “such a cloud will I wrap around you, / golden”, and they lie under it, “a cloud, / fine and golden; and glistening drops of dew fell from it” (14.343–344, 14.350–351; see [[the-golden-cloud]]). The golden clouds under which Ares sat on Olympus in Book 13 are the only others in Homer (13.523). The gold of the book is the gold of Hera's plan: the pins of her adornment, the throne that fails to win Sleep, and the cloud that completes the deception (see [[the-deception-of-zeus]]).

**Men's silver.** On the plain the one precious metal is the silver of Ajax's sword, whose strap, with the strap of his shield, stops Hector's spear: “the one of his shield, the other of his silver-studded sword” (14.405; see [[silver-studded]] and [[sword]]).
