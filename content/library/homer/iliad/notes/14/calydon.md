---
summary: Calydon is “steep” in Diomedes' account of his family, the home, with Pleuron, of the three sons of Portheus (14.116).
---

αἰπεινῇ Καλυδῶνι, “steep Calydon” (14.116; see [[steep]]): the half-verse that described Thoas' realm in Book 13 (13.217; see [[thoas]] and [[pleuron]]) now names the home of Diomedes' family. Of the three sons of Portheus who lived there, Oeneus “stayed there” when his son Tydeus left for Argos (14.115–120; see [[portheus]], [[oeneus]] and [[tydeus]]). He is the king whose forgotten offering to Artemis brought the boar on Calydon in Phoenix's story (9.533–549; see [[the-wrath-of-meleager]]), and his grandson now names the town as the root of his line.
