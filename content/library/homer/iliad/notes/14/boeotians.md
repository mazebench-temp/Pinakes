# Βοιώτιον (14.476), "the Boeotian", of Promachus. Βοιώτιος (17.597), of Peneleos, will need adding.
grc: [Βοιώτιον]
en: [Boeotian]
---

In the chain of killings and boasts that follows Hector's fall, the two Achaeans who die are both Boeotians. Polydamas spears Prothoenor, one of the five Boeotian leaders of the Catalogue (14.449–452; 2.495; see [[prothoenor]] and [[polydamas]]), and Acamas kills “Promachus the Boeotian” as he drags away the body of Acamas' brother Archelochus (14.476–477; see [[promachus]] and [[acamas]]). Ajax answers the first death, killing Archelochus “in return for Prothoenor” (14.459–475; see [[ajax]]). The second is answered by the Boeotians' own leader: Peneleos, first-named of the five (2.494), is stirred most of all by Acamas' boast, kills Ilioneus and holds up his head on the spear, and his taunt names the dead Boeotian's wife, who will not see her husband come home (14.486–505; see [[peneleos]] and [[alegenor]]).

The Boeotians held the line where the wall was lowest in Book 13 (13.685–700; see the note on Book 13). After Prothoenor, two more of their five leaders fall in the next book, Arcesilaus and Clonius (15.329–330, 15.340; see [[arcesilaus]] and [[clonius]]).
