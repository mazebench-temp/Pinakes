summary: Hector's spear strikes where two straps cross Ajax's chest, “the one of his shield, the other of his silver-studded sword”; the book adds the genitive ἀργυροήλου.
grc: [ἀργυροήλου]
---

“At Ajax first shining Hector threw / his spear, since he was turned straight toward him, and he did not miss, / where the two straps were stretched across his chest, / the one of his shield, the other of his silver-studded sword; / and these two protected his tender flesh” (14.402–406; [[ajax]], [[hector]], [[shield]], [[sword]], [[tender]]). The sword of the arming verse (3.334) and of Menelaus' fight with Peisander (13.610) is here known by its strap, and the strap saves the man. The genitive ἀργυροήλου is new to the entry; it returns of the silver-studded throne on which Charis seats Thetis (18.389).
