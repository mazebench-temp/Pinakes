# ὢ πόποι ἦ ῥα καὶ ἄλλοι (14.49): "Ah, truly the other … too", Agamemnon speaking.
except: [14.49]
---

**Speech and effect.** The usual forms close most of the book's speeches: ὣς εἰπών (14.9, 14.147, 14.361), ὣς ἔφαθʼ and ὣς ἔφατʼ (14.133, 14.277, 14.378, 14.458, 14.486), ὣς φάτο (14.222, 14.270, 14.506), and ἦ καί, ἦ ῥα καί (14.214, 14.346). Nearly every one names the effect in the same verse. The council obeys Diomedes and the army obeys Poseidon in one verse, “So he spoke, and they listened closely to him and obeyed” (14.133 = 14.378; see [[peitho]]). Among the gods the effects are feelings and acts: “So she spoke, and ox-eyed queen Hera smiled” (14.222; see [[smiling-at-him]]), “So she spoke, and Sleep rejoiced” (14.270; see [[charis]]), “So he spoke, and the white-armed goddess Hera did not disobey” (14.277; see [[oath]]), “He spoke, and the son of Cronus took his wife in his arms” (14.346). In the battle the boasts close in one couplet, twice, “So he spoke, and grief came upon the Argives at his boasting” (14.458 = 14.486; see [[achos]]), and the last speech of the book ends “So he spoke, and trembling seized the limbs of them all” (14.506; see [[retreat]]).

**Knowing well.** Once the closing verse judges the speaker. Ajax has said that the man he killed looks like a brother or a son of Antenor; “He spoke, knowing well” (ἦ ῥʼ εὖ γιγνώσκων, 14.475; see [[ajax]] and [[genee]]). The narrator confirms what Ajax put as a guess: Archelochus was Antenor's son (14.463–464).
