summary: Nestor's choice between the throng and Agamemnon is the book's one full decision scene, with its shock, its simile, its two courses and the verse of resolution, “And as he pondered, this seemed to him the better way”. Hera's plan to beguile Zeus is decided in the verse of the best plan.
passages:
  - 14.13-26 | Nestor decides
---

All four parts of the scene are in Nestor's choice (see the entry). The shock is what he sees from his hut: the Achaeans driven in confusion, the Trojans pressing, “and the wall of the Achaeans was thrown down” (14.13–15; see [[the-achaean-wall]]). The division is a simile and a formula: like the open sea that heaves but rolls neither way until a settled wind comes from Zeus, “so the old man pondered, torn in his heart / two ways” (14.16–21; see [[the-heaving-sea]] and [[pondering-two-ways]]). The alternatives are the throng of the Danaans or Agamemnon (14.21–22). The resolution is the verse of Deiphobus in the last book: “And as he pondered, this seemed to him the better way: / to go to the son of Atreus” (ὧδε δέ οἱ φρονέοντι δοάσσατο κέρδιον εἶναι, 14.23–24, with 14.23 = 13.458; see [[nestor]] and [[kerdos]]). No god decides for him; he goes to the king, and finds him and the other wounded kings already coming up from the ships (14.27–29).

Hera's decision is told in another verse: having pondered how to beguile Zeus, “this plan seemed to her in her heart the best” (ἀρίστη φαίνετο βουλή, 14.161), the verse of Zeus' decision to send the Dream in Book 2 (2.5; see [[boule]] and [[pondering-two-ways]]).
