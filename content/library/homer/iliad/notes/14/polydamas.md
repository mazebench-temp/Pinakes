summary: Polydamas is the first of the best Trojans who stand over the fallen Hector. Coming to defend the body of Satnius, he kills the Boeotian Prothoenor and boasts that the dead man will lean on the spear as a staff on his way to Hades; Ajax's answering spear misses him and kills Antenor's son Archelochus, and Ajax asks him whether that is not a fair exchange.

**Over Hector.** When Ajax's stone fells Hector, Polydamas is named first of the best men who stand over him, before Aeneas, Agenor, Sarpedon and Glaucus (14.424–426; see [[hector]]).

**Prothoenor.** When the son of Oileus kills Satnius, “to his defense came spear-brandishing Polydamas, / the son of Panthous” (14.449–450; see [[satnius]], [[spear-brandishing]] and [[panthous]]), as Asius came to the defense of Othryoneus' body in Book 13 (13.384). His spear goes through the right shoulder of the Boeotian Prothoenor, “and he fell in the dust and clutched the earth in his palm” (14.450–452; see [[prothoenor]] and [[boeotians]]): the death of Ascalaphus in Book 13, in the same words (14.451–452 ≈ 13.519–520; see [[ascalaphus]]). Then he boasts:

{{quote:c8fed8bf-e27d-5cef-814e-87c6df884de9}}

He speaks of himself in the third person, by his father's name: μεγαθύμου Πανθοΐδαο, “the great-hearted son of Panthous” (14.454; see [[great-hearted]]). The verse that introduces the boast, with another name, is used of Deiphobus over Hypsenor and of Idomeneus over Alcathous in Book 13, and of Acamas a few lines later (14.453 ≈ 13.413, 13.445, 14.478; see [[euchomai]]).

**Archelochus.** The couplet that followed Deiphobus' boast follows his: “So he spoke, and grief came upon the Argives at his boasting, / and most of all he stirred the heart of wise-hearted Ajax” (14.458–459 ≈ 13.417–418; see [[achos]] and [[wise-hearted]]). The dead man fell close to Ajax, who throws at Polydamas as he goes away, but “Polydamas himself avoided black doom, / darting aside” (14.462–463; see [[black-doom]]). λικριφὶς ἀΐξας, “darting aside”, is said elsewhere in Homer only of the boar that gashed Odysseus' thigh on Parnassus (*Odyssey* 19.451). Archelochus, Antenor's son, catches the spear instead, “for the gods had planned his destruction” (14.463–464; see [[archelochus]] and [[antenor]]).

**Ajax's question.** Ajax calls out to him: “Consider, Polydamas, and tell me truly: / is not this man, killed in return for Prothoenor, / worthy of him?” (14.470–472; see [[ajax]] and [[poine]]). He takes up Idomeneus' taunt to Deiphobus, “shall we then think it a fair exchange, / three killed for one” (13.446–447): ἄξιος and ἀντὶ πεφάσθαι, “worthy” and “killed in return”, come together only in these two taunts. Polydamas had boasted of a spear that did not fly in vain; Ajax's spear missed him, but found, as Ajax says, a man worthy of the one Polydamas killed, and no coward's son (14.471–474).

**His brother.** At the end of the book Menelaus kills Hyperenor (14.516–519; see [[hyperenor]] and [[menelaus]]), whom Book 17 counts among the sons of Panthous, the brother of Euphorbus (17.23–28, 17.34–35).
