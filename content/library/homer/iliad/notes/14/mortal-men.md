---
summary: Hera asks Aphrodite for the love and longing with which she overcomes “all the immortals and mortal men”; the book adds the accusative θνητοὺς ἀνθρώπους.
grc: [θνητοὺς ἀνθρώπους]
---

“Give me now love and longing, with which you / overcome all the immortals and mortal men” (14.198–199; [[aphrodite]], [[immortals]], [[philotes]], [[himeros]]). The polar phrase that divides the world between gods and men makes Aphrodite's power cover the whole of it, and Hera means to use it on the greatest of the gods. The book gives the same whole world to Sleep and to Night: Hera greets Sleep as “lord of all gods and of all men” (14.233; [[sleep]]), and Sleep names Night “the tamer of gods and men” (14.259; [[tamer-of-gods-and-men]], [[night]]). The accusative θνητοὺς ἀνθρώπους is new to the entry.
