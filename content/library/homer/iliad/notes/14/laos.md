---
summary: The book speaks of the people in the language of kingship: Agamemnon, Hector and Hyperenor are “shepherd of the people”, Odysseus describes the true king as one “whom the people would obey” and ends his rebuke with “leader of the people”, and on the crowded beach the army is pressed together.
---

“Shepherd of the people” names Agamemnon as Nestor goes to him, Hector lying beyond the reach of the Achaean spears, and the Trojan Hyperenor as Menelaus kills him (ποιμένα λαῶν, 14.22, 14.423, 14.516; see [[shepherd-of-the-people]]). Odysseus, rebuking Agamemnon, describes the man who would never have spoken as he did: one who bore a scepter, “and whom the people would obey, / as many as those you are lord over among the Argives” (καί οἱ πειθοίατο λαοί, 14.93–94; see [[scepter]] and [[peitho]]). He ends with a title: “Then your counsel would do us harm, leader of the people” (ὄρχαμε λαῶν, 14.102; see [[boule]]). In the Iliad the address is otherwise used by Euphorbus to Menelaus, by Briseis to the dead Patroclus and by the river Scamander to Achilles (17.12, 19.289, 21.221).

The λαοί are also simply the mass of men: on the beach, too narrow for all the ships, “the army was crowded together” (στείνοντο δὲ λαοί, 14.34; see [[stern]] and [[ships]]).
