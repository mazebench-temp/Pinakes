---
summary: Hera offers Sleep gifts for lulling Zeus, a golden throne made by Hephaestus with a footstool beneath it; he refuses them, and takes instead the promise of a wife.
passages:
  - 14.233-241 | Hera's offer
---

Hera's first offer to Sleep is gratitude and a gift: “And I will give you gifts: a fine throne, imperishable forever, / golden; and Hephaestus, my son, the god lame in both legs, / will make it with skill, and set a footstool beneath it for your feet, / on which you may rest your shining feet at the banquet” (δῶρα δέ τοι δώσω, 14.238–241; see [[throne]], [[footstool]], [[hephaestus]] and [[charis]]). The gift is a god's: golden, imperishable, and made by the craftsman of the gods. Sleep does not bargain over it. He tells why he will not come near Zeus, and the gift is never mentioned again (14.243–262). What persuades him is not a thing but a person, one of the younger Graces as his wife, and he makes Hera swear to it (14.267–276; see [[pasithea]] and [[oath]]).
