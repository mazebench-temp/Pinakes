Diomedes names his grandfather to show that he is not of base birth (14.110–127; see [[diomedes]] and [[genealogy-in-battle]]). Oeneus is the last named of the three brothers and the best: ἀρετῇ δʼ ἦν ἔξοχος αὐτῶν, “and in prowess he stood out among them” (14.118; see [[arete]], [[portheus]], [[agrius]] and [[melas]]). ἱππότα, “the horseman”, is a title of the older generation in the Iliad: of Nestor, of Phyleus, of Peleus, and of Tydeus himself, “the horseman Tydeus, wielder of the shield” (5.126; see [[gerenian-horseman]]). The family's towns are Pleuron and steep Calydon, which Thoas now rules (14.116, 13.217; see [[pleuron]] and [[calydon]]). The Catalogue explained why: the sons of Oeneus were no more, nor was he himself still living (2.641–643; see [[thoas]] and [[aetolians]]).

“He stayed there; but my father settled in Argos / after his wanderings” (14.119–120; see [[tydeus]] and [[argos]]). Diomedes' grandfather is the Oeneus of Phoenix's story, whose orchard Artemis' boar ravaged because he forgot her harvest offering (9.533–540; see [[the-wrath-of-meleager]]), and of Diomedes' own words to Glaucus, the host of Bellerophon, whose gift of a golden cup still stands in Diomedes' house (6.215–221; see [[bellerophon]]).
