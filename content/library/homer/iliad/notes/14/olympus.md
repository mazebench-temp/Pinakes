---
summary: Hera watches the battle from a peak of Olympus and darts down from it on her way to Lemnos and Ida; to Zeus she pretends that she has come down from Olympus only to tell him of her journey. The Muses of the invocation have their homes there.
---

**Hera's peak.** The deception begins on Olympus. Hera looks out “standing on a peak of Olympus” (στᾶσʼ ἐξ Οὐλύμποιο ἀπὸ ῥίου, 14.154) and sees the two brothers, Poseidon in the battle and Zeus on the top of Ida (14.153–158; see [[hera]] and [[ida]]). The gods watch the war from the heights; Zeus watched from Ida in Books 8 and 11, and Poseidon from Samothrace in Book 13 (8.47–52, 11.181–184, 13.10–16; see [[watching-the-war]] and [[samothrace]]). When she is adorned, “Hera darted down and left the peak of Olympus” (14.225), a verse used once more, when she goes down to Argos to bring on the birth of Eurystheus and hold back Alcmene's labor (19.114–119). ῥίον Οὐλύμποιο, “the peak of Olympus”, is also where Zeus would hang the golden rope in Book 8 (8.25; see [[the-golden-rope]]). From there her road runs by Pieria to the north (14.226; see [[pieria]]).

**Down from Olympus.** On Gargarus Zeus asks her why she has come “down from Olympus” without her horses and chariot, and she answers that she came “down from Olympus” for his sake, so that he would not be angry if she went to Oceanus in silence (14.298–311; see [[zeus]] and [[gargarus]]).

**The Muses' homes.** “Tell me now, Muses, you who have your homes on Olympus” (14.508), the invocation of the Catalogue and of the aristeia of Agamemnon, opens the list of Achaean killings when Poseidon has turned the battle (2.484, 11.218; see [[invocation-of-the-muses]] and [[muses]]).
