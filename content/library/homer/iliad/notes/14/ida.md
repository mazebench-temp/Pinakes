---
summary: Zeus sits on the topmost peak of Ida, where Hera comes to beguile him. She and Sleep reach the mountain at Lectum, where it meets the sea; Sleep hides in its tallest fir, and Zeus lies with Hera in a golden cloud on Gargarus, its peak, and sleeps there.
passages:
  - 14.283-293 | Hera and Sleep come to Ida
---

**Zeus' mountain.** From Olympus Hera sees Zeus “on the topmost peak of Ida of the many springs” (14.157; see [[of-the-many-springs]] and [[zeus]]), where he has sat since Book 11 (11.181–184). Ida is his watchtower and his altar (8.47–52; see [[gargarus]]), and her plan is to go there herself (14.162; see [[hera]]).

**The approach.** Hera and Sleep come over the sea from Lemnos and reach “Ida of the many springs, mother of wild beasts, / to Lectum, where they first left the sea” (14.283–284; see [[mother-of-wild-beasts]] and [[lectum]]). From the headland they go over land, “and the treetops of the forest shook beneath their feet” (14.285), as the mountains and woods of Samothrace trembled under Poseidon's feet (13.18–19; see [[samothrace]]). Sleep climbs into a fir, “which then on Ida / grew the tallest, and reached up through the mist into the upper air” (14.287–288; see [[sleep]] and [[fir-trees]]), and Hera goes up to Gargarus, “on lofty Ida” (14.292–293).

**At the foot and on the peaks.** Hera tells Zeus that her horses wait “at the foot of Ida of the many springs” (ἐν πρυμνωρείῃ, 14.307), a word found only here, built from πρυμνός, “lowest”, and ὄρος, “mountain”. Then she objects to lying with him “on the peaks of Ida, where everything is in plain view” (Ἴδης ἐν κορυφῇσι, 14.332). The phrase is the one used when Zeus came down to the mountain in Book 11 and when he wakes beside her in Book 15 (11.183, 15.5), and Hector burned thighs of oxen for Zeus “on the peaks of Ida” (22.171).
