# Ἀκάμαντος (14.488) occurs only here in Homer; the form is kept to the Iliad.

**Promachus.** “Then Acamas stabbed Promachus the Boeotian with his spear / as he stood over his brother, while the other dragged at the body by the feet” (14.476–477; see [[promachus]], [[archelochus]] and [[fighting-over-the-body]]). His brother has just died by the spear Ajax threw at Polydamas (14.461–464). The verse that brings on his boast, with his name, is the one that brought on Polydamas' (14.478 ≈ 14.453; see [[polydamas]]).

{{quote:1bcf3497-dd1f-568f-8ce2-dd00ef728569}}

**The boast.** He begins with the words of Agamemnon's rebuke to his own men, “Argives, you arrow-fighters” (4.242; see [[arrow-fighters]]), and adds ἀπειλάων ἀκόρητοι, “insatiable of threats”, found only here (see [[insatiable]]). The dead man “sleeps” (εὕδει, 14.482). Another son of Antenor, Iphidamas, “slept the sleep of bronze” when Agamemnon killed him (11.241; see [[iphidamas]]), and the book in which he speaks is the book of Sleep, “the brother of Death” (14.231; see [[sleep]]). Promachus' death is the penalty, ποινή, for his brother (14.483–484; see [[poine]] and [[kasignetos]]), and he ends with a maxim: “that is why a man prays / to leave a kinsman in his halls, to defend him against disaster” (14.484–485). ἀρῆς ἀλκτῆρα, “a defender against disaster”, is from ἀρή, “bane”, not the name of Ares (see [[ares]]).

**Peneleos.** The couplet that followed Polydamas' boast follows his, now of Peneleos (14.486–487 ≈ 14.458–459; see [[peneleos]] and [[achos]]). “He rushed at Acamas; but the other did not await the force / of lord Peneleos” (14.488–489), and Peneleos kills Ilioneus instead (see [[ilioneus]]). Peneleos then answers the boast in kind: the wife of Promachus will not be glad at her husband's coming home (14.503–505).
