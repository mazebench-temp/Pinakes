summary: Sleep will not come near “Zeus, the son of Cronus,” Hera protests to the “most dread son of Cronus,” and “the son of Cronus” takes his wife in his arms; the book adds the genitive Κρονίονος.
grc: [Κρονίονος]
---

The patronymic stands in a book that tells of Cronus himself: Zeus put him beneath the earth and the sea (14.203–204), and the gods below “who are around Cronus” are called to witness Hera's oath (14.274; [[cronus]], [[titans]], [[oath]]). Sleep refuses to put Zeus to sleep: “but I would not come near Zeus, the son of Cronus, / nor lull him to sleep, unless he himself should bid me” (14.247–248; [[sleep]]). Hera protests in her verse of indignation, “Most dread son of Cronus, what a thing you have said!” (14.330; [[most-dread]]). And “the son of Cronus took his wife in his arms” (Κρόνου παῖς, 14.346), Hera, who is herself “daughter of great Cronus” (14.194, 14.243; [[august-goddess]]). The genitive Κρονίονος is new to the entry. See [[zeus]].
