---
summary: The stone with which Ajax strikes down Hector is one of the props of the beached ships, which roll loose at the feet of the fighters now that the battle is among the ships. The ἕρματα of 14.182 are Hera's earrings.
grc: ["ἔχματα νηῶν"]
---

“Then, as he went away, great Telamonian Ajax / with a stone—for many of them, the props of the swift ships, / rolled about at the feet of the fighters—he lifted one of them / and hit him in the chest over the shield rim, near the neck” (τά ῥα πολλὰ θοάων ἔχματα νηῶν, 14.409–412; see [[ajax]], [[hector]] and [[stone-throwing]]). ἔχματα, “holds, supports”, is here the word for the stones that held the hulls upright; the Achaean wall had its ἔχματα too, the buttresses set as props of the towers (12.260), and the usual word for a ship's props is ἕρματα (1.486, 2.154; see the article above). The battle has reached the ships drawn up on the shore in rows (14.30–36; see [[ships]]), and the props lie loose among the fighters' feet, to be picked up as weapons.

ἕρματα at 14.182 are not props but Hera's earrings (see [[earrings]]).
