---
summary: Sleep recalls how Hera carried Heracles off “to well-peopled Cos”; the book adds the accusative εὖ ναιομένην.
grc: [εὖ ναιομένην]
---

“Rousing blasts of grievous winds over the sea, / and then you carried him off to well-peopled Cos, / far from all his friends” (14.254–256; [[cos]], [[heracles]], [[hera-drives-heracles-to-cos]], [[sleep]]). Κόων δʼ εὖ ναιομένην is Κόωνδε, “to Cos,” with the suffix of direction printed apart (see the translation's note on 14.255). The formula that elsewhere praises a town at the moment of its sack (1.164, 5.489, 13.380) here praises the island to which a goddess's storm drove a hero, “far from all his friends.” The phrase εὖ ναιομένην is new to the entry; it takes the same verse when Zeus, awake, tells the story to Hera (15.28).
