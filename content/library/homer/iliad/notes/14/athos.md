summary: Athos is the last land Hera touches on her way from Olympus: “from Athos she stepped out onto the billowing sea” and came to Lemnos.
grc: [Ἀθόω]
---

**A step to the sea.** Hera's flight from Olympus runs over Pieria, Emathia and the Thracian mountains, and then “from Athos she stepped out onto the billowing sea, / and came to Lemnos, the city of divine Thoas” (ἐξ Ἀθόω δʼ ἐπὶ πόντον ἐβήσετο κυμαίνοντα, 14.229–230; see [[hera]], [[thracians]] and [[lemnos]]). Ἀθόω is the epic genitive of Ἄθως, and Homer names the mountain only here. The peaks are her stepping stones: she “stepped on” Pieria and Emathia, sped over the topmost peaks of Thrace without touching the ground, and steps from Athos onto the sea (14.226–229; see [[pieria]] and [[emathia]]). Lemnos lies across the sea to the southeast. Strabo cites the passage, from Olympus to Athos, as an example of the order in which Homer names places (*Geography* 1.2.20; see [[strabo]]).
