---
summary: The book opens with the Trojans driving the Achaeans among the ships and ends with them in flight. While Zeus sleeps, Hector marshals them against Poseidon; when he is carried from the field the Argives fall on them, four killings and four boasts pass grief from side to side, and the head of Ilioneus held up on a spear sends them running.

**At the ships.** Nestor sees the Achaeans in turmoil, “and the others driving them in confusion from behind, / the high-hearted Trojans” (14.14–15; see [[high-hearted]]). Agamemnon says that Zeus “makes those men like the blessed gods / in glory” (14.72–73; see [[kydos]]), and Odysseus that dragging the ships to the sea would only let the Trojans have “what they pray for” (14.98; see [[odysseus]]). Poseidon promises Agamemnon that “the leaders and rulers of the Trojans” will yet fill the plain with dust, “fleeing back to the city” (14.144–146; see [[poseidon]]).

**Among the gods.** Hera asks Aphrodite for help though Aphrodite takes the Trojans' side, “because I help the Danaans and you the Trojans?” (14.192; see [[aphrodite]] and [[hera]]), and she asks Sleep whether he thinks Zeus will help the Trojans as much as he raged for Heracles, his own son (14.265–266; see [[sleep]] and [[heracles]]).

**The clash.** When Zeus sleeps on Gargarus (see [[the-deception-of-zeus]]), “on the other side shining Hector marshaled the Trojans” against Poseidon and the Argives (14.388–391; see [[hector]]), and the shout of the two armies as they meet is louder than the sea on the shore, fire in a mountain forest or the wind in the oaks (14.393–401; see [[louder-than-sea-fire-and-wind]]).

**The rout.** “But when the Argives saw Hector going away, / they sprang on the Trojans more fiercely” (14.440–441). Four killings follow, each with a boast: Polydamas kills Prothoenor, Ajax kills Archelochus, Acamas kills Promachus, and Peneleos kills Ilioneus (14.449–505; see [[polydamas]], [[archelochus]], [[acamas]] and [[ilioneus]]). After Ajax's taunt “grief seized the hearts of the Trojans” (14.475; see [[achos]]). When Peneleos holds up Ilioneus' head on his spear, “trembling seized the limbs of them all, / and each man looked around for a way to escape sheer destruction” (14.506–507), the verse of their panic when Patroclus appears in Achilles' armor (16.283). In the rout that follows, the Achaean leaders kill eight men by name, and the son of Oileus kills the most, for no one could match him in pursuing “men who fled in fear, when Zeus rouses rout among them” (14.508–522; see [[ajax-son-of-oileus]] and [[phobos]]). Zeus is asleep: the battle has been turned by Poseidon (14.510), and the last verse speaks of Zeus as the one who sends rout in general.
