summary: The killings of Book 14 come in two forms: a chain of single killings, each with its victim's lineage, its wound and its boast, in which a spear meant for one man kills another and a brother avenges a brother (14.440–507); and, after an appeal to the Muses, a list of the first Trojans killed in the rout, in which only the last death is told (14.508–522).
passages:
  - 14.440-507 | Killings and boasts
  - 14.508-522 | The first to win the spoils
---

**The chain.** When Hector has been carried off, the Achaeans attack, and the killings follow one another in the order the type-scene allows (see the article above). The son of Oileus kills Satnius, whose parents and birthplace are given (14.442–448). Polydamas, coming to defend him, kills Prothoenor: “the heavy spear went through his shoulder; / and he fell in the dust and clutched the earth in his palm” (ἕλε γαῖαν ἀγοστῷ, 14.451–452), the words of Ascalaphus' death in Book 13 (13.519–520), and the second verse also that of Chersidamas and Oenomaus (11.425, 13.508); Polydamas boasts. Ajax throws at Polydamas, and the spear kills Archelochus, “for the gods had planned his destruction” (14.459–468; see [[boule]]); Ajax boasts. Acamas, Archelochus' brother, kills Promachus as he drags the body, and boasts (14.476–485; see [[poine]]). Peneleos rushes at Acamas, who gives way, and kills Ilioneus instead, cuts off his head and boasts (14.486–505). Every part of Fenik's scheme is here: the killer and his victim, the victim's family, the exact wound, the fall, the fight for the body, and the boast (see [[euchomai]] and [[stripping-the-dead]]).

**The list.** The poet then asks the Muses who was the first to win the bloody spoils when Poseidon turned the battle (14.508–510; see [[invocation-of-the-muses]]), and answers with names: Telamonian Ajax kills Hyrtius, Antilochus Phalces and Mermerus, Meriones Morys and Hippotion, Teucer Prothoon and Periphetes (14.511–515). Only the last of the list, Menelaus' victim Hyperenor, is given a wound and a death (14.516–519; see [[psyche]]). The son of Oileus killed the most, but his victims are not named (14.520–522; see [[ajax-son-of-oileus]] and [[whom-first-whom-last]]).
