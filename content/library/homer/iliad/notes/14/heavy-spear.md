---
summary: Polydamas' heavy spear goes through Prothoenor's shoulder, and Peneleos holds up Ilioneus' head on his heavy spear, still fixed in the eye.
---

The two uses frame a Trojan boast and the Achaean answer to it. Polydamas “hit Prothoenor on the right shoulder, / the son of Areilycus, and the heavy spear went through his shoulder” (14.450–451; [[polydamas]], [[prothoenor]], [[areilycus]]), and boasts that the man will lean on the spear as a staff on his way to Hades (14.453–457). Peneleos strikes off Ilioneus' head, “and the heavy spear was still / in the eye; and holding it up like a poppy head / he showed it to the Trojans” (14.498–500; [[peneleos]], [[ilioneus]], [[the-poppy]]), and boasts in his turn (14.501–505). The weapon that in Book 3 passed through Paris' shield (3.357) is here the thing a boaster holds up.
