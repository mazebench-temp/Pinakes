---
summary: Hera claims to be going to “mother Tethys”, who with Oceanus nursed her; Ida is “mother of wild beasts”; Zeus names the mortal women he loved by the sons they bore; and Peneleos bids the Trojans tell Ilioneus' father and mother to wail for him.
---

**Mother Tethys.** Hera's pretended errand is “to see the limits of the all-nourishing earth, / and Oceanus, the origin of the gods, and mother Tethys, / who nursed me well in their halls and cherished me, / taking me from Rhea” (μητέρα Τηθύν, 14.200–203; repeated to Zeus, 14.301–303; see [[tethys]], [[oceanus]] and [[rhea]]).

**Mothers of sons.** Hera and Sleep come to “Ida of the many springs, mother of wild beasts” (14.283; see [[mother-of-wild-beasts]] and [[ida]]). Zeus' list of his loves names the mortal women by the sons they bore, “who bore Peirithous”, “who bore Perseus”, and so on (ἣ τέκε, 14.317–325), and the goddesses, Demeter and Leto, without them (14.326–327; see [[the-loves-of-zeus]]).

**A Trojan mother.** Ilioneus was an only son: “and to him the mother bore Ilioneus, an only child” (14.492), and Peneleos, holding up his head, bids the Trojans “tell noble Ilioneus' dear father for me / and his mother, to wail for him in their halls” (14.501–502; see [[ilioneus]] and [[possessions]]).
