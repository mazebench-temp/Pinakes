**Tydeus' grave.** “Tydeus, whom the heaped earth covers at Thebes” (Θήβῃσι, 14.114; see [[tydeus]] and [[diomedes]]). Θήβῃσι is the dative of the plural name Θῆβαι, as in Book 6, where Diomedes said that his father left him a child “when the army of the Achaeans perished at Thebes” (6.223; see the note on Book 6). The Iliad never tells how Tydeus died; Sthenelus said only that the fathers perished there through their own recklessness, and that the sons took the city (4.404–409; see [[the-seven-against-thebes]], [[the-epigonoi]] and [[tydeus-at-thebes]]).

**Alcmene in Thebes.** Zeus, telling Hera that he has never desired any goddess or woman as he desires her now, names among his loves “Semele, nor Alcmene in Thebes, / who bore Heracles, a fierce-hearted son” (14.323–324; see [[semele]], [[alcmene]], [[heracles]] and [[the-loves-of-zeus]]). Here the city has its singular name, Θήβη, as in Sthenelus' boast, “we even took the seat of seven-gated Thebes” (4.406; see [[seven-gated]]), and in Agamemnon's story of the day when Alcmene was to bear Heracles in Thebes (ἐνὶ Θήβῃ, 19.98–99).
