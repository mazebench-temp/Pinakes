---
summary: The Danaans are the army whose throng Nestor thinks of joining, whom Zeus once defended and has now abandoned, and whom Poseidon, set on by Sleep, defends while Zeus sleeps (14.21, 14.67–73, 14.357–362, 14.448).
---

Nestor, pondering, thinks first of going “into the throng of the Danaans of the swift horses” (14.21; see [[nestor]] and [[of-the-swift-horses]]). Agamemnon's despair is told in the name: the Danaans suffered much for the ditch, which has been no help, and Zeus, who once “defended the Danaans with a ready heart”, now gives the Trojans glory and “has bound our might and our hands” (14.66–73; see [[agamemnon]], [[the-achaean-wall]] and [[plan-of-zeus]]).

The gods take up the word. Hera, before she asks Aphrodite for love and longing, asks whether the goddess will refuse her, holding a grudge “because I help the Danaans and you the Trojans” (14.190–192; see [[hera]] and [[aphrodite]]). When Zeus is asleep, Sleep runs to Poseidon (see [[the-deception-of-zeus]]): “Now, Poseidon, help the Danaans with a ready heart, / and grant them glory, if only for a little while, while he still sleeps” (14.357–358; see [[sleep]] and [[poseidon]]), and the god is set on “still more to defend the Danaans” (14.362). In the fighting over Satnius, “Trojans and Danaans joined in fierce battle” (14.448; see [[ajax-son-of-oileus]]).
