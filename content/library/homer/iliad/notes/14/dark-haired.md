---
summary: Poseidon and Hector lead the two armies, “dark-haired Poseidon and shining Hector,” and stretch tight the strife of war.
---

“Then they stretched tight the most dread strife of war, / dark-haired Poseidon and shining Hector, / the one helping the Trojans, the other the Argives” (14.389–391; [[poseidon]], [[hector]], [[phaidimos]], [[the-rope-of-war]]). The verse pairs a god and a man as the leaders of the two sides, each with his epithet. It is the second use of κυανοχαῖτα Ποσειδάων, after the god blunted Adamas' spear on Antilochus' shield (13.563). Here he leads the Achaeans openly, with a dread sword “like lightning” in his hand (14.384–387; [[long-edged]], [[like-lightning]], [[poseidon-rallies-the-achaeans]]).
