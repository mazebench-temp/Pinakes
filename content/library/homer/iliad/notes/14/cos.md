---
summary: In Sleep's story to Hera, Cos is the island where Hera's storm carried Heracles, “far from all his friends”, when he sailed home from sacking Troy (14.255–256).
passages:
  - 14.249-256 | Hera drives Heracles to Cos
---

καί μιν ἔπειτα Κόων δʼ εὖ ναιομένην ἀπένεικας, “and then you carried him off to well-peopled Cos” (14.255). The two words Κόων δʼ, as the text divides them, are one, Κόωνδʼ: the accusative of the island's name with the suffix -δε, “to”, elided before εὖ. They are not the name of Coon, Antenor's son (see [[coon]]). ἀπένεικας, “you carried off”, makes Hera herself the one who took Heracles away, with the winds she had roused (14.254; see [[hera-drives-heracles-to-cos]] and [[heracles]]). εὖ ναιομένην, “well-peopled”, is a city's epithet, of Troy above all (2.133, 13.380; see [[well-peopled]]). The island that the Catalogue gives to the grandsons of Heracles (2.676–679) is here the place where Heracles himself was stranded.

Zeus repeats the verse word for word when he reminds Hera of the storm (15.28 = 14.255), “shewing that the same event is spoken of in both passages” (Leaf on 15.28).
