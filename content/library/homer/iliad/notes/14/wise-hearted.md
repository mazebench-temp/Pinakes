---
summary: The boasts of Polydamas and Acamas stir the hearts of “wise-hearted Ajax” and “wise-hearted Peneleos,” in a couplet repeated from Book 13.
---

“So he spoke, and grief came upon the Argives at his boasting, / and most of all he stirred the heart of wise-hearted Ajax” (14.458–459), and after Acamas' boast, “and most of all he stirred the heart of wise-hearted Peneleos” (14.486–487; [[ajax]], [[peneleos]], [[polydamas]], [[acamas]]). The couplet is the one in which Deiphobus' boast stirred Antilochus (13.417–418; [[antilochus]]). Antilochus answered by covering his fallen companion with his shield (13.419–420); here each man answers with a kill, Ajax's spear taking Archelochus and Peneleos' Ilioneus (14.461–464, 14.487–493). The epithet is new to both.
