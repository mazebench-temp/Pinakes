---
summary: Aphrodite's strap is “richly worked” twice, Hector's armor rings “inlaid with bronze” as he falls under Ajax's stone, and his chariot is “inlaid.”
---

**The strap.** Aphrodite loosed from her breast “the embroidered strap, / richly worked, in which all her enchantments were wrought” (14.214–215), and she gives it to Hera: “Here, now, put this strap in your bosom, / richly worked, in which all things are wrought” (14.219–220; [[aphrodites-band]], [[aphrodite]]). Both times ποικίλον runs over into the next verse, and the clause that follows says what is worked in it.

**Hector.** When Ajax's stone strikes him, “the spear dropped from his hand, and his shield fell upon him, / and his helmet, and around him his armor rang, inlaid with bronze” (14.419–420; [[hector]]). The half-verse is the one of men who fall dead, Alcmaon and Imbrius (12.396, 13.181); Hector falls, but he lives.

**The chariot.** His companions carry him out of the fighting to his horses, which were standing behind the battle “with their charioteer and the inlaid chariot” (14.429–431; [[chariot]]).
