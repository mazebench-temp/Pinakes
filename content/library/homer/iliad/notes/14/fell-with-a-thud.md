---
summary: Hector falls under Ajax's stone in the verses of a warrior's death, his shield and helmet falling on him “and around him his armor rang, inlaid with bronze”, the half-verse that ended Alcmaon and Imbrius; but Hector lives.
---

“so the might of Hector fell quickly to the ground in the dust; / and the spear dropped from his hand, and his shield fell upon him, / and his helmet, and around him his armor rang, inlaid with bronze” (ἀμφὶ δέ οἱ βράχε τεύχεα ποικίλα χαλκῷ, 14.418–420; see [[hector]] and [[menos]]). The half-verse of the ringing armor has closed two deaths: Alcmaon, who fell headlong after Sarpedon's spear as it was drawn out (12.394–396), and Imbrius, who fell like an ash tree cut on a mountain top (13.178–181; see the note on Book 13). Every sign of a death is given to Hector's fall, the simile of the oak struck by Zeus, the weapon dropping from the hand, the shield and helmet falling on him (see the translation's note on 14.419), and the armor ringing; and the Achaeans run up to drag him away, as men run to strip the dead (14.421–422; see [[stripping-the-dead]]). He is only stunned, and his companions carry him out of the fighting (see [[darkness-covered-his-eyes]] and [[death-in-battle]]).
