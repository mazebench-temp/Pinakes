---
summary: On her way to Lemnos Hera speeds over “the snowy mountains of the horse-herding Thracians”, touching only their topmost peaks.
---

**Hera's road.** After Pieria and Emathia, Hera “sped over the snowy mountains of the horse-herding Thracians, / over their topmost peaks, and her feet did not touch the ground”, and from Athos she stepped out onto the sea (14.226–229; see [[hera]], [[pieria]], [[emathia]] and [[athos]]). The Thracians are ἱππόπολοι, “horse-herding”, as in the first lines of Book 13, where Zeus turns his eyes from the battle to their land (13.4; see [[horse-herding]] and [[zeus]]). Now Hera crosses it on her way to put him to sleep. Strabo, who gives her route as an example of Homer's order in naming places (*Geography* 1.2.20), says that Pieria and Olympus were once Thracian lands, held in his day by the Macedonians (10.3.17; see [[strabo]]).
