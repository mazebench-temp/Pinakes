---
summary: Diomedes argues from his lineage that no one can call him “a coward and a weakling”, the words that Nestor and Agamemnon used of him before; and Acamas says that a man prays to leave a kinsman behind him as a defender against disaster.
grc: [ἀλκτῆρα]
en: [defend him against disaster]
---

**A coward and a weakling.** Diomedes ends his genealogy, “So you could not say that by birth I am a coward and a weakling / and dishonor the word I speak, if I speak it well” (κακὸν καὶ ἀνάλκιδα, 14.126–127; see [[diomedes]] and [[genee]]). The words have followed him. In Book 8, when he feared that Hector would boast of having driven him off, Nestor told him, “Even if Hector calls you a coward and a weakling”, the Trojans would not believe it (8.152–153; see [[may-the-earth-gape]]); in Book 9 he reminded Agamemnon that he had called him “unwarlike and a coward” before the Danaans (ἀπτόλεμον καὶ ἀνάλκιδα, 9.34–35). Here no one has said it, and he answers it before it can be said, with his father's name.

**A defender.** Acamas, having killed Promachus over his brother's body, boasts that the penalty for his brother has not long gone unpaid, “and that is why a man prays / to leave a kinsman in his halls, to defend him against disaster” (ἀρῆς ἀλκτῆρα, 14.484–485; see [[acamas]], [[poine]] and [[a-kinsman-in-his-halls]]). ἀλκτήρ, “defender”, belongs with ἀλκή and ἀλέξω, “ward off”. Achilles uses the same words of himself when he hears that Patroclus is dead: his friend perished, and needed him “to be his defender against disaster”, and he was not there (ἀρῆς ἀλκτῆρα γενέσθαι, 18.98–100).
