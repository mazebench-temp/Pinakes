---
summary: Odysseus bids Agamemnon be silent so that no other Achaean hears his word; Diomedes asks that his word not be dishonored for his youth; Hera speaks a word to Aphrodite apart from the gods, and answers Zeus with her verse of protest, “Most dread son of Cronus, what a thing you have said!”
---

**Words to be heard or kept quiet.** Odysseus to Agamemnon: “Be silent, so that no other man of the Achaeans hears this / word, which no man would ever let pass through his mouth at all” (τοῦτον ἀκούσῃ / μῦθον, 14.90–91; see [[silence]] and [[what-a-word]]). Diomedes, the youngest, asks the kings not to “dishonor the word I speak, if I speak it well” (μῦθον ἀτιμήσαιτε, 14.127; see [[genee]] and [[arete]]).

**Hera's words.** Hera calls Aphrodite “apart from the other gods” and speaks “a word to her” (πρὸς μῦθον ἔειπε, 14.188–189; see [[aphrodite]]). To Zeus' invitation on Ida she answers, “Most dread son of Cronus, what a thing you have said!” (ποῖον τὸν μῦθον ἔειπες, 14.330), the verse with which she answers Zeus five times elsewhere in the poem (1.552, 4.25, 8.462, 16.440, 18.361; see [[what-a-word]] and [[most-dread]]). Here the protest is part of the deception.
