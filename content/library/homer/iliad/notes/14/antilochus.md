---
summary: In the rout at the end of the book, “Antilochus slew and stripped Phalces and Mermerus” (14.513).
---

Antilochus has one verse in the book, in the list of the Achaeans who win bloody spoils after Poseidon has turned the battle: “and Antilochus slew and stripped Phalces and Mermerus” (14.513; see [[invocation-of-the-muses]], [[bloody-spoils]] and [[stripping-the-dead]]). ἐξενάριξε is to kill and strip a man of his armor (ἔναρα). Phalces was one of the Trojan leaders gathered around Polydamas when Hector led the Trojans back into the fight at the end of Book 13 (13.789–791; see [[phalces]] and [[polydamas]]), and Meriones, named next, kills another of them, Morys (14.514; see [[meriones]]). Μέρμερος is a name only here; the common adjective μέρμερος means “grim” (see [[mermerus]] and [[grim-deeds]]).

The book that began with his father, roused from his wine by the din at the ships (14.1–8; see [[nestor]]), names the son once, among the killers of the rout. His brother Thrasymedes has their father's shield (14.11; see [[thrasymedes]]).
