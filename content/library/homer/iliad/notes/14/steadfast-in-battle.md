---
summary: Poseidon tells the Argives that any man “steadfast in battle” who has a small shield should give it to a worse man and put on a bigger one; the book adds the form μενέχαρμος.
grc: [μενέχαρμος]
---

“And whoever is a man steadfast in battle, but has a small shield on his shoulder, / let him give it to a worse man, and himself put on a bigger shield” (14.376–377; [[poseidon]], [[shield]], [[poseidon-rallies-the-achaeans]]). The epithet that Book 13 gave to Antilochus, a named hero (13.396), here sorts the whole army: the steadfast man deserves the bigger shield. The wounded kings carry out the exchange, “the brave man put on the good, and gave the worse to the worse man” (14.379–382; [[the-wounded-chiefs]], [[warlike]]). The form μενέχαρμος, of the second declension beside Book 13's μενεχάρμης, is new to the entry.
