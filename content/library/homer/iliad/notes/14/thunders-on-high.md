---
summary: Nestor says that not even Zeus, “who thunders on high,” could have fashioned things otherwise: the wall is down, and the fighting is at the ships.
---

“Truly, these things are here and done, and not otherwise / could Zeus himself, who thunders on high, have fashioned them. / For the wall has been thrown down, which we trusted / would be an unbreakable safeguard for the ships and for ourselves” (14.53–56; [[nestor]], [[the-achaean-wall]]). Ζεὺς ὑψιβρεμέτης opens the verse, as it does in all its uses. In Book 12 it began Polydamas' reading of the battle at the wall, “Zeus who thunders on high, and wishes to help the Trojans” (12.68; [[polydamas]]); here Nestor names the same god as the one power that could have changed what has happened.
