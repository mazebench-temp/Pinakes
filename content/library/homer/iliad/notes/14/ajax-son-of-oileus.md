**The first to attack.** When the Argives see Hector carried away, “they sprang on the Trojans more fiercely” (14.440–441), and “by far the first, swift Ajax, the son of Oileus, / leapt upon Satnius and stabbed him with his sharp spear” (14.442–443; see [[swift]] and [[satnius]]). Satnius' birth is told in two verses: he was the son of Enops, borne by a Naiad nymph to Enops as he tended his cattle by the banks of the river Satnioeis (14.444–445; see [[enops]], [[nymphs]] and [[satnioeis]]). “The son of Oileus, famed for his spear” (δουρὶ κλυτός), comes close and stabs him in the flank, and the two armies close in fierce battle over the body (14.446–448; see [[famed-for-his-spear]]). Polydamas, coming to its defense, kills Prothoenor (14.449–452; see [[polydamas]] and [[prothoenor]]).

**The pursuit.**

{{quote:0540f9d9-6e93-5508-9ee6-56b66376fb6c}}

His standing title, ταχύς, “swift”, is shown at work: no one could match him “to pursue on foot” the men who fled. The Catalogue made him small and gave him the spear, with which he “surpassed all the Panhellenes and Achaeans” (2.529–530); in the rout he wins by his feet. In the footrace of Book 23 he leads Odysseus until Athena makes him slip (23.754–777). The two Ajaxes frame the rout: Telamonian Ajax is the first to kill after the invocation of the Muses, the son of Oileus kills the most (14.511–522; see [[ajax]], [[the-two-ajaxes]] and [[rout]]). “When Zeus rouses rout among them” (14.522) is said of any rout; this one is Poseidon's, “when the famous shaker of the earth had turned the battle” (14.510), while Zeus lies asleep on Gargarus (14.352–353; see [[poseidon]] and [[zeus]]).
