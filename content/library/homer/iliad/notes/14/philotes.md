---
summary: Book 14 uses φιλότης a dozen times, and always of love. It is the first gift Hera asks of Aphrodite and the first power worked into her strap, the bond Hera claims she will restore between Oceanus and Tethys, the love in which Zeus and Hera first lay together, and the love in which Zeus is overcome.
passages:
  - 14.197-217 | Love and longing
  - 14.292-296 | As when they first lay together
  - 14.313-328 | Zeus asks Hera to bed
---

**The plan and the gift.** Hera's plan is to go to Ida “to see if he might long to lie in love beside / her body” (παραδραθέειν φιλότητι, 14.163–164; see [[himeros]] and [[hypnos]]). She asks Aphrodite, “Give me now love and longing” (φιλότητα καὶ ἵμερον, 14.198), and receives the strap in which “was love, in it longing, in it intimate talk” (14.216; see [[aphrodites-band]] and [[oaristys]]). To Sleep she promises the bargain's moment, “as soon as I have lain beside him in love” (14.237; see [[sleep]]).

**The false story.** Hera tells Aphrodite, and then Zeus, that she is going to the ends of the earth to end a quarrel: Oceanus and Tethys “have kept away from each other, / from the bed and from love, since anger has fallen upon their hearts” (εὐνῆς καὶ φιλότητος, 14.206–207 = 14.305–306), and she will bring them back “to their bed to be joined in love” (14.209; see [[oceanus]], [[tethys]] and [[truth-and-deception]]). The lie has the shape of her purpose: a husband and wife estranged by anger and brought back to their bed. Zeus was hateful to her heart when she saw him on Ida (14.158), and it is to bed with him that she is going (see [[husbands-and-wives]]).

**Zeus.** When Zeus sees her, desire wraps his mind “as when they first lay together in love, / going to bed together without the knowledge of their dear parents” (ἐμισγέσθην φιλότητι, 14.295–296; see [[eros]] and [[plato-zeus-deceived]]). μίσγεσθαι φιλότητι, literally “to mingle in love”, is the formula of lying together, as at 2.232, 3.445 and 6.25 (see the article above). Zeus answers her story with a verse of seduction: “but come, let the two of us go to bed and take our pleasure in love” (νῶϊ δʼ ἄγʼ ἐν φιλότητι τραπείομεν εὐνηθέντε, 14.314). Paris says almost the same verse to Helen when he has lost the duel (3.441), and in the Odyssey Ares ends his invitation to Aphrodite with the same words, in the song of Demodocus (*Odyssey* 8.292). Hera turns it back on him, “If you are eager now to go to bed in love / on the peaks of Ida” (14.331–332), and Sleep reports the outcome to Poseidon: “Hera has beguiled him into going to bed with her in love” (14.360; see [[apate]]). Between the two, the narrator gives the end: Zeus sleeps on Gargarus “overcome by sleep and love” (14.353; see the note on Book 13).

The treaty and the guest-friendship that the word also names do not appear in Book 14 (see the article above). Every φιλότης in it is the love of Zeus and Hera, of Oceanus and Tethys, or of the gifts that bring it about.
