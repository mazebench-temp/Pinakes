summary: Odysseus tells Agamemnon that no man who “bore a scepter” and was obeyed by his people would let such a word pass his lips; the book adds the phrase σκηπτοῦχός τʼ εἴη.
grc: ["σκηπτοῦχός τʼ εἴη"]
en: [bore a scepter]
---

“Be silent, so that no other man of the Achaeans hears this / word, which no man would ever let pass through his mouth at all / who knew in his mind how to speak fitting words, / and bore a scepter, and whom the people would obey, / as many as those you are lord over among the Argives” (14.90–94; [[odysseus]], [[agamemnon]], [[scepter]]). The epithet of the king becomes the measure of a king: the man who holds the scepter is one who speaks fittingly and is obeyed. In Book 9 Nestor reminded Agamemnon that Zeus had entrusted him with the scepter and the laws so that he might take counsel for his many peoples (9.96–99); here Odysseus tells him that his counsel is unworthy of the scepter. The phrase σκηπτοῦχός τʼ εἴη is new to the entry.
