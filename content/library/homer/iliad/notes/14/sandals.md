---
summary: Hera binds on her sandals last of all, in the verse with which Agamemnon and Nestor dress in Books 2 and 10; she is the only woman in Homer of whom the verse is used.
---

The last verse of Hera's adornment is the sandal verse: “and under her shining feet she bound her fine sandals” (ποσσὶ δʼ ὑπὸ λιπαροῖσιν ἐδήσατο καλὰ πέδιλα, 14.186; see [[the-adornment-of-a-goddess]]). In the Iliad it is otherwise said of Agamemnon, when he rises after the Dream and again in the night of Book 10, and of Nestor (2.44, 10.22, 10.132; see [[dressing-scene]]); in the Odyssey of Telemachus and Menelaus (*Odyssey* 2.4, 4.309, 20.126). Hera is the only woman of whom it is said. The gods' sandals are golden and carry them over land and sea (24.340–342), but Hera's are not described, and she does not walk: she darts from Olympus and steps on the mountaintops of Thrace, “and her feet did not touch the ground” (14.225–228).
