---
summary: In the simile for the shout of the armies, “the roar of blazing fire / in the glens of a mountain” is the second of three sounds less loud than the war cry.
---

When Poseidon and Hector bring the armies together, the shout is greater than the wave on the shore, than the fire, than the wind in the oaks: “nor so loud is the roar of blazing fire / in the glens of a mountain, when it rushes up to burn the forest” (14.396–397; [[forest-fire]], [[high-leafed]], [[grievous]]). Elsewhere the formula πυρὸς αἰθομένοιο gives fire its light and its heat, the fire that men fight like (11.596, 13.673) and the fire that threatens the ships (16.81); here it gives the roar (βρόμος). The three comparisons are measured by sound alone: “as loud as was the voice of the Trojans and the Achaeans / as they shouted terribly” (14.400–401).
