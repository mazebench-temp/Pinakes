---
summary: Odysseus measures Agamemnon against the man who “bore a scepter” and was obeyed; and Polydamas says that the Argive he has killed will use the spear in his body as a staff to lean on, going down to Hades.
grc: [σκηπτόμενον]
en: [lean on it as a staff]
---

**The scepter-bearer.** No man would let Agamemnon's word of flight pass his lips, says Odysseus, “who knew in his mind how to speak fitting words, / and bore a scepter, and whom the people would obey” (σκηπτοῦχός τʼ εἴη, 14.92–93; see [[scepter-bearing]], [[odysseus]] and [[laos]]). σκηπτοῦχος is the word for the kings who rise from the council in Book 2 (2.86), and Nestor uses it when he warns Achilles not to strive with a king, since no ordinary share of honor falls “to a scepter-bearing king, to whom Zeus has given glory” (1.277–279).

**The staff.** The verb of the same family is σκήπτομαι, “lean on a staff”. Polydamas, boasting over Prothoenor, says that “one of the Argives has caught it in his body, and I think / he will lean on it as a staff as he goes down into the house of Hades” (αὐτῷ σκηπτόμενον κατίμεν δόμον Ἄϊδος εἴσω, 14.456–457; see [[polydamas]], [[prothoenor]] and [[euchomai]]). In the Iliad the verb is found only in this boast; in the Odyssey it is the posture of Odysseus disguised as an old beggar, leaning on his staff (*Odyssey* 17.203, 17.338, 24.158).
