---
summary: Nestor answers Agamemnon as “the Gerenian horseman,” and Diomedes names his grandfather “the horseman Oeneus”; the book adds the phrase ἱππότα Οἰνεύς.
grc: ["ἱππότα Οἰνεὺς"]
---

**Nestor.** “Then the Gerenian horseman Nestor answered him” (14.52; [[nestor]], [[then-answered]]). Agamemnon has asked why he left the battle; Nestor answers that the wall is down, that the fighting is at the ships, and that the wounded must not fight (14.53–63; [[the-achaean-wall]], [[the-wounded-chiefs]]).

**Oeneus.** In Diomedes' genealogy the horseman's title goes to his grandfather: “Agrius and Melas, and the third was the horseman Oeneus, / my father's father; and in prowess he stood out among them” (14.117–118; [[oeneus]], [[agrius]], [[melas]], [[diomedes]]). ἱππότα alone is given to men of the older generation: Phyleus, Tydeus, Oeneus, and Peleus (2.628, 5.126, 14.117, 16.33). The phrase ἱππότα Οἰνεύς is new to the entry.
