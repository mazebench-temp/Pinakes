summary: ἀϋτή is the war cry and the battle itself. The wounded kings come “eager to look on the war cry and the war”; Nestor says that the shout reaches the sky; Poseidon shouts like ten thousand men; the armies meet shouting louder than sea, fire and wind; and Polydamas and Acamas boast over the dead, “shouting aloud”.
en: [shouting aloud]
---

**War cry and war.** “So they came on, eager to look on the war cry and the war” (ὀψείοντες ἀϋτῆς καὶ πολέμοιο, 14.37; see [[the-wounded-chiefs]]); Odysseus rebukes Agamemnon for bidding them drag the ships to the sea “while war and the war cry are joined” (πολέμοιο συνεσταότος καὶ ἀϋτῆς, 14.96; see [[polemos]]).

**To the sky.** Nestor tells the kings that the Achaeans are being killed in confusion, “and the shout reaches the sky” (ἀϋτὴ δʼ οὐρανὸν ἵκει, 14.60). The verse-end was heard at the wall (12.338) and first in Book 2, when the army ran to drag the ships down and go home: “their shout reached the sky / as they longed for home” (2.153–154). Agamemnon is about to propose that flight again (14.65–81; see [[no-blame-in-fleeing]]).

**Shouts.** Poseidon “gave a great shout as he sped over the plain” (μέγʼ ἄϋσεν, 14.147), as loud as nine or ten thousand men (14.148–151; see [[ten-thousand-men]]); the armies meet “as they shouted terribly” (δεινὸν ἀϋσάντων, 14.401; see [[louder-than-sea-fire-and-wind]]); and Polydamas and Acamas “boasted terribly, shouting aloud” (ἔκπαγλον ἐπεύξατο μακρὸν ἀΰσας, 14.453, 14.478), the verse of Deiphobus and Idomeneus in Book 13 (13.413, 13.445; see [[euchomai]]).
