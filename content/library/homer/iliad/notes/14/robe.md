  - 14.178-180 | Hera's robe
---

After the bath, the oil and her hair, Hera puts on her robe: “And she put on an ambrosial robe, which Athena / had smoothed and worked for her, and set many intricate designs in it; / and she fastened it at her breast with golden pins” (ἀμφὶ δʼ ἄρʼ ἀμβρόσιον ἑανὸν ἕσαθʼ, 14.178–180; see [[the-adornment-of-a-goddess]], [[brooch]] and [[ambrosial]]). The robe is a goddess's own work. Athena wears one she made herself, “richly worked, which she herself had made and labored at with her hands”, and lets it fall on her father's floor when she arms (5.734–735 = 8.385–386; see [[athena]] and [[weaving]]); Aphrodite's ambrosial robe was made by the Graces themselves (5.338). Hera's comes from her ally in the war, and it serves the war: she wears it to put Zeus to sleep so that Poseidon can help the Achaeans (see [[the-deception-of-zeus]]). δαίδαλα πολλά, “many intricate designs”, are elsewhere the work of Hephaestus, the things he forged for Thetis and Eurynome in their cave and the figures he set on the shield of Achilles (18.400, 18.482).
