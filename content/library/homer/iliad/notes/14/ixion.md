Ἰξιονίης ἀλόχοιο: Ἰξιονίη is not his name in the genitive but an adjective made from it, “the Ixionian wife”, as Nestor's ship is the “Nestorian” ship (Νεστορέῃ παρὰ νηΐ, 2.54) and the might of Heracles is the “Heraclean might” (βίῃ Ἡρακληείῃ, 2.658; see [[might-of-heracles]]). She is the first of Zeus' loves in the list, and the verb that governs all of them, ἠρασάμην, “I loved”, stands in her verse (see [[the-loves-of-zeus]]). Her son is Peirithous, king of the Lapiths, whom the Catalogue already called the son of Zeus (2.741; see [[peirithous]]).

Zeus names to Hera the wife of a man whom later poets punished for desiring Hera herself. In Pindar, Ixion, received among the gods, desired “Hera, who was allotted to the joyful bed of Zeus”, and Zeus deceived him with a cloud in her likeness (*Pythian* 2.27–40; see [[ixion-on-the-wheel]]). Homer says nothing of this; in his line the wrong runs the other way.
