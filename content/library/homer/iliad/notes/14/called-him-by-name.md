summary: Three gestures and one formula: Aphrodite puts the strap into Hera's hands and calls her by name; Hera clings to Sleep's hand and calls him by name, in the verse of Hecuba and Andromache with Hector; and Zeus stands before Hera and calls her by name.
passages:
  - 14.231-233 | Hera meets Sleep
---

The formula marks the three closest meetings of the book, each with its own gesture before it. Aphrodite gives Hera the strap: “This she put into her hands, and spoke to her, and called her by name” (14.218; see [[aphrodites-band]]). Hera, meeting Sleep on Lemnos, “clung to his hand, and spoke to him, and called him by name” (ἔν τʼ ἄρα οἱ φῦ χειρὶ ἔπος τʼ ἔφατʼ ἔκ τʼ ὀνόμαζεν, 14.232; see [[sleep]]), and begins with his name and a title, “Sleep, lord of all gods and of all men” (14.233; see [[anax]]). The whole verse is used for Hecuba and for Andromache taking Hector's hand (6.253, 6.406) and for Charis and Hephaestus welcoming Thetis (18.384, 18.423); in the Odyssey it is the verse with which Ares takes Aphrodite's hand and asks her to bed (*Odyssey* 8.291). Hera's affection is the first move of a bargain. On Gargarus Zeus, seized with desire, “stood before her, and spoke to her, and called her by name” (14.297), and his first word is her name, “Hera, where are you bound” (14.298; see [[zeus]] and [[eros]]).
