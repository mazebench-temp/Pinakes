summary: Hera “of the golden throne” watches from Olympus, and offers Sleep, for lulling Zeus, a golden throne made by Hephaestus, with a footstool for his feet at the banquets of the gods; he refuses it.
passages:
  - 14.238-241 | Hera's offer of a throne
---

Hera enters the book in her epithet: “And Hera of the golden throne looked out with her eyes, / standing on a peak of Olympus” (χρυσόθρονος, 14.153–154; see [[golden-throned]] and [[hera]]). Her first offer to Sleep is a throne of the same kind: “a fine throne, imperishable forever, / golden; and Hephaestus, my son, the god lame in both legs, / will make it with skill, and set a footstool beneath it for your feet, / on which you may rest your shining feet at the banquet” (θρόνον ἄφθιτον αἰεί, 14.238–241; see [[footstool]], [[hephaestus]] and [[doron]]). A throne is a seat of honor among the gods, and this one has all the marks of divine work at once: gold, imperishable, made by Hephaestus (see the article above and [[imperishable]]). Sleep refuses it, and tells her why he fears Zeus (14.243–262; see [[sleep]]). When Zeus wakes in the next book and sends Hera back to Olympus, the gods rise to greet her with cups (15.84–88), and when she has given Zeus' orders to Iris and Apollo she sits down on her throne (ἕζετο δʼ εἰνὶ θρόνῳ, 15.149–150).
