---
summary: Hera, putting on her veil in the adornment, is “brilliant among goddesses,” the only time in the Iliad that the formula is hers.
---

“And with a veil over all she covered herself, brilliant among goddesses, / a fine one, new-made, and it was white like the sun” (14.184–185; [[veil]], [[dressing-scene]], [[hera]]). The formula of praise comes with the last and brightest piece of Hera's adornment, before she binds on her sandals and goes out to Aphrodite (14.186–188). Elsewhere in the Iliad it is given to Dione, Athena, Charis and Thetis; it names nothing of any one goddess but her rank, and here the rank is dressed for a deception.
