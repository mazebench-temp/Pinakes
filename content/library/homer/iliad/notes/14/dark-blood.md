---
summary: Revived by the water of the Xanthus, Hector kneels and vomits “dark blood,” αἷμα κελαινεφές.
---

“There they lifted him down from the chariot to the ground, and poured water / over him; and he got his breath back and looked up with his eyes, / and kneeling he vomited dark blood” (14.435–437; [[hector]], [[scamander]]). The word is κελαινεφής, “dark-clouded,” otherwise an epithet of Zeus the storm god ([[lord-of-dark-clouds]]); the blood comes from within, after the blow of Ajax's stone on his chest near the neck (14.409–413; [[ajax]], [[stone-throwing]]). Then black night covers his eyes again (14.438–439; [[black-night]]). The same phrase names the blood that Apollo washes from the body of Sarpedon (16.667).
