---
summary: The winds are shrill in the simile of the heaving sea, and Sleep sits in the fir on Ida “like a shrill-voiced bird.”
---

In the simile of Nestor's indecision the sea senses “the swift paths of the shrill winds” (14.17; [[the-heaving-sea]], [[nimble]]): λιγέων ἀνέμων, from λιγύς, the adjective of Nestor as a clear-voiced speaker ([[clear-voiced-orator]]). Sleep, hidden among the branches of the tall fir on Ida, sits “like a shrill-voiced bird, which in the mountains / the gods call chalcis, and men cymindis” (14.290–291; [[sleep]], [[chalcis-and-cymindis]], [[language-of-gods-and-men]]). Of the bird the poem gives only its voice and its two names. The dative λιγυρῇ is the form of the wind's shrill blast, πνοιῇ ὕπο λιγυρῇ (13.590).
