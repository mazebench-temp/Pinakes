summary: “Glorious Leto” is the last of Zeus' past loves in the list he recites to Hera, and the one who stands next to Hera herself (14.327).
passages:
  - 14.326-328 | Demeter, Leto, and Hera herself
---

{{quote:e3f87abb-2f75-5513-8021-e237cb0cb9a7}}

οὐδʼ ὁπότε Λητοῦς ἐρικυδέος, οὐδὲ σεῦ αὐτῆς, “nor when I loved glorious Leto, nor you yourself” (14.327): Leto has half a verse, and Hera the other half (see [[the-loves-of-zeus]] and [[hera]]). Her children, Apollo and Artemis, are not named. Hesiod's list of the wives of Zeus ends in the same way, with Leto and then Hera, “last” (*Theogony* 918–921). Elsewhere in the poem Leto counts among the wives of Zeus: Hermes will not fight her, since it is hard to come to blows with “the wives of cloud-gathering Zeus” (ἀλόχοισι Διὸς νεφεληγερέταο, 21.498–499), and the Odyssey calls her “the glorious consort of Zeus” (*Odyssey* 11.580).

ἐρικυδής, “glorious”, is her epithet also in the *Homeric Hymns*, in the phrase “the son of glorious Leto”, Apollo (*Hymn* 3.182; 4.176, 4.189, 4.416; see [[glorious]]).
