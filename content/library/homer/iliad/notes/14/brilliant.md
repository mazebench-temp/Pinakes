---
summary: Nestor calls Machaon “brilliant,” Hera is “brilliant among goddesses,” the earth under Zeus and Hera is “the shining earth,” and Agenor is “brilliant” among those who shield the fallen Hector; the shining sea of 14.76 belongs to shining-sea.
except: [14.76]
---

**Persons.** Nestor addresses the wounded Machaon over their wine: “Consider, brilliant Machaon, how these things are to be” (14.3; [[machaon]], [[nestor]]). The vocative δῖε has been given before to Hector's horse Lampus, to Patroclus and to Thootes (8.185, 11.608, 12.343). When Hector falls under Ajax's stone, the best of the Trojans stand over him, “Polydamas and Aeneas and brilliant Agenor” (14.425; [[agenor]], [[hector]]), the epithet he had among the sons of Antenor in Book 11 and when Aeneas looked to him for help in Book 13 (11.59, 13.490).

**A goddess.** As Hera puts on her veil she is “brilliant among goddesses” (14.184; [[brilliant-among-goddesses]], [[veil]]).

**The earth.** Under Zeus and Hera on Gargarus “the shining earth sent up fresh-sprung grass” (14.347; [[earth]], [[gargarus]]). The Iliad calls the earth δῖα here and once more, in Achilles' words to Priam about the man whom evil hunger drives over the shining earth (24.532). As of the sea, the translation renders the word “shining” ([[shining-sea]]).

**The sea.** εἰς ἅλα δῖαν, “into the shining sea,” in Agamemnon's proposal to launch the ships (14.76), is the formula of [[shining-sea]], and the entry excepts it, as at 1.141 and 2.152.
