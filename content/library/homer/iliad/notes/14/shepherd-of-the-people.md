---
summary: The title names Agamemnon as Nestor goes to him, Hector as he lies fallen beyond the reach of the Achaean spears, and Hyperenor as Menelaus kills him.
---

**Agamemnon.** Nestor ponders whether to go into the throng of the Danaans “or to the son of Atreus, Agamemnon, shepherd of the people” (14.22; [[of-the-swift-horses]], [[agamemnon]]).

**Hector.** When Ajax's stone has felled Hector, the sons of the Achaeans run up and hurl their spears, “but the shepherd of the people no one could / stab or hit; for before that the best stood over him” (14.423–424; [[hector]], [[sons-of-the-achaeans]]). The leader's title is given to the leader at his most helpless, protected by others.

**Hyperenor.** “And then the son of Atreus stabbed Hyperenor, shepherd of the people, / in the flank” (14.516–517; [[hyperenor]], [[menelaus]]). It is the last death named in the list of the rout, and the only one told in full: the bronze lets out his bowels, his soul rushes out through the wound, and darkness covers his eyes (14.517–519; [[psyche]], [[darkness-covered-his-eyes]]). In Book 17 Menelaus recalls the killing, and Euphorbus, son of Panthous, calls Hyperenor his brother (17.24–28, 17.34–35; [[panthous]]).
