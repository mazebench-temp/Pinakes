summary: Hector's companions lift him in their arms and carry him “out of the toil”, as Patroclus' body will be carried; and Acamas tells the Argives that “the toil and the misery” will not be the Trojans' alone.
passages:
  - 14.427-432 | Hector carried from the fight
---

**Out of the toil.** When Ajax's stone has struck Hector down, his companions hold their shields before him, then “lifted him in their arms and carried him out of the toil, until he came to his horses” (χερσὶν ἀείραντες φέρον ἐκ πόνου, 14.428–429; see [[hector]] and [[hetairos]]). πόνος is the fighting itself, the war's work (see the article above). The phrase returns once in the Iliad, when Ajax tells Menelaus and Meriones to lift Patroclus' body and carry it out of the toil while he and the son of Oileus hold off the Trojans: νεκρὸν ἀείραντες φέρετʼ ἐκ πόνου (17.715–721). Hector is carried out of the toil alive, and in the next book Apollo sends him back into it (15.239–270).

**Toil and misery.** Acamas, standing over the man he has killed, tells the Argives, “surely the toil and the misery will not be ours / alone; but some day you too will be killed like this” (πόνος τʼ ἔσεται καὶ ὀϊζύς, 14.480–481; see [[acamas]]). The pair is the one with which Book 13 began: Zeus left the Trojans and Achaeans beside the ships “to have toil and misery / without ceasing” (13.2–3; see the note on Book 13). In Book 13 it was the god's indifference; in Acamas' mouth it is a share that the other side will have too.
