---
summary: Hera's lie recalls the day “when far-seeing Zeus put Cronus / down beneath the earth and the barren sea.”
---

Outside its usual formula, “along the shore of the barren sea” (παρὰ θῖνʼ ἁλὸς ἀτρυγέτοιο, 1.316, 1.327), the epithet marks the edge of the world above the prison of Cronus. Oceanus and Tethys took Hera from Rhea, she says, “when far-seeing Zeus put Cronus / down beneath the earth and the barren sea” (14.203–204; [[cronus]], [[rhea]], [[far-seeing]], [[titans]]). The earth and the sea are the two that Hera touches in her oath, one hand on the bountiful earth and the other on the gleaming sea, so that the gods below, “who are around Cronus,” may be witnesses (14.271–274; [[bountiful-earth]], [[marmareos]], [[oath]]).
