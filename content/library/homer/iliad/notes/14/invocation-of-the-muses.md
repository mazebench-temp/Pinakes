The first verse is the one that opened the Catalogue of Ships (14.508 = 2.484) and the question before Agamemnon's first opponent (11.218); it will come once more before the fire reaches the ships (16.112; see the notes on Books 2 and 11). In the battles the poet asks the Muses at a turn of the fighting, and the question is who or what was first. Here the turn is Poseidon's: “when the famous shaker of the earth had turned the battle” (ἐπεί ῥʼ ἔκλινε μάχην κλυτὸς ἐννοσίγαιος, 14.510; see [[poseidon]] and [[shaker-of-the-earth]]). The god who helped the Achaeans in secret in Book 13 is named as the cause of the rout, and Zeus, who is asleep, is not.

In Book 11 the answer was one man and his history (11.221–231); here it is a list of names with almost no histories: Telamonian Ajax first, then Antilochus, Meriones, Teucer and Menelaus, and the son of Oileus, who killed the most (14.511–522; see [[androktasia]] and [[stripping-the-dead]]).
