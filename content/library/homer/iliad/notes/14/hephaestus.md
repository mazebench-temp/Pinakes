---
summary: Hephaestus does not appear in Book 14, but his work serves Hera's plan. He built the chamber with the secret bolt where she adorns herself, she promises Sleep a golden throne of his making, and she offers Zeus the same chamber; she calls him “my son”, and to Zeus “your dear son”.
refs: [14.166, 14.168, 14.240, 14.338]
---

**The chamber.** Hera adorns herself in the chamber “which her dear son built for her, / Hephaestus, and he fitted close doors to the doorposts / with a secret bolt, which no other god could open” (14.166–168; see [[hera]] and [[houses-of-the-gods]]). κληῗδι κρυπτῇ, “a secret bolt”, is found only here. Every god has a house that Hephaestus built (1.606–608); Hera's has a lock that keeps out even the gods, and there she prepares her deception unseen (see [[the-adornment-of-a-goddess]]).

**The throne.** To win Sleep, Hera promises “a fine throne, imperishable forever, / golden; and Hephaestus, my son, the god lame in both legs, / will make it with skill, and set a footstool beneath it for your feet” (14.238–240; see [[sleep]], [[throne]], [[footstool]] and [[lame-in-both-legs]]). In Book 18 the house of Hephaestus has such a throne, silver-studded and with a footstool, where his wife Charis seats Thetis (18.389–390). Sleep refuses the gift.

**Whose son.** On Gargarus Hera reminds Zeus of the same chamber: “you have a chamber, which your dear son built for you, / Hephaestus, and he fitted close doors to the doorposts” (14.338–339). To Sleep he is “my son” (14.239), to Zeus “your dear son”, and the verse that describes the doors is the one the narrator used (14.167 = 14.339). In the Iliad he is the son of both (1.572, 1.577–578); Hesiod makes him Hera's alone (*Theogony* 927–929).
