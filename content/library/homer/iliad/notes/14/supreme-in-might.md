summary: Agamemnon, proposing flight, says that “such, it seems, must be the pleasure of Zeus, supreme in might”: the Achaeans will perish here, nameless, far from Argos.
---

“Such, it seems, must be the pleasure of Zeus, supreme in might, / that the Achaeans should perish here, nameless, far from Argos” (14.69–70; [[agamemnon]], [[zeus]]). The first verse is Agamemnon's own, from his pretended despair in Book 2 and his proposal of flight in Book 9 (2.116, 9.23); the second is the verse that Polydamas spoke as a wish and Idomeneus as a fear (12.70, 13.227), and Idomeneus had already joined the two thoughts, with Κρονίωνι for Διί (13.226–227). In Agamemnon's mouth they introduce his third proposal to give up the war, this time to launch the ships by night (14.74–81; [[immortal-night]]).
