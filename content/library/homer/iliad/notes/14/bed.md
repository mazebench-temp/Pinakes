summary: The bed is the matter of Hera's false story, the estranged bed of Oceanus and Tethys; of Zeus' memory, the first bed he and Hera shared in secret; and of their exchange on Ida, where Hera asks for the chamber and Zeus makes a bed of the earth's flowers under a golden cloud.

**A bed abandoned.** Hera's pretext is a marriage bed deserted. Oceanus and Tethys “have kept away from each other, / from the bed and from love, since anger has fallen upon their hearts” (εὐνῆς καὶ φιλότητος, 14.206–207 = 14.305–306), and she will bring them back “to their bed to be joined in love” (14.209; see [[philotes]], [[oceanus]] and [[tethys]]).

**The first bed.** When Zeus sees her, desire takes him “as when they first lay together in love, / going to bed together without the knowledge of their dear parents” (εἰς εὐνὴν φοιτῶντε, 14.295–296; see [[eros]] and [[plato-zeus-deceived]]). The marriage of Zeus and Hera began, in this telling, with a secret bed.

**The bed on Ida.** Zeus wants Hera there and then, on the peak. Hera objects that a god might see them, “I would not go back to your house / when I rose from that bed; it would be a thing for blame”, and proposes the chamber that Hephaestus built, “let us go there and lie down, since bed is what pleases you” (ἐπεί νύ τοι εὔαδεν εὐνή, 14.335–340; see [[nemesis]] and [[house]]). Zeus gives her a bed where they are: the earth sends up grass, lotus, crocus and hyacinth, “thick and soft, which held them up off the ground. / On it they lay”, and a golden cloud covers them (14.347–351; see [[the-golden-cloud]], [[lotus]], [[crocus]] and [[hyacinth]]). Book 1 ended with Zeus going up to the bed where he always slept, with Hera beside him (1.609–611; see the note on Book 1); here the bed is the mountain itself, and the sleep that comes on it is Hera's design (see [[hypnos]] and [[the-deception-of-zeus]]).
