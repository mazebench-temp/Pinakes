summary: Poseidon is “the famous shaker of the earth” as he enters the battle and in the invocation that marks his turning of it; Phoenix is “far-famed,” and Sleep goes off “to the famous tribes of men”; the book adds τηλεκλειτοῖο and κλυτὰ φῦλʼ ἀνθρώπων.
grc: [τηλεκλειτοῖο, "κλυτὰ φῦλʼ ἀνθρώπων"]
---

**Poseidon.** “Nor did the famous shaker of the earth keep a blind watch” (14.135) is the verse with which Book 13 began Poseidon's intervention, with κλυτὸς ἐννοσίγαιος in place of κρείων ἐνοσίχθων, “the powerful earth-shaker” (13.10; [[shaker-of-the-earth]], [[earth-shaker]], [[poseidon]]). The same title closes his part in the book, when the poet asks the Muses who was first to win spoils “when the famous shaker of the earth had turned the battle” (14.510; [[invocation-of-the-muses]], [[bloody-spoils]]).

**Phoenix.** Zeus recalls “the daughter of far-famed Phoenix” (14.321; [[phoenix-father-of-europa]], [[the-loves-of-zeus]]). τηλεκλειτός, which the Iliad has so far given only to the Trojans' allies (5.491, 6.111, 9.233, 12.108; [[trojan-allies]]), is here given to a single man. The genitive τηλεκλειτοῖο is new to the entry.

**Men.** When he has given Poseidon his message, Sleep “went away to the famous tribes of men” (14.361; [[sleep]]). The phrase κλυτὰ φῦλʼ ἀνθρώπων is found only here in Homer, and it is new to the entry.
