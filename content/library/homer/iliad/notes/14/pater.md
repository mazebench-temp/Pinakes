---
summary: Nestor goes out with his son's shield while the son has his father's; Diomedes claims a hearing as the son of a brave father and the grandson of Oeneus; Zeus sleeps as “the father”; and Peneleos sends word to Ilioneus' father to wail for his son.
---

**Father and son.** Nestor takes his son Thrasymedes' shield, “and the son had his father's shield” (ὃ δʼ ἔχʼ ἀσπίδα πατρὸς ἑοῖο, 14.11; see [[thrasymedes]] and [[shield]]). Diomedes, the youngest of the kings, claims to be “of the line of a brave father, / Tydeus” (πατρὸς δʼ ἐξ ἀγαθοῦ, 14.113–114), and tells of his father's father, Oeneus, who stood out among his brothers in prowess, and of his father, who settled in Argos after his wanderings, married a daughter of Adrastus and surpassed all the Achaeans with the spear (14.115–125; see [[tydeus]], [[oeneus]], [[genee]] and [[arete]]).

**The father.** On Gargarus Zeus sleeps as “the father” (πατήρ, 14.352), and in the simile of Hector's fall the oak is felled “under the stroke of father Zeus” (14.414; see [[father-zeus]]).

**A father's grief.** Peneleos bids the Trojans tell “noble Ilioneus' dear father” and his mother to wail for him in their halls (14.501–502; see [[ilioneus]] and [[phorbas-father-of-ilioneus]]).
