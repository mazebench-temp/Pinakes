---
summary: Odysseus “of many wiles” answers Agamemnon's proposal of flight, looking at him darkly.
---

“Looking at him darkly, Odysseus of many wiles said: / ‘Son of Atreus, what a word has escaped the barrier of your teeth!’” (14.82–83; [[odysseus]], [[looking-darkly]], [[what-a-word]], [[agamemnon]]). The man of counsel answers a counsel of flight with a rebuke, “Ruinous man” (14.84; [[deadly]]), and with an argument: while war and the war cry are joined, the army will not hold out if the ships are dragged to the sea, but will look away from the fighting (14.96–102). In Book 2, when Agamemnon's test sent the army running to the ships, Odysseus held it back with the king's scepter (2.169–206); here he holds back the king.
