summary: Menelaus stabs Hyperenor in the flank, “and his soul through the stab wound / rushed out in haste”: the only place in Homer that names the wound as the soul's way out.
passages:
  - 14.516-519 | The death of Hyperenor
---

The last death of the book told at length is Hyperenor's. The son of Atreus, Menelaus, stabs him in the flank, “and the bronze let out his bowels / as it tore through; and his soul through the stab wound / rushed out in haste, and darkness covered his eyes” (ψυχὴ δὲ κατʼ οὐταμένην ὠτειλὴν / ἔσσυτʼ ἐπειγομένη, 14.517–519; see [[hyperenor]], [[menelaus]] and [[darkness-covered-his-eyes]]). The ψυχή is a breath that leaves the body (see the article above), and here it takes the shortest way out. Elsewhere it leaves by the mouth, once it has passed “the barrier of his teeth” (9.408–409), or flies from the limbs (16.856 = 22.362); it is drawn out with the spear that Patroclus pulls from Sarpedon's body (16.503–505), and it left Sarpedon once before, when his companion pushed the spear out of his thigh, and came back on the breath of the North Wind (5.694–698; see the note on Book 5). Only here is the wound named as its way out.
