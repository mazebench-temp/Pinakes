---
summary: Hera's tresses fall “from her immortal head,” Aphrodite's love and longing overcome “all the immortals and mortal men,” and the Xanthus is the river “whom immortal Zeus begot.”
---

**Hera's head.** She plaits the shining tresses, “fair and ambrosial, from her immortal head” (14.177; [[ambrosial]], [[dressing-scene]], [[hera]]), with the words that described Zeus' locks as he nodded in Book 1, “from his immortal head” (1.530).

**Love.** Hera asks Aphrodite for “love and longing, with which you / overcome all the immortals and mortal men” (14.198–199; [[aphrodite]], [[philotes]], [[himeros]], [[mortal-men]]). The power that subdues gods as well as men is the power that will subdue Zeus, “overcome by sleep and love” (14.353).

**Zeus.** The Xanthus, to which the Trojans carry Hector, is the river “whom immortal Zeus begot” (14.434; [[scamander]], [[eddying]]).
