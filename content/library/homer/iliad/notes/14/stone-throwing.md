summary: Ajax answers Hector's spear with a stone, one of the props of the ships, and strikes him in the chest over his shield rim; Hector spins like a top and falls like an oak struck by Zeus' thunderbolt. In their duel in Book 7 Ajax's stone laid him on his back, and Apollo set him on his feet.
passages:
  - 14.409-420 | Ajax's stone
---

Hector's spear is stopped by the straps on Ajax's chest, and as he draws back, Ajax picks up a stone: “with a stone—for many of them, the props of the swift ships, / rolled about at the feet of the fighters—he lifted one of them / and hit him in the chest over the shield rim, near the neck, / and with the blow he sent him spinning like a top, and he whirled all around” (χερμαδίῳ, 14.409–413; see [[ajax]], [[hector]], [[props]] and [[like-a-top]]). Hector falls like an oak struck by the thunderbolt, his spear drops from his hand, and his companions carry him from the field (14.414–432; see [[the-oak-struck-by-the-thunderbolt]] and [[shield]]).

It is the second time Ajax's stone has felled Hector. In their duel in Book 7 Ajax lifted “a far greater stone” than Hector's and broke his shield with it, “and he was stretched out on his back, / pressed against his shield; but Apollo at once set him on his feet” (7.268–272; see [[single-combat]] and the note on Book 7). This time no god is near, for Zeus sleeps and Poseidon is on the other side; Hector lies vomiting dark blood at the ford of the Xanthus, and only in the next book does Apollo, sent by Zeus, raise him (14.433–439; 15.239–262).
