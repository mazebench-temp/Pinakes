---
summary: The epithet of praise, rendered “noble,” goes to the three sons of Portheus, to Glaucus, to the Naiad who bore Satnius, and to Polydamas.
---

**Diomedes' grandfathers.** “For to Portheus were born three noble sons, / and they lived in Pleuron and steep Calydon” (14.115–116; [[portheus]], [[agrius]], [[melas]], [[oeneus]], [[steep]]). Diomedes praises his father's line before he gives advice as the youngest of the kings (14.110–127; [[diomedes]], [[genealogy-in-battle]]).

**Glaucus.** Among those who stand over the fallen Hector are “Sarpedon, leader of the Lycians, and noble Glaucus” (14.426; [[glaucus]], [[sarpedon]]), the second half of the verse that named the two Lycians in the Trojan Catalogue (2.876).

**The Naiad.** Satnius is “the son of Enops, whom a noble Naiad nymph bore / to Enops as he tended his cattle by the banks of the Satnioeis” (14.444–445; [[satnius]], [[enops]], [[nymphs]], [[satnioeis]]). The story is the one told of Aesepus and Pedasus in Book 6, whom the Naiad Abarbarea bore to Bucolion while he was tending his sheep (6.21–25; [[bucolion]]). There the epithet went to the herdsman father; here it goes to the nymph mother.

**Polydamas.** “And Ajax in turn called out to noble Polydamas” (14.469; [[polydamas]]), the epithet he has had since Book 11 (11.57, 12.88, 13.790). Ajax's taunt follows: is not Archelochus, killed in his place, a worthy price for Prothoenor? (14.470–474; [[archelochus]], [[prothoenor]]).
