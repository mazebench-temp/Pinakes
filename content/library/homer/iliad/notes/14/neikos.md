---
summary: Hera's false story is that she is going to the ends of the earth to “end their endless quarrels”, the quarrels of Oceanus and Tethys; in Book 1 Zeus complained that she herself was forever quarreling with him.
passages:
  - 14.200-210 | Hera's story to Aphrodite
  - 14.301-311 | Hera's story to Zeus
---

Hera tells Aphrodite, and then Zeus in the same verses, that she is going to see Oceanus and Tethys, who nursed her, “and I will end their endless quarrels” (καί σφʼ ἄκριτα νείκεα λύσω, 14.205 = 14.304; see [[oceanus]], [[tethys]] and [[truth-and-deception]]). ἄκριτος is “undecided, without end”: Helen and Thetis have “griefs past measure” (ἄχεʼ ἄκριτα, 3.412, 24.91). The quarrel is a married one. The old couple have kept away from each other's bed “since anger has fallen upon their hearts” (14.206–207; see [[cholos]] and [[philotes]]). The peacemaker is the goddess of whom Zeus said in Book 1, “Even as it is, among the immortal gods she is forever / quarreling with me, and says that I help the Trojans in battle” (1.520–521; see [[hera]] and [[husbands-and-wives]]). She offers to end a quarrel in order to win one.
