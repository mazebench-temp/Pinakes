---
summary: Zeus asks why Hera has come to Ida with no horses and no chariot, and she claims that they wait at the foot of the mountain; Hector, struck down, is carried to his chariot, which stands behind the battle with its charioteer, and taken toward the city.
except: [14.412]
---

Hera has come without her chariot. “And you have no horses here, and no chariot to mount”, Zeus says, and she answers that her horses stand at the foot of Ida, ready to carry her over land and sea to Oceanus (14.298–299, 14.307–308), though the narrator has shown her coming without them, over the mountains and the sea and from Lemnos clothed in mist (14.225–230, 14.281–285; see [[horses]], [[truth-and-deception]] and [[chariot-journey]]). Hector's chariot waits behind the fighting “with their charioteer and the inlaid chariot” (ἅρματα ποικίλʼ, 14.431), and his companions carry him to it and drive him toward the city, as Polites took his wounded brother Deiphobus out of the battle in the same verses (14.430–432 = 13.536–538; see [[hector]] and [[inlaid]]). At the ford of the Xanthus they lift him down from it (14.435).

The ἄντυξ at 14.412 is the rim of Hector's shield, over which Ajax's stone strikes him, not a chariot rail (see [[shield]]).
