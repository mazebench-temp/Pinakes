---
summary: Sarpedon, “leader of the Lycians”, is one of the best of the Trojans who stand over the fallen Hector when Ajax's stone fells him (14.424–426).
---

Σαρπηδών τʼ ἀρχὸς Λυκίων καὶ Γλαῦκος ἀμύμων, “and Sarpedon, leader of the Lycians, and noble Glaucus” (14.426), recalls the Catalogue's verse on the two Lycian leaders (2.876; see [[glaucus]] and [[lycians]]); ἀρχὸς Λυκίων occurs only here. He has not been seen since the fight at the wall, where he pulled down a battlement and rallied the Lycians (12.397–414). In Book 5, wounded in the thigh by Tlepolemus' spear, he begged Hector not to leave him to lie as a prey for the Danaans, and Hector rushed past him without a word (5.683–691); now he stands over Hector, with Polydamas, Aeneas, Agenor and Glaucus, while the Achaeans try to drag him away (14.421–426; see [[hector]]).
