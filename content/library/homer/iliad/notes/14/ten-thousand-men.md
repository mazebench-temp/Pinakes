summary: Poseidon, in the likeness of an old man, leaves Agamemnon with a shout as loud as nine or ten thousand men in battle, and puts strength into the heart of every Achaean (14.147–152). The verses of Ares' bellow are used of a god who rouses the army.

---

{{quote:14232119-42df-5ae2-ae6b-6a010d2876f9}}

ὣς εἰπὼν μέγʼ ἄϋσεν ἐπεσσύμενος πεδίοιο. / ὅσσόν τʼ ἐννεάχιλοι ἐπίαχον ἢ δεκάχιλοι / ἀνέρες ἐν πολέμῳ ἔριδα ξυνάγοντες Ἄρηος, / τόσσην ἐκ στήθεσφιν ὄπα κρείων ἐνοσίχθων / ἧκεν: “So saying, he gave a great shout as he sped over the plain. As loud as nine thousand or ten thousand men shout in war as they join in the strife of Ares, so loud was the voice that the powerful earth-shaker sent out of his breast” (14.147–151). The middle verses are those of Ares' bellow in Book 5 (14.148–149 = 5.860–861). The frame is new. There the god ἔβραχε, “bellowed”, wounded in the flank; here he ἄϋσεν, gives the war cry, and τόσσην … ὄπα, “so loud the voice”, closes the comparison with the god's voice and title (see [[earth-shaker]] and [[powerful]]).

The effects are opposite. Ares' bellow made both armies tremble (5.862–863). Poseidon's voice works on one side only: “he put great strength into each of the Achaeans, in his heart, to go to war without ceasing and to fight” (14.151–152). These two verses close another divine shout, the one Strife sends up from Odysseus' ship at the dawn of this same day of battle (14.151–152 = 11.11–12; see [[strife]] and [[the-third-day-of-battle]]), and Athena rouses the same strength with the aegis before the Catalogue (2.450–452).

Poseidon has come, keeping no blind watch, in the likeness of an old man, and taken Agamemnon by the right hand. He tells him that Achilles' heart rejoices at the slaughter of the Achaeans, “may a god maim him”, and that the blessed gods do not yet bear Agamemnon a grudge altogether: he will see the leaders of the Trojans fill the plain with dust as they flee back to the city (14.135–146; see [[poseidon]], [[agamemnon]] and [[poseidon-rallies-the-achaeans]]). The shape is an old man's, and the voice is an army's. Later in the book, when Poseidon leads the Achaeans openly, it is the two armies whose shout is measured, by the sea, the fire and the wind (14.394–401; see [[louder-than-sea-fire-and-wind]]).
