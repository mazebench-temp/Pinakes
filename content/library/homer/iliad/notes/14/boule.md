---
summary: Hera's plan to beguile Zeus is introduced in the verse that introduced Zeus' plan to send the Dream, “And this plan seemed to her in her heart the best”. Odysseus tells Agamemnon that his counsel would ruin the army, and the gods plan the destruction of Archelochus.
grc: [βούλευσαν]
passages:
  - 14.159-165 | Hera's plan
---

**The best plan.** “And this plan seemed to her in her heart the best: / to go to Ida, when she had adorned herself well” (ἥδε δέ οἱ κατὰ θυμὸν ἀρίστη φαίνετο βουλή, 14.161–162; see [[hera]] and [[the-deception-of-zeus]]). The verse is used three times in the Iliad, each time for a plan made alone at night or in secret: Zeus' plan at the start of Book 2, “to send a baneful Dream to Agamemnon” (2.5–6; see [[agamemnons-dream]]), Agamemnon's in Book 10, to go first to Nestor (10.17–18), and Hera's here. Zeus' best plan was to deceive a king with a dream; Hera's is to deceive Zeus with love and sleep (see [[apate]] and [[plan-of-zeus]]). In the Odyssey Odysseus uses it three times of himself (*Odyssey* 9.318, 9.424, 11.230).

**Counsel that harms.** Odysseus ends his rebuke of Agamemnon's proposal to launch the ships: “Then your counsel would do us harm, leader of the people” (ἔνθά κε σὴ βουλὴ δηλήσεται, 14.102; see [[odysseus]]). Counsel is what a king owes his people, and a man who bore a scepter, Odysseus says, would never have let such a word pass his lips (14.90–94; see the article above and [[scepter]]); this counsel would destroy them. Agamemnon gives way and asks for “a better plan” from anyone, young or old (14.107–108; see [[metis]]).

**The gods' plan.** Ajax's spear, thrown at Polydamas, takes Archelochus instead, “for the gods had planned his destruction” (τῷ γάρ ῥα θεοὶ βούλευσαν ὄλεθρον, 14.464; see [[archelochus]]). The narrator gives no god a name; a missile that misses one man and finds another is the gods' doing.
