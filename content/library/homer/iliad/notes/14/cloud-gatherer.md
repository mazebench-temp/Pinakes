---
summary: Zeus is “the cloud-gatherer” three times on Gargarus, as he sees Hera and twice as he answers her, and then he gathers a golden cloud around them both.
---

“And Hera quickly went up to the peak of Gargarus / on lofty Ida; and Zeus the cloud-gatherer saw her” (14.292–293; [[gargarus]], [[ida]], [[hera]]). He answers her twice in the line τὴν δʼ ἀπαμειβόμενος προσέφη νεφεληγερέτα Ζεύς, “Answering her, Zeus the cloud-gatherer said” (14.312, 14.341; [[answering-him]]). The second answer makes the epithet an act: “such a cloud will I wrap around you, / golden; not even the Sun could see through it to us” (14.343–344; [[helios]]), and they draw over them “a cloud, / fine and golden; and glistening drops of dew fell from it” (14.350–351; [[the-golden-cloud]]). The god who gathers the storm clouds gathers a cloud for love, and under it he is overcome by sleep (14.352–353; [[zeus]], [[the-deception-of-zeus]]).
