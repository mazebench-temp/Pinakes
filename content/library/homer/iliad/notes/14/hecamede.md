summary: Hecamede's last appearance: Nestor tells Machaon to go on drinking “until Hecamede of the lovely tresses heats a warm bath / for you and washes away the clotted blood” (14.6–7).
---

**The bath.** When the cry of the battle at the ships reaches Nestor, still drinking with Machaon in his hut, he goes out to look and leaves the wounded man to her care:

{{quote:d8d2be35-9eea-50df-9d8f-10afaf7d4bef}}

She is ἐϋπλόκαμος, “of the lovely tresses”, as when she made the mixed drink in Book 11 (11.624; see [[with-lovely-tresses]] and [[kykeon]]). The drink was for “their parching thirst” (11.642); nothing was done then for the arrow wound in Machaon's shoulder. Now she is to heat the water and wash him (see [[machaon]], [[nestor]] and [[bathing]]).

θερμὰ λοετρά, “a warm bath”, occurs once more in the Iliad. Andromache, not yet knowing that Hector is dead, calls to her handmaids “of the lovely tresses” to set a great tripod on the fire, so that there may be a warm bath for Hector when he comes back from the battle (22.442–446). ἄπο βρότον αἱματόεντα, “washes away the clotted blood”, is said elsewhere of washing the dead: the bodies gathered from the plain in Book 7, which “they washed the clotted blood from” before the burning (7.425), and Patroclus (18.345). Achilles will refuse to wash it from himself until Patroclus is burned (23.41). Here it is a living man who is to be washed. The poem does not say that the bath was given, and Hecamede is not named again.
