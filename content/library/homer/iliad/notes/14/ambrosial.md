summary: Hera's adornment is ambrosial three times over, in the olive oil she anoints herself with, the tresses she plaits, and the robe that Athena made; the book adds ἀμβροσίῳ and ἀμβροσίους.
grc: [ἀμβροσίῳ, ἀμβροσίους]
---

The adjective of the gods' food passes, in the adornment, to the things of Hera's beauty ([[dressing-scene]], [[hera]]). She cleanses her body “with ambrosia” (ἀμβροσίῃ, 14.170; [[ambrosia]]) and anoints herself with olive oil, “ambrosial and sweet, which had been perfumed for her” (14.172); she combs her hair and plaits the shining tresses, “fair and ambrosial, from her immortal head” (14.176–177; [[immortals]], [[phaeinos]]); and she puts on “an ambrosial robe, which Athena / had smoothed and worked for her” (14.178–179; [[robe]], [[athena]]). In Book 1 the ambrosial locks were Zeus' own, streaming down as he nodded (1.528–530; [[ambrosial-locks]]); here the goddess makes herself ambrosial to bring him to sleep. The forms ἀμβροσίῳ and ἀμβροσίους are new to the entry. The oil returns in Book 23, when Aphrodite anoints the body of Hector with ambrosial rose oil so that Achilles will not tear it as he drags it (23.185–187).
