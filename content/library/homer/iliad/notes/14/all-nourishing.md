---
summary: Hera tells Aphrodite and then Zeus, in the same words, that she is going “to see the limits of the all-nourishing earth”; the book adds the genitive πολυφόρβου.
grc: [πολυφόρβου]
---

Hera's lie is told twice. To Aphrodite, as the reason she needs love and longing: “For I am going to see the limits of the all-nourishing earth, / and Oceanus, the origin of the gods, and mother Tethys” (14.200–201; [[aphrodite]], [[oceanus]], [[tethys]]). To Zeus on Gargarus, as the reason she has come without horses or chariot: “I am going to see the limits of the all-nourishing earth” (14.301), with the rest of the speech repeated nearly word for word (14.301–306; [[hera]], [[the-deception-of-zeus]]). The earth that feeds all is the earth whose limits Oceanus encircles; Strabo quotes 14.200–201 to show that Homer makes Oceanus lie around the earth at its limits (*Geography* 1.1.7). The genitive πολυφόρβου, from the form πολύφορβος, is new to the entry; Book 9 had the feminine πολυφόρβην (9.568). See [[earth]].
