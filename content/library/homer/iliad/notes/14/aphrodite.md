summary: Hera, adorned, calls Aphrodite apart and asks her, with a false story, for the love and longing with which she overcomes gods and men. Aphrodite cannot refuse the wife of Zeus and gives her the embroidered strap that holds all her enchantments, not knowing that it will serve the Achaeans.

**The request.** When Hera is adorned, she calls Aphrodite “apart from the other gods” and asks whether she will listen to her or refuse, “holding a grudge in your heart / because I help the Danaans and you the Trojans?” (14.188–192; see [[hera]]). She calls her φίλον τέκος, “dear child”: Aphrodite is the daughter of Zeus and Dione (5.370–371), and Hera speaks to her as a stepmother. Aphrodite answers with Hera's titles, “Hera, august goddess, daughter of great Cronus” (14.194; see [[august-goddess]]), and with the promise that Hephaestus will make to Thetis and Calypso to Hermes: “say what is in your mind; my heart bids me accomplish it, / if I can accomplish it and if it is a thing that can be accomplished” (14.195–196 = 18.426–427; *Odyssey* 5.89–90).

**The lie.** Hera, “with treachery in her heart”, asks: “Give me now love and longing, with which you / overcome all the immortals and mortal men” (14.197–199; see [[philotes]] and [[himeros]]). She tells her that she is going to the ends of the earth to end the quarrel of Oceanus and Tethys and bring them back to their bed (14.200–210; see [[oceanus]] and [[tethys]]). The goddess of love is asked to mend a marriage; the love she lends will be spent on Zeus, and against her own Trojans. In Book 5 Zeus sent her from the battle to attend to “the lovely works of marriage” (5.428–430; see [[diomedes-wounds-aphrodite]]).

**The strap.** “Then laughter-loving Aphrodite spoke to her in turn” (14.211; see [[laughter-loving]]). φιλομειδής is printed here with one μ, as at 3.424 and 4.10, though the meter wants φιλομμειδής (see the translation's note on 14.211). It is not possible to refuse, “for you sleep in the arms of Zeus, the greatest of all” (14.212–213).

{{quote:79f9d68f-1117-5703-a2d7-630469c5f2c6}}

κεστὸς ἱμάς, “the embroidered strap”, is found only here, and the poem does not say how Aphrodite wears it, only that she loosens it from her breast and that Hera hides it in her bosom (14.214, 14.219–223; see [[aphrodites-band]]). What it holds are the powers of Aphrodite herself, love, longing, intimate talk and persuasion (see [[oaristys]]). “I do not think / you will come back with nothing done, whatever you have in your mind”, she says (14.220–221), and Hera smiles. Then “Aphrodite, daughter of Zeus, went to her house” (14.224; see [[daughter-of-zeus]]). She has no other part in the book. The strap does its work on Gargarus, where desire wraps the mind of Zeus (14.294; see [[the-deception-of-zeus]]).
