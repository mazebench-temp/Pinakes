en: [close doors]

“She went to her chamber, which her dear son built for her, / Hephaestus, and he fitted close doors to the doorposts / with a secret bolt, which no other god could open” (14.166–168; [[hephaestus]], [[hera]], [[house]]). The words are πυκινὰς δὲ θύρας, “close doors,” not the formula πυκινῶς ἀραρυῖαι, but the doors are of the same kind: they hold fast unless someone breaks them or has the key. Only Hera can open these, and she shuts “the shining doors” behind her to make ready (14.169; [[phaeinos]], [[dressing-scene]]). When Zeus proposes to lie with her on the open peak of Ida, she offers him the same room in the same verse: “you have a chamber, which your dear son built for you, / Hephaestus, and he fitted close doors to the doorposts” (14.338–339). The chamber that hid her preparations is offered as a place where no one could see them, and Zeus prefers a cloud (14.342–345; [[the-golden-cloud]]). The phrase πυκινὰς δὲ θύρας is new to the entry.
