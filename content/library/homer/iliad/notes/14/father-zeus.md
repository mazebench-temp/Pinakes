---
summary: Zeus sleeps on Gargarus as “the father,” and in the simile of Hector's fall an oak is felled “under the stroke of father Zeus.”
refs: [14.352]
---

“So the father slept motionless on the peak of Gargarus, / overcome by sleep and love, and held his wife in his arms” (14.352–353; [[gargarus]], [[hypnos]], [[philotes]]). πατήρ alone names Zeus here. Sixty lines later the narrator compares Hector's fall under Ajax's stone to the work of the same god: “As when under the stroke of father Zeus an oak falls / uprooted, and a dread smell of sulfur rises” (14.414–415; [[the-oak-struck-by-the-thunderbolt]], [[thunderbolt]], [[hector]]). The father's thunderbolt strikes in the simile while the father himself lies asleep, and the oak it fells stands for Hector, the man whom Zeus has been helping.
