---
summary: Poseidon tells Agamemnon that “the deadly heart of Achilles” rejoices at the Achaeans' rout; Hera claims that she means to win over “the dear hearts” of Oceanus and Tethys with words.
en: [hearts]
---

“Son of Atreus, now, I think, the deadly heart of Achilles / rejoices in his breast at the slaughter and the panic of the Achaeans / as he looks on, since there is no sense in him, not even a little” (Ἀχιλλῆος ὀλοὸν κῆρ, 14.139–141; see [[achilles]], [[deadly]] and [[phrenes]]). The god in the likeness of an old man speaks of Achilles to comfort the king (14.142–146; see [[poseidon]]).

Hera, asking Aphrodite for love and longing, gives a false reason: “If with words I could win over the dear hearts of those two / and bring them back to their bed to be joined in love, / I would always be called dear and respected by them” (φίλον κῆρ, 14.208–210; see [[peitho]], [[philos]] and [[truth-and-deception]]).
