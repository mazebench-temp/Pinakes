---
summary: The killings after Hector's fall are a chain of reprisals, each death claimed as payment for the last. Ajax asks whether Archelochus, “killed in return for Prothoenor”, is not worth him, and Acamas kills Promachus “so that the penalty for my brother / may not long go unpaid”.
passages:
  - 14.449-507 | Killings and boasts
---

**The chain.** When Hector has been carried from the field and the son of Oileus has killed Satnius, the narrator tells four killings in a row, each followed by a boast (14.449–507; see [[euchomai]] and [[androktasia]]). Polydamas spears Prothoenor and boasts that the dead man will use the spear as a staff on his way to the house of Hades (14.449–457). Ajax throws at Polydamas, kills Archelochus instead, and asks, “is not this man, killed in return for Prothoenor, / worthy of him?” (ἀντὶ πεφάσθαι / ἄξιος, 14.471–472; see [[ajax]] and [[archelochus]]). Acamas, Archelochus' brother, kills Promachus as he drags the body, and gives the reason: “so that the penalty for my brother / may not long go unpaid” (ἵνα μή τι κασιγνήτοιό γε ποινὴ / δηρὸν ἄτιτος ἔῃ, 14.483–484; see [[acamas]], [[promachus]] and [[kasignetos]]). Peneleos kills Ilioneus and bids the Trojans tell his parents to wail for him, since the wife of Promachus will not be glad at her husband's homecoming either (14.501–505; see [[peneleos]] and [[ilioneus]]). The penalty is not taken from the killer: Ajax killed Archelochus, and Acamas takes it from Promachus, who was dragging the body. Each speaker counts a death against a death: ἀντί, “in return for”, ἄξιος, “of equal worth”, ποινή, “penalty”, and ἄτιτος, “unpaid”, the word Deiphobus used of Asius in the last book (13.414; see the note on Book 13).

**The rule.** Acamas turns his own case into a maxim: “and that is why a man prays / to leave a kinsman in his halls, to defend him against disaster” (14.484–485; see [[a-kinsman-in-his-halls]]). In peace the kin of a dead man may accept a blood-price from the killer, and the killer stays among the people (9.632–636); in war the kinsman pays the debt in kind, at once and on the field.
