{{quote:40e53ba8-9619-50cb-8cac-2819ca9d195c}}

ἀντίθεον Ῥαδάμανθυν, “godlike Rhadamanthys” (14.322; see [[antitheos]]). The Iliad says nothing more of him, and this is the only place in Homer that names his parents (see [[europa]] and [[the-loves-of-zeus]]). The verse is shaped like the one that tells the birth of Sarpedon, another son of Zeus whom later writers made his brother: ἣ δʼ ἔτεκʼ ἀντίθεον Σαρπηδόνα χαλκοκορυστήν, “and she bore godlike Sarpedon, armed in bronze” (6.199; see [[sarpedon]]).
