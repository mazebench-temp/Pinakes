---
summary: Troy is “the wide-streeted city of the Trojans” that Agamemnon would leave behind, the city to which Poseidon foretells the Trojans' flight and to which Hector is carried; Heracles sacked it once. On Hera's way to Ida, Lemnos is “the city of divine Thoas”.
---

**Troy.** Odysseus: “Are you so eager, then, to leave the wide-streeted city of the Trojans / behind, for whose sake we have suffered so many evils?” (Τρώων πόλιν εὐρυάγυιαν, 14.88–89; see [[troy]] and [[wide-streeted]]). Poseidon foretells the Trojans “fleeing back to the city, away from the ships and huts” (προτὶ ἄστυ, 14.146; see [[hut]]), and Hector, struck down, is carried “toward the city, groaning heavily” (14.432; see [[groaning-heavily]]). Sleep recalls the day when Heracles “was sailing from Ilios, when he had sacked the city of the Trojans” (Τρώων πόλιν ἐξαλαπάξας, 14.251; see [[heracles-sacks-troy]]).

**Lemnos.** Hera comes to “Lemnos, the city of divine Thoas” (Λῆμνον … πόλιν θείοιο Θόαντος, 14.230; see [[lemnos]] and [[thoas-of-lemnos]]), and she and Sleep set off “leaving the city of Lemnos and Imbros” (Λήμνου τε καὶ Ἴμβρου ἄστυ, 14.281; see [[imbros]]).
