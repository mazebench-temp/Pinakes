---
summary: Sleep could lull “any other of the everlasting gods,” but not Zeus, and Hera pretends to fear that one “of the everlasting gods” might see her lying with Zeus on Ida.
---

**Sleep.** “Any other of the everlasting gods / I would easily lull to sleep, even the streams of the river / Oceanus, who is the origin of all; / but I would not come near Zeus, the son of Cronus” (14.244–247; [[sleep]], [[oceanus]], [[son-of-cronus]]).

**Hera.** “How would it be if one of the everlasting gods / saw the two of us sleeping, and went among all the gods / and told them?” (14.333–335; [[hera]], [[nemesis]]). She offers instead the chamber that Hephaestus built, and Zeus answers with a cloud that no god or man could see through (14.337–345; [[closely-fitted]], [[the-golden-cloud]]).

The gods as a body stand outside the deception: Sleep could put any of them to sleep, and Hera pretends to fear their eyes. See [[the-deception-of-zeus]].
