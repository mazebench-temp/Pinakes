passages:
  - 14.82-102 | Odysseus' rebuke

**The lord of men.** Agamemnon speaks and leads in his formula, ἄναξ ἀνδρῶν Ἀγαμέμνων, “Agamemnon, lord of men” (14.64, 14.103, 14.134; see [[agamemnon]] and [[lord-of-men]]). Odysseus turns the title against him when he proposes flight: “I wish you were giving orders to some other, unworthy army, / and were not lord over us” (μὴ δʼ ἄμμιν ἀνασσέμεν, 14.84–85), and a man who bore a scepter would not speak so to “as many as those you are lord over among the Argives” (14.93–94; see [[odysseus]] and [[scepter]]). The lordship is not questioned; its holder is told he is unworthy of the men he rules. Agamemnon yields, and leads them back to the battle as lord (14.134).

**The lord of all.** Hera begins her request on Lemnos with a title: “Sleep, lord of all gods and of all men” (Ὕπνε ἄναξ πάντων τε θεῶν πάντων τʼ ἀνθρώπων, 14.233; see [[sleep]]). The words are found only here in Homer, and in the Iliad the lordship over gods and men is Zeus', “who rules over gods and men” (ὅς τε θεοῖσι καὶ ἀνθρώποισιν ἀνάσσει, 2.669; see [[zeus]]). Hera flatters Sleep with a power over everyone, and asks him to use it on the one who rules. Sleep's answer marks its limit: he would lull any other god, but not Zeus, unless Zeus himself bade him (14.244–248).
