---
summary: Nestor arms with his son's shield and “a strong spear, tipped with sharp bronze,” only to go and look at the battle.
---

“So saying, he took the wrought shield of his son / that lay in the hut of Thrasymedes, tamer of horses, / shining with bronze; and the son had his father's shield. / And he took a strong spear, tipped with sharp bronze” (14.9–12; [[nestor]], [[thrasymedes]], [[tamer-of-horses]], [[sharp]]). The verse εἵλετο δʼ ἄλκιμον ἔγχος ends the arming of a hero, as it ended Paris' before the duel (3.338; [[arming-scene]]); here an old man arms to step outside his hut and look (14.13–15). Father and son have each other's shields, as later in the book the army exchanges its armor at Poseidon's advice (14.370–382; [[warlike]]).
