---
summary: Bronze in Book 14 is the soldier's metal. Nestor arms with a spear “tipped with sharp bronze”, the unyielding bronze rings about the fighters, the kings have all been hit by it, the army puts on “the gleaming bronze”, Hector's armor rings as he falls, and the bronze lets out Hyperenor's bowels. The gods have only the bronze-floored house of Zeus.
---

**Arming.** Nestor, leaving his wine, takes his son's shield “shining with bronze” and a spear in the verse with which he armed to go out by night in Book 10: “And he took a strong spear, tipped with sharp bronze” (14.11–12 = 10.135; see [[strong-spear]] and [[nestor]]). When the kings have exchanged the armor, “they had put the gleaming bronze about their bodies” (ἕσσαντο περὶ χροῒ νώροπα χαλκόν, 14.383; see [[gleaming-bronze]] and [[exchange-of-armor]]); the Odyssey uses the verse twice for the arming of its last battle, on Ithaca (*Odyssey* 24.467, 24.500).

**Ringing.** While Nestor ponders, “the unyielding bronze rang about their bodies” (χαλκὸς ἀτειρής, 14.25; see [[unyielding]]). When Hector falls under Ajax's stone, his shield and helmet fall upon him, “and around him his armor rang, inlaid with bronze” (14.420), the words of the falls of Alcmaon and Imbrius (12.396, 13.181; see [[fell-with-a-thud]] and [[inlaid]]).

**The blade.** The wounded kings are “all who had been hit by the bronze” (βεβλήατο χαλκῷ, 14.28), by spear or arrow in Book 11 (see [[the-wounded-chiefs]]). Menelaus stabs Hyperenor in the flank, “and the bronze let out his bowels / as it tore through” (14.517–518; see [[hyperenor]]), as it let out Oenomaus' in Book 13 and will Phorcys' (13.507–508, 17.314–315).

In the scenes among the gods bronze appears only in a compound: Hera's perfumed oil, stirred “in the bronze-floored house of Zeus”, fills earth and heaven with its fragrance (κατὰ χαλκοβατὲς δῶ, 14.173–174; see [[bronze-floored]]).
