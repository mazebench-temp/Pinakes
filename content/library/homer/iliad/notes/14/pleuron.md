---
summary: The sons of Portheus, Oeneus among them, “lived in Pleuron and steep Calydon” (14.116).
---

οἴκεον δʼ ἐν Πλευρῶνι καὶ αἰπεινῇ Καλυδῶνι, “and they lived in Pleuron and steep Calydon” (14.116): in Diomedes' account of his family the two Aetolian towns are the home of the three sons of Portheus, his grandfather Oeneus among them (see [[portheus]], [[oeneus]], [[calydon]] and [[diomedes]]). The second half of the verse named the same pair in Book 13, when Poseidon took the voice of Thoas, “who in all Pleuron and steep Calydon / was lord of the Aetolians” (13.217–218; see [[thoas]] and [[aetolians]]). The family of Oeneus once lived where Thoas rules now. Strabo quotes the verse, with the one before it, to argue that the Curetes who fought at Pleuron were Aetolians (*Geography* 10.3.1; see [[curetes]] and [[strabo]]).
