---
summary: In her false story to Aphrodite, Hera says that Oceanus and Tethys took her from Rhea and reared her in their halls, when Zeus put Cronus beneath the earth and the barren sea (14.200–204).
---

Hera asks Aphrodite for love and longing on the pretext of a journey to “Oceanus, the origin of the gods, and mother Tethys, / who nursed me well in their halls and cherished me, / taking me from Rhea, when far-seeing Zeus put Cronus / down beneath the earth and the barren sea” (14.201–204; see [[hera]], [[oceanus]], [[tethys]] and [[fall-of-cronus]]). δεξάμενοι Ῥείας, “taking me from Rhea”: the two received the child from her mother's hands. It is the first time the Iliad names Rhea, and the only time Homer links her with Hera; in the next book Poseidon names her as the mother of the three sons of Cronus (15.187). When Hera repeats the story to Zeus on Gargarus, she leaves out Rhea and Cronus (14.301–303; see [[zeus]]).
