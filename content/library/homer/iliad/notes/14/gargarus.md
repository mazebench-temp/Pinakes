**Zeus' peak.** Leaving Sleep in the fir, “Hera quickly went up to the peak of Gargarus / on lofty Ida” (Γάργαρον ἄκρον, 14.292–293; see [[hera]] and [[ida]]). It is the summit where Zeus has his precinct and altar and from which he watched the battle in Book 8 (8.47–52). Hera and Sleep reach Ida in the verse that brought Zeus there, “Ida of the many springs, mother of wild beasts”, but where Zeus came to Gargarus they come to Lectum, the headland where the range meets the sea (14.283–284; 8.47–48; see [[lectum]]).

**Open to view.** Hera objects to lying with Zeus “on the peaks of Ida, where everything is in plain view” (14.332), and Zeus answers with a golden cloud that not even the Sun can see through; the earth sends up grass and flowers beneath them (14.342–351; see [[the-golden-cloud]] and [[earth]]). The god's holy summit becomes a bed (see [[the-deception-of-zeus]]). When Apollo and Iris come to Ida in the next book, they find the son of Cronus sitting “on the peak of Gargarus”, ἀνὰ Γαργάρῳ ἄκρῳ, the words of 14.352, “and a fragrant cloud was wreathed around him” (15.151–153).
