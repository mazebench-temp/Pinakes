---
summary: Thrasymedes, Nestor's son, is “tamer of horses” as his father takes his shield, and Ajax calls Archelochus a brother or son “of Antenor, tamer of horses.”
---

**Thrasymedes.** “So saying, he took the wrought shield of his son / that lay in the hut of Thrasymedes, tamer of horses” (14.9–10; [[thrasymedes]], [[nestor]], [[strong-spear]]). It is the first time the epithet is his.

**Antenor.** Ajax, over the dead Archelochus: “He does not look to me like a coward, nor born of cowards, / but a brother of Antenor, tamer of horses, / or a son; for in his lineage he looked most like him” (14.472–474; [[archelochus]], [[antenor]], [[ajax]]). Antenor has had the epithet since Book 6, in the verse of his wife Theano (6.299). In Book 17 Menelaus gives it to Hyperenor, whom he kills in this book (17.24; [[hyperenor]], [[shepherd-of-the-people]]).
