Ajax throws at Polydamas, who has just boasted over Prothoenor, and Polydamas darts aside (see [[polydamas]] and [[ajax]]). The spear strikes Archelochus where the head joins the neck, at the last vertebra, and his head and mouth and nostrils reach the ground before his shins and knees (14.465–468). συνεοχμός, the “joining” of head and neck, occurs only here, and no other fall in Homer is told in these words.

Ajax does not name him. He knows him by his look: “He does not look to me like a coward, nor born of cowards, / but a brother of Antenor, tamer of horses, / or a son; for in his lineage he looked most like him” (14.472–474; see [[antenor]] and [[genee]]), and the narrator adds that he spoke “knowing well” (14.475). The dead man is offered to Polydamas as a fair exchange for Prothoenor (14.470–472).

In the Catalogue and at the wall Archelochus was named with his brother Acamas, the two of them “well skilled in every kind of fighting” (2.823, 12.100; see [[skilled-in-fighting]]). Acamas stands over his body and stabs the Boeotian Promachus as he drags it away by the feet, and boasts that the penalty for his brother has not long gone unpaid (14.476–485; see [[acamas]], [[promachus]] and [[poine]]).
