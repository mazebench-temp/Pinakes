After the five mortal women come the goddesses, and Demeter is the first (see [[the-loves-of-zeus]]). Zeus names no child of hers; in Hesiod she bore him Persephone (*Theogony* 912–914; see [[persephone]]). The only other love of hers in Homer is a mortal: in the Odyssey Calypso recalls how “fair-tressed” Demeter (ἐυπλόκαμος) yielded to Iasion in a thrice-plowed fallow field, and Zeus, learning of it, killed him with his thunderbolt (*Odyssey* 5.125–128).

Her two titles in this verse are rare. καλλιπλόκαμος, “of the lovely tresses”, is given in Homer to Demeter, Thetis, Ariadne and Circe (see [[of-the-lovely-tresses]]). ἄνασσα, “queen”, the feminine of ἄναξ, “lord”, occurs only here in the Iliad; in the Odyssey Nestor prays to Athena with it, and Odysseus greets Nausicaa so, not knowing whether she is a goddess or a mortal (*Odyssey* 3.380, 6.149, 6.175; see [[anax]]).
