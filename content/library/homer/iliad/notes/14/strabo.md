---
summary: Strabo uses Book 14 as a geographer. Hera's “limits of the all-nourishing earth” show that Homer made Oceanus encircle the earth; her flight from Olympus by Pieria, Emathia, the Thracian mountains and Athos shows that he keeps places in their order; Lectum and the peak of Gargarus are the ends and the summit of Ida; and the sons of Portheus and Satnius, son of Oenops, enter his arguments about the Curetes and the Leleges.
---

- **Oceanus at the limits.** Homer shows in other ways too, Strabo says, that Oceanus lies in a circle round the earth, as when Hera says, “For I am going to see the limits of the all-nourishing earth, / and Oceanus, the origin of the gods” (14.200–201). He speaks of Oceanus as bordering on all the limits, and the limits lie round in a circle; and on the shield of Achilles he sets Oceanus round the rim (18.607–608; *Geography* 1.1.7; see [[oceanus]] and [[all-nourishing]]). The same verse served Plato and Aristotle for other doctrines (see [[plato-oceanus-and-tethys]] and [[aristotle-oceanus-and-tethys]]).
- **Hera's route.** Homer must not be lumped together with the other poets in geography, Strabo argues, for where order is needed he keeps the order of the places he names, Greek and foreign alike. His examples are Otus and Ephialtes, who would have piled Ossa on Olympus and Pelion on Ossa (*Odyssey* 11.315–316), and Hera's flight from the peak of Olympus by Pieria and lovely Emathia, over the snowy mountains of the Thracians, and from Athos out over the sea, quoted from 14.225–229. Sophocles' *Triptolemus* and the prologue of Euripides' *Bacchae*, by contrast, join places far apart and tear apart those that adjoin (*Geography* 1.2.20). Elsewhere Strabo says that what is now Macedonia was once called Emathia, and that the Pierians, a Thracian people, held Pieria and the country round Olympus (*Geography* 7, fr. 11; see also 10.3.17). See [[pieria]], [[emathia]], [[thracians]], [[athos]] and [[lemnos]].
- **The sons of Portheus.** Some, Strabo reports, take the Curetes whom Homer names for Aetolians rather than Acarnanians, since the sons of Porthaon were “Agrius and Melas, and the third was the horseman Oeneus”, and they lived “in Pleuron and steep Calydon”, both Aetolian cities of the Catalogue. He quotes 14.117 before 14.116, with ᾤκεον for οἴκεον, and calls the brothers Πορθαονίδαι, “the sons of Porthaon” (*Geography* 10.3.1). Later he places them again: when Aetolia was divided, Oeneus held the country round Calydon, and the sons of Porthaon round Agrius held part of Pleuronia, “if indeed they lived in Pleuron and steep Calydon” (*Geography* 10.3.6). See [[portheus]], [[agrius]], [[melas]], [[oeneus]], [[pleuron]], [[calydon]] and [[curetes]].
- **Lectum and Gargarus.** Ida, Strabo says, ends in many foothills, the farthest of them the headland by Zeleia and the one called Lectum, which runs out into the Aegean on the coasting voyage from Tenedos to Lesbos. “They came to Ida of the many springs, mother of wild beasts, / to Lectum, where they first left the sea”, he quotes (14.283–284, with ἵκανον for ἱκέσθην), and adds the subject, “Sleep and Hera”: the poet describes Lectum fittingly, for he rightly says that it belongs to Ida and that it is the first landing from the sea for those who go up to Ida. Having marked the farthest foothills on either side, Lectum and Zeleia, he marks the ridge between them, calling Gargarus the summit (Γάργαρον ἄκρον, 14.292), and in Strabo's day a place called Gargarum was still shown in the upper parts of Ida, from which the Aeolian city of Gargara takes its name (*Geography* 13.1.5). See [[lectum]], [[gargarus]] and [[ida]].
- **Satnius, son of Oenops.** Strabo quotes the death of Satnius twice. Where our text has Ἠνοπίδην … Ἤνοπι, the son of Enops (14.443–445; see the translation's note), he reads Οἰνοπίδην … Οἴνοπι, the son of Oenops. Arguing that Pedasus stood on the river Satnioeis, not under a mountain of that name, he cites the verses as proof that the poet names the river, “a great torrent”, and made it worthy of memory by naming it (*Geography* 13.1.50). And he cites them again to show that the Leleges, though left out of the Trojan Catalogue, take part in the fighting (13.3.1). See [[satnius]], [[enops]], [[satnioeis]] and [[leleges]].
