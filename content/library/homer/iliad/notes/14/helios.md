---
summary: The Sun is the measure of Hera's veil, “white like the sun”, and the one watcher Zeus names when he promises that his golden cloud will hide them: “not even the Sun could see through it to us”.
passages:
  - 14.342-345 | Zeus promises the golden cloud
---

**White like the sun.** Hera's veil, new-made, “was white like the sun” (λευκὸν δʼ ἦν ἠέλιος ὥς, 14.185; see [[veil]] and [[the-adornment-of-a-goddess]]). In a like verse the tunic that the disguised Odysseus describes to Penelope was “bright like the sun” (λαμπρὸς δʼ ἦν ἠέλιος ὥς, *Odyssey* 19.234).

**The watcher.** To Hera's fear that some god might see them on the open peak, Zeus answers that he will wrap them in a golden cloud: “not even the Sun could see through it to us, / whose light is the sharpest of all for seeing” (14.344–345; see [[zeus]] and [[the-golden-cloud]]). The Sun is called on in oaths because nothing escapes him: “and you, Sun, who see all things and hear all things” (3.277; see [[who-sees-all-things]]), and in the Odyssey it is the Sun who sees Ares and Aphrodite lying together in love and tells Hephaestus, “for the Sun kept watch for him” (*Odyssey* 8.270–271, 8.302). Zeus shuts out the one eye that might have told.
