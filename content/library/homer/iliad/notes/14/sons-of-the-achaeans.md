---
summary: Agamemnon says he is not forcing “the sons of the Achaeans” to drag the ships down, the sons of the Achaeans run up to the fallen Hector, and Peneleos speaks of “we young men of the Achaeans.”
---

Agamemnon, yielding to Odysseus: “but I am not ordering them against their will, / the sons of the Achaeans, to drag the well-benched ships to the sea” (14.105–106; [[agamemnon]], [[well-benched]]). When Hector falls under Ajax's stone, “with a great cry the sons of the Achaeans ran up, / hoping to drag him away” (14.421–422; [[hector]]), but the best of the Trojans stand over him. And Peneleos, over Ilioneus' head, foresees the homecoming “when / we young men of the Achaeans come back from Troy with our ships” (κοῦροι Ἀχαιῶν, 14.504–505; [[peneleos]]). See [[achaeans]].
