---
summary: The patronymic names Agamemnon seven times, in Nestor's choice, in the list of the wounded kings and in the addresses of Odysseus and Poseidon, and Menelaus once, as he kills Hyperenor.
---

**Agamemnon.** Nestor decides “to go to the son of Atreus” (14.22–24; [[nestor]], [[pondering-two-ways]]). The wounded kings who meet him are “the son of Tydeus, and Odysseus, and the son of Atreus, Agamemnon” (14.29), and the same verse returns when they go through the army exchanging armor (14.380; [[the-wounded-chiefs]], [[son-of-tydeus]]). Odysseus opens his rebuke with the patronymic, “Son of Atreus, what a word has escaped the barrier of your teeth!” (14.83; [[what-a-word]], [[odysseus]]), and so does Poseidon, who takes “the right hand of Agamemnon, son of Atreus” and tells him that the gods have not yet turned against him (14.137–143; [[poseidon]], [[blessed-gods]]).

**Menelaus.** “And then the son of Atreus stabbed Hyperenor, shepherd of the people” (14.516; [[menelaus]], [[hyperenor]]). The verse does not name him; he recalls the killing in Book 17 (17.24–28; see the translation's note on 14.516). See [[agamemnon]] and [[atreidae]].
