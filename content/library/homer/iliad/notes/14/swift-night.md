---
summary: Sleep tells Hera that Zeus, raging against him, stopped “for he shrank from doing what would displease swift Night.”
---

“And he would have thrown me from the upper air into the sea, out of sight, / had not Night, the tamer of gods and men, saved me. / To her I came in flight, and he stopped, angry though he was; / for he shrank from doing what would displease swift Night” (14.258–261; [[night]], [[tamer-of-gods-and-men]], [[sleep]], [[zeus]]). The epithet of the night that covers dangerous journeys is here the epithet of the goddess, the one power whom Zeus in his anger would not offend. The dative νυκτὶ θοῇ is the phrase of Hector as he leapt in at the gate of the wall, “his face like swift night” (12.463), and [[swift-ships]] excepts the line here as there.
