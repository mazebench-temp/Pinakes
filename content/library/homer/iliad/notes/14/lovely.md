---
summary: Hera, flying from Olympus to Lemnos, steps on “Pieria and lovely Emathia.”
---

“And Hera darted down and left the peak of Olympus, / and stepping on Pieria and lovely Emathia / she sped over the snowy mountains of the horse-herding Thracians” (14.225–227; [[hera]], [[pieria]], [[emathia]], [[horse-herding]], [[niphoeis]]). The epithet with which the Catalogue praises home towns is given to a land named only here in Homer, the second stage of a route that runs in order from Olympus over Thrace and Athos to Lemnos (14.225–230). Strabo quotes the route as an example of Homer keeping places in their order where the order matters (*Geography* 1.2.20).
