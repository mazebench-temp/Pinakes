---
summary: Tydeus in Argos “lived in a house / wealthy in substance,” in Diomedes' account of his father.
---

“He married one of the daughters of Adrastus, and lived in a house / wealthy in substance, and he had fields enough / bearing wheat” (14.121–123; [[tydeus]], [[adrastus]], [[rich-in-wheat]], [[argos]]). The adjective agrees with the house, δῶμα ἀφνειὸν βιότοιο. Tydeus came to Argos as a wanderer and grew rich there, and Diomedes names his father's wealth, his fields, his orchards, his flocks and his skill with the spear as proof that he is no coward's son (14.119–127; [[diomedes]]).
