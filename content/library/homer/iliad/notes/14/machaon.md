summary: Machaon's last appearance. Hearing the battle draw near, Nestor leaves him in the hut to sit and drink until Hecamede has heated a bath to wash away the clotted blood (14.1–8).
# Μαχᾶον (14.3), the vocative, is found only here.
grc: [Μαχᾶον]
refs: [14.2, 14.5-7]
---

**Left in the hut.** When the cry at the ships grows louder, Nestor turns to “the son of Asclepius”, “brilliant Machaon” (14.2–3; see [[asclepius]] and [[brilliant]]): let him sit and drink the sparkling wine “until Hecamede of the lovely tresses heats a warm bath / for you and washes away the clotted blood” (14.6–7; see [[hecamede]] and [[bathing]]). Nestor goes out to look, and the healer stays behind with the wine (see [[nestor]]).

In Book 11 the drink was all he was given. The arrow had struck his right shoulder (11.507), but nothing was done for the wound, and Eurypylus, needing his own arrow cut out, could only say that Machaon lay in the huts “himself in need of a noble healer” (11.835; see [[eurypylus]] and [[healing]]). Now a bath is promised, and with it Machaon leaves the poem. He is not named again, and he never speaks.
