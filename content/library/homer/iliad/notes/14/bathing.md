summary: Nestor leaves the wounded Machaon to wait for a warm bath that Hecamede is to heat, in words that will return for Hector's bath prepared in vain and for the washing of Patroclus' body; and Hera bathes in ambrosia and anoints herself with oil before she goes to Zeus.
passages:
  - 14.1-8 | Nestor hears the cry
  - 14.170-177 | Hera's bath
---

**A bath planned.** Nestor hears the louder cry from the ships and leaves his wounded guest: “But you, for now, sit and drink the sparkling wine, / until Hecamede of the lovely tresses heats a warm bath / for you and washes away the clotted blood” (14.5–7; see [[machaon]], [[hecamede]] and [[healing]]). The bath is not told; Machaon is not seen again in the poem. Both halves of the promise come back in sadder places. θερμὰ λοετρά, “a warm bath”, is what Andromache orders her handmaids to heat for Hector as he comes home from the battle, not knowing that he is already dead (22.442–446), and “washes away the clotted blood” (λούσῃ ἄπο βρότον αἱματόεντα, 14.7) is the end of the verse with which Achilles orders Patroclus' body washed (18.343–345), and the verse with which the kings try to persuade Achilles himself to wash, which he refuses until he has burned Patroclus (23.39–47).

**A goddess's bath.** Hera's bath has no water and no handmaids. Alone in her chamber she cleanses her body with ambrosia and “anointed herself richly with olive oil” (ἀλείψατο δὲ λίπʼ ἐλαίῳ, 14.170–172; see [[ambrosia]] and [[the-adornment-of-a-goddess]]). The half-verse is the one that ends the bath of men, of Odysseus and Diomedes after the night raid and of Patroclus' body (10.577, 18.350), and of many guests in the Odyssey (*Odyssey* 3.466, 10.364); in the Odyssey the Graces bathe Aphrodite and anoint her with immortal oil (*Odyssey* 8.364–365).
