**Desire wraps the mind.** “And as he saw her, so desire wrapped his shrewd mind” (ὥς μιν ἔρως πυκινὰς φρένας ἀμφεκάλυψεν, 14.294). The words are Paris' of himself (3.442), told now by the narrator, with an epithet added: the mind it covers is πυκινός, “shrewd, close” (see [[phrenes]]). Hera meant to pour sleep on Zeus' “shrewd mind” (φρεσὶ πευκαλίμῃσι, 14.165), and the persuasion in Aphrodite's strap “steals the mind even of men who think shrewdly” (πύκα περ φρονεόντων, 14.217; see [[aphrodites-band]]). The shrewdest mind of all is the one desire overcomes. The text prints the Attic ἔρως here and at 3.442; the Epic nominative ἔρος occurs in Homer only in Zeus' speech (14.315).

**Zeus' speech.** Zeus answers Hera in the order of Paris' answer to Helen (3.441–446). Paris begins, “But come, let us go to bed and take our pleasure in love”, and Zeus, “but come, let the two of us go to bed” (3.441, 14.314; see [[philotes]]). Paris goes on, “for never yet has desire so wrapped my mind”, and Zeus, “For never yet has desire for goddess or for woman / so flooded round the heart in my breast and overcome it” (14.315–316). The verb is περιπροχυθείς, “poured out around”: desire floods the heart as sleep is poured around a sleeper (14.164–165, 14.253; see [[hypnos]]). Paris compares one night, the first, on the island of Cranae (3.443–445); Zeus compares seven women and goddesses, “not when I loved the wife of Ixion” (ἠρασάμην, 14.317), and then Danae, the daughter of Phoenix, Semele, Alcmene, Demeter and Leto, “nor you yourself” (14.319–327; see [[the-loves-of-zeus]]). Both end in the same verse, “as I love you now, and sweet longing takes hold of me” (ἔραμαι, 3.446 = 14.328; see [[himeros]]). The same aorist is used elsewhere in the Iliad of Hermes, who loved Polymele when he saw her singing in the chorus of Artemis (ἠράσατʼ, 16.180–183), and of the North Wind, who loved the mares of Erichthonius (ἠράσσατο, 20.219–223).

Plato's Socrates found it unworthy of Zeus to forget all his plans for desire, not even willing to go to his chamber (*Republic* 3, 390b–c; see [[plato-zeus-deceived]]).
