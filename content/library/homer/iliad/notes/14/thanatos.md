summary: Book 14 names Death only as a person, the brother of Sleep. The word θάνατος does not occur in it, but Aphrodite's gifts overcome “the immortals and mortal men”, and the deaths at the end of the book are told without the word.
grc: [θνητοὺς]
en: [mortal men]
---

Hera meets “Sleep, the brother of Death” on Lemnos (κασιγνήτῳ Θανάτοιο, 14.231); the god has his own entry (see [[death]] and [[sleep]]). The adjective sets the two kinds of being side by side once. Hera asks Aphrodite for love and longing, “with which you / overcome all the immortals and mortal men” (ἀθανάτους ἠδὲ θνητοὺς ἀνθρώπους, 14.198–199; see [[immortals]] and [[philotes]]).

The killings at the end of the book are told without the noun (see [[death-in-battle]]). Hector, struck by Ajax's stone, comes near it: the verse of a death, “and his two eyes / black night covered”, is said of a man who lives (14.438–439; see [[darkness-covered-his-eyes]]). Acamas speaks of the man he has killed as sleeping (14.482; see [[hypnos]]).
