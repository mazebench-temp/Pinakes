---
summary: Odysseus ends his rebuke of Agamemnon's plan of flight with the address “leader of the people”; the book adds the vocative ὄρχαμε λαῶν.
grc: ["ὄρχαμε λαῶν"]
en: [leader of the people]
---

“Then your counsel would do us harm, leader of the people” (14.102; [[odysseus]], [[agamemnon]], [[boule]]). The rebuke began with “Ruinous man” (14.84; [[deadly]]) and ends with the title of a leader whose counsel would lead his people to ruin. Between them Odysseus says that no man who held a scepter and was obeyed would let such a word pass his lips (14.90–94; [[scepter-bearing]]). ὄρχαμε λαῶν is the form the title takes in address, later given to Menelaus, to the dead Patroclus and to Achilles (17.12, 19.289, 21.221); the translation renders it “leader of the people,” and keeps “leader of men” for ὄρχαμος ἀνδρῶν. The phrase is new to the entry.
