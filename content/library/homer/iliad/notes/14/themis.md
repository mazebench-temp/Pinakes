When Zeus is asleep, Poseidon no longer helps in disguise. He leads the Achaeans himself, “holding a dread long-edged sword in his strong hand, / like lightning; it is not lawful to close with it / in wretched battle, but fear holds men back” (τῷ δʼ οὐ θέμις ἐστὶ μιγῆναι, 14.385–387; see [[poseidon]], [[sword]] and [[like-lightning]]). τῷ may be the sword or the god himself (see the translation's note on 14.386); either way, what is not θέμις is for a man to meet a god in the fight. θέμις here is not a custom of men, as in ἣ θέμις ἐστί, “as is the custom” (2.73, 9.33), but a limit set by the order of things, which men keep from fear (δέος, see [[deos]]). Diomedes drew the same line when he saw Ares beside Hector and told the Achaeans to give ground and not fight against gods (5.601–606; see [[retreat]]).

The negative form, οὐ θέμις, marks what may not be done. It was not θέμις for the horsehair helmet of Achilles to be fouled with dust, until Apollo struck it from Patroclus' head (16.793–800), and Achilles swears that it is not θέμις for water to come near his head before he has laid Patroclus on the pyre, heaped his mound and cut his hair (23.43–47; see [[bathing]]).
