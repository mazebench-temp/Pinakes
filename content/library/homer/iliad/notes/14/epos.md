---
summary: Words in Book 14 are threats to be fulfilled, rebukes, and the instruments of the deception. Agamemnon fears that Hector will make good his word; Odysseus asks what word has escaped Agamemnon's teeth; Aphrodite cannot refuse Hera's word, Sleep has obeyed it before, and Hera claims that she will win over Oceanus and Tethys with words.
---

**Threat and rebuke.** “I fear that mighty Hector may make good his word to me” (τελέσῃ ἔπος, 14.44; see [[assembly]]), says Agamemnon, and Odysseus answers his plan of flight, “Son of Atreus, what a word has escaped the barrier of your teeth!” (ποῖόν σε ἔπος φύγεν ἕρκος ὀδόντων, 14.83; see [[what-a-word]]).

**Hera's words.** Aphrodite: “It is not possible, nor would it be fitting, to refuse your word” (τεὸν ἔπος ἀρνήσασθαι, 14.212; see [[aphrodite]]). Hera to Sleep: “once before you listened to my word; so now once more / obey me” (ἐμὸν ἔπος ἔκλυες, 14.234–235; see [[peitho]]). Her pretended errand is itself a work of words: “If with words I could win over the dear hearts of those two” (ἐπέεσσι παραιπεπιθοῦσα, 14.208; see [[truth-and-deception]]).

**Formulas.** Three speeches open “with winged words” (ἔπεα πτερόεντα, 14.2, 14.138, 14.356; see [[winged-words]]), three with the verse “and spoke to her, and called her by name” (ἔπος τʼ ἔφατʼ ἔκ τʼ ὀνόμαζε, 14.218, 14.232, 14.297; see [[called-him-by-name]]), and Peneleos, holding up Ilioneus' head, “spoke in triumph” (εὐχόμενος ἔπος ηὔδα, 14.500; see [[euchomai]]).
