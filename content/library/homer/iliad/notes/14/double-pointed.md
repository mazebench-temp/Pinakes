---
summary: While Nestor ponders, the fighting goes on “with swords and double-pointed spears”; the book adds the form without movable ν.
grc: [ἀμφιγύοισι]
---

“And they kept killing one another, / fighting; and the unyielding bronze rang about their bodies / as they stabbed at one another with swords and double-pointed spears” (14.24–26; [[unyielding]], [[nestor]]). The verse-end ξίφεσίν τε καὶ ἔγχεσιν ἀμφιγύοισι is the formula of close fighting first heard when the Achaeans pushed Hector back from their ranks (13.147). Nestor has just chosen to go to Agamemnon rather than into the throng (14.20–24; [[pondering-two-ways]]), and the battle goes on around the wounded kings. The form ἀμφιγύοισι, without movable ν before the consonant of the next verse, is new to the entry; it returns at 15.712.
