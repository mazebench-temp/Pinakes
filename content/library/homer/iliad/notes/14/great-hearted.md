---
summary: Polydamas, boasting over Prothoenor, speaks of himself as “the great-hearted son of Panthous.”
---

“Truly, I do not think that from the great-hearted son of Panthous, / from his strong hand, the javelin has leapt in vain this time” (14.454–455; [[polydamas]], [[panthous]], [[prothoenor]], [[strong-hand]]). Polydamas names himself in the third person, by his father and with an epithet of praise. The boast costs a Trojan life: Ajax throws at Polydamas, who dodges, and the spear kills Archelochus (14.458–464; [[ajax]], [[archelochus]], [[black-doom]]).
