---
summary: The white-armed goddess Hera does not disobey Sleep, and swears the oath he asks.
---

“So he spoke, and the white-armed goddess Hera did not disobey, / and she swore as he bade, and named all the gods / beneath Tartarus, who are called Titans” (14.277–279; [[hera]], [[sleep]], [[oath]], [[titans]], [[tartarus]]). The verse ὣς ἔφατʼ, οὐδʼ ἀπίθησε θεὰ λευκώλενος Ἥρη is the one in which Hera obeyed Zeus in Book 5 and Athena in Book 8 (5.767, 8.381); here she obeys Sleep, the god she is bribing. The next time it is spoken she obeys Zeus again, awake and angry (15.78). It is the one use of the formula in the deception, where she is otherwise “ox-eyed” and “with treachery in her heart” ([[ox-eyed]], [[with-treachery-in-her-heart]]).
