---
summary: Longing is what Hera means to arouse in Zeus. She asks Aphrodite for “love and longing”, finds longing worked into the strap, and promises Sleep the Grace he has always longed for; and Zeus ends his list of loves with Paris' verse, “sweet longing takes hold of me”.
grc: [ἱμείραιτο, ἱμερόεντος, ἱμείρεαι]
en: [longing, might long]
---

**Hera's aim.** The plan is to see “if he might long to lie in love beside / her body” (εἴ πως ἱμείραιτο, 14.163–164; see [[philotes]]). The first thing she cleanses is her “lovely body” (ἀπὸ χροὸς ἱμερόεντος, 14.170), the adjective of what arouses longing (see the article above and [[the-adornment-of-a-goddess]]). She asks Aphrodite for “love and longing”, with which Aphrodite overcomes all the immortals and mortal men (14.198–199), and receives the strap in which “was love, in it longing” (14.216; see [[aphrodites-band]]).

**Sleep's longing.** Hera's last offer to Sleep is one of the younger Graces as his wife, “[Pasithea, for whom you are always longing, all your days.]” (ἧς αἰὲν ἱμείρεαι, 14.269). The verse is in brackets because the Monro–Allen text, as digitized, omits it (see the translation's note on 14.269). It repeats Sleep's own words with the person changed: he makes Hera swear to give him “Pasithea, whom I myself have longed for all my days” (ἧς τʼ αὐτὸς ἐέλδομαι, 14.276; see [[pasithea]] and [[graces]]). The bribe that works is a longing satisfied.

**Zeus' longing.** Zeus ends the list of his loves with the verse of Paris in Book 3: “as I love you now, and sweet longing takes hold of me” (14.328 = 3.446; see [[eros]], [[the-loves-of-zeus]] and the note on Book 3). Hera has borrowed longing from Aphrodite, and it has taken hold of Zeus.
