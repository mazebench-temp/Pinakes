summary: After its use for battle in Book 13, ὀαριστύς returns to lovers: it is the third of the powers worked into Aphrodite's strap, set beside the persuasion that steals the mind.
grc: [ὀαριστὺς]
passages:
  - 14.214-217 | The embroidered strap
---

The strap that Aphrodite looses from her breast holds three powers, and the next verse says what the third one is: “in it was love, in it longing, in it intimate talk, / the persuasion that steals the mind even of men who think shrewdly” (ἐν δʼ ὀαριστὺς / πάρφασις, 14.216–217; see [[aphrodites-band]], [[philotes]] and [[himeros]]). πάρφασις follows without a conjunction, and the translation takes it as standing in apposition: the intimate talk of lovers is a persuasion that works on the mind (see [[peitho]] and [[the-persuasion-that-steals-the-mind]]).

In Book 13 Idomeneus gave the word to the meeting of the champions in battle (13.291; see the note on Book 13). Here it is in its own place, among Aphrodite's gifts, and on Gargarus it is acted out: Zeus and Hera talk before they lie down, he asking where she is going, she telling her false story, he answering with the list of his loves, and she objecting that someone might see them (14.297–345; see [[the-deception-of-zeus]]).
