---
summary: The talks of the gods in Book 14 are carried by verses of reply: Aphrodite, Sleep and Zeus answer Hera in turn, and Zeus answers her twice on Gargarus in the verse with which he answered her in their quarrels of Books 1, 5 and 8.
grc: ["ἀμειβόμενος δὲ προσηύδα"]
en: [answering he said]
---

Hera's three conversations are built of replies. Aphrodite answers her “in turn” (14.211); Sleep answers twice, “Answering her, sweet Sleep said” (14.242) and, when she has promised him a Grace, “So she spoke, and Sleep rejoiced, and answering he said” (ἀμειβόμενος δὲ προσηύδα, 14.270; see [[sleep]]); and Hera answers him “in turn” (14.263). On Gargarus Zeus answers her twice in the same whole verse, “Answering her, Zeus the cloud-gatherer said” (τὴν δʼ ἀπαμειβόμενος προσέφη νεφεληγερέτα Ζεύς, 14.312, 14.341; see [[zeus]] and [[cloud-gatherer]]). It is the verse with which he answered her in Book 1, when she charged him with secret plans for Thetis (1.560), in Book 5, when she asked leave to drive Ares from the battle (5.764), and in Book 8, when he threatened her (8.469). Here the same verse introduces an invitation to bed and the promise of a golden cloud (see [[the-deception-of-zeus]]). Among men the formula carries the council: “Then Agamemnon, lord of men, spoke to him in turn” (14.64).
