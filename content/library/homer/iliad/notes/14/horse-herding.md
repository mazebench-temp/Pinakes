---
summary: Hera speeds over “the snowy mountains of the horse-herding Thracians” on her way to Lemnos.
---

“And stepping on Pieria and lovely Emathia / she sped over the snowy mountains of the horse-herding Thracians, / over their topmost peaks, and her feet did not touch the ground” (14.226–228; [[thracians]], [[pieria]], [[emathia]], [[niphoeis]], [[lovely]]). The genitive Θρῃκῶν ἱπποπόλων stands in the same place in the verse as at the opening of Book 13, when Zeus turned his eyes away from Troy to the land of the horse-herding Thracians (13.4). There the god looked away to them from Ida; here the goddess flies over their mountains from Olympus toward Lemnos and Sleep (14.229–231; [[hera]], [[lemnos]]).
