---
summary: Poseidon answers Achilles' oath of Book 1: “there will be no great longing for him, if we others / urge ourselves on to defend one another”.
---

Achilles swore in Book 1 that “Someday a longing for Achilles will come upon the sons of the Achaeans, / on all of them together” (Ἀχιλλῆος ποθὴ ἵξεται, 1.240–241; see [[achilles]] and the note on Book 1). Poseidon, leading the Argives while Zeus sleeps, denies it in the same word. Hector boasts because Achilles stays by the ships in anger, “but there will be no great longing for him, if we others / urge ourselves on to defend one another” (κείνου δʼ οὔ τι λίην ποθὴ ἔσσεται, 14.368–369; see [[poseidon]] and [[exhortation]]). The god's answer to the absent hero is the army's own courage, each man defending the next (see [[exchange-of-armor]]). It holds only while Zeus sleeps (see [[the-deception-of-zeus]]).
