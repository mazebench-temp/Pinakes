---
summary: The narrator explains how the ships lie, drawn up in rows with the wall built along the sterns of the first of them; and Agamemnon says twice that the battle is now “at the sterns of the ships”.
grc: ["=πρύμνῃσιν"]
---

**How the ships lie.** The narrator explains why the wounded kings must come up from the ships to see the battle: the ships were drawn up far from it, on the shore, “for the first of them they had hauled up toward the plain, / and built the wall along their sterns” (τεῖχος ἐπὶ πρύμνῃσιν ἔδειμαν, 14.30–32), and since the beach could not hold them all, they hauled them up in rows and filled the whole shore between the headlands (14.33–36; see [[ships]] and [[the-achaean-wall]]).

**At the sterns.** With the wall thrown down, Agamemnon complains that the Achaeans, angry with him like Achilles, “are not willing to fight at the sterns of the ships” (ἐπὶ πρυμνῇσι νέεσσι, 14.51), and he begins his proposal of flight from the same fact: “Nestor, since they are fighting at the sterns of the ships” (14.65; see [[agamemnon]] and [[no-blame-in-fleeing]]). In Book 8 Zeus had foretold the day “when they fight at the sterns of the ships / in the direst strait, over Patroclus dead” (8.475–476; see the note on Book 8). Agamemnon's remedy is to haul down the ships nearest the sea and anchor them out until night (14.74–79).
