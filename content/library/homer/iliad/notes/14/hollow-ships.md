---
summary: Poseidon tells the Argives that Hector boasts because Achilles “stays by the hollow ships, angered at heart,” and that they can do without him.
---

“So he says and boasts, because Achilles / stays by the hollow ships, angered at heart; / but there will be no great longing for him, if we others / urge ourselves on to defend one another” (14.366–369; [[achilles]], [[poseidon]], [[poseidon-rallies-the-achaeans]]). The verse of Achilles' absence answers Agamemnon's fear at the beginning of the book, that the Achaeans are laying up anger against him “just as Achilles is” (14.49–51; [[agamemnon]]). The god, speaking now as a leader, says what the king could not: the army can fight without Achilles. See [[ships]].
