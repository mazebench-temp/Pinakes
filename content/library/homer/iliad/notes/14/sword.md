summary: Poseidon leads the Achaeans with “a dread long-edged sword”, “like lightning”, which no man may meet in battle; the strap of Ajax's silver-studded sword helps to stop Hector's spear; and Peneleos cuts off Ilioneus' head with his sword.
grc: [ἄορ, φασγάνου]
passages:
  - 14.384-387 | Poseidon's sword
---

**The god's sword.** When the kings have redistributed the army's arms, “Poseidon the earth-shaker led them, / holding a dread long-edged sword in his strong hand, / like lightning; it is not lawful to close with it / in wretched battle, but fear holds men back” (δεινὸν ἄορ τανύηκες, 14.384–387; see [[poseidon]], [[like-lightning]], [[long-edged]] and [[themis]]). It is the only weapon a god carries into the fighting in this book, and it is not used: its likeness to the lightning of Zeus is enough to keep men from it, and Poseidon is not said to strike anyone. ἄορ is one of Homer's three words for the sword, with ξίφος and φάσγανον (see the article above).

**Swords of men.** At the start of the book the fighting at the ships is close: “they stabbed at one another with swords and double-pointed spears” (14.26; see [[double-pointed]]). Hector's spear is stopped where the strap of Ajax's shield and the strap “of his silver-studded sword” lie across his chest (φασγάνου ἀργυροήλου, 14.404–406; see [[shield]] and [[silver-studded]]), and Peneleos, his spear fixed in Ilioneus' eye, “drawing his sharp sword, / struck him full in the middle of the neck” and cut off the head (14.496–498; see [[peneleos]] and [[ilioneus]]).
