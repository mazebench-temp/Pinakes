---
summary: The word for the allies does not occur in Book 14, but their leaders and men are there. The Lycian kings Sarpedon and Glaucus stand over the fallen Hector, and in the rout Ajax kills Hyrtius, leader of the Mysians, while Antilochus and Meriones kill Phalces, Morys and Hippotion, of whom Morys at least had come from Ascania as relief (13.792–794).
---

The Lycians, the allies named in the Trojans' own battle cry, are present only in their leaders: Sarpedon, “leader of the Lycians”, and Glaucus stand over Hector with Polydamas, Aeneas and Agenor (14.424–426; see [[sarpedon]], [[glaucus]] and [[lycians]]). When Poseidon has turned the battle, the first man killed is Hyrtius, “leader of the stout-hearted Mysians” (14.511–512; see [[hyrtius]] and [[mysians]]). Antilochus kills Phalces and Meriones kills Morys and Hippotion (14.513–514; see [[phalces]], [[morys]] and [[hippotion]]). In Book 13 Phalces and Morys were among the men around whom the fighting at the ships was thickest, and Morys, with Palmys and Ascanius, had come from Ascania the morning before (13.789–794; see [[ascania]]). Strabo cites the death of Satnius, born by the Satnioeis, the river of the Leleges' town Pedasus, to show that the Leleges were present at the fighting, though the Catalogue does not name them (14.443–445; *Geography* 13.3.1; see [[satnius]] and [[leleges]]).
