{{quote:e6cf642a-afab-5292-a8db-b2f79336820a}}

ἔτι δʼ ὄβριμον ἔγχος / ἦεν ἐν ὀφθαλμῷ· ὃ δὲ φὴ κώδειαν ἀνασχὼν / πέφραδέ τε Τρώεσσι καὶ εὐχόμενος ἔπος ηὔδα: “and the heavy spear was still in the eye; and holding it up like a poppy head he showed it to the Trojans, and spoke in triumph” (14.498–500). φή, “like”, is a rare word; Homer has it once more, in the simile of the assembly stirred “like the long waves of the sea” (2.144; see [[waves-of-the-icarian-sea]]). κώδεια, the head of the poppy, its seed capsule, occurs only here. The translation's note on 14.499 records another reading: accented φῆ, the word would be the verb “said”, and κώδεια the severed head itself.

The likeness is one word, and it calls up the other poppy of the Iliad. Gorgythion's head drooped to one side “weighed down by his helmet”, πήληκι βαρυνθέν, as a poppy in a garden droops its head under its seed and the spring rain (8.306–308). Here Peneleos strikes off αὐτῇ σὺν πήληκι κάρη, “the head, helmet and all” (14.498), and lifts it on the spear: the head with its helmet is the poppy head on its stalk. In Book 8 the flower bowed of itself; here a man raises it and shows it.

Ilioneus was the only son of Phorbas, “rich in flocks”, whom Hermes loved most of the Trojans and to whom he granted wealth (14.489–492; see [[ilioneus]] and [[rich-in-flocks]]). Peneleos had rushed at Acamas, who did not wait for him, and struck Ilioneus instead, under the brow, and the spear went through the eye and the back of the head; Ilioneus sat down with both hands outstretched, and Peneleos cut through the middle of his neck with his sword (14.487–498; see [[peneleos]] and [[acamas]]). The boast answers Acamas' boast over Promachus: Ilioneus' father and mother are to wail in their halls, for the wife of Promachus will not be glad at her husband's homecoming either (14.501–505). “So he spoke, and trembling seized the limbs of them all” (14.506).
