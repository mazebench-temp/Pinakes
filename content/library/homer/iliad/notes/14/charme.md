---
summary: Odysseus warns that the Achaeans would “hang back from battle” if they saw the ships launched; when Hector is carried away, “they sprang on the Trojans more fiercely, and remembered the joy of battle”, in the verse that followed Zeus' eagle in Book 8.
---

**Hanging back.** Odysseus' last argument against Agamemnon's plan is the army's spirit: the Achaeans will not hold out in the war while the ships are dragged to the sea, “but will look away, and hang back from battle” (ἐρωήσουσι δὲ χάρμης, 14.100–101; see [[odysseus]] and [[retreat]]). To launch the ships would tell the army that its leaders had given up.

**Remembering it.** When Hector falls and is carried off, the Argives see him “going away”, and “they sprang on the Trojans more fiercely, and remembered the joy of battle” (μνήσαντο δὲ χάρμης, 14.440–441; see [[hector]]). The second verse is the one that followed the eagle Zeus sent in Book 8, when the Achaeans saw that the bird came from Zeus (8.251–252; see [[eagle]]). There a sign from Zeus gave them heart; here it is the sight of Hector leaving the field, while Zeus sleeps. In the next book the same verse is given to the Trojans, with Argives in its place, when they hear Zeus thunder (15.377–380): the joy of battle goes to whichever side the god favors.
