---
summary: Zeus names “Danae of the lovely ankles, daughter of Acrisius,” the mother of Perseus.
---

“Nor when I loved Danae of the lovely ankles, daughter of Acrisius, / who bore Perseus, preeminent among all men” (14.319–320; [[danae]], [[acrisius]], [[perseus]], [[preeminent]], [[the-loves-of-zeus]]). The verse has the shape of the one that named Marpessa in Book 9: a name, the epithet in the genitive, and a patronymic adjective at the end, “Marpessa of the lovely ankles, daughter of Evenus” (9.557; Μαρπήσσης καλλισφύρου Εὐηνίνης, Δανάης καλλισφύρου Ἀκρισιώνης). Both women were loved by a god; for Marpessa, Idas took up his bow against Apollo (9.558–560; [[idas-and-apollo]]).
