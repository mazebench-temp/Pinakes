---
summary: Poseidon speaks of “the deadly heart of Achilles,” and Odysseus calls Agamemnon “Ruinous man” for proposing flight; the book adds the vocative οὐλόμενʼ.
grc: ["οὐλόμενʼ"]
---

**Odysseus' rebuke.** Agamemnon proposes to haul the ships down and escape by night, and Odysseus, looking at him darkly, answers: “Ruinous man, I wish you were giving orders to some other, unworthy army, / and were not lord over us” (14.84–85; [[odysseus]], [[agamemnon]], [[looking-darkly]]). οὐλόμενε is the vocative of the participle that names the wrath in the poem's second line (1.2); here it names a king whose plan would ruin his army. The form οὐλόμενʼ is new to the entry.

**Achilles.** Poseidon, in the likeness of an old man, tells Agamemnon: “now, I think, the deadly heart of Achilles / rejoices in his breast at the slaughter and the panic of the Achaeans” (14.139–140; [[achilles]], [[poseidon]]). The god wishes that Achilles may perish so, and that a god may maim him (14.142).
