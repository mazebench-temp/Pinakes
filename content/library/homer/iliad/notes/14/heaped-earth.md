---
summary: Diomedes names his father Tydeus, “whom the heaped earth covers at Thebes.”
---

“But I too claim to be of the line of a brave father, / Tydeus, whom the heaped earth covers at Thebes” (14.113–114; [[tydeus]], [[thebes]], [[diomedes]], [[tomb]]). The grave that Hector wished for himself in Book 6 (6.464) is here the grave of a father who died far from home: Tydeus settled in Argos after his wanderings, married a daughter of Adrastus and lived in a wealthy house (14.119–125; [[adrastus]], [[wealthy]]), and he lies buried at Thebes. Agamemnon and Athena held up his deeds at Thebes to his son (4.370–400, 5.800–813; [[tydeus-at-thebes]]); here the son names only the grave, as the proof of a brave father.
