---
summary: Hera promises Sleep “a fine throne, imperishable forever, / golden,” the work of Hephaestus, and Sleep refuses it.
---

“And I will give you gifts: a fine throne, imperishable forever, / golden; and Hephaestus, my son, the god lame in both legs, / will make it with skill, and set a footstool beneath it for your feet” (14.238–240; [[sleep]], [[throne]], [[hephaestus]], [[lame-in-both-legs]], [[golden-throned]]). The half-verse ἄφθιτον αἰεί is the one of Agamemnon's scepter, the work of Hephaestus that passed from Zeus to the king (2.46, 2.186; [[the-scepter-of-agamemnon]]); here it describes a bribe. Sleep refuses the throne, remembering the anger of Zeus, and accepts instead the promise of one of the Graces as his wife (14.242–276; [[pasithea]], [[graces]]).
