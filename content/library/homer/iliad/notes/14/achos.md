---
summary: In the killings after Hector's fall, each boast brings grief on the other side: “grief came upon the Argives at his boasting”, twice, and “grief seized the hearts of the Trojans” at Ajax's taunt.
---

The boasts that follow Hector's fall are answered with grief, and the grief with a killing. Polydamas boasts over Prothoenor: “So he spoke, and grief came upon the Argives at his boasting, / and most of all he stirred the heart of wise-hearted Ajax” (Ἀργείοισι δʼ ἄχος γένετʼ εὐξαμένοιο, 14.458–459), and Ajax kills Archelochus. Ajax taunts Polydamas, “and grief seized the hearts of the Trojans” (Τρῶας δʼ ἄχος ἔλλαβε θυμόν, 14.475), and Acamas kills Promachus. Acamas boasts, and the couplet of 14.458–459 returns with Peneleos in place of Ajax (14.486–487), and Peneleos kills Ilioneus (see [[euchomai]], [[thymos]] and [[poine]]). ἄχος is the grief that a loss brings, and here it is also what moves the next man to strike (see the article above).
