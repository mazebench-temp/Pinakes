---
summary: In the rout at the end of the book, “Meriones killed Morys and Hippotion” (14.514).
---

Meriones has one verse in the book, in the list of the Achaeans who win bloody spoils after Poseidon has turned the battle: “and Meriones killed Morys and Hippotion” (14.514; see [[invocation-of-the-muses]] and [[bloody-spoils]]). Morys was one of the men from Ascania who had come to Troy “as relief” the morning before, and one of the leaders gathered around Polydamas at the end of Book 13, where he is called “Morys, son of Hippotion” (13.792–794; see [[morys]], [[hippotion]], [[ascania]] and [[polydamas]]). Whether the Hippotion killed with him is that father the poem does not say. In Book 13 Meriones was Idomeneus' companion in the fighting on the left of the ships, where he killed Adamas and Harpalion (13.567–575, 13.650–655; see [[idomeneus]], [[adamas]] and [[harpalion]]).
