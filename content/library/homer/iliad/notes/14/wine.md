summary: The book begins with Nestor still drinking with the wounded Machaon, the mixed drink of Pramnian wine that Hecamede prepared in Book 11; he leaves Machaon to “drink the sparkling wine” and goes to see the battle.
passages:
  - 14.1-8 | Nestor hears the cry
---

“The shouting did not escape Nestor, though he was drinking” (πίνοντά περ ἔμπης, 14.1). In Book 11 Hecamede made Nestor and Machaon a mixed drink in Nestor's great cup, “with Pramnian wine”, grated goat's cheese and white barley meal, and they had drunk and were talking when Patroclus came to the door (11.624–644; see [[the-cup-of-nestor]], [[hecamede]] and [[machaon]]). When Book 14 begins, Nestor is still drinking. He tells Machaon: “But you, for now, sit and drink the sparkling wine, / until Hecamede of the lovely tresses heats a warm bath / for you and washes away the clotted blood” (αἴθοπα οἶνον, 14.5–7; see [[bathing]] and [[healing]]), and goes out to look (14.8–15; see [[nestor]] and [[hut]]).

There is no other wine in the book. The only feast is in Hera's promise to Sleep of a golden throne with a footstool, on which he may rest his shining feet “at the banquet” (εἰλαπινάζων, 14.238–241; see [[throne]] and [[footstool]]).
