---
summary: Three replies in Book 14 open with “Then … answered”: Nestor's to Agamemnon's fear, Agamemnon's to Odysseus' rebuke, and Aphrodite's to Hera's first request.
---

“Then the Gerenian horseman Nestor answered him” (τὸν δʼ ἠμείβετʼ ἔπειτα, 14.52) introduces Nestor's verdict that the wall is down and that no one, not even Zeus, could now make it otherwise (14.53–63; see [[nestor]]). “Then Agamemnon, lord of men, answered him” (14.103) introduces the king's retreat from his plan of flight after Odysseus' rebuke (14.104–108; see [[agamemnon]] and [[no-blame-in-fleeing]]). “Then Aphrodite, daughter of Zeus, answered her” (14.193) introduces Aphrodite's readiness to grant whatever Hera asks, before she knows what it is (14.194–196; see [[aphrodite]] and [[daughter-of-zeus]]). In each case the reply is a yielding: Nestor yields to what has happened, Agamemnon to Odysseus, Aphrodite to Hera.
