**Eyes to be closed.** Book 13 began with Zeus turning his shining eyes away from Troy (ὄσσε φαεινώ, 13.3, 13.7; see the note on Book 13). Hera, having seen with her own eyes from Olympus what Poseidon is doing and where Zeus is sitting (14.153–158), asks Sleep to close them: “Lull to sleep for me the shining eyes of Zeus beneath his brows” (Ζηνὸς ὑπʼ ὀφρύσιν ὄσσε φαεινώ, 14.236; see [[hypnos]] and [[sleep]]). On Ida Sleep hides in a fir “before the eyes of Zeus could see him” (14.286). Zeus himself answers Hera's fear of being seen with a cloud that the sharpest eye cannot pierce: “not even the Sun could see through it to us, / whose light is the sharpest of all for seeing” (14.344–345; see [[the-golden-cloud]] and [[helios]]). The book's deception is a contest of seeing and not being seen (see [[watching-the-war]]).

**Eyes in the battle.** Hector, carried to the Xanthus, “got his breath back and looked up with his eyes”, then sank back, “and his two eyes / black night covered” (14.436–439; see [[darkness-covered-his-eyes]] and [[hector]]). Peneleos stabs Ilioneus “under the brow, at the roots of the eye, / and drove out the eyeball” (ἐκ δʼ ὦσε γλήνην), the spear going on through the back of the head; then he cuts off the head, “and the heavy spear was still / in the eye”, and he lifts it like a poppy head for the Trojans to see (14.493–500; see [[peneleos]], [[ilioneus]] and [[the-poppy]]). γλήνη, the eyeball or pupil, is what Hector called Diomedes in contempt, κακὴ γλήνη, “wretched doll” (8.164), and Hera's earrings are τρίγληνα, with three eyes or drops (14.183; see [[earrings]]). Menelaus' victim Hyperenor dies as darkness covers his eyes (14.519; see [[psyche]]).
