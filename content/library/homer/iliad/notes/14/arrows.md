---
summary: No arrow is shot in Book 14. βέλος, “missile”, is used of a thrown spear and a thrown stone, and Diomedes bids the wounded kings keep “out of range of the missiles”.
---

The book has no archery: arrows appear only in Acamas' taunt, “Argives, you arrow-fighters” (Ἀργεῖοι ἰόμωροι, 14.479; see [[arrow-fighters]]). βέλος is anything thrown. Hector is enraged “that his swift missile had flown from his hand in vain” (ὅττί ῥά οἱ βέλος ὠκὺ ἐτώσιον ἔκφυγε χειρός, 14.407), and the missile is his spear, stopped by the straps across Ajax's chest (14.402–406; see [[shield]]). The answer is a stone, and when Hector lies by the Xanthus, “the missile still overpowered his spirit” (βέλος δʼ ἔτι θυμὸν ἐδάμνα, 14.439; see [[stone-throwing]] and [[thymos]]).

Diomedes' plan for the wounded kings is to go to the battle but stay “out of range of the missiles, lest someone take wound upon wound” (ἐκ βελέων, 14.129–130; see [[the-wounded-chiefs]] and [[healing]]). ἐκ βελέων is the phrase for getting a man out of the fighting: Zeus draws Hector out from the missiles in Book 11 (11.163), and the phrase returns for Ajax giving way at the ships, for Apollo carrying Sarpedon's body away, and for the struggle to draw Patroclus' body out of range (16.122, 16.668, 16.678, 18.151–152, 18.232).
