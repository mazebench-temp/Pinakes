---
summary: In the list of his loves Zeus names the two sons whom the daughter of Phoenix bore him, “Minos and godlike Rhadamanthys,” the one time the Iliad names Rhadamanthys.
---

“Nor when I loved the daughter of far-famed Phoenix, / who bore me Minos and godlike Rhadamanthys” (14.321–322; [[the-loves-of-zeus]], [[phoenix-father-of-europa]], [[minos]], [[rhadamanthus]]). Of the two brothers only the second has an epithet, and it fills the end of the verse before his long name, as ἀντίθεον Πολύφημον did in Nestor's list of the Lapiths (1.264). The Iliad names Rhadamanthys only here; the Odyssey twice calls him ξανθός, “fair-haired,” as the ruler in the Elysian plain and as the man whom the Phaeacians once carried to Euboea (*Odyssey* 4.564, 7.323).
