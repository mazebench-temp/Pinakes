---
summary: When Hector faints at the ford of the Xanthus after Ajax's stone, “his two eyes / black night covered”; the book adds the phrase νὺξ ἐκάλυψε μέλαινα.
grc: [νὺξ ἐκάλυψε μέλαινα]
---

Hector's companions carry him from the battle to the ford of the Xanthus and pour water over him; he gets his breath back, kneels and vomits dark blood, “then he sank back again to the ground, and his two eyes / black night covered; and the missile still overpowered his spirit” (14.436–439; [[hector]], [[scamander]], [[dark-blood]]). The words are those that cover the eyes of the dying ([[darkness-covered-his-eyes]]), but Hector is not dead. Twice before, “black night” has covered the eyes of a man struck down who lives: Aeneas, whose hip Diomedes crushed with a stone (5.310), and Hector himself, when Diomedes' spear rang on his helmet (11.356). There the night was κελαινή, here μέλαινα. The phrase νὺξ ἐκάλυψε μέλαινα, with the epithet at the end of the clause, is new to the entry. See [[war-and-night]].
