---
summary: The Trojans are “high-hearted” as they drive the Achaeans past the broken wall, and Sleep calls Heracles “that high-hearted son of Zeus.”
---

**The Trojans.** Nestor steps out of his hut and sees “the one side in turmoil, and the others driving them in confusion from behind, / the high-hearted Trojans; and the wall of the Achaeans was thrown down” (14.14–15; [[trojans]], [[the-achaean-wall]], [[nestor]]). The formula is run over into the next verse, where it stands beside the fall of the wall.

**Heracles.** Sleep recalls the day “when that high-hearted son of Zeus / was sailing from Ilios, when he had sacked the city of the Trojans” (14.250–251; [[heracles]], [[son-of-zeus]], [[heracles-sacks-troy]], [[hera-drives-heracles-to-cos]]). The epithet of the Trojans is given to the hero who once sacked their city.
