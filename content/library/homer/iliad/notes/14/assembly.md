---
summary: There is no assembly in Book 14. ἀγορεύω, the verb of public speech, is used only of Hector's threat, which Agamemnon recalls: that he would not go back to Ilios before he had burned the ships and killed the men.
---

Three wounded kings, who come up “leaning on their spears” (14.38), and an old man hold the council of Book 14 by the ships (see [[the-wounded-chiefs]]); no herald calls the army together. The verb of the assembly appears only in a memory of Hector's speeches. “I fear that mighty Hector may make good his word to me, / as he once threatened, speaking among the Trojans, / that he would not go back from the ships to Ilios / before he had set fire to the ships and killed the men themselves. / So he spoke then; and now it is all being fulfilled” (ἐνὶ Τρώεσσʼ ἀγορεύων … κεῖνος τὼς ἀγόρευε, 14.44–48; see [[agamemnon]] and [[hector]]).

Agamemnon's words echo two of Hector's speeches in Book 8: his call to the Trojans in the battle, to burn the ships with fire and kill the Argives beside them (πυρὶ νῆας ἐνιπρήσω, κτείνω δὲ καὶ αὐτούς, 8.172–183), and his speech in the Trojan assembly that night, that he had thought to destroy the ships and all the Achaeans before going back to Ilios (8.489–499; see [[epos]]).
