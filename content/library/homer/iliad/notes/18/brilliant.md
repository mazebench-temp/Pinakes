---
summary: “Brilliant” is Hector, who killed many Achaeans, and Agamemnon, against whom Achilles nursed his wrath; Achilles as he answers Iris, shouts at the ditch and calls for the bath; the Achaeans as the sun sets; and Dawn.
---

**Achilles.** “Then brilliant swift-footed Achilles answered her” (18.181; [[podarkes]]); “Three times across the ditch brilliant Achilles shouted aloud” (18.228); and “So saying, brilliant Achilles called to his companions” (18.343). Hector takes up the word in the assembly: “And if it is true that brilliant Achilles has risen up beside the ships” (18.305).

**Hector and Agamemnon.** Achilles failed his companions, “the many who were brought down by brilliant Hector” (18.103; [[hector]]); Polydamas speaks of the time when Achilles “nursed his wrath against brilliant Agamemnon” (18.257; [[menis]], [[agamemnon]]).

**The Achaeans and Dawn.** “and the sun set, and the brilliant Achaeans ceased” from battle (18.241; [[achaeans]]), and Polydamas will not wait “for brilliant Dawn / on the plain beside the ships” (18.255–256; [[eos]]). At 18.205 and 18.388 the word is part of the formula [[brilliant-among-goddesses]].
