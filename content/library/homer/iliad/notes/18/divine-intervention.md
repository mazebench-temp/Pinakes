summary: The gods act in every scene of the book. Hera sends Iris to rouse Achilles in secret from Zeus (18.166–168); Athena puts the aegis on his shoulders, sets a golden cloud on his head and kindles a flame from him, and cries out with him at the ditch (18.203–218); Hera sends the unwilling Sun down early (18.239–240); Athena takes away the Trojans' wits so that they cheer Hector's bad counsel (18.311); and Thetis brings armor made by Hephaestus (18.369–617). Thetis tells how Apollo killed Patroclus and gave Hector glory (18.454–456).

The sun goes down “unwilling” (ἀέκοντα, 18.240), because before its time; Walter Leaf notes that the only other interference of a god with the course of nature in Homer is Athena's holding back of the dawn in the Odyssey (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 18.240; *Odyssey* 23.243–246; see [[helios]] and [[the-third-day-of-battle]]). The fire from Achilles' head is like the fire Athena kindled from Diomedes' head and shoulders, ἐκ δʼ αὐτοῦ, “from him” (18.206; 5.4–7; see the translation's note on 18.206). When Zeus taunts Hera for rousing Achilles, she answers that a goddess may do what any mortal does to another (18.356–367). See [[aegis]], [[athena]] and [[hera]].
