---
summary: Achilles is “swift-footed Achilles” as he answers his mother and Iris; Iris is “swift-footed” as she goes; Achilles is “swift-footed” following the litter and in Polydamas' fear; and the Trojans unyoke their “swift horses.”
---

**Achilles and his mother.** “Groaning heavily, swift-footed Achilles said to her” (18.78), the verse with which he answered Thetis by the sea in Book 1 (1.364); and “Deeply troubled, swift-footed Achilles said to her” (18.97; [[deeply-troubled]], [[thetis]]).

**Iris.** “Answering her, swift-footed Achilles said” (18.187), and “So saying, swift-footed Iris went away” (18.202; [[iris]], [[wind-footed]]).

**The litter.** “and swift-footed Achilles followed among them / shedding hot tears” (18.234–235; [[hot-tears]]).

**The Trojans.** They “unyoked their swift horses from under the chariots” (18.244; [[horses]]), and Polydamas fears “the swift-footed son of Peleus,” whom night has stopped (18.261, 18.267; [[son-of-peleus]], [[polydamas]]).
