---
summary: Book 18 ends the day. The Achaeans flee to the ships and the Hellespont, Achilles' shout from the ditch turns the Trojans back, and Hera sends the unwilling sun down to the streams of Oceanus (18.148–150, 18.217–231, 18.239–242). The night that follows holds the Trojan assembly, the mourning for Patroclus and the making of the arms; Thetis has promised to return at dawn (18.136).

**The unwilling sun.**

{{quote:55847896-8abd-599d-82fc-4260cd5bd65d}}

The sun goes down ἀέκοντα, “unwilling”, because before its time. Walter Leaf notes that the only other interference of a god with the course of nature in Homer is Athena's holding back of the dawn (*Odyssey* 23.243–246), and adds of this day, which began with Book 11, that the growth of the poem has so loaded it with events that one can hardly call its end premature “without a smile” (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 18.240; see the translation's note on 18.240). Hera, who sent Iris to rouse Achilles without the knowledge of Zeus, ends the Trojans' day herself (18.165–168; see [[hera]] and [[helios]]). The sun is ἀκάμας, “tireless”, as on the shield that Hephaestus makes that night (18.239, 18.484; see [[tireless]]).

**The promise kept and cut short.** Zeus had promised Hector power to kill until he reached the ships and the sun went down (11.192–194), and renewed the promise to the Trojans in Book 17 (17.453–455; see [[plan-of-zeus]]). The Achaeans are driven to the ships and the Hellespont (18.148–150), but the Trojans are turned back at the ditch by Achilles' shout (18.217–231; see [[the-fight-for-the-body-of-patroclus]]), and the sun sets at Hera's command, not of itself. The Achaeans “ceased / from the fierce din of battle and from war, common to all” (18.241–242).

**All day.** Thetis tells Hephaestus that “All day long they fought around the Scaean gates” (18.453). Leaf objects that the fighting at the gates lasted neither all day nor all the rest of the day, as he objected to “all day long” in the fight over the body (17.384), but adds that this is not the place to expect the accuracy of a chronicler (on 18.453; see the translation's note on 18.453).

**The night.** The Trojans unyoke their horses and gather in assembly before supper, standing, because Achilles has appeared (18.243–248; see [[trojans]]). Polydamas says that night has stopped Achilles for now, “the ambrosial night” (18.267–268; see [[war-and-night]] and [[polydamas]]), and Hector orders supper by companies and a watch, and battle at the ships at dawn (18.298–304; see [[night-watch]] and [[hector]]). The Achaeans lament Patroclus all night (18.314–315, 18.354–355; see [[the-death-of-patroclus]]), and Hephaestus makes the armor (18.468–617; see [[the-making-of-the-arms]]). Thetis promised to come back “at dawn … with the rising sun” (18.136), and the next day dawns at 19.1–2.
