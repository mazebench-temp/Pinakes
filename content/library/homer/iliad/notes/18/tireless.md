---
summary: The fire above Achilles' head is “the tireless fire”; Hera sends “the tireless Sun” down before his time; and Hephaestus sets “the tireless sun” on the shield; the book adds the sun's ἀκάμαντα.
grc: ["Ἠέλιον δʼ ἀκάμαντα", "ἠέλιόν τʼ ἀκάμαντα"]
---

**The fire.** “And the charioteers were struck with terror when they saw the tireless fire / blazing terribly above the head of the great-hearted son of Peleus; / the gray-eyed goddess Athena kindled it” (18.225–227; [[gray-eyed]], [[athena]]). ἀκάματον πῦρ, the formula of fire in battle and on the pyre, is the fire of a goddess here.

**The sun.** “Then ox-eyed queen Hera sent the tireless Sun, / unwilling, to go back to the streams of Oceanus” (18.239–240; [[helios]], [[hera]]): the sun that never tires is sent down against its will, before its time (see the translation's note on 18.240). On the shield: “On it he made the earth, on it the sky, on it the sea, / and the tireless sun, and the moon at the full” (18.483–484; [[the-shield-of-achilles]]). The two phrases of the sun are new to the entry; the short form ἀκάμας was said in Book 16 of a river and a boar (16.176, 16.823), and as a name is kept apart from Acamas ([[acamas]]).
