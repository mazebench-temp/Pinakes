---
summary: Hector ends his answer to Polydamas with a maxim: “Enyalius is impartial, and he kills the man who would kill.”
---

“I will stand, and either he will carry off the great mastery, or I might. / Enyalius is impartial, and he kills the man who would kill” (18.308–309; [[hector]], [[ares]]). Walter Leaf takes it of the god of battle, who is impartial and slays him that would slay, and compares Paris' words, “victory shifts from man to man” (6.339; *Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 18.309; [[victory-shifts]]). Aristotle quotes the first words as a maxim (*Rhetoric* 1395a15; see the translation's note on 18.309). In Book 17 the word stood beside Ares, “dread Enyalius” (17.211); here it stands alone, as the name of the god.
