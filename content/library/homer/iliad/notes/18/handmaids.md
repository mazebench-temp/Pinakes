---
summary: Two kinds of handmaid. The slave women whom Achilles and Patroclus took as spoil cry aloud and beat their breasts at the news of Patroclus' death (18.28–31); and Hephaestus' handmaids are “golden ones, like living young women”, with mind, voice and strength, skilled in handiwork from the gods, who support their lame lord as he walks (18.417–421; see [[the-golden-handmaids]]).
---

{{quote:4830ed16-7a51-5d8e-ba77-c204eb47b5b8}}

The golden handmaids are the most living of the god's works, with a mind in their hearts (see [[noos]]). Walter Leaf takes them as a relic of the tradition that gives magical powers to the first workers of metal, and finds their only Homeric likeness in the gold and silver dogs that Hephaestus made to guard the palace of Alcinous (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 18.418; *Odyssey* 7.91–94; see [[the-golden-handmaids]], [[tripod]] and [[hephaestus]]). The slave women of 18.28 are captives (see [[captive-women]] and [[lament]]).
