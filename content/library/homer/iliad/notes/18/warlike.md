---
summary: Iris hopes that “the warlike sons of the Achaeans may draw breath” if Achilles shows himself.
---

“in the hope that in fear of you they may hold off from the war, / the Trojans, and the warlike sons of the Achaeans may draw breath, / worn out as they are; and brief is the breathing space in war” (18.199–201; [[sons-of-the-achaeans]], [[brief-is-the-breathing-space]]). The verses are Nestor's to Patroclus (11.799–801) and Patroclus' to Achilles (16.41–43); Walter Leaf thinks 18.200–201 interpolated here, since Achilles is roused by his friend's body, not by the weariness of the Greeks (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 18.200).
