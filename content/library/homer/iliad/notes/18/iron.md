---
summary: Antilochus “feared that he would cut his throat with the iron” (18.34), a line many critics have rejected.
---

σίδηρος here is a blade, as it is again at 23.30; Bentley and others rejected the line, which changes the subject abruptly and brings in the thought of suicide, unknown elsewhere in the Iliad, and some ancient critics took Antilochus to fear for his own throat (Walter Leaf, *Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 18.34; see the translation's note on 18.34).
