summary: Death is Achilles' choice in the book: “May I die at once”, he answers when Thetis tells him that his death will follow Hector's (18.98), and he will lie still “when I am dead” (18.121). The book opens on the fact, “Surely now the valiant son of Menoetius is dead” (18.12), and Hephaestus wishes he could hide Achilles from “grim-sounding death” as surely as he can arm him (18.464). Thetis was given a mortal husband (18.87, 18.432–434), and Hera argues that even a mortal can accomplish his purpose (18.362–363).
grc: [τέθνηκε, θνητὴν, τεθναίην, θάνω, τεθνηῶτος, θανόντος, κατατεθνηῶτας]
---

αὐτίκα τεθναίην, “may I die at once” (18.98), is the optative of a wish: he would rather die now than live having failed his companion (see [[the-short-life-of-achilles]] and [[the-two-fates-of-achilles]]). The dead man is named by the participle through the fight for the body, νέκυος πέρι τεθνηῶτος, “the corpse of the dead man” (18.173), περὶ Πατρόκλοιο θανόντος, “over Patroclus dead” (18.195). On the Shield the dead are dragged by the feet by Doom and pulled away by the fighters, “like living mortals” (18.537–540; see [[the-two-cities]] and [[ker-doom]]).
