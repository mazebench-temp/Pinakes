---
summary: Hephaestus must pay “Thetis of the lovely tresses” the price for his life; and Daedalus made a dancing floor in Cnossus “for Ariadne of the lovely tresses.”
---

**Thetis.** “And now she has come to our house; so I must indeed / pay Thetis of the lovely tresses the full price for my life” (18.406–407; [[thetis]], [[hephaestus]]).

**Ariadne.** “like the one that once in wide Cnossus / Daedalus fashioned for Ariadne of the lovely tresses” (18.591–592; [[ariadne]], [[daedalus]], [[cnossus]], [[the-dancing-floor-of-ariadne]]). The epithet of goddesses, of Demeter (14.326) and of Thetis, who has it again in Book 20 (20.207), is given to a heroine of Crete.
