summary: Achilles wishes that Peleus had taken “a mortal wife”; Hera says that even a man who “is mortal” accomplishes his purpose; and no one “of mortal men” knew where Hephaestus was hidden; the book adds θνητὴν and θνητός.
grc: [θνητὴν, θνητός]

**Peleus' marriage.** “If only you had stayed there among the immortal goddesses of the sea, / and Peleus had taken a mortal wife!” (18.86–87; [[immortals]], [[thetis-in-the-house-of-peleus]], [[gods-and-mortals]]).

**Hera.** “Surely even a mortal will accomplish his purpose against another man, / though he is mortal and does not know so many counsels” (18.362–363; [[hera]]); how much more, then, the best of goddesses (18.364–367).

**Hephaestus' cave.** “and no one else / knew of it, neither of the gods nor of mortal men, / but Thetis and Eurynome knew, who had saved me” (18.403–405; [[fall-of-hephaestus]], [[eurynome]]).

θνητήν and θνητός are new to the entry. θνητός also catches the man “who was mortal and ate the grain of Demeter,” to whom Ajax would not give way (13.321–322), the horse Pedasus, “mortal though he was” (16.154), and Meriones' answer to Aeneas, “for you too are mortal” (16.622), all on the same divide between mortal and immortal.
