---
summary: Troy's treasures have gone to Phrygia and Maeonia, “possessions sold away, since great Zeus grew angry” (18.292).
grc: [περνάμενʼ]
en: [sold away]
---

πέρνημι is to sell, especially across the sea, as Achilles sold the Trojans he took alive, sons of Priam among them, in the islands across the sea (21.40, 21.102, 22.44–45, 24.751–753); Walter Leaf takes the sale here as perhaps to pay the allies (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 18.292; see [[possessions]] and [[phrygia]]).
