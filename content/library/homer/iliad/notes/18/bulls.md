---
summary: On the Shield two lions seize a bellowing bull at the head of a herd and drag it off lowing, while the dogs and young men go after it in vain (18.579–586).
en: [bull]
---

“But two terrible lions among the foremost cows / had seized a bellowing bull; and he, lowing loudly, / was being dragged off; and the dogs and the young men went after him” (18.579–581). The bull is ἐρύγμηλος, “bellowing”, a word found only here; Walter Leaf derives it from ἐρυγμός, “roaring” (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 18.580; see [[bellowing]]). μακρὰ μεμυκώς, “lowing loudly”, is the sound of a bull: in Book 21 the river Scamander, bellowing like a bull (μεμυκὼς ἠΰτε ταῦρος), will throw out onto the land the dead that Achilles has killed (21.234–238). In the next verses the bull is βοῦς, “the great bull” whose hide the lions tear open before they gulp down its entrails and black blood (18.582–583; see [[cattle]] and [[the-herd-and-the-lions]]).

The scene recalls the lion similes of the earlier books, where a bull is the lion's grandest prey: Sarpedon dies under Patroclus like a bull that a lion kills among the herd (16.487–491), and Automedon mounts his chariot bloody, like a lion that has devoured a bull (17.542; see [[lion-among-cattle]]). On the Shield the scene has no tenor: the bull stands for no warrior, and the herdsmen cannot save it.
