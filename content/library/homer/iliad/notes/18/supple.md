“With them for nine years I forged many intricate designs, / brooches and curved spirals, rosettes and necklaces, / in a hollow cave” (18.400–402; [[fall-of-hephaestus]], [[brooch]], [[necklace]]). The whole verse recurs in the Homeric Hymn to Aphrodite (see the entry), whose author, Walter Leaf thinks, took the words from this passage without a clear idea of their sense (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 18.401); the English renders the usual guesses (see the translation's note on 18.401). ἕλικες here are spirals of jewelry, not the cattle of [[with-curving-horns]]. The accusative γναμπτάς is new to the entry.
