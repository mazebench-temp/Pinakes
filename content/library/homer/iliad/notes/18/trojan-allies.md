---
summary: At Achilles' shout from the ditch, “three times the Trojans and their famous allies were thrown into confusion” (18.229); twelve of their best men die.
---

Τρῶες κλειτοί τʼ ἐπίκουροι, “the Trojans and their famous allies” (18.229), ends the verse as it does when Diomedes tells Glaucus that there are “many Trojans and famous allies” for him to kill (6.227; see [[famous]]); elsewhere the allies are τηλεκλειτοί, “far-famed” (6.111, 9.233, 12.108). The shout that routs them is Achilles' first part in the fighting since he withdrew from it in Book 1 (18.217–231; see [[achilles]] and [[trojans]]).

Hector tells the assembly that night that the treasures of Priam's city have been sold away to Phrygia and Maeonia (18.288–292), the countries of two of the allied peoples (2.862–866; see [[phrygians]] and [[maeonians]]). Walter Leaf thinks they were sold probably to pay the allies, as Hector said in Book 17 that he wore down his own people with gifts and food for them (17.225–226; *Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 18.292; see [[priam]]).
