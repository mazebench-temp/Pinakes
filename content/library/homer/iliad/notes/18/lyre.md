---
summary: Lyres play at the weddings of the city at peace (18.495); at the vintage a boy plays the lyre and sings the Linus song (18.569–571); and a singer sings to the lyre among the dancers (18.604–605).
grc: [φόρμιγγές, κιθάριζε, φορμίζων]
en: [lyres, to the lyre]
---

The singer of 18.604–605 stands in the printed text on the authority of Athenaeus, who says that Aristarchus removed him; Walter Leaf thinks that evidence worthless and the words no part of any recognized text (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 18.604; see the translation's note on 18.604–606 and [[the-singer-on-the-shield]]). The same verses describe the singer at Menelaus' wedding feast in the Odyssey (*Odyssey* 4.17–19). See [[the-linus-song]], [[dance]] and [[clear-toned]].
