---
summary: The sorrow passes between mother and son. Thetis goes to learn “what grief has come upon him” (18.64) and asks him, in the words of Book 1, “What grief has come upon your heart?” (18.73 = 1.362); he answers that the gods gave her to a mortal so that she too might have “countless grief in your heart / for your son who has perished” (18.88–89).
---

Achilles speaks of himself as already dead, παιδὸς ἀποφθιμένοιο, “your son who has perished” (18.89), the word he used of Patroclus (ἔφθιτʼ, 18.100). Walter Leaf discusses whether εἴη (18.88) is an optative of purpose, “it was so that you too might have countless grief”, as the translation takes it, or a subjunctive (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 18.88; see the translation's note on 18.88).
