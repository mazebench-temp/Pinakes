---
summary: Polydamas, son of Panthous, “alone saw before and after” (18.250), the words used of Agamemnon's failure in Book 1 and of the old man's wisdom in Book 3.
---

{{quote:0f6b25c8-9117-59d9-99fa-b3c29a3b12dd}}

The phrase is usually taken of past and future, but Walter Leaf doubts it, since πρόσσω is never temporal in Homer and ὀπίσσω, when temporal, always looks to the future; he takes the words of a man who looks about him on every side, and compares Polydamas' first words, ἀμφὶ μάλα φράζεσθε, “Consider this well on every side” (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 18.250; 18.254; see the translation's note on 18.250). The translation leaves both open. Polydamas' foresight is borne out (see [[foresight]] and [[polydamas]]).
