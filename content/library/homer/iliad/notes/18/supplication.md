summary: Two supplications are told or made. The elders of the Argives begged Achilles in Book 9 (18.448), and Thetis comes to Hephaestus as a suppliant, “That is why I come now to your knees” (18.457).
grc: ["γούναθʼ ἱκάνομαι"]
en: [come now to your knees]
---

τὰ σὰ γούναθʼ ἱκάνομαι (18.457) is a formula of the suppliant in the Odyssey, where Telemachus says it to Nestor and to Menelaus (*Odyssey* 3.92, 4.322). Thetis does not need to clasp Hephaestus' knees: he has already promised to do what she asks before she speaks (18.426–427; see [[gounata]] and [[visit-scene]]). In Book 1 she clasped the knees of Zeus himself for her son (1.500–502).
