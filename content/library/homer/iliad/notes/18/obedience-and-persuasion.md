summary: Two refusals and an obedience. Achilles tells his mother, “you will not persuade me” (18.126), yet obeys her command to wait for the armor (18.189–191, 18.216). In the Trojan assembly Polydamas asks to be obeyed, “do as I say” (18.266, 18.273); Hector answers that no Trojan will obey him, “I will not allow it”, and commands, “Come then, let us all do as I say” (18.296–297). The Trojans approve the worse counsel, because Athena has taken away their wits (18.310–313).

Hector's “let us all do as I say” (ἀλλʼ ἄγεθʼ ὡς ἂν ἐγὼ εἴπω, πειθώμεθα πάντες, 18.297) is the verse with which Agamemnon proposed flight in Book 2 (2.139), and with which Polydamas gave his own advice to Hector at the ditch (12.75); now Hector turns it against him (see [[peitho]]). On the Shield the besieged “would not yet yield” (οὔ πω πείθοντο, 18.513), and Walter Leaf takes it that they were by no means thinking of accepting terms (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 18.513; see [[the-two-cities]]).
