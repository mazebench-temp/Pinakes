**The shield.** Achilles' own armor is with Hector, and his mother has told him not to arm until she brings him new armor from Hephaestus (18.188–191; see [[achilles]] and [[the-armor-of-peleus]]). Of all the armor in the camp he names only one piece that would serve him:

{{quote:276a1a39-f561-5098-becd-69b833064f28}}

Αἴαντός γε σάκος Τελαμωνιάδαο, “the shield of Ajax, son of Telamon” (18.193; see [[the-shield-of-ajax]] and [[telamon]]): Walter Leaf compares the shield “like a tower” with which Ajax came to fight Hector in Book 7 and behind which Teucer shot in Book 8 (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 18.193; 7.219–223, 8.267). And Achilles supposes that Ajax is using the shield himself, “in the throng among the foremost, / cutting men down with his spear over Patroclus dead” (18.194–195). He is right: in Book 17 Ajax covered the body “with his broad shield” and stood over it like a lion over its young (17.132–133; see [[the-lion-and-its-cubs]]).

**Over the body.** In this book he is one of “the two Ajaxes” who three times drive Hector back from the body and cannot frighten him away, until Achilles shouts from the ditch (18.155–164, 18.228–233; see [[the-two-ajaxes]], [[ajax-son-of-oileus]] and [[hector]]). The narrator does not name him alone.
