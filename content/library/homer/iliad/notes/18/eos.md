summary: Book 18 has no dawn of its own: the third day of battle ends at its sunset, and the night that follows lasts to the end of the book. Dawn is what the speakers look to. Polydamas bids the Trojans go into the city and not wait for brilliant Dawn on the plain; Hector would arm early, at daybreak; and Thetis promises to return at dawn with new armor.
passages:
  - 18.254-256 | Polydamas: not to wait for Dawn on the plain
---

**Waiting for Dawn.** The third day of battle, which began with the dawn of Book 11, ends when Hera sends the Sun down early (18.239–242; see [[helios]] and [[the-third-day-of-battle]]), and the rest of the book belongs to the night: the Trojan assembly, the mourning for Patroclus, and the making of the arms. In the Trojan assembly Polydamas counsels: “Consider this well on every side, friends; for I bid you / go now to the city, and not wait for brilliant Dawn / on the plain beside the ships” (18.254–256; see [[polydamas]]). Ἠῶ δῖαν, “brilliant Dawn”, is the Dawn that Hector prayed to see appear with all speed on the night of the embassy (9.240), and that Phoenix lay down to wait for (9.662; see the note on Book 9); now the Trojans are not to wait for it where they are, for if Achilles finds them still there tomorrow, dogs and vultures will eat many of them (18.268–272). Hector rejects him and orders supper, a watch, and battle at the ships “early, at daybreak” (πρῶϊ δʼ ὑπηοῖοι, 18.303), the words of his plan in Book 8 (8.530; see [[hector]]).

**Thetis' dawn.** Thetis tells her son not to enter the battle until she comes back, “for at dawn I will return with the rising sun, / bringing fine armor from lord Hephaestus” (18.136–137; see [[thetis]]). ἠῶθεν, “at dawn”, is the time of day and is not counted to the goddess. The promise is kept at the start of Book 19, where saffron-robed Dawn rises from the streams of Oceanus to bring light to immortals and mortals, and Thetis comes to the ships with the gifts of the god (19.1–3; see [[saffron-robed]] and [[dawn-line]]).
