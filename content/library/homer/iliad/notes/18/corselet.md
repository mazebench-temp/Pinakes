---
summary: Achilles cannot arm, θωρήσσεσθαι, until his mother brings armor (18.167, 18.189), and Thetis asks Hephaestus for a corselet among the rest, since Patroclus lost his (18.460). Hephaestus makes him “a corselet brighter than the light of fire” (18.610). The Trojans plan to arm at daybreak (18.277, 18.303), and on the Shield the besieged are “secretly arming for an ambush” (18.513).
grc: [θώρηχʼ, ὑπεθωρήσσοντο]
---

θωρήσσομαι is to arm for battle in general, without thought of the breastplate, as Walter Leaf notes on Iris' command (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 18.167). The corselet takes one line in the making of the armor, and Leaf reports Reichel's view that the line is a later addition, given how briefly so important a piece is dismissed (on 18.610; see the translation's note on 18.610).
