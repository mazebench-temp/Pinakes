summary: Night ends the third day of battle early: Hera sends the Sun down unwilling, and the Achaeans cease from the fighting (18.239–242). The Trojans debate whether to spend the night on the plain; Polydamas says that night has stopped Achilles and that they should not wait for dawn outside the walls (18.254–268), and Hector orders supper and a watch (18.298–299). The Achaeans spend the night lamenting Patroclus (18.314–355).
refs: [18.239-242, 18.255-256, 18.267-268, 18.274, 18.298-299, 18.314-315, 18.354-355]

“now night has stopped the swift-footed son of Peleus, / the ambrosial night” (18.267–268): Night stops the fighting as it stopped the duel of Hector and Ajax, when the herald Idaeus said, “Night is already coming on; it is good to yield to night too” (7.282; see [[yield-to-night]] and [[ambrosial]]). Hector repeats the orders of his first night on the plain, supper, fires and watch, as in Book 8 (8.502–522; see [[feast]] and [[night-watch]]). The Achaeans' night is a vigil over the dead (see [[lament]]).
