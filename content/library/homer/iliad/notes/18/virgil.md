summary: Virgil's Venus asks Vulcan for arms for Aeneas, naming Thetis as her precedent, and the shield of Aeneas shows the history of Rome where the shield of Achilles shows the world. Virgil also gives Paris an age-mate born on the same night, as Polydamas was born on the same night as Hector.
---

- Venus' request to Vulcan, the forge of the Cyclopes and the shield of Aeneas, after Thetis' visit to Hephaestus and the Shield (*Aeneid* 8.370–453, 8.608–731, on 18.369–617): [[virgil-shield-of-aeneas]].
- **Born on one night.** Polydamas “was Hector's companion, and they were born in the same night, / but the one excelled far in words, the other with the spear” (18.251–252; see [[polydamas]]). Walter Leaf compares Virgil's Mimas, whom Mezentius kills: Paris' age-mate and companion, whom Theano bore to Amycus on the same night that the Cissean queen, pregnant with a firebrand, gave birth to Paris; “Paris lies in his father's city, the Laurentine shore holds Mimas, unknown” (*Aeneid* 10.702–706; *Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 18.251). Leaf adds that the scholia show the different characters of men born on the same night troubling those who looked for astrology in Homer.
