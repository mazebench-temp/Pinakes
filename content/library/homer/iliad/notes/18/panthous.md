---
summary: Panthous is named only in his son's patronymic, when Polydamas, “the son of Panthous”, rises in the Trojan assembly as the one who “alone saw before and after” (18.249–250).
---

Πανθοΐδης (18.250) is Polydamas, who counsels the Trojans to go back into the city before dawn and is refused by Hector (18.249–313; see [[polydamas]]). Of the three sons of Panthous whom the poem names, he is the one still alive: Menelaus has killed Hyperenor and Euphorbus (14.516–519, 17.43–60; see [[hyperenor]], [[euphorbus]] and [[menelaus]]).
