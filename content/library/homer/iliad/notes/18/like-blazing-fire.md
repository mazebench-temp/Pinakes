summary: The verse opens Book 18: “So they fought on like blazing fire; / and Antilochus, swift of foot, came to Achilles as a messenger” (18.1–2). The battle over Patroclus is left burning, and the story turns to the man who brings the news.
passages:
  - 18.1-2 | Like blazing fire
---

ὣς οἳ μὲν μάρναντο δέμας πυρὸς αἰθομένοιο, / Ἀντίλοχος δʼ Ἀχιλῆϊ πόδας ταχὺς ἄγγελος ἦλθε: “So they fought on like blazing fire; / and Antilochus, swift of foot, came to Achilles as a messenger” (18.1–2). The verse is whole again, as at 11.596 and 13.673, after the variant of Book 17, where it broke off to look at the darkness around the body (17.366–367). Its μέν looks forward, and the δέ is Antilochus, whom Menelaus sent running to Achilles near the end of Book 17 (17.685–701; see [[antilochus]]). Walter Leaf notes that here, as in Book 11, the line is properly used to mark the beginning of a new episode (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 18.1). The fight over the body is not seen again until Antilochus has told his news and Thetis has come and gone; then the narrative returns to it, with the Achaeans fleeing to the ships and the Hellespont and Hector catching up with the body again, “like a flame in his courage” (18.148–154; see [[like-a-flame]] and [[the-fight-for-the-body-of-patroclus]]).

The fire of the formula becomes real later in the book. Athena kindles a flame from Achilles' head that blazes up to the upper air (18.205–214; see [[the-beacon-fires]]), and the Trojan charioteers are struck with terror “when they saw the tireless fire” above him (18.225).
