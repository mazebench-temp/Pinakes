summary: The black ash settles on Achilles' fragrant tunic when he hears of Patroclus' death (18.25). Hephaestus puts on a tunic before he goes out to Thetis (18.416), and on the Shield the young men dance in tunics “well-spun, shining softly with oil” (18.595–596).
grc: [χιτῶνι]
---

The tunic of 18.25 is νεκτάρεος, “fragrant”, like Helen's robe (3.385; see [[nectar]] and [[robe]]); the dancers' tunics are ἐϋννήτους, “well-spun” (see [[well-spun]] and [[dance]]).
