---
summary: Achilles at the ditch is “the grandson of Aeacus,” whose voice rings out like a trumpet and whose “bronze voice” the Trojans hear.
---

“so clear then rang out the voice of the grandson of Aeacus. / And when they heard the bronze voice of the grandson of Aeacus, / the hearts of all were shaken” (18.221–223; [[the-trumpet]], [[aeacus]], [[achilles]]). The patronymic is repeated in two successive verses at the moment when Achilles' voice alone turns the battle. Walter Leaf takes χάλκεον as “unwearied,” perhaps with a ringing quality after the trumpet (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 18.222). At 18.433, Αἰακίδῃ Πηλῆϊ, the patronymic is Peleus himself, Aeacus' son, and belongs to [[aeacus]], as at 16.15.
