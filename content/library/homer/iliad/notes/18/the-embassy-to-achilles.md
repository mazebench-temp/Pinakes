**The elders.** Thetis' account of the quarrel comes to the embassy after the girl taken and the Achaeans penned in at the sterns (18.444–448; see [[quarrel-of-achilles-and-agamemnon]]):

“and the elders begged him, / the elders of the Argives, and named many glorious gifts. / Then he himself refused to ward off ruin” (18.448–450).

The first words, τὸν δὲ λίσσοντο γέροντες, are those with which Phoenix told how the elders of the Aetolians begged Meleager to come out and defend them, promising a great gift (9.574–576; see [[the-wrath-of-meleager]]). The embassy of Book 9 was sent from the elders' council in Agamemnon's hut, and it was three envoys who spoke (9.89–181; see [[elders]]); in Thetis' telling it is the elders who beg. Meleager refused “all the more” (ἀναίνετο, 9.585), and Thetis uses the same verb of her son, ἠναίνετο, “refused” (18.450), the word Odysseus used in his report to Agamemnon, that Achilles “rejects you and your gifts” (σὲ δʼ ἀναίνεται ἠδὲ σὰ δῶρα, 9.679). Thetis' verses are one of the places where the later books plainly remember the embassy.

**Ruin not warded off.** λοιγὸν ἀμῦναι, “to ward off ruin”, names the task the poem gives Achilles. In Book 1 Achilles warned of the day “if ever again / there comes a need of me to ward off shameful ruin” (1.340–341); Phoenix made him his son “so that one day you might ward off shameful ruin from me” (9.495); and Patroclus asked what good anyone would have of him “if you do not ward off shameful ruin from the Argives” (16.32). In Thetis' summary the refusal is the whole embassy: she says nothing of his promise to stay until Hector reached the ships (9.650–655), and goes straight on to Patroclus, sent out in his armor (18.451–452; see [[the-death-of-patroclus]]).
