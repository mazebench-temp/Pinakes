---
summary: Iris says the verse a third time when she sends Achilles, unarmed, to the ditch: if the Trojans hold off in fear of him, the worn-out Achaeans may draw breath, “and brief is the breathing space in war” (18.199–201).
---

The two verses are those of Nestor's plan and of Patroclus' plea (11.800–801, 16.42–43). Walter Leaf thinks them interpolated here, from the likeness of 18.199 to 11.799: Achilles is not to be roused by the weariness of the Achaeans but by his friend's body (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 18.200; see the translation's note on 18.200–201).
