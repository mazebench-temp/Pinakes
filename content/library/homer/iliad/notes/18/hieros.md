---
summary: Polydamas warns that whoever escapes Achilles will be glad “to reach sacred Ilios”; on the shield the elders sit “in the sacred circle.”
---

**Ilios.** “for glad will he be to reach sacred Ilios, / whoever escapes, and dogs and vultures will eat many / of the Trojans” (18.270–272; [[troy]], [[polydamas]]).

**The trial.** “and the elders / sat on polished stones in the sacred circle” (18.503–504; [[polished]], [[the-trial-on-the-shield]]). Walter Leaf explains the circle as a semicircular seat of stone, sacred to the administration of justice, and compares the threshing floors that are ἱεραί by their use (5.499) and the place of assembly and justice at the ships, where the altars of the gods stood (11.807–808; *Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 18.504).
