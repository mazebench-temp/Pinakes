---
summary: In the trial on the shield the elders hold “the scepters of the far-voiced heralds.”
---

“and heralds were holding the people back; and the elders / sat on polished stones in the sacred circle, / and held in their hands the scepters of the far-voiced heralds; / with these they then sprang up, and gave their judgments in turn” (18.503–506; [[heralds]], [[scepter]], [[the-trial-on-the-shield]]). Walter Leaf takes the plural σκῆπτρα loosely: the herald's staff was handed to each speaker in turn (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 18.505). The heralds who keep the crowd back are the voice of the assembly; their epithet occurs only here.
