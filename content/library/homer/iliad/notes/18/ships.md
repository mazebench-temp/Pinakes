summary: Achilles waits by the ships, “a burden on the earth” (18.104), and the Achaeans are driven back to them (18.7, 18.150). The Trojans debate whether to spend the night by the ships, and Hector boasts that Zeus has granted him glory there (18.256–260, 18.294, 18.304–305). Thetis' ships are those she sent her son to Troy with (18.58 = 18.439), and the book's first ships are “straight-horned” (18.3).
---

ὀρθοκραιράων, “straight-horned”, is said of ships here, and of cattle on the Shield (18.573): Walter Leaf, with the scholiast, refers it to the stems and sterns that rise like horns (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 18.3; see the translation's note on 18.3 and [[straight-horned]]).
