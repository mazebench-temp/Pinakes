---
summary: In Thetis' account to Hephaestus of Patroclus' last day, “All day long they fought around the Scaean gates”, and the Achaeans would have sacked the city that day had not Apollo killed him (18.453–456).
---

The fight at the gates is the end of Book 16, where Patroclus three times set foot on the angle of the wall and Apollo thrust him back, and Hector held his horses at the Scaean gates until Apollo sent him against Patroclus (16.698–725; see [[patroclus]], [[apollo]] and [[hector]]). Walter Leaf observes that the fighting there lasted neither all day nor all the rest of the day, since the long fight over Patroclus' body came after it, “but this is not the place where we need expect the accuracy of a chronicler” (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 18.453; see [[thetis]]).
