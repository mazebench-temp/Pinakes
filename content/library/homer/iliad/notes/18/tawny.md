---
summary: In the simile of the hungry lion, shepherds cannot drive “a tawny lion” away from a carcass.
---

“And as shepherds of the fields cannot drive a tawny lion / away from a carcass at all, when he is very hungry, / so the two Ajaxes, helmeted men, could not / frighten Hector, son of Priam, away from the body” (18.161–164; [[lion-on-a-carcass]], [[of-the-fields]], [[helmeted]], [[lion]]). Walter Leaf compares the hungry lion of 3.23 and notes that σῶμα may be the body of an animal the lion has killed (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 18.161).
