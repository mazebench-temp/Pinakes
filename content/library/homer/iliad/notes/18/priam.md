---
summary: Priam does not appear in the book. Hector is twice “son of Priam” as he fights for Patroclus' body (18.154, 18.164), and in the Trojan assembly he recalls that all men once spoke of the city of Priam as rich in gold and bronze, whose treasures have now been sold away to Phrygia and Maeonia (18.288–292).

{{quote:1bb5dbfa-3af4-5d9f-bd83-d725c97be20e}}

Hector sets the city's lost wealth against Polydamas' counsel to go back inside its walls (18.285–287; see [[hector]] and [[polydamas]]). Πριάμοιο πόλιν, “the city of Priam” (18.288), is Troy (see [[troy]]). πολύχρυσος πολύχαλκος, “rich in gold, rich in bronze”, is said elsewhere in Homer only of the Trojan Dolon (10.315; see [[rich-in-gold]], [[brazen]] and [[dolon]]). Walter Leaf compares Achilles' words to Priam in Book 24, that he was once said to surpass in wealth and in sons all men from Lesbos to Phrygia and the Hellespont (24.543–548), and Achilles' answer to the embassy: “For nothing to me is worth my life, not all that they say / Ilios possessed, that well-peopled citadel, / in former days, in time of peace, before the sons of the Achaeans came” (9.401–403; *Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 18.288). He finds the five lines confusing in Hector's speech, since the loss of the city's wealth would argue for defense rather than attack, and takes περνάμενα, “sold away”, to mean sold to pay the allies (on 18.288, 18.292), whom Hector said in Book 17 he was feeding and rewarding at his own people's cost (17.225–226; see [[trojan-allies]]). The cause Hector gives is the anger of Zeus (18.292; see [[zeus]]).

The orders he gives next are Priam's in the Trojan assembly of Book 7, “Now take your supper throughout the city, as before, / and remember to keep watch, and let each man stay awake” (7.370–371), with the army in place of the city: “Now take your supper throughout the army, by companies” (18.298–299; see [[night-watch]]). Πριάμοιο πάϊς and Πριαμίδης, “son of Priam”, name Hector at the body of Patroclus, when he comes on “like a flame in his courage” and when the two Ajaxes cannot frighten him away (18.154, 18.164; see [[son-of-priam]] and [[like-a-flame]]).
