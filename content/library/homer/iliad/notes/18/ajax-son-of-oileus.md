# The plural Αἴαντες (18.157) and the dual Αἴαντε (18.163) are claimed by ajax; this Ajax takes the lines by refs, as in Book 17.
refs: [18.157-158, 18.163-164]
---

**Over the body.** The pair who held back the Trojans at the end of Book 17 (17.715–761) fight on at the start of this book: “and three times the two Ajaxes, clothed in furious courage, / drove him back from the body” (18.157–158). But Hector will not be frightened off, “as shepherds of the fields cannot drive a tawny lion / away from a carcass” (18.161–164; see [[the-two-ajaxes]], [[ajax]] and [[lion-on-a-carcass]]), and only Achilles' shout from the ditch ends the fight (18.217–231). Walter Leaf takes 18.153–165 for an account of the fight that cannot be reconciled with the end of Book 17, where Menelaus and Meriones carry the body while the Ajaxes guard the rear (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 18.151).
