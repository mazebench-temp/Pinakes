---
summary: Achilles groans over Patroclus “like a bearded lion / whose cubs a deer hunter has snatched away.”
---

“laying his man-slaying hands on the breast of his companion, / groaning thick and fast, like a bearded lion / whose cubs a deer hunter has snatched away / out of the thick wood” (18.317–320; [[the-lion-and-its-cubs]], [[lion]], [[man-slaying]]). Walter Leaf reports the scholiast's explanation, that lionesses have the finest beard and males the mane, and that the beast here is a lioness, since the male does not rear the cubs; Leaf himself observes that Homer has only λέων for lion and lioness, λέαινα being a later formation (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 18.318).
