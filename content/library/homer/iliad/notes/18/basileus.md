summary: On the Shield a king stands among the reapers on his own domain, “the king in silence, / holding his scepter, stood by the swath, glad at heart” (18.556–557), while his hired laborers reap and his heralds prepare a feast (18.550–560).
grc: [βασιλήϊον]
en: [a king's domain]
---

βασιλήϊον, “a king's domain” (18.550), is the printed reading; most editors adopt the vulgate βαθυλήϊον, “deep with grain”. Walter Leaf holds both possible, and finds βασιλήϊον meaningful after the plowing of the common field: the royal land, reaped by hired laborers, and the king a king in the full sense, his scepter the badge of office (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 18.550; see the translation's note on 18.550). He observes that the Shield mentions no slaves. See [[the-reapers]], [[scepter]], [[silence]], [[kingship-and-authority]] and [[the-shield-of-achilles]].
