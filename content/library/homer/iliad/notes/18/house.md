summary: Two houses frame Thetis' journey. The house of Peleus is the home to which Achilles will not return (18.60, 18.331, 18.441), and Peleus lies in his halls in old age (18.435). The house of Hephaestus is “imperishable, starry, standing out among the immortals, / of bronze, which the crook-footed god had built for himself” (18.369–371), where Thetis is received (18.385–424). Menoetius heard Achilles' promise in his halls (18.325), the Nereids go home to their father's house (18.141), and Troy's treasures have gone from its houses (18.290). On the Shield brides are led from their chambers (18.492).

Walter Leaf takes ἀστερόεντα as “adorned with star-like ornaments”, and χάλκεον as an allusion to walls coated with polished metal, or simply to the Homeric custom of making the things of the gods of metal (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 18.370; see the translation's note on 18.370 and [[visit-scene]]). The tripods Hephaestus is making will go to the assembly of the gods and come back “to his house” (18.377; see [[tripod]]).
