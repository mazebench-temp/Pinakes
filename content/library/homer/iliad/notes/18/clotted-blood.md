---
summary: Achilles has a tripod set on the fire to wash “the clotted blood” from Patroclus.
---

“to set a great tripod over the fire, so that with all speed / they might wash the clotted blood from Patroclus” (18.344–345; [[bloody]], [[bathing]], [[honoring-the-dead]]). The Trojans and Achaeans washed the clotted blood from their dead in the truce of Book 7 (7.425), and Hecamede warmed a bath to wash it from Machaon (14.6–7). In Book 23 a great tripod is set over the fire in the same words for Achilles, who refuses to wash the clotted blood from himself until Patroclus is burned (23.39–44).
