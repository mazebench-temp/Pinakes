summary: Two kinds of bed. Thetis was put “into the bed of a mortal man” against her will, and endured “the bed of a man” (18.85, 18.433–434); and the dead Patroclus is laid on a bier (λέχεα, 18.233, 18.352), and carried on a litter (φέρτρον, 18.236).
grc: [λεχέεσσι, "ἀνέρος εὐνὴν"]
en: [bier]
---

The plural λέχεα is a bed or bier on which the dead are laid out, here and for Hector at the end of the poem (24.720; see [[honoring-the-dead]]); φέρτρον, the litter on which Patroclus lies when Achilles sees him, occurs only here in Homer (18.236). The marriage bed of Thetis is the bed that Zeus gave her to share with Peleus: “Out of all the other goddesses of the sea he subdued me to a man, / Peleus, son of Aeacus, and I endured the bed of a man, / much against my will” (18.432–434; see [[marriage]], [[thetis-in-the-house-of-peleus]] and [[gods-and-mortals]]).
