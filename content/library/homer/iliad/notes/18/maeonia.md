# Μῃονίην (18.291), the only accusative in Homer; the maeonians' pattern excludes Μῃονίη*.
grc: [Μῃονίην]
---

{{quote:1bb5dbfa-3af4-5d9f-bd83-d725c97be20e}}

Μῃονίην ἐρατεινήν, “lovely Maeonia” (18.291), has the epithet it had when Helen named it beside Phrygia, Μῃονίης ἐρατεινῆς (3.401; see [[lovely]] and [[phrygia]]): both times the two inland countries are named together, as the rich lands beyond Troy. The Maeonians fight among the allies of Troy under Mesthles and Antiphus (2.864–866; see [[maeonians]] and [[trojan-allies]]), and Walter Leaf thinks the treasures were sold to pay the allies (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 18.292; see [[hector]] and [[priam]]).
