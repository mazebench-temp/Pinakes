summary: Achilles will not come home. Thetis laments that she will not welcome him again “returning home to the house of Peleus” (18.59–60 = 18.440–441), and he answers that she will not, since he no longer wishes to live unless Hector dies (18.89–93); he is not going home to his native land (18.101), and he tells the dead Patroclus that neither of them will (18.330–332). Patroclus himself did not come home from the war on which Achilles sent him (18.237–238), though Achilles had promised his father to bring him back to Opus (18.324–327).
grc: [νέομαι]
# νέεσθαι (18.240): the Sun goes down to the streams of Oceanus, not a homecoming.
except: [18.240]
---

The same verb, ὑποδέχομαι, “welcome”, marks each lost return: Thetis “shall not welcome him again” (18.59, 18.440), Peleus and Thetis will not welcome him in their halls (18.330–332), and Achilles “did not welcome him home again”, the friend he had sent into the war (18.238; see [[the-short-life-of-achilles]] and [[the-two-fates-of-achilles]]). The promise to Menoetius was a homecoming with spoils (18.326–327; see [[menoetius]], [[opus]] and [[zeus-does-not-fulfill-all-plans]]).
