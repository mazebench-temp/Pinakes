---
summary: Achilles' armor is now Hector's. Antilochus' message ends with it (18.21), Achilles tells his mother that Hector stripped it from Patroclus (18.82–85), and Thetis that Hector wears it and glories in it, but not for long (18.130–133). Achilles has no armor of his own to go into the battle with (18.188–193), and Thetis tells Hephaestus how he put it on Patroclus, who lost it (18.451, 18.460–461).
refs: [18.21, 18.82-85, 18.130-133, 18.188, 18.197, 18.451, 18.460-461]
---

Thetis' words are a prophecy: Hector “wears it on his own shoulders and glories in it; but I say / he will not exult in it long, since his death is near him” (18.132–133). The armor that Peleus received from the gods will be replaced by armor from a god's own hand (18.84–85, 18.457–467; see [[the-armor-of-peleus]], [[stripping-the-dead]] and [[the-shield-of-achilles]]). Achilles thinks of the only other armor that might fit him, the shield of Ajax (18.192–193; see [[the-shield-of-ajax]]).
