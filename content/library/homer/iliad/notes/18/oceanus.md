---
summary: Oceanus bounds the day and the world. Hera sends the unwilling Sun down to his streams to end the battle; Hephaestus names Eurynome, who with Thetis saved him, as the daughter of “backward-flowing Oceanus”, and remembers the cave around which his stream flowed; and on the shield of Achilles the Bear alone has no share in his baths, and the great river runs around the outermost rim.
passages:
  - 18.607-608 | Oceanus on the rim of the shield
---

**The sunset.** “Then ox-eyed queen Hera sent the tireless Sun, / unwilling, to go back to the streams of Oceanus” (18.239–240; see [[helios]] and [[hera]]). The Sun rises out of Oceanus and sets in it: on the day of the burial truce he was just striking the fields, “rising out of gently flowing, deep-streaming Oceanus” (7.421–422), and at the end of the second day of battle his bright light fell into Oceanus (8.485). The streams of Oceanus are where the cranes fly in Book 3 (3.5), and the dawn of the next day rises from them (19.1; see [[eos]]).

**The father of Eurynome.** Hephaestus tells Charis that Thetis and Eurynome took him in when he fell, “Eurynome, daughter of backward-flowing Oceanus” (18.399; see [[eurynome]] and [[hephaestus]]). ἀψόρροος is found again only at the same verse end in the Odyssey (*Odyssey* 20.65); Walter Leaf reports that the ancients explained it as flowing back into himself, since he surrounds the earth in a circle, and that others have seen in it a vague rumor of the tide (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 18.399; see [[backward-flowing]] and the translation's note on 18.399). For nine years he worked for the two goddesses in a hollow cave, “and around it the stream of Oceanus / flowed boundless, seething with foam” (18.402–403; see [[fall-of-hephaestus]]). ῥόος Ὠκεανοῖο, “the stream of Oceanus”, is where the Harpy Podarge grazed in the meadow when she bore the horses of Achilles (16.150–151; see [[podarge]]).

**The baths of Oceanus.** On the shield of Achilles the constellations end with the Bear, “which turns in its place and watches Orion, / and alone has no share in the baths of Oceanus” (18.488–489 = *Odyssey* 5.274–275). The stars set by bathing in Oceanus, as Sirius rises bright “when it has bathed in Oceanus” (5.6; Leaf on 18.489). For the verse in Aristotle's *Poetics* see [[alone-without-a-share]] and the translation's note on 18.486–489.

**Around the shield.** The last of the scenes on the shield is the river itself: “And on it he set the great strength of the river Oceanus / along the outermost rim of the strongly made shield” (18.607–608; see [[the-shield-of-achilles]]). Leaf comments that Oceanus surrounds the pictured world as he surrounds the real one (on 18.608), and the shield began with the earth, the sky and the sea (18.483). Oceanus is a river, ποταμός, as Sleep calls him when Hera asks his help (14.245–246), and Achilles will name “the great strength” of Oceanus, μέγα σθένος Ὠκεανοῖο, as no match for Zeus (21.195).
