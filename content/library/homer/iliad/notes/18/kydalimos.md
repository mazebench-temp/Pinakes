---
summary: Achilles “groaned in his glorious heart” while Antilochus held his hands.
---

“And Antilochus on the other side mourned, letting tears fall, / holding the hands of Achilles; and he groaned in his glorious heart; / for he feared that he would cut his throat with the iron” (18.32–34; [[antilochus]], [[achilles]]). The verse-end κυδάλιμον κῆρ is the heart of Agamemnon groaning at night (10.16) and of Hector, which never feared (12.45). The Greek changes its subject without naming it, and the English keeps the ambiguity (see the translation's note on 18.34).
