summary: Polydamas proposes a retreat: the Trojans should go into the city tonight and fight from the towers at dawn, since Achilles will not stay on the plain (18.254–283). Hector calls it shameful to be penned in the city again and will not flee from Achilles (18.285–309), and the Trojans cheer him (18.310–313).

Polydamas argues from what Achilles will do: he will roam the plain beneath the city with his horses and go back to the ships, and the dogs will eat him before he sacks it (18.280–283). Hector answers with the city's poverty, its treasures sold away since great Zeus grew angry, and with his own success: Zeus has granted him glory at the ships (18.285–296; see [[possessions]] and [[kydos]]). He will not flee: “I at least / will not flee from him out of grim-sounding war, but face to face / I will stand” (18.306–308; see [[victory-shifts]]). The narrator gives the verdict on the debate, “fools; for Pallas Athena had taken away their wits” (18.311; see [[nepios]]). Hector will remember the advice when he stands alone before the gates (22.99–103; see [[polydamas]] and [[foresight]]).
