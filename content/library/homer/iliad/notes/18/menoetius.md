**The promise.** Achilles speaks among the Myrmidons over the body (18.323; see [[achilles]] and [[patroclus]]). ἥρωα Μενοίτιον, “the hero Menoetius” (18.325; see [[heros]]), is how Nestor named him when he told Patroclus how he and Odysseus came to Peleus' house to gather the army, “And there we found the hero Menoetius within” (11.771), with Patroclus and Achilles; there Menoetius charged his son to give Achilles good counsel (11.785–789; see [[nestor]] and [[peleus]]). The promise was to bring Patroclus home “when he had sacked Ilios and won his share of the spoil” (18.327; see [[division-of-spoils]]). Walter Leaf infers from it that Patroclus' exile from Opus for the killing of a boy (23.85–88) was only for a time, settled after a while by blood-money (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 18.326; see [[opus]], [[exile-and-refuge]] and [[poine]]). ἅλιον ἔπος, “a vain word” (18.324), is followed by its reason: “But Zeus does not fulfill for men all their plans” (18.328), and both of them are fated to redden the same earth at Troy (18.329–330).

**The son's titles.** Achilles, guessing the truth, says “Surely now the valiant son of Menoetius is dead” (18.12), the verse-end that names Patroclus throughout the poem (11.605, 16.307, 16.626); he wants Hector to pay “for making Patroclus, son of Menoetius, his prey” (18.93); and Thetis tells Hephaestus that Apollo killed “the valiant son of Menoetius” (18.455; see [[son-of-menoetius]]).
