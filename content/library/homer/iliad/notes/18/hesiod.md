---
summary: The Hesiodic Shield of Heracles shares four verses with the city at war on the shield of Achilles and has the same plan. The Theogony names the Nereids in a longer list, makes Eurynome the mother of the Graces and Aglaea the wife of Hephaestus, and has Book 18's half-verses for the crowned sky and “backward-flowing Oceanus”; the Works and Days sets the farmer's year by “the Pleiades and the Hyades and the strength of Orion”.
---

- The *Shield of Heracles*, whose shield, made by Hephaestus, has the powers of battle, a city at war and a city at peace, the work of the fields and Oceanus around the rim; its verses 156–159 are 18.535–538: [[shield-of-heracles]].
- **The Nereids.** The *Theogony* names the fifty daughters of Nereus and Doris (*Theogony* 240–264). Many of the names in Book 18's catalogue are among them, Glauce, Thoe, Halia, Cymothoe, Speio, Melite, Agave, Doto, Proto, Pherusa, Dynamene, Nesaea, Actaea, Doris, Galateia, Cymodoce and Nemertes, and others are not (18.39–48). The catalogue was athetized in antiquity as Hesiodic in character, and Walter Leaf thinks its names were chosen from Hesiod's longer list (see the translation's note on 18.39–49 and [[nereids]]).
- **Eurynome and the Graces.** Eurynome, who with Thetis took in Hephaestus when his mother threw him out (18.398–405), is in the *Theogony* one of the daughters of Oceanus and Tethys (358, 362), and the mother by Zeus of the three Graces, Aglaea, Euphrosyne and Thalia (907–909). Hephaestus married Aglaea, “the youngest of the Graces” (945–946), where the Iliad gives him Charis for his wife (18.382–383). See [[eurynome]] and [[charis-wife-of-hephaestus]].
- **Backward-flowing Oceanus.** The half-verse that names Eurynome, “daughter of backward-flowing Oceanus” (θυγάτηρ ἀψορρόου Ὠκεανοῖο, 18.399), names Styx in the *Theogony*, “dread Styx, daughter of backward-flowing Oceanus, / the eldest” (776–777; see [[backward-flowing]]).
- **The crowned sky.** The verse-end of the constellations, “with which the sky is crowned” (τά τʼ οὐρανὸς ἐστεφάνωται, 18.485), ends the *Theogony*'s verse on the shining stars that Dawn bore to Astraeus (382). Zenodotus read ἐστήρικται (see the translation's note on 18.485).
- **Hephaestus' work.** Pandora's golden crown in the *Theogony* is one that Hephaestus made himself, αὐτὸς ποίησε περικλυτὸς Ἀμφιγυήεις (*Theogony* 579), with the words in which “the renowned god, lame in both legs” makes the pasture on the Shield (ποίησε περικλυτὸς ἀμφιγυήεις, 18.587). The crown has δαίδαλα πολλά, “many intricate designs”, as the Shield has (*Theogony* 581; 18.482), and its beasts of land and sea are “like living things with voices” (584), as Hephaestus' golden handmaids are “like living young women” (18.418; see [[hephaestus]]).
- **The farmer's stars.** The verse of the constellations, “the Pleiades and the Hyades and the strength of Orion” (18.486), is in the *Works and Days* with Ὠαρίωνος for Ὠρίωνος: when the Pleiades, the Hyades and the strength of Orion set, it is time to remember the plowing (*Works and Days* 614–617). Hesiod's harvest and plowing are timed by the Pleiades, his threshing and vintage by Orion (383–384, 597–598, 609–611); on the Shield the stars come first, and then the plowing, the harvest and the vintage (18.541–572; see [[pleiades]] and [[hyades]]).
