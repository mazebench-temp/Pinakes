summary: The book's women mourn, are fought for, and keep the walls. The slave women of Achilles mourn Patroclus (18.28–31), and Achilles promises captive Trojan women to weep for him (18.339–342) and imagines Trojan women groaning for their dead (18.122–124); Polydamas warns that Achilles will fight “for our city and our women” (18.265). On the Shield the wives, children and old men guard the wall of the besieged city (18.514–515), and the women of the city at peace watch the weddings from their doors (18.495–496).
refs: [18.122-124, 18.265, 18.339-342, 18.514-515]
---

The guarded wall on the Shield answers the walls of Troy, where the old men sat watching the war and Andromache ran to the tower when she heard that the Trojans were hard pressed (3.146–153, 6.386–389; see [[the-two-cities]], [[captive-women]] and [[lament]]).
