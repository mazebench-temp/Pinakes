summary: The cattle of Book 18 are on the Shield. The ambushers of the city at war cut off the besiegers' herds at a watering place (18.523–530); an ox is sacrificed for the reapers' meal on the king's domain (18.559); and a herd of straight-horned cattle of gold and tin goes lowing out to pasture, where two lions seize a bull at its head (18.573–586).

**Ox-eyed.** Hera is βοῶπις, “ox-eyed”, three times in the book (18.239, 18.357, 18.360), and so is the Nereid Halia in the catalogue of Thetis' sisters (18.40; see [[ox-eyed]] and [[nereids]]). The book's first verses give the cattle's horns to ships: Antilochus finds Achilles “in front of the straight-horned ships”, ὀρθοκραιράων, the epithet of the herd on the Shield (18.3, 18.573; see [[straight-horned]]).

**The raid.** In the city at war the besieged lay an ambush in a river “where there was a watering place for all the herds”, and two lookouts watch for “the sheep and the cattle with curving horns” (18.520–524; see [[with-curving-horns]]). The herds come with two herdsmen playing their pipes, and the ambushers “cut off the herds of cattle and the fine flocks / of white sheep, and killed the shepherds as well” (18.528–529). The besiegers hear “the great din among the cattle” and ride to the rescue, and a battle is fought on the river's banks (18.530–534; see [[the-two-cities]] and [[raiding]]). Cattle are what men fight over: in Book 1 Achilles said that he had no quarrel with the Trojans, who “never drove off my cattle or my horses” (1.152–154).

**The sacrifice.** On the king's domain, while the hired laborers reap, the heralds under an oak “had sacrificed a great ox and were busy over it” for the meal (18.558–559; see [[the-kings-harvest]] and [[feast]]).

**The herd.** “And on it he made a herd of straight-horned cattle; / the cows were made of gold and of tin, / and lowing they hurried out of the dung-yard to the pasture” (18.573–575). Four golden herdsmen walk with them and nine dogs follow, and at the head of the herd two lions seize a bellowing bull, “the great bull” whose hide they tear (18.576–583; see [[bulls]], [[lion]], [[dogs]] and [[the-herd-and-the-lions]]). The herd's colors are its metals, gold and tin, the materials Hephaestus cast into the fire (18.474–475; see [[tin]] and [[gold-and-silver]]).
