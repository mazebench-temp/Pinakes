summary: Peleus is far away in his halls and old, but his marriage runs through the book. Achilles tells his mother that the armor Hector took was the gods' gift to Peleus on the day they put her in a mortal's bed, and wishes that Peleus had married a mortal; Thetis twice says that she will not welcome her son home to the house of Peleus, and tells Hephaestus that Zeus subdued her to Peleus, son of Aeacus, who now lies worn out by old age; and Achilles knows that the old horseman will not see him come home (18.59–60, 18.82–87, 18.330–332, 18.432–441). Seven times his son is named after him.
# Πηλήϊον (δόμον Πηλήϊον "the house of Peleus", 18.60 = 18.441) and the dative Αἰακίδῃ ("Peleus, son of Aeacus",
# 18.433) are found only in these verses in Homer. The genitive Αἰακίδαο (18.221, 18.222) is Achilles.
grc: [Πηλήϊον, Αἰακίδῃ]
refs: [18.85, 18.434-435]
---

**The gods' gift.** Hector has stripped Patroclus of the armor, “the splendid gifts that the gods gave to Peleus / on the day when they put you into the bed of a mortal man” (18.84–85; see [[the-armor-of-peleus]] and [[thetis-in-the-house-of-peleus]]). The same words, “the splendid gifts that the gods gave to Peleus”, were said of the immortal horses (16.381, 16.867), and Achilles will use them to Priam of all the gods' gifts to his father (24.534). The marriage is the root of his mother's grief, and he wishes it undone: “If only you had stayed there among the immortal goddesses of the sea, / and Peleus had taken a mortal wife!” (18.86–87; see [[thetis]] and [[achilles]]).

**The house of Peleus.** Thetis, lamenting among the Nereids and again before Hephaestus: “but I shall not welcome him again / returning home to the house of Peleus” (18.59–60 = 18.440–441; see [[nostos]]). δόμον Πηλήϊον, “the house of Peleus”, uses an adjective of the name found only in these two verses. Achilles knows it too: “since I too shall not come home / to be welcomed in his halls by the old horse-driving Peleus, / nor by my mother Thetis” (18.330–332; see [[horse-driving]]). γέρων ἱππηλάτα Πηλεύς, “the old horse-driving Peleus”, is how Nestor and Phoenix name him when they speak of him (7.125, 9.438, 11.772; see [[nestor]] and [[phoenix]]).

**Son of Aeacus.** To Hephaestus Thetis tells the marriage from her side: “Out of all the other goddesses of the sea he subdued me to a man, / Peleus, son of Aeacus, and I endured the bed of a man, / much against my will. And he now, by grievous old age / lies worn out in his halls” (18.432–435; see [[aeacus]] and [[hephaestus]]). Αἰακίδης, “son of Aeacus”, is given to Peleus himself in the nominative at 16.15 and 21.189 (see [[grandson-of-aeacus]]). ἀρημένος, “worn out”, is otherwise found only in the Odyssey (Walter Leaf, *Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 18.435). In the next book Achilles imagines his father dead already, or barely alive in hateful old age, always waiting for the grievous news of his son's death (19.334–337).

**The patronymic.** Achilles is named by his father seven times in the book: Πηλέος υἱέ (18.18), Πηλεΐων (18.166, 18.226, 18.261, 18.267) and Πηλεΐδης (18.170, 18.316; see [[son-of-peleus]]). Two of them are the Trojans' fear, in Polydamas' speech: “But now I am terribly afraid of the swift-footed son of Peleus” (18.261), and “now night has stopped the swift-footed son of Peleus” (18.267; see [[polydamas]]).
