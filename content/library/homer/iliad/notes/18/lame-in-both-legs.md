grc: ["κλυτὸς ἀμφιγυήεις"]
en: [famous god, lame in both legs]
---

**At home.** Charis is “the lovely one, whom the renowned god, lame in both legs, had married” (18.383; [[charis-wife-of-hephaestus]]). “Then the renowned god, lame in both legs, answered her” (18.393), and the same verse introduces his answer to Thetis (18.462; [[hephaestus]]).

**The shield.** “And on it the renowned god, lame in both legs, made a pasture” (18.587), and “And on it the renowned god, lame in both legs, inlaid a dancing floor” (18.590; [[the-dancing-floor-of-ariadne]], [[the-shield-of-achilles]]). Last, “And when the famous god, lame in both legs, had made all the armor, / he lifted it and set it before the mother of Achilles” (18.614–615). The phrase κλυτὸς ἀμφιγυήεις is new to the entry. The book shows the lameness that the epithet names: the god limps as he rises from the anvil, leaning on a staff and on his golden handmaids (18.410–421; [[crook-footed]], [[the-golden-handmaids]]).
