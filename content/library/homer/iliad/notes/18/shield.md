---
summary: The shield Hephaestus makes first is “great and heavy”, worked all over, with a shining rim, threefold and gleaming, a silver strap and five layers, and on it he puts “many intricate designs” (18.478–482, 18.609); its outermost rim carries the river Oceanus (18.607–608). Thetis asks for a shield first of all (18.458), and Achilles, without armor, could have used only the shield of Ajax (18.193).
grc: [σάκεος, ἄντυγα, πτύχες]
en: [rim, layers]
---

The shield is a σάκος, the great body shield, as Ajax's is (see [[the-shield-of-ajax]]); Thetis' word is ἀσπίς (18.458). Walter Leaf takes the five πτύχες as layers of hide, from the shield of Ajax (7.247), and the threefold rim as a real part of the shield rather than an ornament: perhaps three of the layers turned up to make it, or three metal bands (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 18.480–481; see the translation's note on 18.480). What Hephaestus puts on it is told under [[the-shield-of-achilles]]; the strap is silver (18.480; see [[gold-and-silver]]).
