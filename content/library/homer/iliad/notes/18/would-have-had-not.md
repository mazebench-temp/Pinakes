summary: Twice the fight over the body comes to the edge of another outcome. The Achaeans would not have drawn Patroclus out of the missiles, and Hector “would have dragged him off and won untold glory, / had not swift Iris” come to Achilles (18.151–168); and in Thetis' account the Achaeans “would have sacked the city, had not Apollo” killed Patroclus (18.454–456). Hephaestus would have suffered sorrows after his fall, had not Eurynome and Thetis taken him in (18.397–398).
grc: ["οὐδέ κε Πάτροκλόν", "τότʼ ἂν πάθον"]
en: [Nor would, would have suffered]
---

The first sentence is long: οὐδέ κε (18.151) waits for its condition through the whole struggle of Hector and the two Ajaxes until εἰ μή at 18.166, and Walter Leaf finds the reference of κε obscure, since the natural condition stands so far off (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 18.151; see the translation's note on 18.151–165). Thetis' version makes Patroclus' death the turning point of the war: on that very day Troy would have been sacked (18.454; see [[retelling]] and [[apollo]]).
