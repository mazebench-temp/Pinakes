---
summary: Polydamas and Hector were born on the same night, “but the one excelled far in words, the other with the spear” (18.252).
---

{{quote:0f6b25c8-9117-59d9-99fa-b3c29a3b12dd}}

The verb is ἐνίκα, “won, excelled”: the two men divide between them the two fields of a hero's excellence, which Phoenix was sent to teach Achilles together (9.443; see [[speaker-of-words-doer-of-deeds]] and [[different-gifts]]). Walter Leaf compares Virgil's Mimas, born on the same night as Paris (*Aeneid* 10.702–705), and notes from the scholia that the different characters of two men born under the same stars troubled those who looked for astrology in Homer (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 18.251).
