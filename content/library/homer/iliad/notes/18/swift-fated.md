---
summary: Thetis tells Achilles, “Then you will be swift-fated, my child,” and asks Hephaestus for armor for “my swift-fated son”; the book adds ὠκυμόρῳ.
grc: [ὠκυμόρῳ]
---

**The choice.** “Then you will be swift-fated, my child, from what you say; / for straight after Hector your own fate is ready” (18.95–96; [[death-of-achilles-foretold]], [[the-two-fates-of-achilles]]). It is her word for him in Book 1 (1.417, 1.505), and now it has a date: Hector's death.

**The armor.** “That is why I come now to your knees, in the hope that you may be willing / to give my swift-fated son a shield and a helmet” (18.457–458; [[hephaestus]], [[supplication]]). The dative ὠκυμόρῳ is new to the entry. Aristarchus read υἱεῖ ἐμʼ ὠκυμόρῳ, with a crasis that has no parallel in Homer (see the translation's note on 18.458).
