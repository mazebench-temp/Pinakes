**The rescuers.** Eurynome is named only in Hephaestus' answer to Charis, when he tells her who Thetis is (18.394–409; see [[hephaestus]] and [[charis-wife-of-hephaestus]]): she saved him when he had fallen far by the will of his mother, who wished to hide him away because he was lame, “had not Eurynome and Thetis taken me to their bosom, / Eurynome, daughter of backward-flowing Oceanus” (18.396–399; see [[thetis]], [[oceanus]], [[backward-flowing]] and [[fall-of-hephaestus]]). The verb is singular with the two names (ὑπεδέξατο), as Walter Leaf notes, comparing 1.255 (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 18.398); and the repetition of her name at the start of the next verse is so unusual that Leaf thinks Payne Knight right to expel 18.399 as a gloss (on 18.399; see the translation's note on 18.399).

**Nine years in the cave.** “With them for nine years I forged many intricate designs, / brooches and curved spirals, rosettes and necklaces, / in a hollow cave; and around it the stream of Oceanus / flowed boundless, seething with foam; and no one else / knew of it, neither of the gods nor of mortal men, / but Thetis and Eurynome knew, who had saved me” (18.400–405; see the translation's note on 18.401). Thetis has her own cave in the sea, the silver-white cave where she lamented with the Nereids (18.50; see [[nereids]]), and in Book 24 Iris will find her “in a hollow cave” among the goddesses of the sea (24.83). This Eurynome is not mentioned again in Homer; Hesiod makes her, “daughter of Ocean”, the mother of the Graces by Zeus (*Theogony* 907–909; see [[graces]]).
