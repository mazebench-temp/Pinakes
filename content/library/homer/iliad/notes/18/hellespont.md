---
summary: Fleeing before Hector with a wondrous shout, the Achaeans reach the ships and the Hellespont (18.148–150), the limit that Zeus had set for the Trojan advance.
---

“but the Achaeans, / with a wondrous shout, before man-slaying Hector, / in their flight reached the ships and the Hellespont” (18.148–150; see [[hector]]). The last verse answers, nearly word for word, Zeus' order to Apollo in Book 15, to rouse Hector until the Achaeans “in their flight reach the ships and the Hellespont” (15.232–233), after which Zeus himself would devise how the Achaeans might draw breath from their toil (15.234–235; see [[zeus]] and [[apollo]]). The respite comes now through Hera, who sends Iris to Achilles in secret from Zeus (18.165–168; see [[hera]] and [[iris]]), and Iris bids him show himself at the ditch so that the sons of the Achaeans “may draw breath” (18.198–201; see [[achilles]]).
