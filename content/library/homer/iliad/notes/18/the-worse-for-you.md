---
summary: The Trojans use the threat of Achilles. Polydamas: “and the worse for him, if he wants / to come from the ships and fight us around the wall” (18.278–279); Hector: “the worse it will be for him, if he wants it so” (18.306). Both say ἄλγιον, “more painful”, where Agamemnon and Zeus in Book 1 said ῥίγιον.
grc: ["τῷ δʼ ἄλγιον", "ἄλγιον αἴ κʼ ἐθέλῃσι"]
en: [the worse it will be for him]
---

Walter Leaf renders αἴ κʼ ἐθέλῃσι at 18.306 “if he is determined to have it so” (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 18.306). The two speakers aim the same words at the same man for opposite counsels, Polydamas to stay behind the walls and Hector to stand on the plain (see [[retreat]] and [[victory-shifts]]).
