---
summary: Antilochus tells Achilles “his grievous news,” and the Trojans hold their assembly standing because Achilles, “who had long ceased from grievous battle,” has appeared; the book adds ἀλεγεινήν.
grc: [ἀλεγεινήν]
---

“the son of noble Nestor came near him, / shedding hot tears, and told his grievous news” (18.16–17; [[noble]], [[hot-tears]]). The Trojans gather without sitting, “for trembling held them all, because Achilles / had appeared, who had long ceased from grievous battle” (18.247–248; [[assembly]], [[trojans]]). The accusative ἀλεγεινήν is new to the entry. The news itself is λυγρή, “grievous,” in Antilochus' words (18.18–19; [[grim]]).
