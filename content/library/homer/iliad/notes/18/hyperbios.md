---
summary: Polydamas fears Achilles for “his overweening spirit,” which will not let him stay on the plain.
---

“But now I am terribly afraid of the swift-footed son of Peleus; / such is his overweening spirit, he will not be willing / to stay on the plain, where the Trojans and the Achaeans, / both, in the middle ground, share between them the fury of Ares; / but he will fight for our city and our women” (18.261–265; [[polydamas]], [[thymos]], [[achilles]]). Walter Leaf takes οἷος as giving in advance the reason for “he will not be willing,” as in the same verse of the Odyssey (*Odyssey* 15.212; *Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 18.262).
