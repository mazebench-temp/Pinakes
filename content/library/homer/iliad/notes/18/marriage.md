summary: Three marriages of the gods and the weddings of the Shield. Thetis was put “into the bed of a mortal man”, and Achilles wishes Peleus had married a mortal wife (18.85–87, 18.432–434); Hera is Zeus' glorious wife and claims the title (18.184, 18.356, 18.365–366); Charis is the wife of Hephaestus (18.382–383). In the city at peace there are weddings, with brides led through the streets by torchlight and the bridal song (18.491–496), and in the city at war the wives guard the wall (18.514).

{{quote:2cb8171e-c572-5ebb-a227-01b4a0d6fd15}}

The wedding on the Shield is the Iliad's only picture of the rite: the brides are led from their chambers through the city by torchlight, with song, music and dancing, while the women watch from their doors. ὑμέναιος, “the bridal song”, occurs only here in Homer (18.493). Walter Leaf takes the plurals γάμοι and νύμφας as indefinite, a marriage scene in which one bride stands for many (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 18.491), and notes that Zenodotus read ἐς θαλάμους, “into their chambers”, for ἐκ θαλάμων (on 18.492). The maidens of the dance are ἀλφεσίβοιαι, worth many oxen to a suitor (18.593; see [[bringing-in-oxen]] and [[bride-price]]). See [[the-two-cities]], [[dance]] and [[husbands-and-wives]].
