“Truly then a goddess I fear and respect is in our house, / who saved me when pain came on me after I had fallen far” (18.394–395; [[thetis]], [[fall-of-hephaestus]], [[aidos]]). The epithet of fear is joined to αἰδοίη, “respected,” the word with which Charis and Hephaestus greet Thetis (18.386, 18.425; [[long-robed]]); Helen gave Priam the same pair: “You are one I respect, dear father-in-law, and one I fear” (αἰδοῖός … δεινός τε, 3.172). Here it is the fear of a debtor: Hephaestus owes Thetis his life (18.405–407; [[of-the-lovely-tresses]]).
