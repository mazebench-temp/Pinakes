summary: Ares is war itself in the mouths of the speakers: Thetis bids Achilles not yet enter “the toil of Ares”, Polydamas speaks of the plain where both armies share “the fury of Ares”, Hector plans to rouse “sharp Ares” at the ships and says that “Enyalius is impartial”. He appears in person only on the shield of Achilles, where he leads the sortie from the besieged city with Pallas Athena, both of gold and taller than the men.
# Unnamed: the gods "both of gold" on the shield (18.517-519).
refs: [18.517-519]
passages:
  - 18.516-519 | Ares and Athena on the shield
---

**War itself.** As in the earlier books, the name of Ares is often the word for battle. Thetis tells Achilles: “But do not yet enter the toil of Ares / until you see me come back here with your own eyes” (18.134–135; see [[thetis]]). μῶλος Ἄρηος, “the toil of Ares”, is the phrase of the army's prayers in Book 2, of the armor that Lycurgus stripped from Areithous and wore into battle, and of Achilles' prayer for Patroclus (2.401, 7.147, 16.245); Walter Leaf notes that θωρήσσεσθαι, “arm”, at 18.189 answers to it (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 18.167). In the simile of the beacon fires the men of the besieged island “try their strength in the hateful work of Ares” all day long (18.209), the words of Agamemnon's order before the first battle (2.385; see the translation's note on 18.207–214). Polydamas tells the Trojans that Achilles will not stay on the plain, “where the Trojans and the Achaeans, / both, in the middle ground, share between them the fury of Ares” (18.263–264; see [[polydamas]] and [[menos]]); Leaf compares the Odyssey's μένος κρίνηται Ἄρηος, “the fury of battle is decided” (*Odyssey* 16.269), and calls δατέονται, “share between them”, a phrase that appears to be unique (on 18.264).

**Hector's war.** Hector answers him with his plan of Book 8, word for word: “And early, at daybreak, armed in our armor, / we will rouse sharp Ares beside the hollow ships” (18.303–304 = 8.530–531; see [[hector]] and [[sharp]]). ὀξὺς Ἄρης, “sharp Ares”, is the battle that Nestor and Odysseus speak of rousing (2.440, 4.352, 19.237), and that the two Ajaxes have withstood side by side (17.721). He ends: “Enyalius is impartial, and he kills the man who would kill” (18.309; see [[enyalius]]). Ἐνυάλιος, printed with a capital here, is the god of battle, Ares under his other name (17.211); Leaf renders “The god of battle is impartial and slays him that would slay”, comparing “victory shifts from man to man” (6.339; on 18.309; see the translation's note on 18.309). Aristotle quotes the first words as a maxim (*Rhetoric* 1395a15; see [[victory-shifts]] and [[aristotle-one-omen]]).

**Disaster, not Ares.** ἀρή, “disaster”, whose genitive ἀρῆς has the letters of the god's name, stands twice in the book: Patroclus “lacked me to be his defender against disaster” (ἀρῆς ἀλκτῆρα, 18.100), and the besieged islanders hope that their neighbors will come “to defend them against disaster” (ἄρεω ἀλκτῆρες, 18.213; see the translation's note on 12.334). Parmeniscus is said to have read Ἄρης at 18.100, “war … kept me from being his defender” (see the translation's note on 18.100).

**On the shield.** Ares appears in person only among the figures on the shield of Achilles, where the defenders of the besieged city go out to lie in ambush: “and Ares led them and Pallas Athena, / both of gold, and clad in golden clothing, / fine and tall in their armor, as gods should be, / plain for all to see from every side; and the people were smaller” (18.516–519; see [[athena]] and [[the-shield-of-achilles]]). Leaf calls the two gods the only distinctively Hellenic touch in the shield (on 18.516). In the same battle Strife, Tumult and Doom move through the throng (18.535–538), the powers that are Ares' companions elsewhere: Strife is “the sister and companion of man-slaying Ares” (4.441; see [[strife]], [[tumult]] and [[ker-doom]]).
