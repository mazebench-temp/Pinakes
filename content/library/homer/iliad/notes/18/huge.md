---
summary: Hector stripped from Patroclus the armor of Achilles, “huge, a wonder to see”; Hephaestus rises from his anvil “a huge, panting god”; the book adds πέλωρ.
grc: [πέλωρ]
---

**The armor.** “and Hector, when he had cut him down, / stripped off the armor, huge, a wonder to see, / and fine, the splendid gifts that the gods gave to Peleus” (18.82–84; [[splendid]], [[the-armor-of-peleus]]). The half-verse πελώρια θαῦμα ἰδέσθαι was said of the golden armor of Rhesus (10.439).

**Hephaestus.** “He spoke, and rose from the anvil block, a huge, panting god, / limping” (18.410–411; [[hephaestus]]). πέλωρ, the noun from which πελώριος is formed, means a monster or prodigy; it is an odd word for the god (see the translation's note on 18.410), and is new to the entry.
