summary: The Trojans hold an assembly on the plain at nightfall, standing, in fear of Achilles: Polydamas advises them to go back into the city, Hector overrules him, and the Trojans cheer the worse counsel (18.243–313). In the city at peace on the Shield the people gather in the place of assembly to hear a lawsuit (18.497), and in the city at war the besiegers sit before their places of assembly (εἰράων, 18.531). Achilles admits that in the assembly others are better than he (18.106).

The two assemblies of the book are a pair: on the plain the people cheer the wrong speaker, and on the Shield the elders judge in turn and the straightest judgment wins a prize (see [[assembly-type-scene]] and [[the-trial-on-the-shield]]). In the Trojan assembly the forms of ἀγορεύω, “speak”, are speech in the assembly itself (18.249, 18.253, 18.285, 18.310); elsewhere in the book they are ordinary speech and are excepted (18.95, 18.142, 18.368). Polydamas proposes to keep the army in the place of assembly through the night (18.274), and εἰράων is explained by the ancients as “places of assembly”, though Walter Leaf finds that sense too broad for προπάροιθε, “in front of”, and sees nothing better (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 18.531; see the translation's note on 18.531).
