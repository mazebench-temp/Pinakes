---
summary: Iris sends Achilles, unarmed, to the ditch to show himself to the Trojans. He goes from the wall and stands at the ditch, apart from the Achaeans, and three times shouts across it, and the Trojans fall back in confusion (18.197–231).
# τείχεος at 18.215 is this wall (the line is claimed by τάφρον). τεῖχος at 18.256 and 18.279 is the wall of Troy,
# and at 18.514 the wall of the besieged city on the Shield. The stakes of 18.177 (σκολόπεσσι) are not this wall's.
---

**To the ditch.** Iris tells Achilles to “go just as you are to the ditch and show yourself to the Trojans” (18.198; see [[iris]] and [[achilles]]): αὔτως, “just as you are”, without armor (see the translation's note on 18.198). “And he went from the wall and stood at the ditch, but with the Achaeans / he did not mingle; for he heeded his mother's shrewd command” (18.215–216; see [[thetis]]). Walter Leaf notes that the wall stood some distance back from the ditch (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 18.215; compare 8.213–214). There Athena shouts with him, apart (18.217–218; see [[athena]]), as she will shout again from the ditch outside the wall when the gods go into battle (20.48–50).

**Three shouts.** “Three times across the ditch brilliant Achilles shouted aloud, / and three times the Trojans and their famous allies were thrown into confusion” (18.228–229), and twelve of their best men die among their own chariots and spears (18.230–231; see [[trojans]]). Then the Achaeans can draw the body of Patroclus out of the missiles (18.231–233; see [[the-fight-for-the-body-of-patroclus]]). In Book 9 Achilles had scorned the wall and the ditch that Agamemnon built without him, which could not hold back Hector (9.348–352); now, unarmed, he holds back the Trojans at the ditch with his voice.

**The stakes.** The stakes on which Hector means to fix Patroclus' head (σκολόπεσσι, 18.177) are not, Leaf argues, the palisade of the ditch, since to fix the head there would be to give it up to the Achaeans, but a palisade along the top of the Trojan wall (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 18.177; see the translation's note on 18.177 and [[the-walls-of-troy]]).
