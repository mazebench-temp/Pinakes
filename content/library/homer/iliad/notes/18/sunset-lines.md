summary: Hera sends the tireless Sun, unwilling, down to the streams of Oceanus, “and the sun set” (ἠέλιος μὲν ἔδυ, 18.239–241), ending the third day of battle. In the simile of the beacon fires the fires blaze “as the sun goes down” (18.210).
grc: ["ἠέλιος μὲν ἔδυ"]
en: [the sun set]
---

The day is ended by a god. Walter Leaf notes that the sun goes down unwilling because before its time, and that the only other interference of a god with the course of nature in Homer is Athena's holding back of the dawn in the Odyssey (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 18.240; *Odyssey* 23.243–246). He takes the point of the simile to lie in the moment of sunset, when smoke and fire are seen together, like the cloud and the fire over Achilles' head (on 18.207; see the translation's note on 18.207–214).
