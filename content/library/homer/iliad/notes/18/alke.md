summary: Hector is “like a flame in his courage” and fights over the body “trusting in his courage”, and the two Ajaxes are “clothed in furious courage” (18.154, 18.157–158). Achilles failed to be Patroclus' “defender against disaster” (ἀλκτῆρα, 18.100), and in the simile of the beacons the besieged hope that neighbors will come with their ships “to defend them against disaster” (ἄρεω ἀλκτῆρες, 18.213).
grc: [ἀλκτῆρες]
en: [defender against disaster, defend them against disaster]
---

ἀλκτήρ is the defender who wards off: the phrase ἀρῆς ἀλκτῆρα, with ἀρή “disaster”, was used of the kinsman a man prays to leave in his halls (14.485; see [[a-kinsman-in-his-halls]]), and the translation keeps “disaster” for ἀρή in all three places (see the translation's notes on 12.334 and 18.100). See [[clothed-in-courage]] and [[like-a-flame]].
