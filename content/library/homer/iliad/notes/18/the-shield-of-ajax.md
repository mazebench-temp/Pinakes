---
summary: Achilles knows no armor he could put on, “unless it were the shield of Ajax, son of Telamon” (18.192–193), and Ajax is using it himself in the fight over Patroclus (18.194–195).
refs: [18.193]
---

Walter Leaf points back to the shield Ajax carried “like a tower” (7.219–223, 8.267; *Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 18.193), and finds the grammar of 18.192 puzzling, though the sense is clear (on 18.192). The only arms that would fit Achilles are the greatest shield in the army (see [[ajax]] and [[the-armor-of-peleus]]).
