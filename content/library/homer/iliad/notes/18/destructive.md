summary: Patroclus was to thrust off “the consuming fire”; in the similes of the beacons and of the trumpet the besiegers of a town are “enemies”; the book adds δήϊοι.
grc: [δήϊοι]
---

**The fire.** Achilles had told Patroclus to come back “once he had thrust off the consuming fire” (18.13; [[hard]]), the fire that the Trojans threw on the ship of Protesilaus (16.122–124).

**The enemy.** In the simile of the beacon fires the smoke rises “from an island that enemies are fighting around” (18.208; [[the-beacon-fires]]), and in the simile of the trumpet the voice rings out “because enemies who destroy the spirit surround a city” (18.220; [[the-trumpet]], [[that-destroys-the-spirit]]). The nominative plural δήϊοι is new to the entry; it also catches Nestor's warning that “the enemy” are close by the ships (9.76).
