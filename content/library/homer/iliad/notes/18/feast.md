summary: The Trojans gather in assembly “before they thought of supper”, and Hector orders them to take supper by companies, which they do (18.245, 18.298, 18.314). On the Shield there are banquets with the weddings of the city at peace (18.491), and on the king's land the heralds prepare a feast under an oak, with a great ox sacrificed, while the women sprinkle white barley meal for the laborers' dinner (18.558–560).
grc: [δόρποιο, εἰλαπίναι]
en: [banquets, dinner]
---

δαίς, “feast”, and δεῖπνον, “meal, dinner”, stand side by side at 18.558–560. Walter Leaf asks whether the δαίς is a banquet for the king, too rich for the laborers, or the abundance of a meal prepared for the workers themselves, and inclines to the second (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 18.560; see [[sacrifice]], [[barley-meal]] and [[heralds]]). εἰλαπίνη is the banquet, here of the weddings (18.491; see [[marriage]]). Hector's order of supper and a watch repeats his orders on the plain in Book 8 (8.502–522; see [[war-and-night]] and [[night-watch]]).
