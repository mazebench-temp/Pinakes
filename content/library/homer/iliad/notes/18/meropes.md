summary: Hector recalls that “all mortal men” once spoke of Priam's city as rich; Achilles and Patroclus sacked “the rich cities of mortal men”; and on the shield Hephaestus made “two cities of mortal men”; the book adds μέροπες ἄνθρωποι.
grc: ["μέροπες ἄνθρωποι"]
---

**Troy.** “Once all mortal men used to speak of the city of Priam / as rich in gold, rich in bronze” (18.288–289; [[rich-in-gold]], [[brazen]], [[priam]]). Walter Leaf notes that the nominative stands only here, as the dative at 2.285; elsewhere only μερόπων is found (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 18.288). The phrase μέροπες ἄνθρωποι is new to the entry.

**The captives.** “whom we ourselves won by toil with our strength and our long spear, / as we two sacked the rich cities of mortal men” (18.341–342; [[long-spears]], [[patroclus]]).

**The shield.** “On it he made two cities of mortal men, / fine ones” (18.490–491; [[the-two-cities]], [[the-shield-of-achilles]]).
