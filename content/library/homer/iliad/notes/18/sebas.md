---
summary: Iris tells Achilles to let σέβας come into his heart, the shame that should stop him lying idle while Patroclus' body may become a plaything for the Trojan dogs: “let shame come into your heart” (18.178). The noun occurs only here in the Iliad.
grc: [σέβας]
en: [shame]
---

Walter Leaf explains σέβας here as a feeling like αἰδώς, the shame felt before the common judgment of men (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 18.178; see the translation's note on 18.178 and [[aidos]]). The verse ends on the disgrace that would follow: “yours will be the disgrace, if the corpse comes back disfigured at all” (18.180; see [[lobe]] and [[honoring-the-dead]]).
