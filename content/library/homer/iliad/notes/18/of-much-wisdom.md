---
summary: Achilles curses the anger “which sets even a man of much wisdom to rage.”
---

“may strife perish from among gods and from among men, / and anger, which sets even a man of much wisdom to rage, / and which, far sweeter than dripping honey, / swells in the breasts of men like smoke” (18.107–110; [[cholos]], [[eris]], [[sweeter-than-dripping-honey]]). περ, “even,” makes the point: no wisdom is proof against anger. Achilles has his own anger in mind, and Agamemnon's (18.111; [[lord-of-men]]).
