---
summary: Antilochus finds Achilles “in front of the straight-horned ships,” and on the shield Hephaestus makes “a herd of straight-horned cattle”: the book uses the epithet in both its senses.
---

The book begins with the figurative use: “He found him in front of the straight-horned ships” (18.3; [[ships]]). Walter Leaf, with the scholiast, refers it to the stems and sterns that rise like a cow's horns on ships without the later beaked prow (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 18.3; see the translation's note on 18.3). Near its end the word returns in its proper sense, in the herd that the lions attack on the shield: “And on it he made a herd of straight-horned cattle” (18.573; [[cattle]], [[the-herd-and-the-lions]]).
