summary: Telamon is named once, in his son's patronymic: Achilles knows of no armor he could put on, “unless it were the shield of Ajax, son of Telamon” (18.193). The common noun τελαμών, “strap”, comes twice in the making of Achilles' new shield (18.480, 18.598).
# τελαμῶνα (18.480) and τελαμώνων (18.598) are the common noun "strap", not claimed.
---

εἰ μὴ Αἴαντός γε σάκος Τελαμωνιάδαο, “unless it were the shield of Ajax, son of Telamon” (18.193; see [[ajax]] and [[the-shield-of-ajax]]). Nearly the same words end the verse in which Teucer took his stand “under the shield of Ajax, son of Telamon” to shoot (8.267; see [[teucer]] and [[telamonian]]).

The name is also the common noun τελαμών, a strap or baldric. Hephaestus gives the great shield he makes for Achilles “a silver strap” (ἀργύρεον τελαμῶνα, 18.480), and on it the young men of the dance wear knives of gold “hanging from silver straps” (ἐξ ἀργυρέων τελαμώνων, 18.598; see [[the-shield-of-achilles]]). In Book 17 the strap with which Hippothous dragged Patroclus by the ankle stood three verses before “the son of Telamon” who killed him (17.290–293).
