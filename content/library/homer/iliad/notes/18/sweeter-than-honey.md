---
summary: Achilles turns Nestor's likeness to anger: anger, “far sweeter than dripping honey, / swells in the breasts of men like smoke” (18.109–110).
grc: ["γλυκίων μέλιτος"]
en: [far sweeter than dripping honey]
---

The phrase is Nestor's, γλυκίων μέλιτος, in reversed order, and the sweetness is the same, but here it belongs to a passion that clouds the mind like smoke (18.108–110; see [[cholos]]). Aristotle quotes the lines for the pleasure that goes with anger, the hope of revenge (see [[sweeter-than-dripping-honey]]).
