---
summary: Book 18 tells the marriage from both sides. Achilles wishes that his mother had stayed among the goddesses of the sea and that Peleus had married a mortal wife (18.85–87), and Thetis tells Hephaestus that Zeus subdued her to a mortal man and that she endured his bed much against her will; now Peleus lies worn out by old age in his halls (18.432–435). Twice she laments that she will not welcome her son home “to the house of Peleus” (18.59–60 = 18.440–441).
refs: [18.60, 18.85-88, 18.331-332, 18.432-435, 18.441]
---

**The wedding day.** Achilles names the day on which the gods gave Peleus the armor that Hector now wears: “on the day when they put you into the bed of a mortal man” (18.85; see [[the-armor-of-peleus]]). He wishes it had never been: “If only you had stayed there among the immortal goddesses of the sea, / and Peleus had taken a mortal wife!” (18.86–87). As it is, the marriage has given her a son to grieve for: “it was so that you too might have countless grief in your heart / for your son who has perished” (18.88–89; see the translation's note on 18.88). ἔμβαλον εὐνῇ, “put you into the bed”, makes the marriage the gods' act, not hers.

**Against her will.** Thetis says the same to Hephaestus, from her side: “Out of all the other goddesses of the sea he subdued me to a man, / Peleus, son of Aeacus, and I endured the bed of a man, / much against my will” (18.432–434; see [[thetis]] and [[peleus]]). Zeus is the subject. δάμασσεν, “subdued”, is the verb of taming and of overcoming in battle, and of wives given to other men in the curse on the oath-breakers of Book 3: “and may their wives be subdued by others” (3.301). She counts the marriage first among the sorrows that Zeus has given her beyond all the goddesses on Olympus (18.429–431).

**The old man in his halls.** “And he now, by grievous old age / lies worn out in his halls” (18.434–435). ἀρημένος, “worn out”, occurs only here in the Iliad; elsewhere the word is Odyssean (Walter Leaf, *Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 18.435), and in the Odyssey Teiresias promises Odysseus a gentle death when he is “worn out by sleek old age” (*Odyssey* 11.134–136). The house of Peleus is where she will not welcome her son, δόμον Πηλήϊον εἴσω (18.60, 18.441; see [[nostos]]), and Achilles tells the Myrmidons that neither old Peleus nor his mother Thetis will welcome him in his halls, for the earth will hold him at Troy (18.330–332; see [[death-of-achilles-foretold]]). Thetis herself no longer lives there. When her son groans she hears him “as she sat in the depths of the sea beside her aged father” (18.36 = 1.358; see [[old-man-of-the-sea]]).
