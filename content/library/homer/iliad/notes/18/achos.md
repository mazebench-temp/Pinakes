summary: The news reaches Achilles in the verse used of Hector's grief for Podes in Book 17: “So he spoke, and a black cloud of grief covered him” (18.22 = 17.591). Grief then runs through it: the captive women grieving at heart (18.29), Achilles who “grieves” while he lives, in his mother's words to the Nereids and to Hephaestus (18.62 = 18.443), the grief for Briseis that wasted his heart (18.446), and the lion that grieves for its stolen cubs (18.320).
grc: [ἀκηχέμεναι]
---

Achilles' answer to his mother turns grief into resolve: “But we will let all this be, as done and past, for all our grief, / taming the dear heart in our breasts under compulsion” (18.112–113), verses he repeats when he renounces his anger before the army (19.65–66; see [[cholos]]). Thetis' account to Hephaestus ends with her son lying on the ground, “grieving in his heart” (θυμὸν ἀχεύων, 18.461). See [[penthos]], [[lament]] and [[the-lion-and-its-cubs]].
