---
summary: At sunset the Achaeans cease “from the fierce din of battle and from war, common to all.”
---

“and the sun set, and the brilliant Achaeans ceased / from the fierce din of battle and from war, common to all” (18.241–242; [[krateros]], [[the-third-day-of-battle]]). The verse-end ὁμοιΐου πολέμοιο is used of the war that the young Achilles knew nothing of when Peleus sent him to Agamemnon (9.438–440), and of the battle that Athena lets the Achaeans see when she takes the mist from their eyes (15.668–670). The day of battle that began at 11.1 ends here, when Hera sends the sun down early (18.239–240).
