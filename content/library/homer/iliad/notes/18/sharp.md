grc: [ὀξείας]
en: [shrilly]
---

**The cry.** “and wailing shrilly she took her son's head in her hands” (18.71; [[thetis]], [[lament]]). ὀξύ of sound stays with the entry, as ὀξὺ βοήσας does.

**The bronze and Ares.** Patroclus lies “on the litter, torn by the sharp bronze” (18.236; [[trusted-companion]]). Hector, rejecting Polydamas' advice: “And early, at daybreak, armed in our armor, / we will rouse sharp Ares beside the hollow ships” (18.303–304; [[hollow-ships]], [[ares]]), the verses he spoke at nightfall in Book 8 (8.530–531).

**The sickles.** On the king's domain the hired laborers “were reaping, with sharp sickles in their hands” (18.550–551; [[the-kings-harvest]]). The feminine plural ὀξείας is new to the entry.
