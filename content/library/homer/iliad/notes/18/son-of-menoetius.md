---
summary: Achilles fears that “the valiant son of Menoetius is dead”; Hector must pay “for making Patroclus, son of Menoetius, his prey”; and Thetis tells Hephaestus how Apollo killed “the valiant son of Menoetius.”
---

**The fear.** “Surely now the valiant son of Menoetius is dead” (18.12; [[strong-spear]]), Achilles says before Antilochus reaches him, naming his friend by the father to whom he had promised to bring him home (18.324–327; [[menoetius]]).

**The vengeance.** Achilles will not live “unless Hector / is first struck by my spear and loses his life, / and pays for making Patroclus, son of Menoetius, his prey” (18.91–93; [[hector]]).

**Thetis' account.** “and on that very day they would have sacked the city, had not Apollo, / after the valiant son of Menoetius had done much harm, / killed him among the champions, and given glory to Hector” (18.454–456; [[apollo]], [[would-have-had-not]]). See [[patroclus]].
