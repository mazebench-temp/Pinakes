---
summary: Patroclus' body is covered “with a soft linen cloth,” and Hephaestus makes Achilles “greaves of soft tin.”
---

**The linen.** “and laying him on a bier they covered him with a soft linen cloth / from head to foot, and over it with a white cloak” (18.352–353; [[white]], [[honoring-the-dead]], [[patroclus]]). In Book 23 the same words cover the golden bowl that holds his bones (23.253–254).

**The tin.** “and he made him greaves of soft tin” (18.613; [[greaves]], [[tin]]). Walter Leaf renders ἑανοῦ “pliant” and reports Reichel's explanation of greaves made of so soft a metal: they guarded the shins against the edge of the shield, not against missiles (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 18.613; see the translation's note on 18.613).
