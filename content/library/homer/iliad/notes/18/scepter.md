summary: Three staffs: the thick staff Hephaestus takes to lean on as he limps out to Thetis (18.416), the scepters of the heralds that the elders hold as they rise to judge (18.505), and the scepter of the king who stands in silence by the swath (18.557).
en: [staff]
---

At 18.416 the σκῆπτρον is a walking stick, the one place the translation renders it “staff”; Walter Leaf notes that a σκῆπτρον is not always a badge of office (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 18.550, citing *Odyssey* 13.437, 17.199, 18.103; see the translation's key-word rows). The god who leans on a stick made the scepter that Agamemnon holds (2.101–102). On the Shield the plural σκῆπτρα is loose: the herald hands the staff to each judge in turn (Leaf on 18.505; see [[the-trial-on-the-shield]] and [[heralds]]); the king's scepter is the badge of office (Leaf on 18.550; see [[basileus]]).
