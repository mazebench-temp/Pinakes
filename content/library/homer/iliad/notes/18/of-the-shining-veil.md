---
summary: “Charis of the shining veil” comes out to meet Thetis at the house of Hephaestus.
---

“And Charis of the shining veil came forward and saw her, / the lovely one, whom the renowned god, lame in both legs, had married; / and she clung to her hand, and spoke to her, and called her by name” (18.382–384; [[charis-wife-of-hephaestus]], [[lame-in-both-legs]]). The epithet occurs only here in Homer. The wife of the craftsman is Grace herself, “a more transparent allegory than we find elsewhere in Homer,” as Walter Leaf puts it; in the Odyssey the wife of Hephaestus is Aphrodite (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 18.382; see the translation's note on 18.382).
