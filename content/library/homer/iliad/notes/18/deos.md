---
summary: Polydamas begins his speech, “But now I am terribly afraid of the swift-footed son of Peleus” (18.261), and Iris sends Achilles to the ditch so that the Trojans may hold off “in fear of you” (18.199). Antilochus “feared that he would cut his throat with the iron” (18.34), and the two Ajaxes cannot frighten Hector away from the body (18.164).
grc: [δείδιε, ὑποδείσαντες, δειδίξασθαι]
---

The fear in the Trojan assembly is shown before it is spoken: it is held standing, “and no one dared / to sit down; for trembling held them all, because Achilles / had appeared” (18.246–248; τρόμος, “trembling”; see [[assembly-type-scene]]). Polydamas' αἰνῶς δείδοικα, “I am terribly afraid”, is said by Hera of Thetis' influence on Zeus (1.555), by Odysseus of Hector's threats (9.244) and by Nestor in the night of Book 10 (10.538; see [[polydamas]]). The subject of 18.34 is ambiguous, and some ancient critics took Antilochus to fear for his own throat (see the translation's note on 18.34 and [[iron]]).
