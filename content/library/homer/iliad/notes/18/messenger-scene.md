---
summary: The message sent at the end of Book 17 is delivered. Antilochus finds Achilles in front of the ships, already fearing the news, and gives it in four lines, ending with the words of Menelaus' charge, “naked; for Hector of the gleaming helmet has his armor” (18.2–21 = 17.693). Iris comes as a messenger from Hera, and Achilles asks who of the gods sent her (18.166–202). Thetis sends the Nereids to tell their father everything (18.140–142).

**Antilochus.** The messenger comes “shedding hot tears” and tells “his grievous news” (18.17): Patroclus lies dead, they are fighting over the corpse, and Hector has the armor (18.18–21; see [[antilochus]] and [[shedding-tears]]). The news needs no answer. A black cloud of grief covers Achilles, and Antilochus holds his hands (18.22–34; see [[lament]]).

**Iris.** Hera sends Iris “in secret from Zeus and the other gods” (18.168), and Achilles' first words to her are a question about the sender: “Goddess Iris, who of the gods sent you to me as a messenger?” (18.182). Walter Leaf notes that it is the first speech in the Iliad of a single line (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 18.182). She answers in three lines, and gives her own advice: show yourself at the ditch (18.184–201; see [[iris]] and [[divine-epiphany]]). Leaf suspects 18.168, and with it 18.181–186, since nothing more is said of secrecy (on 18.168; see the translation's note on 18.168).
