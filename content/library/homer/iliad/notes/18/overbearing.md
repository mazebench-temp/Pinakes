---
summary: Hector mocks “whoever of the Trojans is overly troubled about his possessions.”
en: [overly]
---

“and whoever of the Trojans is overly troubled about his possessions, / let him gather them and give them to the people to consume in common” (18.300–301; [[hector]], [[possessions]]). The adverb ὑπερφιάλως, “overbearingly” in Book 13 (13.293), here means beyond measure (see the translation's note on 18.300–302, where Walter Leaf's reading of the passage as irony at Polydamas is reported).
