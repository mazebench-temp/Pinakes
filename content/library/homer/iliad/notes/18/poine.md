---
summary: The penalty is the price of a life. Achilles will not live unless Hector “pays for making Patroclus, son of Menoetius, his prey” (18.93); Hephaestus must “pay” Thetis the full price for his life (τίνειν, 18.407); and on the Shield two men quarrel “over the penalty / for a man who had perished” (18.498–499).
---

The penalty on the Shield is the blood-price that, in Ajax's words, lets a killer stay among his people (9.632–636; see [[the-trial-on-the-shield]]). Achilles' penalty for Patroclus will be paid in lives: Hector's, and the twelve young Trojans taken alive “as the penalty for Patroclus” (ποινὴν Πατρόκλοιο, 21.28; see [[human-sacrifice]]). Walter Leaf infers from Achilles' promise to bring Patroclus home to Opus that his exile for killing a boy there was only for a time, compounded after a while with blood-money (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 18.326; 23.85–88; see [[exile-and-refuge]]). Hephaestus' debt is the ζῳάγρια (see [[zoagria]]).
