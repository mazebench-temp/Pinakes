---
summary: Hephaestus speaks of his fall “by the will of my dog-faced mother,” who wished to hide him because he was lame.
---

“who saved me when pain came on me after I had fallen far / by the will of my dog-faced mother, who wished / to hide me away because I was lame” (18.395–397; [[hera]], [[fall-of-hephaestus]], [[crook-footed]]). The word of shamelessness that Achilles threw at Agamemnon and Helen used of herself is a son's word for his mother. In Book 1 it was Zeus who threw Hephaestus from Olympus (1.590–594; see the translation's note on 18.395–405).
