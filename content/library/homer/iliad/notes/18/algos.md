summary: The Trojans' horses foresee sorrows when Achilles shouts from the ditch (18.224). Hephaestus remembers the pain of his fall from heaven and the sorrows he would have suffered had Thetis and Eurynome not taken him in (18.395–398), and Thetis asks him whether any goddess has endured as many sorrows as Zeus has given her (18.429–431).
en: [pain]
---

ἄλγεα are Thetis' word for her whole life: her marriage to a mortal against her will, her husband's old age, and her son, whom she will not welcome home (18.432–443; see [[thetis]] and [[mothers-and-sons]]). The horses' foreboding is the narrator's: ὄσσοντο γὰρ ἄλγεα θυμῷ, “for they foresaw sorrows in their hearts” (18.224; see [[horses]]).
