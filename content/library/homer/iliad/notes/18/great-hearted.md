---
summary: Achilles speaks “to his own proud heart” before the news comes; the fire blazes above the head of “the great-hearted son of Peleus”; and Achilles vows to bring back the armor and head of Hector, Patroclus' “great-hearted slayer.”
---

**The heart.** “and deeply troubled he spoke to his own proud heart” (18.5) is the verse of a man who debates with himself in danger (11.403, 17.90; [[deeply-troubled]], [[thymos]]). Here it opens Achilles' foreboding: “May the gods not have fulfilled evil sorrows for my heart” (18.8).

**Achilles and Hector.** The charioteers see the fire “blazing terribly above the head of the great-hearted son of Peleus” (18.226; [[son-of-peleus]]). Over Patroclus' body Achilles gives the epithet to his enemy: “I will not give you burial rites until I bring here Hector's / armor and head, your great-hearted slayer's” (18.334–335; [[hector]]).
