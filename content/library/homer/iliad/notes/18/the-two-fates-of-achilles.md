summary: The choice of Book 9 is made. Achilles is not going home, and he will die at once if he must, so long as he kills Hector, “but now may I win noble fame” (18.98–121), in the words of his mother's prophecy, κλέος ἐσθλόν (9.415). Thetis' warning names the cost: straight after Hector his own fate is ready (18.95–96).
refs: [18.95-96, 18.101, 18.121]
---

The two roads of Book 9 were homecoming with a long life and fame with an early death (9.412–416). Book 18 takes away the first in the same words: ὤλετο μέν μοι νόστος, “my homecoming is lost”, there; νῦν δʼ ἐπεὶ οὐ νέομαί γε φίλην ἐς πατρίδα γαῖαν, “But now, since I am not going home to my dear native land”, here (9.413, 18.101; see [[nostos]]). The fame he chooses is the Trojan women's grief (18.121–125; see [[kleos]] and [[lament]]). See [[the-short-life-of-achilles]].
