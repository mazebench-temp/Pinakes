summary: Achilles speaks to his own proud heart before the news comes (18.5), and afterward his heart no longer bids him live, unless Hector loses his life by his spear (18.90–92); he will tame “the dear heart in our breasts under compulsion” (18.113). Iris tells him to let shame come into his heart (18.178); Hector's heart bids him fix Patroclus' head on the stakes (18.176), and Achilles' heart is “overweening”, Polydamas says (18.262). The hearts of the Trojans are shaken by his voice, and their horses foresee sorrows in their hearts (18.223–224).

θυμός is the heart that drives and is driven: “my heart does not bid me / live, or remain among men” (18.90–91), as Hephaestus' heart bids him accomplish what Thetis asks (18.426). See [[phrenes]], [[hyperbios]] and [[deeply-troubled]].
