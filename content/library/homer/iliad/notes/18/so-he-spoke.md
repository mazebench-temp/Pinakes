summary: The book's speeches close on action. The news ends “So he spoke, and a black cloud of grief covered him” (18.22 = 17.591); Thetis' words to the Nereids, to her son and to her sisters are followed by her going (18.65, 18.138, 18.145); Iris goes away (18.202); Hector's speech is cheered (18.310 = 8.542); Achilles' lament ends in orders to wash the body (18.343); Zeus and Hera speak “such words to one another” while Thetis arrives at Hephaestus' house (18.368); and Hephaestus rises from his anvil block and goes to his bellows (18.410, 18.468).
---

“So Hector spoke, and the Trojans shouted in applause” (18.310) is the verse that ended Hector's first assembly on the plain (8.542); this time the narrator adds his verdict, “fools; for Pallas Athena had taken away their wits” (18.311; see [[assembly-type-scene]] and [[nepios]]). The verse of 18.368 marks the turn from the gods' talk to Thetis' visit, as it marks changes of scene elsewhere (see [[visit-scene]]).
