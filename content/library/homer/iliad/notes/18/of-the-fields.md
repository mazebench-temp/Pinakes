---
summary: In the simile of the lion, “shepherds of the fields” cannot drive it from a carcass.
---

“And as shepherds of the fields cannot drive a tawny lion / away from a carcass at all, when he is very hungry” (18.161–162; [[lion-on-a-carcass]], [[tawny]]). The epithet usually given to oxen is given to the herdsmen who stay out in the fields with their flocks, as the entry notes; the shepherds are the Ajaxes, and the lion is Hector.
