summary: The sheep of Book 18 are on the Shield: the flocks of white sheep that the ambushers of the city at war cut off with the herds, killing the shepherds (18.523–529), and the great pasture for white sheep in a lovely glen, with farmsteads, roofed huts and pens (18.587–589).
# μῆλα (18.524) is the sheep with the cattle; the phrase keeps the bare word, which is also the goatherd's flock (4.279), to this line.
grc: ["μῆλα ἰδοίατο"]
---

**The raid.** In the city at war two lookouts wait for the besiegers' beasts to come to the watering place, “waiting for when they should see the sheep and the cattle with curving horns” (18.524). μῆλα are the small livestock, sheep and goats together. They come with two herdsmen playing on their pipes, and the ambushers “cut off the herds of cattle and the fine flocks / of white sheep, and killed the shepherds as well” (18.528–529; see [[the-two-cities]], [[cattle]] and [[syrinx]]). πώεα καλά, “the fine flocks”, is the plural of πῶϋ, the flock of the formula πῶϋ μέγʼ οἰῶν (11.696, 15.323), and the μηλοβοτῆρες, “shepherds”, are named only here. Walter Leaf reports that Zenodotus read πῶυ μέγʼ οἰῶν here and so must have left out the next verse (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 18.528).

**The pasture.** “And on it the renowned god, lame in both legs, made a pasture / in a lovely glen, a great one, for white sheep, / and farmsteads and roofed huts and pens” (18.587–589; see [[the-sheep-pasture]]). ἀργεννός, “white”, is the color of Homer's sheep (3.198, 6.424; see [[white]]). After the herd attacked by lions, the flock is shown at peace. Leaf takes the σταθμοί as the whole sheep station, with the κλισίαι, the shepherds' huts, and the σηκοί, the folds (on 18.587; see [[hut]]).
