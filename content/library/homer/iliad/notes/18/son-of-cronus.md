---
summary: Heracles was “the dearest of all to lord Zeus, son of Cronus”; Iris says “the son of Cronus, throned on high,” does not know her errand; Hector boasts of what “the son of crooked-counseling Cronus” has granted him; Hera answers Zeus “Most dread son of Cronus”; and Thetis blames “Zeus, son of Cronus” for her sorrows.
---

**Heracles.** “For not even mighty Heracles escaped his doom, / who was the dearest of all to lord Zeus, son of Cronus” (18.117–118; [[heracles]], [[might-of-heracles]]).

**Iris and Hector.** “and the son of Cronus, throned on high, does not know” (18.185; [[high-throned]]); “But now, when the son of crooked-counseling Cronus has granted me / to win glory at the ships” (18.293–294; [[crooked-counseling]], [[cronus]]).

**Hera and Thetis.** “Most dread son of Cronus, what a thing you have said!” (18.361; [[most-dread]], [[hera]]). Thetis to Hephaestus: “so many grievous sorrows / as the sorrows Zeus, son of Cronus, has given me beyond all of them” (18.430–431; [[grim]]). See [[zeus]].
