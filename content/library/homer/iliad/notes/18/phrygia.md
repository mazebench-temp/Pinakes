summary: Hector tells the Trojan assembly that many of the treasures of Priam's city have gone to Phrygia and lovely Maeonia, sold away since Zeus grew angry (18.291–292).
---

{{quote:1bb5dbfa-3af4-5d9f-bd83-d725c97be20e}}

Phrygia and Maeonia are named together, as when Helen asked Aphrodite whether she would lead her on to a city “of Phrygia or of lovely Maeonia” (3.401; Walter Leaf, *Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 18.288; see [[maeonia]] and [[helen]]): the rich inland countries beyond the Troad, where Troy's treasures have gone. Their peoples fight for Troy (2.862–866; see [[phrygians]] and [[trojan-allies]]), and Leaf thinks the treasures were sold to pay the allies (on 18.292; see [[hector]] and [[priam]]). In Book 24 Achilles will mark the bounds of Priam's old wealth by Lesbos, “Phrygia inland” and the Hellespont (24.544–545).
