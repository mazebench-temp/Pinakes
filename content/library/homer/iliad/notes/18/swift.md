summary: Antilochus comes to Achilles “swift of foot” with the news; the ships of the Myrmidons are drawn up “around swift Achilles,” who is “swift of foot” again as the Myrmidons lament around him and in Zeus' words to Hera; the book adds ταχὺν.
grc: [ταχὺν]
---

**Antilochus.** “and Antilochus, swift of foot, came to Achilles as a messenger” (18.2; [[antilochus]]). The young runner brings the news that Achilles feared (18.6–14; [[the-death-of-patroclus]]).

**Achilles.** The Nereids go up onto the shore “where thick / the ships of the Myrmidons were drawn up around swift Achilles” (18.68–69; [[myrmidons]]); the Myrmidons groan all night “around Achilles, swift of foot” (18.354); and Zeus says to Hera, “You have had your way after all, ox-eyed queen Hera, / rousing Achilles, swift of foot” (18.357–358; [[hera]]). The single word ταχὺν is new to the entry; it also catches the “swift arrow” that Athena urges Pandarus to let fly at Menelaus (4.94) and Eudorus, “surpassingly swift at running” (16.186). See [[swift-footed]] and [[achilles]].
