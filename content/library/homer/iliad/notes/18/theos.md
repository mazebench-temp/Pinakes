summary: Gods and goddesses throng the book. Thetis is “the goddess” and the Nereids “the goddesses” of the sea (18.37, 18.127, 18.146, 18.381); Achilles fears what the gods have fulfilled (18.8) and will accept his doom whenever Zeus and the other gods will it (18.116); Iris comes “in secret from Zeus and the other gods” (18.168); Athena is “brilliant among goddesses” (18.205); Hera is “the best of goddesses” (18.364); Hephaestus' handmaids know handiwork from the gods (18.420); and on the Shield Ares and Athena are drawn “as gods should be” (18.518).
# θέουσʼ (18.167): Iris "running", from θέω, not θεός.
except: [18.167]
---

Achilles' wish that strife might perish “from among gods and from among men” (18.107) and Hephaestus' refuge known “neither of the gods nor of mortal men” (18.404) set the two orders side by side (see [[gods-and-mortals]]).
