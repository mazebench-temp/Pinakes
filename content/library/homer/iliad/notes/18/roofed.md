---
summary: In the pasture on the shield stand “farmsteads and roofed huts and pens.”
---

“And on it the renowned god, lame in both legs, made a pasture / in a lovely glen, a great one, for white sheep, / and farmsteads and roofed huts and pens” (18.587–589; [[white]], [[sheep]], [[the-sheep-pasture]]). Walter Leaf takes σταθμοί as a general name, like a sheep station, including both the κλισίαι, huts for the shepherds, and the σηκοί, folds for the sheep (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 18.587).
