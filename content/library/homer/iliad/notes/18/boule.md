---
summary: “But no one approved of Polydamas, who had devised good counsel” (18.313). On the Shield the besiegers of the other city “were divided in their counsel” whether to sack it or divide its wealth (18.510).
---

The narrator's verdict sets Polydamas' “good counsel” against Hector's, who “devised evil” (κακὰ μητιόωντι, 18.312; see [[retreat]] and [[metis]]). Walter Leaf thinks that the poet divided the besiegers into two groups for the picture, and took the division as a division of opinion (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 18.510; see [[the-two-cities]]).
