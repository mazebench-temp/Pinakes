summary: The tears of the book are for Patroclus and for Achilles. Antilochus brings the news weeping (18.17, 18.32), Thetis asks her son the question of Book 1, “My child, why are you weeping?” (18.73 = 1.362), and speaks to him and to Hephaestus in tears (18.94, 18.428); the Nereids go up from the sea in tears (18.66), and the companions and Achilles weep beside the bier (18.233–235). Achilles promises that captive women will weep for Patroclus night and day (18.339–340), and wants Trojan women to wipe tears from their cheeks (18.122–124).

The question of 18.73 repeats Thetis' first words to her son in Book 1, and the answer has changed: then it was the girl Agamemnon took from him, now it is Patroclus (1.362–412, 18.79–93; see [[mothers-and-sons]] and [[penthos]]). The book's other weeping is formulaic (see [[shedding-tears]] and [[hot-tears]]), and the tears are mostly those of mourners (see [[lament]]). The women Achilles imagines and the women he promises are one pair: Trojan women weeping for the men he will kill, and Trojan and Dardanian captives weeping for the man Hector has killed (18.122–124, 18.339–342; see [[captive-women]]).
