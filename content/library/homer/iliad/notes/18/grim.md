---
summary: Antilochus brings “grievous news”; Thetis has endured “so many grievous sorrows,” and Peleus lies worn out “by grievous old age.”
---

**The news.** “Ah me, son of wise-hearted Peleus, truly you will hear / grievous news, which I wish had never been” (18.18–19; [[antilochus]]). The second verse is the one with which Menelaus sent Antilochus off with the news (17.685–686).

**Thetis.** To Hephaestus: “is there any, of all the goddesses on Olympus, / who has endured in her heart so many grievous sorrows / as the sorrows Zeus, son of Cronus, has given me beyond all of them?” (18.429–431); and of Peleus, “And he now, by grievous old age / lies worn out in his halls” (18.434–435; [[peleus]], [[thetis-in-the-house-of-peleus]]). γήραϊ λυγρῷ was the old age of Phaenops, “worn down by grievous old age” (5.153).
