summary: The book is full of cities. Polydamas would take the Trojans back “to the city”, whose towers and gates will guard it (18.255, 18.266, 18.274–276), and Hector scorns being penned in it again (18.286–287); once men called Priam's city rich in gold (18.288). Achilles' fire is like the smoke of a besieged island city, and his voice like the trumpet when enemies surround a city (18.207–220); and Hephaestus makes “two cities of mortal men” on the Shield (18.490–540).

Achilles and Patroclus sacked “the rich cities of mortal men” together (18.342), and Thetis says that Troy would have fallen on the day Patroclus died, had not Apollo killed him (18.454; see [[would-have-had-not]]). The Shield's cities are a city at peace and a besieged city like Troy (see [[the-two-cities]] and [[the-city-and-the-household]]).
