{{quote:e0c55243-2732-57aa-9728-583233ca9a91}}

Opus is named here for the second of three times in the poem, between the Locrian towns of the Catalogue (2.531; see [[locrians]]) and the ghost of Patroclus, who tells why Menoetius brought him from Opus to Peleus' house as a boy: he had killed another boy in a quarrel over knucklebones (23.84–90; see [[menoetius]] and [[patroclus]]). Walter Leaf infers from the promise that the exile was only for a time, settled after a while by blood-money (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 18.326; see [[exile-and-refuge]]). Patroclus' Locrian birth may lie behind an ancient doubt about the book's opening: Leaf reports from the scholia that Rhianus and Aristophanes left out the prophecy that “the best of the Myrmidons” would die while Achilles lived, perhaps because Patroclus was not a Myrmidon but a Locrian from Opus (18.10–11; *Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 18.10; see [[myrmidons]] and the translation's note on 18.10–11).
