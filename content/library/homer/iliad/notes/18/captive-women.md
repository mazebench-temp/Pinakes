summary: Two groups of captives mourn in the book. The slave women whom Achilles and Patroclus took as spoil cry aloud at the news and beat their breasts (18.28–31), and Achilles promises Patroclus that the Trojan and Dardanian women “whom we ourselves won by toil with our strength and our long spear” will weep for him night and day (18.339–342). Thetis tells Hephaestus how the prize the army gave Achilles was taken from him (18.444–446).

The two scenes frame the book's mourning (see [[lament]]). The women of 18.28 are the spoils of the raids that Achilles and Patroclus made together, and Achilles names the raids again when he promises the captives' tears: “as we two sacked the rich cities of mortal men” (18.342; see [[raiding]] and [[achilles-raids]]). In Thetis' retelling Briseis is “the girl whom the sons of the Achaeans picked out for him as his prize” (18.444; see [[geras]], [[division-of-spoils]] and [[retelling]]). In the next book Briseis leads the women's lament for Patroclus, and they weep for him in name, each for her own sorrows (19.282–302).
