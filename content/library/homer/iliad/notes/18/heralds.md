---
summary: Heralds keep order in the trial on the Shield, holding the people back, and the elders hold “the scepters of the far-voiced heralds” as they rise to judge (18.503–505); on the king's land heralds prepare a feast under an oak and dress a great ox they have sacrificed (18.558–559).
---

The two scenes show the herald's two offices, order in the assembly and service at the sacrifice and the meal (see [[the-trial-on-the-shield]], [[feast]] and [[sacrifice]]). The epithet ἠεροφώνων, “far-voiced”, occurs only here in Homer (18.505; see [[far-voiced]]). Walter Leaf compares the herald's proclamation that opens the trial before the Areopagus in Aeschylus' *Eumenides* (566; *Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 18.503).
