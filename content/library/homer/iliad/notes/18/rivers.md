---
summary: The rivers of Book 18 are on the Shield: the river where the besieged city lays its ambush at the herds' watering place and fights beside the banks (18.520–534), the sounding river by which the golden cattle go to pasture (18.575–576), and the river Oceanus around the rim (18.607–608).
---

**The ambush.** In the city at war the men who go out from the besieged city stop “in a river, where there was a watering place for all the herds”, and sit there in ambush, wrapped in gleaming bronze (18.520–522); when the besiegers ride up to save their cattle, the two sides “set their battle in array and fought beside the river's banks” (18.533; see [[the-two-cities]] and [[ambush]]). Fighting by a river's banks is the Iliad's own, as on the left of the battle in Book 11, “beside the banks of the river Scamander” (11.499; see [[scamander]]).

**The pasture.** The herd of golden cattle hurries lowing to the pasture “beside a sounding river, beside a bed of swaying reeds” (18.576; see [[the-herd-and-the-lions]]). κελάδων, “sounding”, is said of the stream of the Xanthus when it is choked with men and horses (21.16), and a river of the Peloponnese bears it as a name, the Celadon (7.133; see [[celadon]]).

**Oceanus.** “And on it he set the great strength of the river Oceanus / along the outermost rim of the strongly made shield” (18.607–608). The river that runs round the world runs round the Shield, as Walter Leaf notes (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 18.608; see [[oceanus]] and [[the-shield-of-achilles]]). It is the only river in the book that is named.
