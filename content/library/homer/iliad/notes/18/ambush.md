summary: On the Shield the besieged city answers the siege with an ambush. Its men are “secretly arming for an ambush” (18.513), and led by Ares and Athena they lie in wait at a river where the herds come to drink, with two lookouts; they cut off the besiegers' cattle and sheep and kill the herdsmen, and the besiegers come up and fight them by the river (18.513–534).
grc: [λόχῳ, λοχῆσαι]
en: [lie in ambush]

---

Walter Leaf takes the ambushers to be the besieged, who sit in wait for the besiegers' cattle, and rejects the view that the besieged sent out their own herds as a bait, since the besiegers come up from a distance with their horses, not out of a prepared ambush (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 18.523; see the translation's notes on 18.513 and 18.523). Hector's first night orders had guarded against the same danger, “so that no ambush may enter the city while the army is away” (8.522). See [[the-two-cities]], [[raiding]] and [[spies-and-scouts]].
