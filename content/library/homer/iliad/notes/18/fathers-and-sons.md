---
summary: Two fathers wait at home for sons who will not come back. Achilles had promised Menoetius in Opus to bring Patroclus home with his share of the spoil (18.324–327), and he will not himself be welcomed home by old Peleus (18.330–332), who lies worn out by old age in his halls (18.434–435). The armor Hector took was the gods' wedding gift to Peleus (18.84–85).
refs: [18.324-327, 18.330-332, 18.434-435]
---

The two promises are broken in one sentence: “But Zeus does not fulfill for men all their plans; / for it is fated that both of us redden the same earth / here in Troy” (18.328–330; see [[zeus-does-not-fulfill-all-plans]], [[menoetius]] and [[peleus]]). Thetis' own father is the Old Man of the Sea, beside whom she sits in the depths (18.36, 18.141; see [[old-man-of-the-sea]]). Polydamas is named by his father, “the son of Panthous” (18.250; see [[panthous]]).
