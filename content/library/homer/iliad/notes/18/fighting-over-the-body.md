summary: The fight for Patroclus' body ends. Antilochus' news is that “they are fighting around his corpse” (18.20); three times Hector seizes the body by the feet and three times the two Ajaxes drive him off, and he would have dragged it away had not Iris roused Achilles (18.151–168); when Achilles shouts from the ditch the Achaeans draw the body out from under the missiles and lay it on a bier (18.228–233). On the Shield the fighters of the city at war “dragged away the bodies of one another's dead” (18.540).

The end of the fight in Book 18 does not fit the end of Book 17, where Menelaus and Meriones carry the body while the Ajaxes guard the rear; here the Achaeans are still drawing it out of range, and Walter Leaf takes the passage for an account of independent origin (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 18.151, 18.154; see the translation's note on 18.151–165 and [[the-fight-for-the-body-of-patroclus]]). Iris tells Achilles what the Trojans want with the body, Hector to fix its head on the stakes (18.175–177; see [[honoring-the-dead]]). See [[three-times-and-a-fourth]] and [[would-have-had-not]].
