---
summary: Cronus is named only in his son's titles, in the mouths of the speakers: “lord Zeus, son of Cronus”, to whom Heracles was dearest, the son of Cronus “throned on high” who does not know that Hera has sent Iris, “the son of crooked-counseling Cronus” who has granted Hector glory, Hera's “most dread son of Cronus”, and “Zeus, son of Cronus”, who has given Thetis her sorrows.
---

In Book 18 Cronus appears only in the titles of his son, and only in speeches (see [[zeus]] and [[son-of-cronus]]). Achilles says that Heracles was “the dearest of all to lord Zeus, son of Cronus” (Διὶ Κρονίωνι ἄνακτι, 18.118; see [[heracles]]). Iris tells Achilles that “the son of Cronus, throned on high, does not know” that Hera has sent her (Κρονίδης ὑψίζυγος, 18.185; see [[high-throned]] and [[iris]]), the title that Agamemnon gave Zeus when he foretold that the son of Cronus would shake his aegis over the Trojans for their deceit (4.166–168), and Hector when he said that the son of Cronus had not fulfilled the oaths (7.69). Hector boasts that “the son of crooked-counseling Cronus has granted me / to win glory at the ships” (18.293–294; see [[crooked-counseling]] and [[hector]]): Κρόνου πάϊς ἀγκυλομήτεω ends the verse here as in Books 2, 4, 9, 12 and 16 (2.205, 2.319, 4.75, 9.37, 12.450, 16.431). Hera answers Zeus' taunt with her verse, “Most dread son of Cronus, what a thing you have said!” (αἰνότατε Κρονίδη, 18.361; see [[most-dread]] and [[hera]]), and claims to be the best of goddesses “by birth” (18.365), in the verses with which in Book 4 she followed “crooked-counseling Cronus fathered me, the most senior” (4.59–61). Thetis tells Hephaestus of the sorrows that “Zeus, son of Cronus, has given me beyond all of them” (Κρονίδης Ζεύς, 18.431; see [[thetis]]).
