---
summary: Achilles sees “the long-haired Achaeans” driven back to the ships, and Zeus tells Hera that “the long-haired Achaeans” must have been born of her.
---

Achilles, before the news: “Ah me, why then again are the long-haired Achaeans / driven in confusion toward the ships, in terror across the plain?” (18.6–7; [[achaeans]]). Zeus, when Achilles has shown himself at the ditch: “truly then, from your own self / the long-haired Achaeans must have been born” (18.358–359; [[hera]]). The formula that names the whole army is used once in fear for it and once in mockery of the goddess who favors it.
