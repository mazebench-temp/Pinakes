---
summary: The Nereid Halia is “ox-eyed” in the catalogue of the sea goddesses, and Hera is “ox-eyed queen Hera” three times: when she sends the Sun down early and in her exchange with Zeus.
---

**Halia.** In the catalogue of the Nereids who gather around Thetis: “Nesaea and Speio and Thoe and ox-eyed Halia” (18.40; [[halia]], [[nereids]]). The epithet of the queen of the gods is given here to a sea goddess, as it is given to mortal women in Books 3 and 7 (3.144, 7.10).

**Hera.** “Then ox-eyed queen Hera sent the tireless Sun, / unwilling, to go back to the streams of Oceanus” (18.239–240; [[helios]]). Zeus taunts her when Achilles has been roused: “You have had your way after all, ox-eyed queen Hera” (18.357), and “Then ox-eyed queen Hera answered him” (18.360; [[queenly]], [[hera]], [[most-dread]]).
