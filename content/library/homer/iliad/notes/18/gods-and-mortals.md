summary: The book measures the distance between gods and mortals through Thetis, given against her will to a mortal husband (18.85–87, 18.432–434), and her son, who is to die. Achilles wishes that strife might perish “from among gods and from among men” (18.107); Hera argues that if a mortal can accomplish his purpose against another man, so can the best of goddesses (18.362–367); and on the Shield Ares and Athena lead an army, golden and taller than the people (18.516–519).
refs: [18.85-87, 18.432-434]
---

Achilles wishes that his mother had stayed among the immortal goddesses of the sea, and that Peleus had married a mortal wife (18.86–87; see [[thetis-in-the-house-of-peleus]] and [[marriage]]). Hephaestus' refuge with Thetis and Eurynome was known to no one, “neither of the gods nor of mortal men” (18.404). On the Shield the gods are drawn larger, “as gods should be”, and the fighters fight “like living mortals” (18.518, 18.539; see [[the-two-cities]]).
