---
summary: Hephaestus' tripods are to enter “the divine assembly” of themselves; and on the shield, in the printed text, “a divine singer” sings among the dancers.
---

**The tripods.** “so that of themselves they might enter the divine assembly / and go back again to his house, a wonder to see” (18.376–377; [[tripod]], [[hephaestus]]). θεῖος ἀγών is the gathering of the gods, as in Book 7 (7.298); Walter Leaf compares these self-moving tripods with the gold and silver dogs that Hephaestus made for Alcinous (*Odyssey* 7.91–94) and with the golden handmaids (18.417–420; [[the-golden-handmaids]]; *Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 18.376).

**The singer.** “and among them a divine singer sang / to the lyre” (18.604–605; [[the-shield-of-achilles]]). The words stand in the Odyssey at the wedding feast in Menelaus' house (*Odyssey* 4.17–18); editors since Wolf have printed them here on the authority of Athenaeus, and Leaf thinks them no part of any recognized text (see the translation's note on 18.604–606).
