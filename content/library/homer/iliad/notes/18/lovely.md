summary: Hector says that Troy's treasures have gone to Phrygia and “lovely Maeonia”; on the shield one city's besiegers would divide all the wealth that “the lovely citadel” held; the book adds ἐπήρατον.
grc: [ἐπήρατον]
---

**Maeonia.** “and many have gone to Phrygia and lovely Maeonia, / possessions sold away, since great Zeus grew angry” (18.291–292; [[maeonia]], [[phrygia]]).

**The city at war.** The besiegers are divided, “whether to sack it or to divide in two all / the wealth that the lovely citadel held within it” (18.511–512; [[the-shield-of-achilles]]). Hector will think of the same terms, in the same verse, when he waits for Achilles (22.120–121). The accusative ἐπήρατον is new to the entry.
