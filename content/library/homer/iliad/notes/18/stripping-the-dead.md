summary: Hector “stripped off the armor” of Achilles from Patroclus when he had cut him down (18.82–83), and he has it, Antilochus says, while the Achaeans fight over the naked corpse (18.20–21). Achilles vows to bring back Hector's armor and head before he buries Patroclus (18.334–335).
grc: ["δῃώσας ἀπέδυσε", "τεύχεʼ ἔχει κορυθαίολος Ἕκτωρ"]

---

The book turns the custom against Hector: the armor he stripped is the reason Achilles needs new arms, and the vow promises him the same fate (see [[exchange-of-armor]] and [[honoring-the-dead]]). On the Shield the fighters drag away one another's dead (18.540; see [[the-two-cities]]).
