---
summary: Athenaeus uses “the Hoplopoiia” as the name of this part of the poem, once for the boy who sings to the lyre at the vintage, and again in his attack on Aristarchus, who, he says, took the singer out of the Cretan dance on the Shield and put him into Menelaus' feast in the Odyssey.
---

- Homer knew dancing to song: “and in the Hoplopoiia”, while a boy plays the lyre, the others leap before him in song and dance (*Deipnosophistae* 1.15d–e, on 18.569–572): [[title-of-book-18]].
- The singer and the tumblers of the Cretan dance, the leading of the song, and Aristarchus' supposed cut (*Deipnosophistae* 5.180c–181c, on 18.590–594 and 18.603–606): [[the-singer-on-the-shield]].
