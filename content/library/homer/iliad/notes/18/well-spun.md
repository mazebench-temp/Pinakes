---
summary: The young men of the dance on the shield wear tunics “well-spun, shining softly with oil.”
---

“The girls had on fine linen, and the young men wore tunics / well-spun, shining softly with oil” (18.595–596; [[tunic]], [[dance]], [[the-shield-of-achilles]]). Walter Leaf thinks oil was in fact used to give cloth a gloss, as in the palace of Alcinous (*Odyssey* 7.107), and that ἦκα, “softly,” turns the shine of the tunics into a sheen (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 18.596).
