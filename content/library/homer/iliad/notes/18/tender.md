---
summary: The Dardanian women will wipe the tears “from her soft cheeks”; Iris says Hector means to cut Patroclus' head “from the tender neck”; the book adds ἁπαλάων.
grc: [ἁπαλάων]
---

**The cheeks.** “and make some woman among the Trojan and the deep-bosomed Dardanian women, / with both her hands, from her soft cheeks, / wiping away the tears, groan without ceasing” (18.122–124; [[deep-bosomed]], [[swarming]]). The genitive plural ἁπαλάων is new to the entry.

**The neck.** Iris: “and his heart bids him fix the head / on the stakes, when he has cut it from the tender neck” (18.176–177; [[hector]], [[patroclus]]). ἁπαλῆς ἀπὸ δειρῆς is what the son of Oileus did to Imbrius (13.202). Aristarchus held that Hector meant to exchange the body, not to mutilate it, so that Iris' words are a pious fraud (see the translation's note on 18.154–156 and 18.175–177).
