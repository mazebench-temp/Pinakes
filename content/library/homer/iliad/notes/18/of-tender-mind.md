“And girls and young men, with light hearts, / were carrying the honey-sweet fruit in woven baskets” (18.567–568; [[honey-sweet]], [[woven]], [[the-vintage]]). The two words of Astyanax' epithet ἀταλάφρων stand apart: Walter Leaf renders them “in childish glee,” notes that ἀταλός is regularly used of children, and compares the young girls of the underworld, παρθενικαί τʼ ἀταλαί (*Odyssey* 11.39; *Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 18.567). The phrase is new to the entry.
