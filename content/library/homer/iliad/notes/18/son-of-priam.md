summary: Hector is “son of Priam, like a flame in his courage” as he catches up with the body, and the Ajaxes cannot frighten “Hector, son of Priam,” away from it.
---

“for once more the army and the horses caught up with him, / and Hector, son of Priam, like a flame in his courage” (18.153–154; [[like-a-flame]]), and at the end of the simile of the lion, “so the two Ajaxes, helmeted men, could not / frighten Hector, son of Priam, away from the body” (18.163–164; [[the-two-ajaxes]], [[helmeted]], [[lion-on-a-carcass]]).
