---
summary: At the news of Patroclus' death Achilles lies in the dust “great over a great space” (18.26), in the words used of the dead Cebriones (16.776).
---

The phrase, μέγας μεγαλωστί, was said of a dead man, Cebriones, lying in the dust “forgetful of his horsemanship” (16.776; Walter Leaf, *Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 18.26). Achilles lies like the dead, with dust on his head and ash on his tunic (18.23–27; see [[lament]]). In the Odyssey the shade of Agamemnon tells Achilles that he lay so himself, dead in the whirl of dust while the Trojans and Achaeans fought around him (*Odyssey* 24.38–40).
