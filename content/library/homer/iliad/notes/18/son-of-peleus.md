---
summary: The patronymic is Antilochus' first word to Achilles and Iris' when she rouses him; the fire blazes above the head of “the great-hearted son of Peleus”; Polydamas fears “the swift-footed son of Peleus”; and the son of Peleus leads the lament.
---

**The news and the summons.** Antilochus begins: “Ah me, son of wise-hearted Peleus, truly you will hear / grievous news” (18.18–19; [[wise-hearted]], [[grim]]). Iris comes “to the son of Peleus” from Olympus (18.166; [[wind-footed]]) and rouses him: “Rise, son of Peleus, most terrible of all men” (18.170; [[most-terrible-of-men]]). At the ditch the fire blazes “above the head of the great-hearted son of Peleus” (18.226; [[great-hearted]]).

**The Trojan assembly.** Polydamas: “But now I am terribly afraid of the swift-footed son of Peleus” (18.261), and “now night has stopped the swift-footed son of Peleus” (18.267; [[swift-footed]], [[polydamas]]).

**The lament.** “And among them the son of Peleus led the unceasing lamentation” (18.316; [[swarming]], [[lament]]). See [[achilles]] and [[peleus]].
