summary: Charis and then Hephaestus greet the goddess with the same verse: “Why, Thetis of the long robes, have you come to our house”; the book adds τανύπεπλε.
grc: [τανύπεπλε]
---

“Why, Thetis of the long robes, have you come to our house, / respected and dear? Before now you have not come often” (18.385–386 = 18.424–425; [[thetis]], [[charis-wife-of-hephaestus]], [[hephaestus]]). The greeting is Calypso's to Hermes in the Odyssey, with the name and epithet of the guest changed (*Odyssey* 5.87–88); Charis goes on with Calypso's invitation (18.387 = *Odyssey* 5.91), and Hephaestus with her promise (18.426–427 = *Odyssey* 5.89–90; Walter Leaf, *Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 18.386). The vocative τανύπεπλε is new to the entry.
