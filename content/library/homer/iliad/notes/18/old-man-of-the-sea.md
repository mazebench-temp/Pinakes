# γέρονθʼ ἅλιον (18.141), the accusative of the title; "beside her aged father" (18.36 = 1.358).
grc: ["γέρονθʼ ἅλιον"]
refs: [18.36]
passages:
  - 18.35-38 | Thetis beside her aged father
  - 18.140-142 | The Nereids go home to their father
---

**Her aged father.** Achilles groans aloud, “and his lady mother heard him / as she sat in the depths of the sea beside her aged father” (18.35–36; see [[thetis]]), the verse of Book 1, when she heard him weeping by the shore (18.36 = 1.358). All the Nereids gather around her in the depths (18.37–49; see [[nereids]]), and when she leaves her son she sends them home: “Now go down into the broad bosom of the sea, / to see the Old Man of the Sea and our father's house, / and tell him everything” (18.140–142). The title is the one by which Thetis was called “daughter of the Old Man of the Sea” in the quarrel on Olympus (1.538, 1.556), and Achilles' mother will be called so again by Aeneas and by Achilles himself (20.107, 24.562).

**Nereus.** The Iliad never gives him a name, but it names his daughters together as the Νηρηΐδες (18.38, 18.49, 18.52), a patronymic, “daughters of Nereus”. In the Odyssey the daughters of the Old Man of the Sea mourn Achilles with his mother at his funeral (*Odyssey* 24.58–59). Pausanias found that the people of Gythium took the name of a sea god they worshipped, the Old Man, from Thetis' words here (Pausanias 3.21.9).
