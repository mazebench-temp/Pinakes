---
summary: Achilles' lost armor was bronze, gleaming (18.131), and Patroclus lies torn by the sharp bronze (18.236). The house of Hephaestus is of bronze (18.371), and he casts bronze into the fire with tin, gold and silver (18.474). Achilles' voice at the ditch is “the bronze voice” (18.222), the water for Patroclus' bath boils in the gleaming bronze of the tripod (18.349), and the ambushers on the Shield sit “wrapped in gleaming bronze” (18.522).
---

ὄπα χάλκεον is a voice of bronze: Zenodotus read χαλκέην, and Walter Leaf explains the epithet as unwearied and perhaps ringing, after the trumpet of the simile (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 18.222; see the translation's note on 18.222).
