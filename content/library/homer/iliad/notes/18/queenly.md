---
summary: Thetis is Achilles' “lady mother,” who hears him groan and stands beside him; Hera is “ox-eyed queen Hera” when she sends the Sun down and in her exchange with Zeus.
---

**Thetis.** “And he groaned aloud terribly; and his lady mother heard him / as she sat in the depths of the sea beside her aged father” (18.35–36), as she heard him weeping by the sea in Book 1 (1.357–358; [[thetis]], [[old-man-of-the-sea]]). She comes to him: “And as he groaned heavily his lady mother stood beside him” (18.70; [[mothers-and-sons]]).

**Hera.** “Then ox-eyed queen Hera sent the tireless Sun, / unwilling, to go back to the streams of Oceanus” (18.239–240; [[helios]], [[tireless]]); and when the Achaeans have the body, Zeus says, “You have had your way after all, ox-eyed queen Hera,” and “ox-eyed queen Hera” answers him (18.357, 18.360; [[ox-eyed]], [[hera]]).
