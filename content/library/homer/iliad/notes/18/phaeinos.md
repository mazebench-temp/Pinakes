summary: Hephaestus sits down near Thetis “on a shining throne”; he sets “a shining rim” around the shield and makes Achilles “a corselet brighter than the light of fire”; the book adds φαεινότερον.
grc: [φαεινότερον]
en: [brighter]
---

**The throne.** “and he, hobbling / near to where Thetis was, sat down on a shining throne” (18.421–422; [[throne]], [[hephaestus]]).

**The arms.** “And first he made a shield, great and heavy, / working it intricately all over, and he set around it a shining rim, / threefold, gleaming” (18.478–480; [[marmareos]], [[the-shield-of-achilles]]); and “he made him a corselet brighter than the light of fire” (18.610; [[corselet]]). The comparative φαεινότερον is new to the entry; the Homeric Hymn to Aphrodite gives the same phrase to the goddess's robe (*Homeric Hymn to Aphrodite* 86).
