---
summary: Antilochus comes to Achilles “shedding hot tears,” and Achilles follows Patroclus' litter “shedding hot tears.”
---

The same half-verse marks the news and the sight. Antilochus came “shedding hot tears, and told his grievous news” (18.17; [[antilochus]]); and when Patroclus is carried out of the battle, “swift-footed Achilles followed among them / shedding hot tears, when he saw his trusted companion / lying on the litter, torn by the sharp bronze” (18.234–236; [[trusted-companion]], [[sharp]], [[shedding-tears]]). Patroclus himself came to Achilles in Book 16 “shedding hot tears like a spring of dark water” (16.3) to ask for his armor.
