# Unnamed: "killed him among the champions, and given glory to Hector" (18.455-456).
refs: [18.455-456]
---

**Thetis' account.** When Thetis tells Hephaestus why she has come, she ends the story of the wrath with the death of Patroclus: “and on that very day they would have sacked the city, had not Apollo, / after the valiant son of Menoetius had done much harm, / killed him among the champions, and given glory to Hector” (18.454–456; see [[thetis]], [[patroclus]] and [[would-have-had-not]]). The narrative of Book 16 said the same of that day: “There the sons of the Achaeans would have taken high-gated Troy / at the hands of Patroclus; for he raged ahead with his spear, / had not Phoebus Apollo stood on the well-built tower” (16.698–700), and three times he thrust Patroclus back from the wall (16.702–711). In the battle that followed Apollo struck him from behind and loosened his armor, Euphorbus wounded him and Hector killed him (16.788–857; see [[the-death-of-patroclus]] and [[euphorbus]]). Patroclus named all three: “But deadly fate and the son of Leto have killed me, / and of men Euphorbus; and you are the third to slay me” (16.849–850). Thetis, like Achilles' horse Xanthus in Book 19, gives the killing to Apollo alone: 18.456 = 19.414, where the horse says that it was not their slowness but the best of the gods who killed Patroclus among the champions and gave glory to Hector (19.413–414; see [[the-horses-of-achilles]]). Walter Leaf reports that Aristarchus athetized 18.444–456 (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 18.429; see [[thetis]]).
