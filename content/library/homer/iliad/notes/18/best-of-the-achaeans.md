---
summary: Achilles is “the best” in his mother's words and in his own. Thetis is the “unhappy mother of the best of men” (δυσαριστοτόκεια, 18.54); her prophecy that “the best of the Myrmidons” would die while Achilles lived has come true in Patroclus (18.10–11); and Achilles calls himself such as none of the Achaeans in war, “though in the assembly others too are better” (18.105–106).
refs: [18.10, 18.54, 18.105-106]
---

Achilles' admission is the other half of the Book 1 quarrel over the title (1.91, 1.244, 1.412): he is the best in war and not in counsel. The scholiast defended the self-praise as customary, and Payne Knight and Heyne rejected the two lines; Walter Leaf would object at most to 18.106 (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 18.105; see the translation's note on 18.105–106). Hera claims the same title among goddesses, “the best of goddesses / on both counts, by birth and because I am called / your wife” (18.364–366; see [[aristos]]).
