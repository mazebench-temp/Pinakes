summary: The Trojan assembly keeps the outline of the type-scene and breaks its order. The Trojans gather “before they thought of supper” (18.245); the assembly is held standing, since no one dares to sit (18.246–248); two speakers speak, Polydamas and Hector (18.249–309); the people shout their assent (18.310–313); and instead of a dissolution they take their supper by companies (18.314).
refs: [18.245-248, 18.310, 18.314]
---

“In a regular assembly of course all sat but the speaker; that all should stand is a mark of confusion and haste” (Walter Leaf, *Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 18.246; see [[sat-down-stood-up]] and [[deos]]). Hector's earlier assembly on the plain, after his victory in Book 8, ended in the same way, with supper, fires and a watch (8.489–542; see [[war-and-night]]), and Polydamas recalls the nights the Trojans spent then beside the ships (18.259–260; Leaf on 18.259). Hector's answer opens with the line with which he answered Polydamas at the wall, “Looking at him darkly” (18.284–285 = 12.230–231; see [[looking-darkly]]). See [[assembly]].
