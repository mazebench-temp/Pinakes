summary: The book of Thetis and her son. She hears his groan in the sea, comes with the Nereids and takes his head in her hands, and he tells her that she will not welcome him home (18.35–126); she holds him back from the war until she brings armor (18.127–137, 18.189–191, 18.216), and goes to Hephaestus to beg it for her “swift-fated son”, telling him her story as a mother's (18.428–461). Hephaestus remembers the mother who wished to hide him away because he was lame, and the goddesses who took him in (18.394–405).

**The mother's lament.** Thetis mourns her son as if he were dead, and calls herself “unhappy mother of the best of men” (18.54; see [[lament]] and [[aristos]]). She reared him “like a plant on the slope of an orchard” (18.57; see [[like-a-sapling]]), and she will not welcome him home (18.59–60). Before Hephaestus she tells the whole of her life as her son's: she bore him and reared him, sent him to Troy and cannot help him, and his armor is lost (18.436–461; see [[retelling]]).

**The son's answer.** Achilles answers her first question, “My child, why are you weeping?” (18.73), with Patroclus, and turns her grief back on her: the gods gave her to a mortal “so that you too might have countless grief in your heart / for your son who has perished” (18.88–89; see [[penthos]]). He refuses to be kept from the battle: “Do not keep me from the battle, though you love me; you will not persuade me” (18.126). He obeys her in one thing, to wait for the armor; at the ditch “he did not mingle; for he heeded his mother's shrewd command” (18.216).

**Hephaestus' mother.** Hera, “my dog-faced mother”, wished to hide away her lame son, and he fell far; Thetis, Achilles' mother, took him in (18.395–405; see [[hera]] and [[fall-of-hephaestus]]). The goddess who cannot save her own son from death once saved another's. The book ends with the armor set “before the mother of Achilles” and the mother leaping from Olympus like a hawk to bring it (18.615–617).
