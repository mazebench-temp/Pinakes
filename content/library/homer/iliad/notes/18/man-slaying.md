summary: The Achaeans flee “before man-slaying Hector”; Achilles lays “his man-slaying hands” on the breast of Patroclus as he leads the lament; the book adds ἀνδροφόνους.
grc: [ἀνδροφόνους]
---

**Hector.** “but the Achaeans, / with a wondrous shout, before man-slaying Hector, / in their flight reached the ships and the Hellespont” (18.148–150; [[wondrous]], [[hector]]).

**Achilles' hands.** “And among them the son of Peleus led the unceasing lamentation, / laying his man-slaying hands on the breast of his companion” (18.316–317; [[lament]], [[patroclus]]). Hector's standing epithet is given to the hands that will kill him. The verse returns at the funeral (23.18), and Priam will kiss the same terrible, man-slaying hands (24.478–479). The accusative ἀνδροφόνους is new to the entry.
