---
summary: The book opens with a prophecy fulfilled. Achilles fears that the gods have fulfilled what his mother once foretold, that the best of the Myrmidons would leave the light of the sun while he still lived (18.6–11), and Antilochus' news confirms it. Thetis tells him that what he prayed for has been fulfilled by Zeus (18.74–79), and that his own death is ready straight after Hector's (18.95–96).
refs: [18.4, 18.8-11, 18.74-79, 18.95-96]
en: [fulfilled]
---

The verb τελέω, “fulfill”, frames the scene: Achilles ponders “things that had now been fulfilled” (τετελεσμένα, 18.4), Thetis says “These things have been fulfilled for you / by Zeus” (18.74–75), and he answers “these things the Olympian has indeed fulfilled for me” (18.79). The prophecy of 18.9–11 does not fit the end of Book 17, where Achilles' mother had not told him that his dearest companion had perished (17.408–411); Walter Leaf notes the contradiction, and reports that Rhianus and Aristophanes left out 18.10–11, perhaps because Patroclus was a Locrian from Opus and not a Myrmidon (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 18.10; see the translation's note on 18.10–11).
