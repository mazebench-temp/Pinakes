---
summary: Thetis tells Hephaestus the story of the Iliad from her son's side. She repeats her lament to the Nereids (18.437–443 = 18.56–62) and then the plot from the girl taken by Agamemnon to the armor lost with Patroclus: the Achaeans penned at the sterns, the elders' entreaty and the gifts, the refusal, Patroclus sent out in Achilles' armor, killed by Apollo, and the glory given to Hector (18.444–461).
passages:
  - 18.429-461 | Thetis tells Hephaestus her sorrows
---

In Book 1 Achilles told his mother the story of the quarrel (1.365–392); now she tells it again, and only what bears on her son is kept. The war becomes his grief: Achilles “was wasting his heart away” for the girl, and he lies on the ground, “grieving in his heart”, for Patroclus (18.446, 18.461; see [[achos]]). She says nothing of Hector killing Patroclus or of Euphorbus: Apollo killed him “among the champions, and given glory to Hector” (18.455–456; see [[apollo]] and [[kydos]]). The fighting at the Scaean gates “all day long” is an exaggeration, as Walter Leaf notes, “but this is not the place where we need expect the accuracy of a chronicler” (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 18.453; see the translation's note on 18.453). The armor her son had, his trusted companion lost (18.460–461): the story ends where her request begins (see [[the-armor-of-peleus]], [[supplication]] and [[mothers-and-sons]]).
