---
summary: The house of Hephaestus is the one “the crook-footed god had built for himself.”
---

“imperishable, starry, standing out among the immortals, / of bronze, which the crook-footed god had built for himself” (18.370–371; [[house]], [[hephaestus]]). The first use of the name in the Iliad comes as Thetis arrives at the house; a few verses later the god rises from the anvil block, “limping; and his slender shins moved nimbly beneath him” (18.410–411), and tells of the mother who wished to hide him away because he was lame (18.396–397; [[fall-of-hephaestus]], [[dog-face]]).
