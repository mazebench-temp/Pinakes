---
summary: “The best of the Myrmidons” in Thetis' prophecy is Patroclus (18.10), and she calls herself δυσαριστοτόκεια, “unhappy mother of the best of men” (18.54), a word found only here. Twelve of the Trojans' best men die among their own chariots and spears when Achilles shouts (18.230), and Hera claims to be “the best of goddesses” (18.364).
grc: [δυσαριστοτόκεια]
---

δυσαριστοτόκεια = δυσ- (“ill-”) + ἄριστος (“best”) + τόκος (“bearing”): the scholiast glosses it “having borne the best to an evil end” (Walter Leaf, *Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 18.54; see the translation's note on 18.54). See [[best-of-the-achaeans]] and [[mothers-and-sons]].
