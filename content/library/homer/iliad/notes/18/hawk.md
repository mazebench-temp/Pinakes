---
summary: The hawk of Book 18 is Thetis, who leaps down from snowy Olympus like a hawk, carrying the armor that Hephaestus has made for her son (18.616–617).
---

ἣ δʼ ἴρηξ ὣς ἆλτο κατʼ Οὐλύμπου νιφόεντος, “And she like a hawk leapt down from snowy Olympus” (18.616; see [[like-a-hawk]]). It is the book's last image. In Book 15 the hawk was a god going down to the battle, Apollo sent from Ida to rouse Hector (15.237–238); in Book 16 it was a man, Patroclus charging the Lycians (16.582–583); in Book 17 the bird of prey that scattered the Achaeans was the falcon, the κίρκος (17.755–757; see [[falcon]]). Here it is a mother. The hawk's speed carries the arms of Achilles from the forge of Hephaestus to the ships, and with them the end of his absence from the war (see [[thetis]] and [[the-making-of-the-arms]]).
