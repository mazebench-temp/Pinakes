summary: The bath of the book is given to the dead. Achilles has his companions set a great tripod on the fire, wash the clotted blood from Patroclus and anoint him richly with olive oil, and they fill his wounds with ointment nine years old (18.343–351). On the Shield the Bear alone has no share in “the baths of Oceanus” (18.489).
grc: ["ἀλείφατος ἐννεώροιο"]
en: [wash the clotted blood, for the bath, washed him, anointed him richly with olive oil, ointment nine years old, baths]
---

The verses that heat the water for Patroclus are those that heat a bath for Odysseus in Alcinous' palace: the great tripod set over the fire, the water poured in, the wood kindled, “The fire played around the belly of the tripod, and the water grew warm” (18.344–348; *Odyssey* 8.434–437; see [[tripod]]). After Hector's death the heralds are told, in the same words, to set a great tripod over the fire so that Achilles may wash the clotted blood from himself, and he refuses under oath until Patroclus is burned (23.39–47). The ointment is “nine years old”: Walter Leaf takes ἐννεώροιο as a round number for full maturity, oil being the better for keeping, and would write ἐννώροιο (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 18.351; see the translation's note on 18.351). See [[honoring-the-dead]].

The stars bathe when they set in the stream of Oceanus, as the star of late summer “shines out bright when it has bathed in Oceanus” (5.6; see [[the-autumn-star]]); the Bear never sets (18.489; see [[alone-without-a-share]] and [[oceanus]]).
