summary: On the shield of Achilles Tumult is in the throng with Strife and deadly Doom in the battle of the city at war. The common word is the uproar that Achilles raises among the Trojans with his shout from the ditch.
passages:
  - 18.535-540 | Strife, Tumult and Doom on the shield
---

**On the shield.** In the city at war on the shield of Achilles, “among them Strife and Tumult were in the throng, and deadly Doom” (18.535), while Doom holds the wounded and the unwounded and drags the dead through the fray by the feet (18.536–538; see [[strife]], [[ker-doom]] and [[the-shield-of-achilles]]). Tumult has appeared in person once before, when Enyo brought him into the fighting beside Ares, “shameless in combat” (5.593; see [[enyo]]). The *Shield of Heracles* repeats the verse with another verb, “and Strife and Tumult rushed about” (*Shield* 156; Walter Leaf, *Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 18.535; see [[shield-of-heracles]]).

**The uproar.** The common noun, which the entry does not claim, is the uproar of the rout that Achilles raises with his shout at the ditch: “and among the Trojans he roused untold tumult” (ἄσπετον … κυδοιμόν, 18.218; see [[achilles]] and the translation's note on 18.218).
