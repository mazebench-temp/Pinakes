summary: Thrasymedes and his brother Antilochus fight apart from the struggle for the body, as Nestor had ordered, and do not know that Patroclus is dead; when Antilochus runs with the news to Achilles, Menelaus sends “brilliant Thrasymedes” to the Pylians he has left (17.377–383, 17.702–706).
refs: [17.377, 17.379-383]
---

“But two men had not yet learned, / two glorious men, Thrasymedes and Antilochus, / that noble Patroclus was dead, but they still thought / that he was alive and fighting the Trojans in the front of the throng” (17.377–380; see [[antilochus]] and [[patroclus]]). ἀνέρε κυδαλίμω, “two glorious men”, is a dual, and the two brothers fight together, “watchful against the death and the panic of their companions”, apart from the mist around the body, “since so Nestor had commanded” (17.381–383; see [[nestor]]). In the last book they fought side by side as “the sons of Nestor” and killed the two sons of Amisodarus (16.317–329).

When Menelaus sends Antilochus to Achilles with the news, the Pylians are left without him, and Menelaus “sent brilliant Thrasymedes to them” (17.702–705; see [[menelaus]] and [[pylians]]). Thrasymedes was first among the captains of the watch on the night of the embassy (9.81); here he takes his brother's place at the head of their father's men.
