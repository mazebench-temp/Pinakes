---
summary: Automedon asks the Ajaxes and Menelaus to keep off “the pitiless day” from him and Alcimedon, and Coeranus “kept off the pitiless day” from Idomeneus at the cost of his own life.
---

“and from us two, who are alive, keep off the pitiless day” (17.511; [[automedon]], [[alcimedon]], [[the-two-ajaxes]]); and Coeranus “came as a light to him and kept off the pitiless day, / but he himself lost his life at the hands of man-slaying Hector” (17.615–616; [[coeranus-of-lyctus]], [[idomeneus]], [[man-slaying]]). The formula νηλεὲς ἦμαρ is the day of death, which Coeranus keeps from another and meets himself. See [[death]].
