---
summary: Glory is given by Zeus, and in this book he gives it to the Trojans. “From Zeus come honor and glory”, Menelaus reminds the Achaean leaders (17.251); the Argives would have won glory “even beyond the portion of Zeus”, had Apollo not roused Aeneas (17.321); Zeus will still hold out glory to the Trojans to kill until sunset (17.453–455), and Menelaus tells Phoenix, “Zeus grants him glory” (17.566).
---

Both armies want the glory of the body: the Trojans hope “to drag him to their own city and win glory”, and the Achaeans say it would be better for the earth to gape than to let them (17.287, 17.419; see [[may-the-earth-gape]] and [[plan-of-zeus]]).
