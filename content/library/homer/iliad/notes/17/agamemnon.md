summary: Agamemnon takes no part in the fight over Patroclus. He is named once, when Menelaus calls on the leaders who drink at the public cost beside the two sons of Atreus (17.248–251); everywhere else in the book the son of Atreus is Menelaus.
# Ἀτρεΐδης and Ἀτρεΐδη at 17.12, 17.46, 17.60, 17.71, 17.138 and 17.580 are Menelaus.
except: [17.12, 17.46, 17.60, 17.71, 17.138, 17.580]
refs: [17.249]
---

Menelaus, calling for help over the body, speaks to the leaders “you who beside the sons of Atreus, Agamemnon and Menelaus, / drink at the public cost, and each of you gives orders / to his people” (17.249–251; see [[menelaus]] and [[atreidae]]). It is the only time Agamemnon is named in the book. The feasting of the chiefs at the king's cost is his in earlier books: he honors Idomeneus at the feast “when the sparkling wine of the elders” is mixed, and Nestor tells him in Book 9 that his huts are full of wine, “you have all the means of entertaining” (4.257–260, 4.343–344, 9.70–73; see [[feast-type-scene]]). Walter Leaf found it strange that Menelaus should name himself as a host beside his brother, and reports a variant of 17.249 that names Agamemnon alone (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 17.250; see the translation's note on 17.249–251).
