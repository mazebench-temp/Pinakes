---
summary: Deucalion is named once, in his son's patronymic, when Hector throws at “Idomeneus, son of Deucalion”, misses him, and kills Meriones' charioteer Coeranus (17.608–619).
---

ὃ δʼ Ἰδομενῆος ἀκόντισε Δευκαλίδαο / δίφρῳ ἐφεσταότος, “and he threw at Idomeneus, son of Deucalion, / as he stood in his chariot” (17.608–609; see [[idomeneus]] and [[hector]]). The spear misses Idomeneus by a little and kills Coeranus, who had driven up to save him (17.609–619; see [[coeranus-of-lyctus]]). Δευκαλίδης names Idomeneus three times in the poem (12.117, 13.307, 17.608).
