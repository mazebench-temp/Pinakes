summary: Menelaus and Meriones carry the body of Patroclus out of the battle as mules that have put their strong might into the work drag a beam or a great ship's timber down a rocky mountain path, worn out with labor and sweat (17.742–746).
grc: [ἡμίονοι]
---

οἳ δʼ ὥς θʼ ἡμίονοι κρατερὸν μένος ἀμφιβαλόντες / ἕλκωσʼ ἐξ ὄρεος κατὰ παιπαλόεσσαν ἀταρπὸν / ἢ δοκὸν ἠὲ δόρυ μέγα νήϊον, “and they, as mules that have put their strong might into it / drag down from the mountain along a rocky path / a beam or a great ship's timber” (17.742–744; see [[mules-dragging-a-beam]]). ἡμίονος, “half-ass”, is the other name of the mule, the beast of heavy work, which pulls a plow better than oxen (10.351–353). The likeness is of strength put to labor, not to fighting. The bearers are Menelaus and Meriones (see [[menelaus]] and [[meriones]]); in Book 23 Meriones leads the mules that drag the wood down from Ida for the pyre of Patroclus (23.110–124), and in Book 24 mules carry Hector's body back to Troy (24.697).
