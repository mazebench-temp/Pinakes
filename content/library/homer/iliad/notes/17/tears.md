**The horses.** The horses weep as men weep, and for a man. Their tears are δάκρυα θερμά, “hot tears”, the tears of the armies gathering their dead in Book 7 (7.426) and of Patroclus before Achilles (16.3; see [[hot-tears]] and [[shedding-tears]]), shed “in longing for their charioteer” (ἡνιόχοιο πόθῳ, 17.439; see [[pothe]]). They bow their heads to the ground and their manes trail in the dust, and they stand unmoving like a pillar on a tomb (17.434–440; see [[tomb]] and [[the-horses-of-achilles]]). Zeus sees them weeping and pities them (17.441; see [[pity]]).

**Men.** Ajax prays for light, and “the father pitied him as he shed tears” (17.648; see [[prayer]]). Antilochus hears the news of Patroclus' death, “and for a long time speechlessness took him, and his two eyes / filled with tears, and his strong voice was checked” (17.695–696), and runs from the battle “shedding tears” (17.700; see [[messenger-scene]]). The battle itself is “tearful” three times in the book, πολυδάκρυος, δακρυόεις, πολύδακρυς (17.192, 17.512, 17.544; see [[tearful]]). See also [[virgils-weeping-horse]].
