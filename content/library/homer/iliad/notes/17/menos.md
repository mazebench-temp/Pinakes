---
summary: Might is given and taken. Zeus breathes “noble might” into the weeping horses (μένος ἠΰ, 17.451–456), and Menelaus says that Hector “has the dread might of fire” (17.565); Menelaus threatens to loose Euphorbus' strength, and Hippothous' strength is loosed by Ajax's spear (17.29, 17.298; see [[loosened-his-limbs]]). Glaucus wishes the Trojans had “a might full of daring” (17.156).
---

The anonymous speeches over the body end, “So a man would say, and would rouse the might of each” (17.423; see [[tis-speech]]). Automedon tells Alcimedon that no one but Patroclus could hold in “the taming and the might of immortal horses” (17.476–477), and Ajax fears that the might of man-slaying Hector will not be held back from the ships (17.637–639). At the end the bearers of the body are like mules that put their strong might into dragging a beam (17.742; see [[mules-dragging-a-beam]]).
