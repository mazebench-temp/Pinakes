---
summary: Athena puts in Menelaus the daring of a fly, which keeps trying to bite, “and human blood is sweet to it.”
---

{{quote:735a2793-7697-597a-a730-8dff95de2b8a}}

The fly “keeps on trying to bite, and human blood is sweet to it” (17.572; [[gray-eyed]], [[athena]], [[menelaus]]). The word of the pleasant meal is given to the fly's hunger for blood, and so to Menelaus' eagerness to kill: he throws at once and kills Podes (17.574–580; [[podes]]).
