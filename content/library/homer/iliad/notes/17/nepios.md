---
summary: The narrator's fools in this book are Trojans who hope to win the body or the horses: they hope to drag Patroclus away from Ajax, “the fools” (17.236), and Chromius and Aretus hope to kill Automedon and drive off the horses, “the fools; they were not to come back without bloodshed” (17.497). In speeches the word gives two sayings: “a fool learns a thing once it is done” (17.32) and Ajax's “even one who is an utter fool / would know” (17.629–630).
---

The other uses are the young: the lion's “little ones” (17.134) and the Trojans' “little children”, whom Hector says he gathered the allies to protect (17.223; see [[a-fool-learns-once-it-is-done]], [[even-a-fool-can-see]] and [[foresight]]).
