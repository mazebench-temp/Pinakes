---
summary: The fight for Patroclus' body is the one Zeus foretold in Book 8, “over Patroclus dead”. He gives Hector great power for a time, renews for the Trojans the term he set in Book 11, and gives them the victory that turns the battle; the next step of the plan waits on Achilles, who has not been told.
passages:
  - 17.198-209 | Zeus' word over Hector
  - 17.441-456 | The term renewed
  - 17.626-650 | The victory that turns the battle
---

**The forecast.** In Book 8 Zeus told Hera that Hector would not cease from war “until the swift-footed son of Peleus is roused beside the ships / on the day when they fight at the sterns of the ships / in the direst strait, over Patroclus dead” (8.474–476; see [[the-death-of-patroclus]]). The fight of Book 17 is that fight, though it is fought on the plain and not at the ships (see [[the-fight-for-the-body-of-patroclus]] and the translation's note on 8.475–476). In Book 15 he told her the order of events: Hector would kill Patroclus before Ilios, “And in anger for him brilliant Achilles will kill Hector” (15.65–68; see the note on Book 15). Book 17 lies between those two clauses. Patroclus is dead, and Achilles does not yet know it (17.401–411; see [[achilles]] and [[thetis]]).

**Power for a day.** When Hector puts on the armor of Achilles, Zeus foretells his death and gives him great power in recompense, and he nods in the verse of his promise to Thetis (17.201–209; 17.209 = 1.528; see [[zeus]] and [[neuo]]). Over the weeping horses he renews to himself, now for the Trojans, the term he set for Hector in Book 11, glory “to kill, until they come to the well-benched ships, / and the sun goes down and sacred darkness comes on” (17.453–455; 11.192–194; see [[helios]]). The plan honors Achilles by the Achaeans' defeat, not by their destruction (13.347–350): Zeus keeps the horses of Achilles from Hector and lets them carry Automedon safe (17.448–453; see [[the-horses-of-achilles]]), and when Ajax weeps he gives him light to fight in (17.645–650; see [[ajax]]).

**The mind of Zeus.** The men on the field know the plan only as Zeus' will. Hector answers Glaucus with the narrator's rule of Book 16 (17.176–178 ≈ 16.688–690; see [[the-mind-of-zeus]]), Apollo tells Aeneas that Zeus wills victory for the Trojans (17.331–332; see [[apollo]]), and Ajax sees that he is giving them “the victory that turns the battle” (17.626–632). Achilles used to hear from his mother “the purpose of great Zeus” (17.409); this time she did not tell him.

**A turned mind.** Only once does the book say that Zeus' mind has changed: Athena comes down to rouse the Danaans because “his mind had now turned” (17.546; see [[athena]]). Walter Leaf reports that 17.545 was athetized or left out by ancient editors and thinks the rejection must have covered 546 as well; he argues that there is no change in Zeus' designs, since in 593 he goes on helping the Trojans and is steadily carrying out his purpose of letting the Achaeans be driven back to the ships (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 17.545; see the translation's note on 17.545–546). The book ends with the Achaeans in flight around the ditch (17.759–761), and the next step of the forecast is Achilles' anger.
