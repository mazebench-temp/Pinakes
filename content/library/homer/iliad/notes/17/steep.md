---
summary: Glaucus foresees “sheer destruction” for Troy and Ajax for the Achaeans; Apollo asks how Aeneas could protect “steep Ilios”; and the Achaeans ward off “sheer slaughter” from one another.
---

**Sheer destruction.** The same verse-end serves both sides. Glaucus threatens to take the Lycians home, “and sheer destruction will appear for Troy” (17.155; [[glaucus]]), and Ajax says to Menelaus that Hector covers everything “a cloud of war, / Hector, and for us in turn sheer destruction appears” (17.243–244; [[ajax]]).

**Ilios.** Apollo, in the likeness of Periphas: “Aeneas, how could you protect, even in defiance of a god, / steep Ilios?” (17.327–328; [[periphas-son-of-epytus]], [[aeneas]], [[troy]]).

**Slaughter.** The Danaans lost fewer men, “for they always remembered / to ward off sheer slaughter from one another in the throng” (17.364–365; [[danaans]]).
