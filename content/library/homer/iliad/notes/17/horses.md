---
summary: Most of the horses of Book 17 are the immortal pair of Achilles, who weep for Patroclus. The others are the teams and the noise of the battle: the din of horses that Hector does not fear, the swift horses with which Coeranus saves Idomeneus and dies, the men and horses alike hidden in the mist, and Laodocus wheeling Antilochus' team close by (17.175, 17.610–625, 17.644, 17.699).
---

**The horses of Achilles.** The immortal horses that the gods gave to Peleus are chased by Hector, weep for their charioteer, are pitied by Zeus, and carry Automedon back into the fight, where Hector and Aeneas try and fail to take them (17.75–78, 17.426–542; see [[the-horses-of-achilles]]).

**In the battle.** Hector tells Glaucus, “I do not shudder at battle or at the din of horses” (17.175; see [[hector]] and [[glaucus]]). The fight over the body is a toil “of men and horses” that Zeus stretches tight (17.400–401; see [[the-stretched-hide]]), and at the end a ceaseless din “of horses and of spearmen” comes upon the men who carry it (17.740–741; see [[the-burning-city]]). Idomeneus had come to the battle on foot, and Coeranus, Meriones' charioteer, saved him by driving up “the swift-footed horses”, and was killed by Hector's spear in his place; Meriones gathers up the reins and tells Idomeneus to drive to the ships, and he “lashed the fine-maned horses / toward the hollow ships; for fear had fallen on his heart” (17.605–625; see [[idomeneus]] and [[meriones]]). In the mist around the body Ajax can see no one to send to Achilles, “for they are covered in mist, the men themselves and the horses alike” (17.643–644; see [[ajax]]). Antilochus, running to Achilles with the news, leaves his armor with Laodocus, “who was wheeling his single-hoofed horses close by” (17.698–699; see [[antilochus]] and [[single-hoofed]]).
