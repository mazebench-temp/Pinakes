# Βοιώτιος (17.597), of Peneleos; Βοιώτιον (14.476) is claimed in the Book 14 note.
grc: [Βοιώτιος]
---

πρῶτος Πηνέλεως Βοιώτιος ἦρχε φόβοιο, “Peneleos the Boeotian was the first to begin the rout” (17.597; see [[peneleos]]). Βοιώτιος, “the Boeotian”, is said of a single man only here and of Promachus, killed by Acamas (14.476; see [[promachus]]). Zeus has just shaken the aegis over Ida and given victory to the Trojans (17.593–596; see [[aegis]]). Polydamas' spear has grazed Peneleos' shoulder to the bone, and Hector stabs the other leader, Leitus, “the son of great-hearted Alectryon”, on the wrist, so that he no longer hopes to hold a spear (17.598–604; see [[leitus]], [[polydamas]] and [[hector]]).

The Catalogue gave the Boeotians five leaders (2.494–495). Three have been killed, Prothoenor by Polydamas, Arcesilaus by Hector and Clonius by Agenor (14.449–457, 15.329–330, 15.340; see [[prothoenor]], [[arcesilaus]] and [[clonius]]), and the last two are wounded here. Neither is named again in the poem.
