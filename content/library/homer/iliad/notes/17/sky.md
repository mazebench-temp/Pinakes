---
summary: The sky of Book 17 is bronze: the iron din of the fight over Patroclus goes up through the barren upper air to the bronze sky (17.424–425). Athena comes down from heaven to rouse the Danaans, like the rainbow that Zeus stretches from heaven as a portent (17.544–548).
---

**The bronze sky.** ὣς οἳ μὲν μάρναντο, σιδήρειος δʼ ὀρυμαγδὸς / χάλκεον οὐρανὸν ἷκε διʼ αἰθέρος ἀτρυγέτοιο, “So they fought on, and the iron din / went up through the barren upper air to the bronze sky” (17.424–425). The noise of battle reaches the sky in a formula of the earlier books, “their shout reached the sky” (2.153, 12.338), and the gleam of the army's bronze “went up through the upper air to the sky” (2.458). Here the sky is χάλκεος, “bronze”, as in Book 5 it is πολύχαλκος, when the dust that the horses' hooves beat up rises “into the brazen sky” (5.502–504), and the din is iron; Walter Leaf takes σιδήρειος as “inflexible, unwearying” (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 17.424; see the translation's note on 17.424). The Odyssey has an iron sky, which the suitors' insolence and violence reach (*Odyssey* 15.329, 17.565). Between the battle and the sky is the αἰθήρ, the bright upper air, here ἀτρύγετος, “barren”, the epithet of the sea (see [[barren-sea]]). The verse closes the passage of the mist, in which the best men fight in darkness around the body while the rest of the armies fight “under the upper air” in the light of the sun (17.366–377; see [[like-blazing-fire]]).

**From heaven.** Athena comes down οὐρανόθεν, “from heaven”, sent by Zeus to rouse the Danaans, and she comes like a purple rainbow that Zeus stretches out for mortals “from heaven” as a portent of war or of storm (17.544–548; see [[like-rainbows]] and [[athena]]).
