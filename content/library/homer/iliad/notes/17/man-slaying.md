---
summary: Achilles' horses weep for their charioteer, fallen “at the hands of man-slaying Hector”; Coeranus dies “at the hands of man-slaying Hector”; and Ajax fears the might and “irresistible hands of man-slaying Hector.”
---

**At the hands of Hector.** The verse-end ὑφʼ Ἕκτορος ἀνδροφόνοιο, first heard in Achilles' oath (1.242), is used twice in the book. The horses of Achilles weep “ever since they first learned that their charioteer / had fallen in the dust at the hands of man-slaying Hector” (17.427–428; [[the-horses-of-achilles]], [[patroclus]]). Coeranus, who saves Idomeneus, “himself lost his life at the hands of man-slaying Hector” (17.616; [[coeranus-of-lyctus]], [[idomeneus]]). The next book uses it once more, of the Achaeans' flight before Hector to the ships (18.149).

**Ajax's fear.** When the missiles of the Trojans strike home and those of the Achaeans fall in vain, Ajax says that the Achaeans watching from the ships no longer expect that “the might and the irresistible hands of man-slaying Hector / be held back” (17.638–639; [[irresistible-hands]], [[black-ships]], [[ajax]]).
