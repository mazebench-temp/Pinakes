ἵπποις ἀΐσσων ὥς τʼ αἰγυπιὸς μετὰ χῆνας, “swooping with his horses like a vulture after geese” (17.460; see [[like-vultures]] and [[vultures]]). The geese are the wild birds of the river meadows that the eagle swooped on in Book 15, when Hector made for the ships (15.690–692; see [[the-eagle-and-the-birds]]). Here the bird of prey is an Achaean and the flock the Trojans, but the vulture kills none of them: Automedon, alone in the chariot, cannot both drive and fight (17.461–465; see [[automedon]]).
