---
summary: Menelaus is “good at the war cry” six times, in two pairs of verses that frame Ajax's two orders to him, in his answer to Athena in the likeness of Phoenix, and as he leaves the body like a lion from the farmyard.
---

The book uses the epithet in repeated verses ([[menelaus]]). “And then Ajax spoke to Menelaus, good at the war cry” (17.237) opens Ajax's order to call the leaders, and “So he spoke, and Menelaus, good at the war cry, did not disobey, / and he shouted piercingly, making himself heard to the Danaans” (17.246–247; [[exhortation]]) answers it. Four hundred lines later the same two verses frame the second order, to find Antilochus (17.651, 17.656; [[ajax]], [[antilochus]]). The war cry is what Ajax needs from him the first time: a shout that the scattered leaders can hear.

He answers the disguised Athena: “Then Menelaus, good at the war cry, spoke to her in turn” (17.560; [[phoenix]], [[athena]]). And when he goes to find Antilochus, the lion simile ends “so Menelaus, good at the war cry, went from Patroclus / very unwillingly” (17.665–666; [[lion-driven-from-the-farmyard]]).
