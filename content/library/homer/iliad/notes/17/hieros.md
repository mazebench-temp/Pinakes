---
summary: Hector sends his own armor to “sacred Ilios,” Zeus promises the Trojans victory until “sacred darkness comes on,” and Automedon is alone “in the sacred chariot”; the book adds ἱερῷ.
grc: [ἱερῷ]
---

**Ilios and the darkness.** Hector gives his own armor to the Trojans to carry “to sacred Ilios” (17.193; [[troy]], [[exchange-of-armor]]). Zeus promises the Trojans glory “to kill, until they come to the well-benched ships, / and the sun goes down and sacred darkness comes on” (17.454–455; [[well-benched]]), the verses of his promise to Hector in Book 11 (11.193–194, 11.208–209).

**The chariot.** Automedon kills no one, “for there was no way, alone as he was in the sacred chariot, / to attack with the spear and hold in the swift horses” (17.464–465; [[automedon]], [[swift-footed]]). The dative ἱερῷ is new to the entry. Walter Leaf suggests that the chariot is sacred “as drawn by steeds of divine origin” (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 17.464; see the translation's note on 17.464).
