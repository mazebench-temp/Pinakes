summary: Hector answers Glaucus' charge of cowardice with the narrator's rule of the book before: “but the mind of aegis-bearing Zeus is always stronger; / he puts even a valiant man to flight and takes away victory / easily, and at another time he himself urges him on to fight” (17.176–178). The rule that the narrator gave as the cause of Patroclus' death is Hector's excuse, and it holds against him and for him in the same book.

**Hector's answer.** Glaucus has told Hector that his fame is empty, since he dared not stand against Ajax over Patroclus' body (17.166–168; see [[glaucus]]). Hector answers that he does not fear the fight, “I tell you, I do not shudder at battle or at the din of horses” (17.175), and puts the cause in Zeus, who gives and takes away the courage of men (see [[noos]] and [[alke]]). The three verses are the narrator's of Book 16 with small changes, αἰγιόχοιο, “aegis-bearing”, in place of ἠέ περ ἀνδρῶν, “than that of men” (17.176–178 ~ 16.688–690). Then Hector asks Glaucus to stand by him and watch “whether I shall be a coward all day long, as you say” (17.179–182), and goes to put on Achilles' armor.

**The rule at work.** Zeus himself turns it both ways in the book. He gives Hector great power for the day, “in recompense, since you will not come home from the battle” (17.206–208; see [[poine]]); Apollo tells Aeneas that Zeus wills victory for the Trojans (17.331–332); and Zeus takes up the aegis, thunders from Ida, and gives victory to the Trojans and flight to the Achaeans (17.593–596; see [[aegis]]). Ajax reads the sign in the missiles that strike home for the Trojans and fall idly for the Achaeans (17.629–633; see [[even-a-fool-can-see]]). The book also says once that the mind of Zeus has turned the other way, when he sends Athena to rouse the Danaans, “for his mind had now turned” (17.545–546), two verses that Walter Leaf judges an interpolation: there is no change in the designs of Zeus, who goes on helping the Trojans (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 17.545; see the translation's note on 17.545–546).
