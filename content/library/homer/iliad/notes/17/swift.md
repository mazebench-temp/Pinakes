---
summary: Hector and Automedon are “the equal of swift Ares,” Hector follows his companions “on swift feet,” Ajax son of Oileus is “swift Ajax,” the horses bear “the swift chariot,” Automedon strikes them “with the swift whip,” and the hare and Achilles are “swift of foot”; the book adds μάστιγι θοῇ.
grc: ["μάστιγι θοῇ"]
---

**Ares.** Apollo “roused Hector against him, the equal of swift Ares” (17.72), and “Automedon, the equal of swift Ares, / stripped off his armor” (17.536–537; [[equal-of-ares]]).

**Men.** Hector, going to change his armor, overtook his companions “very quickly, not yet far off, following them on swift feet” (17.190); “swift Ajax, son of Oileus,” is the first to answer Menelaus' call (17.256–257; [[ajax-son-of-oileus]]).

**Horses and whip.** Automedon “often touched them, striking them with the swift whip” (17.430; [[automedon]]), and when Zeus has breathed might into them they “lightly bore the swift chariot among the Trojans and the Achaeans” (17.458; [[the-horses-of-achilles]]). The phrase μάστιγι θοῇ is new to the entry; [[swift-ships]] excepts the line.

**Swift of foot.** In the eagle simile “the hare, swift of foot, does not escape him” (17.676; [[hare]], [[eagle]]), and Menelaus says that he has sent Antilochus “to go to Achilles, swift of foot” (17.709; [[achilles]]). πόδας ταχύς is kept apart from πόδας ὠκύς ([[swift-footed]]; see the translation's table of formulas).
