---
summary: The boar of Book 17 is the beast of fighting spirit. Its spirit is the greatest of all, Menelaus says, yet the sons of Panthous are prouder (17.20–23); Ajax scatters the Trojans over the body of Patroclus like a wild boar that scatters dogs and young men (17.281–285); and the two Ajaxes turn on the pursuing Trojans like a wounded boar on the hounds (17.725–734).
---

**The boar's spirit.** Menelaus answers Euphorbus with three beasts: “Neither is the leopard's might so great, nor the lion's, / nor the deadly wild boar's, whose spirit is greatest of all / in his breast, exulting surpassingly in his strength” (17.20–22; see [[leopard]] and [[lion]]). The boar is the last and greatest of the three, σῦς κάπρος, the wild boar, ὀλοόφρων, “deadly” (see [[oloophron]]). Its spirit σθένεϊ βλεμεαίνει, the words said a hundred verses later of the lion that guards its cubs: “he exults in his strength” (17.135; see [[exulting-in-strength]]). None of the three, Menelaus says, is as proud as the sons of Panthous, and he has already killed one of them, Hyperenor (17.23–28; see [[hyperenor]]).

**Ajax.** When the Trojans drag at the body, Ajax rallies the Achaeans and goes through the champions “like a boar in courage”, a wild boar that easily scatters the dogs and the young men in the mountains, wheeling about through the glens (17.281–285; see [[like-a-boar]] and [[ajax]]).

**The wounded boar.** At the end of the book the Trojans rush at the men carrying the body like hounds that dart at a wounded boar ahead of the young hunters, until it wheels around among them, trusting in its courage, and they scatter; so the Trojans fall back whenever the two Ajaxes turn and stand against them (17.722–734; see [[the-hounds-and-the-wounded-boar]] and [[the-two-ajaxes]]).

In Book 16 the boar was Patroclus, overpowered by Hector as a lion overpowers a tireless boar at a spring (16.823–826). In Book 17 the boar is the Ajaxes, and it is not beaten.
