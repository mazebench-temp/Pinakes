---
summary: Menoetius is named only through his son, five times as Μενοιτιάδης, “son of Menoetius”: in the narrator's voice as the Achaeans fight around the body, and in Automedon's as he avenges him (17.132, 17.267, 17.270, 17.369, 17.538).
---

Ajax covers “the son of Menoetius” with his broad shield (17.132; see [[ajax]] and [[patroclus]]); the Achaeans stand “around the son of Menoetius with one heart”, and Zeus, who had not hated “the son of Menoetius” while he lived, covers them with mist (17.267–270; see [[zeus]]); the best men fight in the mist “around the dead son of Menoetius” (17.369); and Automedon, when he has killed Aretus, says that he has eased his heart a little “for the dead son of Menoetius” (17.538; see [[automedon]] and [[son-of-menoetius]]). The book never names the father himself. In the next book Achilles remembers the promise he made to Menoetius, to bring his son home to Opus (18.324–327).
