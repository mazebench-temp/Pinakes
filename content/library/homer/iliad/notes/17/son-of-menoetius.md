---
summary: The patronymic names Patroclus as a body that others stand around: Ajax covers him with his shield, the Achaeans stand around him with one heart and in the mist, and Automedon kills a man for him.
---

“And Ajax covered the son of Menoetius with his broad shield” (17.132; [[ajax]]), and the Achaeans “stood around the son of Menoetius with one heart, / fenced with their bronze-fitted shields” (17.267–268; [[bronze-fitted]]). The mist covers the best men, who “stood around the dead son of Menoetius” (17.369; [[zeus]]). Over Aretus Automedon says, “Truly, a little at least, for the dead son of Menoetius / I have eased my heart of grief, though it is a lesser man I have killed” (17.538–539; [[aretus]], [[automedon]]). The half-verse ἕστασαν ἀμφὶ Μενοιτιάδῃ, at 17.267 and 17.369, is the posture of the book: the living standing around the dead ([[fighting-over-the-body]]). See [[patroclus]] and [[menoetius]].
