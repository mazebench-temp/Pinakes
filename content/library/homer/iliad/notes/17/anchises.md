# Αἰνείας τʼ Ἀγχισιάδης (17.754 = 20.160); Ἀγχισιάδης alone is Echepolus, son of another Anchises, at 23.296.
grc: ["Αἰνείας τʼ Ἀγχισιάδης"]
# Unnamed: "Aeneas' old father", whom Periphas served (17.324-325).
refs: [17.324-325]
---

παρὰ πατρὶ γέροντι, “beside Aeneas' old father” (17.324): the herald Periphas, son of Epytus, had grown old in Anchises' service, “a man whose mind was kindly toward him” (17.325), and Apollo takes his likeness to shame Aeneas into turning back (see [[periphas-son-of-epytus]], [[aeneas]] and [[apollo]]). The English adds the name of Aeneas, since the Greek says only “beside his old father” (see the translation's note on 17.324). Aeneas is ἐῢς πάϊς Ἀγχίσαο, “the brave son of Anchises”, when he agrees to go with Hector after the horses of Achilles (17.491), as in the Catalogue (2.819), and Αἰνείας τʼ Ἀγχισιάδης, “Aeneas, son of Anchises”, when he and Hector press the men who carry Patroclus at the end of the book (17.754); the same words begin the verse in Book 20, when Aeneas faces Achilles (20.160). Another Anchises, of Sicyon, is the father of Echepolus, who is also called Ἀγχισιάδης (23.296).
