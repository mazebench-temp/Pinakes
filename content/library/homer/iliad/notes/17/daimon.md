---
summary: Menelaus weighs his shame against the gods: “When a man is willing, against a god's will, to fight with a man / whom a god honors, soon a great bane rolls down on him” (πρὸς δαίμονα, 17.98–99); yet with Ajax beside him he would come back “even against a god's will” to save the body (17.104; see [[decision-scene]]).
---

The translation's table gives the three phrases of defiance in the book their own renderings: πρὸς δαίμονα “against a god's will” (17.98, 17.104), ὑπὲρ θεόν “in defiance of a god” (17.327), and ὑπὲρ Διὸς αἶσαν “beyond the portion of Zeus” (17.321; see [[aisa]] and [[would-have-had-not]]).
