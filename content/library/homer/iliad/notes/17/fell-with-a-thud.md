---
summary: The whole verse marks two deaths, Euphorbus' at Menelaus' hands and Schedius' at Hector's: “and he fell with a thud, and his armor clattered upon him” (17.50, 17.311). The half-verse marks a third, Podes', whose body Menelaus drags at once into the crowd of his companions (17.580–581).
---

The armor that clatters on Euphorbus is the prize Menelaus begins to strip and must leave when Hector comes (17.60, 17.91–108; see [[stripping-the-dead]]). Schedius was the best of the Phocians, struck under the collarbone by the spear Hector threw at Ajax (17.304–311; see [[schedius]]).
