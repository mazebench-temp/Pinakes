summary: Ajax fears less for the corpse of Patroclus than for his own head and Menelaus' (17.240–242); “pale fear” keeps dogs and herdsmen from the lion that has taken a cow (17.67); and fear sends Idomeneus to the ships (17.625). Zeus addresses the weeping horses as δειλώ, “wretched pair” (17.443; see [[wretch]]).
