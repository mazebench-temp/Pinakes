summary: Lycomedes kills “Apisaon, son of Hippasus, shepherd of the people,” in nearly the verses of the death of another Apisaon in Book 11.

{{quote:182ad614-2741-5872-86ba-15ebc632b55f}}

Lycomedes avenges his companion on “Apisaon, son of Hippasus, shepherd of the people” (17.348; [[apisaon-son-of-hippasus]], [[lycomedes]], [[phaeinos]]). The cast and the wound are told in the verses of the death of Apisaon, son of Phausius, killed by Eurypylus (11.577–579; [[apisaon]]): the same name, the same title and the same wound, with only the father's name changed (see the translation's note on 13.412–423). This Apisaon is a Paeonian, “after Asteropaeus he was the best in the fighting” (17.351; [[asteropaeus]]).
