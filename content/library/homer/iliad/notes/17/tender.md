---
summary: Menelaus' spear goes “straight through the soft neck” of Euphorbus; the book adds ἁπαλοῖο.
grc: [ἁπαλοῖο]
en: [soft]
---

“and leaned his own weight on it, trusting in his heavy hand; / and straight through the soft neck went the point, / and he fell with a thud” (17.48–50; [[euphorbus]], [[menelaus]]). The genitive ἁπαλοῖο is new to the entry, which already had the feminine ἁπαλῆς (Book 13). The same verse is used of Achilles' spear in Hector's neck (22.327). The tender neck is that of the young man whose hair the next verses describe (17.51–52; [[hair]]).
