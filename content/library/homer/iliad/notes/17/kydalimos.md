**Menelaus.** The lion simile of his victory over Euphorbus ends: “so no one of them had the heart in his breast to dare / to come against glorious Menelaus” (17.68–69; [[lion-among-cattle]], [[menelaus]]). The verse-end Μενελάου κυδαλίμοιο is his formula, as at 4.100, 4.177, 7.392 and three times in Book 13 (13.591, 13.601, 13.606).

**The sons of Nestor.** In the clear part of the field, “two men had not yet learned, / two glorious men, Thrasymedes and Antilochus, / that noble Patroclus was dead” (17.377–379; [[thrasymedes]], [[antilochus]], [[blameless]]). The dual κυδαλίμω is new to the entry. The two brothers will carry the news and take up the fight: Antilochus is sent to Achilles, and Menelaus sends Thrasymedes to the Pylians (17.651–705).
