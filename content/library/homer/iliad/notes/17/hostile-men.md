---
summary: Glaucus speaks of the might that comes into men “who for their country / have set toil and contest against hostile men”; the book adds ἀνδράσι δυσμενέεσσι.
grc: [ἀνδράσι δυσμενέεσσι]
---

“For if there were now in the Trojans a might full of daring, / undaunted, such as comes into men who for their country / have set toil and contest against hostile men, / we would quickly drag Patroclus into Ilios” (17.156–159; [[full-of-daring]], [[glaucus]], [[ponos]]). Here the formula belongs to a general reflection on men who fight for their country. The phrase without final ν is new to the entry; it returns twice in Book 19 (19.168, 19.232).
