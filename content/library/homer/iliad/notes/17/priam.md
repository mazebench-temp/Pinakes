---
summary: Glaucus speaks of Troy as “the great city of lord Priam” (17.160), and Hector is twice “son of Priam”, when Zeus will not let him ride behind the horses of Achilles and when Automedon expects him to try (17.449, 17.503).
---

προτὶ ἄστυ μέγα Πριάμοιο ἄνακτος, “to the great city of lord Priam” (17.160), is where Glaucus would have the Trojans drag Patroclus' body, to win back the arms and body of Sarpedon (17.159–163; see [[glaucus]] and [[troy]]). Πριαμίδης, “son of Priam”, names Hector in the two passages about the horses of Achilles: Zeus says that “Hector, son of Priam” will not ride behind them (17.449), and Automedon that “Hector, son of Priam” will not hold back from his fury until he has mounted behind them or died (17.503–506; see [[hector]], [[son-of-priam]] and [[the-horses-of-achilles]]). Priam himself does not appear in the book.
