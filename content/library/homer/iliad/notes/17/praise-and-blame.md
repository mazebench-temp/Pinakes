summary: The book's speeches rebuke more than they praise, and each rebuke is answered with an act. Glaucus tells Hector that his fame is empty, and Hector answers by putting on Achilles' armor (17.142–214); Apollo rebukes Aeneas and taunts Hector, and both go back into the fight (17.327–342, 17.586–592); Alcimedon asks Automedon what god has taken away his wits, and Automedon gives him the reins (17.469–480). The one praise is for the dead: Menelaus bids the Achaeans remember the gentleness of Patroclus (17.669–672; see [[eneeie]]).
