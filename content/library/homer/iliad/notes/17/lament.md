summary: Euphorbus accuses Menelaus of bringing “accursed lamentation and grief” on the parents of his brother Hyperenor, and hopes to put an end to their lament by bringing them Menelaus' head and armor (17.37–40). The same verse is spoken in Andromache's lament for Hector (17.37 = 24.741).
grc: [γόου]
---

γόου κατάπαυμα, “an end to the lamentation” (17.38): the kin of the dead are to be comforted by the sight of the killer's head and spoils (see [[poine]] and [[stripping-the-dead]]). Euphorbus dies instead, and his parents Panthous and Phrontis are left with a second son to mourn (17.40–60; see [[bereaved-parents]]). ἀρητόν is printed and rendered “accursed”; Walter Leaf doubts the reading (see the translation's note on 17.37). The book's other mourning is silent: the weeping of Achilles' horses (17.426–440; see [[tears]]).
