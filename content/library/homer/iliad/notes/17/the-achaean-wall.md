---
summary: The book ends at the ditch: as the Achaeans flee before Hector and Aeneas, much fine armor falls “around and about the ditch” (17.760–761). The fight for the body began far from the ships, under the wall of Troy (17.403–404).
---

πολλὰ δὲ τεύχεα καλὰ πέσον περί τʼ ἀμφί τε τάφρον / φευγόντων Δαναῶν· πολέμου δʼ οὐ γίγνετʼ ἐρωή, “And much fine armor fell around and about the ditch / as the Danaans fled; and there was no respite from the war” (17.760–761; see [[danaans]]). The young men of the Achaeans have fled screaming before Aeneas and Hector like starlings and jackdaws before a falcon (17.753–759; see [[a-cloud-of-starlings]]). The last half-verse ended the putting out of the fire at the ships in Book 16, when the Danaans drew breath only a little (16.302).

The fight over Patroclus began on the plain, “far away from the swift ships, / under the wall of the Trojans” (17.403–404), where Patroclus had fallen; Menelaus and Meriones carry the body back toward the ships, and the book ends with the rout at the ditch (17.735–761; see the translation's note on 8.475–476). In the next book Achilles, unarmed, stands at the ditch and shouts, and the Trojans fall back (18.215–231).
