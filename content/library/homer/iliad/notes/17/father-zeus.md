summary: Menelaus cries “Father Zeus” at Euphorbus' boast and prays to him as he strikes; Automedon prays to him; Ajax sees that “father Zeus himself is helping the Trojans,” then prays to him for light, and “the father” pities him.
refs: [17.648]
---

**Menelaus.** He answers Euphorbus' boast with an outcry to the god: “Father Zeus, it is no fine thing to boast so overweeningly” (17.19; [[euphorbus]], [[menelaus]]). Then, when Euphorbus' spear has bent against his shield, he strikes, “Menelaus, son of Atreus, praying to father Zeus” (17.46). The three verses 17.44–46 are those of his duel with Paris, when Paris' spear bent against the same shield and Menelaus struck in his turn (3.348–350; [[son-of-atreus]]).

**Automedon.** Before he throws at Aretus, “he prayed to father Zeus, / and his heart, dark on every side, was filled with courage and strength” (17.498–499; [[automedon]], [[prayer]]).

**Ajax.** When the Achaeans' missiles fall in vain, “even one who is an utter fool / would know that father Zeus himself is helping the Trojans” (17.629–630; [[even-a-fool-can-see]]). Then he prays: “Father Zeus, rescue the sons of the Achaeans from under the mist, / and make clear sky, and grant us to see with our eyes” (17.645–646; [[sons-of-the-achaeans]], [[ajax]]). “So he spoke, and the father pitied him as he shed tears” (17.648): πατήρ alone names Zeus, in the verse of his pity for Agamemnon's tears in Book 8 (8.245). The line is counted by reference. See [[zeus]].
