---
summary: The eagle of Menelaus' simile “has the sharpest sight of all the winged birds under heaven.”
---

“looking about everywhere like an eagle, which, they say, / has the sharpest sight of all the winged birds under heaven” (17.674–675; [[eagle]], [[sharp]], [[birds]], [[menelaus]]). This is the one place where ὑπουράνιος is said of birds; elsewhere it is fame that reaches under heaven (10.212). The eagle's sight is the point of the simile: Menelaus is looking for Antilochus (17.679–681; [[phaeinos]]).
