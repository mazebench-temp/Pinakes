---
summary: Athena comes down from heaven to rouse the Danaans, “for far-seeing Zeus had sent her,” in a couplet that some ancient critics rejected.
---

“grievous and tearful, and Athena roused the quarrel, / coming down from heaven; for far-seeing Zeus had sent her / to rouse the Danaans; for his mind had now turned” (17.544–546; [[tearful]], [[athena]]). Walter Leaf reports that 17.545 was athetized in antiquity and judges the couplet an interpolation: there is no change in the designs of Zeus, who in 593 goes on helping the Trojans, and he is not in heaven but on Ida (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 17.545; see the translation's note on 17.545–546). See [[zeus]].
