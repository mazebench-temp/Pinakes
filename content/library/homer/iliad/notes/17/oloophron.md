---
summary: Menelaus says that not even “the deadly wild boar” has so great a spirit as the pride of the sons of Panthous.
---

“Neither is the leopard's might so great, nor the lion's, / nor the deadly wild boar's, whose spirit is greatest of all / in his breast, exulting surpassingly in his strength, / as the pride of the sons of Panthous, of the good ash spear” (17.20–23; [[leopard]], [[lion]], [[boar]], [[exulting-in-strength]], [[with-good-ash-spear]]). The three beasts are a list of fierceness, and the boar's is the greatest; Menelaus sets the boasting of Euphorbus above them all, in scorn. See [[euphorbus]].
