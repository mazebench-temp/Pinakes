summary: Cronus is named only in his son's patronymics, each time at one of Zeus' interventions: the son of Cronus nods and fits the armor of Achilles to Hector, pours mist around the Achaeans who defend the body, pities the horses of Achilles, and takes up the aegis on Ida.
---

In Book 17 Cronus appears only in the titles of his son (see [[zeus]] and [[son-of-cronus]]), and each marks one of Zeus' own acts in the fight for the body. Κρονίων ends the verse in which Zeus nods to Hector, “He spoke, and the son of Cronus nodded with his dark brows” (17.209 = 1.528; see [[dark-brows]]), the verse in which he pours mist around the Achaeans' helmets (17.269), and the verse in which he pities the weeping horses of Achilles (17.441), which returns in Book 19 when he pities Achilles and the elders who mourn with him (19.338–340). Κρονίδης begins the verse in which he takes up the aegis on Ida and turns the battle to the Trojans (17.593; see [[aegis]]).
