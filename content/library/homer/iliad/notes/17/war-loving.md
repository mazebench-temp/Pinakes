summary: Hector sends his own armor to Ilios with “the war-loving Trojans,” and tells the allies that he brought them to protect the Trojans' wives and children “from the war-loving Achaeans”; the book adds φιλοπτολέμων.
grc: [φιλοπτολέμων]
---

“his own he gave to carry to sacred Ilios / to the war-loving Trojans, and he put on the immortal armor / of Achilles” (17.193–195; [[hieros]], [[exchange-of-armor]]); and to the allies: “but so that the wives of the Trojans and their little children / you might protect with a ready heart from the war-loving Achaeans” (17.223–224; [[trojan-allies]]). The epithet is given to both armies within thirty lines, as in Book 16 it was given to the Myrmidons and the Trojans. The genitive φιλοπτολέμων is new to the entry. See [[trojans]] and [[achaeans]].
