---
summary: “Phoebus Apollo” begrudges Menelaus the armor of Euphorbus, and casts a heaven-sent rout among Ajax's companions.
---

Menelaus would have carried off Euphorbus' armor “had not Phoebus Apollo begrudged him that, / who roused Hector against him, the equal of swift Ares, / in the likeness of a man, Mentes, the leader of the Cicones” (17.71–73; [[mentes]], [[cicones]], [[equal-of-ares]]). When Menelaus finds Ajax, he is rallying his companions, “for Phoebus Apollo had cast a heaven-sent rout among them” (17.118; [[wondrous]], [[rout]]). The god who struck Patroclus at the end of Book 16 works against the Achaeans at the beginning of the fight for his body ([[would-have-had-not]]). See [[apollo]].
