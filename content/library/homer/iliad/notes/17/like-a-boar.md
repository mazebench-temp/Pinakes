{{quote:7cb9ac84-34cf-5801-a924-c421283735fc}}

The Trojans have pushed the Achaeans back from the body and are dragging at it, but only for a short while: “for very quickly he rallied them, / Ajax, who in looks and in deeds stood out above the rest / of all the Danaans, after the noble son of Peleus” (17.278–280; see [[ajax]]). ἴθυσεν δὲ διὰ προμάχων συῒ εἴκελος ἀλκὴν / καπρίῳ, ὅς τʼ ἐν ὄρεσσι κύνας θαλερούς τʼ αἰζηοὺς / ῥηϊδίως ἐκέδασσεν, ἑλιξάμενος διὰ βήσσας, “He made straight through the champions, like a boar in courage, / a wild boar, which in the mountains the dogs and the young men in their prime / easily scatters, wheeling about through the glens” (17.281–283). The half-verse of Idomeneus in Book 4 (4.253) runs on here into the next verse with καπρίῳ, and the boar is drawn: σῦς κάπριος is the wild boar of the hunt (11.293, 12.42; see [[boar]]). ἑλιξάμενος is “turning to bay”, Walter Leaf explains, and διὰ βήσσας, “through the glens”, goes with the scattering (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 17.283). The dogs and young men in their prime are the hunters of the boar at bay to which Odysseus is likened in Book 11, κύνες θαλεροί τʼ αἰζηοί (11.414; see [[the-boar-at-bay]]); there they close in and stand their ground, here they scatter. The verse-opening ἴθυσεν δὲ διὰ προμάχων, “He made straight through the champions”, brought on Patroclus “like a hawk” in Book 16 (16.582; see [[like-a-hawk]]).

The tenor: ὣς υἱὸς Τελαμῶνος ἀγαυοῦ φαίδιμος Αἴας / ῥεῖα μετεισάμενος Τρώων ἐκέδασσε φάλαγγας / οἳ περὶ Πατρόκλῳ βέβασαν, “so the son of noble Telamon, shining Ajax, / easily going in among them, scattered the battalions of the Trojans / who stood around Patroclus” (17.284–286). The verb and the ease are the boar's: ῥηϊδίως ἐκέδασσεν, ῥεῖα … ἐκέδασσε, “easily scatters … easily … scattered”. Ajax then kills Hippothous, who was dragging the body by the foot with a strap tied around the ankle, and Phorcys, who stood over Hippothous (17.288–318; see [[hippothous]] and [[phorcys]]).

The boar comes back at the end of the book, turned at bay and wounded. The Trojans rush at the two Ajaxes like hounds at a wounded boar, and scatter in fear when it wheels on them (17.725–734; see [[the-hounds-and-the-wounded-boar]]).
