summary: The horses of Achilles are hard “for mortal men” to master; Zeus asks why the gods gave them to Peleus, “a mortal”; and the rainbow is stretched out “for mortals”; the book adds θνητῷ and θνητοῖσι.
grc: [θνητῷ, θνητοῖσι]
---

**The horses.** Apollo, as Mentes, tells Hector that the horses of Achilles “are hard / for mortal men to master and to drive, / for any other than Achilles, whom an immortal mother bore” (17.76–78; [[grievous]], [[immortals]], [[the-horses-of-achilles]]), the verses of Odysseus to Dolon (10.402–404). Zeus sets the same line between mortal and immortal when he speaks to the horses: “why did we give you to lord Peleus, / a mortal, while you are ageless and immortal?” (17.443–444; [[ageless]], [[peleus]]).

**The rainbow.** “As when a purple rainbow is stretched out for mortals / by Zeus from heaven, to be a portent either of war / or of chilling winter” (17.547–549; [[dysthalpes]], [[teras]]). The dative θνητῷ and the plural θνητοῖσι are new to the entry; θνητῷ returns in Book 24, again of Peleus, a mortal given a goddess for his wife (24.537). See [[gods-and-mortals]].
