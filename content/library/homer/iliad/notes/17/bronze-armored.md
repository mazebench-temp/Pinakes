---
summary: A man of “the bronze-armored Achaeans” would rather the earth gaped than yield the body, and Hector calls Aeneas “counselor of the bronze-armored Trojans.”
---

“and this is what a man would say, of the bronze-armored Achaeans” (17.414; [[tis-speech]], [[black-earth]]), introducing the imagined speech of an Achaean who would rather die than give up Patroclus. The Trojans have their own (17.420–422). Hector addresses Aeneas: “Aeneas, counselor of the bronze-armored Trojans” (17.485; [[man-of-counsel]], [[aeneas]]), the verse of Pandarus in Book 5 (5.180). The epithet usually given to the Achaeans is given there and here to the Trojans. See [[achaeans]] and [[trojans]].
