summary: The “fierce battle” over Patroclus three times, Menelaus' “strong shield,” the lion's “strong teeth,” Patroclus “gentle and strong” in Zeus' words, and the “strong might” of the mules in the last simile.
---

**The fierce battle.** Euphorbus claims to have hit Patroclus first “in the fierce battle” (17.15; [[euphorbus]]); Hippothous drags the body “through the fierce battle” (17.289; [[hippothous]]); and “again over Patroclus the fierce battle was stretched tight, / grievous and tearful” (17.543–544; [[tearful]]).

**Shield, teeth and might.** Euphorbus' point was bent back “in the strong shield” (17.45; [[balanced-on-every-side]]); the lion seizes the cow “in his strong teeth” (17.63; [[lion-among-cattle]]); and the Achaeans carrying the body are like mules “that have put their strong might into it” (17.742; [[mules]]).

**Patroclus.** Zeus says to Hector: “and you have killed his companion, gentle and strong” (17.204). The pairing ἐνηέα τε κρατερόν τε sets the strength of Patroclus beside his gentleness, which Menelaus will recall (17.670–672; [[meilichos]], [[patroclus]]).
