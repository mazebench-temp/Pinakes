---
summary: Polydamas wounds the Boeotian Peneleos, scoring his shoulder to the bone, and Peneleos is the first to begin the rout of the Achaeans when Zeus thunders from Ida (17.597–600).
---

“Peneleos the Boeotian was the first to begin the rout. / For he was hit on the shoulder with a spear, always facing forward, / a grazing blow on the top; and right to the bone it scored him, / the point of Polydamas; for he had come close and hit him” (17.597–600; see [[peneleos]] and [[boeotians]]). It is his only deed in the book, and it comes as Zeus shakes the aegis and gives the Trojans the victory (17.593–596; see [[aegis]]). Walter Leaf reports the scholia's explanation of ἐπιλίγδην, a blow that grazes the surface and does not go deep, and that a shallow wound on the shoulder may still reach the bone, since the shoulder blade has little flesh on it (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 17.599). γράψεν, “scored”, is the verb of the marks on Bellerophon's tablet (6.169; see the translation's note on 6.168–169). His brothers Euphorbus and Hyperenor, the other sons of Panthous named in the book, both died at Menelaus' hands (14.516–519, 17.43–60; see [[euphorbus]], [[hyperenor]] and [[panthous]]).
