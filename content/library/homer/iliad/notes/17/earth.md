---
summary: The earth of Book 17 is the ground of the fight over Patroclus, wet with purple blood around the body; the black earth that an Achaean would rather see gape for them all than give the body up; and the earth on which man is the most miserable of all that breathe and creep (17.360–361, 17.416–417, 17.447).
---

**The ground of the fight.** Euphorbus lies on the ground (17.85), like the olive tree that the storm stretches on the ground (17.58; see [[the-olive-sapling]]); Phorcys falls in the dust “and clutched the earth in his palm” (17.315; see [[phorcys]] and [[androktasia]]); and around the body, as Ajax holds the Achaeans close together, “the earth was wet / with purple blood” (17.360–361; see [[purple]]). At the end Menelaus and Meriones “took the body up from the ground in their arms” (17.722) and carry it away. Outside the mist the rest of the battle is fought in sunlight, and “no cloud was seen over all / the earth or the mountains” (17.372–373; see [[sky]]).

**Let the earth gape.** One of the Achaeans says: “it is no glorious thing for us to go back / to the hollow ships; rather let the black earth here / gape for us all” (17.415–417; see [[may-the-earth-gape]], [[black-earth]] and [[tis-speech]]). Better to go down into the earth on the spot than to give up the body.

**Upon the earth.** Pitying the horses of Achilles, Zeus says that there is nothing more miserable than man “of all the things that breathe and creep upon the earth” (17.446–447; see [[nothing-more-miserable-than-man]]), and in the simile of the rainbow the winter storm “makes men cease upon the earth” from their work (17.549–550; see [[like-rainbows]]).
