---
summary: Zeus asks the weeping horses of Achilles why the gods gave them to Peleus, “a mortal, while you are ageless and immortal.”
---

{{quote:1136d14f-3b27-5cb6-91d7-ad0ad5d8b14f}}

Zeus speaks to the horses as they weep: they are “ageless and immortal” (17.444), and the gods gave them to Peleus, “a mortal” ([[wretch]], [[mortal-men]], [[immortals]], [[the-horses-of-achilles]]). The dual ἀγήρω τʼ ἀθανάτω τε is the one of Sarpedon's speech to Glaucus (12.323), where it named what the two men could not be. Here it is what the two horses are: they cannot die, and they weep for a man who has died (17.426–440; [[hot-tears]]). See [[zeus]] and [[peleus]].
