summary: Hector tells the allies that he brought them to Troy to protect “the wives of the Trojans and their little children” (17.223–224), and Glaucus asks how Hector will save his city and his town alone if the Lycians go home (17.144–155). The last fire of the book is a city's: the war blazes around the bearers of the body like a fire that rushes on a city of men, and the houses dwindle in the blaze (17.737–739; see [[the-burning-city]]).
