---
summary: Menelaus over the body of Euphorbus is like “a mountain-bred lion, trusting in his courage,” that snatches the best cow from a herd.
---

“And as when a mountain-bred lion, trusting in his courage, / snatches from a grazing herd the cow that is the best” (17.61–62; [[lion-among-cattle]], [[menelaus]]). The lion breaks the cow's neck and gulps her blood while the dogs and herdsmen shout from far off and do not dare come near; so no one dares to come against Menelaus (17.63–69; [[pale-fear]], [[kydalimos]]). The verse-end λέων ὀρεσίτροφος ἀλκὶ πεποιθώς is the one of Odysseus going out to Nausicaa (*Odyssey* 6.130); the lion of Sarpedon's attack on the wall was “mountain-bred” too (12.299). See [[lion]].
