**The enemy.** Glaucus says that the Lycians had no thanks “for fighting against the enemy without rest, forever” (17.148), the verse in which Achilles complained of his own service in Book 9 (9.317), and that Hector could not stand against Ajax, “looking him in the eye amid the war cry of the enemy” (17.167; [[glaucus]]). Zeus “hated that he should become a spoil for the dogs of his enemies, / the Trojan dogs” (17.272–273; [[dogs]], [[trojans]]), and Menelaus leaves the body only because he fears “that the Achaeans / in grievous rout would leave him as a prey to the enemy” (17.666–667). The datives δηΐοισι and δηΐοισιν are new to the entry; they also take in four other places where δήϊοι are the enemy (4.373, 6.82, 9.317, 24.684).

**War.** Hector goes off to change his armor “out of the destructive war” (17.189; [[of-the-gleaming-helmet]]). See [[polemos]].
