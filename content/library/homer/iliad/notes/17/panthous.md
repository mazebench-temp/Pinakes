# Unnamed: the parents of Hyperenor and Euphorbus (17.28, 17.37-38).
refs: [17.28, 17.37-38]
relations:
  - wife: phrontis (17.40)
---

Πάνθου υἱός, “the son of Panthous” (17.9, 17.59), and Πανθοΐδης (17.70, 17.81) name Euphorbus in this book (see [[euphorbus]]). Walter Leaf would read the uncontracted Πανθόου for Πάνθου at 17.9, 17.23 and 17.59, and the same stem at 17.40 (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 17.9). Menelaus speaks of “the pride of the sons of Panthous, of the good ash spear” (17.23): Leaf names them, Euphorbus, Hyperenor and Polydamas (on 17.22; see [[hyperenor]] and [[polydamas]]). The old man's grief is in both men's words. Menelaus says that Hyperenor did not go home “to gladden his dear wife and his devoted parents” (17.28), and Euphorbus that Menelaus “brought accursed lamentation and grief upon his parents” (17.37). Euphorbus' hope is to bring them Menelaus' head and armor: “Surely I would put an end to the lamentation of those wretched ones / if I brought back your head and your armor / and laid them in the hands of Panthous and of brilliant Phrontis” (17.38–40; see [[phrontis]] and [[bereaved-parents]]). Menelaus kills him instead, and Panthous, who sat with Priam on the wall to watch Menelaus fight Paris (3.146; see [[trojan-elders]]), has lost two sons to him (14.516–519, 17.43–60; see [[menelaus]]).
