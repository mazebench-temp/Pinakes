---
summary: In the simile of Aretus' death a man in his prime “strikes behind the horns of an ox of the fields.”
---

{{quote:32b1e342-5888-54a2-877a-19a96799b7c2}}

Aretus falls like an ox struck “behind the horns” by a man with an axe, “an ox of the fields” (17.521; [[sharp]], [[cattle]], [[aretus]]). The verse-end βοὸς ἀγραύλοιο was used in Book 10 of the hide on which Diomedes slept (10.155). See [[automedon]].
