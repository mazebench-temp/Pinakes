summary: Hector names “Ennomus the augur” last of the ten leaders of the allies whom he urges on (17.218), with Chromius, as in the Catalogue's verse on the Mysians. The Catalogue said that Achilles killed him in the river; here he is alive.
# The phrase keeps the augur apart from Ennomus the Trojan, whom Odysseus kills (Ἔννομον, 11.422).
grc: ["Ἔννομον οἰωνιστήν"]
---

“and Phorcys and Chromius and Ennomus the augur” (17.218): the last verse of Hector's list joins Chromius and Ennomus as the Catalogue's verse on the Mysians did, Μυσῶν δὲ Χρόμις ἦρχε καὶ Ἔννομος οἰωνιστής, “The Mysians were led by Chromis and Ennomus the augur” (2.858; see [[chromis]], [[mysians]] and [[trojan-allies]]). Walter Leaf notes that the names of 17.216–218 are mostly taken from the Catalogue (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 17.216). The Catalogue said that his birds did not save him, “he was brought down at the hands of the swift-footed grandson of Aeacus / in the river” (2.859–861; see [[the-river-battle-foretold]]); in Book 17 he is still among the living, and the river fighting of Book 21 does not name him. οἰωνιστής, “augur”, is found elsewhere in Homer only of Calchas, whose likeness Poseidon takes (13.70; see [[divination]] and [[calchas]]). He is not Ennomus the Trojan, whom Odysseus killed in Book 11 (11.422; see [[ennomus-the-trojan]]).
