# Σχεδίον (17.306) is this man; the same form at 15.515 is the son of Perimedes, and the entry excepts that line.
grc: [Σχεδίον]
refs: [17.307-311]
relations:
  - slayer: hector (17.304-311)
---

Ajax has just killed Hippothous over the body of Patroclus, and Hector throws at him; Ajax, “looking straight at it, avoided the bronze spear / narrowly”, and the spear strikes another man (17.304–306; see [[ajax]] and [[hector]]): “but Schedius, son of great-hearted Iphitus, / by far the best of the Phocians, who in famous Panopeus / had his home and ruled over many men— / him he hit under the middle of the collarbone” (17.306–309; see [[iphitus]], [[phocians]] and [[panopeus]]). The sentence breaks off after the name and its titles and begins again with τὸν βάλʼ, “him he hit” (see the translation's note on 17.306–309). The point passes through and comes out at the base of the shoulder, “and he fell with a thud, and his armor clattered upon him” (17.309–311; see [[fell-with-a-thud]]).

**Two of the name.** The son of Iphitus is the leader of the Phocians in the Catalogue, with his brother Epistrophus (2.517–518; see [[epistrophus]]). But Hector has already killed a Phocian leader called Schedius at the ships, the son of Perimedes (15.515–516; see [[schedius-son-of-perimedes]]). Walter Leaf reports a scholium that some ancient readers wrote “of the Athenians” for “of the Phocians” at 15.516 to remove the clash; he himself thought the names of minor figures arbitrary, and saw no difficulty in two Phocian leaders of the name (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 15.515). The patronymics keep them apart, and so does the index.
