summary: Zeus twice begins with “Ah”: to Hector putting on Achilles' armor, “Ah, wretch, death is not on your mind at all,” and to the weeping horses, “Ah, wretched pair”; the book adds the dual ἆ δειλώ.
grc: [ἆ δειλώ]
---

**Hector.** Zeus sees Hector arming himself in Achilles' armor and speaks to his own heart: “Ah, wretch, death is not on your mind at all, / which is now close to you; and you put on the immortal armor / of the best of men, before whom others too tremble” (17.201–203; [[exchange-of-armor]], [[hector]]). The address is to a man who cannot hear it and does not see his fate; Hector used it to the dying Patroclus in Book 16 (16.837).

**The horses.** Zeus pities the horses of Achilles as they weep: “Ah, wretched pair, why did we give you to lord Peleus, / a mortal, while you are ageless and immortal? / Was it so that you should have sorrows among unhappy men?” (17.443–445; [[ageless]], [[immortals]], [[mortal-men]], [[the-horses-of-achilles]]). The dual ἆ δειλώ is new to the entry. Zeus pities both: Hector, whose death is near, and the immortal horses that grieve for a mortal. See [[zeus]].
