---
summary: The Achaeans flee before Aeneas and Hector like starlings and jackdaws before a falcon, “screaming a cry of death”; the book adds οὖλον κεκλήγοντες.
grc: ["οὖλον κεκλήγοντες"]
en: [cry of death]
---

“And as a cloud of starlings or of jackdaws goes / screaming a cry of death, when they see coming / a falcon, which brings slaughter to small birds, / so before Aeneas and Hector the young men of the Achaeans / went screaming a cry of death, and forgot the joy of battle” (17.755–759; [[a-cloud-of-starlings]], [[starlings]], [[jackdaws]], [[falcon]], [[sons-of-the-achaeans]]). The phrase is used twice, once in the simile and once of the Achaeans. The translation takes οὖλον as this οὖλος, “deadly,” a death-shriek (see the translation's note on 17.756, 17.759). Walter Leaf, following Buttmann, who distinguished three words of this form, refers the passage to οὖλος “deadly” in the sense “shrieking a cry of destruction, as we speak of a death-shriek,” and rejects the reading as οὖλος “woolly,” “a confused, thick cry” (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 17.756; [[thick-and-woolly]]). The phrase οὖλον κεκλήγοντες is new to the entry.
