“Nor did the son of Panthous, of the good ash spear, neglect / noble Patroclus when he fell” (17.9–10; [[euphorbus]], [[panthous]]). Menelaus throws the words back at him: no beast is so proud “as the pride of the sons of Panthous, of the good ash spear” (17.23; [[hyperenor]]). And the simile of the olive ends, “such was the son of Panthous, Euphorbus of the good ash spear, / whom Menelaus, son of Atreus, killed” (17.59–60; [[flourishing]]). The epithet of Priam is given to the house of Panthous, one of the Trojan elders (3.146), and frames Euphorbus' end. The three forms are new to the entry.
