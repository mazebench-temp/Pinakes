summary: Menelaus stands over Patroclus as a cow stands over her first calf, “lowing plaintively.”

{{quote:f0a72b01-66da-5edc-9e11-d3864f51810f}}

The cow stands over her calf “lowing plaintively” (17.5; [[fair-haired]], [[cattle]], [[menelaus]]). The first simile of the book gives the warrior a mother's anxious care for her young; Walter Leaf takes that care to be its whole point, and not the warding off of a beast of prey (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 17.4). The next verses give him spear and shield (17.7–8; [[balanced-on-every-side]]). See [[patroclus]].
