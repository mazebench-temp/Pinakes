# Unnamed: Apisaon, a Paeonian, and Asteropaeus, their best fighter (17.350-351).
refs: [17.350-351]
---

The Paeonians are not named in the book, but their country and their best men are. Lycomedes kills Apisaon, son of Hippasus, “who had come from deep-soiled Paeonia, / and after Asteropaeus he was the best in the fighting” (17.348–351; see [[apisaon-son-of-hippasus]], [[paeonia]] and [[lycomedes]]), and Asteropaeus, pitying him, charges the Achaeans, but they are fenced around the body with their shields (17.352–355; see [[asteropaeus]]). Hector has named Asteropaeus among the leaders of the allies (17.217; see [[trojan-allies]]). In Book 16 the Paeonians were the first to break before Patroclus when he killed their leader Pyraechmes (16.284–292; see [[pyraechmes]]); in Book 21 Achilles kills Asteropaeus and routs them by the river (21.139–210).
