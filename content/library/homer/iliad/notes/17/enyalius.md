---
summary: Ares enters Hector as he puts on Achilles' armor, “dread Enyalius”; and Meriones is “the equal of Enyalius, slayer of men”; the book adds the nominative.
grc: [Ἐνυάλιος]
---

**Hector.** Zeus fits the armor to Hector's body, “and Ares entered into him, / dread Enyalius, and his limbs within were filled / with courage and strength” (17.210–212; [[ares]], [[exchange-of-armor]], [[hector]]). The word is printed here in lowercase, but the translation renders it as the god's name in apposition to Ares, as everywhere else in the Iliad (see the translation's note on 17.210–211). Walter Leaf calls it here “a mere epithet of Ares” and thinks the word probably not Greek (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 17.211). The nominative ἐνυάλιος is new to the entry.

**Meriones.** “Meriones, the equal of Enyalius, slayer of men” (17.259; [[equal-of-ares]], [[meriones]]), the verse of Books 7 and 8 (7.166, 8.264), as he answers Menelaus' call.
