summary: The killings of Book 17 are few, and nearly all are made over the body or for the horses. Menelaus kills Euphorbus and Podes; Ajax kills Hippothous and Phorcys; Hector kills Schedius and Coeranus with spears thrown at other men; Aeneas kills Leiocritus and Lycomedes Apisaon; Automedon kills Aretus. Phorcys “fell in the dust and clutched the earth in his palm” (17.315).

**Histories.** Most of the dead are given the type-scene's few lines of life: Euphorbus' hair like the Graces' and his fall like an olive sapling (17.51–60; see [[gold-and-silver]]), Hippothous' death far from Larisa without repaying his parents for his rearing (17.300–303; see [[threptra]]), Schedius' rule over many men in Panopeus (17.306–308), Apisaon's home in Paeonia and his rank after Asteropaeus (17.350–351), Podes' wealth and his place at Hector's feasts (17.575–577; see [[feast]]), and Coeranus' service as Meriones' charioteer, which saves Idomeneus and costs his own life (17.610–616). Killing answers killing: Aeneas kills Leiocritus, Lycomedes in pity kills Apisaon, and Asteropaeus, in pity, cannot break through the ring of shields (17.344–355; see [[pity]] and [[loosened-his-limbs]]).

**The count.** The narrator counts the losses once: “the earth was wet / with purple blood, and they fell heaped close together, / the dead of the Trojans and their mighty allies / and of the Danaans; for they too did not fight without bloodshed, / but far fewer of them perished” (17.360–364), since the Achaeans in their ring remembered to ward off slaughter from one another (17.364–365; see [[fighting-over-the-body]] and [[death-in-battle]]).
