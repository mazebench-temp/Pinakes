---
summary: Menelaus holds his shield, “balanced on every side,” over Patroclus; Euphorbus stabs at it in the verses of Paris' cast in Book 3; and Automedon's spear goes through the shield of Aretus.
---

**Menelaus' shield.** Over the body Menelaus “held in front of him his spear and his shield, balanced on every side, / eager to kill whoever came against him” (17.7–8; [[shield]], [[menelaus]]). Euphorbus “stabbed at his shield, balanced on every side; / but the bronze did not break through, and its point was bent back / in the strong shield” (17.43–45; [[krateros]], [[euphorbus]]): the verses of Paris' spear in the duel of Book 3, which bent against the same shield (3.347–349).

**Aretus' shield.** Automedon's spear “struck Aretus on his shield, balanced on every side; / and it did not stop the spear, but the bronze went on through” (17.517–518; [[aretus]], [[automedon]]). The shield that held for Menelaus fails for the Trojan.
