summary: Meriones comes with Idomeneus at Menelaus' call. When Hector's spear kills his charioteer Coeranus, he picks up the reins and sends Idomeneus to the ships, and stays himself; at the end of the book Ajax sets him and Menelaus to lift the body of Patroclus and carry it toward the ships while the two Ajaxes fight behind (17.258–259, 17.610–625, 17.668–672, 17.715–761).

**At the call.** He comes behind Idomeneus when Menelaus calls the leaders to the body, “Idomeneus' squire, / Meriones, the equal of Enyalius, slayer of men” (17.258–259; see [[idomeneus]], [[menelaus]] and [[enyalius]]), in the verses that bring him forward behind his commander in Books 7 and 8 (7.165–166, 8.263–264).

**Coeranus.** Hector's spear, thrown at Idomeneus, kills “Meriones' squire and charioteer, / Coeranus, who had followed him from well-built Lyctus” (17.610–611; see [[coeranus-of-lyctus]] and [[lyctus]]), and the reins fall to the ground (17.619). “And Meriones took them up in his own hands, / stooping down to the plain, and spoke to Idomeneus: / ‘Whip them on now, until you reach the swift ships; / you know yourself that the power is no longer with the Achaeans’” (17.620–623). Idomeneus drives away in fear (17.624–625). Meriones does not go with him: he is still in the fight when Menelaus charges the Ajaxes and him to remember the gentleness of Patroclus (17.668–672; see [[patroclus]]).

**The bearer.** Ajax gives him and Menelaus the last task of the book: “but you and Meriones, getting under it very quickly, / lift the body and carry it out of the toil” (17.717–718; see [[ajax]]). The two lift it high from the ground, and the Trojans shout when they see it raised (17.722–724). They carry it toward the ships as mules that have put their strong might into it drag a beam or a great ship's timber down a rocky mountain path, their hearts worn down by labor and sweat (17.735–746; see [[mules-dragging-a-beam]]), while the two Ajaxes hold back the Trojans behind them (17.746–753; see [[the-two-ajaxes]]).
