---
summary: Automedon says that no one but Patroclus, “a counselor equal to the gods,” could master the immortal horses.
---

“Alcimedon, which other of the Achaeans is your equal / at keeping in hand the taming and the might of immortal horses, / except Patroclus, a counselor equal to the gods, / while he lived? But now death and fate have overtaken him” (17.475–478; [[the-horses-of-achilles]], [[immortals]], [[alcimedon]], [[automedon]]). The formula of Priam and Peirithous is given to Patroclus as he is mourned. In the Odyssey Nestor names him with the same words among the best men who died at Troy (*Odyssey* 3.110). The last verse returns in Menelaus' praise of Patroclus' gentleness (17.672). See [[patroclus]].
