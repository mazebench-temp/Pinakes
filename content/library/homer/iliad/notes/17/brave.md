---
summary: Leiocritus is “the brave companion of Lycomedes,” Patroclus “the brave attendant” of Achilles, Aeneas “the brave son of Anchises,” and Podes “rich and brave” and “brave among the champions.”
---

**Companions.** Aeneas kills Leiocritus, “the son of Arisbas, the brave companion of Lycomedes” (17.345; [[leiocritus]], [[arisbas]], [[lycomedes]]), and the two sides fight “around the brave attendant of the swift-footed grandson of Aeacus” (17.388; [[grandson-of-aeacus]]), as in Book 16 (16.165).

**Aeneas.** “So he spoke, and the brave son of Anchises did not disobey” (17.491; [[aeneas]], [[anchises]]). In the Catalogue ἐῢς πάϊς Ἀγχίσαο is the leader of the Dardanians (2.819).

**Podes.** “There was among the Trojans Podes, son of Eetion, / rich and brave” (17.575–576; [[wealthy]], [[podes]]), and Apollo calls him “brave among the champions” (17.590; [[trusted-companion]]).
