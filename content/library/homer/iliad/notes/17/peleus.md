---
summary: Peleus is named for himself in two of the gods' gifts: the immortal armor that Hector now wears, which the gods gave Peleus and Peleus gave his son “when he grew old”, and the immortal horses that weep for Patroclus, which Zeus regrets giving to a mortal (17.194–197, 17.443–444). Nine times he is named in his son's patronymic.
refs: [17.196-197, 17.444]
---

**The armor.** Hector puts on “the immortal armor / of Achilles, son of Peleus, which the heavenly gods / gave to his dear father; and he granted it to his son / when he grew old; but the son did not grow old in his father's armor” (17.194–197; see [[the-armor-of-peleus]], [[exchange-of-armor]] and [[achilles]]). The poem has not said before where the armor came from; in the next book Achilles will tell his mother that the gods gave it to Peleus as a splendid gift on the day they put her in the bed of a mortal man (18.84–85; see [[thetis]]). ἄμβροτα τεύχεα, “immortal armor”, is said only in this scene (17.194, 17.202). The father grew old, γηράς; the son will not, and the armor will not see him old (see [[the-short-life-of-achilles]]).

**The horses.** When Achilles' horses weep for Patroclus, Zeus pities them: “Ah, wretched pair, why did we give you to lord Peleus, / a mortal, while you are ageless and immortal? / Was it so that you should have sorrows among unhappy men?” (17.443–445; see [[the-horses-of-achilles]], [[zeus]] and [[nothing-more-miserable-than-man]]). The narrator had twice called them “the splendid gifts that the gods gave to Peleus” (16.381, 16.867), and Zeus now speaks as one of the givers.

**The patronymic.** Achilles is named by his father nine times in the book, as Πηλεΐδης and Πηλεΐων, “son of Peleus” (17.105, 17.191, 17.195, 17.199, 17.208, 17.214, 17.280, 17.641, 17.701; see [[son-of-peleus]]); five of the nine name him as the owner of the armor (17.191–214), and two come in the sending of the news (17.641, 17.701).
