---
summary: Hector is “the equal of swift Ares” when Apollo rouses him, Meriones “the equal of Enyalius” as he answers Menelaus' call, and Automedon “the equal of swift Ares” as he strips Aretus.
---

“who roused Hector against him, the equal of swift Ares” (17.72; [[swift]], [[apollo]], [[hector]]); “and after him Idomeneus and Idomeneus' squire, / Meriones, the equal of Enyalius, slayer of men” (17.258–259; [[enyalius]], [[meriones]]), the verse of Meriones in Books 7 and 8 (7.166, 8.264), and with a connective in the Catalogue (2.651); and “and Automedon, the equal of swift Ares, / stripped off his armor, and spoke in triumph” (17.536–537; [[automedon]], [[aretus]]). The charioteer, who has killed his man, is given the warrior's epithet. See [[ares]].
