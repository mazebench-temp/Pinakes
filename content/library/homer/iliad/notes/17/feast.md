summary: Two glimpses of the feast in the middle of the fighting. Menelaus calls the Achaean leaders who “drink at the public cost” beside the sons of Atreus (17.249–250), and Podes, killed by Menelaus, was Hector's “dear companion at the feast” (εἰλαπιναστής, 17.577).
grc: ["δήμια πίνουσιν", εἰλαπιναστής]
en: [drink at the public cost, companion at the feast]
---

δήμια is adverbial, “at the public cost”. Walter Leaf explains that the king has free gifts from his people and in return offers hospitality to the chiefs, and compares the feasts and the wine of the elders in earlier books (2.404, 4.259, 4.343, 9.73; *Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 17.250; see the translation's note on 17.249–251 and [[elders]]). The call reminds the leaders that the honor of the feast is paid for in the fight, as Sarpedon told Glaucus (12.310–321; see [[sarpedons-speech]]), and it ends with the source of both: “and from Zeus come honor and glory” (17.251; see [[time]] and [[kydos]]). Podes was “rich and brave”, and Hector honored him above all the people for his company at the feast; Apollo, in the likeness of another of Hector's friends, uses his death to rouse him (17.575–591; see [[hetairos]] and [[divine-epiphany]]).
