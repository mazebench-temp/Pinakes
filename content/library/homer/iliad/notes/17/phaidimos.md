---
summary: “Shining Ajax” scatters the Trojans like a boar, “the shining son of Lethus” drags Patroclus by the foot, and “shining Hector” gives ground, sees Automedon leap down, and with Aeneas pursues the bearers of the body.
---

**Ajax and Hippothous.** The boar simile ends “so the son of noble Telamon, shining Ajax, / easily going in among them, scattered the battalions of the Trojans” (17.284–285; [[like-a-boar]], [[noble]], [[telamonian]]). Four lines later the same epithet is given to his victim: “Now the shining son of Lethus the Pelasgian, / Hippothous, was dragging him by the foot” (17.288–289; [[hippothous]], [[lethus]]).

**Hector.** The verse-end φαίδιμος Ἕκτωρ comes three times: “The champions and shining Hector gave ground” (17.316); “and Automedon leapt down; and shining Hector saw it” (17.483; [[automedon]]); and at the end of the book, when two men pursue the Achaeans most of all, “Aeneas, son of Anchises, and shining Hector” (17.754; [[aeneas]]). See [[hector]].
