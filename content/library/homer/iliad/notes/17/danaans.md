---
summary: It is the Danaans' indignation that Menelaus fears if he leaves the body, and the Danaans whom he calls to it. Fewer of them die than of the Trojans, because they defend one another; but Apollo tells Aeneas that Zeus wills victory for the Trojans rather than for them, and at the end a god rolls a bane upon them and they flee past the ditch (17.93–101, 17.245–247, 17.331–332, 17.362–365, 17.688–690, 17.760–761).
---

**What the Danaans will say.** Hyperenor, Menelaus says, called him “the most despised warrior among the Danaans” (17.26; see [[hyperenor]]). Over the body he fears their indignation: “I fear that any of the Danaans who sees it will be indignant with me”, if he leaves the armor and Patroclus, and decides that “no one of the Danaans who sees it will be indignant with me / if I give way before Hector, since he fights with help from the gods” (17.93, 17.100–101; see [[menelaus]] and [[nemesis]]). Ajax asks him to call “the best of the Danaans, if any may hear” (17.245), and he shouts, “making himself heard to the Danaans” (17.247), and Ajax is the best of all the Danaans after Achilles (17.280; see [[ajax]]).

**Fewer of them perished.** The earth around the body is wet with blood, and the dead fall heaped together, “the dead of the Trojans and their mighty allies / and of the Danaans; for they too did not fight without bloodshed, / but far fewer of them perished; for they always remembered / to ward off sheer slaughter from one another in the throng” (17.362–365; see [[battle-order]]).

**The bane.** Apollo, in Periphas' likeness, tells Aeneas that “Zeus wills far rather for us than for the Danaans / victory” (17.331–332; see [[apollo]] and [[aeneas]]). Athena is sent “to rouse the Danaans” (17.545–546), in a couplet whose authenticity was doubted in antiquity and by Walter Leaf (see the translation's note on 17.545–546). Menelaus tells Antilochus that “a god is rolling a bane upon the Danaans” and that “great longing for him has come upon the Danaans” (17.688, 17.690; see [[antilochus]] and [[patroclus]]), and in the last verse the armor falls around the ditch “as the Danaans fled; and there was no respite from the war” (17.761; see [[the-achaean-wall]]).
