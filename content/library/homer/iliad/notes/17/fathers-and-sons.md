---
summary: The armor that Hector puts on was a father's gift to his son: the gods gave it to Peleus, and Peleus gave it to Achilles when he grew old, “but the son did not grow old in his father's armor” (17.194–197). Nestor's two sons fight apart as their father ordered, and do not know that Patroclus is dead (17.377–383).
---

The verse about the armor joins father and son by the word for age: γηράς, “when he grew old”, and οὐχ … ἐγήρα, “did not grow old” (17.197; see [[geron]]). Achilles will not live to hand it on; the armor passes instead from Patroclus to Hector (see [[the-armor-of-peleus]] and [[exchange-of-armor]]). Antilochus, Nestor's son, is the one who carries the news to Achilles (17.651–701; see [[messenger-scene]]).
