---
summary: Menelaus' message calls Patroclus by the title: “the best of the Achaeans has been slain, / Patroclus” (ὤριστος Ἀχαιῶν, 17.689–690). The best men fight in the mist around his body, and the body is left to them (17.368, 17.377, 17.509); Hector and Aeneas are “the best of the Trojans” (17.513), Euphorbus was the best of them when he fell (17.80), and Achilles is “the best of men”, whose armor Hector wears (17.203).
---

The title has passed. In Book 16 Patroclus called Achilles “by far the best / of the Argives beside the ships” and complained that Agamemnon paid no honor to “the best of the Achaeans” (16.271–274; see [[best-of-the-achaeans]]); Glaucus says the first verses again of Achilles (17.164–165), and Menelaus gives the title to the dead Patroclus. Ajax and Menelaus both propose to consider “the best plan” (μῆτιν ἀρίστην, 17.634, 17.712; see [[metis]]), and Ajax is in looks and in deeds the best of the Danaans after Achilles (17.279–280).
