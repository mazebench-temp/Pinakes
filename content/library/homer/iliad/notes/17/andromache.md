---
summary: Zeus, seeing Hector put on the armor of Achilles, gives him great power for a time in recompense, since he will never come home from the battle for Andromache to receive that armor from him (17.207–208).
---

τῶν ποινὴν ὅ τοι οὔ τι μάχης ἐκνοστήσαντι / δέξεται Ἀνδρομάχη κλυτὰ τεύχεα Πηλεΐωνος, “in recompense, since you will not come home from the battle / for Andromache to receive from you the glorious armor of the son of Peleus” (17.207–208; see [[zeus]], [[hector]] and [[poine]]). Walter Leaf notes that the negative belongs first to the participle, “thou shalt not return for Andromache to receive at thy hands” (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 17.207). She is named only here in the book, as the woman to whom a husband brings home the spoils of war. In Book 6 Hector prayed that their son might one day come back from war and “bring back bloody spoils / when he has killed his enemy, and may his mother's heart rejoice” (6.480–481; see [[astyanax]] and [[hector-and-andromache]]). The homecoming Zeus denies is the one she will be preparing in Book 22, when she bids her handmaids heat a bath for Hector against his return from the battle, μάχης ἐκ νοστήσαντι, in Zeus' words here (22.442–446; see [[nostos]]).
