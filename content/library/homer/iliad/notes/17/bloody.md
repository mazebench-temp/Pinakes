---
summary: Hippothous' brain spurts out “bloody” from Ajax's blow, and Automedon, having stripped Aretus, is “bloody, like a lion that has devoured a bull”; the book adds the nominative αἱματόεις.
grc: [αἱματόεις]
---

**Hippothous.** “and the brain spurted from the wound along the spear's socket, / bloody; and there his strength was loosed” (17.297–298; [[hippothous]], [[ajax]]). The word stands at the head of the verse after its noun. See the translation's note on 17.297 for the αὐλός, the socket of the spearhead.

**Automedon.** “So saying, he took the bloody spoils and put them in the chariot, / and mounted it himself, his feet and his hands above / bloody, like a lion that has devoured a bull” (17.540–542; [[bloody-spoils]], [[lion]], [[automedon]]). Again the word opens the verse, and the simile follows it. The nominative αἱματόεις is new to the entry.
