---
summary: The “high-hearted Trojans” push the Achaeans back from the body but kill none of them.
---

“they left the body and shrank back; but not one of them / did the high-hearted Trojans kill with their spears, eager though they were, / but they dragged at the corpse” (17.275–277; [[bright-eyed]], [[trojans]]). The epithet of pride stands beside the failure: Zeus has covered the defenders with mist, and Ajax soon drives the Trojans back (17.268–287).
