---
summary: Ajax strikes Hippothous “through the bronze-cheeked helmet,” in the verse-end of Books 12 and 20.
---

“The son of Telamon, rushing at him through the throng, / struck him at close quarters through the bronze-cheeked helmet; / and the horsehair-crested helmet split around the point of the spear” (17.293–295; [[telamonian]], [[horsehair-crested]], [[hippothous]]). The verse-end κυνέης διὰ χαλκοπαρῄου is used of Polypoetes' spear through the helmet of Damasus (12.183) and of Achilles' through the helmet of Demoleon, son of Antenor (20.395–397; see the translation's note on 12.183). The helmet has two epithets in two verses, and the wound follows: “and the brain spurted from the wound along the spear's socket, / bloody” (17.297–298; [[bloody]]). See [[helmet]].
