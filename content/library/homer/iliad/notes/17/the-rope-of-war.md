summary: The fight over Patroclus is “stretched tight” from end to end. “Such was the toil of men and horses that Zeus over Patroclus / stretched tight that day, an evil toil” (17.400–401); “again over Patroclus the fierce battle was stretched tight” when Athena comes down (17.543); and “the war was stretched tight over them” as the body is carried to the ships (17.736). In the middle the two armies stretch the body itself between them, like men stretching a bull's hide (17.389–395).
grc: ["ἐτάνυσσε κακὸν πόνον", "τέτατο κρατερὴ ὑσμίνη", "πτόλεμος τέτατό σφιν"]
en: [stretched tight]
---

The image of Book 13, a rope pulled taut by two gods over both armies (13.358–360), is here a hide pulled taut by men: they take it, stand apart in a circle, and pull until the moisture goes out and the fat sinks in, “so they kept pulling the corpse this way and that in a little space, / both sides” (17.389–395; see [[the-stretched-hide]] and [[fighting-over-the-body]]). τανύω is the verb of both, the stretching of the hide (τανύειν, τανύουσι, τάνυται, 17.390–393) and of the battle (see the translation's table of renderings). Neither side can pull it away, and the toil goes on until Zeus turns the battle (17.593–596; see [[ponos]]).
