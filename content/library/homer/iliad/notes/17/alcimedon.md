summary: Alcimedon sees Automedon fighting alone from the chariot of Achilles' immortal horses, rebukes him, and takes the whip and the reins so that Automedon can fight on foot; when Hector and Aeneas come for the horses, Automedon tells him to keep them close behind him (17.466–483, 17.500–506).
# The vocative Ἀλκίμεδον (17.475, 17.501) and the accusative Ἀλκιμέδοντα (17.500) occur only here.
grc: [Ἀλκίμεδον, Ἀλκιμέδοντα]

passages:
  - 17.459-506 | Alcimedon takes the reins

**The rebuke.** After Patroclus' death Automedon fights alone from the chariot of Achilles' horses, swooping through the Trojans like a vulture after geese, but he kills no one, since he cannot hold in the horses and strike with the spear at once (17.459–465; see [[automedon]] and [[the-horses-of-achilles]]). “But at last a companion saw him with his eyes, / Alcimedon, son of Laerces, son of Haemon” (17.466–467; see [[laerces]] and [[haemon-father-of-laerces]]). He stands behind the chariot and asks which god has put a profitless plan in Automedon's breast and taken away his good wits, “that you fight against the Trojans in the front of the throng / alone? Your companion has been killed, and Hector / wears the armor of the grandson of Aeacus on his own shoulders and glories in it” (17.469–473; see [[hector]] and [[exchange-of-armor]]).

**The reins.** Automedon answers with praise: “Alcimedon, which other of the Achaeans is your equal / at keeping in hand the taming and the might of immortal horses, / except Patroclus, a counselor equal to the gods, / while he lived?” (17.475–478; see [[patroclus]]). He gives him the whip and the reins, “and Alcimedon, leaping onto the chariot swift to the rescue, / quickly took the whip and the reins in his hands, / and Automedon leapt down” (17.481–483). The leader of the fifth company of the Myrmidons (16.197; see [[myrmidons]]) serves here as a charioteer, and the man who drove the immortal horses before him is named as his only equal.

**Close behind.** When Hector and Aeneas set out to take the horses, Automedon prays to Zeus and speaks to Alcimedon, “his trusted companion” (17.500; see [[trusted-companion]]): “Alcimedon, do not keep the horses far from me, / but right behind me, breathing on my back” (17.501–502), for he does not think Hector will hold back before he has killed them both and mounted behind the horses of Achilles, or else is himself taken among the foremost (17.502–506). Alcimedon is not named again in the poem. Automedon's companion in Books 19 and 24 is Alcimus (19.392, 24.474, 24.574), who may be the same man under a shorter name; the poem does not say.
