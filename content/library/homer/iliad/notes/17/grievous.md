---
summary: The horses of Achilles are “hard” for mortal men to drive, and the wooded ridge holds back “the harsh streams of mighty rivers”; the book adds ἀλεγεινά.
grc: [ἀλεγεινὰ]
en: [harsh]
---

**The horses.** Apollo tells Hector that he is chasing “the horses of the wise-hearted grandson of Aeacus; but they are hard / for mortal men to master and to drive” (17.76–77; [[mortal-men]], [[the-horses-of-achilles]]). The verses are Odysseus' words to Dolon about the same horses (10.402–403).

**The rivers.** The two Ajaxes hold back the Trojans like a ridge, “which holds back even the harsh streams of mighty rivers” (17.749; [[mighty]], [[wooded]], [[the-two-ajaxes]]). The neuter plural ἀλεγεινά is new to the entry; in Book 24 it is said of the waves that Achilles remembers crossing with Patroclus (24.8).
