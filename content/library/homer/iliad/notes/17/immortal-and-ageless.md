summary: Zeus says the gods' own condition of the immortal horses of Achilles, weeping for Patroclus: the gods gave them to Peleus, a mortal, “while you are ageless and immortal” (ἀγήρω τʼ ἀθανάτω τε, 17.444).
grc: ["ἀγήρω τʼ ἀθανάτω τε"]
en: [ageless and immortal]
---

The dual is the one Sarpedon used of himself and Glaucus: if, once they had escaped this war, they were to be ageless and immortal forever, he would not fight among the foremost (12.322–325). Sarpedon fights because he and his friend are not; Zeus asks what the horses, who are, gain by living among men, and gives the answer in a saying (17.445–447; see [[nothing-more-miserable-than-man]] and [[the-horses-of-achilles]]).
