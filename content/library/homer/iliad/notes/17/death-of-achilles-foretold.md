---
summary: Book 17 looks past the poem to Achilles' death in a single verse. As Hector puts on the armor that Peleus gave his son in his old age, the narrator adds that “the son did not grow old in his father's armor” (17.197). Achilles, waiting for Patroclus, knows from his mother that Troy will not fall to him (17.404–409).
refs: [17.197, 17.407]
---

**The son did not grow old.** The narrator tells the history of the armor at the moment Hector puts it on: the gods gave it to Peleus, and Peleus “granted it to his son / when he grew old; but the son did not grow old in his father's armor” (17.196–197; see [[the-armor-of-peleus]]). The verse sets the father's old age against the son's, which will not come: γηράς, “when he grew old”, and οὐχ … ἐγήρα, “did not grow old”. Achilles' short life has been in view since Book 1, when he told his mother that she bore him μινυνθάδιος, “short-lived” (1.352; see [[the-short-life-of-achilles]]); here it is told of the armor that the man who killed his friend has just put on (see [[exchange-of-armor]]). Achilles will make the same contrast to Priam: Peleus had one son, born to die untimely, who does not care for his father as he grows old (24.538–542; see [[fathers-and-sons]] and [[peleus]]).

**Not with him either.** Achilles did not expect Patroclus to sack Troy, “without him, nor with him either”, for his mother had often told him the purpose of Zeus (17.406–409; see [[sack-of-troy-foretold]] and [[thetis]]). Apollo has told Patroclus that the city will not fall to Achilles (16.707–709), and Achilles himself has told the embassy that if he stays to fight at Troy he will lose his homecoming but his fame will be imperishable (9.412–413; see [[the-two-fates-of-achilles]]). The poem does not say here why the city will not fall to him; Thetis will tell him, when he decides to kill Hector, that his own death is ready at once after Hector's (18.95–96).
