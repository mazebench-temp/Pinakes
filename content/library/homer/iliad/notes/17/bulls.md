summary: The bulls of Book 17 are in two similes: the two armies pull the body of Patroclus this way and that like men stretching the hide of a great bull soaked in fat (17.389–395), and Automedon, having killed Aretus, mounts his chariot bloody like a lion that has devoured a bull (17.540–542).
grc: [ταύροιο]
en: [bull]
---

ὡς δʼ ὅτʼ ἀνὴρ ταύροιο βοὸς μεγάλοιο βοείην / λαοῖσιν δώῃ τανύειν μεθύουσαν ἀλοιφῇ, “And as when a man gives the hide of a great bull / to his people to stretch, drenched with fat” (17.389–390; see [[the-stretched-hide]]). ταῦρος βοῦς is “a bull”, the word for the male joined to the word for the kind (see [[cattle]]). The hide is worked by many men pulling at once, as the body of Patroclus is pulled between the armies.

αἱματόεις ὥς τίς τε λέων κατὰ ταῦρον ἐδηδώς, “bloody, like a lion that has devoured a bull” (17.542; see [[lion-among-cattle]]). In Book 16 Sarpedon died under Patroclus like a tawny bull that a lion kills among the herd (16.487–491); here the lion's meal is done, and the blood is on its killer, Automedon, who has just avenged Patroclus a little by killing Aretus (17.535–542; see [[automedon]]).
