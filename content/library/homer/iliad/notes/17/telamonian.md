---
summary: “Great Ajax, the son of Telamon,” is the man Menelaus looks for, the man the Trojans hope to drag the body from, the killer of Hippothous, and twice the speaker of the plans to save the body.
---

**The son of Telamon.** Menelaus turns back “looking about for great Ajax, the son of Telamon” (17.115). The Trojans' hearts have high hopes “of dragging the body away from under Ajax, son of Telamon” (17.235). After the boar simile, “so the son of noble Telamon, shining Ajax” (17.284; [[phaidimos]], [[noble]]), and “The son of Telamon, rushing at him through the throng, / struck him at close quarters through the bronze-cheeked helmet” (17.293–294; [[bronze-cheeked]], [[hippothous]]).

**Great Telamonian Ajax.** The verse-end μέγας Τελαμώνιος Αἴας introduces his two speeches on the last part of the fight: “And among them great Telamonian Ajax began to speak” (17.628), when he sees that Zeus is helping the Trojans, and “Then great Telamonian Ajax answered him” (17.715), as he bids Menelaus and Meriones lift the body ([[the-two-ajaxes]]).
