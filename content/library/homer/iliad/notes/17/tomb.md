---
summary: The horses of Achilles stand unmoving with the chariot “as a pillar stays fixed that stands on the tomb / of a man who has died, or of a woman” (17.434–435). The grave marker, mound and pillar, is the image of their mourning, before Patroclus has a grave.
---

τύμβος and στήλη, the mound and the pillar set on it, are the burial Hera and Zeus promise Sarpedon, “the prize of the dead” (16.457 = 16.675; see [[the-prize-of-the-dead]]), and Paris leans on the pillar of Ilus' tomb to shoot (11.371–372). Here the pillar stands for stillness and for mourning at once: the horses will go neither to the ships nor into the battle, their heads bowed to the ground and their tears falling (17.432–440; see [[tears]], [[like-a-pillar]] and [[the-horses-of-achilles]]).
