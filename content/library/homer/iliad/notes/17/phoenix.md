summary: Phoenix takes no part in the fight; Athena comes to Menelaus in his likeness, “in form and in tireless voice”, and Menelaus answers her as the old man, “Phoenix, old father, aged one” (17.553–566).
---

εἰσαμένη Φοίνικι δέμας καὶ ἀτειρέα φωνήν, “in the likeness of Phoenix in form and in tireless voice” (17.555; see [[athena]] and [[divine-epiphany]]). The verse is the one in which Poseidon came to the two Ajaxes in the likeness of Calchas (13.45), and Athena will deceive Hector in the likeness of Deiphobus in the same words (22.227). She borrows the old man's authority to shame Menelaus: it will be a shame and a reproach to him if dogs tear “the trusted companion of noble Achilles” under the wall of the Trojans (17.556–559; see [[menelaus]] and [[katepheie]]).

Menelaus answers the man he sees: “Phoenix, old father, aged one, if only Athena / would give me strength, and keep off the force of the missiles!” (17.561–562). ἄττα, “old father”, is a younger man's word of affection for an elder, and Achilles called Phoenix so in the embassy, Φοῖνιξ ἄττα γεραιὲ διοτρεφές, “Phoenix, old father, nurtured by Zeus” (9.607; see the translation's note on 9.607). παλαιγενής, “aged”, is said elsewhere in the Iliad only of the old woman whose shape Aphrodite takes to call Helen to Paris (3.386), and in the Odyssey of the nurse Eurycleia (Od. 22.395). Athena is glad that he has prayed to her first of all the gods (17.567–568). In the last book Phoenix led the fourth company of the Myrmidons (16.196); he is not named again in Book 17 except as the shape of the goddess.
