---
summary: Ajax says that the Achaeans at the ships no longer expect the might and “the irresistible hands” of Hector to be held back.
---

“who are grieving, I think, as they look this way, and say that no longer / will the might and the irresistible hands of man-slaying Hector / be held back, but will fall upon the black ships” (17.637–639; [[man-slaying]], [[black-ships]], [[ajax]]). The last verse is the Trojans' boast as Odysseus reported it to Achilles: “they say they will no longer / be held back, but will fall upon the black ships” (9.234–235; see the translation's note on 12.106–107). Ajax speaks after Zeus has thundered from Ida and given the victory to the Trojans (17.593–596). See [[hector]].
