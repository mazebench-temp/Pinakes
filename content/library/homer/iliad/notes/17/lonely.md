---
summary: The olive sapling of the simile of Euphorbus' death grows “in a lonely place, where water has drenched it in plenty.”
---

“And as a man rears a flourishing sapling of olive / in a lonely place, where water has drenched it in plenty, / a fine, thriving tree” (17.53–55; [[flourishing]], [[euphorbus]]). The half-verse χώρῳ ἐν οἰοπόλῳ was the boar's place in the simile of Idomeneus (13.473). On the verb ἀναβέβροχεν see the translation's note on 17.54. A sudden storm uproots the tree, as Menelaus brings down the young man (17.57–60).
