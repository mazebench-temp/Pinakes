---
summary: Euphorbus sets the stakes of the first fight, “whether it end in courage or in rout” (17.42), and Apollo reports its end: Menelaus has stopped him “from his furious courage” (17.81). Hector calls the Trojans to remember their furious courage (17.185), Zeus fills Hector's limbs with courage and strength (17.211–212), and Automedon's heart is filled with them when he prays (17.499).
---

The lion of the first simile trusts in his courage, and the wounded boar of the last turns on the hounds “trusting in its courage” (17.61, 17.728; see [[lion-among-cattle]] and [[the-hounds-and-the-wounded-boar]]); Ajax charges through the champions “like a boar in courage” (17.281; see [[like-a-boar]]).
