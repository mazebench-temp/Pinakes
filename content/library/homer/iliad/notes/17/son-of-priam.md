---
summary: Zeus swears that Hector, “son of Priam,” will not ride behind the immortal horses, and Automedon fears that “Hector, son of Priam,” will not stop until he has mounted behind them.
---

The patronymic marks the two sides of the question of the horses. Zeus speaks to them as they weep: “But not behind you, nor in your intricately worked chariot, / will Hector, son of Priam, ride; I will not allow it” (17.448–449; [[intricately-worked]], [[the-horses-of-achilles]], [[zeus]]). Automedon, who does not hear the god, fears the opposite: “for I do not think / that Hector, son of Priam, will hold back from his fury / before he mounts behind the fine-maned horses of Achilles” (17.502–504; [[fine-maned]], [[automedon]]). The verb of Zeus' oath, ἐποχήσεται, is the one Hector used when he swore the horses to Dolon (10.330; see the translation's note on 17.448–449). See [[hector]].
