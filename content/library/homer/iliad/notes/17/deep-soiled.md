---
summary: Hector speaks of the men “who live in deep-soiled Lycia,” Hippothous dies “far from deep-soiled Larisa,” and Apisaon had come “from deep-soiled Paeonia.”
---

Hector to Glaucus: “Ah, truly I thought that you surpassed in wisdom all others / of those who live in deep-soiled Lycia” (17.171–172; [[lycia]], [[glaucus]]). Two of the dead are named by the rich lands they came from: Hippothous dies “far from deep-soiled Larisa” (17.301; [[larisa]], [[hippothous]], [[short-lived]]), and Apisaon “had come from deep-soiled Paeonia” (17.350; [[paeonia]], [[apisaon-son-of-hippasus]]). The epithet of fertile land marks the distance of the allies from home.
