---
summary: Apollo, in the likeness of Mentes, tells Hector that “warlike Menelaus” has killed Euphorbus, and “warlike Asteropaeus” pities Apisaon as he falls.
---

“And meanwhile warlike Menelaus, the son of Atreus, / standing over Patroclus, has killed the best of the Trojans, / Euphorbus, son of Panthous” (17.79–81; [[mentes]], [[son-of-atreus]], [[euphorbus]]). The god turns Hector from the horses of Achilles to the body. Later, “warlike Asteropaeus pitied him as he fell, / and he too made straight for them, eager to fight the Danaans” (17.352–353; [[asteropaeus]], [[apisaon-son-of-hippasus]], [[pity]]), in a verse that answers Lycomedes' a few lines before (17.346; [[dear-to-ares]]). See [[menelaus]].
