**Menelaus.** The book opens with him: “Nor did it escape the son of Atreus, Menelaus, dear to Ares, / that Patroclus had been brought down by the Trojans in the combat” (17.1–2; [[dear-to-ares]], [[menelaus]]). Euphorbus addresses him with the patronymic in a whole verse of titles (17.12; [[zeus-nurtured]], [[leader-of-men]]), and the narrator gives it to him as he kills Euphorbus, “Menelaus, son of Atreus, praying to father Zeus” (17.46), and as he strips him (17.60; [[euphorbus]]). Apollo, in the likeness of Mentes, names him so to Hector, “warlike Menelaus, the son of Atreus” (17.79; [[warlike]], [[mentes]]); Hector's shout does not escape “the son of Atreus” (17.89), with father and son parted by the verb, and the line is counted by reference. He stands across the body from Ajax, “And on the other side the son of Atreus, Menelaus, dear to Ares, / stood, nursing great grief in his breast” (17.138–139); Athena, as Phoenix, speaks first to him, “urging on the son of Atreus” (17.553; [[phoenix]]); and he drags away Podes' body, “and Menelaus, son of Atreus, / dragged the body out from among the Trojans” (17.580–581; [[podes]]). The nominative Ἀτρέος υἱός (17.79) is new to the entry.

**The two brothers.** In his call to the leaders Menelaus names the pair: “you who beside the sons of Atreus, Agamemnon and Menelaus, / drink at the public cost” (17.249–250; [[atreidae]]). Walter Leaf finds strange the change from the second person to the third and Menelaus' use of his own name (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 17.250; see the translation's note on 17.249–251).

At 17.71 the patronymic is the only name of Menelaus in the verse: “he, the son of Atreus, had not Phoebus Apollo begrudged him that” (17.71; [[phoebus]]). The six lines where Ἀτρεΐδης is Menelaus are excepted from [[agamemnon]] in that entry's note for this book. See [[atreus]].
