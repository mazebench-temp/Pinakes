summary: Zeus gives Hector the upper hand for the day: “but now at least I will put great power in your hands” (17.206), the words of his promise in Book 11 (11.192). Idomeneus, on foot, would have put great power in the Trojans' hands (17.613), and Meriones tells him, “you know yourself that the power is no longer with the Achaeans” (17.623).
---

Apollo tells Aeneas of men who saved their cities “trusting in their power and their strength” (17.329), the words in which the narrator said that the Argives would have won glory by their own power and strength (17.322; see [[would-have-had-not]]). Menelaus wishes that Athena would give him strength, κάρτος, and she does (17.561–569; see [[prayer]]).
