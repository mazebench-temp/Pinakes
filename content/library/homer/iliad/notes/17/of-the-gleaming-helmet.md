---
summary: Hector “of the gleaming helmet” leads the Trojans in Menelaus' soliloquy, answers Glaucus, and goes off to put on Achilles' armor; twice the same verse says that he “has his armor,” as the reason the body must be saved naked.
---

**Hector.** In his soliloquy Menelaus gives his reason for yielding: “for Hector of the gleaming helmet is leading all the Trojans here” (17.96; [[menelaus]]). Rebuked by Glaucus, “Looking at him darkly, Hector of the gleaming helmet said” (17.169; [[looking-darkly]], [[glaucus]]), and when he has rallied the allies, “So he spoke, and Hector of the gleaming helmet went away / out of the destructive war” (17.188–189; [[destructive]]) to change his armor ([[exchange-of-armor]]).

**The armor.** Menelaus ends his call to Ajax with the verse “naked; for Hector of the gleaming helmet has his armor” (17.122; [[ajax]]), and he ends his message to Achilles, given to Antilochus, with the same verse (17.693; [[antilochus]]). The body is to be brought back without the armor that Hector is now wearing. See [[hector]].
