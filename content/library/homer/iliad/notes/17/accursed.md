---
summary: Euphorbus tells Menelaus that by killing his brother he “brought accursed lamentation and grief upon his parents.”
---

“and you widowed his wife in the inmost part of her new bridal chamber, / and brought accursed lamentation and grief upon his parents. / Surely I would put an end to the lamentation of those wretched ones / if I brought back your head and your armor” (17.36–39; [[hyperenor]], [[lament]], [[euphorbus]]). The verse returns in Andromache's lament over Hector (24.741). Euphorbus hopes to end his parents' grief with Menelaus' head, and dies instead (17.43–60). See [[panthous]] and [[phrontis]].
