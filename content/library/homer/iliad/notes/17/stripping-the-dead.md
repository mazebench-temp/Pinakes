summary: The armor of Achilles is the stake of the book before the body is. Hector has stripped it from Patroclus, sends it to the city as his fame, and then puts it on; Zeus calls the taking “against all order”. Menelaus is kept from stripping Euphorbus, the Argives strip Hippothous and Phorcys, Automedon strips Aretus, and at the end the routed Achaeans lose much fine armor at the ditch.

**Achilles' armor.** Between the books Hector has taken the armor from Patroclus' body (17.125), and Menelaus tells Ajax that they can save at least the body, “naked; for Hector of the gleaming helmet has his armor” (17.122 = 17.693). Hector gives it to the Trojans to carry to the city, “to be great fame for him” (17.130–131; see [[kleos]]), then runs after them and puts it on, “the fine armor I stripped from mighty Patroclus when I killed him” (17.186–197; see [[exchange-of-armor]] and [[the-armor-of-peleus]]). Zeus judges the taking: Hector has killed the companion of the best of men, “and the armor, against all order, from his head and shoulders / you took” (17.204–206). Walter Leaf notes that at the end of Book 16 it was Apollo who struck the helmet from Patroclus' head and loosed his corselet, and finds it not clear why it should be against all order to take the armor of a slain enemy, “perhaps because it is of divine origin” (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 17.205; see the translation's note on 17.205 and 16.793–804). The prize turns against its winner. Andromache will never receive it from him (17.207–208), Zeus says that he “boasts of it for nothing” (17.450), and Alcimedon tells Automedon that Hector “wears the armor of the grandson of Aeacus on his own shoulders and glories in it” (17.472–473; see [[euchomai]]).

**Other spoils.** Euphorbus bids Menelaus “let the bloody spoils be” (17.13) and means to carry Menelaus' head and armor home to his parents (17.38–40). Menelaus kills him and begins to strip him, but Apollo begrudges him the armor and sends Hector, who sees “the one taking away the glorious armor” (17.60–85; see [[would-have-had-not]]). Hector promises the allies half the spoils if they win the body (17.229–232). The Argives drag away Hippothous and Phorcys “and loosed the armor from their shoulders” (17.317–318), and Automedon strips Aretus, “a lesser man”, and puts the bloody spoils in the chariot (17.537–540). At the end the giving runs the other way: “much fine armor fell around and about the ditch / as the Danaans fled” (17.760–761).
