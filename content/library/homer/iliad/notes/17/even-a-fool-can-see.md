summary: Ajax speaks the half-verse when Zeus has turned the battle with the aegis and the thunder: “even one who is an utter fool / would know that father Zeus himself is helping the Trojans” (17.629–630). Diomedes said it of the Trojans' coming ruin; Ajax says it of the Achaeans' own.
passages:
  - 17.626-647 | Ajax sees the hand of Zeus
---

The sign Ajax reads is in the missiles: “For the missiles of all of them strike home, whoever throws, / coward or brave man; Zeus guides them all the same; / but ours, all of them, fall idly to the ground in vain” (17.631–633; see [[arrows]] and [[the-help-of-zeus-is-easy-to-see]]). What follows is not flight but counsel and a prayer. Ajax proposes that they “consider the best plan”, to save the body and themselves (17.634–636; see [[metis]]), wishes for a messenger to Achilles (17.640–642), and prays to Zeus to lift the mist and destroy them, if he must, in the light (17.645–647; see [[prayer]]).
