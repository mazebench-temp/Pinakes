---
summary: Menelaus recalls “mighty Hyperenor, tamer of horses,” and Hector and an Achaean speak of the “horse-taming Trojans” who would drag Patroclus' body.
---

**Hyperenor.** “Nor indeed did mighty Hyperenor, tamer of horses, / have joy of his youth, when he scorned me and stood up to me” (17.24–25; [[hyperenor]], [[might-of-hyperenor]]). Menelaus killed him in Book 14 (14.516–519), and his brother Euphorbus now stands before him.

**The Trojans.** Hector promises half the spoils to the man who drags Patroclus “among the horse-taming Trojans” (17.229–231; [[trojan-allies]]), and an Achaean would rather the earth gaped “than to yield this man to the horse-taming Trojans / to drag to their own city and win glory” (17.418–419; [[black-earth]]). See [[trojans]].
