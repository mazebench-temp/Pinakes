summary: The first speech of the book fails to persuade: Menelaus warns Euphorbus, “but he did not persuade him” (17.33). The rest are obeyed, Menelaus twice doing what Ajax asks and Aeneas what Hector proposes, in the verse “and … did not disobey” (17.246, 17.491, 17.656; see [[so-he-spoke]]). The perfect is “trusting”: Menelaus trusts in his heavy hand, and the lion and the boar of the similes in their courage (17.48, 17.61, 17.728).
