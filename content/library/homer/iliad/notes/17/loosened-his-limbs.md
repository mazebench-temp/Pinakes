summary: The formula marks two deaths: Apisaon, son of Hippasus, struck in the liver by Lycomedes in the verses of the other Apisaon's death (17.347–349 ~ 11.577–579), and Aretus, whose limbs Automedon's spear loosened “quivering in his bowels” (17.524). Its other form, with μένος, “strength”, closes the death of Hippothous and opens Menelaus' threat to Euphorbus (17.298, 17.29).

en: [his strength was loosed, loose your strength]

**Strength.** Menelaus warns Euphorbus, “So surely I will loose your strength too, if you come / and stand against me” (17.29–30), as he did his brother Hyperenor's (see [[menos]] and [[a-fool-learns-once-it-is-done]]). Ajax's spear splits Hippothous' helmet and the brain spurts along the socket, “and there his strength was loosed”, and he lets fall the foot of Patroclus that he was dragging (17.297–299; see [[fighting-over-the-body]]).

**The two Apisaons.** The poem gives two men named Apisaon the same death in nearly the same three verses. Eurypylus “came and stood beside him, and threw his shining spear, / and struck Apisaon, son of Phausius, shepherd of the people, / in the liver beneath the midriff, and at once loosened his knees” (11.577–579); Lycomedes, pitying his companion Leiocritus, “came up close and stood and threw his shining spear, / and hit Apisaon, son of Hippasus, shepherd of the people, / in the liver beneath the midriff, and at once loosened his knees” (17.347–349; see [[gounata]]).
