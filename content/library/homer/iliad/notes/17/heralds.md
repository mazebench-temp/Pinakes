---
summary: Apollo rouses Aeneas in the likeness of Periphas, son of Epytus, a herald who had grown old in the service of Anchises, “a man whose mind was kindly toward him” (17.322–326; see [[divine-epiphany]]). The god chooses the old servant of the house, and the verb is his calling: κηρύσσων γήρασκε, “had grown old as a herald” (17.325).
grc: [κηρύσσων]
---

The father's name fits the son's work. Walter Leaf takes Ἠπυτίδης, “son of Epytus”, as a name formed from the herald's calling, the ἠπύτα κῆρυξ, “the loud-voiced herald” (7.384), as the craftsman Phereclus is “son of the builder, / the son of Harmon” (5.59–60; *Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 17.324; see the translation's note on 17.324).
