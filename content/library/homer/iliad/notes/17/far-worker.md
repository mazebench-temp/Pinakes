---
summary: “Far-working Apollo” speaks to Hector in the likeness of Phaenops, son of Asius, and sends him back against Menelaus.
---

“And Apollo stood close beside Hector and urged him on, / like Phaenops, son of Asius, who of all / his guest-friends was dearest to him, and had his home at Abydos; / in his likeness far-working Apollo spoke to him” (17.582–585; [[phaenops-son-of-asius]], [[abydos]]). The last verse is that of 17.326 with ἑκάεργος in place of Διὸς υἱός ([[son-of-zeus]]). The god reproaches Hector for shrinking from Menelaus, “a soft spearman” (17.588; [[soft]], [[spearmen]]), and grief covers him (17.591). See [[apollo]] and [[hector]].
