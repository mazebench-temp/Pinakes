summary: The book's gods come in the likeness of men: Apollo as Mentes, as Periphas and as Phaenops, and Athena as Phoenix (17.73, 17.326, 17.555, 17.585; see [[divine-epiphany]]). Of looks, Glaucus calls Hector “best in looks” in rebuke (17.142), and the narrator says that Ajax “in looks and in deeds stood out above the rest / of all the Danaans, after the noble son of Peleus” (17.279–280).
---

Glaucus' address, Ἕκτορ εἶδος ἄριστε, “Hector, best in looks” (17.142), turns on Hector the insult he threw at Paris, Δύσπαρι εἶδος ἄριστε (3.39, 13.769): a handsome man who will not fight (see [[best-in-looks]]). The narrator's praise of Ajax joins looks and deeds, as the poem does in its best men.
