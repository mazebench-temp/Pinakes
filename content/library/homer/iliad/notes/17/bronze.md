---
summary: Menelaus and Hector go through the champions “armored in gleaming bronze” (17.3, 17.87, 17.592), the best men around the body are worn down by “the pitiless bronze” (17.376), and the iron din of the battle goes up “to the bronze sky” (17.424–425).
---

The sky of bronze and the din of iron meet in one sentence, σιδήρειος δʼ ὀρυμαγδὸς / χάλκεον οὐρανὸν ἷκε (17.424–425). Walter Leaf takes σιδήρειος as inflexible, that is, indomitable and unwearying, and finds the juxtaposition of the two metals awkward, since no contrast between them is meant (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 17.424; see the translation's note on 17.424). The shields of Hector and Aeneas have “much bronze … beaten over them” on the oxhides (17.492–493; see [[shield]]).
