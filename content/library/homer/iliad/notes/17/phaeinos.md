---
summary: Four times a man “threw his shining spear”: Hector at Ajax and at Automedon, Lycomedes at Apisaon, and Menelaus filled with Athena's daring; and the narrator speaks of Menelaus' “shining eyes.”
---

**The shining spear.** The verse-end ἀκόντισε δουρὶ φαεινῷ marks four casts: “And Hector in turn threw his shining spear at Ajax” (17.304), and it kills Schedius ([[schedius]]); Lycomedes “came up close and stood and threw his shining spear” (17.347; [[lycomedes]], [[shepherd-of-the-people]]); “And Hector threw his shining spear at Automedon” (17.525; [[automedon]]); and Menelaus, filled with Athena's daring, “went to stand over Patroclus, and threw his shining spear” (17.574), killing Podes ([[podes]]).

**Menelaus' eyes.** The eagle simile ends with an address: “So then, Menelaus, nurtured by Zeus, your shining eyes / roved everywhere through the crowd of your many companions” (17.679–680; [[zeus-nurtured]], [[eagle]], [[apostrophe]]). The dual ὄσσε φαεινώ gives Menelaus the sight of the eagle that “has the sharpest sight of all the winged birds under heaven” (17.675; [[sharp]]). See [[spear]].
