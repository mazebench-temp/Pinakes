Λήϊτον αὖθʼ Ἕκτωρ σχεδὸν οὔτασε χεῖρʼ ἐπὶ καρπῷ, “And Hector, coming close, stabbed Leitus on the hand at the wrist” (17.601; see [[hector]]). He is the second of the Boeotian leaders hurt in the rout that follows Zeus' thunder from Ida, after Peneleos (17.593–600; see [[peneleos]] and [[boeotians]]). The verse names his father for the first time, “the son of great-hearted Alectryon” (17.602; see [[alectryon]]); the Catalogue gave only his name (2.494). Hector “stopped him from battle” (παῦσε δὲ χάρμης, 17.602; see [[charme]]), “and he fell back in fear, glancing around, since he no longer hoped in his heart / to hold a spear in his hand and fight with the Trojans” (17.603–604). τρέσσε δὲ παπτήνας, “he fell back in fear, glancing around”, is said of Ajax when Zeus put fear in him in Book 11 (11.546; see [[ajax]]).

As Hector rushes after him, Idomeneus throws and strikes Hector's corselet (17.605–607; see [[idomeneus]]). Leitus is not named again in the Iliad. His only kill was Phylacus, in the first day's fighting (6.35–36).
