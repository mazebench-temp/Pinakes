summary: The Achaeans over the body of Patroclus make the wish a choice: “rather let the black earth here / gape for us all” than yield the body to the Trojans to drag to their city (17.415–419). Spoken by one nameless voice for the army, it is the only time the wish is the whole army's.
grc: ["πᾶσι χάνοι"]
en: [gape for us all]
passages:
  - 17.412-423 | What the two armies said
---

Agamemnon, Hector and Diomedes wished for the earth to open before they would hear an enemy's boast or see a brother's shame (4.182, 6.281–282, 8.150). Here the shame is a body that the enemy would carry off: “Friends, it is no glorious thing for us to go back / to the hollow ships” (17.415–416; see [[kleos]] and [[aidos]]). The speech is a [[tis-speech]], and a Trojan voice answers it in kind: even if it is their fate to be brought down beside this man, let no one hang back (17.420–422; see [[moira]] and [[fighting-over-the-body]]).
