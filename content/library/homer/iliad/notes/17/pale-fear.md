---
summary: In the simile of the lion and the cow, the dogs and herdsmen do not dare come against the lion, “for pale fear takes hold of them.”
---

“and around him the dogs and the herdsmen / raise a great clamor from far off, but they are not willing / to come against him; for pale fear takes hold of them” (17.65–67; [[lion-among-cattle]], [[mountain-bred]]). The fear is in the simile, and the next verses give it to the Trojans: no one dared “to come against glorious Menelaus” (17.68–69; [[kydalimos]]). See [[deos]].
