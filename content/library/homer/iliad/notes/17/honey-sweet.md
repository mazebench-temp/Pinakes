---
summary: Euphorbus threatens to take away Menelaus' “honey-sweet life.”
---

“so let me win noble fame among the Trojans, / lest I hit you and take away your honey-sweet life” (17.16–17; [[kleos]], [[euphorbus]], [[menelaus]]). The phrase μελιηδέα θυμόν was used of Rhesus, the thirteenth Thracian whose life Diomedes took in Book 10 (10.494–495), and in the Odyssey of the life that Anticleia lost for longing for her son (*Odyssey* 11.203). Euphorbus loses his own instead (17.43–60). See [[thymos]].
