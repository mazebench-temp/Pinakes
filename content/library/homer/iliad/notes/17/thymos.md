---
summary: The θυμός is the heart men speak to and the spirit they share. Menelaus debates with his own heart, and Zeus speaks to his own heart twice (17.90–97, 17.200, 17.442); the Achaeans stand around the body “with one heart” (17.267), and the two Ajaxes fight “with equal spirit, of one name” (17.720). It is also the life taken: over the body Ajax “took the lives of many” (17.236), and Coeranus “lost his life” saving Idomeneus (17.616).
---

Three times the narrator says of a side hoping to win the body or the horses that “their hearts had high hopes” (17.234, 17.395, 17.495), and each time the hope fails. Menelaus' own heart answers Athena: Patroclus' death “has touched my heart deeply” (17.564; see [[decision-scene]] and [[the-two-ajaxes]]).
