summary: Euphorbus falls like “a flourishing sapling of olive”; the boar scatters the dogs and “the young men in their prime”; the weeping horses' “rich manes” are stained; and Antilochus' “strong voice” is checked; the book adds ἐριθηλές and θαλερή.
grc: [ἐριθηλὲς, θαλερὴ]
en: [rich, strong]
---

**The olive.** “And as a man rears a flourishing sapling of olive / in a lonely place” (17.53–54; [[lonely]], [[euphorbus]]). ἐριθηλής, “very flourishing,” is the compound of the same root.

**The young men.** Ajax goes through the champions like “a wild boar, which in the mountains the dogs and the young men in their prime / easily scatters” (17.282–283; [[like-a-boar]]).

**The manes.** The horses of Achilles weep “in longing for their charioteer; and their rich manes were stained, / streaming from under the yoke-cushion” (17.439–440; [[the-horses-of-achilles]]).

**The voice.** When Menelaus tells Antilochus the news, “his two eyes / filled with tears, and his strong voice was checked” (17.695–696; [[antilochus]], [[tears]]), verses that the Odyssey gives to Penelope (*Odyssey* 4.704–705). The neuter ἐριθηλές and the feminine θαλερή are new to the entry; the voice verse returns in Book 23 (23.397).
