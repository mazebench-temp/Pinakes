---
summary: Aeneas knows “Apollo who shoots from afar” when he looks him in the face; the book adds the accusative ἑκατηβόλον.
grc: [ἑκατηβόλον]
---

“So he spoke, and Aeneas knew Apollo who shoots from afar / as he looked him in the face, and he shouted aloud to Hector” (17.333–334; [[aeneas]]). The god has spoken to him in the likeness of the herald Periphas (17.322–332; [[periphas-son-of-epytus]], [[son-of-zeus]]), and Aeneas, seeing through the likeness, tells Hector that “one of the gods” has come to him (17.338; [[most-high]]). The accusative ἑκατηβόλον is new to the entry. See [[apollo]].
