---
summary: Hector answers Glaucus that “the mind of aegis-bearing Zeus is always stronger.”
---

“I tell you, I do not shudder at battle or at the din of horses; / but the mind of aegis-bearing Zeus is always stronger” (17.175–176; [[the-mind-of-zeus]], [[glaucus]], [[hector]]). The verse is the narrator's reflection over Patroclus in Book 16, “But the mind of Zeus is always stronger than that of men” (16.688), with the epithet in place of the comparison. Hector answers the charge of cowardice with the will of the god; four hundred lines later Zeus takes up the aegis and gives him the victory (17.593–596; [[aegis]]). See [[zeus]].
