---
summary: Glaucus speaks of Achilles' “attendants who fight hand to hand,” in Patroclus' words of Book 16, and Hector calls on the “Dardanians who fight hand to hand.”
---

**Achilles' men.** Glaucus says that the Trojans have killed the attendant of the best of the Argives, “by far the best / of the Argives beside the ships, he and his attendants who fight hand to hand” (17.164–165; [[glaucus]]). The verse is Patroclus' own, spoken to the Myrmidons as he led them out (16.272; [[myrmidons]]).

**The Dardanians.** Hector calls out: “Trojans and Lycians and Dardanians who fight hand to hand, / be men, my friends, and remember your furious courage” (17.184–185; [[furious]], [[dardanians]], [[exhortation]]). The two verses are his exhortation in earlier books (8.173–174, 11.286–287, 15.486–487).
