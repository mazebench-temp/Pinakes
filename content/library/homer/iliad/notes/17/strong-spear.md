---
summary: The lion's “valiant heart” grows chill as he leaves the farmyard, Zeus “puts even a valiant man to flight,” and Automedon is “the valiant son of Diores.”
---

**The lion.** Menelaus leaves the body like a lion driven from the steading: “and the valiant heart within him / grows chill, and he goes unwillingly from the farmyard” (17.111–112; [[lion-driven-from-the-farmyard]]).

**Zeus.** Hector answers Glaucus with the verses that the narrator spoke over Patroclus in Book 16 (16.688–690): the mind of Zeus “puts even a valiant man to flight and takes away victory / easily” (17.177–178; [[the-mind-of-zeus]]).

**Automedon.** “Truly Automedon, the valiant son of Diores, / often touched them, striking them with the swift whip” (17.429–430; [[automedon]], [[diores-father-of-automedon]], [[the-horses-of-achilles]]).
