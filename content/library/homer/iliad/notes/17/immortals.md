---
summary: Achilles alone can drive his horses, “whom an immortal mother bore”; Zeus asks the horses why they were given to a mortal, “while you are ageless and immortal”; and Automedon names “immortal horses” that only Patroclus could master.
---

**Thetis.** Apollo, as Mentes, tells Hector that the horses are hard for mortal men to master, “for any other than Achilles, whom an immortal mother bore” (17.77–78; [[mortal-men]], [[thetis]]).

**The horses.** Zeus speaks to them as they weep: “Ah, wretched pair, why did we give you to lord Peleus, / a mortal, while you are ageless and immortal?” (17.443–444; [[ageless]], [[wretch]], [[the-horses-of-achilles]]). Automedon tells Alcimedon that no one was his equal “at keeping in hand the taming and the might of immortal horses, / except Patroclus” (17.476–477; [[counselor-equal-to-the-gods]]).
