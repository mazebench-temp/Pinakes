---
summary: The patronymic names Achilles through his armor, which Hector carries off, puts on, and wears, and through the news that Antilochus must bring him; Ajax is the best of the Danaans “after the noble son of Peleus.”
---

**The armor.** Hector overtakes the men “who were carrying to the city the glorious armor of the son of Peleus” (17.191; [[famous]]) and puts on “the immortal armor / of Achilles, son of Peleus, which the heavenly gods / gave to his dear father” (17.194–196; [[heavenly-gods]], [[exchange-of-armor]]). Zeus sees him “arming himself in the armor of the divine son of Peleus” (17.199; [[divine]]) and foretells that Andromache will not receive from him “the glorious armor of the son of Peleus” (17.208; [[andromache]]). Then he goes among the allies “flashing in the armor of the great-hearted son of Peleus” (17.214; [[great-hearted]]).

**The news.** Menelaus hopes to save the body “for Achilles, son of Peleus” (17.105). Ajax wishes for a companion to take the news “to the son of Peleus, since I do not think he has even heard / the grievous news” (17.641–642; [[grim]]), and Antilochus runs “to bring the evil news to Achilles, son of Peleus” (17.701; [[antilochus]]).

**Ajax.** He “in looks and in deeds stood out above the rest / of all the Danaans, after the noble son of Peleus” (17.279–280; [[blameless]], [[ajax]]), as in the Catalogue he was the best by far “while Achilles kept up his wrath” (2.768–769). See [[achilles]] and [[peleus]].
