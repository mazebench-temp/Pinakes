---
summary: Book 17 has no night, but darkness falls on one part of the field in the middle of the day. Zeus pours mist over the men around Patroclus' body, so that “you would not have said / that the sun was still safe, or the moon”, while the rest of the armies fight under a clear sky (17.268–273, 17.366–377). Ajax prays to die, if he must, in the light, and Zeus lifts the mist (17.645–650). The night itself is promised: Zeus will give the Trojans glory to kill until the sun goes down (17.453–455).
---

**Mist and light.** The mist (ἀήρ) is the gods' cover, in which Aphrodite hid Paris from Menelaus and Hera her horses (3.381, 5.775–776; see [[divine-rescue]]); here it covers the best men around the body, “for they were covered in mist, in all that part of the battle where the best men / stood around the dead son of Menoetius” (17.368–369). Outside it “the sharp light / of the sun was spread over them, and no cloud was seen over all / the earth or the mountains”, and the others fight at their ease, with pauses (17.370–375). The translation keeps the words apart, ἀήρ “mist” over the body and αἰθήρ “upper air” over the rest (see the translation's note on 17.371). Ajax cannot find a messenger in it, “for they are covered in mist, the men themselves and the horses alike”, and his prayer asks for clear sky (αἴθρη) and light, “and in the light destroy us, since that is now your pleasure” (17.643–647; see [[prayer]]). Zeus “scattered the mist and thrust away the fog, / and the sun shone out, and all the battle was seen” (17.649–650).

**The promised night.** Zeus' promise to the Trojans of glory “to kill, until they come to the well-benched ships, / and the sun goes down and sacred darkness comes on” (17.454–455) repeats the promise of Book 11 (11.193–194, 11.208–209). The sun sets in the next book, when Hera sends it down unwilling into the stream of Ocean (18.239–242).
