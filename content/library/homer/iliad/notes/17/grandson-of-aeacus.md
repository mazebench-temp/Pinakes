---
summary: The patronymic names Achilles through what is his: his horses, which Hector chases, which weep, and which Hector hopes to take; Patroclus, his attendant; and his armor on Hector's shoulders.
---

**The horses.** Apollo tells Hector that he is chasing “the horses of the wise-hearted grandson of Aeacus” (17.76; [[wise-hearted]], [[grievous]]); “But the horses of the grandson of Aeacus, standing apart from the battle, / were weeping” (17.426–427); and Hector sees “the two horses of the swift-footed grandson of Aeacus” come out with poor charioteers (17.486; [[swift-footed]]). See [[the-horses-of-achilles]].

**The attendant.** Zeus had not hated Patroclus “while he was alive and was the attendant of the grandson of Aeacus” (17.271), and the two sides fight “around the brave attendant of the swift-footed grandson of Aeacus” (17.388; [[brave]]), the verse that set the Myrmidons fighting around Patroclus in Book 16 (16.165).

**The armor.** Alcimedon tells Automedon that Hector “wears the armor of the grandson of Aeacus on his own shoulders and glories in it” (17.473; [[alcimedon]], [[exchange-of-armor]]). See [[achilles]] and [[aeacus]].
