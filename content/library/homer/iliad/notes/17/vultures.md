---
summary: The vulture of Book 17 is Automedon, who swoops through the battle with the horses of Achilles like a vulture after geese, though alone in the chariot he can kill no one (17.459–465).
---

ἵπποις ἀΐσσων ὥς τʼ αἰγυπιὸς μετὰ χῆνας, “swooping with his horses like a vulture after geese” (17.460; see [[like-vultures]]). The αἰγυπιός is the bird of the similes, a hunter of smaller birds, here of geese (see [[geese]]); in Book 16 two of them fought each other on a high rock, as Sarpedon and Patroclus met (16.428–430). Automedon has the vulture's swoop but not its kill: alone, he cannot both drive the horses and strike with the spear (17.461–465; see [[automedon]] and [[the-horses-of-achilles]]). The other vulture, the γύψ that eats the dead, is not named in this book; the body of Patroclus is threatened instead by “the dogs and the birds of the Trojans” (17.241; see [[dogs]] and [[birds]]).
