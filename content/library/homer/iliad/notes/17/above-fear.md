---
summary: Apollo tells Aeneas that he has seen other men defend their city against a god, trusting in their strength and their numbers, “and having a people above fear.”
---

{{quote:5cb5169f-d2c5-5385-a425-c815a450b798}}

Apollo, in the likeness of the herald Periphas, speaks of other men who held out “trusting in their power and their strength and their manhood / and in their numbers, and having a people above fear” (17.329–330; [[steep]], [[untold]], [[periphas-son-of-epytus]], [[aeneas]]). With the printed word the other men hold out with a fearless people behind them; with Leaf's ὑπὲρ Δία they hold out “beyond Zeus,” as Aeneas is asked whether he could save Troy “in defiance of a god” (17.327). See [[apollo]] and [[demos]].
