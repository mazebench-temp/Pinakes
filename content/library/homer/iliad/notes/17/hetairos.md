---
summary: Patroclus is in this book what he was to Achilles, his companion: “his companion, gentle and strong” (17.204), “the trusted companion of noble Achilles” (17.557), “by far the dearest of his companions” (17.411, 17.655). Around his body the fight is told in companions: Automedon grieving for his companion, Hector for Podes, his dear companion at the feast, Lycomedes for Leiocritus.
---

**Achilles' companion.** The phrase that names the loss is the narrator's first: Thetis did not tell Achilles “that by far the dearest of his companions had perished” (17.411). Ajax makes it the message, that someone should tell Achilles “that his dear companion has perished”, “that by far the dearest of his companions has perished” (17.642, 17.655; see [[messenger-scene]]). Zeus calls Patroclus the companion of the best of men (17.204; see [[eneeie]]), and Athena “the trusted companion of noble Achilles” (17.557; see [[katepheie]]). Automedon fights on, “grieving though he was for his companion” (17.459), Alcimedon reminds him, “Your companion has been killed” (17.472), and his kill eases his heart a little “for the dead son of Menoetius” (17.538–539).

**Trusted companions.** πιστὸς ἑταῖρος, “trusted companion”, names three men in the book: Alcimedon, whom Automedon calls to take the reins (17.500), Patroclus (17.557), and Podes, Hector's “dear companion at the feast”, whose death Apollo uses to rouse Hector (17.577, 17.589–591; see [[feast]]). Pity for a fallen companion starts two charges (17.345–352; see [[pity]]). The best men call their companions and are answered: the Ajaxes come “at their companion's call” (17.532), and Menelaus finds Antilochus, as he found Ajax, “encouraging his companions” (17.683, 17.117).
