The two Ajaxes hold back the Trojans like “a wooded ridge that stretches right across the plain” (17.748; [[the-two-ajaxes]], [[grievous]], [[mighty]]). The nominative ὑλήεις is new to the entry. The ridge is the last fixed thing in the book's run of similes; then the Achaeans flee like starlings before a falcon (17.755–759).
