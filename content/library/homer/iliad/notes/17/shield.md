---
summary: The book's shields stand between the body and the enemy. Menelaus holds his in front of Patroclus, and Euphorbus' spear bends on it (17.7, 17.43–45); Ajax comes with his shield like a tower and covers the son of Menoetius with it (17.128–132; see [[the-shield-of-ajax]]); and the Achaeans stand around the body “fenced with their bronze-fitted shields” (17.268, 17.354–355).
---

One shield fails: Automedon's spear goes through the shield of Aretus, “and it did not stop the spear” (17.517–518). Hector and Aeneas come on against Automedon “their shoulders wrapped in oxhides, / dry and tough; and much bronze was beaten over them” (17.492–493; see [[bronze]]).
