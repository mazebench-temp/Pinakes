summary: Zeus watches the battle from Ida, and there he takes up the aegis, covers the mountain in clouds, flashes his lightning and thunders, and gives the Trojans the victory.

**The aegis over Ida.** Zeus has been on Ida since he woke there beside Hera (15.4–5; see [[zeus]]), seated on Gargarus, its peak, in a fragrant cloud (15.152–153; see [[gargarus]]), and from there he sees Hector arming “from afar” (17.198). When Apollo has sent Hector back into the fight, “the son of Cronus took up the tasseled aegis, / gleaming, and covered Ida in clouds” (17.593–594; see [[aegis]]), and with lightning and thunder he gives the Trojans the victory (17.595–596). In Book 8 he thundered loudly from Ida and sent a blazing flash among the Achaeans (8.75–77), and thundered three times from the mountains of Ida, “giving the Trojans a sign of the victory that turns the battle” (8.170–171); in Book 12 he roused a storm of wind from the mountains of Ida that carried the dust straight at the ships (12.252–255). When Athena is said to come down from heaven at Zeus' bidding, Walter Leaf objects that Zeus is “not in heaven but on Ida” (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 17.545; see [[athena]]).
