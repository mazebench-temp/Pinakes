---
summary: Athena, in the likeness of Phoenix, tells Menelaus that it will be “a shame and a reproach” to him if dogs tear the trusted companion of Achilles under the wall of Troy (17.556–558), the words of Sarpedon's charge to Glaucus (16.498–500; see [[katepheie]]).
---

Sarpedon laid the reproach on the companion who would let his body be stripped; Athena lays it on the man who would let Patroclus' body be torn by dogs. Menelaus answers that he would stand over Patroclus if Athena gave him strength, and she does (17.560–573; see [[prayer]]). Glaucus' rebuke of Hector earlier in the book is a reproach of the same kind without the word: Hector left Sarpedon, his guest-friend and companion, “to become the prey and spoil of the Argives” (17.150–151; see [[glaucus]] and [[xenia]]).
