---
summary: An Achaean says it would be better that “the black earth here” gape for them all than to yield Patroclus to the Trojans.
---

“Friends, it is no glorious thing for us to go back / to the hollow ships; rather let the black earth here / gape for us all; that would at once be far better for us / than to yield this man to the horse-taming Trojans” (17.415–418; [[hollow-ships]], [[tamer-of-horses]], [[tis-speech]]). The wish that the earth may gape is Agamemnon's and Diomedes', “then may the wide earth gape for me” (4.182, 8.150), and Hector's for Paris (6.282). Here it is the wish of an unnamed man of the army.
