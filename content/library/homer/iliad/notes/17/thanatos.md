---
summary: “Now death and fate have overtaken him”, Automedon says of Patroclus, and Menelaus says it again in the same verse (17.478, 17.672; again 22.436). Zeus tells Hector that death is not on his mind at all, though it is close to him (17.201–202); Achilles never expected Patroclus to be dead (17.404–405); and the Achaeans plan how they may “escape death and doom” (17.714).
---

The verse is a half-line of life and a half-line of death: ζωὸς ἐών· νῦν αὖ θάνατος καὶ μοῖρα κιχάνει, “while he lived; but now death and fate have overtaken him”. Automedon uses it of the charioteer Patroclus, who alone could manage the immortal horses (17.475–478), and Menelaus of the gentle Patroclus, who was kind to all (17.670–672; see [[eneeie]] and [[moira]]).
