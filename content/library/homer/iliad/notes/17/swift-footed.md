---
summary: Achilles is “the swift-footed grandson of Aeacus” in the verse of his attendant and in Hector's sight of his horses; Automedon cannot hold in “the swift horses,” and Coeranus drives up Idomeneus' “swift-footed horses.”
---

**Achilles.** The epithet stands before Αἰακίδαο twice: the fighting goes on “around the brave attendant of the swift-footed grandson of Aeacus” (17.388; [[grandson-of-aeacus]], [[brave]]), and Hector says to Aeneas, “I see the two horses of the swift-footed grandson of Aeacus here” (17.486). See [[achilles]].

**Horses.** Alone in the chariot, Automedon could not “attack with the spear and hold in the swift horses” (17.465; [[automedon]], [[the-horses-of-achilles]]). Idomeneus would have died “had not Coeranus quickly driven up the swift-footed horses” (17.614; [[coeranus-of-lyctus]], [[idomeneus]]).
