---
summary: “Wise-hearted” names Achilles in Apollo's words and in Ajax's, Ajax as Menelaus stirs his heart, and Phorcys, son of Phaenops, killed by Ajax.
---

**Achilles.** Apollo speaks of “the horses of the wise-hearted grandson of Aeacus” (17.76; [[grandson-of-aeacus]]), and Ajax bids Menelaus send Antilochus “to go quickly to wise-hearted Achilles” (17.654; [[antilochus]]).

**Ajax.** “So he spoke, and he stirred the heart of wise-hearted Ajax” (17.123; [[menelaus]], [[ajax]]).

**Phorcys.** “And Ajax in turn, as Phorcys, the wise-hearted son of Phaenops, / stood over Hippothous, struck him in the middle of the belly” (17.312–313; [[phorcys]], [[phaenops-father-of-phorcys]], [[hippothous]]).
