---
summary: Schedius, son of Iphitus, “by far the best of the Phocians”, is killed by Hector's spear in the fight over the body of Patroclus (17.306–311).
---

Φωκήων ὄχʼ ἄριστον, “by far the best of the Phocians” (17.307): the title comes as Hector's spear, thrown at Ajax, strikes Schedius, son of Iphitus, “who in famous Panopeus / had his home and ruled over many men” (17.306–311; see [[schedius]], [[panopeus]] and [[hector]]). The phrase is the one that named Periphas “by far the best of the Aetolians” when Ares killed him (5.843; see [[periphas]]). Schedius and his brother Epistrophus led the Phocians in the Catalogue (2.517); Hector has now killed a Phocian leader of that name twice, for the son of Perimedes, “leader of the Phocians”, fell at the ships in Book 15 (15.515–516; see [[schedius-son-of-perimedes]]). Epistrophus is not named again in the poem (see [[epistrophus]]).
