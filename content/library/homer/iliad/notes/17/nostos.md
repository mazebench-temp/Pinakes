summary: The homecoming in question is Hector's and the Achaeans'. Zeus tells Hector that he will not come home from the battle for Andromache to receive the armor from him (17.207–208); Ajax no longer expects that he and Menelaus “by ourselves will come back from the war” (17.238–239), and later asks how they may save the body “and how we ourselves / may come back and be a joy to our dear companions” (17.635–636). Achilles expected Patroclus to come back alive from the gates (17.405–406).
grc: [ἐκνοστήσαντι, νοστησέμεν, νοστήσαντες]
en: [come home, come back from the war, may come back]
---

The Achaeans' anonymous voice says that it is no glorious thing to go back to the ships without the body (17.415–416; see [[may-the-earth-gape]]), and Glaucus threatens that the Lycians will go home (17.154–155). Chromius and Aretus, who came for the horses, “were not to come back without bloodshed” (17.497–498).
