---
summary: “Menelaus, nurtured by Zeus,” in the addresses of Euphorbus and of Ajax and twice in the narrator's own voice, and once of Antilochus, whom Menelaus calls to hear the news.
---

**In address.** Euphorbus opens his challenge with a whole verse of Menelaus' titles: “Menelaus, son of Atreus, nurtured by Zeus, leader of the people” (17.12; [[son-of-atreus]], [[leader-of-men]]). The verse is the Odyssey's address to Menelaus in his palace (*Odyssey* 4.156, 4.291, 4.316, 15.64, 15.87); in the Iliad it is spoken only here, by an enemy. Euphorbus uses the vocative again as he threatens him, “Now, Menelaus, nurtured by Zeus, now you will surely pay / for my brother” (17.34–35; [[hyperenor]]). Ajax uses it twice: “My dear Menelaus, nurtured by Zeus, no longer do I expect / that we two by ourselves will come back from the war” (17.238–239; [[my-dear]]), and “Look now, Menelaus, nurtured by Zeus, if you can see / Antilochus” (17.652–653).

**The narrator.** Twice the narrator turns to Menelaus with the epithet ([[apostrophe]]): “So then, Menelaus, nurtured by Zeus, your shining eyes / roved everywhere through the crowd of your many companions” (17.679–680; [[phaeinos]]), and “Nor did your heart, Menelaus, nurtured by Zeus, wish / to help the hard-pressed companions from whom he had gone” (17.702–703). He has addressed Menelaus so in earlier books (4.127, 4.146, 7.104, 13.603), but without the epithet.

**Antilochus.** Menelaus gives the same title to the young man he sends to Achilles: “Antilochus, come here, nurtured by Zeus, so that you may hear / grievous news” (17.685–686; [[antilochus]], [[grim]]). See [[menelaus]] and [[basileus]].
