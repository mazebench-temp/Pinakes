**Among the allies.** He is the eighth of the ten leaders whom Hector urges on by name, beside Chromius and “Ennomus the augur” (17.218; see [[trojan-allies]], [[chromis]] and [[ennomus]]). Walter Leaf notes that these names are mostly taken from the Catalogue, and identifies him with Phorcys, leader of the Phrygians (2.862; *Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 17.216 and 17.312; see [[phrygians]]). The poem names his father only here: δαΐφρονα Φαίνοπος υἱόν, “the wise-hearted son of Phaenops” (17.312; see [[phaenops-father-of-phorcys]] and [[wise-hearted]]). Leaf takes Φόρκυνα (17.312) as the natural accusative and Φόρκυν (17.218, 17.318) as a shorter form like κόρυν beside κόρυθα, not a reading to be corrected (on 17.312).

The last two verses are those of Oenomaus' death at Idomeneus' hands (17.314–315 = 13.507–508; see [[oenomaus-the-trojan]]), and the clutching of the earth closes several deaths (11.425, 13.520, 14.452). “The champions and shining Hector gave ground; / and the Argives shouted aloud, and dragged away the dead, / Phorcys and Hippothous, and loosed the armor from their shoulders” (17.316–318; see [[hippothous]], [[ajax]] and [[stripping-the-dead]]). Hector's promise was that the man who dragged Patroclus away would share the spoils (17.229–232); the two allies who tried are dragged away and stripped themselves.
