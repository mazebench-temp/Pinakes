---
summary: Apollo takes the likeness of Phaenops, son of Asius, the dearest of Hector's guest-friends, who “had his home at Abydos” (17.584).
grc: [Ἀβυδόθι]
---

ξείνων φίλτατος ἔσκεν Ἀβυδόθι οἰκία ναίων: Phaenops, “who of all / his guest-friends was dearest to him, and had his home at Abydos” (17.583–584; see [[phaenops-son-of-asius]], [[hector]] and [[xenia]]). Ἀβυδόθι is “at Abydos”, with the suffix -θι of place. Abydos was one of the towns of Asius, son of Hyrtacus, in the Catalogue (2.836; see [[asius]]), and Priam's bastard son Democoon came to Troy from his mares there (4.499–500; see [[democoon]]). In the likeness of this friend of Hector's house Apollo taunts Hector for shrinking from Menelaus (17.585–590; see [[apollo]]).
