---
summary: The Pelasgians are named in the father's name of their leader Hippothous, “the shining son of Lethus the Pelasgian” (17.288), who dies over the body of Patroclus “far from deep-soiled Larisa” (17.301).
---

Πελασγοῦ, “the Pelasgian” (17.288), is said of Lethus, as in the Catalogue (2.843; see [[lethus]]), when his son Hippothous, who led the Pelasgians of Larisa (2.840–843), ties a strap to the ankle of Patroclus' body and drags it by the foot until Ajax kills him (17.288–303; see [[hippothous]] and [[ajax]]). He dies “far from deep-soiled Larisa” (17.301), the town of the Pelasgians in the Catalogue; on where that Larisa was, and how far from Troy, see [[larisa]].
