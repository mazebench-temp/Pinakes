summary: On the Trojan side fame is what the fight is for. Euphorbus asks for Patroclus' spoils so as to “win noble fame among the Trojans” (17.16); Hector sends Achilles' armor to the city “to be great fame for him” (17.131) and promises the ally who wins the body fame “as great as mine” (17.232); and Glaucus tells him that his noble fame is his “for nothing”, a runaway as he is (17.143).
grc: [ἐϋκλεὲς]
---

Glaucus' charge joins the word to one found only here, φύξηλις, “runaway”: ἦ σʼ αὔτως κλέος ἐσθλὸν ἔχει φύξηλιν ἐόντα (17.143; see [[glaucus]]). Hector answers it by putting on Achilles' armor and going among the allies (17.179–214), and the fame he wins in it is for a day (17.206–208; see [[the-mind-of-zeus]] and [[stripping-the-dead]]). The Achaeans speak of glory once, and in the negative: “Friends, it is no glorious thing for us to go back / to the hollow ships” (οὐ μὰν ἧμιν ἐϋκλεὲς ἀπονέεσθαι, 17.415–416; see [[may-the-earth-gape]]). The allies are κλειτοί, “famous” (17.14, 17.212).
