**Among the allies.** He is the fifth of the ten leaders whom Hector, in Achilles' armor, urges on by name (17.217; see [[hector]] and [[trojan-allies]]). Walter Leaf notes that these names are mostly taken from the Catalogue (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 17.216); Asteropaeus is not in it, but Sarpedon chose him with Glaucus as the best of the allies after himself (12.101–104; see [[sarpedon]] and [[glaucus]]).

**Pity and revenge.** The fight over the body runs in a chain of pity. Aeneas kills Leiocritus, and “Lycomedes, dear to Ares, pitied him as he fell” and kills the Paeonian Apisaon, son of Hippasus, who “had come from deep-soiled Paeonia, / and after Asteropaeus he was the best in the fighting” (17.344–351; see [[aeneas]], [[lycomedes]], [[apisaon-son-of-hippasus]] and [[paeonia]]). Then Asteropaeus answers in the verse that told of Lycomedes: “And warlike Asteropaeus pitied him as he fell” (17.352 ≈ 17.346; see [[warlike]]). He charges, eager to fight the Danaans, “but he could do nothing more; for they were fenced all around with their shields, / standing around Patroclus, and held their spears before them” (17.353–355), for Ajax had ordered every man to stand firm around the body and fight at close quarters (17.356–359; see [[ajax]] and [[fighting-over-the-body]]). It is the best of the Paeonians who cannot break through, and his charge ends the round of killings. In Book 21 he will face Achilles and be killed (21.139–204; see [[achilles]]).
