---
summary: Hector calls Aeneas “counselor of the bronze-armored Trojans” as he proposes to take the horses of Achilles.
---

“Aeneas, counselor of the bronze-armored Trojans, / I see the two horses of the swift-footed grandson of Aeacus here, / come out into the battle with poor charioteers” (17.485–487; [[bronze-armored]], [[the-horses-of-achilles]], [[aeneas]]). The verse is Pandarus' address to Aeneas in Book 5 (5.180), before the two ride out together against Diomedes. See [[hector]].
