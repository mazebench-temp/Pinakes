---
summary: Hector, coming through the champions at Menelaus as he strips Euphorbus, is “like the flame of Hephaestus, / unquenchable”: the god's name stands for fire. The armor Hector will put on is the gods' gift to Peleus; Hephaestus will make Achilles new armor in Book 18.
---

**The flame.** When Apollo has told Hector that Menelaus has killed Euphorbus, Hector goes through the champions, “shouting shrilly, like the flame of Hephaestus, / unquenchable” (φλογὶ εἴκελος Ἡφαίστοιο / ἀσβέστῳ, 17.88–89; see [[hector]] and [[like-a-flame]]). The god's name is the fire itself, as at Agamemnon's sacrifice, where the entrails are held “over the flame of Hephaestus” (2.426), and in Phoenix's story (9.468; see the notes on Books 2 and 9). The comparison with flame is nearly always Hector's: Poseidon calls him “that frenzied man ... like a flame” (13.53), the Achaeans cannot push him from the ships, “brilliant Hector, like a flame” (13.688), and he will come at the body of Patroclus again “like a flame in courage” (18.154) and at Achilles (20.423); only Idomeneus shares it (13.330). Menelaus says the same of him in this book: “But Hector has the dread might of fire” (17.565). ἄσβεστος, “unquenchable”, is the word of the flame that poured over Protesilaus' ship (16.123).

**The armor.** The armor that Hector puts on in this book is the gods' gift to Peleus (17.194–197; see [[the-armor-of-peleus]] and [[exchange-of-armor]]). The poem does not say who made it. In Book 18 Hephaestus will make new armor for Achilles at Thetis' request (18.468–617; see [[thetis]]).
