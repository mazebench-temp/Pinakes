summary: Hector would cut off Patroclus' head “with the sharp bronze”; spears are “sharpened,” an axe and a spear “sharp,” and Ares “sharp” in Ajax's last words; Hector's shout is “shrill”; the sun's light is “sharp” and the eagle's sight “the sharpest”; the book adds ὀξεῖα and ὀξύτατον.

en: [sharpest]

---

**Weapons.** Hector was dragging Patroclus “to cut the head from his shoulders with the sharp bronze, / and drag off the corpse and give it to the Trojan dogs” (17.126–127; [[hector]], [[trojans]]). The men around the body hold “their sharpened spears” (17.412). In the simile of the ox, a man is “holding a sharp axe” (17.520; [[of-the-fields]]), and Automedon's spear in Aretus' belly is “very sharp, quivering in his bowels” (17.524; [[aretus]]).

**Ares.** Ajax ends his answer to Menelaus: “we who even before now / have withstood sharp Ares, standing side by side” (17.720–721; [[the-two-ajaxes]], [[ares]]).

**Sound and sight.** Hector comes on “shouting shrilly, like the flame of Hephaestus, / unquenchable; nor did his shrill shout escape the son of Atreus” (17.88–89); ὀξὺ βοήσας stays with the entry, but ὀξέα κεκλήγων (17.88) is excluded, as everywhere. Over the part of the field without mist, “the sharp light / of the sun was spread over them” (17.371–372; [[helios]]), and the eagle “has the sharpest sight of all the winged birds under heaven” (17.675; [[eagle]], [[under-heaven]]). ὀξεῖα and the superlative ὀξύτατον are new to the entry; the superlative also catches the light of the Sun in Zeus' words to Hera, “whose light is the sharpest of all for seeing” (14.345). At 17.256 ὀξὺ δʼ ἄκουσεν, “heard him clearly,” is excepted, as ὀξὺ νόησε “was quick to see” is excluded.
