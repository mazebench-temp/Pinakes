summary: Old age marks the armor and the disguises of the gods. Peleus gave his son the armor the gods gave him “when he grew old; but the son did not grow old in his father's armor” (17.197). Apollo comes to Aeneas as Periphas, who “had grown old as a herald” beside Anchises (17.324–325), and Athena to Menelaus as old Phoenix, “old father, aged one” (17.561).
grc: [ἐγήρα, γήρασκε]
---

The verse about the armor has a parallel in Nestor's story of Book 7: Lycurgus wore the armor he had stripped from Areithous, “but when Lycurgus grew old in his halls, / he gave it to Ereuthalion, his dear attendant, to wear” (7.148–149). Old men hand on armor; Achilles will not live to (see [[the-armor-of-peleus]] and [[fathers-and-sons]]).
