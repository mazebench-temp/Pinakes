---
summary: Ajax prays to Zeus to rescue “the sons of the Achaeans” from the mist, and at the end of the book “the young men of the Achaeans” flee before Aeneas and Hector like starlings before a falcon.
---

**The prayer.** “Father Zeus, rescue the sons of the Achaeans from under the mist, / and make clear sky, and grant us to see with our eyes; / and in the light destroy us, since that is now your pleasure” (17.645–647; [[father-zeus]], [[ajax]]). Zeus pities him and scatters the mist (17.648–650).

**The flight.** “so before Aeneas and Hector the young men of the Achaeans / went screaming a cry of death, and forgot the joy of battle” (17.758–759; [[starlings]], [[jackdaws]], [[aeneas]], [[hector]]), with the variant κοῦροι Ἀχαιῶν. See [[achaeans]].
