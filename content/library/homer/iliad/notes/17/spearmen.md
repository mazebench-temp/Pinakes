---
summary: Apollo tells Hector that Menelaus was before “a soft spearman,” and at the end of the book “the din of horses and of spearmen” comes on the bearers of the body.
---

“Hector, which other of the Achaeans would still fear you, / now that you have shrunk back before Menelaus, who before now at least / was a soft spearman?” (17.586–588; [[soft]], [[far-worker]], [[menelaus]]). The taunt sets the noun against its adjective: a spearman who is soft. Later “the din of horses and of spearmen, / ceaseless, came upon them as they went” (17.740–741), as Menelaus and Meriones carry the body.
