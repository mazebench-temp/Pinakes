---
summary: Achilles has accepted it. He had hoped that he alone would die at Troy and that Patroclus would go home (19.328–330); his horse tells him that “the day of your destruction is near” and that he will fall by a god and a man (19.409, 19.417); and he answers, “I know well myself that it is my fate to perish here, / far from my dear father and mother”, and drives out to battle (19.421–424).
refs: [19.328-330, 19.409-410, 19.416-417, 19.421-423]
---

The choice was made in Book 18, when Thetis told him that his death was ready straight after Hector's and he answered, “May I die at once” (18.95–98). Book 19 leaves him nothing to decide. “but all the same / I will not stop until I have given the Trojans their fill of war” (19.422–423; see [[death-of-achilles-foretold]], [[the-two-fates-of-achilles]] and [[prophecy]]).
