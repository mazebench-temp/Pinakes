---
summary: Odysseus speaks of those who survive “the hateful war,” and Achilles of Peleus grieving “in hateful old age”; the book adds στυγεροῖο.
grc: [στυγεροῖο]
---

**The war.** Odysseus, urging the army to eat: “and all who survive the hateful war / must remember drink and food, so that still more / we may fight against hostile men without rest, forever” (19.230–232; [[odysseus]], [[polemos]], [[hostile-men]]).

**Old age.** Achilles, lamenting Patroclus: “For by now I think Peleus is either altogether / dead, or still alive just barely, and grieving / in hateful old age” (19.334–336; [[peleus]], [[grim]]). Achilles knows that he will not come home “to be welcomed in his halls by the old horse-driving Peleus” (18.331); the epithet gives the old man's waiting its weight.
