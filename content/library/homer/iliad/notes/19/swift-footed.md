summary: “Swift-footed Achilles” stands up to renounce his wrath, twice answers Agamemnon, and answers Xanthus' prophecy “deeply troubled”; Briseis remembers “swift Achilles” killing her husband; the book adds ὠκὺς Ἀχιλλεύς without πόδας.
grc: ["ὠκὺς Ἀχιλλεὺς"]
---

**The assembly.** “swift-footed Achilles stood up among them and spoke” (19.55), the verse with which he rose to call for a seer in the first assembly of the poem (1.58); and twice “Answering him, swift-footed Achilles said” (19.145, 19.198), to Agamemnon's offer of the gifts and to his proposal of the oath ([[most-glorious]], [[answering-him]]).

**Xanthus.** “Deeply troubled, swift-footed Achilles said to him: / ‘Xanthus, why do you prophesy death for me? You have no need to’” (19.419–420; [[deeply-troubled]], [[xanthus-the-horse]]). The verse introduced his answer to Patroclus' tears in Book 16 (16.48).

**Briseis.** “Nor would you let me, when swift Achilles killed my husband / and sacked the city of divine Mynes, / weep” (19.295–297; [[briseis]], [[mynes]]). ὠκὺς Ἀχιλλεύς without πόδας, rendered “swift Achilles” like ταχὺς Ἀχιλλεύς (18.69; [[swift]]), is new here; the Iliad uses it five times more (21.211, 22.188, 22.229, 23.218, 24.621).
