---
summary: Agamemnon could not forget Blind Folly “when in turn great Hector of the gleaming helmet / was killing the Argives at the sterns of the ships.”
---

“So I too, when in turn great Hector of the gleaming helmet / was killing the Argives at the sterns of the ships, / could not forget Blind Folly, by whom I was first blinded” (19.134–136; [[hector]], [[ate]], [[agamemnon]]). Agamemnon ends his story of Zeus, who groaned over Ate whenever he saw his son at Eurystheus' labors, by setting his own case beside it: Hector at the ships was the sight that reminded him of his blindness (19.132–136). Hector's standing epithet stands in the verse that turns the story back to the speaker.
