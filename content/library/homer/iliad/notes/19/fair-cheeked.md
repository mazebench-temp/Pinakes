---
summary: Briseis comes back to Achilles among Agamemnon's gifts: “seven, and the eighth was fair-cheeked Briseis.”
---

“and quickly they led out women skilled in flawless handiwork, / seven, and the eighth was fair-cheeked Briseis” (19.245–246; [[briseis]], [[blameless]], [[the-gifts-of-agamemnon]]). In Book 1 the same accusative, Βρισηΐδα καλλιπάρῃον, ended the verse in which Patroclus “led fair-cheeked Briseis out of the hut” and gave her to Agamemnon's heralds (1.346–347; [[taking-of-briseis]]). Now she is led out again, counted among the gifts, and brought back to Achilles' huts (19.280). She is still the object of other men's acts; when she speaks, over Patroclus, the poem praises her with other words (19.282, 19.286; [[golden]], [[like-the-goddesses]]).
