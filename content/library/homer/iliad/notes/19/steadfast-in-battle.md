---
summary: Diomedes, “the son of Tydeus, steadfast in battle,” comes to the assembly limping on his spear.
---

“And the two attendants of Ares came limping, / the son of Tydeus, steadfast in battle, and brilliant Odysseus, / leaning on their spears; for they still had grievous wounds” (19.47–49; [[son-of-tydeus]], [[diomedes]], [[attendants-of-ares]]). The epithet of the man who holds his ground in war is given as he comes hobbling to the assembly, wounded and out of the fighting.
