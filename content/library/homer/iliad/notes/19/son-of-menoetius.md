---
summary: Achilles fears that flies will make their way into “the valiant son of Menoetius” while he is away fighting.
---

“Now I will arm myself; but terribly / I fear that in the meantime into the valiant son of Menoetius / flies will make their way, through the wounds the bronze has struck, / and breed worms, and defile the body” (19.23–26; [[patroclus]], [[strong-spear]], [[flies]], [[struck-by-bronze]]). The formula of the living warrior, Μενοιτίου ἄλκιμος υἱός, names the body that Achilles fears will rot; Thetis promises to keep the flies away (19.29–33; [[silver-footed]]).
