---
summary: In Agamemnon's story Hera brings Zeus the news of Eurystheus' birth with “Father Zeus of the gleaming thunderbolt,” and Achilles, accepting the gifts, begins “Father Zeus, truly great are the blind follies you give to men.”
---

**Hera's news.** “Father Zeus of the gleaming thunderbolt, I will put a word in your mind: / now a brave man is born who will be lord of the Argives” (19.121–122; [[of-the-gleaming-thunderbolt]], [[hera]], [[eurystheus]]). The address of respect opens the news that turns Zeus' own oath against his son (19.108–124; [[ate]]).

**Achilles.** After the oath, Achilles stands up and speaks among the Argives: “Father Zeus, truly great are the blind follies you give to men: / never would the heart in my breast have been stirred / through and through by the son of Atreus” (19.270–272; [[achilles]], [[quarrel-of-achilles-and-agamemnon]]). He takes up Agamemnon's account of his blindness (19.86–90) and agrees with it: Zeus, it seems, wanted death for many Achaeans (19.273–274).
