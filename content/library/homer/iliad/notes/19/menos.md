summary: Might comes from the gods and from food. Thetis puts “a might full of daring” into her son (19.37), and in battle “a god breathes might into both sides” (19.159); Odysseus says that food and wine are “might and courage” (19.161), and Achilles answers that there will be time for meals when “there is not so much might in my breast” (19.202). The verb μενεαίνω, “rage”, runs from the quarrel to the new war: the two kings “raged in heart-devouring strife” (19.58), Achilles will not “rage stubbornly forever” (19.68), and he arms “raging against the Trojans” (19.367).
grc: [μενεήναμεν]
---

μένος πολυθαρσές, “a might full of daring”, is what Glaucus wished the Trojans had, the might that comes into men who fight for their country (17.156–158; see [[full-of-daring]] and [[tharsos]]). The book sets two sources of μένος side by side, the god who breathes it in and the meal that sustains it, and its hero takes his from the gods: he will not eat, and Athena feeds him nectar and ambrosia (19.347–354; see [[food-and-wine-are-might-and-courage]] and [[fasting]]).
