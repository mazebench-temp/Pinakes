---
summary: Zeus sees the elders weeping with Achilles and pities them, “and the son of Cronus saw them weeping and pitied them” (19.340), in the verse with which he pitied Achilles' horses weeping for Patroclus (17.441). He sends Athena to feed the man who will not eat (19.341–354).
---

The pity of Zeus leads to action both times: in Book 17 he breathed might into the horses (17.456), and here he has nectar and ambrosia dripped into Achilles' breast (19.347–354; see [[fasting]], [[nectar]] and [[divine-intervention]]). His words to Athena are spoken in the verse with which Hera, pitying the Achaeans, spoke to her in Book 8 (19.341 = 8.351; see [[winged-words]]).
