---
summary: The helmets and shields pour from the ships like snowflakes “under the rush of the North Wind, born of the clear sky.”
---

“And as when snowflakes fly thick and fast from Zeus, / cold, under the rush of the North Wind, born of the clear sky, / so then, thick and fast, the helmets, shining brightly, / were carried out from the ships” (19.357–360; [[boreas]], [[bossed]]). The second verse is the one of Iris' flight from Ida (15.171): there it measured the speed of one goddess, here the number of the army's arms, flakes thick and fast (ταρφειαί) beside the helmets thick and fast. The snow comes “from Zeus,” Διός, which Walter Leaf takes in the old sense of the sky (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 19.357; see the translation's note on 19.357).
