---
summary: The fourth day of battle dawns from the streams of Oceanus, to which the Sun was sent down at the end of the third.
---

“Now saffron-robed Dawn rose from the streams of Oceanus / to bring light to the immortals and to mortals” (19.1–2; see [[eos]] and [[the-fourth-day-of-battle]]). The third day ended in Book 18 when Hera sent the tireless Sun, unwilling, “to go back to the streams of Oceanus” (18.239–240; see [[helios]] and the note on Book 18); the new day rises out of the same river at the edge of the world. The sun rises out of Oceanus and sets in it (7.421–422, 8.485), and the streams of Oceanus are where the cranes fly in the simile that opens Book 3 (3.3–5); in the Odyssey early-born Dawn comes from them (*Odyssey* 22.197–198).
