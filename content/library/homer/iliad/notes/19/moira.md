---
summary: Fate in person ([[fates]]) stands beside a god in two pleas: Agamemnon blames “Zeus and Fate and the Erinys” (19.87), and Xanthus “a great god and mighty Fate” (19.410). The horse tells Achilles that “it is fated” for him to fall (μόρσιμον, 19.417), and Achilles answers that he knows “it is my fate to perish here” (μόρος, 19.421). The common sense is there too: Odysseus has told it all “rightly”, and the Argives listen to the king “as was right” (19.186, 19.256).
---

Μοῖρα κραταιή, “mighty Fate”, is said of the share of death that overpowers a man (see [[mighty-fate]]). The two personifications differ (see [[fates]]). Agamemnon's Fate is one of three powers that cast blind folly into his mind (19.87–88; see [[ate]] and [[erinyes]]); Xanthus' Fate, with Apollo, killed Patroclus and will kill Achilles “by a god and a man” (19.413–417; see [[death-of-achilles-foretold]]). ἐν μοίρῃ (19.186) stands for the usual κατὰ μοῖραν, as in the Odyssey (*Odyssey* 22.54; Walter Leaf, *Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 19.186).
