---
summary: Achilles remembers how Patroclus used to set “a pleasant meal” before him in the hut; the translation renders λαρόν “pleasant.”
en: [pleasant]
---

“Truly once, you too, most ill-fated one, dearest of my companions, / would yourself set a pleasant meal before me in the hut, / swiftly and quickly, whenever the Achaeans were in haste / to bring tearful Ares against the horse-taming Trojans” (19.315–318; [[patroclus]], [[most-ill-fated]], [[tearful]], [[tamer-of-horses]]). The meal Patroclus served is remembered while Achilles refuses the meal the kings press on him: “But now you lie torn, and my heart / goes without drink and food, though they are here within, / for longing for you” (19.319–321; [[feast]]).
