---
summary: Two pleas of “not to blame” frame the book. Agamemnon says before the army, “I am not to blame, / but Zeus and Fate and the Erinys who walks in darkness” (19.86–87); Achilles' horse says that the death of Patroclus was not their fault, “nor are we / to blame, but a great god and mighty Fate” (19.409–410).
---

The king's plea is made to excuse his own act, which he pays for in gifts all the same (19.137–138; see [[a-god-brings-all-things-to-fulfillment]] and [[ate]]). The horse's is made for an act that was not theirs: “it was not through our slowness or sloth / that the Trojans took the armor from the shoulders of Patroclus”, but Apollo killed him (19.411–414; see [[xanthus-the-horse]]). In both the blame passes to a god and to Μοῖρα, Fate (see [[moira]] and [[who-is-to-blame]]).
