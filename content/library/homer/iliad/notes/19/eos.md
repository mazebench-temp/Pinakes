# Unnamed: Dawn rising "to bring light to the immortals and to mortals" (19.2).

{{quote:33138573-dfc6-5f72-88bc-e15913d3829a}}

The night of Book 18, with the mourning for Patroclus and the making of the arms, ends here, and the dawn is the one Thetis promised: “for at dawn I will return with the rising sun, / bringing fine armor from lord Hephaestus” (18.136–137; see [[thetis]] and [[hephaestus]]). It opens the fourth day of battle, the day of Achilles' return (see [[the-fourth-day-of-battle]] and [[dawn-line]]). The first verse begins as the dawn of the second day of battle, “Now saffron-robed Dawn was spreading over all the earth” (8.1; again 24.695), and the second is the verse of the dawn of Book 11, when Dawn rose from the bed of Tithonus (19.2 = 11.2; Walter Leaf, *Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 19.1; see [[saffron-robed]]). Dawn rises from the streams of Oceanus, to which Hera sent the Sun down at the end of the third day (18.239–240; see [[oceanus]] and [[helios]]); in the Odyssey Eumaeus tells Melanthius that early-born Dawn, coming from the streams of Oceanus, will not escape his notice (*Odyssey* 22.197–198).
