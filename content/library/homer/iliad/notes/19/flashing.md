---
summary: Xanthus, “the horse with flashing feet,” answers Achilles from under the yoke; the book adds πόδας αἰόλος.
grc: ["πόδας αἰόλος"]
---

“And from under the yoke the horse with flashing feet spoke to him, / Xanthus, and at once he bowed his head” (19.404–405; [[xanthus-the-horse]], [[the-horses-of-achilles]]). πόδας αἰόλος occurs only here. Walter Leaf compares the “flashing of feet” of the Phaeacian dancers (μαρμαρυγὰς … ποδῶν, *Odyssey* 8.265), the sparkle of feet in rapid movement (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 19.404; see the translation's note on 19.404). The phrase is built like πόδας ὠκύς, the epithet of the master who answers him (19.419; [[swift-footed]]).
