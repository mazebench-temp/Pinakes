---
summary: Alcmene, whom Zeus named among his loves on Ida (14.323–324), is to bear his son Heracles “in well-crowned Thebes” on the day of Hera's trick (19.98–99), and Hera holds back her labor (19.119).
refs: [19.98-99, 19.119]
---

Zeus boasts that the child born that day will be “of my blood” (19.105), and the rival whom Hera brings to birth first, Eurystheus, is of his blood too, through Perseus, the son Danae bore him, another of the loves he named on Ida (14.319–320; see [[the-birth-of-heracles]], [[alcmene]] and [[perseus]]).
