---
summary: Dawn rises “to bring light to the immortals and to mortals,” and Achilles sees that the armor is fit “for the works of immortals.”
---

**Dawn.** “Now saffron-robed Dawn rose from the streams of Oceanus / to bring light to the immortals and to mortals” (19.1–2; [[eos]], [[saffron-robed]], [[oceanus]]). The second verse is the one that brought the dawn of Book 11 (11.2), the morning of [[the-third-day-of-battle]]; now it opens the day on which Achilles returns to the war.

**The armor.** “My mother, the armor the god has given is such as is fitting / for the works of immortals, and no mortal man could make it” (19.21–22; [[the-making-of-the-arms]], [[hephaestus]]). The line between gods and men is drawn by the craftsmanship: the armor is a work no mortal hand could make.
