---
summary: Odysseus checks Achilles in the verse with which Agamemnon checked him in Book 1, “godlike Achilles, brave as you are”; and Achilles names his son, “godlike Neoptolemus.”
---

**Achilles.** “Not like this, godlike Achilles, brave as you are: / do not urge the sons of the Achaeans to Ilios fasting” (19.155–156; [[odysseus]], [[sons-of-the-achaeans]]). The verse is the one that opened Agamemnon's refusal in Book 1 (1.131). Walter Leaf finds the same thought in both places: do not, because you are mighty, expect all men to do what you can (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 19.155). Here it opens advice, not a refusal.

**Neoptolemus.** “or of my dear son, who is being reared for me in Scyros, / if indeed godlike Neoptolemus is still alive” (19.326–327; [[neoptolemus]], [[scyros]]). It is the only place where the Iliad names Achilles' son. Aristarchus and Aristophanes athetized the verse, among other reasons because the epithet did not suit (see the translation's note on 19.326–337).
