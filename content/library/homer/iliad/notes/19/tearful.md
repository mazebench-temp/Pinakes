---
summary: Achilles remembers the meals Patroclus served “whenever the Achaeans were in haste / to bring tearful Ares against the horse-taming Trojans.”
---

“Truly once, you too, most ill-fated one, dearest of my companions, / would yourself set a pleasant meal before me in the hut, / swiftly and quickly, whenever the Achaeans were in haste / to bring tearful Ares against the horse-taming Trojans” (19.315–318; [[ares]], [[tamer-of-horses]], [[laros]]). The verse is the one in which Hector, in Book 8, wished the Achaeans to shrink from war (8.516). In the lament it marks the mornings of battle when Patroclus fed his friend before the fighting, and Achilles, who will not eat now, weeps over them (19.319–321).
