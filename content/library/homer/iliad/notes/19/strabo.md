---
summary: Strabo quotes Agamemnon's order to choose κούρητες, “young men”, to show that Homer called young soldiers by the name of the Curetes, and Briseis' lament three times, to show that the city of Mynes was Lyrnessus and not Thebe and to explain why the people of Mynes and of Eetion are missing from the Catalogue. Those whom he reports took Ἄλκιμος, Achilles' groom, for a shortened Ἀλκιμέδων.
---

- κούρητες, “young men”, in his account of the name of the Curetes, with a text that differs from ours in four words (*Geography* 10.3.8, on 19.193–195 and 19.248): [[strabo-on-the-kouretes]].
- “The city of divine Mynes”: Lyrnessus, which Mynes ruled and died defending, not Thebe, which was Eetion's (*Geography* 13.1.7, 13.1.61, 13.3.1, on 19.295–296): [[strabo-on-the-city-of-mynes]].
- **Alcimus.** Strabo reports that those who took Homer's Messe for a shortened Messene cited other shortenings from the poet, among them “the hero Automedon and Alcimus”, Ἄλκιμος “instead of Ἀλκιμέδων” (*Geography* 8.5.3). The verse he quotes is 24.474; in this book the same pair yokes Achilles' horses, “And Automedon and Alcimus, busying themselves with the horses” (19.392): [[alcimedon]] and [[automedon]].
