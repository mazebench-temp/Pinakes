**Athena's descent.** Zeus sends Athena to drip nectar and ambrosia into Achilles' breast, and she “leapt down from the sky through the upper air” (οὐρανοῦ ἐκκατεπᾶλτο διʼ αἰθέρος, 19.351) like a long-winged, shrill-voiced bird of prey (see [[like-a-bird-of-prey]], [[athena]] and [[sky]]).

**The blaze of the shield.** When Achilles takes up the shield, its blaze goes up like a fire burning high in the mountains, which sailors see from the sea: “so from the shield of Achilles the blaze reached the upper air” (ὣς ἀπʼ Ἀχιλλῆος σάκεος σέλας αἰθέρʼ ἵκανε, 19.379; see [[the-fire-seen-by-sailors]] and [[the-shield-of-achilles]]). The verse is the one of the flame from his head at the ditch in Book 18, with the shield in place of the head (18.214; see [[the-beacon-fires]]). The gleam of the whole army has just reached the sky (19.362), as the gleam of the army going out in Book 2 went up “through the upper air to the sky” (2.458; see [[snowflakes-from-zeus]]).
