summary: The breast holds the heart, and it is where the gods' food goes. Athena drips nectar and ambrosia “into his breast” (19.348, 19.352–354); the heart in the breast is tamed, hopes and is stirred (19.66, 19.271, 19.328), and there is “not so much might in my breast” in the time of peace that Achilles foresees (19.202). Briseis tears her breasts in grief (19.285), and Achilles puts on the corselet around his chest (19.371).
grc: [στήθεσσʼ]
en: [breast]
---

στάξον ἐνὶ στήθεσσʼ, “drip … into his breast” (19.348), puts the food of the gods where Homer puts the heart and the spirit, not in the belly; Odysseus had called the belly the wrong organ for mourning (19.225; see [[weep-for-a-day-and-bury]], [[nectar]] and [[ambrosia]]). Tearing the breast is a mourner's act (see [[lament]]).
