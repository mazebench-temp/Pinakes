---
summary: In Agamemnon's story Hera, “female though she was, deceived” Zeus “with her treachery” (19.97): she called his boast false in advance, made him swear an oath whose terms she could turn, and he “did not perceive her treachery at all” (19.106–113).
refs: [19.97, 19.106-113]
---

ἀπάτησεν, “deceived”, and δολοφροσύνη, “treachery”, are the words of the story (19.97, 19.112). Hera's address, “And queen Hera, with treachery in her heart, addressed him” (19.106), is the verse that introduced her speeches to Zeus on Ida, where she deceives him a second time (14.300, 14.329; see [[the-deception-of-zeus]]). ψευστήσεις, “You will prove false” (19.107), is her charge against Zeus, made so that he will bind himself (see [[oath]] and [[ate]]).
