---
summary: Achilles tames his heart and ends the quarrel, “taming the dear heart in our breasts under compulsion” (19.66 = 18.113), but his heart will not be comforted, “not at all in his heart / would he be comforted, before he entered the mouth of bloody war” (19.312–313). Odysseus asks that Achilles' heart be “gracious” (19.178), Agamemnon's heart bids him swear (19.187), and the dead must be buried by men “having a pitiless heart” (19.229).
---

The heart is where the quarrel was felt: “never would the heart in my breast have been stirred / through and through by the son of Atreus” but for Zeus (19.271–272; see [[ate]]). It hoped for what did not come, that Achilles alone would die at Troy (19.328–330), and it is the measure of Briseis' love for the dead man, “Patroclus, dearest to my heart” (19.287). In Agamemnon's story Zeus speaks “what the heart in my breast bids me” (19.102; see [[hear-me]]). νηλέα θυμόν, “a pitiless heart” (19.229), is what Phoenix told Achilles he had no need to have, in the same words with ἦτορ for θυμός (νηλεὲς ἦτορ, 9.496–497; see [[weep-for-a-day-and-bury]]); here Odysseus asks it of every mourner in the army. See [[phrenes]], [[stethos]] and [[propitiation]].
