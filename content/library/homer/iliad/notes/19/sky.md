# οὐρανοῦ is always the sky: besides 19.130 and 19.351 it catches 5.749, 5.769, 6.108, 6.128, 8.46 and 8.393.
grc: [οὐρανοῦ]

**Blind Folly thrown down.** Zeus swore “that never to Olympus and the starry sky / would Blind Folly come again”, and “So saying, he hurled her from the starry sky, / whirling her round in his hand; and soon she came to the works of men” (19.128–131; see [[ate]], [[zeus]] and [[starry]]). The sky with Olympus is the gods' home, and Zeus throws down from it the gods who cross him: Hephaestus, seized by the foot and hurled from the divine threshold to fall on Lemnos (1.590–594), and the gods who tried to free Hera when he hung her in the upper air (15.18–24; see [[hera-hung-from-the-sky]]); he threatens to hurl into Tartarus any god who helps either side (8.10–16). Blind Folly is thrown down to men for good.

**Looking up.** εὐξάμενος δʼ ἄρα εἶπεν ἰδὼν εἰς οὐρανὸν εὐρύν, “And praying he spoke, looking up into the wide sky” (19.257). The half-verse is the army's, praying while the lots are shaken for the duel with Hector (7.178), and Menelaus', groaning when his sword broke on Paris' helmet (3.364; see [[wide-sky]] and [[prayer]]). Agamemnon calls Zeus to witness first, “highest and best of the gods” (19.258; see [[oath]]).

**From the sky and to it.** Athena “leapt down from the sky through the upper air” (19.351; see [[like-a-bird-of-prey]] and [[upper-air]]). When the army arms, “the gleam reached the sky” (19.362), as the gleam of the army marching out in Book 2 went up “through the upper air to the sky” (2.458; see [[snowflakes-from-zeus]]).
