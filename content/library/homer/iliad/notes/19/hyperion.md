---
summary: Achilles, armed and standing in his chariot, gleams “like shining Hyperion”, ἠλέκτωρ Ὑπερίων, two names of the Sun joined only here.
---

“And behind him Achilles took his stand, armed, / gleaming in his armor like shining Hyperion” (19.397–398; see [[achilles]], [[helios]] and [[like-the-sun]]). The nominative Ὑπερίων is found in Homer only here; elsewhere the name stands beside Ἠέλιος, as in the rays of “Hyperion the Sun” that the Titans in Tartarus never see (8.480; see the translation's note on 8.480). ἠλέκτωρ, “the beaming one”, is the sun in the half-verse said of Paris in Book 6, “gleaming in his armor like the shining sun” (6.513), and Walter Leaf refers to his notes on those two places (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 19.398). Here both names of the Sun come together, at the end of an arming in which the shield shines like the moon and the helmet like a star (19.374, 19.381; see [[moon]] and [[like-a-star]]).
