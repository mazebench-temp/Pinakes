---
summary: Xanthus names Apollo only by his mother: “the best of the gods, whom Leto of the lovely hair bore.”
---

“For it was not through our slowness or sloth / that the Trojans took the armor from the shoulders of Patroclus; / but the best of the gods, whom Leto of the lovely hair bore, / killed him among the champions, and gave glory to Hector” (19.411–414; [[apollo]], [[leto]], [[the-death-of-patroclus]]). The relative clause ὃν ἠΰκομος τέκε Λητώ is the one with which Chryses named the god in his prayer in Book 1, “to lord Apollo, whom Leto of the lovely hair bore” (1.36). The horse does not speak the god's name. See [[xanthus-the-horse]].
