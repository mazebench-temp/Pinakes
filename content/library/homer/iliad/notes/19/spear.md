---
summary: Diomedes and Odysseus come to the assembly leaning on their spears, and Agamemnon still has the wound that Coon's spear gave him (19.47–53); Achilles longs to be seen again destroying the Trojan battalions with his bronze spear (19.151–152); the army's ash spears pour out of the ships (19.361); and Achilles draws from its case his father's spear, the Pelian ash that no other Achaean could wield (19.387–391).
---

**The wounded.** “And the two attendants of Ares came limping, / the son of Tydeus, steadfast in battle, and brilliant Odysseus, / leaning on their spears; for they still had grievous wounds” (19.47–49), and Agamemnon came last, wounded by Coon's bronze-fitted spear (19.51–53; see [[the-wounded-chiefs]] and [[coon]]).

**The spear of Achilles.** Achilles wants to fight at once, “so that once again a man may see Achilles among the foremost / destroying the battalions of the Trojans with his bronze spear” (19.151–152), and Odysseus grants him that he is “stronger than I, and better by no small amount / with the spear”, but claims the better understanding for himself (19.217–218; see [[odysseus]]). When the army arms, its μείλινα δοῦρα, “ash spears”, pour out of the ships with the helmets, shields and corselets (19.361; see [[ash-spear]] and [[snowflakes-from-zeus]]).

**His father's spear.** Last of the arms, Achilles draws his father's spear from its case: ἐκ δʼ ἄρα σύριγγος πατρώϊον ἐσπάσατʼ ἔγχος (19.387). The σῦριγξ is here a socket in which to set a spear, a sense found only here; Walter Leaf compares the spear-stand by a pillar in the house of Odysseus (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 19.387; *Odyssey* 1.127–129; see [[syrinx]] and the translation's note on 19.387). The next four verses are those with which Book 16 told why Patroclus did not take it: it is “heavy, huge, and massive; no other of the Achaeans / could wield it, but Achilles alone knew how to wield it”, the Pelian ash that Chiron gave to Peleus (19.388–391 = 16.141–144; see [[the-pelian-ash]] and [[arming-scene]]). It is the one piece of Achilles' arms that did not go out with Patroclus (16.140).
