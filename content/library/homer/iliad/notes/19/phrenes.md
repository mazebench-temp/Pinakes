---
summary: The mind is where blindness is cast and from which wits are taken. Agamemnon says that Zeus, Fate and the Erinys “in the assembly cast savage blind folly into my mind” (19.88), and that “Zeus took away my wits” (19.137); Hera's news strikes Zeus with grief “deep in his heart” (19.125). Thetis tells her son, “do not let these things trouble your mind” (19.29), and he says that food is not in his mind, “but slaughter and blood” (19.213–214); Zeus asks Athena whether Achilles is “no longer at all in your mind” (19.343).
---

φρένας ἐξέλετο, “took away my wits” (19.137), is the act of a god, and ἄτη is what a god puts in their place (see [[ate]]). Odysseus asks that the gifts be seen so that Achilles “may be warmed in your heart” (φρεσὶ … ἰανθῇς, 19.174), and that his heart be gracious “in your breast” (19.178; see [[thymos]]). See [[noos]].
