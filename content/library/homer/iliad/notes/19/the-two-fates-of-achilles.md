---
summary: The choice of Book 9 is now spoken of as knowledge. Answering Xanthus, Achilles says, “I know well myself that it is my fate to perish here, / far from my dear father and mother”, and that he will not stop until the Trojans have had their fill of war (19.421–423): of the two fates, his is the one without a homecoming.
refs: [19.421-423]
---

In Book 9 the two roads were set side by side: “If I stay here and fight around the city of the Trojans, / my homecoming is lost, but my fame will be imperishable” (9.412–413), or a long life at home without fame (9.414–416). In Book 18 Achilles took the first, “since I am not going home to my dear native land” (18.101; see the note on Book 18). In Book 19 he no longer chooses. He answers the horse that foretells his death, “I know well myself that it is my fate to perish here, / far from my dear father and mother” (μόρος ἐνθάδʼ ὀλέσθαι / νόσφι φίλου πατρὸς καὶ μητέρος, 19.421–422; see [[death-of-achilles-foretold]], [[xanthus-the-horse]] and [[nostos]]). ἐνθάδε, “here”, answers the αὖθι μένων of Book 9, “If I stay here and fight” (9.412). His lament for Patroclus says the same of the past: he had hoped that he alone would die “here in Troy”, and that Patroclus would go home to Phthia (19.328–330).

Achilles does not speak of fame in this book. What he sets against his death is the war: “but all the same / I will not stop until I have given the Trojans their fill of war” (19.422–423; see [[the-fourth-day-of-battle]]).
