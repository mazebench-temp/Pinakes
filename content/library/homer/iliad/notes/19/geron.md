summary: The elders of the Achaeans gather around Achilles and beg him to eat (19.303–304); the old horse-driving Phoenix stays with him (19.311); and when Achilles imagines his father Peleus “in hateful old age”, the elders groan with him, each remembering what he left at home (19.336–339).
---

γέροντες here names the kings of the council, not their age (see [[elders]]): Achilles sends “the other kings” away, and the sons of Atreus, Odysseus, Nestor, Idomeneus and Phoenix stay (19.309–311; see [[basileus]]). Peleus' old age is the subject of the end of Achilles' lament: he may be dead, or barely alive and grieving, “always waiting for / the grievous news of me” (19.334–337; see [[old-age-and-memory]] and [[bereaved-parents]]). Odysseus' claim to know more because he was born first is the claim of age without the word (19.219).
