---
summary: “Odysseus of many wiles” twice answers Achilles with the counsel that the army must eat before it fights.
---

“Answering him, Odysseus of many wiles said” (19.154, 19.215; [[odysseus]], [[answering-him]]). Both speeches argue for food: the army should not be sent to fight fasting, since no man can fight all day without eating (19.155–183); and Achilles, stronger with the spear, should yield to the older man's understanding, for the Achaeans cannot mourn the dead with the belly (19.216–237). In the second speech Odysseus names his own gift: “but I would surpass you in understanding / by far, since I was born first and know more” (19.218–219), the counsel that his epithet names (μῆτις).
