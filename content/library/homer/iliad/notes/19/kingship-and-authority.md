summary: The king of the army admits his wrong before his people, pays and swears (19.76–144, 19.249–266), and Odysseus tells him that it is no shame for a king to make amends (19.182–183). In Agamemnon's story lordship is the prize of an oath: Zeus swears that the child of his blood born that day will be lord over all who dwell around him, and Eurystheus becomes lord of the Argives, with Heracles his laborer (19.103–124, 19.133).
refs: [19.76-77, 19.103-111, 19.122-124, 19.182-183, 19.256]
---

The king keeps his place while he yields. The Argives sit in silence at his oath, “listening to the king, as was right” (19.256; see [[silence]]), and Achilles leaves the gifts to his will, “to give them if you wish, as is fitting, / or to keep them by you” (19.147–148).
