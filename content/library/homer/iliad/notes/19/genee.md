summary: Zeus' boast and Hera's oath turn on lineage. Zeus says that the child born that day will be “of the lineage of those men who are of my blood” (19.105); Hera makes him swear it of anyone “sprung from the blood of your stock” (19.111), and tells him that Eurystheus is “of your race” (19.124).
grc: [γενέθλης]
---

Eurystheus is of the blood of Zeus through Perseus, as Heracles is through Alcmene, Perseus' granddaughter, and so the oath holds for him (Walter Leaf, *Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 19.105; see [[perseus]] and [[eurystheus]]). Some found Hera's guile in the change from Zeus' words to hers; Leaf finds it in Zeus' rashness in swearing at all (on 19.111; see [[oath]] and [[ate]]).
