summary: Three speakers retell what the poem has told. Agamemnon names the act of Book 1, “on the day when I myself took away the prize of Achilles”, and lays it on Zeus, Fate and the Erinys (19.86–89); Briseis tells the sack of Lyrnessus as the woman taken (19.291–296); and Xanthus tells Patroclus' death as Apollo's work, ending in Thetis' verse to Hephaestus (19.413–414; 19.414 = 18.456).
refs: [19.86-89, 19.291-296, 19.411-414]

Briseis' version differs from the Catalogue's. There the narrator told how Achilles took her from Lyrnessus and struck down Mynes and Epistrophus (2.690–693); she speaks of a husband killed before the city and three brothers dead, and names Mynes only as the lord of the city (19.291–296; see [[mynes]] and [[the-sack-of-lyrnessus]]). Xanthus gives the killing to Apollo and the glory to Hector, says nothing of Euphorbus, and so clears himself and his yokemate: “it was not through our slowness or sloth” (19.411; see [[the-death-of-patroclus]] and [[xanthus-the-horse]]).
