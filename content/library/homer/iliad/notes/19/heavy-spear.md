---
summary: Achilles draws his father's spear from its case, “heavy, huge, and massive,” which no other Achaean could wield.
---

“And from its case he drew his father's spear, / heavy, huge, and massive; no other of the Achaeans / could wield it, but Achilles alone knew how to wield it, / the Pelian ash, which Chiron gave to his dear father / from the peak of Pelion, to be death for heroes” (19.387–391; [[the-pelian-ash]], [[chiron]], [[pelion]], [[peleus]]). The four verses 388–391 are those of Book 16, where Patroclus armed in Achilles' armor but left this spear behind (16.141–144); Walter Leaf sees no reason to doubt them here (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 19.388). The spear that Patroclus could not take ends the arming of its owner (19.364–391; [[arming-scene]]).
