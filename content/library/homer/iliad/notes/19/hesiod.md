---
summary: The Hesiodic Shield of Heracles tells the begetting of Heracles that Agamemnon's story passes over, gives Iphicles the folly that Zeus sent Agamemnon in the same words, and shares the verses of Achilles' arming and his gnashing teeth. The Theogony makes Ate a daughter of Strife, calls Thebes well-crowned, and strengthens the Hundred-Handers for battle with the nectar and lovely ambrosia that Athena drips into Achilles.
---

- The begetting of Heracles, the folly of Iphicles, the arming of Heracles and the gnashing of teeth ([Hesiod], *Shield* 1–56, 77–94, 122–138, 144–146, 161–165): [[heracles-in-the-hesiodic-shield]].
- **Ate.** Agamemnon's Ate is “the eldest daughter of Zeus” (19.91). In the *Theogony* she is a daughter of Strife, born with Lawlessness, the two “close companions of each other”, among Toil and Hunger, Battles and Quarrels, and Oath, who most harms men on earth whenever anyone swears a false oath knowingly (*Theogony* 226–232). Walter Leaf cites the verse in his note on Agamemnon's Ate (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 19.91). Hesiod's line on Oath ends in the words of Agamemnon's oath, which calls on the Erinyes who punish “whoever has sworn a false oath” (ἐπίορκον ὀμόσσῃ, 19.260; *Theogony* 232): [[ate]], [[hesiods-two-strifes]] and [[oath]].
- **Well-crowned Thebes.** Homer calls a city ἐϋστέφανος only once, Thebes at 19.99, where Heracles was to be born; the *Theogony* uses the same verse-end, ἐυστεφάνῳ ἐνὶ Θήβῃ, of Polydorus, whom Harmonia bore to Cadmus there (*Theogony* 975–978; Leaf on 19.99). It also tells that Alcmene bore βίην Ἡρακληείην, “mighty Heracles”, to Zeus (*Theogony* 943–944): [[well-crowned]], [[thebes]] and [[the-birth-of-heracles]].
- **Nectar and lovely ambrosia.** Before the battle with the Titans Zeus gives the Hundred-Handers nectar and ambrosia, the food of the gods, and their spirit grows in their breasts “once they had tasted nectar and lovely ambrosia”, νέκταρ τʼ ἐπάσαντο καὶ ἀμβροσίην ἐρατεινήν (*Theogony* 639–642). Zeus sends Athena to drip the same into Achilles' breast before his battle, “so that hunger may not come upon him” (19.347–348, 19.353–354): [[nectar]] and [[fasting]].
