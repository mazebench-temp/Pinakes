---
summary: Briseis remembers her husband, “the husband to whom my father and lady mother gave me”, whom Achilles killed before her city (19.291–296). She calls him ἀνήρ, not πόσις; in this book πόσις means “drink” (19.210).
# πόσις at 19.210 (οὐ πόσις οὐδὲ βρῶσις, "neither drink nor food") is the noun "drink", from the root of πίνω, not "husband".
except: [19.210]
# Unnamed: Briseis' husband, ἀνήρ (19.291, 19.295).
refs: [19.291, 19.295]
---

“The husband to whom my father and lady mother gave me / I saw before the city, torn by the sharp bronze” (19.291–292), and “Nor would you let me, when swift Achilles killed my husband / and sacked the city of divine Mynes, / weep” (19.295–297; see [[briseis]] and [[marriage]]). Both times her word for him is ἀνήρ, “man”, as Andromache's is for Hector when she begins her lament over him, ἆνερ (24.725; see [[andromache]]). The wife in the Odyssey's simile, who throws herself on the husband fallen before his city, weeps for her πόσις (*Odyssey* 8.523; see [[lament]]). πόσις, “drink”, in Achilles' refusal of food (19.210), and its genitive πόσιος (19.231, 19.320), are another word, from the root of πίνω, “drink”.
