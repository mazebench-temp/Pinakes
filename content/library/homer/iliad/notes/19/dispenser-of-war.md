---
summary: Odysseus, urging the army to eat before it fights, says that men soon have their fill of battle when Zeus tips the scales, “Zeus, who is the dispenser of war for men.”
---

“Quickly men come to satiety of the din of battle, / in which the bronze strews the most straw on the ground, / but the harvest is smallest, when he tips the scales, / Zeus, who is the dispenser of war for men” (19.221–224; [[odysseus]], [[zeus]], [[the-scales-of-zeus]]). The verse is the one the soldiers spoke in Book 4 as Athena came down like a star (4.84). Odysseus uses it to argue that the army must eat: battle is a harvest of much straw and little grain, and men need food to bear such thankless work (see the translation's note on 19.221–224). Walter Leaf, after Lendrum, suggests that ταμίης may have a point here: Zeus is a steward, but not of food, unlike the stewards “dealing out the food” beside the ships (19.44; *Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 19.224).
