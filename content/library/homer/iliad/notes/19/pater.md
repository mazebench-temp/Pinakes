---
summary: Achilles goes to war with his father's gifts, “his father's spear” from Chiron (19.387–391) and “the horses of his father” (19.399), and imagines Peleus in Phthia weeping for him or dead (19.322–323, 19.334–337); he knows he will die “far from my dear father and mother” (19.422). In Agamemnon's story Zeus is a father who groans to see his son labor (19.132–133), and he is “Father Zeus” to Hera and to Achilles (19.121, 19.270). Briseis was given in marriage by “my father” (19.291).
---

The spear and the horses were gifts to Peleus, the spear from Chiron and the horses from the gods (19.390; 16.143–144, 16.381; see [[the-pelian-ash]] and [[the-horses-of-achilles]]). The son goes out to die in his father's arms and behind his father's horses (see [[fathers-and-sons]] and [[peleus]]).
