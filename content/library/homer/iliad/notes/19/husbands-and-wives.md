---
summary: Briseis remembers the husband her parents gave her, killed by Achilles before her city, and Patroclus' promise to make her Achilles' “wedded wife” in Phthia (19.291–299). Agamemnon swears that he never took her to his bed (19.261–263).
refs: [19.291-299]
---

The captive's lament holds both kinds of marriage the war allows her: the one it destroyed and the one it promised. κουριδίη ἄλοχος, “the wedded wife”, is elsewhere a wife married as a maiden, and a marriage of Achilles to a captive is foreign to the rest of the poem; Aristarchus seems to have rejected the lines (Walter Leaf, *Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 19.298; see the translation's note on 19.295–300).
