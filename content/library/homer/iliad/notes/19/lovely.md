---
summary: Zeus sends Athena to drip “nectar and lovely ambrosia” into the fasting Achilles' breast, and she does so.
---

“But go, drip nectar and lovely ambrosia / into his breast, so that hunger may not come upon him” (19.347–348; [[zeus]], [[athena]]), and in the verses of the deed: “and into Achilles' / breast, nectar and lovely ambrosia / she dripped, so that joyless hunger might not come upon his knees” (19.352–354; [[ambrosia]], [[nectar]]). Achilles will not eat while Patroclus lies unburied, and the gods feed him with their own food. In the morning Thetis had dripped ambrosia and “red nectar” into the dead Patroclus, to keep his flesh sound (19.38–39; [[red]]).
