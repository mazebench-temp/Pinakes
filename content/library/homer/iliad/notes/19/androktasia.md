---
summary: Achilles counts the cost of his wrath in a formula of death in battle: had Artemis killed the girl, “then not so many Achaeans would have bitten the boundless ground with their teeth / at the hands of their enemies, while I nursed my wrath apart” (19.61–62).
---

ὀδὰξ ἕλον οὖδας, “bit the ground with their teeth”, ends a man's fall; Nestor used it of the Epeians who fell beneath his spear in his youth (11.749; see [[death-in-battle]]). Achilles uses it for all the Achaean dead of his withdrawal, the men his wrath has killed (see [[menis]] and [[quarrel-of-achilles-and-agamemnon]]). He names their killer too: “those whom he brought down, / Hector, son of Priam, when Zeus gave him glory” (19.203–204), the verse with which the narrator asked whom Hector killed first and whom last in Book 11 (11.299–300; see [[whom-first-whom-last]]).
