# Unnamed: "He spoke out, boasting among all the gods" (19.100).
refs: [19.100]
---

“He spoke out, boasting among all the gods: / ‘Hear me, all you gods and all you goddesses, / so that I may say what the heart in my breast bids me” (19.100–102; see [[zeus]], [[hear-me]] and [[the-birth-of-heracles]]). The address is the one with which Zeus opened the assembly of the gods in Book 8, when he forbade them to help either side (8.5–6, with κελεύει, “commands”, for ἀνώγει, “bids”; see the note on Book 8). Before all the gods he boasts that a man of his blood will be born that day to rule all who dwell around him, and Hera, calling him “Olympian”, makes him swear to it (19.103–113; see [[hera]] and [[olympian]]). The gods are the audience of his folly: when he learns that the man born is Eurystheus, he hurls Blind Folly out of Olympus and the starry sky, so that she will never come among them again (19.125–131; see [[ate]] and [[olympus]]).
