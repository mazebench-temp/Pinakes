summary: Book 19 has the oath of words and the oath of ritual. In Agamemnon's story Zeus swears “a mighty oath” and “a great oath” with ἦ μέν, “that he truly will be lord” (19.108–113, 19.127). In the assembly Agamemnon swears “trusty oaths” over a boar: Zeus is called to witness with ἴστω, the terms are sworn with μή and the indicative, and the man who swears curses himself if he swears falsely (19.191, 19.258–265).
# καρτερὸν ὅρκον, "a mighty oath": in the Iliad only 19.108 and 19.127 (the Odyssey has it at 4.253, 10.381, 12.298, 18.55).
grc: ["καρτερὸν ὅρκον"]
en: [mighty oath]
refs: [19.109, 19.258, 19.261, 19.264-265]
---

**Mighty and great.** Hera asks Zeus for καρτερὸν ὅρκον, “a mighty oath” (19.108), and when he learns how he was deceived he swears another, that Blind Folly will never come back to Olympus (19.127–129). Between the two the narrator says that he swore μέγαν ὅρκον, “a great oath” (19.113), the phrase of Achilles' oath by the scepter (1.233) and of the oath Agamemnon promised over Briseis (9.132, 9.274; see [[oath]] and [[zeus]]). In the Iliad καρτερὸν ὅρκον is found only in this story; the Odyssey has it four times (*Odyssey* 4.253, 10.381, 12.298, 18.55).

**ἦ μέν.** The oath Hera dictates has the solemn form ἦ μέν with a future infinitive: ἦ μὲν τὸν πάντεσσι περικτιόνεσσιν ἀνάξειν, “that he truly will be lord over all who dwell around him” (19.109). It is the form of the oath Calchas asked of Achilles, “that you will readily defend me” (ἦ μέν … ἀρήξειν, 1.77), and Sleep of Hera (ἦ μὲν … δώσειν, 14.275). Each time the weaker party binds the stronger (see [[the-birth-of-heracles]]).

**Trusty oaths.** Agamemnon keeps the army “until the gifts / come from my hut and we swear trusty oaths” (ὅρκια πιστὰ τάμωμεν, 19.190–191). The phrase, literally “cut trusty oath-offerings”, belongs to the truce of Book 3, where it is joined to friendship between enemies (3.73, 3.94; see the note on Book 3 and [[philotes]]). Here it binds the king of the army to one of his own men, and the offering is a boar (19.197, 19.251–266; see [[boar]]). Later Achilles will tell Hector that there are no trusty oaths between lions and men (ὅρκια πιστά, 22.262).

**Witness, terms and curse.** The oath Agamemnon swears has the parts of the oath in Book 3, in fewer verses (19.258–265; 3.276–301):

- the witnesses, called with ἴστω, “let … be witness”: ἴστω νῦν Ζεὺς πρῶτα θεῶν ὕπατος καὶ ἄριστος, “Now let Zeus be witness first, highest and best of the gods” (19.258), and Earth, the Sun and the Erinyes after him (19.259). The verse returns in Odysseus' oath to Penelope (*Odyssey* 19.303), and ἴστω νῦν Ζεύς opened Hector's oath to Dolon (10.329; see [[erinyes]]);
- the punishers of perjury, ὅτις κʼ ἐπίορκον ὀμόσσῃ, “whoever has sworn a false oath” (19.260), the half-verse of Book 3 (3.279);
- the terms, sworn with μή and the indicative, μὴ μὲν ἐγὼ κούρῃ Βρισηΐδι χεῖρʼ ἐπένεικα, “that I never laid a hand on the girl Briseis” (19.261), as Hector swore μὴ μὲν … ἐποχήσεται, “that no other man shall ride behind those horses” (10.330; Walter Leaf, *Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 19.261, who notes that most manuscripts have the infinitive ἐπενεῖκαι, which cannot stand with ἐγώ);
- the curse, which here falls on the man who swears: εἰ δέ τι τῶνδʼ ἐπίορκον ἐμοὶ θεοὶ ἄλγεα δοῖεν, “And if any of this is falsely sworn, may the gods give me sorrows” (19.264–265). In Book 3 it was spoken by both armies over the poured wine, against whichever side should first break the oaths (3.298–301).

No one swears “by” a god in the book: ναὶ μά and οὐ μά are not used (compare 1.86, 1.234). When Achilles swears in Agamemnon's hut after the killing of Hector, it is οὐ μὰ Ζῆνʼ, “No, by Zeus”, who is called “highest and best of the gods” in the words of Agamemnon's oath (23.43; 19.258).
