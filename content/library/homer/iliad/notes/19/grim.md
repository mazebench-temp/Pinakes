---
summary: Diomedes and Odysseus come to the assembly with “grievous wounds,” and Achilles imagines Peleus waiting for “the grievous news” of his death; the book adds λυγρήν.
grc: [λυγρὴν]
---

**The wounds.** “leaning on their spears; for they still had grievous wounds” (19.49; [[attendants-of-ares]], [[diomedes]], [[odysseus]]).

**The news.** “in hateful old age, and always waiting for / the grievous news of me, when he hears that I have perished” (19.336–337; [[peleus]], [[hateful]]). The grievous news in Books 17 and 18 was the death of Patroclus, which Antilochus carried to Achilles (17.642, 17.686; 18.18–19). Achilles, mourning Patroclus, now thinks of the same news about himself reaching his father. The accusative λυγρήν is new to the entry.
