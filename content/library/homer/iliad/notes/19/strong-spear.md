---
summary: Achilles fears for the body of “the valiant son of Menoetius”; the name Alcimus, Ἄλκιμος, which has the letters of the adjective, is kept out of the entry.
---

**Patroclus.** “Now I will arm myself; but terribly / I fear that in the meantime into the valiant son of Menoetius / flies will make their way, through the wounds the bronze has struck” (19.23–25; [[son-of-menoetius]], [[patroclus]], [[struck-by-bronze]]). The formula of the living warrior names the body that Achilles fears will rot.

**Alcimus.** “And Automedon and Alcimus, busying themselves with the horses, / yoked them” (19.392–393). Ἄλκιμος here is a man's name, the companion who yokes Achilles' horses with Automedon; in Book 24 the two are the companions Achilles honors most after Patroclus (24.474, 24.573–575). Walter Leaf takes the name for the familiar short form of Ἀλκιμέδων (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 19.392; see [[alcimedon]]). The name has the letters of the adjective and is kept out of the entry, here and in Book 24. See [[automedon]].
