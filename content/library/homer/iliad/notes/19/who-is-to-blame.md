summary: Book 19 answers the question three times, and each time the answer is a god. Agamemnon blames “Zeus and Fate and the Erinys” for the blind folly that made him take Achilles' prize (19.86–90); Achilles blames Zeus, who “wanted death to come upon many of the Achaeans” (19.270–274); and Achilles' horse blames Apollo and Fate for Patroclus' death, “nor are we / to blame” (19.409–414).
refs: [19.86-90, 19.270-274, 19.409-414]
---

None of the pleas undoes the act or its price. Agamemnon pleads blind folly and pays the gifts and swears the oath (19.137–144, 19.258–265); Achilles, who once said that the heralds were not to blame “but Agamemnon” (1.335), now accepts Agamemnon's account in his own words and goes to fight (19.270–275); the horses who could not save Patroclus will carry Achilles to his death (19.408–417). Agamemnon proves his case with a story in which Zeus himself is blinded (19.95–133; see [[ate]], [[a-god-brings-all-things-to-fulfillment]] and [[the-gods-mirror-the-mortals]]).
