summary: Perseus is named only in the patronymic of Sthenelus, Περσηϊάδαο, “son of Perseus” (19.116, 19.123). Through him the newborn Eurystheus is “of your race” (19.124), of the blood of Zeus, and Zeus' oath gives the rule to his grandson.
# Περσηϊάδαο occurs only at 19.116 and 19.123 in Homer; by the patronymic rule it names both Sthenelus
# (sthenelus-son-of-perseus) and his father.
grc: [Περσηϊάδαο]
---

Hera came to Argos, where she knew “the strong wife of Sthenelus, son of Perseus” (Σθενέλου Περσηϊάδαο, 19.116), and told Zeus that a man was born to rule the Argives, “Eurystheus, child of Sthenelus the son of Perseus, / of your race” (19.123–124; see [[sthenelus-son-of-perseus]], [[eurystheus]] and [[the-birth-of-heracles]]). The patronymic, in the long form in -ηϊάδης, is used nowhere else in Homer.

The name carries the claim. Zeus had sworn that the man born that day who was “sprung from the blood of your stock” would rule all who dwell around him (19.111), and Perseus is the son whom Danae bore to Zeus (14.319–320; see [[danae]] and [[zeus]]). So the grandson of Perseus falls under the oath as Zeus' own son would have done. Walter Leaf adds that Alcmene, in the later genealogy, was a granddaughter of Perseus through Electryon, so that Heracles and Eurystheus were both of the lineage of Zeus (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 19.105; see [[alcmene]] and [[heracles]]). Apollodorus makes the line explicit in Zeus' own words: the descendant of Perseus then about to be born would reign over Mycenae (*Library* 2.4.5).
