---
summary: Xanthus tells Achilles that the horses were not to blame for Patroclus' death, “but a great god and mighty Fate.”
---

“but the day of your destruction is near; nor are we / to blame, but a great god and mighty Fate” (19.409–410; [[moira]], [[aitios]], [[xanthus-the-horse]]). The formula usually stands beside death at the end of the verse (16.853); here Μοῖρα is printed as a person, “Fate,” as in Agamemnon's apology, where he blamed “Zeus and Fate and the Erinys who walks in darkness” (19.87; [[who-walks-in-darkness]]). The horse, like the king, says “not to blame” (οὐκ αἴτιος, 19.86; αἴτιοι, 19.410) and names a god and Fate instead.
