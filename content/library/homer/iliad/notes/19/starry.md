---
summary: In Agamemnon's story Zeus swears that Ate will never come again “to Olympus and the starry sky,” and hurls her “from the starry sky” down to the works of men.
---

“angered in his heart, and swore a mighty oath / that never to Olympus and the starry sky / would Blind Folly come again, who blinds all. / So saying, he hurled her from the starry sky, / whirling her round in his hand; and soon she came to the works of men” (19.127–131; [[ate]], [[zeus]], [[olympus]], [[sky]]). The starry sky is the gods' world from which Ate is banished; the same verb ῥίπτω is used by Hephaestus, who tells how Zeus “seized me by the foot and hurled me from the divine threshold” (1.591). Agamemnon's story explains why blind folly is now found among men.
