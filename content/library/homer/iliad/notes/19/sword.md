summary: Agamemnon draws the knife that always hangs beside the great sheath of his sword (19.252–253), and Achilles slings on a silver-studded sword of bronze, in the verse of Paris' and Patroclus' armings (19.372–373).
---

ἀμφὶ δʼ ἄρʼ ὤμοισιν βάλετο ξίφος ἀργυρόηλον / χάλκεον, “And around his shoulders he slung his silver-studded sword / of bronze” (19.372–373 = 3.334–335, 16.135–136; see [[arming-scene]] and [[silver-studded]]). Hephaestus made Achilles a shield, a corselet, a helmet and greaves (18.609–613), the pieces Thetis had asked for (18.457–461); a sword is not among them, and the poem does not say where this one came from (see [[the-making-of-the-arms]]). At the oath Agamemnon's knife hangs “beside the great sheath of his sword” (19.253 = 3.272; see [[knife]] and [[sheath]]).
