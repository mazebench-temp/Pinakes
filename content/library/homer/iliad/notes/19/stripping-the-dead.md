---
summary: Xanthus says that the Trojans took the armor from the shoulders of Patroclus, and that the fault was Apollo's, not the horses' (19.411–414).
grc: ["Πατρόκλου τεύχεʼ ἕλοντο"]
---

Τρῶες ἀπʼ ὤμοιιν Πατρόκλου τεύχεʼ ἕλοντο, “that the Trojans took the armor from the shoulders of Patroclus” (19.412). The words are those of Patroclus' own account of his death, “for they themselves took the armor from my shoulders”, where the subject is the gods (16.846), and of the stripping of Cebriones and Sarpedon (16.782, 16.663; see the note on Book 16). Hector stripped the armor from the body and wears it still (17.125, 17.192–197; see [[the-armor-of-peleus]] and [[xanthus-the-horse]]).
