summary: On the morning after their wounding the chiefs come to the assembly: “the two attendants of Ares came limping, / the son of Tydeus, steadfast in battle, and brilliant Odysseus, / leaning on their spears; for they still had grievous wounds”, and Agamemnon came “last of all … with his wound” (19.47–53).
refs: [19.47-53]
---

They sit in the front of the assembly (19.50). Walter Leaf notes that only Diomedes' limp is explained by a wound in the foot, and that the heroes heal fast (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 19.47; see [[healing]]). Agamemnon speaks from his seat; the ancient view that he would not stand because of his wound is weakened, Leaf observes, by the place of the wound, which was in the arm (on 19.76; see [[listen-to-the-man-who-stands]]). Odysseus, wounded in the side, leads the young men who carry the gifts and weighs out the gold (19.238–248; see [[kouretes]]).
