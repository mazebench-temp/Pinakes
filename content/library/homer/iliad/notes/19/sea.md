---
summary: Achilles calls the army along the shore of the sea (19.40); the boar of Agamemnon's oath is flung into the great gulf of the gray sea, food for the fishes (19.267–268); and in the simile of the shield, sailors out at sea see a fire on the mountains while storm winds carry them far from their friends (19.375–378).
---

**The shore.** “But brilliant Achilles went along the shore of the sea, / shouting terribly, and he roused the Achaean heroes” (19.40–41), in the words of Chryses' silent walk in Book 1 (1.34; see [[shore]]).

**The oath victim.** When Agamemnon has sworn that he never touched Briseis and has cut the boar's throat, “Talthybius flung it into the great gulf of the gray sea, / whirling it around, food for the fishes” (τὸν μὲν Ταλθύβιος πολιῆς ἁλὸς ἐς μέγα λαῖτμα / ῥῖψʼ ἐπιδινήσας βόσιν ἰχθύσιν, 19.267–268; see [[boar]], [[fish]], [[talthybius]] and [[gray-sea]]). λαῖτμα, “gulf”, is elsewhere found only in the Odyssey (see the translation's note on 19.267). Walter Leaf takes the victim of an oath as neither burnt nor eaten but given over to the gods below, and compares the lambs of the truce in Book 3, which Priam takes back to Troy in his chariot (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 19.267; 3.310; see [[oath]]).

**The sailors.** In the simile of the shield the sea is the open sea, πόντος: sailors “out at sea” see the blaze of a fire burning high in the mountains, “and storm winds bear them, unwilling, / over the fish-filled sea, far from their friends” (19.375–378; see [[the-fire-seen-by-sailors]] and [[fish-filled]]).
