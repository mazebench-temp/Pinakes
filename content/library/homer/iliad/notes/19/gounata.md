summary: Hunger works on the knees. Odysseus says that a man who fights unfed is overtaken by thirst and hunger, “and his knees are hindered as he goes” (19.166), and Athena drips nectar and ambrosia into Achilles “so that joyless hunger might not come upon his knees” (19.354). Achilles foresees the Trojans who escape him “glad to bend his knees and rest” (19.72).
grc: ["γόνυ κάμψειν"]
---

The knees are the seat of a fighter's strength (see [[fasting]]). γόνυ κάμψειν, “bend his knees”, and the verse after it are Agamemnon's words of Hector in Book 7, when he holds Menelaus back from the duel: fearless as Hector is, he will be glad to rest if he escapes (19.72–73 ≈ 7.117–119; see [[single-combat]]).
