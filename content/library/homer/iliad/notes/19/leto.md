---
summary: Achilles' horse Xanthus names Apollo by his mother: it was “the best of the gods, whom Leto of the lovely hair bore”, who killed Patroclus and gave glory to Hector.
---

“But the best of the gods, whom Leto of the lovely hair bore, / killed him among the champions, and gave glory to Hector” (19.413–414; see [[apollo]], [[xanthus-the-horse]] and [[lovely-haired]]). ὃν ἠΰκομος τέκε Λητώ, “whom Leto of the lovely hair bore”, is the half-verse with which the narrator names Apollo as Chryses goes apart to pray to him in Book 1 (1.35–36), and the Odyssey uses it when Apollo kills Otus and Ephialtes (*Odyssey* 11.318). The dying Patroclus also named his killer by his mother, “the son of Leto” (16.849; see the note on Book 16).
