---
summary: Agamemnon was wounded “in the fierce battle”; in his story Hera makes Zeus swear “a mighty oath,” and Zeus swears another when he flings Ate from Olympus.
---

**The wound.** “And last of all came Agamemnon, lord of men, / with his wound; for him too in the fierce battle / Coon, son of Antenor, had stabbed with his bronze-fitted spear” (19.51–53; [[bronze-fitted]], [[coon]]).

**The two oaths of Zeus.** The by-form καρτερός names both oaths of Agamemnon's story. Hera: “But come now, Olympian, swear me a mighty oath / that he truly will be lord over all who dwell around him” (19.108–109; [[olympian]], [[hera]]). Zeus swears it, “a great oath” (19.113), and is blinded. When he learns the truth he swears again, “angered in his heart, and swore a mighty oath / that never to Olympus and the starry sky / would Blind Folly come again” (19.127–129; [[ate]], [[starry]], [[oath-formulas]]). The first oath binds him to his mistake, the second banishes the power that caused it.
