---
summary: Agamemnon, speaking from his seat, says that in a great din no one can be heard: “He is hindered, clear-voiced orator though he is.”
---

“But in the great din of men how could anyone listen / or speak? He is hindered, clear-voiced orator though he is” (19.81–82; [[assembly]], [[agamemnon]]). The half-verse is the one in which Odysseus granted Thersites his gift before beating him (2.246). Here it is a general truth, with which Agamemnon asks the Argives for a hearing before he turns to Achilles (19.79–84; see the translation's notes on 19.76–84 and 19.82). Walter Leaf glosses λιγύς here as “loud-voiced” (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 19.82). The praise that belonged to Nestor, “the clear-voiced orator of the Pylians” (1.248), is now any good speaker's, and it is not enough.
