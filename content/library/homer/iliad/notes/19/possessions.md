---
summary: Achilles had hoped that Patroclus would take his son home and show him "my wealth and my slaves and my great high-roofed house" (19.333).
---

κτῆσις is "wealth" in the translation, κτήματα "possessions" (see the translation's row for κτῆσις / κτήματα). Walter Leaf thinks the next lines, in which Achilles supposes that Peleus may already be dead (19.334–337), sit ill with 19.322 and were perhaps meant to explain how he could call the wealth his own while his father lives (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 19.334).
