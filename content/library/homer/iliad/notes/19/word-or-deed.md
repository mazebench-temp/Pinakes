summary: When Agamemnon agrees to Odysseus' counsel, the gifts follow at once: “No sooner was the word spoken than the work was done” (αὐτίκʼ ἔπειθʼ ἅμα μῦθος ἔην, τετέλεστο δὲ ἔργον, 19.242).
grc: ["ἅμα μῦθος ἔην"]
en: [No sooner was the word spoken than the work was done]
---

Walter Leaf compares later phrases of word and deed done together (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 19.242). In the *Homeric Hymn to Hermes* the newborn god makes a lyre from a tortoise as quick as thought: “so at once did glorious Hermes devise word and deed together” (ὣς ἅμʼ ἔπος τε καὶ ἔργον ἐμήδετο κύδιμος Ἑρμῆς, 46). In Herodotus, Darius says what he will do “and did the word and the deed at once” (ἅμα ἔπος τε καὶ ἔργον ἐποίεε, 3.135.1). See [[mythos]].
