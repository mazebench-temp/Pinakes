summary: Briseis, coming back to Achilles' huts, is “like golden Aphrodite” as she sees Patroclus dead; the book adds the dative χρυσέῃ Ἀφροδίτῃ.
grc: ["χρυσέῃ Ἀφροδίτῃ"]

“And then Briseis, like golden Aphrodite, / when she saw Patroclus torn by the sharp bronze, / threw herself around him and wailed shrilly” (19.282–284; [[briseis]], [[aphrodite]], [[sharp]]). The comparison is the standard of a woman's beauty, and it introduces her lament (19.287–300); a few verses later the narrator praises her again as “the woman, like the goddesses” (19.286; [[like-the-goddesses]]). Cassandra is “like golden Aphrodite” when she sees her father bringing Hector home, in the same verse-end with the contracted χρυσῇ (24.699). The dative χρυσέῃ Ἀφροδίτῃ is new to the entry.
