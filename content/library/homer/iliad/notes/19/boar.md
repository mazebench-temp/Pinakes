summary: The victim of Agamemnon's oath to Achilles is a boar. Talthybius makes it ready and holds it while Agamemnon cuts the first hairs from it and swears that he never touched Briseis; then Agamemnon cuts its throat, and Talthybius flings it into the sea, food for the fishes (19.196–197, 19.250–268).
# κάπρου also catches 17.21 (συὸς κάπρου), a boar already claimed by συὸς.
grc: [κάπρον, κάπρου]
---

**The oath victim.** Agamemnon orders it: “And let Talthybius quickly, through the wide army of the Achaeans, / make ready a boar for me, to sacrifice to Zeus and the Sun” (19.196–197; see [[talthybius]] and [[helios]]). At the oath the herald stands beside him “holding the boar in his hands” (19.251), and Agamemnon, “cutting the first hairs from the boar” (κάπρου ἀπὸ τρίχας ἀρξάμενος), lifts his hands to Zeus and swears (19.254–265; see [[oath]] and [[prayer]]). “He spoke, and cut the boar's throat with the pitiless bronze” (19.266; see [[knife]]). The truce of Book 3 was sworn over lambs, a white ram and a black ewe for Earth and the Sun and a third for Zeus, in some of the same verses (3.103–104, 3.271–273, 3.292); here a single boar is made ready for Zeus and the Sun.

**Into the sea.** “And Talthybius flung it into the great gulf of the gray sea, / whirling it around, food for the fishes” (19.267–268; see [[sea]] and [[fish]]). The victim of an oath is not eaten: Walter Leaf takes it as neither burnt nor eaten but given over to the gods below (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 19.267).

**At Olympia.** Leaf notes that the competitors at the Olympic games swore on a boar to Zeus Horkios, Zeus of Oaths (on 19.197). Pausanias describes the statue of Zeus Horkios in the council house at Olympia, with a thunderbolt in each hand, before which the athletes, their fathers and brothers and their trainers swear on slices of a boar's flesh that they will do no wrong against the games. He forgot to ask what was done with the boar afterwards, but says that among the ancients a victim on which an oath had been sworn was no longer to be eaten by men, and he quotes Homer as proof, the boar on which Agamemnon swore that Briseis had not been in his bed, which the herald threw into the sea (Pausanias 5.24.9–11, quoting 19.266–268). In his quotation the first verse reads ἀπὸ σφάραγον, “the throat”, where the Iliad has ἀπὸ στόμαχον.
