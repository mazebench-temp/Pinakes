summary: Patroclus is “dearest of my companions” (19.315) and “his dear companion” (19.345). The dear sons of the book are Thetis' (19.4), Zeus' Heracles (19.132), the dear son that Sthenelus' wife was carrying (19.117), and Achilles' Neoptolemus, “my dear son, who is being reared for me in Scyros” (19.326); Achilles' “dear father” is Peleus, to whom Chiron gave the ash spear (19.390, 19.422).
---

In some phrases φίλος is simply “one's own”: φίλον κατὰ λαιμόν, “down my throat” (19.209), and φίλον ἦτορ, “my dear heart” (19.307). Agamemnon opens his speech with the address “Friends, Danaan heroes” (19.78), and Achilles appeals to the elders as “my dear companions” (19.305; see [[hetairos]]). In the simile of the shield the sailors are carried by storms “far from their friends” (19.378). See [[fathers-and-sons]].
