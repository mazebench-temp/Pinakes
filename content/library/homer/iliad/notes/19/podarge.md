summary: Achilles calls his horses “far-famed children of Podarge” as he drives out to battle (19.400), the second and last time the Iliad names their mother.
# The genitive Ποδάργης occurs only here; the entry claims the nominative (16.150).
grc: [Ποδάργης]
---

“Xanthus and Balius, far-famed children of Podarge” (19.400): Achilles names the horses by their mother, as he bids them bring him back safe (see [[the-horses-of-achilles]], [[xanthus-the-horse]] and [[achilles]]). τηλεκλυτός, “far-famed”, occurs in the Iliad only here (see the translation's row on τηλεκλυτός, which sets it beside τηλεκλειτός, 5.491, 6.111, 14.321). Book 16 gave their descent, “whom the Harpy Podarge bore to the West Wind / as she grazed in a meadow beside the stream of Oceanus” (16.150–151; see [[harpies]] and [[zephyrus]]).

The verse bears on a reading of that line. Walter Leaf reports that Zenodotus read πόδαργος at 16.150 as an adjective (“swift-footed”), taking Ἅρπυια, “the Harpy”, for the proper name, and answers him by pointing to this verse, where the horses are the children of Podarge (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 16.150).
