---
summary: The Sun is a witness of the reconciliation. Agamemnon has a boar made ready for Zeus and the Sun and calls on him with Zeus, Earth and the Erinyes. The setting sun marks the limit of the fast that Odysseus argues against and Achilles keeps, and Achilles, armed, gleams “like shining Hyperion”, the Sun.
---

**A witness.** Agamemnon bids Talthybius “make ready a boar for me, to sacrifice to Zeus and the Sun” (19.197; see [[talthybius]], [[boar]] and [[zeus]]). The Sun was a party to the truce of Book 3, with a lamb of his own (3.103–104), and Agamemnon called on him there as “you, Sun, who see all things and hear all things” (3.277; see [[who-sees-all-things]]). As seeing all things, Walter Leaf observes, he was qualified to watch the keeping of a promise (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 19.197). Over the boar Agamemnon swears: “Now let Zeus be witness first, highest and best of the gods, / and Earth and the Sun and the Erinyes, who beneath the earth / punish men, whoever has sworn a false oath” (19.258–260; see [[oath]], [[earth]] and [[erinyes]]).

**Until the sun goes down.** The sunset bounds the fast. Odysseus warns that “no man all day long, until the sun goes down, / will be able to fight face to face, going without food” (19.162–163; see [[odysseus]] and [[fasting]]); πρόπαν ἦμαρ ἐς ἠέλιον καταδύντα are the words that ended the feast of the gods in Book 1, “So all day long, until the sun went down” (1.601). Achilles would send the army out “fasting and unfed, and as the sun goes down / make ready a great supper, when we have avenged the insult” (19.207–208), and he tells the elders who beg him to eat: “until the sun goes down I will wait and endure all the same” (19.308; see [[sunset-lines]] and [[achilles]]).

**Like shining Hyperion.** When Achilles has armed and mounted behind Automedon, he stands “gleaming in his armor like shining Hyperion” (19.398; see [[hyperion]] and [[like-the-sun]]). The half-verse τεύχεσι παμφαίνων ὥς τʼ ἠλέκτωρ was said of Paris, running down from Pergamus “gleaming in his armor like the shining sun” (6.513; see the note on Book 6), and Leaf refers to his notes there and on 8.480 (on 19.398). ἠλέκτωρ and Ὑπερίων are both names of the Sun, and only here are they joined. In the arming the shield gives off a blaze “as of the moon” (19.374), the helmet shines “like a star” (19.381), and the man himself like the sun (see [[moon]] and [[like-a-star]]).
