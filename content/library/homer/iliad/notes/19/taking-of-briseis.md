summary: The taking of Briseis is ended in Book 19. Agamemnon dates his blind folly from the day he took Achilles' prize and lays the blame on Zeus, Fate and the Erinys, and Achilles too says that Zeus willed it (19.85–90, 19.270–274). Briseis is given back with an oath that Agamemnon never touched her, and comes back to the hut from which Patroclus had led her out, to find him dead (19.243–246, 19.258–265, 19.282–290).

**Blamed on the gods.** Agamemnon owns the deed and denies the blame:

{{quote:36d7f3f6-4e1d-575b-905a-bed57d0c47e8}}

αὐτὸς ἀπηύρων, “I myself took away” (19.89). In Book 1 he threatened to take her himself, “coming with more men” (1.324–325), and sent his heralds instead (1.320–326; see [[talthybius]] and [[heralds]]); Achilles complained that “he has taken my prize and keeps it, having seized it himself” (1.356; see [[geras]]). In Book 9 Agamemnon said only “I was blinded; I do not deny it myself” (9.116); now the blinding is the work of the gods, cast into his mind “in the assembly”, the quarrel of Book 1 (19.87–88; see [[ate]], [[aitios]] and [[erinyes]]). Achilles gives the same cause from his side: the son of Atreus would never have stirred his heart, “nor would he have led the girl / away against my will—there was no dealing with him; but Zeus, it seems, / wanted death to come upon many of the Achaeans” (19.272–274; see [[zeus]]). ἀέκοντος, “against my will”, is the word of Book 1, where Thetis left him angry over the woman “whom they had taken by force against his will” (1.430). Each man now tells the taking as the will of Zeus (see [[who-is-to-blame]], [[quarrel-of-achilles-and-agamemnon]] and [[the-renunciation-of-wrath]]).

**Undone.** The gifts promised in Book 9 are brought out of Agamemnon's hut, and with them Briseis, “seven, and the eighth was fair-cheeked Briseis” (19.246; see [[the-gifts-of-agamemnon]]); Agamemnon swears “that I never laid a hand on the girl Briseis” (19.261; see [[oath]]). κούρῃ Βρισηΐδι, “the girl Briseis”, is how Achilles named her to the heralds who came for her, “for the girl Briseis” (1.336). She comes back to the hut she left: “I left you alive when I went from the hut, / and now I find you dead, leader of the people, / as I come back” (19.288–290). In Book 1 it was Patroclus who led her out of the hut and gave her to the heralds (1.345–348; see [[briseis]] and [[patroclus]]).
