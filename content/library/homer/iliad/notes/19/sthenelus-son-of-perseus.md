summary: Sthenelus is named twice in Agamemnon's story, both times as “son of Perseus”. Hera comes to Argos, where his strong wife is in her seventh month, and brings their son Eurystheus out into the light before his time, so that the son of Sthenelus, “of your race”, falls under Zeus' oath (19.114–124).
# Σθενέλου (19.116) and Σθενέλοιο (19.123) occur only here in Homer; Περσηϊάδαο is the patronymic, which perseus also
# claims. sthenelus (the son of Capaneus) excepts both lines.

Hera “swiftly came to Achaean Argos, where she knew / the strong wife of Sthenelus, son of Perseus. / She was carrying her dear son, and the seventh month had come” (19.115–117; see [[argos]], [[hera]] and [[the-birth-of-heracles]]). The wife is not named. ἰφθίμη ἄλοχος, “the strong wife”, is said elsewhere in the Iliad only of Aegialeia, the wife of Diomedes (5.415), and in the Odyssey of Arete, when Odysseus speaks to Alcinous (*Odyssey* 12.452). Walter Leaf calls ἄλοχον an anticipated subject: the woman is the object of ᾔδη, “she knew”, as though a clause “that she was carrying” were to follow, and the next verse makes her the subject, ἣ δʼ ἐκύει (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 19.116).

In Hera's news to Zeus the father's name and the grandfather's make the claim: the newborn is “Eurystheus, child of Sthenelus the son of Perseus, / of your race” (19.123–124; see [[eurystheus]]). Zeus had sworn that the man born that day who was “sprung from the blood of your stock” would rule all who dwell around him (19.109–111; see [[oath]] and [[zeus]]), and the son of Sthenelus is of that blood through Perseus, whom Danae bore to Zeus (14.319–320; see [[perseus]]). Leaf warns that this Sthenelus is not to be confused with the son of Capaneus (on 19.116; see [[sthenelus]]).
