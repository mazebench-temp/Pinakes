---
summary: The Achaeans rejoice that “the great-hearted son of Peleus” has renounced his wrath; Agamemnon turns to “the son of Peleus” to open his mind; and Odysseus addresses him, “Achilles, son of Peleus, by far the strongest of the Achaeans.”
---

**The renunciation.** “that the great-hearted son of Peleus had renounced his wrath” (19.75; [[great-hearted]], [[menis]]).

**Agamemnon.** “To the son of Peleus I will open my mind; but you others, / Argives, take heed, and each of you mark my word well” (19.83–84; [[agamemnon]]). He speaks to Achilles, and the army is to hear (see the translation's note on 19.76–84).

**Odysseus.** “Achilles, son of Peleus, by far the strongest of the Achaeans, / you are stronger than I, and better by no small amount / with the spear, but I would surpass you in understanding” (19.216–218; [[odysseus]], [[of-many-wiles]]). The first verse is the one with which Patroclus began his plea in Book 16 (16.21), and Odysseus will greet Achilles' shade with it in the Odyssey (*Odyssey* 11.478).
