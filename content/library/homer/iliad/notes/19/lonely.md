---
summary: In the simile of Achilles' shield the sailors see the fire of “a lonely farmstead” high in the mountains.
---

“And as when to sailors out at sea there appears the blaze / of a burning fire, which burns high up in the mountains / in a lonely farmstead; and storm winds bear them, unwilling, / over the fish-filled sea, far from their friends” (19.375–378; [[fish-filled]], [[the-shield-of-achilles]]). In two earlier similes the phrase χώρῳ ἐν οἰοπόλῳ was a wild place, where a boar awaits the hunters and an olive sapling grows (13.473, 17.54); here the lonely place is a place of men, a steading high in the mountains. Walter Leaf reports that the scholia explained the word as a place “where sheep range,” a sense that happens to suit this verse but not the others, and takes σταθμός here for a sheep-station (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 19.377).
