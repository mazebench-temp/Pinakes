---
summary: Achilles renounces his wrath by asking whether it was better for both kings to rage “in heart-devouring strife for the sake of a girl”; the book adds the dative θυμοβόρῳ.
grc: [θυμοβόρῳ]
---

“Son of Atreus, was this then better for us both, / for you and for me, that we two, grieving at heart, / raged in heart-devouring strife for the sake of a girl?” (19.56–58; [[eris]], [[son-of-atreus]], [[quarrel-of-achilles-and-agamemnon]]). In Books 7 and 16 the epithet described the strife of duels, Ajax's with Hector and Patroclus' with Sarpedon (7.210, 7.301, 16.476); here Achilles gives it to the strife of the quarrel, which he goes on to call “my strife and yours” (19.64). The dative θυμοβόρῳ is new to the entry.
