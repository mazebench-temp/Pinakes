summary: Talthybius serves the oath. Agamemnon sends him to make ready a boar (19.196–197); he stands beside the king holding it, “like a god in his voice” (19.250–251), and when the throat is cut he flings it into the sea (19.267–268). No herald summons this assembly: Achilles calls it himself, shouting along the shore (19.40–41).
refs: [19.196-197, 19.250-251, 19.267-268]
---

As in Book 3, where the heralds of both armies fetched the lambs, mixed the wine and gave out the hair (3.116–120, 3.268–274), the herald here is the servant of the rite, not the bearer of a message (see [[talthybius]] and [[oath]]). θεῷ ἐναλίγκιος αὐδήν, “like a god in his voice”, is a herald's praise only here; in the Odyssey the singers Phemius and Demodocus are “like the gods in voice” (θεοῖς ἐναλίγκιος αὐδήν, *Odyssey* 1.371, 9.4).
