---
summary: The Danaans are named twice: in Agamemnon's address to the assembly, “Friends, Danaan heroes, attendants of Ares” (19.78), and in Achilles' charge to his horses to bring their charioteer back “to the throng of the Danaans” (19.402).
---

**Danaan heroes.** ὦ φίλοι ἥρωες Δαναοὶ θεράποντες Ἄρηος, “Friends, Danaan heroes, attendants of Ares” (19.78; see [[attendants-of-ares]] and [[heros]]), is the verse with which Agamemnon opened his speech to the army when he tested it in Book 2 (2.110; see [[the-test-of-the-army]]). Nestor and Ajax use it to rally the army in battle (6.67, 15.733). Agamemnon speaks it from his seat, to an assembly that has just rejoiced at Achilles' words (19.74–82; see [[agamemnon]]).

**The throng.** Achilles bids his horses “take thought in another way to bring your charioteer back / safe to the throng of the Danaans, when we have had our fill of war” (19.401–402; see [[the-horses-of-achilles]] and [[achilles]]). The charioteer is himself, the fighter in the chariot, as Patroclus had been (see the translation's note on 19.401).
