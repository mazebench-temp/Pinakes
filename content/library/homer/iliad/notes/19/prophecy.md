---
summary: Achilles' horse Xanthus, given a voice by Hera, foretells his master's death: “but the day of your destruction is near”, and “it is fated to be brought down by force by a god and a man” (19.409, 19.417), until the Erinyes check his voice (19.418). Achilles answers that he knows it (19.420–423).
refs: [19.407-418, 19.420-423]
passages:
  - 19.404-424 | Xanthus speaks
---

Prophecy comes here from a creature that has no business to speak. Hera gives the horse a human voice (αὐδήεντα, 19.407), and the Erinyes take it away (19.418; see [[xanthus-the-horse]] and [[erinyes]]). Aristarchus rejected 19.407, since the god who gave the voice should have taken it away; Walter Leaf answers that the objection tells as much against 19.418, and observes that the horse tells Achilles nothing his mother has not told him, except the detail of 19.417, “a god and a man” (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 19.407; see the translation's note on 19.407). The god and the man are named by the dying Hector, Paris and Phoebus Apollo at the Scaean gates (22.359–360). Achilles has heard such words before, from Thetis, that his death is ready straight after Hector's (18.95–96), and he answers as a man who needs no telling (see [[divination]], [[death-of-achilles-foretold]] and [[the-short-life-of-achilles]]).
