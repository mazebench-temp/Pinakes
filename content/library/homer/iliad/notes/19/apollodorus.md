---
summary: The Library tells the birth of Heracles and Eurystheus as Agamemnon does, with Hera's jealousy as the motive and the Eileithyiai persuaded to delay Alcmene's labor, and names the mother of Neoptolemus, the son whom Achilles remembers in his lament: Deidamia, daughter of Lycomedes of Scyros.
---

- **The birth of Eurystheus.** When Heracles was about to be born, Zeus declared among the gods that the descendant of Perseus then about to be born would reign over Mycenae; Hera, out of jealousy, persuaded the Eileithyiai to delay Alcmene's labor, and arranged for Eurystheus, the son of Sthenelus, to be born a seven-month child (*Library* 2.4.5). Apollodorus names Sthenelus' wife Nicippe, daughter of Pelops; Homer calls her only “the strong wife of Sthenelus, son of Perseus” (19.116), and Walter Leaf notes that the mythographers give a choice of several names for her (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 19.116): [[the-birth-of-heracles]], [[eurystheus]] and [[sthenelus-son-of-perseus]].
- **Neoptolemus.** Thetis, knowing that Achilles would die if he went to the war, dressed him as a girl and entrusted him to Lycomedes; at his court Achilles fathered Pyrrhus, later called Neoptolemus, on Lycomedes' daughter Deidamia (*Library* 3.13.8). Achilles names his son once in the Iliad, “who is being reared for me in Scyros” (19.326), and never his mother: [[euripides-scyrians]] and [[neoptolemus]].
