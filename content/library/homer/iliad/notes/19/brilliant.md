---
summary: “Brilliant” is Achilles as he goes along the shore to call the assembly, as he arms and as he tries the new armor; Odysseus as he comes limping, in Agamemnon's account of his promise, and as he stays with Achilles; and Thetis among goddesses.
---

**Achilles.** “But brilliant Achilles went along the shore of the sea, / shouting terribly, and he roused the Achaean heroes” (19.40–41; [[assembly]]). At the end of the book the epithet marks the arming: “and in their midst brilliant Achilles armed himself” (19.364), and “And brilliant Achilles tried himself in his armor, / whether it fitted him and his splendid limbs moved freely in it” (19.384–385; [[arming-scene]], [[splendid]]).

**Odysseus.** He comes limping to the assembly beside Diomedes, “the son of Tydeus, steadfast in battle, and brilliant Odysseus” (19.48; [[attendants-of-ares]]); Agamemnon offers the gifts that “yesterday in your huts, brilliant Odysseus promised” (19.141; [[the-embassy-to-achilles]]); and when Achilles dismisses the kings, “the two sons of Atreus stayed, and brilliant Odysseus” (19.310; [[odysseus]]).

**Thetis.** At 19.6 the word is part of the formula [[brilliant-among-goddesses]].
