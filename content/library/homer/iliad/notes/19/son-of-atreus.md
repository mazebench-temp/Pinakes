---
summary: “Son of Atreus” is Achilles' first word in the assembly and his twice-spoken address of courtesy; Odysseus uses it to counsel Agamemnon; Agamemnon draws the knife for the oath as “the son of Atreus”; Achilles blames “the son of Atreus” for his anger; and the two sons of Atreus stay with him at the end.
---

**Achilles' words.** The patronymic opens the speech that renounces the wrath: “Son of Atreus, was this then better for us both, / for you and for me, that we two, grieving at heart, / raged in heart-devouring strife for the sake of a girl?” (19.56–58; [[heart-devouring]], [[quarrel-of-achilles-and-agamemnon]]). Twice more Achilles uses it in the full verse “Most glorious son of Atreus, Agamemnon, lord of men” (19.146, 19.199; [[most-glorious]]). After the oath he names Agamemnon by it as the man who stirred his heart: “never would the heart in my breast have been stirred / through and through by the son of Atreus” (19.271–272).

**Odysseus' counsel.** “And you, son of Atreus, will be more just hereafter / toward another too” (19.181–182; [[odysseus]]).

**The gifts and the oath.** The young men go “to the hut of Agamemnon, son of Atreus” for the gifts (19.241; [[the-gifts-of-agamemnon]]), and at the oath “the son of Atreus drew with his hand the knife” (19.252), the verse of the oath sacrifice in Book 3 (3.271; [[oath]], [[knife]]).

**The two brothers.** In the dual: “but the two sons of Atreus stayed, and brilliant Odysseus” (19.310; [[atreidae]], [[menelaus]]).
