---
summary: Xanthus tells Achilles that it was not through the horses' slowness that the Trojans took the armor from the shoulders of Patroclus, but because Apollo killed him and gave Hector glory (19.411–414).
refs: [19.411-412]
---

οὐδὲ γὰρ ἡμετέρῃ βραδυτῆτί τε νωχελίῃ τε / Τρῶες ἀπʼ ὤμοιιν Πατρόκλου τεύχεʼ ἕλοντο: “For it was not through our slowness or sloth / that the Trojans took the armor from the shoulders of Patroclus” (19.411–412). The horse answers Achilles' reproach that they left Patroclus dead on the field (19.403): it was “the best of the gods, whom Leto of the lovely hair bore”, who killed him (19.413–414; see [[xanthus-the-horse]], [[apollo]] and [[stripping-the-dead]]). The armor that Achilles wears now is the armor Hephaestus made in the night (see [[the-making-of-the-arms]]); the old armor, the gods' gift to Peleus, is still with Hector, who will wear it when Achilles kills him (22.322–323).
