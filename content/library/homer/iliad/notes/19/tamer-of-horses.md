summary: The “horse-taming Trojans” stand at the head of two verses of war: Odysseus' “let us rouse sharp Ares against the horse-taming Trojans,” and Achilles' memory of the meals Patroclus served when the Achaeans hurried “to bring tearful Ares against the horse-taming Trojans.”
---

“let us rouse sharp Ares against the horse-taming Trojans” (19.237; [[sharp]], [[odysseus]]), and “whenever the Achaeans were in haste / to bring tearful Ares against the horse-taming Trojans” (19.317–318; [[tearful]], [[laros]]). Both verses begin with the half-line Τρωσὶν ἐφʼ ἱπποδάμοισι(ν) and end with Ares. The first is Odysseus' own verse from Book 4 (4.352); the second is the verse of Hector's wish in Book 8 that the Achaeans might shrink “from bringing tearful Ares against the horse-taming Trojans” (8.516).
