---
summary: Twelve horses are among the gifts that Agamemnon gives Achilles, and Achilles' attendants drive them to his herd (19.244, 19.281). Automedon and Alcimus yoke Achilles' own horses; Achilles calls on them to bring their charioteer back safe, Xanthus answers with the voice that Hera gives him, and Achilles drives his single-hoofed horses among the foremost (19.392–424).
---

**The horses of the gifts.** “and twenty gleaming cauldrons, and twelve horses” (δώδεκα δʼ ἵππους, 19.244), in the verse of the promise of Book 9, where the next verse made them racehorses, “strong prize-winners, which have won prizes with their feet” (9.123–124); here they are only counted (see [[the-gifts-of-agamemnon]]). When the assembly breaks up, the Myrmidons carry the gifts to Achilles' huts, “and the noble attendants drove the horses to the herd” (ἵππους δʼ εἰς ἀγέλην ἔλασαν θεράποντες ἀγαυοί, 19.281; see [[myrmidons]]).

**Achilles' horses.** Automedon and Alcimus yoke them, with breast straps and bits (19.392–395; see [[chariot]] and [[automedon]]), and Achilles “called terribly to the horses of his father” (19.399): they are the immortal horses that the gods gave to Peleus (16.380–381; see [[the-horses-of-achilles]] and [[peleus]]). He calls them by name, “Xanthus and Balius, far-famed children of Podarge”, and tells them not to leave their charioteer dead on the field as they left Patroclus (19.400–403; see [[podarge]]). Xanthus answers, given a voice by Hera: the fault was not theirs, “But we two could run even as fast as the breath of the West Wind, / which they say is the swiftest of all” (19.404–417; see [[xanthus-the-horse]] and [[zephyrus]]). Walter Leaf observes that the poet here seems not to know that the West Wind is Xanthus' own father (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 19.416; 16.149–151). The Erinyes stop the horse's voice, and Achilles, answering that he knows his fate, drives “his single-hoofed horses among the foremost” (19.418–424; see [[erinyes]] and [[single-hoofed]]).
