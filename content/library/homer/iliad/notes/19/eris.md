---
summary: Achilles begins the assembly by asking whether the strife was worth it: “was this then better for us both, / for you and for me, that we two, grieving at heart, / raged in heart-devouring strife for the sake of a girl?” (19.56–58), and he says that the Achaeans “will long remember my strife and yours” (19.64).
---

The strife of the proem, which divided the two men (1.6), is renounced in the verses of Achilles' wish in Book 18, that strife might perish from among gods and men (19.65–66 = 18.112–113; see [[cholos]] and [[menis]]). θυμοβόρος, “heart-devouring”, is said of strife as it was of the strife of Hector and Ajax (7.210, 7.301; see [[heart-devouring]]).
