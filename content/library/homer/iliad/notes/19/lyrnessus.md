summary: Achilles names Lyrnessus once, wishing that Artemis had killed Briseis “on the day when I took her, when I destroyed Lyrnessus” (19.60). Briseis remembers its fall without naming it: her husband killed “before the city”, and “the city of divine Mynes” sacked (19.292, 19.296).
# Unnamed: the city before which Briseis' husband fell (19.292) and "the city of divine Mynes" (19.296).
refs: [19.292, 19.296]
---

The day Achilles took the girl was the day he took the town: the Catalogue said that he took her “out of Lyrnessus after much toil, / when he sacked Lyrnessus and the walls of Thebe” (2.690–691), and Walter Leaf, on 19.60, refers to those lines for Lyrnessus as her home (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 19.60). Achilles' word for the sack is ὀλέσσας, “destroyed” (19.60); Briseis' is πέρσεν, “sacked” (19.296; see [[raiding]]). Her husband fell πρὸ πτόλιος, “before the city” (19.292), outside its walls, as the husband in the Odyssey's simile falls before his city, defending it and his children (*Odyssey* 8.523–525).
