summary: Agamemnon's oath is told in the order of a sacrifice and in some of its verses, more briefly than the oath of Book 3: the knife drawn, the first hairs cut, hands lifted in prayer, the throat cut “with the pitiless bronze”; then, instead of the god's portion and the feast, the victim is flung into the sea (19.250–268).
refs: [19.250-257, 19.266-268]
passages:
  - 19.249-268 | The oath over the boar
---

Two verses are those of Book 3 (19.252–253 = 3.271–272), and the killing is the same half-line with one boar for the lambs (19.266; 3.292). The washing of hands, the mixing bowl and the poured wine of Book 3 are left out (3.268–270, 3.295–296), and the first hairs are not given to the leaders (3.274). What remains is the frame of a sacrifice with nothing given to the gods but the oath itself (see [[oath]] and [[sacrifice]]).
