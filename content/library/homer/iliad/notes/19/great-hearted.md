---
summary: The Achaeans rejoice “that the great-hearted son of Peleus had renounced his wrath,” and “the great-hearted Myrmidons” carry Agamemnon's gifts to Achilles' ship.
---

**The renunciation.** “So he spoke, and the well-greaved Achaeans rejoiced / that the great-hearted son of Peleus had renounced his wrath” (19.74–75; [[son-of-peleus]], [[well-greaved]], [[menis]]). The verse-end μεγαθύμου Πηλεΐωνος was last used of the fire that blazed above his head at the ditch (18.226), and before that of the armor Hector put on (17.214).

**The Myrmidons.** “and the great-hearted Myrmidons busied themselves with the gifts, / and went carrying them to the ship of divine Achilles” (19.278–279; [[myrmidons]], [[divine]], [[the-gifts-of-agamemnon]]). The synonym μεγαλήτωρ is given to the people who receive the amends on their lord's behalf.
