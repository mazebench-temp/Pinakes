---
summary: Thetis comes to the ships at dawn with the gifts of Hephaestus, stands among the mourners, takes her son's hand and speaks to him (19.1–11), and she drips ambrosia and nectar into Patroclus' nostrils (19.38–39); her going is not told. Athena comes down “like a long-winged, shrill-voiced bird of prey” to feed Achilles, and returns to her father's house (19.349–356).
refs: [19.3-8, 19.349-356]
---

Thetis' coming is seen: the Myrmidons cannot look at the armor she lays down (19.14–15). Athena's is told only by the narrator (see [[thetis]], [[athena]] and [[divine-intervention]]).
