---
summary: Thetis drips “ambrosia and red nectar” through the nostrils of the dead Patroclus, to keep his flesh sound.
---

“So she spoke, and put in him a might full of daring, / and into Patroclus in turn ambrosia and red nectar / she dripped through his nostrils, so that his flesh might be sound” (19.37–39; [[thetis]], [[ambrosia]], [[nectar]], [[patroclus]]). It is the only place in the Iliad where the nectar of the gods has a color. Walter Leaf suggests that it is red as taking the place of blood, and reports Heyne's view that the verses recall Egyptian embalming (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 19.38; see the translation's note on 19.38–39). Later in the book Athena drips nectar and ambrosia into the living Achilles (19.347–354; [[lovely]]).
