---
summary: Agamemnon asks the Argives to “mark my word well” and says that the Achaeans have often spoken “this word” to him (19.84–85); Hera tells Zeus that he “will not put fulfillment to your word” (19.107); Agamemnon rejoices “to hear your word”, Odysseus' counsel (19.185), and Odysseus asks Achilles to be patient “with my words” (19.220). Then “No sooner was the word spoken than the work was done” (19.242).
---

αὐτίκʼ ἔπειθʼ ἅμα μῦθος ἔην, τετέλεστο δὲ ἔργον (19.242) joins word and deed in one verse; Walter Leaf compares the Homeric Hymn to Hermes and Herodotus (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 19.242; see [[word-or-deed]]).
