---
summary: Ovid's Alcmene tells how Juno's ill will made the goddess of childbirth hold back her labor for seven days and nights with crossed legs and locked fingers, until a servant's lie set the birth free.
---

- **Alcmene's labor.** In the *Metamorphoses* Alcmene speaks to Iole, who is carrying the child of her grandson Hyllus: may the gods cut short Iole's delays when her time comes and she calls on Ilithyia, whom Juno's favor made hard for Alcmene. For seven nights and as many days Alcmene was in labor and called on Lucina, who came already won over by Juno, sat on the altar before the door with her right knee pressed over her left and her fingers locked together, and held back the birth with silent spells. The servant Galanthis, seeing a goddess sitting so, cried out that Alcmene had been delivered; the goddess leapt up and loosed her hands, and the child was born. Galanthis laughed at the deceived goddess, and was changed into a creature that bears its young through the mouth, since a lying mouth had helped a mother in labor (*Metamorphoses* 9.281–323). In Agamemnon's story Hera “stopped Alcmene's childbirth, and held back the Eileithyiai” (19.119): [[the-birth-of-heracles]], [[eileithyia]] and [[alcmene]].
