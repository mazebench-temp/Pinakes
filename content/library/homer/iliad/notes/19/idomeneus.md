---
summary: Idomeneus is named once, as one of the six who stay with Achilles when he refuses food and sends the other kings away (19.309–312).
---

“but the two sons of Atreus stayed, and brilliant Odysseus, / Nestor and Idomeneus and the old horse-driving Phoenix, / comforting him in his deep grief” (19.310–312; see [[achilles]], [[elders]] and [[kings]]). He is named after Nestor, as when Agamemnon called the elders to his sacrifice before the first battle, “Nestor first of all, and lord Idomeneus” (2.405). Walter Leaf counts the verse among the lists of names joined without a connective (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 19.259). With Nestor and Phoenix he is one of the older men among the six: Book 13 called him “half gray” (μεσαιπόλιος, 13.361).
