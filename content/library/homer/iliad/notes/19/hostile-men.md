---
summary: Odysseus twice names the enemy “hostile men” in his counsel that the army must eat before it fights.
---

The fed man: “But the man who has had his fill of wine and food / and fights all day long against hostile men— / the heart in his breast is bold” (19.167–169). The survivors: “and all who survive the hateful war / must remember drink and food, so that still more / we may fight against hostile men without rest, forever” (19.230–232; [[hateful]]). In both verses the formula begins the line and stands for the day's long fighting that food makes possible ([[odysseus]], [[feast]]). In its first uses it was spoken by men who feared to fall into the enemy's hands (5.487–488, 6.453); here it names the work ahead.
