---
summary: The dead of Book 19 are “torn by the sharp bronze”, Patroclus in Achilles' hut and Briseis' husband before his city (19.211, 19.283, 19.292); in Odysseus' harvest of war “the bronze strews the most straw on the ground” (19.222); and when the army arms, all the earth around laughs “under the flash of the bronze” (19.362–363).
---

**The sharp bronze.** δεδαϊγμένος ὀξέϊ χαλκῷ, “torn by the sharp bronze”, is said of Patroclus by Achilles, who will eat nothing while his companion lies dead in the hut (19.209–211), and by the narrator when Briseis sees him (19.283); Briseis says it of the husband she saw killed before the city (19.292; see [[sharp]], [[patroclus]] and [[briseis]]). The bronze of the knife that cuts the boar's throat at the oath is νηλής, “pitiless” (19.266; see [[pitiless-bronze]] and [[knife]]).

**The harvest of war.** Odysseus tells Achilles that men quickly have their fill of battle, “in which the bronze strews the most straw on the ground, / but the harvest is smallest, when he tips the scales, / Zeus” (19.221–224). The bronze is the sickle, and the dead are its straw. Walter Leaf, after Valeton and Lendrum, explains that battle is hard work with little reward, like a harvest that yields much straw and little grain, in which the dead take the place of the crop, and that soldiers need food for such thankless work; he compares the reapers of Book 11 (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 19.221; 11.67–69; see the translation's note on 19.221–224, [[the-reapers]] and [[the-scales-of-zeus]]). The men who are left must eat and fight on, “our bodies clothed in unyielding bronze” (χαλκὸν ἀτειρέα, 19.233; see [[unyielding]]).

**The flash of the bronze.** When the Achaeans arm, “the gleam reached the sky, and all the earth around laughed / under the flash of the bronze” (χαλκοῦ ὑπὸ στεροπῆς, 19.362–363). στεροπή is the flash of lightning (see [[snowflakes-from-zeus]] and [[earth]]). Achilles arms in the midst of them; his sword is “of bronze” (19.373; see [[sword]]), and he wants to be seen again destroying the Trojan battalions “with his bronze spear” (19.152; see [[spear]]).
