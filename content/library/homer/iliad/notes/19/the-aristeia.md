---
summary: The arming of Achilles is the shining start of his aristeia. His eyes shine like fire, his shield like the moon and a fire seen from the sea, his helmet like a star, and he mounts behind Automedon “gleaming in his armor like shining Hyperion” (19.365–398). The rout begins in the next book.
refs: [19.364-398, 19.424]
---

The pattern begins with armor and light (see [[arming-scene]] and [[like-the-sun]]). The book ends with the hero's cry as he drives his horses “among the foremost” (19.424), and Xanthus' prophecy has already named the end of his career, though not of this day (19.408–417; see [[the-fourth-day-of-battle]] and [[the-short-life-of-achilles]]).
