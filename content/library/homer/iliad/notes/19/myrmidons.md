**The armor.** Book 18 ended with the Myrmidons groaning all night around Achilles over Patroclus (18.354–355), and at dawn Thetis finds him lying over the body, “and many of his companions around him / were weeping” (19.5–6; see [[achilles]] and [[thetis]]). Achilles will say the same to Agamemnon: Patroclus lies in his hut, “and around him his companions / weep” (19.212–213). When Thetis sets the armor down, “And trembling seized all the Myrmidons, and no one dared / look at it face to face, but they fled in fear” (19.14–15; see [[the-making-of-the-arms]]). The night before, the Trojans had stood in their assembly afraid to sit, “for trembling held them all”, because Achilles had appeared at the ditch (18.246–248). Now his own men cannot bear the sight of the god's armor, while he takes delight in it (19.15–19; see [[hephaestus]]).

**The gifts.** When Achilles has dissolved the assembly, “they scattered, each to his own ship, / and the great-hearted Myrmidons busied themselves with the gifts, / and went carrying them to the ship of divine Achilles. / And they set them in the huts, and seated the women, / and the noble attendants drove the horses to the herd” (19.277–281; see [[the-gifts-of-agamemnon]] and [[great-hearted]]). The young men of the Achaeans had carried the gifts from Agamemnon's hut into the assembly (19.238–249); the Myrmidons take them home. Among the women is Briseis, who finds Patroclus dead in the hut (19.282–283; see [[briseis]]). In her lament she recalls that Patroclus promised to make her Achilles' wedded wife and to give the marriage feast “among the Myrmidons” in Phthia (19.297–299; see [[phthia]] and [[marriage]]).
