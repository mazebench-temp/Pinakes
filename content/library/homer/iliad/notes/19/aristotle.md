summary: A note in the scholia, collected among the fragments of Aristotle's Homeric Problems, asks why Hera made Zeus swear an oath rather than nod; the History of Animals gives the name ἅρπη, the bird of Athena's descent from the sky, to a bird that lives from the sea.
---

- Why Hera demanded an oath of Zeus, whose nod is pledge enough (*Homeric Problems*, fr. 387 Gigon, on 19.108): [[aristotle-the-oath-of-zeus]].
- **The ἅρπη.** Athena leaps down from the sky “like a long-winged, shrill-voiced bird of prey” (ἅρπῃ, 19.350), a bird that cannot be identified. In his account of the friendships and enmities of animals Aristotle names the ἅρπη among the birds that live from the sea and are at war with one another, with the βρένθος and the gull, and among the friends of the kite; the bird called φῶυξ is its enemy, because the two live alike (*History of Animals* 9.1, 9.18). Walter Leaf reports that Aristotle seems to give the name to a sea-bird (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 19.350; see the translation's note on 19.350): [[harpe]].
