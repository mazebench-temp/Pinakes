---
summary: “The white-armed goddess Hera gave him speech,” and the horse Xanthus foretells Achilles' death until the Erinyes check his voice.
---

“And from under the yoke the horse with flashing feet spoke to him, / Xanthus, and at once he bowed his head; and all his mane, / streaming from under the yoke-cushion down beside the yoke, reached the ground; / and the white-armed goddess Hera gave him speech” (19.404–407; [[xanthus-the-horse]], [[flashing]], [[white-armed]]). The speech Hera gives, the Erinyes take away when he has spoken: “So he spoke, and the Erinyes checked his voice” (19.418; [[erinyes]]). Aristarchus athetized 19.407 as superfluous and as contradicting 19.418; Walter Leaf answers that the objection tells as much against 19.418 (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 19.407; see the translation's notes on 19.407 and 19.418). In Book 17 the same horses wept for Patroclus and Zeus pitied them, but they did not speak (17.426–456).
