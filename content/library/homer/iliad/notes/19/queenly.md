summary: “Queen Hera, with treachery in her heart,” speaks to Zeus in Agamemnon's story, and Briseis names the husband “to whom my father and lady mother gave me.”
---

**Hera.** “And queen Hera, with treachery in her heart, addressed him” (19.106; [[hera]], [[with-treachery-in-her-heart]]), the whole verse that introduced her deceiving speeches in Book 14 (14.197, 14.300, 14.329).

**Briseis' mother.** “The husband to whom my father and lady mother gave me / I saw before the city, torn by the sharp bronze” (19.291–292; [[briseis]], [[sharp]], [[marriage]]). The formula πότνια μήτηρ, which in the Iliad belongs above all to Thetis and Hecuba, here names the mother of a captive woman, who gave her in marriage before the city fell.
