---
summary: Odysseus asks, of an army that would fast for every man who falls, “when could anyone draw breath from toil?” (19.227).
---

πόνοιο is the toil of the fasting to Walter Leaf, the toil of battle to Monro, whose reading Leaf thinks breaks the thought (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 19.226; see the translation's note on 19.225–229). ἀναπνεύσειε, “draw breath”, is the verb of the breathing space that Nestor hoped Patroclus would win for the Achaeans (11.799–801; see [[brief-is-the-breathing-space]] and [[weep-for-a-day-and-bury]]).
