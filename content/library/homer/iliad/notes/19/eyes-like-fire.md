---
summary: Twice in Book 19 Achilles' eyes blaze. When Thetis lays the new armor before him, “his eyes / shone out terribly beneath his lids, like a blaze” (19.16–17); and when he puts it on, “his two eyes / shone like the blaze of fire” (19.365–366).
grc: ["ὡς εἰ σέλας", "πυρὸς σέλας"]
en: [like a blaze, like the blaze of fire]
---

**Seeing the armor.** ὡς εἶδʼ, ὥς μιν μᾶλλον ἔδυ χόλος, ἐν δέ οἱ ὄσσε / δεινὸν ὑπὸ βλεφάρων ὡς εἰ σέλας ἐξεφάανθεν: “as he saw it, anger entered him the more, and his eyes / shone out terribly beneath his lids, like a blaze” (19.16–17). The Myrmidons tremble and none dares look at the armor face to face; the sight makes Achilles angrier, and the anger shows in his eyes, while he delights in holding the god's gifts (19.14–18; see [[cholos]], [[achilles]] and [[the-making-of-the-arms]]). Walter Leaf points to the like phrase at 365 and reports L. Lange's view that σέλας is in both places an accusative of the inner object, “shone as it were with a flame” (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 19.17). δεινόν and ἐξεφάανθεν recall the line in which Athena appeared to Achilles in Book 1, δεινὼ δέ οἱ ὄσσε φάανθεν, “and her eyes blazed terribly” (1.200), whose eyes the translation gives to the goddess and some readers to Achilles (see [[whose-eyes-blaze]]).

**Arming.** τοῦ καὶ ὀδόντων μὲν καναχὴ πέλε, τὼ δέ οἱ ὄσσε / λαμπέσθην ὡς εἴ τε πυρὸς σέλας (19.365–366). The words τὼ δέ οἱ ὄσσε / λαμπέσθην were said of Hector raging at the ships, with foam about his mouth: “and his two eyes / blazed under his grim brows” (15.607–608). Aristarchus athetized 365–368, finding the gnashing of teeth grotesque, though Dionysius Sidonius, as Didymus reports, said that he later removed the obeli; Leaf keeps the lines, in which a sympathetic reader may find a barbaric grandeur like that of Hector's rage, and suspects only that 368 was altered after the Making of the Arms was added (on 19.365; see the translation's note on 19.365–368). The blaze in the eyes goes with ἄχος ἄτλητον, “unbearable grief”, in the heart (19.367; see [[achos]]): the anger of the first sight has become grief for Patroclus and rage against the Trojans.

σέλας, “blaze”, is the word of the book's lights. After the eyes it is the blaze of the shield, “as of the moon”, and of the fire that sailors see from the sea (19.374–379; see [[the-fire-seen-by-sailors]]). In the earlier books the fire in the eyes was Agamemnon's anger, Hector's onset and the fury of a boar at bay (1.104, 12.466, 13.474).
