---
summary: “The white-armed goddess Hera” gives the horse Xanthus human speech.
---

“and the white-armed goddess Hera gave him speech” (19.407; [[hera]], [[with-human-voice]], [[xanthus-the-horse]]). The line-end formula θεὰ λευκώλενος Ἥρη names the goddess who makes the horse speak; Aristarchus athetized the verse, as superfluous and as contradicting 19.418, where the Erinyes stop the voice (see the translation's note on 19.407). Walter Leaf answers that the objection tells equally against 19.418, and finds it hard to see why Hera should work the miracle (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 19.407).
