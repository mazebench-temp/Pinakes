---
summary: Scyros is where Achilles' son is being reared. Achilles names it twice in his lament for Patroclus, who was to have fetched the boy from Scyros and brought him home to Phthia (19.326–333).
---

Achilles could suffer nothing worse, he says, not even the news of the death “of my dear son, who is being reared for me in Scyros, / if indeed godlike Neoptolemus is still alive” (19.326–327; see [[neoptolemus]] and [[achilles]]). He had hoped that he alone would die at Troy, and that Patroclus would go back to Phthia, “so that you might take my son in a swift black ship / from Scyros, and show him everything” (19.331–332; see [[patroclus]] and [[phthia]]). Σκυρόθεν, “from Scyros”, is found only here in Homer. Walter Leaf reports that Aristarchus and Aristophanes of Byzantium rejected 19.327 partly because Scyros is near Troy, so that Achilles could not be in doubt whether his son was alive (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 19.327; see the translation's note on 19.326–337). The Odyssey keeps the same tradition: Odysseus himself brought Neoptolemus from Scyros to the Achaeans at Troy (*Odyssey* 11.506–509).

In Book 9 Scyros was a city that Achilles sacked, “steep Scyros, the citadel of Enyeus” (9.668; see [[achilles-raids]]). The poem does not say how the city he took and the place where his son is reared are related.
