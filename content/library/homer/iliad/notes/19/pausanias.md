---
summary: Pausanias quotes the boar of Agamemnon's oath, flung into the sea, as proof that in the old days no one ate a victim over which an oath had been sworn; he reports that a scene on the chest of Cypselus was taken for the Nereids and Thetis receiving the arms from Hephaestus; and at Thebes he was shown the Witches whom Hera sent to hold back Alcmene's labor.
---

- The athletes' oath over a boar at Olympia, and the boar of Agamemnon's oath, thrown into the sea, “food for the fishes” (*Description of Greece* 5.24.9–11, on 19.266–268): [[pausanias-the-oath-boar]].
- The Nereids in winged chariots, Thetis receiving the arms from a lame Hephaestus, and Chiron come to console Achilles, on the topmost band of the chest of Cypselus (*Description of Greece* 5.19.7–9): [[the-nereids-and-the-arms]].
- **The Witches.** At Thebes there are figures of women in relief, already worn, whom the Thebans call the Witches (Φαρμακίδες). They say that Hera sent them to hinder Alcmene's labor, and that they held it back until Historis, daughter of Teiresias, raised a cry of joy within their hearing that Alcmene had given birth; deceived, they went away, and Alcmene bore her child (*Description of Greece* 9.11.3). In Agamemnon's story Hera herself “stopped Alcmene's childbirth, and held back the Eileithyiai” (19.119): [[the-birth-of-heracles]] and [[eileithyia]].
