summary: The women of Book 19 are prizes and mourners. Seven captive women skilled in handiwork, and Briseis the eighth, are among Agamemnon's gifts (19.245–246). Briseis, back in Achilles' huts, laments over Patroclus and tells how the war took her husband and her brothers, and the women weep with her for their own sorrows (19.282–302). Achilles wishes that Artemis had killed Briseis on the day he took her (19.59–60), and he fights “for the sake of Helen, at whom men shudder” (19.325).

The quarrel was over a girl, and Achilles now calls it so: the two kings “raged in heart-devouring strife for the sake of a girl” (19.58; see [[eris]]). Agamemnon swears that he never touched her (19.258–265; see [[oath]]). In Briseis' lament the war is told from the side of the captive: a husband and three brothers dead, a city sacked, and a promise of marriage from the man who has died (19.287–300; see [[briseis]], [[captive-women]] and [[evil-follows-evil]]).
