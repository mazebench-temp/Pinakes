---
summary: Thetis tells her son to “arm yourself at once for the war, and put on your courage” (δύσεο δʼ ἀλκήν, 19.36), and Odysseus says that food and wine are “might and courage” (19.161).
---

δύσεο ἀλκήν, “put on your courage”, uses the verb of putting on armor (19.36): Achilles will put on the armor that Thetis has brought and the courage with it (see [[arming-scene]]). Odysseus' verse locates courage in the stomach (see [[food-and-wine-are-might-and-courage]] and [[menos]]).
