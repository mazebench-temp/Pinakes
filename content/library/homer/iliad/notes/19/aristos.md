---
summary: Zeus is “the best / of men and gods”, and even he was blinded (19.95–96); Agamemnon swears by Zeus “highest and best of the gods” (19.258), and Xanthus names Apollo only as “the best of the gods” (19.413). Odysseus chooses “young men, the best of all the Achaeans” to carry the gifts (19.193); Achilles asks whether the quarrel was “better” for them both (19.56), and Thetis promises that Patroclus' flesh will stay sound “or even better” (19.33).
---

The superlative is given to the gods in three ways: as a title of Zeus, which Agamemnon's story undercuts (19.95–96; see [[ate]]), in an oath (19.258; see [[oath-formulas]]), and as a name for a god who is not named (19.413; see [[apollo]]). Odysseus calls Achilles by the other word for the best fighter, “by far the strongest of the Achaeans”, in Patroclus' verse of Book 16 (19.216 = 16.21; see [[best-of-the-achaeans]]).
