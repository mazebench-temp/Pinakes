---
summary: Lamenting Patroclus, Briseis remembers her three brothers, “whom one mother bore with me, / dearly loved, who all met their day of destruction” (19.293–294).
---

“and my three brothers, whom one mother bore with me, / dearly loved, who all met their day of destruction” (19.293–294; see [[briseis]] and [[the-sack-of-lyrnessus]]). τούς μοι μία γείνατο μήτηρ, “whom one mother bore with me”, is Helen's phrase for Castor and Polydeuces, “my own brothers” (3.238), and their fate recalls that of Andromache's seven brothers, whom Achilles killed on a single day when he sacked Thebe (6.421–423; see [[andromache]]). κηδείους, “dearly loved”, is found only here in Homer (Walter Leaf, *Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 19.294).
