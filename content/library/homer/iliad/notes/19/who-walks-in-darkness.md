---
summary: Agamemnon blames his blindness on “Zeus and Fate and the Erinys who walks in darkness.”
---

“but I am not to blame, / but Zeus and Fate and the Erinys who walks in darkness, / who in the assembly cast savage blind folly into my mind” (19.86–88; [[erinyes]], [[moira]], [[ate]], [[aitios]]). The epithet's other place is Phoenix's story, where the Erinys hears Althaea's curse out of Erebus (9.571–572). Walter Leaf reports an ancient variant, εἰαροπῶτις, explained as “blood-drinking” from a Cyprian word for blood, and notes that the Erinys brings ἄτη also in the Odyssey (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 19.87; *Odyssey* 15.233–234; see the translation's note on 19.87).
