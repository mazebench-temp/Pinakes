---
summary: Cronus is named only in his son's patronymic: Hera brings the news of Eurystheus' birth to “Zeus, son of Cronus”, and “the son of Cronus” pities Achilles and the elders who mourn with him.
---

In Book 19 Cronus appears only in the titles of his son (see [[zeus]] and [[son-of-cronus]]). In Agamemnon's story Hera, having brought Eurystheus to birth, “bringing the news, addressed Zeus, son of Cronus” (Δία Κρονίωνα, 19.120; see [[hera]] and [[the-birth-of-heracles]]), with the verse-end of Book 1, where Hera addressed him “with cutting words” (1.539). Late in the book, “the son of Cronus saw them weeping and pitied them” (Κρονίων, 19.340), in the verse in which he pitied the weeping horses of Achilles (17.441; see the note on Book 17 and [[pity]]), and he sends Athena to Achilles with nectar and ambrosia (19.341–354; see [[athena]]).
