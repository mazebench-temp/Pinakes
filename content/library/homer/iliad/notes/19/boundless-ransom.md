---
summary: Agamemnon repeats the offer he made through the envoys: “I am willing to make amends and give a boundless ransom.”
---

“But since I was blinded, and Zeus took away my wits, / I am willing to make amends and give a boundless ransom” (19.137–138; [[ate]], [[apoina]], [[propitiation]]). The second verse is the one with which he began the list of his gifts in Book 9 (9.120), and the first varies 9.119, where he was blinded “obeying my wretched mind”; now the blame is laid on Zeus, after the story of Ate (19.86–136). This time the ransom is paid: the tripods, the cauldrons, the horses, the women and the gold are brought out before the assembly (19.243–249; [[the-gifts-of-agamemnon]]). Walter Leaf, who suspects 19.137–141, counts the borrowing from Book 9 among the reasons (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 19.136; see the translation's note on 19.136–141).
