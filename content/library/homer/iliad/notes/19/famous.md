---
summary: Thetis tells Achilles to receive “this glorious armor from Hephaestus,” and Achilles calls his horses the “far-famed children of Podarge”; the book adds τηλεκλυτά.
grc: [τηλεκλυτὰ]
---

**The armor.** “but you, receive this glorious armor from Hephaestus, / very fine, such as no man has ever yet worn on his shoulders” (19.10–11; [[thetis]], [[the-making-of-the-arms]]). κλυτὰ τεύχεα, the phrase of the armor that Patroclus wore and Hector took, now names the new armor made by the god.

**The horses.** “Xanthus and Balius, far-famed children of Podarge” (19.400; [[the-horses-of-achilles]], [[podarge]]). τηλεκλυτός occurs in Homer only here and once in the Odyssey, of Orestes (*Odyssey* 1.30); the Iliad's other word for the far-famed is τηλεκλειτός, of the Trojans' allies and of Phoenix, father of Europa (5.491, 6.111, 14.321). The neuter plural τηλεκλυτά is new to the entry.
