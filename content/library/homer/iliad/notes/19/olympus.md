---
summary: In Agamemnon's story Hera darts down from the peak of Olympus to bring about the early birth of Eurystheus, and Zeus, deceived, swears that Blind Folly will never come back to Olympus and the starry sky, and hurls her down to the works of men.
---

**Hera's descent.** “And Hera darted down and left the peak of Olympus, / and swiftly came to Achaean Argos” (19.114–115; see [[hera]] and [[the-birth-of-heracles]]): the first verse is the one with which she set out from Olympus in Book 14 to deceive Zeus on Ida (14.225; see [[the-deception-of-zeus]]). Walter Leaf reports a scholium that would have had her leave “the starry sky” instead, and takes the objection to mean that Agamemnon knows too much for a mortal of the ways of the gods, a difficulty inherent in the whole story (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 19.114; see the translation's note on 19.114).

**Blind Folly banished.** When Hera has brought Eurystheus to birth and told Zeus what he has sworn to, he seizes Blind Folly by the hair, “angered in his heart, and swore a mighty oath / that never to Olympus and the starry sky / would Blind Folly come again, who blinds all. / So saying, he hurled her from the starry sky” (19.127–130; see [[zeus]], [[ate]] and [[starry]]). Olympus and the sky together are the gods' realm, as when Thetis went up to “great heaven and Olympus” to supplicate Zeus (1.497) and the gates of heaven are kept by the Hours, “to whom great heaven and Olympus are entrusted” (5.750 = 8.394; see [[sky]] and [[hours]]). Blind Folly is shut out of both, and comes down “to the works of men” (19.131).
