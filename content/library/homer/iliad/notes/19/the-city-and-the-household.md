---
summary: Achilles had hoped that Patroclus would bring his son from Scyros and show him “my wealth and my slaves and my great high-roofed house” (19.333), and the elders who mourn with him each remember “what he had left in his halls” (19.339). Briseis lost her husband “before the city” when Achilles sacked it (19.292–296).
refs: [19.326-333, 19.339]
---

The house in Phthia is what Achilles has given up by staying at Troy, and the house in Lyrnessus is what the war took from Briseis (see [[phthia]], [[neoptolemus]] and [[the-sack-of-lyrnessus]]). The verse of the high-roofed house was spoken by Pandarus, who wished he might see his own again (19.333 ≈ 5.213).
