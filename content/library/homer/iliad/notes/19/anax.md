summary: Agamemnon is “lord of men” throughout the assembly (19.51, 19.76, 19.146, 19.172, 19.184, 19.199), and Odysseus addresses him as “my lord” (19.177). In Agamemnon's story lordship is the prize of Zeus' oath: the child born that day “will be lord over all who dwell around him”, and Hera brings Eurystheus to birth, “who will be lord of the Argives” (19.104, 19.109, 19.122, 19.124).
grc: [ἀνάξει, ἀνάξειν]
---

The verb ἀνάσσω marks the stakes of the story: Zeus means his son Heracles to rule, and the oath makes Eurystheus ruler and Heracles his laborer (19.122–124, 19.133; see [[eurystheus]], [[heracles]] and [[oath]]). Agamemnon tells the story as a king who was blinded as Zeus was (see [[ate]] and [[kingship-and-authority]]).
