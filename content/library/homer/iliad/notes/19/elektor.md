---
summary: Achilles takes his stand behind Automedon, “gleaming in his armor like shining Hyperion.”
---

“Automedon; and behind him Achilles took his stand, armed, / gleaming in his armor like shining Hyperion, / and he called terribly to the horses of his father” (19.397–399; [[like-the-sun]], [[hyperion]], [[automedon]]). The half-verse is the one that brought Paris down to the war in Book 6, where ἠλέκτωρ stood alone for the sun (6.513); here it is joined to the Sun's own name, Ὑπερίων, which the Iliad uses only here and in Book 8 (8.480; Walter Leaf, *Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 8.480).
