---
summary: The book begins and ends at the ships. Thetis brings the armor "to the ships" (19.3); even the helmsmen and stewards who always stayed "in the gathering of the ships" come to the assembly (19.42–44); the gifts are carried from Agamemnon's ship to Achilles' (19.143, 19.194, 19.277–279); and the army pours out "away from the swift ships" to arm (19.356–360). Achilles sits mourning "before the straight-horned ships" (19.344).
---

νεῶν ἐν ἀγῶνι, "in the gathering of the ships", is the phrase of 15.428 and 16.500 (see the translation's note on 7.298). Patroclus had promised to take Briseis "in the ships / to Phthia" (19.298–299), and Achilles had hoped that he would take his son home "in a swift black ship" (19.331; see [[briseis]], [[neoptolemus]] and [[patroclus]]).
