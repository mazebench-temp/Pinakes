summary: Achilles and Briseis remember the sack from its two sides. Renouncing his wrath, Achilles wishes that Artemis had killed Briseis on the day he took her and destroyed Lyrnessus (19.59–60); lamenting Patroclus, Briseis tells how she saw her husband killed before the city and lost her three brothers, and how Patroclus would not let her weep but promised her a marriage to Achilles (19.291–299).

For Achilles the sack is the day he took a girl who has cost the Achaeans dear: “I wish Artemis had killed her with an arrow among the ships / on the day when I took her, when I destroyed Lyrnessus” (19.59–60; see [[achilles]] and [[briseis]]). For Briseis it is the day she lost her family:

{{quote:d8df0b2f-5179-5b23-ba8a-e24546a6d415}}

She does not name the town; it is “the city of divine Mynes” (19.296; see [[mynes]] and [[lyrnessus]]). The Catalogue told the sack from the victor's side, as a labor: Achilles took her “out of Lyrnessus after much toil” (2.690) and struck down the sons of Euenus (2.692–693). Briseis tells it as Andromache told the sack of Thebe, where Achilles killed her father and her seven brothers (6.414–424; see [[sack-of-thebe]] and [[andromache]]): from inside, as the loss of a household. Her consolation came from the victor's camp, when Patroclus promised to make her the wedded wife of the man who had killed her husband (19.295–299; see [[patroclus]], [[marriage]] and [[captive-women]]).
