---
summary: Achilles, putting a stop to his anger, says that many a Trojan will be glad to rest if he escapes “from the destructive war, from under our spear.”
---

“but I think / many a man among them will be glad to bend his knees and rest, who escapes / from the destructive war, from under our spear” (19.71–73; [[achilles]], [[polemos]]). The words are those Agamemnon used of Hector in Book 7, when he held Menelaus back from the duel: fearless though Hector is, “I say he will be glad to bend his knees and rest, if he escapes / from destructive war and grim combat” (7.118–119; [[grim-combat]]). There the champion the Achaeans would set up was to make Hector glad of rest; here it is Achilles, in the speech in which he puts a stop to his anger (19.67), who will make the Trojans glad of it.
