---
summary: The book stands between battles. Thetis tells Achilles to arm “at once for the war” (19.36), Achilles asks for the army to be urged “on to war” (19.69), and Odysseus argues that a man must eat to fight “all day long” (19.162–170). Achilles will rest only when “there comes some pause in the war” (19.201), and he goes out to give the Trojans “their fill of war” (19.423). Zeus is “the dispenser of war for men” (19.224).
grc: [μαχέσθω, μαχησομένους, πολεμίζῃ, μαχώμεθα, πολεμίζω]
---

The phrases of the book's end are those of satiety: Achilles bids the horses bring him back “when we have had our fill of war” (ἕωμεν πολέμοιο, 19.402), and he will not stop until he has driven the Trojans to “their fill” (ἅδην ἐλάσαι πολέμοιο, 19.423; 13.315; Walter Leaf, *Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 19.402, 19.423). Odysseus had said that men soon have their fill of battle (19.221; see [[satiety-of-battle]]). πολέμου στόμα, “the mouth of bloody war” (19.313), is a phrase of Book 10, “the great mouth of bitter war” (10.8; Leaf on 19.313). See [[dispenser-of-war]] and [[charme]].
