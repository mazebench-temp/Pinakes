---
summary: In Agamemnon's story Hera hurries to Argos, where she knew “the strong wife of Sthenelus, son of Perseus”; the book adds ἰφθίμην.
grc: [ἰφθίμην]
---

“and swiftly came to Achaean Argos, where she knew / the strong wife of Sthenelus, son of Perseus. / She was carrying her dear son, and the seventh month had come” (19.115–117; [[hera]], [[argos]], [[eurystheus]]). ἰφθίμη ἄλοχος is the phrase of Diomedes' wife, “the strong wife of Diomedes, tamer of horses” (5.415); the woman is not named, and Walter Leaf observes that later writers give her several names (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 19.116). The accusative ἰφθίμην is new to the entry.
