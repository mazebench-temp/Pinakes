The spear comes last, after the greaves, the corselet, the sword, the shield and the helmet, as the arming verses have it (19.369–383; see [[arming-scene]] and [[achilles]]). It is not the work of Hephaestus, who made a shield, a corselet, a helmet and greaves (18.478–613; see [[hephaestus]] and [[the-making-of-the-arms]]): it is πατρώϊον … ἔγχος, “his father's spear” (19.387), the gift of the Centaur to Peleus (see [[chiron]] and [[peleus]]), and it was never lost. Patroclus armed in all the rest of Achilles' armor and left this behind, because no other Achaean could wield it, and Hector stripped the rest from his body (16.140–144, 17.125; see [[patroclus]] and [[the-armor-of-peleus]]). The four verses that told why he left it describe it again now that its owner takes it up (19.388–391 = 16.141–144); Walter Leaf sees no reason to doubt them here (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 19.388).

σύριγξ, the “case” from which he draws it, is a socket to hold the spear, in this sense found only here; Leaf compares the spear stand in Odysseus' house (on 19.387; *Odyssey* 1.128; see the translation's note on 19.387). Elsewhere σῦριγξ is the panpipe (10.13, 18.526; see [[syrinx]]).
