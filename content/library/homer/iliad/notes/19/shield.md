---
summary: The army's “bossed shields” pour out of the ships with the helmets, corselets and spears (19.360), and Achilles takes up the shield that Hephaestus made, “great and heavy”, whose blaze, as of the moon, reaches the upper air like a fire seen by sailors from the sea (19.373–380).
---

The army's shields are ἀσπίδες ὀμφαλόεσσαι, “bossed shields” (19.360; see [[bossed]] and [[snowflakes-from-zeus]]). Achilles takes his up fourth, after the greaves, the corselet and the sword, in the half-verse of the armings of Paris and Patroclus: αὐτὰρ ἔπειτα σάκος μέγα τε στιβαρόν τε, “and then his shield, great and heavy” (19.373 = 3.335, 16.136; see [[arming-scene]]). The same words were the narrator's when Hephaestus began the shield and when he finished it (18.478, 18.609; see [[the-shield-of-achilles]]). What follows the formula is new: “he took up, and from it, far off, came a blaze as of the moon” (19.374; see [[moon]]), and the blaze reaches the upper air like the fire that sailors see from the sea; the tenor names the shield again, σάκεος … / καλοῦ δαιδαλέου, “the shield … / the fine shield, intricately worked” (19.379–380; see [[the-fire-seen-by-sailors]] and [[intricately-worked]]).
