---
summary: The Argives are the army in assembly. Agamemnon bids them take heed, the oath is sworn among them while they sit in silence, and Achilles speaks among “the war-loving Argives” before he dissolves the assembly (19.84, 19.175, 19.255–256, 19.269). In Agamemnon's story of Heracles' birth the Argives are the people of Argos, whom Eurystheus is born to rule (19.122–124).
refs: [19.255]
---

**In the assembly.** Agamemnon will open his mind to the son of Peleus, “but you others, / Argives, take heed, and each of you mark my word well” (19.83–84; see [[agamemnon]]). σύνθεσθε, “take heed”, is Calchas' word to Achilles in the first assembly (σὺ δὲ σύνθεο, 1.76; Walter Leaf, *Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 19.84). Odysseus asks that Agamemnon “stand up among the Argives and swear you an oath” (19.175), and when he does, “they all sat there in silence in their places, / the Argives, listening to the king, as was right” (19.255–256; see [[oath]], [[silence]] and [[moira]]). After the oath Achilles “stood up and spoke among the war-loving Argives” (19.269; see [[war-loving]]), and dissolved the assembly (19.276; see [[assembly]]).

**At the ships.** Agamemnon dates his memory of Blind Folly to the day when Hector “was killing the Argives at the sterns of the ships” (19.135; see [[ate]] and [[hector]]), and Odysseus ends his speech with the summons that will mean evil “for whoever is left behind / at the ships of the Argives” (19.235–236; see [[odysseus]]).

**The Argives of Eurystheus.** In Agamemnon's story Hera tells Zeus, “now a brave man is born who will be lord of the Argives, / Eurystheus, child of Sthenelus the son of Perseus, / of your race; it is not unseemly for him to be lord of the Argives” (19.122–124; see [[eurystheus]], [[hera]] and [[the-birth-of-heracles]]). These Argives are the people of Achaean Argos, where Hera went to bring Eurystheus to birth (19.115): for Walter Leaf the Argive plain of the Peloponnese, where Eurystheus' towns were Mycenae and Tiryns rather than the later city of Argos (on 19.115; see [[argos]]). Zeus' boast had promised the rule of “all who dwell around him” (19.104); Hera's report names the kingdom.
