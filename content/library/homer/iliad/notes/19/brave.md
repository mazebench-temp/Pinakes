---
summary: Hera tells Zeus that “a brave man is born”; Odysseus checks Achilles, “brave as you are”; and Zeus tells Athena that she has abandoned “your brave man”; the book adds three phrases.
grc: ["γέγονʼ ἐσθλὸς", "ἀγαθός περ ἐὼν", "ἀνδρὸς ἑῆος"]
---

**Eurystheus.** Hera's news in Agamemnon's story: “now a brave man is born who will be lord of the Argives, / Eurystheus” (19.122–123; [[eurystheus]], [[hera]]). The praise goes to the child who has taken Heracles' place.

**Achilles.** Odysseus: “Not like this, godlike Achilles, brave as you are” (19.155; [[godlike]], [[odysseus]]). The concessive ἀγαθός περ ἐών grants the quality in order to check the man, as in the verse of Agamemnon that Odysseus repeats (1.131), in Nestor's words to Agamemnon, “great as you are” (1.275), and in Poseidon's of Zeus, “brave as he is” (15.185). The phrase now catches those lines too.

**Athena's charge.** Zeus to Athena: “My child, you have utterly abandoned your brave man” (19.342; [[athena]], [[achilles]]). ἑῆος is taken in Aristarchus' sense, “good, brave,” the genitive of ἐΰς; Zenodotus read ἑοῖο, “your own” (see the translation's note on 19.342).
