---
summary: Odysseus asks for Agamemnon's oath that he never lay with Briseis, “as is the custom, my lord, of men and of women” (19.177), and for a feast of amends, “so that you may have no lack of justice”; and he tells Agamemnon that he “will be more just hereafter / toward another too” (19.180–182).
grc: [δίκης, δικαιότερος]
en: [justice, more just]
---

θέμις is what is laid down as right between men and women; the verse is Odysseus' report of Agamemnon's promise in Book 9 (19.177; 9.276). δίκη here is the satisfaction owed to the wronged man: the feast completes what is due to Achilles, and ἐπιδευές, “lack”, is used as an abstract noun (Walter Leaf, *Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 19.180). The adjective δίκαιος occurs in the Iliad only in degrees of comparison: Chiron is “the most just of the Centaurs” (11.832), the Abii “the most just of men” (13.6), and Agamemnon is to be “more just” (19.181).
