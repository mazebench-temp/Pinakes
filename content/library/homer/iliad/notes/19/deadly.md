---
summary: Ate, the eldest daughter of Zeus, is “ruinous,” the epithet of the wrath in the second verse of the poem, given in the speech that ends the wrath; the book adds οὐλομένη.
grc: [οὐλομένη]
---

“Blind Folly is the eldest daughter of Zeus, who blinds all, / ruinous; her feet are tender, for she does not step / upon the ground” (19.91–93; [[ate]], [[daughter-of-zeus]], [[tender]]). οὐλομένη stands at the head of the verse, as οὐλομένην did of the wrath in the second verse of the poem (1.2), and Walter Leaf reads it as a curse: “a curse upon her!” (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 19.92). The nominative οὐλομένη is new to the entry.
