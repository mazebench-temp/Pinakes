---
summary: In Agamemnon's story Hera, planning her trick, addresses Zeus as “Olympian” and asks him to swear a mighty oath.
---

“You will prove false, and hereafter will not put fulfillment to your word. / But come now, Olympian, swear me a mighty oath” (19.107–108; [[hera]], [[krateros]], [[with-treachery-in-her-heart]]). The vocative Ὀλύμπιε is the address of a request: Thetis used it to beg Zeus to honor her son (1.508), and Nestor to pray that he ward off the pitiless day (15.375). Here the title opens the oath that will turn against the god (19.113; [[ate]], [[zeus]]).
