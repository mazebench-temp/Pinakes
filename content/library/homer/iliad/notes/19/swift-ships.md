---
summary: Odysseus bids the Achaeans eat “by the swift ships”; Achilles had hoped Patroclus would fetch his son “in a swift black ship”; and the army pours out “away from the swift ships” to arm.
---

“But bid the Achaeans eat by the swift ships / of food and wine; for in that is might and courage” (19.160–161; [[odysseus]]); “so that you might take my son in a swift black ship / from Scyros” (19.331–332; [[black-ships]], [[neoptolemus]]); and as Athena goes back to Olympus, “they poured out away from the swift ships” (19.356; [[eristhenes]]). The last is the genitive plural θοάων, which in the Iliad is always of ships.
