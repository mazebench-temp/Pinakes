---
summary: Achilles fears that flies will breed worms in the wounds of Patroclus and defile the body while he goes out to fight (19.24–27); Thetis keeps them away (19.30–39).
---

εὐλὰς ἐγγείνωνται, “and breed worms” (19.26): the flies, making their way into the wounds, are to breed the maggots, and “the life is slain out of it—and all his flesh will rot” (19.27; see [[flies]] and the translation's note on 19.24–27). ἐγγείνομαι, “engender in”, occurs only here in Homer. Thetis promises to ward off the flies and to keep the flesh sound for a full year, and drips ambrosia and red nectar into his nostrils (19.29–39; see [[ambrosia]] and [[nectar]]).
