summary: The quarrel that began in an assembly ends in one. At Thetis' bidding Achilles calls the Achaean heroes together (19.34), and all of them come (19.42–53). The gifts are brought “into the middle of the assembly, so that all the Achaeans / may see them with their eyes” (19.173–174), and there Agamemnon swears his oath (19.249–266); then Achilles sends the army to its meal and dissolves the assembly (19.275–276). Agamemnon remembers the assembly of Book 1 as the place where the gods cast blind folly into his mind (19.88).

The wrong was done in public, and it is put right in public: the prize was taken before the army, and the gifts are counted out before it (see [[the-gifts-of-agamemnon]] and [[amends-and-reconciliation]]). The speakers have to make themselves heard. Agamemnon, who speaks seated, complains of the din: “in the great din of men how could anyone listen / or speak? He is hindered, clear-voiced orator though he is” (19.81–82; see [[clear-voiced-orator]] and [[listen-to-the-man-who-stands]]). Walter Leaf takes him to be hampered by the applause that has just greeted Achilles (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 19.80). The army speaks only by its joy at the renunciation (19.74–75) and by its silence at the oath, where the Argives sit “in silence in their places, … listening to the king, as was right” (19.255–256; see [[silence]]). For the order of the meeting see [[assembly-type-scene]].
