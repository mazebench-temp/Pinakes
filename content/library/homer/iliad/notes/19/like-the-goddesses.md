---
summary: Briseis begins her lament over Patroclus as “the woman, like the goddesses.”
---

“And weeping, the woman, like the goddesses, spoke: / ‘Patroclus, dearest to my heart, wretched that I am’” (19.286–287; [[briseis]], [[dear-to-my-heart]], [[lament]]). The formula of a woman's beauty, which named Hecamede in Book 11 in the same words, γυνὴ ἐϊκυῖα θεῇσιν (11.638), introduces a captive's lament. Four verses earlier she was “like golden Aphrodite” (19.282; [[golden]]); the poem praises her beauty twice as she grieves, and between the two praises she tears her breasts, her neck and her face (19.284–285; [[tender]]).
