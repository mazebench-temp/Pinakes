**Diomedes and Odysseus.** “And the two attendants of Ares came limping, / the son of Tydeus, steadfast in battle, and brilliant Odysseus, / leaning on their spears; for they still had grievous wounds” (19.47–49; [[son-of-tydeus]], [[steadfast-in-battle]], [[grim]]). Elsewhere the pair so named are the two Ajaxes (8.79, 10.228); here the dual Ἄρεος θεράποντε, in this order only here, names the two kings wounded in Book 11, Diomedes by Paris' arrow through the foot and Odysseus by Socus' spear along the ribs (11.369–377, 11.434–438; [[diomedes]], [[odysseus]]). Walter Leaf notes that only Diomedes' wound accounts for a limp (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 19.47).

**Agamemnon's address.** “Friends, Danaan heroes, attendants of Ares” (19.78) is the verse with which he began the test of the army in Book 2 (2.110), and which Nestor and Ajax used to rally it (6.67, 15.733). Here it opens the speech he makes from his seat, the apology for his blindness (19.76–77; [[agamemnon]], [[danaans]], [[ate]]).
