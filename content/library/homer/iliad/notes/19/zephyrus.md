# Unnamed: the breath of the West Wind, "which they say is the swiftest of all" (19.416).
refs: [19.416]
---

“But we two could run even as fast as the breath of the West Wind, / which they say is the swiftest of all; but for you yourself / it is fated to be brought down by force by a god and a man” (19.415–417; see [[xanthus-the-horse]] and [[the-horses-of-achilles]]). In Book 16 the same horses “flew as fast as the winds” (ἅμα πνοιῇσι πετέσθην, 16.149), and the poet gave their descent there: the Harpy Podarge bore them to the West Wind as she grazed beside the stream of Oceanus (16.149–151; see [[podarge]] and the note on Book 16). Aristarchus athetized 416–417 as needless, objecting that φασί, “they say”, is used of what is not certainly known, and does not suit a horse. Walter Leaf finds the reasons unconvincing, since φασί is often used of things well known, as of Zeus at 19.96, but notes that the poet seems not to know here that the West Wind is Xanthus' own father (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 19.416; see the translation's note on 19.416–417).
