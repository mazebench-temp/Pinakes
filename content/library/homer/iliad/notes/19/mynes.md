# Μύνητος (19.296), the genitive, is found only here.
grc: [Μύνητος]
---

“Nor would you let me, when swift Achilles killed my husband / and sacked the city of divine Mynes, / weep” (19.295–297; see [[briseis]] and [[patroclus]]). The city is named by its lord, so Mynes was its king (see [[lyrnessus]] and [[divine]]). The Catalogue tells how Achilles struck down Mynes and his brother Epistrophus, the sons of lord Euenus, when he sacked Lyrnessus and took Briseis (2.690–693; see [[epistrophus-of-lyrnessus]], [[euenus]] and [[the-sack-of-lyrnessus]]). Briseis does not name her husband. Walter Leaf reports that tradition made Mynes her husband, though nothing in Homer shows him to be anything but her king (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 19.296).
