**The army's helmets.** “so then, thick and fast, the helmets, shining brightly, / were carried out from the ships” (ταρφειαὶ κόρυθες λαμπρὸν γανόωσαι, 19.359–360), first of the arms that pour out like snowflakes (see [[snowflakes-from-zeus]]); λαμπρὸν γανόωντες is said of helmets and corselets in Idomeneus' hut (13.265).

**Achilles' helmet.** περὶ δὲ τρυφάλειαν ἀείρας / κρατὶ θέτο βριαρήν· ἣ δʼ ἀστὴρ ὣς ἀπέλαμπεν / ἵππουρις τρυφάλεια, περισσείοντο δʼ ἔθειραι / χρύσεαι, ἃς Ἥφαιστος ἵει λόφον ἀμφὶ θαμειάς: “and lifting the helmet, / he set it, heavy, on his head; and like a star it shone, / the helmet with its horsehair crest, and around it waved the plumes / of gold, which Hephaestus had set thick about the crest” (19.380–383; see [[like-a-star]]). It is the “heavy helmet fitted to his temples, / fine and intricately worked”, on which Hephaestus “set a golden crest” (18.611–612; see [[heavy-helmet]] and [[gold-and-silver]]). The verses of the other armings set a κυνέη on the warrior's head, “and the plume nodded terribly above it” (3.336–337, 16.137–138; see [[arming-scene]]); Achilles' helmet is a τρυφάλεια, and its ἔθειραι, the plumes of the crest, are gold. The words from περισσείοντο to the end of 383 return, nearly the same, when Achilles runs at Hector (22.315–316); Walter Leaf thinks 382 was made from that passage and 383 added in both places to recall the Making of the Arms (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 19.382; see the translation's note on 19.382–383 and [[the-making-of-the-arms]]).

The helmet he lost was fouled. When Apollo struck it from Patroclus' head, it rolled under the horses' feet, “and the horsehair was stained / with blood and dust”, as it had never been before (16.793–797; see [[the-armor-of-peleus]]). The ἔθειραι of the new helmet are of gold.
