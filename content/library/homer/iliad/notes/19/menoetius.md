---
summary: Menoetius is named once, in his son's title, when Achilles fears that flies will defile the body of “the valiant son of Menoetius” (19.24).
---

Μενοιτίου ἄλκιμον υἱόν, “the valiant son of Menoetius”, is the verse-end that named Patroclus in Books 16 and 18 (16.278, 16.827, 18.455; see [[son-of-menoetius]] and [[patroclus]]). In Book 16 Achilles had heard that Menoetius was still alive (16.14), and in Book 18 he remembered his promise to bring Patroclus home to his father in Opus (18.324–327; see [[opus]]). In Book 19 he says that he had hoped Patroclus would go back to Phthia (19.330; see [[phthia]]), to the house of Peleus where Menoetius had brought him as a boy (23.84–90; see [[peleus]]).
