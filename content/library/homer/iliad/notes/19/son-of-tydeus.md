---
summary: Diomedes, “the son of Tydeus, steadfast in battle,” comes limping to the assembly with Odysseus.
---

“And the two attendants of Ares came limping, / the son of Tydeus, steadfast in battle, and brilliant Odysseus, / leaning on their spears; for they still had grievous wounds” (19.47–49; [[diomedes]], [[steadfast-in-battle]], [[attendants-of-ares]]). It is Diomedes' only appearance in the book. Paris' arrow went through his foot in Book 11 (11.376–377).
