summary: Agamemnon makes the mirror himself. To excuse his blindness in the quarrel, he tells how Zeus was blinded: Zeus boasted before the gods, Hera bound him by an oath, and he groaned whenever he saw his son Heracles laboring for Eurystheus (19.95–133). Then “So I too” (19.134): the king was blinded as the god was.

The parts match. Zeus speaks in the assembly of the gods, “Hear me, all you gods and all you goddesses” (19.101), as Agamemnon was blinded “in the assembly” (19.88; see [[hear-me]] and [[assembly]]); each is moved by ἄτη to an act he cannot undo, an oath and the taking of a prize; and each learns the cost later, Zeus from Hera's news of Eurystheus' birth, Agamemnon when Hector was killing the Argives at the ships (19.120–125, 19.134–136; see [[achos]]). The difference is in what follows. Zeus seizes Blind Folly by the hair and hurls her out of heaven forever, and she comes down to the works of men (19.126–131); Agamemnon, a man, can only pay (19.137–138; see [[the-gifts-of-agamemnon]]). Walter Leaf observes that in this story the deeds and the very words of the gods are told by a character, which elsewhere only the poet does (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 19.95; see [[mythological-paradigm]] and [[ate]]).
