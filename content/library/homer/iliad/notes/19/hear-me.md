---
summary: In Agamemnon's story Zeus opens his boast to the gods with the verses that opened his assembly in Book 8: “Hear me, all you gods and all you goddesses, / so that I may say what the heart in my breast bids me” (19.101–102; 8.5–6).
---

In Book 8 the formula of a speech to a gathering introduced a decree, that no god should help either side (8.5–27). Here it introduces a boast that Hera turns into a trap: the child of Zeus' blood born that day will rule all who dwell around him, and she makes him swear it (19.103–113; see [[oath]], [[ate]] and [[assembly]]). Agamemnon tells the scene as the narrator tells scenes among the gods, with the gods' own words, and Walter Leaf notes that elsewhere in the poem only the poet does that (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 19.95).
