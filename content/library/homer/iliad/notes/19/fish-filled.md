---
summary: In the simile of Achilles' shield, storm winds carry the sailors “over the fish-filled sea, far from their friends.”
---

“And as when to sailors out at sea there appears the blaze / of a burning fire, which burns high up in the mountains / in a lonely farmstead; and storm winds bear them, unwilling, / over the fish-filled sea, far from their friends; / so from the shield of Achilles the blaze reached the upper air” (19.375–379; [[lonely]], [[the-shield-of-achilles]]). The formula πόντον ἐπʼ ἰχθυόεντα, which in the Odyssey carries men away from home or back to it, is in the Iliad only here: the sailors see the fire of a farmstead they cannot reach, and the light of Achilles' shield is set beside it.
