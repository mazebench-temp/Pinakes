---
summary: The gifts are counted out before the army: seven tripods, twenty cauldrons, twelve horses, seven women skilled in handiwork and Briseis the eighth, and ten talents of gold, which Odysseus weighs (19.243–247). In Agamemnon's story Eurystheus is born in the seventh month (19.117–118), and Thetis can keep Patroclus' body sound for “a full year's round” (19.32).
refs: [19.32, 19.117-118, 19.243-247]
---

The numbers are those of the promise of Book 9 (9.122–123, 9.128; see [[the-gifts-of-agamemnon]]). στήσας, “weighed out” (19.247), shows to Walter Leaf that the Homeric talent was a known weight of gold, though he thinks it more likely that talents were bars to be counted, not weighed; the stress laid on the weighing seems to him to make ten talents a very large sum, whereas it was small, and may point to a later time, when the talent had grown large (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 19.247). ἕβδομος … μείς, “the seventh month”, and ἠλιτόμηνος, “short of his months” (19.117–118), make Eurystheus a child born too early by Hera's hand (see [[eurystheus]]).
