summary: The kings wounded on the third day of battle come to the assembly unhealed: Diomedes and Odysseus limping, leaning on their spears, “for they still had grievous wounds”, and Agamemnon last, “with his wound” (19.47–53).
grc: [ἕλκεα]
en: [wounds]
---

They were wounded only the day before (11.252–253, 11.376–377, 11.434–438; see [[the-wounded-chiefs]]). Walter Leaf notes that only Diomedes' limp is explained by his wound, Paris' arrow through his foot, and that both men compete in the funeral games of Book 23: “the rapid healing of wounds is a privilege of the heroic age” (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 19.47). The book's other care for a body is not healing but preservation: Thetis drips ambrosia and nectar into Patroclus' nostrils, “so that his flesh might be sound” (19.38–39; see [[ambrosia]] and [[honoring-the-dead]]).
