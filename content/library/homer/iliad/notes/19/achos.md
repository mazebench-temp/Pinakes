summary: Grief runs through the book from its first speech to the arming. Thetis and Achilles both put the past aside “for all our grief” (19.8, 19.65), and the two kings raged in strife “grieving at heart” (19.57). Achilles refuses food “since dread grief has come upon me” (19.307), and as he arms “into his heart / entered unbearable grief” (19.366–367). In Agamemnon's story “sharp grief struck” Zeus when he learned that Eurystheus was born (19.125).

ἄχος is what Zeus feels at Hera's trick and what Achilles feels for Patroclus, and the same word turns into anger in both: Zeus seizes Blind Folly “angered in his heart” (19.125–127), and Achilles arms “raging against the Trojans” (19.367; see [[cholos]] and [[the-gods-mirror-the-mortals]]). The verses of 19.65–66 are those of Achilles' wish in Book 18 (18.112–113; see [[eris]]).
