summary: Agamemnon orders Talthybius to make ready a boar to sacrifice to Zeus and the Sun. At the oath the herald stands beside the king, “like a god in his voice”, holding the boar, and when Agamemnon has cut its throat he flings it into the sea (19.196–197, 19.250–251, 19.266–268).

**The boar.** “And let Talthybius quickly, through the wide army of the Achaeans, / make ready a boar for me, to sacrifice to Zeus and the Sun” (19.196–197; see [[boar]], [[wide-army]] and [[agamemnon]]). In Book 3 the king sent him to the ships for the lamb of the oath of the truce (3.118–120; see the note on Book 3).

**At the oath.** “and Talthybius, like a god in his voice, / holding the boar in his hands, stood beside the shepherd of the people” (19.250–251; see [[shepherd-of-the-people]]). θεῷ ἐναλίγκιος αὐδήν, “like a god in his voice”, is said nowhere else in the Iliad; in the Odyssey the same words, with the plural θεοῖς, praise the singers Phemius and Demodocus (*Odyssey* 1.371, 9.4; see [[like-a-god-in-his-voice]] and [[heralds]]). When Agamemnon has sworn and cut the boar's throat, “Talthybius flung it into the great gulf of the gray sea, / whirling it around, food for the fishes” (19.267–268; see [[oath]] and [[gray-sea]]). ῥῖψʼ ἐπιδινήσας, “flung … whirling it around”, is what Menelaus did with Paris' empty helmet (3.377–378). The victim of an oath is not eaten (see the oath's notes on Books 3 and 19).

**Briseis.** In Book 1 Talthybius and Eurybates fetched Briseis from Achilles' hut, and Achilles told them, “You are not to blame in my eyes, but Agamemnon” (1.335; see [[taking-of-briseis]] and [[eurybates]]). Now Talthybius holds the victim over which Agamemnon swears that she remained untouched in his huts (19.261–263; see [[briseis]]), and the king has said of himself, “I am not to blame, / but Zeus and Fate and the Erinys” (19.86–87; see [[who-is-to-blame]]). Eurybates is not named in the book.
