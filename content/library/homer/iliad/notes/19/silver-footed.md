---
summary: “Silver-footed Thetis” answers her son's fear for Patroclus' body and promises to keep the flies from it.
---

“Then the goddess silver-footed Thetis answered him: / ‘My child, do not let these things trouble your mind. / From him I will try to ward off the savage tribes, / the flies, which feed on men slain in battle’” (19.28–31; [[thetis]], [[slain-in-battle]], [[flies]]). The standing epithet that marked each stage of her journey for the armor in Book 18 now introduces her care for the dead Patroclus (19.29–39; [[red]]).
