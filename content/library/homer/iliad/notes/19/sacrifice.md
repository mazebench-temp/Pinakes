summary: The one victim killed in Book 19 is the boar of Agamemnon's oath, made ready “to sacrifice to Zeus and the Sun” (19.197). Its first hairs are cut and its throat, but it is not offered: Talthybius flings it into the sea, “food for the fishes” (19.250–268).

ταμέειν, “to sacrifice” (19.197), is literally to cut, the verb of the oath-offerings (ὅρκια πιστὰ τάμωμεν, 19.191). ἀπὸ τρίχας ἀρξάμενος, “cutting the first hairs” (19.254), is the word of the first-fruits of a sacrifice, the hairs offered as the beginning of the rite (Walter Leaf, *Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 19.254; see [[hair]]). The rite stops there, as with the lambs of Book 3: there is no altar, no fire, no portion for the gods and no meal, and the victim is thrown away (see [[oath]] and [[sacrifice-type-scene]]). It is the only killing of an animal in a book that is full of talk of food, and nobody eats it.
