---
summary: In the Tusculan Disputations Cicero turns four lines of Odysseus' answer to Achilles into Latin verse, as Homer's witness that circumstances can put an end to grief, and so that grief is a matter of opinion and will.
---

- Odysseus' rule, “we must bury the man who dies, / having a pitiless heart, after weeping for him a single day” (19.228–229), in Latin hexameters, *Quo magis est aequum tumulis mandare peremptos / Firmo animo et luctum lacrimis finire diurnis* (*Tusculan Disputations* 3.65, on 19.226–229): [[cicero-on-a-day-of-mourning]] and [[weep-for-a-day-and-bury]].
