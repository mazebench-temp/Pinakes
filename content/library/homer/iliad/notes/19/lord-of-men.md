---
summary: Agamemnon, “lord of men,” comes last to the assembly with his wound, speaks from his seat, and answers Odysseus; Odysseus bids him bring the gifts, and Achilles twice addresses him in the full verse of his titles.
---

**The assembly.** “And last of all came Agamemnon, lord of men, / with his wound” (19.51–52; [[bronze-fitted]]), and after Achilles has spoken, “And among them spoke also Agamemnon, lord of men, / from where he sat, and not standing up in their midst” (19.76–77; [[agamemnon]]). The title of the commander introduces the speech in which he admits his blindness (19.78–144; [[ate]]).

**The gifts.** Odysseus proposes that “Agamemnon, lord of men, bring the gifts / into the middle of the assembly” (19.172–173; [[the-gifts-of-agamemnon]]), and “Agamemnon, lord of men,” agrees (19.184).

**Achilles' address.** Twice Achilles answers him with the whole verse “Most glorious son of Atreus, Agamemnon, lord of men” (19.146, 19.199), the courteous verse of Nestor, Odysseus and Diomedes in Books 2, 9 and 10. In Book 1 his first words to Agamemnon had been “Most glorious son of Atreus, most covetous of all men” (1.122; [[most-glorious]]).
