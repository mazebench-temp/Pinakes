---
summary: Briseis' brothers “all met their day of destruction,” and Xanthus tells Achilles that “the day of your destruction is near.”
---

**Briseis' brothers.** “and my three brothers, whom one mother bore with me, / dearly loved, who all met their day of destruction” (19.293–294; [[briseis]], [[kasignetos]]).

**Achilles.** “Yes, we will save you still, this time at least, mighty Achilles; / but the day of your destruction is near; nor are we / to blame, but a great god and mighty Fate” (19.408–410; [[xanthus-the-horse]], [[obrimos]], [[mighty-fate]], [[death-of-achilles-foretold]]). The phrase, which Homer uses only in this book, is spoken of the dead of Briseis' city and of Achilles himself, who sacked it (19.295–296).
