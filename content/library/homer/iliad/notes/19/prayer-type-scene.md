summary: Agamemnon's oath is spoken as a prayer: “he lifted up his hands to Zeus / and prayed”, and, looking up into the wide sky, he calls Zeus, Earth, the Sun and the Erinyes to witness (19.254–265). No claim is made and no answer is told.
refs: [19.254-265]
passages:
  - 19.254-265 | Agamemnon's oath
---

1. **Gesture.** The hands lifted to Zeus, χεῖρας ἀνασχών, as in the oath of Book 3 (19.254; 3.275), and the gaze “looking up into the wide sky” (19.257), in the half-verse of Menelaus' complaint to Zeus and of the soldiers' prayers in Book 7, at the lots and when Ajax arms (3.364; 7.178, 7.201).
2. **Invocation.** Witnesses, not a giver: Zeus “highest and best of the gods”, Earth, the Sun and the Erinyes (19.258–260; see [[oath-formulas]]).
3. **Claim.** None.
4. **Request.** In its place the terms, and a curse on himself if he swears falsely (19.261–265).
5. **Answer.** None is told. The narrator turns at once to the cutting of the boar's throat (19.266; see [[sacrifice-type-scene]] and [[oath]]).
