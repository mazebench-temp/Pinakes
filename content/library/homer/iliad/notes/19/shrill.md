---
summary: Briseis throws herself on Patroclus' body “and wailed shrilly”; the book adds λίγʼ ἐκώκυε.
grc: ["λίγʼ ἐκώκυε"]
en: [shrilly]
---

“threw herself around him and wailed shrilly, and with her hands she tore / her breasts and her tender neck and her beautiful face” (19.284–285; [[briseis]], [[lament]], [[tender]]). λίγα, the neuter of λιγύς, is the clear, piercing cry of mourning women; in Book 18 Thetis wailed “shrilly” with another adjective, ὀξύ (18.71; [[sharp]]). The phrase λίγʼ ἐκώκυε is new to the entry. Athena's bird later in the book is λιγύφωνος, “shrill-voiced,” from the same root (19.350; [[shrill-voiced]]).
