---
summary: Achilles, putting a stop to his anger, asks Agamemnon to “urge the long-haired Achaeans on to war.”
---

“Now I put a stop to my anger; there is no need for me / to rage stubbornly forever; but come, quickly / urge the long-haired Achaeans on to war” (19.67–69; [[cholos]], [[achaeans]], [[agamemnon]]). The man who withdrew from the war asks its commander to rouse the army, and the formula κάρη κομόωντας Ἀχαιούς names the men he has let die (19.61–62).
