---
summary: As the Achaeans arm, “ash spears” pour out of the ships with the helmets, the shields and the corselets.
---

“so then, thick and fast, the helmets, shining brightly, / were carried out from the ships, and bossed shields / and corselets with strong plates and ash spears” (19.359–361; [[bossed]], [[with-strong-plates]]). The spears close the list of arms that stream from the ships like snowflakes before the North Wind (19.357–358; [[born-of-the-clear-sky]]). The plural μείλινα δοῦρα is the one the Locrians lacked in Book 13 (13.715). Walter Leaf takes 19.361 for a late addition, betrayed by its awkward position (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 19.361; see the translation's note on 19.361). Achilles' own spear, drawn from its case a few verses later, is the Pelian ash (19.387–391; [[the-pelian-ash]]).
