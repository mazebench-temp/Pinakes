summary: The language of appeasing returns from the embassy and is carried out. Agamemnon repeats the verse of Book 9, “I am willing to make amends and give a boundless ransom” (19.138 = 9.120); Odysseus asks that Achilles' heart be “gracious” (ἵλαος, 19.178) and that Agamemnon “make amends” with a feast (19.179), and he ends with the maxim that a king may make amends (19.182–183).
grc: [ἀρεσάσθω, ἀπαρέσσασθαι]
---

ἀρέσκομαι, “make amends”, is said of a man as it is said of a god appeased, and ἵλαος is the word for a god made gracious (1.583; see the note on Book 9). In Book 9 Ajax asked Achilles to make his heart gracious, and he did not (9.639); here Odysseus asks it of a man who has already renounced his wrath (19.67, 19.178). The amends are gifts, an oath and a feast (see [[amends-and-reconciliation]], [[the-gifts-of-agamemnon]], [[apoina]] and [[no-blame-for-a-king-to-make-amends]]).
