---
summary: Odysseus calls Achilles “by far the strongest of the Achaeans” and claims for himself the better understanding (19.216–219); no other of the Achaeans can wield Achilles' father's spear (19.388–389); and Odysseus chooses for the gifts “young men, the best of all the Achaeans” (19.193).
refs: [19.193, 19.216-219, 19.388-389]
---

The verse with which Odysseus begins is the one with which Patroclus began his plea for the armor (19.216 = 16.21), and Odysseus divides the praise as a speaker must who disagrees: the spear to Achilles, the mind to himself, “since I was born first and know more” (19.218–219; see [[metis]] and [[noos]]). The quarrel that began over who was best ends with the best of the Achaeans paid in gifts (see [[aristos]] and [[the-gifts-of-agamemnon]]).
