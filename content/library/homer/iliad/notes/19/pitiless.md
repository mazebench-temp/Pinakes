---
summary: Odysseus says that the dead must be buried by men “having a pitiless heart, after weeping for him a single day”; the book adds νηλέα θυμόν.
grc: ["νηλέα θυμὸν"]
---

“But we must bury the man who dies, / having a pitiless heart, after weeping for him a single day” (19.228–229; [[odysseus]], [[thymos]]). The word that Phoenix, Ajax and Patroclus used in reproach of Achilles (9.497, 9.632, 16.33) is here a counsel: the living must harden their hearts to mourn and then eat and fight. Achilles, who will not eat while Patroclus lies unburied, does not take the counsel (19.303–308). Cicero, translating 19.226–229 into Latin verse, renders the phrase *firmo animo*, “with a firm heart” (*Tusculan Disputations* 3.65; see the translation's note on 19.225–229). The phrase νηλέα θυμόν is new to the entry.
