---
summary: Odysseus and Achilles argue over “the sons of the Achaeans,” whether they should fight fasting; and “the other young men of the Achaeans” carry Agamemnon's gifts; the book adds κούρητες Ἀχαιῶν.
grc: ["κούρητες Ἀχαιῶν"]
---

**The army.** Odysseus: “do not urge the sons of the Achaeans to Ilios fasting / to fight with the Trojans” (19.156–157; [[odysseus]]). Achilles answers with the same formula: “I / would now bid the sons of the Achaeans fight / fasting and unfed” (19.205–207; [[achilles]]). The two speakers argue in the same words over the same men.

**The young men.** “and with him the other young men of the Achaeans carried the gifts” (19.248; [[the-gifts-of-agamemnon]]). κούρητες Ἀχαιῶν stands at the end of the verse where the formula's variant κοῦροι Ἀχαιῶν stands (1.473, 3.82); κούρητες means κοῦροι, “young men,” a form Walter Leaf counts among the peculiarities of this book (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 19.193); it is not the people the Curetes (see the translation's note on 19.193, 19.248; [[curetes]]). Strabo quotes the verse, in the form δῶρα φέρον κούρητες Ἀχαιοί, to show that Homer called young soldiers κούρητες (*Geography* 10.3.8).
