---
summary: Zeus boasts that “Eileithyia, who brings hard birth,” will bring a lord into the light that day; Hera holds back the Eileithyiai from Alcmene.
---

“Today Eileithyia, who brings hard birth, will bring into the light / a man who will be lord over all who dwell around him” (19.103–104; [[eileithyia]], [[zeus]]). Zeus' boast names the goddess of the birth he expects, Heracles'; Hera turns it by hastening another birth and holding back the goddesses: “and she stopped Alcmene's childbirth, and held back the Eileithyiai” (19.119; [[hera]], [[alcmene]]). The verse-end μογοστόκος Εἰλείθυια told the birth of Eudorus in Book 16 (16.187); in Book 11 the plural Eileithyiai are the daughters of Hera (11.270–271). Walter Leaf compares the *Homeric Hymn to Apollo* (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 19.119), where Hera keeps “Eileithyia, who brings hard birth,” on Olympus while Leto is in labor (*Homeric Hymn to Apollo* 97–101).
