---
summary: Death fills Achilles' speeches. He will not eat “while my companion is dead” (19.210); he thinks of Peleus as perhaps “altogether / dead” (19.334–335); he bids his horses not to leave him dead “as you left Patroclus” (19.403), and answers Xanthus, “why do you prophesy death for me?” (19.420). Briseis finds Patroclus dead (19.289), and Achilles says that Zeus “wanted death to come upon many of the Achaeans” (19.274).
---

The verb is often the perfect, τεθνηώς, “dead”, the state that follows the moment of dying (19.210, 19.289, 19.335, 19.403). See [[death-of-achilles-foretold]] and [[the-short-life-of-achilles]].
