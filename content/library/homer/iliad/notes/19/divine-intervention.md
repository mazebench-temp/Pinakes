---
summary: The gods act on bodies and voices. Thetis keeps Patroclus' flesh sound and puts might into her son (19.29–39); Athena, sent by Zeus, drips nectar and ambrosia into the breast of the fasting Achilles (19.340–354); Hera gives his horse a human voice, and the Erinyes stop it (19.407, 19.418). Agamemnon lays his folly on Zeus, Fate and the Erinys (19.86–90).
refs: [19.37-39, 19.340-354, 19.407, 19.418]
---

Athena comes down “like a long-winged, shrill-voiced bird of prey” while the Achaeans arm, feeds Achilles without a word, and goes back to her father's house (19.350–356; see [[athena]] and [[fasting]]); Agamemnon's gods acted unseen, in his mind (19.88; see [[ate]]). The horse speaks by a god's gift and falls silent by the Erinyes' act, and Walter Leaf notes that Aristarchus rejected the first verse because the god who gave the voice should have taken it away (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 19.407; see [[prophecy]] and [[xanthus-the-horse]]).
