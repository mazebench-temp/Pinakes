---
summary: Achilles tells his mother, “terribly / I fear” that flies will breed worms in Patroclus' wounds while he fights (19.23–27). The Myrmidons tremble at the sight of the armor and dare not look at it (19.14–15).
---

The fear is for the body, not for himself, and Thetis answers it (19.29–33; see [[flies]] and [[honoring-the-dead]]). The Myrmidons' fear is the fear of a god's work: “trembling seized all the Myrmidons, and no one dared / look at it face to face, but they fled in fear” (19.14–15; τρόμος, ἔτρεσαν; see [[the-making-of-the-arms]]).
