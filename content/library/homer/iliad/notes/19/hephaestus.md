summary: Hephaestus does not appear, but his work is in every scene of Achilles. Thetis brings “the gifts from the god” at dawn, the armor rings as she sets it down, and Achilles judges it fit for the works of immortals; at the arming he puts on “the gifts of the god, which Hephaestus had toiled to make”, and the golden plumes that Hephaestus set about the crest wave over his helmet.

**The gifts of the god.** Thetis comes to the ships “bringing the gifts from the god” (θεοῦ πάρα δῶρα, 19.3), and she names the giver as she hands them over: “but you, receive this glorious armor from Hephaestus” (19.10; see [[thetis]]). Ἡφαίστοιο πάρα keeps the words of her promise, “bringing fine armor from lord Hephaestus” (παρʼ Ἡφαίστοιο ἄνακτος, 18.137), and of her flight down from Olympus, “carrying the gleaming armor from Hephaestus” (18.617; see [[the-making-of-the-arms]]). As she sets it down, “all its intricate designs rang” (19.13). The δαίδαλα are the god's work on the shield, the “many intricate designs” he made “with his cunning skill” (18.482; see [[the-shield-of-achilles]] and [[with-cunning-skill]]), and Achilles delights in holding them, until he has had his fill of gazing on the designs (19.18–19). Then he gives his verdict:

Hephaestus had promised Thetis armor “such that many a man / of the many men will marvel at it, whoever sees it” (18.466–467). The Myrmidons do not marvel; they cannot look at it, and flee in fear (19.14–15; see [[myrmidons]]).

**The arming.** Achilles arms in the midst of the army as it pours out from the ships (19.356–364): “and raging against the Trojans / he put on the gifts of the god, which Hephaestus had toiled to make” (19.367–368; see [[arming-scene]] and [[achilles]]). Ἥφαιστος κάμε τεύχων ends the verse of Agamemnon's scepter, “the scepter that Hephaestus had toiled to make” (2.101; see [[the-scepter-of-agamemnon]]), and of the corselet of Diomedes (8.195); Tychius, a mortal craftsman, “had toiled to make” Ajax's shield (7.220; see [[tychius]]). Aristarchus athetized 19.365–368. Walter Leaf keeps the lines, but suspects that 368 was altered after the Making of the Arms was added to the poem (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 19.365; see the translation's note on 19.365–368). The pieces go on in the order of the arming verses of Book 3 (19.369–373 = 3.330–332, 334–335): greaves, corselet, sword, shield, “the fine shield, intricately worked” (19.380), and the helmet, around which “waved the plumes / of gold, which Hephaestus had set thick about the crest” (19.382–383; see [[helmet]]). That is the golden crest he set on it in Book 18 (18.612). The two verses recur when Achilles charges Hector (22.315–316), and Leaf takes 383 in both places for an addition meant to recall the Making of the Arms (on 19.382; see the translation's note on 19.382–383). The armor fits: “it was like wings to him, and it lifted the shepherd of the people” (19.386; see [[like-wings]]).

**What he did not make.** The spear is not his. Achilles draws from its case “his father's spear”, the Pelian ash that Chiron gave to Peleus (19.387–391; see [[the-pelian-ash]] and [[chiron]]), the one weapon of Achilles that Patroclus left behind, since no other Achaean could wield it (16.140–144). Hephaestus made a shield, a corselet, a helmet and greaves (18.478–613), and Book 19 arms Achilles in the god's work and his father's ash. In Book 20 the shield proves itself when its layer of gold, the god's gift, stops the spear of Aeneas (20.264–272; see [[aeneas]]).
