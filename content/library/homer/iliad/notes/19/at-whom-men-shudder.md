---
summary: Achilles, lamenting Patroclus, says he is fighting the Trojans “for the sake of Helen, at whom men shudder.”
---

“who now, I think, in Phthia is letting his tender tears fall / for lack of such a son; while I, in a foreign land, / for the sake of Helen, at whom men shudder, am fighting the Trojans” (19.323–325; [[helen]], [[peleus]], [[tender]]). Achilles sets the cause of the war beside the father grieving at home. In Book 9 he had asked whether the war was not fought “for the sake of Helen of the lovely hair” (9.339; [[lovely-haired]]); now, with Patroclus dead, her epithet is one of horror.
