---
summary: When the Achaeans arm, their helmets, shields, corselets and spears pour out of the ships as thick and fast as snowflakes from Zeus, cold, under the rush of the North Wind, “born of the clear sky”.
---

“And as when snowflakes fly thick and fast from Zeus, / cold, under the rush of the North Wind, born of the clear sky, / so then, thick and fast, the helmets, shining brightly, / were carried out from the ships” (19.357–360; see [[snowflakes-from-zeus]] and [[born-of-the-clear-sky]]). The second verse is the one in which Iris flew down from Ida in Book 15, as swiftly as snow or hail “under the rush of the North Wind, born of the clear sky” (19.358 ≈ 15.171, with ψυχραί for ψυχρή; see [[snow-and-hail]] and the note on Book 15). There the snow is a figure of speed; here it is a figure of number, the arms of a whole army coming out of the ships at once.
