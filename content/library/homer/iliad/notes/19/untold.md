---
summary: Had Briseis died at Lyrnessus, Achilles says, not so many Achaeans “would have bitten the boundless ground with their teeth.”
---

“then not so many Achaeans would have bitten the boundless ground with their teeth / at the hands of their enemies, while I nursed my wrath apart” (19.61–62; [[menis]], [[achilles]]). The phrase of dying men, ὀδὰξ ἕλον οὖδας, “bit the ground with their teeth,” is Nestor's in Book 11 (11.749); here the ground is ἄσπετον, rendered “boundless,” and the dead are the Achaeans whom Achilles' anger cost.
