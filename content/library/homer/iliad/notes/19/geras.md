---
summary: Agamemnon names the act that began the quarrel: “on the day when I myself took away the prize of Achilles” (19.89). The prize comes back among the gifts, Briseis the eighth of the women, sworn untouched (19.246, 19.261–263).
---

αὐτός, “I myself”, is Agamemnon taking the act on himself, on his own authority, as Achilles said of it in Book 1: “he has taken my prize and keeps it, having seized it himself” (1.356; Walter Leaf, *Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 19.89). In the same breath he lays the cause on Zeus, Fate and the Erinys (19.86–88; see [[ate]]). The prize is returned with the gifts that make amends for taking it (see [[briseis]], [[the-gifts-of-agamemnon]] and [[taking-of-briseis]]).
