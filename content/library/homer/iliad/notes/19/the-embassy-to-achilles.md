summary: Agamemnon offers again “all the gifts that, coming to you / yesterday in your huts, brilliant Odysseus promised”, and sends Odysseus for “all that we promised Achilles / yesterday” (19.140–141, 19.194–195). The embassy is remembered as Odysseus' promise; the gifts and the oath it carried are delivered, and Achilles, who refused them then, now lets them come.
refs: [19.140-141, 19.194-195]
---

**“Yesterday.”** Agamemnon names the gifts by the night of the embassy: “all the gifts that, coming to you / yesterday in your huts, brilliant Odysseus promised” (19.140–141), and again, to Odysseus, “all that we promised Achilles / yesterday that we would give” (19.194–195; see [[the-gifts-of-agamemnon]]). The embassy came in the night after the second day of battle, and a whole day of battle and a night have passed since, the day of Patroclus' death (see [[the-third-day-of-battle]]). Walter Leaf reckons χθιζός here as “really ‘the day before yesterday’”, though the embassy was late at night, and thinks the slip too slight to bear the theories built on it: that the heroic Greeks counted the day from sunset, or, as Bergk argued, that before the making of the armor was added Achilles killed Hector on the day of Patroclus' death (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 19.141). Leaf finds much in favor of rejecting 137–141 as lines added to join this book to Book 9, but the second “yesterday” (19.195) stands outside them (on 19.136; see the translation's note on 19.136–141).

**The promise and the envoy.** In Book 9 Odysseus began the list with “all the gifts that Agamemnon promised you in his huts” (9.263); here Agamemnon gives the promise to Odysseus, in the same words turned round: ἐν κλισίῃσιν ὑπέσχετο δῶρʼ Ἀγαμέμνων (9.263), ἐνὶ κλισίῃσιν ὑπέσχετο δῖος Ὀδυσσεύς (19.141). The huts are now Achilles', where the envoys found him. Odysseus is again the go-between. He proposes the order in which the gifts are to be given, leads the young men who carry them out of Agamemnon's hut, and weighs out the gold (19.172–174, 19.238–248; see [[odysseus]]). The oath that Agamemnon promised with Briseis is proposed again in the verses in which Odysseus passed the promise on that night (19.176–177 = 9.275–276), and sworn in wider terms (19.258–265; see [[oath]]).

**Refused, then taken.** On the night of the embassy Achilles would not be persuaded by any gifts “until he has paid me back for all his heart-grieving insult” (9.387). Now he says that the gifts are Agamemnon's to give or to keep (19.147–148); they are given, and his Myrmidons carry them to his ship (19.277–281; see [[achilles]]). Phoenix had told him to come “while the gifts are offered”, for if he went into the war without them, he would no longer be held in the same honor (9.602–605; see [[phoenix]] and [[the-wrath-of-meleager]]). Phoenix is among the six who stay with him after the assembly (19.311).

**The elders again.** After the gifts the elders of the Achaeans gather round Achilles, “begging him to take his meal” (λισσόμενοι, 19.303–304; see [[elders]]). λίσσομαι is the word of the embassy: Agamemnon “has sent the best men to entreat you” (9.520), and Thetis remembers it as “the elders begged him” (18.448). The elders who begged him to fight now beg him to eat, and he refuses them too (19.305–308; see [[supplication]]).
