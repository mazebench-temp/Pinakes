---
summary: In Agamemnon's story “sharp grief” strikes Zeus; Patroclus lies “torn by the sharp bronze” in Achilles' words and before Briseis' eyes, as her husband once lay; and Odysseus bids the army “rouse sharp Ares.”
---

**Grief.** “So she spoke, and sharp grief struck him deep in his heart” (19.125; [[zeus]], [[ate]]): Zeus hears from Hera that Eurystheus, not his own son, has been born to rule.

**The bronze.** The half-verse δεδαϊγμένος ὀξέϊ χαλκῷ, used in Book 18 of Patroclus on the litter (18.236), returns three times. Achilles refuses food while his companion “lies in my hut, torn by the sharp bronze, / turned toward the doorway” (19.211–212); Briseis throws herself on Patroclus “when she saw Patroclus torn by the sharp bronze” (19.283); and in her lament she remembers her husband, whom “I saw before the city, torn by the sharp bronze” (19.292; [[briseis]], [[patroclus]]). The same words join the two men she has seen dead.

**Ares.** Odysseus ends his counsel: “but rushing on all together / let us rouse sharp Ares against the horse-taming Trojans” (19.236–237; [[ares]], [[tamer-of-horses]]). The verse is his own from Book 4, where he answered Agamemnon's charge that he held back from war (4.351–352).
