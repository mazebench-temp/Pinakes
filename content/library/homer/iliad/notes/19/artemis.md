---
summary: Renouncing his wrath before the assembly, Achilles wishes that Artemis had killed Briseis with an arrow among the ships on the day he took her, when he destroyed Lyrnessus; then not so many Achaeans would have died while he nursed his wrath apart.
---

Achilles opens the assembly by asking Agamemnon whether their strife over a girl has done either of them any good (19.56–58; see [[achilles]], [[quarrel-of-achilles-and-agamemnon]] and [[the-renunciation-of-wrath]]):

{{quote:69f565ae-5c56-591c-b8c8-97bd92d55920}}

Artemis' arrow is the sudden death of women, as Apollo's is of men: she killed Laodameia, the mother of Sarpedon, in her anger (6.205), and struck down Andromache's mother in her father's halls (6.428; see [[of-the-showering-arrows]]), and in Book 21 Hera will tell her that Zeus has made her a lion to women and allowed her to kill whom she will (21.483–484). Achilles wishes on Briseis that quick death, on “the day when I took her, when I destroyed Lyrnessus” (see [[briseis]], [[lyrnessus]] and [[the-sack-of-lyrnessus]]). ὄφελʼ … ἤματι τῷ ὅτε, “I wish … on the day when”, is the form of Helen's wish that a storm had carried her away on the day her mother bore her (6.345–348; see [[helen]]). In Book 9 he had said that he loved Briseis from his heart, though she was won by his spear (9.342–343); now he would rather she had died than cost so many Achaeans their lives. The girl he wishes dead comes back to his huts in this book with Agamemnon's gifts, “like golden Aphrodite”, and laments over Patroclus (19.246, 19.282–300; see [[aphrodite]]).
