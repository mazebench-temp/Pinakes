---
summary: Sunset in Book 19 is the hour of the meal, and the end of a fast that Achilles sets himself. Odysseus says that no man can fight unfed all day “until the sun goes down” (19.162); Achilles would send the army out unfed and make ready a great supper “as the sun goes down”, when they have avenged the insult (19.207–208), and he will eat nothing “until the sun goes down” (19.308).
grc: ["δύντα δʼ ἐς ἠέλιον"]
en: [the sun goes down]
---

The first two phrases are formulas of the end of the day. πρόπαν ἦμαρ ἐς ἠέλιον καταδύντα, “all day long, until the sun goes down” (19.162), is the measure of the gods' feast in Book 1 (1.601); ἅμα δʼ ἠελίῳ καταδύντι, “as the sun goes down” (19.207), is the hour of the beacon fires in a simile of Book 18 (18.210; see [[feast]]). The third, δύντα δʼ ἐς ἠέλιον (19.308), occurs only here. The day that dawns at the start of the book does not end in it (see [[dawn-line]] and [[fasting]]).
