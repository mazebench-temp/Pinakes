summary: The Aeneid turns Menelaus' insult in Book 7 against the Trojans, in the mouth of the Italian Numanus, and models its truce for the dead on the truce of Book 7, when Trojans and Latins cut wood side by side and burn their dead.
---

- Numanus: “O truly Phrygian women, for you are not Phrygian men” (*Aeneid* 9.598–620), and Ascanius' first kill: [[virgil-numanus]].
- The Latin envoys, Aeneas' truce of twelve days, and the burning of the dead (*Aeneid* 11.100–138, 11.182–212): [[virgil-truce-for-the-dead]].
- Hypsipyle in Apollonius, one of the models for Dido: [[apollonius-lemnos]].
