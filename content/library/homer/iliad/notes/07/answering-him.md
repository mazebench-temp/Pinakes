---
summary: The formula comes once in Book 7, for Ajax's answer to the herald Idaeus (7.283). Hector's two speeches to Ajax, before the fight and after it, are brought in by the same verse of reply “in turn” (7.233, 7.287), and twice a man rises at last to break a silence (7.94, 7.399).
---

**Answering him.** “Answering him, Telamonian Ajax said” (7.283). The herald has told the two men to stop, and Ajax answers by passing the decision to the man who made the challenge: “Idaeus, bid Hector say these things” (7.284–286; see [[heralds]] and [[single-combat]]).

**In turn.** “Then great Hector of the gleaming helmet spoke to him in turn” (τὸν δʼ αὖτε προσέειπε μέγας κορυθαίολος Ἕκτωρ) brings in both of Hector's speeches to Ajax (7.233, 7.287), and it brought in his answer to Andromache in Book 6 (6.440). The frame is the same both times, and the speeches are opposites. Before the duel Hector warns Ajax not to test him “as if I were some feeble boy or a woman” (7.235–236; see [[like-a-boy-or-a-woman]]); after it he praises the stature, strength and good sense that a god has given him and proposes that they part (7.288–302; see [[hector]]). The gods of the book answer each other in the same frame, as Athena and Apollo plan the duel (7.33, 7.37).

**At last.** Twice in the book a crowd falls silent and a man rises to answer at last: “At last Menelaus stood up and spoke among them” after Hector's challenge (7.94), and “At last Diomedes, good at the war cry, spoke among them” after Idaeus' message (7.399). The second verse returns twice in Book 9, each time for Diomedes breaking a silence (9.31, 9.696; see [[silence]] and [[diomedes]]).

**Other frames.** Paris answers Antenor “with winged words” (7.356), the only winged words of the book (see [[winged-words]]). Zeus answers Poseidon “deeply troubled” (μέγʼ ὀχθήσας, 7.454; see [[deeply-troubled]]).
