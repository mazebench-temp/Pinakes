---
summary: The Achaeans are “bronze-armored” twice in Book 7: when their herald comes to part the champions, and when the gods look down on the great work of their wall.
---

The heralds come “one from the Trojans and one from the bronze-armored Achaeans” (7.275), in a verse that sets the two sides side by side at the moment the duel is stopped ([[heralds]], [[messengers-of-zeus]]). The second use is in the scene among the gods: they sit beside Zeus and gaze at “the great work of the bronze-armored Achaeans” (7.444; [[lord-of-the-lightning]], [[the-achaean-wall]]). The army is named by its armor when its work is not fighting but building, and Poseidon objects to what he sees (7.446–453). See [[achaeans]].
