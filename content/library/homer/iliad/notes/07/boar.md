---
summary: The boars of Book 7 are in the couplet of the duel. Hector and Ajax fall upon each other “like lions that eat raw flesh or wild boars, whose strength is no weakling's” (7.256–257).
---

ἢ συσὶ κάπροισιν, τῶν τε σθένος οὐκ ἀλαπαδνόν, “or wild boars, whose strength is no weakling's” (7.257), is the line of Book 5 (5.783). There the Argives stood crowded around Diomedes, and the boar was the beast at bay, that waits for the hunters and turns on them. Here the two champions charge each other, and the boar attacks (see [[lions-and-boars]] and [[lion]]).

The comparison suits both men. Ajax will go through the champions over Patroclus' body “like a boar in courage” (17.281–285), in the half-line first given to Idomeneus (4.253; see [[like-a-boar]]), and Hector turns among his enemies like a boar or a lion among dogs and hunters (12.41–48). See [[the-duel-of-hector-and-ajax]].
