“And up rose Thoas, son of Andraemon, and brilliant Odysseus” (7.168; see [[odysseus]]). The patronymic Ἀνδραιμονίδης, “son of Andraemon”, occurs only here in Homer; elsewhere he is Ἀνδραίμονος υἱός (2.638, 15.281; see [[andraemon]]). The leader of the Aetolians stands with the greatest of the Achaeans, and Book 15 shows why he belongs among them: he is Αἰτωλῶν ὄχʼ ἄριστος, far the best of the Aetolians, skilled with the javelin and brave in the standing fight, and few of the Achaeans could beat him in the assembly (15.282–284; see [[aetolians]]). The lot falls to Ajax, and Thoas has no other part in the book.
