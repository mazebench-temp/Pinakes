{{quote:e5e698a5-f8cb-58d8-be86-2e911e62618d}}

Ajax's last two lines are the lines of the Catalogue (7.229–230 = 2.771–772). There the narrator used them to picture the withdrawal from outside, just after naming Ajax the best of the men “while Achilles kept up his wrath” (2.768–769; see [[best-of-the-achaeans]]). Here the man who stands in Achilles' place says them himself, to Hector's face. καὶ μετʼ Ἀχιλλῆα, “even after Achilles” (7.228), means next after him in rank, or even without him now that he has withdrawn (see the translation's note on 7.228 and [[ajax]]).

Agamemnon measures Hector by the absent man. Holding Menelaus back, he says that “even Achilles, in the battle where men win glory, shudders to meet this man” (7.113–114). Achilles tells it differently: while he fought, Hector would not come out beyond the Scaean gates and the oak, and once barely escaped him there (9.352–355). Agamemnon's claim is usually taken as an exaggeration meant to keep his brother out of the fight (see the translation's note on 7.114 and [[achilles]]).

The book is shaped by the absence. The Achaeans have no answer to Hector's challenge until Nestor shames them (7.92–169), and the wall they build is, in Achilles' own words, what Agamemnon has had to do “without me” (9.348–352; see [[the-building-of-the-wall]]).
