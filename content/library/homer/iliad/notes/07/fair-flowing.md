ἐΰρροος, “fair-flowing,” is the river's epithet in a verse of slaughter. The Scamander is the river of the battlefield, and the dead of the day's fighting lie about it (7.329; [[scamander]], [[dark-blood]]). The adjective returns for the same river when Achilles, killing Trojans in its stream, tells them that not even the fair-flowing, silver-eddying river will save them (ἐΰρροος ἀργυροδίνης, 21.130–132), and the river rises against him; the ford of the Scamander is “the ford of the fair-flowing river” (ἐϋρρεῖος ποταμοῖο, 14.433, 21.1, 24.692). Nestor's answer to the dead on its banks is the truce to burn them (7.331–335; [[the-truce-for-the-dead]]).
