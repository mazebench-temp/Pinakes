---
summary: Eurypylus rises among the volunteers as “the splendid son of Euaemon,” and the army prays that Ajax may “win a splendid triumph.”
---

The phrase of the Catalogue returns for Eurypylus: “after them Eurypylus, the splendid son of Euaemon” (7.167), the words that named him when he led the men of Ormenius (2.736; [[eurypylus]], [[euaemon]]).

In the army's prayer the adjective describes the prize: “grant victory to Ajax, and that he win a splendid triumph” (7.203). εὖχος is the triumph a victor claims, and the translation keeps it apart from κῦδος, “glory,” which the next verse asks for both men alike (7.205; [[euchomai]], [[kydos]]). Hector used the same noun when he imagined Apollo giving him triumph (7.81), and Nestor when he told how Athena gave him his (7.154). See [[the-duel-of-hector-and-ajax]].
