---
summary: The Argives are killed in the fighting that opens the book, rejoice at the sight of Ajax going out against Hector, and fetch their dead and wood for the pyres at the truce. Nestor recalls how old Peleus rejoiced to learn “the lineage and birth of all the Argives” (7.128).
---

Athena comes down from Olympus when she sees Hector, Paris and Glaucus “killing the Argives in the fierce battle” (7.17–18; see [[athena]]). Nestor rises “among the Argives” to shame them (7.123; see [[nestor]]), and remembers how Peleus questioned him in his house “to learn the lineage and birth of all the Argives” (7.127–128; see [[peleus]]). When Ajax goes out to fight, “the Argives rejoiced as they looked at him” (7.214), while the Trojans trembled (7.215; see [[ajax]]). At the truce “the Argives on the other side, from their well-benched ships” go for their dead and for wood (7.419–420; see [[the-truce-for-the-dead]]).

The adjective describes Helen once more, “Argive Helen” (7.350; see [[argive-helen]]), in Antenor's proposal to give her back, and Paris says that he brought the possessions “from Argos” (7.363; see [[argos]]).
