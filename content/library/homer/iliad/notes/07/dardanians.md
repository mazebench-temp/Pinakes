κέκλυτέ μευ Τρῶες καὶ Δάρδανοι ἠδʼ ἐπίκουροι, “Hear me, Trojans and Dardanians and allies”. Agamemnon used the line to address the whole Trojan side after the duel of Book 3 (3.456). In Book 7 it opens the speeches of Antenor and of Priam to their own assembly (7.348, 7.368), and Hector will use it when he speaks to the army on the plain (8.497; see [[hear-me]], [[trojans]] and [[trojan-allies]]). The three names cover the whole Trojan side: the people of the city, the Dardanians of Aeneas, and the allies who have come to fight for them.

Δαρδανίωνες (7.414, and again at 8.154) is a longer form of the name, built like the patronymic Κρονίων, “son of Cronus”: the Dardanians as descendants of Dardanus. Priam himself is Δαρδανίδης, “descendant of Dardanus” (7.366; see [[dardanus]] and [[priam]]). When Idaeus is due back from the ships, “the Trojans and the Dardanians sat in assembly, all gathered together, waiting for the moment when Idaeus would come” (7.414–416; see [[idaeus]]).
