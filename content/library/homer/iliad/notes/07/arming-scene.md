summary: Two men arm to meet Hector. Menelaus puts on his armor in one verse, and his attendants take it off when Agamemnon holds him back (7.103, 7.122). Ajax arms in two verses, the first and the last of a full arming scene with nothing between, and the one piece described is his shield (7.206–223).

**Menelaus.** Shamed by the army's silence, Menelaus declares, “Against this man I will arm myself” (θωρήξομαι, 7.101), and “put on his fine armor” in a single verse (7.103), as he armed for his duel in Book 3 (3.339). Agamemnon holds him back, and the arming is undone in a single verse too: “his attendants gladly took the armor from his shoulders” (7.122; see [[menelaus]]).

**Ajax.** Ajax asks the army to pray to Zeus “while I put on my armor for war” (7.193). Then his arming is told in two verses: “So they spoke, and Ajax armed himself in gleaming bronze. And when he had put all his armor about his body” (7.206–207). The first verse opens the full arming scene of Patroclus in Book 16, with Patroclus' name (16.130). The second is a closing verse: Hera's dressing ends with the same turn, when she has put all her adornment about her body (14.187), and so do the armings of whole companies (14.383; Od. 24.467). Between the two verses the listener knows the order of the pieces, greaves, corselet, sword, shield, helmet and spear, and the poem does not give it.

**What is given instead.** The scene passes straight to the man in motion: Ajax “hurried forward, as huge Ares goes forth when he goes to war among men”, smiling with a grim face and striding long, and brandishing his long-shadowed spear (7.208–213; see [[like-huge-ares]] and [[ajax]]). And when he comes near Hector, the one piece of his armor that is described gets the history that a full arming gives to one piece: the shield of seven oxhides with its eighth layer of bronze, and the leather-worker of Hyle who made it (7.219–223; see [[the-shield-of-ajax]] and [[tychius]]).
