---
summary: For the first time a Trojan uses the standing name of the war's cause: Antenor proposes to “give Argive Helen, and the possessions with her,” back to the sons of Atreus.
---

{{quote:b71c86cc-1d70-56b9-a7f0-249b19e66c6a}}

Until now the formula has been spoken by gods and Achaeans: by Hera and Athena, who could not bear to leave Argive Helen in Troy (2.160–162, 2.176–178), by Zeus (4.19), and twice by Agamemnon, who at the end of the first duel demanded that the Trojans give back “Argive Helen and the possessions with her” (3.458), the words that Antenor now uses, and who feared, over his wounded brother, that the army would go home and leave Argive Helen to the Trojans as a thing to boast of (4.171–174). The narrator has used it once, of Helen at her work in Paris' house (6.323). The Trojan elder proposes to do what the Achaean king demanded. He had received Menelaus and Odysseus when they came to ask for her before the war (3.205–224), and he gives his reason: the Trojans are fighting with the oaths broken, and nothing good will come of it (7.351–353; [[antenor]], [[oaths-and-their-breaking]]).

Paris refuses to give back the woman, though he will give the possessions (7.357–364), and Diomedes answers the offer for the Achaeans: let no one accept the possessions of Alexander, “nor Helen” (7.400–401). See [[helen]] and [[the-abduction-of-helen]].
