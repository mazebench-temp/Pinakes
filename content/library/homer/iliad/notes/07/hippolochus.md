---
summary: Hippolochus is named only in his son's title, “Glaucus, son of Hippolochus, leader of the Lycian men”, as Glaucus kills Iphinous (7.13).
---

The father appears in the Iliad only as a name in his son's title. The first half of the verse is the one that brought Glaucus out between the armies in Book 6, “And Glaucus, son of Hippolochus, and the son of Tydeus” (6.119); here it ends “leader of the Lycian men”, and Glaucus kills an Achaean (7.13–16; see [[glaucus]] and [[iphinous]]). The whole verse returns in Book 17, when Glaucus rebukes Hector for leaving Sarpedon's body (17.140). Ἱππολόχοιο, “of Hippolochus”, occurs six times, always of Glaucus' father (6.119, 6.144, 7.13, 12.309, 12.387, 17.140). Who Hippolochus was is told only in Glaucus' account of his lineage, as the son of Bellerophon who sent him to Troy (6.196–210; see [[bellerophon]]).
