summary: Nestor sets the fight of his youth by “the swift-flowing Celadon,” where the Pylians and the Arcadians met.

{{quote:6b4ee1ef-0542-5537-acd9-5c854a070e5f}}

The adjective's only other use is in Book 5, of an unnamed river in flood (5.598). The Celadon is named nowhere else in Homer, and its name means “the Roarer” (see the translation's note on 7.135; [[celadon]]). Nestor places his story exactly, with a river, a town and a second river, Pheia and the Iardanus ([[pheia]], [[iardanus]], [[nestor-and-ereuthalion]]). Where the fight took place was already disputed in antiquity. Strabo reports readings that put it on the river Acidon, near Chaa (*Geography* 8.3.21; [[strabo]]). See [[nestor]].
