summary: The long day of the first battle ends at nightfall, which the herald and then Hector give as the reason to stop the duel, “Night is already coming on” (7.282, 7.293). The day of the wall ends as “the sun set, and the work of the Achaeans was finished” (7.465), and the night that follows is filled with feasting and with Zeus' thunder (7.476–482).

**Night ends the duel.** The day that dawned in Book 2 (2.48–49) ends in Book 7, and its last event is stopped by the dark. The herald Idaeus tells Hector and Ajax, “Night is already coming on; it is good to yield to night too” (7.282), and Hector repeats the verse when he proposes that they part (7.293; see [[yield-to-night]], [[heralds]] and [[single-combat]]). The narrator gives no sunset of his own. The coming night is in the speakers' mouths, as a reason that neither side can refuse.

**Sunset on the wall.** The day of the wall ends with a new form of the sunset line: “and the sun set, and the work of the Achaeans was finished” (δύσετο δʼ ἠέλιος, τετέλεστο δὲ ἔργον Ἀχαιῶν, 7.465). The sun and the work end together; the building took the day from before dawn to sunset (7.433–465; see [[dawn-line]] and [[the-achaean-wall]]). In Book 1 the sunset line was followed by sleep (1.475–476). Here it is followed by supper, wine and a feast, and sleep comes only in the last verse of the book (7.466–482).

**All night.** παννύχιοι, “all night long”, is given to the feasters and then to Zeus: the Achaeans feasted all night, and “all night long Zeus the counselor devised evil for them, thundering terribly” (7.476–479; see [[feast]], [[teras]] and [[war-and-night]]). The verse that opens the night, παννύχιοι μὲν ἔπειτα, opens the Myrmidons' night of mourning for Patroclus as well (18.354).
