summary: In Book 7 the Trojans speak of the abduction as a debt they might repay. Paris offers to give back “the possessions that I brought from Argos to our house”, but not Helen (7.362–364), and his herald, reporting the offer, wishes that Paris had perished before he brought them to Troy (7.389–390).
passages:
  - 7.385-393 | Idaeus delivers Paris' offer
---

The abduction took a wife and a house's wealth together (3.70–72; 13.626–627; see [[war-for-menelaus]]). In Book 7 Paris divides them for the first time. He will give back the possessions and add more of his own, but he refuses the woman outright (7.357–364; see [[paris]]). Twice the Trojans name Paris as the man “for whose sake the quarrel arose” (7.374, 7.388).

{{quote:98e7a647-0403-54a2-95bc-46f3e90eeb6c}}

The herald's aside, “I wish he had perished before that!” (ὡς πρὶν ὤφελλʼ ἀπολέσθαι, 7.390), is not in the message Priam gave him, and neither is his remark that the Trojans urge Paris to give Helen back (7.393; see [[idaeus]]). It joins the Trojans' other wishes against Paris: Hector wished he had never been born, or had died unwed (3.40), and wished that the earth would gape for him (6.281–282; see [[may-the-earth-gape]] and [[like-black-doom]]). The Achaeans refuse both the possessions and Helen (7.400–402). In Book 22 Hector will think of offering Achilles “all that Alexander brought to Troy in the hollow ships”, the beginning of the quarrel (22.114–116).
