---
summary: Agamemnon's fuller title marks him twice in Book 7: as he takes Menelaus by the hand to hold him back from Hector, and as he honors Ajax with the chine.
---

“The son of Atreus himself, wide-ruling Agamemnon” holds his brother back from the duel with Hector, taking him by the right hand and calling him by name (7.107–108; [[called-him-by-name]], [[menelaus]]). The title of wide power is given to the king in an act of care for his brother, with advice that the narrator calls “what was due” (7.121).

The second use is a whole verse: “by the hero, son of Atreus, wide-ruling Agamemnon” (7.322). It is the verse that introduced Agamemnon's angry answer to Calchas in Book 1 (1.102). There it opened the quarrel; here it closes the scene in which the king honors the man who fought for the army, giving him the long chine of the ox (7.321–322; [[geras]], [[ajax]]).
