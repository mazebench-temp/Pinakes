ἀγρόμενοι Πύλιοί τε καὶ Ἀρκάδες ἐγχεσίμωροι, “gathered together, the Pylians and the Arcadians, fighters with the spear” (7.134): these are the Pylians of Nestor's youth, not the men of his ninety ships at Troy (2.602). Homer mentions their war with the Arcadians only in this story (see [[arcadians]], [[celadon]], [[pheia]] and [[nestor-and-ereuthalion]]). Their best men “trembled greatly and were afraid, and no one dared” to meet Ereuthalion's challenge (7.150–151; see [[ereuthalion]]), as the Achaeans now sit silent before Hector's. In Nestor's telling the Pylians are the Achaeans of an older war, and he himself, the youngest of them, is the one who answered (see [[nestor]]). His other war story, in Book 11, sets the Pylians against the Epeians of Elis (11.670–761).
