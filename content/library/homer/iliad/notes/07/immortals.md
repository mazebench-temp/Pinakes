---
summary: The gods are “the immortals” four times in Book 7: Apollo's name for the goddesses who want Troy sacked, Menelaus' for the gods who hold the bounds of victory, Nestor's for the gods Peleus would pray to, and Poseidon's for the gods whom mortals no longer consult.
---

Apollo tells Athena that the war will go on until the Achaeans find the end of Ilios, “since this is what has pleased the hearts of you immortal goddesses, to sack this city” (7.31–32). The feminine ἀθανάτῃσι takes in Hera and Athena, the goddesses who hate Troy (4.20–21; [[hera]], [[athena]]).

Menelaus, arming to face Hector, says that victory is not his to give: “above, the bounds of victory are held among the immortal gods” (7.101–102; [[nike]]). Nestor imagines old Peleus, hearing that the Achaeans cower before Hector, lifting his hands “again and again to the immortals” to pray for death (7.129–131; [[peleus]]). And Poseidon asks whether any mortal “on the boundless earth” will still tell his mind and his plans “to the immortals,” now that the Achaeans have built a wall without sacrifice (7.446–450; [[boundless-sea]], [[earth-shaker]]). In the first three the immortals decide what men cannot; in the last, a god complains that men no longer ask them. See [[mortal-men]].
