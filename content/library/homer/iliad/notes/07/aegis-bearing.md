---
summary: The oak on which Athena and Apollo perch to watch the duel belongs to “aegis-bearing father Zeus.”
---

Athena and Apollo have agreed to stop the general battle and to set Hector against a single champion, and they sit down to watch:

{{quote:41c45a50-456a-56e4-a9c8-3fa86088b492}}

The oak near the Scaean gates is Zeus' tree, and the epithet names its owner. Book 5 used the same phrase for it, when Sarpedon's companions laid him down, wounded, “under the beautiful oak of aegis-bearing Zeus” (5.693; [[the-oak-of-zeus]]). Zeus himself does not appear in the scene, but the two gods who have planned the duel watch it from his tree, and the heralds who end it are the “messengers of Zeus” (7.274; [[messengers-of-zeus]]).
