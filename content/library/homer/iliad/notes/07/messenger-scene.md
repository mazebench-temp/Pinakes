summary: Idaeus is sent to the Achaean ships at dawn, finds the Danaans in assembly beside the stern of Agamemnon's ship, delivers Priam's message standing in their midst, receives Agamemnon's answer, and brings it back to the Trojans waiting in their own assembly (7.372–417).

The herald's errand follows the pattern step by step.

1. **Sent.** Priam gives the message and the time: “at dawn let Idaeus go to the hollow ships” (7.372).
2. **Goes.** “And at dawn Idaeus went to the hollow ships” (7.381): the order becomes the act in the same verse, with ἔβη, “went”, for ἴτω, “let him go”.
3. **Finds.** “He found the Danaans, attendants of Ares, in assembly beside the stern of Agamemnon's ship” (τοὺς δʼ εὗρʼ, 7.382–383). The verb is the pattern's verb of arrival, and the Danaans are found, as the one sought usually is, in the middle of something.
4. **Delivers.** “Standing in their midst the loud-voiced herald spoke among them” (7.383–384), and the message is Priam's, with the herald's own additions (7.385–397; see [[message-repeated]]).
5. **Answer.** The army answers first, with Diomedes' refusal and a shout; then Agamemnon speaks to the herald by name and grants the truce (7.398–412).
6. **Return.** “And Idaeus went back to sacred Ilios” (7.413). The Trojans are sitting in assembly, “waiting for the moment when Idaeus would come”, and he gives them the answer “standing in their midst”, as he gave the message (7.414–417).

The messenger of this scene crosses from one army to the other, alone, as only a herald can (see [[heralds]] and [[idaeus]]). The answer he brings back is not repeated in his words, and the Trojans set out at once for the dead and for wood (7.417–418; see [[truce]]).
