---
summary: The horses of Book 7 belong to the chariot fight. Iphinous is killed as he leaps into his chariot, Hector boasts that he knows how to charge into the fray of swift horses, and the Achaean wall is given gates wide enough for chariots and a ditch to hold back “horses and men”.
grc: [ἵππον, ἱππηλασίη]
---

**In battle.** Glaucus strikes Iphinous “as he was leaping up behind his swift horses”, and he falls “from the chariot to the ground” (ἵππων … ἐξ ἵππων, 7.15–16; see [[iphinous]]). As often, ἵπποι are the chariot and team together (see [[chariot]]). Hector, answering Ajax, lists the chariot fight among the things a warrior knows: “I know how to charge into the fray of swift horses” (7.240). The next line sets against it the standing fight on foot, “in close fighting” (7.241; see the translation's note on 7.241 and [[like-a-boy-or-a-woman]]). The duel itself is fought on foot.

**The wall.** Nestor's plan provides for the horses of both sides. The wall is to have gates “that fit well, so that through them there may be a way for driving horses” (ἱππηλασίη ὁδός, 7.339–340), and a deep ditch outside “which, running all around, may hold back horses and men” (ἵππον καὶ λαόν, 7.341–342). The gates and the ditch are built in the same words (7.438–441). ἱππηλασίη, “fit for driving horses”, occurs only in these two lines. The ditch will be tested by the Trojans' horses: Hector boasts that they will leap it easily (8.179), and in Book 12 the horses balk at its edge (see [[the-building-of-the-wall]] and [[the-achaean-wall]]).

**Epithets.** Book 7 names horsemen in its epithets: Hector (7.38) and Diomedes (7.404) are “tamer of horses”, the Trojans are “horse-taming” (7.361), Peleus is “horse-driving” (7.125), and Nestor is “the Gerenian horseman” (7.170, 7.181). See [[tamer-of-horses]] and [[horse-driving]].
