summary: Priam's request for a truce, repeated by his herald in almost the same words, asks the Achaeans “to cease from grim-sounding war until we have burned the dead.”
---

The phrase πολέμοιο δυσηχέος is spoken twice in the same verse, first by Priam in the Trojan assembly (7.375–378) and then by Idaeus in the Achaean assembly:

{{quote:f2c07c88-ac62-5158-8359-7d1756636cac}}

The herald repeats the king's words almost exactly, as heralds do ([[message-repeated]], [[loud-voiced]]). The request is for a pause in the din of war so that both sides can bury their dead, and it is granted. Agamemnon refuses the offer of the possessions and allows the burning (7.405–411; [[the-truce-for-the-dead]], [[shrewd-word]]). See [[polemos]].
