{{quote:79fe687a-d34d-50d7-9450-1cf14f883189}}

The title of the god of the lightning comes without the name Olympian that goes with it in Book 1 (1.580, 1.609), in the dative, at the end of the verse. The gods are an audience, as they were for the duel (7.58–61; [[watching-the-war]]), and the thing they watch is the “great work” of the Achaeans (μέγα ἔργον, 7.444). Poseidon's complaint follows, and it is to the lord of the lightning that he speaks (7.446–453; [[earth-shaker]]). The same god thunders over the camp all night at the end of the book (7.478–479; [[pale-fear]]). See [[zeus]] and [[the-achaean-wall]].
