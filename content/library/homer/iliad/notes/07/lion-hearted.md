---
summary: Ajax names the absent Achilles as “breaker of men, lion-hearted” when he tells Hector what champions the Achaeans have even without him.
---

{{quote:e5e698a5-f8cb-58d8-be86-2e911e62618d}}

The accusative θυμολέοντα ends the verse, as in its four other uses in Homer, and it follows the other epithet of Achilles' warfare, [[breaker-of-men]]. In Book 5 Tlepolemus gave it to Heracles, his father, to shame Sarpedon (5.639). Here it is Ajax who uses it, of the man whose place he has taken for the day. Achilles lies idle among his ships, nursing his wrath (7.229–230; [[curved-ships]]), and Ajax offers the Achaeans' strength without him. See [[achilles]], [[ajax]] and [[lion]].
