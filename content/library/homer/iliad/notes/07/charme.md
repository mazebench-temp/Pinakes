summary: Twice in the duel the word names the battle to which Hector challenged the Achaeans. It is the reason he cannot retreat when Ajax comes toward him, and the reason Ajax gives for leaving it to Hector to end the fight.
grc: [χάρμῃ]
en: [to battle]
passages:
  - 7.214-218 | Hector's heart pounds
  - 7.283-286 | Ajax answers Idaeus
---

The phrase προκαλέσσατο χάρμῃ, “challenged … to battle”, is found only in Book 7, twice, and both times it is a reason. When Ajax comes out like Ares, Hector's heart pounds in his breast, “but he could no longer shrink back or slip away into the throng of the people, since he had made the challenge to battle” (7.217–218; see [[thymos]] and [[aidos]]). And when the heralds part the two at nightfall, Ajax answers that Hector must be the one to speak of stopping, “for it was he who challenged all the best to battle” (αὐτὸς γὰρ χάρμῃ προκαλέσσατο πάντας ἀρίστους, 7.285; see [[single-combat]] and [[yield-to-night]]). The challenger is bound by his challenge, to fight and then to end the fight.

χάρμη here is the fight itself rather than the joy of it, as the translation renders it in these two lines (see its table of key words). Elsewhere the word keeps its sense of eagerness, “the joy of battle” that an army remembers when it turns to fight again (4.222; see the note on Book 4).
