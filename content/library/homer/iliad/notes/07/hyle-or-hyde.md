In Monro and Allen the line reads σκυτοτόμων ὄχʼ ἄριστος Ὕλῃ ἔνι οἰκία ναίων, “by far the best of leather-workers, who lived in Hyle” (7.221; see [[tychius]] and [[the-shield-of-ajax]]). Some ancient texts had Ὕδῃ ἔνι, “in Hyde”. Strabo rejects that reading in his account of Boeotia, as he rejects it in the line on Oresbius: “for Ajax did not send to Lydia for his shield” (*Geography* 9.2.20). He adds that Homer keeps the first syllable of the name short here and in Book 5, and lengthens it in the Catalogue (ἠδʼ Ὕλην, 2.500).

In his account of Lydia Strabo reports the other side (13.4.6). Some added a fourth line to the entry for the Maeonians in the Catalogue (2.864–866), “under snowy Tmolus, in the rich land of Hyde”, the line that Book 20 gives to the birthplace of Iphition (20.385), and they made Tychius come from there. They added that the place was wooded and struck by lightning, and put there the Arimi, among whom Typhon was said to have his bed (2.783). No Hyde, says Strabo, is to be found among the Lydians, though some gave the name to Sardis, or to its citadel.

Monro and Allen print Ὕλῃ, and the translation follows them. See [[hyle]] and [[strabo]].
