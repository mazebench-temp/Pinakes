---
summary: Hector promises to hang the armor of the man he kills on the temple of “Apollo the Far-Shooter.”
---

Hector sets out what he will do if he wins:

{{quote:b5208b8c-0919-5af1-899b-06860a14b717}}

The short name of the archer god comes in a promise of dedication. The armor of the slain man will be carried to sacred Ilios and hung on Apollo's temple, which stands on Pergamus: there Apollo set the wounded Aeneas down, and Leto and Artemis healed him in the great inner sanctuary (5.445–448; [[temple]], [[hieros]]). The god he names is the one on his side: two lines before, Hector calls Apollo the giver of his triumph (δώῃ δέ μοι εὖχος Ἀπόλλων, 7.81), and Apollo will set him on his feet when Ajax knocks him down (7.272). The victory and the dedication do not come: the duel ends undecided, and the two men exchange gifts instead of armor (7.299–305). See [[apollo]] and [[stripping-the-dead]].
