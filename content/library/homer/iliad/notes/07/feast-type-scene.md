summary: The feast after the duel follows the pattern to the end of the eating, with a portion of honor for Ajax, and then turns to Nestor's counsel, as the feast of the kings did in Book 2 (7.319–325). The feast after the wall lasts all night and ends not in song but in thunder, libation and sleep (7.466–482).

**The feast for Ajax.** The first three steps are the verses of Chryse and of Book 2: the work done and the feast made ready, the equal feast, and the desire for drink and food put away (7.319–320, 7.323 = 1.467–469, 2.430–432; see [[equal-feast]] and [[desire-for-drink-and-food]]). Between the second and third comes something new, a portion of honor, the long chine that Agamemnon gives Ajax (7.321–322; see [[feast]] and [[geras]]). The equal share and the share of honor sit side by side.

**Counsel in place of song.** At Chryse the meal led to wine and a day of song (1.470–474). In Book 2 it led to Nestor's call to arms (2.433–440). Here, too, it leads to Nestor: “the old man first of all began to weave a plan for them” (7.324), the plan for a truce, a mound and a wall (7.327–343). The same verses bring him to his feet again at the elders' feast in Book 9, where the plan is the embassy to Achilles (7.323–326 = 9.92–95; see [[with-good-intent]]). The feast of the kings is where counsel is taken.

**The night of thunder.** The second feast has the pattern's parts spread through a night. Oxen are slaughtered and supper taken (7.466), wine is brought and bought (7.467–475), “and they set out a rich feast” (7.475). The feasting lasts “all night long”, among the Achaeans and in Troy (7.476–477). Where Book 1 had song, Book 7 has the thunder of Zeus, and the wine goes first to the ground (7.478–481; see [[libation]]). The last verse is the pattern's own ending, sleep: “Then they lay down to rest and took the gift of sleep” (7.482). Its second half, καὶ ὕπνου δῶρον ἕλοντο, “and took the gift of sleep”, ends Book 9 as well (9.713), and in the Odyssey the night after Autolycus' feast for Odysseus (Od. 19.427).
