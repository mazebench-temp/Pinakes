Poseidon, watching the Achaeans at work, asks Zeus whether any mortal will still tell the gods his mind and his plans, for they “have built a wall to protect their ships, and driven a ditch around it, and have not given glorious hecatombs to the gods” (7.449–450). A great work has been begun without a great sacrifice. Poseidon adds a grievance of his own, that the fame of the new wall will make men forget the wall that he and Apollo built for Laomedon (7.451–453; see [[the-achaean-wall]] and [[poseidon]]). Zeus answers the second complaint and promises him the wall's destruction when the Achaeans have gone home (7.455–463).

When the narrator tells how the wall was swept away after the war, he repeats the charge in the same verse and adds that the wall was built against the will of the immortal gods, and so did not stand for long (12.5–9; 12.5–6 ≈ 7.449–450; see [[the-building-of-the-wall]]). The one sacrifice the Achaeans offer in Book 7 is a meal of honor for their champion (7.313–322; see [[sacrifice]]).
