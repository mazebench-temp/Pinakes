---
summary: Zeus ties the destruction of the Achaean wall to the army's return home: when the Achaeans have gone “with their ships to their dear native land,” Poseidon may sweep the wall into the sea.
---

{{quote:83653725-580e-5895-a91a-4f274572ffb7}}

The formula of the return names the moment after the war, which the Iliad does not narrate. The wall will stand while the army stays and be broken when it leaves. Book 12 tells the rest, looking ahead to the time after Troy has fallen, “in the tenth year,” and the Argives have gone “in their ships to their dear native land” (12.15–16), when Poseidon and Apollo turn the rivers against the wall (12.17–33; [[the-achaean-wall]]). Earlier in the book Nestor spoke of the same return, when each man would carry the bones of the dead home to their children, “whenever we return again to our native land” (7.334–335; see the translation's note on 7.334–335).
