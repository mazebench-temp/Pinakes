summary: Agamemnon grants the truce for the dead with an oath to “Zeus, the loud-thundering husband of Hera,” and lifts his scepter to the gods.

The Trojans' offer is refused and their request granted in the same speech. Agamemnon lets the army's shout answer the offer of the possessions (7.400–407), and grants the burning of the dead in his own voice, “for one should not grudge the corpses of the dead, once they have died, the quick comfort of fire” (7.409–410; [[the-truce-for-the-dead]]). Then he calls Zeus to witness, in the line-end formula with which Hector, scepter in hand, swears his oath to Dolon (10.328–329), and raises his scepter to all the gods (7.411–412; [[oath]], [[scepter]]). The truce of Book 3 was sworn with sacrifice and broken by Pandarus' arrow (3.264–301, 4.104–140); this one is sworn with a word and a gesture, and both sides keep it (7.421–432). At the end of the book the loud-thundering god fills the night with his thunder (7.478–479; [[pale-fear]]). See [[zeus]] and [[agamemnon]].
