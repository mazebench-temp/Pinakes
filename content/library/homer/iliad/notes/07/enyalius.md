---
summary: Meriones is again “the equal of Enyalius, slayer of men” as he stands up with the eight other champions to face Hector (7.166).
---

When Nestor shames the Achaeans, nine men stand up to fight Hector, and Meriones comes after Idomeneus: “and after them Idomeneus and Idomeneus' squire / Meriones, the equal of Enyalius, slayer of men” (7.165–166; see [[meriones]] and [[idomeneus]]). The verse is the one that named him in the Catalogue (2.651), and it returns twice more (8.264, 17.259). It is always Meriones', and here it comes with his title, ὀπάων Ἰδομενῆος, “Idomeneus' squire”. The second in command of the Cretans carries the war god's old name, in a verse that may be among the oldest in the poem. The same book gives Ares his usual name when Ajax goes out to fight like him (7.208; see [[ares]]).
