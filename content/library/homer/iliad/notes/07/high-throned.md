summary: Hector opens his challenge by blaming Zeus, “throned on high,” for the oaths that were not fulfilled.

{{quote:ddb17e1d-32bf-5f01-a125-f46fc3634e59}}

The title that Agamemnon gave Zeus when he foresaw the fall of Troy as the punishment for the broken oaths (4.160–168) is now Hector's. He lays the failure of the truce on the god and says nothing of Pandarus' arrow, which Agamemnon and, later in this book, Antenor call the Trojans' breach (4.155–157, 7.351–352; see the translation's note on 7.69–70; [[oaths-and-their-breaking]], [[pandarus-breaks-the-truce]]). In Hector's words the god who sits above all means evil for both sides, until the Achaeans take Troy or are beaten at their ships (7.70–72; [[of-the-fine-towers]]).
