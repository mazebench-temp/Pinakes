summary: Nestor uses the word twice in his rebuke. Old Peleus once rejoiced to learn “the lineage and birth of all the Argives”, and of the day he faced Ereuthalion Nestor says, “by birth I was the youngest of them all”.
passages:

  - 7.150-156 | Nestor fights Ereuthalion
---

**Lineage.** Nestor reminds the Achaeans of a visit to Peleus, who questioned him in his house and “rejoiced greatly to learn the lineage and birth of all the Argives” (γενεήν τε τόκον τε, 7.127–128). To know a man's γενεή is to know whose son he is and what his fathers did, as Glaucus told his to Diomedes in Book 6 (6.145–211; see [[genealogy-in-battle]]). The old king took pleasure in the families of the men who would go to Troy; now, Nestor says, he would pray to die if he heard that they all cower before Hector (7.129–131; see [[peleus]] and [[fathers-and-sons]]). A lineage is a claim that its sons must live up to.

**Birth.** In the story of Ereuthalion the word is age: no one dared to face the Arcadian champion, but Nestor's heart sent him to fight, “and by birth I was the youngest of them all” (γενεῇ δὲ νεώτατος, 7.153; see [[nestor-and-ereuthalion]]). The youngest man dared what the grown men would not. The old man who tells it has since outlived two generations of men and rules among the third (1.250–252; see [[old-age-and-memory]]).
