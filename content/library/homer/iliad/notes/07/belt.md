---
summary: Ajax gives Hector his belt, “bright with purple”, in return for Hector's sword (7.305), in the verse that told Oeneus' gift to Bellerophon.
passages:
  - 7.303-305 | Hector and Ajax exchange gifts
---

“And Ajax gave a belt bright with purple” (Αἴας δὲ ζωστῆρα δίδου φοίνικι φαεινόν, 7.305). The verse is the one that told Oeneus' gift to Bellerophon, with Ajax's name in place of Oeneus' (6.219). There the belt was a host's gift to a guest, and the guest answered with a golden cup (6.219–220); here it answers a sword, and both gifts are a warrior's gear (see [[guest-gifts]], [[sword]] and [[purple]]). The ζωστήρ was worn over the lower edge of the corselet, and it had saved Menelaus from Pandarus' arrow (4.132–138, 4.185–187). Hector receives it after a fight in which Ajax's spear went through his shield and corselet and cut the tunic beneath (7.251–254). The Iliad does not mention the belt again; in Sophocles' Ajax it becomes the belt by which Hector was dragged (see [[sophocles-sword-and-belt]]).
