summary: The lesser Ajax is one of the nine leaders who stand up to accept Hector's challenge, as one of “the two Ajaxes” (7.164).
refs: [7.164]
---

τοῖσι δʼ ἐπʼ Αἴαντες θοῦριν ἐπιειμένοι ἀλκήν, “and after them the two Ajaxes, clothed in furious courage” (7.164). When Nestor has shamed the Achaeans, nine leaders stand up to fight Hector, and the two Ajaxes rise third, after Agamemnon and Diomedes (7.161–169). The pair are Telamonian Ajax and the son of Oileus, as in Books 2, 4, 5 and 6 (see [[ajax]]). θοῦριν ἐπιειμένοι ἀλκήν, “clothed in furious courage” (see [[furious]] and [[alke]]), is their verse. It returns with the three verses that follow it, when the Achaean leaders charge out after Diomedes in Book 8 (7.164–167 = 8.262–265), and again when the two Ajaxes three times drive Hector back from the body of Patroclus (18.157). Each of the nine marks a lot, and the lot that leaps from Agamemnon's helmet is the other Ajax's (7.175–183; see [[lots]]). The son of Oileus is not named alone in Book 7.
