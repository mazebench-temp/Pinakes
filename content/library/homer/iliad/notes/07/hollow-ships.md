---
summary: The hollow ships are the Achaean camp in Book 7: where the victor would carry Hector's armor, where the Trojan herald goes with his message, and where the Achaeans return after burning their dead; Paris' possessions came to Troy in hollow ships.
---

The line-end κοίλας ἐπὶ νῆας names the camp as a destination. Hector proposes that if he falls, the victor may carry his armor “to the hollow ships” (7.78). Priam sends Idaeus at dawn “to the hollow ships” (7.372), and the narrator repeats the verse, with the command turned into the act, when he goes (7.381), as orders are repeated when they are carried out ([[message-repeated]], [[idaeus]]). When both armies have burned their dead, the Trojans return to sacred Ilios and the Achaeans “went back to the hollow ships” (7.432; [[hieros]]).

Idaeus' message names the ships of the Trojans too: the possessions that “Alexander in his hollow ships brought to Troy” (7.389–390) are the goods Paris brought from Argos with Helen, which he now offers to give back (7.363; [[possessions]], [[the-abduction-of-helen]]). See [[ships]].
