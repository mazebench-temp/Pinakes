summary: Book 7 has four speeches of “a man”. Twice the army prays for Ajax in one anonymous voice (7.178–180, 7.201–205). Twice Hector imagines what men will say in time to come: a sailor passing the mound of the man he has killed (7.87–91), and men of both armies telling how he and Ajax fought and parted in friendship (7.300–302).

**The army prays.** Both of the army's prayers are introduced with the verse ὧδε δέ τις εἴπεσκεν ἰδὼν εἰς οὐρανὸν εὐρύν, “and this is what a man would say, looking up at the wide sky” (7.178, 7.201). The second half of the verse replaces the neighbor at whom the speaker looks in Book 2 (2.271) with the sky to which he prays. One nameless voice asks Zeus for the lot to fall to Ajax, Diomedes or Agamemnon, and then for victory for Ajax, or an equal outcome if Zeus loves Hector too; both speeches are closed with the plural “So they spoke” (7.181, 7.206). Both prayers are answered, the first by the lot and the second by the drawn duel (see [[prayer-type-scene]] and [[lots]]).

**Men to come.** Hector's challenge ends with an imagined speech, introduced by the formula he used of Andromache's slavery in Book 6, καί ποτέ τις εἴπῃσι, “and someday one … will say” (7.87; 6.459). A sailor of a later generation, passing a mound by the Hellespont, will say, “This is the mound of a man who died long ago, whom once, when he was the best in the fighting, shining Hector killed”, and the challenge closes, “So someone will say one day; and my fame will never perish” (7.89–91; see [[tomb]], [[kleos]] and [[hectors-epitaph]]). In Book 6 the stranger's words were Hector's fame spoken over his captive wife; here they are his fame spoken over his victim's grave.

**Both armies.** Hector's proposal at the end of the duel imagines a speech of another kind: “so that this is what a man will say, of Achaeans and Trojans alike: ‘They fought in heart-devouring strife, and then parted in friendship, reconciled’” (7.300–302). The second half of the verse, Ἀχαιῶν τε Τρώων τε, is the one that gave a single voice to both armies at the oath and the lots of Book 3 (3.297, 3.319). Here that voice will remember the two champions together, and the gifts are to prove it (7.299, 7.303–305; see [[guest-gifts]] and [[parted-in-friendship]]).
