---
summary: The patronymic names Hector in Book 7: in Helenus' address, in Agamemnon's warning to Menelaus, and twice in the duel with Ajax.
grc: [υἱὲ Πριάμοιο]
---

Helenus addresses his brother with the full formula of respect, “Hector, son of Priam, the equal of Zeus in counsel” (7.47; [[equal-of-zeus-in-counsel]], [[helenus]]). Agamemnon uses the patronymic to warn Menelaus off: he must not fight a better man, “Hector, son of Priam, whom others too dread” (7.111–112; [[menelaus]]).

In the duel the narrator names Hector by his father twice. Ajax's spear strikes “the son of Priam on his shield, balanced on every side” (7.250), in the verse of Menelaus' throw at Paris in Book 3 (3.356); and when the two close in with their spears, “The son of Priam then stabbed the middle of the shield with his spear” (7.258), without breaking through. The same patronymic had named Paris in the first duel. Of the two sons of Priam who fight single combats before the city, Paris is carried off by Aphrodite, and Hector is parted from Ajax by the heralds (3.380–382, 7.273–282; [[single-combat]]).
