---
summary: Meriones, “the equal of Enyalius, slayer of men”, rises with Idomeneus, whose squire he is, among the nine leaders who volunteer to meet Hector (7.165–166).
passages:
  - 7.161-169 | The nine volunteers
---

He is the sixth to stand, and the only one of the nine named as another man's attendant: Idomeneus' ὀπάων, “squire” (7.165; see [[idomeneus]]). His own verse gives him the war god's measure, “Meriones, the equal of Enyalius, slayer of men” (7.166; see [[equal-of-ares]] and [[enyalius]]). It is his line in the Catalogue (2.651), and it follows him into later lists of the Achaean leaders (8.264, 17.259). The squire offers himself for the single combat as a chief among chiefs (see [[single-combat]]). In Book 10 he volunteers again, to go with Diomedes among the Trojans (10.229), and he lends Odysseus the helmet of boar's tusks (10.260–271).
