---
summary: Hector imagines the mound of the man he will kill heaped up “beside the broad Hellespont”, where sailors will see it and tell of his victory (7.86–91).
---

σῆμά τέ οἱ χεύωσιν ἐπὶ πλατεῖ Ἑλλησπόντῳ, “and heap up a mound for him beside the broad Hellespont” (7.86). If Hector kills his challenger, he will give back the body to the ships, and the Achaeans will bury it on the shore. In time a man “sailing in his many-benched ship over the wine-dark sea” will see the mound and name Hector as the killer (7.84–91; see [[hector]], [[fame-and-the-tomb]] and [[many-benched]]). πλατύς, “broad”, suits the open water off the Troad better than the narrow strait (see [[hellespont]]).

The Odyssey puts the tomb of Achilles and Patroclus in the same place, in the same half-verse: the army heaped it “on a jutting headland, beside the broad Hellespont”, to be seen far out at sea by the men of that time and of time to come (Od. 24.80–84). Visitors to the Troad in later times were shown such mounds on this shore: Strabo describes the tomb, temple and statue of Ajax at Rhoeteum, and the tomb of Achilles near Sigeum (13.1.30, 13.1.32). The mound that the Achaeans do raise in Book 7 is a single one over all their dead, with the wall built against it (7.336–337, 7.435–436; see [[the-achaean-wall]]).
