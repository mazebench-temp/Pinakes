**The mound by the Hellespont.** If Hector kills his opponent, he will give the body back so that the Achaeans may bury him “and heap up a mound for him beside the broad Hellespont” (7.85–86). Then he imagines a stranger passing it:

{{quote:49b62789-a319-5614-b469-0ea22b08bbda}}

The mound is a σῆμα, a sign, and the sailor's words have the shape of an inscription on a grave, “this is the mound of …” (τόδε σῆμα), like the verse epitaphs of later Greek tombs (see [[hectors-epitaph]]). They name the dead man only as the best fighter of his day, and they name his killer. The mound will keep Hector's fame, not its owner's: “my fame will never perish” (7.91; see [[kleos]], [[tis-speech]] and [[fame-and-the-tomb]]). In Book 4 Agamemnon imagined the opposite, a Trojan leaping on Menelaus' tomb and mocking the Achaeans (4.174–182). The Odyssey places the tomb of Achilles and Patroclus on the same shore, on a headland by the broad Hellespont, where it can be seen far out at sea by men of the present and of time to come (Od. 24.80–84).

**One mound for all.** The real tomb of Book 7 has no name on it. Nestor proposes, and the Achaeans build, “a single tomb … one for all alike” around the pyre of their dead (τύμβον … ἕνα … ἄκριτον, 7.336–337, 7.435–436; see [[cremation]] and [[pyres]]). ἄκριτος is usually taken as “undistinguished”, with no man's grave set apart; the bodies were gathered when “it was hard to tell each man apart” (7.424). Elsewhere in the Iliad ἄκριτος means “endless” or “past measure”, of words and of griefs (2.796, 3.412). The men who build it are a κριτὸς λαός, “a chosen band” (7.434).

**Tomb and wall.** Against the mound the Achaeans build their wall, with towers, gates and a ditch (ποτὶ δʼ αὐτόν, 7.337, 7.436; see [[the-achaean-wall]]). The dead of the first battle lie at the foot of the defense of the ships. Hector's imagined mound was to keep his fame for all time; this one stands beside a wall that the gods agree to sweep away (7.459–463).

At 7.189 σῆμα is the mark on Ajax's lot, and that line is not counted here (see [[lots]]).
