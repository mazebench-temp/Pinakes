summary: Hector's challenge names “Troy of the fine towers”, Apollo speaks of the day when the Achaeans will find the end of Ilios, and the Trojans hold their assembly on the height of the city. Poseidon fears that the Achaean wall will make men forget the wall he and Apollo built for Laomedon.
grc: ["Τροίηνδ*"]
refs: [7.32, 7.296, 7.310, 7.370, 7.477]
---

**Its names.** The book calls the city “sacred Ilios” (7.20, 7.82, 7.413, 7.429; see [[hieros]]), and Hector, challenging the Achaeans, calls it Τροίην εὔπυργον, “Troy of the fine towers” (7.71; see [[of-the-fine-towers]]). It is “the great city of lord Priam” (7.296; see [[priam]]). In Idaeus' message Troy is the place to which Paris brought the possessions, Τροίηνδʼ, “to Troy” (7.390; see [[paris]]).

**Its end.** Apollo proposes to stop the war for a day: “afterward they will fight again, until they find the end of Ilios, since this is what has pleased the hearts of you immortal goddesses, to sack this city” (7.30–32; see [[apollo]]). τέκμωρ Ἰλίου is the appointed end of Ilios, or the way to take it (see the translation's note on 7.30–31). Hector sets out the alternatives: Zeus is ordaining evil for both sides “until either you take Troy of the fine towers or are yourselves beaten beside your seafaring ships” (7.70–72; see [[hector]]). Diomedes answers the Trojan offer with certainty: “the bounds of destruction are already made fast upon the Trojans” (7.402; see [[diomedes]] and [[even-a-fool-can-see]]).

**Inside.** The Trojans bring Hector back “to the city” (7.310). Their assembly meets beside Priam's doors, Ἰλίου ἐν πόλει ἄκρῃ, “on the height of the city of Ilios” (7.345; see [[priams-palace]] and [[pergamus]]), where Athena's temple and the houses of Priam, Hector and Paris stood (6.297, 6.317). Priam tells them to take their supper “throughout the city” (7.370), and on the night the Achaean wall is finished they feast there all night long with their allies (7.477).

**Its wall.** Poseidon complains that the fame of the Achaean wall will make men forget “the wall that I and Phoebus Apollo toiled to build for the hero Laomedon” (7.452–453; see [[the-walls-of-troy]], [[laomedon]] and [[the-achaean-wall]]). The walls of Troy were built by gods for a king. The Achaeans' wall, built without hecatombs to the gods, is to be broken down and swept into the sea (7.450, 7.459–463).
