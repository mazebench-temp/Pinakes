---
summary: Ajax, “the bulwark of the Achaeans,” goes out to meet Hector, in the book in which the Achaeans build a wall of stone.
---

{{quote:84a83657-6f10-570a-956c-7f66f042fa45}}

The phrase πελώριος ἕρκος Ἀχαιῶν is the one with which Helen named Ajax for Priam from the wall (3.229; [[huge]]). Here the title comes as he goes out to fight, and it is borne out: Hector's spear stops in the seventh hide of his shield, and at the end of the duel the Achaeans lead him back to Agamemnon “exulting in his victory” (7.245–248, 7.311–312; [[seven-oxhide]]).

The same book gives the Achaeans a bulwark of another kind. That night Nestor proposes a wall with high towers, “a safeguard for the ships and for ourselves” (εἶλαρ, 7.338), and two days later they build it (7.433–441; [[the-achaean-wall]]). The man called a bulwark holds the field in Book 7, and in the books that follow the wall of stone must do what he does. In Book 12 Ajax fights on the wall itself (12.265–289, 12.370–386).
