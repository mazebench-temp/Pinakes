---
summary: The army's prayer before the duel asks Zeus for victory and triumph for Ajax, or else “equal strength and glory to them both”, and the day grants the second request. It is a prayer for a glory that can be shared, as victory cannot.
passages:
  - 7.200-205 | The army prays for Ajax
---

**The prayer.** While Ajax arms, the Achaeans pray to Zeus in the words with which both armies prayed at the oaths and the lots in Book 3, “Father Zeus, who rule from Ida, most glorious, greatest” (κύδιστε μέγιστε, 7.202 = 3.276, 3.320; Priam prays with it again at 24.308). The superlative κύδιστος is a formal address, of Zeus in prayer as of Agamemnon among men (1.122). Then they ask for two things in turn (7.203–205):

- “grant victory to Ajax, and that he win a splendid triumph” (δὸς νίκην Αἴαντι καὶ ἀγλαὸν εὖχος ἀρέσθαι);
- “but if you love Hector too and care for him, grant equal strength and glory to them both” (ἴσην ἀμφοτέροισι βίην καὶ κῦδος ὄπασσον).

The first request is for victory and εὖχος, the triumph a victor takes from a beaten man (see [[nike]] and [[euchomai]]); the translation keeps εὖχος, “triumph”, apart from κῦδος, “glory”. The second is for strength and κῦδος, and it can be given to both men at once, as victory and triumph cannot. The army allows that Zeus may love Hector too, and the herald Idaeus will tell the two champions that Zeus does love them both (7.280). The duel ends as the second request asked: neither man is beaten, both have shown their strength, and each goes back to his own side to be welcomed (7.306–312; see [[the-duel-of-hector-and-ajax]]).

**Glory granted and withdrawn.** κῦδος is the glory of the moment, and Zeus gives it to one side at a time. The next day of battle he gives it to the Trojans. Nestor tells Diomedes that “now Zeus, son of Cronus, grants glory to this man”, Hector (8.141), and Hector knows that Zeus has nodded to him “victory and great glory” (8.175–176). The night before, Zeus had given the Achaeans only his thunder (7.478–479; see [[war-and-night]]).

**The family.** The book has two other words from the stem. Agamemnon tells Menelaus that even Achilles shudders to meet Hector “in the battle where men win glory” (μάχῃ ἔνι κυδιανείρῃ, 7.113; see [[where-men-win-glory]]), and Idaeus calls Helen “the wedded wife of glorious Menelaus” (κυδαλίμοιο, 7.392).
