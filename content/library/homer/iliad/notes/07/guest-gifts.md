**Gifts between enemies.** The two men are not guest-friends, and the gifts are not called guest-gifts. They are δῶρα περικλυτά, “glorious gifts” (7.299; see [[doron]]), given at the end of a fight that neither has won. Hector proposes them in the speech in which he proposes to stop, and he gives the reason: what men will say of the two of them afterward (see [[tis-speech]]). The gifts are the evidence for the words “parted in friendship” (ἐν φιλότητι, 7.302; see [[philotes]] and [[parted-in-friendship]]), as Diomedes' cup is the evidence of his grandfather's bond with Bellerophon (6.220–221). ἀρθμήσαντε, “reconciled”, is from ἀρθμέω, “join, be at one”, which occurs only here in Homer; the adjective ἄρθμιος, “at peace, allied”, is used once in the Odyssey (Od. 16.427).

**Sword and belt.** Hector gives the sword he did not use in the fight, “with its sheath and its well-cut strap”, and Ajax gives a belt (7.303–305; see [[sword]], [[sheath]] and [[belt]]). The verse of Ajax's gift is the verse of Oeneus' gift to Bellerophon, with Ajax's name for Oeneus' (7.305 = 6.219). There a host gave a warrior's belt to his guest in his own hall, and the guest's cup was still in Diomedes' house two generations later (6.217–221). Here the gifts pass on the field, and they are arms, like the gold and bronze armor of Glaucus and Diomedes (6.230–236; see [[glaucus-and-diomedes]]).

**A pause, not a bond.** Glaucus and Diomedes exchanged armor to show a bond that made fighting between them unthinkable (6.226–231). Hector and Ajax exchange gifts in the same speech in which Hector says that they will fight again, “until a god decides between us” (7.291–292), and they do meet again in battle (14.402–420, 16.114–122). The gifts honor the fight and the parting, not a friendship to come, and the Iliad does not mention them again. Sophocles made them the instruments of both men's deaths (see [[sophocles-sword-and-belt]]).
