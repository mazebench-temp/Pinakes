summary: Ships from Lemnos, sent by Euneus, son of Jason, bring wine to the Achaean camp, and the Achaeans buy it with bronze, iron, hides, cattle and slaves (7.467–475). It is the Iliad's one glimpse of the story of the Argonauts on the island.

**A market.** Lemnos lies west of the Troad, across the sea from the Achaean camp, and it trades with the army. Euneus sends many ships of wine, gives the sons of Atreus a thousand measures apart, and sells the rest for bronze, iron, hides, cattle and slaves (7.467–475; see [[euneus]], [[wine]] and [[trade]]). ἀνδράποδα, people taken in war and sold, are named only here in Homer (7.475). The island is a market for captives in later books too. Achilles sold Priam's son Lycaon there, and Euneus paid the price (21.40–41, 21.58, 21.79; see [[lycaon-son-of-priam]]); Hecuba says that Achilles sold her other sons across the sea, to Samos, Imbros and Lemnos (24.751–753). Agamemnon reminds the Achaeans of the boasts they made on Lemnos over the meat of cattle and bowls brimming with wine (8.229–234), and Nestor tells him that Achaean ships bring wine to his huts every day from Thrace (9.71–72).

**The Argonauts.** The man who sends the wine is the son of Jason and Hypsipyle (7.468–469; see [[jason]] and [[hypsipyle]]). The line takes for granted the story that later poets told: the Argo put in at Lemnos, where the women had killed the men of the island, and the Argonauts stayed with them (see [[the-lemnian-women]]). It is the Iliad's only allusion to the voyage. The island is also “the city of godlike Thoas” (14.230), whom later writers made Hypsipyle's father.
