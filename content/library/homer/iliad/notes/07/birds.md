---
summary: The only birds of Book 7 are gods in likeness: Athena and Apollo sit on the oak of Zeus “like birds, like vultures” to watch Hector's challenge and the duel (7.58–61).
grc: [ὄρνισιν]
---

ὄρνισιν ἐοικότες αἰγυπιοῖσι, “like birds, like vultures” (7.59): the common word for a bird, ὄρνις, is followed by the name of the kind, αἰγυπιός (see [[vultures]]). The same pairing sends Hermes over the waves “like a bird, a gull” (λάρῳ ὄρνιθι, *Odyssey* 5.51). Whether Athena and Apollo take the birds' shape or only sit like them is debated (see [[like-vultures]]).

The birds of the proem feed on the dead of Achilles' wrath (1.4–5). Book 7 turns that fear aside. Hector's challenge provides for the burial of whichever champion falls (7.76–86), and the truce lets both armies gather and burn their dead (7.327–335, 7.408–432; see [[the-truce-for-the-dead]]). The gods perched like birds watch a duel in which no one dies.
