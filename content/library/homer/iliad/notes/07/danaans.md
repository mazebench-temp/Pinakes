---
summary: The name is used five times in Book 7, four of them by speakers. Apollo asks Athena whether she has come to give the Danaans victory, Menelaus is ashamed that none of them will face Hector, and Ajax tells Hector what chiefs the Danaans have; Idaeus finds “the Danaans, attendants of Ares” in assembly by Agamemnon's ship (7.382).
---

Apollo asks Athena whether she has come “to give the Danaans the victory that turns the battle” (7.26; see [[apollo]] and [[athena]]), and proposes instead that Hector challenge “one of the Danaans, man to man, alone” (7.39). Menelaus calls it a disgrace “if none of the Danaans now goes to face Hector” (7.98; see [[menelaus]]), and Ajax tells Hector that he will learn “what kind of chiefs are among the Danaans too, even after Achilles” (7.227–228; see [[ajax]]).

Δαναοὶ θεράποντες Ἄρηος, “Danaans, attendants of Ares”, is a formula of address to the whole army: “Friends, Danaan heroes, attendants of Ares” (2.110, 6.67). Here the narrator uses it as Idaeus finds the army in assembly beside the stern of Agamemnon's ship (7.382; see [[ares]] and [[idaeus]]). A θεράπων is the attendant who serves a lord in war, as Meriones serves Idomeneus (23.113); the phrase makes the army the retinue of the war god.
