---
summary: The formula κάρη κομόωντες Ἀχαιοί closes seven verses of Book 7: in Hector's challenge, in Nestor's count of the dead, in the building of the wall, in the speeches of Poseidon and Zeus, and at the night's feast.
---

**The dead.** Hector promises that if he kills his man, “the long-haired Achaeans” will give him burial and heap up a mound by the Hellespont (7.85–86; [[broad-hellespont]]). Nestor begins his proposal of the truce: “many long-haired Achaeans are dead” (7.328; [[dark-blood]]).

**The wall.** The narrator ends the building of the wall with the formula: “So the long-haired Achaeans toiled” (7.442). Poseidon uses it in his complaint (7.448), and Zeus in his answer, when he promises that the wall will be destroyed once “the long-haired Achaeans” have gone home (7.459–460; [[dear-native-land]], [[the-achaean-wall]]).

**The feast.** At the end of the book “the long-haired Achaeans bought their wine” from the Lemnian ships, and “All night long then the long-haired Achaeans feasted” (7.472, 7.476–477; [[rich-feast]], [[wine]]). The army named by its hair in the formula is seen burying, building, trading and feasting, and the formula is the same for all of it. See [[achaeans]].
