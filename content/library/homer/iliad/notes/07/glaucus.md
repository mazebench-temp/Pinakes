**The third kill.** Hector and Paris come out through the gates, and the Trojans kill three Achaeans in nine lines: Paris kills Menesthius, Hector kills Eioneus, and Glaucus kills Iphinous, son of Dexius (7.8–16; see [[menesthius]], [[eioneus]] and [[iphinous]]). Two of the killers are sons of Priam; the third is an ally, one of the leaders who came “from far-off Lycia” (2.877; see [[lycians]]). It is the first man the poem shows Glaucus killing. The spear catches Iphinous in the shoulder “as he was leaping up behind his swift horses”, and “he fell from the chariot to the ground, and his limbs were loosened” (7.15–16; see [[loosened-his-limbs]]). The three deaths bring Athena down from Olympus, for she sees the Trojans “killing the Argives in the fierce battle” (7.17–18; see [[athena]]).

**After the exchange.** In Book 6 Glaucus and Diomedes agreed to avoid each other's spears, since each had enemies enough: “for there are many Trojans and famous allies for me / to kill … and many Achaeans in turn for you to slay, whomever you can” (6.227–229; see [[glaucus-and-diomedes]] and [[diomedes]]). Iphinous is the first of those Achaeans. The poem does not say whether Glaucus is now wearing Diomedes' bronze.

**The verse.** Γλαῦκος δʼ Ἱππολόχοιο πάϊς Λυκίων ἀγὸς ἀνδρῶν, “And Glaucus, son of Hippolochus, leader of the Lycian men” (7.13), returns whole when Glaucus rebukes Hector for leaving Sarpedon's body to the Achaeans (17.140; see [[hippolochus]] and [[sarpedon]]). In Book 16 Sarpedon, and after his death Glaucus, is Λυκίων ἀγὸς ἀσπιστάων, “leader of the Lycian shield-bearers” (16.490, 16.541, 16.593). After this kill Glaucus is not named again until the Trojans attack the Achaean wall (12.102; see [[the-achaean-wall]]).
