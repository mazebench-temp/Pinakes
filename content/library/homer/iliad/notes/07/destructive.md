---
summary: The epithet of war and of Ares in Book 7: “destructive war,” from which a man is glad to come out alive, and “destructive Ares,” for whom Hector says he knows how to dance.
grc: [δηΐου, δηΐῳ]
---

**Destructive war.** Agamemnon, holding Menelaus back, and Nestor, calling for the lots, end their speeches with the same verse, of the man who comes out alive “from destructive war and grim combat” (7.119, 7.174; [[grim-combat]], [[insatiable]]). The phrase δηΐου ἐκ πολέμοιο returns in later books for those who leave the fighting or escape from it (17.189, 21.422). Achilles uses the whole thought of the Trojans in Book 19: any of them who escapes from destructive war under his spear will be glad to bend his knee and rest (19.71–73).
