---
summary: The last lines of Book 7 give Zeus his epithet of counsel: all night long “Zeus the counselor devised evil for them,” thundering over both armies.
---

The Achaeans feast through the night on the wine from Lemnos, and the Trojans in their city:

{{quote:f233a103-34a1-5742-8956-6efeb72d8ce7}}

μητίετα, the god of μῆτις (“cunning, counsel”), stands beside μήδετο, “devised,” from the same field of planning (compare μήστωρ, “counselor,” [[counselor-equal-to-the-gods]]). Zeus has just granted Poseidon the destruction of the Achaean wall (7.455–463), and now his thunder is heard over the camp and the city alike. The book ends with the plan of Zeus at work and unexplained: the next morning he forbids the gods to help either side and goes to Ida to watch (8.1–52), and the battle turns against the Achaeans.
