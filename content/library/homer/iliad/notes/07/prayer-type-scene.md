summary: The army prays to Zeus twice in one anonymous voice, at the lots and while Ajax arms (7.177–180, 7.200–205). Neither prayer makes a claim, both are answered by the event, and the second asks for an equal outcome if Zeus loves Hector too, which is what it gets.

**At the lots.**

1. **Setting and gesture.** “And the people prayed, and lifted up their hands to the gods” (7.177), the verse of the prayer at the lots in Book 3 (3.318), and the speaker looks “up at the wide sky” (7.178; see [[tis-speech]]).
2. **Invocation.** “Father Zeus” (Ζεῦ πάτερ), with no titles.
3. **Claim.** None.
4. **Request.** “Let the lot fall to Ajax, or the son of Tydeus, or the king himself of Mycenae rich in gold” (7.179–180). In Book 3 the prayer at the lots named no one and asked for a death (3.320–323).
5. **Answer.** No formula tells it. The lot that leaps out of the helmet is “the lot that they themselves wanted” (7.182; see [[lots]]).

**For Ajax.** Ajax asks the army to pray while he arms, and they do:

{{quote:0ce2ea4e-b83e-5c42-a26b-d3d31c0c40ed}}

1. **Setting and gesture.** Only the look to the sky (7.201).
2. **Invocation.** The whole verse of Agamemnon's oath and of the prayer at the lots in Book 3, “Father Zeus, who rule from Ida, most glorious, greatest” (7.202 = 3.276, 3.320; see [[who-rules-from-ida]]). Ida is the mountain above Troy.
3. **Claim.** None.
4. **Request.** Victory for Ajax, “and that he win a splendid triumph”, and then, “if you love Hector too and care for him”, “equal strength and glory to them both” (7.203–205). The second request is a condition that the army can accept, and it hedges the first.
5. **Answer.** Again the event gives it. Ajax has the better of the fight, but no one wins, and the herald who parts the two men gives Zeus' love for both as his reason: “for Zeus the cloud-gatherer loves you both” (ἀμφοτέρω γὰρ σφῶϊ φιλεῖ, 7.280). The verb is the prayer's (φιλέεις, 7.204). The prayer is granted in its second form (see [[single-combat]] and [[prayer]]).
