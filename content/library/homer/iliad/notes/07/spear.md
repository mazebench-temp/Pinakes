---
summary: The spear fights the duel of Hector and Ajax, thrown and then thrust, in verses shared with the duel of Book 3, and it draws the only blood of the two duels (7.244–262). In Nestor's story Areithous fought with an iron mace instead of the long spear, and a spear killed him (7.140–145); the herald calls Hector and Ajax both spearmen (7.281).
passages:
  - 7.244-262 | Spears in the duel
  - 7.137-146 | Areithous and the spear
---

**Held by the middle.** Hector stops the fighting to make his challenge as he did in Book 3, going into the middle and holding back the Trojan battalions “gripping his spear by the middle” (7.55–56 = 3.77–78), the spear held level across the front of the line (see [[single-combat]]).

**Thrown, then thrust.** Ajax comes on “brandishing his long-shadowed spear” (7.213; see [[long-shadowed]]). Hector throws first, and his spear cuts through six layers of Ajax's shield and stops in the seventh (7.244–248; see [[shield]] and [[the-shield-of-ajax]]). Ajax's spear goes through Hector's shield and corselet and cuts his tunic, and Hector swerves and escapes, in the verses of Menelaus' throw at Paris (7.250–254 = 3.356–360, with the names changed). Then the two draw out the long spears and close with them like lions or boars (7.255–257; see [[lions-and-boars]]). Hector's thrust bends back on Ajax's shield, in the verse of Paris' failed throw (7.259 = 3.348). Ajax's thrust goes right through Hector's shield, drives him back, and grazes his neck, “and the black blood welled up” (7.260–262). In Book 3 the spears drew no blood at all.

**Not the long spear.** In Nestor's story Areithous was called the mace-man “because he did not fight with bows or the long spear, but broke the battalions with an iron mace” (7.140–141; see [[mace]] and [[areithous]]). He was killed by the weapon he did without. Lycurgus caught him in a narrow road where there was no room to swing the mace, and struck first, and “pierced him through the middle with his spear” (7.142–145; see [[lycurgus-of-arcadia]]).

**Spearmen.** The herald Idaeus parts the fighters with praise: “both of you are spearmen” (ἄμφω δʼ αἰχμητά, 7.281; see [[spearmen]]). Hector, proposing to stop, grants Ajax that “with the spear you are the mightiest of the Achaeans” (7.289).
