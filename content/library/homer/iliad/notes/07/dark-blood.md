---
summary: The black blood wells up from Hector's neck, the only wound of the duel, and Nestor speaks of the dark blood of the dead that Ares has scattered about the Scamander.
grc: ["μέλαν δʼ ἀνεκήκιεν αἷμα"]
---

**Hector's wound.** Ajax's spear goes through Hector's shield and drives him back, “and it grazed his neck with a cut, and the black blood welled up” (7.262). The verb ἀνακηκίω, “gush up,” is used in the Iliad otherwise only of sweat, on a team of plowing oxen and on racehorses (13.705, 23.507). The wound does not stop him: “But not even so did Hector of the gleaming helmet cease from battle” (7.263; [[of-the-gleaming-helmet]]). It is the only blood drawn in the duel.

**The dead.** Nestor, proposing the truce, begins with the dead: “many long-haired Achaeans are dead, whose dark blood about the fair-flowing Scamander sharp Ares has now scattered, and their souls have gone down to Hades” (7.328–330; [[long-haired]], [[fair-flowing]], [[sharp]], [[scamander]]). The dark blood of the dead is the reason for the truce and for the burning (7.331–335; [[the-truce-for-the-dead]]). The next morning the Achaeans and Trojans wash “the clotted blood” from the bodies before they can tell one man from another (7.424–425; [[clotted-blood]]).
