He is named last, with Thoas, and with his plainest epithet, δῖος, “brilliant” (7.168; see [[brilliant]] and [[thoas]]). In Book 3 he and Hector measured out the ground for the first duel and shook the lots in a helmet (3.314–316); here his own lot goes into the helmet, and Nestor shakes it (7.175–181; see [[lots]] and [[nestor]]). The army's prayer names Ajax, Diomedes and Agamemnon, not him (7.179–180). In Book 10 he volunteers again, in a longer list of the willing: ἤθελε δʼ ὁ τλήμων Ὀδυσεύς, “and enduring Odysseus was willing” to slip into the throng of the Trojans (10.231–232), and Diomedes chooses him as his companion (10.242–247).
