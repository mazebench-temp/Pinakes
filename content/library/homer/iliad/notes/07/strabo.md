---
summary: Strabo reads Book 7 as a geographer. He quotes Aristotle on the Achaean wall in his argument about the site of Troy, places Pheia and the Iardanus of Nestor's tale in Elis, defends Hyle against Hyde in the line on Tychius, and uses Euneus on Lemnos to show that Homer knew the voyage of the Argo.
---

- The Achaean wall, “or it never existed, but the poet who invented it made it vanish, as Aristotle says” (*Geography* 13.1.36, 2.3.6): [[aristotle-and-the-wall]].
- **Pheia and the Iardanus.** Nestor fought the Arcadians “beside the walls of Pheia, about the streams of the Iardanus” (7.135). Following the coast of Elis, Strabo comes to a cape called Pheia, where there was once a small town, and quotes the verse, “for there is a small river nearby” (8.3.12). Farther south, in Triphylia, the meadow and tomb of Iardanus were shown (8.3.20). Some, he says, would read Ἀκίδοντι for Κελάδοντι and Χάας for Φειᾶς, putting the battle at Chaa, beside the river Acidon, which flows past the tomb of Iardanus, a place nearer to the Arcadians (8.3.21). See [[pheia]], [[iardanus]], [[celadon]] and [[nestor-and-ereuthalion]].
- The reading Hyde for Hyle in the line on Tychius (*Geography* 9.2.20, 13.4.6): [[hyle-or-hyde]].
- **Euneus.** Demetrius of Scepsis held that Homer did not know of Jason's voyage to Phasis. Strabo answers him from his own words: Demetrius says that Achilles sacked Lesbos but spared Lemnos, because of his kinship with Jason and with Jason's son Euneus, who then held the island. How could the poet know that, Strabo asks, and not know how Jason, a Thessalian of Iolcus, came to make his son master of Lemnos (1.2.38)? Homer agrees with the historians, he concludes, when he names Aeetes, speaks of Jason and the Argo, settles Euneus on Lemnos and makes the island dear to Achilles (1.2.40). See [[euneus]] and [[jason]].
