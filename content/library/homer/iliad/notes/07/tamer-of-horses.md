---
summary: The horse-tamer's epithet goes to Hector in Apollo's plan, to the Trojans in Paris' refusal, and to Diomedes when the Achaeans applaud his answer to the Trojan offer.
grc: [ἱπποδάμοις]
---

Apollo proposes to Athena: “Let us rouse the strong might of Hector, tamer of horses” (7.38), so that he will challenge one of the Danaans to single combat ([[krateros]]). The epithet that ends the Iliad with Hector's funeral (24.804) marks his entry into the plan of this book.

In the Trojan assembly Paris turns from Antenor to the people: “But I will speak out among the horse-taming Trojans” (7.361), and refuses to give back Helen ([[paris]], [[trojans]]). In the Achaean assembly the next morning, when Diomedes answers the Trojan offer that even a fool can see the end of Troy, “all the sons of the Achaeans shouted aloud, admiring the word of Diomedes, tamer of horses” (7.403–404; [[diomedes]], [[sons-of-the-achaeans]]).
