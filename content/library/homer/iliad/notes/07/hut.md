---
summary: The chiefs feast in Agamemnon's huts after the duel (7.313), and when the wall is built the army slaughters oxen among the huts for its supper (7.466).
grc: [κλισίῃσιν]
passages:
  - 7.313-322 | The feast in Agamemnon's huts
---

After the duel the Achaeans lead Ajax to Agamemnon, “and when they had come to the huts of the son of Atreus” (κλισίῃσιν ἐν Ἀτρεΐδαο, 7.313), the king sacrifices an ox and the chiefs feast (see [[sacrifice]] and [[feast]]). The king's hut is the place where the elders eat at his table and give counsel: here Nestor proposes the truce and the wall (7.324–344), and in Book 9 Nestor tells Agamemnon to give the elders a feast, since his huts are full of wine (9.70–72). The verse of 7.313 returns when the embassy comes back to Agamemnon from Achilles (9.669). When the wall is finished, “they slaughtered oxen among the huts and took their supper” (κατὰ κλισίας, 7.466), at the end of a day of building beside the ships (see [[the-achaean-wall]]).
