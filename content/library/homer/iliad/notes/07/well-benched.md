---
summary: Hector promises to give his victim's body back to “the well-benched ships,” and the Achaeans go out from their well-benched ships to gather their dead.
grc: [ἐϋσσέλμων]
---

In Hector's terms the ships are where the dead go home: “but his corpse I will give back to the well-benched ships, so that the long-haired Achaeans may give him burial” (7.84–85; [[long-haired]], [[tomb]]). On the day of the truce the same ships are where the burial parties set out from: “and the Argives on the other side, from their well-benched ships, hurried, some to bring the dead, and others to go for wood” (7.419–420). The second verse ends as the verse of the Trojans' preparations ended two lines before (7.418, 7.420; [[the-truce-for-the-dead]]). See [[ships]].
