---
summary: Apollo is “lord Apollo, son of Zeus” in the two verses that introduce his speeches to Athena at the oak.
---

The whole-verse formula of address, “Lord Apollo, son of Zeus, spoke to her first” (7.23) and “Then lord Apollo, son of Zeus, spoke to her in turn” (7.37), frames the conversation in which the god who favors the Trojans and the goddess who favors the Achaeans agree to stop the fighting for the day. He addresses her as “daughter of great Zeus” (7.24; [[daughter-of-zeus]]): the two children of Zeus settle the day between them. His plan, a duel between Hector and a single Achaean, is carried out, and his hand is in it at the end, when he sets Hector on his feet (7.272). See [[apollo]] and [[athena]].
