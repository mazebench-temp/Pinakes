---
summary: The Achaean dead lie along the river. Nestor asks for a truce because their “dark blood about the fair-flowing Scamander sharp Ares has now scattered” (7.328–330).
grc: [Σκάμανδρον]
---

Nestor's proposal begins at the river: “many long-haired Achaeans are dead, whose dark blood about the fair-flowing Scamander sharp Ares has now scattered, and their souls have gone down to Hades” (7.328–330; see [[nestor]], [[ares]] and [[hades]]). The battle of Books 5 and 6 was fought on the plain “between the streams of the Simoeis and the Xanthus” (6.4), and the dead are to be gathered from it with oxen and mules and burned a little way from the ships (7.332–334; see [[cremation]] and [[truce]]). ἐΰρροος, “fair-flowing”, is used in Homer only of this river (also 21.130; see [[fair-flowing]]).
