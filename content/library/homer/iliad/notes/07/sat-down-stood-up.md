summary: The line carries the Trojan assembly of Book 7 from speaker to speaker: Antenor sits and Paris stands, Paris sits and Priam stands (7.354–355, 7.365–366).
passages:
  - 7.345-379 | The Trojan assembly
---

The Trojan assembly is run by the procedure of the Achaean assemblies of Books 1 and 2 (1.68, 1.101, 2.76). Antenor proposes to give back Helen, and “He spoke thus and sat down, and among them stood up brilliant Alexander, the husband of Helen of the lovely hair” (7.354–355). Paris refuses the woman and offers the possessions, and the verse comes again, “and among them stood up Priam, descendant of Dardanus, a counselor equal to the gods” (7.365–366; see [[paris]], [[antenor]] and [[priam]]). The next verse names each new speaker with his titles, and Priam's is vouched for with the line of the good counselor (7.367; see [[with-good-intent]]).

In Book 1 the order broke down once the quarrel began. Here it holds through a quarrel: three men speak in turn, each answering the last, and the king speaks last and is obeyed (7.379; see [[assembly]]).
