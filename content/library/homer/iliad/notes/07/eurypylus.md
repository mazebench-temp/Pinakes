“And after them Eurypylus, the splendid son of Euaemon” (7.167; see [[euaemon]] and [[splendid]]). The second half of the verse is his title in the Catalogue and in the first battle (2.736, 5.79), and the whole verse returns in Book 8, among the leaders who follow Diomedes out against the Trojans, where Teucer comes ninth (8.265–266). The leader of forty ships from Thessaly (2.734–737) stands up with the foremost chiefs of the army. The lot falls to Ajax. His own scene comes in Book 11, when Paris wounds him in the thigh and Patroclus tends him (11.575–592, 11.806–848).
