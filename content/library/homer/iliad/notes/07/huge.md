---
summary: Ajax goes out to fight Hector “as huge Ares goes forth,” and when the simile ends the narrator gives him the same epithet, with his title “the bulwark of the Achaeans.”
---

{{quote:84a83657-6f10-570a-956c-7f66f042fa45}}

The adjective passes from the god to the man across the simile: “as huge Ares goes forth” (7.208), “Such was huge Ajax” (7.211). The words Αἴας … πελώριος ἕρκος Ἀχαιῶν in 7.211 are the ones with which Helen named Ajax for Priam from the wall (3.229; [[bulwark-of-the-achaeans]]). There his size was a thing seen from a distance; here it is the size of a man coming on in armor, and its effect is shown at once. The Argives rejoice, trembling seizes every Trojan, and Hector's heart pounds (7.214–216). See [[like-huge-ares]], [[ajax]], [[ares]] and [[blosyros]].
