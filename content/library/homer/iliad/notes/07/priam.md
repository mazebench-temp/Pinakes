**In the assembly.** The Trojans meet at his own doors (7.345–346; see [[priams-palace]]). He speaks last, after Antenor and Paris, and the narrator introduces him by his descent and his wisdom: “Priam, descendant of Dardanus, a counselor equal to the gods” (7.366; see [[dardanus]]). θεόφιν μήστωρ ἀτάλαντος is said also of Peirithous (14.318) and of Patroclus (17.477).

{{quote:adbe27b7-6231-5bdf-98ce-2034f6a3f811}}

He answers neither speaker. He does not take up Antenor's proposal to give Helen back, and he does not reprove his son. He orders supper and a watch, in words that Hector will use to the army on the plain (7.371 = 18.299), and sends Paris' offer to the ships with a proposal of his own, a truce “until we have burned the dead” (7.376–377; see [[the-truce-for-the-dead]] and [[cremation]]). Its last words are Hector's to Ajax at the end of the duel: “afterward we will fight again, until a god decides between us and gives victory to one side or the other” (7.377–378; compare 7.291–292). The Trojans “listened closely to him and obeyed” (7.379). The poem gives no reason for his silence about Helen. In Book 3 he told her that he did not blame her: “it is the gods who are to blame” (3.164; see [[who-is-to-blame]]).

**His herald.** At the ships Idaeus names as his senders “Priam and the other noble Trojans” (7.386), and delivers the offer with additions of his own (7.385–397; see [[idaeus]]).

**The dead.** When the truce is granted, both sides gather their dead at sunrise. On the Trojan side “great Priam did not allow them to wail; and in silence they heaped the dead on the pyre, grieving at heart” (7.427–428; see [[lament]], [[silence]] and [[honoring-the-dead]]). The poem does not say why he forbade it. The same father will wail aloud for Hector on the wall, and the whole city with him (22.408–409).

**His sons.** Helenus is “the dear son of Priam” (7.44; see [[helenus]]), and Hector is “son of Priam” to his brother (7.47) and to Agamemnon (7.112), and Πριαμίδης in the duel (7.250, 7.258; see [[hector]] and [[sons-of-priam]]). When the duel ends, Hector says he will go to gladden the Trojans “throughout the great city of lord Priam” (7.296).
