summary: The Trojans welcome Hector and Paris back to the battle, tremble when Ajax comes out against Hector, and rejoice to see Hector return alive. That night their assembly hears Antenor urge them to give back Helen; the next day they burn their dead in silence at Priam's command, and on the following night they feast in the city while Zeus thunders.

**On the field.** The Trojans welcome Hector and Paris as weary rowers welcome a fair wind (7.4–7; see [[the-fair-wind]]). Apollo pleads their cause to Athena: “you have no pity at all for the Trojans as they perish” (7.27; see [[apollo]]). When Ajax comes out, “a dread trembling came over the limbs of every Trojan” (7.215), and when Hector comes back from the duel alive and unhurt, they rejoice and bring him to the city, “though they had not hoped to see him safe” (7.306–310; see [[hector]] and [[ajax]]). Hector's promises are made in their name. If he falls, “the Trojans and the wives of the Trojans” are to give him his portion of fire (7.79–80); if he lives, he will gladden “the Trojans and the Trojan women with trailing robes”, who will pray for him (7.297–298; see [[trojan-women]]).

**The assembly.** At nightfall they meet on the height of the city, beside Priam's doors, in “a dread and turbulent” assembly (7.345–346; see [[priams-palace]]). Antenor tells them that they are fighting with the oaths broken and should give Helen back, Paris refuses, and Priam sends his son's offer to the Achaeans with a request for a truce (7.347–378; see [[antenor]], [[paris]] and [[priam]]). The only word of what the people thought comes from the herald at the ships: “though the Trojans, truly, urge him” to give her up (7.393; see [[idaeus]]). In Book 3 they hated Paris “like black doom” (3.454). Their epithets in the book are the familiar ones: “horse-taming” (7.361; see [[tamer-of-horses]]) and, in Nestor's mouth, “proud” (ἀγερώχων, 7.343; see [[proud]]).

**The dead.** At sunrise the two sides meet on the plain to gather their dead; “it was hard to tell each man apart”, and they washed off the blood and lifted the bodies onto wagons, weeping (7.421–426). Priam forbade the Trojans to wail, and they burned their dead in silence and went back to sacred Ilios (7.427–429; see [[the-truce-for-the-dead]], [[cremation]], [[lament]] and [[honoring-the-dead]]).

**The night.** Both armies feast all night, the Trojans in the city with their allies (7.476–477; see [[trojan-allies]]), while Zeus “devised evil for them, thundering terribly”. Pale fear takes hold of them, and no one dares to drink before he has poured wine to the son of Cronus (7.478–481; see [[libation]], [[zeus]] and [[war-and-night]]).
