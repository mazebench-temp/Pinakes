**Two stones.** Hector's stone lies ready on the plain, “black and jagged and great”, and he takes it up as he gives ground (7.264–265). The two verses return in the battle of the gods, where Athena steps back and takes up a boundary stone of the same description to strike Ares (21.403–404). Ajax answers with “a far greater stone”, whirled round and thrown with all his “measureless strength” (7.268–269). πολὺ μείζονα λᾶαν ἀείρας, “lifted a far greater stone”, is also said of the Cyclops, as he hurls his second rock at Odysseus' ship (Od. 9.537). μυλοειδής, “like a millstone”, occurs only here in Homer (see [[like-a-millstone]]). Hector's stone only makes the bronze ring; Ajax's breaks Hector's shield inward and knocks him flat, until Apollo sets him on his feet (7.270–272; see [[shield]] and [[divine-rescue]]). Of the Iliad's two formal duels, only this one comes to stones (see [[single-combat]]).

**Ajax's stones.** Ajax fells Hector with a stone once more. In Book 14 he picks up one of the stones that propped the ships and strikes Hector on the chest above the rim of his shield; Hector spins like a top and falls, and his companions carry him from the field (14.409–432). Both times the stone is Ajax's blow, and both times Hector lives to fight again (see [[ajax]] and [[hector]]).
