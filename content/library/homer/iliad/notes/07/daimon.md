At the end of the duel Hector proposes to stop: “let us now cease from battle and combat for today; afterward we will fight again, until a god decides between us and gives victory to one side or the other” (εἰς ὅ κε δαίμων ἄμμε διακρίνῃ, δώῃ δʼ ἑτέροισί γε νίκην, 7.290–292). That evening Priam uses the same words when he tells the Trojans to ask for a truce to burn their dead (7.376–378), and the next morning Idaeus repeats them in the Achaean assembly (7.395–397). The verse and a half is found only in this book (see [[nike]] and [[the-truce-for-the-dead]]).

δαίμων here is a god who is not named. Hector does not say whether it will be Zeus, whom Idaeus has just said loves both men (7.280), or Apollo, who raised him from the ground in the duel (7.272). The outcome of the war belongs to a divine power, and the speakers leave it at that. διακρίνω, “part, decide between”, is the verb of separating two fighters or two armies. Agamemnon said that there would be no pause in the war unless night came and parted the fury of men (διακρινέει, 2.387), and when Hector and Ajax go their ways, “the two parted” (διακρινθέντε, 7.306). Night parts them for a day; a god will part the armies for good (see [[yield-to-night]] and [[war-and-night]]).

Hector names a god once more in the speech, when he praises Ajax: “since a god (θεός) has given you stature and strength and good sense” (7.288; see [[strength-is-a-gift]] and [[theos]]).
