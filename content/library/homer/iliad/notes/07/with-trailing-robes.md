---
summary: Hector says that he will gladden “the Trojans and the Trojan women with trailing robes,” who will pray for him, when he returns from the duel.
---

{{quote:60545487-2876-59f2-8ac9-655fb4223a87}}

Hector names the same pair, Τρῶας … καὶ Τρῳάδας ἑλκεσιπέπλους, when he tells Andromache in Book 6 that he would feel shame before them if he held back from the war (6.442), and again as he waits for Achilles in Book 22 (22.105). Here the same people are those whom his survival will gladden, as Ajax's will gladden the Achaeans (7.294–297). The women who went to Athena's temple in Book 6 will go to the gods' gathering again, praying for him (7.298; [[divine]]). When the duel is over, the Trojans receive him as a man they had not hoped to see alive (7.306–310). See [[trojan-women]] and [[hector]].
