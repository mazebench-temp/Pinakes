---
summary: Hector's parting gift to Ajax is “a silver-studded sword,” given with its sheath and strap.
---

{{quote:55017447-7604-5bda-a664-2dcf193d8463}}

Elsewhere the silver-studded sword is slung from the shoulders when a hero arms (2.45, 3.334, 16.135, 19.372). Here it passes from one hero to the other as a gift, whole, “with its sheath and its well-cut strap” ([[well-cut]], [[sheath]], [[sword]]). Ajax answers with a belt ([[belt]], [[phaeinos]]). The same verse of the sheath and strap comes back at the funeral games of Patroclus, when Achilles gives Diomedes the silver-studded sword of Asteropaeus after an armed combat with Ajax that the Achaeans stop (23.807–808, 23.824–825). In the Odyssey the Phaeacian Euryalus makes amends for an insult with a silver-studded sword, and Odysseus accepts it with a blessing (Od. 8.396–415). In Sophocles' *Ajax* the sword Hector gave is the one on which Ajax falls ([[sophocles]]). See [[guest-gifts]] and [[the-duel-of-hector-and-ajax]].
