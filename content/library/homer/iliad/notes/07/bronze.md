---
summary: The bronze of Book 7 is the long-edged blade of Hector's challenge, the gleaming armor Ajax puts on, the spear-point that cuts through six layers of his shield, and a price paid for Lemnian wine (7.77, 7.206, 7.246–247, 7.473). Areithous' armor was the gift of “bronze Ares” (7.146).
passages:
  - 7.244-267 | Bronze in the duel
---

**The weapon.** The victor in Hector's duel will take his opponent's life “with the long-edged bronze” (ταναήκεϊ χαλκῷ, 7.77; see [[long-edged]]), and in the duel itself the bronze is the spear-point. Hector's strikes the bronze face of Ajax's shield and cuts its way inward: “Through six layers went the unyielding bronze, cutting its way” (χαλκὸς ἀτειρής, 7.247; see [[unyielding]]). His thrust fails on the same shield, “but the bronze did not break through” (7.259), the verse of Paris' failed throw in Book 3 (3.348).

**Armor.** Ajax “armed himself in gleaming bronze” (νώροπι χαλκῷ, 7.206; see [[gleaming-bronze]] and [[arming-scene]]). Over the seven oxhides of his shield Tychius laid an eighth layer of bronze (7.223), and when Hector's stone strikes its boss, “the bronze rang all around” (7.267; see [[the-shield-of-ajax]]). The Achaeans are “bronze-armored” in Book 7 as in Book 1 (7.275, 7.444; see [[bronze-armored]]), and in Nestor's story Areithous wore “the armor that bronze Ares had given him” (7.146; see [[bronze-ares]]).

**A price.** The Lemnian wine is bought “some with bronze, and some with gleaming iron” (7.473; see [[trade]]). Bronze stands first, as it does in the list of a rich man's treasures, “bronze and gold and iron worked with much toil” (6.48; see [[iron]]).
