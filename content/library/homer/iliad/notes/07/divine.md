θεῖον ἀγῶνα is “the divine gathering”: the sanctuary where the images of the gods stand together, or the gathering of worshippers there; at 18.376 the same phrase is the assembly of the gods on Olympus, into which Hephaestus' self-moving tripods will roll (see the translation's note on 7.298). The women with trailing robes who prayed to Athena in Book 6, and were refused (6.297–311), will go to the gods again, praying for him ([[with-trailing-robes]], [[trojan-women]], [[prayer]]). The Trojans do receive Hector “alive and unhurt,” though they had not hoped to see him safe (7.306–310). See [[hector]].
