summary: The possessions Paris brought from Sparta with Helen come back into the story. Antenor proposes to give back Helen and her possessions; Paris will give up the possessions and add more of his own, but not the woman; and Diomedes tells the Achaeans to accept neither (7.350–364, 7.389–393, 7.400–402).

**Antenor's proposal.** “Come now, let us give Argive Helen, and the possessions with her, to the sons of Atreus to take away” (7.350–351). The pair was the stake of the duel of Book 3, “Helen and all her possessions” (3.70, 3.91), and Antenor answers Agamemnon's demand at the end of that book in its own words: Ἀργείην Ἑλένην καὶ κτήμαθʼ ἅμʼ αὐτῇ, “Argive Helen and the possessions with her” (7.350 = 3.458; see [[antenor]] and [[helen]]).

**Paris' offer.** Paris refuses the woman outright, “but the possessions that I brought from Argos to our house I am willing to give, all of them, and to add more from my own” (7.363–364; see [[paris]]). He separates what Book 3 had joined. Priam sends the offer to the ships as Paris made it, and Idaeus delivers it in his words: all the possessions “that Alexander in his hollow ships brought to Troy”, and more besides, but not “the wedded wife of glorious Menelaus” (7.389–393; see [[heralds]] and [[message-repeated]]).

**Refused.** Diomedes answers for the army: “Let no one now accept the possessions of Alexander, nor Helen; it is plain even to one who is an utter fool that the bounds of destruction are already made fast upon the Trojans” (7.400–402; see [[diomedes]]). The possessions without Helen are not enough, and now Helen with them would not be enough either. The Achaeans shout their approval, and Agamemnon takes the shout for his answer (7.403–407; see [[assembly]]).
