---
summary: The formula of the challenge to single combat, “to fight face to face in grim combat,” is used of Apollo's plan and of Helenus' advice, and its genitive closes Agamemnon's and Nestor's speeches about the champion.
---

The verse ἀντίβιον μαχέσασθαι ἐν αἰνῇ δηϊοτῆτι, in which Paris challenged “all the best of the Argives” in Book 3 (3.19–20), is used twice at the start of Book 7. Apollo proposes that Hector challenge one of the Danaans “to fight him face to face in grim combat” (7.40), and Helenus passes the plan on to Hector in the same words: “to fight you face to face in grim combat” (7.51; [[single-combat]], [[helenus]]). The gods' plan and the seer's advice use the language of the formal duel.

The genitive αἰνῆς δηϊοτῆτος ends the same verse twice, “from destructive war and grim combat” (7.119, 7.174). Agamemnon speaks it of the man who comes out alive, and Nestor of the champion who will do his own heart good if he escapes ([[destructive]], [[insatiable]]). See [[the-duel-of-hector-and-ajax]].
