summary: Helen does not appear in Book 7, but the Trojans debate her return. Antenor proposes to give her back with her possessions, Paris refuses to give up the woman, and at the ships Idaeus reports his refusal and Diomedes tells the Achaeans to take neither the possessions nor Helen.
refs: [7.362, 7.392]
passages:
  - 7.345-364 | The Trojans debate Helen's return
---

Helen is named three times in the book (7.350, 7.355, 7.401) and is not seen. The Iliad shows her in person only in Books 3, 6 and 24.

**Argive Helen and the possessions.** Antenor proposes: “let us give Argive Helen, and the possessions with her, to the sons of Atreus to take away” (7.350–351; see [[antenor]] and [[argive-helen]]). The words are Agamemnon's demand after the duel of Book 3, “Argive Helen and the possessions with her” (3.458). The possessions (κτήματα) went with her in every settlement: whichever man won the duel was to take “all the possessions and the woman” home (3.72, 3.93; see [[possessions]]).

**The woman.** Paris refuses: “I will not give back the woman” (7.362; see [[paris]]). He does not name her. The narrator names him by her as he rises to speak, “the husband of Helen of the lovely hair” (7.355; see [[husband-of-helen]]).

**The wedded wife.** At the ships Idaeus gives her another name, “the wedded wife of glorious Menelaus” (7.392), the phrase Menelaus himself uses of her in Book 13 when he names the wrongs the Trojans did him (13.626; see [[idaeus]] and [[menelaus]]). The herald's aside, that Paris “had perished before that” (7.390), is the wish Helen will make for herself in her lament for Hector: “who brought me to Troy; I wish I had died before that” (ὡς πρὶν ὤφελλον ὀλέσθαι, 24.764). In Book 3 she wished that evil death had pleased her before she followed Paris to Troy (3.173–175; see [[never-born]]).

**Refused.** Diomedes refuses more than was offered: the army is to accept neither the possessions “nor Helen”, for “the bounds of destruction are already made fast upon the Trojans” (7.400–402; see [[diomedes]] and [[even-a-fool-can-see]]). The same offer comes into Hector's mind as he waits for Achilles below the wall, Helen and the possessions with her and half the wealth of Troy besides, and he puts the thought away (22.111–122).
