summary: The heralds Talthybius and Idaeus are “both of them wise,” and Idaeus “knew wise counsels,” when they part Hector and Ajax; in the Trojan assembly “wise Antenor” proposes to give Helen back.
grc: [πεπνυμένα μήδεα]
---

**The heralds.** The two heralds come between the champions, one from each side, “Talthybius and Idaeus, both of them wise” (7.276), the dual πεπνυμένω ἄμφω that Book 3 gave to Ucalegon and Antenor on the wall (3.148). Idaeus speaks for both, “the herald Idaeus, who knew wise counsels” (7.278). The phrase belongs to heralds. πεπνυμένα μήδεα εἰδώς describes one other man in Homer, Peisenor, the herald of Ithaca who puts the scepter in Telemachus' hand at the assembly (Od. 2.37–38), and the shorter πεπνυμένα εἰδώς (“knowing wise things”) is the standing epithet of Medon, the herald of Odysseus' house (Od. 4.696, 4.711, 22.361, 24.442). The Trojan herald Periphas is one who knows “kindly counsels” (φίλα φρεσὶ μήδεα εἰδώς, 17.325). See [[heralds]], [[talthybius]] and [[idaeus]].

**Antenor.** “And among them wise Antenor began to speak” (7.347). The elder whose house received the Achaean embassy in Book 3 (3.205–207) now proposes that the Trojans give back Helen and her possessions, since they are fighting with the oaths broken (7.348–353). Paris refuses (7.357–364). Wisdom in the Iliad's Trojan counselors is regularly the wisdom that is not taken: Polydamas, who has the same epithet, will advise Hector to withdraw into the city and be refused (18.249–313). See [[antenor]] and [[the-abduction-of-helen]].
