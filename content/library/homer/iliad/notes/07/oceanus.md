---
summary: On the morning of the truce the sun rises out of “gently flowing, deep-streaming Oceanus”.
---

The river that encircles the earth is the place where the sun rises and sets. On the morning of the truce for the dead, “The sun was just striking the fields, / rising out of gently flowing, deep-streaming Oceanus / up into the sky” (7.421–423; see [[helios]]). In the next book the sun sets in the same river: “and the bright light of the sun fell into Oceanus” (8.485).

The two epithets are rare. ἀκαλαρρείτης, “gently flowing”, from ἀκαλός, “quiet, gentle”, and ῥέω, “flow”, is found only here and in the same verse in the Odyssey, on the morning of the boar hunt (Od. 19.434; see [[gently-flowing]]). βαθύρροος, “deep-streaming”, is Oceanus' epithet again at 14.311 and in the Odyssey (Od. 11.13, 19.434; see [[deep-streaming]]), and once the Scamander's (21.8). βαθυρρείτης is its variant in Achilles' boast that not even the great strength of Oceanus can fight with Zeus (21.195). The river is quiet and deep at once, the calm edge of the world from which the light comes up onto the field of the dead.
