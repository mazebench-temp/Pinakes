---
summary: Apollo, “son of Zeus,” greets Athena as “daughter of great Zeus” when the two meet at the oak to settle the day's fighting.
---

The two children of Zeus meet between the armies, and the narrator and the god name them by their father in consecutive verses:

{{quote:198bebce-6f38-55e1-bf34-cea79b9a6f9d}}

Apollo is “son of Zeus” in the line that introduces him (7.23; [[son-of-zeus]]), and he addresses Athena as “daughter of great Zeus” (7.24). Brother and sister stand on opposite sides of the war. He asks her whether she has come to give the Danaans the victory, and proposes instead that they stop the war for the day (7.26–32; [[that-turns-the-battle]]). She agrees, and the plan they make, a single combat, is heard by Helenus as the voice of the gods (7.44–53; [[helenus]]). See [[athena]] and [[apollo]].
