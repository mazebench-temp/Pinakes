---
summary: Antenor proposes to give Helen “to the sons of Atreus to take away” (7.351), Priam sends Idaeus to the two brothers with Paris' offer (7.373), and Euneus of Lemnos gives them a thousand measures of wine apart from what he sells to the army (7.470–471).
---

The brothers are named together three times in the book, each time in dealings between the Achaean army and those outside it, the Trojans and the Lemnian Euneus. Antenor's proposal, δώομεν Ἀτρεΐδῃσιν ἄγειν, “let us give … to the sons of Atreus to take away” (7.350–351; see [[antenor]]), returns in Hector's thoughts as he waits for Achilles below the wall, δωσέμεν Ἀτρεΐδῃσιν ἄγειν (22.117; see [[hector]]). Priam sends Idaeus “to tell the sons of Atreus, Agamemnon and Menelaus”, the word of Alexander (7.373; see [[priam]]): the one is the commander, the other the husband whose wife is at issue. Idaeus delivers it to the whole assembly by Agamemnon's ship, “Son of Atreus, and you others” (7.385), and Agamemnon answers for all (7.405–411; see [[idaeus]], [[agamemnon]] and [[menelaus]]). Euneus, son of Jason, sends wine from Lemnos for the army to buy, and to the two brothers “apart” he gives a thousand measures (7.470–471; see [[euneus]] and [[wine]]).
