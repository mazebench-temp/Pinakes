{{quote:b6836b37-7d54-5541-9139-6ca3b7a637d8}}

This is the only use of ταλαύρινος outside the verse in which Ares, the warrior with the oxhide shield, is glutted with blood (5.289, 20.78, 22.267). Hector's own words explain it. He speaks of the oxhide, which he can swing to either side (7.238–239), so the epithet names the man who fights with the shield, as the translation takes it, and this passage is the best support in Homer for that sense (see the translation's notes on 5.289 and 7.238–239). The boast is made to the man with the greatest shield in the army ([[seven-oxhide]]), and the duel tests it: Ajax's spear goes through Hector's shield twice, and his stone breaks it inward (7.249–254, 7.260–262, 7.268–272).
