---
summary: Troy is “sacred Ilios” four times in Book 7: the goal of Athena's descent, the place where Hector would carry his victim's armor, and the city to which the herald and the burial parties return.
---

The formula Ἴλιος ἱρή marks the movements between the plain and the city. Athena darts down from Olympus “to sacred Ilios” (7.20), where Apollo comes to meet her. Hector promises that if he wins he will carry his victim's armor “to sacred Ilios” and hang it on Apollo's temple (7.82–83; [[hekatos]]). Idaeus goes back “to sacred Ilios” with Agamemnon's answer (7.413), and after the burning of the dead the Trojans “went back to sacred Ilios” (7.429), while the Achaeans went back to their hollow ships (7.432; [[hollow-ships]]). The verses of the two returns are built alike (7.429, 7.432), and the epithet sets the city against the camp in a day of mourning shared by both. See [[troy]] and [[the-truce-for-the-dead]].
