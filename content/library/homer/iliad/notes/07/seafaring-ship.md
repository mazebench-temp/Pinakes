---
summary: The seafaring ships are the Achaeans' camp and their way home: Hector threatens that the Achaeans may be beaten beside them, and Ajax says that Achilles lies idle among them.
---

Hector's challenge names the two ends of the war: “until either you take Troy of the fine towers or are yourselves beaten beside your seafaring ships” (7.71–72; [[of-the-fine-towers]]). The ships that crossed the sea are where the Achaeans may be destroyed, and the Trojans will fight their way to them in later books.

Ajax, answering Hector, puts Achilles among the same ships: “But he lies among his curved, seafaring ships, nursing his wrath against Agamemnon, shepherd of the people” (7.229–230; [[curved-ships]], [[shepherd-of-the-people]]). The seafaring ships do not sail: Achilles, who threatened to leave in Book 1, stays beside them (1.169–171). See [[ships]].
