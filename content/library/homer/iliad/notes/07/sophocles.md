---
summary: Sophocles' Ajax makes the gifts that Hector and Ajax exchange after their duel the instruments of both their deaths, has Teucer recall that Ajax's lot leapt first from the helmet, and names Ajax's son after the shield of seven oxhides.
---

- The sword Ajax falls on, the belt by which Hector was dragged, and the lot (*Ajax* 654–665, 815–822, 1026–1039, 1283–1287): [[sophocles-sword-and-belt]].
- **The shield.** Ajax leaves his son the shield of seven oxhides, “unbreakable”, and the boy's name, Eurysaces, “broad shield”, comes from it (*Ajax* 574–576). It is the shield Tychius made, which Ajax carries “like a tower” against Hector (7.219–223; see [[the-shield-of-ajax]] and [[sophocles-tecmessa]]).
