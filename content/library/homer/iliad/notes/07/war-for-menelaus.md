summary: Book 7 offers the terms of the war and refuses them. Menelaus volunteers to face Hector and is held back; in the Trojan assembly Antenor proposes giving back Helen and her possessions, and Paris will give back only the possessions; the Achaeans, led by Diomedes, will now take neither (7.94–122, 7.345–411).

  - 7.385-411 | Paris' offer refused
---

**The husband.** When no one answers Hector's challenge, Menelaus stands up and arms, as he fought Paris for Helen in Book 3. Agamemnon holds him back, since Hector is “far the mightier” (7.104–122; see [[menelaus]]). The man for whose honor the war is fought is not allowed to risk his life in it again.

**Antenor's proposal.** In the Trojan assembly Antenor urges the terms that Agamemnon demanded when the duel of Book 3 was over, in his words: “let us give Argive Helen, and the possessions with her” (7.350 = 3.458; see [[antenor]] and [[the-duel-of-paris-and-menelaus]]).

{{quote:940fba46-2889-589f-aad2-39ef84a8ed7c}}

Paris offers the possessions he brought from Argos, and more of his own, but not the woman (see [[paris]] and [[possessions]]). Priam does not argue. He sends Idaeus to carry Paris' word to the Achaeans, with a request for a truce to burn the dead (7.368–378; see [[the-truce-for-the-dead]]).

**The refusal.** Idaeus gives the offer in the assembly at the ships, and speaks of Helen as Menelaus did of his loss: “the wedded wife of glorious Menelaus he says he will not give” (κουριδίην ἄλοχον, 7.392; compare 13.626). The Achaeans are silent until Diomedes answers that no one should take the possessions “nor Helen”, for the Trojans are already doomed (7.400–402; see [[diomedes]] and [[sack-of-troy-foretold]]). The army shouts its approval, and Agamemnon makes their answer his (7.403–407). In Book 3 the return of Helen and her possessions was the prize of the duel (3.70–72, 3.458–460). Now the Trojans are divided on it, and the Achaeans will not have it even if offered. Hector will think once more of offering “Helen and the possessions with her” to save himself, and reject the thought (22.111–121; see [[hector]]).
