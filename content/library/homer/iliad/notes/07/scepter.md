**The heralds' scepters.** When the heralds come between Hector and Ajax, “between the two of them they held out their scepters, and a word was spoken by the herald Idaeus” (μέσσῳ δʼ ἀμφοτέρων σκῆπτρα σχέθον, 7.277–278; see [[heralds]]). The scepter is the herald's staff and the sign of his office, and held out between two fighting men it calls on them to stop and hear. On the shield of Achilles the elders who judge a case hold the heralds' scepters in their hands as they speak (18.505), and at the funeral games a herald puts the scepter into Menelaus' hand before he speaks to the army (23.567–568). Here the heralds' staffs come before the heralds' word, and both champions obey it (see [[single-combat]]).

**The king's oath.** Agamemnon, granting the Trojans' request for a truce to burn their dead, swears by Zeus, and “so saying, he lifted up his scepter to all the gods” (τὸ σκῆπτρον ἀνέσχεθε πᾶσι θεοῖσιν, 7.412; see [[oath]] and [[truce]]). His scepter came to him from Zeus through the house of Pelops (2.100–108), and it is raised to the gods as the witnesses of his word. Achilles swore by a scepter and threw it down (1.233–246), and Hector swears to Dolon holding one in his hands (10.321–331).
