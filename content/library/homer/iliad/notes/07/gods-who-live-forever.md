---
summary: Helenus tells Hector that he has heard “the voice of the everlasting gods” promising that he will not die in the duel.
---

Helenus has understood the plan of Athena and Apollo, and he urges Hector to challenge the best of the Achaeans:

{{quote:17f3cde2-8d84-59c8-8f1c-fe107efe024e}}

The narrator says that Helenus understood the plan “in his heart” (σύνθετο θυμῷ, 7.44); Helenus says that he heard the gods' voice (7.53). Whether he overheard Athena and Apollo or describes a seer's insight as hearing is debated (see the translation's note on 7.53; [[helenus]], [[divination]]). The formula of the gods' everlasting life stands beside his promise about a mortal's death: “it is not yet your fate to die and meet your end” (7.52; [[moira]]). The promise holds for this day. See [[hector]].
