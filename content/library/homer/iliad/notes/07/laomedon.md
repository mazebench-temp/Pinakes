---
summary: Poseidon, jealous of the Achaean wall, fears that men will forget the wall that he and Apollo built for “the hero Laomedon”.
---

Laomedon is named once in Book 7, at its end. As the gods watch the Achaeans build their wall, Poseidon complains that its fame will reach as far as the dawn spreads, “and men will forget the wall that I and Phoebus Apollo / toiled to build for the hero Laomedon” (7.452–453; see [[poseidon]] and [[the-walls-of-troy]]).

The words carry the old grievance. ἥρῳ, “the hero”, is a title of rank, like ἀγαυός, “noble”, which Sarpedon gave him when he told how his folly lost the city to Heracles (5.649). ἀθλήσαντε, “having toiled”, is the verb used of Heracles' labors (ἀθλήσαντα, 15.30), and the dual makes Apollo Poseidon's fellow laborer. In Book 21 Poseidon tells the rest. The two gods served Laomedon for a year for an agreed wage, and he cheated them of it and drove them away with threats (21.441–457; see [[poseidon-and-apollo-serve-laomedon]]). The wall he did not pay for is the one Poseidon is proud of here. Zeus answers the pride, not the grievance: Poseidon's fame will reach as far as the dawn spreads (7.458).
