**What is told.** The first verses name the victim and the god, as in Agamemnon's sacrifice before the first battle: an ox, five years old, “to the son of Cronus, supreme in might” (7.314–315 ~ 2.402–403; see [[sacrifice]]). Then the scene goes straight to the butchering:

**What is left out.** At Chryse and in Book 2 the pattern ran from the barley and the prayer through the killing to the thigh-bones wrapped in fat and burned for the god, and only then to the men's portion (1.458–466, 2.410–429). Book 7 names none of these. It keeps the last half-verse of the men's portion, “and drew it all off” (ἐρύσαντό τε πάντα, 7.318 = 1.466, 2.429), and the verses of the feast that follows (7.319–320 = 1.467–468; see [[feast-type-scene]]). The rest comes from another set of verses. “They flayed it and dressed it, and jointed it all” (7.316) is used when Autolycus' sons prepare an ox, “a male, five years old”, for their young kinsman Odysseus (Od. 19.420–422), and the next two verses when Achilles' companions prepare a sheep for Priam's supper (7.317–318 = 24.623–624). These are verses of a meal given in someone's honor, and the meal is the point of the scene: it ends with the chine for Ajax (7.321–322; see [[feast]]).
