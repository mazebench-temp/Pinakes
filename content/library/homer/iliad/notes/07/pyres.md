Nestor proposes one mound “around the pyre” for all the dead (ἀμφὶ πυρήν, 7.336), and both sides fetch wood on the day of the truce, “some to bring the dead, and others to go for wood” (7.418, 7.420; see [[cremation]] and [[truce]]). Each army heaps its dead on a single pyre: “in silence they heaped the dead on the pyre, grieving at heart” (νεκροὺς πυρκαϊῆς ἐπινήνεον, 7.428, 7.431). πυρκαϊή, the place where the fire burns (πῦρ, καίω), is the word for a pyre in the funeral of Patroclus as well (23.158, 23.225, 23.237) and for Hector's in the last book (24.791). The Achaean pyre becomes the center of their building: before dawn “a chosen band of the Achaeans was astir” around it, heaped a mound around it, and built the wall against the mound (7.434–437; see [[tomb]] and [[the-achaean-wall]]). At Hector's funeral the Trojans gather “around the pyre” at dawn in the same verse (24.789 ~ 7.434), and quench it with wine before they gather his bones (24.791–793).
