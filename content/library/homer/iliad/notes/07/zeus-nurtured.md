---
summary: Agamemnon calls his brother mad, “Menelaus, nurtured by Zeus,” as he holds him back from fighting Hector.
grc: [διοτρεφές]
---

{{quote:d3d7b0a7-bf5c-566a-8f50-c67948c29fd2}}

The honorific of kings frames a rebuke: “You are mad, Menelaus, nurtured by Zeus” (7.109). The address grants Menelaus his rank as it tells him that his courage is folly (ἀφραίνεις, ἀφροσύνης). Agamemnon's reason is Menelaus' life: Hector is the better man, and even Achilles, he says, shudders to meet him (7.111–114; [[where-men-win-glory]]). The narrator approves: “urging what was due” (7.121). The vocative διοτρεφές is common in address to kings and heroes (for example 10.43, 17.685). See [[menelaus]] and [[agamemnon]].
