---
summary: Book 7 names Zeus as son of Cronus six times: in Hector's charge that he did not fulfill the oaths, in the army's prayers for Ajax, in the simile of Ares, and at the sacrifice and the libation that close the two feasts.
---

**Oaths and prayers.** Hector opens his challenge with the patronymic and the charge: “The son of Cronus, throned on high, did not fulfill our oaths” (7.69; [[high-throned]]). Ajax tells the army to pray “to lord Zeus, son of Cronus,” in silence or openly, while he arms, and they do so in the same words (7.194, 7.200; [[prayer]]).

**The simile.** Ajax goes out “as huge Ares goes forth when he goes to war among men, whom the son of Cronus has brought together to fight in the fury of heart-devouring strife” (7.208–210; [[huge]], [[heart-devouring]]). It is Zeus who sets men against each other, and the war god who goes among them.

**Sacrifice and libation.** Agamemnon sacrifices an ox “to the son of Cronus, supreme in might” when Ajax comes back from the duel (7.314–315; [[five-years-old]]), and at the end of the book the Achaeans, afraid of the thunder, will not drink before they have poured a libation “to the son of Cronus, supreme in might” (7.480–481; [[libation]]). The same line-end closes both scenes ([[supreme-in-might]]). See [[zeus]].
