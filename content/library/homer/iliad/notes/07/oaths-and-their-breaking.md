summary: The broken truce of Book 4 is argued over in Book 7. Hector lays the failure of the oaths on Zeus, Antenor admits that the Trojans are fighting with the oaths broken, and Paris will not make amends; at the end Agamemnon swears a new and smaller truce, to burn the dead, and both sides keep it.

**Hector blames Zeus.** Hector opens his challenge with the oaths of Book 3: “The son of Cronus, throned on high, did not fulfill our oaths, but, meaning evil, he ordains it for both sides” until one side is destroyed (7.69–72; see [[tekmor]]). He says nothing of Pandarus' arrow, which Agamemnon had called the Trojans' breach of the truce (4.155–168; see [[who-is-to-blame]]). For Hector the oaths failed because Zeus did not bring them to fulfillment, as Zeus had foreseen: when the armies swore, the narrator said that Zeus “was not yet going to fulfill their prayer” (3.302). Then he makes a sworn agreement of his own for the duel: “And this I declare, and let Zeus be our witness” (ἐπιμάρτυρος, 7.76), and gives his terms for the loser's body (7.77–86; see [[mythos]] and [[honoring-the-dead]]).

**Antenor admits it.** In the Trojan assembly Antenor says what Hector would not:

{{quote:b71c86cc-1d70-56b9-a7f0-249b19e66c6a}}

ψευσάμενοι, “having played false”, is the word of Agamemnon's charge that the Trojans are liars whom Zeus will not help (ψευδέσσι, 4.235; see [[no-helper-to-liars]]). Antenor draws the conclusion that the war will go badly unless the Trojans give back what the oaths promised, Helen and the goods (3.285; see [[antenor]]). Paris refuses the woman (7.357–364), and the Achaeans refuse everything else (7.400–404). The breach stands, and Diomedes reads it as a sign of Troy's end (see [[even-a-fool-can-see]]).

**A truce kept.** Agamemnon grants the Trojans' second request, a truce to burn the dead, and swears it without sacrifice: “As for the oaths, let Zeus be witness, the loud-thundering husband of Hera”, and he lifts his scepter to all the gods (7.411–412; see [[oath]], [[scepter]] and [[loud-thundering]]). The Trojans set out to gather their dead as soon as Idaeus reports it, and both armies burn their dead the next morning without a blow struck (7.414–432; see [[the-truce-for-the-dead]]). The great oaths of Book 3 were broken within a day; this small one, for the dead, is kept.
