summary: Brothers stand beside brothers in Book 7. Paris goes out to battle with Hector, Helenus claims a brother's hearing for his advice, and Agamemnon persuades his brother Menelaus not to fight Hector; Hector tells Ajax to go and gladden his kinsmen and companions.

**Hector's brothers.** Book 6 ended with Paris catching up with Hector at the gates, and Book 7 begins with them going out together: “and with him went Alexander, his brother” (ἀδελφεός, 7.2), both eager for war (see [[paris]] and [[hector]]). Hector's rebuke is behind them, and Paris kills the first man of the book (7.8–10). Then Helenus, who sent Hector into the city in Book 6, comes to him with the gods' plan and gives the tie as his reason to be heard: “will you listen to me now? For I am your brother” (κασίγνητος, 7.48; see [[helenus]] and [[peitho]]).

**The sons of Atreus.** On the other side Agamemnon holds back his brother. Menelaus has armed to face Hector, and Agamemnon takes him by the right hand and talks him out of it; “the hero persuaded his brother's mind” (ἀδελφειοῦ φρένας, 7.120; see [[menelaus]], [[agamemnon]] and [[phrenes]]). He calls the wish madness, and tells him to hold back, “though you are grieved” (κηδόμενός περ, 7.109–110). The two sons of Atreus are named together as the Trojans' addressees (7.373) and as the receivers of Euneus' special gift of wine, a thousand measures given them “apart” (7.470–471; see [[atreidae]] and [[euneus]]).

**Kinsmen.** Hector ends the duel by telling Ajax that he may now gladden all the Achaeans at the ships, “and most of all your kinsmen and companions, who are yours” (ἔτας καὶ ἑταίρους, 7.295; see [[hetairos]]). ἔται are the men of one's own family and clan, the kin who bury a man (16.456; see [[honoring-the-dead]]).
