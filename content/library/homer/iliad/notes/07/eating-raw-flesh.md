---
summary: Hector and Ajax fall on each other “like lions that eat raw flesh or wild boars,” in the two verses of a simile from Book 5.
---

{{quote:f3c34dc7-e6ed-5219-ba4b-a5e8a643656f}}

The two verses are used in Book 5 of the Achaean chiefs crowded around Diomedes (5.782–783), and in Book 15 the first of them describes the Trojans as they rush at the ships (15.592–593). Here they describe two men at the moment when the duel turns from throwing to close fighting ([[long-spears]]). The lions are beasts of prey, and so are the two champions; the boars add the strength of an animal that stands and fights, “whose strength is no weakling's” (7.257; [[lion]], [[boar]], [[lions-and-boars]]).
