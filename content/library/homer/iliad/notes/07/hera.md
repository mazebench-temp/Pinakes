---
summary: Hera does not appear in Book 7. Apollo counts her among the goddesses who want Troy sacked, and Agamemnon swears the truce for the dead by “the loud-thundering husband of Hera”.
refs: [7.32]
---

**The goddesses who want Troy sacked.** Apollo tells Athena that the city will fall in the end, “since this is what has pleased the hearts / of you immortal goddesses, to sack this city” (7.31–32). ὑμῖν ἀθανάτῃσι is feminine and plural. The goddesses are Athena and Hera, who sat together in Book 4 “devising evils for the Trojans” (4.20–21; see [[athena]] and [[apollo]]). The next day Hera tries to rouse Poseidon to help the Achaeans against the will of Zeus (8.198–211; see [[poseidon]]).

**The husband of Hera.** When Agamemnon grants the Trojans a truce to burn their dead, he swears: “As for the oaths, let Zeus be witness, the loud-thundering husband of Hera” (7.411; see [[oath]], [[loud-thundering]] and [[zeus]]). ἐρίγδουπος πόσις Ἥρης is a phrase of oaths and wishes. Hector swears by it to Dolon (10.329), and in the Odyssey it closes wishes for a safe homecoming (Od. 8.465, 15.112, 15.180).
