Hector's first victim in Book 7 is struck in the neck below his helmet “with his sharp spear” (ἔγχεϊ ὀξυόεντι, 7.11; [[well-bronzed]], [[eioneus]]), the compound adjective that Book 5 gave to Menelaus' spear, and to his and Aeneas' spears as the two faced each other (5.50, 5.568).

The simple adjective describes the war god, as in Odysseus' “sharp Ares” (4.352). Nestor, proposing the truce and the wall, speaks of the dead:

{{quote:772d4e21-48e0-5d4e-bfd2-b1454b4f3214}}

Here Ares is the name for the killing itself: “sharp Ares” scatters the blood, and the souls go down to Hades (7.329–330; [[ares]], [[dark-blood]], [[hades]]). Nestor's answer is to stop the war for a day and burn the dead (7.331–335; [[the-truce-for-the-dead]]).
