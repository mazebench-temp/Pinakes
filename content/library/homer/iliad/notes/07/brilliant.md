---
summary: δῖος marks Hector four times as the man to be fought, and praises Areithous in Nestor's story, Odysseus among the volunteers, Agamemnon receiving the victor, and Alexander rising to refuse to give back Helen.
grc: [δίου, "!^Δίου"]
---

**Hector.** The dative Ἕκτορι δίῳ closes the verse three times, always of the man the Achaeans must face. Apollo plans that the Achaeans will send out “one man alone to fight with brilliant Hector” (7.42); Hector himself asks for a man “to be champion against brilliant Hector” (7.75), giving himself the epithet in his own challenge; and the nine volunteers “were willing to fight with brilliant Hector” (7.169). Ajax, when his lot is drawn, says he thinks he will defeat “brilliant Hector” (7.192).

**Others.** In Nestor's story the mace-man is “brilliant Areithous” (7.138; [[areithous]]). The genitive δίου must be kept apart from Δίου, the name of the town Dium (2.538; [[dium]]), which differs from it only by its capital. Odysseus is “brilliant Odysseus” among the nine volunteers (7.168; [[odysseus]]). The Achaeans lead Ajax “to brilliant Agamemnon” after the duel (7.312; [[agamemnon]]). And the epithet goes, as in Book 3, to Paris: in the Trojan assembly “brilliant Alexander, the husband of Helen of the lovely hair,” stands up to refuse to give Helen back (7.355; [[husband-of-helen]], [[paris]]). See [[phaidimos]].
