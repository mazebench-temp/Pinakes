The kings spring up to stop Menelaus from facing Hector, “and the son of Atreus himself, wide-ruling Agamemnon, taken you by the right hand, and spoken to you, and called you by name” (7.107–108). δεξιτερῆς ἕλε χειρός, “took by the right hand”, fills the first half of the verse, where the whole-line formula has a stroke of the hand or a clinging grip (1.361, 6.253), and ἔπος τʼ ἔφατʼ ἔκ τʼ ὀνόμαζεν follows. The whole verse occurs only here; in the Odyssey Nestor takes Telemachus by the hand before the same half-line (Od. 3.374).

The sentence is spoken to Menelaus himself. The narrator has turned to him (“Then, Menelaus, the end of your life would have appeared”, 7.104), and the name Agamemnon calls out is the one the narrator has just used: “You are mad, Menelaus, nurtured by Zeus” (7.109; see [[apostrophe]]). The gesture is a brother's, and it restrains as much as it comforts. The half-line does not always introduce kind words: Hector rebukes Melanippus with it (15.552), and in the Odyssey Antinous rebukes the beggar Irus (Od. 18.78). Agamemnon's speech does both. It calls Menelaus' eagerness madness, and it sends him back to sit among his companions with a reason that spares his pride: even Achilles, Agamemnon says, shudders to meet Hector (7.109–119). The claim is usually taken as an exaggeration meant to keep Menelaus out of the fight (see [[agamemnon]] and [[menelaus]]).
