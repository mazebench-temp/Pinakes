The long day of fighting that began in Book 2 ends in Book 7 without a sunset. The heralds part Hector and Ajax because “night is already coming on” (7.282, 7.293; see [[yield-to-night]] and [[war-and-night]]). The sun marks the next two days. On the morning of the truce, as both sides go out to gather their dead, the sun rises out of Oceanus:

{{quote:88ccc4c6-1bc4-57dc-b184-f1835f43bdaf}}

προσέβαλλεν ἀρούρας, “was just striking the fields”, gives the moment when the first light reaches the plain. The same two verses begin the day of the boar hunt on Parnassus in the Odyssey, where Odysseus got the scar by which his nurse knows him (Od. 19.433–434). Most days in the Iliad begin with a verse for Dawn (see [[dawn-line]] and [[eos]]). This day began with Idaeus setting out “at dawn” (7.381), and the sun rises only when the two armies meet on the plain, on the grim work of washing the dead and burning them (7.424–432; see [[cremation]] and [[oceanus]]).

The next day ends with a sunset: “and the sun set, and the work of the Achaeans was finished” (δύσετο δʼ ἠέλιος, 7.465). The Achaeans have built their wall from the half-light before dawn to the going down of the sun (7.433–465; see [[the-achaean-wall]] and [[sunset-lines]]).
