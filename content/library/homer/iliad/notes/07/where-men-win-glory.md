---
summary: Agamemnon warns Menelaus that even Achilles, “in the battle where men win glory,” shudders to meet Hector.
---

{{quote:d3d7b0a7-bf5c-566a-8f50-c67948c29fd2}}

The epithet of battle comes in a claim that has long puzzled readers. Achilles himself says that while he fought, Hector would not venture beyond the Scaean gates, and once barely escaped him (9.352–355), and the claim here is usually taken as Agamemnon's exaggeration, meant to keep his brother out of the fight (see the translation's note on 7.114). The battle where men win glory is the place where Menelaus would win death instead: “the end of your life would have appeared at the hands of Hector” (7.104–105; [[apostrophe]], [[would-have-had-not]]).
