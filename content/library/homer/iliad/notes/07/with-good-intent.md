summary: The line vouches for two counselors in Book 7: Nestor at the feast in Agamemnon's huts, proposing the truce and the wall (7.326), and, for the first time, a Trojan, Priam in the Trojan assembly (7.367). Both are heeded, and their counsels meet in the same truce.
grc: ["ἐϋφρονέων ἀγορήσατο"]
passages:

  - 7.365-379 | Priam's counsel
---

**Nestor.** The credentials come first: “the old man first of all began to weave a plan for them, Nestor, whose counsel had shown itself best before” (7.324–325). Then the line (7.326), and then the plan: stop the fighting at dawn, burn the dead, heap a single mound, and build against it a wall with towers and gates and a ditch (7.327–343; see [[truce]] and [[the-achaean-wall]]). “So he spoke, and all the kings approved” (7.344). The four verses that bring him in return in Book 9, where his counsel is the embassy to Achilles (7.323–326 = 9.92–95), and in the Odyssey Nestor is introduced with the verse of his credentials when he stops the Achaeans' flight at the funeral of Achilles (Od. 24.51–53).

**Priam.** The Trojan assembly has quarreled: Antenor proposed to give back Helen, and Paris refused (7.347–364). Then “Priam, descendant of Dardanus, a counselor equal to the gods” stands up, and the line vouches for him (7.366–367). It is the first time the Iliad gives it to a Trojan, and it will give it to Polydamas, Hector's adviser, in Book 18 (18.253). His counsel steps around the quarrel: supper, a watch for the night, and a herald at dawn with Paris' offer and a request for a truce to burn the dead (7.370–378). “So he spoke, and they listened closely to him and obeyed” (7.379; see [[priam]] and [[assembly]]).

**Heeded.** In Book 1 the line introduced two advisers who were rebuffed, and in Book 2 two who were followed. In Book 7 both are followed, and the two counsels meet: the truce that Nestor proposed to the Achaean kings is the truce that Priam's herald comes to ask for (7.331–335, 7.375–378).
