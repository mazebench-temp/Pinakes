summary: Nestor recalls Peleus as “the brave counselor and speaker of the Myrmidons,” who questioned him about the lineage of all the Argives.

{{quote:b9d3ffb7-ddf4-5eea-b575-5273c1a8392e}}

βουληφόρος ἠδʼ ἀγορητής, “counselor and speaker,” names the two parts of a leader's work with words: advice in council and speech in the assembly. It is what Peleus wanted for his son. In Book 9 Phoenix says that Peleus sent him with Achilles to teach him “to be a speaker of words and a doer of deeds” (9.442–443). Nestor, himself the counselor of the Achaeans, remembers Peleus as a man of counsel who delighted in the lineages of the Argives, and imagines his grief at their cowardice ([[peleus]], [[horse-driving]], [[myrmidons]]).
