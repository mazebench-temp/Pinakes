---
summary: On the night after the duel the Trojans hold their assembly “beside Priam's doors” (7.346), where they had met in Book 2.
---

{{quote:dec92053-39d6-5682-88f7-1574288839ed}}

The Trojans met at Priam's doors in Book 2 as well, “all gathered together, young and old”, when Iris came to them in the likeness of Polites (2.788–789). The palace stands “on the height of the city” (6.317), and the assembly of Book 7 is placed “on the height of the city of Ilios” (7.345; see [[troy]]). It is “dread and turbulent” (δεινὴ τετρηχυῖα, 7.346): Antenor proposes to give Helen back, Paris refuses, and the king speaks last, at his own doors (7.347–378; see [[antenor]], [[paris]], [[priam]] and [[assembly]]). The next day the Trojans and Dardanians sit in assembly again to wait for Idaeus (7.414–416); the poem does not say where.
