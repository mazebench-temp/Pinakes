---
summary: Helenus gives Hector the title of Odysseus, “the equal of Zeus in counsel,” as he passes on the plan he has heard from the gods.
grc: [Διὶ μῆτιν ἀτάλαντε]
---

{{quote:17f3cde2-8d84-59c8-8f1c-fe107efe024e}}

The formula belongs to Odysseus in Book 2 (2.169, 2.407, 2.636). Hector has it twice in the poem, both times in the vocative and both times from a messenger who brings him the gods' will: Helenus here, who has heard “the voice of the everlasting gods” (7.53; [[gods-who-live-forever]]), and Iris in Book 11, who opens her message from Zeus with the same verse (11.200–201). The praise of his counsel comes when the counsel is not his own. Hector takes the advice at once and with joy (7.54; [[helenus]], [[hector]]).
