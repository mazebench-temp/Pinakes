**The absent judge.** Nestor shames the Achaeans with an old man's grief, and he chooses the father of the man who is missing from the fight (see [[nestor]] and [[achilles]]). Peleus is γέρων ἱππηλάτα, “old horse-driving Peleus” (7.125; see [[horse-driving]]), a phrase that returns three times (9.438, 11.772, 18.331). He is also ἐσθλὸς Μυρμιδόνων βουληφόρος ἠδʼ ἀγορητής, “the brave counselor and speaker of the Myrmidons” (7.126; see [[myrmidons]] and [[man-of-counsel]]). ἀγορητής is Nestor's own title, “the clear-voiced orator of the Pylians” (1.248, 4.293; see [[clear-voiced-orator]]): one old speaker praises another.

**The host.** Peleus once questioned him in his house and “rejoiced greatly to learn the lineage and birth of all the Argives” (7.127–128; see [[genee]]). Book 11 tells of a visit that Nestor and Odysseus paid to Peleus' house when they were gathering the army, and found the old man burning the thighs of an ox for Zeus in his courtyard (11.769–775). Now Nestor pictures him lifting his hands to the gods, praying that his spirit might go down to the house of Hades (7.129–131). The poem's other pictures of Peleus are of a father far away, growing old without his son. Achilles thinks him dead already, or barely alive and waiting for news of his son's death (19.334–337), and he tells Priam that he will not be there to care for Peleus in his old age (24.538–542; see [[fathers-and-sons]]).
