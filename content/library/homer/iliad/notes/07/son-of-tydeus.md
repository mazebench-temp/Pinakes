---
summary: Diomedes is the son of Tydeus twice in Book 7: as he rises second among the volunteers, and in the army's prayer that the lot may fall to him.
---

When Nestor has shamed the Achaeans, “after him rose the son of Tydeus, mighty Diomedes” (7.163; [[krateros]]), second only to Agamemnon. When the lots are shaken, the army prays that the lot may fall “to Ajax, or the son of Tydeus, or the king himself of Mycenae” (7.179–180; [[rich-in-gold]]). The hero of Books 5 and 6 is the army's second choice. He is named by his father as in Book 4, where Agamemnon held up Tydeus' deeds to shame him (4.370–400). Nestor has just used the same kind of story of his own youth to shame them all (7.132–160). See [[diomedes]], [[tydeus]] and [[nestor-and-ereuthalion]].
