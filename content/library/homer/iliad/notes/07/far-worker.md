---
summary: Athena addresses Apollo as “Far-Worker” when she agrees to his plan to stop the fighting for the day.
grc: [ἑκάεργε]
---

Apollo has proposed that the two of them stop the war for the day. Athena answers:

{{quote:84f9d3f8-77b7-5d5c-9967-a104554f3e98}}

The title is the one she gives the god who, a few lines earlier, “wanted victory for the Trojans” (7.21). The vocative ἑκάεργε is used twice more in the Iliad, both times in a moment of hostility: Artemis taunts Apollo for giving way to Poseidon in the battle of the gods (21.472–473), and Achilles, tricked into chasing Apollo away from the city, calls him the most destructive of the gods (22.15). Here the address is courteous, and the two gods, on opposite sides of the war, make a plan together (7.37–42; [[athena]], [[apollo]]). See [[hekatos]] and [[god-of-the-silver-bow]], his other titles in this book.
