{{quote:49b62789-a319-5614-b469-0ea22b08bbda}}

The singular νηῒ πολυκλήϊδι belongs to the traveler's verse: a single ship, rowed by many, on the open sea ([[wine-dark]]). The man who passes the tomb on the Hellespont and speaks of it is a sailor, and the tomb stands on the shore by which he sails (7.86; [[broad-hellespont]], [[born-hereafter]]). In Book 2 the plural named the ships in which the army might flee home (2.74, 2.175). The same singular returns when Agamemnon, in Book 8, reminds Zeus that he never passed his altar “in my many-benched ship” on the way to Troy without sacrifice (8.238–241). See [[ships]].
