summary: Hector challenges “the best of all the Achaeans”, and Book 7 is a trial of who that is while Achilles is away. Nine leaders volunteer, the army prays for Ajax, Diomedes or Agamemnon, and the lot falls to the one they wanted, Ajax, who fights “even after Achilles” and is praised by Hector as the mightiest of the Achaeans with the spear.

**The measure is Achilles.** The first to measure the Achaeans against Hector is Agamemnon, holding his brother back: Hector is “a man better than you”, and “even Achilles … shudders to meet this man, and he is far better than you” (7.111–114). The claim is usually taken as Agamemnon's exaggeration, meant to keep Menelaus out of the fight; Achilles himself says that while he fought, Hector would not come out beyond the Scaean gates (9.352–355; see the translation's note on 7.114 and [[achilles]]). Either way, Achilles is the standard by which the others are judged, and he is absent.

**Nine volunteers.** Shamed by Nestor, nine men stand up: Agamemnon first, then Diomedes, the two Ajaxes, Idomeneus and Meriones, Eurypylus, Thoas and Odysseus (7.162–169). The list is a roll of the best Achaeans after Achilles, and the army's prayer at the lots narrows it to three: “Father Zeus, let the lot fall to Ajax, or the son of Tydeus, or the king himself of Mycenae rich in gold” (7.179–180). The two great fighters come first, and the king last. The lot leaps out of the helmet, “the lot that they themselves wanted, the lot of Ajax” (7.182–183; see [[lots]]).

**After Achilles.** Ajax names the absent man as he faces Hector:

{{quote:e5e698a5-f8cb-58d8-be86-2e911e62618d}}

καὶ μετʼ Ἀχιλλῆα may mean “next after Achilles”, in rank, or “even without Achilles”, now that he has withdrawn; the translation keeps both open (see its note on 7.228). The Catalogue of Ships had given the poem's own verdict: “Of the men the best by far was Telamonian Ajax, while Achilles kept up his wrath; for he was by far the strongest” (2.768–769). Later books put it in a formula: Ajax, who in form and in deeds surpassed the other Danaans “after the blameless son of Peleus” (17.279–280; *Odyssey* 11.469–470, 11.550–551, 24.17–18). See [[ajax]].

**Praised by the enemy.** When the heralds part them, Hector concedes it: “Ajax, since a god has given you stature and strength and good sense, and with the spear you are the mightiest of the Achaeans” (7.288–289; see [[strength-is-a-gift]]). That evening Agamemnon honors Ajax with the chine at his feast (7.321; see [[geras]]). The duel settled nothing between the armies, but it settled Ajax's standing among the Achaeans while Achilles is away.
