summary: The Trojan herald finds the Achaeans in assembly, “the Danaans, attendants of Ares,” beside the stern of Agamemnon's ship.
grc: [θεράποντας Ἄρηος]
---

{{quote:a5dd33b4-b391-5218-a1af-8b9806d27637}}

The formula is usually heard in the address ὦ φίλοι ἥρωες Δαναοί, θεράποντες Ἄρηος, with which Agamemnon opened his speech to test the army in Book 2 and Nestor urged it on in Book 6 (2.110, 6.67). Here the narrator uses it, in the accusative, of the army at rest: the attendants of Ares are sitting in assembly on the morning after the duel. They answer the Trojan offer with silence, then with Diomedes' refusal and their own shout (7.398–404; [[good-at-the-war-cry]]). Through Agamemnon the war god's servants grant only a truce to burn the dead (7.405–411; [[the-truce-for-the-dead]]). See [[danaans]] and [[assembly]].
