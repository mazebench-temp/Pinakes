---
summary: Poseidon begins his complaint about the Achaean wall by asking whether any mortal “on the boundless earth” will still tell his mind and his plans to the immortals.
---

{{quote:d1ff4c64-f30c-58eb-b8b9-2a8e35dcc30e}}

The epithet that describes the sea when Achilles looks out over it in Book 1 (1.350) describes the earth here, in the phrase ἐπʼ ἀπείρονα γαῖαν. The boundless earth is the whole world of mortals, set against the immortals whom they no longer consult. Poseidon's complaint is that men have built without asking the gods and without offering hecatombs (7.449–450; [[famous]], [[hecatomb]]). The god of the sea and the earthquake speaks for the gods against the whole race of men ([[earth-shaker]], [[immortals]]).
