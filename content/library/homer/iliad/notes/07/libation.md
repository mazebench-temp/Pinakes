**Poured in fear.** The custom is the one Hecuba stated to Hector: the gods' share is poured before men drink (6.258–260). What is new is the reason. The Achaeans are feasting on the wine they have just bought from the Lemnian ships, and the Trojans are feasting in the city (7.467–477; see [[trade]] and [[feast]]). Zeus “devised evil for them, thundering terribly”, and the libation answers the thunder (see [[teras]] and [[propitiation]]). λεῖψαι is the aorist of λείβω, “pour in drops”, the verb of Chryses' libation over the thigh-pieces (λεῖβε, 1.463) and of Hector's refusal to pour with unwashed hands (λείβειν, 6.266). οὐδέ τις ἔτλη, “no one dared”, makes the pouring a thing no one would risk leaving undone.

**On the ground.** The wine goes “on the ground” (χαμάδις), as the wine of the oath did in Book 3, poured out with a curse on whoever broke the oaths (3.295–300; see [[oath]]). There it was the image of the perjurer's fate. Here no words are spoken, and it is not said whether the feasters of either side knew what Zeus intended. The audience learns it at the next dawn. Zeus forbids the gods to help either side, then thunders from Ida and sends a blazing flash among the Achaeans, and pale fear takes hold of them all, in the words of 7.479 (8.1–27, 8.75–77).
