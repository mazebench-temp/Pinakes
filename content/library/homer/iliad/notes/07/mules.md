---
summary: Nestor proposes that the Achaeans gather their dead and bring them to the pyre “with oxen and mules” (7.332–333). Mules and oxen draw the wagons of the dead as they will draw the wood for the funerals of Patroclus and Hector.
grc: [ἡμιόνοισιν]
---

After the duel Nestor advises a pause in the fighting: “and we ourselves will gather and wheel the dead here with oxen and mules; and then we will burn them” (7.332–333). κυκλήσομεν, “we will wheel”, is from κύκλος, “wheel”: the dead are to be carried on wagons, and on the day of the truce the Trojans lift their washed dead onto wagons (ἀμαξάων, 7.426). See [[the-truce-for-the-dead]] and [[cattle]].

The mule is the beast of the Iliad's funerals. For Patroclus' pyre Agamemnon sends mules and men to cut wood on Ida, and the mules drag the timber down to the shore (23.110–124). For Hector's the Trojans yoke oxen and mules to their wagons and bring wood for nine days (24.782–784). Priam drives to Achilles in a mule wagon to ransom his son's body (24.265–280).
