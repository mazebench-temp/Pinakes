---
summary: “Agamemnon, lord of men,” is the first of the nine to rise against Hector, and he sacrifices the ox for the feast that honors Ajax.
---

Nestor's rebuke brings the volunteers to their feet: “First by far rose Agamemnon, lord of men” (7.162). The king who held his brother back from the duel (7.107–119) offers himself first, and the army's prayer at the lots names him among the three it hopes for, as “the king himself of Mycenae rich in gold” (7.180; [[rich-in-gold]]). The lot falls to Ajax.

After the duel the title returns at the sacrifice: “Agamemnon, lord of men, sacrificed an ox for them” (7.314; [[five-years-old]]), in nearly the verse of his sacrifice before the first battle (2.402). The commander honors the champion, and gives him the chine (7.321–322; [[geras]], [[wide-ruling]]). See [[agamemnon]].
