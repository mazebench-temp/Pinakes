**To both armies.** Hector's challenge opens with the verse that opened his announcement of the duel in Book 3: “Hear me, Trojans and well-greaved Achaeans” (7.67 = 3.86). κέκλυτε, “hear me” said to many, is the plural of the prayer's κλῦθι. The next verse is new to the poem: “so that I may say what the heart in my breast commands me” (7.68). The two verses become a pair. Zeus uses it to address the gods at the start of Book 8 (8.5–6), and Agamemnon puts it, with one word changed, in Zeus' mouth in the story of Heracles' birth (19.101–102).

**In the Trojan assembly.** Antenor and Priam open their speeches with the same pair, but with the Trojan address, “Hear me, Trojans and Dardanians and allies” (7.348–349, 7.368–369). Agamemnon used that address to the Trojans at the end of the duel of Book 3 (3.456). Here the Trojans use it in their own assembly, and Hector will use it again in Book 8 (8.497; see [[assembly]] and [[dardanians]]). Priam, the second to use it, is introduced as a speaker “with good intent” (7.367; see [[with-good-intent]]).

**Not in prayer.** The army's two prayers to Zeus begin with his name, “Father Zeus” (7.179, 7.202), not with κλῦθί μευ (see [[prayer-type-scene]]).
