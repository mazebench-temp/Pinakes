Αἴας δʼ ἐγγύθεν ἦλθε φέρων σάκος ἠΰτε πύργον: “And Ajax came near, carrying his shield like a tower”. ἠΰτε, “as, like”, makes a short comparison with no scene, as ὡς ὅτε does in the fall of Echepolus (4.462). But the likeness is turned the other way. In Book 4 a man went down as a tower falls. Here the tower stands, and it is carried. σάκος is the great shield, set against the round ἀσπίς that Hector carries, “balanced on every side” (7.250; see [[shield]]). πύργος is a tower on a city's wall, such as the great tower of Ilios from which the old men watched (3.153–154; see [[the-tower-of-ilios]]). That evening Nestor proposes that the Achaeans build “high towers” of their own before their ships, and two days later they build them (πύργους ὑψηλούς, 7.338, 7.437; see [[the-achaean-wall]]).

The points of likeness are size and cover. The next lines give the shield's making: “bronze, of seven oxhides”, the work of Tychius of Hyle, “by far the best of leather-workers”, with an eighth layer of bronze hammered over the hides (7.220–223; see [[the-shield-of-ajax]], [[tychius]] and [[hyle]]). The fight proves the comparison. Hector's spear cuts through six layers and stops in the seventh hide (7.245–248), and his great stone rings on the boss (7.266–267). Ajax's spear goes through Hector's shield twice (7.251, 7.260–261). Tall shields covering the body are known from Mycenaean art, and Ajax's has often been compared with them; how much of such old equipment the epic remembers is disputed.

The shield is the man. Ajax is ἕρκος Ἀχαιῶν, “the bulwark of the Achaeans” (3.229, 6.5, 7.211; see [[bulwark-of-the-achaeans]]), and in the underworld Odysseus tells his shade that the Achaeans lost “such a tower” in him (*Odyssey* 11.556). Behind the shield his brother Teucer shoots his arrows and ducks back, “like a child under its mother” (8.266–272; see [[teucer]] and [[like-a-child-under-its-mother]]).

The line is a formula. It returns whole when Ajax comes to rescue the wounded Odysseus and the Trojans scatter (11.485–486), and when he comes to stand over the body of Patroclus and Hector draws back into the throng (17.128–129). In Book 7 the tower brings Ajax to Hector alone. Later it brings him to protect a comrade, and Hector gives way before it again. See [[ajax]] and [[the-duel-of-hector-and-ajax]].
