---
summary: The long-shadowed spear of the formal duel returns in Book 7: Ajax brandishes it as he advances, and both champions throw it in the verses of the duel of Book 3.
---

Ajax goes out “brandishing his long-shadowed spear” (7.213; [[huge]]). In the duel the first throws are told in the verses of Book 3. Hector throws first, as Paris did: “He spoke, and poising his long-shadowed spear, he let it fly” (7.244), the verse of Menelaus' throw (3.355). Ajax answers: “Then second in his turn Ajax, sprung from Zeus, let fly his long-shadowed spear” (7.248–249).

The borrowed verses bring the whole of Menelaus' throw with them: Ajax's spear goes through Hector's shield and corselet and cuts his tunic, and Hector swerves aside and escapes death (7.250–254 = 3.356–360; [[balanced-on-every-side]], [[heavy-spear]], [[intricately-worked]], [[black-doom]]). The second duel of the poem repeats the first where the exchange of spears is concerned, and goes further. The two men draw out their spears and fight at close quarters, then with stones (7.255–272; [[long-spears]]). See [[single-combat]] and [[the-duel-of-hector-and-ajax]].
