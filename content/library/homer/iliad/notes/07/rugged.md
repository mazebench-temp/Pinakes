The epithet of rough country is here the roughness of a stone that lies on the plain, the kind of stone that Homer's fighters pick up when their spears have failed (compare τρηχὺς λίθος, 5.308; [[stone-throwing]]). The verse is used once more, with the one before it nearly unchanged, when Athena picks up a boundary stone “black and jagged and great” to strike Ares in the battle of the gods (21.403–404; [[strong-hand]]). Hector's stone rings on Ajax's shield without breaking it; Ajax answers with a greater one, “like a millstone” (7.268–272; [[like-a-millstone]]).
