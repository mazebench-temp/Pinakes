---
summary: Ajax tells Hector that Achilles lies among his “curved, seafaring ships,” nursing his wrath against Agamemnon.
---

{{quote:e5e698a5-f8cb-58d8-be86-2e911e62618d}}

In Book 1 Achilles threatened to go home to Phthia “with my curved ships” (1.169–170). He did not go, and Ajax now places him beside the same ships, idle. The verb κεῖτʼ, “lies,” and the participle ἀπομηνίσας, “nursing his wrath,” from the family of μῆνις (“wrath”), keep the quarrel of Book 1 in view on the day of the duel ([[menis]], [[achilles-withdraws]]). Ajax's point is that the Achaeans have champions enough without him. See [[achilles]], [[seafaring-ship]] and [[breaker-of-men]].
