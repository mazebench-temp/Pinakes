He stands up after Agamemnon, Diomedes and the two Ajaxes, and brings his companion with him: “Idomeneus and Idomeneus' squire / Meriones” (7.165–166; see [[meriones]]). ὀπάων, “squire, attendant”, is Meriones' title beside him here and in later books (8.263, 10.58, 17.258). The two verses return whole among the leaders who follow Diomedes out against the Trojans in Book 8 (8.263–264), and with a new beginning in Book 17 (17.258–259). The lot falls to Ajax, and Idomeneus has no other part in the book. His great day is the battle at the ships in Book 13.
