---
summary: The adjective of shining things describes Hector's shield, pierced by Ajax's spear, and the belt “bright with purple” that Ajax gives Hector when the duel is over.
---

The first use is in the verse of the spear that goes “through the shining shield” (7.251), taken from Menelaus' throw at Paris (3.357; [[heavy-spear]]).

The second comes in the exchange of gifts:

{{quote:55017447-7604-5bda-a664-2dcf193d8463}}

The verse ζωστῆρα δίδου φοίνικι φαεινόν, “gave a belt bright with purple,” differs only in the giver's name from the one that described the gift of Oeneus to Bellerophon, when the two became guest-friends (6.219; [[glaucus-and-diomedes]], [[guest-gifts]]). In Book 6 two champions met between the armies and parted as guest-friends, exchanging armor; in Book 7 two champions fight and then part with gifts, given “so that this is what a man will say, of Achaeans and Trojans alike” (7.300; [[belt]], [[purple]], [[silver-studded]]).
