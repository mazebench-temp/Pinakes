---
summary: Telamonian Ajax faces Hector behind his great shield and answers the herald; Hector addresses him as “Telamonian Ajax, sprung from Zeus.”
grc: [Τελαμώνιε]
---

The epithet marks Ajax at the three moments when he takes his stand. When Tychius' shield has been described, “Holding this before his chest, Telamonian Ajax stood very near Hector, and spoke to him threateningly” (7.224–225; [[seven-oxhide]]). Hector answers him with the vocative and two further titles: “Telamonian Ajax, sprung from Zeus, lord of the people” (7.234; [[sprung-from-zeus]]). And when Idaeus tells the two men to stop, “Answering him, Telamonian Ajax said” (7.283; [[answering-him]]) that Hector, who made the challenge, must be the one to stop it.

The epithet keeps the greater Ajax apart from the lesser, and Book 7 has both: the two Ajaxes volunteer together (7.164; [[clothed-in-courage]]), and the lot falls to the son of Telamon. See [[ajax]] and [[telamon]].
