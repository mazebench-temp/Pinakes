---
summary: In Nestor's story the Arcadian champion Ereuthalion, whom the young Nestor killed, stood forth as “a man equal to the gods.”
---

Nestor, telling how the Pylians and the Arcadians fought on the Celadon, comes to the enemy's champion: “Among them Ereuthalion stood forth as champion, a man equal to the gods, wearing on his shoulders the armor of lord Areithous” (7.136–137). The formula of praise is given to the enemy, and it serves the teller: the greater the champion, the greater the young man who alone dared to fight him. Nestor calls him the tallest and strongest man he ever killed (7.155–156). The story is told to shame the Achaeans, who hold back before Hector's challenge as the Pylians once held back before Ereuthalion's (7.151, 7.159–160; [[ereuthalion]], [[nestor-and-ereuthalion]], [[much-enduring]]).
