---
summary: The heralds who stop the duel of Hector and Ajax are “messengers of Zeus and of men.”
---

{{quote:65a6e814-e8fa-5be3-8d71-4fbf16f27c52}}

The heralds come in the verse of a turn that almost happened: the two men “would have been stabbing each other hand to hand with swords, had not the heralds … come” ([[would-have-had-not]]). The formula gives them the authority to stop the fight. They come as the messengers of Zeus, and Idaeus speaks for Zeus when he says that the god loves both men (7.280; [[cloud-gatherer]]), and as the messengers of men, one from each army. Their scepters, held out between the fighters, are the sign of their office (7.277; [[scepter]]). In Book 1 Achilles honored the same title when Agamemnon's heralds came to take Briseis (1.334). See [[heralds]], [[talthybius]] and [[idaeus]].
