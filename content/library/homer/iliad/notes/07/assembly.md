The Achaeans hear Idaeus in assembly by Agamemnon's ship and answer with a shout for Diomedes' refusal, and the Trojans sit in assembly until he returns (7.381–417).

**The Trojan assembly.** “And the Trojans held an assembly on the height of the city of Ilios, a dread and turbulent one, beside Priam's doors” (7.345–346). Priam's doors were the Trojans' meeting place in Book 2 as well (2.788; see [[priams-palace]]), and τετρηχυῖα, “turbulent”, is the word for the Achaean assembly in uproar there (τετρήχει, 2.95). Three men speak, each rising as the last sits down, “He spoke thus and sat down, and among them stood up” (7.354, 7.365; see [[sat-down-stood-up]]), and two of them open with the Trojan form of address, “Hear me, Trojans and Dardanians and allies” (7.348, 7.368; see [[hear-me]]). Antenor proposes to give back Helen and her possessions, since the Trojans are fighting with the oaths broken (7.350–353; see [[antenor]]). Paris refuses to give up the woman, but offers the possessions and more of his own (7.357–364; see [[paris]] and [[possessions]]). Priam, “a counselor equal to the gods”, sends Paris' offer, not Antenor's, to the Achaeans, with a request for a truce to burn the dead, and orders supper and a watch (7.366–378; see [[priam]]). “So he spoke, and they listened closely to him and obeyed” (7.379).

**The Achaean assembly.** At dawn Idaeus finds the Danaans “in assembly beside the stern of Agamemnon's ship” and delivers the message standing in their midst (7.381–397; see [[heralds]] and [[message-repeated]]). The army answers first. “So he spoke, and they all fell hushed in silence” (7.398), the verse that followed Hector's challenge (7.92; see [[silence]]). Then Diomedes: “Let no one now accept the possessions of Alexander, nor Helen” (7.400–401), “and all the sons of the Achaeans shouted aloud, admiring the word of Diomedes, tamer of horses” (7.403–404; see [[diomedes]]). Agamemnon lets the shout stand for the answer: “Idaeus, you yourself hear the word of the Achaeans, how they answer you; and my pleasure is the same” (7.406–407). The truce he decides himself, and he swears to it with his scepter raised to all the gods (7.408–412; see [[truce]] and [[scepter]]).

**Waiting.** The Trojans meanwhile “sat in assembly, all gathered together, waiting for the moment when Idaeus would come” (7.414–416). He gives them the answer standing in their midst, and they set out at once for the dead and for wood (7.416–418).

**The council.** The plan for the truce and the wall was not made in an assembly. Nestor proposed it to the kings at the feast in Agamemnon's huts, “and all the kings approved” (7.324–344; see [[feast]] and [[with-good-intent]]). The Trojan king decides before his people; the Achaean plan is made by the kings at their meal.

“So they spoke such words to one another” (ἀγόρευον, 7.464) closes the conversation of Poseidon and Zeus. It is not an assembly and is not counted here (see [[so-he-spoke]]).
