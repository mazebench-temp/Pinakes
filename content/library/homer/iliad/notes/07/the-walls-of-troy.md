---
summary: Poseidon fears that the fame of the new Achaean wall will make men forget the wall of Troy, which he and Apollo built for Laomedon.
refs: [7.452-453]
---

In his challenge Hector names the city by its towers: the war will go on until the Achaeans take “Troy of the fine towers” (Τροίην εὔπυργον, 7.71; see [[of-the-fine-towers]]) or are beaten beside their ships. At the end of the book the wall of Troy appears as a work of the gods. Watching the Achaeans build their own wall, Poseidon complains that its fame will spread as far as the dawn, “and men will forget the wall that I and Phoebus Apollo / toiled to build for the hero Laomedon” (7.451–453; see [[poseidon]] and [[laomedon]]). πολίσσαμεν, “we built”, found only here, is from πολίζω, the verb for founding a city: in the days of Dardanus, Aeneas says, Ilios “had not yet been built in the plain” (πεπόλιστο, 20.216–217). The god speaks of the wall as the making of the city.

The two walls, the one built by gods and the one built by men, face each other across the plain for the rest of the war. Achilles will measure the new one against the old: while he fought, Hector would not come out beyond the Scaean gates, and now Agamemnon needs a wall (9.348–355; see [[the-achaean-wall]]). Zeus does not dispute Poseidon's claim to the wall of Troy. He tells him that his fame is safe, and gives him the Achaean wall to destroy (7.455–463).
