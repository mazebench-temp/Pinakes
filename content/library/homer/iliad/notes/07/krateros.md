---
summary: κρατερός describes the “fierce battle” in which Glaucus kills Iphinous and the Trojans kill the Argives, “the strong might of Hector” in Apollo's plan, and “mighty Diomedes” among the nine volunteers.
---

The book's opening killings take place “in the fierce battle”: Glaucus strikes Iphinous κατὰ κρατερὴν ὑσμίνην (7.14; [[iphinous]], [[glaucus]]), and Athena sees Hector and Paris “killing the Argives in the fierce battle” (7.18). Apollo proposes to stop that battle by rousing “the strong might of Hector, tamer of horses” (7.38; [[menos]], [[tamer-of-horses]]).

When the volunteers rise to answer Hector, the second is “the son of Tydeus, mighty Diomedes” (7.163; [[son-of-tydeus]]), with the line-end formula that Book 4 introduced (4.401, 4.411). The mighty man of Books 5 and 6 does not draw the lot, and he returns to speak at the end of the book, when he refuses the Trojan offer (7.399–402; [[good-at-the-war-cry]], [[diomedes]]).
