---
summary: The duel of Hector and Ajax is fought shield against shield. Ajax's great shield of seven oxhides and bronze stops Hector's spear in its seventh hide and turns his stone, while Hector's shield, “balanced on every side”, is pierced twice and broken inward (7.244–272). Hector calls his own shield “the dry oxhide” (7.238).

**Two shields.** Ajax comes on “carrying his shield like a tower, bronze, of seven oxhides” (7.219–220), the work of Tychius of Hyle, who laid an eighth layer of bronze over seven hides of well-fed bulls (7.220–223; see [[the-shield-of-ajax]], [[seven-oxhide]] and [[tychius]]). Through the whole duel the narrator calls Ajax's shield σάκος and Hector's ἀσπίς (σάκος 7.245, 7.258, 7.266; ἀσπίς 7.250, 7.251, 7.260, 7.270, 7.272). In Book 3 Paris took up a σάκος and was struck on an ἀσπίς (3.335, 3.356). Here the two words never cross, and σάκος, the usual word for the greatest shields, stays with Ajax's. Hector's is “balanced on every side” (πάντοσʼ ἐΐσην, 7.250), the phrase of Paris' shield in the same verse of Book 3 (3.356; see [[balanced-on-every-side]]); in Book 6 the rim of his shield knocked against his neck and his ankles (6.117–118).

**The dry oxhide.** Before the fight Hector boasts of his training: “I know how to swing to the right, I know how to swing to the left, the dry oxhide, and that, to me, is fighting as a warrior with the oxhide shield” (7.238–239). βῶν ἀζαλέην, “the dry oxhide”, is the shield named by what it was made of, and ταλαύρινος is the warrior who carries such a shield (see [[with-the-oxhide-shield]]). To swing it to either side is to meet a spear wherever it comes.

**Layer by layer.** Hector's spear strikes Ajax's shield “on the outermost bronze, which was the eighth layer upon it. Through six layers went the unyielding bronze, cutting its way, but in the seventh hide it stopped” (7.246–248). πτύχες are the layers, and ῥινός the hide. The narrator counts the layers as the point passes them, six cut through and the seventh holding; whether the bronze is counted among the six is not said. Achilles' shield is counted the same way in Book 20, where Aeneas' spear goes through two of its five layers and is held in the gold (20.267–272). Ajax's spear, thrown and then thrust, goes through Hector's shield both times (7.251, 7.260–261); Hector's thrust bends back on Ajax's (7.258–259).

**The stones.** Hector's stone strikes Ajax's shield “in the middle, on the boss; and the bronze rang all around” (ἐπομφάλιον, 7.267); ὀμφαλός, “navel”, is the raised center of a shield (see [[bossed]]). Ajax's stone, like a millstone, breaks Hector's shield inward and lays him on his back “pressed against his shield” (7.270–272; see [[stone-throwing]]). The shields decide the duel as much as the men: Ajax is never wounded, and Hector is cut on the neck and knocked down (7.262, 7.271).

When the armies sit to hear Hector's challenge, their ranks are “bristling with shields and helmets and spears” (7.62; see [[helmet]] and [[spear]]).
