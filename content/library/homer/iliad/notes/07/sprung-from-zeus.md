---
summary: Ajax is “sprung from Zeus” twice in the duel: in Hector's address to him and in the verse of his spear throw.
---

Hector begins his answer to Ajax's threat with a string of titles: “Telamonian Ajax, sprung from Zeus, lord of the people” (7.234; [[telamonian]], [[koiranos]]). The honorific opens a speech that tells Ajax not to test him like a feeble boy or a woman (7.235–236); Hector is courteous in the address and proud in what follows. The narrator gives Ajax the same title at the moment of his reply to Hector's spear: “Then second in his turn Ajax, sprung from Zeus, let fly his long-shadowed spear” (7.248–249; [[long-shadowed]]). The throw goes through Hector's shield and corselet (7.250–254). See [[ajax]].
