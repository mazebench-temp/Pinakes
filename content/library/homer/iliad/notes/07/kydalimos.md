---
summary: The Trojan herald gives Menelaus his standing epithet when he reports that Paris will not give back “the wedded wife of glorious Menelaus.”
---

{{quote:98e7a647-0403-54a2-95bc-46f3e90eeb6c}}

The line-end Μενελάου κυδαλίμοιο is the standing formula for Menelaus, used even by Athena when she urged Pandarus to shoot him (4.100), and here the enemy's herald uses it as he delivers a refusal. Idaeus speaks with courtesy, and he adds two comments of his own: that Paris should have perished before he brought the possessions to Troy, and that the Trojans themselves urge him to give Helen back (7.390, 7.393; [[loud-voiced]]). Helen is named by her marriage, κουριδίη ἄλοχος, the wife a man married, not a woman taken in war ([[marriage]]). See [[menelaus]], [[helen]] and [[idaeus]].
