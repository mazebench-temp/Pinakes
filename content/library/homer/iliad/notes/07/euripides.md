summary: Euripides' Hypsipyle gives a play to the mother of Homer's Euneus, a queen of Lemnos turned slave at Nemea, whom her two sons by Jason find at last; his Suppliants turns on the taking up of the dead, the act that names the second half of Book 7.
---

- *Hypsipyle*: [[euripides-hypsipyle]].
- **The recovery of the dead.** In the *Suppliants* the mothers of the Seven who fell at Thebes beg Athens to win back their sons' bodies, which Thebes will not give up. The later Greek term for the act, νεκρῶν ἀναίρεσις, “the taking up of the dead”, is the traditional title of the truce in Book 7: [[title-of-book-7]] and [[the-truce-for-the-dead]].
