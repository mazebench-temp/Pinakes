  - 7.421-432 | The burning of the dead
---

The funeral of the war dead is told without a lament. The bodies are washed and lifted onto the wagons with hot tears (7.425–426; see [[tears]]), “but great Priam did not allow them to wail; and in silence they heaped the dead on the pyre, grieving at heart” (7.427–428). κλαίω is to weep aloud, the cry that a lament gives voice to. The poem does not say why Priam forbids it. The grief stays in the heart (ἀχνύμενοι κῆρ), and the Achaeans heap their own dead in the same verse, with no word of wailing either (7.430–431; see [[silence]] and [[cremation]]).

The great funerals of the poem are loud. Achilles leads the lament over Patroclus, Briseis mourns him and the women answer, and at Hector's funeral singers lead the dirge and Andromache, Hecuba and Helen speak in turn (18.314–317, 19.282–302, 24.719–776). Those laments are spoken by the people closest to one dead man. The dead of the truce are many and nameless, the living could hardly tell them apart (7.424), and no one speaks for them.
