summary: In the simile that opens Book 7, sailors are weary “with polished oars of fir” when a god gives them a fair wind.
grc: [ἐϋξέστῃς]
---

{{quote:99780865-2820-5014-968f-e53d664b39f4}}

ἐΰξεστος (“well-planed, well-polished”) is a compound of ξεστός, the dressed stone of Priam's palace (6.243–244), and it has the same sense as ἐΰξοος, the entry's other word: wood worked smooth. The oars are of fir (ἐλάτη). The finish of the oars stands beside the weariness of the rowers, whose limbs are loosened with toil. Hector and Paris come to the Trojans as the wind comes to such men (7.1–7; [[the-fair-wind]], [[hector]], [[paris]]). See [[ships]].
