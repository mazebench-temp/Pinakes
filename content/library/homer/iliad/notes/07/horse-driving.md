summary: Nestor imagines “the old horse-driving Peleus” groaning to hear that the Achaeans cower before Hector.

{{quote:b9d3ffb7-ddf4-5eea-b575-5273c1a8392e}}

The old horseman's title belongs to Peleus again in Book 9 (9.438), where Phoenix recalls how Peleus sent him to Troy with Achilles. Here Nestor remembers Peleus as a host who questioned him in his house about the lineage of all the Argives, and he imagines the old man praying for death if he heard of their shame ([[peleus]], [[fathers-and-sons]]). The father of the absent Achilles is invoked to shame the men who will not fight Hector. His epithets are of rank and counsel: he is also “the brave counselor and speaker of the Myrmidons” (7.126; [[man-of-counsel]]). See [[nestor]].
