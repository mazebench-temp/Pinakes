---
summary: The book's one φιλότης is the one Hector proposes at the end of the duel, that men may say of him and Ajax that they fought in heart-devouring strife “and then parted in friendship, reconciled”. The friendship of the two armies, sworn in Book 3 and broken in Book 4, becomes the friendship of two men for one evening.
passages:
  - 7.299-305 | Hector and Ajax exchange gifts
---

In Book 3 the duel was to end the war, with the two armies “making friendship and swearing a trusty truce” (φιλότητα καὶ ὅρκια πιστὰ ταμόντες, 3.73, 3.256; so 3.94), and in Book 4 the men wondered whether Zeus would set friendship between the two sides, just before Pandarus' arrow broke the truce (4.82–84; see the note on Book 4 and [[oaths-and-their-breaking]]). Book 7 never speaks of friendship between the armies. They agree to a truce to burn their dead, and the Trojans admit that they have played false with the oaths (7.351–352, 7.408–411; see [[the-truce-for-the-dead]]). The word comes only once, at the end of the duel, in the words Hector wants men to say:

{{quote:7e48128d-5964-5456-a8ff-16c710799c78}}

The friendship is that of two men who have just tried to kill each other. ἀρθμήσαντε, “having joined in friendship, reconciled”, is found only here in Homer, and the gifts that follow, Hector's sword and Ajax's belt, are its tokens, as the victims and the wine were the tokens of the oaths in Book 3 (7.303–305; see [[parted-in-friendship]] and [[guest-gifts]]). The heralds have prepared it. Idaeus calls the two “dear sons” (παῖδε φίλω) and tells them that Zeus “loves you both” (φιλεῖ, 7.279–280; see [[philos]]), as the army had prayed that Zeus might love Hector too (φιλέεις, 7.204). The love of Zeus for both men allows them to part as friends.

The friendship lasts one evening. Hector and Ajax meet again in the battles at the ships (14.402–420, 15.415–418, 16.114–122; see [[ajax]] and [[hector]]).
