---
summary: The army prays twice to Zeus “looking up at the wide sky,” once while the lots are shaken and once while Ajax arms.
---

The verse ὧδε δέ τις εἴπεσκεν ἰδὼν εἰς οὐρανὸν εὐρύν introduces the two prayers of the army before the duel (7.178, 7.201): “this is what a man would say, looking up at the wide sky.” In Book 3 the people prayed in the same way at the oath and as the lots were shaken for the first duel, and there the speaker was a man “of Achaeans and Trojans alike” (3.297, 3.319; [[tis-speech]]); the verse before the prayer at the lots is the same in both books (3.318 = 7.177). Here only the Achaeans pray, and they look to the sky where Zeus has his share of the world (15.192). The first prayer asks for the lot to fall to Ajax, Diomedes or Agamemnon; the second, for victory for Ajax, or equal glory for both (7.179–180, 7.202–205; [[father-zeus]], [[who-rules-from-ida]], [[lots]]).
