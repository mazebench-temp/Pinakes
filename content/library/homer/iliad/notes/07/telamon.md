Τελαμώνιος Αἴας, “Telamonian Ajax”, names the son as he takes his stand before Hector behind his shield and as he answers the herald (7.224, 7.283; see [[telamonian]] and [[ajax]]). Hector addresses him with a whole verse of titles, Αἶαν διογενὲς Τελαμώνιε κοίρανε λαῶν, “Telamonian Ajax, sprung from Zeus, lord of the people” (7.234; see [[sprung-from-zeus]] and [[koiranos]]). Achilles and Menelaus address Ajax with the same verse (9.644, 11.465), and Agamemnon gives its second half to Teucer, Telamon's other son (8.281; see [[teucer]]).

τελαμών is also a common noun, the strap from which a shield or a sword hung over the shoulder (see [[shield]]). Hector's gift to Ajax is a sword “with its sheath and its well-cut strap” (ἐϋτμήτῳ τελαμῶνι, 7.304; see [[sword]]). The noun is usually derived from the root of τλῆναι, “bear, endure”: the strap bears the weight. The father of the man with the tower shield bears the name of a shield strap.
