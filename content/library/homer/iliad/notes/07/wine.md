summary: Ships from Lemnos bring wine to the Achaean camp, sent by Euneus, son of Jason, who gives a thousand measures to the sons of Atreus and sells the rest for bronze, iron, hides, cattle and slaves (7.467–475). Both armies drink through the night, and pour the wine on the ground for Zeus before they dare to drink (7.476–481).

**From Lemnos.** “And ships from Lemnos stood by, bringing wine, many of them, which Euneus, son of Jason, had sent” (7.467–468; see [[lemnos]], [[euneus]] and [[jason]]). The army before Troy drinks wine brought over the sea: in Book 9 Nestor tells Agamemnon that his huts are full of wine that the Achaean ships bring every day from Thrace (9.71–72). The Lemnian wine comes in two ways. To the sons of Atreus, “apart”, Euneus has given a thousand measures (χίλια μέτρα, 7.470–471). What a measure held is not said; the silver mixing bowl that Euneus paid as the price of a son of Priam held six (23.741–747). The rest the army buys: “From these the long-haired Achaeans bought their wine” (7.472), with bronze, iron, hides, cattle and slaves (7.473–475; see [[trade]]). οἰνίζομαι is literally to get wine for oneself; the price is in the datives that follow. μέθυ (7.471) is a poetic word for wine, related to English *mead* and to Sanskrit *madhu*, “honey”.

**All night.** The wine is drunk at a night-long feast on both sides of the plain (7.475–477; see [[feast]]). Because Zeus thunders through the night, the first of it goes on the ground: “they poured the wine from their cups on the ground, and no one dared drink before he had poured a libation to the son of Cronus, supreme in might” (7.480–481; see [[libation]] and [[teras]]).
