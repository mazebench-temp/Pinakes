---
summary: Athena darts down from the peaks of Olympus to help the Argives, and when Apollo asks why she has come from Olympus, she says that she came to stop the fighting, as he wishes.
---

Olympus is named three times in Book 7, all in the scene where Athena comes down and meets Apollo. Athena “went darting down from the peaks of Olympus” (7.19), in the verse that brought her down in Books 2 and 4 (2.167, 4.74; see [[athena]]). Apollo asks her why she has come “from Olympus” (7.25), and she answers that she too came “from Olympus” with the same purpose, to stop the fighting between the Trojans and the Achaeans (7.35; see [[apollo]]). The mountain is the gods' starting point; the scene itself is set lower down, by the oak before the city (see [[the-oak-of-zeus]]).

At the end of the book the gods are again together, “seated beside Zeus” to watch the Achaeans build their wall (7.443). The poem does not say where they sit, and Olympus is not named. The next book begins there, with Zeus calling the gods to assembly “on the topmost peak of many-ridged Olympus” (8.2–3; see [[many-ridged-olympus]]).
