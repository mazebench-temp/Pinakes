The whole verse Μηριόνης ἀτάλαντος Ἐνυαλίῳ ἀνδρειφόντῃ (“Meriones, the equal of Enyalius, slayer of men”) is used four times in the Iliad, always of Meriones (2.651, 7.166, 8.264, 17.259; [[enyalius]]). Here and in Book 8 it follows the same verse about Idomeneus and his squire (7.165 = 8.263), in a list of the best of the Achaeans. The squire is praised with the war god's name, and he stands in the list next to his master ([[meriones]], [[idomeneus]]). See [[the-duel-of-hector-and-ajax]].
