---
summary: Cattle run through Book 7 as hides, victims, draft animals and payment. Ajax's shield is made of seven oxhides, Agamemnon sacrifices a five-year-old ox to Zeus after the duel, oxen and mules bring in the dead, and the Achaeans slaughter oxen for their supper and buy Lemnian wine with hides and with the cattle themselves.
grc: [βῶν, βουφόνεον, βόεσσιν]
---

**Hides.** Ajax's shield is ἑπταβόειος, “of seven oxhides” (7.220, 7.222, 7.245, 7.266), made from the hides “of well-fed bulls” (ταύρων ζατρεφέων, 7.223; see [[the-shield-of-ajax]] and [[bulls]]). Hector's spear stops in the seventh hide (ῥινός, 7.248). Hector calls his own shield βῶν ἀζαλέην, “the dry oxhide” (7.238–239): βῶν is an old accusative of βοῦς, found only here in Homer, and the word that follows, ταλαύρινος, is built on ῥινός, “hide” (see [[with-the-oxhide-shield]], [[shield]] and the translation's note on 7.238–239). In this speech the shield is simply called an ox.

**The victim.** When the Achaeans bring Ajax back from the duel, Agamemnon “sacrificed an ox for them, a male, five years old, to the son of Cronus, supreme in might” (7.314–315). The lines are those of his sacrifice on the morning of the first battle, where the ox was “a fat one” (2.402–403). Ajax is honored with the long chine (7.321; see [[the-duel-of-hector-and-ajax]] and [[sacrifice]]). Poseidon complains that the Achaeans built their wall without giving the gods glorious hecatombs (7.450; see [[hecatomb]] and [[the-building-of-the-wall]]). When the wall is finished, “they slaughtered oxen among the huts and took their supper” (7.466). βουφονέω, “kill oxen”, occurs only here in Homer.

**Draft animals.** Nestor proposes that the Achaeans bring in their dead “with oxen and mules” (βουσὶ καὶ ἡμιόνοισιν, 7.333), and the bodies are lifted onto wagons (7.426; see [[mules]] and [[the-truce-for-the-dead]]). For Hector's funeral the Trojans yoke oxen and mules to their wagons to bring in wood (24.782–784).

**Payment.** The Achaeans buy Euneus' wine “some with bronze, and some with gleaming iron, some with hides, and some with the cattle themselves, and some with slaves” (7.473–475; see [[the-lemnian-women]]). Elsewhere cattle measure value: Glaucus' gold armor was worth a hundred oxen, and Diomedes' bronze nine (6.236). Here the beasts themselves change hands.

**Ox-eyed.** The mother of Menesthius, killed by Paris in the book's first lines, is “ox-eyed Phylomedusa” (βοῶπις, 7.10; see [[phylomedusa]] and [[ox-eyed]]).
