---
summary: Hector proposes that he and Ajax exchange “glorious gifts,” and Poseidon complains that the Achaeans built their wall without giving the gods “glorious hecatombs.”
grc: [περικλυτὰ, κλειτὰς]
---

**The gifts.** “But come, let us both give each other glorious gifts, so that this is what a man will say, of Achaeans and Trojans alike” (7.299–300). περικλυτός, “renowned all around,” is the adjective of works and gifts that people talk of, and the gifts are meant to be talked of: the saying Hector imagines follows at once (7.301–302; [[tis-speech]], [[heart-devouring]]). He gives a silver-studded sword, and Ajax a belt (7.303–305; [[silver-studded]], [[phaeinos]], [[guest-gifts]]).

**The hecatombs.** Poseidon asks whether Zeus does not see that the Achaeans have built a wall and dug a ditch around it, “and have not given glorious hecatombs to the gods” (7.450; [[hecatomb]]). κλειτὰς ἑκατόμβας recurs in the same verse at the beginning of Book 12, where the wall was built against the will of the gods and did not stand for long (12.6–9; [[the-achaean-wall]]). The glory of the offering is what the gods expect in return for a great work, and its absence is the grievance ([[reciprocity-with-the-gods]]).
