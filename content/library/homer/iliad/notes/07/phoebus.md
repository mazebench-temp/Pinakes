---
summary: Poseidon names his partner in building Laomedon's wall, “Phoebus Apollo,” when he complains that the Achaean wall will make men forget it.
---

{{quote:d1ff4c64-f30c-58eb-b8b9-2a8e35dcc30e}}

Poseidon gives Apollo his full formula, Phoebus Apollo, as the narrator does. The dual ἀθλήσαντε (“we two toiled”) makes the two gods partners in the work. At 21.441–457 Poseidon tells the story differently: he built the wall, while Apollo herded Laomedon's cattle, and both were cheated of their wages (see the translation's note on 7.443–464; [[poseidon-and-apollo-serve-laomedon]], [[the-walls-of-troy]], [[laomedon]]). In Book 12 the two gods who built Troy's wall together destroy the Achaeans' wall together, Apollo turning the rivers against it and Poseidon leading them with his trident (12.17–33; [[the-achaean-wall]]).
