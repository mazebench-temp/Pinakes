---
summary: “Shining Hector” rushes out of the gates at the start of the book and names himself so in the epitaph he imagines; “shining Ajax” is the man whose lot is drawn.
---

The book begins where Book 6 ended, with the formula: “So saying, shining Hector rushed out through the gates” (7.1), with Paris beside him ([[paris]], [[scaean-gates]]).

In his challenge Hector imagines what a sailor will say of the tomb of the man he kills: “This is the mound of a man who died long ago, whom once, when he was the best in the fighting, shining Hector killed” (7.89–90; [[born-hereafter]], [[tomb]]). The epithet that the narrator gives him he gives himself, in the mouth of a man not yet born, and the words he imagines are the words that will keep his fame (7.91; [[kleos]]).

The same epithet goes to his opponent when the lot is drawn. The herald carries it through the crowd until he comes to the man “who had scratched his mark on it and thrown it into the helmet, shining Ajax” (7.187; [[lots]], [[ajax]]). The duel of the book is between the two men who have it. See [[hector]].
