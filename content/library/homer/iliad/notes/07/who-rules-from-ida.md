---
summary: The army prays to “Father Zeus, who rule from Ida” for victory for Ajax, or for equal glory to both champions, in the verse of the prayers at the duel of Book 3.
---

{{quote:0ce2ea4e-b83e-5c42-a26b-d3d31c0c40ed}}

The prayer repeats the address of Book 3, where Agamemnon swore the oaths for the first duel (3.276) and the people prayed as the lots were shaken (3.320). Both are prayers before single combats between the lines, made to the god whose mountain overlooks the plain. The prayer here has two parts: victory for Ajax, or, if Zeus loves Hector too, equal strength and glory for both (7.203–205). The duel ends in the second way ([[the-duel-of-hector-and-ajax]], [[single-combat]]). The next book takes Zeus to Ida itself, to his precinct on Gargarus, from which he watches the battle (8.47–52; [[ida]]). See [[father-zeus]] and [[most-glorious]].
