**Given.** Priam tells the Trojans that at dawn Idaeus is to go to the ships “to tell the sons of Atreus, Agamemnon and Menelaus, the word of Alexander, for whose sake the quarrel arose” (7.373–374), and a second word, a request for a pause in the war until the dead are burned (7.375–378; see [[truce]]).

**Delivered.** Idaeus keeps Priam's words and changes what delivery requires.

1. **Frame.** He opens with the address that Nestor used for his own proposal of the same truce the night before, “Son of Atreus, and you others, the best of all the Achaeans” (7.385 = 7.327), names his senders, “Priam and the other noble Trojans”, and adds a courtesy, “if perhaps this should be welcome and pleasing to you” (7.386–387).
2. **Paris' word.** Priam's verse comes back unchanged, “the word of Alexander, for whose sake the quarrel arose” (7.388 = 7.374). Priam did not say what the word was, and the herald supplies it from Paris' own speech, with the person changed: Alexander “is willing to give, all of them, and to add more from his own” (7.391 ~ 7.364), but “the wedded wife of glorious Menelaus he says he will not give” (7.392–393; see [[possessions]] and [[paris]]).
3. **His own words.** Two additions are the herald's. In the middle of the offer he curses the man who makes it, “I wish he had perished before that!” (7.390), and he lets the Achaeans know that the refusal is Paris' alone, “though the Trojans, truly, urge him” (7.393; see [[heralds]] and [[idaeus]]).
4. **The truce.** The second message is Priam's verses again, with “they also bade me say” for “let him also say” (7.394–397 ~ 7.375–378).

The Achaeans hear what Priam sent and what Idaeus thinks of it, and they answer the offer before Agamemnon can: Diomedes refuses, and the army shouts its approval (7.399–404; see [[assembly]]).
