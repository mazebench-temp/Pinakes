Ἄρνῃ ναιετάοντα, “who lived in Arne” (7.9): the town gives a dying man his home, one of the details the poem grants its victims along with their fathers (see [[menesthius]] and [[androktasia]]). It is the only time the Iliad names Arne outside the Catalogue, where it is the Boeotian town “rich in grapes” (2.507; see [[boeotians]]). Menesthius' father is κορυνήτης, “the mace-man”, whom Nestor places in the war of his youth between the Pylians and the Arcadians (7.137–146; see [[areithous]]). Nothing in the poem ties the father to Boeotia except his son's home.
