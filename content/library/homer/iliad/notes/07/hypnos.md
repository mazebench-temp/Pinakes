summary: The book ends with sleep after a night of feasting and fear: “Then they lay down to rest and took the gift of sleep.” The line closes Book 9 as well, after a libation, and two evening meals of the Odyssey.
grc: [κοιμήσαντʼ]
en: [lay down to rest]
passages:
  - 7.476-482 | Zeus thunders through the night
---

The last line of Book 7 follows the thunder of Zeus and the libations poured in fear (7.478–481; see [[libation]] and [[war-and-night]]): “Then they lay down to rest and took the gift of sleep” (κοιμήσαντʼ ἄρʼ ἔπειτα καὶ ὕπνου δῶρον ἕλοντο, 7.482). Sleep is a gift, δῶρον, taken as one takes a portion at a feast, and the verse closes a day as the feast-formulas close a meal (see [[doron]] and [[feast-type-scene]]). The whole line ends Book 9 in almost the same form, after the Achaean leaders have heard Achilles' refusal: the kings approve, “admiring the word of Diomedes, tamer of horses”, and pour libations (9.710–713). The first of those lines is the one that followed Diomedes' answer to the Trojans in this book (7.404 = 9.711), so that both books end with the army approving Diomedes, pouring to the gods and going to sleep. In the Odyssey the line ends the evening meal in Eumaeus' hut (*Odyssey* 16.479–481) and the feast at Autolycus' house (*Odyssey* 19.425–427).

The sleep of the armies is not Zeus' sleep. In Book 2 all the others slept while he lay awake pondering (2.1–4; see the note on Book 2); here he spends the night devising evil, thundering (7.478–479).
