**The lots.** The nine mark their lots and throw them “into the helmet of Agamemnon, son of Atreus” (ἐν δʼ ἔβαλον κυνέῃ Ἀγαμέμνονος Ἀτρεΐδαο, 7.176). Nestor shakes it, and the lot of Ajax leaps “out of the helmet” (ἐκ … κυνέης, 7.182), and the herald carries it round until it comes to the man “who had scratched his mark on it and thrown it into the helmet” (7.186–187; see [[lots]]). In Book 3 the lots for the first throw were shaken “in a bronze-fitted helmet” (3.316), and the archers' lots at the funeral games of Patroclus are shaken in the same words (23.861); in the Odyssey Odysseus and Eurylochus shake lots in a helmet to decide which of them will go to Circe's house (Od. 10.206–207). A helmet is the vessel that an army has to hand. Here it is the king's own.

**The rim.** Hector's first kill of the book goes in where the helmet ends. He strikes Eioneus “in the neck below the rim of his bronze helmet” (αὐχένʼ ὑπὸ στεφάνης εὐχάλκου, 7.12; see [[eioneus]], [[androktasia]] and [[well-bronzed]]). στεφάνη is the rim of a helmet, and in later books a helmet itself (10.30–31, 11.96). The neck is where a helmeted man is open: Ajax's spear grazes Hector's neck in the duel (7.262), and at the funeral games Diomedes keeps reaching for Ajax's neck over his great shield (23.820–821).

**The ranks.** When the armies sit down to hear Hector's challenge, “their ranks sat close-packed, bristling with shields and helmets and spears” (7.61–62). κορύθεσσι is the dative plural of κόρυς. πεφρικυῖαι, “bristling”, is a participle of φρίσσω, and the φρίξ, the “ripple” of the West Wind in the simile that follows, comes from the same root (7.62–66; see [[the-ripple-of-the-west-wind]], [[shield]] and [[spear]]).
