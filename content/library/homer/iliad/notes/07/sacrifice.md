summary: After the duel Agamemnon sacrifices a five-year-old ox to Zeus for the chiefs, and the meat is butchered, roasted and shared in Ajax's honor (7.313–322). When the wall is finished the army slaughters oxen for its supper (7.466), and Poseidon complains that the wall was built without hecatombs for the gods (7.450).

**For Ajax.** The Achaeans bring Ajax to Agamemnon's huts, and “Agamemnon, lord of men, sacrificed an ox for them, a male, five years old, to the son of Cronus, supreme in might” (7.314–315). It is the offering Agamemnon made to Zeus before the first battle, in nearly the same verses, where the ox was “a fat one” (2.402–403; see [[five-years-old]]). A five-year-old is a full-grown beast; the ox that Autolycus' sons kill for the young Odysseus is “a male, five years old” in the same words (Od. 19.420).

**Told as a meal.** The rite is told from the flaying on: the ox is flayed and jointed, the meat cut up, spitted, roasted and drawn off (7.316–318; see [[spits]]). The narrative says nothing of the barley, the prayer, or the thigh-bones burned in fat for the god, which filled the sacrifices of Book 1 and Book 2 (1.458–463, 2.410–426), and its verses are those used elsewhere for a meal given to a guest (see [[sacrifice-type-scene]]). What matters here is the meat, and the feast that honors Ajax with the chine (7.319–322; see [[feast]]).

**Oxen among the huts.** When the wall is finished at sunset, “they slaughtered oxen among the huts and took their supper” (7.466). βουφονέω, “kill oxen”, is found only here in Homer. The meat goes with the wine bought from the Lemnian ships, and the feast lasts all night (7.467–477; see [[trade]] and [[wine]]).

**Without hecatombs.** Poseidon's complaint against the wall begins with an offering not made: the Achaeans have built it “and have not given glorious hecatombs to the gods” (7.450; see [[hecatomb]]).
