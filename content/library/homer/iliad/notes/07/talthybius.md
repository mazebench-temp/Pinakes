He is named once in the book, beside his Trojan counterpart: “Talthybius and Idaeus, both of them wise” (7.276). The heralds come “one from the Trojans and one from the bronze-armored Achaeans” (7.275), and together they hold out their scepters between the two champions (7.277; see [[scepter]] and [[heralds]]). The narrator calls them “messengers of Zeus and of men” (7.274), the words with which Achilles greeted Talthybius and Eurybates when they came to his hut for Briseis (1.334; see [[taking-of-briseis]]). In Book 3 the heralds of both sides brought the offerings together for the oath (3.268–270); here they end a duel together. The Achaean herald says nothing, and Idaeus speaks for both (7.278–282; see [[idaeus]]).

The herald who carries Ajax's lot through the ranks until its owner knows his mark is not named (7.183–189; see [[lots]]).
