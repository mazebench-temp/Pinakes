---
summary: Achilles is named in Book 7 without his formula; the adjective ὠκύς belongs to horses, the swift team from which Iphinous falls and the “fray of swift horses” that Hector boasts of charging into.
en: [swift]
---

Achilles is absent from the fighting and is named twice, by Agamemnon, who says that even he shudders to meet Hector (7.113–114), and by Ajax, who calls him “breaker of men, lion-hearted” (7.228; [[breaker-of-men]], [[lion-hearted]]). His formula πόδας ὠκὺς Ἀχιλλεύς (“swift-footed Achilles”) does not occur.

The adjective describes horses twice, in the genitive ἵππων ὠκειάων that closes the verse. Glaucus strikes Iphinous in the shoulder “as he was leaping up behind his swift horses,” and he falls from the chariot (7.15–16; [[iphinous]], [[glaucus]]). And Hector tells Ajax: “I know how to charge into the fray of swift horses” (7.240), the chariot fighting that he sets beside the standing fight on foot in the next verse (7.241; [[destructive]], [[chariot]]).
