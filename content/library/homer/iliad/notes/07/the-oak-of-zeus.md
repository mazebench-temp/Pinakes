---
summary: Athena and Apollo meet beside the oak and agree to stop the battle with a single combat; then they sit in “the high oak of aegis-bearing father Zeus”, like vultures, to watch the armies.
passages:
  - 7.17-22 | Athena and Apollo meet at the oak
  - 7.58-61 | Athena and Apollo like vultures
---

**A meeting place.** Athena darts down from the peaks of Olympus to help the Argives, and Apollo comes out from Pergamus to meet her, “and the two of them met each other beside the oak” (παρὰ φηγῷ, 7.22; see [[athena]], [[apollo]], [[olympus]] and [[pergamus]]). The tree stands where the plain meets the city, by the Scaean gates (6.237, 9.354; see [[scaean-gates]]), between the gods' high places and the field. There they agree to end the day's fighting with a duel (7.24–43).

**A seat for watching.** When the armies have sat down, the gods take their seats in the tree:

{{quote:41c45a50-456a-56e4-a9c8-3fa86088b492}}

It is the tree of Zeus, and it is “high” (ὑψηλή), the only word the book gives it. In Book 5 it was “beautiful” (περικαλλής, 5.693). Whether the gods take the vultures' shape or only sit in the tree as vultures sit is disputed. Gods take the shape of birds elsewhere: Athena leaves Nestor's feast as a φήνη, a bird of prey, to the amazement of all who see her (Od. 3.371–372), and Sleep sits hidden in a fir on Ida “like a clear-voiced bird” (14.286–291). But ἐοικώς can describe manner as well as form, and the translation leaves the question open (see the translation's note on 7.59, [[like-vultures]] and [[vultures]]). The αἰγυπιός of Homer's similes is a bird of prey that watches from a height, and the two gods sit high above the armies “delighting in the men” (ἀνδράσι τερπόμενοι, 7.61), as the duel they have planned is about to begin below (see [[watching-the-war]]).

**Later.** In Book 5 the oak sheltered the wounded Sarpedon (5.692–698), and in Book 6 it marked the edge of the city as Hector came in from the battle (6.237). It will mark how far Hector would venture while Achilles fought (9.354), and where the fleeing Trojans stop to wait for one another (11.170). In the last battle Apollo stands against it, hidden in mist, to keep death from Agenor (21.549).
