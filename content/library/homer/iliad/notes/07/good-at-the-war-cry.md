---
summary: Diomedes, “good at the war cry,” breaks the Achaeans' silence to refuse the Trojan offer, and the whole army shouts its approval.
---

{{quote:aea09b7d-48bb-5279-a0f3-eeb8cdc40ef7}}

The scene is told as it was after Hector's challenge. “So he spoke, and they all fell hushed in silence” (7.92 = 7.398), and then “at last” (ὀψὲ δὲ δὴ) one man speaks: Menelaus then, and now “Diomedes, good at the war cry” (7.94, 7.399). His answer is brief: accept neither the possessions nor Helen, since even a fool can see that the bounds of destruction are made fast upon the Trojans (7.400–402). The man of the war cry is answered by the army's own shout, ἐπίαχον (7.403; [[sons-of-the-achaeans]], [[tamer-of-horses]]). See [[diomedes]] and [[silence]].
