---
summary: Hector's formula follows him through the duel with Ajax: in Nestor's taunt, in the two verses that introduce his speeches to Ajax, and when, wounded, he will not give up the fight.
---

Nestor uses it first, to shame the Achaeans: if he were young, “Then Hector of the gleaming helmet would soon find his fight” (7.158). The fuller formula μέγας κορυθαίολος Ἕκτωρ introduces Hector's two speeches to Ajax, his boast of his skill in war and his proposal to stop at nightfall and exchange gifts, in the same verse: “Then great Hector of the gleaming helmet spoke to him in turn” (7.233, 7.287; [[answering-him]]).

Between the two speeches the epithet comes at the turn of the duel. Ajax's spear has gone through Hector's shield and grazed his neck, and the black blood has welled up:

The epithet of the warrior in his armor stands in the verse of his refusal to stop ([[strong-hand]], [[dark-blood]]). A few lines later his shield is broken and he is stretched on his back, and Apollo sets him on his feet (7.270–272). See [[hector]] and [[the-duel-of-hector-and-ajax]].
