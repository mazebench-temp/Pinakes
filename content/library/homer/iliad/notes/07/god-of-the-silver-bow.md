---
summary: Apollo, “god of the silver bow,” sits down with Athena on the oak of Zeus to watch the duel he has planned, both of them like vultures.
---

{{quote:41c45a50-456a-56e4-a9c8-3fa86088b492}}

The archer god does not shoot in Book 7. He has planned the duel with Athena (7.37–42), and now he watches it from the oak, in the form or the manner of a bird of prey (see the translation's note on 7.59; [[like-vultures]], [[watching-the-war]]). His part in the fight comes at its lowest point for Hector: when Ajax's stone knocks Hector flat, “Apollo at once set him on his feet” (7.272). The god of the silver bow is also the god whose temple on Pergamus Hector promises to adorn with his victim's armor (7.82–83; [[hekatos]], [[temple]]). See [[apollo]].
