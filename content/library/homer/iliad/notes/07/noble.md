---
summary: The Trojan herald speaks for “Priam and the other noble Trojans” when he brings Paris' offer to the Achaean assembly.
---

“Priam and the other noble Trojans bade me tell you, if perhaps this should be welcome and pleasing to you, the word of Alexander” (7.386–388). Idaeus gives his own people the old adjective of praise as he speaks before their enemies, and he separates them from the man whose word he carries: Paris will not give back Helen, “though the Trojans, truly, urge him” (7.393; [[loud-voiced]], [[kydalimos]]). The noble Trojans are the assembly that heard Antenor's proposal and Paris' refusal the night before (7.345–379; [[trojans]], [[assembly]]). See [[idaeus]].
