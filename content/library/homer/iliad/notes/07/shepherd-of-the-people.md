---
summary: The pastoral title of kings goes to Agamemnon in Ajax's words about Achilles' wrath, and to Jason, the father of Euneus, the only title the Iliad gives him.
---

Ajax tells Hector that Achilles lies among his ships “nursing his wrath against Agamemnon, shepherd of the people” (7.230; [[curved-ships]]). The title of the king who tends his people stands beside the wrath that has cost them so much ([[menis]], [[agamemnon]]).

At the end of the book the title goes to a king of an older story:

{{quote:9e295d6b-7ab0-5acc-81d5-db760bb71c25}}

Only here does the Iliad give Jason a title of his own; elsewhere he is simply the father of Euneus, the king on Lemnos who sends the wine, in the patronymic Ἰησονίδης (7.468, 7.471, 23.747) or as “the son of Jason” (21.41). The poem gives him the royal title and no more; his voyage with the Argo is mentioned only in the Odyssey (Od. 12.69–72; [[jason]], [[euneus]], [[hypsipyle]], [[the-lemnian-women]]).
