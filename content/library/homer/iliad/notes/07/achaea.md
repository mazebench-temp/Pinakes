---
summary: When no Achaean will face Hector, Nestor laments “what great sorrow is coming upon the land of Achaea”, in the line he spoke in Book 1 when Achilles and Agamemnon quarreled (7.124 = 1.254).
---

Nestor's lament, ὢ πόποι ἦ μέγα πένθος Ἀχαιΐδα γαῖαν ἱκάνει, “Ah, what great sorrow is coming upon the land of Achaea!” (7.124; see [[o-popoi]]), is the line with which he began his speech to Achilles and Agamemnon in Book 1 (1.254). There the sorrow was their quarrel, which would make Priam and his sons rejoice (1.255–258). Here it is the shame of an army whose best men will not face Hector (7.123–160; see [[nestor]] and [[achaeans]]). Both speeches go on into the past, to the Lapiths beside whom he fought the Centaurs in his youth (1.260–272; see [[nestor-and-the-centaurs]]), and to his fight with Ereuthalion (7.132–157; see [[nestor-and-ereuthalion]]).
