---
summary: The verse ends the supper that Achilles called “the hateful feast” (23.48, 23.57); the others go to their huts, and he lies down on the shore among the Myrmidons.
---

Achilles has sworn not to wash until Patroclus is burned, but he yields to the meal: “But now let us yield to the hateful feast” (23.48; [[hateful]], [[oath-formulas]]). The meal itself is told in the formulas of any feast, “and no one's heart lacked an equal share of the feast” (23.56; [[equal-feast]]). When it is over, Achilles alone does not go to his hut: he lies “on the shore of the loud-roaring sea” (23.59–61; [[groaning-heavily]]), and there the soul of Patroclus comes to him (23.62–65; [[dream-scene]]).
