summary: Victory is what the competitors strive for, and the book hands it out with care. There is a prize for the loser of the boxing and of the wrestling (23.656, 23.704); when the wrestlers cannot throw each other, Achilles rules that “victory is for both; take equal prizes” (23.736); and Nestor remembers how in his youth he defeated everyone at Buprasium but the two sons of Actor, “grudging me the victory” (23.634–639).

νίκης ἱέμενοι, “striving for victory”, and περὶ νίκης, “for victory”, occur in Homer only in this book: of the drivers whose hearts were pounding (23.371), of Menelaus' fear of a crash in the narrow road and Achilles' rebuke to the spectators (23.437, 23.496), of the sons of Actor (23.639), of the wrestlers (23.718), and of Odysseus in the footrace (23.767). The contests make victory a thing that is wanted for its own sake, as Diomedes “greatly wished him victory” for his kinsman Euryalus (23.682), in the words in which Zeus “wished for victory for the Trojans and for Hector” in Book 13 (βούλετο νίκην, 13.347; see [[diomedes]] and [[euryalus]]).

The gods still grant it. Apollo gives the boxer endurance, καμμονίη (23.660–661); Athena gives Diomedes his whip and his horses might, and makes Odysseus' limbs light (23.388–400, 23.770–772; see [[divine-intervention]] and [[kydos]]). But the men may also share it out. Achilles ends the wrestling with a draw, and Walter Leaf observes that Odysseus, who had put Ajax on his back, might have been thought the winner, and that it is not easy to see how the tripod and the woman were to be divided equally (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 23.736; see [[contests]]).

The verb has its wider senses too. Menelaus tells Antilochus that “this time youth has overcome your mind” (νόον νίκησε νεοίη, 23.604; see [[geron]]), and the silver mixing bowl “in beauty … surpassed all others on the whole earth” (23.742; see [[mixing-bowl]]). Leaf notes that this is the only place where the subject of νικάω is a concrete thing (on 23.742). Mecisteus “defeated all the Cadmeians” at the funeral games of Oedipus (πάντας ἐνίκα, 23.680), as Tydeus had defeated the Cadmeians at Thebes (πάντα δʼ ἐνίκα, 4.389; see [[mecisteus]]), and Antilochus “beat all the young men in running” (23.756).
