# Πολυποίταο (23.848), the epic genitive, occurs only here.
grc: [Πολυποίταο]
passages:
  - 23.836-849 | Polypoetes wins the throw
---

**The throw.** Achilles sets out the rough-cast lump of iron that Eetion used to hurl, and promises the winner iron for five years, so that his shepherd and plowman need not go to the city for it (23.826–835; see [[eetion]] and [[iron]]). “So he spoke, and then Polypoetes, steadfast in battle, rose” (23.836), the first of the four to stand up, with his fellow Lapith Leonteus, Telamonian Ajax and Epeius (23.837–838; see [[leonteus]], [[ajax]], [[epeius]] and [[steadfast-in-battle]]). He throws last. Epeius' throw draws laughter, Leonteus' is not described, and Ajax “overshot the marks of all” (23.839–843); then Polypoetes takes the lump, and overshoots the whole gathering by as far as a herdsman can hurl his throwing stick, which flies spinning through the cattle of the herd (23.844–847; see [[the-herdsmans-throwing-stick]]), and the Achaeans shout. The prize is the lump itself, and his companions carry it to the ships: “And the companions of mighty Polypoetes stood up / and carried the king's prize to the hollow ships” (23.848–849; see [[hollow-ships]]). He and Leonteus lead forty ships of Lapiths in the Catalogue (2.738–747; see [[lapiths]]).

The two Lapiths who held the gate of the Achaean wall side by side in Book 12 rise together again here (12.127–194); Leaf refers the reader to that scene (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 23.836).
