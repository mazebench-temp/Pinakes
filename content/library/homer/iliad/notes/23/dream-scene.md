summary: The soul of Patroclus comes to Achilles in his sleep in the form of the dream scene: it “stood above his head and spoke to him,” and its first word, εὕδεις, “You are asleep,” is the Dream's first word to Agamemnon (23.62–69; 2.20–23).
en: [You are asleep]
---

The scene keeps the parts of the type and changes the visitor. Zeus' Dream came in the likeness of Nestor (2.20–22); what comes to Achilles is no messenger in another's shape but the soul of Patroclus itself, “like him in all things, in stature and in the fair eyes / and the voice” (23.66–67; [[psyche]]). It speaks not for a god but for itself: it asks for burial and for one urn for their bones (23.69–92). Achilles answers and reaches out, “but did not take hold of it; and the soul went beneath the earth like smoke, / gibbering; and Achilles sprang up in amazement” (23.100–101); the waking comes after the embrace. The soul has stood over him “all night long” (23.105–106; [[eidolon]]).

The verse στῆ δʼ ἄρʼ ὑπὲρ κεφαλῆς brings Hermes to the sleeping Priam in the next book (24.682). In the Odyssey it brings the phantom that Athena sends to Penelope in her sister's likeness, which also begins with εὕδεις (*Odyssey* 4.795–804). The English “You are asleep” is now linked here and in the two verses of the Dream (2.23, 2.60). See [[the-ghost-of-patroclus]], [[achilles]] and [[sleep]].
