---
summary: The Myrmidons sit down to the funeral feast for Patroclus “beside the ship of the swift-footed grandson of Aeacus” (23.28).
---

ποδώκεος Αἰακίδαο, “the swift-footed grandson of Aeacus”, ends the verse here as it does in the Trojan Catalogue, where two allies are foretold to fall “at the hands of the swift-footed grandson of Aeacus” in the river (2.860, 2.874), and in Books 16 and 17, where Patroclus is “the brave attendant of the swift-footed grandson of Aeacus” (16.165, 17.388; see [[grandson-of-aeacus]] and [[achilles]]). It is the only time the book names Aeacus; elsewhere Achilles is the son of Peleus (see [[peleus]] and [[the-funeral-of-patroclus]]).
