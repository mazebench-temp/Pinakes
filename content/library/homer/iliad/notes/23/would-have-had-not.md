summary: Five times the narrator brings the story to the edge of another outcome: the sun would have set on the mourners, Diomedes would have passed Eumelus, the quarrel of Idomeneus and the son of Oileus would have gone further, Eumelus would have been given the mare, and the wrestlers would have gone on to a third fall (23.154, 23.382, 23.490, 23.540, 23.733).
---

**Mortals who intervene.** In the games those who turn the story are mostly men: Achilles, who speaks to Agamemnon about the pyre (23.155), stands up to stop the quarrel and the wrestling, “had not Achilles himself stood up” (23.491, 23.734), and Antilochus, who rises to claim the second prize for himself (23.541–542). Only once is it a god, “had not Phoebus Apollo borne a grudge against the son of Tydeus” (23.383), and Athena at once undoes his work (23.388–400; [[divine-intervention]]).

**Leaving the race in doubt.** At 23.382 Diomedes “would have passed him or left the race in doubt”; at the finish the same words are used of Menelaus, who would have passed Antilochus “and not left it in doubt” had the course been longer (23.526–527). The conditional of Antilochus' offer at 23.592 is not the narrator's turn and is excepted. See [[achilles]] and [[contest-scene]].
