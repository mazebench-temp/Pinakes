---
summary: The Myrmidons drive around the body weeping, and “the sands were wet, and wet was the armor of the men / with tears” (23.15–16); the soul of Patroclus stands over Achilles “lamenting and weeping” (23.106); and in the race Diomedes weeps for anger and Eumelus for pain (23.385, 23.396–397).
---

The mourning of the first scene is told in tears: the Myrmidons drive three times around the body “weeping”, and “the sands were wet, and wet was the armor of the men / with tears; such a master of rout did they long for” (23.13–16; see [[lament]]). Even the soul weeps: Achilles tells how the soul of Patroclus “stood over me, lamenting and weeping” (23.106; see [[the-ghost-of-patroclus]]).

At the games tears are a sign of anger and of hurt. Diomedes, robbed of his whip, weeps “in his anger” (23.385), and Eumelus, tumbled out of his chariot with his forehead bruised, “his two eyes / filled with tears, and his strong voice was checked” (23.396–397; see [[the-mares-of-eumelus]]).
