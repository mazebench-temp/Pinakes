---
summary: Nestor ends his thanks for the prize Achilles has given him with a prayer: “And for this may the gods give you thanks in return, to satisfy your heart” (23.650).
---

The gift and its return are named in one verse: τῶνδʼ ἀντί, “for this … in return”, and χάριν μενοεικέα, “thanks … to satisfy your heart” (see [[reciprocity-with-the-gods]] and [[doron]]). The old man cannot repay a gift that honors him, so he asks the gods to repay it. Before the prayer he says that he accepts it gladly, “and my heart rejoices” (χαίρει, 23.647), the verb to which χάρις belongs. Achilles had given the bowl as a memorial of Patroclus, and as a prize for an old man who can no longer compete (23.618–623; see [[fame-and-the-tomb]] and [[time]]).
