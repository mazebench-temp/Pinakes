summary: As the Myrmidons drive three times around the body of Patroclus, Thetis rouses among them the longing for lament. The ghost of Patroclus asks that their bones lie together in the golden two-handled jar that Achilles' lady mother gave him, a verse rejected in antiquity as taken from the Odyssey, where Thetis gives the jar for her son's bones.

**The longing for lament.** The Myrmidons drive their horses three times around the body, “weeping; and Thetis roused among them the longing for lament” (23.13–14; see [[myrmidons]], [[lament]] and [[the-funeral-of-patroclus]]). Heyne suspected the verse as a needless expansion, since Thetis is not among the Myrmidons (Walter Leaf, *Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 23.14; see the translation's note on 23.14). Leaf compares her lament with the Nereids in Book 18, “and Thetis led the lamentation” (18.51; see the note on Book 18). Twice more in the book the same longing is roused, by Achilles' words: “and in them all he roused the longing for lament” (23.108, 23.153; see [[himeros]]). Only here is it a goddess who rouses it.

**The golden jar.** The ghost asks that Achilles not lay their bones apart, “so too let one urn enclose the bones of us both, / the golden two-handled jar that your lady mother gave you” (23.91–92; see [[the-golden-urn]] and [[the-ghost-of-patroclus]]). The T scholia report that 92 was athetized as transferred from the *Odyssey*, where Thetis gives the golden jar for Achilles' bones after his death: here she would have given it to him before he sailed, an act of ill omen, and an urn is not a two-handled jar. Leaf thinks the athetesis unquestionably right (on 23.92; see the translation's note on 23.91–92). In the *Odyssey* the soul of Agamemnon tells the soul of Achilles that his mother gave a golden ἀμφιφορεύς, which she said was the gift of Dionysus and the work of Hephaestus, and that his white bones lie in it mingled with those of Patroclus (*Odyssey* 24.73–77; see [[hephaestus]] and [[patroclus]]). The scholia, after Stesichorus, tell that Hephaestus gave the jar to Dionysus, and Dionysus to Thetis, in return for the shelter she gave him (Leaf on 23.92), when Lycurgus drove him into the sea and she took him into her bosom (6.135–137; see [[lycurgus-and-dionysus]]).
