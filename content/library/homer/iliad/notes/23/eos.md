---
summary: The book has two dawns. After the night of the ghost, rosy-fingered Dawn appears to the Myrmidons as they weep around the corpse, and Agamemnon sends men for wood; after the night of the burning, when the morning star goes forth, saffron-robed Dawn spreads over the sea, and the pyre dies down.
---

**The first dawn.** The ghost of Patroclus has gone, and Achilles' words have roused the longing for lament in them all, “and rosy-fingered Dawn appeared to them as they wept / around the pitiable corpse” (23.109–110; see [[rosy-fingered]] and [[the-funeral-of-patroclus]]). The usual verse of the dawn, ἦμος δʼ ἠριγένεια φάνη ῥοδοδάκτυλος Ἠώς, “and when early-born Dawn appeared, the rosy-fingered” (1.477; see [[dawn-line]]), is here bent to the mourners, as in the *Odyssey* Dawn would have found Odysseus and Penelope weeping, had Athena not held her back (καί νύ κʼ ὀδυρομένοισι φάνη ῥοδοδάκτυλος Ἠώς, *Odyssey* 23.241). Later that day they would have mourned until sunset, had Achilles not spoken to Agamemnon: “And now the light of the sun would have set on them as they mourned” (23.154; see [[helios]]).

**The second dawn.** All night the winds blow on the pyre and Achilles pours wine and mourns (23.217–225; see [[boreas]]). Then, “When the morning star goes forth to herald the light over the earth, / the star after which saffron-robed Dawn spreads over the sea, / then the pyre was dying down, and the flame ceased” (23.226–228; see [[saffron-robed]] and [[pyres]]). ἑωσφόρος, “the morning star”, found only here in Homer, is an Attic and not an epic form, and Walter Leaf takes it for a sign of a late passage (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 23.226; see the translation's note on 23.226–227). The evening star, “the fairest star of all”, is a simile of Book 22 (22.317–318; see [[the-evening-star]]). Leaf denies that the dawn spreading over the sea shows the poet living on an eastern coast: to anyone on a shore the first light glints on the waves, whichever way he looks; Book 8 has Dawn spreading over all the earth, and Book 19 has her rising “from the streams of Oceanus” (8.1, 19.1; Leaf on 23.227; see [[oceanus]]).
