---
summary: Antilochus, “steadfast in battle,” sees the narrow place in the road where he will pass Menelaus (23.419); and Polypoetes, “steadfast in battle,” rises for the weight throw and takes the lump that he will throw beyond the whole gathering (23.836, 23.844).
---

μενεχάρμης is Antilochus' epithet as a fighter (13.396, 15.582); here it introduces his boldest act of driving (23.419–424). Polypoetes has μενεπτόλεμος in the Catalogue (2.740) and in both verses of the weight throw, where his cast is likened to a herdsman's throwing stick (23.845–847; [[the-herdsmans-throwing-stick]], [[polypoetes]]). See [[antilochus]].
