---
summary: Aristotle quotes this book twice, Nestor's words on the stump at the turning post, among the problems that are solved by an accent, and the line that ends the night of the ghost, for the pleasure that is mixed with mourning.
---

- Nestor's stump at the turning post, “part of which rots in the rain” as Aristotle's text has it, a problem that Hippias of Thasos solved by an accent (*Poetics* 25, 1461a21–23; *Sophistical Refutations* 4, 166b3–6; on 23.328): [[the-stump-that-rots]] and [[hippias-of-thasos]].
- The line that follows Achilles' account of the ghost, quoted for the pleasure in mourning, which lies in remembering the dead and in a way seeing them (*Rhetoric* 1.11, 1370b25–29, quoting 23.108 = *Odyssey* 4.183): [[the-longing-for-lament]].
