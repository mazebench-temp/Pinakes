---
summary: Achilles tells the river Spercheius that Peleus vowed him sacrifices “into your waters, where you have a precinct and a fragrant altar” (23.148).
---

τέμενος βωμός τε θυήεις is the half-verse of Zeus' precinct on Gargarus (8.48), and in the Odyssey of Aphrodite's at Paphos (*Odyssey* 8.363): the Spercheius, a river god, has his own precinct and altar in Phthia (see [[spercheius]] and [[hieros]]). Aphrodite's oil for Hector's body is ῥοδόεις (23.186), which Walter Leaf thinks may mean no more than “fragrant” (see [[rose-sweet]]). See [[peleus]] and [[sacrifice]].
