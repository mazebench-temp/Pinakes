---
summary: The soul of Patroclus begins its last request with the first half of the formula and a new ending: “And I will tell you something else, and charge you with it, if you will obey me” (23.82).
---

καὶ ἐφήσομαι αἴ κε πίθηαι, found only here, takes the place of “and you lay it to heart” (1.297). What follows is the request of the dead: “do not lay my bones apart from yours, Achilles, / but together,” in one urn, the golden jar that Thetis gave (23.83–92; [[the-golden-urn]]). Achilles promises to “accomplish them all and obey as you bid me” (23.96), and after the burning he has the bones kept in a golden bowl “until I myself am hidden in Hades” (23.243–244).
