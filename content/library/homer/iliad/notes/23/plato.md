---
summary: Plato quotes this book twice in the Republic, the soul of Patroclus going beneath the earth like smoke with Achilles' cry that follows, and the half-verse in which Achilles gives his hair to the dead, which he names with the slaughter of the captives among the things not to be believed. In the Ion the chariot race is “the horse race in honor of Patroclus”, and Nestor's lesson on the turn is a passage that only a charioteer can judge.
---

- The soul that goes beneath the earth like smoke, gibbering, and Achilles' cry that there is some soul and phantom even in the house of Hades, but no mind in it (*Republic* 3.386d–387a, quoting 23.103–104 and 23.100–101): [[plato-the-terrors-of-hades]] and [[the-ghost-of-patroclus]].
- The hair sacred to the Spercheius given to the dead Patroclus, in the half-verse “I would grant my hair to the hero Patroclus to take with him”, and the slaughters of the captives at the pyre (*Republic* 3.391b, quoting 23.151; on 23.175–176): [[plato-achilles-and-the-river]] and [[the-lock-for-the-spercheius]].
- “The draggings of Hector around the tomb of Patroclus” (*Republic* 3.391b) are those of Book 24, after the mound of this book is heaped; here Achilles lays the body face down beside the bier and vows it to the dogs, and the gods keep it whole (23.24–26, 23.182–191): [[the-dragging-of-hector-defended]].
- Nestor's advice to Antilochus on the turn “in the horse race in honor of Patroclus”, which Ion recites, and which only a charioteer can judge (*Ion* 537a–c, quoting 23.335–340): [[nestors-lesson-in-driving]], [[turning-post]] and [[title-of-book-23]].
- **The elder.** Phaedrus, in the *Symposium*, says that Aeschylus talks nonsense in making Achilles the lover of Patroclus, since Achilles was the more beautiful, still beardless, and “much the younger, as Homer says” (*Symposium* 180a). In this book Achilles greets the soul of Patroclus as ἠθείη κεφαλή, “dear heart”, a word that the scholia call the address of a younger man to an elder; Walter Leaf adds that Patroclus was the elder, citing 11.787 and the *Symposium* (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 23.94): [[plato-the-choice-of-achilles]] and [[dear-heart]].
