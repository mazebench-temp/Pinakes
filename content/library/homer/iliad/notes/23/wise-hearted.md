---
summary: Antilochus will not try to beat “the horses of the wise-hearted son of Tydeus, to which Athena / has now held out speed” (23.405–406).
---

The epithet is Diomedes' in the speech of a rival who has seen the goddess at work, though Antilochus could not know of it, an objection on which the verses were athetized in antiquity; Walter Leaf finds the grounds insufficient (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 23.405; see the translation's note on 23.405–406).
