---
summary: Menelaus asks the leaders to judge between him and Antilochus “lest someday one of the bronze-clad Achaeans say” that he won the mare by lies and by his rank, not by his horses (23.573–578).
en: [lest someday]
---

The imagined verdict is the one Menelaus fears: “‘Menelaus overpowered Antilochus with lies, / and has gone off leading the mare, because his horses were far worse, / but he himself was the greater in prowess and strength’” (23.576–578; [[arete]]). Like Hector before the gates (22.106–108), he frames the speech with μή ποτέ τις εἴπῃσιν, the words of Hector's imagined speeches in Book 6 (6.459, 6.479). To forestall it he judges the case himself and calls for Antilochus' oath (23.579–585; [[oath-formulas]], [[bronze-armored]]). See [[menelaus]] and [[antilochus]].
