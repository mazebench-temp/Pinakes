---
summary: The verse brings Ajax and Diomedes face to face in the armed duel of the games: “And when they had come close, advancing against each other, / three times they sprang at each other, and three times they rushed in close” (23.816–817).
---

Here alone the verse begins with ἀλλʼ ὅτε δή instead of οἳ δʼ ὅτε δή. In battle it leads to a speech, as in Diomedes' meeting with Glaucus and Hector's with Achilles (6.121–123, 22.248–250), or to the first cast of a spear (5.14–15, 11.232–233); here it leads to the rushes of a match for prizes, which is stopped before either man is wounded: the Achaeans, “terribly afraid for Ajax,” bid the two “stop and take equal prizes” (23.822–823). Walter Leaf (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 23.798) calls the whole episode patchwork, and 811–816 are borrowed lines (see the translation's note on 23.798–825).
