---
summary: At the funeral feast the hogs are “rich with lard” (23.32); and Eumelus, thrown from his chariot, weeps, “and his strong voice was checked” (23.397).
---

The second is the verse of Antilochus' grief when Menelaus told him of Patroclus' death (17.696; again in the Odyssey, 4.705, 19.472); here it tells of the bruised Eumelus, whose mares have run off the road (23.391–397). θαλέθοντες ἀλοιφῇ, “rich with lard,” has the participle of the same root (23.32; [[white-tusked]]). See [[eumelus]].
