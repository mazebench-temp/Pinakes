---
summary: Achilles wakes from the vision of Patroclus, “Ah, truly even in the house of Hades there is some / soul and phantom” (23.103–104); and the son of Oileus rises from the dung, “Ah, truly the goddess hindered my feet” (23.782).
---

Both exclamations are followed by ἦ, “truly,” and both speak of what a man has just learned for himself. Achilles has reached for the soul and grasped nothing, and he concludes that there is “no mind in it at all” (23.104; [[psyche]], [[eidolon]]; on the reading, see the translation's note on 23.103–104). Ajax' complaint that Athena “stands by Odysseus like a mother and helps him” turns the contest into laughter ([[like-a-mother]]): “So he spoke, and they all laughed heartily at him” (23.782–784; [[so-he-spoke]]).
