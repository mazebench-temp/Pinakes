---
summary: The soul of Patroclus tells how he came to Peleus' house: as a boy he killed the son of Amphidamas, “a foolish child, not meaning to, in anger over the knucklebones” (23.88).
en: [foolish child]
---

The word has both its senses here: a child in years, and a child's want of sense. It is Patroclus' own judgment of himself, not the narrator's, and it excuses as much as it condemns: οὐκ ἐθέλων, “not meaning to” (see [[patroclus]], [[amphidamas-of-opus]] and [[childhood]]). Walter Leaf takes the story as an instance of exile for an unintended killing, and notes that the game of knucklebones is mentioned only here in Homer (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 23.87, 23.88; see the translation's note on 23.86–90). Menoetius brought the boy from Opus to Peleus, who reared him with Achilles (23.85–90; see [[menoetius]], [[opus]] and [[peleus]]).
