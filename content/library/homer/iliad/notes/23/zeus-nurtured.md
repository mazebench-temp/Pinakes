---
summary: Menelaus and Antilochus give each other the title of kings in their dispute over the mare: Menelaus calls Antilochus to the oath, “Antilochus, come here, nurtured by Zeus” (23.581), and Antilochus yields rather than fall from his favor, “nurtured by Zeus, for all my days” (23.594).
---

Menelaus calls Antilochus with the words he used in Book 17 to send him with the news of Patroclus' death: then “Antilochus, come here, nurtured by Zeus, so that you may hear / grievous news” (17.685–686), now “as is the custom,” to swear (23.581; [[oath-formulas]]). Aristarchus athetized 23.581 because the honorific is out of place in anger; Walter Leaf finds that ground “singularly inadequate,” since such formal epithets are used even by enemies, as δῖος of Paris by Menelaus and of Achilles by Andromache (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 23.581; 3.352, 22.455; see the translation's note on 23.581).
