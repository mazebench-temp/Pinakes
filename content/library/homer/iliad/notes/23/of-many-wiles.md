---
summary: Odysseus rises for the wrestling, “and up stood Odysseus of many wiles, who knew crafty ways” (23.709), and again for the footrace (23.755).
---

In the wrestling the epithet is joined to κέρδεα εἰδώς, “who knew crafty ways,” the phrase of Nestor's lesson to Antilochus (23.322; [[kerdos]]), and the match bears it out: lifted by Ajax, Odysseus “did not forget his trickery” and kicks him behind the knee (23.725–726). In the footrace he wins with Athena's help (23.768–783; [[hear-me]]). See [[odysseus]] and [[much-enduring]].
