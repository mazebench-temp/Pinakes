---
summary: Among Achilles' prizes are “fair-belted women” (23.261); and in the footrace Odysseus runs as close behind the son of Oileus “as close as to a fair-belted woman's / breast is the rod” of her loom (23.760–761).
---

The women of the prizes are the captives whom Achilles brings from his ships with the cauldrons, tripods, horses and mules (23.259–261; [[captive-women]]). The simile gives the epithet to a woman at work, drawing the spool past the warp (23.760–763; [[the-weaving-womans-rod]], [[weaving]]). See [[contest-scene]].
