# Πυλοιγενέες (23.303), of horses; Πυλοιγενέος (2.54), of Nestor, is claimed by the Book 2 note.
grc: [Πυλοιγενέες]
---

Πυλοιγενέες δέ οἱ ἵπποι / ὠκύποδες φέρον ἅρμα, “and horses born in Pylos, / swift-footed, drew his chariot” (23.303–304; see [[antilochus]]). Πυλοιγενής, “Pylos-born”, is said twice in Homer: of Nestor, “the king born in Pylos” (2.54), and here of his son's horses (see [[pylos-born]] and [[nestor]]). Walter Leaf reports a variant παλαιγενέες, “old”, which he thinks was suggested by Menelaus' taunt later in the race, that Antilochus' horses are “both of them … lacking in youth” (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 23.303, citing 23.445). For all their birth, Nestor tells his son, they are “the slowest at running” (23.310), and Antilochus wins with them by crafty ways, not by speed (23.515; see [[kerdos]]). They are his father's horses (23.402), as Nestor's team in Book 11 were “the Neleian mares” (11.597; see [[neleus]]).
