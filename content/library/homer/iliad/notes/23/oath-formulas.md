summary: Achilles swears “No, by Zeus, who is highest and best of the gods” that he will not wash until Patroclus is burned (23.42–47); and Menelaus calls on Antilochus to swear, touching his horses, by the god who shakes the earth, that he did not hamper his chariot by a trick (23.581–585).
# ὄμνυθι μὴ μὲν ἑκὼν (23.585, only here): the sworn denial with μὴ μέν, as 19.261.
grc: ["ὄμνυθι μὴ μὲν ἑκὼν"]
---

**Achilles' oath.** “But he refused stubbornly, and swore an oath besides” (23.42): οὐ μά with the god's name in the accusative is the oath of denial, as in Achilles' first oath, by Apollo (1.86). The second half-verse, θεῶν ὕπατος καὶ ἄριστος, is the one with which Agamemnon called Zeus to witness that he never touched Briseis (19.258; [[most-high]]). Monro, quoted by Walter Leaf (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 23.43; see the translation's note on 23.43), took the line for an old formula: “Zeus, or by whatever name the highest of the gods is to be called.”

**The oath that is not sworn.** Menelaus prescribes the rite: Antilochus is to stand before his horses and chariot, whip in hand, and “touching the horses, by the earth-holder, the shaker of the earth, / swear that you did not willingly hamper my chariot by a trick” (23.581–585; [[earth-holder]], [[shaker-of-the-earth]]). The words of the denial, μὴ μὲν ἑκών, are shaped like Agamemnon's, μὴ μὲν ἐγώ (19.261). Antilochus does not swear; he gives up the mare (23.587–595), and Leaf (on 23.595) takes his words “an offender against the gods” as a confession that he cannot take the oath (see the translation's note on 23.595). Menelaus had warned him in the race: “not even so will you carry off the prize without an oath” (23.441). See [[oath]], [[achilles]], [[menelaus]] and [[antilochus]].
