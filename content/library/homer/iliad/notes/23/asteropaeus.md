**The corselet.** When Antilochus will not give up the second prize of the chariot race, Achilles offers Eumelus something from his own store: “I will give him the corselet that I took from Asteropaeus, / of bronze, around which a casting of shining tin / runs in a circle; it will be worth much to him”, and Automedon brings it from the hut (23.558–565; see [[eumelus]], [[corselet]] and [[tin]]). Walter Leaf observes that the fight in the river says nothing of a corselet, though if Asteropaeus wore one Achilles' sword must have gone through it when it struck him in the belly, and he concludes that the games belong to a time when the corselet had become a regular part of the armor (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 23.560; 21.180).

**The sword.** For the armed duel Achilles sets out the armor of Sarpedon, which Patroclus took, for the two who fight to share, and for the one who first reaches the other's flesh “this silver-studded sword, / a fine Thracian one, which I took from Asteropaeus” (23.798–808; see [[silver-studded]], [[sword]] and [[sarpedon]]). When the Achaeans stop the fight between Telamonian Ajax and Diomedes, Achilles gives the sword with its sheath and strap to Diomedes (23.822–825; see [[diomedes]] and [[ajax]]). Sarpedon, whose armor is the shared prize, was the leader who chose Asteropaeus to lead the allies with him at the wall (12.101–104; see [[the-funeral-games-of-patroclus]]).
