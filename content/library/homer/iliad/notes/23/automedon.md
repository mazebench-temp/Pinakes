When Achilles has promised Eumelus the corselet of Asteropaeus as a second prize, “He spoke, and bade Automedon, his dear companion, / bring it from the hut” (23.563–564; see [[achilles]], [[eumelus]] and [[asteropaeus]]). It is Automedon's only part in the funeral and the games; he does not drive in the race, since Achilles keeps his horses out of it (23.274–284; see [[the-horses-of-achilles]]). Walter Leaf thinks the verse in which he puts the corselet in Eumelus' hands, and Eumelus receives it with joy, was interpolated from the verse of Nestor's prize, as the manuscripts show (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 23.565; 23.624). In the last book Automedon and Alcimus are the two companions who wait on Achilles when Priam comes, the men he honored most after the dead Patroclus (24.473–475, 24.574–575; see [[priam]]).
