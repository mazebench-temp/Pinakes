---
summary: Achilles stops the quarrel of Idomeneus and Ajax, son of Oileus, by turning their own sense of what is fitting against them: “No longer now answer each other with harsh words, / Ajax and Idomeneus, with evil words, since it is not fitting. / You would be indignant with another man who did such things” (23.492–494).
---

The appeal is to the νέμεσις each of them would feel if he saw another man quarrel so (see [[aidos]]). Nausicaa says nearly the same in the Odyssey, of a girl who would go about with men before her marriage against her parents' will: καὶ δʼ ἄλλῃ νεμεσῶ, ἥ τις τοιαῦτά γε ῥέζοι, “and I would blame another girl who did such things” (*Odyssey* 6.286–288). Walter Leaf notes the parallel, and, after Monro's *Homeric Grammar*, that the optative after a present “avoids assuming that the case will ever occur” (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 23.494).

The quarrel is told through [[neikos]] and [[eris]]. Achilles ends it as he will end the wrestling, with the same half-verse, εἰ μὴ Ἀχιλλεὺς αὐτὸς ἀνίστατο, “had not Achilles himself stood up” (23.491, 23.734; see [[would-have-had-not]], [[idomeneus]] and [[ajax-son-of-oileus]]).
