# Ὀπόεντος (23.85) is found only here.
grc: [Ὀπόεντος]
---

{{quote:b2f5a129-4570-5315-a2d2-b40843bc5575}}

The town is named three times in the Iliad: among the Locrian towns of the Catalogue (2.531), in Achilles' lament for the promise he made Menoetius to bring his son home to Opus (18.326), and here, as the place from which Patroclus came (see [[locrians]], [[menoetius]] and [[patroclus]]). The ghost tells of the killing that sent him away, and of his welcome in Peleus' house (23.83–90; see [[the-ghost-of-patroclus]], [[amphidamas-of-opus]] and [[exile-and-refuge]]). Walter Leaf thinks the story is told to reconcile the promise of Book 18 with Patroclus' life in Peleus' house (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 23.87).

**Outside Homer.** Strabo, describing Opus, takes from Homer that Patroclus was from Opus and fled to Peleus after an involuntary killing, while his father stayed at home; but Menoetius was not king of the Opuntians, he says, for that was Ajax the Locrian. The boy Patroclus killed, he reports, is called Aeanes, and a precinct called the Aeaneum and a spring called Aeanis are shown after him (*Geography* 9.4.2; see [[strabo]] and [[ajax-son-of-oileus]]). Apollodorus places the killing at Opus and names the boy Clitonymus, son of Amphidamas (*Library* 3.13.8; see [[apollodorus]]).
