# στυγερή (23.48 στυγερῇ, 23.79 στυγερή; the pattern takes both, and in the Odyssey 7.216, 11.201, 15.408, 24.200).
grc: [στυγερή]
---

The feast is hateful to Achilles because he must eat while Patroclus lies unburned; Walter Leaf notes that he speaks of food as a power that must be obeyed, and that the variant τερπώμεθα, “let us take pleasure,” would be out of place in his mouth (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 23.48; see the translation's note on 23.48 and [[equal-feast]]). The doom that gapes for Patroclus is the doom “allotted me even at my birth” (23.79; [[ker-doom]]). στυγερή is new to the entry. See [[achilles]] and [[patroclus]].
