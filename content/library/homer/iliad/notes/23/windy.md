---
summary: Achilles' limbs are weary “from chasing Hector toward windy Ilios” (23.64); and Echepolus gave Agamemnon his mare Aethe “so that he might not have to follow him to windy Ilios” (23.297).
---

Ἴλιον ἠνεμόεσσαν is the formula of Troy. The second use belongs to the story of a rich man who bought himself out of the war with a horse, a payment in place of service, as Walter Leaf puts it (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 23.297; see the translation's note on 23.297).
