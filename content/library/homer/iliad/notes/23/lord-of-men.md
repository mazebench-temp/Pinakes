---
summary: Agamemnon is “lord of men” as Achilles bids him send for wood (23.49), as he dismisses the army from the pyre (23.161), and in the last speech-close of the book, when he accepts the javelin prize (23.895); and Eumelus is “lord of men” as he rises first for the chariot race (23.288).
---

“First by far rose Eumelus, lord of men” (23.288) is the verse of Agamemnon rising first to face Hector in Book 7 (7.162), with Eumelus in the king's place; Walter Leaf notes that Eumelus is named elsewhere only in the Catalogue (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 23.288; 2.714, 2.763–767). Agamemnon's own last verse with the title is the verse of his obedience to Nestor in Book 2, “So he spoke, and Agamemnon, lord of men, did not disobey” (23.895; 2.441; [[so-he-spoke]]).
