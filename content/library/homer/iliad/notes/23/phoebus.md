---
summary: “Phoebus Apollo” brings a dark cloud over Hector's body to keep the sun from it (23.188), and in the chariot race bears a grudge against Diomedes and strikes the whip from his hands (23.383–384).
---

Both are acts of care: for the body of Hector, which Aphrodite anoints while Apollo shades it (23.184–191), and for the mares of Eumelus, which Apollo bred (2.766; see the translation's note on 23.383). The second is undone at once by Athena (23.388–390; [[divine-intervention]]). In the archery he is Apollo without the name Phoebus, the god whom Teucer forgets and Meriones remembers (23.862–873; [[far-shooter]]). See [[apollo]].
