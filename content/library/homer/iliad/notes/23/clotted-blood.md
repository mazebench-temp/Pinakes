---
summary: The kings have a tripod set over the fire “in the hope that they might persuade / the son of Peleus to wash off the clotted blood,” and he refuses with an oath (23.40–44).
---

The formula is the washing of the dead and the wounded: the Achaeans and Trojans washed their dead after battle (7.425), Hecamede was to bathe the wounded Machaon (14.7), and Achilles had a tripod set on the fire to wash it from the body of Patroclus (18.343–345), as the kings now set one for him (23.40). Here it is offered to Achilles himself, alive and unwounded, and he will not wash until Patroclus is on the fire (23.42–47; [[oath-formulas]], [[bathing]]). See [[achilles]] and [[bloody]].
