---
summary: The soul of Patroclus addresses Achilles “like the gods” as it foretells his death: “and it is your fate too, Achilles, like the gods, / to die beneath the wall of the wealthy Trojans” (23.80–81).
---

θεοῖς ἐπιείκελʼ Ἀχιλλεῦ is a vocative address: Phoenix uses it twice in his appeal (9.485, 9.494), Hector in his taunt after Achilles' miss (22.279), and Priam will begin his plea with it (24.486); in the Odyssey the soul of Agamemnon greets the soul of Achilles with it in Hades (*Odyssey* 24.36). The dead Patroclus gives the godlike address to a man who will die (see [[death-of-achilles-foretold]], [[moira]] and [[wealthy]]). See [[achilles]] and [[patroclus]].
