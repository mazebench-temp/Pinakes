summary: Meriones, “a brave man,” is in charge of the woodcutters (23.112); twelve “brave sons” of the Trojans die at the pyre (23.175, 23.181); at the games Meriones is three times “the good attendant of Idomeneus” (23.528, 23.860, 23.888), Epeius rises “tall and brave” (23.664), and Eumelus and Iphiclus are “good as he is” and “good as he was” (23.546, 23.636); and Menelaus remembers what Antilochus and “your brave father” have suffered for him (23.608).
# ἀνὴρ ἐσθλὸς (23.112) and πατὴρ ἀγαθὸς (23.608): each only here.
grc: ["ἀνὴρ ἐσθλὸς", "πατὴρ ἀγαθὸς"]
---

**Brave.** The adjectives of praise run through the book in their formulas. “A brave man watched over them, / Meriones” (23.112–113) opens the first appearance of Meriones in the book (see [[manly]]). The twelve Trojans are “brave sons of the great-hearted Trojans” in the narrative and again in Achilles' address to the dead (23.175, 23.181; [[great-hearted]], [[human-sacrifice]]). ἠΰς τε μέγας τε, “tall and brave,” introduces Epeius before his boast (23.664; [[epeius]]), and μένος ἠΰ is “the noble might” of Aethe, Agamemnon's mare (23.524).

**Good as he is.** The concessive ἐσθλὸς ἐών is said by Antilochus of Eumelus, whom he will not let have the second prize, “and he himself, good as he is” (23.546), and by Nestor of Iphiclus, whom he outran in his youth, “good as he was” (23.636). Menelaus' “your brave father” is Nestor, who, like his sons, “suffered much and toiled much” for Menelaus' sake (23.607–608; [[amends-and-reconciliation]]).

**The good attendant.** θεράπων ἐῢς Ἰδομενῆος (13.246) is Meriones' formula in the games: in the chariot race, the archery and the javelin (23.528, 23.860, 23.888). See [[meriones]] and [[nestor]].
