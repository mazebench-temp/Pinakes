summary: The range of a discus is the measure of Antilochus' lead over Menelaus in the chariot race (23.431–433, 23.523), and the lump of iron of the weight throw is whirled and thrown like a discus, its throws marked as in the discus throw of the Phaeacians (23.839–843).
# δίσκου (23.431) and δίσκουρα (23.523), the compound found only here, are first met here.
grc: [δίσκου, δίσκουρα]
---

**A measure of distance.** When Antilochus forces his way past Menelaus in the narrow road, “As far as the range of a discus swung from the shoulder carries, / which a man in his prime lets fly, testing his young strength, / so far they ran ahead” (23.431–433; see [[antilochus]] and [[menelaus]]). The measure is like the cast of a spear “when a man throws it to test his strength” (15.358–359) and the cast of a long javelin “which a man throws, trying his strength, either in a contest” or in war (16.589–590); Walter Leaf compares those lines and takes κατωμάδιος, “swung from the shoulder”, with κατωμαδόν, the whip swung from the shoulder (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 23.431; 15.352, 23.500). οὖρα is a measured stretch, as in the mules' furrow of 10.351 (see the translation's note on 10.351–353). At the finish the measure returns as one word: “At first, it is true, he was left behind as far as the range of a discus, / but he quickly caught up with him” (23.523–524). Leaf notes that δίσκουρα occurs only here and seems to have offended some ancient critics, who read δίσκου οὖρα or δίσκʼ οὖρα (on 23.523). Meriones is behind by another measure, “the cast of a spear” (23.529; see [[spear]]).

**The weight throw.** The weight thrown for the lump of iron is no discus, but it is thrown like one: Epeius “whirled it and threw” (23.840), and Ajax hurled it “from his strong hand, and overshot the marks of all” (23.843; see [[iron]], [[epeius]] and [[ajax]]). Odysseus' discus among the Phaeacians went from his strong hand and “flew past the marks of all” (ὑπέρπτατο σήματα πάντων, *Odyssey* 8.189–192). Leaf reports that the line was athetized in antiquity as brought in from the Odyssey, since after only two throws “of all” is out of place, and keeps it; the σήματα are pegs stuck in the ground to mark how far each man threw (on 23.843).
