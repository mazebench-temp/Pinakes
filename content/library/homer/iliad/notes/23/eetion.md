summary: The lump of iron that Achilles sets out at the funeral games once belonged to Eetion, Andromache's father: “the great strength of Eetion used to hurl” it, until Achilles killed him and carried it off in his ships with the rest of his possessions (23.826–829).
# Unnamed: "him" whom Achilles killed (τόν, 23.828).
refs: [23.828]
---

ὃν πρὶν μὲν ῥίπτασκε μέγα σθένος Ἠετίωνος· / ἀλλʼ ἤτοι τὸν ἔπεφνε ποδάρκης δῖος Ἀχιλλεύς, “which once the great strength of Eetion used to hurl; / but him brilliant swift-footed Achilles killed” (23.827–828; see [[iron]] and [[achilles]]). μέγα σθένος Ἠετίωνος, “the great strength of Eetion”, names the man by his strength, like “the might of Heracles” (βίη Ἡρακληείη) and “mighty lord Teucer” later in the book (23.859; see [[teucer]]). ῥίπτασκε, “used to hurl”, is an iterative: the king of Thebe threw this lump again and again, and now the competitors throw it. Walter Leaf notes that the form should be ῥίπτεσκε or ῥίψασκε, and identifies this Eetion as Andromache's father (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 23.827).

The prize is one more piece of the plunder of Thebe that stays with Achilles, like the lyre he plays in Book 9, taken “when he destroyed the city of Eetion” (9.186–188), and the horse Pedasus (16.152–154; see [[sack-of-thebe]] and [[pedasus-the-horse]]). In Book 6 Andromache told Hector that Achilles killed her father but did not strip him, and burned him in his armor (6.414–420; see [[andromache]]). The lump of iron that Eetion threw is given away at the funeral of Patroclus, and Polypoetes wins it (23.844–849; see [[polypoetes]]).
