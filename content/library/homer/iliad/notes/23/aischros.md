summary: Ajax, son of Oileus, answers Idomeneus' report of the race “shamefully” (αἰσχρῶς, 23.473), with taunts about his age, his eyes and his tongue; and Menelaus tells Antilochus, who has passed him by a trick, “You have shamed my prowess” (ᾔσχυνας, 23.571).
grc: [ᾔσχυνας]
en: [shamefully, shamed]
---

αἰσχρῶς ἐνένιπεν, “rebuked him shamefully”, is found in the Iliad only here, and once in the Odyssey, of the maid Melantho abusing the disguised Odysseus (*Odyssey* 18.321). Walter Leaf renders the adverb “insultingly” and compares the shaming words, αἰσχροῖς ἐπέεσσι, with which Hector rebukes Paris (3.38; *Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 23.473). The shame is in the words and falls on the man they are aimed at: Ajax tells Idomeneus that he is not so very young, that his eyes are not the sharpest, and that he is always blustering (23.474–481; see [[neikos]] and [[idomeneus]]).

Menelaus' verb, αἰσχύνω, is the verb of fouling and disfiguring (18.24, 22.75), and of disgracing one's descent, as Glaucus' father bade him “not to shame the race of my fathers” (6.209). A trick has made his ἀρετή look less than it is (see [[arete]]). Nestor had warned Antilochus of the other disgrace: if he smashed his chariot on the stone at the turning post, it would be “a joy to the others, and a disgrace to yourself” (ἐλεγχείη, 23.342; see [[elenchos]]).
