---
summary: Answering Plato, Proclus defends Achilles' gift of the hair as the second-best course once he knew he would not go home, and reads the pyre of Patroclus, the winds, the night's libation from the golden mixing bowl and the twelve Trojans, as a rite for the soul. He takes Achilles' cry on waking for Homer's own distinction of soul, image and mind.
