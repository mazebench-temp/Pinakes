---
summary: The only fish of Book 23 is in a simile of the boxing: Euryalus, struck by Epeius, leaps up as a fish leaps under the ripple of the North Wind on a shore full of seaweed, before a dark wave hides it (23.692–694).
---

ὡς δʼ ὅθʼ ὑπὸ φρικὸς Βορέω ἀναπάλλεται ἰχθὺς / θίνʼ ἐν φυκιόεντι, μέλαν δέ ἑ κῦμα κάλυψεν, “And as when under the ripple of the North Wind a fish leaps up / on a shore full of seaweed, and a dark wave hides it” (23.692–693; see [[the-leaping-fish]]). Two books before, the fish of the Scamander were the eaters of the dead: Achilles told Lycaon, as he threw his body into the river, that a fish would dart up under the dark ripple to eat his white fat (21.122–127). Here the fish is the man struck down, lifted by the blow as the fish is thrown up by the wind and the wave. Walter Leaf takes θίς, the shore, to be the sand where shallow water covers it (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 23.692).
