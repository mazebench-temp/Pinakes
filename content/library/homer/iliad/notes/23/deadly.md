---
summary: Achilles bids the Myrmidons weep until “we have taken our fill of deadly lament” (23.10), and asks the soul of Patroclus to stand near so that they may “take our fill of deadly lament” (23.98); and Menelaus rebukes Antilochus, “no other mortal is more baneful than you” (23.439).
---

**The lament.** ὀλοοῖο τεταρπώμεσθα γόοιο is a phrase of this book and the Odyssey (see the translation's note on 23.10). In the Odyssey Odysseus says it to the shade of his mother, wishing that even in Hades, καὶ εἰν Ἀΐδαο, they might throw their arms around each other and take their fill of chill lament, κρυεροῖο τεταρπώμεσθα γόοιο (*Odyssey* 11.210–212); here Achilles says it to the soul of Patroclus, and the embrace fails (23.97–101; [[lament]], [[psyche]]).

**Menelaus.** Ἀντίλοχʼ οὔ τις σεῖο βροτῶν ὀλοώτερος ἄλλος is the verse with which Menelaus reproached Zeus in Book 3, “Father Zeus, no other god is more baneful than you” (3.365), with a mortal in place of the god; and in Book 15 he had encouraged Antilochus with the same opening, “Antilochus, no other of the Achaeans is younger than you” (15.569). See [[menelaus]] and [[antilochus]].
