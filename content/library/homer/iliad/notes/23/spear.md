summary: The spear, shield and helmet of Sarpedon are the prizes of the armed duel, in which Diomedes keeps reaching Ajax's neck with his spear-point (23.798–821); and for the javelin, the last contest, Achilles sets out a long-shadowed spear, which goes to Meriones when Agamemnon is given the prize without a contest (23.884–896).

**Sarpedon's armor.** “Then the son of Peleus brought a long-shadowed spear / and set it down in the gathering, and a shield and a helmet, / the armor of Sarpedon, which Patroclus had taken from him” (23.798–800; see [[sarpedon]], [[long-shadowed]] and [[patroclus]]). It is the armor that Patroclus gave his companions to carry to the ships after Sarpedon's death (16.663–665; Walter Leaf, *Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 23.800). The two fighters are to “put on their armor and take the flesh-cutting bronze” (23.803; see [[flesh-cutting]]), and in the bout Diomedes, over the great shield, “kept reaching his neck with the point of his shining spear” (23.820–821; see [[diomedes]], [[ajax]] and [[shield]]). Leaf takes κῦρε as touching without wounding, enough to win the sword that was promised (on 23.821; see [[sword]]).

**The javelin.** “Then the son of Peleus brought a long-shadowed spear / and a cauldron untouched by fire, worth an ox, flowered, / and set them down in the gathering; and men who throw the javelin stood up” (23.884–886; see [[cauldron]]). ἥμονες, “men who throw”, and ἥμασιν, “in throwing” (23.891), are found only here, and Leaf reports that some ancient readers read ῥήμονες, “speakers”, as if the contest were one of words; he calls the reading extraordinary, since such a contest is entirely alien to the epic spirit (on 23.886). Agamemnon and Meriones rise, and Achilles, saying that all know how far Agamemnon is the best “in strength and in throwing”, gives him the prize without a contest and bids “let us give the spear to the hero Meriones” (23.887–894; see [[agamemnon]] and [[meriones]]); “and he gave Meriones the bronze spear” (23.896). Leaf thinks the spear can hardly be worth more than the cauldron, and reports Jordan's suggestion that it was not a prize but the weapon of the contest, given to Meriones as an afterthought. He notes that the javelin had been foretold in Achilles' words to Nestor, “nor enter the javelin throw any more” (23.622), and sees a certain dignity in the way Agamemnon is honored without a contest in which his position would not allow him to be beaten (on 23.884).

The spear is also the measure and the boast of other contests: Meriones finished “behind famous Menelaus by the cast of a spear” (23.529), and Nestor remembers that at the games for Amarynceus “with the spear I outthrew Phyleus and Polydorus” (23.637; see [[the-funeral-games-of-amarynceus]]).
