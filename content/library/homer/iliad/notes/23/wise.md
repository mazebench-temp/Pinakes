summary: Menelaus throws Antilochus' good name for prudence back at him, “We Achaeans were wrong to say that you were wise” (23.440), and “Antilochus, who were wise before, what a thing you have done!” (23.570); and the narrator gives him the epithet as he answers and gives way, “Then wise Antilochus answered him face to face” (23.586).
# πεπνῦσθαι (23.440; also Odyssey 10.495) and πεπνυμένε (23.570, only here).
grc: [πεπνῦσθαι, πεπνυμένε]
---

The three uses follow the quarrel from the race to the reconciliation. Antilochus' answer justifies the word: he admits the rashness of youth, “for his mind is quicker, and his counsel thin” (23.590; [[metis]]), and gives up the mare (23.591–595), and Menelaus grants that he was never “flighty or light of mind / before” (23.603–604). The verse of the answer is the one given to Meriones in Book 13, and in the Odyssey to Telemachus, with Antilochus' name (23.586; 13.254, 13.266). The two new forms are found only in these verses of the Iliad. See [[antilochus]], [[menelaus]] and [[amends-and-reconciliation]].
