summary: The horses of Tros run their last race. Diomedes yokes them for the chariot race at Patroclus' funeral games, and with Athena's help they come in first (23.290–292, 23.377–400, 23.499–513); Nestor names “the horses of Laomedon”, of the same breed, among the swiftest horses he knows (23.348).

**In the race.** Diomedes leads under the yoke “the horses of Tros, of which he had once robbed / Aeneas, though Apollo saved the man himself” (23.291–292; see [[diomedes]] and [[aeneas]]). Τρῳός and Τρώϊος are adjectives that elsewhere mean “Trojan”; of these horses the translation renders them “of Tros”, as in Book 5, where Aeneas boasted of them and Diomedes told their pedigree (5.222, 5.265–267; see [[tros]]). On the way back they come up behind the mares of Eumelus, which Apollo reared, “and they were not far behind, but very close” (23.377–381; see [[the-mares-of-eumelus]]). Apollo knocks the whip from Diomedes' hand; Athena gives it back and puts might into the horses, and they leap out ahead of the others (23.382–400; see [[apollo]] and [[athena]]). At the finish they lift themselves high as they run, and stand in the gathering streaming with sweat; the first prizes are the woman and the tripod (23.499–513; see [[sthenelus]]). The breed that Zeus gave Tros in recompense for his son was taken from Aeneas as a spoil, and wins the prize at the funeral of a dead companion.

**The horses of Laomedon.** Nestor, telling his son that no one will catch him if he passes the others at the post, names the swiftest horses he can think of: Arion, the horse of Adrastus, “or the horses of Laomedon, the good ones that were reared here” (23.346–348; see [[arion-the-horse]] and [[laomedon]]). Laomedon's horses were of the breed of Tros (5.265–269), so that the breed is named twice in the race, by Nestor as a standard and on the course in Diomedes' hands. Walter Leaf thought 23.348 an interpolation (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 23.348).
