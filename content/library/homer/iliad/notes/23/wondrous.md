summary: The winds rise “with a wondrous din” and fall on the pyre, “and the wondrous fire roared loud” (23.213, 23.216); and Achilles says of the soul of Patroclus, “and it was wondrously like him” (23.107).
# θέσκελον (23.107, only here), the adverb of θέσκελος, as θέσκελα (3.130).
grc: [θέσκελον]
en: [wondrously]
---

θεσπιδαὲς πῦρ, “the wondrous fire,” is elsewhere the fire of battle at the wall and the ships and the fire of the simile of Achilles' rampage (12.177, 15.597, 20.490); here it is the fire of the pyre, kindled by the winds that Iris brings (23.198–216; [[pyres]]). θέσκελον, “wondrously,” said of the likeness of the soul to the living man (23.107; [[psyche]]), is new to the entry. See [[zephyrus]] and [[boreas]].
