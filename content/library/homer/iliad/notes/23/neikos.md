summary: Two quarrels at the chariot race. Menelaus, passed by a trick, rebukes Antilochus (νεικείων, 23.438); and Idomeneus, insulted by Ajax, son of Oileus, calls him “Ajax, best in quarreling, ill-counseling” (νεῖκος ἄριστε, 23.483).
en: [rebuking him]
---

νεῖκος ἄριστε is a mock title, built like εἶδος ἄριστε, “best in looks”, with which Hector twice abuses Paris (3.39, 13.769); Walter Leaf prefers the accusative νεῖκος, as more idiomatic, to the variant νείκει (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 23.483). The translation keeps νεῖκος, “quarrel”, apart from ἔρις, “strife” (see its key-word row for ἔρις / νεῖκος), and both words are used of this quarrel: Ajax is best at νεῖκος, and the ἔρις between the two would have gone further if Achilles had not stood up (23.490–491; see [[eris]], [[nemesis]] and [[aischros]]). Idomeneus does not answer insult with insult. He proposes a wager, and Leaf sees his resentment cooling even as he speaks (on 23.483; see [[idomeneus]] and [[ajax-son-of-oileus]]).

Menelaus' rebuke is the other kind: “Antilochus, no other mortal is more baneful than you. / Off with you! We Achaeans were wrong to say that you were wise” (23.439–440; see [[menelaus]] and [[antilochus]]).
