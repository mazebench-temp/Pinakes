---
summary: Jason is named once in the book, in his son's patronymic: the silver mixing bowl of the footrace had been given to Patroclus by “Euneus, son of Jason” (23.747).
---

Ἰησονίδης Εὔνηος, “Euneus, son of Jason” (23.747), is the name and title under which Euneus sent wine to the camp from Lemnos in Book 7 (7.468; see [[euneus]] and [[lemnos]]); in Book 21 he is “the son of Jason” who paid the price for Lycaon (21.41; see the note on Book 21). The bowl he gave as that price had come to him from Thoas, the king of Lemnos (23.745), whom later writers make the father of Hypsipyle, the Lemnian mother of Jason's son (7.469; see [[thoas-of-lemnos]], [[hypsipyle]] and [[the-lemnian-women]]). Of Jason himself the Iliad tells nothing more than his son's descent.
