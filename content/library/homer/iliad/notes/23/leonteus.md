summary: Leonteus is one of the four who throw the lump of iron at the funeral games; he throws second, after Epeius, and Polypoetes, his fellow leader of the Lapiths, wins (23.836–849).
# Λεοντῆος (23.837), the genitive, occurs only here.
grc: [Λεοντῆος]
---

ἂν δὲ Λεοντῆος κρατερὸν μένος ἀντιθέοιο, “and up rose the strong might of godlike Leonteus” (23.837): the hero named by his strength, κρατερὸν μένος, as Teucer is “mighty lord Teucer”, βίη Τεύκροιο ἄνακτος, later in the book (23.859; see [[menos]] and [[teucer]]). He rises after Polypoetes, with whom he held the gate of the Achaean wall in Book 12 (12.127–194; see [[polypoetes]]), and with Telamonian Ajax and Epeius (23.838; see [[ajax]] and [[epeius]]). “Then second in his turn Leonteus, offshoot of Ares, let it fly” (23.841): the verse-end Λεοντεὺς ὄζος Ἄρηος is the one that names him in the Catalogue and in the fight at the wall (2.745, 12.188; see [[offshoot-of-ares]]). Nothing is said of his throw. Ajax, third, overshoots the marks of all, and Polypoetes, last, overshoots the whole gathering (23.842–847).
