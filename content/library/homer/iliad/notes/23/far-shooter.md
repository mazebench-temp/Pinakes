---
summary: In the archery Meriones at once “promised far-shooting Apollo” a hecatomb of firstborn lambs, and his arrow strikes the dove (23.872–876), where Teucer, who made no promise, had only cut the cord (23.862–867).
---

The vow is the one that Athena urged on Pandarus in Book 4 and that he made, word for word, “that he would offer a glorious hecatomb of firstborn lambs” (23.864, 23.873; 4.102, 4.120; [[firstborn]]); Walter Leaf takes the hecatomb of lambs as “Apollo's fixed price for a successful shot” (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 23.864). Teucer missed the bird, “for Apollo begrudged him that” (23.865). See [[apollo]], [[meriones]] and [[teucer]].
