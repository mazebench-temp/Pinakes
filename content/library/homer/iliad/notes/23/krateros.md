---
summary: Diomedes is “mighty Diomedes” as he rises for the chariot race and for the armed duel and in Idomeneus' guess at the leader (23.290, 23.472, 23.812); Polypoetes is “mighty” as his companions carry off the lump of iron (23.848); and the word is “strong” of the force of Odysseus and the might of Leonteus (23.720, 23.837).
---

κρατερὸς Διομήδης, his standing epithet, ends three verses (23.290, 23.472, 23.812; [[son-of-tydeus]]). In the wrestling neither man can throw the other, “but the strong force of Odysseus held” (23.720), κρατερὴ … ἲς Ὀδυσῆος, a periphrasis of the kind of “the strong might of godlike Leonteus” in the weight throw (23.837; [[antitheos]]) and “the great strength of Eetion” (23.827). See [[diomedes]], [[odysseus]], [[polypoetes]] and [[leonteus]].
