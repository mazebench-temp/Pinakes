---
summary: Achilles calls to the chariot race “whoever of the Achaeans / trusts in his horses and his well-joined chariot” (23.285–286).
---

ἵπποισι … καὶ ἅρμασι κολλητοῖσιν is the phrase of men standing ready in their chariots (4.366, 11.198). Nestor answers it in his lesson to Antilochus: he who wheels wide, “trusting in his horses and his chariot,” loses to him who knows crafty ways (23.319–322; [[kerdos]]).
