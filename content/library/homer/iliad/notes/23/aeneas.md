---
summary: Aeneas is named in Book 23 only as the man from whom Diomedes took the horses of Tros that win the chariot race, “though Apollo saved the man himself” (23.291–292).
---

Diomedes yokes for the race “the horses of Tros, of which he had once robbed / Aeneas, though Apollo saved the man himself” (23.291–292; see [[diomedes]] and [[ganymede-and-the-horses-of-tros]]). The two verses recall Book 5. Diomedes struck Aeneas on the hip with a stone, Aphrodite carried her son out of the fight until Diomedes wounded her hand, and Apollo took him up in a dark cloud and brought him to his temple on Pergamus, where Leto and Artemis healed him; meanwhile Sthenelus drove his horses off to the Achaeans (5.302–346, 5.432–448; see [[aphrodite]], [[apollo]] and [[sthenelus]]). They were two of the six foals that his father Anchises bred in secret from the stallions of Laomedon (5.268–272; see [[anchises-steals-the-horses]]). Aeneas has since been saved once more, by Poseidon, from Achilles (20.318–340). ἀπηύρα, “robbed”, is the verb of taking spoils from an enemy, which Achilles uses in this book of the corselet and the sword he took from Asteropaeus (23.560, 23.808; see [[asteropaeus]]).
