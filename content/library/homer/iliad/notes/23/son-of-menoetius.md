---
summary: Hector lies face down “beside the bier of the son of Menoetius” (23.25), and Achilles bids the Achaeans gather “the bones of Patroclus, son of Menoetius” (23.239).
---

Both verses belong to the funeral: the first to the defilement of Hector, which Achilles carries on before the dead man (23.24–26; [[brilliant]]), the second to his instructions after the burning, when the bones must be told apart from those of the horses and men burned with him (23.239–242). The father is named in the soul's own story: “when Menoetius brought me, still a little boy, from Opus” (23.85; [[menoetius]], [[androktasia]]). See [[patroclus]].
