summary: Lemnos is not named in the book, but the silver mixing bowl of the footrace passed through it: Phoenician traders gave it to Thoas, the island's king, and Euneus, Jason's son, gave it to Patroclus as the price of Lycaon (23.744–747).
# Λήμν* does not occur in Book 23: the lines of the bowl refer to the island through its king and its prince.
refs: [23.745-747]
---

“and Phoenician men brought it over the misty sea / and set it out in harbors, and gave it as a gift to Thoas; / and as the price of Lycaon, son of Priam, it was given / to the hero Patroclus by Euneus, son of Jason” (23.744–747). The harbors are not named, but Thoas is the king of Lemnos, “the city of divine Thoas” (14.230; see [[thoas-of-lemnos]]), and Euneus, the son of Jason and the Lemnian Hypsipyle, sends ships of wine from Lemnos to the camp (7.467–469; see [[euneus]] and [[hypsipyle]]). The second verse ends as the line of Book 21 in which Achilles sold Lycaon “into well-built Lemnos, / taking him there in his ships, and the son of Jason paid the price” (ὦνον ἔδωκε, 21.40–41; see the note on Book 21 and [[lycaon-son-of-priam]]).

In the Iliad Lemnos is a place of trade. Its wine is bought in the camp “with bronze, and some with gleaming iron, / some with hides, and some with the cattle themselves, / and some with slaves” (7.473–475; see [[trade]]), Achilles sold his captives there, and Hecuba names it with Samos and Imbros as the islands to which he sold her sons (24.751–753). The bowl that bought Lycaon was a Sidonian treasure, brought by Phoenician traders to the island's king (see [[sidonians]], [[phoenicians]] and [[mixing-bowl]]).
