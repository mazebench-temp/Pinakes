---
summary: Virgil gives Anchises funeral games built on those of this book, and models his unburied dead, who may not cross the river, on the ghost of Patroclus. The funeral of Pallas and the pyres of the truce take details from the funeral of Patroclus, and the chariot race of the Georgics follows Homer's.
---

- The games for Anchises: the ship race around a turning post on a rock, the runner who slips in the blood of the sacrifice, the old boxer and the beaten boaster led away spitting blood, and the archers' dove tied to a mast (*Aeneid* 5.42–603; *Georgics* 3.103–112): [[virgil-the-games-for-anchises]] and [[the-funeral-games-of-patroclus]].
- The unburied who may not cross the river, and Palinurus, who asks for Aeneas' hand as the ghost asks for Achilles' (*Aeneid* 6.295–383, on 23.71–76); Charon's “fresh and green” old age, with which Walter Leaf explains ὠμογέρων (on 23.791): [[virgil-the-unburied]] and [[the-ghost-of-patroclus]].
- The procession for Pallas, with the captives bound for the pyre, the weeping war-horse and Aeneas' farewell to the dead (*Aeneid* 11.29–99): [[virgils-weeping-horse]] and [[human-sacrifice]].
- The pyres of the truce, ridden around three times, with the earth and the arms wet with tears (*Aeneid* 11.182–202; Leaf on 23.15): [[virgil-truce-for-the-dead]].
