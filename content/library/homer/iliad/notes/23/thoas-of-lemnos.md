“and Phoenician men brought it over the misty sea / and set it out in harbors, and gave it as a gift to Thoas” (23.744–745; see [[phoenicians]] and [[mixing-bowl]]). This Thoas is not the Aetolian leader, son of Andraemon (2.638; see [[thoas]]): the bowl goes from him to Euneus, son of Jason, who trades from Lemnos (23.746–747; see [[euneus]]), and Lemnos is “the city of divine Thoas” (14.230; see [[lemnos]]). Homer names him only in these two places.

Homer does not say why the traders gave him the bowl, nor how it passed to Euneus. Walter Leaf suggests that they gave the king a present to buy leave to trade in his country, and calls Thoas the father of Hypsipyle and grandfather of Euneus (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 23.745). The poem itself names Hypsipyle only as Euneus' mother (7.469); her descent from Thoas is told by later writers (see [[hypsipyle]] and [[the-lemnian-women]]). If it is right, the bowl came to Euneus from his grandfather.
