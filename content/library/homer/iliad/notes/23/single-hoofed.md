---
summary: The Myrmidons keep their single-hoofed horses under the yoke to mourn Patroclus (23.7); Achilles' own horses stay out of the race (23.279); and in the race the formula marks the two overtakings, “And the son of Tydeus turned aside and drove his single-hoofed horses past” and “But Antilochus turned aside and drove his single-hoofed horses” (23.398, 23.423), and Menelaus' fear that the horses will collide (23.435).
---

**The race.** The two overtakings are told in the same verse, Τυδεΐδης δὲ παρατρέψας and Ἀντίλοχος δὲ παρατρέψας ἔχε μώνυχας ἵππους (23.398, 23.423): Diomedes passes the fallen Eumelus with Athena's help, Antilochus forces his way past Menelaus in the narrow place (23.388–400, 23.417–437; [[divine-intervention]], [[kerdos]]). At the finish Achilles pities “the best man,” who “is driving in his single-hoofed horses last” (23.536), and Antilochus answers that Achilles has “slave women and single-hoofed horses” enough to give him another prize (23.550).

**The horses of Achilles.** In his speech opening the games Achilles says that he and his horses will stay out of the race, since they mourn their charioteer (23.279–284; [[the-horses-of-achilles]]). See [[horses]] and [[chariot]].
