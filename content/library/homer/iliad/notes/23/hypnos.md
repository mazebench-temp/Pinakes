---
summary: Sleep frames the visit of Patroclus' soul and the morning after the pyre. The Achaeans go “to lie down, each to his own hut” (23.58), and Achilles, lying on the shore, is seized by sleep, “loosening the cares of his heart, / sweet sleep, poured around him” (23.62–63); the soul begins, “You are asleep, and you have forgotten me, Achilles” (23.69). After the night at the pyre he lies down worn out, “and sweet sleep leapt upon him” (23.232), until the leaders gathering around the son of Atreus wake him.
---

The verse 23.62 recurs in the Odyssey, of Odysseus asleep on the night before the slaughter of the suitors (*Odyssey* 20.56), and νήδυμος ἀμφιχυθείς, “sweet … poured around”, is what Sleep says of himself when he lulled Zeus (14.252–253), as Walter Leaf notes (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 23.62–63; see [[sweet-sleep]] and [[sleep]]). The soul comes when sleep has loosened his cares, and its first word, εὕδεις, “You are asleep”, is the first word of the Dream to Agamemnon (2.23; see [[dream-scene]] and [[psyche]]); here it is a reproach that he has forgotten the dead (see [[honoring-the-dead]]). The second sleep is broken by “the din and the thudding” of the leaders as they come (23.234).
