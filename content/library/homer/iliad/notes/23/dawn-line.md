**The day of the pyre.** The first dawn keeps the verse-end of the dawn line and puts the mourners in its first half: μυρομένοισι δὲ τοῖσι φάνη ῥοδοδάκτυλος Ἠώς (23.109; [[rosy-fingered]]). In the Odyssey the same frame, with the dawn held back by Athena, closes the night of Odysseus and Penelope (*Odyssey* 23.241–242).

**The day of the games.** The second has a form found nowhere else in Homer: ἦμος δʼ ἑωσφόρος εἶσι φόως ἐρέων ἐπὶ γαῖαν, “When the morning star goes forth to herald the light over the earth,” followed by the saffron-robed Dawn and by τῆμος, “then,” for the dying fire (23.226–228; [[saffron-robed]]). Walter Leaf (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 23.226) takes ἑωσφόρος for an Attic form, a sign of a late passage, and compares the star of the Odyssey ὅς τε μάλιστα / ἔρχεται ἀγγέλλων φάος ἠοῦς ἠριγενείης (*Odyssey* 13.93–94); on the sea at dawn, see the translation's note on 23.226–227.
