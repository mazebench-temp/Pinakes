summary: Achilles promises Patroclus “twelve / splendid children of the Trojans” for his pyre (23.22–23); for the swift horsemen he sets out “splendid prizes” (23.262); and Antilochus enters the race as “the splendid son of Nestor” (23.302).
# ἀγλάʼ (23.262), the elided neuter plural; it also takes in ἀγλάʼ ἄποινα, "the splendid ransom" of Chryseis (1.111).
grc: [ἀγλάʼ]
---

The children of the Trojans are “splendid” in the vow Achilles made in Book 18 (18.336–337) and in its fulfillment at the pyre (23.175–176; [[human-sacrifice]]). ἀγλαὸς υἱός is the formula of a noble father's son, here Antilochus (23.302; [[high-hearted]]). The elided ἀγλάʼ is new to the entry and also gives it the “splendid ransom” of Book 1 (1.111). See [[achilles]], [[antilochus]] and [[contest-scene]].
