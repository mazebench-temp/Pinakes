---
summary: Two of Achilles' prizes are unbroken animals: a mare “six years old, unbroken, carrying a mule foal in her womb” for the chariot race (23.266), and for the boxing a mule “six years old, unbroken, the hardest to tame” (23.655).
---

ἑξέτεʼ ἀδμήτην opens both verses. Walter Leaf takes an unbroken spirit as a merit, and ἥ τʼ ἀλγίστη δαμάσασθαι as meaning that six years is the hardest age for breaking (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 23.266; see the translation's note on 23.266). The mare is claimed by Antilochus, yielded to Menelaus and given back to Antilochus (23.553, 23.591–613); the mule goes to Epeius (23.664–699; [[hard-working]]).
