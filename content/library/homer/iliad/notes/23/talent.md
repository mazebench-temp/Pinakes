---
summary: Two talents of gold are the fourth prize of the chariot race, which Meriones takes (23.269, 23.614), and half a talent of gold is the last prize of the footrace, to which Achilles adds another half in return for Antilochus' praise (23.751, 23.795–796).
---

**The chariot race.** “and for the fourth he set two talents of gold” (23.269): the gold ranks below the woman and the tripod, the mare and the cauldron, and above only the two-handled bowl (23.262–270; see [[the-funeral-games-of-patroclus]], [[cauldron]] and [[phiale]]). Walter Leaf refers here to the small value of the Homeric talent (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 23.269). Meriones, who came in fourth with the slowest horses, “took up the two talents of gold” (23.614; see [[meriones]]).

**The footrace.** The prizes for speed are the silver mixing bowl, an ox “great and rich with fat”, “and he set half a talent of gold for the last” (23.750–751; see [[mixing-bowl]]). λοισθήϊα, “last prize”, is formed, Leaf notes, like the later πρωτεῖα and δευτερεῖα (on 23.751). Antilochus, last of the three runners, carries it off smiling and praises the speed of Odysseus and of Achilles (23.785–792; see [[antilochus]]), and Achilles answers, “Antilochus, your praise shall not be spoken in vain, / but I will add half a talent of gold for you” (23.795–796). With the second half Antilochus goes home with a whole talent.
