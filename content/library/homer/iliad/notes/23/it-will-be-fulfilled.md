# καὶ μὴν τετελεσμένον ἔσται (23.410, only here in the Iliad; Odyssey 16.440, 19.487).
grc: ["καὶ μὴν τετελεσμένον ἔσται"]
---

In Book 1 the whole verse is Athena's promise to Achilles, and in Book 8 Zeus' threat to Hera and Athena (1.212, 8.401). Antilochus speaks it to his horses as they lag behind Menelaus: there will be no keep for them with Nestor, “but he will kill you at once with the sharp bronze” (23.410–413; [[sharp]]). Epeius speaks it before the boxing: “I will tear his flesh right through and crush his bones” (23.672–673). His blow ends the fight at once, and Euryalus is led out of the gathering “spitting thick blood, his head lolling to one side” (23.689–697). καὶ μὴν τετελεσμένον ἔσται is new to the entry.
