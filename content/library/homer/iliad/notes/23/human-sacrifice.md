summary: Over the body Achilles repeats the vow of Book 18 to cut the throats of twelve young Trojans before the pyre (23.22–23). At the pyre he kills them with the bronze, “and he devised evil deeds in his mind”, and tells Patroclus that the fire is eating them with him; they burn at the pyre's edge with the horses (23.175–182, 23.241–242).

**The vow repeated.** Achilles lays his hands on the breast of the dead man and tells him that he is fulfilling all he promised, to give Hector to the dogs “and before your pyre to cut the throats of twelve / splendid children of the Trojans, in my anger over your killing” (23.20–23). The last two verses are the vow of Book 18, with the future “I will cut” turned into an infinitive (18.336–337; see the note on Book 18). Walter Leaf reports that Payne Knight and others thought 23.21–23 an interpolation, made up from the vow of Book 18 and a verse of the Odyssey, and calls it “quite possible” (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 23.21; see the translation's note on 23.21–23). The twelve were taken alive in the river for this purpose (21.26–32; see the note on Book 21).

**The deed.** On the pyre Achilles throws four horses and two of the nine table dogs, whose throats he cuts, δειροτομήσας, with the verb of the vow, and then the twelve, killed with the bronze (23.171–176; see [[dogs]] and [[cremation]]):

Leaf denies that κακὰ … ἔργα passes a moral judgment on Achilles: the work was ill for its victims, as Zeus “devised evil” all night without condemnation (on 23.176; 7.478; see the note on Book 21). In the verses that follow Achilles tells the dead man that the fire is eating the twelve with him, “but Hector, / the son of Priam, I will not give to the fire to devour, but to the dogs” (23.179–183; see [[achilles]] and [[the-dragging-of-hector]]). In the morning Patroclus' bones lie apart in the middle of the pyre, “and the others apart / were burned at its edge, horses and men all mingled together” (23.241–242).

**Plato.** Plato's Socrates names the slaughter of the captives at the pyre among the things he will not have said of Achilles (*Republic* 3.391b; see [[plato]] and [[plato-achilles-and-the-river]]).
