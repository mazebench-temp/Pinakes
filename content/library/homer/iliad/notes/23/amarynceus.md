# Ἀμαρυγκέα (23.630) occurs only here; the patronymic forms (2.622, 4.517) are claimed by the entry and the
# Book 4 note. Unnamed: the king (βασιλῆος, 23.631).
grc: [Ἀμαρυγκέα]
refs: [23.631]

ὡς ὁπότε κρείοντʼ Ἀμαρυγκέα θάπτον Ἐπειοὶ / Βουπρασίῳ, παῖδες δʼ ἔθεσαν βασιλῆος ἄεθλα, “as when the Epeians were burying powerful Amarynceus / at Buprasium, and his sons set out prizes in honor of the king!” (23.630–631; see [[epeians]] and [[buprasium]]). The Iliad's other mentions of him are patronymics of his son Diores, an Epeian leader in the Catalogue who is killed in the first battle (2.622, 4.517; see [[diores]]); only here is he named for himself, and only at his funeral. κρείων, “powerful”, is the title that the poem gives most often to Agamemnon (κρείων Ἀγαμέμνων), and βασιλεύς, “king”, names his rank (see [[basileus]]).

His sons set out the prizes, as the sons of Amphidamas did at Chalcis when Hesiod won a tripod there with a song (*Works and Days* 654–657); Leaf compares the two verses for the genitive βασιλῆος, prizes “of” the king (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 23.631). The contests themselves, and what Nestor makes of them, are told under [[the-funeral-games-of-amarynceus]].
