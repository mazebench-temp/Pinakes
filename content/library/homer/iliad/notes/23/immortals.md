---
summary: Iris goes back to the Ethiopians, “where they are offering hecatombs / to the immortals” (23.206–207); Achilles' horses “are immortal” (23.277); Antilochus says that Eumelus should have prayed “to the immortals” (23.546–547); and, beaten in the footrace by Odysseus, he says “the immortals honor older men” (23.788).
---

The last two are Antilochus' words, and both explain a result of the games by the gods: Eumelus' fall, which Athena brought about (23.391–397), and the victory of Odysseus, whom Athena helped (23.770–783; [[divine-intervention]]). The horses are immortal because Poseidon gave them to Peleus (23.277–278; [[the-horses-of-achilles]]). See [[gods-and-mortals]] and [[ethiopians]].
