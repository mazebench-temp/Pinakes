---
summary: Meriones directs the woodcutters on Ida as “the attendant of manly Idomeneus” (23.113, 23.124).
---

The whole verse, Μηριόνης θεράπων ἀγαπήνορος Ἰδομενῆος, is said twice, before and after the felling of the oaks (23.113, 23.124), and these are the last two of the epithet's five uses in the Iliad (see the entry). Walter Leaf reports that Fick rejected both pairs of verses, which bring in Meriones and Idomeneus (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 23.112; see the translation's note on 23.112). In the games Meriones is “the good attendant of Idomeneus” (23.528, 23.860, 23.888; [[brave]]). See [[meriones]] and [[idomeneus]].
