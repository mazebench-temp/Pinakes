# κλῦθι θεά (23.770, only here).
grc: ["κλῦθι θεά"]
---

The answer is the couplet of Diomedes' prayer in Book 5, word for word: Athena “made his limbs light, his feet and his hands above” (23.771–772 = 5.121–122). Aristarchus marked the second verse here, since if Athena had made Odysseus' limbs light he would have won without Ajax's fall, and Walter Leaf (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 23.772) agrees (see the translation's note on 23.772). The goddess helps twice over: she makes Ajax slip in the dung of the slaughtered oxen (23.773–777), and Ajax complains that she “stands by Odysseus like a mother and helps him” (23.782–783; [[o-popoi]], [[divine-intervention]]). κλῦθι θεά is new to the entry.
