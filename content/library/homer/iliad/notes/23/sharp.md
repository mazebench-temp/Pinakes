---
summary: Antilochus threatens his horses that Nestor “will kill you at once with the sharp bronze” (23.412); and the son of Oileus tells Idomeneus that the eyes in his head are not “the sharpest” (23.477).
---

ὀξέϊ χαλκῷ, the bronze of killing in battle, is here a young man's threat to his own horses (23.410–413; [[it-will-be-fulfilled]], [[shepherd-of-the-people]]). The superlative ὀξύτατον, of sight, is a taunt to an older man in the dispute over who is leading the race (23.474–481; [[high-stepping]]). See [[antilochus]], [[idomeneus]] and [[ajax-son-of-oileus]].
