---
summary: Agamemnon is “son of Atreus” in the verses that open Achilles' speeches to the leaders, “Son of Atreus, and you others” (23.236, 23.272, 23.658), and at the javelin, where Achilles gives him the prize without a throw (23.887, 23.890); Menelaus is the son of Atreus who rises for the chariot race and is outdriven by Antilochus (23.293, 23.355, 23.401, 23.407, 23.425, 23.434).
---

**Agamemnon.** Achilles addresses him with the patronymic when he asks him to dismiss the army from the pyre (23.156), and the leaders gather around “the son of Atreus” when Achilles wakes after the night of burning (23.233). Achilles' instructions for the bones begin “Son of Atreus, and you others, the best of all the Achaeans” (23.236), the verse with which Nestor proposed the burning of the dead in Book 7 (7.327–336); the games open with “Son of Atreus, and you other well-greaved Achaeans,” for the chariot race and again for the boxing (23.272, 23.658; [[well-greaved]]). Idomeneus would make “Agamemnon, son of Atreus, the arbiter” of his wager with the son of Oileus (23.486). At the javelin “the son of Atreus, wide-ruling Agamemnon” rises, and Achilles gives him the prize before a spear is thrown, “Son of Atreus, for we know how far you are ahead of all” (23.887–894; [[wide-ruling]]).

**Menelaus.** In the chariot race the patronymic is his: “And after him rose the son of Atreus, fair-haired Menelaus” (23.293); he draws his place and drives in the same words (23.355, 23.401; [[fair-haired]], [[famed-for-his-spear]]). Antilochus urges his horses to catch “the horses of the son of Atreus” (23.407), and at the narrow place “the son of Atreus was afraid” and held back his mares (23.425–434).
