summary: The soul of Patroclus remembers “our dear companions” (23.77), Achilles reaches for it “with his own hands” (23.99), and Achilles twice speaks of “my dear native land”, once for his father's vow and once for his own homecoming, which he gives up (23.145, 23.150). In the games φίλος is the word of address: Nestor calls his son and Achilles “my friend” (23.313, 23.627), and Idomeneus and Antilochus speak to the Argives as “friends” (23.457, 23.787).
grc: [φίλῃσιν]
en: [his own hands]
---

φίλος is “one's own” as well as “dear”: χερσὶ φίλῃσιν, “with his own hands”, Achilles reaches for the soul and takes nothing (23.99–100; see [[psyche]]). The native land is “dear” in both of Achilles' verses about it, the father's vow and the son's refusal (23.145, 23.150; see [[nostos]] and [[spercheius]]).

Not every “dear” of the translation is φίλος. ἠθείη κεφαλή, “dear heart” (23.94), is an address that the scholia call a younger man's to an elder, and Patroclus was older than Achilles (11.787; see the translation's note on 23.94). The leaders “to whom most of all / the corpse is dear” are those for whom it is κήδεος, a grief of the family, and they are κηδεμόνες, “those who cared for the dead” (23.159–163; see the translation's note on 23.157–163). When Nestor accepts his prize he is glad that Achilles remembers him ἐνηέος, “a gentle friend” (23.648), the word of Patroclus' gentleness (23.252, 17.670; see [[hetairos]]).
