summary: The book opens with the Trojans groaning in the city over Hector (23.1); after that Ilios is seen from the camp, as the city toward which Achilles chased Hector, the wall beneath which the soul of Patroclus says he is fated to die, and the land to which the winds come to kindle the pyre (23.64, 23.80–81, 23.215).
# Ἴλιον (23.64, 23.297) and Τροίην (23.215) are caught by the forms of the entry. Unnamed: the city (πτόλιν) in 23.1.
refs: [23.1]
---

**The city mourns.** “So they groaned throughout the city” (23.1). The verse takes up the end of Book 22, where the people “were seized by wailing and groaning throughout the city” over Hector (22.408–409) and the women groaned with Andromache as she lamented (22.515; see the note on Book 22, [[lament]] and [[hector]]). The poem then leaves Ilios until the last book, and the Achaeans go back to “the ships and the Hellespont” (23.2; see [[hellespont]]).

**Seen from the camp.** Achilles' limbs are weary “from chasing Hector toward windy Ilios” (23.64); Walter Leaf takes προτί with its full force, assailing Hector even up to Ilios (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 23.64; see [[windy]] and [[the-pursuit-of-hector]]). In his sleep the soul of Patroclus tells him that it is his fate too, “like the gods, / to die beneath the wall of the wealthy Trojans” (23.80–81; see [[the-walls-of-troy]], [[achilles]] and [[death-of-achilles-foretold]]). The two winds that Iris summons come over the sea, “and they came to deep-soiled Troy, / and fell on the pyre” (23.215–216; see [[boreas]] and [[zephyrus]]), as Thetis and the Nereids came “to deep-soiled Troy” to mourn with Achilles (18.67). Τροίη is here the land of Troy, and ἐρίβωλος, “deep-soiled”, is an epithet of lands, given also to Achilles' Phthia (9.363; see [[deep-soiled]] and [[troad]]).

**In the games.** Echepolus of Sicyon gave Agamemnon his mare Aethe “so that he might not have to follow him to windy Ilios” (23.297; see [[sicyon]]). Nestor tells his son that no one could catch him at the post, not even with Arion, “or the horses of Laomedon, the good ones that were reared here” (23.348; see [[laomedon]]); Leaf thinks the line an interpolation, its second half modeled on 21.279 (on 23.348). And from his lookout Idomeneus searches for the leading mares “across the Trojan plain” (23.464; πεδίον τὸ Τρωϊκόν also at 10.11; see [[idomeneus]]).
