---
summary: Achilles' threat to give Hector to the dogs is not carried out, “for Aphrodite, daughter of Zeus, kept the dogs from him / by day and by night” (23.185–186).
---

Διὸς θυγάτηρ Ἀφροδίτη, her formula from Book 3 on, here names a goddess who protects the dead: she anoints the body with “rose-sweet oil, / ambrosial,” so that it should not be torn as Achilles drags it (23.186–187; [[rose-sweet]], [[ambrosial]]). Walter Leaf reports that Fäsi and Düntzer rejected 184–191, and the defense of the lines by von Christ (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 23.184; see the translation's note on 23.184–191).
