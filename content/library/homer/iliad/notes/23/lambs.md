---
summary: In the archery the lambs are a vow: Teucer shoots without promising Apollo “a glorious hecatomb of firstborn lambs” and misses the dove, and Meriones promises it and hits her (23.862–876). The verse is the one in which Pandarus vowed the same hecatomb before his treacherous shot in Book 4.
---

Teucer “shot an arrow / with force, but did not promise the lord / that he would offer a glorious hecatomb of firstborn lambs” (23.862–864), and Apollo begrudges him the bird; Meriones snatches the bow, “And at once he promised far-shooting Apollo / that he would offer a glorious hecatomb of firstborn lambs” (23.872–873), and strikes her high under the clouds (see [[doves]], [[apollo]] and [[hecatomb]]). Walter Leaf takes the hecatomb of lambs to be regarded as Apollo's fixed price for a successful shot (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 23.864). The verse is the vow that Athena, in the likeness of Laodocus, told Pandarus to make to Apollo the Lycian-born, famed for his bow, “that you will offer a glorious hecatomb of firstborn lambs / when you come home to the city of sacred Zeleia”, and that he made before he shot Menelaus and broke the truce (4.101–103, 4.119–121; see [[pandarus]]). The archer's vow is the same in war and in the games. Pandarus' arrow wounded Menelaus but did not kill him, and Pandarus never came home to pay his vow: Diomedes killed him in the battle that followed (4.134–140, 5.290–296; see [[sacrifice]]).
