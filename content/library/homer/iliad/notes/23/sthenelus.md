# Unnamed: the prizes he gives away (23.512-513).
refs: [23.512-513]
---

“Nor did he dally, / mighty Sthenelus, but quickly took the prize, / and gave the woman to his high-hearted companions to lead away / and the tripod with handles to carry” (23.510–513; see [[diomedes]] and [[tripod]]). Diomedes drives his own chariot in the race, and his companion and charioteer of Book 5 waits at the finish to take what he has won. Walter Leaf notes the active ἄγειν, “to lead away”, for a prize taken for another; when the woman was first set out as a prize, the verb was the middle, ἄγεσθαι, of the winner leading her away for himself (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 23.512; 23.263).

The horses that win the race are the horses of Tros that Sthenelus drove out of the Trojan lines for Diomedes in Book 5 (5.319–327, 23.290–292; see [[ganymede-and-the-horses-of-tros]]). In that book Athena pushed him down from the chariot to drive it herself against Ares (5.835–837), and here too it is Athena who wins the race for Diomedes (23.388–400; see [[athena]]). ἴφθιμος, “mighty”, is his epithet in the race as when he took charge of Nestor's horses with Eurymedon (8.113–114).
