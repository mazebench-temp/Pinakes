---
summary: The son of Oileus slips in the footrace “where the dung was spread from the loud-bellowing oxen that were slain, / which swift-footed Achilles killed in honor of Patroclus” (23.775–776).
---

This is the second and last use of the epithet in the Iliad; the first is the oxen treading barley in the simile of Achilles' chariot (20.495–497). The oxen of the funeral feast (23.30) return as the dung of the footrace, and the son of Oileus rises from it to take an ox as his prize (23.777–781; [[of-the-fields]], [[ajax-son-of-oileus]]).
