---
summary: The Myrmidons weep around the body of Patroclus as they drive their horses around it: “such a master of rout did they long for” (23.16).
---

μήστωρα φόβοιο is elsewhere a phrase of fear, κρατερὸν μήστωρα φόβοιο, said of Diomedes by the Trojans and of Hector by the Achaeans (6.97, 6.278, 12.39). Here, without κρατερόν, it is a phrase of longing, and the warrior it names is dead (23.13–16; [[lament]]). See [[patroclus]] and [[myrmidons]].
