---
summary: Achilles is “great-hearted” as he covers the body with fat (23.168); the twelve victims are “brave sons of the great-hearted Trojans” (23.175, 23.181); Antilochus is twice “son of great-hearted Nestor” (23.541, 23.596); and the epithet goes to the Aetolians of Nestor's youth and to Epeius after his blow (23.633, 23.694).
---

**The pyre.** μεγάθυμος Ἀχιλλεύς lays the fat over the body “from head to foot” (23.168–169). A few verses later the twelve Trojans die at the pyre, and the same verse, “twelve brave sons of the great-hearted Trojans,” is spoken again by Achilles as he tells Patroclus that the fire is eating them (23.175, 23.181; [[brave]], [[human-sacrifice]]).

**The games.** “The son of great-hearted Nestor” is Antilochus as he rises to claim the second prize and as he hands the mare to Menelaus (23.541, 23.596). Epeius is “great-hearted” in the moment after his knock-out blow, when he takes Euryalus in his hands and sets him upright (23.694–695). See [[achilles]], [[antilochus]] and [[epeius]].
