summary: Athena speeds after Diomedes, “the shepherd of the people,” to give him back his whip (23.389); and Antilochus warns his horses that there will be no keep for them “with Nestor, shepherd of the people,” if they lose (23.411).
---

The title of kings is given to the two men of the chariot race who have gods and a father behind them: Diomedes, whom Athena helps against Apollo (23.383–400; [[divine-intervention]]), and Nestor, whose son threatens his horses that the old king will kill them “with the sharp bronze” (23.411–413; [[it-will-be-fulfilled]], [[sharp]]). See [[diomedes]] and [[nestor]].
