summary: Ajax, son of Oileus, is “swift Ajax” as he rebukes Idomeneus, rises to answer him, and rises for the footrace, which he loses by slipping in the dung (23.473, 23.488, 23.754); the word is also said of the horsemen who gather for the race (23.287), of the horse Arion (23.347), of Eumelus' horses (23.545), and of the feet of the footrace's winner (23.749).
# ταχέες δʼ ἱππῆες (23.287) and ποσσὶ κραιπνοῖσι (23.749): each only here.
grc: ["ταχέες δʼ ἱππῆες", "ποσσὶ κραιπνοῖσι"]

**Swift Ajax.** Ὀϊλῆος ταχὺς Αἴας, his formula from the Catalogue on (2.527), comes three times; the first two bring him into the quarrel at the lookout (23.473–498; [[ajax-son-of-oileus]]). In the footrace the epithet meets its test: he leads until the last stretch, when Athena makes him slip, and he comes in second (23.758–779; [[divine-intervention]]).

**Swift horses and feet.** Nestor tells Antilochus that if he passes the others at the post, no one could catch him, not even with Arion, “the swift horse of Adrastus” (23.344–347; [[arion-the-horse]]). Antilochus pleads that Eumelus' “chariot and swift horses came to grief” (23.545). The prize for speed goes to whoever “should prove the nimblest with his swift feet” (23.749), the phrase of Paris and of Achilles running (6.505, 22.138), with ποσσί in place of ποσί.
