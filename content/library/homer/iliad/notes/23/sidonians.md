summary: The silver mixing bowl that Achilles sets out for the footrace is Sidonian work, the most beautiful on earth, though Phoenician men carried it over the sea (23.741–744).
# Σιδόνες (23.743) is found only here; Σιδονίων (6.290) is claimed in the entry.
grc: [Σιδόνες]
---

“a mixing bowl of silver, well wrought; it held six measures, / and in beauty it surpassed all others on the whole earth / by far, since Sidonians, skilled in intricate work, had worked it finely, / and Phoenician men brought it over the misty sea” (23.741–744; see [[mixing-bowl]] and [[phoenicians]]). πολυδαίδαλος, “of much cunning work”, is elsewhere said of things, a corselet, a shield, a chair (3.358, 11.32, 24.597; see [[intricately-worked]]); only here is it said of the craftsmen. Walter Leaf notes that Homer always keeps the Sidonians as craftsmen apart from the Phoenicians as traders (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 23.743): the robes in Hecuba's chamber were “the work of women / of the Sidonians” (6.289–290), and in the Odyssey the silver mixing bowl that Menelaus gives Telemachus came from Phaedimus, king of the Sidonians (*Odyssey* 4.615–619, 15.115–119). Its verse begins with the same words as this one, κρητῆρα τετυγμένον, “a mixing bowl, well wrought” (Leaf on 23.741). Leaf also remarks on the short ι of Σιδόνες against the long ι of Σιδών in the Odyssey (*Odyssey* 15.425; on 23.743).

From the Sidonian makers the bowl passed to Phoenician traders, to Thoas of Lemnos, to Euneus, and to Patroclus as the price of Lycaon (23.744–747; see [[thoas-of-lemnos]], [[euneus]] and [[lycaon-son-of-priam]]); Odysseus wins it in the footrace (23.778; see [[odysseus]]).
