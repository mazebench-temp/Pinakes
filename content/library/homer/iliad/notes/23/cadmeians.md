---
summary: At the funeral games of Oedipus, Mecisteus, the father of Euryalus, “defeated all the Cadmeians” (23.680).
---

ἔνθα δὲ πάντας ἐνίκα Καδμείωνας, “and there he defeated all the Cadmeians” (23.680; see [[mecisteus]] and [[oedipus]]). Καδμεΐωνες is the form used of the many Cadmeians among whom Tydeus came alone (πολέας … Καδμεΐωνας, 4.385; 5.804), and the story is of the same kind. Tydeus, alone in Thebes, challenged the Cadmeians to contests “and won them all” (4.389, 5.807; see [[tydeus-at-thebes]]); Mecisteus, a stranger at the funeral of Oedipus, beat all of them. The Cadmeians are the Thebans of the generation before Troy, beaten in contests by the Argive fathers of the men who fight at Troy, Tydeus the father of Diomedes and Mecisteus the father of Euryalus (see [[euryalus]] and [[diomedes]]).
