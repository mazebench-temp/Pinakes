---
summary: Ajax is “great Telamonian Ajax” as he rises to wrestle Odysseus and speaks to him (23.708, 23.722), as he rises for the armed duel (23.811), and as he throws the lump of iron beyond the marks of all (23.842); and “Ajax, son of Telamon” rises for the weight throw (23.838).
---

μέγας Τελαμώνιος Αἴας fills the end of the verse of rising, “So he spoke, and then great Telamonian Ajax rose” (23.708, 23.811; [[contest-scene]]), and in the games the patronymic keeps him apart from the other Ajax, the son of Oileus, who quarrels with Idomeneus and runs in the footrace (23.473, 23.754; [[ajax-son-of-oileus]], [[swift]]). He wins no contest outright: the wrestling and the duel end with equal prizes (23.735–737, 23.822–823), and Polypoetes outthrows him with the iron (23.842–847). See [[ajax]].
