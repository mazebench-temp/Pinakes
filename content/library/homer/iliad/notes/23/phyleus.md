# Φυλῆά (23.637) occurs only here.
grc: [Φυλῆά]
---

δουρὶ δʼ ὑπειρέβαλον Φυλῆά τε καὶ Πολύδωρον, “and with the spear I outthrew Phyleus and Polydorus” (23.637; see [[the-funeral-games-of-amarynceus]], [[nestor]] and [[polydorus-at-buprasium]]). The spear is the last of Nestor's four victories. At Patroclus' games it is the one contest that is never held: Agamemnon is given its prize without a throw (23.884–897; see [[agamemnon]]).

The poem does not say which Phyleus this is. Walter Leaf has no doubt that he is the Phyleus of the Catalogue, the father of Meges, who leads the men of Dulichium (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 23.637; 2.627–629; see [[meges]]). That Phyleus once went off to Dulichium “angry with his father” (2.629), and later tradition made the father Augeas of Elis (see [[phyleus-and-augeas]] and [[augeas]]); the Phyleus of the games would then be a prince of the Epeians, competing at the funeral of an Epeian king. Pausanias says that Augeas banished Phyleus, the elder of his two sons, for objecting that he was wronging Heracles, his benefactor (*Description of Greece* 5.1.10; see [[pausanias]]).
