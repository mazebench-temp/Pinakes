# λευκὸν ἔτʼ αὔτως (23.268), δύο λευκὼ (23.329) and λευκὸν σῆμα (23.455): each only here.
grc: ["λευκὸν ἔτʼ αὔτως", "δύο λευκὼ", "λευκὸν σῆμα"]
---

ὀστέα λευκά are the bones a spear splits in battle (16.347); here they are gathered from the pyre, as Hector's will be at the end of the poem (24.793; [[cremation]]). The cauldron is white “as it was,” in its natural brightness, as Walter Leaf takes αὔτως (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 23.268; [[untouched-by-fire]]). The white stones and the white mark are signs to be seen from afar: Nestor's sign of the turning post, “very easy to see” (23.326–330), and the blaze by which Idomeneus knows the horse (23.453–455; [[like-the-moon]], [[preeminent]]). The three phrases are new to the entry. See [[horses]].
