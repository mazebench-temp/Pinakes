---
summary: The soul of Patroclus asks that their bones lie together in “the golden two-handled jar that your lady mother gave you” (23.92).
---

The lady mother is Thetis. The T scholia, quoted by Walter Leaf (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 23.92), report that the verse was athetized as taken from the second descent to Hades in the Odyssey, where Thetis gives the golden jar for Achilles' bones after his death (*Odyssey* 24.73–75; see the translation's note on 23.91–92). Here she would have given it to him as he left for the war, which Leaf calls “an act of incredible ill-omen”; he holds the athetesis “unquestionably right.” See [[thetis]] and [[the-golden-urn]].
