---
summary: Achilles bids the two best men put on their armor “and take the flesh-cutting bronze / and make trial of each other in front of the throng” (23.803–804).
---

ταμεσίχροα χαλκόν is the bronze that the Achaeans' flesh is not stone or iron to withstand, as Apollo says in Book 4 (4.510–511). Here the duel is a contest, and the winner is whoever first reaches “the other's fair flesh” through the armor (23.805–806); it is stopped before that happens (23.822–823).
