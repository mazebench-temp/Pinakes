summary: The helmet of Sarpedon is among the prizes of the armed duel (23.799–800), and before the archery the lots are shaken in a bronze-fitted helmet (23.861).
# τρυφάλειαν (23.799) and κυνέῃ (23.861) are claimed by the entry and its note on Book 13.
---

Achilles sets out for the armed duel a spear, “and a shield and a helmet, / the armor of Sarpedon, which Patroclus had taken from him” (23.799–800; see [[sarpedon]], [[spear]] and [[shield]]); the helmet is a τρυφάλεια.

Before the archery Teucer and Meriones “took the lots and shook them in a bronze-fitted helmet, / and Teucer drew the first lot” (23.861–862; see [[lots]], [[teucer]] and [[meriones]]). The verse is that of the lots shaken before the duel of Paris and Menelaus (3.316; Walter Leaf, *Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 23.861), and in Book 7 the lots of the champions who would face Hector were thrown into the helmet of Agamemnon (7.175–176). Leaf notes that the lot was needed here, since if the first archer hit the bird the second would have no chance (on 23.861).
