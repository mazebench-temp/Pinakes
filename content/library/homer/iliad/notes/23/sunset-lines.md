---
summary: The sun would have set on the mourners around Patroclus “had not Achilles quickly stood beside Agamemnon and said” (23.154–155).
---

The sunset verse is joined to the narrator's “would have … had not” ([[would-have-had-not]]): καί νύ κʼ ὀδυρομένοισιν ἔδυ φάος ἠελίοιο, “And now the light of the sun would have set on them as they mourned” (23.154). Achilles' words send the army to supper while those closest to the dead build the pyre (23.155–163). The whole verse returns twice in the Odyssey at reunions in tears, of Odysseus with Telemachus and with his two herdsmen, each time broken off by a speech (*Odyssey* 16.220–221, 21.226–227), and once more with the dawn in place of the sunset, when Athena holds back the dawn from Odysseus and Penelope (*Odyssey* 23.241–242; Walter Leaf, *Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 23.154; [[dawn-line]]).
