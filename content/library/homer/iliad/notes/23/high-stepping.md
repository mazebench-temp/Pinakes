---
summary: The son of Oileus tells Idomeneus that the leading mares are the same as before, “the high-stepping mares, speeding over the wide plain” (23.474–475).
---

ἵπποι ἀερσίποδες is the phrase of the horses that stood by the seated ranks before the duel of Book 3 (3.326–327). The son of Oileus is wrong: the mares of Eumelus have come to grief, and the leader is Diomedes, as Idomeneus guessed (23.391–400, 23.456–472; [[ajax-son-of-oileus]]). See [[horses]].
