summary: Achilles, pitying Eumelus, “standing among the Argives … spoke winged words” (23.535), and answers Antilochus' protest in the same formula (23.557); Menelaus, his heart warmed, and Nestor, receiving his prize, both speak “addressing him with winged words” (23.601, 23.625).
---

ἔπεα πτερόεντʼ ἀγόρευε with στάς is the verse of Achilles after Hector's death, who “stood among the Achaeans and spoke winged words” (22.377), with the Argives in place of the Achaeans (23.535). All four speeches that the formula opens in this book concern the prizes of the chariot race: Achilles' proposal of the second prize for Eumelus (23.536–538), his offer of a corselet instead (23.558–562), Menelaus' gift of the mare to Antilochus (23.602–611), and Nestor's thanks for the bowl that Achilles gives him “to be a memorial of the funeral of Patroclus” (23.619, 23.626–650).
