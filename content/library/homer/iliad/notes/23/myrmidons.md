---
summary: Achilles keeps the Myrmidons by the body of Patroclus when the rest of the army scatters: they drive three times around it in their chariots, weeping, and feast beside it; they lie with Achilles on the shore that night; and in the morning they arm, carry the body to the pyre and cover it with their hair (23.4–34, 23.60, 23.128–137).
---

**The mourners.** When the Achaeans scatter to their ships, Achilles will not let the Myrmidons go, and speaks to “his war-loving companions”: “Myrmidons of the swift horses, my trusty companions, / let us not yet loose the single-hoofed horses from under the chariots” (23.4–7; see [[war-loving]], [[of-the-swift-horses]] and [[trusty]]). ταχύπωλοι, “of the swift horses”, is said of the Myrmidons only here; it is otherwise an epithet of the Danaans (8.161). They are to weep for Patroclus with their horses and chariots, “for that is the prize of the dead” (23.9; see [[the-prize-of-the-dead]]). They drive three times around the body, and their armor and the sands are wet with tears (23.12–16; see [[lament]] and [[patroclus]]). Walter Leaf reports that Heyne suspected the verse in which Thetis rouses in them the longing for lament, as a needless addition, since Thetis is not among them (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 23.14; see [[thetis]]). Then they unarm and sit down, μυρίοι, “countless”, to the funeral feast Achilles gives them (23.26–29); Leaf notes that the number of the Myrmidons in Book 16 is 2,500, fifty ships of fifty men (on 23.29; 16.168–170).

**On the shore.** That night they lie on the shore around Achilles, groaning, while the rest go to their huts (23.58–61), and it is to them that he speaks of the ghost, rousing the longing for lament until dawn (23.103–110; see [[the-ghost-of-patroclus]]).

**The procession.** At dawn Achilles orders “the war-loving Myrmidons / to gird on their bronze, and each to yoke beneath the chariot / his horses” (23.129–131). They go out with the fighters and charioteers in front and a cloud of foot soldiers behind, “countless”, and the companions carry Patroclus in the middle, cutting their hair and casting it on the body (23.131–137; see [[hair]] and [[the-funeral-of-patroclus]]). The Myrmidons are not named again in the book; at the games Achilles' companions are Automedon and Antilochus, his “dear companion” (23.556, 23.563; see [[automedon]] and [[hetairos]]).
