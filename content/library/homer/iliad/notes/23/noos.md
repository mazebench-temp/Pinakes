---
summary: Antilochus excuses himself to Menelaus with a young man's mind, “for his mind is quicker, and his counsel thin” (23.590), and Menelaus forgives him: “this time youth has overcome your mind” (23.604). Idomeneus tells the son of Oileus that “your mind is harsh” (23.484), and the Spercheius did not fulfill “what he had in mind”, Peleus' prayer (23.149).
---

νόος is twice a young man's in the quarrel over the second prize. Antilochus pleads his youth: “You know what the transgressions of a young man are like; / for his mind is quicker, and his counsel thin” (23.589–590), and Menelaus gives way: “before; but this time youth has overcome your mind” (23.604; see [[metis]] and [[amends-and-reconciliation]]). Idomeneus' reproach of the son of Oileus is the opposite charge, a mind that will not bend: “you fall short of the Argives, since your mind is harsh” (23.484).

The verb νοέω marks Achilles' turns of thought at the funeral: twice “brilliant swift-footed Achilles thought of another thing” (ἄλλʼ ἐνόησε, 23.140, 23.193), the gift of his hair and the prayer to the winds. Antilochus uses it of his plan to pass Menelaus: “this I myself will contrive and devise” (23.415; see [[kerdos]]).
