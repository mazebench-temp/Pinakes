# Τυδέος υἷϊ (23.383) and Τυδέος ἱπποδάμου υἱὸς (23.472): each only here.
grc: ["Τυδέος υἷϊ", "Τυδέος ἱπποδάμου υἱὸς"]
---

**The chariot race.** Apollo's grudge falls on “the son of Tydeus” (23.383), and Athena gives him back his whip (23.388–390). Idomeneus, guessing at the leader, spells out the patronymic in full with his Aetolian lineage: “the son of Tydeus, tamer of horses, mighty Diomedes” (23.471–472; [[tamer-of-horses]], [[krateros]]). Antilochus will not race against “the horses of the wise-hearted son of Tydeus,” to which Athena has given speed (23.405–406; [[wise-hearted]]). Achilles, proposing the second prize for Eumelus, leaves the first to “the son of Tydeus” (23.538); Sthenelus has already taken it (23.510–513).

**The boxing and the duel.** “The son of Tydeus, famed for his spear,” girds and encourages his kinsman Euryalus (23.681–684; [[famed-for-his-spear]]). In the duel “the son of Tydeus then, over the great shield, / kept reaching his neck with the point of his shining spear” (23.820–821), and Achilles gives him the great sword (23.824–825). The two new forms take only these lines. See [[diomedes]] and [[tydeus]].
