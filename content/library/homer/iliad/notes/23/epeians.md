---
summary: In Nestor's memory of his youth the Epeians bury their king Amarynceus at Buprasium with funeral games, and none of them is a match for the young Nestor (23.630–633).
---

ὡς ὁπότε κρείοντʼ Ἀμαρυγκέα θάπτον Ἐπειοὶ / Βουπρασίῳ, “as when the Epeians were burying powerful Amarynceus / at Buprasium” (23.630–631; see [[amarynceus]], [[buprasium]] and [[the-funeral-games-of-amarynceus]]). In the war of Nestor's youth they were the enemies of Pylos (11.670–761; see [[nestor-and-the-epeians]]); here they hold games for their dead king, and the Pylians and the Aetolians compete beside them. “There no man was a match for me, neither of the Epeians / nor of the Pylians themselves nor of the great-hearted Aetolians” (23.632–633; see [[pylians]] and [[aetolians]]). The Epeians are named first, as the hosts, and Nestor's own people come second, with αὐτῶν, “themselves”.

The Epeian houses of Nestor's story are those of the Catalogue: Amarynceus is the father of Diores, and the twin sons of Actor, who beat Nestor in the chariot race, are the fathers of Amphimachus and Thalpius, three of the four Epeian leaders at Troy (2.620–622; see [[diores]], [[the-moliones]], [[amphimachus]] and [[thalpius]]).

The boxer Epeius, son of Panopeus, who wins the next contest of Patroclus' games, has the people's name in the singular (Ἐπειός, 23.665), but Homer does not connect him with them (see [[epeius]]).
