**The vow.** After Book 16 the river comes back into the poem only at the funeral of Patroclus. Achilles stands apart from the pyre, cuts off the hair “which he had grown thick and long for the river Spercheius” (23.142), and speaks to the river, looking out over the sea (23.140–143; see [[hair]] and [[achilles]]). ἄλλως, “in vain”, opens the prayer (Walter Leaf, *Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 23.144). Peleus vowed the hair for the day his son came home, with a holy hecatomb and fifty uncut rams, ἔνορχα, sacrificed “into your waters, where you have a precinct and a fragrant altar” (23.146–148; see [[peleus]], [[hecatomb]], [[altar]] and [[fragrant]]). Leaf takes πηγάς here as “waters”, not “sources”, since Phthia lies on the lower course of the river; ἱερεύσειν ἐς πηγάς may mean that the victims were dropped into the water, as the Trojans throw horses into the Scamander, or only that their blood ran into it (on 23.147, 23.148; 21.132; see the translation's note on 23.141–151). “So the old man prayed, but you did not fulfill what he had in mind” (23.149). Then the gift: since Achilles is not going home, he would grant his hair to the hero Patroclus to take with him, and he puts it in his companion's hands (23.150–153; see [[patroclus]] and [[nostos]]).

**A river with a cult.** A river with a precinct and an altar is a god with a cult, like the Scamander, which has a priest and receives bulls and horses (5.77–78, 21.130–132; see [[scamander]], [[rivers]] and [[priesthood]]). In Book 16 the Spercheius was the divine father of Menesthius, one of Achilles' captains (16.173–176; see [[menesthius-son-of-spercheius]]); here the vow makes it the god of Achilles' home country, to whom his homecoming was promised.

**Outside Homer.** Plato's Socrates will not have it believed that Achilles gave the hair consecrated to the Spercheius, “the other river”, to Patroclus when he was dead (*Republic* 3.391b; see [[plato]]). Pausanias took Peleus' vow as proof that all the Greeks had cut their hair for rivers from of old (Pausanias 1.37.3; see [[hair]] and [[the-lock-for-the-spercheius]]).
