---
summary: On Ida the men “cut the high-leafed oaks” for Patroclus' pyre with the long-edged bronze, and they fall with a great crashing (23.118–120).
---

δρῦς ὑψικόμους are the oaks around which the wind roared in the simile of the armies' shout in Book 14 (14.398); here they are felled. The whole verse joins three epithets of the scene: the high-leafed oaks, the long-edged bronze, and Ida “of the many springs” in the verse before (23.117–118; [[long-edged]], [[of-the-many-springs]]).
