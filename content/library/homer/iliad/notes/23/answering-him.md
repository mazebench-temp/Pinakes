**The soul.** Achilles answers the soul as he would answer a living man, in the verse of his replies from Book 1 on: “Answering him, swift-footed Achilles said” (23.93; 1.84), and he promises to do all it asks (23.94–98; [[swift-footed]], [[dream-scene]]).

**The quarrels.** ἀντίον ηὔδα opens the two answers that the chariot race provokes. Idomeneus answers “in anger” the son of Oileus, who has called him a blusterer, and offers a wager (23.482–487; [[ajax-son-of-oileus]]); Antilochus answers Menelaus' demand for an oath by giving way (23.586–595; [[oath-formulas]], [[amends-and-reconciliation]]).

**Achilles and Antilochus.** Antilochus' protest makes Achilles smile, “and answering him he addressed him with winged words” (23.557): he will give Eumelus the corselet he took from Asteropaeus (23.558–562; [[winged-words]]). After the footrace Antilochus praises him, and “answering him with these words, Achilles said” that the praise shall not be spoken in vain (23.794–796). The new forms take in two earlier verses of reply (7.356, 15.48). See [[achilles]] and [[antilochus]].
