---
summary: Achilles twice brings “a long-shadowed spear” into the gathering as a prize: for the armed duel, with the shield and helmet of Sarpedon (23.798–800), and for the javelin, with a cauldron (23.884–886).
---

The spear of the duel's verse, “He spoke, and poising his long-shadowed spear, he let it fly” (22.273), is here a thing set down among the prizes, in the formulaic verse αὐτὰρ Πηλεΐδης κατὰ μὲν δολιχόσκιον ἔγχος (23.798, 23.884; see the translation's formula rows). Walter Leaf reports Jordan's suggestion that the spear of the javelin contest is not a prize but the weapon to be thrown, given to Meriones only as an afterthought (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 23.884; see the translation's note on 23.884–886).
