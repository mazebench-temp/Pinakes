summary: “Brilliant” is Achilles eight times, as he holds the head of Patroclus and thinks of the hair and the winds (23.136, 23.140, 23.193), in Nestor's description of the turning post (23.333), and as he pities Eumelus, smiles at Antilochus, is named as Eetion's killer and speaks to Agamemnon (23.534, 23.555, 23.828, 23.889); it is also Odysseus and Epeius at the games, Agamemnon, Hector in the verse of the defilement, and the horse Arion (23.24, 23.36, 23.346).
---

**Achilles.** All but one of his eight verses join the epithet to [[podarkes]] in ποδάρκης δῖος Ἀχιλλεύς; the first stands alone, at the head of the procession, where “brilliant Achilles held his head, / grieving; for he was sending his noble companion to Hades” (23.136–137; [[blameless]]).

**The champions.** At the games the epithet passes to the contestants. Epeius is “brilliant” as he strikes Euryalus and as he takes up the lump of iron, which he throws so that the Achaeans laugh (23.689, 23.838–840). Odysseus is “much-enduring brilliant Odysseus” as he tries to lift Ajax and as he takes up the mixing bowl, and “brilliant Odysseus” as he runs close behind the son of Oileus, pouring his breath on his head (23.729, 23.778, 23.759, 23.765; [[much-enduring]]).

**Others.** “He spoke, and devised shameful deeds against brilliant Hector” (23.24) is the verse with which Achilles began the defilement (22.395; [[so-he-spoke]]). Agamemnon is “brilliant” as the kings lead Achilles to his hut (23.36). Nestor gives the word to a horse, “brilliant Arion,” Adrastus' horse “of the stock of the gods” (23.346–347; [[arion-the-horse]], [[swift]]). See [[achilles]], [[odysseus]] and [[epeius]].
