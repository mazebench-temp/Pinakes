---
summary: Iris finds the winds feasting “in the house of the stormy West Wind” (23.200).
---

Walter Leaf notes that the West Wind is always a stormy and dangerous wind in Homer, except once in the Odyssey, where its coolness is meant (*Odyssey* 4.567), and he finds in the winds' party and their behavior to the goddess “an unmistakable touch of humour” (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 23.200; see the translation's note on 23.200). The epithet's other use in the Iliad is of the stormy wind in the simile of Ares going up to heaven (5.864–865). Achilles calls the same wind “clamorous” in his prayer (23.208; [[clamorous]]).
