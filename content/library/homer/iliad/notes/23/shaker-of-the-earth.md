---
summary: Menelaus bids Antilochus swear, touching his horses, “by the earth-holder, the shaker of the earth” (23.584).
---

γαιήοχος ἐννοσίγαιος is Poseidon's double title (9.183, 13.43; [[earth-holder]]). Walter Leaf notes that this passage has often been quoted to show Poseidon as god of horses, as in later Greece, but that other indications of this in Homer are too doubtful to bear weight; Poseidon is the natural god for Antilochus to swear by, as the god of his house and the father of his grandfather Neleus (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 23.584; see also his notes on 23.277 and 23.307). Achilles' immortal horses were Poseidon's gift to Peleus (23.277–278), and Nestor tells his son that Zeus and Poseidon taught him horsemanship (23.306–308). See [[poseidon]] and [[oath-formulas]].
