---
summary: Iris will not stay with the winds, for she is going back to the streams of Oceanus, to the land of the Ethiopians, where they are offering hecatombs to the immortals, to share in the sacrifices herself.
---

The Ethiopians are named only twice in the Iliad, and both times the gods go to them for their sacrifices. In Book 1 Zeus and all the gods have gone “to Oceanus, to the blameless Ethiopians, / yesterday, to a feast” (1.423–424; see [[zeus-among-the-ethiopians]] and the note on Book 1). Here Iris turns down a seat among the feasting winds: “for I am going back to the streams of Oceanus, / to the land of the Ethiopians, where they are offering hecatombs / to the immortals, so that I too may feast among them on the sacrifices” (23.205–207; see [[iris]], [[oceanus]] and [[hecatomb]]). μεταδαίσομαι ἱρῶν, “feast among them on the sacrifices”, is to share in the sacrificial feast (Walter Leaf, *Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 23.207; see [[sacrifice]]). Leaf supposes that Iris heard Achilles' prayer to the winds from there, and has come from the Ethiopians to the winds (on 23.206; see the translation's note on 23.206). In the *Odyssey* Poseidon is away among them for a hecatomb when the gods decide on Odysseus' return (*Odyssey* 1.22–26; see [[poseidon]]).
