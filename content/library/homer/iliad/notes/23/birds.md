summary: The only bird of Book 23 is the dove of the archery, called simply ὄρνις, “the bird”: the lesser prize goes to the man who hits the cord, “missing the bird”, and Teucer misses the bird and cuts the cord, and the bird darts up toward the sky (23.857–881).
# ὄρνιθος (23.857, 23.865) occurs only in this book.
grc: [ὄρνιθος]
---

The archers' mark is a dove tied by the foot to a ship's mast (23.852–855; see [[doves]]), but in the contest itself she is ὄρνις, the bird, and the cord that holds her is the other mark: “and whoever hits the cord, missing the bird, / since he is the lesser, he shall carry off the single axes” (23.857–858). Teucer “missed the bird; for Apollo begrudged him that; / but he hit the cord beside its foot, where the bird was tied” (23.865–866), and when the cord is cut, “the bird darted up toward the sky” (23.868). Meriones strikes her high under the clouds, and “the bird, / settling on the mast of the dark-prowed ship, / let her neck hang down” and dies (23.877–881; see [[meriones]] and [[teucer]]). The birds of the earlier books were birds of omen and the eaters of the dead, with the dogs (22.335–336, 22.354; see [[dogs]]); in this book no bird touches the dead, and the only bird is a mark for the bow.
