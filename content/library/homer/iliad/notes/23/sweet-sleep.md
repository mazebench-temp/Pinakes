---
summary: Sleep comes to Achilles twice in the book: on the shore, “sweet sleep, poured around him,” when the soul of Patroclus comes (23.62–63), and after the night of burning, “sweet sleep leapt upon him,” until the leaders wake him (23.232–234).
---

The first sleep is the one in which the dead come (23.62–69; [[dream-scene]]); the second is broken by the din of the leaders gathering around Agamemnon (23.233–235). Both come after long grief: on the shore, “loosening the cares of his heart” (23.62), and by the pyre, “worn out” (23.232). See [[achilles]] and [[sleep]].
