---
summary: When the morning star rises, “the star after which saffron-robed Dawn spreads over the sea,” the pyre of Patroclus dies down (23.226–228).
---

This is the third of the epithet's four dawns in the Iliad (8.1, 19.1, 24.695): it opens the day of the bones, the mound and the games. Walter Leaf notes that the line says nothing of an eastern coast, for the dawn spreads over the sea for any observer on a shore (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 23.227; see the translation's note on 23.226–227 and [[dawn-line]]).
