---
summary: Diomedes leaps to the ground “from the all-gleaming chariot” at the finish (23.509); and Menelaus, having given the mare to Antilochus, takes “the all-gleaming cauldron,” the third prize (23.613).
---

The translation renders παμφανόων “all-gleaming” in both verses (see the entry). The chariot is the one “overlaid with gold and tin” that ran close behind the horses (23.503–504; [[tin]]); the cauldron is the prize set out for the third place, “untouched by fire” (23.267–268; [[cauldron]]). See [[diomedes]] and [[menelaus]].
