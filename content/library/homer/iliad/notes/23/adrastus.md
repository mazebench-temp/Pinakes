---
summary: Adrastus is named in Book 23 only as the master of Arion, “the swift horse of Adrastus, who was of the stock of the gods”, whom Nestor names as the swiftest horse a charioteer could drive (23.346–347).
# Ἀδρήστου (23.347) occurs only here in Homer; the Trojans adrastus-the-trojan and adrastus-killed-by-patroclus are other men.
grc: [Ἀδρήστου]
---

Nestor tells his son that if he gets past the others at the turning post, no one will catch him, “not even if he drove brilliant Arion behind you, / the swift horse of Adrastus, who was of the stock of the gods” (23.346–347; see [[arion-the-horse]], [[nestor]] and [[turning-post]]). It is the only mention of his horse in Homer, and of Adrastus in this book. The Catalogue remembers him as a former king of Sicyon (2.572), and in this book Sicyon is the home of Echepolus, who gave Agamemnon the mare that Menelaus drives in the same race (23.296–299; see [[echepolus-son-of-anchises]] and [[sicyon]]). His grandson Diomedes wins the race (14.121; see [[diomedes]]). In the boxing Diomedes seconds Euryalus, son of Mecisteus, son of Talaus (23.677–684); Walter Leaf, who makes Adrastus and Mecisteus brothers, explains Diomedes' care for him by this kinship (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 23.677; see [[euryalus]] and [[mecisteus]]). The Iliad does not name Adrastus' father.
