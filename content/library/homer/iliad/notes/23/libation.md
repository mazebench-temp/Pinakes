# χαμάδις χέε (23.220) is found only here.
grc: ["χαμάδις χέε"]
en: [poured it on the ground]
---

**To the winds.** “And pouring many libations from a golden cup he entreated them / to come” (23.196–197): the prayer to the winds is made, like other prayers, with wine poured out, and with the promise of “fine sacrifices” (23.194–195; see [[prayer]], [[boreas]] and [[zephyrus]]). So Achilles prayed to Zeus for Patroclus' safe return, pouring wine from the cup from which he poured to no other god (16.225–248; see [[chest]]).

**To the dead.**

{{quote:7f76d500-086e-5839-8b75-f9ac371963d7}}

This libation has no god. The wine is poured on the ground for the soul of Patroclus, and the earth is soaked with it (see [[psyche]] and [[the-funeral-of-patroclus]]). Walter Leaf prefers the present participle ἀφυσσόμενος, since the pouring was a series of acts (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 23.220). χαμάδις, “on the ground”, is where the feasters of both armies poured their wine all night in fear of Zeus' thunder at the end of Book 7 (χαμάδις χέον, 7.476–480), and where the wine of the oath in Book 3 was poured, the image of the oath-breakers' brains (3.300; see [[wine]] and [[oath]]). In the Odyssey Odysseus pours a drink offering for all the dead around the pit, first a honey drink, then sweet wine, then water (*Odyssey* 10.518–520, 11.26–28). The two-handled cup, δέπας ἀμφικύπελλον, is the loser's prize in the boxing at the games (23.656, 23.699; see [[cup]] and [[mixing-bowl]]).

At dawn the pyre is quenched with wine, a different act, done for the bones (23.237–238, 23.250; see [[cremation]]).
