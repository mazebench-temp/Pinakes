---
summary: Achilles holds the head of the dead as he sends “his noble companion to Hades” (23.137); the first prize of the chariot race is a woman “skilled in flawless handiwork” (23.263); and Menelaus finishes close behind “noble Antilochus” (23.522).
---

The woman of the first prize is described in the formula of the seven women of Agamemnon's gifts, ἀμύμονα ἔργα ἰδυίας (9.128, 9.270, 19.245); Sthenelus takes her for Diomedes (23.511–513; [[captive-women]]). “Noble Antilochus” is said in the simile that measures the gap at the finish, “so far was Menelaus behind noble Antilochus” (23.517–522; [[the-horse-and-the-wheel]]), just before the dispute in which Menelaus denies him that praise (23.570–585; [[wise]]). See [[patroclus]] and [[antilochus]].
