---
summary: Antilochus urges Achilles to give Eumelus a prize from his own store, “so that the Achaeans may praise you” (23.552). Achilles goes back through the throng “when he had listened to all the praise of the son of Neleus” (23.652), and when Antilochus has praised Odysseus' speed and Achilles' own, he answers, “your praise shall not be spoken in vain”, and adds half a talent of gold (23.795–796).

The two speeches called αἶνος both answer a prize. Nestor's is his thanks for the bowl given him without a contest, with the story of his victories at Buprasium (23.626–650; see [[nestor]] and [[the-funeral-games-of-amarynceus]]); Antilochus' turns his own last place into a compliment, that the gods honor older men and that no one but Achilles could beat Odysseus in running (23.787–792; see [[geron]] and [[time]]). Both honor the giver of the games, and the second is paid for (23.796; see [[contests]] and [[kydos]]). Walter Leaf takes the word as “praise” in both places, not as a pithy tale (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 23.652, 23.795; see the translation's note on 23.652).

Nestor's speech begins with his wish, εἴθʼ ὣς ἡβώοιμι, “If only I were young like that” (23.629), and in the Odyssey the disguised Odysseus begins with the same verse the story that Eumaeus calls an αἶνος (*Odyssey* 14.468, 14.508).
