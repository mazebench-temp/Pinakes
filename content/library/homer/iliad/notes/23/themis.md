summary: Achilles swears that “it is not lawful that water for washing should come near my head” until Patroclus is burned (23.44). At the games Antilochus stands up “claiming his right” to the second prize (23.542), and Menelaus, robbed of it, first asks the leaders to judge between them and then judges himself, “for the judgment will be straight”: Antilochus is to swear by Poseidon, touching his horses, “as is the custom”, that he did not hamper Menelaus' chariot by a trick (23.573–585).
grc: [δίκῃ, δικάσσατε, δικάσω, ἰθεῖα]
en: [claiming his right, judge, judgment]

**Not lawful.** οὐ θέμις, “not lawful”, is said of what may not be done: no man may close with Poseidon's sword in the fight, and Achilles' helmet had never before been stained with dust (14.386, 16.796; see the translation's key-word row for θέμις). Achilles binds himself by it with an oath by Zeus: the mourner stays unwashed until the dead man's rites are done (23.42–47; see [[oath]], [[bathing]] and [[honoring-the-dead]]).

**The custom of the race.** Walter Leaf takes ἣ θέμις ἐστί at 581 as the custom at races, with the formalities of the next lines, and compares the oath of the competitors at Olympia (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 23.581). Antilochus is to stand before his horses and chariot, whip in hand, touch the horses, and swear by Poseidon, the god of his house, the father of his grandfather Neleus (23.581–585; Leaf on 23.584; see [[poseidon]]). He does not swear. He gives up the mare rather than “be an offender against the gods”, and Leaf takes the words as a confession that he cannot take the oath (23.586–595; on 23.595; see [[amends-and-reconciliation]]).

**Judgment.** δίκῃ at 542 is Antilochus' answer to Achilles, who out of pity meant to give the second prize to Eumelus (23.536–554; see [[pity]]). Leaf takes it as a formal appeal, “answered by the custom”, between the senses of custom and of legal judgment, and doubts “with justice”, since Antilochus is in the right against Eumelus but in the wrong toward Menelaus (on 23.542; see the translation's note). Menelaus speaks the language of a trial: “judge between the two of us impartially, and not to help one” (ἐς μέσον … μὴ δʼ ἐπʼ ἀρωγῇ, 23.574), where Leaf explains ἐπʼ ἀρωγῇ by the partisans of a public trial, as on the shield (on 23.574; 18.502; see [[the-trial-on-the-shield]]). Then he judges himself, by putting Antilochus to the oath (Leaf on 23.579); ἰθεῖα, “straight”, agrees with the δίκη implied in δικάσω (Leaf on 23.580), as the elders on the shield compete for the prize of “the straightest judgment” (δίκην ἰθύντατα, 18.508). Before the quarrel of the drivers comes the quarrel of the spectators, in which Idomeneus proposes Agamemnon as ἵστωρ, “arbiter”, of a wager (23.486; see [[istor]]).
