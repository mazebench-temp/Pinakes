---
summary: The book's only γέρας is the one owed to the dead. Achilles bids the Myrmidons drive their chariots up to the body “and weep for Patroclus; for that is the prize of the dead” (23.9). The prizes of the games are ἄεθλα.
---

The half-verse γέρας ἐστὶ θανόντων ends Zeus' words over Sarpedon, where the prize is a tomb and a pillar (16.457, 16.675). Here it is the weeping, as in the Odyssey, where it is the washing of the body and the lament (*Odyssey* 24.188–190) or the closing of the eyes (*Odyssey* 24.295–296; see [[the-prize-of-the-dead]] and [[lament]]). The translation's note on 23.9 records that the verse has ὅ, not the usual τό, only here and at *Odyssey* 24.190, and Walter Leaf's view that the two were meant to be the same (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 23.9).

The games' prizes are another word (see [[contests]]), but the quarrels over them recall the quarrel of Book 1 over a γέρας. Antilochus tells Achilles, “you mean to take away my prize” (ἀφαιρήσεσθαι ἄεθλον, 23.544), with the verb that Achilles had used when Agamemnon threatened to take away his own (γέρας … ἀφαιρήσεσθαι, 1.161; see [[quarrel-of-achilles-and-agamemnon]]). This time Achilles smiles, and gives Eumelus something else from his own store (23.555–565; see [[amends-and-reconciliation]]).
