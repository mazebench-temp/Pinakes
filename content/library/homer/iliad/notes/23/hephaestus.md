---
summary: At the funeral feast the hogs are singed “across the flame of Hephaestus”, the god's name standing for fire, in verses from Phoenix's story in Book 9.
---

“And many white-tusked hogs, rich with lard, / were stretched to singe across the flame of Hephaestus” (23.32–33; see [[feast]] and [[white-tusked]]). The two verses are those of Phoenix's story, when his kinsmen feasted in his father's house to keep him from leaving (9.467–468; Walter Leaf, *Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 23.32), with ἀργιόδοντες ὕες, “white-tusked hogs”, for σύες. The god's name is the fire itself, as when Agamemnon's companions held the entrails “over the flame of Hephaestus” (2.426; see the article above and the note on Book 2). In the *Odyssey* the soul of Agamemnon tells Achilles that “the flame of Hephaestus” consumed his body, and that his bones were laid in a golden jar, the work of Hephaestus (φλὸξ Ἡφαίστοιο, *Odyssey* 24.71–76; see [[thetis]] and [[the-golden-urn]]).
