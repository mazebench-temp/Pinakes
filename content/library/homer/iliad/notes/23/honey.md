# μέλιτος (23.170) is claimed by the entry.
---

“And he set in it two-handled jars of honey and of ointment, / leaning them against the bier” (23.170–171; see [[cremation]], [[pyres]] and [[olive]]). They go on the pyre with the fat of the sheep and cattle, the four horses, the two dogs and the twelve Trojans (23.166–176). Walter Leaf takes κλίνων, “leaning”, to imply the pointed amphora, made to be stuck in the ground or leaned against a wall, and compares the small pointed jars ranged round the bier at Attic funerals (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 23.171). The Odyssey gives Achilles himself the same rite: the soul of Agamemnon tells him that he was burned in the clothing of the gods, “with much ointment and sweet honey” (ἀλείφατι πολλῷ / καὶ μέλιτι γλυκερῷ, *Odyssey* 24.67–68). Honey is given to the dead in the Odyssey also as the first of the libations, honey-milk, then sweet wine, then water (*Odyssey* 10.519–520, 11.27–28).
