---
summary: In Nestor's memory of the games of Amarynceus, the Pylians compete with the Epeians and the Aetolians, and none of them is a match for him (23.632–633).
---

ἔνθʼ οὔ τίς μοι ὁμοῖος ἀνὴρ γένετʼ, οὔτʼ ἄρʼ Ἐπειῶν / οὔτʼ αὐτῶν Πυλίων οὔτʼ Αἰτωλῶν μεγαθύμων, “There no man was a match for me, neither of the Epeians / nor of the Pylians themselves nor of the great-hearted Aetolians” (23.632–633; see [[epeians]], [[aetolians]] and [[the-funeral-games-of-amarynceus]]). αὐτῶν, “themselves”, sets Nestor's own people apart: he outdid the strangers at their own king's funeral, and his countrymen too. In his first war the Pylians fought the Epeians and drove them back to Buprasium (11.737–761; see [[nestor-and-the-epeians]]); at Buprasium they meet again as competitors. Pylos itself is named in the book only in the horses of Antilochus, “born in Pylos” (23.303; see [[pylos]]).
