---
summary: The soul of Patroclus says of his own death: “hateful doom / has gaped around me, the doom allotted me even at my birth” (23.78–79).
---

κὴρ ἀμφέχανε, “has gaped around me”, makes the doom a mouth; the scholia gloss the verb “devoured and swallowed” (Walter Leaf, *Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 23.79). It was allotted at birth (λάχε γιγνόμενόν περ), as Hera says that Achilles will suffer what Aisa “spun for him with her thread at his birth” (20.127–128; see [[aisa]]). Leaf reports that Zenodotus of Mallos concluded from this verse that Homer was a Chaldaean, an astrologer (on 23.79). The next verse turns from Patroclus' doom to Achilles': it is his μοῖρα too to die beneath the wall of Troy (23.80–81; see [[moira]] and [[the-short-life-of-achilles]]).
