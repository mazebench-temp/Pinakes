---
summary: The narrator speaks to Menelaus for the last time as Antilochus hands him the mare: “so, Menelaus, was the spirit in your heart warmed” (23.600).
---

The address closes the simile of the dew on the growing crop (23.597–600; [[dew-on-the-growing-crop]]) and is the seventh and last of the narrator's addresses to Menelaus. The earlier ones came when Pandarus' arrow wounded him (4.127, 4.146), when he would have died at Hector's hands (7.104), when Peisander came on “to be beaten down by you, Menelaus” (13.603), and as he searched for Antilochus to send him with the news of Patroclus' death and then went back to the body (17.679, 17.702). This one comes at his reconciliation with the same Antilochus (23.596–611; [[amends-and-reconciliation]]). See [[menelaus]] and [[antilochus]].
