summary: Achilles swears by Zeus that no water will come near his head until Patroclus is burned (23.42–47). At the games Menelaus tells Antilochus that he will not have the prize without an oath, and bids him stand before his horses with the whip in his hand and swear by Poseidon that he did not hamper Menelaus' chariot by a trick; Antilochus gives up the mare instead (23.441, 23.579–595).

**Achilles' oath.** When the kings bring Achilles to Agamemnon's hut and beg him to wash off the blood, “he refused stubbornly, and swore an oath besides: / ‘No, by Zeus, who is highest and best of the gods’”, that it is not right for water to come near his head until he has laid Patroclus on the fire, heaped up a mound and cut his hair (23.42–47; see [[achilles]], [[oath-formulas]] and [[themis]]).

**The oath at the race.** Antilochus has forced past Menelaus where the road narrows, and Menelaus shouts after him: “But not even so will you carry off the prize without an oath” (23.441; see [[menelaus]] and [[antilochus]]). After the race he makes good the threat. He first asks the leaders to judge between them impartially, and then proposes to judge the case himself, by an oath that no one could fault (23.573–580):

{{quote:1d6b8335-89b0-51f6-837f-93d4c7ff49d5}}

The oath is sworn by touch: the man stands before the horses and the chariot, holds the whip with which he drove, and lays his hand on the horses as he names the god. So Sleep asked Hera to swear by the water of the Styx with one hand on the earth and the other on the sea (14.271–276). μὴ μέν with the infinitive is the form of a sworn denial, as in Hector's oath to Dolon about the horses of Achilles, “that no other man shall ride behind those horses” (10.329–330), which proved false (10.332). Walter Leaf takes ἣ θέμις ἐστί, “as is the custom”, to refer to the custom at races, and compares the oath sworn by competitors at Olympia; Poseidon, he notes, is the god of Antilochus' house, the father of Neleus, and the passage does not prove that Homer knew him as a god of horses (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 23.581 and 23.584; see [[poseidon]]).

**The oath refused.** Antilochus does not swear. He pleads his youth and gives Menelaus the mare, and would give something greater besides rather than “fall from your heart and be an offender against the gods” (23.586–595). Leaf reads ἀλιτρός, “an offender”, as a confession that he could not take the oath (on 23.595). Menelaus gives the mare back (23.602–611; see [[amends-and-reconciliation]]). No oath is sworn in the games.
