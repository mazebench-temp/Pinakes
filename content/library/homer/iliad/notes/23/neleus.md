Book 23 gives Neleus' name in three forms, each standing for a son or a grandson (see [[son-of-neleus]]):

- the patronymic in -ιάδης, τοῦ Νηληϊάδαο, “the son of Neleus” (23.303), as when Diomedes stands before the horses of “the old son of Neleus” (8.100). Here it names Nestor as the father of Antilochus, whose horses are “born in Pylos” (see [[nestor]], [[antilochus]] and [[pylos-born]]);
- the adjective Νηλήϊος, “of Neleus”, twice. Of Nestor, when he has given his advice: “So saying, Nestor, son of Neleus, sat down again in his place” (23.349), as the adjective served for him in Book 10 (Νέστορʼ ἔπι πρῶτον Νηλήϊον, 10.18). Of Antilochus, when he drives in second: “And after him Antilochus, of the line of Neleus, drove in his horses” (23.514), the only time it is used of the grandson; Leaf compares Αἰακίδης, “grandson of Aeacus”, of Achilles (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 23.514; see [[grandson-of-aeacus]] and the translation's note on 23.514);
- the patronymic in -ίδης, Νηλεΐδαο, “the son of Neleus” (23.652), found only here: Achilles goes away “when he had listened to all the praise of the son of Neleus” (23.651–652).

The family of Neleus runs through the chariot race: the father advises (23.306–348), the son drives horses born in Pylos (23.303), and the narrator gives the son his grandfather's name as he comes in by “crafty ways” (23.514–515; see [[kerdos]]). Neleus himself once sent four prize-winning horses to race for a tripod in Elis, and Augeas kept them (11.698–702; see [[augeas]]).
