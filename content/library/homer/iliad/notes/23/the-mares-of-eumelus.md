---
summary: The mares of the grandson of Pheres lead the chariot race until Athena breaks their yoke, and Eumelus comes in last (23.375–397, 23.532–536); from the gathering Idomeneus and the son of Oileus quarrel over whether they still lead (23.458–481).
refs: [23.376, 23.386, 23.391-393, 23.460-461, 23.474-475, 23.480-481, 23.532-533, 23.536]
---

At the start “the swift-footed mares of the grandson of Pheres took the lead” (23.376), the horses that the Catalogue called the best by far (2.763–767). Diomedes is close behind when Apollo strikes the whip from his hands, and he weeps to see “the mares going on still faster” (23.383–386); Athena gives the whip back and breaks the yoke of Eumelus, “and his mares / ran apart, off the road” (23.391–393). Apollo, who reared the mares in Pereia (2.766), is the god who strikes the whip; Athena is the stronger.

From the gathering Idomeneus makes out another horse in the lead and guesses that “the mares must have come to grief / somewhere there on the plain” (23.460–461). The son of Oileus will not have it: “The mares in front are the same that were in front before, / those of Eumelus” (23.480–481), and the quarrel would have gone further had Achilles not stood up (23.490–498; see [[idomeneus]] and [[ajax-son-of-oileus]]). Eumelus comes in last, “dragging his fine chariot, driving his horses in front of him”, and Achilles pities him: “The best man is driving in his single-hoofed horses last” (23.532–536; see [[eumelus]] and [[the-funeral-games-of-patroclus]]).
