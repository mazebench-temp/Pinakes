---
summary: Talaus is named in the patronymic of his son Mecisteus when his grandson Euryalus stands up to box: “son of lord Mecisteus, son of Talaus” (23.678).
---

Μηκιστῆος υἱὸς Ταλαϊονίδαο ἄνακτος, “son of lord Mecisteus, son of Talaus” (23.678), repeats the Catalogue's verse for Euryalus (2.566), with Μηκιστῆος for Μηκιστέος (see [[mecisteus]] and [[euryalus]]). As there, the line reaches back three generations, and the next two verses give the middle one his deed, a victory over all the Cadmeians at the funeral of Oedipus (23.679–680; see [[oedipus]]). Of Talaus himself the poem says nothing more.
