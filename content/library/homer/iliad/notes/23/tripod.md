---
summary: The kings have a great tripod set over the fire to wash Achilles, who refuses (23.39–42); at the games a tripod with handles, holding twenty-two measures, is the first prize of the chariot race with a woman, and a great tripod to stand on the fire, valued at twelve oxen, is the wrestling prize (23.262–264, 23.702–703).
---

**For the bath.** At Agamemnon's hut the kings order the heralds “to set a great tripod over the fire, in the hope that they might persuade / the son of Peleus to wash off the clotted blood” (23.40–41). The verses are those with which the Myrmidons heated water to wash the body of Patroclus (18.344–345; see [[bathing]]), and Achilles refuses with an oath to wash before Patroclus is burned (23.42–47; see [[achilles]]).

**The chariot race.** Achilles “brought prizes out of the ships, cauldrons and tripods” (23.259; see [[the-funeral-games-of-patroclus]] and [[cauldron]]). The first prize of the race is “a woman to lead away, skilled in flawless handiwork, / and a tripod with handles, holding twenty-two measures” (23.263–264), and Sthenelus takes them for Diomedes, giving the woman to his companions to lead away “and the tripod with handles to carry” (23.511–513; see [[sthenelus]] and [[diomedes]]). The pair is the prize of the simile in the previous book, where racehorses run for “a tripod or a woman, in honor of a man who has died” (22.164; see the note on Book 22 and [[racehorses-at-the-turning-post]]). ὠτώεις, “with handles”, is said only of this tripod (23.264, 23.513); Walter Leaf compares the οὔατα, the handles, of Hephaestus' tripods (18.378), and takes the μέτρον for a definite liquid measure (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 23.264). In the quarrel at the lookout Idomeneus offers to wager “a tripod or a cauldron” on which team is in front (23.485; see [[idomeneus]] and [[ajax-son-of-oileus]]).

**The wrestling.** The third set of prizes is “for the winner a great tripod to stand on the fire, / which the Achaeans valued among themselves at twelve oxen; / and for the man who lost he set a woman in their midst, / and she was skilled in much handiwork, and they valued her at four oxen” (23.702–705). ἐμπυριβήτης, “to stand on the fire”, is found only here; Leaf sets it beside the tripods and cauldrons ἄπυροι, “untouched by fire” (on 23.702, referring to 9.122 and 23.267). He takes ἐνὶ σφίσι, “among themselves”, for a rough reckoning in talk rather than an actual exchange (on 23.703), and finds four oxen a very low value for so skilled a woman, when Laertes gave twenty for Eurycleia (*Odyssey* 1.430–431; Leaf on 23.705). Ajax and Odysseus strive “for the well-made tripod” (23.718), until Achilles stops them and bids them take equal prizes (23.735–736; see [[ajax]] and [[odysseus]]).
