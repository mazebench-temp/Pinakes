---
summary: After the supper Achilles “lay groaning heavily among the many Myrmidons” on the shore (23.60), until sleep took him and the soul of Patroclus came.
---

The participle introduces no speech here, as it does in Achilles' replies to Thetis (1.364, 18.78). His last groaning before this was among the Myrmidons over the body, when he vowed not to bury Patroclus until he had brought back Hector's armor and head, and to cut the throats of twelve Trojans before the pyre (18.323–337); in this book the vow is kept (23.19–23, 23.175–183; [[human-sacrifice]]).
