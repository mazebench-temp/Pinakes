---
summary: The soul of Patroclus remembers that he came to Peleus' house “because of a grievous killing” (23.86); and Nestor, too old to compete, says “I must yield to grievous old age” (23.644).
---

γήραϊ λυγρῷ is the phrase of Nestor himself in Book 10, who led his men to war, “for he did not give way to grievous old age” (10.79), and of Peleus, worn out by it in his halls (18.434–435). In the games Nestor yields to it, and remembers the games of his youth (23.629–645; [[old-age-and-memory]]). The “grievous killing” is the manslaughter of Patroclus' childhood (23.86–88; [[androktasia]]). See [[nestor]] and [[patroclus]].
