summary: The ghost of Patroclus remembers how “the horseman Peleus” received him as a boy, reared him with care and named him Achilles' attendant (23.89–90). At the pyre Achilles tells the Spercheius that Peleus vowed his son's hair and a hecatomb for his homecoming, in vain (23.144–149); at the games he says that Poseidon gave his immortal horses to Peleus, and Peleus to him (23.277–278), and he sets Phoenix, “his father's squire”, by the turning post (23.360). The son of Peleus is Achilles' name fifteen times.

**The house of Peleus.** The soul of Patroclus asks that their bones lie together, as the two were reared together in Achilles' house: “then the horseman Peleus received me into his halls / and reared me with care and named me your attendant” (23.89–90; see [[patroclus]], [[the-ghost-of-patroclus]] and [[exile-and-refuge]]). ἱππότα, “horseman”, is his title once before, in Patroclus' reproach to Achilles, “the horseman Peleus was not your father after all” (16.33); elsewhere he is γέρων ἱππηλάτα, the old horse-driving Peleus (7.125, 9.438, 11.772, 18.331). Peleus' house took in other exiles, Phoenix and Epeigeus (9.478–484, 16.570–574; see [[phoenix]]).

**The vow.** Achilles tells the river Spercheius that Peleus prayed in vain that his son would come home to cut his hair for it and offer a hecatomb: “So the old man prayed, but you did not fulfill what he had in mind” (23.144–149; see [[spercheius]] and [[hair]]). Achilles knew it already when he lamented Patroclus in Book 18, that he would not come home “to be welcomed in his halls by the old horse-driving Peleus” (18.330–332; see [[nostos]] and [[the-two-fates-of-achilles]]).

**The horses and the squire.** Achilles' horses are immortal, “and Poseidon gave them / to my father Peleus, and he in turn put them into my hands” (23.277–278; see [[the-horses-of-achilles]] and [[poseidon]]); elsewhere the gods gave them to Peleus (16.381, 17.443–444). At the turning post he sets as a lookout “godlike Phoenix, his father's squire” (23.360; see [[phoenix]]), whom Peleus had sent with him to Troy (9.438–443).

**The patronymic.** Achilles is the son of Peleus fifteen times in the book, Πηλεΐδης and its cases (23.17, 23.41, 23.59, 23.231, 23.287, 23.542, 23.651, 23.700, 23.740, 23.798, 23.826, 23.884) and Πηλεΐων (23.35, 23.249, 23.793; see [[son-of-peleus]]). At the games it names him as host each time he brings out a new set of prizes (23.700, 23.740, 23.798, 23.826, 23.884).
