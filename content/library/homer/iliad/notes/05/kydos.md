summary: In Book 5 κῦδος is held out by a god to one side or one man, and everyone knows it. Athena leaves the fighting to see to whom Zeus will give it; Aeneas fears Zeus will give it to Diomedes, and Diomedes hopes Athena will; Leto and Artemis give it to the wounded Aeneas, and Ares ends the book exulting in it.
grc: [κύδαινον]
passages:

  - 5.445-448 | Aeneas in Apollo's temple

**Held out.** The phrase of Book 5 is κῦδος ὀρέγειν, “hold out glory”, as one holds out a prize to be taken. Athena proposes to Ares that they leave the Trojans and Achaeans to fight, and see “to which side father Zeus will hold out glory” (5.33). Aeneas, offering Pandarus his chariot, says that the horses will bring them safe to the city “even if once again Zeus holds out glory to Diomedes, son of Tydeus” (5.224–225). Diomedes uses the same words of his own patron: “if Athena of many counsels holds out to me the glory of killing them both” (5.260–261). The two men name different gods as the giver, and both are right in their way: Athena gives Diomedes his day, and Zeus lets her.

**Given in secret.** After Apollo carries the wounded Aeneas to his temple on Pergamus, Leto and Artemis “healed him in the great inner shrine and gave him glory” (ἀκέοντό τε κύδαινόν τε, 5.448). The verb κυδαίνω, “give κῦδος”, is here the gods' care for a man who has lost the fight: the loser is honored in the temple while the armies fight over his phantom (see [[eidolon]]).

**Exulting.** When Ares has been wounded, rebuked and healed, Hebe bathes him and he sits beside Zeus “exulting in his glory” (κύδεϊ γαίων, 5.906; see [[exulting-in-glory]]). The phrase belongs to Zeus himself on Ida (8.51). A god's κῦδος survives a defeat that would have ended a man's.
