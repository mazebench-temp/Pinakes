---
summary: In the simile of the river in flood, the torrent sweeps away the walls of “the flourishing orchards”; the adjective there is ἐριθηλής, a compound from the same root as θαλερός.
grc: [ἐριθηλέων]
---

Diomedes rushes across the plain like a winter torrent that scatters the dikes, “nor do the walls of the flourishing orchards stop it when it comes on suddenly, when the rain of Zeus falls heavy” (5.87–91; [[river-in-flood]]). The word is ἐριθηλής, from the intensive ἐρι- (as in [[deep-soiled]]) and the root of θάλλω (“bloom”) that gives θαλερός. The bloom of the orchards measures the destruction: “many fair works of young men fall in ruin before it” (5.92). A river in spate returns later in the book, and this time Diomedes is the man who stops before it (5.597–599; [[swift-flowing]]).
