Aphrodite, wounded by Diomedes, comes to Olympus and falls on the knees of her mother Dione, “and she took her daughter in her arms, and stroked her with her hand, and spoke to her, and called her by name” (5.370–372). The whole line, gesture and address, is the one used when Thetis comes up from the sea to comfort Achilles in Book 1 (1.361 = 5.372), and it returns for Hector and Andromache and for Thetis and Achilles again (6.485, 24.127). Here a goddess comforts a goddess, and the gesture is still a mother's. Dione's first words are a mother's question: “Who of the heavenly gods has done such things to you, dear child, for no reason?” (5.373–374), as Thetis asked her son, “My child, why are you weeping?” (1.362).
