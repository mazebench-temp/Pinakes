summary: Book 5 is the book of κρατερὸς Διομήδης, “mighty Diomedes,” five times at the end of the verse; the adjective also describes the battle, a rebuke, and a ring of Trojans.
grc: [κρατερὴν]
en: [harsh]
---

The formula κρατερὸς Διομήδης, which Book 4 introduced, marks Diomedes' strength at each turn of his day. He closes with the Trojans after his wound “so furiously” (5.143), kills the sons of Eurydamas (5.151), silences Sthenelus' plea to retreat, “looking at him darkly” (5.251; [[looking-darkly]]), answers Pandarus' boast “unafraid” (5.286), and answers Athena's rebuke (5.814). The Aloadae who bound Ares are Otus and “mighty Ephialtes” (5.385), and Heracles is the “mighty son of Amphitryon” (5.392).

The feminine describes battle, “the fierce battle” (5.84, 5.627, 5.712), and a rebuke: Sarpedon tells Hector “to put aside harsh rebuke” (5.492). When Ajax tries to strip Amphius, he fears “the strong ring of the proud Trojans around the body” (5.623; [[proud]], [[fighting-over-the-body]]).
