{{quote:1b4e8496-e3fb-5283-82cb-29c70f2de4ad}}

ἠπεροπεύεις, “you deceive”, is the verb of seduction. Helen used it to Aphrodite's face in Book 3: “Strange one, why do you long to deceive me like this?” (3.399), and Hector called Paris ἠπεροπευτής, “seducer” (3.39). Diomedes gives the goddess her proper work and her proper victims: women, and ἀνάλκιδες, “feeble”, without courage (see [[alke]]). War is for others. On Olympus Athena makes a joke of it, picturing her scratching her hand on a brooch as she urges an Achaean woman to run off with the Trojans (5.421–425), and Zeus says the same thing more gently when he tells her to attend to “the lovely works of marriage” (5.429; see [[the-works-of-war]] and [[aphrodite]]).
