Ἑρμέᾳ ἐξήγγειλεν· ὃ δʼ ἐξέκλεψεν Ἄρηα, “brought word to Hermes; and he stole Ares away” (5.390). Ἑρμέᾳ is the dative of Ἑρμέας, of which Ἑρμῆς is the contracted form (see [[hermes]]). A woman tells him, and he frees the god by theft, not by force. ἐκκλέπτω is “steal away”. He was just in time, for “the cruel bonds were overpowering” Ares (5.391; see [[the-aloadae-bind-ares]], [[eeriboea]] and [[otus]]).

Theft is his art elsewhere too. At the start of Book 24 the gods keep urging “the keen-sighted slayer of Argus” to steal Hector's body (κλέψαι, 24.23–24), and in the *Homeric Hymn to Hermes* he steals Apollo's cattle on the day he is born. In the same book of the Iliad Anchises “stole” a strain of the horses of Tros from Laomedon (ἔκλεψεν, 5.268; see [[anchises]]).
