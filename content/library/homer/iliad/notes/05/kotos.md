---
summary: In Book 5 κότος is a god's resentment. The Trojans take Diomedes' rampage for the work of some god who resents them, and Athena's spear is the one with which she overpowers the heroes she resents.
grc: [κοτεσσάμενος, κοτήεις, κοτέσσεται]
en: [resentful]
---

The Trojans explain Diomedes by a god's grudge. Aeneas wonders whether the man is “some god who bears resentment against the Trojans, in wrath over sacrifices” (κοτεσσάμενος, 5.177–178; see [[menis]]), and Pandarus, whose arrow did not kill him, decides that “surely some god is resentful” (κοτήεις, 5.191). Both are half right. The man is mortal, but a god is standing beside him, and she has a grudge against Troy (see [[athena]] and [[seeing-the-gods]]). κοτήεις is found only here in Homer.

As Athena arms to fight Ares, the poet describes her spear, “heavy, huge, and massive, with which she overpowers the ranks of men, of heroes, against whom she, the daughter of the mighty father, bears resentment” (5.745–747). The lines are formulaic: they return when she arms in Book 8, and in the Odyssey when she sets out for Ithaca (8.389–391, *Odyssey* 1.99–101). The god's κότος is part of her standing description, like the spear.

Diomedes meets a god's κότος again. In the chariot race of the funeral games Diomedes would have passed Eumelus, “had not Phoebus Apollo resented the son of Tydeus” (κοτέσσατο, 23.383) and knocked the whip from his hands; Athena gives it back (23.382–390). The poem gives no reason for the grudge; Apollo is the god whom Diomedes charged three times in Book 5 (5.432–444; see [[gods-and-men-not-alike]]).
