---
summary: The powers on the aegis are listed in four clauses, each beginning ἐν δέ, “and on it” (5.740–741), and Dione's three stories each begin with τλῆ, “endured” (5.385, 5.392, 5.395).
refs: [5.385, 5.392, 5.395, 5.740-741]
---

**The aegis.** After Rout, set around the aegis as a crown, the figures on it follow in four clauses of the same shape: “and on it is Strife, and on it Courage, and on it chilling Onslaught, and on it is the Gorgon's head” (ἐν δʼ Ἔρις, ἐν δʼ Ἀλκή, ἐν δὲ κρυόεσσα Ἰωκή, / ἐν δέ τε Γοργείη κεφαλή, 5.740–741). The repeated ἐν δέ is the way Homer describes the pictures on a work of art, as Hephaestus' making of Achilles' shield is told scene by scene with it (18.483–607; see [[aegis]]).

**Dione's stories.** Dione tells her daughter to be patient (τέτλαθι), for many of the gods “have endured” (τλῆμεν) pains at the hands of men (5.382–384), and then each example begins with the same verb in the same place: τλῆ μὲν Ἄρης, “Ares endured it” (5.385), τλῆ δʼ Ἥρη, “Hera endured it” (5.392), τλῆ δʼ Ἀΐδης, “and … Hades endured” (5.395). The repetition makes three different stories into one list of the same suffering (see [[mythological-paradigm]]).

**Two negatives.** The narrator's explanation of the gods' blood is built the same way: “they do not eat bread, they do not drink sparkling wine” (οὐ γὰρ σῖτον ἔδουσʼ, οὐ πίνουσʼ αἴθοπα οἶνον, 5.341; see [[ichor]] and [[ambrosia]]).
