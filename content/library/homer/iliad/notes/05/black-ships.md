---
summary: The black ships bring the sons of Diocles to Troy, and the Argives, giving ground before Ares and Hector, do not flee to them.
grc: [μελαινάων ἐπὶ νηῶν]
except: [5.22, 5.652]
---

The sons of Diocles came to Troy young: “These two, grown to manhood, on the black ships followed with the Argives to Ilios of the fine horses” (5.550–551; [[of-the-fine-horses]], [[crethon]], [[orsilochus-son-of-diocles]]). Later, when Ares and Hector advance together, the Argives “neither turned and fled to the black ships nor ever charged against them in battle, but kept giving ground backward” (5.699–702; [[armed-in-bronze]]). The ships are the way home and the place of refuge, and here neither is taken.

At 5.22 and 5.652 the adjective μέλαιναν belongs to κῆρα, “black doom,” not to the ships ([[black-doom]]).
