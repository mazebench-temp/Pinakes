---
summary: Aeneas stands over the body of Pandarus behind his shield, “balanced on every side.”
grc: [πάντοσʼ ἐΐσην]
---

When Pandarus falls, Aeneas leaps down from the chariot to keep the Achaeans from his body: “He bestrode it like a lion trusting in his courage, and held in front of it his spear and his shield, balanced on every side” (5.299–300; [[fighting-over-the-body]], [[aeneas]]). In Book 3 the formula described the shields struck in the duel; here the shield protects a dead man. Diomedes answers with a stone that two men of today could not carry, and crushes Aeneas' hip (5.302–310; [[stone-throwing]]).
