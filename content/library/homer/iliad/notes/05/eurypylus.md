The last of the six leaders' kills that open Book 5 (5.37–83; see [[androktasia]]), and Eurypylus' first action in the fighting. He is named twice, each time with his father: Εὐρύπυλος Εὐαιμονίδης, “Eurypylus, son of Euaemon” (5.76), and Εὐαίμονος ἀγλαὸς υἱός, “the splendid son of Euaemon” (5.79; see [[euaemon]] and [[splendid]]). Between the two the narrator tells who the victim was, Hypsenor, son of Dolopion, the priest of the Scamander (5.76–78; see [[hypsenor]] and [[dolopion]]), and the sentence begins again with “him Eurypylus” (5.79).

He kills with the sword. He runs after Hypsenor as he flees, darts at him and strikes his shoulder with the φάσγανον, shearing off the arm, and “the bloody arm fell to the plain” (5.82). The death that follows is told in a verse used only after sword strokes, “purple death and mighty fate” (5.83; 16.334, 20.477; see [[purple-death]]).

Eurypylus has a larger part later. In Book 11 Paris wounds him in the thigh, and Patroclus stays to tend him (11.575–584, 11.806–848).
