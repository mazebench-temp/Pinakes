summary: Book 5 strips the dead and takes their horses at every turn. Idomeneus' attendants strip Phaestus, Diomedes strips the sons of Eurydamas and of Priam and sends their horses to the ships, Sthenelus takes the horses of Tros from Aeneas, Ajax is driven from the armor of Amphius, and Ares himself is stripping Periphas when Diomedes wounds him.

**Armor.** The first spoils of the book are taken by servants. Idomeneus kills Phaestus, and “the attendants of Idomeneus stripped him of his armor” (5.48): the leader kills, and his θεράποντες collect (see [[phaestus-son-of-borus]]). Diomedes “slew them and stripped them”, the two sons of the dream-reader Eurydamas (5.151), and brings down two sons of Priam from their chariot, “and then stripped off their armor” (5.164). Even under Apollo's hands he is eager “to kill Aeneas and strip off his glorious armor” (5.435). Ajax runs up to strip Amphius and cannot, for the spears fly thick (5.617–622; see [[fighting-over-the-body]]).

**Horses.** The richest spoils of Book 5 are horses. Diomedes drives off the horses of the sons of Dares and of the sons of Priam and gives them to his companions to take to the ships (5.25–26, 5.165). Pandarus left his horses at home, “sparing the horses” (5.202), and his fear in Aeneas' chariot is that Diomedes will kill them both “and drive off the single-hoofed horses” (5.236). Diomedes wants just that. The horses of Aeneas are of the breed Zeus gave Tros for Ganymede, and “if we could take these two, we would win noble fame” (5.273). Sthenelus takes them while Diomedes pursues Aphrodite, and gives them to Deipylus to drive to the ships (5.319–327). Diomedes drives them in Book 8, “I took them once from Aeneas” (8.105–108), and in the chariot race for Patroclus (23.290–292). Antilochus, having killed Mydon, whips his team off to the Achaean army (5.589). See [[horses]], [[tros]] and [[deipylus]].

**A god at the work.** When Athena and Diomedes come against Ares, he is stripping “huge Periphas, by far the best of the Aetolians” (5.842–844). The narrator gives the god the verb twice, ἐξενάριζεν and ἐνάριζε, and the god of war is seen at the work that Idomeneus left to his attendants. He leaves the body lying to turn on Diomedes (5.847–849). See [[ares]] and [[periphas]].

**The word.** ἐξεναρίζω, “strip of his arms”, is also the word for killing. Diomedes ἐνάριζε the two sons of Phaenops, and the translation says that he “slew them” (5.155); the narrator asks whom first, whom last Hector and Ares ἐξενάριξαν, “did they slay” (5.703; see [[whom-first-whom-last]]).
