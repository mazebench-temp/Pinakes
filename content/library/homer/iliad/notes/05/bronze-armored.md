---
summary: Pandarus calls Aeneas “counselor of the bronze-armored Trojans,” giving the Trojans the epithet that is usually the Achaeans'.
---

“Aeneas, counselor of the bronze-armored Trojans” (5.180; [[man-of-counsel]]). In Books 1 to 3 χαλκοχίτωνες described the Achaeans, and the formula of Book 3 set “the horse-taming Trojans and the bronze-armored Achaeans” side by side ([[tamer-of-horses]]). Here a Trojan ally gives the bronze to the Trojans. The epithet is not fixed to one side: it names men in bronze armor, and both armies wear it.
