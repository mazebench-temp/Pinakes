---
summary: In Book 5 it is gods who keep ruin away from their favorites: Diomedes sees that a god always stands beside Hector, and Zeus still keeps ruin from his son Sarpedon.
---

The formula λοιγὸν ἀμύνειν, “keep ruin away”, which in Book 1 joined Apollo's plague to Achilles' help and Thetis' service to Zeus, is used twice in Book 5 of a god protecting a man. When Diomedes sees Ares beside Hector, he tells the Achaeans why Hector has seemed so formidable: “always at his side is one of the gods, who keeps ruin away” (ὃς λοιγὸν ἀμύνει, 5.603). When Tlepolemus' spear goes through Sarpedon's thigh, grazing the bone, the narrator adds, “but his father still kept ruin away” (πατὴρ δʼ ἔτι λοιγὸν ἄμυνεν, 5.662).

The word ἔτι, “still”, looks ahead. Zeus keeps ruin from Sarpedon now; in Book 16 he will weigh saving him again, and Hera will persuade him to let Patroclus kill him (16.431–461). The same god who wards off ruin can stop warding it off. See [[sarpedon]] and [[divine-intervention]].
