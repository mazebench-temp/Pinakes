---
summary: Enyo brings Tumult with her into the battle, “shameless in combat”: the power of war itself is without αἰδώς.
grc: [ἀναιδέα]
---

When Hector charges with Ares and Enyo beside him, Enyo brings “Tumult, shameless in combat” (Κυδοιμὸν ἀναιδέα δηϊοτῆτος, 5.593; see [[tumult]] and [[enyo]]). In Book 1 shamelessness was a charge against a man, Agamemnon “clothed in shamelessness” (1.149; see [[clothed-in-shamelessness]]). Here it belongs to a power of battle, which knows no restraint because restraint is not in its nature. The same book shows what the want of αἰδώς looks like in a mortal: Diomedes “had no awe even of the great god” (5.434; see [[aidos]]). A stone thrown in battle can be ἀναιδής too, the stone that crushes Diores in the first battle (4.521), and so is the stone that Sisyphus rolls in the Odyssey (*Odyssey* 11.598; see [[shameless-stone]]).
