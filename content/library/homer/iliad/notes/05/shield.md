summary: The shields of Book 5 are struck and pierced. Pandarus' spear goes through Diomedes' shield to his corselet, Apollo three times batters back his shining shield, the armies hack at oxhide shields and bucklers around the phantom of Aeneas, and Ajax's shield catches the Trojan spears. At rest, Diomedes is worn out by the sweat under his shield's broad strap (5.796–798).

**Pierced and held.** Fire blazes from Diomedes' shield as from his helmet (5.4), and Pandarus knows him “by his shield and his helmet” (5.182). Pandarus' spear strikes it, “and straight through it the bronze point flew on and reached his corselet” (5.281–282): the shield does not stop the spear, and the corselet does (see [[corselet]]). Agamemnon's spear goes through Deicoon's shield as well, and the shield “did not stop the spear” (5.537–539). Ajax, trying to strip Amphius, is saved by his: the Trojans shower spears on him, “and his shield caught many” (5.618–619; see [[fighting-over-the-body]]).

**In defense.** Aeneas stands over Pandarus' body holding in front of it “his spear and his shield, balanced on every side” (5.300; see [[balanced-on-every-side]]). Apollo, shielding Aeneas in turn, three times “battered back his shining shield” when Diomedes attacked (5.437; see [[three-times-and-a-fourth]]).

**Oxhide shields and bucklers.** Around the phantom of Aeneas the armies “hacked at the oxhide shields around one another's chests, the well-rounded shields and the fluttering bucklers of hide” (5.452–453). βοεῖαι, “of oxhide”, stands alone for shields made of it, as ῥινοί did in the clash of Book 4, where the translation renders it “shields of hide” (4.447). The λαισήϊον is a light shield of hide, named only here and where the two verses return (12.425–426). It is πτερόεις, “winged, feathered”, the word of feathered arrows and winged words (see [[feathered]]); here it may describe a fringe, or the lightness of the shield. εὔκυκλος, “well-rounded”, describes both the shields of this passage and Diomedes' own (5.453, 5.797).

**The strap.** Before the first battle Agamemnon foresaw that “many a man will sweat through the strap across his chest of his man-covering shield, and his hand will grow weary on his spear” (2.388–389). In Book 5 it comes true of the best of the Achaeans. Athena finds Diomedes by his chariot, cooling the wound Pandarus gave him:

The τελαμών is the strap from which the shield hung, and it lies over the arrow wound in his shoulder: he has to lift it to wipe the blood away. κάμνε δὲ χεῖρα, “his arm was weary”, answers Agamemnon's χεῖρα καμεῖται, “his hand will grow weary”. See [[diomedes]] and [[healing]].
