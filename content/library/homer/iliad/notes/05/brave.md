summary: The praise of bravery goes to Diomedes' victims, to a charioteer, to the Trojans who drive Ajax back, to Aeneas in Ares' mouth, and in its old formula to Tlepolemus.
grc: [ἠΰν τε μέγαν τε, ἐσθλὸν Ἀτυμνιάδην, πολλῶν τε καὶ ἐσθλῶν, πολλοί τε καὶ ἐσθλοὶ]
---

The formula ἠΰς τε μέγας τε, which the Catalogue gave to Tlepolemus, introduces him again in Book 5 in the accusative, just before his death: “Tlepolemus, son of Heracles, tall and brave, was roused against godlike Sarpedon by mighty fate” (5.628–629; [[mighty-fate]], [[tlepolemus-and-sarpedon]]).

ἐσθλός praises men on both sides. Aeneas tells Pandarus that Diomedes “has loosened the knees of many brave men” (5.176; [[loosened-his-limbs]]). Ares, in the likeness of Acamas, calls Aeneas “our brave companion” (5.469). Antilochus kills Mydon, “the brave son of Atymnius,” Pylaemenes' charioteer (5.580–581; [[mydon]]). The Trojans who stand over the body of Amphius and push Ajax back are “many and brave” (5.624; [[fighting-over-the-body]]).
