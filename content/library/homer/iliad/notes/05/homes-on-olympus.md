---
summary: Dione consoles Aphrodite in the name of all “who have our homes on Olympus,” and twice in Book 5 the gods are named as those “who hold Olympus.”
grc: [οἳ Ὄλυμπον ἔχουσι, οἳ Ὄλυμπον ἔχουσιν]
en: [who have our homes, who hold Olympus]
---

Dione's consolation begins with the whole community of the gods: “Be patient, my child, and endure it, grieved though you are; for many of us who have our homes on Olympus have endured cruel pains at the hands of men, bringing them on one another” (5.382–384; [[dione]], [[mythological-paradigm]]). The formula of the gods' dwelling introduces a list of gods who suffered.

A shorter phrase, θεοὶ οἳ Ὄλυμπον ἔχουσι, “the gods who hold Olympus,” comes twice. It closes Dione's judgment of Heracles, “who with his bow brought sorrow on the gods who hold Olympus” (5.404; [[doer-of-violent-deeds]]), and it opens Zeus' rebuke of Ares: “Of all the gods who hold Olympus, you are the most hateful to me” (5.890; [[strife-is-always-dear]]).
