---
summary: Diomedes, resting from the wound Pandarus gave him, wipes away the dark blood under his shield strap.
grc: [κελαινεφὲς αἷμʼ]
---

Athena finds Diomedes beside his chariot, cooling the wound “where Pandarus had hit him with his arrow”; the sweat under the strap of his shield is wearing him out, “and lifting the strap he was wiping away the dark blood” (5.795–798; [[well-rounded]]). The phrase is the one of Book 4 in reverse order, κελαινεφὲς αἷμα for αἷμα κελαινεφές (4.140). The same archer has drawn it from both men, Menelaus and Diomedes, as Pandarus himself boasts: “from both of them I drew real blood with my shots” (5.207–208; [[pandarus]]). Book 5 also has the other blood, the gods' “immortal blood,” ichor, which flows from Aphrodite's hand and Ares' flank (5.339–340, 5.870; [[ichor]]).
