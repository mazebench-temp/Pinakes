**The first sack of Troy.** Tlepolemus tells Sarpedon what his father did: Heracles “once came here for the sake of the horses of Laomedon with only six ships and fewer men, and sacked the city of Ilios, and widowed its streets” (5.640–642). It was a raid for horses, and it ended as raids on towns ended: ἐξαλαπάζω, “sack utterly”, and χηρόω, “widow”, the men killed and the women left (see [[heracles-sacks-troy]] and [[horses]]).

**Carry off and drive away.** Sarpedon, rebuking Hector, says that he fights hard though “there is nothing of mine here that the Achaeans could carry off or drive away” (5.483–484). φέρειν and ἄγειν are the two verbs of plunder: goods are carried off, and cattle and captives driven. His wife, his son and his many possessions are far away in Lycia (5.479–481; see [[possessions]]). The Trojans, whose city is at stake, have everything to lose, and he warns Hector that the Achaeans “will soon sack your well-peopled city” (5.489; see [[sarpedon]]).

**A promise.** Hera, seeing Ares kill the Argives, fears that the goddesses' promise to Menelaus, “that he would sack well-walled Ilios and then sail home”, was empty (5.715–717).
