The epithet and the narrative pull against each other. The man “dear to Ares” is urged on by Ares, and the god does it “intending that he should be brought down at the hands of Aeneas.” Ares is fighting for the Trojans in Book 5, and his favor, if the epithet names it, is a trap. Antilochus sees the danger and takes his stand beside Menelaus, and Aeneas gives way before the two (5.565–572; [[antilochus]], [[shepherd-of-the-people]]).
