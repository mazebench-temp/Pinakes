---
summary: Book 5 names Diomedes by his father thirty-five times, ten more than by his own name, and Athena makes the comparison of son and father the frame of his great day.
grc: [Τυδεΐδ*, Τυδέος υἱός, Τυδέος ἔκγονός]
en: [offspring of Tydeus]
---

The book opens with the patronymic: “Then in turn to Diomedes, son of Tydeus, Pallas Athena gave might and daring” (5.1–2). From there to the last scene the narrator, his friends, his enemies and the gods call him by his father more often than by his name. The narrator uses it through the rampage: “as for the son of Tydeus, you could not have told which side he was on” (5.85). Pandarus uses it when he recognizes him (5.181, 5.184), and Sthenelus and Athena in the same verse of address (5.243, 5.826; [[dear-to-my-heart]]). The gods complain of him in it. Aphrodite and Apollo both call him “the son of Tydeus, who now would fight even with father Zeus” (5.362, 5.457), Dione says that “the son of Tydeus does not know” that the man who fights the gods does not live long (5.406–407), Apollo warns him with it, “Take thought, son of Tydeus” (5.440), and Ares uses it in his complaint (5.881; [[overbearing]]).

Athena builds her part on the comparison. When she answers his prayer she puts in his breast “your father's might ... such as the horseman Tydeus, wielder of the shield, used to have” (5.125–126; [[wielder-of-the-shield]], [[menos]]). At the end she finds him resting and begins with a reproach: “Tydeus has begotten a son little like himself.” She tells again how Tydeus beat the Cadmeians at Thebes, and ends: “then you are no offspring of Tydeus, the wise-hearted son of Oeneus” (5.800–813; [[tydeus-at-thebes]], [[wise-hearted]]). Agamemnon made the same comparison in Book 4, and Diomedes kept silent (4.370–402). To the goddess he answers: he is holding back from the gods as she herself told him to (5.815–824). Then she sends him against Ares, and the son of Tydeus wounds a god his father never fought ([[diomedes]], [[fathers-and-sons]]).
