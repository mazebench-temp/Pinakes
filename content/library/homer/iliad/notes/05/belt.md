---
summary: Two men die of the same wound, through the belt into the lower belly: Deicoon, killed by Agamemnon (5.537–540), and Amphius, killed by Ajax (5.615–617). In Book 4 the belt saved Menelaus; in Book 5 it saves no one.
---

Agamemnon's spear goes through Deicoon's shield, “and he drove it through the belt into the lower belly” (νειαίρῃ δʼ ἐν γαστρὶ διὰ ζωστῆρος ἔλασσε, 5.539), and Deicoon “fell with a thud” (5.540). Seventy-six lines later Ajax strikes Amphius “on the belt, and the long-shadowed spear stuck in his lower belly” (κατὰ ζωστῆρα … νειαίρῃ δʼ ἐν γαστρὶ πάγη, 5.615–616), and he too “fell with a thud” (5.617). The two killings share the wound, the half-verse of the lower belly and the fall (see [[deicoon]], [[amphius-son-of-selagus]], [[fell-with-a-thud]] and [[androktasia]]).

In Book 4 the belt was a defense. Athena guided Pandarus' arrow to the golden clasps of Menelaus' belt, where the armor was double, and the belt, with the kilt and the band beneath it, kept the arrow from the flesh (4.132–138, 4.185–187; see [[band]]). In Book 5 the spear goes through.
