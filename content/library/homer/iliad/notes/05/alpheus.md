**A river as ancestor.** γένος δʼ ἦν ἐκ ποταμοῖο Ἀλφειοῦ, … ὃς τέκετʼ Ὀρτίλοχον: Diocles' “lineage was from the river Alpheus”, which “begot Ortilochus” (5.544–546). The river is a god who fathers a mortal line, and its grandsons, the twins Crethon and Orsilochus, die at Troy (see [[ortilochus]], [[diocles]] and [[crethon]]). Asteropaeus gives his own descent in the same form in Book 21: his lineage is from the Axius, “flowing broad”, who begot Pelegon, his father (21.157–159; also 21.141–142). The Spercheius is the father of Menesthius (16.173–176). The Odyssey repeats the pedigree of Diocles, “son of Ortilochus, whom the Alpheus begot” (Od. 3.488–489, 15.186–187).

**The land of the Pylians.** The river “flows broad through the land of the Pylians” (5.545). In Homer the Pylian kingdom reaches it. The Catalogue lists Thryum, “the ford of the Alpheus”, among Nestor's towns (2.592), and Nestor places Thryoessa “far off on the Alpheus, on the border of sandy Pylos” (11.711–712). See [[pylians]] and [[pylos]]. The river's descendants lived farther south, at Pherae, on the road from Pylos to Sparta (see [[pherae-in-messenia]]).
