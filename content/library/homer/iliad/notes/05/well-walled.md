---
summary: Hera fears that the goddesses' promise to Menelaus, that he would sack well-walled Ilios, will prove empty.
---

Seeing the Argives killed by Hector and Ares, Hera says to Athena: “Ah, child of aegis-bearing Zeus, Atrytone, surely it was an empty word we promised Menelaus, that he would sack well-walled Ilios and then sail home, if we let baneful Ares rage like this” (5.714–717; [[baneful]], [[atrytone]]). The walls that make the city hard to take are named in the promise to take it. The poem does not say when the promise was made. The epithet names the obstacle, as it did in Achilles' offer to Agamemnon in Book 1 (1.128–129).
