---
summary: Book 5 gives the epithet to Ares twice: as he rouses the Trojans in the likeness of Acamas, and in Hera's protest to Athena.
grc: [οὖλος Ἄρης, οὖλον Ἄρηα]
---

In Book 2 the epithet belonged to the Dream. Book 5 gives it twice to Ares, and these are its only uses for the god. After Apollo has asked him to drag Diomedes out of the battle, “baneful Ares went among the ranks of the Trojans and urged them on, in the likeness of Acamas, the swift leader of the Thracians” (5.461–462; [[acamas-of-thrace]], [[swift]]). The Dream deceived Agamemnon in the likeness of Nestor; the war god rouses the Trojans in the likeness of a man.

The second use is Hera's. Seeing the Argives killed by Hector and Ares, she tells Athena that the promise they made to Menelaus will be empty, “if we let baneful Ares rage like this” (5.715–717). The epithet names what she will not allow, and the goddesses arm ([[august-goddess]], [[furious]]).
