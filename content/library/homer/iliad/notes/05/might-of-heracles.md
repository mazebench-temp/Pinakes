---
summary: Tlepolemus names his father by the periphrasis in his boast to Sarpedon, and Book 5 tells what the might of Heracles did to Troy and to the gods.
grc: [βίην Ἡρακληείην]
---

Tlepolemus, son of Heracles, taunts Sarpedon: “but what a man, they say, was mighty Heracles, my father, bold in endurance, lion-hearted” (5.638–639; [[bold-in-endurance]], [[lion-hearted]]). He goes on to the first sack of Troy, when Heracles came with six ships for the horses of Laomedon (5.640–642; [[heracles-sacks-troy]]). In Book 2 the phrase named the father of the exile; here it is the son's boast, and the son dies at Sarpedon's hand. Earlier in the book Dione told what the same might did to gods: Heracles shot Hera and Hades with his arrows (5.392–404; [[heracles-wounds-hera-and-hades]], [[doer-of-violent-deeds]]).
