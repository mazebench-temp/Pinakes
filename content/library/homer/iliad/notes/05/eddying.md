---
summary: Sarpedon names his home as the Catalogue did: “Lycia is far away, on the eddying Xanthus.”
grc: [δινήεντι]
---

Rebuking Hector, Sarpedon says that he has come from far away to fight for Troy: “for Lycia is far away, on the eddying Xanthus, where I left my dear wife and my infant son, and many possessions” (5.479–481; [[xanthus-of-lycia]], [[lycia]], [[sarpedon]]). The last line of the Trojan Catalogue named the same river with the same epithet (2.877). There it marked the edge of the list; here it measures what an ally has left behind to fight for another man's city ([[trojan-allies]]).
