---
summary: Declining to write epic, Horace asks who could worthily write of Mars in his armor, or of the son of Tydeus, by Pallas' help a match for the gods.
---

In an ode to Agrippa, Horace says that his slender Muse is not equal to great themes, the anger of Achilles, the wanderings of Odysseus, the house of Pelops. Then he asks: who could worthily write of Mars clad in a tunic of adamant, or Meriones black with the dust of Troy, or the son of Tydeus, “by the help of Pallas a match for the gods” (*ope Palladis Tydiden superis parem*, *Odes* 1.6.13–16)? The son of Tydeus is the Diomedes of Book 5, who with Athena beside him wounds Aphrodite and Ares. See [[diomedes]] and [[gods-wounded-by-mortals]].

Horace also allowed that in a long work sleep may creep over even good Homer (*Ars Poetica* 359–360; see [[pylaemenes-dead-and-alive]]).
