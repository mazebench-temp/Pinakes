---
summary: The reply frames of Book 5 fit their speakers. Pandarus answers Aeneas “in turn”, Aeneas and Sarpedon answer “face to face”, Diomedes answers Pandarus' boast “unafraid”, and the formula itself brings in Zeus' answer to Hera and Diomedes' answer to Athena (5.764, 5.814).
grc: ["οὐ ταρβήσας προσέφη"]
en: [Unafraid, spoke to him face to face, answered him face to face]
---

**The formula.** “Answering her, Zeus the cloud-gatherer said” (5.764): Zeus gives Hera leave to rouse Athena against Ares. “Answering her, mighty Diomedes said” (5.814): Diomedes answers Athena's rebuke with her own command not to fight the gods (see [[zeus]] and [[diomedes]]).

**In turn.** Pandarus answers Aeneas twice in the same verse, “Then the splendid son of Lycaon spoke to him in turn” (5.179, 5.229; see [[pandarus]]).

**Face to face.** ἀντίον ηὔδα, “spoke face to face”, is the phrase of a confrontation. Aeneas finds Pandarus, stands before him and speaks to him face to face (5.170), and answers him the same way (5.217); Sarpedon answers Tlepolemus' taunt face to face, a moment before their spears fly (5.647; see [[sarpedon]]).

**Unafraid.** Pandarus boasts that his spear has gone clean through Diomedes' flank. “Unafraid, mighty Diomedes answered him” (τὸν δʼ οὐ ταρβήσας προσέφη κρατερὸς Διομήδης, 5.286): “You missed; you did not hit me” (5.287). The participle takes the place of “answering”, as in the frames of anger and grief (see [[looking-darkly]] and [[groaning-heavily]]). The same verse brings in Diomedes' answer to the other archer who wounds him, Paris, in Book 11 (11.384), and Hector's to Achilles (20.430).
