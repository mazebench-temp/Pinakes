**Two in one chariot.** Diomedes catches “two sons of Priam, descendant of Dardanus”, Echemmon and Chromius, riding in one chariot, and brings them down like a lion breaking the neck of a heifer (5.159–165; see [[echemmon]], [[chromius-son-of-priam]] and [[lion-among-cattle]]). They are the second and third sons of Priam to die in the poem, after Democoon in Book 4 (4.499; see [[democoon]]).

**Ares' call.** Ares, in the likeness of the Thracian Acamas, calls to them:

{{quote:d4234302-5e53-5c39-872b-8887e1eb2aee}}

διοτρεφής, “whom Zeus had nurtured”, is said of the sons and of their father (5.463–464; see [[zeus-nurtured]]). The god speaks to them as an ally would, and asks how long they will let their people die; the next speech, Sarpedon's, makes the same charge from a real ally (see [[acamas-of-thrace]]). He ranks their kinsman Aeneas with Hector, and tells them to save him from the din.

**Sarpedon's charge.** Sarpedon says that Hector boasted he could hold the city with his brothers-in-law and his brothers alone, and that now they cower like dogs around a lion while the allies fight (5.473–477; see [[sarpedon]] and [[hector]]). Hector himself is Πριάμοιο πάϊς, “the son of Priam” (5.704), and Πριαμίδη, “son of Priam”, in Sarpedon's plea (5.684; see [[son-of-priam]]).

Two others are measured against them. The Trojans honored Deicoon, Aeneas' companion, “as they did the sons of Priam” (5.535; see [[deicoon]]), and fate led Amphius of Paesus “to be an ally to Priam and his sons” (5.614; see [[amphius-son-of-selagus]]).
