---
summary: Three of the Achaean leaders who each take a man at the start of Book 5 are “famed for the spear,” Idomeneus, Menelaus and Meges, and each kills with the spear.
grc: [δουρικλυτὸς, δουρικλειτὸς]
---

When the Trojans give way, “each of the leaders took his man” (5.37–38), and the epithet marks three of them. “Idomeneus, famed for his spear,” stabs Phaestus with his long spear as he mounts his chariot (5.45–47; [[phaestus-son-of-borus]]). “Menelaus famed for his spear” stabs Scamandrius in the back as he flees (5.55–58; [[scamandrius]]). “The son of Phyleus, famed for his spear,” Meges, strikes Pedaeus on the back of the head (5.72–75; [[meges]], [[pedaeus]]). Each epithet stands a line or two before the spear it praises goes in. The fourth leader, Eurypylus, kills with a sword (5.81). Menelaus has the epithet again when he kills Pylaemenes (5.578–579; [[pylaemenes]]).

Book 5 has the epithet in two forms. Beside the phrase δουρὶ κλυτός (5.72) it has the one-word compounds δουρικλυτός (5.45) and δουρικλειτός (5.55, 5.578), the second built with -κλειτός, the adjective of [[famous]].
