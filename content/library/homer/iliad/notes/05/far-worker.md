---
summary: Apollo, “far-working,” turns Diomedes back at his fourth assault with a shout and a warning.
---

In Book 1 the Far-Worker's work was the plague and its end. In Book 5 his work is protection, and it is done with his hands and his voice. Diomedes attacks Aeneas three times, though he knows Apollo is holding his hands over him, and three times the god batters his shield back; at the fourth assault “far-working Apollo shouted at him terribly” (5.436–439; [[like-a-god]], [[three-times-and-a-fourth]]). His words are a warning about the difference between gods and men (5.440–442), and they are enough: “the son of Tydeus drew back a little way, avoiding the wrath of Apollo who shoots from afar” (5.443–444; [[hekatebolos]], [[menis]]). The archer god does not shoot in Book 5. He carries Aeneas to his temple on Pergamus and leaves a phantom in his place (5.445–453; [[god-of-the-silver-bow]]).
