---
summary: Book 5 twice recalls Aeneas' parentage, and shows the goddess acting as his mother. Sthenelus warns that Aeneas claims to be the son of Anchises and of Aphrodite (5.247–248), and when Aeneas falls, Aphrodite, “who bore him to Anchises as he tended his cattle”, carries him out of the battle (5.311–318).
passages:
  - 5.311-313 | His mother, who bore him to Anchises
---

Sthenelus names the parents as a reason to retreat: of the two men coming against Diomedes, “Aeneas claims that he was born the son of noble Anchises, and his mother is Aphrodite” (5.247–248). The narrator names them at the moment the goddess acts. Diomedes has crushed Aeneas' hip, and he “would have perished there, had not Aphrodite, daughter of Zeus, been quick to see it, his mother, who bore him to Anchises as he tended his cattle” (5.311–313). μήτηρ, “his mother”, stands first in its line, and βουκολέοντι, “tending cattle”, gives the only detail of the story that the Iliad tells: Anchises was herding his cattle on Ida when the goddess came to him (see [[cattle]] and [[anchises]]).

What follows is the consequence of that union. The goddess throws her white arms around her son and covers him with a fold of her robe, is wounded by Diomedes, and drops him (5.314–343; see [[diomedes-wounds-aphrodite]]). On Olympus she tells her own mother that Aeneas is “dearest to me by far of all” (5.378). A goddess's mortal son is her care and her grief. See [[mothers-and-sons]] and [[aeneas]].
