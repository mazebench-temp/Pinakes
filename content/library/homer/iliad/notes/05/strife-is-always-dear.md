---
summary: Zeus rebukes the wounded Ares with the verse Agamemnon used against Achilles: “strife is always dear to you, and wars and battles”.
passages:
  - 5.888-898 | Zeus rebukes Ares
---

{{quote:9e8e2638-58c8-5466-9e8c-e3b92358623c}}

In Book 1 the line was a king's insult to his best warrior (1.176–177). Here the father of the gods says it to the god of war, who has come to him bleeding and complaining of Athena. Only the end of the first verse changes, from “of all the kings whom Zeus has nurtured” to “of all the gods who hold Olympus”. Said of Ares, the charge is simply true: strife and war are his province, as Zeus told Aphrodite earlier in the book (5.430; see [[the-works-of-war]]). The echo makes Achilles, in Agamemnon's eyes, a kind of Ares.

Zeus goes on to blame the god's mother, whose fury he has inherited (5.892–894), and then heals him because he is his son (5.895–904). Zeus hates strife in his son, but he does not stop it: in the same book Strife rages “without end” beside Ares in the battle (5.518), and she stands on the aegis that his own daughter wears (5.740).
