---
summary: Book 5 shows the fight for the fallen in every form. Idaeus does not dare to stand over his brother, Aeneas stands over Pandarus like a lion, Menelaus and Antilochus carry the dead twins back to their friends, and Ajax is driven from the body of Amphius; around the phantom of Aeneas both armies fight over a body that is not there.

  - 5.449-453 | The fight around the phantom
  - 5.610-626 | Ajax and Amphius

**Not daring.** The book's first killing ends in a refusal. Diomedes kills Phegeus, and his brother Idaeus leaps from the chariot “and did not dare to stand over his slain brother” (5.20–21). περιβῆναι, “stand over”, is the verb of the defense, and the narrator excuses him: he would not have escaped either, had Hephaestus not hidden him (5.22–24). The horses go to the Achaean ships (5.25–26). See [[idaeus-son-of-dares]].

**Aeneas over Pandarus.** Pandarus falls from the chariot, and Aeneas springs down, “fearing that the Achaeans might somehow drag the body away from him”:

{{quote:8eeb1e44-9409-5129-8371-c33459751088}}

The two verses of the spear and the round shield held in front of the body return for Menelaus over Patroclus (5.300–301 = 17.7–8; see [[like-a-lion]]). Diomedes answers with a stone, and the defender becomes the man who needs defending (5.302–317; see [[stone-throwing]] and [[divine-rescue]]). Pandarus' body is not mentioned again.

**The phantom.** Apollo takes Aeneas to Pergamus and leaves an image “like Aeneas himself, and in armor such as his”, and “around the phantom the Trojans and the brilliant Achaeans hacked at the oxhide shields around one another's chests” (5.449–453). The armies fight over a body that is not there. Ares, in the likeness of Acamas, rallies the sons of Priam with the words of the rescue: “A man lies fallen whom we honored as highly as brilliant Hector … let us save our brave companion from the din” (5.467–469). The man they mean to save is already safe, and he comes back to them “alive and unhurt” (5.515). See [[the-phantom-of-aeneas]] and [[eidolon]].

**Carried back.** Menelaus and Antilochus drive Aeneas from the twins he has killed, “and when they had dragged the dead back to the army of the Achaeans, they laid the two poor men in the arms of their companions” (5.573–574). The bodies go from the enemy's reach into friends' hands, and the rescuers turn back to fight (5.575; see [[crethon]] and [[orsilochus-son-of-diocles]]).

**Ajax and Amphius.** The fullest scene of the book. Ajax kills Amphius and runs up “to strip his armor”; the Trojans shower spears on him, and his shield catches many. He sets his heel on the body to pull his spear out, but cannot take the rest of the armor, “for he was hard pressed by missiles”, and he fears “the strong ring of the proud Trojans around the body” (5.617–623). ἀμφίβασις, “a standing around”, the noun of ἀμφιβαίνω, occurs only here in Homer. The last two verses, in which the ring pushes him back, were used of Thoas at the body of Peirous (5.625–626 = 4.534–535). See [[amphius-son-of-selagus]] and [[ajax]].

**The living and the dead.** Sarpedon, wounded, asks Hector not to leave him “as prey for the Danaans to lie here, but defend me” (5.684–685), and his companions carry him out of the fighting, as the Achaeans carry Tlepolemus' body out on the other side (5.663–669, 5.692). His fear is the fear of every man who falls, and in Book 16 the fight over his body will fulfill it. Ares himself is stripping Periphas when Diomedes comes, and he leaves the body lying “where he had first killed him” to attack the man (5.842–849; see [[periphas]]).
