---
summary: Pandarus came to Troy “to do a favor to brilliant Hector”, and Ares complains that the gods suffer “when we do favors for men”; both learn in Book 5 what a χάρις can cost. Theano rears her husband's bastard son to please him.
grc: [χαριζομένη]
en: [favor, favors, to please]
---

**A favor to Hector.** Pandarus, regretting that he left his horses at home, says that it was in an evil hour that he took his bow from its peg “on that day when to lovely Ilios I led the Trojans, to do a favor to brilliant Hector” (φέρων χάριν Ἕκτορι δίῳ, 5.209–211). χάριν φέρειν is to do someone a kindness that will be owed back. Pandarus came from Zeleia as an ally, for Hector's sake, and he dies without the return (5.290–296; see [[pandarus]] and [[trojan-allies]]). Sarpedon makes the ally's complaint to Hector's face in the same book: “we are the ones who fight, we who are only allies among you” (5.477).

**Favors to men.** Ares, wounded, uses the same phrase of the gods: “We gods are forever enduring the most chilling things by one another's will, when we do favors for men” (χάριν ἄνδρεσσι φέροντες, 5.873–874). The favor runs the other way, from gods to men, and the cost falls on the gods who give it (see [[gods-wounded-by-mortals]]).

**To please a husband.** Theano, the wife of Antenor, reared Pedaeus, her husband's bastard, “like her own dear children, to please her husband” (χαριζομένη πόσεϊ ᾧ, 5.70–71; see [[nothos]] and [[theano]]). The verb χαρίζομαι is to do what pleases, to show χάρις.

The Graces, the Χάριτες, the goddesses of the word, made the robe through which Diomedes' spear pierces Aphrodite's hand (5.338; see [[graces]]).
