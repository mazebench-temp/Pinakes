summary: In Book 5 grief belongs mostly to the gods. Hephaestus saves one son of Dares so that the old man may not be wholly stricken with grief, but it is Aphrodite, Hades and Ares who grieve at their wounds, and Hera who grieves at the Achaean dead.
grc: [ἀκαχήμενος, ἀκηχεμένη, ἀχεύων]
en: [grieved]
---

The book's one grieving mortal is spared the worst. When Diomedes kills Phegeus, Hephaestus hides his brother Idaeus in night, “so that the old man might not be wholly stricken with grief” (ὡς δή οἱ μὴ πάγχυ γέρων ἀκαχήμενος εἴη, 5.24; see [[dares]] and [[bereaved-parents]]). The god halves the father's grief.

The rest of the ἄχος in Book 5 is divine, and it comes from wounds. Aphrodite mounts Ares' chariot “grieved at heart” (ἀκηχεμένη φίλον ἦτορ, 5.364). Hades, in Dione's story, went up to Olympus with Heracles' arrow in his shoulder, “grieving at heart, pierced through with pains” (κῆρ ἀχέων, 5.399). Ares, wounded by Diomedes, sits down beside Zeus “grieving in his heart” (θυμὸν ἀχεύων, 5.869). Hera's grief is for others: she complains that Ares has destroyed a great army of the Achaeans, ἐμοὶ δʼ ἄχος, “to my grief”, while Cypris and Apollo take their ease (5.759–760). In the Iliad ἄχος is often the pain of loss and humiliation, and the gods of Book 5 feel both. See [[gods-wounded-by-mortals]] and [[algos]].
