summary: Two fathers are wealthy in Book 5, Dares the priest and Diocles of Pherae, and each loses his sons; Amphius of Paesus, “rich in possessions, rich in grainland,” is led by fate to die at Troy.
grc: [ἀφνειὸς, πολυκτήμων πολυλήϊος]
en: [rich in possessions, rich in grainland]
---

The first killings of Diomedes' day begin with a rich man: “There was among the Trojans one Dares, wealthy and noble, a priest of Hephaestus” (5.9–10; [[dares]], [[blameless]]). One of his two sons is killed, and the god saves the other so that the old man should not be wholly stricken with grief (5.11–24). Later Aeneas kills the twin sons of Diocles, whose father “lived in well-built Pherae, wealthy in substance” (ἀφνειὸς βιότοιο, 5.543–544; [[well-built]], [[crethon]], [[orsilochus-son-of-diocles]]). Wealth is part of the obituary: it is what the father has and what his sons will not inherit. The same point is made plainly of Phaenops, whose goods pass to distant kinsmen (5.158; [[darling]]).

Amphius, son of Selagus, has two words of wealth of his own, πολυκτήμων and πολυλήϊος, found in Homer only here. He lived in Paesus, “rich in possessions, rich in grainland; but his fate led him to be an ally to Priam and his sons” (5.612–614; [[amphius-son-of-selagus]], [[apaesus]]). Telamonian Ajax kills him, and the Trojans' spears keep Ajax from taking his armor (5.615–626). On the other side, among the Achaeans whom Hector and Ares kill, is the Boeotian Oresbius, who lived in Hyle, “much intent on his wealth” (5.707–710; [[oresbius]], [[of-the-flashing-band]]).
