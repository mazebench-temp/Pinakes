συμβάλλετον is a dual: the two rivers “join their streams” (5.774; see [[scamander]]). There, on the plain of Troy, Hera sets down her chariot and hides the horses in mist, and the goddesses go on foot to the battle (5.778–780). The river does the rest. ἀνέτειλε, “made spring up”: the gods' own food grows from the Trojan river for the gods' horses, as Iris set “ambrosial fodder” before Ares' horses on Olympus (5.369) and the Hours will tie Hera's at ambrosial mangers (8.434; see [[ambrosia]] and [[horses]]). A river of Troy feeds the horses of Troy's enemies. In Book 21 the Scamander calls on the Simoeis as “dear brother” to help him against Achilles (21.308).
