---
summary: Agamemnon's exhortation asks the Achaeans to “take a valiant heart,” ἄλκιμον ἦτορ, giving the spirit the adjective of the strong spear.
grc: [ἄλκιμον ἦτορ]
en: [valiant]
---

As the Trojans rally, the son of Atreus goes through the throng: “Friends, be men, and take a valiant heart, and feel shame before one another in the fierce battles” (5.529–530; [[exhortation]], [[shame-saves-more]], [[aidos]]). ἄλκιμος, from ἀλκή (“strength, defense”), is the adjective that the arming verse gives to the spear. Here it is the heart that the men must take up. The phrase ἄλκιμον ἦτορ comes back in Achilles' words to the Myrmidons (Il. 16.209) and in similes of the wasps and of the lion (Il. 16.264, 17.111, 20.169).
