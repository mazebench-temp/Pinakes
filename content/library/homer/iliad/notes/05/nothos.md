The obituary gives Pedaeus a place in his father's house. He was νόθος, born to Antenor by another woman, but Antenor's wife Theano “had reared him with care” (πύκα δʼ ἔτρεφε), “like her own dear children” (ἶσα φίλοισι τέκεσσι), “to please her husband” (χαριζομένη πόσεϊ ᾧ). The Iliad's bastards are raised in their fathers' houses, and here the wife's care is part of her duty to her husband (see [[theano]], [[antenor]] and [[charis]]). The care ends in a grim death. Meges comes close and strikes him on the back of the head, the bronze cuts through the teeth under the tongue, “and he fell in the dust, and bit the cold bronze with his teeth” (5.72–75; see [[pedaeus]] and [[meges]]). In Book 4 Odysseus killed Democoon, a bastard son of Priam (4.499–500); the sons of Antenor die too in later books (11.221–263).
