The fifth of the six leaders' kills when the Trojans give way (5.37–83; see [[androktasia]]). Meges is named twice, by name and by patronymic, Φυλεΐδης δουρὶ κλυτός, “the son of Phyleus, famed for his spear” (5.72; see [[phyleus]] and [[famed-for-his-spear]]). His man is Pedaeus, a bastard son of the Trojan elder Antenor, whom Antenor's wife Theano had raised like her own children (5.69–71; see [[pedaeus]] and [[antenor]]). Meges comes close (ἐγγύθεν ἐλθών) and drives his spear into the back of the head; the point comes out under the tongue, and the dying man “bit the cold bronze with his teeth” (5.75). The wound is told with grim exactness, from the nape to the tongue to the teeth.

This is his only kill in Book 5. At the ships in Book 15 a corselet that his father brought home from Ephyra saves his life (15.529–534).
