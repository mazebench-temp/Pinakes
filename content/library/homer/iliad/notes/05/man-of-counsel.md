---
summary: Two leaders of Troy's side are addressed as counselors: Aeneas by Pandarus, and Sarpedon, with contempt, by Tlepolemus.
grc: [βουληφόρε]
en: [counselor]
---

The vocative βουληφόρε, rendered “counselor,” opens two speeches. Pandarus answers Aeneas' call for help: “Aeneas, counselor of the bronze-armored Trojans” (5.180; [[bronze-armored]], [[aeneas]]). Tlepolemus begins his taunt with the same title: “Sarpedon, counselor of the Lycians, what need do you have to cower here, a man with no knowledge of battle?” (5.633–634; [[sarpedon]]). The courtesy of the address is turned into mockery by the next line. The two are also each called “leader” (ἀγός) of his people in Book 5: “Aeneas, leader of the Trojans” (5.217) and “Sarpedon, leader of the Lycians” (5.647). Neither is a son of Priam, and each commands his own people, Aeneas the Dardanians and Sarpedon the Lycians ([[dardanians]], [[lycians]]).
