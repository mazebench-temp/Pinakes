summary: Sarpedon, wounded, begs Hector not to leave him, since he was not, it seems, to return to his dear native land.

{{quote:9ee140e1-ce4a-5f6c-b0c9-de1cf6dd92b5}}

The formula of the return names what Sarpedon gives up. Earlier in the book he told Hector that he had left his wife and infant son far away in Lycia (5.480; [[eddying]]); now, wounded, he repeats them in the verse after the formula, and asks only that he may die in Troy and not lie as prey for the Danaans ([[nostos]], [[sarpedon]]). He does not die here. He dies in Book 16, and Sleep and Death carry his body home to Lycia (Il. 16.666–683).
