---
summary: Pandarus' horses, left at home in Lycaon's halls, stand eating “white barley,” κρῖ λευκόν, a formula of fodder with the common word for white, λευκός.
grc: [κρῖ λευκὸν]
---

Pandarus tells Aeneas why he has no chariot at Troy. In his father's halls there are eleven new chariots, and beside each a yoke of horses “stands, munching white barley and spelt” (5.195–196; [[lycaon]], [[pandarus]]). His father told him to lead the Trojans from a chariot, but he left the horses at home, sparing them, “for fear they would go short of fodder” in the crowded city, and came on foot, trusting in his bow (5.197–205; [[curved-bow]]). The white barley belongs to the good life of the horses he spared. The same words are used of the Trojans' horses by their watch fires (Il. 8.564), and in a simile Achilles' horses trample the dead as oxen tread white barley on a threshing floor (Il. 20.495–499).
