---
summary: The Danaans of the swift horses are the danger from which Aphrodite and then Apollo shield Aeneas, in the same two verses.
---

Two gods save Aeneas, and the narrator gives each rescue the same pair of verses. Aphrodite spreads the fold of her robe before her son “as a shelter from missiles, for fear one of the Danaans of the swift horses might strike the bronze into his chest and take away his life” (5.315–317). When Diomedes wounds her and she lets him fall, Apollo takes him up and covers him “in a dark cloud, for fear one of the Danaans of the swift horses might strike the bronze into his chest and take away his life” (5.344–346; [[divine-rescue]], [[aeneas]]). The formula for the Achaean army names the threat both times. The robe fails; the cloud holds.
