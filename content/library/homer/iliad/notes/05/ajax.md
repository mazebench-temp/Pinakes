summary: Telamonian Ajax is one of the two Ajaxes who hold the line against Hector's rally. When Hector kills Menesthes and Anchialus, Ajax pities them and kills Amphius of Paesus, but the Trojans' spears drive him back before he can strip the body (5.519, 5.610–626).
refs: [5.520, 5.611-612, 5.616, 5.618-623, 5.625-626]
passages:
  - 5.610-626 | Ajax kills Amphius and is driven back
---

**The line holds.** When the Trojans rally under Hector and Ares, “the two Ajaxes and Odysseus and Diomedes urged on the Danaans to fight” (5.519–520), and the Achaeans stand like clouds set motionless on the mountains (5.522–527; see [[clouds-on-the-mountains]]). The two Ajaxes are Telamonian Ajax and the son of Oileus, as in Books 2 and 4 (see [[ajax-son-of-oileus]]).

**Pity and a spear.** Hector kills two Achaeans who ride in one chariot, Menesthes and Anchialus (5.608–609; see [[menesthes]]). “And great Telamonian Ajax pitied them as they fell” (5.610): the verse, with another name, that sent Menelaus against Aeneas (5.561; see [[menelaus]]). He comes close, throws, and strikes Amphius, son of Selagus, a rich man of Paesus whose fate had led him to fight for Priam (5.611–617; see [[amphius-son-of-selagus]]).

**Driven back.** He runs up to strip the armor, and the Trojans shower spears on him; his great shield (σάκος) catches many (see [[shield]]). He sets his heel on the body and pulls his spear free (λὰξ προσβάς), as Hector will pull his spear from Patroclus (16.863), but he cannot take the armor. He fears the Trojans' “strong ring” around the body (ἀμφίβασις, 5.623, a word found only here), and they push him back, “big and mighty and noble as he was” (5.625). The last two verses are those that told how the Thracians pushed Thoas back from the body of Peirous at the end of the first battle (5.625–626 = 4.534–535; see [[thoas]] and [[peirous]]). The killer who tries to strip his man is driven off by the dead man's friends (see [[fighting-over-the-body]] and [[stripping-the-dead]]).

The scene belongs to the Achaean retreat before Hector and Ares. Diomedes has told the army to give ground with their faces to the Trojans (5.605–606), and Ajax himself gives ground, “reeling” (χασσάμενος πελεμίχθη, 5.626).
