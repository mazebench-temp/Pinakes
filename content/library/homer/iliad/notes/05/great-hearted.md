---
summary: Book 5 gives the epithet to fathers named in their sons' stories (Tydeus, Anchises, Nestor, Diocles), to peoples on both sides, and to Odysseus and to Stentor, whose likeness Hera takes.
grc: [μεγάθυμον, μεγαλήτορι]
---

The epithet most often names a father beside his son. Diomedes is “the son of great-hearted Tydeus” (5.25, 5.235, 5.335), Aeneas the “son of great-hearted Anchises” (5.468), Antilochus “the son of great-hearted Nestor” (5.565), and in the pedigree of the twins who fall to Aeneas, “Ortilochus begot great-hearted Diocles” (5.547; [[crethon]]). Deicoon is “companion of great-hearted Aeneas” (5.534). Of peoples it goes to the Trojans, whom Pandarus calls on as “great-hearted Trojans, goaders of horses” (5.102; [[goaders-of-horses]]), and to the Paphlagonians (5.577; [[shield-bearing]]).

The synonym μεγαλήτωρ names two men in the dative. It was not fated “for great-hearted Odysseus” to kill Sarpedon (5.674; [[odysseus]]), and Hera shouts to the Argives “in the likeness of great-hearted Stentor with the voice of bronze” (5.785; [[bronze-voiced]], [[stentor]]).
