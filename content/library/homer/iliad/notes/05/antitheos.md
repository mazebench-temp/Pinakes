---
summary: The epithet goes to Pandarus, to Sarpedon three times as he fights and is carried wounded from the field, and to Teuthras, the first of the Achaeans killed by Hector and Ares.
---

Aeneas goes through the battle “seeking godlike Pandarus” (5.168; [[pandarus]]). Sarpedon is “godlike” when mighty fate rouses Tlepolemus against him (5.629; [[mighty-fate]]) and twice as his companions carry him from the fighting and set him down under the oak of Zeus (5.663, 5.692; [[the-oak-of-zeus]]). The son of Zeus is called a match for the gods in the lines where he is most helpless. The last use heads a list of the dead: “Godlike Teuthras, and after him Orestes, driver of horses” (5.705; [[teuthras]], [[driver-of-horses]]). As often, the praise says nothing about the man but that he was a hero.
