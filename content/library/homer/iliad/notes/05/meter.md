---
summary: Book 5 is full of mothers: Aphrodite, who saves her son and runs to her own mother Dione; Eeriboea, the stepmother who saves Ares; and Hera, whose fury Zeus finds in her son.
grc: [μητρός, μητρυιὴ]
en: [stepmother]
---

**Mothers and daughters, mothers and sons.** Aphrodite is named as Aeneas' mother twice, by Sthenelus, “and his mother is Aphrodite” (5.248), and by the narrator as she saves him, “his mother, who bore him to Anchises as he tended his cattle” (5.313; see [[aphrodite-and-anchises]]). Wounded for it, she falls on the knees of Dione, “her mother” (μητρὸς ἑῆς, 5.371). The book shows a mother saving her son and then being comforted by her own. See [[mothers-and-sons]] and [[dione]].

**A stepmother.** In Dione's story, Ares would have perished in the bronze jar where the sons of Aloeus bound him, had not “their stepmother, the beautiful Eeriboea” brought word to Hermes (μητρυιὴ περικαλλὴς Ἠερίβοια, 5.389–390; see [[eeriboea]]). The poem says nothing of her reasons. The word μητρυιή occurs only twice more in Homer, both times of Eriopis, the stepmother of Medon (13.697 = 15.336).

**A mother's fury.** Zeus tells the wounded Ares that he has “your mother's fury, uncontrollable and unyielding, Hera's” (μητρός τοι μένος, 5.892–893), and that he will heal him only because “it was to me your mother bore you” (5.896). The son inherits the mother's temper as Diomedes inherits his father's might (see [[menos]]).
