---
summary: “The splendid son of Lycaon” is Pandarus' formula in Book 5, six times, at every stage of his last day; the same phrase of a father's pride names Eurypylus, Sthenelus and Periphas.
---

Pandarus is named as his father's splendid son when he sees Diomedes rushing across the plain (5.95), when he shouts his boast over the wound (5.101), when he answers Aeneas twice (5.179, 5.229), when he challenges Diomedes (5.276), and when he boasts again that Diomedes is hit (5.283; [[pandarus]], [[lycaon]]). The Catalogue first gave him the phrase ({{ref:Iliad 2.826}}). Here it frames a man who never stops talking of his father: he did not listen to the old man's advice to bring his horses, and he wishes he had (5.197–205; [[spearmen]]). The splendid son dies with his father's instructions unheeded.

The phrase honors other sons: Eurypylus, “the splendid son of Euaemon,” as he kills Hypsenor (5.79), Sthenelus, “the splendid son of Capaneus,” as he urges retreat (5.241; [[sthenelus]]), and Periphas, “the splendid son of Ochesius,” whom Ares kills (5.843; [[periphas]], [[ochesius]]).
