---
summary: Odysseus weighs whether to pursue the wounded Sarpedon or to kill more of the Lycians, and the choice is made for him: it was not fated for him to kill the son of Zeus, and Athena turns his heart against the Lycians (5.671–676).
refs: [5.671-676]
---

The scene has its usual parts, the pondering “in his mind and in his heart” and the two alternatives, “whether … or” (5.671–673; see [[pondering-two-ways]]). Its resolution is not the usual one. In Book 1 Achilles' choice was interrupted by Athena and then given back to him to decide (1.188–222); in Book 2 Zeus' deliberation ended in “this plan seemed to him in his heart the best” (2.5). Here the narrator states the outcome before the man can reach it, “it was not fated for great-hearted Odysseus to kill the mighty son of Zeus”, and the goddess brings it about by turning his heart (5.674–676). What was fated and what the goddess does come to the same thing, and Odysseus acts on the choice as his own, killing seven Lycians (5.677–678; see [[moira]] and [[odysseus]]).
