---
summary: Book 5 has two journeys of the gods by chariot. Iris drives the wounded Aphrodite to Olympus in Ares' chariot, and Hera, with Athena armed beside her, drives her own chariot out through the gates of heaven, stops before Zeus, and comes down to the rivers of Troy.
passages:
  - 5.363-369 | Iris drives Aphrodite to Olympus
  - 5.720-732 | Hera harnesses her horses
  - 5.745-777 | Hera and Athena drive to Troy
---

**Aphrodite.** The wounded goddess cannot drive herself. She begs her brother Ares for his horses with golden headbands, mounts grieving, and Iris takes the reins and whips them on, “and the pair flew on not unwilling” (5.357–366). On Olympus Iris halts the horses, unyokes them and sets ambrosial fodder before them (5.367–369), in the verses of Poseidon's arrival in Book 13 (13.34–35). The goddess who came to rescue her son has to be rescued in a borrowed chariot (see [[divine-rescue]] and [[aphrodite]]).

**Hera.** The second journey is the fullest in the poem. Hera harnesses the horses herself, and Hebe fits the wheels to the chariot, which is described part by part in gold, silver and bronze (5.720–732; see [[chariot]]). Athena arms and mounts with her spear (5.733–747; see [[arming-scene]]). Hera touches the horses with the whip, and the gates of heaven, kept by the Hours, open of themselves (5.748–752; see [[hours]]). The journey is broken by a stop: they find Zeus sitting apart on the highest peak of Olympus, and Hera halts the horses to ask his leave to strike Ares (5.753–766). Then she whips them again, and they fly between earth and sky, each bound as far as a man on a lookout can see over the sea (5.767–772; see [[the-lookout]] and [[longinus-horses-of-the-gods]]). At the meeting of the Simoeis and the Scamander she halts them, unyokes them and pours a thick mist around them, and the Simoeis makes ambrosia spring up for them to graze (5.773–777; see [[ambrosia]] and [[simoeis]]).

**Repeated.** In Book 8 the two goddesses set out again in the same verses: the harnessing, the arming without the aegis and the helmet, and the gates of the Hours (8.382–396 ~ 5.720–721, 5.733–737, 5.745–752). This time Zeus sees them from Ida and sends Iris to turn them back (8.397–437).
