Book 1 named Leto only as Apollo's mother (1.9, 1.36). Here she acts, with Artemis, in her son's temple, and the three appear together for the first time: Apollo brings the wounded man, and his mother and sister tend him (see [[apollo]], [[artemis]] and [[pergamus]]). ἀκέοντο, “healed”, is the verb of Paeëon's cure of Hades later in Dione's speech (ἠκέσατο, 5.402; see [[healing]]). κύδαινον, “gave him glory”, is from κῦδος (see [[kydos]]). They send him back not only whole but splendid, “alive and unhurt and with his might unimpaired” (5.515–516).

The Iliad keeps the three together on the Trojan side. In the battle of the gods Apollo, Artemis and Leto go to the Trojans (20.38–40), Leto faces Hermes, who will not fight her, and she gathers up the arrows of her daughter after Hera has beaten her (20.72, 21.497–504).
