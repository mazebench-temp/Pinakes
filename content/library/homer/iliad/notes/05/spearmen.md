---
summary: The word names three men in Book 5: Lycaon, “the old spearman,” in his son's regrets; Hector, as Diomedes remembers how the Achaeans admired him; and the Aetolian Trechus, killed by Hector and Ares.
grc: [αἰχμητὰ]
---

Pandarus remembers his father's advice: “Truly the old spearman Lycaon gave me many instructions as I was setting out,” to take his horses and chariot to Troy, and he did not listen (5.197–205; [[lycaon]], [[pandarus]]). The old man is named as a spearman; the son came as an archer and regrets it. Diomedes, giving ground before Ares, recalls how the Achaeans used to marvel “at brilliant Hector for being a spearman and a bold warrior,” and explains that a god is always at his side (5.601–604; [[hector]], [[tharsos]]). In the list of Hector and Ares' victims the title goes to one man, “Trechus, the Aetolian spearman” (5.706; [[trechus]], [[aetolians]]).
