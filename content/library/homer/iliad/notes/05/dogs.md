---
summary: Dogs appear in Book 5 only in Sarpedon's rebuke of Hector. The Trojan princes “cower like dogs around a lion” and leave the fighting to the allies (5.476).
---

**Around a lion.** Sarpedon asks Hector where the brothers and brothers-in-law are with whom he boasted he would hold the city alone: “they cower like dogs around a lion” (5.475–476; see [[dogs-around-a-lion]]). The dogs of the lion similes are herdsmen's and hunters' dogs. They rush at the lion in Book 3 and do not stop it (3.26; see [[lion-on-a-carcass]]), and on the shield of Achilles they bark at two lions from close by but shrink from biting them (18.585–586). Sarpedon's dogs do not even bark. The insult is in the verb, καταπτώσσω, “cower”, the reproach that Agamemnon threw at the Achaeans in Book 4 (4.340, 4.371).

**The helmet.** The word for the helmets of Book 5's gods, κυνέη, is formed from κύων: it was once a cap of dogskin. Athena puts on a golden κυνέη when she arms (5.743), and she wears the κυνέη of Hades, the helmet that makes her invisible to Ares (5.845; see [[helmet]] and [[helmet-of-hades]]).
