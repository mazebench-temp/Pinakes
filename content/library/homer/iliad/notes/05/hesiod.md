---
summary: Hesiod gives Aphrodite another birth, from the sea foam, and makes Dione an Oceanid; his Hours are daughters of Zeus and Themis, where Homer's keep the gates of heaven; and in the Shield ascribed to him Perseus wears the helmet of Hades that Athena puts on in Book 5.
---

- **Aphrodite's birth.** In the Iliad Aphrodite is the daughter of Zeus and Dione (5.370–371). Hesiod has her born from the foam that rose around the severed genitals of Uranus, and names her from the foam, ἀφρός. He also calls her φιλομμηδής, “lover of genitals”, a play on her epithet φιλομμειδής, “laughter-loving” (*Theogony* 188–206; 5.375). His Dione is a daughter of Oceanus (*Theogony* 353). Plato's *Symposium* made the two births into two goddesses: [[plato-two-aphrodites]]. See [[dione]].
- **The Hours.** In Book 5 the Hours keep the gates of heaven and open and close the thick cloud (5.749–751). Hesiod makes them daughters of Zeus and Themis, named Eunomia, Dike and Eirene, Good Order, Justice and Peace, who watch over the works of mortal men (*Theogony* 901–903). See [[hours]].
- **Rout.** Rout is set around the aegis as a crown (5.739). Hesiod makes Rout and Terror sons of Ares and Aphrodite (*Theogony* 933–936). See [[rout]].
- **The Gorgon.** Homer puts the Gorgon's head on Athena's aegis (5.741). Hesiod names the three Gorgons, Stheno, Euryale and Medusa, and tells how Perseus cut off Medusa's head (*Theogony* 274–281). See [[gorgon]] and [[aristotle-gorgon]].
- **The helmet of Hades.** Athena puts it on so that Ares will not see her (5.845). In the *Shield*, Perseus wears it as he flies from the Gorgons with Medusa's head, and it holds “the dread darkness of night” ([Hesiod], *Shield* 216–237, 226–227). See [[helmet-of-hades]] and [[plato-helmet-of-hades]].
- **Achlys.** The mist of death over the eyes (5.696) is on the *Shield* a figure among the powers of death, pale and wasted with hunger ([Hesiod], *Shield* 264–270). See [[achlys]].
