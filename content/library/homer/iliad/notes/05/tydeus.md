**The name.** Book 5 is the first to call Diomedes Τυδεΐδης, “son of Tydeus”, and it does so twenty-five times, besides ten phrases like Τυδέος υἱός. The father is named in almost every scene of the son's aristeia (see [[son-of-tydeus]] and [[diomedes]]).

**The father's might.** Wounded, Diomedes prays to Athena “if ever in friendship you stood by my father and me” (5.116). She answers that she has put in his breast “your father's might, undaunted, such as the horseman Tydeus, wielder of the shield, used to have” (5.125–126). σακέσπαλος, “shield-wielder” (σάκος, “shield”, and πάλλω, “brandish”), is found only here in Homer. The might is the father's own, and the son fights with it for the rest of the book (see [[menos]]).

**Athena's story.**

{{quote:cf050df9-3e88-5a9d-92e1-9c511c8e4374}}

She finds Diomedes resting and rebukes him as Agamemnon did in Book 4, and in many of the same words. The Cadmeians are “many” in both tellings (πολέας … Καδμεΐωνας, 4.385; πολέας μετὰ Καδμείωνας, 5.804). Tydeus challenged them and won everything easily, “such a helper was Athena to him” (4.389–390); in her mouth the line becomes “such a helper was I to him” (5.807–808; see [[epitarrhothos]]). Her verdict repeats the king's with the same verb, γείνατο: Tydeus fathered a son little like himself (5.800; 4.399–400). But Agamemnon knew Tydeus only by report (4.374–375), and the goddess was there.

She adds two things the king did not say. Tydeus was “small in build” (μικρὸς … δέμας, 5.801), the only word in Homer about his body (see [[small-but-a-fighter]]). And she herself had told him to feast at his ease and not to fight, but he challenged the young Cadmeians anyway (5.802–807). She leaves out the ambush and the fifty dead (4.391–398). Her Tydeus won against her advice and with her help. The comparison is hardly fair, and Diomedes' answer shows it: he is holding back because he obeys her (5.815–824).

The rebuke ends with the lineage: “then you are no offspring of Tydeus, the wise-hearted son of Oeneus” (5.812–813). Οἰνεΐδης, “son of Oeneus”, is Tydeus' own patronymic; its only other use is in Book 10, where Diomedes is Οἰνεΐδαο πάϊς, “the son of Oeneus' son” (10.497; see [[oeneus]] and [[wise-hearted]]). When Athena releases Diomedes to fight Ares, she tells him what she was to his father: “such a helper am I to you” (5.828). See [[tydeus-at-thebes]] and [[fathers-and-sons]].
