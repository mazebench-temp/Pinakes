The fourth of the six leaders' kills when the Trojans give way (5.37–83; see [[androktasia]]). Meriones' man is the builder of the ships in which Alexander sailed for Helen, “the balanced ships that began the evil”, a craftsman whom Athena loved above all others (5.59–64; see [[phereclus]]). Meriones “caught up with him in pursuit” (5.65) and struck him from behind.

The wound is one he gives again. In Book 13 Harpalion, son of the Paphlagonian king Pylaemenes, turns back from an attack on Menelaus, and Meriones shoots him as he goes, “in the right buttock”, and the arrow passes “under the bone and into the bladder” (13.650–652): the same words as here. Pylaemenes himself is killed later in this book by Menelaus (5.576–579), and in Book 13 he follows his son's body, weeping (see [[pylaemenes]]).

Meriones' part in Book 5 ends here. In Book 16 he kills Acamas with the verse that told Idomeneus' kill in this book (16.343 = 5.46; see [[idomeneus]]).
