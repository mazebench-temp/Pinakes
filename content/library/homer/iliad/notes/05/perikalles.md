---
summary: The beautiful things of Book 5 are a chariot abandoned by a frightened brother, a stepmother who saves Ares, and the oak of Zeus under which Sarpedon is laid.
grc: [περικαλλὴς]
---

Idaeus, son of Dares, sees his brother killed and runs, “leaving the beautiful chariot” (5.20; [[idaeus-son-of-dares]]). In Dione's story the Aloadae's stepmother, “the beautiful Eeriboea,” tells Hermes where Ares lies bound, and saves him (5.389; [[eeriboea]], [[the-aloadae-bind-ares]]). And Sarpedon's companions set him down, wounded, “under the beautiful oak of aegis-bearing Zeus” (5.693; [[the-oak-of-zeus]]). Each time the fine thing stands beside a rescue: Hephaestus saves Idaeus once he has left the chariot, Eeriboea saves Ares, and the oak shelters the wounded son of Zeus.
