---
summary: Book 5 gives the adjective to the gods' own things: Aphrodite's robe, made by the Graces, through which Diomedes' spear passes, and the fodder of Ares' horses on Olympus.
grc: [ἀμβροσίου, ἀμβρόσιον]
---

Diomedes' spear pierces Aphrodite's skin “through the ambrosial robe that the Graces themselves had made for her” (5.338; [[graces]], [[robe]]). The next verse names what flows from the wound, the goddess's “immortal blood,” ἄμβροτον αἷμα, from the same root as ἀμβρόσιος ([[ichor]]). The divine robe does not keep the mortal's spear from the divine body.

On Olympus Iris unyokes Ares' horses and sets “ambrosial fodder” before them (5.369; [[with-golden-headbands]]), and at the end of the book the Simoeis makes ambrosia itself spring up for Hera's horses to graze on (5.777; [[ambrosia]], [[simoeis]]). The gods' horses eat as the gods do.
