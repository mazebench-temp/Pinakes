summary: When the wounded Aphrodite reaches Olympus, Athena and Hera mock her with cutting words, and Zeus smiles. The goddess of laughter is laughed at, and her wound becomes a joke among the gods.

---

Aphrodite, who has just been wounded by a mortal, answers her mother as φιλομμειδής, “laughter-loving” (5.375; see [[laughter-loving]]). The epithet is a standing one, and here it is given to a goddess in pain. The laughter of the scene is at her expense. While Dione heals her, Athena and Hera look on and provoke Zeus “with cutting words” (5.418–419; see [[kertomia]]), and Athena makes the joke:

{{quote:aa77859a-d270-5011-98d6-856cb8b793c5}}

The wound that Diomedes' spear made through the robe of the Graces becomes a scratch from a woman's brooch, and the war becomes a seduction. “So she spoke, and the father of men and gods smiled” (5.426; see [[smiling-at-him]]). Zeus' smile settles the matter as the gods' laughter settled Book 1: he calls his daughter and tells her that the works of war are not for her (5.428–430; see [[the-works-of-war]]). No one laughs at Ares when he comes up wounded at the end of the book; Zeus meets him with anger.

The comedy returns in the battle of the gods. Athena fells Ares with a stone and laughs over him, and then strikes down Aphrodite as she leads him away; Hera smiles as she boxes Artemis' ears with her own bow (21.403–426, 21.489–492).
