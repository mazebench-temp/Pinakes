---
summary: Diomedes goes after Aphrodite “with the pitiless bronze,” knowing she is a feeble goddess.
---

“But he was going after Cypris with the pitiless bronze, knowing that she was a feeble god, and not one of those goddesses who rule over the war of men” (5.330–332; [[aphrodite]], [[diomedes-wounds-aphrodite]]). In Book 3 the formula described the knife that cut the throats of the oath lambs. Here it is turned on a goddess, and a few lines later the bronze pierces her hand (5.336–339). The weapon that has no pity for men has none for a god.
