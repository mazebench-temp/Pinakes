---
summary: Aphrodite complains that it is no longer the dread din of battle between Trojans and Achaeans, since the Danaans now fight the gods; and Hector, rebuked by Sarpedon, awakes it again.
---

The wounded Aphrodite uses the formula in its full half-verse to measure how far things have gone: “For it is no longer the dread din of battle between Trojans and Achaeans; now the Danaans are fighting even with the immortals” (5.379–380; [[immortals]], [[aphrodite]]). The war of men has become a war on gods. The second use is the war of men renewed. Stung by Sarpedon's rebuke, Hector leaps from his chariot and goes through the army, “urging them to fight, and he awoke the dread din of battle” (5.494–496; [[hector]], [[exhortation]]).
