summary: Aeneas asks Pandarus where his feathered arrows are, and around the phantom of Aeneas the armies hack at “fluttering” bucklers, the other use of πτερόεις.
grc: [πτερόεντες ὀϊστοὶ, λαισήϊά τε πτερόεντα]
en: [fluttering]
---

Aeneas finds Pandarus and asks the question that sets up the archer's last scene: “Pandarus, where are your bow and your feathered arrows and your fame?” (5.171–172; [[pandarus]], [[kleos]]). Pandarus answers that his arrows have drawn blood from two chieftains without killing either, and that he will break his bow if he gets home (5.206–216; [[bow]], [[swift-shaft]]).

The same adjective describes the λαισήϊα, light shields of hide, in the fight over the phantom of Aeneas: “the well-rounded shields and the fluttering bucklers of hide” (5.453; [[well-rounded]], [[the-phantom-of-aeneas]]). Whether they are “winged” for their lightness or for fringes that flutter as a man runs is not known, and the translation keeps “feathered” for arrows and gives the shields “fluttering.”
