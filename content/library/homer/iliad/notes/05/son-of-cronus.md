---
summary: The patronymic names Zeus as the goddesses provoke him and as Hera finds him on Olympus; Ares, wounded and then healed, twice sits down beside the son of Cronus.
---

Athena and Hera, watching Aphrodite comforted, “provoked Zeus, son of Cronus, with cutting words” (5.418–419; [[kertomia]]). In the simile of the steadfast Danaans the clouds are those that “the son of Cronus has set in windless weather on the tops of the mountains” (5.522–523; [[clouds-on-the-mountains]]). Hera finds “the son of Cronus sitting apart from the other gods” and asks “Zeus, the most high son of Cronus” for leave to strike Ares (5.753–756; [[most-high]], [[many-ridged-olympus]]).

The last two uses frame Ares' return. Wounded, he “sat down beside Zeus, son of Cronus, grieving in his heart” (5.869); healed, “he sat down beside Zeus, son of Cronus, exulting in his glory” (5.906; [[exulting-in-glory]]). The two verses differ only in their last words, and the difference is the healing between them.
