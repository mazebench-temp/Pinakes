---
summary: Death covers the men of Book 5, “purple death and mighty fate”, “the end of death”, while two wounded gods are healed, “for he was not made to be mortal”.
grc: [καταθνητός]
en: [mortal]
---

**Death that covers.** Death in Book 5 comes over a man like a covering. Phereclus falls to his knees with a groan, “and death enfolded him” (θάνατος δέ μιν ἀμφεκάλυψε, 5.68). Hypsenor's eyes “were seized by purple death and mighty fate” (5.83; see [[purple-death]] and [[moira]]). Of the twin sons of Diocles the poet says, “there the end of death covered them both” (τέλος θανάτοιο κάλυψεν, 5.553). The same half-line will cover Sarpedon, Patroclus and Hector at the ends of their dying speeches (16.502, 16.855, 22.361). Darkness seizes the dying and night covers their eyes (5.47, 5.659; see [[darkness-covered-his-eyes]]).

**Not made to be mortal.** The gods wounded in Book 5 are healed at once, and the poet gives the reason in the same verse for Hades and for Ares: Paeëon spread pain-killing drugs on the wound “and healed him; for he was not made to be mortal” (οὐ μὲν γάρ τι καταθνητός γε τέτυκτο, 5.401–402 = 5.900–901). καταθνητός, “mortal”, is a strengthened θνητός. A mortal's wound can be the end; a god's is only pain. The book gives the gods' deathlessness a physical reason, too: they eat no bread and drink no wine, and so they are bloodless, “and are called immortals” (ἀθάνατοι, 5.341–342; see [[ichor]] and [[immortals]]). See [[gods-wounded-by-mortals]].
