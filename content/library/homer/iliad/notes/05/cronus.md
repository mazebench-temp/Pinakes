Ἥρη πρέσβα θεὰ θυγάτηρ μεγάλοιο Κρόνοιο, “Hera, the august goddess, daughter of great Cronus” (5.721). The line names her father as she harnesses her horses to go against Ares. It returns when she harnesses them again in Book 8 (8.383), and Aphrodite addresses her with it, in the vocative, when Hera comes to ask her help (14.194). See [[hera]].

Zeus is named through his father six times: Κρονίδης (5.419, 5.756) and Κρονίων (5.522, 5.753, 5.869, 5.906; see [[son-of-cronus]]). In a simile the son of Cronus sets the clouds motionless on the mountain tops (5.522–523).

Cronus himself is not named in Zeus' threat to Ares, but he is in it. Had he been born of any other god, Zeus says, Ares would long ago have been “lower down than the sons of Uranus” (5.898): the Titans, “the gods below, around Cronus” (15.225; so 14.274, 14.279), whom Zeus set beneath the earth and the sea (14.203–204). See [[fall-of-cronus]] and [[heavenly-gods]].
