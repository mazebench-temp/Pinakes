---
summary: Book 5 shows the battle line at work. Diomedes throws the Trojan battalions into confusion, the champions fight in front, the Achaeans stand firm like motionless clouds, and an army gives ground in order, facing the enemy, before a god.
grc: [προμάχοισιν]
passages:
  - 5.519-527 | The Achaeans stand like clouds
  - 5.600-606 | Give ground, facing the Trojans
  - 5.699-702 | The Argives give ground
---

**Battalions in confusion.** Athena sends Diomedes “into the middle, where the most men were thrown into confusion” (5.8), and as he sweeps across the plain like a river in flood, “the close-packed battalions of the Trojans were thrown into confusion” (5.93–94); Pandarus sees him “driving the battalions in confusion before him” (5.96). κλονέω, “throw into confusion, drive in a rout”, is the verb of a broken line (see [[river-in-flood]]).

**The champions.** The πρόμαχοι fight in front of the ranks. Diomedes, strengthened by Athena, goes back and “mingled with the champions” (5.134), and Sthenelus begs him not to “rush like this among the champions” (5.250). Menelaus, Antilochus and Hector each go “through the champions” to reach the fighting (5.562, 5.566, 5.681). Agamemnon kills Deicoon, “a man in the front rank”, whom the Trojans honored “since he was swift to fight among the foremost” (5.533–536).

**Standing firm.** When Hector rallies the Trojans, “the Argives held their ground in a body and were not put to flight” (5.498), and they stood “like clouds that the son of Cronus has set in windless weather on the tops of the mountains, motionless” (5.522–524; see [[clouds-on-the-mountains]]). Agamemnon goes through the line with his exhortation to stand together in shame (5.528–532; see [[exhortation]]).

**Giving ground in order.** When Diomedes sees Ares beside Hector, he orders a retreat that is not a rout: “keep your faces turned toward the Trojans and give ground always backward” (5.605–606). The army does it: “the Argives, before Ares and Hector armed in bronze, neither turned and fled to the black ships nor ever charged against them in battle, but kept giving ground backward” (5.699–702). To turn one's back was to be killed from behind, as four of the six Trojans killed at the start of the book were (5.40, 5.56, 5.65–66, 5.80).

**Ranks.** Ares goes “among the ranks of the Trojans” to rouse them (5.461), Hector sees Menelaus and Antilochus “across the ranks”, and the Trojan battalions follow him (5.590–591), and Athena's spear is the one “with which she overpowers the ranks of men” (5.746). See [[battle-order]].
