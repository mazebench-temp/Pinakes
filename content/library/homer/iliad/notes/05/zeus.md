summary: Zeus does not come down to the battle in Book 5, but men and gods invoke him throughout. On Olympus he smiles at Aphrodite's wound and sends her back to the works of marriage, gives Hera leave to set Athena on Ares, and rebukes the wounded Ares as the most hateful of the gods before he has him healed.

**Invoked.** Zeus stays on Olympus in Book 5, and the men and gods below speak of him constantly. Athena leads Ares out of the fighting to leave the glory to the side Zeus chooses and “avoid the wrath of Zeus” (5.33–34; see [[menis]] and [[kydos]]). Aeneas trusts his horses to bring them safe to the city even if Zeus holds out glory to Diomedes again (5.224–225); they are of the breed Zeus gave to Tros in recompense for his son Ganymede (5.265–267; see [[tros]] and [[ganymede]]). Twice Diomedes is said to be ready to “fight even with father Zeus” (5.362, 5.457). Zeus' own son and grandson meet in battle, Sarpedon and Tlepolemus, son of Heracles (5.631), and Sarpedon's father “still kept ruin away” from him (5.662). His companions lay him under the oak of Zeus (5.693; see [[the-oak-of-zeus]]).

**The smile.** Athena and Hera provoke him with a joke about the wounded Aphrodite (5.418–425), “and the father of men and gods smiled” (5.426; see [[smiling-at-him]]). He calls his daughter to him:

{{quote:5138b2fe-7b43-5b65-8872-95dd551a8a6f}}

The father gives each child a domain: marriage to Aphrodite, and war to Ares and Athena. See [[the-works-of-war]], [[fathers-and-daughters]] and [[the-gods-mirror-the-mortals]].

**Hera's request.** The two goddesses find him “sitting apart from the other gods on the topmost peak of many-ridged Olympus” (5.753–754), where Thetis found him in Book 1 (1.498–499). He grants what Hera asks and names the goddess for it: “Come then, rouse Athena, driver of spoil, against him, who more than anyone is used to bringing evil pains upon him” (5.765–766; see [[driver-of-spoil]]).

**The rebuke.** Wounded, Ares sits beside him and accuses him: “We are all at war with you; for you begot that senseless daughter” (5.875). Zeus answers him “looking at him darkly” (5.888; see [[looking-darkly]]), in the words Agamemnon used to Achilles: “most hateful to me … strife is always dear to you, and wars and battles” (5.890–891 = 1.176–177; see [[strife-is-always-dear]]). The king of the gods speaks to his son as the king of the Achaeans spoke to his best warrior. Then the father relents:

{{quote:c31d0de1-b7d3-5791-955d-fd6fa9a0f160}}

“The sons of Uranus” are the Titans, the gods below the earth around Cronus, where Zeus had put his father (14.203–204, 14.274–279, 15.225; see [[heavenly-gods]] and [[cronus]]). Elsewhere Zeus threatens any god who disobeys him with Tartarus, as far below Hades as heaven is above the earth (8.13–16). Here he orders Paeëon to heal his son (5.899; see [[paeeon]]). His daughter Aphrodite was smiled at and sent back to her own work; his son Ares is scolded, healed, and seated beside him again (5.906; see [[ares]]).
