summary: Phereclus built for Alexander “the balanced ships that began the evil” (5.62–63), and he dies for it. Captured horses are driven to the Achaean ships, Tlepolemus boasts that Heracles sacked Troy with only six, and Hera, rebuking the Argives, says that the Trojans now fight by the hollow ships (5.791).

**The ships that began the evil.** Meriones kills Phereclus, the son of the builder, whom Athena loved above all others for his skill:

{{quote:cb7137e3-5623-5455-9d45-c133625056b4}}

ἀρχέκακος, “beginning evils”, occurs only here (see [[the-beginning-of-evils]]). The ships are ἐΐσαι, “balanced”, the standing epithet of well-built ships (see [[balanced-ships]]). They are the ships that carried Paris to Sparta and Helen to Troy, and they began the war in which their builder dies, struck from behind as he runs (5.65–68; see [[phereclus]] and [[the-abduction-of-helen]]). The Iliad does not tell the voyage.

**To the ships.** The ships are the Achaeans' camp and storehouse, and the spoils of the field go there. Diomedes' companions drive the captured horses “down to the hollow ships” and “to the ships” (5.26, 5.165), and Deipylus takes the horses of Aeneas “to the hollow ships” (5.327; see [[stripping-the-dead]]).

**Six ships.** Tlepolemus tells Sarpedon that Heracles came to Troy for Laomedon's horses “with only six ships and fewer men, and sacked the city of Ilios, and widowed its streets” (5.641–642; see [[heracles-sacks-troy]]). The Catalogue counted the ships of the present army by the hundred (see [[catalogue-of-ships]]).

**By the hollow ships.** The twin sons of Diocles came to Ilios “on the black ships” with the Argives (5.550–551), and the Argives, giving ground before Hector and Ares, do not turn and flee “to the black ships” (5.700). Hera, in the likeness of Stentor, shames them: while Achilles fought, the Trojans never came out before the Dardanian gates, “but now they are fighting far from the city, by the hollow ships” (5.788–791; see [[exhortation]]). It is a rebuke's exaggeration. The fighting of Book 5 is still on the plain, and the Trojans will fight at the ships only after they break through the wall at the end of Book 12.

At 5.446 νηός is Apollo's temple, not the genitive of νηῦς, “ship” (see [[temple]]).
