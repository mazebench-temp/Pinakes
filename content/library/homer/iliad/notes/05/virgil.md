---
summary: The Aeneid remembers Iliad 5 from the Trojan side. Aeneas wishes he had died at Diomedes' hands; Diomedes, settled in Italy, will not fight Aeneas again after paying for his wound to Venus; Venus clears Aeneas' eyes as Athena cleared Diomedes'; and Aeneas' enemies sneer at the rescues of Book 5.
---

- Aeneas' wish to have died at Diomedes' hands (*Aeneid* 1.94–101), and Diomedes' refusal to fight him again (*Aeneid* 11.225–295): [[virgils-diomedes]].
- The companions of Diomedes turned into birds (*Aeneid* 11.271–274): [[birds-of-diomedes]].
- Venus takes the cloud from Aeneas' eyes and shows him the gods destroying Troy (*Aeneid* 2.604–623): [[mist-from-the-eyes]].
- **The rescue as a reproach.** In the council of the gods Juno taunts Venus: “you can snatch Aeneas from the hands of the Greeks, and put forward a mist and empty winds in place of the man” (*Aeneid* 10.81–82). Turnus, demanding his duel with Aeneas, tells King Latinus that this time Aeneas' goddess mother will be far away, and will not hide the fugitive in a woman's cloud and conceal herself in empty shadows (*Aeneid* 12.52–53). Both remember Book 5, where Aphrodite carries her son out of the battle, Apollo hides him in a dark cloud, and a phantom takes his place on the field (5.311–318, 5.343–346, 5.445–453; see [[the-phantom-of-aeneas]]).
