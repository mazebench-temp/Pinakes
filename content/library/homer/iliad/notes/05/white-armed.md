summary: Hera is “white-armed” five times in the last part of Book 5, as she sees the Argives killed, halts her horses twice, obeys Zeus, and shouts in Stentor's likeness; and Aphrodite throws her white arms around Aeneas.
grc: [πήχεε λευκώ]
en: [white arms]
---

The formula θεὰ λευκώλενος Ἥρη names Hera at each stage of her intervention. She sees the Argives killed by Ares and Hector (5.711), halts her horses before Zeus (5.755), obeys his permission and whips them on (5.767), halts them again where the Simoeis and the Scamander meet (5.775), and shouts to the Argives in the likeness of Stentor (5.784; [[bronze-voiced]]). In the whole episode she is never the ox-eyed queen; she is the white-armed helper of the Achaeans, as in Book 1 ([[ox-eyed]]).

The compound's two parts appear separately in another verse, when Aphrodite saves her son: “and she flung her white arms around her dear son” (πήχεε λευκώ, 5.314; [[aphrodite]], [[divine-rescue]]). A few lines later Diomedes' spear pierces her hand at the wrist, and she lets her son fall (5.336–343).
