---
summary: Pandarus regrets that he did not listen to his father, Diomedes refuses to be persuaded to retreat, the goddesses do not disobey one another, and Ares complains that every god obeys Zeus except the daughter he will not restrain.
grc: [πιθόμην, πεισέμεν, ἐπιπείθονται]
en: [did not listen]
---

**Advice not taken.** Pandarus tells Aeneas that his old father Lycaon gave him many instructions as he set out, to take his horses and chariot to Troy, “but I did not listen—it would have been far better” (ἀλλʼ ἐγὼ οὐ πιθόμην· ἦ τʼ ἂν πολὺ κέρδιον ἦεν, 5.197–201; see [[lycaon]]). He came on foot, trusting in his bow, and the bow has failed him. The son who would not be persuaded pays for it, as the sons of Merops did (2.830–834; see [[bereaved-parents]]).

**Persuasion refused.** When Sthenelus begs him to draw back in the chariot, Diomedes answers, “Do not talk to me of flight, for I do not think you will persuade me” (οὐδὲ σὲ πεισέμεν οἴω, 5.252). The refusal is right; he kills Pandarus and wounds Aeneas.

**Obedience among the gods.** Hera proposes that she and Athena go down to the battle, “and the gray-eyed goddess Athena did not disobey” (5.719); Zeus tells Hera to rouse Athena against Ares, “and the white-armed goddess Hera did not disobey” (5.767). Ares, wounded, complains to Zeus that the order of Olympus has one exception: “all the others, all the gods there are on Olympus, obey you (ἐπιπείθονται), and each of us is subject to you; but her you never check, by word or by deed” (5.877–879). See [[obedience-and-persuasion]].
