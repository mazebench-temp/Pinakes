summary: The epithet goes to a wife, Aegialeia, “the strong wife of Diomedes,” and to three men: Ajax, driven back from Amphius, Sarpedon, whom Odysseus is not fated to kill, and Sarpedon's companion Pelagon.
grc: [ἰφθίμη, ἴφθιμος]
en: [strong]
---

Dione ends her warning with the wife Diomedes will leave behind: Aegialeia, “the wise daughter of Adrastus,” longing for her wedded husband, “the strong wife of Diomedes, tamer of horses” (5.412–415; [[periphron]], [[tamer-of-horses]]). The epithet of a wife of rank is ἰφθίμη, here rendered “strong”; the Odyssey gives it to Penelope (Od. 16.332, 23.92).

Of men it keeps its sense of strength. Telamonian Ajax, “big and mighty and noble as he was,” is pushed away from the body of Amphius by the Trojans (5.625; [[noble]]). It was not fated for Odysseus “to kill the mighty son of Zeus” (5.674–675; [[son-of-zeus]]), and it is Sarpedon's companion, “mighty Pelagon,” who draws the spear from his thigh under the oak of Zeus (5.694–695; [[pelagon-of-lycia]], [[ash-spear]]).
