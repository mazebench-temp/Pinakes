In Book 4 the Lycians were only a name in Agamemnon's mouth (4.197). In Book 5 they fight. Sarpedon, “leader of the Lycians” (5.647), speaks for them as allies: “But even so I urge on the Lycians, and am eager myself to fight my man” (5.482–483), though they have nothing at Troy to lose (see [[sarpedon]] and [[trojan-allies]]). Tlepolemus calls him “counselor of the Lycians” in mockery, and tells him that his people are dwindling away (5.633, 5.643).

When Sarpedon is wounded, his “brilliant companions” carry him out of the war and lay him under the oak of Zeus, where Pelagon draws out the spear (5.663–667, 5.692–695; see [[pelagon-of-lycia]] and [[the-oak-of-zeus]]). Then Odysseus, kept from Sarpedon, turns “against the multitude of the Lycians” and kills seven:
