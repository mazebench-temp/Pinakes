---
summary: In Book 5 the words of swiftness belong to horses: the swift horses of Aeneas and Pandarus, the swift-footed horses that shy when Pandarus falls, and Hera's swift-footed team.
grc: [ὠκέες ἵπποι, ὠκύποδες, ὠκύποδας]
---

Achilles is absent from Book 5, named only in Hera's rebuke of the Argives, where he is “brilliant” (5.788; [[brilliant]]). The adjectives of his speed describe horses. Aeneas and Pandarus drive “the swift horses” at Diomedes (5.240, 5.275), and Diomedes tells Sthenelus that “their swift horses will not carry these two back again” and to hold “these swift horses of ours” while he fights (5.257–262; [[master-of-rout]]). When Pandarus falls from Aeneas' chariot, “his horses shied aside, the swift-footed horses” (5.295–296). Hera leads under the golden yoke “the swift-footed horses, eager for strife and the war cry” (5.731–732; [[august-goddess]]). The horses of the gods and the horses of men share the epithet.
