summary: Ares takes his likeness to rouse the sons of Priam, “in the likeness of Acamas, the swift leader of the Thracians” (5.462).
# ^: ἀκάμαντι “tireless” is a common adjective (16.176).
grc: ["^Ἀκάμαντι"]
---

The gods often come among the Trojans in the likeness of one of their own. Iris takes the voice of Polites, a son of Priam (2.791), and Athena the likeness of Laodocus, a son of Antenor (4.86–87). Ares chooses an ally, the leader of the Thracians, and speaks as an ally would: how long will the sons of Priam let their people be killed by the Achaeans (5.463–469; see [[sons-of-priam]])? The next speech, Sarpedon's, makes the same charge from a real ally (5.471–492; see [[sarpedon]] and [[trojan-allies]]). See [[ares]] and [[divine-epiphany]].

Acamas himself has no part in Book 5. At the start of Book 6 Telamonian Ajax kills him, “the man who was the best among the Thracians”, with a spear through the forehead (6.5–11).
