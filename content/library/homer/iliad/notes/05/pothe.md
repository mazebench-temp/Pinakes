summary: Aegialeia, in Dione's threat, wails for her husband, “longing for her wedded husband, the best of the Achaeans”; and Pandarus fears that Aeneas' horses, missing their driver's voice, will balk.
grc: [ποθέοντε, ποθέουσα]
en: [longing for, missing]
---

The verb ποθέω is longing for what is absent, and Book 5 uses it of a wife and of a pair of horses. Dione tells Aphrodite that Diomedes had better take care that no one stronger fights him, or his wife Aegialeia will long wake her household from sleep with her wailing, “longing for her wedded husband, the best of the Achaeans” (κουρίδιον ποθέουσα πόσιν τὸν ἄριστον Ἀχαιῶν, 5.412–414; see [[aegialeia]] and [[posis]]). The longing of a wife for a husband dead in war is Dione's threat.

The same word is given to horses. When Aeneas offers Pandarus the reins, Pandarus refuses: the horses may take fright and balk, and refuse to carry them out of the fighting, “missing your voice” (τεὸν φθόγγον ποθέοντε, 5.231–234). The horses of Tros know their driver, and miss him when he is gone. Achilles' horses, weeping for Patroclus, are the Iliad's great example (17.426–440).
