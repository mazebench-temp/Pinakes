summary: Oeneus is named once, as the father of Tydeus, when Athena rebukes Diomedes: “then you are no offspring of Tydeus, the wise-hearted son of Oeneus” (5.812–813).
grc: [Οἰνεΐδαο]
relations:
  - child: tydeus (5.813)
---

Οἰνεΐδαο is the genitive of the patronymic Οἰνεΐδης, “son of Oeneus”, and here it names Tydeus. The line spans three generations: Diomedes, his father Tydeus, and Tydeus' father Oeneus. It is the first time the Iliad connects Oeneus with Tydeus. The Catalogue named him only to say that he and his sons were dead (2.641–642), and Diomedes tells the whole descent in Book 14, from Oeneus' father Portheus to Tydeus' move from Calydon to Argos (14.113–121). Later in Book 5 Ares kills the Aetolian Periphas, a man of Oeneus' people (5.842–843; see [[periphas]]). See [[tydeus]].
