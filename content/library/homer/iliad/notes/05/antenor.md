Book 5 opens a window on Antenor's household. Pedaeus is Ἀντήνορος υἱόν, “son of Antenor”, but a νόθος, born to him by a woman who was not his wife (see [[nothos]]). His wife Theano, named here for the first time, raised the boy “like her own dear children, to please her husband” (χαριζομένη πόσεϊ ᾧ, 5.71). The house of the elder who will counsel giving Helen back (7.347–353) holds his children by more than one woman, and his wife raises them all as her own. In Book 6 Theano appears as the priestess of Athena, “the wife of Antenor, tamer of horses” (6.298–300; see [[theano]]).

Pedaeus is the first of Antenor's sons to die in the poem. In Book 11 Agamemnon kills Iphidamas, Theano's son, and then his elder brother Coon, one after the other (11.221–263). See [[pedaeus]] and [[meges]].
