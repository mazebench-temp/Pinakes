---
summary: Three replies of Book 5 are framed by the formula, all spoken by goddesses. Aphrodite and Dione answer each other on Olympus (5.375, 5.381), and Athena answers Diomedes and sends him against Ares (5.825).
---

**Mother and daughter.** Two verses of the formula follow each other in the dialogue on Olympus. Dione asks who has hurt her child, and “then laughter-loving Aphrodite answered her” (5.375): the wounded goddess keeps her standing epithet, φιλομμειδής, “laughter-loving”, while she tells of her wound (see [[laughter-loving]]). Aphrodite complains that the Danaans are fighting even the immortals, and “then Dione, brilliant among goddesses, answered her” (5.381), with the longest speech of the scene, the stories of gods who suffered at mortal hands (5.382–415; see [[dione]] and [[mythological-paradigm]]).

**Athena.** Diomedes has explained that he gave ground because he saw Ares, and “then the gray-eyed goddess Athena answered him” (5.825), releasing him from her command and sending him against the god (5.826–834; see [[athena]]).
