---
summary: In the simile of the winnowers, “fair-haired Demeter” separates the grain from the chaff as the winds drive on (5.499–502).
grc: [Δημήτηρ]
---

The goddess appears only in a simile. The Achaeans, whitened by the dust that the horses' hooves beat up, are like the heaps of chaff that grow white on the sacred threshing floors “when men are winnowing, when fair-haired Demeter separates the grain from the chaff as the winds drive on” (5.499–502; see [[winnowing]]). Demeter herself does the work of the wind and the winnowers: the harvest is hers, and so is the sorting of the grain. ξανθή, “fair-haired” or “golden”, fits the goddess of the ripe grain (see [[fair-haired]]).
