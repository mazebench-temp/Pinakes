---
summary: The epithet is given to two fathers, Tydeus in Pandarus' address to his son and Laomedon in Sarpedon's answer to Tlepolemus, and to Ajax, pushed back from Amphius' body.
grc: [ἀγαυοῦ]
---

Two of the three uses name a father in a challenge. Pandarus calls Diomedes “son of noble Tydeus” before his last throw (5.277; [[wise-hearted]]). Sarpedon answers Tlepolemus that Heracles sacked Troy “through the folly of a man, noble Laomedon,” who refused him the horses he had earned (5.648–651; [[laomedon]], [[heracles-sacks-troy]]). There the praise sits beside the blame, a noble man who was a fool. The third use is Telamonian Ajax, driven back from the body of Amphius by many Trojans, “big and mighty and noble as he was” (5.625; [[mighty]], [[ajax]]).
