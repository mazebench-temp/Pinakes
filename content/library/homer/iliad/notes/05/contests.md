---
summary: Athena retells how Tydeus, at the feast of the Cadmeians, challenged their young men “and won every contest easily” (5.807–808), though she had told him to feast quietly.
grc: ["πάντα δʼ ἐνίκα"]
en: [won every contest]
---

Athena rebukes Diomedes with the story Agamemnon told in Book 4. When Tydeus went as a messenger to Thebes, “I told him to feast at his ease in the halls; yet he, with the strong spirit he always had, challenged the young men of the Cadmeians, and won every contest easily; such a helper was I to him” (5.805–808). The last verse and a half are nearly Agamemnon's (4.389–390): his “challenged them to contests” has become “challenged the young men of the Cadmeians”, and his “such a helper was Athena to him” has become “such a helper was I to him”. The new detail is the goddess's own command to feast quietly, which Tydeus ignored and she then made good (see [[tydeus-at-thebes]], [[mythological-paradigm]] and [[epitarrhothos]]). The contests, as in Book 4, are the guest's challenge at his hosts' feast.
