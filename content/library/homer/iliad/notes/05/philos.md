summary: Diomedes asks Athena to be his friend again as she was his father's; Aphrodite calls Aeneas dearest of all to her, and Athena mocks her for the Trojans “whom she now loves beyond measure”.

en: [be my friend]
---

**A god's friendship.** Diomedes' prayer to Athena rests on an old bond: “if ever in friendship you stood by my father and me in destructive war, now be my friend in turn, Athena” (εἴ ποτέ μοι καὶ πατρὶ φίλα φρονέουσα παρέστης … νῦν αὖτʼ ἐμὲ φῖλαι Ἀθήνη, 5.116–117). φῖλαι, “be friendly, love”, asks the goddess to renew a friendship with the family. She does, and at the end of the book she names the bond herself, “such a helper was I to him” and “such a helper am I to you” (5.808, 5.828; see [[epitarrhothos]] and [[reciprocity-with-the-gods]]).

**Dearest.** Aphrodite tells Dione that she was wounded because she was carrying her son out of the war, “Aeneas, who is dearest to me by far of all” (5.378). In Athena's joke on Olympus, Aphrodite is urging an Achaean woman to follow “the Trojans, whom she now loves beyond measure” (τοὺς νῦν ἔκπαγλα φίλησε, 5.423). The love that makes Aphrodite a Trojan partisan is the same that sent Helen to Troy (see [[aphrodite]]).

**Dear brother, dear to you.** Wounded, Aphrodite kneels to Ares, “her dear brother” (κασιγνήτοιο φίλοιο, 5.357; see [[kasignetos]]). Zeus uses the word against his son: “strife is always dear to you, and wars and battles” (5.891; see [[strife-is-always-dear]]).
