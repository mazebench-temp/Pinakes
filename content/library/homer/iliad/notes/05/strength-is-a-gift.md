---
summary: Book 5 acts out the premise of Agamemnon's taunt. Diomedes' strength is Athena's gift, and the first half of the line, “if you are very strong”, returns twice as a threat that strength will not be enough.
refs: [5.410, 5.645]
---

Agamemnon's line in Book 1 has two halves: “If you are very strong, it was a god, I think, who gave you that” (εἰ μάλα καρτερός ἐσσι, θεός που σοὶ τό γʼ ἔδωκεν, 1.178). Book 5 uses both.

**The gift.** The second half is the plot of Diomedes' day. Athena gives him might and daring (5.1–3), puts his father's might in his breast (5.125–126) and guides his spear (5.290, 5.856–857). The Trojans say so too: Pandarus thinks that he does not rage “without a god” (5.185). Here the thought is no insult. See [[menos]] and [[divine-intervention]].

**The threat.** The first half returns as a warning that strength is not enough. Dione says, “So now let the son of Tydeus, strong as he is (εἰ καὶ μάλα καρτερός ἐστι), take care that no one better than you fights him” (5.410–411; see [[who-fights-the-gods]]). Tlepolemus tells Sarpedon that he will be no defense to the Trojans, “not even if you are very strong” (οὐδʼ εἰ μάλα καρτερός ἐσσι), but will pass the gates of Hades (5.644–646). Tlepolemus is killed instead, and Dione's threat is not fulfilled.
