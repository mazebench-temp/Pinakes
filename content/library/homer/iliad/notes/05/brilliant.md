---
summary: Book 5 uses the epithet of praise sixteen times, for men and women on both sides, for Dione among goddesses, and for whole groups, the Achaeans and Sarpedon's companions.
grc: [=δῖοι]
---

The epithet is spread across the book. It praises Theano, who reared her husband's bastard son (5.70; [[theano]]), the priest's son Hypsenor (5.76), Hector (5.211, 5.467, 5.471, 5.601), Aphrodite (5.370), Odysseus (5.669, 5.679), Achilles in Hera's rebuke (5.788), and Diomedes as Athena mounts his chariot and as Ares sees him coming (5.837, 5.846). Dione is “brilliant among goddesses” (5.381; [[dione]]).

The plural is new in Book 5. Around the phantom of Aeneas fight “the Trojans and the brilliant Achaeans” (5.451; [[the-phantom-of-aeneas]]), and Sarpedon's “brilliant companions” carry him out of the fighting and set him down under the oak of Zeus (5.663, 5.692; [[the-oak-of-zeus]]). The general praise, which says little of any single bearer, suits groups as well as men.
