---
summary: Two wives stand behind Book 5's warriors: Theano, who reared her husband's bastard son to please him, and Aegialeia, whose longing for her husband Dione uses as a threat.
grc: [πόσεϊ]
---

**Theano.** Pedaeus was a bastard son of Antenor, “but brilliant Theano had reared him with care like her own dear children, to please her husband” (χαριζομένη πόσεϊ ᾧ, 5.70–71; see [[theano]] and [[nothos]]). The wife's care for her husband's son by another woman is praise of her.

**Aegialeia.** Dione ends her warning against Diomedes with his wife: let him take care, or Aegialeia, the wise daughter of Adrastus, will long wake her household from sleep with her wailing, “longing for her wedded husband, the best of the Achaeans, the strong wife of Diomedes, tamer of horses” (κουρίδιον ποθέουσα πόσιν, 5.412–415; see [[aegialeia]] and [[pothe]]). κουρίδιος πόσις, “wedded husband”, is the husband a woman married as a girl; it is also what Agamemnon calls Clytemnestra, his κουριδίη ἄλοχος, when he says he prefers Chryseis to her (1.113–114). See [[husbands-and-wives]].
