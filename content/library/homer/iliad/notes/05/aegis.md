**The arming.** Athena lets her own robe fall on her father's floor, puts on the tunic of Zeus, and arms (5.733–737; see [[arming-scene]]). The aegis comes first:

**Tasseled.** θυσσανόεσσα, “tasseled”, from θύσανος, “tassel”, recalls the hundred golden tassels of Book 2, each worth a hundred oxen (2.448–449). It is the aegis' standing epithet: Zeus gives the tasseled aegis to Apollo and takes it up himself on Ida, Athena throws it over Achilles' shoulders, and Ares stabs at it in the battle of the gods (15.229, 17.593, 18.204, 21.400).

**Dreadful.** The passage returns to one word, δεινός, “dreadful”: the aegis is δεινή (5.739), the Gorgon is a δεινὸν πέλωρ, a “dread monster” (5.741), and its head is δεινή τε σμερδνή τε, “dreadful and terrible” (5.742).

**The figures.** Rout (Φόβος) is set around it “as a crown” (ἐστεφάνωται), and within are Strife, Courage and chilling Onslaught, each in its own ἐν δέ, “and on it”, four times over (see [[rout]], [[strife]], [[courage]], [[onslaught]] and [[anaphora]]). The powers are those that drive a battle: rout and strife, the courage that stands and the onslaught that charges. At the center, as the verse order suggests, is the Gorgon's head (see [[gorgon]]). The arrangement is that of Agamemnon's shield, on which the Gorgon “was set as a crown”, with Terror and Rout around her (11.36–37), and the verb is the same. But the aegis is not carried like a shield. Athena slings it around her shoulders, as she will throw it over Achilles' shoulders (18.204), and in Greek art she wears it as a cape (see [[aegis]]).

**The Gorgon.** Γοργείη κεφαλή, “the Gorgon's head”, is Homer's first mention of the Gorgon. The same phrase returns in the Odyssey, where Odysseus fears that Persephone will send up the Gorgon's head from Hades (Odyssey 11.633–635); how the head can be both there and on the aegis was a problem for Aristotle (see [[aristotle-gorgon]]). In the Iliad the Gorgon is otherwise only a face on Agamemnon's shield (11.36) and a look in Hector's eyes, which have “the eyes of the Gorgon or of Ares, ruin of mortals” (8.349). The head is “the portent of aegis-bearing Zeus” (Διὸς τέρας αἰγιόχοιο, 5.742). A τέρας is a sign sent by a god (see [[teras]]), and the aegis is Zeus' before it is Athena's: she arms in her father's house, in her father's tunic, with his aegis (see [[aegis-bearing]]).

**The aegis and Ares.** Athena wears the aegis to the fight in which she drives Diomedes' spear into Ares (5.855–859). In the battle of the gods Ares reminds her of it, calls her a dog-fly, and stabs at the tasseled aegis, “which not even the thunderbolt of Zeus can overcome”; she answers by felling him with a stone (21.391–406).
