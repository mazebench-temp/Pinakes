---
summary: Book 5 is a book of the sons of Zeus. Sarpedon, son of Zeus, kills Tlepolemus, grandson of Zeus, whose father Heracles, another son of Zeus, once shot the gods; and Pandarus claims that Apollo sent him.
grc: [υἱὸς Διὸς, Διὸς γόνον, υἱός θʼ υἱωνός τε Διὸς]
en: [offspring]
---

Pandarus, boasting over Diomedes, names his patron Apollo by the title: he came from Lycia, he says, because “the lord, the son of Zeus” roused him (5.105). The rest of the book gives the title to mortal sons.

In Dione's story Heracles is “the son of aegis-bearing Zeus” who shot Hades (5.396; [[heracles-wounds-hera-and-hades]]). His son Tlepolemus meets Sarpedon: “the son and the grandson of Zeus the cloud-gatherer” (5.631). Tlepolemus attacks the title itself: “They lie when they say you are the offspring of aegis-bearing Zeus, since you fall far short of those men who were born of Zeus among men of former times” (5.635–637; [[bold-in-endurance]]). The duel is a test of whose descent from Zeus is real, and the son kills the grandson. Then the narrator gives Sarpedon his title three times as he is carried wounded from the field: Odysseus wonders whether to pursue “the son of loud-thundering Zeus” (5.672; [[loud-thundering]]), it was not fated for him to kill “the mighty son of Zeus” (5.675), and “Sarpedon, son of Zeus” begs Hector not to leave him (5.683). His father saves him here; in Book 16 he will not ([[sarpedon]], [[tlepolemus-and-sarpedon]]).
