---
summary: Hera calls Ares a fool “who knows no law” (θέμιστα): the god of war is lawless even among the gods.
grc: [θέμιστα]
en: [law]
---

Complaining to Zeus of Ares' slaughter of the Achaeans, Hera says that Cypris and Apollo take their ease “having let loose this fool, who knows no law” (ἄφρονα τοῦτον ἀνέντες, ὃς οὔ τινα οἶδε θέμιστα, 5.761). To know θέμιστες is to know what is laid down as right, the ways that kings and judges keep from Zeus (see [[themis]]). The Cyclops of the Odyssey, who has no assemblies and no θέμιστες, is described in the same terms: he “knew no laws” (ἀθεμίστια ᾔδη, *Odyssey* 9.189; see also 9.112–115). Hera puts the war god in the Cyclops' place, a being outside the order that holds gods and men together. Zeus agrees, and calls him “the most hateful to me” of all the gods (5.890).

Hera also complains that Ares has destroyed the army “idly and against all order” (μὰψ ἀτὰρ οὐ κατὰ κόσμον, 5.759), the phrase used of Thersites' disorderly abuse (2.214). See [[nemesis]] and [[the-gods-mirror-the-mortals]].
