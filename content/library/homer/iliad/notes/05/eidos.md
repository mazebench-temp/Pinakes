---
summary: Gods take mortal likenesses in Book 5, Ares that of Acamas and Hera that of Stentor, and Hera shames the Argives as “admirable in looks”; Apollo makes a likeness of Aeneas with no one inside it.
grc: [εἰδόμενος, εἰσαμένη]
---

**Likenesses.** Ares rouses the Trojans “in the likeness of Acamas, the swift leader of the Thracians” (εἰδόμενος Ἀκάμαντι, 5.462; see [[acamas-of-thrace]]), and Hera rallies the Argives “in the likeness of great-hearted Stentor with the voice of bronze, who could shout as loud as fifty other men” (Στέντορι εἰσαμένη, 5.785–786; see [[stentor]]). The Iliad's gods usually take the shape of a particular man when they speak to mortals, as Iris took Polites' voice and Athena Laodocus' form (2.791, 4.86–87). Hera takes Stentor's for his voice. Not every disguise holds. Diomedes sees Ares beside Hector, “in the likeness of a mortal man” (βροτῷ ἀνδρὶ ἐοικώς, 5.604), and knows him (see [[seeing-the-gods]]).

**Looks.** Hera's shout begins with an insult: αἰδὼς Ἀργεῖοι, κάκʼ ἐλέγχεα, εἶδος ἀγητοί, “Shame, Argives, wretched disgraces, admirable in looks!” (5.787). Admirable looks without courage are what Hector found in Paris, “best in looks” (εἶδος ἄριστε, 3.39; see [[best-in-looks]]). See [[aidos]] and [[elenchos]].

**A likeness with no one inside.** The same root gives εἴδωλον, the phantom that Apollo makes “like Aeneas himself, and in armor such as his” (5.449–450; see [[eidolon]]).
