summary: Agamemnon makes the first kill when the Trojans give way, spearing Odius, leader of the Halizones, in the back. Later, when the Achaeans stand against Hector's rally, he goes through the ranks telling them to feel shame before one another, and kills Aeneas' companion Deicoon.
# Ἀτρεΐδης at 5.50, 5.55 and 5.578, and Ἀτρεΐδῃ at 5.207, are Menelaus.

**First.** Athena leads Ares out of the battle, and the Danaans turn the Trojans back, “and each of the leaders took his man” (5.37–38). The narrator begins with the king, “First Agamemnon, lord of men” (πρῶτος δὲ ἄναξ ἀνδρῶν Ἀγαμέμνων, 5.38; see [[lord-of-men]]):

{{quote:42bdeef4-2c47-5a9c-bc79-261ec63f798d}}

Six leaders kill six men in turn: Agamemnon, Idomeneus, Menelaus, Meriones, Meges and Eurypylus (5.37–83; see [[androktasia]]). The king's man is himself a leader, “great Odius, leader of the Halizones” (see [[odius]]). πρῶτος is said twice. Agamemnon is the first to kill, and Odius was the first to turn and run (πρώτῳ γὰρ στρεφθέντι, 5.40). The spear in the back “between the shoulders” is a formula for a man struck as he turns to flee (8.258–259, 11.447–448), and Menelaus kills Scamandrius the same way a few lines later (5.56–57). Book 16 opens its own round of leaders' kills with the same thought: “there, as the battle scattered, man took man among the leaders” (16.306–307).

**Shame.** When Hector and Ares bring the Trojans on again, the Achaeans stand fast like clouds on the mountains (5.519–527; see [[clouds-on-the-mountains]]), and Agamemnon goes through the ranks:

{{quote:5eab5b38-2112-58ad-b538-a73e0e747a2e}}

ἀνέρες ἔστε, “be men”, is a common call to arms (“be men, friends, and remember your furious courage”, 6.112, 15.734). Agamemnon adds αἰδώς, the regard for the eyes of others (see [[aidos]]), and he makes it mutual: “feel shame before one another”. His argument is practical. Where men feel shame, more are saved than killed; when men run, they win neither fame nor courage (see [[kleos]] and [[alke]]). Telamonian Ajax speaks the same four verses at the ships in Book 15, with “put shame in your hearts” in place of “take a valiant heart” (15.561–564), and Nestor begins the same way soon after (15.661). In this book Hera, in the likeness of Stentor, will shout the word itself at the Argives: “Shame, Argives” (5.787). See [[exhortation]].

**Deicoon.** “He spoke, and quickly threw his spear” (5.533): the king follows his words with an example. His man stands in the front rank, Deicoon, the companion of Aeneas, whom the Trojans honored like the sons of Priam (5.533–540; see [[deicoon]]). The shield does not stop the spear, and Deicoon falls with a thud, and his armor clatters upon him, as Odius fell (5.540 = 5.42; see [[fell-with-a-thud]]). Agamemnon's two kills in the book end in the same verse.

**The cause.** A few lines later Aeneas kills the twin sons of Diocles, who had come to Troy “seeking satisfaction for the sons of Atreus, Agamemnon and Menelaus” (5.552; see [[crethon]] and [[war-for-menelaus]]). Agamemnon does not appear again in the book. The “son of Atreus” whom Pandarus boasts of having wounded is his brother (5.206–208; see [[menelaus]]).
