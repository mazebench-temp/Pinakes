---
summary: Pandarus curses the day he led the Trojans to lovely Ilios with his bow.
---

“So it was in an evil hour that I took my curved bow from its peg on that day when to lovely Ilios I led the Trojans, to do a favor to brilliant Hector” (5.209–211; [[curved-bow]], [[pandarus]]). The epithet of desire, which the Catalogue gave to towns of Greece, names Troy in the mouth of an ally who wishes he had never come to it. He will not see his own home again (5.212–216; [[high-roofed]]).
