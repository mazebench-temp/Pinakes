summary: Zeus names “swift Ares and Athena” as the gods to whom the works of war belong, and speed follows Ares through the book, down to the swift feet that carry him away wounded. θοός also marks the Thracian Acamas, whose likeness Ares takes, and two warriors quick to fight.
grc: [θοῷ, ταχέʼ ἵππω, ταχέες πόδες]
---

Zeus tells the wounded Aphrodite that the works of war are not given to her: “you attend to the lovely works of marriage, and all these things will be the business of swift Ares and Athena” (5.428–430; [[aphrodite]]). The two gods he pairs as the war's proper keepers fight each other at the end of the book. Ares' speed is his standing quality; later, in the likeness of “Acamas, the swift leader of the Thracians,” he rouses the sons of Priam (5.462; [[acamas-of-thrace]], [[baneful]]). Of men the word praises readiness to fight: Deicoon was honored like the sons of Priam, “since he was swift to fight among the foremost” (5.535–536; [[deicoon]]), and Aeneas gives way before Menelaus and Antilochus, “swift warrior though he was” (5.571; [[aeneas]]).

The other word, ταχύς, is also given to Ares. When the wounded Aphrodite finds him on the left of the battle, “his spear and his swift horses were resting in a mist” (5.356), and she borrows the horses to ride to Olympus ([[with-golden-headbands]]). After Diomedes has wounded him, Ares tells Zeus that only speed saved him: “But my swift feet carried me away” (5.885; [[bronze-ares]]).
