---
summary: Dione calls Diomedes a fool who does not know that the man who fights the gods does not live long; Sarpedon twice remembers the infant son he left in Lycia.
en: [infant]
---

**The fool.** Dione's warning begins with the narrator's word for a man who does not know what is coming: νήπιος, “fool, the son of Tydeus does not know this in his heart, that the man who fights with the immortals does not live long” (5.406–407; see [[who-fights-the-gods]]). Here a goddess says it, and she is wrong. Diomedes lives, and goes home (see [[diomedes]]). Dione speaks as a mother consoling her child, and the word is part of the consolation.

**The infant son.** The word's first sense is a small child. Sarpedon tells Hector that he left in Lycia “my dear wife and my infant son” (ἄλοχόν τε φίλην καὶ νήπιον υἱόν, 5.480), and when he lies wounded he asks Hector not to leave him to the Danaans, since it seems he will never go home “and gladden my dear wife and my infant son” (5.688). The words are the same both times, and the son is never named. See [[husbands-and-wives]] and [[bereaved-parents]].
