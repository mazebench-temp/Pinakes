---
summary: The Cadmeians return in Athena's version of Tydeus' embassy, in which he came alone “as a messenger to Thebes among the many Cadmeians” and challenged their young men and beat them all (5.803–808).
---

{{quote:cf050df9-3e88-5a9d-92e1-9c511c8e4374}}

Athena retells the story Agamemnon told in Book 4, in some of the same words. The Cadmeians are “many” in both versions (πολέας … Καδμεΐωνας, 4.385; πολέας μετὰ Καδμείωνας, 5.804), and in both Tydeus challenged them and won everything easily (4.389–390 = 5.807–808, but for the helper's name). Her version is shorter. It does not name Eteocles, and it leaves out the fifty men who lay in wait for Tydeus on his way home (4.391–398). Of the Cadmeians it keeps only the κοῦροι, “the young men”, whom he challenged (5.807). The form Καδμείων (5.807) is the genitive of Καδμεῖοι.
