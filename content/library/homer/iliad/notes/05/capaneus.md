---
summary: Capaneus is named only in the patronymics of his son Sthenelus, who is “the son of Capaneus” four times in Book 5 (5.108, 5.109, 5.241, 5.319).
grc: [Καπανήϊον, Καπανηϊάδη]
relations:
  - child: sthenelus (5.108-109, 5.241, 5.319)
---

Book 5 names the father in three ways. The narrator calls Sthenelus Καπανήϊος υἱός, with an adjective formed from the father's name (5.108, 5.241; so 4.367), and υἱὸς Καπανῆος, with the genitive (5.319; so 4.403). Diomedes, asking him to draw the arrow from his shoulder, calls him Καπανηϊάδη, the patronymic proper, a form found only here in Homer (5.109). Nothing else is said of Capaneus in the book. See [[sthenelus]].
