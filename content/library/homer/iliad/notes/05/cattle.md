**Prey.** Diomedes takes Echemmon and Chromius from one chariot “as a lion leaps among cattle and breaks the neck of a heifer or a cow as they graze in a thicket” (5.161–162; see [[lion-among-cattle]]). πόρτις is a young cow, a heifer, and βοῦς the grown beast; the herd is grazing and does not see the lion coming. The two young lions to which the twin sons of Diocles are compared “seize cattle and fat sheep and ravage the farmsteads of men” (5.556–557; see [[two-lions]]). Cattle are what a lion takes, and what men defend.

**The herdsman.** Aphrodite bore Aeneas to Anchises “as he tended his cattle” (βουκολέοντι, 5.313). The *Homeric Hymn to Aphrodite* tells the story: Zeus put desire for Anchises in the goddess' heart while he was herding cattle on the high hills of Ida (*Hymn to Aphrodite* 53–55; see [[aphrodite-and-anchises]] and [[anchises]]). A prince of the royal house of Troy keeps cattle on the mountain, as Apollo kept the cattle of Laomedon on Ida for a year (21.448–449).

The shields of Book 5 are made of oxhide. Ares is ταλαύρινος, “with the oxhide shield” (5.289; see [[with-the-oxhide-shield]]), and around the phantom of Aeneas the armies hack at one another's “oxhide shields” (βοείας, 5.452).
