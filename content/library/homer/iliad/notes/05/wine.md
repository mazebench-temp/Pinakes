---
summary: The gods “do not eat bread, they do not drink sparkling wine”, and so they are bloodless and are called immortals (5.341–342).
---

When Diomedes' spear pierces Aphrodite's hand, the narrator explains what flows from the wound: ichor, “such as flows in the blessed gods; for they do not eat bread, they do not drink sparkling wine, and so they are bloodless, and are called immortals” (5.340–342; see [[ichor]]). Bread and wine are the food of men, and blood is made of them. The gods have their own food and drink, ambrosia and nectar (see [[ambrosia]] and [[nectar]]). αἴθοψ οἶνος, “sparkling wine”, is the standing phrase for the wine men drink (see [[wine]]). Later critics asked how the gods could drink no wine when Ganymede was carried off to pour wine for Zeus (see [[aristotle-wine-of-the-gods]]).
