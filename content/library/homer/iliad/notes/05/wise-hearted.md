---
summary: Pandarus twice calls the man he fears “the wise-hearted son of Tydeus,” and addresses him so before his last throw; Athena gives the epithet to Tydeus himself, “the wise-hearted son of Oeneus.”
grc: [δαΐφρονι]
---

The epithet belongs to Diomedes and his father in Book 5. Pandarus uses it as he tells Aeneas what he has seen: “I liken him in everything to the wise-hearted son of Tydeus” (5.181), and “if he is the man I say, the wise-hearted son of Tydeus,” then a god stands beside him (5.184). When they meet, he addresses Diomedes with a string of epithets: “Stout-hearted and wise-hearted son of noble Tydeus” (5.277; [[karterothymos]], [[noble]]). In Book 4 Athena called Pandarus himself δαΐφρων, as she persuaded him to shoot (4.93). At the end of Book 5 she gives the word to Tydeus, in her rebuke of his son: “then you are no offspring of Tydeus, the wise-hearted son of Oeneus” (5.812–813; [[son-of-tydeus]], [[oeneus]]).
