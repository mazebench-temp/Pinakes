summary: Two Aetolians die in Book 5, Trechus, “the Aetolian spearman”, killed by Hector and Ares, and Periphas, “by far the best of the Aetolians”, whom Ares himself kills and strips. Athena reminds Diomedes that his father was the son of the Aetolian Oeneus.
grc: [Αἰτώλιον]
---

Τρῆχόν τʼ αἰχμητὴν Αἰτώλιον, “Trechus, the Aetolian spearman” (5.706), is the first Aetolian to die in the poem, one of the six men killed by Hector and Ares as the Argives give ground (see [[trechus]]). Later in the book Ares himself is stripping “huge Periphas, by far the best of the Aetolians, the splendid son of Ochesius”, when Athena, wearing the helmet of Hades, turns Diomedes against him (5.842–857; see [[periphas]], [[ochesius]] and [[ares]]).

The Aetolians' old royal house is recalled in the same scene. Athena ends her rebuke of Diomedes by telling him that he is no true offspring “of Tydeus, the wise-hearted son of Oeneus” (5.813; see [[tydeus]] and [[oeneus]]). Oeneus was the king of Calydon whose sons were dead when the Aetolians sailed under Thoas (2.641–642). So the Aetolian Periphas is stripped by Ares at the moment when the grandson of Oeneus drives against the god.
