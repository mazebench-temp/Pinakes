---
summary: Zeus is the cloud-gatherer four times in Book 5: as the father and grandfather of Sarpedon and Tlepolemus, as the owner of the tunic Athena puts on, and in the formulas of his answers to Hera and to Ares.
grc: [νεφεληγερέταο]
---

The genitive names Zeus as the source of others' standing. Sarpedon and Tlepolemus advance on each other, “the son and the grandson of Zeus the cloud-gatherer” (5.630–631; [[son-of-zeus]], [[tlepolemus-and-sarpedon]]). Athena arms in her father's clothes, “putting on the tunic of Zeus the cloud-gatherer” (5.736; [[arming-scene]], [[tunic]]).

The nominative stands in the speech formulas, as it did in Book 1. “Answering her, Zeus the cloud-gatherer said” introduces his permission to Hera to set Athena on Ares (5.764; [[answering-him]], [[driver-of-spoil]]). “Looking at him darkly, Zeus the cloud-gatherer said” introduces his rebuke of the wounded Ares, the most hateful of the gods to him (5.888; [[looking-darkly]]). Both answers settle the quarrels of the gods, and both go against Ares.
