---
summary: Telamonian Ajax pities Menesthes and Anchialus, kills Amphius, and is driven back from the body; Book 5 also calls him “shining Ajax.”
---

Ajax has one scene in Book 5, and his epithet opens it and marks his blow. “And great Telamonian Ajax pitied them as they fell,” the two men Hector has killed, and he throws his shining spear (5.610–611; [[phaeinos]]). “Telamonian Ajax struck him on the belt,” and Amphius, son of Selagus, falls (5.615–617; [[amphius-son-of-selagus]]). Then “shining Ajax” runs up to strip the body, and is driven back by the Trojans (5.617–626; [[phaidimos]], [[proud]]). The greater Ajax, who in Book 2 was the best of the Achaeans while Achilles kept up his wrath, fails here to take a dead man's armor ([[fighting-over-the-body]], [[ajax]]).
