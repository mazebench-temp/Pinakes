---
summary: The companions of Book 5 drive captured horses to the ships, carry the wounded out of the battle and receive the dead; Sthenelus honors Deipylus above all his age-mates, and Pelagon draws the spear from Sarpedon's thigh.
grc: [ἑταίροισιν, ἑτάρῳ, ἕταρον, ἑταίρων]
---

**The work of companions.** In Book 5 the ἑταῖροι do what a fighting man cannot do for himself. Diomedes gives the horses he captures to his companions to drive to the hollow ships (5.25–26, 5.165), and Sthenelus gives the horses of Aeneas to Deipylus, “his dear companion, whom above all the men of his age he honored, because his mind was in tune with his own” (5.325–326; see [[deipylus]]). Menelaus and Antilochus drag the dead sons of Diocles back to the army: “they laid the two poor men in the arms of their companions” (5.573–574). Sarpedon's companions carry him out of the war and set him down under the oak of Zeus, and it is Pelagon, “his dear companion”, who pushes the spear out of his thigh (5.663–664, 5.692–695; see [[pelagon-of-lycia]]).

**Companions in the list of the dead.** Deicoon is “companion of great-hearted Aeneas” (Αἰνείω ἕταρον, 5.534) when Agamemnon kills him. Ares, rousing the Trojans in the likeness of Acamas, calls the fallen Aeneas “our brave companion” (5.469).
