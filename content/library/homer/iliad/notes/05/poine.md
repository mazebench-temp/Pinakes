ποινή is usually the price paid to the kin of a man who has been killed (see [[apoina]]). Here it is paid by a god, for a son who has been taken, not killed. The Iliad tells the story later: Ganymede, “the most beautiful of mortal men”, was carried off by the gods to be Zeus' cupbearer and live among the immortals (20.232–235; see [[ganymede]] and [[ganymede-and-the-horses-of-tros]]). The father receives horses, the best under the dawn and the sun, in place of the son. The gift goes on being paid out: Anchises steals a strain of the breed by putting his mares to them, and two of the foals are Aeneas' (5.268–272), until Diomedes takes them (5.319–327; see [[tros]] and [[horses]]).
