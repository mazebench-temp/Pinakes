---
summary: Book 5 gives the epithet only to Diomedes, three times, in the genitive Διομήδεος ἱπποδάμοιο: in Dione's picture of his widow, where the best stand around him, and as Ares goes straight for him.
---

The Trojans are not called horse-tamers in Book 5. The epithet goes to the Achaean who wins the finest horses of the war, the horses of Tros, which Sthenelus takes on his orders (5.319–327; [[master-of-rout]]). Dione pictures his wife waking her household with her wailing, “the strong wife of Diomedes, tamer of horses” (5.415; [[mighty]], [[periphron]]). Hera and Athena find the best of the Achaeans standing “around mighty Diomedes, tamer of horses” (5.781; [[might-of-diomedes]]). And Ares, seeing him in Athena's chariot, leaves Periphas lying and “went straight for Diomedes, tamer of horses” (5.849; [[diomedes-wounds-ares]]). All three uses close the verse, and the last two are in the scenes of the god's defeat.
