---
summary: Strife rages without end beside Apollo and Ares as the Trojans rally (5.518), and she is set on Athena's aegis among the powers that go out against Ares (5.740).
passages:
  - 5.517-518 | Apollo, Ares and Strife
---

When the Trojans rally, the toil of battle is the work of three powers: the toil “that the god of the silver bow had awakened, and Ares, ruin of mortals, and Strife, raging without end” (5.517–518). ἄμοτον μεμαυῖα, “raging without end”, is her half-line from Book 4 (4.440), where she came with Ares, Terror and Rout. Here she is on the Trojan side with Apollo and Ares. Two hundred lines later she is on Athena's aegis, among the powers that go out against Ares (5.740; see [[rout]], [[courage]], [[onslaught]] and [[aegis]]). Strife serves whoever brings her.

The common word runs through the end of the book. Hera, harnessing her horses, is “eager for strife” (μεμαυῖʼ ἔριδος, 5.732), with the participle of Strife's own half-line; the participle is feminine, so it is Hera who is eager, not the horses. Ares bellows as loud as ten thousand men “as they join in the strife of Ares” (5.861). And Zeus tells him that “strife is always dear to you” (5.891; see [[eris]] and [[strife-is-always-dear]]).
