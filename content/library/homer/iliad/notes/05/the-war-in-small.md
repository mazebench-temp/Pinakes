**The beginning.** The war's first cause appears in an obituary. Meriones kills Phereclus, the craftsman who built for Alexander “the balanced ships that began the evil”, an evil for all the Trojans and for himself (5.59–64; see [[the-beginning-of-evils]] and [[the-abduction-of-helen]]). The twin sons of Diocles die having come to Troy for the reason Achilles gave in Book 1, “seeking satisfaction for the sons of Atreus” (5.552–553; see [[time]]). On Olympus, Athena's joke about Aphrodite and the Achaean woman she lures toward the Trojans is Helen's story again, told as comedy (5.421–425).

**The broken oath paid for.** Pandarus, who broke the truce in Book 4, dies in Book 5 with Athena guiding the spear into his face (5.290–296). The first bond of the war was broken by Alexander, the guest who took his host's wife; the man who broke the second pays first (see [[oaths-and-their-breaking]] and [[no-helper-to-liars]]).

**A Troy that fell before.** Tlepolemus tells Sarpedon how his father Heracles came for the sake of Laomedon's horses, “with only six ships and fewer men, and sacked the city of Ilios, and widowed its streets” (5.640–642). Sarpedon answers that the city fell then through the folly of its king (5.648–651). The war the poem tells has a precedent in which Troy was taken, and the audience knows it will be taken again (see [[heracles-sacks-troy]] and [[sack-of-troy-foretold]]).

**The nine years.** Hera, rallying the Argives in the likeness of Stentor, reminds them of the war before the quarrel: as long as Achilles fought, the Trojans never came out before the Dardanian gates, “for they dreaded his heavy spear; but now they are fighting far from the city, by the hollow ships” (5.788–791). Achilles is absent from Book 5, and the goddess measures the battle by him. See [[achilles-withdraws]].
