---
summary: Twice in Book 5 a reply comes with a glare. Diomedes silences Sthenelus, who has urged him to retreat (5.251), and Zeus answers Ares' complaint by calling him the most hateful of the gods, and then has him healed (5.888–904).
passages:
  - 5.251-273 | Diomedes refuses to retreat
  - 5.888-898 | Zeus rebukes Ares
---

**Diomedes and Sthenelus.** Sthenelus sees Aeneas and Pandarus coming and begs Diomedes to draw back in the chariot, “or you may lose your dear life” (5.243–250). “Looking at him darkly, mighty Diomedes said” (5.251): “Do not talk to me of flight, for I do not think you will persuade me” (5.252). It is the second time he glares at his companion: in Book 4 he silenced him for answering Agamemnon (4.411; see [[sthenelus]]). This time the offense is advice to flee, given to a man whom Athena has sent into the battle: “It is not in my blood to fight shrinking back or to cower … Pallas Athena does not let me flee” (5.253–256; see [[gennaios]]). The glare, as J. P. Holoka read the formula, answers a breach of what is fitting. Then Diomedes gives his orders, and Sthenelus carries them out (5.259–273, 5.319–327).

**Zeus and Ares.** Ares shows Zeus the immortal blood running from his wound and blames Zeus himself for letting Athena loose on him (5.870–887). “Looking at him darkly, Zeus the cloud-gatherer said” (5.888): “Do not sit beside me and whine, you turncoat. Of all the gods who hold Olympus, you are the most hateful to me” (5.889–890; see [[alloprosallos]]). Ares had just told Zeus that every other god on Olympus obeys him, “and each of us is subject to you” (5.878); his own complaint breaks the order he describes. The glare ends in mercy: “But I will not endure it any longer that you should be in pain; for you are of my stock” (5.895–896), and Zeus has Paeëon heal him (5.899–904; see [[zeus]] and [[ares]]).
