---
summary: Book 5 gives Ares his epithet θοῦρος seven times, from the moment Athena leads him out of the battle to the moment Paeëon heals his wound.
grc: [θοῦρον, θοῦρος]
---

The masculine θοῦρος, “rushing, furious,” is the epithet of Ares, and Book 5 uses it at each stage of his day. Athena takes “furious Ares” by the hand and leads him out of the battle (5.30, 5.35; [[ruin-of-mortals]]). Aphrodite finds him sitting on the left of the battle, resting (5.355). Apollo sends him back in (5.454), and “furious Ares” covers the battle in night to help the Trojans (5.507). Athena tells Diomedes to have no awe of “furious Ares, this madman” (5.830), and the last use comes after his wound: “so quickly he healed furious Ares” (5.904; [[fig-juice]]). The epithet of rushing names the god whenever he is at rest, led, or healed.

The feminine keeps its formula. Hera says to Athena “let us two also think of our furious courage” (5.718), the verse Diomedes spoke to Sthenelus at the end of Book 4 (4.418). In Book 4 it began the man's fighting, and here it begins the goddesses' ([[alke]], [[exhortation]]).
