---
summary: The Poetics explains how Ganymede can pour “wine” for gods who, Book 5 says, drink none, and a fragment of the Homeric Problems asks how the Gorgon's head can be both on Athena's aegis and in Hades.
---

- The gods drink no wine (5.341), yet Ganymede pours wine for Zeus: the word follows custom, or it is a metaphor (*Poetics* 25, 1461a27–31): [[aristotle-wine-of-the-gods]].
- The Gorgon's head on the aegis (5.741) and in Hades (*Odyssey* 11.634): perhaps not the head itself, but the fear it causes (*Homeric Problems*, fr. 377 Gigon): [[aristotle-gorgon]].
- The collection of marvels handed down under his name tells of the islands of Diomedes and their birds (*On Marvellous Things Heard* 79): [[birds-of-diomedes]].
