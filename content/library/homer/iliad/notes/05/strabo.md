---
summary: Strabo reads Book 5 as a geographer. He defends the reading Hyle and locates the Cephisian lake, explains the two forms of the name Paesus, and argues from the Alpheus where Nestor's Pylos lay; he also reports the Italian legends of Diomedes and the kings who claimed descent from Pylaemenes.
---

- Hyle, not Hyde, and the Cephisian lake (*Geography* 9.2.20): [[hyle-or-hyde]].
- **Paesus.** Homer names the town both ways, Strabo notes, adding the first syllable in the Catalogue, “the land of Apaesus” (2.828), and leaving it off in Book 5, where Amphius lived “in Paesus” (5.612). The town, between Lampsacus and Parium, had been destroyed and its people moved to Lampsacus, but the river still bore the name (*Geography* 13.1.19; see [[apaesus]]).
- **Nestor's Pylos.** In Book 5 the Alpheus “flows broad through the land of the Pylians” (5.545). Strabo quotes the line in the old debate over which of the three towns called Pylos was Nestor's. The Alpheus does not flow past the Pylos of Messenia, and so the critics who follow Homer's words most closely placed Nestor's Pylos in Triphylia, whose land the river crosses (*Geography* 8.3.1, 8.3.7; see [[alpheus]] and [[pylos]]).
- Diomedes in Daunia, the islands of Diomedes and their birds, and his cult among the Heneti (*Geography* 5.1.9, 6.3.9): [[birds-of-diomedes]].
- The kings of inland Paphlagonia “descended from Pylaemenes” (*Geography* 12.3.1): [[pylaemenes-dead-and-alive]].
