---
summary: Statius gives his Tydeus the stature of Athena's rebuke in Book 5, a small man with a great spirit.
---

Athena reminds Diomedes that his father “was small in build, but he was a fighter” (5.801), and that he challenged the Cadmeians at their own feast and won every contest. When Tydeus first comes to Argos in the *Thebaid* and fights Polynices at the door of Adrastus, Statius says the same in a line that became proverbial: “a greater courage reigned in his small body” (*maior in exiguo regnabat corpore virtus*, *Thebaid* 1.417).
