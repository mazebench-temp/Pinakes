---
summary: Book 5 uses the patronymic for Menelaus three times as he kills, for Agamemnon as he exhorts the army, and for both brothers as the cause for which the sons of Diocles came to Troy.
---

Menelaus is “the son of Atreus” in the verses of his killings: Scamandrius (5.49–50, 5.55) and Pylaemenes (5.578; [[famed-for-his-spear]]). Pandarus names him with Diomedes as the two chieftains he has wounded, “the son of Tydeus and the son of Atreus” (5.207; [[son-of-tydeus]]). Agamemnon is the son of Atreus who “ranged through the throng, giving many orders,” before his exhortation (5.528; [[exhortation]]). The plural names the brothers together in the obituary of the sons of Diocles, who came to Troy “seeking satisfaction for the sons of Atreus, Agamemnon and Menelaus” (5.552; [[time]], [[atreidae]]). The brothers' cause brings the young men to their deaths.
