---
summary: Diomedes prays to Athena as Atrytone after his wound, and Hera calls her so when she proposes that they go down against Ares.
---

The title of the unwearied goddess opens two appeals. Diomedes, wounded in the shoulder, prays: “Hear me, child of aegis-bearing Zeus, Atrytone” (5.115; [[hear-me]], [[prayer-type-scene]]), and she answers by making his limbs light (5.121–122). Hera, seeing the Argives killed by Ares and Hector, begins with the same half-verse after ὢ πόποι (5.714; [[o-popoi]]), and Athena arms. The man and the goddess call on Athena with the same words, and both are answered. The epithet is set against weariness: at the end of the book Athena finds Diomedes worn out under his shield strap (5.796–798; [[well-rounded]]).
