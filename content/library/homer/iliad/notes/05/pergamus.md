The temple of Apollo at Troy (νηός, 5.446) is named in the Iliad only here and in Hector's challenge in Book 7, where he promises to hang a beaten champion's armor “on the temple of Apollo the Far-Shooter” (7.83). Its innermost room, the ἄδυτον (from ἀ-, “not”, and δύω, “enter”: the place not to be entered), is named in Homer only here and when Apollo sends Aeneas back “out of the rich inner shrine” (5.512). πίων, “rich”, is also the word for Athena's temple at Athens (2.549). Athena's temple at Troy stands on the height of the city too (6.88, 6.297; see [[temple]]). Aeneas is healed in the god's own house, and by his mother and sister (see [[leto]], [[artemis]] and [[healing]]).

After rousing Ares, Apollo “sat down on the height of Pergamus” (Περγάμῳ ἄκρῃ, 5.460), the place from which he looked down on the battle in Book 4 and will look again in Book 7 (4.508, 7.21). See [[apollo]] and [[aeneas]].
