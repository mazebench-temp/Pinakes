Diomedes tells the pedigree of Aeneas' horses as he orders Sthenelus to take them. The breed was the one Zeus gave Tros in recompense for Ganymede (5.265–267; see [[tros]] and [[ganymede]]), and Laomedon, Priam's father, owned it. Then:

{{quote:cf5e5c11-2895-5b03-b124-a1d45373dffb}}

ἔκλεψεν, “stole”, and λάθρῃ, “without his knowledge”: Anchises had his own mares covered by the king's stallions in secret, and kept four of the six foals for himself. ἄναξ ἀνδρῶν, “lord of men”, is almost always Agamemnon's title. This book gives it to Anchises here and to Aeneas forty lines later (5.311; see [[lord-of-men]]). The two foals he gave his son are “masters of rout” (μήστωρε φόβοιο), and Diomedes means to have them: “If we could take these two, we would win noble fame” (5.273). The strain that Anchises stole from the king is taken from his son by the enemy (5.319–327). See [[anchises-steals-the-horses]] and [[horses]].

The other mentions are of the father. Sthenelus says that Aeneas “claims that he was born the son of noble Anchises” (5.247), and Ares, in the likeness of Acamas, calls Aeneas “son of great-hearted Anchises” (5.468). The narrator adds a scene to the Catalogue's “on the slopes of Ida” (2.821): Aphrodite bore Aeneas to Anchises “as he tended his cattle” (βουκολέοντι, 5.313). The prince as herdsman on Ida is the setting of the *Homeric Hymn to Aphrodite* (see [[aphrodite-and-anchises]]), and the Iliad keeps the custom in the family: Achilles once caught Aeneas alone among his cattle on Ida and chased him down the mountain (20.89–92, 20.188–191).
