---
summary: Hector “of the gleaming helmet” sees Odysseus killing the Lycians and comes on, and passes the wounded Sarpedon without a word.
---

Odysseus would have killed still more of the Lycians, “had not great Hector of the gleaming helmet been quick to see it” (5.679–680; [[would-have-had-not]]). Sarpedon, wounded, is glad at his coming and begs him not to leave him as prey for the Danaans; “Hector of the gleaming helmet said nothing to him, but darted past, eager as quickly as he could to thrust back the Argives” (5.684–691; [[sarpedon]], [[hector]]). The speech formula is the one Book 1 used of Zeus' silence before Thetis (τὴν δʼ οὔ τι προσέφη, 1.511). Hector's silence is the silence of haste, and the man he passes is the ally who rebuked him earlier in the book (5.471–492).
