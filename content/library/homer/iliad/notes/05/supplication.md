Iris leads the wounded Aphrodite out of the fighting, and they find Ares sitting on the left of the battle. “And she fell on her knees and begged her dear brother, with many entreaties, for the horses with golden headbands” (γνὺξ ἐριποῦσα … πολλὰ λισσομένη, 5.357–358). She kneels, as a suppliant does, and asks for a loan, not for her life; he gives her the horses at once (5.359–363; see [[ares]] and [[chariot-journey]]).

On Olympus, “brilliant Aphrodite fell on the knees of Dione, her mother” (ἐν γούνασι πῖπτε, 5.370–371). The gesture is the suppliant's, falling at the knees, and it is also a child's, falling into its mother's lap; Dione takes her daughter in her arms and strokes her with her hand (5.371–372; see [[called-him-by-name]] and [[dione]]). Thetis in Book 1 clasped the knees of Zeus to beg a favor for her son (1.500–502); here the goddess on her knees asks for nothing but comfort.
