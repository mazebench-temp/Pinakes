---
summary: Apollo forbids Diomedes to set his mind on equality with the gods; Hera calls Ares mindless, and Ares says the same of Athena.
grc: [φρονέειν, φρονέουσα]
---

**Thinking equal to the gods.** φρονέω, the verb of the φρένες, is to have in mind, to set one's thoughts on something. Apollo's warning to Diomedes uses it: “do not wish to think yourself equal to the gods” (μηδὲ θεοῖσιν ἶσʼ ἔθελε φρονέειν, 5.440–441; see [[gods-and-men-not-alike]]). To think equal things is to aim at being equal. In his prayer, Diomedes had used the verb of a god's goodwill: Athena once stood by his father φίλα φρονέουσα, “with friendly mind” (5.116; see [[philos]]).

**Without mind.** On Olympus the gods call each other ἄφρων. Hera complains to Zeus of Ares, “this fool, who knows no law” (ἄφρονα τοῦτον, 5.761; see [[themis]]), and Ares complains of Athena, “you begot that senseless daughter, ruinous” (ἄφρονα κούρην, 5.875). Each speaks of the other's side as Agamemnon and Achilles spoke of each other (see [[the-gods-mirror-the-mortals]]).
