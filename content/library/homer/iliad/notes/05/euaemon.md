---
summary: Euaemon is named twice when his son Eurypylus kills Hypsenor, in the patronymic Εὐαιμονίδης and in the phrase “the splendid son of Euaemon” (5.76, 5.79).
grc: [Εὐαιμονίδης]
---

The patronymic Εὐαιμονίδης, “son of Euaemon” (5.76), first appears here, as Eurypylus comes into the fighting; three lines later the same fact is given in full, Εὐαίμονος ἀγλαὸς υἱός, “the splendid son of Euaemon” (5.79). The father is named only through his son. See [[eurypylus]].
