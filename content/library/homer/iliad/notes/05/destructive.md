---
summary: Diomedes, praying to Athena, recalls how she stood by his father “in destructive war.”
grc: [δηΐῳ ἐν πολέμῳ]
---

Wounded by Pandarus, Diomedes prays: “if ever in friendship you stood by my father and me in destructive war, now be my friend in turn, Athena” (5.116–117; [[prayer-type-scene]], [[tydeus]]). The epithet that in Book 4 described the war a storm cloud foretells is here the war in which a goddess stood by a man. Book 5 gives the English “destructive” also to ἀΐδηλος, in the quarrel of Ares and Zeus (5.880, 5.897; [[aidelos]]).
