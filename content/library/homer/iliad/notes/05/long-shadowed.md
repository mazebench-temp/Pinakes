---
summary: Three long-shadowed spears are thrown in Book 5: Phegeus' at Diomedes, Pandarus' at Diomedes in the verse of the formal duels, and Ajax's into Amphius.
---

The first spear of Diomedes' day is his enemy's: “Phegeus was the first to let fly his long-shadowed spear” (5.15; [[phegeus]]). It misses, and Diomedes kills him. Pandarus, his arrows spent, throws his spear with the whole verse that opens the formal duels, “He spoke, and poising his long-shadowed spear, he let it fly” (5.280), the verse of Menelaus' throw at Paris (3.355). It pierces Diomedes' shield and fails, and Pandarus dies (5.281–296; [[with-the-oxhide-shield]]). The third is Ajax's: “the long-shadowed spear stuck in his lower belly,” and Amphius falls (5.616; [[amphius-son-of-selagus]]).
