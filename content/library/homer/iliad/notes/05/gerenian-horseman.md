---
summary: Athena calls Tydeus “the horseman,” ἱππότα, the title of the older generation of heroes.
grc: [ἱππότα Τυδεύς]
---

Athena puts into Diomedes “your father's might, undaunted, such as the horseman Tydeus, wielder of the shield, used to have” (5.125–126; [[wielder-of-the-shield]], [[tydeus]]). The single ἱππότα belongs to men of the fathers' generation: Nestor in his formula, Phyleus in the Catalogue, Tydeus here, and later Oeneus and Peleus. Nestor's son Antilochus fights in Book 5, where Nestor himself is named only as his father (5.565; [[great-hearted]]).
