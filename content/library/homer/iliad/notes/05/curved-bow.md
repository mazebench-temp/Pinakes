---
summary: The curved bow of Pandarus, which he wishes he had never taken down from its peg; and the curved chariot and curved wheels, the other things the adjective describes.
grc: [ἀγκύλα τόξα, καμπύλον ἅρμα, καμπύλα κύκλα]
---

Pandarus sees Diomedes rushing across the plain and “at once he drew his curved bow at the son of Tydeus” (5.97). A hundred lines later he curses the same weapon, in a synonym: “So it was in an evil hour that I took my curved bow from its peg” (ἀγκύλα τόξα, 5.209), and he swears to break it and throw it in the fire if he ever gets home (5.212–216; [[bow]], [[pandarus]]). ἀγκύλος (“bent”) is another adjective of the bow, from the same root as ἀγκών (“elbow, bend”).

καμπύλος describes two other bent things in Book 5: the “curved chariot” that Pandarus thinks the horses will draw better under Aeneas, the driver they know (5.231; [[chariot]]), and the “curved wheels” that Hebe puts on Hera's chariot (5.722; [[hebe]]).
