summary: The sheep of Book 5 are prey. A wounded lion scatters a woolly flock left undefended in its fold (5.136–142), and two young lions carry off fat sheep from the farmsteads of men (5.556).
grc: [ὀΐεσσι, ἴφια μῆλα]
refs: [5.140-141]
---

**The undefended flock.** Diomedes, wounded, goes back into the fight like a lion that a shepherd “watching over his woolly sheep” (ἐπʼ εἰροπόκοις ὀΐεσσι, 5.137) has grazed but not killed. The shepherd hides in the farm buildings, and the sheep, “left undefended, flee in panic; they lie heaped close one upon another” (5.140–141), huddled or dead, while the lion leaps out of the fold (see [[wounded-lion]]). εἰροπόκος, “woolly” (εἶρος, “wool”, and πόκος, “fleece”), is found only twice in Homer, here and of the Cyclops' sheep under whose bellies Odysseus' men escape (Odyssey 9.443; see [[woolly]]). In the simile the flock is the Trojan army, and its shepherd, who wounded the lion but could not stop it, is Pandarus.

**Fat sheep.** The two lions of the twins' simile “seize cattle and fat sheep” (βόας καὶ ἴφια μῆλα, 5.556; see [[two-lions]]). μῆλα are the small livestock, sheep and goats together, and ἴφια μῆλα, “fat sheep”, is a formula for them beside the cattle (see [[fat-sheep]] and [[cattle]]).

In Book 4 the Trojan army was a fold of bleating ewes (4.433–436; see [[sheep-in-the-fold]]). In Book 5 the fold is broken into.
