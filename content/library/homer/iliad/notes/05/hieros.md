---
summary: Sacred are Apollo's citadel of Pergamus, where Aeneas is healed, the threshing floors of the winnowing simile, and Ilios in Sarpedon's answer to Tlepolemus.
grc: [ἱερῇ, ἱερὰς]
---

Apollo sets Aeneas apart from the throng “in sacred Pergamus, where his temple had been built” (5.446; [[pergamus]], [[temple]]). The winnowing simile has “the sacred threshing floors” where the wind carries off the chaff (5.499; [[winnowing]]). The threshing floor may be sacred as the place of Demeter's work, which the simile names in the next verse ([[fair-haired]]), or the adjective may keep its older and wider range. The third use is the city. Sarpedon grants Tlepolemus that Heracles “did indeed destroy sacred Ilios” (5.648; [[heracles-sacks-troy]]). The holiness of the city did not save it from the first sack.
