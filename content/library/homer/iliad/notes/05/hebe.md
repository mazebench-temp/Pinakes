The wheels go on when the chariot is to be driven; while Hera harnesses the horses, Hebe fits the bronze wheels to the iron axle (see [[chariot]] and [[hera]]). She works beside Hera as a daughter of the house.

At the end of the book she bathes Ares and dresses him “in lovely clothes” (5.905), the care that the women of a house give a man home from battle or a journey. Andromache has a bath heated for Hector's return from the fighting (22.442–444), and in the Odyssey Nestor's youngest daughter bathes Telemachus (Od. 3.464–468). Washed and dressed, the god whom Zeus has just called the most hateful of all sits down beside him “exulting in his glory” (5.906; see [[ares]] and [[exulting-in-glory]]).
