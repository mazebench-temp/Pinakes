---
summary: Hera recalls the heavy spear of Achilles, and Athena takes up a spear “heavy, huge, and massive” in the verse that later describes Achilles' own.
grc: [βριθὺ μέγα στιβαρόν]
---

Two heavy spears are named in Book 5, neither in use on the field. Hera, in Stentor's likeness, reminds the Argives that while Achilles fought the Trojans never came out before the Dardanian gates, “for they dreaded his heavy spear” (5.788–790; [[bronze-voiced]], [[achilles]]). And as Athena mounts the flaming chariot she takes up her spear, “heavy, huge, and massive, with which she overpowers the ranks of men” (5.745–746; [[daughter-of-a-mighty-father]]). The half-verse βριθὺ μέγα στιβαρόν describes Athena's spear again in Book 8 and in the Odyssey (Il. 8.390; Od. 1.100), Achilles' Pelian ash, which no other Achaean could wield (Il. 16.141, 19.388), and the spear that shatters in Patroclus' hands when Apollo strikes him (Il. 16.801–802). βριθύς (“heavy”) is from the family of ὄβριμος. The goddess's spear and Achilles' are described in the same words.
