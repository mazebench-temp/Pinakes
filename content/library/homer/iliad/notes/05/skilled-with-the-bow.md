---
summary: Sthenelus names Pandarus to Diomedes as a man “well skilled with the bow,” just before Pandarus gives up the bow for the spear.
---

Sthenelus sees the chariot of Aeneas and Pandarus coming and names the two: “one is well skilled with the bow, Pandarus, and he claims to be the son of Lycaon” (5.245–246; [[sthenelus]], [[pandarus]]). In Book 4 Agamemnon did not know the name of the man, “someone well skilled with the bow,” who had wounded Menelaus (4.196). Here the archer is named with his skill, and a few lines later he abandons it, throwing his spear because his arrows have failed him (5.276–280; [[swift-shaft]], [[long-shadowed]]). The skill that made him famous does not save him ([[skilled-in-fighting]]).
