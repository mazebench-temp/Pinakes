summary: Sarpedon tells Hector to beg the leaders of his far-famed allies to hold on, and Diomedes presses on to strip Aeneas of his glorious armor.
grc: [τηλεκλειτῶν, κλυτὰ τεύχεα]
en: [far-famed]
---

The fuller form τηλεκλειτός, “famous far and wide,” is used of the allies in the speech of an ally. Sarpedon ends his rebuke by telling Hector what he should be doing night and day: “begging the leaders of your far-famed allies to hold on unceasingly” (5.490–492; [[trojan-allies]], [[sarpedon]]). The praise of the allies' fame comes from one of them, who has just told how far he came and what he left at home (5.478–481; [[eddying]]).

The adjective κλυτός, from the same family, describes the armor that a victor strips from the dead. Diomedes, though he knows Apollo is protecting Aeneas, is eager “to kill Aeneas and strip off his glorious armor” (5.434–435; [[stripping-the-dead]], [[like-a-god]]).
