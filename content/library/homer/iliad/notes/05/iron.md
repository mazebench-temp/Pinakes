---
summary: The wheels of Hera's chariot are fitted “at either end of the iron axle” (5.723): the one part of the gods' finest chariot made of iron, among wheels and tires of bronze, rims of gold and hubs of silver.
grc: [σιδηρέῳ]
---

Hebe puts the curved bronze wheels on Hera's chariot, “of bronze, with eight spokes, at either end of the iron axle” (σιδηρέῳ ἄξονι, 5.722–723). Every part of the chariot has its metal: the rims are gold, the tires bronze, the hubs and the pole silver, the yoke and the breast straps gold (5.724–731; see [[chariot]] and [[gold-and-silver]]). Iron goes where strength is needed, in the axle that bears the whole. In Homer iron is the metal of tools and the image of what does not yield (see [[iron]]). Diomedes' mortal chariot has an axle of oak, and it groans when the goddess mounts it (5.838–839).
