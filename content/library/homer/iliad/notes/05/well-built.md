---
summary: Book 5 has the epithet once, of Pherae, home of the sons of Diocles; the translation's “well-built” also renders ποιητός, of Lycaon's house and of the gates of Troy.
grc: [ἐϋκτιμένῃ, δόμοις ἔνι ποιητοῖσιν, εὖ ποιητῇσι]
---

ἐϋκτίμενος names the home of two brothers about to die: their father Diocles “lived in well-built Pherae, wealthy in substance” (5.543–544; [[pherae-in-messenia]], [[wealthy]]).

The translation uses the same English for another adjective, ποιητός (“made,” and so “well-made”), the verbal adjective of ποιέω. Pandarus remembers the instructions his father gave him “in his well-built house” (δόμοις ἔνι ποιητοῖσιν, 5.198; [[lycaon]]), and Ares, disguised as Acamas, asks the sons of Priam whether they will wait until the Achaeans are fighting “around the well-built gates” (πύλῃς εὖ ποιητῇσι, 5.466; [[stormer-of-walls]]). The first names a house the speaker will not see again; the second, gates the enemy has not yet reached.
