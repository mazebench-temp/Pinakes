---
summary: Diomedes draws back before Apollo, “avoiding the wrath of Apollo who shoots from afar.”
---

After the fourth assault and Apollo's warning, “the son of Tydeus drew back a little way, avoiding the wrath of Apollo who shoots from afar” (5.443–444; [[far-worker]], [[three-times-and-a-fourth]]). The archer's epithet names the god's reach at the moment when Diomedes, who has come too close, steps back out of it. μῆνις, the wrath of the poem's first word, is the gods' anger here as in Book 1, and Diomedes avoids it ([[menis]]). In Book 1 the phrase named the god whose priest Agamemnon dishonored.
