  - 5.418-430 | Athena mocks Aphrodite, and Zeus smiles
---

Athena and Hera, watching Dione heal Aphrodite, provoke Zeus “with cutting words”: Cypris, Athena says, must have been urging one of the Achaean women to follow the Trojans, and scratched her hand on a golden brooch while caressing her (5.418–425; see [[brooch]] and [[kertomia]]).

{{quote:a1050ae1-7a2c-5e7d-a764-e9674819233c}}

The verse is not the reply formula, τὸν δʼ ἐπιμειδήσας προσέφη, but its narrative form, ὣς φάτο, μείδησεν δέ, “So she spoke, and … smiled”. The same verse, with the same subject, gives Zeus' smile at Hera's oath in Book 15 (15.47), and Hera smiles in the same way at Hephaestus in Book 1 (1.595; see [[laughter-of-the-gods]]). The smile is again the stronger speaker's. Zeus is amused by the joke, and indulgent to the daughter it mocks: he calls her τέκνον ἐμόν, “my child”, and sends her back to the works of marriage (see [[aphrodite]] and [[zeus]]).
