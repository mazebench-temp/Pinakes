---
summary: Book 5 names Athena “gray-eyed” eight times, from the moment she leads Ares from the battle to the moment she catches his spear in her hand.
---

The epithet marks the goddess in her own actions. “Gray-eyed Athena” takes Ares by the hand and leads him out of the battle (5.29–30) and leaves Diomedes after strengthening him (5.133). In Dione's words it is “the gray-eyed goddess Athena” who set Diomedes on Aphrodite (5.405), and the same formula introduces Athena's mockery of Aphrodite before Zeus (5.420). In the second half of the book it follows her decision to act: she does not disobey Hera (5.719), she speeds to Diomedes (5.793), she answers him (5.825), and she catches Ares' spear in her hand and pushes it away from the chariot (5.853). The last use is the most physical. The goddess with the gleaming eyes, who wears the helmet of Hades so that Ares cannot see her, is not seen by her enemy ([[helmet-of-hades]], [[athena]]).
