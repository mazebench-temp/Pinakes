**The ships.** Among the Trojans killed in the first part of the book is Phereclus, son of the builder, “the son of Harmon” (5.59–60), a craftsman whom Pallas Athena loved above all others.

{{quote:9906a6ab-fb26-5b59-8bab-d82d9ba5c3f5}}

νῆας ἐΐσας are “balanced ships” (see [[balanced-ships]]), and ἀρχέκακοι, “beginning the evil” (ἀρχή, “beginning”, and κακόν, “evil”), is found only here (see [[that-began-the-evil]]). The ships in which Alexander sailed to Sparta are the first link in the chain that ends in the war, and the man who built them dies in it (see [[phereclus]], [[harmon]] and [[the-beginning-of-evils]]). Whether it was Phereclus or Alexander who “knew nothing of the oracles of the gods” (5.64) the Greek does not make plain, and Homer does not say what the oracles were.

**Athena's joke.** When Aphrodite comes to Olympus with her wounded hand, Athena teases Zeus.

{{quote:aa77859a-d270-5011-98d6-856cb8b793c5}}

The joke has a target. The Achaean woman whom Cypris coaxed into following the Trojans is Helen, and the goddess who brought Helen to Alexander's bed in Book 3 (3.383–420; see [[helen-and-aphrodite]]) is imagined at the same work again, scratched by a brooch as she caresses her next conquest (see [[brooch]] and [[diomedes-wounds-aphrodite]]).
