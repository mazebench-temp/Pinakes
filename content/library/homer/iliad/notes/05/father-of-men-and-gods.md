---
summary: Athena mocks the wounded Aphrodite, “and the father of men and gods smiled.”
---

“So she spoke, and the father of men and gods smiled, and he called golden Aphrodite and said to her” (5.426–427; [[smiling-at-him]], [[golden]]). The title of the head of the household comes as he settles a quarrel among his daughters with a smile, as a father might. He tells Aphrodite that the works of war are not given to her and leaves them to “swift Ares and Athena” (5.428–430; [[swift]]). At the end of the book he is less indulgent with his son (5.888–898; [[aidelos]]).
