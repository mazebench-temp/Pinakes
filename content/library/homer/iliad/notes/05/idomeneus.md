He is the second of the six leaders who kill a man each when the Trojans give way (5.37–83; see [[agamemnon]] and [[androktasia]]). In Book 4 Agamemnon praised him above all the Danaans in war (4.257); here he is named with the epithet δουρικλυτός, “famed for his spear” (5.45; see [[famed-for-his-spear]]). His man is a Maeonian from Tarne whose name is that of a Cretan city, Phaestus (see [[phaestus-son-of-borus]]). He stabs him in the right shoulder as he mounts his chariot, and the man falls from it. The same verse and half-verse tell how Idomeneus' squire, Meriones, kills Acamas in Book 16 (5.46–47 ~ 16.343–344; see [[meriones]]).

“The attendants of Idomeneus stripped him of his armor” (5.48): the king leaves the spoils to his θεράποντες, his squires. He has no more part in Book 5. His great day comes in the battle at the ships in Book 13.
