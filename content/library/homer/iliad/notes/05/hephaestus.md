Dares is “a priest of Hephaestus” (ἱρεὺς Ἡφαίστοιο, 5.10), and his son is the first man Diomedes kills in his great day (see [[dares]], [[phegeus]] and [[idaeus-son-of-dares]]). The god hides the second son in the gods' usual cover, darkness, here “night” (νυκτὶ καλύψας), as Apollo will hide Aeneas in a dark cloud (5.344–345; see [[divine-rescue]]). The narrator gives the god's reason, and it is not the young man's life but the old priest's grief. A god keeps faith with the man who serves him, as Apollo does for Chryses in Book 1 (see [[chryses]] and [[priesthood]]).

The favor has limits. One son dies, and Hephaestus saves the other, so that Dares keeps an heir; Phaenops and Diocles lose both their sons later in the book (5.152–158, 5.541–553; see [[phaenops]], [[diocles]] and [[bereaved-parents]]). And the god who saves a Trojan here takes the Achaean side when the gods go to war (20.33–37). Here a priest's claim on his god counts for more than the sides of the war.
