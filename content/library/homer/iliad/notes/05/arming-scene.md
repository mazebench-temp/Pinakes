summary: Athena arms for war on Olympus, the fullest arming of a god in the poem (5.733–747). She lets fall her robe and puts on the tunic of Zeus, the aegis and a golden helmet, and takes up her huge spear as she steps into Hera's chariot. The scene is repeated, without the aegis and the helmet, in Book 8.

**A god's order.** A mortal arms in a fixed order: greaves, corselet, sword, shield, helmet, spear (see [[arming-scene]]). Athena's arming keeps the frame and changes the pieces. She begins by putting off her own robe, the work of her hands (see [[robe]]). In place of a corselet she puts on the tunic of her father, and the verb of the corselet, θωρήσσετο, “she armed herself”, sums up the arming (5.736–737; see [[tunic]] and [[corselet]]). She has no greaves and no sword. The aegis goes “around her shoulders”, in the half-verse with which Paris slings his sword around his (ἀμφὶ δʼ ἄρʼ ὤμοισιν βάλετο, 5.738; 3.334), and it takes the place of the shield (5.738–742; see [[aegis]]). Then the helmet: κρατὶ δʼ ἐπʼ, “and on her head”, the start of every helmet verse, with a golden helmet of her own (5.743–744; see [[helmet]] and [[helmet-of-a-hundred-cities]]). Last, as in every arming, the spear, taken up as she steps into the chariot, “heavy, huge, and massive, with which she overpowers the ranks of men, of heroes, against whom she, the daughter of the mighty father, bears resentment” (5.745–747; see [[spear]] and [[daughter-of-a-mighty-father]]).

**A king arms like a goddess.** Agamemnon's arming in Book 11 shares Athena's helmet verse (11.41 = 5.743), and his shield carries the Gorgon with Terror and Rout around her, as the aegis carries the Gorgon's head crowned with Rout (11.36–37, 5.739–741). When he has armed, Athena and Hera thunder to honor him (11.45–46).

**Repeated.** In Book 8 Athena arms again in the same verses, the robe, the tunic of Zeus and the spear, but without the aegis and the helmet (8.384–391 ~ 5.733–737, 5.745–747). That time Zeus sends Iris to turn the goddesses back before they reach the battle (8.397–437). In Book 5 the arming leads to battle: the goddess drives Diomedes' chariot against Ares and drives his spear home (5.835–863; see [[athena]] and [[chariot-journey]]).
