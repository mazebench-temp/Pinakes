summary: Book 5's long spears are μακρός, not δολιχός; the translation renders both “long,” and the phrase follows the spears of Idomeneus, Aeneas, Tlepolemus and Sarpedon.
grc: [ἔγχεϊ μακρῷ, δουρί τε μακρῷ, δούρατα μακρὰ, δόρυ μακρὸν]
en: [long spear]
---

Book 5 does not use δολίχʼ ἔγχεα. Its long spear is μακρός (“long, tall”), the adjective of [[high-olympus]], in several phrases. Idomeneus stabs Phaestus “with his long spear” (5.45). Aeneas leaps down “with his shield and his long spear” to stand over Pandarus (5.297; [[fighting-over-the-body]]). In the duel of Tlepolemus and Sarpedon “the long spears of both” fly at the same moment, and Tlepolemus strikes Sarpedon's thigh “with his long spear” (5.656–660). As Sarpedon is carried away, “the long spear weighed him down as it dragged,” because no one thought to draw it out (5.664–667; [[ash-spear]], [[tlepolemus-and-sarpedon]]). The length that keeps an enemy at a distance becomes the weight of the wound.
