---
summary: In Book 5 the gods stop watching and come down. Athena and Hera look on only to mock Aphrodite, Hera sees the Argives dying and drives to the battle, and Zeus alone sits apart on the peak of Olympus.
refs: [5.418, 5.711-712, 5.753-754]
---

In Book 4 the gods sat on the golden floor of Zeus' house, drinking and “looking out upon the city of the Trojans” (4.1–4). In Book 5 they leave their seats. Athena drives in a mortal's chariot, Ares fights beside Hector, Aphrodite and Apollo carry Aeneas from the field, and Enyo brings Tumult (see [[gods-and-mortals]]). The watching that is left is brief. On Olympus Athena and Hera, “looking on” (εἰσορόωσαι) as Dione heals Aphrodite, turn what they see into a joke (5.418–425; see [[laughter-of-the-gods]]). Hera watches the battle only long enough to act: “when the white-armed goddess Hera saw them killing the Argives in the fierce battle”, she proposes to Athena that they go down (5.711–718). They find Zeus “sitting apart from the other gods on the topmost peak of many-ridged Olympus” (5.753–754), the one god who has kept out of the fighting. He will watch the battle from Ida in Book 8 (8.51–52).
