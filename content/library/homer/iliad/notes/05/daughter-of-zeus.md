---
summary: Aphrodite is “daughter of Zeus” when Athena permits Diomedes to wound her, when she rescues Aeneas, and when Diomedes, wounding her, orders her out of the war.
grc: [Διὸς θύγατερ]
---

The title follows Aphrodite through the book. Athena gives Diomedes leave to wound her in a verse that names her by her father: “but if Aphrodite, daughter of Zeus, comes into the war, her you may wound with the sharp bronze” (5.131–132), and Diomedes repeats it word for word at the end of the book (5.820–821). She comes into the war as “daughter of Zeus” to save Aeneas, in the verse that brought her to save Paris in Book 3 (5.312; [[divine-rescue]], [[would-have-had-not]]). And Diomedes, having wounded her, shouts at her with the title in the vocative: “Keep away, daughter of Zeus, from war and combat” (5.348; [[aphrodite]]). Her father confirms the order in his own words: the works of war are not given to her (5.428–430; [[golden]]).
