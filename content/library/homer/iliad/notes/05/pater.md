---
summary: In Book 5 Athena gives Diomedes his father's might, Phaenops is left a father without sons, and on Olympus Athena, Hera and Ares each come to Zeus with a complaint that begins “Father Zeus”.
grc: [πατρώϊον, πατέρι]
---

**The father's might.** Athena tells Diomedes that she has put into his breast μένος πατρώϊον, “your father's might, undaunted, such as the horseman Tydeus, wielder of the shield, used to have” (5.125–126; see [[menos]] and [[tydeus]]). The adjective πατρώϊος, “of the father”, makes the gift an inheritance.

**A father left.** Diomedes kills the two sons of old Phaenops, “and to their father he left lamentation and grievous sorrows” (πατέρι δὲ γόον καὶ κήδεα λυγρὰ λεῖπʼ, 5.156–157; see [[bereaved-parents]]).

**Father Zeus.** On Olympus the gods bring their quarrels to their father. Athena begins her mockery of Aphrodite with “Father Zeus, will you be angry with me for what I say?” (5.421); Hera begins her complaint against Ares with “Father Zeus, do you not blame Ares for these violent deeds” (5.757), and asks, in Athena's words, “Father Zeus, will you be angry with me” if she strikes him (5.762); Ares, wounded, begins “Father Zeus, do you see these violent deeds and not blame them?” (5.872). Zeus answers them as a father answers children who complain of one another (see [[father-zeus]], [[fathers-and-sons]] and [[the-gods-mirror-the-mortals]]).
