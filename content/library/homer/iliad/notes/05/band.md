---
summary: Athena drives Diomedes' spear into Ares “into the lowest part of his flank, where he girded on his band” (5.857), the place where the band saved Menelaus from Pandarus' arrow in Book 4.
---

Ares lunges at Diomedes, and Athena pushes his spear away; then Diomedes drives at him with his bronze spear, “and Pallas Athena drove it home into the lowest part of his flank, where he girded on his band” (ὅθι ζωννύσκετο μίτρῃ, 5.856–857). The spear tears through the god's skin and is drawn out again (5.858–859; see [[diomedes-wounds-ares]]). The μίτρη is the guard worn around the belly under the belt, made by bronze-smiths (4.187, 4.216). In Book 4 it held: Pandarus' arrow went through Menelaus' belt and corselet, but “the band he wore to guard his flesh” protected him most, and the arrow only grazed the skin (4.137–139; see [[band]]). A god wears a band too, and with a goddess's weight behind a man's spear it does not hold.

Oresbius, killed by Hector and Ares, is αἰολομίτρης, “of the flashing band” (5.707; see [[of-the-flashing-band]] and [[oresbius]]).
