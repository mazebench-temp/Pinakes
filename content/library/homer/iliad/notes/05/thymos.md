---
summary: In Book 5 the θυμός is taken from the dying, gasped away and breathed back by Sarpedon, turned by Athena in Odysseus' deliberation, and troubled in wounded gods.
grc: [μεγάθυμον]
en: [lives]
passages:
  - 5.669-679 | Odysseus ponders
---

**Life taken.** Most often in Book 5 the θυμός is the life a killer takes. Diomedes “took away the dear life” of the two sons of Phaenops (φίλον δʼ ἐξαίνυτο θυμὸν, 5.155), and Ares, stripping Periphas, has “taken away his life” (5.848). Aphrodite spreads her robe before Aeneas and Apollo hides him in a cloud, both “for fear one of the Danaans of the swift horses might strike the bronze into his chest and take away his life” (5.316–317 = 5.345–346). Hector darts past the wounded Sarpedon to “take the lives of many” (5.691).

**Life breathed back.** Sarpedon, when the spear is drawn from his thigh, faints, and the breath of the North Wind “revived his spirit, which he had so grievously gasped away” (κακῶς κεκαφηότα θυμόν, 5.697–698; see [[psyche]]). The θυμός, like the ψυχή, is close to breath.

**The seat of choice.** Seeing Tlepolemus carried off, Odysseus “pondered in his mind and in his heart” (κατὰ φρένα καὶ κατὰ θυμόν) whether to pursue Sarpedon or kill more Lycians; it was not fated for him to kill the son of Zeus, “so Athena turned his heart against the multitude of the Lycians” (5.671–676; see [[pondering-two-ways]]). The choice is Odysseus' and the goddess's at once (see [[divine-intervention]]). His θυμός is τλήμων, “enduring” (5.670), the quality for which he is called πολύτλας, “much-enduring”; here the enduring heart rages, and a goddess turns it.

**Gods have one too.** The arrow in Hades' shoulder “troubled his spirit” (κῆδε δὲ θυμόν, 5.400), and Ares sits beside Zeus “grieving in his heart” (θυμὸν ἀχεύων, 5.869). Tlepolemus tells Sarpedon that his is “a coward's heart” (κακὸς μὲν θυμός, 5.643), and Athena tells Diomedes that his father had “the strong spirit he always had” (5.806). The θυμός measures a man, and a god, as well as keeping him alive. See [[achos]].
