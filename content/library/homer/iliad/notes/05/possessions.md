---
summary: Old Phaenops loses his only two sons to Diomedes and has no heir, so that “distant kinsmen divided his wealth among them” (5.154–158); Sarpedon left his possessions in Lycia with his wife and son (5.480–481).
grc: [κτεάτεσσι, κτῆσιν]
en: [wealth]
---

**No heir.** Diomedes kills Xanthus and Thoon, the two sons of Phaenops, “both his darlings; and he was worn down by grievous old age, and had begotten no other son to leave as heir to his possessions” (5.152–154). The narrator follows the loss past the deaths to the house: “to their father he left lamentation and grievous sorrows, since he did not receive them back alive, returned from battle; and distant kinsmen divided his wealth among them” (5.156–158). χηρωσταί, “distant kinsmen”, is from χῆρος, “bereft”: the heirs of a house left empty. κτέαρ and κτῆσις are what a man has acquired, the words of κτῆμα (see [[possessions]]); what should have gone to the sons goes to strangers (see [[phaenops]], [[bereaved-parents]] and [[androktasia]]).

**Left behind.** Sarpedon, an ally, came from far-off Lycia, where he left his dear wife, his infant son, “and many possessions, which the needy man longs for” (5.480–481). He fights though he has nothing at Troy to lose (see [[raiding]] and [[sarpedon]]). Amphius, killed by Ajax, lived in Paesus “rich in possessions, rich in grainland” (πολυκτήμων πολυλήϊος, 5.613), but his fate led him to Troy.
