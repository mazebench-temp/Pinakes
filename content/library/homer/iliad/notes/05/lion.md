Before Book 5 the Iliad has one lion, the hungry lion to which Menelaus is compared when he sees Alexander (3.23–28; see [[lion-on-a-carcass]]). Book 5 has six, and they follow the course of the battle.

**Diomedes.** The first two lions are Diomedes after his wound. Pandarus' arrow has only grazed the beast: he goes back into the fighting like a lion that a shepherd has wounded but not killed as it leapt into the fold, and that scatters the flock and leaps out again (5.136–143; see [[wounded-lion]]). A few lines later he takes two sons of Priam from one chariot as a lion leaps among cattle and breaks the neck of a heifer (5.161–164; see [[lion-among-cattle]]). The first lion is hurt and grows fiercer; the second kills without a check.

**Aeneas.** When Pandarus falls, Aeneas stands over the body “like a lion trusting in his courage”, spear and shield held in front of it (5.299–302; see [[like-a-lion]]). This lion is not hunting but defending. It is felled at once, by a stone two men of today could not lift (5.302–310).

**The dogs.** Sarpedon turns the image against the Trojans. Hector's brothers and brothers-in-law “cower like dogs around a lion” (5.476; see [[dogs-around-a-lion]]): the lion is the enemy, and the princes of Troy are the dogs that bark and keep their distance.

**The lions killed.** The twin sons of Diocles fall to Aeneas like two lions reared by their mother on the mountain peaks, which raid men's farms “until they themselves too are killed at the hands of men with the sharp bronze” (5.554–558; see [[two-lions]]). Here the lion is the hunted beast, and men win.

**Lions and boars.** At the end of the book the best of the Argives stand crowded around Diomedes “like lions that eat raw flesh or wild boars” (5.782–783; see [[lions-and-boars]] and [[boar]]).

The lion is the beast of the aristeia (see [[the-aristeia-of-diomedes]]). It gives the hero's fury and his speed, and it can be wounded and killed. Tlepolemus uses the lion in an epithet for his father Heracles, θυμολέων, “lion-hearted” (5.639; see [[lion-hearted]]).
