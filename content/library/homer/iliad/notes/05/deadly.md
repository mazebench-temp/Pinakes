---
summary: Ares calls Athena οὐλομένη, “ruinous,” the participle of the Iliad's first epithet, in his complaint to Zeus.
grc: [οὐλομένην]
---

The participle οὐλόμενος, from the verb ὄλλυμι (“destroy”), is the first epithet of the Iliad: “that ruinous wrath” (1.2; [[menis]]). Book 5 uses it once, in the same form and the same place at the head of the verse. Ares complains of his half-sister: “for you begot that senseless daughter, ruinous, whose mind is always on lawless deeds” (5.875–876; [[athena]], [[aidelos]]). The word that named Achilles' wrath is spoken by one god of another, in a quarrel that Zeus settles by calling the complainer the most hateful of the gods (5.890).
