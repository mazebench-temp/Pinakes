---
summary: In Book 5 Athena puts on the aegis, with the Gorgon's head, “the portent of aegis-bearing Zeus,” and the epithet names Zeus as father of Athena, Heracles and Sarpedon.
---

Most uses name Zeus as a father. Athena is “child of aegis-bearing Zeus” in prayer and address (5.115, 5.714; [[child-of-aegis-bearing-zeus]]), “daughter of aegis-bearing Zeus” as she arms and as Diomedes recognizes her (5.733, 5.815). Heracles is “the son of aegis-bearing Zeus” in Dione's story (5.396), and Tlepolemus tells Sarpedon that men lie when they call him “the offspring of aegis-bearing Zeus” (5.635; [[son-of-zeus]]). Sarpedon is laid “under the beautiful oak of aegis-bearing Zeus” (5.693; [[the-oak-of-zeus]]).

The aegis itself appears in the arming of Athena, with Rout, Strife, Courage, Onslaught and the Gorgon's head on it, “dreadful and terrible, the portent of aegis-bearing Zeus” (5.738–742; [[aegis]], [[tasseled]], [[gorgon]]). The goddess who puts on her father's tunic and the aegis is named, a few lines later, as the daughter of a mighty father ([[daughter-of-a-mighty-father]]).
