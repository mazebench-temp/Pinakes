summary: Tlepolemus tells Sarpedon that those who call him a son of Zeus are lying; the narrator has just called him one, and Zeus keeps ruin from him in the duel.
grc: [ψευδόμενοι]
passages:
  - 5.628-662 | Tlepolemus and Sarpedon
---

Before their duel Tlepolemus, the son of Heracles, taunts Sarpedon: “They lie when they say you are the offspring of aegis-bearing Zeus, since you fall far short of those men who were born of Zeus among men of former times” (ψευδόμενοι δέ σέ φασι Διὸς γόνον αἰγιόχοιο εἶναι, 5.635–637). His proof is his own father, who sacked Troy with six ships (5.638–642). The audience knows better. The narrator has introduced the two men as “the son and the grandson of Zeus the cloud-gatherer” (5.631), and it is the grandson who dies. When Tlepolemus' spear goes into Sarpedon's thigh, “his father still kept ruin away” (5.662; see [[loigos]]). The insult is itself the lie (see [[sarpedon]], [[tlepolemus]] and [[men-of-today]]).
