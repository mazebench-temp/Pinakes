---
summary: Idaeus, son of Dares, would not have escaped black doom had Hephaestus not saved him; Sarpedon promises black doom to Tlepolemus, and deals it.
---

The formula follows its usual verb in the first use: Idaeus “himself would not have escaped black doom either, but Hephaestus protected him, and saved him, covering him in night” (5.22–23; [[idaeus-son-of-dares]], [[divine-rescue]], [[would-have-had-not]]). In the second, Sarpedon turns it into a threat: “But for you, I say, slaughter and black doom will be made here at my hands” (5.652–653; [[of-the-famous-horses]]). Nearly the same three verses are Odysseus' threat to Socus in Book 11 (Il. 11.443–445). Sarpedon's spear goes through Tlepolemus' neck a few lines later (5.657–659).
