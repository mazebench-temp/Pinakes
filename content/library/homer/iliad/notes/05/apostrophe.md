---
summary: Once in Book 5 the narrator turns to his listener: “as for the son of Tydeus, you could not have told which side he was on” (5.85–86), so fast does Diomedes move through both armies.
grc: ["οὐκ ἂν γνοίης"]
en: [you could not have told]
---

Six Achaean leaders have each killed a man, “so they toiled on through the fierce battle” (5.84), and the narrator comes back to Diomedes by speaking to his hearer: “but as for the son of Tydeus, you could not have told which side he was on, whether he was mingling with the Trojans or with the Achaeans” (5.85–86). The potential optative γνοίης, “you could have known”, puts an imagined witness on the field, as “you would not have seen” and “you would not have said” did in Book 4 (4.223, 4.429). What the witness cannot tell is the measure of the hero's speed: he is everywhere at once. The simile that follows gives the same speed another shape, a winter torrent breaking down dikes and orchard walls (5.87–94; see [[river-in-flood]] and [[the-aristeia]]).
