**The oracles.** Meriones kills Phereclus, whose father was a builder and whom Athena loved above all others. He built Alexander's ships, “that began the evil, which became an evil for all the Trojans and for himself, since he knew nothing of the oracles of the gods” (5.63–64). θέσφατα are “things spoken by a god”, from θεός and the root of φημί, “speak”: divine decrees made known. The poem does not say what the oracles were, only that ignorance of them brought ruin on the builder and his city (see [[phereclus]], [[ships]] and [[the-beginning-of-evils]]). Later tradition supplied them. In the *Cypria*, as Proclus summarizes it, Alexander built his ships at Aphrodite's prompting, and Helenus and Cassandra foretold what would come of the voyage (see [[proclus]]).

**Foreboding.** Sarpedon, carried out of the battle with a spear in his thigh, begs Hector not to leave him for the Danaans: “and afterward let life leave me in your city, since I, it seems, was not to return home to my dear native land and gladden my dear wife and my infant son” (5.685–688). ἄρα, “it seems”, marks a man who sees now what was always so. He lives this time; his death is kept for Book 16, and then Sleep and Death carry his body home to Lycia (16.666–683; see [[sarpedon]]).

**A threat unfulfilled.** Dione tells her daughter that Diomedes does not know “that the man who fights with the immortals does not live long, and his children do not call him papa at his knees when he comes home from war”, and that his wife Aegialeia will wake the household with her wailing for him (5.406–415; see [[who-fights-the-gods]] and [[aegialeia]]). The poem does not fulfill it. Diomedes survives the war, and in the Odyssey Nestor tells how his companions brought their ships safe to Argos (Odyssey 3.180–182).

**An empty word?** Hera, seeing Ares and Hector cut down the Argives, fears that the goddesses' promise to Menelaus, “that he would sack well-walled Ilios and then sail home”, was “an empty word” (5.715–716). The fall of Troy is certain in the poem (see [[sack-of-troy-foretold]]), and it is a goddess who doubts it.
