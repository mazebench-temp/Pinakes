---
summary: The last scene of Book 5: Ares, healed and bathed, sits down beside Zeus, exulting in his glory, in the verse that seated Briareus beside Zeus in Book 1.
---

{{quote:44f2e8fa-520b-5ce6-aa3b-b634e1381606}}

Paeëon has healed Ares' wound as fig juice curdles milk, and Hebe bathes him and dresses him (5.899–905; [[pain-killing]], [[fig-juice]], [[hebe]]). He takes his place beside his father, “exulting in his glory,” in almost the verse that sat the Hundred-Hander beside Zeus when he came to protect him (1.405; [[hundred-handed]]). A few lines earlier the same god sat down beside Zeus “grieving in his heart” (5.869; [[son-of-cronus]]). His glory is restored as quickly as his flesh, although he has just lost to a mortal and heard his father call him the most hateful of the gods (5.889–890).
