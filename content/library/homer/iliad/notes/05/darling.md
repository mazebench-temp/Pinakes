The dual τηλυγέτω, “both his darlings,” is explained by what follows. Phaenops is old and has no other son to inherit his possessions, and the heirs will be χηρωσταί, distant kinsmen who take the goods of a house left without an heir ([[phaenops]], [[xanthus-son-of-phaenops]], [[thoon-son-of-phaenops]]). Of the senses proposed for the word, “late-born” and “only” both fit here. The passage is one of Book 5's short tales of a father's loss: Dares, Eurydamas and Diocles also lose their sons to Diomedes or Aeneas ([[the-war-in-small]], [[fathers-and-sons]]).
