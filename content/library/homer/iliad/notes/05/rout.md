{{quote:2f8333cd-89e4-5542-83cf-db1ae04bef8c}}

περὶ μὲν πάντῃ Φόβος ἐστεφάνωται, “all around which Rout is set as a crown” (5.739). ἐστεφάνωται is from στέφανος, “crown, circlet”: Rout runs all around, and within are Strife, Courage and Onslaught and the Gorgon's head (see [[strife]], [[courage]], [[onslaught]] and [[gorgon]]). The shield of Agamemnon is described with the same verb, the Gorgon set on it as a crown and Terror and Rout around her (ἐστεφάνωτο, 11.36–37; see [[terror]]). Whether these are figures worked on the aegis or the powers themselves riding on it, the text does not say. Either way the aegis carries panic flight as its first power. When Apollo carries it in Book 15, it is the aegis that Hephaestus gave Zeus “to put men to flight” (ἐς φόβον ἀνδρῶν, 15.310; see [[aegis]]).

The common noun is used twice in the book. Aeneas' horses are “masters of rout” (μήστωρε φόβοιο, 5.272), and Diomedes tells Sthenelus, “Do not talk to me of flight” (5.252).
