---
summary: Dione asks which of the heavenly gods has wounded Aphrodite, and Zeus threatens Ares with a place lower than the sons of Uranus, the gods below the earth.
grc: [Οὐρανιώνων]
en: [sons of Uranus]
---

Dione takes her wounded daughter in her arms and asks: “Who of the heavenly gods has done such things to you, dear child, for no reason, as if you were caught doing some wrong openly?” (5.373–374). She assumes that only a god could have hurt a goddess, and the answer is a mortal, the son of Tydeus (5.376; [[dione]], [[aphrodite]]). The same two verses return in Book 21, when Zeus asks them of Artemis, who has come to his knees in tears after Hera has beaten her (Il. 21.509–510).

The word has another sense at the end of the book. Zeus tells Ares that if he had been born of any other god, “destructive as you are, you would long ago have been lower down than the sons of Uranus” (5.897–898; [[aidelos]]). Here Οὐρανίωνες seem to be the Titans, the older gods whom Zeus keeps under the earth around Cronus. Elsewhere they are “the gods below, who are around Cronus” (Il. 14.274, 15.225), and Hera swears by “the gods beneath Tartarus, who are called Titans” (Il. 14.278–279). ἐνέρτερος, “lower,” at 5.898 is the adjective of those gods below (ἐνέρτεροι, Il. 15.225). The translation therefore renders the word here “the sons of Uranus,” though some take it as “the heavenly gods” in this line too ([[cronus]]).
