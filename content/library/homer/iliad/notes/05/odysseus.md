---
summary: Odysseus sees the dead Tlepolemus carried off and rages. Kept by fate from pursuing the wounded Sarpedon, he turns on the Lycians and kills seven of them, until Hector comes.

---

Odysseus has one scene in Book 5. Before it he is only one of the four who urge on the Danaans, with the two Ajaxes and Diomedes (5.519).

{{quote:8be185d8-2649-5d00-9ec7-51c4ed9059a0}}

τλήμονα θυμὸν ἔχων, “he of the enduring spirit”, belongs with his standing epithet πολύτλας, “much-enduring” (8.97 and often). Both are made from τλῆναι, “endure”, and so is the name of the dead man whose fall rouses him, Τληπόλεμος (see [[tlepolemus]]). His heart “raged” (μαίμησε), the verb just used of Tlepolemus' spear point as it sped through Sarpedon's thigh (μαιμώωσα, 5.661). He ponders two courses (see [[pondering-two-ways]]), and fate and Athena choose for him. He may not kill Sarpedon, whose death is kept for Patroclus (16.462–505), and Athena turns his heart against the Lycians (5.674–676; see [[athena]] and [[sarpedon]]).

{{quote:9663c102-c0b1-50ba-9933-3484579695a2}}

Seven names in two lines, with no father, home or wound (see [[androktasia]], [[coeranus-of-lycia]], [[alastor-of-lycia]], [[chromius-of-lycia]], [[alcandrus]], [[halius]], [[noemon-of-lycia]] and [[prytanis]]). The second half of the first line, Ἀλάστορά τε Χρομίον τε, named two of Nestor's captains in Book 4 (4.295). “And brilliant Odysseus would have killed still more of the Lycians, had not great Hector of the gleaming helmet been quick to see it” (5.679–680; see [[would-have-had-not]] and [[hector]]). In Book 4 he killed Democoon in anger for his companion Leucus (4.491–504); here the death of a fellow Achaean sends him among the Lycians.

Ovid's Ulysses, in the contest for the arms of Achilles, lists the seven among his own kills, in Homer's order (*Metamorphoses* 13; see [[ovid]]).
