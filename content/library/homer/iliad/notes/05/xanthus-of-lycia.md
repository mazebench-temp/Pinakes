---
summary: Sarpedon names the river as the mark of his far-off home, “for Lycia is far away, on the eddying Xanthus”, where he left his wife and infant son (5.479–480).
grc: ["=Ξάνθῳ"]
---

Sarpedon's words take up the last line of the Trojan catalogue, τηλόθεν ἐκ Λυκίης, Ξάνθου ἄπο δινήεντος, “from far-off Lycia, from the eddying Xanthus” (2.877). In both the river is δινήεις, “eddying” (see [[eddying]]), and in both it stands for distance: the Lycians have come farther than any other ally (see [[sarpedon]] and [[lycia]]).

Another Xanthus, a son of Phaenops, dies at Diomedes' hands earlier in the book (5.152; see [[xanthus-son-of-phaenops]]).
