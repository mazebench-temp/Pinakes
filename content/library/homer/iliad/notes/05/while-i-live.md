---
summary: Pandarus boasts that Diomedes “shall not look much longer on the bright light of the sun”, and Diomedes quotes the boast in his prayer to Athena (5.119–120). To see the light is to live.
grc: ["ὄψεσθαι λαμπρὸν φάος ἠελίοιο"]
en: [look much longer on the bright light of the sun]
---

Diomedes asks Athena to let him kill the man “who struck me first, and boasts over me, and says that I shall not look much longer on the bright light of the sun” (5.119–120; see [[prayer-type-scene]]). Pandarus had said only that the best of the Achaeans would not hold out long against his arrow (5.103–104). In the prayer his boast becomes a sentence of death, put in the language that makes life a matter of seeing: the living “see the light of the sun” (ὁρᾶν φάος ἠελίοιο), as Thetis says of her son while he lives (18.61), and the dead do not. The man who said it is the one who dies, struck through the face beside the eye (5.290–296; see [[pandarus]]).
