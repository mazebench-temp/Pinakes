---
summary: Captured horses are driven “to the well-greaved Achaeans,” in Diomedes' order and in its fulfillment, and the well-greaved Achaeans carry the dead Tlepolemus from the field.
---

Diomedes tells Sthenelus to rush at Aeneas' horses “and drive them away from the Trojans to the well-greaved Achaeans” (5.263–264), and Sthenelus does so in the same verse (5.323–324; [[fine-maned]], [[master-of-rout]]). The formula of the army closes the order and the act. The third use is after the duel of Tlepolemus and Sarpedon: “And on the other side the well-greaved Achaeans carried Tlepolemus out of the war” (5.668; [[tlepolemus-and-sarpedon]]), as the Lycians carry Sarpedon on theirs.
