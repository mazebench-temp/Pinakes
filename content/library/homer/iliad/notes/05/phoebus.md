---
summary: Phoebus Apollo rescues Aeneas, sends Ares into the battle, and through Ares keeps the Trojans fighting.
grc: [Φοίβου]
---

The full name marks Apollo's three acts in the middle of the book. When Aphrodite drops her wounded son, “Phoebus Apollo took him up in his arms and protected him in a dark cloud” (5.344–345; [[divine-rescue]], [[of-the-swift-horses]]). Having set Aeneas in his temple and made a phantom of him, “Phoebus Apollo addressed furious Ares” and sent him into the fight (5.454; [[ruin-of-mortals]]). And Ares covers the battle in night, “fulfilling the commands of Phoebus Apollo of the golden sword” (5.508–509; [[of-the-golden-sword]]). The genitive Φοίβου is new in the poem here.
