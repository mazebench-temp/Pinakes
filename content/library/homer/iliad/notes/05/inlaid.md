---
summary: Aeneas and Pandarus mount the inlaid chariot, and Athena lets fall her richly worked robe as she arms.
grc: [ποικίλον]
en: [richly worked]
---

Aeneas and Pandarus, having agreed who will drive and who will fight, “mounted the inlaid chariot and eagerly drove the swift horses at the son of Tydeus” (5.239–240; [[chariot]], [[swift-footed]]). In the arming of Athena the adjective describes cloth: she “let her soft robe fall on her father's floor, richly worked, which she herself had made and labored at with her hands” (5.734–735; [[robe]], [[arming-scene]]). The goddess of crafts takes off the work of her own hands to put on her father's tunic and the aegis.
