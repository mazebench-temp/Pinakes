---
summary: The sword does three of the killings of Book 5, all at close quarters and all with a terrible cut. Eurypylus shears off Hypsenor's arm, Diomedes cuts Hypeiron's shoulder from his neck, and Antilochus strikes Mydon on the temple (5.80–83, 5.146–147, 5.584).
grc: [φασγάνῳ, ξίφεϊ, ξίφει]
---

The spear does most of the killing in Book 5, and the sword comes out three times, each time at close quarters. Eurypylus runs down Hypsenor as he flees, “darting at him with his sword, and sheared off his heavy arm; and the bloody arm fell to the plain” (5.80–82), and the verse of purple death follows (see [[purple-death]] and [[hypsenor]]). The formula is used three times in the Iliad, and each time after a sword stroke (16.332–334, 20.475–477).

Diomedes kills a pair, Astynous with the spear and Hypeiron with the sword: “the other with his great sword on the collarbone beside the shoulder he struck, and cut the shoulder away from the neck and from the back” (5.146–147; see [[hypeiron]]). Antilochus stuns Mydon with a stone as he turns his horses, and then “struck him on the temple with his sword” (5.582–584; see [[mydon]] and [[stone-throwing]]).

Book 5 uses two of Homer's three words for the sword, φάσγανον for Eurypylus' (5.81) and ξίφος for Diomedes' and Antilochus' (5.146, 5.584). See [[androktasia]].
