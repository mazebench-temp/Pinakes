---
summary: The epithet of praise, rendered “noble,” is given to a priest, an archer and a father: Dares, Pandarus and Anchises.
---

“There was among the Trojans one Dares, wealthy and noble, a priest of Hephaestus” (5.9–10; [[dares]], [[wealthy]]). Aeneas finds Pandarus, “the son of Lycaon, noble and strong” (5.169), in the words that found him in Book 4 (4.89; [[krateros]]). Sthenelus tells Diomedes that Aeneas “claims that he was born the son of noble Anchises” (5.247; [[anchises]]). In none of these is the sense “blameless” needed. The word is general praise of a man of standing, the sense that Anne Amory Parry argued for.
