**His sons.** Diomedes "caught two sons of Priam, descendant of Dardanus, who were in one chariot, Echemmon and Chromius" (5.159–160; see [[echemmon]] and [[chromius-son-of-priam]]), and brings them down like a lion breaking the neck of a heifer. Ares, in the likeness of Acamas, calls on "Priam's sons, whom Zeus had nurtured", and asks how long they will let their people die (5.463–469; see [[sons-of-priam]]). Sarpedon tells Hector that he promised to hold the city "with your brothers-in-law and your brothers", and that none of them is to be seen (5.473–476).

**His honor.** Deicoon, companion of Aeneas, was honored by the Trojans "as they did the sons of Priam" (5.535–536; see [[deicoon]]), and Amphius of Paesus came to Troy because fate led him "to be an ally to Priam and his sons" (5.614; see [[amphius-son-of-selagus]]). The patronymic Δαρδανίδης, "descendant of Dardanus", given to Priam at 3.303, returns here in the genitive (5.159; see [[dardanus]]).
