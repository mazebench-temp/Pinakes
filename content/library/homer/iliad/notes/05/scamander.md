---
summary: The river of Troy appears as a god with a priest, Dolopion, whose son Eurypylus kills (5.76–78). Athena sets Ares down on its banks (5.36), and Hera halts her chariot where it meets the Simoeis (5.773–777).
grc: [Σκαμάνδρῳ, Σκαμάνδρου, Σκάμανδρος]
---

**A god with a priest.** Hypsenor is the son of Dolopion, “who had been made priest of the Scamander and was honored by the people like a god” (5.77–78; see [[dolopion]] and [[hypsenor]]). This is the first sign in the poem that the river is a god with a cult. Its priest is an ἀρητήρ, as Chryses is Apollo's (1.11). In Book 21 Achilles tells the Trojans that the river to which they sacrifice bulls and throw live horses will not save them (21.130–132), and the river himself rises against Achilles (21.211–382).

**The banks.** Athena takes Ares by the hand, leads him out of the fighting, and “sat him down by the high banks of the Scamander” (5.36). The god of war sits out the first part of the book beside the river, until Apollo sends him back into the battle (5.454–461).

**The meeting of the rivers.** Hera and Athena drive down from Olympus to Troy “and its two flowing rivers, where the Simoeis and the Scamander join their streams”, and there Hera unyokes her horses and the Simoeis makes ambrosia spring up for them to graze (5.773–777; see [[simoeis]]).

The river also gives its name to men. The hunter Scamandrius, whom Menelaus kills, is named for it (5.49; see [[scamandrius]]), as is Hector's son Astyanax (6.402).
