---
summary: Diomedes tells how far-seeing Zeus gave the horses of Tros in recompense for Ganymede.
---

“For they are of that breed which far-seeing Zeus gave to Tros in recompense for his son Ganymede, because they are the best of all horses under the dawn and the sun” (5.265–267; [[tros]], [[ganymede-and-the-horses-of-tros]], [[poine]]). The nominative εὐρύοπα Ζεύς, here at the end of the verse, names the god who took the boy and paid his father. Diomedes tells the story to explain why Aeneas' horses are worth taking ([[master-of-rout]]).
