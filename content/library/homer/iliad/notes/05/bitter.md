---
summary: Pandarus' bitter arrow goes through Diomedes' shoulder; Diomedes asks Sthenelus to draw it out; and Pandarus himself admits that the bitter arrow did not overcome him.
---

Pandarus' next bitter arrow strikes Diomedes. It hits him in the right shoulder, “on the plate of his corselet; and the bitter arrow flew through it” (5.98–99; [[corselet]]). Diomedes asks Sthenelus to get down “so that you may draw the bitter arrow out of my shoulder” (5.110; [[healing]], [[sthenelus]]). When the archer meets the wounded man again, he admits the failure in the same words: “so my swift shaft did not overcome you after all, the bitter arrow” (5.278; [[swift-shaft]]). The arrow that broke the truce in Book 4 had the same epithet; this one only makes Diomedes fiercer ([[wounded-lion]]).
