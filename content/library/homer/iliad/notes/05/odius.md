He is the first of six men whom the Achaean leaders kill one after another (5.37–83; see [[agamemnon]] and [[androktasia]]). The Catalogue named him with Epistrophus as leader of the Halizones (2.856), and he dies with that title and one epithet, “great Odius, leader of the Halizones” (5.39). He was the first to turn and run, and the first of the six to die: Agamemnon hurls him from his chariot with a spear in the back, driven between the shoulders and through the chest, and “he fell with a thud, and his armor clattered upon him” (5.42; see [[fell-with-a-thud]]). His people are not named again in the poem (see [[halizones]]).
