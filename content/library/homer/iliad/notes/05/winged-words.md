---
summary: The formula introduces four speeches in Book 5: Athena's answer to Diomedes' prayer, Sthenelus' warning, Hera's call to Athena to arm, and Ares' complaint to Zeus (5.123, 5.242, 5.713, 5.871).
---

The four lines: 5.123, 242, 713, 871.

**Athena.** “And standing near him she addressed him with winged words” (ἀγχοῦ δʼ ἱσταμένη ἔπεα πτερόεντα προσηύδα, 5.123). It is the verse in which Athena spoke to Pandarus in Book 4, in disguise, to persuade him to shoot (4.92). Now she speaks in her own person to the man Pandarus shot, and gives him his father's might (see [[divine-epiphany]]).

**At a sight.** The other three speeches each follow something seen. Sthenelus sees Aeneas and Pandarus bearing down on them, “and at once addressed the son of Tydeus with winged words” (5.241–242), urging retreat. Hera sees Hector and Ares killing the Argives, and “at once she addressed Athena with winged words” (5.711–713), beginning with the cry ὢ πόποι (see [[o-popoi]]). Ares, wounded, shows Zeus the immortal blood running from his wound, “and lamenting addressed him with winged words” (5.870–871). αἶψα and αὐτίκα, “at once”, mark the speed of the first two, and ὀλοφυρόμενος, “lamenting”, gives the tone of the third. If the formula marks speech that bursts out at a sight or under strong feeling, as Paolo Vivante argued, Book 5 bears him out (see [[winged-words]]).
