summary: The verse of Argive Hera and Athena of Alalcomenae returns in the last lines of Book 5, as the goddesses go home having stopped Ares.
---

{{quote:844ac1c4-67bb-530e-ba06-c135d7fabbc5}}

In Book 4 Zeus used this verse to mock the two goddesses for sitting apart and looking on while Aphrodite saved Paris (4.7–12). The book ends with the same verse in the narrator's voice, after they have acted: Hera harnessed the chariot and shamed the Argives, Athena drove Diomedes' spear into Ares, and the war god is out of the battle ([[of-alalcomenae]], [[ruin-of-mortals]], [[diomedes-wounds-ares]]). The titles of place that were a jibe are now a closing formula of success.
