---
summary: In Book 5 the gods address Zeus as “Father Zeus” in their quarrels: Athena twice, Hera twice, and Ares in his complaint.
---

The address of prayer becomes the address of the gods' family quarrels. Athena opens her mockery of Aphrodite with “Father Zeus, will you be angry with me for what I say?” (5.421). Hera uses it twice in one speech, “Father Zeus, do you not blame Ares for these violent deeds,” and again, “Father Zeus, will you be angry with me if I strike Ares” (5.757, 5.762). Ares, wounded, begins with the same words as Hera: “Father Zeus, do you see these violent deeds and not blame them?” (5.872; [[nemesis]]). The two parties to the quarrel over Ares appeal to the same father in the same verse ([[the-gods-mirror-the-mortals]]).

The nominative names the father's power. Athena proposes to leave the mortals to fight and see “to which side father Zeus will hold out glory” (5.33; [[kydos]]), and both Aphrodite and Apollo complain that Diomedes “now would fight even with father Zeus” (5.362, 5.457).
