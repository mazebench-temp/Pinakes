**The fire.** Athena “kindled a tireless fire from his helmet and his shield”, and from “his head and shoulders” (5.4–7). The helmet is where the enemy sees the hero first, and the star-like blaze is the sign of his day (see [[the-aristeia]] and [[the-autumn-star]]).

**Known by his helmet.** Pandarus thinks the man who is routing the Trojans is Diomedes, “knowing him by his shield and his helmet with its socket for the plume, and by the look of his horses”, though he cannot be sure it is not a god (5.182–183). The helmet is αὐλῶπις, a word used four times in the Iliad and always of a helmet (5.182, 11.353, 13.530, 16.795). It is usually taken from αὐλός, “pipe”, as a helmet with a tube or socket in which the crest was set, as the translation has it; some have explained it from ὤψ, “eye”, as a helmet with eye-holes.

**Athena's helmet.** After the aegis Athena puts on her helmet:

{{quote:44fc7188-f42c-5697-ab35-ffc3aeddb7a6}}

The first verse is the verse of Agamemnon's helmet in his arming (5.743 = 11.41). ἀμφίφαλος, “with two ridges”, is from φάλος, the ridge or boss of the helmet (see [[helmet]]), and τετραφάληρος, “with four plates”, from φάλαρα, metal pieces fixed to it; neither can be pictured with certainty. The second verse is Athena's own: the helmet is golden, ἑκατὸν πολίων πρυλέεσσʼ ἀραρυῖαν, “fitted with the foot soldiers of a hundred cities” (5.744). πρυλέες are men who fight on foot (11.49, 12.77). The helmet may be decorated with figures of the soldiers of a hundred cities, as Achilles' shield is worked with cities and armies (18.490–540), or it may be big enough to cover them all, a helmet on a goddess's scale; the Greek allows both (see [[helmet-of-a-hundred-cities]] and [[arming-scene]]).

**The helmet of Hades.** When Athena mounts Diomedes' chariot to fight Ares, she puts on Ἄϊδος κυνέη, “the helmet of Hades, so that mighty Ares should not see her” (5.845). The helmet of the god below makes its wearer invisible even to a god. It is named nowhere else in Homer (see [[helmet-of-hades]], [[hades]] and [[plato-helmet-of-hades]]).
