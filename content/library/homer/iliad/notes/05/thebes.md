---
summary: Thebes is named once, in Athena's account of Tydeus' embassy, when he left the army and went alone “as a messenger to Thebes among the many Cadmeians” (5.803–804).
grc: [Θήβας]
---

Θήβας is the accusative of the plural name Θῆβαι. Book 4 used the singular, Θήβη, for the same city (4.378, 4.406). Athena calls the army of the Seven “the Achaeans”, as Agamemnon did (5.803; 4.384): Tydeus came to Thebes “alone, without the Achaeans”, as a messenger (ἄγγελος; see [[embassy]]). She tells the story to shame his son, as the king did (5.800–813; see [[tydeus]], [[cadmeians]] and [[tydeus-at-thebes]]). In the next book Diomedes will say that he does not remember his father, who left him when he was small, when the host of the Achaeans perished at Thebes (6.222–223).
