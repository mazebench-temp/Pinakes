---
summary: In the winnowing simile the epithet belongs to Demeter, the goddess of grain, who separates the grain from the chaff.
grc: [ξανθὴ]
---

{{quote:1c74f7ad-86c0-5607-ba4d-dc7527333a4a}}

The Achaeans, white with the dust the horses raise, are like the heaps of chaff that whiten on the threshing floor while “fair-haired Demeter separates the grain from the chaff as the winds drive on” ([[winnowing]], [[brazen]]). The color of her hair is the color of ripe grain. The epithet that Books 3 and 4 gave to Menelaus (ξανθὸς Μενέλαος) here belongs to a goddess named only in a comparison. Book 5 also has two others called Xanthus, the son of Phaenops (5.152) and the Lycian river (5.479). They are names, not the adjective, and this entry does not claim them ([[xanthus-son-of-phaenops]], [[xanthus-of-lycia]]).
