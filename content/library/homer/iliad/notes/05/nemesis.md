Book 5 asks Zeus for νέμεσις twice, in the same half-line. Hera finds him apart on the peak of Olympus and complains of Ares:

{{quote:a0f53299-5830-56f4-b1ea-3c6967973dbf}}

Later Ares, wounded, sits down beside Zeus and shows him the immortal blood: “Father Zeus, do you see these violent deeds and not blame them?” (Ζεῦ πάτερ οὐ νεμεσίζῃ ὁρῶν τάδε καρτερὰ ἔργα, 5.872). The “violent deeds” (καρτερὰ ἔργα) Hera means are Ares' killing of the Achaeans; the ones Ares means are Athena's, who set a mortal on him. Each god appeals to the father's indignation against the other's side, as men appeal to it against one another (see [[the-gods-mirror-the-mortals]]).

Zeus answers each in his own way. To Hera he gives leave: rouse Athena against Ares (5.765–766). To Ares he gives the blame he asked for, but turned on the one who asked: “Of all the gods who hold Olympus, you are the most hateful to me; strife is always dear to you, and wars and battles” (5.890–891; see [[strife-is-always-dear]]). Then he has him healed. The father's νέμεσις is real, and it does not end his care.
