ὀνειροπόλος is an interpreter of dreams, one who “deals with” them (πολέω); Achilles names the dream-reader in Book 1 among those who might explain Apollo's anger, “for a dream, too, comes from Zeus” (1.62–63; see [[a-dream-comes-from-zeus]]). The obituary of the two sons turns on their father's art. Eurydamas could read what the gods send in sleep, but “the old man read no dreams for them when they set out” (τοῖς οὐκ ἐρχομένοις ὃ γέρων ἐκρίνατʼ ὀνείρους, 5.150). The line is compressed and has been read in more than one way; on the usual reading, the old man read no dreams for his sons when they left, and so foresaw nothing. Either way the father's art did not reach his sons, as the prophet Merops' did not save his (2.830–834). See [[eurydamas]], [[divination]] and [[bereaved-parents]].
