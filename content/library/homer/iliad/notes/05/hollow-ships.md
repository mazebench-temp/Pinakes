---
summary: Captured horses are driven to the hollow ships, and Hera, in Stentor's likeness, shames the Argives with the Trojans fighting by them.
grc: [κοίλας ἐπὶ νῆας, γλαφυρῇσιν]
---

Twice the ships receive the spoils of Diomedes' day. He gives the horses of the sons of Dares to his companions “to take down to the hollow ships” (5.25–26), and Sthenelus hands the horses of Aeneas to Deipylus “to drive to the hollow ships” (5.325–327; [[deipylus]], [[master-of-rout]]). The second phrase uses the synonym γλαφυρός. At the end of the battle Hera turns the ships into a reproach. In the voice of Stentor she tells the Argives that while Achilles fought, the Trojans never came out before the Dardanian gates, “but now they are fighting far from the city, by the hollow ships” (5.788–791; [[bronze-voiced]]). The Trojans are not yet at the ships, but the goddess speaks as if they were. In Books 12 to 16 they will be.
