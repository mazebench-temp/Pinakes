**A mother in the battle.** When Diomedes' stone brings Aeneas to his knees, his mother sees it, “who bore him to Anchises as he tended his cattle”:

{{quote:404d182a-8d6a-524c-9d87-0a281dd00a1a}}

It is the one time in the Iliad that a divine mother comes onto the field and carries her son in her arms. Thetis comes to Achilles by the ships, and goes to Olympus and to Hephaestus for him, but never into the fighting (see [[thetis]]). Aphrodite's rescue costs her a wounded hand, and when she cries out she lets her son fall (5.343), and Apollo has to take him up. Telling her mother what happened, she gives her love as the reason: she was wounded “because I was carrying my dear son out of the war, Aeneas, who is dearest to me by far of all” (5.377–378; see [[aphrodite]] and [[aeneas]]).

**A mother comforts.** On Olympus Aphrodite falls on the knees of Dione, her mother, and Dione takes her in her arms, strokes her, and calls her by name (5.370–372). Her comfort is the comfort of a mother to a child: others have suffered worse, and the man who hurt you will be punished (5.382–415; see [[dione]] and [[who-fights-the-gods]]). Then she wipes the ichor from the hand, and it is healed (5.416–417). The scene is the one in Book 1 where Thetis comes up from the sea to her weeping son, told in the same verse: she “stroked him with her hand, and spoke to him, and called him by name” (1.361 = 5.372). Here it is played between two goddesses, a mother and a daughter.

**A mother's temper.** Zeus tells the wounded Ares that he has his mother's fury, Hera's, “so I think it is by her promptings that you suffer this” (5.892–894; see [[meter]]). The son has the mother's temper, and the mother, who asked Zeus' leave to strike him, is on the other side of the war (5.757–763).

In the simile for the twin sons of Diocles the mother is an animal: two lions reared by their mother in the thickets of the forest, who raid the farms until men kill them (5.554–558; see [[two-lions]] and [[bereaved-parents]]).
