---
summary: The gods' horses fly “not unwilling” (5.366, 5.768), and the goddesses obey each other and Zeus in the line “did not disobey” (5.719, 5.767).
refs: [5.366, 5.719, 5.767, 5.768]
---

Two understatements of Book 5 belong to the gods. When Iris drives Aphrodite to Olympus, and when Hera drives Athena down to Troy, the whip is followed by the same half-verse: “and the pair flew on not unwilling” (τὼ δʼ οὐκ ἀέκοντε πετέσθην, 5.366, 5.768). οὐκ ἀέκοντε, “not unwilling”, means that the horses flew gladly; the verse belongs to the [[chariot-journey]]. And when Hera proposes that the two goddesses arm, “the gray-eyed goddess Athena did not disobey” (5.719), and when Zeus gives Hera leave to strike Ares, “the white-armed goddess Hera did not disobey” (5.767). οὐκ ἀπίθησε, “did not disobey”, means that she obeyed at once (see [[so-he-spoke]]).
