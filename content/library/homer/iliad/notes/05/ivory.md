---
summary: The reins of Pylaemenes' chariot are “white with ivory”, and they fall in the dust when Antilochus strikes the charioteer Mydon (5.580–583). It is the Iliad's second mention of ivory.
grc: [ἐλέφαντι]
---

Menelaus kills Pylaemenes, leader of the Paphlagonians, and Antilochus strikes his charioteer Mydon as he turns the horses. He hits him “with a stone full on the elbow; and from his hands the reins, white with ivory, fell to the ground in the dust” (5.582–583). ἡνία λεύκʼ ἐλέφαντι: the reins were fitted with ivory, perhaps in studs or plates, a sign of the rank of the king whose team they guided (see [[pylaemenes]], [[mydon]] and [[chariot]]). The Iliad's other ivory is the cheekpiece of the simile in Book 4, stained with purple and kept in a storeroom for a king, “an ornament for the horse and a glory to the driver” (4.141–145; see [[ivory-stained-with-purple]]). Both are horse trappings of kings, and here the ornament falls in the dust with the driver.
