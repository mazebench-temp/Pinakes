---
summary: Olympus is where the wounded gods of Book 5 go. Aphrodite flees there in Ares' chariot, Ares goes up to it in a cloud to complain to Zeus, and in Dione's story Hades once went there to be healed. Hera and Athena drive out through its gates of cloud to stop Ares.

---

**Refuge.** Aphrodite asks Ares for his horses “so that I may reach Olympus, where the seat of the immortals is” (5.360), and Iris drives her to “the seat of the gods, steep Olympus” (5.367). The wounded Ares arrives in the same words (5.868 = 5.367). In Dione's story Hades, shot by Heracles, went “to the house of Zeus and high Olympus” to be healed (5.398; see [[high-olympus]] and [[paeeon]]). The gods are hurt on the battlefield; on Olympus they are comforted, mocked, rebuked and healed (see [[dione]] and [[zeus]]).

**Gates of cloud.** When Hera and Athena drive out, “the gates of heaven groaned open, which the Hours kept, to whom great heaven and Olympus are entrusted” (5.749–750; see [[hours]] and [[sky]]). They find Zeus “on the topmost peak of many-ridged Olympus” (5.754; see [[many-ridged-olympus]]), fly “between earth and the starry sky” (5.769), and come down by the rivers of Troy. Ares goes up “with the clouds into the wide sky” and reaches Olympus (5.867–868). Book 5 does not separate the mountain from the sky: the gates of Olympus are a cloud in heaven.

**Its people.** The gods are “the gods who hold Olympus” (5.404, 5.890; see [[olympians]]), and Ares speaks of “all the gods there are on Olympus” (5.877). The house of Zeus stands there (5.398, 5.907), and Athena lets her robe fall “on her father's floor” before she arms (5.734; see [[houses-of-the-gods]]).
