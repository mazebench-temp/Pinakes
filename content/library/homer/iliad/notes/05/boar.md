---
summary: The boars of Book 5 are in a single simile. The best of the Argives stand crowded around Diomedes “like lions that eat raw flesh or wild boars, whose strength is no weakling's” (5.782–783).
grc: [συσὶ, κάπροισιν]
en: [wild boars]
---

σῦς κάπρος joins the word for any pig, wild or tame (σῦς), to the word for the boar (κάπρος); here it stands in the dative plural: ἢ συσὶ κάπροισιν, τῶν τε σθένος οὐκ ἀλαπαδνόν, “or wild boars, whose strength is no weakling's” (5.783). The two beasts of the simile are the two ways of fighting. The lion goes out to kill; the boar stands and turns on its hunters. The Argives around Diomedes have both kinds of strength, and at this moment they are using neither, having given ground before Hector and Ares (5.699–702). Hera, in the likeness of Stentor, shames them for it at once (5.784–791; see [[lions-and-boars]]).

In Book 4 Idomeneus stood among the champions “like a boar in courage” (4.253; see [[like-a-boar]]). The couplet of Book 5 returns whole when Hector and Ajax fall on each other in their duel (7.255–257). See [[lion]].
