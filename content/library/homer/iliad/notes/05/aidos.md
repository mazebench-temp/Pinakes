**Shame in battle.** When the Trojans rally, Agamemnon goes through the Achaeans: “Friends, be men, and take a valiant heart, and feel shame before one another in the fierce battles” (ἀλλήλους τʼ αἰδεῖσθε, 5.529–530). The shame is mutual: each man watches and is watched. He gives the reason in a maxim, “when men feel shame, more are saved than are killed” (5.531; see [[shame-saves-more]]). αἰδώς, “Shame!”, is the cry of the rally. Agamemnon repeats Hera's whole verse in Book 8 (8.228), and Poseidon, Ajax and Sarpedon each begin an appeal with it (13.95, 15.502, 16.422). Here it is joined with ἐλέγχεα, “disgraces” (see [[elenchos]]), and with a taunt about looks, εἶδος ἀγητοί, “admirable in looks”, the insult Hector gave Paris in another form, εἶδος ἄριστε, “best in looks” (3.39; see [[eidos]] and [[best-in-looks]]). The goddess shames the Argives with the memory of Achilles: while he fought, the Trojans never came out of their gates (5.788–791). On the other side fights αἰδώς' opposite, Tumult, whom Enyo brings into the battle, “shameless in combat” (5.593; see [[anaideia]]).

**Awe before gods.** ἅζομαι is the verb of reverence for the gods, and Book 5 twice uses it in the negative. Diomedes springs at Aeneas “though he knew that Apollo himself was holding his hands over him; but he had no awe even of the great god” (οὐδὲ θεὸν μέγαν ἅζετο, 5.433–434), and Apollo has to stop him. Later Athena herself tells him to set it aside: “have no awe of furious Ares” (μηδʼ ἅζεο θοῦρον Ἄρηα, 5.830). What is impiety at Apollo's shield is obedience at Athena's side.
