---
summary: Book 5 gives bronze to the gods. Ares is bound in a bronze jar and is himself “bronze Ares”, Hera's chariot runs on wheels and tires of bronze, the sky is “brazen”, and Stentor has a voice of bronze; and Ares, wounded by a mortal's bronze spear, imagines living on strengthless “from the blows of the bronze”.
grc: [χαλκέῳ, χάλκεα, "χάλκεʼ", χαλκοῖο]
---

**The weapon.** The bronze of Book 5 is first the blade and the point. It cuts its way under Pedaeus' tongue, and he “bit the cold bronze with his teeth” (5.74–75); “the unyielding bronze” cuts off Pandarus' tongue at the root (5.292). Athena tells Diomedes that he may wound Aphrodite “with the sharp bronze” (5.132, 5.821), and he goes after her “with the pitiless bronze” (5.330; see [[pitiless-bronze]]).

**The gods' bronze.** Ares, the god of war, is χάλκεος Ἄρης, “bronze Ares” (5.704, 5.859, 5.866; see [[bronze-ares]]), and in Dione's story he lay bound for thirteen months “in a bronze jar” (χαλκέῳ … κεράμῳ, 5.387; see [[the-aloadae-bind-ares]]). The wheels of Hera's chariot are “of bronze, with eight spokes”, and over their golden rims are fitted “tires of bronze, a wonder to see” (5.722–725; see [[chariot]]). The sky is πολύχαλκος, “brazen” (5.504; see [[brazen]]); Stentor, whose likeness Hera takes, has “the voice of bronze” (5.785; see [[bronze-voiced]]); and Hector fights beside Ares “armed in bronze” (5.699; see [[armed-in-bronze]]).

**A god struck by bronze.** The wounded Ares tells Zeus that, but for his swift feet, he would have suffered long among the heaps of the dead, “or lived on strengthless from the blows of the bronze” (ζὼς ἀμενηνὸς … χαλκοῖο τυπῇσι, 5.887). The bronze god has been struck by a man's bronze, and what it can do to a god is not death but weakness (see [[gods-wounded-by-mortals]]).
