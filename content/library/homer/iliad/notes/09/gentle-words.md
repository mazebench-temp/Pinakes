---
summary: Nestor proposes “kindly gifts and soothing words,” and Phoenix speaks of the “gentle vows” with which men turn the gods aside: the adjective ἀγανός without its usual noun.
grc: [ἀγανοῖσιν, ἀγανῇσι]
en: [kindly, gentle]
---

Book 9 has ἀγανός twice, neither time with ἔπεα. Nestor's plan for winning back Achilles is “kindly gifts and soothing words” (δώροισίν τʼ ἀγανοῖσιν ἔπεσσί τε μειλιχίοισι, 9.113; [[soothing-words]]). Phoenix, arguing that even the gods can be turned, says that men turn them “with burnt offerings and gentle vows, with libation and the savor of the burnt fat” (εὐχωλῇς ἀγανῇσι, 9.499–500; [[even-the-gods-can-be-turned]], [[prayer]], [[propitiation]]). The gentleness belongs to what is offered to appease: gifts to a man, vows to the gods. The translation renders it “kindly” and “gentle.”
