**The proud spirit.** Achilles says that his “proud spirit was very eager” to marry a wedded wife in Phthia and enjoy his father's possessions (9.398–400; [[wedded]]). Ajax uses the formula of the kinsman who accepts the blood-price for a brother or a son: “the other's heart and proud spirit are held in check once he has accepted the penalty” (9.635–636; [[poine]]). Achilles' proud spirit is the one that will not be checked.

**Diomedes' word.** At the end of the book Diomedes uses the adjective alone, of the man: “he is proud enough as it is; now you have driven him much further into his pride” (9.699–700; ἀγήνωρ, ἀγηνορίῃσιν). The noun ἀγηνορίη, “pride” or “manliness,” returns in a simile of a beast whose courage is its death (12.46) and in Andromache's fear for Hector's pride (22.457), and Apollo will say that Achilles, like a lion, gives way to his “proud spirit” (24.41–42). The capitalized Ἀγήνωρ, the Trojan Agenor, is kept apart ([[agenor]]). See [[achilles]].
