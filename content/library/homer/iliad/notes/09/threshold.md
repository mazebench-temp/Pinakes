summary: Achilles counts the treasures “shut within the stone threshold of the Archer” at Pytho among the riches not worth his life (9.404–405), and in Phoenix's story Oeneus stands on the threshold of his son's chamber, begging him to come out (9.582).

**At Pytho.** Nothing is worth Achilles' life, “nor all that is shut within the stone threshold of the Archer, Phoebus Apollo, in rocky Pytho” (9.404–405; see [[pytho]], [[apollo]] and [[possessions]]). The threshold stands for the whole sanctuary and the offerings stored behind it. In the Odyssey Demodocus sings how Agamemnon crossed the stone threshold at Pytho to consult the oracle, and Apollo foretold that the best of the Achaeans would quarrel; Agamemnon rejoiced when Odysseus and Achilles did (Od. 8.75–82). ἀφήτωρ, “the Archer”, occurs only here (see the translation's note on 9.404).

**At the chamber door.** Meleager's father “entreated him again and again, standing on the threshold of the high-roofed chamber, shaking the jointed doors, imploring his son” (9.581–583; see [[oeneus]] and [[supplication-type-scene]]). He stands at the door and does not cross it. In the Odyssey Penelope sits on the threshold of her chamber in her grief (Od. 4.716–718).
