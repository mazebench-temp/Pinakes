---
summary: Two participles of the same root describe fat swine “rich with lard”: the chine that Achilles roasts for the envoys and the hogs slaughtered in Phoenix's father's house.
grc: ["τεθαλυῖαν ἀλοιφῇ", "θαλέθοντες ἀλοιφῇ"]
en: [rich with lard]
---

θαλερός is formed from θάλλω (“bloom, flourish”), and Book 9 uses the verb itself, and its lengthened form θαλέθω, of animals swelling with fat. Patroclus sets on the chopping block “the chine of a fat hog, rich with lard” (ῥάχιν τεθαλυῖαν ἀλοιφῇ, 9.208; [[feast-type-scene]]). In Phoenix's story his kinsmen slaughter “many hogs, rich with lard” (σύες θαλέθοντες ἀλοιφῇ, 9.467; [[shambling]]). The fat that “blooms” on the swine is the sign of a well-fed house, and the second phrase returns at Patroclus' funeral feast, “white-tusked hogs, rich with lard” (23.32; [[white-tusked]]).
