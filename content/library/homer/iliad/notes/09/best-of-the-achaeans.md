---
summary: Nestor tells Agamemnon that he dishonored “the best of men, whom the immortals themselves honored”, and the king concedes that the man whom Zeus loves is worth many people. Achilles proves the claim by Hector's fear of him, and complains that the best and the worst are held in one honor.
passages:
  - 9.104-120 | Nestor's charge and Agamemnon's admission
  - 9.352-355 | While I fought, Hector stayed by the wall
---

**Conceded.** The quarrel of Book 1 was a quarrel over who was best, and Achilles twice named the king's offense: he “paid no honor to the best of the Achaeans” (1.244, 1.412). Nestor now says it to the king in the council: “the best of men, whom the immortals themselves honored, you dishonored” (ἄνδρα φέριστον, 9.110–111). Agamemnon concedes it in a maxim: “Worth many people is the man whom Zeus loves” (9.116–117; see [[the-man-whom-zeus-loves]]). He does not name the man.

**Proved.** Achilles' proof is the enemy. While he fought among the Achaeans, Hector would not rouse battle far from the wall, but came only as far as the Scaean gates and the oak, and once waited for him alone “and barely escaped my charge” (9.352–355; see [[hectors-escape-at-the-oak]]). Odysseus offers him the chance to prove it again: Hector, in his frenzy, “says there is no one equal to him among the Danaans” (9.304–306; see [[hector]]).

**Not recognized.** What makes Achilles angry is that the distinction is not paid. “The coward and the brave are held in a single honor” (9.319; see [[no-man-escapes-his-fate]]), and the prize was taken from him alone (9.335–336; see [[geras]]). The envoys are “the best men” chosen to entreat him (ἀρίστους, 9.520), and the gifts include “seven, by far the best” of the women (9.638). None of it changes his answer (see [[honor-and-dishonor]]).
