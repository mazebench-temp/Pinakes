summary: Achilles gives the Iliad's fullest account of how the spoils were shared out among the chiefs: he brought everything from twenty-three cities to Agamemnon, who handed out a little and kept much, and of all the chiefs took back a prize from him alone (9.328–336). The offer itself is made of spoil, the women Agamemnon chose for himself from Achilles' conquest of Lesbos and the first pick of the plunder of Troy.

**Achilles' account.** In Book 1 Achilles complained that when a city fell his share was always the smaller (1.163–168). In Book 9 he tells how it was done:

{{quote:c4d0bf56-b778-56ef-9f3b-4663d3e15b75}}

The verbs are iteratives, δόσκον, δασάσκετο, ἔχεσκεν: this was what happened every time. Achilles took the treasures out (ἐξελόμην) and brought them all to Agamemnon, who “staying behind beside the swift ships” shared out a few and kept the most. The prizes Agamemnon gave the other chiefs and kings “they keep … safe”, and only Achilles' was taken back (see [[geras]] and [[achilles-raids]]). Here the king himself is the giver: the prize was taken by “the very man who gave it” (ὅς περ ἔδωκεν, 9.367). In Book 1 Achilles called Briseis the prize that “the sons of the Achaeans gave me” (1.162, 1.392), and in Book 16 he says the same again, with a verse of his answer to Ajax here, “as if I were some dishonored vagrant” (16.56–59; 16.59 = 9.648). His own goods he won by lot, “all that I won by lot” (ἔλαχον, 9.367), and those he means to take home: gold, red bronze, fair-belted women and gray iron (9.364–367; see [[lots]] and [[possessions]]).

**Spoil in the offer.** Agamemnon's gifts come from the same division. The seven women of Lesbos are those he chose out for himself “when he himself took well-built Lesbos” (ἐξελόμην, 9.129–130; see [[lesbos]]): the king offers back, as a gift, part of what Achilles won. And if Troy falls, Achilles may load his ship with gold and bronze and choose twenty Trojan women “going in when we Achaeans divide the spoil” (δατεώμεθα ληΐδʼ, 9.137–140), before the division begins. He is offered first choice of the spoil, the larger share he says he never had (1.163–167; see [[the-gifts-of-agamemnon]] and [[captive-women]]).
