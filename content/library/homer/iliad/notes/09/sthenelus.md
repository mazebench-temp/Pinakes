“But the two of us, I and Sthenelus, will fight until we find the end of Ilios; for we came here with a god” (9.48–49; see [[diomedes]]). In Book 4, when Agamemnon rebuked Diomedes, it was Sthenelus who answered for the pair and Diomedes who silenced him (4.403–418); here Diomedes speaks for both. The dual νῶϊ, “we two”, is the word with which he ended that exchange, “let us two also think of our furious courage” (4.418). Their claim to have come “with a god” recalls Sthenelus' boast that the sons took Thebes, where the fathers failed, “trusting in the portents of the gods and the help of Zeus” (4.406–408; see [[the-epigonoi]]).
