summary: The night begins with Agamemnon weeping before the army like a spring of dark water, and Phoenix begins his plea in tears. Achilles refuses to let weeping move him; in Phoenix's story the tears of Althaea bring a curse, and the tears of Meleager's wife bring him to fight.
grc: [δάκρυσι]
passages:
  - 9.13-16 | Agamemnon weeps like a spring
  - 9.430-433 | Phoenix bursts into tears
---

**The king's tears.** Agamemnon stands up in the assembly “shedding tears like a spring of dark water that pours its murky water down a sheer rock” (9.13–15; see [[the-spring-of-dark-water]] and [[shedding-tears]]). The same simile will be used of Patroclus when he comes to Achilles in tears (16.3–4). The king's tears go with the proposal to flee, and they do not move the army: the sons of the Achaeans are silent a long time, and Diomedes answers with a rebuke (9.29–36).

**Phoenix's tears.** After Achilles' refusal Phoenix speaks “bursting into tears, for he feared greatly for the ships of the Achaeans” (δάκρυʼ ἀναπρήσας, 9.433). His tears are for the army, and his speech is an appeal from the man who reared Achilles. Achilles answers that Phoenix must not confuse his heart “with weeping and grieving to do a favor to the hero son of Atreus” (ὀδυρόμενος καὶ ἀχεύων, 9.612; see [[charis]]). The tears are taken as a plea for the enemy, and refused.

**Tears in the story.** Phoenix's story has two weeping women. Althaea, grieving for her brother, kneels and beats the earth, “and her bosom was wet with tears” (δεύοντο δὲ δάκρυσι κόλποι, 9.570), as she calls on the gods below to give her son death (see [[althaea]] and [[curses]]). Meleager's wife comes last and “weeping” (ὀδυρομένη, 9.591) tells him what happens when a city is taken, and he goes out to fight (see [[cleopatra]]). Her own name records her mother's weeping, the halcyon's lament for a daughter carried off by Apollo (9.562–564; see [[halcyon]]). The tears of the curse drive Meleager from the war; the tears of the plea bring him back.
