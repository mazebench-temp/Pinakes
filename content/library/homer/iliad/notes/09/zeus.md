summary: No god takes part in Book 9, but Zeus is in every speech. Agamemnon blames him in earnest for the deceit he alleged as a test in Book 2 and then owns that Zeus is honoring Achilles; Odysseus reports his lightning for the Trojans, Achilles trusts in the portion of Zeus, and Phoenix makes the Prayers his daughters.

**Blamed in earnest.** Agamemnon stands up in the night assembly weeping and says again the words with which he tested the army in Book 2: Zeus has bound him in heavy blind folly, “hard god”, who promised him Ilios and now bids him go home in disgrace, “for his power is the greatest” (9.18–25 = 2.111–118, with τότε for πρίν in 9.19; see [[the-test-of-the-army]]). In Book 2 the charge was a pretense, and truer than he knew, since Zeus had sent him a lying Dream. Now he means it. Nestor had dated the promise to the day the army sailed, when Zeus “flashed his lightning on our right, showing signs of good omen” (2.350–353; see [[ate]] and [[agamemnon]]).

**The gifts of Zeus.** After a long silence Diomedes turns Agamemnon's last word against him:

{{quote:a56833bd-6319-5132-85f5-19c3f754de55}}

Agamemnon gave the greatest power to Zeus (τοῦ γὰρ κράτος ἐστὶ μέγιστον, 9.25); Diomedes gives it to courage. Nestor makes the gift a duty. Zeus “has put into your hands / the scepter and the ordinances, so that you may take counsel for them” (9.98–99), the words with which Odysseus defended the one king in Book 2 (2.205–206), and so Agamemnon above all must listen to good counsel (9.100–102; see [[scepter]], [[themis]], [[diomedes]] and [[nestor]]).

**Zeus honors Achilles.** Nestor tells Agamemnon that he dishonored the man “whom the immortals themselves honored” (9.110), and Agamemnon agrees:

{{quote:ad9f746c-0a85-5ae2-bc04-1c611f2900c1}}

He has never before said that Zeus is on Achilles' side. His words answer what Achilles asked of Thetis in Book 1, that Zeus should help the Trojans “so that the son of Atreus … may learn / his own blind folly, in that he paid no honor to the best of the Achaeans” (1.411–412). Agamemnon now names his ἄτας, “blind folly” (9.115), and says that Zeus “has honored” Achilles (ἔτισε, 9.118). Achilles says the same from the other side. He needs none of the honor Phoenix promises with the gifts: “I think I am honored by the portion of Zeus” (9.608), the honor Thetis begged for (τίμησόν μοι υἱόν, 1.505). In Book 1 he complained that Zeus had not honored him “even a little” (1.353–354). Now both men say that Zeus honors Achilles (see [[the-man-whom-zeus-loves]], [[plan-of-zeus]], [[time]] and [[aisa]]).

**Signs for the Trojans.** Nestor sends the envoys off with a prayer “to Zeus, son of Cronus, if he will have pity” (9.172). The poem tells of the libation (9.174–177) but gives neither the words of the prayer nor any answer (see [[prayer]]). At Achilles' hut Odysseus tells him what Zeus is doing:

{{quote:c5a9f748-cf0b-514f-a795-c96be9a8b832}}

Signs on the right were the good omen Nestor claimed for the Achaeans when they sailed (ἐπιδέξια, 2.353); now they shine for the Trojans, and Hector rages “trusting in Zeus” (9.238; see [[hector]] and [[teras]]). Achilles draws his own conclusion: the others should sail home too, “for far-seeing Zeus has held / his hand over it” (9.419–420; reported at 9.686–687). What he calls Zeus' care for Troy is the answer to his own request (1.407–412). Of Agamemnon he says that “Zeus the counselor has taken away his wits” (9.377), nearly the words the narrator used of Glaucus (6.234) and Agamemnon will use of himself (19.137; see [[counselor]]).

**The Prayers.** In Phoenix's plea the Prayers are “the daughters of great Zeus” (9.502). When a man refuses them, they go to Zeus, son of Cronus, and ask that Blind Folly follow him (9.508–512; see [[litai]]). A few lines later Artemis, “the daughter of great Zeus”, sends the boar on Calydon when Oeneus forgets her offering (9.533–539; see [[artemis]]).

**Zeus of the underworld.** The Ζεὺς καταχθόνιος who fulfills Amyntor's curse with “dread Persephone” (9.457) is Hades; compare Ζεὺς χθόνιος, to whom Hesiod's farmer prays at the plowing (*Works and Days* 465). The index counts the line to [[hades]] (see [[zeus-of-the-underworld]]).
