summary: The night assembly keeps the outline of the procedure, a summons by heralds, a seated crowd, speakers who stand, but every step is muted. The heralds call each man by name and do not shout, the king stands weeping, a long silence follows his speech, and the meeting ends not in dissolution but in obedience (9.9–80).

Set against the pattern (see [[assembly-type-scene]]), the assembly of Book 9 has its parts, and each is held down.

1. **Summons.** Agamemnon goes to and fro himself, ordering the heralds “to summon every man by name to the assembly, but not to shout” (9.10–12). In Book 2 they proclaimed the assembly and then had to shout the crowd into order (2.50–52, 2.96–98; see [[heralds]] and [[assembly]]).
2. **Gathering.** No verse tells the gathering. “They sat in the assembly, grieving” (ἷζον δʼ εἰν ἀγορῇ τετιηότες, 9.13).
3. **Speakers.** Agamemnon stands up, shedding tears “like a spring of dark water”, and speaks groaning heavily (9.13–16; see [[shedding-tears]] and [[groaning-heavily]]); no scepter is named, though he rose with it to speak in Book 2 (2.100–101). His proposal is met with silence, and “at last” Diomedes speaks (9.29–31; see [[silence]]). The army shouts its approval (9.50–51), and Nestor rises in the verse with which Achilles rose in the first assembly and will rise again in Book 19, τοῖσι δʼ ἀνιστάμενος (9.52; 1.58, 19.55).
4. **Close.** There is no dissolution. Nestor's orders end the meeting: “So he spoke, and they listened closely to him and obeyed”, and the sentries go out at once (9.79–80; see [[so-he-spoke]]).

The council that follows in Agamemnon's hut keeps the same procedure on a smaller scale, a meal, a speaker who “began to weave a plan”, the king's answer and an order carried out (9.89–181; see [[boule]] and [[feast]]). In Book 2 the council met before the assembly (2.53–86); here it follows it.
