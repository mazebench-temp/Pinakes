**The summons.** At nightfall Agamemnon goes to and fro, “ordering the clear-voiced heralds to summon every man by name to the assembly, but not to shout” (9.10–12; see [[clear-voiced]] and [[assembly]]). κλήδην, “by name”, occurs only here in Homer. At dawn in Book 2 the heralds called the army with a cry, and nine of them shouted to quiet it (2.50–52, 2.96–98). Now each man is called quietly, and the king himself toils among the foremost (9.12). The poem gives no reason; the Trojans are camped on the plain with their fires (8.553–565).

**With the envoys.** Nestor sends two heralds with the envoys: “and of the heralds let Odius and Eurybates go with them” (9.170; see [[odius-the-herald]] and [[eurybates]]). Before the embassy sets out, “the heralds poured water over their hands” for the prayer and the libation (9.174; see [[hand-washing]] and [[libation]]). In Book 1 Agamemnon's heralds went to Achilles' hut to take Briseis, and the envoys' walk to the hut is told in their verses (1.327–329; compare 9.182–186). Then the two heralds stood in fear and silence, and Achilles greeted them as “messengers of Zeus and of men” (1.331–334; see [[messengers-of-zeus]]). This time the heralds are not mentioned in the hut at all, and some have taken the two men whom Achilles greets in the dual to be the heralds (9.196–198; see [[the-embassy-to-achilles]]).

**Witnesses.** At the report Odysseus calls on them: “and these men too who came with me are here to tell it, Ajax and the two heralds, both wise men” (9.688–689). πεπνυμένω ἄμφω, “both wise”, was said of the heralds Talthybius and Idaeus when they parted Hector and Ajax (7.276). Heralds serve as witnesses, as when Achilles made Agamemnon's heralds witnesses of his wrong (1.338; see [[embassy]]).
