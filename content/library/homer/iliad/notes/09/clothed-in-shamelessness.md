---
summary: Achilles calls Agamemnon “clothed forever in shamelessness,” the phrase of his first insult in Book 1.
grc: ["ἀναιδείην ἐπιειμένος"]
en: [clothed forever in shamelessness]
---

“If he still hopes to deceive some other of the Danaans, clothed forever in shamelessness; but he would not, dog though he is, dare to look me in the face” (9.371–373). Achilles takes up the words with which he first abused Agamemnon in the assembly, ἀναιδείην ἐπιειμένε (1.149), now in the nominative and with αἰέν, “forever,” and again with the image of the shameless dog, as in Book 1 ([[dog-face]], [[dog-eyes-deer-heart]]). He speaks of Agamemnon in the third person to the envoys, as if the king were absent from his own offer. The shamelessness is the failure of αἰδώς, the respect that Ajax will ask Achilles to show for his own roof (9.640; [[aidos]], [[anaideia]]). See [[agamemnon]].
