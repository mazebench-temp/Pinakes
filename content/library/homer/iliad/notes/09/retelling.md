---
summary: Book 9 is full of the past retold by those who were there. Diomedes recalls Agamemnon's taunt in harsher words than Agamemnon used, Nestor recalls his own warning against taking Briseis, Odysseus and Phoenix each recall the day Peleus sent Achilles to Troy, and Achilles tells of his raids and of a day Hector barely escaped him that the poem never shows.
passages:
  - 9.32-36 | Diomedes recalls the taunt
  - 9.252-259 | Odysseus recalls Peleus' charge
---

**Diomedes.** “It was my courage you first reproached among the Danaans, saying I was unwarlike and a coward” (ἀπτόλεμον καὶ ἀνάλκιδα, 9.34–35). In Book 4 Agamemnon had asked why he was cowering and called him worse than his father in battle, “though better in assembly” (4.370–400); the two words of the retelling are the ones Odysseus used to the common soldier in Book 2, “you are unwarlike and weak” (2.201; see [[privative-adjectives]]). Diomedes answers the taunt where it said he was strong, in the assembly (see [[assembly]] and [[diomedes]]).

**Nestor.** He tells Agamemnon that the taking of Briseis was “not at all as we would have it; for I, for my part, urged you hard against it” (9.108–109), and so he had: “You, great as you are, do not take the girl away from him” (1.275; see [[taking-of-briseis]] and [[nestor]]).

**Peleus' charge.** Odysseus quotes the words with which Peleus sent his son from Phthia:

{{quote:4fc6a8eb-4665-57e8-8cf9-e26fb24f762c}}

Phoenix recalls the same day, “on that day when he sent you from Phthia to Agamemnon”, but what he remembers is that Peleus sent him with the boy, to teach him to be “a speaker of words and a doer of deeds” (9.438–443). In Book 11 Nestor tells the day a third time, and he was there with Odysseus: in his telling Peleus told his son “always to be the best and to excel others”, and Menoetius told Patroclus to advise him (11.765–790). Each speaker remembers the precept that serves his own plea: restraint for Odysseus, Phoenix's own office for Phoenix, the fight for Nestor (see [[peleus-sends-achilles-to-troy]] and [[peleus]]).

**Achilles.** His reply retells years of war: sleepless nights and bloody days, twelve cities sacked from the ships and eleven by land, and the spoils handed to Agamemnon (9.325–336; see [[division-of-spoils]] and [[achilles-raids]]). He also tells of a time when Hector would not fight far from the wall, and once waited for him at the Scaean gates and the oak “and barely escaped my charge” (9.352–355), a meeting the Iliad does not narrate (see the translation's note on 9.352–355).

**After.** Odysseus' report retells Achilles' first answer only (9.677–692; see [[message-repeated]]), and Thetis will tell the embassy to Hephaestus in words from Phoenix's story of Meleager (18.448–449; see [[embassy]]).
