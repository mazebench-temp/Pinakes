summary: Ajax's last appeal is to φιλότης, the friendship of companions, with which the army honored Achilles above all others and which he now disregards. Peleus' parting charge, as Odysseus reports it, set φιλοφροσύνη, “kindliness”, above strife.
passages:
  - 9.628-642 | Ajax's appeal
---

**The friendship of companions.** Ajax speaks of Achilles first to Odysseus, as if he were not there:

{{quote:781271ce-8490-5518-a737-946a311ea7e1}}

φιλότης ἑταίρων, “the friendship of his companions”, is the bond of the men who fight together, the third kind of φιλότης beside the treaty of enemies and the love of man and woman (see the article above and [[hetairos]]). Ajax sets it against everything else that has been offered. Agamemnon offered goods; Odysseus asked him to pity the army; Phoenix promised the honor that goes with gifts. Ajax asks for nothing but regard for the men who have honored him “beside the ships above all others” (9.631), and he ends by calling the envoys his guests and “the nearest and dearest to you” (9.640–642; see [[philos]] and [[xenia]]). The verb with which he accuses him, οὐδὲ μετατρέπεται, “does not regard”, is the one Achilles used of Agamemnon in the quarrel: “This you neither heed nor care about” (τῶν οὔ τι μετατρέπῃ, 1.160). The charge Achilles made against the king is now made against him.

Achilles does not deny it. He says that Ajax has spoken after his own heart, and that his anger is stronger (9.644–648). When Patroclus goes out in his armor, the Trojans hope that Achilles “had cast away his wrath and chosen friendship” (φιλότητα δʼ ἑλέσθαι, 16.282; see [[menis]]).

**Kindliness.** The abstract φιλοφροσύνη, “kindliness, a friendly mind”, is found only once in Homer, in Peleus' charge as Odysseus recalls it: “hold back your proud heart in your breast; for kindliness is better; and cease from strife that devises evil” (9.255–257; see [[kindliness-is-better]]). It is the opposite of ἔρις, the strife that began the quarrel (see [[eris]]).
