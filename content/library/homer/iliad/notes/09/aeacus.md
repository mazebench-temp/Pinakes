---
summary: Aeacus is named twice through his grandson's patronymic as the envoys come to Achilles' hut: they pray to win over “the great mind of the grandson of Aeacus”, and Patroclus waits for “the grandson of Aeacus” to leave off his singing (9.184, 9.191).
grc: [Αἰακίδην]
---

Αἰακίδης, “grandson of Aeacus”, had been heard before only in the Trojan Catalogue (2.860, 2.874). In Book 9 it comes twice in eight lines, in the genitive, Αἰακίδαο (9.184), and in the accusative, Αἰακίδην (9.191), as the envoys walk along the shore praying to Poseidon and find Achilles at his lyre (see [[grandson-of-aeacus]] and [[achilles]]). The name carries Achilles' descent from Zeus, the father of Aeacus, which Achilles himself sets out to the river god in Book 21 (21.187–189).
