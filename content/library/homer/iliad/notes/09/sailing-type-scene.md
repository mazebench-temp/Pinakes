summary: Achilles tells Odysseus how he will sail home, step by step, in the future tense: a sacrifice, the loading, the launching, the rowers, a fair passage from Poseidon, and Phthia on the third day (9.357–363). Odysseus reports it as a threat (9.682–683).
refs: [9.357-363, 9.682-683]
passages:
  - 9.356-363 | Achilles will sail at dawn
---

The voyage is imagined, not made, but it is told in the pattern's order (see [[sailing-type-scene]]):

{{quote:10f5ee64-7bbb-5d09-bd27-7ffd1469f126}}

1. **Sacrifice.** “When I have made sacrifice to Zeus and all the gods” (9.357; see [[sacrifice]]).
2. **Loading and launching.** νηήσας, “having loaded”, and ἅλα δὲ προερύσσω, “when I have drawn them down to the sea”, the verb with which Agamemnon launched the ship for Chryse (9.358; 1.308).
3. **Rowing.** The ships will be seen “very early, sailing on the fish-filled Hellespont”, with men in them “eager to row” (9.360–361; see [[hellespont]]).
4. **The god's wind.** “If the famous shaker of the earth grants me fair sailing” (9.362; see [[poseidon]]). εὐπλοΐη, “fair sailing”, occurs only here in Homer.
5. **Arrival.** “On the third day I would reach deep-soiled Phthia” (9.363; see [[phthia]]). In the Odyssey Nestor tells how Diomedes' companions brought their ships into Argos on the fourth day after Troy (Od. 3.180–182).

“You will see, if you wish and if you care about it” (9.359) is the verse with which Odysseus answered Agamemnon's rebuke in Book 4 (4.353); Achilles turns it on Odysseus. In the report the voyage shrinks to a threat: “he himself threatened that when dawn appears he will drag his well-benched, curving ships to the sea” (9.682–683; see [[message-repeated]] and [[ships]]).
