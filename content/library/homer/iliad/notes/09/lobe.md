---
summary: Achilles sets his condition in one word: Agamemnon will not persuade him “until he has paid me back for all his heart-grieving insult” (λώβην). The gifts do not answer it, and he never says what would.
passages:
  - 9.379-387 | Not for all the wealth of Orchomenus and Thebes
---

**The condition.** At the end of the list of things that would not persuade him, ten and twenty times the gifts, the wealth of Orchomenus and of Egyptian Thebes, gifts as many as the sand and the dust, Achilles names the one thing that would:

{{quote:9169bac7-9b25-53a5-bb68-9544ce6fb669}}

πρίν γʼ ἀπὸ πᾶσαν ἐμοὶ δόμεναι θυμαλγέα λώβην, literally “until he gives back to me the whole heart-grieving outrage”. The verb, ἀποδίδωμι in tmesis, is the verb of paying what is owed, used on the shield of the man who claims he has paid a blood-price in full (πάντʼ ἀποδοῦναι, 18.499); the outrage is to be paid off whole, πᾶσαν. Gifts are paid in goods, and they have already been offered without limit. What would pay off an insult, Achilles does not say, and the envoys do not ask (see [[poine]] and [[honor-and-dishonor]]). In the quarrel he told Agamemnon that only the worthlessness of his subjects saved him: “otherwise, son of Atreus, this insult would be your last” (λωβήσαιο, 1.232). Here he holds him to the debt.

**Another outrage.** When the λώβη is next named between them, the debt has changed. In Book 19 Achilles tells Agamemnon and Odysseus that he would send the army into battle unfed, and feast at sunset, “when we have avenged the outrage” (ἐπὴν τεισαίμεθα λώβην, 19.208). The outrage now is Patroclus' death, and the payment will be taken from Hector.
