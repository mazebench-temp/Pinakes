---
summary: Diomedes' last words look to the dawn: “when fair rosy-fingered Dawn appears,” lead out the army and fight among the foremost.
---

“But when fair rosy-fingered Dawn appears, quickly draw up the army and the horses before the ships, urging them on, and fight yourself among the foremost” (9.707–709; [[eos]]). The verse joins καλή, “fair,” to the epithet, a combination found only here. The night of the embassy ends with the dawn of the next battle in view, and the dawn comes at the start of Book 11 (11.1–2), after the night raid of Book 10. See [[diomedes]].
