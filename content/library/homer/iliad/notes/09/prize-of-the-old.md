summary: Achilles sends the envoys back with his message, “for that is the prize of the old”, the formula with which Nestor claimed counsel as the privilege of age. The elders' prize, in Achilles' mouth, is to find a better plan than the one that has failed.
passages:
  - 9.421-426 | Declare my message
---

Achilles ends his answer to Odysseus by giving the envoys their task: “But you go to the chiefs of the Achaeans and declare my message—for that is the prize of the old—so that they may think of some other, better plan in their minds” (9.421–423). The half-line is Nestor's from Book 4, where the old king, too old to fight, will command the horsemen “with counsel and with words; for that is the prize of the old” (4.323). The γέροντες are the council of chiefs, whether or not they are old, and Nestor and Phoenix, who are, sit among them (see [[elders]] and [[geras]]).

The formula has an edge here. The prize of the old is counsel, and the counsel they gave, to win Achilles back with gifts, is the one he has just refused; “this one is not ready to their hand, the one they devised now, while I nurse my wrath apart” (9.425–426; see [[menis]] and [[boule]]). Achilles gives the elders back their prize, the right to think again. The γέρας of the old is the one prize in the book that he does not dispute.

The old man of the embassy is not sent back with the others. “But let Phoenix stay here with us and lie down to sleep, so that he may follow me in the ships to our dear native land tomorrow, if he wishes” (9.427–429). The elders are to plan again; the old man who reared Achilles is to sail home with him (see [[phoenix]]).
