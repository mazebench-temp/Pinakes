---
summary: “Phoebus Apollo” is the god of the treasure-house at Pytho in Achilles' reply, and the god who took Marpessa from Idas in Phoenix's story.
---

In Achilles' reply Apollo is the god whose stone threshold at Pytho shuts in a hoard that is not worth his life: “the Archer, Phoebus Apollo, in rocky Pytho” (9.404–405; [[aphetor]], [[pytho]]). In Phoenix's story he is twice “Phoebus Apollo,” the god against whom Idas took up his bow “for the sake of the bride of the lovely ankles” (9.559–560), and “the far-working Phoebus Apollo” who snatched Marpessa away (9.564; [[far-worker]], [[of-the-lovely-ankles]], [[idas-and-apollo]]).
