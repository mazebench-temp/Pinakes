---
summary: The lyre on which Achilles sings the famous deeds of men came from the spoils “when he destroyed the city of Eetion” (9.188).
---

The envoys find Achilles playing a finely wrought lyre with a crossbar of silver, “he had taken it from the spoils when he destroyed the city of Eetion” (9.186–188; see [[lyre]], [[thebe]] and [[sack-of-thebe]]). Eetion's city has given Agamemnon Chryseis (1.366–369) and Achilles a lyre, and it will give him the trace horse Pedasus (16.152–153) and the lump of iron he sets as a prize for Patroclus' games (23.826–829). The man who killed Eetion and buried him in his armor (6.416–419) now sings to a lyre from his city (see [[achilles]]).
