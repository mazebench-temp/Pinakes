**Orestes.** If they come home to Argos, Achilles will be Agamemnon's son-in-law, “and I would honor him equally with Orestes, who is reared as my darling son in great abundance” (9.142–143; Odysseus' repetition, 9.284–285; [[orestes]], [[udder-of-the-plowland]]). This is the first time the Iliad names Agamemnon's son, who is not the Achaean Orestes of Book 5 ([[orestes-the-achaean]]). The epithet of the cherished heir is given to the son on whom the house depends.

**Phoenix.** Peleus received the fugitive Phoenix “and loved me as a father loves his son, his only son, his darling, heir to great possessions” (9.481–482; [[as-a-father-loves-his-son]], [[wealthy]]). The man cursed by his own father never to have a son (9.453–457) was loved as one, and made Achilles his son in turn (9.494–495). Achilles has been offered a place beside Agamemnon's darling son, and refuses it; Phoenix, who was given that place in Peleus' house, pleads with him. See [[phoenix]].
