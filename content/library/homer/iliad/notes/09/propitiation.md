summary: The embassy uses the language of appeasing a god on a man. Agamemnon will “make amends” with gifts, the envoys promise that Achilles will be honored “like a god”, Phoenix argues that even the gods can be turned by offerings, and Ajax asks him to make his heart “gracious”, ἵλαος, the word for a god appeased; Agamemnon, for his part, compares him to Hades, the god no one can soothe.

**Amends.** Nestor's counsel is to win Achilles back: “let us consider how we may make amends to him and win him over with kindly gifts and with soothing words” (ἀρεσσάμενοι, 9.112–113). Agamemnon agrees in the same verb: “I am willing to make amends and give a boundless ransom” (ἀρέσαι, 9.120; see [[apoina]]). He will say the verse again when he gives the gifts in Book 19 (19.138), and there Odysseus asks that he make amends with a feast as well (ἀρεσάσθω, 19.179; see [[feast]]).

**Like a god.** The gifts are offered as honors fit for a god. The men of the seven cities “will honor him with gifts like a god” (θεὸν ὣς, 9.155, 9.297); if Achilles hates Agamemnon, let him pity the other Achaeans, who “like a god … will honor you” (9.302–303); and Phoenix ends, “come; for the Achaeans will honor you as equal to a god” (9.603; see [[time]] and [[the-seven-cities]]).

Burnt offerings, vows, libation and the savor of fat are the means by which men win back an angry god (see [[sacrifice]], [[libation]] and [[prayer]]). If the gods, “and theirs is the greater prowess and honor and strength”, can be turned, Achilles has no need of “a pitiless heart” (9.496–498). Ajax, the last of the envoys to speak, uses the word of an appeased god: “so make your heart gracious” (ἵλαον ἔνθεο θυμόν, 9.639). ἵλαος is what Hephaestus promised the Olympian would be if Hera spoke to him gently (1.582–583), and what Odysseus will ask Achilles' heart to be in Book 19 (19.178).

**The god who cannot be soothed.** Agamemnon's own words put Achilles beside the one god who takes no appeasement: “Let him be tamed: Hades, you know, is implacable and untamable, and for that he is the most hateful to mortals of all the gods” (9.158–159). ἀμείλιχος, “not to be soothed”, answers Nestor's “soothing words” (μειλιχίοισι, 9.113), and the Erinys who hears Althaea's curse has the same “implacable heart” (9.572; see [[hades]], [[implacable]] and [[curses]]). Ajax says the same of Achilles in plainer words: the gods have put in him “a heart that is unrelenting and evil” (9.636–637).
