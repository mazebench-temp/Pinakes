summary: Achilles tells the envoys what his mother has told him, that two fates carry him toward death, imperishable fame without a homecoming if he stays at Troy, a long life without fame if he goes home. Only here is his early death offered as a choice, and that night he chooses the long life.

**The choice.** In Book 1 the short life is simply his lot: “since you bore me to be short-lived” (1.352; also 1.416–418, 1.505). In Book 9 he adds what Thetis has told him (φησι, “tells me”): διχθαδίας κῆρας, “two different fates”, are carrying him “toward the end of death” (θανάτοιο τέλος δέ, 9.411; see [[ker-doom]] and the translation's note on 9.401–416). Both fates end in death, and they differ in time and in fame. If he stays and fights at Troy, his homecoming is lost but his fame will be “imperishable” (κλέος ἄφθιτον); if he goes home, his “noble fame” is lost, and the end of death will be slow to come (9.412–416; see [[kleos]], [[nostos]] and [[the-two-fates-of-achilles]]). κλέος ἄφθιτον occurs only here in Homer.

**Life chosen.** That night Achilles chooses the second fate. Nothing is worth his life, not all the wealth of Ilios or of Pytho, for a man's soul cannot be raided back once it has passed the barrier of his teeth (9.401–409; see [[a-mans-soul-cannot-come-back]]); he will sail home tomorrow (9.356–363), and he advises the others to sail too (9.417–420). The choice does not hold. By the end of the night he has promised to stay, though not to fight (9.650–655; see [[the-embassy-to-achilles]]).

**Death accepted.** The rest of the poem knows no choice, only a short life and a death near at hand. When Achilles resolves to avenge Patroclus, Thetis tells him that his death is ready for him at once after Hector's (18.95–96), and he takes the first fate, naming its prize with the words of 9.415: “now let me win noble fame”, κλέος ἐσθλόν (18.121). The horse Xanthus foretells that the day of his death is near (19.408–417), and the dying Hector names his killers, Paris and Apollo, at the Scaean gates (22.358–360; see [[the-short-life-of-achilles]]).
