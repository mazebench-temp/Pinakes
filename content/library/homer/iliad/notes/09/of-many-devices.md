---
summary: Achilles opens his great reply with the formal address of Odysseus, “Odysseus of many devices,” and Ajax uses the same verse to tell Odysseus that it is time to go.
---

“Son of Laertes, sprung from Zeus, Odysseus of many devices” (9.308). Achilles answers the most skillful speaker of the Achaeans with his full titles, and in the next verses rejects skill: he will speak out his word without regard for anyone, for hateful to him as the gates of Hades is the man “who hides one thing in his mind and says another” (9.309–313; [[son-of-laertes]], [[sprung-from-zeus]]). Whether Achilles means Odysseus, Agamemnon, or no one in particular has been debated since antiquity; Plato's Hippias takes the verses as Achilles' truthfulness set against Odysseus' cunning (Plato, *Hippias Minor* 364e–365b; [[plato-hippias-minor]], [[hateful-as-the-gates-of-hades]]). Ajax uses the same address at the end of the embassy: “let us go” (9.624–625). See [[odysseus]].
