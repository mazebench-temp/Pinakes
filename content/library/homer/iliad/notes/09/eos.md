---
summary: Book 9 is a night book, and Dawn is what its speakers wait for. Hector prays for brilliant Dawn to appear, Achilles puts off his choice until dawn, Phoenix lies down to wait for brilliant Dawn, and Diomedes ends the council with orders for the moment when fair rosy-fingered Dawn appears.
# ἠώς at 9.618 and 9.682 (ἅμʼ ἠοῖ φαινομένηφι, "when dawn appears") is the time of day, not the goddess.
grc: ["ἠῶ δῖαν"]
passages:
  - 9.704-709 | Diomedes' orders for the dawn
---

All of Book 9 belongs to the night after the second day of battle, and the night goes on through Book 10 (see [[the-second-day-of-battle]] and [[war-and-night]]). The dawn that everyone waits for rises only at the start of Book 11, from the bed of Tithonus (11.1–2).

On the Trojan side, Odysseus tells Achilles, Hector “prays for brilliant Dawn to appear with all speed” (9.240), so that he can burn the ships. At nightfall he had ordered fires kept burning “all night long until early-born Dawn” (8.508), and he means to arm “early, at daybreak” (8.530; see [[early-born]] and [[hector]]).

On the Achaean side, dawn is when Achilles' answers fall due. To Odysseus he says that “tomorrow … very early” his ships will be sailing on the Hellespont (9.357–360). To Phoenix he says that “when dawn appears / we will consider whether to go home to our own or to stay” (9.618–619). Odysseus reports only the first answer, though he puts it in the phrase of the second: “when dawn appears / he will drag his well-benched, curving ships to the sea” (9.682–683; see [[achilles]] and [[odysseus]]). In ἅμʼ ἠοῖ φαινομένηφι, “when dawn appears”, ἠώς is the time of day, as at 8.470 and 8.525, and the index does not count those lines as hers.

Phoenix stays the night in Achilles' hut: “There the old man lay down and waited for brilliant Dawn” (9.662; see [[phoenix]]). The text prints ἠῶ lowercase here and capitalized at 9.240; the phrase is the same, and the translation gives “brilliant Dawn” both times (see [[brilliant]]). The verse is close to one in the Odyssey, where Telemachus goes to his chamber, lies down and waits for Dawn, while his father stays in the hall (ἔνθʼ ἄρα καὶ τότʼ ἔλεκτο καὶ Ἠῶ δῖαν ἔμιμνεν, *Odyssey* 19.47–51).

Diomedes has the last word of the council: “but when fair rosy-fingered Dawn appears, / quickly draw up the army and the horses before the ships” (9.707–708). καλή, “fair”, is given to rosy-fingered Dawn only here in Homer (see [[rosy-fingered]], [[dawn-line]] and [[diomedes]]). When she comes, Agamemnon does as Diomedes said: he shouts to the Argives to arm and puts on his own bronze (11.15–16).
