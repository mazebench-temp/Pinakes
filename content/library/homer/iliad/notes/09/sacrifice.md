**At the meal.** When the meat is served, Achilles “bade his companion sacrifice to the gods, Patroclus; and he threw the offerings into the fire” (9.219–220). In Homer θύω is to burn offerings for the gods, not to kill, and θυηλαί, found only here, are the portions thrown into the fire. No animal is killed for this meal; the gods' share is taken from the food before the men eat (see the translation's note on 9.219–220 and [[feast-type-scene]]). Eumaeus does the same at his supper with the disguised Odysseus, burning the first portions to the gods and pouring a libation before he serves his guest (Od. 14.446–447).

**Before sailing.** Achilles tells Odysseus that tomorrow, “when I have made sacrifice to Zeus and all the gods”, he will load his ships and put to sea (9.357–358). The sacrifice belongs to the departure, as the libation belongs to the envoys' (see [[sailing-type-scene]] and [[libation]]).

The offerings are the ones Zeus called the gods' due at Troy, “libation and the savor of the burnt fat” (4.49), and here they turn a god's anger aside after a wrong. Phoenix's point is that a man who refuses to be turned is harder than the gods (see [[propitiation]], [[prayer]] and [[gods-and-mortals]]).

**The forgotten offering.** In the story of Meleager the war of Calydon began with a sacrifice not made. Artemis was angry “because no harvest offering on the slope of his orchard was made to her by Oeneus; the other gods feasted on hecatombs, but to her alone, the daughter of great Zeus, he made no sacrifice. Either he forgot, or did not think of it; and he was greatly blinded in his heart” (9.534–537). θαλύσια, found only here, are the first-fruits of the harvest. The omission is told in the verb of Agamemnon's confession: Oeneus “was greatly blinded in his heart” (ἀάσατο, 9.537), as Agamemnon said “I was blinded” (ἀασάμην, 9.116, 9.119; see [[ate]]). The goddess answers the omission with the boar (9.538–542; see [[oeneus]], [[artemis]] and [[the-calydonian-boar]]). Poseidon made a like complaint against the Achaeans, who built their wall without giving the gods hecatombs (7.450; see [[hecatomb]]).
