summary: The envoys' visit to Achilles is one of the fullest visit scenes in the Iliad: they find him singing to his lyre with Patroclus sitting silent opposite, he springs up, greets them, seats them and feeds them before they state their errand, and at the end one guest stays the night in a bed made for him (9.185–221, 9.656–662).
refs: [9.193-200]
passages:
  - 9.185-200 | The envoys are received
---

The scene has all the parts of the pattern (see [[visit-scene]]).

1. **Arrival and finding.** The envoys come to the huts and ships of the Myrmidons and find Achilles “delighting his heart with a clear-toned lyre” and singing the famous deeds of men, while “Patroclus alone sat facing him in silence, waiting for the grandson of Aeacus to leave off his singing” (9.185–191; see [[lyre]] and [[messenger-scene]]).
2. **Reception.** Achilles springs up in amazement with the lyre still in his hand, and Patroclus stands too; Achilles greets them, “Welcome, both of you! Surely you come as friends; surely the need is great”, leads them in and seats them on chairs with purple rugs (9.193–200; see [[throne]] and [[purple]]).
3. **Food and drink.** Before anything is asked, the wine is mixed stronger and a meal is made, and the guests eat (9.201–222; see [[feast-type-scene]]). Achilles needs no question to know why they have come: “surely the need is great”.
4. **The errand.** Ajax nods to Phoenix, and Odysseus raises his cup and begins (9.223–225). The speeches that follow are a guest's pleas made under the host's roof, and Ajax ends by asking Achilles to “respect your own roof; we are here beneath your roof” (9.640; see [[xenia]] and [[embassy]]).
5. **Departure.** The guests pour a libation and go (9.656–657; see [[libation]]). One stays. Achilles has a bed made for Phoenix, who sleeps in the hut and waits for dawn (9.617–622, 9.658–662; see [[bed]]).

In the Iliad's other visits a visitor in haste refuses the seat (11.646–648, 23.205). The envoys sit, and eat for the second time that night, as Odysseus says in his toast (9.225–228; see [[feast]]).
