---
summary: The envoys pray to “the earth-holder, the shaker of the earth” as they walk by the sea, and Achilles says he will be in Phthia on the third day if “the famous shaker of the earth” grants him fair sailing.
---

The envoys walk by the sea “praying earnestly to the earth-holder, the shaker of the earth, that they might easily win over the great mind of the grandson of Aeacus” (9.183–184; [[earth-holder]], [[grandson-of-aeacus]]). It is the first time the two titles of Poseidon stand together in the poem. Achilles names the same god for the opposite wish: “if the famous shaker of the earth grants me fair sailing, on the third day I would reach deep-soiled Phthia” (9.362–363; [[famous]], [[deep-soiled]]). The god of the sea is asked to bring Achilles back to the war and to carry him away from it. See [[poseidon]].
