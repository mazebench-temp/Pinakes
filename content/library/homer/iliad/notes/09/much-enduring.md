---
summary: “Much-enduring brilliant Odysseus” reports to Agamemnon that Achilles refuses him and his gifts.
---

“Then much-enduring brilliant Odysseus spoke to him in turn: Most glorious son of Atreus, Agamemnon, lord of men, that man will not quench his anger” (9.676–678; [[brilliant]], [[most-glorious]]). The formula of the Odyssey's hero introduces the report of a failure, which Odysseus delivers in full: Achilles rejects Agamemnon and his gifts, threatens to sail at dawn, and advises the others to go home (9.677–692; [[message-repeated]]). He reports only the first of Achilles' three answers. See [[odysseus]].
