---
summary: Diomedes calls Agamemnon “Strange one” as he asks whether the king really thinks the Achaeans are cowards.
---

“Strange one, do you really think the sons of the Achaeans are so unwarlike and cowardly as you say?” (9.40–41; [[sons-of-the-achaeans]]). The vocative δαιμόνιε, here elided to δαιμόνιʼ, is how a speaker addresses someone whose behavior he cannot understand. Diomedes uses it to a king to whom he owes obedience, after reminding him that he once called Diomedes himself unwarlike and a coward (9.34–36; 4.370–400). He turns the insult back on the man who gave it. See [[diomedes]] and [[agamemnon]].
