summary: The night moves between two huts. In Agamemnon's, full of wine, the elders feast and choose the envoys, and the envoys report; in Achilles' the envoys are guests “under my roof”, and Achilles says he will not fight until Hector reaches the huts and ships of the Myrmidons. The night ends there with Achilles and Patroclus asleep beside women taken in war, as Achilles will sleep beside Briseis in the last book.

**Agamemnon's huts.** Nestor reminds Agamemnon that his huts are full of wine (9.71), and the elders feast and take counsel in his hut (9.89–90; see [[feast]]). The envoys set out from it and return to it, in the verse that brought the chiefs to Agamemnon after the duel, “when they had come to the huts of the son of Atreus” (9.669 = 7.313).

**Achilles' hut.** The envoys come “to the huts and ships of the Myrmidons” (9.185), in the verse of the heralds who came for Briseis (1.328), and Nestor recalls how Agamemnon took her “from the hut of Achilles” (κλισίηθεν, 9.106–107). The hut is a house. Achilles has the wine mixed because the dearest of men are “under my roof” (ἐμῷ ὑπέασι μελάθρῳ, 9.204), he sits facing Odysseus “by the other wall” (9.218–219), and Ajax appeals to him as a host: “respect your own roof; we are here beneath your roof” (9.640; see [[xenia]] and [[visit-scene]]). When Achilles nods to Patroclus to make up a bed for Phoenix, the others understand that it is time to think of going home from the hut (9.620–622).

**The line of defense.** Achilles' answer to Ajax draws a line at his own hut: he will not think of fighting “until the son of wise-hearted Priam, brilliant Hector, comes to the huts and ships of the Myrmidons, killing the Argives, and burns the ships with fire. But around my hut and my black ship, I think, Hector will be held back, eager for battle though he is” (9.651–655; see [[ships]]).

**The night.** Patroclus bids his companions and the slave women make a bed for Phoenix, and the old man lies down; “But Achilles slept in the inner part of the well-built hut, and beside him lay a woman, whom he had brought from Lesbos, the daughter of Phorbas, fair-cheeked Diomede”, and Patroclus on the other side with Iphis (9.658–668; see [[bed]], [[diomede]] and [[iphis]]). The last night in the hut in the Iliad is told in the same verses. Achilles bids his companions and the slave women make beds for Priam and his herald under the portico, and Achilles “slept in the inner part of the well-built hut”, with fair-cheeked Briseis beside him (24.643–648, 24.673–676; 24.675 = 9.663). Briseis, refused with the gifts in Book 9 and given back with them in Book 19, lies where Diomede lay.
