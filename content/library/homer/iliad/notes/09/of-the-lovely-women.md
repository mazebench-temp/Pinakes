---
summary: Phoenix left “Hellas of the lovely women” when he fled from his father.
---

Not even if a god made him young again would Phoenix leave Achilles: young “as I was when I first left Hellas of the lovely women, fleeing the quarrels of my father Amyntor” (9.446–448; [[hellas]], [[amyntor]], [[the-flight-of-phoenix]]). The verse-end formula of Hellas, used in the Catalogue of the land of Achilles' own men (2.683), names the country of a man whose story turns on a woman, his father's concubine. Thirty verses later he flees “through Hellas with its wide dancing floors” to Phthia ([[with-wide-dancing-floors]]). Demetrius of Scepsis read “Ormenium, rich in flocks” in place of “Hellas of the lovely women,” to fit a genealogy that made Phoenix a Thessalian ([[hellas-or-ormenium]]).
