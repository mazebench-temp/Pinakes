---
summary: Achilles mocks the Achaean wall: not even with it can Agamemnon hold back “the strength of man-slaying Hector.”
---

“He has built a wall, and driven a ditch along it, wide and great, and in it he planted stakes; but not even so can he hold the strength of man-slaying Hector in check” (9.349–352; [[the-achaean-wall]]). Achilles names Hector by the epithet with which he first named him in the poem, in his oath of Book 1: when “man-slaying Hector” kills many, the Achaeans will long for Achilles (1.242). The oath is coming true, and Achilles now adds that while he fought, Hector would not come out beyond the Scaean gates and the oak (9.352–355; [[hectors-escape-at-the-oak]]). See [[hector]].
