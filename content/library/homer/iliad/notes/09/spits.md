  - 9.210-215 | The roasting

“And he cut them up well and threaded them on spits” (καὶ ἀμφʼ ὀβελοῖσιν ἔπειρε, 9.210) is the verse of the sacrifice at Chryse turned to the singular (1.465; see [[sacrifice-type-scene]]). Then the roasting, which the sacrifice scenes pass over in a half-verse (ὤπτησάν τε περιφραδέως, 1.466), is told step by step: “when the fire had burned down and the flame had died away, he spread out the embers and stretched the spits above them, and sprinkled them with divine salt, lifting them onto the firedogs” (9.212–214). The meat is cooked over embers, not in the flame, with the spits resting on supports. κρατευταί, the stones or stands that held them, are named only here, and the salt is called θεῖος, “divine”, for reasons the poem does not give (see the translation's note on 9.214). In the Odyssey Eumaeus spits and roasts two piglets for his guest and sets them before him “hot, on the spits themselves” (Od. 14.74–77). See [[feast-type-scene]].
