---
summary: Odysseus tells Achilles that Hector, “exulting greatly in his strength,” rages, trusting in Zeus, and honors neither men nor gods.
---

“And Hector, exulting greatly in his strength, rages terribly, trusting in Zeus, and pays no honor at all to men or gods; a mighty frenzy has entered into him” (9.237–239; [[krateros]], [[hector]]). Odysseus' picture of Hector is meant to move Achilles: here is the enemy, and only Achilles can meet him. He ends his speech on the same note: “now you might kill Hector, since he would come very close to you in his deadly frenzy” (9.304–305; [[deadly]]).
