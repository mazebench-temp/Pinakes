---
summary: The women of Lesbos are “skilled in flawless handiwork,” and Achilles is “the noble son of Peleus” to Nestor, who sends the envoys, and to Diomedes, who regrets they were sent.
en: [flawless]
---

**Flawless handiwork.** Agamemnon offers “seven women skilled in flawless handiwork, women of Lesbos” (9.128–129), and Odysseus repeats the verse (9.270). The phrase γυναῖκας ἀμύμονα ἔργα ἰδυίας describes the women when they are handed over in Book 19 (19.245) and the first prize of the chariot race in Book 23 (23.263). Here ἀμύμων praises the work, not the woman: the translation renders it “flawless” ([[captive-women]], [[lesbos]]).

**The noble son of Peleus.** Nestor sends the envoys off with instructions “to try how they might win over the noble son of Peleus” (9.181). When they come back refused, Diomedes uses the same words: “I wish you had never entreated the noble son of Peleus” (9.698). The praise stays in place while the men who use it pass from hope to anger. See [[son-of-peleus]].
