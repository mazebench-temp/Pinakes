---
summary: The sons of Atreus are named together once, in Achilles' bitter question whether they are “the only ones of mortal men who love their wives” (9.340–341).
refs: [9.340]
---

Achilles asks why the Argives must fight the Trojans at all: “Why did he gather the army and lead it here, the son of Atreus? Was it not for the sake of Helen of the lovely hair?” (9.338–339). Then he widens the charge to both brothers, the husband who lost his wife and the king who took Achilles' own: “Are they the only ones of mortal men who love their wives, the sons of Atreus? For any man who is good and sensible loves his own and cares for her, as I too loved her from my heart, though she was won by my spear” (9.340–343; see [[briseis]], [[helen]], [[menelaus]] and [[agamemnon]]). The war for one man's wife is set beside the wrong done to another's (see [[captive-women]]).
