summary: Agamemnon offers Achilles “seven well-peopled citadels,” and Achilles answers that not all that Ilios possessed, “that well-peopled citadel,” is worth his life.
grc: ["εὖ ναιόμενα πτολίεθρα"]
en: [well-peopled citadels]
---

The plural εὖ ναιόμενα πτολίεθρα, “well-peopled citadels,” describes the seven towns near Pylos that Agamemnon offers (9.149) and Odysseus repeats (9.291; [[the-seven-cities]]). Achilles uses the singular formula for Troy. Nothing is worth his life, “not all that they say Ilios possessed, that well-peopled citadel, in former days, in time of peace, before the sons of the Achaeans came” (9.401–403; [[aphetor]], [[psyche]]). Seven towns are offered to buy his return to the war, and he weighs against his life a single town as it was before the war came. See [[troy]].
