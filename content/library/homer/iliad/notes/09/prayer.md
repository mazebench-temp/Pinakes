**Before the embassy.** Nestor calls for water and holy silence, “so that we may pray to Zeus, son of Cronus, if he will have pity” (9.171–172; see [[hand-washing]] and [[libation]]). The narrator does not give the prayer or say whether Zeus heard it. On the way the envoys pray again, walking “along the shore of the loud-roaring sea, praying earnestly to the earth-holder, the shaker of the earth, that they might easily win over the great mind of the grandson of Aeacus” (9.182–184; see [[poseidon]]). Chryses walked along the same shore, in the same half-verse, before his prayer to Apollo (1.34–35). His prayer was heard; the envoys' is not (see [[embassy]]).

**Hector's prayer.** Odysseus tells Achilles what the enemy is praying for: “He prays for brilliant Dawn to appear with all speed”, so that he can burn the ships (9.240–243; see [[hector]] and [[dawn-line]]). Achilles, for his part, means to sacrifice to Zeus and all the gods before he sails, and hopes that “the famous shaker of the earth grants me fair sailing” (9.357, 9.362).

**Phoenix on prayer.** Men turn the gods “with burnt offerings and gentle vows” (εὐχωλῇς, 9.499; see [[sacrifice]] and [[propitiation]]), and the Prayers themselves, the daughters of Zeus, help the man who respects them, “and they hear him when he prays” (9.509). The man who refuses them they pray against: “then they go to Zeus, son of Cronus, and pray that Blind Folly may follow him” (9.511–512). The entreaties of men become goddesses, and to refuse the envoys is to refuse Zeus' daughters (see [[litai]] and [[ate]]).

**Prayers below.** In Phoenix's story Meleager's mother prays to the gods under the earth. “To the gods she prayed long in her grief over the killing of her brother, and many times she beat the all-nourishing earth with her hands, calling on Hades and dread Persephone, kneeling down, and her bosom was wet with tears, to give death to her son” (9.566–571). Prayers to the gods above are made standing, with hands lifted to the sky (χεῖρας ἀνασχών, 1.450); this one is made on the knees, striking the ground, and the Erinys hears it “out of Erebus” (9.571–572; see [[erinyes]], [[hades]] and [[persephone]]). ἠρᾶτο is “prayed”, and her prayer is a curse (ἀρέων, “curses”, 9.566). Phoenix's own father cursed him in the same way, calling on the Erinyes, “and the gods fulfilled his curses, Zeus of the underworld and dread Persephone” (9.454–457; see [[curses]]).

**Claims.** At 9.60 and 9.161 εὔχομαι is “claim”, not “pray”. Nestor claims to be older than Diomedes, and Agamemnon to be older than Achilles, with the same end of the verse (γεραίτερος, προγενέστερος εὔχομαι εἶναι; see [[euchomai]]).
