summary: Achilles turns the cause of the war against the man who leads it. Agamemnon gathered the army for the sake of Helen; are the sons of Atreus the only men who love their wives? Agamemnon, for his part, measures the Trojan women of his offer against “Argive Helen”.
passages:
  - 9.335-345 | The wife dear to my heart
---

In Book 1 Achilles said that he had come to Troy not for any wrong the Trojans had done him but to please the sons of Atreus, “to win satisfaction for Menelaus” (1.152–160). In Book 9 he names the cause of the war, and turns it into an argument:

{{quote:1b69306b-0186-52ec-a5b8-423edd5528d5}}

If the war is fought to recover a wife, then Agamemnon has done to Achilles what Paris did to Menelaus. Achilles calls Briseis his ἄλοχος, “wife”, the word for the wives of the Atreidae in the same sentence, though she was won by his spear (see [[taking-of-briseis]], [[briseis]] and [[helen]]). The ground of the war becomes a reason not to fight in it. Earlier in the speech he says that he spent bloody days in battle “fighting with men for the sake of their wives” (9.327). Whose wives the Greek leaves open, the enemies' own, whom they defend, or the Achaeans', Helen above all (see the translation's note on 9.327).

Agamemnon, too, measures his offer by Helen. When Troy is sacked, Achilles may choose for himself twenty Trojan women, “the most beautiful after Argive Helen” (9.139–140, 9.281–282). The woman the war is fought for is the standard of the women it will take.
