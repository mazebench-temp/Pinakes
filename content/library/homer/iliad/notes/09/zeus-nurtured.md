---
summary: Odysseus addresses Achilles as “nurtured by Zeus” in his appeal, and Achilles gives the kings' epithet to Phoenix: “Phoenix, old father, nurtured by Zeus.”
---

Odysseus' plea turns from the feast to the danger: “no, we look on a calamity all too great, you who are nurtured by Zeus, and we are afraid” (9.229–230). The vocative διοτρεφές names Achilles as a king, one of those whom Zeus rears ([[basileus]]). Achilles uses it once in the book, to his old tutor: “Phoenix, old father, nurtured by Zeus, I have no need of this honor” (9.607–608; [[phoenix]], [[time]]). The address that kings use among themselves is given with affection to the man who reared Achilles, and in the same speech Achilles offers him “half my honor” and a share of his kingship (9.616).
