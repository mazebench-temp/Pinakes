The words of honor, τίω, τιμάω and τιμή with their negatives, come some two dozen times in the night's speeches. Every speaker uses them, and they measure everything: a king's rank, a son-in-law's place, a city's tribute, a god's worship.

**Honor offered.** Diomedes begins. Zeus gave Agamemnon “to be honored above all men for your scepter, but courage he did not give you” (9.38–39): a king's honor without the thing that earns it (see [[alke]]). Nestor turns Achilles' own complaint into an accusation: “the best of men, whom the immortals themselves honored, you dishonored; for you took his prize and keep it” (9.110–111). The second line is the one Achilles spoke to Thetis and Thetis to Zeus (1.356, 1.507), now said to the king's face. Agamemnon grants that Zeus “has honored this man” (9.118), and offers honor in its most concrete forms (see [[the-gifts-of-agamemnon]]). As his son-in-law Achilles will be honored “equally with Orestes” (9.142), and the men of the seven cities “will honor him with gifts like a god” (θεὸν ὣς τιμήσουσι, 9.155; see [[the-seven-cities]]). Odysseus adds the army, which “like a god” will honor him if he pities it (9.302–303), and Peleus' charge, that kindliness would make the Argives honor him the more (9.256–258; see [[kindliness-is-better]]). Phoenix closes the bidding: the Achaeans will honor him “as equal to a god” (ἶσον … θεῷ, 9.603). To be honored and looked on like gods is what Sarpedon says the Lycians give their kings (12.310–312).

**Honor refused.** Achilles' answer is that the scale is broken: “the coward and the brave are held in a single honor” (9.319; see [[no-man-escapes-his-fate]]). He prices Agamemnon at nothing, τίω δέ μιν ἐν καρὸς αἴσῃ, “I honor him at a hair's worth” (9.378; see [[at-a-hairs-worth]]), keeping τίω, the verb of the offer, for the refusal. The honor Thetis asked Zeus to give him (1.505–510) is the honor he now claims, and against it the honor that men pay in gifts is worth nothing to him (see [[aisa]]). In the same speech he gives honor himself: “Be king equally with me, and take half my honor” (9.616). The Lycian king gave Bellerophon “half of all his royal honor” (6.193); Achilles offers the same share to the old man who reared him (see [[moira]] and [[phoenix]]).

**Honor at home.** The word frames Phoenix's story too. His father loved his concubine “and he dishonored his wife, my mother” (ἀτιμάζεσκε, 9.450), and the quarrel that drove Phoenix from home began there (see [[the-flight-of-phoenix]]). Even Hector, in Odysseus' report, “pays no honor at all to men or gods” (9.238).

**Dishonor remembered.** Ajax says that the companions honored Achilles beside the ships above all others (ἐτίομεν, 9.631). Achilles grants it, but his heart swells whenever he remembers how Agamemnon treated him, “as if I were some dishonored vagrant” (ἀτίμητον μετανάστην, 9.648). ἀτίμητος is a man without honor or price, and a μετανάστης one who has left his own people to live among strangers, with no kin to stand up for him (see [[exile-and-refuge]]). Achilles says the line again to Patroclus (16.59). In the Odyssey, the honor that Phoenix promised is remembered as paid: “before, while you lived, we Argives honored you equal to the gods”, Odysseus tells Achilles' shade (*Odyssey* 11.484).
