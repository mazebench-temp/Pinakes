---
summary: Achilles seats the envoys “on chairs with purple rugs” (9.200), the seats of honor of a host's house; the only throne of the book is in Artemis' epithet, “of the golden throne” (9.533).
grc: [κλισμοῖσι]
en: [chairs]
---

Achilles leads the envoys in “and seated them on chairs with purple rugs” (ἐν κλισμοῖσι τάπησί τε πορφυρέοισιν, 9.200). κλισμός, from κλίνω, “lean”, is a chair with a back, the seat Hera and Athena take among the gods in Book 8 (8.436), and the seat Nestor and Machaon sit on when they come into Nestor's hut from the battle (11.623). In the Odyssey guests are seated “on chairs and thrones” (κατὰ κλισμούς τε θρόνους τε, Od. 1.145, 10.233, and often), and Circe's handmaids spread fine purple coverings on the thrones (Od. 10.352–353; see [[purple]]). The seat is the first part of a host's welcome, after the greeting and before the wine (see [[visit-scene]] and [[feast-type-scene]]). Achilles himself had sprung up from his own seat (ἕδος) with the lyre in his hand (9.194).

The only throne of the book is Artemis', in her epithet χρυσόθρονος, “of the golden throne” (9.533; see [[golden-throned]]).
