Here the town has the plural name, Φηραί; in Book 5 it is the singular Φηρή (5.543). Its epithet ζάθεος, “holy”, is given to Cilla, Nisa and Crisa elsewhere in the Iliad (1.38, 2.508, 2.520; see [[zatheos]]). It is the only one of the seven cities that Homer names outside the offer (see [[the-seven-cities]]).

Strabo places Pherae on the Messenian Gulf beside Thuria and Gerena, where the Nedon flows into the sea, and adds that Pharae stands five stades from the shore, with a summer anchorage (8.4.4–5; see [[strabo]]). Pausanias puts Pharae seventy stades from Abia and about six from the sea, with a grove of Apollo Carneius; the emperor Augustus had attached its Messenians to Laconia. There he heard the family of Diocles traced as Homer gives it in Book 5, from the river Alpheus to the twin sons killed at Troy, with an ancestress, Telegone, whom Homer leaves out (4.30.2–4.31.1; see [[diocles]] and [[orsilochus-son-of-diocles]]).
