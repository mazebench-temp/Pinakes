---
summary: Achilles says that tripods can be won “and the tawny heads of horses,” but not a man's life.
grc: [ξανθὰ]
en: [tawny]
---

“For cattle and fat sheep can be had by raiding, and tripods can be won, and the tawny heads of horses, but a man's soul cannot come back again” (9.406–408; [[a-mans-soul-cannot-come-back]], [[fat-sheep]]). Of horses ξανθός is the chestnut or bay of their coats, and the translation renders it “tawny” here; of men it is fair hair. Achilles' own horse Xanthus takes his name from the color (16.149; 19.400). See [[horses]].
