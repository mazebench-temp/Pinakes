---
summary: In Book 9 ἄναξ is the title of the king who offers, "Agamemnon, lord of men" (9.96, 9.114, 9.163, 9.672, 9.677, 9.697), and of the men who refuse and receive: "lord Achilles" (9.164), "lord Peleus", to whom Phoenix fled (9.480), and "the lord Phoebus Apollo", against whom Idas drew his bow (9.559).
grc: [ἄναχθʼ]
---

Nestor opens his counsel with the king's title and the reason for it: Agamemnon is "lord of many people", and Zeus has put into his hands "the scepter and the ordinances" (9.97–99; see [[scepter]] and [[themis]]). Agamemnon claims the rank in return, demanding that Achilles submit because he is "more kingly" (βασιλεύτερος, 9.160; see [[basileus]]). The vocative ἄναξ, "my lord", is how Diomedes claims his right to speak in the assembly (9.33) and how Odysseus, repeating Agamemnon's oath, addresses Achilles (9.276). Nestor gives Achilles the title too: the gifts Agamemnon offers "lord Achilles" are no longer to be scorned (9.164).

Phoenix came as an exile to "lord Peleus" (ἐς Πηλῆα ἄναχθʼ, 9.480), who made him lord in turn, "ruling over the Dolopians" (Δολόπεσσιν ἀνάσσων, 9.484; see [[dolopians]]). At 9.247, ἀλλʼ ἄνα is "Up, then", not the vocative of ἄναξ.
