Phoenix ends his story with Meleager alive, warding off the evil from the Aetolians “all the same”, without the gifts (9.597–599), and he does not say what became of the curse. But a curse that the Erinys has heard is one that will be fulfilled. ἔκλυεν, “heard” (9.572), is the word for a god who grants a prayer, as the Prayers “hear him when he prays” who respects them (9.509; see [[litai]]). Earlier in the same speech Phoenix told how his own father's curse came true: Amyntor called on the Erinyes, “and the gods fulfilled his curses, / Zeus of the underworld and dread Persephone” (9.456–457), the two powers to whom Althaea prays (see [[the-flight-of-phoenix]], [[erinyes]], [[persephone]] and [[curses]]). The Catalogue had already said that “fair-haired Meleager was dead” (2.642). Pausanias took these lines as Homer's account of the death: Homer says, he writes, that the Erinys heard Althaea's curses and that Meleager died for this cause (10.31.3).

Phoenix's lesson is the gifts that Meleager lost by waiting, and he stops there. The death that the curse implies lies outside the lesson, but a hearer who knows it may find that the story says more than Phoenix means: Achilles too will go back to the war, and his own death is to follow soon after Hector's (18.95–96; see [[death-of-achilles-foretold]], [[the-wrath-of-meleager]] and [[althaea]]).
