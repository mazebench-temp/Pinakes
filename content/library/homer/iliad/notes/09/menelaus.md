---
summary: Menelaus is not named in Book 9. He is present only as one of “the sons of Atreus” in Achilles' question whether they alone love their wives, and as the husband of the Helen for whom the army was gathered (9.339–341).
refs: [9.339-340]
---

Achilles' complaint turns on Menelaus' cause. The army was led to Troy “for the sake of Helen of the lovely hair”, and if the sons of Atreus love their wives, so does any man who is good and sensible (9.339–343; see [[atreidae]], [[helen]] and [[the-abduction-of-helen]]). Helen is named twice more in the book, as the measure of beauty for the twenty Trojan women Achilles may choose, “the most beautiful after Argive Helen” (9.140, 9.282; see [[argive-helen]]). In the same night Menelaus himself cannot sleep for fear for the Argives, “who for his sake had come over the wide waters to Troy” (10.25–28).
