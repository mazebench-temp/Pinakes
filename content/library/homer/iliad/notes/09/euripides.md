---
summary: Euripides wrote lost tragedies on two stories that Book 9 tells: his Phoenix made Achilles' tutor a son wronged by his father and blinded, and his Meleager put on stage the hunt of the Calydonian boar, with Atalanta among the hunters. In his Orestes Agamemnon's three daughters are Chrysothemis, Iphigenia and Electra.
---

- **Phoenix.** The fragments include complaints about an old man who takes a young wife (frr. 804, 807 Nauck), a judge who weighs conflicting testimony by looking to a man's nature and the company he keeps (fr. 812), a speaker who once mocked the blind as cowards for clinging to life and has now fallen into their plight himself (fr. 816), and a farewell to the land of his fathers (fr. 817). The later story, in which Amyntor blinds his son on his concubine's false charge, is told under [[phoenix]]; Homer's Phoenix did lie with the girl, at his mother's urging (9.449–453; see [[the-flight-of-phoenix]]).
- **Meleager.** One fragment sets the scene at Calydon, where Oeneus, son of Porthaon, rules, husband of Althaea, daughter of Thestius (fr. 515); another tells of Oeneus offering the first fruits of a rich harvest (fr. 516), the offering in which, in Homer, he forgot Artemis (9.533–537). A messenger describes the hunters of the boar: Telamon with a golden eagle on his shield, Atalanta, “hateful to Cypris”, with hounds and bow, Ancaeus with a double axe, and the sons of Thestius with the left foot bare (fr. 530). Homer's version has no Atalanta: see [[the-wrath-of-meleager]] and [[the-calydonian-boar]].
- **Agamemnon's daughters.** In the *Orestes* Electra counts the daughters of Agamemnon as three, “Chrysothemis and Iphigenia and I, Electra”, with one son, Orestes (*Orestes* 22–24). Homer's three are Chrysothemis, Laodice and Iphianassa (9.145, 9.287): see [[chrysothemis]], [[iphianassa]] and [[orestes]].
