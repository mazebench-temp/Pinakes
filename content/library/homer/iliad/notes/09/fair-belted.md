---
summary: Achilles will take home “fair-belted women” among his spoil; Meleager's “fair-belted wife” Cleopatra moves him to fight; and “fair-belted Iphis” lies beside Patroclus.
grc: [ἐϋζώνους, ἐΰζωνος]
---

Book 9 gives the epithet three times. Achilles counts among the wealth he will take home “fair-belted women” (9.366; [[red]], [[gray-sea]], [[captive-women]]). In Phoenix's story, when all others have failed, “then at last his fair-belted wife begged Meleager” (9.590; [[cleopatra]], [[deep-girdled]]). And at the end of the night Patroclus lies down, “and beside him too lay fair-belted Iphis, whom brilliant Achilles gave him when he took steep Scyros” (9.666–668; [[iphis]], [[scyros]]). Two of the three are captives; the third, a wife, wins Meleager over by describing what happens to women when a city falls (9.591–594).
