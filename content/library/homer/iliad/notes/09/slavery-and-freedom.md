summary: Cleopatra's plea to Meleager gives the Iliad's plainest list of what befalls a captured city, the men killed, the city burned, the women and children led away. The offer to Achilles disposes of women won in raids and of the women of a city not yet taken, and slave women spread the beds in his hut and kept watch on the young Phoenix.
refs: [9.477, 9.591-594, 9.658]
passages:
  - 9.588-596 | Meleager's wife moves him to fight
---

**The taken city.** Meleager refuses elders, priests, father, mother, sisters and friends, and yields only when his wife, weeping, recounts to him “all the sorrows that come to men whose city is taken: they kill the men, and fire levels the city, and strangers lead away the children and the deep-girdled women” (9.591–594). The list is told to a man whose own wife will be among the women if he does not fight, and it moves him where gifts did not (see [[the-wrath-of-meleager]] and [[women-and-war]]). Phoenix tells it to a man who has sacked twenty-three cities (9.328–329).

**Persons as goods.** The book's offers count captives with tripods and horses. The seven women of Lesbos were taken when Achilles sacked the island (9.128–130); the twenty Trojan women Achilles may choose when Troy falls are promised before the city is taken (9.139–140; see [[captive-women]]). Achilles calls Briseis δουρικτητή, “won by my spear” (9.343), and his account of what can be had by raiding begins with cattle and sheep (ληϊστοί, 9.406; see [[a-mans-soul-cannot-come-back]]); the same raids brought him Diomede of Lesbos and gave Patroclus Iphis of Scyros (9.664–668).

**Slave women.** The δμῳαί are in the background of both houses the book describes. When Phoenix broke out of his father's house he leapt the fence of the court unseen “by the watchmen and the slave women” (9.477), and in Achilles' hut “the slave women” spread Phoenix's bed (δμωῇσι, 9.658; see [[handmaids]]).
