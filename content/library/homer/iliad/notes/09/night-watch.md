**The Trojan watch.** “So the Trojans kept their watch” (9.1): the first words of the book take up the end of Book 8, where a thousand fires burn on the plain all night (8.553–565). Nestor warns the assembly that “the enemy close by the ships are burning many fires” (9.76–77), and Odysseus tells Achilles the same (9.232–234).

**Nestor's order.** Nestor proposes supper and sentries in the verses with which Hector gave his own orders at sunset: “But now let us yield to black night and make ready our supper” (9.65–66 ≈ 8.502–503; see [[yield-to-night]]). The sentries, φυλακτῆρες, are to “spend the night beside the ditch they have dug, outside the wall”, and the task is laid on the young men (9.66–68; see [[the-achaean-wall]]).

**The sentries go out.**

{{quote:7ea2fcf3-5669-5008-bf20-e038ab044b53}}

Seven captains, “leaders of the sentries” (ἡγεμόνες φυλάκων), each with a hundred young men, make seven hundred spearmen. The captains are Nestor's son Thrasymedes, the sons of Ares Ascalaphus and Ialmenus, Meriones, and three men the poem has not named before, Aphareus, Deipyrus and Lycomedes, son of Creon (9.81–84; see [[thrasymedes]], [[ascalaphus]], [[ialmenus]], [[meriones]], [[aphareus]], [[deipyrus]] and [[lycomedes]]). Ascalaphus, Aphareus and Deipyrus will all be killed in Book 13 (13.518–520, 13.541–544, 13.576–580).

**The watch in the night.** The watch goes on into Book 10. Agamemnon names Nestor's son and Meriones as its leaders (10.56–59), and Nestor and the kings find the guards awake in their armor, like dogs keeping watch over sheep when they hear a wild beast in the woods (10.180–193). In the last book the same scene appears at its homeliest: when Hermes brings Priam to the ships, the sentries are “busy about their supper”, and he puts them all to sleep (24.443–445).

**A watch in Phoenix's story.** Phoenix tells Achilles how his kinsmen kept him at home for nine nights after his father's curse: “they kept watch by turns, and the fire never went out”, one fire in the court and one in the porch, until on the tenth night he broke out “unseen by the watchmen and the slave women” (9.470–477; see [[phoenix]]). φυλακὰς ἔχον, “kept watch”, are the words of the book's first line (9.1, 9.471).
