---
summary: With the North Wind, the West Wind blows “from Thrace” in the simile for the Achaeans' despair (9.4–8).
refs: [9.4, 9.6]
---

Zephyrus is one of the “two winds” that stir up the fish-filled sea in the simile that opens the book (9.4–8; see [[two-winds-from-thrace]] and the note on [[boreas]]). Here, as in the similes of Books 2, 4 and 7, the West Wind of the Iliad is a wind that sets things moving, not the gentle breeze of later poetry. The same pair blows on the pyre of Patroclus, when Iris finds the winds feasting in the house of Zephyrus, δυσαής, “ill-blowing” (23.200), and in her message from Achilles he is κελαδεινός, “clamorous” (23.208). That the West Wind should blow “from Thrace” (9.5) has seemed odd, since Thrace lies to the north; seen from the Troad it lies north and northwest across the sea (see the translation's note on 9.4–7).
