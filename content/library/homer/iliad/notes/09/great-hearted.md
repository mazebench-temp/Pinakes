summary: μεγαλήτωρ describes the heart itself for the first time, θυμὸς μεγαλήτωρ, “the proud heart,” four times in Book 9, always of a heart that will not give way; μεγάθυμος is given to the Aetolians.
grc: [μεγαλήτορα]
en: [proud]
---

**The proud heart.** In Books 1–8 μεγαλήτωρ describes men and peoples: Erechtheus, Oeneus, Anchises, Odysseus, Priam, Eetion, the Trojans. In Book 9 it describes the heart, and the translation renders the phrase “proud heart.” Nestor tells Agamemnon that he took Briseis “giving way to your proud heart” (9.109). Odysseus recalls Peleus' advice to Achilles: “hold back your proud heart in your breast; for kindliness is better” (9.255–256). Ajax says that Achilles “has made savage the proud heart in his breast” (9.629; [[hard]]), and Agamemnon asks, “does anger still hold his proud heart?” (9.675; [[cholos]]). The great heart is the one that will not yield, Agamemnon's first and then Achilles' ([[thymos]], [[proud]]).

**The Aetolians.** In Phoenix's story the din over the boar's head and hide rises “between the Curetes and the great-hearted Aetolians” (9.549; [[aetolians]], [[curetes]]).
