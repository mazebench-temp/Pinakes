---
summary: Nestor begins his answer to Diomedes with the patronymic, “Son of Tydeus,” and praises him as the best in counsel of the men of his age.
---

“Son of Tydeus, you are mighty in war above all others, and in counsel you are the best among all men of your age” (9.53–54). Nestor addresses the young man by his father's name, which in Book 4 Agamemnon had used to shame him ([[tydeus]], [[tydeus-at-thebes]]). Here the name opens praise. Nestor adds a correction: Diomedes has spoken well but has not reached the end of the matter, and Nestor, old enough to be his father, will go through it all (9.56–61; [[nestor]]). See [[diomedes]].
