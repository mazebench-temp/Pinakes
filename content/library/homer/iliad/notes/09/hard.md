---
summary: Agamemnon calls Zeus “hard god” as he proposes flight in earnest, and Ajax calls Achilles “hard man” as he gives up on persuading him.
---

The word of reproach frames the night. Agamemnon repeats the complaint of Book 2, now in earnest: Zeus, “hard god, who once promised me and nodded his assent that I would sack well-walled Ilios and then sail home,” has deceived him (9.19–22; [[well-walled]]). At the end of the embassy Ajax uses it of Achilles, speaking of him to Odysseus as if he were not there: Achilles has made savage the proud heart in his breast, “hard man, and does not regard the friendship of his companions” (9.628–630; [[pitiless]], [[great-hearted]]). The god who will not keep his promise and the hero who will not be moved receive the same word. See [[agamemnon]] and [[achilles]].
