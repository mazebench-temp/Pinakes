The war is fought “around the city of Calydon”, the Aetolians defending it and the Curetes trying to sack it (9.530–532; see [[aetolians]] and [[curetes]]). Both times Phoenix names it in the genitive it is Καλυδῶνος ἐραννῆς, “lovely Calydon” (9.531, 9.577). In the Catalogue it is “rocky” (2.640; see [[rocky]]), and in later books “steep” (13.217, 14.116; see [[steep]]). The story shows a walled city under siege: the Curetes cannot stand outside the wall while Meleager fights (9.551–552), and when he holds back, the din rises about the gates, the towers are battered, and at last the enemy climb the towers and set fire to the city (9.573–574, 9.588–589). In the likeness Phoenix draws, Calydon answers to the Achaean camp, its wall threatened and its ships in danger of fire (9.347–352, 9.602; see [[the-achaean-wall]]).

The land is rich. The elders offer Meleager a domain “where the plain of lovely Calydon was richest”, fifty acres, half vineyard and half plowland, to be cut from the plain (9.577–580). Such a τέμενος, land set apart for a king or a hero, is the reward the Lycians gave Bellerophon, “of orchard and plowland” (6.194–195), the domain Sarpedon and Glaucus hold beside the Xanthus (12.313–314), and the one Achilles mocks Aeneas for hoping to win by killing him (20.184–186). The vineyard suits the house of Oeneus, whose name is wine (see [[oeneus]] and [[the-wrath-of-meleager]]).
