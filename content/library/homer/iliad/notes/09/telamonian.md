---
summary: Ajax is “the godlike son of Telamon” as he turns to leave Achilles' hut, and Achilles answers him as “Telamonian Ajax, sprung from Zeus, lord of the people”; in Nestor's choice of envoys he is “great Ajax.”
grc: [Τελαμωνιάδης]
---

**Great Ajax.** Nestor names the envoys: “First of all let Phoenix, dear to Zeus, lead the way, and after him great Ajax and brilliant Odysseus” (9.168–169). The μέγας of the formula μέγας Τελαμώνιος Αἴας (5.610) is here alone, and it is enough to tell this Ajax from the son of Oileus, “the lesser Ajax” (2.527–528; [[dear-to-zeus]]).

**Son of Telamon.** When Achilles has answered Phoenix, “Ajax, the godlike son of Telamon, spoke among them” (9.622–623; [[antitheos]]). The nominative Τελαμωνιάδης is new in Book 9; Book 8 had the genitive (8.224, 8.267). Achilles answers with the whole address that Hector used of Ajax in Book 7, “Telamonian Ajax, sprung from Zeus, lord of the people” (9.644; 7.234; [[sprung-from-zeus]], [[koiranos]]), and grants that “all that you have said seems to have been spoken after my own heart” (9.645). His answer to Ajax goes furthest of the three, from sailing home at dawn (9.356–363) and deciding at dawn (9.618–619) to staying until Hector reaches his ships (9.650–655).
