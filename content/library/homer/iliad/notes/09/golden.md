“And I will not marry the daughter of Agamemnon, son of Atreus, not if she rivaled golden Aphrodite in beauty and equaled gray-eyed Athena in handiwork” (9.388–390; [[gray-eyed]], [[marriage]]). The two goddesses are the measures of what a bride could be, beauty and skill, and the epithet of Aphrodite's radiance stands for the first. Achilles refuses even the best of brides from this man ([[laodice-daughter-of-agamemnon]], [[chrysothemis]], [[iphianassa]]). See [[aphrodite]].
