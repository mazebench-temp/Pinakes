summary: The army that Agamemnon's quarrel has cost so many men sits silent at his proposal to flee and shouts for Diomedes' refusal. Nestor turns the night to use with a watch of seven captains and seven hundred young men, and the embassy pleads for the worn-down army; at the end Diomedes sends the king to lead it at dawn.

**The army in the assembly.** Agamemnon has the heralds call each man to the assembly by name, without shouting (9.10–12; see [[assembly]] and [[heralds]]). He tells the army that Zeus bids him go home “when I have lost so many men” (ἐπεὶ πολὺν ὤλεσα λαόν, 9.22), and proposes flight. The army's answer is silence, “for a long time the sons of the Achaeans were silent in their grief” (9.29–30); when Diomedes refuses, they shout their approval (9.50–51). The leader proposes, and the army judges.

**The watch.** Nestor gives the night its order. The sentries go out “around Thrasymedes, son of Nestor, shepherd of the people”, around Ascalaphus and Ialmenus, Meriones, Aphareus, Deipyrus and Lycomedes: “Seven were the leaders of the sentries, and with each a hundred young men marched” (9.80–88; see [[night-watch]], [[thrasymedes]] and [[lycomedes]]). The young men keep the watch, and the elders feast and take counsel in the king's hut (9.68–75; see [[elders]]).

**The army in the huts.** In the council and in Achilles' hut the leaders speak for the λαός. Agamemnon admits that Zeus has beaten down “the army of the Achaeans” to honor Achilles (9.118); Odysseus asks Achilles to pity the Achaeans “worn down throughout the army” (9.301–302). Achilles asks, “Why did he gather the army and lead it here, the son of Atreus?” (9.338–339), and sends the envoys back to the chiefs to find a better way to save “their ships and the army of the Achaeans” (9.424–425; see [[laos]]).

**At dawn.** Diomedes ends the night with a soldier's orders: eat, sleep, and at dawn “draw up the army and the horses before the ships”, and let the king fight among the foremost (9.705–709). The kings approve (9.710).
