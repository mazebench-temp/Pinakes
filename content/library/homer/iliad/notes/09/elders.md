summary: The council of elders meets at Agamemnon's table on the night of the embassy. Nestor proposes the feast, the elders hear his counsel and the king's offer, send the envoys and wait in the hut for the answer (9.70, 9.89–181, 9.669–713); Achilles calls carrying his message “the prize of the old” (9.422).

**The feast.** In the assembly Nestor tells Agamemnon to “give a feast for the elders; it is fitting for you, and not unseemly”, and to follow whoever gives the best counsel (9.70–75). The council meets apart from the army, as in Book 2 (2.53–55), and its seat is the king's table: “the son of Atreus led the elders of the Achaeans all together to his hut, and set before them a satisfying feast” (9.89–90; see [[feast]] and [[agamemnon]]). Book 4 had spoken of the elders' wine as a privilege that binds them to fight in front (4.259–263, 4.343–346).

**The council.** The poem does not list the elders. Those present include Nestor, who speaks first, Odysseus and Ajax, who are chosen as envoys, Phoenix, and Diomedes, who has the last word (9.93–95, 9.168–169, 9.696). The elders approve Nestor's choice of envoys, pour libations and send them off (9.173–178; see [[boule]], [[libation]] and [[embassy]]). When the envoys return, the “sons of the Achaeans” rise in the hut to pledge them with golden cups, and after Odysseus' report they sit in silence until Diomedes speaks, and the kings approve his counsel (9.669–671, 9.693–711).

**The prize of the old.** Achilles sends the envoys back to the chiefs with his answer, “for that is the prize of the old” (τὸ γὰρ γέρας ἐστὶ γερόντων, 9.422; see [[prize-of-the-old]] and [[geras]]). The half-line is the one with which Nestor claimed counsel as the privilege of age (4.323), and the elders are now to think of a better plan than the one Achilles has refused (9.423–426). Thetis remembers the embassy as an embassy of elders, “the elders of the Argives begged him” (18.448), with the half-verse that brings the Aetolian elders to Meleager in Phoenix's story, “and the elders begged him” (τὸν δὲ λίσσοντο γέροντες, 9.574; see [[the-wrath-of-meleager]]).

Elsewhere in the book γέροντες are the old in general: the Argives, “both young and old”, know of Diomedes' disgrace and are to honor Achilles (9.36, 9.258).
