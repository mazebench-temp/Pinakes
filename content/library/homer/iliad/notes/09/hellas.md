---
summary: Hellas is named three times in Book 9, always beside Phthia. Achilles says there are many Achaean women “throughout Hellas and Phthia”, and Phoenix tells how he left “Hellas of the lovely women”, fleeing his father, and fled “through Hellas with its wide dancing floors” to Peleus in Phthia.
grc: [Ἑλλάδος]
---

Achilles names Hellas when he refuses Agamemnon's daughter: Peleus will find him a wife among the “daughters of chieftains who guard the citadels” in Hellas and Phthia (9.394–397; see [[phthia]] and [[marriage]]). For Phoenix, Hellas is the land he came from. He left it “of the lovely women” (καλλιγύναικα, 9.447; see [[of-the-lovely-women]]), the epithet of the Catalogue (2.683), and fled “through Hellas with its wide dancing floors” (διʼ Ἑλλάδος εὐρυχόροιο, 9.478; see [[with-wide-dancing-floors]]) to Phthia, where Peleus received him (see [[the-flight-of-phoenix]]). Hellas and Phthia are neighbors here and not the same land.

In antiquity Demetrius of Scepsis, who made Phoenix a native of Ormenium in Thessaly, read Ὀρμένιον πολύμηλον, “Ormenium rich in flocks”, for Ἑλλάδα καλλιγύναικα at 9.447 (Strabo 9.5.18; see [[hellas-or-ormenium]]).
