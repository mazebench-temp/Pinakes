When Odysseus has told Achilles of the danger to the ships, he reminds him of what his father said on the day he sent him to Troy:

{{quote:4fc6a8eb-4665-57e8-8cf9-e26fb24f762c}}

Odysseus quotes the father against the son (see [[peleus]] and [[odysseus]]). Peleus names the two goddesses who favor the Achaeans, and they had already held Achilles back once. When he drew his sword on Agamemnon, Athena came down from the sky, “for the white-armed goddess Hera had sent her, / loving both men alike in her heart and caring for them both” (1.195–196; see [[athena]]). Hera has her own reason to care for Peleus' son: she raised Thetis herself and gave her to Peleus as his wife (24.59–61; see [[thetis]]).

The strength Peleus promised comes when Achilles returns to the war, and Hera's part in it is large. She sends Iris to rouse him at the ditch (18.166–168, 18.184) and sends the sun down early to end the fighting (18.239–240). She asks Poseidon and Athena to stand by him and give him great strength, κράτος μέγα, the gift Peleus had named (κάρτος, 9.254; 20.115–121), and she sends Hephaestus against the river that threatens to sweep him away (21.328–331; see [[achilles]]). “If they will” (αἴ κʼ ἐθέλωσι, 9.255) leaves the gift to the goddesses. The advice that follows is the father's own: hold back your proud heart, “for kindliness is better” (9.256; see [[kindliness-is-better]]).
