---
summary: Achilles counts “gray iron” among the wealth he will take home from Troy.
grc: [πολιόν]
---

“And from here I will bring other gold, and red bronze, and fair-belted women, and gray iron, all that I won by lot” (9.365–367; [[red]], [[fair-belted]], [[iron]]). The verse-end πολιόν τε σίδηρον, the color of worked iron, returns in the prizes of the funeral games (23.261) and in the Odyssey, of the iron axes of the contest of the bow (Od. 21.3, 21.81, 24.168). It is the second sense of the word in the poem, after the gray sea of Book 1 (1.350, 1.359). See [[gold-and-silver]].
