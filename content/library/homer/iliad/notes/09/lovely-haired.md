summary: Achilles asks whether the war was not fought “for the sake of Helen of the lovely hair,” and Phoenix's father quarrels with him over his “fair-haired concubine,” in a synonym of the epithet.
grc: [καλλικόμοιο]
en: [fair-haired]
---

**Helen.** “Why did he gather the army and lead it here, the son of Atreus? Was it not for the sake of Helen of the lovely hair?” (9.338–339; [[helen]], [[son-of-atreus]]). Achilles uses Helen's epithet to set Agamemnon's war for a wife beside his own loss of Briseis ([[thymares]]).

**The concubine.** Phoenix fled from his father Amyntor, “who was enraged at me over his fair-haired concubine, whom he himself loved” (9.448–450; [[amyntor]], [[the-flight-of-phoenix]]). The word is καλλίκομος, from καλός (“beautiful”) and κόμη (“hair”), a synonym of ἠΰκομος; the Odyssey gives it to Helen (Od. 15.58) and Hesiod to Mnemosyne (*Theogony* 915). The translation renders it “fair-haired.” As in Achilles' quarrel, a woman stands at the center of a quarrel between two men of one house.
