---
summary: The Argives are named in Book 9 as the army in assembly and as the people whose regard a hero earns or loses: Agamemnon weeps before the Argives, Peleus told Achilles to cease from strife so that the Argives would honor him, and Achilles asks why the Argives must fight the Trojans at all (9.16, 9.258, 9.338).
---

Agamemnon speaks “among the Argives” and addresses their “leaders and rulers” (9.16–17), and Diomedes reminds him that “the Argives know, both young and old” how he once reproached him (9.36). Nestor praises Diomedes for speaking wisely “to the kings of the Argives” (9.59). In Achilles' hut the name measures honor: Peleus told his son to hold back his proud heart and cease from strife, “so that all the more the Argives may honor you, both young and old” (9.257–258; see [[peleus]]), and Achilles remembers how the king treated him with contempt “among the Argives” (9.647). Achilles asks why the Argives must fight the Trojans, if not for Helen (9.337–339; see [[atreidae]]). Phoenix will not ask him to defend the Argives unless gifts are offered, and reminds him that the envoys are the dearest to him “of the Argives” (9.517–522), and Achilles foresees Hector reaching his own huts “killing the Argives” (9.653). Odysseus reports that Agamemnon must take counsel “among the Argives” how to save the ships (9.680).
