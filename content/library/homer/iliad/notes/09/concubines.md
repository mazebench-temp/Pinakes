summary: The word παλλακίς comes only in Phoenix's account of his father's concubine (9.449, 9.452). The captive women of the book are offered as gifts, sworn about and slept beside: seven women of Lesbos and twenty Trojan women in the offer, Briseis untouched under oath, and Diomede and Iphis in Achilles' hut at the end of the night.
passages:
  - 9.447-457 | Phoenix and his father's concubine
---

**Phoenix's father.** Phoenix tells how his father loved a concubine and dishonored his wife, how his mother begged him to lie with the woman first, “so that she would hate the old man”, and how his father cursed him with childlessness (9.447–457; see [[curses]] and [[the-flight-of-phoenix]]). It is the story of a quarrel over a woman, told to Achilles on the night of his own.

**Captives.** Agamemnon offers seven women of Lesbos, chosen from the spoil of Achilles' own conquest, and, when Troy falls, twenty Trojan women, “the most beautiful after Argive Helen”, and he will swear that he never went up into Briseis' bed (9.128–140; see [[captive-women]] and [[oath]]). Achilles says that he loved Briseis from his heart, “though she was won by my spear”, and calls her his wife (9.336–343; see [[briseis]] and [[marriage]]). The night ends with Achilles and Patroclus asleep beside the women they took at Lesbos and Scyros, Diomede, daughter of Phorbas, and Iphis (9.663–668; see [[diomede]], [[iphis]] and [[bed]]).
