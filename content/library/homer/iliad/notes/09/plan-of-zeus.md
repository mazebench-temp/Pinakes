---
summary: In Book 9 the men who suffer the plan of Zeus name it. Agamemnon reads it rightly at last, that Zeus has honored Achilles and beaten down the Achaeans; Achilles thinks it means that Troy will not fall; and his last answer to the embassy sets the term that Zeus foretold, that Hector will not stop until Achilles is roused beside the ships.
passages:
  - 9.115-120 | Agamemnon admits his blind folly
  - 9.417-420 | Zeus has held his hand over Troy
  - 9.650-655 | Until Hector reaches the ships
---

**Named.** Agamemnon begins the night with the words he spoke as a pretense in Book 2: Zeus once promised him the sack of Ilios, “but now he has devised an evil deceit” (9.19–21 = 2.112–114; see [[the-test-of-the-army]]). In the council he reads the plan rightly: “Worth many people is the man whom Zeus loves in his heart, as now he has honored this man and beaten down the army of the Achaeans” (9.116–118). ἔτισε, δάμασσε, “honored” and “beat down”, are the two halves of the plan as the narrator stated it at the start of Book 2, that Zeus would honor Achilles and destroy many beside the ships (2.3–4), and of what Thetis asked, strength for the Trojans until the Achaeans honor her son (1.508–510). Agamemnon does not know of Thetis' request. He reads the plan from the defeat (see [[the-man-whom-zeus-loves]], [[zeus]] and [[longing-for-achilles]]).

**Misread.** Achilles reads the same signs the other way. He advises the army to sail home, “since you will no longer find the end of steep Ilios; for far-seeing Zeus has held his hand over it” (9.418–420), and Odysseus carries the words back to Agamemnon (9.685–687). Zeus has done so only for a time. In Book 15 he tells Hera that after Hector's death he will turn the rout back from the ships until the Achaeans take Ilios “through the counsels of Athena” (15.68–71).

**The term.** At nightfall in Book 8 Zeus told Hera how long the Trojan victory would last: Hector “will not cease from war until the swift-footed son of Peleus is roused beside the ships” (8.473–474). The embassy cannot change that term. Achilles' last answer sets the same one from his side: he will not think of war until Hector comes to the huts and ships of the Myrmidons, killing the Argives and burning the ships (9.650–655; see [[the-embassy-to-achilles]]). Diomedes leaves the rest to the gods: Achilles will fight “when the heart in his breast bids him and a god rouses him” (9.702–703). In Book 18 Hera sends Iris to rouse him (18.165–180).
