---
summary: Achilles longs to marry “a wedded wife” in Phthia, and Meleager in his anger lies “beside his wedded wife, the beautiful Cleopatra.”
grc: [μνηστὴν, μνηστῇ]
---

Achilles answers the offer of Agamemnon's daughter with a marriage of his own: “there my proud spirit was very eager to marry a wedded wife, a fitting mate, and enjoy the possessions that the old man Peleus acquired” (9.398–400; [[proud]], [[marriage]]). The wedded wife, won by courtship, is set against the bride offered “without bride-price” as part of a settlement (9.146). In Phoenix's story Meleager, angry with his mother, “lay beside his wedded wife, the beautiful Cleopatra” (9.556; [[cleopatra]]), and it is she who at last persuades him to fight (9.590–596).
