summary: The lyre Achilles is playing when the envoys find him is a spoil of Thebe: “he had taken it from the spoils when he destroyed the city of Eetion” (9.188).
refs: [9.188]
passages:
  - 9.185-191 | Achilles and the lyre
---

When the envoys come to his hut, Achilles is delighting his heart with a clear-toned lyre, beautiful and finely wrought, with a crossbar of silver, and he is singing κλέα ἀνδρῶν, “the famous deeds of men” (9.186–189; see [[lyre]] and [[song-and-fame]]). The narrator stops to say where the lyre came from: Achilles took it from the spoils of Eetion's city (9.188; see [[eetion]]). Andromache told the sack from inside the walls. Achilles killed her father and her seven brothers in a single day, and took her mother captive and freed her for a ransom (6.414–428). The instrument of his song came out of that plunder. He tells the envoys that he has sacked twenty-three cities (9.328–329; see [[achilles-raids]]), and the lyre is a token of one of them. The things of Thebe go with him through the poem: the lyre in his hut, the trace horse Pedasus (16.152–154), and the lump of iron that Eetion used to throw, which Achilles sets as a prize at the funeral games (23.826–829).
