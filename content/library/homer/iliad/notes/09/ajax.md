Achilles grants that he has spoken after his own heart, and answers that he will not fight until Hector reaches the Myrmidons' ships.

**The second envoy.** Nestor names “great Ajax” after Phoenix and before Odysseus (9.169). In Achilles' hut, when the meal is over, “Ajax nodded to Phoenix” (9.223), as if to bid the old man begin, but Odysseus saw the nod and spoke first (see [[phoenix]] and [[odysseus]]). Ajax says nothing through the long speeches of Odysseus, Achilles and Phoenix.

**The appeal.** When Achilles has told Phoenix to stay, “Ajax, the godlike son of Telamon”, speaks (9.622–623; see [[telamon]]), and he speaks first to Odysseus: “let us go”, for nothing will come of their errand, and the Danaans are waiting for the word, “though it is not good” (9.624–628). Of Achilles he speaks in the third person, as of a man past hearing: Achilles “has made savage the proud heart in his breast, hard man, and does not regard the friendship of his companions” (9.628–630; see [[philotes]]). Then he turns to Achilles himself:

{{quote:3db83441-e9c1-5c1e-ad29-877877e2235f}}

The comparison comes from the law of the community. If a man takes a price from the killer of his brother or his son, Achilles can take one for a woman (see [[poine]]). The gods have given him an unrelenting heart “for the sake of a girl, one girl; and now we offer you seven, by far the best” (9.637–638). Ajax ends with the claims of the roof and of friendship: “respect your own roof; we are here beneath your roof”, and they want to be “the nearest and dearest to you, of all the Achaeans” (9.640–642; see [[xenia]] and [[aidos]]). His appeal is the shortest of the three, nineteen verses against Odysseus' eighty-two and Phoenix's hundred and seventy-two.

**Achilles' answer.**

{{quote:fb3ff388-449c-5976-94ef-61450f7f87e7}}

Achilles addresses him with the verse that Hector used before their duel (9.644 = 7.234; see [[telamonian]]), and he will say the line of the “dishonored vagrant” again to Patroclus (16.59; see [[like-a-dishonored-vagrant]]). Of the three envoys only Ajax is told that he has spoken after Achilles' own heart, and only he hears Achilles' last position: Achilles will not think of war “until the son of wise-hearted Priam, brilliant Hector, comes to the huts and ships of the Myrmidons” (9.650–652). To Odysseus he said that he would sail at dawn, and to Phoenix that they would consider it at dawn; to Ajax he says that he will stay (see [[achilles]]). In Agamemnon's hut Odysseus names Ajax first among the witnesses, “Ajax and the two heralds, both wise men” (9.689), but reports only the first answer (see [[the-embassy-to-achilles]]).
