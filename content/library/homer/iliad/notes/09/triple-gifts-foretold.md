**The promise kept.** Athena held Achilles back from killing Agamemnon with a promise: “someday three times as many splendid gifts will be laid before you because of this outrage” (1.213–214). In Book 9 the gifts are offered. Agamemnon names them before the elders, and Odysseus repeats them to Achilles (9.121–157, 9.260–299; see [[the-gifts-of-agamemnon]] and [[message-repeated]]). Both use Athena's verb: “All these things shall be at hand at once” (ταῦτα μὲν αὐτίκα πάντα παρέσσεται, 9.135 = 9.277; compare παρέσσεται, 1.213). The offer is far more than three times the prize that was taken. For one girl it gives back Briseis herself with seven women of Lesbos, and adds tripods, gold, cauldrons and horses, a shipload of Troy's spoil and twenty Trojan women, a king's daughter with a dowry, and seven cities (see [[the-seven-cities]]).

**The answer.** Achilles answers with multiples of his own. Not ten times or twenty times as much as Agamemnon has now, nor the wealth of Orchomenus or of Egyptian Thebes, nor as much as the sand and the dust would persuade him (9.379–387; see [[the-sand-and-the-dust]]). Athena called the outrage ὕβρις (1.214), taking up Achilles' own word (1.203), and Achilles still does: Agamemnon took back his prize “in his arrogance” (ἐφυβρίζων, 9.368). He begins his reply with the words of a thing fated to happen, ὡς τετελεσμένον ἔσται, “as it will be accomplished” (9.310), as Athena began her promise (τὸ δὲ καὶ τετελεσμένον ἔσται, 1.212).

**Too soon or too late.** Athena offered compensation in place of revenge, and Achilles accepted it then (1.216–218). In Book 9 the compensation comes before he wants it, and with Agamemnon's demand that he submit (9.158–161; see [[the-embassy-to-achilles]]). Phoenix warns him that if he fights later without the gifts he will not be held in the same honor (9.604–605). In Book 19 the gifts are given after all, laid out in the assembly with Briseis (19.243–248), and Achilles tells Agamemnon to give them or keep them as he likes (19.146–148).
