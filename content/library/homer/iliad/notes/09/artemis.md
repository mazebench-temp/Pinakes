summary: In Phoenix's story of Meleager, Artemis of the golden throne is the cause of the war at Calydon. When Oeneus left her alone out of his harvest offerings, she sent a wild boar against his orchard, and when it was killed she raised the quarrel over its head and hide.

**The forgotten offering.** Phoenix begins his story of the men of old with the goddess:

{{quote:6d19c735-d628-58d8-986e-8e1f1da17bec}}

θαλύσια, the harvest offering of first fruits, occurs only here in Homer; Oeneus should have made it “on the slope of his orchard” (9.534). The other gods feasted on hecatombs, and Artemis alone, “the daughter of great Zeus”, was passed over. “Either he forgot, or did not think of it; and he was greatly blinded in his heart” (9.537). ἀάσατο, “was blinded”, is the verb of Agamemnon's confession (ἀασάμην, 9.116, 9.119), and Phoenix has just told how Blind Folly runs ahead of the Prayers over all the earth (9.502–512; see [[ate]] and [[litai]]). He gives Artemis the formula he gave the Prayers, Διὸς κούρῃ μεγάλοιο (9.536; Διὸς κοῦραι μεγάλοιο, 9.502). In one speech, then, two daughters of Zeus are denied their due: the Prayers by the man who refuses them, and Artemis by Oeneus, who forgot her. A god's anger over a missing offering is the first cause Achilles thinks of in Book 1, when he wants to know of Apollo “whether he finds fault with us for some vow or hecatomb” (1.65), and Poseidon complains that the Achaeans built their wall without giving the gods hecatombs (7.449–450; see [[reciprocity-with-the-gods]] and [[hecatomb]]).

**The boar and the war.** In her anger she “roused an evil” (ὦρσε, 9.533): first the boar (ὦρσεν, 9.539), which tore up the trees of Oeneus' orchard until Meleager gathered huntsmen and dogs from many cities and killed it (9.540–546; see [[the-calydonian-boar]], [[boar]] and [[oeneus]]). Then “she raised around it a great din and shouting / over the boar's head and its shaggy hide” between the Curetes and the Aetolians (9.547–549). So the war in which Meleager withdraws is her doing too, though Phoenix does not say how the quarrel over the spoils arose (see [[the-wrath-of-meleager]], [[meleager]], [[curetes]] and [[aetolians]]). In the verse that tells of her anger she is “the divine child, she of the showering arrows” (δῖον γένος ἰοχέαιρα, 9.538). δῖον γένος is used of no one else in Homer (see [[of-the-showering-arrows]]).

**The golden throne.** χρυσόθρονος, “of the golden throne” (9.533), belongs to Hera in the Iliad (1.611, 14.153, 15.5) and to Dawn in the Odyssey. Artemis has it only here and once in the Odyssey, where she kills Orion (*Odyssey* 5.123; see [[golden-throned]]). The Odyssey also pictures her as a huntress on Taygetus or Erymanthus, “delighting in boars and swift deer” (*Odyssey* 6.102–104). In Phoenix's story the goddess of the hunt sends the beast, and the hunt that kills it begins the war.
