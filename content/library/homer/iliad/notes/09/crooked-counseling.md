---
summary: Diomedes tells Agamemnon that “the son of crooked-counseling Cronus” gave him the scepter but not courage, taking up the verse with which Odysseus praised the one king in Book 2.
---

{{quote:a56833bd-6319-5132-85f5-19c3f754de55}}

The formula Κρόνου πάϊς ἀγκυλομήτεω names Zeus as the giver of kingship. In Book 2 Odysseus used it to silence the army: let there be one king, “to whom the son of crooked-counseling Cronus has given the scepter and the ordinances” (2.205–206). Diomedes repeats the words and divides the gift: Zeus gave Agamemnon only half, honor for his scepter, but not courage, “and that is the greatest power” (9.37–39; [[alke]], [[kingship-and-authority]]). Nestor answers by restoring the whole: “Zeus has put into your hands the scepter and the ordinances” (9.98–99; [[themis]]). See [[son-of-cronus]] and [[cronus]].
