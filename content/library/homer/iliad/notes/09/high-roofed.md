---
summary: Oeneus stands on the threshold of his son's “high-roofed chamber,” begging Meleager to fight; the word is ὑψηρεφής, a synonym of ὑψόροφος.
grc: [ὑψηρεφέος]
---

“And the old horse-driving Oeneus entreated him again and again, standing on the threshold of the high-roofed chamber, shaking the jointed doors, imploring his son” (9.581–583; [[horse-driving]], [[well-joined]], [[oeneus]]). ὑψηρεφής = ὕψι (“high”) + ἐρέφω (“roof over”): “high-roofed,” the same sense as ὑψόροφος, and the translation renders both alike. Meleager has shut himself in the inner room with his wife (9.556), as Achilles sits in his hut, and the father stands outside its door. Later the chamber itself is struck by missiles as the Curetes climb the towers (9.588–589). See [[house]] and [[the-wrath-of-meleager]].
