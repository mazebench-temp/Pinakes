---
summary: Achilles tells the Achaeans that they will never find the end of “steep Ilios,” and Iphis was taken when Achilles captured “steep Scyros.”
grc: [αἰπεινῆς, "=αἰπεῖαν"]
---

**Ilios.** “Since you will no longer find the end of steep Ilios; for far-seeing Zeus has held his hand over it” (9.418–420; [[far-seeing]]). Odysseus repeats the verses in his report (9.685–687). The city on its height, beyond the Achaeans' reach, has the longer form αἰπεινός, as it has in later books (13.773, 15.215, 15.558, 17.328).

**Scyros.** Patroclus lies beside “fair-belted Iphis, whom brilliant Achilles gave him when he took steep Scyros, the citadel of Enyeus” (9.667–668; [[scyros]], [[enyeus]], [[iphis]]). In the shorter form, αἰπύς, the island town is one of the steep places Achilles sacked. See [[troy]].
