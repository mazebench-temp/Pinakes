**Not.** Achilles' refusal of the gifts is built on one word:

{{quote:9169bac7-9b25-53a5-bb68-9544ce6fb669}}

οὐδʼ εἰ … οὐδʼ ὅσʼ … οὐδʼ ὅσα … οὐδʼ εἰ … οὐδέ κεν ὧς: each clause raises the price, from ten and twenty times what Agamemnon has to the wealth of Orchomenus and Egyptian Thebes and at last to the sand and the dust, and the last “not even so” closes the series (see [[possessions]] and [[thebes-in-egypt]]). The refusal of the daughter is built the same way, κούρην δʼ οὐ γαμέω … οὐδʼ εἰ … οὐδέ μιν ὧς γαμέω, “I will not marry … not if … not even so will I marry her” (9.388–391; see [[marriage]]), and so is the weighing of his life: “nothing to me is worth my life, not all that they say Ilios possessed … nor all that is shut within the stone threshold of the Archer” (οὐδʼ ὅσα … οὐδʼ ὅσα, 9.401–404; see [[psyche]]).

**Many.** Phoenix's kinsmen tried to keep him at home, and the attempt is told in four clauses that begin with πολύς, “much, many”: they begged him hard, many sheep and cattle were slaughtered, many hogs were singed, much wine was drunk (9.464–469; see [[feast]]). The appeals to Meleager are counted the same way: πολλὰ δέ μιν λιτάνευε … πολλὰ δὲ τόν γε … πολλὰ δʼ ἑταῖροι, his father “again and again”, his sisters and mother “many times”, “and many times his companions” (9.581–585; see [[supplication-type-scene]]). The repetition makes the refusal heavier with each appeal.
