The cups mark each stage of the embassy. Before it leaves, the young men serve the wine, “first pouring drops for the libation into the cups” (ἐπαρξάμενοι δεπάεσσιν, 9.176 = 1.471; see [[libation]]). In Achilles' hut Patroclus is to “make ready a cup for each” (9.203), and when the meal is over Odysseus opens his speech with one: “filling a cup with wine he pledged Achilles” (δείδεκτʼ, 9.224). When Achilles has answered, “each man took a two-handled cup and poured libation” before going (9.656–657). Back in Agamemnon's huts, “the sons of the Achaeans pledged them with golden cups, standing up, one here, one there, and questioned them” (9.670–671; see [[embassy]]).

δειδίσκομαι and δείκνυμαι, “greet, pledge”, are the verbs of welcome with a cup in the hand. The gods pledge one another in golden cups as they look down on Troy (4.3–4), and spring up to pledge Hera when she comes back to Olympus (15.84–86), as the Achaeans stand up for the envoys. Achilles greets the envoys with the same verb, without a cup, as they stand before him (δεικνύμενος, 9.196). In the Odyssey Peisistratus hands Athena, in Mentor's shape, a golden cup and greets her with it (Od. 3.40–42).
