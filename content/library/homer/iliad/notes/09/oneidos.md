**A reproach remembered.** Diomedes' answer to the king's proposal begins with an old score: “It was my courage you first reproached among the Danaans” (ἀλκὴν μέν μοι πρῶτον ὀνείδισας, 9.34). The reproach was spoken in Book 4, at the review of the army, and Diomedes, “in awe of the rebuke of the king he revered”, said nothing then (4.401–402; see [[praise-and-blame]] and [[alke]]). He returns it in the assembly, where speaking against a king is “the custom” (9.33; see [[themis]]), and he returns it in kind: the king who called him unwarlike and a coward has proposed to run (9.40–46). ὀνειδίζω is to throw a reproach in a man's face; Athena gave Achilles leave to do it with words instead of the sword (ὀνείδισον, 1.211).

**Reproaches feared.** The four bracketed lines in Phoenix's story give the other side of reproach, the fear of it. A god checked his anger by putting in his heart “the talk of the people and the many reproaches of men, so that I might not be called a father-killer among the Achaeans” (ὀνείδεα, 9.460–461; see [[aidos]] and the translation's note on 9.458–461). Reproach is what others will say, and the thought of it can hold a man back from a crime, as the memory of it drives Diomedes to speak.
