---
summary: The Aetolians in Phoenix's story are “steadfast in battle,” μενεχάρμαι, a synonym of the entry's word.
grc: [μενεχάρμαι]
---

“The Curetes and the Aetolians, steadfast in battle, were fighting around the city of Calydon, and slaying one another” (9.529–530; [[aetolians]], [[curetes]], [[calydon]]). μενεχάρμης = μένω (“stand fast”) + χάρμη (“battle, the joy of battle”), formed like μενεπτόλεμος and with the same sense, and the translation renders both “steadfast in battle.” Homer uses it of single warriors later in the poem, of Antilochus above all (13.396, 15.582, 23.419). Here the whole people of Calydon receive it as Phoenix begins his story. See [[the-wrath-of-meleager]].
