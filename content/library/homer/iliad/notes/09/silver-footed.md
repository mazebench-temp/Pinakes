---
summary: Achilles names the source of his knowledge of the two fates: “my mother, the goddess silver-footed Thetis.”
---

{{quote:47e1e08a-eeb7-5938-9759-5a184966aa85}}

“For my mother, the goddess silver-footed Thetis, tells me that two different fates are carrying me toward the end of death” (9.410–411). The epithet stands at the end of the verse, in the nominative, where Book 1 had the whole-line formula of Hera's view of Thetis (1.538, 1.556). Here the goddess is named by her son as the one who knows his fate. The two fates are offered as a choice only here; elsewhere Achilles' life is simply short ([[the-two-fates-of-achilles]], [[imperishable]], [[thetis]]).
