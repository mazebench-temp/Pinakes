---
summary: Achilles names the wealth of Orchomenus with that of Egyptian Thebes: not all that comes in to either city would persuade him (9.381–382).
---

“Not all that comes in to Orchomenus, nor all that comes to Thebes in Egypt, where the most treasures lie stored in the houses” (9.381–382). ποτινίσεται, “comes in”, found only here, is the revenue that flows into the city. This is the Minyan Orchomenus of Boeotia (2.511), not the Arcadian town of the Catalogue (2.605; see [[orchomenus-arcadia]]). With Egyptian Thebes it is a byword for a wealth no gift could match (see [[thebes-in-egypt]] and [[the-gifts-of-agamemnon]]). In the Odyssey Amphion once ruled “with might in Minyan Orchomenus” (Od. 11.284), and Agamemnon's shade asks whether his son is alive “in Orchomenus, or in sandy Pylos” (Od. 11.459; see [[orestes]]).
