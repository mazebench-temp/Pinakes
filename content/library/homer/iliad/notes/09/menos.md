---
summary: Odysseus reports that Achilles, far from quenching his anger, “is filled still more with fury” (μένεος), the words the poem used of Agamemnon's rage in the first quarrel. Diomedes ends the night by saying that food and wine give μένος and courage.
passages:
  - 9.677-679 | Odysseus reports the refusal
---

**Filled with fury.** Asked whether Achilles will help, Odysseus answers: “that man will not quench his anger, but is filled still more with fury, and rejects you and your gifts” (πιμπλάνεται μένεος, 9.678–679). Anger is a fire not put out, and μένος fills him as a liquid fills a vessel. The image is the one the narrator used of Agamemnon when Calchas named the cause of the plague: “his heart, dark on every side, was filled with mighty fury” (μένεος δὲ μέγα φρένες ἀμφιμέλαιναι πίμπλαντʼ, 1.103–104). The king's rage began the quarrel; now Achilles' keeps it going. Odysseus' report may also be his own reading, since the answers Achilles gave Phoenix and Ajax were softer than the first one, which is all he reports (see [[message-repeated]] and [[cholos]]).

**Might from food.** In his last speech Diomedes tells the kings to sleep once they have satisfied their hearts “with food and wine; for in that is might and courage” (μένος … καὶ ἀλκή, 9.705–706; see [[food-and-wine-are-might-and-courage]] and [[alke]]). μένος here is the plain strength of a fed body, the same word that names the surge a god breathes into a fighter (see the article above). Odysseus repeats the half-line in Book 19 (19.161), adding that a god breathes μένος into both sides once the armies meet (19.159).
