---
summary: The formula of home runs through the night: in Agamemnon's proposal to flee and Diomedes' scorn of it, in Achilles' second fate, and in his invitation to Phoenix to sail home with him.
grc: ["φίλην ἐς πατρίδʼ"]
---

**Flight.** Agamemnon's proposal ends with the verse of Book 2: “let us flee with our ships to our dear native land” (9.27 = 2.140). Diomedes throws it back: let the others flee with their ships to their dear native land, if they wish; he and Sthenelus will fight on (9.46–49; [[sthenelus]]).

**The second fate.** Achilles uses the formula for the life he could choose: “if I come home to my dear native land, my noble fame is lost, but my life will be long” (9.414–415; [[imperishable]], [[the-two-fates-of-achilles]]). Phoenix, he says, may stay the night, “so that he may follow me in the ships to our dear native land tomorrow, if he wishes” (9.428–429), and Odysseus reports the words (9.691–692). In these two verses the phrase is shortened by elision, φίλην ἐς πατρίδʼ ἕπηται. The man who will one day say that he is “not going home to my dear native land” (18.101) here makes it the goal of his voyage. See [[nostos]].
