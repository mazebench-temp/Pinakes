---
summary: The only hecatombs of Book 9 are the gods' meal in Phoenix's story: “the other gods feasted on hecatombs”, but Oeneus forgot Artemis, and she sent the boar (9.535–536).
---

“The other gods feasted on hecatombs, but to her alone, the daughter of great Zeus, he made no sacrifice” (ἄλλοι δὲ θεοὶ δαίνυνθʼ ἑκατόμβας, 9.535–536). The hecatomb is the gods' feast: the verb is the one used of men at table (see [[feast]]). Zeus goes with all the gods to feast among the Ethiopians (1.423–424), Iris hurries back to the Ethiopians, who are offering hecatombs to the immortals, “so that I too may share in the sacred feast” (23.205–207), and Poseidon sits feasting at a hecatomb among them in the first book of the Odyssey (Od. 1.25–26). Oeneus' sacrifice was a feast for every god but one, and the goddess left out of it is the one who sends the boar (see [[sacrifice]], [[oeneus]] and [[artemis]]).
