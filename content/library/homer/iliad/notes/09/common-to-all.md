---
summary: Peleus sent Achilles to Troy as a child “who knew nothing yet of war, common to all, nor of assemblies.”
grc: [ὁμοιΐου]
---

“The old horse-driving Peleus sent me with you on that day when he sent you from Phthia to Agamemnon, a child, who knew nothing yet of war, common to all, nor of assemblies, where men become distinguished” (9.438–441; [[horse-driving]], [[preeminent]], [[nepios]]). This is the first use in the poem of the line-end formula ὁμοιΐου πολέμοιο, which returns five times in the Iliad and twice in the Odyssey (13.358, 13.635, 15.670, 18.242, 21.294; Od. 18.264, 24.543). Phoenix's two schools are war and the assembly, and he was sent to teach both: “to be a speaker of words and a doer of deeds” (9.443; [[speaker-of-words-doer-of-deeds]], [[peleus-sends-achilles-to-troy]]).
