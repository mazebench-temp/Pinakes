summary: Achilles' lyre is “a beautiful one, finely wrought,” with a crossbar of silver, taken from the spoils of Eetion's city.
grc: [δαιδαλέῃ]
en: [finely wrought]
---

“And found him delighting his heart with a clear-toned lyre, a beautiful one, finely wrought, and on it was a crossbar of silver; he had taken it from the spoils when he destroyed the city of Eetion” (9.186–188; [[clear-toned]], [[lyre]], [[eetion]]). The adjective δαιδάλεος, of the family of δαίδαλον, “a work of art,” praises the craft of the instrument, as πολυδαίδαλος praises the craft of armor. The lyre is spoil from Thebe, the city whose king was Andromache's father, and Achilles plays it to sing the famous deeds of men ([[sack-of-thebe]], [[kleos]]). See [[achilles]].
