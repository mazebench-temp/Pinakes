---
summary: Pylos marks the place of the seven cities that Agamemnon offers Achilles: “All lie near the sea, on the borders of sandy Pylos” (9.153, 9.295).
---

In νέαται Πύλου ἠμαθόεντος, “on the borders of sandy Pylos”, the adjective νέατος, “lowest, outermost”, puts the seven towns at the edge of Nestor's kingdom, and “sandy” is the standing epithet of his Pylos (see [[sandy]] and [[the-seven-cities]]). None of the towns appears among the nine that send Nestor his ninety ships in the Catalogue (2.591–602; see [[nestor]]). Strabo took the phrase as “near the sea of Pylos”, a sea that the poet, he says, extends as far as the seven cities (8.3.22; see [[strabo]]). The poem does not say how Agamemnon can give towns on the borders of Nestor's land, and Nestor, who hears the offer, says only that the gifts are “no longer to be scorned” (9.164).
