---
summary: The formal address “Most glorious son of Atreus, Agamemnon, lord of men” is spoken four times: twice by Nestor, once by Odysseus with the news of the refusal, and once by Diomedes.
---

“Most glorious son of Atreus, Agamemnon, lord of men”: the whole-verse address opens Nestor's two speeches in the council, when he tells Agamemnon that he wronged Achilles (9.96) and when he accepts the gifts and chooses the envoys (9.163). Odysseus uses it to begin his report that Achilles “will not quench his anger” (9.677–679), and Diomedes to begin his answer, “I wish you had never entreated the noble son of Peleus” (9.697–698; [[blameless]]). The superlative of honor introduces, each time, a word the king would rather not hear. See [[son-of-atreus]], [[lord-of-men]] and [[agamemnon]].
