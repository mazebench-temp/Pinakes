---
summary: Agamemnon is “lord of men” as he admits his blind folly to Nestor, as he asks Odysseus for Achilles' answer, and in the whole-verse address of his counselors.
---

The title of the commander of the whole army stands at the moments when he is least in command. “Then Agamemnon, lord of men, spoke to him in turn”: he admits to Nestor, “I was blinded; I do not deny it myself” (9.114–116; [[ate]]). On the envoys' return, “first Agamemnon, lord of men, asked” whether Achilles will defend the ships (9.672; [[much-praised]]). Four speakers address him with the full verse, “Most glorious son of Atreus, Agamemnon, lord of men” (9.96, 9.163, 9.677, 9.697; [[most-glorious]]). In Book 9 Achilles does not give him the title.
