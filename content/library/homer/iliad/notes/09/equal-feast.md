---
summary: Odysseus opens his toast to Achilles with the formula, turned into a guest's compliment to both his hosts (9.225–226); Achilles answers with the other side of the equal share, “the same share for the man who stays behind and for one who fights his hardest” (9.318).
grc: ["δαιτὸς μὲν ἐΐσης"]
en: [lack an equal share of the feast]
---

“We do not lack an equal share of the feast, either in the hut of Agamemnon, son of Atreus, or here now” (δαιτὸς μὲν ἐΐσης οὐκ ἐπιδευεῖς, 9.225–226). Elsewhere the narrator says it of a company, “no one's heart lacked an equal share of the feast” (1.468, 1.602, 2.431, 7.320). Here a guest says it of himself, at the second meal of the night, and the compliment is paid to both his hosts at once, the king who sent him and the man he has come to persuade (see [[feast]] and [[embassy]]).

Achilles' answer turns to the other side of the equal share. “The same share for the man who stays behind and for one who fights his hardest; the coward and the brave are held in a single honor” (9.318–319). ἴση μοῖρα, “an equal portion”, is the wrong when the deeds are unequal (see [[moira]] and [[time]]). And the portions were not even equal: he gave Agamemnon all the spoils of twenty-three cities, and the king, “staying behind beside the swift ships, would take them, and share out a few, and keep many” (9.330–333; see [[division-of-spoils]]). In Book 1 the equal feasts at Chryse and on Olympus were held without him, while he sat by his ships (1.468, 1.602, 1.488–492); in Book 9 the formula is spoken in his own hut, to a man who says that the shares have never been fair.
