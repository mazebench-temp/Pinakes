summary: The prayers of the embassy are told without words or answer (9.171–177, 9.182–184). The one full prayer scene of the book is in Phoenix's story, Althaea's prayer to the gods below for her son's death, with every part of the pattern turned downward: kneeling, beating the earth, calling on Hades and Persephone, and heard by the Erinys out of Erebus (9.566–572).

**Prayers without words.** The elders wash their hands and keep holy silence “so that we may pray to Zeus, son of Cronus, if he will have pity” (9.171–172), and the envoys pray to Poseidon as they walk along the shore (9.182–184). Neither prayer is quoted, and neither is said to be heard (see [[prayer]] and [[embassy]]).

**A prayer downward.** Althaea's prayer has the parts of the pattern (see [[prayer-type-scene]]), each turned toward the gods below:

1. **Gesture.** Not standing with hands lifted to the sky, but “kneeling down”, as “many times she beat the all-nourishing earth with her hands”, her bosom wet with tears (9.568–570).
2. **Invocation.** “Calling on Hades and dread Persephone” (9.569; see [[hades]] and [[persephone]]).
3. **Ground.** Her grief “over the killing of her brother” (9.567).
4. **Request.** “To give death to her son” (9.571).
5. **Answer.** “And the Erinys who walks in darkness heard her out of Erebus, she of the implacable heart” (ἔκλυεν, 9.571–572; see [[erinyes]] and [[erebus]]). The verb is the one with which Apollo hears Chryses (ἔκλυε, 1.43, 1.457).

The prayer is a curse (ἀρέων, 9.566; see [[curses]]). In the allegory that comes just before the story, the same verb is used of the Prayers, who hear the man who respects them “when he prays” (ἔκλυον εὐχομένοιο, 9.509; see [[litai]]).
