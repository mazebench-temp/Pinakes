---
summary: “A mighty frenzy has entered into” Hector, Odysseus tells Achilles.
---

“A mighty frenzy has entered into him” (κρατερὴ δέ ἑ λύσσα δέδυκεν, 9.239). λύσσα is raging madness, the word from which Teucer's “mad dog” for Hector is formed (κύνα λυσσητῆρα, 8.299; [[mad-dog]]), and Odysseus calls it “deadly” at the end of his speech (λύσσαν ἔχων ὀλοήν, 9.305; [[deadly]], [[exulting-in-strength]]). Here the adjective of strength describes the frenzy, not the man: Hector is driven by something stronger than himself. See [[hector]].
