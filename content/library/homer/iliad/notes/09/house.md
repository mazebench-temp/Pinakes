**Amyntor's house.** Phoenix's heart “could no longer at all be held to staying in the halls while my father was angry” (κατὰ μέγαρα, 9.462–463). His kinsmen kept him there for nine nights, sleeping around him and keeping watch by turns, “and the fire never went out, one under the colonnade of the well-fenced court, and another in the porch, before the doors of my chamber” (9.471–473). On the tenth night he broke “the closely fitted doors of the chamber”, went out, and “leapt over the fence of the court easily, unseen by the watchmen and the slave women” (9.475–477; see [[the-flight-of-phoenix]] and [[handmaids]]). The escape follows the plan of a great house from the inside out: the son's chamber (θάλαμος), the porch (πρόδομος) before it, the court (αὐλή) with its colonnade (αἴθουσα), and the fence (ἑρκίον) around it. Achilles' hut in the last book has the same parts, a court fenced with close-set stakes and a door barred with a beam of fir, and Priam and his herald sleep in its porch (24.448–456, 24.673).

**Meleager's chamber.** Meleager lies in his chamber with his wife while his father begs at its threshold, “standing on the threshold of the high-roofed chamber, shaking the jointed doors, imploring his son”, and he comes out only when “his chamber was being hit hard”, the Curetes are on the towers, and his wife has begged him (9.581–596; see [[threshold]] and [[supplication-type-scene]]).

**The hall and the house of Peleus.** Agamemnon has three daughters “in my well-built hall” (ἐνὶ μεγάρῳ εὐπήκτῳ), and Achilles may lead whichever he chooses “to the house of Peleus” (πρὸς οἶκον Πηλῆος, 9.144–147; see [[marriage]]). The bride goes to her husband's father's house. Achilles' measure of untold wealth is the treasure “stored in the houses” of Egyptian Thebes (9.382; see [[possessions]]).
