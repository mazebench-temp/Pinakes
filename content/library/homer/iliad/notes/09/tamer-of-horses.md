---
summary: The same verse closes Diomedes' first and last speeches in Book 9: the Achaeans and then the kings approve, “admiring the word of Diomedes, tamer of horses.”
---

After his rebuke of Agamemnon, “all the sons of the Achaeans shouted aloud, admiring the word of Diomedes, tamer of horses” (9.50–51). After his dismissal of Achilles, at the end of the book, “all the kings approved, admiring the word of Diomedes, tamer of horses” (9.710–711). The verse frames the book. The first time the army as a whole approves; the second time, only the kings who are present in Agamemnon's hut. Diomedes' epithet is the same as the Trojans' standing epithet, and he is the man who took Aeneas' horses in Book 5 ([[good-at-the-war-cry]], [[diomedes]]).
