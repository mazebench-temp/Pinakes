**The Prayers.** “For the Prayers too are the daughters of great Zeus, lame and wrinkled and with eyes that look askance” (9.502–503; [[litai]]). Phoenix repeats the title as he draws the moral: whoever respects “the daughters of Zeus when they draw near” is helped by them (9.508–509), and Achilles must grant that “the daughters of Zeus be attended by the honor that bends the minds of other men” (9.513–514; [[time]]). Phoenix does not name the parent of Ate, the Blind Folly they follow; in Book 19 Agamemnon calls her “the eldest daughter of Zeus” (19.91; [[ate]]). Taken together, the two passages make the powers sisters, one swift and harmful, the others slow and healing.

**Artemis.** In the story of Meleager, Oeneus sacrificed to the other gods, “but to her alone, the daughter of great Zeus, he made no sacrifice” (9.536; [[artemis]], [[golden-throned]]). The verse-end formula Διὸς κούρῃ μεγάλοιο is the one in which the Trojan women prayed to Athena in Book 6 (6.304, 6.312). See [[zeus]].
