---
summary: Patroclus, kindling the fire for the envoys' meal, is “the son of Menoetius, a man equal to the gods.”
---

“And the son of Menoetius, a man equal to the gods, made the fire blaze high” (9.211; [[son-of-menoetius]], [[patroclus]]). The heroic epithet comes in the middle of the work of the hearth: Patroclus sets out the mixing bowl, kindles the fire, sets the bread on the table and makes the offering to the gods, while Achilles carves (9.202–220; [[feast-type-scene]]). It is the first time the formula is used of him. It describes him once more, in Book 11, when he stands in the doorway of Nestor's hut on Achilles' errand (11.644), the errand that the narrator calls “the beginning of evil for him” (11.604).
