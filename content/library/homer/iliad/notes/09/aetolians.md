These are the Aetolians of a generation before the war, the people of Oeneus. At Troy they follow Thoas, because the house of Oeneus has died out (2.638–643; see [[thoas]] and [[oeneus]]). Phoenix gives them two epithets of warriors, μενεχάρμαι, “steadfast in battle” (9.529; see [[steadfast-in-battle]]), and μεγάθυμοι, “great-hearted” (9.549; see [[great-hearted]]). They are the defenders, ἀμυνόμενοι (9.531), and the war they fight against the Curetes began over the spoils of the hunt (9.547–549; see [[the-calydonian-boar]] and [[curetes]]).

When Meleager holds back, their elders beg him and send the best priests of the gods with the promise of a domain from the richest land of the plain (9.574–580; see [[calydon]] and [[elders]]). In the parallel Phoenix draws they are the Achaeans, whose elders have sent the envoys with Agamemnon's gifts (see [[the-embassy-to-achilles]]), and the half-verse of their plea returns when Thetis tells the embassy in Book 18: τὸν δὲ λίσσοντο γέροντες, “the elders begged him” (18.448 = 9.574). Meleager saves them at the last, and they no longer pay him the gifts (9.597–599; see [[meleager]] and [[the-wrath-of-meleager]]).
