---
summary: “Far-seeing Zeus has held his hand over” Ilios, says Achilles in advising the Achaeans to sail home, and Odysseus repeats the words in his report.
---

“And I would advise the rest of you, too, to sail away home, since you will no longer find the end of steep Ilios; for far-seeing Zeus has held his hand over it, and its people have taken heart” (9.417–420; [[steep]]). Odysseus repeats the four verses to Agamemnon (9.684–687; [[message-repeated]]). The god whose epithet may mean “far-seeing” or “wide-voiced” protects the city the Achaeans came to take, as Agamemnon himself feared at the start of the night (9.18–25; [[supreme-in-might]]). See [[zeus]].
