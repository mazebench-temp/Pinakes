---
summary: Achilles sacked eleven cities on foot “throughout deep-soiled Troy,” and could be in “deep-soiled Phthia” on the third day; Phoenix fled to Phthia, “deep-soiled, mother of flocks.”
grc: [ἐρίβωλον]
---

Book 9 uses both forms of the word, the short ἐρίβωλος and the longer ἐριβῶλαξ. Achilles counts his service: “Twelve cities of men I have sacked with my ships, and eleven, I say, on foot throughout deep-soiled Troy” (9.328–329; [[achilles-raids]]). With a fair voyage, “on the third day I would reach deep-soiled Phthia” (9.363; [[shaker-of-the-earth]]). Phoenix, fleeing his father, “came to deep-soiled Phthia, mother of flocks” (9.479; [[mother-of-flocks]]). The fertile land of the enemy is the land Achilles has plundered; the fertile land of home is the land he means to return to, and the land that took in Phoenix. See [[phthia]] and [[troy]].
