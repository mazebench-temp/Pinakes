---
summary: The ships are the stake of the night. Agamemnon proposes to flee in them and Diomedes tells him to go; the envoys warn that Hector means to burn them; Achilles says he will launch his own at dawn, then that he will not fight until Hector's fire reaches the ships of the Myrmidons; and Odysseus reports that he will drag his ships to the sea.

---

**Ships for flight.** Agamemnon's proposal ends in the verses of his test in Book 2: “let us flee with our ships to our dear native land” (9.27 = 2.140). Diomedes throws it back at him: “the road is open to you, and your ships stand by the sea, the many ships that followed you from Mycenae” (9.43–44), the hundred of the Catalogue (2.576; see [[mycenae]]); let the others flee too, if they wish, “with their ships” (9.47). Nestor's counsel turns the talk from the ships to the fires of the enemy “close by the ships” (9.76–77).

**Ships in danger.** Odysseus begins his appeal with them: “it is in doubt whether we save or lose the well-benched ships, unless you put on your courage” (9.230–231). The Trojans say they will fall upon the black ships, and Hector “vows to hack the high sternposts from the ships, and to burn the ships themselves with ravening fire” (9.235–242; see [[hector]]). κόρυμβα, the high curving ends of the sterns, are named only here (see the translation's note on 9.241). Phoenix speaks twice of the same danger, the destroying fire and the ships already burning (9.435–436, 9.602). The threat comes true at the ship of Protesilaus, whose stern ornament Hector seizes, calling for fire (15.704–718), and when the fire takes it, Achilles sends Patroclus out (16.112–129).

**Ships for home.** Achilles answers that tomorrow, after a sacrifice, he will load his ships and draw them down to the sea, and Odysseus will see them “very early, sailing on the fish-filled Hellespont, my ships, and in them men eager to row” (9.357–361; see [[sailing-type-scene]] and [[hellespont]]). It is the reply that Odysseus carries back (9.682–683; see [[message-repeated]]). To Ajax he gives another: he will not think of war until Hector comes to the huts and ships of the Myrmidons and burns the ships with fire, “but around my hut and my black ship, I think, Hector will be held back” (9.650–655; see [[hut]]). The ships of Achilles and of Ajax were drawn up at the two ends of the line, trusting in their own strength (11.5–9).

**Ships of spoil.** The ships also carry wealth. They bring Agamemnon wine from Thrace every day (9.71–72; see [[wine]]); Achilles sacked twelve cities “with my ships” (9.328; see [[achilles-raids]]); and if Troy falls, Achilles may “load his ship full with gold and bronze” (9.137, 9.279).
