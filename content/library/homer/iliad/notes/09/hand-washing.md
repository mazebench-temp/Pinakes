---
summary: Before the embassy sets out Nestor calls for water for the hands and holy silence, so that the elders may pray to Zeus, and the heralds pour the water (9.171–174).
grc: ["χερσὶν ὕδωρ"]
en: [water for our hands]
---

“Bring water for our hands, and bid them keep holy silence, so that we may pray to Zeus, son of Cronus, if he will have pity” (9.171–172). Water, silence and prayer come in that order, and the libation follows: “At once the heralds poured water over their hands” (9.174), in the half-verse of the oath in Book 3, where the heralds poured water over the kings' hands (3.270; see [[libation]] and [[prayer]]). εὐφημῆσαι, “to keep holy silence”, to let no word of ill omen be spoken during the rite, occurs only here in Homer (see [[silence]]). When Priam sets out for Achilles' hut in the last book, he says that it is good to lift up one's hands to Zeus, “if he will have pity” (αἴ κʼ ἐλεήσῃ, 24.301, as at 9.172), and has his housekeeper pour water over his hands before he prays and pours (χερσὶν ὕδωρ ἐπιχεῦαι, 24.302–306). Both journeys to Achilles begin with clean hands and a prayer for Zeus' pity.
