---
summary: Achilles says that “cattle and fat sheep can be had by raiding,” but not a man's life; Phoenix's kinsmen slaughter many fat sheep to keep him at home.
---

“For cattle and fat sheep can be had by raiding, and tripods can be won, and the tawny heads of horses, but a man's soul cannot come back again” (9.406–408; [[a-mans-soul-cannot-come-back]], [[raiding]], [[fair-haired]]). The formula of a household's flocks is the first item in Achilles' list of things that can be replaced. In Phoenix's story his kinsmen try to keep him in his father's house with feasting: “many fat sheep and shambling cattle with curving horns they slaughtered” (9.466–467; [[shambling]], [[with-curving-horns]]).
