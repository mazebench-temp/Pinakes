---
summary: Odysseus asks Achilles to let go his “heart-grieving anger,” Achilles will not yield until Agamemnon has paid back “all his heart-grieving insult,” and Meleager lies by his wife “brooding on his heart-grieving anger.”
---

The epithet joins the anger to its cause. Odysseus: “stop, and let go your heart-grieving anger” (9.260; [[cholos]]). Achilles answers that not even gifts as many as the sand and the dust would persuade him “until he has paid me back for all his heart-grieving insult” (9.387; [[honor-and-dishonor]]). In Phoenix's story Meleager, angry with his mother, lies beside his wife Cleopatra, “brooding on his heart-grieving anger” (9.565), the phrase used of Achilles himself in Book 4 (4.513). The anger of Meleager is told in the words of Achilles' anger ([[the-wrath-of-meleager]], [[meleager]]).
