summary: Phoenix appeals to "the famous deeds of the men of old, the heroes", whom gifts and words could move (9.524–526), and Achilles turns the title against Agamemnon, "the hero son of Atreus", whom Phoenix must not serve (9.613).
grc: [ἥρωϊ]
---

{{quote:43f3c214-2c44-5c97-a120-347ed50004c3}}

ἡρώων at 9.525 is in apposition to ἀνδρῶν: "the men of old, the heroes". They are the generation of Meleager, whose story follows (see [[the-wrath-of-meleager]] and [[men-of-today]]), and Achilles has been singing of men like them to his lyre (κλέα ἀνδρῶν, 9.189; see [[kleos]] and [[song-and-fame]]).

At 9.613 the title is a sneer: Phoenix is not to weep and grieve "to do a favor to the hero son of Atreus" (Ἀτρεΐδῃ ἥρωϊ φέρων χάριν). Elsewhere ἥρωϊ is a plain title of honor, for Helenus (13.582), Cebriones (16.751), Patroclus (17.137, 23.151) and Meriones (23.893).
