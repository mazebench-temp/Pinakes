---
summary: One of the two heralds who go with the embassy to Achilles is a Eurybates (9.170). He is most probably Agamemnon's herald, who once went with Talthybius to the same hut to take Briseis away, and not Odysseus' herald from Ithaca.
---

Nestor chooses the heralds with the envoys: “and of the heralds let Odius and Eurybates go with them” (9.170; see [[odius-the-herald]] and [[heralds]]). The poem does not say whose herald this Eurybates is, but three things point to Agamemnon's (1.320). The errand is Agamemnon's: the offer is his, the envoys set out from his hut and report back to him there, and heralds carry their own king's words, as Agamemnon's heralds carried his order to the same hut in Book 1 (1.320–325). The bare name points to the herald already named; when Book 2 means Odysseus' herald it marks him as “the herald Eurybates of Ithaca, who attended him” (2.184; see [[eurybates-of-ithaca]]). And the walk to Achilles' hut is told in the verses of the heralds' walk in Book 1, “the two went along the shore … and came to the huts and ships of the Myrmidons” (1.327–328; compare 9.182, 9.185; see [[the-embassy-to-achilles]]). The herald who led Briseis away would now go back with the offer to return her (9.131–134).

Against this, Talthybius, Agamemnon's usual herald (1.320, 3.118, 4.192, 7.276), does not go; Nestor, not Agamemnon, names the heralds; and Odysseus, who leads the embassy, had a herald of this name. In Book 1 Eurybates and Talthybius stood silent before Achilles (1.331–332). In Book 9 the heralds say nothing in the hut, and Odysseus calls on them at the end as witnesses, “Ajax and the two heralds, both wise men” (9.689).
