---
summary: Achilles measures honor twice with αἶσα, “portion”. He honors Agamemnon “at a hair's worth” (ἐν καρὸς αἴσῃ), and is honored himself “by the portion of Zeus” (Διὸς αἴσῃ). Odysseus fears that it is the army's portion to perish at Troy.
passages:
  - 9.378 | I honor him at a hair's worth
  - 9.607-610 | Honored by the portion of Zeus
---

**A fated portion.** Odysseus ends his account of Hector's threats with a fear: “that it may be our fate to perish in Troy, far from horse-pasturing Argos” (9.245–246). αἴσιμον, “fated”, is the adjective of αἶσα: the portion he fears for the army is death.

**Two measures.** Achilles uses the noun itself in two answers, and each time it measures honor. To Odysseus he says of Agamemnon, τίω δέ μιν ἐν καρὸς αἴσῃ, “I honor him at a hair's worth” (9.378): αἶσα is the share or measure of a thing's value, and κάρ, a word found only here, names something of no worth, a hair or, as some ancient readers took it, a Carian (see [[at-a-hairs-worth]]). Διὸς αἴσῃ is the portion that Zeus allots, the honor Thetis asked him to give her son (1.505–510); others take it more loosely as “by the will of Zeus” (see the translation's note on 9.608). Elsewhere Διὸς αἶσα is what Zeus has allotted, for good or ill: the Argives would have won glory “even beyond the portion of Zeus” (ὑπὲρ Διὸς αἶσαν, 17.321), and in the Odyssey “an evil portion from Zeus” stood by Odysseus' men when the Cicones came against them (*Odyssey* 9.52–53). Achilles sets the two measures side by side: the least he can give Agamemnon, and the most that Zeus has given him. In Book 1 his mother told him that his αἶσα, his portion of life, was short (1.416; see [[the-short-life-of-achilles]]). Here he claims the portion of honor that was meant to make up for it.
