---
summary: In five verses of the meal in Achilles' hut the same form describes the “divine salt” that Achilles sprinkles on the meat and “divine Odysseus,” who sits facing him.
---

Achilles “sprinkled them with divine salt, lifting them onto the firedogs” (9.214), and a few verses later “he himself sat facing divine Odysseus, by the other wall” (9.218). Both are θείοιο, at the same place in the verse. Why salt is called divine is not explained in Homer; it has been taken as purifying, as preserving, or as precious ([[feast-type-scene]], [[spits]]). Odysseus is often θεῖος, and here the honorific marks the guest whom Achilles places opposite himself, the envoy who will speak first. See [[odysseus]].
