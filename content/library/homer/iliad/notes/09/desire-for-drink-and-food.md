---
summary: The line closes both meals of the night, in Agamemnon's hut and in Achilles', and each time a speech of persuasion follows it, Nestor's counsel and Odysseus' toast (9.92, 9.222).
---

After the elders' feast the line brings Nestor to his feet, in the four verses that brought him in after the feast of Book 7 (9.92–95 = 7.323–326; see [[with-good-intent]]), and his counsel is to win Achilles back “with kindly gifts and with soothing words” (9.112–113). After Achilles' meal it is followed by a nod: “Ajax nodded to Phoenix; and brilliant Odysseus saw it”, and Odysseus raises his cup (9.222–224). Both meals of the night end in the same verse, and both lead to the embassy, the first to its planning and the second to its speeches (see [[feast-type-scene]] and [[embassy]]).

The line ends one more meal in Achilles' hut, his supper with Priam in the last book, and there nothing is asked at once: the two men look at each other in wonder before Priam speaks (24.628–634).
