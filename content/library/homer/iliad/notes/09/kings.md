---
summary: The kings of Book 9 are the leaders in council, to whom Diomedes speaks wisely and who approve his last word (9.59, 9.710). The word also comes in degrees: Nestor calls Agamemnon “the most kingly”, Agamemnon claims to be “more kingly” than Achilles, and Achilles throws the word back (9.69, 9.160, 9.392).
grc: [βασιλεῦσι]
---

**The council.** Nestor tells Diomedes that he speaks wisely “to the kings of the Argives” (9.59), and at the end of the night “all the kings approved” Diomedes' counsel, in the verse that closed Nestor's counsel of the wall (9.710 = 7.344; see [[diomedes]] and [[elders]]).

**More and most kingly.** βασιλεύς admits degrees. Nestor calls Agamemnon βασιλεύτατος, “the most kingly”, and on that ground bids him lead and feast the elders (9.69). Agamemnon's offer ends with the demand that Achilles submit, “inasmuch as I am more kingly” (βασιλεύτερος, 9.160). Achilles never hears it, for Odysseus leaves it out, but he answers with the same word: let Agamemnon choose for his daughter another of the Achaeans, “who is more kingly” (9.392; see [[agamemnon]] and [[basileus]]). In the night that follows Agamemnon uses it again, when he tells Diomedes not to choose his companion for the raid by birth, “not even if he is more kingly” (10.239).

**Kings and prizes.** Achilles counts the kings among those who kept their prizes: Agamemnon gave “prizes to the chiefs and the kings; they keep theirs safe, but from me alone of the Achaeans he has taken mine” (9.334–336; see [[geras]]). He leaves the defense of the ships to “you and the other kings” (9.346–347; see [[odysseus]]).
