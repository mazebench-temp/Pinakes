summary: The speakers of Book 9 invoke θέμις, “custom”, for their rights: Diomedes for a speaker's freedom in the assembly, Agamemnon and Odysseus for the oath over Briseis. Nestor reminds Agamemnon that Zeus gave him the θέμιστες, “ordinances”, with the scepter, and Agamemnon offers Achilles seven cities that will fulfill his own; the outcast of Nestor's maxim is ἀθέμιστος, “without law”.

**A right to speak.** Diomedes begins his rebuke of the king with a claim: “I will fight with you first, for your folly, as is the custom, my lord, in the assembly” (ἣ θέμις ἐστὶν ἄναξ ἀγορῇ, 9.32–33). The phrase ἣ θέμις ἐστί, “as is the custom”, appeals to what everyone recognizes as laid down. Here it grants a younger man the right to contradict a king in public, and Agamemnon does not answer him (see [[diomedes]] and [[praise-and-blame]]).

**Without law.** Nestor's maxim names the man who has lost every tie: “Without clan, without law, without hearth is the man who loves war among his own people” (9.63–64; see [[without-clan-law-or-hearth]]). ἀθέμιστος, “without θέμις”, occurs only here in the Iliad. In the Odyssey it belongs to the Cyclopes, who have no assemblies for counsel and no θέμιστες (*Odyssey* 9.106, 9.112), and to the suitors who are not righteous (*Odyssey* 17.363).

**The scepter and the ordinances.** In the council Nestor reminds the king what his power is for:

{{quote:20041419-f7ee-50ca-bce4-a1cc3bb0bb3d}}

The line about the scepter is the one Odysseus used of the one king in Book 2, with “you” for “he”: “to whom the son of crooked-counseling Cronus has given the scepter and the ordinances, so that he may take counsel for them” (2.205–206). Nestor adds the duty that goes with them: the king must listen as well as speak, and carry out another man's good counsel (see [[kingship-and-authority]] and [[boule]]). θέμιστες come back in the offer. The men of the seven cities “will honor him with gifts like a god and under his scepter will bring his ordinances to rich fulfillment” (λιπαρὰς τελέουσι θέμιστας, 9.155–156 = 9.297–298). The phrase is usually taken of the dues the people pay to their ruler, what his ordinances assign, or else of their keeping his ordinances in prosperity (see the translation's note on 9.156). Either way Agamemnon is offering a kingdom, with the θέμιστες that Zeus gives to kings (see [[the-seven-cities]]).

**The custom of the bed.** Agamemnon will swear that he never lay with Briseis, “as is the custom of mankind, of men and women” (ἣ θέμις ἀνθρώπων πέλει, 9.134). Odysseus repeats the line with Diomedes' formula, “as is the custom, my lord, of men and of women” (9.276). The custom is that a man lies with the woman who shares his quarters, and the oath is needed because everyone would assume it (see [[oath]] and [[briseis]]). Agamemnon swears the oath in Book 19 (19.258–265).
