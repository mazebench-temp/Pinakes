---
summary: Three old men of Phoenix's speech are “old horse-driving”: Phoenix himself, Peleus who sent him with Achilles, and Oeneus who begs his son Meleager to fight.
---

The formula γέρων ἱππηλάτα gathers the fathers of Book 9. “At last the old horse-driving Phoenix spoke among them” (9.432; [[phoenix]]). He tells how “the old horse-driving Peleus sent me with you” to teach the boy to be a speaker of words and a doer of deeds (9.438–443; [[peleus]], [[peleus-sends-achilles-to-troy]]). In his story of Meleager, “the old horse-driving Oeneus” stands on the threshold of his son's chamber, shaking the doors and begging him to fight (9.581–583; [[oeneus]], [[the-wrath-of-meleager]]). Phoenix is Achilles' foster father, Peleus his father, and Oeneus the father who pleads in vain. The title of an old chariot-fighter is given to men who can no longer fight and can only advise and beg. See [[fathers-and-sons]].
