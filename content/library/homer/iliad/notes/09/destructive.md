---
summary: “The consuming fire” is what Achilles tells Agamemnon to ward off from the ships himself, and what Agamemnon asks whether Achilles will ward off.
grc: ["δήϊον πῦρ"]
---

Achilles sends a message to Agamemnon: “No, Odysseus, with you and the other kings let him consider how to ward off the consuming fire from the ships” (9.346–347). When the envoys return, Agamemnon's first question takes up the same words: “is he willing to ward off the consuming fire from the ships, or has he refused?” (9.674–675; [[much-praised]]). The verse-end δήϊον πῦρ, an accusative of the formula of Book 2, is the fire that Hector threatens (9.241–243; [[ravening]]). In this book δήϊος also means “the enemy”: the δήϊοι burn fires near the ships (9.76), and Achilles is tired of fighting “against the enemy without rest” (9.317). See [[ships]].
