summary: On the night of the embassy the wall and ditch are the Achaeans' front line. Nestor posts the sentries beside the ditch outside the wall, Odysseus tells Achilles that the Trojans are camped close to it, and Achilles scorns it: Agamemnon has built a wall and a ditch without him, but cannot hold back Hector (9.66–67, 9.87, 9.232, 9.348–355).

**The sentries.** Nestor proposes that the sentries “spend the night beside the ditch they have dug, outside the wall” (9.67; see [[night-watch]] and [[dug]]), and the seven companies go out and sit “midway between the ditch and the wall”, where they light their fires and make their supper (9.87–88). That afternoon the Trojans had penned the Achaeans inside the ditch (8.213–215); now the guards hold it against them. The verse of 9.67 returns when Athena shouts “beside the ditch they have dug, outside the wall” as the gods go into battle (20.49).

**The camp beside it.** Odysseus tells Achilles where the danger lies: “For near the ships and the wall they have pitched their camp, the high-hearted Trojans and their far-famed allies” (9.232–233), with their fires burning through the army (9.234).

**Achilles' scorn.**

{{quote:aac0208f-47d4-5c6c-af91-775f2d153f8f}}

Achilles describes the work in the narrator's words for its building, “wide and great, and in it he planted stakes” (9.350 ≈ 7.441), and gives it to Agamemnon alone (see [[agamemnon]]). The wall is what the Achaeans have had to build because he no longer fights. While he fought, Hector would not lead the battle out from the walls of Troy beyond the Scaean gates and the oak (9.352–355; see [[hectors-escape-at-the-oak]] and [[the-walls-of-troy]]). Those who defend the wall's place in the poem point to these lines as the poem's own reason for building it in the tenth year: it is built when Achilles has withdrawn (see [[the-wall-in-the-tenth-year]]).
