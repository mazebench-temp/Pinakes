---
summary: Nestor names Phoenix first among the envoys: “First of all let Phoenix, dear to Zeus, lead the way.”
---

“First of all let Phoenix, dear to Zeus, lead the way, and after him great Ajax and brilliant Odysseus” (9.168–169; [[phoenix]], [[brilliant]]). The epithet comes with Phoenix's first appearance in the poem. Nothing has explained why he is in Agamemnon's hut, and the duals of the walk to Achilles' hut leave his place in the embassy uncertain ([[the-embassy-to-achilles]]). In the same book Agamemnon speaks of a man whom Zeus loves: “Worth many people is the man whom Zeus loves in his heart” (9.116–117), meaning Achilles. See [[zeus]].
