---
summary: Achilles calls Agamemnon “clothed forever in shamelessness”, the insult with which he opened the quarrel in Book 1, and adds that the dog would not dare to look him in the face.
passages:
  - 9.369-377 | Never again will he beguile me
---

Achilles tells Odysseus to report his words openly, so that the other Achaeans may be indignant if Agamemnon hopes to deceive anyone else, “clothed forever in shamelessness” (αἰὲν ἀναιδείην ἐπιειμένος, 9.372). The phrase is his first insult in Book 1, ὤ μοι ἀναιδείην ἐπιειμένε, “you clothed in shamelessness” (1.149), with αἰέν, “forever”, added: nothing has changed since the quarrel. Shamelessness is worn like a garment, as courage is put on (see [[alke]]). The next words take up his other insult of Book 1, the dog (1.159, 1.225): “but he would not, dog though he is, dare to look me in the face” (κύνεός περ ἐών, 9.373; see [[dog-face]]). The dog in Homer is the shameless animal, and even this one would not meet Achilles' eyes. The opposite is the respect Ajax asks Achilles to show his guests (9.640; see [[aidos]]).

In the Iliad only Achilles uses the word family of a man, and only of Agamemnon: “clothed in shamelessness” and “you utterly shameless one” in the quarrel (1.149, 1.158), and this phrase now. Otherwise it belongs to things that will not yield: the stone that crushed Diores' ankle (4.521), a rock in a simile (13.139), the Tumult that Enyo brings into battle (5.593). Achilles grants the gods a part in the king's conduct, “for Zeus the counselor has taken away his wits” (9.377), as Agamemnon had blamed his own blindness on Zeus (9.115–120; see [[ate]]). For Achilles it excuses nothing: “he deceived me and did me wrong” (9.375; see [[apate]]).
