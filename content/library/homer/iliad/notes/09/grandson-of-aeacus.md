---
summary: The patronymic names Achilles twice as the envoys come to his hut: they pray to win over “the great mind of the grandson of Aeacus”, and they find Patroclus waiting for “the grandson of Aeacus” to leave off his singing (9.184, 9.191).
grc: [Αἰακίδην]
---

{{quote:2422a6ae-8a96-5126-b386-278cfc3e6a3f}}

The genitive Αἰακίδαο ends the verse, as it does in the Trojan Catalogue, where it follows ποδώκεος, “swift-footed” (2.860, 2.874); here it follows μεγάλας φρένας, “the great mind” that the envoys pray to win over (9.184). The accusative Αἰακίδην (9.191) is new. Both come in the narrator's voice, before Achilles is named in the scene (9.193), and the envoys never use the patronymic to his face (see [[achilles]] and [[aeacus]]). Later books use it of Peleus too, Αἰακίδης Πηλεύς (16.15, 21.189).
