---
summary: Sophocles' Electra names Chrysothemis and Iphianassa, two of the three daughters Agamemnon offers Achilles, as Electra's sisters still living in the house, and in his Philoctetes Odysseus comes to fetch Neoptolemus with “the man who reared my father”, Phoenix.
---

- **Agamemnon's daughters.** The chorus reminds Electra that she is not alone in her grief, since Chrysothemis and Iphianassa live on in the house (*Electra* 157). Homer's three daughters are Chrysothemis, Laodice and Iphianassa (9.145, 9.287); the play also tells of the daughter whom Agamemnon sacrificed, whom Homer never mentions. See [[chrysothemis]] and [[iphianassa]].
- **Phoenix after Achilles.** Neoptolemus tells Philoctetes that “divine Odysseus and the man who reared my father” came for him by ship to bring him to Troy (*Philoctetes* 343–344). Sophocles does not name the tutor; Homer's foster father of Achilles is Phoenix (9.485–495). See [[phoenix]].
