summary: Phoenix escapes from his father's house unseen by the watchmen and the slave women (9.477); in Achilles' hut the slave women spread Phoenix's bed with fleeces, a rug and fine linen (9.658–661); and seven women of Lesbos, “skilled in flawless handiwork”, are among Agamemnon's gifts (9.128).
grc: [δμωῇσι]
---

**In Amyntor's house.** On the tenth night Phoenix breaks the doors of his chamber and leaps the fence of the court “easily, unseen by the watchmen and the slave women” (δμῳάς τε γυναῖκας, 9.477; see [[the-flight-of-phoenix]] and [[house]]). The household that kept watch on him had its women servants too.

**In Achilles' hut.** At the end of the night “Patroclus bade his companions and the slave women spread a thick bed for Phoenix as quickly as possible. And they obeyed and spread the bed as he bade, fleeces and a rug and the fine nap of linen” (9.658–661; see [[bed]]). δμωῇσι is printed here without the iota subscript. The women of the hut were taken in the raids of Achilles and Patroclus (18.28). In the last book the verse returns with Achilles' name, when he has beds made under the portico for Priam and his herald (24.643–648; see [[hut]]).

**As gifts.** Agamemnon's gifts include “seven women skilled in flawless handiwork, women of Lesbos” (9.128–129; see [[weaving]] and [[captive-women]]), and in Book 19 they are led out of his hut with Briseis as the eighth (19.245–246).
