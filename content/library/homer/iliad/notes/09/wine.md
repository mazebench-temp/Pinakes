**From Thrace.** Nestor reminds Agamemnon that his huts are full of wine, “which the ships of the Achaeans bring you day by day from Thrace over the wide sea” (9.71–72; see [[thrace]]). At the end of Book 7 the wine came in ships from Lemnos, sent by Euneus, and the army bought it with bronze, iron, hides, cattle and slaves (7.467–475; see [[trade]]). Here the army's own ships fetch it, and the store is the king's: it is what lets him feast the elders (9.70–73; see [[feast]]). The Odyssey knows the wine of that coast. Odysseus carried to the Cyclops' cave the wine of Maron, priest of Apollo at Ismarus among the Cicones, a people the Catalogue names beside the Thracians (2.844–846), and it was so strong that one cup of it was mixed with twenty measures of water (Od. 9.196–211).

**Stronger for the envoys.** Achilles' first order is for the wine: “Set out a larger mixing bowl, son of Menoetius, and mix the wine stronger, and make ready a cup for each; for these are the dearest of men, and they are under my roof” (9.202–204; see [[mixing-bowl]]). ζωρότερον, “stronger”, with less water, occurs only here in Homer; Aristotle read it as “more quickly” (see the translation's note on 9.203). A larger bowl and a stronger mixture are the guest's honor, as Agamemnon's cup of honor “stands full” for Idomeneus (4.262–263). When the meal is over, Odysseus fills a cup with wine and pledges Achilles in it before he speaks (9.224; see [[cup]]).

**In Phoenix's story.** When Phoenix quarreled with his father, his kinsmen kept him at home with a feast, “and much wine was drunk from the old man's jars” (9.469). The jars (κέραμοι) are the store of a house; Ares was once bound in a bronze one for thirteen months (5.387; see [[the-aloadae-bind-ares]]). Phoenix also remembers feeding the child Achilles on his knees and holding the wine to his lips, and the result: “Many times you wet the tunic on my chest, sputtering out the wine in your troublesome infancy” (9.489–491; see [[phoenix]]). Andromache remembers Astyanax eating marrow and fat on his father's knees (22.500–501).

**Food and wine.** Diomedes sends the kings to bed “when you have satisfied your hearts with food and wine; for in that is might and courage” (9.705–706), the verse with which Odysseus urges Achilles to let the army eat in Book 19 (19.161; see [[feast]]).
