---
summary: Birth fixes rank in Book 9's council. Nestor calls Diomedes young enough to be his son, “the youngest by birth”, and claims to be the elder himself; Agamemnon ends his offer by demanding that Achilles yield to him as “the elder by birth”.
grc: [γενεῆφιν]
passages:
  - 9.53-62 | Nestor praises Diomedes
  - 9.157-161 | Agamemnon's demand that Achilles yield
---

**The youngest.** Nestor answers Diomedes' rebuke of the king with praise and a correction. Diomedes is mighty in war and the best in counsel “among all men of your age”, yet young: “you might even be my son, the youngest by birth” (ὁπλότατος γενεῆφιν, 9.54–58). His speech has not reached its end (9.56), and Nestor, “who claim to be older than you” (γεραίτερος εὔχομαι εἶναι, 9.60), will finish it. Diomedes says the same of himself in Book 14, when he rises to speak in council, since he is “the youngest by birth among you” (γενεῆφι νεώτατος, 14.112; see [[diomedes]] and [[nestor]]).

**The elder.** Agamemnon ends his offer with the claim of rank:

{{quote:07e672bc-3b87-50b2-8055-78166e3440b0}}

γενεῇ προγενέστερος, “the elder by birth”, claims the right of the elder to be obeyed. It is the ground on which Zeus demands that Poseidon leave the battle in Book 15: he is “far stronger in might and elder by birth” (γενεῇ πρότερος, 15.165–166, 15.181–182), and Iris reminds Poseidon that “the Erinyes always follow the elder” (15.204). Poseidon yields, protesting that he was given an equal share (15.209–211). Agamemnon's verb is the one Nestor used, εὔχομαι εἶναι, “I claim to be” (see [[euchomai]]), and in the Catalogue Nestor alone could rival Menestheus at marshaling, “for he was older” (προγενέστερος, 2.555). Odysseus leaves the demand out when he repeats the offer (9.264–299; see [[message-repeated]]), and Achilles never hears it; but he answers it all the same, telling Agamemnon to choose another son-in-law, one “who is more kingly” (9.392; see [[basileus]]). Achilles is young, “a child” when Peleus sent him to the war (9.440), and the night is full of old men who would guide him: Nestor, who chooses the envoys; Phoenix, who reared him; Peleus, whose charges are remembered (see [[old-age-and-memory]]).

**Divine stock.** γένος is used once, of Artemis, δῖον γένος, “the divine child” (9.538).
