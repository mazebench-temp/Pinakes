---
summary: Agamemnon submits to the pleasure of “Zeus, supreme in might,” in a verse repeated from his test of the army in Book 2.
---

“Such, it seems, must be the pleasure of Zeus, supreme in might, who has brought down the heights of many cities and will bring down more” (9.23–25 = 2.116–118). The epithet of the god's surpassing strength serves Agamemnon's argument for flight: if Zeus wills it, nothing can be done. Diomedes answers with the other side of Zeus' gifts, which gave Agamemnon the scepter but not courage, “and that is the greatest power” (9.37–39; [[crooked-counseling]]). See [[zeus]].
