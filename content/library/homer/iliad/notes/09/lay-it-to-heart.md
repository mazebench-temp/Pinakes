---
summary: Achilles turns from his answer to Phoenix to a warning with the line he used to Agamemnon in the first quarrel (9.611 = 1.297): Phoenix must not take Agamemnon's side, or he will be hateful to the man who loves him.
---

“And I will tell you something else, and you lay it to heart” (9.611). In Book 1 the line drew Achilles' limit before Agamemnon: he would not fight for the girl, but let no one touch anything else of his (1.297–303). Here it draws a line around Phoenix: “do not confuse my heart with weeping and grieving to do a favor to the hero son of Atreus; you have no need to love him, or you will make yourself hateful to me, who love you. It is good for you to vex with me the one who vexes me” (9.612–615). The warning ends in a gift: “Be king equally with me, and take half my honor” (9.616; see [[phoenix]], [[philos]] and [[time]]). In Book 1 the line was spoken to the king; in Book 9 to the old man who is to choose between Achilles and the king.
