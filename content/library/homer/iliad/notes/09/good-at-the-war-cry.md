---
summary: “At last Diomedes, good at the war cry, spoke among them”: the same verse introduces Diomedes' rebuke of Agamemnon at the start of the book and his dismissal of Achilles at the end.
---

Diomedes speaks first and last in the book, and both times into a silence. After Agamemnon proposes flight, “For a long time the sons of the Achaeans were silent in their grief; at last Diomedes, good at the war cry, spoke among them” (9.30–31). After Odysseus reports Achilles' refusal, the same two verses bring him in again (9.695–696; [[sons-of-the-achaeans]], [[silence]]). The first time he challenges the king to his face and offers to fight on alone with Sthenelus (9.32–49); the second time he tells the king to leave Achilles alone, rest, and lead the army out at dawn (9.697–709; [[rosy-fingered]]). The warrior known for his shout breaks the army's silence twice, and each time the army takes up his words ([[tamer-of-horses]]). See [[diomedes]].
