---
summary: In Phoenix's story his kinsmen, trying to keep him at home, singed fat hogs “across the flame of Hephaestus” (9.468). The god's name stands for the fire, as at Agamemnon's sacrifice in Book 2.
---

After his father's curse Phoenix's kinsmen and cousins begged him to stay. For nine nights they kept watch around him, slaughtering sheep and cattle, drinking the old man's wine, and singeing fat hogs “across the flame of Hephaestus” (9.464–470; see [[phoenix]] and [[the-flight-of-phoenix]]). The god's name is the fire itself, as when the entrails are held “over the flame of Hephaestus” at Agamemnon's sacrifice (Ἡφαίστοιο, 2.426) and when Hector, shouting, is φλογὶ εἴκελος Ἡφαίστοιο, “like the flame of Hephaestus” (17.88). The verse returns word for word at the funeral feast of Patroclus, where many white-tusked hogs are singed in the same way (23.32–33; 23.33 = 9.468).
