---
summary: Phoenix's kinsmen, trying to keep him at home, slaughter “many fat sheep and shambling cattle with curving horns.”
grc: [εἰλίποδας]
---

“And many fat sheep and shambling cattle with curving horns they slaughtered, and many hogs, rich with lard, were stretched to singe across the flame of Hephaestus” (9.466–468; [[fat-sheep]], [[with-curving-horns]], [[flourishing]]). This is the first time the full line-end formula εἰλίποδας ἕλικας βοῦς appears in the poem; the whole verse returns at the pyre of Patroclus (23.166). The feast meant to keep Phoenix at home is a feast of a great house, and it does not hold him. See [[cattle]] and [[the-flight-of-phoenix]].
