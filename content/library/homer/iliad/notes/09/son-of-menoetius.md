---
summary: Patroclus is called by his father's name twice at the meal for the envoys: Achilles addresses him as Μενοιτίου υἱέ, “son of Menoetius”, and the narrator calls him Μενοιτιάδης as he makes the fire (9.202, 9.211).
grc: ["Μενοιτίου υἱὲ", Μενοιτιάδης]
---

The two ways of naming a man by his father stand nine lines apart: the genitive with υἱός, in Achilles' order to set out a larger mixing bowl (9.202), and the patronymic proper, in the narrator's verse, “the son of Menoetius, a man equal to the gods, made the fire blaze high” (9.211; see [[equal-to-the-gods]]). Achilles has the same pair, Πηλῆος υἱός beside Πηλεΐδης (see [[son-of-peleus]]). With an epithet, Μενοιτίου ἄλκιμος υἱός, “the brave son of Menoetius”, becomes one of Patroclus' standing titles from Book 11 on (11.605, 11.814; see [[patroclus]] and [[menoetius]]).
