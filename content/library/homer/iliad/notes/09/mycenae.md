---
summary: Diomedes tells Agamemnon that the road home is open to him and his ships stand by the sea, “the many ships that followed you from Mycenae” (9.43–44).
grc: [Μυκήνηθεν]
---

Μυκήνηθεν, “from Mycenae”, the name with the suffix -θεν (“from”), occurs only here. Diomedes points the king to the ships that are his own, the hundred of the Catalogue, which brought “by far the most and the best men” (2.576–578; see [[diomedes]] and [[agamemnon]]): if he wants to go home, he has the means. The rest of the Achaeans, he says, will stay until Troy is sacked (9.45–46). When Agamemnon speaks of home in this book he calls it Argos: he would go back “to Argos in disgrace” (9.22), and Achilles is to be his son-in-law if they reach “Achaean Argos” (9.141; see [[argos]]).
