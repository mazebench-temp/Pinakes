“And I made you what you are, Achilles, like the gods, loving you from my heart” (9.485–486), and “it was you, Achilles, like the gods, that I made my son” (9.494–495; [[phoenix]]). The words are a variant of the formula ἐπιείκελος ἀθανάτοισιν with θεοῖς, “gods,” and in the vocative they become a way of addressing Achilles alone. Hector, unhurt by Achilles' first spear, mocks him with it (22.279); the ghost of Patroclus uses it to remind him of his own death (23.80); and Priam begins his supplication with it, “Remember your father, Achilles, like the gods” (24.486). Phoenix is the first, and he uses it with the tenderness of a foster father. See [[achilles]] and [[godlike]].
