summary: Achilles names the taking of his prize an act of ὕβρις: Agamemnon “has taken back again in his arrogance” (ἐφυβρίζων) the prize he gave. He named it so in the quarrel, when Athena promised him gifts three times as great for the outrage.
grc: [ἐφυβρίζων]
passages:
  - 9.367-369 | The man who gave it took it back
---

Achilles tells the envoys what he will take home from Troy, the gold, bronze, women and iron that he won by lot, and what he will not: “but my prize, the very man who gave it has taken back again in his arrogance, powerful Agamemnon, son of Atreus” (αὖτις ἐφυβρίζων ἕλετο, 9.367–369). ἐφυβρίζω, “commit outrage against, insult over”, occurs only here in Homer. The prefix ἐπί marks the outrage as aimed at someone, done over him. It is his word for the act of Book 1. When Athena came to stop his sword, he asked her whether she had come “to see the arrogance of Agamemnon, son of Atreus” (ὕβριν, 1.203), and she promised him that “someday three times as many splendid gifts will be laid before you because of this outrage” (ὕβριος εἵνεκα τῆσδε, 1.213–214; see [[triple-gifts-foretold]]).

Book 9 brings the promised gifts, and Achilles refuses them in the speech that names the ὕβρις. The goddess spoke as if the outrage could be paid for in gifts; Achilles answers that it is still to be paid off whole (9.387; see [[lobe]] and [[the-gifts-of-agamemnon]]).

The word family is rare in the Iliad. The noun ὕβρις occurs only in the two lines of Book 1. Besides Achilles' verb here, Nestor says that the Epeans, “overbearing”, devised reckless deeds against his people “in their outrage” (ὑβρίζοντες, 11.694–695), and Menelaus complains that Zeus favors “men of outrage, the Trojans” (ὑβριστῇσι, 13.633–634). Each time the outrage is a taking: Augeas kept the prize horses that Neleus had sent to race (11.698–702), and the Trojans carried off Menelaus' wedded wife and many possessions (13.626–627). In this book the taking is still Agamemnon's.
