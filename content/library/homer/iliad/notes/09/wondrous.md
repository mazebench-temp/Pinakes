---
summary: Book 9 opens with the Achaeans “held by heaven-sent Panic, companion of chilling Rout.”
en: [heaven-sent]
---

The night after the second day of battle begins in fear: “the Achaeans were held by heaven-sent Panic, companion of chilling Rout” (9.1–2; [[rout]], [[chilling]]). Panic (φύζα) and Rout (φόβος) are printed lowercase in the Greek but are treated as powers, as Rout is one of the powers set around the aegis (5.739). Here θεσπέσιος keeps its sense of what comes from the gods, as in θεσπεσίῃ, “by the will of heaven” (2.367), and the translation renders it “heaven-sent.” The panic is not only felt; it is sent. Agamemnon says as much when he rises to speak: “Zeus, son of Cronus, has bound me fast in heavy blind folly” (9.18; [[ate]], [[son-of-cronus]]).
