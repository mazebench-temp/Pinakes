summary: Diomede, the woman Achilles brought from Lesbos, lies beside him at the end of the night: “the daughter of Phorbas, fair-cheeked Diomede.”

{{quote:6fe13aea-71c8-5d38-89ce-9d0171ec9f1c}}

“And beside him lay a woman, whom he had brought from Lesbos, the daughter of Phorbas, fair-cheeked Diomede” (9.664–665; [[diomede]], [[phorbas-father-of-diomede]], [[lesbos]]). The epithet is the one the Iliad gives Briseis and Chryseis, the captive women of Book 1. Achilles has told the envoys that he loved Briseis “from my heart” (9.343); the night ends with another captive beside him, as Patroclus has Iphis ([[fair-belted]], [[captive-women]]). In Book 24 the same place is Briseis', “fair-cheeked Briseis” (24.676). See [[achilles]].
