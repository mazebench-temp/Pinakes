---
summary: The night after the second day of battle, seen from the Achaean side. Panic and grief hold the army, Nestor posts sentries because the enemy's fires burn close to the ships, and Odysseus tells Achilles what the day has left behind, the Trojans camped by the wall with Zeus' lightning on their side and Hector waiting for the dawn to burn the ships.

  - 9.76-88 | The sentries between the ditch and the wall
  - 9.232-243 | Odysseus describes the Trojan camp
---

**The other side of the ditch.** Book 8 ended in the Trojan camp: a thousand fires burning on the plain, and the horses beside the chariots waiting for the dawn (8.553–565). Book 9 begins on the other side of the ditch. “So the Trojans kept their watch; but the Achaeans were held by heaven-sent Panic, companion of chilling Rout” (9.1–2), and their hearts are torn like a sea struck by two winds (9.4–8; see [[two-winds-from-thrace]]).

**The day measured.** The speeches of the night measure the defeat. Nestor sends the young men to keep watch between the ditch and the wall, “since the enemy close by the ships are burning many fires; who could rejoice at this? This night will either shatter the army or save it” (9.76–78; see [[night-watch]], [[the-achaean-wall]] and [[this-night-will-shatter-or-save]]). Odysseus tells Achilles that the Trojans and their allies have pitched their camp near the ships and the wall and say they will fall on the black ships; Zeus shows them signs on the right with his lightning, and Hector rages, trusting in Zeus, and prays for the dawn (9.232–240; see [[zeus]] and [[hector]]). His report of Hector's vow, to burn the ships and slaughter the Achaeans beside them, “bewildered by the smoke” (9.242–243), is close to Hector's own boast of the afternoon, to kill the Argives beside the ships, “dazed by the smoke” (8.180–183), and it adds the high sternposts that Hector means to hack from the ships (9.241). Hector takes hold of the stern of Protesilaus' ship in Book 15 and calls for fire (15.704–725).

**Toward the next day.** Nestor's words make the night a crisis, and the embassy is the plan it produces (see [[the-embassy-to-achilles]]). It fails, and Diomedes, closing the book, turns the army back to the day's work: when rosy-fingered Dawn appears, Agamemnon is to draw up the army and the horses before the ships and fight among the foremost (9.707–709). The night goes on through the spying raid of Book 10, and the third day of battle dawns at 11.1.
