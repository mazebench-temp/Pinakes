**The envoys.** At the elders' feast Nestor proposes that “chosen men” (κλητούς) go at once to the hut of Achilles, and he names them himself: “First of all let Phoenix, dear to Zeus, lead the way, and after him great Ajax and brilliant Odysseus; and of the heralds let Odius and Eurybates go with them” (9.165–170; see [[phoenix]], [[ajax]], [[odysseus]], [[odius-the-herald]], [[eurybates]] and [[heralds]]). The poem's other embassies went to an enemy: Menelaus and Odysseus to Troy to ask for Helen (3.205–206, 11.138–141), and Tydeus to Thebes (4.384). This one goes to a man of the army who has withdrawn from it, and the envoys are the men closest to him. Achilles greets them as “the dearest of the Achaeans” (9.198), and Phoenix tells him that Agamemnon has sent “the best men … the ones who to you yourself are dearest of the Argives” (9.520–522). Why Phoenix is in Agamemnon's hut at all is not said (see the translation's note on 9.168–170).

**The setting out.** “Bring water for our hands, and bid them keep holy silence, so that we may pray to Zeus, son of Cronus, if he will have pity” (9.171–172). The heralds pour the water, the young men fill the mixing bowls, and all pour libations and drink before the envoys leave (9.174–178; see [[hand-washing]], [[prayer]] and [[libation]]). Nestor gives them “many instructions, glancing at each of them, and at Odysseus most of all” (9.179–181). On the shore they pray again, to Poseidon, “that they might easily win over the great mind of the grandson of Aeacus” (9.182–184). The walk is told in the dual, “the two”, though five set out, and Odysseus, not Phoenix, leads them in (9.192). The duals have been explained as Ajax and Odysseus, the envoys proper, with Phoenix gone ahead; as the two heralds; as a dual used loosely for a plural; or as a trace of an older telling without Phoenix (see the translation's note on 9.182–198, [[messenger-scene]] and [[the-embassy-to-achilles]]).

**In the hut.** Achilles receives the envoys as guests and feeds them before a word is said of their errand (9.193–221; see [[visit-scene]] and [[feast-type-scene]]). Then “Ajax nodded to Phoenix; and brilliant Odysseus saw it”, and Odysseus speaks first (9.223–224). Each envoy pleads in his own way: Odysseus with the danger to the ships, the words of Peleus and the list of gifts (9.225–306); Phoenix with his own life, the Prayers and the story of Meleager (9.434–605); Ajax, speaking first to Odysseus, with the claims of friendship (9.624–642). The verse that brings in each of Achilles' answers is the same (9.307, 9.606, 9.643; see [[answering-him]]), and each answer is different.

**The message.** Achilles makes the envoys his messengers. “Tell him everything, just as I charge you, openly” (9.369–370); “go to the chiefs of the Achaeans and declare my message, for that is the prize of the old” (9.421–422; see [[prize-of-the-old]]); “These men will carry the message” (9.617); “But you go and declare my message” (9.649). Ajax agrees that “we must report the word to the Danaans at once, though it is not good” (9.626–627). One envoy is kept back: Phoenix is to sleep in the hut and sail with Achilles if he wishes (9.427–429, 9.620–622, 9.658–662).

**The report.** The others pour a libation from two-handled cups and go back along the ships, with Odysseus leading (9.656–657). In Agamemnon's huts the Achaeans stand to pledge them in golden cups and question them, and the king asks first (9.669–675; see [[cup]]). Odysseus answers, and he names his witnesses: “these men too who came with me are here to tell it, Ajax and the two heralds, both wise men” (9.688–689). The heralds have no part in the scene at Achilles' hut; their task is to vouch for the word, as Achilles made Talthybius and Eurybates witnesses when they took Briseis (1.338). The report gives only Achilles' first answer (see [[message-repeated]]), and the kings meet it with silence until Diomedes speaks (9.693–709; see [[silence]]).

**Remembered.** Thetis tells Hephaestus of the embassy in the words of Phoenix's story: “the elders of the Argives begged him, and named many glorious gifts” (18.448–449). The first half-verse is the one used of the elders who begged Meleager (τὸν δὲ λίσσοντο γέροντες, 9.574; see [[the-wrath-of-meleager]]), and the gifts are named as Agamemnon named them (περικλυτὰ δῶρα, 9.121). In Book 19 Agamemnon offers “all the gifts that brilliant Odysseus came yesterday and promised you in your huts” (19.140–141), and Odysseus leads the young men who carry them out (19.238–248; see [[the-gifts-of-agamemnon]]). Elsewhere Achilles speaks as though no envoys had come and no gifts been offered (11.609–610, 16.72–73, 16.84–86). Analysts took these passages as signs that Book 9 was added late; whether they show that, or Achilles' own view of the night, is still debated (see [[the-embassy-to-achilles]]).
