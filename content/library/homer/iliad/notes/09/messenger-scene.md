**Sending.** Nestor names the messengers and gives them their instructions, and they leave after a libation (9.165–181; see [[embassy]] and [[libation]]).

**Going, arriving, finding.** The arrival is told in the verses of Book 1, where Agamemnon's two heralds went to take Briseis:

- “So the two went along the shore of the loud-roaring sea” (τὼ δὲ βάτην παρὰ θῖνα, 9.182); “Unwilling, the two went along the shore of the barren sea” (τὼ δʼ ἀέκοντε βάτην παρὰ θῖνʼ, 1.327).
- “And they came to the huts and ships of the Myrmidons” (9.185 = 1.328).
- “And found him delighting his heart with a clear-toned lyre” (τὸν δʼ εὗρον, 9.186); “They found him beside his hut and his black ship” (1.329).

In Book 1 the dual fitted the two heralds. In Book 9 five men set out, and the verbs and pronouns of the walk and the greeting are dual (9.182–198). Whether the duals mean Ajax and Odysseus, the heralds, a plural loosely put, or an older telling with two envoys is still argued (see the translation's note on 9.182–198 and [[the-embassy-to-achilles]]).

**The greeting.** The heralds stood before Achilles in fear and said nothing, and he spoke first: “Welcome, heralds, messengers of Zeus and of men” (χαίρετε, 1.331–334). The envoys too stand before him (9.193), and again he speaks first, “Welcome, both of you! Surely you come as friends” (χαίρετον, 9.197). He received the heralds who came to take; he receives the envoys who come to give back.

**The message and the answers.** Odysseus delivers Agamemnon's message, much of it in Agamemnon's own words (9.225–306; see [[message-repeated]]), and the answers go back with the envoys (9.421–422, 9.649).

**The return.** Book 1 ended the errand with the heralds' going (1.347–348). Book 9 follows the envoys home. They come to Agamemnon's huts, are pledged in golden cups and questioned, and Odysseus answers the king's question with the report, calling Ajax and the heralds to witness (9.669–692; see [[cup]]).
