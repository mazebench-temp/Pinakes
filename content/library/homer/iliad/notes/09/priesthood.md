summary: In Phoenix's story the elders of the Aetolians send “the best priests of the gods” to beg Meleager to come out and fight, promising him a great gift (9.574–576): a city sends its priests to beg one of its own men.
grc: [ἱερῆας]
---

“And the elders begged him, the elders of the Aetolians, and sent the best priests of the gods, to come out and defend them, promising a great gift” (9.574–576). The priests belong to the first and most formal appeal, the city's own, before Meleager's father, his sisters and mother, and his companions come in turn (see [[supplication-type-scene]] and [[the-wrath-of-meleager]]). The poem does not say what the priests could add, but they bring the gods of a city whose walls are being battered, and they are “the best”, as the envoys to Achilles are “the best men” of the army (9.520; see [[embassy]]). Elsewhere in the Iliad a priest is “honored by the people like a god” (5.78, 16.605), prays and sacrifices for the people (1.450–457), or comes himself to beg for his own, as Chryses comes to ransom his daughter (1.12–21). Here the priests beg for the city, and the man they beg refuses them.
