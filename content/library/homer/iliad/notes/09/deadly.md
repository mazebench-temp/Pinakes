---
summary: Odysseus tells Achilles that Hector would come close to him now, “in his deadly frenzy.”
grc: [ὀλοήν]
---

“For now you might kill Hector, since he would come very close to you in his deadly frenzy, since he says there is no one equal to him among the Danaans whom the ships brought here” (9.304–306; [[krateros]], [[hector]]). Odysseus' last argument is glory: Achilles could win “very great glory” from the Achaeans by killing the man whose frenzy makes him reckless (9.303; [[kydos]]). The adjective of ruin, from ὄλλυμι, describes a madness that destroys its owner as well as its victims.
