---
summary: The envoys walk “along the shore of the loud-roaring sea” to Achilles' hut, in a verse that recalls Chryses' walk in Book 1 and the two heralds sent to take Briseis.
---

“So the two went along the shore of the loud-roaring sea” (9.182). The verse takes up two walks of Book 1. Chryses, refused by Agamemnon, “went in silence along the shore of the loud-roaring sea” (1.34), and the two heralds sent to take Briseis from Achilles went, unwilling, “along the shore of the barren sea, and came to the huts and ships of the Myrmidons” (1.327–328), in the dual as here. The envoys too “came to the huts and ships of the Myrmidons” (9.185). The embassy goes over the ground where the quarrel began. Whether the echo of the two heralds bears on the duals of this passage is one of the questions of the book ([[the-embassy-to-achilles]], [[earth-holder]]). See [[sea]].
