summary: Cattle in Book 9 are wealth, feast and plunder. The men of the seven cities are rich in cattle, Phoenix's kinsmen slaughter shambling cattle with curving horns to keep him at home, and Achilles says that cattle can be had by raiding but a man's life cannot; in Phoenix's story the gods feast on hecatombs while Artemis is forgotten.
grc: [βόες]

  - 9.533-536 | The forgotten offering

**Wealth.** The men of the seven cities that Agamemnon offers Achilles are πολυβοῦται, “rich in cattle” (9.154 = 9.296; see [[rich-in-cattle]] and [[the-seven-cities]]), and they will honor him with gifts like a god. Cattle are the measure of a rich country, as they are of a rich man (see [[cattle]]).

**Plunder.** Achilles sets them first in his list of things that can be replaced: “For cattle and fat sheep can be had by raiding, and tripods can be won … but a man's soul cannot come back again” (9.406–408; see [[achilles-raids]], [[raiding]] and [[a-mans-soul-cannot-come-back]]). In Book 1 he said that the Trojans had never driven off his cattle or his horses, and so he had no quarrel of his own with them (1.154–156; see [[war-for-menelaus]]). Wars begin with cattle raids in the old men's stories: Nestor's war with the men of Elis began in “a quarrel over a cattle raid” (νεῖκος … ἀμφὶ βοηλασίῃ, 11.671–672), when he drove fifty herds of their cattle into Pylos (11.670–709).

**Feast and offering.** Phoenix's kinsmen, begging him to stay, slaughter “many fat sheep and shambling cattle with curving horns” (εἰλίποδας ἕλικας βοῦς, 9.466; see [[shambling]] and [[with-curving-horns]]). In his story of Meleager, Oeneus forgot Artemis at the harvest offering, though “the other gods feasted on hecatombs” (9.535; see [[hecatomb]] and [[sacrifice]]). The hecatomb, by its name a sacrifice of a hundred oxen, is the gods' share of the herds, and the goddess left without hers sent the boar (see [[the-calydonian-boar]]).
