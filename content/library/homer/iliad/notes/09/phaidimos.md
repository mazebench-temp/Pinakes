---
summary: Phoenix begins his speech with “shining Achilles,” the first time Achilles is called φαίδιμος in the poem.
grc: ["φαίδιμʼ"]
---

“If it is homecoming, shining Achilles, that you are turning over in your mind” (9.434; [[nostos]], [[aidelos]]). The vocative φαίδιμʼ Ἀχιλλεῦ, elided before the name, is used of Achilles four times more: by the Paeonian Asteropaeus and the Trojan Agenor, who stand against him, and by Athena before he kills Hector (21.160, 21.583, 22.216), and in the Odyssey by the shade of Agamemnon, speaking of his burial (Od. 24.76). Phoenix is the first to use it, and he uses it as the man who raised him. See [[achilles]] and [[phoenix]].
