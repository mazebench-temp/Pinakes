---
summary: The envoys come “to the huts and ships of the Myrmidons” in the verse that brought Agamemnon's heralds to take Briseis, and Achilles says that he will not think of war until Hector reaches those huts and ships (9.185, 9.652).
---

Μυρμιδόνων δʼ ἐπί τε κλισίας καὶ νῆας ἱκέσθην, “and they came to the huts and ships of the Myrmidons” (9.185), is the verse of the heralds' errand in Book 1 (1.328; see [[taking-of-briseis]]). Both errands end at the camp of Achilles' people, the heralds' who took Briseis away and the envoys' who offer her back (see [[the-embassy-to-achilles]]). Achilles' last answer turns the verse into a threat: he will not think of bloody war until Hector comes “to the huts and ships of the Myrmidons, / killing the Argives, and burns the ships with fire” (9.651–653; see [[achilles]] and [[hector]]). In his first answer the Myrmidons are the rowers who will take him home at dawn, “men eager to row” (9.358–361).

They stay out of the fighting until Book 16, when Achilles recalls that he would not end his wrath until the war reached his own ships (16.61–63), arms them, and sends them out under Patroclus (16.155–220).
