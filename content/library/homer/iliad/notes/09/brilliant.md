---
summary: δῖος honors Lycomedes among the captains of the watch, Odysseus five times in the embassy and its report, Achilles as host and giver, Hector twice in Achilles' mouth, and Dawn twice; once it is Artemis, “the divine child.”
en: [divine child]
---

**Odysseus.** The epithet follows Odysseus through the embassy: chosen by Nestor with “great Ajax and brilliant Odysseus” (9.169), he leads the way into Achilles' hut (9.192), though Nestor had told Phoenix to lead (9.168; [[the-embassy-to-achilles]]); he sees Ajax nod to Phoenix and speaks first (9.223); and “much-enduring brilliant Odysseus” makes the report (9.676; [[much-enduring]]).

**Achilles.** As host, “brilliant Achilles” leads the envoys in and carves the meat (9.199, 9.209; [[feast-type-scene]]); at the end of the night it is “brilliant Achilles” who gave Iphis to Patroclus when he took Scyros (9.667; [[iphis]]).

**Others.** Lycomedes, son of Creon, is “brilliant Lycomedes” among the captains of the watch (9.84; [[lycomedes]]). Achilles twice calls his enemy “brilliant Hector,” first when he refuses to fight him (9.356) and then when he names the moment he will (9.651; [[wise-hearted]]). Dawn is “brilliant” twice: Hector prays for her to come quickly (9.240), and Phoenix lies down and waits for her (9.662), in a verse nearly the same as the one in which Odysseus lies down on the night before he kills the suitors (Od. 19.50). Once the translation renders the word “divine”: Artemis, the daughter of Zeus, is δῖον γένος, “the divine child” (9.538; [[of-the-showering-arrows]]), a phrase Hesiod uses in addressing his brother Perses (*Works and Days* 299). See [[achilles]] and [[odysseus]].
