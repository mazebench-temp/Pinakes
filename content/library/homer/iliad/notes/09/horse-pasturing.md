---
summary: Odysseus fears that the Achaeans' fate may be to perish at Troy, “far from horse-pasturing Argos.”
---

“I am terribly afraid at heart of this, that the gods may fulfill his threats for him, and that it may be our fate to perish in Troy, far from horse-pasturing Argos” (9.244–246; [[argos]]). The epithet of the homeland stands at the end of Odysseus' account of the danger, before he appeals to Achilles to save the army. Earlier in the night Agamemnon spoke of the same Argos with another epithet, “Achaean Argos, the udder of the plowland” (9.141; [[udder-of-the-plowland]]).
