---
summary: In Book 9 Phthia is the home Achilles means to reach on the third day of sailing, the land from which Peleus sent him to Agamemnon with Phoenix, and the kingdom at whose far edge Phoenix ruled the Dolopians.
grc: [Φθίης]
passages:
  - 9.356-363 | Achilles will sail for Phthia
  - 9.478-484 | Peleus receives Phoenix
---

**Home.** Achilles tells Odysseus that at dawn he will load his ships, “and if the famous shaker of the earth grants me fair sailing, on the third day I would reach deep-soiled Phthia” (9.362–363; see [[deep-soiled]]). He has great possessions there, and Peleus will find him a wife among the Achaean women “throughout Hellas and Phthia” (9.364, 9.393–400; see [[hellas]] and [[peleus]]). In Book 1 he had threatened the same voyage (1.169–171).

**The day of leaving.** Odysseus and Phoenix both recall the day when Peleus sent Achilles off, in the same verse, “on that day when he sent you from Phthia to Agamemnon” (9.253 = 9.439): Odysseus to bring in Peleus' charge to keep his temper, Phoenix to tell how Peleus sent him with the boy to teach him (see [[odysseus]] and [[phoenix]]). In Book 11 Nestor recalls the same day with the same verse, and says that he and Odysseus were in Peleus' house to gather the army (11.766–770).

**The far edge.** Phoenix fled to “deep-soiled Phthia, mother of flocks” (9.479; see [[mother-of-flocks]]), and Peleus set him to rule the [[Dolopians]] “at the far edge of Phthia” (9.484; see [[the-flight-of-phoenix]]). Strabo took the verse to show that the Dolopians lay at the farthest bounds of Phthia, under the same lord (Strabo 9.5.11).
