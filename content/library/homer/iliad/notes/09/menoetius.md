---
summary: Menoetius is named only through his son, when Achilles bids Patroclus, “son of Menoetius”, mix the wine stronger for the envoys, and when Patroclus makes the fire for their meal (9.202, 9.211).
grc: [Μενοιτίου]
---

Achilles addresses Patroclus as Μενοιτίου υἱέ, “son of Menoetius” (9.202), and the narrator calls him Μενοιτιάδης as he kindles the fire (9.211; see [[son-of-menoetius]] and [[patroclus]]). With an epithet, the genitive and υἱός become one of Patroclus' standing titles from Book 11 on, Μενοιτίου ἄλκιμος υἱός, “the valiant son of Menoetius” (11.605, 11.814, 16.278).

The father is present in another way. The envoys come to remind Achilles of his own father's charge (9.252–259; see [[peleus]]), and in Book 11 Nestor reminds Patroclus of the charge Menoetius gave him on the same day, in Peleus' house: Achilles is higher in birth and far stronger, but Patroclus is the elder, and should give him good counsel (11.765–790).
