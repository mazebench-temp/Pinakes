summary: “As long as Meleager, dear to Ares, was fighting,” the Curetes could not hold their ground outside the walls of Calydon.
grc: ["ἄρηι φίλος"]
---

“Now as long as Meleager, dear to Ares, was fighting, so long it went badly for the Curetes” (9.550–551; [[meleager]], [[curetes]]). The text prints the epithet as two words, ἄρηι φίλος, with ἄρης lowercase, as war rather than the god ([[ares]]); elsewhere it is the single word ἀρηΐφιλος. The phrase that names Achilles in Book 2, idle by his ships while his men long for their leader “dear to Ares” (2.778), here names the hero of Phoenix's example while he still fights, before his anger takes him out of the war (9.553).
