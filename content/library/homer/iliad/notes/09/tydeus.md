---
summary: Tydeus is named only in his son's patronymic, when Nestor begins his praise of Diomedes, “Son of Tydeus, you are mighty in war above all others” (9.53).
---

In Book 4 Agamemnon measured the son against the father and found him “worse than he in battle, though better in assembly” (4.399–400; see [[tydeus-at-thebes]]). Diomedes has just reminded the king of that reproach (9.34–36), and Nestor answers it without naming it. He calls him by his father's name and grants him both halves of the comparison: “Son of Tydeus, you are mighty in war above all others, and in counsel you are the best among all men of your age” (9.53–54; see [[diomedes]], [[nestor]] and [[son-of-tydeus]]).
