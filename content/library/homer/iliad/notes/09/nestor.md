summary: Nestor turns the night from flight to the embassy. He praises Diomedes and completes his argument, proposes supper, a watch outside the wall and a council of the elders; at the feast he tells Agamemnon to his face that he wronged Achilles and must win him back, and he chooses the envoys and sends them off with his eyes on Odysseus.

**Praise and correction.** When the army has shouted for Diomedes, Nestor stands up: “Son of Tydeus, you are mighty in war above all others, and in counsel you are the best among all men of your age” (9.53–54). Agamemnon had called him worse than his father in battle and better in assembly (4.399–400); Nestor grants him both. But “you have not reached the end of the matter” (9.56): the young man could be Nestor's youngest son, and the old man will draw the conclusion, which no one will dishonor, “not even powerful Agamemnon” (9.57–62; see [[diomedes]], [[tydeus]] and the translation's note on 9.56).

**Without clan, without law.**

{{quote:9c26cdbe-1ad3-5f12-838d-1795aabc2911}}

The lines name no one; they set the strife inside the army, “war among his own people”, beside the war with Troy (see [[without-clan-law-or-hearth]]). ἀφρήτωρ and ἀνέστιος occur only here: the man has no φρήτρη, the clan by which Nestor told Agamemnon to marshal the army in Book 2 (2.362–363), no θέμιστες (“ordinances”; see [[themis]]) and no hearth. Aristotle quotes the first verse of the man who is by nature without a city, “at the same time eager for war” (*Politics* 1.2, 1253a5; see [[aristotle-man-without-a-city]]).

**The night.** Nestor bids the army “yield to black night” and make supper, in the verse with which Hector gave the Trojans their orders at sunset (9.65–66; 8.502–503; see [[yield-to-night]]), and sets sentries beside the ditch outside the wall (9.66–68; see [[night-watch]]). Agamemnon is to feast the elders and follow the best counsel, for the enemy's fires are near the ships (9.69–77): “This night will either shatter the army or save it” (9.78; see [[this-night-will-shatter-or-save]]). The army obeys, and his son Thrasymedes is the first captain named (9.79–81; see [[thrasymedes]]).

**The charge.** At the feast “the old man first of all began to weave a plan for them” (9.93), in the verses that brought in his plan of the wall (7.324–325; see [[boule]] and [[metis]]). He begins as a hymn begins with its god, “with you I shall end, and with you I shall begin”, and reminds the king that the scepter and the ordinances from Zeus oblige him to listen (9.96–102; see [[scepter]]). Then the charge: Agamemnon took Briseis against Nestor's advice (9.106–109; 1.275–276) and dishonored the best of men, “for you took his prize and keep it” (9.111). It is the verse in which Achilles, Thetis and Thersites spoke of the wrong (1.356, 1.507, 2.240), now said to the king's face. The remedy is “kindly gifts” and “soothing words” (9.113), and Agamemnon admits it all: “Old man, it is no lie, your account of my blind folly” (9.115; see [[agamemnon]]).

**The envoys.** Nestor answers the offer as if it had no last verses, the demand that Achilles submit (9.158–161): “the gifts you offer lord Achilles are no longer to be scorned” (9.164; see [[the-gifts-of-agamemnon]]). He picks the envoys himself, “Phoenix, dear to Zeus”, to lead, then great Ajax and Odysseus, with the heralds Odius and Eurybates, and calls for water and holy silence for a prayer to Zeus (9.165–172; see [[embassy]], [[phoenix]] and [[hand-washing]]). Why Phoenix is in Agamemnon's hut is not said (see the translation's note on 9.168–170).

**The last look.** “And the Gerenian horseman Nestor gave them many instructions, glancing at each of them, and at Odysseus most of all” (9.179–180; see [[gerenian-horseman]]). δενδίλλων, “glancing”, occurs only here; it is usually taken of signals made with the eyes. In the hut Odysseus speaks first, though Phoenix was to lead (9.223–225; see [[odysseus]]). When the embassy has failed, Nestor tries again through Patroclus, in the verse with which Odysseus recalled Peleus' charge (11.790 = 9.259), and proposes that Patroclus go out in Achilles' armor (11.794–803; see [[patroclus]]).
