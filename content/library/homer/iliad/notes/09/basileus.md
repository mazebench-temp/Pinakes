summary: Book 9 gives the word degrees. Nestor calls Agamemnon “the most kingly” and tells him to lead; Agamemnon demands that Achilles submit because he is “more kingly”; Achilles, who never hears the demand, tells him to choose a son-in-law “who is more kingly”, and offers Phoenix to “be king equally with me”.
grc: [βασιλεύτατός, βασιλεύτερός, βασιλεῦσι, βασίλευε]
en: [most kingly, more kingly, be king]
passages:
  - 9.157-161 | Agamemnon's demand that Achilles yield
  - 9.388-392 | Achilles refuses Agamemnon's daughter
---

**Degrees of kingship.** A noun has no degrees, but Homer gives βασιλεύς a comparative and a superlative, as if kingship were a quality one could have more or less of. The superlative occurs only here. Nestor, having told the young men to keep watch, turns to the king: “son of Atreus, you take the lead, for you are the most kingly” (σὺ γὰρ βασιλεύτατός ἐσσι, 9.69), and tells him to give the elders a feast and listen to their counsel (9.70–75; see [[kingship-and-authority]]). The rank is a reason for duties.

**More kingly.** Agamemnon uses the comparative as a claim. After the gifts he demands: “And let him submit to me, inasmuch as I am more kingly, and inasmuch as I claim to be the elder by birth” (9.160–161; see [[genee]]). Odysseus leaves the lines out when he repeats the offer (9.264–299; see [[message-repeated]]). Achilles answers them anyway. He will not marry Agamemnon's daughter, not if she rivaled Aphrodite in beauty: “Let him choose another of the Achaeans, one who befits him and who is more kingly” (9.391–392). Whether he guesses what Agamemnon said or simply knows the man, the word comes back to the king in scorn (see the translation's note on 9.158–161). The comparative occurs once more in the Iliad, later the same night, when Agamemnon tells Diomedes to choose the best companion for his spying and not the worse man out of respect for birth, “not even if he is more kingly” (10.237–239); he is afraid for Menelaus.

**Equal kingship.** Achilles answers Phoenix with the verb: “Be king equally with me, and take half my honor” (ἶσον ἐμοὶ βασίλευε, 9.616). Agamemnon offered seven cities and a kingdom under his scepter (9.149–156); Achilles offers his old tutor an equal share of his own (see [[moira]] and [[phoenix]]).

**The kings.** The plural keeps the council's rank in view. Nestor praises Diomedes for speaking wisely “to the kings of the Argives” (9.59). Achilles complains that the prizes Agamemnon gave “to the chiefs and the kings” are safe, while his was taken (9.334), and tells Odysseus to consider “with you and the other kings” how to save the ships (9.346–347). At the end “all the kings approved” Diomedes' word (9.710).
