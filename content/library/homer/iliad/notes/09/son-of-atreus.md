---
summary: Agamemnon is “son of Atreus” throughout the night, in the formal address of his counselors and in Achilles' bitter use of the name; once the plural joins the two brothers as husbands who love their wives.
---

**The address.** Diomedes' first words are a challenge in the assembly: “Son of Atreus, I will fight with you first, for your folly” (9.32). Nestor gives the same name the weight of office: “son of Atreus, you take the lead, for you are the most kingly” (9.69). The full address, “Most glorious son of Atreus, Agamemnon, lord of men,” is spoken four times: twice by Nestor in the council (9.96, 9.163), by Odysseus as he reports the refusal (9.677), and by Diomedes, who dismisses Achilles (9.697; [[most-glorious]], [[lord-of-men]]).

**In Achilles' mouth.** Achilles uses the patronymic as a mark of distance. “Neither, I think, will Agamemnon, son of Atreus, persuade me” (9.315); the son of Atreus stayed by the ships and took the spoil (9.331–333). He asks why the Argives must fight the Trojans: “Was it not for the sake of Helen of the lovely hair? Are they the only ones of mortal men who love their wives, the sons of Atreus?” (9.339–341; [[atreidae]], [[meropes]]). The plural Ἀτρεΐδαι, which elsewhere names the two kings as commanders, here names two husbands, and Achilles sets his love for Briseis beside theirs ([[thymares]]). To Phoenix he forbids “a favor to the hero son of Atreus” (9.613), and to Ajax he recalls “how he treated me with contempt among the Argives, the son of Atreus, as if I were some dishonored vagrant” (9.647–648).
