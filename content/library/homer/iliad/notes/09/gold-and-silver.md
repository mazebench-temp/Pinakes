**The offer.** Agamemnon's list begins with metal: seven tripods untouched by fire, "ten talents of gold" and twenty gleaming cauldrons (9.122–123), and his horses have won prizes enough that their owner would not be "lacking in precious gold" (ἐριτίμοιο χρυσοῖο, 9.126). When Troy falls Achilles may load his ship "full with gold and bronze" (9.137). Odysseus repeats every item (9.264–268, 9.279; see [[the-gifts-of-agamemnon]] and [[message-repeated]]).

**The answer.** Achilles has his own: he will carry home "other gold, and red bronze" won at Troy (9.365), and his refusal heaps up wealth beyond counting, ten times and twenty times all Agamemnon has, all that comes in to Orchomenus and to Thebes in Egypt, where the most treasures lie stored in the houses, as much as the sand and the dust (9.379–385; see [[thebes-in-egypt]] and [[orchomenus]]). Nothing is worth his life (9.401).

**Gold and silver at the feast.** The lyre on which Achilles sings has a crossbar of silver, ἀργύρεον ζυγόν (9.187; see [[lyre]]), and the Achaeans pledge the returning envoys "with golden cups" (χρυσέοισι κυπέλλοις, 9.670). The gods keep their gold: "golden Aphrodite" (9.389; see [[golden]]) and "Artemis of the golden throne" (9.533; see [[golden-throned]]).
