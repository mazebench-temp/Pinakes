summary: The young men fill the mixing bowls for the libation before the envoys set out (9.175), and Achilles welcomes them with “a larger mixing bowl” and stronger wine (9.202–203).
---

Before the envoys leave Agamemnon's hut, “the young men filled the mixing bowls brim-full with drink” (9.175), the verse of the feast at Chryse (1.470), and the wine is served round for the libation (see [[cupbearing]] and [[libation]]). In Achilles' hut the bowl itself is a welcome. His first words to Patroclus are “Set out a larger mixing bowl, son of Menoetius, and mix the wine stronger” (μείζονα δὴ κρητῆρα, 9.202–203), because the dearest of men are under his roof (9.204; see [[wine]] and [[feast-type-scene]]). The size of the bowl and the strength of the mixture measure the honor done to the guests.
