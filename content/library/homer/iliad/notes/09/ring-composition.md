summary: The book is framed by the same verses at its start and end: a proposal that begins “Come then, let us all do as I say”, a silence, and Diomedes speaking “at last” (9.26–31, 9.693–704). Inside the frame the embassy leaves Agamemnon's hut and comes back to it, and Phoenix's speech and his story of Meleager are built as rings.
refs: [9.26-31, 9.693-704]
---

**The frame of the book.** Agamemnon ends his proposal to flee with the verse of his test in Book 2, “Come then, let us all do as I say” (9.26 = 2.139), and the army falls silent: “So he spoke, and they all fell hushed in silence. For a long time the sons of the Achaeans were silent in their grief; at last Diomedes, good at the war cry, spoke among them” (9.29–31). When Odysseus has reported Achilles' refusal, the three verses return (9.693–696; see [[silence]]), Diomedes speaks again, and he gives his own orders in Agamemnon's verse: “Come then, let us all do as I say” (9.704). The same words that opened the night with a call to flight close it with a call to sleep and to fight at dawn, and the same man answers both. His first speech is met with the army's shout (9.50–51), his last with the kings' approval (9.710–711; see [[so-he-spoke]]).

**The journey.** The envoys set out from Agamemnon's hut after a libation and come back to it, and they leave Achilles' hut after a libation too (9.174–178, 9.656–657, 9.669; see [[embassy]] and [[libation]]). The walk to Achilles is told in the verses of Book 1, and the return is told in the verse that brought the chiefs to Agamemnon after the duel in Book 7 (9.185 = 1.328; 9.669 = 7.313; see [[messenger-scene]]).

**Phoenix's speech.** Phoenix begins with Achilles and his own bond to him (9.434–495), widens to the gods and the Prayers (9.496–523), goes back to the men of old and Meleager (9.524–599), and returns to Achilles: “But do not have such thoughts in your mind” (9.600–605; see [[phoenix]] and [[mythological-paradigm]]). The story of Meleager is itself told in a ring, beginning with the war, going back to its cause in Oeneus' forgotten offering and the boar, and returning to the war (9.529–549; see [[the-wrath-of-meleager]]).
