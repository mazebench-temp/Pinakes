summary: Three mothers shape the sons of Book 9. Thetis has told Achilles of his two fates; Phoenix's mother begged him to lie with his father's concubine, and he obeyed; and Althaea, grieving for her brother, cursed her son Meleager and later joined the women who begged him to fight.

  - 9.566-572 | Althaea's curse
---

**Thetis.** Achilles' knowledge of his life comes from his mother: “For my mother, the goddess silver-footed Thetis, tells me that two different fates are carrying me toward the end of death” (9.410–411; see [[thetis]] and [[the-two-fates-of-achilles]]). In Book 1 she told him his life was short and wept for him (1.414–418); here her prophecy gives him a choice. In Book 18 she will tell him again, when he has made it (18.95–96).

**Phoenix's mother.** Phoenix's ruin began with his mother's plea. His father had taken a concubine and dishonored his wife, “and she kept begging me by my knees to lie with the concubine first, so that she would hate the old man. I obeyed her and did it” (9.451–453). The verb is the suppliant's, λισσέσκετο, and the son's obedience brought his father's curse on him (see [[the-flight-of-phoenix]] and [[curses]]).

**Althaea.** Meleager's anger is directed at his mother. Althaea “prayed long in her grief over the killing of her brother”, beat the earth with her hands, kneeling, her bosom wet with tears, calling on Hades and Persephone “to give death to her son”, and the Erinys heard her (9.566–572; see [[althaea]] and [[the-death-of-meleager]]). Homer does not tell how her brother died, or whether her son died of her curse; the curse implies both. Yet when the city is burning his “lady mother” is among those who beg him to fight (9.584–585). The mother who cursed him asks him to save her.

**A mother's grief in a name.** Cleopatra was called Alcyone by her parents, “because her mother, having the lot of the halcyon bird of many sorrows, wept that the far-working Phoebus Apollo had snatched her away” (9.562–564; see [[marpessa]] and [[cleopatra]]). The daughter carries her mother's lament in her name.
