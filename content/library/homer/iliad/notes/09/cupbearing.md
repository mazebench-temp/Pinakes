---
summary: In Agamemnon's hut the young men fill the mixing bowls and serve the wine for the libation before the embassy sets out, in the verses of the feast at Chryse (9.175–176 = 1.470–471); in Achilles' hut the hosts serve their guests themselves.
---

“And the young men filled the mixing bowls brim-full with drink, and served it out to all, first pouring drops for the libation into the cups” (9.175–176). The two verses are those of the feast at Chryse (1.470–471), and the first returns in the Odyssey when the suitors sit down in Odysseus' hall (Od. 1.148). The serving belongs to the young (κοῦροι), here in the king's hut (see [[mixing-bowl]] and [[libation]]).

In Achilles' hut the hosts serve. Patroclus is told to set out the bowl and mix the wine, and obeys; Automedon holds the meat for the carving; and Patroclus and Achilles serve the bread and the meat with their own hands (9.202–205, 9.209, 9.216–217; see [[feast-type-scene]]). The service is part of the honor: “these are the dearest of men, and they are under my roof” (9.204).
