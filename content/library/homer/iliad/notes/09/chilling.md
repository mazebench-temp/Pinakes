---
summary: The book opens with “chilling Rout,” and Nestor calls civil war “that chilling thing,” in the variant form ὀκρυόεις.
grc: [κρυόεντος, ὀκρυόεντος]
---

**Rout.** “The Achaeans were held by heaven-sent Panic, companion of chilling Rout” (9.1–2; [[rout]], [[wondrous]]). It is the second and last use of κρυόεις in Homer, after Onslaught on the aegis (5.740). The cold of fear is the first thing said of the Achaeans on this night.

**Civil war.** Nestor answers Diomedes' defiance with a warning: “Without clan, without law, without hearth is the man who loves war among his own people, that chilling thing” (9.63–64; [[without-clan-law-or-hearth]]). The form ὀκρυόεις is usually explained as κρυόεις wrongly divided from a preceding genitive in -οο, as with Helen's ὀκρυοέσσης (6.344); the translation renders both “chilling.” The two uses frame the same evening: the panic of an army beaten by the enemy, and the danger of an army turning on itself. See [[polemos]].
