---
summary: The goat of Book 15 is the wild goat that dogs and country men chase with the stag, until a rock or a forest saves it and a lion turns the hunters back (15.271–276).
---

ἢ ἔλαφον κεραὸν ἢ ἄγριον αἶγα, “an antlered stag or a wild goat” (15.271). ἄγριος αἴξ is the wild goat or ibex of the mountains, and αἴξ, usually feminine, is masculine here, as in the same half-verse of Book 3, where the hungry lion finds the carcass of one (3.24; see [[lion-on-a-carcass]]). Pandarus' bow was made from the horns of a wild goat he had shot himself (4.105–111). In Book 15 the goat is one of the two beasts the hunters cannot catch, “but a lofty rock or a shady forest shelters it” (15.273–274), and the hunt ends when a bearded lion appears in their path (15.275–276; see [[the-hunters-and-the-lion]] and [[deer]]).
