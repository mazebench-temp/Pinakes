---
summary: “Ox-eyed queen Hera” shudders at Zeus' threats, and Zeus addresses her with the formula as he tells her to bring Iris and Apollo.
---

“So he spoke, and ox-eyed queen Hera shuddered, / and she spoke to him, addressing him with winged words” (15.34–35; [[hera]], [[zeus]], [[queenly]], [[winged-words]]). In Book 1, after Zeus had threatened her, “So he spoke, and ox-eyed queen Hera was afraid” (1.568); here the verb is ῥίγησεν, and she answers with an oath (15.36–46; [[oath]]). When she has sworn, Zeus uses the formula to her in the nominative of address: “If from now on, ox-eyed queen Hera, / you were to sit among the immortals thinking as I do” (15.49–50; [[immortals]]). The deceiver of Book 14 is addressed with her title of dignity as she is put back in her place.
