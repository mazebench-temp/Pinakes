summary: Hector kills Schedius, son of Perimedes, “leader of the Phocians”, after the leaders' exhortations at the ships (15.515–516).
---

ἔνθʼ Ἕκτωρ μὲν ἕλε Σχεδίον Περιμήδεος υἱὸν / ἀρχὸν Φωκήων, “Then Hector killed Schedius, son of Perimedes, / leader of the Phocians” (15.515–516; see [[schedius-son-of-perimedes]] and [[hector]]). The Phocian leaders of the Catalogue are Schedius and Epistrophus, sons of Iphitus (2.517–518; see [[schedius]] and [[epistrophus]]). This Schedius has another father, and in Book 17 Hector kills the son of Iphitus, Φωκήων ὄχʼ ἄριστον, by far the best of the Phocians (17.306–311). The poem names two Phocian leaders called Schedius, and Hector kills both.
