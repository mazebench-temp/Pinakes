---
summary: Iris and Apollo find “the far-seeing son of Cronus” on the peak of Gargarus, and Hector claims that “far-seeing Zeus” once harmed the Trojans' minds and now urges them on.
---

“And they found the far-seeing son of Cronus on the peak of Gargarus, / sitting, and a fragrant cloud was wreathed around him” (15.152–153; [[iris]], [[apollo]], [[gargarus]], [[son-of-cronus]], [[fragrant]]). The verse begins as Thetis found him on Olympus in Book 1, εὗρεν δʼ εὐρύοπα Κρονίδην (1.498), and as Iris and Thetis will find him in Book 24 (24.98). The god who in Book 14 saw nothing of what was done is awake and watching. At the end of the book Hector, holding the stern of a ship, says: “but if far-seeing Zeus was harming our minds then, / now he himself is urging us on and commanding us” (15.724–725; [[hector]], [[elders]]), when the elders held him back (15.721–723).
