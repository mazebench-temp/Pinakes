---
summary: Hector makes for a ship as “a tawny eagle swoops on a tribe of winged birds”; the book adds the phrase αἰετὸς αἴθων.
grc: ["αἰετὸς αἴθων"]
---

“Nor did Hector / stay in the throng of the close-armored Trojans; / but as a tawny eagle swoops on a tribe of winged birds / as they feed beside a river, / geese or cranes or long-necked swans, / so Hector made straight for a dark-prowed ship” (15.688–693; [[hector]], [[eagle]], [[long-necked]], [[dark-prowed]], [[close-armored]]). αἴθων, “fiery, tawny,” names the color of lions and horses, and of the iron of the smith; here it is given to the eagle, for the only time in the Iliad. The phrase αἰετὸς αἴθων is new to the entry; the bare form αἴθων is avoided, since Αἴθων is also the name of one of Hector's horses (8.185).
