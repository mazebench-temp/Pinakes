---
summary: When Zeus tells of Hera's storm against Heracles, he leaves out the occasion that Sleep gave it, the voyage home from the sack of Troy (15.24–30; 14.250–251). In the battle at the ships three grandsons of Laomedon, the king whose city Heracles sacked, are killed.
passages:
  - 15.24-30 | Zeus' account of the storm
---

**The voyage home.** Sleep dated Hera's storm by the first sack of Troy: “on the day when that high-hearted son of Zeus / was sailing from Ilios, when he had sacked the city of the Trojans” (14.250–251). When Zeus tells the same storm in Book 15, he leaves the occasion out. Hera sent Heracles “over the barren sea” and carried him off to Cos, and Zeus brought him back to Argos, “though he had suffered many labors” (15.26–30; see [[hera-drives-heracles-to-cos]] and [[heracles]]). Troy is not named.

**Laomedon's line.** The Iliad does not say what became of Laomedon's house when Heracles took the city. His sons are alive in the poem: Lampus, Clytius and Hicetaon sit with Priam among the elders on the wall (3.146–147; 20.236–238; see [[laomedon]], [[lampus]], [[clytius]] and [[hicetaon]]). In Book 15 three of their sons, Hector's cousins, die at the ships. Ajax kills Caletor, son of Clytius, as he brings fire to a ship (15.419–423; see [[caletor]]); Menelaus kills Dolops, “the son of Lampus, the strongest son that Lampus, / son of Laomedon, fathered” (15.525–543; see [[dolops-son-of-lampus]]); and Antilochus kills Melanippus, son of Hicetaon (15.575–578; see [[melanippus-son-of-hicetaon]]).
