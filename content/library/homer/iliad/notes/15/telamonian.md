---
summary: Thoas hoped that Hector had died “at the hands of Ajax, son of Telamon,” Zeus takes the triumph from “Teucer, son of Telamon,” and “great Telamonian Ajax” answers his brother and urges on the Argives.
---

**Ajax.** Thoas, seeing Hector risen: “Truly the heart of each of us hoped / that he had died at the hands of Ajax, son of Telamon” (15.288–289; [[thoas]], [[ajax]], [[hector]]), with the patronymic Τελαμωνιάδαο. The fuller formula, μέγας Τελαμώνιος Αἴας, introduces two of his speeches: his answer to Teucer when the bowstring breaks, “Then great Telamonian Ajax answered him” (15.471; [[teucer]], [[my-dear]]), and his call to shame, “And great Telamonian Ajax urged on the Argives” (15.560; [[aidos]], [[exhortation]]).

**Teucer.** “But he did not escape the shrewd mind of Zeus, who was guarding / Hector, and he took the triumph away from Teucer, son of Telamon” (15.461–462; [[zeus]], [[shrewd-word]]). The adjective that usually names Ajax names his half-brother here, as it did when Agamemnon praised him in Book 8 and when he made the first kill of the battle at the ships in Book 13 (8.281, 13.170). See [[telamon]].
