---
summary: Apollo goes before the Trojans “holding the aegis, highly prized,” and throws down the wall of the Achaeans.
---

“There they poured forward in battalions, and before them Apollo, / holding the aegis, highly prized; and he threw down the wall of the Achaeans / very easily” (15.360–362; [[apollo]], [[aegis]], [[the-achaean-wall]]). The verse begins as the one of Athena going through the army with the aegis in Book 2, “holding the aegis, highly prized, ageless and immortal” (2.447): the same prized possession of Zeus is carried by each of his children for the side each favors. The simile that follows, of a boy scattering sand on the shore, measures how easily the wall falls (15.362–366; [[ieios]]).
