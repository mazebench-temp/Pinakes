**The sea-spear.** When the Trojans come over the wall, the Achaeans fight them from the ships “with the long spears that lay ready for them on the ships, / for fighting at sea, jointed, clad in bronze at the tip” (ξυστοῖσι … ναύμαχα κολλήεντα, 15.388–389; see [[ships]]). Later Ajax takes one of them onto the decks:

{{quote:847327a8-d1b5-5899-b18e-d57395a7e027}}

ξυστόν is a pike, a polished shaft (4.469, 13.497), and the one for fighting at sea is made in lengths joined with bands, βλήτροισι, a word found only here (see the translation's note on 15.389, 15.677–678). It is twice as long as the spear of eleven cubits that Hector holds in Paris' house and at the Trojans' assembly by night (6.319, 8.494). With it Ajax keeps the Trojans from the ships, and when he is driven back to the seven-foot bench, “with his spear he always / kept the Trojans from the ships, whoever brought tireless fire” (15.730–731), until he has stabbed twelve men in front of the ships “with his long spear” (15.745–746; see [[ajax]] and [[the-light-is-in-our-hands]]). In the next book Hector strikes the spear behind the point with his sword and shears the point away, and Ajax knows the work of the gods (16.114–121).

**Spears in the battle.** The book's first spear is a god's: Athena takes Ares' helmet and shield, and “the spear from his strong hand”, the bronze spear, and stands it aside (15.125–127; see [[ares]]). When Apollo leads the Trojans with the aegis, the spears thrown from bold hands are fixed in the flesh of young men, “and many, too, in between, before they could reach the white flesh, / stood fixed in the ground, longing to glut themselves on flesh” (15.316–317), the verses that Ajax's shield drew in Book 11 (11.573–574). Aristotle quotes the line among Homer's lifeless things made alive, with the spear-point that “sped through his chest in its fury” (αἰχμὴ … μαιμώωσα, 15.542) when Menelaus kills Dolops (*Rhetoric* 3.11, 1411b31–1412a4; see [[homers-lifeless-things-alive]]). Apollo bridges the ditch with a path as wide “as far as the cast of a spear / goes, when a man throws it to test his strength” (15.358–359; see [[the-cast-of-a-spear]]).

**From bow to spear.** Teucer's bowstring breaks, and Ajax tells him to let bow and arrows lie and to take “a long spear in your hands, and a shield on your shoulder” (15.474). He arms with a shield, a helmet with a horsehair crest, and “a strong spear, tipped with sharp bronze” (15.479–482; see [[arming-scene]], [[bow]] and [[teucer]]). In the last fighting of the book the spearmen come so close that they no longer stand apart to await arrows or javelins, but fight with axes and swords and “double-pointed spears” (15.707–712; see [[axe]] and [[double-pointed]]).
