---
summary: Routed by Apollo, the Achaeans flee “plunging into the ditch they had dug and the stakes,” and go inside the wall under compulsion.
---

“While these were stripping them of their armor, the Achaeans meanwhile, / plunging into the ditch they had dug and the stakes, / fled this way and that, and went inside the wall under compulsion” (15.343–345; [[the-achaean-wall]], [[stripping-the-dead]]). In Book 12 Polydamas feared that if the Achaeans turned back on them, the Trojans would “plunge into the ditch they have dug” (12.72; [[polydamas]]); here the Achaeans fall into their own ditch, and a few verses later Apollo kicks its banks into it and bridges a road for the Trojan chariots (15.355–359; [[apollo]]).
