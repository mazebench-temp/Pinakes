---
summary: Patroclus, sitting with the wounded Eurypylus, spreads on his wound “healing drugs against the black pains.”
---

“And Patroclus, as long as the Achaeans and Trojans / were fighting around the wall, outside the swift ships, / so long sat in the hut of manly Eurypylus / and cheered him with talk, and on the grievous wound / he spread healing drugs against the black pains” (15.390–394; [[patroclus]], [[eurypylus]], [[manly]], [[grim]], [[healing]]). The line-end is the one in which Agamemnon promised the wounded Menelaus that a healer would lay on his wound “drugs that will stop the black pains” (4.190–191), and Patroclus has already cut the arrow from Eurypylus' thigh and laid on it a root that “stayed all his pains” (11.844–848). He stays while the battle is at the wall; when he sees the Trojans rush it, he leaves to go to Achilles (15.395–404).
