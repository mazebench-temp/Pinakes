“And he held the furious aegis, / dreadful, shaggy all around, conspicuous, which the smith / Hephaestus gave to Zeus to carry, to put men to rout” (15.308–310; [[aegis]], [[apollo]], [[furious]], [[shaggy-all-around]], [[hephaestus]]). Three epithets stand in one verse, and ἀριπρεπής, “standing out,” is rendered “conspicuous”: the aegis is seen, as the stars are when the upper air is windless (8.556). The word that praises a man who stands out among his people (6.477) here praises a terror that cannot be missed. The elided form ἀριπρεπέʼ is new to the entry.
