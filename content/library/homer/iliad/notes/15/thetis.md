---
summary: Thetis does not appear, but Zeus' plan runs on her request of Book 1. He will let no god help the Danaans until Achilles' wish is fulfilled, as he promised on the day she clasped his knees, and he drives the Trojans to the ships to fulfill her “excessive prayer” in full.
---

**The day she clasped his knees.** At the end of his forecast Zeus says that he will not cease from his anger, nor let any other god defend the Danaans, “until the wish of the son of Peleus is fulfilled, / as I promised him at first, and nodded my head to it, / on the day when the goddess Thetis clasped my knees, / begging me to honor Achilles, sacker of cities” (15.74–77; see [[zeus]] and [[thetis-supplicates-zeus]]). Twelve days after the quarrel Thetis sat before him on Olympus, took his knees with her left hand and his chin with her right, and asked him to give strength to the Trojans until the Achaeans honored her son (1.493–510; see [[supplication]]). He nodded, and Olympus shook (1.524–530). Achilles' own wish, which she carried, was that Zeus should “pen the Achaeans in among their ships' sterns and along the sea / as they are slaughtered” (1.409–410; see [[achilles]]); in Book 15 the Trojans fight at the sterns (15.385). οἱ in “as I promised him” may be Achilles or Thetis (see the translation's note on 15.56–77).

**The excessive prayer.** When the Trojans rush on the ships, Zeus' heart wishes to give Hector glory, “so that on the curved ships he might cast wondrous fire, / tireless fire, and fulfill the excessive prayer of Thetis / in full; for Zeus the counselor was waiting for this, / to see with his eyes the blaze of a burning ship” (15.596–600; see [[plan-of-zeus]] and [[prayer]]). ἐξαίσιον ἀρήν: ἀρή is “prayer”, and ἐξαίσιος, found only here in the Iliad, is what goes beyond measure, beyond one's due (αἶσα; see [[aisa]] and the translation's note on 15.598). The narrator, not Zeus, judges the prayer excessive. When a ship has burned, Zeus will turn the battle back against the Trojans (15.601–602).
