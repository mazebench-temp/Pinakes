---
summary: Iris flies from Ida as swiftly as snow or hail “under the rush of the North Wind, born of the clear sky.”
---

“As when snow or hail flies from the clouds, / cold, under the rush of the North Wind, born of the clear sky, / so swiftly swift Iris flew in her eagerness” (15.170–172; [[iris]], [[boreas]], [[snow-and-hail]], [[wind-footed]]). The simile measures the messenger's speed by the snow driven before the North Wind, and the epithet names the clear, cold sky from which that wind comes. The same verse returns in Book 19, of the snow to which the helmets pouring from the ships are compared (19.358). The North Wind has already helped Hera drive Heracles over the sea in Zeus' story at the start of the book (15.26; [[hera-drives-heracles-to-cos]]).
