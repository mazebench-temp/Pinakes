---
summary: “Far-working Apollo” speaks to the fallen Hector and, as “far-working lord Apollo,” names himself as the helper Zeus has sent.
---

“And standing near him far-working Apollo spoke to him: / ‘Hector, son of Priam, why is it that you, apart from the others, / sit here with little strength left?’” (15.243–245; [[apollo]], [[hector]], [[son-of-priam]]). Hector does not know him, and asks, “Who are you, good sir, among the gods” (15.247; [[good-sir]]). The answer begins with the fuller verse: “Then far-working lord Apollo spoke to him in turn: / ‘Take heart now: such a helper has the son of Cronus / sent forth from Ida’” (15.253–255; [[son-of-cronus]], [[of-the-golden-sword]]). The god who works from afar comes close to the man he saves.
