---
summary: In Book 15 the θυμός of an army rises and falls with Hector. His own spirit gathers again as he revives; the Achaeans' hearts sink to their feet at the sight of him and are bewitched when Apollo shakes the aegis; the heart in their breasts is torn as he falls on them; and at the end both armies fight “with one heart”. Among the gods the θυμός is the seat of anger, grief and threat.
passages:
  - 15.277-299 | The Achaeans see Hector restored
---

**Life returning.** In Book 14 the stone still overpowered Hector's spirit as he lay by the river (14.439; see the note on Book 14). When Apollo finds him he is “sitting up, no longer lying down, and he was just gathering his spirit again” (νέον δʼ ἐσαγείρετο θυμόν, 15.240), for the mind of Zeus had roused him (15.242; see [[hector]] and [[noos]]). The θυμός is the life that can be lost or taken: Teucer would have “taken away his life” had Zeus not broken the bowstring (ἐξείλετο θυμόν, 15.460; see [[bow]]).

**Fear and hope.** The Achaeans have been driving the Trojans, “but when they saw Hector going along the ranks of men, / they were afraid, and the hearts of all sank to their feet” (πᾶσιν δὲ παραὶ ποσὶ κάππεσε θυμός, 15.279–280; see [[the-hunters-and-the-lion]] and [[deos]]). Thoas says that “the heart of each of us hoped” that Hector had died (15.288), and that he will now be afraid in his heart to plunge into the Danaans (15.299). Apollo shakes the aegis and bewitches “their hearts / in their breasts” (θυμὸν … ἔθελξε, 15.321–322; see [[aegis]]), and Zeus, rousing the Trojans, “bewitched the heart / of the Argives” (θέλγε δὲ θυμόν, 15.594). When Hector falls on them like a wave on a ship, “the heart in the breasts of the Achaeans was torn” (ἐδαΐζετο θυμός, 15.629; see [[the-ship-in-the-storm]]). At the end the two armies are matched heart against heart: the Achaeans think they will perish, “and the heart in the breast of each of the Trojans hoped / to set fire to the ships” (15.700–702), and they fight hand to hand around Protesilaus' ship, “with one heart” (ἕνα θυμὸν ἔχοντες, 15.710). The exhortations work on the same organ, urging on “the might and spirit of every man” (15.500, 15.514, 15.667; see [[menos]] and [[exhortation]]) and bidding men “set shame in your hearts” (15.561, 15.661; see [[aidos]]).

**The gods' hearts.** Zeus' grief for Heracles did not let go of his heart (15.24–25; see [[heracles]]); Hera tells Themis what kind of heart he has, “overbearing and harsh” (15.94); and Zeus, seeing Iris and Apollo come quickly at Hera's word, “was not angry in his heart” (15.155; see [[cholos]]). Hera swears that it is Poseidon's own heart that urges him on (15.43; see [[oath]]), and Poseidon, yielding, feels “this dread grief” come upon his heart and spirit (15.208; see [[achos]]) and threatens Zeus “from my heart” (15.212). In the end the θυμός of Zeus decides the battle: “his heart wished to hold out glory to Hector” until a ship should burn (15.596; see [[plan-of-zeus]]).
