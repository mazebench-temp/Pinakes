summary: Hera returns to Olympus and finds the gods gathered at a feast in the house of Zeus. She bids Themis begin “the gods' equal share of the feast”, and warns that no one, god or mortal, will feast in good cheer when they hear what Zeus intends; then she tells Ares of his son's death.
grc: [δαίνυται]
en: [feasts in good cheer]
passages:
  - 15.84-103 | Hera among the feasting gods
---

The gods are gathered in the house of Zeus when Hera comes from Ida, and they spring up to greet her with their cups; she takes Themis' cup (15.84–91; see [[cup]] and [[themis-the-goddess]]). Themis asks what has frightened her. Hera will not say: “But you begin the gods' equal share of the feast in the halls; / and you will hear, with all the immortals, / what evil deeds Zeus is making known; and I do not think / that the hearts of all alike will rejoice, either among mortals / or among gods, if anyone still feasts in good cheer now” (15.95–99; see [[hera]]). The δαὶς ἐΐση, the feast in which each has his fair share (see the article above), is the gods' order at its most peaceful, and Hera opens it with bad news. The feast of the gods in Book 1 ended a quarrel in laughter (1.595–611; see [[laughter-of-the-gods]]); this one begins with Hera's laugh “with her lips” over brows that are not warmed (15.101–103), and breaks up in the grief and rage of Ares (15.110–142; see [[ares]]).
