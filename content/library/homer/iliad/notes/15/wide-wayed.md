---
summary: The Trojans come over the wall as “a great wave of the wide-wayed sea” comes down over the sides of a ship.
---

“And as a great wave of the wide-wayed sea / comes down over the sides of a ship, when it is driven on / by the force of the wind; for that above all swells the waves: / so the Trojans with a great shout went down over the wall” (15.381–384; [[trojans]], [[the-achaean-wall]], [[sea]], [[ships]]). It is the only use of the epithet in the Iliad. The wall is the ship's side, and the Trojans pour over it as the sea pours over a hull; later in the book the same image returns, when Hector falls on the Achaeans “as when a wave falls on a swift ship” (15.624; [[wind-nurtured]], [[swift-ships]]).
