---
summary: Poseidon complains that Zeus rebukes a brother “ordained to the same portion as himself” and praises the messenger who “knows what is due”; in a simile the hunters chasing a stag find that “it is not their lot to catch it”.
---

The word and its adjective αἴσιμος appear three times in the book, each time in a different sense of the share. In the dispute between the brothers it is the portion fixed for each: Poseidon is ὁμῇ πεπρωμένον αἴσῃ, “ordained to the same portion” as Zeus (15.209; see [[moira]] and [[the-lots-of-the-three-brothers]]). In his praise of Iris it is the measure of what is right, αἴσιμα, “what is due” (15.207; see [[a-messenger-who-knows-what-is-due]]). In the simile of the stag, which a rock or a shady forest shelters from the dogs and the country men, “it is not their lot to catch it” (αἴσιμον, 15.274), until a lion appears in their path and turns them all back, as Hector, revived, turns the Danaans (15.271–280; see [[the-hunters-and-the-lion]]).
