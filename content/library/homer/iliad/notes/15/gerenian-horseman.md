---
summary: Nestor, “the Gerenian, the guardian of the Achaeans,” prays above all the others at the ships and later begs each man by his parents to stand firm.
---

The verse Νέστωρ αὖτε μάλιστα Γερήνιος οὖρος Ἀχαιῶν introduces both of Nestor's acts in the book, with Γερήνιος alone in place of the usual Γερήνιος ἱππότα. When the Achaeans are driven back to the ships and pray, “above all Nestor, the Gerenian, the guardian of the Achaeans, / prayed, stretching out his hands to the starry sky” (15.370–371; [[nestor]], [[prayer]], [[starry]]). And when they give way from the first row of ships, “above all Gerenian Nestor, the guardian of the Achaeans, / begged each man by his parents, imploring him” (15.659–660; [[supplication]], [[aidos]]). The old man prays to the god and supplicates his own men. See [[guardian-of-the-achaeans]].
