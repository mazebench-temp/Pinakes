summary: Polydamas kills Mecisteus in the rout behind Apollo and the aegis, and Otus of Cyllene at the ships. When Teucer's arrow kills his charioteer Cleitus, he is the first to reach the runaway horses and gives them to Astynous; when Meges springs at him, Apollo will not let “the son of Panthous” fall.

**Mecisteus.** In the rout that follows Apollo's shaking of the aegis, “Polydamas killed Mecisteus” (15.339; see [[mecisteus-son-of-echius]]), the son of Echius who twice carried a wounded companion back to the ships, Teucer in Book 8 and Hypsenor in Book 13 (8.332–334, 13.421–423). He is one of six Trojan leaders who kill eight Achaeans as the battle scatters (15.328–342; see [[trojans]]).

**His charioteer.** Teucer's arrow strikes Cleitus, “the companion of Polydamas, the noble son of Panthous” (15.446; see [[cleitus]] and [[teucer]]), as he drives into the thick of the fighting, and the horses bolt with the empty chariot. “And the lord was quickest to notice, / Polydamas, and was the first to come to meet the horses” (15.453–454). He gives them to Astynous, son of Protiaon, tells him to keep them close, and goes back among the champions (15.455–457; see [[astynous-son-of-protiaon]]). When Hector lost his charioteers in Book 8, he found another each time, Archeptolemus and then Cebriones (8.124–129, 8.316–319; see [[archeptolemus]] and [[cebriones]]).

**Otus.** At the ships he slays and strips Otus of Cyllene, the companion of Meges and a leader of the Epeians (15.518–519; see [[otus-of-cyllene]] and [[meges]]). Meges springs at him, “but he ducked under and slipped aside” (15.520), as he darted aside from Ajax's spear in Book 14 (14.462–463), “for Apollo / would not let the son of Panthous be overpowered among the champions” (15.521–522; see [[apollo]] and [[panthous]]). Meges' spear finds Croesmus instead (15.523; see [[croesmus]]). The god who revives Hector in this book, and who has protected him “before now too” (15.256), keeps his companion from harm as well.
