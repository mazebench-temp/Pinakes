Μηκιστῆ δʼ ἕλε Πουλυδάμας, Ἐχίον δὲ Πολίτης / πρώτῃ ἐν ὑσμίνῃ, “And Polydamas killed Mecisteus, and Polites Echius, / in the front line of battle” (15.339–340; see [[polydamas]], [[polites]] and [[echius-killed-by-polites]]). He dies in the rout that follows Apollo's shaking of the aegis (15.318–342; see [[aegis]] and [[androktasia]]). No other Achaean Mecisteus appears in the Iliad's battles, and this is presumably the bearer who, with Alastor, carried the wounded Teucer and then Hypsenor to the ships, in the same four verses each time (8.331–334 = 13.420–423; see [[alastor-who-carries-teucer]], [[teucer]] and [[hypsenor-son-of-hippasus]]). No one carries him: the Trojans strip the dead, and the Achaeans flee across the ditch and inside the wall (15.343–345). The Echius whom Polites kills in the same verse shares the name of Mecisteus' father; whether he is that father, the poem does not say (see [[echius]] and the translation's note on 15.339).
