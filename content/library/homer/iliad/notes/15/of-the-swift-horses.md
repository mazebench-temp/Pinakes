---
summary: Apollo looks “the Danaans of the swift horses” in the face and shakes the aegis at them.
---

“But when he looked the Danaans of the swift horses in the face / and shook it, and himself shouted very loudly besides, then their hearts / in their breasts he bewitched, and they forgot their furious courage” (15.320–322; [[apollo]], [[aegis]], [[danaans]], [[furious]]). As long as the god held the aegis still, missiles struck on both sides and men fell; when he shook it, the Danaans' courage went (15.318–322). The line-end Δαναῶν ταχυπώλων names the Achaeans as horsemen at the moment they lose the will to fight. They flee like cattle or sheep before two beasts at night (15.323–326; [[black-night]]).
