**The great oath.** Zeus has woken, seen Poseidon among the Achaeans and threatened Hera with blows (15.4–33; see [[zeus]] and [[hera]]). She answers with the oath that binds the gods, calling Earth and Heaven to witness with the water of Styx, and adding Zeus' “sacred head” and “the wedded bed of us two, / by which I would never swear idly” (15.39–40; see [[earth]], [[sky]] and [[oath]]). Styx is the oath itself: ὅς τε μέγιστος / ὅρκος, “which is the greatest oath”, as the Titaressus of the Catalogue is a branch of the water of Styx, “the dread oath” (2.755). κατειβόμενον, “down-flowing”, is said in the Iliad otherwise only of water running down a channel (21.261). Calypso swears the same three verses to Odysseus (*Odyssey* 5.184–186), that she plans him no harm.

**Twice in two books.** In Book 14 Hera swore by “the inviolable water of Styx” to Sleep, who would not lull Zeus without it (14.271–280; see [[sleep]]). Now she swears to Zeus, and her oath is true to the letter: it is not through her will that Poseidon helps the Achaeans, for he came of his own accord, and Sleep, not Hera, sent him into the battle (15.41–46; 13.15–16, 14.354–360; see [[poseidon]] and the translation's note on 15.41–46). What she did herself the oath does not touch.
