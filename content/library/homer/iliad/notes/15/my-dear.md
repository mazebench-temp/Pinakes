---
summary: The two sons of Telamon address each other with πέπον: Ajax calls on Teucer when Lycophron falls, and tells him to put down his bow when Zeus breaks the string.
---

**Ajax to Teucer.** “Teucer, my friend, our trusted companion has been killed, / the son of Mastor, who came from Cythera and lived in our house” (15.437–438; [[ajax]], [[teucer]], [[lycophron]], [[trusted-companion]]). The address is a brother's, and the loss is shared: “whom we honored in our halls like our own dear parents” (15.439).

**Ajax again.** When Teucer's string has broken and he blames a god, Ajax answers: “My friend, let your bow and your thick-flying arrows / lie, since a god has confounded them, begrudging the Danaans” (15.472–473; [[bow]], [[blameless]]). The vocative πέπον, which in the plural is a taunt (2.235, 13.120), is in the singular the word of affection between companions in arms. See [[telamonian]].
