---
summary: Ajax, driven back by missiles, leaves “the deck of the balanced ship” for the seven-foot bench; the book adds the genitive νηὸς ἐΐσης.
grc: ["νηὸς ἐΐσης"]
---

“And Ajax no longer stood his ground; for he was hard pressed by missiles; / but he drew back a little way, thinking he would die, / to the seven-foot bench, and left the deck of the balanced ship” (15.727–729; [[ajax]], [[ships]]). He has gone up and down the decks of the ships with his great spear for fighting at sea (15.676–688), and now he holds the line from the bench. Elsewhere in the Iliad the epithet stands with the plural, νῆας ἐΐσας, at the verse-end (1.306, 8.217, 11.8); the genitive νηὸς ἐΐσης is new to the entry, and the Odyssey uses it often (*Odyssey* 3.10, 11.508). See [[stern]].
