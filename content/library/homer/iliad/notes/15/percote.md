---
summary: Melanippus, son of Hicetaon, grazed his cattle in Percote “while the enemy were still far away”, and came back to Ilios when the Achaean ships came (15.547–550).
---

ὃ δʼ ὄφρα μὲν εἰλίποδας βοῦς / βόσκʼ ἐν Περκώτῃ δηΐων ἀπὸ νόσφιν ἐόντων, “For a time he grazed shambling cattle / in Percote, while the enemy were still far away” (15.547–548; see [[melanippus-son-of-hicetaon]]). In the Catalogue Percote is one of the towns of Asius' realm, with Abydos and Arisbe (2.835–837; see [[asius]]); Iphidamas left his ships there and came to Ilios on foot (11.229–230; see [[iphidamas]]). The verses that follow, on his coming back to Ilios and living in Priam's house, were said of Imbrius, who had lived at Pedaeum (15.549–551 = 13.174–176; see [[imbrius]]).

## Outside Homer

[[Strabo]] quotes these lines with those on Democoon, Priam's bastard son, who came from his horses at Abydos (4.499–500): the son of Hicetaon, like Democoon, grazed herds that were not another's, so that Percote and the land beyond it as far as Adrasteia were Trojan land under Priam (*Geography* 13.1.7; see [[democoon]] and [[adrasteia]]).
