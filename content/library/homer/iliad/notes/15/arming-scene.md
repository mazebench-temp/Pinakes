summary: Two short armings and one disarming. Ares puts on his gleaming armor to avenge his son, and Athena takes it off him piece by piece; Teucer, his bowstring broken, puts his bow away and arms as a spearman in three verses, the verses with which Odysseus, his arrows spent, arms against the suitors.

**Ares armed and disarmed.** Told that his son has fallen, Ares tells Terror and Rout to yoke his horses, “and he himself put on his gleaming armor” (αὐτὸς δʼ ἔντεʼ ἐδύσετο παμφανόωντα, 15.120; see [[ares]] and [[terror]]), the half-verse of Meleager arming at last in Phoenix's story (9.596). The arming is told in a single half-verse, and its undoing takes four. Athena rushes from her throne, “and taken the helmet from his head and the shield from his shoulders, / and she took the spear from his strong hand and stood it aside, / the bronze spear” (15.125–127; see [[athena]]): helmet, shield and spear, the last three pieces of every arming (see [[helmet]], [[shield]] and [[spear]]).

**Teucer.** When Zeus breaks his bowstring, Ajax tells Teucer to take a long spear and a shield (15.471–477; see [[bow]]). The arming that follows has only the pieces he needs:

{{quote:e4196fc9-1ddc-5280-acb3-555505738285}}

Greaves, corselet and sword are not mentioned: Teucer takes only what he lacked as an archer, a shield, a helmet and a spear. The helmet verses are those of Paris and Agamemnon (3.336–337, 11.41–42), and the spear verse is Nestor's in the night (10.135, 14.12). The first three verses return in the Odyssey in the same situation. When the arrows run out, Odysseus leans his bow against the doorpost of the hall and “put a shield of four layers” on his shoulders, a helmet with a nodding horsehair crest on his head, and takes two strong spears (*Odyssey* 22.119–125). In both poems the archer becomes a spearman with the same words.
