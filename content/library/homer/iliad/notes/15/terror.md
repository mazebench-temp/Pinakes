---
summary: When Ares learns that his son is dead, he tells Terror and Rout to yoke his horses (15.119–120). It is the last of the three times Homer names Terror, always beside Rout.
---

Hera has told the gods that Ascalaphus is dead, and Ares, striking his thighs, means to go down to the ships of the Achaeans and avenge him: “So he spoke, and he told Terror and Rout to yoke / his horses, and he himself put on his gleaming armor” (15.119–120; see [[ares]], [[rout]] and [[ascalaphus]]). In Book 4 the two drove the armies on with Ares, Athena and Strife (4.439–440), and on Agamemnon's shield they surround the Gorgon (11.36–37); here they serve the god as his grooms, as Hebe readies Hera's chariot and Poseidon himself unyokes Zeus' horses (5.722, 8.440; see [[hebe]]). They do not go out: Athena disarms Ares and sits him down before he can leave (15.121–142; see [[athena]]).
