---
summary: Zeus sends Apollo “to Hector armed in bronze,” and Teucer takes another arrow “for Hector armed in bronze,” but Zeus breaks his bowstring.
---

The epithet marks Hector at the two moments when a god decides how this book's fighting will go. Zeus sends Apollo to him: “Go now, dear Phoebus, to Hector armed in bronze; / for already the earth-holder, the shaker of the earth, / has gone into the shining sea” (15.221–223; [[apollo]], [[phoebus]], [[zeus]]). The accusative χαλκοκορυστήν is given to Hector here for the first time; Glaucus used it of Sarpedon (6.199). And when Teucer aims at him, “Teucer took another arrow for Hector armed in bronze, / and he would have stopped him from battle at the ships of the Achaeans, / if he had hit him” (15.458–460; [[teucer]], [[bow]]); but Zeus, “who was guarding / Hector,” breaks the string (15.461–465; [[shrewd-word]], [[blameless]]). The line-end Ἕκτορι χαλκοκορυστῇ names the armored man whom the archer cannot reach. See [[hector]].
