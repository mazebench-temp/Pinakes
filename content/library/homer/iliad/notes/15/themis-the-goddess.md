**The cup.** Hera flies from Ida to Olympus as swiftly as a traveler's thought and finds the immortal gods gathered in the house of Zeus; they all spring up and greet her with their cups (δεικανόωντο δέπασσιν, 15.84–86; see [[hera]], [[olympians]] and [[cup]]). She lets the others be and takes the cup from Themis, “for she came running first to meet her” (15.87–88). It is Themis' only appearance in the poem before Book 20, where she calls the gods to assembly (20.4–6).

**Her question.** Themis reads Hera's face: “You look like one in terror; / surely the son of Cronus, your husband, has put you to flight” (15.90–91; see [[zeus]] and [[son-of-cronus]]). ἐφόβησε is the word of the battlefield, where φόβος is rout and flight (see [[phobos]]): Zeus has just complained that Hera's trick “put the army to rout” (ἐφόβησε δὲ λαούς, 15.15), and now Hera has been routed in her turn. Themis has guessed rightly. Zeus has threatened her with blows and reminded her how he once hung her from the sky (15.14–33; see [[hera-hung-from-the-sky]]).

**The feast.** Hera will not tell the story: “Do not ask me about these things, goddess Themis; you know yourself / what kind of heart he has, overbearing and harsh” (15.93–94; see [[overbearing]]). Instead she bids her “begin the gods' equal share of the feast in the halls” (ἄρχε θεοῖσι δόμοις ἔνι δαιτὸς ἐΐσης, 15.95; see [[equal-feast]] and [[feast]]), where she will hear, with all the immortals, what evil deeds Zeus is making known (15.96–99). The goddess of what is right is asked to see that the gods' feast begins in due order, as in Book 20 she gathers them to assembly. Then Hera sits down and turns on the other gods (15.100–112).
