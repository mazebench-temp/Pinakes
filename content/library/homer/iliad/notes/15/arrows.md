**Teucer's arrows.** Ajax asks his brother where his arrows are, ἰοὶ / ὠκύμοροι, “arrows / of swift death” (15.440–441; see [[moira]]), and Teucer comes with the quiver “that holds the arrows” (ἰοδόκον, 15.444; see [[quiver]]). “The arrow of many groans” strikes Cleitus in the neck from behind (πολύστονος … ἰός, 15.451; see [[cleitus]]); the next, aimed at Hector, goes astray when the string breaks, “the arrow heavy with bronze” (ἰὸς χαλκοβαρής, 15.464–465; see [[bow]]). Teucer had strung the bow that morning to bear “the arrows springing thick and fast” (15.470), and Ajax tells him to let the bow and “thick-flying arrows” lie (ταρφέας ἰοὺς, 15.472; see [[teucer]]).

**Foiled by Zeus.** βέλεμνα, “missiles”, is the word of the next speech: Hector saw “that Teucer's missiles had been foiled” and tells the Trojans, “I have seen with my eyes / the missiles of their best man foiled by Zeus” (15.484, 15.488–489; see [[the-help-of-zeus-is-easy-to-see]] and [[aristos]]). The best man of the Achaeans is Teucer here, by Hector's account, because his arrows were the danger.

**Missiles in the battle.** When the armies meet, “arrows leapt from the bowstrings” and spears from bold hands (15.313–314), and while Apollo holds the aegis still “the missiles of both sides struck home, and the people fell” (15.319; see [[aegis]]). The Trojans pour “groaning missiles” on Antilochus as he flees (βέλεα στονόεντα, 15.590; see [[antilochus]]), and Ajax at the last is “hard pressed by missiles” (15.727; 16.102). Around Protesilaus' ship the fighting is too close for them: the men no longer stand apart “and await the rush of arrows any longer, nor of javelins” (15.709; see [[spear]]).
