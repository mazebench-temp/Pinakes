---
summary: Minds made and unmade by gods: Athena calls Ares “crazed in your mind”, Poseidon refuses to live “by the mind of Zeus”, Iris tells him that the hearts of good men can be turned, and Hector allows that Zeus may have been harming the Trojans' minds when the elders held him back from the ships.
---

Athena's rebuke of Ares begins with the φρένες: “Madman, crazed in your mind, you are lost!” (μαινόμενε φρένας ἠλέ, 15.128; see [[ares]] and [[noos]]). Poseidon's claim to equality is a refusal to think as Zeus thinks, “So I will not live by the mind of Zeus” (οὔ τι Διὸς βέομαι φρεσίν, 15.194; see [[time]]), the very thing Zeus had wished of Hera, that she might sit among the immortals “thinking as I do” (ἶσον ἐμοὶ φρονέουσα, 15.50). Iris answers him with the maxim that the φρένες of good men can be turned (15.203; see [[the-hearts-of-good-men-can-be-turned]]). Zeus himself acts “with this in mind” (τὰ φρονέων, 15.603) when he rouses Hector against the ships. Hector, at the stern of Protesilaus' ship, blames the cowardice of the elders who held him back from the ships, and then allows another cause: “but if far-seeing Zeus was harming our minds then, / now he himself is urging us on and commanding us” (βλάπτε φρένας, 15.724–725; see [[elders]] and [[who-is-to-blame]]). Phoenix used the verb of Ate, who runs ahead over all the earth “doing harm to men” (9.507).
