summary: Geese lead the line of birds on which Hector, like a tawny eagle, swoops as they feed beside a river, “geese or cranes or long-necked swans” (15.690–692).
---

χηνῶν ἢ γεράνων ἢ κύκνων δουλιχοδείρων: the verse is the one of the Asian meadow in Book 2 (15.692 = 2.460; see [[birds-on-the-asian-meadow]]). There the flock was the Achaean army in its numbers, flying here and there and settling with loud cries; here it is feeding by a river, and an eagle falls on it, as Hector falls on the ships (15.690–695; see [[the-eagle-and-the-birds]] and [[eagle]]). Geese are the prey of the great birds again in Book 17, when Automedon drives against the Trojans like a vulture after geese (17.460), and in the Odyssey, where an eagle carries off a white goose from the farmyard and, in Penelope's dream, kills her twenty geese (*Odyssey* 15.160–163, 19.535–543). See [[cranes]] and [[swans]].
