---
summary: Hector makes for a ship as an eagle swoops on a flock of “geese or cranes or long-necked swans” feeding beside a river.
---

“But as a tawny eagle swoops on a tribe of winged birds / as they feed beside a river, / geese or cranes or long-necked swans, / so Hector made straight for a dark-prowed ship” (15.690–693; [[eagle]], [[tawny]], [[geese]], [[cranes]], [[swans]], [[dark-prowed]], [[hector]]). The verse of the three birds is the one of the Achaean army pouring out like the flocks on the Asian meadow (2.460; [[birds-on-the-asian-meadow]]). There the birds were the army itself; here they are the prey, and Hector is the eagle that falls on them.
