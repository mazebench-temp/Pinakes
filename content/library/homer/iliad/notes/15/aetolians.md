---
summary: The Aetolians are named once, in the praise of their leader Thoas, “by far the best of the Aetolians”, who advises the chiefs to stand against Hector (15.281–282).
---

Αἰτωλῶν ὄχʼ ἄριστος, “by far the best of the Aetolians” (15.282; see [[thoas]] and [[andraemon]]): the phrase was said of Periphas, son of Ochesius, whom Ares killed (5.842–843; see [[periphas]]). Thoas speaks for the whole army, not for his people, and the Aetolians do not fight as a body in the book.
