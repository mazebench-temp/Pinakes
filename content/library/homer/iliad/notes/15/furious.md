---
summary: Athena restrains “furious Ares” and sits him down on his throne; Apollo carries “the furious aegis”; and on both sides men remember, forget, or are stopped from their “furious courage.”
---

**Ares.** Athena takes his helmet, shield and spear, “and she approached furious Ares with words” (15.127), and when she has done, “So saying, she sat furious Ares down on his throne” (15.142; [[ares]], [[athena]], [[throne]]). The war god's standing epithet frames the scene in which he is kept from war.

**The aegis.** Apollo goes before Hector “and he held the furious aegis, / dreadful, shaggy all around, conspicuous” (15.308–309; [[aegis]], [[shaggy-all-around]], [[preeminent]]). The feminine θοῦρις, otherwise said of courage and of the shield (11.32, 20.162), is given to the aegis only here.

**Courage.** θούριδος ἀλκῆς ends five verses of the book. Hector, revived, tells Apollo that Ajax “stopped me from my furious courage” (15.250); when Apollo shakes the aegis, the Achaeans “forgot their furious courage” (15.322); Hector and Ajax each call on their men, “be men, my friends, and remember your furious courage” (15.487, 15.734; [[exhortation]], [[alke]]); and Dolops was “well skilled in furious courage” (15.527; [[dolops-son-of-lampus]]). The exhortation is the verse that Hector used in Books 6, 8 and 11 (6.112, 8.174, 11.287); here, for the first time, an Achaean speaks it.
