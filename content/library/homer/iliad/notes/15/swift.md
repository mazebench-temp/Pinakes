---
summary: Antilochus does not stand his ground before Hector, “swift warrior though he was.”
---

“And Antilochus did not stand his ground, swift warrior though he was, / but fled in fear, like a wild beast that has done some harm” (15.585–586; [[antilochus]], [[hector]]). The verse is the one in which Aeneas did not stand his ground in Book 5, when he saw Antilochus come to stand beside Menelaus (5.571); now Antilochus gives way. Menelaus has just praised him as the swiftest of the young men (15.569–570; [[strong-spear]]), and the speed that the epithet names carries him back to his companions (15.591).
