summary: Teucer runs to his brother's side “holding in his hand the back-bent bow and the quiver / that holds the arrows”; the book adds the accusative παλίντονον.
grc: [παλίντονον]
---

Ajax has called for Teucer's arrows and “the bow that Phoebus Apollo gave you” (15.440–441), and Teucer comes “holding in his hand the back-bent bow and the quiver / that holds the arrows; and very swiftly he let fly his missiles at the Trojans” (15.443–444; [[teucer]], [[bow]], [[quiver]], [[arrow-holding]]). It is the bow with which he came into the fighting in Book 8 (8.266), and Zeus breaks its string a few lines later (15.461–465; [[blameless]]). The verse returns in the Odyssey for the great bow of Odysseus, which Penelope carries to the suitors with its quiver (*Odyssey* 21.59–60; compare 21.11–12). The accusative παλίντονον is new to the entry.
