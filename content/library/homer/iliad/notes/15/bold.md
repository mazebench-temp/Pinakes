---
summary: As Apollo leads the Trojans with the aegis, “many spears from bold hands” fly on both sides, and many stand in the ground short of the flesh they long for.
---

“And many spears from bold hands / were fixed, some of them, in the flesh of young men swift in war, / and many, too, in between, before they could reach the white flesh, / stood fixed in the ground, longing to glut themselves on flesh” (15.314–317; [[spear]], [[swift-in-war]], [[white]], [[apollo]]). The passage repeats, with a change, the spears thrown at Ajax as he gave ground in Book 11: there some stuck in his great shield, here in the flesh of young men, and the last two verses are the same (11.571–574; [[ajax]]). The hands are bold, and the spears themselves are given desire. Aristotle quotes the last verse among Homer's lifeless things made alive (*Rhetoric* 3.11, 1412a1; [[homers-lifeless-things-alive]]).
