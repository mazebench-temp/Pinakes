---
summary: Priam is named in his son's patronymic (15.239, 15.244, 15.597, 15.604), and as the head of the house in which his nephew Melanippus, son of Hicetaon, lived and was honored like one of Priam's own children (15.551).
---

ναῖε δὲ πὰρ Πριάμῳ, ὃ δέ μιν τίεν ἶσα τέκεσσι, “and lived beside Priam; and Priam honored him as he honored his children” (15.551). The three verses that bring Melanippus from his cattle at Percote to Priam's house (15.549–551) were said in Book 13 of Imbrius, who had married Priam's bastard daughter (13.174–176; see [[melanippus-son-of-hicetaon]] and [[imbrius]]). Melanippus is Priam's nephew, the son of his brother Hicetaon (20.237–238; see [[hicetaon]]), and two more of Priam's nephews, Caletor and Dolops, die at the ships in this book (15.419–421, 15.540–543; see [[caletor]] and [[dolops-son-of-lampus]]).

Apollo finds “the son of wise-hearted Priam, brilliant Hector” (15.239), in the verse that brought Iris to Hector in Book 11 (11.197), and Πριαμίδης, “son of Priam”, is Hector when Zeus gives him glory (15.597, 15.604; see [[hector]] and [[son-of-priam]]).
