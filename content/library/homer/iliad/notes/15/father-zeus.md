---
summary: Nestor begins his prayer at the ships “Father Zeus,” and the Achaeans flee “before Hector and father Zeus.”
---

**The prayer.** “Father Zeus, if ever anyone in Argos rich in wheat, / burning to you the fat thigh pieces of ox or sheep, / prayed that he might return, and you promised and nodded your assent, / remember these things, Olympian, and ward off the pitiless day” (15.372–375; [[nestor]], [[prayer]], [[rich-in-wheat]], [[thigh-pieces]], [[olympian]], [[pitiless]]). The old man reminds the god of the thigh pieces burned to him at home, and of the nod with which he assented to the prayers for return; Zeus thunders (15.377).

**The rout.** In the simile of the lion among the cattle, “so then the Achaeans / fled in wondrous rout before Hector and father Zeus, / all of them” (15.636–638; [[hector]], [[wondrous]], [[oloophron]]). The narrator joins the man and the god as the two who drive the Achaeans, as Zeus himself pushes Hector on with his hand a little later (15.694–695). See [[zeus]].
