summary: Told of his son's death, Ares strikes “both his sturdy thighs” with the flat of his hands; the book adds the dual θαλερώ.
grc: [θαλερὼ]
en: [sturdy]
---

“So she spoke, and Ares struck both his sturdy thighs / with the flat of his hands, and lamenting he spoke a word” (15.113–114; [[ares]], [[ascalaphus]], [[hera]], [[lament]]). The gesture of grief and alarm is a man's: Asius struck his thighs and reproached Zeus when the Achaeans held the gate against him (12.162–166; [[asius]]), and Patroclus makes the same gesture in this book when he sees the Trojans storm the wall (15.397–398; [[patroclus]]). Only the god's thighs have the epithet. θαλερός, “in its prime, vigorous,” is rendered “sturdy” here, and the dual θαλερώ is new to the entry.
