---
summary: Apollo, having promised to clear the road to the ships, “breathed great might into the shepherd of the people,” Hector.
---

“So saying, he breathed great might into the shepherd of the people” (15.262; [[apollo]], [[hector]], [[menos]]). In Book 14 the title named Hector lying under Ajax's stone, protected by others (14.423–424); here the same leader is filled with a god's strength, and the simile of the stalled horse follows (15.263–268; [[the-stallion]]). Apollo did the same for Aeneas in Book 5, when he sent him back healed from his shrine “and put might into the breast of the shepherd of the people” (5.512–513; [[aeneas]]), and the verse of this book is spoken of Aeneas again in Book 20 (20.110).
