summary: Ilios is “steep” in three speeches about its fall, Olympus is “steep” as Hera reaches it, Apollo protects Hector's “steep citadel,” and Zeus' anger is “sheer”; the book adds the form αἰπεινόν.
grc: [αἰπεινὸν]
---

**Ilios.** The city is named with its epithet each time its capture is spoken of. Zeus foretells a counterattack “until the Achaeans / take steep Ilios through the counsels of Athena” (15.70–71; [[plan-of-zeus]]); Poseidon threatens lasting anger if Zeus “spares steep Ilios, and will not consent / to sack it” (15.215–216; [[driver-of-spoil]]); and Hector tells Melanippus that they must fight on “until either we kill them, or from top to bottom / they take steep Ilios and kill its citizens” (15.557–558; [[hector]], [[melanippus-son-of-hicetaon]]). Apollo tells Hector that he has protected “both you yourself and your steep citadel” (15.257; [[of-the-golden-sword]]), with the neuter αἰπεινόν, which is new to the entry. See [[troy]].

**Olympus.** Hera, flying from Ida, “came to steep Olympus” (15.84; [[high-olympus]]).

**A figure.** Zeus tells Apollo that Poseidon has gone into the sea, “avoiding the sheer anger / of mine” (15.223–224; [[shining-sea]], [[cholos]]), as Diomedes drew back from Apollo “avoiding the wrath of Apollo who shoots from afar” (5.444). αἰπύς is rendered “sheer,” as in αἰπὺς ὄλεθρος, “sheer destruction.”
