---
summary: Hector takes hold of the stern of “a seafaring ship,” the ship that brought Protesilaus to Troy.
---

“With these thoughts they stood against one another; / and Hector took hold of the stern of a seafaring ship, / a fine, swift-sailing ship, which had brought Protesilaus / to Troy, and did not take him back again to his native land” (15.703–706; [[hector]], [[protesilaus]], [[stern]], [[swift-sailing-ships]], [[dear-native-land]]). Three epithets crowd on the ship that Hector seizes, the seafaring, the fine and the swift-sailing, as it becomes the center of the battle. The genitive νεὸς … ποντοπόροιο closes the verse as it did when Chryseis stepped out of the seafaring ship at Chryse (1.439). The ship that crossed the sea will not cross it again; it burns at the beginning of Book 16 (16.122–124).
