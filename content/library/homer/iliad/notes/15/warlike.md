---
summary: “Warlike Menelaus” comes to Meges' defense and kills Dolops from behind.
---

“While he stood his ground and fought him, and still hoped for victory, / warlike Menelaus came to his defense, / and stood off to one side with his spear, unseen, and hit him in the shoulder from behind” (15.539–541; [[menelaus]], [[meges]], [[dolops-son-of-lampus]]). The epithet ἀρήϊος, “belonging to Ares,” stands with Menelaus as he kills; the point “sped through his chest in its fury” (15.542), in a verse that Aristotle quotes (*Rhetoric* 3.11, 1412a2; [[homers-lifeless-things-alive]]). Menelaus then urges on Antilochus (15.568; [[good-at-the-war-cry]]).
