Αἰνείας δὲ Μέδοντα καὶ Ἴασον ἐξενάριξεν, “and Aeneas slew and stripped Medon and Iasus” (15.332; see [[aeneas]], [[iasus]] and [[stripping-the-dead]]). He is one of the Achaeans named among the dead when Apollo has shaken the aegis in their faces and the battle scatters (15.318–342; see [[aegis]] and [[androktasia]]). The narrator gives him the four verses that introduced him in Book 13: “One was the bastard son of divine Oileus, / Medon, the brother of Ajax; but he lived / in Phylace, away from his native land, since he had killed a man, / a kinsman of his stepmother Eriopis, whom Oileus had as wife” (15.333–336 = 13.694–697; see [[oileus]], [[ajax-son-of-oileus]], [[phylace]], [[eriopis]] and [[nothos]]). In Book 13 they told who was leading the Phthians at the ships with Podarces (13.693–700; see [[phthians]] and [[podarces]]); here they are said over his body. The exile who found a home at Phylace, away from his father's land, dies at Troy (see [[exile-and-refuge]]). A hundred verses later Hector kills Lycophron, who had likewise come to live with Ajax after a killing (15.430–432; see [[lycophron]]), and at the end of the book Hector seizes the stern of the ship that brought Protesilaus, the lord of Phylace, to Troy (15.704–706; see [[protesilaus]]).
