---
summary: Ajax tells the Danaans that there is no city near “where we could defend ourselves, with a people to turn the battle.”
---

“There is no city near, fitted with towers, / where we could defend ourselves, with a people to turn the battle; / but here in the plain of the close-armored Trojans / we sit with our backs to the sea, far from our native land” (15.737–740; [[ajax]], [[close-armored]], [[dear-native-land]], [[demos]]). Elsewhere ἑτεραλκής is always the epithet of victory, ἑτεραλκέα νίκην, the victory that Zeus gives to one side (7.26, 8.171); here, for the only time, it describes a people, a reserve of men who could turn the battle. The Achaeans have none: their only city is the camp, and their only wall has fallen (15.736; [[the-achaean-wall]]).
