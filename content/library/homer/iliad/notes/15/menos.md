---
summary: Zeus' plan for Hector is a gift of μένος: Apollo is to “breathe might into him again”, and does, “he breathed great might into the shepherd of the people”; Zeus keeps rousing great might in the Trojans and diminishes the might of the Argives, while the leaders' rallies urge on “the might and spirit of every man”.
passages:
  - 15.59-62 | Apollo to breathe might into Hector
  - 15.254-262 | Apollo breathes might into Hector
---

The word marks the stages of Zeus' plan. Zeus tells Hera that Apollo will urge Hector into battle “and breathe might into him again” (αὖτις δʼ ἐμπνεύσῃσι μένος, 15.60; see [[plan-of-zeus]]); he tells Apollo to “rouse great might in him” until the Achaeans reach the ships (15.232); and the narrator closes Apollo's speech to Hector with the verse that fulfils it: “So saying, he breathed great might into the shepherd of the people” (ἔμπνευσε μένος μέγα, 15.262; see [[apollo]] and [[hector]]). Later Zeus himself “kept rousing great might in them”, the Trojans, and bewitched the heart of the Argives (15.594). Hector reads the other side of the same gift in Teucer's broken bowstring: Zeus “diminishes the might of the Argives, and helps us” (15.493; see [[the-help-of-zeus-is-easy-to-see]]). The men's own μένος is what the rallies call up: “So saying, he urged on the might and spirit of every man” (15.500, 15.514, 15.667; see [[exhortation]]), and Ajax's counsel is to “mingle our hands and our might with theirs hand to hand” (15.510).
