---
summary: Ascalaphus is dead before the book begins, but on Olympus Hera gives the news to his father Ares, who sets out to avenge him against Zeus' command until Athena disarms him.
refs: [15.111, 15.116, 15.138-140]
passages:
  - 15.110-142 | Ares learns of his son's death
---

Deiphobus killed Ascalaphus in Book 13 with a spear meant for Idomeneus, and the narrator said then that Ares, sitting on Olympus, “had not yet heard at all” (13.516–525; see [[deiphobus]]). In Book 15 Hera tells him before all the gods: “for his son has perished in battle, the dearest of men to him, / Ascalaphus, whom mighty Ares says is his own” (15.111–112; see [[hera]] and [[ares]]). τόν φησιν ὃν ἔμμεναι, “whom he says is his own”: the god acknowledges the son that Astyoche bore him in secret, as the Catalogue told it (2.512–515; see [[the-birth-of-ascalaphus-and-ialmenus]]).

Ares strikes his thighs and calls for his horses, to go to the ships of the Achaeans and avenge “the killing of my son”, even if he must lie among the dead under Zeus' thunderbolt (15.113–120). Athena takes away his arms and tells him to let go his anger “for your own son”, since many a man better than Ascalaphus “in strength and hands / has been killed, or will be killed hereafter” (15.138–140; see [[athena]]). The son of a god dies like any mortal, and his father is kept from avenging him. Zeus' own son Sarpedon will die in the next book (15.67), and Zeus too will let him go; he calls him φίλτατον ἀνδρῶν, the words Hera uses of Ascalaphus (16.433; see [[sarpedon]]).
