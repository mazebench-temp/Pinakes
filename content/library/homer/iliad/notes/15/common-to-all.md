---
summary: When Athena clears the mist from the Achaeans' eyes, light comes to them “both toward the ships and toward the war, common to all.”
---

“And from their eyes Athena thrust away the cloud of mist, / wondrous; and a great light came to them on both sides, / both toward the ships and toward the war, common to all” (15.668–670; [[athena]], [[achlys]], [[wondrous]]). The verse-end ὁμοιΐου πολέμοιο, which Phoenix used of the war that Achilles knew nothing of as a child (9.440), and which named the rope of war that Zeus and Poseidon pulled tight (13.358), here ends a sentence of sight: the men see the battle both ways, and they see Hector and his companions (15.671–673; [[good-at-the-war-cry]]). See [[polemos]].
