summary: The long spears kept on the ships for fighting at sea are “jointed,” and Ajax wields one “joined with bands, twenty-two cubits long”; the book adds κολλητόν and the related κολλήεντα.
grc: [κολλητὸν, κολλήεντα]
en: [joined, jointed]
---

The word of the joiner's work on chariots and doors is used in this book of spears too long to be cut from one shaft. The Achaeans fight from the ships “with the long spears that lay ready for them on the ships, / for fighting at sea, jointed, clad in bronze at the tip” (15.388–389; [[long-spears]], [[ships]]), where κολλήεις, “glued, jointed,” is the related adjective of κόλλα, “glue.” Later Ajax strides over the decks and “wielded in his hands a great spear for fighting at sea, / joined with bands, twenty-two cubits long” (15.677–678; [[ajax]], [[spear]]), κολλητὸν βλήτροισι, twice the length of Hector's spear of eleven cubits (6.319, 8.494; see the translation's note on 15.389 and 15.677–678). The forms κολλητόν and κολλήεντα are new to the entry; κολλητόν will also claim the well-joined chariot of 19.395.
