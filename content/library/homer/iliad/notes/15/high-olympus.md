summary: The gods chafed “throughout high Olympus” when Zeus hung Hera from the sky, Hera returns to high Olympus, and Poseidon says that the earth and high Olympus are common to the three sons of Cronus; the book adds the nominative μακρὸς Ὄλυμπος.
grc: ["μακρὸς Ὄλυμπος"]
---

**Hera hung from the sky.** “And you hung in the upper air and the clouds, / and the gods chafed throughout high Olympus, / but they could not stand beside you and set you free” (15.20–22; [[hera]], [[hera-hung-from-the-sky]], [[olympus]]).

**Hera's return.** Zeus sends her back: “and she went from the mountains of Ida to high Olympus” (15.79; [[ida]]), and she arrives at “steep Olympus” (15.84; [[steep]]).

**The common share.** In Poseidon's account of the lots, “Zeus got the wide sky, in the upper air and the clouds; / but the earth is still common to all, and high Olympus” (15.192–193; [[poseidon]], [[the-lots-of-the-three-brothers]], [[wide-sky]]). The mountain that is the gods' home, and the seat from which Zeus rules them, is in Poseidon's telling common ground. The nominative μακρὸς Ὄλυμπος is new to the entry.
