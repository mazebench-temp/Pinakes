---
summary: Meges' corselet was a gift to his father Phyleus from his guest-friend “Euphetes, lord of men.”
---

“For his guest-friend, Euphetes, lord of men, had given it to him / to wear into war, a defense against enemy men” (15.532–533; [[euphetes]], [[phyleus]], [[meges]], [[corselet]], [[xenia]], [[destructive]]). The title of Agamemnon is given to a few other men, kings and fathers of kings: Anchises, Aeneas, Augeas, and in the games Eumelus (5.268, 5.311, 11.701, 23.288). Here it names a man known only from this gift, which Phyleus brought home from Ephyra on the river Selleis (15.531; [[ephyra]], [[selleis-ephyra]]); the guest-gift saves the son of the guest a generation later.
