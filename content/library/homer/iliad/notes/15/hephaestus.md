---
summary: Hephaestus is named twice in Book 15: he made the aegis that Apollo carries before the Trojans, and Poseidon counts him with Athena, Hera and Hermes among the gods who want Ilios sacked. Zeus' memory of hurling gods from his threshold recalls his own fall in Book 1.
---

**The aegis.** Apollo goes before Hector holding “the furious aegis, / dreadful, shaggy all around, conspicuous, which the smith / Hephaestus gave to Zeus to carry, to put men to rout” (15.308–310; see [[aegis]], [[apollo]] and [[zeus]]). χαλκεύς, “smith”, is properly a worker in bronze. The verse-end ἣν ἄρα χαλκεύς is used of Sarpedon's shield, which a smith beat out of bronze (12.295); here the smith is the god. In Book 5 Athena put on the aegis with the Gorgon's head on it (5.738–742; see [[gorgon]]), and Hephaestus also made the scepter that came down from Zeus to Agamemnon (2.101–108; see [[the-scepter-of-agamemnon]]).

**On the Achaean side.** Yielding to Zeus, Poseidon warns that if Zeus spares Ilios against his will and that of Athena, “of Hera and Hermes and lord Hephaestus”, there will be anger between them beyond healing (15.212–217; see [[poseidon]]). The five gods go to the ships together in Book 20, Hephaestus limping behind them (20.33–37), and in Book 21 he turns his fire on the river Scamander for Achilles (21.342–382; see [[scamander]]).

**The threshold.** Zeus reminds Hera that when he hung her from the sky, any god he caught he would “seize and hurl from the threshold” until he reached the earth with little strength left (15.22–24; see [[hera-hung-from-the-sky]]). The words τεταγὼν ἀπὸ βηλοῦ occur only here and in Hephaestus' story of his fall: when he was eager to defend his mother, Zeus “seized me by the foot and hurled me from the divine threshold”, and he fell on Lemnos as the sun went down, with little life left in him (1.590–594; see [[fall-of-hephaestus]] and [[lemnos]]). The poem does not say whether the two stories tell the same day (see the translation's note on 15.18–30).
