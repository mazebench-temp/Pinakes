---
summary: Routed by Apollo and the aegis, the Achaeans scatter like a herd or flock thrown into confusion by two wild beasts “in the dead of black night”; the book adds the genitive μελαίνης νυκτός.
grc: ["μελαίνης νυκτὸς"]
---

“And as a herd of cattle or a great flock of sheep / is thrown into confusion by two wild beasts in the dead of black night, / coming on them suddenly when the herdsman is not there, / so the Achaeans fled in rout, their courage gone; for Apollo / sent rout among them, and granted glory to the Trojans and Hector” (15.323–327; [[cattle]], [[sheep]], [[apollo]], [[aegis]]). The god has just shaken the aegis in the Danaans' faces and shouted (15.320–322). νυκτὸς ἀμολγῷ is the hour of the lion in the simile of Agamemnon's pursuit, which routs a herd “coming on them in the dead of night” (11.173; [[lion-among-cattle]]). The genitive μελαίνης νυκτός is new to the entry; [[black-ships]] excepts the line.
