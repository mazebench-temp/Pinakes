summary: Poseidon got by lot “the gray sea, to dwell in forever,” and the Danaans hold against Hector like a great rock “close to the gray sea”; the book adds the accusative πολιὴν ἅλα.
grc: ["πολιὴν ἅλα"]
---

**The lot.** “I got by lot the gray sea, to dwell in forever, / when the lots were shaken, and Hades got the misty darkness, / and Zeus got the wide sky” (15.190–192; [[poseidon]], [[lots]], [[misty]], [[wide-sky]], [[the-lots-of-the-three-brothers]]). The accusative πολιὴν ἅλα is new to the entry; the Odyssey uses it in the verse of rowing, πολιὴν ἅλα τύπτον ἐρετμοῖς (*Odyssey* 9.104).

**The rock.** The Danaans hold “like a tower, like a rock, / lofty and great, close to the gray sea, / which withstands the swift paths of the shrill winds / and the swollen waves that belch against it” (15.618–621; [[lofty]], [[nimble]], [[shrill]]). The sea that is Poseidon's share beats against the rock to which the Achaeans are compared, after the god has left them. See [[sea]].
