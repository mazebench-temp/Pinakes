**The lots of the gods.** The world itself was divided by lot (see [[the-lots-of-the-three-brothers]]). Poseidon tells it in the words of the ritual. The lots were shaken, παλλομένων (15.191), as Hector shook them in a helmet before the duel of Book 3 and Nestor before Ajax was chosen to meet Hector (πάλλον, 3.316; πάλλεν, 7.181). Each brother “got by lot” (ἔλαχον, ἔλαχε, ἔλαχʼ, 15.190–192) his part, with the verb of the man whose lot leaps out (λάχῃσιν, 7.171; λαχεῖν, 7.179). The genitive παλλομένων has no noun, and may be taken “as we cast lots” or “as the lots were shaken”; Walter Leaf took it in the first way, and compared Herodotus' story of the thirty Persians who offered to rid Darius of the satrap Oroetes: Darius bade them cast lots, παλλομένων δὲ λαγχάνει ἐκ πάντων Βαγαῖος, “and when they cast lots, Bagaeus got it out of them all” (Herodotus 3.128.1; *Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 15.191). Hermes, disguised as a Myrmidon, will use the middle participle of himself: παλλόμενος κλήρῳ λάχον, “casting lots with them I drew the lot” (24.400).

**A lot between equals.** In Books 3 and 7 the army prays to Zeus over the helmet, and the lot leaves the choice to the gods (3.318–325, 7.177–183). The lot of the three brothers is cast by gods, and no one is said to guide it. What it gives is a share: Zeus is to stay “in his third share” (τριτάτῃ ἐνὶ μοίρῃ, 15.195), and Poseidon is “one who has an equal lot” (ἰσόμορον, 15.209; see [[moira]]). A lot makes those who draw it equal, and that is Poseidon's answer to Zeus' claim to be the elder (15.166, 15.182, 15.186).

**The allotment.** κλῆρος, the lot, is also what is got by lot, a man's share of land. Hector tells the Trojans that a man who dies fighting for his native land leaves his wife and children safe, “and his house and his allotment unharmed” (οἶκος καὶ κλῆρος ἀκήρατος, 15.494–499; see [[hector]]). The swineherd Eumaeus pairs the same two words, a house and an allotment, among the gifts a kind master gives a servant (οἶκόν τε κλῆρόν τε, *Odyssey* 14.64), and in Odysseus' false tale a Cretan's sons divide his property and cast lots for it (ἐπὶ κλήρους ἐβάλοντο, *Odyssey* 14.208–209).
