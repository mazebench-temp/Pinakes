---
summary: Teucer comes to his brother's side with “the back-bent bow and the quiver / that holds the arrows.”
---

“So he spoke, and the other understood, and ran and stood beside him, / holding in his hand the back-bent bow and the quiver / that holds the arrows; and very swiftly he let fly his missiles at the Trojans” (15.442–444; [[teucer]], [[quiver]], [[bow]], [[back-bent]]). The epithet is run over into the next verse, as in the Odyssey's two uses of the same phrase for the bow of Odysseus (*Odyssey* 21.11–12, 21.59–60). Teucer's arrows kill Cleitus; then Zeus breaks his string, and he puts the bow away in the huts (15.445–478; [[cleitus]], [[blameless]]).
