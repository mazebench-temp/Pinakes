---
summary: Patroclus sits in the hut of “manly Eurypylus” and tends his wound; the book adds the genitive ἀγαπήνορος.
grc: [ἀγαπήνορος]
---

“And Patroclus, as long as the Achaeans and Trojans / were fighting around the wall, outside the swift ships, / so long sat in the hut of manly Eurypylus / and cheered him with talk” (15.390–393; [[patroclus]], [[eurypylus]], [[black-pains]]). Eurypylus was wounded by Paris' arrow and Patroclus tended him at the end of Book 11 (11.809–848). The epithet was given to Polydamas in Book 13 (13.756); it will be given to Idomeneus at the funeral of Patroclus (23.113). The genitive ἀγαπήνορος is new to the entry.
