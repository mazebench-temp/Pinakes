---
summary: The Danaans hold against Hector like a sea cliff that withstands “the swift paths of the shrill winds” and the swollen waves.
---

“For they held, fitted together in close order like a tower, like a rock, / lofty and great, close to the gray sea, / which withstands the swift paths of the shrill winds / and the swollen waves that belch against it: / so the Danaans awaited the Trojans steadfastly and did not flee” (15.618–622; [[danaans]], [[lofty]], [[gray-sea]], [[nimble]]). The phrase λιγέων ἀνέμων λαιψηρὰ κέλευθα is the one of the heaving sea in Book 14, which felt the winds' coming (14.17; [[the-heaving-sea]]). There the sea was Nestor's doubt; here the rock is the Achaeans' resolve, and the winds and the waves are Hector's onset, as the next simile, of a wave falling on a ship, shows (15.624–628; [[wind-nurtured]]).
