---
summary: Periphetes of Mycenae trips on the rim of the shield he carried, “reaching to his feet, a barrier against javelins,” and Hector kills him as he lies on his back.
---

“For as he turned back, he tripped on the rim of his shield, / which he carried himself, reaching to his feet, a barrier against javelins; / caught in it, he fell on his back, and his helmet / rang terribly around his temples as he fell” (15.645–648; [[periphetes-of-mycenae]], [[shield]], [[helmet]], [[hector]]). In Book 10 ποδηνεκής described the lion skins that Agamemnon and Diomedes put on at night, “reaching to his feet” (10.24, 10.178; [[lion-skin]]); here it is the great shield that reached to its bearer's feet, and that brings him down. The narrator has just called Periphetes a better man than his father in running and in fighting (15.641–643); the shield that should guard him trips him as he turns.
