---
summary: A hunted stag is sheltered by “a lofty rock or a shady forest,” and the Danaans hold against Hector like a rock “lofty and great, close to the gray sea.”
---

**The stag.** In the simile of Hector's return, the stag or wild goat escapes the hunters: “but a lofty rock or a shady forest / shelters it, and it is not their lot to catch it” (15.273–274; [[antlered]], [[bearded]]).

**The rock.** When Hector tries to break the ranks, the Danaans hold “like a tower, like a rock, / lofty and great, close to the gray sea, / which withstands the swift paths of the shrill winds / and the swollen waves that belch against it” (15.618–621; [[danaans]], [[gray-sea]], [[shrill]], [[nimble]]). The translation renders ἠλίβατος “lofty” in both passages, keeping “sheer” for αἰγίλιψ. The rock that shelters the hunted animal in the first simile is the image of the Achaeans' resistance in the second; then Hector falls on them like a wave on a ship, and their hearts are torn (15.623–629; [[wind-nurtured]]).
