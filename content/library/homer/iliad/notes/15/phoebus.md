---
summary: Phoebus Apollo is sent to revive Hector and leads the Trojans with the aegis, breaking down the wall; Zeus calls him “dear Phoebus,” he names himself “Phoebus Apollo of the golden sword,” and the narrator turns to address him, “archer Phoebus”; the book adds the vocative Φοῖβε and the accusative Φοῖβον.
grc: [Φοῖβε, Φοῖβον]
---

**The god's part.** The book is Apollo's from his sending to the breaking of the wall. Zeus plans “so that Phoebus Apollo may urge Hector on into battle” (15.59; [[plan-of-zeus]]); “before him went Phoebus Apollo, / his shoulders wrapped in cloud, and he held the furious aegis” (15.307–308; [[aegis]], [[furious]]); “As long as Phoebus Apollo held the aegis motionless in his hands, / so long the missiles of both sides struck home” (15.318–319); and “before them Phoebus Apollo / easily kicked down the banks of the deep trench” (15.355–356; [[the-achaean-wall]]). Ajax reminds Teucer of “the bow that Phoebus Apollo gave you” (15.441; [[teucer]], [[bow]]).

**The new forms.** Zeus sends him with the vocative of affection, “Go now, dear Phoebus, to Hector armed in bronze” (15.221; [[armed-in-bronze]]); Apollo names himself “Phoebus Apollo of the golden sword” (15.256; [[of-the-golden-sword]]); and the narrator, at the end of the simile of the boy and the sand, turns to the god: “so you, archer Phoebus, scattered the long labor and misery / of the Argives” (15.365–366; [[ieios]], [[apostrophe]]). The vocative Φοῖβε and the accusative Φοῖβον are new to the entry. See [[apollo]].
