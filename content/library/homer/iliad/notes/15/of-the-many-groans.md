---
summary: Teucer's arrow, “the arrow of many groans,” strikes Cleitus in the neck as he drives the chariot of Polydamas.
---

“To do a favor to Hector and the Trojans; but quickly upon him / came the evil that no one could keep from him, eager though they were. / For the arrow of many groans fell on his neck from behind” (15.449–451; [[cleitus]], [[teucer]], [[arrows]], [[polydamas]]). It is the only time Homer gives the epithet to a weapon; it was the epithet of Strife rejoicing over the battle (11.73; [[strife]]) and of the sorrows that Apollo sent on the Argives in the plague (1.445). The arrow is described by what it brings, as the missiles poured on Antilochus are “groaning” (15.590; [[groaning]]).
