---
summary: Hector falls on the Achaeans as a wave falls on a swift ship, “violent under the clouds, wind-nurtured.”
---

“And fell on them as when a wave falls on a swift ship, / violent under the clouds, wind-nurtured; and the ship is all / hidden under the foam, and the dread blast of the wind / roars in the sail, and the sailors tremble at heart / in fear” (15.624–628; [[hector]], [[swift-ships]]). The epithet was given in Book 11 to the spear of Agamemnon as he sprang at Coon, “holding his wind-nurtured spear” (11.256; [[agamemnon]], [[coon]]); here it is given to a wave, which the wind raises and drives. It follows the simile of the rock that withstands the winds and the waves (15.618–622; [[shrill]], [[lofty]]): the Achaeans were the rock, and now they are the ship.
