---
summary: Athena warns Ares not to defy Hera, “who has just now come from Olympian Zeus,” and Nestor calls on Zeus as “Olympian” in his prayer at the ships; the book adds the genitive Ὀλυμπίου.
grc: [Ὀλυμπίου]
---

**Athena's warning.** “Do you not hear what the white-armed goddess Hera says, / who has just now come from Olympian Zeus?” (15.130–131; [[athena]], [[ares]], [[hera]], [[white-armed]]). Hera has come back from Ida, where Zeus is (15.78–79); the title is not a place but a rank. The genitive Ὀλυμπίου is new to the entry; the Odyssey has Ζηνὸς Ὀλυμπίου often (*Odyssey* 1.27, 2.68).

**Nestor's prayer.** “Remember these things, Olympian, and ward off the pitiless day, / and do not let the Achaeans be so beaten down by the Trojans” (15.375–376; [[nestor]], [[prayer]], [[pitiless]], [[father-zeus]]). The vocative stands alone as a name, as when Hera makes Zeus swear in Book 19 (19.108); Thetis joined it to his title, “Olympian Zeus the counselor” (1.508; [[counselor]]). See [[zeus]].
