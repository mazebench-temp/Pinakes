---
summary: “Lord Idomeneus” is named among the leaders who, on Thoas' advice, gather the best men to stand against Hector while the multitude goes back to the ships (15.301–305).
---

“Those around Ajax and lord Idomeneus, / Teucer and Meriones and Meges, the equal of Ares, / called the best men and set the battle in order / against Hector and the Trojans” (15.301–304; see [[thoas]], [[ajax]], [[meriones]], [[meges]] and [[battle-order]]). ἄνακτα, “lord” (see [[anax]]). It is his only part in the book. His great day was the battle at the ships in Book 13, until he gave way before Aeneas (13.210–520; see [[the-battle-at-the-ships]]), and he and his companion Meriones are named together here as there. The line they form breaks when Apollo shakes the aegis (15.318–327; see [[aegis]]).
