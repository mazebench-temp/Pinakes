---
summary: Apollo goes down from Ida “like a hawk, / a swift dove-killer, which is the swiftest of winged birds.”
---

“So he spoke, and Apollo did not fail to heed his father, / and he went down from the mountains of Ida like a hawk, / a swift dove-killer, which is the swiftest of winged birds” (15.236–238; [[apollo]], [[hawk]], [[ida]], [[birds]]). The hawk is named by its speed twice, ὠκέϊ and ὤκιστος, and by its prey once. Apollo's errand is to put the Achaeans to flight and to revive Hector (15.229–235), and the epithet of the hunter of doves suits the god who goes to rout an army. In the Iliad the dove is the timid bird (5.778) and the hawk's prey, as Hector will be Achilles' (22.139–142; [[doves]]).
