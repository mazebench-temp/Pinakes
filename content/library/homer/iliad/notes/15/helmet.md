summary: Athena takes the helmet from Ares' head; Teucer arms in a helmet with a nodding horsehair crest; Meges' spear shears the plume from Dolops' helmet, bright with fresh purple; Hector's helmet shakes about his temples as he rages, and Periphetes' rings about his as he falls.
grc: [κύμβαχον, "ἵππειον λόφον"]
en: [topmost peak, horsehair plume]
passages:
  - 15.535-538 | Meges shears Dolops' plume
---

**Taken off and put on.** Athena stops Ares by disarming him, and the helmet comes first, “the helmet from his head and the shield from his shoulders” (15.125; see [[ares]] and [[athena]]). Teucer, his bowstring broken, arms as a spearman: “and on his mighty head he set a well-made helmet / with a horsehair crest, and the plume nodded terribly above it” (15.480–481), the verses with which Paris and Agamemnon put on their helmets (3.336–337, 11.41–42; see [[arming-scene]] and [[teucer]]).

**Dolops' plume.** Meges strikes back at Dolops, who has stabbed his shield: “And Meges, on his bronze-fitted, horsehair-crested helmet, / stabbed the topmost peak with his sharp spear, / and broke off the horsehair plume from it; and it all fell / to the ground in the dust, bright with fresh purple” (15.535–538; see [[meges]] and [[dolops-son-of-lampus]]). κύμβαχον ἀκρότατον is the crown of the helmet, where the plume is set; the same word is “headfirst” when the charioteer Mydon falls in Book 5 (κύμβαχος, 5.586; see the translation's note on 15.536). The plume is “bright with fresh purple” (νέον φοίνικι φαεινός, 15.538), newly dyed (see [[purple]]). Menelaus kills Dolops a moment later (15.540–543).

**Hector and Periphetes.** Two verses of the book are built alike. Hector rages like Ares or a forest fire, his eyes blazing under his grim brows, “and his helmet / shook terribly around his temples as he fought” (ἀμφὶ δὲ πήληξ / σμερδαλέον κροτάφοισι τινάσσετο, 15.608–609; see [[hector]]). Periphetes of Mycenae trips on the rim of his shield and falls on his back, “and his helmet / rang terribly around his temples as he fell” (ἀμφὶ δὲ πήληξ / σμερδαλέον κονάβησε, 15.647–648; see [[periphetes-of-mycenae]] and [[shield]]). The helmet that shakes on the living man rings on the falling one, and Hector kills him (15.649–652).
