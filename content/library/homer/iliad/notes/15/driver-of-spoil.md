---
summary: Poseidon threatens Zeus with lasting anger if he spares Ilios “against my will and that of Athena, driver of spoil, / of Hera and Hermes and lord Hephaestus.”
---

“If, against my will and that of Athena, driver of spoil, / of Hera and Hermes and lord Hephaestus, / he spares steep Ilios, and will not consent / to sack it, and to give great power to the Argives, / let him know this, that between us two there will be incurable anger” (15.213–217; [[poseidon]], [[athena]], [[hera]], [[hermes]], [[hephaestus]], [[steep]]). Poseidon names the gods who favor the Achaeans; the same five go down to the Achaean side when Zeus lets the gods into the battle in Book 20 (20.33–36). Athena, named first after Poseidon himself, has her title as the goddess of plunder in a threat about the sack of a city. See [[troy]].
