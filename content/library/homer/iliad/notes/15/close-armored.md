---
summary: Hector does not stay “in the throng of the close-armored Trojans,” and Ajax reminds the Danaans that they fight “in the plain of the close-armored Trojans,” far from home.
---

The genitive πύκα θωρηκτάων closes two verses near the end of the book, once in the narrative and once in a speech. “Nor did Hector / stay in the throng of the close-armored Trojans; / but as a tawny eagle swoops on a tribe of winged birds” (15.688–690; [[hector]], [[tawny]], [[eagle]]), he makes for a ship. And Ajax tells the Danaans that there is no city near to shelter them: “but here in the plain of the close-armored Trojans / we sit with our backs to the sea, far from our native land” (15.739–740; [[ajax]], [[dear-native-land]]). The formula was Sarpedon's for the Lycians (12.317; [[lycians]]); here it names the Trojans, and the plain that is theirs. See [[trojans]].
