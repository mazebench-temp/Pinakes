---
summary: Hector makes straight for “a dark-prowed ship” as an eagle swoops on birds feeding beside a river.
---

“So Hector made straight for a dark-prowed ship, / darting at it face to face; and Zeus pushed him on from behind / with his very great hand, and urged on the army with him” (15.693–695; [[hector]], [[zeus]], [[eagle]], [[tawny]], [[long-necked]]). It is the epithet's first use in the Iliad. A few verses later he takes hold of the stern of the ship that brought Protesilaus to Troy (15.704–706; [[protesilaus]], [[seafaring-ship]], [[swift-sailing-ships]]). See [[ships]].
