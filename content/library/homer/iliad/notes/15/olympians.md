---
summary: The gods as a body are the audience of Hera's return to Olympus. They spring up to greet her with their cups, are troubled by her news and hear her call them fools to rage against Zeus, and only Athena's quick hands keep Ares from bringing Zeus' anger down on them all.
refs: [15.21-22, 15.84-86, 15.101, 15.104-109, 15.121-123, 15.136-137]
passages:
  - 15.84-142 | The gods on Olympus
---

**Shut out from the war.** Since Zeus' ban of Book 8 the gods have sat on Olympus, “shut out from the war” (13.524–525; 8.5–27; see [[zeus]]), while Poseidon helped the Achaeans in secret. When Zeus wakes he renews the ban: until Achilles' wish is fulfilled he will not “let any other / of the immortals defend the Danaans here” (15.72–73; see [[immortals]]). Even the memory he throws at Hera is of the gods' helplessness: when he hung her from the sky, “the gods chafed throughout high Olympus, / but they could not stand beside you and set you free” (15.21–22; see [[hera-hung-from-the-sky]]).

**Hera among them.** Hera finds them gathered in the house of Zeus, and “they all sprang up and greeted her with their cups” (15.84–86; see [[hera]], [[themis-the-goddess]] and [[cup]]). At her words “throughout the house of Zeus the gods were troubled” (15.101), and she tells them that they are fools: “Fools, we who rage against Zeus in our folly!” He sits apart and does not care, “So accept whatever evil he sends to each of you” (15.104–109). In Book 1 the gods laughed when Hephaestus served them after the quarrel of Zeus and Hera (1.595–600; see [[quarrel-on-olympus]] and [[laughter-of-the-gods]]); here Hera laughs only with her lips (15.101–103).

**All the gods in danger.** Ares appeals to them as “you who have your homes on Olympus” (Ὀλύμπια δώματʼ ἔχοντες, 15.115; see [[homes-on-olympus]] and [[ares]]), but it is for all of them that Athena is afraid: a greater anger from Zeus would have fallen “upon the immortals”, “had not Athena, terribly afraid for all the gods”, disarmed him (15.121–127; see [[athena]]). If Ares goes, she tells him, Zeus will come back to Olympus “to throw us into tumult, / and he will seize us one after another, the one to blame and the one who is not” (15.136–137).

**Two sides.** The gods are divided over the war. Poseidon names those who want Ilios sacked, Athena, Hera, Hermes, Hephaestus and himself (15.213–214; see [[poseidon]], [[hermes]] and [[hephaestus]]), and tells Zeus to keep his threats for his own daughters and sons, “who will listen to him when he urges them, even under compulsion” (15.197–199).
