---
summary: Athena thrusts “the cloud of mist” from the eyes of the Achaeans, driven back to their huts, and a great light comes to them on both sides, so that they see Hector and all his men.
passages:
  - 15.668-673 | Athena takes the mist from the Achaeans' eyes
---

Nestor has begged the Achaeans by their parents to stand (15.659–667; see [[aidos]]). Then “from their eyes Athena thrust away the cloud of mist, / wondrous; and a great light came to them on both sides, / both toward the ships and toward the war, common to all” (15.668–670; see [[athena]]). They see Hector and his companions, both those who stood back and those who fought beside the ships (15.671–673), and Ajax strides up and down the decks of the ships with his great spear (15.674–678; see [[ajax]]).

No mist has been mentioned before, and Zeus has said that until Achilles' wish is fulfilled he will let no other immortal defend the Danaans (15.72–77; see the translation's note on 15.668–673). The poem does not say where it came from. What Athena gives is sight, not strength: the Achaeans see what they are facing. The φόως, “light”, that comes to them is the word with which Ajax ends his last speech, “so in our hands lies the light” (15.741; see [[the-light-is-in-our-hands]]). In Book 17, when a mist hangs over the fighters around Patroclus' body, Ajax prays Zeus to free the sons of the Achaeans from it and let them see, “and destroy us in the light”, and Zeus scatters it (ἀήρ, 17.643–650).
