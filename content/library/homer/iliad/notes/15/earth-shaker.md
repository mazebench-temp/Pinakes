---
summary: Hera swears that it is not by her will “that Poseidon the earth-shaker / harms the Trojans,” and Poseidon the earth-shaker answers Iris and gives way.
---

“It is not through my will that Poseidon the earth-shaker / harms the Trojans and Hector and helps the others, / but perhaps it is his own heart that urges him on” (15.41–43; [[hera]], [[oath]], [[poseidon]]). Hera's oath names the god with his formula, Ποσειδάων ἐνοσίχθων, and keeps to the letter: she had not sent him; it was Sleep who urged him on (14.354–360; [[sleep]]). When Iris asks whether he will turn his answer, “Then Poseidon the earth-shaker spoke to her in turn: / ‘Goddess Iris, this word you have spoken is fair and right’” (15.205–206; [[iris]]), and he yields, though with a threat (15.211–217; [[driver-of-spoil]]).
