summary: Hector kills Stichius, “a trusted companion of great-hearted Menestheus”, in the rout that follows Apollo's shaking of the aegis (15.329–331).
# Στιχίον (15.329) occurs only here. The τὸν μέν … τὸν δέ of 15.330–331 go in reverse order: the companion of
# Menestheus is Stichius, the Boeotian leader Arcesilaus (see the translation's note on 15.329–331).

Ἕκτωρ μὲν Στιχίον τε καὶ Ἀρκεσίλαον ἔπεφνε, / τὸν μὲν Βοιωτῶν ἡγήτορα χαλκοχιτώνων, / τὸν δὲ Μενεσθῆος μεγαθύμου πιστὸν ἑταῖρον (15.329–331). The Greek names Stichius first and then describes the two men in the reverse order: the first described, “a leader of the bronze-armored Boeotians” (15.330), is Arcesilaus, one of the Boeotian leaders of the Catalogue (2.495), and the second, “a trusted companion of great-hearted Menestheus” (15.331; see [[trusted-companion]]), is Stichius, who served with Menestheus at the ships in Book 13 (13.195–196, 13.689–691). The translation names Arcesilaus first, so that “the one … the other” point to the right men: “Hector killed Arcesilaus and Stichius” (15.329; see the translation's note on 15.329–331, [[arcesilaus]] and [[menestheus]]). He dies among the Achaeans whom the Trojans kill when Apollo has shaken the aegis and the battle scatters (15.318–342; see [[hector]], [[aegis]] and [[androktasia]]). In the same list Aeneas kills Iasus, “a leader of the Athenians” (15.337; see [[iasus]] and [[athenians]]), so that two Athenians fall together.
