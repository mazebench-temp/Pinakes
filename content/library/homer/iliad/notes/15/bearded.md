---
summary: In the simile of Hector's return, the hunters chasing a stag are turned back when “a bearded lion appears / in their path.”
---

“But a lofty rock or a shady forest / shelters it, and it is not their lot to catch it; / and at their shouting a bearded lion appears / in their path, and at once turns them all back, for all their eagerness” (15.273–276; [[lion]], [[antlered]], [[lofty]], [[hector]]). The lion is Hector, risen after the Achaeans thought him dead (15.286–289); the epithet of the full-maned beast comes as he appears. It is the first of the three similes of the Iliad in which the lion is ἠϋγένειος, and in the other two the lion stands for Menelaus and for Achilles (17.109, 18.318).
