summary: Meges, “the equal of Ares”, stands with the best men against Hector. At the ships Polydamas kills his companion Otus of Cyllene; Meges kills Croesmus instead, and as he strips him Dolops stabs through his shield, but his father's corselet, a guest-gift from Ephyra, saves him. He shears the plume from Dolops' helmet, and Menelaus kills Dolops from behind (15.302, 15.518–545).

**The equal of Ares.** He is named last of the leaders who gather the best men against Hector on Thoas' advice: “Teucer and Meriones and Meges, the equal of Ares” (15.302; see [[thoas]], [[battle-order]] and [[equal-of-ares]]). ἀτάλαντος Ἄρηϊ is his title from the Catalogue (2.627).

**Otus and Croesmus.** At the ships, after Hector's and Ajax's exhortations, Polydamas kills “Otus of Cyllene, / companion of the son of Phyleus, and a leader of the great-hearted Epeians” (15.518–519; see [[otus-of-cyllene]], [[polydamas]] and [[epeians]]). Meges was the son of an Elean, and where Hector broke in he was counted among the Epeians' leaders (13.691–692); Strabo explains the companionship by the Epeians who had come from Elis to live under Meges on Dulichium and the Echinades (*Geography* 10.2.14; see [[dulichium]]). Meges springs at the killer, but Polydamas ducks aside, “for Apollo / would not let the son of Panthous be overpowered among the champions” (15.520–522; see [[apollo]]), and Meges' spear takes Croesmus in the middle of the chest; he is stripping the armor from his shoulders when Dolops attacks (15.523–525; see [[croesmus]] and [[stripping-the-dead]]).

**The corselet.** Dolops, son of Lampus, Hector's cousin, “stabbed the middle of the shield of the son of Phyleus with his spear, / rushing at him from close by” (15.528–529; see [[dolops-son-of-lampus]] and [[shield]]), and the corselet beneath it held:

{{quote:28a3931d-6ba8-5008-9828-6f5d07b0eb04}}

γυάλοισιν ἀρηρότα, “fitted with plates” (15.530; see [[corselet]]). The corselet's history is a guest-friendship: Euphetes of Ephyra gave it to Phyleus to wear into war, and now it saves the guest's son (see [[euphetes]], [[phyleus]], [[ephyra]], [[selleis-ephyra]] and [[guest-gifts]]). καὶ τότε, “then too”, implies an earlier rescue, the father's. Strabo puts this Ephyra in Elis, the country of Meges' father (*Geography* 8.3.5).

**The plume.** Meges answers at the helmet: “And Meges, on his bronze-fitted, horsehair-crested helmet, / stabbed the topmost peak with his sharp spear, / and broke off the horsehair plume from it; and it all fell / to the ground in the dust, bright with fresh purple” (15.535–538; see [[helmet]] and [[purple]]). κύμβαχον ἀκρότατον is the crown of the helmet, in which the plume is set (see the translation's note on 15.536). The plume falls in the dust before its wearer does, as in Book 16 the horsehair crest of Achilles' helmet, struck from Patroclus' head by Apollo, is fouled with blood and dust before Patroclus dies (16.793–800). Dolops fights on and “still hoped for victory”, until Menelaus comes to Meges' defense and spears him from behind (15.539–543; see [[menelaus]]). “The two hurried to strip the bronze-fitted armor from his shoulders” (15.544), and Hector, rebuking Melanippus, points to them: “Do you not see how they are busy about the armor of Dolops?” (15.555; see [[melanippus-son-of-hicetaon]]).

Meges is not named again in the book. In Book 5 he killed Pedaeus, a bastard son of Antenor (5.69–75); in Book 16 he kills Amphiclus (16.313–315).
