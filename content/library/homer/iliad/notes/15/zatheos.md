summary: Lycophron, Ajax's attendant, had left “holy Cythera” after killing a man and lived in Ajax's house; the book adds the dative plural ζαθέοισι.
grc: [ζαθέοισι]
---

“Lycophron, son of Mastor, / the attendant of Ajax, from Cythera, who lived with him, / since he had killed a man in holy Cythera” (15.430–432; [[lycophron]], [[mastor]], [[cythera]], [[ajax]], [[exile-and-refuge]]). The island is called holy with the epithet that Homer gives to places dear to the gods, Cilla, Nisa and Crisa (1.38, 2.508, 2.520). The plural Κύθηρα takes ζαθέοισι, and the form is new to the entry. The only other Cytheran in the Iliad is Amphidamas, who received the helmet that Odysseus wears in Book 10 (10.268).
