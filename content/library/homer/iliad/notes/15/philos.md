---
summary: The book's φίλοι are a god's son, “the dearest of men to him”, the friends whom Hector, Ajax and Nestor address in their rallies, the “dear parents” of the household, and the “dear native land” to which the Achaeans hope to sail. As often, φίλος is also simply “one's own”: the “dear heart” that does not shrink.
---

Ares' son Ascalaphus was “the dearest of men to him” (φίλτατος ἀνδρῶν, 15.111; see [[ares]] and [[ascalaphus]]), and Zeus sends Apollo to Hector with a father's address, “Go now, dear Phoebus” (15.221; see [[apollo]]). In the battle φίλοι is the word of the rally: Hector's “be men, my friends” (15.487), Ajax's “Friends, be men” and “Friends, Danaan heroes” (15.561, 15.733–734), Nestor's “Friends, be men” (15.661; see [[exhortation]]). The circle of φίλοι reaches home in Ajax's lament for Lycophron, “whom we honored in our halls like our own dear parents” (15.439; see [[lycophron]] and [[time]]), and in Hector's promise that the Achaeans will go “to their dear native land” (15.499). The φίλον ἦτορ, the heart that is one's own, is the heart of Poseidon that does not shrink from speaking to Zeus as an equal (15.166, 15.182), of Hector gasping it out (15.252), and of Melanippus, which Hector asks whether it is “not moved at all” by his cousin's death (15.553–554; see [[etor]]).
