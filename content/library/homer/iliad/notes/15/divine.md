---
summary: Zeus speaks of his “ceaseless pain for divine Heracles,” and Medon is “the bastard son of divine Oileus.”
---

**Heracles.** Zeus reminds Hera how he punished her: “Yet not even so did it let my heart go, / the ceaseless pain for divine Heracles, / whom you, winning over the storms with the help of the North Wind, / sent over the barren sea” (15.24–27; [[heracles]], [[hera]], [[barren-sea]], [[hera-drives-heracles-to-cos]]). The father's grief is for a son who is θεῖος, “divine,” born of a god.

**Oileus.** “One was the bastard son of divine Oileus, / Medon, the brother of Ajax” (15.333–334; [[medon]], [[oileus]], [[ajax-son-of-oileus]]). The verses repeat those of Book 13, where Medon fought in front of the Phthians (13.693–697), and here Aeneas kills him (15.332; [[aeneas]]). Of Oileus the epithet is a standing one in the genitive at the verse-end (13.694).
