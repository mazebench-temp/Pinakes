summary: Apollo finds “the son of wise-hearted Priam, brilliant Hector,” and addresses him as “Hector, son of Priam”; Zeus gives glory to Hector, son of Priam, and rouses him at the ships; the book adds the phrase υἱὸν Πριάμοιο.
grc: ["υἱὸν Πριάμοιο"]
---

**Apollo.** “He found the son of wise-hearted Priam, brilliant Hector, / sitting up, no longer lying down” (15.239–240; [[apollo]], [[wise-hearted]], [[brilliant]]), and he speaks to him: “Hector, son of Priam, why is it that you, apart from the others, / sit here with little strength left?” (15.244–245; [[far-worker]]).

**Zeus.** “For his heart wished to hold out glory to Hector, / son of Priam, so that on the curved ships he might cast wondrous fire” (15.596–597), and “With this in mind he roused at the hollow ships / Hector, son of Priam, though he was very eager himself” (15.603–604; [[zeus]], [[plan-of-zeus]], [[curved-ships]], [[hollow-ships]]). Twice in eight verses the patronymic names the man whom Zeus' plan now serves.

The accusative υἱὸν Πριάμοιο is new to the entry. It also claims the same phrase in earlier books: of Democoon, “a bastard son of Priam” (4.499; [[democoon]]), and of Hector in the verse of 15.239, in Achilles' answer to Ajax in Book 9 and when Iris finds Hector in Book 11 (9.651, 11.197). See [[hector]], [[priam]].
