summary: Retreat at the ships: Antilochus, steadfast in battle, flees from Hector like a beast that has done some harm and turns when he reaches his companions; the Argives give way from the first ships “under compulsion” but stand by the huts; and Ajax, hard pressed by missiles, draws back a little way “thinking he would die”, and holds the bench of the ship.
refs: [15.585-591, 15.655-658, 15.727-731]
---

Three retreats in the last part of the book show what giving ground can mean. Antilochus has killed Melanippus, but when Hector comes running, “Antilochus did not stand his ground, swift warrior though he was, / but fled in fear, like a wild beast that has done some harm” (15.585–586; see [[antilochus]] and [[the-beast-that-has-done-harm]]); he turns and stands again when he reaches his companions (15.591). The Argives, hemmed in by their own first row of ships, “gave way from the ships, under compulsion” (ἀνάγκῃ, 15.655) and stood by the huts, held by shame and fear (15.656–658; see [[aidos]] and [[ships]]). Ajax gives way last and least: “And Ajax no longer stood his ground; for he was hard pressed by missiles; / but he drew back a little way, thinking he would die” (ἀνεχάζετο τυτθόν, 15.727–728), to the seven-foot bench, and there he stands and keeps off the fire with his spear (15.729–731; see [[ajax]] and [[footstool]]). The book's other withdrawal is a god's: Poseidon leaves the battle at Zeus' order, against his will (15.211–219; see [[poseidon]]).
