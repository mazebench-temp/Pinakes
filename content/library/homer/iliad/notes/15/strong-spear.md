---
summary: Teucer takes “a strong spear, tipped with sharp bronze,” and Menelaus tells Antilochus that no Achaean is “as valiant as you in fighting.”
---

**Teucer.** His bow broken, he arms as a spearman: “and he took a strong spear, tipped with sharp bronze, / and went his way” (15.482–483; [[teucer]], [[sharp]], [[arming-scene]]), in the verse that ended both of Nestor's armings, at night in Book 10 and at the start of Book 14 (10.135, 14.12); in Book 10, too, βῆ δʼ ἰέναι follows. The archer becomes a spearman by the verse of the spear.

**Antilochus.** Menelaus urges him on: “Antilochus, no other of the Achaeans is younger than you, / nor swifter of foot, nor as valiant as you in fighting” (15.569–570; [[antilochus]], [[menelaus]]). ἄλκιμος is rendered “valiant.” He leaps out and kills Melanippus, then flees before Hector (15.573–589; [[swift]], [[steadfast-in-battle]]).
