---
summary: Periphetes of Mycenae is the son of Copreus, who carried the messages of Eurystheus “to mighty Heracles.”
---

“He killed only Periphetes of Mycenae, / the dear son of Copreus, who used to go for lord Eurystheus / with messages to mighty Heracles” (15.638–640; [[periphetes-of-mycenae]], [[copreus]], [[eurystheus]], [[heracles]]). The periphrasis βίῃ Ἡρακληείῃ, “the Heraclean might,” names the hero in the verse that recalls his labors under Eurystheus, which Agamemnon tells of in Book 19 (19.132–133). The narrator then judges the son better than the father, “a far worse father,” in every kind of prowess (15.641–643).
