---
summary: Hector's men shout and drive “the chariot-drawing horses” after him as Apollo kicks down the banks of the trench and bridges a road over it.
---

“So saying, he drove on the horses with the whip from the shoulder, / calling to the Trojans along the ranks; and with him they / all shouted and drove the chariot-drawing horses / with a wondrous din; and before them Phoebus Apollo / easily kicked down the banks of the deep trench with his feet” (15.352–356; [[hector]], [[apollo]], [[wondrous]], [[the-achaean-wall]]). The chariots that could not cross the ditch in Book 12, where Polydamas made the Trojans leave them behind (12.61–81; [[polydamas]]), now go over it on the road the god makes, “long and wide, as far as the cast of a spear / goes” (15.358–359). In Book 16 the same epithet names the horses that break their chariots in the ditch as the Trojans flee (16.370–371).
