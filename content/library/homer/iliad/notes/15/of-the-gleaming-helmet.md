---
summary: “Hector of the gleaming helmet” answers Apollo feebly, and Ajax asks whether the Achaeans expect to go home on foot “if Hector of the gleaming helmet takes the ships.”
---

**The weak Hector.** “Then Hector of the gleaming helmet spoke to him feebly” (15.246; [[hector]], [[apollo]], [[good-sir]]). The verse τὸν δʼ ὀλιγοδρανέων προσέφη κορυθαίολος Ἕκτωρ will be heard once more, when he speaks to Achilles as he dies (22.337). Here he is still dazed from Ajax's stone; Apollo will give him back his strength (15.253–262).

**The threat.** Ajax to the Argives: “Do you expect, if Hector of the gleaming helmet takes the ships, / that you will each come on foot to your native land?” (15.504–505; [[ajax]], [[dear-native-land]]). The verse-end ἕλῃ κορυθαίολος Ἕκτωρ was Odysseus' warning to Diomedes in Book 11 (11.315); it is closer to coming true now.
