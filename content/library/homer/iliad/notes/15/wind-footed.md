---
summary: “Swift Iris, wind-footed,” obeys Zeus and goes down from Ida to Poseidon, and later answers him, asking whether he will soften his reply.
---

“So he spoke, and swift Iris, wind-footed, did not disobey, / and she went down from the mountains of Ida to sacred Ilios” (15.168–169; [[iris]], [[zeus]], [[hieros]]), the two verses with which she went to Hector in Book 11 (11.195–196). Between them and her arrival the narrator gives the simile of snow and hail driven by the North Wind, and the formula loses its epithet: “so swiftly swift Iris flew in her eagerness” (15.170–172; [[snow-and-hail]], [[born-of-the-clear-sky]]), with ὠκέα Ἶρις alone, in the verse that brought Hera to Olympus (15.83). The full formula returns when she answers Poseidon: “Then swift Iris, wind-footed, answered him” (15.200; [[poseidon]], [[dark-haired]]).
