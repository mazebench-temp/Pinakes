---
summary: The lions of Book 15 are all Trojan. Hector, restored by Apollo, is the bearded lion that turns back the hunters (15.275–276); the Trojans rush on the ships like lions that eat raw flesh (15.592); and Hector falls on the Achaeans like a deadly lion on a herd of cattle in a marsh (15.630–636).
---

**Hector's lions.** The first lion of the book is the one that appears at the hunters' shouting. Dogs and country men chase a stag or a wild goat until a rock or a forest saves it, and then λὶς ἠϋγένειος, “a bearded lion”, stands in their path and turns them all back: so the Danaans, pursuing the Trojans, see Hector back in the battle and lose heart (15.271–280; see [[the-hunters-and-the-lion]]). λίς is the short form of λέων, used only in comparisons. The last is the deadly lion that comes on a countless herd grazing in a marsh, leaps into the middle past the young herdsman and devours one cow, while the rest all flee; so the Achaeans flee before Hector and Zeus, and he kills only Periphetes (15.630–638; see [[lion-among-cattle]]). Between them the whole Trojan army rushes on the ships “like lions that eat raw flesh” (15.592; see [[lions-and-boars]]).

**The lion turned.** In Book 11 the lions were mostly Achaean: Agamemnon's in the morning, and Ajax's in the afternoon, when dogs and country men drove the lion from the farmyard (11.113–121, 11.172–178, 11.548–557; see [[lion-driven-from-the-farmyard]]). Book 15 repeats the verse of the dogs and country men (15.272 = 11.549), but now the lion turns them back, and it is Hector. The Achaeans of Book 15 are given the likenesses of what lions prey on and what lions scatter: hunters, a herd, a fawn's pursuer that turns into a beast in flight (15.579–588).
