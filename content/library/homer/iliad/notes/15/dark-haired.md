---
summary: Iris twice addresses Poseidon as “dark-haired earth-holder” as she brings him Zeus' order and asks whether he will soften his answer.
---

Iris opens both her speeches with the vocative pair γαιήοχε κυανοχαῖτα: “A message for you, dark-haired earth-holder, / I have come here bringing, from aegis-bearing Zeus” (15.174–175), and, after his angry reply, “Am I really to carry it in just this way, dark-haired earth-holder, / this word to Zeus, so harsh and stern, / or will you turn it somewhat?” (15.201–203; [[iris]], [[poseidon]], [[earth-holder]], [[krateros]]). The epithet so far stood with his name, κυανοχαῖτα Ποσειδάων (13.563, 14.390); here it is spoken to him. The Odyssey has the same address in the Cyclops' prayer to his father (*Odyssey* 9.528).
