---
summary: Zeus and Poseidon each grant the other strength, “strong though he is,” Iris calls Zeus' message “so harsh and stern,” and Ajax bids the Argives feel shame before one another “in the fierce battles.”
en: [stern]
---

**The brothers.** Zeus tells Iris to warn Poseidon “lest, strong though he is, he may not dare, when I come at him, / to await me” (15.164–165), and Poseidon answers, “but at his ease, / strong though he is, let him stay in his third share” (15.194–195; [[zeus]], [[poseidon]], [[the-lots-of-the-three-brothers]]). The concessive κρατερός περ ἐών is used in Book 15 only by the two brothers of each other, each conceding the other's strength before denying him the right to use it.

**The message.** Iris asks Poseidon: “Am I really to carry it in just this way, dark-haired earth-holder, / this word to Zeus, so harsh and stern” (15.201–202; [[iris]], [[dark-haired]]), or will he turn it? κρατερός of a word is the harsh command, as Agamemnon laid a stern command on Chryses (1.25; [[stern-command]]).

**The battles.** Ajax: “Friends, be men, and set shame in your hearts, / and feel shame before one another in the fierce battles” (15.561–562; [[ajax]], [[aidos]], [[exhortation]]).
