---
summary: Hector rages at the ships “as when spear-brandishing Ares, or deadly fire, / rages in the mountains.”
---

“He raged as when spear-brandishing Ares, or deadly fire, / rages in the mountains, in the thickets of the deep forest” (15.605–606; [[hector]], [[spear-brandishing]], [[forest-fire]], [[blosyros]]). ὀλοὸν πῦρ is the fire that the Trojans are eager “to throw” on the ships (13.629); here it is the likeness of the man who leads them. The simile names the war god and the fire together, and Hector is soon “blazing all over with fire” as he leaps into the throng (15.623). A deadly lion follows a few verses later, with the compound ὀλοόφρων (15.630; [[oloophron]]).
