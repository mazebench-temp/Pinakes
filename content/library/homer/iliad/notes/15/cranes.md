---
summary: Cranes are the second of the three birds on which Hector, like a tawny eagle, swoops as they feed beside a river (15.690–692).
---

The crane's flock in Book 2 was noise and movement, the Achaean army pouring onto the plain (2.459–466), and in Book 3 the cranes' clamor as they fly to the streams of Ocean was the Trojans' advance (3.2–7; see [[cranes-and-pygmies]]). In Book 15 the same verse, “geese or cranes or long-necked swans” (15.692 = 2.460), makes them prey: they are feeding by a river when the eagle swoops, and the eagle is Hector, making for the ships with Zeus' hand at his back (15.690–695; see [[the-eagle-and-the-birds]] and [[birds-on-the-asian-meadow]]).
