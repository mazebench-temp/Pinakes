---
summary: Zeus foretells that Achilles will rouse “his companion, Patroclus”, and Patroclus goes to him trusting in “the persuasion of a companion”. In the battle the companions of the leaders die one after another: Stichius, “a trusted companion” of Menestheus, Lycophron, the “trusted companion” of Ajax and Teucer, Cleitus, the companion of Polydamas, and Otus, the companion of Meges; and Periphetes dies close to his companions, who cannot help him.
---

The word frames the book's story of Patroclus. Zeus' forecast names him by it: Achilles “will rouse up his companion, / Patroclus” (ἑταῖρον, 15.64–65; see [[plan-of-zeus]] and [[patroclus]]), and Patroclus leaves Eurypylus with Nestor's maxim, “Good is the persuasion of a companion” (15.404; see [[the-persuasion-of-a-friend]]).

In the battle the ἑταῖροι are the men who serve and stand by a leader, and the killings fall on them. Hector kills Stichius, “a trusted companion of great-hearted Menestheus” (πιστὸν ἑταῖρον, 15.331; see [[stichius]]); his spear, meant for Ajax, kills Lycophron, whom Ajax mourns as “our trusted companion” (15.437; see [[lycophron]]); Teucer's arrow kills Cleitus, “the companion of Polydamas” (15.446; see [[cleitus]]); and Polydamas kills Otus, “companion of the son of Phyleus” (15.519; see [[otus-of-cyllene]]). Companions also save. Hector, revived, knows “his companions around him” (15.241), and Antilochus, fleeing Hector, turns and stands “when he reached the crowd of his companions” (15.591; see [[antilochus]]). But Periphetes, tripped by his own shield, is killed “close to his dear companions”, who “could not, though grieving for their companion, / help him; for they themselves feared brilliant Hector greatly” (15.650–652; see [[periphetes-of-mycenae]]).
