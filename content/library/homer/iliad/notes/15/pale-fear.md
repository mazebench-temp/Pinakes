summary: The book opens with the Trojans halted by their chariots beyond the ditch, “pale with fear, in their rout”; the book adds the phrase χλωροὶ ὑπαὶ δείους.
grc: ["χλωροὶ ὑπαὶ δείους"]
en: [pale with fear]
---

“But when they had crossed the stakes and the ditch / in their flight, and many had been brought down at the hands of the Danaans, / they halted beside their chariots and stood their ground, / pale with fear, in their rout” (15.1–4; [[trojans]], [[the-achaean-wall]], [[deos]], [[phobos]]). The phrase χλωρὸς ὑπαὶ δείους is the one of Dolon, standing terrified as Odysseus and Diomedes came up on him (10.376; [[dolon]]); here it is plural, of a whole army that Poseidon has routed while Zeus slept (14.506–522; [[the-deception-of-zeus]]). In the same verse Zeus wakes (15.4), and the fear passes to the other side before the book is out. The plural χλωροὶ ὑπαὶ δείους is new to the entry.
