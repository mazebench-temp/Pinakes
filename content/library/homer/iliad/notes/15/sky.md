summary: Hera swears by Earth and “the wide Heaven above”; in Poseidon's account of the lots Zeus got “the wide sky, in the upper air and the clouds”; and Nestor stretches his hands “to the starry sky” as he prays.
# Οὐρανὸς, capitalized at 15.36 as the power called to witness; the same nominative is the sky at 5.750, 8.16 and 8.394
# (later 18.485, 21.388).
grc: [Οὐρανὸς]
---

**Heaven as witness.** Hera's oath begins: “Let Earth now be witness to this, and the wide Heaven above” (Γαῖα καὶ Οὐρανὸς εὐρὺς ὕπερθε, 15.36; see [[earth]], [[styx]] and [[oath]]). The digitized Iliad capitalizes Earth and Heaven here, as powers called on by name, and the translation renders them “Earth” and “Heaven”; elsewhere οὐρανὸς εὐρύς is “the wide sky” (see the translation's note on 15.36–38 and [[wide-sky]]). In Hesiod Earth and Heaven are the parents of the Titans (*Theogony* 132–138; see [[titans]]).

**Zeus' share.** In the division by lot, “Zeus got the wide sky, in the upper air and the clouds” (ἐν αἰθέρι καὶ νεφέλῃσι, 15.192; see [[the-lots-of-the-three-brothers]] and [[zeus]]). The upper air and the clouds are where he hung Hera with anvils on her feet (“And you hung in the upper air and the clouds”, 15.20; see [[hera-hung-from-the-sky]]), and from the upper air he defends Hector at the ships (ἀπʼ αἰθέρος, 15.610). The sky is Zeus' alone; the earth and Olympus are common to all three brothers (15.193; see [[olympus]]).

**Hands to the sky.** When the Trojans reach the ships, Nestor above all the Achaeans “prayed, stretching out his hands to the starry sky” (15.371; see [[nestor]], [[prayer]] and [[starry]]), and Zeus thunders in answer (15.377). The Cyclops prays to Poseidon in the same verse, stretching his hands to the starry sky (*Odyssey* 9.527).
