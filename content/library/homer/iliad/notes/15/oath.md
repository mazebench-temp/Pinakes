**The witnesses.** Zeus wakes on Ida, sees the rout of the Trojans and Hector vomiting blood, and threatens Hera with the memory of her punishment (15.4–33; see [[hera-hung-from-the-sky]]). She shudders and swears:

{{quote:4b7688f4-e800-52d1-ac2e-18a0d40e18bb}}

ἴστω, “let … be witness”, calls the witnesses in the order of the world: Earth and Heaven, then the water of Styx, “the greatest / and most dread oath for the blessed gods” (see [[styx]], [[earth]] and [[sky]]). Calypso swears to Odysseus with the same three verses (*Odyssey* 5.184–186). Hera adds two witnesses of her own, “your own sacred head, and the wedded bed of us two, / by which I would never swear idly” (15.39–40; see [[bed]] and [[marriage]]). The head is the one with which Zeus nods his promises, the greatest sign he can give (1.524–527; see [[neuo]]); the bed is the one in which, as Zeus has just said, she lay with him and deceived him (15.31–33; see [[the-deception-of-zeus]]). Earlier the same day Sleep had made her swear by the same water of Styx, with the Titans below as witnesses (14.270–280; see the note on Book 14).

**To the letter.** The oath itself is narrow: “it is not through my will that Poseidon the earth-shaker / harms the Trojans and Hector” (15.41–42). That is true. Poseidon came to the Achaeans of his own accord, pitying them and angry with Zeus (13.15–16), and it was Sleep, not Hera, who sent him word to help them while Zeus slept (14.354–360). She hedges even so: “perhaps it is his own heart that urges him on” (που, 15.43), and she offers to advise him to go wherever Zeus leads (15.45–46). Hera does not swear that she did nothing; she swears that Poseidon did not act by her will. Zeus smiles and takes her at her word, and sends her to bring Iris and Apollo (15.47–77; see [[smiling-at-him]] and [[truth-and-deception]]).
