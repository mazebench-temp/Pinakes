---
summary: Meges' corselet came from Ephyra on the river Selleis, where Phyleus' guest-friend Euphetes gave it to him (15.530–532).
---

Φυλεὺς / ἤγαγεν ἐξ Ἐφύρης, ποταμοῦ ἄπο Σελλήεντος, “Phyleus had once / brought it from Ephyra, from the river Selleis” (15.530–531; see [[phyleus]], [[meges]], [[euphetes]] and [[corselet]]). The half-verse is the one in which Heracles brought Astyocheia, the mother of Tlepolemus, from her home (2.659; see [[astyocheia]] and [[heracles]]), and it names the same place. Strabo takes the two passages together. He puts this Ephyra in Elis, on the Selleis that flows from Pholoe and reaches the sea between Cyllene and Cape Chelonatas, a fourth Ephyra beside the Thesprotian, the Thessalian and Corinth; from it, he says, came Tlepolemus' mother, Meges' corselet, and the man-killing drugs that Odysseus is said to have sought in Ephyra, and that the suitors fear Telemachus will fetch from there (*Geography* 8.3.5; *Odyssey* 1.259–262, 2.328–329; see [[strabo]] and [[selleis-ephyra]]). Heracles' campaigns, he argues, were rather in that country, and no river Selleis flows by the other Ephyras. If he is right, Phyleus, an Elean prince, found his guest-friend in his own country (see [[phyleus-and-augeas]]).
