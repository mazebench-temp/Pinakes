summary: Poseidon, who got the gray sea by lot, is ordered out of the battle and back into the shining sea, and plunges into it. Zeus recalls the storms Hera sent over the barren sea, and at the ships the Achaeans fight with the sea at their backs.
# πόντῳ is always the sea: it also catches 4.424 and 14.258 (later 16.746, 23.316, 24.79).
grc: [πόντῳ]
---

**Poseidon's lot.** When the sons of Cronus drew lots, “I got by lot the gray sea, to dwell in forever”, Poseidon says (πολιὴν ἅλα, 15.190; see [[poseidon]], [[gray-sea]] and [[the-lots-of-the-three-brothers]]). Zeus orders him to leave the battle and go “among the tribes of the gods, or into the shining sea” (15.161, 15.177; see [[shining-sea]]), and Poseidon yields: he “went and plunged into the sea; and the Achaean heroes missed him” (15.219). Zeus tells Apollo that he “has gone into the shining sea, avoiding the sheer anger / of mine” (15.223–224; see [[zeus]]).

**Hera's storm.** Zeus reminds Hera that she sent Heracles “over the barren sea, devising evil”, winning over the storms with the help of the North Wind (15.26–27; see [[barren-sea]], [[boreas]] and [[hera-drives-heracles-to-cos]]).

**The sea at the camp.** The book's similes keep the sea in view of the fighting at the ships. Apollo throws down the wall as a boy beside the sea scatters the sand (15.362–364; see [[the-boy-and-the-sand]] and [[apollo]]), the Trojans come over the wall like a great wave of the wide-wayed sea over a ship's side (15.381–384; see [[the-wave-over-the-ships-side]]), the Danaans hold like a sheer rock “close to the gray sea” against the winds and the waves (15.618–622; see [[the-rock-by-the-sea]]), and Hector falls on them like a wave on a swift ship (15.624–628; see [[the-ship-in-the-storm]]). At the end Ajax tells the Achaeans that they have no wall or city to fall back on, “but here in the plain of the close-armored Trojans / we sit with our backs to the sea, far from our native land” (πόντῳ κεκλιμένοι, 15.739–740; see [[ajax]] and [[the-light-is-in-our-hands]]). In Book 14 Agamemnon had proposed launching the ships and escaping by sea (14.74–81).
