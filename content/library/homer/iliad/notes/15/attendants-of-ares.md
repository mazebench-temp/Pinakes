summary: Ajax, fighting from the ships, calls on the Danaans with the full formal address, “Friends, Danaan heroes, attendants of Ares.”
---

“Friends, Danaan heroes, attendants of Ares, / be men, my friends, and remember your furious courage” (15.733–734; [[ajax]], [[danaans]], [[furious]], [[exhortation]]). The verse is the address with which Agamemnon opened the assembly in which he tested the army (2.110), and with which Nestor, in the rout of Book 6, forbade the men to stay behind for spoils (6.67); Agamemnon will use it once more in the assembly of reconciliation (19.78). Here it is spoken by a man on a ship's bench with a spear in his hand, at the last line of the Achaean defense: there is no city near and no stronger wall, “so in our hands lies the light, not in softness in war” (15.735–741; [[the-achaean-wall]], [[heros]]).
