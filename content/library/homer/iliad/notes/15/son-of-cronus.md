---
summary: Themis asks whether “the son of Cronus” has frightened Hera, Iris and Apollo find “the far-seeing son of Cronus” on Gargarus, and Apollo tells Hector that the son of Cronus has sent him; Poseidon reminds Zeus that they are three brothers born of Cronus.
---

The patronymic names Zeus three times in the book. Themis: “surely the son of Cronus, your husband, has put you to flight” (15.91; [[themis-the-goddess]], [[hera]]), with Κρόνου πάϊς. Iris and Apollo “found the far-seeing son of Cronus on the peak of Gargarus” (15.152; [[far-seeing]], [[gargarus]]). And Apollo to Hector: “Take heart now: such a helper has the son of Cronus / sent forth from Ida” (15.254–255; [[apollo]], [[hector]], [[of-the-golden-sword]]).

Between these, Poseidon gives the name its full meaning: “For we are three brothers, born of Cronus, whom Rhea bore, / Zeus and I, and the third is Hades, lord of those below” (15.187–188; [[poseidon]], [[cronus]], [[rhea]], [[hades]], [[the-lots-of-the-three-brothers]]). The title that sets Zeus apart is one that he shares with his brothers, and Poseidon uses it to claim equality. Zeus, for his part, claims to be “the elder by birth” (15.166, 15.182). See [[zeus]].
