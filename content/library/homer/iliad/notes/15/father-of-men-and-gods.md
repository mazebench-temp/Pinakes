---
summary: Waking on Ida, the father of men and gods pities Hector as he lies vomiting blood, and smiles when Hera has sworn her oath.
---

The formula marks the two turns of Zeus' first scene with Hera. Seeing Hector lying on the plain, gasping and vomiting blood, “the father of men and gods pitied him, / and looking at Hera darkly and terribly he spoke a word to her” (15.12–13; [[hector]], [[hera]], [[pity]], [[looking-darkly]]); it is the only time the formula stands with ἐλέησε, “pitied.” After his threats and Hera's oath, “So she spoke, and the father of men and gods smiled, / and answering her he addressed her with winged words” (15.47–48; [[oath]], [[winged-words]]). The verse of the smile is the one in which Zeus smiled at Athena's mockery of Aphrodite (5.426). The anger of the waking god turns to command: he sends Hera to fetch Iris and Apollo, and foretells the course of the war (15.49–77; [[plan-of-zeus]]). See [[zeus]].
