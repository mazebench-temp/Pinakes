---
summary: Hera is “queen Hera” as she shudders at Zeus' threats, as he addresses her, and in the three verses of her flight to Olympus, her sitting down among the gods and her going back to her throne.
---

With βοῶπις the title marks Hera's fear and Zeus' address to her (15.34, 15.49; [[ox-eyed]]). Then it marks her movements on her errand. She flies to Olympus as swiftly as a traveler's thought, “so swiftly queen Hera flew in her eagerness” (15.83; [[shrewd-mind]]); after her bitter speech to Themis, “So saying, queen Hera sat down” (15.100; [[themis-the-goddess]]); and when she has given Iris and Apollo their orders, “So saying, queen Hera went back, / and sat on her throne” (15.149–150; [[throne]], [[iris]], [[apollo]]). In Book 14 the title went with her treachery (14.197, 14.300, 14.329); here it goes with her obedience. See [[hera]].
