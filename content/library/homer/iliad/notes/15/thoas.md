**The man.** The narrator introduces him with his skills before he speaks (see [[andraemon]] and [[aetolians]]). Αἰτωλῶν ὄχʼ ἄριστος, “by far the best of the Aetolians”, was said of Periphas, son of Ochesius, whom Ares killed and was stripping when Diomedes wounded him (5.842–843; see [[periphas]]). Thoas has both of the excellences that Phoenix was sent to teach Achilles, “to be a speaker of words and a doer of deeds” (9.443; see [[speaker-of-words-doer-of-deeds]]): the javelin and the standing fight (σταδίη, 15.283), and the debates in which “the young men contended in speeches” (15.284). He is counted among the young men, as Nestor counted Diomedes, “in counsel … the best among all men of your age” (9.54; see [[diomedes]]). In Book 13, when Poseidon took his voice, Idomeneus praised Thoas for urging on “another man too, wherever you see him holding back” (13.228–229); here he does it in his own voice.

**The counsel.** ὅ σφιν ἐϋφρονέων ἀγορήσατο, “with good intent he addressed them” (15.285; see [[with-good-intent]]). He begins with the verse of Poseidon's amazement at the sight of the Trojans among the ships, “Ah, truly this is a great wonder that I see with my eyes” (15.286 = 13.99; see [[o-popoi]]). Hector has risen up again, “escaping the fates”, when every Achaean hoped he had died at the hands of Ajax (15.287–289; see [[hector]] and [[ajax]]); “But once again one of the gods has rescued and saved / Hector”, and “not without / loud-thundering Zeus does he stand forth as champion” (15.290–293). Thoas reads the battle rightly without knowing the god's name: Apollo has revived Hector and is about to go before him with the aegis (15.243–262, 15.306–311; see [[apollo]] and [[aegis]]). His plan is to divide the army. “Let us order the multitude to go back to the ships; / but let us ourselves, all of us who claim to be the best in the army, / stand firm”, meeting Hector “with our spears held up”, and Hector will be afraid to plunge into the throng of the Danaans (15.295–299; see [[commoners]], [[aristos]] and [[euchomai]]).

**Obeyed.** “So he spoke, and they listened closely to him and obeyed” (15.300). Ajax, Idomeneus, Teucer, Meriones and Meges call the best men together and set the battle in order against Hector, while the multitude goes back to the ships (15.301–305; see [[battle-order]]). The line holds as long as men alone are fighting: the Argives stand their ground in a body, and the missiles of both sides strike home. Then Apollo shakes the aegis in their faces and shouts, “and they forgot their furious courage” (15.312–322). The counsel was sound, and it could not hold against a god. Thoas is not named again in the book.
