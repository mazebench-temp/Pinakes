---
summary: Telamonian Ajax speaks Agamemnon's lines at the ships, with “set shame in your hearts” for Agamemnon's “take a valiant heart”, and this time the effect is told at once: the Argives lay his word to heart and fence the ships with a bulwark of bronze.
passages:
  - 15.560-567 | Ajax: set shame in your hearts
---

The lines come in the fight over Dolops, son of Lampus. Menelaus has killed him, and Hector calls on all his kinsmen, Melanippus first, to come and save the armor (15.525–559; see [[dolops-son-of-lampus]] and [[fighting-over-the-body]]). “And great Telamonian Ajax urged on the Argives” (15.560) with the four verses of Agamemnon's exhortation in Book 5. Only the first half-verse is new. Agamemnon said “and take a valiant heart” (καὶ ἄλκιμον ἦτορ ἕλεσθε, 5.529); Ajax says “and set shame in your hearts” (καὶ αἰδῶ θέσθʼ ἐνὶ θυμῷ, 15.561), so that the word αἰδώς stands in the first verse and its verb in the second (see [[aidos]] and [[exhortation]]).

The narrator follows the effect: “So he spoke, and they too were eager to defend themselves, / and they laid his word to heart, and fenced the ships / with a bulwark of bronze; but Zeus roused the Trojans against them” (15.565–567). In Book 5 the maxim was proved by Agamemnon's own spear (5.533–540); here it is answered by a wall of men, and by Zeus. The first verse returns once more in the book, in Nestor's mouth, with shame “before other men” in place of shame before one another (15.661–662).
