---
summary: In the simile of the lion among the cattle, the herdsman does not yet know how to fight a wild beast “over the slaughter of a cow with curving horns”; the book adds the genitive ἕλικος.
grc: [ἕλικος]
---

“But he, like a deadly lion coming upon cattle / that graze in the low wet ground of a great marsh, / countless, and among them a herdsman who does not yet well know / how to fight a wild beast over the slaughter of a cow with curving horns” (15.630–633; [[hector]], [[lion]], [[cattle]], [[oloophron]]). The epithet usually stands in the plural, in the formula εἰλίποδας ἕλικας βοῦς (9.466; [[shambling]]); here it is a single cow, the one that the lion kills while the herdsman walks with the first and the last of the herd (15.634–636). The genitive ἕλικος is new to the entry.
