grc: [ἀτειρέας]

“And again a bitter battle was fought beside the ships; / you would have said that unwearied and unyielding they met one another / in war, so furiously did they fight” (15.696–698; [[apostrophe]], [[polemos]], [[ships]]). The narrator turns to the listener, φαίης κε, “you would have said” (as of Odysseus' look in Book 3, 3.220), and gives the fighters the epithet that elsewhere belongs to bronze and to a heart that will not bend (3.60, 14.25): the men themselves seem made of something that does not wear down. The accusative plural ἀτειρέας is new to the entry.
