summary: Zeus, sending Apollo with the aegis, tells him, “let shining Hector be your own care, you who shoot from afar”; the book adds the vocative ἑκατηβόλε.
grc: [ἑκατηβόλε]
en: [who shoot from afar]
---

“But take the tasseled aegis in your hands, / and shake it hard over them, and put the Achaean heroes to flight; / and let shining Hector be your own care, you who shoot from afar” (15.229–231; [[zeus]], [[apollo]], [[aegis]], [[tasseled]], [[phaidimos]], [[hector]]). The father calls his son by the archer's title at the moment he gives him a weapon that is not a bow. In the Iliad the vocative ἑκατηβόλε is found only here; elsewhere the epithet stands in the genitive, as in the verse of Diomedes giving way before “the wrath of Apollo who shoots from afar” (5.444). The vocative is new to the entry.
