summary: The mind of Zeus works through the book: it rouses Hector, and its shrewdness guards him from Teucer's arrow. The mind darts like a traveler's thought in the simile of Hera's flight; Ares' mind is gone, Athena says; and at the end each army has its own mind, the Achaeans thinking they will perish, the Trojans hoping to burn the ships.
passages:
  - 15.458-465 | The shrewd mind of Zeus
---

**The mind of Zeus.** In Book 14 Hera beguiled Διὸς νόον αἰγιόχοιο, the mind of aegis-bearing Zeus (14.160, 14.252; see the note on Book 14). Awake, it acts: Hector's gasping and sweat “had stopped, since the mind of aegis-bearing Zeus had roused him” (Διὸς νόος, 15.242; see [[hector]]), and Teucer's arrow “did not escape the shrewd mind of Zeus, who was guarding / Hector” (πυκινὸν νόον, 15.461–462; see [[bow]] and [[shrewd-word]]). Zeus had wished that Hera might think as he does, so that Poseidon too “would quickly turn his mind to follow your heart and mine” (μεταστρέψειε νόον, 15.52; see [[the-hearts-of-good-men-can-be-turned]]).

**Minds of gods and men.** The simile of Hera's flight compares her speed to a thought: “As when the mind of a man darts, who has traveled / over much of the earth, and in his shrewd mind he thinks, / ‘If only I were there, or there’” (15.80–82; see [[the-travelers-thought]]). Athena tells Ares, raging to avenge his son, that his “mind is gone, and your sense of shame” (νόος δʼ ἀπόλωλε, 15.129; see [[aidos]]). Ajax gives the Argives his counsel as a νόος: “For us there is no better thought or counsel than this, / to mingle our hands and our might with theirs hand to hand” (νόος καὶ μῆτις, 15.509–510; see [[metis]] and [[better-to-perish-once-and-for-all]]). Periphetes, better than his father, “in mind … was among the first of the Mycenaeans” (15.643; see [[periphetes-of-mycenae]]). At the height of the battle the narrator gives both armies their minds: “And as they fought, this was their mind: the Achaeans / thought they would not escape from the evil, but would perish, / and the heart in the breast of each of the Trojans hoped / to set fire to the ships and kill the Achaean heroes” (15.699–702; see [[thymos]]).
