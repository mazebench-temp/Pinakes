---
summary: Zeus reminds Hera that she sent Heracles “over the barren sea” with the storms of the North Wind; the book adds the accusative ἀτρύγετον.
grc: [ἀτρύγετον]
---

“The ceaseless pain for divine Heracles, / whom you, winning over the storms with the help of the North Wind, / sent over the barren sea, devising evil, / and then you carried him off to well-peopled Cos” (15.25–28; [[heracles]], [[hera]], [[boreas]], [[cos]], [[hera-drives-heracles-to-cos]]). Zeus, awake, tells from his side the story that Sleep told from his in Book 14 (14.249–262; [[sleep]]), and he tells it as a threat. The sea that bore his son away is the barren sea of the formula πόντον ἐπʼ ἀτρύγετον, frequent in the Odyssey (*Odyssey* 5.84, 5.140, 7.79). The accusative ἀτρύγετον is new to the entry. See [[sea]].
