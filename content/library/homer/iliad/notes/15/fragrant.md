---
summary: Iris and Apollo find Zeus sitting on the peak of Gargarus with “a fragrant cloud” wreathed around him; the book adds the form θυόεν.
grc: [θυόεν]
---

“And they found the far-seeing son of Cronus on the peak of Gargarus, / sitting, and a fragrant cloud was wreathed around him” (15.152–153; [[zeus]], [[gargarus]], [[far-seeing]], [[cloud-gatherer]]). θυόεις is formed from θύος, “offering, incense,” like θυήεις, the epithet of the altar of Zeus on the same peak, “where he has a precinct and a fragrant altar” (8.48; [[altar]]). The cloud is fragrant as an altar is, with the smoke of offerings, on the mountain where Zeus has his precinct, and it follows the golden cloud of Book 14 in which he lay with Hera (14.350–351; [[the-golden-cloud]]). The form θυόεν, found only here in Homer, is new to the entry.
