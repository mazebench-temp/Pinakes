---
summary: Iris twice addresses Poseidon as “dark-haired earth-holder,” and Zeus tells Apollo that “the earth-holder, the shaker of the earth” has gone into the sea; the book adds the vocative γαιήοχε.
grc: [γαιήοχε]
---

**Iris' address.** Bringing Zeus' order, Iris begins: “A message for you, dark-haired earth-holder” (15.174), and after his angry reply, “Am I really to carry it in just this way, dark-haired earth-holder” (15.201; [[iris]], [[dark-haired]], [[poseidon]]). The vocative γαιήοχε is new to the entry. Between the two addresses Poseidon answers that the earth is common to all three sons of Cronus (15.193; [[the-lots-of-the-three-brothers]]): the god whose title is “holder of the earth” claims a share in it equal to Zeus'.

**Zeus.** When Poseidon has gone, Zeus tells Apollo: “for already the earth-holder, the shaker of the earth, / has gone into the shining sea, avoiding the sheer anger / of mine” (15.222–224; [[zeus]], [[shaker-of-the-earth]], [[shining-sea]], [[steep]]). The line-end γαιήοχος ἐννοσίγαιος named him in Book 14 as Sleep ran to give him the message that let him rule the battle (14.354–355); here it names him as he leaves it.
