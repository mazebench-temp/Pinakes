---
summary: Poseidon claims to be Zeus' “equal in honor”, since the world was divided in three by lot and each brother “has his share of honor”. The honor Zeus promised Thetis for Achilles now runs through Hector, whom Zeus honors and gives glory for a short while; and in the houses of Ajax and of Priam an exile and a cousin are honored like kin.
grc: [ὁμότιμον, τίμα]
passages:
  - 15.184-199 | Poseidon's claim to equal honor
  - 15.596-614 | Zeus honors Hector
---

**Equal in honor.** Zeus' order through Iris claims the right of the stronger and the elder (15.165–167, 15.181–183). Poseidon answers with a claim of honor: Zeus “has spoken arrogantly, / if he will hold me back by force against my will, when I am his equal in honor” (ὁμότιμον, 15.185–186; the word only here in Homer). He gives the ground of it in the story of the lots:

{{quote:bacffc5f-a805-5f8a-818e-1919b31d69f4}}

“All things have been divided in three, and each has his share of honor” (ἕκαστος δʼ ἔμμορε τιμῆς, 15.189; see [[the-lots-of-the-three-brothers]] and [[moira]]). Here τιμή is a domain, the sea, the darkness or the sky, held as a share and defended as a right, as Nestor said that “no ordinary share of honor” falls to a scepter-bearing king (1.278; see [[kingship-and-authority]]). In the Odyssey the same half-verse is spoken of guests: Arete says of Odysseus, “he is my guest, and each has his share of honor” (*Odyssey* 11.338). Poseidon's τιμή is equal to Zeus' by lot, and he will live by no one's mind but his own (15.194–195). Iris answers with the elder's right, and he yields (15.200–211; see [[the-erinyes-follow-the-elder]] and [[honor-and-dishonor]]).

**Honor for Achilles, through Hector.** Zeus recalls that he promised Thetis, when she clasped his knees, to honor Achilles (τιμῆσαι, 15.77; 1.503–510; see [[thetis-supplicates-zeus]]). The honor comes by way of the man who will die for it. Zeus wishes to give Hector glory until a ship burns and Thetis' prayer is fulfilled (15.596–602), and so he stands by Hector, “who, though Hector was alone among many men, / honored him and gave him glory. Short-lived he was to be” (τίμα καὶ κύδαινε, 15.611–612; see [[kydos]] and [[hector]]). The honor given Hector is the instrument of Achilles' honor, and it ends with his life.

**Honored like kin.** Two men of the book are honored in houses not their own. Ajax mourns Lycophron, the exile from Cythera, “whom we honored in our halls like our own dear parents” (ἐτίομεν, 15.439; see [[lycophron]] and [[exile-and-refuge]]), and Priam honored Melanippus, his kinsman, “as he honored his children” (τίεν ἶσα τέκεσσι, 15.551; see [[melanippus-son-of-hicetaon]]). The aegis that Apollo carries is ἐρίτιμος, “highly prized” (15.361; 2.447; see [[aegis]]).
