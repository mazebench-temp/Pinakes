---
summary: Zeus, waking, threatens Hera that she may be the first to reap the fruit of her “grievous mischief.”
---

“I do not know whether, for your grievous mischief, you will not again / be the first to reap the fruit, and I lash you with blows” (15.16–17; [[zeus]], [[hera]], [[the-deception-of-zeus]]). κακορραφίη, “the stitching of evil,” is found only here in the Iliad; the Odyssey has the same pair of words in Circe's warning, that Odysseus may not suffer through grievous mischief (κακορραφίῃ ἀλεγεινῇ, *Odyssey* 12.26). The threat of blows leads Zeus to recall how he once hung Hera from the sky (15.18–24; [[hera-hung-from-the-sky]]).
