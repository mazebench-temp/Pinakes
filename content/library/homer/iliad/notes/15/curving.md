---
summary: Melanippus came back from Percote to Ilios “when the curving ships of the Danaans came,” in the verses that told the story of Imbrius.
---

“For a time he grazed shambling cattle / in Percote, while the enemy were still far away; / but when the curving ships of the Danaans came, / he came back to Ilios, and stood out among the Trojans, / and lived beside Priam; and Priam honored him as he honored his children” (15.547–551; [[melanippus-son-of-hicetaon]], [[percote]], [[shambling]], [[priam]]). The last three verses are those of Imbrius, Priam's son-in-law, whom Teucer killed (13.174–176; [[imbrius]]). The coming of the ships divides each man's life into before and after: a herdsman or a householder in his own place, then a fighter in Priam's city. See [[ships]].
