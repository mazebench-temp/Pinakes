---
summary: Hera flies from Ida to Olympus as swiftly as the mind of a much-traveled man who “in his shrewd mind” wishes himself in one place and another.
---

“As when the mind of a man darts, who has traveled / over much of the earth, and in his shrewd mind he thinks, / ‘If only I were there, or there,’ and longs for many things, / so swiftly queen Hera flew in her eagerness” (15.80–83; [[hera]], [[queenly]], [[noos]]). The line-end φρεσὶ πευκαλίμῃσι, which named the mind of Zeus that Hera and Sleep set out to overcome in Book 14 (14.165), here belongs to an ordinary traveler, and it measures the speed of a goddess. The shrewd mind is the one that has seen many places and can be in any of them at once in thought.
