---
summary: The ship of Protesilaus, which Hector seizes, is “a fine, swift-sailing ship”; the book adds ὠκύαλος, a second word for “swift-sailing.”
grc: [ὠκυάλου]
---

“And Hector took hold of the stern of a seafaring ship, / a fine, swift-sailing ship, which had brought Protesilaus / to Troy, and did not take him back again to his native land” (15.704–706; [[hector]], [[protesilaus]], [[seafaring-ship]], [[stern]]). The word is ὠκύαλος, not the ὠκύπορος of Book 1, but the translation renders both “swift-sailing.” It stands run over at the start of the verse, after the epithet of the sea crossing, with καλῆς, “fine”: the ship is praised as it is taken. The Iliad has it only here; the Odyssey uses it of the ship that the Sirens see approaching and of the Phoenicians' ship (*Odyssey* 12.182, 15.473). The genitive ὠκυάλου is new to the entry.
