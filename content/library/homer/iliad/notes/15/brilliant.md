---
summary: Hector is “brilliant” in the mouths of Zeus and of the narrator, Sarpedon and Achilles in Zeus' forecast, and Agenor as he kills Clonius; the shining sea of 15.161, 15.177 and 15.223 belongs to shining-sea.
except: [15.161, 15.177, 15.223]
---

**Zeus' forecast.** The epithet names the three men whose deaths Zeus foretells. Patroclus will kill many young men before Ilios, “and among them my son, brilliant Sarpedon. / And in anger for him brilliant Achilles will kill Hector” (15.67–68; [[sarpedon]], [[achilles]], [[plan-of-zeus]]). Zeus opens the book with the same word for Hector: Hera's trick “has stopped brilliant Hector from battle” (15.15; [[hera]], [[the-deception-of-zeus]]).

**Hector.** Apollo finds “the son of wise-hearted Priam, brilliant Hector, / sitting up” (15.239–240; [[apollo]], [[wise-hearted]], [[son-of-priam]]), in the verse in which Iris found him in Book 11 (11.197). Later the formula Ἕκτορα δῖον marks the fear he spreads: Antilochus “did not escape brilliant Hector's notice” and flees (15.583), and the companions of Periphetes cannot help him, “for they themselves feared brilliant Hector greatly” (15.652; [[antilochus]], [[periphetes-of-mycenae]]).

**Agenor.** In the Trojan advance, “brilliant Agenor killed Clonius” (15.340; [[agenor]], [[clonius]]).

**The sea.** εἰς ἅλα δῖαν, “into the shining sea,” in Zeus' message to Poseidon and its report (15.161, 15.177, 15.223), is the formula of [[shining-sea]], and the lines are excepted, as at 1.141, 2.152 and 14.76.
