grc: [πολυπύρῳ]
---

“Father Zeus, if ever anyone in Argos rich in wheat, / burning to you the fat thigh pieces of ox or sheep, / prayed that he might return, and you promised and nodded your assent, / remember these things, Olympian” (15.372–375; [[nestor]], [[argos]], [[father-zeus]], [[thigh-pieces]], [[prayer]]). Argos is named as the land where the Achaeans sacrificed for their return before they sailed; the land of grain is where the offerings came from. The epithet has been given to Buprasium in Nestor's tale of his youth (11.756; [[buprasium]]), and Tydeus' estate in Argos had fields “bearing wheat” (14.122–123). The dative πολυπύρῳ is new to the entry.
