---
summary: Zeus sends Apollo to rouse Hector until the Achaeans in their flight “reach the ships and the Hellespont” (15.233).
---

Zeus' order to Apollo sets the limit of the Trojan advance: “rouse great might in him for so long, until the Achaeans / in their flight reach the ships and the Hellespont. / From then on I myself will devise both deed and word, / so that the Achaeans may draw breath again from their toil” (15.232–235; see [[zeus]], [[apollo]] and [[hector]]). The Hellespont is the sea at the Achaeans' backs, along whose shore the ships are drawn up (14.30–36; see [[ships]]). Driven back to it, they have nowhere else to go: at the end of the book Ajax tells them that they sit “with our backs to the sea, far from our native land” (15.740; see [[sea]], [[ajax]] and [[the-light-is-in-our-hands]]).
