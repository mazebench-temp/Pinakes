summary: Zeus foretells that the Achaeans will fall among the ships “of Achilles, son of Peleus,” and that he will let no god help them “until the wish of the son of Peleus is fulfilled”; Athena urges Hector's fated day “under the strength of the son of Peleus”; the book adds the forms Πηλεΐδεω and Πηλεΐδαο.
grc: [Πηλεΐδεω, Πηλεΐδαο]
---

**Zeus' forecast.** “So that in their flight they tumble among the many-benched ships / of Achilles, son of Peleus” (15.63–64; [[many-benched]], [[plan-of-zeus]]), and “until then I do not cease from my anger, nor will I let any other / of the immortals defend the Danaans here, / until the wish of the son of Peleus is fulfilled” (15.72–74; [[thetis-supplicates-zeus]]). The patronymic names Achilles twice in the speech in which Zeus foretells the deaths of Sarpedon, Patroclus and Hector.

**Hector's death.** “For already she was rousing his fated day against him, / Pallas Athena, under the strength of the son of Peleus” (15.613–614; [[pallas]], [[hector]], [[short-lived]]). The phrase ὑπὸ Πηλεΐδαο βίηφιν names the hand by which Hector will die, as the narrator speaks of his glory.

The forms Πηλεΐδεω (15.64) and Πηλεΐδαο (15.74, 15.614) are new to the entry. See [[achilles]], [[peleus]].
