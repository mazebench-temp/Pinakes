---
summary: In the fight around the ship of Protesilaus swords fall from hands and shoulders, “and the black earth ran with blood.”
---

“And many fine swords, with black-bound hilts, / fell to the ground, some from the hands, some from the shoulders / of fighting men; and the black earth ran with blood” (15.713–715; [[sword]], [[protesilaus]]). When the armies first clashed, in Book 4 and again in Book 8, the verse ended “and the earth ran with blood” (ῥέε δʼ αἵματι γαῖα, 4.451, 8.65; [[the-armies-clash]]); here the epithet completes it, and the whole phrase returns once more when Achilles drives his horses over the dead (20.494). The slaughter is now at the stern of a single ship, where Achaeans and Trojans fight hand to hand with axes and swords (15.704–712; [[axe]]). See [[earth]].
