---
summary: Ajax asks Teucer, “Where now are your arrows / of swift death,” when Hector kills their companion; the book adds the plural ὠκύμοροι.
grc: [ὠκύμοροι]
en: [of swift death]
---

“And great-hearted Hector has killed him. Where now are your arrows / of swift death, and the bow that Phoebus Apollo gave you?” (15.440–441; [[ajax]], [[teucer]], [[arrows]], [[lycophron]]). Of arrows ὠκύμορος means that they bring a swift death, not that they suffer one; the Odyssey uses it so of the arrows of Odysseus (*Odyssey* 22.75). Of Achilles the same word is “swift-fated,” his own swift death (1.417, 18.95). The plural ὠκύμοροι is new to the entry, and the translation's note on 15.441 explains the two senses.
