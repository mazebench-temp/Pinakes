**The third brother.** In his answer to Zeus, Poseidon counts the three sons of Cronus and Rhea: “Zeus and I, and the third is Hades, lord of those below” (Ἀΐδης ἐνέροισιν ἀνάσσων, 15.188; see [[poseidon]], [[cronus]] and [[rhea]]). When the lots were shaken, Poseidon got the gray sea and Zeus the wide sky, “and Hades got the misty darkness” (ζόφον ἠερόεντα, 15.190–192; see [[the-lots-of-the-three-brothers]] and [[lots]]). ζόφος is the darkness of the west, where the sun goes down: Hector does not care whether the birds fly to the right, “toward the dawn and the sun, / or to the left, toward the misty darkness” (12.239–240). Later the dead are said to go under the misty darkness (23.51; *Odyssey* 11.57, 11.155), and Achilles, seeing Lycaon alive again, wonders whether the Trojans he killed will rise again from beneath it (21.56). Poseidon names Hades only to make the point that the three are equals; he takes no part in the quarrel, and the earth and Olympus belong to all three (15.193; see [[earth]] and [[olympus]]).

**The house of Hades.** Revived by Apollo, Hector tells the god that when Ajax's stone struck him, “truly I thought that I would come to the dead and the house of Hades / on this very day, since I was gasping out my heart” (15.251–252; see [[hector]] and [[apollo]]). δῶμʼ Ἀΐδαο, “the house of Hades”, is where the dead go in the Iliad; Hector's own soul will go there in Book 22 (22.362).
