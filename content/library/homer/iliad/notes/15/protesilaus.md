**The ship.** The Catalogue told that Protesilaus was killed as he leapt from his ship, “by far the first of the Achaeans” (2.698–702; see [[the-death-of-protesilaus]]). His ship is still on the beach. ποντοπόρος, “seafaring”, and ὠκύαλος, “swift-sailing”, are its epithets (see [[seafaring-ship]]), and the last verse gives it a history: it brought him to Troy, “and did not take him back again to his native land” (15.706; see [[nostos]]). Where it lay the poem told in Book 13: Hector held on “where the ships of Ajax and of Protesilaus were”, below the lowest stretch of the wall (13.681–684; see [[the-achaean-wall]]).

**The fight around it.** “It was around his ship that the Achaeans and Trojans / were cutting one another down hand to hand” (15.707–708), no longer with arrows and javelins but with axes and battle-axes, great swords and double-pointed spears, until “the black earth ran with blood” (15.709–715; see [[axe]] and [[sword]]). Hector, “once he had taken hold of the stern, would not let go, / holding the stern ornament in his hands” (15.716–717; see [[stern]] and [[hector]]), and calls for fire: “Now Zeus has given us a day worth all the rest, / to take the ships” (15.719–720). Ajax, driven back to the seven-foot bench, keeps the fire off with his spear and stabs twelve men who bring it (15.727–746; see [[ajax]]). In Book 16 it is the first ship to burn, and Patroclus' first spear flies by its stern as he drives the Trojans from it, leaving it half-burned (16.122–124, 16.284–294; see [[patroclus]] and [[the-battle-at-the-ships]]).
