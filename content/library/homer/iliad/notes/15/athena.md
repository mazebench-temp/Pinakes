---
summary: When Ares sets out to avenge his son against Zeus' command, Athena, afraid for all the gods, takes his helmet, shield and spear, rebukes him and sits him down. Zeus foretells that Ilios will fall through her counsels; she is already rousing Hector's fated day, and at the end she clears the mist from the Achaeans' eyes.

**Against Ares.** When Hera tells the gods that Ascalaphus is dead, Ares calls for his horses and puts on his armor to go and avenge his son, and a greater anger would have come from Zeus upon all the immortals (15.110–122; see [[ares]] and [[ascalaphus]]):

{{quote:71587d26-213c-57fa-8da4-f27c39153b5a}}

She takes his arms away one by one, the helmet, the shield and the spear. Her rebuke is blunt: “Madman, crazed in your mind, you are lost!” His ears are for nothing, “your mind is gone, and your sense of shame” (15.128–129; see [[aidos]]). If he goes, Zeus will leave the Trojans and the Achaeans and come back to Olympus to throw them all into tumult, seizing them one after another, “the one to blame and the one who is not” (15.132–137; see [[zeus]]). Let him give up his anger for his son, “for already many a man better than he in strength and hands / has been killed, or will be killed hereafter; and it is hard / to save the lineage and birth of all men” (15.139–141; see [[genee]]). “So saying, she sat furious Ares down on his throne” (15.142; see [[throne]]). In Book 5 she led Ares out of the battle by the hand, so that the two might avoid the wrath of Zeus (5.29–36), and in the battle of the gods she will strike him down with a stone (21.403–408).

**Her counsels.** In Zeus' forecast the Achaeans will take steep Ilios “through the counsels of Athena” (15.71; see [[plan-of-zeus]] and [[troy]]), the stratagem that the Odyssey tells as the wooden horse, which Epeius made “with Athena” (*Odyssey* 8.493; see the translation's note on 15.56–77). Poseidon names her first among the gods who want Ilios sacked, as Ἀθηναίη ἀγελείη, “Athena, driver of spoil” (15.213; see [[driver-of-spoil]] and [[poseidon]]). In a simile she is the teacher of craft: the carpenter's chalk line makes a ship's timber straight in the hands of a man who knows his craft “by the promptings of Athena” (15.410–412; see [[the-chalk-line]]), as Phereclus, whom she loved, knew every kind of intricate work (5.59–61).

**Hector's fated day.** Zeus honors Hector because his life is short, “for already she was rousing his fated day against him, / Pallas Athena, under the strength of the son of Peleus” (15.613–614; see [[hector]], [[achilles]] and [[pallas]]). She is named here as the power behind his death. In Book 22 she deceives him in the likeness of Deiphobus (22.226–247), and Achilles tells him that Pallas Athena will subdue him with his spear (22.270–271).

**The mist.** When Nestor has begged the Achaeans to stand for their children and parents, “from their eyes Athena thrust away the cloud of mist, / wondrous; and a great light came to them on both sides” (15.668–669; see [[achlys]] and [[nestor]]), so that they see Hector and his companions, those who stand back and those who fight beside the ships (15.670–673). In Book 5 she took the mist from Diomedes' eyes so that he could tell god from man (5.127–128; see [[diomedes]]). No mist has been mentioned before, and Zeus has said that he will let no other god defend the Danaans until Achilles' wish is fulfilled (15.72–77; see the translation's note on 15.668–673).
