---
summary: Hector, not knowing the god who stands over him, asks Apollo, “Who are you, good sir, among the gods, who question me face to face?”
---

“Then Hector of the gleaming helmet spoke to him feebly: / ‘Who are you, good sir, among the gods, who question me face to face?’” (15.246–247; [[hector]], [[apollo]], [[of-the-gleaming-helmet]], [[divine-epiphany]]). The question begins as Diomedes' to Glaucus, “Who are you, good sir, among mortal men?” (6.123), and as Priam's to Hermes in Book 24 (24.387); here the speaker knows that the stranger is a god and asks which. Apollo names himself: “Phoebus Apollo of the golden sword” (15.256; [[of-the-golden-sword]], [[far-worker]]).
