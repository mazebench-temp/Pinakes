---
summary: Hera tells Themis that she knows “what kind of heart he has, overbearing and harsh,” of Zeus; the book adds the nominative ὑπερφίαλος.
grc: [ὑπερφίαλος]
---

“Do not ask me about these things, goddess Themis; you know yourself / what kind of heart he has, overbearing and harsh” (15.93–94; [[hera]], [[zeus]], [[themis-the-goddess]], [[thymos]]). A word of blame for arrogant men, the Trojans and Diomedes (3.106, 5.881, 13.621), is here given by a goddess to the heart of the king of the gods. Zeus has just threatened her with blows and reminded her how he once hung her from the sky (15.16–24; [[hera-hung-from-the-sky]]), and she goes on to tell the gods that he claims to be far the best of them in power and strength (15.107–108). The other adjective, ἀπηνής, “harsh,” is Achilles' word for Agamemnon (1.340; [[harsh-king]]). The nominative ὑπερφίαλος is new to the entry.
