summary: Hera laughs with her lips, “but her forehead above her dark brows / was not warmed”; the book adds the phrase ὀφρύσι κυανέῃσιν.
grc: ["ὀφρύσι κυανέῃσιν"]
---

Back on Olympus after Zeus has threatened her, Hera sits down among the gods, “and she laughed / with her lips, but her forehead above her dark brows / was not warmed; and in her indignation she spoke among them all” (15.101–103; [[hera]], [[brows]], [[nemesis]]). The dark brows are those under which Zeus nods his assent (1.528); here they belong to the goddess who has just had to obey him, and the laugh does not reach them. Her speech then turns the gods against one another: she tells Ares that his son Ascalaphus is dead (15.104–112; [[ares]], [[ascalaphus]]). The phrase ὀφρύσι κυανέῃσιν, in the reverse order of the formula, is new to the entry.
