---
summary: “Zeus the counselor” thunders in answer to Nestor's prayer, and waits to see the blaze of a burning ship, which will mark the turn of his plan.
---

**The thunder.** Nestor prays at the ships that Zeus remember the thigh pieces burned to him in Argos: “So he spoke in prayer, and Zeus the counselor thundered loudly, / hearing the prayers of the old son of Neleus” (15.377–378; [[nestor]], [[prayer]], [[son-of-neleus]], [[thigh-pieces]]). The Trojans hear the same thunder, and spring on the Argives more fiercely (15.379–380; [[aegis-bearing]]): the sign that answers the Achaean prayer drives the enemy on.

**The burning ship.** Zeus keeps rousing Hector, to give him glory and fulfill the prayer of Thetis in full, “for Zeus the counselor was waiting for this, / to see with his eyes the blaze of a burning ship” (15.599–600; [[plan-of-zeus]], [[thetis-supplicates-zeus]]). From then on he means to bring about the counterattack from the ships (15.601–602; compare 15.69–71). The god of counsel waits for one sight, and the end of the book stops just short of it ([[protesilaus]]). See [[zeus]].
