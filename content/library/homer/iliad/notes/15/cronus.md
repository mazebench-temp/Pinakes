summary: Poseidon names Cronus as the father of the three brothers who divided the world by lot, and Zeus speaks of the gods below “who are around Cronus”; Zeus is “the son of Cronus” to Themis, to Apollo and to the narrator.
relations:
  - child: poseidon (15.187)
  - child: hades (15.187-188)
---

**The father of the three.** Answering Zeus' claim to be the elder, Poseidon begins with their birth: “For we are three brothers, born of Cronus, whom Rhea bore, / Zeus and I, and the third is Hades, lord of those below” (15.187–188; see [[poseidon]], [[hades]], [[rhea]] and [[the-lots-of-the-three-brothers]]). Homer does not say when the lots were drawn, only that the world was divided among the sons; Zeus put Cronus beneath the earth and the sea (14.203–204; see [[fall-of-cronus]]). In Hesiod Zeus is the youngest of Rhea's children (*Theogony* 478), and when the Titans are beaten the gods urge him to be king, and he apportions their honors among them (*Theogony* 881–885; see [[hesiod]]). In the Iliad Zeus is the elder (15.166, 15.182), as the narrator says in Book 13 (13.355).

**The gods around Cronus.** Zeus tells Apollo that if Poseidon had fought him, “surely others too would have heard of our fighting, / even the gods below, who are around Cronus” (15.224–225; see [[zeus]] and [[titans]]), the words in which Sleep named the witnesses of Hera's oath, the Titans beneath Tartarus (14.274–279; see [[tartarus]]). When the gods do meet in battle in Book 20, Zeus thunders from above and Poseidon shakes the earth from below, and Hades leaps from his throne in fear that the earth will be broken open over the house of the dead (20.56–65).

**The son of Cronus.** Zeus is Κρόνου πάϊς, “the son of Cronus”, when Themis asks whether he has frightened Hera (15.91; see [[themis-the-goddess]]), “the far-seeing son of Cronus” on Gargarus (Κρονίδην, 15.152; see [[far-seeing]]), and Κρονίων when Apollo tells Hector who has sent him (15.254; see [[son-of-cronus]]).
