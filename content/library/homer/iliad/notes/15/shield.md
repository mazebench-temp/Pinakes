**Shields put on and taken off.** Athena disarms Ares of helmet, shield and spear (15.125–126; see [[ares]]). Ajax tells Teucer to take “a shield on your shoulder” (15.474), and Teucer “around his shoulders … put a shield of four layers” (σάκος … τετραθέλυμνον, 15.479), the verse with which Odysseus arms against the suitors (*Odyssey* 22.122; see [[arming-scene]] and [[teucer]]). Dolops' spear strikes the middle of Meges' shield, and the corselet beneath stops it (15.528–529; see [[corselet]]).

**Periphetes' shield.** Periphetes of Mycenae, better than his father in every kind of prowess, is the one man Hector kills in the Achaean rout before the ships (15.637–644; see [[periphetes-of-mycenae]]). His own shield brings him down: “For as he turned back, he tripped on the rim of his shield, / which he carried himself, reaching to his feet, a barrier against javelins” (ἐν ἀσπίδος ἄντυγι … ποδηνεκέʼ ἕρκος ἀκόντων, 15.645–646). It is the great body shield that covers a man from neck to ankle, like the one that knocked against Hector's neck and ankles as he walked back to Troy (6.117–118; see [[reaching-to-the-feet]]). Caught in it, he fell on his back, his helmet rang about his temples, and Hector ran up and fixed his spear in his chest (15.647–652; see [[helmet]]). The shield that kept off javelins left him helpless on the ground.
