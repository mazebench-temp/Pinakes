---
summary: The narrator turns to Melanippus as he dies: “so at you, Melanippus, Antilochus, steadfast in battle, leapt / to strip your armor.”
---

“And Antilochus sprang at him like a dog that darts / at a wounded fawn … / so at you, Melanippus, Antilochus, steadfast in battle, leapt / to strip your armor” (15.579–583; [[antilochus]], [[melanippus-son-of-hicetaon]], [[apostrophe]], [[dogs]]). The epithet that Book 13 made Antilochus' own (13.396) stands in a verse in which the narrator speaks to the dead man in the second person. Antilochus does not get the armor: Hector comes running, and the steadfast fighter flees “like a wild beast that has done some harm” (15.583–588; [[hector]], [[swift]]). See [[stripping-the-dead]].
