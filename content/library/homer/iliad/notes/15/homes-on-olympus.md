---
summary: Ares, told of his son's death, asks the gods “who have your homes on Olympus” not to be indignant if he goes to avenge him.
---

“Do not be indignant with me now, you who have your homes on Olympus, / if I go to the ships of the Achaeans to avenge the killing of my son, / even if it is my fate to be struck by the thunderbolt of Zeus / and lie with the dead among the blood and the dust” (15.115–118; [[ares]], [[ascalaphus]], [[nemesis]], [[thunderbolt]]). The formula names the gods in the third person in Chryses' prayer and in the false dream (1.18, 2.13), and Dione uses it of the gods' own sufferings at the hands of men (5.383); here, for the only time, a god addresses the other gods with it, as they sit gathered in the house of Zeus (15.84–85). The god who has a home on Olympus imagines himself lying among the dead. Athena takes his armor from him before he can go (15.121–142; [[athena]], [[gleaming-all-over]]). See [[olympians]].
