---
summary: Revived by Apollo, “Hector plied his nimble feet and knees,” and the Danaans hold like a sea cliff that withstands “the swift paths of the shrill winds.”
---

**Hector.** At the end of the simile of the stalled horse, “so Hector plied his nimble feet and knees, / urging on the horsemen, once he had heard the voice of the god” (15.269–270; [[hector]], [[the-stallion]], [[apollo]]). The verse ὣς … λαιψηρὰ πόδας καὶ γούνατʼ ἐνώμα will return for Achilles, who also runs like a horse, a prize-winner with its chariot, back toward Troy and Hector (22.22–24); and in Book 22 it is Hector who plies his nimble knees in flight (22.144).

**The winds.** The Danaans hold in close order “like a rock, / lofty and great, close to the gray sea, / which withstands the swift paths of the shrill winds” (15.618–620; [[lofty]], [[gray-sea]], [[shrill]]). The phrase λαιψηρὰ κέλευθα is the one of the heaving sea in Book 14 (14.17; [[the-heaving-sea]]).
