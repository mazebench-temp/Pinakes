---
summary: Hector, revived by Apollo, runs to the battle like a stalled horse “used to bathing in the fair-flowing river.”
---

“As when a stalled horse, fed full of barley at the manger, / breaks his halter and gallops across the plain, pounding the ground, / used to bathing in the fair-flowing river” (15.263–265; [[the-stallion]], [[horses]], [[hector]], [[apollo]]). The six verses of the simile were first given to Paris running down from Pergamus (6.506–511); here they describe Hector, his strength given back by a god. In Book 14 the fair-flowing river was the Xanthus, where Hector's companions poured water over him as he lay unconscious (14.433–436; [[scamander]]); the horse that bathes in the river is now the image of his return.
