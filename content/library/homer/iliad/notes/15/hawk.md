---
summary: Apollo, sent by Zeus to revive Hector, goes down from the mountains of Ida “like a hawk, a swift dove-killer, which is the swiftest of winged birds” (15.236–238).
---

βῆ δὲ κατʼ Ἰδαίων ὀρέων ἴρηκι ἐοικὼς / ὠκέϊ φασσοφόνῳ, ὅς τʼ ὤκιστος πετεηνῶν (15.237–238; see [[like-a-hawk]]). The hawk's two epithets here are its speed and its prey: ὠκύς, “swift”, and φασσοφόνος, “dove-killer”, found only here, from φάσσα, the wood pigeon. ὤκιστος πετεηνῶν, “the swiftest of winged birds”, is the hawk's rank among birds, as the κίρκος is ἐλαφρότατος πετεηνῶν, the lightest of them (22.139; *Odyssey* 13.87).

In Book 13 Poseidon left the two Ajaxes like a swift-winged hawk that rises from a rock to chase another bird (13.62–65; see [[the-hawk-from-the-rock]]); in Book 15 Apollo comes down like one. Poseidon's hawk was a god leaving the men he had helped against Zeus' will; Apollo's comes down at Zeus' command to rout the Achaeans with the aegis (15.229–235). In the Odyssey a hawk is “Apollo's swift messenger” (*Odyssey* 15.526).
