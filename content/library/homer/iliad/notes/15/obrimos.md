---
summary: Hera tells the gods that Ascalaphus is dead, “whom mighty Ares says is his own.”
---

“For his son has perished in battle, the dearest of men to him, / Ascalaphus, whom mighty Ares says is his own” (15.111–112; [[hera]], [[ares]], [[ascalaphus]]). In Book 13 the narrator said that “loud-voiced, mighty Ares had not yet heard at all / that his son had fallen in the fierce battle” (13.521–522; [[briepyos]]); here Hera tells him, with the same epithet, and the god of war strikes his thighs and arms himself to avenge the death (15.113–120; [[flourishing]], [[gleaming-all-over]]). Hera's phrase “says is his own” leaves the paternity to Ares' own word.
