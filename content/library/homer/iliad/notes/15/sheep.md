---
summary: Sheep in Book 15 are a great flock in the simile of the rout before the aegis, scattered with a herd of cattle by two wild beasts in the night (15.323–325), and the victims whose fat thigh pieces were burned to Zeus in Argos, in Nestor's prayer (15.372–374).
grc: ["βοὸς ἢ οἰὸς"]
---

**The flock in the night.** “And as a herd of cattle or a great flock of sheep is thrown into confusion by two wild beasts in the dead of black night, coming on them suddenly when the herdsman is not there” (15.323–325; see [[two-beasts-in-the-night]]). πῶϋ μέγʼ οἰῶν, “a great flock of sheep”, is a formula: Nestor's father took one from the spoils of Elis (11.696), and in the Odyssey Odysseus makes his men swear not to kill a herd of cattle or a great flock of sheep on the island of the Sun (*Odyssey* 12.299). Here the flock is the Achaean army, put to rout by Apollo with the aegis (15.318–327).

**Sacrifice.** Nestor prays to Zeus to remember the sacrifices of home: “if ever anyone in Argos rich in wheat, burning to you the fat thigh pieces of ox or sheep, prayed that he might return, and you promised and nodded your assent, remember these things” (15.372–375; see [[thigh-pieces]], [[cattle]] and [[prayer]]). The sheep is the ὄϊς, here in the genitive οἰός. Penelope prays to Athena in the same form in the Odyssey: if ever Odysseus burned to her in his halls the fat thigh pieces of an ox or a sheep, ἢ βοὸς ἢ ὄϊος, let her remember it now and save her son (*Odyssey* 4.762–766).
