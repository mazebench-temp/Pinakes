---
summary: Hera swears by the water of Styx, “the greatest / and most dread oath for the blessed gods.”
---

“Let Earth now be witness to this, and the wide Heaven above, / and the down-flowing water of Styx, which is the greatest / and most dread oath for the blessed gods” (15.36–38; [[hera]], [[styx]], [[oath]], [[earth]], [[wide-sky]]). The three verses are Calypso's oath in the Odyssey (*Odyssey* 5.184–186). In Book 14 Hera swore by Styx at Sleep's demand, and the gods below were called to witness (14.271–279); here she swears unasked, to clear herself before Zeus, and the oath keeps to the letter: it was not by her will that Poseidon helped the Achaeans (15.41–44; [[poseidon]]). The dative μακάρεσσι θεοῖσι, without movable ν, came into the entry with Book 14 (14.143).
