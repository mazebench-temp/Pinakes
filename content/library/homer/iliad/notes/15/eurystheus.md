summary: Eurystheus is named as the master of Copreus, who carried his messages to Heracles; Copreus' son, Periphetes of Mycenae, a better man than his father, is the only Achaean Hector kills when the army flees from the first ships (15.638–641).

Κοπρῆος φίλον υἱόν, ὃς Εὐρυσθῆος ἄνακτος / ἀγγελίης οἴχνεσκε βίῃ Ἡρακληείῃ, “the dear son of Copreus, who used to go for lord Eurystheus / with messages to mighty Heracles” (15.639–640; see [[copreus]], [[periphetes-of-mycenae]], [[heracles]] and [[might-of-heracles]]). Athena spoke of the labors that Eurystheus set Heracles, and named the descent to Hades for the hound (8.362–369); here the poem names the herald who went between the king and the hero (see [[heralds]]). The son is a man of Mycenae (15.638, 15.643; see [[mycenae]]). In Apollodorus it is to Mycenae that Copreus fled after a killing, and there that Eurystheus purified him; through him Eurystheus sent Heracles his commands for the labors (*Library* 2.5.1).
