---
summary: At the ship of Protesilaus “many fine swords, with black-bound hilts,” fall to the ground from the hands and shoulders of the fighters.
---

“And many fine swords, with black-bound hilts, / fell to the ground, some from the hands, some from the shoulders / of fighting men; and the black earth ran with blood” (15.713–715; [[sword]], [[black-earth]], [[protesilaus]]). Three epithets fill the verse after the noun: καλά, “fine,” μελάνδετα, “black-bound,” found only here, and κωπήεντα, “hilted,” here for the first time. The fine swords lie in the dust, fallen from the hands that held them and from the shoulders where they hung, as the fighting at the stern comes to axes, swords and spears (15.707–712; [[axe]], [[double-pointed]]).
