summary: The Trojans drive their horses with “a wondrous din,” pour missiles on Antilochus with a wondrous din, and Zeus wishes that Hector may cast “wondrous fire” on the ships; the Achaeans flee “in wondrous rout,” and Athena lifts the “wondrous” mist from their eyes; the book adds the adverb θεσπεσίως.
grc: [θεσπεσίως]
---

**The din.** ἠχῇ θεσπεσίῃ opens two verses. Hector's men shout and drive their horses on “with a wondrous din” as Apollo kicks down the banks of the trench (15.354–355; [[chariot-drawing]], [[apollo]]), and when Antilochus flees, the Trojans and Hector “with a wondrous din poured their groaning missiles on him” (15.590; [[groaning]]), the verse of Diomedes' flight in Book 8 (8.159).

**The fire.** Zeus wishes that Hector “might cast wondrous fire, / tireless fire” on the curved ships (15.597–598; [[tireless]], [[curved-ships]]), with θεσπιδαὲς πῦρ, “wondrously blazing” fire, the phrase that in Book 12 named the fighting at the wall and Hector's call to burn the ships (12.177, 12.441). What Hector ordered there is now Zeus' own wish.

**The rout and the mist.** In the simile of the lion, “so then the Achaeans / fled in wondrous rout before Hector and father Zeus” (15.636–637; [[father-zeus]]); the adverb θεσπεσίως, found only here in Homer, is new to the entry. And Athena “thrust away the cloud of mist, / wondrous” (15.668–669; [[athena]], [[achlys]], [[common-to-all]]), with the adjective run over into the next verse.
