---
summary: The Trojans rush on the ships “like lions that eat raw flesh”; the book adds the dative ὠμοφάγοισι.
grc: [ὠμοφάγοισι]
---

“And the Trojans, like lions that eat raw flesh, / rushed upon the ships, and fulfilled the commands of Zeus” (15.592–593; [[trojans]], [[lion]], [[lions-and-boars]], [[plan-of-zeus]]). The comparison λείουσιν ἐοικότες ὠμοφάγοισι was made of the Achaeans around Diomedes (5.782) and of Hector and Ajax closing in their duel (7.256); here it is a whole army, and it is the instrument of Zeus' will. The form without movable ν, ὠμοφάγοισι, is new to the entry.
