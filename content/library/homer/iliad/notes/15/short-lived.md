---
summary: At the height of Hector's success the narrator says that “short-lived he was to be.”
---

“For from the upper air he himself was his defender, / Zeus, who, though Hector was alone among many men, / honored him and gave him glory. Short-lived he was to be; / for already she was rousing his fated day against him, / Pallas Athena, under the strength of the son of Peleus” (15.610–614; [[hector]], [[zeus]], [[athena]], [[pallas]], [[kydos]], [[time]]). The word with which Achilles began his complaint to his mother, “since you bore me to be short-lived” (1.352; [[achilles]]), and with which the narrator mourned the young Simoeisius (4.478), is here given to Hector in the narrator's voice, in the same sentence as his honor. The fated day that Athena urges on is the day of his death at Achilles' hands (Book 22).
