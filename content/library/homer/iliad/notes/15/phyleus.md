# Φυλεΐδεω (15.519) and Φυλεΐδαο (15.528), "son of Phyleus", occur only here and count for father and son.
grc: [Φυλεΐδεω, Φυλεΐδαο]
refs: [15.532-534]
relations:
  - guest-friend: euphetes (15.532)
---

Φυλεΐδεω ἕταρον, “companion of the son of Phyleus” (15.519), of Otus of Cyllene, and Φυλεΐδαο μέσον σάκος, “the middle of the shield of the son of Phyleus” (15.528), when Dolops' spear strikes Meges (see [[otus-of-cyllene]], [[dolops-son-of-lampus]] and [[meges]]). The Catalogue and Book 13 named Meges Φυλεΐδης (2.628, 13.692); these are the genitives of the same patronymic.

Then the father appears in his own name, in the history of the corselet that stops the spear: “Phyleus had once / brought it from Ephyra, from the river Selleis. / For his guest-friend, Euphetes, lord of men, had given it to him / to wear into war, a defense against enemy men; / and then too it kept destruction from his son's body” (15.530–534; see [[euphetes]], [[ephyra]], [[selleis-ephyra]], [[corselet]] and [[guest-gifts]]). καὶ τότε, “then too”, lets the listener infer that it had kept the father safe before. The Catalogue told of Phyleus' quarrel with his father and his move from Elis to Dulichium (2.629; see [[phyleus-and-augeas]]); this book tells of a friendship, and of a gift that outlived his own wars. Strabo places the Ephyra on the Selleis in Elis (*Geography* 8.3.5; see [[strabo]]), so that the guest-friend would have lived in the country Phyleus left. Nestor will remember outthrowing Phyleus with the spear at the funeral games of Amarynceus (23.637).
