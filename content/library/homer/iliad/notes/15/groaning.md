---
summary: As Antilochus flees from Hector, the Trojans and Hector “with a wondrous din poured their groaning missiles on him.”
---

“So the son of Nestor fled in fear, and the Trojans and Hector / with a wondrous din poured their groaning missiles on him; / and he turned and stood, when he reached the crowd of his companions” (15.589–591; [[antilochus]], [[hector]], [[wondrous]], [[arrows]]). The second verse is the one in which the Trojans and Hector poured missiles on Diomedes as he turned his horses to flight in Book 8 (8.159; [[diomedes]]). Both times it follows the flight of an Achaean who has been forced to give way, and both times the man escapes. The missiles are “groaning” because they bring groans, as the arrow that kills Cleitus is “the arrow of many groans” (15.451; [[of-the-many-groans]]).
