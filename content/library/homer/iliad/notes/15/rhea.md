When Zeus orders him out of the war as the elder and the stronger, Poseidon answers with the family: “For we are three brothers, born of Cronus, whom Rhea bore, / Zeus and I, and the third is Hades, lord of those below” (15.187–188; see [[poseidon]], [[zeus]], [[hades]] and [[cronus]]). The brothers have one father and one mother, and so, Poseidon argues, an equal claim, which the lots confirmed (15.189–193; see [[the-lots-of-the-three-brothers]]). It is the second and last time Homer names Rhea; in Book 14 Hera spoke of being taken from her as a child (14.203; see [[hera]]). Hesiod counts all six of Rhea's children by Cronus: Hestia, Demeter, Hera, Hades, Poseidon and Zeus (*Theogony* 453–457; see [[hesiod]]).
