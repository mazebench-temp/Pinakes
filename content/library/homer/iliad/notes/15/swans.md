summary: Swans, “long-necked”, close the line of birds on which Hector, like a tawny eagle, swoops as they feed beside a river (15.690–692), in the one verse that names them in Homer (15.692 = 2.460).
---

κύκνων δουλιχοδείρων, “long-necked swans”, end the verse that Book 15 takes from the simile of the Asian meadow (15.692 = 2.460; see [[birds-on-the-asian-meadow]] and [[long-necked]]). The flock that pictured the Achaean army going out to war in Book 2 is now the quarry of an eagle, and the eagle is Hector, darting at a dark-prowed ship (15.690–694; see [[the-eagle-and-the-birds]]). See [[geese]] and [[cranes]].
