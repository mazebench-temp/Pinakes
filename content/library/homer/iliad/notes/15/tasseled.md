---
summary: Zeus bids Apollo take “the tasseled aegis” in his hands and shake it over the Achaeans.
---

“But take the tasseled aegis in your hands, / and shake it hard over them, and put the Achaean heroes to flight” (15.229–230; [[zeus]], [[apollo]], [[aegis]]). The line-end αἰγίδα θυσσανόεσσαν was the aegis that Athena put on in Book 5 (5.738); Zeus will take it up himself in Book 17 (17.593). Here it passes from father to son, and the tassels are described again when Apollo uses it: “dreadful, shaggy all around” (15.309; [[shaggy-all-around]], [[furious]]). It is the weapon of rout: Apollo holds it still while men fall on both sides, and when he shakes it in their faces, the Danaans forget their courage (15.318–322; [[of-the-swift-horses]]).
