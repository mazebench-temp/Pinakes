---
summary: Indignation among the gods, and its giving way: Hera speaks to the gods “in her indignation”; Ares begs them not to be indignant if he goes to avenge his son; and Poseidon yields to Zeus “for all my indignation”, as Zeus says of him in the same words, “for all his indignation, he gave way”.
---

Hera, laughing with her lips while her brow stays cold, “in her indignation … spoke among them all” (νεμεσσηθεῖσα, 15.103; see [[hera]] and [[laughter-of-the-gods]]). Ares, struck by the news of his son's death, asks the Olympians the opposite: “Do not be indignant with me now, you who have your homes on Olympus” (μὴ … νεμεσήσετʼ, 15.115; see [[ares]] and [[aidos]]). The word then binds Poseidon's yielding to Zeus' account of it. Poseidon tells Iris, “But now, for all my indignation, I will give way” (νεμεσσηθεὶς ὑποείξω, 15.211; see [[the-hearts-of-good-men-can-be-turned]]), and Zeus, sending Apollo, says that it was far better for both of them “that before it came to that, for all his indignation, he gave way / before my hands” (νεμεσσηθεὶς ὑπόειξε, 15.227–228; see [[zeus]]). The same participle and verb, from the yielder and from the one yielded to, close the quarrel of the brothers.
