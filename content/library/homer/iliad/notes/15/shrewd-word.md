“But he did not escape the shrewd mind of Zeus, who was guarding / Hector, and he took the triumph away from Teucer, son of Telamon” (15.461–462; [[zeus]], [[teucer]], [[hector]], [[noos]], [[blameless]]). The adjective of the shrewd word and the shrewd plan here describes the mind of Zeus, as in Book 14 it described the mind that desire wrapped (πυκινὰς φρένας, 14.294). There the mind was overcome; here it is awake, and nothing escapes it. The phrase πυκινὸν νόον, found only here in Homer, is new to the entry.
