---
summary: In Poseidon's account of the three lots, “Hades got the misty darkness.”
---

“I got by lot the gray sea, to dwell in forever, / when the lots were shaken, and Hades got the misty darkness, / and Zeus got the wide sky, in the upper air and the clouds” (15.190–192; [[hades]], [[poseidon]], [[zeus]], [[gray-sea]], [[wide-sky]], [[lots]], [[the-lots-of-the-three-brothers]]). Each brother's share has its epithet. ζόφος ἠερόεις is the dark of the west and of the dead: in Book 12 Hector names the left hand of the bird-omens as the side “toward the misty darkness” (12.240), and in Book 23 the dead Patroclus is to go ὑπὸ ζόφον ἠερόεντα (23.51). The Odyssey uses the phrase of the dead in the underworld (*Odyssey* 11.57, 11.155).
