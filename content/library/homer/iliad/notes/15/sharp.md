summary: The war cry rises “shrill” on both sides, Hector kills Lycophron “with the sharp bronze,” Teucer takes a spear “tipped with sharp bronze,” Meges and Ajax strike with their sharp spears, and at the ship of Protesilaus men fight “with sharp axes”; the book adds the dative ὀξέσι.
grc: [ὀξέσι]
en: [shrill]
---

**The cry.** As Apollo leads the Trojans with the aegis, “the war cry rose / shrill on both sides, and arrows leapt from the bowstrings” (15.312–313; [[aute]], [[apollo]]). Of a sound ὀξύς is rendered “shrill.”

**The bronze.** Hector, aiming at Ajax, kills Lycophron: “him he struck on the head above the ear with the sharp bronze” (15.433; [[lycophron]], [[hector]]). Teucer, his bow broken, arms as a spearman, “and he took a strong spear, tipped with sharp bronze” (15.482; [[teucer]], [[strong-spear]], [[arming-scene]]), in the verse of Nestor's arming at the start of Book 14 (14.12). Meges strikes the crown of Dolops' helmet “with his sharp spear” (15.536; [[meges]], [[bronze-fitted]]), and Ajax, having called on the Danaans, “in his fury pressed on with his sharp spear” (15.742; [[ajax]]), with the longer adjective ὀξυόεις.

**The axes.** At the ship of Protesilaus, “they fought with sharp axes and battle-axes / and with great swords and double-pointed spears” (15.711–712; [[axe]], [[double-pointed]], [[protesilaus]]). The dative ὀξέσι, without movable ν, is new to the entry. At 15.649, ὀξὺ νόησε, “was quick to see,” is excluded as before.
