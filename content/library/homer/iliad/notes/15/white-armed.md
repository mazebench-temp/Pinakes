---
summary: The white-armed goddess Hera obeys Zeus and goes to Olympus, answers Themis, and is named by Athena as the bearer of Zeus' orders.
---

The full formula θεὰ λευκώλενος Ἥρη names Hera three times in the book. After Zeus' threats, “So he spoke, and the white-armed goddess Hera did not disobey, / and she went from the mountains of Ida to high Olympus” (15.78–79; [[zeus]], [[high-olympus]]), the verse of her obedience in Books 5, 8 and 14 (5.767, 8.381, 14.277). To Themis' question, “Then the white-armed goddess Hera answered her” (15.92; [[themis-the-goddess]]). And Athena, restraining Ares, asks him: “Do you not hear what the white-armed goddess Hera says, / who has just now come from Olympian Zeus?” (15.130–131; [[athena]], [[ares]], [[olympian]]). In Book 14 the formula was used only once, of her oath to Sleep (14.277); here it marks her return to obedience. See [[hera]].
