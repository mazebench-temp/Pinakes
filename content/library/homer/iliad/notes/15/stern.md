summary: Achilles' wish of Book 1 comes true at the sterns. The Trojans drive their chariots in and fight at the sterns; Lycophron falls dead from a stern; Hector takes hold of the stern of Protesilaus' ship, holds on to its curved ornament and calls for fire, cursing the elders who once kept him from fighting at the sterns.

**At the sterns.** Hector, struck down by Ajax's stone, remembers that it happened “at the sterns of the ships of the Achaeans” (15.248; see [[stone-throwing]]). Now the Trojans come over the wall, “and drove their horses in and fought at the sterns / hand to hand” (15.385–386), the Achaeans fighting back from the decks above (see [[ships]] and [[spear]]). Hector's spear, thrown at Ajax, strikes Lycophron, who falls on his back “from the stern of the ship to the ground” (νηὸς ἄπο πρυμνῆς, 15.435; see [[lycophron]]).

**The stern of Protesilaus' ship.** “And Hector took hold of the stern of a seafaring ship, / a fine, swift-sailing ship, which had brought Protesilaus / to Troy, and did not take him back again to his native land” (15.704–706; see [[protesilaus]]). He will not let go: “once he had taken hold of the stern, would not let go, / holding the stern ornament in his hands” (πρύμνηθεν … ἄφλαστον, 15.716–717). ἄφλαστον, found only here in Homer, is the curved ornament at the top of the stern; in Book 9 Odysseus told Achilles of Hector's vow to hack the high sternposts from the ships and burn them (ἄκρα κόρυμβα, 9.241–242; see the translation's notes on 9.241 and 15.717). Holding it, Hector calls for fire, and blames the cowardice of the elders, “who, when I wanted to fight at the sterns of the ships, / held me back” (15.722–723; see [[elders]] and [[hector]]). In Book 1 Achilles asked that the Achaeans be penned in “among their ships' sterns and along the sea” (1.409; see the note on Book 1); at the stern of Protesilaus' ship the wish is all but fulfilled, and the ship burns at the start of Book 16 (16.122–124).
