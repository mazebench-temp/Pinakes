καί μιν ἔπειτα Κόων δʼ εὖ ναιομένην ἀπένεικας, “and then you carried him off to well-peopled Cos” (15.28). It is the verse with which Sleep told the same storm in Book 14 (14.255), now in the mouth of Zeus, who tells Hera how she sent Heracles over the barren sea with the North Wind (15.26–28; see [[hera-drives-heracles-to-cos]] and [[boreas]]). Κόων δʼ is again Κόωνδε, “to Cos”, written as two words, as the digitized text writes Οὔλυμπον δέ, “to Olympus”, a hundred verses later (15.133; see the translation's notes on 14.255 and 15.133); it is not the name of Coon, son of Antenor (see [[coon]]).

Zeus' account goes on where Sleep's stopped. Sleep left Heracles on Cos, “far from all his friends” (14.256); Zeus adds, “From there I rescued him and brought him back again / to horse-pasturing Argos” (ἔνθεν ῥυσάμην, 15.29–30; see [[heracles]] and [[argos]]).
