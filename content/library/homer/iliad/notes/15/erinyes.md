---
summary: When Poseidon refuses to yield to Zeus, Iris reminds him that “the Erinyes always follow the elder”, and he gives way.
---

Zeus orders Poseidon out of the war as “the elder by birth” (15.166, 15.182; see [[zeus]]), and Poseidon answers that the three sons of Cronus are equal in honor (15.185–199; see [[poseidon]] and [[the-lots-of-the-three-brothers]]). Iris asks whether she is to carry back so harsh a word, and ends: “You know how the Erinyes always follow the elder” (οἶσθʼ ὡς πρεσβυτέροισιν Ἐρινύες αἰὲν ἕπονται, 15.204; see [[the-erinyes-follow-the-elder]] and [[iris]]). ἕπονται, “follow”: they are on the side of the elder and uphold his right (see the translation's note on 15.204). In Book 9 they fulfill a father's and a mother's curse (9.454, 9.571); here the claim is the right of the first-born among brothers. Poseidon praises the messenger who “knows what is due” and gives way (15.206–211).
