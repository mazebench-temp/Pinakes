---
summary: Athena warns Ares that Zeus will leave “the high-hearted Trojans and the Achaeans” and come to punish the gods, and Antilochus kills “high-hearted Melanippus.”
---

**The Trojans.** “For at once the high-hearted Trojans and the Achaeans / he will leave, and come to Olympus to throw us into tumult” (15.135–136; [[athena]], [[ares]], [[trojans]], [[achaeans]]). The epithet of praise for the Trojans stands in a warning about the gods: if Ares goes to avenge his son, Zeus will leave the war and seize them one after another (15.137).

**Melanippus.** “But struck the son of Hicetaon, high-hearted Melanippus, / as he was going into the war, in the chest beside the nipple” (15.576–577; [[antilochus]], [[melanippus-son-of-hicetaon]], [[hicetaon]]). Hector has rebuked him a few verses earlier and he has followed, “a man equal to the gods” (15.559; [[equal-to-the-gods]]); his praise and his death come close together.
