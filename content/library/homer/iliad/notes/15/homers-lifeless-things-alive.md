summary: Two of Aristotle's examples stand in Book 15. The spears that “stood fixed in the ground, longing to glut themselves on flesh” fall in the volley of the Trojan charge, in a verse also found in Book 11; the spear point that “sped through his chest in its fury” is the one with which Menelaus kills Dolops.
refs: [15.317, 15.542]

The two verses come from the two halves of the book's fighting. The first belongs to the volley that opens the Trojan charge, when Apollo goes before Hector holding the aegis: arrows leap from the bowstrings, and of the many spears some are fixed in the flesh of young men, and many fall short and “stood fixed in the ground, longing to glut themselves on flesh” (15.313–317; see [[aegis]] and [[spear]]). The same verse ends the volley that falls short of Ajax in his retreat (11.571–574), and Aristotle's words fit either place (*Rhetoric* 3.11, 1412a1). The second is the death of Dolops, son of Lampus, in the fight over Meges. Menelaus comes unseen to Meges' defense and strikes Dolops in the shoulder from behind, and the point passes through his chest (15.540–543; see [[menelaus]], [[dolops-son-of-lampus]] and [[meges]]). Half of that verse, αἰχμὴ δὲ διέσσυτο μαιμώωσα, is used of the spear point that passes through Sarpedon's thigh, grazing the bone (5.661); with στέρνοιο, “through his chest”, it is found only here.

Aristotle's explanation turns on the verbs: “being shameless and raging and the rest are activity” (*Rhetoric* 3.11, 1412a3–4). The verb of the raging point, μαιμάω, is used of men too. At the end of this book Ajax, having called on the Danaans to remember their courage, “in his fury pressed on with his sharp spear” (μαιμώων, 15.742; see [[ajax]]); in Book 13, when Poseidon has filled the two Ajaxes with might, their hands and feet quiver for the fight (13.75, 13.78). The point that rages through Dolops' chest is given a warrior's fury.

Plutarch quotes the spears' longing, too, among the poets' metaphors that give movement to what they describe (*Table Talk* 9.15; see [[plutarch]]).
