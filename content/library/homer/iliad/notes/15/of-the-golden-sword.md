---
summary: Apollo names himself to the dazed Hector as the helper Zeus has sent, “Phoebus Apollo of the golden sword”; the book adds the accusative χρυσάορον.
grc: [χρυσάορον]
---

“Take heart now: such a helper has the son of Cronus / sent forth from Ida to stand by you and defend you, / Phoebus Apollo of the golden sword, who before now too / have protected you, both you yourself and your steep citadel” (15.254–257; [[apollo]], [[hector]], [[phoebus]], [[son-of-cronus]], [[steep]]). Hector has asked the stranger who he is (15.247; [[good-sir]]), and the god answers with his full name and title, in the accusative after “such a helper.” The epithet of the golden sword introduces a god who in this book carries neither sword nor bow, but the aegis of Zeus (15.229, 15.307–308; [[aegis]]). The accusative χρυσάορον is new to the entry.
