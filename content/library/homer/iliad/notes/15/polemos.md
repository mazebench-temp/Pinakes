summary: Zeus orders Poseidon to cease from battle and war; Patroclus goes to urge Achilles to fight; and at the ships the battle is stretched as evenly as a carpenter's line, then becomes “a bitter battle” fought hand to hand, a war “common to all” in which, as Ajax says, there is no room for softness.
grc: [μάχεσθʼ, μαχέσσασθαι]
passages:

  - 15.696-715 | The battle at Protesilaus' ship
---

**Ceasing from war.** Zeus wants Poseidon “to cease from war and go to his own house” (παυσάμενον πολέμοιο, 15.58), and his order through Iris begins “Bid him cease from battle and war” (μάχης ἠδὲ πτολέμοιο, 15.160 = 15.176; see [[message-repeated]]). Poseidon's withdrawal is the end of his war in this part of the poem, and Zeus is glad that the quarrel between the brothers was settled without the fighting that even the gods below would have heard (15.223–228; see [[poseidon]]). Among men the war goes the other way: Patroclus leaves Eurypylus to go to Achilles, “to urge him to fight” (πολεμίζειν, 15.402; see [[the-persuasion-of-a-friend]]).

**The even battle.** At the ships the fight is level: “so evenly was their battle and war stretched tight” (ἐπὶ ἶσα μάχη τέτατο πτόλεμός τε, 15.413), the verse of the even fight at the wall in Book 12, just before Hector broke the gate (12.436; see [[the-chalk-line]] and [[the-rope-of-war]]). Hector calls the Trojans not to draw back “from the battle yet, in this narrow place” (15.426), and Ajax tells the Argives that Hector calls his men “not to a dance … but to fight” (15.508; see [[not-to-a-dance-but-to-fight]]).

**A bitter battle.** After Athena has cleared the Achaeans' eyes, the light comes “toward the war, common to all” (ὁμοιΐου πολέμοιο, 15.670; see [[common-to-all]]). Hector makes for a ship, “And again a bitter battle was fought beside the ships” (δριμεῖα μάχη, 15.696): “you would have said that unwearied and unyielding they met one another / in war, so furiously did they fight” (15.697–698; see [[apostrophe]]). It is fought with axes and swords, hand to hand, until the black earth runs with blood (15.707–715; see [[the-armies-clash]]). Ajax's last words set war against softness: “so in our hands lies the light, not in softness in war” (15.741; see [[the-light-is-in-our-hands]]).
