---
summary: Zeus foretells that the Achaeans will flee and tumble “among the many-benched ships / of Achilles.”
---

“And the Achaeans / he may turn back again, rousing a craven panic among them, / so that in their flight they tumble among the many-benched ships / of Achilles, son of Peleus; and he will rouse up his companion, / Patroclus” (15.61–65; [[plan-of-zeus]], [[achilles]], [[patroclus]], [[son-of-peleus]]). The ships of Achilles are named as the place where the Achaean rout is to end and Patroclus' part to begin. The forecast is not fulfilled to the letter: the ship that Hector seizes is the ship of Protesilaus, and it is its burning that brings Patroclus into the battle (15.704–706; 16.122–129; [[protesilaus]]).
