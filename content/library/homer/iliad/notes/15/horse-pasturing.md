---
summary: Zeus tells Hera that he rescued Heracles from Cos “and brought him back again / to horse-pasturing Argos.”
---

“From there I rescued him and brought him back again / to horse-pasturing Argos, though he had suffered many labors” (15.29–30; [[zeus]], [[heracles]], [[argos]], [[hera-drives-heracles-to-cos]]). The verse begins with the formula Ἄργος ἐς ἱππόβοτον, which in Book 3 named the home to which Helen was to be taken back with the treasure, “to horse-pasturing Argos and Achaea of the lovely women” (3.75, 3.258). Argos is the land over which Eurystheus was made king when Hera delayed the birth of Heracles in Thebes (19.98–124; [[eurystheus]]). Later in the book the son of Copreus, the man who carried Eurystheus' messages to Heracles, is killed at the ships (15.638–640; [[copreus]], [[might-of-heracles]]).
