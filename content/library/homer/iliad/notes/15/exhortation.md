summary: Book 15 is built on exhortations answered by exhortations. Three times Hector rallies the Trojans and Ajax answers him on the other side; between them Menelaus spurs Antilochus and Nestor implores the army by their parents. Hector's reasons are Zeus' help and the safety of the household; Ajax's are shame, the lack of any way home, and the fact that there is no help but their own hands.

**Hector's rallies.** Hector speaks in the forms of his earlier rallies (see the notes on Books 6, 8 and 11). He calls out “shouting aloud” (ἐκέκλετο μακρὸν ἀΰσας, 15.346, 15.424, 15.485) and addresses “Trojans and Lycians and Dardanians who fight hand to hand” (15.425, 15.486), with the whole verse “be men, my friends, and remember your furious courage” (15.487). His first order is to rush on the ships and leave the spoils, with a threat of death without burial for any man he finds hanging back (15.346–351; see [[stripping-the-dead]] and [[cremation]]); his second, to save the body of Caletor (15.425–428; see [[caletor]]). The third is the fullest. He reads a sign, the breaking of Teucer's bowstring, and draws from it the help of Zeus and the order to fight in a body, and he answers the fear of death with a promise for the household: whoever is struck, “let him die”, for his wife and children will be safe if the Achaeans sail away (15.486–499; see [[the-help-of-zeus-is-easy-to-see]] and [[it-is-not-unseemly-to-die-for-ones-country]]). At the stern of Protesilaus' ship he calls for fire: Zeus has given them “a day worth all the rest” (15.718–725; see [[hector]] and [[stern]]).

**Ajax answers.** Each time Ajax answers on the other side. To Hector's sign he answers with αἰδώς, “Shame, Argives!”, with the plain stakes, perish or be saved, and with a choice between a quick death and a slow one at the hands of worse men (15.502–513; see [[aidos]], [[not-to-a-dance-but-to-fight]] and [[better-to-perish-once-and-for-all]]). To Hector's rebuke of Melanippus he answers with the lines of Agamemnon on shame (15.560–564; see [[shame-saves-more]]). To Hector's call for fire he answers from the seven-foot bench, “always shouting terribly” to the Danaans (15.687, 15.732), with the formal address of Agamemnon and Nestor, “Friends, Danaan heroes, attendants of Ares” (15.733; see [[attendants-of-ares]]), Hector's own verse “be men, my friends, and remember your furious courage” (15.734), and an argument from what is missing: no helpers behind, no stronger wall, no city near, only “in our hands … the light” (15.735–741; see [[the-light-is-in-our-hands]]). Hector's speeches tell the Trojans what they have to gain; Ajax's tell the Achaeans what they have not got.

**Spurring one man.** Between the pairs the exhortation narrows to a single fighter. Hector rebukes his cousin Melanippus by name: “Are we to hold back like this, Melanippus? Is your dear heart / not moved at all, now that your cousin is killed?” (15.553–554; see [[melanippus-son-of-hicetaon]]). On the other side Menelaus spurs Antilochus with praise: no other Achaean is younger, swifter or as valiant, “if only you would leap out and strike some man of the Trojans” (15.569–571; see [[antilochus]] and [[menelaus]]). Both men answer at once; Melanippus follows Hector into the fight, and Antilochus kills him (15.559, 15.573–578).

**Nestor.** The old man's exhortation is a supplication. He begs each man by his parents, asks for shame before other men and remembrance of children, wives, possessions and parents, and implores them “to stand firm, and not to turn to rout” (15.659–666; see [[supplication]] and [[nestor]]). It ends, like Hector's and Ajax's speeches at the ships, with the verse of the rally, “So saying, he urged on the might and spirit of every man” (15.500, 15.514, 15.667; see [[so-he-spoke]]).
