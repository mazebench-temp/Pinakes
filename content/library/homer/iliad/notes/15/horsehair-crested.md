---
summary: Teucer arms with a helmet “with a horsehair crest,” and Meges shears the horsehair plume from Dolops' “bronze-fitted, horsehair-crested helmet.”
---

**Teucer.** When his bowstring breaks, Teucer arms as a spearman: “and on his mighty head he set a well-made helmet / with a horsehair crest, and the plume nodded terribly above it” (15.480–481; [[teucer]], [[mighty]], [[well-made]], [[arming-scene]]). The two verses are those of the arming of Paris (3.336–337), the second is Agamemnon's (11.42), and both return for Patroclus (16.137–138).

**Dolops.** “And Meges, on his bronze-fitted, horsehair-crested helmet, / stabbed the topmost peak with his sharp spear, / and broke off the horsehair plume from it; and it all fell / to the ground in the dust, bright with fresh purple” (15.535–538; [[meges]], [[dolops-son-of-lampus]], [[bronze-fitted]], [[phaeinos]]). The plume that the epithet describes is struck off, and its new purple lies in the dust; ἵππειον λόφον, “the horsehair plume,” is found only here.
