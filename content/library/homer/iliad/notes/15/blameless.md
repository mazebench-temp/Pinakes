---
summary: Zeus breaks “the well-twisted string on his flawless bow” as Teucer draws it against Hector.
---

“But he did not escape the shrewd mind of Zeus, who was guarding / Hector, and he took the triumph away from Teucer, son of Telamon, / for he broke the well-twisted string on his flawless bow / as he was drawing it against him” (15.461–464; [[teucer]], [[zeus]], [[bow]], [[shrewd-word]], [[telamonian]]). Of things ἀμύμων is rendered “flawless,” as of the handiwork of the women in Agamemnon's gifts (9.128) and of the dancing of which, Menelaus says, a man would rather have his fill than of war (13.636–639). The bow is flawless, and the fault is not in it: Teucer says that a god has broken the string he bound on that morning (15.467–470; [[daimon]]), and Ajax tells him to let the bow lie and take up a spear (15.472–477).
