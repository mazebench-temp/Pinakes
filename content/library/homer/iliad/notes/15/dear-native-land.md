---
summary: Hector promises that the Trojan who dies fighting saves his family if the Achaeans go back “to their dear native land,” Ajax asks whether the Achaeans expect to walk home, and he reminds them that they fight “far from our native land.”
---

**Hector.** A man who dies fighting for his country leaves his wife and children safe, and his house and allotment unharmed, “if the Achaeans / are gone with their ships to their dear native land” (15.498–499; [[hector]], [[exhortation]], [[nostos]]). The return that the Achaeans long for is the Trojans' rescue.

**Ajax.** Answering him on the other side, Ajax asks: “Do you expect, if Hector of the gleaming helmet takes the ships, / that you will each come on foot to your native land?” (15.504–505; [[ajax]], [[of-the-gleaming-helmet]]). The ships are the only way home. At the end of the book he says it plainly: “we sit with our backs to the sea, far from our native land” (15.740; [[close-armored]]).

**The dead.** Medon lived “in Phylace, away from his native land, since he had killed a man” (15.335 = 13.696; [[medon]], [[exile-and-refuge]]), and the ship that Hector seizes is the one that brought Protesilaus to Troy, “and did not take him back again to his native land” (15.705–706; [[protesilaus]], [[seafaring-ship]]).
