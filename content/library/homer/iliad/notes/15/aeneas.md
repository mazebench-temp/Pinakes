---
summary: In the rout behind Apollo and the aegis, Aeneas kills two Achaeans, Medon, the bastard son of Oileus, and Iasus, a leader of the Athenians (15.332–338).
---

Αἰνείας δὲ Μέδοντα καὶ Ἴασον ἐξενάριξεν, “and Aeneas slew and stripped Medon and Iasus” (15.332). The narrator stops to give each a history. Medon, the half-brother of the Locrian Ajax, marshaled Philoctetes' men in the Catalogue (2.726–728) and lived at Phylace, an exile for killing a kinsman of his stepmother (15.333–336 = 13.694–697); Iasus was a leader of the Athenians, “the son of Sphelus, son of Bucolus” (15.337–338; see [[medon]] and [[iasus]]). Aeneas is one of six Trojan leaders who kill eight Achaeans as the battle scatters (15.328–342; see [[trojans]]). In Book 13 he killed Aphareus at the ships (13.541; see [[aphareus]]).
