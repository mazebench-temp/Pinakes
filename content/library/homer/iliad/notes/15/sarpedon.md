---
summary: Sarpedon is named only in Zeus' forecast: Patroclus will kill many young men before Ilios, “and among them my son, brilliant Sarpedon” (15.66–67).
---

τοὺς ἄλλους, μετὰ δʼ υἱὸν ἐμὸν Σαρπηδόνα δῖον, “others besides, and among them my son, brilliant Sarpedon” (15.67). Zeus speaks of the death of his own son as a thing settled, one step on the way from Patroclus' going out to Hector's death (15.64–68; see [[zeus]] and [[plan-of-zeus]]). In Book 12 he kept death from him, “so that he should not be beaten down at the sterns of the ships” (12.403). In Book 16, when the moment comes, Zeus thinks of snatching him alive out of the battle, and Hera dissuades him (16.431–461; see [[patroclus]]).
