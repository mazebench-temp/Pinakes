---
summary: Teucer sets a helmet “on his mighty head,” and Hector rebukes his cousin, “mighty Melanippus.”
---

**Teucer's head.** Arming as a spearman, “on his mighty head he set a well-made helmet / with a horsehair crest” (15.480–481; [[teucer]], [[well-made]], [[horsehair-crested]], [[arming-scene]]). The verse κρατὶ δʼ ἐπʼ ἰφθίμῳ κυνέην εὔτυκτον ἔθηκεν is the one of Paris before the duel (3.336); it returns for Patroclus (16.137) and for Odysseus in the Odyssey (*Odyssey* 22.123).

**Melanippus.** “But Hector called on his kinsmen, / all of them, and first he rebuked the son of Hicetaon, / mighty Melanippus” (15.545–547; [[hector]], [[melanippus-son-of-hicetaon]], [[hicetaon]]). The epithet is run over to the start of the verse with his name, before the story of his life at Percote (15.547–551; [[curving]]).
