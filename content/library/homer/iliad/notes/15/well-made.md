---
summary: Teucer, arming as a spearman, sets “a well-made helmet” on his head.
---

“And around his shoulders he put a shield of four layers, / and on his mighty head he set a well-made helmet / with a horsehair crest, and the plume nodded terribly above it” (15.479–481; [[teucer]], [[helmet]], [[mighty]], [[horsehair-crested]], [[arming-scene]]). The verse κρατὶ δʼ ἐπʼ ἰφθίμῳ κυνέην εὔτυκτον ἔθηκεν belongs to the arming scene: Paris before the duel (3.336), and later Patroclus (16.137). In Teucer's arming the shield of four layers, τετραθέλυμνον, takes the place of the usual verses of greaves and corselet, and the Odyssey arms Odysseus in the hall with the same shield verse and helmet verse (*Odyssey* 22.122–123).
