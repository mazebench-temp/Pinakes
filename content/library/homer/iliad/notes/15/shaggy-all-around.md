---
summary: Apollo holds before Hector the furious aegis, “dreadful, shaggy all around, conspicuous,” which Hephaestus gave to Zeus.
---

“And before him went Phoebus Apollo, / his shoulders wrapped in cloud, and he held the furious aegis, / dreadful, shaggy all around, conspicuous, which the smith / Hephaestus gave to Zeus to carry, to put men to rout” (15.307–310; [[apollo]], [[aegis]], [[furious]], [[preeminent]], [[hephaestus]]). The aegis receives four epithets in two verses, and ἀμφιδάσειαν stands between δεινήν, “dreadful,” and ἀριπρεπέʼ, “conspicuous.” Zeus had given it to Apollo as “the tasseled aegis” (15.229; [[tasseled]]); here, as it goes into battle, it is shaggy all around. The history of the aegis, made by Hephaestus for Zeus, is told only here.
