summary: Zeus recalls the day “when the goddess Thetis clasped my knees” (15.76; see [[supplication]]); in the simile of the stallion “his knees carry him swiftly”, and so Hector plies “his nimble feet and knees” (15.268–269; see [[the-stallion]]); and Thoas calls Hector the man “who has loosened the knees of many of the Danaans” (15.291; see [[loosened-his-limbs]]).
