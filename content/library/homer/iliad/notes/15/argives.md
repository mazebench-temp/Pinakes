---
summary: Apollo scatters “the long labor and misery of the Argives”, their wall; Hector tells the Trojans that Zeus diminishes the might of the Argives, and Zeus bewitches their hearts and takes away their glory; Ajax rallies them with “Shame, Argives!” and the old appeal to shame; at last they give way from the first ships to the huts (15.312–366, 15.493, 15.502–513, 15.560–564, 15.594–595, 15.655–658).
---

**Their labor scattered.** At first “the Argives held their ground in a body” against the Trojans and Apollo (15.312), until the god shook the aegis. Then he kicked the banks of the ditch into a causeway and threw down the wall, as a boy at the seashore scatters the sand he has heaped up in play: “so you, archer Phoebus, scattered the long labor and misery / of the Argives, and roused panic among them” (15.355–366; see [[apollo]], [[the-achaean-wall]] and [[the-boy-and-the-sand]]). κάματος, “labor”, is the toil of building, now undone by a god's feet and hands.

**Zeus against them.** Hector reads the broken bowstring as a sign: Zeus puts glory into the hands of some, and diminishes others, “as now he diminishes the might of the Argives, and helps us” (15.488–493; see [[hector]], [[teucer]] and [[the-help-of-zeus-is-easy-to-see]]). The narrator confirms it: the Trojans rush on the ships “and fulfilled the commands of Zeus, / who kept rousing great might in them, and bewitched the heart / of the Argives, and took away their glory” (15.593–595; see [[zeus]], [[plan-of-zeus]] and [[kydos]]). Poseidon, leaving them, had threatened incurable anger if Zeus would not let Ilios be sacked “and … give great power to the Argives” (15.213–217; see [[poseidon]]).

**Shame, Argives!** Ajax answers Hector with the cry that Hera, Agamemnon and Poseidon have used before him (5.787, 8.228, 13.95): “Shame, Argives! Now it is sure: either we perish / or we are saved and thrust the evil away from the ships” (15.502–503; see [[ajax]] and [[aidos]]). Later “great Telamonian Ajax urged on the Argives” with the verses of Agamemnon's exhortation, to feel shame before one another (15.560–564; see [[shame-saves-more]]). Hector, rebuking Melanippus, says that the Trojans can no longer fight the Argives “from a distance” (15.556–557).

**Giving way.** “And the Argives gave way from the ships, under compulsion, / from the first ones”, but stood together beside the huts, “for shame held them / and fear” (15.655–658; see [[deos]]). After Hector's last command “they sprang more fiercely on the Argives” (15.726), and Ajax, alone on the decks, keeps the fire from the ships (15.727–746; see [[ajax]]).
