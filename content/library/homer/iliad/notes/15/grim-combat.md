---
summary: Ajax tells the Argives that it is better to perish at once or live than to be slowly drained away “in grim combat” beside the ships.
---

“Better to perish once and for all, or to live, / than to be slowly drained away in grim combat / like this, beside the ships, by worse men than we are” (15.511–513; [[ajax]], [[exhortation]], [[argives]]). The verse-end ἐν αἰνῇ δηϊοτῆτι is the formula of the challenge to single combat (3.20, 7.40, 7.51) and of men killed in battle (13.207, 13.603); here it names a slow attrition that Ajax rejects in favor of a decision. The choice between perishing and being saved is the one with which his speech begins, “either we perish / or we are saved” (15.502–503; [[aidos]]).
