---
summary: Zeus wishes that Hector may cast “wondrous fire, / tireless fire” on the ships, and Ajax keeps from the ships “whoever brought tireless fire”; the book adds ἀκάματον standing alone.
grc: [ἀκάματον]
---

“So that on the curved ships he might cast wondrous fire, / tireless fire, and fulfill the excessive prayer of Thetis / in full” (15.597–599; [[zeus]], [[hector]], [[wondrous]], [[curved-ships]], [[thetis-supplicates-zeus]]). The epithet is run over to the start of the next verse, apart from its noun (θεσπιδαὲς πῦρ / … ἀκάματον), and the translation repeats the noun. At the end of the book the formula is complete at the verse-end: Ajax “kept the Trojans from the ships, whoever brought tireless fire” (15.731; [[ajax]], [[blazing-fire]]). The form ἀκάματον standing alone is new to the entry; in Homer it is always said of fire.
