summary: Meges' “close-fitted corselet”, “fitted with plates”, turns Dolops' spear; it was a guest-gift to his father Phyleus from Euphetes of Ephyra, and it kept destruction from the son as it had from the father.
grc: [γυάλοισιν]
en: [plates]
passages:
  - 15.528-534 | Meges' corselet
---

Dolops stabs the middle of Meges' shield from close by, “but the close-fitted corselet kept it off, / which he wore, fitted with plates” (πυκινὸς … θώρηξ, / τόν ῥʼ ἐφόρει γυάλοισιν ἀρηρότα, 15.529–530; see [[meges]] and [[dolops-son-of-lampus]]). γύαλα are the curved plates of the corselet, as on Diomedes' corselet in Book 5 (5.99; see the note on Book 5). Phyleus brought it from Ephyra, a gift from his guest-friend Euphetes “to wear into war, a defense against enemy men; / and then too it kept destruction from his son's body” (15.531–534; see [[xenia]] and [[guest-gifts]]). Like Agamemnon's corselet from Cinyras (11.19–28), it is armor with a giver and a history. The Trojans of the book are “close-armored” (πύκα θωρηκτάων, 15.689, 15.739; see [[close-armored]]), with the verb of the corselet.
