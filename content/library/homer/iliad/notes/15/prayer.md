# Thoas' "claim" (εὐχόμεθʼ, 15.296) and Teucer's lost "triumph" (εὖχος, 15.462) are not prayers.
except: [15.296, 15.462]
passages:
  - 15.367-380 | The Achaeans pray, and Nestor's prayer
---

**The army prays.** Apollo has thrown down the wall, and the Achaeans halt beside the ships, “calling to one another, and to all the gods / they lifted up their hands, and each man prayed aloud” (χεῖρας ἀνίσχοντες μεγάλʼ εὐχετόωντο ἕκαστος, 15.368–369; see [[the-achaean-wall]]). Above all Nestor, “stretching out his hands to the starry sky” (15.371; see [[nestor]]):

{{quote:250a1c90-5d8d-5fc8-803e-1dc152f49345}}

The prayer has the three parts of the form (see the article above). The invocation is short, “Father Zeus”. The claim is not Nestor's own: any Achaean at Argos who burned thigh-pieces and prayed to come home had Zeus' promise and his nod (ὑπέσχεο καὶ κατένευσας, 15.374; see [[thigh-pieces]], [[neuo]] and [[nostos]]). It is the promise Nestor himself invoked in the assembly of Book 2, when he said that Zeus nodded his assent and flashed his lightning on the right on the day the Argives went aboard their ships (2.350–353). The request is for the present: “remember these things, Olympian, and ward off the pitiless day” (15.375).

**The answer.** “So he spoke in prayer, and Zeus the counselor thundered loudly, / hearing the prayers of the old son of Neleus” (ἀράων ἀΐων, 15.377–378; see [[hear-me]]). The thunder is a reply, but the Trojans hear it too, and “sprang on the Argives more fiercely, and remembered the joy of battle” (15.379–380; see [[charme]]). In Book 8 Zeus thundered from Ida as a sign to the Trojans (8.170–171); here a sign meant for the Achaeans drives the Trojans on, and they come over the wall like a wave over a ship's side (15.381–384).

**Thetis' prayer.** The narrator names the prayer that governs the book when Zeus rouses Hector against the ships: “so that on the curved ships he might cast wondrous fire, / tireless fire, and fulfill the excessive prayer of Thetis / in full” (Θέτιδος δʼ ἐξαίσιον ἀρὴν / πᾶσαν ἐπικρήνειε, 15.597–599; see [[thetis-supplicates-zeus]] and [[plan-of-zeus]]). ἀρή is a prayer here, a different word from the ἀρή that means harm (12.334; see the translation's note on 15.598), and the verb is the one of the formula of request, “fulfill for me this wish” (κρήηνον ἐέλδωρ, 1.41, 1.455). ἐξαίσιος, “beyond due measure”, judges Thetis' request: it is more than is fitting (see [[aisa]]). Zeus grants it and waits for the blaze of a burning ship (15.599–600). Nestor's prayer is heard with thunder; Thetis' is the one being fulfilled.
