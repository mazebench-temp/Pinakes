---
summary: Helen calls herself a “chilling” dog, giving to herself the word that Book 5 gave to Onslaught on the aegis; the form is ὀκρυόεις, an old misdivision of κρυόεις.
grc: [ὀκρυοέσσης]
---

“Brother-in-law to me, dog that I am, evil-scheming and chilling” (κυνὸς κακομηχάνου ὀκρυοέσσης, 6.344; [[dog-face]], [[evil-scheming]]). ὀκρυόεις is usually explained as κρυόεις wrongly divided from an older genitive ending in -οο, κακομηχάνοο κρυοέσσης, so that the ο of the ending was taken as the first letter of the next word; the same form appears in Book 9 (ἐπιδημίου ὀκρυόεντος, Il. 9.64). In Book 5 κρυόεις described Onslaught on the aegis (5.740), and in Book 9 it describes Rout (Il. 9.2). Helen gives the cold of fear and war to herself: she is the cause of what chills men ([[helen]], [[who-is-to-blame]]).
