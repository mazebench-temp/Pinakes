summary: The tears of Book 6 are Andromache's. She weeps on the tower, comes to Hector shedding tears, will be led away in tears in his vision of her captivity, laughs through her tears when he gives her the child, and goes home shedding big tears; then her handmaids mourn him while he is still alive. Hector does not weep.

**Andromache's tears.** When Hector reaches his house she is not there. She has gone to the tower with the child and a handmaid, and “was standing on the tower, lamenting and weeping” (γοόωσά τε μυρομένη τε, 6.373). She meets him at the gates, and “stood close beside him, shedding tears” (δάκρυ χέουσα, 6.405), in the formula used of Achilles weeping by the sea (δάκρυ χέων, 1.357; see [[shedding-tears]]). Her tears come before her words.

**Tears foreseen.** Hector's answer puts her tears into the future twice. An Achaean will lead her away “in tears” (δακρυόεσσαν, 6.455), and a man in Argos will say, “as he sees you shedding tears”, that she was Hector's wife (6.459–461; see [[captive-women]] and [[kleos]]).

**Laughter and tears.** When he gives her the child back, “she took him to her fragrant bosom, laughing through her tears” (δακρυόεν γελάσασα, 6.484). The adverb δακρυόεν, “tearfully”, occurs only here in Homer. Hector sees it and pities her (see [[laughter-of-the-gods]] and [[posis]]). At the parting she goes home “turning back again and again, shedding big tears” (θαλερὸν κατὰ δάκρυ χέουσα, 6.496). θαλερός is “flourishing, swelling”, the word she had used of Hector himself, her “husband in his prime” (θαλερὸς παρακοίτης, 6.430; see [[flourishing]]).

At home she rouses lamentation among her handmaids, and they mourn Hector in his house while he still lives (6.498–502; see [[lament]] and [[handmaids]]). The mourning for the living man anticipates the lament for the dead one in the last book, where Andromache leads the women's wailing over his body (24.723–746).

**Who does not weep.** Hector smiles as he looks at his son in silence (6.404), laughs with Andromache at the child's fear (6.471), and pities her (6.484). He does not weep in Book 6. Besides Andromache and her women, only the child cries, shrinking from the helmet (ἰάχων, 6.468). See [[andromache]] and [[hector-and-andromache]].
