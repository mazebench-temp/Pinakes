**The refuge.** Lycurgus drove the nurses of Dionysus down the Nysean mount and struck them with his ox-goad (6.130–135; see [[lycurgus]]), and the god fled to the sea:

{{quote:fe9aa362-4737-5fa0-a0d8-673f51709556}}

ὑπεδέξατο κόλπῳ, “took him to her bosom”, are the words Hephaestus uses when he tells how Thetis and Eurynome caught him after Hera threw him from heaven, and kept him hidden for nine years in a cave by the stream of Oceanus (18.394–405; see [[fall-of-hephaestus]]). In Book 1 Achilles reminds his mother that she alone saved Zeus when the other Olympians wanted to bind him (1.396–406; see [[binding-of-zeus]]). The Iliad tells three such stories, and in each the sea goddess saves a god in trouble: the king of the gods in bonds, a son thrown out by his mother, and a child driven into the sea by a man (see [[dionysus]]).

In the Odyssey the golden jar in which the bones of Achilles and Patroclus lie was Thetis' gift, and she said it was a gift of Dionysus and the work of Hephaestus, the two gods she had taken in (Od. 24.73–76). Homer does not say why Dionysus gave it.

**The mother of Achilles.** Thetis is not named elsewhere in Book 6, but her son is measured by her. Helenus tells Hector that the Trojans never feared even Achilles as they fear Diomedes, “who they say was born of a goddess” (6.99–100; see [[achilles]]).
