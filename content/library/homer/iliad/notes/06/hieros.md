---
summary: Sacred are the house of Athena on the citadel, whose doors Hecuba is to open with the key, and Ilios itself, from which the Trojans pray that Diomedes be kept and whose fall Hector foresees.
grc: [ἱεροῖο, ἱρῆς]
---

Helenus' instructions give the temple the epithet: Hecuba is to “open with the key the doors of the sacred house” (ἱεροῖο δόμοιο, 6.89; [[temple]]), and the whole prayer asks Athena to “hold the son of Tydeus back from sacred Ilios” (6.96, 6.277; [[son-of-tydeus]]). The goddess refuses (6.311).

The formula Ἴλιος ἱρή comes last in Hector's answer to Andromache, in the verse that Agamemnon spoke in Book 4: “a day will come when sacred Ilios will perish, and Priam, and the people of Priam of the good ash spear” (6.448–449; 4.164–165; [[a-day-will-come]], [[with-good-ash-spear]]). In Book 4 the enemy foretold it; here the city's own defender does ([[foresight]], [[hector]]).
