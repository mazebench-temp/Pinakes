---
summary: The Nicomachean Ethics cites Glaucus' exchange of gold armor for bronze as the case of a man who gives away his own and is not wronged, and the Problems handed down under Aristotle's name take Bellerophon's lonely wandering as a sign of melancholy.
---

- Glaucus' gift (*Nicomachean Ethics* 5.9, 1136b9–13): [[gold-for-bronze]].
- **Melancholy.** A chapter of the *Problems* asks why all men who have been outstanding in philosophy, politics, poetry or the arts seem to be melancholic. Among the heroes it names Ajax, who went wholly out of his mind, and Bellerophon, who sought out desert places, and quotes Homer: “he wandered alone over the Aleian plain, eating his heart out, avoiding the paths of men” ([Aristotle], *Problems* 30.1, 953a, quoting 6.200–202). See [[bellerophon]] and [[aleian-plain]].
