---
summary: The phrase of well-inhabited places names Hector's “well-appointed house,” which he finds empty and to which Andromache returns to mourn him, and Thebe, “the well-peopled city of the Cilicians” that Achilles sacked.
grc: [δόμους εὖ ναιετάοντας, εὖ ναιετάουσαν]
en: [well-appointed]
---

As in Book 2, the longer verb ναιετάω (“dwell, be inhabited”) takes the place of ναίω. Of a house the translation renders the phrase “well-appointed.” Hector leaves Helen and Paris, “and quickly he came to his well-appointed house,” and does not find Andromache there (6.370–371). The same verse brings her back after their parting: “And quickly she came to the well-appointed house of man-slaying Hector,” where she and her handmaids mourn him while he is still alive (6.497–500; [[man-slaying]], [[lament]], [[handmaids]]). The two verses frame the scene at the gates, which begins and ends at the empty house.

Of a city the phrase keeps its sense of a prosperous, peopled place. Andromache tells how Achilles “utterly sacked the well-peopled city of the Cilicians, high-gated Thebe” (6.415–416; [[thebe]], [[cilicians]], [[high-gated]]). In Book 1 the formula named the towns of the Troad that the army had sacked (1.164); Andromache's Thebe is one of them (1.366; [[sack-of-thebe]]).
