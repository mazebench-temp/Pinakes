---
summary: The epithet of holy places names the Nysean mount, down which Lycurgus drove the nurses of Dionysus.
grc: [ἠγάθεον]
---

Lycurgus “once drove the nurses of raving Dionysus down the sacred Nysean mount” (κατʼ ἠγάθεον Νυσήϊον, 6.132–133; [[nysa]], [[lycurgus-and-dionysus]]). Homer gives ἠγάθεος to Pylos and Lemnos, and in the Odyssey to Pytho, the sanctuary of Apollo (Od. 8.80). Here it marks the place of the god's nursery, where his nurses carried their sacred things (θύσθλα, 6.134), and so the sacrilege of the attack. Νυσήϊον occurs only here, and where Nysa lay was disputed in antiquity ([[dionysus]], [[lycurgus]]).
