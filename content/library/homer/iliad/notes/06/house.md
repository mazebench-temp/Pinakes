**Three houses.** Hector's errand takes him to three houses that stand near one another “on the height of the city” (6.317): his father's, his brother's and his own (see [[hectors-visit-to-troy]] and [[visit-scene]]). The narrator describes the first two. The third is only “well-appointed” (δόμους εὖ ναιετάοντας, 6.370, 6.497), and what is told of it is who is not there and who is.

**Priam's.** “The beautiful house of Priam, built with polished colonnades” (6.242–243). The αἴθουσα is the covered porch or colonnade along a courtyard, where guests were given beds (24.644, Od. 4.297), and the gods sit in the same “polished colonnades” in the house Hephaestus built for Zeus (ξεστῇς αἰθούσῃσιν, 20.11). Inside, fifty chambers of polished stone house Priam's sons and their wives, and across the courtyard twelve roofed chambers his daughters and their husbands (6.244–250). A king's house holds the whole family, the married sons and the sons-in-law with the rest (see [[priams-palace]], [[sons-of-priam]] and [[in-laws]]).

**Alexander's.** Paris built his own house:

{{quote:514d22c8-e34e-5605-9e89-27c45034e2b0}}

θάλαμος, δῶμα and αὐλή, bedchamber, hall and courtyard, are the parts of a Homeric house, from the innermost room to the open court. This is the house to which Aphrodite brought Helen in Book 3, “the beautiful house of Alexander” (3.421), and the high-roofed chamber where she left the two of them (3.423–424). Hector finds Paris there again, in the chamber, with his armor, and Helen with her women (6.321–324; see [[paris]] and [[weaving]]). At the end of the book Paris hurries out of his “lofty house” to catch up with his brother (ὑψηλοῖσι δόμοισιν, 6.503).

**Hector's.** He comes to his house and does not find his wife in the halls; he stands on the threshold and asks the slave women where she has gone (6.370–377; see [[threshold]] and [[handmaids]]). At the end of the book the house is where he sends her, to her own work (6.490), and where the household mourns him: “So they lamented Hector in his own house while he was still alive” (6.500; see [[lament]]).

**The fragrant chamber.** Hecuba goes down to her “fragrant chamber” (θάλαμον κηώεντα, 6.288) to choose the robe for Athena. The θάλαμος is also the treasure room of a house, and the verse returns when Priam goes down to his to fetch the ransom for Hector (24.191; see [[robe]]).

**Other houses.** Axylus lived “in a house beside the road” and welcomed everyone (6.15; see [[axylus]]). Oeneus kept Bellerophon twenty days in his halls, and the cup Bellerophon gave him is still in Diomedes' house (6.217–221; see [[guest-gifts]]). Andromache grew up with seven brothers in her father's halls in Thebe, and her mother, set free for ransom, died in them (6.421, 6.428; see [[sack-of-thebe]]).
