---
summary: Ovid's Hector goes from Andromache's arms to battle, and it is his wife who hands him his helmet; in the Metamorphoses Astyanax is thrown from the towers from which his mother used to show him his father fighting.
---

- **The helmet.** In a poem arguing that every lover is a soldier, Ovid writes that “Hector went from Andromache's embrace to arms, and it was his wife who gave him the helmet for his head” (*Amores* 1.9.35–36). In Homer, Hector takes off the helmet for his son and takes it up again himself (6.472–473, 6.494–495; see [[helmet]]).
- **The tower.** At the fall of Troy, Astyanax “is hurled from those towers from which he used to see his father, pointed out by his mother, fighting for him and defending the kingdom of his forefathers” (*Metamorphoses* 13.415–417). In Book 6 Andromache has taken the child to the tower to watch the battle (6.372–373, 6.386–389; see [[astyanax]] and [[the-tower-of-ilios]]).
