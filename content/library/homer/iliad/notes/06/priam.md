---
summary: Priam does not appear in Book 6, but his house and his fate do. Hector passes through his palace, calls Paris a bane “to great-hearted Priam and his sons”, and tells Andromache that a day will come when Priam will perish with his city.
---

**His house.** Hector comes to “the beautiful house of Priam”, with fifty chambers of polished stone for his sons and their wives, and twelve across the courtyard for his daughters and their husbands (6.242–250; see [[priams-palace]] and [[sons-of-priam]]). Hecuba meets him there (6.251; see [[hecuba]]). The houses of Paris and of Hector stand near it “on the height of the city” (6.317).

**His sons.** Helenus is “son of Priam” (Πριαμίδης, 6.76; see [[helenus]]), and so is Paris as he runs down from Pergamus to join his brother (υἱὸς Πριάμοιο, 6.512; see [[paris]]). Hector wishes the earth would swallow Paris, “for the Olympian has raised him as a great bane to the Trojans and to great-hearted Priam and his sons” (6.282–283; see [[may-the-earth-gape]]).

**His fate.** Hector's words to Andromache repeat Agamemnon's prophecy of Book 4: “a day will come when sacred Ilios will perish, and Priam, and the people of Priam of the good ash spear” (6.448–449; 4.164–165; see [[a-day-will-come]] and [[with-good-ash-spear]]). He names the sorrow of “lord Priam” among the griefs that trouble him less than Andromache's slavery (6.451; see [[andromache]]). The Iliad does not tell Priam's death (see [[sack-of-troy-foretold]]).
