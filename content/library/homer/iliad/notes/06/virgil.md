---
summary: The Aeneid returns to Book 6 again and again. The Trojan women's robe is painted on Juno's temple at Carthage, Andromache mourns Hector by a false Simois in Epirus, the dead crowd to the river like autumn leaves, the Latin women pray to Pallas in Theano's words while Turnus runs out like Paris' stallion, and Aeneas kisses his son through his helmet.
---

- **The robe.** On the walls of Juno's temple at Carthage, Aeneas sees the Trojan women going with loosened hair to the temple of unfriendly Pallas, carrying the robe and beating their breasts, while the goddess keeps her eyes turned away, fixed on the ground (*Aeneid* 1.479–482): [[the-robe-and-the-panathenaea]].
- Andromache at Buthrotum (*Aeneid* 3.294–505): [[virgils-andromache]].
- The dead as many as the leaves that fall at the first frost of autumn (*Aeneid* 6.305–312): [[leaves-of-the-dead]].
- The Latin women's prayer to Pallas, and Turnus running out like a horse loosed from its stall (*Aeneid* 11.477–497): [[virgils-stallion]].
- Aeneas' farewell to Ascanius, “Learn courage from me, my son, and true labor; fortune from others” (*Aeneid* 12.433–440): [[sophocles-tecmessa]].
