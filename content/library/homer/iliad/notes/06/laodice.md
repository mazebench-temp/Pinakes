Laodice appears once in the book and says nothing. Hecuba comes to meet Hector in Priam's palace, “leading in Laodice, the most beautiful in form of her daughters” (6.252; see [[hecuba]] and [[priams-palace]]). θυγατρῶν εἶδος ἀρίστην is the phrase used of her in Book 3, when Iris took her likeness to call Helen to the wall (3.124; see [[best-in-looks]]). Λαοδίκην ἐσάγουσα is “leading in Laodice”, as the verb most naturally means; an ancient explanation takes it as “going in to Laodice”, to her daughter's rooms. Either way the poem gives no reason for her being there, and Hector speaks only to his mother.
