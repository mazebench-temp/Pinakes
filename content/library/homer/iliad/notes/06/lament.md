summary: Andromache is on the tower “lamenting and weeping” when Hector looks for her (6.373). Her plea at the gates speaks as a lament speaks, and when she comes home she rouses the handmaids, and they mourn Hector in his own house while he is still alive (6.497–502).

**On the tower.** When Hector reaches his house, Andromache is not there. She has gone to the tower with the child and a handmaid, “lamenting and weeping” (γοόωσά τε μυρομένη τε, 6.373), because she heard that the Trojans were hard pressed (6.386–389; see [[the-tower-of-ilios]]).

**Her plea.** What she says to Hector at the gates has the matter of a lament. She speaks to him of his death, of the widow and the orphan he will leave, of the family Achilles killed, and of what he is to her: “Hector, you are father to me, and lady mother, and brother, and you are my husband in his prime” (6.407–432). When he is dead she will say these things again over his body, in the laments of the last two books (22.477–514, 24.725–745; see [[andromache]] and [[hector-and-andromache]]). Hector answers with his own picture of her future, and ends: “But may I be dead, and the heaped earth cover me, before I hear your cries as they drag you away” (6.464–465; see [[tomb]] and [[captive-women]]).

**Mourned alive.**

{{quote:d3ce9b98-8bd2-5b85-91ab-943eced4cb7f}}

The form γόον is here both noun and verb: she “roused lamentation” (γόον … ἐνῶρσεν, 6.499), and they “lamented” him (γόον, the imperfect of γοάω, 6.500). The women do for the living man what is done for the dead, “for they thought he would never come back again from the war” (6.501), and the listener knows that they are right. The Nereids lament Achilles in the same way, while he still lives, because he will not come home (18.50–64; see [[lament]]). When Briseis laments Patroclus, the women answer “for Patroclus as a pretext, but each for her own sorrows” (19.301–302). The handmaids' grief may be for themselves too: with Hector dead, as Andromache says in the last book, the women of Troy will soon be carried off in the ships (24.729–731; see [[handmaids]]).
