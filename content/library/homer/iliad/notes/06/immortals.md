---
summary: The word marks the question of Book 6, whether a man is facing a god: the Argives think an immortal has come to help the Trojans, Diomedes asks whether Glaucus is one of the immortals, and Lycurgus, who fought a god, was “hated by all the immortal gods.”
---

After the gods have left the field, men keep suspecting their presence. When Hector rallies the Trojans, the Argives “thought that one of the immortals had come down from the starry sky to bring help to the Trojans, so sharply did they rally” (6.108–109; [[starry]]). Diomedes asks the stranger who stands up to him: “But if you are one of the immortals come down from heaven, I would not fight with the gods of heaven” (6.128–129; [[heavenly-gods]], [[good-sir]]). In Book 5 he wounded two of them; in Book 6 he will not fight one, and he tells why.

His story ends with the verse of the gods' hatred: Lycurgus “did not live long after, since he was hated by all the immortal gods” (6.139–140; [[lycurgus-and-dionysus]], [[who-fights-the-gods]]). Glaucus' story of Bellerophon answers it with the same words, “But when he too was hated by all the gods” (6.200; [[bellerophon]]). The other use is Hecuba's: she offers Hector wine to pour to “father Zeus and the other immortals” (6.259; [[father-zeus]], [[libation]]).
