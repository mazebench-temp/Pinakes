ἀλλʼ ὅτε δὴ Λυκίην ἷξε Ξάνθόν τε ῥέοντα: “when he came to Lycia and the flowing Xanthus” (6.172). The river stands for the land, as in the Catalogue and in Sarpedon's words, where it is “eddying” (2.877, 5.479; see [[eddying]] and [[lycia]]). ῥέων, “flowing”, is the plain participle of ῥέω. The Lycian kings' estate lies “by the banks of the Xanthus”, and it is described in the words used of the domain the Lycians gave Bellerophon, “of orchard and plowland” (12.313–314; 6.195; see [[bellerophon]]).

The Xanthus of the book's opening lines is another river, the Scamander of the Trojan plain, under the name the gods give it (6.4; see [[scamander]]).
