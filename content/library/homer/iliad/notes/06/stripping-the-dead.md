**Slain and stripped.** The verb ἐξεναρίζω, “strip of his arms”, and so “slay”, comes four times in the kill-list that opens the book. Euryalus “slew and stripped” Dresus and Opheltius, Odysseus Pidytes, Eurypylus Melanthius (6.20, 6.30, 6.36), and the son of Mecisteus took the armor from the shoulders of the two sons of Bucolion (τεύχεʼ ἐσύλα, 6.28). The translation keeps both senses of the verb in one phrase (see [[androktasia]]).

**Kill first.** Adrastus has just offered a ransom for his life and been killed (6.37–65). Nestor draws the lesson for the army:

{{quote:b6e8719e-c531-5e32-a06b-256c26995258}}

ἔναρα are the arms taken from the slain. A man who bends over a corpse to strip it is not killing, and he may be killed himself (see [[fighting-over-the-body]]). The spoils can wait, Nestor says, for the dead will lie on the plain, and the army can strip them “at your ease” (ἕκηλοι). The Achaeans are winning, and Nestor wants the rout kept up (6.72–74; see [[nestor]] and [[exhortation]]). Hector gives the Trojans the same order in Book 15, when they stop to strip the dead while the Achaeans flee behind their wall: make for the ships and leave the bloody spoils (15.343–347).

**Not stripped.** Andromache says that Achilles killed her father Eetion “but he did not strip him, for his heart held back in awe from that”, and burned him in his armor and heaped a mound over him (6.416–419; see [[sack-of-thebe]], [[eetion]] and [[cremation]]). σεβάσσατο, “held back in awe”, is the verb of Proetus, who would not kill Bellerophon with his own hand (6.167). Achilles, who strips the men he kills, left this one his armor.

**Given, not taken.** Glaucus and Diomedes, who met to kill each other, exchange armor instead (6.230–236). Each man's armor, which would have gone to the victor, is given away as a pledge (see [[guest-gifts]]).

**Bloody spoils.** Hector ends his prayer for his son with the spoils: “may he bring back bloody spoils when he has killed his enemy, and may his mother's heart rejoice” (6.480–481). ἔναρα βροτόεντα, “bloody spoils”, is a phrase of the battlefield (8.534, 15.347, 17.13, 22.245). Here it is a father's wish. The spoils are the proof of a kill, brought home to the women of the house (see [[bloody-spoils]], [[prayer]] and [[astyanax]]).
