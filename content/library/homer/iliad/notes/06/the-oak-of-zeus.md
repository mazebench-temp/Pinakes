---
summary: Hector reaches “the Scaean gates and the oak” as he comes back from the battle into the city (6.237).
grc: [φηγόν]
---

Ἕκτωρ δʼ ὡς Σκαιάς τε πύλας καὶ φηγὸν ἵκανεν, “And when Hector came to the Scaean gates and the oak” (6.237). The gate and the tree are named together as one place, the edge of the city where the plain begins (see [[scaean-gates]]). The same words return when Achilles says that while he fought, Hector would not come out beyond the Scaean gates and the oak (9.354), and when the Trojans fleeing from Agamemnon stop there to wait for one another (11.170). Here it is where Hector leaves the war behind him, and where the wives and daughters of the Trojans run to meet him, asking after their men (6.238–241; see [[trojan-women]]).
