---
summary: The child shrinks back crying into “the bosom of his fair-belted nurse” when Hector reaches for him in his helmet.
---

“So saying, shining Hector reached out for his son; but the child shrank back crying into the bosom of his fair-belted nurse, frightened at the sight of his dear father” (ἐϋζώνοιο τιθήνης, 6.466–468; [[handmaids]], [[astyanax]], [[horsehair-crested]]). In Book 1 the epithet was Briseis', the woman taken from Achilles (1.429). Here it praises a servant of Hector's house, the nurse who carries the child at her breast (6.399–400, 6.389). The epithet of a woman's dress and figure is given to the one who holds the child when his father cannot ([[phaidimos]]).
