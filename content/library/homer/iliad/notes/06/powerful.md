---
summary: “Powerful Agamemnon” stabs the suppliant Adrastus whom his brother has pushed away from him.
---

The title of rule stands at the killing of a suppliant: Menelaus “pushed away from him with his hand the hero Adrastus; and powerful Agamemnon stabbed him in the flank” (6.62–64; [[adrastus-the-trojan]], [[supplication]]). A few lines before, the narrator called Agamemnon “the hero” as he turned his brother's mind, “urging what was due” (αἴσιμα παρειπών, 6.61–62). Whether those words approve the killing is disputed; the Trojans had broken the oaths (4.157–162), and Agamemnon's speech demands that no Trojan escape, not even the unborn (6.57–60; [[oaths-and-their-breaking]], [[agamemnon]]). As in Book 5, where “powerful Agamemnon” kills Deicoon just after his exhortation (5.533–537), the title marks the king's own blow.
