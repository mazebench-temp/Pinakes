---
summary: Andromache enters the poem with the epithet of Hera and Helen: Hector does not find “white-armed Andromache” in his halls, and asks where she has gone.
grc: [λευκώλενον]
---

“But he did not find white-armed Andromache in the halls” (6.371), and Hector asks the slave women, “where has white-armed Andromache gone from the hall?” (6.377; [[andromache]], [[handmaids]]). Helen too entered the poem with this epithet, when Iris found her at her loom in the hall (3.121). Andromache's first appearance is an absence from the place where a white-armed wife belongs: she has gone to the tower with her child and a handmaid, “lamenting and weeping” (6.372–373; [[the-tower-of-ilios]]). At the end of the scene Hector sends her back to the house, to the loom and the distaff (6.490–491; [[weaving]], [[husbands-and-wives]]).
