---
summary: Diomedes tells Glaucus that he does not remember his father Tydeus, who left him as a small child “when the army of the Achaeans perished at Thebes” (6.222–223).
---

When Diomedes has recognized in Glaucus a guest-friend of his house, he speaks of the grandfather who made the bond and of the father he never knew:

{{quote:b13cc5cc-0224-5666-b5db-edabe90259b9}}

The war of the Seven took Tydeus before his son could know him (see [[tydeus]] and [[diomedes]]). Diomedes calls the attackers “the army of the Achaeans” (λαὸς Ἀχαιῶν, 6.223); Agamemnon too called them Achaeans (4.384). What he has of his father comes from others. Agamemnon told Tydeus' deeds at Thebes from report, “for I myself never met him or saw him” (4.374–375; see [[tydeus-at-thebes]]), and Athena held up his father to him as the better fighter (5.800–813). What he has of his grandfather Oeneus is a cup, left in his house (6.220–221), and the guest-friendship that it proves. In Book 14 he claims his father with pride, “the son of a good father, Tydeus, whom the heaped earth covers at Thebes” (14.113–114).
