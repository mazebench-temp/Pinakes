---
summary: Achilles burned Eetion's body “with his intricate armor,” honoring the king he had killed.
grc: [δαιδαλέοισιν]
en: [intricate]
---

Andromache tells how Achilles killed her father, “but he did not strip him, for his heart held back in awe from that, but he burned him with his intricate armor and heaped a mound over him” (σὺν ἔντεσι δαιδαλέοισιν, 6.417–419; [[eetion]], [[stripping-the-dead]], [[cremation]]). δαιδάλεος, “elaborately worked,” is the adjective that Book 4 gave to Menelaus' belt (4.135–136). The richly made armor would have been the killer's prize; Achilles leaves it to the dead man. The mountain nymphs plant elms around the tomb (6.419–420; [[nymphs]], [[tomb]], [[sack-of-thebe]]).
