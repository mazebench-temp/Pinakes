**The rebuke.** Hector finds Paris in his chamber with his armor and rebukes him “with shaming words” (6.325), the verse that introduced his rebuke in Book 3 (3.38; see [[aischros]]). He measures Paris by Paris' own standard: the people are dying around the wall for his sake, “and you yourself would quarrel with any other man whom you saw holding back from hateful war” (6.327–330). “Holding back from hateful war” is the charge Agamemnon made against the slack in his review (4.240). Paris accepts it as fair, “fairly and not beyond what is fair” (6.333; see [[aisa]]), explains, and promises to follow. Hector does not answer him: “Hector of the gleaming helmet said nothing to him” (6.342; see [[silence]]).

**Praise and blame together.** When Paris catches up with him at the gates and asks whether he has kept him waiting, Hector gives his judgment:

{{quote:810aaf86-0bf4-5bc4-a724-99a2f33672e1}}

The praise comes first and is real: no fair-minded man would dishonor his work in battle, ἐπεὶ ἄλκιμός ἐσσι, “since you are valiant” (see [[time]]). The blame follows, and it is exact: he holds back ἑκών, “of his own will”. What grieves Hector is what others say, “when I hear shameful things said of you by the Trojans” (see [[achos]]). Then he stops and turns to what they will do together, “But let us go” (6.526). The rebuke of Book 3 was an outburst; this one is a verdict. See [[paris]] and [[hector]].

**Other rebukes.** At the start of the book Agamemnon runs up to Menelaus, who is about to spare Adrastus, “and rebuked him”, asking whether the Trojans had treated him so well in his house (6.53–60). The rebuke is for mercy, and it works (see [[aisa]] and [[apoina]]). Helen, speaking to Hector, blames herself, “dog that I am”, and blames Paris, whose mind is not steadfast and never will be (6.344–356; see [[who-is-to-blame]]).

**Praise foreseen.** The praise Hector will have is imagined in the mouth of a stranger: “This is the wife of Hector, who was always the best in the fighting of the horse-taming Trojans” (6.460–461; see [[kleos]] and [[tis-speech]]).
