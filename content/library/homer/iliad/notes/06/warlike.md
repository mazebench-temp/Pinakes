---
summary: Paris asks Hector to wait while he puts on his “armor of war,” Ἀρήϊα τεύχεα, the use of the adjective for war gear.
grc: [Ἀρήϊα τεύχεα]
en: [armor of war]
---

“But come now, wait while I put on my armor of war; or go, and I will follow; and I think I will overtake you” (Ἀρήϊα τεύχεα δύω, 6.340–341; [[paris]], [[arming-scene]]). ἀρήϊος, “of Ares,” describes armor as the gear of war; the Oxford text prints it here with a capital, as if “the armor of Ares,” but the phrase is the same one that names arms and armor elsewhere: ἔντεʼ ἀρήϊα (Il. 10.407), ἀρήϊα τεύχεʼ (Il. 14.381), and in the Odyssey the arms stored in Odysseus' hall (Od. 16.284, 19.4). Paris' armor is in his chamber, where Hector found him handling it (6.321–322; [[perikalles]]), and he puts it on and comes down from Pergamus like a stallion (6.503–514; [[inlaid]], [[the-stallion]]). The capitalized form is the adjective, not the name of the god, and the entry for Ares does not claim it ([[ares]]).
