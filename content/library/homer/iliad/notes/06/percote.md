---
summary: Pidytes, whom Odysseus kills, is “of Percote”, the town near the Hellespont (6.30).
grc: [Περκώσιον]
---

Περκώσιον, “of Percote” (6.30), is the adjective the Catalogue gave the seer Merops (Περκωσίου, 2.831; see [[merops]]). It stands where a father's name would stand, and it is all the poem tells of Pidytes (see [[pidytes]]). Percote was one of the towns near the Hellespont under Asius (2.835; see [[asius]]), and so was Arisbe, the home of Axylus, killed a few lines earlier (6.13; see [[arisbe]]). The men of the opening list come from all over the Troad: from Arisbe and Percote in the north, and from Pedasus on the Satnioeis in the south (6.13, 6.30, 6.34–35; see [[pedasus-on-the-satnioeis]]).
