---
summary: Hecuba comes to meet Hector with Laodice, “the most beautiful in form of her daughters.”
---

“There his generous mother came to meet him, leading in Laodice, the most beautiful in form of her daughters” (6.251–252; [[generous]], [[laodice]]). The half-verse θυγατρῶν εἶδος ἀρίστην is the one that named Laodice when Iris took her likeness to call Helen to the wall (3.124). The phrase of the loveliest daughter frames the house of Priam, whose daughters' chambers the poem has just described (6.247–250; [[priams-palace]]). Whether Hecuba leads Laodice in or goes in to her is disputed; the translation takes the verb in its natural sense.
