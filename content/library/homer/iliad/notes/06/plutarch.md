summary: In the Life of Brutus, Porcia weeps before a painting of Andromache's farewell to Hector, and Brutus will not give his wife Hector's answer; in On the Bravery of Women, the women of Lycia turn back the angry Bellerophon.
---

- Porcia and the painting of Andromache (*Brutus* 23): [[porcia-and-andromache]].
- **The women of Lycia.** Plutarch tells how Bellerophon, cheated of his reward by the Lycian king, prayed to Poseidon against the land, and a wave followed him across the plain and flooded it. The men could not persuade him to stop; the women came to meet him with their garments raised, and he turned back in shame, and the sea with him (*On the Bravery of Women* 9). Glaucus' story ends with the king giving Bellerophon his daughter and half his kingdom (6.191–195). See [[bellerophon]].
