---
summary: Diomedes opens his challenge by asking the stranger who he is “among mortal men.”
grc: [καταθνητῶν ἀνθρώπων]
---

“Who are you, good sir, among mortal men?” (6.123; [[good-sir]]). καταθνητός is a longer form of θνητός, “mortal.” The question takes for granted what Diomedes goes on to doubt: a man who has never been seen in battle and who dares to face him may be one of the immortals, and Diomedes will not fight the gods (6.128–129; [[immortals]]). His speech ends with the other formula of human mortality, “the mortals who eat the fruit of the field” (6.142; [[who-eat-the-fruit-of-the-field]]), and Glaucus answers with the generations of leaves (6.146–149; [[the-generations-of-leaves]]).
