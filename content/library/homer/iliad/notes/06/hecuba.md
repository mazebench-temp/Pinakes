summary: Hecuba's first appearance. She meets Hector in Priam's palace and offers him wine, which he refuses; at his word she takes the finest of her Sidonian robes, brought home on the voyage that brought Helen, and leads the old women of Troy to Athena, who refuses their prayer.

**Mother and son.** Hecuba enters the poem as a mother. Helenus names her first, “your mother and mine” (6.87; see [[helenus]]), and when Hector comes to the palace she comes to meet him, leading in Laodice (6.251–252; see [[laodice]] and [[priams-palace]]). Her first words to him are a mother's guesses, and they are wrong:

{{quote:f2698c71-8911-5b69-bf78-63eddf204fb8}}

She supposes that he has come to pray from the citadel, and offers him wine to pour and to drink, since “in a weary man wine greatly increases might” (6.261; see [[wine]] and [[menos]]). He refuses both: it would unnerve him, and he will not pour to Zeus with unwashed hands (6.264–268; see [[hector]] and [[libation]]). She does not answer. She does what he tells her.

**The robe.** She sends her handmaids to gather the old women and goes down herself into the fragrant chamber (6.286–288; see [[handmaids]]). Her robes there are the work of Sidonian women whom Alexander brought home on the voyage on which he brought Helen (6.289–292; see [[sidonians]] and [[paris]]). She takes the largest and the most beautiful in its embroidery, the one that “shone like a star; and it lay beneath all the others” (6.293–295; see [[like-a-star]] and [[robe]]). The gift for the goddess comes from the voyage that began the war. She leads the old women to the temple on the citadel; Theano lays the robe on Athena's knees and prays that Diomedes may fall before the Scaean gates, and Athena refuses (6.296–311; see [[theano]], [[athena]] and [[the-robe-for-athena]]).

**Named by her son.** Her name comes once more in the book, in Hector's answer to Andromache. The sorrow of the Trojans hereafter does not trouble him so much, “nor of Hecuba herself, nor of lord Priam”, nor of his brothers, as hers (6.450–455; see [[andromache]]).
