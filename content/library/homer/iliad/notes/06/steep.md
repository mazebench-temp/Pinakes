---
summary: Steep are Pedasus on the Satnioeis, the “sheer destruction” that Agamemnon wishes on every Trojan, and the wall of Troy around which the people are dying.
en: [sheer]
---

The epithet of hill towns names the home of Elatus, whom Agamemnon kills: “steep Pedasus” on the Satnioeis (Πήδασον αἰπεινήν, 6.35; [[pedasus-on-the-satnioeis]], [[fair-flowing]]).

The figurative sense comes a few lines later, from the same king. Menelaus is ready to take Adrastus alive for ransom, and Agamemnon forbids it: “Let not one of them escape sheer destruction and our hands, not even the boy whom his mother carries in her womb” (αἰπὺν ὄλεθρον, 6.57–59; [[adrastus-the-trojan]], [[my-dear]]). The fall with no way back is wished on a whole people.

The last use is the wall itself. Hector tells Paris that “the people are dying around the city and the steep wall in the fighting” (αἰπύ τε τεῖχος, 6.327–328; [[paris]]). The steep wall of Troy is the place where the book's last scenes are set, from the tower where Andromache stands to the gates where she meets Hector ([[the-tower-of-ilios]], [[scaean-gates]]).
