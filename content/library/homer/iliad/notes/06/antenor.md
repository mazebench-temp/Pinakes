---
summary: Antenor is named as the husband of Theano, the priestess who opens Athena's temple to the Trojan women and prays in vain for the city (6.297–311).
---

{{quote:cd5aaf78-3d68-5944-a7f6-87ac728a0c44}}

Theano is “the wife of Antenor, tamer of horses” (Ἀντήνορος ἱπποδάμοιο, 6.299), the epithet of the Trojans themselves, which the poem gives Antenor again at 14.473 (see [[tamer-of-horses]] and [[theano]]). The elder who once received the Achaean envoys in his house (3.205–207) is also the husband of the city's priestess of Athena.

Twice in one day his house tries to save the city, and twice it is refused. Theano prays to Athena to break Diomedes' spear and pity the city, and the goddess shakes her head (6.305–311). That night, in the Trojan assembly, Antenor proposes to give back Helen and her possessions, since the Trojans are fighting with the oaths broken, and Paris refuses to give up the woman (7.345–364; see [[oaths-and-their-breaking]]).
