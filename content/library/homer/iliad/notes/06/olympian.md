---
summary: Hector names Zeus “the Olympian” as the god who raised Paris to be a bane to Troy.
---

“There and then may the earth gape for him! For the Olympian has raised him as a great bane to the Trojans and to great-hearted Priam and his sons” (6.281–283; [[may-the-earth-gape]], [[great-hearted]]). As in Books 2 and 4, the title alone, without the name, stresses the authority of Zeus. Hector does not blame the gods instead of his brother; he wishes his brother dead, and would forget his own misery if he saw him go down into the house of Hades (6.284–285; [[paris]], [[hades]], [[who-is-to-blame]]).
