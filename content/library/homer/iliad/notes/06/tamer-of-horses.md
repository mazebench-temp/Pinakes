---
summary: The epithet names Antenor, “tamer of horses,” as the husband of the priestess Theano, and the Trojans in the words Hector imagines a man saying of Andromache in slavery.
---

Theano is “daughter of Cisseus, the wife of Antenor, tamer of horses” (6.299; [[theano]], [[antenor]], [[cisseus]]). The singular, which Books 2 to 5 gave to Atreus, Castor, Tydeus and Diomedes, is here given to a Trojan elder.

The plural comes in the speech that Hector imagines for a passer-by in Argos, seeing Andromache weep as she carries water: “This is the wife of Hector, who was always the best in the fighting of the horse-taming Trojans, when they fought around Ilios” (6.460–461; [[captive-women]], [[kleos]]). The Trojans' standing epithet is heard in the mouth of an enemy, in the country of their conquerors, as a memory. Hector's fame will live on as the measure of his wife's grief ([[trojans]], [[andromache]]).
