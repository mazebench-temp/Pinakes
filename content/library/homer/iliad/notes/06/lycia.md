---
summary: Lycia is the land to which Proetus sends Bellerophon with the grim signs, “wide Lycia” on the flowing Xanthus, where Bellerophon's line comes to rule; Diomedes tells Glaucus that he will be his guest there.
grc: [Λυκίην]
---

Book 6 names Lycia seven times, all in the meeting of Glaucus and Diomedes. Proetus sends Bellerophon “to Lycia” (Λυκίην δέ, with the suffix -δε, “to”, 6.168, 6.171), and he reaches “Lycia and the flowing Xanthus” (6.172; see [[xanthus-of-lycia]]). Its king, “the lord of wide Lycia” (ἄναξ Λυκίης εὐρείης, 6.173; see [[wide-lycia]]), entertains him for nine days before he asks to see the sign (see [[xenia]]), and the ambush he sets is made of “the best men out of wide Lycia” (6.188). The land that tried to kill Bellerophon gives him a king's daughter and a domain (6.192–195; see [[bellerophon]] and [[lycians]]), and his descendants “were by far the best in Ephyra and in wide Lycia” (6.209–210). Diomedes ends the family story with the present: “you are mine in Lycia, whenever I come to that land” (6.225).

This is Sarpedon's Lycia in the southwest of Asia Minor, not the Lycia of Pandarus (5.105, 5.173). Strabo set the Chimaera's mountains in western Lycia, at Cragus (14.3.5; see [[chimaera]]).
