summary: Hector finds Paris in his chamber handling his curved bow (6.322). Teucer, the Achaeans' archer, makes his first kill (6.31), and Artemis of the showering arrows strikes down Andromache's mother (6.428).
passages:
  - 6.318-324 | Hector finds Paris in his chamber
---

**Paris' bow.** Hector finds Paris “handling his curved bow” (ἀγκύλα τόξʼ ἁφόωντα, 6.322). ἀγκύλος is “curved” (see [[curved-bow]]), and ἁφάω, “handle, feel with the hands”, occurs only here in Homer. The bow is the weapon Paris is known by. He came out before the armies in Book 3 with a leopard's skin on his shoulders and his curved bow (3.17), and in Book 11 he will wound Diomedes, Machaon and Eurypylus with it; Diomedes calls him an archer, “glorying in your bow” (11.369–385, 11.505–507, 11.581–584; see [[paris]]). Here he handles it in his chamber, with his shield and corselet, while Helen sits among her women (see [[corselet]]).

**Teucer.** The Achaeans' archer enters the poem in the kill-list that opens the book: “and Teucer slew brilliant Aretaon” (6.31). How he killed him is not said. His bow and his arrows come in Book 8 (8.266–334; see [[teucer]] and [[aretaon]]).

**The arrows of Artemis.** Andromache's mother, set free for ransom, died in her father's halls, where “Artemis of the showering arrows struck her down” (6.428; see [[artemis]] and [[of-the-showering-arrows]]). The arrows of Artemis bring sudden death to women, as Apollo's do to men. In the Odyssey the shade of Odysseus' mother is asked whether Artemis came upon her “with her gentle shafts” (Od. 11.172–173), and on the island of Syria Apollo and Artemis kill men and women grown old in the same way (Od. 15.409–411; see [[arrows]]).
