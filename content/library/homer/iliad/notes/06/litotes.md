---
summary: Book 6 understates death: Lycurgus “did not live long” (6.130–131, 6.139–140), and the Lycians who lay in wait for Bellerophon “never came home again” (6.189). Hector “did not disobey his brother” (6.102), and he praises Paris by a denial (6.521–522).
refs: [6.102, 6.130-131, 6.139-140, 6.189, 6.521-522]
---

**Not long.** “For not even the son of Dryas, mighty Lycurgus, lived long” (οὐδὲ … δὴν ἦν, 6.130–131), and after Zeus blinded him “he did not live long after” (οὐδʼ ἄρʼ ἔτι δὴν ἦν, 6.139–140). The negative means that he soon died, as Dione's οὐ δηναιός, “does not live long”, meant death for the man who fights the gods (5.407; see [[mythological-paradigm]]).

**Not home.** The best men of Lycia lay in ambush for Bellerophon, “but they never came home again” (τοὶ δʼ οὔ τι πάλιν οἶκον δὲ νέοντο, 6.189). The next verse gives the plain fact: “for noble Bellerophon killed them all” (6.190; see [[ambush]] and [[nostos]]).

**Not disobey.** “So he spoke, and Hector did not disobey his brother” (6.102). οὐ … ἀπίθησεν means that he obeyed at once, as the goddesses did in Book 5 (5.719, 5.767; see [[so-he-spoke]]): he leaps from his chariot in the next verse.

**Praise by denial.** Hector's last speech opens with a litotes: “Strange one, no man who was fair-minded would dishonor your work in battle, since you are valiant” (6.521–522). The denial praises Paris' fighting, and the next words take the praise back: he holds back of his own will (6.523; see [[paris]]).
