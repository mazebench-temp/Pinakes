summary: The epithet of praise belongs to Bellerophon three times in Glaucus' story, and once to the gods' escort that brings him to Lycia; it names Bucolion, father of twins, and Andromache, the wife Hector does not find at home.
grc: [ἀμύμονι]
---

Bellerophon is ἀμύμων in each part of his story: when Glaucus names him (“noble Bellerophon,” 6.155), when he kills the best men of Lycia in their ambush (6.190), and when Diomedes recalls how Oeneus entertained him (6.216; [[bellerophon]], [[xenia]]). He goes to Lycia “under the blameless escort of the gods” (θεῶν ὑπʼ ἀμύμονι πομπῇ, 6.171), and there the translation keeps the traditional sense: the escort is faultless because it comes from the gods, and it carries a man who bears a message meant to kill him (6.168–170; [[grim]]).

Two other uses are plain praise. Bucolion, the eldest son of Laomedon, is “noble Bucolion,” whose twin sons by a nymph Euryalus kills (6.22–28; [[bucolion]], [[abarbarea]]). And when Hector comes home he does not find “his noble wife” within (ἀμύμονα … ἄκοιτιν, 6.374; [[andromache]]). The word of general praise, as Anne Amory Parry argued it to be, suits a hero, a father and a wife alike.
