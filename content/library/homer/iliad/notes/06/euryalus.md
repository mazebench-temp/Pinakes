**Four kills.** Euryalus has the largest share of the list of kills that opens the book (6.5–36): two men in half a line, then two more in eight lines (see [[dresus]], [[opheltius]], [[aesepus-son-of-bucolion]] and [[pedasus-son-of-bucolion]]). He is the third of the leaders from Argos and Tiryns, after Diomedes and Sthenelus (2.563–566), and these are his only kills in the Iliad. His one other scene is the boxing at Patroclus' funeral games, where he alone stands up to Epeius and is knocked senseless (23.677–699).

**Son of Mecisteus.** Μηκιστηϊάδης, “the son of Mecisteus” (6.28), is formed from his father's name with the patronymic suffix -ιάδης (see [[mecisteus]]). The sentence that kills the twins holds back the killer's name to the start of its second line, after the story of their birth: “and of these too he loosened the strength and the shining limbs, the son of Mecisteus, and stripped the armor from their shoulders” (6.27–28; see [[bucolion]] and [[stripping-the-dead]]).
