---
summary: The formula names Diomedes and Menelaus as they take their men in the opening killings, and Diomedes twice in his meeting with Glaucus, as he challenges him and as he rejoices to find a guest-friend.
---

In the killings that open the book the formula belongs to both its usual bearers. “Diomedes, good at the war cry, killed Axylus” (6.12; [[axylus]]), and “Menelaus, good at the war cry, took Adrastus alive” (6.37–38; [[adrastus-the-trojan]]). Menelaus' capture ends in his brother's refusal of ransom (6.51–65).

The other two uses frame the meeting of Glaucus and Diomedes. “Diomedes, good at the war cry, spoke to him first” (6.122), and the man of the shout begins with a courteous question, “Who are you, good sir?” ([[good-sir]]). When Glaucus has told his lineage, “Diomedes, good at the war cry, was glad” (6.212), and he plants his spear in the ground (6.213; [[bountiful-earth]]). The formula of the war cry introduces the one scene of the book in which two enemies agree not to fight ([[glaucus-and-diomedes]], [[xenia]], [[diomedes]]).
