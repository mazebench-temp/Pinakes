---
summary: The two sons of Atreus act apart over the suppliant Adrastus, and Andromache names them together among the best of the Achaeans who have tried the wall by the fig tree.
grc: [Ἀτρεΐδας]
---

**Over Adrastus.** Menelaus takes Adrastus alive and is ready to accept his ransom; Agamemnon runs up, rebukes him, and kills the man himself (6.37–65; see [[adrastus-the-trojan]], [[menelaus]] and [[agamemnon]]). The narrator calls both of them “the son of Atreus” in the same scene (6.44, 6.64).

**At the wall.** Andromache tells Hector that three times the best of the Achaeans have come to the place by the wild fig tree where the wall can be scaled, “with the two Ajaxes and far-famed Idomeneus and with the sons of Atreus and the valiant son of Tydeus” (6.435–437; see [[the-fig-tree]] and [[andromaches-advice]]). ἀμφʼ Ἀτρεΐδας, literally “around the sons of Atreus”, names the leaders together with their men. No such assault is narrated in the Iliad.
