---
summary: Hector takes off his helmet and lays it, “all gleaming,” on the ground, so that his son will not be afraid.
grc: [παμφανόωσαν]
---

“And at once shining Hector took the helmet from his head and laid it, all gleaming, on the ground” (κατέθηκεν ἐπὶ χθονὶ παμφανόωσαν, 6.472–473; [[helmet]], [[of-the-gleaming-helmet]], [[phaidimos]]). In Book 5 the word described the armor clattering on the dead Pandarus and the spears the Trojans threw at Ajax (5.295, 5.619). Here the gleaming bronze is set aside: its brightness is what frightened the child, and Hector puts it down to take him in his arms (6.466–474; [[astyanax]], [[horsehair-crested]]).
