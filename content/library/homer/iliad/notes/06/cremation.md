summary: Achilles did not strip the dead Eetion but “burned him with his intricate armor” and heaped a mound over him (6.417–419), the burial with his arms that the dead Elpenor begs for in the Odyssey.
grc: [κατέκηε]
en: [burned him]

  - 6.414-420 | The tomb of Eetion
---

Andromache tells how Achilles killed her father, “but he did not strip him, for his heart held back in awe from that, but he burned him with his intricate armor and heaped a mound over him” (ἀλλʼ ἄρα μιν κατέκηε σὺν ἔντεσι δαιδαλέοισιν, 6.417–419). The armor that belonged to the victor by right was burned with the dead king (see [[stripping-the-dead]]). δαιδάλεος, “intricate”, is richly worked armor, a king's. To be burned with one's arms is an honor the dead ask for: Elpenor's ghost begs Odysseus, “burn me with my armor, all that I have, and heap up a mound for me on the shore of the gray sea” (Od. 11.74–75), and his companions burn him with his armor and set his oar on the mound (Od. 12.13–15). Achilles gave his enemy that honor (see [[eetion]], [[tomb]] and [[sack-of-thebe]]).

Andromache speaks of the burning to the man whose own body will lie unburned for days after Achilles kills him. When she hears that he is dead, she says that she will burn the fine clothes in the house instead, since he will not lie in them (22.508–514; see [[andromache]]).
