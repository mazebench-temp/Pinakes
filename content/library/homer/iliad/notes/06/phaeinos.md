---
summary: The shining things of Book 6 are Antilochus' spear in the opening killings and the belt bright with purple that Oeneus gave Bellerophon as a guest-gift.
en: [bright]
---

“And Antilochus slew Ablerus with his shining spear, the son of Nestor” (6.32–33; [[antilochus]], [[ablerus]]). The formula δουρὶ φαεινῷ is one of the commonest of the killings ([[spear]]).

The second use is a gift. When Oeneus entertained Bellerophon, the two exchanged guest-gifts: “Oeneus gave a belt bright with purple” (ζωστῆρα … φοίνικι φαεινόν, 6.219), and Bellerophon a two-handled golden cup, which Diomedes says he left at home when he came to Troy (6.218–221; [[guest-gifts]], [[cup]], [[purple]]). The shining belt of the fathers' friendship is set against the armor that the grandsons exchange a few lines later, gold for bronze (6.230–236; [[glaucus-and-diomedes]]).
