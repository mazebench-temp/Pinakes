---
summary: The translation's “polished” renders ξεστός, from the same verb as ἐΰξοος, in the description of Priam's palace: its polished colonnades and its chambers of polished stone.
grc: [ξεστῇς αἰθούσῃσι, ξεστοῖο λίθοιο]
---

ξεστός (“smoothed, polished”) is the verbal adjective of ξέω (“scrape, plane, smooth”), the verb in ἐΰξοος. Where ἐΰξοος praises wood and horn, ξεστός describes dressed stone. Priam's house is “built with polished colonnades” (ξεστῇς αἰθούσῃσι, 6.243), and its chambers for the sons and the daughters are “of polished stone” (ξεστοῖο λίθοιο, 6.244, 6.248; [[priams-palace]], [[house]]). The same phrase for colonnades describes the house of Zeus on Olympus, where the gods assemble in Book 20 (Il. 20.11), and the elders of the city on the shield of Achilles sit “on polished stones” (Il. 18.504). The palace of Troy is built like the house of a god ([[perikalles]]).
