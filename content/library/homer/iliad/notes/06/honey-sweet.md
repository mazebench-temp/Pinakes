---
summary: Hecuba offers Hector “honey-sweet wine,” and he refuses “wine sweet to the heart”; the translation keeps the two words, μελιηδής and μελίφρων, apart.
grc: [μελιηδέα, μελίφρονα]
en: [sweet to the heart]
---

Hecuba offers the wine of welcome: “But wait while I bring you honey-sweet wine, so that you may pour a libation to father Zeus and the other immortals first, and then you yourself will be the better for it, if you drink. For in a weary man wine greatly increases might” (μελιηδέα οἶνον, 6.258–261; [[wine]], [[libation]], [[generous]]). Hector refuses it with the other word: “Do not bring me wine sweet to the heart, lady mother, for fear you unnerve me and rob me of my might, and I forget my courage” (οἶνον … μελίφρονα, 6.264–265; [[menos]], [[furious]]). μελίφρων, literally honey to the mind, is the word Book 2 gave to sleep (2.34), and the translation renders it here more literally. The mother offers the wine to restore his might; the son fears it will take his might away. Nor will he pour to Zeus with unwashed hands, spattered with blood (6.266–268; [[hand-washing]]).
