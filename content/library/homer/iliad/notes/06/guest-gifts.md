**The grandfathers' gifts.** Oeneus kept Bellerophon twenty days in his halls, “and they gave each other fine guest-gifts: Oeneus gave a belt bright with purple, and Bellerophon a two-handled cup of gold, which I left in my house when I came here” (6.217–221). The host gives the belt of a warrior, the guest the cup of a feast (see [[belt]], [[cup]] and [[purple]]). The cup is still in Diomedes' house in Argos, the evidence of a bond he learns about only now. He cannot remember his father, who died at Thebes when he was small (6.222–223), but he keeps his grandfather's guest-gift (see [[xenia]] and [[oeneus]]).

**New gifts for an old bond.** “And let us exchange armor with each other, so that these men too may know that we claim to be guest-friends from our fathers' time” (6.230–231). The grandfathers' gifts were given in a house and kept in a treasury. The grandsons' are given on the field, before both armies, and they will be worn. They leap down from their chariots, take each other's hands and pledge their faith (6.232–233).

**Gold for bronze.**

{{quote:db540de2-4ce8-55df-a865-408804c2074a}}

The exchange is unequal, and the narrator puts a price on it, “the worth of a hundred oxen for the worth of nine” (see [[hecatomb]] and [[gold-and-silver]]). The verse that introduces it, “Zeus, son of Cronus, took away Glaucus' wits”, uses the phrase with which Agamemnon excuses his own delusion (φρένας ἐξέλετο Ζεύς, 19.137), and Achilles says much the same of Agamemnon (9.377). Whether the comment is a joke at Glaucus' expense, a sober verdict on a bad bargain, or a way of marking his generosity is disputed. In a society where a gift puts its receiver in debt, the greater gift is also the grander one, and the exchange has been read in the light of Homeric gift-giving (Walter Donlan, “The Unequal Exchange between Glaucus and Diomedes in Light of the Homeric Gift-Economy”, *Phoenix* 43, 1989). The phrase “gold for bronze” became proverbial (see [[gold-for-bronze]]). See [[glaucus]], [[diomedes]] and [[glaucus-and-diomedes]].
