---
summary: The Amazons are the third of the tasks the king of Lycia sets Bellerophon: “Third, he killed the Amazons, the equals of men” (6.186).
---

τὸ τρίτον αὖ κατέπεφνεν Ἀμαζόνας ἀντιανείρας. The tasks go from a monster of divine race to the fiercest battle of men and then to these women, and each should have been Bellerophon's death (6.179–186; see [[bellerophon]], [[chimaera]] and [[solymi]]). The phrase is the one Priam used in Book 3, when he remembered fighting the Amazons beside the Phrygians on the Sangarius (Ἀμαζόνες ἀντιάνειραι, 3.189; see [[equals-of-men]] and [[priam-and-the-amazons]]). Both memories belong to the generations before the war, and in both the Amazons are enemies of a people of Asia Minor, the Phrygians and the Lycians. Strabo pairs the two, saying that Priam and Bellerophon both fought them (12.8.6). Homer never brings an Amazon to Troy.
