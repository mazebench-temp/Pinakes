---
summary: The Trojan women pray “to the daughter of great Zeus,” and the phrase frames their prayer and its refusal.
grc: [Διὸς κούρῃ μεγάλοιο]
---

“And made her prayer to the daughter of great Zeus” (6.304), and after the refusal, “So they prayed to the daughter of great Zeus” (6.312; [[prayer]], [[the-robe-for-athena]]). Διὸς κούρη μεγάλοιο is a variant of Διὸς θυγάτηρ. Elsewhere it names Athena (Il. 10.296; Od. 6.323, 24.521) and once Artemis, to whom Oeneus failed to sacrifice (Il. 9.536).

Between the two verses stands the narrator's one line of refusal: “So she spoke in prayer, but Pallas Athena shook her head” (6.311; [[pallas]], [[athena]]). In Book 10 the same two verses come in the same order with the opposite answer. Diomedes and Odysseus pray to her before the night raid, “and Pallas Athena heard them,” and then, “when they had prayed to the daughter of great Zeus,” they set out (Il. 10.295–297; [[never-touched-by-the-goad]]).
