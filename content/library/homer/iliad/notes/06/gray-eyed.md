---
summary: Athena is gray-eyed once in Book 6, in Helenus' instruction to take the robe to her temple on the citadel of Troy.
grc: [γλαυκώπιδος]
---

Helenus tells Hector to have their mother gather the old women “to the temple of gray-eyed Athena on the height of the city” (6.88; [[temple]], [[helenus]]). It is the only time in the book that she has her chief epithet. As the robe is laid on her knees she is “Athena of the lovely hair” (6.92, 6.273, 6.303; [[lovely-haired]]); in Hector's words to his mother she is “driver of spoil” (6.269, 6.279; [[driver-of-spoil]]); in Theano's prayer she is “lady,” “protector of the city” and “brilliant among goddesses” (6.305; [[protector-of-the-city]]); and in the verse of her refusal she is Pallas Athena (6.311; [[pallas]]). The Trojans have a whole vocabulary of praise for the goddess who will not help them ([[athena]]).
