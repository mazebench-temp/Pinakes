**Four times.** When Hector smiles at his son, “Andromache stood close beside him, shedding tears” (δάκρυ χέουσα, 6.405), and she speaks. In his answer he pictures her a slave in Argos, and a man who “sees you shedding tears” (κατὰ δάκρυ χέουσαν) says, “This is the wife of Hector” (6.459–460; see [[tis-speech]] and [[captive-women]]). When he gives the boy back to her after his prayer, she takes him δακρυόεν γελάσασα, “laughing through her tears” (6.484). δακρυόεις, “tearful”, is a word for war (πόλεμον … δακρυόεντα, 5.737), and Hector has just used it of her as a captive led away “in tears” (δακρυόεσσαν, 6.455); here it is her laughter. At the end she goes home “turning back again and again, shedding big tears” (θαλερὸν κατὰ δάκρυ χέουσα, 6.496). θαλερός, “big, swelling”, is used of tears that come in a flood: Thersites sheds one when Odysseus strikes him (2.266), and Achilles weeps so for Patroclus in the last book (24.9).

The tears frame the scene, which has often been read as a lament for Hector before his death. At home the handmaids take up the lament with her (6.497–502; see [[lament]], [[tears]] and [[andromache]]).
