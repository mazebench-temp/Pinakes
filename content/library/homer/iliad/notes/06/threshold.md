“And when Hector did not find his noble wife within, he went and stood on the threshold, and spoke among the slave women” (ἔστη ἐπʼ οὐδὸν ἰών, μετὰ δὲ δμῳῇσιν ἔειπεν, 6.374–375). οὐδός is the threshold of a house, and this is the word the Odyssey uses again and again for the doorway of Odysseus' hall. Telemachus, in nearly the same verse, stands on the threshold and speaks to the housekeeper Eurycleia (Od. 20.128). The master of the house stands in his own doorway and questions the women (see [[house]], [[handmaids]] and [[visit-scene]]). The housekeeper's answer sends him straight back through the streets to the Scaean gates (6.390–393). This threshold is not the divine one of Olympus (see [[threshold]]).
