---
summary: Diomedes answers Glaucus “with soothing words” and offers friendship; Helen speaks to Hector “with soothing words” and offers him a seat, which he refuses.
grc: [μειλιχίοισι]
---

The two uses in Book 6 introduce two offers. Diomedes plants his spear and speaks to Glaucus “with soothing words” (6.214), proposing that their grandfathers' guest-friendship hold between them (6.215–231; [[xenia]], [[glaucus-and-diomedes]]). Glaucus accepts. Helen speaks to Hector “with soothing words” (6.343) after he has rebuked Paris, and asks him to come in and sit (6.354–358). Hector refuses: “Do not ask me to sit, Helen, though you love me; you will not persuade me” (6.360; [[helen]], [[hector]]). Helen's soothing speech begins by calling herself a dog, “evil-scheming and chilling” (6.344; [[evil-scheming]]). In the formula without a noun (μειλιχίοισι προσηύδα, 6.214), “words” is understood.
