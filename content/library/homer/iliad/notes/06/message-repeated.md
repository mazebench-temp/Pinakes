**Seer, son, mother.** Helenus gives Hector the message in the third person: “tell your mother and mine; let her gather the old women to the temple of gray-eyed Athena” (6.86–88). Hector delivers it to Hecuba in the second person (6.269–278). The eight verses on the robe and the vow return with the persons changed, “the robe that seems to her the loveliest and largest” becoming “the robe that is the loveliest and largest in your hall” (6.90–92, 6.271–273), and the vow of twelve yearling cows and the plea for pity unchanged (6.93–95 = 6.274–276). ὥς κεν, “so that”, becomes αἴ κεν, “if”: “if she will hold the son of Tydeus back from sacred Ilios” (6.96, 6.277).

**What changes.** Hector's version begins and ends with the same verse, “But you go to the temple of Athena, driver of spoil” (6.269 = 6.279), where Helenus had called her “gray-eyed” (6.88; see [[driver-of-spoil]]). He adds “with burnt offerings” (σὺν θυέεσσιν, 6.270; see [[sacrifice]]), and he leaves out the key with which Hecuba was to open the doors (6.89), and in the event the priestess opens them (6.298; see [[temple]]). Helenus' praise of Diomedes and his comparison with Achilles (6.98–101) were for Hector's ears, and they are not passed on.

**In the temple.** Theano's prayer keeps the vow and the plea for pity, now in the first person: “so that we may now at once sacrifice twelve cows to you in your temple” (6.308–310). The request changes. Helenus asked that Athena hold Diomedes back; Theano asks her to “break the spear of Diomedes, and grant that he himself may fall headlong before the Scaean gates” (6.306–307). The message grows harsher as it passes from the seer to the priestess (see [[prayer-type-scene]] and [[theano]]).

**Question and answer.** Hector asks the slave women whether Andromache has gone to his sisters, to his brothers' wives, or to Athena's temple (6.377–380). The housekeeper answers in the same three verses, turned negative, “she has not gone to your sisters or to your brothers' well-robed wives, nor to the temple of Athena” (6.383–385), and then tells him where she has gone (see [[propitiation]] and [[handmaids]]).
