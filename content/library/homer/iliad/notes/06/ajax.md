summary: Telamonian Ajax, “the bulwark of the Achaeans”, is the first to break a Trojan battalion when the gods have left the field, and kills the Thracian leader Acamas (6.5–11). Later Andromache names the two Ajaxes among the Achaean leaders who have three times tried the wall by the fig tree (6.435–437).

**First.** The book opens with the gods gone. Ares, Hera and Athena have returned to Olympus, and the din of battle “was left to itself” (6.1; 5.906–909). The first man to act in it is Ajax, under the epithet Helen gave him on the wall, ἕρκος Ἀχαιῶν, “the bulwark of the Achaeans” (6.5, 3.229; see [[bulwark-of-the-achaeans]]). He “brought light to his companions” (φόως δʼ ἑτάροισιν ἔθηκεν, 6.6). φόως, “light”, is rescue in battle: Agamemnon tells Teucer to shoot on and be “a light to the Danaans” (8.282), and Patroclus asks to lead the Myrmidons out in the hope that he may be one (16.39). The kill itself is told in the verses of the poem's first kill, Antilochus' (6.9–11 = 4.459–461; see [[acamas-of-thrace]]).

**The list.** Ajax's is the first of fourteen kills that ten Achaean leaders make in thirty-two lines (6.5–36; see [[androktasia]]). His half-brother Teucer comes into the poem in the same list, with no word that they are kin (6.31; see [[teucer]]).

**The two Ajaxes.** Andromache asks Hector to station the army by the wild fig tree, “where most of all the city can be scaled”, because “three times the best of them have come there and made trial of it, with the two Ajaxes and far-famed Idomeneus and with the sons of Atreus and the valiant son of Tydeus” (6.433–437; see [[the-fig-tree]]). Αἴαντε δύω are Telamonian Ajax and the son of Oileus, as in Books 2, 4 and 5 (see [[ajax-son-of-oileus]]). The three attempts are told nowhere in the poem, and Aristarchus rejected the passage as advice unfit for Andromache to give (6.433–439; see [[andromache]]).
