summary: Ares has left the battle, and in Book 6 he is present only in words. Nestor calls the Danaans “attendants of Ares”, the Achaeans are “dear to Ares”, Paris puts on his armor of war, and in Glaucus' story Ares kills Bellerophon's son Isander.
passages:
  - 6.203-204 | Ares kills Isander
---

**Gone from the field.** At the end of Book 5 Ares sits beside Zeus on Olympus, healed, and Hera and Athena have stopped him “from his slaughter of men” (5.905–909). Book 6 opens with the battle “left to itself” (6.1). The god does not come down to the fighting again until the gods take sides in Book 20 (20.38, 20.51–53); in Book 13 he is still sitting on Olympus, kept from the war by Zeus' will, when his son Ascalaphus is killed (13.518–525; see [[ascalaphus]]).

**His name.** Without him his name still means war. Nestor rallies the Achaeans with the formal address, “Friends, Danaan heroes, attendants of Ares” (6.67; also 2.110, 15.733, 19.78; see [[attendants-of-ares]]). The Trojans would have fled into Ilios before “the Achaeans dear to Ares” (ἀρηϊφίλων, 6.73; see [[dear-to-ares]]). Paris asks Hector to wait while he puts on “my armor of war”, Ἀρήϊα τεύχεα, the arms that belong to Ares (6.340; see [[warlike]]).

**In Glaucus' story.** When Bellerophon had become hateful to the gods, “Ares, insatiable of war, killed his son Isander as he fought the glorious Solymi” (6.203–204; see [[isander]], [[solymi]] and [[insatiable-of-war]]). The narrator of Book 5 showed Ares killing a man with his own hand, Periphas the Aetolian (5.842–848; see [[periphas]]). Here a grandson tells how the god killed his uncle, and the death in battle is Ares' deed. Artemis kills the daughter in the next line (6.205; see [[artemis]]).
