summary: Book 6 has twenty-three speeches and no winged words. Its answers come “in turn”, from Glaucus, Paris, the housekeeper and Hector (6.144, 6.332, 6.381, 6.440), and the formula itself brings in the book's last speech, Hector's reply to Paris (6.520).
grc: ["αὖτε προσέειπεν"]
en: [spoke to her in turn]
refs: [6.144, 6.381]
---

**No winged words.** The formula “addressed him with winged words” does not occur in Book 6, though the book is made largely of speeches; in the whole Iliad only Books 6 and 9 are without it (see [[winged-words]]). The speeches are brought in by the frames of reply, by the verse of intimate address, “and spoke to him, and called him by name” (6.253, 6.406, 6.485; see [[called-him-by-name]]), by speech “with soothing words” (6.214, 6.343; see [[gentle-words]]), and by the verse that names who spoke first, τὸν πρότερος προσέειπε (6.122, 6.517; see [[when-they-came-close]]).

**In turn.** “Then godlike Alexander spoke to him in turn” (τὸν δʼ αὖτε προσέειπεν Ἀλέξανδρος θεοειδής, 6.332) is the verse that brought in Paris' answer to Hector's rebuke in Book 3 (3.58), and the answer begins with the same verse too: “Hector, since you rebuke me fairly and not beyond what is fair” (6.333 = 3.59; see [[paris]] and [[aisa]]). Hector answers Andromache in the same frame, “Then great Hector of the gleaming helmet spoke to her in turn” (6.440). Two variants bring in Glaucus' answer to Diomedes' challenge, “Then the shining son of Hippolochus spoke to him in turn” (6.144; see [[genealogy-in-battle]]), and the housekeeper's answer to Hector (6.381; see [[handmaids]]).

**Answering him.** The formula proper comes once, for the last speech of the book: “Answering him, Hector of the gleaming helmet said” (6.520). Paris has asked whether he kept his brother waiting, and Hector answers that no fair-minded man would dishonor his work in battle, but that he holds back of his own will (6.518–525).
