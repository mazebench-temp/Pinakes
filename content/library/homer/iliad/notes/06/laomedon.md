**An eldest son.** Book 6 gives Laomedon a son the rest of the Iliad does not name. Bucolion was “the eldest by birth, but his mother bore him in secret” (6.24): born out of wedlock, he is absent from the line of Troy that Aeneas recites, where Laomedon's sons are Tithonus, Priam, Lampus, Clytius and Hicetaon (20.236–238; see [[bucolion]] and [[nothos]]). ἀγαυός, “noble” (6.23), is the epithet Sarpedon gave Laomedon when he told how the king's folly lost the city to Heracles (5.649; see [[noble]]).

**His grandsons.** The twins whom Euryalus kills, Aesepus and Pedasus, are Laomedon's grandsons and Priam's nephews (see [[aesepus-son-of-bucolion]] and [[pedasus-son-of-bucolion]]). The poem does not say so in those words; it tells the birth of their father and leaves the kinship to be counted.
