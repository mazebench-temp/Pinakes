---
summary: Helenus, and then Hector, ask the Trojan women to pray that Athena keep Diomedes, “that savage spearman, mighty master of rout,” away from Ilios.
grc: [μήστωρα φόβοιο]
en: [master of rout]
---

In Book 5 the phrase was given to the horses of Aeneas, which Diomedes took (5.272). In Book 6 it names Diomedes himself, in the verse that the Trojans' prayer is to use: Athena is to hold back from Ilios the son of Tydeus, “that savage spearman, mighty master of rout, who, I say, has become the strongest of the Achaeans” (6.96–98; Hector repeats it at 6.277–278; [[spearmen]], [[krateros]]). Helenus explains: “We never feared like this even Achilles, leader of men” (6.99; [[leader-of-men]]). When the prayer is made, Theano asks for more than this: that Athena break the spear of Diomedes and let him fall headlong before the Scaean gates (6.305–307). The goddess, who drove Diomedes' chariot against Ares the day before, refuses (6.311; [[diomedes]], [[the-robe-for-athena]]).
