---
summary: Thebes is named once, when Diomedes explains that he does not remember his father, who left him as a small child “when the army of the Achaeans perished at Thebes” (6.223).
grc: [Θήβῃσιν]
# Θήβῃσιν recurs for Eetion's Thebe in the Troad (22.479), which this entry will need to except;
# Θήβῃ (6.397) and Θήβην (6.416) are also Thebe (thebe).
---

ἐν Θήβῃσιν, “at Thebes”, is the dative of the plural name Θῆβαι, as Θήβας is its accusative (5.804); Book 4 used the singular Θήβη for the same city (4.378, 4.406). The “army of the Achaeans” that perished there is the army of the Seven, whom Homer calls Achaeans, as Agamemnon and Athena did in telling Tydeus' embassy (4.384, 5.803; see [[the-seven-against-thebes]] and [[tydeus]]). Diomedes was then “still little”; he took the city himself in the next generation with the Epigoni (4.405–410; see [[the-epigonoi]]). The same case names Thebes as the place of Tydeus' grave: “the heaped earth covers him at Thebes” (Θήβῃσι, 14.114).

The Thebe of the end of the book, Andromache's city “under Placus”, is another place (6.397, 6.416; see [[thebe]]).
