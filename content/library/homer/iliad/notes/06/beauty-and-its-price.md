**Bellerophon.** Glaucus' grandfather is introduced by his gifts: “To him the gods gave beauty and lovely manhood” (κάλλος τε καὶ ἠνορέην ἐρατεινήν, 6.156). The next lines show what the beauty costs. Anteia, the wife of Proetus, “was mad with longing to lie with him in secret love”, and when he refused her she accused him to her husband, who sent him to Lycia with signs meant to kill him (6.160–170; see [[anteia]] and [[teras]]). Elsewhere in the Iliad the price of beauty is paid by others, by the armies that fight for Helen and the city that shelters Paris. Here it falls on the man himself, and he survives it by the manhood that came with it: he kills the Chimaera, fights the Solymi, kills the Amazons and the men of the ambush, until the king knows him for “the noble offspring of a god” (6.179–191; see [[bellerophon-in-lycia]]).

**Paris.** Three times in the book Paris is “godlike Alexander” (Ἀλέξανδρος θεοειδής, 6.290, 6.332, 6.517). Hector finds him in his chamber “busy with his beautiful armor” (6.321), and at the end of the book he runs down from Pergamus like a stalled horse that has broken loose and, “trusting in his splendor” (ἀγλαΐηφι πεποιθώς), races to the pasture; Paris comes “gleaming in his armor like the shining sun, laughing aloud” (6.506–514; see [[the-stallion]] and [[like-the-sun]]). The splendor is his to trust, and the simile does not judge it. Hector does, a moment later, with praise and blame together: Paris is valiant, but holds back of his own will (6.521–523; see [[praise-and-blame]] and [[paris]]).

**Beautiful things.** Hecuba chooses for Athena the robe that “was most beautiful in its embroidery and the largest, and it shone like a star” (6.294–295), and Athena refuses the prayer that goes with it (6.311; see [[the-robe-for-athena]]). Laodice, the daughter who comes to meet Hector with her mother, is “the most beautiful in form of her daughters” (6.252; see [[best-in-looks]]). And the child in the nurse's arms is “the beloved son of Hector, like a lovely star” (6.401; see [[like-a-star]] and [[astyanax]]).
