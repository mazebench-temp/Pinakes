summary: The same half-line, “for his heart held back in awe from that” (σεβάσσατο γὰρ τό γε θυμῷ), is said of two men who refrain from an act: Proetus, who will not kill Bellerophon with his own hands, and Achilles, who kills Eetion but will not strip him.
grc: [σεβάσσατο]
en: [held back in awe]
passages:
  - 6.166-170 | Proetus sends Bellerophon to Lycia
  - 6.414-420 | Achilles and Eetion
---

The verb σεβάζομαι, “shrink from in awe”, comes only twice in Homer, both times in Book 6 and both times in the same words: σεβάσσατο γὰρ τό γε θυμῷ, “for his heart held back in awe from that”. In each case a man stops short of an act, and the poem does not say what he feared.

**Proetus.** When Anteia accuses Bellerophon, “anger seized the lord at what he heard; he shrank from killing him, for his heart held back in awe from that, but he sent him to Lycia, and gave him grim signs” (6.166–168). Proetus' awe does not save Bellerophon; it only moves the killing to another man's hands, the Lycian king's. In Apollodorus Bellerophon had come to Proetus to be purified of a killing (2.3.1), which would make him a suppliant under Proetus' protection; the Iliad says nothing of this (see [[xenia]], [[bellerophon]] and [[proetus]]).

**Achilles.** Andromache tells how Achilles sacked Thebe: “he killed Eetion, but he did not strip him, for his heart held back in awe from that, but he burned him with his intricate armor and heaped a mound over him” (6.416–419). Here the awe leads to honor. To strip a dead enemy of his armor is the victor's right, and in this book Euryalus strips his victims and Nestor tells the Achaeans to strip the dead later (6.28, 6.67–71). Achilles gives up the armor and gives Eetion a funeral, and the mountain nymphs plant elms around the mound (6.419–420). The Achilles of Andromache's story is a man who could be moved; the poem will show him again when he gives Hector's body back to Priam (24.582–590). See [[eetion]], [[sack-of-thebe]] and [[stripping-the-dead]].

Two men, then, feel the same awe. The one turns it into a deadlier plan, the other into a burial.
