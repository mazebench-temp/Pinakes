summary: Book 6 opens with a run of deaths, most in a line or two, some with a life attached, and a suppliant killed in spite of his ransom. In Troy the talk is of Hector's death, foreseen by his wife, accepted by himself, and mourned by his household while he still lives.

**The last of Diomedes' day.** With the gods gone, the Achaean leaders kill one after another (6.5–36; see [[androktasia]]). The deaths are told in the poem's short forms. Acamas is struck on the ridge of his helmet, the point goes into his forehead and through the bone, “and darkness covered his eyes” (6.9–11; see [[darkness-covered-his-eyes]]). Axylus and his charioteer die together: “the two of them went down beneath the earth” (6.19). Euryalus “loosened the strength and the shining limbs” of Bucolion's twin sons “and stripped the armor from their shoulders” (6.27–28; see [[menos]]). Phylacus is killed “as he fled” (6.35–36). Three of the victims are given a life: Axylus, the host who welcomed every traveler (6.12–19; see [[axylus]] and [[xenia]]); the twins, born to a Naiad nymph by the eldest son of Laomedon as he tended his sheep (6.21–26; see [[bucolion]]); and Elatus, who lived beside the Satnioeis in steep Pedasus (6.34–35).

**A death refused and given.** Adrastus is taken alive, and for a moment the book shows the other end of a battle, a captive worth a ransom. Agamemnon will have none of it. He stabs the suppliant in the flank, sets his heel on his chest and pulls out the spear (6.63–65; see [[apoina]] and [[adrastus-the-trojan]]). Nestor then tells the army not to stop for the spoils: kill now, “and afterward, at your ease, you can strip the dead bodies all over the plain” (6.67–71; see [[stripping-the-dead]]).

**Honor and slaughter.** Andromache's story sets an honored death beside a massacre. Achilles killed her father but did not strip him, and burned him in his armor and heaped a mound over him, and the mountain nymphs planted elms around it; her seven brothers “all went down on a single day into the house of Hades”, killed among their cattle and sheep (6.414–424; see [[sack-of-thebe]] and [[sebas]]).

**Hector's death.** Hector tells Helen that he does not know whether he will come back, “or if the gods will now bring me down at the hands of the Achaeans” (6.367–368). Andromache tells him that the Achaeans will soon kill him (6.409–410). He answers that no man escapes his fate, and that none will send him to Hades beyond his portion (6.486–489; see [[no-man-escapes-his-fate]]).
