---
summary: Andromache calls Hector “my husband in his prime,” and she goes home shedding “big tears”: the two senses of θαλερός, blooming and welling.
grc: [θαλερὸς]
en: [in his prime]
---

The two uses in the scene of Hector and Andromache are the word's two senses. Andromache ends the list of what Hector is to her with “and you are my husband in his prime” (θαλερὸς παρακοίτης, 6.430; [[husbands-and-wives]], [[queenly]]). In Book 3 Hector used the feminine of Helen, the “flourishing wife” Paris had taken from another man (3.53). At the parting Andromache goes home, “turning back again and again, shedding big tears” (θαλερὸν κατὰ δάκρυ χέουσα, 6.496; [[tears]], [[andromache]]), the phrase of the tear that fell from Thersites' eye (2.266). The bloom of the husband and the swelling tears of the wife stand seventy lines apart.
