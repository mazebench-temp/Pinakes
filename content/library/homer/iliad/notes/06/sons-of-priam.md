**The fifty chambers.** The sons of Priam live in their father's house with their wives, in fifty chambers of polished stone “built close to one another”, and across the courtyard are twelve for his daughters and their husbands (6.244–250; see [[priams-palace]] and [[in-laws]]). Fifty is the number of his sons. In the last book Priam tells Achilles that he had fifty when the Achaeans came, nineteen of them from one womb, and that Ares has loosened the knees of most of them (24.495–498). Hector and Paris have houses of their own nearby (6.313–317, 6.370). When Hector does not find Andromache at home, he asks whether she has gone to his sisters or to his brothers' wives (6.378), the women of these chambers.

**Brothers.** Helenus, “son of Priam” (Πριαμίδης, 6.76), sends Hector to “your mother and mine” (6.87; see [[helenus]] and [[son-of-priam]]), and Hector “did not disobey his brother” (6.102). To Hecuba Hector calls Paris a bane “to the Trojans and to great-hearted Priam and his sons” (6.283). At the end of the book “Paris, son of Priam” catches up with Hector, his brother (6.512–515; see [[paris]]). When Hector foresees the fall of Troy, he names his brothers with the Trojans, Hecuba and Priam, “who, many and brave, may fall in the dust at the hands of hostile men”, and says that none of their sorrows troubles him as much as Andromache's (6.450–455; see [[hector]]).
