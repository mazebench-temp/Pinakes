**The broken chariot.** Adrastus' team bolts in the rout: “his horses, bolting in terror across the plain, caught in a tamarisk branch and broke the curved chariot at the end of the pole” (6.38–40). μυρίκινος, “of tamarisk”, occurs only here; tamarisks grow on the plain by the rivers, and Odysseus hangs Dolon's spoils on one, and Achilles leans his spear against them (10.466–467, 21.17–18). The pole (ῥυμός) snaps ἐν πρώτῳ ῥυμῷ, at its front end, where the yoke was bound on (5.729–730), and the horses run on by themselves toward the city. Adrastus “tumbled out of the chariot beside the wheel, face down in the dust” (6.42–43), and Menelaus is standing over him (see [[supplication]] and [[adrastus-the-trojan]]). The same wreck happens to many Trojan chariots in Patroclus' rout, when their horses break them at the front of the pole in the ditch and leave them behind (16.370–371). In Book 11 the sons of Antimachus lose their reins, their horses panic, and they too are caught and killed (11.126–147; see [[supplication-type-scene]]).

**The charioteer.** Axylus dies with his attendant Calesius, “who at that time drove his horses as his charioteer” (6.18–19). ὑφηνίοχος, “under-driver”, the man who drives for a master (ὑπό and ἡνίοχος), occurs only here (see [[calesius]] and [[axylus]]).

**Leaping down.** Hector “leapt from his chariot to the ground in his armor” to rally the Trojans (6.103), in the verse that brought Menelaus down to meet Paris and Hector down to answer Sarpedon (3.29, 5.494; see [[exhortation]]). Glaucus and Diomedes, having agreed to be friends, “leapt down from their chariots” (καθʼ ἵππων ἀΐξαντε, 6.232) to take each other's hands. ἵπποι, “horses”, stands for the team and the car together (see [[horses]] and [[guest-gifts]]).

At 6.354 δίφρος is not a chariot but the seat Helen offers Hector (see [[stool]]).
