---
summary: Plato quotes Book 6 twice. In the Symposium Socrates refuses to trade “gold for bronze” with Alcibiades, and in the Cratylus he asks which of the two names of Hector's son, Astyanax or Scamandrius, Homer thought the more correct.
---

- Beauty for beauty, “gold for bronze” (*Symposium* 218e–219a): [[gold-for-bronze]].
- The names of Astyanax, and Hector as “the holder” (*Cratylus* 392b–393b): [[plato-astyanax]].
- **The robe.** In the *Euthyphro* Socrates asks whether Euthyphro believes the stories of war among the gods that the poets tell and the painters depict, and that are embroidered on the robe carried up to the Acropolis at the Great Panathenaea (*Euthyphro* 6b–c). That Athenian robe is often compared with the Trojan women's gift to Athena: [[the-robe-and-the-panathenaea]].
