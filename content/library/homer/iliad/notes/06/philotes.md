**Love and bed.** The obituary of Aesepus and Pedasus goes back to their begetting. Bucolion, the eldest son of Laomedon, “while he was tending his sheep he lay with her in love and bed” (μίγη φιλότητι καὶ εὐνῇ), and the Naiad nymph Abarbarea bore him twin sons (6.25–26). The half-line is the formula of love, used of Paris and Helen on the island of Cranae (3.445) and, in the Odyssey, of Demeter and Iasion in a thrice-plowed field and of Helen with the stranger from Troy (*Odyssey* 5.125–127, 23.218–219). A herdsman prince and a nymph by the flocks belong to a familiar pattern: Anchises tended his cattle when Aphrodite bore him Aeneas (5.313; see [[aphrodite-and-anchises]] and [[bucolion]]).

**Secret love.** Anteia, the wife of Proetus, “was mad with longing to lie with him in secret love” (κρυπταδίῃ φιλότητι μιγήμεναι, 6.160–161). Bellerophon refused her, and she turned the words round. She told her husband, “Either die, Proetus, or kill Bellerophon, who wanted to lie with me in love against my will” (φιλότητι μιγήμεναι οὐκ ἐθελούσῃ, 6.164–165). The same phrase names her desire and her charge, with the parts reversed. See [[anteia]], [[proetus]] and [[husbands-and-wives]].
