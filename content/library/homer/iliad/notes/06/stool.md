“But come now, come in and sit on this chair” (ἀλλʼ ἄγε νῦν εἴσελθε καὶ ἕζεο τῷδʼ ἐπὶ δίφρῳ, 6.354). The seat is the one a host offers a visitor, and in this house in Book 3 Aphrodite set a δίφρος for Helen herself, facing Paris (3.424–425; see [[stool]]). Hector refuses: “Do not ask me to sit, Helen, though you love me; you will not persuade me. For already my heart is eager to go and help the Trojans” (6.360–362). He has refused his mother's wine in the same way (6.264–265). Patroclus refuses Nestor's offer of a seat with the same words, “you will not persuade me”, because he has an errand from Achilles (11.646–648). The visitor in a hurry will not sit down (see [[visit-scene]], [[helen]] and [[hector]]).
