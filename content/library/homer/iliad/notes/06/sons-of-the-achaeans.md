---
summary: Hecuba names the enemy with the army's formula and a word of her own: “the sons of the Achaeans, of evil name.”
---

“Surely the sons of the Achaeans, of evil name, are wearing us down as they fight around the city” (6.255–256). The narrator's formula for the army is framed in the queen's mouth by δυσώνυμοι, “of evil name,” a word the narrator never gives them ([[of-evil-name]], [[hecuba]]). In Book 1 the sons of the Achaeans were the givers of prizes, and in Book 2 the army whose will was in question. Here they are what they are to Troy's women, the men wearing down the city.
