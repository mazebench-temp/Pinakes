---
summary: Hecuba goes down into her fragrant chamber to choose a robe for Athena, and Andromache takes her son back “to her fragrant bosom”; the second word, κηώδης, is found only here.
grc: [κηώεντα, κηώδεϊ]
---

Hecuba “went down into the fragrant chamber, where her robes were, richly embroidered, the work of women of the Sidonians” (6.288–290; [[robe]], [[sidonians]], [[inlaid]]). The same verse brings Priam down to his storeroom for Hector's ransom (Il. 24.191), and in the Odyssey Menelaus to his, where Helen chooses the finest of her robes as a gift for Telemachus in the words that describe Hecuba's choice here (Od. 15.99–108; 6.293–295).

The second word is κηώδης, “fragrant,” found only here in Homer. When Hector has prayed for his son, he puts him in his wife's arms, “and she took him to her fragrant bosom, laughing through her tears” (κηώδεϊ … κόλπῳ, 6.482–484; [[tearful]], [[andromache]], [[astyanax]]).
