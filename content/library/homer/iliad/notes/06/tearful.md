---
summary: δακρυόεις, “full of tears,” which Book 5 gave to war, describes Andromache in Book 6: led away “in tears” in Hector's foresight, and “laughing through her tears” as she takes back her son.
grc: [δακρυόεσσαν, δακρυόεν]
en: [in tears, through her tears]
---

In Book 5 the adjective described the war for which Athena armed (5.737). In Book 6 it describes the woman whom the war will make a slave. Hector foresees one of the Achaeans who “leads you away in tears, taking away your day of freedom” (δακρυόεσσαν ἄγηται, 6.455; [[bronze-armored]], [[eleutheros]], [[captive-women]]). A few lines later the tears are present, and joined with laughter: when Hector has prayed for their son he gives him back to her, “and she took him to her fragrant bosom, laughing through her tears” (δακρυόεν γελάσασα, 6.483–484; [[fragrant]]). The neuter is used as an adverb. Hector pities her and speaks the words of consolation that end the scene (6.484–493; [[tears]], [[andromache]]).
