---
summary: “Argive Helen” sits among her slave women in Paris' house, directing their work, as Hector comes in to rebuke her husband.
grc: [Ἀργείη δʼ Ἑλένη]
---

“And Argive Helen sat among her slave women and was directing her handmaids in their glorious work” (6.323–324; [[helen]], [[handmaids]], [[famous]]). It is the only time the Iliad gives her the formula in the nominative, and the first time the narrator uses it. In Books 2 to 4 “Argive Helen” was a phrase of gods and kings, the prize the Achaeans would leave behind if they fled, or take home if Menelaus won (2.161, 3.458, 4.19). Here she is at home in Paris' house, at a wife's work, as she was at her loom in Book 3 (3.125–128; [[weaving]]). The Odyssey has the same words once more, of Helen in Menelaus' house at Sparta, again giving orders to her slave women (Ἀργείη δʼ Ἑλένη δμῳῇσι κέλευσεν, Od. 4.296). She speaks to Hector soon after, and calls herself a dog (6.343–358; [[dog-face]], [[evil-scheming]]).
