summary: The family of κλέος gives Book 6 its famous things: the “far-famed” and “famous” allies of Troy, far-famed Idomeneus in Andromache's list, the “glorious work” of Helen's handmaids, and Paris' “glorious armor.”
grc: [τηλεκλειτοί, κλειτοί, ἀγακλυτὸν, περικλυτὰ ἔργα]
---

The allies have their two formulas in two speeches. Hector calls them “far-famed” (τηλεκλειτοί) as he rallies the army (6.111; [[high-hearted]]), and Diomedes counts them among the enemies he may kill, “many Trojans and famous allies” (κλειτοί, 6.227; [[trojan-allies]]). Andromache gives the compound ἀγακλυτός, “very famous,” to an enemy: the best of the Achaeans have tried the wall three times, “with the two Ajaxes and far-famed Idomeneus” (6.436; [[idomeneus]], [[the-fig-tree]]). The translation gives τηλεκλειτός and ἀγακλυτός the same English.

Two things are glorious. Helen sits among her women and directs “her handmaids in their glorious work” (περικλυτὰ ἔργα, 6.324; [[weaving]]), and Paris goes out to fight when he has put on “his glorious armor, inlaid with bronze” (κλυτὰ τεύχεα, 6.504; [[inlaid]]). The words are from the family of κλέος, “fame” ([[kleos]]), the fame that Hector says he fights to win for his father and himself (6.446).
