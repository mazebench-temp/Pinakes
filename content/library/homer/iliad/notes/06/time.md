**Honor as a share.** The Lycian king first honors Bellerophon as a guest: he “honored him with a ready heart” (τῖεν, 6.173) and entertained him for nine days (see [[xenia]]). After the tasks, when he “knew that he was the noble offspring of a god”, he kept him in Lycia, gave him his daughter, “and gave him half of all his royal honor” (τιμῆς βασιληΐδος ἥμισυ πάσης, 6.191–193). τιμή here is a portion of the kingship itself, divided as land or booty is. The Lycians add land: “a domain finer than all others, a fair one, of orchard and plowland, for him to hold” (6.194–195). Sarpedon, Bellerophon's grandson, names the same kind of domain among the honors that oblige the Lycian kings to fight in front, “a great domain by the banks of the Xanthus, a fair one, of orchard and wheat-bearing plowland” (12.313–314; see [[sarpedon]] and [[lycia]]). The elders of the Aetolians offered Meleager a domain, half vineyard and half plowland, to come out and fight (9.574–580).

**Dishonor refused.** At the end of the book Hector gives Paris his due before his blame: “no man who was fair-minded would dishonor your work in battle (ἔργον ἀτιμήσειε μάχης), since you are valiant; but you hold back of your own will and do not want to fight” (6.521–523). To dishonor a man's work is to deny it its worth; Hector refuses to do that even to Paris, and still says what the Trojans say (6.524–525; see [[aischros]] and [[praise-and-blame]]).
