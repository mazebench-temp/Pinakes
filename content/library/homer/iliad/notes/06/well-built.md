---
summary: Book 6 gives the epithet to Arisbe, the home of Axylus, and to the streets of Troy through which Hector hurries back to the gates.
---

The first obituary of the book praises a town and a host. Axylus, son of Teuthras, “lived in well-built Arisbe, wealthy in substance, and he was a friend to all men” (6.13–14; [[arisbe]], [[axylus]], [[wealthy]]). The phrase is the one that named Pherae, home of the sons of Diocles, in Book 5 (5.543–544): a good town and a rich father, before a death.

The second use is inside the city. Told that Andromache has gone to the tower, Hector “hurried from the house back the same way through the well-built streets” (ἐϋκτιμένας κατʼ ἀγυιάς, 6.390–391) to the Scaean gates, where she runs to meet him (6.392–394; [[scaean-gates]]). It is the only time in the book that the streets of Troy are described.
