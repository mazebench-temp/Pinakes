**A name and a kill.** Teucer enters the Iliad without a father, an epithet or a weapon, in a half-line that borrows its verb from Odysseus' kill in the line before (6.30–31; see [[aretaon]]). The Catalogue of Ships did not name him; there Ajax brings twelve ships from Salamis alone (2.557). Nothing here tells the listener that Teucer is an archer, or that he is the brother of the Ajax who opens the same list (6.5–11; see [[ajax]]). The poem brings in other men who will matter in the same way: Antilochus, too, first appears in the act of killing (4.457; see [[antilochus]]).

**The archer to come.** His weapon is not named, and he stands in the list between Odysseus with his bronze spear and Antilochus with his shining spear (6.30–32). His art is shown in Book 8, where he shoots from behind his brother's shield “like a child under its mother” (8.266–272), and Idomeneus will call him the best of the Achaeans at archery (13.313–314).
