---
summary: Euripides followed Andromache and her son beyond the Iliad. In the Trojan Women Astyanax is thrown from the walls and buried in Hector's shield, and in the Andromache his mother is a slave in Phthia; in the Hecuba the captive Trojan women imagine weaving Athena's robe at Athens, and two lost plays told the later story of Bellerophon.

- *Andromache*, Hector's widow in Phthia: [[euripides-andromache]].
- **The robe.** The captive women of Troy wonder whether they will be taken to the city of Pallas, to embroider her saffron robe (*Hecuba* 466–474): [[the-robe-and-the-panathenaea]].
- The lost *Stheneboea* and *Bellerophon*: [[bellerophon]].
