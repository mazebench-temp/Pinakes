summary: The horsehair crest marks the first death of Book 6, where Ajax's spear strikes the ridge of Acamas' helmet, and the scene of Hector and his son, where the nodding plume frightens the child; the book adds a third compound, ἱππιοχαίτης.

en: [plume of horsehair]

Ajax strikes Acamas “on the ridge of his horsehair-crested helmet” (κόρυθος φάλον ἱπποδασείης, 6.9; [[acamas-of-thrace]]), the verse that described the first killing of the poem, Antilochus' blow against Echepolus (4.459; [[echepolus]]).

The crest returns in the most famous scene with a helmet in the Iliad. Hector reaches for his son, and the child shrinks back crying into his nurse's bosom:

{{quote:4652039c-fef8-5563-b635-78da092fde71}}

The plume here is λόφος ἱππιοχαίτης, “of horse's hair” (ἵππειος, “of a horse,” + χαίτη, “mane, flowing hair”), a word found only here in Homer. With the helmet on the ground he kisses and dandles his son and prays for him; when he leaves he takes it up again, κόρυθʼ … ἵππουριν, “his helmet with its horsehair crest” (6.472–473, 6.494–495; [[of-the-gleaming-helmet]], [[helmet]], [[astyanax]]). In Book 3 the plume “nodded terribly” on Paris' helmet as he armed for the duel (3.337); here the same terror is a child's, who does not know his father in his armor.
