**The vow.** Helenus prescribes it, Hector repeats it, and Theano makes it in the temple, each time in the same two verses: δυοκαίδεκα βοῦς ἐνὶ νηῷ / ἤνις ἠκέστας ἱερευσέμεν, “to sacrifice twelve cows to her in her temple, yearlings, never touched by the goad” (6.93–94, 6.274–275; in the first person, ἱερεύσομεν, 6.308–309). The adjectives are feminine, and the victims are cows for a goddess. ἦνις is “a year old” and ἠκέστας “ungoaded”, following the ancient glosses. ἠκέστας occurs only in these three lines, and its sense is uncertain. The victim is young and has never worked for men. ἦνις occurs elsewhere only in vows to Athena. Diomedes, praying before the night raid, vows her “a yearling heifer, broad of brow, unbroken, which no man has yet led under the yoke”, with gold on its horns (10.292–294), and Nestor vows her the same in the same verses at Pylos (Od. 3.382–384).

The sacrifice is promised, not made. It is to be paid “if she will pity the city and the wives of the Trojans and their little children” (6.94–95, 6.309–310), and the goddess refuses (6.311; see [[prayer-type-scene]] and [[reciprocity-with-the-gods]]).

**Burnt offerings.** Hector adds one thing to Helenus' instructions: Hecuba is to go to the temple “with burnt offerings” (σὺν θυέεσσιν, 6.270). θύεα are things burned for a god, from θύω, “burn in offering”. The word comes again in Phoenix' speech in Book 9, where men turn the gods themselves with burnt offerings and gentle prayers, libation and savor (9.499–500; see [[libation]]). Athena is not turned.

**For a guest.** “Nine days he entertained him and sacrificed nine oxen” (6.174). ἱερεύω is to kill a victim, and the victims killed for the gods fed the feast. The king of Lycia honors his guest with sacrifice and a meal for nine days before he asks to see the sign he carries (see [[xenia]], [[feast]] and [[writing]]).
