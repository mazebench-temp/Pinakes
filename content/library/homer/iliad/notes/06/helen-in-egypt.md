---
summary: Herodotus' proof that Homer knew of Paris' wanderings is in Book 6: the robes made by Sidonian women whom Paris brought from Sidon “on that voyage on which he brought back Helen”.
passages:
  - 6.288-292 | The robes from Sidon
---

{{quote:ee8d70a6-1700-5b5d-9f29-89bc6c3e804f}}

Herodotus quotes these lines, and two passages of the Odyssey on Helen's drugs from Egypt and Menelaus' delay there, to show that Homer knew the story he heard in Egypt and set it aside (2.116). Sidon lay in Phoenicia, and Phoenicia borders on Egypt, he argues, so a voyage by Sidon was a voyage by way of Egypt. In the Iliad the robes are the finest thing in Hecuba's storeroom, and the one she chooses is offered to Athena, who refuses the prayer that goes with it (6.288–311; see [[sidon]], [[sidonians]] and [[the-robe-for-athena]]).
