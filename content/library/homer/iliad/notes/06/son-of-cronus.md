---
summary: Zeus is the son of Cronus when he blinds Lycurgus, when he takes away Glaucus' wits in the exchange of armor, and when Hector will not pray to him with bloody hands.
---

In Diomedes' story “the son of Cronus made him blind” (Κρόνου πάϊς, 6.139; [[lycurgus]], [[who-live-at-ease]]). The narrator's comment on the exchange of armor names Zeus the same way: “But then Zeus, son of Cronus, took away Glaucus' wits, for he exchanged armor with Diomedes, son of Tydeus, gold for bronze” (6.234–236; [[glaucus-and-diomedes]]). The phrase φρένας ἐξέλετο Ζεύς is the one Agamemnon uses of his own delusion in Book 19 (Il. 19.137); whether it is a joke at Glaucus' expense, a sober verdict on a bad bargain, or a way of pointing to his generosity is debated ([[ate]]).

The dative Κρονίωνι comes in Hector's refusal of the libation: “nor may a man in any way offer the son of Cronus, lord of the dark clouds, a prayer when he is spattered with blood and gore” (6.267–268; [[lord-of-dark-clouds]], [[hand-washing]], [[libation]]).
