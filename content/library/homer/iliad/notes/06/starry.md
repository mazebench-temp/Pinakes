summary: The Argives think that one of the immortals has come down “from the starry sky” to help the Trojans, so sharply do they rally.
---

“And the Argives gave ground and ceased from slaughter, and thought that one of the immortals had come down from the starry sky to bring help to the Trojans, so sharply did they rally” (6.107–109). In Book 5 Hera's horses flew “between earth and the starry sky” (5.769). Here the starry sky is where the Argives imagine a god coming from, and they are wrong: the gods have left the battle (6.1), and what turns it is Hector, rallying the army on Helenus' advice (6.102–106; [[immortals]], [[dread-din-of-battle]]).
