summary: Mecisteus is named in his son's patronymic, when Euryalus, “the son of Mecisteus”, kills the twin sons of Bucolion (6.28).
grc: [Μηκιστηϊάδης]
relations:
  - child: euryalus (6.28)
---

Μηκιστηϊάδης = Μηκιστεύς + the patronymic suffix -ιάδης: “the son of Mecisteus” (6.28). The Catalogue named him in a longer phrase, “son of lord Mecisteus, son of Talaus” (2.566). Here the patronymic stands alone for the son, at the head of the verse in which he strips the dead twins (6.27–28; see [[euryalus]]). The father's own deed, his victory over all the Cadmeians at the funeral games of Oedipus, is told when his son boxes in Book 23 (23.677–680).
