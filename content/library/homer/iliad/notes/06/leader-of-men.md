---
summary: Helenus gives the title to Achilles, whom the Trojans never feared as they now fear Diomedes.
grc: [ὄρχαμον ἀνδρῶν]
---

“We never feared like this even Achilles, leader of men, who they say was born of a goddess; but this man rages beyond measure, and no one can match his might” (6.99–101). It is the only time the Iliad gives Achilles this title, and it comes in a Trojan's comparison that sets Diomedes above him. Achilles is absent and known by report, “who they say was born of a goddess” ([[thetis]]); Diomedes is present and raging ([[master-of-rout]], [[spearmen]]). The Book 5 aristeia has often been read as Diomedes standing in for Achilles, and Helenus says so from the Trojan side ([[the-aristeia]], [[achilles]], [[helenus]]).
