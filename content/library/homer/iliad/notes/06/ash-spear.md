---
summary: Agamemnon sets his heel on the chest of the dead Adrastus and pulls out his ash spear.
---

“And the son of Atreus set his heel on his chest and pulled out the ash spear” (6.64–65). Bracing a foot against a corpse to draw out the spear is a recurring gesture: Ajax does it over Amphius in Book 5 (5.620–621), and the half-verse λὰξ ἐν στήθεσι βαίνων, “setting his heel on his chest,” returns in later books (Il. 13.618, 16.503; [[stripping-the-dead]]). In Book 5 the ash spear was left in Sarpedon's wound until a friend drew it out (5.694–698); here it is pulled from a suppliant's body by the king who refused his ransom. Nestor's order follows at once: kill now, strip the dead later (6.66–71; [[adrastus-the-trojan]], [[spear]]).
