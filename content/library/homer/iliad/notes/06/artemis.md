---
summary: Artemis kills two women in the stories of Book 6. In Glaucus' genealogy she kills Laodameia, the mother of Sarpedon, in anger; in Andromache's plea she strikes down Andromache's mother in her father's halls.
passages:
  - 6.200-205 | The ruin of Bellerophon's house
  - 6.425-428 | The death of Andromache's mother
---

**Laodameia.** Glaucus ends the story of Bellerophon with the ruin of his house. When Bellerophon too was hated by all the gods, Ares killed his son Isander in battle, “and Artemis of the golden reins killed his daughter in her anger” (6.200–205; see [[laodameia]], [[isander]] and [[bellerophon]]). The poem does not say why she was angry (χολωσαμένη). χρυσήνιος, “of the golden reins”, is given to Artemis only here; the Odyssey gives it to Ares (Od. 8.285; see [[of-the-golden-reins]]). The son dies by the god of war and the daughter by the goddess of women's deaths, as Apollo kills Niobe's six sons and Artemis her six daughters (24.603–606; see [[ares]] and [[apollo]]).

**Andromache's mother.** Andromache counts her dead: her father and her seven brothers, killed by Achilles, and last her mother. Achilles brought her to the Achaean camp with the rest of the spoil and set her free for a boundless ransom, “and in her father's halls Artemis of the showering arrows struck her down” (6.425–428; see [[andromache]], [[sack-of-thebe]] and [[of-the-showering-arrows]]). She came through capture and ransom and died at home, by the arrows that bring women a sudden death. The half-line βάλʼ Ἄρτεμις ἰοχέαιρα, “Artemis of the showering arrows struck”, returns in the Odyssey for the death at sea of the Phoenician woman who carried off the child Eumaeus (Od. 15.477–479). When Odysseus meets his mother among the dead, he asks her whether a long sickness killed her, or Artemis with her gentle arrows (Od. 11.171–173).

In both stories Artemis' arrow ends a list of losses told by one of the family: Glaucus tells of his father's sister, Andromache of her mother.
