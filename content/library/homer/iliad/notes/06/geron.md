The feminine of γεραιός, “old”, names a group in Book 6: αἱ γεραιαί, the old women of Troy. Helenus tells Hector that his mother is to go “gathering the old women” (ξυνάγουσα γεραιάς) to Athena's temple on the citadel (6.87–88), and Hector passes the instruction on to her (ἀολλίσσασα γεραιάς, 6.270). Hecuba sends her handmaids, “and they gathered the old women throughout the city” (6.287), and when she sets out with the robe, “many old women hurried after her” (6.296). It is the senior women of the city who make the offering, and the priestess Theano speaks for them (6.297–311; see [[the-robe-for-athena]], [[trojan-women]] and [[hecuba]]).

The old men have their own part. Hector tells the army that he is going into Ilios to tell “the elders who sit in council” (γέρουσιν … βουλευτῇσι) and the wives to pray to the gods and promise them hecatombs (6.113–115; see [[trojan-elders]]). The city's old men counsel and its old women pray, while those of fighting age are on the plain (see [[the-city-and-the-household]]).
