---
summary: Hector foresees “one of the bronze-armored Achaeans” leading Andromache away into slavery.
---

“But it is not so much the sorrow of the Trojans hereafter that troubles me … as yours, when one of the bronze-armored Achaeans leads you away in tears, taking away your day of freedom” (6.450–455; [[eleutheros]], [[captive-women]], [[tearful]]). The army's formula names the man who will own her. In Book 3 the bronze-armored Achaeans and the horse-taming Trojans were the two sides of the war Helen wove into her web (3.126–128); here the formula is the victor, and the verse that follows is the captive's ([[andromache]], [[slavery-and-freedom]]).
