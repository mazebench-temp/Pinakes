---
summary: Paris does not linger “in his lofty house”; the adjective is ὑψηλός, the plain word for height, which the translation renders “lofty.”
grc: [ὑψηλοῖσι δόμοισιν]
en: [lofty]
---

“Nor did Paris linger long in his lofty house, but when he had put on his glorious armor, inlaid with bronze, he hurried through the city” (ἐν ὑψηλοῖσι δόμοισιν, 6.503–505; [[paris]], [[house]], [[inlaid]]). ὑψηλός (“high, lofty”) is the common adjective of which ὑψόροφος and ὑψερεφής are the compounds. It is the house that Paris built with the best builders of Troy (6.313–317; [[deep-soiled]]), and he leaves it at last. In Book 22 the same adjective describes Hector's house, where Andromache sits weaving at the back of the lofty hall, not yet knowing that her husband is dead (μυχῷ δόμου ὑψηλοῖο, Il. 22.440; [[andromache]]).
