---
summary: In the supplication of Adrastus the patronymic names both brothers in turn, Menelaus who would spare him and Agamemnon who kills him; Andromache names them together among the best of the Achaeans who have tried the wall.
---

Adrastus falls from his chariot at the feet of the younger brother: “beside him stood Menelaus, son of Atreus, holding his long-shadowed spear” (6.43–44; [[long-shadowed]]). He opens his plea with the patronymic, “Take me alive, son of Atreus, and accept a worthy ransom” (6.46), the verse the sons of Antimachus will speak to Agamemnon in Book 11 (Il. 11.131; [[boundless-ransom]], [[supplication]]). Twenty lines later the patronymic has passed to the elder: Agamemnon stabs Adrastus, “and the son of Atreus set his heel on his chest and pulled out the ash spear” (6.64–65; [[ash-spear]], [[adrastus-the-trojan]]). The same name covers the brother who would spare and the brother who kills.

The plural names both in Andromache's advice: the best of the Achaeans have tried the wall by the fig tree three times, “with the two Ajaxes and far-famed Idomeneus and with the sons of Atreus and the valiant son of Tydeus” (6.435–437; [[the-fig-tree]], [[atreidae]]).
