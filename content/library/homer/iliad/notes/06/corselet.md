“He found him in his chamber busy with his beautiful armor, his shield and his corselet, and handling his curved bow” (τὸν δʼ εὗρʼ ἐν θαλάμῳ περικαλλέα τεύχεʼ ἕποντα, 6.321–322). ἕπω, “be busy with, see to”, is a verb of handling and tending; what Paris is doing with the armor, polishing it, checking it, or only turning it over, the verse does not say. The scene answers Hector's rebuke: the armor is in the house and not on the field (6.326–331; see [[paris]] and [[visit-scene]]).

In Book 3 Paris armed in the corselet of his brother Lycaon, “and fitted it to himself” (3.332–333). Whether this is the same one Book 6 does not say. He promises to put on “my armor of war” and follow Hector (6.340), and at the end of the book he does, in one line: “when he had put on his glorious armor, inlaid with bronze” (6.504). The corselet that had its own verse in his arming in Book 3 is not named (see [[arming-scene]] and [[corselet]]).
