---
summary: Book 6 gives the epithet of praise to a Trojan whom Teucer kills, to Anteia and Oeneus in the stories of Bellerophon, to Athena among goddesses, to Achilles twice in Andromache's account of her family's deaths, and to Hector.
---

As in Book 5, δῖος praises men and women on both sides and says little about any one of them. Teucer, in his first appearance in the poem, “slew brilliant Aretaon” (6.31; [[teucer]], [[aretaon]]). In Glaucus' story “brilliant Anteia,” the wife of Proetus, is mad with longing for Bellerophon and lies about him to her husband (6.160–165; [[anteia]]), and in Diomedes' answer “brilliant Oeneus” entertains Bellerophon for twenty days (6.216–217; [[oeneus]]). Athena is δῖα θεάων in the Trojan women's prayer (6.305; [[brilliant-among-goddesses]]).

The epithet of Achilles comes in the speech of the woman he orphaned. “For brilliant Achilles killed my father” (6.414), and “brilliant swift-footed Achilles killed them all,” her seven brothers (6.423; [[podarkes]], [[sack-of-thebe]]). Andromache gives the killer his formula, the same praise the narrator gives him in the proem (1.7). The last use is Hector's: Paris “came upon brilliant Hector, his brother,” as he turned from Andromache (6.515–516; [[hector]]).
