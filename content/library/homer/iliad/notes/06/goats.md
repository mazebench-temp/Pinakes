grc: [χίμαιρα]
en: [she-goat]

The Chimaera that Bellerophon killed was πρόσθε λέων, ὄπιθεν δὲ δράκων, μέσση δὲ χίμαιρα, “a lion in front, a serpent behind, and in the middle a she-goat” (6.181; see [[chimaera]], [[lion]], [[serpent]] and [[bellerophon-in-lycia]]). χίμαιρα is a common noun, a young she-goat; the masculine χίμαρος is a young he-goat, and both are usually connected with χεῖμα, “winter”, as a beast that has lived through one winter. The monster's name, Χίμαιρα (6.179), is the same word. The line plays on it: the Chimaera is a χίμαιρα in her middle.

The *Theogony* gives her three heads, the second a she-goat's (ἡ δὲ χιμαίρης, *Theogony* 321–322), and [[apollodorus|Apollodorus]] places the goat's head in the middle, breathing fire (*Library* 2.3.1). The bronze Chimaera from Arezzo, an Etruscan work of about 400 BC now in Florence, has a goat's head rising from the back of the lion.

The goats of Books 1–4 were victims, working flocks, and the wild goat of the hunt. Book 6 has only this one, and it is part of a monster.
