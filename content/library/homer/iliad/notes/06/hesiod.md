---
summary: Hesiod's Chimaera contains two lines of Book 6 word for word, and the next line gives Bellerophon the winged horse Pegasus as his partner in killing her.
---

**The Chimaera.** In the *Theogony* the Chimaera breathes “irresistible fire” (ἀμαιμάκετον πῦρ; in Homer the word is her own epithet, 6.179, see [[irresistible]]) and has three heads, of a fierce-eyed lion, of a goat and of a serpent (319–322). The next two lines are Book 6's: “a lion in front, a serpent behind, and in the middle a she-goat, breathing out the terrible might of blazing fire” (323–324 = 6.181–182). Many editors bracket them as borrowed from the Iliad. The line after them names her killers, “Pegasus and noble Bellerophon” (325). Homer has no Pegasus: his Bellerophon kills her “obeying the portents of the gods” (6.183). See [[chimaera]] and [[bellerophon]].
