---
summary: Two rich men's wealth fails to save them in the opening killings of Book 6: Axylus, “wealthy in substance” and a friend to all, and Adrastus, whose wealthy father would have paid a ransom that is refused.
grc: [ἀφνειοῦ]
---

The formula ἀφνειὸς βιότοιο, “wealthy in substance,” which in Book 5 named the father of the sons of Diocles (5.544), here names the victim himself. Axylus lived in well-built Arisbe, wealthy, and a friend to all men, keeping a house by the road where he welcomed everyone; “but not one of them was there then to keep grim destruction from him” (6.13–17; [[axylus]], [[xenia]], [[grim]]).

The second rich man is a father far away. Adrastus, clasping Menelaus' knees, promises that “many treasures lie stored in the house of my wealthy father,” and a boundless ransom (6.46–50; [[adrastus-the-trojan]], [[worked-with-much-toil]], [[boundless-ransom]]). Agamemnon kills him (6.53–65). Wealth is again part of the obituary, what the dead man had and what could not buy his life ([[the-war-in-small]]).
