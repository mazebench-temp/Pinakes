Odysseus is one of the ten Achaean leaders who each kill a man in the list that opens the book (6.5–36; see [[androktasia]]). He has no epithet here, only the verb ἐξενάριξεν, “slew and stripped”, and the weapon, ἔγχεϊ χαλκείῳ, “with his bronze spear” (see [[pidytes]] and [[percote]]). The half-line after his kill brings Teucer into the poem (6.31; see [[teucer]]).

The verb from which the Odyssey derives his name comes later in the book without him. The gods “were angry” (ὀδύσαντο) with Lycurgus, and Zeus blinded him (6.138–139; see [[lycurgus]]). In the Odyssey Autolycus names his grandson Odysseus because he himself has come “angered” (ὀδυσσάμενος) with many (Od. 19.406–409), and the same verb is used of the gods' anger against Odysseus (Od. 1.62, 5.423). The entry's pattern takes only the capitalized name, so that the verb is not counted as a mention of him.
