---
summary: The beautiful things of Book 6 are the house of Priam, which Hector reaches at the start of his visit, and the armor Paris is handling in his chamber.
---

Hector's visit to the city is set among fine things. “But when he came to the beautiful house of Priam, built with polished colonnades” (δόμον περικαλλέα, 6.242–243; [[priams-palace]], [[polished]]): the description of its chambers follows, fifty for the sons and twelve for the daughters (6.244–250). In the house of Alexander, which he built with the best craftsmen of Troy, Hector “found him in his chamber busy with his beautiful armor, his shield and his corselet, and handling his curved bow” (6.321–322; [[paris]], [[curved-bow]]). The beautiful armor is being polished instead of worn, and Hector's rebuke follows (6.325–331; [[strange-one]]).
