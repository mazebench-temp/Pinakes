**King of the Cilicians.** Book 1 gave only his name and his city, “Thebe, the sacred city of Eetion” (1.366). Book 6 gives him a people, a mountain and a daughter. Andromache is “the daughter of great-hearted Eetion, Eetion who lived beneath wooded Placus, in Thebe under Placus, lord over the Cilician men” (6.395–397; see [[andromache]], [[placus]], [[thebe]] and [[cilicians]]). His name ends one line and begins the next, Ἠετίωνος / Ἠετίων (see [[polyptoton]]). μεγαλήτωρ, “great-hearted”, is his epithet here and again when Hector speaks to his horses of Andromache (8.187; see [[great-hearted]]).

**His death.**

{{quote:9d599b86-619c-5f1e-b62b-9c8441071a6c}}

Achilles killed him but did not strip him, “for his heart held back in awe from that” (σεβάσσατο γὰρ τό γε θυμῷ, 6.417). The same half-line is said earlier in the book of Proetus, who would not kill Bellerophon with his own hand (6.167; see [[sebas]]). He burned him “with his intricate armor” and heaped a mound over him (6.418–419; see [[cremation]] and [[tomb]]), the burial Elpenor asks of Odysseus in the Odyssey (Od. 11.74–75, 12.13–15). The book began with Nestor's order to kill now and strip the dead later (6.67–71; see [[stripping-the-dead]]); here the man who leaves an enemy king his armor is Achilles. The mountain nymphs, “daughters of aegis-bearing Zeus”, planted elms around the mound (6.419–420; see [[nymphs]]).

**His family.** His seven sons “all went down on a single day into the house of Hades”, killed by Achilles among their cattle and sheep (6.421–424; see [[cattle]] and [[sheep]]). His wife, “who was queen beneath wooded Placus”, was brought to the camp with the rest of the spoil and set free for a boundless ransom, and Artemis struck her down “in her father's halls” (6.425–428; see [[boundless-ransom]] and [[artemis]]). Andromache alone is left, and she tells the story so that Hector will see that he is all she has (6.429–430; see [[sack-of-thebe]]).
