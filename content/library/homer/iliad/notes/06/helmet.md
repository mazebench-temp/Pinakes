**Struck on the ridge.** The book's first kill is told in three verses from the first kill of the poem: Ajax strikes Acamas “on the ridge of his horsehair-crested helmet”, the point fixes in his forehead and goes into the bone, and darkness covers his eyes (6.9–11 ~ 4.459–461; see [[acamas-of-thrace]] and [[darkness-covered-his-eyes]]). φάλος, the ridge, is the part of the helmet whose shape is least certain (see [[helmet]]).

**The child and the crest.** Hector's standing epithet is κορυθαίολος, “of the gleaming helmet”, and Book 6 gives it to him seven times (6.116, 6.263, 6.342, 6.359, 6.369, 6.440, 6.520; see [[of-the-gleaming-helmet]]). At the gates the helmet stands between him and his son:

{{quote:4652039c-fef8-5563-b635-78da092fde71}}

ἱππιοχαίτης, “of horsehair”, occurs only here. The crest “nodding terribly” (δεινὸν … νεύοντα) is the crest of an arming scene, made to frighten the enemy, as Paris' plume “nodded terribly” above his helmet when he armed for the duel (3.337; see [[arming-scene]]). It frightens the child, and the parents laugh (see [[astyanax]]). Hector lays it on the ground, “all gleaming” (παμφανόωσαν), kisses and dandles his son, and prays for him bareheaded (6.472–481; see [[prayer-type-scene]]).

**On again.** When he has sent Andromache home, “shining Hector took up his helmet with its horsehair crest” (κόρυθʼ … ἵππουριν, 6.494–495). ἵππουρις is the word of Paris' helmet in his arming (3.337). The helmet goes back on, and the war with it (see [[hector]]).
