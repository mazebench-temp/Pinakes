summary: Herodotus quotes the lines on Hecuba's Sidonian robes as proof that Homer knew of Paris' wanderings with Helen, and says that they stand “in the Aristeia of Diomedes”. He also places Bellerophon's Aleian plain in Cilicia, and tells of a folded tablet used to carry a secret message.
passages:
  - 6.288-292 | The robes from Sidon
---

- “In the Aristeia of Diomedes” (*Histories* 2.116, quoting 6.289–292): [[title-of-book-5]] and [[helen-in-egypt]].
- **The Aleian plain.** The Persian generals Datis and Artaphernes gathered their army “in Cilicia, on the Aleian plain”, before sailing against Eretria and Athens (6.95). Bellerophon wandered there alone, hated by the gods (6.200–202; see [[aleian-plain]]).
- **A folded tablet.** Demaratus warned Sparta of Xerxes' plans in writing hidden under the wax of a folded tablet (7.239): [[bellerophons-letter]].
