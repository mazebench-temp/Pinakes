---
summary: The epithet names the Achaeans as a whole, “dear to Ares,” as they drive the Trojans back toward the city until Helenus intervenes.
grc: [ἀρηϊφίλων]
---

With Ares gone from the field, the epithet goes to the army that the god fought against in Book 5: “Then once more the Trojans, before the Achaeans dear to Ares, would have gone up into Ilios, beaten by their own cowardice, had there not stood beside Aeneas and Hector, and spoken to them, Helenus, son of Priam” (6.73–76; [[would-have-had-not]], [[helenus]]). In Books 3 to 5 the epithet belonged to Menelaus; here the whole army is favored by war. The phrase ἀρηϊφίλων ὑπʼ Ἀχαιῶν recurs in later books of the Trojans' flight (for example Il. 16.303; [[achaeans]]).
