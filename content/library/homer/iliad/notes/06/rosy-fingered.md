---
summary: Dawn is “rosy-fingered” on the tenth day of Bellerophon's stay in Lycia, when his host at last asks to see the message he has brought.
---

“Nine days he entertained him and sacrificed nine oxen. But when the tenth rosy-fingered Dawn appeared, then he questioned him and asked to see the sign that he was bringing from his son-in-law Proetus” (6.174–177; [[bellerophon]], [[writing]]). The host asks his guest's business only after the entertainment, as the rules of hospitality require ([[xenia]]). The dawn that brings the question brings the message meant to kill him, and the labors follow (6.178–186). The half-verse differs from the dawn line of Book 1 (1.477), but the image is the same ([[eos]], [[dawn-line]]).
