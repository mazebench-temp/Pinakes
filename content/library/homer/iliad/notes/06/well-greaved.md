---
summary: The last verse of Book 6 hopes for the day when the Trojans will have “driven the well-greaved Achaeans out of Troy.”
---

Hector ends the book with a hope: they will make things right later, “if ever Zeus grants us, for the everlasting gods of heaven, to set up the mixing bowl of freedom in our halls, when we have driven the well-greaved Achaeans out of Troy” (6.526–529; [[mixing-bowl]], [[eleutheros]], [[gods-who-live-forever]]). In Book 1 Chryses wished the well-greaved Achaeans the sack of Priam's city (1.17–19); the book that began with Andromache's slavery foreseen ends with the Trojans' wish to be free of the Achaeans ([[hector]], [[slavery-and-freedom]]).
