---
summary: The epithet of Zeus names him once in Book 6, as the father of the mountain nymphs who plant elms around Eetion's tomb.
---

“By the mountain nymphs, daughters of aegis-bearing Zeus” (6.420; [[child-of-aegis-bearing-zeus]], [[nymphs]]). As in Books 2 and 5, the epithet names Zeus as a father; the Odyssey gives the nymphs the same title (Od. 6.105). The aegis itself does not appear in the book. Athena, who put it on to go into battle in Book 5 (5.738–742), is in Book 6 the goddess of a temple on the citadel, and she does not come into the fighting ([[aegis]], [[athena]]).
