---
summary: The patronymic introduces Helenus, “the best of the bird-seers,” whose advice sends Hector into the city, and names Paris as he runs down from Pergamus to rejoin his brother.
grc: [υἱὸς Πριάμοιο]
---

Two sons of Priam frame Hector's visit to the city. It begins with “Helenus, son of Priam, by far the best of the bird-seers” (Πριαμίδης Ἕλενος, 6.76; [[helenus]], [[best-of-bird-seers]]), who tells Hector to rally the army and then to go to the city and tell “your mother and mine” to take a robe to Athena (6.77–101). It ends with the simile of the stallion: “so Paris, son of Priam, came down from the height of Pergamus” (υἱὸς Πριάμοιο Πάρις, 6.512; [[the-stallion]], [[pergamus]]). Between them Hector passes through the palace where the fifty sons of Priam sleep beside their wives (6.242–246; [[sons-of-priam]], [[priams-palace]]). The brother who knows the gods' will sends him in; the brother who holds back comes out with him ([[paris]], [[hector]]).
