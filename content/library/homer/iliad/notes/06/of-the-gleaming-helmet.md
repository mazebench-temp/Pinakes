summary: The formula follows Hector through the city in seven lines, from his departure from the army to his last answer to Paris; in the one scene where the helmet itself matters, he takes it off.
---

The same verse frames Hector's visit: “So he spoke, and Hector of the gleaming helmet went away,” first from the army (6.116) and then from Helen and Paris to look for his wife (6.369). The formula of reply introduces his answers to each of the women he meets: “Then great Hector of the gleaming helmet answered her,” to his mother (6.263) and to Helen (6.359), and “spoke to her in turn” to Andromache (6.440). To Paris he gives first a silence, “So he spoke, and Hector of the gleaming helmet said nothing to him” (6.342), the silence with which he passed Sarpedon in Book 5 (5.689), and at the end of the book an answer (6.520; [[answering-him]], [[then-answered]]).

In one scene the epithet is not used, and the helmet is. The child cries at the sight of it, “afraid of the bronze and the plume of horsehair,” and Hector takes it from his head and lays it, all gleaming, on the ground (6.466–473; [[horsehair-crested]], [[gleaming-all-over]], [[helmet]], [[astyanax]]). When he goes back to the war he takes it up again (6.494–495).
