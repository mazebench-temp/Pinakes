Οἰνεὺς … δῖος, “brilliant Oeneus” (see [[brilliant]]). Diomedes does not call him his grandfather here; the descent is told in Book 14, where Oeneus is “my father's father” (14.117–118; see [[tydeus]]). The Lycian king kept Bellerophon nine days before he asked for the sign that was to kill him (6.174); Oeneus kept him twenty, and the gifts they exchanged sealed a bond between the two houses (see [[xenia]] and [[guest-gifts]]). His gift, a belt “bright with purple” (ζωστῆρα … φοίνικι φαεινόν), is given in the same words by Ajax to Hector when they part after their duel (7.305; see [[belt]] and [[purple]]). Bellerophon's, a golden two-handled cup (δέπας ἀμφικύπελλον; see [[cup]]), is still in Diomedes' house. The generation between, Tydeus, Diomedes cannot remember (6.222–223), but he has the cup.

This is one of the two stories in the Iliad in which Oeneus acts. In the other, Phoenix tells how he forgot Artemis at the harvest sacrifice and brought the boar on Calydon (9.533–549; see [[meleager]]). See [[bellerophon]] and [[diomedes]].
