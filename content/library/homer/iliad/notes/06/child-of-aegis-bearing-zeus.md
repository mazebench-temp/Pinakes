---
summary: The plural formula names the mountain nymphs who plant elms around Eetion's tomb, “daughters of aegis-bearing Zeus.”
---

“And elms were planted around it by the mountain nymphs, daughters of aegis-bearing Zeus” (κοῦραι Διὸς αἰγιόχοιο, 6.419–420; [[nymphs]], [[mountain-dwelling]]). In Book 2 the same plural named the Muses (2.491, 2.598); here it names the nymphs, whom Homer also calls daughters of Zeus in the Odyssey (Od. 6.105, 13.356). The daughters of the god honor the king whom Achilles killed and did not strip ([[eetion]], [[intricately-worked]], [[aegis-bearing]]).
