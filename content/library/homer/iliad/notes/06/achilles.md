summary: Achilles does not fight in Book 6, but he is its measure and its shadow. Helenus says that the Trojans never feared even Achilles as they fear Diomedes, and Andromache tells how Achilles killed her father and her seven brothers when he sacked Thebe.
passages:
  - 6.97-101 | Helenus compares Diomedes with Achilles
  - 6.414-428 | Andromache's family
---

**The measure.** Helenus asks for Athena's help against Diomedes, “who, I say, has become the strongest of the Achaeans. We never feared like this even Achilles, leader of men, who they say was born of a goddess; but this man rages beyond measure” (6.98–101; see [[helenus]] and [[diomedes]]). In Book 5 Hera said the opposite, that while Achilles fought the Trojans never came out beyond the Dardanian gates (5.788–791). Diomedes' great day is measured against the man who is away.

**The killer of Andromache's family.** In her plea to Hector, Andromache tells what Achilles did at Thebe (see [[sack-of-thebe]], [[thebe]] and [[andromache]]):

{{quote:9d599b86-619c-5f1e-b62b-9c8441071a6c}}

He killed her father, and “he did not strip him, for his heart held back in awe from that” (σεβάσσατο, 6.417), the word used of Proetus, who would not kill Bellerophon with his own hand (6.167; see [[sebas]]). He burned Eetion in his armor and heaped a mound over him (6.418–419; see [[eetion]]). He killed her seven brothers on a single day among their cattle and sheep (6.421–424), and her mother he brought to the camp with the rest of the spoil and freed for a boundless ransom (6.425–427; see [[apoina]]). Book 1 names Thebe as the city from which Chryseis was taken (1.366–369), and other spoils of it return later: the lyre Achilles plays in his hut (9.186–189), his trace horse Pedasus (16.152–153), and a lump of iron that Eetion used to throw, which Achilles sets as a prize (23.826–829).

**Before and after.** The Achilles of Andromache's story honors a dead enemy and takes ransom for the living. He says so himself in Book 21: before Patroclus died he was glad to spare Trojans, and took many alive and sold them (21.100–102). Andromache does not say what the audience knows, that the man who killed her father and brothers will kill her husband.
