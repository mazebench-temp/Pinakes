---
summary: Euryalus “loosened the strength and the shining limbs” of the twin sons of Bucolion (6.27), a variant of the formula with the verb ὑπολύω and the might of the living joined to the limbs.
grc: ["ὑπέλυσε μένος καὶ φαίδιμα γυῖα"]
en: [loosened the strength and the shining limbs]
passages:
  - 6.21-28 | The sons of Bucolion
---

“And of these too he loosened the strength and the shining limbs, the son of Mecisteus, and stripped the armor from their shoulders” (καὶ μὲν τῶν ὑπέλυσε μένος καὶ φαίδιμα γυῖα, 6.27–28). ὑπολύω is to loosen from beneath, as the knees give way under a man. The same verb is used of a hunter bringing down a fawn in a simile of Book 15, and of Odysseus kicking Ajax behind the knee in the wrestling (ὑπέλυσε δὲ γυῖα, 15.581, 23.726). μένος, the might that fills the living, goes with the limbs (see [[menos]]), and φαίδιμα γυῖα, “shining limbs”, are the limbs of men in their strength: when Apollo strikes Patroclus, “his shining limbs were loosened beneath him” (λύθεν δʼ ὑπὸ φαίδιμα γυῖα, 16.805; see [[loosened-his-limbs]]). The twins are young, sons of a nymph, born to a herdsman prince, and their deaths are told with their birth (6.21–26; see [[aesepus-son-of-bucolion]], [[pedasus-son-of-bucolion]] and [[androktasia]]).

The limbs come up in another way when Hector refuses his mother's wine: it would “unnerve” him (ἀπογυιώσῃς, 6.265), a verb made from γυῖα, as if the wine would take away his limbs (see [[wine]]).
