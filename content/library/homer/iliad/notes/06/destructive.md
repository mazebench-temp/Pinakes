---
summary: Hector warns Paris that the city may soon burn “with consuming fire.”
---

“Up, then, for fear the city will soon burn with consuming fire” (μὴ τάχα ἄστυ πυρὸς δηΐοιο θέρηται, 6.331). The formula πυρὸς δηΐοιο names the fire that destroys cities and ships. Hector's rebuke ends with the image of what the war is fought to prevent. In Book 2 Agamemnon prayed to throw down the hall of Priam and burn “its doors with consuming fire” (2.414–415); here the city's defender uses the same words to rouse his brother ([[paris]], [[troy]]).
