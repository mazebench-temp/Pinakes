---
summary: Andromache's seven brothers are killed among their cattle and “white sheep,” the standing epithet of the flock.
grc: [ἀργεννῇς]
---

“For brilliant swift-footed Achilles killed them all among their shambling cattle and white sheep” (βουσὶν ἐπʼ εἰλιπόδεσσι καὶ ἀργεννῇς ὀΐεσσι, 6.423–424; [[shambling]], [[sheep]], [[sack-of-thebe]]). The formula ἀργεννῇς ὀΐεσσι, “white sheep,” names the flock, as in Book 3 Odysseus ranged the ranks like a ram “through a great flock of white sheep” (3.198). The herds are the wealth of a royal house, and the sons of the house died tending them ([[raiding]], [[eetion]]).
