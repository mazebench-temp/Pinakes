---
summary: The “ready housekeeper” answers Hector's question and tells him that Andromache has gone to the tower; the adjective of Agamemnon's heralds names a woman of Hector's household.
grc: [ὀτρηρὴ ταμίη]
en: [ready housekeeper]
---

“Then the ready housekeeper spoke to him in turn” (τὸν δʼ αὖτʼ ὀτρηρὴ ταμίη πρὸς μῦθον ἔειπεν, 6.381; [[handmaids]]). ὀτρηρός (“quick, ready”), which in Book 1 described Agamemnon's heralds as they went unwillingly to take Briseis (1.321), here describes the servant who keeps the stores of Hector's house. In the Odyssey it describes the servants of the great houses, ready at the table and at the door (Od. 1.109, 4.23). The housekeeper's readiness is in her answer: she repeats Hector's words and corrects them, and tells him that his wife has gone hurrying to the wall “like a woman out of her mind,” with the nurse carrying the child (6.382–389; [[like-a-woman-out-of-her-mind]], [[andromache]]).
