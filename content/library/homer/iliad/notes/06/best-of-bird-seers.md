---
summary: The half-line that introduced Calchas in Book 1 introduces the Trojan seer Helenus, Priam's son, as he sends Hector into the city.
---

The two seers of the Iliad are introduced by the same half-verse, each with his father's name: Κάλχας Θεστορίδης, οἰωνοπόλων ὄχʼ ἄριστος (1.69; [[son-of-thestor]]) and Πριαμίδης Ἕλενος, οἰωνοπόλων ὄχʼ ἄριστος, “Helenus, son of Priam, by far the best of the bird-seers” (6.76; [[son-of-priam]]). Calchas told the Achaeans why the plague had come. Helenus tells the Trojans what to do when they are on the point of fleeing into the city: rally the army before the gates, and have the women offer Athena a robe and a vow (6.77–101; [[helenus]], [[the-robe-for-athena]]).

No bird is seen and no sign is read; the poem does not say how the seer knows. In Book 7 it does: Helenus “understood in his heart the plan that pleased the gods,” and he tells Hector that he has heard the voice of the gods (Il. 7.44–53; [[divination]]).
