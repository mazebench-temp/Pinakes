---
summary: Hector tells Paris that he would himself quarrel with any man he saw “holding back from hateful war.”
---

“And you yourself would quarrel with any other man whom you saw holding back from hateful war” (6.329–330). The half-verse repeats the narrator's line from Agamemnon's review, “And those whom he saw holding back from hateful war, these he would rebuke with angry words” (4.240–241). The rebuke that Agamemnon gave to hanging-back Achaeans Hector gives to his brother, and turns on him: Paris would blame in another man what he is doing himself ([[paris]], [[polemos]]). At the end of the book Hector says it plainly: “you hold back of your own will and do not want to fight” (6.523; [[strange-one]]).
