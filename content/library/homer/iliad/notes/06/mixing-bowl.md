---
summary: The book ends with Hector's hope that Zeus may one day let the Trojans “set up the mixing bowl of freedom in our halls” for the everlasting gods of heaven, when they have driven the Achaeans out of Troy (6.526–529).
passages:
  - 6.526-529 | The mixing bowl of freedom
---

Hector's last words in the book are a hope: “we will make these things right later, if ever Zeus grants us, for the everlasting gods of heaven, to set up the mixing bowl of freedom in our halls, when we have driven the well-greaved Achaeans out of Troy” (6.526–529). κρητὴρ ἐλεύθερος, “the mixing bowl of freedom”, occurs only here. It is a bowl set up in thanks for deliverance, as ἐλεύθερον ἦμαρ is the “day of freedom” (6.455; see [[eleutheros]]). To set up a bowl, κρητῆρα στήσασθαι, is to begin a feast and its libations. When Telemachus sails from Ithaca his crew “set up mixing bowls brim-full of wine and poured libations to the immortal gods who are forever” (Od. 2.431–432), the gods Hector names in the same words (θεοῖς αἰειγενέτῃσι, 6.527; see [[libation]] and [[mixing-bowl]]).

The bowl would be set up “in our halls”, at home. Seventy lines earlier Hector foresaw the day Andromache would be led away to slavery, “taking away your day of freedom” (6.455–463; see [[doulios]]). The book ends with the other outcome, hoped for by a man who has just said that he knows Ilios will fall (6.447–449; see [[a-day-will-come]] and [[hector]]).
