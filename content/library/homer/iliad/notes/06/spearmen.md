---
summary: The Trojans' prayer names Diomedes “that savage spearman,” in a verse of three epithets that Helenus speaks and Hector repeats.
grc: [ἄγριον αἰχμητὴν]
en: [savage spearman]
---

Helenus asks that Athena hold back from Ilios the son of Tydeus, “that savage spearman, mighty master of rout” (ἄγριον αἰχμητὴν κρατερὸν μήστωρα φόβοιο, 6.97), and Hector repeats the verse to his mother (6.278; [[master-of-rout]], [[krateros]]). ἄγριος (“wild, savage”) is the word for wild beasts: the wild goat whose horns made Pandarus' bow (αἰγὸς ἀγρίου, 4.105–106; [[goats]]). Of a man it is strong blame. In Book 9 Ajax says that Achilles “has made savage the great-hearted spirit in his breast” (ἄγριον … θυμόν, Il. 9.629). The Trojans see in Diomedes what the Achaeans will later see in Achilles. Helenus says as much: they never feared even Achilles as they fear this man, who “rages beyond measure” (6.99–101; [[leader-of-men]], [[diomedes]]).
