summary: Book 6 is a book of fathers. Glaucus and Diomedes discover the bond of their grandfathers, and Diomedes cannot remember his own father; Andromache has lost hers to Achilles and calls Hector her father; and Hector, who fights to win fame for his father, prays that his son may be better than he.
grc: [πατρώϊος, πατρώϊοι]

**The fathers' bond.** Glaucus was sent to Troy by his father Hippolochus with a charge: to be the best, “and not to shame the race of my fathers (γένος πατέρων), who were by far the best in Ephyra and in wide Lycia” (6.209–210; see [[genee]] and [[always-to-be-the-best]]). The lineage he recites makes Diomedes his ξεῖνος πατρώϊος, a guest-friend “from our fathers' time” (6.215, 6.231). πατρώϊος, “of the fathers”, here reaches back to the grandfathers, Oeneus and Bellerophon (see [[xenia]]). Diomedes knows the bond from a cup in his house, but not his own father: “Tydeus I do not remember, since I was still little when he left me” (6.222–223; see [[tydeus]]).

**A father killed.** Andromache's plea is built on her losses, and her father comes first: “I have no father and no lady mother. For brilliant Achilles killed my father” (6.413–414). Eetion's death is told with honor: Achilles did not strip him, but burned him in his armor and heaped a mound over him (6.416–420). Her mother died “in her father's halls” (6.428). Then she gives the name to Hector: “Hector, you are father to me, and lady mother, and brother” (6.429–430). See [[eetion]] and [[sack-of-thebe]].

**Hector as son and father.** Hector fights to win “great fame for my father and for myself” (6.446; see [[kleos]]). A few lines later he is a father. The child shrinks from “his dear father” in the horsehair helmet, and “his dear father and his lady mother laughed aloud” (6.468–471). Zeus is “father” as always: Hecuba would have Hector pour wine “to father Zeus and the other immortals” (Διὶ πατρί, 6.259; see [[father-zeus]]). And the ransoming father appears once, in Adrastus' plea: “many treasures lie stored in the house of my wealthy father”, and his father would pay a boundless ransom to hear that he was alive (6.47–50; see [[apoina]]).
