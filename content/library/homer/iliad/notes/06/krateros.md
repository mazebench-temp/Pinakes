---
summary: The adjective of strength describes Diomedes in the Trojans' prayer, Lycurgus and the trembling of Dionysus in Diomedes' story, and the harsh necessity that will lie on Andromache as a slave.
---

In the Trojans' prayer Diomedes is κρατερὸν μήστωρα φόβοιο, “mighty master of rout” (6.97, 6.278; [[master-of-rout]], [[spearmen]]). In his own story of the man who fought the gods the word goes first to the man and then to the god's fear: “not even the son of Dryas, mighty Lycurgus, lived long” (6.130), and Dionysus plunged into the sea in terror, “for a strong trembling had hold of him at the man's shouting” (6.137; [[lycurgus]], [[dionysus]], [[lycurgus-and-dionysus]]). The mighty man makes a god tremble, and does not live long.

The last use is harsh, as in Book 5's “harsh rebuke” (5.492). Hector pictures Andromache a slave in Argos, carrying water “much against your will, and harsh necessity will lie upon you” (κρατερὴ δʼ ἐπικείσετʼ ἀνάγκη, 6.458; [[captive-women]], [[slavery-and-freedom]]).
