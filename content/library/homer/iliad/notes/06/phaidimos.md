summary: The shining limbs of Bucolion's sons are loosened in death; Glaucus is “the shining son of Hippolochus”; and “shining Hector” reaches for his son, takes off his helmet, and takes it up again.
grc: [φαίδιμα]
---

The epithet describes the body in the obituary of the twins: Euryalus “loosened the strength and the shining limbs” of the sons of Bucolion (φαίδιμα γυῖα, 6.27; [[loosened-his-limbs]], [[bucolion]]). Glaucus answers Diomedes' challenge as “the shining son of Hippolochus” (6.144; [[glaucus]], [[hippolochus]]).

The formula φαίδιμος Ἕκτωρ, which Book 4 gave to Hector in retreat (4.505), marks the three gestures of the scene with his son. “Shining Hector reached out for his son” (6.466), and the child shrank from his helmet; “at once shining Hector took the helmet from his head” (6.472); and at the parting, “shining Hector took up his helmet” (6.494; [[horsehair-crested]], [[of-the-gleaming-helmet]]). The epithet of radiance, from the same root as φάος, “light,” stands beside the bronze that gleams on his head and frightens the boy ([[hector]], [[astyanax]]).
