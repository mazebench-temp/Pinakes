---
summary: The adjective ἀγκύλος describes the chariot that Adrastus' horses break in the tamarisk and the bow that Paris is handling in his chamber.
grc: [ἀγκύλον ἅρμα, ἀγκύλα τόξʼ]
---

Book 5 added the synonym ἀγκύλος (“bent”) to καμπύλος, and the curved chariot to the curved bow (5.209, 5.231). Book 6 has both again with ἀγκύλος. Adrastus' horses, bolting across the plain, “caught in a tamarisk branch and broke the curved chariot at the end of the pole” (ἀγκύλον ἅρμα, 6.38–40; [[chariot]], [[adrastus-the-trojan]]); thrown out face down in the dust, he is taken alive, and killed. Hector finds Paris in his chamber “busy with his beautiful armor, his shield and his corselet, and handling his curved bow” (ἀγκύλα τόξʼ, 6.321–322; [[bow]], [[paris]], [[perikalles]]). The archer's weapon is the one he handles at home. When he goes out at the end of the book he puts on the armor of a spearman (6.504; [[inlaid]]).
