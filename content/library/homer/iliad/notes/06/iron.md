summary: Adrastus offers “iron worked with much toil” among his father's treasures (6.48), in the verse of the ransom that Dolon and the sons of Antimachus offer too.
passages:
  - 6.45-50 | Adrastus begs for his life
---

“Bronze and gold and iron worked with much toil” (χαλκός τε χρυσός τε πολύκμητός τε σίδηρος, 6.48): the verse lists the metals stored in a rich man's house, and it recurs in the ransom offers of Dolon and of the sons of Antimachus (10.379, 11.133; see [[supplication]] and [[possessions]]). Iron stands with bronze and gold as a treasure. πολύκμητος, “worked with much toil” (πολύ, “much”, and κάμνω, “toil”), marks it as metal on which labor has been spent, and the word is used in the Iliad only in this verse. At the funeral games Achilles sets out a lump of iron as a prize, and says that it will last its winner five years, so that his shepherd and his plowman need not go to town for iron (23.826–835; see [[iron]]).
