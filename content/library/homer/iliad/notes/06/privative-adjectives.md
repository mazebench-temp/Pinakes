---
summary: Agamemnon wants every Trojan, even the unborn, to perish “unmourned and without a trace” (ἀκήδεστοι καὶ ἄφαντοι, 6.60). The book's single privatives gather around Hector: his unwashed hands, his joyless misery, the ransom without limit, and Andromache's life without a share.
grc: ["ἀκήδεστοι καὶ ἄφαντοι"]
en: [unmourned and without a trace]
---

**The pair.** Agamemnon's demand ends in two privatives: “but let them all together perish from Ilios, unmourned and without a trace” (6.59–60). ἀκήδεστος, “uncared for”, is from κήδομαι, “care for”, the verb of care for the dead as well as the living: the men who see to Patroclus' burning are κηδεμόνες (23.163). The Trojans are to die with no one to mourn or bury them (see [[lament]]). The adverb ἀκηδέστως, “without care”, is used of Achilles dragging Hector's body (22.465, 24.417). ἄφαντος, “vanished”, from φαίνω, “show”, is to leave no trace. Poseidon saves Aeneas so that the race of Dardanus may not perish “without seed and without a trace” (ἄσπερμος … καὶ ἄφαντος, 20.303). Here the two words sum up the end of a people (see [[supplication]] and [[agamemnon]]).

**Singles.** Hector's hands are ἄνιπτοι, “unwashed”, a word found only here (6.266; see [[hand-washing]]). If he could see Paris go down to Hades, he would forget his ἄτερπος ὀϊζύς, his “joyless misery” (6.285). The ransom that Adrastus offers and that Andromache's mother fetched is ἀπερείσια, “boundless” (6.49, 6.427; see [[apoina]]). Andromache calls herself “ill-fated” (6.408), ἄμμορος, literally one without a share of good fortune, and Hector sees her carrying water as a slave “much against your will” (ἀεκαζομένη, 6.458). The Chimaera is ἀμαιμάκετος, “irresistible” (6.179; see [[chimaera]]).
