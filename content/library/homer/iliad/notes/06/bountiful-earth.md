---
summary: Diomedes, glad to find a guest-friend, fixes his spear in the bountiful earth.
---

“So he spoke, and Diomedes, good at the war cry, was glad; he fixed his spear in the bountiful earth, and spoke to the shepherd of the people with soothing words” (6.212–214; [[good-at-the-war-cry]], [[shepherd-of-the-people]], [[soothing-words]]). In Book 3 the bountiful earth received the armor laid down for the truce (3.89, 3.195). Here it receives the spear of a man who will not fight, and the gesture comes before the words: before Diomedes proposes that they avoid each other's spears, he has put his own out of his hand (6.226; [[glaucus-and-diomedes]], [[xenia]]).
