---
summary: “Fair-cheeked Theano,” the priestess of Athena, opens the temple doors to the Trojan women and lays the robe on the goddess's knees.
grc: [καλλιπάρῃος]
---

In Book 1 the epithet belonged to the two captive women, always in the accusative, the object of other men's acts (1.143, 1.184). In Book 6 it is in the nominative, of a woman who acts. “Fair-cheeked Theano opened the doors for them, daughter of Cisseus, the wife of Antenor, tamer of horses; for the Trojans had made her priestess of Athena” (6.298–300), and “fair-cheeked Theano took the robe and laid it on the knees of Athena of the lovely hair” (6.302–303; [[theano]], [[priesthood]], [[lovely-haired]]). The verse of her act is the verse Helenus and Hector used in their orders (6.92, 6.273), and her prayer follows (6.305–310; [[protector-of-the-city]]).
