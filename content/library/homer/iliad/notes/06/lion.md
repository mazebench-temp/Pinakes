summary: After the six lion similes of Book 5, Book 6 has no lion simile. Its only lion is the front of the Chimaera, “a lion in front, a serpent behind, and in the middle a she-goat”, which Bellerophon killed in Lycia (6.181).
passages:
  - 6.179-183 | The Chimaera
---

The lion of Book 6 is part of a monster. The king of Lycia sends Bellerophon first to kill the Chimaera, “of divine race, not of men”:

{{quote:1b9cc57b-d835-5c1d-9333-f9aa99b25cec}}

πρόσθε λέων, ὄπιθεν δὲ δράκων, μέσση δὲ χίμαιρα: the lion is her forepart, the serpent her hind part, the she-goat her middle (see [[chimaera]], [[serpent]] and [[goats]]). Homer does not say whether she had three heads, or where the fire came from. The *Theogony* gives her three heads, a lion's, a she-goat's and a serpent's, and then has the two lines of the Iliad (*Theogony* 319–324). Apollodorus gives her the forepart of a lion, the tail of a serpent and, in the middle, a goat's head, through which she breathed fire (*Library* 2.3.1). See [[bellerophon-in-lycia]].

The lion is the beast of the Iliad's similes, and in Book 5 it stood for Diomedes in his fury (see [[the-aristeia-of-diomedes]]). In Book 6 Diomedes meets Glaucus, and the fighting gives way to talk; the book has few similes of any kind, and the only lion in it belongs to a story told between the armies.
