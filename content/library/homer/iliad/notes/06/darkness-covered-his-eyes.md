{{quote:1e18e8b8-88ff-5677-8735-3505f77b19f0}}

The blow is Antilochus' blow against Echepolus in Book 4, the first killing of the Iliad, in the same three verses: “He was first to strike him on the ridge of his horsehair-crested helmet” (6.9 = 4.459), the forehead, the bone, “and darkness covered his eyes” (6.11 = 4.461). πρῶτος, “first”, fits both. The fighting that the gods have left to men begins again as the first battle began, and the victim is again a man of rank, here “the best among the Thracians” (6.7; see [[acamas-of-thrace]] and [[androktasia]]).

The other deaths of the book have other endings. Axylus and his charioteer “went down beneath the earth” (γαῖαν ἐδύτην, 6.19). Euryalus “loosened the strength and the shining limbs” of the sons of Bucolion (6.27; see [[loosened-his-limbs]]). Andromache's brothers “went down on a single day into the house of Hades” (6.422; see [[hades]]).
