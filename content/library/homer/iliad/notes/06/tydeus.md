relations:
  - child: diomedes (6.96, 6.119, 6.222-223)

Τυδέα δʼ οὐ μέμνημαι, “Tydeus I do not remember” (6.222). Diomedes has been told about his father by others: by Agamemnon, who knew him only by report (4.370–400), and by Athena (5.800–813; see [[tydeus-at-thebes]]). Here he speaks of him for himself, and only to say that he has no memory of him. He knows his grandfather's guest better, for Bellerophon's cup is in his house (6.220–221; see [[oeneus]] and [[diomedes]]). In Book 14 he will say where his father lies: “the heaped earth covers him at Thebes” (14.114; see [[thebes]] and [[the-seven-against-thebes]]).

Glaucus' story brings Tydeus to mind in another way. Bellerophon, like Tydeus at Thebes, is a stranger whose hosts try to kill him, and on his way back he kills every man in the ambush they set (6.187–190; 4.391–398), and the half-line “obeying the portents of the gods” is used only of the two of them (4.398, 6.183; see [[bellerophon]] and [[ambush]]).

Otherwise Tydeus is present in Book 6 in his son's names: “the son of Tydeus” (Τυδέος υἱός, 6.96, 6.119, 6.277), once “the valiant son of Tydeus” (6.437), and Τυδεΐδης (6.145, 6.235; see [[son-of-tydeus]]).
