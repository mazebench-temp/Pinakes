---
summary: The nymphs who plant elms around Eetion's tomb are ὀρεστιάδες, “of the mountains,” a word found only here in Homer.
grc: [ὀρεστιάδες]
en: [mountain]
---

“And elms were planted around it by the mountain nymphs, daughters of aegis-bearing Zeus” (νύμφαι ὀρεστιάδες, 6.419–420; [[nymphs]], [[child-of-aegis-bearing-zeus]]). ὀρεστιάς is formed from ὄρος (“mountain”), like ὀρεσκῷος, the epithet of the Centaurs, “who lived in the mountains” (1.268). The nymphs of the mountains are the goddesses of the wild land around Thebe under wooded Placus ([[wooded]], [[placus]]). They plant trees around the tomb of a king whom Achilles honored in death ([[eetion]], [[intricately-worked]], [[sack-of-thebe]]).
