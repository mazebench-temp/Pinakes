---
summary: Hector twice sends his mother to “the temple of Athena, driver of spoil,” in the order that frames his instructions for the robe.
grc: [ἀγελείης]
---

The same verse opens and closes Hector's instructions: “But you go to the temple of Athena, driver of spoil, with burnt offerings, gathering the old women” (6.269–270), and again, after the robe and the vow, “But you go to the temple of Athena, driver of spoil” (6.279; [[temple]], [[the-robe-for-athena]]). The genitive Ἀθηναίης ἀγελείης is the phrase the Iliad uses again in Book 15 (Il. 15.213). The warlike title is given to the goddess from whom the women will ask mercy for the city and its children (6.275–278). Whether it means “driver of spoil” or “leader of the host” was debated in antiquity; the translation follows the first ([[athena]]).
