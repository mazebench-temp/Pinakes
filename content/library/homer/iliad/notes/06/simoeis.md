The first lines of the book set the scene with the two rivers of Troy. The Simoeis joins the Scamander in front of the city (5.773–774), and the fighting ranges over the plain between them, now that the gods have left it to the armies (6.1–2). The Xanthus is the Scamander under the name the gods give it (20.74; see [[scamander]]).

An ancient variant of the verse, reported in the scholia, reads μεσσηγὺς ποταμοῖο Σκαμάνδρου καὶ στομαλίμνης, “between the river Scamander and the lagoon”, and does not name the Simoeis. The translation follows the text as printed.
