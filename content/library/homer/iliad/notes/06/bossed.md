---
summary: As Hector leaves the battle, the black hide rim of his bossed shield knocks against his ankles and his neck.
grc: [ὀμφαλοέσσης]
---

“So he spoke, and Hector of the gleaming helmet went away; and the black hide knocked against his ankles and his neck, the rim that ran around the outer edge of his bossed shield” (6.116–118). The shield is slung on his back as he goes, and it is long enough to strike both his neck and his ankles. Such a shield covers the whole man, like the shield “like a tower” that Ajax carries (Il. 7.219); the epithet ὀμφαλόεις is usually that of the round shield with a boss. How the two descriptions fit together is a much-discussed question of Homeric armor ([[shield]], [[hector]]). ἄντυξ here is the rim of the shield, not the rail of a chariot ([[chariot]]).
