summary: Hector gives Paris no answer when Paris promises to follow him, and at the Scaean gates he smiles at his son in silence before a word is spoken. Athena answers the Trojan women's prayer with a silent shake of the head.
refs: [6.311, 6.342]
passages:
  - 6.399-406 | Hector smiles at his son
---

**A silence for Paris.** Paris has answered Hector's rebuke: the rebuke is fair, he sat apart from grief and not from anger, Helen has urged him back, and he will follow. “So he spoke, and Hector of the gleaming helmet said nothing to him” (6.342). It is the rebuker's silence this time, not the rebuked man's, as Diomedes' was before Agamemnon (4.401–402). Helen fills it with her own speech (6.343–358; see [[praise-and-blame]] and [[paris]]).

**A silence for his son.** At the Scaean gates the handmaid brings the child, and before anyone speaks, “he smiled as he looked at his son in silence” (ἤτοι ὃ μὲν μείδησεν ἰδὼν ἐς παῖδα σιωπῇ, 6.404). Andromache answers the smile with tears, and speaks first (6.405–407; see [[laughter-of-the-gods]], [[tears]] and [[astyanax]]).

**A goddess's refusal.** The Trojan women lift up their hands with a loud cry, and Theano prays. The goddess answers without a word: “So she spoke in prayer, but Pallas Athena shook her head” (ἀνένευε, 6.311). The nod is the gods' sign of assent, and its reverse is a refusal that needs no speech (see [[neuo]] and [[reciprocity-with-the-gods]]).
