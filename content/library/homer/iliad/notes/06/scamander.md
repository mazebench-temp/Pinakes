**Xanthus.** Ξάνθοιο (6.4) is the genitive of Ξάνθος, “Xanthus”, the river's name among the gods: “whom the gods call Xanthus, and men Scamander” (20.74; see [[language-of-gods-and-men]]). The narrator uses both names. Book 5 called the river Scamander (5.36, 5.77, 5.774). Here, and when Hector is carried to its ford after Ajax's stone (14.433–434), and in the river's fight with Achilles (21.2, 21.146, 21.383), it is Xanthus. The same name belongs to the river of Lycia, which Glaucus names later in this book (6.172; see [[xanthus-of-lycia]]), and to a son of Phaenops killed by Diomedes (5.152; see [[xanthus-son-of-phaenops]]). The form Ξάνθοιο is the Trojan river here and at 8.560, 21.337 and 21.383, but the Lycian one at 12.313.

An ancient variant of the verse, reported in the scholia, reads μεσσηγὺς ποταμοῖο Σκαμάνδρου καὶ στομαλίμνης, “between the river Scamander and the lagoon”, naming the river by its human name and leaving out the Simoeis. The translation follows the text as printed (see [[simoeis]]).

**Scamandrius.** Hector calls his son Σκαμάνδριος, though the others call him Astyanax (6.402–403; see [[astyanax]]). The name means “of the Scamander”. It is the boy's there, not the river's, and the entry does not claim it.
