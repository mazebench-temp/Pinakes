---
summary: Athena is “of the lovely hair” three times in Book 6, each time in the verse that lays the robe on her knees: Helenus' instruction, Hector's repetition, and Theano's act.
---

The same verse is spoken twice and then carried out. Helenus tells Hector what his mother is to do with the finest robe in her hall: “let her lay on the knees of Athena of the lovely hair” (6.92); Hector repeats the order to Hecuba (6.273); and Theano “took the robe and laid it on the knees of Athena of the lovely hair” (6.302–303; [[robe]], [[the-robe-for-athena]]). The knees are those of the goddess's seated image in her temple ([[temple]]).

The epithet of beauty, which Book 1 gave to Leto and Books 2 and 3 to Briseis and Helen, is here given to the warrior goddess, in the verse of a gift of women's work, a robe embroidered by the women of Sidon (6.289–295). In the book's later scenes the Trojan women themselves have “lovely tresses” as they pray (6.380, 6.385; [[with-lovely-tresses]], [[athena]]).
