---
summary: The sun appears in Book 6 only in a simile. Paris comes down from the citadel gleaming in his armor “like the shining sun”, ἠλέκτωρ.
grc: [ἠλέκτωρ]
---

As Paris runs down from Pergamus to overtake Hector, he goes “gleaming in his armor like the shining sun” (τεύχεσι παμφαίνων ὥς τʼ ἠλέκτωρ, 6.512–513; see [[paris]] and [[like-the-sun]]). ἠλέκτωρ, “the beaming one”, is a name for the sun. It occurs in the Iliad only here and in Book 19, where the same half-line describes Achilles in his new armor, “like the shining Hyperion” (ἠλέκτωρ Ὑπερίων, 19.398). Ὑπερίων is itself a name of the Sun, often joined to Ἠέλιος (8.480; Od. 1.8), and Hesiod makes Hyperion the Sun's father (*Theogony* 371–374). So the words that describe Paris here describe Achilles in Book 19, and Hector, dying, foretells that Paris and Apollo will kill Achilles at the Scaean gates (22.359–360).

The sun does not set in Book 6. The day of battle that began at dawn in Book 2 goes on through the whole book and ends in Book 7.
