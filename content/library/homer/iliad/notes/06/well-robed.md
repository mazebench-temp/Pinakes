---
summary: The epithet names the handmaid who goes with Andromache to the tower, and the wives of Hector's brothers, whom he thinks she may have gone to visit.
grc: [ἐϋπέπλῳ]
---

Andromache “had gone with her child and a well-robed handmaid and was standing on the tower, lamenting and weeping” (6.372–373; [[handmaids]]). Hector, not knowing this, asks whether she has gone “to my sisters, or to my brothers' well-robed wives” (εἰνατέρων ἐϋπέπλων, 6.378), and the housekeeper answers in the same words (6.383; [[in-laws]]). The word εἰνάτερες, the wives of a husband's brothers, is one of Homer's precise words for kin by marriage. In Book 5 the well-robed women were Achaean women in Athena's joke (5.424); here they are the women of Hector's family. The phrase returns in Helen's lament over Hector in Book 24, where the brothers' well-robed wives are among those in the house who reproached her, as Hector never did (Il. 24.767–772).
