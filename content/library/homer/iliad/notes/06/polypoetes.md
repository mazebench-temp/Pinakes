His one line keeps the epithet the Catalogue gave him, μενεπτόλεμος, “steadfast in battle” (6.29, 2.740; see [[steadfast-in-battle]]). The Lapith leader is one of the ten Achaeans who each kill a man in the opening list, and his victim is given only a name (see [[astyalus]] and [[androktasia]]). His larger scene comes in Book 12, where he and Leonteus stand at the gate of the Achaean wall like two oaks on a mountain and hold off Asius and his men; Polypoetes kills three Trojans there (12.127–187). At Patroclus' funeral games he wins the throwing of the lump of iron (23.826–849).
