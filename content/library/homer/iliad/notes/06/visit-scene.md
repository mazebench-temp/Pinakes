summary: Hector's errand in the city is a chain of visits: his mother meets him in Priam's house and offers him wine, he finds Paris in his chamber with Helen and her women and will not sit, and at his own house he does not find his wife, who comes running to meet him at the gates. He refuses every rest a visitor is offered.

**Priam's house.** Hector arrives (6.242), and the house is described (6.243–250; see [[priams-palace]]). The host comes out to him: “there his generous mother came to meet him” (6.251), leading Laodice, and she clings to his hand and calls him by name (6.253; see [[called-him-by-name]]).

{{quote:7d252193-6f88-5d8f-8c22-418b5aa8bdff}}

Her first words are the host's question, with τίπτε: “My child, why have you left the bold war and come here?” (6.254). It is the question for a fighter found away from the fighting: Idomeneus asks it of Meriones, and Agamemnon of Nestor (13.249–250, 14.42–43). Then she offers him wine, and he refuses it (6.258–268; see [[wine]] and [[then-answered]]).

**Paris' house.** Hector arrives at a house the narrator describes, the one Paris built with the best builders in Troy (6.313–317; see [[house]]), and he goes in, spear in hand (6.318–320). Then the finding: “He found him in his chamber busy with his beautiful armor” (τὸν δʼ εὗρʼ ἐν θαλάμῳ, 6.321), and Helen sits among her slave women, directing their work (6.323–324). In Book 3 Iris found Helen in the hall, weaving at her loom (3.125–128). Helen offers him a seat, and he will not sit: “you will not persuade me” (6.354–360), the words with which Patroclus refuses a seat at Nestor's (11.648; see [[stool]]).

**His own house.** He arrives at his own house, and the finding is turned round: “but he did not find white-armed Andromache in the halls” (οὐδʼ εὗρʼ, 6.371). He stands on the threshold and asks the slave women where she has gone (6.374–380; see [[threshold]] and [[handmaids]]), and the housekeeper's answer sends him back the way he came (6.381–391).

**At the gates.** The last reception takes place in the open. “There his richly dowered wife came running to meet him” (ἐναντίη ἦλθε θέουσα, 6.394), in the words used of his mother's coming (ἐναντίη ἤλυθε, 6.251), and she too clings to his hand and calls him by name (6.406). In three houses Hector does not sit down, eat or drink. He is the hurried visitor of the pattern, whom the war calls back (see [[visit-scene]], [[hectors-visit-to-troy]] and [[hector]]).
