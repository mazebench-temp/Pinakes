**On the citadel.** Troy's second temple stands, like Apollo's on Pergamus (5.445–448), “on the height of the city” (ἐν πόλει ἄκρῃ, 6.88, 6.297). The houses of Priam, Hector and Paris are there too (6.317; see [[house]] and [[pergamus]]). The city's gods and its royal family live side by side at its highest point.

**A locked house.** Helenus tells Hecuba to open “with the key the doors of the sacred house” (οἴξασα κληῗδι θύρας ἱεροῖο δόμοιο, 6.89). νηός is the god's dwelling, from ναίω, “dwell” (see [[temple]]), and here it is called simply a house, δόμος. A κληΐς is the bar that closed a door and the hooked key that drew it back. Penelope opens her storeroom with a curved key of bronze with an ivory handle (Od. 21.6–7, 21.46–48), and Hera's chamber on Olympus is shut with a secret bolt that no other god could open (14.168). The temple is locked like a treasure room, for it keeps the goddess and what is given to her. When the women come, it is not the queen but the priestess who opens the doors (6.298; see [[theano]] and [[priesthood]]).

**The image.** The robe is to be laid “on the knees of Athena of the lovely hair” (6.92, 6.273, 6.303). The image inside must be seated, since the robe goes on its knees, and it is the only image of a god in Homer that receives worship. Suppliants clasp the knees, and what is still undecided “lies on the knees of the gods” (θεῶν ἐν γούνασι κεῖται, 17.514, 20.435, Od. 1.267): the women put their gift where favor is granted (see [[gounata]] and [[robe]]). The twelve cows are promised “in her temple” (ἐνὶ νηῷ, 6.93, 6.274, 6.308), that is, at the sanctuary, where the victims would be killed (see [[sacrifice]]).

**Where the women are.** Hector, looking for his wife, asks whether she has gone ἐς Ἀθηναίης, literally “to Athena's”, which the translation renders “to the temple of Athena” (6.379). The goddess's name stands for her house, as in Ἄϊδος εἴσω, “into the house of Hades” (6.284, 6.422). “The other Trojan women with lovely tresses” are there, “appeasing the dread goddess” (6.379–380, 6.384–385; see [[propitiation]] and [[trojan-women]]). The temple is where the women of Troy go when their men are losing, as the tower is where Andromache goes (6.386–389; see [[the-tower-of-ilios]]). See [[athena]] and [[the-robe-for-athena]].
