**Paris.** In Book 3 Paris' arming for the duel took eleven verses, piece by piece, from the greaves to the spear (3.328–338; see [[arming-scene]]). In Book 6 it is promised, “But come now, wait while I put on my armor of war” (Ἀρήϊα τεύχεα δύω, 6.340), and then reported as done: “but when he had put on his glorious armor, inlaid with bronze” (ἐπεὶ κατέδυ κλυτὰ τεύχεα ποικίλα χαλκῷ, 6.504). The shield, corselet and bow he was handling in his chamber (6.321–322) are not named again (see [[corselet]] and [[bow]]). What the poem gives him in place of an arming is his run through the city in the armor, “gleaming in his armor like the shining sun”, and the simile of the stallion broken loose (6.505–514; see [[the-stallion]], [[like-the-sun]] and [[paris]]).

**Hector's helmet.** At the gates Hector takes off one piece and puts it back. He lifts the helmet from his head, because its crest has frightened his son, and lays it on the ground; when he has sent Andromache home he takes it up again (6.472–473, 6.494–495; see [[helmet]]). His first act in the book was to leap from his chariot “in his armor” (σὺν τεύχεσιν, 6.103), and he leaves the book armed again at the gate.
