summary: Hector rallies the army with the Trojans' standing formula, “High-hearted Trojans and far-famed allies.”
grc: [ὑπέρθυμοι]
---

“High-hearted Trojans and far-famed allies, be men, my friends, and remember your furious courage” (6.111–112; [[exhortation]], [[famous]], [[furious]]). Τρῶες ὑπέρθυμοι is the epithet of the Trojans as a people; Books 4 and 5 gave the singular to Diomedes and to a Trojan priest (4.365, 5.77, 5.376). The whole verse returns in Book 9, where Odysseus tells Achilles that “the high-hearted Trojans and their far-famed allies” are camped close to the ships (Il. 9.232–233). Here Hector calls on their spirit as he leaves them: he is going to the city to have the elders and the women pray (6.113–115; [[trojans]], [[trojan-allies]]).
