---
summary: The vocative of reproach and intimacy passes between the brothers and the spouses of Book 6: Hector to Paris twice, Andromache to Hector, and Hector to Andromache.
grc: [δαιμόνιε]
---

Hector's rebuke of Paris opens with it: “Strange one, you do wrong to keep this anger in your heart” (6.326; [[paris]], [[cholos]]). Andromache's plea opens with it too: “Strange one, your own might will destroy you, and you have no pity for your infant son or for me” (δαιμόνιε, 6.407–408; [[menos]], [[andromache]]). Hector answers her with the feminine: “Strange one, do not grieve too much in your heart for me” (δαιμονίη, 6.486; [[moira]]). And his last words in the book, to Paris, begin with it again: “Strange one, no man who was fair-minded would dishonor your work in battle, since you are valiant” (6.521–522; [[dear-brother]], [[strong-spear]]).

The word is said between people who are close: in Book 1 by Zeus to Hera (1.561), and here within one family. It carries reproach from brother to brother and from wife to husband, and consolation from husband to wife ([[husbands-and-wives]], [[hector]]).
