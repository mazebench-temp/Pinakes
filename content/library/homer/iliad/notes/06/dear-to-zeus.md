---
summary: “Hector, dear to Zeus,” enters Paris' house with a spear eleven cubits long in his hand.
---

“There Hector, dear to Zeus, went in, and in his hand he held a spear eleven cubits long; and before him shone the bronze point of the spear, and a golden ring ran around it” (6.318–320; [[spear]], [[hector]]). The epithet that Book 1 gave to Achilles and to Apollo is here Hector's, at the moment he comes armed into his brother's chamber, where Paris is handling his own armor (6.321–322). In Book 8, when Hector addresses the Trojans at nightfall, the same verses describe him and his spear, beginning again with “Hector, dear to Zeus” (Il. 8.493–495). Zeus' favor is real in the books that follow: he means to give Hector glory until fire is thrown on the ships (Il. 15.596–604).
