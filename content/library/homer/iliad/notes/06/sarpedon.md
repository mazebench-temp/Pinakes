This is the only account of his birth in Homer. μητίετα Ζεύς, “Zeus the counselor” (see [[counselor]]), is his father, as the poem has said since Book 5 (5.631, 5.635, 5.683), and his mother is [[Laodameia]], the daughter of [[Bellerophon]]. So Sarpedon is the first cousin of [[Glaucus]], the son of her brother Hippolochus, and both are grandsons of the hero who came to Lycia with the grim signs. ἀντίθεος, “godlike” (see [[antitheos]]), is his epithet in Book 5 as well (5.663, 5.692; again 12.307); χαλκοκορυστής, “armed in bronze” (see [[armed-in-bronze]]), is elsewhere Hector's (5.699, 6.398) and is given to Sarpedon only here.

The poem does not say why the daughter's son leads the Lycians and the son's son comes second (2.876), though Sarpedon speaks of the two of them as the kings who rule Lycia (12.318–319). Herodotus says that the Lycians of his own day reckoned descent through their mothers: asked who he is, a Lycian recites his mother's line and her mothers' (1.173.4–5). Glaucus, asked the same question, recites his fathers'.

Artemis killed his mother “in her anger” (6.205), and Zeus, listing his past loves to Hera, does not name her (14.315–328). Later tradition gave Sarpedon another mother, Europa (see [[laodameia]]).
