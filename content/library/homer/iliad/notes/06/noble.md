---
summary: The epithet names Laomedon, “noble Laomedon,” in the pedigree of Bucolion, his eldest son, whose twin sons die in the opening killings.
---

“Bucolion was the son of noble Laomedon, the eldest by birth, but his mother bore him in secret” (6.23–24). In Book 5 Sarpedon gave Laomedon the same epithet in the verse that blamed him for the first sack of Troy, “through the folly of a man, noble Laomedon” (5.649–650; [[heracles-sacks-troy]]). Here the praise stands in a family story. Laomedon's eldest son was born out of wedlock, lay with a nymph while he tended sheep, and fathered the twins Aesepus and Pedasus, whom Euryalus kills and strips (6.21–28; [[laomedon]], [[bucolion]], [[aesepus-son-of-bucolion]], [[pedasus-son-of-bucolion]]). The Trojan royal line is traced from the king's praise to his grandsons' death in one obituary ([[the-war-in-small]]).
