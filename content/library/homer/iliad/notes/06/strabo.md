---
summary: Strabo turns Book 6 against the people of Ilium in his day, who claimed that their city had never been wiped out. Homer says plainly that sacred Ilios will perish, and the robe laid on Athena's knees shows that her image was seated, while the one shown at Ilium stands.
---

The people of Ilium in Strabo's day claimed that their city had survived the Greek sack, and showed an old image of Athena as proof. Strabo answers from Homer (13.1.41). The poet “has said explicitly” that the city was wiped out, “a day will come when sacred Ilios will perish” (6.448, the line of Agamemnon at 4.164; see [[a-day-will-come]]). And the image at Ilium stands, but Homer's was seated, since the robe is laid on the goddess's knees; many ancient images of Athena are seated, as at Phocaea, Massalia, Rome and Chios. See [[the-robe-and-the-panathenaea]] and [[temple]].
