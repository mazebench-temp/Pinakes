summary: The frame brings in two of Hector's answers in the city, in the same verse, and both are refusals: he will not drink his mother's wine or sit in the chair Helen offers him (6.263, 6.359).
---

“Then great Hector of the gleaming helmet answered her” (τὴν δʼ ἠμείβετʼ ἔπειτα μέγας κορυθαίολος Ἕκτωρ) comes twice, both times after a woman has offered him rest. Hecuba offers wine, to pour to Zeus and to drink (6.258–262); Helen asks him to come in and sit down (6.354–358). Both answers begin with μή, “do not”: “Do not bring me wine sweet to the heart, lady mother” (6.264), and “Do not ask me to sit, Helen, though you love me; you will not persuade me” (6.360). Both give the war as the reason. The wine would take away his might, and the Trojans are longing for him while he is away (6.265, 6.361–362). See [[wine]], [[stool]], [[visit-scene]] and [[hector]].
