---
summary: The Chimaera is “of divine race, not of men,” the first of the tasks that the king of Lycia sets Bellerophon.
---

“First he told him to kill the irresistible Chimaera; and she was of divine race, not of men, a lion in front, a serpent behind, and in the middle a she-goat” (θεῖον γένος οὐδʼ ἀνθρώπων, 6.179–181; [[chimaera]], [[irresistible]]). The monster belongs to the gods' side of the world, and Bellerophon kills her “obeying the portents of the gods” (6.183). Homer does not say whose child she was.

At 6.507 θείῃ is not the adjective but a verb, “runs,” in the simile of the stallion, and the entry's forms exclude it ([[the-stallion]]).
