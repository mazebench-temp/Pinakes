---
summary: The title of mothers runs through Hector's visit: he calls Hecuba “lady mother,” Theano's prayer calls Athena “lady,” and Andromache, who has no lady mother, finds one in Hector, and is one as she laughs at her son's fear.
---

The formula πότνια μήτηρ, “lady mother,” which in Book 1 named Thetis (1.357), belongs in Book 6 to the women of Troy. Hector refuses his mother's wine: “Do not bring me wine sweet to the heart, lady mother” (6.264; [[hecuba]], [[honey-sweet]]). Theano begins her prayer with the title of a goddess, “Lady Athena, protector of the city” (πότνιʼ Ἀθηναίη, 6.305; [[protector-of-the-city]]).

In Andromache's speech the formula names a loss and its replacement. Achilles killed her father and her brothers, and Artemis her mother: “I have no father and no lady mother” (6.413). Then she turns to her husband: “Hector, you are father to me, and lady mother, and brother, and you are my husband in his prime” (6.429–430; [[andromache]], [[husbands-and-wives]]). The last use gives her the title herself: when the child shrinks from the helmet, “his dear father and his lady mother laughed aloud” (6.471; [[astyanax]]).
