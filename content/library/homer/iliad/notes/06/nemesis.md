  - 6.343-358 | Helen speaks to Hector
---

The noun comes twice in Book 6, a few lines apart, once from each partner of the marriage that caused the war.

**Paris.** Hector finds him in his chamber handling his armor and rebukes him: “you do wrong to keep this anger in your heart” (6.326). Hector does not say what the anger is. Paris answers as if the charge were resentment of the Trojans: “It was not so much because I was angry or indignant with the Trojans (χόλῳ οὐδὲ νεμέσσι) that I sat in my chamber; I wanted to give myself up to grief” (6.335–336). The Trojans had reason to expect it. At the end of Book 3 they would not have hidden him, “for he was hateful to them all, like black doom” (3.454). Paris names the indignation only to deny it, and puts grief (ἄχος) in its place; he does not say for what, and the defeat of Book 3 is the likeliest cause. See [[cholos]], [[achos]] and [[paris]].

**Helen.** When Paris has promised to follow, Helen speaks. She curses herself and wishes that she had been swept away on the day she was born, and then comes to the husband she has:

{{quote:af28d0ec-7eec-5c60-b06c-ad176c9b063f}}

νέμεσις here is not a feeling in Paris but the indignation of other people, joined with their αἴσχεα, “reproaches” (see [[aischros]]). ᾔδη, “knew”, is to be aware of it and to care. A better man would know what people say of him and act on it; Paris does not, and his mind “is not steadfast now, nor will it ever be” (see [[phrenes]]). The man she is speaking to is the one who does care. Hector's refusal to stay out of the war rests on exactly this knowledge: he “would feel shame before the Trojans and the Trojan women” (6.442; see [[aidos]]). νέμεσις is what others feel, and αἰδώς is what a man feels for fear of it.

At the end of the book Hector tells Paris what the Trojans are saying: “my heart grieves in my spirit when I hear shameful things said of you by the Trojans, who have much toil for your sake” (6.524–525). The Trojans' indignation is real, and Paris hears of it from his brother. See [[helen]], [[husbands-and-wives]] and [[praise-and-blame]].
