---
summary: Hector foresees Andromache as a slave in Argos, carrying water “from Messeis or Hypereia” (6.457).
grc: [Ὑπερείης]
---

The Thessalian spring of the Catalogue (2.734) returns in Hector's picture of Andromache's slavery. In Argos she will weave at another woman's bidding “and carry water from Messeis or Hypereia, much against your will” (6.456–458; see [[messeis]] and [[andromache]]). Here fetching water is a slave's labor, laid on her by “harsh necessity” (6.458; see [[doulios]]). Before the war the wives and daughters of the Trojans went freely to the springs of the Scamander to wash their clothes (22.153–156).

The two springs lie in no single region, and “Argos” may mean all of Greece (see [[argos]]). Hector does not know where she will be taken, only what she will do there.
