---
summary: Diomedes tells the stranger that he has never seen him before “in the battle where men win glory.”
grc: [κυδιανείρῃ]
---

“For I have never seen you in the battle where men win glory before now; but now you have come out far in front of all in your daring” (6.124–126). The battle that brings men κῦδος, “glory” ([[kydos]]), is the place where a warrior makes himself known, and a man never seen there who comes out in front of all is either a newcomer or a god. Diomedes considers both and says he will fight only a mortal (6.128–143; [[good-sir]], [[mortal-men]]). The stranger answers with his lineage, as if a man's name were made by his fathers rather than by the battle (6.145–211; [[genee]], [[glaucus]]).
