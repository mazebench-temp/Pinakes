  - 6.215-221 | Oeneus and Bellerophon

“And Bellerophon a two-handled cup of gold, which I left in my house when I came here” (Βελλεροφόντης δὲ χρύσεον δέπας ἀμφικύπελλον, 6.220–221). The δέπας ἀμφικύπελλον is the cup of the feast and the libation, the cup Hephaestus puts in Hera's hand on Olympus (1.584; see [[cup]]). As a gift it passes from guest to host: when Telemachus leaves Sparta, Menelaus puts a two-handled cup in his hand (Od. 15.120). Bellerophon's cup has passed from Oeneus to his grandson, and it stays in the house in Argos while Diomedes is at war. It is the evidence for the story Diomedes tells, and the reason he can greet Glaucus as a guest-friend (6.215–231; see [[guest-gifts]], [[xenia]] and [[belt]]).
