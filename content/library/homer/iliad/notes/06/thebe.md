---
summary: Thebe is Andromache's home, “Thebe under Placus”, where her father Eetion ruled the Cilicians. She tells Hector how Achilles sacked the “high-gated” city and killed her father and her brothers there.
grc: ["Θήβῃ Ὑποπλακίῃ"]
---

Andromache's father lived “in Thebe under Placus, lord over the Cilician men” (6.397; see [[eetion]] and [[cilicians]]). Θήβη Ὑποπλακίη names the city by the mountain above it (see [[placus]]), and sets it apart from Boeotian Thebes, which Diomedes named earlier in the book (Θήβῃσιν, 6.223; see [[thebes]]).

In her account Achilles “utterly sacked the well-peopled city of the Cilicians, high-gated Thebe” (6.415–416; see [[high-gated]]). Book 1 called it “the sacred city of Eetion” (1.366), and Book 2 remembered its walls (2.691). ὑψίπυλος, “high-gated”, is otherwise said in the Iliad only of Troy (16.698, 21.544). The raid that gave the Achaeans Chryseis (1.366–369) took from Andromache her father and her seven brothers. The poem tells it twice, from the Achaean camp and from the Scaean gates (see [[sack-of-thebe]] and [[andromache]]). In Book 22 she will remember that she was born there, “under wooded Placus” (22.479).
