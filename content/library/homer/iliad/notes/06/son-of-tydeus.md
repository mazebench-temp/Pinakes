---
summary: Diomedes is named by his father in the Trojans' prayer that Athena keep him from Ilios, in his meeting with Glaucus, where he says he does not remember Tydeus, and in Andromache's list of the best of the Achaeans.
grc: [Τυδέος ἄλκιμον υἱόν]
---

To the Trojans the son of Tydeus is the danger itself. Helenus and then Hector tell the women to pray that Athena may “hold the son of Tydeus back from sacred Ilios” (6.96, 6.277; [[hieros]], [[master-of-rout]]). In the meeting with Glaucus the narrator and Glaucus use the patronymic too: “Glaucus, son of Hippolochus, and the son of Tydeus came together” (6.119), “Great-hearted son of Tydeus, why do you ask about my lineage?” (6.145; [[great-hearted]]), and Glaucus exchanged his gold armor “with Diomedes, son of Tydeus” (6.235; [[glaucus-and-diomedes]]).

The man named by his father more than by his own name has no memory of him: “Tydeus I do not remember, since I was still little when he left me, when the army of the Achaeans perished at Thebes” (6.222–223; [[tydeus]], [[the-seven-against-thebes]]). What he inherits from his house is his grandfather Oeneus' guest-friendship with Bellerophon (6.215–221; [[xenia]], [[oeneus]]). Andromache names him last among the best of the Achaeans who have tried the wall: “the valiant son of Tydeus” (6.437; [[strong-spear]], [[diomedes]]).
