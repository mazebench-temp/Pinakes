summary: Hector leaves the battle to tell “the elders who sit in council” and the wives of Troy to pray to the gods and promise hecatombs (6.113–115).

ἠδὲ γέρουσιν / εἴπω βουλευτῇσι, “and tell the elders / who sit in council” (6.113–114). βουλευτής, “councillor”, from βουλή, “council” (see [[boule]]), occurs only here in Homer. These are the old men who sat with Priam on the tower in Book 3, “the leaders of the Trojans” (3.153; see [[the-tower-of-ilios]]). Hector names them with the wives: the prayers of the city belong to those who do not fight (see [[trojan-women]] and [[hecatomb]]). In the event the elders have no part in it. Helenus' plan (6.86–97) and Hector's instructions to his mother (6.269–278) give the offering to the women alone, and it is the old women who carry the robe to Athena (6.286–311; see [[the-robe-for-athena]]).
