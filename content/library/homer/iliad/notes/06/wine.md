summary: Hecuba offers Hector honey-sweet wine, to pour to Zeus and then to drink, for wine increases the might of a weary man. Hector refuses it, for fear that it will unnerve him (6.258–268).

**The offer.** Hecuba's first thought when her son comes in from the battle is to give him something:

{{quote:32072cf9-565f-545a-99e3-17df5efac408}}

Her offer keeps the order of the custom, a libation first and then a drink (see [[libation]]). Her reason is a maxim, “for in a weary man wine greatly increases might” (6.261), and Odysseus makes the same argument before the battle of Book 19: men who have had their fill of food and wine can fight all day, for wine and bread are “might and courage” (19.160–170; see [[menos]]).

**The refusal.** Hector answers with the same two words, turned the other way. The wine would take away the might and the courage that his mother thinks it will give him: “for fear you unnerve me and rob me of my might, and I forget my courage” (μή μʼ ἀπογυιώσῃς μένεος, ἀλκῆς τε λάθωμαι, 6.265). ἀπογυιόω, “unnerve”, literally “take the limbs from” (γυῖα, “limbs”), occurs only here; it is the loosening of the limbs that ends men in battle, done by a cup (see [[loosened-his-limbs]] and [[alke]]). And he may not pour it: his hands are unwashed and spattered with blood (6.266–268; see [[hand-washing]]).

**Two epithets.** Hecuba's wine is μελιηδής, “honey-sweet” (μέλι, “honey”, and ἡδύς, “sweet”, 6.258), and Hector's word for it is μελίφρων, “sweet to the heart” (μέλι and φρήν, 6.264). The wine he will not pour is αἴθοψ, “sparkling” (6.266). μελίφρων is the word for the wine Hecuba brings Priam in the last book, which he does pour (24.284; see [[libation]]).
