---
summary: Paris built his house with “the men who were then the best builders in deep-soiled Troy.”
---

“And Hector went to the house of Alexander, a beautiful house, which he himself had built with the men who were then the best builders in deep-soiled Troy” (6.313–315; [[paris]], [[house]]). In Book 3 the epithet named the land the Trojans would keep if the duel settled the war (3.74, 3.257). Here it frames a house built in peacetime by men who “were then the best”: the phrase looks back from the war to the city's prosperity, as the rich fields of the epithet do ([[troy]]).
