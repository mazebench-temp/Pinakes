---
summary: Hecuba asks Hector to pour a libation “to father Zeus and the other immortals” before he drinks.
---

“So that you may pour a libation to father Zeus and the other immortals first, and then you yourself will be the better for it, if you drink” (Διὶ πατρί, 6.259–260; [[libation]], [[honey-sweet]]). The order is the order of the feast: the gods' share first, then the man's. Hector refuses both, since his hands are unwashed from battle (6.264–268; [[hand-washing]]). When he prays to Zeus later in the book, it is not for himself but for his son, with the helmet off and the child in his arms (6.475–481; [[prayer]], [[fathers-and-sons]]).
