grc: [γόνον ἠῢν, ἔμμεναι ἐσθλὸς, πολέες τε καὶ ἐσθλοὶ, οὐδὲ μὲν ἐσθλόν, βίην τʼ ἀγαθόν]
en: [noble, good in strength]

The formula ἠΰς τε μέγας τε closes the obituary of the first man killed in the book: “the son of Eussorus, Acamas, tall and brave” (6.8; [[acamas-of-thrace]]). The old adjective comes back once, of Bellerophon, when the king of Lycia recognizes him as “the noble offspring of a god” (θεοῦ γόνον ἠΰν, 6.191; [[bellerophon]]).

The common word ἐσθλός runs through Hector's speeches to Andromache. He cannot hold back from the war, “since I have learned to be brave always, and to fight among the foremost of the Trojans” (6.444–445; [[kleos]], [[aidos]]). He foresees his brothers, “who, many and brave, may fall in the dust at the hands of hostile men” (6.452–453; [[hostile-men]]). And he comforts her with a rule that sets courage aside: no man has escaped his fate, “no coward and no brave man, once he has been born” (6.488–489; [[moira]]). Between these, in his prayer, he asks that his son be “as good in strength” as he is (6.478; [[fathers-and-sons]]). The man who has learned to be ἐσθλός knows that it will not save him.
