---
summary: The formula opens Book 6 with the battle “left to itself” once the gods have gone, and Hector, sent to the city by Helenus, awakes it again as he rallies the Trojans.
---

The book's first verse repeats the half-line that Aphrodite used to measure how far the war had gone, when “it is no longer the dread din of battle between Trojans and Achaeans” but a war of men against gods (5.379–380). Now the gods have left the field: “the dread din of battle between Trojans and Achaeans was left to itself” (οἰώθη, 6.1). Ares has gone up to Olympus, and Hera and Athena have gone home (5.906–909), and the war is the men's again ([[the-war-in-small]], [[androktasia]]).

The second use repeats three whole verses of Book 5. There Hector, stung by Sarpedon's rebuke, leapt from his chariot and went through the army, “and he awoke the dread din of battle” (5.494–496). Here the same verses follow Helenus' advice to rally the army before he goes to the city (6.103–105; [[helenus]], [[exhortation]]). Twice in two books the Trojan rally is Hector's answer to another man's words.
