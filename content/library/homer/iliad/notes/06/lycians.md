summary: Helenus names the Lycians with the Trojans as the peoples whose toil rests on Hector and Aeneas (6.78). In Glaucus' story the Lycians give Bellerophon a domain of his own, and their best men die in the ambush their king set for him (6.188–195).
grc: [Λύκιοι]
refs: [6.188-190]
---

In the present of the book the Lycians are named once, when Helenus tells Aeneas and Hector that on them “rests the toil of the Trojans and the Lycians” (6.77–78; see [[helenus]]). Sarpedon does not appear in the book, and Glaucus meets Diomedes alone, without his Lycians (see [[glaucus]]).

The rest is the past of Glaucus' family. The king of Lycia chose “the best men out of wide Lycia” to ambush Bellerophon, and “they never came home again” (6.188–190). When the king kept him and gave him his daughter, “the Lycians marked out for him a domain finer than all others, a fair one, of orchard and plowland” (6.194–195; see [[bellerophon]]). The τέμενος, a piece of land “cut off” (τέμνω) for a king or a god, is the reward of the Lycian kings: Sarpedon reminds Glaucus that they hold “a great domain by the banks of the Xanthus” and must earn it in the front rank (12.313–321). Achilles mocks Aeneas with nearly the same two lines, asking whether the Trojans have marked out such a domain for him if he kills Achilles (20.184–186). See [[lycia]] and [[sarpedon]].
