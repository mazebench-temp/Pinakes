{{quote:d95bbd19-b30a-5d7b-8e3c-7363820490c6}}

Hector comes into the city “to the Scaean gates and the oak” (6.237; see [[the-oak-of-zeus]] and [[trojan-women]]), and he leaves it the way he came, “back the same way through the well-built streets” (6.391). He and Andromache meet at the gate, not in his house: she had gone to the great tower (6.386), which probably stands over it (see [[the-tower-of-ilios]]). The name of the gates is held over into the next line, πύλας … / Σκαιάς: he came “to the gates, the Scaean gates, by which he meant to go out to the plain” (6.392–393).

Theano names the same gates in her prayer, asking Athena to let Diomedes “fall headlong before the Scaean gates” (6.307; see [[theano]]). Athena refuses. It is Hector who will stand before the Scaean gates to wait for Achilles (22.5–6), and there, the dying Hector foretells, Paris and Apollo will kill Achilles (22.359–360).
