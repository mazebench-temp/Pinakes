**The order.** Nestor speaks as soon as Adrastus is dead, and he draws the army's lesson from the scene: this is the time to kill men, not to take them alive or strip them (see [[adrastus-the-trojan]]). No one is to “fling himself on the spoils and stay behind” to bring the most back to the ships (6.68–69). A man bent over a corpse is not fighting, and the rout is kept up only if no one stops (see [[stripping-the-dead]]). Hector gives the Trojans the same order in Book 15, when they stop to strip the dead: make for the ships and leave the bloody spoils (15.346–351).

**The forms.** He calls “shouting aloud” (μακρὸν ἀΰσας, 6.66), in the verse with which Hector calls to the Trojans forty lines later (6.110), and he addresses the army with Agamemnon's words in the Book 2 assembly: ὦ φίλοι ἥρωες Δαναοὶ θεράποντες Ἄρηος, “Friends, Danaan heroes, attendants of Ares” (6.67 = 2.110; also 15.733, 19.78; see [[attendants-of-ares]] and [[heros]]). The verse that follows the speech, “So saying, he urged on the might and spirit of every man” (6.72), closes his exhortation in Book 15 as well (15.667; see [[exhortation]]).

**Counsel and the spear.** In Book 4 Nestor said that he would give orders “with counsel and with words”, for that is the prize of the old, and left the spears to the younger men (4.322–325). In Book 6 the order is his and the spear is his son's: Νεστορίδης, “the son of Nestor”, kills Ablerus a few lines earlier (6.32–33; see [[antilochus]]).
