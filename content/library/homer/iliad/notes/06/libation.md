**The gods first.** Hecuba will bring wine “so that you may pour a libation to father Zeus and the other immortals first, and then you yourself will be the better for it, if you drink” (6.259–260). The order is the custom: before men drink, the gods' share is poured out (see [[libation]] and [[wine]]). Book 6 has both of Homer's verbs for it, σπένδω, “pour a libation” (σπείσῃς, 6.259), and λείβω, “pour in drops” (λείβειν, 6.266).

**Not with these hands.** Hector refuses: “with unwashed hands I shrink from pouring sparkling wine to Zeus” (χερσὶ δʼ ἀνίπτοισιν Διὶ λείβειν αἴθοπα οἶνον ἅζομαι, 6.266–267). ἅζομαι is the awe that holds a man back from what is not permitted: Chryses asked the Achaeans to free his daughter “in reverence for the son of Zeus, far-shooting Apollo” (ἁζόμενοι, 1.21). A man who has come from killing cannot make an offering until he is clean (see [[hand-washing]]).

**The cup in Book 24.** Hecuba's cup comes back. When Priam is setting out for Achilles' hut, she comes to him “holding in her right hand wine sweet to the heart” (μελίφρονα, Hector's word) in a golden cup, “so that they might pour a libation before they went”, and tells him to pour to father Zeus and pray that he may come home (24.283–298). Priam has a handmaid pour water over his hands, washes, takes the cup from his wife, and prays standing in the middle of the courtyard as he pours (24.302–306). The son refused the cup his mother held out, and the father takes it on his way to ransom the son's body. See [[hecuba]] and [[priam]].
