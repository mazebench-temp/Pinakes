**The seer.** “Helenus, son of Priam, by far the best of the bird-seers” (Πριαμίδης Ἕλενος οἰωνοπόλων ὄχʼ ἄριστος, 6.76). The second half of the verse introduced Calchas in Book 1 (1.69): each army has its best bird-seer. οἰωνοπόλος is one who is busied with birds of omen (οἰωνοί). Here Helenus reads no birds. He gives counsel, for the battle and for the city, and both are followed (6.77–102; see [[helenus]]). In Book 7 he understands in his heart the plan the gods have agreed on, and says that he heard their voice (7.44–53).

**Portents.** Bellerophon killed the Chimaera “obeying the portents of the gods” (6.183), in the words used of Tydeus sparing Maeon when he killed the others who lay in wait for him (4.398; see [[ambush]]). The heroes of the older stories act on signs from the gods (see [[teras]] and [[bellerophon]]).

**Oracles.** Andromache cannot say why the best of the Achaeans have three times attacked the wall by the fig tree: “perhaps someone well skilled in oracles told them of it, or perhaps their own heart urges them on and bids them” (6.438–439). θεοπρόπιον is an oracle or a prophecy (see [[theopropion]]). She sets a god's knowledge and the men's own heart side by side, as possible causes, without choosing between them (see [[the-fig-tree]] and [[andromaches-advice]]).
