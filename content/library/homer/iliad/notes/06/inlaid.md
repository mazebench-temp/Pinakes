---
summary: Hecuba's robes from Sidon are “richly embroidered,” and Paris puts on his armor “inlaid with bronze” to go back to the war.
grc: [παμποίκιλα]
en: [richly embroidered]
---

παμποίκιλος, “all-embroidered,” is ποικίλος with πᾶν (“all”) prefixed. Hecuba's storeroom holds “her robes, richly embroidered, the work of women of the Sidonians” (πέπλοι παμποίκιλα ἔργα γυναικῶν, 6.289–290; [[sidonians]], [[robe]], [[fragrant]]), and she chooses the one “most beautiful in its embroidery” (ποικίλμασιν, 6.294). The Odyssey uses the word once, of the robes that Helen made with her own hands (Od. 15.105), in lines that repeat the choice of Hecuba's robe.

At the end of the book Paris arms: “when he had put on his glorious armor, inlaid with bronze, he hurried through the city” (κλυτὰ τεύχεα ποικίλα χαλκῷ, 6.504–505; [[famous]], [[paris]]). In Book 3 the armies' inlaid armor lay on the ground for the truce (3.327). The armor Hector found him handling in his chamber is now on him ([[perikalles]]).
