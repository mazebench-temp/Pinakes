**The best of the Thracians.** The Catalogue named Acamas and Peirous as the leaders of the Thracians, “all those whom the strong-flowing Hellespont encloses” (2.844–845), and gave Acamas no father. His death gives him one, and a description: “the son of Eussorus, Acamas, tall and brave” (6.8; see [[eussorus]]). ἠΰς τε μέγας τε, “tall and brave”, is praise given to warriors of both sides (2.653, 3.226, 11.221). Peirous fell at the end of the first battle (4.517–531; see [[peirous]]), so with Acamas both leaders of the Thracians are dead. When Thracians are next seen at Troy, they are newcomers, camped apart under their king Rhesus (10.434–435; see [[thracians]]).

**The first kill again.** The blow is told in the three lines that told the first kill of the Iliad, when Antilochus struck Echepolus: the spear strikes the ridge of the horsehair-crested helmet, fixes in the forehead and pierces into the bone, “and darkness covered his eyes” (6.9–11 = 4.459–461; see [[echepolus]] and [[darkness-covered-his-eyes]]). The gods have left the field (6.1), and the fighting starts again as it started in Book 4.

**Ares' mask.** In Book 5 Ares took his likeness to rouse the sons of Priam (5.462; see the Book 5 note). The man whose face the god of war wore is the first to die when the gods have gone.
