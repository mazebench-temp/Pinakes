summary: Glaucus and Diomedes come together between the armies, and the verse that brings duelists face to face is followed by a question, “Who are you?” (6.121–123). The duel ends in friendship.
grc: ["σχεδὸν ἦσαν ἐπʼ ἀλλήλοισιν ἰόντε"]
passages:
  - 6.119-143 | Diomedes challenges Glaucus
---

“And when they had come close, advancing against each other, Diomedes, good at the war cry, spoke to him first” (6.121–122). The formula is in the dual here, ἰόντε, for two men who have stepped out “between the two armies, eager to fight” (6.120). Elsewhere the first move is usually a spear cast or a taunt (see [[when-they-came-close]]). Diomedes' first words are a question, “Who are you, good sir, among mortal men?” (6.123), and they lead not to a fight but to Glaucus' account of his lineage and to a friendship between the two (6.145–236; see [[genealogy-in-battle]] and [[glaucus-and-diomedes]]).

The same verse, followed by the same “spoke to him first” (τὸν πρότερος προσέειπε), brings Achilles face to face with Aeneas and with Asteropaeus, and each time a genealogy follows; Achilles too asks Asteropaeus who he is (20.176–177, 21.148–150). Those meetings end in fighting; this one ends in a handshake and an exchange of armor (6.232–236; see [[guest-gifts]]).
