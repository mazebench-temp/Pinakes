**Alexander's blind folly.** Helen asks Hector to come in and sit down:

{{quote:68891035-1f22-566e-874e-02ece79cf6f4}}

She names two causes of the war, and herself first. εἵνεκʼ ἐμεῖο κυνὸς καὶ Ἀλεξάνδρου ἕνεκʼ ἄτης, “for my sake, dog that I am, and for Alexander's blind folly”, is built like Menelaus' line in Book 3, where he says that both armies have suffered “because of my quarrel and because Alexander began it” (εἵνεκʼ ἐμῆς ἔριδος καὶ Ἀλεξάνδρου ἕνεκʼ ἀρχῆς, 3.100). An ancient variant read ἄτης there too.

The half-line returns at the start of the last book. Hera, Poseidon and Athena keep their hatred of Ilios “because of Alexander's blind folly, who insulted the goddesses when they came to his steading” (24.28–29). Book 24 ties the folly to the Judgment of Paris. Helen does not say what it was, and in her mouth it is most naturally the voyage that brought her to Troy (see [[the-abduction-of-helen]]). She does not use it to excuse herself. The folly is Paris', the evil fate is from Zeus (see [[moira]]), and she takes the blame for herself as well (see [[who-is-to-blame]] and [[dog-face]]).

The Odyssey tells it from the other side. Home again in Sparta, Helen remembers how in Troy she lamented “the blind folly that Aphrodite gave, when she led me there from my dear native land” (*Odyssey* 4.261–262), and Penelope, excusing her, says that a god roused her to the shameful deed and that she had not foreseen the ἄτη (*Odyssey* 23.222–224). There the blind folly is Helen's own.

**Glaucus.** The word does not appear in the exchange of armor, but its language does. “Zeus, son of Cronus, took away Glaucus' wits” (φρένας ἐξέλετο, 6.234), and he gave gold for bronze. Achilles says the same of Agamemnon in Book 9 (φρένας εἵλετο, 9.377), and Agamemnon says it of himself when he names his ἄτη before the army: “but since I was blinded, and Zeus took away my wits” (ἀλλʼ ἐπεὶ ἀασάμην καί μευ φρένας ἐξέλετο Ζεύς, 19.137). How seriously the narrator means the comment on Glaucus is disputed (see [[phrenes]] and [[gold-and-silver]]).
