---
summary: Glaucus begins the story of his family in “Ephyra, in a corner of horse-pasturing Argos.”
---

“There is a city, Ephyra, in a corner of horse-pasturing Argos, and there lived Sisyphus” (6.152–153; [[ephyra-in-argos]], [[sisyphus]]). The half-verse μυχῷ Ἄργεος ἱπποβότοιο is used in the Odyssey of Aegisthus, who stayed at ease “in a corner of horse-pasturing Argos” beguiling Clytemnestra (Od. 3.263). Here Argos has its wide sense, the Peloponnese or the Argolid rather than the city (see [[argos]]). Later writers identified this Ephyra with Corinth. The Lycian ally of Troy traces his line to Argos, and the Argive Diomedes answers that they are guest-friends: “So now I am your dear guest-friend in the heart of Argos” (6.224; [[xenia]], [[glaucus-and-diomedes]]).
