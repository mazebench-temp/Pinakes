Νεστορίδης, “the son of Nestor” (6.33), stands alone at the head of the verse after his name, as it will again when he runs from Hector in Book 15 (15.589; see [[nestor]]). In Book 4 he came into the poem with no father; in Book 5 he was “the son of great-hearted Nestor” (5.565); here the patronymic is enough. δουρὶ φαεινῷ, “with his shining spear” (6.32), is the whole description of the kill (see [[ablerus]]).

Father and son act within a few lines of each other. Antilochus kills his man, and after the death of Adrastus Nestor tells the army to go on killing and leave the spoils for later (6.66–71). In Book 4 the old man left the spears to the younger men and kept counsel for himself (4.322–325); here each does his part.
