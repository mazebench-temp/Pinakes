---
summary: Paris tells Hector that Helen has persuaded him to go back to the war “with gentle words”; the adjective here is μαλακός, “soft.”
grc: [μαλακοῖς ἐπέεσσιν]
---

“But just now my wife, persuading me with gentle words, spurred me to the war” (παρειποῦσʼ … μαλακοῖς ἐπέεσσιν, 6.337–338; [[helen]], [[paris]]). μαλακός (“soft”) is another word for the ἀγανά ἔπεα of this entry, and the translation gives both the same English. Paris' account of her words does not match what Helen says a few lines later, when she calls him a man whose mind is not steadfast (6.350–353; [[soothing-words]]). The verb παρειποῦσα, “persuading,” is the one used of Agamemnon turning his brother's mind at the start of the book (παρειπών, 6.62).
