**Eleven cubits.** Hector comes into his brother's house armed:

{{quote:838d4d0c-32a1-5d44-b2b0-2537c61b1a9b}}

ἑνδεκάπηχυς, “eleven cubits long”: a πῆχυς, “forearm”, is a cubit, something under half a meter, so that the spear is nearly five meters long. Ajax's pike for fighting at the ships is twice as long, twenty-two cubits (15.677–678). πόρκης, the “ring” around the point, is a band that bound the bronze head to the shaft; the word occurs only in these verses and where they return. At nightfall in Book 8 Hector addresses the Trojans leaning on the same spear, described in the same words (8.493–496; see [[hector]]). He carries it into the chamber where Paris is busy with his armor and Helen sits among her women (6.321–324; see [[paris]]).

**Planted in the earth.** When Diomedes learns that Glaucus is his guest-friend, “he fixed his spear in the bountiful earth” (6.213). A spear driven upright into the ground, on the spike at its butt, is a spear at rest: Diomedes' companions sleep with theirs standing so beside them in Book 10 (10.152–154), and in the truce of Book 3 the armies sit with their long spears fixed beside them (3.135). “Let us avoid each other's spears, even in the throng” (6.226; see [[guest-gifts]] and [[xenia]]).

**Break his spear.** Theano asks Athena to “break the spear of Diomedes, and grant that he himself may fall headlong before the Scaean gates” (6.306–307). The spear stands for the man, and Athena, who guided it all through Book 5, will not break it (5.290–291, 5.855–857; see [[prayer-type-scene]]).

**In the rout.** The book opens with the armies aiming their “bronze-fitted spears” at one another (χαλκήρεα δοῦρα, 6.3). Menelaus stands over Adrastus “holding his long-shadowed spear” (6.44), and Agamemnon, having stabbed him, “set his heel on his chest and pulled out the ash spear” (6.65), as Ajax set his heel on the dead Amphius to draw out his spear in Book 5 (5.620–621). Hector rallies the Trojans “brandishing his sharp spears” in the verses that described his rally in Book 5 (6.103–105 = 5.494–496; see [[exhortation]]).
