---
summary: Andromache tells the sack of Thebe from inside the city. Achilles killed her father Eetion but honored his body, killed her seven brothers on one day among their herds, and took her mother captive and freed her for a ransom (6.414–428).
passages:
  - 6.414-428 | Andromache's family
---

In Book 1 Achilles told the sack of Thebe to Thetis as a victor: the Achaeans sacked the city, brought the spoil to the ships, and chose Chryseis from it for Agamemnon (1.366–369). In Book 2 the narrator named Thebe beside Lyrnessus among his conquests (2.690–691). In Book 6 the story is told by a daughter of the city's king. Andromache is begging Hector not to leave her, and she explains why he is all she has (see [[hector-and-andromache]] and [[andromache]]):

{{quote:20d38ae9-a6e4-5cbe-98c4-a84f11d67ad4}}

**Eetion.** Achilles killed the king, “but he did not strip him, for his heart held back in awe from that” (σεβάσσατο γὰρ τό γε θυμῷ, 6.417). He burned him with his intricate armor and heaped a mound over him, and the mountain nymphs, daughters of Zeus, planted elms around it (6.417–420; see [[eetion]], [[nymphs]] and [[tomb]]). The same half-line tells how Proetus shrank from killing Bellerophon (6.167; see [[bellerophon-in-lycia]] and [[sebas]]). Stripping the armor was the victor's right, and burning a man with his armor was a funeral honor: Elpenor asks Odysseus for it, and receives it (*Odyssey* 11.74, 12.13). The man who gave it to Eetion will drag Hector's body behind his chariot (22.395–404).

**The brothers.**

{{quote:2ab282c3-c19f-5c9b-a3ba-c96b517a38f7}}

ἰῷ ἤματι is “on a single day”: ἰῷ is the dative of ἴος, “one”, not the word for an arrow. The sons of the king were out with the herds, the wealth of the house, when the raider came (see [[cattle]], [[sheep]] and [[raiding]]).

**The mother.** Eetion's queen, “who was queen beneath wooded Placus”, Achilles brought to the camp “with the rest of the possessions”, and then set free, “taking a boundless ransom”; and “in her father's halls Artemis of the showering arrows struck her down” (6.425–428; see [[placus]], [[apoina]] and [[artemis]]). A death that comes without a long sickness is the work of Artemis' “gentle arrows” for a woman (*Odyssey* 11.171–173), and of Apollo's and hers together (*Odyssey* 15.409–411); Apollo killed Niobe's sons and Artemis her daughters (24.605–606). Achilles took ransom for captives then, as he says himself: before Patroclus died he spared many Trojans and sold them (21.100–102), and he had released two sons of Priam for ransom (11.104–106).

**The spoils.** The sack of Thebe is remembered in the things Achilles took from it. When the envoys come to his hut in Book 9, he is playing a lyre with a silver crossbar: “he had taken it from the spoils when he destroyed the city of Eetion” (9.186–189). His trace horse Pedasus, a mortal horse that runs beside the immortal ones, was taken “when he captured the city of Eetion” (16.152–154). At the funeral games of Patroclus he sets out as a prize the lump of iron “which the mighty Eetion used to throw”, taken with the rest of the possessions (23.826–829). And Andromache, who was born at Thebe under Placus in the house of Eetion (22.479–480), left it as a bride before the sack: Hector led her from Eetion's house “when he gave countless bride-gifts” (22.471–472).

**Hector is everything.** Andromache ends her account: “Hector, you are father to me, and lady mother, and brother, and you are my husband in his prime” (6.429–430). The war took her family, and the same enemy will take her husband (see [[achilles-raids]] and [[captive-women]]).
