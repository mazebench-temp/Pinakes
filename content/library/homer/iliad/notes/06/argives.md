summary: Nestor calls to the Argives to kill first and strip the dead later (6.66), and when Hector rallies the Trojans the Argives give ground, thinking that a god has come down to help them (6.107–109). In Glaucus' story the name has its older sense, the people of Argos under Proetus (6.159).
---

Nestor speaks to the army by this name after the killing of Adrastus (Ἀργείοισιν, 6.66), though his address itself calls them Danaans (6.67; see [[nestor]] and [[danaans]]). When Hector rallies the Trojans, “the Argives gave ground and ceased from slaughter” (6.107), and thought that one of the immortals had come down to help the Trojans (6.108–109; see [[trojans]]). The book's fighting ends there.

In Glaucus' story of his grandfather the word keeps its first sense, the men of Argos. Proetus drove Bellerophon out, the translation reads, because he was “far the mightier among the Argives”, whom Zeus had made subject to his scepter (6.158–159; on the other readings of the lines see [[proetus]]). The adjective describes Helen, “Argive Helen”, when Hector finds her among her women (6.323; see [[argive-helen]]).
