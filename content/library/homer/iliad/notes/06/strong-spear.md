---
summary: ἄλκιμος praises two men in Book 6: Diomedes, “the valiant son of Tydeus,” in Andromache's list, and Paris, whom Hector grants to be valiant though he holds back.
grc: [ἄλκιμον υἱόν, ἄλκιμός ἐσσι]
---

The adjective of the strong spear and the valiant heart describes men in Book 6. Andromache names the best of the Achaeans who have tried the wall by the fig tree, “with the sons of Atreus and the valiant son of Tydeus” (6.437; [[son-of-tydeus]], [[the-fig-tree]]). Hector's last words to Paris grant his brother the same quality and refuse him credit for it: “no man who was fair-minded would dishonor your work in battle, since you are valiant; but you hold back of your own will and do not want to fight” (6.521–523; [[strange-one]], [[paris]]). ἄλκιμος is formed from ἀλκή, “strength, courage” ([[alke]]), the courage that Hector told the army to remember (6.112; [[furious]]).
