---
summary: Glaucus addresses Diomedes as “great-hearted son of Tydeus”; the synonym μεγαλήτωρ names two fathers, Priam in Hector's curse on Paris and Eetion in the verse that names Andromache.
grc: [μεγάθυμε]
---

Glaucus' answer to the challenge begins with courtesy: “Great-hearted son of Tydeus, why do you ask about my lineage?” (6.145; [[son-of-tydeus]], [[genee]]).

As in Book 5, the synonym μεγαλήτωρ names fathers. Hector wishes the earth would gape for Paris, whom the Olympian raised “as a great bane to the Trojans and to great-hearted Priam and his sons” (6.282–283; [[may-the-earth-gape]], [[priam]]). And Andromache is “the daughter of great-hearted Eetion, Eetion who lived beneath wooded Placus” (6.395–396; [[eetion]], [[wooded]]). The fathers of the husband and of the wife are both great-hearted kings: Achilles has already destroyed the city of one (6.414–416), and Hector foresees the fall of the other's (6.447–449).
