---
summary: Two long-shadowed spears are held and not thrown in Book 6: Menelaus' over the fallen Adrastus, and Diomedes' in his challenge to Glaucus, who has stood up to it.
---

The spear of the formal duels is not thrown in this book. Menelaus stands over Adrastus “holding his long-shadowed spear” (6.44), and Adrastus clasps his knees and begs to be taken alive (6.45–50; [[adrastus-the-trojan]], [[son-of-atreus]]). Diomedes tells the stranger who faces him that he has come out in front of all “in your daring, since you have stood up to my long-shadowed spear” (6.126; [[good-sir]]). The spear that elsewhere opens a duel opens a conversation. By its end Diomedes has fixed it in the ground, and the two men exchange armor instead of blows (6.213, 6.230–236; [[bountiful-earth]], [[glaucus-and-diomedes]]).
