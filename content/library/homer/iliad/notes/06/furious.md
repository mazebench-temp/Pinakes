---
summary: Hector's exhortation ends with the verse of furious courage as he leaves the army to go to the city.
---

“Be men, my friends, and remember your furious courage, while I go up to Ilios” (6.112–113; [[exhortation]], [[high-hearted]]). The verse ends exhortations throughout the poem (for example Il. 15.734), and Agamemnon began his review with the same phrase of courage (4.234). Here it is spoken by a leader who is leaving the fighting: Hector tells the men to remember their courage while he goes to have the elders and the women pray (6.113–115; [[alke]]). In the city he refuses his mother's wine for fear that he will “forget my courage” (ἀλκῆς τε λάθωμαι, 6.265; [[honey-sweet]]). The courage he asks the army to remember is the thing he guards in himself ([[hector]]).
