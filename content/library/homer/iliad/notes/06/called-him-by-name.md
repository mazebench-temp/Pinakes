**Holding on.** “And she clung to his hand, and spoke to him, and called him by name” (ἔν τʼ ἄρα οἱ φῦ χειρὶ ἔπος τʼ ἔφατʼ ἔκ τʼ ὀνόμαζε): the whole verse greets Hector twice in the city, once from his mother in Priam's house (6.253) and once from his wife at the Scaean gates (6.406). ἐμφύω is to grow into, to cling. Each woman holds him by the hand, and each asks him to stay: Hecuba until she has brought him wine (6.258), Andromache on the tower, away from the fighting (6.431). The verse is used again when Hera takes Sleep by the hand to ask a favor (14.232), when Charis and Hephaestus welcome Thetis (18.384, 18.423), and when Thetis comes to Achilles with his new armor (19.7). See [[hecuba]], [[andromache]] and [[visit-scene]].

**Comfort.** Hector answers his wife with the other whole-verse form: “and stroked her with his hand, and spoke to her, and called her by name” (χειρί τέ μιν κατέρεξεν ἔπος τʼ ἔφατʼ ἔκ τʼ ὀνόμαζε, 6.485). It is the verse of Thetis comforting Achilles (1.361, 24.127) and of Dione comforting Aphrodite (5.372). In the other three it is a mother's gesture. Here a husband comforts his wife, and what he says is “Strange one, do not grieve too much in your heart for me” (6.486; see [[strange-one]], [[husbands-and-wives]] and [[hector-and-andromache]]).
