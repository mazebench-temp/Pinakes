summary: With the gods gone from the field, ten Achaean leaders kill fourteen Trojans in thirty-two verses, and Agamemnon kills a fifteenth, the suppliant Adrastus; no Achaean dies in Book 6 (6.5–65). The list opens with a full killing scene and a victim's life and ends in a run of names, one kill to a half-verse.

**The shape of the list.** Ares has left the battle and Athena has gone home, and “the dread din of battle between Trojans and Achaeans was left to itself” (6.1). What follows is a rout told as a list. It begins at full length. Ajax kills Acamas, the best of the Thracians, and the blow is told in the three verses that told the first kill of the poem: the helmet's ridge, the forehead, the bone, the darkness (6.9–11 = 4.459–461; see [[darkness-covered-his-eyes]] and [[acamas-of-thrace]]). Then a victim's life:

{{quote:ede2cb55-9eac-5da2-b257-286bc1d25213}}

Axylus welcomed every traveler, and none of them was there to stand in front of him. The kindness that made him known is the thing that could not save him (see [[axylus]], [[xenia]] and [[no-help-to-you]]). Next come parentage and a birth: Euryalus kills Dresus and Opheltius, and then the twins Aesepus and Pedasus, whom the Naiad Abarbarea bore to Bucolion, the eldest son of Laomedon, born in secret, who lay with her while he was tending his sheep (6.20–28; see [[bucolion]], [[abarbarea]] and [[aesepus-son-of-bucolion]]). Simoeisius in Book 4 and the twin sons of Diocles in Book 5 were given such lives (4.473–479, 5.541–549). After that the list quickens. Polypoetes kills Astyalus, Odysseus kills Pidytes of Percote, Teucer Aretaon, Antilochus Ablerus, Agamemnon Elatus, who lived in steep Pedasus by the Satnioeis, Leitus Phylacus “as he fled”, and Eurypylus Melanthius: seven men in eight verses, each with only his name, a town or a single detail (6.29–36).

**The fifteenth.** The list breaks off for a man who is taken alive. Adrastus' killing becomes a supplication, a rebuke and a death, and Nestor draws the lesson for the army: kill now, strip later (6.37–71; see [[supplication]] and [[stripping-the-dead]]).

**One side only.** Every killer in the battle of Book 6 is an Achaean, and every victim a Trojan or an ally. The rout nearly carries the Trojans back into the city (6.73–74; see [[would-have-had-not]]). After Hector's rally no one is killed in the rest of the book, except in the stories its speakers tell. The next meeting of champions, Diomedes and Glaucus, ends in friendship instead of a kill (6.119–236; see [[genealogy-in-battle]]). See [[the-war-in-small]] and [[death-in-battle]].
