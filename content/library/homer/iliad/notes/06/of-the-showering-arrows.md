---
summary: Artemis “of the showering arrows” struck down Andromache's mother in her father's halls after Achilles had ransomed her.
---

“And my mother, who was queen beneath wooded Placus, he brought here with the rest of the possessions, and then he set her free, taking a boundless ransom, and in her father's halls Artemis of the showering arrows struck her down” (6.425–428; [[wooded]], [[boundless-ransom]], [[sack-of-thebe]]). In Book 5 the epithet came with Artemis twice and she did not shoot (5.53, 5.447). Here she does, and it is her work as Homer knows it: the sudden death of a woman, at home ([[artemis]], [[arrows]]). Earlier in the book Glaucus has told how she killed Bellerophon's daughter in her anger, under another epithet ([[of-the-golden-reins]]). Andromache has lost father, mother and brothers, and Hector is all of them to her (6.429–430; [[queenly]]).
