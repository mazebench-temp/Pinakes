---
summary: Hector foresees Andromache in Argos weaving at the loom at another woman's bidding, and then sends her home to her own loom and distaff (6.456, 6.490–491).
passages:
  - 6.454-458 | Andromache at another woman's loom
---

Two looms stand in Hector's last words to Andromache. The first is in Argos: “and in Argos you may weave at the loom at another woman's bidding” (πρὸς ἄλλης ἱστὸν ὑφαίνοις, 6.456). πρὸς ἄλλης, “at the bidding of another woman”, makes her a slave in a stranger's house, the fate Agamemnon promised Chryseis (1.29–31; see [[captive-women]]). The second is her own: “attend to your own work, the loom and the distaff” (ἱστόν τʼ ἠλακάτην τε, 6.490–491). The same word, ἱστός, is the upright loom and the web on it, as in Helen's “great web” (3.125). It is also the ship's mast, and these lines are not the mast's (see [[mast]]). In Book 22 Andromache is at her loom when she hears the wailing from the tower, and the shuttle falls from her hand (22.440–448; see [[weaving]] and [[andromache]]).
