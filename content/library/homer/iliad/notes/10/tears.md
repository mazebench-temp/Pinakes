summary: The only tears of Book 10 are Dolon's. Caught and held by the hands, “he burst into tears and said” the plea for ransom; the night that began with the king groaning and tearing his hair ends without a tear on the Achaean side.
passages:
  - 10.372-381 | Dolon caught
---

Book 9 was a night of tears: Agamemnon weeping “like a spring of dark water” before the assembly (9.13–15), Phoenix bursting into tears for the ships (9.432–433; see the note on Book 9). In Book 10 the king's grief takes other forms. He groans as thick and fast as Zeus' lightning and tears out hairs from his head “to Zeus on high” (10.5–16; see [[agamemnon]]), but he does not weep.

The tears come from the enemy. When Diomedes' spear flies over his shoulder and the two spies catch up with him and seize his hands, Dolon, teeth chattering and pale with fear, “burst into tears and said” (δακρύσας ἔπος ηὔδα, 10.377), and begs to be taken alive for ransom (10.378–381; see [[apoina]] and [[deos]]). The participle δακρύσας is used only once elsewhere in the Iliad, of Achilles withdrawing from his companions to weep by the sea after Briseis is taken (1.348–349). The suppliant's tears, like the hero's, are an appeal, and here they go unanswered (see [[supplication]] and [[pity]]). Hippocoon, waking to the empty place and the dying men, groans aloud (ᾤμωξεν, 10.522), but the book has no lament for its dead.
