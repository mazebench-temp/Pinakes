---
summary: Odysseus and Diomedes both begin their prayers to Athena with the title, Diomedes in a shortened form, and Nestor gives it to her when the two return.
grc: ["Διὸς τέκος Ἀτρυτώνη", "κούρη τʼ αἰγιόχοιο Διὸς"]
---

Odysseus prays with the full formula, “Hear me, child of aegis-bearing Zeus” (10.278), the opening of the verse in which Diomedes prayed to her in Book 5 (5.115). Diomedes, praying second, has only “child of Zeus, Atrytone” (10.284; [[atrytone]]): his verse begins “Now hear me too,” and the epithet of the father drops out. At the end of the night Nestor names her in another order, κούρη τʼ αἰγιόχοιο Διὸς γλαυκῶπις Ἀθήνη, “and the daughter of aegis-bearing Zeus, gray-eyed Athena,” who loves the two men with Zeus (10.552–553; [[gray-eyed]], [[aegis-bearing]]). See [[athena]] and [[prayer]].
