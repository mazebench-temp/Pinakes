---
summary: Book 10 looks back on the second day of battle from the night that follows it. Agamemnon laments that no man ever did so much harm in a day as Hector (10.47–52), the Trojans' fires burn near the ships (10.11–13, 10.160–161), and the Achaean chiefs hold their council beyond the ditch, on ground clear of corpses where Hector turned back at nightfall (10.194–202).
passages:
  - 10.43-52 | The day measured
  - 10.194-202 | Where Hector turned back
---

**The day measured.** Agamemnon tells Menelaus what the day has done: “For I never yet saw, nor heard anyone tell, / that one man devised so many grim deeds in a single day / as Hector, dear to Zeus, has done to the sons of the Achaeans, / just as he is, the dear son of no goddess and of no god” (10.47–50). ἐπʼ ἤματι, “in a single day”, is the measure of Book 8, the day that Zeus gave to Hector (see [[hector]] and [[plan-of-zeus]]). The harm will last, he says, “long and for many a day” (10.52).

**The camp on the plain.** At nightfall Hector kept the Trojans on the plain and ordered fires to burn all night, so that the Achaeans could not slip away by sea (8.497–511), and a thousand fires shone between the ships and the Xanthus like the stars around the moon (8.553–563; see [[stars-around-the-moon]]). Those are the fires at which Agamemnon marvels in the night, “the many fires that burned before Ilios”, with the sound of flutes and pipes (10.11–13), and Nestor rouses Diomedes with the same picture: “Do you not hear how the Trojans are camped on the rise of the plain / near the ships, and only a little space still holds them off?” (10.160–161). Each side fears what the other may do in the dark. Hector built his fires “in case even by night the long-haired Achaeans / set out to flee” (8.510–511); Agamemnon fears that the enemy “may be eager to fight even by night” (10.101), and Hector sends his spy to learn whether the Achaeans are planning flight (10.309–312; see [[war-and-night]] and [[the-doloneia]]).

**Where Hector turned back.** The chiefs cross the ditch and sit down “in a clear space, where the ground showed free of corpses / of the fallen; from there mighty Hector had turned back again / from killing the Argives, when night covered all around” (10.198–201). The first of these verses placed Hector's own assembly at nightfall, by the river, away from the ships (8.489–491); here it marks the line of his furthest advance, outside the Achaean ditch. Hector said then that darkness came first and saved the Argives and their ships (8.500–501). Why the leaders go out beyond the ditch to sit there is not said (see the translation's note on 10.199–201 and [[the-achaean-wall]]).
