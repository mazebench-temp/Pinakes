---
summary: Odysseus answers Dolon's confession “smiling at him”: the spy's heart was reaching for great gifts, the horses of Achilles, which no mortal but Achilles can master (10.400–404).
---

The smile follows Dolon's account of what Hector promised him, the horses and the chariot of Achilles, and of the orders he was given (10.391–399). It grants nothing. Agamemnon's smile took back a rebuke (4.356), and Zeus' gave Athena leave to help the Achaeans with counsel (8.38); Odysseus' smile leads to more questions (10.405–411; see [[give-me-an-exact-account]]). Its object is the size of the prize. Hector offered his spy “a great gift” (δώρῳ ἔπι μεγάλῳ, 10.304), and Odysseus answers that Dolon's heart was reaching for great gifts, μεγάλων δώρων, the horses that no mortal man but Achilles can master and drive (10.401–404; see [[the-horses-of-achilles]]).

The other captor speaks once, “looking at him darkly” (10.446; see [[looking-darkly]]), and kills him.
