---
summary: Book 10 is a night of toil in place of sleep. Agamemnon says that Zeus has plunged him “into toils without end” and tells his brother “let us toil ourselves”; Nestor blames Menelaus for leaving the toil to the king; Diomedes tells Nestor that he “never ceases from toil”; and Odysseus, eager “in every toil”, prays to Athena, who stands by him “in all my toils”.

The word comes eight times in the first three hundred lines of the book, more often than in any book before it, and it is the opposite of the sleep that the others enjoy (see [[hypnos]]).

**The king's toil.** Agamemnon tells Menelaus to wake the chiefs without pride, “but let us toil ourselves as well; so, it seems, at our birth Zeus laid heavy misery on us” (πονεώμεθα, 10.70–71). To Nestor he names himself as the man “whom above all men Zeus has plunged into toils without end, as long as breath stays in my breast” (10.88–90). The toil is the king's share, set on him by Zeus (see [[gods-and-mortals]] and [[agamemnon]]).

**Who toils.** Nestor's blame of Menelaus is about toil: he is sleeping “and has left the toil to you alone. Now he should be toiling among all the chiefs” (πονέεσθαι twice, 10.116–117). Agamemnon grants that his brother often “is unwilling to toil”, out of deference, not sloth, and says that tonight he woke first (10.121–124; see [[menelaus]] and [[praise-and-blame]]). Diomedes, woken by the old man's heel, says it of Nestor with exasperated admiration: “You are a hard man, old sir; you never cease from toil” (σὺ μὲν πόνου οὔ ποτε λήγεις, 10.164; see [[nestor]] and [[neikos]]).

**Odysseus' toils.** Diomedes chooses Odysseus “whose heart and proud spirit are eager above all others in every toil” (ἐν πάντεσσι πόνοισι, 10.244–245), and Odysseus, praying at the heron, uses the same words of Athena: “you who always stand by me in all my toils” (ἥ τέ μοι αἰεὶ ἐν πάντεσσι πόνοισι παρίστασαι, 10.278–279; see [[reciprocity-with-the-gods]]). In the Odyssey the goddess answers him in the same verse when she reveals herself on Ithaca: “Pallas Athena, daughter of Zeus, who always stand by you in all your toils and guard you” (*Odyssey* 13.300–301; see [[athena]] and [[odysseus]]).
