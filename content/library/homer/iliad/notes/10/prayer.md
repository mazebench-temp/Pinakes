summary: Before the raid Odysseus and Diomedes pray in turn to Athena, who has sent them a heron: Odysseus for a return with glory, Diomedes with his father's story and the vow of a heifer, and she hears them (10.277–295). Holding up Dolon's spoils, Odysseus prays to her again as “goddess of the spoil” (10.460–464).
except: [10.368]

  - 10.460-464 | Odysseus offers Dolon's spoils to Athena

**Two prayers.** The heron Athena sends on the right is heard and not seen, and Odysseus “rejoiced at the bird, and prayed to Athena” (10.274–277; see [[heron]]); then Diomedes “prayed second” (10.283). Between them they divide the words of Diomedes' prayer in Book 5 (5.115–120; see [[prayer-type-scene]]). Odysseus has its invocation, κλῦθί μευ αἰγιόχοιο Διὸς τέκος, “Hear me, child of aegis-bearing Zeus” (10.278; see [[hear-me]]), and its request, νῦν αὖτε … με φῖλαι Ἀθήνη, “now again love me above all, Athena” (10.280; 5.117). Diomedes has its title for the goddess, Atrytone, and its claim, her help to his father (10.284–290; see [[atrytone]]).

**Odysseus' claim.** He claims no sacrifice and no past favor, only her presence: “you who always stand by me in all my toils, and whatever I do you see it” (10.278–280). The phrase ἐν πάντεσσι πόνοισι, “in all toils”, is the one with which Diomedes has just chosen him, as the man “whose heart and proud spirit are eager above all others in every toil, and Pallas Athena loves him” (10.244–245), and in the Odyssey the goddess uses it of herself when she reveals herself to him on Ithaca: “Pallas Athena, daughter of Zeus, who always stand by you and guard you in all your toils” (Od. 13.300–301). He asks for glory: “grant that we come back to the ships with glory, having done a great deed that the Trojans will rue” (10.281–282; see [[kleos]]).

**Diomedes' claim and vow.** Diomedes asks her to go with him as she went with Tydeus to Thebes, and tells of the embassy and the “very grim deeds” done on the way back (10.285–290; see [[tydeus-at-thebes]]). His request, “So now be willing to stand by me, and guard me” (παρίσταο καί με φύλασσε, 10.291), is what Athena in the Odyssey says she does for Odysseus (παρίσταμαι ἠδὲ φυλάσσω, Od. 13.301). He adds a vow, a yearling heifer with gold poured around her horns, in the three verses of Nestor's prayer to Athena at Pylos (10.292–294 = Od. 3.382–384; see [[sacrifice]]).

**Heard.** “So they spoke in prayer, and Pallas Athena heard them” (10.295) is the verse that closed Diomedes' prayer in Book 5, in the plural (5.121). The answer comes in the deeds of the night. She puts might into Diomedes as he chases Dolon (10.366–368) and breathes might into him among the Thracians (10.482), and she warns him to go (10.507–511; see [[athena]]).

**With the spoils.** Odysseus holds Dolon's spoils high in his hand to Athena, “goddess of the spoil”, and prays: “Rejoice in these, goddess; for you, first on Olympus of all the immortals, we will call upon; but once again guide us to the horses and the beds of the Thracian men” (10.460–464; see [[of-the-spoil]] and [[dedication-of-spoils]]). The gift is the claim, and the request follows. ἐπιδωσόμεθα is taken as “we will call upon”, like θεοὺς ἐπιδώμεθα, “let us call the gods to witness” (22.254), or as “we will give gifts”.

**A boast.** At 10.368 ἐπευξάμενος is not a prayer: Athena strengthens Diomedes so that no other Achaean “might boast that he struck him first” (see [[euchomai]]). That line is not counted here.
