---
summary: The bull of Book 10 is leather. Thrasymedes puts on Diomedes' head a skullcap “of bull's hide, without ridge or crest”, the kind worn by young men in their prime (10.257–259).
grc: [ταυρείην]
en: [bull's hide]
---

ἀμφὶ δέ οἱ κυνέην κεφαλῆφιν ἔθηκε / ταυρείην, ἄφαλόν τε καὶ ἄλλοφον, ἥ τε καταῖτυξ / κέκληται, “and he put a helmet on his head, / of bull's hide, without ridge or crest, the kind called a skullcap” (10.257–258). ταύρειος, “of bull's hide”, is said elsewhere in the Iliad of shields, the bull's-hide shield of Deiphobus that breaks Meriones' spear (13.161–163) and the shield of bull's hide under which Hector covers his shoulders (16.360). Here it is a light cap for the night, with no metal ridge and no plume to catch the light or the eye (see [[helmet]] and the translation's note on 10.257–259). The night's arming is in skin and leather: a lion's skin for Diomedes, a boar's-tusk helmet for Odysseus, a wolf's skin and a cap of ferret skin for Dolon (10.177–178, 10.261–265, 10.334–335; see [[the-boars-tusk-helmet]], [[cattle]] and [[the-doloneia]]).
