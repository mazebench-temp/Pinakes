---
summary: Dolon ends his information with a plea to be held until the two have tested “whether I have spoken to you fairly, or not” (κατʼ αἶσαν). He has spoken fairly, and is killed.
passages:
  - 10.442-445 | Dolon's plea
---

Having told the Achaeans everything, from Hector's council to the Thracians' horses, Dolon asks to be taken to the ships, “or bind me with a pitiless bond and leave me here, until you two have gone and put me to the test, whether I have spoken to you fairly, or not” (ἠὲ κατʼ αἶσαν ἔειπον ἐν ὑμῖν, ἦε καὶ οὐκί, 10.442–445). κατʼ αἶσαν, “in due measure”, is the phrase of a fair word: Paris twice tells Hector that he has rebuked him “fairly, and not beyond what is fair” (3.59 = 6.333), and Ajax will tell Menelaus, “All that you have said is fair” (πάντα κατʼ αἶσαν ἔειπες, 17.716), as Nestor tells Diomedes in this book with the other word of portion, κατὰ μοῖραν (10.169; see [[moira]]). Dolon offers his own truth as security for his life. The test is made, and his words prove true (10.469–475), but he does not live to see it (10.446–457; see [[dolon]] and [[truth-and-deception]]).
