summary: The two prayers before the raid follow the pattern step by step, after a sign instead of a gesture, and one verse answers both (10.274–295). Odysseus' prayer over Dolon's spoils has a gesture, the spoils lifted high, and its answer is the way to the Thracians (10.460–470).
refs: [10.277-295, 10.460-464]
passages:
  - 10.274-296 | The prayers before the raid
---

Three prayers to Athena are told in full, two before the raid and one in the middle of it (see [[prayer]]).

1. **Setting and gesture.** The first two follow a sign: a heron sent by Athena on the right, which the two hear but cannot see in the dark (10.274–277; see [[heron]]). No gesture is named. In the third, Odysseus lifts Dolon's spoils “high in his hand” (ὑψόσʼ ἀνέσχεθε χειρί, 10.461), where a man at prayer lifts up his hands (χεῖρας ἀνασχών, 1.450).
2. **Invocation.** “Hear me” (κλῦθί μευ, 10.278) and “Now hear me too” (κέκλυθι νῦν καὶ ἐμεῖο, 10.284), with the goddess's titles, child of aegis-bearing Zeus and Atrytone (see [[hear-me]] and [[atrytone]]). The third opens with a greeting instead, “Rejoice in these, goddess” (χαῖρε θεά, 10.462).
3. **Claim.** Odysseus claims her constant presence in all his toils (10.278–280), Diomedes her help to his father at Thebes (10.285–290; see [[tydeus-at-thebes]]). In the third prayer the claim is the gift in Odysseus' hand, and a promise to call on her first of all the gods (10.462–463).
4. **Request.** A return with glory (10.281–282); “stand by me, and guard me”, with the vow of a heifer (10.291–294; see [[sacrifice]]); and “once again guide us to the horses and the beds of the Thracian men” (10.463–464).
5. **Answer.** One verse answers the first two: “So they spoke in prayer, and Pallas Athena heard them” (10.295), the verse that closed Diomedes' prayer in Book 5, put into the plural (5.121). The third has no answering verse. The next verses bring the two through the armor and the blood to the Thracians (10.469–470).
