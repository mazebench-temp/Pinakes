summary: The night's plans begin with Agamemnon's need for “shrewd counsel” (βουλῆς κερδαλέης), and the mission is justified by Diomedes' maxim that of two men going together one sees first “how the advantage lies”.
passages:
  - 10.43-52 | Agamemnon asks for shrewd counsel
  - 10.224-226 | When two go together
---

**Shrewd counsel.** Agamemnon's first words to his brother name what the night requires: “You and I have need, Menelaus, … of counsel, shrewd counsel, that will protect and save the Argives and the ships” (10.43–45). κερδαλέη, “shrewd”, held over to the start of the next verse, is the quality the plan must have: it must find the advantage in a lost position. The adjective is found nowhere else in the Iliad except in the compound κερδαλεόφρων, “with your mind set on gain”, which is an insult both times (1.149, 4.339; see [[mind-set-on-gain]]). Here the craft is what the army needs (see [[boule]] and [[metis]]).

**Seeing the advantage.** Diomedes asks for a companion because of two men “one sees before the other how the advantage lies” (ὅππως κέρδος ἔῃ, 10.225), and chooses Odysseus, the man of craft (10.242–247; see [[when-two-go-together]]). The advantage is found in the dark by the means the word's plural names, tricks: the two let Dolon run past them before they spring (10.344–350), Odysseus questions him with reassuring words (10.383), and the sleeping Thracians are reached because a captured spy has told the truth (10.433–441; see [[spies-and-scouts]] and [[truth-and-deception]]).
