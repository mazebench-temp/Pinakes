---
summary: Troy stands behind the Trojan camp in Book 10. Agamemnon sees the fires burning “before Ilios”, Menelaus fears for the Argives who came to Troy for his sake, and both sides' spies are sent to learn whether the Trojans mean to stay by the ships or go back to their city.
refs: [10.209-210, 10.348, 10.410-411]
---

The Trojans are camped on the plain, and the city is behind them. Agamemnon marvels at “the many fires that burned before Ilios” (Ἰλιόθι πρό, 10.12), the phrase of the Trojan fires at the end of Book 8 (8.561; see [[trojans]] and [[stars-around-the-moon]]). Menelaus cannot sleep for fear for the Argives, “who for his sake had come over the wide waters / to Troy, bent on bold war” (10.27–28; see [[war-for-menelaus]] and [[bold-war]]).

Nestor wants to learn whether the Trojans mean “to stay there by the ships, far from home, or to the city / go back again” (10.209–210), and Odysseus asks the captured Dolon the same in the same verses (10.410–411). In the chase Odysseus tells Diomedes to keep Dolon from getting away “toward the city” (10.348). The Trojans keep watch because their wives and children are in the city behind them; the allies, whose families are far away, sleep (10.418–422; see [[trojan-allies]]).
