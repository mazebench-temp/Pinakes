---
summary: The two spies set out “like two lions through the black night,” and Dolon and Odysseus speak of the journey “through the swift black night.”
---

“And when they had prayed to the daughter of great Zeus, they went like two lions through the black night, through the slaughter, through the corpses, through the armor and the black blood” (10.296–298; [[two-lions]], [[dark-blood]], [[daughter-of-zeus]]). The black night and the black blood frame the verse of the corpses. The longer phrase θοὴν διὰ νύκτα μέλαιναν, “through the swift black night,” describes the journey between the camps: Hector's orders to Dolon as Dolon reports them, and the reason Odysseus marks the place of Dolon's spoils (10.394, 10.468; [[swift-night]]). Night has four epithets in Book 10, black, swift, ambrosial and dark ([[ambrosial]], [[dark]]). See [[war-and-night]].
