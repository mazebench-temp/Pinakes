---
summary: Agamemnon is named by his father as he lies awake and as he names himself to Nestor in the dark; Menelaus is “the son of Atreus” among the volunteers.
---

**Agamemnon.** “But the son of Atreus, Agamemnon, shepherd of the people, was not held by sweet sleep” (10.3–4; [[shepherd-of-the-people]]). Nestor, raised on his elbow, speaks “to the son of Atreus” (10.81), and Agamemnon answers by naming himself: “you will know Agamemnon, son of Atreus, whom above all men Zeus has plunged into toils without end” (10.88–89). Nestor replies with the formal address, “Most glorious son of Atreus” (10.103; [[most-glorious]]).

**Menelaus.** Among the volunteers, “the son of Atreus was willing, Menelaus famed for his spear” (10.230; [[famed-for-his-spear]]). Both sons of Atreus are awake on this night, the king groaning and his brother trembling for the Argives who came to Troy for his sake (10.25–28; [[menelaus]]). See [[agamemnon]] and [[atreus]].
