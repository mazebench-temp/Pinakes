---
summary: The two Ajaxes, “attendants of Ares,” are the first of the chiefs who volunteer to go with Diomedes.
---

“The two Ajaxes were willing, attendants of Ares” (10.228). The verse gives them the title in the dual, as Book 8 did when they did not stand against Zeus' thunder (8.79), and it begins the list of volunteers that ends with Odysseus (10.227–232; [[enduring]]). Αἴαντε names both men: Telamonian Ajax, whom Menelaus was sent to call, and the son of Oileus, whom Diomedes roused (10.53, 10.175–179; [[ajax]], [[ajax-son-of-oileus]]). See [[ares]].
