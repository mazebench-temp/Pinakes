---
summary: Odysseus tells Dolon that the horses of Achilles are “hard” for any mortal man but Achilles to master.
grc: [ἀλεγεινοὶ]
en: [hard]
---

“The horses of the wise-hearted grandson of Aeacus; but they are hard for mortal men to master and to drive, for any other than Achilles, whom an immortal mother bore” (10.402–404; [[grandson-of-aeacus]], [[mortal-men]], [[the-horses-of-achilles]]). Here the translation renders ἀλεγεινός “hard”: the horses are troublesome to a mortal. The same verses return in Book 17, when Apollo, in the likeness of Mentes, tells Hector to stop chasing those horses (17.75–78). See [[dolon]] and [[achilles]].
