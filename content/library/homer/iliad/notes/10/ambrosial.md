---
summary: “Through the ambrosial night” Menelaus doubts that anyone will spy alone, and Odysseus asks Nestor and Agamemnon why they wander through the camp.
grc: ["νύκτα διʼ ἀμβροσίην"]
---

Menelaus doubts that anyone will undertake “to go out alone and spy on the enemy through the ambrosial night; he would have to be very bold of heart” (10.40–41; [[bold-hearted]], [[hostile-men]]). Odysseus, woken by Nestor's call, asks: “Why do you wander like this alone along the ships through the camp through the ambrosial night? What need so great has come?” (10.141–142; [[of-many-devices]]). The phrase νύκτα διʼ ἀμβροσίην fills the opening of the verse as νύκτα διʼ ὀρφναίην, “through the dark night,” does in Nestor's challenge to Agamemnon and Odysseus' to Dolon (10.83, 10.386; [[dark]]); in Book 2 the words stand in another order, ἀμβροσίην διὰ νύκτα (2.57). In Book 24 Hermes stops Priam on his night journey with the same epithet: why does he drive “through the ambrosial night, when other mortals are asleep?” (24.362–363). See [[war-and-night]].
