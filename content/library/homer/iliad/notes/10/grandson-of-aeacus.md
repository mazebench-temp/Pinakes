---
summary: Odysseus tells Dolon that the horses of Achilles, “the wise-hearted grandson of Aeacus,” are hard for any mortal man but Achilles to master.
---

“Truly your heart was reaching for great gifts, the horses of the wise-hearted grandson of Aeacus; but they are hard for mortal men to master and to drive, for any other than Achilles, whom an immortal mother bore” (10.401–404; [[wise-hearted]], [[grievous]], [[mortal-men]], [[the-horses-of-achilles]]). The verse names Achilles by his grandfather Aeacus, son of Zeus, and ends with his immortal mother: Odysseus, smiling, measures the spy's ambition against Achilles' descent ([[smiling-at-him]]). See [[achilles]] and [[dolon]].
