---
summary: The night ends with a libation. Back from the raid, bathed and anointed, Odysseus and Diomedes sit down to their meal and pour honey-sweet wine to Athena from the full mixing bowl (10.577–579), as the night's first half, in Book 9, ended with libations before sleep (9.712–713).
grc: ["ἀφυσσόμενοι λεῖβον"]
---

The last words of the book are a libation: “they sat down to their meal; and to Athena, from the full mixing bowl, they drew honey-sweet wine and poured it out” (10.578–579). λεῖβον is the verb of libation, λείβω, “pour in drops”. ἀφυσσόμενοι, “drawing”, is the word of the libation at the oath-sacrifice of Book 3: “And drawing wine from the mixing bowl in cups they poured it out, and prayed to the everlasting gods” (3.295–296; see [[mixing-bowl]]). Here the god is named. It is Athena, who heard the two men's prayers, gave Diomedes might, and is still owed the offering for which Dolon's spoils are kept (10.295, 10.482, 10.570–571; see [[athena]] and [[sacrifice]]).

The same night, in Book 9, ended with libations: “And then they poured libations and went each to his hut, and there they lay down to rest and took the gift of sleep” (9.712–713). Book 10 ends with a libation at a meal near dawn (see [[feast]] and [[bathing]]).
