---
summary: The tusks on the helmet Meriones gives Odysseus are the white teeth “of a white-tusked boar.”
grc: [ἀργιόδοντος]
en: [white-tusked]
---

“And on the outside the white teeth of a white-tusked boar were set close, this way and that, well and skillfully” (10.263–265; [[the-boars-tusk-helmet]], [[boar]], [[white]]). The epithet of the living animal describes the tusks cut from it and set in rows on a leather cap. The genitive ἀργιόδοντος ὑός is the form here, and the translation renders it “white-tusked.” See [[odysseus]] and [[meriones]].
