summary: At night the shield is a pillow and a loan. Nestor's lies beside his bed, Diomedes' companions sleep with their shields under their heads, Odysseus takes his inlaid shield from his hut, and Thrasymedes gives Diomedes one for the raid.
grc: [ἀσπὶς]
---

Nestor's armor beside his bed begins with a shield (ἀσπίς, 10.76), and he leaves it there; in Book 8 Hector coveted Nestor's shield, “all of gold, its rods and the shield itself” (8.191–193). Odysseus, called out of his hut, “put his inlaid shield about his shoulders” (ποικίλον … σάκος, 10.149). Diomedes' companions sleep around him “with their shields under their heads” (10.152; see [[bed]]). In the Odyssey's tale of a night ambush under Troy the men sleep with their shields wrapped round their shoulders, and ice forms on the shields (Od. 14.477–479; see [[ambush]]).

For the raid Thrasymedes gives Diomedes a shield with his sword (10.255–257; see [[arming-scene]]). At the start of Book 14 Nestor, arming in the verse of 10.135, takes his son Thrasymedes' shield, “for his son had his father's” (14.9–12): the shields of Nestor's house pass from hand to hand. No shield is used in the fighting of the night. Dolon carries none, and the Thracians' armor lies beside them on the ground (10.333–335, 10.471–473).
