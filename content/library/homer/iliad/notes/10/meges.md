---
summary: Meges is named in Book 10 only as “the son of Phyleus”. Nestor would wake him with swift Ajax, and sends Diomedes to rouse the two (10.110, 10.175–179).
grc: ["Φυλέος ἄλκιμον υἱόν", "Φυλέος υἱὸν"]
en: [son of Phyleus]
refs: [10.179]
---

Nestor names the men he would wake: “the son of Tydeus, famed for his spear, and Odysseus, and swift Ajax, and the valiant son of Phyleus” (10.109–110). When he has woken Diomedes, he sends him for the last two, “swift Ajax and the son of Phyleus”, since Diomedes is the younger man, and Diomedes “roused them from there and brought them” (10.175–179; see [[nestor]], [[diomedes]] and [[ajax-son-of-oileus]]). Both times the pair is named in the same words, and neither man by his own name. Elsewhere Meges is Φυλεΐδης, “son of Phyleus”, the patronymic in -ίδης (2.628, 5.72); here his father's name stands in the genitive beside υἱός, “son” (see [[phyleus]]). He is not mentioned again in the book.
