---
summary: Nestor, hearing hoofbeats in the dark, asks himself whether he will “be wrong” (ψεύσομαι) or speak the truth; the verb of lying here means being mistaken.
grc: [ψεύσομαι]
en: [be wrong]
passages:
  - 10.532-540 | Nestor hears the horses
---

The book's one ψεύδομαι is a doubt, not a lie. Nestor hears the sound of horses before anyone else and tells the chiefs, “shall I be wrong, or speak the truth? But my heart bids me speak” (ψεύσομαι, ἦ ἔτυμον ἐρέω; κέλεται δέ με θυμός, 10.534). In the dark he cannot see, and he is not sure of his ears; he hopes it is Odysseus and Diomedes, and fears that they have come to harm (10.535–539). Before he has finished speaking they ride in (10.540). Helen says the same verse in the Odyssey when she recognizes Telemachus by his likeness to his father, before anyone has told her who he is (*Odyssey* 4.140). Both speakers risk a guess and are right. The book's falsehood of the other kind is Hector's oath to Dolon, which “would prove false” (ἐπίορκον, 10.332; see [[truth-and-deception]] and [[oath]]).
