---
summary: Dolon's plea has the parts of the scene out of order. There is no approach, for he is caught; the appeal comes first, a ransom in the verses of Adrastus; the answer is put off by a questioning; and the gesture comes last and is cut short by the answer (10.372–457).
refs: [10.377-381, 10.442-457]
passages:
  - 10.377-381 | Dolon begs for his life
  - 10.446-457 | The death of Dolon
---

1. **Approach.** None. Dolon is run down and stopped by a spear thrown over his shoulder, and the two “seized his hands” (10.372–377). A suppliant takes hold of the man he begs; here the captors take hold of him.
2. **Appeal.** It comes first: “Take me alive, and I will ransom myself” (10.378). The ransom of bronze, gold and iron is the one Adrastus offered Menelaus and the sons of Antimachus will offer Agamemnon, in the same verses (6.46–50, 11.131–135; see [[apoina]]). It is made to two captors, in the plural (ζωγρεῖτʼ).
3. **Answer deferred.** Odysseus answers with “Take heart” and a question (10.383–389), and the answer to the plea is put off through the whole questioning (see [[give-me-an-exact-account]]). At the end Dolon renews the appeal and asks to be taken to the ships or left bound until his word is tested (10.442–445).
4. **Gesture and answer.** Diomedes refuses, “looking at him darkly” (10.446–453; see [[looking-darkly]]). Only then does Dolon reach for the gesture: he “was about to take him by the chin with his strong hand and beg him” (10.454–455). The chin that Thetis held as she begged Zeus (1.500–502) is not reached. The refusal and the sword-stroke are one act (10.455–457; see [[supplication]]).
