---
summary: μώνυχας ἵππους ends the verse four times in the second half of the book: Achilles' horses in Dolon's complaint, and the horses of Rhesus as Odysseus unties them, as Nestor hears them, and as Odysseus drives them across the ditch.
---

Dolon says that Hector promised him “the single-hoofed horses of the noble son of Peleus” (10.392; [[the-horses-of-achilles]]). At the Thracian camp “enduring Odysseus untied the single-hoofed horses” (10.498; [[enduring]]); Nestor hopes that it is Odysseus and mighty Diomedes “driving single-hoofed horses from the Trojans, so soon” (10.536–537); and Odysseus “drove the single-hoofed horses across the ditch, laughing aloud” (10.564–565). The formula marks the horses Dolon wanted and the horses the Achaeans took ([[the-horses-of-rhesus]], [[horses]]). See [[odysseus]].
