summary: Both spies of the night carry bows, and neither shoots. Meriones gives Odysseus a bow and a quiver, the only bow he carries in the Iliad, and Odysseus uses it to drive the horses of Rhesus; Dolon's curved bow is taken from his body (10.260, 10.333, 10.459, 10.500, 10.513–514).
grc: ["=βιὸν"]
---

**Odysseus' bow.** “Meriones gave Odysseus a bow and a quiver and a sword” (βιὸν ἠδὲ φαρέτρην, 10.260–261; see [[quiver]] and [[arming-scene]]). It is the only time in the Iliad that Odysseus carries a bow. βιὸν ἠδὲ φαρέτρην is the half-verse with which the Odyssey puts the bow in his hands for the killing of the suitors, when he leaps onto the great threshold “with the bow and the quiver” (Od. 21.233, 22.2). In the Odyssey he boasts that at Troy only Philoctetes surpassed him with the bow (Od. 8.219–220), and the great bow of Iphitus he never took to war (Od. 21.38–41). In Book 10 he shoots nothing. He drives the horses of Rhesus with the bow, “since he had not thought of the shining whip”, and strikes them with it again as they set off (10.500–501, 10.513–514; see [[horses]]).

**Dolon's bow.** The Trojan spy “put his curved bow about his shoulders” before the wolf's skin (καμπύλα τόξα, 10.333), and when he is stripped it is “the back-bent bow” (τόξα παλίντονα, 10.459; see [[back-bent]] and [[wolf-skin]]). He never draws it, nor throws his javelin; caught, he weeps and offers a ransom (10.374–381; see [[spear]] and [[supplication]]).

**The god of the bow.** Apollo, “god of the silver bow”, does not “keep a blind watch” (10.515): he sees Athena with Diomedes and wakes the Thracian Hippocoon (10.515–519; see [[apollo]] and [[hippocoon]]).
