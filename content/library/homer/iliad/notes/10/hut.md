---
summary: The night's visits go from hut to hut along the ships. Nestor lies beside his hut, Odysseus comes out of his and goes back in for his shield, Diomedes sleeps outside his, and at the end the horses of Rhesus are tied at the manger by Diomedes' well-made hut (10.74, 10.140–151, 10.566–569).
---

Agamemnon “found him beside his hut and his black ship” (10.74), the verse with which the heralds found Achilles when they came for Briseis (1.329; see [[visit-scene]]). Each leader's hut stands by his ship. Odysseus, woken by Nestor's call, “came out of his hut” to speak, and then goes back in for his shield (10.140, 10.148–149). Diomedes sleeps “outside his hut with his armor”, among his companions (10.151; see [[bed]]).

At the end of the night the horses of Rhesus are driven to “the well-made hut of the son of Tydeus” and tied at the manger where his own horses stand (10.566–569; see [[horses]]). Idomeneus comes to his “well-made hut”, in the same words, in Book 13 (κλισίην εὔτυκτον, 13.240). A leader's hut has room for his horses, and Achilles' in the last book has a great courtyard fenced with stakes (24.452–453).
