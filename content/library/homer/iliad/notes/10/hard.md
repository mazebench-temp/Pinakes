---
summary: Woken by Nestor's heel, Diomedes calls the old man σχέτλιος, “a hard man,” who never ceases from toil.
---

“You are a hard man, old sir; you never cease from toil. Are there not other, younger sons of the Achaeans who could then wake each one of the kings, going around everywhere? But there is no dealing with you, old sir” (10.164–167; [[sons-of-the-achaeans]]). The word of reproach is half admiration. Nestor agrees that it is all fair and right, but the need is great, and everything stands on the edge of a razor (10.169–174; [[on-the-razors-edge]], [[blameless]]). See [[diomedes]] and [[nestor]].
