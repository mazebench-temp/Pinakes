---
summary: The only sun of Book 10 is a comparison for horses. When Odysseus and Diomedes ride into the camp before dawn on the horses of Rhesus, Nestor finds them “terribly like the rays of the sun” (10.547).
grc: ["ἀκτίνεσσιν ἐοικότες"]
en: [terribly like the rays of the sun]
---

αἰνῶς ἀκτίνεσσιν ἐοικότες ἠελίοιο, “they are terribly like the rays of the sun” (10.547). The other comparisons of this entry measure men in bronze by the sun: Paris coming down from Pergamus “gleaming in his armor like the shining sun” (6.513), Achilles mounting his chariot in the same half-line (19.398), and Achilles' bronze, shining “like the gleam of blazing fire or of the rising sun” as he comes at Hector (22.134–135). Here, for the only time, the sun is the measure of horses. ἀκτίς, “ray”, occurs nowhere else in the Iliad (see [[helios]]).

Nestor speaks while it is still night, the third portion of it (10.251–253), and the horses seem to bring the light before the sun does. αἰνῶς, “terribly”, is the word he used a moment before of his fear for the two men, “I am terribly afraid” (10.538); now it marks his wonder. He has never seen or noticed horses like these, and he guesses that a god met the two and gave them (10.546–551). Dolon had given the same horses the measures of snow and wind, “whiter than snow, and like the winds in running” (10.437; see [[whiter-than-snow]] and [[the-horses-of-rhesus]]).
