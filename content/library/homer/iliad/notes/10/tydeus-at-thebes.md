Then he vows her a yearling heifer with gold poured around her horns (10.292–294; see [[cattle]]), and Athena hears them both (10.295).

**The third telling.** Agamemnon told the story in Book 4 to shame Diomedes: the embassy from the Asopus, the contests that Tydeus won alone among the feasting Cadmeians, and the ambush of fifty men on his way back, all of whom he killed but Maeon (4.382–398). Athena told it again in Book 5 to rouse him, in her own person, and left out the ambush (5.800–813; see the Book 5 note). Diomedes tells it a third time, and his version keeps what the other two did not share. The contests are gone. Tydeus carried “a soothing word” (μειλίχιον μῦθον) to the Cadmeians, and “on his way back he devised very grim deeds / with you, brilliant goddess, when you stood by him with a ready heart” (10.288–290; see the translation's note on 10.285–290). The grim deeds are the ambush and the killing of the fifty, which Agamemnon told and Athena did not; Diomedes gives them to Athena as much as to his father. On a night mission into enemy ground he asks the goddess for what she gave Tydeus on his night of killing (see [[tydeus]], [[cadmeians]] and [[asopus]]).

**From reproach to prayer.** The story was first used against Diomedes, and he heard it in silence (4.401–402). In Book 5 he prayed to Athena in his father's name, “if ever in friendship you stood by my father and me in destructive war, now be my friend in turn” (5.116–117), and she gave him his father's might (5.125–126). Now he tells the father's story himself, as the ground of his prayer: “go with me, as when you went with my father” (10.285). The paradigm has become his own (see [[fathers-and-sons]] and [[mythological-paradigm]]).

**Grim deeds.** μέρμερα ἔργα, “grim deeds”, runs through the book (see [[grim-deeds]]). Agamemnon began the night lamenting that no man ever devised “so many grim deeds in a single day” as Hector (10.48). Diomedes asks for his father's grim deeds (10.289), and at the end the Trojans, roused too late, gaze at “the grim deeds / the men had done before they went off to the hollow ships” (10.524–525). The prayer is answered: like his father, Diomedes kills many men in the night with Athena standing by him, twelve Thracians and their king (10.482–497; see [[the-doloneia]]).
