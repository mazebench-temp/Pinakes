summary: Diomedes recalls that Tydeus carried “a soothing word” to the Cadmeians, and the Achaeans welcome the two spies “with soothing words.”
grc: ["μειλίχιον μῦθον"]
en: [soothing word]
---

In his prayer Diomedes tells how his father Tydeus left the Achaeans at the Asopus “and himself carried a soothing word to the Cadmeians there” (10.287–289; [[tydeus-at-thebes]], [[embassy]]): the conciliatory message of an envoy, which Agamemnon told of in Book 4 (4.384). On his way back Tydeus devised grim deeds (10.289–290; [[grim-deeds]]). At the end of the raid the Achaeans welcome Odysseus and Diomedes “with handclasps and with soothing words” (10.541–542).
