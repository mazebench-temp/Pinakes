---
summary: Ares takes no part in Book 10, and his name is heard once, when the two Ajaxes volunteer for the night mission as “attendants of Ares” (10.228).
---

Many are willing to go with Diomedes, and the list begins: “The two Ajaxes were willing, attendants of Ares” (10.228; see [[ajax]], [[ajax-son-of-oileus]] and [[attendants-of-ares]]). The half-verse θεράποντες Ἄρηος named the same pair when they fled from Zeus' thunder in Book 8 (8.79). Otherwise it belongs to the address to the whole army, “Friends, Danaan heroes, attendants of Ares” (2.110, 6.67, 15.733, 19.78).

The killing of the night is Athena's work, not his: she puts might into Diomedes (10.366, 10.482; see [[athena]]). The poems elsewhere connect Ares with Thrace. In a simile he and his son Rout set out from Thrace to fight (13.298–302), and in the Odyssey he goes off to Thrace when Hephaestus' net is loosed (*Odyssey* 8.360–361). He has no part in the killing of the Thracians in their sleep, and the god who wakes them is Apollo (10.515–519; see [[thracians]] and [[apollo]]).

## Outside Homer

Herodotus says that the Thracians worship only three gods, Ares, Dionysus and Artemis (*Histories* 5.7).
