---
summary: Agamemnon counts the evils Hector has done “to the sons of the Achaeans,” and Diomedes asks Nestor whether there are no younger “sons of the Achaeans” to run his errands.
---

“As Hector, dear to Zeus, has done to the sons of the Achaeans” (10.49; [[dear-to-zeus]]). Diomedes, woken by Nestor's heel: “Are there not other, younger sons of the Achaeans who could then wake each one of the kings?” (10.165–166; [[hard]]). In Agamemnon's words the phrase is the army as Hector's victim; in Diomedes' it is the young men of the army, who could spare an old man the night's errands. See [[achaeans]].
