---
summary: Oeneus is named once, in the patronymic of a patronymic: at the killing of Rhesus, Diomedes is Οἰνεΐδαο πάϊς, “the son of Oeneus' son” (10.497).
---

“For an evil dream stood over his head / that night, the son of Oeneus' son, by the design of Athena” (10.496–497). Οἰνεΐδης, “son of Oeneus”, is Tydeus' patronymic, and Athena used it of him in Book 5, “the wise-hearted son of Oeneus” (5.813; see [[tydeus]]). With πάϊς, “child”, it reaches back two generations: only here is Diomedes named by his grandfather, at the moment he kills a sleeping king (see [[diomedes]] and [[rhesus]]). The descent from Oeneus to Tydeus and his son, from Calydon to Argos, is told by Diomedes himself in Book 14, where Oeneus, the best of Portheus' three sons, is πατρὸς ἐμοῖο πατήρ, his father's father (14.113–118).
