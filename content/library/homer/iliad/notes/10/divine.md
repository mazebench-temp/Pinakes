---
summary: θεῖος praises Odysseus in Diomedes' choice, Dolon's father the herald Eumedes, and Ilus, by whose mound Hector holds council.
grc: [θείου]
---

Diomedes chooses his companion: “how then could I forget divine Odysseus” (10.243; [[odysseus]]). Dolon is the son of Eumedes, “the divine herald” (10.314–315; [[eumedes]]), as Agamemnon calls Talthybius a divine herald in Book 4 (4.192). And Dolon tells his captors that Hector holds council “by the mound of divine Ilus, away from the din” (10.414–416; [[ilus]], [[the-tomb-of-ilus]]). Ilus, son of Tros, is long dead; the epithet goes with his mound, a landmark on the plain. See [[hector]].
