---
summary: Agamemnon is “lord of men” five times in the night: in his orders to Menelaus, in his talk with Nestor, and when he tells Diomedes not to choose his companion by rank.
---

The formula introduces his orders to Menelaus (10.64), his answer to Nestor's challenge in the dark (10.86) and his defense of his brother (10.119). Nestor addresses him with the full verse, “Most glorious son of Atreus, Agamemnon, lord of men” (10.103; [[most-glorious]]). The last use is the most pointed: “And among them spoke also Agamemnon, lord of men,” and he tells Diomedes not to choose “looking to his lineage, not even if he is more kingly” (10.233–239; [[dear-to-my-heart]]). The lord of men asks that the choice not be made by rank. See [[agamemnon]].
