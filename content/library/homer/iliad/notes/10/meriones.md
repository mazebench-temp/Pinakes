**The watch.** Agamemnon names him with Nestor's son as the captains of the sentries: “to them above all we entrusted it” (10.57–59; see [[night-watch]] and [[thrasymedes]]). He was the fourth of the seven captains who led the sentries out at the start of the night (9.83). The two captains go with the kings across the ditch, “for the kings themselves had called them to take counsel with them” (10.196–197; see [[kings]]), and both volunteer to go with Diomedes (10.229).

**The arms.** When Diomedes chooses Odysseus, the two captains arm the spies. Thrasymedes gives Diomedes a sword, a shield and a cap of bull's hide; Meriones gives Odysseus “a bow and a quiver and a sword”, and sets on his head a helmet of leather faced with rows of a boar's white tusks (10.255–265; see [[the-boars-tusk-helmet]], [[bow]] and [[arming-scene]]). Meriones is an archer. He shoots Harpalion with an arrow (13.650–652), and at the funeral games of Patroclus he wins the archery, bringing down the dove whose cord Teucer's arrow has cut (23.859–883). Odysseus has come out with only his shield (10.149), and it is with this bow that he later drives the Thracian horses (10.500, 10.513–514; see [[odysseus]]).

**His father's helmet.** The helmet has a history of five owners. Autolycus stole it from Amyntor's house at Eleon and gave it at Scandeia to Amphidamas of Cythera, “and Amphidamas gave it to Molus to be a guest-gift, and Molus gave it to his son Meriones to wear” (10.266–270; see [[autolycus]], [[amyntor]], [[eleon]], [[amphidamas]], [[molus]] and [[guest-gifts]]). This is the only mention of Meriones' father in Book 10; in Book 13 Idomeneus calls him Μηριόνη Μόλου υἱέ, “Meriones, son of Molus” (13.249). The helmet that Odysseus' grandfather stole comes back, through three more hands, to Odysseus' head (10.271; Od. 19.394–396).
