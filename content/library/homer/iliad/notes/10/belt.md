---
summary: Beside Nestor's bed lies the gleaming belt with which he girded himself whenever he armed for war, for the old man did not give way to old age (10.77–79); he leaves it there when he goes out with Agamemnon.
---

{{quote:02a776f6-c40b-5e7d-a066-d2ae96d2f76c}}

The belt is παναίολος, “gleaming”, the word for Menelaus' belt, which stopped Pandarus' arrow (4.186; see [[panaiolos]]). To gird oneself is to arm: ζώννυθʼ ὅτʼ ἐς πόλεμον … θωρήσσοιτο, “girded himself whenever he armed” (10.78), and at dawn Agamemnon orders the Argives to gird themselves (ζώννυσθαι, 11.15). The belt is kept for war, and it is the old man's sign that he still goes to war at the head of his men (see [[nestor]] and [[old-age-and-memory]]). He lies beside it with his shield, two spears and helmet, and when he goes out with Agamemnon he takes only a spear (10.75–79, 10.135; see [[arming-scene]]).
