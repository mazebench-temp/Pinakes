---
summary: “Gray-eyed Athena” breathes might into Diomedes before the killing of the Thracians, and Nestor names her with Zeus as the gods who love the two men.
---

Odysseus tells Diomedes to kill the men or untie the horses: “So he spoke, and gray-eyed Athena breathed might into him, and he killed, turning this way and that” (10.482–483; [[menos]], [[krateros]]). At the return Nestor names her in a full verse, “the daughter of aegis-bearing Zeus, gray-eyed Athena” (10.553; [[child-of-aegis-bearing-zeus]]). Elsewhere in the book she is Pallas Athena, or Athena alone ([[pallas]]). See [[athena]] and [[diomedes]].
