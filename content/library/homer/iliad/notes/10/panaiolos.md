---
summary: Beside Nestor's bed lies “the gleaming belt” he wears to war.
---

“And beside them lay the gleaming belt with which the old man girded himself whenever he armed for man-destroying war” (10.77–78; [[man-destroying]], [[belt]]). In Book 4 the same epithet described the belt of Menelaus that stopped the arrow of Pandarus (4.186, 4.215). The old man keeps his war belt by his bed, with his shield, his two spears and his shining helmet (10.75–76; [[phaeinos]]). See [[nestor]].
