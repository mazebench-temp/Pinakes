summary: Each side's spying orders end in the other side's hands. Captured, Dolon repeats to Odysseus and Diomedes the orders Hector gave him, nearly word for word, and Odysseus asks Dolon, word for word, the questions Nestor put to the Achaean council (10.308–312 ≈ 10.394–399; 10.208–210 = 10.409–411).
refs: [10.394-399, 10.409-411]
passages:

  - 10.405-411 | Odysseus repeats Nestor's questions
---

**Hector's orders.** Hector asks for a man “to go close to the swift-sailing ships and find out whether the swift ships are guarded as before, or whether, beaten down already by our hands, they are planning flight among themselves, and are unwilling to keep watch through the night, worn out by dreadful weariness” (10.308–312). Dolon takes up the first verse when he volunteers (10.320). Captured, he repeats all five to the men he was sent to spy on. Only the beginning changes: Hector ordered him “to go through the swift black night and come close to the enemy men, and find out” (10.394–395). The rest is word for word (10.396–399 = 10.309–312), down to “by our hands”, the Trojans' hands. The message is delivered, but to the enemy (see [[give-me-an-exact-account]]).

**Nestor's questions.** Nestor asked for a man who might learn “what they are planning among themselves, whether they mean to stay there by the ships, far from home, or to the city go back again, now that they have beaten the Achaeans” (10.208–210). Odysseus puts the same three verses to Dolon unchanged, even the Achaean speaking of the Achaeans as beaten (10.409–411). Each camp asks the same thing of the other, and the verse that names the question is shared too. Nestor wants to wake a man whom it befits “to take counsel, whether to flee or to fight” (10.147), and Dolon imagines the Achaean chiefs at Agamemnon's ship “taking counsel, whether to flee or to fight” (10.326–327; see [[boule]]).

**The report.** At the end Odysseus tells Nestor what has been done, with a count of thirteen that holds only if the king is set apart and his twelve companions and Dolon are counted together (10.555–563; see the translation's note on 10.559–561, and [[retelling]]). His last verse, Dolon was sent “by Hector and the other noble Trojans” (10.563), is close to the verse with which the herald Idaeus named the senders of his message in Book 7, “Priam and the other noble Trojans bade me” (7.386; see [[heralds]]).
