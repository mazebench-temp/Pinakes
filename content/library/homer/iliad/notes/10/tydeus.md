**The father as precedent.** Diomedes asks Athena to do for him what she did for his father, and the father's story becomes the ground of the prayer. Tydeus went ahead of the Achaeans as their messenger, left them at the Asopus, carried “a soothing word” to the Cadmeians, and on his way back “devised very grim deeds” with the goddess beside him (10.285–290; see [[asopus]] and [[cadmeians]]). Agamemnon and Athena told the story to measure the son against the father (4.370–400, 5.800–813); the son tells it as his own claim on her (see [[tydeus-at-thebes]] for the three tellings). The father is Τυδέϊ δίῳ, “brilliant Tydeus” (10.285; see [[brilliant]]), and Athena stood by him πρόφρασσα, “with a ready heart” (10.290). The vow that follows, of a yearling heifer, is the son's own (10.292–294; see [[diomedes]] and [[athena]]).

**The name.** Tydeus is named in fifteen lines of the book as the father in his son's titles, Τυδεΐδης and Τυδέος υἱός, “son of Tydeus” (see [[son-of-tydeus]]). Athena sends Diomedes back to the ships as “son of great-hearted Tydeus” (10.509), and at the killing of Rhesus he is “the son of Oeneus' son” (Οἰνεΐδαο πάϊς, 10.497), named through Tydeus' own patronymic (see [[oeneus]]).
