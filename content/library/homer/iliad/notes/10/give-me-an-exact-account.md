summary: Odysseus questions the captured Dolon in two rounds, each opened with “But come, tell me this, and give me an exact account”, and Dolon answers “very exactly”, telling more than he is asked: the Thracians, newly come, their king and his horses (10.384–445).
passages:
  - 10.382-445 | The questioning of Dolon
---

**First round.** “But come, tell me this, and give me an exact account” (10.384), and three possibilities follow: is Dolon out alone in the dark to strip the dead, or has Hector sent him to spy on the ships, or has his own heart sent him (10.385–389; see [[when-other-mortals-are-asleep]])? Dolon answers that Hector sent him, and repeats the orders Hector gave (10.391–399; see [[message-repeated]]).

**Second round.** The verse again (10.405), and more questions: where Dolon left Hector, where his armor and horses are, how the watches and the sleeping places of the Trojans are kept, and then Nestor's own questions, word for word, whether the Trojans mean to stay by the ships or go back to the city (10.406–411; 10.208–210). Dolon begins, “Well then, I will tell you all this very exactly” (10.413), and tells of Hector's council by the mound of Ilus and of the Trojan watch (10.414–422). Asked again, he begins again in the same words, and gives the places of the allies (10.427–431).

**More than he is asked.** Then he breaks off, “But why do you question me about all these things, one by one?” (10.432), and offers what no one has asked for: if the two want to get into the Trojan camp, the Thracians are lying apart, newly come, with their king Rhesus, his horses whiter than snow, his chariot and his golden armor (10.433–441; see [[rhesus]] and [[the-horses-of-rhesus]]). The exact account he was asked for hands the raiders their prey. His last words ask the two to bind him and leave him “until you two have gone and put me to the test, whether I have spoken to you fairly, or not” (10.444–445). Diomedes kills him instead (10.446–457; see [[supplication]]).

The other two uses of the question verse in the Iliad are in the night of Book 24, when Hermes and then Achilles question Priam (24.380, 24.656).
