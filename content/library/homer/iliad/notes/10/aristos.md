---
summary: The book begins with “the best of all the Achaeans” asleep and is full of “the best”: the best companion, chosen against rank; the best horses, promised by Hector and taken from Rhesus; the best men on both sides, left behind, called to council or killed in their sleep.
grc: [ἀρείω]
passages:
  - 10.233-247 | Choosing the best
---

**Choose the best.** When many offer to go with Diomedes, Agamemnon tells him to take “the best of those who offer” (φαινομένων τὸν ἄριστον, 10.236), and not to leave “the better man” (τὸν ἀρείω) behind out of respect for rank (10.237–239; see [[aidos]] and [[genee]]). The best man for this task is not the bravest in battle, nor the most kingly, but the one whose mind is quickest: Diomedes chooses Odysseus, “since he is wise beyond all others” (10.247; see [[when-two-go-together]] and [[noos]]). In the quarrel of Book 1 “the best of the Achaeans” was a title disputed between Agamemnon and Achilles (1.91, 1.244; see [[best-of-the-achaeans]]); here the best is measured by what the night requires.

**The best on both sides.** The word runs through the book in the plural. The opening verse leaves “the best of all the Achaeans” asleep by the ships (ἀριστῆες Παναχαιῶν, 10.1). Nestor's reward is to come from “all the best men who hold command over the ships” (10.214), the two spies leave “all the best” behind them (10.273), and Hector calls “all the best” of the Trojans to his council (10.300), as the Achaean “chiefs” are, Dolon supposes, taking counsel at Agamemnon's ship (10.326). Hector promises his spy the horses that are “the best there are at the swift ships of the Achaeans” (10.306). At the end Nestor fears that “the best men of the Argives” have come to harm (10.538–539), and Odysseus reports that Rhesus died with “twelve companions, all of the best” (10.560; see [[numbers-and-counting]]).
