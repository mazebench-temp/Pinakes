---
summary: Zeus is “aegis-bearing” twice in Book 10, both times in a formula that names Athena as his child: in Odysseus' prayer as the raid begins and in Nestor's welcome when it ends.
---

Odysseus hears the heron and prays first: “Hear me, child of aegis-bearing Zeus, you who always stand by me in all my toils” (10.278–279; [[child-of-aegis-bearing-zeus]], [[prayer]]). At the end of the night Nestor thinks that a god gave the two men Rhesus' horses, “for Zeus the cloud-gatherer loves you both, and the daughter of aegis-bearing Zeus, gray-eyed Athena” (10.552–553; [[cloud-gatherer]], [[gray-eyed]]). The father's epithet frames the daughter's part in the raid, from the first prayer to the return. See [[zeus]], [[athena]] and [[aegis]].
