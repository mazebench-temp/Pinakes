---
summary: Hector holds a scepter only here in the poem. Dolon asks him to hold it up and swear, and he takes it in his hands and swears the oath that would prove false (10.321, 10.328).
---

“But come, hold up your scepter for me, and swear to me” (10.321). Raising the scepter is the gesture of an oath. Achilles swore by his scepter in the quarrel (1.233–239), and Agamemnon lifted his to all the gods to confirm the truce for the dead (7.412). Aristotle says that the kings of the heroic age judged cases, some under oath and some not, “and the oath was the raising of the scepter” (*Politics* 3.14, 1285b10–12). Hector “took the scepter in his hands and swore to him” (10.328; see [[oath]]). It is the only time he is seen with a scepter, at the council of Trojan leaders he has called for the night (10.299–302; see [[hector]] and [[boule]]).
