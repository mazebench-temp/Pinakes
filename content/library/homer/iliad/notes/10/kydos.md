**Giving glory.** Agamemnon sends Menelaus through the camp to wake the chiefs: “Call out wherever you go, and bid them stay awake, naming each man by his father and his lineage, and giving glory to them all; and do not be proud in your heart” (10.67–69). κυδαίνω is to give κῦδος, and elsewhere its subject is usually a god: Leto and Artemis heal Aeneas in Apollo's temple “and gave him glory” (5.448), and Zeus “honored and gave glory” to Hector, who was to be short-lived (15.612). Here the glory is given by a man, and in words: to call a chief by his father's name is to honor him (see [[genee]]). The king's brother is to do it without standing on his own rank, “so, it seems, at our birth Zeus laid heavy misery on us” (10.70–71; see [[kingship-and-authority]]).

**Great glory of the Achaeans.** The title μέγα κῦδος Ἀχαιῶν passes back and forth in the night. Agamemnon greets the old man with it when he comes to his bed, “Nestor, son of Neleus, great glory of the Achaeans” (10.87); Nestor greets Odysseus with it on his return, “Come, tell me, much-praised Odysseus, great glory of the Achaeans” (10.544, as Agamemnon did after the embassy, 9.673); and Odysseus answers Nestor with Agamemnon's verse (10.555 = 10.87). Elsewhere it belongs to these two men alone (11.511, 14.42; *Odyssey* 3.79, 12.184; see [[great-glory-of-the-achaeans]]). Nestor in turn calls Agamemnon “most glorious” (κύδιστε, 10.103), the courtesy that opened four speeches to the defeated king in Book 9.

**Glory to be won.** Hector promises the chariot and horses “to whoever dares, and wins glory for himself” (οἷ τʼ αὐτῷ κῦδος ἄροιτο, 10.307), and Dolon dares for the horses alone. On the other side the glory of the capture is guarded. When Dolon is about to reach the Achaean sentries, Athena puts might into Diomedes “so that none of the bronze-armored Achaeans might boast that he struck him first, and he himself come second” (10.366–368; see [[menos]]). In Book 22 Achilles shakes his head at the army to keep them from throwing at Hector, for the same reason and with the same close of the verse, “so that no one should win glory by hitting him, and he himself come second” (μή τις κῦδος ἄροιτο βαλών, ὃ δὲ δεύτερος ἔλθοι, 22.205–207), using Hector's phrase for the glory.
