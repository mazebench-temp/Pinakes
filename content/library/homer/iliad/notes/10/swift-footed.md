summary: Dolon is ποδώκης, “swift-footed,” an epithet of Achilles, whose horses he asks for; the horses of Rhesus and Diomedes' own team are swift, and Nestor hears the beat of swift-footed horses.
grc: [ποδώκης]
---

**Dolon.** “He was ill-favored to look at, but swift-footed” (10.316; [[eidos]], [[aristotle-dolons-face]]). ποδώκης is elsewhere in the Iliad an epithet of Achilles (2.860, 8.474) and of horses (2.764), and Dolon asks for Achilles' horses (10.322–323; [[the-horses-of-achilles]]). His speed does not save him (10.358–364; [[nimble]], [[sharp-toothed]]).

**Horses.** The horses of Rhesus are ὠκέες ἵπποι, “swift horses,” when they stand tied to his chariot, when Hippocoon finds their place empty, and when Odysseus holds them in where Dolon was killed (10.474, 10.520, 10.527); Nestor hears “the sound of swift-footed horses” (10.535), and Diomedes' own horses stand “swift-footed” at the manger (10.568–569). See [[horses]] and [[dolon]].
