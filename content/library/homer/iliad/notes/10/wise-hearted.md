---
summary: Odysseus calls Achilles “the wise-hearted grandson of Aeacus” as he tells Dolon that Achilles' horses are for Achilles alone.
---

“Truly your heart was reaching for great gifts, the horses of the wise-hearted grandson of Aeacus” (10.401–402; [[grandson-of-aeacus]], [[the-horses-of-achilles]]). The epithet, whose sense was disputed in antiquity, is given to the absent hero in the middle of Odysseus' answer to Dolon's ambition ([[smiling-at-him]]). The same two verses return when Apollo, in the likeness of Mentes, tells Hector to stop chasing the horses of Achilles (17.75–78). See [[achilles]].
