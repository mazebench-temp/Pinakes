---
summary: Nestor names Diomedes “the son of Tydeus, famed for his spear,” and Menelaus volunteers as “Menelaus famed for his spear.”
grc: [δουρὶ κλυτὸν]
---

Nestor proposes to wake “the son of Tydeus, famed for his spear, and Odysseus” (10.109; [[son-of-tydeus]]), with the phrase in the accusative, δουρὶ κλυτόν. Among the volunteers, “the son of Atreus was willing, Menelaus famed for his spear” (10.230), with the compound δουρικλειτός, which is Menelaus' in Book 5 and in the Odyssey (5.55, 5.578; Od. 15.52, 17.116, 17.147). Of the two men named by the spear, Diomedes is the one who goes; Agamemnon fears that his brother will be chosen (10.240; [[fair-haired]]). See [[diomedes]] and [[menelaus]].
