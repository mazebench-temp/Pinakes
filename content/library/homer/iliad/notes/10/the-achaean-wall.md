summary: Book 10 never names the wall itself, only its gates and the ditch. The chiefs meet the sentries “before the gates”, cross the ditch to hold their council on the open plain, and at the end Odysseus drives the captured horses back across it (10.126, 10.194–202, 10.564).
refs: [10.126]
---

Book 10 names the ditch three times and the wall not at all. Agamemnon tells Nestor that they will find the chiefs “before the gates among the sentries” (10.126–127), the gates built into the wall to let the chariots through (7.339–340; see [[night-watch]]). The sentries had gone out to sit “midway between the ditch and the wall” (9.87). From there Nestor “hurried across the ditch”, the kings following, with Meriones and Nestor's son (10.194–197; see [[nestor]] and [[kings]]).

{{quote:a067eef5-6d4b-5abc-b20c-22618eb3e1db}}

τάφρον ὀρυκτήν, “the ditch they had dug”, is the ditch of Nestor's order for the sentries (9.67). The next verse, “in a clear space, where the ground showed free of corpses”, placed Hector's assembly on the plain the evening before (10.199 = 8.491). Here the clear space is the ground from which Hector turned back at nightfall, between the ditch and the Trojans, who are camped so near the ships that “only a little space still holds them off” (10.160–161; see [[hector]]). Why the kings cross the ditch to sit outside it is not said (see the translation's note on 10.199–201). They are still there when the spies come back, and the ditch is the last obstacle of the night: Odysseus “drove the single-hoofed horses across the ditch, laughing aloud; and the other Achaeans went with him, rejoicing” (10.564–565; see [[odysseus]] and [[the-horses-of-rhesus]]). In Book 8 Hector boasted that his horses would easily leap it (8.179); they will balk at its edge in Book 12 (12.50–59).
