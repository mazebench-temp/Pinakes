---
summary: Goats appear in Book 10 only in the lion simile of the Thracian camp. Diomedes kills the sleepers as a lion falls on flocks that have no herdsman, “on goats or sheep” (10.485–486).
grc: [αἴγεσιν]
---

ὡς δὲ λέων μήλοισιν ἀσημάντοισιν ἐπελθὼν / αἴγεσιν ἢ ὀΐεσσι κακὰ φρονέων ἐνορούσῃ, “as a lion, coming on flocks that have no herdsman, / on goats or sheep, leaps on them with evil intent” (10.485–486; see [[lion-among-the-flocks]]). μῆλα, the small livestock, are goats and sheep together, and the simile names both (see [[sheep]]). What matters is the missing herdsman. The goatherd of Book 4 sees a storm cloud coming over the sea and drives his flock into a cave (4.275–279; see [[storm-cloud]]); these flocks have no one to drive them, as the sleeping Thracians have no one to keep watch.
