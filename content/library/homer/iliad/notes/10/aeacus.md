---
summary: Odysseus calls Achilles “the wise-hearted grandson of Aeacus” when he tells the captured Dolon that no mortal but Achilles can master his horses (10.402).
---

ἵππων Αἰακίδαο δαΐφρονος, “the horses of the wise-hearted grandson of Aeacus” (10.402; see [[grandson-of-aeacus]], [[wise-hearted]] and [[achilles]]). The name of the grandfather carries the descent from Zeus that Achilles himself sets out to the river god in Book 21 (21.187–189), and it comes in the verse that sets the immortal horses against mortal men (10.401–404; see [[the-horses-of-achilles]]). Apollo uses the verse again when he warns Hector off the same horses (17.76).
