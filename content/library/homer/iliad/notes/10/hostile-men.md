---
summary: “Hostile men” are the enemy in Menelaus' doubt that anyone will spy on them, in Agamemnon's fear of a night attack, in Diomedes' offer, and in Dolon's orders.
grc: [ἄνδρας δυσμενέας, "δυσμενέες δʼ ἄνδρες", ἀνδρῶν δυσμενέων]
en: [enemy]
---

Menelaus doubts that anyone will undertake “to go out alone and spy on the enemy through the ambrosial night” (10.40–41; [[bold-hearted]]). Agamemnon tells Nestor that “hostile men are camped close by,” and may be eager to fight even by night (10.100–101; [[night-watch]]). Diomedes offers “to enter the camp of the hostile men close by, the Trojans” (10.221–222), and Dolon tells his captors that Hector ordered him to “come close to the enemy men” (10.395). In Books 5 and 6 the phrase named the enemy into whose hands a man or a city falls; here it names the men whom the spies must approach. The translation renders it “hostile men” and “the enemy.” See [[spies-and-scouts]].
