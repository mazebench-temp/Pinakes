---
summary: Diomedes answers the captured Dolon “looking at him darkly”, refuses his plea and kills him (10.446–457). In the Odyssey Odysseus refuses the suppliant Leodes with the same look and the same stroke (Od. 22.320–329).
---

Diomedes' answer to Dolon is the only speech of Book 10 introduced by the glare: “Looking at him darkly, mighty Diomedes said” (10.446), the verse with which he refused Sthenelus' advice to retreat in Book 5 (5.251). Dolon has answered every question, and more, and has asked to be taken to the ships or left bound until his word is tested (10.442–445). The look tells what the answer will be before it is given: no ransom and no release (10.447–453; see [[supplication]]).

The glare closes a supplication in the Odyssey too. The seer Leodes clasps Odysseus' knees among the suitors and begs for his life, and Odysseus answers “looking at him darkly” (Od. 22.310–325). Then he picks up a sword and strikes him in the middle of the neck, and the verse of Dolon's death follows: “while he was still speaking his head was mingled with the dust” (Od. 22.326–329 ≈ 10.455–457).

In the questioning of Dolon the two captors answer him with two faces. Odysseus speaks once “smiling at him” (10.400; see [[smiling-at-him]]), and Diomedes ends it with the glare.
