**Asleep.** The Trojans keep watch at their fires, Dolon says, “but the allies, called from many lands, / are asleep; for they leave it to the Trojans to keep watch, / since their children are not near them, nor their wives” (10.420–422; see [[called-from-many-lands]] and [[night-watch]]). The Trojans guard their own families, who are in the city behind them; the allies' families are far away. Hector puts the same bond the other way when he rallies the allies in Book 17: he did not summon them for their numbers, but so that they might defend “the wives and little children of the Trojans” (17.220–224; see [[trojans]]).

**The camp.** Odysseus asks whether the allies sleep “mingled with the horse-taming Trojans … or apart” (10.424–425), and Dolon gives the whole order of the camp:

{{quote:7f7baf55-76e2-5b17-904e-9964c19283fd}}

The allies lie in two wings, “toward the sea” and “toward Thymbre” (see [[thymbre]]): the [[Carians]], [[Paeonians]], [[Leleges]], [[Caucones]] and [[Pelasgians]] on one side, the [[Lycians]], [[Mysians]], [[Phrygians]] and [[Maeonians]] on the other. Dolon gives them epithets: the Paeonians “with their curved bows”, as in the Catalogue (ἀγκυλότοξοι, 2.848; see [[with-curved-bows]]), the “brilliant” Pelasgians, the “proud” Mysians (see [[proud]]), the Phrygians “who fight from horses” (ἱππόμαχοι, only here; see [[horse-fighting]]) and the Maeonians “who fight from chariots” (see [[chariot-fighters]]). The list does not follow the Trojan Catalogue (2.816–877). The Leleges and the Caucones are not in the Catalogue, and the Catalogue's Cicones, Paphlagonians and Halizones are not here (see [[trojan-catalogue]] and the translation's note on 10.428–431).

Last come the Thracians, “apart, newly come, the farthest out of all” (10.434; see [[thracians]], [[newly-come]] and [[rhesus]]). Dolon's ἀπάνευθε, “apart”, takes up Odysseus' question, ἦ ἀπάνευθε, “or apart?” (10.425), and points the spies to the camp they will raid.
