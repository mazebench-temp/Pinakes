**The heart in fear.** Agamemnon groans as thick and fast as Zeus' lightning, “from the depths of his heart” (νειόθεν ἐκ κραδίης, 10.10), a phrase found only here. To Nestor he describes his fear as the body feels it: “my heart is not steady, but I am distraught, and my heart leaps out of my breast, and my shining limbs tremble beneath me” (κραδίη δέ μοι ἔξω στηθέων ἐκθρῴσκει, 10.93–95; see [[deos]]). The leaping, pounding heart is the coward's sign in Idomeneus' account of who shows his worth in an ambush: the coward's color changes, he cannot sit still, “his heart pounds loudly against his chest / as he foresees the fates, and his teeth chatter” (13.279–283). Agamemnon's heart pounds for the army, not for himself, and Dolon, caught in the ambush of this book, will show the coward's signs, teeth chattering and limbs trembling (10.374–376, 10.390).

**The heart that urges.** The κραδίη is also the seat of impulse. Diomedes and Dolon volunteer in the same words, “my heart and my proud spirit urge me” (κραδίη καὶ θυμὸς ἀγήνωρ, 10.220, 10.319), and Diomedes praises Odysseus' heart and proud spirit, eager in every toil (10.244; see [[thymos]]).
