---
summary: Agamemnon and Nestor bind on their sandals at night in the verse of Agamemnon's dressing after the Dream (10.22, 10.132), two of the four uses of the full verse in the Iliad.
---

“And under his shining feet he bound his fine sandals” (ποσσὶ δʼ ὑπὸ λιπαροῖσιν ἐδήσατο καλὰ πέδιλα, 10.22, 10.132 = 2.44). The full verse occurs in the Iliad four times: of Agamemnon after the Dream, of Agamemnon and Nestor in this night, and of Hera dressing to beguile Zeus (2.44, 10.22, 10.132, 14.186; see [[dressing-scene]]). It belongs to dressing, not to arming: the armings name greaves and no footwear, and the arming of the two spies names neither (10.254–271; see [[arming-scene]] and [[greaves]]).

The only other sandals bound on for a night errand in the Iliad are Hermes'. When Zeus sends him to bring Priam unseen to Achilles, he binds on his fine sandals, golden and immortal, and takes up the wand with which he charms men's eyes to sleep or wakes them (24.334–345).
