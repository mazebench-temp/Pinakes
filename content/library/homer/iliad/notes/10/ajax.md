summary: Telamonian Ajax is one of the chiefs Agamemnon sends Menelaus to call, and Nestor wishes someone would fetch him and Idomeneus, whose ships lie farthest off; he is one of “the two Ajaxes” who volunteer to go with Diomedes into the Trojan camp (10.53, 10.112–113, 10.228).
refs: [10.113]
---

Agamemnon sends Menelaus to “call Ajax and Idomeneus” (10.53), and Nestor wishes that someone would call “godlike Ajax and lord Idomeneus; for their ships are farthest off” (10.112–113; see [[idomeneus]] and [[godlike]]). Ajax's huts are at one end of the line of ships, and Achilles' at the other (8.222–226 = 11.5–9). The other Ajax, “swift Ajax”, is the one Nestor sends Diomedes to rouse with Meges (10.110, 10.175; see [[ajax-son-of-oileus]]). When Diomedes asks for a companion, “the two Ajaxes were willing, attendants of Ares” (10.228), the phrase in which the narrator told how the pair did not stay when Zeus thundered (8.79; see [[attendants-of-ares]]). Agamemnon leaves the choice to Diomedes, who chooses Odysseus (10.234–247; see [[diomedes]]).
