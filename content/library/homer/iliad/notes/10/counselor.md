---
summary: Nestor tells Agamemnon that Zeus the counselor will not fulfill for Hector all the plans he now hopes for.
---

“Surely Zeus the counselor will not fulfill for Hector all the plans that he now hopes for, I suppose; but I think he will labor under sorrows still greater, if Achilles turns his dear heart from its grievous anger” (10.104–107). Agamemnon fears that the mind of Zeus has turned to Hector, who has devised more grim deeds in a day than any man (10.45–52; [[grim-deeds]]); Nestor answers that the god's counsel is not the same as Hector's hopes. His own hope rests on Achilles, whom the embassy has just failed to move ([[plan-of-zeus]], [[achilles]]). See [[zeus]] and [[nestor]].
