---
summary: The man who will spy on the Trojans is promised “a place at feasts and banquets” for ever (10.217), and the night ends with the two raiders sitting down to a meal near dawn, after their bath (10.578).
grc: [δείπνῳ, δαίτῃσι, εἰλαπίνῃσι]
en: [feasts and banquets]
---

**A place at the feast.** Nestor's promise to the man who will spy on the Trojans ends with honor at table: a black ewe from every chief, “and he will always have a place at feasts and banquets” (ἐν δαίτῃσι καὶ εἰλαπίνῃσι, 10.217). The feast is where honor is shown. The Danaans honored Diomedes “with a seat of honor, with meat, and with full cups”, as Hector reminded him the day before (8.161–162), and Agamemnon keeps Idomeneus' cup always full (4.257–263; see [[time]]). εἰλαπίνη, “banquet”, occurs in the Iliad only here, in a city on the shield of Achilles, and at the feast of the winds (18.491, 23.201).

**The meal before dawn.** When the two have bathed and anointed themselves, they “sat down to their meal” (δείπνῳ ἐφιζανέτην, 10.578; see [[bathing]]) and pour wine to Athena (see [[libation]]). δεῖπνον is the Iliad's word for the meal the army takes in the morning before battle (2.381, 2.399, 8.53, 19.171), and dawn is near (10.251, 11.1). The night began with Agamemnon's feast for the elders and the meal in Achilles' hut (9.89–92, 9.199–221), and it ends with this one.
