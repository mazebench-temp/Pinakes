---
summary: Nestor goes first to Odysseus, “the equal of Zeus in counsel,” and wakes him.
---

“Then first Odysseus, the equal of Zeus in counsel, the Gerenian horseman Nestor roused from sleep” (10.137–138; [[gerenian-horseman]]). Of the men to be called, Nestor goes first to the one whose counsel matches Zeus', and Odysseus comes out of his hut at the first call (10.139–140; [[odysseus]]). The night is a search for counsel: Agamemnon goes to Nestor for a plan (10.17–20), and Diomedes chooses Odysseus “since he is wise beyond all others” (10.247; [[metis]], [[noos]]). See [[nestor]].
