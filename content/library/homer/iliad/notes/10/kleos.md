**Fame and a gift.** Nestor's call for a volunteer ends with the two rewards a hero can hope for: if the man comes back unharmed, “great would be his fame under heaven among all men, and he will have a fine gift” (10.212–213), a black ewe from every chief who commands a ship (see [[doron]] and [[spies-and-scouts]]). ὑπουράνιον κλέος, “fame under heaven”, recurs only in the Odyssey, when Odysseus tells the Cyclops that Agamemnon's fame is now the greatest under heaven (*Odyssey* 9.264). Fame comes first in Nestor's offer, and it is promised to whoever will dare, before anyone has done anything. Hector's offer to the Trojans puts the gift first, the chariot and the two best horses at the Achaean ships, and then κῦδος, the glory of success, for the man who dares (10.305–307; see [[kydos]]). Both leaders offer a gift; Nestor adds what will be said of the man, and Hector the glory he will win.

**With glory.** Odysseus asks Athena for the same thing in his prayer: “grant that we come back to the ships with glory, having done a great deed that the Trojans will rue” (ἐϋκλεῖας, 10.281–282). ἐϋκλεής, “of good fame”, is the adjective of κλέος; Hector, before he faces Achilles, will think it better to die “with glory” before the city (ἐϋκλειῶς, 22.110). The prayer is granted (10.295), the two return with the horses of Rhesus, and the first praise they receive comes from Nestor, who promised the fame: “much-praised Odysseus, great glory of the Achaeans” (10.544; see [[do-not-praise-me-too-much]]).
