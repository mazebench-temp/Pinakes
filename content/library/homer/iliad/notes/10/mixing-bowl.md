---
summary: The libation that ends the night is drawn “from the full mixing bowl” (10.578–579).
---

To Athena, “from the full mixing bowl, they drew honey-sweet wine and poured it out” (ἀπὸ δὲ κρητῆρος … πλείου ἀφυσσόμενοι, 10.578–579). πλεῖος, “full”, occurs only here in the Iliad. A full bowl marks a generous table: the young men fill the mixing bowls “brim-full with drink” for the libations at Chryse and before the embassy (1.470, 9.175; see [[brim-full]]), and earlier the same night Achilles told Patroclus to set out a larger mixing bowl and mix the wine stronger for the envoys (9.202–203). The wine for a libation is drawn from the bowl, as at the oath-sacrifice of Book 3, where ἀφυσσόμενοι is used as here (3.295–296; see [[libation]]).
