---
summary: The ride home is told in two verses of the chariot journey, “and he whipped the horses; and the pair flew on not unwilling toward the hollow ships” (10.530–531), though the riders have neither chariot nor whip. The same verses bring Nestor and Machaon back to the ships in the next book (11.519–520).
refs: [10.513-514, 10.531]
---

The verse of the drive, μάστιξεν δʼ ἵππους, τὼ δʼ οὐκ ἀέκοντε πετέσθην, “and he whipped the horses; and the pair flew on not unwilling” (10.530 = 5.768, 11.519; see [[litotes]]), ends the ride of Odysseus and Diomedes on the horses of Rhesus. They have left Rhesus' chariot behind in the Thracian camp (10.503–513), and Odysseus forgot to take its whip and drove the horses with his bow (10.500–501, 10.513–514). The formula brings back the whip with the drive (see the translation's note on 10.498–501, 10.530, and [[horses]]). The next verse, “toward the hollow ships; for there their hearts were glad to go” (10.531), recurs with it when Nestor drives the wounded Machaon to the ships the next day (11.519–520).

The other steps of the journey are there too, changed for riders. At the start the horses “flew toward the swift ships of the Achaeans” (10.514), as the horses of a chariot journey fly (5.366). At the end the riders get down “onto the ground” instead of unyoking, and the horses are tied at a manger (10.541, 10.566–568), as the Hours tie the goddesses' horses “at their ambrosial mangers” (8.433–434).
