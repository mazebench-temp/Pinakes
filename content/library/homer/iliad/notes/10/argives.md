---
summary: The Argives of Book 10 are the army whose danger keeps its leaders awake. Menelaus fears for the Argives who came to Troy for his sake, Agamemnon seeks counsel to save them, and at the end of the night Nestor fears that the best of the Argives have come to harm (10.27, 10.45, 10.539).
---

Menelaus cannot sleep for fear that harm will come to the Argives, “who for his sake had come over the wide waters to Troy” (10.27–28; see [[menelaus]]), and he goes to the brother who “ruled mightily over all the Argives” (10.32–33). The words recall Calchas' in Book 1, “a man who rules with great power over all the Argives” (1.78–79; see [[agamemnon]]). Agamemnon wants counsel “that will protect and save the Argives and the ships”, and says that Hector's deeds “will weigh on the Argives long and for many a day” (10.44–52; see [[hector]]). Nestor grants that no one “among the Argives” will blame or disobey Menelaus (10.130). The council beyond the ditch is “the kings of the Argives” (10.195; see [[kings]]), and they sit where Hector turned back “from killing the Argives” at nightfall (10.201). Odysseus turns aside Diomedes' praise because he speaks “among Argives who know it” (10.250; see [[do-not-praise-me-too-much]]), and Diomedes kills Dolon so that he will never again be “a bane to the Argives” (10.453; see [[dolon]]). When the spies come back, Nestor addresses the “leaders and rulers of the Argives” and fears that “the best men of the Argives” have come to harm (10.533–539; see [[nestor]]).
