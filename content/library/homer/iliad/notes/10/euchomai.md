---
summary: The verb is used in both its senses in Book 10. The two spies pray to Athena, and Odysseus prays as he holds up Dolon's spoils; and Athena gives Diomedes might so that no other Achaean “might boast that he struck him first”.
passages:
  - 10.295-296 | They pray to Athena
  - 10.364-368 | No other Achaean to boast
---

**Prayer.** After the heron, Odysseus and Diomedes pray to Athena, and the narrator closes the scene with the verb: “So they spoke in prayer (εὐχόμενοι), and Pallas Athena heard them” (10.295; see [[prayer]] and [[reciprocity-with-the-gods]]). When Dolon is dead, Odysseus lifts his spoils to the goddess “and he prayed and said” (εὐχόμενος ἔπος ηὔδα, 10.461), asking her to guide them to the Thracians (10.462–464; see [[dedication-of-spoils]]).

**Boast.** Between the prayers the verb turns to its other sense. As Dolon, fleeing, is about to reach the Achaean sentries, Athena puts might into Diomedes “so that none of the bronze-armored Achaeans might boast that he struck him first, and he himself come second” (φθαίη ἐπευξάμενος βαλέειν, 10.367–368; see [[menos]] and [[kydos]]). ἐπεύχομαι is to declare one's claim over a beaten enemy, as victors in the Iliad do over the man they have felled (11.449, 13.373, 22.330). The three words of 10.368, “strike”, “first” and “boast”, are those of Diomedes' prayer in Book 5, when he asked Athena to let him kill the man “who struck me first and boasts” (ὅς μʼ ἔβαλε φθάμενος καὶ ἐπεύχεται, 5.119; see the note on Book 5). There it was Pandarus' boast; here Athena sees to it that no one else can make it, and the capture of Dolon is Diomedes'. The same verb names a prayer to the gods and a man's claim before other men, and Book 10 uses it both ways (see the article above).
