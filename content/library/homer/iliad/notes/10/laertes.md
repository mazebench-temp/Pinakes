---
summary: Laertes is named once, in his son's patronymic, when Nestor wakes Odysseus with the full verse of his titles, “Son of Laertes, sprung from Zeus, Odysseus of many devices” (10.144).
---

Nestor wakes Odysseus by calling out, and when Odysseus asks what need has brought them through the night, the old man answers with the formal address (10.143–147; see [[odysseus]] and [[nestor]]). It is the verse with which Athena roused Odysseus to stop the flight to the ships (2.173), with which Diomedes called on him to stand by Nestor (8.93), and with which Achilles and Ajax addressed him on the embassy (9.308, 9.624; see [[son-of-laertes]], [[sprung-from-zeus]] and [[of-many-devices]]).

Laertes' father-in-law has a part in the same book, though the Iliad does not say who he is. The helmet Odysseus wears on the raid was stolen by Autolycus (10.266–267), whom the Odyssey makes the father of Laertes' wife Anticleia; naming the newborn Odysseus, Autolycus calls Laertes γαμβρὸς ἐμός, “my son-in-law” (*Odyssey* 11.85, 19.406; see [[autolycus]] and [[the-boars-tusk-helmet]]).
