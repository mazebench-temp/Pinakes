---
summary: Lineage is honored and set aside in the same night. Agamemnon tells Menelaus to wake each man “naming each man by his father and his lineage”, and tells Diomedes not to choose his companion “looking to his lineage, not even if he is more kingly”: the words with which Agamemnon himself claimed precedence over Achilles in Book 9.
passages:
  - 10.65-71 | Agamemnon's instructions to Menelaus
  - 10.233-240 | Agamemnon's warning to Diomedes
---

**Lineage as honor.** Agamemnon sends his brother through the camp to wake the chiefs with a courtesy: “Call out wherever you go, and bid them stay awake, naming each man by his father and his lineage, and giving glory to them all” (πατρόθεν ἐκ γενεῆς ὀνομάζων ἄνδρα ἕκαστον, 10.67–69; see [[kydos]]). πατρόθεν, “from his father”, is found only here: each man is to be called by his father's name, which is to name him with honor (see the article above). The king's brother is to do it without pride of his own, “but let us toil ourselves as well” (10.69–70).

**Lineage set aside.** A hundred and seventy lines later Agamemnon gives the opposite rule for the choice of a companion: Diomedes is not to take a worse man “out of respect”, “looking to his lineage, not even if he is more kingly” (ἐς γενεὴν ὁρόων, μηδʼ εἰ βασιλεύτερός ἐστιν, 10.237–239; see [[aidos]] and [[aristos]]). In the night of Book 9 Agamemnon had claimed Achilles' submission with the same two words: “let him submit to me, inasmuch as I am more kingly, and inasmuch as I claim to be the elder by birth” (βασιλεύτερος, γενεῇ, 9.160–161), and Achilles threw the comparative back, telling him to find for his daughter another Achaean “who is more kingly” (9.392; see [[basileus]]). Here the king himself rules out rank and birth as reasons for a choice. The narrator adds why: he feared for Menelaus (10.240; see [[deos]]).
