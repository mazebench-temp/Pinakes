---
summary: Agamemnon calls Hector “dear to Zeus” as he counts the evils Hector has done; at the end of the raid the title is Odysseus'.
---

“For I never yet saw, nor heard anyone tell, that one man devised so many grim deeds in a single day as Hector, dear to Zeus, has done to the sons of the Achaeans, just as he is, the dear son of no goddess and of no god” (10.47–50; [[grim-deeds]], [[sons-of-the-achaeans]]). The epithet carries Agamemnon's complaint: Hector is beloved of Zeus, though no god's son ([[hector]]). It returns for the other side when the raid is done: “there Odysseus, dear to Zeus, held in the swift horses” at the place where they killed Dolon (10.526–527; [[swift-footed]]). Nestor gives the reason a few lines later: “Zeus the cloud-gatherer loves you both” (10.552; [[cloud-gatherer]]). See [[zeus]] and [[odysseus]].
