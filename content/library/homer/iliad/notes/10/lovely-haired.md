---
summary: In the simile that opens the book Zeus is “the husband of Hera of the lovely hair,” the only time Hera is given this epithet.
---

“As when the husband of Hera of the lovely hair flashes his lightning” (10.5; [[husband-of-hera]]). ἠΰκομος belongs elsewhere to Leto, Thetis, Helen, Briseis and Athena (1.36, 4.512, 3.329, 2.689, 6.92); Hera has it only here, in a verse-end shaped like the one that names Paris, Ἑλένης πόσις ἠϋκόμοιο, “husband of Helen of the lovely hair” ([[husband-of-helen]]). Her own epithets are [[white-armed]] and [[ox-eyed]]. See [[hera]].
