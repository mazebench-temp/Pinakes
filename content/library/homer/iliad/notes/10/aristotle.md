---
summary: In the chapter of the Poetics on problems Aristotle draws on Book 10 more than on any other book of the Iliad, for Dolon's ugly face, the spears planted on their butt-spikes, the ambiguous reckoning of the night and the flutes heard while “all” slept. He quotes “when two go together” twice, and in the Parts of Animals he denies that Dolon's severed head could have spoken.
---

- Dolon was ugly in the face, not misshapen in body (*Poetics* 25, 1461a12–14): [[aristotle-dolons-face]].
- The spears stood upright on their butt-spikes, “as the Illyrians still do now” (*Poetics* 25, 1461a1–4): [[aristotle-upright-spears]].
- πλέω, “more”, in Odysseus' reckoning of the night is ambiguous (*Poetics* 25, 1461a25–26): [[aristotle-portions-of-the-night]].
- If all slept, how did Agamemnon hear the flutes and pipes? “All” is a metaphor for “many” (*Poetics* 25, 1461a16–20, on 2.1–2 and 10.11–13): [[all-the-gods-slept]].
- The mules of 1.50 may be guards; the same word stands at 10.84 (*Poetics* 25, 1461a10–12): [[aristotle-mules]].
- “When two go together” (*Nicomachean Ethics* 8.1, 1155a15; *Politics* 3.16, 1287b13–15): [[when-two-go-together]].
- A severed head cannot speak, whatever some made of 10.457 (*Parts of Animals* 3.10, 673a10–27): [[aristotle-the-speaking-head]].
