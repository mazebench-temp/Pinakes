**A night errand.** When Odysseus sees a man coming from the Trojan camp, he guesses two errands: to spy on the ships, “or to strip one of the corpses of the dead” (10.341–343), and he puts the second guess to Dolon as a question (10.387). The plain between the armies is strewn with the dead of the day and their armor, and the spies go out “through the slaughter, through the corpses, through the armor and the black blood” (10.298, 10.469). Nestor told the Achaeans not to stop for spoils in the fighting but to kill men first, “and afterward, at your ease, you can strip the dead bodies all over the plain” (6.70–71). Odysseus supposes that a Trojan might come out at night to do it.

**Dolon's spoils.** “Then they took from his head the cap of ferret skin, and the wolfskin, and the back-bent bow, and the long spear” (10.458–459), the pieces of his arming, taken from the head down (see [[arming-scene]]). They are not kept. Odysseus holds them up to Athena, sets them on a tamarisk, and marks the place (10.460–468; see [[dedication-of-spoils]]). On the way back Diomedes leaps down and puts “the bloody spoils” into Odysseus' hands (10.528–529), and at the ships Odysseus sets them in the stern of a ship “until they could make ready an offering to Athena” (10.570–571). ἔναρα βροτόεντα, “bloody spoils”, are what Hector prayed his son would one day bring home from the war, and what he hoped, the evening before, to take from Diomedes (6.480, 8.534; see [[bloody-spoils]]). In the night it is Diomedes who carries them.

**What is not taken.** The Thracians' fine armor lies beside them in three rows (10.471–473), and Rhesus has brought armor of gold that Dolon calls fit for the gods, not for mortal men (10.438–441). Diomedes ponders whether to take the chariot “where the inlaid armor lay”, dragging it out by the pole or carrying it, or to kill more of the Thracians, and Athena sends him away with neither (10.503–511; see [[chariot]] and [[decision-scene]]). Of the king's possessions the two take only his horses, the richest spoil of the field (see [[horses]] and [[the-horses-of-rhesus]]).
