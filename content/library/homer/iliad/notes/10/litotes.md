---
summary: Book 10's understatements belong to the night: Dolon promises that he will not be a spy “in vain”, Rhesus' armor is “not fit at all” for mortal men to wear, Apollo does not “keep a blind watch”, and the horses of Rhesus fly to the ships “not unwilling”.
refs: [10.324, 10.440, 10.515, 10.530]
---

- **A spy's promise.** “I will not be a spy for you in vain, nor disappoint your hopes” (οὐχ ἅλιος σκοπὸς … οὐδʼ ἀπὸ δόξης, 10.324). Dolon promises success by denying failure. δόξα occurs in Homer only here and at Od. 11.344 (see the translation's note on 10.324, and [[dolon]]).
- **Armor for gods.** Rhesus' golden armor “is not fit at all for mortal men to wear, but for the immortal gods” (10.440–441). The denial sets it above all human use, and no one wears it (see [[arming-scene]]).
- **A god's eye.** “Nor did Apollo … keep a blind watch” (οὐδʼ ἀλαοσκοπιὴν εἶχε, 10.515), a phrase used only of gods who watch keenly (13.10, 14.135; Od. 8.285; see [[divine-epiphany]]).
- **The horses.** The pair fly to the ships “not unwilling” (οὐκ ἀέκοντε, 10.530), in the verse of the chariot journey (see [[chariot-journey]]).
