summary: The helmet of boar's tusks that Odysseus wears on the night raid was stolen by Autolycus, who broke into the house of Amyntor, son of Ormenus, at Eleon (10.266–267). Name and patronymic are those of Phoenix's father, but the poem does not say that the two are one man.
passages:
  - 10.266-271 | The history of the helmet
---

“It was taken once from Eleon, from Amyntor, son of Ormenus, / by Autolycus, who broke into his well-built house” (10.266–267; see [[the-boars-tusk-helmet]] and [[autolycus]]). The verse ends as 9.448 ends, Ἀμύντορος Ὀρμενίδαο, with the name of the father whom Phoenix fled (see [[phoenix]] and [[ormenus-father-of-amyntor]]), and this index takes them as one man. The poem does not say so, and its places do not agree. Phoenix left “Hellas of the lovely women” and fled “through Hellas” to Phthia (9.447, 9.478; see [[hellas]]), while the Catalogue's Eleon is a town of Boeotia (2.500; see [[eleon]]). Ancient critics tried to join them, putting an Eleon on Parnassus or giving Phoenix another home (Strabo 9.5.18; see [[hellas-or-ormenium]]).

If the two are one, his house is breached twice in the poem's stories, once from inside and once from outside. Phoenix broke through the closely fitted doors of his chamber by night and got away unseen by the watchmen (9.474–477), and Autolycus broke into the πυκινὸν δόμον, the “well-built house”, and carried off the helmet (10.267).
