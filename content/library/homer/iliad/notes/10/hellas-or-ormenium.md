  - 10.266-271 | The history of the helmet
---

Crates' argument starts from Book 10. Meriones lends Odysseus a helmet set with boars' tusks, which Autolycus had once taken “from Eleon, from Amyntor, son of Ormenus” (10.266–267; see [[the-boars-tusk-helmet]] and [[autolycus]]). Phoenix's father is Amyntor, son of Ormenus, too (9.448), and the poem does not say whether they are one man (see [[amyntor]]). Crates took them to be the same, put Eleon on Parnassus, where Autolycus lived, and so made Phoenix a Phocian; Demetrius of Scepsis answered that no Eleon is shown on Parnassus; and others, Strabo adds, read “from Heleon”, a place in the country of Tanagra, in this verse (*Geography* 9.5.18). The Catalogue's Eleon is a Boeotian town (2.500; see [[eleon]]).

Strabo's report adds two details about Book 10. He calls the episode the night-rousing (νυκτεγερσία), a name for the book beside the familiar Doloneia (see [[title-of-book-10]]). And he speaks of “the helmet of Meges, which Odysseus used”, though in our text it is Meriones who gives it (10.260–271). Meges, son of Phyleus, is among the chiefs roused that night (10.110, 10.175), but he lends Odysseus nothing. Whether the slip is Crates', Strabo's or a copyist's cannot be told (see [[meges]] and [[meriones]]).
