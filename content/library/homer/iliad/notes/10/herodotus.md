---
summary: Herodotus gives Nestor's “edge of a razor” to Dionysius of Phocaea before the battle of Lade, uses Homer's rare word for a spear's butt-spike, and tells of the Carians who were once called Leleges and of the Caucones of the Peloponnese, peoples whom Dolon names among the Trojan allies.
---

- “Our affairs stand on the razor's edge, men of Ionia” (*Histories* 6.11.2): [[on-the-razors-edge]].
- **The butt-spike.** σαυρωτήρ, named in Homer only at 10.153, is Herodotus' word for the butt of the spears of Xerxes' picked foot soldiers, a thousand of whom carried golden pomegranates in its place (*Histories* 7.41.2): [[aristotle-upright-spears]].
- **Leleges and Caucones.** The Carians, Herodotus says, came to the mainland from the islands, where in old times they were subjects of Minos and were called Leleges (*Histories* 1.171.2). Some of the Ionian cities took for their kings Caucones of Pylos, descendants of Codrus (*Histories* 1.147.1), and the Minyae drove the Caucones from the land in the western Peloponnese where they then founded Lepreum and five other towns (*Histories* 4.148.4). Dolon places Carians, Leleges and Caucones together by the sea (10.428–429; see [[carians]], [[leleges]] and [[caucones]]).
