---
summary: Agamemnon says that Hector has devised more grim deeds in a day than any man; Diomedes recalls the grim deeds of Tydeus at Thebes; the Trojans gaze at the grim deeds done in the Thracian camp.
grc: [μέρμερα, μέρμερʼ]
---

Agamemnon: “that one man devised so many grim deeds in a single day as Hector” (10.48–49; [[hector]], [[dear-to-zeus]]). Diomedes, praying to Athena, tells how his father Tydeus, on his way back from Thebes, “devised very grim deeds with you, brilliant goddess” (10.289–290; [[tydeus-at-thebes]]). At the end the Trojans wake and “gazed at the grim deeds the men had done before they went off to the hollow ships” (10.524–525; [[untold]]). The word that measures Hector's day measures the two men's night. Hector and Tydeus devise the grim deeds (μητίσασθαι, μήσατο); the Trojans can only look at them. See [[diomedes]].
