---
summary: Nestor asks for a man to go “among the great-hearted Trojans,” and Athena calls Diomedes “son of great-hearted Tydeus” when she warns him to go back.
---

“Is there no man who would trust his own daring heart, and go among the great-hearted Trojans” (10.204–205; [[daring]]). The enemy's epithet measures the danger of the errand. Among the Thracians Athena tells Diomedes, “Think now of your return, son of great-hearted Tydeus, to the hollow ships” (10.509–510; [[hollow-ships]], [[tydeus]]). The father's greatness is held up to the son at the moment he is pondering whether to do more (10.503–507; [[pondering-two-ways]]). See [[diomedes]].
