---
summary: Agamemnon fears for “fair-haired Menelaus” as Diomedes chooses his companion.
---

“So he spoke, and he feared for fair-haired Menelaus” (10.240). Agamemnon has told Diomedes not to choose by birth or rank (10.234–239; [[dear-to-my-heart]]), and the narrator explains why: his brother is among the volunteers (10.230), and he fears that Diomedes will take him into danger. The book began with Menelaus' own fear, for the Argives who came to Troy for his sake (10.25–28; [[bold-war]]); now his brother fears for him. See [[menelaus]] and [[agamemnon]].
