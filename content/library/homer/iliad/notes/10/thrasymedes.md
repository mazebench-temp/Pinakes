**Captain of the watch.** Agamemnon tells Menelaus that the sentries will heed Nestor above all, “for his son commands the sentries”, with Meriones (10.57–59). The son is not named, but in Book 9 Thrasymedes was the first of the seven captains of the watch (9.81; see [[night-watch]] and [[meriones]]). Νέστορος ἀγλαὸς υἱός, “the splendid son of Nestor”, who crosses the ditch with Meriones to the council, is the same man: the kings have called the two captains “to take counsel with them” (10.196–197; see [[kings]]).

**Which son volunteers?** Among the volunteers “Meriones was willing, and very willing the son of Nestor” (10.229). Nestor has another son at Troy, Antilochus, and the verse does not say which is meant. Thrasymedes is the likelier. Besides the kings, only he and Meriones have been called to the council, and the verse names the two together again. Antilochus has no part in the night and was not among the captains of the watch (9.81–84). And the two captains who volunteer and are passed over are the two who then arm the spies (10.255–271; see [[antilochus]] and [[nestor]]).

**Arming Diomedes.** μενεπτόλεμος, “steadfast in battle”, is his epithet here and in the Odyssey (Od. 3.442; see [[steadfast-in-battle]]).

Diomedes was found asleep “with his armor” (10.151), but his own sword “had been left behind at his ship” (10.256; see [[sword]] and [[diomedes]]). καταῖτυξ, “skullcap”, occurs only here, with ἄφαλος and ἄλλοφος, “without ridge or crest” (see the translation's note on 10.257–259 and [[helmet]]). Both spies go out in helmets of hide, not bronze: Diomedes in Thrasymedes' cap of bull's hide, Odysseus in the leather helmet that Meriones gives him (10.261–265; see [[the-boars-tusk-helmet]] and [[arming-scene]]).
