---
summary: When Diomedes asks for a companion, the volunteers are counted in five clauses that each begin with “was willing” (ἤθελε, 10.228–232), and the two spies go out “through the slaughter, through the corpses, through the armor and the black blood” (10.298).
refs: [10.227-232, 10.297-298]
---

**The willing.** “So he spoke, and many were willing to follow Diomedes” (10.227), and the many are named, each with the verb at the head of his clause: ἠθελέτην Αἴαντε δύω … ἤθελε Μηριόνης, μάλα δʼ ἤθελε Νέστορος υἱός, ἤθελε δʼ Ἀτρεΐδης … ἤθελε δʼ ὁ τλήμων Ὀδυσεύς (10.228–231). A dual for the two Ajaxes comes first, then four singulars; the son of Nestor is “very willing”, and the last and longest clause, Odysseus', gives a reason: “for the heart in his breast was always daring” (10.231–232). The list ends with the man Diomedes will choose (10.242–247; see [[odysseus-and-diomedes]]).

**Through.** The two go out “like two lions through the black night, through the slaughter, through the corpses, through the armor and the black blood” (ἂμ φόνον, ἂν νέκυας, διά τʼ ἔντεα καὶ μέλαν αἷμα, 10.297–298). The repeated preposition carries them across the battlefield of the day, and the last half-verse brings them to the Thracians later in the night (10.469; see [[war-and-night]]).
