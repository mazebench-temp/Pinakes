---
summary: The Danaans are named twice in Book 10, both times in Agamemnon's fear for the army: he hopes for a plan “that would ward off evil from all the Danaans”, and tells Nestor that he is “terribly afraid for the Danaans” (10.20, 10.93).
---

Both mentions belong to the king's sleepless fear. He resolves to go to Nestor for a plan that would be ἀλεξίκακος, “that would ward off evil from all the Danaans” (10.20; see [[nestor]]); ἀλεξίκακος occurs only here in Homer. To Nestor he says, “For I am terribly afraid for the Danaans” (αἰνῶς γὰρ Δαναῶν περιδείδια, 10.93; see [[agamemnon]] and [[deos]]). After that the book calls the army Achaeans and Argives (see [[achaeans]] and [[argives]]).
