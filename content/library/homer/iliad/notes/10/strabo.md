---
summary: Strabo uses Book 10 as evidence for Odysseus' wisdom, for the plain of Troy and for Phoenix's homeland. He quotes Diomedes' praise of his companion and Dolon's list of the allies' camps, reports Crates' argument from the boar's-tusk helmet, which he calls the helmet of Meges “in the night-rousing”, and notes that Rhesus shares his name with a river of the Troad.
---

- **Odysseus' wisdom.** Strabo, arguing that Homer adorns Odysseus above all with every virtue, calls him the sacker of cities who took Ilios by counsel and persuasion and deceit, and quotes Diomedes: “If he goes with me, even out of blazing fire / we would both come home” (*Geography* 1.2.4, quoting 10.246–247; see [[odysseus]] and [[odysseus-and-diomedes]]).
- **The helmet and Phoenix's home.** Crates made Phoenix a Phocian from the history of the helmet that Odysseus wore “in the night-rousing” (κατὰ τὴν νυκτεγερσίαν), which Strabo calls the helmet of Meges; Demetrius of Scepsis objected, and others read “Heleon” for “Eleon” in the verse (*Geography* 9.5.18, on 10.266–267): [[hellas-or-ormenium]], [[the-boars-tusk-helmet]] and [[title-of-book-10]].
- **The distance from the ships.** Arguing that the naval station lay far from the city, Strabo cites the spies sent to learn whether the Trojans will stay by the ships “far off” (ἀπόπροθεν), which he takes as far from their own wall, or go back to the city (13.1.36, on 10.208–210; see [[spies-and-scouts]]).
- **Thymbre.** “And toward Thymbre the lot fell to the Lycians” suits the old site of Ilium better than the new, for near it lies the plain of Thymbra, through which the Thymbrius flows into the Scamander at the temple of Thymbraean Apollo, fifty stades from the Ilium of Strabo's day (13.1.35, on 10.430): [[thymbre]].
- **The Leleges.** Some made the Leleges Carians, but Homer distinguishes them, “Toward the sea are the Carians and the Paeonians with their curved bows, / and the Leleges and the Caucones”; the Leleges lived between Aeneas' people and the Cilicians until Achilles sacked their land, and moved to Caria near Halicarnassus (13.1.58, quoting 10.428–429). Their presence in Dolon's list shows that they still fought for Troy under their old king Altes (13.3.1; 21.85–86). Strabo gathers the Leleges and the Caucones among the wandering peoples who had once lived in many places and had since vanished (7.7.2; see [[leleges]] and [[caucones]]).
- **Rhesus.** Rhesus ruled among the Thracian peoples beyond the Strymon (7, fragment 36), and his name is one of the many shared by Thracians and Trojans, since there was also a river Rhesus in the Troad (13.1.21; 12.20; see [[rhesus]]).
- **The tomb of Ilus.** The mound of Ilus, where Hector holds his night council (10.415), is among the landmarks that Strabo says were shown on the Scamandrian plain, and he explains Ilus' burial in the middle of the plain as the founder's who first dared to settle it (13.1.25, 13.1.34; see [[the-tomb-of-ilus]]).
