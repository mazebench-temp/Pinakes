---
summary: Agamemnon and Nestor put on their tunics first when they rise at night, “about his chest”, in a verse found only here (10.21, 10.131). No one in the book puts on a corselet over it.
---

ἔνδυνε περὶ στήθεσσι χιτῶνα, “he put on his tunic about his chest” (10.21, 10.131), is said only of these two night dressings (see [[dressing-scene]]). When he dressed after the Dream, Agamemnon put on “his soft tunic, fine and new-made” (2.42–43); here the verse names only the place, the chest, where an arming puts on the corselet: δεύτερον αὖ θώρηκα περὶ στήθεσσιν ἔδυνεν, “second, he put on the corselet around his chest” (3.332; see [[corselet]] and [[arming-scene]]).

No one in Book 10 puts on a corselet, yet three times the Achaeans are χαλκοχίτωνες, “bronze-armored”, men with tunics of bronze (10.136, 10.287, 10.367; see [[bronze-armored]]). In the Odyssey's tale of a night ambush under Troy, the teller has come out in his tunic alone, οἰοχίτων, and nearly dies of cold (Od. 14.478–489; see [[cloak]] and [[ambush]]).
