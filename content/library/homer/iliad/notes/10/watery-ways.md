summary: Menelaus lies awake for the Argives who for his sake “had come over the wide waters to Troy.”

en: [wide waters]
---

“The Argives, who for his sake had come over the wide waters to Troy, bent on bold war” (10.27–28; [[bold-war]]). ὑγρή, “the wet,” stands for the sea itself, as in the verse of the golden sandals that carry Hermes, and in the Odyssey Athena, over the wet and over the boundless land (ἠμὲν ἐφʼ ὑγρὴν ἠδʼ ἐπʼ ἀπείρονα γαῖαν, 24.341–342; Od. 1.97–98, 5.45–46), and πουλύς, “wide, much,” measures the crossing. See [[menelaus]] and [[sea]].
