---
summary: “Nor did Apollo, god of the silver bow, keep a blind watch”: Apollo sees Athena with Diomedes and wakes the Thracians.
---

“Nor did Apollo, god of the silver bow, keep a blind watch, when he saw Athena attending the son of Tydeus; in resentment at her he went down into the great throng of the Trojans, and roused Hippocoon” (10.515–518; [[hippocoon]], [[kotos]]). Apollo has had no part in the night until now, and he comes too late: the two men are already riding away (10.513–514). The archer god does not shoot; he wakes a sleeper, and the uproar of the Trojans follows (10.519–525; [[untold]]). The litotes οὐδʼ ἀλαοσκοπιὴν εἶχε is used of gods who watch keenly, as of Poseidon, who “kept no blind watch” (13.10; also 14.135). See [[apollo]] and [[son-of-tydeus]].
