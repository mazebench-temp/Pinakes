---
summary: As Walter Leaf reports from the scholia, Aristarchus rejected five passages of Book 10, among them Nestor's question about the mules, which he read as guards, Odysseus' repeated questions that Dolon leaves unanswered, and the verse that makes Diomedes the evil dream. At 10.463 the text follows his reading.
---

Leaf's *Commentary on the Iliad* (1900) reports these judgments from the scholia:

- **10.84.** He rejected Nestor's question whether Agamemnon was looking for one of his οὐρῆες, taking the word here to mean “guards”: [[aristotle-mules]].
- **10.240.** He rejected “So he spoke, and he feared for fair-haired Menelaus” as superfluous; Zenodotus left the verse out. Leaf thought it said openly, in the epic manner, what the verses before it imply (on 10.240; see [[menelaus]]).
- **10.253.** With Zenodotus and Aristophanes he rejected the last verse of Odysseus' reckoning of the night: [[aristotle-portions-of-the-night]].
- **10.409–411.** He rejected the verses in which Odysseus asks Dolon what the Trojans are planning, whether to stay by the ships or go back to the city, as brought in from Nestor's speech (10.208–210), on the ground that Dolon answers the other questions and not this one (on 10.409; see [[spies-and-scouts]]).
- **10.497.** He rejected the verse that names the “evil dream” as Diomedes: [[the-evil-dream]].
- **10.463.** He read ἐπιδωσόμεθʼ, which he explained as “we will honor with gifts”, where nearly all the manuscripts have ἐπιβωσόμεθʼ, “we will call aloud on”. Monro and Allen print his word, and the translation takes it in the sense of θεοὺς ἐπιδώμεθα at 22.254, “we will call upon” (on 10.463; see the translation's note on 10.463).
- **10.10.** He disapproved Zenodotus' φοβέοντο, “fled”, for τρομέοντο, “trembled”, since φοβέομαι in Homer means “flee”, not “fear” (on 10.10; see [[zenodotus]]).
