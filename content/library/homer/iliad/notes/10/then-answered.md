---
summary: Seven replies open with “Then … answered him”, three of them Nestor's in the same whole verse. The first answer of the captured Dolon puts his fear in the place of a name and title: “Then Dolon answered him, and his limbs trembled beneath him” (10.390).
---

The replies with ἠμείβετʼ ἔπειτα: 10.60 (Menelaus), 10.86 (Agamemnon), 10.102, 10.128 and 10.143 (Nestor), 10.390 and 10.426 (Dolon). Nestor's three answers use the same verse, “Then the Gerenian horseman Nestor answered him” (10.102 = 10.128 = 10.143 = 9.162; see [[gerenian-horseman]]).

The second half of the verse usually holds the speaker's name and titles, in a phrase of the right length. At 10.390 it holds Dolon's name and his fear: τὸν δʼ ἠμείβετʼ ἔπειτα Δόλων, ὑπὸ δʼ ἔτρεμε γυῖα, “Then Dolon answered him, and his limbs trembled beneath him”. The half-verse is found only here. Trembling limbs are a mark of fear, and Agamemnon spoke of his own earlier in the night (10.95). At his second answer Dolon has a patronymic in place of his fear, “Then Dolon, son of Eumedes, answered him” (10.426; see [[eumedes]]).
