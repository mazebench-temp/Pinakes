---
summary: θυμὸς ἀγήνωρ, “proud spirit,” urges Diomedes and Dolon to the night's errand and drives Odysseus in every toil; the Trojans and the Mysians are proud peoples.
grc: [ἀγήνορας, ἀγέρωχοι]
---

**The proud spirit.** Diomedes and Dolon volunteer in the same verse: “my heart and my proud spirit urge me” (10.220, 10.319; [[thymos]], [[kradie]]). Diomedes chooses Odysseus, “whose heart and proud spirit are eager above all others in every toil” (10.244). The two volunteers of the night speak the same words; one comes back and the other does not ([[diomedes]], [[dolon]]).

**Proud peoples.** Hector does not let “the proud Trojans” sleep (Τρῶας ἀγήνορας, 10.299), and in Dolon's account of the allies the Mysians are “proud” (Μυσοί τʼ ἀγέρωχοι, 10.430; [[mysians]]). See [[trojans]].
