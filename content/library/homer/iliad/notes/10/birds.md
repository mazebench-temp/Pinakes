summary: The only bird of Book 10 is the heron that Athena sends on the right of Odysseus and Diomedes as they set out. In the dark they cannot see it, but they hear its cry, and Odysseus rejoices at the bird as her sign and prays to her (10.274–282).
grc: ["ὄρνιθʼ"]
passages:
  - 10.274-282 | The heron
---

χαῖρε δὲ τῷ ὄρνιθʼ Ὀδυσεύς, “and Odysseus rejoiced at the bird” (10.277). ὄρνις is the common word for a bird, and it can name a bird of omen, like the eagle that flies on the right after Ajax has spoken, a δεξιὸς ὄρνις, a bird “on the right” (13.821); οἰωνός is the word for the large birds whose flight the seers read (see [[divination]]). The bird of Book 10 is ἐρῳδιός, usually taken as a heron, named only here in Homer (see [[heron]]). It is sent by Athena, not by Zeus, and it is known by its cry, not by its flight, for the night is dark: “they did not see it with their eyes … but they heard it cry” (10.275–276). No seer is needed to read it. Odysseus takes it as the goddess' sign at once, and both men pray to her before they go (10.277–295; see [[prayer]] and [[the-doloneia]]).
