---
summary: Menelaus, sleepless like his brother, covers his back with a dappled leopard's skin before he goes to rouse him (10.29–30). In Homer only the two husbands of Helen wear one: Paris when he steps out to challenge the Achaeans (3.17), and Menelaus here.
grc: [παρδαλέῃ]
---

“First he covered his broad back with a leopard's skin, dappled” (παρδαλέῃ μὲν πρῶτα μετάφρενον εὐρὺ κάλυψε ποικίλῃ, 10.29–30). ποικίλος, “dappled”, is the spotted hide; the same word is “inlaid” of armor in the same night (10.75, 10.149; see [[inlaid]]). The skin is the first of the three pieces of Menelaus' arming, with a bronze helmet and a spear (10.29–31; see [[arming-scene]]). Agamemnon and Diomedes wear lion skins the same night, and the Trojan spy Dolon a wolf's (10.23, 10.177, 10.334; see [[lion-skin]] and [[wolf-skin]]).

The leopard's skin is worn in Homer only by Paris, when he steps out before the Trojans with a bow and two spears to challenge the best of the Argives (3.16–20), and by Menelaus here, the two husbands of Helen. Menelaus cannot sleep for fear for the Argives, “who for his sake had come over the wide waters to Troy” (10.25–28; see [[menelaus]]).
