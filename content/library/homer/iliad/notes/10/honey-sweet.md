---
summary: Three things are honey-sweet in Book 10: the life Diomedes takes from Rhesus, the wheat Diomedes' horses eat, and the wine the two men pour to Athena.
---

Diomedes takes from the thirteenth man, the Thracian king, “his honey-sweet life” (10.494–495; [[rhesus]], [[son-of-oeneus]]). At the end of the raid the captured horses are tied where Diomedes' own horses stand “eating honey-sweet wheat” (10.568–569; [[well-cut]]), and the two men draw “honey-sweet wine” from the full mixing bowl and pour it out to Athena (10.578–579; [[libation]], [[mixing-bowl]]). The life taken and the food and drink of the return share the epithet. See [[diomedes]].
