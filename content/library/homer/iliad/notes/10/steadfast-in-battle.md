summary: “Thrasymedes, steadfast in battle,” arms Diomedes for the raid.
---

“Thrasymedes, steadfast in battle, gave the son of Tydeus a two-edged sword (his own had been left behind at his ship) and a shield; and he put a helmet on his head” (10.255–257; [[two-edged]], [[of-bulls-hide]], [[without-ridge-or-crest]]). Thrasymedes commands the sentries (10.57–59), and the epithet of the man who holds his ground suits the leader of the watch. Meriones, who shares the command, arms Odysseus (10.260–271; [[the-boars-tusk-helmet]]). See [[thrasymedes]] and [[diomedes]].
