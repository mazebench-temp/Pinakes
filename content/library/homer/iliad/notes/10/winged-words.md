---
summary: Twice in Book 10 a speech is introduced with “winged words”, both times after a waking or a sight: Diomedes, roused by Nestor's heel, protests to the old man, and Nestor, glad to find the sentries awake, cheers them on (10.163, 10.191).
---

Both speeches open with the whole-line formula, “and he spoke to him, addressing him with winged words” (10.163 = 8.101), the second in the plural, to the sentries (10.191 = 4.337). Diomedes “sprang up out of sleep very quickly”, and his first words are a protest: “You are a hard man, old sir; you never cease from toil” (10.162–164; see [[diomedes]] and [[nestor]]). Nestor “was glad when he saw them, and cheered them with his words”, and says: “Keep watch like this now, dear children” (10.190–192; see [[night-watch]]). Each speech answers something felt or seen the moment before, as Paolo Vivante observed of the formula.

None of the other speeches of the book, not those of the two councils or of the questioning of Dolon, is brought in with the formula (see [[answering-him]] and [[then-answered]]).
