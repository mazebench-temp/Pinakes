summary: Odysseus opens his prayer before the raid with κλῦθί μευ, Diomedes his with κέκλυθι νῦν καὶ ἐμεῖο, “Now hear me too”, and a single verse closes both: “and Pallas Athena heard them” (10.278, 10.284, 10.295).
grc: [κέκλυθι, "τῶν δʼ ἔκλυε"]
en: [Now hear me too, heard them]
---

Odysseus' prayer begins with the opening of Diomedes' prayer in Book 5, κλῦθί μευ αἰγιόχοιο Διὸς τέκος, “Hear me, child of aegis-bearing Zeus” (10.278; 5.115). Diomedes, praying second, uses κέκλυθι, the reduplicated form of the same imperative, and ends his line with the title that ended it in Book 5: “Now hear me too, child of Zeus, Atrytone” (10.284; see [[atrytone]]). κέκλυθι occurs nowhere else in the Iliad. In the Odyssey it opens two speeches of the disguised Odysseus in Eumaeus' hut, “Hear me now, Eumaeus, and all you other companions”, the first of them his tale of a night ambush at Troy (Od. 14.462–506, 15.307; see [[ambush]]).

The close is the close of Book 5 put into the plural, τῶν δʼ ἔκλυε Παλλὰς Ἀθήνη, “and Pallas Athena heard them” (10.295; τοῦ δʼ ἔκλυε, 5.121). One verse answers two prayers (see [[prayer-type-scene]] and [[prayer]]).
