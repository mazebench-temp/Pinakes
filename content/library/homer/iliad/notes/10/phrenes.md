summary: Agamemnon's mind trembles within him, and he believes that “the mind of Zeus has turned” toward Hector's sacrifices. Nestor's call comes “around the mind” of the sleeping Odysseus, and Diomedes ponders in his mind what more to do among the sleeping Thracians.
grc: [φρήν]
passages:
  - 10.1-16 | Agamemnon sleepless
  - 10.43-52 | The mind of Zeus has turned
---

**A mind that trembles.** The king lies awake “as he pondered many things in his mind” (πολλὰ φρεσὶν ὁρμαίνοντα, 10.4), and he groans so often “from the depths of his heart, and his mind trembled within him” (τρομέοντο δέ οἱ φρένες ἐντός, 10.10). The φρένες, the organ of thought in the chest, shake with fear like limbs, as the sailors of a simile in Book 15 “tremble in their mind” when a wave breaks over the ship (τρομέουσι δέ τε φρένα, 15.627; see [[kradie]] and [[deos]]).

**The mind of Zeus.** Agamemnon explains the defeat to his brother: they need shrewd counsel to save the army, “since the mind of Zeus has turned. He has set his heart more on the sacrifices of Hector” (ἐπεὶ Διὸς ἐτράπετο φρήν· Ἑκτορέοις ἄρα μᾶλλον ἐπὶ φρένα θῆχʼ ἱεροῖσιν, 10.45–46). The singular φρήν, in the nominative, occurs only here in Homer, and the word is used twice in two verses for the god's turning and fixing of his mind. Agamemnon's explanation is a guess about what moves Zeus, and it is not the narrator's: the poem has shown Zeus acting on his promise to Thetis (1.524–530, 8.470–477; see [[plan-of-zeus]] and [[the-mind-of-zeus]]). Zeus does speak later of Hector's many sacrifices to him (22.170–172; see the translation's note on 10.46).

**Around the mind.** Nestor wakes Odysseus by calling out, “and at once the cry came around his mind” (περὶ φρένας ἤλυθʼ ἰωή, 10.139): the sound reaches the sleeper's φρένες and he wakes. The same phrase tells how the wine “came around the wits” of the Cyclops, who then falls asleep (*Odyssey* 9.362). Agamemnon warns Diomedes not to choose his companion “out of respect in your heart” (σῇσι φρεσί, 10.237; see [[aidos]]), and among the sleeping Thracians Diomedes “was pondering this in his mind” (κατὰ φρένα, 10.507), whether to take the chariot or kill more men, until Athena speaks (see [[athena]]). The verb φρονέω, “have in mind”, gives the lion of the simile its “evil intent” and Odysseus his thought for the horses (κακὰ φρονέων, 10.486; τὰ φρονέων, 10.491).
