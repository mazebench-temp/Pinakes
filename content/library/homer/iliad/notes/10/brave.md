---
summary: Hippocoon, whom Apollo wakes, is “the brave cousin of Rhesus,” and Odysseus tells Nestor that the Thracian king was killed by “brave Diomedes.”
grc: [ἀνεψιὸν ἐσθλόν, "ἄνακτʼ ἀγαθὸς Διομήδης"]
---

Apollo “roused Hippocoon, a counselor of the Thracians, the brave cousin of Rhesus” (10.518–519; [[hippocoon]], [[man-of-counsel]]), who wakes to find the horses gone and the men gasping in their blood. Odysseus reports to Nestor: “their lord was killed by brave Diomedes, and beside him twelve companions, all of the best” (10.559–560). The translation renders ἐσθλός and ἀγαθός of persons “brave”; in the formula βοὴν ἀγαθός the same ἀγαθός is “good” ([[good-at-the-war-cry]]). See [[diomedes]].
