---
summary: Readings of Zenodotus in Book 10 survive in the scholia, as Walter Leaf reports them. He wrote “fled” for “trembled” at 10.10, made Dolon the only survivor of five brothers rather than the only son among five sisters, left out two verses that Aristarchus rejected, and held Nestor's “shall I be wrong, or speak the truth?” to be borrowed from the Odyssey.
---

From Leaf's *Commentary on the Iliad* (1900):

- **10.10.** φοβέοντο, “fled”, for τρομέοντο, “trembled”, of Agamemnon's mind; Aristarchus objected that the verb means “flee” in Homer (on 10.10; see [[aristarchus]]).
- **10.240.** He left out the verse in which Agamemnon fears for Menelaus (on 10.240).
- **10.253.** He rejected the last verse of Odysseus' reckoning of the night: [[aristotle-portions-of-the-night]].
- **10.317.** κασιγνήτοισιν for κασιγνήτῃσιν, so that Dolon is the only survivor among five brothers, not the only son among five sisters (on 10.317; see [[dolon]]).
- **10.497.** With Aristophanes he left out the verse that makes Diomedes the evil dream: [[the-evil-dream]].
- **10.515.** ἀλαὸν σκοπιήν, “a blind watch”, in two words, for the compound ἀλαοσκοπιήν (on 10.515).
- **10.534.** He held that Nestor's “shall I be wrong, or speak the truth?” was borrowed from the Odyssey, where Helen speaks the verse (*Odyssey* 4.140; on 10.534).
- **10.545.** λαβέτην for λάβετον, two forms of the dual “you two took” (on 10.545).
