---
summary: “The Gerenian horseman Nestor” is named eight times in Book 10: as he answers Agamemnon, wakes Odysseus and Diomedes, calls for a volunteer beyond the ditch, and questions the two spies on their return.
---

**Waking the chiefs.** The full formula follows Nestor through the night. He answers Agamemnon twice (10.102, 10.128), rouses Odysseus and answers his question (10.138, 10.143), stands over Diomedes and wakes him with his heel (10.157; [[hypnos]]), and answers his protest that he is a hard old man (10.168; [[hard]]).

**The council and the return.** Beyond the ditch “the Gerenian horseman Nestor began to speak” and asked for a man to go among the Trojans (10.203–217; [[spies-and-scouts]], [[under-heaven]]). When Odysseus and Diomedes ride in on the horses of Rhesus, “first the Gerenian horseman Nestor asked” how they took them (10.543–546), in a verse shaped like the one in which Agamemnon questioned the envoys' return the same night (9.672; [[much-praised]]). The old horseman is the first to hear the hooves, and the one who marvels at the horses (10.532–550; [[swift-footed]], [[the-horses-of-rhesus]]). See [[nestor]] and [[horses]].
