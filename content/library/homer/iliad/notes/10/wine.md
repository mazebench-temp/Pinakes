---
summary: The night ends with honey-sweet wine poured to Athena (10.579). Honey-sweet is the word of three things in the last ninety lines of the book: the life of Rhesus, the wheat of Diomedes' horses, and this wine.
---

μελιηδέα οἶνον, “honey-sweet wine” (10.579), is the wine of a libation here, as when Hecuba offers Hector honey-sweet wine to pour to Zeus and the other immortals before he drinks (6.258–260; see [[libation]]). The epithet is used three times at the end of the book. Diomedes robs Rhesus, the thirteenth man, “of his honey-sweet life” (10.495), Diomedes' own horses stand at their manger “eating honey-sweet wheat” (10.569), and the two pour honey-sweet wine to Athena (10.579; see [[honey-sweet]]).
