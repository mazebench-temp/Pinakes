summary: The first half of the book is a chain of night visits from hut to hut, with finding, challenge and errand but no seat and no food. At the end of the night the two raiders come home to the welcome of the Odyssey's guests, with handclasps, a bath, a meal and a libation (10.32–193, 10.540–579).
refs: [10.32-35, 10.73-85, 10.136-147, 10.150-161]

**Finding.** Each visit has its finding verse, which says where the visitor finds the man he seeks and what he is doing. Menelaus finds his brother “putting his fine armor about his shoulders beside the stern of his ship” (10.34–35), and his coming is welcome, the one warm reception of the night. Agamemnon finds Nestor “beside his hut and his black ship, in a soft bed”, his armor beside him (10.74–79), in the verse with which the heralds found Achilles in Book 1 (1.329; see [[hut]]). Nestor and Odysseus find Diomedes asleep outside his hut among his companions (10.150–156), and the visitors find the leaders of the sentries not asleep but “all sitting awake with their arms” (10.181–182; see [[night-watch]]).

**No seat and no meal.** At night the host, not the visitor, asks the first question, and it is a challenge. Nestor asks from his bed: “Who is this, coming alone along the ships through the camp through the dark night, when other mortals are asleep? … Speak, and do not come at me in silence. What need brings you?” (10.82–85; see [[when-other-mortals-are-asleep]]). Odysseus, coming out of his hut, asks: “Why do you wander like this alone along the ships through the camp through the ambrosial night? What need so great has come?” (10.141–142). τίπτε, “why?”, is the host's question in a visit scene (18.385), but the answer here is an alarm, and no one is seated or fed. Diomedes is not even asked: Nestor wakes him with his heel and rebukes him (10.157–161).

**The welcome.** At the end of the night the visits are turned around. The two come back to the ships, get down from the horses, and are welcomed “with handclasps and with soothing words” (10.541–542). The second half of the verse is the one in which Autolycus and his sons welcome the young Odysseus at Parnassus in the Odyssey (Od. 19.414–415). Then the two bathe, anoint themselves and sit down to a meal, pouring wine to Athena (10.572–579), in the order of a guest's welcome in the Odyssey (Od. 4.39–58; see [[bathing]]). A night of visits without hospitality ends with hospitality.
