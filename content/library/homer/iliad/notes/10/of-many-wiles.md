---
summary: “Odysseus of many wiles” is named six times in Book 10: as he takes his shield, in three answers to Dolon, as he drags the dead aside among the Thracians, and in his answer to Nestor.
---

**Before the raid.** “Odysseus of many wiles went into his hut” and came out with his shield (10.148–149; [[inlaid]]).

**With Dolon.** The formula introduces each stage of the questioning: “Answering him, Odysseus of many wiles said: Take heart” (10.382–383; [[answering-him]]); “Smiling at him, Odysseus of many wiles said” (10.400; [[smiling-at-him]]); and the question about the allies' camp (10.423; [[horse-fighting]]).

**Among the Thracians and the return.** While Diomedes kills, “Odysseus of many wiles” drags each man aside by the foot, so that the horses will not shy at the dead (10.488–493; [[fine-maned]]). At the ships the formula introduces his answer to Nestor's question about the horses (10.554). Odysseus does the thinking of the raid, Diomedes the killing. See [[odysseus]] and [[metis]].
