summary: Chariots are promised and left behind. Hector offers his spy a chariot, Dolon asks for Achilles' chariot inlaid with bronze, and the chariot of Rhesus, worked with gold and silver, is the one prize of the Thracian camp that the raiders do not take; Odysseus forgets its whip, and Diomedes is still pondering how to carry it off when Athena sends him away (10.498–511).
grc: [ἐπιδιφριάδος, ῥυμοῦ]

**Promised chariots.** Hector's reward for a spy is “a chariot and two horses with high-arched necks” (10.305). Dolon asks for Achilles' horses “and the chariot inlaid with bronze” (ἅρματα ποικίλα χαλκῷ, 10.322, 10.393), the words for the chariot Agamemnon leaves with his driver when he reviews the army on foot (4.226). Hector swears that no other Trojan “shall ride behind those horses” (10.330), standing in the car they draw, and Odysseus answers that they are hard for mortal men “to master and to drive” (10.403; see [[horses]] and [[the-horses-of-achilles]]).

**Rhesus' chariot.** Dolon tells of it with the horses and the golden armor: “his chariot is finely worked with gold and silver” (10.438), as Hera's chariot has wheels of bronze and gold and a car strung with straps of gold and silver (5.722–731). In the Thracian camp the king's horses are tied by straps to the end of its rail (ἐξ ἐπιδιφριάδος πυμάτης, 10.475). ἐπιδιφριάς occurs only here; it is formed from δίφρος, the car, and seems to be the rail that runs around it, called ἄντυξ elsewhere (5.728).

**Left behind.** Odysseus drives the horses off with his bow, “since he had not thought of the shining whip, to take it in his hands from the inlaid chariot” (10.500–501). Diomedes stays:

{{quote:909e6eec-5b07-5616-8066-f492c01689ff}}

The chariot is light enough, in Diomedes' thought, for one man to lift and carry, or to drag by its pole (ῥυμός, 5.729). He is still deciding when Athena warns him to think of his return before another god wakes the Trojans, and he leaves it (10.507–513; see [[decision-scene]]). In battle a chariot is driven off with its team or abandoned when the team bolts (5.323–327, 6.38–41); this one is left standing, with the armor in it.

**A ride without a chariot.** The raiders ride the horses home (see [[horses]]), and the ride is told in the verses of a chariot drive, “and he whipped the horses; and the pair flew on not unwilling” (10.530; see [[chariot-journey]]). Neither chariot nor whip has been taken.
