Nestor's words to the night assembly were that “this night will either shatter the army or save it” (9.78). Waking Diomedes later the same night, he says it again: “For now it stands for all of us on the edge of a razor, whether grim destruction comes on the Achaeans, or life” (10.173–174; see [[on-the-razors-edge]]). The two alternatives, destruction or life, are the two verbs of Book 9, shatter or save.

The night does not end in either. The embassy has failed, but the raid succeeds: Dolon is taken, Rhesus and twelve Thracians are killed, and the horses are brought in. Nestor, hearing them come, hopes it is Odysseus and Diomedes, “but I am terribly afraid at heart that the best men of the Argives have come to some harm” (10.536–539), and then welcomes them and their horses “like the rays of the sun” (10.544–553). The army is not saved; the next day's battle drives it back to the ships (see [[war-and-night]]).
