---
summary: Peleus is named in Book 10 only in his son's patronymic, in the talk of the horses that Dolon wants, “the horses … that carry the noble son of Peleus” (10.322–323, 10.392). They were the gods' gift to Peleus.
---

Πηλεΐων, “son of Peleus”, names Achilles twice in the book, both times with his horses: “that carry the noble son of Peleus” (ἀμύμονα Πηλεΐωνα, 10.323), in the words with which the Catalogue praised the horses (2.770), and “the single-hoofed horses of the noble son of Peleus” (Πηλεΐωνος ἀγαυοῦ, 10.392; see [[son-of-peleus]] and [[achilles]]). The horses were Peleus' before they were his son's: the gods gave them to Peleus as glorious gifts (16.381, 16.867), Poseidon gave them, Achilles says, and his father gave them to him (23.277–278; see [[the-horses-of-achilles]]). Later writers made them Poseidon's gift at Peleus' wedding to Thetis (Apollodorus, *Library* 3.13.5; see [[thetis]]).
