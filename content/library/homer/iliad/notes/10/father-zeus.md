---
summary: The spears of Diomedes' sleeping companions shine far off “like the lightning of father Zeus.”
---

“And their spears were driven upright on their butt-spikes, and far off the bronze shone like the lightning of father Zeus” (10.152–154; [[like-lightning]], [[spear]]). The comparison takes up the simile that opened the book, in which Agamemnon's groans came as thick as the flashes of Zeus' lightning (10.5–10; [[lightning-before-the-storm]]). The custom of sleeping with spears planted upright was criticized by later readers and defended by Aristotle ([[aristotle-upright-spears]]). See [[zeus]] and [[diomedes]].
