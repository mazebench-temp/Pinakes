---
summary: “Much-enduring brilliant Odysseus” answers Diomedes' praise with a warning against praise and a reckoning of the night.
---

“Son of Tydeus, do not praise me too much, nor rebuke me at all; for you are saying this among Argives who know it” (10.249–250; [[do-not-praise-me-too-much]], [[son-of-tydeus]]). The formula of the Odyssey's hero introduces him at the moment he is chosen for the raid, and he turns at once to the hour: the night is far spent, and two of its three portions are gone (10.251–253; [[moira]], [[aristotle-portions-of-the-night]]). A few lines earlier he volunteered as “enduring Odysseus,” with τλήμων, from the same root as πολύτλας (10.231; [[enduring]]). See [[odysseus]].
