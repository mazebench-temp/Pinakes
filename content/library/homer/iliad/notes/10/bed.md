summary: Book 10 is a night of beds: Nestor's soft bed beside his hut, the ox hide and rug on which Diomedes sleeps among his men, the “sleeping places” of the Trojans and the “beds” of the Thracian men that Odysseus asks about and prays to reach, and Rhesus asleep in the middle of his men.

**Nestor.** Agamemnon finds the old man “in a soft bed” beside his hut and his ship, with his armor beside him (εὐνῇ ἔνι μαλακῇ, 10.74–79), and Nestor raises himself on his elbow to challenge the visitor (10.80; see [[nestor]]).

**Diomedes.** He sleeps outside his hut on the ground, his companions around him with their shields under their heads and their spears planted upright, “and under him was spread the hide of an ox of the fields, and under his head a shining rug was stretched” (10.151–156; see [[aristotle-upright-spears]]). It is a soldier's bed in the open. Earlier the same night a bed was spread for Phoenix in Achilles' hut with fleeces, a rug and fine linen (9.658–662), and in the last book Achilles has beds made for Priam and his herald in his porch, with purple blankets, rugs and woolly cloaks (24.643–646).

**The enemy's beds.** Odysseus asks Dolon about “the watches and the sleeping places of the other Trojans” (φυλακαί τε καὶ εὐναί, 10.408), and prays to Athena to guide them “to the horses and the beds of the Thracian men” (ἵππους τε καὶ εὐνάς, 10.464). The Thracians lie on the ground, worn out, with their armor beside them and a yoke of horses beside each man, and Rhesus sleeps in the middle (10.471–475). He is killed in his sleep, “as he lay gasping; for an evil dream stood over his head” (10.496; see [[onar]] and [[rhesus]]).
