---
summary: Nestor says that Zeus the cloud-gatherer loves Odysseus and Diomedes, and that some god must have given them Rhesus' horses.
---

“No, I think some god met you and gave them to you; for Zeus the cloud-gatherer loves you both, and the daughter of aegis-bearing Zeus, gray-eyed Athena” (10.551–553). Nestor has never seen horses like these, “terribly like the rays of the sun” (10.547), and he explains them by the favor of the gods. Odysseus answers that a god could easily give even better horses, but that these are Thracian, taken from the king Diomedes killed (10.556–560; [[gods-and-mortals]], [[the-horses-of-rhesus]]). In the narrative only Athena has helped them; Nestor gives the credit to her father too. See [[zeus]] and [[nestor]].
