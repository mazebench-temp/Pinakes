---
summary: Sweet sleep does not hold Agamemnon at the start of the night, nor settle on his eyes when he goes to Nestor; the sentries lose it as they keep watch.
grc: ["ὕπνος ἔχε γλυκερὸς"]
---

The book opens with the others asleep, “overcome by soft sleep,” and Agamemnon “not held by sweet sleep” (10.2–4; [[soft]]), with γλυκερός, a longer form of γλυκύς. He tells Nestor that he wanders “because sweet sleep does not settle on my eyes” (10.91), and the sentries at the ditch stay awake: “so sweet sleep was lost from their eyelids as they kept watch through the evil night” (10.187–188; [[night-watch]], [[fierce-hearted]]). The opening repeats the pattern of Book 2, where all sleep and sweet sleep does not hold Zeus (2.1–2). In Book 10 the Achaean leaders do not sleep, and the Thracians who do are killed in their sleep ([[hypnos]], [[war-and-night]]). See [[agamemnon]].
