---
summary: Both sides send out spies on the same night, in matching scenes: Nestor and Hector each ask for a volunteer and promise a reward, each call is met with the same silence, and the volunteers answer in the same verse. The Achaean pair catch the Trojan spy and learn from him what they were sent to learn.
passages:
  - 10.204-232 | Volunteers for the night
  - 10.299-337 | Hector sends Dolon
  - 10.338-350 | The two spies meet
---

**Two calls.** Menelaus guesses the plan before it is made: is his brother arming to send someone “to go and spy on the Trojans”? No one will take on that task, “to go out alone and spy on the enemy through the ambrosial night” (10.37–41). In the council beyond the ditch Nestor asks for a man to go among the Trojans, catch a straggler or hear some talk, and learn “what they are planning among themselves”, whether they mean to stay by the ships or go back to the city; he would win “great fame under heaven”, and from every chief a black ewe with her lamb (10.204–217; see [[kleos]] and [[doron]]). Across the plain Hector asks for a man to go close to the ships and learn whether they are still guarded or the Achaeans are planning flight, for the reward of a chariot and the two best horses at the ships (10.303–312). The two scenes are built alike. Each call is met with the same verse, “So he spoke, and they all fell hushed in silence” (10.218 = 10.313; see [[silence]]), and each volunteer begins with the same words, “my heart and my proud spirit urge me” (10.220 = 10.319; see [[thymos]]).

**Two volunteers.** Here the scenes part. Diomedes asks for a companion, “when two go together, one sees before the other” (10.224–226; see [[when-two-go-together]]), and is given the choice of the best; Dolon asks for an oath on the scepter that he will have the horses of Achilles, and goes alone (10.319–337; see [[dolon]] and [[oath]]). The Achaeans dress in leather caps and borrowed arms, the Trojan in a wolf's skin and a cap of ferret skin (10.254–271, 10.333–335; see [[arming-scene]]).

**The spies meet.** Odysseus sees a man coming from the Trojan camp and cannot tell “whether he comes to spy on our ships, or to strip one of the corpses of the dead” (10.341–343). The two lie down among the dead and let him run past “in his thoughtlessness” (10.350), then cut him off from his own lines. The man sent alone to learn becomes the enemy's source. Odysseus asks him, in the very verses of Nestor's speech, “what they are planning among themselves, whether they mean to stay there by the ships, far from home, or to the city go back again” (10.409–411 = 10.208–210), and Dolon, asked why he came, repeats Hector's question in Hector's words (10.395–399 = 10.308–312). Each side's question is asked in the same verses; only the Achaeans get an answer. Dolon tells them where Hector holds council, how the watch is kept, where each of the allies sleeps, and where the newly come Thracians lie (10.413–441; see [[trojan-allies]]). Diomedes kills him because a spy let go would come back “either to spy, or to fight face to face” (10.451), and Odysseus reports him to Nestor as “a thirteenth, a spy … one sent out to be a scout upon our army” (10.561–562).

**The reward.** The spying turns into a raid, and what the two bring back is not news but horses. Hector promised Dolon the best horses at the Achaean ships; the Achaeans take the best horses in the Trojan camp, “the finest horses I have seen, and the biggest”, as Dolon himself described them (10.436; see [[the-horses-of-rhesus]]). Whether Nestor's ewes were ever given is not told.
