---
summary: In the chase of Dolon, Odysseus is “Odysseus, sacker of cities.”
---

“So the son of Tydeus and Odysseus, sacker of cities, cut him off from the army and chased him without letting up” (10.363–364; [[sharp-toothed]], [[son-of-tydeus]]). The epithet of the destroyer of Troy is given in the middle of a hunt, with the article, ὃ πτολίπορθος Ὀδυσσεύς, as the article stands with ὁ τλήμων Ὀδυσεύς (10.231, 10.498; [[enduring]]). Book 2 gave him the epithet as he rose to speak (2.278). See [[odysseus]] and [[dolon]].
