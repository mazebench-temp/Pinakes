---
summary: Hera takes no part in Book 10 and is named only as the wife of Zeus, in the simile of the lightning of “the husband of Hera of the lovely hair” (10.5) and in Hector's oath by “the loud-thundering husband of Hera” (10.329).
---

Both lines name Zeus by his marriage (see [[zeus]]). The second is a formula. ἐρίγδουπος πόσις Ἥρης ended Agamemnon's oath at the truce for the dead, “As for the oaths, let Zeus be witness, the loud-thundering husband of Hera” (7.411; see [[loud-thundering]] and [[oath]]), and it returns twice more in the Iliad (13.154, 16.88) and three times in the Odyssey (*Odyssey* 8.465, 15.112, 15.180).

The first is not. Only here in Homer is Hera ἠΰκομος, “of the lovely hair”, an epithet of Leto, Thetis, Helen and Briseis (1.36, 4.512, 3.329, 2.689), and of Athena in three verses of Book 6 (6.92, 6.273, 6.303; see [[lovely-haired]]). The verse-end πόσις Ἥρης ἠϋκόμοιο is shaped like the one that names Paris, “the husband of Helen of the lovely hair” (3.329, 7.355, 8.82). Hera's own epithets are “white-armed” and “ox-eyed” (see [[white-armed]] and [[ox-eyed]]).
