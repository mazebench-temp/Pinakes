summary: The helmets of the night are caps. Menelaus alone puts on a bronze helmet, and Nestor leaves his shining one beside his bed; Thrasymedes sets on Diomedes' head a skullcap of bull's hide “without ridge or crest”, Meriones gives Odysseus the leather helmet faced with boar's tusks, and the Trojan spy Dolon wears a cap of ferret skin, the first thing taken from his corpse.
grc: [στεφάνην, καταῖτυξ]
en: [skullcap, cap]
passages:
  - 10.257-265 | The two caps
---

**Bronze.** Menelaus is the only man of the night to arm with a bronze helmet: “lifting a helmet of bronze he set it on his head” (10.30–31). The word is στεφάνη, which names the rim of a helmet at 7.12 and a whole helmet here and at 11.96. Nestor's “shining helmet” (τρυφάλεια) lies beside his bed with his shield and spears, and he leaves it there (10.76).

**Caps of hide.** For the raid Thrasymedes puts on Diomedes' head “a helmet … of bull's hide, without ridge or crest, the kind called a skullcap, and it guards the heads of young men in their prime” (10.257–259). καταῖτυξ, ἄφαλος and ἄλλοφος occur only here (see [[without-ridge-or-crest]] and [[of-bulls-hide]]), and the Suda explains καταῖτυξ as a kind of helmet without a crest (κ 901). The cap is a battle helmet with its parts taken away: no φάλος, the ridge on which Menelaus' sword shattered (3.361–363), and no λόφος, the horsehair crest that nods terribly above a helmet in a full arming (3.337; see [[arming-scene]] and [[horsehair-crested]]). Meriones puts on Odysseus' head a helmet “made of leather”, stretched inside with thongs and set outside with a boar's white tusks, with a lining of felt (10.261–265; see [[the-boars-tusk-helmet]]). The Trojan spy wears “a cap of ferret skin” (κτιδέη κυνέη, 10.335; see [[ferret]]), and his killers take it from his head before anything else (10.458).

**κυνέη.** All three are κυνέαι. The word is formed from κύων, “dog”, as if the first helmets had been caps of dogskin, and the helmets of the day's battles are κυνέαι too (3.316, 3.336). In the night of Book 10 every κυνέη is made of hide, and not one has a crest. When Agamemnon arms at dawn his helmet has two ridges, four plates and a horsehair crest that nods terribly (11.41–42).
