---
summary: The verse of address is spoken three times in Book 10: by Agamemnon to Nestor when he wakes him, by Nestor to Odysseus when the spies return, and by Odysseus to Nestor in answer.
---

Agamemnon names himself to Nestor in the dark: “Nestor, son of Neleus, great glory of the Achaeans, you will know Agamemnon” (10.87–88; [[son-of-neleus]]). At the end of the night the two verses of address are exchanged. Nestor greets Odysseus, “Come, tell me, much-praised Odysseus, great glory of the Achaeans” (10.544; [[much-praised]]), the verse with which Agamemnon greeted him on his return from Achilles earlier the same night (9.673), and Odysseus answers, “Nestor, son of Neleus, great glory of the Achaeans” (10.555). The greeting that met the failed embassy now meets a raid that has succeeded. See [[nestor]] and [[odysseus]].
