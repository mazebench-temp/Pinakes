summary: Odysseus tells Dolon that Achilles' horses are hard for mortal men to master, and Dolon says that Rhesus' golden armor is fit for the gods and not for mortal men.
grc: ["ἀνδράσι γε θνητοῖσι", καταθνητοῖσιν]
en: [mortal]
---

Two of the prizes Dolon names are too great for men. Odysseus answers his hope of Achilles' horses: they are hard “for mortal men to master and to drive, for any other than Achilles, whom an immortal mother bore” (10.403–404; [[grievous]], [[the-horses-of-achilles]]). Dolon says of the golden armor of Rhesus that “it is not fit at all for mortal men to wear, but for the immortal gods” (10.440–441; [[huge]], [[immortals]]). In both the line between mortal and immortal is drawn by what a man may own. See [[dolon]].
