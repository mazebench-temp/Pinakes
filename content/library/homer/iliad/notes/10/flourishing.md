The cap that Thrasymedes puts on Diomedes is the kind that “guards the heads of young men in their prime” (θαλερῶν αἰζηῶν, 10.259; [[without-ridge-or-crest]], [[of-bulls-hide]]). Odysseus marks the tamarisk where he has set Dolon's spoils, “gathering together reeds and the luxuriant branches of the tamarisk” (10.467; [[swift-night]]). ἐριθηλής, “very flourishing,” comes from the same root as θαλερός, θάλλω, “bloom.” See [[odysseus]].
