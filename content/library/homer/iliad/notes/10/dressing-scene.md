Both dressings begin as the dressing of Book 2 began. Agamemnon rises (ὀρθωθείς, as at 2.42), puts on his tunic (ἔνδυνε … χιτῶνα, as at 2.42), “and under his shining feet he bound his fine sandals”, the sandal verse of Book 2 (10.21–22; 2.42–44); Nestor dresses in the same two verses:

{{quote:8a953499-8276-5e06-8207-313312dd9caf}}

The tunic goes on “about his chest” (περὶ στήθεσσι), where an arming puts the corselet (3.332); no other verse puts a tunic there (see [[tunic]] and [[sandals]]).

What follows changes with the man. Agamemnon puts on “the blood-red skin of a lion”, where in Book 2 he threw a great mantle around him, and takes up a spear where he took the scepter of his fathers (10.23–24; 2.43, 2.46; see [[lion-skin]]). He has no sword. The dressing of a king going to the council has become the arming of a man going out into the dark (see [[arming-scene]]). Nestor puts on a crimson cloak, double and wide, takes “a strong spear, tipped with sharp bronze”, and goes “along the ships of the bronze-armored Achaeans” (10.133–136), the half-verse with which Agamemnon went out with his scepter in Book 2 (2.47; see [[cloak]]).
