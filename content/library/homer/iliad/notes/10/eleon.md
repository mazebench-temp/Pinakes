---
summary: Eleon is named a second time as the home of Amyntor, son of Ormenus, from whose house Autolycus stole the boar's-tusk helmet (10.266).
grc: [Ἐλεῶνος]
---

“It was taken once from Eleon, from Amyntor, son of Ormenus” (ἐξ Ἐλεῶνος, 10.266; see [[the-boars-tusk-helmet]], [[amyntor]] and [[autolycus]]). The only other Eleon in Homer is the Boeotian town of the Catalogue (2.500), and the verse gives no sign that another is meant. But if this Amyntor is Phoenix's father, his home was in Hellas (9.447–448), and ancient critics looked for another Eleon. Crates of Mallos placed one on Parnassus, where Autolycus lived, so that the thief robbed a neighbor; Demetrius of Scepsis answered that no Eleon was shown on Parnassus, only Neon, founded after the Trojan war. Others read ἐξ Ἑλεῶνος, “from Heleon”, a village of Tanagra (Strabo, *Geography* 9.5.18; see [[hellas-or-ormenium]] and [[strabo]]); Strabo explains the name of that Heleon from the marshes, ἕλη, around it (9.2.12).
