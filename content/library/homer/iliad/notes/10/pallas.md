---
summary: Pallas Athena loves Odysseus, sends the heron, and hears the two men's prayers.
---

Diomedes chooses Odysseus because his heart is eager in every toil, “and Pallas Athena loves him” (10.244–245). As the two set out, the heron on the right is sent them “by Pallas Athena” (10.274–275; [[heron]], [[divination]]), and after the two prayers, “Pallas Athena heard them” (10.295; [[prayer]], [[yearling]]). The double name stands at the three points before the raid where the goddess's favor is declared. See [[athena]].
