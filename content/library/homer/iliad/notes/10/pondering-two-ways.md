en: [pondered many things in his mind, pondered, pondering this in his mind]

Both verbs of deliberation are used, each in a shortened form. ὁρμαίνω, “turn over in the mind”, describes Agamemnon awake while the others sleep, πολλὰ φρεσὶν ὁρμαίνοντα (10.4), where Zeus, at the start of Book 2, μερμήριζε κατὰ φρένα, “was pondering in his mind” (2.3). μερμηρίζω describes Diomedes in the Thracian camp: μερμήριζε μένων, “he stayed and pondered” (10.503), with three courses instead of two (see [[decision-scene]]). ὁρμαίνω returns with the interruption, εἷος ὃ ταῦθʼ ὥρμαινε κατὰ φρένα, “While he was pondering this in his mind” (10.507).

The fuller verse, with καὶ κατὰ θυμόν, “and in his heart”, marks an interruption every time it is used. While Achilles ponders whether to draw his sword on Agamemnon, Athena comes (1.193–194); while Odysseus, cut off, ponders whether to flee, the ranks of the Trojans close on him (11.411–412); while Achilles ponders why the Achaeans are fleeing, Antilochus arrives with the news of Patroclus' death (18.15–16). In Book 10 the verse is shortened and the interrupter is Athena again.
