---
summary: Andromache mixed wine for Hector's horses, the only wine given to horses in Homer; Agamemnon recalls the bowls brim-full of wine over which the army boasted on Lemnos; and the Trojans fetch honey-sweet wine from the city for their night on the plain.
grc: [οἴνοιο, οἰνίζεσθε]
passages:
  - 8.185-190 | Hector speaks to his horses
  - 8.229-232 | The boasts on Lemnos
---

**For horses.** Hector reminds his horses that Andromache set honey-sweet wheat before them “and mixing wine for you to drink whenever your heart bade you, before me, who boast that I am her husband in my prime” (8.188–190). ἐγκεράσασα, “mixing in”, is the word for mixing wine with water. Wine given to horses occurs nowhere else in Homer (see the translation's note on 8.188–190 and [[the-horses-of-hector]]). The horses were served first, before their master, and their wine was poured as freely as the cup of honor that Agamemnon keeps full for Idomeneus, “to drink whenever your heart bids you” (4.263, πιέειν ὅτε θυμὸς ἀνώγοι, nearly the words of 8.189; see [[feast]]).

**On Lemnos.** Agamemnon recalls the boasts the army made on Lemnos, “eating much meat of straight-horned cattle and drinking mixing bowls brim-full of wine” (κρητῆρας ἐπιστεφέας οἴνοιο, 8.231–232; see [[mixing-bowl]] and [[brim-full]]). ἐπιστεφής, “brim-full”, pictures a bowl crowned with drink, as the young men “filled the mixing bowls brim-full with drink” (ἐπεστέψαντο) at the feast after the plague (1.470). The wine the boasts were made over is wine of Lemnos: at the end of Book 7 ships come from Lemnos with wine sent by Euneus, son of Jason, and the Achaeans buy it with bronze and iron, hides, cattle and slaves (7.467–475; see [[lemnos]]).

**On the plain.** Hector orders the Trojans to bring cattle and sheep from the city, “and provide yourselves with honey-sweet wine” (οἶνον δὲ μελίφρονα οἰνίζεσθε, 8.506; see [[honey-sweet]]), and they do (8.546). οἰνίζομαι, “get wine”, occurs only here and of the Achaeans buying the Lemnian wine (7.472). Both armies have their wine for the night: the Achaeans from the Lemnian ships, the Trojans from their own halls.
