---
summary: Agamemnon reminds Zeus that he never passed his “beautiful altar” without sacrifice, and Zeus' eagle drops the fawn “beside the beautiful altar of Zeus.”
---

The epithet marks the altar at the two ends of the prayer and its answer. Agamemnon: “Yet I say that I never passed by your beautiful altar in my many-benched ship on my ill-starred way here, but on every one I burned the fat and the thighs of oxen” (8.238–240; [[many-benched]], [[sacrifice]]). Zeus answers with a sign at an altar: the eagle drops the fawn “beside the beautiful altar of Zeus … where the Achaeans used to sacrifice to Zeus, lord of all omens” (8.249–250; [[lord-of-all-omens]], [[surest-of-birds]], [[altar]]). The altar that the king had honored is where the god's sign comes down. See [[prayer]].
