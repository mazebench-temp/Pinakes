---
summary: The full patronymic Νηληϊάδης appears for the first time in the Iliad when Diomedes takes his stand before the horses of “the old son of Neleus.”
grc: ["Νηληϊάδ*"]
---

Book 2 called Nestor “the son of Neleus” with the adjective Νηλήϊος (2.20). Here the patronymic itself appears for the first time in the poem: Diomedes “stood in front of the horses of the old son of Neleus” (8.100). The genitive Νηληϊάδαο γέροντος ends the verse, as it does again in Book 15, when the Trojans have reached the ships and Zeus thunders, “hearing the prayers of the old son of Neleus” (15.377–378). Both times the old man is in need. In Book 8 Diomedes answers: the young fighters are pressing him, “your strength is loosed, and grievous old age is upon you,” and he takes Nestor into his own chariot (8.102–108; [[geron]], [[diomedes]]).

The vocative is the address of the later books, ὦ Νέστορ Νηληϊάδη, μέγα κῦδος Ἀχαιῶν, “Nestor, son of Neleus, great glory of the Achaeans” (10.87, 10.555, 11.511, 14.42). See [[nestor]] and [[neleus]].
