---
summary: Zeus holds the assembly of the gods “on the topmost peak of many-ridged Olympus,” the verse that found him sitting apart in Books 1 and 5.
---

The verse ἀκροτάτῃ κορυφῇ πολυδειράδος Οὐλύμποιο occurs three times in the Iliad, and each time the highest peak is Zeus' place. Thetis found him there alone (1.498–499), and Hera and Athena found him there before they asked leave to strike Ares (5.753–754). Here he calls all the gods to it and forbids them the war (8.2–27; [[delighting-in-thunder]], [[assembly-type-scene]]). It is from a peak of Olympus that he imagines hanging the golden rope, with the earth and the sea and all the gods dangling from it in midair (8.24–26; [[the-golden-rope]]). He does not stay there: he drives to Ida and watches the battle from Gargarus (8.41–52; [[gargarus]]), and comes home at nightfall to “great Olympus,” which shakes beneath his feet (8.438–443; [[well-wheeled]]).
