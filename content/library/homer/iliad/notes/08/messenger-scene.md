---
summary: Zeus sees Hera and Athena from Ida and sends Iris to turn them back; she flies from Ida to Olympus, meets them at the outermost gates, delivers his threat, and goes. There is no answer to carry back.
refs: [8.397-399, 8.409-413, 8.425]
passages:
  - 8.397-425 | Iris turns the goddesses back
---

The scene has its usual parts (see [[messenger-scene]]). The sender sees and is angry: “when father Zeus saw them from Ida, he was terribly angry, and he urged on golden-winged Iris to carry a message” (8.397–398; see [[iris]]). He gives the message in direct speech (8.399–408). The messenger goes, “from the mountains of Ida to high Olympus” (8.409–410), finds those she was sent to, and delivers the message in the sender's words (8.411–424; see [[message-repeated]]). Then “swift-footed Iris went away” (8.425).

Two things are unusual. The messenger meets her listeners on the road, “at the outermost gates of many-folded Olympus”, and “held them back” (8.411–412), so that the delivery of the message is itself the barrier. And no answer goes back to the sender: Hera gives up at once and turns the horses round (8.426–432).

The verses of the sending are Iris' own formulas. “And he urged on golden-winged Iris to carry a message” (8.398) and “Go, swift Iris” (8.399) return when Zeus sends her to Hector in Book 11 (11.185–186), “Go, swift Iris” again when he sends her to Poseidon and to Priam (15.158, 24.144; see [[swift-iris]]), and “storm-footed Iris rose to carry the message” (8.409) when he sends her to Thetis (24.77; see [[golden-winged]] and [[storm-footed]]).
