---
summary: The formula μώνυχας ἵππους marks two retreats before Zeus: Nestor turns Diomedes' horses to flight from the thunderbolt, and Hera turns the goddesses' horses back from the gates of Olympus.
---

**Nestor's flight.** When Zeus' thunderbolt falls in front of Diomedes' horses, Nestor tells him: “Son of Tydeus, come, turn the single-hoofed horses to flight” (8.139), and when Diomedes has spoken his fear of Hector's boast, “So he spoke, and turned the single-hoofed horses to flight” (8.157; [[groaning]]). The same verse-end gives the command and the deed.

**The goddesses' retreat.** Athena tells Hera, “But now harness the single-hoofed horses for the two of us” (8.374), and when Iris has delivered Zeus' threat, Hera gives in: “So she spoke, and turned back the single-hoofed horses” (8.432; [[child-of-aegis-bearing-zeus]]). The mortals and the goddesses retreat with the same horses' epithet, both turned by the same god. See [[horses]].
