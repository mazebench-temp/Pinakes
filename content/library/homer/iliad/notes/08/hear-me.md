summary: Zeus opens his assembly with the formula of Book 7, “Hear me … so that I may say what the heart in my breast commands me”, and Hector opens his with the Trojan form of address, “Hear me, Trojans and Dardanians and allies”.
passages:
  - 8.5-6 | Zeus addresses the gods
  - 8.497 | Hector addresses the Trojans
---

**Zeus.** “Hear me, all you gods and all you goddesses, so that I may say what the heart in my breast commands me” (8.5–6). The second verse was first heard in Book 7, in Hector's challenge to both armies and in the Trojan assembly (7.68, 7.349, 7.369). The address to “all you gods and all you goddesses” is repeated in Zeus' challenge to pull against him on the golden rope (8.20; see [[the-golden-rope]]), and the two verses return together when Agamemnon, in Book 19, tells how Zeus announced the birth of Heracles to the gods (19.101–102, with ἀνώγει, “bids”, in place of κελεύει). The formula of a speech to a gathering introduces a decree (see [[assembly]]).

**Hector.** “Hear me, Trojans and Dardanians and allies” (8.497). It is the Trojan form of address, used by Agamemnon to the Trojans after the duel of Book 3 (3.456) and by Antenor and Priam in the Trojan assembly (7.348, 7.368). Hector uses it here for the first time, in the field and to the whole army, allies and all (see [[trojan-allies]] and [[dardanians]]).
