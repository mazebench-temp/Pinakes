---
summary: Zeus names himself “the son of Cronus, supreme in might” in his prophecy to Hera; Agamemnon uses the word of “the mighty kings” whom Zeus has struck with blind folly.
grc: [ὑπερμενέων]
en: [mighty]
---

**Of kings.** Agamemnon begins his prayer with a reproach: “Father Zeus, have you ever before struck any of the mighty kings with such blind folly, and robbed him of great glory?” (8.236–237; [[ate]], [[father-zeus]]). Homer gives the epithet to kings and to the Trojan allies as well as to Zeus, and the translation renders it “mighty” when it describes men.

**Of Zeus.** At nightfall Zeus uses the epithet of himself, in the accusative ὑπερμενέα Κρονίωνα: “At dawn you will see the son of Cronus, supreme in might, more than ever, if you wish, ox-eyed queen Hera, destroying the great army of the Argive spearmen” (8.470–472; [[son-of-cronus]], [[ox-eyed]], [[spearmen]]). The king complains that the mightiest of men has been struck down; the mightiest of the gods promises a worse day tomorrow ([[plan-of-zeus]]). See [[zeus]].
