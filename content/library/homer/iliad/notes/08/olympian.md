---
summary: “The Olympian” rouses the Trojans' might again after Hector has struck down Teucer, and the Achaeans are driven back to their ditch.
---

“And again the Olympian roused might in the Trojans” (8.335). The name stands alone for Zeus, without Ζεύς ([[zeus]]), and it marks the turn of the afternoon. Teucer has killed eight men and two more, Hector has broken his bowstring with a stone, and the archer has been carried to the ships (8.266–334; [[back-bent]]). Now Zeus gives the Trojans their strength back ([[menos]]), and they drive the Achaeans straight back to the deep ditch, with Hector among the foremost (8.336–337; [[exulting-in-strength]], [[dug]]). The rally that the eagle began is over ([[surest-of-birds]]). See [[plan-of-zeus]].
