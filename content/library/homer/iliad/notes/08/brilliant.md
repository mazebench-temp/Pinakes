---
summary: δῖος honors Alexander as he shoots Nestor's horse, Odysseus as he runs past Diomedes' call, Lampus among Hector's horses, and Alastor as he carries the wounded Teucer.
grc: ["=δῖε"]
---

**Alexander.** The shot that leaves Nestor stranded is by “brilliant Alexander, the husband of Helen of the lovely hair” (8.82; [[husband-of-helen]], [[lovely-haired]]), the archer who in Book 11 will wound Diomedes, Machaon and Eurypylus.

**Odysseus.** “So he spoke, but much-enduring brilliant Odysseus did not hear” (8.97): the formula of the Odyssey's hero first appears here, in the verse of his flight ([[much-enduring]]).

**Lampus.** Hector calls on his four horses, “Xanthus, and you, Podargus, and Aethon and brilliant Lampus” (8.185; [[the-horses-of-hector]]). The honorific of heroes is given to a horse, in the vocative δῖε, as it is to Arion, the horse of Adrastus, in Nestor's advice for the chariot race (23.346). Hector addresses the four with dual verbs, as if they were a pair, and the verse that names them is reported to have been rejected in antiquity.

**Alastor.** Teucer, struck by Hector's stone, is carried to the ships by “two trusty companions … Mecisteus, son of Echius, and brilliant Alastor” (8.332–334; [[trusty]], [[mecisteus-son-of-echius]]). The same verses carry the wounded Hypsenor in Book 13 (13.421–423). See [[phaidimos]].
