---
summary: Hector wheels his horses before the Achaean ditch "with the eyes of the Gorgon or of Ares, ruin of mortals" (8.349).
grc: [Γοργοῦς]
---

The Trojans have driven the Achaeans back across the ditch, and Hector, like a hound at the heels of a boar or a lion, keeps killing the hindmost (8.335–342; see [[the-hound-at-the-heels]]). Then "Hector wheeled his fine-maned horses this way and that, / with the eyes of the Gorgon or of Ares, ruin of mortals" (8.348–349; see [[hector]], [[ares]] and [[ruin-of-mortals]]). Γοργοῦς is the genitive of Γοργώ. The Gorgon is named only here and on Agamemnon's shield, where she is set as a crown with Terror and Rout around her, "glaring terribly" (11.36–37); elsewhere she is only a head, on the aegis (5.741) and in the world of the dead (*Odyssey* 11.634; see [[aegis]]). Her power is in her look, and Hector's gaze carries the same terror. ἠδέ, "and", joins the two likenesses; the translation's "or" presents them as alternatives (see the translation's note on 8.349 and [[aristotle-gorgon]]).
