---
summary: The two sons of Atreus come out second in the charge across the ditch, behind Diomedes, when the eagle of Zeus has rallied the Achaeans (8.261).
---

“After him came the sons of Atreus, Agamemnon and Menelaus” (8.261; see [[agamemnon]] and [[menelaus]]). The plural with both names after it is the phrase of Priam's message in Book 7, “to tell the sons of Atreus, Agamemnon and Menelaus” (7.373); here it stands in the nominative, at the head of a charge. In Book 7 Agamemnon stood up alone, first of the nine volunteers, while Menelaus, whom he had held back from Hector, stayed seated (7.104–122, 7.161–169). Now the brothers come out together.

The first six men of the charge are the six whom Andromache named in Book 6, when she told Hector that the best of the Achaeans had three times tried the wall by the fig tree, “with the two Ajaxes and far-famed Idomeneus and with the sons of Atreus and the valiant son of Tydeus” (6.435–437; see [[the-fig-tree]] and [[diomedes]]). In Book 8 they come out in a new order, Diomedes first, then the sons of Atreus, the two Ajaxes and Idomeneus (8.253–263).
