---
summary: Ares takes no part in Book 8, kept out like the other gods by Zeus' ban, but his name measures the fighting. The two Ajaxes are "attendants of Ares", Hector is "the equal of swift Ares" and has "the eyes of the Gorgon or of Ares, ruin of mortals", and Hector speaks of "tearful Ares" and of rousing "sharp Ares" at the ships.
---

When the Achaean leaders flee from Zeus' thunder, "nor did the two Ajaxes stay, attendants of Ares" (8.79; see [[attendants-of-ares]] and [[ajax]]). When the Achaeans are shut in between the ditch and the ships, the man who hems them in is Hector, "the equal of swift Ares" (θοῷ ἀτάλαντος Ἄρηϊ, 8.215; see [[equal-of-ares]]). The phrase belongs to the poem's great fighters: Meriones (13.295, 13.328, 13.528), Patroclus (16.784), Automedon (17.536), and Hector again (17.72). Driving the Achaeans to the ships, Hector wheels his horses "with the eyes of the Gorgon or of Ares, ruin of mortals" (8.349; see [[gorgon]] and [[ruin-of-mortals]]). The god's name is also war itself. In his night assembly Hector wants the Achaeans to carry home wounds that will make others shrink "from bringing tearful Ares against the horse-taming Trojans" (8.513–516; see [[tearful]]), and at dawn the Trojans will "rouse sharp Ares beside the hollow ships" (8.531; see [[sharp]]). Ares himself does not appear. When his son Ascalaphus is killed in Book 13, he wants to break the ban and avenge him, and Athena holds him back (15.110–142; see [[ascalaphus]]).
