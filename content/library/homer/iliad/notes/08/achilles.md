summary: Achilles takes no part in the fighting, but the book keeps him in view. His ships are drawn up at one end of the line (8.222–226); Athena complains that Zeus has fulfilled the plans of Thetis, who begged him to honor Achilles (8.370–372); and Zeus foretells that Hector will not stop until Achilles is roused beside the ships, over the dead Patroclus (8.473–477).

**At the end of the line.** Agamemnon stands by Odysseus' ship, in the middle of the camp, so that his shout can carry to both ends, “both to the huts of Ajax, son of Telamon, and to those of Achilles”, who had drawn up their ships at the farthest ends, “trusting in their manhood and the strength of their hands” (8.222–226; see [[agamemnon]] and [[ajax]]). The strongest fighters hold the flanks. But the shout is meant for the Danaans who are fighting (8.227), and Achilles is out of the war; 8.224–226 are reported to have been rejected here in antiquity (see the translation's note on 8.222–226). The same verses return in Book 11, when Strife shouts from Odysseus' ship to rouse the army (11.5–9).

**Thetis' plans.** Athena, arming to defy her father's ban, complains that Zeus “hates me, and has fulfilled the plans of Thetis, who kissed his knees and took his chin in her hand, begging him to honor Achilles, sacker of cities” (8.370–372; see [[athena]], [[thetis-supplicates-zeus]] and [[sacker-of-cities]]). The gods know why the Achaeans are losing: the prayer of Book 1 is being answered (1.503–510). Athena adds a kiss that Book 1 does not mention (see the translation's note on 8.371).

It is the first time the poem says what will bring Achilles back, “the swift-footed son of Peleus” (8.474; see [[swift-footed]] and [[peleus]]), and the first word of Patroclus' death (see [[the-death-of-patroclus]], [[patroclus]] and [[plan-of-zeus]]). The same night the Achaeans send an embassy with Agamemnon's gifts, and Achilles refuses them (9.182–655; see [[embassy]]); the listener already knows from Zeus that gifts will not move him and a death will. The prophecy is not fulfilled to the letter. Patroclus is killed on the plain, the fight over his body is fought there, and Achilles shows himself at the ditch (16.777–867, 18.203–231; see the translation's note on 8.475–476).
