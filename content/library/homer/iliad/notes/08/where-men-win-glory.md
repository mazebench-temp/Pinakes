---
summary: Zeus asks Hera and Athena, in mockery, whether they have tired themselves “in the battle where men win glory” destroying the Trojans.
---

“Surely you have not tired yourselves in the battle where men win glory destroying the Trojans, against whom you have conceived a dread grudge” (8.448–449; [[kotos]]). The epithet of battle stands in a question meant to sting: the goddesses never reached the battle at all, and the glory of the day has gone to Hector, to whom Zeus gave it (8.216; [[kydos]]). Zeus goes on to say that their limbs trembled before they saw the war and its grim deeds (8.452–453; [[grim-deeds]], [[phaidimos]]). See [[zeus]].
