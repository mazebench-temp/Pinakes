summary: Cattle are food and offering in Book 8. On Lemnos the Achaeans boasted over the meat of straight-horned cattle, Agamemnon burned the fat and thighs of oxen on every altar of Zeus he passed on his way to Troy, and at nightfall the Trojans bring cattle and fat sheep from the city for their camp on the plain.

**The feast on Lemnos.** Agamemnon shames the army with the boasts it made on Lemnos, “eating much meat of straight-horned cattle and drinking mixing bowls brim-full of wine”, that each man would stand against a hundred or two hundred Trojans (8.229–234; see [[straight-horned]], [[where-have-our-boasts-gone]] and [[lemnos]]). Beef is the meat of heroes' feasts, and the feast is remembered as the place of empty words.

**The offerings.** In the same speech Agamemnon reminds Zeus that he never passed an altar of his in his ship on the way to Troy without burning on it “the fat and the thighs of oxen”, βοῶν δημὸν καὶ μηρία (8.238–241; see [[sacrifice]] and [[altar]]). The thigh bones wrapped in fat are the gods' portion of a sacrifice (1.460–461). Zeus answers with the eagle (8.245–252; see [[eagle]]).

**The camp.** Hector tells the Trojans to bring cattle and fat sheep from the city, with wine and bread and firewood, and they do so in the same words (βόας καὶ ἴφια μῆλα, 8.505, 8.545; see [[fat-sheep]] and [[feast]]). The next verse, which is not in the manuscripts but is known from the Second Alcibiades, adds that they offered perfect hecatombs to the immortals; the winds carry the savor up to heaven, and in three more verses from the same source the gods take no share of it (8.548–552; see [[hecatomb]], [[the-refused-sacrifice]] and [[plan-of-zeus]]).
