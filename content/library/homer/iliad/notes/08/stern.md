{{quote:49a53fa3-4b0f-5958-b526-0294b982193c}}

The Achaean ships were beached with their sterns toward the plain, so a fight “at the sterns” (ἐπὶ πρύμνῃσι) is a fight for the ships themselves. Achilles asked for it in Book 1, that Zeus help the Trojans “and pen the Achaeans in among their ships' sterns and along the sea” (1.409). Zeus now names the day and its price: it will come “in the direst strait” (στείνει ἐν αἰνοτάτῳ), and over Patroclus dead, the first mention in the poem of his death (see [[patroclus]] and [[plan-of-zeus]]).

In the event Hector seizes the stern of Protesilaus' ship and calls for fire (15.704–706, 15.716–718), and the fire falls on it in Book 16, when Patroclus goes out (16.112–124). Patroclus is killed on the plain, and the fight over his body ends at the ditch, where Achilles shows himself, not at the sterns (16.786–857, 18.148–231; see the translation's note on 8.475–476).
