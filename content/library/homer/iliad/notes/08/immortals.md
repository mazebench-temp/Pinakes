---
summary: Olympus is “the seat of the immortals,” Hector wishes he were “immortal and ageless all my days,” and in verses not found in the manuscripts the Trojans offer hecatombs to the immortals.
---

**The seat of the immortals.** Struck by the thunderbolt, Zeus tells Hera and Athena, they would never have come back to Olympus, “where the seat of the immortals is” (8.455–456; [[olympus]], [[circling-years]]).

**Hector's wish.** At the end of his night speech Hector wishes to be what the gods are:

{{quote:55efc135-2a0d-5109-a2f8-0388855b9dea}}

“Ageless and immortal” is the pair that names the life of the gods: Zeus says it of the horses he gave Peleus, which are ageless and immortal while their master is mortal (17.443–444), Sarpedon says that if he and Glaucus were to be ageless and immortal forever, he would not fight in the front rank (12.322–325), and in the Odyssey Calypso offers it to Odysseus (Od. 5.135–136). Hector, at the height of the day Zeus has given him, wishes for it as surely as the day brings evil to the Argives ([[gods-and-mortals]]).

**The hecatombs.** “And they offered perfect hecatombs to the immortals” (8.548) is one of the four verses that are not in the manuscripts and are known from the Second Alcibiades ascribed to Plato (149d); editors print them in brackets ([[hecatomb]], [[blessed-gods]]). See [[mortal-men]].
