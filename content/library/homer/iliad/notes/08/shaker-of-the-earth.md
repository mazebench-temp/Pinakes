---
summary: Hera appeals to Poseidon as “shaker of the earth, wide in strength,” and at the end of the day “the famous shaker of the earth” unyokes Zeus' horses.
---

**Hera's appeal.** “Ah, shaker of the earth, wide in strength, not even your heart grieves in your breast for the Danaans as they perish” (8.201–202; [[wide-in-strength]], [[o-popoi]]). She reminds him of the gifts the Achaeans bring to Helice and Aegae, and proposes that they hold off Zeus. Poseidon refuses (8.208–211; [[earth-shaker]], [[reckless-in-speech]]).

**Poseidon's service.** At nightfall Zeus drives home from Ida: “For him the famous shaker of the earth unyoked the horses, and set the chariot on its stand and spread a cloth over it” (8.440–441; [[famous]], [[well-wheeled]]). The god who would not oppose Zeus by day stables his chariot at night. See [[poseidon]].
