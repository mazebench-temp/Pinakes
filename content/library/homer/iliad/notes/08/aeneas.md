---
summary: Aeneas is named once, as the man from whom Diomedes took the horses of Tros that now carry him and Nestor against Hector (8.105–108).
---

{{quote:4efcde6d-b304-553c-8e7a-60605c80fae5}}

Diomedes invites Nestor into his chariot with Aeneas' own words. In Book 5 Aeneas asked Pandarus to mount his chariot and see “what the horses of Tros are like” (5.221–223 = 8.105–107), Pandarus was killed, and Sthenelus drove the horses off while Aphrodite was carrying Aeneas out of the battle (5.311–327; see [[tros]] and [[sthenelus]]). μήστωρε φόβοιο, “masters of rout”, is a dual, said of the horses, as it was when they were first given to Aeneas (8.108; 5.272; see [[master-of-rout]]). Diomedes drives the same horses to victory in the chariot race, where the poem again names the man he took them from (23.290–292).
