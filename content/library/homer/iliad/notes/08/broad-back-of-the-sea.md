---
summary: Hector keeps fires burning on the plain all night, in case the Achaeans try to flee “over the broad back of the sea.”
---

“So that all night long until early-born Dawn we may burn many fires, and their glare may reach the sky, in case even by night the long-haired Achaeans set out to flee over the broad back of the sea” (8.508–511; [[early-born]], [[long-haired]]). The formula names the sea as the road of flight, as it did when Hera saw the army about to flee home in Book 2 (2.159). Hector does not want the Achaeans to escape; he wants them to board their ships wounded, so that others will fear the Trojans (8.512–516; [[sharp]], [[tearful]]). See [[sea]].
