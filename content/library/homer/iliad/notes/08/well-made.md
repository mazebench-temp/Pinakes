---
summary: Zeus takes up his golden whip, “golden and well made,” to drive to Ida.
---

“And he himself put on gold about his body, and took the whip, golden and well made, and mounted his chariot” (8.43–44). The verses describe Zeus' arming for the drive to Ida, and they return almost word for word for Poseidon in Book 13 (13.25–26; [[bronze-footed]], [[swift-flying]]). εὔτυκτος is usually the epithet of the helmet in the verses of arming ([[arming-scene]]); here it is the god's whip of gold. See [[zeus]] and [[chariot-journey]].
