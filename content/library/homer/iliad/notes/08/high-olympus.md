---
summary: Hera, indignant at Hector's boast, shakes on her throne and “made high Olympus quake”; Iris goes from Ida “to high Olympus” to turn back the goddesses.
---

“She shook on her throne, and made high Olympus quake” (8.199). In Book 1 Zeus' nod shook great Olympus (1.528–530); here Hera shakes it with her anger at Hector's boast, and then tries to stir Poseidon against Zeus (8.198–211; [[queenly]], [[reckless-in-speech]]). At the end of the afternoon Iris “went from the mountains of Ida to high Olympus” with Zeus' threat (8.410; [[storm-footed]], [[many-folded]]), and at nightfall Zeus himself sits down on his throne, and great Olympus shakes beneath his feet (8.443; [[far-seeing]]).
