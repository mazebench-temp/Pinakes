{{quote:6a4f6ab0-5181-529c-998e-e509be5ffdac}}

The mangers are ambrosial because the gods' horses eat the food of the gods ([[ambrosia]]). In Book 5 Iris set ambrosial fodder before the horses of Ares on Olympus, and the river Simoeis made ambrosia spring up for Hera's horses on the plain (5.368–369, 5.775–777); in Book 13 Poseidon gives his team ambrosial fodder in his cave under the sea (13.34–35). Only here is the epithet given to a thing of the stable. The scene closes the goddesses' failed sortie: the Hours, who opened the gates of heaven for them (8.393–396; [[hours]]), now unyoke their horses and lean the chariot against the gleaming walls ([[fine-maned]], [[gleaming-all-over]]).
