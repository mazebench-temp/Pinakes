summary: Hera answers Zeus' mockery with her verse of protest, “Most dread son of Cronus, what a thing you have said!”; earlier, Iris calls Athena “most dread” in the insult she adds to Zeus' message.
grc: [αἰνοτάτη]
en: [most dread]
---

**Hera's protest.** Zeus has mocked the goddesses for turning back, and “Hera's breast could not contain her anger” (8.461; [[cholos]]). She speaks the verse of protest with which she answered him in Books 1 and 4 (8.462; 1.552, 4.25; [[what-a-word]]), and the rest of her speech repeats almost word for word Athena's plea of the morning (8.463–468 ≈ 8.32–37): they know his strength, they grieve for the Danaans, and they will keep out of the war but give the Argives counsel. The day is framed by the same request, from Athena at dawn and from Hera at night. Zeus answers her with the prophecy of the battle at the ships (8.469–483; [[plan-of-zeus]]).

**Iris' insult.** The superlative was turned on Athena earlier in the afternoon. Iris ends Zeus' message with two verses of her own: “But you are most dread, you shameless dog, if you really dare to lift your huge spear against Zeus” (8.423–424; [[shameless-dog]], [[huge]]). See [[hera]] and [[athena]].
