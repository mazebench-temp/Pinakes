---
summary: Book 8 uses understatement in its divine scenes and its battles alike. A god who disobeys will come home struck “in no seemly way”, Zeus' horses fly “not unwilling”, Nestor stays “not of his own will”, and orders are followed by “and he did not disobey”.
refs: [8.12, 8.40, 8.45, 8.81, 8.112, 8.319, 8.330, 8.381, 8.400]
---

Litotes, saying a thing by denying its opposite, runs through the book:

- **As menace.** A god who goes to help either side “struck in no seemly way, he will come back to Olympus” (οὐ κατὰ κόσμον, 8.12), and Zeus tells Iris that if the goddesses come on, “it will not be a fair thing if we meet in war” (οὐ … καλὰ συνοισόμεθα, 8.400). Understatement makes the threat worse.
- **As reassurance.** Zeus answers Athena, “I do not speak in full earnest” (οὔ … θυμῷ πρόφρονι, 8.39–40; see [[smiling-at-him]]).
- **In formulas.** The gods' horses fly “not unwilling” (οὐκ ἀέκοντε, 8.45; see [[chariot-journey]]); an order is followed by “and he did not disobey” (οὐδʼ ἀπίθησε), of Nestor, Cebriones and Hera (8.112, 8.319, 8.381).
- **In the battle.** Nestor stays behind “not of his own will” (οὔ τι ἑκών, 8.81), and when Teucer falls, “Ajax did not neglect his brother” (οὐκ ἀμέλησε, 8.330; see [[the-shield-of-ajax]]).
