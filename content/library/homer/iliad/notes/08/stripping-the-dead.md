**Spoils named in advance.** Hector tells his horses what they are to win for him:

{{quote:8772f28f-852b-5c9d-8056-86167878fb15}}

The spoils he names are famous things, a shield of gold and a god's work (see [[shield]] and [[corselet]]), and he thinks their capture would be enough to send the Achaeans to their ships that night. Hera hears the boast and is indignant (8.198; see [[nemesis]]). Neither piece is taken.

**Bloody spoils.** At nightfall Hector's speech ends with the same hope, aimed at Diomedes: “I will know whether the son of Tydeus, mighty Diomedes, will push me back from the ships to the wall, or whether I will kill him with the bronze and carry off his bloody spoils” (8.532–534). ἔναρα βροτόεντα, “bloody spoils”, are what he once prayed his son would bring home from the war (6.480; see [[bloody-spoils]]). In the night that follows it is Diomedes who carries off bloody spoils, those of the Trojan scout Dolon (10.528–529).

**Spoils already taken.** Diomedes rides into the battle behind spoils of his own, the horses of Tros he took from Aeneas (8.105–108; 5.319–327; see [[horses]]).

**The dead left lying.** The day moves too fast for stripping. Hector leaves his dead charioteers where they fall, “grieving though he was for his comrade” (8.124–126, 8.316–317), and no victor in Book 8 stoops over the man he has killed (see [[androktasia]]).
