---
summary: Diomedes, imagining Hector's boast that he fled, wishes “then may the wide earth gape for me,” in the verse that Agamemnon spoke in Book 4.
---

{{quote:c38930ee-9402-5ac4-9c8f-11117508bd02}}

Nestor has told Diomedes to flee from Zeus' thunderbolt, and Diomedes agrees, but fears what Hector will say ([[good-at-the-war-cry]], [[son-of-tydeus]]). The wish to vanish into the earth rather than live to hear the enemy's boast is Agamemnon's in Book 4, when he imagines a Trojan boasting over the tomb of Menelaus (4.176–182; [[may-the-earth-gape]]). Nestor answers that the Trojans will never believe Hector (8.152–156). Hector does make the boast, in the taunt that follows (8.161–166; [[wretched-doll]]). See [[diomedes]].
