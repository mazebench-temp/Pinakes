---
summary: Agamemnon reminds Zeus that he never passed his altar “in my many-benched ship” without sacrifice on the voyage to Troy.
---

“Yet I say that I never passed by your beautiful altar in my many-benched ship on my ill-starred way here, but on every one I burned the fat and the thighs of oxen, eager to sack well-walled Troy” (8.238–241; [[perikalles]], [[well-walled]]). The singular νηῒ πολυκλήϊδι is the ship of a single voyager, as in Hector's challenge in Book 7, where a man of later times sails past a tomb in his many-benched ship (7.88). Here the voyager is the king himself, and the ship is the one that brought him to Troy with his offerings at every altar on the way. Zeus answers with the eagle (8.245–252; [[surest-of-birds]]).
