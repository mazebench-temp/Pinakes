summary: The two attendants who take charge of Nestor's horses are “mighty Sthenelus and manly Eurymedon”; Nestor tells Diomedes that no man, however mighty, can hold back the mind of Zeus.
grc: [ἴφθιμοι]
---

**The attendants.** “Then the two attendants took charge of Nestor's horses, mighty Sthenelus and manly Eurymedon” (8.113–114; [[manly]], [[sthenelus]], [[eurymedon-attendant-of-nestor]]). The plural ἴφθιμοι covers both names. A few verses earlier Diomedes has told Nestor that his attendant is “a weakling” (ἠπεδανός, 8.104); the poem does not say whether he means Eurymedon.

**The limit of might.** After the thunderbolt Nestor tells Diomedes to flee: “No man can hold back the mind of Zeus, however mighty he is, since he is far the stronger” (8.143–144; [[noos]], [[victory-shifts]]). The strength of a mighty man is the measure that Zeus exceeds, and Poseidon gives the gods' version of the same verdict later in the day (8.210–211; [[reckless-in-speech]]).
