---
summary: Zeus drives to Gargarus on Ida, “where he has a precinct and a fragrant altar.”
grc: [θυήεις]
---

Zeus' journey from Olympus ends on the peak of Ida, at his own precinct and altar (8.47–48; [[gargarus]], [[altar]], [[of-the-many-springs]], [[mother-of-wild-beasts]]). The line-end τέμενος βωμός τε θυήεις names a god's own place on earth: the river Spercheius has one at his springs, where Peleus vowed that Achilles would sacrifice (23.144–148), and Aphrodite has one at Paphos, to which she flies when she is freed from Hephaestus' net (Od. 8.362–363). On Ida it is the seat from which Zeus watches the war (8.51–52; [[watching-the-war]]), and Hector has burned many thighs of oxen for him on its peaks (22.169–172). At nightfall the winds carry the savor of the Trojans' feast up from the plain (8.549), and in verses not found in the manuscripts the gods take no share of it (8.548, 8.550–552; [[sacrifice]]).
