---
summary: Meriones is again "the equal of Enyalius, slayer of men" as he follows Idomeneus across the ditch behind Diomedes (8.264).
---

When the eagle of Zeus has heartened the Achaeans, Diomedes is the first to drive out across the ditch, and the leaders follow in order: the sons of Atreus, the two Ajaxes, then "Idomeneus and Idomeneus' squire / Meriones, the equal of Enyalius, slayer of men", then Eurypylus, and Teucer ninth (8.261–266; see [[meriones]] and [[idomeneus]]). The two verses are the ones that brought Meriones to his feet among the nine champions in Book 7 (8.263–264 = 7.165–166), and the second is his whole-verse title in the Catalogue and again in Book 17 (2.651, 17.259). It is always his.
