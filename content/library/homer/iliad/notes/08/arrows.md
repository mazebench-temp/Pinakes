**Paris' arrow.** The rout begins with an arrow. Paris strikes Nestor's trace horse on the crown, “and the arrow sank into its brain” (8.81–85; see [[horses]]).

**Teucer's count.** Teucer kills eight men, named in a list (8.273–277; see [[androktasia]]), and his own count agrees: “Eight long-barbed arrows I have let fly, and all of them were fixed in the flesh of young men swift in war; but this mad dog I cannot hit” (8.297–299; see [[long-barbed]]). Every arrow has found a man. The next two are shot “straight at Hector” and miss him: the first kills Gorgythion, son of Priam (8.300–308; see [[gorgythion]]), and the second misses “for Apollo made it swerve” (8.311), and kills Archeptolemus, Hector's charioteer (8.309–315; see [[divine-rescue]]). The eleventh, “a bitter arrow” taken from the quiver, is on the string when Hector's stone strikes (8.323–328; see [[quiver]], [[bitter]] and [[bow]]).

**Missiles.** βέλος is any missile, arrow or spear. The morning's fighting is told in a verse, “so long the missiles of both sides struck home, and the people fell” (8.67 = 11.85; at 16.778 it is noon), and the Trojans pour “groaning missiles” on Diomedes as he turns back (8.159; see [[groaning]]). Hector hopes that the Achaeans will not board their ships at their ease: “let many a one of them nurse a wound even at home, struck by an arrow or a sharp spear as he leaps aboard his ship” (8.513–515). βέλος … πέσσῃ, literally “digest a missile”, is to take the wound home and brood over it (see the translation's note on 8.513).
