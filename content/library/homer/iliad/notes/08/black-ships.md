Agamemnon “stood by Odysseus' black ship, huge in the hull” (8.222; [[huge-hulled]]), the ship in the middle of the line from which his shout could reach both ends of the camp. At night Hector's hope is to drive away “these dogs borne along by the fates, whom the fates bring here on the black ships” (8.527–528; [[borne-by-the-fates]]). The black ships are the Achaeans' home on the shore and the vessels of their doom in the enemy's mouth.

The same adjective describes the night twice in the book, νύκτα μέλαιναν and νυκτὶ μελαίνῃ (8.486, 8.502); those lines belong to [[black-night]]. See [[ships]].
