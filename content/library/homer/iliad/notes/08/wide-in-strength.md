---
summary: Hera addresses Poseidon in the verse with which Zeus answered him in Book 7, “Ah, shaker of the earth, wide in strength,” to urge him against Zeus.
---

The vocative verse ὢ πόποι, ἐννοσίγαιʼ εὐρυσθενές is the one Zeus used in Book 7 to answer Poseidon's complaint about the wall (7.455), and the Odyssey gives it to Zeus again (Od. 13.140). Only here does someone else speak it: Hera, to stir Poseidon against Zeus (8.201–207; [[shaker-of-the-earth]]). The epithet of his strength serves her argument, since he and the other gods who favor the Danaans would be strong enough together to hold Zeus off. Poseidon answers that Zeus “is far the stronger” (8.211; [[reckless-in-speech]]). See [[poseidon]] and [[hera]].
