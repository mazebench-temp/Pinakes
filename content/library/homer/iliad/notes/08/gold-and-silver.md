summary: Book 8 is a book of the gods' gold: Zeus puts on gold and drives golden-maned horses with a golden whip, weighs the fates in golden scales, dares the gods to pull on a golden rope, and sits on a golden throne while the goddesses sit on golden chairs. On the plain Hector covets Nestor's shield, “all of gold”.

**The gods' gold.** Gold marks everything Zeus uses in Book 8. His horses have “flowing manes of gold”, and “he himself put on gold about his body, and took the whip, golden and well made” (8.42–44; see [[arming-scene]]). At dawn he dares the gods to hang “a golden rope from the sky” and pull against him (8.19; see [[the-golden-rope]]); at noon he holds out “his golden scales” (8.69; see [[the-scales-of-zeus]]); at evening he sits “on his golden throne” (8.442; see [[throne]]), and the goddesses he has turned back sit “on golden chairs” (8.436). His messenger Iris is “golden-winged” (8.398; see [[golden-winged]]), and Hera's horses wear golden headbands (8.382; see [[with-golden-headbands]]). The gods sit on a golden floor and drink from golden cups (4.2–4), but in Book 8 their gold is the gold of power: the rope, the scales and the throne are the instruments of Zeus' rule.

**The gold of men.** On the plain gold is a prize. Hector covets Nestor's shield, whose fame reaches the sky, “that it is all of gold, its rods and the shield itself” (8.192–193; see [[shield]]), and his own spear has “a golden ring” around its bronze point (8.495; see [[spear]]).
