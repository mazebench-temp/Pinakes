---
summary: Poseidon answers Hera's call to defy Zeus with “Hera, reckless in speech,” the only use of ἀπτοεπής in Homer.
grc: [ἀπτοεπὲς]
---

{{quote:e30792ba-ffae-5030-a911-8de4e47442d2}}

Hera, angered by Hector's boast, has asked Poseidon whether his heart does not grieve for the dying Danaans, who bring him gifts at Helice and Aegae, and has proposed that the gods who help the Danaans drive back the Trojans and hold off Zeus, leaving him to sit “alone on Ida and fret” (8.198–207; [[shaker-of-the-earth]], [[wide-in-strength]], [[helice]], [[aegae]]). Poseidon calls the proposal reckless and refuses ([[deeply-troubled]], [[powerful]], [[earth-shaker]], [[what-a-word]]). His reason is the one that Nestor gave Diomedes a few lines before, in the same half-verse: Zeus “is far the stronger” (8.144, 8.211). Gods and men agree on the day's one fact. See [[poseidon]] and [[hera]].
