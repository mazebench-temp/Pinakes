---
summary: Hera is “the white-armed goddess” as she pities the Achaeans, as she obeys Athena's call to harness the horses, and as she falls silent before Zeus' prophecy.
---

The formula marks the turns of Hera's afternoon. “Seeing the men, the white-armed goddess Hera pitied them” (8.350): the Achaeans are penned at their ships, and she rouses Athena ([[child-of-aegis-bearing-zeus]], [[ruin-of-mortals]]). “So she spoke, and the white-armed goddess Hera did not disobey” (8.381): in Book 5 the same kind of verse had Athena obeying Hera (5.719), and here Hera obeys Athena and harnesses the horses ([[august-goddess]], [[with-golden-headbands]]). At nightfall, after Zeus has foretold the battle at the ships and told her he does not care for her anger, “white-armed Hera said nothing to him” (8.484; [[silence]]). See [[hera]].
