---
summary: Apollo saves Hector from Teucer's second arrow by making it swerve, and it kills Hector's charioteer Archeptolemus instead. At nightfall Hector wishes he were honored as Athena and Apollo are honored.
passages:
  - 8.309-315 | Apollo turns Teucer's arrow
---

**The swerving arrow.** Teucer, shooting from under Ajax's shield, aims twice at Hector. The first arrow kills Gorgythion, a son of Priam (8.300–308; see [[teucer]] and [[gorgythion]]). The second misses as well, "for Apollo made it swerve", and strikes Hector's charioteer Archeptolemus in the chest (8.309–315; see [[archeptolemus]]). παρασφάλλω, "make to slip aside", occurs only here in Homer. It is the one deed of a god on the battlefield in Book 8 apart from the deeds of Zeus. Zeus has forbidden the gods to help either side (8.10–11), but Apollo's help goes the way the day is going. He has protected Hector before, setting him on his feet after Ajax's stone (7.272), and he will do so until the end, when Zeus' scales sink and "Phoebus Apollo left him" (22.213; see [[hector]] and [[the-scales-of-zeus]]).

**Honored as a god.** Hector ends his night assembly with a wish: "If only I were / immortal and ageless all my days, / and honored as Athena and Apollo are honored, / as surely as this day brings evil to the Argives" (8.538–541; see [[immortal-and-ageless]] and [[athena]]). He names the two gods as the measure of honor, though they are on opposite sides in the war; they sat together on the oak of Zeus to watch his duel with Ajax (7.58–61). He repeats the wish in Book 13, adding that he would be the son of Zeus and Hera (13.825–828).
