summary: The word appears only in the formula of assent, κατὰ μοῖραν, “fair and right”: Diomedes grants that Nestor's counsel to flee is right, in the verse with which Agamemnon once answered Nestor, and then objects, as Agamemnon did.
passages:
  - 8.145-156 | Diomedes answers Nestor
---

Nestor tells Diomedes to turn his horses, for Zeus is giving the glory to Hector today (8.139–144; see [[the-mind-of-zeus]]). Diomedes answers: “Yes, old man, all that you have said is fair and right. But this dread grief comes upon my heart and spirit” (8.146–147). κατὰ μοῖραν, “according to the share”, is what is due and fitting, said of words that give each thing its proper measure. The whole verse, ναὶ δὴ ταῦτά γε πάντα γέρον κατὰ μοῖραν ἔειπες, is the one with which Agamemnon answered Nestor in the quarrel of Book 1, before he turned to “but this man wants to be above all others” (1.286–287). Twice the old man is told that he is right, and twice “but” follows. Agamemnon objected out of pride, Diomedes out of shame: he fears that Hector will boast that the son of Tydeus fled before him (8.148–150; see [[may-the-earth-gape]] and [[retreat]]). Nestor answers with a better reason than Diomedes', and Diomedes obeys (8.151–157). Later the same night, in Book 10, the verse is turned around: Nestor uses it to answer Diomedes, who has told the old man that he never rests from toil (10.164–169).

The fate of the armies is weighed in this book too, but in other words: the two κῆρες in Zeus' scales and “the fated day of the Achaeans” (8.69–74; see [[ker-doom]] and [[aisa]]).
