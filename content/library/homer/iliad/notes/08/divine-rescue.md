summary: Zeus' thunderbolt saves the Trojans from being penned in their city, and Apollo turns Teucer's arrow away from Hector, the one rescue by another god on the day Zeus has forbidden the gods the war. Nestor's rescue by Diomedes is told in the verse of a god's rescue, with a man in the god's place.
grc: ["παρέσφηλεν γὰρ Ἀπόλλων"]
en: [Apollo made it swerve]

**Zeus.** “And they would have been penned up in Ilios like lambs, had not the father of men and gods been quick to see it” (8.131–132). The one rescued is a whole people, and Zeus does it with a thunderbolt thrown before Diomedes' horses (8.133–136; see [[thunderbolt]] and [[would-have-had-not]]). The same verse, εἰ μὴ ἄρʼ ὀξὺ νόησε, “had not … been quick to see it”, was used a few lines before of Diomedes seeing Nestor in danger (8.91); there the rescuer is a man, and that line is not counted here (see [[nestor]]).

**Apollo.** Teucer shoots twice at Hector. The first arrow kills Gorgythion; at the second, “he missed again, for Apollo made it swerve”, and the arrow kills Hector's charioteer (8.311–313; see [[archeptolemus]]). παρασφάλλω, “make to swerve”, occurs only here in Homer. Zeus has forbidden the gods to help either side (8.10–12), but Apollo acts for the side Zeus is helping, and nothing is said of it (see [[apollo]]).

**By men.** The book's other rescues are made by men. Diomedes takes Nestor into his chariot (8.99–117; see [[chariot]]), and Ajax hides Teucer under his shield “like a child under its mother”, stands over him when he falls, and two companions carry him to the ships (8.266–272, 8.330–334; see [[the-shield-of-ajax]]).

**Refused.** Hera and Athena set out to save the Achaeans, and Zeus turns them back before they reach the field (8.350–437; see [[chariot-journey]]).
