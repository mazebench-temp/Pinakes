summary: Nestor tells Diomedes that no man can hold back the mind of Zeus. The verb of the same family, νοέω, “see, perceive”, marks two rescues forty lines apart in one formula: Diomedes was “quick to see” Nestor's danger, and then Zeus was “quick to see” the Trojans' and threw his thunderbolt.
passages:
  - 8.87-98 | Diomedes sees Nestor's danger
  - 8.130-144 | The thunderbolt
---

**The mind of Zeus.** Nestor's counsel to turn and flee ends with its reason: “No man can hold back the mind of Zeus, however mighty he is, since he is far the stronger” (8.143–144; see [[the-mind-of-zeus]]). νόος is the mind with its intent, what Zeus means to do. In this book the reader has been shown it: Zeus has weighed the armies' fates at noon and the Achaeans' have sunk (8.69–74; see [[ker-doom]]).

**Quick to see.** The verb νοέω is to see and understand at once. The book uses the formula of a sudden rescue, εἰ μὴ ἄρʼ ὀξὺ νόησε, “had not … been quick to see it”, twice in forty lines. Nestor would have lost his life when Hector's horses came at him, “had not Diomedes, good at the war cry, been quick to see it” (8.90–91), and Diomedes takes him into his chariot. Then the Trojans “would have been penned up in Ilios like lambs, had not the father of men and gods been quick to see it” (8.131–132), and Zeus throws his thunderbolt before Diomedes' horses. The same verse that makes the man a rescuer makes the god one, and the god rescues the other side (see [[would-have-had-not]] and [[the-gods-mirror-the-mortals]]). Elsewhere the formula belongs to gods who save their favorites, Aphrodite twice and Poseidon (3.374, 5.312, 20.291), and once to Hector (5.680). At dawn Zeus had begun his threat to the gods with the same verb: “Whomever I see going off apart from the gods” (ὃν … νοήσω, 8.10).
