---
summary: Zeus sends Iris from Ida to turn back Hera and Athena. She meets them at the outermost gates of Olympus, repeats his threat word for word, and adds an insult of her own for Athena.
grc: [Ἶριν, Ἶρι]
refs: [8.400-408, 8.410-424]
passages:
  - 8.397-425 | Iris turns them back
---

**Sent.** Zeus sees the two goddesses drive out and "urged on golden-winged Iris to carry a message" (8.398; see [[golden-winged]]). The verse returns when he sends her to Hector (11.185), and his command begins as it does each time he sends her, "Go, swift Iris" (8.399; 11.186, 15.158, 24.144). She goes as "storm-footed Iris", ἀελλόπος (8.409; again 24.77, 24.159; see [[storm-footed]]), "from the mountains of Ida to high Olympus", and meets the goddesses "at the outermost gates of many-folded Olympus" (8.410–411; see [[many-folded]] and [[olympus]]). The goddesses have got no farther than the gates of heaven, which the Hours had just opened for them (8.392–396; see [[hours]]).

**The message.** She asks, "Where are you rushing? What madness is in your hearts?" (8.413), and then gives them Zeus' threat in his own words, with "I" turned into "he" and "they" into "you" (8.416–422 ≈ 8.402–408; see [[message-repeated]] and [[messenger-scene]]). Then she adds something he did not say:

{{quote:039ef026-5606-52ca-a9eb-f6439ec3a5ee}}

κύον ἀδεές, "shameless dog", is abuse among goddesses; Hera calls Artemis the same in the battle of the gods (21.481; see [[dogs]]). The verses 8.420–424 are reported to have been rejected in antiquity (see the translation's note on 8.420–424). Iris does not always carry a message bare. When she brings Poseidon Zeus' order to leave the battle, she adds advice of her own: the hearts of the noble can be turned, and the Erinyes always side with the elder brother (15.200–204; see [[poseidon]]). Here her words reach the goddess the threat was aimed at (see [[athena]]). "So saying, swift-footed Iris went away" (8.425; see [[swift-footed]]).
