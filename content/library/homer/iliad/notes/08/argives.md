---
summary: The Argives are named in Book 8 in promises and threats. Athena and Hera promise to put counsel in their minds (8.36, 8.467), Agamemnon shames them, “Shame, Argives” (8.228), Hector means to kill them beside their burning ships (8.183), and at night he says that the darkness has saved them (8.500–501).
---

**Counsel for the Argives.** Athena promises Zeus that the goddesses will keep out of the war, “but we will put counsel in the Argives' minds that will help them” (8.36), and Hera repeats the promise to him at nightfall (8.467; see [[boule]]). When the two goddesses set out to help in person, Iris brings them Zeus' answer: “The son of Cronus will not let you help the Argives” (8.414; see [[iris]]). The one help a goddess gives the Argives that day is Hera's, when she puts it in Agamemnon's mind to rally the army (8.218–219; see [[agamemnon]]).

**Shame and threat.** Agamemnon's rebuke begins “Shame, Argives, wretched disgraces, admirable in looks!”, the line Hera shouted in the likeness of Stentor (8.228 = 5.787; see [[aidos]]). Hector means to kill “the Argives, beside the ships, dazed by the smoke” (8.183), Athena wishes him dead “at the hands of the Argives in his native land” (8.359), and Zeus tells Hera that at dawn she will see him “destroying the great army of the Argive spearmen” (8.472). At night Hector tells the Trojans that the darkness “has now saved the Argives and their ships on the shore of the sea” (8.500–501), and his speech ends with his wish to be immortal and ageless, measured against what he is sure of: “as surely as this day brings evil to the Argives” (8.538–541; see [[hector]] and [[immortal-and-ageless]]).
