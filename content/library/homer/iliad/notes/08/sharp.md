---
summary: In his night speech Hector speaks of the “sharp spear” that will wound the Achaeans as they flee, and of rousing “sharp Ares” at the ships in the morning.
---

Both uses are Hector's. He wants the Achaeans to carry wounds home, “struck by an arrow or a sharp spear as he leaps aboard his ship” (8.514–515), and he promises that “early, at daybreak, armed in our armor, we will rouse sharp Ares beside the hollow ships” (8.530–531; [[hollow-ships]]). The second is a formula of the call to battle, ἐγείρειν ὀξὺν Ἄρηα (2.440, 4.352). The phrase ὀξὺ νόησε, “was quick to see,” used of Diomedes and of Zeus earlier in the book (8.91, 8.132), is a different sense of the word, and the entry excludes it ([[divine-rescue]]). See [[ares]].
