---
summary: Book 8 calls Zeus father in Athena's address, “Our father,” in Agamemnon's tearful prayer, in the verses that send him from Ida and bring him home, and when Athena sulks at him.
grc: ["πάτερ ἡμέτερε", "Ζεὺς δὲ πατὴρ"]
---

**Athena.** The day begins with her address, “Our father, son of Cronus, highest of lords” (8.31), a verse that is hers alone in Homer ([[most-high]]). In the afternoon she complains that “my father rages with a mind that is not good” (8.360; [[hard]]), and at the end of the day she sits silent, “sulking at father Zeus,” gripped by savage anger (8.459–460; [[cholos]]).

**Agamemnon's prayer.** “Father Zeus, have you ever before struck any of the mighty kings with such blind folly, and robbed him of great glory?” (8.236–237; [[ate]], [[supreme-in-might]]). The king prays in tears, and “the father pitied him as he shed tears” (8.245) and sent the eagle ([[surest-of-birds]], [[shedding-tears]]).

**The father's journeys.** Two verses with the phrase Ζεὺς δὲ πατήρ turn the action on Olympus: “But when father Zeus saw them from Ida, he was terribly angry” sends Iris after Hera and Athena (8.397; [[golden-winged]]), and “But father Zeus drove his well-wheeled chariot and horses from Ida” brings him home after them (8.438; [[well-wheeled]]).
