---
summary: The sunset of the second day of battle draws black night “over the grain-giving earth.”
grc: [ζείδωρον]
---

“And the bright light of the sun fell into Oceanus, drawing black night over the grain-giving earth” (8.485–486; [[black-night]], [[oceanus]], [[sunset-lines]]). The plowed land that gives grain is the epithet's usual subject; it bore Erechtheus in the Catalogue (2.547–548), and the mares of Erichthonius run over it in Book 20 (20.226). Here it is the ground of the battlefield, covered by the night that ends the fighting. See [[earth]].
