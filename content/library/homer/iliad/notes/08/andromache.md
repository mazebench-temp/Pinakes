“Andromache, the daughter of great-hearted Eetion” (8.187) is the verse that introduced her in Book 6, when she came running to meet Hector at the gates (6.395; see [[eetion]]). Here she is named for her care of his horses. She set wheat before them and mixed them wine to drink, ἢ ἐμοί, “before me”, before she served her husband, and wine for horses occurs nowhere else in Homer (8.188–190; see [[the-horses-of-hector]] and [[wine]]). Hector calls himself her θαλερὸς πόσις, “her husband in my prime” (8.190; see [[posis]]): in Book 6 she called him her θαλερὸς παρακοίτης, “my husband in his prime”, all she had left (6.429–430; see [[husbands-and-wives]]).

The care of the house for the man at war returns when she learns of his death. She is weaving in the inner room, and has a great tripod set on the fire so that Hector may have a warm bath when he comes home from the battle, not knowing that he is already dead (22.440–446).
