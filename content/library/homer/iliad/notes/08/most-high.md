---
summary: Zeus calls himself “the highest counselor” in his challenge to the gods, and Athena answers him as “highest of lords.”
grc: [ὕπατε κρειόντων]
en: [highest of lords, highest]
---

**The challenge.** Let all the gods and goddesses hang on a golden rope, Zeus says, “still you could not drag down from the sky to the plain Zeus, the highest counselor” (8.21–22; [[the-golden-rope]]). The phrase Ζῆνʼ ὕπατον μήστωρα returns in Book 17, where Aeneas tells the Trojans that Zeus, the highest counselor, is their helper in battle (17.339). μήστωρ, “deviser, counselor,” is the word in [[master-of-rout]] and [[counselor-equal-to-the-gods]].

**Athena's address.** Athena answers him: “Our father, son of Cronus, highest of lords” (8.31; [[father-zeus]], [[son-of-cronus]]). The verse is Athena's alone in Homer. She speaks it three times in the Odyssey, each time on behalf of Odysseus (Od. 1.45, 1.81, 24.473). Here she asks only that the goddesses may give the Argives counsel, and Zeus answers her kindly (8.32–40; [[tritogeneia]]). See [[zeus]].
