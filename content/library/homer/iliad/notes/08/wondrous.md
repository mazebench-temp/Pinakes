---
summary: The Trojans pursue Nestor and Diomedes “with a wondrous din,” pouring their groaning missiles on them.
---

“And the Trojans and Hector with a wondrous din poured their groaning missiles on them” (8.158–159; [[groaning]]). ἠχῇ θεσπεσίῃ, “with a sound beyond the human,” is the noise of an army in full cry, and the verse returns when the Trojans pursue Nestor's son Antilochus in Book 15 (15.589–590). It follows Diomedes as he turns from the thunderbolt, and Hector's taunt rises above it (8.160–166; [[wretched-doll]]). See [[nestor]] and [[diomedes]].
