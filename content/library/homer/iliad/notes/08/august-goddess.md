---
summary: The whole-verse formula names Hera as she harnesses the horses with golden headbands to drive out against Zeus' command.
---

“She went about harnessing the horses with golden headbands, Hera, the august goddess, daughter of great Cronus” (8.382–383; [[with-golden-headbands]]). The two verses repeat her preparations in Book 5, when she and Athena drove out to stop Ares (5.720–721); there Zeus gave them leave, and here they go without it. The title gives her rank: she is Cronus' daughter, as Zeus is his son, and in Book 4 she claims the same birth as his, “crooked-counseling Cronus fathered me, the most senior” (4.58–59; [[son-of-cronus]]). By nightfall Zeus has named Cronus himself as a prisoner in Tartarus (8.479; [[cronus]]). See [[hera]].
