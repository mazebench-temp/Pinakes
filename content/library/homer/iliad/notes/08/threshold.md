---
summary: Tartarus, as Zeus describes it, has gates of iron and a threshold of bronze.
grc: ["χάλκεος οὐδός"]
---

Zeus threatens to hurl any god who disobeys him into Tartarus, “where the gates are of iron and the threshold of bronze” (8.15; see [[tartarus]] and [[iron]]). Hesiod's Tartarus has the same bronze threshold, with gates of marble, in a verse built like Homer's: ἔνθα δὲ μαρμάρεαί τε πύλαι καὶ χάλκεος οὐδός (*Theogony* 811). A threshold of bronze belongs to the houses of gods and of the godlike. Odysseus stands and ponders before he reaches the bronze threshold of Alcinous' palace (Od. 7.83), and Zeus' house on Olympus is “bronze-floored” (1.426; see [[bronze-floored]]). The riches of Apollo at Pytho lie behind a threshold of stone (9.404).
