---
summary: Hector is “the equal of swift Ares” as he hems the Achaeans in behind their ditch, and Meriones is “the equal of Enyalius” as he follows Diomedes back across it.
---

The Achaeans have been driven inside their ditch, and the space between the wall and the ditch is packed with horses and men, “hemmed in; and the one who hemmed them in, the equal of swift Ares, was Hector, son of Priam, now that Zeus had given him glory” (8.215–216; [[swift]], [[son-of-priam]], [[kydos]]). When the eagle has heartened the Achaeans, Meriones follows Diomedes across the ditch as “the equal of Enyalius, slayer of men” (8.264; [[enyalius]]). The champions of both armies are matched with the war god on the same day. Hector is likened to Ares once more as he drives at the ships, with “the eyes of the Gorgon or of Ares” (8.349; [[ruin-of-mortals]]). See [[ares]].
