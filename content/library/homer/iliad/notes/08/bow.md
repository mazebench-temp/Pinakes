**Teucer's bow.** Teucer comes ninth across the ditch, “stretching his back-bent bow” (παλίντονα τόξα, 8.266; see [[back-bent]]), and shoots from under Ajax's shield (8.266–272; see [[the-shield-of-ajax]] and [[teucer]]). Agamemnon sees him “destroying the battalions of the Trojans with his strong bow” (8.279), and Teucer says that since the Trojans were driven back he has been “lying in wait with my bow and killing men” (τόξοισι δεδεγμένος, 8.296). The archer needs cover, as Pandarus shot at Menelaus from behind the shields his companions held before him (4.113–115). Archery could be despised: Diomedes will call Paris a “bowman” and a disgrace, whose bow and arrows would not help him in a fight face to face (11.385–387). The first shot of the rout is Paris' own, the arrow that kills Nestor's horse (8.81–86; see [[arrows]]).

**The broken string.** Hector, on foot with a stone, catches Teucer as he draws the string to his shoulder, “and broke his bowstring; and his hand went numb at the wrist, and he sank to his knees and stayed there, and the bow fell from his hand” (8.328–329; see [[stone-throwing]]). In Book 15 Teucer's bowstring breaks again as he aims at Hector, snapped by Zeus, and the same half-verse follows: “and the bow fell from his hand” (15.461–465). Twice the archer aims at Hector, and twice the bow is struck from his hands. The second time he lays it aside in his hut and takes up a shield, a helmet and a spear (15.478–482).
