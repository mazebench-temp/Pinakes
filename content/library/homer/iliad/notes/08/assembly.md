summary: The book opens and closes with an assembly held by one speaker. At dawn Zeus calls the gods to the highest peak of Olympus and forbids them the war; at nightfall Hector gathers the Trojans on the plain and orders the camp and the watch, and they shout their applause.

---

**Two assemblies.** Book 8 begins and ends with an assembly, in almost the same words: Ζεὺς δὲ θεῶν ἀγορὴν ποιήσατο, “Zeus … held an assembly of the gods” (8.2), and Τρώων αὖτʼ ἀγορὴν ποιήσατο φαίδιμος Ἕκτωρ, “Then shining Hector held an assembly of the Trojans” (8.489). Each is a single speech of command, and neither ends in debate (see [[assembly-type-scene]]).

**On Olympus.** The gods meet at dawn “on the topmost peak of many-ridged Olympus” (8.3), where Thetis found Zeus sitting apart from the others in Book 1 (1.498–499; see [[many-ridged-olympus]]). “He himself spoke to them, and all the gods listened” (8.4). He forbids any of them to help either side, on pain of a beating or of Tartarus, and dares them all to a tug-of-war on a golden rope (8.5–27; see [[tartarus]] and [[the-golden-rope]]). They fall silent, “amazed at his word” (8.28–29; see [[silence]]), and only Athena speaks, to plead rather than to argue (8.30–37). The assembly is not dismissed: Zeus yokes his horses and drives to Ida (8.41).

**On the plain.** At sunset Hector leads the Trojans away from the ships, “beside the eddying river, in a clear space, where the ground showed free of corpses” (8.490–491). They step down from their chariots, and he speaks leaning on his spear, as Agamemnon leaned on his scepter (8.492–496; see [[spear]]). His speech orders the night, supper, fodder, fires, a watch on the city, battle at dawn (8.497–541; see [[night-watch]]), “and the Trojans shouted in applause” (κελάδησαν, 8.542). No one else speaks. The Trojans hold a second assembly on the plain in Book 18, after Achilles has shown himself at the ditch. They stand, and no one dares sit; Polydamas advises them to go back to the city, Hector overrules him, and the assembly closes with the same verse, with a comment from the narrator: “fools, for Pallas Athena took away their wits” (18.243–313; 18.310–311). Book 8's assembly is held at the height of Hector's success; Book 18's repeats its close at the turn, when Achilles has come back.

**Speaking.** ἀγορεύω, the verb of the assembly, is used of speech in general: Zeus “had spoken very forcefully” (8.29), Hera and Poseidon “spoke such words to one another” (8.212), and Diomedes fears what Hector will say one day, “speaking among the Trojans” (8.148; see [[tis-speech]]).
