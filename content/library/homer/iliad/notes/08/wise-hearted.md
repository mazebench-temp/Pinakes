---
summary: Nestor answers Diomedes' fear of disgrace with “Ah, son of wise-hearted Tydeus, what a thing you have said!”
---

“Ah, son of wise-hearted Tydeus, what a thing you have said! Even if Hector calls you a coward and a weakling, the Trojans and the Dardanians will not believe him” (8.152–154; [[what-a-word]]). Diomedes has said that he would rather the earth swallowed him than hear Hector boast that he fled (8.146–150; [[wide-earth]]). Nestor answers by naming his father, as the army knows him: the son of Tydeus, who has flung so many Trojans in the dust (8.155–156), cannot be made a coward by a boast ([[son-of-tydeus]], [[tydeus]]). The meaning of δαΐφρων was debated in antiquity, “wise-hearted” or “warlike in mind”; the translation follows the first. See [[diomedes]] and [[nestor]].
