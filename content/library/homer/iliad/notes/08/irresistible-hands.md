---
summary: Zeus boasts to Hera and Athena of his might and his “irresistible hands,” which all the gods on Olympus could not turn back.
grc: [χεῖρες ἄαπτοι]
---

“In any case, such is my might and such are my irresistible hands, that all the gods there are on Olympus could not turn me back” (8.450–451; [[menos]]). The boast repeats the challenge of the morning, that all the gods together could not pull him down with the golden rope (8.18–27; [[the-golden-rope]]), and the threat of Book 1, where he warned Hera that all the gods on Olympus would be no help to her if he laid his irresistible hands on her (1.565–567). The pair μένος καὶ χεῖρας ἀάπτους, “might and irresistible hands,” is also the formula of a great warrior's strength: the Trojans were glad to see Hector escape the might and the irresistible hands of Ajax (7.309), and the phrase returns of Hector himself (17.638).
