Hector's orders for the night include one for the heralds:

{{quote:edfcf01c-a053-5056-8301-1d77396c2099}}

The heralds carry the commander's word to those who cannot hear him, the people inside the walls, as at the oath of Book 3 the heralds went “through the city” with the offerings (κήρυκες δʼ ἀνὰ ἄστυ, 3.245). Heralds are elsewhere “messengers of Zeus and of men” (1.334, 7.274); Hector calls them Διῒ φίλοι, “dear to Zeus” (8.517), the title the narrator has just given Hector himself (8.493). Their message sets the city's watch, with the boys and the old men on the towers (see [[night-watch]], [[the-walls-of-troy]], [[in-first-youth]], [[gray-templed]] and [[god-built]]).
