---
summary: Agamemnon reminds the penned Achaeans of the boasts they made on Lemnos over meat and brimming bowls of wine, that each of them would stand against a hundred, two hundred Trojans; now they are not worth one man, Hector (8.228–235).
passages:
  - 8.228-235 | Agamemnon shames the Achaeans
---

“Where have our boasts gone, when we said that we were the best, the empty boasts you uttered on Lemnos, eating much meat of straight-horned cattle and drinking mixing bowls brim-full of wine” (8.229–232; see [[where-have-our-boasts-gone]], [[empty-boasting]], [[straight-horned]] and [[brim-full]]). The boasts were made at a feast, and the boast of facing a hundred or two hundred Trojans each (8.233–234) suits men who had not yet met them, on the way to Troy. The Iliad tells of no stay on Lemnos beyond this verse, though the Catalogue says that the Achaeans left Philoctetes there, wounded by a water snake (2.721–723; see [[philoctetes-on-lemnos]] and the translation's note on 8.230).

The island's wine is close at hand. On the night before this battle the army drank the wine that Euneus had sent from Lemnos, while Zeus “devised evil for them, thundering terribly” and no one dared drink before he had poured a libation (7.467–482; see [[euneus]] and [[wine]]). The next day the evil comes, and Agamemnon recalls what the army said over its wine on Lemnos.
