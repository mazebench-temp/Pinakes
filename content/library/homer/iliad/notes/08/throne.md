**Hera's throne.** When Hector boasts that he will take Nestor's golden shield and Diomedes' corselet, Hera is indignant: “she shook on her throne, and made high Olympus quake” (8.199). The verb is the one used of Zeus' nod to Thetis, when “he made great Olympus shake” (ἐλέλιξεν, 1.530). Zeus shakes Olympus with a nod; Hera, from her seat, with her anger.

**Zeus' throne.** Back from Ida, Zeus “came to the seats of the gods” (θεῶν δʼ ἐξίκετο θώκους, 8.439), “and far-seeing Zeus himself sat down on his golden throne, and great Olympus shook beneath his feet” (8.442–443; see [[gold-and-silver]]). The god's weight is his power. At dawn he boasted that he could haul up earth and sea on a rope against all the gods together (8.19–27; see [[the-golden-rope]]); at evening the mountain shakes when he sits down.

**The goddesses' chairs.** Hera and Athena, turned back by Iris, sit down “on golden chairs among the other gods, grieving at heart” (ἐπὶ κλισμοῖσι, 8.436–437). κλισμός, from κλίνω, “lean”, is a chair with a back. When Zeus comes, “Athena and Hera alone sat apart from Zeus” (8.444–445; see [[silence]]). On Olympus where one sits is a statement. In Book 1 all the gods rose from their seats to meet Zeus when he came home, and “no one dared to wait for him to approach” (1.533–536; see [[rising-for-zeus]]); here two of them keep their distance.
