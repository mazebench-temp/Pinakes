summary: Tydeus is named in his son's titles throughout Diomedes' part in the book, most often in the patronymic Τυδεΐδης, “son of Tydeus”. Nestor consoles Diomedes as “son of wise-hearted Tydeus” (8.152), the words with which Agamemnon began his rebuke in Book 4.
relations:
  - child: diomedes (8.99, 8.118, 8.152)

Of the fifteen lines in Book 8 that name Diomedes, nine name him by his father (8.99, 8.118, 8.139, 8.149, 8.152, 8.161, 8.167, 8.254, 8.532; see [[son-of-tydeus]] and [[diomedes]]). The patronymic is the name Diomedes fears to hear in Hector's boast, “The son of Tydeus fled before me to the ships” (8.149), and the name Hector uses for his taunt (8.161; see [[hector]]). Nestor's “Ah, son of wise-hearted Tydeus” (8.152) is the opening of Agamemnon's rebuke in Book 4, where it began the tale of Tydeus at Thebes told to shame his son (4.370; see [[tydeus-at-thebes]] and [[wise-hearted]]). Here the same words open a consolation.
