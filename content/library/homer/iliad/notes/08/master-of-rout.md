---
summary: Diomedes names the horses he took from Aeneas, “those masters of rout,” as he takes Nestor into his chariot.
---

“But come, mount my chariot, so that you may see what the horses of Tros are like, how they know the plain, how to pursue and to flee very swiftly this way and that; I took them once from Aeneas, those masters of rout” (8.105–108). The horses that Sthenelus drove off in Book 5 (5.319–327) carry Diomedes and Nestor against Hector, and the verse of Aeneas' boast about them returns in Diomedes' mouth (8.107 = 5.223; [[tros]], [[ganymede-and-the-horses-of-tros]], [[aeneas]]). The epithet is soon tested: Zeus' thunderbolt falls in front of them, and Nestor turns them to flight (8.133–157; [[single-hoofed]]). See [[horses]] and [[diomedes]].
