**Agamemnon.** The prayer has every part of the pattern, in the order of Chryses' prayers in Book 1 (see [[prayer-type-scene]]).

1. **Setting.** Agamemnon prays from Odysseus' ship, in the middle of the line, straight after shaming the army (8.222–235). No gesture is told before he speaks; his tears are told after (8.245; see [[shedding-tears]]).
2. **Invocation.** Ζεῦ πάτερ, “Father Zeus” (8.236), with no titles and no places, and at once a reproach: has Zeus ever struck a king with such blind folly (8.236–237)?
3. **Claim.** He never passed an altar of Zeus on the voyage without burning the fat and thighs of oxen on it (8.238–241; see [[sacrifice]]).
4. **Request.** “But, Zeus, fulfill for me at least this wish: let us at least escape and get away” (8.242–243; see [[fulfill-this-wish]]).
5. **Answer.** “So he spoke, and the father pitied him as he shed tears, and nodded to him that his people would be saved and not perish” (8.245–246). The nod is given a sign the army can see, the eagle with the fawn (8.247–252; see [[teras]] and [[eagle]]).

The verse of the answer returns when Ajax, in the mist over Patroclus' body, prays for light, and Zeus scatters the mist at once (17.645–650). Priam's prayer for a sign in Book 24 is answered by an eagle sent in the same verse (24.315 = 8.247). The grant is partial, as Zeus' answers can be (16.249–250): the Achaeans are saved, but the day stays with the Trojans (see [[plan-of-zeus]]).

**At the ships.** The army's prayer is told in two verses, with the gesture and the voice and no words: “to all the gods they lifted up their hands, and each man prayed aloud” (8.346–347). In Book 15 the same verses are followed by Nestor's prayer in words and by Zeus' thunder in answer (15.367–378). Here no answer is given, and the next voice is Hera's, who pities them (8.350–353).
