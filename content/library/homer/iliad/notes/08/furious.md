---
summary: Hector tells the Trojans to “remember your furious courage,” and the two Ajaxes cross the ditch “clothed in furious courage.”
---

θοῦρις ἀλκή, “furious courage,” is named once on each side. Hector ends his call to the Trojans with “be men, my friends, and remember your furious courage” (8.174; [[hand-to-hand-fighters]], [[exhortation]], [[alke]]). When the eagle has heartened the Achaeans and the leaders cross the ditch after Diomedes, “the two Ajaxes, clothed in furious courage” follow the sons of Atreus (8.262; [[clothed-in-courage]]), in the verse that brought them forward among the volunteers in Book 7 (7.164).
