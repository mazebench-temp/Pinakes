**Zeus in gold.** Before he drives to Ida, Zeus yokes his horses, “and he himself put on gold about his body, and took the whip, golden and well made” (8.43–44). The god's arming takes half a verse, and no piece is named: he puts on gold. The same verses arm Poseidon for his drive across the sea in Book 13 (13.25–26; see [[chariot-journey]] and [[gold-and-silver]]). Zeus does not fight; he sits on Gargarus and watches (8.51–52).

**The armies.** The Achaeans eat their meal, “and straight after it they armed themselves” (θωρήσσοντο, 8.54), and the Trojans arm in the city, fewer as they are, “under hard necessity, for their children and for their wives” (ὁπλίζοντο, 8.55–57). Each army's arming is told in a word (see [[corselet]]).

**Athena.** Athena goes into her father's house to arm for war, while Hera harnesses the horses:

{{quote:470d57b9-22f0-5f9c-90da-3daf385ce8e2}}

The verses are those of her arming in Book 5 (5.733–737, 5.745–747), but seven are missing between them: the tasseled aegis with Rout, Strife, Courage and Onslaught on it and the Gorgon's head, and the golden helmet fitted with the foot soldiers of a hundred cities (5.738–744; see [[aegis]] and [[helmet]]). In Book 5 Zeus gave the two goddesses leave to go against Ares (5.753–766); here she arms against his command, and Iris turns her back at the gates of Olympus before she reaches the battle (8.409–425). She lets her robe fall “on her father's floor”, the robe she had made herself (8.385–386; see [[robe]] and [[weaving]]), and puts on the tunic of Zeus (8.387; see [[tunic]]).

**Tomorrow.** Hector tells the Trojans that “early, at daybreak, armed in our armor”, they will rouse Ares beside the ships (8.530). That morning comes only in Book 11, and it begins with Agamemnon's arming, told in full (11.15–46).
