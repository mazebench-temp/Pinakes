---
summary: Hector “of the gleaming helmet” shouts his taunt after Diomedes, strikes Teucer down with a stone, and is the enemy Athena hopes to face on the bridges of war.
---

The formula follows Hector through the afternoon. “And over him great Hector of the gleaming helmet shouted aloud” (8.160) introduces his taunt as Diomedes turns to flee ([[wretched-doll]]). When Teucer sets an arrow to his string, “Hector of the gleaming helmet” strikes him with a jagged stone by the collarbone and breaks the bowstring (8.324–329; [[jagged]], [[bitter]]). And Athena, arming against Zeus' will, wants to see “whether the son of Priam, Hector of the gleaming helmet, will be glad when we two appear along the bridges of war” (8.377–378; [[son-of-priam]]); she never reaches him.
