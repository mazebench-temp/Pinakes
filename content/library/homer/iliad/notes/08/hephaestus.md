---
summary: Hector hopes to strip from Diomedes "the intricately worked corselet that Hephaestus toiled to make" (8.195).
---

Urging on his horses, Hector names two prizes he wants: Nestor's golden shield, whose fame reaches the sky, and "from the shoulders of Diomedes, tamer of horses, / the intricately worked corselet that Hephaestus toiled to make" (8.191–195; see [[hector]], [[diomedes]] and [[corselet]]). With these, he says, he could make the Achaeans board their ships that night (8.196–197). Ἥφαιστος κάμε τεύχων, "Hephaestus toiled to make", is the half-verse for the god's handiwork. It describes Agamemnon's scepter (2.101) and the new armor of Achilles, "the gifts of the god" (19.368; see [[the-scepter-of-agamemnon]]). The poem does not say how Diomedes came by the corselet, and he does not lose it; Hector takes neither prize. δαιδάλεος, "intricately worked", is the word for fine craftsmanship (see [[intricately-worked]]).
