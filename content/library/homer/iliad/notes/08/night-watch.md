summary: Hector keeps the Trojans on the plain for the night. They are to burn fires all night so that the Achaeans cannot slip away by sea, to guard the city with boys and old men on the towers and a fire in every house, and to keep a steadfast watch; and a thousand fires burn between the ships and the Xanthus.

**Hector's orders.** The first night watch of the poem is set out in Hector's speech at nightfall (8.497–541; see [[assembly]]). It has three parts.

1. **The fires.** The army is to camp on the plain and make supper, “so that all night long until early-born Dawn we may burn many fires, and their glare may reach the sky, in case even by night the long-haired Achaeans set out to flee over the broad back of the sea” (8.508–511). The fires watch the enemy. Hector wants the Achaeans to take their wounds home with them, if they go at all (8.512–516; see [[ships]]).
2. **The city.** With the army out, the heralds are to proclaim that boys and gray-templed old men spend the night on the god-built towers, and the women are to burn a great fire in every hall, “and let there be a steadfast watch, so that no ambush may enter the city while the army is away” (8.517–522; see [[heralds]], [[ambush]] and [[the-walls-of-troy]]).
3. **The army.** “But for the night we will guard ourselves, and early, at daybreak, armed in our armor, we will rouse sharp Ares beside the hollow ships” (8.529–531).

**The fires.** The Trojans sit all night “along the bridges of war” (8.553; see the translation's note on 4.371), with their fires burning among them, as many as the stars that shine out round the moon on a windless night, when the shepherd is glad at heart (8.555–559):

{{quote:571a0630-c99f-55ed-821c-0f311cd3e32c}}

A thousand fires and fifty men at each give the Trojan army a size, fifty thousand men, which the poem states nowhere else. The passage became a test piece for English translators (see [[the-watch-fires-in-english]]). The fires are seen from the ships. When the night continues in Book 9, Odysseus tells Achilles that the Trojans have camped close to the wall and “kindled many fires throughout the army” (9.234), and in Book 10 Agamemnon lies awake gazing at them (10.11–13).

**The answer.** The Achaeans answer with a watch of their own. Book 9 opens on “So the Trojans kept their watch” (9.1), and Nestor proposes supper and guards for the Achaeans in the first two verses of Hector's order (9.65–66 = 8.502–503; see [[feast]] and [[yield-to-night]]).
