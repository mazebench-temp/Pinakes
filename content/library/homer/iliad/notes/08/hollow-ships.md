---
summary: The hollow ships are where Odysseus runs, where Hector means to bring fire, where the wounded Teucer is carried, and where Hector will rouse the war at dawn.
---

Both epithets of the entry occur in Book 8. κοῖλος names the camp as the goal of a retreat: Odysseus, deaf to Diomedes' call, “darted past toward the hollow ships of the Achaeans” (8.98; [[much-enduring]]). γλαφυρός names it three times. Hector imagines his arrival: “But when I come among the hollow ships, then let there be some remembrance of consuming fire” (8.180–181; [[destructive]]). The wounded Teucer is carried “groaning heavily, to the hollow ships” (8.334; [[brilliant]], [[trusty]]). And at night Hector promises: “early, at daybreak, armed in our armor, we will rouse sharp Ares beside the hollow ships” (8.530–531; [[sharp]]). The ships are the Achaeans' refuge and the Trojans' target in the same book. See [[ships]].
