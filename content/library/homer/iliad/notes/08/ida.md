**Zeus' seat.** Zeus drives from Olympus to "Ida of the many springs, mother of wild beasts, / to Gargarus, where he has a precinct and a fragrant altar" (8.47–48; see [[of-the-many-springs]], [[mother-of-wild-beasts]] and [[gargarus]]). The first verse brings Hera and Sleep to the mountain in Book 14, and Apollo and Iris in Book 15 (14.283, 15.151). From the peaks he watches the city and the ships (8.51–52; see [[watching-the-war]]), and the mountain is where the day's signs come from. He thunders "from Ida" after weighing the fates (ἐξ Ἴδης, 8.75), and three times "from the mountains of Ida" while Diomedes ponders whether to turn and fight (ἀπʼ Ἰδαίων ὀρέων, 8.170; see [[zeus]] and [[thunderbolt]]). Iris goes "from the mountains of Ida" to Olympus with his message (8.410; see [[iris]]).

**Seen from Olympus.** Hera, urging Poseidon to join her, pictures the result: Zeus "would sit there alone on Ida and fret" (8.207; see [[hera]]). Twice "father Zeus" acts "from Ida", Ἴδηθεν (8.397, 8.438): he sees the two goddesses set out, and at evening he drives his chariot home to Olympus (see [[who-rules-from-ida]] and [[olympus]]). He goes back to it for the next day of battle, and sits on its peaks with the lightning in his hands (11.181–184).
