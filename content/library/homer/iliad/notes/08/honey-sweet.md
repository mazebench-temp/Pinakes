---
summary: Andromache sets “honey-sweet wheat” before Hector's horses, and at night the Trojans provide themselves with “honey-sweet wine” for their camp on the plain.
---

**The horses' wheat.** Hector reminds his horses of Andromache's care: she gave them first “honey-sweet wheat” and mixed them wine to drink, before she served him (8.186–190; [[the-horses-of-hector]], [[andromache]], [[flourishing]]). Wine given to horses occurs nowhere else in Homer ([[wine]]).

**The army's wine.** At night Hector orders the Trojans to bring cattle and sheep from the city, “and provide yourselves with honey-sweet wine and bread from the halls” (8.505–507), and the next verses say that they do it in the same words (8.545–547; [[fat-sheep]], [[feast]]). The epithet of sleep and wine marks the comforts of the night before the next day's fighting. See [[trojans]].
