summary: Agamemnon, praying at the ships, asks Zeus whether he has ever before struck one of the mighty kings “with such blind folly” and robbed him of glory. His ἄασας, “you have blinded”, is the only active form of the verb in the Iliad, with Zeus as the one who blinds, and the prayer stands between the king's pretended complaint in Book 2 and his real one in Book 9.

Agamemnon has shamed the army and now turns to Zeus. His question joins the two things the god can give and take: ἄτη and κῦδος. τῇδʼ ἄτῃ ἄασας, “struck … with such blind folly”, is literally “blinded with this blindness”, the noun with its own verb, ἀάω. The verb is active only here in the Iliad, with Zeus as the one who blinds; elsewhere it is middle or passive, as when Agamemnon says that he “was blinded” (9.116, 19.137), and in Book 19 Zeus himself “was blinded” by Ate (19.95). The glory taken from him is the glory Zeus is giving Hector the same day (see [[kydos]]).

The rest of the prayer shows what the blindness was. Agamemnon never passed an altar of Zeus without sacrifice, “eager to sack well-walled Troy” (8.241). In Book 2, testing the army, he pretended that Zeus had bound him in blind folly because he had promised “that I would sack well-walled Ilios and then sail home”, and now bade him go home in disgrace (2.111–115; see the note on Book 2). The pretense has become his complaint: the hope Zeus gave him, and the sacrifices he made for it, have ended in this rout. He does not yet say that the folly was his own. At night, before the assembly of Book 9, he repeats the lines of Book 2 in earnest (9.18–25), and in the council that follows he admits to Nestor, “I was blinded, I do not deny it”, of his quarrel with Achilles (9.115–120). In Book 19 he will blame Zeus, Moira and the Erinys, who cast ἄτη into his mind (19.86–89).

Zeus does not reproach him. He pities the king's tears, nods that the people will be saved, and sends an eagle (8.245–252; see [[pity]] and [[neuo]]). The prayer asks for nothing more than escape, and that is granted for the day.
