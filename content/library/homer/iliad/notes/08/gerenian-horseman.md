---
summary: Nestor is “the Gerenian horseman” twice in Book 8, as he takes Diomedes' reins and as he answers Diomedes' fear of disgrace; once he is “the Gerenian” alone, the guardian of the Achaeans, left on the field with a wounded horse.
grc: [Γερήνιος]
en: [Gerenian]
---

**The Gerenian.** When Zeus' lightning falls among the Achaeans, “only Nestor stayed, the Gerenian, the guardian of the Achaeans” (8.80). Γερήνιος stands without ἱππότα, in its other formula, Γερήνιος οὖρος Ἀχαιῶν ([[guardian-of-the-achaeans]]). The horseman is held on the field by a horse: Paris has shot his trace horse in the head, and the old man is cutting it loose with his sword when Hector bears down on him (8.81–91; [[paris]]).

**The horseman as driver.** Diomedes takes him into his chariot, “and the Gerenian horseman Nestor did not disobey” (8.112). The old man who cannot fight drives: he takes the glossy reins and brings Diomedes close to Hector (8.116–117; [[glossy]]), and when Zeus' thunderbolt falls before the horses, the reins slip from his hands and he is the one who tells Diomedes to turn and flee (8.133–144). “Then the Gerenian horseman Nestor answered him” (8.151) introduces his reply to Diomedes' fear that Hector will call him a coward: the Trojans and their wives will never believe it (8.152–156).
