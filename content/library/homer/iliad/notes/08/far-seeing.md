---
summary: Zeus is “far-seeing” in Hera's plan to hold him off from the battle and as he sits down on his golden throne when he comes home to Olympus.
---

Hera proposes to Poseidon that the gods who help the Danaans “drive back the Trojans and hold off far-seeing Zeus,” who would then sit alone on Ida and fret (8.205–207; [[reckless-in-speech]]). Poseidon refuses. At the end of the day the god she meant to hold off comes home: “and far-seeing Zeus himself sat down on his golden throne, and great Olympus shook beneath his feet” (8.442–443; [[well-wheeled]], [[throne]]). The shaking of Olympus under the seated god recalls his nod in Book 1, which made great Olympus quake (1.528–530). Whether the epithet means “far-seeing” or “wide-voiced” is disputed; in Book 8 the god sees everything from Ida, Hera's and Athena's chariot included (8.397). See [[zeus]].
