---
summary: In Book 8 the Danaans are above all the people the gods grieve for. Athena and then Hera tell Zeus, “we grieve for the Danaan spearmen, who will fill up the measure of an evil fate and perish” (8.33–34, 8.464–465), and Hera appeals to Poseidon and to Athena on behalf of “the Danaans as they perish” (8.202, 8.353).
---

**The gods' grief.** The name occurs twelve times in the book, and seven of them are spoken by gods. Zeus forbids any god to go “to help either the Trojans or the Danaans” (8.11; see [[zeus]]). Athena alone answers him: the gods will keep out of the war, “but still we grieve for the Danaan spearmen, who will fill up the measure of an evil fate and perish” (8.33–34; see [[athena]]). Hera reproaches Poseidon that even his heart does not grieve “for the Danaans as they perish”, though they bring him gifts at Helice and Aegae (8.201–204; see [[poseidon]], [[helice]] and [[aegae]]). Later she asks Athena whether the two of them will no longer “care for the Danaans as they perish, even at the last” (8.352–354). The verse “who will fill up the measure of an evil fate and perish” occurs only in this book, three times: in Athena's plea to Zeus, in Hera's to Athena, and in Hera's to Zeus at nightfall (8.34, 8.354, 8.465). When Iris has turned the goddesses back, Hera gives way: let Zeus “judge between Trojans and Danaans, as is fitting” (8.431; see [[hera]]). Yet at nightfall she says Athena's plea of the morning over again to Zeus, almost word for word (8.463–468 ≈ 8.32–37; see [[pity]]).

**In mortal mouths.** Hector taunts Diomedes, whom “the Danaans of the swift horses honored … above others” (8.161), and boasts that Zeus has promised him victory and great glory, “and … a bane to the Danaans” (8.176; see [[hector]]). Agamemnon shouts to rouse the Danaans (8.227), and tells Teucer that he may become “a light to the Danaans” (8.282; see [[agamemnon]] and [[teucer]]).
