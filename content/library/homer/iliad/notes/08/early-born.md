---
summary: Hector orders fires kept burning all night “until early-born Dawn,” so that the Achaeans cannot slip away by night.
grc: [ἠριγενείης]
---

“So that all night long until early-born Dawn we may burn many fires, and their glare may reach the sky, in case even by night the long-haired Achaeans set out to flee over the broad back of the sea” (8.508–511; [[long-haired]], [[broad-back-of-the-sea]]). Elsewhere in the Iliad the epithet stands in the whole-verse formula of dawn, with Dawn as “rosy-fingered” (1.477, 24.788; [[rosy-fingered]], [[dawn-line]]). Here it ends a verse of waiting: the Trojans keep watch through the night for a morning that, Hector expects, will bring him the ships. The book ends with their horses waiting for it (8.565; [[of-the-fair-throne]]). See [[eos]].
