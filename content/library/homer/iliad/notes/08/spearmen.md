---
summary: Athena and then Hera grieve for “the Danaan spearmen” in the same words, and Zeus promises that at dawn Hera will see him destroying “the great army of the Argive spearmen.”
---

The line-end Δαναῶν … αἰχμητάων stands in the plea that frames the day. At dawn Athena says, “but still we grieve for the Danaan spearmen, who will fill up the measure of an evil fate and perish” (8.33–34), and at night Hera repeats her words (8.464–465; [[most-dread]]). Zeus answers Hera with the same epithet: “At dawn you will see the son of Cronus, supreme in might, more than ever, if you wish, ox-eyed queen Hera, destroying the great army of the Argive spearmen” (8.470–472; [[supreme-in-might]], [[ox-eyed]]). The goddesses grieve for the spearmen; Zeus promises to destroy them. See [[danaans]] and [[argives]].
