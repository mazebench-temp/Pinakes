---
summary: Hera addresses Athena as “child of aegis-bearing Zeus” twice in Book 8, first to rouse her to help the Danaans and then to give up, and the narrator calls her his daughter as she arms against him.
en: [daughter]
---

**Hera's two speeches.** “Ah, child of aegis-bearing Zeus, shall we two no longer care for the Danaans as they perish, even at the last” (8.352–353; [[o-popoi]]): with this Hera rouses Athena when she sees Hector driving the Achaeans to their ships. After Iris has delivered Zeus' threat, the same opening begins Hera's surrender: “Ah, child of aegis-bearing Zeus, I will no longer let the two of us fight against Zeus for the sake of mortals” (8.427–428).

**The arming.** Between the two speeches, “Athena, daughter of aegis-bearing Zeus,” lets her soft robe fall on her father's floor and puts on the tunic of Zeus the cloud-gatherer to arm for war (8.384–388; [[cloud-gatherer]], [[arming-scene]]). The formula that names her as Zeus' daughter frames her attempt to act against him, and she ends the day sulking at her father (8.459–460; [[father-zeus]]). See [[athena]].
