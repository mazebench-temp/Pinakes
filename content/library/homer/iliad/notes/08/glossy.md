---
summary: Nestor takes “the glossy reins” of Diomedes' chariot and drives at Hector, and the reins slip from his hands when Zeus' thunderbolt falls.
---

Two verses frame Nestor's drive. “Nestor took the glossy reins in his hands and whipped the horses, and soon they were close to Hector” (8.116–117): the old man, who cannot fight, drives for Diomedes ([[gerenian-horseman]]). When the thunderbolt falls in front of the horses, “The glossy reins slipped from Nestor's hands” (8.137; [[thunderbolt]]). The same verse, in the plural, describes the reins slipping from the hands of the sons of Antimachus before Agamemnon kills them (11.128). ἡνία σιγαλόεντα is the formula for reins taken up and let fall (5.226, 5.328, 17.479).
