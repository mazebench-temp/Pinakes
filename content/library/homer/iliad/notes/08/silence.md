**The gods' silence.** “So he spoke, and they all fell hushed in silence, amazed at his word, for he had spoken very forcefully” (8.28–29). The first verse is the one in which the Achaeans met Hector's challenge (7.92) and Idaeus' message (7.398), and the pair returns when the embassy reports Achilles' refusal (9.693–694). Then “at last” (ὀψὲ δὲ δή) a speaker breaks the silence, as Diomedes does in Books 7 and 9 (7.399, 9.31, 9.696); here it is Athena (8.30). The silence, Athena's plea and Zeus' mild answer are made of verses found elsewhere, and the thirteen verses 8.28–40 are reported to have been rejected in antiquity (see the translation's note on 8.28–40 and [[smiling-at-him]]).

**The goddesses' silence.** At evening Athena and Hera sit apart from Zeus, “and said nothing to him, and asked him nothing; but he understood in his heart” (8.445–446). When he has mocked them, “Athena kept silent and said nothing, sulking at father Zeus, and a savage anger gripped her; but Hera's breast could not contain her anger” (8.459–461). The three verses are those of the quarrel on Olympus at the start of Book 4 (4.22–24), where Athena is silent and Hera speaks. Hera speaks, Zeus answers with the prophecy of the battle at the ships and with contempt, “and white-armed Hera said nothing to him” (8.484). The day on Olympus ends as the quarrel of Book 1 ended, with Hera silent before Zeus (1.568–569).
