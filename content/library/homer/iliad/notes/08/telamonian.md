---
summary: Ajax is “son of Telamon,” Τελαμωνιάδης, in the verse that places his huts at the end of the line and in the verse in which Teucer takes his stand under his shield; once, in Agamemnon's praise, the title of Ajax is given to Teucer.
grc: [Τελαμωνιάδαο]
en: [son of Telamon]
---

**Ajax.** The patronymic in -ιάδης names Ajax twice, in the genitive at the end of the verse. Agamemnon's shout from Odysseus' ship reaches “both to the huts of Ajax, son of Telamon, and to those of Achilles” (8.224–225; [[huge-hulled]]), and Teucer “took his stand under the shield of Ajax, son of Telamon” (8.267; [[back-bent]], [[the-shield-of-ajax]]).

**Teucer.** Agamemnon praises the archer with “Teucer, dear heart, son of Telamon, lord of the people” (8.281; [[dear-heart]]). The half-verse Τελαμώνιε κοίρανε λαῶν is the one with which Hector addresses Ajax in Book 7 (7.234), and with which Achilles and Menelaus address Ajax later (9.644, 11.465). Here it goes to Teucer, Telamon's son by another mother, in the speech that recalls that Telamon reared him in his own house, “bastard though you were” (8.283–284; [[telamon]], [[nothos]]). In Book 13 the narrator gives Teucer the same adjective, Τεῦκρος … Τελαμώνιος, “Teucer, son of Telamon” (13.170). See [[ajax]] and [[teucer]].
