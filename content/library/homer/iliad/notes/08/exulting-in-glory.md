---
summary: Zeus sits on the peaks of Ida “exulting in his glory,” looking out on the city of the Trojans and the ships of the Achaeans.
---

{{quote:6709371f-855c-5e81-a9a4-8106563d4945}}

The phrase described Briareus, seated beside Zeus to frighten the gods who would have bound him (1.405), and Ares, seated beside Zeus after Paeeon had healed his wound (5.906). Here it is Zeus himself, alone on the mountain, with the whole war below him: the city on one side, the ships on the other ([[watching-the-war]], [[gargarus]], [[mother-of-wild-beasts]]). The same two verses seat him on Ida again at the start of Book 11, when he has sent Strife among the Achaeans (11.80–83). From his seat he weighs the fates of the armies at noon and thunders on the Achaeans (8.68–77; [[the-scales-of-zeus]]). See [[zeus]].
