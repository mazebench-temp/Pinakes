summary: The divine scenes of Book 8 begin and end on Olympus. Zeus holds assembly on its topmost peak and threatens to hang the world from it, Hera makes it quake in her anger, Iris meets the goddesses at its outermost gates, and it shakes again beneath Zeus when he returns from Ida.
grc: [Ὄλυμπος]
passages:

  - 8.438-443 | Zeus returns to Olympus

**Morning.** Zeus calls the gods to assembly "on the topmost peak of many-ridged Olympus" (8.3; see [[many-ridged-olympus]] and [[olympians]]). Any god who disobeys him "will come back to Olympus" beaten, or be hurled into Tartarus (8.12–13; see [[tartarus]]). Οὔλυμπον δέ is "to Olympus", with the suffix -δε printed as a separate word. With the golden rope he could bind earth and sea "around a peak of Olympus" and leave them hanging in midair (8.25–26; see [[the-golden-rope]]). Then he leaves the mountain for Ida (8.41–48; see [[ida]]).

**Shaken.** Olympus shakes twice in the book. When Hector boasts, Hera "shook on her throne, and made high Olympus quake" (8.199; see [[high-olympus]] and [[hera]]). At evening Zeus returns:

{{quote:8019f30d-636f-5095-b2cd-14d07569f0ad}}

In Book 1 the mountain shook when he nodded to Thetis (1.528–530). Here it shakes beneath his feet as he takes his throne, at the end of a day his nod decided.

**The gates.** Between the two scenes Hera and Athena drive out through the gates of heaven, which the Hours keep, "to whom great heaven and Olympus are entrusted" (8.393–394; see [[hours]]), and Iris stops them "at the outermost gates of many-folded Olympus" (8.411; see [[iris]]). πολύπτυχος, "with many folds", is said of Olympus again at 20.5 and of Ida at 21.449 and 22.171 (see [[many-folded]]). In his mockery Zeus names it as the gods' home: all the gods on Olympus could not turn him back, and the two goddesses, struck by his thunderbolt, would not have come back "to Olympus, where the seat of the immortals is" (8.451, 8.455–456).
