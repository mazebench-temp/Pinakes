{{quote:3a0e2a1b-df4f-5bd7-ab27-d9d570a06f03}}

Zeus' threat measures the world from top to bottom. A god who disobeys him will come back to Olympus beaten, or be thrown into Tartarus, as far below Hades as the sky is above the earth, behind gates of iron and a threshold of bronze (8.10–17; [[tartarus]], [[hades]]). The epithet is that of the dark below: in later books it describes the “misty gloom” that Hades received as his share of the world (15.191) and into which the dead go down (21.56, 23.51). At the end of the book Zeus names Tartarus again, the deep place where Iapetus and Cronus sit with no joy in the sun or the winds (8.478–481; [[iapetus]], [[cronus]], [[fall-of-cronus]]).
