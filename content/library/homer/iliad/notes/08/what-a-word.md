---
summary: Three speeches of Book 8 open in protest at what has just been said: Nestor's to Diomedes, Poseidon's to Hera, and Hera's to Zeus, in the verse of her protest in Book 4.
grc: [οἷον ἔειπες]
---

“Ah, son of wise-hearted Tydeus, what a thing you have said!” (οἷον ἔειπες, 8.152): Nestor answers Diomedes' fear of what Hector will say of him (see [[tis-speech]]). The same half-verse opened Zeus' answer to Poseidon's complaint at the end of Book 7 (7.455). Poseidon answers Hera's proposal that the gods fight Zeus, “Hera, reckless in speech, what a thing you have said!” (8.209; see [[reckless-in-speech]]). Hera answers Zeus' mockery, “Most dread son of Cronus, what a thing you have said!” (8.462; see [[most-dread]]), the verse she used against him in Book 4 (4.25) and will use again in Books 16 and 18 (16.440, 18.361). The protest comes from every side: an old man to a young one, a god to a goddess, a wife to her husband.
