---
summary: Paris has one act in Book 8. His arrow kills Nestor's trace horse and leaves the old man stranded in the path of Hector's chariot (8.80–86).
refs: [8.81]
passages:
  - 8.80-86 | Paris shoots Nestor's horse
---

The shot is introduced by the whole verse that names him as Helen's husband, δῖος Ἀλέξανδρος Ἑλένης πόσις ἠϋκόμοιο, “brilliant Alexander, the husband of Helen of the lovely hair” (8.82). It named him as he armed for the duel (3.329) and as he refused to give her up (7.355), and its last words introduce his arrows again when he wounds Diomedes and Machaon (11.369, 11.505; see [[husband-of-helen]] and [[helen]]). He fights as an archer (see [[bow]] and [[arrows]]), and his arrow finds the deadliest place on a horse, the crown of the head, “where the foremost hairs of horses grow on the skull” (8.83–84). The horse rears and throws the team into confusion, and Nestor, cutting it loose, is left in the path of Hector's chariot until Diomedes comes to save him (8.85–117; see [[nestor]] and [[diomedes]]).

**Outside Homer.** In Pindar's account of the death of Antilochus, who saved his father from Memnon, it is again a horse wounded by Paris' arrows that hampers Nestor's chariot (*Pythian* 6.28–42; see [[antilochus]]).
