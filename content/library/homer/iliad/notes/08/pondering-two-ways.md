---
summary: Diomedes is “torn two ways in thought” whether to turn and fight Hector, and the deliberation is counted out in threes: “Three times he pondered in his mind and in his heart”, and three times Zeus thunders.
grc: ["μερμήριξε κατὰ φρένα καὶ κατὰ θυμόν"]
---

Both formulas of deliberation meet in three verses. διάνδιχα μερμήριξεν, “was torn two ways in thought”, is the verb of Achilles' pondering in Book 1 (8.167; 1.189). μερμήριξε κατὰ φρένα καὶ κατὰ θυμόν, “pondered in his mind and in his heart” (8.169), joins that verb to the phrase of the other formula, ὥρμαινε κατὰ φρένα καὶ κατὰ θυμόν (1.193), in a combination found only here. τρὶς μὲν … τρὶς δέ, “three times … three times”, sets the man's mind against the god's sign, turn for turn (8.169–170). The rhythm is that of the hero's three attempts and the god's resistance, but here there is no fourth attempt: Diomedes gives up the thought (see [[three-times-and-a-fourth]] and [[decision-scene]]).
