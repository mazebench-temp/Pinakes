summary: “The powerful earth-shaker” answers Hera's proposal to defy Zeus with a refusal.

{{quote:e30792ba-ffae-5030-a911-8de4e47442d2}}

The verse that introduces Poseidon's answer joins two of his titles, κρείων ἐνοσίχθων ([[powerful]]), to a formula of troubled reply ([[deeply-troubled]]). The god of the earthquake, who will shake the earth and the mountains in the battle of the gods (20.57–65), will not fight Zeus, and he gives the reason Nestor gave Diomedes: Zeus is “far the stronger” (8.144, 8.211; [[reckless-in-speech]]). In Book 15, ordered out of the battle, he will protest that he is Zeus' equal in honor, one of the three sons of Cronus who divided the world, and then give way (15.184–217).
