summary: Most of Book 8's speeches close with the formula, and what follows is often a refusal. Odysseus “did not hear”; Hector's speech to his horses closes “So he spoke, boasting”; Zeus' last words are met by Hera's silence; and the book's last speech ends “So Hector spoke, and the Trojans shouted in applause”, a verse that returns in Book 18.
grc: ["ὣς ἄρα φωνήσασα", "ὣς Ἕκτωρ ἀγόρευʼ"]
en: [So Hector spoke]
---

**Variants.** Book 8 uses the formula in most of its shapes: ὣς ἔφατʼ and ὣς φάτο, “So he spoke” (8.97, 8.167); ὣς εἰπών, “So saying” (8.41, 8.184); ὣς ἄρα φωνήσας and ὣς ἄρα φωνήσασα, “So he spoke, and” and “So she spoke, and”, each time before the horses are turned back, by Nestor in flight and by Hera giving up (8.157, 8.432); and ἦ ῥα καί, “He spoke, and”, as Teucer lets fly another arrow (8.300). After Hera and Poseidon's exchange the formula is plural: “So they spoke such words to one another” (8.212).

**What follows.** The verse turns from the speech to its effect, and in Book 8 the effect is often a refusal. Diomedes calls to Odysseus, “So he spoke, but much-enduring brilliant Odysseus did not hear” (8.97; see the translation's note on 8.97). Hector's speech to his horses closes ὣς ἔφατʼ εὐχόμενος, “So he spoke, boasting”, and Hera is indignant (8.198); with the same words a prayer is closed elsewhere, “So he spoke in prayer” (24.314; see [[euchomai]]). Zeus' threats are met by the gods' silence (8.28), and his last words by Hera's: “So he spoke, and white-armed Hera said nothing to him” (8.484; see [[silence]]).

**The last speech.** Hector's orders for the night are answered by the whole army: “So Hector spoke, and the Trojans shouted in applause” (8.542). The verse returns at the end of the Trojans' second assembly on the plain, where the narrator adds, “fools, for Pallas Athena took away their wits” (18.310–311; see [[assembly]]).
