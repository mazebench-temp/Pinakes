---
summary: Zeus is “aegis-bearing” five times in Book 8: in Agamemnon's promise to Teucer, and in the formulas that name Athena as his daughter as she goes to arm against his will.
---

Agamemnon promises Teucer a gift of honor “if aegis-bearing Zeus and Athena grant me to sack the well-built citadel of Ilios” (8.287–288; [[well-built]], [[dear-heart]], [[geras]]). The other four uses all concern Athena. Hera twice calls her “child of aegis-bearing Zeus” (8.352, 8.427; [[child-of-aegis-bearing-zeus]]); Athena says she will go “into the house of aegis-bearing Zeus” to arm herself (8.375); and as she arms she is “daughter of aegis-bearing Zeus” (8.384). The epithet of the father's power stands beside the daughter who sets out to defy him, and who is turned back by his threat (8.397–437). See [[zeus]], [[athena]] and [[aegis]].
