---
summary: Eniopeus, Hector's charioteer, killed by Diomedes, is the son of “high-hearted Thebaeus.”
---

Diomedes' spear misses Hector and strikes “the charioteer, his attendant, Eniopeus, son of high-hearted Thebaeus” (8.119–121; [[eniopeus]], [[thebaeus]]). The verse has the shape of 5.77, where Eurypylus kills Hypsenor, “son of high-hearted Dolopion”: the father of a minor victim receives the epithet as the son dies ([[genealogy-in-battle]]). The same epithet describes Diomedes himself in Books 4 and 5 (4.365, 5.376), and the Trojans as a people (6.111). See [[androktasia]].
