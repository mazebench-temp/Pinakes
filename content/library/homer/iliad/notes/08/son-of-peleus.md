---
summary: Achilles is named by his father in Zeus' prophecy: Hector will not stop “until the swift-footed son of Peleus is roused beside the ships.”
---

{{quote:49a53fa3-4b0f-5958-b526-0294b982193c}}

Achilles takes no part in Book 8, and his name comes up twice: in Athena's complaint that Zeus has granted Thetis her wish to honor him (8.370–372; [[sacker-of-cities]], [[thetis-supplicates-zeus]]), and in Zeus' prophecy, where he is “the swift-footed son of Peleus” (8.474; [[swift-footed]]). The prophecy is the poem's first word of Patroclus' death, and it sets the turning point of the war at the moment when the son of Peleus will be roused ([[plan-of-zeus]], [[the-death-of-patroclus]]). See [[achilles]] and [[peleus]].
