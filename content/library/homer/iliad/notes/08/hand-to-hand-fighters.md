---
summary: Hector calls on the “Trojans and Lycians and Dardanians who fight hand to hand,” the first of six uses of his verse of exhortation.
---

“Trojans and Lycians and Dardanians who fight hand to hand, be men, my friends, and remember your furious courage” (8.173–174; [[exhortation]], [[furious]]). The verse is Hector's alone in the Iliad: he uses it here, and again in Books 11, 13, 15 and 17, whenever he calls the Trojans and their allies to a new effort (11.286, 13.150, 15.425, 15.486, 17.184). The close fighters are the Dardanians, the people of the Troad ([[dardanians]], [[lycians]]). Here the call follows Zeus' thunder, which Hector takes as a promise of victory (8.175–176; [[that-turns-the-battle]]). See [[hector]].
