---
summary: “Agamemnon, lord of men,” rejoices to see Teucer destroying the Trojan battalions with his bow.
---

“Agamemnon, lord of men, rejoiced when he saw him destroying the battalions of the Trojans with his strong bow” (8.278–279; [[krateros]]). The title of the commander of the whole army stands at the start of his praise of Teucer, in which he promises him a gift of honor second only to his own share when Troy is taken (8.280–291; [[dear-heart]], [[geras]]). That morning the same king had fled before Zeus' lightning, and at midday he had prayed in tears for the army's escape (8.78, 8.236–246). See [[agamemnon]].
