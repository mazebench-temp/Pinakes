---
summary: Agamemnon goes through the camp to rally the Achaeans “holding a great purple cloak in his strong hand”.
passages:
  - 8.220-227 | Agamemnon at Odysseus' ship
---

Prompted by Hera, Agamemnon goes along the huts and ships of the Achaeans, “holding a great purple cloak in his strong hand” (πορφύρεον μέγα φᾶρος, 8.221), and stands on Odysseus' ship, in the middle of the line, to shout to both ends (8.222–227; see [[ships]] and [[exhortation]]). The poem does not say why he carries the cloak in his hand instead of wearing it. It is usually taken as a way of being seen, a great purple cloth held up by the king at the center of the camp (see [[purple]]). The same words, πορφύρεον μέγα φᾶρος, name the cloak Odysseus draws over his head at the Phaeacian court to hide his tears (Od. 8.84–86). Agamemnon put on his great φᾶρος when he rose from his dream in Book 2 (2.43); here, in the rout, he carries it in his hand.
