---
summary: “Shining Hector” holds his assembly on the plain at nightfall; in Zeus' mockery the word describes the “shining limbs” of the goddesses, which trembled before they saw the war.
---

**Hector.** The formula φαίδιμος Ἕκτωρ opens the night assembly: “Then shining Hector held an assembly of the Trojans, leading them away from the ships, beside the eddying river” (8.489–490; [[assembly]], [[eddying]], [[dear-to-zeus]]). It is the end of the day that Zeus has given him, and the speech that follows gives his orders for the night and his boast for the morning (8.497–541).

**The goddesses' limbs.** Zeus uses the word of Hera and Athena in his mockery: “but trembling seized your shining limbs before you ever saw the war and the grim deeds of war” (8.452–453; [[grim-deeds]]). φαίδιμα γυῖα is the phrase for a man's limbs as they fail him: loosed in death (6.27, 16.805), or trembling under Agamemnon in his sleepless fear for the army (10.95). Here it is the limbs of goddesses who turned back. See [[hector]].
