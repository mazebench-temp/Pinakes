summary: Athena is “the gray-eyed goddess” as she speaks to Zeus and to Hera; she hopes Zeus will call her “his dear gray-eyed one” again, and soon he names her “the gray-eyed one” in the threat that turns her back.
grc: [γλαυκώπιδα, γλαυκῶπι]
---

**The formula.** θεὰ γλαυκῶπις Ἀθήνη introduces Athena's two speeches: her plea after the gods' silence, “At last the gray-eyed goddess Athena spoke among them” (8.30; [[most-high]]), and her answer to Hera, “Then the gray-eyed goddess Athena spoke to her in turn” (8.357), in which she complains of her father ([[hard]], [[shrewd-mind]]).

**The name.** Her complaint ends with a hope: “Yet the day will come when he calls me his dear gray-eyed one again” (8.373). The epithet stands alone as a name, the name a father uses for a favorite daughter. Zeus uses it soon after, not in affection but in a threat: he will smash the chariot “so that the gray-eyed one may know what it is to fight her father” (8.406), and Iris says it to her face, “so that you may know, gray-eyed one, what it is to fight your father” (8.420; [[message-repeated]], [[shameless-dog]]). See [[athena]].
