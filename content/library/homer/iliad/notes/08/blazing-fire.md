---
summary: Hector would have burned the ships “with blazing fire,” and Agamemnon says he soon will; at nightfall the Trojans sit by a thousand fires on the plain, “in the glare of the blazing fire.”
grc: [πυρὶ κηλέῳ]
---

**The ships.** The new formula πυρὶ κηλέῳ appears twice, both times of Hector's threat to the ships. The narrator says that he would have done it: “And now he would have burned the balanced ships with blazing fire, had not queen Hera put it in Agamemnon's mind” to rally the army (8.217–219; [[would-have-had-not]], [[balanced-ships]], [[queenly]]). Agamemnon repeats the threat in his rebuke: now they are not worth even one man, “Hector, who will soon burn the ships with blazing fire” (8.234–235; [[empty-boasting]]). Hector himself has told the Trojans to remember the fire when he reaches the ships (8.180–183; [[destructive]]). The burning of the ships is the goal of his attack from here on. The first ship burns in Book 16 (16.122–124), and when he lies dead the Achaeans say he is softer to handle now “than when he burned the ships with blazing fire” (22.373–374).

**The watch-fires.** At the end of the day the older formula, πυρὸς αἰθομένοιο, describes the Trojan camp on the plain: a thousand fires, and beside each “sat fifty men in the glare of the blazing fire” (8.562–563; [[preeminent]]). The fire that was to burn the ships lights the Trojans' watch through the night.
