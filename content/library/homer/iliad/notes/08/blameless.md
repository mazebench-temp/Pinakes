---
summary: “Noble Teucer” kills eight Trojans and answers Agamemnon's praise, and his ninth arrow kills “noble Gorgythion,” a son of Priam.
---

The epithet marks Teucer's day. “Whom then did noble Teucer kill first of the Trojans?” (8.273): the narrator's question introduces the list of his eight victims ([[whom-first-whom-last]], [[long-barbed]]). “Answering him, noble Teucer said” (8.292) introduces his reply to Agamemnon's praise ([[dear-heart]]). The arrow he aims at Hector then strikes “noble Gorgythion, the brave son of Priam” (8.302–303; [[gorgythion]], [[brave]], [[like-the-goddesses]]). The same epithet honors the archer and his victim. See [[teucer]].
