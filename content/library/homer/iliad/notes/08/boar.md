---
summary: The boar of Book 8 is the quarry of the hound simile. Hector harries the Achaeans as a hound catches at a wild boar or a lion from behind, at hips and buttocks, and watches for it as it wheels (8.338–342).
grc: [συὸς]
---

συὸς ἀγρίου, “a wild boar” (8.338): σῦς is the word for any pig, and ἄγριος makes it the boar of the mountains, the beast that turns on its hunters (see [[the-hound-at-the-heels]]). That is what the hound in the simile fears. It catches at the boar from behind and ἑλισσόμενόν τε δοκεύει, “watches for it as it wheels” (8.340), since a boar that turns can kill a dog. The Achaeans are the boar, in flight but dangerous, and Hector the hound that picks off the hindmost (8.341–342).

The roles are reversed elsewhere. In Book 12 Hector is the boar or lion that wheels among dogs and huntsmen (12.41–49), and in Book 17 the Trojans who run at the Achaeans carrying Patroclus' body are dogs that rush at a wounded boar until it turns on them (17.725–734).
