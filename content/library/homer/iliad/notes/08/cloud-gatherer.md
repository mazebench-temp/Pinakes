---
summary: Zeus the cloud-gatherer answers Athena with a smile at the start of the day and Hera with the prophecy of the battle at the ships at its end; between them Athena arms in the tunic of Zeus the cloud-gatherer.
---

“Smiling at her, Zeus the cloud-gatherer said” (8.38) introduces his answer to Athena's plea: “Take heart, Tritogeneia, dear child; I do not speak in full earnest” (8.39–40; [[smiling-at-him]], [[tritogeneia]]). The mildness sits oddly after his threats, and the thirteen verses of the gods' silence, Athena's plea and Zeus' answer (8.28–40), which are made of verses found elsewhere, are reported to have been rejected in antiquity.

In the afternoon Athena arms against his will “putting on the tunic of Zeus the cloud-gatherer” (8.387), in the verses of her arming in Book 5 (5.733–737; [[arming-scene]], [[tunic]], [[inlaid]]). At nightfall the formula of the answer, “Answering her, Zeus the cloud-gatherer said” (8.469; [[answering-him]]), brings in his reply to Hera's protest: at dawn he will be stronger still, and Hector will not stop until Achilles is roused beside the ships over the dead Patroclus (8.470–477; [[plan-of-zeus]], [[the-death-of-patroclus]]).
