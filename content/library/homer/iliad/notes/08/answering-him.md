---
summary: Teucer answers Agamemnon's praise, Athena answers Hera's call to arms, and Zeus answers Hera's protest with the prophecy of Patroclus' death, each in a formula of reply.
grc: ["ἀπαμειβόμενος προσεφώνεε"]
en: [face to face]
---

“Answering him, noble Teucer said” (τὸν δʼ ἀπαμειβόμενος προσεφώνεε, 8.292): the formula takes προσεφώνεε, “addressed”, in place of προσέφη, a variant used again of Sleep answering Hera (14.242) and twice in the Odyssey (Od. 14.401, 16.308). Athena “spoke to her in turn” when Hera calls her to arms (αὖτε προσέειπε, 8.357). Zeus answers Hera's protest in the commonest form, “Answering her, Zeus the cloud-gatherer said” (8.469; see [[cloud-gatherer]]), and the answer is the prophecy of the battle at the ships and the death of Patroclus (8.470–477; see [[plan-of-zeus]]). Hera addresses Poseidon ἀντίον, “face to face” (8.200).

The book's other replies have their own openings: Diomedes and Nestor answer each other with “Then … answered him” (8.145, 8.151; see [[then-answered]]), Poseidon answers Hera “deeply troubled” (8.208; see [[deeply-troubled]]), and Zeus answers Athena “smiling at her” (8.38; see [[smiling-at-him]]). Some speeches get no reply at all: Odysseus does not hear Diomedes (8.97), and Hera says nothing to Zeus' last words (8.484; see [[silence]]).
