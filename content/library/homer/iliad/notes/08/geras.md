**The word.** πρεσβήϊον is formed from πρέσβυς, “elder”: the share first due to age. γέρας has been connected in the same way with γέρων, “old man”, though that link is uncertain. The translation keeps the two apart, πρεσβήϊον “gift of honor” and γέρας “prize” (see the translation's table of key words). πρεσβήϊον occurs only here in Homer.

**First after the king.** The promise has the shape of a γέρας, a thing chosen out of the spoils of a sacked city before the rest is shared (see [[division-of-spoils]]), but it is the king who will give it, “into your hand”, and its rank is set by its place, “first after me”. In Book 9 Agamemnon will promise Achilles a share of the same kind: if the gods grant the sack of Priam's city, let him load his ship with gold and bronze when the Achaeans divide the spoils, and choose twenty Trojan women (9.135–140). Both promises are made on a city not yet taken, and the poem tells neither sack.

**The gifts.** A tripod, a team with its chariot and a woman are the goods of a hero's wealth (see [[tripod]], [[chariot]] and [[captive-women]]). Tripods, horses and women are among the prizes Achilles brings out for the funeral games, and the first prize of the chariot race is a woman and a tripod with ears (23.259–264); in the simile of Hector's flight the racehorses run for “a tripod or a woman” (22.162–164). Teucer answers that he needs no urging (8.292–299; see [[teucer]]).
