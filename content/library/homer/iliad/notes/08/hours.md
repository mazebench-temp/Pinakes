**The gates.** Hera whips on the horses, and "of themselves the gates of heaven groaned open, which the Hours kept, / to whom great heaven and Olympus are entrusted, / both to throw open the thick cloud and to close it" (8.393–395). The three verses are those of the goddesses' drive in Book 5 (5.749–751; see [[olympus]] and [[sky]]). In Book 5 the goddesses went on down to the plain and put Ares to flight; this time Iris meets them at the outermost gates of Olympus with Zeus' threat, and they turn back (8.409–432; see [[iris]], [[hera]] and [[athena]]).

**The stable.**

{{quote:6a4f6ab0-5181-529c-998e-e509be5ffdac}}

The Hours do what a driver or a groom does at the end of a journey: unyoke, tie the horses at the manger, and lean the chariot against the wall (see [[chariot-journey]]). The gods' horses eat the gods' food, at ambrosial mangers (see [[ambrosia]]). The third verse is used in the Odyssey when Telemachus' chariot is stabled in the house of Menelaus (8.435 = *Odyssey* 4.42). A few lines later Poseidon does the same service for Zeus (8.440–441; see [[poseidon]]).
