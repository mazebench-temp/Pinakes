---
summary: Cronus is named twice in Book 8, as the father of Hera, "daughter of great Cronus", and as the prisoner who sits with Iapetus in Tartarus, at the lowest limits of earth and sea. His son is called "son of Cronus" eight times.
passages:
  - 8.477-483 | Iapetus and Cronus in Tartarus
---

**Father.** When Hera harnesses her horses she is "Hera, the august goddess, daughter of great Cronus" (8.383), in the verse of her setting out in Book 5 (5.721; see [[hera]] and [[august-goddess]]).

**Prisoner.** At nightfall Zeus tells Hera that he will not care about her anger even if she goes "to the lowest limits / of earth and sea, where Iapetus and Cronus / sit and have no joy in the rays of Hyperion the Sun / or in the winds, and deep Tartarus is all around them" (8.478–481; see [[tartarus]], [[iapetus]] and [[hyperion]]). It is the Iliad's only picture of the defeated father, and Zeus draws it for Cronus' daughter. The rest of the poem assumes the story: Zeus "set Cronus beneath the earth and the barren sea" (14.203–204), and the gods below are "around Cronus" (14.274, 15.225; see [[fall-of-cronus]]).

**The son.** Book 8 names Zeus by his father eight times. Athena calls him "son of Cronus, highest of lords" (8.31), Nestor tells Diomedes that "Zeus, son of Cronus, grants glory to this man today" (8.141), Hector knows that "the son of Cronus has readily nodded his promise" to him (8.175), Poseidon will not fight "Zeus, son of Cronus" (8.210), Iris twice names "the son of Cronus" in his threat (8.414–415), and Hera answers him "Most dread son of Cronus" (8.462; see [[son-of-cronus]] and [[most-dread]]). The last is Zeus himself, who speaks of himself in the third person: "At dawn you will see the son of Cronus, supreme in might, / … destroying the great army of the Argive spearmen" (8.470–472; see [[supreme-in-might]]). In the same speech, a few verses later, he names the father he has put in Tartarus.
