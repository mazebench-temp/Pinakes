---
summary: Hector wheels his horses before the Achaean ships “with the eyes of the Gorgon or of Ares, ruin of mortals.”
grc: [βροτολοιγοῦ]
---

“And Hector wheeled his fine-maned horses this way and that, with the eyes of the Gorgon or of Ares, ruin of mortals” (8.348–349; [[fine-maned]]). The epithet of the war god's destruction completes the likeness of Hector's gaze. The Gorgon is named only here and on Agamemnon's shield (11.36); elsewhere she is a head, on the aegis (5.741) and in the underworld (Od. 11.634) ([[gorgon]]). The Greek joins the two likenesses with “and” (ἠδέ), where the English presents them as alternatives. Hera sees the Achaeans penned at their ships and pities them (8.350–353; [[white-armed]]).
