**Athena's version.** Hera has proposed that the two of them go down to help the Achaeans, and Athena answers with a complaint against her father. He forgets how often she saved his son Heracles when he was worn out by the labors that Eurystheus set him (8.360–369; see [[heracles-and-cerberus]]). Then she names the cause of the day's defeat:

{{quote:20548812-4ac3-5546-8f95-aa59c171db4b}}

Her account adds to the scene of Book 1. There Thetis took Zeus' knees with her left hand and his chin with her right, and held on, clinging, when he was silent (1.500–502, 1.512–513); she did not kiss his knees. To kiss the knees is found nowhere else in Homer. Priam, clasping Achilles' knees, kisses his hands (24.478; see [[supplication]] and the translation's note on 8.371). Her words also change whose the plan is. In Book 1 Zeus nodded, and the proem calls what follows the plan of Zeus (1.5, 1.524–530). In Athena's mouth the βουλαί, the “plans”, are Thetis', and Zeus has only carried them out (see [[plan-of-zeus]] and [[boule]]). ἐξήνυσε, “has fulfilled”, speaks of them as done, on the evening of the first day on which they have worked.

**Service and favor.** A suppliant's claim rests on past service. Achilles sent his mother to Zeus with the reminder that she had once saved him, when Hera, Poseidon and Pallas Athena wanted to bind him (1.393–406; see [[binding-of-zeus]]). Athena now sets her own services beside Thetis': she helped Zeus' son through his labors at Zeus' own bidding (8.364–365), and Zeus does not remember it (8.362). The goddess who once plotted against Zeus resents the goddess who saved him. She expects the quarrel to pass: “Yet the day will come when he calls me his dear gray-eyed one again” (8.373; see [[athena]] and [[thetis]]).

**Zeus' version.** Zeus himself tells the story of the supplication in Book 15, when he sets out the whole plan to Hera. He will not cease from his anger until the wish of Achilles is fulfilled, “as I promised and nodded with my head, on the day when the goddess Thetis took my knees, begging me to honor Achilles, sacker of cities” (15.72–77). The last verse is Athena's (8.372 = 15.77), and Zeus says nothing of a kiss.
