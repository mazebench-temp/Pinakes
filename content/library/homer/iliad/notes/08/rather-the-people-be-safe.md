summary: Zeus grants Agamemnon, in almost the same words, what Agamemnon claimed to prefer in Book 1: when the king prays in tears at the ships, Zeus nods to him “that his people would be saved and not perish”.
refs: [8.246]
passages:

---

In Book 1 Agamemnon agreed to give back Chryseis with the line “I would rather the people be safe than perish” (βούλομʼ ἐγὼ λαὸν σῶν ἔμμεναι ἢ ἀπολέσθαι, 1.117), and demanded a new prize in the next breath. In Book 8 the people are perishing. Agamemnon shames the army, then prays to Zeus: he never passed an altar of Zeus without burning the fat and thighs of oxen, and he asks only that the Achaeans may escape and not be beaten down by the Trojans (8.236–244; see [[reciprocity-with-the-gods]]). The narrator gives Zeus' answer in the words of Agamemnon's old line: the father pitied him as he shed tears, “and nodded to him that his people would be saved and not perish” (νεῦσε δέ οἱ λαὸν σόον ἔμμεναι οὐδʼ ἀπολέσθαι, 8.245–246; see [[neuo]] and [[pity]]). The phrase is found only in these two verses. The king who once spoke of the people's safety as a preference now has it as a god's gift, and only for the day: the next morning Zeus will give Hector the battle again (8.470–477).
