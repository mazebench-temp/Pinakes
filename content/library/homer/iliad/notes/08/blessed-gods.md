---
summary: In verses not found in the manuscripts, “the blessed gods” take no share of the savor of the Trojans' sacrifice, because Ilios was hateful to them.
---

{{quote:18d63079-8ab9-5bd9-804b-224fb982d362}}

The phrase θεοὶ μάκαρες stands in one of the four verses (8.548, 8.550–552) that the manuscripts do not have. They are known from the Second Alcibiades ascribed to Plato (149d–e), which reports in indirect speech that the Trojans, as they made camp, offered perfect hecatombs, and that the winds carried the savor to heaven, but the gods took no share of it, because Ilios, Priam and his people were hateful to them. Editors print the verses in brackets ([[the-refused-sacrifice]], [[hecatomb]], [[sacrifice]]). The refusal of the blessed gods sits against Zeus' word in Book 4 that of all cities under the sky he honored sacred Ilios most, and that his altar there never lacked its share of the feast; 8.552 is 4.47 (4.44–49; [[hieros]], [[with-good-ash-spear]]). See [[olympians]].
