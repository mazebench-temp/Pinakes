---
summary: Gorgythion's head droops to one side like a poppy, “weighed down by his helmet”. When Athena arms in Book 8, the golden helmet of her arming in Book 5 is left out.
grc: [πήληκι]
---

Teucer's arrow strikes Gorgythion, son of Priam, in the chest, and his head falls like a poppy heavy with its seed and the spring rain, “so his head drooped to one side, weighed down by his helmet” (πήληκι βαρυνθέν, 8.306–308; see [[gorgythion]]). The helmet does to the dying man what the seed head does to the flower. πήληξ is the fourth of Homer's words for the helmet (see [[helmet]]).

Diomedes kills Agelaus, “a helmeted man of the Trojans” (κορυστήν, 8.256; see [[helmeted]]), and Hector is three times “of the gleaming helmet” (8.160, 8.324, 8.377; see [[of-the-gleaming-helmet]]). When Athena arms on Olympus, the verses of her arming in Book 5 are repeated without the aegis and the golden helmet “fitted with the foot soldiers of a hundred cities” (5.738–744; see [[arming-scene]]).
