---
summary: Idomeneus is named first among the leaders who run when Zeus thunders, and he comes out again with his squire Meriones among the nine who follow Diomedes across the ditch (8.78, 8.263–264).
---

“Then neither Idomeneus dared to stay, nor Agamemnon, nor did the two Ajaxes stay, attendants of Ares” (8.78–79; see [[agamemnon]] and [[ajax]]). τλῆ μίμνειν, “dared to stay”: the narrator does not soften the flight. Only Nestor is left, “not of his own will”, because Paris has shot his horse (8.80–86; see [[nestor]]).

When the eagle of Zeus has rallied the army, he comes out behind Diomedes with his squire, in the two verses that brought them to their feet for Hector's challenge: “and after them Idomeneus and Idomeneus' squire Meriones, the equal of Enyalius, slayer of men” (8.263–264 = 7.165–166; see [[meriones]]). He is sixth in the order, after the two Ajaxes. His great day is the battle at the ships in Book 13.
