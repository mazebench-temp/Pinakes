---
summary: Zeus who delights in thunder calls the gods to assembly at the start of Book 8, and his thunder and thunderbolt rule the day's battle.
grc: [τερπικέραυνος]
---

The first books named Zeus “who delights in thunder” only in the dative, Διὶ τερπικεραύνῳ (1.419, 2.478, 2.781). Book 8 opens with the nominative, in the verse of his assembly: “and Zeus who delights in thunder held an assembly of the gods on the topmost peak of many-ridged Olympus” (8.2–3; [[many-ridged-olympus]], [[assembly-type-scene]]). The epithet names the weapon with which he threatens the gods: whoever helps either side will be “struck in no seemly way” (8.12).

The day is his. He thunders loudly from Ida at noon and sends a blazing flash among the Achaeans (8.75–77), throws his thunderbolt in front of Diomedes' horses (8.133–136), thunders three times from the mountains of Ida (8.170–171), and threatens Hera and Athena with wounds from the thunderbolt that would not heal in ten years (8.402–405, 8.455–456; [[thunderbolt]], [[circling-years]]).
