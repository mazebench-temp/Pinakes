---
summary: In Book 8 the words of swiftness belong to horses, to Iris, “swift-footed Iris,” and to Achilles, named “the swift-footed son of Peleus” in Zeus' prophecy of the battle at the ships.
grc: [ὠκυπόδων, ποδώκεα]
except: [8.197]
---

**Horses.** The adjectives of speed run through the chariot fighting. “Hector's swift horses” carry him at Nestor (8.88), the “swift-footed horses” shy when Eniopeus and then Archeptolemus fall (8.122–123, 8.314–315), and Hector sets Archeptolemus behind “the swift-footed horses” (8.128–129). No Achaean could boast that he had held “his swift horses” ahead of Diomedes when he drove across the ditch (8.254). Zeus threatens to lame the “swift horses” of Hera and Athena (8.402, 8.416). In 8.197 the same adjective describes the Achaean ships, and that verse belongs to [[swift-ships]].

**Iris.** “So saying, swift-footed Iris went away” (8.425) ends her mission to the goddesses ([[swift-iris]], [[storm-footed]]).

**Achilles.** Achilles takes no part in Book 8, but Zeus names him in his prophecy: Hector will not stop “until the swift-footed son of Peleus is roused beside the ships” (8.474; [[son-of-peleus]], [[plan-of-zeus]]). The first epithet the book gives him is the one of his formula, πόδας ὠκὺς Ἀχιλλεύς, in the compound ποδώκης. See [[achilles]] and [[horses]].
