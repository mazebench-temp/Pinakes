---
summary: Zeus lets fly “a gleaming thunderbolt” in front of Diomedes' horses, and at the end of the book the Trojan horses munch “white barley.”
grc: [ἀργῆτα]
en: [gleaming]
---

**The thunderbolt.** “Thundering terribly, he let fly a gleaming thunderbolt and sent it down to the ground in front of Diomedes' horses” (8.133–134; [[thunderbolt]]). ἀργής is the white flash of the bolt, as it is the shining white of the robe in which Helen wraps herself (3.419); in the Odyssey the thunderbolt is ἀργής again (Od. 5.128, 7.249). A flame of burning sulfur rises, and the horses cower under the chariot (8.135–136).

**The barley.** The book ends with the Trojan horses “munching white barley and spelt” beside their chariots, in the verse that described Lycaon's horses in Book 5 (8.564 = 5.196; [[of-the-fair-throne]]). See [[zeus]].
