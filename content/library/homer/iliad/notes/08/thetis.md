Thetis does not appear in Book 8, but the day is hers. Athena names her as the cause of her father's hostility:

{{quote:20548812-4ac3-5546-8f95-aa59c171db4b}}

Θέτιδος … βουλάς, "the plans of Thetis": the plan the proem calls the plan of Zeus (1.5) is, in Athena's angry words, Thetis' plan, which Zeus has carried out (see [[plan-of-zeus]], [[boule]] and [[thetis-supplicates-zeus]]). Her account of the supplication adds a kiss. In Book 1 Thetis clasped Zeus' knees with her left hand and took him under the chin with her right (1.500–502, 1.512), and no kiss is mentioned (see the translation's note on 8.371 and [[supplication]]). Priam kisses the hands of Achilles when he begs for Hector's body (24.478). "Begging him to honor Achilles, sacker of cities" (8.372) puts into a report what Thetis asked in Book 1, "honor my son" (1.505), and Zeus uses the same verse when he recalls the day "when the goddess Thetis touched my knees, / begging me to honor Achilles, sacker of cities" (15.76–77). That evening Zeus foretells what the plan will cost: Hector will not stop fighting until Achilles is roused beside the ships, over the dead Patroclus (8.473–476; see [[zeus]] and [[achilles]]).
