**Fire.** Hector's speech to the Trojans ends with the ships: “But when I come among the hollow ships, then let there be some remembrance of consuming fire, so that I may set fire to the ships and kill the men themselves, the Argives, beside the ships, dazed by the smoke” (8.180–183; see [[hollow-ships]]). His hope is that the Achaeans will “board their swift ships this very night” (8.197). Agamemnon fears it, Hector “will soon burn the ships with blazing fire” (8.235), and the narrator brings it to the edge: “And now he would have burned the balanced ships with blazing fire, had not queen Hera put it in Agamemnon's mind” (8.217–218; see [[would-have-had-not]] and [[balanced-ships]]). The fire is put off to Book 16, when the Trojans throw fire on the ship of Protesilaus, whose stern Hector had seized (15.704–706, 16.112–124).

The verses give the layout of the camp for the first time, with Odysseus' ship, “huge in the hull”, in the middle and Ajax and Achilles at the two ends (see [[huge-hulled]]), and they return when Strife shouts from the same ship at the start of Book 11 (11.5–9). The verses 8.224–226 are reported to have been rejected here in antiquity (see the translation's note on 8.222–226). The camp's altars and its place of assembly were by Odysseus' ships (11.806–808; see [[altar]]).

**The sterns.** Zeus foretells that Hector will not stop fighting “until the swift-footed son of Peleus is roused beside the ships, on the day when they fight at the sterns of the ships in the direst strait, over Patroclus dead” (8.474–476; see [[stern]] and [[plan-of-zeus]]).

**Flight by sea.** At nightfall Hector fears that the Achaeans will take to their ships. The fires must burn all night, “in case even by night the long-haired Achaeans set out to flee over the broad back of the sea. Let them not board their ships at their ease, without a struggle” (8.510–512; see [[night-watch]] and [[broad-back-of-the-sea]]). The Trojans camp “between the ships and the streams of the Xanthus” (8.560).

**Agamemnon's voyage.** In his prayer Agamemnon recalls sailing to Troy “in my many-benched ship on my ill-starred way here” (8.239; see [[many-benched]]).
