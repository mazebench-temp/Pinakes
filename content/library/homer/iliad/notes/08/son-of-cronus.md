---
summary: Every speaker of Book 8 names Zeus by his father, from Athena's “Our father, son of Cronus” at dawn to Zeus' own “the son of Cronus, supreme in might” at nightfall.
---

The patronymic passes from mouth to mouth through the day. Athena: “Our father, son of Cronus, highest of lords” (8.31; [[most-high]]). Nestor, to Diomedes: “For now Zeus, son of Cronus, grants glory to this man today” (8.141–142; [[kydos]], [[victory-shifts]]). Hector, to the Trojans: “I know that the son of Cronus has readily nodded his promise to me of victory and great glory” (8.175–176; [[neuo]], [[nike]]). Poseidon, to Hera: “I would not wish that we should fight against Zeus, son of Cronus” (8.210; [[reckless-in-speech]]). Iris, to the goddesses: “The son of Cronus will not let you help the Argives,” and the threat is that of “the son of Cronus,” Κρόνου πάϊς (8.414–415). Hera, in protest: “Most dread son of Cronus, what a thing you have said!” (8.462; [[most-dread]]). Zeus, of himself: “At dawn you will see the son of Cronus, supreme in might” (8.470; [[supreme-in-might]]).

At the end of that speech Zeus names the father himself: Cronus sits with Iapetus at the lowest limits of earth and sea, with deep Tartarus around them (8.478–481; [[cronus]], [[iapetus]], [[fall-of-cronus]]). The god whose name the patronymic carries is the god his son put below the earth. See [[zeus]].
