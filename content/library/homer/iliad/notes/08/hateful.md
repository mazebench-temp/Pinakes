---
summary: Eurystheus sent Heracles for “the hound of hateful Hades”; in three verses Athena's speech has three words of the root of Styx.
---

“To bring from Erebus the hound of hateful Hades, he would never have escaped the sheer streams of the water of Styx. But now he hates me” (8.368–370). στυγερός, “hateful,” has the root of Styx ([[styx]]), and Athena's speech uses it three times in three verses: the hateful god (στυγεροῦ), the river (Στυγός), and Zeus, who now hates her (στυγέει; [[hard]]). Hades is the god most hateful to mortals because he cannot be moved, Agamemnon says in Book 9 (9.158–159; [[hades]]). Hector uses the verb of the Achaeans: wounded as they flee, so that others too may shrink from war with the Trojans (στυγέῃσι, 8.515; [[tearful]]). See [[heracles-and-cerberus]] and [[gatekeeper]].
