---
summary: “Queen Hera” is indignant at Hector's boast, puts it in Agamemnon's mind to rally the Achaeans, and is “ox-eyed queen Hera” in Zeus' prophecy.
---

**Indignation.** Hector ends his speech to his horses with the boast that he will make the Achaeans board their ships that night, “So he spoke, boasting, and queen Hera was indignant” (8.198; [[nemesis]]). She shakes on her throne, so that Olympus quakes, and tries to stir Poseidon against Zeus (8.199–211; [[reckless-in-speech]]).

**Action.** Poseidon refuses, and she acts through a man instead: Hector would have burned the ships, “had not queen Hera put it in Agamemnon's mind to bestir himself and quickly urge on the Achaeans” (8.217–219; [[would-have-had-not]], [[blazing-fire]]). It is the one help she manages to give this day.

**The prophecy.** At night Zeus addresses her as “ox-eyed queen Hera” in his answer to her protest (8.471; [[ox-eyed]], [[plan-of-zeus]]). See [[hera]].
