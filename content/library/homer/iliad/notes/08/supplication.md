Athena, angry that Zeus is keeping her from the war, says that her father has forgotten her help to Heracles and remembers only Thetis:

{{quote:20548812-4ac3-5546-8f95-aa59c171db4b}}

In Book 1 Thetis sat before Zeus, took his knees with her left hand and his chin with her right, and held on to his knees when he sat silent (1.500–502, 1.512–513; see [[thetis-supplicates-zeus]]). Athena tells the scene with a kiss, which Book 1 does not mention (see the translation's note on 8.371). To kiss the knees is a suppliant's act elsewhere too: Odysseus, in his Cretan tale, says he took the knees of the king of Egypt and kissed them, and the king spared him and pitied him (Od. 14.279), and Priam kisses the hands of Achilles (24.478). The chin is the other point of contact: Dolon, in the night raid, is reaching to touch Diomedes' chin when Diomedes kills him (10.454–456). Athena's λισσομένη, “begging” (8.372), is the word of Thetis' plea in Book 1 (1.502).

Athena speaks of the supplication from the other side, as the ritual that has bound her father against her: “But now he hates me, and has fulfilled the plans of Thetis” (8.370). She is sure he will relent, “Yet the day will come when he calls me his dear gray-eyed one again” (8.373; see [[athena]] and [[zeus]]).
