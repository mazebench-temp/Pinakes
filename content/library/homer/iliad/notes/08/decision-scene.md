---
summary: Diomedes, retreating at Nestor's word, is torn whether to turn and fight Hector; three times he ponders, and three times Zeus thunders from Ida, and the god's sign decides.
refs: [8.167-171]
passages:
  - 8.161-171 | Diomedes torn two ways
---

The scene has the usual parts (see [[decision-scene]]), and a god in place of the resolution.

**Shock.** Hector shouts after the retreating chariot that the Danaans will dishonor Diomedes, “you are no better than a woman after all. Off with you, wretched doll!” (8.163–164; see [[hector]] and [[wretched-doll]]).

**Division.** “So he spoke, and the son of Tydeus was torn two ways in thought, whether to turn his horses and fight him face to face” (8.167–168; see [[pondering-two-ways]]). Only one alternative is spoken; the other is what he is already doing.

**Resolution.** “Three times he pondered in his mind and in his heart, and three times Zeus the counselor thundered from the mountains of Ida, giving the Trojans a sign of the victory that turns the battle” (8.169–171; see [[teras]]). Each time the man's mind turns toward the fight, the god's sign turns it back, and the next voice is Hector's, exhorting the Trojans (8.172).

The choice was argued before the taunt. Nestor told Diomedes that no man can hold back the mind of Zeus (8.139–144), and Diomedes answered that he could not bear what Hector would say of him (8.146–150; see [[tis-speech]]). The deliberation is that argument again, and the thunder ends it.
