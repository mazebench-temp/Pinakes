---
summary: The horses of Tros, which Diomedes took from Aeneas in Book 5, carry Diomedes and Nestor against Hector, until Zeus' thunderbolt falls in front of them (8.105–136).
passages:
  - 8.105-108 | The horses of Tros
---

Τρώϊοι ἵπποι, “the horses of Tros” (8.106), are the breed Zeus gave Tros in return for his son Ganymede, the best horses under the sun, from which Anchises bred in secret (5.265–272; see [[ganymede-and-the-horses-of-tros]] and [[anchises-steals-the-horses]]). Anchises gave two of them to Aeneas, and Diomedes took them in Book 5 (5.319–327; see [[aeneas]]). Diomedes praises them to Nestor in the words Aeneas used to Pandarus: they know the plain, “how to pursue and to flee very swiftly this way and that” (8.106–107 = 5.222–223; see [[diomedes]] and [[nestor]]). With Nestor driving, they bring Diomedes close to Hector (8.115–117). When Zeus' thunderbolt falls before them, “the two horses in terror cowered beneath the chariot” (8.136), and Nestor turns them to flight (8.157). In the chariot race Diomedes drives them again, “the horses of Tros”, and wins (23.290–292, 23.377–378, 23.499–513; see [[horses]]).
