---
summary: Poseidon is “the famous shaker of the earth,” κλυτὸς ἐννοσίγαιος, as he unyokes Zeus' horses on Olympus.
grc: [κλυτὸς ἐννοσίγαιος]
---

κλυτός is the simplest member of the family of κλέος, “fame” ([[kleos]]): formed from κλύω, “hear,” it is what is heard of. The line-end κλυτὸς ἐννοσίγαιος, “the famous shaker of the earth,” is one of Poseidon's formulas in both poems (8.440, 9.362, 14.135, 14.510, 15.184; Od. 5.423, 6.326; [[shaker-of-the-earth]]). Here it stands in a verse of service: “For him the famous shaker of the earth unyoked the horses” (8.440), when Zeus has driven home from Ida (8.438–441; [[well-wheeled]]). The famous god tends his brother's team. See [[poseidon]].
