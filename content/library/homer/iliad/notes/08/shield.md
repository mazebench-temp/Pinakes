**The clash.** The armies meet, and “they dashed together their shields of hide, and their spears, and the fury of men in bronze corselets; and the bossed shields pressed against one another” (8.61–63), in the verses of the first battle (4.447–449; see [[the-armies-clash]] and [[bossed]]).

**Nestor's shield.** Hector urges his horses on to take two pieces of famous armor, Diomedes' corselet and “Nestor's shield, whose fame now reaches the sky, that it is all of gold, its rods and the shield itself” (8.192–193; see [[corselet]] and [[stripping-the-dead]]). The shield is named nowhere else, and the poem does not say how its fame was won. κανόνες are the rods fixed across the inside of a shield, by which it was held: Idomeneus' round shield is “fitted with two rods” (13.407), as the translation's note on 8.192–193 explains. Its fame, κλέος οὐρανὸν ἵκει, “reaches the sky”, as Odysseus says of his own fame when he names himself to the Phaeacians (Od. 9.20; see [[kleos]] and [[gold-and-silver]]).

**Teucer's cover.** Teucer comes ninth across the ditch and takes his stand “under the shield of Ajax, son of Telamon”, shooting when Ajax moves the shield aside and slipping back under it after every shot (8.266–272; see [[the-shield-of-ajax]] and [[teucer]]). When Hector's stone fells him, Ajax runs and stands over him “and covered him with his shield” (8.330–331).

**Behind the back.** Diomedes asks Odysseus where he is fleeing, μετὰ νῶτα βαλών, rendered “turning your back” (8.94). The words can also be read with “your shield” understood, “throwing it behind your back”, as Ajax swings his great shield behind him when he retreats (11.545; see the translation's note on 8.94).
