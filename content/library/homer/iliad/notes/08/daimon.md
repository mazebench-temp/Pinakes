summary: Hector ends his taunt of the retreating Diomedes with a threat found only here, πάρος τοι δαίμονα δώσω, “before that I will deal you your doom”: the δαίμων as the power that deals a man his lot, here his death, in the hand of a man.
grc: [δαίμονα]
en: [doom]
passages:
  - 8.160-166 | Hector taunts Diomedes
---

Hector shouts after Diomedes, whom Nestor has turned to flight, that it will not be through his giving way that Diomedes climbs the towers of Troy or carries off its women in his ships: “before that I will deal you your doom” (πάρος τοι δαίμονα δώσω, 8.166). The phrase occurs only here. δαίμων is a divine power, often one that is not named, and also what such a power allots to a man, his lot or fortune; here the lot is death (see the translation's note on 8.166). On the first day of battle, at the end of his duel with Ajax, Hector left the outcome of the war to a δαίμων, “until a god decides between us and gives victory to one side or the other” (7.291–292; see the note on Book 7). Now, with Zeus' thunder behind him, he speaks as if he were the power that deals the lot. The threat is not carried out. Three times Diomedes ponders whether to turn and fight him, and three times Zeus thunders from Ida (8.167–171); and the next day it is Diomedes' spear that strikes Hector's helmet and leaves him in a faint, until he recovers and escapes (11.349–360; see [[diomedes]] and [[hector]]).
