The verbs are iterative (ὑπεξέφερεν, δύσκεν, κρύπτασκε): Ajax moves the shield aside, Teucer looks about and shoots, and slips back under it, again and again, until eight men are down (8.273–277; see [[teucer]]). The shield that held against Hector's spear and stone in the duel of Book 7 (7.244–248, 7.264–267) is now cover for an archer, as Pandarus shot at Menelaus from behind the shields his companions held up (4.113–115; see [[bow]]). The simile of the child under its mother makes the warrior the shelter and the archer the child.

The four verses 8.331–334 return in Book 13, when Antilochus covers the fallen Hypsenor and the same two companions, Mecisteus and Alastor, carry him to the ships (13.420–423; see [[mecisteus-son-of-echius]]).
