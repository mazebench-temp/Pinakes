**Two sons.** Book 8 gives Telamon his second son. The patronymic Τελαμωνιάδης, “son of Telamon”, appears here for the first time, of Ajax (8.224, 8.267; see [[ajax]]), and a few lines later Agamemnon gives Ajax's own title to his half-brother: Τελαμώνιε κοίρανε λαῶν, “son of Telamon, lord of the people” (8.281; compare 7.234; see [[teucer]] and [[telamonian]]). Later books call Teucer Τελαμώνιος too (13.170, 15.462).

**The father far away.** Agamemnon makes the absent father the measure of the son. Telamon reared Teucer “when you were small” and cared for him in his own house, though his mother was not Telamon's wife (8.283–284; see [[nothos]]), and the son can repay him with glory that will reach him “far away though he is” (8.285). The Iliad's fathers stay at home and send their sons out with charges: Hippolochus and Peleus told Glaucus and Achilles “always to be the best” (6.208, 11.783–784; see [[always-to-be-the-best]]), and Menoetius told Patroclus to give Achilles good counsel (11.785–789). Nestor imagines old Peleus hearing that the Achaeans cower before Hector (7.124–131; see [[peleus]] and [[fathers-and-sons]]). Telamon is to hear of a bastard son's arrows.
