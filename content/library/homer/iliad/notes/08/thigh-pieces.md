---
summary: Agamemnon reminds Zeus that on every altar he passed on the way to Troy he “burned the fat and the thighs of oxen”.
grc: ["μηρίʼ ἔκηα"]
en: [thighs]
---

Agamemnon's prayer claims Zeus' help on the strength of his offerings: “on every one I burned the fat and the thighs of oxen, eager to sack well-walled Troy” (βοῶν δημὸν καὶ μηρίʼ ἔκηα, 8.240–241; see [[prayer]] and [[sacrifice]]). The half-verse μηρίʼ ἔκηα is Chryses' in the first prayer of the poem, “or if ever I burned for you the fat thigh-pieces” (1.40). In the rite the thigh-bones are wrapped in a double fold of fat and burned (1.460–461; see [[sacrifice-type-scene]]); δημός, “fat”, is Agamemnon's word for it.
