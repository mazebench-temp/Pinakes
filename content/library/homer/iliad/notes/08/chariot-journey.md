summary: Zeus drives his golden-maned horses from Olympus to Gargarus on Ida and sits there watching, and drives back at evening, when Poseidon unyokes his horses for him. Between, Hera and Athena set out in Hera's chariot in the verses of Book 5 and are turned back at the gates of heaven, where the Hours unyoke their horses.

**To Ida.** Zeus' drive has every step of the pattern (see [[chariot-journey]]). He yokes his bronze-footed horses with golden manes, puts on gold, and takes a golden whip (8.41–44 = 13.23–26, Poseidon's yoking; see [[bronze-footed]] and [[arming-scene]]). He whips them on, “and the pair flew on not unwilling between earth and the starry sky” (8.45–46; the verses of 5.366 and 5.769), and comes to Gargarus, where he has a precinct and an altar (8.47–48; see [[gargarus]] and [[altar]]). He halts the horses, unyokes them and pours a thick mist around them (8.49–50), as Hera did at the meeting of the rivers (5.775–776). Then the god takes his seat:

The journey ends in a place to watch from (see [[watching-the-war]] and [[exulting-in-glory]]).

**The goddesses.** Hera harnesses her horses with golden headbands and Athena arms and mounts (8.381–391); Hera touches the horses with the whip, and the gates of heaven, kept by the Hours, groan open of themselves (8.392–396 = 5.748–752; see [[hours]]). In Book 5 the same journey went on to the rivers of Troy. Here Zeus sees them from Ida, and Iris meets them “at the outermost gates of many-folded Olympus” (8.411; see [[messenger-scene]] and [[many-folded]]). Hera turns the horses back (8.432), and the Hours take them in:

{{quote:6a4f6ab0-5181-529c-998e-e509be5ffdac}}

The stabling is told almost in the verses of a mortal arrival, when the horses of Telemachus and Peisistratus are unyoked at Menelaus' palace, tied at their mangers, and the chariot leaned against the gleaming walls (8.434–435 ≈ Od. 4.40, 4.42). The Hours, keepers of the gates, serve as the goddesses' grooms.

**Back from Ida.** “But father Zeus drove his well-wheeled chariot and horses from Ida to Olympus, and came to the seats of the gods” (8.438–439; see [[well-wheeled]]). Poseidon, who would not join Hera against Zeus (8.208–211), now serves him as groom: “For him the famous shaker of the earth unyoked the horses, and set the chariot on its stand and spread a cloth over it” (8.440–441; see [[chariot]] and [[poseidon]]). Zeus sits on his golden throne, and Olympus shakes beneath his feet (8.442–443; see [[throne]]).
