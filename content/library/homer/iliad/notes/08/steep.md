“He would never have escaped the sheer streams of the water of Styx” (8.369; [[styx]], [[shrewd-mind]], [[gatekeeper]]). The neuter plural of αἰπύς describes the steep fall of water: the same phrase is the roaring stream of the Xanthus, into which the Trojans plunge as they flee from Achilles (21.8–9). The water of Styx is the one by which the gods swear their greatest oath (15.36–38), and here it stands for the world below, from which Heracles came back with the hound of Hades ([[heracles-and-cerberus]]).
