---
summary: Hector leaps down from his “all-gleaming chariot” to strike Teucer with a stone, and the Hours lean the goddesses' chariot against “the gleaming walls” of Olympus.
grc: [παμφανόωντος]
en: [all-gleaming]
---

**Hector's chariot.** Teucer has killed Archeptolemus, and Cebriones has taken the reins (8.309–319). “And he himself leapt to the ground from the all-gleaming chariot, shouting terribly; and he took up a stone in his hand” (8.320–321; [[jagged]], [[cebriones]]). The bright chariot is left behind as Hector goes on foot against the archer, and the stone breaks Teucer's bowstring (8.322–329; [[teucer]]).

**The gods' walls.** When Iris has turned Hera and Athena back, the Hours unyoke their horses and lean the chariot “against the gleaming walls” (πρὸς ἐνώπια παμφανόωντα, 8.435; [[ambrosial]], [[hours]]). The same phrase describes the walls of Idomeneus' hut, against which captured spears stand (13.260–261). See [[chariot]].
