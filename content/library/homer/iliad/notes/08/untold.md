---
summary: In the simile of the watch-fires “the boundless upper air breaks open” and all the stars are seen.
grc: [ἄσπετος]
en: [boundless]
---

The stars shine out around the moon on a windless night, the peaks and headlands and glens stand out, “and from the sky the boundless upper air breaks open, and all the stars are seen, and the shepherd is glad at heart” (8.558–559; [[preeminent]]). The verses about the headlands and the opened sky (8.557–558) are the ones in which, in Book 16, Zeus clears a thick cloud from a mountain peak (16.297–300), and they are reported to have been rejected here in antiquity. ἄσπετος, “beyond telling,” measures the upper air, as it measures the din of the Trojans when they find the dead of the night raid (10.523).
