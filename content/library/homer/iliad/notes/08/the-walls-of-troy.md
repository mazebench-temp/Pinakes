**Our towers.** Hector's taunt makes the wall the limit of Diomedes' hopes: “It will not be through my giving way that you climb our towers, or carry off our women in your ships” (8.164–166; see [[hector]] and [[diomedes]]). To climb the towers is to take the city, and the women go with it.

**The god-built towers.** At nightfall, with the army camped on the plain, the city is left to those too young or too old to fight, “boys in their first youth and gray-templed old men” (8.518; see [[gray-templed]]). θεόδμητοι, “god-built” (8.519; see [[god-built]]), occurs only here in Homer, and it agrees with Poseidon's claim that he and Apollo built the wall of Troy for Laomedon (7.452–453; 21.446–447; see [[poseidon-and-apollo-serve-laomedon]]). λέξασθαι is taken by the translation as “spend the night”, from the root of λέχος, “bed”; it can also be read as “be mustered”, from λέγω, as at 2.125. The old men's place on the wall is where Priam and the elders of Troy sat to watch the war in Book 3 (3.146–153; see [[trojan-elders]]). The women are to keep fires burning in their halls, and a watch is to guard the city against an ambush while the army is away (8.520–522; see [[ambush]]).
