summary: The wall and ditch built the night before face their first battle. Hector scorns them as “feeble things, worth nothing”; twice the Achaeans are driven back inside the ditch and penned there; Diomedes leads the rally out across it; and at nightfall Hector, camped on the plain, waits to see whether Diomedes will push him back “from the ships to the wall” (8.177–179, 8.213–216, 8.253–255, 8.335–345, 8.532–533).

**Hector's scorn.** On the day after it is built, Hector tells the Trojans what he thinks of the wall:

{{quote:4e53e53b-2f75-52f3-b3f6-52647836fda1}}

ἀβλήχρʼ οὐδενόσωρα, “feeble things, worth nothing”: οὐδενόσωρος occurs only here (see the translation's note on 8.178). The ditch is τάφρος ὀρυκτή, “the ditch they have dug” (8.179; see [[dug]]). Hector speaks as the man to whom Zeus “has readily nodded his promise … of victory and great glory” (8.175–176), and he looks past the wall to the ships he means to burn (8.180–183; see [[hector]]). He is wrong about the horses. When the Trojans reach the ditch in Book 12, their horses stand neighing at its edge, for it is not easy to leap over (οὔτʼ … ὑπερθορέειν, 12.53; ὑπερθορέονται, 8.179), and Polydamas persuades Hector to leave the chariots and attack on foot (12.49–87).

**Penned inside the ditch.** By the afternoon “all the space that the ditch enclosed, out from the ships beyond the wall, was filled alike with horses and shield-bearing men hemmed in” (8.213–215). The compressed Greek may mean the strip between the ditch and the wall, or the whole ground from the ships out to the ditch (see the translation's note on 8.213–214). ἀπὸ πύργου, literally “from the tower”, stands for the wall, which was built as “high towers” (7.338, 7.437). Hector would have burned the ships then, had Hera not roused Agamemnon to rally the army (8.217–219; see [[agamemnon]]).

**Out over the ditch.** When the eagle of Zeus has put heart into them, the Achaeans come out again, and none of them could boast “that he had held his swift horses ahead of the son of Tydeus, driven them out over the ditch, and fought face to face” (8.254–255; see [[diomedes]] and [[agelaus]]). The other leaders follow him out, and Teucer shoots from under the shield of Ajax (8.261–334; see [[teucer]]).

**Back again.** Zeus rouses the Trojans once more, and they drive the Achaeans “straight back to the deep ditch”, Hector at their heels like a hound at a boar's (8.335–342; see [[the-hound-at-the-heels]]). “But when they had crossed the stakes and the ditch in their flight, and many had been brought down at the hands of the Trojans, they halted beside the ships and stood their ground” (8.343–345). The stakes that the builders set in the ditch (7.441) are named here for the first time in battle. Outside, Hector wheels his horses this way and that (8.348–349).

**Night.** At sunset Hector leads the Trojans “away from the ships” to camp on the plain (8.490), and he promises to find out at dawn “whether the son of Tydeus, mighty Diomedes, will push me back from the ships to the wall” (8.532–533). The Achaean camp is now besieged, and that night Nestor posts sentries between the ditch and the wall (9.66–67, 9.80–88).
