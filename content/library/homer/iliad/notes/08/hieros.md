---
summary: The battle goes on while “the sacred day was growing,” and in verses not found in the manuscripts the gods refuse the Trojans' sacrifice because “sacred Ilios was hateful to them.”
---

**The sacred day.** “As long as it was morning and the sacred day was growing, so long the missiles of both sides struck home, and the people fell” (8.66–67). The two verses return at the start of the battle in Book 11 (11.84–85), and the first in the Odyssey (Od. 9.56). The day is sacred because it comes from the gods.

**Sacred Ilios.** In the verses that are not in the manuscripts but are known from the Second Alcibiades (149d), the winds carry the savor of the Trojans' sacrifice to heaven, but the gods take no share of it, “for sacred Ilios was hateful to them” (8.551; [[blessed-gods]], [[the-refused-sacrifice]]). The formula of the city's holiness stands in the verse of the gods' hatred. In Book 4 Zeus said that of all cities he honored sacred Ilios most (4.44–49). See [[troy]].
