---
summary: The epithet follows the horses of Book 8 to the end of their day: Hector's team as he wheels before the ships, the goddesses' team unyoked by the Hours, and the Trojans' teams unyoked on the plain.
---

“And Hector wheeled his fine-maned horses this way and that” (8.348) at the height of his advance, before the Achaean ships ([[ruin-of-mortals]]). When Iris has turned Hera and Athena back, “The Hours unyoked their fine-maned horses for them” (8.433; [[ambrosial]], [[hours]]). At nightfall Hector orders the Trojans to “unyoke the fine-maned horses from the chariots, and set fodder beside them” (8.503–504), and they do it, tying them beside their chariots (8.543–544). The book ends with the horses standing there, munching barley and spelt, waiting for the dawn (8.564–565; [[of-the-fair-throne]]). See [[horses]].
