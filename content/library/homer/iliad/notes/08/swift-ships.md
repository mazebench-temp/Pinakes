---
summary: Hector hopes to make the Achaeans board their “swift ships” that very night; here the epithet is ὠκύς, not the usual θοός.
grc: [νηῶν ἐπιβησέμεν ὠκειάων]
---

“If we could take these two, I would hope to make the Achaeans board their swift ships this very night” (8.196–197). The ships are swift with the adjective ὠκύς, which elsewhere in the book describes horses ([[swift-footed]], which keeps the word's other uses), in a phrase that sets the noun at one end of the verse and the epithet at the other. Hector's hope is that the Achaeans will flee by sea; at night he lights fires on the plain so that they cannot do it unseen (8.508–511; [[broad-back-of-the-sea]]). Hera, hearing his boast, is indignant (8.198; [[queenly]]). See [[ships]].
