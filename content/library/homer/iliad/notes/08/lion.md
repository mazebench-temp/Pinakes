---
summary: The only lion of Book 8 is a hunted one. Hector harries the retreating Achaeans as a hound catches at a wild boar or a lion from behind and watches for it as it wheels (8.338–342).
grc: [λέοντος]
---

ὡς δʼ ὅτε τίς τε κύων συὸς ἀγρίου ἠὲ λέοντος / ἅπτηται κατόπισθε (8.338–339): the lion is the quarry, and the hound that bites at its hindquarters is Hector (see [[the-hound-at-the-heels]] and [[dogs]]). In the similes of Book 5 the Achaean champions were lions and boars, the beasts that attack or stand at bay (5.782–783; see [[lions-and-boars]]). Here the same beasts are running away, and the dog that would never face them from the front keeps at their heels. They are still dangerous, and the hound watches for the turn, which comes only when the Achaeans halt at their ships (8.343–347).

Elsewhere the lion of the similes is the attacker, the picture of a warrior on the charge (see [[lion]]). In Book 12 the roles are turned back: Hector himself is the boar or lion that wheels among dogs and huntsmen, exulting in his strength (12.41–49; see [[boar]]).
