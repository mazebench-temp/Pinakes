---
summary: “Diomedes, good at the war cry,” sees Nestor's danger in time, and answers Nestor's counsel to flee with his fear of Hector's boast.
---

“Now the old man would have lost his life there, had not Diomedes, good at the war cry, been quick to see it” (8.90–91; [[would-have-had-not]], [[divine-rescue]]). He shouts to Odysseus for help, and when Odysseus runs past, he takes Nestor into his own chariot (8.92–115; [[much-enduring]]). The second use introduces his answer to Nestor: “Then Diomedes, good at the war cry, answered him” (8.145), and he speaks of the grief that Hector's boast will cause him (8.146–150; [[wide-earth]]). The warrior named for his shout rescues the old man and then is forced to retreat. See [[diomedes]].
