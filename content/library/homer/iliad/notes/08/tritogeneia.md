---
summary: Zeus answers Athena's plea with “Take heart, Tritogeneia, dear child,” the verses with which he will release her to go down against Hector in Book 22.
---

{{quote:b95f7f35-63a2-5e09-bd85-150cb5c7fefd}}

The title comes in Zeus' gentlest words of the day, after he has threatened to throw any god who disobeys him into Tartarus (8.10–17; [[cloud-gatherer]], [[smiling-at-him]]). It had named Athena once before, as she went through the Achaean ranks urging on the men she saw hanging back (4.514–516). The two verses of Zeus' answer return in Book 22, when Athena protests his wish to save Hector, and he tells her to do as she intends (22.182–185). The thirteen verses 8.28–40 are made of lines found elsewhere in the poem, and they are reported to have been rejected in antiquity. See [[athena]] and [[zeus]].
