---
summary: Ajax hides Teucer “with his shining shield,” and the stars shine around “the shining moon” in the simile of the watch-fires.
---

**The shield.** Teucer shoots from behind Ajax's shield, and when he has hit his man, “he would go back again, like a child under its mother, and slip to Ajax, who would hide him with his shining shield” (8.271–272; [[the-shield-of-ajax]], [[back-bent]]). The shield's brightness is the brightness of polished bronze, as with the shining shields and spears of other books.

**The moon.** At night the Trojan fires are compared to the stars that shine out “around the shining moon” on a windless night (8.555; [[preeminent]], [[untold]]). The epithet of polished bronze becomes the epithet of the moon's light. See [[shield]].
