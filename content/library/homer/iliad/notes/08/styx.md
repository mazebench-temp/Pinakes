---
summary: Athena says that without her Heracles would never have escaped "the sheer streams of the water of Styx" when Eurystheus sent him down to bring back the hound of Hades.
---

Athena ends the story of Heracles' descent with the river: "If I had known all this in my shrewd mind … he would never have escaped the sheer streams of the water of Styx" (8.366–369; see [[heracles]], [[heracles-and-cerberus]] and [[athena]]). Στυγὸς ὕδατος αἰπὰ ῥέεθρα: αἰπύς is "steep, sheer", and the same phrase is used of the Xanthus' streams roaring as the Trojans fall into them (αἰπὰ ῥέεθρα, 21.9). In Hesiod the water of Styx falls from a high, sheer rock (*Theogony* 785–787). Here the Styx is not the gods' oath but a river of the world below, which a living man must get past to come back. To escape its streams is to come back from the dead, as Heracles did (see [[hades]]). The next verse in Athena's speech uses the word from which the river takes its name: Zeus "hates" her now (στυγέει, 8.370).
