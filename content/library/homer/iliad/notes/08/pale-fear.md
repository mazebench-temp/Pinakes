---
summary: At noon Zeus thunders from Ida and sends his lightning among the Achaeans, and “pale fear took hold of them all.”
---

“And he himself thundered loudly from Ida, and sent a blazing flash among the army of the Achaeans; and when they saw it they were amazed, and pale fear took hold of them all” (8.75–77). The night before, Zeus had thundered over the Achaeans' feast, and pale fear had seized them then (7.478–479; [[deos]]). Now the scales have tilted against them (8.69–74; [[the-scales-of-zeus]]), and the fear is followed by flight: neither Idomeneus nor Agamemnon nor the two Ajaxes stand their ground (8.78–80; [[attendants-of-ares]], [[guardian-of-the-achaeans]]).
