---
summary: Hector is “son of Priam” as he hems the Achaeans in, in Hera's complaint and in Athena's challenge; Gorgythion, killed by Teucer's arrow, is “the brave son of Priam.”
grc: [Πριάμοιο πάϊς, υἱὸν ἐῢν Πριάμοιο]
---

**Hector.** The patronymic names him at the height of his day: “the one who hemmed them in, the equal of swift Ares, was Hector, son of Priam, now that Zeus had given him glory” (8.215–216; [[equal-of-ares]]). Hera uses it in her complaint to Athena, “he rages past all bearing, Hector, son of Priam, and has done much harm” (8.355–356), and Athena in her reply, in the longer phrase Πριάμοιο πάϊς: she wants to see “whether the son of Priam, Hector of the gleaming helmet, will be glad when we two appear along the bridges of war” (8.377–378; [[of-the-gleaming-helmet]]).

**Gorgythion.** The arrow that Teucer meant for Hector strikes another of Priam's sons, “noble Gorgythion, the brave son of Priam” (8.302–303; [[gorgythion]], [[brave]], [[blameless]]), born to Priam by Castianeira of Aesyme (8.304–305; [[like-the-goddesses]]). Later in the day Hector gives the reins to his brother Cebriones, a bastard son of Priam (8.318–319; [[cebriones]]). See [[priam]] and [[hector]].
