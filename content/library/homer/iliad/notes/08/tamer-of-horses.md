---
summary: The Trojans are “horse-taming” in the scales, in Diomedes' challenge and in Hector's night orders; Hector gives the epithet to Diomedes, whose corselet he wants.
grc: [ἱπποδάμοισι]
---

**The Trojans.** Zeus weighs the fates “of the horse-taming Trojans and the bronze-armored Achaeans” (8.71; [[bronze-armored]]). Diomedes, with Nestor in his chariot, means to drive the horses of Tros “straight at the horse-taming Trojans” (8.110; [[tros]]). In his night speech Hector uses the epithet twice of his own people: let the wounded Achaeans teach others to shrink “from bringing tearful Ares against the horse-taming Trojans” (8.516; [[tearful]]), and he will speak again at dawn “among the horse-taming Trojans” (8.525).

**Diomedes.** Hector, urging his horses on, names the two prizes he wants: Nestor's golden shield, and “from the shoulders of Diomedes, tamer of horses, the intricately worked corselet that Hephaestus toiled to make” (8.194–195; [[intricately-worked]]). The Trojans' standing epithet is Diomedes' too: he is the man who took the horses of Aeneas (5.319–327, 8.105–108; [[master-of-rout]]). See [[trojans]] and [[horses]].
