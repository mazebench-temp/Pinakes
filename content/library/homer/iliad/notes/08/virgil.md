---
summary: The Aeneid takes from Book 8 the scales of Jupiter, which weigh the fates of Aeneas and Turnus, and Gorgythion's drooping poppy, which it gives to the dying Euryalus. Its Tartarus is twice as deep as Homer's, and Pallas' warhorse bears the name of one of Hector's horses.
---

- Jupiter's scales (*Aeneid* 12.725–727): [[the-weighing-of-souls]].
- Euryalus' poppies (*Aeneid* 9.433–437): [[the-poppy-in-later-poets]].
- **Tartarus.** Homer's Tartarus lies as far beneath Hades as the sky is from the earth (8.16). Virgil's Sibyl tells Aeneas that Tartarus goes down sheer twice as far as one looks up to the sky and Olympus (*Aeneid* 6.577–579; see [[tartarus]]).
- **Aethon.** Pallas' warhorse Aethon, stripped of his trappings, walks weeping in his master's funeral procession and wets his face with great tears (*Aeneid* 11.89–90). Aethon is one of the four horses that Hector calls by name in Book 8 (8.185; see [[the-horses-of-hector]]).
