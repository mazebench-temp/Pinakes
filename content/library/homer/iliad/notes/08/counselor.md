---
summary: Zeus the counselor thunders three times from the mountains of Ida while Diomedes ponders whether to turn and fight Hector.
---

{{quote:f596657f-dd68-577a-a590-70b1b66c818d}}

Three times Diomedes thinks of turning to fight, and three times Zeus answers ([[pondering-two-ways]]). The epithet of his counsel stands in the verse of his sign, and his counsel is the plan of the day: the Trojans are to win ([[that-turns-the-battle]], [[teras]], [[plan-of-zeus]]). Hector reads the thunder at once: “I know that the son of Cronus has readily nodded his promise to me” (8.175; [[son-of-cronus]]). At the end of Book 7 Zeus the counselor thundered all night over the Achaeans as they feasted, devising evil for them (7.478–479); on the second day of battle he speaks the same language by day. See [[zeus]].
