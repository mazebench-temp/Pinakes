Twice Teucer's arrows, aimed at Hector, have killed men beside him, and Hector answers with a stone. He gives the reins to Cebriones and goes on foot, straight at the archer (8.318–322; see [[chariot]]). The archer must stand clear of his cover to shoot, and Hector catches him as he draws the string to his shoulder, at the collarbone, “where the collarbone parts the neck and the chest, and a blow is deadliest” (8.325–326), the half-verse used of the spot where Paris' arrow struck Nestor's horse (8.84; see [[horses]]). The stone breaks the bowstring and numbs Teucer's hand, and the bow falls (see [[bow]]). The stone is “jagged” (ὀκριόεις), like the stone with which Peirous crushed Diores' ankle (4.518) and the one with which Patroclus will kill Cebriones (16.735; see [[jagged]]).

Teucer is not killed. Ajax covers him with his shield, and two companions carry him to the ships (8.330–334; see [[the-shield-of-ajax]]). Stones decide Hector's fights with the sons of Telamon: Ajax's stone laid him on his back in their duel (7.268–272), Hector's stone fells Teucer here, and Ajax's stone will fell Hector again in Book 14 (14.409–418).
