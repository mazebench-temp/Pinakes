---
summary: Athena arms herself “for tearful war,” and Hector hopes the wounded Achaeans will teach others to shrink from bringing “tearful Ares” against the Trojans.
---

The two forms of the epithet are both in Book 8. δακρυόεις describes war in the verse that ends Athena's arming, repeated from Book 5: she “armed herself in armor for tearful war” (8.388 = 5.737; [[daughter-of-a-mighty-father]], [[arming-scene]]). πολύδακρυς describes the war god himself in Hector's night speech: let the Achaeans be wounded as they board their ships, “so that others too may shrink from bringing tearful Ares against the horse-taming Trojans” (8.515–516; [[tamer-of-horses]]). Iris used the same phrase in Book 3 of the war the two armies had been fighting over Helen (3.131–132). See [[ares]] and [[polemos]].
