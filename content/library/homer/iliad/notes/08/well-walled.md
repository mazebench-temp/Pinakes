---
summary: Agamemnon tells Zeus that he burned offerings at every altar on his way to Troy, “eager to sack well-walled Troy.”
---

“But on every one I burned the fat and the thighs of oxen, eager to sack well-walled Troy” (8.240–241; [[many-benched]], [[perikalles]]). The epithet of Troy's walls stands, as it usually does, in a verse about taking the city ([[of-the-fine-towers]]): Achilles promised Agamemnon a threefold return if Zeus ever let them sack the well-walled city (1.128–129), and Agamemnon complained in Book 2 that Zeus had promised he would sack well-walled Ilios and go home (2.111–113; [[hard]]). Here he reminds Zeus of the sacrifices he made for that hope, now that the Trojans are at his ships ([[raiding]]). See [[troy]] and [[the-walls-of-troy]].
