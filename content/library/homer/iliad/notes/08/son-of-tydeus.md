---
summary: Diomedes is “the son of Tydeus” nine times in Book 8: as he rescues Nestor, in Nestor's counsel to flee, in his fear of Hector's boast and in Hector's taunt, as he crosses the ditch first, and in Hector's boast at night.
---

**The rescue.** “But the son of Tydeus, alone as he was, mingled with the champions” (8.99) and takes Nestor into his chariot; “the son of Tydeus threw his spear” at Hector and killed his charioteer (8.118–123; [[eniopeus]]).

**The retreat.** When the thunderbolt falls, Nestor says: “Son of Tydeus, come, turn the single-hoofed horses to flight” (8.139; [[single-hoofed]]). Diomedes fears Hector's boast, which he imagines in his enemy's words: “The son of Tydeus fled before me to the ships” (8.149). Nestor answers, “Ah, son of wise-hearted Tydeus, what a thing you have said!” (8.152; [[wise-hearted]]). Hector shouts after him, “Son of Tydeus, the Danaans of the swift horses honored you above others” (8.161; [[wretched-doll]]), and “the son of Tydeus was torn two ways in thought” (8.167; [[pondering-two-ways]]).

**The rally and the boast.** After the eagle, no Achaean could boast of crossing the ditch ahead of “the son of Tydeus” (8.254). At night Hector names him again: “I will know whether the son of Tydeus, mighty Diomedes, will push me back from the ships to the wall” (8.532–533; [[krateros]]). The patronymic that Agamemnon used in Book 4 to shame Diomedes by his father carries Diomedes' fear of shame through the day ([[tydeus]]). See [[diomedes]].
