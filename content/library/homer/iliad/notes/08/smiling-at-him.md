summary: Zeus answers Athena's plea with a smile, “Take heart, Tritogeneia, dear child; I do not speak in full earnest”, in verses he will speak to her again before Hector's death.
en: [Smiling at her]
passages:
  - 8.38-40 | Zeus answers Athena
---

The threats of the dawn assembly (8.5–27) are followed by the gods' silence and Athena's plea for the Danaans (8.28–37), and Zeus answers her smiling:

{{quote:b95f7f35-63a2-5e09-bd85-150cb5c7fefd}}

ἐπιμειδήσας, “smiling at her”, is the smile of a speaker who takes no offense (see [[smiling-at-him]]), and the words take back the edge of the threat: οὔ … θυμῷ πρόφρονι, “not in full earnest”. Zeus speaks the same two verses to Athena in Book 22, when she protests at his wish to save Hector (22.183–184; see [[tritogeneia]]). The mildness sits oddly after the threats of 8.10–27, and 8.28–40 are reported to have been rejected in antiquity (see the translation's note on 8.28–40). At evening Zeus does not smile: he mocks the two goddesses and tells Hera that nothing is more shameless than she (8.447–456, 8.477–483).
