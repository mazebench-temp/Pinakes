summary: Patroclus does not appear in Book 8, but Zeus names him in his prophecy to Hera: Hector will not stop until Achilles is roused, “on the day when they fight at the sterns of the ships in the direst strait, over Patroclus dead” (8.475–476). It is the poem's first word of his death.
passages:
  - 8.470-477 | Zeus foretells the battle at the ships
---

Patroclus has not been named since Book 1, where he led Briseis out of Achilles' hut and gave her to Agamemnon's heralds (1.337–347). He comes back into the poem as a dead man in a prophecy: περὶ Πατρόκλοιο θανόντος, “over Patroclus dead” (8.476; see [[the-death-of-patroclus]], [[plan-of-zeus]] and [[achilles]]). Zeus speaks to Hera alone, and no mortal hears him. The listener knows that Patroclus will die before he has done anything in the war, and every later scene of his is heard with that knowledge: his errand to Nestor, which the narrator calls “the beginning of evil for him” (11.604), his plea to Achilles to send him out (16.20–45), and his last charge (16.684–867).

Zeus tells Hera more in Book 15: Achilles will send out his companion, Hector will kill him before Ilios, and Achilles will kill Hector (15.64–68). The prophecy of Book 8 sets the fight “at the sterns of the ships”. In the event Patroclus dies on the plain, and the fight over his body is fought there (see the translation's note on 8.475–476). The phrase of Agamemnon's praise of Teucer, “and you may become a light to the Danaans” (8.282), will be Nestor's words to Patroclus and Patroclus' to Achilles when he asks to go out (11.797, 16.39; see [[teucer]]).
