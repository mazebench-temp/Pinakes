---
summary: Hera reminds Poseidon that the Danaans bring him "many and pleasing" gifts at Helice and Aegae.
---

Hera's appeal to Poseidon rests on what he owes his worshippers: "Yet they bring you gifts to Helice and Aegae, / many and pleasing; so you should will them victory" (8.203–204; see [[poseidon]], [[aegae]] and [[hera]]). Helice, on the southern shore of the Gulf of Corinth, is named in the Catalogue among the towns of Agamemnon (2.575), and its god is "the lord of Helice" in a simile of a bull dragged to sacrifice (20.403–405). Strabo lists Aegae, "with a temple of Poseidon", two places before Helice among the twelve districts of the Achaeans on that coast (8.7.4). Poseidon does not answer the appeal to his gifts; he answers that Zeus is far the stronger (8.208–211).
