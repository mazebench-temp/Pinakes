---
summary: “Godlike Lycophontes” is one of the eight Trojans whom Teucer brings down, one after another.
---

“Orsilochus first, and Ormenus and Ophelestes, Daetor and Chromius and godlike Lycophontes, and Amopaon, son of Polyaemon, and Melanippus” (8.274–276; [[lycophontes]], [[long-barbed]]). Of the eight names in Teucer's list, only Lycophontes receives an epithet, and Amopaon a father ([[amopaon]], [[polyaemon]]). The honorific that the poem gives to heroes of the past, and to the men of the Catalogue, gives a moment's distinction to a man named only to fall ([[bountiful-earth]]). See [[teucer]].
