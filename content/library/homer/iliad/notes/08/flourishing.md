---
summary: Two husbands in their prime: the Trojans whom Diomedes has killed, “whose husbands in their prime you have flung in the dust,” and Hector, Andromache's “husband in my prime.”
grc: [θαλεροὺς]
en: [in my prime]
---

θαλερός of a husband is the vigor of a young man married. Nestor tells Diomedes that the wives of the Trojans will not believe Hector's boast, the wives “whose husbands in their prime you have flung in the dust” (8.156; [[great-hearted]]). A little later Hector, urging on his horses, reminds them that Andromache gave them wheat and wine before she served him, “who boast that I am her husband in my prime” (8.188–190; [[andromache]], [[the-horses-of-hector]]). The widows Diomedes has made and the wife whom Hector will leave a widow are named in the same book ([[husbands-and-wives]]). See [[hector]].
