summary: Teucer destroys the Trojan battalions “with his strong bow,” and at night Hector names his enemy “the son of Tydeus, mighty Diomedes.”

---

**The bow.** “Agamemnon, lord of men, rejoiced when he saw him destroying the battalions of the Trojans with his strong bow” (8.278–279; [[lord-of-men]], [[back-bent]], [[bow]]).

**Diomedes.** At the end of his night speech Hector names the one Achaean he expects to meet: “I will know whether the son of Tydeus, mighty Diomedes, will push me back from the ships to the wall” (8.532–533; [[son-of-tydeus]]). κρατερὸς Διομήδης is Diomedes' formula ([[diomedes]]). Hector boasts that Diomedes will lie wounded among the foremost at sunrise (8.535–538); in Book 11 Diomedes is wounded, by Paris' arrow in the foot (11.369–378). See [[hector]].
