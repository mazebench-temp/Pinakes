---
summary: Teucer takes “a bitter arrow” from his quiver to shoot at Hector, and Hector's stone stops him.
---

“Teucer had taken a bitter arrow from his quiver and set it on the string” (8.323–324; [[quiver]], [[arrows]]). The line-end πικρὸν ὀϊστόν is the arrow's standing epithet, and here it is the arrow that is never shot: as Teucer draws it back, Hector strikes him with a jagged stone by the collarbone and breaks the string (8.324–329; [[jagged]], [[of-the-gleaming-helmet]]).
