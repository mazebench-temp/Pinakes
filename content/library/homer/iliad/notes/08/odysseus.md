**The flight.** Diomedes calls him by the full verse of his titles, “Son of Laertes, sprung from Zeus, Odysseus of many devices” (8.93; see [[son-of-laertes]], [[sprung-from-zeus]] and [[of-many-devices]]), and then rebukes him: “where are you fleeing, turning your back like a coward in the throng?” (8.94; see [[diomedes]]). He wants him to help drive Hector away from Nestor (8.96; see [[nestor]]). ἐσάκουσε occurs only here in Homer. It may mean that Odysseus “did not hear”, as the translation takes it, which leaves his flight unexplained but makes it no refusal; or that he “did not heed”, and ran on past the call. The narrator gives no reason (see [[retreat]]). He gives him his epithet of endurance, πολύτλας, “much-enduring” (see [[much-enduring]]), at the moment he runs.

Book 11 turns the scene around. Its verse of ruin is this book's, “Then there would have been ruin, and deeds past mending would have been done” (11.310 = 8.130), but there it is Odysseus who calls on Diomedes to stand by him (11.312–315). When Diomedes is wounded, Odysseus, left alone, reasons that “cowards” leave the fighting while the best must stand their ground, and stays (11.401–410). κακός, the word of Diomedes' rebuke here (8.94), is his own word there.

**The ship in the middle.** To rouse the army, Agamemnon stands on “Odysseus' black ship, huge in the hull, which lay in the middle, so that a shout could carry to both ends”, as far as the huts of Ajax and of Achilles, whose ships lay at the two ends of the line (8.222–226; see [[agamemnon]], [[ships]] and [[huge-hulled]]). The same verses place Strife on the ship in Book 11 (11.5–9). Here 8.224–226 are reported to have been rejected in antiquity.
