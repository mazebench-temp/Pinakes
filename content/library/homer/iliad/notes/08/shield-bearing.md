---
summary: The word describes the Trojan “shield-bearing” men whom Diomedes has killed, and the Achaean shield-bearers hemmed in between their wall and ditch.
---

The word names fighting men by the shield they carry, on both sides. Nestor speaks of the wives “of the great-hearted shield-bearing Trojans, whose husbands in their prime you have flung in the dust” (8.155–156; [[great-hearted]], [[flourishing]]). Later in the day the narrator describes the Achaeans driven back inside the ditch: the space “was filled alike with horses and shield-bearing men hemmed in” by Hector (8.213–216; [[equal-of-ares]]).
