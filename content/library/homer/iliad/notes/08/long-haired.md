---
summary: The long-haired Achaeans eat and arm at dawn, are hunted back to their ditch by Hector in the afternoon, and at night are the army that Hector fears may flee by sea.
---

The formula marks the Achaeans at three points of the day. In the morning “the long-haired Achaeans took their meal quickly among the huts, and straight after it they armed themselves” (8.53–54). In the afternoon Hector drives them to the ditch: “so Hector pressed hard on the long-haired Achaeans, always killing the hindmost” (8.341–342; [[exulting-in-strength]], [[dogs]]). At night he orders fires on the plain “in case even by night the long-haired Achaeans set out to flee over the broad back of the sea” (8.510–511; [[early-born]], [[broad-back-of-the-sea]]). The army that ate and armed at dawn is, by nightfall, an enemy who may slip away. See [[achaeans]].
