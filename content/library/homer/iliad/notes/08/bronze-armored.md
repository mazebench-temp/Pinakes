---
summary: Zeus sets in his scales the fates “of the horse-taming Trojans and the bronze-armored Achaeans.”
---

{{quote:77eb0408-3f65-5f0c-b442-d4815e180737}}

The verse that names the two armies by their epithets, Τρώων θʼ ἱπποδάμων καὶ Ἀχαιῶν χαλκοχιτώνων, has stood until now in scenes of watching and truce: in the battles that Helen wove into her web, in Iris' summons to Helen to come and watch the duel, and in the herald's summons to Priam to come and swear the oaths (3.126–127, 3.130–131, 3.250–251). In Book 8 it stands in the scales, and the pair is weighed for death ([[tamer-of-horses]], [[that-lays-men-low]], [[the-scales-of-zeus]]). See [[achaeans]].
