summary: The gods meet in assembly at dawn to hear Zeus forbid them the war, and they fall silent before his threat. They take no part in the day's fighting; Hera speaks of the gods who help the Danaans as a party, and at night, in verses known only from the Second Alcibiades, the gods refuse the savor of the Trojans' hecatombs.

**The assembly.** "Zeus who delights in thunder held an assembly of the gods" (θεῶν ἀγορὴν ποιήσατο, 8.2). The book ends its day with the same verse for Hector, who "held an assembly of the Trojans" on the plain (8.489; see [[assembly]] and [[the-gods-mirror-the-mortals]]). Zeus addresses the whole company, "Hear me, all you gods and all you goddesses" (8.5), and names them again in his challenge, "lay hold of it, all you gods and all you goddesses" (8.20; see [[the-golden-rope]]). The address returns once more, when Zeus announces to the gods the birth of a man of his blood on the day Heracles was born (19.101). No god, "female" or "male", may try to frustrate his word (8.7–8). They answer with silence, "amazed at his word, for he had spoken very forcefully" (8.28–29; see [[silence]]), until Athena speaks for them (8.30–37; see [[athena]]).

**The party of the Danaans.** The gods are divided over the war, as they were in Books 4 and 5. Hera speaks of "all of us who help the Danaans" (ὅσοι Δαναοῖσιν ἀρωγοί, 8.205), and Poseidon of "we others" against Zeus (8.211; see [[hera]] and [[poseidon]]). In the evening Zeus tells Hera and Athena that "all the gods there are on Olympus could not turn me back" (8.450–451), as he warned Hera in Book 1 (1.566). When the Achaeans are penned at the ships, they lift their hands "to all the gods" (8.346–347; see [[prayer]]), and at night Hector prays "to Zeus and the other gods" (8.526).

The four bracketed verses are not in the manuscripts (see [[hecatomb]] and [[sacrifice]]). They are known from the Second Alcibiades ascribed to Plato, which reports the scene in indirect speech (149d–e; see the translation's note on 8.548, 8.550–552, and [[the-refused-sacrifice]]). They contradict what Zeus said in Book 4: of all the cities of men he honored Ilios most, and his altar there never lacked its share of the feast (4.44–49). The last verse is the one with which he named Priam and his people there (8.552 = 4.47).
