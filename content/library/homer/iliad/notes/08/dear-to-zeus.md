---
summary: Hector is “dear to Zeus” as he speaks to the Trojans on the plain at nightfall, and in his speech so are the heralds he sends to the city.
grc: [Διῒ φίλοι]
---

The Trojans step down from their chariots “and listened to the word that Hector, dear to Zeus, spoke” (8.492–493). The epithet opens his night assembly, at the end of the day that Zeus has given him ([[assembly]], [[phaidimos]]). In the speech he gives it to others: “let the heralds, dear to Zeus, proclaim through the city” that the boys and old men are to keep watch on the walls (8.517–519; [[heralds]], [[messengers-of-zeus]], [[god-built]]). Heralds are under the god's protection, as Achilles says when he calls Talthybius and Eurybates “messengers of Zeus and of men” (1.334). In Hector's speech the favor of Zeus covers the leader and those who carry his orders. See [[hector]].
