---
summary: Agamemnon's rebuke to the Argives who hold back from battle opens with three blows, “you arrow-fighters, you disgraces, have you no shame?” (οὔ νυ σέβεσθε;), the only use of σέβομαι in the Iliad.
passages:
  - 4.240-249 | Agamemnon rebukes the laggards
---

Agamemnon goes through the ranks encouraging the eager and rebuking the slack (4.231–241). The rebuke begins: Ἀργεῖοι ἰόμωροι ἐλεγχέες, οὔ νυ σέβεσθε; “Argives, you arrow-fighters, you disgraces, have you no shame?” (4.242). The question asks whether they feel no awe, and the rest of the speech shows what they ought to draw back from. They stand dazed like fawns (4.243–246; see [[like-fawns]]), and they seem to be waiting for the Trojans to reach the ships, “to see if the son of Cronus will hold his hand over you” (4.247–249). What they lack is awe before their own disgrace.

The same kind of rebuke is made in later books with αἰδώς. “Shame, Argives, base disgraces, admirable only in looks!” (αἰδώς, Ἀργεῖοι, κάκʼ ἐλέγχεα, εἶδος ἀγητοί) cries Hera in the likeness of Stentor (5.787), and Agamemnon repeats the line (8.228). See [[aidos]], [[elenchos]], [[arrow-fighters]], [[obscure-iomoroi]] and [[praise-and-blame]].
