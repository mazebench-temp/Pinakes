**Contingent by contingent.** The review goes from one leader and his men to the next: the Cretans arming around Idomeneus, with Meriones urging on the rearmost battalions (4.251–254); the two Ajaxes, followed by “a cloud of foot soldiers”, dark and bristling with shields and spears (4.273–282); Nestor and his captains (4.293–296); the Athenians around Menestheus and the Cephallenians around Odysseus (4.327–335); the Argives of Diomedes and Sthenelus (4.365–367). The Catalogue listed these contingents by their ships; the review shows them arming for battle. See [[the-review-of-the-army]].

**Order.** Nestor's arrangement is a picture of an army held together by its structure: horsemen in front, the best of the foot soldiers behind “to be a bulwark against war”, and the cowards in the middle, “so that even an unwilling man would have to fight” (4.297–300; see [[battle-order]]). His orders to his horsemen are about keeping the line, not riding out alone ahead of the others nor falling back (4.303–307).

**Silence and noise.** When the armies advance, the difference between them is heard. The Danaan battalions move on rank after rank like waves on a shore, and “to his own men each one of the leaders gave orders; and the rest went in silence … silent, in fear of their commanders” (4.427–431). The Trojans are like ewes bleating in a rich man's fold, “for not all of them had the same speech or one language, but their tongues were mingled, and the men were called from many lands” (4.433–438). An army of allies speaks many languages; Iris had told Hector so (2.803–804). See [[silence]], [[semaino]], [[sheep-in-the-fold]] and [[trojan-allies]].

**Leaders fall.** In the first battle the leaders are in front, and they are killed there: Elephenor, leader of the Abantes (4.463–470), and Diores and Peirous, leaders of the Epeians and the Thracians, who lie side by side at the end (4.517–538). See [[hegemon]] and [[death-in-battle]].
