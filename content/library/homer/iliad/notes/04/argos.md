Hera names Argos first. It is her own city: Zeus has just called her “Argive Hera” (Ἥρη Ἀργείη, 4.8; see [[argive]]), and her great sanctuary stood in the plain of Argos. She offers it to be sacked whenever it becomes hateful to Zeus, as the price of Troy's fall (see [[hera]] and [[the-bargain-of-zeus-and-hera]]). In the Catalogue Argos is the city of Diomedes (2.559), who in this book stands silent under Agamemnon's rebuke (4.401–402).

Argos is also the army's home. Agamemnon, fearing that his brother will die of Pandarus' arrow, says that the Achaeans will at once remember their native land, and that he would come “in utter disgrace to thirsty Argos” (4.171), leaving Argive Helen for the Trojans to boast of. πολυδίψιον, “thirsty”, is used of Argos only here in Homer (see [[thirsty]]). In Book 2 Agamemnon told the army, to test it, that Zeus bade him “go back to Argos in disgrace” (2.115).
