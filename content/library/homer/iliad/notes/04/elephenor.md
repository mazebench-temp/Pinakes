**The Catalogue line.** “Son of Chalcodon, leader of the great-hearted Abantes” (4.464) repeats word for word the line that introduced him in the Catalogue of Ships (2.541). He is κρείων, “powerful” ([[powerful]]), a title of rank.

**Haste.** He drags the body out from under the missiles “in eager haste” (λελιημένος) “to strip off his armor”, and the poem judges him in half a line: μίνυνθα δέ οἱ γένεθʼ ὁρμή, “but his effort lasted only a little while” (4.466). His own eagerness uncovers him. As he stoops to drag the corpse, his side comes out from behind his shield, and Agenor stabs him there (see [[agenor]]). The Abantes of the Catalogue were spearmen “eager with outstretched ash spears to tear the corselets around the chests of their enemies” (2.543–544). Their leader dies reaching for a dead man's armor. In Book 6 Nestor tells the Achaeans not to hang back over the spoils while there are men to kill; they can strip the dead at their leisure afterward (6.66–71).

**Over his body.** “So his spirit left him, and over him there was work, grievous work, of Trojans and Achaeans” (4.470–471), and the two sides leap on one another like wolves (see [[like-wolves]]). The poem does not say who carried him off, and he is not named again. See [[abantes]], [[chalcodon]], [[echepolus]] and [[the-first-battle]].
