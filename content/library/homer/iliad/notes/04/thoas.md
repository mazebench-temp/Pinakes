Θόας Αἰτωλός, “Thoas the Aetolian” (4.527): the one place where the poem gives him his people's name as an epithet, though he leads the forty ships of the Aetolians in the Catalogue (2.638–644; see [[aetolians]]). The Diores he avenges was not one of his own men but a leader of the Epeians (see [[diores]]). He kills in two strokes. His spear catches Peirous as he springs back from Diores' body, in the chest above the nipple, and sticks in the lung; then he comes up close, pulls the spear out of the chest, draws his sword and strikes him in the belly.

He does not get the armor. The Thracians “with the topknots” stand around their leader with their long spears in their hands and push him away, “big and mighty and noble as he was” (4.534). The line gives him three epithets and a concessive περ (“though”): the poem praises the man it shows driven back. See [[peirous]], [[thracians]] and [[the-first-battle]].
