---
summary: The father of men and gods yields to Hera and sends Athena to break the truce.
---

“So she spoke, and the father of men and gods did not disobey” (4.68). In Book 1 the title introduced Zeus' answer to Hera's questions ({{ref:Iliad 1.544}}). Here the grandest of his titles introduces his compliance: he sends Athena at once to make the Trojans break the oaths (4.69–72; [[exultant]], [[the-bargain-of-zeus-and-hera]]). The same words of obedience were used of Agamemnon, lord of men, when he accepted Nestor's plan in Book 2 ({{ref:Iliad 2.441}}).
