{{quote:6912aca5-7766-5232-a212-c4e195edfdf6}}

In Book 1 Achilles used the word of himself: his mother bore him to be short-lived ({{ref:Iliad 1.352}}). Here the narrator gives it to a young Trojan whom the poem names only to kill. Simoeisius was born on the banks of the Simoeis and named after it, and he “did not repay his dear parents for his rearing” (θρέπτρα, 4.477–478; [[simoeisius]]). The adjective that stated Achilles' fate in advance states this death after the event, and joins the greatest hero to one of the least ([[flourishing]], [[death-in-battle]]).
