---
summary: In the simile of the mother and the fly the child lies “in sweet sleep”; the adjective is the plain ἡδύς.
grc: [ἡδέϊ λέξεται ὕπνῳ]
---

Athena brushes the arrow away from Menelaus “as a mother brushes a fly from her child, when he lies in sweet sleep” (4.130–131; [[mother-and-fly]]). The word is ἡδύς, the ordinary adjective for what is sweet, separated from ὕπνος by the verb: ἡδέϊ λέξεται ὕπνῳ. The sleeping child is safe because someone is watching over him, and the warrior in the middle of the battle is compared to him ([[hypnos]], [[driver-of-spoil]]).
