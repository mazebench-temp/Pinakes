Echepolus, son of Thalysias, is the first man killed in the Iliad (see [[echepolus]]). The poem marks the moment twice: Antilochus was “the first” (πρῶτος) to kill a Trojan (4.457), and was “first” (πρῶτος) to strike him on the ridge of his helmet (4.459). The spear point fixes in his forehead and pierces the bone, “and darkness covered his eyes” (4.461; see [[darkness-covered-his-eyes]]). Then he falls. The scene has the parts of the Iliad's killings: killer and victim, the victim's father, the wound, the death and the fall (see [[androktasia]]). The killer is Antilochus, son of Nestor, who is named here for the first time in the poem (see [[antilochus]]).

The book has used πύργος twice already, for a body of men. The Athenians and Cephallenians stood waiting “until another column of Achaeans should come on” (πύργος, 4.334), and Agamemnon told their leaders they would gladly watch “even if ten columns of Achaeans were fighting in front of you” (πύργοι, 4.347). The towers of the review were columns of soldiers. The first tower to fall in the battle is one man. The poem will never show the towers of Troy falling; its first death gives their fall to a man.
