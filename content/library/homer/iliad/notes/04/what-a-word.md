summary: Hera protests to Zeus in the line of Book 1, “Most dread son of Cronus, what a thing you have said!” (4.25 = 1.552), and Odysseus rebukes Agamemnon with the related exclamation, “what a word has escaped the barrier of your teeth!” (4.350).
grc: ["ποῖόν σε ἔπος φύγεν ἕρκος ὀδόντων"]
en: [what a word has escaped the barrier of your teeth]
---

**Hera.** Zeus has proposed peace, with Troy spared and Helen returned. Hera answers:

{{quote:e671a048-6dff-52cb-9ab8-0f4621599875}}

It is the line of her protest in Book 1 (1.552), and she will use it four times more (8.462, 14.330, 16.440, 18.361). In Book 8 the whole scene returns: the two goddesses mutter, Athena keeps silent in her anger, Hera's breast cannot contain hers, and she protests in the same words (8.457–462 = 4.20–25). In Book 1 the line opened an attack that Zeus silenced; here it opens a bargain that Hera wins (see [[most-dread]] and [[hera]]).

**Odysseus.** Agamemnon has rebuked Odysseus and Menestheus for standing back. Odysseus answers, “looking at him darkly”:

{{quote:0da97f4f-efab-55c3-9acf-086b8aed91c6}}

ἕρκος ὀδόντων, “the barrier of the teeth”, is the fence that should have kept the word in: the rebuke was spoken rashly, and should never have been let out. In the Iliad the line is spoken twice, both times by Odysseus to Agamemnon; the second time he rejects the king's proposal to launch the ships and flee (14.83). The Odyssey has it six times, among them from Zeus to Athena and from the old nurse Eurycleia to Odysseus and to Penelope (Odyssey 1.64, 5.22, 19.492, 23.70). Agamemnon smiles and takes back his word (4.356–363; see [[smiling-at-him]] and [[looking-darkly]]).
