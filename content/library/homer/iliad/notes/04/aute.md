  - 4.327-335 | Menestheus and Odysseus wait
  - 4.507-513 | Apollo rouses the Trojans
---

The war cry marks the start of fighting. Agamemnon finds Menestheus standing among the Athenians, “masters of the war cry” (μήστωρες ἀϋτῆς, 4.328; see [[masters-of-the-war-cry]]), and Odysseus with the Cephallenians, who stood waiting, “for their people had not yet heard the war cry” (4.331): the battalions were only beginning to move, and they waited for another column to charge first (4.332–335). Agamemnon takes their waiting for reluctance, and rebukes them (4.336–348).

At the end of the book a god gives the cry. Apollo, indignant that the Argives are driving the Trojans back, looks down from Pergamus and “shouted a command to the Trojans” (κέκλετʼ ἀΰσας, 4.508). See [[apollo]].
