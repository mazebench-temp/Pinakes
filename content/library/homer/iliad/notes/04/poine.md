---
summary: Agamemnon declares that the breakers of an oath “pay a great price, with their own heads and their wives and their children”.
grc: [ἀπέτισαν]
passages:
  - 4.158-162 | The oath not in vain
---

In Book 3 the oath set a penalty (ποινή) on the Trojans if they would not pay the satisfaction the terms required (3.288–290). In Book 4 the Trojans owe a greater one, for breaking the oath itself. “For even if the Olympian has not fulfilled it at once, he fulfills it in the end, even late, and they pay (ἀπέτισαν) a great price, with their own heads and their wives and their children” (4.160–162). The past tense states a rule: this is what always happens to oath-breakers. The payment is the one the curse named when the wine was poured, their brains and their children's, and their wives subdued by others (3.300–301).
