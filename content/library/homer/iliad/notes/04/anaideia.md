---
summary: Diores is brought down by “the shameless stone” (λᾶας ἀναιδής), which crushes the tendons and bones of his ankle.
grc: [ἀναιδὴς]
passages:
  - 4.517-526 | The death of Diores
---

In Book 1 ἀναιδής was an insult to a man: Achilles called Agamemnon “clothed in shamelessness” and “utterly shameless” (1.149, 1.158). In Book 4 it is said of a thing. Peirous hits Diores with a jagged stone beside the ankle, and “the shameless stone crushed both the tendons and the bones utterly” (ἀμφοτέρω δὲ τένοντε καὶ ὀστέα λᾶας ἀναιδὴς ἄχρις ἀπηλοίησεν, 4.521–522). A stone has no αἰδώς. It respects nothing and does not stop, and the word makes the missile as pitiless as the man who threw it. The Odyssey uses the same phrase for the stone of Sisyphus, which rolls back down each time he has pushed it to the top (*Odyssey* 11.598).
