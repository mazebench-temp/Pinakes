summary: The numbers of Book 4 are few and typical. Hera offers three cities; the wild goat's horns are sixteen palms long; Agamemnon speaks of ten columns of Achaeans; and the Theban ambush is fifty young men with two leaders, of whom Tydeus lets one go home.
refs: [4.51, 4.109, 4.347, 4.393-397]
passages:
  - 4.51-56 | Hera's three cities
  - 4.105-111 | Pandarus' bow
  - 4.391-398 | The ambush of the fifty
---

Hera names “three cities” as the dearest to her (4.51). The horns of the wild goat from which Pandarus' bow was made grew “sixteen palms long” (ἑκκαιδεκάδωρα, 4.109), a δῶρον being the breadth of a hand; the number is given as a measure, and it makes the bow a marvel (see [[bow]] and [[the-bow-of-pandarus]]). Agamemnon tells Menestheus and Odysseus that they would gladly watch “even if ten columns of Achaeans” were fighting ahead of them (4.347), as in Book 2 he wished for “ten such counselors” as Nestor (2.372). The Cadmeians set an ambush of “fifty young men” under two leaders, and Tydeus kills them all and lets one go home (4.393–397): fifty and one, the round number and the survivor who carries the news. Of the Trojan sheep in the simile there are “thousands” (μυρίαι, 4.434), the number that is not counted. See [[sheep-in-the-fold]].
