summary: Agamemnon does not know who shot Menelaus: “someone well skilled with the bow,” a Trojan or a Lycian.

{{quote:23f3728d-ba36-5748-9c8d-cbee148ea8be}}

Agamemnon gives the unknown archer the praise of skill, and Talthybius repeats the message word for word to Machaon (4.206–207). The archer's fame (κλέος) is the Achaeans' grief (πένθος; [[kleos]], [[penthos]]). The same turn of phrase describes Nestor, “long skilled in wars” (πάλαι πολέμων ἐῢ εἰδώς, 4.310). See [[pandarus]], [[lycians]].
