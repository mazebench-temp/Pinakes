summary: In Book 4 the Achaeans are the injured party. The gods arrange that the Trojans shall be the first to harm them against the oaths; Agamemnon fears that they will remember home if Menelaus dies, rouses them rank by rank, and they advance in silence to the first battle of the poem.

  - 4.422-432 | The Achaeans advance in silence
---

**Watched from Olympus.** The gods look down on the city of the Trojans (4.4) and decide how the war shall go on. The plan is Hera's, and its victims are named with a word of praise: Athena is to contrive that the Trojans be the first to harm “the exultant Achaeans” (ὑπερκύδαντας Ἀχαιούς, 4.66–67, 4.71–72; see [[exultant]]). When she comes down like a shooting star, one voice speaks for “Achaeans and Trojans alike”, wondering whether Zeus will bring war again or friendship (4.79–85; see [[tis-speech]]).

**The army and its king.** Agamemnon's lament shows what he fears from them: if Menelaus dies, “at once the Achaeans will remember their native land” (4.172), as they did when he tested them in Book 2. Menelaus, wounded, tells him not to frighten “the army of the Achaeans” (4.184). In the review Agamemnon calls the ranks Argives (4.234, 4.242; see [[argives]]), and both he and the narrator speak of the Achaeans in columns: a πύργος (“tower”) of Achaeans is a column that should lead the charge (4.334, 4.347). Diomedes, silencing Sthenelus, puts the king's fortune in the army's hands: glory if the Achaeans take Ilios, great grief if they are cut down (4.415–417).

**Silence and the charge.** When the review is over, the Danaan battalions move to war like waves breaking on a shore, each leader giving orders and the rest going in silence, “in fear of their commanders” (4.422–432; see [[danaans]], [[waves-on-the-shore]] and [[silence]]). The Trojans bleat like ewes in a rich man's fold, in many tongues (4.433–438). In the first battle the Achaeans drive the Trojans back until Apollo rouses them with a taunt, that Achilles is not fighting (4.505–513). Athena answers by going through the Achaeans wherever she sees them holding back (4.514–516).

**Another army of Achaeans.** In Agamemnon's story of Tydeus, “the Achaeans” are the army that marched against Thebes a generation earlier (4.384; see [[tydeus]]). The name is not only that of the army at Troy.
