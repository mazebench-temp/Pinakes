summary: Rebuked as a man who waits for others to fight, Odysseus tells Agamemnon that what he says is “empty wind”; Agamemnon smiles, takes back his word, and asks that the gods make it all “blow away”.
passages:
  - 4.338-363 | Agamemnon, Menestheus and Odysseus
---

{{quote:0da97f4f-efab-55c3-9acf-086b8aed91c6}}

Agamemnon has found Menestheus and Odysseus standing with their men, waiting for another column to begin the fighting, and has called them the first at his feasts and the last to fight (4.338–348). Odysseus answers “looking at him darkly”: the king will see the father of Telemachus mingling with the champions, “what you say is empty wind” (σὺ δὲ ταῦτʼ ἀνεμώλια βάζεις, 4.355). In his account the rebuke has no weight, because it is not true.

Agamemnon agrees. He smiles, “when he saw that he was angry”, and takes back his word (4.356–357). He did not mean to rebuke him beyond measure; they think alike; “we will make these things good later, if anything bad has been said now; and may the gods make it all blow away” (τὰ δὲ πάντα θεοὶ μεταμώνια θεῖεν, 4.362–363). Both men call the words wind, one in anger and one in apology, and the quarrel is over in fifteen lines. It is the reverse of the quarrel of Book 1, where a king's words to a warrior were not taken back. See [[mythos]], [[odysseus]], [[agamemnon]] and [[praise-and-blame]].
