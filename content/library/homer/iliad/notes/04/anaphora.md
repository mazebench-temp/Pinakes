---
summary: Sthenelus' boast opens two clauses with “we” (4.405–406), Agamemnon's rebuke of Diomedes two questions with “why” (4.371), and the clash of the armies two clauses with “together” (4.447).
refs: [4.371, 4.405-406, 4.447]
---

**“We.”** Sthenelus answers the king's praise of the fathers with the sons: ἡμεῖς τοι πατέρων μέγʼ ἀμείνονες εὐχόμεθʼ εἶναι· / ἡμεῖς καὶ Θήβης ἕδος εἵλομεν ἑπταπύλοιο, “We boast that we are far better than our fathers; we even took the seat of seven-gated Thebes” (4.405–406). The pronoun at the head of both verses sets “we” against “our fathers” and “they” (κεῖνοι, 4.409). See [[better-than-our-fathers]] and [[sthenelus]].

**“Why.”** Agamemnon's rebuke begins with two questions, τί πτώσσεις, τί δʼ ὀπιπεύεις πολέμοιο γεφύρας; “why do you cower, why do you stare at the bridges of war?” (4.371). The repeated τί (“why”) presses the charge before the story of Tydeus answers it (see [[mythological-paradigm]]).

**“Together.”** When the armies meet, σύν, “together”, stands at the head of two clauses: σύν ῥʼ ἔβαλον ῥινούς, σὺν δʼ ἔγχεα, “they dashed together their shields of hide, and their spears” (4.447). The preverb is separated from its verb, as ἐκ was in the landing at Chryse (1.436–439), and it carries the collision (see [[the-armies-clash]]).
