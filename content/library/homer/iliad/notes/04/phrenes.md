**The fool.** The narrator closes Athena's speech to Pandarus with a judgment: “So spoke Athena, and she persuaded the mind of the fool” (τῷ δὲ φρένας ἄφρονι πεῖθεν, 4.104). ἄφρων, “without φρένες, senseless”, does the work that νήπιος does elsewhere in the narrator's mouth: it marks a man who acts without seeing what his act will bring. The goddess works on his φρένες, and the adjective says what she found there. See [[nepios]], [[pandarus]] and [[who-is-to-blame]].

**Friendly thoughts.** Machaon's drugs are the ones “which Chiron once gave to his father in friendship” (φίλα φρονέων, 4.219), “thinking kindly”. Agamemnon, making peace with Odysseus, says that his heart “knows kindly thoughts; for you think (φρονέεις) as I do” (4.360–361). See [[anemolios]].
