---

Gold marks the gods' world and the finest things of men's. The gods sit “on the golden floor” and pledge one another “with golden cups” (χρυσέῳ ἐν δαπέδῳ … χρυσέοις δεπάεσσι, 4.2–4). The craftsman who made Pandarus' bow from the wild goat's horns smoothed it and “set on it a golden tip” (χρυσέην … κορώνην, 4.111), the hook that holds the string. Athena guides the arrow to the place on Menelaus' body “where the golden clasps (ὀχῆες χρύσειοι) of the belt held it together, and the corselet met it in a double layer” (4.132–133).
