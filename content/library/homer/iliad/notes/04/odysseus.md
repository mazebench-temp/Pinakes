**The rebuke.** Agamemnon finds Odysseus standing with the Cephallenians beside Menestheus, both waiting for another column to begin the charge (4.329–335; see [[cephallenians]]). He speaks to Menestheus with a compliment to his father, and to Odysseus with an insult: κακοῖσι δόλοισι κεκασμένε κερδαλεόφρον, “you, skilled in evil tricks, with your mind set on gain” (4.339). The tricks (δόλοι) for which Helen named him from the wall (3.202) have become vices, and κερδαλεόφρων is the word Achilles threw at Agamemnon himself (1.149; see [[mind-set-on-gain]]). Then he rebukes them both for hanging back and feasting first (4.340–348; see [[menestheus]]).

**The answer.**

{{quote:0da97f4f-efab-55c3-9acf-086b8aed91c6}}

He looks at the king “darkly”, as he looked at Thersites (2.245; see [[looking-darkly]]), and begins with the exclamation for a word that should never have been spoken, “what a word has escaped the barrier of your teeth!” (see [[what-a-word]]). He does not explain why he was waiting. He offers a sight instead: the king will see “the dear father of Telemachus” among the champions. In Book 2 he swore by the same title that he would strip Thersites (2.259–260); here he stakes it on his courage (see [[telemachus]]). His last words give insult for insult: what the king says is “empty wind” (ἀνεμώλια, 4.355).

**The king gives way.** Agamemnon smiles, “when he saw that he was angry”, and takes back his word (4.356–357; see [[smiling-at-him]]). He addresses him with his full titles, “Son of Laertes, sprung from Zeus, Odysseus of many devices”, the words with which Athena sent him to save the army (2.173), says that he does not rebuke him beyond measure, “for you think as I do”, and promises to make it good later (4.358–363; see [[agamemnon]]). Of the leaders Agamemnon rebukes, Odysseus is the only one who protests, and the only one who is given amends.

**The promise kept.** In the first battle Antiphus, son of Priam, throws at Ajax and kills Odysseus' companion [[Leucus]] as he drags a corpse away (4.489–493). Odysseus, “very angry at heart”, goes “through the champions” (διὰ προμάχων, 4.495), where he told Agamemnon he would be seen (προμάχοισι, 4.354), and the Trojans shrink back from his throw. He kills [[Democoon]], a bastard son of Priam who had come from Abydos, with a spear through both temples, and the Trojan front and Hector himself give ground (4.494–505).
