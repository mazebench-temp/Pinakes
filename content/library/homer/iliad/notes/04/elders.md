summary: Agamemnon's review twice recalls the elders' feast, where the best of the Argives drink "the sparkling wine of the elders" (4.259–263) and Menestheus and Odysseus are the first called to table (4.343–346).
grc: [γερούσιον, γέρουσιν]
en: [of the elders, for the elders]
---

The γέροντες are not named as a council in Book 4, but their feast is, twice, in Agamemnon's review. He honors Idomeneus at the feast "when the sparkling wine of the elders is mixed in the mixing bowl by the best of the Argives", and Idomeneus' cup, like Agamemnon's own, always stands full (4.259–263). He rebukes Menestheus and Odysseus as "the first to hear my call to the feast whenever we Achaeans prepare a feast for the elders", where they eat roast meat and drink honey-sweet wine as long as they like (4.343–346). In both speeches the privilege of the table is a claim on courage in the field: the king who feeds his elders expects them in the front rank. See [[feast]], [[wine]] and [[the-review-of-the-army]].
