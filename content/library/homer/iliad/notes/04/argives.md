---
summary: Agamemnon addresses the army as Argives in both of his exhortations, the encouragement of the eager and the rebuke of the slack (4.234, 4.242). In the first battle the Argives drive the Trojans back until Apollo rouses them.
---

Both of Agamemnon's speeches to the ranks begin with the name. To the eager: “Argives, do not yet let go of your furious courage” (4.234). Father Zeus will not help liars, the oath-breakers' flesh will feed the vultures, and their wives and children will be carried off in the ships (4.235–239). To the slack: “Argives, you arrow-fighters, you disgraces, have you no shame?” (4.242). They stand dazed like fawns worn out from running, and wait for the Trojans to reach the ships (4.243–249; see [[like-fawns]]). The meaning of ἰόμωροι, rendered “arrow-fighters”, was already uncertain in antiquity (see [[obscure-iomoroi]]). See [[agamemnon]] and [[the-review]].

The best of the Argives mix the elders' wine at the king's feasts (4.259–260), and the Ajaxes are the “leaders of the bronze-armored Argives” (4.285). In the first battle “the Argives shouted aloud, and dragged away the dead”, and drove the Trojans back, until Apollo called to the Trojans from Pergamus that the Argives' flesh was not stone or iron (4.506–511).
