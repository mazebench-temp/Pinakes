---
summary: The words for death are spoken in Book 4 and not narrated: Zeus, Agamemnon and Idomeneus use them, but when men die in the first battle the narrator tells it without them.
grc: [θανέεσθαι, θάνῃς]
passages:
  - 4.155-157 | Agamemnon's lament
  - 4.266-271 | Idomeneus answers
---

In Book 4 θάνατος and θνῄσκω are spoken, not narrated. Zeus teases Hera that Aphrodite has saved Paris, “when he thought that he would die” (ὀϊόμενον θανέεσθαι, 4.12). Agamemnon, holding his wounded brother, says “it was your death (θάνατον), then, that I swore in the oaths” (4.155), and fears for him “if you die” (αἴ κε θάνῃς, 4.170). Idomeneus says of the Trojans who broke the oaths, “for them there will be death (θάνατος) and sorrows hereafter” (4.270).

When men die, at the end of the book, the narrator does not use these words. He says that darkness covered a man's eyes, that his limbs were loosened, that his spirit left him or was breathed out, and that he fell like a tower or a poplar (4.461–462, 4.469–470, 4.482, 4.503–504, 4.524–526).
