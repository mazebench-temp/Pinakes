**Ready.** The review begins with the Cretans. Idomeneus stands among the champions (πρόμαχοι), “like a boar in courage” (συῒ εἴκελος ἀλκήν, 4.253; see [[like-a-boar]]), while [[Meriones]] urges on the rearmost battalions (4.254). Agamemnon rejoices and speaks to him “with soothing words” (μειλιχίοισιν, 4.256):

{{quote:feda20d5-6b6a-59ab-9d28-2a00672292eb}}

The praise is of honor shown at table. The best of the Argives mix “the sparkling wine of the elders” (γερούσιον οἶνον) in the bowl, and the others drink a measured portion (δαιτρόν), but Idomeneus' cup stands full like the king's, to drink whenever his heart bids. He is one of the elders whom Agamemnon calls to his feasts (2.405; see [[elders]] and [[feast]]), and a later book makes him “half-gray” (μεσαιπόλιος, 13.361). The last line turns the praise into a demand: “be the man you have always boasted you are” (4.264; εὔχεαι, see [[euchomai]]).

**The answer.** Idomeneus, “leader of the Cretans” (Κρητῶν ἀγός, 4.265), accepts and sends the king on. “Son of Atreus, I will indeed be your trusty companion” (ἐρίηρος ἑταῖρος), “as I promised at first and nodded my assent” (4.266–267). The promise may be the one the leaders made when they set out from Argos, which Odysseus recalled when the army wanted to go home (2.286–288). Then he tells Agamemnon to urge on the other long-haired Achaeans, since the Trojans have confounded the oaths, and “for them there will be death and sorrows hereafter” (4.268–271; see [[oaths-and-their-breaking]]). He needs no urging, and says so. Agamemnon passes on “glad at heart” (4.272). Of the three leaders he finds ready, Idomeneus is the only one to whom he gives an order.
