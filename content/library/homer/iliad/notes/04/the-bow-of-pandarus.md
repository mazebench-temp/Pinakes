---
summary: Book 4 tells a second story of the bow. Pandarus made it himself from the horns of a wild goat he had shot from ambush, and a horn-worker joined the horns and tipped them with gold (4.105–111). With it he breaks the truce.
passages:
  - 4.105-111 | The wild goat and the bow
---

The Catalogue said that Apollo himself gave Pandarus his bow (2.827). Book 4 tells how the bow was made, and Apollo has no part in it. Pandarus lay in wait for a wild goat, hit it under the breast as it came out from a rock, and took its horns, sixteen palms long; a craftsman who worked in horn dressed them and fitted them together, smoothed the whole, and set a golden tip on it (4.105–111; see [[goats]] and [[bow]]). The two stories can stand together if Apollo's gift is the archer's skill, or his favor, rather than the weapon. Pandarus prays to the god as he shoots, to “Apollo, the Lycian-born, famed for his bow” (4.119; see [[lycian-born]]), and the poem does not say that Apollo heard.

The bow is uncovered only to break the truce (4.105, 4.112–126; see [[pandarus-breaks-the-truce]]). In Book 5 Pandarus complains that it has hit two of the best Achaeans and only roused them, and swears to break it and throw it in the fire if he ever gets home (5.206–216). He does not get home.
