summary: Zeus counts libation among the gods' due at Troy (4.49), and Agamemnon names “the libations of unmixed wine” among the pledges of the oath the Trojans have broken, in Nestor's line from Book 2 (4.159 = 2.341).
grc: [λοιβῆς]
en: [libation]
---

**The gods' due.** Zeus loved Troy because his altar there never lacked “an equal share of the feast, libation and the savor of the burnt fat; for that is the prize allotted to us” (λοιβῆς τε κνίσης τε, 4.48–49). λοιβή is the noun of λείβω, “pour in drops”, the verb of Chryses' libation in Book 1 (1.463). The same two lines return in Book 24, of the offerings of Hector (24.69–70). See [[sacrifice]] and [[altar]].

**The pledge.** Over his wounded brother Agamemnon says that the oath is not in vain, “and the blood of lambs, and the libations of unmixed wine and the right hands in which we trusted” (4.158–159). The second line is Nestor's, from Book 2, where it named the compacts of the expedition (2.341). Here it names the rite of Book 3, where wine was drawn from the bowl and poured on the ground with a curse (3.295–301). σπονδαί, the libations that seal an agreement, gave later Greek its word for a truce. See [[oath]] and [[wine]].
