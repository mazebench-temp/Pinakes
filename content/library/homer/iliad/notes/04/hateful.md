---
summary: Agamemnon rebukes the men he sees “holding back from hateful war.”
grc: [στυγεροῦ]
---

“And those whom he saw holding back from hateful war, these he would rebuke with angry words” (4.240–241). The epithet of war is the narrator's, and it explains the men who hang back: war is what everyone hates ([[polemos]]). Agamemnon's rebuke compares them to fawns standing dazed on a plain (4.242–246; [[arrow-fighters]], [[like-fawns]]). In Book 2 he had called the army to “the hateful work of Ares” ({{ref:Iliad 2.385}}).
