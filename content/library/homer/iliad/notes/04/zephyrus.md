---
summary: The West Wind blows in two similes of Book 4, both over the sea. It drives the storm cloud that a goatherd sees coming from his lookout, and it raises the waves that break on the shore.
grc: [Ζεφύροιο, Ζεφύρου]
---

Zephyrus blows twice in Book 4, both times in a simile and both times over the sea. A goatherd on his lookout sees a cloud “coming over the sea before the blast of the West Wind” (ὑπὸ Ζεφύροιο ἰωῆς, 4.276), blacker than pitch and bringing a great storm, and he drives his flocks into a cave: so the battalions of the two Ajaxes move into battle (4.275–282; see [[storm-cloud]]). When the armies are about to meet, the Achaean companies come on like the swell of the sea “stirred by the West Wind” (Ζεφύρου ὕπο κινήσαντος, 4.423), which rears its head on the open sea and then breaks roaring on the shore (4.422–428; see [[waves-on-the-shore]]). The verb is the one used of the West Wind in Book 2, when it stirs a deep field of grain (κινήσῃ, 2.147; see [[wind-in-the-wheat]]). In the Iliad this is a strong wind that sets things moving.
