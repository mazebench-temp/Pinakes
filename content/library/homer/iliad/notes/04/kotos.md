---
summary: Agamemnon foresees Zeus shaking his dark aegis over the Trojans “in resentment at this deceit” (τῆσδʼ ἀπάτης κοτέων), a grudge that will be fulfilled late.
grc: [κοτέων]
passages:
  - 4.158-168 | The oath not in vain
---

κότος is anger kept “until he has fulfilled it” (1.82), and Agamemnon's speech over his brother gives Zeus exactly such a grudge. The oath and the blood of lambs are not in vain; “even if the Olympian has not fulfilled it at once, he fulfills it in the end, even late” (4.160–161). A day will come when Ilios perishes, and Zeus will shake his aegis over the Trojans, “in resentment (κοτέων) at this deceit” (4.166–168). The delay belongs to the grudge: it waits, and it does not forget. See [[fulfilled-late]], [[a-day-will-come]] and [[apate]].
