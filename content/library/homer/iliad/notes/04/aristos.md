---
summary: Book 4 weighs men as better and worse. Agamemnon says that Diomedes is worse than his father in battle and better in assembly; Sthenelus answers that the sons are far better than their fathers, and took Thebes against a stronger wall.
grc: [ἄρειον]
passages:
  - 4.370-400 | Agamemnon rebukes Diomedes
  - 4.404-410 | Sthenelus answers Agamemnon
---

The best men of the army are gathered around the wounded Menelaus (ὅσσοι ἄριστοι, 4.211), and the best of the Argives mix the elders' wine (4.260). But the book is more interested in comparisons than in superlatives. Agamemnon ends his story of Tydeus with a verdict on his son: “the son he fathered is worse (χέρεια) than he in battle, though better (ἀμείνω) in assembly” (4.400). Sthenelus reverses it: “We boast that we are far better (ἀμείνονες) than our fathers; we even took the seat of seven-gated Thebes, leading a smaller army under a stronger wall (ὑπὸ τεῖχος ἄρειον)” (4.405–407). ἄρειον, “stronger”, is the comparative of the family of ἄριστος; the same letters can be read Ἄρειον, “of Ares”, which would make the wall of Thebes the war god's (see [[wall-of-ares]]).
