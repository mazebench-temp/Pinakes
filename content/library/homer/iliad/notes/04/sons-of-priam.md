summary: On Olympus the sons of Priam are named with their father as the household Hera wants destroyed. In the first battle two of them fight, Antiphus, who kills Odysseus' companion Leucus, and the bastard Democoon, the first son of Priam to die in the poem.
grc: ["Πριάμοιό τε παῖδας"]
refs: [4.28, 4.490, 4.499]
---

**On Olympus.** Hera gathered the army “to bring evils on Priam and his sons” (4.28), and Zeus asks her what evils “Priam and the sons of Priam” have done to her, that she would go in through the gates and “eat Priam raw, and the sons of Priam, and the other Trojans” (4.31–36). The sons are named with their father as the house the war is to destroy, as they were in Agamemnon's oath, which bound “Priam and the sons of Priam” to pay (3.288). See [[priam]] and [[hera]].

**In the battle.** Two sons of Priam fight in the first battle. Antiphus “of the flashing corselet, son of Priam” throws at Ajax, misses, and kills Leucus, a companion of Odysseus (4.489–493; see [[antiphus-son-of-priam]]). Odysseus, angry for his friend, kills “a bastard son of Priam, Democoon”, who had come from Abydos, with a spear through both temples (4.499–504; see [[democoon]]). Democoon is the first of Priam's sons to die in the Iliad. Many follow him, and in the last book Priam counts them: of his fifty sons, furious Ares has loosened the knees of most (24.495–498).
