summary: The gods' assembly asks the question of the duel, war or friendship, and the men below repeat it in the gods' words. Hera has worn herself out gathering an army, as men do; the goddesses show their anger in the two ways Achilles showed his; and Zeus and Hera settle their quarrel as kings would, by an exchange.

**The same question.** Zeus asks the gods “whether we shall rouse evil war again and the dread din of battle, or put friendship between the two sides” (4.15–16). When Athena comes down like a star, the men of both armies ask each other the same thing in the same words: “Surely evil war and the dread din of battle will come again, or else friendship is set between the two sides by Zeus” (4.82–84). The question put on Olympus is repeated on the plain by men who do not know that it has been answered. See [[philotes]] and [[tis-speech]].

**Gathering an army.** Hera protests that Zeus would make her labor vain, “the sweat I sweated in my toil; my horses were worn out as I gathered the army (λαὸν ἀγειρούσῃ), to bring evils on Priam and his sons” (4.26–28). The goddess did what men did before the war: Nestor remembers going with Odysseus to the house of Peleus, “gathering the army throughout Achaea” (λαὸν ἀγείροντες, 11.769–770). In Agamemnon's story Tydeus came to Mycenae with Polynices “gathering an army” (λαὸν ἀγείρων, 4.377) against Thebes. War is raised the same way by gods and men.

**Two angers.** Zeus' proposal angers both goddesses. “Athena kept silent and said nothing, sulking at father Zeus, and a savage anger gripped her; but Hera's breast could not contain her anger, and she spoke” (4.22–24). In Book 1 Achilles did both: he held back his sword at Athena's word and spoke his anger aloud (1.188–244). See [[cholos]] and [[silence]].

**A bargain of kings.** Zeus and Hera settle their quarrel as kings settle theirs, by an exchange. Zeus gives up the city he loves, Hera offers three of hers, and each says what the other may do (4.37–67). In Book 1 the kings' quarrel was over a prize and could not be settled; on Olympus a whole city changes hands in thirty lines, and the gods go on with their feast. See [[the-bargain-of-zeus-and-hera]].
