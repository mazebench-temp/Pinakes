---
summary: Book 4 gives the epithet to Pandarus, “noble and strong,” as Athena finds him, and to Asclepius, “the noble healer.”
grc: [ἀμύμονος]
---

Athena finds “the son of Lycaon, noble and strong” (4.89; [[krateros]]), and a hundred lines later Agamemnon sends for Machaon, “the son of Asclepius, the noble healer” (4.194; [[asclepius]]). The same word praises the man who breaks the truce and the father of the man who heals its first wound. Of Pandarus it says nothing about his conduct: a few lines later he is “the fool” whose mind Athena persuades (4.104). It is a clear case of the epithet as general praise, and the translation's “noble” suits both men ([[pandarus]], [[machaon]]).
