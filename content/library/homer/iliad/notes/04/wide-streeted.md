In Book 2 the wide-streeted city was always Troy, the city the Achaeans hoped to take ({{ref:Iliad 2.12}}, {{ref:Iliad 2.329}}). Here the epithet goes to Agamemnon's own city, and Hera offers it for destruction: Zeus may sack Argos, Sparta and wide-streeted Mycenae whenever they become hateful to him, if Troy may fall ([[the-bargain-of-zeus-and-hera]], [[mycenae]], [[argive]]). The formula that measured the prize now measures the price. Readers have asked whether the lines point to the later fall of these cities; the poem itself never tells of Zeus sacking them.
