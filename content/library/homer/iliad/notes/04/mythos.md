**A word taken back.** Agamemnon has rebuked Menestheus and Odysseus, and Odysseus has answered that his words are empty wind (4.338–355). Agamemnon smiles, “when he saw that he was angry, and he took back his word” (πάλιν δʼ ὅ γε λάζετο μῦθον, 4.357). In Book 1 Zeus told Thetis that no word he confirms with a nod can be taken back (παλινάγρετον, 1.526; see [[no-word-taken-back]]). A king among men can do what Zeus will not, and Agamemnon, who would not take back his words to Achilles, takes these back at once. See [[anemolios]] and [[praise-and-blame]].

**Words as a prize.** Nestor, too old to fight, will be among the horsemen and command them “with counsel and with words (βουλῇ καὶ μύθοισι); for that is the prize of the old” (4.322–323; see [[prize-of-the-old]] and [[boule]]).

**A word obeyed.** Diomedes tells Sthenelus, “Friend, sit in silence, and obey my word” (ἐμῷ δʼ ἐπιπείθεο μύθῳ, 4.412), the half-line of Zeus to Hera in Book 1 (1.565). Hera begins her protest in Book 4 with the same noun: “Most dread son of Cronus, what a thing (μῦθον) you have said!” (4.25). See [[silence]].
