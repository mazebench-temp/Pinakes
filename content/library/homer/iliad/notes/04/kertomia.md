summary: Zeus opens Book 4 by provoking Hera “with cutting words, speaking with sly intent” (κερτομίοις ἐπέεσσι παραβλήδην ἀγορεύων): he praises Aphrodite's care for Paris and proposes peace.
grc: [κερτομίοις]
passages:
  - 4.1-19 | Zeus teases Hera and Athena
---

The gods are feasting on Olympus and looking down on Troy when “at once the son of Cronus set about provoking Hera with cutting words, speaking with sly intent” (4.5–6). The speech that follows does not insult anyone. It compares. Two goddesses are Menelaus' helpers, Argive Hera and Athena of Alalcomenae, and they sit apart and look on, while Aphrodite stands by Paris and has just saved him (4.7–12). Then Zeus proposes peace: Menelaus has won, so let the city stand and Menelaus take Helen home (4.13–19). The cut is in the comparison, and in a proposal he must know Hera will not bear.

παραβλήδην, which comes only here in Homer, is explained in two ways: “provocatively, with a hidden meaning”, words that say one thing and aim at another, or “by comparison”, setting Aphrodite beside Hera and Athena. The translation gives “with sly intent”. Either way the words work. Athena sulks in silence, Hera cannot contain her anger (4.20–24), and the quarrel ends with Athena sent down to break the truce (4.25–72). Whether Zeus wanted peace or wanted to be refused, the poem does not say. See [[peira]], [[zeus]] and [[the-bargain-of-zeus-and-hera]].
