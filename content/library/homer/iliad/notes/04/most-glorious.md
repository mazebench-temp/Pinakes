---
summary: Book 4 gives the superlative in the feminine to Athena, “most glorious Tritogeneia,” as she rouses the Achaeans.
grc: [κυδίστη]
---

Apollo shouts to the Trojans from Pergamus, and Athena answers among the Achaeans: “the Achaeans were roused by the daughter of Zeus, most glorious Tritogeneia” (4.514–515; [[tritogeneia]], [[daughter-of-zeus]]). In Books 1–3 the superlative was an address to Agamemnon and to Zeus. Here it goes to the goddess at the moment the gods take sides in the battle. The same verse names Athena in the Odyssey, when Nestor recognizes her (Od. 3.378).
