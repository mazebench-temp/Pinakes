**The wife of Zeus.** Hera claims her standing “on both counts, by birth and because I am called your wife (σὴ παράκοιτις), and you are lord among all the immortals” (4.60–61). Her marriage is part of her rank, and she uses it to make a bargain with her husband over the cities of men (see [[the-bargain-of-zeus-and-hera]]).

**Wives as the price.** The oath of Book 3 cursed its breakers with the loss of their wives, “subdued by others” (3.301). Agamemnon repeats the curse as a law: the breakers “pay a great price, with their own heads and their wives and their children” (4.162). In the review he makes it a promise: “we will lead away their dear wives and little children in our ships, when we take the citadel” (4.238–239). The war fought to bring one wife home will end with the enemy's wives taken from theirs. See [[captive-women]] and [[oaths-and-their-breaking]].
