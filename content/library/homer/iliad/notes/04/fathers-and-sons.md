**Names.** Almost every man in Book 4 is named with his father. Pandarus is “the son of Lycaon” (4.89, 4.93), Machaon “the son of Asclepius” (4.194, 4.204), Menestheus “the son of Peteos” (4.327, 4.338), Diomedes “the son of Tydeus” and Sthenelus “the son of Capaneus” (4.365, 4.367). In the first battle the dead are named the same way: Echepolus, son of Thalysias; Simoeisius, son of Anthemion; Diores, son of Amarynceus; Peirous, son of Imbrasus (4.458, 4.488, 4.517, 4.520). Odysseus names himself by his son instead, “the dear father of Telemachus” (4.354; see [[pater]]).

**Tydeus and his son.** The review ends with the book's longest speech about a father. Agamemnon asks Diomedes why he cowers, and answers himself with the story of Tydeus: a guest at Mycenae, an envoy alone among the Cadmeians, the victor in all their contests and the killer of fifty men in an ambush. He heard it from others, for he never saw Tydeus himself (4.370–399). His verdict comes in the last line:

Diomedes says nothing, in awe of the king (4.401–402). His companion answers for both sons, and turns the verdict upside down: “We boast that we are far better than our fathers” (4.405). They took Thebes, which the fathers failed to take; the fathers died by their own recklessness (4.406–409; see [[better-than-our-fathers]] and [[atasthalie]]). Diomedes silences him and goes to fight (4.412–421). The son is judged by the father, and answers by what he does: the next book is his.

**A father's gift.** Machaon draws the arrow from Menelaus and spreads on the wound “soothing drugs, which Chiron once gave to his father in friendship” (4.218–219). A father's knowledge is his son's inheritance. See [[machaon]], [[asclepius]] and [[healing]].

**The first kill.** The first man killed in the poem is killed by a son of old Nestor, Antilochus (4.457–462). Earlier in the book Nestor has told Agamemnon that the spears belong to the younger men now (4.324–325). Book 4 does not say whose son Antilochus is; the next book calls him “son of great-hearted Nestor” (5.565; see [[antilochus]]).

**The parents of Simoeisius.** Ajax kills a young man born by the Simoeis as his mother came down from Ida with her parents, “but he did not repay his dear parents for his rearing, and short was his life” (4.473–479; see [[threptra]] and [[simoeisius]]). In the same battle Odysseus kills a bastard son of Priam (4.499; see [[nothos]]).
