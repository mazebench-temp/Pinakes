πυρῆς ἐπιβάντʼ ἀλεγεινῆς is literally “having mounted the grievous pyre”, a phrase for dying: the Calydonian boar “set many men on the grievous pyre” (9.546). Athena promises Pandarus gifts from Alexander if Paris sees his rival dead and burned. The pyre is the proof of a death, and the sight is the reward (see [[pandarus]] and [[paris]]). Menelaus does not die, and Agamemnon's lament imagines another end for him, a body rotting in Troy and a Trojan leaping on his tomb (4.174–177; see [[tomb]]).
