**Sent for.** When Menelaus has calmed him, Agamemnon turns practical: “a healer will examine the wound and lay on it drugs that will stop the black pains” (4.190–191). He sends [[Talthybius]] for Machaon, “the man who is the son of Asclepius, the noble healer” (4.194). The son is named by the father's craft, as the Catalogue called him and his brother “good healers” (2.732; see [[asclepius]] and [[podalirius]]). The herald finds him as Athena found Pandarus, standing among the strong ranks of his shield-bearers, men “who had followed him from horse-pasturing Tricca” (4.200–202 ~ 4.89–91; see [[tricca]]). The same lines introduce the man who wounds and the man who heals. The message “stirred the heart in his breast” (4.208), and he goes.

**The healing.**

{{quote:84d9aa0b-c27e-587f-8648-27da907075ba}}

He comes into the circle of the best men, “the man equal to the gods” (4.212; see [[equal-to-the-gods]]), and works in order. He draws the arrow back out of the belt, and its barbs break as it comes; he loosens belt, kilt and band; he looks at the wound; he sucks out the blood; and he spreads on it ἤπια φάρμακα, “soothing drugs”. He does it εἰδώς, “knowing” how, which the translation renders “skillfully” (4.218). In Book 11 Idomeneus will say that a healer is worth many other men, to cut out arrows and spread soothing drugs, when Machaon himself has been hit by an arrow of Paris (11.504–515).

**Chiron's drugs.** The drugs came to his father from [[Chiron]], who gave them “in friendship” (4.219). The knowledge passes from the Centaur to Asclepius, and from Asclepius to his son. See [[healing]] and [[chiron-and-asclepius]].

Like Pandarus, Machaon says nothing in Book 4. One man wounds and the other heals, and neither speaks.
