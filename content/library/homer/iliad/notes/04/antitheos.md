---
summary: Book 4 calls Pandarus “godlike” as Athena looks for him, and Polynices “godlike” in the story of Tydeus.
grc: [ἀντιθέῳ]
---

Athena goes through the Trojan throng “looking for godlike Pandarus” (4.88; [[pandarus]]). The honorific stands at the start of the scene in which the narrator calls him “the fool” whose mind the goddess persuaded (4.104). The second use is in Agamemnon's story: Tydeus came to Mycenae “with godlike Polynices, gathering an army” against Thebes (4.377; [[polynices]]). As in Books 1 and 3, the epithet honors a hero of an earlier war ([[the-seven-against-thebes]]).
