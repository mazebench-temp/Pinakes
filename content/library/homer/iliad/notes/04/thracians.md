summary: The Thracian leader Peirous, from Aenus, kills the Epeian Diores and is killed by Thoas, and his Thracians, “with the topknots”, keep his body from being stripped (4.517–538).

The Thracians are the first of Troy's allies to kill in the poem and the first to lose a leader. “The leader of the Thracian men” (Θρῃκῶν ἀγὸς ἀνδρῶν, 4.519) is Peirous, and Book 4 gives him a home, Aenus, on the coast of Thrace at the mouth of the Hebrus (4.520; see [[peirous]] and [[aenus]]). When Thoas kills him, his men close around the body:

{{quote:11f8b239-70df-50b8-9e67-53609aa28116}}

Θρήϊκες ἀκρόκομοι: ἀκρόκομος = ἄκρος (“at the top”) + κόμη (“hair”), “with hair on the crown of the head”, the Thracian way of wearing it (see [[topknotted]]). The Abantes, who lose their leader earlier in the same battle, wear their hair long at the back (2.542; see [[abantes]]). δολίχʼ ἔγχεα, “long spears”, keep Thoas at a distance, and the Thracians push him back, “big and mighty and noble as he was” (4.534; see [[thoas]]). The dead leaders lie side by side, and the line names their peoples: “one of the Thracians, the other of the bronze-armored Epeians” (4.537; see [[epeians]]).
