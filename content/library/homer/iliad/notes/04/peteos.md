ὦ υἱὲ Πετεῶο διοτρεφέος βασιλῆος, “Son of Peteos, the king whom Zeus has nurtured” (4.338). The epithet is in the genitive and belongs to Peteos, not to Menestheus. It is the only description Homer gives of the father, and the herald Thootes repeats it in Book 12, “the dear son of Peteos, whom Zeus has nurtured” (12.355; see [[zeus-nurtured]]). Here it comes at the start of a rebuke of the son. Agamemnon honors the father before he shames the son, as he will measure Diomedes against Tydeus (4.370–400). The narrator too has just named Menestheus as “the son of Peteos” (4.327). See [[menestheus]].
