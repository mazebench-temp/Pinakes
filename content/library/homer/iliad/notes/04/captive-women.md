In Book 1 the captive women were the prizes of raids on the towns of the Troad (see the note on Book 1). In Book 4 they are the future of Troy itself. The curse of the oath had asked that the wives of whoever broke it “be subdued by others” (3.301). When the Trojans have broken it, Agamemnon tells the eager Argives what their victory will bring: vultures will eat the oath-breakers' flesh, “and we will lead away their dear wives and little children in our ships, when we take the citadel” (4.237–239). The women of the city are named with its children as the spoils of the sack, and the words the army hears as encouragement are the words of the Trojans' curse fulfilled.
