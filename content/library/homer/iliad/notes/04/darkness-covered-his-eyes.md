---
summary: The formula ends three of the seven killings of the first battle, the first deaths in the poem: Echepolus, killed by Antilochus (4.461), Democoon, killed by Odysseus (4.503), and Diores, killed by Peirous (4.526).
---

The first two uses share a whole verse. In each the spear enters the head, a verb of piercing ends the line before, and its subject, “the bronze point”, runs over into the next verse, which the formula completes:

- ἐν δὲ μετώπῳ πῆξε, πέρησε δʼ ἄρʼ ὀστέον εἴσω / αἰχμὴ χαλκείη· τὸν δὲ σκότος ὄσσε κάλυψεν, “and fixed it in his forehead, and it pierced into the bone, the bronze point; and darkness covered his eyes” (4.460–461), of Echepolus, the first man killed in the Iliad (see [[echepolus]] and [[antilochus]]);
- ἣ δʼ ἑτέροιο διὰ κροτάφοιο πέρησεν / αἰχμὴ χαλκείη· τὸν δὲ σκότος ὄσσε κάλυψε, “and it passed through the other temple, the bronze point; and darkness covered his eyes” (4.502–503), of Democoon (see [[democoon]]).

Each is followed by a fall: Echepolus “fell like a tower in the fierce battle” (4.462; see [[like-a-tower]]), and Democoon “fell with a thud, and his armor clattered upon him” (4.504; see [[fell-with-a-thud]]).

The third death is slower. Diores' ankle has been crushed by a stone, and he lies on his back in the dust, stretching out his hands to his companions, “breathing out his life”; Peirous runs up and stabs him beside the navel, “and all his bowels poured out on the ground, and darkness covered his eyes” (4.521–526; see [[diores]] and [[peirous]]). Here the darkness comes at the end of the verse and the end of the man, with no fall to follow, for he has already fallen.

Of the other killings of the book, Elephenor's is ended by [[loosened-his-limbs]] (4.469), Simoeisius' by a simile (4.482–487), Peirous' by “took away his life” (4.531), and Leucus' by his fall over the body he was dragging (4.493). See [[androktasia]].
