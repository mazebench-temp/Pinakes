---
summary: Book 4 names Zeus by his father as he provokes Hera, in Hera's angry address, in the simile of the star, in Agamemnon's prophecy, and in his rebuke of the army.
---

The patronymic follows Zeus through the book. “At once the son of Cronus set about provoking Hera” (4.5), and she answers “Most dread son of Cronus” (4.25; [[most-dread]]). In the simile, Athena comes down “as the son of crooked-counseling Cronus sends a star” (4.75; [[crooked-counseling]]). Agamemnon's prophecy names “Zeus, son of Cronus, throned on high” as the god who will punish Troy (4.166; [[high-throned]]), and his rebuke asks whether the army is waiting “to see if the son of Cronus will hold his hand over you” (4.249). In Book 3 the son of Cronus would not yet fulfill the prayer spoken over the oath ({{ref:Iliad 3.302}}); Book 4 shows how he lets the oath be broken.
