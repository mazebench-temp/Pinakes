  - 4.457-504 | Spears in the first battle
---

**Thrown and thrust.** Five of the seven killings of the first battle are done with the spear (see [[androktasia]]). Antilochus' spear goes through the ridge of Echepolus' helmet into his forehead, “the bronze point” (αἰχμὴ χαλκείη, 4.459–461). Agenor stabs Elephenor with his “bronze-fitted spear” (ξυστῷ χαλκήρεϊ, 4.469); the ξυστόν is a long spear for thrusting, and the naval pike Ajax wields at the ships is one, twenty-two cubits long (15.677–678). Ajax's spear goes through Simoeisius' chest and out at the shoulder (4.480–481). Antiphus “threw his sharp spear through the throng” at Ajax and hit Leucus (ἀκόντισεν, 4.489–492), and Odysseus comes close and “threw his shining spear”, “and he did not throw the shaft in vain” (4.496–498): the βέλος of 4.498 is his spear. Peirous stabs the fallen Diores beside the navel (οὖτα, 4.525), and Thoas strikes Peirous in the chest, pulls “the heavy spear” out, and finishes him with the sword (4.527–531). The Thracians who save the body hold “long spears” (δολίχʼ ἔγχεα, 4.533; see [[long-spears]]).

**Nestor.** Nestor tells his horsemen that “whoever from his own chariot reaches an enemy's chariot, let him thrust with his spear” (ἔγχει ὀρεξάσθω, 4.306–307; see [[battle-order]]). And to Agamemnon's wish that he were young again he answers that he will give counsel: “the spears will be wielded by the younger men” (αἰχμὰς δʼ αἰχμάσσουσι, 4.324). αἰχμάζω, “wield the spear”, occurs only here in Homer, made from αἰχμή to set noun and verb side by side (see [[old-age-and-memory]] and [[nestor]]).

**Ranks.** The Ajaxes' battalions come on “bristling with shields and spears” (σάκεσίν τε καὶ ἔγχεσι, 4.282), and when the armies meet “they dashed together their shields of hide, and their spears” (4.447; see [[the-armies-clash]]).
