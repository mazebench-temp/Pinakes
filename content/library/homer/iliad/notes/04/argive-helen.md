---
summary: Zeus proposes that Menelaus take “Argive Helen” home and the war end; Agamemnon fears leaving her to the Trojans as a thing to boast of.
---

The phrase frames the two outcomes of Book 4. Zeus opens the council by proposing peace: the city of Priam would still be inhabited, “and Menelaus would take Argive Helen back home” (4.18–19). The gods refuse, and the oath is broken. When Menelaus is wounded, Agamemnon fears the other outcome: the army going home and leaving “to Priam and the Trojans a thing to boast of, Argive Helen” (4.173–174; [[thirsty]]). The words are almost those of Hera and of Athena in Book 2, who feared that the Argives would flee and “leave” Helen to the Trojans ({{ref:Iliad 2.160-161}}, {{ref:Iliad 2.176-177}}). The king now says “we would leave” (λίποιμεν): the fear that the goddesses spoke for the army is his own ([[the-bargain-of-zeus-and-hera]], [[helen]]).
