The verb μενεαίνω, “be furiously eager, rage”, is formed on μένος. Zeus asks Hera what great evils Priam and his sons have done her, “that you rage (μενεαίνεις) so furiously to sack the well-built citadel of Ilios?” (4.31–33). Her fury is a goddess' will to destruction, and Zeus answers that only eating Priam raw would heal it (4.34–36; see [[cholos]]). The same verb gives Pandarus' arrow a warrior's will: it leapt from the string, “sharp-pointed, eager (μενεαίνων) to fly into the throng” (4.126).

When the armies close, the noun comes in its plural: “they dashed together their shields of hide, and their spears, and the fury of men (μένεʼ ἀνδρῶν) in bronze corselets” (4.447–448). The μένος of each man is counted among the things that collide, with the shields and the spears. The passage returns word for word when the armies meet in Book 8 (8.60–65).
