---
summary: The epithet names Menelaus four times in Book 4, most often in the line-end phrase “warlike Menelaus, the son of Atreus,” as the arrow wounds him and the healer is summoned.
grc: [ἀρήϊον, ἀρήϊοι]
---

The phrase Μενέλαον ἀρήϊον Ἀτρέος υἱόν fills the second half of the verse three times. Athena uses it to Pandarus: Paris would give him gifts if he saw “warlike Menelaus, the son of Atreus, brought down by your arrow” (4.98–99). The narrator uses it as Pandarus' companions hold their shields in front of him, “so that the warlike sons of the Achaeans would not spring up before warlike Menelaus, the son of Atreus, was struck” (4.114–115; [[sons-of-the-achaeans]]). Agamemnon uses it when he sends for Machaon (4.195), and the herald, repeating the message, calls him “warlike Menelaus, leader of the Achaeans” (4.205). The warrior's epithet stays with him while he stands wounded ([[son-of-atreus]], [[menelaus]]).
