---
summary: Agamemnon laments his wounded brother “groaning heavily”, and his companions groan with him (4.153–154).
---

{{quote:c0711eda-b774-5db9-9728-99640c92c26d}}

The speech is introduced by a variant of the line: τοῖς δὲ βαρὺ στενάχων μετέφη κρείων Ἀγαμέμνων, “Groaning heavily, powerful Agamemnon spoke among them” (4.153). μετέφη, “spoke among”, replaces προσέφη, “spoke to”: the king speaks to Menelaus, holding his hand, but his lament is heard by the companions around them, and “his companions groaned with him” (ἐπεστενάχοντο δʼ ἑταῖροι, 4.154). The grief is shared. In Book 1 the participle described Achilles before his mother (1.364); here it describes a brother who thinks his brother is dying. Agamemnon groans heavily again when he speaks to the army in Book 9, proposing that they sail home (ὣς ὃ βαρὺ στενάχων ἔπεʼ Ἀργείοισι μετηύδα, 9.16). See [[agamemnon]] and [[menelaus]].
