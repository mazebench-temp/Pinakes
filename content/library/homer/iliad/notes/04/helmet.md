---
summary: The first killing of the poem goes through the ridge of a helmet: Antilochus strikes Echepolus “on the ridge of his horsehair-crested helmet”, in the words of Menelaus' sword-stroke in Book 3 (4.459).
---

“Antilochus was the first to kill a helmeted man of the Trojans” (κορυστήν, 4.457). κορυστής, “helmeted”, is a man in armor, from κόρυς, “helmet” (see [[helmeted]]). Antilochus' spear strikes “the ridge of his horsehair-crested helmet” (κόρυθος φάλον ἱπποδασείης) and goes on into the forehead and the bone (4.459–461). The words are those of Menelaus' sword-stroke on Paris' helmet in Book 3, where the sword broke on the ridge (3.362, 3.369). Here the bronze goes through. The same three verses give Ajax his first killing in Book 6 (6.9–11). See [[androktasia]], [[antilochus]], [[echepolus]] and [[horsehair-crested]].

The verb κορύσσω, “helmet, arm”, is used twice more in the book in a sense of its own. The wave on the shore first “rears its head” on the open sea (κορύσσεται, 4.424), and so does Strife, who “is small when first she rears her head” and then plants it against the sky (4.442). The image is of something that raises a crest, as a warrior helmets himself for battle (see [[waves-on-the-shore]] and [[strife]]).
