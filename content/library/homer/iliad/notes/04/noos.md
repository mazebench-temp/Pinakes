  - 4.301-309 | Nestor instructs his horsemen
---

Nestor gives his horsemen their orders: no one is to ride out alone ahead of the others, and no one is to fall back; a man who reaches an enemy chariot from his own should thrust with his spear. “This is how the men of old sacked cities and walls, keeping this purpose (νόον) and this spirit in their breasts” (4.303–309). νόος here is a plan held in the mind, the discipline that makes the men of a line act together. See [[nestor]], [[battle-order]] and [[old-age-and-memory]].

The verb comes once, of seeing: Talthybius goes through the army looking for Machaon, “and he saw him” (τὸν δὲ νόησεν) standing among his men (4.200–201).
