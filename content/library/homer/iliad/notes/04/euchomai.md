summary: Book 4 uses the word in all its senses. Pandarus vows a hecatomb to Apollo; Agamemnon fears leaving the Trojans “a thing to boast of” and tells Idomeneus to be the man he has boasted he is; Sthenelus boasts that the sons are better than their fathers; and in the battle the killers cry out in triumph.

**A vow.** Athena tells Pandarus to pray (εὔχεο) to Apollo, the Lycian-born, famed for his bow, that he will offer a glorious hecatomb of firstborn lambs when he comes home to Zeleia, and he makes the vow in her words as he fits the arrow to the string (4.101–103, 4.119–121). See [[prayer]] and [[reciprocity-with-the-gods]].

**A thing to boast of.** Agamemnon fears that if Menelaus dies the army will go home, “and we would leave to Priam and the Trojans a thing to boast of (εὐχωλήν), Argive Helen” (4.173–174). The line was spoken twice in Book 2, by Hera and then by Athena, when the army was running for the ships (2.160–161, 2.176–177). The enemy's boast is the Achaeans' disgrace (see [[elenchos]]).

**A claim to live up to.** Agamemnon ends his praise of Idomeneus, “But rouse yourself for war, and be the man you have always boasted (εὔχεαι) you are” (4.264). Sthenelus answers Agamemnon's rebuke with a counterclaim: “We boast (εὐχόμεθʼ) that we are far better than our fathers” (4.405; see [[better-than-our-fathers]]). A claim of this kind commits the one who makes it, and the men who hear it will hold him to it. Idomeneus answers that he will be the king's trusty companion, as he once promised (4.266–267); Diomedes answers Sthenelus by telling him to be silent and going to fight (4.412–421).

**The cry of triumph.** When the armies meet, “there were cries of pain and cries of triumph together (οἰμωγή τε καὶ εὐχωλή) from men killing and being killed, and the earth ran with blood” (4.450–451). The two cries are heard as one sound. No victor in the first battle boasts in words; the εὐχωλή here is the shout of the killer over the man he has killed. See [[the-armies-clash]] and [[death-in-battle]].
