---
summary: Book 4 names Menelaus by his father as the arrow is aimed and strikes, and Agamemnon throughout the review.
---

Two forms divide the brothers in Book 4. Menelaus is Ἀτρέος υἱός, “the son of Atreus,” in the line-end phrase Μενέλαον ἀρήϊον Ἀτρέος υἱόν ([[warlike]]): when Athena tempts Pandarus (4.98), when the arrow is about to strike (4.115), and when Agamemnon sends for the healer (4.195). Agamemnon is Ἀτρεΐδης. The vocative Ἀτρεΐδη opens the answers of the men he reviews: Idomeneus (4.266), Nestor (4.318), Odysseus (4.350), and Sthenelus, who tells him not to lie (4.404; [[pseudos]]). Twice the narrator notes that “the son of Atreus passed on, glad at heart” (4.272, 4.326; [[the-review]]).
