---
summary: Agamemnon leaves his chariot and goes through the ranks on foot, “commanding” so many men; “so, like a master (κοιρανέων), he ranged through the ranks of men”, as Odysseus went through the army in Book 2.
passages:
  - 4.223-250 | Agamemnon goes through the army
---

The verb κοιρανέω, “command, act as lord”, frames the first part of Agamemnon's review. He leaves his horses and chariot with his attendant Eurymedon and tells him to keep them close, for the time when weariness takes his limbs, “as he went commanding (κοιρανέοντα) so many” (4.226–230). Then, after he has encouraged the eager and rebuked the slack, the narrator sums up: “So, like a master (κοιρανέων), he ranged through the ranks of men” (ὣς ὅ γε κοιρανέων ἐπεπωλεῖτο στίχας ἀνδρῶν, 4.250).

In Book 2 the same half-line described Odysseus, going through the army with Agamemnon's scepter and turning back the men who ran for the ships (2.207). Then the king sat silent while his deputy restored order. Here the king goes himself, on foot, and the narrator praises him for it: “Then you would not have seen brilliant Agamemnon drowsing, nor cowering, nor unwilling to fight” (4.223–224).
