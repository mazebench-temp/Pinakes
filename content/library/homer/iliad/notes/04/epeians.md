grc: [Ἐπειῶν]
---

Ἐπειῶν χαλκοχιτώνων, “the bronze-armored Epeians” (4.537): χαλκοχίτων = χαλκός (“bronze”) + χιτών (“tunic”), “with tunics of bronze” (see [[bronze-armored]]). Nestor gives them the same epithet in the story of his youth, when they were the enemies of Pylos (11.694).

Of the four leaders who brought the Epeians to Troy in forty ships (2.615–624), Diores is the first to fall. Thoas, the leader of the Aetolians, avenges him at once (4.527–531). The line that lays the two dead leaders side by side names the two peoples against each other, the Thracians and the Epeians (4.536–538). See [[diores]], [[peirous]] and [[thracians]].
