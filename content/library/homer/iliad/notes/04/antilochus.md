**A name, then a death.** Antilochus comes into the poem without an introduction. He is not in the Catalogue of Ships, where Nestor alone leads the ninety ships of Pylos (2.601–602), nor among the captains Nestor has just been marshaling (4.295–296), and Book 4 does not say whose son he is. His first action is the poem's first killing.

**First.** πρῶτος (“first”) is said twice: Antilochus was “the first to kill a helmeted man of the Trojans” (4.457), and “he was first to strike him” (4.459). The blow is told exactly, as the wounds of the Iliad usually are. The spear strikes the φάλος of the horsehair-crested helmet, a ridge or boss whose exact form is disputed, fixes in the forehead and goes into the bone. Then comes the first death formula of the poem, “darkness covered his eyes” (τὸν δὲ σκότος ὄσσε κάλυψεν; see [[darkness-covered-his-eyes]]), and the first simile of a falling man: Echepolus fell “like a tower in the fierce battle” (see [[like-a-tower]]). Victim and father, wound, death and fall: the pattern of the killings to come is all here (see [[androktasia]] and [[the-first-battle]]).

**The son of the old man.** Antilochus does not strip his man. Elephenor takes the body by the feet to drag it away and is killed doing it (4.463–469; see [[echepolus]] and [[elephenor]]). Earlier in the book Nestor, marshaling the Pylians, told Agamemnon that “the spears will be wielded by the younger men, who are born later than I” (4.324–325; see [[nestor]]). The first man to kill in the poem is Nestor's son. Book 4 does not point this out, but an audience that knew Antilochus knew his father. His part grows from here: he stands beside Menelaus in the next book (5.561–589), and in Book 18 he brings Achilles the news of Patroclus' death (18.16–21).
