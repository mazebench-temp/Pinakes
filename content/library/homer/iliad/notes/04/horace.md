---
summary: In the ode in which Nereus foretells the war to Paris, Diomedes is “the son of Tydeus, better than his father”, the claim that Sthenelus makes for the sons of the Seven in Book 4.
---

Among the Greeks who will hunt Paris, Nereus names “the fierce son of Tydeus, better than his father” (*Tydides melior patre*, *Odes* 1.15.27–28), and Paris will flee him like a deer that has seen a wolf. Agamemnon had said the opposite: the son of Tydeus is “worse than he in battle, though better in assembly” (4.400). Sthenelus answered for both sons of the Seven, “We boast that we are far better than our fathers” (4.405). Horace takes Sthenelus' side. See [[horace-nereus-prophecy]] and [[fathers-and-sons]].
