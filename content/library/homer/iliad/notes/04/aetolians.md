Αἰτώλιος and Αἰτωλός are two forms of the adjective “Aetolian”. Agamemnon ends his story of Tydeus at Thebes with it: “Such was Tydeus the Aetolian; but the son he fathered is worse than he in battle, though better in assembly” (4.399–400). Tydeus was a son of Oeneus of Calydon who went to Argos and settled there (14.113–125), so the Argive leader Diomedes is an Aetolian by descent, and Idomeneus will call him “an Aetolian by birth” who rules among the Argives (23.471). See [[tydeus]] and [[diomedes]].

The Aetolians' own leader, “Thoas the Aetolian”, kills the Thracian Peirous, who has just killed the Epeian Diores (4.527–531; see [[thoas]]).
