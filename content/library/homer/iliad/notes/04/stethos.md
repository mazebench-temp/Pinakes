summary: Hera's breast cannot contain her anger; Menelaus' spirit is gathered back into his breast; the silent Achaeans march with voices in their breasts; and in the first battle Simoeisius is struck in the chest.
grc: [στήθεσιν]
passages:
  - 4.20-24 | Hera's anger
  - 4.427-431 | The Achaeans march in silence
---

The chest holds what a person feels and what gives him life. “Hera's breast could not contain her anger” (Ἥρῃ δʼ οὐκ ἔχαδε στῆθος χόλον, 4.24), and it spills out in speech. Menelaus, seeing that the arrow's barbs are outside the wound, feels his spirit “gathered back into his breast” (4.152). The Achaeans march in such silence that “you would not have said that so great an army followed with voices in their breasts” (ἔχοντʼ ἐν στήθεσιν αὐδήν, 4.430). In the first battle the chest is a target: Ajax strikes Simoeisius “in the chest beside the nipple” (4.480), and Thoas strikes Peirous “in the chest above the nipple”, where the bronze sticks in his lung (4.528).
