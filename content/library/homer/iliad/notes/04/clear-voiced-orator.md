---
summary: Agamemnon finds Nestor, “the clear-voiced orator of the Pylians,” marshaling his men.
grc: [λιγὺν Πυλίων ἀγορητὴν]
---

{{quote:7919e2f4-76dd-5d98-a73c-c95912d8cbda}}

The speaker's epithet introduces Nestor in the act of arranging his army: the chariots in front, the foot soldiers behind, and the cowards in the middle (4.297–300; [[battle-order]]). His speech to the horsemen is advice about tactics, drawn from the men of old (4.303–309). The orator's gift serves the war, as Nestor says himself: counsel and words are “the prize of the old” (4.323; [[nestor]], [[old-age-and-memory]]).
