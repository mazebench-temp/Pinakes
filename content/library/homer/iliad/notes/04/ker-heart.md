περὶ κῆρι, “in the heart, above all”, comes twice in the bargain of Zeus and Hera. Zeus says that of all the cities where men live, “sacred Ilios was the most honored in my heart” (τάων μοι περὶ κῆρι τιέσκετο Ἴλιος ἱρή, 4.46). Hera answers with her three cities, Argos, Sparta and Mycenae: “sack them whenever they become hateful to your heart” (ὅτʼ ἄν τοι ἀπέχθωνται περὶ κῆρι, 4.53). The phrase for the city a god loves most is the phrase for the city he might come to hate.

Agamemnon twice leaves a contingent “glad at heart” (γηθόσυνος κῆρ), after Idomeneus has promised his loyalty and after Nestor has answered him (4.272, 4.326). See [[polis]] and [[the-bargain-of-zeus-and-hera]].
