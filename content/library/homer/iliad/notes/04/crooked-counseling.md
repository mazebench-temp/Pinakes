---
summary: Hera names her father by the epithet when she claims equal birth with Zeus, and the narrator uses the formula for Zeus in the simile of the star.
grc: [ἀγκυλομήτης]
---

Hera claims a share in the decision by her birth: “for I too am a god, and my birth is from the same stock as yours, and crooked-counseling Cronus fathered me, the most senior” (4.58–59). The nominative Κρόνος ἀγκυλομήτης names the father whom both gods share ([[cronus]]). In Hesiod the first-born child of Cronus is Hestia (*Theogony* 453–454), and Hera's “most senior” is a claim of rank as much as of birth: she is senior both by her birth and as the wife of Zeus (4.60–61).

A few lines later the formula that names Zeus through his father returns in a simile: Athena darts down to the battlefield “as the son of crooked-counseling Cronus sends a star” (4.75; [[son-of-cronus]], [[shooting-star]]). In Book 2 the same formula named the giver of the scepter and of the portent at Aulis ({{ref:Iliad 2.205}}, {{ref:Iliad 2.319}}); here it names the sender of a sign whose meaning the watching armies cannot read (4.79–85).
