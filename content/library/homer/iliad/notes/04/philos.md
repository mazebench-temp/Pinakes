**What the gods hold dear.** The bargain on Olympus is an exchange of things held dear. Zeus asks Hera to let him sack any city “where men dear (φίλοι) to you were born” (4.41), and Hera names hers: “three cities are by far the dearest (φίλταται) to me, Argos and Sparta and wide-streeted Mycenae” (4.51–52). Zeus' own word for Ilios is not φίλος but “most honored” (4.46; see [[time]]).

**Dear brother.** Agamemnon's lament begins φίλε κασίγνητε, “dear brother” (4.155), and his answer to Menelaus' comfort begins “dear Menelaus” (φίλος ὦ Μενέλαε, 4.189). See [[kasignetos]].

**What a man likes.** φίλος also means “welcome, pleasing”, what a man enjoys. Zeus asks the gods whether peace would be “welcome and pleasing to all” (φίλον καὶ ἡδύ, 4.17). Agamemnon tells Menestheus and Odysseus that at his feasts they are glad (φίλʼ) to eat roast meat and drink honey-sweet wine for as long as they like, “but now you would gladly (φίλως) watch, even if ten columns of Achaeans were fighting in front of you” (4.345–348). And of Tydeus: “Tydeus was not one to cower like this” (οὐ μὲν Τυδέϊ γʼ ὧδε φίλον πτωσκαζέμεν ἦεν, 4.372), literally “it was not dear to Tydeus to cower”. What a man finds dear is what he is.
