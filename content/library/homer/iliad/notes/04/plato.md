---
summary: The Republic draws on Book 4 three times, to forbid the poets to blame the breaking of the oaths on Athena and Zeus, to praise the sons of Asclepius for healing Menelaus without a regimen, and to teach obedience with Diomedes' words to Sthenelus and the silent march of the Achaeans.
---

- The confounding of the oaths and the truce, which the poets must not say came about through Athena and Zeus (*Republic* 2.379e): [[plato-broken-oaths]].
- The sons of Asclepius, who sucked the blood from Menelaus' wound, spread soothing drugs on it and prescribed nothing more (*Republic* 3.408a): [[plato-asclepiads]].
- Diomedes' command to Sthenelus to sit in silence and obey, and the Achaeans who march in silence, fearing their commanders (*Republic* 3.389e): [[plato-silent-achaeans]].
