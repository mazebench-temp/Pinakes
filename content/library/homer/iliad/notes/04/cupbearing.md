summary: Hebe serves the nectar at the gods' assembly (4.2–3). The verb, ἐοινοχόει, “poured out”, is the verb for pouring wine, used in Book 1 of Hephaestus.
grc: [ἐοινοχόει]
en: [poured out]
---

“And among them lady Hebe poured out the nectar” (μετὰ δέ σφισι πότνια Ἥβη / νέκταρ ἐοινοχόει, 4.2–3). In Book 1 the lame Hephaestus took the young servant's part and made the gods laugh (1.597–600). Here the server is the goddess of youth herself, πότνια, “lady”, and the service is told in a line and a half, without comment. Elsewhere in the Iliad she fits the wheels to Hera's chariot and bathes the wounded Ares (5.722, 5.905), and in the Odyssey she is the wife of Heracles among the gods (Odyssey 11.602–604).
