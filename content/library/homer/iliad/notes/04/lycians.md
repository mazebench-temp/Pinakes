---
summary: Agamemnon, who does not know who shot his brother, calls the archer “one of the Trojans or Lycians” (4.197), and Talthybius repeats the words to Machaon (4.207).
---

The phrase names the enemy by its two chief peoples: the Trojans of the city and the Lycians, the most prominent of their allies. It happens to fit the archer. [[Pandarus]] leads men whom the Catalogue calls Trojans (2.826), but he prays to Apollo “the Lycian-born” (4.101, 4.119; see [[lycian-born]]), and in Book 5 he speaks of setting out from Lycia (5.105). Which Lycia is meant is disputed (see [[lycia]]). The phrase ends on “fame for him, and grief for us” (4.197; see [[kleos]] and [[penthos]]).
