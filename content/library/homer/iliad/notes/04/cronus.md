Cronus is named here for the first time in the nominative, and for the first time the poem says that he fathered Hera. She uses him to claim equal standing with Zeus: her birth is “from the same stock” as his (γένος … ἔνθεν ὅθεν σοί), and she is the most senior (πρεσβυτάτην), by birth and by marriage (see [[hera]]). ἀγκυλομήτης, “crooked-counseling”, is his standing epithet (4.59, 4.75; see [[crooked-counseling]]). The star to which Athena is compared is sent by “the son of crooked-counseling Cronus” (Κρόνου πάϊς ἀγκυλομήτεω, 4.75), the phrase of Book 2 (2.205, 2.319). The patronymics name Zeus four more times: Κρονίδης (4.5, 4.166), Κρονίδη in Hera's “most dread son of Cronus” (4.25; see [[most-dread]]), and Κρονίων (4.249). See [[son-of-cronus]].
