{{quote:2f616469-d39c-5c58-b7ba-997c641df646}}

“And Zeus, son of Cronus, throned on high, who dwells in the upper air, will himself shake his dark aegis over them all, in resentment at this deceit” (4.166–168). In Book 2 Athena carried the aegis through the Achaean ranks and roused every man to fight (2.446–452). Here it is the weapon of Zeus' punishment. Shaken over men, it brings panic and defeat: Zeus shakes it over Ida with thunder and lightning to give the Trojans victory (17.593–596), and Apollo, given it by Zeus, shakes it at the Achaeans to put them to flight (15.229–230, 15.307–322). ἐρεμνή, “dark”, is used of a black squall, of night, and of the earth into which the dead go down (12.375, 20.51; Odyssey 11.606, 24.106), and it may point to the old explanation of the aegis as a storm cloud (see [[aegis]]). αὐτός, “himself”, makes Zeus the punisher of the broken oath in person (see [[oath]] and [[dwelling-in-the-upper-air]]).
