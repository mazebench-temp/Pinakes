---
summary: Zeus declares the victory to be Menelaus', “dear to Ares,” and Menelaus himself shudders at his wound.
---

The formula passes from Agamemnon's claim at the end of Book 3 (3.457) to Zeus' words at the beginning of Book 4: “But the victory belongs to Menelaus, dear to Ares” (4.13). The gods acknowledge the victory and decide to set it aside. When the arrow strikes, Agamemnon shudders at the sight of the blood, “and Menelaus himself, dear to Ares, shuddered too” (4.150), until he sees that the barbs are outside the flesh ([[dark-blood]]). His other epithets in the book are [[warlike]], [[kydalimos]], [[fair-haired]] and [[good-at-the-war-cry]].
