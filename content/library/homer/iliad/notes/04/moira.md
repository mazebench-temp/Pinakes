---
summary: In the first battle “fate fettered Diores” (μοῖρα πέδησε) before the stone that crushed his ankle and held him where he fell.
passages:
  - 4.517-526 | The death of Diores
---

The narrator names fate before he names the stone: “Then fate fettered Diores, son of Amarynceus; for he was hit by a jagged stone beside the ankle, on the right shin” (4.517–519). πεδάω, “fetter, bind the feet”, is exact. The stone crushes the tendons and bones of his ankle (4.521–522), and he cannot run; he falls on his back, stretching out his hands to his companions, and Peirous comes up and kills him with a spear (4.522–526). The fate and the wound are one event, seen from two sides. The same verb binds Hector at the Scaean gates when the others have fled into the city: “his deadly fate fettered him to stay there” (22.5). See [[diores]], [[peirous]] and [[death-in-battle]].

Agamemnon speaks of death as a measure filled up. He fears for Menelaus “if you die and fill up the measure of your life” (πότμον ἀναπλήσῃς βιότοιο, 4.170), and πότμος, the doom that is a man's lot, is what Tydeus brought on the Theban ambush (4.396).
