---
summary: In the simile of the mother and the fly, the child “lies in sweet sleep” while his mother keeps the fly away.
grc: [ὕπνῳ]
passages:
  - 4.127-133 | Athena turns the arrow
---

Athena brushes the arrow from Menelaus' flesh, “as a mother brushes a fly from her child, when he lies in sweet sleep” (ὅθʼ ἡδέϊ λέξεται ὕπνῳ, 4.130–131). The sleeping child cannot guard himself, and does not know that he is guarded. The simile puts the peace of a house into the moment of the shot. See [[mother-and-fly]] and [[meter]].

The narrator uses a sleep word in praise a hundred lines later: once the Trojans come on, “you would not have seen brilliant Agamemnon drowsing” (βρίζοντα, 4.223).
