**The gods' horses.** Hera complains that her labor for the war is being wasted, “the sweat I sweated in my toil”, and that her horses were worn out as she gathered the army against Priam (4.26–28; see [[hera]]).

**An ornament for the horse.** The ivory cheekpiece stained with purple, in the simile of Menelaus' wound, is kept for a king, “both an ornament for the horse and a glory to the driver” (ἵππῳ, 4.145); horsemen long for it (ἱππῆες, 4.144). See [[ivory]] and [[ivory-stained-with-purple]].

**In the review.** Agamemnon leaves his “snorting” horses with Eurymedon (4.226–230). Nestor puts his horsemen (ἱππῆας, 4.297) in front with their horses and chariots and tells them to keep their horses in hand, and let no one fight alone ahead of the others “trusting in his horsemanship and his manhood” (ἱπποσύνῃ, 4.303). He himself, too old for the spear, will be “among the horsemen” and command them (ἱππεῦσι, 4.322). A ἱππεύς in Homer is a man who fights from a chariot, not a rider. Diomedes stands “among his horses” (ἵπποισι, 4.366). See [[chariot]] and [[battle-order]].

**Abydos.** Democoon, a bastard son of Priam, had come to the war “from Abydos, from his swift mares” (παρʼ ἵππων ὠκειάων, 4.500): the phrase suggests that he kept Priam's horses there (see [[democoon]] and [[abydos]]).

**Epithets.** Horses run through the book's epithets: the horse-taming Trojans (4.80, 4.333, 4.352, 4.355, 4.509), the Danaans “of the swift horses” (4.232, 4.257), “horse-pasturing” Tricca (4.202), Nestor the horseman (4.317), Menestheus “driver of horses” (4.327), Tydeus “tamer of horses” and “horse-driving” (4.370, 4.387), the Cadmeians “goaders of horses” (4.391), and Echepolus' “horsehair-crested” helmet (4.459). See [[tamer-of-horses]], [[of-the-swift-horses]], [[horse-pasturing]], [[gerenian-horseman]], [[driver-of-horses]], [[horse-driving]], [[goaders-of-horses]] and [[horsehair-crested]].
