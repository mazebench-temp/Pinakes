Book 4 names Capaneus only as a father. The narrator calls Sthenelus Καπανήϊος υἱός, “the son of Capaneus” (4.367), with an adjective formed from the father's name, and then “the son of glorious Capaneus” (υἱὸς Καπανῆος … κυδαλίμοιο, 4.403). Both of Homer's descriptions of Capaneus are praise: “famous” in the Catalogue (ἀγακλειτός, 2.564) and “glorious” here. A few lines later his son passes judgment on him and the other fathers who attacked Thebes. They “perished through their own recklessness”, and they are not to be put in equal honor with their sons (4.409–410; see [[sthenelus]] and [[atasthalie]]). The narrator's praise and the son's verdict stand side by side.
