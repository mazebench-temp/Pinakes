Τηλεμάχοιο φίλον πατέρα, “the dear father of Telemachus”: Odysseus speaks of himself in the third person, and names himself by his son. Both of the Iliad's mentions of Telemachus come in angry speeches of Odysseus. In Book 2 he swore by the title that he would strip and beat Thersites (2.259–260); here he stakes it on a promise to fight among the champions, and keeps the promise the same day (4.494–504; see [[odysseus]]).
