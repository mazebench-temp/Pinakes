---
summary: Agamemnon uses the word of Odysseus, with “skilled in evil tricks,” when he rebukes him for standing apart, and then takes it back.
---

In Book 1 Achilles threw the word at Agamemnon. In Book 4 Agamemnon uses it of Odysseus:

{{quote:7e48bc0b-a377-5c41-9e37-b742e3bf7c3f}}

Joined to κακοῖσι δόλοισι κεκασμένε (“skilled in evil tricks”), κερδαλεόφρων here means “crafty” rather than “greedy” (see the Meaning above). Odysseus answers “looking at him darkly” (4.349; [[looking-darkly]]), and Agamemnon takes back his words with a smile and the formal address “Son of Laertes, sprung from Zeus, Odysseus of many devices” (4.356–363; [[of-many-devices]]). The insult that Achilles hurled at Agamemnon in Book 1 is spoken now by Agamemnon, and this time it is withdrawn.
