summary: Two errands of Book 4 bring the wound and the cure: Zeus sends Athena, who finds Pandarus and persuades him to shoot (4.68–104), and Agamemnon sends Talthybius, who finds Machaon and brings him to heal (4.192–209). The two arrivals are told in the same verses.

  - 4.192-209 | Agamemnon sends Talthybius to Machaon
---

The two errands follow the pattern step by step, and the poet makes them match.

1. **The order.** Zeus gives Athena Hera's order in Hera's words (4.70–72); Agamemnon gives Talthybius his message (4.193–197; see [[message-repeated]]).
2. **Obedience.** Zeus “did not disobey” Hera, and urged on Athena, “who was eager already” (4.68, 4.73); the herald “heard him and did not disobey” (4.198).
3. **The going.** Athena “went darting down from the peaks of Olympus” (4.74, the verse of her descent in Book 2, 2.167) like a shooting star (4.75–79); Talthybius “went his way through the army of the bronze-armored Achaeans” (4.199).
4. **The search.** She goes into the Trojan throng in the likeness of Laodocus, “looking for godlike Pandarus” (4.86–88); he goes “looking for the hero Machaon” (4.200).
5. **The finding.** “She found the son of Lycaon, noble and strong, standing, and around him were the strong ranks of shield-bearing men, who had followed him from the streams of the Aesepus” (4.89–91). “And he saw him standing, and around him were the strong ranks of shield-bearing men, who had followed him from horse-pasturing Tricca” (4.200–202).
6. **The address.** “And standing near him she addressed him with winged words” (4.92); “And standing near him he addressed him with winged words” (4.203).
7. **The effect.** “So spoke Athena, and she persuaded the mind of the fool” (4.104); “So he spoke, and stirred the heart in his breast” (4.208), and the herald and the healer go through the army to Menelaus (4.209).

The goddess brings a temptation, and the man she finds brings the wound; the herald brings a summons, and the man he finds brings the cure. The shared verses set the archer from the Aesepus and the healer from Tricca side by side, each standing among his own men (see [[pandarus]], [[machaon]], [[athena]] and [[talthybius]]).
