**Hera's city.**

{{quote:b9f6ebb9-3565-53c5-bd48-647d61afbfa9}}

Mycenae is the last of the three cities that Hera loves most and offers to Zeus. She calls it εὐρυάγυια, “wide-streeted” (see [[wide-streeted]]), the epithet the Achaeans use of the city they have come to take (2.12, 2.141, 2.329). Here the name is singular, Μυκήνη; the Catalogue and the story of Tydeus use the plural, Μυκῆναι (2.569, 4.376). It is Agamemnon's city (2.569), and Hera's offer puts the home of the leader of the war in the scale against Troy. Some readers have seen in the lines an allusion to the later destruction of Mycenae by Argos; see [[hera]] and [[the-bargain-of-zeus-and-hera]].

**Tydeus at Mycenae.** Rebuking Diomedes, Agamemnon tells how Tydeus “came to Mycenae, not in war, a guest, with godlike Polynices, gathering an army” for the war against Thebes (4.376–377). The Mycenaeans were willing to send allies, “but Zeus turned them back, showing ill-omened signs” (4.380–381), and Mycenae stayed out of the war of the Seven. Agamemnon tells it as a story he has heard: he never saw Tydeus himself (4.374–375). See [[tydeus]], [[polynices]] and [[the-seven-against-thebes]].
