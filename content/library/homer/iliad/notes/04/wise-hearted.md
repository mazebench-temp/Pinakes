---
summary: Book 4 gives the epithet to Pandarus in Athena's address, to Idomeneus arming his Cretans, and to Tydeus in Agamemnon's rebuke of his son.
grc: [δαΐφρον, δαΐφρονα]
---

Athena, in disguise, addresses Pandarus with courtesy: “Will you listen to me now, wise-hearted son of Lycaon?” (4.93). When he obeys, the narrator says that she “persuaded the mind of the fool” (4.104). The translation's choice of sense is tested here: the man called wise-hearted proves a fool within a dozen lines, and the other sense of δαΐφρων, “warlike in mind,” would suit him better ([[pandarus]]). The Cretans arm “around wise-hearted Idomeneus” (4.252; [[idomeneus]]), and Agamemnon rebukes Diomedes as “son of wise-hearted Tydeus, tamer of horses” (4.370), the half-line the Dream used of Atreus in Book 2 ([[tamer-of-horses]]).
