---
summary: Democoon, a bastard son of Priam, had come to the war “from Abydos, from his swift mares” before Odysseus killed him (4.499–500).
grc: [Ἀβυδόθεν]
---

ὅς οἱ Ἀβυδόθεν ἦλθε παρʼ ἵππων ὠκειάων, “who had come to him from Abydos, from his swift mares” (4.500). Ἀβυδόθεν = Ἄβυδος + -θεν (“from”). The adjective ὠκειάων (“swift”) is feminine, so the horses are mares, and οἱ (“to him”) is Priam. The line is usually taken to mean that Priam kept a stud of mares at Abydos and that Democoon, his bastard son, had charge of it (see [[democoon]]). The district is horse country in the Catalogue too: Asius, whose realm included Abydos, came to Troy behind “great and tawny horses” (2.835–839; see [[asius]]).
