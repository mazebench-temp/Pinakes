summary: Book 4 speaks of the oath in the words of its breaking. The gods plan that the Trojans shall be the first to do harm “in defiance of the oaths”, and Agamemnon and Idomeneus repeat the phrase; the oaths once “cut” have been “trampled” and “confounded”.
grc: ["ὅρκιʼ ἔταμνον", "ὅρκιʼ ἔχευαν"]
en: [swore in the oaths, have been confounded]
---

**“First … in defiance of the oaths.”** The curse of Book 3 fell on “whichever side first does harm in defiance of the oaths” (ὁππότεροι πρότεροι ὑπὲρ ὅρκια πημήνειαν, 3.299). In Book 4 the phrase returns four times, with another verb of harm, δηλέομαι: Hera's plan and Zeus' order, that the Trojans “be the first to do harm in defiance of the oaths” (πρότεροι ὑπὲρ ὅρκια δηλήσασθαι, 4.67, 4.72), and then, the deed done, Agamemnon's “those who first did harm in defiance of the oaths” (4.236) and Idomeneus' “since they were the first to do harm” (4.271). The words of the curse have become a description of what the Trojans did.

**Cut, trampled, confounded.** An oath is “cut” (τάμνω), the verb of killing the victims (see [[oath]]). Agamemnon turns the phrase against himself: θάνατόν νύ τοι ὅρκιʼ ἔταμνον, “it was your death, then, that I swore in the oaths” (4.155). The Trojans “trampled on the trusty oaths” (κατὰ δʼ ὅρκια πιστὰ πάτησαν, 4.157), and in Idomeneus' words “the oaths have been confounded” (σύν γʼ ὅρκιʼ ἔχευαν, 4.269), “poured together” like things spilled and mixed. The verbs treat the oaths as things, as the heralds treated them when they carried “the offerings for the trusty oaths” through Troy (3.245).

**The pledges.** Agamemnon's list of what made the oath repeats Nestor's line on the compacts of the expedition: “the libations of unmixed wine and the right hands in which we trusted” (4.159 = 2.341), with ὅρκιον, “the oath”, and “the blood of lambs” before it (4.158). See [[libation]].

**Promise and nod.** Idomeneus answers the king with his own word kept: “as I promised at first and nodded my assent” (ὡς τὸ πρῶτον ὑπέστην καὶ κατένευσα, 4.267). The promise (ὑπόσχεσις) of the expedition, which Odysseus feared the army would not keep (2.286), is kept by one of its leaders in the book in which the enemy's oath is broken. See [[neuo]].
