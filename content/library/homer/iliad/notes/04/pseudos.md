**Liars.** Agamemnon's first words to the eager Argives give them a reason to fight: “for father Zeus will be no helper to liars (ἐπὶ ψευδέσσι), but those who first did harm in defiance of the oaths, vultures will eat their tender flesh” (4.235–237). The oath-breakers are liars because an oath is a word about the future, and they have made it false. The text is printed ψευδέσσι, “liars”; with the other accent, ψεύδεσσι, it would mean “lies”, and both readings are ancient. See [[no-helper-to-liars]] and [[oaths-and-their-breaking]].

**Speaking falsely.** Sthenelus opens his retort to Agamemnon's praise of Tydeus: “Son of Atreus, do not lie (μὴ ψεύδεʼ), when you know how to speak plainly” (4.404). He does not accuse the king of inventing his story. He says that the judgment is false, that the sons are better than the fathers (4.405–410). ψεύδομαι covers both a lie and a mistake, and Sthenelus leaves the king room to have been only wrong. See [[better-than-our-fathers]].
