---
summary: Two replies in Book 4 are framed by this family of lines: Agamemnon's answer to Menelaus, in the line of his answers in Books 1 and 2 (4.188), and Idomeneus' answer to the king, “face to face” (4.265).
---

“Answering him, powerful Agamemnon said” (4.188 = 1.130, 1.285, 2.369). In Book 1 the line brought in the king's answers in the quarrel, and in Book 2 his answer to Nestor. Here it brings in his answer to his wounded brother, who has just told him not to frighten the army: “May it be so, dear Menelaus! But a healer will examine the wound” (4.189–190). The frame is the frame of debate, and the speech is relief (see [[agamemnon]] and [[healing]]).

Idomeneus answers the king's praise with ἀντίον ηὔδα, “answered him face to face” (4.265), the phrase of Antenor's answer to Helen in Book 3 (3.203). It is the only answer of the review that agrees with the king entirely (see [[the-review]]).

Other replies of the book use the participle frames: Zeus “deeply troubled” (4.30; [[deeply-troubled]]), Agamemnon “groaning heavily” (4.153; [[groaning-heavily]]), Menelaus “encouraging him” (ἐπιθαρσύνων, 4.183), Odysseus and Diomedes “looking at him darkly” (4.349, 4.411; [[looking-darkly]]), and Agamemnon “smiling at him” (4.356; [[smiling-at-him]]).
