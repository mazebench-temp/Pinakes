---
summary: Book 4 gives the epithet to Menestheus, the Athenian leader, as Agamemnon finds him standing idle.
grc: [πλήξιππον]
---

“He found the son of Peteos, Menestheus, driver of horses, standing” (4.327–328). The Catalogue had praised Menestheus above all men for marshaling horses and shield-bearing men ({{ref:Iliad 2.552-554}}), and the epithet fits his fame. But he is standing still with his Athenians, “masters of the war cry,” who have not yet heard the war cry (4.328–331; [[masters-of-the-war-cry]]). Agamemnon rebukes him and Odysseus together (4.336–348; [[menestheus]]).

Book 4 is full of words for horsemanship. Besides Menestheus, the driver of horses, Nestor is the Gerenian horseman (4.317; [[gerenian-horseman]]), the Danaans have swift horses (4.232, 4.257; [[of-the-swift-horses]]), the Trojans tame them (4.333, 4.509; [[tamer-of-horses]]), and in Agamemnon's story Tydeus is horse-driving and the Cadmeians goad their horses (4.387, 4.391; [[horse-driving]], [[goaders-of-horses]]).
