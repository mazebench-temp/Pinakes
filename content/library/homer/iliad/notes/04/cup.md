**Golden cups.** On Olympus “they with golden cups pledged one another, looking out upon the city of the Trojans” (χρυσέοις δεπάεσσι / δειδέχατʼ ἀλλήλους, 4.3–4). δειδέχατο is a verb of greeting: each god raises his cup to another. The golden cups of the gods are a fixture of Olympus; in Book 1 Hephaestus put a two-handled cup into Hera's hand (1.584–585). See [[nectar]], [[feast]] and [[gold-and-silver]].

**A full cup.** At the elders' feast the others drink a measured portion, but Idomeneus' cup (δέπας) “always stands full, as mine does, to drink whenever your heart bids you” (4.262–263). The cup is the measure of honor: most men drink their share, the king and his most honored companion drink as they please. Agamemnon reminds Menestheus and Odysseus of the “cups of honey-sweet wine” they enjoy at the same feasts (κύπελλα, 4.345–346), and asks them to earn them (see [[wine]]).
