---
summary: Zeus answers Hera's protest “deeply troubled”, in the whole line with which he answered Thetis in Book 1 (4.30 = 1.517).
---

“Deeply troubled, Zeus the cloud-gatherer said to her” (4.30). In Book 1 the line brought in his answer to Thetis, and his trouble was Hera: “This is a sorry business, since you will set me at odds with Hera” (1.518–519). In Book 4 the trouble is Hera herself. She has protested that her labor for the war will be wasted, and he answers with a question she never answers: what have Priam and his sons done to her, that she would eat them raw (4.31–36)? Then he gives way (see [[zeus]], [[hera]] and [[the-bargain-of-zeus-and-hera]]).

The same line brings in Zeus' answer to Poseidon's complaint about the Achaean wall (7.454), and with other names the replies of Poseidon, Achilles and Menelaus (8.208, 15.184, 16.48, 17.18). Each time the speaker answers a request or a complaint he does not like.
