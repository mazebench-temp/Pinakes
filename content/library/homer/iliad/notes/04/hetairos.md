**Before the battle.** A man's companions act with him. Pandarus' “brave companions held their shields in front of him” while he strung his bow, so that the Achaeans would not spring up before Menelaus was hit (4.113–115). Agamemnon's companions groan with him over his wounded brother (4.154). Idomeneus answers Agamemnon's praise with a promise: “I will indeed be your trusty companion (ἐρίηρος ἑταῖρος), as I promised at first and nodded my assent” (4.266–267). In Agamemnon's story Tydeus used to fight “far ahead of his dear companions” (4.373). Strife is Ares' sister and his companion (ἑτάρη, 4.441).

**In the battle.** The first battle shows what companions owe one another when one of them falls. Leucus, “the brave companion (ἑταῖρον) of Odysseus”, is killed dragging away a body (4.491–493). Odysseus, “very angry at heart over his killing”, goes through the champions and kills Democoon, “in his anger for his companion” (ἑτάροιο χολωσάμενος, 4.494–501). Diores, struck down by a stone, falls “stretching out both his hands to his dear companions” (4.523), who cannot save him. When Thoas has killed Peirous, “his companions stood around him, the Thracians with the topknots”, and pushed Thoas back before he could strip the armor (4.532–535).
