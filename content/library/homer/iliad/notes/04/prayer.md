**Pandarus.** Athena, in the likeness of Laodocus, tells him to shoot, “and pray to Apollo, the Lycian-born, famed for his bow, that you will offer a glorious hecatomb of firstborn lambs when you come home to the city of sacred Zeleia” (4.101–103). He fits the arrow to the string and prays in the same words (4.118–121). The prayer is reported, not quoted, and it is all vow: no claim on past favor, no request in so many words, only the promise of a sacrifice if he comes home. The god is named by titles that fit an archer from Lycia or its neighborhood (see [[lycian-born]] and [[famed-for-his-bow]]). No answer is told. Athena, who urged the shot, turns the arrow from a mortal wound (4.127–133), and Pandarus will not come home to pay (see [[prayer-type-scene]], [[hecatomb]] and [[pandarus]]).

**Wishes.** Agamemnon's praise of the Ajaxes ends as a prayer: “If only, father Zeus and Athena and Apollo, there were such a spirit in the breasts of all! Then the city of lord Priam would soon bow its head” (4.288–291). The first and last lines are his wish for ten counselors like Nestor in Book 2 (2.371–374; see [[if-only-father-zeus]]). Other wishes of the book use the same optatives without naming a god: Menelaus' “May it be so” (4.189), the imagined Trojan's “May Agamemnon fulfill his anger like this against all” (4.178), and Agamemnon's “then may the wide earth gape for me” (4.182; see [[may-the-earth-gape]]).

**Boasts.** εὔχομαι means “declare, claim” as well as “pray”, and in Book 4 it usually means to boast (see [[euchomai]]). Leaving Helen would give the Trojans “a thing to boast of” (εὐχωλή, 4.173, as at 2.160 and 2.176). Agamemnon tells Idomeneus to be “the man you have always boasted you are” (4.264). Sthenelus answers the king: “We boast that we are far better than our fathers” (4.405; see [[better-than-our-fathers]]). And when the armies meet, “there were cries of pain and cries of triumph together” (οἰμωγή τε καὶ εὐχωλή, 4.450; see [[the-armies-clash]]). These lines are not counted here, nor the horsemen who “have prayed” for the ivory cheekpiece of the simile, that is, longed for it (ἠρήσαντο, 4.143).
