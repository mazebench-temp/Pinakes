---
summary: Agamemnon, holding his wounded brother, says “I will have dread grief for you, Menelaus, if you die” and imagines the shame that would follow.
passages:
  - 4.169-182 | Agamemnon fears the return
---

“But I will have dread grief (αἰνὸν ἄχος) for you, Menelaus, if you die and fill up the measure of your life” (4.169–170). Agamemnon's ἄχος is a brother's grief, and it is at once a king's. The next lines turn from Menelaus to himself: he would come to Argos in disgrace, the army would go home, Helen would be left to the Trojans, and a Trojan would leap on his brother's tomb and mock him (4.171–182). He ends by wishing that the earth would gape for him (see [[may-the-earth-gape]]). Menelaus answers the grief with a practical word: the arrow did not strike a fatal place (4.183–187). See [[kasignetos]], [[elenchos]] and [[penthos]].
