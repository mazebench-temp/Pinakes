summary: Book 4 gives the epithet to Ilios in the words of Zeus, Agamemnon and Diomedes, to Pandarus' Zeleia, and to the walls of Thebes.
grc: [ἱρὴ, ἱρήν, ἱερὰ πρὸς τείχεα]
---

The formula Ἴλιος ἱρή, “sacred Ilios,” names Troy in the book's three statements of its fate. Zeus says that of all the cities under the sky “sacred Ilios was the most honored in my heart” (4.46), because his altar there never lacked an equal share of the feast (4.48–49). Agamemnon says that “a day will come when sacred Ilios will perish” (4.164; [[a-day-will-come]]). And Diomedes says that glory will go with Agamemnon if the Achaeans “cut down the Trojans and take sacred Ilios” (4.415–416). Sacred as it is, the city will not be saved, any more than Thebe was in Book 1 ({{ref:Iliad 1.366}}). In the Odyssey Troy is still the “sacred citadel” that Odysseus sacked (Od. 1.2).

Pandarus, told to vow a hecatomb when he comes home “to the city of sacred Zeleia” (4.103), repeats the vow in the same words (4.121; [[zeleia]]). He will not come home. And Agamemnon's story of Tydeus gives the epithet to another besieged city: the Seven were “making war against the sacred walls of Thebes” (4.378; [[thebes]], [[seven-gated]]).
