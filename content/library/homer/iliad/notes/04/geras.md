---
summary: Zeus names the gods' share of sacrifice, “libation and the savor of the burnt fat”, as “the prize allotted to us”; Nestor names counsel and words as “the prize of the old”.
passages:
  - 4.44-49 | Zeus' love for Ilios
  - 4.318-325 | Nestor answers Agamemnon
---

In Book 1 the γέρας was a woman, taken from the spoils and given by the army, and the quarrel was over who would keep his. Book 4 uses the word twice, and neither time is it a thing that can be taken away. It is a share that belongs to a class of beings.

**The gods' prize.** Zeus tells Hera why he loves Ilios above all the cities under the sun: “For my altar never lacked an equal share of the feast, libation and the savor of the burnt fat; for that is the prize allotted to us” (τὸ γὰρ λάχομεν γέρας ἡμεῖς, 4.48–49). λάχομεν, “we were allotted”, is the verb of the lot by which the gods divided their honors (15.187–193). The gods' γέρας is paid by men at the altar, and Troy has always paid it. Zeus says the same lines of Hector in the last book (24.69–70). See [[reciprocity-with-the-gods]] and [[equal-feast]].

**The prize of the old.** Agamemnon wishes that old age had taken some other man and left Nestor among the young (4.313–316). Nestor answers that the gods do not give men everything at once; he can no longer fight, but he will be among the horsemen “with counsel and with words; for that is the prize of the old” (τὸ γὰρ γέρας ἐστὶ γερόντων, 4.323). The young will wield the spears (4.324–325). The line sets γέρας beside γέρων, “old man”; whether the two words are related is uncertain. Achilles uses the same half-line in Book 9, when he tells the envoys to carry his answer back to the Achaean leaders (9.422), and the dead have their γέρας too, a mound and a gravestone (16.457, 16.675). See [[prize-of-the-old]] and [[old-age-and-memory]].
