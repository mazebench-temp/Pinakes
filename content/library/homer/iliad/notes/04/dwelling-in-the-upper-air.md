---
summary: Agamemnon names Zeus “who dwells in the upper air” as the god who will shake the aegis over Troy.
en: [who dwells in the upper air]
---

“And Zeus, son of Cronus, throned on high, who dwells in the upper air, will himself shake his dark aegis over them all, in resentment at this deceit” (4.166–168). This is the second of the phrase's three uses in Homer, after Agamemnon's prayer in Book 2 ({{ref:Iliad 2.412}}). There Zeus refused the prayer; here Agamemnon is sure that the god in the upper air will see the oaths fulfilled, even if late (4.160–162; [[high-throned]], [[a-day-will-come]]).
