---
summary: The formula occurs seven times in Book 4: for Zeus' order to Athena, for Athena's and Talthybius' messages, and four times in Agamemnon's review, for praise and rebuke alike.
---

The seven lines: 4.69, 92, 203, 284, 312, 337, 369.

**Messages.** “At once he addressed Athena with winged words”, and Zeus gives her Hera's order (4.69). Athena finds Pandarus, and Talthybius finds Machaon, and each speaks in the same verse: “And standing near him she addressed him with winged words” (ἀγχοῦ δʼ ἱσταμένη ἔπεα πτερόεντα προσηύδα, 4.92), and for the herald, ἱστάμενος, “he” (4.203). The goddess who brings the wound and the herald who brings the healer arrive in the same words (see [[messenger-scene]]).

**The review.** Four of Agamemnon's five visits bring in his speech with the whole-line formula, καί σφεας φωνήσας ἔπεα πτερόεντα προσηύδα, “and he spoke to them, addressing them with winged words” (4.284, 4.337), or with μιν, “him”, for a single man (4.312, 4.369): to the Ajaxes and to Nestor in praise, to Menestheus and Odysseus and to Diomedes in rebuke. The first visit, to Idomeneus, has another verb: “at once addressed Idomeneus with soothing words” (μειλιχίοισιν, 4.256). If the formula marks speech that bursts out at a sight, it fits the review, where every speech follows “seeing them” (see [[the-review]]).
