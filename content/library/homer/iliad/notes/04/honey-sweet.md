---
summary: Agamemnon reminds Menestheus and Odysseus of the “honey-sweet wine” they drink at his feasts; the word is μελιηδής, a close relative of μελίφρων.
grc: [μελιηδέος]
---

μελιηδής = μέλι (“honey”) + ἡδύς (“sweet”): “sweet as honey.” It is a near synonym of μελίφρων, and the translation renders both “honey-sweet.” Homer uses it of wine, and in the Odyssey of the homecoming that Odysseus seeks (νόστον … μελιηδέα, Od. 11.100).

Agamemnon's rebuke of Menestheus and Odysseus sets the feast against the fighting:

{{quote:7e48bc0b-a377-5c41-9e37-b742e3bf7c3f}}

The men who are first to drink the honey-sweet wine of the elders should be first in the blazing battle ([[blazing]], [[feast]], [[wine]]). Idomeneus hears the same argument as praise: his cup always stands full, “as mine does” (4.262–263; [[of-the-swift-horses]]).
