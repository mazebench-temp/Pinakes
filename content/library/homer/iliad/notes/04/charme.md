---

The word frames the fighting of Book 4. It comes first when the truce collapses. While the leaders are busy about the wounded Menelaus, the ranks of the Trojans advance, “and the Achaeans put on their armor again, and remembered the joy of battle” (οἳ δʼ αὖτις κατὰ τεύχεʼ ἔδυν, μνήσαντο δὲ χάρμης, 4.222). They had taken their armor off and laid it on the ground to watch the duel, in the hope of an end to the war (3.111–115). “Again” and “remembered” both mark a return: the day of the truce is over, and the men go back to what they know.

At the other end of the book the word changes sides. Apollo, looking down from Pergamus, sees the Trojans give ground, and calls to them: “Rise up, horse-taming Trojans, and do not yield the joy of battle to the Argives” (4.509). His reasons are plain. The Argives' flesh is not stone or iron, and Achilles is not fighting (4.510–513; see [[not-stone-or-iron]]). The Achaeans remembered their χάρμη by themselves; the Trojans have to be told by a god to keep theirs. See [[apollo]], [[the-first-battle]] and [[alke]].
