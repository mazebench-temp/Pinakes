---
summary: Thetis is named once, when Apollo tells the Trojans that “Achilles, son of Thetis of the lovely hair” is not fighting.
grc: [Θέτιδος]
---

Apollo names Achilles by his mother in the speech that gives the Trojans heart: “Achilles, son of Thetis of the lovely hair, is not fighting, but by the ships he broods over his heart-grieving anger” (4.512–513; see [[apollo]]). ἠΰκομος, “of the lovely hair”, is given to Thetis here for the first time in the poem (see [[lovely-haired]]), and the same line-end names Achilles again when Hector answers the dying Patroclus (16.860). The mother's name recalls her plea to Zeus in Book 1, that he “give strength to the Trojans until the Achaeans pay honor to my son” (1.509–510; see [[thetis-supplicates-zeus]]). The Trojans' god now tells them that the son is away.
