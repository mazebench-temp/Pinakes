---
summary: In the simile of the poplar the chariot-maker cuts the tree “with the gleaming iron,” in the formula αἴθωνι σιδήρῳ.
grc: [αἴθωνι σιδήρῳ]
en: [gleaming]
---

Simoeisius falls like a poplar: “a chariot-maker has cut it down with the gleaming iron, to bend a wheel rim for a beautiful chariot” (4.485–486; [[the-poplar]], [[perikalles]], [[simoeisius]]). αἴθωνι σιδήρῳ closes the verse, as it does twice more in the Iliad (Il. 7.473, 20.372). The adjective that makes horses and lions tawny makes iron gleam. See [[iron]].
