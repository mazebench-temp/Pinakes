**Who he is.** Book 2 named him beside Acamas as a leader of the Thracians (2.844). Book 4 gives his father and his town: “the leader of the Thracian men threw it, Peirous son of Imbrasus, who had come from Aenus” (4.519–520; see [[imbrasus]] and [[aenus]]). His name has two forms, Πείρως (4.520) and Πείροος (2.844, 4.525); the translation calls him Peirous in both.

**The kill.** He crushes Diores' ankle with a jagged stone, runs up, and spears him beside the navel as he lies stretching out his hands to his companions (4.517–526; see [[diores]]). He is the first of Troy's allies to kill a man in the poem.

**The death.**

{{quote:b53e89be-db2b-5d7d-81fe-c375ac608688}}

Thoas' spear catches him as he springs back (ἀπεσσύμενον) and sticks in his lung; Thoas comes up, pulls out the spear, and finishes him with the sword (see [[thoas]]). But Thoas does not get his armor. The Thracians “with the topknots” (ἀκρόκομοι; see [[topknotted]]) stand around their leader with their long spears and push Thoas back. The two leaders lie “stretched in the dust beside each other” (4.536). In the first battle the first man who tried to strip a body, Elephenor, died in the attempt (4.463–469); its last named killing ends with a body that is not stripped. See [[thracians]] and [[the-first-battle]].
