summary: Idomeneus promises to be Agamemnon's trusty companion, “as I promised at first and nodded my assent” (ὑπέστην καὶ κατένευσα).

passages:
  - 4.264-271 | Idomeneus answers
---

Agamemnon has praised Idomeneus above all the Danaans and reminded him of his boasts (4.257–264). Idomeneus answers with a promise already made: “Son of Atreus, I will indeed be your trusty companion, as I promised at first and nodded my assent” (ὡς τὸ πρῶτον ὑπέστην καὶ κατένευσα, 4.266–267). A nod seals a promise, among men as among gods. The pair of verbs is the one Agamemnon used of Zeus in Book 2, when he complained that the god “once promised me and nodded his assent” (ὑπέσχετο καὶ κατένευσεν, 2.112) that he would sack Troy (see [[apate]]). Zeus' nod cannot be taken back (1.526–527); Idomeneus keeps his, and turns at once to the Trojans who have not kept theirs (4.268–271). See [[idomeneus]] and [[oaths-and-their-breaking]].
