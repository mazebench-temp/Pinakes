“But you, Menelaus, the blessed gods did not forget, the immortals, and first of all the daughter of Zeus, driver of spoil” (4.127–128; [[immortals]], [[driver-of-spoil]]). The narrator addresses Menelaus directly, as he does again after the simile of the ivory (4.146). The gods who live free of care take thought for him. The same gods had agreed, earlier in the book, that the Trojans should break the oath ([[the-bargain-of-zeus-and-hera]]).
