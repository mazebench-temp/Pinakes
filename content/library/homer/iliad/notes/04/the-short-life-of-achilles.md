Achilles appears in Book 4 only in Apollo's words: “Achilles, son of Thetis of the lovely hair, is not fighting, but by the ships he broods over his heart-grieving anger” (4.512–513). The man who told his mother that she bore him “to be short-lived” (μινυνθάδιον, 1.352) is keeping himself out of the war that will kill him.

In his absence the word falls on others. Simoeisius, killed by Ajax in the first battle, did not repay his parents for his rearing, “and short was his life” (μινυνθάδιος δέ οἱ αἰών, 4.478; see [[threptra]]). The same sentence is used again in Book 17, of Hippothous (17.302). A few lines earlier Elephenor's effort to strip his enemy's armor “lasted only a little while” (μίνυνθα, 4.466), and he is killed as he stoops. What Achilles chose knowingly, the young men of the first battle meet without choosing. See [[short-lived]], [[death-in-battle]] and [[simoeisius]].
