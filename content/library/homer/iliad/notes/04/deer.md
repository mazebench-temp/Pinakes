---

νεβρός is a fawn, the young of the deer. In Book 1 Achilles gave Agamemnon “the eyes of a dog and the heart of a deer” (1.225; see [[dog-eyes-deer-heart]]). In Book 4 Agamemnon, going through the army, turns the deer on his own men. The fawns of his simile are worn out from running across a wide plain, and they stand still with no ἀλκή, no strength to defend themselves, left in their hearts. The Argives stand dazed (τεθηπότες) without having fought at all (see [[like-fawns]] and [[alke]]).

The picture belongs to the defeated. The same words, “dazed like fawns”, describe the twelve Trojan youths whom Achilles takes alive from the river to kill at Patroclus' pyre (21.26–29), and the Trojans who have fled into the city at the start of Book 22 are “like fawns” (22.1). Agamemnon's rebuke gives the Argives the posture of Trojans in flight.
