---
summary: At the start of Book 4 Hebe pours the nectar for the gods in assembly, and they pledge one another in golden cups while they look out on Troy (4.1–4).
---

{{quote:2490a58c-3f84-5ecf-b764-42420587d9c5}}

In Book 1 the nectar ended a quarrel: the lame Hephaestus poured it round, and the gods laughed (1.597–600). In Book 4 it begins one. The gods drink and look down on the city of the Trojans, and Zeus sets about provoking Hera (4.5–6). The server is Hebe, “Youth”, the goddess who pours for the gods (see [[hebe]] and [[cupbearing]]). The verb is ἐοινοχόει, literally “poured wine”, as in Book 1, and the cups are golden (see [[cup]]). The gods watch the war as a spectacle, and drink while they decide it (see [[the-bargain-of-zeus-and-hera]] and [[gods-and-mortals]]).
