---
summary: The common word ἔρις is not used in Book 4, but its goddess is, when Strife walks through the armies at the first clash. On Olympus Zeus gives way to Hera so that their quarrel will not become “a great cause of strife” (ἔρισμα) between them.
grc: [ἔρισμα]
en: [cause of strife]
---

Zeus yields to Hera: “Do as you wish; let this quarrel not become hereafter a great cause of strife between you and me” (4.37–38). ἔρισμα, “cause of strife, object of contention”, is formed from ἐρίζω and occurs nowhere else in Homer. The gods settle their quarrel (νεῖκος) before it can become ἔρις, and they settle it by trading cities (see [[the-bargain-of-zeus-and-hera]]). In Book 1 Hephaestus warned his parents against quarreling like this “for the sake of mortals” (ἐριδαίνετον, 1.574), and they ended it with laughter. In Book 4 the settlement is paid for by men.

When the armies meet, the word is a goddess. Strife, “the sister and companion of man-slaying Ares”, casts “the quarrel, common to all” (νεῖκος) into the midst of both armies (4.440–445; see [[strife]]). The Greek text prints her name with a capital, and the translation keeps the two words apart, ἔρις “strife” and νεῖκος “quarrel” (see [[neikos]]).
