---
summary: Athena is “the daughter of Zeus” when she wards off the arrow and when she rouses the Achaeans.
---

The formula marks Athena's two acts for the Achaeans in the book. She is “the daughter of Zeus, driver of spoil” as she turns the arrow from Menelaus (4.128; [[driver-of-spoil]]), and “the daughter of Zeus, most glorious Tritogeneia” as she rouses the Achaeans against the Trojans (4.515; [[tritogeneia]], [[most-glorious]]). In Book 3 the title belonged to Aphrodite at her rescue of Paris; in Book 4 it is Athena's.
