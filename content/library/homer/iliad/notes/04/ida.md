---
summary: The mother of Simoeisius bore him beside the Simoeis as she came down from Ida, where she had gone with her parents to watch over the flocks (4.474–476).
---

Ἴδηθεν κατιοῦσα, “coming down from Ida” (4.475). Book 4 shows the mountain as the Trojans' pasture. Simoeisius' grandparents kept their flocks there, and his mother was on her way down when he was born. The same slopes are pasture for the royal house: Aphrodite bore Aeneas to Anchises as he herded cattle (5.313; 2.820–821), and Achilles caught two sons of Priam as they tended sheep on the spurs of Ida (11.104–106; see [[antiphus-son-of-priam]]). The river by which Simoeisius was born and named flows down from the mountain (12.19–22; see [[simoeis]] and [[simoeisius]]).
