ἐσθλός (“good, noble, brave”) and ἀγαθός (“good”) are the common words of praise for a man's worth. Used of persons, the translation renders both “brave,” as it renders the old ἠΰς of this entry's formulas. In Book 4 ἐσθλός names Pandarus' “brave companions,” who hold their shields in front of him as he strings the bow (4.113); the foot soldiers “many and brave” whom Nestor sets behind the chariots (4.298; [[battle-order]]); Echepolus, the first man killed in battle in the poem, “brave among the champions” (4.458; [[echepolus]]); and Leucus, the “brave companion” of Odysseus (4.491; [[leucus]]). ἀγαθός is used of Menelaus in the boast Agamemnon imagines a Trojan making over his tomb: Agamemnon has gone home, “leaving brave Menelaus behind” (4.181; [[overweening]]). The same ἀγαθός is part of Menelaus' formula [[good-at-the-war-cry]].
