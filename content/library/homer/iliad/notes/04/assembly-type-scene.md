summary: Book 4 opens on the gods already in assembly around Zeus (4.1–4). There is no summons and no dissolution; the debate ends in a bargain between Zeus and Hera, and Athena is sent down.

{{quote:2490a58c-3f84-5ecf-b764-42420587d9c5}}

ἠγορόωντο, “were holding assembly”, is from ἀγοράομαι, the verb of the assembly (ἀγορή). The poem joins the gods when their assembly is already in session, seated around Zeus on the golden floor, with Hebe pouring the nectar (see [[olympians]] and [[nectar]]). The steps of the human assembly are missing: no summons, no gathering line, no speaker rising and sitting, no dissolution (see [[assembly]]). The speeches are between Zeus and Hera alone. Athena and Hera “sat side by side” and muttered, and Athena kept silent in her anger (4.20–23). The meeting ends in the gods' way, with a bargain between husband and wife and an order carried out at once: Athena darts down from Olympus (4.68–74; see [[the-bargain-of-zeus-and-hera]]).

In Book 8 Zeus himself calls the gods to assembly, “Zeus who delights in thunder held an assembly of the gods” (8.2), and forbids them to help either side.
