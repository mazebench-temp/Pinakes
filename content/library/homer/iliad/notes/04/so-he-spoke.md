---
summary: The closing formulas of Book 4 turn from speech to deed: the gods obey, Athena goes, Pandarus is persuaded, the herald and the healer go, the king passes on from leader to leader, and Diomedes, rebuked, says nothing.
grc: ["ὣς φάτʼ", "ὣς ὃ γέρων ὄτρυνε"]
en: [So the old man urged them on]
except: [4.374]
---

The closings of Book 4: 4.20, 68, 73, 104, 192, 198, 208, 272, 292, 310, 326, 364, 401, 419, 514. The new forms are ὣς φάτʼ, “So spoke”, with the speaker named in the same verse (4.104, 4.514), and ὣς ὃ γέρων ὄτρυνε, “So the old man urged them on” (4.310), which closes Nestor's instructions with a verb of exhortation. The tis-speech of 4.82–84 has its own close, “So a man would say” (4.85; see [[tis-speech]]).

**Obedience.** Hera's bargain is closed with the line of obedience: “So she spoke, and the father of men and gods did not disobey” (4.68), the words used of Athena obeying Hera in Book 2 (2.166). Talthybius “heard him and did not disobey” (4.198). Zeus' order to Athena ends in the departure verse, “So saying he urged on Athena, who was eager already” (4.73; see [[litotes]] and [[messenger-scene]]).

**Persuasion.** “So spoke Athena, and she persuaded the mind of the fool” (4.104). The second half of the verse gives the narrator's judgment of Pandarus in the moment he agrees (see [[pandarus]]). The herald's summons “stirred the heart in his breast” (4.208), the half-line that closed Agamemnon's test in Book 2 and Aphrodite's summons in Book 3 (2.142, 3.395); here it sends a healer to his work.

**The review.** In Agamemnon's round the closing formulas mark the end of each visit: “So he spoke, and the son of Atreus passed on, glad at heart” (4.272, 4.326), and “So saying he left them there and went on to others” (4.292, 4.364; see [[the-review]]). The last visit ends differently: “So he spoke, and mighty Diomedes said nothing to him, in awe of the rebuke of the king he revered” (4.401–402). Then ἦ ῥα καί, “He spoke, and”, leads from speech straight into the leap from the chariot (4.419).

**The god.** Apollo's exhortation from Pergamus is closed by the narrator's word for him: “So spoke the dread god from the city” (4.514).

In Agamemnon's story of Tydeus, ὡς φάσαν, “so said those who saw him” (4.374), is not the formula but a clause of report inside the speech.
