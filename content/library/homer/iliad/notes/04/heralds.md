summary: Agamemnon sends Talthybius, “the divine herald”, to fetch Machaon to his wounded brother, and the herald finds him and brings him (4.192–209).
grc: [κήρυκα]
passages:
  - 4.192-209 | Talthybius fetches Machaon
---

“He spoke, and addressed Talthybius, the divine herald” (θεῖον κήρυκα, 4.192). θεῖος, “divine”, is the herald's epithet: heralds are “messengers of Zeus and of men” (1.334), and their persons are sacred. Agamemnon gives him a message for the healer, and “the herald heard him and did not disobey” (4.198). He goes through the army looking for Machaon, finds him standing among his men, and gives him the king's words, changed only as delivery requires (4.199–207; see [[messenger-scene]] and [[message-repeated]]).

In Book 1 Talthybius went to take Briseis from Achilles, and in Book 3 to fetch a lamb for the oath (1.320–348, 3.118–120). Here, after the oath has been broken, the herald's errand is to bring help to the wounded man. See [[talthybius]] and [[healing]].
