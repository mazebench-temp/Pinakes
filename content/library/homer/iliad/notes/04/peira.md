---

In Book 2 πεῖρα was Agamemnon's test of the army (2.73). In Book 4 the verb comes three times, all on Olympus, in its other sense: to try, to set about. “At once the son of Cronus set about (ἐπειρᾶτο) provoking Hera with cutting words” (4.5): Zeus' proposal of peace is an attempt to provoke, perhaps a test of how far Hera will go (see [[kertomia]]). Hera's answer turns the verb on the war. Let Zeus bid Athena go into the battle “and contrive (πειρᾶν) that the Trojans, against the exultant Achaeans, be the first to do harm in defiance of the oaths” (4.64–67), and Zeus gives the order in the same words (4.70–72). πειρᾶν ὥς κε, “try how, see to it that”, puts the breach in the hands of the goddess and leaves its manner to her. She finds her way through Pandarus (4.86–104). See [[oaths-and-their-breaking]].
