**The making.** Athena has persuaded Pandarus to shoot Menelaus, and the poem stops to tell where his bow came from:

{{quote:5dd436c6-5897-53c3-9423-4ede84cf22d3}}

The bow is ἐΰξοον, “polished”, literally “well scraped”, the word for worked wood (of a chariot, 2.390). Its horn came from a wild goat that Pandarus shot himself, lying in wait in a hunter's hide (ἐν προδοκῇσι) as it came out from a rock; the arrow took it in the chest, and it fell backwards on the rock (4.106–108; see [[goats]]). ἴξαλος, usually taken as “leaping”, occurs only here. The horns were ἑκκαιδεκάδωρα, “sixteen palms long”: a δῶρον is a hand's breadth, so they measured well over a meter, and the text does not say whether that was each horn or the two together. A κεραοξόος τέκτων, “a craftsman who worked in horn”, dressed them and fitted them together (ἤραρε), smoothed the whole, and set on it a golden κορώνη, literally “crow”, the hooked tip on which the loop of the string was caught. κεραοξόος and ἑκκαιδεκάδωρος occur only here.

What kind of bow this is has long been argued. Most scholars take it for a composite bow, built of horn and sinew on a core of wood: the short, powerful bow of the Near East, which bends back on itself when unstrung, and of which examples survive in Egypt, among them the bows from the tomb of Tutankhamun. Homer knows bows of this kind. Teucer's and Odysseus' are παλίντονα, “back-bent” (8.266, 15.443; Odyssey 21.11), and before he strings his own, Odysseus turns it this way and that, “to see whether worms had eaten the horns” (μὴ κέρα ἶπες ἔδοιεν, Odyssey 21.395). Others have taken the lines literally, as a bow made of two horns joined at a grip. The poem names only the horn and the gold.

**Stringing.** “He strung the bow against the ground and laid it carefully down, leaning it there” (4.112–113). τανύω, “stretch”, is the verb of stringing, and a strong bow had to be braced against the ground to bend it far enough to slip the loop over the tip. In the Odyssey the suitors cannot string Odysseus' bow, and he strings it “as easily as a man who knows the lyre and song stretches a string on a new peg” (Odyssey 21.404–409). Meanwhile Pandarus' companions hold their shields in front of him, so that the Achaeans will not spring up before Menelaus is hit (4.113–115). An archer fights from cover, as Teucer shoots from behind Ajax's shield and ducks back under it “like a child under its mother” (8.266–272; see [[shield]]).

**The draw.** He takes the arrow from his quiver, fits it “to the string”, and prays to Apollo with a vow (4.116–121; see [[arrows]], [[quiver]] and [[prayer-type-scene]]). Then “he drew, gripping the notches and the ox-sinew string together; the string he brought to his breast, and the iron to the bow” (4.122–123). The notches (γλυφίδες) are the nock of the arrow, and the head is of iron (see [[iron]]). The draw is full: the bow is bent “into a circle”.

{{quote:4df5ff6c-adce-5c77-b61c-9b5901fca372}}

βιός is the old word for the bow, used in Book 1 of Apollo's: “terrible was the twang of the silver bow” (1.49). The archer who prays to Apollo draws a bow that sings like the god's. λίγξε, “twanged”, occurs only here.

**The archer.** The Iliad values the bow less than the spear. Diomedes, wounded by Paris' arrow, calls him “archer” as an insult (τοξότα, 11.385), and Agamemnon rebukes the slack Argives as ἰόμωροι, rendered “arrow-fighters” (4.242, again at 14.479). If the word comes from ἰός, “arrow”, it is formed like ἐγχεσίμωροι, “fighters with the spear” (2.692, 2.840; see [[fighters-with-the-spear]]); its meaning was already disputed in antiquity (see [[arrow-fighters]] and [[obscure-iomoroi]]). Pandarus shoots from behind shields at a man who trusts the truce, and the narrator calls him a fool (ἄφρων, 4.104). In Book 5 his bow fails him: he has hit two of the best Achaeans and only roused them, and he swears to break the bow and burn it if he ever gets home (5.206–216). The Catalogue made the bow a gift of Apollo (2.827); here Pandarus made it from his own kill (see [[the-bow-of-pandarus]] and [[pandarus]]).
