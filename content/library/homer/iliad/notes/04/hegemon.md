The review of the army is a review of its leaders: Idomeneus, “leader of the Cretans” (Κρητῶν ἀγός, 4.265), the two Ajaxes, “leaders of the bronze-armored Argives” (Ἀργείων ἡγήτορε, a dual, 4.285), Nestor, Menestheus and Odysseus, Diomedes and Sthenelus (4.251–421). Menelaus himself is “leader of the Achaeans” when Talthybius summons the healer (ἀρχὸν Ἀχαιῶν, 4.205).

When the armies advance, the leaders are the only Achaeans who speak: “to his own men each one of the leaders gave orders; and the rest went in silence” (4.428–429; see [[silence]] and [[semaino]]). In the first battle the leaders are the ones who fall. Elephenor, “leader of the great-hearted Abantes” (4.464), is killed dragging a corpse. Peirous, “leader of the Thracian men” (Θρῃκῶν ἀγὸς ἀνδρῶν, 4.519), kills Diores, who led the Epeians, and is killed in turn by Thoas; the book leaves them lying in the dust beside each other, “one of the Thracians, the other of the bronze-armored Epeians, both of them leaders” (ἡγεμόνες, 4.536–538). In Agamemnon's story the ambush laid for Tydeus also had two leaders (ἡγήτορες), Maeon and Polyphontes (4.393–395). See [[the-army-and-its-leaders]] and [[death-in-battle]].
