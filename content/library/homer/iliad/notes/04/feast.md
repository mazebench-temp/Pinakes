**The gods.** The book opens on Olympus:

{{quote:2490a58c-3f84-5ecf-b764-42420587d9c5}}

Hebe serves the nectar, and the gods pledge one another in golden cups while they look out at the city whose fate they are about to decide (see [[nectar]], [[cup]] and [[hebe]]). Zeus' love for that city rests on feasting too: his altar there “never lacked an equal share of the feast, libation and the savor of the burnt fat” (4.48–49; see [[equal-feast]] and [[sacrifice]]).

**The elders' feast.** The kings who sit in Agamemnon's council eat and drink at his table (see [[elders]]). Agamemnon praises Idomeneus by that table: he honors him above all the Danaans in war and in any other work, “and at the feast, when the sparkling wine of the elders is mixed in the mixing bowl by the best of the Argives”. The others drink a measured portion (δαιτρόν), but Idomeneus' cup, like the king's own, always stands full, to drink whenever his heart bids him (4.257–263; see [[wine]] and [[mixing-bowl]]). δαίς, “feast”, and δαιτρόν, “portion”, both come from δαίομαι, “divide”: the feast is a sharing out, and honor is the larger share.

The same honors are turned into a rebuke. Agamemnon tells Menestheus and Odysseus that it would be fitting for them to stand among the foremost, “for you two are the first to hear my call to the feast whenever we Achaeans prepare a feast for the elders”, and they are glad to eat roast meat and drink honey-sweet wine for as long as they like (4.341–346). Honor at the table is a debt paid in battle. Sarpedon states the rule in the same words in Book 12: he and Glaucus are honored in Lycia with seats, meat and full cups, and so they must “stand among the foremost and meet the blazing battle” (12.310–316; 12.316 = 4.342). See [[exhortation]] and [[honor-and-dishonor]].

**The Cadmeians.** In Agamemnon's story, Tydeus came to Thebes on an embassy and found the Cadmeians “feasting in the house of mighty Eteocles” (4.385–386). A stranger alone among many, he challenged them to contests and won them all (4.387–390). Athena tells it with a detail of her own: she had bidden him feast quietly in their halls (5.805). See [[embassy]] and [[contests]].
