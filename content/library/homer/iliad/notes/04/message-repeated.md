---
summary: Three passages of Book 4 are given twice: Hera's words become Zeus' order to Athena (4.64–67, 4.70–72), Athena's instructions become Pandarus' prayer (4.101–103, 4.119–121), and Agamemnon's message becomes the herald's summons to Machaon (4.193–197, 4.204–207).
passages:
  - 4.64-72 | Hera's words become Zeus' order
  - 4.193-207 | Agamemnon's message to Machaon
---

**Hera to Zeus to Athena.** Hera ends the bargain by telling Zeus what to do: “bid Athena quickly go into the dread din of battle between the Trojans and the Achaeans, and contrive that the Trojans, against the exultant Achaeans, be the first to do harm in defiance of the oaths” (4.64–67). Zeus obeys and gives the order:

{{quote:6641d430-827c-5d56-95ba-0ffbb6f9fa71}}

The first verse is his own, and shorter: “Go quickly to the army, among the Trojans and the Achaeans” (4.70). The last two are Hera's, word for word (4.71–72 = 4.66–67). The father of gods and men passes on his wife's order to his daughter unchanged, and the order he passes on is the plan to break the oath (see [[zeus]], [[hera]] and [[oath]]).

**Athena to Pandarus.** Athena tells Pandarus to pray to Apollo and vow a hecatomb, and he prays in her three verses, with one word changed, εὔχεο, “pray”, to εὔχετο, “prayed” (4.101–103, 4.119–121). The instruction becomes the deed without a word of his own (see [[prayer-type-scene]] and [[pandarus]]).

**Agamemnon to Machaon.** Agamemnon tells Talthybius: “Talthybius, call Machaon here as fast as you can, the man who is the son of Asclepius, the noble healer, so that he may see warlike Menelaus, the son of Atreus, whom someone well skilled with the bow has hit with an arrow, one of the Trojans or Lycians: fame for him, and grief for us” (4.193–197). The herald finds Machaon and says: “Rise, son of Asclepius; powerful Agamemnon calls you, so that you may see warlike Menelaus, leader of the Achaeans, whom someone well skilled with the bow has hit with an arrow, one of the Trojans or Lycians: fame for him, and grief for us” (4.204–207).

The changes are those delivery requires, and one more. The herald addresses Machaon by his father's name (Ἀσκληπιάδη) and names the sender. Where the king called his brother “the son of Atreus”, the herald calls him “leader of the Achaeans” (ἀρχὸν Ἀχαιῶν, 4.205), a title he owes the king's brother. And he keeps the last two verses exactly, the king's words of grief with the rest: “fame for him, and grief for us” (4.197 = 4.207; see [[fame-for-him-grief-for-us]], [[kleos]], [[penthos]] and [[talthybius]]).
