---
summary: Agamemnon, sending for the healer, says that whoever shot Menelaus has won fame, “and grief for us”. The fame belongs to an archer no one can name, won by breaking a truce.
grc: [κλειτὴν, κλειτοὺς]
en: [glorious]
passages:
  - 4.193-197 | Agamemnon sends for Machaon
  - 4.204-207 | Talthybius finds Machaon
---

The noun comes in one line, spoken twice. Agamemnon tells Talthybius to fetch Machaon to see Menelaus, “whom someone well skilled with the bow has hit with an arrow, one of the Trojans or Lycians: fame for him, and grief for us” (τῷ μὲν κλέος, ἄμμι δὲ πένθος, 4.196–197). Talthybius gives Machaon the message in the same words (4.206–207; see [[message-repeated]] and [[fame-for-him-grief-for-us]]).

The man who has the fame is “someone” (τις). Agamemnon does not know his name or even his people, only his skill; the audience knows him as Pandarus, the son of Lycaon. κλέος here is what will be said of the deed, and it does not depend on whether the deed was right. The shot broke the truce, and still it wins its archer fame, because it wounded a king's brother in front of the army. It is the reward Athena promised when she tempted him, though she called it by other names: thanks and glory (χάριν καὶ κῦδος) from the Trojans and gifts from Alexander (4.95–97; see [[charis]] and [[kydos]]). Pandarus will not live to enjoy it. Diomedes kills him in the next book (5.290–296).

The adjective κλειτός, “famous, glorious”, comes twice more. Pandarus vows Apollo “a glorious hecatomb” (κλειτὴν ἑκατόμβην, 4.102, 4.120), and in Agamemnon's story Tydeus and Polynices ask the men of Mycenae for “famous allies” (κλειτοὺς ἐπικούρους, 4.379), the phrase used of Troy's allies (3.451; see [[famous]]).
