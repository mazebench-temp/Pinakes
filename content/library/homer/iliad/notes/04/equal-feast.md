---
summary: Zeus gives the gods' view of the equal feast: his altar at Troy “never lacked an equal share of the feast, libation and the savor of the burnt fat” (4.48–49).
---

The half-line of the feast, οὐδέ τι θυμὸς ἐδεύετο δαιτὸς ἐΐσης, “no one's heart lacked an equal share of the feast”, closed the feasts of Chryse, of Olympus and of the kings in Books 1 and 2 (1.468, 1.602, 2.431). Zeus changes its subject: οὐ γάρ μοί ποτε βωμὸς ἐδεύετο δαιτὸς ἐΐσης, “my altar never lacked an equal share of the feast” (4.48). The equal share is now the gods' part of the sacrifice, the libation and the savor of fat, which Zeus calls “the prize allotted to us” (γέρας, 4.49). At the feast of men the equal share is each man's due; at the altar it is the god's. Zeus repeats the two lines in Book 24, of the offerings of Hector (24.69–70).
