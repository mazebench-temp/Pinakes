{{quote:118b8e20-94d4-56d8-9fd6-3fee41c4159d}}

Agenor, “great-hearted” (μεγάθυμος; [[great-hearted]]), enters the poem as he kills. Book 4 does not name his father. The first Achaean kill brings the first Trojan one at once. He sees Elephenor “dragging the corpse” and strikes the one place that the man's haste has uncovered, “his side, which was exposed beside his shield as he stooped” (4.468). His weapon is a ξυστόν, literally a “polished” shaft, the thrusting spear, and it is “bronze-fitted” (χαλκήρης; see [[bronze-fitted]]). λῦσε δὲ γυῖα, “and loosened his limbs”, is the second death formula of the poem (see [[loosened-his-limbs]]).

The first man to try to strip a body pays for it with his life, and the fight closes over him: “they like wolves leapt upon one another, and man hurled man about” (4.471–472; see [[like-wolves]]). See [[elephenor]] and [[the-first-battle]].
