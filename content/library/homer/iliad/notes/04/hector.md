---
summary: Hector is named once in the first battle of the poem, as he gives ground. When Odysseus kills Democoon, “the champions and shining Hector gave ground” (4.505).
---

χώρησαν δʼ ὑπό τε πρόμαχοι καὶ φαίδιμος Ἕκτωρ, “the champions and shining Hector gave ground” (4.505). φαίδιμος (“shining, glorious”; see [[phaidimos]]) is one of his standing epithets. His first appearance in battle is a retreat. Odysseus has just killed Democoon, a son of Priam, to avenge his companion Leucus (see [[democoon]]); the Trojan front gives way, the Argives shout and drag away the dead, and Apollo, watching from Pergamus, calls on the Trojans to stand, for Achilles is not fighting (4.505–513; see [[apollo]]).

The line is a formula. It returns when the Trojans give way before Patroclus (16.588) and before Ajax in the fight over Patroclus' body, where it is followed, as here, by “the Argives shouted aloud, and dragged away the dead” (17.316–317 = 4.505–506).

Hector has no other part in Book 4. In Book 5 Sarpedon rebukes him for holding back while the allies fight, and he leaps from his chariot and rallies the Trojans (5.471–496).
