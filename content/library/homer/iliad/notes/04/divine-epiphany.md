summary: Athena comes down like a shooting star, seen by both armies but not known, and in the likeness of Laodocus persuades Pandarus, who never learns who spoke to him (4.73–104). Later Apollo shouts to the Trojans from Pergamus, and Athena goes unseen through the Achaean ranks (4.507–516).

**A sign, not a goddess.** Athena darts down from Olympus “as the son of crooked-counseling Cronus sends a star as a portent for sailors or for a wide army of men” (4.75–76), leaps into the middle, “and amazement held those who watched, the horse-taming Trojans and the well-greaved Achaeans” (4.79–80). θάμβος, “amazement”, is the mortal's response to a god, as when Achilles turned and knew Athena (1.199) and Helen knew Aphrodite (3.398). But the armies see a sign, not a goddess, and they ask what it means: war again, or friendship (4.81–84; see [[shooting-star]] and [[tis-speech]]).

**In disguise.** She slips into the Trojan throng “in the likeness of a man, Laodocus, son of Antenor, a strong spearman” (4.86–87; see [[laodocus]]). The shape is chosen to be trusted, a Trojan of good family, as the Dream took Nestor's shape (2.20–22) and Iris Polites' and Laodice's (2.791, 3.122). She finds Pandarus, stands near him and speaks, and he is persuaded (4.88–104). Odysseus and Hector knew a goddess by her voice (2.182, 2.807), and Helen knew Aphrodite by her beauty (3.396–398). Nothing shows that Pandarus knows who spoke to him, and the narrator calls him a fool (4.104; see [[pandarus]]).

**Voices over the battle.** When the Trojans give ground, Apollo, “looking down from Pergamus”, shouts to them (4.507–513; see [[pergamus]] and [[exhortation]]). He does not take a mortal's shape, and no one is said to see him; the narrator calls him “the dread god from the city” (4.514). On the other side Athena “went through the throng, wherever she saw them holding back” (4.515–516), as she went through the army with the aegis in Book 2 (2.446–452), unseen. At the height of the clash Ares and Athena drive the two armies, with Terror, Rout and Strife (4.439–445; see [[strife]]).
