---
summary: Pandarus' arrow passes through Menelaus' “elaborate” belt and his “intricately worked” corselet, two words of the same family.
grc: [δαιδαλέοιο]
en: [elaborate]
---

δαιδάλεος (“elaborately worked”) and πολυδαίδαλος (“of much cunning work”) are adjectives of the same family, from δαιδάλλω (“work with art”). The translation keeps them apart as “elaborate” and “intricately worked.”

{{quote:a7f48efd-9a91-5c3b-b0a6-7c2613822677}}

Each layer of the craftsmen's work is praised as the arrow goes through it. The fine work does not stop the arrow. Athena does, by guiding it to where the golden clasps of the belt met the doubled corselet, so that it only grazes the skin (4.132–133, 4.139; [[driver-of-spoil]]). In Book 3 the same epithet described the corselet of Paris, which Menelaus' spear pierced ({{ref:Iliad 3.358}}). See [[belt]], [[band]], [[corselet]].
