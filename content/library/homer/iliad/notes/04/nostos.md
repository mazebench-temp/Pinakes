Three homecomings are imagined in Book 4, and none is a happy one.

**Pandarus'.** Athena tells him to vow Apollo a glorious hecatomb of firstborn lambs “when you come home (οἴκαδε νοστήσας) to the city of sacred Zeleia”, and he vows it in her words (4.101–103, 4.119–121). The vow depends on a return that does not come: Diomedes kills him in the next book (5.290–296). See [[pandarus]] and [[zeleia]].

**The army's.** If Menelaus dies, Agamemnon says, the Achaeans “will remember their native land” at once, and he will come home in disgrace, leaving Helen to the Trojans (4.171–175). He imagines a Trojan mocking him on his brother's tomb: Agamemnon “has gone home to his dear native land with empty ships” (4.180–181). In Book 2 the army nearly made that return (2.155–156); here it is Agamemnon's fear. See [[elenchos]].

**Maeon's.** Tydeus killed all fifty of the Cadmeians who ambushed him, “and let only one go home” (ἕνα δʼ οἶον ἵει οἶκον δὲ νέεσθαι, 4.397), Maeon, obeying the portents of the gods. One man is sent home to tell what happened. See [[maeon]] and [[tydeus-at-thebes]].
