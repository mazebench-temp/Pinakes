---
summary: Paris does not appear in Book 4, but its first scenes turn on him. Zeus says that Aphrodite saved him from death, and Athena tempts Pandarus with the gifts that “king Alexander” would give for Menelaus' death.
refs: [4.10-12, 4.97-98]
---

On Olympus Zeus teases Hera and Athena with the goddess who helps Paris: laughter-loving Aphrodite “is always at his side and wards off doom from him, and now she has saved him, when he thought that he would die” (4.11–12; see [[aphrodite-rescues-paris]]). He does not name him. The victory, Zeus says, is Menelaus' (4.13).

Athena's temptation of [[Pandarus]] turns on him. The archer will win thanks and glory from all the Trojans, “and most of all from king Alexander” (Ἀλεξάνδρῳ βασιλῆϊ, 4.96), who would give him splendid gifts if he saw Menelaus dead on the pyre (4.97–99). Paris has most to gain from the death of Helen's husband. The promise of thanks from all the Trojans sits oddly after Book 3, where Paris “was hateful to them all, like black doom” (3.454).
