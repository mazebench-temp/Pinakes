**The wine of the elders.** Agamemnon praises Idomeneus by the honors of his table. When “the sparkling wine of the elders” (γερούσιον αἴθοπα οἶνον) is mixed in the mixing bowl by the best of the Argives, the other long-haired Achaeans drink a measured portion, but Idomeneus' cup, like the king's, always stands full (4.259–263). γερούσιος, “of the elders”, names the wine kept for the council of kings; Alcinous uses the same phrase of the wine his elders drink in his hall (γερούσιον αἴθοπα οἶνον, Odyssey 13.8). See [[elders]], [[mixing-bowl]] and [[cup]].

To Menestheus and Odysseus, whom he rebukes, Agamemnon recalls the same feasts: they are glad there “to eat roast meat and to drink cups of honey-sweet wine for as long as you like” (οἴνου … μελιηδέος, 4.345–346; see [[honey-sweet]] and [[feast]]). The wine of honor is a debt to be paid in the front line.

**Unmixed.** Agamemnon lists among the pledges of the broken oath “the libations of unmixed wine” (σπονδαί τʼ ἄκρητοι, 4.159), Nestor's line from Book 2 (2.341). Men drank their wine mixed with water; the wine poured for the gods to seal an agreement was neat (see [[libation]] and [[oath]]).

**Nectar.** The gods drink nectar, but the verb for serving it is the verb for pouring wine: Hebe ἐοινοχόει, “poured out”, the nectar (4.3), as Hephaestus did in Book 1 (1.598; see [[nectar]] and [[cupbearing]]).
