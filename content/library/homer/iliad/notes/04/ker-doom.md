summary: Zeus teases Hera and Athena that Aphrodite is always at Paris' side and “wards off doom from him” (κῆρας ἀμύνει).
grc: [κῆρας]
passages:
  - 4.7-12 | Zeus teases Hera and Athena
---

Zeus opens Book 4 by comparing the goddesses. Hera and Athena are Menelaus' helpers but sit apart and look on, “while laughter-loving Aphrodite is always at his side and wards off doom (κῆρας) from him, and now she has saved him, when he thought that he would die” (4.10–12). The plural κῆρες are the dooms of death that come for each man; to “ward them off” is to keep a man alive. Zeus is describing Aphrodite's rescue of Paris at the end of the duel (3.373–382; see [[aphrodite-rescues-paris]]), and he means it as a taunt: the goddess of love does more for her man than the goddesses of war do for theirs. See [[kertomia]].
