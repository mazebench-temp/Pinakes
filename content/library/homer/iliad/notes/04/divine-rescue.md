summary: Zeus taunts Hera and Athena with Aphrodite's rescue of Paris (4.10–12), and Athena, who arranged the shot, turns Pandarus' arrow from a mortal wound (4.127–133): a rescue without mist or flight. The first battle ends with an imagined man led through the fighting by Athena's hand (4.541–542).
grc: [ἐξεσάωσεν]
en: [she has saved him]
refs: [4.10-12, 4.127-133, 4.541-542]
---

**Remembered.** Zeus opens the book by setting the two helpers of Menelaus against the one helper of Paris. Hera and Athena sit apart and watch, while laughter-loving Aphrodite “is always at his side and wards off doom from him, and now she has saved him, when he thought that he would die” (4.10–12). ἐξεσάωσεν, “saved him out of it”, is the rescue of Book 3 in a word (see [[aphrodite-rescues-paris]] and [[aphrodite]]).

**The arrow turned.** Athena has contrived the shot, and she stands before its target:

{{quote:90ce77e5-b5e5-516b-bda1-7e6075564a18}}

There is no “had not” and no snatching away in mist. The goddess turns the missile aside, as she will turn Hector's spear from Achilles with a breath (20.438–440), and guides it to the belt, where the armor is double (see [[belt]] and [[mother-and-fly]]). She wanted the oath broken, not Menelaus dead, and her rescue saves the victim of her own plan.

**Imagined.** The first battle ends with an onlooker who could walk through it unhurt only if Pallas Athena “should lead him by the hand, and keep off the force of the missiles” (4.541–542), and even he could not make light of it (see [[apostrophe]]).
