---
summary: Agamemnon, lord of men, shudders at his brother's wound, rejoices at the Cretans, and rebukes Menestheus and Odysseus.
---

The title marks three moments of Agamemnon's day in Book 4. “Then Agamemnon, lord of men, shuddered when he saw the black blood flowing down from the wound” (4.148–149; [[dark-blood]]). In the review, seeing the Cretans arming, “Agamemnon, lord of men, rejoiced” (4.255), and seeing Menestheus and Odysseus standing apart, “Agamemnon, lord of men, rebuked them” (4.336). The formula serves for joy and for anger alike; the other reactions in the review are given to “powerful Agamemnon” ([[powerful]], [[the-review]]).
