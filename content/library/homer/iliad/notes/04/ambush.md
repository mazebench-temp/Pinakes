**Tydeus.** In Agamemnon's story, Tydeus has come to Thebes on an embassy and beaten the Cadmeians at their own feast in every contest (see [[embassy]] and [[contests]]). They answer with an ambush:

{{quote:7c0ba908-601b-5172-acf4-16a57a6e9274}}

πυκινὸν λόχον, “a close ambush”, is a band set to wait for one man: fifty young men under two leaders, Maeon son of Haemon and Polyphontes son of Autophonus. Tydeus kills them all and sends Maeon home, “obeying the portents of the gods” (see [[maeon]] and [[polyphontes]]). The ambush that should have been the hardest test of courage (13.276–286) is a trap laid by the many for one man, and it ends in a “shameful doom” (ἀεικέα πότμον) for the many. Athena and Diomedes tell the same return from Thebes in Books 5 and 10: in Diomedes' words, on his way back from the Asopus Tydeus “devised very grim deeds” with Athena's help (10.289–290). See [[tydeus]] and [[tydeus-at-thebes]].

**The hunter.** Pandarus' bow was made from the horns of a wild goat that he shot “as it came out from a rock, lying in wait for it in ambush” (δεδεγμένος ἐν προδοκῇσι, 4.107). προδοκή is a place of waiting, a hunter's hide. The archer who lay in wait for a goat now shoots from behind his companions' shields at a man who trusts the truce (4.113–115; see [[bow]] and [[pandarus]]).
