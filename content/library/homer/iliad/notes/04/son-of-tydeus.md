---
summary: Agamemnon names Diomedes by his father when he finds him and when he rebukes him, and his rebuke is the story of Tydeus.
grc: [Τυδέος υἱὲ]
---

Agamemnon finds “the son of Tydeus, high-hearted Diomedes” (4.365; [[high-hearted]]) and opens his rebuke with the father's name: “Ah, son of wise-hearted Tydeus, tamer of horses” (4.370). The patronymic becomes the argument. Tydeus, he says, never cowered; he went on an embassy to Thebes and beat the Cadmeians alone, “but the son he fathered is worse than he in battle, though better in assembly” (4.399–400; [[tydeus-at-thebes]], [[fathers-and-sons]]). Diomedes accepts the rebuke in silence. Sthenelus, “the son of Capaneus,” answers for the sons (4.367, 4.403–410; [[sthenelus]]). In Book 5 Athena spurs Diomedes on with the same comparison (Il. 5.800–813).
