summary: As the armies advance, the Trojans are like the countless ewes of a rich man, bleating without pause at milking time when they hear their lambs; their allies have no common language (4.433–438).
grc: [ὄϊες]
passages:
  - 4.433-438 | Sheep in the fold
---

The sheep of Book 4 are ewes: the words that go with ὄϊες (4.433), “countless”, “being milked” and “bleating”, are feminine. They stand in the yard (αὐλή) of a πολυπάμων, “a man of many possessions”, in their thousands, milked of their white milk (γάλα λευκόν), and they bleat without pause as they hear their lambs (see [[lambs]]). At milking the young are kept apart from their mothers; the Cyclops milks his ewes and goats and only then puts a young one under each (Odyssey 9.244–245). The fold is loud with many voices, each calling on its own, and so the Trojan army is loud, “for not all of them had the same speech” (4.437; see [[sheep-in-the-fold]] and [[trojan-allies]]).

In Book 3 Priam saw the Achaean ranks as a flock, with Odysseus going through them like a thick-fleeced ram through a great flock of white sheep (3.196–198; see [[ram-among-the-sheep]]). In Book 4 the flock is the Trojan army, and there is no ram in the picture, only noise. The silent Achaeans are given the image of the sea (see [[waves-on-the-shore]]).
