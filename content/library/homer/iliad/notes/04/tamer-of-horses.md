---
summary: Book 4 calls the Trojans horse-tamers five times, from the armies watching Athena's descent to Apollo's call to battle, and gives the singular to Tydeus.
grc: [ἱπποδάμοισιν, ἱππόδαμοι]
---

The line “the horse-taming Trojans and the well-greaved Achaeans” describes both armies watching Athena come down like a star (4.80; [[well-greaved]], [[shooting-star]]), as it described them watching the duel in Book 3, and the narrator uses it again as the battalions begin to move (4.333). Odysseus uses the epithet twice in his angry answer to Agamemnon, of the enemy he fights: “whenever we Achaeans rouse sharp Ares against the horse-taming Trojans” (4.351–352; [[sharp]]) and “the champions of the horse-taming Trojans” (4.354–355). At the turn of the battle Apollo calls on the Trojans by their epithet: “Rise up, horse-taming Trojans” (4.509; [[flesh-cutting]]).

The singular is Tydeus', in Agamemnon's rebuke of his son: “son of wise-hearted Tydeus, tamer of horses” (4.370; [[wise-hearted]], [[horse-driving]]). In Book 2 the Dream used the same words of Atreus when it addressed Agamemnon ({{ref:Iliad 2.23}}).
