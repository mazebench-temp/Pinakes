---
summary: Book 4 has a second word for gleaming bronze, αἶθοψ, as Odysseus goes through the champions to avenge Leucus.
grc: [αἴθοπι χαλκῷ]
---

αἶθοψ (“fiery-looking, flashing,” from αἴθω, “burn, blaze”) is an epithet of bronze and of wine, where the translation renders it “sparkling” (“the sparkling wine of the elders,” 4.259; [[wine]]). With bronze it means what νῶροψ means, and the formula κεκορυθμένος αἴθοπι χαλκῷ describes a man “armored in gleaming bronze.” Odysseus, angry at the death of his companion Leucus, “went through the champions, armored in gleaming bronze, and came up close and stood and threw his shining spear” (4.495–496; [[phaeinos]], [[leucus]]). The gleam belongs to the moment of attack: the Trojans shrink back from him as he throws, and his spear kills Democoon (4.497–504; [[democoon]], [[odysseus]]).
