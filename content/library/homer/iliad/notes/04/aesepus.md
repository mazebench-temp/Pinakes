---
summary: When Athena finds Pandarus, he is standing among the men who “had followed him from the streams of the Aesepus” (4.91).
---

The line recalls the Catalogue, where the men of Zeleia “drink the dark water of the Aesepus” (2.825; see [[zeleia]]). The river marks the home of Pandarus' men, and the same frame of lines introduces Machaon, standing among the men who “had followed him from horse-pasturing Tricca” (4.202; see [[machaon]] and [[tricca]]). See [[pandarus]].
