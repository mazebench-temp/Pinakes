summary: No lambs are offered in Book 2, but their kin appear twice: the rams sacrificed at Athens to Erechtheus, and the flocks of Thessalian Iton, “mother of flocks”.
grc: [μήλων]
en: [flocks]
---

Book 2's sacrifices are of cattle: hecatombs at Aulis and Agamemnon's fat ox (see [[sacrifice]] and [[cattle]]). The small livestock appear only in the Catalogue. At Athens the young men seek the favor of Erechtheus “with bulls and rams” (ταύροισι καὶ ἀρνειοῖς) as the years come round (2.550–551; see [[bulls]] and [[athena-and-erechtheus]]). ἀρνειός, “ram”, is formed from the stem of ἀρήν, “lamb” (see [[ram]]). In Thessaly, Protesilaus' realm includes Iton, “mother of flocks” (μητέρα μήλων, 2.696; see [[iton]]); μῆλα are sheep and goats together, the flocks of the pasture.
