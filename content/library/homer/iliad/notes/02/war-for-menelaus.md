summary: Book 2 names Helen as the cause. Hera and Athena speak of “Argive Helen, for whose sake many of the Achaeans have perished”, and Nestor and Menelaus want the Trojans to pay for “the struggles and groans for Helen's sake”.
passages:
  - 2.157-165 | Hera to Athena
  - 2.354-356 | The wife of some Trojan
  - 2.586-590 | Menelaus longs to pay them back
---

In Book 1 the war was a debt of honor owed to Menelaus, and Helen was not named. In Book 2 the gods and the old men name her.

**A thing to boast of.** When the army runs for the ships, Hera asks Athena whether the Argives will flee home and leave to Priam and the Trojans “a thing to boast of (εὐχωλήν), Argive Helen, for whose sake many of the Achaeans have perished in Troy” (2.160–162). Athena repeats the words to Odysseus (2.176–178). Helen is here a trophy, and the war is measured by its dead: to go home without her is to leave the dead unpaid for (see [[helen]] and [[argive-helen]]).

{{quote:03927479-7a62-5ee8-ba3a-9e2e4cce9237}}

**Paying them back.** Nestor turns the cause into a program. No one should hurry home “before he has lain beside the wife of some Trojan and paid them back for the struggles and groans for Helen's sake” (2.354–356). The verb is τίσασθαι, “make them pay” (from τίνω, “pay”; compare the τιμή, “satisfaction”, of 1.159), and the payment is in kind: a wife taken for a wife. In the Catalogue the same line describes what Menelaus longs for most as he goes among his sixty ships (2.586–590).

{{quote:485361a5-ef0f-52ca-9e60-3af29173d4d1}}

**Whose groans?** Ἑλένης ὁρμήματά τε στοναχάς τε can mean the struggles and groans the Achaeans have suffered for Helen, or Helen's own struggles and groans. The second reading would make her an unwilling captive, and it was debated in antiquity. The translation takes the first. Either way, the line makes Helen the reason for the fighting, first in the mouth of the oldest counselor and then as the wish of her husband.
