---
summary: Agamemnon addresses the army as “Danaan heroes, attendants of Ares”, and the poet asks the Muses for “the leaders and lords of the Danaans”.
en: [Danaan]
---

The narrator first uses the name for the victims of Zeus' plan, who will suffer “sorrows and groaning” with the Trojans (2.40). Agamemnon opens his test with “Friends, Danaan heroes, attendants of Ares” (2.110), and Odysseus, rebuking Thersites, speaks of all that “the Danaan heroes” give the king (2.256). See [[heros]].

The Catalogue begins and ends with the name. The poet asks the Muses to tell him “who were the leaders and lords of the Danaans” (2.487), and he closes the list with the same line: “These, then, were the leaders and lords of the Danaans” (2.760). Nireus is the most handsome man “of all the Danaans, after the noble son of Peleus” (2.674). See [[catalogue-of-ships]].
