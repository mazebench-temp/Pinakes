grc: [βουληφόρον]

{{quote:836e20ea-a0df-5a3c-b96c-dacb08847e3a}}

The words are the Dream's opening rebuke, and they have become a saying ([[a-man-of-counsel-should-not-sleep]]). A leader who takes counsel for his people must not sleep the whole night through. The irony is sharp: the one who has not slept is Zeus himself, who lay awake planning the Achaeans' defeat ({{ref:Iliad 2.1-4}}), and the counsel the Dream brings is false. Agamemnon repeats the lines to the council without a change ({{ref:Iliad 2.61}}).
