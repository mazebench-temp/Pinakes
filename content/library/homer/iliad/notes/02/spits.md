summary: At Agamemnon's sacrifice to Zeus the entrails are spitted and held over the fire, and the rest of the meat is threaded on spits, roasted and drawn off for the kings' feast.

---

At Agamemnon's sacrifice before battle, the kings spit the entrails (ἀμπείραντες, from πείρω, “pierce”) and hold them over “the flame of Hephaestus” while the thigh-pieces burn (2.426). Then they cut up the rest of the meat, thread it on spits (ἀμφʼ ὀβελοῖσιν ἔπειραν), roast it with care and draw it off (2.428–429), and the feast follows. For the scene as a whole and its relation to the sacrifice at Chryse, see [[sacrifice-type-scene]].
