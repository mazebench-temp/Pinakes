The goats of Book 1 were victims; those of Book 2 are a working flock. When the wide flocks of goats (αἰπόλια πλατέʼ αἰγῶν) have mingled in the pasture, the goatherds (αἰπόλοι ἄνδρες) sort them out “easily” (ῥεῖα), and so the leaders marshal the army, this way and that, for battle (see [[goatherds-sorting-their-flocks]]). The point is order made out of a mixed crowd, with the ease of men who know their animals. αἰπόλια πλατέʼ αἰγῶν is a formula of wealth in livestock. It counts the goats that Nestor drove off from Elis in his youth (11.679) and the flocks of Odysseus (Odyssey 14.101, 14.103).
