**A hidden mind.** Agamemnon tells the council of elders that he will test the army with words and order it to flee, and that they are to hold the men back (2.73–75). The army has not heard the council, and it takes the order at its word. When Odysseus goes through the camp, he stops each king he meets with a warning: “you do not yet clearly know the mind (νόος) of the son of Atreus; now he is testing them, but soon he will strike the sons of the Achaeans” (2.192–193). “Not all of us heard what he said in the council” (2.194). The king's words and his νόος are different things, and only the council knows the difference. Agamemnon has been deceived in the same way himself: he believed the Dream, “the fool, who did not know what deeds Zeus was planning” (2.38). See [[the-test-of-the-army]], [[peira]] and [[truth-and-deception]].

Achilles will say that he hates like the gates of Hades the man who hides one thing in his heart and says another (9.312–313).

**What the king sees.** The verb returns in Agamemnon's call to battle: “any man I see (νοήσω) wishing to hang back from the fighting and linger beside the curved ships, for him there will be no hope then of escaping the dogs and the birds” (2.391–393). The line has the shape of one spoken by Zeus in Book 1, “whatever I wish to devise (νοῆσαι) apart from the gods” (1.549): ὃν δέ κʼ ἐγὼν ἀπάνευθε … ἐθέλ- … νο-. In Zeus' line the verb means “devise”; in Agamemnon's it means “see”.
