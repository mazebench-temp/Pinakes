---
summary: Book 2 names κήρ four times, for the fates of death that have carried men off since Aulis, the doom the Argives brought to Troy, and the black doom that no seer could turn from the sons of Merops or from the augur Ennomus.
grc: [κῆρες, κῆρα]
en: [fates, doom]
---

**Since Aulis.** Odysseus calls the army to witness what happened at Aulis: “you are all witnesses, all whom the fates of death have not carried away” (οὓς μὴ κῆρες ἔβαν θανάτοιο φέρουσαι, 2.302). The κῆρες are persons here, who come and carry men off, and after nine years of war there are fewer witnesses. Nestor too goes back to the start of the expedition. The Argives went aboard their ships “bringing slaughter and doom to the Trojans” (Τρώεσσι φόνον καὶ κῆρα φέροντες, 2.352), and Zeus flashed his lightning on the right. In both speeches κήρ is carried (φέρω, “carry”): the κῆρες carry the Achaeans off, and the Achaeans carry κήρ to Troy. See [[the-portent-at-aulis]] and [[the-departure-from-aulis]].

**Against the seers.** The Trojan Catalogue uses the word twice, both times against a man's knowledge of the future. Merops of Percote knew prophecy above all men and would not let his sons go to war, “but they would not obey him at all, for the fates of black death were leading them on” (κῆρες γὰρ ἄγον μέλανος θανάτοιο, 2.831–834). The same lines return when Diomedes kills them (11.329–332). The augur Ennomus fared no better: “his birds did not save him from black doom” (κῆρα μέλαιναν, 2.859), and he was brought down by Achilles in the river. See [[the-sons-of-merops]], [[ennomus]] and [[prophecy]].

κῆρ, “heart”, which differs from κήρ in accent and gender, occurs once in the book, in the “shaggy heart” of Pylaemenes (λάσιον κῆρ, 2.851; see [[ker-heart]]).
