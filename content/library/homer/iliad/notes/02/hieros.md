summary: Book 2 gives the epithet to the altars at Aulis and to three places of the Catalogue: Onchestus, Euboea and the Echinae islands.

---

At Aulis the army sacrificed “around a spring, at the holy altars,” when the portent of the serpent came ({{ref:Iliad 2.305}}; [[the-portent-at-aulis]]). The epithet marks the place as the gods', and the sign that follows is theirs.

In the Catalogue it is given to places:

{{quote:9c79af76-3332-5672-ac34-978986748ff5}}

Onchestus in Boeotia was a sanctuary of Poseidon, and the line names its grove. Holiness is also given, less obviously, to the island of Euboea ({{ref:Iliad 2.535}}) and to the Echinae, the islands off the coast of Acarnania ({{ref:Iliad 2.625}}). The text gives no reason for the epithet in these two cases.
