---
summary: Book 2 has two hearts, Odysseus', struck with grief as the army runs for home, and every man's, filled with strength by Athena as the army marches out, in the rare form καρδίη.
grc: [καρδίῃ]
---

The first κραδίη of the book is Odysseus'. When the army runs for the ships he stands still, “for grief had come upon his heart and spirit” (ἄχος κραδίην καὶ θυμὸν ἵκανεν, 2.171; see [[achos]]).

The second is every man's. Athena goes through the ranks with the aegis and rouses strength “in each man's heart, to go to war without ceasing and to fight” (ἐν δὲ σθένος ὦρσεν ἑκάστῳ / καρδίῃ ἄλληκτον πολεμίζειν ἠδὲ μάχεσθαι, 2.451–452). At once war is sweeter to them than going home (2.453–454). Between the two lines the army has turned from flight to battle. See [[aegis]] and [[divine-intervention]].

{{quote:f2807638-11ec-5655-b4a0-f115cea8c7ff}}

καρδίη is the same word as κραδίη with the ρ moved, the form that became Attic καρδία. It has a different rhythm: κραδίη begins with two short syllables, καρδίη with a long one. Homer uses καρδίη only three times, always in this line, and always when a god puts the will to fight into the Achaeans: Athena here, Strife in Book 11 (11.11–12), and Poseidon in Book 14 (14.151–152).
