---
summary: Book 2 is steered by its elders and by memory. Agamemnon consults the council of old men before he speaks to the army, and Odysseus and Nestor win the army back by making it remember Aulis and the day the fleet sailed.
---

**The council of elders.** Before the assembly Agamemnon seats a council of "the great-hearted elders" beside Nestor's ship (2.53–54; see [[elders]]). The word is γέροντες, "old men", but the council is a council of rank. Not every member is old, and all of them have the right to advise.

**Memory as argument.** When the army has run for the ships, Odysseus holds it by making it remember. The sign at Aulis "was yesterday, it seems, or the day before", and "you are all witnesses" (2.301–303; see [[the-portent-at-aulis]]). **Nestor's years.** The Catalogue ranks him by age. Menestheus had no equal at marshaling horses and men, "Nestor alone could rival him, for he was older" (2.555). Agamemnon's praise, "Once again, old man, you surpass the sons of the Achaeans in the assembly" (2.370), and his wish for ten such counselors (2.371–372) make age the source of good counsel. See [[nestor]].

**Old men at Troy.** The Trojans hold their assembly at Priam's doors, "young and old" (2.788–789), and Iris, in the voice of Priam's son, turns the old king's love of talk against him: "Old man, endless words are always dear to you, as once in time of peace" (2.796–797). The Trojan lookout sits on the tomb of "old Aesyetes" (2.793; see [[aesyetes]]).

The poet's own memory is borrowed. For the Catalogue he asks the Muses, who were present and know everything, to remind him (2.484–492; see [[memory-and-the-muses]]).
