**Thersites.** The quarrel of Book 1 was between two leaders over their shares. In Book 2 a common soldier describes the system from below.

{{quote:23e54e06-f664-5473-95ff-d45cf6f3b1aa}}

Agamemnon's huts are full of bronze and of women ἐξαίρετοι, “chosen out”, whom the Achaeans “give to you first of all, whenever we take a citadel” (2.227–228). The word names the selection that Book 1 described, when the army “chose out” Chryseis for Agamemnon after the sack of Thebe (ἐκ δʼ ἕλον, 1.369). Thersites speaks as one of the givers, “we Achaeans”, and as one of the takers too: a Trojan's ransom comes to Agamemnon, though “I or another of the Achaeans” bound the captive and brought him in (2.229–231; see [[apoina]]). He ends with Achilles: Agamemnon “has taken his prize and keeps it” (2.240, repeating 1.356; see [[geras]]).

Odysseus does not deny the facts. He rebukes Thersites for abusing Agamemnon “because they give him so very much, the Danaan heroes” (2.255). His objection is that such a man should have the kings in his mouth at all (2.250; see [[odysseus-and-thersites]]). Agamemnon himself names the cause of the quarrel plainly: “Achilles and I fought for the sake of a girl” (2.377).

**Briseis.** The Catalogue says where that girl came from. Achilles lies idle by the ships, angry over Briseis, “whom he had taken out of Lyrnessus after much toil (ἐξείλετο πολλὰ μογήσας), when he sacked Lyrnessus and the walls of Thebe” (2.689–691). ἐξαιρέω is the verb of choosing a prize out of the spoils, and here Achilles is its subject. In Book 1 he says that the army gave her to him, the prize “for which I toiled hard” (1.162, 1.392). See [[briseis]] and [[the-sack-of-lyrnessus]].

**Spoils of the slain.** The book ends with another kind of spoil. One of the Carian leaders, Nastes or Amphimachus (the Greek does not say which), came to the war wearing gold “like a girl”, and when Achilles killed him in the river, “wise-hearted Achilles carried off the gold” (2.872–875). What a man wore when he was killed went to the man who killed him. See [[like-a-girl]] and [[gold-and-silver]].
