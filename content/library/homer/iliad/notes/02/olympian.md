---
summary: Book 2 names Zeus “the Olympian” as the sender of the portent at Aulis, and calls the Muses “the Olympian Muses” in the invocation.
grc: [Ὀλυμπιάδες]
---

At Aulis the serpent that devoured the sparrows was one “which the Olympian himself had sent into the light” ({{ref:Iliad 2.309}}; [[the-portent-at-aulis]]). The title alone, without the name, stresses the god's authority behind the sign.

Before the Catalogue the poet calls on “the Olympian Muses, daughters of aegis-bearing Zeus” (Ὀλυμπιάδες Μοῦσαι, {{ref:Iliad 2.491}}). Ὀλυμπιάς is the feminine adjective of Ὄλυμπος. It places the Muses among the Olympian gods and makes them witnesses to all things ([[invocation-of-the-muses]], [[muses]]).
