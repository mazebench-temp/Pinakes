The geese are named first of three birds, “geese or cranes or long-necked swans” (χηνῶν ἢ γεράνων ἢ κύκνων δουλιχοδείρων). All three are big birds of the water meadows that live and fly in flocks, and the simile needs them for their numbers and their noise. On the meadow by the streams of the Cayster they fly here and there, glorying in their wings, and settle with loud cries, and the meadow resounds; so the Achaeans pour out onto the plain of the Scamander, and the earth rings under them (see [[birds-on-the-asian-meadow]], [[asian-meadow]] and [[cayster]]). The whole line returns at 15.692, where the flock feeds by a river and an eagle falls on it (see [[cranes]] and [[swans]]).
