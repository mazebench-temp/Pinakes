---
summary: The one temple in Book 2 is Athena's “rich temple” at Athens, where she set down Erechtheus, and where the young men of Athens offer bulls and rams as the years come round.
grc: [νηῷ]
passages:
  - 2.547-549 | Erechtheus
---

In the Athenian entry of the Catalogue, Athena raised Erechtheus, whom the grain-giving earth had borne, “and set him down in Athens, in her own rich temple” (ἑῷ ἐν πίονι νηῷ, 2.549). “And there with bulls and rams they seek his favor, the young men of the Athenians, as the years come round” (2.550–551): a yearly sacrifice in the goddess's temple. Whether the favor is sought from Erechtheus or from Athena depends on whom the pronoun μιν means; the translation takes it as Erechtheus. See [[athena-and-erechtheus]] and [[erechtheus]].

{{quote:4d7ddc23-d806-5091-bfc6-f4186cb3670b}}

It is the only νηός in Book 2. The Catalogue names two other holy places that are not temples: Poseidon's grove at Onchestus (ἄλσος, 2.506) and the precinct of Demeter at Pyrasus (τέμενος, 2.695–696).
