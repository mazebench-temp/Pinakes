Book 1 ended with Achilles sitting by his ships (1.488–492). The Catalogue finds him still there, and it describes the withdrawal from outside, through the men he no longer leads. κεῖτο, "he lay", is the verb of idleness, and the Catalogue uses it again of [[Philoctetes]], lying wounded on Lemnos (2.721, 2.724). Achilles is χωόμενος, "angry" (see [[cholos]]), over [[Briseis]], and "for her sake he lay grieving" (ἀχέων).

The notice ends with a prophecy: "but soon he was to rise up" (τάχα δʼ ἀνστήσεσθαι ἔμελλεν, 2.694). It points to Book 19, when Achilles gives up his wrath and returns to the fighting.

The withdrawal returns when the Muse is asked who was the best of the Achaeans. The answer is Telamonian Ajax, "while Achilles kept up his wrath" (ὄφρʼ Ἀχιλεὺς μήνιεν, 2.769; see [[best-of-the-achaeans]]). Then comes a picture of the wrath from the outside:

{{quote:59741a90-1c33-5381-b0fe-b6be01b9bd7a}}

ἀπομηνίσας, "nursing his wrath" against Agamemnon, is a compound of the verb of [[menis]]. Achilles himself does nothing in these lines. The scene is filled with the idleness of his men and his horses and with the Myrmidons' longing for their leader (see [[myrmidons]]). The Catalogue ends, and the army marches out, with his absence in view.
