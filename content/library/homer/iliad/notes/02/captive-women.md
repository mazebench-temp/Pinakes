---
summary: Book 2 counts captive women as a king's wealth and a soldier's reward. Thersites taunts Agamemnon with the women in his huts, Nestor promises each man a Trojan's wife, and the Catalogue recalls how Briseis was taken.
---

**Thersites' inventory.** Thersites lists what Agamemnon already has: "Your huts are full of bronze, and there are many women in your huts, the choicest, whom we Achaeans give to you first of all, whenever we take a citadel." Then he asks whether Agamemnon wants "a young woman, to lie with in love, and keep apart for yourself alone", a jab at the quarrel over [[Briseis]]:

{{quote:23e54e06-f664-5473-95ff-d45cf6f3b1aa}}

**The promised reward.** Nestor holds the army with the same prize. No one should go home "before he has lain beside the wife of some Trojan":

{{quote:485361a5-ef0f-52ca-9e60-3af29173d4d1}}

**Briseis.** The Catalogue explains Achilles' absence by her. He lay angry over the girl "whom he had taken out of Lyrnessus after much toil, when he sacked Lyrnessus and the walls of Thebe" and killed Mynes and Epistrophus, the sons of Euenus (2.689–693; see [[the-sack-of-lyrnessus]]).

**Astyocheia.** The mother of Tlepolemus had also been taken in war. Heracles brought her "from Ephyra, from the river Selleis, after he had sacked many cities" (2.658–660; see [[astyocheia]]).

**Women as an insult.** Thersites' worst name for the army is "Achaean women, Achaean men no longer" (2.235). In the camp's language, to be a woman is to be good for nothing in war.
