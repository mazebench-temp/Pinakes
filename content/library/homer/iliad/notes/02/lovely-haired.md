---
summary: The Catalogue gives the epithet to Briseis, the girl over whom Achilles is angry.
grc: [ἠϋκόμοιο]
---

{{quote:8dabd452-362d-5346-b996-1374b97efce9}}

In the entry for Achilles' people the Catalogue stops to say why they are not fighting. Achilles lies by his ships “angry over the girl, Briseis of the lovely hair” (κούρης χωόμενος Βρισηΐδος ἠϋκόμοιο, {{ref:Iliad 2.689}}). In Book 1 she was “fair-cheeked” ([[fair-cheeked]], {{ref:Iliad 1.184}}). The epithet of beauty is the whole of the explanation the Catalogue gives for the quarrel: she was taken, she was lovely, and he is angry. The following lines recall where he won her, in the sack of Lyrnessus ([[the-sack-of-lyrnessus]], [[briseis]]).
