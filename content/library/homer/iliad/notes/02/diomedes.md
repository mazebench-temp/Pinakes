**Among the elders.** Diomedes is fifth in the list of chief men whom Agamemnon calls to his sacrifice, named only as “the son of Tydeus” (2.406). He will soon prove himself the youngest of them (9.57–58). The council of γέροντες (“elders”) is a matter of rank, not of years. See [[elders]].

**The Catalogue.**

{{quote:b4b62d85-007d-5e08-9601-21954889bfa3}}

His realm is the Argolid: [[argos|Argos]] itself, [[tiryns|Tiryns]] “of the great walls”, the towns of the Argolic peninsula, and the island of Aegina. His eighty ships are one of the largest contingents, after Agamemnon's hundred and Nestor's ninety and equal to Idomeneus' eighty. Three leaders are named, and the entry takes care to say that Diomedes “led them all” (2.567).

The three are sons of the generation that fought at Thebes. [[sthenelus|Sthenelus]] is the son of Capaneus, and [[euryalus|Euryalus]] a son of Mecisteus and grandson of Talaus. Sthenelus later boasts that he and Diomedes took Thebes where their fathers failed (4.404–410).

The Catalogue gives Argos to Diomedes, although Agamemnon's scepter makes him lord of “all Argos” (2.108) and his own kingdom is centered on Mycenae (2.569–580). How the poem divides the Argolid between the two kings has long been discussed. See [[agamemnon]] and [[catalogue-of-ships]].
