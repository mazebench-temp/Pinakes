**The last voice.** Odysseus has driven the army back to the assembly and the others are seated, but “only Thersites still kept up his brawling” (2.212). The verb ἐκολῴα comes from κολῳός, the “brawl” that Hephaestus feared among the gods in Book 1 (see [[kolooos]]). He does not speak for himself. He says aloud what Agamemnon's test pretended to want, that the army should go home, and he says it as an insult.

{{quote:c85422e7-fb25-5294-9d07-199abffb1184}}

**Achilles' words.** His speech takes over Achilles' case against Agamemnon. The king's huts are full of bronze and women, the pick of every sack, while others do the fighting (2.225–234; compare 1.163–168). Two lines are Achilles' own. “He has taken his prize and keeps it, having seized it himself” (2.240) is what Achilles told Thetis (1.356), and “this insult would be your last” (2.242) is the line he threw at Agamemnon in the quarrel (1.232). Thersites adds a sneer of his own, that there is no anger in Achilles at all. The poem has shown the opposite (1.488–492). See [[achilles]] and [[quarrel-of-achilles-and-agamemnon]].

“Achaean women, Achaean men no longer” (Ἀχαιΐδες, οὐκέτʼ Ἀχαιοί) is an insult Menelaus will use when no Achaean dares to face Hector (7.96).

**Odysseus' answer.** Odysseus does not argue. He attacks the speaker's standing: no mortal is worse than Thersites among all who came to Ilios (2.248–249). He threatens to strip him naked and send him howling to the ships, and he stakes his own head and his name as the father of Telemachus on the threat (2.258–264). Then he strikes.

{{quote:8d88137d-0792-5d3c-a5c4-435ddf466f85}}

The scepter that came down from Zeus through Hermes and Pelops (2.100–108) raises a bloody welt on Thersites' back. See [[scepter]] and [[words-and-violence]].

**The laughter.** “Troubled though they were, they laughed heartily at him.” The army's comment, spoken by an anonymous “someone”, praises the beating as the best thing Odysseus has ever done for the Argives (2.271–277; see [[tis-speech]]). Book 1 ended a quarrel among the gods in laughter at the lame Hephaestus (1.599–600). Book 2 ends a quarrel among men in laughter at the lame Thersites, and the assembly is whole again. See [[odysseus-and-thersites]].
