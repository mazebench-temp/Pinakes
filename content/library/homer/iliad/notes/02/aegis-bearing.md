---
summary: Book 2 uses the epithet six times, in the formulas that make Athena and the Muses daughters of aegis-bearing Zeus and in appeals to his promises; Athena herself carries the aegis through the army.
grc: [αἰγίοχος]
---

Three uses name daughters of Zeus through their father. Hera addresses Athena as “child of aegis-bearing Zeus” ({{ref:Iliad 2.157}}; [[child-of-aegis-bearing-zeus]]), and the Muses are twice “the daughters of aegis-bearing Zeus”: in the invocation before the Catalogue ({{ref:Iliad 2.491}}) and in the story of Thamyris, whom they silenced ({{ref:Iliad 2.598}}). Iris comes to the Trojans “from aegis-bearing Zeus” ({{ref:Iliad 2.787}}).

The other two concern Zeus' word. Nestor warns against going home before the army learns whether “the promise of aegis-bearing Zeus” is a lie ({{ref:Iliad 2.348}}), and Agamemnon, in the nominative αἰγίοχος, blames him for his troubles: “aegis-bearing Zeus, son of Cronus, has given me sorrows” ({{ref:Iliad 2.375}}).

Book 2 also shows the aegis itself, in Athena's hands:

{{quote:8043fe94-871f-5509-a302-0d0eb8e2bf9d}}

It is “ageless and immortal,” with a hundred golden tassels, and as she sweeps through the ranks with it she fills every man with the will to fight ([[aegis]]).
