**A claim.** Nestor answers the dream with Achilles' taunt.

{{quote:7364e943-ed47-5eb2-9b39-9b5b6b982d37}}

From any other man they would call the dream a lie, “but now the man who has seen it claims to be by far the best of the Achaeans” (2.82). The words are those Achilles used of Agamemnon with contempt (1.91). In Nestor's mouth the king's claim to be ἄριστος is a reason to believe him.

The word has lesser uses in the book too. The plan that seemed best to Zeus was the Dream (ἀρίστη, 2.5). The crowd calls Odysseus' beating of Thersites “by far the best thing he has done among the Argives” (2.274). The elders called to Agamemnon's sacrifice are “the best of all the Achaeans” (ἀριστῆας Παναχαιῶν, 2.404).

**By numbers.** In the Catalogue Agamemnon brings the largest contingent, a hundred ships.

{{quote:4d6d85aa-2bbf-5c97-bfe8-855eff55cfda}}

He stood out among the heroes “because he was the best, and led by far the most men” (2.580). The narrator grants Agamemnon the title Achilles denied him, and gives Nestor's reason from Book 1: he is the greater “since he rules over more” (1.281). Hector is given the same line in the Trojan Catalogue (2.817).

**The Muse's verdict.** When the Achaean list is done, the poet asks a question of his own.

{{quote:bd4b4b5c-3fde-5525-8c25-d2251c015e8f}}

The best horses were the mares that Eumelus drove (ἄρισται, 2.763; see [[the-mares-of-eumelus]]). The best man was Telamonian Ajax, “while Achilles kept up his wrath; for he was by far the strongest” (2.768–769; see [[best-of-the-achaeans]] and [[menis]]). The reason, ὃ γὰρ πολὺ φέρτατος, is what Hephaestus said of Zeus (1.581). The Muse's answer to the question of Book 1 is conditional: the first place is Achilles', and only his absence lets anyone else hold it.

**Other superlatives.** The Catalogue ranks by other measures as well. Protesilaus was both older and braver (ἀρείων) than his brother (2.707); Alcestis was “most beautiful in form” (εἶδος ἀρίστη) of the daughters of Pelias (2.715); Nireus was the most handsome man who came to Ilios after Achilles (2.673–674), in a line that ends like the one that made Thersites the ugliest (2.216).
