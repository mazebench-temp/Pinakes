Book 2 names cranes once, between the geese and the swans. What the flock gives the army is its restless movement and its noise: the birds settle “with loud cries” (κλαγγηδόν), each ahead of the next, and the meadow resounds (σμαραγεῖ). The assembly earlier in the book had to be brought to cease from its κλαγγή (“clamor”, 2.100). The cry is the crane's mark, and at the start of the next book the cranes return in a simile of their own; there the clamor is the Trojans', and the Achaeans come on in silence (3.2–9; see [[birds-on-the-asian-meadow]]).
