---
summary: The sons of Atreus are named together in Book 2 in the plural, as the leaders of the whole expedition: all “who came with the sons of Atreus to Ilios”, and the horses “who followed the sons of Atreus”.
grc: ["=Ἀτρεΐδῃς", Ἀτρεΐδῃσιν]
---

Book 1 named the brothers in the dual, “the two sons of Atreus”. Book 2 uses the plural. Odysseus tells Thersites that no man is worse than he “of all who came with the sons of Atreus to Ilios” (2.249), and the poet asks the Muse who was best “of the men and of the horses who followed the sons of Atreus” (2.762). The whole army is theirs.

The brothers act together once in Book 2: Menelaus comes unbidden to Agamemnon's sacrifice, “for he knew in his heart how his brother was laboring” (2.409). In the Catalogue their kingdoms, Mycenae and Lacedaemon, are listed one after the other (2.569–590), but Menelaus' men arm apart (2.587). See [[agamemnon]] and [[menelaus]].
