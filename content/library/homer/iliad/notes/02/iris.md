{{quote:f11430bb-1410-56f5-ab67-fcb77b5351d7}}

Zeus opened the book by sending the Dream to Agamemnon with a lie (2.8–15). At its end he sends Iris to the other side with the truth, "grievous news" (ἀγγελίη ἀλεγεινή, 2.787). She finds the Trojans in assembly at Priam's doors, young and old together, while the Achaean army is already crossing the plain.

She takes the voice (φθογγή) of [[Polites]], a son of Priam posted as a lookout on the tomb of old [[Aesyetes]]. The disguise gives the warning a source the Trojans would trust: the man whose task is to watch the ships. The Dream came to Agamemnon in the same way, in the likeness of Nestor (2.20–22).

{{quote:bead43ef-f2ab-581d-a787-282407a09bbc}}

Her first words are for Priam, and they are a rebuke. "Endless words" (μῦθοι ἄκριτοι) are dear to the old king, as they were in peacetime. ἄκριτος means "undistinguished, without end or decision"; Odysseus called Thersites ἀκριτόμυθος (2.246). This is the first time the Iliad speaks of peace, [[eirene|εἰρήνη]], and it is peace already lost. The army she describes is uncountable, "like leaves or like the sands" ([[like-leaves-or-sand]]).

{{quote:6b37cb01-e030-5a84-a6e6-90706c2d6c42}}

Then she turns to [[Hector]] with practical orders. The city is full of allies, and "the tongues of men scattered far and wide are many and various", so each leader should give commands to his own people and lead them out. It is the Trojan counterpart of Nestor's advice to Agamemnon to separate the men by tribes and clans (2.362–363), and it introduces the Trojan catalogue that follows. See [[trojan-allies]] and [[semaino]].

{{quote:4563bb8b-5f2b-5d21-b4db-b519ba187ad9}}

Hector "did not fail to know the goddess's word". The phrase may mean that he recognized a goddess behind his brother's voice, as Odysseus "knew the voice of the goddess" when Athena spoke to him earlier in the book (2.182), or simply that he heeded the message. Either way he acts at once. The assembly breaks up, the gates open, and the army pours out.
