The water snake is named in the Catalogue entry for the men of [[Philoctetes]] (2.716–725). The poet does not tell the story. He says where Philoctetes lies (“in sacred Lemnos”), who left him there (“the sons of the Achaeans”), and in what state: “in agony from the evil wound” (ἕλκεϊ μοχθίζοντα κακῷ) of a snake he calls ὀλοόφρων, “deadly”. Where the snake struck, and why a wound should cost a man his place in the army, the Iliad leaves unsaid; the *Cypria* told both (see [[philoctetes-on-lemnos]] and [[lemnos]]).

The wound keeps Philoctetes out of the muster as anger keeps Achilles, and the lines on the two men are built alike: each “lay” apart (κεῖτο, 2.721, 2.771–772), and of each the poet says “he lay grieving; but soon” his absence would end (2.694, 2.724; see [[achilles-withdraws]]).
