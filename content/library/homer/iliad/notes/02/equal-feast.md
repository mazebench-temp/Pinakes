summary: The line returns at the feast after Agamemnon's sacrifice to Zeus (2.431), where the equal shares go to the chief kings alone.
---

The line returns after Agamemnon's sacrifice, its third occurrence in the poem (2.431 = 1.468 = 1.602; see [[feast-type-scene]]). This time the company is small: the king, the six leaders he called, and Menelaus (2.404–409). The army eats in the huts (2.399), and the book has just heard Thersites complain of how the spoils are shared out (2.225–242; see [[division-of-spoils]]). As at Chryse, the feast follows an offering the god accepts (2.420). Unlike Chryse, it follows no reconciliation, and it leads to war.
