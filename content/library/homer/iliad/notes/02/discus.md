---
summary: While Achilles lies by his ships nursing his wrath, his Myrmidons amuse themselves on the shore with the discus, the javelin and the bow.
passages:
  - 2.768-779 | Achilles by his ships
---

After the Catalogue the poet asks the Muse who was the best of the men and of the horses. The best man was Telamonian Ajax, “while Achilles kept up his wrath” (2.768–769). Achilles lies among his ships, and his people pass the time on the shore.

{{quote:2c0a391c-d115-5778-8cb8-7da83a12508f}}

They “amused themselves throwing the discus and the javelin / and shooting their bows” (δίσκοισιν τέρποντο καὶ αἰγανέῃσιν ἱέντες / τόξοισιν, 2.774–775). On the first day of battle in the poem, the best fighters in the army are at play, their horses idle and their chariots covered in the huts, while the rest march out across the plain (2.780–785; see [[achilles-withdraws]], [[chariot]] and [[bow]]). The Odyssey gives the same verse to the suitors at their games before Odysseus' house (*Odyssey* 4.626).
