summary: At Agamemnon's sacrifice to Zeus before battle, the thigh-bones are cut out, wrapped in fat and burned; Zeus accepts the offering but refuses the prayer.

---

At Agamemnon's sacrifice to Zeus the kings cut out the thigh-bones (μηρούς), wrap them in a double fold of fat, lay raw flesh on them, and burn them on split logs stripped of their leaves; “when the thigh-pieces were burned up” (κατὰ μῆρε κάη), they taste the entrails (2.423–427). The god receives his portion: Zeus “accepted the offerings” (δέκτο μὲν ἱρά), but he did not grant the prayer (2.419–420). For the scene as a whole and its relation to the sacrifice at Chryse, see [[sacrifice-type-scene]].
