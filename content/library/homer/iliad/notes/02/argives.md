---
summary: In Book 2 the Argives are the army that nearly sails home “against fate”, shouts its approval of Odysseus and Agamemnon, and will soon remember Philoctetes.
grc: [Ἀργεῖοι, Ἀργείοισιν]
---

Agamemnon speaks “among the Argives” (2.109) as he leans on the scepter that makes him lord of “all Argos” (2.108). The name is used where the army moves as one: the Argives would have had their homecoming “against fate” (2.155), they are about to flee over the broad back of the sea (2.159), they shout their approval twice (2.333, 2.394), and at the end of Philoctetes' entry “the Argives beside their ships” are soon to remember him (2.725). Thersites says whatever will make the Argives laugh (2.215), and the army's anonymous speaker calls his beating the best thing Odysseus has done “among the Argives” (2.274).

The adjective Ἀργείη describes Helen, “Argive Helen” (2.161, 2.177): she is Greek, and the Greeks' to win back. See [[argive-helen]] and [[argos]].
