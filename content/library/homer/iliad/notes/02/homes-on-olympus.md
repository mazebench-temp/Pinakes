---
summary: Book 2 uses the formula three times in the message of the Dream, and once, in the feminine, for the Muses.
grc: [Ὀλύμπια δώματʼ ἔχουσαι]
en: [who have your homes]
---

The formula belongs first to a lie. Zeus gives the Dream its message: “the immortals who have their homes on Olympus are no longer of two minds,” for Hera has won them over ({{ref:Iliad 2.13-15}}). The Dream repeats the words to Agamemnon ({{ref:Iliad 2.30}}) and Agamemnon to the council ({{ref:Iliad 2.67}}). In Book 1 Chryses used the formula to wish the Achaeans the sack of Troy; here it tells Agamemnon that the gods have granted it, and they have not.

Before the Catalogue the narrator uses the feminine to call on the Muses:

{{quote:147d6490-c3f4-5e31-ab50-6db152914776}}

The Muses who have their homes on Olympus are present at all things and know them. The poet asks them for the truth that the Dream's message lacked ([[invocation-of-the-muses]]).
