---
summary: The Athenians' yearly sacrifices of bulls and rams in Athena's temple are the one propitiation named in Book 2, a standing cult rather than the appeasing of an angry god.
grc: [ἱλάονται]
en: [seek his favor]
---

The verb occurs once, in the Catalogue's entry for Athens. Athena raised Erechtheus, whom the grain-giving earth had borne, and set him down in her own rich temple, “and there with bulls and rams they seek his favor (ἱλάονται), the young men of the Athenians, as the years come round” (2.547–551). Here ἱλάσκομαι has no anger to turn aside. It names a yearly cult that keeps a power gracious (see [[sacrifice]]). The translation takes μιν (“him” or “her”) as Erechtheus; it could also be Athena who receives the offerings. See [[erechtheus]], [[athena-and-erechtheus]] and [[athens]].
