φῆρας λαχνήεντας: "the shaggy beasts". φήρ is the word Nestor used in Book 1 (1.268), and λαχνήεις, from λάχνη ("woolly hair, down"), is "hairy, shaggy"; the Iliad uses it again of Hephaestus' hairy chest (18.415). It is the only detail of the Centaurs' bodies that Homer gives. Whether he imagined them as half horses, as later art did, cannot be told from the text.

The lines also give the Centaurs a home. They lived on [[Pelion]] until Peirithous drove them out and pushed them toward the [[Aethices]], in the mountains to the west. ἐτίσατο, "took vengeance", implies a wrong done first, and the Odyssey tells what it was (21.295–304).
