---
summary: Book 2 names Zeus by his father nine times: in the history of the scepter, in Agamemnon's complaints and prayer, in Nestor's sign from Aulis, and in Odysseus' formula “the son of crooked-counseling Cronus.”
grc: [Κρονίδης, Κρόνου πάϊς]
---

The patronymic follows the question of what Zeus has promised. Agamemnon complains that “Zeus, son of Cronus, has bound me fast in heavy blind folly” ({{ref:Iliad 2.111}}), and later, in the nominative again, that “aegis-bearing Zeus, son of Cronus, has given me sorrows” ({{ref:Iliad 2.375}}). Nestor recalls the day at Aulis when “the son of Cronus, supreme in might, nodded his assent” with lightning on the right ({{ref:Iliad 2.350}}; [[supreme-in-might]]). Agamemnon sacrifices to the same god before battle ({{ref:Iliad 2.403}}), and the answer is given under the same name: “the son of Cronus was not yet going to fulfill his prayer” ({{ref:Iliad 2.419}}).

Odysseus twice uses a longer form, Κρόνου πάϊς ἀγκυλομήτεω, “the son of crooked-counseling Cronus” ([[crooked-counseling]]): of the god who gave the one king his scepter and his right to judge ({{ref:Iliad 2.205}}), and of the god who turned the serpent at Aulis to stone ({{ref:Iliad 2.319}}).

The book's first use is in the history of the scepter: Hephaestus gave it “to lord Zeus, son of Cronus” ({{ref:Iliad 2.102}}). The last is in the Catalogue, where the son of Cronus pours wondrous wealth on the Rhodians who settled in three tribes ({{ref:Iliad 2.670}}).
