Altars appear in Book 2 only in Odysseus' memory of Aulis. The army was offering perfect hecatombs to the immortals “around a spring, at the holy altars” (ἀμφὶ περὶ κρήνην ἱεροὺς κατὰ βωμούς), beneath a [[plane-tree|plane tree]].

{{quote:2e79eb4b-16fd-560d-9477-73317eb56fac}}

From that place the portent came. The serpent “darted out from beneath the altar” (βωμοῦ ὑπαΐξας, 2.310) and made for the tree, and “the dread portents of the gods broke in upon the hecatombs” (2.321). The sign comes out of the sacrifice itself, and Calchas reads it as a message from Zeus (see [[the-portent-at-aulis]], [[hecatomb]] and [[sacrifice]]).

Book 1 had one “well-built altar”, Apollo's at Chryse; here there are several altars at a spring, a place of worship in the open air. Agamemnon's sacrifice to Zeus later in the book names no altar (2.402–431).
