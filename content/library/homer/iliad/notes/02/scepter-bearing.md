The phrase names the kings at the end of the council, as a body, just before the people stream out to the assembly. Book 2 is the book of the scepter. Agamemnon takes up his ancestral scepter when he dresses ([[imperishable]], {{ref:Iliad 2.46}}) and leans on it to address the army; its history is told from Hephaestus to Agamemnon ({{ref:Iliad 2.100-108}}; [[the-scepter-of-agamemnon]]); and Odysseus borrows it to restrain the kings and to beat the commoners and Thersites ({{ref:Iliad 2.185-206}}, {{ref:Iliad 2.265-268}}). The kings who carry scepters are the audience of Odysseus' gentler words ([[gentle-words]], [[kings]]).
