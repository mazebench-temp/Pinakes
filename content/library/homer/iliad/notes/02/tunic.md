**Agamemnon dresses.** Waking from the Dream, Agamemnon sits up and puts on his tunic before anything else, “soft ... fine and new-made” (μαλακὸν ... χιτῶνα / καλὸν νηγάτεον). Cloak, sandals, sword and scepter follow.

{{quote:e33c248a-9e1b-596b-9d6c-bac21690432c}}

νηγάτεος occurs only here and of Hera's veil (14.185), and its sense is uncertain: “new-made”, as the translation has it, or “glossy”. The soft tunic belongs to a dressing, not an arming (see [[dressing-scene]]). Agamemnon believes he will take Troy that very day (2.37), but what he puts on is a king's dress for the council and the assembly. He puts on his bronze only when the army marches out (2.578; see [[agamemnons-dream]]).

**Thersites' tunic.** Odysseus ends his rebuke of [[Thersites]] with a threat: if he finds him playing the fool again, he will strip off his clothes, “your cloak and your tunic, which cover your nakedness” (χλαῖνάν τʼ ἠδὲ χιτῶνα, τά τʼ αἰδῶ ἀμφικαλύπτει), and drive him out of the assembly with blows.

{{quote:bf74ce06-bece-5327-bd43-c6de308885bb}}

αἰδώς is shame, and here the parts a man is ashamed to show (see [[aidos]]). To be stripped before the army would be the worst of disgraces. Odysseus carries out only the beating (see [[odysseus-and-thersites]]).

**Hector's tunic.** Agamemnon prays that the sun may not set before he has burned Priam's hall and “torn the tunic of Hector around his chest, rent with the bronze” (Ἑκτόρεον δὲ χιτῶνα περὶ στήθεσσι δαΐξαι / χαλκῷ ῥωγαλέον). The torn tunic stands for the wound in the chest beneath it.

{{quote:e9e44cde-e73c-57fb-9ec1-19f624604c58}}

Zeus accepts the offering but not the prayer (2.419–420). Hector will fall in Book 22, to Achilles.
