**The two Ajaxes.** Among the elders at the sacrifice are Αἴαντε δύω, “the two Ajaxes” (2.406), named together in the dual. The Catalogue shows who they are: the great Ajax, son of Telamon, and the lesser, swift Ajax, son of Oileus, who leads the Locrians. See [[ajax-son-of-oileus]]. Some scholars think the dual Αἴαντε once meant Ajax and his half-brother Teucer, and the question is still debated; in Book 2 the pair are the two men named Ajax.

**The measure of the lesser Ajax.** The Locrian leader is introduced by comparison with this one: “the lesser Ajax, not so great as Telamonian Ajax, but much smaller” (2.528–529). Τελαμώνιος (“son of Telamon”) is heard here for the first time. The great Ajax is known by his size before he has done anything in the poem. See [[telamon]].

**Salamis.**

{{quote:98e07671-c4cf-5d1c-9d99-96c78ec704d5}}

His entry is the shortest of any leader's: two lines, twelve ships, and no list of towns. The second line, which stations his ships beside the Athenians, became famous. Athens cited it in its quarrel with Megara over Salamis, and some said that Solon or Pisistratus had added it to the text. See [[solon-and-salamis]] and [[salamis]].

**The best of the men.** When the poet asks the Muse who was best, the answer is Telamonian Ajax, “while Achilles kept up his wrath; for he was by far the strongest” (2.768–769). The highest praise the Catalogue gives him depends on another man's absence. See [[best-of-the-achaeans]].
