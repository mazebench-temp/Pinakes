---
summary: Agamemnon tells the elders that he will first test the army with words, “as is the custom”, by ordering it to flee. When the army takes him at his word, Odysseus tells each king that Agamemnon is only testing them.
passages:
  - 2.72-75 | A test with words
  - 2.188-197 | Odysseus and the kings
---

**The plan.** The Dream told Agamemnon to arm the Achaeans “with all speed” (πανσυδίῃ, 2.12). Instead he calls the elders to Nestor's ship, tells them the dream, and adds a step of his own before the arming.

{{quote:5c269b4c-6904-58ac-9ae6-b48cdbc739b4}}

πρῶτα δʼ ἐγὼν ἔπεσιν πειρήσομαι: “but first I will test them with words”. The test is made of words on both sides. He will order flight, and the elders, “each from his own place”, are to hold the men back with words (ἐπέεσσιν, 2.75). The only reason he gives is ἣ θέμις ἐστί, “as is the custom” (see [[themis|θέμις]]). The poem does not say what custom he means. Nestor, who answers, speaks of the dream and says nothing of the test (2.79–83; see [[pseudos]]).

**The test fails.** In the assembly Agamemnon gives the army every reason to go home (2.110–141), and it believes him. The narrator says whom the speech moved: “every man in the crowd who had not heard the council” (2.143). The men run for the ships, and the elders who were to hold them back are not heard from. Hera has to send Athena, and Athena Odysseus (see [[the-test-of-the-army]]).

**“Now he is testing them.”** Odysseus takes the king's scepter and goes through the camp. To each king and man of note he explains the test.

{{quote:34021398-91b1-54ae-9cf7-9fdeddb4aa7c}}

νῦν μὲν πειρᾶται, τάχα δʼ ἴψεται υἷας Ἀχαιῶν: “now he is testing them, but soon he will strike the sons of the Achaeans”. Odysseus lets the kings into the plan, since “not all of us heard what he said in the council” (2.194), and adds a warning: the test may end in the king's anger, and a king's honor comes from Zeus. To the men of the people he says nothing of a test. He strikes them with the scepter and tells them to sit and listen (2.198–206; see [[commoners]] and [[demos]]). The secret of the test is shared only with those who count in council.

The book never says what the test found out. Later in the day Nestor offers Agamemnon a better way of knowing his army: array it by tribes and clans, and he will learn which of the leaders and which of the men are cowards and which are brave (2.362–368; see [[phylon]]).
