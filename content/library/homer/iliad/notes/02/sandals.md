---
summary: Agamemnon binds on his fine sandals as he dresses after the Dream, in the verse Homer uses whenever sandals are put on.
passages:
  - 2.41-47 | Agamemnon dresses
---

Sandals are named once in Book 2. Agamemnon, dressing after the Dream, “under his shining feet ... bound his fine sandals” (ποσσὶ δʼ ὑπὸ λιπαροῖσιν ἐδήσατο καλὰ πέδιλα, 2.44), after tunic and cloak and before the sword and the scepter.

{{quote:e33c248a-9e1b-596b-9d6c-bac21690432c}}

This is the first use of a verse that recurs for Agamemnon himself when he rises in the night in Book 10 (10.22), and for Nestor, Hera and Telemachus. Here it marks the scene as a dressing, not an arming: Agamemnon puts on sandals, not greaves, and takes a scepter, not a spear (see [[dressing-scene]]).
