---
summary: Book 2 uses the patronymic twice, in the same phrase, to make the absent Achilles the measure of other men in beauty and in strength.
---

Both uses are in the formula ἀμύμονα Πηλεΐωνα, “the noble son of Peleus” ([[blameless]]). Nireus of Syme was the most handsome man at Troy “after the noble son of Peleus” ({{ref:Iliad 2.673-674}}), and the best horses were those “that carried the noble son of Peleus” ({{ref:Iliad 2.768-770}}; [[best-of-the-achaeans]]). The Trojan Catalogue names Achilles by another patronymic, Αἰακίδης, “grandson of Aeacus” ([[grandson-of-aeacus]]).
