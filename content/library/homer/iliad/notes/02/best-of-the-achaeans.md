The title is contested in Book 2 as in Book 1, but by other voices.

**Nestor.** In the council, Nestor accepts the Dream because of who saw it. Had any other Achaean reported it they would call it a lie, "but now the man who has seen it claims to be by far the best of the Achaeans" (2.82). It is the phrase Achilles used with scorn in Book 1 (1.91). Nestor uses it as a reason to trust the dream.

**Thersites.** In the assembly Thersites turns it around: Agamemnon "has dishonored Achilles, a far better man than himself" (2.239–240). He takes Achilles' side in Achilles' own words, and Odysseus beats him with the scepter.

**The Catalogue.** When it reaches Agamemnon's contingent, the Catalogue says that he "stood out among all the heroes, because he was the best, and led by far the most men" (2.579–580). The two reasons come together, and the second explains the first. This is the best by rank, the claim Agamemnon made in Book 1.

**The Muse.** At the end of the Catalogue the narrator puts the question outright: "But who was the best of them, tell me, Muse, of the men and of the horses?" (2.761–762).

{{quote:0be28ff3-cc33-54ba-8282-cbb582990e73}}

The answer is careful. The best horses were Eumelus' mares, and the best man was Telamonian [[Ajax]]. Both answers hold only "while Achilles kept up his wrath", for Achilles was φέρτατος, "far the strongest", and so were his horses. What Achilles claimed in Book 1 and Agamemnon denied is here affirmed by the narrator, on the Muse's authority. The two measures that Nestor tried to balance in Book 1, the "stronger" man and the "greater" (1.280–281), are settled in favor of strength. See [[the-mares-of-eumelus]] and [[aristos]].
