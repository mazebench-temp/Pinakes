The Catalogue does not use the name Lapiths, but the people are unmistakable. Their towns, [[Argissa]], [[Gyrtone]], [[Orthe]], [[Elone]] and [[Oloosson]], lie in the north of Thessaly, along the Peneius and under Olympus, and their leaders are the descendants of the heroes Nestor named in Book 1. [[Polypoetes]] is the son of [[Peirithous]], and [[Leonteus]] the grandson of [[Caeneus]]. Book 12 confirms it: the two are "the high-hearted sons of the Lapith spearmen" (12.128).

The genealogy carries a story. Polypoetes was born "on the day when he took vengeance on the shaggy beasts and drove them from Pelion and pushed them to the Aethices" (2.743–744; see [[lapiths-drive-out-the-centaurs]]). Nestor told that war as a thing of the past, a standard the present generation could not meet (1.260–272). The Catalogue shows the next generation of Lapiths at Troy, forty ships strong, and Book 12 shows them holding the gate of the Achaean wall like two oaks on a mountain (12.127–134).
