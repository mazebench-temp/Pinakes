The history of Agamemnon's scepter runs from lord to lord. Hephaestus made it and gave it “to lord Zeus, son of Cronus” (Διὶ Κρονίωνι ἄνακτι); Zeus gave it to Hermes, and “lord Hermes” (Ἑρμείας δὲ ἄναξ) gave it to Pelops; it passed to Atreus and Thyestes, and last to Agamemnon, “to be lord (ἀνάσσειν) of many islands and of all Argos” (2.102–108). The sentence ends on the verb: the scepter exists to rule. See [[the-scepter-of-agamemnon]] and [[scepter]].

In speech ἄναξ is the address to a king. Odysseus and Nestor both call Agamemnon “my lord” in their speeches to the assembly (2.284, 2.360). Nestor himself “was king (ἄναξ) of sandy Pylos” (2.77). At the sacrifice and after it Agamemnon is ἄναξ ἀνδρῶν, “lord of men” (2.402, 2.434, 2.441; see [[lord-of-men]]), and Idomeneus is “lord Idomeneus” (2.405).

In the Catalogue ἄνακτος often ends a line of descent: “son of lord Mecisteus” (2.566), and so of Agasthenes, Charopus, Thessalus and Euenus (2.624, 2.672, 2.679, 2.693). Tlepolemus' Rhodians were loved by Zeus, “who rules over gods and men” (2.669). Among the Aetolians rule had passed by default: the sons of Oeneus were no more, and so the whole command had been given to Thoas, “to rule the Aetolians” (ἀνασσέμεν, 2.641–643; see [[thoas]]). And by the ships of the idle Myrmidons the chariots of their ἄνακτες, their “masters”, lie covered in the huts (ἀνάκτων, 2.777; see [[menis]]).
