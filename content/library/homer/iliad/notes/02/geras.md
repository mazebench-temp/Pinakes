{{quote:23e54e06-f664-5473-95ff-d45cf6f3b1aa}}

Thersites describes the system that Book 1 fought over. Agamemnon's huts are full of bronze and of women, “the choicest” (ἐξαίρετοι), “whom we Achaeans give to you first of all (πρωτίστῳ δίδομεν), whenever we take a citadel” (2.226–228). The word γέρας is not used, but this is the prize of honor: chosen before the spoils are shared out, and given by the army, as Achilles said of his own, “the sons of the Achaeans gave” (1.162, 1.276, 1.392; see [[division-of-spoils]]). Thersites speaks as one of the givers, and asks what more the king can want: gold brought as a Trojan's ransom, or a young woman to keep for himself (2.229–233).

Then he proposes that the rest go home and leave Agamemnon at Troy γέρα πεσσέμεν, “to stew over his prizes” (2.237). πέσσω is “cook, ripen, digest”; Calchas used its compound of a king who swallows down his anger for the day (καταπέσσω, 1.81). Left alone with what he has taken, the king would see whether the army was any help to him (2.238).

{{quote:6e9cc963-7c75-593c-8598-2eb519a7eceb}}

The charge that follows is Achilles' own line: Agamemnon “has taken his prize and keeps it, having seized it himself” (2.240 = 1.356, 1.507). Odysseus does not dispute the facts. He tells Thersites that he sits reproaching the king “because they give him so very much, the Danaan heroes” (2.255–256), and answers him with the scepter.
