---
summary: The Catalogue is framed as a list of “the leaders and lords of the Danaans”, and its gaps are leaders who are missing: two dead or left behind, and Achilles, present but refusing to lead.
passages:
  - 2.484-493 | The invocation of the Muses
  - 2.698-710 | Without a leader, though they longed for their leader
  - 2.768-779 | Achilles by his ships
---

**The leader's duty.** Thersites states it in his own way: “it is not right for one who is their leader (ἀρχὸν ἐόντα) to bring the sons of the Achaeans to grief” (2.234). Nestor makes leadership something to be tested. Marshal the men by tribes and clans, and “you will know which of the leaders is a coward, and which of the people” (2.365; see [[phylon]]). When the army goes out, the leaders sort the men as goatherds sort their flocks (2.476; see [[goatherds-sorting-their-flocks]]).

**Leaders and lords.** The invocation asks the Muses “who were the leaders and lords of the Danaans”; the poet cannot name the multitude, but promises “the leaders of the ships” (ἀρχοὺς … νηῶν), and the Catalogue ends with the same line as its question: “These, then, were the leaders and lords of the Danaans” (2.487, 2.493, 2.760; see [[invocation-of-the-muses]] and [[catalogue-of-ships]]). Inside it, Elephenor is “leader of the great-hearted Abantes” (2.541), the Epeians have four leaders with ten ships each (2.618), and Achilles is “the leader” of fifty ships (2.685).

**The missing leaders.** Two contingents have lost their leaders. Protesilaus was killed as he leapt ashore, and Philoctetes lies on Lemnos with his wound. Twice the same line reassures and grieves at once: “Nor were they without a leader (ἄναρχοι), though they longed for their leader” (2.703, 2.726). Podarces and Medon marshal them instead. The third gap is the Myrmidons. Their leader is at Troy, lying by his ships in his wrath, and they wander through the camp “longing for their leader, dear to Ares”, and do not fight (2.778; see [[pothe]] and [[achilles-withdraws]]). On the Trojan side the word appears once more: Euphemus is “leader of the Cicones” (2.846).
