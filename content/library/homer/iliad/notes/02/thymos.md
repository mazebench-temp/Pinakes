---
summary: In Book 2 the θυμός is where Zeus chooses his plan and Agamemnon holds his false hope, where the crowd is stirred to flight, where the great spirit of kings resides, and where Menelaus longs for revenge.
grc: [προθυμίῃσι]
en: [hearts, eagerness]
passages:
  - 2.35-40 | Things not to be fulfilled
---

The book opens with a decision made in the θυμός: the plan to send the Dream “seemed to him in his heart the best” (κατὰ θυμόν, 2.5; see [[decision-scene]]). The Dream leaves Agamemnon “pondering in his heart things that were not to be fulfilled” (ἀνὰ θυμόν, 2.36).

{{quote:130425b2-40e3-5355-8921-905c49fc817a}}

In the assembly the king's speech “stirred the heart in the breast” of every man who had not heard the council (θυμὸν ἐνὶ στήθεσσιν ὄρινε, 2.142). ὀρίνω is used of the sea stirred up by storms (2.294), and the similes that follow are of waves and wind (2.144–149; see [[waves-of-the-icarian-sea]] and [[wind-in-the-wheat]]). Odysseus stands still and does not touch his ship, for grief has come on his heart and spirit (κραδίην καὶ θυμόν, 2.170–171; see [[achos]] and [[kradie]]).

Odysseus warns the kings that “great is the spirit of kings whom Zeus has nurtured” (θυμὸς δὲ μέγας, 2.196): a king's θυμός is his pride and his capacity for anger, and it is to be feared (see [[cholos]] and [[basileus]]). The army resents Thersites and is “indignant in their hearts” (νεμέσσηθέν τʼ ἐνὶ θυμῷ, 2.223; see [[nemesis]]). After the beating the crowd says that his “proud spirit” (θυμὸς ἀγήνωρ) will never again drive him to abuse the kings (2.276).

Knowledge and desire live there too. Menelaus comes to the sacrifice unbidden, “for he knew in his heart how his brother was laboring” (2.409). In the Catalogue he goes among his men “trusting in his own eagerness” (προθυμίῃσι, a word formed from πρό and θυμός), and “most of all he longed in his heart” (ἵετο θυμῷ) to pay the Trojans back for Helen (2.588–590). At the feast “no one's heart lacked an equal share” (2.431), as twice in Book 1 (1.468, 1.602; see [[equal-feast]]). For the epithet μεγάθυμος, “great-hearted”, see [[great-hearted]].
