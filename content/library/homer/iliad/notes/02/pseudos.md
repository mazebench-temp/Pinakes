summary: Nestor twice weighs a message from Zeus against the possibility of a lie. He would call the Dream a lie if anyone but Agamemnon had seen it, and he tells the army to stay until it knows whether Zeus' promise at the sailing was a lie or not.
passages:
  - 2.79-83 | Nestor on the dream
  - 2.348-353 | Lightning on the right
---

Book 2 has the word twice, both times in Nestor's mouth, and both times of a message from Zeus. The audience knows that one of the two messages is false.

**The dream.** Agamemnon has told the council his dream and his plan to test the army. Nestor answers.

{{quote:7364e943-ed47-5eb2-9b39-9b5b6b982d37}}

Nestor judges the dream by its witness. Had any other Achaean told it, they would call it a ψεῦδος and “turn away from it all the more”; but the man who saw it “claims to be by far the best of the Achaeans” (2.82). The line recalls Achilles' sneer at Agamemnon in Book 1, “who now claims to be far the best of the Achaeans” (1.91). What Achilles said in scorn, Nestor offers as a reason for belief. The audience knows better. The narrator has already called the dream's promise “things that were not to be fulfilled” (2.36), and the Dream came in Nestor's own likeness (2.20–22). The man whose judgment vouches for it is the man it impersonated. See [[agamemnons-dream]] and [[dream]].

**The promise.** After Odysseus has recalled the portent at Aulis, Nestor rebukes the men who want to leave.

{{quote:7440031a-789f-5696-b741-28c4914b88a7}}

No one should go to Argos “before they know whether the promise of aegis-bearing Zeus is a lie or not” (2.348–349). He means the nod and the lightning on the right on the day the fleet sailed (see [[the-departure-from-aulis]] and [[teras]]). His test is time: stay, and see whether the promise is kept. It is the promise Agamemnon had pretended to doubt when he said that Zeus “promised me and nodded his assent”, and then devised an evil deceit (2.112–114; see [[apate]]). Nestor's alternative, a lie or not, is the question the book itself leaves open. The Dream was false, the promise will prove true, and both came from Zeus. See [[truth-and-deception]].
