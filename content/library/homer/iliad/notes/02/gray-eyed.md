---
summary: Book 2 names Athena by the formula four times, at each of her interventions: sent by Hera, speaking to Odysseus, silencing the assembly, and rousing the army with the aegis.
---

Athena acts four times in Book 2, and each time the narrator names her θεὰ γλαυκῶπις Ἀθήνη or γλαυκῶπις Ἀθήνη. She obeys Hera and darts down from Olympus ({{ref:Iliad 2.166}}); she stands by Odysseus and tells him to stop the flight ({{ref:Iliad 2.172}}); in the likeness of a herald she silences the army so that Odysseus can speak ({{ref:Iliad 2.279-280}}); and she passes through the ranks with the aegis, filling each man with the will to fight ({{ref:Iliad 2.446}}; [[aegis-bearing]]).

{{quote:06d00367-d344-5250-8104-60769d21b3c2}}

In Book 1 Athena stopped one man from fighting. In Book 2 she stops a whole army from going home and then sends it into battle.
