Κρόνου πάϊς ἀγκυλομήτεω, “the son of crooked-counseling Cronus”. Odysseus uses the phrase for the god who gave the one king “the scepter and the ordinances” (2.205–206), and again for the god who turned the serpent at Aulis to stone (2.319). ἀγκυλομήτης = ἀγκύλος (“crooked”) + μῆτις (“counsel, cunning”): the cunning of the god who swallowed his children. It is Cronus' standing epithet in Hesiod (*Theogony* 137, 168, 473). See [[crooked-counseling]].

The patronymics continue as well: Κρονίδης (2.111, 2.375) and Κρονίων (2.102, 2.350, 2.403, 2.419, 2.670). See [[son-of-cronus]].
