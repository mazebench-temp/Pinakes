---
summary: After Odysseus beats Thersites, the army laughs, and “a man” speaks for all. Odysseus has done countless good deeds, but silencing this slanderer is the best of them (2.270–278).
---

{{quote:fda9a5f1-b0db-55dc-9c11-c6c0d27f40e2}}

The speech comes at the turn of the episode. The men are “troubled” (ἀχνύμενοι), since the homecoming they ran for is lost, and yet they laugh (2.270). Then one nameless voice gives the army's verdict in three steps. First comes praise of Odysseus in the two fields of a hero's excellence, “leading the way in good counsel and marshaling the battle” (2.273). Then the deed of the day, “by far the best”: he has stopped “this word-slinging slanderer” (τὸν λωβητῆρα ἐπεσβόλον) from haranguing them (2.274–275). Last, a forecast: Thersites' “proud spirit” will never drive him to abuse the kings again (2.276–277). θυμὸς ἀγήνωρ (“proud, manly spirit”) belongs to heroes; said of Thersites, it is a sneer.

The voice takes the kings' side. Thersites had spoken to the army as one of it, “let us go home with our ships” (2.236), and had called the men “Achaean women” for staying (2.235). The army answers with laughter and a vote for Odysseus. The narrator gave his own judgment of Thersites before he spoke (2.212–223); after the beating he lets the army give its judgment. The iterative εἴπεσκεν says that such things were said again and again, and the closing verse turns the single voice back into the many: ὣς φάσαν ἣ πληθύς, “so said the crowd” (2.278). πληθύς (“multitude”) is the mass of the army, the men “who had not heard the council” (2.143), whom the poet could not name one by one (2.488).

Then Odysseus rises with the scepter, and the crowd that has just spoken falls silent to hear him (2.278–282; see [[with-good-intent]]). See [[odysseus-and-thersites]], [[thersites]] and [[commoners]].
