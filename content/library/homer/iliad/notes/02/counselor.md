---
summary: Odysseus uses the epithet twice: of Zeus as the source of a king's honor, and in Calchas' reading of the portent at Aulis.
---

Both uses are in Odysseus' speeches, and both claim Zeus' plan for the side of staying. To a king about to flee he says that “a king's honor comes from Zeus, and Zeus the counselor loves him” ({{ref:Iliad 2.197}}; [[zeus-nurtured]]). To the army he recalls Calchas' words at Aulis:

{{quote:61cce681-3bc9-50a0-907b-080ef3797900}}

The audience knows what Odysseus does not: on this very day Zeus of counsel is carrying out a different plan, to honor Achilles by the Achaeans' defeat ({{ref:Iliad 2.3-4}}; [[plan-of-zeus]]). The Dream he sent was a lie. The portent at Aulis was true, but it promised Troy only in the tenth year, and it said nothing of what the tenth year would cost.
