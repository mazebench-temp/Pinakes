---
summary: The formula itself does not occur in Book 2, but its verb reports the answer to Agamemnon's prayer before battle: Zeus “was not yet going to fulfill” it.
refs: [2.419]
passages:
  - 2.410-418 | Agamemnon's prayer
---

Agamemnon's prayer to Zeus before the first battle asks for the sack of Troy before sunset (2.412–418). It has no “if ever” and no “fulfill for me this wish”; the request comes straight after the god's titles (see [[prayer-type-scene]]). The answer is told with the verb of the formula, κραιαίνω (“bring to pass”), and a negative: οὐδʼ ἄρα πώ οἱ ἐπεκραίαινε Κρονίων, “but the son of Cronus was not yet going to fulfill his prayer” (2.419). Zeus accepts the offering and refuses the wish (see [[sack-of-troy-foretold]]).

The formula returns in Book 8, when Agamemnon prays again, in defeat, and asks only that the Achaeans may escape: ἀλλὰ Ζεῦ τόδε πέρ μοι ἐπικρήηνον ἐέλδωρ (8.242). This time Zeus pities him and nods assent (8.245–246).
