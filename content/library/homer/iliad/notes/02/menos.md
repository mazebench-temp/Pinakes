---
summary: In Book 2 μένος is the fury of the whole army, which only night will part, and the word lives on in epithets, the Abantes “breathing fury” and Zeus “supreme in might”.
---

In Book 1 μένος belonged to single men, and others tried to check it: Athena came to stop the μένος of Achilles, and Nestor told Agamemnon to check his (1.207, 1.282). In Book 2 it belongs to armies, and Agamemnon wants it unchecked. He sends the men to their meal before the battle and promises a fight without rest “unless night comes and parts the fury of men” (εἰ μὴ νὺξ ἐλθοῦσα διακρινέει μένος ἀνδρῶν, 2.387). Only nightfall will separate the two sides. See [[polemos]].

The word also lives in epithets. The Abantes of Euboea are μένεα πνείοντες, “breathing fury” (2.536; see [[breathing-fury]]). Zeus is ὑπερμενής, “supreme in might”, three times in the book (2.116, 2.350, 2.403; see [[supreme-in-might]]), a compound of μένος.
