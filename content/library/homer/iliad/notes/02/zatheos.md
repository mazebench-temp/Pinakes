---
summary: The Catalogue gives the epithet to two Greek places, Nisa in Boeotia and Crisa near Delphi.
---

Both uses come in the same half-line pattern, a place name followed by τε ζαθέην: Νῖσάν τε ζαθέην (“holy Nisa,” {{ref:Iliad 2.508}}) and Κρῖσάν τε ζαθέην (“holy Crisa,” {{ref:Iliad 2.520}}). Crisa lies near Pytho, the sanctuary of Apollo at Delphi, which is named in the line before ([[rocky]], [[pytho]]); like Cilla in Book 1, it belongs to Apollo's country. The site of Nisa is unknown, and nothing is known of a cult there.
