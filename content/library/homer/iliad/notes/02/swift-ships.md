# Θόας at 2.638 is Thoas, leader of the Aetolians.
except: [2.638]
---

The line-end phrase θοὰς ἐπὶ νῆας Ἀχαιῶν (“to the swift ships of the Achaeans”) marks arrivals from the gods. Zeus sends the Dream “to the swift ships” ({{ref:Iliad 2.8}}), and the Dream “quickly reached the swift ships” ({{ref:Iliad 2.17}}); Athena darts down from Olympus and reaches them in the same words ({{ref:Iliad 2.168}}). The repetition joins the two divine interventions of the morning, the one that sets the army in motion and the one that saves it from its own flight.

Odysseus turns the phrase into a threat: he will send Thersites “howling back to the swift ships” ({{ref:Iliad 2.263}}). In the Catalogue the Epeians' four leaders each command “ten swift ships” ({{ref:Iliad 2.618-619}}).

The name of the Aetolian leader Thoas (Θόας, {{ref:Iliad 2.638}}) differs from the adjective θοάς only in its accent.
