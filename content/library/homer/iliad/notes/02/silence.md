---
summary: Book 2 is a struggle to make the army silent and listen. Nine heralds shout it down, Athena as a herald calls for quiet so that Odysseus can be heard, Calchas asks the stunned army why it is silent, and the poet says he could not speak the whole multitude.
---

**Quieting the assembly.** When the army gathers, the earth groans and there is a din, and nine heralds shout to make the men hold back from their clamor and listen to the kings (2.95–100). Silence here is order. The men sit, and the kings speak.

{{quote:9af04649-5dbb-5633-8a14-d186df2b660a}}

**Odysseus' hearing.** When Odysseus rises with the scepter, Athena stands beside him in the likeness of a herald and orders the people to be silent, "so that the nearest and the farthest of the sons of the Achaeans alike might hear his words":

{{quote:7c812926-3bba-5fbf-b80f-b2c11a9ff52c}}

**Speechless at Aulis.** In the story Odysseus tells, the portent struck the army dumb, and Calchas began, "Why are you silent (ἄνεῳ), long-haired Achaeans?" (2.323; see [[the-portent-at-aulis]]).

**Thersites.** He was the one man who "kept up his brawling" when the rest had sat down (2.211–212). The scepter silences him, and he wipes away a tear (2.265–269; see [[odysseus-and-thersites]]).

**The king's silence.** Agamemnon says nothing while the army runs for the ships, and nothing while Thersites abuses him to his face. He speaks again only when Nestor has restored order (see the Book 2 note of [[agamemnon]]).

**The poet's silence.** "The multitude I could not tell or name, not even if I had ten tongues and ten mouths" (2.488–490). The Catalogue names leaders and counts ships; the rest of the army stays nameless. See [[ten-tongues]] and [[invocation-of-the-muses]].
