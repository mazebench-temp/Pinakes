The lyre appears in Book 2 only through its art. In the Catalogue the Muses meet Thamyris the Thracian at Dorium; he had boasted that he would win even if they sang against him, and in their anger they maimed him, “took away his wondrous song and made him forget his lyre-playing” (ἀοιδὴν / θεσπεσίην ἀφέλοντο καὶ ἐκλέλαθον κιθαριστύν, 2.599–600). κιθαριστύς occurs only here in Homer. It is the art of the κίθαρις, Homer's other word for the lyre: a herald puts a κίθαρις into the hands of the singer Phemius, and he begins his song “playing the lyre” (φορμίζων, *Odyssey* 1.153–155). The Muses take song and lyre together (see [[thamyris]], [[thamyris-and-the-muses]] and [[muses]]).
