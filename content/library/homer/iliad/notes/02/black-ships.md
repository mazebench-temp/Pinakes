# κῆρα μέλαιναν, “black doom,” is not a ship.
except: [2.859]
---

The Catalogue closes most of its entries with a count of ships, and the commonest form is a whole line: τῷ δ’ ἅμα τεσσαράκοντα μέλαιναι νῆες ἕποντο, “and with him followed forty black ships.” The number changes (forty, fifty, eighty) and the pronoun changes with the number of leaders; the rest stays. The formula occurs twelve times:

{{quote:e3cfd78c-ed60-5e0d-80d0-a30708f8c2bd}}

The other counting formula uses “hollow ships” ([[hollow-ships]]). Between them they give the Catalogue its refrain ([[catalogue-of-ships]]).

Before the Catalogue the epithet appears in the genitive formula νηὸς ἐϋσσέλμοιο μελαίνης, “the black, well-benched ship” ([[well-benched]]): Odysseus does not touch his ship when the army runs for home ({{ref:Iliad 2.170}}), and Nestor tells any man who wants to go home to lay hands on his ({{ref:Iliad 2.358}}).

The adjective has other uses in the book that are not ships: “black doom” (κῆρα μέλαιναν, {{ref:Iliad 2.859}}), “the black earth” ([[black-earth]]), and the dark water of the Aesepus.
