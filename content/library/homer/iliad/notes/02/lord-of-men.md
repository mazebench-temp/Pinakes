---
summary: Book 2 uses the title four times, at the sacrifice before battle, in Nestor's formal address, and in the Catalogue.
---

The title comes in the second half of the book, when Agamemnon acts as commander. “Agamemnon, lord of men” sacrifices an ox to Zeus and calls the elders ({{ref:Iliad 2.402}}). After the feast Nestor addresses him with the whole formal line:

{{quote:65faa45d-8060-5bb2-b6fd-92e3b331a177}}

This is the address that Achilles began in Book 1 and broke off. There Ἀτρεΐδη κύδιστε was followed by “most covetous of all men” ({{ref:Iliad 1.122}}; [[most-glorious]], [[most-covetous]]). Here Nestor completes it with ἄναξ ἀνδρῶν Ἀγάμεμνον, and Agamemnon, lord of men, “did not disobey” ({{ref:Iliad 2.441}}).

In the Catalogue the title explains a gift: “Agamemnon, lord of men,” gave the Arcadians the ships in which they sailed ({{ref:Iliad 2.612}}; [[wine-dark]]).
