**The plan of Zeus.** The book's first βουλή is Zeus'. Lying awake, he ponders how to honor Achilles, “and this plan seemed to him in his heart the best” (ἀρίστη φαίνετο βουλή, 2.5): to send a baneful Dream to Agamemnon. The plan announced in the proem (1.5) is being carried out, and its first step is a deception (see [[plan-of-zeus]], [[agamemnons-dream]] and [[decision-scene]]). The Dream then rebukes Agamemnon in the language of counsel: “a man of counsel (βουληφόρον ἄνδρα) should not sleep the whole night through” (2.24, repeated at 2.61; see [[a-man-of-counsel-should-not-sleep]]).

**The council.** Before the assembly Agamemnon seats a council of the elders beside Nestor's ship. It is the poem's first council, and the first time the chiefs meet apart from the assembly (ἀγορή) of the whole army (see [[elders]] and [[assembly]]).

{{quote:08e9f920-2c4b-5999-957e-efeb7ad4fd7d}}

In three lines the word passes from the body to its business: he seats a βουλή and lays out a “shrewd plan” (πυκινὴν … βουλήν), the test of the army (see [[the-test-of-the-army]]). What is said there stays with the few. Nestor leads the elders out of the council (βουλῆς, 2.84), and Agamemnon's speech stirs “every man in the crowd who had not heard the council” (2.143): they run for the ships. Odysseus tells the kings that “not all of us heard what he said in the council” (2.194); the line can also be punctuated as a question, “did we not all hear…?”.

**Counsel and war.** Odysseus tells a man of the people that he is “never counted for anything in war or in council” (2.202), and ends with the one king whom Zeus has given the scepter and the ordinances “so that he may take counsel for them” (βουλεύῃσι, 2.206; see [[themis]] and [[one-ruler]]). The crowd praises Odysseus in the same pair of terms, for “good counsel” and for marshaling the battle (2.273), as Nestor had called Achilles and Agamemnon first among the Danaans in counsel and in battle (1.258). When Odysseus rises to speak, Athena in the likeness of a herald silences the army so that all may hear him “and take thought for his counsel” (2.282).

**Nestor.** Nestor turns on the assembly.

{{quote:008866c5-f953-520f-b78f-646b2ecc723a}}

If the army goes home, then into the fire with “the counsels (βουλαί) and plans of men”, the libations and the pledges they made when they set out (2.340–341). He tells Agamemnon to hold to his “steadfast purpose” (ἀστεμφέα βουλήν, 2.344), and to let the one or two who “make plans apart from the rest” waste away (νόσφιν βουλεύωσʼ, 2.347).

**Agamemnon.** In the test the king says that Zeus “has devised (βουλεύσατο) an evil deceit” (2.114). It is a pretense, and exactly what Zeus has done (see [[ate]] and [[apate]]). After Nestor's speech he admits that he began the quarrel with Achilles, and looks ahead: “if ever we are of one mind in counsel” (ἔς γε μίαν βουλεύσομεν), the Trojans will have no respite from evil (2.379–380).
