This is the second double name in the poem, and the order is reversed. In Book 1 the gods' name came first: "whom the gods call Briareus, but all men Aegaeon" (1.403). Here men's name comes first, [[Batieia]], and then the gods' name. The two are also of different kinds. Batieia is an ordinary place-name, perhaps "Bramble Hill". The gods' name is a description, "the tomb of much-bounding [[Myrine]]", and it tells what men have forgotten: the hill is a grave, and whose grave it is. Here, as with Briareus, the divine name is the one that carries a story.

The contrast suits a book that has just made much of the difference between divine and human knowledge. Before the Catalogue the poet calls on the Muses, who "are present and know all things, but we hear only the report, and know nothing" (2.485–486).
