---
summary: After Agamemnon's sacrifice the line ends the meal as at Chryse (2.432), but what follows is Nestor's call to war, not wine and song.
---

The line follows [[equal-feast]] after the sacrifice to Zeus, as it did at Chryse (2.431–432 = 1.468–469). At Chryse it led to the wine and the paean (1.470–474). Here it leads to a speech, “the Gerenian horseman Nestor began to speak among them” (2.433), and what Nestor urges is the muster for battle (see [[feast-type-scene]]).
