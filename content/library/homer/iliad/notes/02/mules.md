---
summary: The only mules of Book 2 are the wild mules of the land of the Eneti, from which the Paphlagonians came to Troy.
grc: [ἡμιόνων]
---

The Paphlagonians came “from the land of the Eneti, where the breed of wild mules comes from” (ἡμιόνων γένος ἀγροτεράων, 2.852; see [[eneti]] and [[paphlagonians]]).

{{quote:322afae5-54df-5816-bdd8-94b207a119de}}

ἡμίονος (“half-ass”) is Homer's usual word for the mule; οὐρεύς, the word of Book 1 (1.50), is the rarer one. A breed of wild mules is a puzzle, since mules are sterile, and many readers take the animals to be wild asses: the zoological name of the Asian wild ass, *Equus hemionus*, comes from this word. Mules came to Troy from its Asian neighbors: those that draw Priam's wagon to Achilles were a gift of the Mysians (24.277–278).
