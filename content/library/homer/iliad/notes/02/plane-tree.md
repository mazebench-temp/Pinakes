Odysseus sets the scene of the portent with the tree. At [[aulis|Aulis]] the army was offering hecatombs at the holy altars around a spring, “beneath a fine plane tree, where bright water flowed” (καλῇ ὑπὸ πλατανίστῳ ὅθεν ῥέεν ἀγλαὸν ὕδωρ).

{{quote:2e79eb4b-16fd-560d-9477-73317eb56fac}}

Spring, tree and altars together make a place of worship in the open air (see [[altar]]). The sign moves from one to the other. The serpent darts out from beneath the altar and makes for the plane tree (2.310), where the nestlings cower under the leaves on the topmost branch (ὄζῳ ἐπʼ ἀκροτάτῳ πετάλοις ὑποπεπτηῶτες, 2.312).

{{quote:b33ee2be-da52-52ed-baa2-e6651787495e}}

The details are a witness's. Odysseus reminds the army that they all saw it (μάρτυροι, “witnesses”, 2.302), and the tree, the spring and the altar fix the memory in a place. For the portent and its reading, see [[the-portent-at-aulis]], [[serpent]] and [[sparrow]].
