In Book 1 the bow was Apollo's weapon. In Book 2 it picks out a few men in the lists of the two armies.

**Philoctetes' archers.** In the Catalogue of Ships only one contingent is described as archers. [[philoctetes|Philoctetes]], “well skilled with the bow” (τόξων ἐῢ εἰδώς), led seven ships from Methone and its neighbors, with fifty rowers in each, “well skilled with the bow, to fight with might” (2.718–720; see [[skilled-with-the-bow]]). But he is not at Troy.

{{quote:15686eac-cd87-50f0-8ba1-285554a937d2}}

The Achaeans left him on [[lemnos|Lemnos]] in agony from the wound of a [[water-snake]], and Medon leads his men. “Soon they were to remember” him; the poem does not say why (see [[philoctetes-on-lemnos]]).

**Apollo's bow.** The god's title ἀργυρότοξος, “of the silver bow”, returns in a gentler setting: Apollo reared the mares of Eumelus, the best horses in the army (2.766; see [[god-of-the-silver-bow]] and [[the-mares-of-eumelus]]).

**At play.** While Achilles keeps up his wrath, his Myrmidons amuse themselves on the shore “throwing the discus and the javelin / and shooting their bows” (τόξοισιν, 2.774–775; see [[discus]]).

**The Trojan archers.** In the Trojan Catalogue, [[Pandarus]] is marked by his bow, which “Apollo himself had given” him (ᾧ καὶ τόξον Ἀπόλλων αὐτὸς ἔδωκεν, 2.827). The gift prepares the shot that breaks the truce in Book 4 (see [[the-bow-of-pandarus]]). Pyraechmes leads the Paeonians “with their curved bows” (Παίονας ἀγκυλοτόξους, 2.848; ἀγκύλος, “curved”, + τόξον; see [[paeonians]]).
