---
summary: In Book 2 the Achaeans are an army under test. They run for home, are driven back, and are marshaled and counted, and the Catalogue names their leaders and their ships. Thersites calls them “Achaean women, Achaean men no longer”.
grc: [Ἀχαιΐδες, Παναχαιῶν]
en: [all the Achaeans]
---

**New names.** Παναχαιοί (“all the Achaeans”): the elders Agamemnon calls to his sacrifice are “the best of all the Achaeans” (2.404). Ἀχαιΐδες (“Achaean women”) is Thersites' insult, “Weaklings, wretched disgraces, Achaean women, Achaean men no longer” (2.235). Menelaus throws the same words at the army when no one will face Hector (7.96). See [[thersites]].

**The army as a crowd.** Book 2 shows the army above all as a mass. It streams to the assembly like bees (2.87–93), heaves like the sea and bows like wheat in the wind as it runs for the ships (2.142–154), roars like surf on a beach (2.209–210) and like waves on a headland (2.394–397), and at last pours onto the plain like flocks of birds, as many as the leaves and flowers, or the flies around the milk pails (2.459–473). See [[swarming-bees]], [[waves-of-the-icarian-sea]], [[surf-on-the-beach]], [[birds-on-the-asian-meadow]] and [[flies-around-the-milk-pails]].

**Counted.** Agamemnon claims that if the two armies were counted, many a company of ten Achaeans would lack a Trojan to pour its wine (2.123–128). Nestor tells him to marshal them by tribes and clans (2.362–363), and the Catalogue counts them by their ships, contingent by contingent (2.494–759). See [[catalogue-of-ships]].

**One people among many.** In the Catalogue “Achaeans” is also the name of one people among others. The men of Phthia “were called Myrmidons and Hellenes and Achaeans” (2.684), and the lesser Ajax surpassed “all the Panhellenes and Achaeans” with the spear (2.530). See [[hellenes]], [[panhellenes]] and [[myrmidons]].
