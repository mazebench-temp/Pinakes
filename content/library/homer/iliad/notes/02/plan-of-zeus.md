**The plan made plain.** Book 1 ended with the gods asleep. Book 2 begins with the one god who is not.

{{quote:f27e722e-0793-5781-bd01-1ef76081bef8}}

The plan is now stated by the narrator: to honor Achilles and destroy many beside the ships of the Achaeans (2.3–4). Hera guessed it in the same words at 1.559. The means is the “best plan” (ἀρίστη βουλή, 2.5; see [[boule]] and [[decision-scene]]): a baneful Dream that sends Agamemnon to battle in the hope of taking Troy (see [[agamemnons-dream]]).

**Wider than the promise.** The narrator then says where it leads: Zeus “was yet to lay more sorrows and groaning on Trojans and Danaans alike in the course of fierce battles” (2.39–40). The promise to Thetis concerned the Achaeans' losses. This line speaks of suffering on both sides, and it can be read with either view of the proem's “plan of Zeus”: the plan made for Achilles, or the larger design of a war that thins out mankind.

**Named, but misread.** In the test Agamemnon accuses Zeus of an “evil deceit” (κακὴν ἀπάτην βουλεύσατο, 2.114): Zeus promised him the sack of Ilios and now sends him home. He says it to deceive the army, and it is true in a way he cannot see (see [[apate]] and [[the-test-of-the-army]]).

**Signs and a refusal.** Zeus' older signs are recalled to hold the army at Troy: the portent at Aulis, nine years of war and the city taken in the tenth (2.308–329), and his lightning on the right on the day the fleet sailed (2.350–353; see [[the-portent-at-aulis]] and [[the-departure-from-aulis]]). Both promise the end of the war; neither says when. When Agamemnon prays to take Priam's hall before sunset, Zeus accepts the offering and refuses the prayer, and “made the toil greater” (2.419–420). He makes Agamemnon stand out among the heroes “on that day” (2.482), and sends Iris to bring the Trojans out to meet him (2.786–787). Every part of the plan, deception included, drives both armies onto the plain.
