---
summary: The verb κολῳάω, “brawl”, occurs once in Homer, of Thersites, who “still kept up his brawling” when the rest of the army had sat down.
grc: [ἐκολῴα]
---

Odysseus has driven the army back to its seats, and “only Thersites still kept up his brawling, a man of unmeasured words” (Θερσίτης δʼ ἔτι μοῦνος ἀμετροεπὴς ἐκολῴα, 2.212). The verb κολῳάω occurs only here in Homer, as the noun κολῳός occurs only in Book 1, where Hephaestus complains that Zeus and Hera “set the gods brawling” (1.575). The two words belong to two assemblies, one of gods and one of men, and both brawls are quarrels. Hephaestus sets κολῳός beside ἐριδαίνετον (“you two quarrel”, 1.574), and Thersites' words are for “quarreling with the kings” (ἐριζέμεναι βασιλεῦσιν, 2.214). Both scenes end in laughter at a lame figure: the gods laugh at Hephaestus (1.599–600), and the army at Thersites, who is “lame in one foot” (2.217, 2.270).
