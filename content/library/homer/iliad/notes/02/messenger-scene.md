**The Dream.** The book opens with a messenger scene that keeps closely to the pattern. Zeus gives the order in direct speech and tells the Dream to deliver it “exactly” (ἀτρεκέως, 2.10):

{{quote:0403172b-3f2d-5dc9-b9a0-1e713afaf334}}

The Dream goes (βῆ), arrives (ἵκανε) and finds (κίχανεν) Agamemnon asleep (2.16–19), as the heralds went, came and found Achilles in Book 1 (1.327–329). It stands above his head in the likeness of Nestor and delivers the message in Zeus' words, with the persons changed (2.28–32, from 2.11–15). What it adds is its own: a rebuke to the sleeper (2.23–25), the claim to be “a messenger to you from Zeus” (Διὸς … ἄγγελος), who “cares greatly for you and pities you” (2.26–27), the words “from Zeus” (2.33), and a warning not to forget (2.33–34). Then it goes away (2.35). The exact report carries a lie. Agamemnon tells it a third time, to the council (2.56–70). See [[dream]], [[dream-scene]] and [[message-repeated]].

**Hera, Athena, Odysseus.** When the army runs for the ships, Hera gives Athena her message (2.157–165). Athena “did not disobey”: she darts down from the peaks of Olympus, as Apollo did in Book 1 (2.167; 1.44), reaches the ships, finds Odysseus standing still, and stands near him (2.166–172).

{{quote:b05f02b3-8f00-5cea-94cc-9b8ec6268ef9}}

The speech is Hera's, adapted. Hera's question about “the Argives” becomes a question to Odysseus: “you will flee, tumbling into your many-benched ships” (2.174–175). Hera's order to Athena, “go now through the army … with your gentle words hold back every man” (2.163–165), becomes Athena's order to Odysseus (2.179–181). The command passes from goddess to goddess to man, and the man carries it out in its own words: he “hold him back with gentle words” (ἀγανοῖς ἐπέεσσιν ἐρητύσασκε, 2.189). See [[divine-epiphany]].

**Iris.** The last messenger scene of the book lacks its first step. The poem does not show Zeus giving the order; Iris simply comes “from aegis-bearing Zeus, with grievous news” (2.786–787).

{{quote:f11430bb-1410-56f5-ab67-fcb77b5351d7}}

She finds the Trojans in assembly, stands near them, and speaks in the voice of Priam's son Polites. Her message is a warning to Priam and orders for Hector (2.796–806). There is no reply and no return; Hector acts at once (2.807–808; see [[iris]]).

Zeus' messengers open and close the book. The first carries a lie to the Achaean king in the shape of his most trusted counselor; the last carries the truth to the Trojans in the shape of one of their own. Between them Rumor, “the messenger of Zeus”, drives the Achaeans to their assembly (2.93–94; see [[ossa]]).
