---
summary: Sixteen lines of Book 2 close a speech with the formula, and the rest of the verse shows what the words did. The Dream goes, the army stampedes or shouts, a goddess and a king obey, a blow falls, and Zeus refuses a prayer.
grc: ["ὣς φάθʼ", "ὣς ἔφαθʼ", "ὣς φάσαν", "ὣς ὃ μὲν εἰπὼν"]
---

The closings of Book 2: {{ref:Iliad 2.16}}, 35, 70, 76, 84, 142, 166, 182, 243, 265, 278, 333, 394, 419, 441, 807. Three forms are new: ὣς φάθʼ and ὣς ἔφαθʼ, “so she spoke” (2.182, 2.807); the plural ὣς φάσαν, “So said the crowd” (2.278); and, inside a speech, ὣς ὃ μὲν εἰπών, “so he spoke” (2.70).

Book 2 is a book of orders and of speeches to crowds, and hardly any of its speeches is answered by another (see [[answering-him]]). The answer comes in the second half of the closing verse.

**Obedience.** “So he spoke, and the Dream went, when he had heard the command” (2.16). Athena “did not disobey” Hera (οὐδʼ ἀπίθησε, 2.166), and after the sacrifice Agamemnon, in the same words, “did not disobey” Nestor (2.441). When Nestor leaves the council, the kings rise and follow him (2.84–86). In Book 1 the formula twice led to fear and obedience together (ἔδεισεν, 1.33, 1.568). In Book 2 obedience comes without fear, from the goddess down to the king.

**Recognition.** Twice a god speaks, unseen or in disguise, and the closing tells that the hearer knew. Odysseus “knew the voice of the goddess as she spoke” (2.182), and Hector “did not fail to know the goddess's word”, though Iris had taken the voice of Polites (2.807).

**The army.** Agamemnon's test “stirred the heart in the breast” of every man who had not heard the council, and the assembly broke for the ships (2.142–154). After Odysseus' speech, and again after Agamemnon's, the verse turns the other way: “So he spoke, and the Argives shouted aloud” (Ἀργεῖοι δὲ μέγʼ ἴαχον, 2.333, 2.394). The second time the shout runs on into a simile (see [[wave-on-the-headland]]). In between, the crowd has had its own say, closed with a plural: “So said the crowd” (ὣς φάσαν ἣ πληθύς, 2.278; see [[tis-speech]]). After Thersites' abuse the verse holds back the speaker's name until the next line: “So he spoke, abusing Agamemnon, shepherd of the people, / Thersites” (2.243–244).

**Word and blow.** “So he spoke, and with the scepter he struck him on the back and shoulders” (2.265). The second half-line does at once what the speech had threatened (see [[it-will-be-fulfilled]]).

**Departure.** The Dream leaves Agamemnon in the line that ended Thetis' visit to Achilles: “So he spoke and went away, and left him there” (2.35 = 1.428, with the masculine participle). Achilles was left angry at heart; Agamemnon is left “pondering in his heart things that were not to be fulfilled” (2.36). When Agamemnon retells the dream, he closes its words himself: “So he spoke, and flew away and was gone” (2.70–71; see [[retelling]]).

**The refused prayer.** In Book 1 the closing of a prayer was followed by the god's hearing: “So he spoke in prayer, and Phoebus Apollo heard him” (1.43, 1.457; see [[hear-me]]). Book 2 turns the line the other way.
