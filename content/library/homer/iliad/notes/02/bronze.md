summary: Bronze in Book 2 is the army's wealth, its weapon and, above all, its gleam: the marvelous bronze of the marching host flashes to the sky like a forest fire.
grc: [χαλκοῦ, χαλκῷ, χαλκὸν, χάλκεον]
passages:
  - 2.455-458 | Like a forest fire
  - 2.488-490 | Ten tongues
---

**Wealth.** Thersites opens his abuse with Agamemnon's riches: “Your huts are full of bronze, and there are many women in your huts” (2.226). The bronze in the king's huts is spoil, like the women whom the army gives him first “whenever we take a citadel” (2.227–228; see [[hut]] and [[division-of-spoils]]).

**Weapon.** As in Book 1, “the bronze” can stand for the blade. Agamemnon prays to tear the tunic of Hector around his chest, “rent with the bronze” (χαλκῷ ῥωγαλέον, 2.417; see [[tunic]]).

**Gleam.** The formula “bronze-armored Achaeans” recurs (2.47, 2.163, 2.187, 2.437), and when the army marches out, the bronze is what is seen. The first of the similes of the muster compares the glare of their armor to a forest fire on a mountain, seen from far off: “so, as they marched, from their marvelous bronze (χαλκοῦ θεσπεσίοιο) the dazzling gleam went up through the upper air to the sky” (2.457–458; see [[forest-fire]]). In the Catalogue Agamemnon himself “put on the gleaming bronze (νώροπα χαλκόν), exulting, and he stood out among all the heroes” (2.578–579; see [[gleaming-bronze]]).

**A heart of bronze.** Before the Catalogue the poet says that he could not tell the multitude even with ten tongues and ten mouths, “a voice that could not break, and a heart of bronze within me” (χάλκεον … ἦτορ, 2.490). Bronze is what does not break or tire (see [[ten-tongues]]).
