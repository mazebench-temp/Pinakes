The flies come in the run of similes as the army forms up on the plain of the Scamander (see [[flies-around-the-milk-pails]]). The point of the comparison is number: “so many” (τόσσοι) were the Achaeans. The first words echo the first simile of the book. “The many tribes of thronging flies” (μυιάων ἁδινάων ἔθνεα πολλά) is built like “the tribes of thronging bees” (2.87; see [[bees]]), and the season is again spring. The scene is a sheepfold (σταθμὸν ποιμνήϊον, “a shepherd's steading”) at milking time, loud with flies drawn to the milk. The application turns the homely picture to war: the Achaeans stand on the plain “eager to tear them apart” (διαρραῖσαι μεμαῶτες, 2.473), drawn to battle as the flies are to the pails. The picture returns, with its last line repeated, when the fighters crowd around the body of Sarpedon (16.641–643).
