---
summary: Book 2 adds γλαφυρός, “hollowed,” in the Catalogue's formula for the number of ships and in the line that tells how war became sweeter than going home.
grc: [γλαφυραὶ, γλαφυρῇσι]
---

γλαφυρός, from γλάφω (“hollow out, carve”), means “hollow, hollowed out”; Homer uses it of ships, of a cave or a rock (πέτρης ἐκ γλαφυρῆς, “from a hollow rock,” {{ref:Iliad 2.88}}), and in the Odyssey of the lyre. Of ships it says what κοῖλος says, and the translation renders both “hollow.”

In the Catalogue it is one of two formulas that count a contingent's ships. The usual one is “and with him followed forty black ships” ([[black-ships]]). The other is τοῖς δὲ τριήκοντα γλαφυραὶ νέες ἐστιχόωντο, “and with them thirty hollow ships were ranged in line” ({{ref:Iliad 2.516}}, {{ref:Iliad 2.680}}, {{ref:Iliad 2.733}}; ninety for Nestor at {{ref:Iliad 2.602}}). The two formulas vary the list without changing its content ([[catalogue-of-ships]]).

The other use comes at the turn of the book. Athena passes through the army with the aegis, and the men who ran for the ships in the morning now want to fight:

{{quote:8043fe94-871f-5509-a302-0d0eb8e2bf9d}}

The hollow ships, which had been the means of flight, are now what the army no longer wants.
