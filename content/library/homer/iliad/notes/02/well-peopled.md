---
summary: Book 2 gives the formula to Ilios itself, and a related phrase to two cities of Crete.
grc: [εὖ ναιετοώσας]
en: [well-peopled]
---

In Book 1 the formula described the towns of the Troad that the army had sacked. In Book 2 Agamemnon uses it of the city it has not been able to take: the Trojan allies “will not let me, much as I want to, sack the well-peopled citadel of Ilios” ({{ref:Iliad 2.132-133}}).

In the Cretan entry of the Catalogue a related phrase praises two of Idomeneus' towns, “Phaestus and Rhytium, well-peopled cities” (πόλεις εὖ ναιετοώσας, {{ref:Iliad 2.648}}). ναιετάω is a longer form of ναίω (“dwell”), and the phrase means “cities well inhabited.”
