summary: A sparrow and her eight nestlings are the victims of the sign at Aulis; Calchas counts the nine birds the serpent ate as nine years of war.

**The nest.** On the topmost branch of the plane tree at Aulis sat the nestlings of a sparrow, “cowering beneath the leaves” (πετάλοις ὑποπεπτηῶτες), eight of them, “and the mother who bore them was the ninth” (2.311–313). Odysseus calls them νήπια τέκνα, “helpless young”, the words Agamemnon used earlier in the book for the soldiers' own little children, waiting at home with their wives (2.136; see [[nepios]]).

**The kill.** Odysseus tells it with the birds' voices in it. The nestlings cheep pitifully (ἐλεεινὰ … τετριγῶτας) as they are eaten, and the mother flies around them “lamenting her dear young” (ὀδυρομένη φίλα τέκνα) until the [[serpent]] catches her by the wing as she shrieks (2.314–316). ὀδύρομαι (“lament, wail”) is the verb Odysseus has just used of the army itself, wailing to one another about going home like young children or widows (2.290; see [[children-and-widows]]).

**Nine.** For [[Calchas]] the birds are a number. He repeats the line of the count (2.313 = 2.327) and draws the sum.

{{quote:61cce681-3bc9-50a0-907b-080ef3797900}}

Eight nestlings and the mother make nine, the years the army must fight; Troy falls in the tenth. When Odysseus tells the story the count is nearly complete, for the army is in its ninth year (2.134, 2.295). See [[numbers-and-counting]] and [[the-portent-at-aulis]].
