summary: The huts of Book 2 are the camp the army pours out of, “from the ships and huts”; the king's quarters, where the Dream finds him asleep; and, in Thersites' taunt, storehouses full of bronze and women.
grc: [κλισιάων, κλισίαι]
passages:
  - 2.225-234 | What do you lack?
---

Zeus sends the Dream “into the hut of Agamemnon”, and it finds him “sleeping in his hut” (2.9, 2.19; see [[agamemnons-dream]]). Three times the army pours out of the camp “from the ships and huts” (νεῶν ἄπο καὶ κλισιάων), each time with a simile: to the assembly like swarming bees (2.87–91), back to the assembly like surf on the beach (2.207–210), and out onto the plain like birds on the Asian meadow (2.459–465). See [[swarming-bees]], [[surf-on-the-beach]] and [[birds-on-the-asian-meadow]].

In Thersites' abuse the huts are the king's storehouses: “Your huts are full of bronze, and there are many women in your huts, the choicest, whom we Achaeans give to you first of all, whenever we take a citadel” (2.226–228; see [[captive-women]] and [[division-of-spoils]]).

{{quote:23e54e06-f664-5473-95ff-d45cf6f3b1aa}}

After the assembly the men “made fires in the huts and took their meal” (2.399). At the camp of Achilles, while the Myrmidons amuse themselves by the sea, “the chariots of their masters lay well covered in the huts” (2.777–778): the war gear of men who do not fight (see [[achilles-withdraws]]).
