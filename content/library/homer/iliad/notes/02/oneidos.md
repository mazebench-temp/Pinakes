In Book 1 Athena allowed Achilles to reproach Agamemnon with words instead of the sword (1.211). In Book 2 Thersites uses the same freedom and learns its limits. He screams “shrill insults” (ὀξέα … ὀνείδεα, 2.222) at Agamemnon, in words largely taken from Achilles (see [[thersites]]).

{{quote:b9a63d7d-6b35-50db-b7d3-f763b62f49c9}}

Odysseus tells him not to have the kings in his mouth, “casting reproaches at them” (ὀνείδεά τε προφέροις, 2.251), and charges that he sits “hurling reproaches” (ὀνειδίζων) at Agamemnon because the Danaans give him so much (2.255). Then he answers words with the scepter (2.265–269). The crowd approves: Thersites will not again abuse the kings “with abusive words” (ὀνειδείοις ἐπέεσσιν, 2.277), the phrase Zeus used of Hera's provocations in Book 1 (1.519). See [[words-and-violence]], [[neikos]] and [[odysseus-and-thersites]].
