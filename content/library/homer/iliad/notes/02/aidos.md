{{quote:bf74ce06-bece-5327-bd43-c6de308885bb}}

Odysseus threatens to strip Thersites of his cloak and tunic, “which cover your nakedness” (τά τʼ αἰδῶ ἀμφικαλύπτει, 2.262), and send him howling back to the ships. Here αἰδώς is the body that shame covers, as when Priam pictures the dogs defiling the gray head, the gray beard and the αἰδώς of an old man killed (22.74–75). The threat is one of public shame, and the beating that follows ends with Thersites in tears and the army laughing (2.265–270; see [[odysseus-and-thersites]] and [[aischros]]).

In the Catalogue the adjective αἰδοῖος (“modest, revered”) describes Astyoche, “a modest maiden” (παρθένος αἰδοίη), who went up to the upper room where Ares lay with her in secret (2.513–515; see [[astyoche]]).
