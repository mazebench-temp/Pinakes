---
summary: In Book 2 the φρένες hold what a man keeps and knows, from the Dream's false message that Agamemnon must keep in his mind to Thersites' mind full of disorderly words and the army's memory of Aulis.
grc: [φρεσίν, φρονέοντʼ, ἀφραίνοντα]
en: [hearts, playing the fool]
passages:
  - 2.33-34 | The Dream's last words
---

Zeus ponders “in his mind” (κατὰ φρένα, 2.3) how to honor Achilles (see [[pondering-two-ways]]). The Dream ends its message with a charge to remember.

{{quote:242350e3-57d2-5ce1-8785-010d5f90a383}}

“Keep this in your mind” (σῇσιν ἔχε φρεσί): the φρένες hold a message against forgetfulness (λήθη) when sleep lets a man go. Agamemnon repeats the charge to the council (2.70), and he has kept it; but what he keeps is false. The Dream leaves him “pondering” (φρονέοντʼ) things that were not to be fulfilled (2.36).

The φρένες can be full of the wrong things. Thersites' mind “was full of words, many and disorderly” (ἔπεα φρεσὶν ᾗσιν ἄκοσμά τε πολλά τε ᾔδη, 2.213; see [[thersites]]), and he claims that there is no anger in Achilles' φρένες (2.241). Odysseus threatens him if he finds him “playing the fool” again (ἀφραίνοντα, 2.258): ἀφραίνω, “be senseless”, is formed from ἄφρων, “without φρένες”. Then he strikes him on the back, the μετάφρενον (2.265, 2.267), a word that means “what lies behind the φρένες” and keeps the old picture of an organ in the chest.

Odysseus reminds the army of the sign at Aulis: “this we know well in our hearts, and you are all witnesses” (ἴδμεν ἐνὶ φρεσίν, 2.301; see [[the-portent-at-aulis]]). Nestor in the council and Odysseus in the assembly both speak “with good intent” (ἐὺ φρονέων, 2.78, 2.283; see [[with-good-intent]]).
