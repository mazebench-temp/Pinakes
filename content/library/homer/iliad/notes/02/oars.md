---
summary: The oars of Book 2 are the fifty rowers in each of Philoctetes' seven ships, who were archers as well.
grc: [ἐρέται]
en: [rowers]
---

Philoctetes brought seven ships, “and in each went fifty rowers (ἐρέται), well skilled with the bow, to fight with might” (2.719–720). The rowers are the fighting men, and these fight as archers, like their leader, “well skilled with the bow” (2.718; see [[bow]] and [[philoctetes-on-lemnos]]). The Catalogue gives the number of men in a ship only twice: fifty here, and a hundred and twenty in each Boeotian ship (2.509–510). Thucydides took these as the largest and the smallest ships, and used Philoctetes' archers to show that the rowers were also the fighters (1.10.4; see [[thucydides-and-the-catalogue]] and [[numbers-and-counting]]).
