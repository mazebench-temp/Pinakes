**The scepter.** The history of Agamemnon's scepter is told as a chain of gifts. Each new owner is named first in the dative, as the one who receives it, and then, at the head of the next clause, in the nominative, as the one who gives it: Διί … / Ζεύς, Πέλοπι … / Πέλοψ, Ἀτρέϊ … / Ἀτρεύς, Θυέστῃ … / Θυέστʼ (Zeus, Pelops, Atreus, Thyestes; 2.102–107). Only Hermes changes his name on the way: he receives it as ἀργεϊφόντης (“the slayer of Argus”) and passes it on as Ἑρμείας. The chain ends with Agamemnon in the dative. He is the last to receive it, and he holds it as he speaks. See [[scepter]] and [[the-scepter-of-agamemnon]].

**Tribes and clans.** Nestor tells Agamemnon to separate the men “by tribes and by clans” (κατὰ φῦλα κατὰ φρήτρας), “so that clan may help clan, and tribe tribe” (ὡς φρήτρη φρήτρηφιν ἀρήγῃ, φῦλα δὲ φύλοις, 2.362–363). Each noun meets itself in another case, and the grammar says what the plan intends: every group fights beside its own kind. φρήτρηφιν has the old ending -φι(ν), used here as a dative. See [[phylon]].

{{quote:f16ae393-9e49-5b21-a212-5efe1cc9e567}}

**The most and the best.** In the Catalogue, the men who follow Agamemnon are “by far the most and the best” (πολὺ πλεῖστοι καὶ ἄριστοι / λαοί). A few lines later the same words come back in other cases and numbers for the king himself: he stood out “because he was the best, and led by far the most men” (ἄριστος … πλείστους … λαούς, 2.577–580). What is said of the army is said again of its leader. The same words describe Hector's Trojans (2.817–818), but they are not turned back on Hector.
