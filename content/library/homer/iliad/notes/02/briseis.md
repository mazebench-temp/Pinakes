Achilles lies idle "angry over the girl, Briseis of the lovely hair" (ἠϋκόμοιο; see [[lovely-haired]]). The narrator states the cause simply: in these lines the quarrel with Agamemnon is not mentioned, but the girl is.

The lines also give her past. Achilles took her "out of Lyrnessus after much toil" (πολλὰ μογήσας), when he sacked Lyrnessus and the walls of Thebe and killed the sons of Euenus (see [[the-sack-of-lyrnessus]]). The phrase confirms Achilles' own complaint in Book 1, that his prize was one "for which I toiled much" (ᾧ ἔπι πολλὰ μόγησα, 1.162). The narrator takes his side on the facts.

"For her sake he lay grieving" (τῆς ὅ γε κεῖτʼ ἀχέων): ἄχος, "grief, pain" (see [[achos]]), is the word for his loss, not only for his anger. Then the narrator looks ahead: "but soon he was to rise up" (2.694).

Briseis tells the rest herself in Book 19. Achilles killed her husband and her three brothers when he sacked the city "of divine Mynes", and Patroclus comforted her with the promise that Achilles would make her his wedded wife (19.291–299). [[Mynes]], whom Achilles killed at Lyrnessus (2.692), is often taken to be her husband, but Homer does not say so.
