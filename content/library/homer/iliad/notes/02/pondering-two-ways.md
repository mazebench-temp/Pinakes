---
summary: Book 2 opens with Zeus awake and pondering (2.3), in a one-sided form of the formula. His question is not whether, but how.
grc: [μερμήριζε κατὰ φρένα]
en: [was pondering]
---

{{quote:f27e722e-0793-5781-bd01-1ef76081bef8}}

The other gods and the men are asleep, but Zeus “was pondering in his mind” (μερμήριζε κατὰ φρένα, 2.3). The verb is the one used of Achilles' deliberation in Book 1 (μερμήριξεν, 1.189), but the thought is of another kind. Achilles was torn two ways (διάνδιχα), and the alternatives followed as a double question, “whether … or” (ἤ … ἦε, 1.190–192). Zeus is not divided. He has already promised Thetis what he will do, and he ponders only how (ὡς) he might honor Achilles and destroy many beside the ships of the Achaeans (2.3–4). These are the words in which Hera had charged him with his promise (1.558–559).

The resolution comes at once, in a verse that closes deliberations: “And this plan seemed to him in his heart the best” (ἥδε δέ οἱ κατὰ θυμὸν ἀρίστη φαίνετο βουλή, 2.5; see [[decision-scene]]). The plan is the Dream (see [[agamemnons-dream]] and [[plan-of-zeus]]). The same sequence opens Book 10 with Agamemnon in Zeus' place: the others sleep, he lies awake pondering, and the same verse gives his decision (10.1–4, 10.17). Hera's thought before she deceives Zeus is another “how”, with the same resolution: she pondered how (ὅππως) she might beguile his mind (14.159–161).

A few lines later the Dream leaves Agamemnon “pondering in his heart things that were not to be fulfilled” (τὰ φρονέοντʼ ἀνὰ θυμόν, 2.36). The god's night of thought ends in a plan; the king's ends in a delusion.
