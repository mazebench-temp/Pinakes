{{quote:569ff1e4-c3ab-59dc-8f9d-45eaacd2915f}}

τὸν ἀθάνατος τέκετο Ζεύς: "whom immortal Zeus begot". The relative must refer to Peirithous, not to his son, since the next line names the son's parents, Peirithous and [[Hippodameia]]. Book 14 confirms it: Zeus names among his loves "the wife of Ixion, who bore Peirithous, equal to the gods in counsel" (14.317–318). The man whom Nestor named first among the mightiest he had ever seen (1.262–263) is a son of Zeus, and his son [[Polypoetes]] has come to Troy.

Peirithous is the subject of all three verbs of the Centaur story: he took vengeance, drove the beasts from Pelion, and pushed them to the Aethices. See [[lapiths-drive-out-the-centaurs]].
