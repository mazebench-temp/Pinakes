{{quote:e7ee5ec4-5e3e-5deb-8295-b58b728f5a85}}

Hector is first named in Book 2 in Agamemnon's prayer before battle: to burn Priam's hall and to tear "the tunic of Hector" around his chest with bronze. Ἑκτόρεος, "Hector's", is an adjective formed from his name. The Achaean king aims at the Trojan leader, but Zeus does not grant the prayer (2.419–420).

{{quote:6b37cb01-e030-5a84-a6e6-90706c2d6c42}}

Iris speaks to Priam first but gives her orders to Hector, by name: "Hector, to you above all I give this charge." He is to see that each allied leader commands his own people.

{{quote:4563bb8b-5f2b-5d21-b4db-b519ba187ad9}}

He "did not fail to know the goddess's word", and acts at once. Priam's assembly, which Iris has just mocked for its endless words, is broken up; the gates open, and the army pours out.

{{quote:cb28467a-86c3-5c18-acc9-cbe0e89341ae}}

The Trojan catalogue begins with him. κορυθαίολος, "of the gleaming helmet" ([[of-the-gleaming-helmet]]), is his most frequent epithet. Πριαμίδης, "son of Priam", stands at the start of the next line, and the phrase that follows, "with him by far the most and the best men", is the one used of Agamemnon's own contingent in the Achaean catalogue, where Ἀτρεΐδης, "son of Atreus", stands in the same place (2.576–578). The two catalogues open their greatest contingents in the same words, and set the two commanders side by side.
