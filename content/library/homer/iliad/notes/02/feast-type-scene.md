---
summary: After Agamemnon's sacrifice the feast is told in the verses of Chryse up to the end of the eating; then, in place of wine, song and sleep, Nestor speaks and the army is summoned to war.
refs: [2.430-433]
---

The feast after Agamemnon's sacrifice is told in the three lines that told it at Chryse: the work ceased and the feast made ready, the equal feast, the desire for drink and food put away (2.430–432 = 1.467–469; see [[equal-feast]] and [[desire-for-drink-and-food]]). Then the pattern breaks off. At Chryse the next line brought the mixing bowls, and the day ended in song and sleep (1.470–476). Here it brings a speech:

{{quote:4321e784-3ed2-5d4f-87ef-890135c807d7}}

The line that ends the eating usually leads to what follows a meal: drink, talk, song. Here the talk is a call to stop talking, “let us not sit here talking any longer” (2.435), and what follows is war. There is no wine, no libation and no song. The heralds summon the army at once (2.441–444), and the feast of the kings leads straight into the marshaling of the army. Only the chief men share this feast (2.404–409); the army has eaten its meal in the huts (2.399; see [[feast]]).
