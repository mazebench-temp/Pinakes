summary: Heracles is present only through his descendants. His son Tlepolemus leads the Rhodians, and two grandsons, the sons of Thessalus, lead the men of Cos and the islands.

Book 2 names Heracles four times, and always as a father. [[Tlepolemus]] is Ἡρακλεΐδης, "son of Heracles" (2.653), and his mother [[Astyocheia]] was a woman Heracles brought home from [[Ephyra]] after sacking "many cities" (2.658–660). The feud that drives Tlepolemus overseas is a family quarrel among "the other sons and grandsons of the might of Heracles" (2.665–666), and its victim, [[Licymnius]], is Heracles' own uncle. The next contingent in the Catalogue is led by [[Pheidippus]] and [[Antiphus]], "the two sons of lord [[Thessalus]], son of Heracles" (2.679).

So the Catalogue sets a clan of Heraclids in the southeastern Aegean, on Rhodes, Cos and the islands around them. In historical times these were Dorian islands, and Dorian kings claimed descent from Heracles. Many scholars see in these entries a reflection of that later world; others point to the Mycenaean settlements on Rhodes and Cos. See [[the-flight-of-tlepolemus]].

Two other places in the Catalogue belong to his later legend, though Homer does not connect them with him here: [[Oechalia]], the city of Eurytus (2.596, 2.730), which he sacked for the sake of Eurytus' daughter Iole, and [[Trachis]] (2.682), his last home.
