---
summary: When the army rushes to the ships to sail home, the men begin to pull the props from under the hulls, the last step before a launch.
passages:
  - 2.149-154 | The rush to the ships
---

When Agamemnon's test sends the army running for home, the men call to one another to lay hold of the ships and drag them to the sea, clear out the launching-trenches, and “they began to pull the props from under the ships” (ὑπὸ δʼ ᾕρεον ἕρματα νηῶν, 2.154). With the props gone, a beached ship can be hauled down to the water. The imperfect ᾕρεον (“they were taking away”) leaves the work unfinished: at that moment Hera speaks to Athena, and the homecoming “against fate” does not happen (2.155–156; see [[ships]] and [[the-test-of-the-army]]).
