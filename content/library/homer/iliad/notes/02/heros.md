---
summary: Book 2 calls the whole army “Danaan heroes”, twice sets Agamemnon above the heroes, and gives the title to single men on both sides, among them Protesilaus, who is already dead.
grc: [ἥρωες, ἡρώεσσιν]
---

**The army.** Agamemnon opens his test with ὦ φίλοι ἥρωες Δαναοὶ θεράποντες Ἄρηος, “Friends, Danaan heroes, attendants of Ares” (2.110). The line is a formula for addressing the army. Nestor and Ajax use it in battle (6.67, 15.733), and Agamemnon again when he makes his peace with Achilles (19.78). Here it opens a speech that tells the heroes to run home. Odysseus names them the same way, as the men who give Agamemnon so much, against the one man who reviles him (ἥρωες Δαναοί, 2.256).

**The first among them.** Twice the book sets Agamemnon above the heroes. At the end of the similes of the muster, Zeus makes him “conspicuous among many and preeminent among heroes” (ἔξοχον ἡρώεσσιν, 2.483). In the Catalogue he “stood out among all the heroes, because he was the best, and led by far the most men” (2.579–580; see [[aristos]]). The king whom a lying dream deceived in the night is, by the time the army marches, the first man in it.

**Single men.** The title also goes to single fighters. Protesilaus is “the warlike hero” (ἥρως Πρωτεσίλαος ἀρήϊος, 2.708), the first Achaean to leap ashore at Troy and the first to be killed. The Thracian Peirous, an ally of Troy, is “the hero Peirous” (Πείροος ἥρως, 2.844). Protesilaus is already dead when the Catalogue names him, as the first heroes of the poem were dead ones (1.3–4).
