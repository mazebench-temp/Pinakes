Book 1 named the spear once, in a threat. Book 2 brings in Homer's other words for it. In Agamemnon's orders before battle, each man is to “sharpen his spear well” (εὖ μέν τις δόρυ θηξάσθω, 2.382), and “his hand will grow weary on his spear” (περὶ δʼ ἔγχεϊ χεῖρα καμεῖται, 2.389). δόρυ and ἔγχος stand a few lines apart for the same weapon.

{{quote:b905eac7-92ec-5d74-a6cb-4f92cb4fac83}}

In the Catalogue the spear measures a man. The lesser Ajax, son of Oileus, is small and wears a linen corselet, “but with the spear he surpassed all the Panhellenes and Achaeans” (ἐγχείῃ δʼ ἐκέκαστο, 2.530; see [[ajax-son-of-oileus]]). The Abantes of Euboea come as “spearmen, eager with outstretched ash spears” (μελίῃσι, 2.543). μελίη is the ash tree, and so a spear with a shaft of ash.

{{quote:24a7e911-4372-5505-98fd-4d3980647084}}

The Trojans too arm “eager with their spears” (μεμαότες ἐγχείῃσι, 2.818). Several epithets of Book 2 name men by the spear. Idomeneus and Tlepolemus are “famed for his spear” (δουρὶ κλυτός, 2.645, 2.650, 2.657; [[famed-for-his-spear]]), the Trojan allies “spear-brandishing” (ἐγχέσπαλοι, 2.131; [[spear-brandishing]]), Mynes and Epistrophus and the Pelasgians “fighters with the spear” (ἐγχεσίμωροι, 2.692, 2.840; [[fighters-with-the-spear]]), and the Abantes and the Cicones “spearmen” (αἰχμηταί, 2.543, 2.846; [[spearmen]]).

Achilles' idle Myrmidons throw the javelin (αἰγανέη, a light spear for throwing) for sport, with the discus and the bow (2.774; see [[discus]]). And in Agamemnon's test speech the word keeps its old sense: the “timbers” of the ships have rotted (δοῦρα, 2.135; see [[ships]]).
