The cupbearer appears only in a figure of speech. If the two armies swore a truce and were counted, the Achaeans in companies of ten with a Trojan to pour each company's wine, “many a ten would go without a wine-pourer” (οἰνοχόος). The figure makes the Trojans the servants at an Achaean feast, the task young men do at a real one (1.470–471), and it makes a count concrete: the Achaeans outnumber the Trojans of the city more than ten to one. But the Trojans have allies from many cities (2.130–133). See [[feast]] and [[numbers-and-counting]].
