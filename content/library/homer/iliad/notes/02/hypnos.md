**Zeus awake.** Book 1 ended with Zeus going to bed beside Hera (1.609–611). Book 2 begins with everyone asleep but him. The other gods and men “slept all night long” (εὗδον παννύχιοι), “but sweet sleep did not hold Zeus” (Δία δʼ οὐκ ἔχε νήδυμος ὕπνος). He lies pondering how to honor Achilles, and the plan that seems best is the Dream. On Aristotle's text of the first line, see [[all-the-gods-slept]].

{{quote:f27e722e-0793-5781-bd01-1ef76081bef8}}

**The sleeper.** The Dream finds Agamemnon “sleeping in his hut, and ambrosial sleep was poured around him” (εὕδοντʼ … περὶ δʼ ἀμβρόσιος κέχυθʼ ὕπνος, 2.19). Its first word is a reproach, εὕδεις, “you are asleep”: a man of counsel, with a people in his care, should not sleep the whole night through (2.23–25; see [[a-man-of-counsel-should-not-sleep]]). The god who plans lies awake; the king who ought to plan is asleep, and so he can be deceived. Other dream-figures open with the same word: the ghost of Patroclus speaking to Achilles (23.69), and the phantom Athena sends to Penelope (Odyssey 4.804). See [[dream-scene]].

{{quote:778f87ad-7b57-521e-aef3-1117ebe920b5}}

**Sleep lets him go.** Sleep holds a man, as it did not hold Zeus, and lets him go. The Dream tells Agamemnon to remember its words “when honey-sweet sleep lets you go” (εὖτʼ ἄν σε μελίφρων ὕπνος ἀνήῃ, 2.34). “He woke from his sleep” (ἔγρετο δʼ ἐξ ὕπνου) with the divine voice “poured around him” (ἀμφέχυτʼ, 2.41), as sleep had been poured around him before. In the council he says that the Dream came to him ἐνύπνιον, “in my sleep”, and flew away when “sweet sleep let me go” (2.56, 2.71).

Sleep has a different epithet each time it is given one: νήδυμος (“sweet”, 2.2), ἀμβρόσιος (“ambrosial”, 2.19), μελίφρων (“honey-sweet”, 2.34), γλυκύς (“sweet”, 2.71). See [[sweet-sleep]], [[ambrosial]] and [[honey-sweet]]. With the dawn at 2.48 the first day of battle begins, and it lasts until Book 7. Sleep is not named again in Book 2.
