---
summary: Book 2 uses the epithet twice, both times in comparisons: Agamemnon looks like Zeus, and the earth groans under the army as it groans under Zeus in anger.
---

Both uses are in the dative, Διὶ τερπικεραύνῳ, and both measure men against the god. As the leaders marshal the army, Zeus makes Agamemnon stand out, “in eyes and head like Zeus who delights in thunder, in waist like Ares, and in chest like Poseidon” ({{ref:Iliad 2.478-479}}; [[like-zeus-ares-and-poseidon]]). And as the army marches out, the earth groans under their feet:

{{quote:07c77e5c-9453-5206-9ae0-9537e78b2b79}}

Here the thunder is present in the comparison. Zeus lashes the earth with his bolts around the monster Typhoeus ([[zeus-and-typhoeus]], [[the-earth-groans-as-for-typhoeus]]), and the march of the Achaeans shakes the ground like his anger.
