**Summons.** At dawn Agamemnon has the heralds summon the long-haired Achaeans (see [[heralds]]).

{{quote:2e65d0a3-c87c-5f94-83e7-d14332bd53d0}}

**A council first.** Before the assembly meets, Agamemnon seats a council of the elders at Nestor's ship (2.53–55). It follows the assembly's procedure on a smaller scale. Agamemnon speaks and sits, Nestor stands up (2.76; [[sat-down-stood-up]]), and when he has spoken he leads the way out, and the scepter-bearing kings rise after him (2.84–86). See [[boule]] and [[elders]].

**Gathering.** In Book 1 a single line gathered the assembly (1.57; [[when-they-had-gathered]]). Here the gathering takes fifteen lines: the bees, Rumor, the uproar, the groaning earth, and nine heralds shouting before the people sit (2.86–100; see [[swarming-bees]]).

{{quote:9af04649-5dbb-5633-8a14-d186df2b660a}}

**The speaker.** Agamemnon “stood up, holding the scepter”, and the history of the scepter fills the pause before he speaks, “leaning on it” (2.100–109; see [[the-scepter-of-agamemnon]]). Odysseus rises in the same words later (ἀνὰ … ἔστη σκῆπτρον ἔχων, 2.278–279).

**Dissolution by flight.** No one dissolves this assembly. After the king's speech it is “stirred” like the sea and like a field of grain, and the men run for the ships (2.142–154). The formula “they dissolved the assembly” (λῦσαν δʼ ἀγορήν, 1.305) never comes. The assembly has to be gathered a second time: Odysseus drives the men back, and they sit and are held in their places, in the words that seated them the first time (2.211, echoing 2.99).

**Out of order.** Thersites speaks without the scepter and “against all order” (2.214), and the scepter comes down on his back (2.265–268; see [[odysseus-and-thersites]]). Then the procedure resumes. Odysseus rises with the scepter, Athena in a herald's likeness calls for silence (2.278–282), and Nestor and Agamemnon speak in turn (2.336–393).

**Dismissal.** Agamemnon ends by sending the men to their meal, and they “stood up and hurried off, scattering among the ships” (ἀνστάντες … κεδασθέντες, 2.398). Later the summons of the morning is repeated with πόλεμον (“war”) in place of ἀγορήν (2.443, echoing 2.51): the next gathering is for battle.

**The Trojan assembly.** At Troy an assembly is already in session “at Priam's doors, all gathered together” (ὁμηγερέες, the word of the gathering line), when Iris arrives (2.788–790). Here the formula comes: Hector “at once he broke up the assembly” (αἶψα δʼ ἔλυσʼ ἀγορήν, 2.808). The Achaean assembly broke up in panic at a king's word; the Trojan one is dissolved in good order at a goddess's.
