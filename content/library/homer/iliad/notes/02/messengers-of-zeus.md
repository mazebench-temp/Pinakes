In Book 1 the heralds were messengers “of Zeus and of men.” In Book 2 the messengers of Zeus are divine.

The Dream introduces itself with the words “I am a messenger to you from Zeus” (Διὸς δέ τοι ἄγγελός εἰμι, {{ref:Iliad 2.26}}), and Agamemnon repeats them to the council ({{ref:Iliad 2.63}}). The claim is true, and the message it brings is false ([[dream]], [[baneful]]).

As the army streams to the assembly, Rumor goes among them:

{{quote:5bad5cca-13a1-53f0-b4e4-a77cbd254402}}

Ὄσσα is the voice that spreads without a speaker, and she too is called Διὸς ἄγγελος ([[ossa]]).

At the end of the book Iris comes to the Trojans as “a messenger from aegis-bearing Zeus, with grievous news” ({{ref:Iliad 2.786-787}}; [[iris]], [[wind-footed]]). Her message is true: the Achaeans are coming. The book that began with Zeus' lie to the Achaeans ends with his truthful warning to the Trojans.

The human heralds still do their work in Book 2, summoning the army twice ([[clear-voiced]]).
