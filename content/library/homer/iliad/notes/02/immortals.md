---
summary: Book 2 uses the word eight times: in Zeus' false message, of the gods who receive sacrifice, of the aegis, of Zeus himself, and for the gods' own name of a mound at Troy.
---

Three uses repeat a lie. Zeus' message says that “the immortals who have their homes on Olympus are no longer of two minds” ({{ref:Iliad 2.13-14}}), and the Dream and Agamemnon repeat it ({{ref:Iliad 2.31}}, {{ref:Iliad 2.68}}; [[homes-on-olympus]]).

The others show the range of the word. Dawn announces the light “to Zeus and the other immortals” ({{ref:Iliad 2.49}}); the army offered hecatombs “to the immortals” at Aulis ({{ref:Iliad 2.306}}); Athena's aegis is “ageless and immortal” ({{ref:Iliad 2.447}}); and in the Catalogue Zeus himself is “immortal Zeus” ({{ref:Iliad 2.741}}). The last use distinguishes the language of gods from that of men:

{{quote:a84a77ea-69ec-5a2d-a46d-865ef648689c}}

Men call the mound Batieia; the immortals call it the tomb of Myrine ([[language-of-gods-and-men]]).
