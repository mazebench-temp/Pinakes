---
summary: Book 2 uses the epithet three times, each time of ships beside which men wait, chafe or linger.
grc: [κορωνίσι]
---

The curved ships in Book 2 are the place where the army waits. Odysseus does not blame the Achaeans for “chafing beside the curved ships” after nine years ({{ref:Iliad 2.297}}). Agamemnon threatens death to any man he sees lingering “beside the curved ships” instead of fighting ({{ref:Iliad 2.392}}). And the Catalogue leaves Achilles among his “curved, seafaring ships,” nursing his wrath ({{ref:Iliad 2.771}}; [[seafaring-ship]]). In Book 1 Achilles threatened to sail home with his curved ships; in Book 2 they are where he sits still.
