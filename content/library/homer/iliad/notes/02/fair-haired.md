{{quote:bb48ee81-128e-54a9-832a-a7ddff93ab4d}}

The Aetolian entry explains why Thoas leads the contingent: the old king Oeneus and his sons are dead, and “fair-haired Meleager was dead.” The epithet of youth and beauty stands beside the verb of death (θάνε δὲ ξανθὸς Μελέαγρος). Meleager's story, of his anger and his withdrawal from battle, is told at length by Phoenix in Book 9 (Il. 9.529–599), as a warning to Achilles ([[the-death-of-meleager]]).

ξανθός is above all the standing epithet of Menelaus, ξανθὸς Μενέλαος, from Book 3 on.
