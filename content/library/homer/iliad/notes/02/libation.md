{{quote:008866c5-f953-520f-b78f-646b2ecc723a}}

Nestor names the pledges that bound the Achaeans to the expedition: “our compacts and our oaths” (2.339), and “the libations of unmixed wine and the right hands in which we trusted” (2.341). Since the army acts as if these meant nothing, he says bitterly, they may as well go “into the fire” (2.340). σπονδαί, “libations”, from σπένδω, “pour a libation”, is a word the poem has not used before (see [[oath]]). They are ἄκρητοι, “unmixed”: neat wine, not the mixture of wine and water that men drank. With the handshake, the libation made a promise visible. The same line returns when Pandarus' arrow breaks the truce sworn in Book 3 (4.159).

No libation is actually poured in Book 2. At Agamemnon's sacrifice no wine is poured over the burning thigh-pieces, and the feast of the kings ends without the drink-offering of Chryse (1.462–463, 1.470–471; see [[sacrifice-type-scene]]).
