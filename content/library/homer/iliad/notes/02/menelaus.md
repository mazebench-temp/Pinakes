**Unbidden.** Agamemnon calls six of the elders to his sacrifice. Menelaus comes without being called:

{{quote:523fef7e-1e66-53ec-8643-dab33e11e5f5}}

αὐτόματος (“of his own accord”): he knows without being told how hard his brother is pressed. In Plato's *Symposium* Socrates remembers the line when he brings a friend uninvited to a feast. See [[plato-menelaus-uninvited]].

**The Catalogue.** Menelaus leads sixty ships from hollow Lacedaemon and its towns, Sparta, Amyclae, Helos and the rest (2.581–586). His men arm “apart” (ἀπάτερθε) from Agamemnon's, and he goes among them “trusting in his own eagerness”, longing most of all “to pay them back for the struggles and groans for Helen's sake” (2.587–590). These are the words Nestor used to the army (2.356). The brothers' kingdoms stand side by side in the Catalogue, but this is Menelaus' own war. See [[helen]], [[war-for-menelaus]] and [[lacedaemon]].

βοὴν ἀγαθός (“good at the war cry”, 2.408, 2.586) is his standing epithet, which he shares with Diomedes. See [[good-at-the-war-cry]].

**The sons of Atreus.** Odysseus speaks of all “who came with the sons of Atreus to Ilios” (2.249), and the poet asks the Muse about the horses “who followed the sons of Atreus” (2.762). See [[atreidae]].
