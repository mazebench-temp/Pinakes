{{quote:f27e722e-0793-5781-bd01-1ef76081bef8}}

The scene has the frame of a deliberation without its middle. Zeus “was pondering in his mind” (μερμήριζε κατὰ φρένα; [[pondering-two-ways]]), but not whether to act. He has promised Thetis to honor Achilles (1.524–527), and he is pondering how. No alternatives are set out, and the resolution follows at once: “And this plan seemed to him in his heart the best” (ἥδε δέ οἱ κατὰ θυμὸν ἀρίστη φαίνετο βουλή, 2.5). The line is a formula of decision. Agamemnon, lying awake in Book 10 while the others sleep, decides in the same words to go to Nestor (10.1–4, 10.17), and Hera, pondering how to deceive Zeus, chooses her plan with it (14.159–161).

Achilles' decision in Book 1 was all alternatives and no resolution: Athena's arrival cut it off (1.188–194). Zeus' decision at the opening of Book 2 is all resolution. The plan he chooses is the first act of the [[plan-of-zeus]], and its instrument is a deception, the [[dream|Dream]] (see [[agamemnons-dream]]).
