The council breaks up, the kings rise, and “the people came streaming on” (ἐπεσσεύοντο δὲ λαοί, 2.86). Then comes the first simile of the book. The bees are ἔθνεα μελισσάων ἁδινάων, “the tribes of thronging bees”: ἔθνος (“tribe, swarm, band”) is the word the application repeats for the men (ἔθνεα πολλά, 2.91), and ἁδινός (“thronging, dense”) returns with the flies (2.469; see [[flies]]). What the simile shows is a stream that does not stop: bees always coming on anew from the hollow rock (αἰεὶ νέον ἐρχομενάων), flying in clusters over the spring flowers (βοτρυδόν, “like bunches of grapes”, from βότρυς), some here and some there. So the men come from the ships and huts to the assembly, crowd after crowd (ἰλαδόν; see [[swarming-bees]]).

The bees open a pattern. When the army comes out again for battle, a run of similes gives its numbers in the same words, “the many tribes” of birds and of flies (2.459, 2.469), and the spring of the bees returns in the spring of the flies (2.471; see [[numbers-and-counting]]).
