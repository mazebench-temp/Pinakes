**The ox for Zeus.** Before the battle each man sacrifices to one god or another, praying to escape death. Agamemnon sacrifices an ox to Zeus, “a fat one, five years old” (βοῦν … πίονα πενταέτηρον), and calls the chief men of the army to it.

{{quote:86067180-92bd-5759-ba87-c71a5f01e797}}

A full-grown, fat beast is a choice victim, and its fat is what wraps the god's portion (2.423). Nearly the same two lines, with “male” (ἄρσενα) in place of “fat”, tell of his sacrifice to Zeus after the duel of Ajax and Hector (7.314–315). The chiefs stand around the ox (βοῦν δὲ περιστήσαντο) and take up the barley grains, and the rite follows the pattern of Book 1 (2.410–431; see [[sacrifice-type-scene]]). Zeus accepts the offering but not the prayer (2.419–420).

**A hundred oxen.** The aegis that Athena carries through the army has a hundred golden tassels, “each worth a hundred oxen” (ἑκατόμβοιος, 2.449; see [[aegis]]). Cattle are the unit of value, and the adjective is formed like the name of the [[hecatomb|hecatomb]], from ἑκατόν (“a hundred”) and βοῦς. Homer uses it again of Glaucus' golden armor (6.236).

**The herd.** In the last simile before the Catalogue, the gathered army is a herd of cattle (βόεσσι … ἀγρομένῃσι) and Agamemnon the bull that stands out above it (2.480–483; see [[bulls]] and [[bull-above-the-herd]]).
