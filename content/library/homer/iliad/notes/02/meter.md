**The sparrow.** In the sign at Aulis that Odysseus recalls, a serpent darts from beneath the altar and devours eight nestlings, “and the mother who bore them was the ninth” (2.313). She flutters around them, “lamenting her dear young” (ὀδυρομένη φίλα τέκνα, 2.315), until he coils and catches her by the wing. When Calchas reads the sign he repeats the line (2.327): the nine birds are nine years of war, and the mother makes the ninth. See [[sparrow]], [[serpent]], [[the-portent-at-aulis]] and [[numbers-and-counting]].

**Mothers in the Catalogue.** The Catalogue names many mothers, but with the verb τίκτω, “bear”, not the noun. Astyoche, Astyocheia, Alcestis, Rhene, Hippodameia and Aphrodite bore leaders of the two armies (2.513, 2.658, 2.714, 2.728, 2.742, 2.820); the earth bore Erechtheus (2.548), and the Gygaean lake the two leaders of the Maeonians (2.865). The one μήτηρ in the Catalogue is a town: Iton, “mother of flocks” (μητέρα μήλων, 2.696), a land that breeds sheep, as Ida is “mother of wild beasts” (μητέρα θηρῶν, Iliad 8.47, 14.283). The same line begins with the precinct of Demeter, whose name is usually taken to end in the word for mother; the first part of the name is disputed. See [[iton]].
