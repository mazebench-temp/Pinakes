---
summary: The one supplication in Book 2 is invented: the Dream's message claims that Hera “with her pleading” has bent all the gods to the Achaean side.
---

The word occurs in one line, said three times: “she has bent them all, Hera, with her pleading (λισσομένη), and sorrows hang over the Trojans” (2.14–15). Zeus gives it to the Dream, the Dream repeats it to Agamemnon (2.31–32), and Agamemnon repeats it to the council (2.68–69; see [[message-repeated]]). It is part of a lie. Zeus at least has not been won over, and he plans the opposite of what the Dream announces (2.3–4, 2.38–40; see [[dream]] and [[agamemnons-dream]]). The lie is believable because gods can be moved by entreaty, as Book 1 has just shown, when Zeus granted Thetis' supplication (1.500–530; see [[thetis-supplicates-zeus]]). The pleading the Dream reports is Hera's; the pleading that set the plan in motion was Thetis'. In the text Aristotle knew, the line ended differently (see [[aristotle-and-hippias]]).
