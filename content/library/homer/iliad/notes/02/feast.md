**The meal before battle.** Agamemnon closes the assembly with an order: “But now go to your meal (δεῖπνον), so that we may join in the clash of Ares” (2.381). The meal belongs to the preparation for war, with the spears sharpened, the shields made ready and the chariots looked over (2.382–384). δεῖπνον is the plain word for a meal, whenever it is taken, and the next lines use it even of the horses' feed (2.383). The men scatter to the huts, light their fires “and took their meal”, and each sacrifices to a god (2.398–401; see [[sacrifice]]).

**The feast of the kings.** At Agamemnon's sacrifice the chief men share the ox, and the feast is told in the verses of the feast at Chryse (2.430–432 = 1.467–469): the work done and the feast made ready, “no one's heart lacked an equal share”, the desire for drink and food put away. See [[feast-type-scene]] and [[equal-feast]]. Only the leaders eat together: the king, the six he called, and Menelaus (2.404–409). There is no wine and no song afterward. Nestor speaks, and the heralds summon the army to war (2.433–444).

**An imagined feast.** Agamemnon's test speech measures the two armies by a banquet. If Achaeans and Trojans made a truce and were counted, the Achaeans in companies of ten with a Trojan to pour each company's wine, “many a ten would go without a wine-pourer” (2.123–128). The Trojans would be too few even to serve. See [[cupbearing]] and [[numbers-and-counting]].
