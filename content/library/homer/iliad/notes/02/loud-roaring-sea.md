In Book 1 the roar of the sea filled the silence of Chryses. In Book 2 the sea's roar is the army's own noise. Odysseus has driven the men back from the ships, and they pour into the assembly “with a roar, as when a wave of the loud-roaring sea crashes on the long beach” ([[surf-on-the-beach]]). The simile answers the earlier one in which the assembly was stirred like the waves of the Icarian sea ([[waves-of-the-icarian-sea]]). Both times the crowd is the sea; the first time it moved toward the ships, the second time back to its seats.
