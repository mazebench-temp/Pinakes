{{quote:e9e44cde-e73c-57fb-9ec1-19f624604c58}}

1. **Setting and gesture.** Like Chryses' second prayer, this one is spoken at a sacrifice, with the barley grains in the worshipers' hands (2.410; 1.449–450). No gesture is mentioned. See [[sacrifice-type-scene]].
2. **Invocation.** There is no “hear me” ([[hear-me]]). Agamemnon opens with the god's name and four titles: κύδιστε, “most glorious” ([[most-glorious]]); μέγιστε, “greatest”; κελαινεφές, “lord of the dark clouds” ([[lord-of-dark-clouds]]); and αἰθέρι ναίων, “who dwell in the upper air” ([[dwelling-in-the-upper-air]]). He names no place.
3. **Claim.** There is none, and no “if ever” (see [[reciprocity-with-the-gods]]). The sacrifice under way must serve instead.
4. **Request.** There is no “fulfill for me this wish” ([[fulfill-this-wish]]). The wish is put in commanding infinitives with a time limit: the sun is not to set before Priam's hall is thrown down and burned, Hector's tunic torn, and his companions in the dust. The limit repeats the Dream's “now” (2.29) and Agamemnon's own “that very day” (2.37).
5. **Answer.** The closing line opens as in Book 1, ὣς ἔφατʼ, “so he spoke”, and then turns:

{{quote:dcc1fe5e-205b-5baf-b9fe-5a78e31a6144}}

Both of Chryses' prayers closed with the same line, “So he spoke in prayer, and Phoebus Apollo heard him” (1.43, 1.457). Here the god receives the offering and refuses the wish, and the narrator adds what Zeus does instead. The verb of refusal, ἐπεκραίαινε, is the verb of the request formula, κρήηνον, “fulfill” (1.41). Elsewhere an answer can be divided, as when Zeus grants one of Achilles' requests for Patroclus and refuses the other (16.249–250). Here only the offering is received. Apollo heard both prayers of his priest in Book 1; the king's prayer in Book 2 is refused.
