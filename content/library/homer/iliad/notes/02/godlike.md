---
summary: Book 2 adds θεοειδής, “godlike in form,” for two leaders of the Catalogues, Polyxeinus the Epeian and Ascanius the Phrygian.
grc: [θεοειδὴς]
---

θεοειδής = θεός (“god”) + εἶδος (“form, appearance”): “godlike in looks.” It praises beauty and bearing more than strength. In the Iliad it is the standing epithet of Paris, Ἀλέξανδρος θεοειδής, and in Book 24 of old Priam; in the Odyssey it is often given to Telemachus. The translation renders it, like θεοείκελος, “godlike.”

Book 2 uses it twice, both times at the end of a verse that names a leader. Polyxeinus commands the fourth division of the Epeians ({{ref:Iliad 2.623}}), and Ascanius leads the Phrygians from far-off Ascania with Phorcys ({{ref:Iliad 2.862}}). Neither is heard of again in the poem; the epithet gives each a moment of distinction in the list.
