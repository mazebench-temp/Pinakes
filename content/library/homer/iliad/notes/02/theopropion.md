Odysseus reminds the army of the portent at Aulis and of what Calchas said: “Calchas at once spoke out, prophesying” (θεοπροπέων ἀγόρευε, 2.322). In Book 1 Agamemnon used the same participle and verb with contempt: “now among the Danaans you prophesy and declare” (θεοπροπέων ἀγορεύεις, 1.109). Here the prophecy is remembered with respect, and its fulfillment is Odysseus' argument: “now it is all being fulfilled” (2.330).

{{quote:61cce681-3bc9-50a0-907b-080ef3797900}}

The Trojan Catalogue names two more men of prophecy, and neither is saved by his art. Merops of Percote, who knew the art of prophecy (μαντοσύνας) above all men, could not keep his sons from the war (2.831–834; see [[merops]]), and the birds of Ennomus the augur did not save him from death at Achilles' hands (2.858–861; see [[ennomus]]).
