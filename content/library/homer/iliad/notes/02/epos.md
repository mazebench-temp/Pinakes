**A test with words.** The first ἔπεα (“words”) in the book are the “winged words” with which Zeus addresses the Dream (2.7; see [[winged-words]]). Then Agamemnon tells the council that he will test the army “with words” (ἔπεσιν), as is the custom, and the elders are to hold the men back “with words” (ἐπέεσσιν). Words are to set off the flight, and words are to stop it. In the assembly he speaks his words leaning on the scepter (ἔπεʼ, 2.109). See [[peira]] and [[the-test-of-the-army]].

{{quote:5c269b4c-6904-58ac-9ae6-b48cdbc739b4}}

**Gentle words.** When the test goes wrong, Hera tells Athena to hold back every man “with your gentle words” (σοῖς ἀγανοῖς ἐπέεσσιν, 2.164). Athena gives Odysseus the order in the same line (2.180), and the narrator reports that he did so, ἀγανοῖς ἐπέεσσιν again (2.189). But the gentle words are for kings and men of note. A man of the people gets the scepter and a rebuke, and the word for that is μῦθος (2.199; see [[mythos]]). In Book 1 Hephaestus told his mother to approach Zeus “with gentle words” (ἐπέεσσι μαλακοῖσιν, 1.582).

**Disorderly words.** Thersites is defined by his words before he speaks. He is ἀμετροεπής, “of unmeasured words” (see [[of-unmeasured-words]]), and his mind is full of words “many and disorderly” (ἔπεα … ἄκοσμά τε πολλά τε).

{{quote:88ad2396-b31f-5d7f-9d58-56787e854433}}

After the beating the army calls him ἐπεσβόλος, “word-slinging” (2.275), a thrower of words (ἔπεα and βάλλω, “throw”), and hopes that he will not again abuse the kings “with abusive words” (ὀνειδείοις ἐπέεσσιν, 2.277). Zeus used the same phrase for the words he feared from Hera (1.519). See [[thersites]] and [[words-and-violence]].

**Quarreling with words.** Nestor complains that the Achaeans “go on quarreling like this with words” (ἐπέεσσʼ ἐριδαίνομεν, 2.342) and find no remedy. Then he gives his own counsel with a promise: “the word I speak will not be one to cast aside” (οὔ τοι ἀπόβλητον ἔπος, 2.361; see [[litotes]]). Agamemnon's answer ends in a confession. He and Achilles fought over a girl “with hostile words” (ἀντιβίοις ἐπέεσσιν), and he was the first to grow angry. Book 1 used nearly the same words for their quarrel (ἀντιβίοισι μαχεσσαμένω ἐπέεσσιν, 1.304).

{{quote:0c5f3124-263c-53cc-abb0-a656385f67c5}}

**The goddess's word.** When Iris speaks to the Trojans in the voice of Polites, “Hector did not fail to know the goddess's word” (οὔ τι θεᾶς ἔπος ἠγνοίησεν, 2.807), and at once he broke up the assembly. Odysseus, too, had known the voice of a goddess (2.182). See [[iris]].
