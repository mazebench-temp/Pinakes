Book 1 ended with Zeus nodding to Thetis. Book 2 recalls a nod given years before, to the Achaeans.

**Agamemnon's complaint.** In the test of the army Agamemnon calls Zeus “hard”, because he “once promised me and nodded his assent” (ὑπέσχετο καὶ κατένευσεν) that he would sack well-walled Ilios and then sail home, and now bids him go home in disgrace (2.112–115). He says it to deceive the army, and the complaint is truer than he knows: that night Zeus sent him a lying dream (see [[the-test-of-the-army]] and [[agamemnons-dream]]).

**Nestor's reply.** Nestor holds Zeus to the same nod: “I say that the son of Cronus, supreme in might, nodded his assent (κατανεῦσαι) on that day when they went aboard their swift-sailing ships”, and flashed his lightning on the right as a sign (2.350–353; see [[the-departure-from-aulis]]). No one should go home before they know whether the promise is a lie (2.348–349).

Both nods stand. The nod at the sailing promised that Troy would fall; the nod to Thetis, that the Achaeans would first be defeated (see [[plan-of-zeus]]). When Zeus accepts Agamemnon's sacrifice but not his prayer to take the city before sunset, he is keeping the second without breaking the first (2.419–420).
