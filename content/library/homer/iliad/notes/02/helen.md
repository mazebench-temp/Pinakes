**What the Achaeans would leave behind.** When the army runs for the ships, [[Hera]] puts the case against leaving to [[Athena]], and Athena repeats it to [[Odysseus]] in the same words (2.160–162, 2.176–178). If the Achaeans sail home, they will leave the Trojans “a thing to boast of” (εὐχωλή), Argive Helen, “for whose sake many of the Achaeans have perished in Troy, far from their dear native land”. The argument looks backward. The men already dead would have died for nothing, and the Trojans would keep the woman as a trophy. See [[argive-helen]].

**Payment in kind.** [[Nestor]] turns the war into a debt to be paid in the same coin.

{{quote:485361a5-ef0f-52ca-9e60-3af29173d4d1}}

Every man is to lie with a Trojan wife before he goes home, in return for Helen. In the Catalogue the same line describes what [[Menelaus]] longs for “most of all” (2.590). See [[war-for-menelaus]].

**Whose groans?** Ἑλένης ὁρμήματά τε στοναχάς τε can be read in two ways. If the genitive is subjective, these are Helen's own struggles and groans: she was taken against her will, and the Achaeans avenge her distress. If it is objective, they are the struggles and groans the Achaeans have suffered over Helen. The translation renders it “the struggles and groans for Helen's sake”. When Helen first appears in Book 3 she is weaving the struggles (ἀέθλους) that Trojans and Achaeans endured for her (3.125–128).
