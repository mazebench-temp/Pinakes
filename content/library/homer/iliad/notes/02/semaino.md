The problem Iris addresses is command in an army of many languages. Her answer is to divide authority by people. Each man is to σημαίνειν, to give the signal, to those he leads (οἷσί περ ἄρχει), and to marshal "his own countrymen" (πολιήτας). In Book 1 the verb expressed a claim to command everyone, Achilles' as Agamemnon saw it (1.289), and it was refused in the same word: "give your orders to others, not to me" (1.295–296). Here it is shared out among many leaders, each over his own. Nestor gives the Achaeans similar advice, to fight by tribes and clans (2.362–363).
