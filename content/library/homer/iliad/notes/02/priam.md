**The city of Priam.** In the Achaean camp Priam is a name for the goal of the war. Agamemnon wakes from the Dream believing that he will take "the city of Priam" that very day (2.37). Odysseus ends his speech with "until we take the great city of Priam" (2.332), and Agamemnon wishes for ten counselors like Nestor, for then "the city of lord Priam would soon bow its head" (2.373). Hera and Athena use his name for the shame of retreat: the Achaeans would "leave to Priam and the Trojans a thing to boast of, Argive Helen" (2.160–161, 2.176–177). At Aulis the fleet gathered "bringing evils to Priam and the Trojans" (2.304). And before the first battle Agamemnon prays to throw down Priam's hall:

{{quote:e7ee5ec4-5e3e-5deb-8295-b58b728f5a85}}

Zeus accepts the offering but not the prayer (2.419–420). The hall stands until the sack ([[sack-of-troy-foretold]]).

**At his doors.** Priam himself appears only at the end of the book. The Trojans are holding an assembly "at Priam's doors", young and old together (2.788–789), as they do again in Book 7 (7.345–346). It is the first scene inside Troy in the poem. Iris comes in the voice of his son [[Polites]] and speaks first to him:

{{quote:bead43ef-f2ab-581d-a787-282407a09bbc}}

"Endless words are always dear to you, as once in time of peace." μῦθοι ἄκριτοι are words without end or decision, and the rebuke is that of the young to the old. Priam does not answer. It is Hector who acts (2.807–808). In the next book Priam sits on the wall with the Trojan elders, too old to fight but "good speakers, like cicadas" (3.146–153), and there he speaks for the first time, to Helen.

Two of his sons appear in Book 2: Polites, the lookout whose voice Iris borrows (2.791), and Hector, Πριαμίδης, "son of Priam", who leads the Trojans out (2.817). See [[sons-of-priam]].
