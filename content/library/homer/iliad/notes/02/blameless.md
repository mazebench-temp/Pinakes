---
summary: Book 2 gives the epithet twice to Achilles, both times as the measure against which other men are set, and to Glaucus in the book's last lines.
grc: [ἀμύμονα]
---

The phrase ἀμύμονα Πηλεΐωνα, “the noble son of Peleus,” ends two verses of the Catalogue, and each time Achilles is the standard. Nireus of Syme was the most handsome man who came to Troy, “after the noble son of Peleus” ({{ref:Iliad 2.673-674}}). And when the Muse is asked who was best, the answer is Telamonian Ajax, “while Achilles kept up his wrath,” for Achilles was by far the strongest, and so were the horses “that carried the noble son of Peleus”:

{{quote:7a36300d-342c-59c6-9f8f-a6b668b1f744}}

Achilles is absent from the fighting, and the Catalogue still ranks everyone below him ([[best-of-the-achaeans]]).

The last use is in the last line but one of the book: “Sarpedon led the Lycians, and noble Glaucus” ({{ref:Iliad 2.876}}). As in Book 1, the translation renders the word “noble.”
