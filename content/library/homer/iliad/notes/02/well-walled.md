---
summary: Book 2 uses the epithet twice, in the same line, for the promise that the Achaeans would sack well-walled Ilios and go home.
---

The line Ἴλιον ἐκπέρσαντ’ εὐτείχεον ἀπονέεσθαι (“that I would sack well-walled Ilios and then sail home”) is spoken twice, and the promise changes hands. Agamemnon says it was Zeus who promised him this, and now deceives him ({{ref:Iliad 2.113}}). Odysseus says it was the army that promised it to Agamemnon, and now wants to break its word:

{{quote:6829645b-0fe0-5f11-82d9-60928be5bbb3}}

The walls in the epithet are the reason the promise is so hard to keep. Agamemnon uses the line again in Book 9, in a speech that repeats his words of Book 2, this time in earnest (Il. 9.20).
