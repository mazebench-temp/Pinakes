grc: [πέρσας, διαπορθήσας, ἐκπέρσαντʼ]

---

Book 2 mentions raids in passing, and each time for what they brought back. Thersites tells Agamemnon that his huts are full of bronze and of the choicest women, “whom we Achaeans give to you first of all, whenever we take a citadel” (2.226–228; see [[division-of-spoils]]). The Catalogue names two sackers of towns. Heracles brought [[astyocheia|Astyocheia]], the mother of Tlepolemus, from Ephyra, “after he had sacked (πέρσας) many cities of young men whom Zeus had nurtured” (2.658–660; see [[heracles]]). Achilles took Briseis out of Lyrnessus “when he sacked (διαπορθήσας) Lyrnessus and the walls of Thebe” (2.690–691), the campaign in which Thebe fell and Chryseis was taken (1.366–369; see [[the-sack-of-lyrnessus]], [[achilles-raids]] and [[sack-of-thebe]]). Both sackings yield a woman: Astyocheia bears Heracles a son, and Briseis becomes Achilles' prize (see [[captive-women]]).

As in Book 1, the same verbs name the goal of the whole war, the sack of Troy: Agamemnon says Zeus promised he would “sack well-walled Ilios” (ἐκπέρσαντʼ, 2.113, repeated by Odysseus at 2.288), and complains that the allies will not let him “sack the well-peopled citadel of Ilios” (ἐκπέρσαι, 2.133). The raids on the lesser towns are the war's past; the sack of the great one is its promised end (see [[sack-of-troy-foretold]]).
