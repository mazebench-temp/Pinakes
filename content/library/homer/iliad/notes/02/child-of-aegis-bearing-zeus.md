Hera's appeal to Athena opens with the whole line ὢ πόποι, αἰγιόχοιο Διὸς τέκος, Ἀτρυτώνη ({{ref:Iliad 2.157}}; [[atrytone]]), the same address she uses in Books 5 and 21.

The Muses are Zeus' daughters too, and Book 2 names them so twice. In the invocation before the Catalogue the poet can tell the army's numbers only if “the Olympian Muses, daughters of aegis-bearing Zeus” remind him ({{ref:Iliad 2.491-492}}; the phrase runs across the verse end, Διὸς αἰγιόχοιο / θυγατέρες). In the story of Thamyris the same title marks the goddesses he dared to challenge:

{{quote:6a1b3048-96a6-51f6-be66-100c76396d62}}

The father's epithet adds his authority to theirs. Thamyris boasted he would win “even if they themselves, the Muses, were to sing, the daughters of aegis-bearing Zeus,” and they maimed him and took away his song ([[thamyris-and-the-muses]]).
