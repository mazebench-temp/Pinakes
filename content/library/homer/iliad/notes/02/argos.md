Book 2 uses the name in three ways, and a fourth Argos lies in Thessaly.

## All Argos

{{quote:f17d6bc0-d5a9-5b11-9ab0-9d7245a2e0e9}}

The scepter passes from Thyestes to Agamemnon, "to be lord of many islands and of all Argos" (Ἄργεϊ παντί). Here Argos is the kingdom of the house of Atreus, the plain of Argos and more. Thucydides read the line as evidence of Agamemnon's sea power: a king on the mainland could rule many islands only with a fleet (1.9.4; see [[thucydides-and-the-catalogue]]). The Catalogue does not fit it. There Agamemnon's own contingent comes from [[Mycenae]] and the towns toward the Gulf of Corinth, the city of Argos belongs to Diomedes, and the islands to other leaders (see [[catalogue-and-mycenae]]).

## Going home

{{quote:539c222b-74c7-5d91-bbdd-a2b7f903c1c9}}

In his test of the army Agamemnon says that Zeus now bids him "go back to Argos in disgrace" (δυσκλέα, "without good report", from [[kleos|κλέος]], 2.115). Argos here is simply home, the land the army came from. Odysseus reminds the men of the promise they made "while they were still on their way here from horse-pasturing Argos" (ἀπ' Ἄργεος ἱπποβότοιο, 2.287; [[horse-pasturing]]), and Nestor curses anyone who would "go to Argos" before learning whether Zeus' promise is false (2.348).

## The city of Diomedes

{{quote:b4b62d85-007d-5e08-9601-21954889bfa3}}

In the Catalogue Argos is a city, the first of nine: [[Tiryns]], [[Hermione]], [[Asine]], [[Troezen]], [[Eionae]], [[Epidaurus]], [[Aegina]] and [[Mases]] follow. Their leaders are [[Diomedes]], [[Sthenelus]] and [[Euryalus]], sons and grandsons of the heroes who fought at Thebes. Their eighty ships equal the Cretans' and are fewer only than Agamemnon's hundred and Nestor's ninety. Agamemnon, lord of "all Argos" in the scepter passage, leads from Mycenae, two lines later (2.569).

A separate region, "Pelasgian Argos", opens the list of Achilles' lands (2.681; [[pelasgian-argos]]).
