---
summary: Book 2 uses κρείων Ἀγαμέμνων five times, at the turning points of Agamemnon's day, and once gives the title to Agapenor of Arcadia.
---

The formula follows Agamemnon through the book. Powerful Agamemnon stands up with the scepter to test the army ({{ref:Iliad 2.100}}); answers Nestor's advice, this time accepting it ({{ref:Iliad 2.369}}); prays to Zeus before battle ({{ref:Iliad 2.411}}); moves among the leaders as they marshal the army, marked out by Zeus:

{{quote:f4d7205e-a9c2-590b-97df-ff5395c7a1ba}}

and leads the hundred ships of Mycenae in the Catalogue ({{ref:Iliad 2.576}}). In Book 1 both uses of the formula introduced a refusal of advice; in Book 2, at 369, the same whole-line formula introduces his acceptance of Nestor's plan.

The title is not Agamemnon's alone. The Catalogue gives it to Agapenor, son of Ancaeus, who leads the Arcadians in ships Agamemnon lent him ({{ref:Iliad 2.609}}).
