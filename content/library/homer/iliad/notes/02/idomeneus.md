Agamemnon calls the elders to his sacrifice “Nestor first of all, and lord Idomeneus” (2.405). The order is one of age and honor: Idomeneus is among the older kings, and in Book 4 Agamemnon honors him above the other Danaans at the feast (4.257–263).

{{quote:48248bc9-f36a-5050-a76f-1920c2fbb93a}}

In the Catalogue he leads the Cretans of Cnossus, Gortyn, Lyctus, Miletus, Lycastus, Phaestus and Rhytium, “and others who lived in Crete of the hundred cities” (ἑκατόμπολιν, 2.649). The Odyssey gives Crete ninety cities (19.174). His eighty ships equal Diomedes' and are exceeded only by Agamemnon's hundred and Nestor's ninety. His companion [[Meriones]] shares the command, “the equal of Enyalius, slayer of men” (2.651). δουρὶ κλυτός (“famed for his spear”, 2.645, 2.650) is Idomeneus' epithet here.
