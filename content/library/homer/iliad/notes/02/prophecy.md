summary: In Book 2 prophecy is remembered, doubted and defeated. A dream from Zeus lies, Odysseus recalls Calchas' reading of the portent at Aulis as proof that Troy will fall, and the Trojan Catalogue names a seer and an augur whose art could not save them.

**A dream that lies.** Achilles had said that “a dream, too, comes from Zeus” (1.63). Book 2 puts the maxim to the test. Zeus sends Agamemnon a dream that promises Troy that day, and the narrator says at once that these were things “not to be fulfilled” (οὐ τελέεσθαι, 2.36; see [[a-dream-comes-from-zeus]] and [[onar]]). Nestor accepts it only because of who has seen it: from anyone else “we would call it a lie” (2.80–81; see [[pseudos]]).

**The seer remembered.** Calchas does not speak in Book 2, but Odysseus quotes him. At Aulis a serpent ate eight sparrow chicks and their mother, and Calchas, “prophesying” (θεοπροπέων, 2.322; see [[theopropion]]), read the sign.

The prophecy has the parts of every omen in the poem: a sign from Zeus (σῆμα, τέρας, πέλωρα; see [[teras]]), a seer who reads it, and a fulfillment still to come (see [[the-portent-at-aulis]]). Odysseus uses it as evidence. The army should stay “so that we may learn whether Calchas' prophecy is true or not” (ἢ ἐτεὸν Κάλχας μαντεύεται, 2.300), and the ninth year has come round: “now it is all being fulfilled” (2.330). In Book 1 Agamemnon called Calchas a prophet of evils (1.106–108). Here the same seer's prophecy is the army's reason to hope. Nestor adds a second sign from the same departure, Zeus' lightning on the right, “showing signs of good omen” (ἐναίσιμα σήματα, 2.353; see [[the-departure-from-aulis]]).

**The words of fulfillment.** The verb τελέω runs through Book 2 as it ran through Book 1. The Dream's promise will not be fulfilled (2.36). Odysseus' threat to Thersites “will surely come to pass” (τετελεσμένον ἔσται, 2.257; see [[it-will-be-fulfilled]]). The army will not fulfill (ἐκτελέουσιν) its promise to Agamemnon (2.286). Calchas' sign was “late in fulfillment” (ὀψιτέλεστον, 2.325), and it is being fulfilled now (τελεῖται, 2.330). The failure of fulfillment has its words too. After nine years “no end (τέλος) has yet come in sight”, and the work “lies unfinished” (ἀκράαντον, 2.122, 2.138). Zeus “was not yet going to fulfill” (ἐπεκραίαινε) Agamemnon's prayer (2.419). These last two words belong to κραιαίνω, “accomplish”, the verb of Chryses' prayer in Book 1 (1.41; see [[fulfill-this-wish]]).

**Seers who could not save themselves.** The Trojan Catalogue names two diviners. Merops of Percote “above all men knew the art of prophecy” (μαντοσύνας, 2.832), and would not let his sons go to war, but they did not obey him (see [[the-sons-of-merops]]). Ennomus the augur (οἰωνιστής) led the Mysians, “but his birds did not save him from black doom” (2.858–859). Prophecy in Book 2 can read the future. It cannot change it.
