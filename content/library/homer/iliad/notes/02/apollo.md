---
summary: Apollo is named three times in Book 2: in Agamemnon's wish for ten counselors like Nestor, as the god who reared Eumelus' mares, and as the giver of Pandarus' bow.
grc: [Ἄπολλον]
---

Agamemnon calls on “father Zeus and Athena and Apollo” in his wish for ten counselors like Nestor (2.371).

In the Catalogue, the best horses in the army are the mares of Eumelus, which “Apollo, god of the silver bow, had reared … in Pereia”, “carrying the panic of Ares” (2.763–767). Eumelus is the son of Admetus, and later poets told how Apollo once served Admetus as his herdsman (Euripides, *Alcestis* 1–9). See [[the-mares-of-eumelus]] and [[eumelus]].

In the Trojan Catalogue, Apollo himself gave Pandarus his bow (2.827). With it Pandarus will break the truce in Book 4 (4.104–126). See [[the-bow-of-pandarus]] and [[pandarus]].
