## Zeus' order, three times

Zeus gives the Dream five lines to deliver, and they are heard three times within sixty verses: from Zeus, from the Dream, and from Agamemnon quoting the Dream.

{{quote:0403172b-3f2d-5dc9-b9a0-1e713afaf334}}

The Dream keeps Zeus' words and changes only what delivery requires. “Bid him arm” (θωρῆξαί ἑ κέλευε) becomes “he bids you arm” (θωρῆξαί σε κέλευσε), and “he may take” the city (ἕλοι) becomes “you may take” (ἕλοις) (2.28–29). The next three lines are Zeus' own (2.30–32 = 2.13–15).

What the Dream adds is the frame. Before the message it rebukes the sleeper, names its sender, and claims for him a care that Zeus never expressed: “I am a messenger to you from Zeus, who, though he is far away, cares greatly for you and pities you” (2.26–27). At the end the sentence runs on by two words: the sorrows hang over the Trojans “from Zeus” (ἐκ Διός, 2.33). Then comes a close of its own, a warning not to forget when sleep lets him go (2.33–34). Zeus asked for exactness (2.10). The Dream is exact where it repeats, and persuasive where it adds.

{{quote:eb181036-fe48-5223-b1b1-19a767a1e8cb}}

Agamemnon, telling the council, quotes the Dream from its first word, and 2.60–69 repeat 2.23–32. He keeps “from Zeus. But keep this in your mind” (2.70), drops the warning against forgetting, and tells instead how the Dream flew away and sleep let him go (2.70–71; see [[retelling]]). Then he adds what no one told him to do: first he will test the army (2.72–75; see [[the-test-of-the-army]]). The message has passed from a god to a dream to a king. At each stage the words are kept and the frame changes, and the last speaker adds a plan of his own.

## Hera's words, twice

When the army runs for the ships, Hera tells Athena to go and stop it (2.157–165). Athena finds Odysseus and says nearly the same to him.

{{quote:b05f02b3-8f00-5cea-94cc-9b8ec6268ef9}}

What changes:

- **The address.** Hera's “Ah, child of aegis-bearing Zeus, Atrytone” (2.157) becomes “Son of Laertes, sprung from Zeus, Odysseus of many devices” (2.173), and the cry ὢ πόποι drops out (see [[o-popoi]]).
- **The person.** The Argives who “will flee, over the broad back of the sea” (2.159) become “you” who “will flee, tumbling into your many-benched ships” (2.175), and “they would leave” (λίποιεν, 2.160) becomes “you would leave” (λίποιτε, 2.176). With the person the picture changes, from the open sea to the scramble aboard.
- **The spur.** “Go now through the army of the bronze-armored Achaeans” (2.163) loses its epithet and gains an order, “and hang back no longer” (μηδʼ ἔτʼ ἐρώει, 2.179). Odysseus has been standing still in grief (2.169–171).

The rest is unchanged. “With your gentle words hold back every man” is said first to a goddess (2.164) and then to a man (2.180), and then it becomes a deed: Odysseus “would stand beside him and hold him back with gentle words” (ἀγανοῖς ἐπέεσσιν ἐρητύσασκε, 2.189). Neither messenger answers. Athena goes, and Odysseus runs (2.166–167, 2.182–183).
