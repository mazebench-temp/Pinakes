Both words for the cloak occur in Book 2. Agamemnon, dressing after the Dream, “threw his great cloak around him” (περὶ δὲ μέγα βάλλετο φᾶρος, 2.43): the φᾶρος, the large mantle of a king, over his soft new [[tunic]] (see [[dressing-scene]]).

Odysseus wears the woolen χλαῖνα. When Athena tells him to stop the flight to the ships, he knows the goddess's voice and runs, throwing the cloak off as he goes.

{{quote:41f3032b-4390-5ba9-91a7-1b60ce3beeec}}

A heavy woolen cloak would hamper a runner. His herald, [[eurybates-of-ithaca|Eurybates of Ithaca]], takes it up (κόμισσε, “took care of it”), and Odysseus goes on to Agamemnon and comes back with the king's [[scepter]] (2.185–187).

Soon after, Odysseus threatens [[Thersites]] with the loss of his: “your cloak and your tunic, which cover your nakedness” (χλαῖνάν τʼ ἠδὲ χιτῶνα, 2.262). Cloak and tunic are a man's whole dress, and to be stripped of them before the army would be shame itself (see [[aidos]] and [[odysseus-and-thersites]]).
