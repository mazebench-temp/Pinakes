---
summary: Achilles takes no part in the action of Book 2, and his formula does not occur; the speed of feet passes to Iris and to horses, and returns to him only at the end, as “the swift-footed grandson of Aeacus.”
grc: [πόδας ὠκέα, ποδώκεας, ποδώκεος, ὠκυπόδεσσιν]
---

The formula πόδας ὠκὺς Ἀχιλλεύς does not occur in Book 2. Achilles appears in the Catalogue only as the leader who lies idle ([[podarkes]], {{ref:Iliad 2.688}}). The book uses the family of words for swiftness of foot in other ways:

- πόδας ὠκέα, the feminine of the same phrase, for Iris, messenger of the gods: “swift-footed Iris spoke to them” ({{ref:Iliad 2.790}}, {{ref:Iliad 2.795}}). Her other formula is [[wind-footed]].
- ὠκύπους (“swift-footed”), the ordinary epithet of horses: Agamemnon orders each man to feed his swift-footed horses before battle ({{ref:Iliad 2.383}}).
- ποδώκης, the compound form of πόδας ὠκύς, for the best horses in the army, the mares of Eumelus, “swift-footed like birds”:

{{quote:3d3eb9d9-432c-5e1a-8b5b-b5977726e84c}}

The last two uses bring the epithet home to Achilles. In the Trojan Catalogue, twice in the same verse, Ennomus the augur and one of the Carian leaders are killed “at the hands of the swift-footed grandson of Aeacus in the river” ({{ref:Iliad 2.860}}, {{ref:Iliad 2.874}}; [[grandson-of-aeacus]]). While he sits out the war, his speed is spoken of only in the future, as the speed of the man who will kill.
