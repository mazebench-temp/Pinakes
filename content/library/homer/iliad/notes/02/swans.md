---
summary: Swans close the line of birds in the simile of the Asian meadow, “long-necked swans”, with the only epithet in the line.
passages:
  - 2.459-466 | Like birds on the Asian meadow
---

{{quote:d65fb152-00f9-5b63-bd8c-3537efdc1c0b}}

The swans come last of the three birds and take the line's only epithet, δουλιχοδείρων (“long-necked”), which fills its close. The flock is wild and mixed, gathered on the Asian meadow by the streams of the Cayster, the river that reaches the sea at Ephesus, and readers have often taken the scene as one the poet knew at first hand (see [[asian-meadow]], [[cayster]] and [[birds-on-the-asian-meadow]]). The line returns at 15.692, where an eagle swoops on the same flock (see [[geese]] and [[cranes]]).
