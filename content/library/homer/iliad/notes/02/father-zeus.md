summary: Book 2 names father Zeus once in a simile of the winds and once in Agamemnon's wish, where the invocation joins Athena and Apollo.

en: [father]
---

In the simile of the stirred assembly, the East and South Winds raise the long waves of the Icarian sea, “rushing down from the clouds of father Zeus” (πατρὸς Διὸς ἐκ νεφελάων, {{ref:Iliad 2.146}}; [[waves-of-the-icarian-sea]]). The genitive names the sky god as the source of the winds and the weather.

After Nestor's speech Agamemnon wishes for ten such counselors:

{{quote:1daba1f6-719c-507a-9675-42dda5c658eb}}

The wish formula αἲ γὰρ Ζεῦ τε πάτερ καὶ Ἀθηναίη καὶ Ἄπολλον (“if only, father Zeus and Athena and Apollo”) occurs four times in the Iliad and five in the Odyssey. It calls on Zeus and two of his children, and it is less a prayer than a sigh: it introduces a wish, usually for something that cannot happen.
