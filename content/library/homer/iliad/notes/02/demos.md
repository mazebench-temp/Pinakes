summary: Odysseus strikes with the scepter the “man of the people” he finds shouting, after speaking gently to the kings. In the Catalogue δῆμος is the land a people holds, the land of Erechtheus and the land of Apaesus.
passages:
  - 2.198-202 | The scepter for a man of the people
---

**A man of the people.** When the army runs for the ships, Odysseus deals with men in two ways. Whenever he came upon “a king or a man of note” (βασιλῆα καὶ ἔξοχον ἄνδρα, 2.188), he stood beside him and held him back with gentle words and an argument (2.188–197; see [[kings]]). The other kind of man got the scepter.

{{quote:3ff0ea06-6c45-5250-b491-d8e60acb746e}}

δήμου … ἄνδρα, “a man of the people”: the genitive places him in the community at large, below the kings and the ἔξοχοι, the men who “stand out”. Odysseus tells him what that means. He is to listen to “others who are better than you” (φέρτεροι, 2.201), and he is “never counted for anything in war or in council” (οὔτέ ποτʼ ἐν πολέμῳ ἐναρίθμιος οὔτʼ ἐνὶ βουλῇ, 2.202). He has no place in the council where the test was planned ([[boule]]), and no name in the fighting. He is struck, not persuaded. See [[commoners]].

Polydamas will use the same pair, council and war, when he complains that a man of the δῆμος may not speak against Hector (12.213–214).

Thersites, who speaks next, is never called a δήμου ἀνήρ. The poem gives him no father, no city and no rank. But Odysseus answers him as he answered the men of the people, with the scepter (2.265–269; see [[thersites]] and [[odysseus-and-thersites]]).

**The land of a people.** In the Catalogue δῆμος has its sense of territory. The Athenians held “Athens, the well-built citadel, the land of great-hearted Erechtheus” (δῆμον Ἐρεχθῆος μεγαλήτορος, 2.546–547): the citadel and the land around it, called after the earthborn king whom Athena raised (see [[athens]], [[erechtheus]] and [[athena-and-erechtheus]]). In the Trojan catalogue Adrestus and Amphius lead the men who “held Adrasteia and the land of Apaesus” (δῆμον Ἀπαισοῦ, 2.828; see [[apaesus]]). Both times the δῆμος is something a people holds (εἶχον).
