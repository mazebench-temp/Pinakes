---
summary: Book 2 has the full address that Achilles broke off in Book 1, spoken by Nestor, and the same superlative in Agamemnon's prayer to Zeus.
en: [most glorious]
---

Nestor addresses Agamemnon with the whole line, Ἀτρεΐδη κύδιστε, ἄναξ ἀνδρῶν Ἀγάμεμνον (“most glorious son of Atreus, Agamemnon, lord of men,” {{ref:Iliad 2.434}}; [[lord-of-men]]). In Book 1 Achilles began the same address and replaced its second half with an insult ({{ref:Iliad 1.122}}). Here it is complete, from the old man whose counsel Agamemnon has just accepted.

A little earlier Agamemnon has used the superlative of Zeus: Ζεῦ κύδιστε μέγιστε, “Zeus, most glorious, greatest” ({{ref:Iliad 2.412}}). The same opening begins the prayer at the oath-sacrifice in Book 3 (Il. 3.298). The king and the god share the word, and both uses come on the day when Zeus is deceiving the king.
