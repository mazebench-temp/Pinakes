---
summary: Book 2 gives the title to Agamemnon three times, to his father Atreus, and to Nestor. The narrator uses it as Thersites abuses the king and as Achilles nurses his wrath against him.
grc: [ποιμένι λαῶν]
---

Book 1 kept the title from Agamemnon; Book 2 gives it back. It passes through his line in the history of the scepter: Pelops gave the scepter “to Atreus, shepherd of the people” ({{ref:Iliad 2.105}}; [[the-scepter-of-agamemnon]]). Nestor has it as the kings rise from the council and follow him ({{ref:Iliad 2.85}}).

For Agamemnon it comes at the moments when his authority is challenged. The narrator names him “Agamemnon, shepherd of the people” as Thersites finishes abusing him ({{ref:Iliad 2.243}}), and Odysseus uses the same words as he rebukes Thersites for it ({{ref:Iliad 2.254}}). The last use is in the Catalogue, where Achilles lies by his ships:

{{quote:59741a90-1c33-5381-b0fe-b6be01b9bd7a}}

The title stands against the insult it answers in Book 1, “people-devouring king” ([[people-devouring-king]]). The quarrel over whether Agamemnon cares for his people or feeds on them runs through the formulas as well as the speeches.
