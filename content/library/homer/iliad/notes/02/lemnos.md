Λήμνῳ ἐν ἠγαθέῃ, "in sacred Lemnos" (ἠγάθεος, "very holy"; see [[egatheos]]). In Book 1 the same epithet belongs to Pylos (1.252), and the Iliad gives it to Lemnos again in Book 21 (21.58). Homer does not say why the island is holy; it was the island of Hephaestus, where he fell from heaven (1.593).

The Achaeans left [[Philoctetes]] there "in agony from the evil wound of a deadly water snake", and there he lay grieving. Lemnos lies on the way from Greece to Troy, and the Iliad remembers it as a stopping place. Agamemnon reminds the Achaeans of the boasts they made on Lemnos, as they ate the meat of cattle and drank bowls brimming with wine (8.229–234). Wine still comes to the camp from Lemnos (7.467–469), and Achilles sells a captured son of Priam there (21.40, 21.58).

In the Iliad the island is inhabited and friendly. Sophocles made it deserted, so that Philoctetes could live there alone for ten years (*Philoctetes* 1–2). See [[philoctetes-on-lemnos]].
