---
summary: Atreus is named in his own right for the first time, in the Dream's address to Agamemnon as “son of wise-hearted Atreus, tamer of horses”, and in the descent of the scepter, which Pelops gave to him and which he left at his death to Thyestes.
grc: [Ἀτρέος, Ἀτρέϊ, Ἀτρεύς]
---

{{quote:836e20ea-a0df-5a3c-b96c-dacb08847e3a}}

The Dream greets Agamemnon through his father, and Agamemnon repeats the line to the council (2.60). δαΐφρων (“wise-hearted”) and ἱππόδαμος (“tamer of horses”) are the only epithets Homer gives Atreus. See [[wise-hearted]] and [[tamer-of-horses]].

{{quote:778cced3-e225-5437-8786-4ee584223e25}}

In the line of the scepter Atreus receives it from [[Pelops]] and, at his death, leaves it to [[Thyestes]], who leaves it to Agamemnon. Homer says nothing of the feud between Atreus and Thyestes that tragedy made famous, and does not even call them brothers. The scepter passes in peace. See [[the-scepter-of-agamemnon]].

Book 2 also adds new forms of the patronymic: Ἀτρεΐδεω (2.185), Ἀτρεΐωνος (2.192), and the plural Ἀτρεΐδῃς and Ἀτρεΐδῃσιν, “the sons of Atreus” (2.249, 2.762). See [[son-of-atreus]] and [[atreidae]].
