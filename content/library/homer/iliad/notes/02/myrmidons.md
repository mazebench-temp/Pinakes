{{quote:273f86a4-b3cf-5f3b-9d04-2dc7f1ea0aec}}

Achilles' men "were called Myrmidons and Hellenes and Achaeans". Of the three names, Myrmidons is the one the poem uses for them everywhere else. [[Hellenes]] names the people of [[Hellas]], one region of the kingdom, and Achaeans is the name of the whole army.

The Catalogue lists them only to say that they are not fighting: "they gave no thought to grim-sounding war, for there was no one to lead them into the ranks" (2.686–687). Two other contingents whose leaders are missing find a substitute: [[Podarces]] leads the men of Protesilaus, and [[Medon]] those of Philoctetes (2.703–709, 2.726–728). The Myrmidons have none, because their leader is alive and chooses to stay away.

{{quote:59741a90-1c33-5381-b0fe-b6be01b9bd7a}}

The picture returns at the end of the Catalogue. While the rest of the army marches out, the Myrmidons pass the time on the shore with the discus, the javelin and the bow. Their horses stand by the chariots, munching lotus, a clover-like fodder plant, and marsh-grown parsley, and the chariots lie covered up in the huts. The men wander about the camp "longing for their leader, dear to Ares". Achilles swore in Book 1 that the Achaeans would long for him (1.240; see [[longing-for-achilles]]). Here the first to feel that longing are his own men.

The sports are the pastimes of peace. The line that names them, δίσκοισιν τέρποντο καὶ αἰγανέῃσιν ἱέντες, "amused themselves throwing the discus and the javelin" (2.774), recurs in the Odyssey of the suitors at play before Odysseus' house (4.626, 17.168). The Myrmidons will go into battle in Book 16, led by Patroclus in Achilles' armor.
