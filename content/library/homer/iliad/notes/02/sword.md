The sword is named once in Book 2. Dressing after the Dream, Agamemnon “around his shoulders ... slung his silver-studded sword” (ἀμφὶ δʼ ἄρʼ ὤμοισιν βάλετο ξίφος ἀργυρόηλον, 2.45; see [[silver-studded]]). The same verse is used when Paris, Patroclus and Achilles arm for battle (3.334, 16.135, 19.372), but here it stands among a soft tunic, a cloak and sandals, and Agamemnon takes up a scepter rather than a spear (see [[dressing-scene]]). A man wore his sword without armor too: Telemachus puts his on to go to the assembly of Ithaca (*Odyssey* 2.3).
