The line is made from two lines of Book 1. Its first half, ἀλλʼ ἔκ τοι ἐρέω (“but I will speak out to you”), is how Achilles began his forecast of Agamemnon's fall (1.204) and his great oath (1.233). Its second half is the strong form with which Athena corrected him (1.212). In Book 1 the formula announced what would come of Agamemnon's arrogance. Here it announces a threat, and what follows is a condition: if Odysseus finds Thersites playing the fool again, he will strip him and drive him howling from the assembly. He stakes his own head and his name on it (see [[oath-formulas]]). And the fulfillment does not wait for the condition: “So he spoke, and with the scepter he struck him” (2.265).

**The language of fulfillment.** As in Book 1, the verb τελέω (“fulfill”) runs through the book. The Dream leaves Agamemnon “pondering in his heart things that were not to be fulfilled” (οὐ τελέεσθαι, 2.36). Odysseus tells Agamemnon that the Achaeans “will not fulfill the promise” they made him on their way from Argos (οὐδέ τοι ἐκτελέουσιν ὑπόσχεσιν, 2.286). The sign at Aulis was “late in coming, late in fulfillment” (ὀψιτέλεστον, 2.325), “and now it is all being fulfilled” (τελεῖται, 2.330). Against these stands the one fulfillment the book withholds: Zeus “was not yet going to fulfill” Agamemnon's prayer (ἐπεκραίαινε, from another verb, κραιαίνω, 2.419).
