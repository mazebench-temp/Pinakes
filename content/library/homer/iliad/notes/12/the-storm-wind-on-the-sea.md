summary: As the fight at the wall begins, Hector “fought as before, like a storm wind” (12.40), in the half-verse of his onslaught in Book 11; the simile of the boar or lion among the hunters follows at once.
en: [like a storm wind]
---

αὐτὰρ ὅ γʼ ὡς τὸ πρόσθεν ἐμάρνατο ἶσος ἀέλλῃ: “and he fought as before, like a storm wind” (12.40). ὡς τὸ πρόσθεν, “as before”, looks back to the fighting of the same day, when Hector fell on the battle “like a blustering storm wind” that stirs up the violet sea (11.296–298). Here the half-verse stands alone, without the sea. It closes a picture of the Achaeans' fear: beaten down by the lash of Zeus, they are penned in beside their ships “in fear of Hector, the mighty master of rout” (12.37–39; see [[master-of-rout]]). A full simile follows, the boar or lion that turns among hounds and hunting men (12.41–50; see [[the-boar-or-lion-at-bay]] and [[hector]]). The wind returns at the end of the next book, when the Trojans come on behind Hector like a blast of grievous winds (ἀτάλαντοι ἀέλλῃ, 13.795).
