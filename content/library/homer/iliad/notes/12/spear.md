summary: Polypoetes' spear goes through Damasus' helmet and Leonteus' strikes Hippomachus at the belt; Sarpedon draws his out of Alcmaon and the dead man follows it; Ajax's is stopped by Sarpedon's shield; and Sarpedon and Hector each go into the attack with two spears (12.183–189, 12.298, 12.394–405, 12.464–465).
grc: [δούρεσσι, "δοῦρʼ ἔχεν"]
---

**Through the helmet, at the belt.** Polypoetes strikes Damasus “with his spear through the bronze-cheeked helmet”, and the bronze point breaks the bone (12.183–186; see [[helmet]] and [[damasus]]). Leonteus strikes Hippomachus with his spear at the belt, then draws his sword (12.188–190; see [[belt]] and [[sword]]). Asius' death “through the spear of Idomeneus” is foretold (12.116–117) and comes in Book 13, where the spear goes into his throat under the chin and through (13.387–388; see [[asius]] and [[idomeneus]]).

**Drawn out.** Sarpedon hits Alcmaon with his spear “and stabbed, and drew the spear out; and following the spear he fell / headlong, and around him his armor rang, inlaid with bronze” (12.394–396; see [[alcmaon]]). The man comes away with the weapon and falls forward on his face (πρηνής).

**Two spears.** Sarpedon goes against the wall holding his shield before him and “shaking two spears” (12.298), and Hector leaps through the broken gate with “two / spears” in his hands (12.464–465; see [[hector]]). Two spears are the arms of Paris when he challenges the Achaeans, of Agamemnon and of Patroclus when they arm (3.18, 11.43, 16.139; see [[arming-scene]]).

**Held off.** Against Sarpedon's shield the thrust fails: Ajax “leapt on him and stabbed his shield; but not right through / went the spear, and it drove him back as he pressed on” (12.404–405; see [[shield]] and [[sarpedon]]).

**At the battlements.** The Trojans climb onto the copings “holding their sharpened spears” (12.444; see [[battlements]]), the half-verse of the men who stand around Patroclus' body (17.412); across the battlements many are stabbed “with the pitiless bronze” (12.427). Hector's threat to Polydamas is a spear: if he holds back from the fighting or turns another man from the war, “at once, struck by my spear, you will lose your life” (12.250; see [[exhortation]]).

**In the similes.** The hunters “hurl thick and fast / their spears from their hands” at the boar or lion (12.44–45; see [[the-boar-or-lion-at-bay]]); the herdsmen guard the fold “with dogs and spears”, and the lion may be hit “by a javelin from a swift hand” (12.303–306; see [[the-mountain-lion-and-the-fold]]).

**Timbers.** At the start of the battle “the timbers of the towers rang / as they were struck” (δούρατα πύργων, 12.36–37): δόρυ in its first sense, wood, as the timbers of the ships have rotted in Book 2 (2.135; see the entry and the translation's note on 12.36).
