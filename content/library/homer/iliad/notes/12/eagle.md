summary: The eagle of Book 12 brings the portent at the ditch. Flying high on the left of the Trojans with a blood-red serpent in its talons, it is bitten, drops the serpent among them and flies off screaming, and Polydamas reads it as a warning not to attack the ships (12.200–229).

αἰετὸς ὑψιπέτης ἐπʼ ἀριστερὰ λαὸν ἐέργων: “an eagle flying high, on the left, skirting the army” (12.201, repeated by Polydamas at 12.219). ὑψιπέτης, “flying high”, is the eagle's epithet here, in Book 13 and in the Odyssey (13.822; *Odyssey* 20.243; see [[high-flying]]). The bird carries its prey in its talons, φέρων ὀνύχεσσι, as the eagle of Book 8 held a fawn in its talons (8.247–248), and the eagle of Telemachus' departure from Sparta carries a goose in the same words (χῆνα φέρων ὀνύχεσσι πέλωρον, *Odyssey* 15.161; δράκοντα φέρων ὀνύχεσσι πέλωρον, 12.202). It comes on the left, the side of ill omen; Hector's scorn names the two sides (12.238–240; see [[divination]]).

This eagle loses its prey. The serpent bends back and strikes it on the breast beside the neck, and “the eagle flung it away to the ground in the pain of its wounds, and dropped it in the middle of the throng, and with a scream it flew off on the blasts of the wind” (12.204–207). κλάγξας, “screaming”, is the cry by which Odysseus and Diomedes knew Athena's heron in the dark (κλάγξαντος, 10.276). No other eagle of omen in Homer is hurt by its prey.

Polydamas makes the eagle the Trojans: it let its prey go before it reached its home, and did not bring it to its young, and so the Trojans will break the wall but not come back from the ships in good order (12.217–227). Hector will not be ruled by “long-winged birds” (12.237; see [[birds]]). The eagle of Book 8 came to the Achaeans in answer to Agamemnon's prayer and dropped its fawn by the altar of Zeus (8.245–252); this one comes unasked to the Trojans in their attack, and is ignored. The next eagle flies on the right for the Achaeans, when Ajax challenges Hector (13.821–823). See [[the-eagle-and-the-serpent]] and [[teras]].
