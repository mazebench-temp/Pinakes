---
summary: The words of swiftness belong to horses that cannot use it: Hector's horses balk at the ditch, “swift-footed though they were,” and Polydamas calls it folly to drive “our swift horses” through it.
---

Hector urges his men across the ditch, “but his horses did not dare, swift-footed though they were, and neighed loudly, standing at the very edge” (12.50–52; [[horses]], [[hector]]). The verse opens with τόλμων, “did not dare,” and sets ὠκύποδες against it. Polydamas draws the lesson: “it is foolish of us to drive our swift horses through the ditch; it is very hard to cross” (12.62–63; [[polydamas]]), and the Trojans fight on foot (12.75–87).
