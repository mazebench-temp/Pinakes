summary: Five sons of Priam lead against the wall: Hector and his half-brother Cebriones in the first company, Paris in the second, and Helenus and Deiphobus, “two sons of Priam”, in the third (12.88–95).
refs: [12.95]
---

υἷε δύω Πριάμοιο, “two sons of Priam” (12.95), names Helenus and Deiphobus, as the same words named Isus and Antiphus, whom Agamemnon killed together in Book 11 (11.101–102; see [[helenus]], [[deiphobus]], [[isus]] and [[antiphus-son-of-priam]]). The first company is Hector's, with Polydamas and Cebriones, whom Book 16 calls a bastard son of Priam (16.738), and Paris leads the second (12.88–93; see [[hector]], [[cebriones]] and [[paris]]). Only Hector is given the patronymic, as he leaps in at the gate (12.438; see [[son-of-priam]]). By the end of Book 13 Deiphobus and Helenus have both left the battle, wounded in the hand (13.781–783), and Hector has rebuked Paris (13.765–787; see [[the-fight-at-the-wall]]).
