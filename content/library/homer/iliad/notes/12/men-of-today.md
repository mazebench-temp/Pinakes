summary: In Book 12 the narrator speaks again and again from his own time. He looks past the war to the day the wall is washed away and calls the men who fell at Troy “the race of men half-divine”, he says that it is hard for him to tell the battle “like a god”, and twice he measures a stone by men “such as mortals are now”.

**After the war.** The book opens far from its battle. The narrator tells how long the wall stood, while Hector lived, Achilles kept up his wrath and Priam's city was unsacked, and how Poseidon and Apollo destroyed it after the Argives had gone home (12.3–35; see [[the-achaean-wall]] and [[the-building-of-the-wall]]). In that future the heroes are a race that is gone, and he gives them the name that later Greek used for them: the Simoeis is the river “where many oxhide shields and helmets / had fallen in the dust, and the race of men half-divine” (12.22–23; see [[hemitheos]]).

**Not a god.** In the middle of the battle he speaks of himself: “it is hard for me to tell all these things like a god” (12.176). Elsewhere he speaks of himself in this way only when he calls on the Muses (see the translation's note on 12.175–181 and [[invocation-of-the-muses]]).

**Such as mortals are now.** Twice he measures a stone by the men of his own day. Ajax kills Epicles with a jagged boulder that lay inside the wall, and “nor could / a man easily hold it in both hands, not even one in his prime, / such as mortals are now” (12.381–383; see [[epicles]] and [[ajax]]). Hector lifts a stone that “not even two men, the best of the people, / could easily heave” onto a wagon, “such as mortals are now; but he wielded it easily even alone” (12.445–449; see [[hector]]), the verse that ended Diomedes' stone in Book 5 (5.304 = 12.449; see the article above). But the narrator adds that “the son of crooked-counseling Cronus made it light for him” (12.450), which he never said of Diomedes' stone. The hero of the past is stronger than the men of now, and here his strength is also a god's gift (see [[stone-throwing]] and the translation's note on 12.450).
