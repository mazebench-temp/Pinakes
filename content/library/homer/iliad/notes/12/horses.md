---
summary: Horses in Book 12 are what the Trojans must leave behind. Hector's horses balk at the edge of the ditch (12.50–59); on Polydamas' counsel the Trojans leave them with the charioteers and go on foot (12.60–87); only Asius drives his horses on through the open gate, to his death (12.110–124).
passages:
  - 12.50-59 | The horses balk at the ditch
  - 12.110-124 | Asius will not leave his horses
---

**At the ditch.** Hector's horses “did not dare, swift-footed though they were, and neighed loudly, standing at the very edge” (12.50–52); χρεμετίζω, “neigh”, occurs only here. The ditch is too wide to leap and too hard to cross, with overhanging banks and sharp stakes (12.52–59; see [[the-achaean-wall]]). In Book 8 Hector had boasted that “the horses will easily leap over the ditch they have dug” (8.179); now they will not try. Polydamas explains that there is no room for horsemen to fight beyond it (12.62–66), and the Trojans leave their horses there, each man ordering his charioteer to hold them in good order at the ditch (12.75–85). The order is given in the verses with which the Achaeans left theirs at the ditch that morning (12.84–85 = 11.47–48; see [[battle-order]] and [[chariot]]).

**Asius.** Asius alone “would not leave his horses there, and his charioteer and attendant” (12.110–111). He came from Arisbe behind “great and tawny horses” (12.96–97 = 2.838–839), and the narrator says that he will never go home, “glorying in his horses and chariot”, to windy Ilios (12.113–115). He drives through the gate on the left that was held open for fugitives (12.118–124; see [[asius]]). His death comes in Book 13, on foot in front of his horses, and Antilochus drives the team away to the Achaeans (13.384–401).

The ditch that stops Hector's horses is leapt in Book 16 by the immortal horses that the gods gave to Peleus, carrying Patroclus after the fleeing Trojans (16.380–381).
