---
summary: Sarpedon turns from the honors of kings to the fact of death with an endearment: “My friend, if, once we had escaped this war, we two were to be ageless and immortal forever.”
---

The second half of Sarpedon's speech to Glaucus begins with πέπον: “My friend, if, once we had escaped this war, we two were to be ageless and immortal forever, then neither would I myself fight among the foremost, nor would I send you into the battle where men win glory” (12.322–325; [[sarpedons-speech]], [[glaucus]], [[immortals]], [[where-men-win-glory]]). The word of affection marks the turn from what the Lycians give their kings to what the kings owe in return, and to the thought of death that makes them pay it. Sarpedon uses the same address in his last words to Glaucus, Γλαῦκε πέπον (16.492), when he lies dying and asks him to fight for his body. See [[sarpedon]].
