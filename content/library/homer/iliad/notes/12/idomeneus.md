# Δευκαλίδης, "son of Deucalion" (Δευκαλίδαο 12.117, 17.608; Δευκαλίδη 13.307), is Idomeneus and, by
# the patronymic rule, his father (deucalion).
grc: ["Δευκαλίδ*"]
relations:
  - father: deucalion (12.117)
passages:
  - 12.110-117 | Asius' death foretold
---

When Asius alone of the Trojans keeps his chariot and drives for the open gate, the narrator breaks off to tell his end (see [[asius]] and [[the-gates-of-the-wall]]):

{{quote:9bd01e70-43d9-51d2-bf9d-618f6314cbed}}

νήπιος, “the fool!” (12.113; see [[nepios]]); μοῖρα δυσώνυμος, “fate of evil name” (12.116; see [[moira]]). The prophecy is kept in Book 13. Asius comes on foot in front of his horses, which his charioteer keeps so close that they breathe on his shoulders, and Idomeneus strikes him in the throat; he falls like an oak or a poplar or a tall pine that carpenters fell in the mountains for a ship's timber, and lies stretched in front of his horses and chariot (13.384–393). The man who would not leave his horses (12.110–112) dies in front of them.

The verse gives Idomeneus his father's name for the first time: ἀγαυοῦ Δευκαλίδαο, “noble son of Deucalion” (12.117; see [[noble]] and [[deucalion]]). In Book 13 Meriones addresses him by the patronymic (13.307), and Idomeneus traces the line himself, from Zeus to Minos, Deucalion and himself (13.449–453).
