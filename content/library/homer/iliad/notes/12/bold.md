---
summary: Both of Polydamas' counsels open with the same verse, in which he comes and stands beside “bold Hector.”
---

“Then Polydamas came and stood beside bold Hector and said” (12.60 = 12.210; [[polydamas]], [[hector]]). The verse introduces his advice to leave the chariots at the ditch, which Hector takes (12.61–81), and his reading of the eagle and the serpent, which Hector rejects with a threat (12.210–250; [[the-eagle-and-the-serpent]]). The cautious man comes to Hector by his epithet of daring each time: the verse returns, with “had not” in place of “then,” for his third counsel, when the Trojans scattered among the ships are about to fall back to the city (13.723–725). Hector's answer to the second counsel turns the word around: it is Polydamas whose heart is “not staunch against the enemy, nor eager for battle” (12.247). See [[tharsos]].
