---
summary: Sharp are the stakes in the ditch, Leonteus' sword, the spears with which the Trojans climb the wall, and the top of the stone with which Hector breaks the gate.
grc: [ὀξέσιν, "ἀκαχμένα δούρατʼ"]
en: [sharpened]
---

**The stakes.** The ditch is fitted on top “with stakes, sharp ones, which the sons of the Achaeans had planted” (12.55–56; [[sons-of-the-achaeans]]), and Polydamas warns the Trojans of the same stakes, “sharp ones, standing in it” (12.63–64; [[polydamas]]). The narrator's dative ὀξέσιν is added to the entry's forms.

**The sword and the spears.** Leonteus, having killed Hippomachus, draws “his sharp sword from its sheath” (12.190; [[leonteus]], [[sword]]). When the Trojans climb onto the copings they hold ἀκαχμένα δούρατʼ, “their sharpened spears” (12.444): ἀκαχμένος, “sharpened,” is the participle of the formula ἄλκιμον ἔγχος ἀκαχμένον ὀξέϊ χαλκῷ, “a strong spear, tipped with sharp bronze” (10.135), and the half-verse returns in the fight over Patroclus (17.412). It is added to the entry, with the translation's “sharpened.”

**The stone.** Hector's stone stood before the gates, “thick at the base, but at the top it was sharp” (12.446–447; [[stone-throwing]]).
