**On the towers.** The Trojans are tearing at the copings and battlements, and the Danaans are shooting from behind oxhides (12.258–264), when the pair goes into action:

{{quote:7377b60d-ac2b-5fbd-aa98-fe32cb0abb50}}

The participle and the verb are dual: κελευτιόωντε, “calling out orders”, and φοιτήτην, “ranged” (12.265–266). When Odysseus turned the army back in Book 2, he kept gentle words for a king and the staff for a man of the people (2.188–206); the Ajaxes have soothing words and harsh ones for whoever holds back, and speak to every rank at once. It is the only speech in the poem that the pair speaks together (see [[exhortation]] and [[not-all-alike-in-war]]). μεσήεις, “middling”, occurs only here (see the translation's note on 12.269–270). The man “who shouts at us” (ὁμοκλητήρ, 12.273) is the enemy's leader, Hector. Their hope that Olympian Zeus will let them drive the enemy back to the city (12.275–276) runs against what the narrator has said of Zeus, whose heart “wished to hold out glory to Hector” (12.174; see [[zeus]]). “So the two, shouting out in front, urged on the battle of the Achaeans” (12.277), and the stones fly from both sides as thick as snow (12.278–289; see [[the-great-snowfall]]).

**Parted.** Menestheus, looking along the wall for help, catches sight of “the two Ajaxes, insatiable of war” (12.335; see [[insatiable]]), and asks for both, “for that would be by far the best of all” (12.344; see [[menestheus]]). His herald greets them with the verse in which Agamemnon praised them in the review, “Ajaxes, leaders of the bronze-armored Argives” (12.354 = 4.285; see [[thootes]]). The request parts them. Telamonian Ajax goes, and leaves the son of Oileus with Lycomedes to “stand and urge the Danaans to fight with all their might” (12.364–369; see [[ajax]], [[ajax-son-of-oileus]] and [[lycomedes]]), the work for which Agamemnon had praised the pair: “for you yourselves bid the people fight with all their might” (4.287). When Poseidon comes to the ships in Book 13, they are together again (13.46–82).
