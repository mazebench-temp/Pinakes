---
summary: Cronus is named once in Book 12, in his son's title. When Hector lifts the stone that breaks the gate, “The son of crooked-counseling Cronus made it light for him” (12.450).
---

Book 12 never calls Zeus Κρονίδης or Κρονίων, the patronymics that name him six times in Book 11 (see [[son-of-cronus]]). Its one “son of Cronus” is the verse-end Κρόνου πάϊς ἀγκυλομήτεω, “the son of crooked-counseling Cronus”, when Zeus makes Hector's stone light (12.449–450; see [[crooked-counseling]] and [[zeus]]). The verse-end comes eight times in Homer, and nearly always the god gives or sends: the scepter (2.205), a star (4.75), his gifts by halves (9.37), glory to Hector (18.293), a portent to Odysseus (Od. 21.415). He also turns the serpent at Aulis to stone (2.319), makes this stone light, and pities Sarpedon (16.431). Twice he changes the nature of a thing with ἔθηκε, “made”: the serpent into stone (λᾶαν γάρ μιν ἔθηκε, 2.319), and here the stone (λᾶαν, 12.445) light (ἐλαφρὸν ἔθηκε).

ἀγκυλομήτης belongs to Cronus himself. Hera uses it when she names her father: “and crooked-counseling Cronus fathered me, the most senior” (4.59). Hesiod gives it to him five times (*Theogony* 18, 137, 168, 473, 495), from his birth, youngest of the Titans, to the day he brought up again the children he had swallowed (see [[fall-of-cronus]]).
