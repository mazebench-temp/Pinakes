---
summary: Hector's charioteer goes on foot as the third leader of Hector's company, and Hector leaves “another man, weaker than Cebriones” with the chariot at the ditch (12.91–92).
passages:
  - 12.86-92 | The first company
---

On Polydamas' advice each Trojan gives his horses to his charioteer to hold at the ditch (12.84–85; see [[polydamas]] and [[battle-order]]). Cebriones has held Hector's reins since Teucer killed Archeptolemus (8.318–319; see [[archeptolemus]] and [[hector]]), but Hector does not leave him with the horses: καί σφιν Κεβριόνης τρίτος εἵπετο, “and Cebriones went with them as a third”, for beside the chariot Hector had left ἄλλον Κεβριόναο χερείονα, “another man, weaker than Cebriones” (12.91–92). Each of the five companies has three leaders, and Cebriones is the third of the first, with Hector and Polydamas (see the translation's note on 12.86–104 and [[the-fight-at-the-wall]]). The man who stays with the horses is not named.

At the ships the fighting is thickest “around Cebriones and noble Polydamas” (13.789–790), and in Book 16 Cebriones is driving Hector again when Patroclus kills him (16.727–743).
