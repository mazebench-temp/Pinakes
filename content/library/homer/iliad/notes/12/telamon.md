Τελαμώνιος Αἴας, “Telamonian Ajax”, names the great Ajax in Menestheus' message, which asks for him alone if both Ajaxes cannot come, “valiant Telamonian Ajax” (12.349 = 12.362), as he obeys and goes (12.364, 12.370), and as he kills Epicles (12.378; see [[telamonian]] and [[ajax]]). The adjective sets him apart from the other Ajax, whom he addresses in the same scene and whom the poem here calls for the first time Ὀϊλιάδης, “son of Oileus” (12.365; see [[ajax-son-of-oileus]] and [[oileus]]). Telamon's other son goes with him, “his brother by the same father” (κασίγνητος καὶ ὄπατρος, 12.371; see [[teucer]] and [[kasignetos]]); Teucer is the bastard whom Telamon raised in his own house (8.283–284). The common noun τελαμών, “strap”, comes in the same fight: Teucer's arrow hits “the shining strap” of Sarpedon's shield (τελαμῶνα, 12.401; see [[shield]]).
