en: [his face like]
passages:
  - 12.462-466 | Hector leaps through the gate
---

ὃ δʼ ἄρʼ ἔσθορε φαίδιμος Ἕκτωρ / νυκτὶ θοῇ ἀτάλαντος ὑπώπια: “and shining Hector leapt in, his face like swift night” (12.462–463). ἀτάλαντος, “equal to, a match for”, is the word of the formula θοῷ ἀτάλαντος Ἄρηϊ, “the equal of swift Ares”, said of Hector himself in Book 8 (8.215; also 13.295), and the half-verse puts night in the war god's place. θοή, “swift”, is night's own epithet (see [[swift-night]]). ὑπώπια, the face below the brows, is found only here (see the translation's note on 12.463).

The darkness is set against light. Hector is φαίδιμος, “shining”, and λάμπε δὲ χαλκῷ / σμερδαλέῳ, “he shone with the bronze, terrible, that he wore on his body” (12.463–464). His face is dark and his armor bright, and his eyes blaze with fire (12.466; see [[eyes-like-fire]]). No one could have held him back, “except the gods” (12.465–466).

The first night of the poem was Apollo's, who came down from Olympus to bring the plague “like the night” (1.47). The god came against the Achaean camp, and Hector breaks into it (see [[hector]], [[the-gates-of-the-wall]] and [[the-fight-at-the-wall]]).
