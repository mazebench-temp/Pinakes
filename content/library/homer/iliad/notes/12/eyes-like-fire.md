---
summary: As Hector leaps through the broken gate of the Achaean wall, no one but the gods could have stopped him, “and his eyes blazed with fire” (12.465–466).
grc: ["πυρὶ δʼ ὄσσε δεδήει"]
en: [blazed with fire]
---

οὔ κέν τίς μιν ἐρύκακεν ἀντιβολήσας / νόσφι θεῶν ὅτʼ ἐσᾶλτο πύλας· πυρὶ δʼ ὄσσε δεδήει: “no one could have held him back, meeting him, except the gods, when he leapt through the gates; and his eyes blazed with fire” (12.465–466). Here there is no “like”: the eyes are not compared with fire but burn with it. δεδήει is the pluperfect of δαίω, “kindle, blaze”. It is the verb with which the fight at the wall began, when “battle and cries blazed around the well-built wall” (12.35–36), and the fight ends with the blaze in Hector's eyes and his call to throw “the wondrous fire on the ships” (12.441).

In Book 1 the fire in Agamemnon's eyes was anger (1.104). Hector's is the fire of the onset, as his face is “like swift night” (12.463; see [[like-night]]). The eyes of the beast at bay shine with fire in Idomeneus' boar simile (13.474), and Achilles' eyes, when he arms, shine like the gleam of fire (19.365–366). See [[hector]] and [[eyes]].
