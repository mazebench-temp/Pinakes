---
summary: “Godlike Sarpedon” goes at the wall like a mountain lion, and he calls on “the godlike Lycians” to follow him.
grc: [ἀντιθέοισιν]
---

The lion simile ends with the man: “so then his spirit sent godlike Sarpedon to rush at the wall and break through the battlements” (12.307–308; [[sarpedon]], [[the-mountain-lion-and-the-fold]]). He is ἀντίθεος here as in Book 5, when his companions carried him wounded from the field (5.663, 5.692), and in the verse of his birth (6.199). When Ajax and Teucer have driven him back from the battlement, “he called out to the godlike Lycians, wheeling around” (12.408; [[lycians]]): the epithet of the leader is given to the whole people he commands. The same verse, with a word of reproach in place of “wheeling around,” brings him before the Lycians again when they flee from Patroclus, in his last fight (κέκλετʼ ἄρʼ ἀντιθέοισι καθαπτόμενος Λυκίοισιν, 16.421).
