---
summary: The Lapith Leonteus, holding the left gate, is “the equal of Ares, ruin of mortals.”
---

At the gate Asius' men find “two men, the best, high-hearted sons of Lapith spearmen, the one the son of Peirithous, mighty Polypoetes, the other Leonteus, the equal of Ares, ruin of mortals” (12.127–130; [[leonteus]], [[polypoetes]], [[high-hearted]]). The comparison was Hector's in Book 11 (11.295), and it will be Hector's again (13.802); here it is given to an Achaean defender, a man of the second rank, who will kill five Trojans (12.188–194). A little later the narrator gives him his Catalogue phrase, [[offshoot-of-ares]] (12.188). See [[ares]] and [[equal-of-ares]].
