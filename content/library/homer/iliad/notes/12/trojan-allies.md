---
summary: Polydamas speaks to the leaders “of the Trojans and the allies”; Sarpedon leads the “famous allies” as the fifth company, with the Lycian Glaucus and the Paeonian Asteropaeus, and the “far-famed” allies obey Polydamas' counsel with the Trojans (12.61, 12.101–109).
---

Σαρπηδὼν δʼ ἡγήσατʼ ἀγακλειτῶν ἐπικούρων, “And Sarpedon led the famous allies” (12.101; see [[sarpedon]] and [[famous]]). In the Catalogue each allied people came under its own leaders (2.840–877; see [[trojan-catalogue]]). At the wall one man leads them all, and he chooses as the best after himself his cousin Glaucus, a Lycian, and Asteropaeus, a Paeonian whom the Catalogue does not name; there the Paeonians came under Pyraechmes (12.101–104, 2.848; see [[glaucus]] and [[asteropaeus]]).

“Then the other Trojans and their far-famed allies / obeyed the counsel of blameless Polydamas” (12.108–109; see [[polydamas]]). The first verse ends with τηλεκλειτοί τʼ ἐπίκουροι, as when Hector called on the Trojans and the allies in Book 6 (6.111) and when Odysseus told Achilles of the enemy camped by the ships (9.233). The allies are not named as a body again in the book. Their fight is the Lycians' at Menestheus' tower (12.290–429; see [[lycians]] and [[the-fight-at-the-wall]]).
