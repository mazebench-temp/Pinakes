---
summary: The ram of Book 12 is a fleece. Hector carries the stone against the gate of the Achaean wall as easily as a shepherd carries the fleece of a ram in one hand (12.451–452).
grc: ["ἄρσενος οἰὸς"]
---

ὡς δʼ ὅτε ποιμὴν ῥεῖα φέρει πόκον ἄρσενος οἰὸς: “And as a shepherd easily carries the fleece of a ram” (12.451). The ram here is not ἀρνειός or κτίλος but ἄρσην ὄϊς, “a male sheep”, the phrase of the Cyclops' rams, under which Odysseus ties his men to escape from the cave (ἄρσενες ὄιες, *Odyssey* 9.425). πόκος, the shorn fleece, occurs only here in Homer. The shepherd carries it in one hand, and its weight is the measure of a stone that two of the best men of today could not heave onto a wagon (12.445–453; see [[the-shepherd-and-the-fleece]], [[sheep]] and [[hector]]).
