---
summary: In the narrator's look ahead, “Phoebus Apollo” turns the rivers of Ida against the Achaean wall.
---

When Troy has fallen and the Achaeans have gone home, Poseidon and Apollo will bring the wall to nothing: “Phoebus Apollo turned the mouths of them all together, and for nine days he sent their stream against the wall” (12.24–25; [[the-achaean-wall]]). The narrator names the pair twice without the epithet (12.17, 12.34) and gives Apollo the full formula at the verse end for his part in the work, the turning of the rivers ([[rhesus-river]], [[scamander]], [[simoeis]]). Poseidon used the same formula in Book 7 for the partner with whom he once built Laomedon's wall, the wall he feared the Achaean wall would make men forget (7.452–453). The nine days are as long as the god's arrows ranged through the army in the plague (1.53). See [[apollo]], [[poseidon]] and [[the-building-of-the-wall]].
