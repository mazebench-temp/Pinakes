---
summary: The Lapiths kill and strip the Trojans at the gate, and the narrator passes to the omen at the ditch “while these were stripping them of their gleaming armor” (12.187, 12.195).
grc: ["ἐνάριζον ἀπʼ ἔντεα"]
---

Polypoetes kills Damasus with a spear through the helmet, “And then he slew and stripped Pylon and Ormenus” (ἐξενάριξεν, 12.187; see [[polypoetes]], [[pylon]] and [[ormenus-killed-by-polypoetes]]); the verb is both. Leonteus strikes Hippomachus and kills Antiphates, Menon, Iamenus and Orestes (12.188–194; see [[leonteus]]). The narrator then turns from the gate to the ditch by way of the spoils:

“While these were stripping them of their gleaming armor, / the young men who followed Polydamas and Hector … / still stood hesitating beside the ditch” (12.195–199). Here ἐναρίζω has its first sense, the taking of the armor (ἀπʼ ἔντεα, “of their armor”), and the stripping marks the time: the Lapiths strip the dead in front of the gate while Hector's company waits at the ditch and the eagle appears (see [[divination]] and [[the-eagle-and-the-serpent]]). The construction, ὄφρα … τόφρα, “while … so long”, carries the story from one part of the field to another.
