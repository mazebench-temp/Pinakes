---
summary: As Hector leads the Trojans at the wall, “Zeus who delights in thunder” sends a storm of dust from Ida and bewitches the Achaeans.
---

Hector has dismissed the omen and leads the way, and the god he trusts answers at once: “So he spoke, and led the way, and they followed with him with a wondrous din; and Zeus who delights in thunder roused from the mountains of Ida a storm of wind that carried the dust straight at the ships; and the Achaeans' minds he bewitched, and granted glory to the Trojans and Hector” (12.251–255; [[wondrous]], [[ida]], [[kydos]]). The god of the thunderbolt acts here through wind and dust, from the mountains where he sat to watch the battle in Book 8 and thundered a sign of victory for the Trojans (8.47–52, 8.170–171). The Trojans attack the wall “trusting in his portents and in their own strength” (12.256; [[teras]]). See [[zeus]], [[hector]] and [[plan-of-zeus]].
