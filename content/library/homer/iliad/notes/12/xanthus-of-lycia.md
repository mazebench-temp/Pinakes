Ξάνθοιο παρʼ ὄχθας, “by the banks of the Xanthus” (12.313). The river has stood for far-off Lycia (2.877, 5.479) and for the end of Bellerophon's journey (6.172). Here it marks the kings' land. τέμενος, “domain”, from τέμνω, “cut”, is land marked out for a king or a hero (6.194, 9.578, 18.550) or for a god (2.696, 8.48). Sarpedon's “a fair one, of orchard and … plowland” (12.314) repeats the words of the domain the Lycians marked out for Bellerophon (6.195), with πυροφόροιο, “wheat-bearing”, added. Whether it is the same land, handed down to his grandsons, the poem does not say (see [[bellerophon-in-lycia]], [[sarpedon]] and [[glaucus]]).
