---
summary: Book 12 has three lions. Hector at the ditch is like a boar or a lion that turns on the hunters (12.41–50); Zeus rouses Sarpedon “like a lion against cattle with curving horns” (12.293); and Sarpedon goes at the wall like a mountain-bred lion that will try the guarded fold (12.299–308).
passages:
  - 12.41-50 | A boar or a lion
  - 12.290-308 | Sarpedon like a lion
---

**Hector.** The lion of 12.42 shares its simile with the boar. The hunters stand against it in close order and hurl their spears, but it does not fear or flee, and its own pride kills it (12.41–48; see [[the-boar-or-lion-at-bay]]).

**Sarpedon.** Sarpedon's lion is given twice. Zeus rouses him “like a lion against cattle with curving horns” (12.293; see [[lion-among-cattle]]), and as he goes he is “like a mountain-bred lion, which has long gone without meat”, whose proud spirit bids him go even into the close-built fold. If he finds herdsmen there with dogs and spears, he will not be driven off without a try, “but either he springs on one and seizes it, or he himself is hit among the foremost by a javelin from a swift hand” (12.299–306; see [[the-mountain-lion-and-the-fold]]). The lion's choice is the choice of Sarpedon's speech to Glaucus, to win glory or give it to another (12.328; see [[sarpedons-speech]]).

The lions that come to a fold or a herd in the Iliad are often kept off: a shepherd wounds one as it leaps into the fold, and dogs and country men drive another from the cattle at dawn (5.136–142, 11.548–555). Sarpedon's lion comes on in spite of the guards. When he dies in Book 16 the image is turned against him: Patroclus kills him as a lion kills a bull among the cattle (16.487–491; see [[sarpedon]]).
