The Trojans lock theirs together and hold them up as they rush the wall, the Achaeans fence the battlements with hides, and across the battlements the two sides hack at each other's shields. Sarpedon's round shield of beaten bronze, lined with oxhides stitched with golden wires, holds against Teucer's arrow and Ajax's spear (12.294–297, 12.400–405).

**Oxhides.** A shield is a hide, and the book calls it one again and again. By the Simoeis “many oxhide shields and helmets / had fallen in the dust” (βοάγρια, 12.22–23), a word that Homer uses elsewhere only of the two shields Telemachus is to keep back for Odysseus and himself (Od. 16.296). The Trojan companies lock themselves together “with their well-made oxhides” (τυκτῇσι βόεσσι, 12.105), and Asius' men rush the wall “holding their dry oxhides / up high” (βόας αὔας, 12.137–138). βοῦς, “ox”, is the shield itself, as in Hector's boast that he knows how to swing “the dry oxhide” to right and left (βῶν ἀζαλέην, 7.238–239). The defenders “fenced the battlements with oxhides” (ῥινοῖσι βοῶν, 12.263), most naturally their shields, for Idomeneus' round shield is made “of oxhides and gleaming bronze” in the same words (13.406; see [[battlements]]).

**Struck.** The helmets ring dry under the stones, “and the bossed shields” (12.160–161; see [[bossed]]), and the din at Menestheus' tower rises “as shields were struck, and helmets with horsehair crests, / and the gates” (12.339–340). At the battlements the two sides “hacked at the oxhide shields around one another's chests, / the well-rounded shields and the fluttering bucklers of hide” (12.425–426; see [[well-rounded]] and [[feathered]]), the two verses of the fight around the phantom of Aeneas (12.425–426 = 5.452–453). Many are stabbed “right through the shield itself” (12.429).

**Sarpedon's shield.** When Zeus rouses his son, Sarpedon takes up his shield:

{{quote:5a7d69b1-16e5-5393-a25c-7fa76e62c758}}

It is an ἀσπίς, round, “balanced on every side” (see [[balanced-on-every-side]]), beaten out of bronze: ἐξήλατον (only here) and ἤλασεν are from ἐλαύνω, the verb for hammering metal, as Tychius “hammered an eighth layer of bronze” onto the hides of Ajax's shield (ἤλασε, 7.223; see [[the-shield-of-ajax]]). Inside, oxhides are stitched close by golden ῥάβδοι that run unbroken round the rim, wires or rivets that hold the hides to the bronze, not the κανόνες, the bars across the inside of a shield (8.193, 13.407; see the translation's note on 12.294–297). Holding it before him and shaking two spears, he goes against the wall like a mountain lion (12.298–308; see [[the-mountain-lion-and-the-fold]]).

**Held.** At the battlement Teucer's arrow hits “the shining strap around his chest / of his man-covering shield” (12.401–402), the τελαμών by which the shield hung (see [[man-covering]]); and Ajax “leapt on him and stabbed his shield; but not right through / went the spear, and it drove him back as he pressed on” (12.404–405). Between the two blows Zeus wards off death from his son (12.402–403; see [[sarpedon]] and [[zeus]]). The same shield is round and ἀμφιβρότη, “man-covering”, the word of the great shields that cover a man from head to foot (2.389, 11.32): Homer's epithets of shape and of size meet in one weapon. After Sarpedon's death the Achaeans strip “the bronze, gleaming armor” from his shoulders, and Patroclus gives it to his companions to carry to the ships (16.663–665).
