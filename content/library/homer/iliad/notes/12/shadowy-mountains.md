---
summary: In the simile of the stones, a blustering wind drives “the shadowy clouds” and pours down the snow.
---

“And the stones fell to the ground like snowflakes, that a blustering wind, driving the shadowy clouds, pours down thick upon the bountiful earth” (12.156–158; [[a-snow-of-stones]], [[blustering]], [[bountiful-earth]]). As in Books 5 and 11, σκιόεις describes clouds, not mountains: in Book 5 the violent winds scatter the shadowy clouds with their blasts (5.525–526; [[violent]]), and in Book 11 the baleful star sinks into them (11.63). Here the wind does not scatter the clouds but drives them, and their snow is the stones that the Achaeans throw from the towers. See [[stone-throwing]].
