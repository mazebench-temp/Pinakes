---
summary: Asius' death is told before it happens: as he drives for the gate, “fate of evil name enfolded him / through the spear of Idomeneus”.
passages:
  - 12.110-117 | Asius' fate foretold
---

Asius will not leave his chariot with the others at the ditch, and drives for the gate on the left. The narrator calls him a fool, who will not escape the evil fates and go home to windy Ilios, “for before that, fate of evil name enfolded him / through the spear of Idomeneus, noble son of Deucalion” (12.113–117; see [[asius]], [[ker-doom]] and [[nepios]]).

μοῖρα δυσώνυμος, “fate of evil name”, is found only here. δυσώνυμος, from δυσ-, “ill”, and ὄνυμα, “name”, is said of what is hateful to name; Hecuba gives it to the Achaeans who wear the Trojans down, “the sons of the Achaeans, of evil name” (6.255; see [[of-evil-name]]). The verb is an aorist, ἀμφεκάλυψεν, “enfolded”, as death enfolds a man when he dies: “and death enfolded him” (5.68), or the black cloud of death (θανάτου δὲ μέλαν νέφος ἀμφεκάλυψεν, 16.350). The narrator speaks of the end as already reached, though Asius dies only in the next book, on foot in front of his horses, struck in the throat by Idomeneus' spear (13.384–393; see [[idomeneus]] and [[prophecy]]).
