The gods who favor the Danaans grieve, Zeus is “lord over all mortals and immortals”, the Lycians look on their kings “as gods”, Sarpedon reasons from the gap between gods and men, and no one but a god could have stopped Hector at the gate.

---

**Against the will of the gods.** The wall “was built against the will of the gods, / the immortals; and so it did not stand firm for long” (12.8–9). The Achaeans gave the gods no hecatombs for it (12.6; see [[hecatomb]]), and after the war three gods destroy it, Poseidon and Apollo with the rivers and Zeus with his rain (12.17–33; see [[the-building-of-the-wall]] and [[fame-and-the-tomb]]).

**Like a god.** Three times the book measures men by gods. The narrator cannot tell all the fighting at all the gates “like a god” (12.176; see [[invocation-of-the-muses]]). The Lycians “look on us as gods”, Sarpedon tells Glaucus (12.312). And when Hector leaps through the gate, “no one could have held him back, meeting him, / except the gods” (12.465–466; see [[hector]]), as Zeus has made his stone light for him (12.450).

**Gods who watch.** The gods who help the Danaans “were grieved at heart” as the Argives defend their ships of necessity (12.179–180; see [[achos]] and [[the-gods-mirror-the-mortals]]), but none of them comes down, for Zeus has forbidden the gods to help either side (8.10–17). In this book he alone acts: he sends the storm from Ida, rouses his son Sarpedon and wards off the fates from him, and gives Hector the higher glory (12.252–255, 12.292–293, 12.402–403, 12.437; see [[zeus]]). Hector names him “lord over all mortals and immortals” (12.242).

**Ageless and immortal.** Sarpedon's speech turns on the difference. If he and Glaucus, once they had escaped this war, were to be “ageless and immortal forever”, he would not fight in the front rank; but since the fates of death stand over them, which no mortal can escape, they must go and either give triumph to another or win it (12.322–328; see [[sarpedons-speech]], [[immortal-and-ageless]] and [[ker-doom]]). Immortality would make the fighting pointless; mortality leaves no other choice.
