---
summary: ἄλκιμος, “valiant,” praises Patroclus in the first verse of the book and Telamonian Ajax in Menestheus' message.
---

The book begins with Patroclus in the formula of Book 11: “So in the hut the valiant son of Menoetius was tending wounded Eurypylus” (12.1–2; [[son-of-menoetius]]). Menestheus' message asks for “valiant Telamonian Ajax” if both Ajaxes cannot come (12.349), and Thootes repeats the verse (12.362; [[telamonian]], [[menestheus]]). The phrase Τελαμώνιος ἄλκιμος Αἴας occurs only in this message and its repetition; elsewhere the formula is μέγας Τελαμώνιος Αἴας, “great Telamonian Ajax,” as in the verse that tells how he obeyed (12.364). See [[ajax]] and [[patroclus]].
