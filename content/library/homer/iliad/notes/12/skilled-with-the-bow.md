---
summary: Menestheus asks for Telamonian Ajax and for Teucer, “well skilled with the bow,” and the herald repeats the verse.
---

“Then at least let valiant Telamonian Ajax come alone, and let Teucer follow with him, well skilled with the bow” (12.349–350), Menestheus tells his herald, and Thootes repeats it to the Ajaxes (12.362–363; [[teucer]], [[menestheus]], [[thootes]]). Teucer has just come from his hut (12.336), back in the fighting after Hector's stone broke his bowstring and numbed his hand in Book 8 (8.324–334). The archer's phrase is borne out at the tower: Pandion carries his curved bow for him (12.372; [[pandion]], [[curved-bow]]), and his arrows hit Glaucus in the bared arm and the strap of Sarpedon's shield (12.387–391, 12.400–402; [[glaucus]], [[sarpedon]]).
