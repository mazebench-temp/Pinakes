---
summary: Teucer's arrow hits “the shining strap around his chest of his man-covering shield,” but Zeus wards off death from Sarpedon.
---

Ajax and Teucer come at Sarpedon together, “and the one with an arrow hit the shining strap around his chest of his man-covering shield; but Zeus warded off the fates from his son, so that he should not be beaten down at the sterns of the ships” (12.400–403; [[teucer]], [[sarpedon]], [[phaeinos]], [[ker-doom]]). The half-verse ἀσπίδος ἀμφιβρότης, with the strap in the verse before, is the one of Agamemnon's order in Book 2, where many a man would sweat through “the strap across his chest of his man-covering shield” (2.388–389). The great shield covers Sarpedon's body, and the arrow strikes the strap that carries it; Ajax's spear, thrust at the shield itself, does not go through (12.404–405).
