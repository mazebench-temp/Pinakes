---
summary: The Simoeis is the last of the rivers of Ida that destroy the Achaean wall, and the only one given a clause of its own, for by it “many oxhide shields and helmets had fallen in the dust, and the race of men half-divine” (12.22–23).
passages:
  - 12.17-23 | The rivers of Ida
---

The list of rivers ends on the battlefield. The Simoeis meets the Scamander in front of Troy (5.773–774), the fighting of Book 6 ranged “between the streams of the Simoeis and the Xanthus” (6.4), and Simoeisius, born on its banks and named for it, was killed by Ajax in the first battle of the poem (4.473–489; see [[simoeisius]] and [[scamander]]). βοάγρια, “oxhide shields” (12.22), occurs elsewhere in Homer only at Od. 16.296.

The men who fell there are seen from the time of the wall's destruction, when the war is long over: ἡμιθέων γένος ἀνδρῶν, “the race of men half-divine” (12.23), the only place where Homer uses the word (see [[hemitheos]], [[men-of-today]] and [[the-building-of-the-wall]]). Hesiod gives “divine Simoeis” a verse shaped like 12.21, Γρήνικόν τε καὶ Αἴσηπον θεῖόν τε Σιμοῦντα, “the Granicus and Aesepus and divine Simoeis” (*Theogony* 342; see [[hesiod]]).
