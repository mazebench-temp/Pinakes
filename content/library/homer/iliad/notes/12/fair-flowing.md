---
summary: When the wall is gone, Poseidon turns the rivers of Ida back to their channels, “where before they had poured their fair-flowing water.”
---

The narrator's account of the wall's destruction ends with the rivers restored: “and he turned the rivers back to flow down their channels, where before they had poured their fair-flowing water” (12.32–33; [[shaker-of-the-earth]], [[the-achaean-wall]]). The rivers named at the start of the passage, from the Rhesus to the Simoeis (12.20–22), were turned against the wall for nine days (12.24–25; [[phoebus]]); the epithet belongs to their ordinary course, which returns when the work is done and the shore is smooth again (12.30–31). See [[rhesus-river]], [[scamander]] and [[simoeis]].
