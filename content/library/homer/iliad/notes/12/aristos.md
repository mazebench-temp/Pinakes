---
summary: The book's best are counted in groups: the best of the Trojans, who will die before the wall is gone; Hector's column, “the most and the best”; the two best after Sarpedon; the two best Lapiths at the gate; and the two men, “the best of the people”, who could not lift Hector's stone. Its speakers argue over what is best to do.
grc: [ἀρίστω]
---

**The best of an army.** The narrator dates the wall's end by the deaths of “the best of the Trojans” (12.13; see [[the-achaean-wall]]). Hector's column, with Polydamas and Cebriones, is made of those “who were the most and the best, and were most eager / to break through the wall” (12.89–90, again 12.197–198), the words with which the Catalogue gives Agamemnon and Hector the largest and best contingents (πολὺ πλεῖστοι καὶ ἄριστοι, 2.577, 2.817). Sarpedon chooses Glaucus and Asteropaeus, “for they seemed to him to be the best by far / of the rest, after himself; but he stood out even among them all” (12.103–104; see [[asteropaeus]] and [[sarpedon]]). At the gate Asius' men find “two men, the best”, the Lapiths Polypoetes and Leonteus (12.127; see [[lapiths]]).

**The best counsel.** Polydamas speaks again although Hector rebukes him, “as it seems to me best” (ἄριστα, 12.215), and Hector's answer is a claim to the best sign: “One omen is best: to fight in defense of our native land” (12.243; see [[one-omen-is-best]]). Menestheus, under attack, says that it would be “by far the best of all” if both Ajaxes came, and the herald repeats it (12.344, 12.357; see [[menestheus]] and [[thootes]]).

**The best of the people.** The stone that Hector carries to the gate is one that “not even two men, the best of the people, / could easily heave” onto a wagon (δήμου ἀρίστω, 12.447–448; see [[demos]] and [[men-of-today]]). The dual is the phrase used of the two sons of Merops, “two men, the best of their people” (11.328). The best two men of the poet's day are the measure of one hero with a god's help.
