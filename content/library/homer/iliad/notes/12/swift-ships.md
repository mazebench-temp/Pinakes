---
summary: The wall was built to keep safe “the swift ships and the abundant plunder,” and Asius drives his chariot close to the swift ships; the “swift hand” of 12.306 and the “swift night” of 12.463 belong to other entries.
except: [12.306, 12.463]
---

The narrator gives the purpose of the wall: the Achaeans built it, without hecatombs, “so that it might keep safe the swift ships and the abundant plunder that it held within” (12.7–8; [[the-achaean-wall]], [[famous]]). Asius alone keeps his chariot and “drove close to the swift ships with them, the fool!” (12.112–113; [[asius]], [[leader-of-men]]).

Two other verses of the book have θοός in the forms this entry claims for ships, and they are given to their own entries: the javelin thrown “from a swift hand” in Sarpedon's lion simile (θοῆς ἀπὸ χειρός, 12.306; [[swift]]) and Hector's face “like swift night” (νυκτὶ θοῇ, 12.463; [[swift-night]]).
