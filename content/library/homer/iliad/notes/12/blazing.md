---
summary: Sarpedon tells Glaucus that the honors the Lycians give them oblige them to “meet the blazing battle,” in the verse with which Agamemnon reproached Menestheus and Odysseus.
---

“So now we must take our stand among the foremost Lycians and meet the blazing battle, so that one of the close-armored Lycians may say this: ‘Truly not without glory are they who rule over Lycia’” (12.315–318; [[sarpedons-speech]], [[close-armored]]). The verse 12.316 is the same as 4.342, and the verse before it differs only in the address: Agamemnon told Menestheus and Odysseus that it was “fitting for you two to stand among the foremost” (4.341), because they were first at his feasts. Sarpedon draws the same conclusion from the same premise, the seat of honor, the meat and the full cups (12.310–312; [[feast]], [[honey-sweet]]), but he draws it about himself. See [[sarpedon]] and [[glaucus]].
