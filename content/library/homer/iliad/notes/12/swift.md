---
summary: In Sarpedon's lion simile the beast that goes for the fold may be hit “by a javelin from a swift hand.”
grc: ["θοῆς ἀπὸ χειρὸς"]
---

The mountain lion goes for the sheep though herdsmen guard them, “but either he springs on one and seizes it, or he himself is hit among the foremost by a javelin from a swift hand” (12.305–306; [[the-mountain-lion-and-the-fold]], [[sarpedon]]). θοός describes the herdsman's quick throwing hand, the speed of the man who strikes first. The genitive θοῆς is the form that names a swift ship elsewhere, and the [[swift-ships]] entry leaves this verse to this one. The simile gives Sarpedon the same two outcomes he names to Glaucus a few verses later: to win glory or to give it to another (12.328; [[sarpedons-speech]]).
