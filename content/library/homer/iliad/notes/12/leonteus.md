**At the gate.** He is the second of the “two men, the best” whom Asius' column finds at the gate, “the other Leonteus, the equal of Ares, ruin of mortals” (12.127–130; see [[equal-of-ares]] and [[ruin-of-mortals]]). The half-verse is given to Hector (11.295, 13.802) and to Achilles (20.46). With [[Polypoetes]] he stands before the gate like an oak and fights before it like a wild boar (12.131–153; see [[polypoetes]], [[oaks-on-the-mountains]] and [[wild-boars-in-the-mountains]]).

**His victims.** “And the Lapiths joined in the war and the combat” (12.181; see [[lapiths]]). After Polypoetes' three, Leonteus kills five:

{{quote:bad3e3c9-f23a-5df2-98f5-380ae121c759}}

Λεοντεὺς ὄζος Ἄρηος, “Leonteus, offshoot of Ares”, is the verse-end that names him in the Catalogue and at the funeral games (2.745, 23.841; see [[offshoot-of-ares]]). He fights first with the spear and then with the sword, hand to hand (12.189–192; see [[sword]]). Hippomachus is the son of an Antimachus, whether of the Antimachus whose sons Agamemnon killed in Book 11 the poem does not say (11.122–147; see [[hippomachus]] and [[antimachus]]). Iamenus and Orestes came on with Asius (12.139; see [[iamenus]] and [[orestes-the-trojan]]); Antiphates and Menon are named only here (see [[antiphates]] and [[menon]]). The last verse closes Teucer's eight kills in Book 8 and Patroclus' nine in Book 16 (12.194 = 8.277 = 16.418; see [[androktasia]]).
