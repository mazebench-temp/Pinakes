---
summary: The ships are what the wall was built to protect and what the Trojans mean to burn. The Argives are penned in beside them, Polydamas fears a counterattack from them, Hector calls for fire on them, and at the end of the book the Danaans flee among them (12.5–8, 12.37–39, 12.71–74, 12.198, 12.441, 12.470–471).
passages:
  - 12.437-441 | Hector calls for fire on the ships
---

**What the wall protects.** The wall was built “to protect their ships”, “so that it might keep safe the swift ships and the abundant plunder / that it held within” (12.5–8; see [[the-achaean-wall]] and [[hecatomb]]). Now the Argives, beaten down by the lash of Zeus, are “penned in beside the hollow ships” (12.38). ἐελμένοι, “penned in”, is the verb of Achilles' request to Thetis in Book 1, that Zeus should “pen the Achaeans in among their ships' sterns and along the sea” (ἔλσαι, 1.409; see [[stern]]).

**The Trojans' goal.** The Trojans say that the Achaeans “would no longer / hold out, but would fall among the black ships” (12.106–107, 12.125–126; see the translation's note on 12.106–107, 12.125–126), and Hector's company is “most eager / to break through the wall and set the ships on fire” (12.197–198). Polydamas' fear is the way back: a counterattack “from the ships” would trap them in the ditch (12.71–74), and the omen means that “we … shall not come back in good order from the ships by the same paths” (12.223–225; see [[divination]]). Hector answers that if the rest of them are killed “at the ships of the Argives”, Polydamas at least is in no danger (12.245–247). When Zeus gives him the glory, he calls out: “break through the wall / of the Argives, and throw the wondrous fire on the ships” (12.440–441; see [[wondrous]] and [[exhortation]]). He called for fire on the ships in Book 8 (8.181–182); in Book 15 he takes hold of the stern of Protesilaus' ship and calls for fire, and in Book 16 the fire falls on it (15.704–718, 16.112–124).

**At the sterns.** Zeus wards off death from Sarpedon “so that he should not be beaten down at the sterns of the ships” (12.403; see [[sarpedon]] and [[stern]]). His death is kept for Book 16, when Patroclus has driven the Trojans back from the ships and kills them “between the ships and the river and the high wall” (16.394–398).

**Flight.** The book ends with the wall broken: “the Danaans fled in rout / among the hollow ships, and a relentless din arose” (12.470–471; see [[rout]]). From here to the end of Book 15 the fighting is at the ships.
