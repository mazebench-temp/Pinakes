---
summary: The Lycian kings “eat fat sheep and choice wine, honey-sweet,” with πίονα μῆλα, a formula the translation renders like ἴφια μῆλα.
grc: ["πίονα μῆλα"]
---

In Sarpedon's speech a Lycian says of his kings: “Truly not without glory are they who rule over Lycia, these kings of ours, and they eat fat sheep and choice wine, honey-sweet” (12.318–320; [[sarpedons-speech]], [[honey-sweet]]). The phrase is πίονα μῆλα, with πίων (“fat, rich”), the word of “the rich tilled fields” in the snow simile a few verses before (12.283) and of the fat thigh pieces of sacrifice ([[thigh-pieces]]). It is not the headword's ἴφια μῆλα, but the translation gives it the same English. The Iliad has it only here; in the Odyssey πίονα μῆλα are the Cyclops' flocks, which he drives into his cave and out to pasture (Od. 9.217, 9.237, 9.312, 9.315, 9.337). The meat of the feast is the kings' honor, and the next verses say what it costs them (12.320–321; [[feast]]). See [[sheep]].
