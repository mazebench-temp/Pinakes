“And Sarpedon seized the battlement in his strong hands and pulled, and it all came away, through and through; and above, the wall was laid bare, and he made a path for many” (12.397–399; [[sarpedon]], [[battlements]]). The formula is not χειρὶ παχείῃ but its plural counterpart with στιβαρός (“stout, firm”), χερσὶ στιβαρῇσι(ν), which also closes the verse and is also the formula of hands that grip: the boxers and the wrestlers of the funeral games (23.686, 23.711), and in the Odyssey Poseidon taking up his trident (ἑλὼν χερσὶ στιβαρῇσιν, Od. 4.506), with the same participle as here. It is added to the entry, with the translation's “strong hands.” See [[the-fight-at-the-wall]].
