---
summary: The two Ajaxes end their call along the towers with “the Olympian lord of the lightning,” the only time in Homer that mortals speak the title.
---

“But press forward, and urge one another on, in the hope that Zeus may grant it, the Olympian lord of the lightning, that we thrust back the quarrel and drive the enemy to the city” (12.274–276; [[olympian]], [[the-two-ajaxes]], [[exhortation]]). Of the four uses of ἀστεροπητής in Homer, this is the only one in a mortal's mouth: Hephaestus used it to warn of his father's power (1.580), and the narrator when Zeus went to his bed (1.609) and when the gods sat beside him looking at the wall the Achaeans had just built (7.443). Now the men who defend that wall call on him, though he has promised the day to Hector (12.235–236, 12.255; [[plan-of-zeus]]). See [[zeus]].
