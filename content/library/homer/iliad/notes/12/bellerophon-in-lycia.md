summary: Sarpedon reminds Glaucus of the great domain the two of them hold by the banks of the Xanthus, “a fair one, of orchard and wheat-bearing plowland” (12.313–314), in the words of the domain that the Lycians gave their grandfather Bellerophon (6.194–195).
passages:
  - 12.310-321 | The honors of the Lycian kings
---

καὶ τέμενος νεμόμεσθα μέγα Ξάνθοιο παρʼ ὄχθας / καλὸν φυταλιῆς καὶ ἀρούρης πυροφόροιο: “and we hold a great domain by the banks of the Xanthus, a fair one, of orchard and wheat-bearing plowland?” (12.313–314). The second verse is the verse of Bellerophon's reward. When the king of Lycia knew that his guest was the offspring of a god, he gave him his daughter and half his royal honor, “and the Lycians marked out for him a domain finer than all others, a fair one, of orchard and plowland, for him to hold” (καλὸν φυταλιῆς καὶ ἀρούρης, ὄφρα νέμοιτο, 6.194–195). A τέμενος is land “cut off” (τέμνω) for a king or a god, and the verse is the formula of a domain given as a reward: Achilles taunts Aeneas with the hope that the Trojans have cut him one (20.184–185).

The two men who hold the domain now are Bellerophon's grandsons. Sarpedon is the son of his daughter Laodameia by Zeus, and Glaucus of his son Hippolochus (6.196–206; see [[sarpedon]] and [[glaucus]]). They hold it by the river at which Bellerophon first came to Lycia, “Lycia and the flowing Xanthus” (6.172; see [[xanthus-of-lycia]]). The poem does not say that it is the same land; it describes it in the same words.

The story and the speech make the same argument. Bellerophon was honored after he had killed the Chimaera, the Solymi and the Amazons and the ambush laid for him (6.179–193). Sarpedon tells Glaucus that the honors of the kings of Lycia, the seat of honor, the meat and the full cups, and the domain, must be earned among the foremost Lycians, so that one of them may say that their kings rule “not without glory” (12.310–321; see [[sarpedons-speech]]).
