---
summary: Rebuked by Sarpedon, the Lycians bear down harder “around their lord, the counselor.”
---

Sarpedon has called on the Lycians not to let go of their courage, “and they, fearing their lord's rebuke, bore down the harder around their lord, the counselor” (12.413–414; [[sarpedon]], [[lycians]]). The title is the one Tlepolemus used of him in contempt before their fight in Book 5, “Sarpedon, counselor of the Lycians” (5.633); here it is the narrator's own word for the king whose people obey him. Sarpedon has just explained to Glaucus why the Lycians honor their kings and what the kings owe them in return (12.310–321; [[sarpedons-speech]]). See [[basileus]] and [[boule]].
