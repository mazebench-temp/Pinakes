---
summary: The narrator gives the reason why the Achaean wall did not last: the Achaeans built it “and they did not give glorious hecatombs to the gods”, and so it was built against the will of the immortals (12.5–9).
passages:
  - 12.3-9 | The wall built without hecatombs
---

The book opens with the wall's future. The Achaeans had built it to protect their ships, “and they did not give glorious hecatombs to the gods, / so that it might keep safe the swift ships and the abundant plunder / that it held within. It was built against the will of the gods, / the immortals; and so it did not stand firm for long” (12.6–9; see [[the-achaean-wall]] and [[the-building-of-the-wall]]).

The verse of the missing hecatombs is Poseidon's (12.6 = 7.450). In Book 7 it was a god's complaint, set beside his fear that men would forget the wall that he and Apollo had built for Laomedon (7.446–453); here the narrator speaks it in his own voice, as the reason for the wall's end. The purpose clause can be taken with the building, as the translation takes it, the wall being built to keep the ships safe, or with the hecatombs: the sacrifice should have been given so that the wall might keep them safe, and the gods' favor would have made it stand (see the translation's note on 12.6–8). On either reading a great work was begun without the great sacrifice owed for it, and it is the gods who take the wall away: Poseidon and Apollo turn the rivers of Ida against it, Apollo drives their flood at it for nine days while Zeus rains without ceasing, and Poseidon sweeps its foundations into the sea (12.17–33; see [[poseidon]] and [[apollo]]).
