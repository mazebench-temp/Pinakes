---
summary: The two Lapiths at the gate are “high-hearted sons of Lapith spearmen,” and the “Danaan spearmen” cannot push the Lycians back from the wall.
---

**The Lapiths.** “At the gates they found two men, the best, high-hearted sons of Lapith spearmen” (12.127–128; [[lapiths]], [[high-hearted]], [[polypoetes]], [[leonteus]]). The epithet goes to the fathers, the Lapiths of the war with the Centaurs (1.262–268; [[lapiths-drive-out-the-centaurs]]), and through them to the sons at the gate.

**The Danaans.** At Menestheus' tower neither side can move the other: the mighty Lycians cannot break the wall, “nor could the Danaan spearmen ever thrust the Lycians back from the wall, once they had first come close to it” (12.419–420; [[mighty]], [[lycians]]). The goddesses grieved in Book 8 for “the Danaan spearmen” (8.33, 8.464); here the phrase, in the nominative, names them holding their ground. See [[danaans]].
