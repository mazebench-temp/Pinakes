---
summary: The narrator foretells that Asius will die “through the spear of Idomeneus, noble son of Deucalion.”
---

Asius drives for the gate with his horses and chariot, and the narrator looks ahead: “for before that, fate of evil name enfolded him through the spear of Idomeneus, noble son of Deucalion” (12.116–117; [[of-evil-name]], [[idomeneus]], [[deucalion]], [[asius]]). The verse gives the killer his full honors, epithet and patronymic, before the deed; it comes at 13.384–393, where Asius, on foot before his horses, is struck in the throat and falls like an oak. The phrase Ἰδομενῆος ἀγαυοῦ occurs only here in Homer.
