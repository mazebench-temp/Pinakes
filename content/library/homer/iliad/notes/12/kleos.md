---
summary: The Lycian kings are to be praised as rulers “not without glory”, if they fight among the foremost; the hecatombs not given for the wall are “glorious”, and Sarpedon leads “famous” allies. The wall whose fame Poseidon feared in Book 7 is washed away.
grc: [ἀκλεέες, ἀγακλειτῶν]
---

**Not without glory.** Sarpedon wants one of the close-armored Lycians to say of their kings, “Truly not without glory are they who rule over Lycia, / these kings of ours” (οὐ μὰν ἀκλεέες, 12.318–319; see [[sarpedons-speech]] and [[tis-speech]]). Fame is what others say, and Sarpedon imagines the speaker and his words. The adjective ἀκλεής, “without fame”, is the word of Menelaus' curse on the Achaeans who sat silent before Hector's challenge, that they might sit there “spiritless, without glory” (7.99–100; see [[water-and-earth]]). The negative turns it into praise.

**Glorious and famous.** The book uses the old adjectives of κλέος as epithets. The Achaeans gave no “glorious hecatombs” to the gods for their wall (κλειτὰς ἑκατόμβας, 12.6; see [[hecatomb]]); Sarpedon leads the “famous allies” (ἀγακλειτῶν ἐπικούρων, 12.101), and the other Trojans and their “far-famed allies” obey Polydamas (τηλεκλειτοί, 12.108; see [[famous]]).

**The fame of the wall.** In Book 7 Poseidon complained that the fame of the Achaeans' wall would reach as far as the dawn spreads, and men would forget the wall he and Apollo built for Laomedon; Zeus gave him leave to sweep it into the sea when the Achaeans had gone home (7.446–463). Book 12 tells how it was done, and it is the narrator who keeps the fame of a wall that has left no trace on the shore (12.17–33; see [[fame-and-the-tomb]] and [[the-building-of-the-wall]]).
