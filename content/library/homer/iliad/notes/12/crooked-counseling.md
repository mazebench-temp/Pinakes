---
summary: The line-end formula names Zeus through his father as the god who makes Hector's stone light.
---

“The son of crooked-counseling Cronus made it light for him” (12.450; [[son-of-cronus]]). As in its other uses, the epithet belongs to the father and the act to the son. The verse before has just said that Hector “wielded it easily even alone” (12.449), the verse the poem used of Diomedes' stone in Book 5 (5.304); the second verse gives to Zeus what the first gave to Hector's strength. The formula keeps its place at the end of the verse, as in Odysseus' praise of the one king (2.205) and in Diomedes' answer to Agamemnon (9.37). See [[zeus]], [[cronus]] and [[hector]].
