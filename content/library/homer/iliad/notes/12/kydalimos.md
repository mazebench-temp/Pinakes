---
summary: In the simile of Hector at the ditch, the “glorious heart” of the boar or lion at bay never fears or flees.
---

“But his glorious heart never fears or flees, and it is his pride that kills him” (12.45–46; [[the-boar-or-lion-at-bay]], [[proud]]). The beast of the simile is Hector, urging his men across the ditch (12.49–50). κυδάλιμον κῆρ was the phrase of Agamemnon's groaning heart in Book 10 (10.16); here the heart is a beast's, and its glory is the courage that will not let it turn away from the hunters' spears. See [[hector]] and [[kydos]].
