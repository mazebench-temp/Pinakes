summary: Sarpedon's domain by the Xanthus is “a fair one, of orchard and wheat-bearing plowland,” with πυροφόρος, a word of the same meaning as πολύπυρος.
grc: [πυροφόροιο]
en: [wheat-bearing]
---

Sarpedon counts the honors of the Lycian kings: “and we hold a great domain by the banks of the Xanthus, a fair one, of orchard and wheat-bearing plowland?” (12.313–314; [[sarpedons-speech]], [[xanthus-of-lycia]]). The verse is the one that described the domain the Lycians gave Bellerophon (6.195; [[bellerophon-in-lycia]]), and Achilles will use it again to mock Aeneas (20.185); only here does it end with an epithet of the land. πυροφόρος = πυρός (“wheat”) + -φόρος (“bearing”): “wheat-bearing.” Homer gives it also to the plowlands that Tydeus held in Argos (14.122–123) and to the plain of Troy (21.602). It is not πολύπυρος, the headword of this entry, but a word of the same sense built on the same πυρός; the form and the translation's “wheat-bearing” are added to the entry. See [[lycia]].
