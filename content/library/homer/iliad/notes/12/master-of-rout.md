---
summary: Penned in by their ships, the Argives hold back “in fear of Hector, the mighty master of rout,” the words the Trojans used of Diomedes in Book 6.
---

“And the Argives, beaten down by the lash of Zeus, were penned in beside the hollow ships, and held back there in fear of Hector, the mighty master of rout; and he fought as before, like a storm wind” (12.37–40; [[hollow-ships]], [[krateros]], [[the-storm-wind-on-the-sea]]). κρατερὸν μήστωρα φόβοιο is the half-verse with which Helenus and Hector named Diomedes when they asked the Trojan women to pray against him (6.97, 6.278). The fear that the Trojans felt then is now the Achaeans', and the title passes to the man they fear; the simile that follows makes him a boar or a lion turning on the hunters (12.41–50; [[the-boar-or-lion-at-bay]]). See [[hector]] and [[rout]].
