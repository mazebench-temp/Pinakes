---
summary: δῖος honors the Scamander among the rivers that will wash the wall away, Hector as the Trojans follow him down from their chariots, and Menestheus' herald Thootes.
grc: ["=δῖός"]
---

**A river.** Of the rivers of Ida that Poseidon and Apollo turn against the wall, only the Scamander has an epithet: “the Granicus and Aesepus, and brilliant Scamander” (12.21; [[scamander]], [[the-achaean-wall]]). δῖος is a river's epithet as it was the Cephisus' in the Catalogue (2.522; [[cephisus]]). The form here, δῖός, with the second accent that the enclitic τε throws onto it, is added to the entry's forms.

**Hector.** When Hector takes Polydamas' advice and leaps down from his chariot, the army follows him: “Nor did the other Trojans gather on their chariots, but all sprang down, when they saw brilliant Hector” (12.82–83; [[polydamas]], [[chariot]]).

**A herald.** Menestheus sends for help from his tower: “Go, brilliant Thootes, run and call Ajax, both of them, rather” (12.343–344; [[thootes]], [[heralds]]). The vocative δῖε is an address of honor to a companion or helper, as when Achilles calls Patroclus “Brilliant son of Menoetius” (11.608; [[son-of-menoetius]]).
