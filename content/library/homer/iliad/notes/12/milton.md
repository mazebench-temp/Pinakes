---
summary: Satan in Paradise Lost claims the hazard of the journey out of Hell in the terms of Sarpedon's speech, that the honors of a king are owed in danger; Theodore Buckley's notes to Pope's Iliad also set Milton's battle that hung “in even scale” beside the working woman's scales, and his Son of God “Gloomy as night” beside Hector at the gate.
---

- Satan takes on the journey out of Hell alone, since he would ill become his throne if he refused “as great a share / Of hazard as of honour” (*Paradise Lost* 2.445–456): [[sarpedons-speech-in-english]].
- **The even scale.** The battle at the wall hangs level, as a working woman holds the balance of weight and wool until they are equal, “so evenly was their battle and war stretched tight” (12.433–436). In the war in heaven, “Long time in even scale / The battle hung” (*Paradise Lost* 6.245–246); Buckley's notes set the lines side by side (see [[the-working-womans-scales]] and [[the-scales-of-zeus]]).
- **Gloomy as night.** Hector leaps through the gate “his face like swift night” (12.463), and Pope's version makes him “Gloomy as night!”, the words with which Milton's Son of God drives his chariot against the rebel angels (*Paradise Lost* 6.831–832; see [[pope]] and [[like-night]]).
