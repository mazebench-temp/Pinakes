---
summary: αἰόλος describes two living things of the book's similes and omens: the wasps “with nimble waists” of Asius' simile and the “writhing snake” that falls among the Trojans.
grc: [αἰόλοι]
en: [writhing, nimble]
---

**The wasps.** Asius complains that the two Lapiths hold the gate “like wasps with nimble waists, or bees, that have made their homes beside a rocky road” (12.167–168; [[wasps-at-the-roadside]], [[rocky]]). σφῆκες μέσον αἰόλοι is literally “wasps quick at the middle,” for their narrow, supple waists, or “glinting at the middle,” for their banded bodies; the word holds both movement and changing light.

**The snake.** The eagle drops its prey among the Trojans, “and the Trojans shuddered when they saw the writhing snake lying in their midst, the portent of aegis-bearing Zeus” (12.208–209; [[the-eagle-and-the-serpent]], [[aegis-bearing]]). Here the sense is the snake's movement, still struggling after the eagle let it fall (12.203).

The translation renders the word by its sense in each place, “nimble” and “writhing”; both are added to the entry's English, and the plural αἰόλοι to its forms. See [[serpent]].
