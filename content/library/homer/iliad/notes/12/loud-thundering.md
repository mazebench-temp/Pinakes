---
summary: Hector refuses to forget “loud-thundering Zeus' counsels, which he himself promised me,” and sets them against the bird of omen.
---

Hector's answer to Polydamas begins with the god: “you who tell me to forget loud-thundering Zeus' counsels, which he himself promised me and nodded his assent to; and you tell me to obey long-winged birds” (12.235–237; [[boule]], [[long-winged]]). Zeus had sent his promise through Iris, power to kill until Hector reached the ships and the sun went down (11.200–209); Hector speaks of it as given by the god himself and sealed with his nod, the sign that bound Zeus to Thetis (1.524–528). The god of thunder whose word he trusts is the god whose portent lies before him, “the portent of aegis-bearing Zeus” (12.209; [[aegis-bearing]], [[the-eagle-and-the-serpent]]).
