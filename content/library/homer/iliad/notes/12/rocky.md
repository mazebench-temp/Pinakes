---
summary: In Asius' simile the wasps or bees make their homes “beside a rocky road,” with παιπαλόεις, which the translation also renders “rocky.”
grc: [παιπαλοέσσῃ]
---

Asius complains that the two Lapiths will not give way from the gate, “like wasps with nimble waists, or bees, that have made their homes beside a rocky road, and do not leave their hollow house, but stand their ground and fight off the hunting men for the sake of their young” (12.167–170; [[wasps-at-the-roadside]], [[flashing]]). The adjective is παιπαλόεις, not πετρήεις, the headword of this entry, but the translation renders it “rocky” as it renders κλωμακόεσσα in the Catalogue (2.729). Homer gives παιπαλόεις to roads and paths (12.168, 17.743; Od. 17.204), to a mountain (13.17), to the lookouts from which Odysseus spies out the land (Od. 10.97, 10.148, 10.194), and to islands: Imbros (13.33, 24.78), Chios (Od. 3.170), Samos (Od. 4.671, 4.845, 15.29) and Ithaca (Od. 11.480). This is its first use in the Iliad. Its origin and exact sense are uncertain; it is usually understood as “rugged” or “craggy.”
