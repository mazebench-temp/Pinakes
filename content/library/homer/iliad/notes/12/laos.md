---
summary: The two Lapiths at the gate fight “trusting in the men above them”, the eagle comes on the left “skirting the army”, and Polydamas speaks of a prophet “whom the people would obey”.
grc: [λαοῖσιν]
---

**The men above.** Polypoetes and Leonteus, out in front of the gate, fight very fiercely, “trusting in the men above them and in their own strength” (λαοῖσιν καθύπερθε πεποιθότες, 12.152–153; see [[polypoetes]], [[leonteus]] and [[lapiths]]). The λαοί are the Achaeans on the towers, who throw down stones as thick as snowflakes (12.154–161; see [[stone-throwing]]).

**The army.** The eagle comes “on the left, skirting the army” (ἐπʼ ἀριστερὰ λαὸν ἐέργων, 12.201 = 12.219). λαὸν ἐέργων is literally “shutting in the army”, and is taken of a flight along the flank of the Trojan host (see the translation's note on 12.200–209 and [[the-eagle-and-the-serpent]]).

**The people who obey.** Polydamas wants his reading heard as a prophet's would be, a prophet “whom the people would obey” (καί οἱ πειθοίατο λαοί, 12.229; see [[theopropion]] and [[obedience-and-persuasion]]). The people follow Hector instead: he leads the way, “and they followed with him / with a wondrous din” (12.251–252; see [[hector]]).
