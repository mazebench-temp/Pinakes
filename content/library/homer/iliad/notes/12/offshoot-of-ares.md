---
summary: “Leonteus, offshoot of Ares,” the Lapith of the Catalogue, kills Hippomachus at the left gate and then four more Trojans.
---

“And Leonteus, offshoot of Ares, struck the son of Antimachus, Hippomachus, with his spear, hitting him at the belt” (12.188–189; [[leonteus]], [[hippomachus]], [[antimachus]]). Then he draws his sword and kills Antiphates, Menon, Iamenus and Orestes, “all of them, one after another” (12.190–194; [[antiphates]], [[menon]], [[iamenus]], [[orestes-the-trojan]]). The phrase that gave him a warrior's dignity in the Catalogue (2.745) now goes with a run of killings. In this book Leonteus is twice measured by the war god: he was “the equal of Ares, ruin of mortals” when the narrator introduced him at the gate (12.130; [[ruin-of-mortals]], [[equal-of-ares]]). See [[ares]] and [[lapiths]].
