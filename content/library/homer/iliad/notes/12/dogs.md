---
summary: The dogs of Book 12 are hunting dogs and sheepdogs in three similes: the hounds around Hector's boar or lion (12.41), the dogs whose din two wild boars await in the mountains (12.147), and the dogs that guard the fold against Sarpedon's lion (12.303).
grc: [κύνεσσι]
---

The hounds of 12.41, ἔν τε κύνεσσι καὶ ἀνδράσι θηρευτῇσι, “among hounds and hunting men”, are the Achaeans as a hunting party, standing in close order against the beast that is Hector (see [[the-boar-or-lion-at-bay]]). A hundred verses later the roles are reversed: the dogs that come on with the men against two wild boars are the Trojans, and the boars are the Lapiths at the gate (ἀνδρῶν ἠδὲ κυνῶν, 12.147; see [[wild-boars-in-the-mountains]]). In Sarpedon's simile the dogs are the guard of the fold, with the herdsmen and their spears (σὺν κυσὶ καὶ δούρεσσι, 12.303; see [[the-mountain-lion-and-the-fold]]), like the watchdogs of the night in Book 10 (10.183–189; see [[the-watchdogs]]).

The κυνέη, the helmet of 12.183 and 12.384, takes its name from the dog: it was once a cap of dog-skin, though in the Iliad a κυνέη is of bronze or of hide (12.183–184; 10.257–262; see [[helmet]] and the translation's note on 12.183).
