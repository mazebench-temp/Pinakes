---
summary: The boar or lion to which Hector is compared at the ditch turns among the hunters “exulting in his strength.”
---

“And as when among hounds and hunting men a boar or a lion turns this way and that, exulting in his strength, and they, arraying themselves in close order like a tower, stand against him and hurl thick and fast their spears from their hands” (12.41–45; [[the-boar-or-lion-at-bay]], [[boar]], [[lion]], [[dogs]]). The formula is in its usual place, the beast at bay among the hunters; in Book 8 the same picture was turned around and Hector was the hound (8.338–342). Here Hector is the beast, and the simile ends with the cost of his courage: “it is his pride that kills him” (12.46; [[proud]], [[kydalimos]]). See [[hector]].
