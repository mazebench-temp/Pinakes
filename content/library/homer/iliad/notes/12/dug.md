---
summary: Polydamas fears that if the Achaeans turn, the Trojans will be caught in “the ditch they have dug,” a fear that comes true for the Achaeans in Book 15.
grc: [ὀρυκτῇ]
---

Polydamas' first counsel weighs the ditch: “but if they turn back, and a counterattack comes from the ships, and we plunge into the ditch they have dug, then I think not even a messenger will get back to the city again” (12.71–74; [[polydamas]], [[the-achaean-wall]]). The verb and the epithet come back together in Book 15, of the other side: driven back by the Trojans, the Achaeans “plunging into the ditch and the stakes” (τάφρῳ καὶ σκολόπεσσιν ἐνιπλήξαντες ὀρυκτῇ, 15.344) flee this way and that inside the wall. In Book 16 the ditch traps the Trojans as Polydamas foresaw, when they flee from Patroclus (16.369). The dative ὀρυκτῇ is added to the entry's forms. See [[sons-of-the-achaeans]] and [[horses]].
