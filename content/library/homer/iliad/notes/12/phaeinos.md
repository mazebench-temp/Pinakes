---
summary: The “shining bronze” rings on the Lapiths' chests as stones hit them, and Teucer's arrow hits “the shining strap” across Sarpedon's chest.
---

Both shining things are on the chest. At the gate the stones fall on the two Lapiths: “so rang the shining bronze on the chests of these two as they were hit face on; for they fought very fiercely, trusting in the men above them and in their own strength” (12.151–153; [[polypoetes]], [[leonteus]], [[wild-boars-in-the-mountains]]). At Menestheus' tower Teucer's arrow “hit the shining strap around his chest of his man-covering shield” (12.401–402; [[teucer]], [[sarpedon]], [[man-covering]]). The bronze rings under the stones; the strap is struck, and Zeus keeps death from his son (12.402–403). See [[bronze]] and [[shield]].
