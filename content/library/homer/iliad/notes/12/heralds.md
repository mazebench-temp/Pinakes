  - 12.331-364 | Menestheus sends his herald to the Ajaxes

**A herald in battle.** When the Lycians come at his tower, Menestheus looks along the rampart for a leader who might ward off disaster, and sees the two Ajaxes and Teucer close by, “but there was no way for him to make them hear by shouting, / so great was the din” of shields, helmets and gates under the blows (12.331–341; see [[menestheus]]). So “Quickly he sent the herald Thootes to Ajax” (12.342; see [[thootes]]). The heralds who act in the poem so far are mostly the kings' men, Talthybius and Eurybates for Agamemnon and Idaeus for Priam; Thootes belongs to Menestheus, the leader of the Athenians (2.552). Menestheus calls him δῖε Θοῶτα, “brilliant Thootes” (12.343; see [[brilliant]]), as Talthybius is θεῖος, “divine”, in Book 4 (4.192), and gives him a runner's errand: “run and call Ajax” (θέων, 12.343).

**The verse of Talthybius.** “So he spoke, and the herald heard him and did not disobey” (12.351) is the verse in which Talthybius set out to fetch Machaon for the wounded Menelaus (4.198; see [[talthybius]]). The two errands are told alike. Each herald is sent in the middle of the fighting to find a man, goes along the army, Talthybius “through the army of the bronze-armored Achaeans” (4.199) and Thootes “running along the wall of the bronze-armored Achaeans” (12.352), finds the man where he stands, and gives him his master's words, changed only as delivery requires (4.199–207, 12.353–363; see [[message-repeated]] and [[messenger-scene]]). Like Talthybius (4.204), Thootes names the sender: “the dear son of Peteos, whom Zeus has nurtured, bids you” (12.355; see [[peteos]] and [[zeus-nurtured]]).

The message is obeyed at once: Telamonian Ajax leaves the son of Oileus and Lycomedes to hold their place and goes with Teucer (12.364–372; see [[ajax]] and [[the-two-ajaxes]]). The herald is not mentioned again.
