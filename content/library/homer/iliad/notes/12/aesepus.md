# Αἴσηπος occurs in Homer only here; Αἴσηπον (6.21) is Bucolion's son (aesepus-son-of-bucolion).
grc: [Αἴσηπος]
passages:
  - 12.17-23 | The rivers of Ida
---

Γρήνικός τε καὶ Αἴσηπος, “the Granicus and Aesepus” (12.21; see [[granicus]]). In the Catalogue the Aesepus is the river whose “dark water” the men of Zeleia drink (2.825; see [[zeleia]]); here it is one of the eight “that flow from the mountains of Ida to the sea” and bring the Achaean wall to nothing (12.19; see [[the-achaean-wall]]). [[strabo|Strabo]] makes the Granicus and the Aesepus run north to the Propontis (13.1.43), far from the shore of the camp, and the narrator has Apollo turn “the mouths of them all together” against the wall (12.24; see [[apollo]]).

Hesiod has the same pair in the same order: “the Granicus and Aesepus and divine Simoeis” (*Theogony* 342; see [[hesiod]]). Strabo, reporting Demetrius of Scepsis, says that the Aesepus rises on Cotylus, a height of Ida, close to the springs of the Scamander and the Granicus, and that of the three its mouth lies farthest from its source, almost five hundred stades (13.1.43). The Caresus flows into it, he says, and some said that the Rhodius does too (13.1.44, 13.1.28; see [[caresus]] and [[rhodius]]).
