---
summary: Arisbe is named in the Catalogue's verses on Asius, repeated as he joins the third company: “whom his horses carried from Arisbe” (12.96 = 2.838).
---

Ἄσιος Ὑρτακίδης, ὃν Ἀρίσβηθεν φέρον ἵπποι / αἴθωνες μεγάλοι ποταμοῦ ἄπο Σελλήεντος, “Asius, son of Hyrtacus, whom his horses carried from Arisbe, / great and tawny horses, from the river Selleis” (12.96–97 = 2.838–839; see [[asius]], [[selleis-arisbe]] and [[tawny]]). The horses that brought him from Arisbe are the ones he will not leave at the ditch, “glorying in his horses and chariot” (12.110–115), and in Book 13 Antilochus drives them off to the Achaeans (13.396–401).
