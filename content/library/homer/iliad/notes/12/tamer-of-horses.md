---
summary: Hector, inside the wall, calls on the “horse-taming Trojans” to break it down, though they have left their horses at the ditch.
---

“Rise up, horse-taming Trojans, break through the wall of the Argives, and throw the wondrous fire on the ships” (12.440–441; [[hector]], [[wondrous]], [[exhortation]]). The call begins with the words Apollo shouted to the Trojans from Pergamus in the first battle, ὄρνυσθʼ ἱππόδαμοι Τρῶες (4.509). The horse-tamers of the formula are fighting on foot: on Polydamas' advice they left their horses with their charioteers at the ditch (12.75–87), and the one man who drove his chariot to the wall, Asius, was called a fool for it (12.110–115).
