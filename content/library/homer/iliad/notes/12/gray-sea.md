---
summary: In the simile of the snow, the snow lies even on the harbors and shores of “the gray sea,” where only the waves hold it off.
---

Zeus snows until he has covered the mountain peaks, the headlands, the lotus plains and the tilled fields, “and it is poured over the harbors and shores of the gray sea, but the wave that washes up against it holds it off; and all else is wrapped over from above, when the rain of Zeus falls heavy” (12.284–286; [[the-great-snowfall]], [[counselor]]). The gray of the sea near the shore, the color of foam on the water, meets the white of the snow at the one place it cannot cover. So the stones fly thick from both sides at the wall (12.287–289; [[stone-throwing]]). See [[sea]].
