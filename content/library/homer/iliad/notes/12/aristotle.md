---
summary: Aristotle cites Hector's “one omen is best” in the Rhetoric as a well-worn maxim for a man urging others into danger without a sacrifice, and is reported to have said that the poet who invented the Achaean wall made it vanish, as the opening of Book 12 does. The Rhetoric also counts calling Achilles “one of the demigods” among the commonplaces of praise.
---

- “One omen is best” as a hackneyed but useful maxim, beside “Enyalius is impartial”, Hector's other answer to Polydamas (*Rhetoric* 2.21.11, 1395a): [[aristotle-one-omen]].
- The wall that never existed, which the poet who invented it made vanish, as Strabo reports him (*Geography* 13.1.36): [[aristotle-and-the-wall]].
- **One of the demigods.** Praise, Aristotle says, must be drawn from what is proper to its object. To praise Achilles because he is a man, “one of the demigods” (τῶν ἡμιθέων), and joined the expedition against Ilium is to say what is true of many others, and praises him no more than Diomedes; what is proper to Achilles alone is to have killed Hector, the best of the Trojans, and Cycnus, who was invulnerable and kept the whole army from landing (*Rhetoric* 2.22.12). Homer uses the word once, of the men who fell by the Simoeis, “the race of men half-divine” (ἡμιθέων γένος ἀνδρῶν, 12.23; see [[hemitheos]]).
