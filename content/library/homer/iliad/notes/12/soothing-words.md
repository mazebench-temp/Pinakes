---
summary: The two Ajaxes range along the towers rebuking the men who hold back, “one with soothing words, another with harsh words.”
---

“And the two Ajaxes, calling out orders, ranged along the towers in every direction, urging on the might of the Achaeans. One with soothing words, another with harsh words they rebuked, whomever they saw holding back from the battle altogether” (12.265–268; [[the-two-ajaxes]], [[exhortation]]). μειλίχιος is set against στερεός, “hard, harsh,” which describes words only here in Homer. The two kinds of speech are the ones Agamemnon used at the review, angry words for the slack and soothing words for Idomeneus (4.241, 4.256); here both are used to rebuke, and the speech that follows addresses every rank, “outstanding, or middling, or worse” (12.269–270; [[not-all-alike-in-war]]). See [[neikos]].
