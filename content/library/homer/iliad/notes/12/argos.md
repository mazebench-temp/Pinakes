---
summary: Polydamas, advising the Trojans at the ditch, would wish the Achaeans to “perish here, nameless, far from Argos”, if Zeus means to destroy them (12.67–70).
---

νωνύμνους ἀπολέσθαι ἀπʼ Ἄργεος ἐνθάδʼ Ἀχαιούς, “that the Achaeans should perish here, nameless, far from Argos” (12.70). Argos is the homeland of the whole army, as when Odysseus feared that they would “perish in Troy, far from horse-pasturing Argos” (9.246; see [[horse-pasturing]]). νώνυμνος, “nameless”, is the opposite of the fame for which men die at Troy (see [[kleos]]). Polydamas speaks the verse as a wish he does not trust, for the Achaeans may yet turn on the Trojans at the ditch (12.67–74; see [[polydamas]]). The Achaeans speak it twice in fear, each time after a guess at Zeus' will: Idomeneus, to Poseidon in the likeness of Thoas (13.226–227), and Agamemnon, when the wall has fallen (14.66–70; see [[idomeneus]] and [[agamemnon]]).
