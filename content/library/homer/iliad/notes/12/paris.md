τῶν δʼ ἑτέρων Πάρις ἦρχε, “the second were led by Paris” (12.93). The poem calls him Paris here, the name it uses far less often than Alexander (see [[paris]]). He leads with Alcathous, Anchises' son-in-law, and Agenor, son of Antenor (see [[alcathous]] and [[agenor]]); his brother Hector leads the first company, and his brothers Helenus and Deiphobus the third (12.88–95; see [[sons-of-priam]] and [[the-fight-at-the-wall]]). He is not named again in the book. In Book 13 Aeneas calls Deiphobus, Paris and Agenor to his side over the body of Alcathous (13.489–490), and Hector, searching for the leaders of the third company, finds Paris on the left of the battle and rebukes him in the words of their first meeting in Book 3 (13.765–773; 13.769 = 3.39; see [[evil-paris]]).
