---
summary: The narrator measures the Achaean wall by Priam's city, which stood “unsacked” while the wall stood and was “sacked in the tenth year” before the gods destroyed it (12.11, 12.15). Five of his sons lead against the wall, and Hector breaks the gate as “son of Priam” (12.88–95, 12.438).
---

**His city.** The wall stood firm while Hector lived, Achilles kept up his wrath, “and the city of lord Priam was unsacked” (12.10–12); Poseidon and Apollo destroyed it after “the city of Priam was sacked in the tenth year” and the Argives had gone home (12.15–18; see [[troy]] and [[the-achaean-wall]]). The king himself does not appear in the book. His city's fall is told as past, before the battle at the wall begins.

**His sons.** Five of the fifteen leaders of the five companies are his sons: Hector and Cebriones in the first, Paris in the second, and Helenus and Deiphobus, “two sons of Priam”, in the third (12.88–95; see [[sons-of-priam]], [[hector]], [[cebriones]], [[paris]], [[helenus]] and [[deiphobus]]). Book 16 calls Cebriones Priam's bastard son (16.738). When the battle at the wall has hung level, Zeus gives the higher glory “to Hector, / son of Priam, who was the first to leap inside the wall of the Achaeans” (12.437–438; see [[son-of-priam]]).
