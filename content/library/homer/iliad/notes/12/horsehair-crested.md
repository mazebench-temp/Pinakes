---
summary: The din at Menestheus' tower is so great that he cannot be heard, as stones strike shields, “helmets with horsehair crests,” and the gates; the book adds a fourth compound of horsehair, ἱππόκομος.
grc: [ἱπποκόμων]
en: [with horsehair crests]
---

Menestheus sees the two Ajaxes and Teucer close by, “but there was no way for him to make them hear by shouting, so great was the din, and the shout reached the sky, as shields were struck, and helmets with horsehair crests, and the gates” (12.337–340; [[menestheus]], [[helmet]]). ἱππόκομος = ἵππος (“horse”) + κόμη (“hair”): “with horsehair,” another word for the crested helmet beside ἵππουρις and ἱπποδάσεια. This is its first use; it returns in the verse of the closed ranks, ψαῦον δʼ ἱππόκομοι κόρυθες λαμπροῖσι φάλοισι, “the helmets with horsehair crests touched with their bright ridges” (13.132 = 16.216), and in the fighting of Book 16 (16.338, 16.797). The genitive ἱπποκόμων and the translation's “with horsehair crests” are added to the entry. See [[thootes]].
