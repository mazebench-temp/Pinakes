---
summary: Antimachus is named as the father of Hippomachus, whom Leonteus spears at the left gate (12.188–189). The poem does not say whether he is the Antimachus of Book 11.
---

υἱὸν δʼ Ἀντιμάχοιο Λεοντεὺς ὄζος Ἄρηος / Ἱππόμαχον βάλε δουρὶ, “And Leonteus, offshoot of Ares, struck the son of Antimachus, / Hippomachus, with his spear” (12.188–189; see [[hippomachus]] and [[leonteus]]). The father comes first, as he did when Agamemnon met “the sons of wise-hearted Antimachus” (υἱέας Ἀντιμάχοιο δαΐφρονος, 11.123). The only other Antimachus in Homer is the man of Book 11, who took Alexander's gold to keep Helen in Troy and once urged the Trojans to kill the envoy Menelaus, and Agamemnon killed his sons Peisander and Hippolochus for it (11.122–147; see [[sons-of-antimachus]]). If the father of Hippomachus is the same man, he has lost a third son to the Achaeans; the poem does not make the connection.
