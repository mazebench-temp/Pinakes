---
summary: Zeus gives the higher glory to “Hector, son of Priam, who was the first to leap inside the wall of the Achaeans.”
---

The even battle at the wall ends with a gift of Zeus: “until Zeus gave the higher glory to Hector, son of Priam, who was the first to leap inside the wall of the Achaeans” (12.437–438; [[kydos]], [[the-working-womans-scales]]). The patronymic is run over to the start of the verse, as when the narrator asked whom Hector killed “when Zeus gave him glory” in Book 11 (11.299–300; [[whom-first-whom-last]]). Zeus' promise to Hector is being kept in nearly the same words. It is the only use of the patronymic in the book; Priam's sons are otherwise named as “two sons of Priam,” Helenus and Deiphobus (12.94–95; [[sons-of-priam]]). See [[hector]] and [[priam]].
