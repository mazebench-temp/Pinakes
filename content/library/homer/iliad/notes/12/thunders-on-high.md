---
summary: Polydamas names “Zeus who thunders on high” as the god who may mean the Achaeans evil and wish to help the Trojans.
---

Polydamas' first counsel at the ditch turns on the god's will: “For if, meaning them evil, he is destroying them utterly, Zeus who thunders on high, and wishes to help the Trojans, I for my part would wish it to happen even now, that the Achaeans should perish here, nameless, far from Argos” (12.67–70; [[polydamas]]). The other alternative, a rout back through the ditch, is why he advises the Trojans to leave their chariots (12.71–79).

In all six of its uses in Homer, Ζεὺς ὑψιβρεμέτης opens a verse, the subject held back from the verse before, as in Achilles' prayer (1.353–354). The same turn as here returns when Ajax knows that Zeus who thunders on high is cutting short his plans of battle, Τρώεσσι δὲ βούλετο νίκην (“and wished victory for the Trojans,” 16.121), and Nestor uses the title when the wall has fallen, a thing that not even Zeus could now make otherwise (14.53–55).
