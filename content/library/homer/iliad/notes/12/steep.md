---
summary: Menestheus warns that “sheer destruction will soon be wrought here,” and his herald repeats it with “there.”
---

Menestheus asks for both Ajaxes, “for that would be by far the best of all, since sheer destruction will soon be wrought here” (12.344–345; [[menestheus]]). Thootes gives the message to the Ajaxes in the same words, but from where he stands: “since sheer destruction will soon be wrought there” (12.357–358; [[thootes]], [[heralds]]). The figurative αἰπὺς ὄλεθρος, a fall with no way back, is the danger at Menestheus' tower, where the Lycians are climbing the battlements (12.375–376; [[lycians]]). See [[the-two-ajaxes]].
