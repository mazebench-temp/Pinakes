summary: Book 12 counts its dead in quick lists: the eight Trojans the Lapiths kill at the gate, Epicles and Alcmaon at Menestheus' tower, and the many nameless men stabbed across the battlements. It foretells the death of Asius and the end of a whole race of men, and Sarpedon gives the reason why men go to meet death in the front rank.

**The dead of the war.** The book begins at the war's end. When the gods destroy the wall, “the best of the Trojans had died, / and of the Argives many were beaten down and some were left” (12.13–14), and the Simoeis has seen fall “the race of men half-divine” (12.22–23; see [[hemitheos]] and [[men-of-today]]). One death is told before its time: Asius will not escape the evil fates, for “fate of evil name enfolded him / through the spear of Idomeneus” (12.113–117; see [[asius]] and [[moira]]).

**A list at the gate.** The fighting at the gate is told as a list. Polypoetes kills Damasus with a spear through his bronze-cheeked helmet, so that the brain inside was all spattered, and then Pylon and Ormenus; Leonteus spears Hippomachus, son of Antimachus, at the belt, kills Antiphates with his sword, and brings down Menon, Iamenus and Orestes, “all of them, one after another” (12.182–194; see [[polypoetes]], [[leonteus]], [[damasus]], [[pylon]], [[ormenus-killed-by-polypoetes]], [[hippomachus]], [[antiphates]], [[menon]], [[iamenus]] and [[orestes-the-trojan]]). There are eight names in thirteen verses. Only the first death is told in full, and only one father is named; two of the dead, Iamenus and Orestes, came to the gate with Asius (12.139). Then the victors strip them of their gleaming armor (12.195; see [[stripping-the-dead]] and [[androktasia]]).

**At the tower.** At Menestheus' tower the deaths are told at more length. Ajax's boulder crushes Epicles' helmet and skull, and he, “like a diver, / fell from the high tower, and his spirit left his bones” (12.378–386; see [[epicles]] and [[thymos]]). Sarpedon stabs Alcmaon and draws the spear out, “and following the spear he fell / headlong, and around him his armor rang, inlaid with bronze” (12.394–396; see [[alcmaon]]). The rest are nameless. Many were stabbed in the flesh, whoever turned and laid his back bare and many right through the shield, and everywhere the towers and battlements “were spattered with the blood of men / on both sides” (12.427–431).

**The reason.** Between the lists Sarpedon tells Glaucus why men go to meet such deaths. Since the fates of death stand over them, countless, which no mortal can flee or escape, “let us go, and either hold out triumph to another, or another to us” (12.326–328; see [[sarpedons-speech]] and [[ker-doom]]).
