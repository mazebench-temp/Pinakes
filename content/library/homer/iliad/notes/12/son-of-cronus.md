---
summary: “The son of crooked-counseling Cronus” makes Hector's stone light, the only time Book 12 names Zeus by his father.
---

Hector carries the stone that stood before the gates, which not even two men, the best of the people, could easily heave onto a wagon, “such as mortals are now; but he wielded it easily even alone. The son of crooked-counseling Cronus made it light for him” (12.447–450; [[crooked-counseling]], [[men-of-today]]). The verse is built like the one in which Odysseus told how, at Aulis, “the son of crooked-counseling Cronus turned him to stone” (λᾶαν γάρ μιν ἔθηκε, 2.319; [[the-portent-at-aulis]]): there Zeus made a stone of the serpent, here he makes a stone light. With it Hector breaks the gate (12.453–462). See [[zeus]] and [[stone-throwing]].
