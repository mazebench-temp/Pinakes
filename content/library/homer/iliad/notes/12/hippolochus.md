---
summary: Hippolochus is named only in his son's title, as Sarpedon turns to “Glaucus, son of Hippolochus” (12.309) and as Teucer's arrow strikes “Glaucus, the mighty son of Hippolochus” (12.387).
---

Both verses end παῖδʼ Ἱππολόχοιο, “son of Hippolochus”, a variant of the title that brought Glaucus out between the armies in Book 6 and announced his kill in Book 7 (Γλαῦκος δʼ Ἱππολόχοιο πάϊς, 6.119, 7.13; see [[glaucus]]). The father who sent his son to Troy “always to be the best and to excel above the others” (6.208; see [[always-to-be-the-best]]) is named as Sarpedon begins the speech in which he tells Glaucus what the honors of the best require (12.310–328; see [[sarpedons-speech]]), and again as the son is wounded and leaves the wall (12.387–391).
