---
summary: The Argives of Book 12 are the army that will one day sail home, and that now, “beaten down by the lash of Zeus”, is penned in by its ships; troubled, they defend the ships of necessity and strengthen their battalions inside the wall (12.14–16, 12.37–39, 12.178–179, 12.415–416).
refs: [12.38-39, 12.179, 12.416]
---

In the narrator's look past the end of the war, many of the Argives were beaten down and some were left, and the rest “had gone in their ships to their dear native land” before Poseidon and Apollo swept their wall away (12.14–16; see [[the-achaean-wall]]). In the battle they are the army under pressure. “Beaten down by the lash of Zeus”, they are “penned in beside the hollow ships” in fear of Hector (12.37–39; see [[master-of-rout]] and [[hollow-ships]]); and while the Lapiths hold the gate, “the Argives, troubled though they were, of necessity / defended the ships” (12.178–179). Their own leaders call them by this name: the two Ajaxes rally “whoever among the Argives is outstanding, or middling, / or worse” (12.269–270; see [[the-two-ajaxes]]), and the herald Thootes greets the Ajaxes as “leaders of the bronze-armored Argives” (12.354; see [[bronze-armored]]). Against Sarpedon “the Argives on the other side strengthened their battalions / inside the wall” (12.415–416), in the verse with which they met Hector's rally in Book 11 (11.215). Hector names them too: he speaks of “the ships of the Argives” (12.246) and bids the Trojans break “the wall / of the Argives” and throw fire on the ships (12.440–441).
