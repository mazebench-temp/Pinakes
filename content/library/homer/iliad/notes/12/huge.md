---
summary: The serpent the eagle carries is “monstrous,” πέλωρος, the simple adjective from which πελώριος is formed.
grc: [πέλωρον]
en: [monstrous]
---

“Carrying in its talons a blood-red serpent, monstrous, alive and still struggling” (12.202–203; [[the-eagle-and-the-serpent]], [[blood-red]], [[serpent]]); Polydamas repeats the first verse when he reads the sign (12.220). πέλωρος, like πελώριος, comes from πέλωρ (“monster, prodigy”), and the prodigy is in the word here: the creature is the portent of Zeus (12.209; [[teras]]). The half-verse φέρων ὀνύχεσσι πέλωρον returns in the Odyssey, of an eagle that flies by on the right carrying off a goose, a portent that Helen reads (Od. 15.160–161, 15.171–178). The translation renders the word “monstrous,” which is added to the entry's English. See [[eagle]].
