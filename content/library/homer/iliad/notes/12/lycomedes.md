---
summary: When Telamonian Ajax goes to help Menestheus, he leaves “mighty Lycomedes” with the son of Oileus to hold their place on the wall and urge on the Danaans (12.366–367).
refs: [12.367]
---

“Ajax, you two stay here, you and mighty Lycomedes, / and stand and urge the Danaans to fight with all their might” (12.366–367; see [[ajax]] and [[ajax-son-of-oileus]]). He is the only man besides his namesake whom the great Ajax names as he leaves, and the order is the one Agamemnon said the two Ajaxes needed no one to give (4.287; see [[the-two-ajaxes]]). κρατερός, “mighty” (see [[krateros]]), is his epithet here; in Book 9 he was “brilliant Lycomedes”, the last of the seven captains of the watch who sat with their men “midway between the ditch and the wall” (9.84–87; see [[night-watch]]). On the third day of battle he holds the wall itself. He is not named again in the book.
