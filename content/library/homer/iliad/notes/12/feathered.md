---
summary: The light hide shields struck over the battlements are “fluttering,” λαισήϊα πτερόεντα, as in the fight over the phantom of Aeneas.
---

“And over them they hacked at the oxhide shields around one another's chests, the well-rounded shields and the fluttering bucklers of hide” (12.425–426; [[well-rounded]], [[battlements]]). The verse is the one of Book 5 (5.453), and πτερόεις here is the other use the entry describes, not the feathers of arrows: the λαισήϊα, light shields of hide, are “winged” or “fluttering,” for their lightness or for their fluttering fringes. In this book the arrows are Teucer's, but they are not called feathered (12.387–389, 12.400–402; [[teucer]]). See [[shield]].
