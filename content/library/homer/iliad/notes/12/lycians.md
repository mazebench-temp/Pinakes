**The people and their kings.** In Sarpedon's speech the Lycians are the people who honor their kings and judge them. The kings must take their stand “among the foremost Lycians” (Λυκίοισι μέτα πρώτοισιν, 12.315; again 12.321), so that “one of the close-armored Lycians may say this”: that their kings rule “not without glory”, since there is strength in them too (12.317–321; see [[sarpedon]], [[sarpedons-speech]] and [[close-armored]]).

**At the tower.** Sarpedon and Glaucus lead “the great host of the Lycians” against Menestheus' tower (12.330–332). Menestheus sends for the Ajaxes, for “the leaders of the Lycians have pressed on so hard, who even before now / have been violent in the fierce battles” (12.346–347 = 12.359–360; see [[menestheus]] and [[violent]]); the poem has shown them in battle before, with Sarpedon at their head in Book 5 (5.471–698). “The mighty leaders and rulers of the Lycians” climb onto the battlements “like a dark storm” (12.375–376; see [[like-a-black-storm]]). Ajax kills Epicles, Teucer wounds Glaucus, and Sarpedon pulls a battlement away (12.378–399; see [[epicles]] and [[glaucus]]). Then he turns on his men:

{{quote:ebade78c-2406-5a38-be99-a48177874dba}}

ἀντιθέοισιν … Λυκίοισιν, “the godlike Lycians”, gives the whole people the epithet of their king (12.307; see [[antitheos]]). The rebuke works, but not enough: “neither could the mighty Lycians break the Danaans' / wall and make a path to the ships, / nor could the Danaan spearmen ever thrust the Lycians / back from the wall” (12.417–420). The battlements keep the two sides apart as two men with measuring rods contend over boundary stones in a common field (12.421–424; see [[the-boundary-dispute]] and [[battlements]]).

**Later.** In Book 16 Sarpedon calls on the godlike Lycians in nearly the same verse, now to stop their flight from Patroclus (16.421 ≈ 12.408), and when he falls “not even the mighty Lycians” hold their ground, for they see their king lying among the dead (16.659–662). Glaucus, rebuking Hector over the body, threatens to take them home (17.154–155).
