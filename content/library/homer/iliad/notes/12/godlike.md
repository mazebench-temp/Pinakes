---
summary: Deiphobus enters the poem as “godlike Deiphobus,” with θεοειδής, among the leaders of the third column.
---

“The third by Helenus and godlike Deiphobus, two sons of Priam; and a third with them was Asius, the hero” (12.94–95; [[deiphobus]], [[helenus]], [[asius]]). It is Deiphobus' first appearance in the poem, and θεοειδής, Paris' standing epithet in Book 3, gives him the looks of a prince ([[sons-of-priam]]). In the Odyssey Helen's companion at the wooden horse is “godlike Deiphobus” with the other word of this entry, Δηΐφοβος θεοείκελος (Od. 4.276).
