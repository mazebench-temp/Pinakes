---
summary: Hector calls Zeus the god “who is lord over all mortals and immortals.”
grc: ["θνητοῖσι καὶ ἀθανάτοισιν"]
en: [mortals]
---

Hector answers Polydamas' reading of the bird with the rank of the god who promised him the day: “But let us obey the counsel of great Zeus, who is lord over all mortals and immortals” (12.241–242; [[immortals]], [[boule]]). The polar pair, θνητοί and ἀθάνατοι, takes in the whole world, as θνητοὶ ἄνθρωποι did beside the gods in Achilles' call for witnesses (1.339). Two verses later Hector's own maxim puts the bird aside: “One omen is best: to fight in defense of our native land” (12.243; [[one-omen-is-best]]). See [[zeus]] and [[hector]].
