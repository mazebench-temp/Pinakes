---
summary: Hector does not care whether the birds fly to the right, toward the dawn and the sun, or to the left, “toward the misty darkness.”
---

“And you tell me to obey long-winged birds, which I do not heed at all, nor do I care whether they go to the right, toward the dawn and the sun, or to the left, toward the misty darkness” (12.237–240; [[long-winged]], [[divination]], [[hector]]). The two directions are east and west. The same pair names the two quarters of the world in the Odyssey, “toward the dawn and the sun” and “toward the misty darkness” (Od. 13.240–241; compare Od. 9.25–26). ζόφος ἠερόεις is elsewhere the darkness of the dead, the share of Hades (15.191) and the place to which the dead go down (23.51); for Hector it is only the unlucky side, which he refuses to fear (12.243; [[one-omen-is-best]]).
