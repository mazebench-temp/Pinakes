---
summary: Sarpedon names Lycia twice in his speech to Glaucus, as the land where the two of them are honored like gods (12.312) and which they rule as kings who must earn their honors in the front line (12.318).
---

ἐν Λυκίῃ, “in Lycia” (12.312): the honors of the kings belong to the land at home, far from Troy, where the two cousins are given a seat of honor, meat and full cups, the regard of all “as gods”, and a domain by the Xanthus (12.310–314; see [[xanthus-of-lycia]] and [[lycians]]). Λυκίην κάτα κοιρανέουσιν, “rule over Lycia” (12.318), is the praise that Sarpedon imagines a Lycian giving them, if they fight among the foremost (see [[sarpedon]] and [[sarpedons-speech]]). When Sarpedon has fallen, Glaucus says that he “guarded Lycia with his judgments and his strength” (16.542), and Sleep and Death carry his body home “to the rich land of wide Lycia” (16.683; see [[wide-lycia]]).
