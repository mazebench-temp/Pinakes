---
summary: The stakes that balk the Trojan horses at the ditch were planted by “the sons of the Achaeans.”
---

The horses will not cross the ditch, for its banks overhang and on top it is fitted “with stakes, sharp ones, which the sons of the Achaeans had planted, close-set and great, a defense against enemy men” (12.55–57; [[sharp]], [[overhanging]], [[destructive]]). The stakes were part of the building in Book 7, when the Achaeans dug a deep ditch “and in it they planted stakes” (7.440–441; [[the-building-of-the-wall]], [[the-achaean-wall]]). The army's formula names the builders as a body, and Polydamas will warn the Trojans about the same stakes (12.63–64). See [[achaeans]].
