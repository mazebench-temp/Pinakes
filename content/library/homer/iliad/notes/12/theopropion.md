---
summary: Polydamas ends his reading of the eagle with what a seer would say: “So would a prophet interpret it, one who clearly in his heart / knew of portents, and whom the people would obey.”
grc: [θεοπρόπος]
en: [prophet]
---

Polydamas has read the eagle that let its serpent go as the Trojans' future: they will break the wall but not come back from the ships in good order. He ends by naming the authority his reading lacks: “So would a prophet interpret it, one who clearly in his heart / knew of portents, and whom the people would obey” (12.228–229; see [[teras]], [[divination]] and [[polydamas]]). θεοπρόπος, “prophet”, is found in the Iliad only here and in the next book, where the son of Oileus sees that the man who has urged the two Ajaxes on is not “Calchas, the prophet, the bird-diviner”, but a god (θεοπρόπος οἰωνιστής, 13.66–72; see [[calchas]]). ὑποκρίναιτο, “would interpret”, is the Odyssey's verb for reading an omen or a dream (*Odyssey* 15.170, 19.535, 19.555; see the translation's note on 12.228–229).

Polydamas does not claim to be a prophet. He says what a prophet would say, and names what a prophet has that he does not: a people that obeys him. Calchas, “who had guided the ships of the Achaeans to Ilios / by his gift of prophecy, which Phoebus Apollo had granted him” (1.71–72), had that; Polydamas has Hector, who answers that he trusts the counsels of Zeus and not birds (12.231–243; see [[one-omen-is-best]]).
