---
summary: The two Lapiths at the left gate are “high-hearted sons of Lapith spearmen.”
grc: [ὑπερθύμους]
---

Asius' men drive at the open gate with shrill cries, sure that the Achaeans will not hold, “the fools! At the gates they found two men, the best, high-hearted sons of Lapith spearmen, the one the son of Peirithous, mighty Polypoetes, the other Leonteus, the equal of Ares, ruin of mortals” (12.127–130; [[polypoetes]], [[leonteus]], [[lapiths]], [[spearmen]]). In the Catalogue the epithet went to the older generation, Leonteus' father, “high-hearted Coronus” (2.746); here the sons have it themselves. The word that is most often the Trojans' own, Τρῶες ὑπέρθυμοι (6.111, 9.233), is given to the two Achaeans who stop them. See [[catalogue-of-lapiths]] and [[nepios]].
