summary: Polydamas complains that a man of the people may not speak against Hector “neither in council / nor ever in war, but always to increase your power”; the power Hector trusts is the one Zeus promised him by Iris.
passages:
  - 12.211-215 | Polydamas' complaint
---

Before he reads the eagle, Polydamas complains that Hector always rebukes him in the assemblies when he gives good counsel, “since it is not fitting at all / for a man of the people to speak against you, neither in council / nor ever in war, but always to increase your power” (12.212–214; see [[demos]] and [[polydamas]]). σὸν δὲ κράτος αἰὲν ἀέξειν: the people's part is to add to the leader's power, not to question it, as Calchas said of the king “whom the Achaeans obey” (1.78–79; see [[obedience-and-persuasion]]). The complaint is bitter, for Polydamas is no commoner. He leads the first column beside Hector, the largest and best (12.88–89), and in Book 18 the poem calls him Hector's companion, born on the same night, the better of the two with words as Hector was with the spear (18.249–252).

The power Hector has is Zeus' to give. Iris brought him the promise in Book 11: when Agamemnon leaves the battle, Zeus “will put power in your hands” to kill until he reaches the ships (11.207). It is the promise he sets against the bird, “loud-thundering Zeus' / counsels, which he himself promised me” (12.235–236; see [[one-omen-is-best]] and [[boule]]). In this book the narrator names the gift κῦδος. Zeus' heart wished to hold out glory to Hector, and at last he “gave the higher glory to Hector” (12.174, 12.437; see [[kydos]]).
