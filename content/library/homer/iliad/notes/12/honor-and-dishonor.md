---
summary: Sarpedon's speech states the bargain of honor: the seat, the meat, the full cups and the domain that the Lycians give their kings bind the kings to fight in front. Elsewhere in the book Polydamas complains that his counsel gets no hearing, Glaucus hides his wound so that no Achaean can boast over him, and Zeus gives Hector “the higher glory”.
refs: [12.211-214, 12.310-321, 12.390-391, 12.437]
passages:
  - 12.310-321 | The honors of the Lycian kings
---

**The bargain.** Sarpedon names the honors the Lycians give him and Glaucus, “a seat of honor, with meat, and with full cups”, the regard men give to gods, and a great domain by the Xanthus, and draws the conclusion: “So now we must take our stand among the foremost Lycians” (12.310–316; see [[sarpedons-speech]] and [[time]]). Hector had used the same verse of honors to taunt Diomedes, whom the Danaans honored with them, “but now they will dishonor you” (8.161–163); Agamemnon had used Sarpedon's next verses to shame Menestheus and Odysseus, the first to be called to his feasts, for standing back from the battle (4.341–346). Sarpedon says of himself what others said to shame a man. The honor is given in advance, and the front rank is where it is earned, so that a common soldier may say that the kings rule “not without glory” (12.317–321; see [[kleos]] and [[kingship-and-authority]]).

**A hearing.** Honor in the assembly is a hearing. Polydamas complains that Hector always rebukes him “when I give good counsel”, since a man of the people may not speak against him (12.211–214; see [[demos]] and [[polydamas]]), and Hector answers him looking darkly, with a threat (12.230–250; see [[looking-darkly]]). In Book 18, when Polydamas' good counsel is rejected again, the narrator notes that no one praised him (18.312–313).

**Not to be seen.** Glaucus, hit by Teucer's arrow, leaps back from the wall unseen, “so that none of the Achaeans / should see him hit and boast over him with words” (12.390–391; see [[glaucus]] and [[euchomai]]). A wound that the enemy sees is a triumph handed to him.

**The higher glory.** Near the end of the book the visible honor of the day goes to one man: “Zeus gave the higher glory to Hector, / son of Priam, who was the first to leap inside the wall of the Achaeans” (12.437–438; see [[kydos]] and [[hector]]).
