---
summary: Within six verses the epithet is given to an Achaean and a Lycian on either side of the battlement: “great-hearted Menestheus,” whose tower the Lycians attack, and “great-hearted Epicles,” whom Ajax kills.
---

Ajax and Teucer go along inside the wall to the threatened tower: “When they came to the tower of great-hearted Menestheus, going along inside the wall, they came to men hard pressed” (12.373–374; [[menestheus]], [[the-achaean-wall]]). The first man killed there is a Lycian: “And Telamonian Ajax was the first to kill his man, Sarpedon's companion, great-hearted Epicles, hitting him with a jagged boulder” (12.378–380; [[epicles]], [[jagged]], [[telamonian]]). The defender of the tower and the attacker who dies at its battlement have the same word of praise, as the epithet is given to Trojans and Achaeans alike throughout the poem. See [[thymos]].
