---
summary: Acamas, son of Antenor, goes against the wall with his brother Archelochus in Aeneas' company, in the Catalogue's verse (12.100 = 2.823).
---

In Book 11 “young Acamas” stood with two of his brothers at the head of the Trojans (11.59–60). Here he and Archelochus go with Aeneas, “well skilled in every kind of fighting”, in the verse of the Catalogue (12.99–100 ≈ 2.822–823; see [[archelochus]], [[aeneas]], [[antenor]] and [[skilled-in-fighting]]). The Thracian Acamas of the Catalogue is another man, killed by Ajax in Book 6 (6.5–11; see [[acamas-of-thrace]]). In Book 14 this Acamas avenges Archelochus, killing the Boeotian Promachus as he drags the body away and boasting over him (14.476–485).
