summary: Patroclus is still in Eurypylus' hut, tending him, as the battle for the wall begins (12.1–2); the defenders on the towers fight for themselves, the huts and the ships (12.155); and Teucer comes to Menestheus' aid fresh from his hut (12.336).
grc: [κλισίῃσι]
---

“So in the hut the valiant son of Menoetius / was tending wounded Eurypylus” (12.1–2; see [[patroclus]] and [[eurypylus]]). The book begins where Book 11 ended, in the hut to which Patroclus led Eurypylus and where he cut out the arrow (11.842–848). The Greek is plural, ἐν κλισίῃσι, as when Eurypylus said that Machaon lay wounded “in the huts” (ἐνὶ κλισίῃσιν, 11.834); the translation gives “in the hut”, for the hut is Eurypylus' own (κλισίῃ, 15.392). The poem leaves Patroclus there for three books: in Book 15 he is still sitting “in the hut of manly Eurypylus”, cheering him with talk and spreading drugs on the wound, when he sees the Trojans rushing at the wall and leaves him to run to Achilles; the verses that tell what he saw are those of the Lapiths at the gate (15.390–404; 15.395–396 ≈ 12.143–144).

The huts stand behind the wall with the ships. The defenders on the towers hurl their stones “defending themselves and the huts / and the swift-sailing ships” (12.155–156; see [[ships]] and [[stone-throwing]]). Teucer, carried to the ships wounded in Book 8 (8.330–334), comes “from his hut” to stand beside the Ajaxes when Menestheus needs help (κλισίηθεν, 12.336; see [[teucer]] and [[bow]]).
