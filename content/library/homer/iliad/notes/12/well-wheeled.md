summary: At the ditch “a horse drawing a well-wheeled chariot could not easily go in,” but the men on foot are eager to try.
---

Hector's horses balk at the edge of the ditch, with its overhanging banks and sharp stakes: “There a horse drawing a well-wheeled chariot could not easily go in; but the men on foot were eager to see if they could accomplish it” (12.58–59; [[overhanging]], [[sharp]], [[chariot]]). The epithet of the chariot stands in the verse that rules it out, and Polydamas' advice to leave the chariots at the ditch follows (12.60–79; [[polydamas]]). It is the epithet's only use of a war chariot in the fighting; its other use of a chariot is Zeus' as he drives home from Ida (8.438). See [[horses]] and [[dug]].
