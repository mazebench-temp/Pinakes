---
summary: Pride drives the beasts of two similes: ἀγηνορίη kills the boar or lion to which Hector is compared, and the “proud spirit” of the mountain lion sends it at the fold, as Sarpedon goes at the wall.
grc: [ἀγηνορίη]
---

**Hector's simile.** The boar or lion at bay never fears or flees, “and it is his pride that kills him” (12.46; [[the-boar-or-lion-at-bay]], [[kydalimos]]). ἀγηνορίη, the noun of ἀγήνωρ, is the word Diomedes used of Achilles' pride (9.700). It is given here to the beast that stands for Hector, and Andromache will use it of Hector himself, fearing that Achilles has put an end to “the grievous pride” that kept him running ahead of the other men (ἀγηνορίης ἀλεγεινῆς, 22.457–459).

**Sarpedon's simile.** Zeus rouses Sarpedon, and he goes “like a mountain-bred lion, which has long gone without meat, and his proud spirit bids him make an attempt on the sheep, and go even into the close-built fold” (12.299–301; [[the-mountain-lion-and-the-fold]], [[mountain-bred]]). The formula θυμὸς ἀγήνωρ is the lion's, and the man it stands for goes at the wall and tears down a battlement (12.307–308, 12.397–399; [[sarpedon]]). See [[thymos]].
