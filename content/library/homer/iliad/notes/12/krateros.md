---
summary: κρατερός marks Hector as “the mighty master of rout,” the Lapith Polypoetes twice, Lycomedes, Glaucus, and the “fierce battles” of the Lycian leaders.
---

**Men.** Hector is κρατερὸν μήστωρα φόβοιο, “the mighty master of rout,” as the Argives cower by their ships (12.39; [[master-of-rout]]). The Lapith at the left gate is “the son of Peirithous, mighty Polypoetes,” both when he is introduced and when he kills Damasus (12.129, 12.182; [[polypoetes]], [[peirithous]]); elsewhere he is μενεπτόλεμος, “steadfast in battle” (2.740, 6.29, 23.836; [[steadfast-in-battle]]), and κρατερός again only at the end of the games, where his companions carry off his prize (Πολυποίταο κρατεροῖο, 23.848). Ajax leaves the son of Oileus with “mighty Lycomedes” (12.366; [[lycomedes]], [[ajax-son-of-oileus]]), and Teucer's arrow finds “Glaucus, the mighty son of Hippolochus” (12.387; [[glaucus]]).

**Battle.** Menestheus' message and Thootes' repetition of it give the Lycian leaders the same verse: they “have been violent in the fierce battles” (12.347 = 12.360; [[violent]], [[lycians]]).
