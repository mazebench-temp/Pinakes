---
summary: Zeus rouses Sarpedon against the Argives “like a lion against cattle with curving horns.”
grc: [ἕλιξιν]
---

“Not even then would the Trojans and shining Hector have broken the gates of the wall and the long bar, had not Zeus the counselor roused his own son, Sarpedon, against the Argives, like a lion against cattle with curving horns” (12.290–293; [[sarpedon]], [[counselor]], [[lion]], [[cattle]]). The comparison takes half a verse, ending with the dative βουσὶν ἕλιξιν, which is added to the entry's forms; the full simile that follows makes the prey sheep in a guarded fold (12.299–306; [[the-mountain-lion-and-the-fold]]). The Achaeans are the herd, and Sarpedon the beast that goes for it.
