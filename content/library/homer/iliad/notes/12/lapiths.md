# Λαπιθάων (12.128), Λαπίθαι (12.181); Λαπίθας at Od. 21.297.
grc: ["Λαπιθ*"]

υἷας ὑπερθύμους Λαπιθάων αἰχμητάων, “high-hearted sons of Lapith spearmen” (12.128; see [[high-hearted]] and [[spearmen]]). The phrase is built like υἷες Ἀχαιῶν, “the sons of the Achaeans”, a way of naming a people (see [[sons-of-the-achaeans]]), but these two are also sons of the Lapiths of Nestor's story: Polypoetes is the son of Peirithous, and Leonteus the grandson of Caeneus (1.262–268, 2.740–746; see [[peirithous]], [[caeneus]] and [[coronus]]). Their fathers fought “the beasts who lived in the mountains” (1.268); the sons fight before the gate like two wild boars “in the mountains” (12.146; see [[wild-boars-in-the-mountains]]).

“And the Lapiths joined in the war and the combat” (12.181). The name returns as the killing begins, and the verses that follow tell only the two leaders' kills, three for Polypoetes and five for Leonteus (12.182–194; see [[polypoetes]] and [[leonteus]]). The Iliad does not name the Lapiths again; the Odyssey names them once, in the story of the Centaur Eurytion in Peirithous' house (Od. 21.295–304; see [[centaurs]]).
