summary: Teucer, back the day after Hector's stone laid him low, goes with his brother Ajax to the tower of Menestheus while Pandion carries his bow. He shoots Glaucus in the bared arm and drives him from the wall, and his arrow hits Sarpedon's shield strap, but Zeus keeps death from his son (12.336, 12.370–403).

**Back from his hut.** The day before, Hector's stone broke Teucer's bowstring at the collarbone, and two companions carried him groaning to the ships (8.324–334). Now Menestheus sees him near the two Ajaxes, “Teucer, who had just come from his hut” (12.336; see [[hut]]). The poem does not say how he recovered.

**Wanted for his bow.** Menestheus asks for the Ajaxes, or at least for Telamonian Ajax, “and let Teucer follow with him, well skilled with the bow” (12.350 = 12.363; see [[skilled-with-the-bow]]). The two go together: “and with him went Teucer, his brother by the same father” (12.371), the phrase used of Antenor's sons Iphidamas and Coon (11.257; see [[kasignetos]]). Teucer is Telamon's bastard son (8.284; see [[nothos]] and [[telamon]]); an ancient note argued from ὄπατρος that he is no bastard in Homer and that 8.284 should be athetized (scholium T on 12.371). Pandion carries his “curved bow” (12.372, a line itself athetized in antiquity, "for why does he not carry it himself?", scholium T; see [[pandion]] and [[curved-bow]]). In Book 8 he shot from under Ajax's shield, “like a child under its mother” (8.266–272); here the brothers fight as a pair again, Ajax with the spear and Teucer with the bow (12.400).

**Glaucus.**

{{quote:15fc586a-8ecb-5201-971c-4659a010ad88}}

Glaucus leaps back unseen, to spare the Achaeans a boast over him (εὐχετόῳτο, 12.391; see [[glaucus]], [[arrows]] and [[euchomai]]), and Sarpedon grieves to see him go (12.392). The wound outlasts the day. When Sarpedon falls, Glaucus prays to Apollo, pressing his arm, and the narrator recalls the shot in the words of this scene: the wound “which Teucer had dealt him with an arrow as he rushed at the high wall, warding off disaster from his companions” (16.510–512), as Menestheus had hoped someone would (ἀρὴν ἑτάροισιν ἀμύναι, 12.334). Apollo heals it (16.527–531).

**Sarpedon.** Then Teucer shoots at Sarpedon himself:

{{quote:833f6514-96e9-57f8-8ac2-98ae50cfae38}}

In Book 8 Apollo turned his arrow from Hector (8.311), and at the ships Zeus will break his bowstring as he draws on Hector (15.458–465). Here the arrow hits, but Zeus will not have his son die at the ships; his death is kept for Patroclus' spear (16.479–505; see [[sarpedon]] and [[zeus]]). Ajax's spear drives Sarpedon back from the battlement (12.404–407; see [[ajax]]).
