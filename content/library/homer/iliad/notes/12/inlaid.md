---
summary: Alcmaon, killed by Sarpedon, falls headlong, “and around him his armor rang, inlaid with bronze.”
---

Sarpedon does not forget the joy of battle when Glaucus leaves the field: he hits Alcmaon, son of Thestor, with his spear, “and drew the spear out; and following the spear he fell headlong, and around him his armor rang, inlaid with bronze” (12.394–396; [[alcmaon]], [[sarpedon]]). The half-verse ἀμφὶ δέ οἱ βράχε τεύχεα ποικίλα χαλκῷ is a formula of the fall, used again of men killed in Books 13 and 14 (13.181, 14.420), a variant of the clatter of armor in [[fell-with-a-thud]]; τεύχεα ποικίλα χαλκῷ was the armor Paris put on to go back to the war (6.504). See [[androktasia]].
