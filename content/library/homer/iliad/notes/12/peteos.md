summary: Peteos is named twice in Book 12, as the father of Menestheus: by the narrator, when the Lycians come at Menestheus' tower, and by the herald Thootes, who calls him “Peteos, whom Zeus has nurtured” (12.331, 12.355).
relations:

---

υἱὸς Πετεῶο Μενεσθεύς, “Menestheus, son of Peteos”, is the verse-end with which the Catalogue names the son (2.552), and it returns here and in Book 13 (12.331, 13.690; see [[menestheus]]). The herald, speaking for Menestheus, names him by his father: “the dear son of Peteos, whom Zeus has nurtured, bids you / go there” (12.355–356; see [[thootes]]). The epithet, in the genitive, belongs to the father, as when Agamemnon called Peteos “the king whom Zeus has nurtured” (4.338; see [[zeus-nurtured]]). There it opened a rebuke of the son; here it opens his call for help.
