---
summary: The eagle of the omen carries “a blood-red serpent,” φοινήεις, a word of the same family as δαφοινός, found only in this verse and its repetition.
grc: [φοινήεντα]
---

The eagle comes on the left, “carrying in its talons a blood-red serpent, monstrous, alive and still struggling” (12.202–203), and Polydamas repeats the verse when he reads the sign (12.220; [[the-eagle-and-the-serpent]], [[serpent]], [[huge]]). φοινήεις is formed from φοινός (“red, blood-red”) with the suffix -ήεις, as δαφοινός is with the intensive δα-; it occurs only in these two verses. The serpent of the earlier portent at Aulis was “blood-red on its back” (δαφοινός, 2.308; [[the-portent-at-aulis]]), and the translation gives both words the same English. The red serpent is the portent; the eagle that drops it is the Trojans, in Polydamas' reading (12.217–227). See [[teras]].
