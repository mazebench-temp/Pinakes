οἳ δʼ ἐπʼ ἐπάλξεις βαῖνον ἐρεμνῇ λαίλαπι ἶσοι / ἴφθιμοι Λυκίων ἡγήτορες ἠδὲ μέδοντες: “and onto the battlements, like a dark storm, were climbing the mighty leaders and rulers of the Lycians” (12.375–376). The half-verse is Nestor's (κελαινῇ λαίλαπι ἶσος, 11.747), with ἶσοι, plural, for a body of men, and ἐρεμνός, “dark”, the color of the aegis (4.167), for κελαινός, “black”. In Book 11 the storm was one young man sweeping down on the fleeing Epeians. Here it is a band of leaders coming up a wall, and it breaks on Menestheus' tower just as Ajax and Teucer, “going along inside the wall”, reach the men hard pressed there (12.373–377; see [[lycians]], [[menestheus]] and [[the-fight-at-the-wall]]). The first to fall is a Lycian, Sarpedon's companion Epicles, under Ajax's boulder (12.378–386; see [[like-a-diver]]).

The same words return in Book 20 for Ares, who shouts “like a dark storm” to the Trojans from the height of the city (20.51).
