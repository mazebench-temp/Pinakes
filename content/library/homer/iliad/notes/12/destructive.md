---
summary: As a noun, δήϊοι are “the enemy” three times in Book 12: the stakes are a defense against “enemy men,” the defenders shoot at “the enemy” below the wall, and the Ajaxes hope to drive “the enemy” back to the city.
grc: [δηΐους]
---

All three name the Trojans from the Achaean side of the wall. The stakes in the ditch are “a defense against enemy men” (δηΐων ἀνδρῶν ἀλεωρήν, 12.57; [[sons-of-the-achaeans]]). The Danaans fence the battlements with oxhides “and shot from them at the enemy as they came up under the wall” (12.263–264; [[battlements]]). And the two Ajaxes end their call along the towers with the hope “that we thrust back the quarrel and drive the enemy to the city” (12.276; [[the-two-ajaxes]], [[lord-of-the-lightning]]). The accusative δηΐους, in the phrase ἄνδρας δηΐους of Dolon's flight (10.358), is added on its own to the entry's forms. See [[achaeans]] and [[trojans]].
