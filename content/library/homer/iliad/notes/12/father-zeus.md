---
summary: Asius opens his complaint with the address of prayer: “Father Zeus, so you too, it seems, have become a lover of lies.”
---

The Lapiths hold the gate, the stones fall like snow, and Asius groans and strikes his thighs: “Father Zeus, so you too, it seems, have become a lover of lies through and through; for I did not think the Achaean heroes would hold back our might and our irresistible hands” (12.162–166; [[asius]], [[lover-of-lies]], [[irresistible-hands]]). The address is the one that opens a prayer, but what follows is reproach, in the turn Ζεῦ πάτερ, ἦ ῥα … that Agamemnon used when he asked whether Zeus had ever before struck a king with such blind folly (8.236–237) and that Poseidon used in his complaint about the wall (7.446). Zeus is not persuaded: “his heart wished to hold out glory to Hector” (12.173–174; [[plan-of-zeus]]). See [[zeus]].
