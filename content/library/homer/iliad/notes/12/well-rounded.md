---
summary: Over the battlements the two sides hack at “the well-rounded shields and the fluttering bucklers of hide,” in the verses of the fight over the phantom of Aeneas.
---

The battlements part the armies as the line parts two men disputing a boundary, “and over them they hacked at the oxhide shields around one another's chests, the well-rounded shields and the fluttering bucklers of hide” (12.424–426; [[the-boundary-dispute]], [[battlements]], [[feathered]]). The two verses repeat the fight over the phantom of Aeneas (5.452–453). There the armies struck real shields over a false body; here they strike across a wall, in a battle so even that the narrator compares it to the scales of a working woman (12.433–436; [[the-working-womans-scales]]).
