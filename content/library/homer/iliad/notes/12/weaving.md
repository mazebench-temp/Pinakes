---
summary: In a simile an honest working woman weighs wool against a weight to win a meager wage for her children (12.433–435), the one woman in the Iliad who works wool for hire.
grc: [εἴριον]
en: [wool, working woman, a meager wage]
refs: [12.433, 12.435]
---

“but they held, as an honest working woman holds the scales, / who, holding the weight and the wool, lifts them up on either side / until they are equal, so that she may win a meager wage for her children” (12.433–435; see [[the-working-womans-scales]] and [[the-scales-of-zeus]]). χερνῆτις, “one who works with her hands”, occurs only here (see the translation's note on 12.433–435). She balances the wool (εἴριον) against a weight (σταθμός). Whether she is weighing out raw wool to be spun or the yarn she has spun, the poem does not say; it says that she weighs honestly (ἀληθής), and that she is paid.

The other women who work wool in the Iliad do it at home or as slaves: Helen weaves the trials of the war into a purple web (3.125–128), Hector sends Andromache back to her loom and distaff (6.490–492), and he foresees her weaving in Argos “at another woman's bidding” (6.456; see [[captive-women]]). This woman works for others for a wage, μισθός, and the wage is ἀεικής, “meager”, literally “unseemly”. The only other wage for work in the Iliad is the one Laomedon promised Poseidon and Apollo for a year's service and then refused to pay (21.441–457); μισθός is also the reward Hector promises Dolon (10.304).
