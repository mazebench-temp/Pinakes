---
summary: No golden scales are held out in Book 12, but the battle at the wall hangs level like the scales of an honest working woman weighing wool, until Zeus gives the higher glory to Hector (12.432–438).
passages:
  - 12.432-438 | The battle hangs level until Zeus gives Hector the glory
---

The Lycians cannot break through the wall, and the Danaans cannot drive them back from it (12.417–420). Two similes from ordinary life hold the battle still: two men disputing their boundary with measuring rods in a common field (12.421–424; see [[the-boundary-dispute]] and [[boundary-stones]]), and a woman at her balance:

{{quote:e47002dc-a533-5f88-80a5-cedd028c4f5e}}

τάλαντα is the word for the golden scales of Zeus (8.69, 22.209), and the next verse names the god who ends the balance: “until Zeus gave the higher glory to Hector” (κῦδος ὑπέρτερον, 12.437; see [[kydos]] and [[the-working-womans-scales]]). The woman keeps her beam level by care and honesty (ἀληθής); Zeus tips it with glory. In Book 8 the scales were held out at midday and settled the day for the Trojans, and Zeus thundered from Ida against the Achaeans (8.68–77). Here no weighing is told, but the turn comes at the same point of the story: the battle has stood level, and Zeus gives it to Hector, who breaks the gate (12.437–471; see [[the-gates-of-the-wall]]). The verse of the level battle returns at the ships, after a simile of a carpenter's line (12.436 = 15.413), and in Book 16 Hector turns to flight, for he knows “the sacred scales of Zeus” (Διὸς ἱρὰ τάλαντα, 16.658).
