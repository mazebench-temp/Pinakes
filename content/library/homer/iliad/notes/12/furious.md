---
summary: Sarpedon rebukes the Lycians: “why do you let go of your furious courage like this?”
---

Driven back from the battlement by Ajax and Teucer, Sarpedon wheels round to his men: “Lycians, why do you let go of your furious courage like this? It is hard for me, mighty though I am, to break through alone and make a path to the ships” (12.409–411; [[sarpedon]], [[lycians]], [[mighty]]). The words are Agamemnon's at the review, “Argives, do not yet let go of your furious courage” (4.234), turned from a word of encouragement to the eager into a reproach. The Lycians answer it: fearing their lord's rebuke, they bear down the harder (12.413–414; [[man-of-counsel]]). See [[alke]] and [[exhortation]].
