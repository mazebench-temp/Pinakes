---
summary: Asius complains to Zeus that he did not think the Achaeans could hold back “our might and our irresistible hands.”
---

“Father Zeus, so you too, it seems, have become a lover of lies through and through; for I did not think the Achaean heroes would hold back our might and our irresistible hands” (12.164–166; [[asius]], [[father-zeus]], [[lover-of-lies]]). The pair μένος καὶ χεῖρας ἀάπτους, “might and irresistible hands,” ends the verse as when the Trojans rejoiced that Hector had escaped “the might and the irresistible hands of Ajax” (7.309). Asius speaks to the god who used the same pair of himself, “such is my might and such are my irresistible hands” (8.450), and claims it for the Trojans just as two Lapiths stop them at the gate (12.167–172; [[wasps-at-the-roadside]]).
