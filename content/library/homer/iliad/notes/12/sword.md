---
summary: The one sword of Book 12 is Leonteus'. He draws it from its sheath when his spear has struck Hippomachus, and kills Antiphates with it hand to hand at the gate (12.190–192).
---

“And then, drawing his sharp sword from its sheath, / first he darted through the throng at Antiphates / and struck him hand to hand; and he was flung on his back on the ground” (12.190–192; see [[leonteus]], [[antiphates]] and [[sheath]]). The sword follows the spear: Leonteus has just struck Hippomachus with his spear (12.189; see [[belt]]), and draws for the close work, αὐτοσχεδίην, “hand to hand”. ἐρυσσάμενος ξίφος ὀξύ, “drawing his sharp sword”, is a formula of the rush that follows the spear (14.496, 20.284, 21.116); here the sheath is named too, ἐκ κολεοῖο, as when Achilles began to draw his sword against Agamemnon (1.194).

No other sword is named in the book. The fight at the wall is fought with stones from the towers and the battlements, with arrows from the wall, and with spears (see [[stone-throwing]], [[arrows]] and [[spear]]); of the men killed and wounded across the battlements the poem says only that many were stabbed “with the pitiless bronze” (12.427).
