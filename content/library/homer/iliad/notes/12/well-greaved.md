---
summary: Before they dart out of the gate, the two Lapiths stand inside rousing “the well-greaved Achaeans” to defend the ships.
---

“For a while the two, being inside, kept rousing the well-greaved Achaeans to fight in defense of the ships; but when they saw them rushing at the wall, the Trojans, and among the Danaans there rose shouting and rout, the two darted out and fought in front of the gates” (12.141–145; [[polypoetes]], [[leonteus]], [[rout]]). The verses go back in time to explain how the Lapiths came to be standing before the gate when Asius arrived (12.131–136). It is the only use of the formula in the book. See [[achaeans]].
