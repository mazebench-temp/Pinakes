summary: Asius drives to the gate as “Asius, the hero”, and when the Lapiths hold it he complains that he did not think “the Achaean heroes” would hold back the Trojans' might; the narrator has another word for the whole generation, “the race of men half-divine”.
grc: [ἥρωας]
---

The title is given on both sides. Asius, who leads the third column with Helenus and Deiphobus, is “Asius, the hero, / Asius, son of Hyrtacus” (12.95–96; see [[asius]]). A few verses later the narrator says that he will die by the spear of Idomeneus (12.113–117), and in the next book the killer has the same title, ἥρως Ἰδομενεύς, as Asius comes at him on foot in front of his horses (13.384–387; see [[idomeneus]]).

When the two Lapiths hold the gate, Asius blames Zeus, “for I did not think the Achaean heroes / would hold back our might and our irresistible hands” (12.165–166; see [[pseudos]] and [[menos]]). ἥρωας Ἀχαιούς is said here for the first time in the poem. It returns in the threats and hopes of the books that follow, as the Trojans mean to burn the ships and kill the Achaean heroes (13.629, 15.702), and Apollo promises Hector to turn them (15.261).

ἥρως is the word the fighting men use of each other. The narrator, looking back on the war from his own time, calls the whole generation that fell at Troy by another name, “the race of men half-divine” (12.23; see [[hemitheos]] and [[men-of-today]]).
