---
summary: Menestheus looks along the wall for help and sees the two Ajaxes, “insatiable of war.”
grc: [ἀκορήτω]
en: [insatiable of war]
---

The Lycians are coming at Menestheus' tower, and he looks for a leader who might ward off disaster: “and he caught sight of the two Ajaxes, insatiable of war, standing there, and Teucer, who had just come from his hut, close by” (12.335–337; [[the-two-ajaxes]], [[menestheus]], [[teucer]]). The adjective is in the dual, ἀκορήτω, to match Αἴαντε δύω, and the dual form is added to the entry's forms. It names the two men the moment before Menestheus finds that he cannot make them hear him above the din (12.337–340). The phrase πολέμου ἀκόρητος occurs only here; Menelaus will turn the idea against the Trojans, who are “insatiable of battle” (μάχης ἀκόρητοι, 13.639). See [[insatiable-of-war]].
