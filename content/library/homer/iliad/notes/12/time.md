  - 12.310-321 | The honors of the Lycian kings
---

“Glaucus, why is it that we two are honored most” (τετιμήμεσθα μάλιστα, 12.310): the perfect of τιμάω, “we have been honored, we stand honored”. Sarpedon counts the honor in things, as the poem does (see the article above and [[honor-and-dishonor]]). There is the seat of honor, the meat and the full cups of the feast (12.311; see [[feast]]); the regard of the people, who “look on us as gods” (12.312); and land, “a great domain by the banks of the Xanthus, / a fair one, of orchard and wheat-bearing plowland” (12.313–314; see [[xanthus-of-lycia]]). When Bellerophon had come through the tasks the king of Lycia set him, the king gave him half of all his kingly honor, and the Lycians cut him such a domain (τιμῆς βασιληΐδος ἥμισυ πάσης, 6.191–195; see [[bellerophon-in-lycia]]). Sarpedon and Glaucus are his grandsons, and lead the Lycians after him (2.876, 6.196–206).

The verse of the feast is the one with which Hector taunts Diomedes: the Danaans honored him above others “with a seat of honor, with meat, and with full cups; / but now they will dishonor you” (8.161–163). There honor given is taken back from a man who fails. Sarpedon turns the threat into a duty: because they are honored, they must stand among the foremost and meet the blazing battle, so that a Lycian may say their kings rule “not without glory” (12.315–321; see [[sarpedons-speech]], [[kleos]] and [[kingship-and-authority]]). Honor received is a debt, and the front rank is where it is paid.
