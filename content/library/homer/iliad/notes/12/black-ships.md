---
summary: Twice the Trojans boast that the Achaeans will no longer hold out “but would fall among the black ships,” and the second time the narrator calls them fools.
---

The same verse closes two Trojan charges. When the five columns are formed, the men lock their shields together and go at the Danaans, “and said the enemy would no longer hold out, but would fall among the black ships” (12.105–107; [[trojans]]). Asius' men follow his chariot to the open gate “with shrill cries; for they said the Achaeans would no longer hold out, but would fall among the black ships, the fools!” (12.124–127; [[asius]], [[nepios]]). The verse is the one Odysseus used in Book 9 to report the Trojans' boast (9.234–235), and Eurypylus said almost the same of his own army at the end of Book 11, that the Achaeans “will fall among the black ships” (11.823–824). Here the boast meets the two Lapiths at the gate. See [[ships]].
