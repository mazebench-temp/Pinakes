---
summary: Across the battlements “many were stabbed in the flesh with the pitiless bronze,” some in the back as they turned, some through the shield.
---

The two sides hack at each other over the battlements, “and many were stabbed in the flesh with the pitiless bronze, both whoever turned and laid his back bare as they fought, and many right through the shield itself. Everywhere the towers and battlements were spattered with the blood of men on both sides, from Trojans and Achaeans” (12.427–431; [[battlements]], [[well-rounded]]). The formula has no single killer here: it describes the whole struggle at the wall, which stays even until Zeus gives Hector the higher glory (12.432–437; [[the-working-womans-scales]]).
