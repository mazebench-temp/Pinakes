---
summary: Hector leaps through the broken gate “his face like swift night.”
grc: ["νυκτὶ θοῇ"]
---

The stone has split the doors, “and shining Hector leapt in, his face like swift night; and he shone with the bronze, terrible, that he wore on his body” (12.462–464; [[phaidimos]], [[like-night]]). νυκτὶ θοῇ ἀτάλαντος ὑπώπια is literally “equal to swift night in his face” (ὑπώπια occurs only here): the face is dark and terrible while the bronze around it shines. Apollo came down from Olympus “like the night” to bring the plague (1.47). The phrase νυκτὶ θοῇ, which this note adds to the entry's forms, returns once more in the Iliad, of Night as a goddess, whom even Zeus feared to displease (14.261); the [[swift-ships]] entry, whose forms also catch θοῇ, leaves the verse to this one.
