---
summary: The serpent the eagle drops among the Trojans is “the portent of aegis-bearing Zeus.”
---

The eagle lets the wounded serpent fall into the throng, “and the Trojans shuddered when they saw the writhing snake lying in their midst, the portent of aegis-bearing Zeus” (12.208–209; [[the-eagle-and-the-serpent]], [[teras]]). The half-verse Διὸς τέρας αἰγιόχοιο named the Gorgon's head on the aegis itself in Book 5 (5.741–742; [[gorgon]]): there the portent was the god's own emblem of terror, here it is a sign sent to men, which Polydamas reads as a warning and Hector refuses to heed (12.210–250; [[divination]]). In the Odyssey Telemachus ends a speech to his father with the same words, εἰ ἐτεόν γέ τι οἶσθα Διὸς τέρας αἰγιόχοιο (“if you truly know of some portent of aegis-bearing Zeus,” Od. 16.320).
