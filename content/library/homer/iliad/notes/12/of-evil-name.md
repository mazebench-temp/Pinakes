---
summary: The narrator foretells Asius' death: “fate of evil name enfolded him through the spear of Idomeneus.”
grc: [δυσώνυμος]
---

“The fool! He was not going to escape the evil fates and, glorying in his horses and chariot, from the ships go back home to windy Ilios; for before that, fate of evil name enfolded him through the spear of Idomeneus, noble son of Deucalion” (12.113–117; [[asius]], [[moira]], [[ker-doom]], [[noble]]). The narrator speaks of the death as already done: ἀμφεκάλυψεν, “enfolded” (12.116). It comes only in the next book (13.384–393). Of the word's three uses in Homer this is the only one in the narrator's voice; Hecuba gave it to the Achaeans (6.255) and Penelope gives it to the dreaded dawn (Od. 19.571). The nominative δυσώνυμος is added to the entry's forms. See [[nepios]] and [[windy]].
