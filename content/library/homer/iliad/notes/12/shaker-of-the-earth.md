---
summary: “The shaker of the earth himself,” trident in hand, leads the rivers and the waves against the Achaean wall in the narrator's look ahead.
---

The name stands alone for Poseidon in the destruction of the wall: “And the shaker of the earth himself, holding his trident in his hands, led the way, and sent out on the waves all the foundations of logs and stones that the Achaeans had laid with toil, and made all smooth beside the strong-flowing Hellespont, and covered the great beach over again with sand, when he had brought the wall to nothing” (12.27–32; [[strong-flowing]], [[the-achaean-wall]]). He does what Zeus gave him leave to do when he complained of the wall in Book 7, and verse 12.31 repeats Zeus' words there almost exactly (7.459–463). It is the only time the Iliad puts a trident in his hands; the Odyssey has it twice (Od. 4.506, 5.292). See [[poseidon]], [[phoebus]] and [[the-building-of-the-wall]].
