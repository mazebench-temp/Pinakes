---
summary: Polydamas leads the first column as “noble” (ἀμύμων), and the army obeys the counsel of “blameless” Polydamas (ἀμώμητος, the only use of the word in Homer).
grc: [ἀμωμήτοιο]
---

Two words of praise for Polydamas stand twenty-one verses apart. When the Trojans form their five columns on foot, “Some went with Hector and noble Polydamas, who were the most and the best” (12.88–89), with ἀμύμων, the epithet that named him at his first appearance (11.57; [[polydamas]]). When the columns go forward, “the other Trojans and their far-famed allies obeyed the counsel of blameless Polydamas” (12.108–109; [[famous]], [[boule]]), all but Asius ([[asius]]).

The second adjective is ἀμώμητος, which occurs only here in Homer. Its sense is plain: ἀ- (“not”) + μωμητός, from μωμάομαι (“blame, find fault”), the verb of Helen's fear that “the Trojan women would all hereafter blame me” (3.411–412): “not to be blamed.” It is the sense that ancient scholars gave to ἀμύμων, and it is given to Polydamas in the verse that praises the counsel he has just given (12.61–79). Later in the book Hector will find fault with his next counsel (12.231–250). See [[the-fight-at-the-wall]].
