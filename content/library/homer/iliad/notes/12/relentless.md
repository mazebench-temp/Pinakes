---
summary: The book ends as the Danaans flee among the hollow ships “and a relentless din arose”; the same verse returns when Patroclus turns the rout around.
---

“At once some climbed over the wall, and others poured in through the well-built gates themselves; and the Danaans fled in rout among the hollow ships, and a relentless din arose” (12.469–471; [[hollow-ships]], [[rout]], [[well-built]]). The last words of the book are ὅμαδος δʼ ἀλίαστος ἐτύχθη. The whole verse comes back at 16.296, when Patroclus has put out the fire at Protesilaus' ship and the Trojans flee: then it is the Danaans who pour back “among the hollow ships,” and the same relentless din arises. Iris used the word of the war itself, “relentless war has come” (πόλεμος δʼ ἀλίαστος, 2.797). See [[the-fight-at-the-wall]].
