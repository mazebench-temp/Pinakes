---
summary: Sarpedon says that if they were to be ageless and immortal, he would not send Glaucus “into the battle where men win glory.”
---

“Then neither would I myself fight among the foremost, nor would I send you into the battle where men win glory” (12.324–325; [[sarpedons-speech]], [[glaucus]]). The epithet names what the battle gives, κῦδος, in the speech that weighs glory against death: since the fates of death stand over every mortal, the two must go and “either hold out triumph to another, or another to us” (12.326–328; [[kydos]], [[ker-doom]]). The phrase μάχην ἐς κυδιάνειραν is the one of Agamemnon, “eager for the battle where men win glory,” at the review (4.225). See [[sarpedon]].
