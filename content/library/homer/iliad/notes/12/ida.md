---
summary: The rivers that will wash away the Achaean wall are “all that flow from the mountains of Ida to the sea” (12.19), and from the same mountains Zeus sends the storm of wind that carries dust against the ships (12.252–255).
---

**The rivers' mountain.** All eight rivers that Poseidon and Apollo turn against the wall flow “from the mountains of Ida to the sea” (12.19; see [[the-achaean-wall]]). Ἰδαίων ὀρέων, “the mountains of Ida”, is the phrase of Zeus' thunder in Book 8 and of Iris' journeys (8.170, 8.410, 11.196). [[strabo|Strabo]] says that Ida is called “of many springs” (πολυπῖδαξ, 8.47; see [[of-the-many-springs]]) above all for the number of rivers that flow from it. He reports Demetrius of Scepsis: the Scamander, the Granicus and the Aesepus rise on Cotylus, a height of Ida about a hundred and twenty stades above Scepsis, from springs that lie within twenty stades of one another (13.1.43; see [[scamander]], [[granicus]] and [[aesepus]]).

**Zeus' wind.** Zeus has sat on the peaks of Ida since Book 11 (11.182–184), and Hera will find him there (14.157–158). When Hector leads the Trojans against the wall, Zeus sends the wind down from the mountain:
