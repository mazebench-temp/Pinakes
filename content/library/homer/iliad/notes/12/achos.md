summary: The Argives defend their ships “troubled though they were”, the gods who help them are “grieved at heart”, and grief comes on Sarpedon when Glaucus, wounded, leaves the fight. In Book 16 the grief is Glaucus' for Sarpedon.
grc: [ἀκαχήατο]
passages:
  - 12.175-181 | Fighting at all the gates
  - 12.387-396 | Glaucus wounded
---

**Troubled men, grieving gods.** After the narrator says that it is hard for him to tell all of it like a god, he sees the whole wall at once, with fire everywhere, “and the Argives, troubled though they were, of necessity / defended the ships; and the gods were grieved at heart, / all who were the helpers of the Danaans in battle” (12.178–180; see [[gods-and-mortals]] and [[the-gods-mirror-the-mortals]]). ἀχνύμενοι and ἀκαχήατο, from ἄχνυμαι and ἀκαχίζω, both belong with ἄχος (see the article above). The men below fight in distress, and the gods above share it without coming down.

**Sarpedon's grief.** Teucer's arrow hits Glaucus in the bared arm, and he leaps back from the wall unseen (12.387–391). “And grief came upon Sarpedon as Glaucus went away, / as soon as he noticed; but even so he did not forget the joy of battle” (12.392–393; see [[sarpedon]], [[glaucus]] and [[charme]]). Grief for a companion is a spur, and Sarpedon kills Alcmaon in the next verses (12.394–396; see [[alcmaon]]). In Book 16 the same words are said of Glaucus, as he hears the dying Sarpedon call on him (Γλαύκῳ δʼ αἰνὸν ἄχος γένετο, 16.508), and he cannot help him, for the arm that Teucer hit at the high wall still pains him (16.508–512). The wound of this book outlasts it.
