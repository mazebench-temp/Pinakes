---
summary: The Trojan columns lock themselves together “with their well-made oxhides,” τυκτῇσι βόεσσι.
grc: [τυκτῇσι]
---

“And when they had locked themselves together with their well-made oxhides, they went straight at the Danaans in eager haste” (12.105–106; [[shield]], [[black-ships]]). The adjective is τυκτός, the simple verbal adjective of τεύχω (“make, fashion”) from which εὔτυκτος is formed: “made, wrought,” and here, of shields made of hide, “well-made.” Homer uses it otherwise of Ares, τυκτὸν κακόν, literally a fashioned evil, which the translation renders “an evil through and through” (5.831), and in the Odyssey of a leveled floor and a built spring (Od. 4.627, 17.206). The translation gives it the English of εὔτυκτος, and the form is added to the entry. See [[the-fight-at-the-wall]].
