---
summary: Peirithous is named twice in Book 12, as the father of Polypoetes, who holds the gate of the Achaean wall with Leonteus (12.129, 12.182).
relations:
  - child: polypoetes (12.129, 12.182)
---

“The one the son of Peirithous, mighty Polypoetes” (12.129), and again as the Lapiths join battle, “the son of Peirithous, mighty Polypoetes” (12.182; see [[polypoetes]]). Nestor named Peirithous first among the mightiest men he had known, who fought “the beasts who lived in the mountains” (1.262–268; see [[nestor-and-the-centaurs]]), and the Catalogue set his son's birth on the day he drove the Centaurs from Pelion (2.740–744; see [[lapiths-drive-out-the-centaurs]]). Book 12 shows that son at the gate, one of the “high-hearted sons of Lapith spearmen” (12.128; see [[lapiths]]).
