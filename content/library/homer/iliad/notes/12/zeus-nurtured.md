---
summary: Thootes gives Menestheus' message as the request of “the dear son of Peteos, whom Zeus has nurtured.”
---

Menestheus himself sent his herald with a plain command (12.343); the herald, speaking to the two Ajaxes, gives the sender his father and his father's epithet: “Ajaxes, leaders of the bronze-armored Argives, the dear son of Peteos, whom Zeus has nurtured, bids you go there” (12.354–356; [[thootes]], [[menestheus]], [[peteos]], [[bronze-armored]]). The phrase Πετεῶο διοτρεφέος is the one Agamemnon used when he rebuked Menestheus at the review, “Son of Peteos, the king whom Zeus has nurtured” (4.338). The royal epithet of the father lends weight to the son's request. See [[basileus]] and [[heralds]].
