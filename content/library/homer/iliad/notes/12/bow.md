summary: Teucer, newly come from his hut after the wound of Book 8, is sent for as the man “well skilled with the bow”; Pandion carries his curved bow to Menestheus' tower, and from the wall Teucer wounds Glaucus and hits Sarpedon's shield strap (12.336, 12.350, 12.372, 12.387–403).
passages:
  - 12.370-372 | Pandion carries Teucer's bow
  - 12.387-403 | Teucer shoots from the wall
---

**The archer sent for.** Menestheus looks along the wall for help and sees the two Ajaxes, “and Teucer, who had just come from his hut” (12.336; see [[teucer]] and [[hut]]). It is Teucer's first appearance since Hector's stone broke his bowstring and two companions carried him groaning to the ships (8.324–334; see [[stone-throwing]]). The message names him by his skill: “let Teucer follow with him, well skilled with the bow” (τόξων ἐῢ εἰδώς, 12.350, 12.363; see [[skilled-with-the-bow]] and [[message-repeated]]). The archer is wanted with the man whose shield covers him: in Book 8 he shot from under the shield of Ajax (8.266–272; see [[the-shield-of-ajax]]).

**Pandion.** “and with them Pandion carried Teucer's curved bow” (12.372; see [[pandion]] and [[curved-bow]]). Pandion is named only here, and the poem does not say why another man carries the bow. καμπύλα τόξα, the plural for a single weapon, is the bow of Paris, of Pandarus and of Dolon too (3.17, 5.97, 10.333).

**From the wall.** At the tower Teucer hits Glaucus “with an arrow as he rushed at the high wall, / where he saw his arm laid bare, and stopped him from battle” (12.388–389; see [[glaucus]] and [[arrows]]), and then hits the strap of Sarpedon's shield while Ajax stabs the shield itself (12.400–405; see [[shield]] and [[sarpedon]]). The bow takes Glaucus out of the fight; against Sarpedon it fails, for Zeus wards off death from his son (12.402–403). Twice in the poem the bow is struck from Teucer's hand as he aims at Hector, by Hector's stone and then by Zeus, who snaps the string (8.324–329, 15.458–465); at the wall his targets are the Lycians, and Zeus keeps only one of them from harm.
