---
summary: Menestheus' message, repeated by his herald, says the Lycian leaders “have been violent in the fierce battles.”
grc: [ζαχρηεῖς]
---

Menestheus sends Thootes to the Ajaxes with the reason for his call: “For the leaders of the Lycians have pressed on so hard, who even before now have been violent in the fierce battles” (12.346–347; [[lycians]], [[menestheus]], [[krateros]]). Thootes repeats the two verses word for word (12.359–360; [[thootes]]). The adjective of the winds in Book 5 (5.525) here describes men; its one other use in Homer is also of the fighting at the wall, where it was built lowest (13.683–684). See [[sarpedon]] and [[glaucus]].
