---
summary: “Shining Hector” frames the breach of the wall: he could not have broken the gates without Sarpedon, and at the end of the book he leaps through them.
---

The formula names Hector twice, at the two points of the assault. First in the narrator's “not even then”: “Not even then would the Trojans and shining Hector have broken the gates of the wall and the long bar, had not Zeus the counselor roused his own son, Sarpedon” (12.290–292; [[counselor]], [[sarpedon]]). Then at the breach itself, when the stone has split the doors: “and shining Hector leapt in, his face like swift night; and he shone with the bronze, terrible, that he wore on his body” (12.462–464; [[swift-night]], [[like-night]]). The epithet of radiance is followed by darkness in his face and light from his armor. See [[hector]] and [[the-fight-at-the-wall]].
