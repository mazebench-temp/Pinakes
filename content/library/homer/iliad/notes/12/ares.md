---
summary: Ares takes no part in Book 12. His name is heard only in two titles of the Lapith Leonteus, “the equal of Ares, ruin of mortals” and “offshoot of Ares”.
---

**Leonteus.** At the open gate Asius finds two Lapiths, Polypoetes and “the other Leonteus, the equal of Ares, ruin of mortals” (12.130; see [[leonteus]], [[ruin-of-mortals]] and [[equal-of-ares]]). βροτολοιγῷ ἶσος Ἄρηϊ is otherwise said of Hector, when he sets the Trojans on in Book 11 and when he leads them in Book 13 (11.295, 13.802), and of Achilles when he returns to the battle (20.46). When Leonteus kills Hippomachus he is “Leonteus, offshoot of Ares” (12.188), in the words with which the Catalogue brought him in (Λεοντεὺς ὄζος Ἄρηος, 2.745; see [[offshoot-of-ares]]). ἀρήϊος, “warlike”, the adjective of the god's name, is given to Asteropaeus when Sarpedon chooses him (12.102; see [[warlike]] and [[asteropaeus]]).

**On Olympus.** The god himself sits out the battle at the wall, as Zeus' ban requires (8.10–12). In Book 13 he does not hear that his son Ascalaphus has fallen, for he is sitting on the peak of Olympus under golden clouds, “held back by the counsels of Zeus, where the other / immortal gods were also, shut out from the war” (13.521–525; see [[ascalaphus]] and [[olympians]]). ἐελμένος, “held back”, is the participle that pens the Argives beside their ships in this book (ἐελμένοι, 12.38).
