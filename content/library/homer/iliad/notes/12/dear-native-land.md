---
summary: In the narrator's look ahead, the Argives have sailed home “to their dear native land,” the condition Zeus set for the wall's destruction.
---

“And the city of Priam was sacked in the tenth year, and the Argives had gone in their ships to their dear native land, then Poseidon and Apollo took counsel to bring the wall to nothing” (12.15–18; [[the-achaean-wall]], [[poseidon]], [[apollo]]). Zeus had given Poseidon leave to destroy the wall “when the long-haired Achaeans again are gone with their ships to their dear native land” (7.459–460; [[the-building-of-the-wall]]); the narrator tells the condition as fulfilled, and with it the fall of Troy and the homecoming that the poem itself never reaches. The formula, the goal of every return ([[nostos]]), is shortened here to φίλην ἐς πατρίδʼ before the verb ἔβησαν. See [[fame-and-the-tomb]].
