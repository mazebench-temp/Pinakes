---
summary: The two Lapiths dart out of the gate “like two wild boars” that face the din of men and dogs in the mountains.
grc: [ἀγροτέροισι]
---

“The two darted out and fought in front of the gates, like two wild boars that in the mountains await the din of men and dogs coming on, and darting sideways they break the wood around them, cutting it off at the root, and a gnashing of tusks goes on, until someone hits them and takes their life” (12.145–150; [[wild-boars-in-the-mountains]], [[boar]], [[polypoetes]], [[leonteus]]). The wild boar of Book 11 was the hunter's quarry, to which Hector set his men like hounds (11.292–293); here the boars are the Achaean defenders, and the hunters are the Trojans. The dative plural ἀγροτέροισι is added to the entry's forms. See [[dogs]].
