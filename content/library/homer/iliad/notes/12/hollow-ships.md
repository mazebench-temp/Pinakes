---
summary: The hollow ships frame the fight at the wall: the Argives are penned in beside them at the start, the Trojans' best are eager to fight at them, and the Danaans flee among them in the book's last verse.
---

The narrator's first look at the battle finds the Argives “beaten down by the lash of Zeus, … penned in beside the hollow ships” (12.37–38; [[master-of-rout]]). Hector's column, “the most and the best,” are “most eager to break through the wall and fight at the hollow ships” (12.89–90). And when Hector has broken the gate and the Trojans pour over the wall, “the Danaans fled in rout among the hollow ships, and a relentless din arose” (12.470–471; [[relentless]], [[rout]]). Both words of the entry are used: γλαφυρός at the beginning and the end, κοῖλος in the middle. See [[ships]] and [[the-fight-at-the-wall]].
