---
summary: In Sarpedon's speech the Lycian kings eat fat sheep and drink “choice wine, honey-sweet,” the feast that obliges them to fight in front.
---

Sarpedon imagines what a Lycian will say of him and Glaucus: “Truly not without glory are they who rule over Lycia, these kings of ours, and they eat fat sheep and choice wine, honey-sweet; but there is strength in them too, noble strength, since they fight among the foremost Lycians” (12.318–321; [[sarpedons-speech]], [[fat-sheep]], [[lycians]]). The word is μελιηδής, as in Agamemnon's reproach to Menestheus and Odysseus, who are glad to drink “cups of honey-sweet wine” at his feasts and should therefore be first in battle (4.343–346; [[blazing]]). The honey-sweet wine stands for the honors of the kings, and the speech sets the duty of fighting in front beside it. See [[feast]] and [[wine]].
