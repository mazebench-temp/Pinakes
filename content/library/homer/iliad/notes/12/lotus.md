---
summary: In the simile of Zeus' snowfall the snow covers even “the lotus plains,” πεδία λωτοῦντα, the only use of the adjective in Homer.
---

“And he lulls the winds and pours it down steadily, until he has covered the peaks of the high mountains and the high headlands and the lotus plains and the rich tilled fields of men” (12.281–283; [[the-great-snowfall]], [[counselor]]). λωτοῦντα is contracted from λωτόεις, “full of lotus,” and it is found only here. The simile names the land from the highest ground down, mountain peaks, headlands, the low plains where the lotus grows and horses graze (2.776), and the fields that men work; only the waves at the shore hold the snow off (12.284–285; [[gray-sea]]). So the stones fly from both sides at the wall (12.287–289; [[a-snow-of-stones]]).
