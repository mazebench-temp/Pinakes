---
summary: The formula opens the description of Sarpedon's shield, beaten out of bronze and lined with oxhides stitched with golden wires.
---

“At once he held before him his shield, balanced on every side, beautiful, of bronze, beaten out, which the smith had beaten, and inside it he stitched oxhides close together with golden wires running all around the circle” (12.294–297; [[sarpedon]], [[shield]]). The line-end formula, usually a passing description of a round shield, here begins the account of one shield in particular, and the next verses tell how it was made. Sarpedon holds it before him as he goes at the wall like a mountain lion (12.298–308; [[the-mountain-lion-and-the-fold]]); Teucer's arrow will hit its strap and Ajax's spear its face (12.400–405; [[man-covering]]). The gesture of holding the shield out in front, with the form πάντοσʼ ἐΐσην, is the one of Aeneas standing over the body of Pandarus (5.300).
