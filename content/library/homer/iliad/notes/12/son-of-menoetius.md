---
summary: The book opens with “the valiant son of Menoetius” tending wounded Eurypylus in his hut.
---

“So in the hut the valiant son of Menoetius was tending wounded Eurypylus; and they fought on, the Argives and Trojans, in a mass” (12.1–3; [[patroclus]], [[eurypylus]]). The first verse of the book takes up the last scene of Book 11, where Patroclus cut the arrow from Eurypylus' thigh and laid a root on the wound (11.842–848; [[healing]]), with the formula Μενοιτίου ἄλκιμος υἱός that named him three times there (11.605, 11.814, 11.837; [[strong-spear]]). The narrative then leaves him in the hut through the whole fight at the wall, and comes back to him there when the Trojans have crossed it (15.390–394). See [[menoetius]] and [[hut]].
