---
summary: Aeneas says that “there are insults enough for both of us to speak, / a great many, so that a ship of a hundred benches could not carry the load” (20.246–247).
---

ὀνείδεα μυθήσασθαι is Agamemnon's phrase for Achilles in the quarrel: “do his insults therefore rush forth to be spoken?” (1.291). The ship of a hundred benches, ἑκατόζυγος, found only here, is a ship bigger than any ever seen, and ἄροιτο, “carry”, is the only place where ἄρνυμαι does not mean “win” (Walter Leaf, *Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 20.247; see the translation's note on 20.247).
