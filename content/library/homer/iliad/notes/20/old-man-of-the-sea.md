---
summary: Apollo, urging Aeneas against Achilles, sets the two mothers against each other: Aphrodite is the daughter of Zeus, Thetis only the daughter of the Old Man of the Sea.
---

“for they say that you too were born of Zeus' daughter Aphrodite, / and he is from a lesser goddess; / for one is the daughter of Zeus, and the other of the Old Man of the Sea” (20.105–107; see [[apollo]], [[aeneas]], [[aphrodite]] and [[thetis]]). ἐξ ἁλίοιο γέροντος, “of the Old Man of the Sea”, is the title under which the Iliad knows Thetis' father, who is never named (1.358, 1.538, 1.556, 18.141; see [[nereids]]). Apollo's argument ranks the sea god below Zeus, and the son of Zeus' daughter above the son of the Old Man's daughter. Aeneas, answering Achilles, names both mothers again, Thetis “daughter of the sea” and his own mother Aphrodite (20.207–209).
