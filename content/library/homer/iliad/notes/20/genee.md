summary: Aeneas answers Achilles' taunts with his lineage. “We know each other's lineage”, he begins (20.203); he brings it in with Glaucus' verses, “if you wish to learn this too, so that you may know well / our lineage—and many men know it” (20.213–214), and ends it with Glaucus' line, “This is the lineage and the blood I claim” (20.241). Poseidon saves him “so that the lineage may not perish without seed and without a trace, / the lineage of Dardanus”, since the son of Cronus “has come to hate the lineage of Priam” (20.303–306). Achilles tells the dying Iphition, “your birthplace is by the lake” (20.390).
grc: [γόνοιο]
en: [youngest born]
---

The lineage runs from Zeus through Dardanus, Erichthonius and Tros to Priam and Anchises, and ends in Hector and Aeneas, each fourth in descent from Tros (20.215–240; Walter Leaf, *Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 20.240). Leaf thinks the genealogy the kernel of this “Aeneid”, around which the rest of the speech was written (on 20.215; see [[genealogy-in-battle]], [[dardanus]] and [[fathers-and-sons]]). γενεή at 20.390 is “birthplace”, as in the Odyssey (*Odyssey* 1.407; Leaf on 20.390; see the translation's row on γενεή). Polydorus was “the youngest born among his children” (νεώτατος … γόνοιο, 20.409), a collective use of γόνος that Leaf calls peculiar (on 20.409; see [[polydorus-son-of-priam]]).
