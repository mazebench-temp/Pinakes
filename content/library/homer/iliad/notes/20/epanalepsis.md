---
summary: Hector, promising the Trojans that he will meet Achilles, takes up the end of one verse at the head of the next: “I will go against him, even if his hands are like fire, / if his hands are like fire, and his might like gleaming iron” (20.371–372).
refs: [20.371-372]
---

The repetition gives the threat its weight, and it is the first of the figure's three uses in the Iliad (see the entry). Hector answers Achilles' claim that not even a god could manage so great a battle (20.358–359) by granting all he can, fire in the hands, iron in the might, and going against him still; Apollo forbids him at once (20.375–378).
