---
summary: Apollo, in the likeness of Priam's son Lycaon, rouses Aeneas against Achilles with the question of Book 13: “Aeneas, counselor of the Trojans, where have your threats gone, / which you promised the kings of the Trojans as you drank your wine, / that you would fight face to face with Achilles, son of Peleus?” (20.83–85).
---

Walter Leaf compares Poseidon's question to Idomeneus and Agamemnon's to the army (13.219–220, 8.229–233; *Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 20.83). Aeneas answers with the memory of his flight from Achilles on Ida (20.87–102), and the god's reply is not about the threats but about birth: Aeneas' mother is the greater goddess (20.104–109; see [[mothers-and-sons]]). The threats made at the wine are kept, and Aeneas goes out to meet Achilles (20.110–111, 20.156–175; see [[basileus]] and [[eidos]]).
