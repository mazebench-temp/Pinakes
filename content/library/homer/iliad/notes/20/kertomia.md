---
summary: Aeneas and Hector answer Achilles' taunts in the same verse: they too know “how to speak both taunts and evil words” (20.202 = 20.433).
grc: [κερτομίας]
en: [taunts]
---

Achilles' taunts are the questions he puts to Aeneas, whether he hopes for Priam's honor or for a domain of orchard and plowland, and the story of his flight from Ida (20.178–198). Walter Leaf thinks that ἠμὲν … ἠδέ wants an antithesis, and would read αἴσιμα with Düntzer, “as well abuse as seemly speech” (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 20.202); the translation keeps αἴσυλα, “evil words” (see the translation's note on 20.202).
