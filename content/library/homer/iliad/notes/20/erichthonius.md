---
summary: In Aeneas' genealogy Erichthonius is the son of Dardanus and the father of Tros, a king and the richest of mortal men, whose three thousand mares the North Wind loved (20.219–230).
# Ἐριχθόνιον (20.219) and Ἐριχθόνιος (20.230), the only places in Homer; kept to the Iliad because in later Greek the
# name is the Athenian king's.
grc: [Ἐριχθόνιον, Ἐριχθόνιος]
en: [Erichthonius]
---

Δάρδανος αὖ τέκεθʼ υἱὸν Ἐριχθόνιον βασιλῆα, “And Dardanus in turn fathered a son, king Erichthonius” (20.219). The verse has the shape of the genealogy, the father's name, αὖ, “in turn”, and the son's: so “And Ilus in turn fathered a son, noble Laomedon” (20.236), as in Idomeneus' line, “and Minos in turn fathered a son, noble Deucalion” (13.451; see [[genealogy-in-battle]], [[dardanus]] and [[ilus]]). Of all the line only Erichthonius and his grandson Ganymede are given praise and a story (20.220–229, 20.233–235; see [[ganymede]]). He “was the richest of mortal men; / three thousand horses of his were pastured along the marshland” (20.220–221), and the North Wind loved his mares (20.223–229; see [[the-mares-of-erichthonius]] and [[boreas]]). Then the line goes on: “And Erichthonius fathered Tros, lord of the Trojans” (20.230; see [[tros]]).

Walter Leaf reports that Fick, who took the passage for an Attic addition, joined 219 directly to 230, and remarked that by 5.265 the divine horses were given not to Erichthonius but to Tros (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 20.219; see [[ganymede-and-the-horses-of-tros]]). Leaf himself allows that the whole genealogy may be of a piece (on 20.219).
