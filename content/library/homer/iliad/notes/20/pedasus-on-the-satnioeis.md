---
summary: Aeneas names Pedasus with Lyrnessus as the towns that Achilles sacked when he drove Aeneas from his cattle on Ida (20.92).
---

“and sacked Lyrnessus and Pedasus; but Zeus / rescued me” (20.92–93; see [[the-sack-of-lyrnessus]] and [[lyrnessus]]). Aeneas does not say which Pedasus he means, but four lines later he says that Athena bade Achilles “slay the Leleges and the Trojans” (20.96), and Pedasus on the Satnioeis is the town of Altes, king of the Leleges (21.85–87; see [[altes]] and [[leleges]]). Lycaon, whose voice Apollo has just borrowed, is Altes' grandson (20.81; 21.85–88; see [[lycaon-son-of-priam]]). Altes still rules the Leleges there in Book 21 (ἀνάσσει, 21.86); if his is the town that Achilles sacked, the poem does not say how its king survived. Strabo counts “Pedasus of the Leleges” among the places that Achilles sacked, and quotes this line (*Geography* 13.1.7; see [[strabo-on-the-city-of-mynes]]). Achilles, retelling the raid, names only Lyrnessus (20.191).
