---
summary: Hera tells Poseidon that she and Athena “have sworn many oaths / among all the immortals” never to ward off the evil day from the Trojans, “not even when all Troy blazes with ravening fire” (20.313–316); whether to save Aeneas he must decide for himself.
grc: [ὅρκους, ὠμόσσαμεν]
en: [sworn]
---

The plural ὅρκους, found only here, Walter Leaf takes as oaths sworn by many different objects, and so of the most solemn kind (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 20.313). In the next book the river Xanthus, begging Hera to call off Hephaestus, swears the same oath in the same verses (21.373–376; see the translation's note on 20.315–317). The oath binds Hera and Athena, not Poseidon, who goes at once to save Aeneas (20.318–329; see [[oaths-and-their-breaking]] and [[hera]]).
