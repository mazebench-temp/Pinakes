---
summary: Poseidon pleads for Aeneas that “he always gives pleasing / gifts to the gods who hold the wide sky” (20.298–299).
grc: [κεχαρισμένα]
en: [pleasing]
---

A gift that pleases a god earns his favor in return, and that is the ground of Poseidon's plea. Zeus says that his altar at Troy “never lacked an equal share of the feast” (4.48), and in the last book he says of Hector that he was dearest to the gods of all mortals in Ilios, since he never failed in pleasing gifts (24.66–70; see [[reciprocity-with-the-gods]] and [[doron]]).
