summary: The book sets its first duel in words before blows. Achilles taunts Aeneas (20.178–198); Aeneas answers that he too can taunt, recites his lineage, and cuts the talk short, “But come, let us no longer talk like this, like children” (20.244), “You will not turn me from courage with words” (20.256). Hector says that he could fight “even with the immortals in words, / but with the spear it is hard” (20.367–368), and answers Achilles in Aeneas' verses before he throws (20.431–437).
refs: [20.176-258, 20.364-372, 20.428-437]
---

20.244 is Idomeneus' verse to Meriones in Book 13 (13.292), and Patroclus had given the rule in Book 16: “there is no need to heap up words, but to fight” (16.631; see [[the-outcome-of-war]]). Walter Leaf finds Aeneas' repeated calls to stop talking (20.200–202, 20.211–212, 20.244–258) excused only by the long genealogy between them (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 20.215; see [[achilles-and-aeneas]], [[the-tongue-of-mortals]], [[such-will-you-hear-back]] and [[kertomia]]).
