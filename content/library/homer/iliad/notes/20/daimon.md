---
summary: Twice in his rampage Achilles is “like a god” (δαίμονι ἶσος): when he charges the mist that hides Hector “for the fourth time” (20.447), and when he rushes everywhere with his spear like a fire through a mountain forest (20.490–494; see [[forest-fire]]).
---

The half-line is said of Diomedes at his fourth charge against Apollo (5.438) and of Patroclus at his fourth charges against the wall of Troy and against the Trojans (16.705, 16.786), and each time Apollo answers the fourth charge, with a warning or, the last time, with a blow (5.440–442, 16.707–709, 16.788–793; see [[three-times-and-a-fourth]] and [[like-a-god]]). Walter Leaf notes that most manuscripts omit 20.447, and thinks it added from those three passages, where the fourth charge brings a god's answer; here it brings only Achilles' speech (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 20.444; see the translation's note on 20.447).
