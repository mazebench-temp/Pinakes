---
summary: Apollo, in Lycaon's likeness, reminds Aeneas of the threats he made to “the kings of the Trojans as you drank your wine” (20.84), and Aeneas' genealogy names Dardanus' son “king Erichthonius”, “the richest of mortal men” (20.219–220).
---

The kings at the wine are the Trojan leaders before whom Aeneas boasted, as the Achaeans boasted on Lemnos over their cups (8.229–234; see [[where-have-our-boasts-gone]]). Erichthonius is the only man of the genealogy called king (see [[erichthonius]]); his son Tros is “lord of the Trojans” (20.230; see [[anax]]).
