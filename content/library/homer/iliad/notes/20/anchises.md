---
summary: Aeneas is “the son of Anchises” when Hera sees him go against Achilles and when the two men meet (20.112, 20.160). He claims before Achilles to be the son of great-hearted Anchises and Aphrodite, and his genealogy makes Anchises the son of Capys, of the branch of Assaracus (20.208–209, 20.239–240).
# Ἀγχίσην (20.239), found only here in Homer.
grc: [Ἀγχίσην]
---

**The son of Anchises.** οὐδʼ ἔλαθʼ Ἀγχίσαο πάϊς λευκώλενον Ἥρην, “Nor did the son of Anchises escape the notice of white-armed Hera” (20.112), as he went to face Achilles; and the two who meet between the armies are Αἰνείας τʼ Ἀγχισιάδης καὶ δῖος Ἀχιλλεύς, “Aeneas, son of Anchises, and brilliant Achilles” (20.160), with the half-verse that named him beside Hector in Book 17 (17.754; see [[aeneas]] and [[achilles-and-aeneas]]).

**Aeneas' parents.** Achilles is the son of Peleus and Thetis, “but I claim that I was born the son of great-hearted Anchises, / and my mother is Aphrodite” (20.208–209; see [[aphrodite]] and [[thetis]]). The verses are Sthenelus' words in Book 5 in the first person, with “great-hearted” for “noble”: “and Aeneas claims that he was born the son of noble Anchises, / and his mother is Aphrodite” (5.247–248; see the translation's note on 20.205–209). “Of these, one pair or the other will weep for their dear son / today” (20.210–211): Anchises and Aphrodite, or Peleus and Thetis (see [[bereaved-parents]]).

**In the genealogy.** “and Assaracus fathered Capys, and he fathered Anchises, his son; / and Anchises fathered me, and Priam brilliant Hector” (20.239–240). Anchises is a great-grandson of Tros, as Priam is, from the other branch of the house (see [[capys]], [[assaracus]], [[priam]] and [[genealogy-in-battle]]). Poseidon's prophecy that Aeneas and his children's children will rule the Trojans keeps the house of Dardanus alive in this branch, since Zeus has come to hate the lineage of Priam (20.302–308; see [[dardanus]] and [[the-rule-of-aeneas]]).
