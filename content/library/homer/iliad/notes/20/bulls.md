---
summary: Two similes of Book 20 are of bulls. The dying Hippodamas bellows like a bull dragged by young men around the Heliconian lord, Poseidon (20.403–405), and at the end of the book Achilles' horses trample the dead as broad-browed bulls tread out barley on a threshing floor (20.495–497).
grc: ["βόας ἄρσενας", "βοῶν ὑπὸ πόσσʼ"]
---

**Poseidon's bull.** αὐτὰρ ὃ θυμὸν ἄϊσθε καὶ ἤρυγεν, ὡς ὅτε ταῦρος / ἤρυγεν ἑλκόμενος Ἑλικώνιον ἀμφὶ ἄνακτα: “And he gasped out his life and bellowed, as when a bull / bellows as he is dragged around the Heliconian lord” (20.403–404). The bull is the god's own victim, like the bull that the Pylians offered to Poseidon in Nestor's story and the black bulls they sacrifice to him on the shore in the *Odyssey* (11.728; *Odyssey* 3.5–8), and it bellows as it is dragged, like the bull that two lions drag off from the herd on the Shield (18.580–581). Hippodamas, stabbed in the back as he flees, dies with the victim's cry, and the earth-shaker is glad (20.401–406; see [[the-bull-of-the-heliconian-lord]], [[poseidon]] and [[hippodamas]]).

**The threshing bulls.** ὡς δʼ ὅτε τις ζεύξῃ βόας ἄρσενας εὐρυμετώπους, “As when a man yokes broad-browed bulls” (20.495). The Greek is βόας ἄρσενας, “male cattle”: the word for the kind, βοῦς, with the word for the sex, as ταῦρος βοῦς, “a bull”, joins the two in the other order in Book 17 (17.389; see [[cattle]]). They are broad-browed and loud-bellowing (20.495, 20.497; see [[broad-browed]] and [[loud-bellowing]]), and they are yoked to tread out white barley on a threshing floor; as the barley is husked under their feet, so Achilles' horses trample corpses and shields (20.495–499; see [[bulls-on-the-threshing-floor]]). The bull at the altar and the bulls at the threshing are both likenesses of men dying under Achilles.
