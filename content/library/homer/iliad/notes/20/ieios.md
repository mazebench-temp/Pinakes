---
summary: The narrator turns to Apollo a second time, as the gods of the Trojan side sit on Callicolone: “around you, archer Phoebus, and Ares, sacker of cities” (20.152).
---

The first address, at the wall, came at the moment of the god's greatest ease (15.365; see the Book 15 note). This one places him with Ares at the center of the gods who take the Trojans' part, sitting apart from the war (20.151–155; [[callicolone]], [[apostrophe]]). See [[apollo]] and [[phoebus]].
