summary: Might comes from the gods. Apollo “put noble might into” Aeneas and “breathed great might into the shepherd of the people” (20.80, 20.110), as Zeus once “roused my might and my nimble knees” (20.93). Achilles goes to meet Aeneas driven by “his might and his proud spirit”, like the lion that rushes on “in his fury” (20.172–174); Hector says that Achilles' might is “like gleaming iron” (20.372), and when the armies meet “their might mingled together” (20.374). Twice Achilles springs at a man “eager to kill him” (20.346, 20.442).
grc: ["φέρεται μένει"]

μένος ἠΰ, “noble might”, is what Zeus breathed into Achilles' horses as they wept for Patroclus (17.456). Hector's comparison doubles itself, “even if his hands are like fire, / if his hands are like fire” (20.371–372; see [[hands-like-fire]] and [[epanalepsis]]). See [[wounded-lion]] and [[thymos]].
