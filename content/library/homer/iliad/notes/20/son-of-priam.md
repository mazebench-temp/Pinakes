---
summary: Achilles longs above all to meet Hector, “son of Priam” (20.76–77); Aeneas answers Apollo, who speaks in Lycaon's likeness, “Son of Priam” (20.87); and Polydorus, Priam's youngest son, is the son of Priam whom Achilles kills (20.407–408).
---

Aeneas addresses the god by the patronymic of the man whose voice he has taken: Apollo “made his voice like that of Priam's son Lycaon” (20.81; [[lycaon-son-of-priam]], [[apollo]]). Achilles' wish to meet the son of Priam is granted at the end of the book, when Hector sees his brother Polydorus fall (20.419–423). See [[hector]], [[priam]] and [[polydorus-son-of-priam]].
