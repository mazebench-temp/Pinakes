---
summary: Strabo uses Book 20 for the Troad, for Lydia and for the date of the poet. He places Callicolone near the old site of Ilium, finds Dardania and the three stages of settlement in Aeneas' genealogy, and answers Demetrius of Scepsis, who made Scepsis Aeneas' royal seat, with Poseidon's prophecy, which keeps Aeneas in Troy; some, he says, rewrote it for the Romans. He reports that some took the bull of the Heliconian lord for the Ionians' sacrifice at the Panionia, that later writers made an Erichthonius a founder of both Trojans and Athenians, and that some added the verse on Hyde to the Catalogue.
---

- The bull dragged around the Heliconian lord, taken for the Ionians' sacrifice at the Panionia or for the rites of Helice, and the drowning of Helice (*Geography* 8.7.2, on 20.403–404): [[helice-or-the-panionia]].
- **Lyrnessus and Pedasus.** Arguing that the whole coast opposite Lesbos was Trojan and subject to Priam, Strabo counts the places that Achilles sacked, Thebe, Lyrnessus and Pedasus of the Leleges among them, and quotes Aeneas' “and sacked Lyrnessus and Pedasus” (*Geography* 13.1.7, on 20.92). Pedasus belonged to Altes, king of the Leleges, whose daughter bore Priam Lycaon and Polydorus, the two sons of Priam named in this book: Apollo takes Lycaon's voice, and Achilles kills Polydorus (20.81, 20.407–418; 21.84–91; see [[lyrnessus]], [[pedasus-on-the-satnioeis]], [[leleges]], [[lycaon-son-of-priam]] and [[polydorus-son-of-priam]]).
- Dardania in the foothills of Ida, and Plato's three stages of settlement, with Dardanus for the second and Ilus for the third (*Geography* 13.1.24–25, quoting 20.215–218): [[plato-the-slopes-of-ida]] and [[dardania]].
- **Callicolone.** Ten stades above the Village of the Ilians, where the old Ilium is thought to have stood, is Callicolone, a hill past which the Simoeis flows five stades away. So it is reasonable, he says, that Ares should call to the Trojans now from the height of the city and now from beside the Simoeis on Callicolone, since the battle was fought on the Scamandrian plain; but Callicolone is forty stades from the Ilium of his own day, too far for the battle line to reach (*Geography* 13.1.35, quoting 20.51–53). His text has ὦρτο, “rose up”, where ours has αὖε, “shouted”, at the start of 20.51. See [[callicolone]], [[simoeis]] and [[ares]].
- **Erichthonius.** Later writers, Strabo says, brought a Teucer to the Troad from the Attic deme of Troes, now called Xypete, and denied that any Teucrians came from Crete; as a sign of the bond between the Trojans and the Athenians they cited an Erichthonius among the founders of both peoples. The traces shown in the plain of Thebe and at Chryse, he thinks, agree better with Homer's verses (*Geography* 13.1.48, on 20.219–230): [[erichthonius]] and [[smintheus-and-the-mice]].
- Demetrius' Scepsis, the royal seat of Aeneas between his land and Lyrnessus, and Homer's answer in Poseidon's prophecy (*Geography* 13.1.52–53, quoting 20.188–192 and 20.306–308): [[strabo-on-the-rule-of-aeneas]].
- Hyde, “beneath snowy Tmolus”, which some added to the Catalogue's Maeonians, and the Gygaean lake, forty stades from Sardis, with the Hyllus flowing into the Hermus (*Geography* 13.4.5–6, 9.2.20, on 20.385–392): [[hyle-or-hyde]], [[gygaean-lake]], [[hyllus]] and [[hermus]].
