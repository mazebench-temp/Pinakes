summary: Aeneas goes “through the champions” to face Achilles (20.111); Achilles leaps “along the ranks” to call on every man, and promises, “I will go right through their ranks” (20.353, 20.362); Apollo warns Hector, “no longer step out at all as a champion against Achilles, / but wait for him in the crowd and out of the din” (20.376–377). Polydorus, whom his father would not let fight, “rushed through the champions, until he lost his dear life” (20.412).
grc: [προμάχιζε, στιχὸς]
---

The champions (πρόμαχοι) fight in front of the crowd. Walter Leaf takes φλοῖσβος, “din” (20.377), as the surging mass of warriors behind the champions, the οὐλαμός, “crowd”, into which Hector goes back (20.379; *Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 20.377), and Achilles' στιχός (20.362) as the enemy's line of battle, a singular found again only at 16.173 (Leaf on 20.362). Poseidon carries Aeneas “over many ranks of heroes, and many ranks of horses” to the far edge of the war, where the Caucones are arming (20.326–329; see [[caucones]] and [[retreat]]).
