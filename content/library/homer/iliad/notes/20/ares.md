summary: Ares goes to the Trojans and, when the gods come into the throng, shouts to them like a dark storm, now from the height of the city, now running beside the Simoeis on Callicolone; Athena is set against him under his name Enyalius. He sits with Apollo on Callicolone. His name stands for war in Achilles' mouth, who longs to glut Ares with Hector's blood and says that not even Ares could deal alone with so great a battle.
# Unnamed: his war cry along the Simoeis (20.52-53).
refs: [20.52-53]
passages:
  - 20.51-53 | Ares' war cry
---

**To the Trojans.** “And to the Trojans went Ares of the gleaming helmet” (20.38; see [[the-battle-of-the-gods]] and [[of-the-gleaming-helmet]]). κορυθαίολος is said only here of anyone but Hector (Walter Leaf, *Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 20.38; see the translation's note on 20.38–39). Before the gods come down, Achilles is already “the equal of Ares, ruin of mortals” in the Trojans' eyes (20.46; see [[equal-of-ares]] and [[ruin-of-mortals]]).

**The war cry.** Athena shouts by the ditch and on the shore (20.48–50; see [[athena]]), and Ares answers her: “And Ares on the other side shouted, like a dark storm, / shrilly calling on the Trojans from the topmost height of the city, / and now running beside the Simoeis on Callicolone” (20.51–53; see [[like-a-black-storm]], [[simoeis]] and [[callicolone]]). In Book 5 he went among the Trojan ranks in the likeness of Acamas, the leader of the Thracians, and urged them on (5.461–462); here he calls to them in his own voice. Strabo quotes the three lines, beginning ὦρτο δʼ Ἄρης, “Ares rose up”, for αὖε δʼ Ἄρης, “Ares shouted”, in an argument about where the old city of Troy stood (*Geography* 13.1.35; see [[callicolone]]).

**Against Athena.** In the pairs, “against Enyalius the gray-eyed goddess Athena” (20.69; see [[enyalius]]). In Book 21 Ares begins the fighting: he reminds Athena that she drove Diomedes' spear into him (5.855–859) and strikes her aegis, and she fells him with a boundary stone, so that his fall covers seven plethra (21.391–408).

**On Callicolone.** Poseidon advises his party to sit apart, unless Ares or Phoebus Apollo begins the battle (20.138), and the gods of the Trojans sit “on the brows of Callicolone, / around you, archer Phoebus, and Ares, sacker of cities” (20.151–152; see [[apollo]] and [[sacker-of-cities]]).

**War itself.** Achilles longs to meet Hector, “for it was with his blood above all that his heart bade him / glut Ares, the warrior with the oxhide shield” (20.77–78; see [[achilles]] and [[with-the-oxhide-shield]]). The second verse ends Diomedes' answer to Pandarus and Aeneas in Book 5: they will not stop “before one or the other of you has fallen / and glutted Ares with blood, the warrior with the oxhide shield” (5.288–289). Hicetaon, Priam's brother, is an “offshoot of Ares” (20.238; see [[offshoot-of-ares]] and [[hicetaon]]), and Achilles, rallying the Achaeans, says that “not even Ares, though he is an immortal god, nor Athena / could deal with the mouth of so great a battle and toil through it” (20.358–359).
