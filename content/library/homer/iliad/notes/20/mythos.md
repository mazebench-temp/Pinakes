summary: Hera and Poseidon speak among the gods (20.114, 20.292); Aeneas will go down to Hades, Poseidon fears, “because he obeyed the words of Apollo the Far-Shooter” (20.294–295); the tongue of mortals has “many words in it / of every kind” (20.248–249); and Hector says that Achilles will not “put fulfillment to all his words, / but one he will fulfill, and another he will cut short halfway” (20.369–370).
grc: [μύθοις]
---

Hector's words echo the maxim of Achilles' lament for Patroclus, “Zeus does not fulfill for men all their plans” (18.328; see [[zeus-does-not-fulfill-all-plans]]), now said of Achilles himself. Walter Leaf would read the future κολούσει, or else take the line as a maxim first said of Zeus that has found its way into the speech (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 20.370; see the translation's note on 20.370). μυθήσασθαι is the verb of the taunts and insults that Aeneas and Hector are able to speak (20.202, 20.246, 20.433; see [[kertomia]] and [[oneidos]]).
