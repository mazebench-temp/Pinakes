summary: Aeneas begins his genealogy with Dardanus, the son whom Zeus fathered first, who founded Dardania on the slopes of Ida before Ilios was built in the plain and fathered Erichthonius (20.215–219). Poseidon saves Aeneas so that the lineage of Dardanus, whom Zeus loved above all his children by mortal women, may not perish (20.302–305).

**The head of the line.** Aeneas begins with the god:

{{quote:838514ba-e275-58b0-8eda-7d0d2d7532f2}}

Δάρδανον αὖ πρῶτον τέκετο νεφεληγερέτα Ζεύς, “first Zeus the cloud-gatherer fathered Dardanus” (20.215). Idomeneus' lineage begins in the same way, “Zeus first fathered Minos” (13.450; see [[genealogy-in-battle]]). Walter Leaf printed ἄρ for αὖ, since αὖ, “in turn”, cannot begin a story, and he observes that the verse can only ever have been a beginning: the line can go no higher than Zeus, and πρῶτον shows that it was not one of a series of genealogies of sons of Zeus (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 20.215; see the translation's note on 20.215). Dardanus founded [[dardania]] (20.216; see [[plato-the-slopes-of-ida]]), and “Dardanus in turn fathered a son, king Erichthonius” (20.219; see [[erichthonius]]). From Erichthonius' son Tros the house divides into the branches of Ilus and Assaracus, which end in Hector and in Aeneas himself (20.230–240; see [[tros]] and [[assaracus]]).

**The lineage of Dardanus.** When Achilles is about to kill Aeneas, Poseidon gives the gods the reason to save him:

{{quote:5de3e051-3cfc-5965-b98b-2b5d23d08729}}

The lineage of Dardanus, γενεή, is the word of Aeneas' own genealogy (20.203, 20.214, 20.241; see [[genee]]). φίλατο, “loved”, is used elsewhere in Homer only of Athena's love for the craftsman Phereclus (5.61). Zeus has many children by mortal women, and he has himself listed some of them (14.317–327; see [[the-loves-of-zeus]]); Dardanus, Poseidon says, was the one he loved most. The next verses set the two branches apart: the son of Cronus “has come to hate the lineage of Priam”, and Aeneas and his children's children shall rule the Trojans (20.306–308; see [[priam]] and [[the-rule-of-aeneas]]). The house of Dardanus is to live on in the branch of Assaracus, not in Priam's.
