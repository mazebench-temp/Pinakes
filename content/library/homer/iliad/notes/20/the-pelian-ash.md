**The cast.**

{{quote:e236fa40-b294-5b03-b16b-f756668821f5}}

Πηλιὰς … μελίη, the name in the nominative, is found only here; the other four times it is the accusative Πηλιάδα μελίην (16.143, 19.390, 21.162, 22.133). The spear goes through the edge of the shield, where the metal and the hide were thinnest, “and the spear, passing over his back, stood fixed in the ground, / still straining” (20.279–280). ἔστη ἱεμένη, “was stopped in its eager course”, as Walter Leaf renders it, comparing 15.543 and 20.399 (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 20.280). It had parted both circles of the shield (20.280–281; see [[shield]]), and Aeneas, who had crouched under it, stood “in fear that the shaft had stuck so near him” (20.283; see [[aeneas]]). Achilles throws only once in the duel, and then draws his sword (20.283–284; see [[sword]]). The cast follows the order of Ajax's against Hector, which also came “second in his turn” and struck the shield “balanced on every side” (7.248–250; see [[achilles]] and [[spear]]).

**Poseidon and the spear.** When Poseidon has poured a mist over Achilles' eyes, “he drew the ash spear with its good bronze / out of the shield of great-hearted Aeneas, / and set it down before the feet of Achilles” (20.322–324; see [[poseidon]] and [[well-bronzed]]). Aristarchus athetized the three verses: the spear did not stick in the shield, but went through it, as 20.276–279 say, and stood in the ground, so how could Poseidon draw it out of the shield? Leaf answers that the difficulty could be evaded by supposing that the shaft had carried the shield with it, but that this is not the simple sense of 276–279, and the verses are needless (on 20.322; see the translation's note on 20.322–324). When the mist lifts, Achilles sees it first: “here is my spear lying on the ground, and I do not see the man at all / at whom I hurled it, eager to kill him” (20.345–346).

After the duel the narrator calls Achilles' weapon ἔγχος and δόρυ, and three times ἄκων, “javelin” (20.413, 20.455, 20.486), and does not say again that it is his father's spear. He will throw the Pelian ash at Asteropaeus in the river (21.161–162) and come at Hector brandishing it (22.133).
