summary: In Aeneas' genealogy Ilus is the first named of the three sons of Tros and the father of noble Laomedon, Priam's father (20.231–237).
# Ἶλός (20.232) and Ἶλος (20.236): the nominative occurs only here in Homer.
grc: [Ἶλος]
---

Ἶλός τʼ Ἀσσάρακός τε καὶ ἀντίθεος Γανυμήδης, “Ilus and Assaracus and godlike Ganymede” (20.232): Ilus is named first of the three sons of Tros (see [[tros]], [[assaracus]] and [[ganymede]]). With him begins the branch that leads to Priam: “And Ilus in turn fathered a son, noble Laomedon, / and Laomedon fathered Tithonus and Priam” (20.236–237; see [[laomedon]] and [[priam]]). The verse of Laomedon's birth is the verse of Deucalion's in Idomeneus' lineage with the names changed, Μίνως δʼ αὖ τέκεθʼ υἱὸν ἀμύμονα Δευκαλίωνα (13.451) and Ἶλος δʼ αὖ τέκεθʼ υἱὸν ἀμύμονα Λαομέδοντα (20.236; see [[genealogy-in-battle]]).

The genealogy says nothing else of him. Elsewhere in the Iliad he is a grave mound in the middle of the plain, “the mound of ancient Ilus, descendant of Dardanus” (11.166; see [[the-tomb-of-ilus]]); here he takes his place in the line between Tros and Laomedon, a great-grandson of Dardanus (20.215–236; see [[dardanus]]). The poem does not connect his name with Ilios, which in the genealogy is “not yet / built in the plain” when Dardanus founds Dardania (20.216–217; see [[dardania]] and [[troy]]).
