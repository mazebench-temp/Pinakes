---
summary: Proclus answers Plato's censure of the battle of the gods. He reads Zeus' assembly through Themis as the gods turned back to their maker before he sends them to care for the world, and the eleven gods of the battle and their pairs as the oppositions of the world below the moon, reconciled by Aphrodite. He also defends the dank dwellings of Hades, and quotes the river that the gods call Xanthus for the difference between divine and human names.
---

- Zeus' assembly through Themis, the gods sent to help either side, the two parties and the pairs (*On the Republic* I 87–95, 106–107 Kroll; *On the Timaeus* III 200–201, 227 Diehl; *On the Cratylus* 99, on 20.4–74): [[proclus-on-the-battle-of-the-gods]].
- The dwellings of Hades, “terrible and dank, which even the gods hate”, fitting for the lowest places of the world (*On the Republic* I 118, 122 Kroll, on 20.64–65): [[plato-the-terrors-of-hades]].
- The river “whom the gods call Xanthus, and men Scamander”, with the bird of 14.291, for names that show the whole being of what they name (*On the Timaeus* I 273 Diehl; *On the Cratylus* 71, on 20.74): [[plato-chalcis-and-cymindis]].
- Achilles' fight with the river Xanthus, set beside the poem's battles of men and of “the so-called Theomachy” (*On the Republic* I 148–149 Kroll): [[proclus-on-the-battle-of-the-gods]] and [[title-of-book-20]].
