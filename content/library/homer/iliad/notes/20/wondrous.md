---
summary: Poseidon scatters “the heaven-sent mist” from Achilles' eyes (20.341–342), and Achilles rages through the Trojans as “a wondrous fire rages through the deep valleys / of a dry mountain” (20.490–491).
---

θεσπιδαὲς πῦρ is the fire of the fight at the wall and the ships (12.177, 12.441, 15.597), and in the next book it is Hephaestus' fire against the river (21.342, 21.381). Here it is the likeness of Achilles himself (20.490–494; [[forest-fire]], [[like-a-god]]). The mist that Poseidon scatters is the one he had poured over Achilles' eyes to take Aeneas away (20.321; [[achlys]], [[divine-rescue]]).
