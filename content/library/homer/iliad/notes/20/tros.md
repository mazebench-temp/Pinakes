Τρῶα δʼ Ἐριχθόνιος τέκετο Τρώεσσιν ἄνακτα, “And Erichthonius fathered Tros, lord of the Trojans” (20.230): the eponym and his people stand side by side in the verse, Τρῶα and Τρώεσσιν (see [[trojans]] and [[erichthonius]]). The next verse, “and to Tros in turn were born three noble sons”, is the verse of the three sons of Portheus, which has the father's name in the dative (14.115; Walter Leaf, *Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 20.231; see [[portheus]]). Two of the sons carry the line on, Ilus to Laomedon, Priam and Hector, and Assaracus to Capys, Anchises and Aeneas (20.236–240; see [[ilus]], [[assaracus]] and [[capys]]), so that Hector and Aeneas are both fourth in descent from Tros, “third cousins”, as Leaf puts it (on 20.240). The third, godlike Ganymede, the gods snatched up to pour wine for Zeus (20.232–235; see [[ganymede]]). Book 5 tells what Zeus gave Tros for him: a breed of horses, the best under the dawn and the sun (5.265–267; see [[ganymede-and-the-horses-of-tros]]).
