---
summary: Aeneas answers Achilles' taunt with a lineage told from father to son, from Zeus to Dardanus, Erichthonius, Tros and his three sons, and down to “and Anchises fathered me, and Priam brilliant Hector” (20.215–240). Achilles had mocked his hope of Priam's place, since Priam “has sons” (20.183), and Priam's youngest son dies against his father's will (20.407–418).
refs: [20.183, 20.206-209, 20.215-241, 20.304-308, 20.407-418]
---

The genealogy ends in two cousins, Hector and Aeneas, each fourth in descent from Tros (Walter Leaf, *Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 20.240), and Poseidon's prophecy carries Aeneas' line forward to “his children's children” (20.308; see [[genee]], [[genealogy-in-battle]], [[pater]] and [[bereaved-parents]]).
