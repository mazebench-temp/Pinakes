summary: The speeches of the book end in deeds: Zeus' in the war he rouses (20.31), Apollo's in the might he breathes into Aeneas (20.110), Poseidon's in his leading the gods to the wall of Heracles (20.144), Aeneas' and Hector's in spear casts (20.259, 20.438), and Achilles' threat over the mist in a killing (20.455).
except: [20.347, 20.437]
---

“So he spoke, boasting” ends Achilles' boast over Iphition, and darkness covers the dead man's eyes in the same verse (20.393; [[euchomai]], [[darkness-covered-his-eyes]]). The two exhortations close alike, “So he spoke, urging them on” (20.364, 20.373; [[exhortation]]). In two verses ἦ καί is not the formula: “Truly Aeneas too was dear to the immortal gods” (ἦ ῥα καί, 20.347) and “since my weapon too is sharp at the point” (ἐπεὶ ἦ καί, 20.437), and this note excepts them.
