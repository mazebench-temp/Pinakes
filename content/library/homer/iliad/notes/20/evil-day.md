---
summary: Hera reminds Poseidon that she and Athena swore “never to ward off the evil day from the Trojans, / not even when all Troy blazes with ravening fire” (20.315–316).
---

The oath turns the formula of Book 9 around: there the question was whether Achilles would ward off the evil day from the Danaans (9.251; see the entry); here two goddesses have sworn never to ward it off from the Trojans. In Book 21 the same three verses are the oath that the river Xanthus swears to Hera, begging her to stop Hephaestus' fire (21.373–376 = 20.315–317; Walter Leaf, *Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 20.315). See [[hera]], [[oath]] and [[ravening]].
