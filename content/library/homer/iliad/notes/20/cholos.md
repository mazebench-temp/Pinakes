summary: Zeus fears what Achilles will do “now that he is terribly enraged at heart over his companion” (20.29). In Aeneas' comparison the women quarrel “in their anger over heart-devouring strife”, “for anger bids them say these too” (20.253–255); Poseidon fears that the son of Cronus will “be angry, if Achilles / kills this man” (20.301–302).
grc: [χώεται]
---

ἑταίρου is the genitive of cause, “over his companion”, as Poseidon is “angry over the Cyclops” (Κύκλωπος κεχόλωται, *Odyssey* 1.69; Walter Leaf, *Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 20.29) and as Thetis left Achilles by the sea “angry at heart for the sake of the fair-belted woman” (1.429). The anger of the quarrel has become anger for Patroclus, and it is turned on the Trojans (see [[hetairos]] and [[patroclus]]). Aeneas gives anger the last word on quarrels: it is what makes the women say what is not true (20.255; see [[women-quarreling-in-the-street]], [[eris]] and [[neikos]]).
