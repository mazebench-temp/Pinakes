---
summary: In the simile of the lion, the men gathered to kill him are “a whole people” (πᾶς δῆμος, 20.166), and Iphition was born “in the rich land of Hyde” (20.385).
grc: [δῆμος]
---

Walter Leaf takes the δῆμος of the simile as the community of a country district, a “village”, as with δήμου ἀρίστω, “the best of their people” (11.328), and notes the plural participle ἀγρόμενοι, “gathered together”, after the collective noun (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 20.166; see [[wounded-lion]]). At 20.385 the word is the land itself, in the phrase used of Sarpedon's Lycia, “in the rich land of Lycia” (Λυκίης ἐν πίονι δήμῳ, 16.437; see [[iphition]] and [[hyde]]).
