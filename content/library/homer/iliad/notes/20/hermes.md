# Ἑρμῆς, the contracted name (20.72); elsewhere in the Iliad Ἑρμείας, and Ἑρμέᾳ (5.390, in the Book 5 note).
grc: [Ἑρμῆς]
---

“Hera went to the gathering of the ships, and Pallas Athena, / and Poseidon the earth-holder, and the great helper / Hermes, who excels in his shrewd mind” (20.33–35; see [[the-battle-of-the-gods]] and [[shrewd-mind]]). He is one of the gods whom Poseidon named in Book 15, when he threatened Zeus with incurable anger if he spared Ilios against their will (15.213–217; see [[poseidon]]). ἐπὶ … κέκασται, “excels”, is a verb in tmesis; ἐπὶ φρεσί cannot be taken together (Walter Leaf, *Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 20.35; see the translation's note on 20.35).

In the pairs of gods, “against Leto stood strong Hermes, the great helper” (20.72; see [[leto]]). His titles here are rare. ἐριούνης is found in the Iliad only at 20.34 (again *Odyssey* 8.322, also beside Poseidon the earth-holder), and ἐριούνιος here and four times in Book 24 (24.360, 24.440, 24.457, 24.679). Both are usually derived from ἐρι- and ὀνίνημι, “the great helper”, though Leaf thinks a derivation from ἔριον, “wool”, equally possible for the shepherds' god (on 20.34; see [[great-helper]]). σῶκος, “strong”, is found only here, and as a man's name at 11.427, and its sense is guessed from the tragedians' σωκεῖν (on 20.72; see [[sokos]] and the translation's notes on 20.34 and 20.72). Ἑρμῆς, the contracted form of his name, is found four times in the Odyssey and nowhere else in the Iliad; Book 5 has the other contracted form, Ἑρμέᾳ (5.390), and elsewhere he is Ἑρμείας (Leaf on 20.72).

He does not fight even in Book 21, where he tells Leto that it is hard to come to blows with the wives of Zeus the cloud-gatherer, and lets her boast among the immortals that she beat him (21.497–501).
