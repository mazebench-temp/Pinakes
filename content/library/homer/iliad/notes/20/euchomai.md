---
summary: The verb has all its senses in the book. Aeneas says that Achilles would not win easily, “not even if he boasts that he is all of bronze” (20.102), and claims his own birth and lineage, “I claim that I was born the son of great-hearted Anchises” and “This is the lineage and the blood I claim” (20.208, 20.241). Apollo bids him “pray to the everlasting gods” (20.104), and Achilles tells Hector that Apollo is the god “to whom you must pray when you go into the thud of javelins” (20.451). Achilles thought that Aeneas “boasted idly” (20.348), and he boasts over Iphition and speaks “in triumph” at the sight of Hector (20.388, 20.393, 20.424).
---

The claim and the boast are one verb, and Achilles hears Aeneas' claim to a divine mother as a boast until he sees a god's work (20.347–348; see [[seeing-the-gods]]). Aeneas' words at 20.208–209 are Sthenelus' of him in Book 5, put into the first person, with “great-hearted” for “noble” (5.247–248; see the translation's note on 20.205–209), and his last line is Glaucus' (6.211; see [[genealogy-in-battle]]). παγχάλκεος, “all of bronze”, means invulnerable (Walter Leaf, *Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 20.101).
