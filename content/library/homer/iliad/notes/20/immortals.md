---
summary: The book sets mortals and immortals side by side: Hades fears that his dwellings will be laid bare “to mortals and immortals” (20.64); Ganymede is snatched up “so that he might dwell among the immortals” (20.235); Achilles is “dearer to the immortals” than Aeneas (20.334), and Aeneas, Achilles finds, “was dear to the immortal gods” (20.347); Hector could fight “even with the immortals in words” (20.367).
---

Hera wants Achilles to know “that the best of the immortals love him” (20.122–123), and Poseidon and Hera speak among the immortals of their oaths and plans (20.292, 20.314). Achilles' wonder at Aeneas' escape is the turn of the argument: Poseidon has told Aeneas that Achilles is dearer to the gods, and Achilles concludes that Aeneas too is dear to them (20.334, 20.347).
