---
summary: The Xanthus stands against Hephaestus as “the great deep-eddying river, / whom the gods call Xanthus, and men Scamander” (20.73–74).
---

The pairing ends with fire against water, the one pair in which Walter Leaf sees a principle (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 20.67; see the translation's note on 20.67–74). The river and the fire fight in Book 21. See [[scamander]], [[hephaestus]] and [[language-of-gods-and-men]].
