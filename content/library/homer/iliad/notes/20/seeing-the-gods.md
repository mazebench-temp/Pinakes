summary: Apollo rouses Aeneas in the likeness of Lycaon, and Aeneas answers him as “Son of Priam” (20.81–87). Hera says that “gods are hard to face when they appear in plain sight” (20.131), and the gods of both parties sit apart, the Achaean party wrapped in cloud (20.149–152). Poseidon pours a mist over Achilles' eyes and takes it away, and Achilles calls what he sees “a great wonder” (20.321–344); Hector knows Apollo by his voice, “when he heard the voice of the god as he spoke” (20.380).
refs: [20.79-87, 20.129-131, 20.149-152, 20.321-348, 20.375-380, 20.443-447]

Achilles knows the god by his work. His spear lies at his feet and the man is gone, and he concludes, “Truly Aeneas too was dear to the immortal gods” (20.345–347; see [[achlys]]). When Apollo hides Hector “in thick mist”, Achilles strikes the deep mist three times, and the fourth time rushes on “like a god” (20.444–447; see [[daimon]]).
