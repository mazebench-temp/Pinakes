---
summary: “so his might and his proud spirit urged Achilles on / to go to meet great-hearted Aeneas” (20.174–175); and Hippodamas, killed as he flees, bellows like the bull, and “his proud spirit left his bones” (20.406).
---

The first passage ends the simile of the lion, whose proud spirit drives it on in another lion simile (12.300; [[like-a-lion]], [[wounded-lion]]). The second ends the simile of the bull dragged around the Heliconian lord (20.403–406; [[the-bull-of-the-heliconian-lord]]); the Odyssey uses the same half-verse of the steersman killed by the falling mast (*Odyssey* 12.414). See [[thymos]].
