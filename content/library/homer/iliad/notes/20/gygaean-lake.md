γενεὴ δέ τοί ἐστʼ ἐπὶ λίμνῃ / Γυγαίῃ, “but your birthplace is by the lake, / the Gygaean” (20.390–391; see [[iphition]], [[genee]] and [[achilles]]). In the Catalogue the lake “bore” the Maeonian leaders Mesthles and Antiphus, as a nymph bears a son (2.865); here, Walter Leaf notes, it is purely a place, not a person (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 20.390). Iphition's mother was a Naiad, and his father's τέμενος, his “domain”, lay by the lake, between the Hyllus and the Hermus (20.384, 20.391–392; see [[otrynteus]], [[hyllus]], [[hermus]] and [[nymphs]]). The boast sets the place where the man dies against the place he came from: ἐνθάδε τοι θάνατος, “here is your death” (20.390). Strabo, who puts the lake forty stadia from Sardis, calls it the lake “named by the poet” (*Geography* 13.4.5).
