---
summary: Achilles' first killings after his return: fourteen men by name, from Iphition, “first,” to Rhigmus and his attendant Areithous (20.381–489). Iphition and Polydorus are given histories, Hippodamas is likened to a bellowing bull, Tros clasps his knees, and none is stripped of his armor.
passages:
  - 20.381-418 | Iphition, Demoleon, Hippodamas, Polydorus
  - 20.455-489 | Dryops to Areithous
---

**The first man.** The scene begins in the type's own words: “and first he killed Iphition” (πρῶτον δʼ ἕλεν, 20.382). His father, his mother, a Naiad, and his birthplace under Tmolus are given before the blow, and Achilles' boast after it names his father's domain by the Gygaean lake (20.383–392; [[iphition]], [[most-terrible-of-men]], [[fell-with-a-thud]]). Walter Leaf suspects 383–395 as a later insertion (see the translation's note on 20.381–395).

**Blows and falls.** The wounds are told with the type's precision, and some are told in verses from earlier books: Demoleon's brain spattered inside the bronze helmet (20.397–400 ~ 12.183–186; [[bronze-cheeked]]), Echeclus' eyes seized by purple death (20.476–477 = 16.333–334; [[purple-death]]). Mulius is speared through one ear and out the other (20.472–474); Deucalion, his arm pierced, waits “seeing death before him,” and his head flies off with the helmet (20.478–483). Hippodamas, stabbed in the back as he leaps from his chariot, bellows like the bull dragged around the Heliconian lord (20.401–406; [[the-bull-of-the-heliconian-lord]]).

**Histories.** Polydorus, the youngest and dearest of Priam's sons, whom his father would not let fight, is killed showing off his speed (20.407–418; [[polydorus-son-of-priam]]), and his fall brings Hector against Achilles (20.419–423). Tros, son of Alastor, clasps Achilles' knees and is killed as he begs (20.463–472; [[supplication]], [[sweet-tempered]]).

**No stripping.** After Hector's rescue the killings follow one another without a pause, Dryops, Demuchus, the two sons of Bias, Tros, Mulius, Echeclus, Deucalion, Rhigmus and Areithous (20.455–489; [[dryops]], [[demuchus]]). Achilles takes no armor: he stabs Dryops, “and he left him there” (20.456). The series ends in two similes, the forest fire and the bulls on the threshing floor (20.490–503; [[forest-fire]], [[bulls-on-the-threshing-floor]]). See [[the-aristeia]], [[death-in-battle]] and [[achilles]].
