---
summary: Aeneas would have hit Achilles with the stone, and Achilles would have killed him with the sword, “had not Poseidon the earth-shaker been quick to see it” (20.288–291).
grc: ["ἔνθά κεν Αἰνείας"]
---

“Then Aeneas would have hit him with the stone as he rushed on, / on helmet or on the shield that had kept grim destruction from him, / and the son of Peleus would have taken his life with his sword at close quarters, / had not Poseidon the earth-shaker been quick to see it” (20.288–291). Two outcomes are set aside at once, the stone's and the sword's, and the second is the one that counts. The second verse says what the shield had done, not what it would have done (see the translation's note on 20.289 and [[grim]]). The god who sees the danger is the one who will save Aeneas (20.318–329; [[divine-rescue]], [[earth-shaker]]).
