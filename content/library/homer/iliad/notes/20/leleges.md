---
summary: Aeneas recalls that when Achilles sacked Lyrnessus and Pedasus, Athena went before him and bade him “slay the Leleges and the Trojans with his bronze spear” (20.96).
---

The Leleges are named in Aeneas' memory of his flight from Ida, with Lyrnessus and Pedasus (20.92–96; see [[the-sack-of-lyrnessus]]). Walter Leaf takes them here for the people of Lyrnessus (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 20.96). The poem itself gives them Pedasus: in Book 21 their king Altes holds steep Pedasus on the Satnioeis (Πήδασον αἰπήεσσαν ἔχων ἐπὶ Σατνιόεντι, 21.85–87; see [[altes]] and [[pedasus-on-the-satnioeis]]). Strabo too sets Pedasus among the Leleges and makes Lyrnessus the town of Mynes, the seat of one of the two dynasties of the Cilicians (*Geography* 13.1.7, 13.1.60–61; see [[cilicians]] and [[strabo-on-the-city-of-mynes]]). Apollo has just spoken in the voice of Lycaon, a grandson of Altes (20.81; 21.85–88; see [[lycaon-son-of-priam]]).

Dolon named the Leleges and the Caucones together among the allies camped toward the sea (10.428–429). Both return in Aeneas' part of this book, the Leleges in his memory of his flight and the Caucones at the edge of the battle where Poseidon sets him down (20.96, 20.329; see [[caucones]] and [[aeneas]]).
