**The gold of the shield.** “Nor did the heavy spear of wise-hearted Aeneas then / break the shield; for the gold held it back, the gifts of the god” (20.267–268; see [[the-shield-of-achilles]]). The verse returns when Asteropaeus' spear strikes the same shield (21.164–165). Walter Leaf takes the gold, as the most precious part, to stand for the whole metal facing, and δῶρα for its several pieces (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 20.268). The next four verses make the five layers metals, “two of bronze, and two of tin inside, / and one of gold, and in that the ash spear was stopped” (20.271–272; see [[bronze]] and [[tin]]); Aristarchus athetized them, and Leaf objects that a layer of gold hidden in the middle would be neither useful nor ornamental (on 20.269; see the translation's note on 20.269–272). Gold was one of the four metals Hephaestus cast into his fire when he made the shield (18.474–475; see [[hephaestus]]).

**The golden clasps.** Polydorus was struck in the back “where the golden clasps of the belt / held it together, and the corselet met it in a double layer” (20.414–415; see [[polydorus-son-of-priam]] and [[belt]]). The verses are those of Athena guiding Pandarus' arrow to Menelaus' belt (4.132–133).

Artemis is “she of the golden distaff” (20.70; see [[of-the-golden-distaff]]).
