---
summary: Achilles and Aeneas meet “between the two armies, eager to fight” (20.159), in the verse of Glaucus and Diomedes (6.120), but no terms are set: the meeting is a challenge in battle, not an agreed duel (20.156–352). Like the formal duel of Book 3 it ends with a god's rescue, and so does Achilles' first meeting with Hector (20.419–454).
refs: [20.156-160, 20.176-177, 20.259-352, 20.419-454]
---

Poseidon is “quick to see it” (20.291), as Aphrodite was when Menelaus was dragging Paris away by his helmet (3.374), and Apollo carries Hector off “very easily, as a god can, and hid him in thick mist” (20.444), in the verse with which Aphrodite snatched Paris away (3.381; Walter Leaf, *Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 20.444). The gods who might have fought one another sit apart and watch (20.144–155; see [[watching-the-war]], [[achilles-and-aeneas]] and [[divine-rescue]]).
