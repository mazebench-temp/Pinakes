summary: Darkness covers the eyes of Iphition, killed by Achilles' spear (20.393), and of Tros, son of Alastor, as his life fails (20.471); a dark cloud enfolds Polydorus as he falls to his knees with a groan (20.417–418).
grc: ["νεφέλη δέ μιν ἀμφεκάλυψε"]
en: [a cloud enfolded him]
---

**Iphition and Tros.** The half-verse ends Achilles' first killing in the book, after his boast: “So he spoke, boasting, and darkness covered his eyes” (20.393; [[iphition]]); and it ends the death of the suppliant, “and darkness covered his eyes / as his life failed” (20.471–472; [[tros-son-of-alastor]], [[supplication]]).

**Polydorus.** “and he fell to his knees with a groan, and a cloud enfolded him, / a dark cloud” (20.417–418). The first half-verse is Phereclus' fall, where death enfolds him (5.68; see the Book 5 note), and the cloud is the black cloud of death of Book 16 (16.350; see the Book 16 note). But Polydorus is not yet dead: Hector sees him holding his bowels and sinking to the ground, and a mist is poured over Hector's own eyes (20.419–421; [[achlys]], [[polydorus-son-of-priam]]).
