---
summary: When Apollo has snatched Hector away, Achilles promises to finish him later, “if there is any god who is a helper to me as well” (20.453).
---

The six verses of Achilles' speech (20.449–454) are the words of Diomedes to Hector when Apollo saved him in Book 11 (11.362–367), and Walter Leaf thinks that they are in their own place here and were copied there (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 20.449). Aeneas had said that one of the gods is always at Achilles' side (20.98; see [[loigos]]), and Athena has just turned Hector's spear from him (20.438–440); in the river Poseidon will tell him that he and Athena are his helpers, with Zeus' consent (21.288–290).
