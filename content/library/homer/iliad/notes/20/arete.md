---
summary: Aeneas ends his genealogy: “But it is Zeus who increases and diminishes the prowess of men, / as he wishes; for he is the mightiest of all” (20.242–243). Polydorus, the youngest son of Priam, whom his father would not let fight, dies “showing off the prowess of his feet” (20.411).
---

Polydorus' prowess is speed, for “he surpassed them all in speed of foot” (20.410), and he shows it “in his childishness” (νηπιέῃσι, 20.411; see [[polydorus-son-of-priam]] and [[nepios]]). The phrase recalls Periphetes of Mycenae, better than his father “in every kind of prowess, both in running and in fighting” (15.642). Walter Leaf takes Aeneas' maxim as an answer to Achilles' sarcasm about his flight from Ida to Lyrnessus (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 20.242; see [[zeus-increases-and-diminishes]]).
