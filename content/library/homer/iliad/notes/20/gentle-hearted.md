---
summary: “for he was no sweet-tempered man, nor gentle-hearted, / but in a great fury” (20.467–468): the narrator on Achilles as Tros, son of Alastor, begs for his life; the word's only use in Homer.
---

Tros hoped that Achilles would spare him, “pitying a man of his own age” (20.465), and the narrator says at once why he would not (20.466–468; [[sweet-tempered]], [[pity]]). See [[tros-son-of-alastor]] and [[supplication]].
