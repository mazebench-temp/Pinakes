---
summary: Achilles asks whether Aeneas has come out “in the hope that you will rule over the horse-taming Trojans / with the honor of Priam” (20.180–181); Aeneas' genealogy makes Tros “lord of the Trojans” (20.230), and Poseidon foretells that “mighty Aeneas shall be lord over the Trojans, / and his children's children” (20.307–308). Among the gods the title goes to “Aidoneus, lord of those below” (20.61), “lord Poseidon” (20.67), “lord Apollo” (20.103) and “the Heliconian lord” (20.404).
---

The verb ἀνάσσω carries the book's question about Aeneas: Achilles mocks the hope (ἀνάξειν, 20.180), and Poseidon grants it in the same verb (ἀνάξει, 20.307; see [[the-rule-of-aeneas]] and [[kingship-and-authority]]). ἄναξ ἐνέρων Ἀϊδωνεύς answers Poseidon's “Hades, lord of those below” (Ἀΐδης ἐνέροισιν ἀνάσσων, 15.188; see [[hades]]), and the Heliconian lord is Poseidon himself (see [[heliconian-poseidon]]).
