---
summary: The women of Lyrnessus are led off as captives when Achilles sacks it (20.193–194). Aeneas likens the quarrel of two warriors to that of women who “go out into the middle of the street and quarrel with each other, / saying many things, true and not true” (20.254–255), and he will have no more of it.
refs: [20.193-194, 20.251-255]
---

Aristarchus athetized the comparison as unworthy of the speakers; the scholion adds that among barbarians, as among the Egyptians, women go out and abuse one another, and Walter Leaf answers that the scene suits the heroic age, when women were comparatively free (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 20.251; see the translation's note on 20.251–255). The warrior measures himself by not being like a woman, and turns from words to the bronze (20.256–258; see [[women-quarreling-in-the-street]], [[neikos]] and [[captive-women]]).
