---
summary: Leto goes to the Trojans with her children, Apollo and Artemis, and in the pairs of gods strong Hermes, the great helper, is set against her.
---

“and Leto and Xanthus and laughter-loving Aphrodite” (20.40) close the list of the gods who go to the Trojans, after Ares, Apollo and Artemis (20.38–40; see [[the-battle-of-the-gods]], [[apollo]] and [[artemis]]). In the pairs, “against Leto stood strong Hermes, the great helper” (20.72; see [[hermes]]). As the mother of Apollo and Artemis she stands with her children, as she did when she and Artemis healed Aeneas in Apollo's temple on Pergamus (5.445–448; see [[pergamus]]).

The pair never fight. In Book 21 Hermes tells her that he will not fight her, since it is hard to come to blows with the wives of Zeus the cloud-gatherer, and that she may boast among the immortals that she beat him by main force; Leto gathers up the bow and arrows that Artemis has dropped and follows her daughter (21.497–504).
