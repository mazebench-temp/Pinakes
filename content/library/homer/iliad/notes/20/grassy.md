---
summary: The nymphs who come to Zeus' assembly dwell in groves, in the springs of rivers “and the grassy meadows” (20.8–9).
grc: [ποιήεντα]
---

“nor any of the nymphs who dwell in the lovely groves / and the springs of rivers and the grassy meadows” (20.8–9; [[nymphs]], [[rivers]]). The second verse comes again in the Odyssey, where Odysseus wakes to the cries of Nausicaa's maids and wonders whether they are nymphs (*Odyssey* 6.122–124; see the translation's note on 20.7–9). πίσεα are water meadows (Walter Leaf, *Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 20.8). The Odyssey uses the same form of grassy glens (*Odyssey* 4.337, 17.128).
