  - 20.4-30 | Zeus calls the gods to assembly
---

Set against the pattern (see [[assembly-type-scene]]), the assembly of the gods at the start of Book 20 has all its parts but the last.

1. **Summons.** In Book 8 the poem says only that Zeus held an assembly of the gods (8.2); here he has Themis call them, and she goes everywhere and bids them come to his house (20.4–6; see [[themis-the-goddess]] and [[zeus]]), as the heralds call the Achaeans to assembly for Agamemnon (2.50–52). Her summons reaches the rivers and the nymphs, and only Oceanus stays away (20.7–9; see [[oceanus]]).
2. **Gathering.** “So they were gathered within the house of Zeus” (ἀγηγέρατʼ, 20.13), and the gods sit down in the colonnades that Hephaestus made (20.11–12; see [[hephaestus]]). Poseidon comes up out of the sea to join them, and sits down “in their midst” (20.14–15; see [[poseidon]]).
3. **Speeches.** There is no rising to speak, and no scepter. Poseidon, seated, asks Zeus his plan, and Zeus answers him and the others (20.15–30).
4. **Dissolution.** Nobody dissolves the assembly. Zeus ends his answer with his reason, and the narrator goes straight on: “So spoke the son of Cronus, and roused relentless war. / And the gods set out to go to war, their hearts divided” (20.31–32; see [[the-battle-of-the-gods]]). Book 8's assembly also ended without dismissal, when Zeus yoked his horses and drove to Ida (8.41).
