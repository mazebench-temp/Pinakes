---
summary: Aeneas says that Zeus saved him from Achilles on Ida, and “roused my might and my nimble knees” (20.93). Hector leaves his fight with Achilles to the gods, “these things lie on the knees of the gods” (20.435). Achilles stops Demuchus with a spear “on the knee” (20.458), and Tros, son of Alastor, comes “to clasp his knees”, and is killed while he tries (20.463, 20.468).
grc: [γόνυ]
en: [knee]
---

Walter Leaf takes λαβών (20.464) as “taking him prisoner”, with γούνων governed by ἀντίος, and, after Hentze, the imperfect ἥπτετο (20.468) as an attempt that failed: Tros never reaches the knees (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 20.464). κὰγ γόνυ, “on the knee”, is the only place in Homer where κατά is cut short before γ (Leaf on 20.458).
