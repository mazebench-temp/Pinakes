summary: The critic's quotation of Poseidon's journey in Book 13 takes in a verse of the earthquake that opens the battle of the gods, “and its peaks, and the city of the Trojans and the ships of the Achaeans”. In Book 20 the god shakes the earth as a combatant, and the verses that follow, Hades' fear, are the ones the critic has just quoted for the terror he now sets aside.

In Book 20 the verse closes a sentence about the earth, not about the god's walk. Zeus thunders from on high, “and below, Poseidon shook / the boundless earth and the steep peaks of the mountains. / And all the feet of Ida of the many springs were shaken, / and its peaks, and the city of the Trojans and the ships of the Achaeans” (20.57–60; see [[poseidon]], [[earth-shaker]] and [[ida]]). The trembling is the work of his hands as a god at war, the counterpart of Zeus' thunder above, and it reaches the world below: Aidoneus leaps from his throne in fear that the earth will split (20.61–65; see [[the-battle-of-the-gods]]). Those are the verses that *On the Sublime* has just quoted as an image of the terror of the battle of the gods (*On the Sublime* 9.6; see [[longinus-wounded-gods]]). In the critic's version the peaks, the city and the ships tremble instead under the immortal feet of Poseidon as he walks, as the mountains and the forest do in Book 13 (13.18–19; *On the Sublime* 9.8).
