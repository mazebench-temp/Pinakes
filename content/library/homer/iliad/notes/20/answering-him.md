summary: Zeus answers Poseidon (20.19), Apollo answers Aeneas (20.103), and Hector, unafraid, answers Achilles (20.430); Aeneas answers Apollo and Achilles with two other forms of the reply formula (20.86, 20.199).
grc: ["ἀπαμειβόμενος προσέειπε", "ἀπαμείβετο φώνησέν τε"]
en: [answering, answered him in turn]
---

Aeneas' two replies are the longer speeches they introduce: to Apollo, disguised as Lycaon, “Then Aeneas, answering, spoke to him in turn” (20.86), and to Achilles' taunt, “Then Aeneas answered him in turn and said” (20.199), which opens his genealogy (20.200–258; [[genealogy-in-battle]]). Hector's reply has the participle of courage in place of the participle of answering: τὸν δʼ οὐ ταρβήσας προσέφη, “Unafraid, Hector of the gleaming helmet answered him” (20.430), a few verses after Apollo had sent him back into the crowd in fear (ταρβήσας, 20.380).
