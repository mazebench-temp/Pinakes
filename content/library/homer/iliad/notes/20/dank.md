---
summary: Hades fears that Poseidon will break the earth open and that his dwellings, “terrible and dank, which even the gods hate,” will be laid bare (20.61–65), the epithet's only use in the Iliad.
---

“And beneath, Aidoneus, lord of those below, was afraid, / and in fear he leapt from his throne and cried out, lest above him / Poseidon the earth-shaker should break the earth open, / and his dwellings be laid bare to mortals and immortals, / terrible and dank, which even the gods hate” (20.61–65). The author of *On the Sublime* quotes the passage for the terror of the battle of the gods (see the translation's note on 20.61–65 and [[longinus-wounded-gods]]). See [[hades]] and [[earth-shaker]].
