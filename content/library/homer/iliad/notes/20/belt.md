---
summary: Achilles' javelin strikes Polydorus in the back “where the golden clasps of the belt / held it together, and the corselet met it in a double layer”, the verses of Pandarus' arrow in Book 4 (20.414–415 = 4.132–133).
---

In Book 4 “through the elaborate belt it was driven, / and it forced its way through the intricately worked corselet”, and the band beneath held the arrow to a graze (4.135–139; see [[pandarus-breaks-the-truce]] and [[band]]). Here nothing holds: “the point of the spear went straight through, beside the navel”, and Polydorus sinks down clutching his bowels (20.416–418; see [[polydorus-son-of-priam]] and [[corselet]]).
