---
summary: The book begins with an assembly of the gods, called by Themis to the house of Zeus, in which Zeus lets them go to war (20.4–30), and the gods then take sides and stand against one another in pairs as the armies do: “So gods went to meet gods” (20.75). But their battle is put off. Both parties sit apart, and neither will begin (20.133–155), while the men's duel goes on to its end.
refs: [20.4-30, 20.67-75, 20.133-155]
---

Poseidon's reason for waiting is a reason of strength: the gods of his side are “far the stronger”, and if Ares or Apollo begins, the other gods will be beaten back to Olympus “under compulsion” (20.133–143). Walter Leaf reports a proposal to cut 20.66–74, so that a fight announced with so much circumstance would not end in nothing, and answers that 20.75 shows the whole opening of the book to hang together (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 20.55; see [[assembly]], [[the-battle-of-the-gods]] and [[war-is-the-concern-of-men]]).
