summary: Aeneas tells the first half of the story in his genealogy: the gods snatched up Ganymede, the most beautiful of mortal men, to pour wine for Zeus (20.231–235). He says nothing of the horses that Zeus gave Tros for him, which Book 5 told.
passages:
  - 20.230-235 | The sons of Tros
---

In Book 5 Diomedes spoke of horses “of that breed which far-seeing Zeus gave to Tros / in recompense for his son Ganymede” (5.265–266), and did not say what had become of Ganymede. Aeneas tells it, as one generation of his line: Tros had three noble sons, and the gods snatched up the third, godlike Ganymede, “for the sake of his beauty, so that he might dwell among the immortals” (20.231–235; see [[tros]] and [[ganymede]]). In his account it is “the gods” who take the boy, to pour wine for Zeus (20.234), and the horses are not mentioned; in Book 5 the payment is Zeus' alone. Aeneas' genealogy has another story of horses from a god, the mares of Erichthonius, Tros' father, which bore twelve foals to the North Wind (20.221–229; see [[the-mares-of-erichthonius]]).
