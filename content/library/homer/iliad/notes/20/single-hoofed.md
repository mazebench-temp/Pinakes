---
summary: “so beneath great-hearted Achilles his single-hoofed horses / trampled corpses and shields alike” (20.498–499), and the blood thrown up from their hooves spatters the chariot.
---

The verses that follow nearly repeat those in which Hector's chariot drove through the Achaeans (20.499–502 ~ 11.534–537). Several editors rejected 495–503, for the repetition and because Achilles, on foot until now, is suddenly in his chariot; Walter Leaf keeps them, since heroes mount and dismount as they need without a word (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 20.495; see the translation's note on 20.495–503).
