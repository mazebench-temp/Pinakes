---
summary: Hera would keep Achilles from harm today; later he will suffer what fate spun for him “when his mother bore him” (20.128). Aeneas sets the two mothers side by side: “your mother is Thetis of the lovely tresses, daughter of the sea”, “and my mother is Aphrodite” (20.207–209).
---

Apollo has already weighed them: Aeneas' mother is the daughter of Zeus, Achilles' the daughter of the Old Man of the Sea (20.105–107), and Aeneas ends the comparison with the grief to come, one pair of parents “will weep for their dear son / today” (20.210–211; see [[mothers-and-sons]], [[thetis]] and [[aphrodite]]).
