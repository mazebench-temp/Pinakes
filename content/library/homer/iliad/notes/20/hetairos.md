---
summary: The companion of the book is Patroclus, who is never named in it. Zeus fears Achilles “now that he is terribly enraged at heart over his companion” (20.29), and Achilles, seeing Hector, calls him “the man who has touched my heart most deeply, / who killed my honored companion” (20.425–426).
---

ἑταίρου (20.29) is the genitive of cause, “because of his friend” (Walter Leaf, *Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 20.29; see [[cholos]]), and τετιμένον, “honored”, is a participle that has become an adjective (Leaf on 20.426; see [[time]] and [[patroclus]]).
