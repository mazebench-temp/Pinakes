---
summary: Achilles' boast over Iphition places his father's domain “by the fish-filled Hyllus and the eddying Hermus” (20.392), the epithet's one use of a river in the Iliad.
---

“here is your death, but your birthplace is by the lake, / the Gygaean, where your father's domain is, / by the fish-filled Hyllus and the eddying Hermus” (20.390–392). The translation's note on 20.392 reports Strabo on the lake and the rivers. See [[hyllus]], [[hermus]], [[gygaean-lake]] and [[iphition]].
