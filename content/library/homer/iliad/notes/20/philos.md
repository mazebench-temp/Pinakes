summary: The love of the gods decides the duel. Hera wants Achilles to know “that the best of the immortals love him” (20.122); Poseidon saves Aeneas for the lineage of Dardanus, “whom the son of Cronus loved above all the children / who were born to him from mortal women” (20.304–305), though Achilles is “at once stronger than you and dearer to the immortals” (20.334); and Achilles concludes, “Truly Aeneas too was dear to the immortal gods” (20.347). Among men, one pair of parents “will weep for their dear son” (20.210), and Polydorus was Priam's youngest and “the dearest to him” (20.410).
grc: [φιλέουσιν, φίλατο, φίλτερος]
en: [dearer]
---

In other phrases φίλος is simply “one's own”: Polydorus “lost his dear life” (20.412), and Achilles' spear goes through Deucalion's arm (φίλης διὰ χειρός, 20.479). See [[the-loves-of-zeus]], [[the-man-whom-zeus-loves]] and [[gods-and-mortals]].
