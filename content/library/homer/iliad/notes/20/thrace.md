---
summary: The Thracian Rhigmus, son of Peirous, “who had come from deep-soiled Thrace”, is one of the last men Achilles kills in the book (20.484–487).
---

{{quote:e2f7bb1b-e15b-5456-add6-a098907669f1}}

ὃς ἐκ Θρῄκης ἐριβώλακος εἰληλούθει, “who had come from deep-soiled Thrace” (20.485; see [[rhigmus]] and [[deep-soiled]]). The verse-end is the formula of a man from a distant land: Phaestus “had come from deep-soiled Tarne”, and the Paeonian Apisaon “from deep-soiled Paeonia” (5.44, 17.350; see [[tarne]] and [[paeonia]]). Thrace had the same epithet when Antenor's son Iphidamas was reared there by his grandfather, “in deep-soiled Thrace, mother of flocks” (11.222; see [[iphidamas]]). Rhigmus' father Peirous, whom the index takes for the Thracian leader of the Catalogue, “had come from Aenus”, on the Thracian coast (4.520; see [[peirous]], [[aenus]] and [[thracians]]). Rhigmus falls from his chariot with Achilles' javelin in his belly, and his attendant Areithous is stabbed as he turns the horses (20.486–489; see [[areithous-the-attendant]] and [[achilles]]).
