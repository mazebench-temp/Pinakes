summary: Zeus sends the gods to help either side, “whichever way each of you has a mind” (20.25), and Poseidon checks Hera, “do not rage beyond good sense” (20.133). Hera bids Poseidon “consider for yourself in your own mind” whether to save Aeneas (20.310). The verb νοέω marks who sees in time: Achilles did not consider that the gods' gifts are not easily mastered (20.264), Poseidon was “quick to see it” (20.291), and Hector saw his brother fall (20.419).
grc: [νόησον]
en: [consider]
---

The digitized text prints παρʼ ἐκ νόον in two words; the phrase is παρὲκ νόον, “beyond good sense”, as at 10.391, where Dolon says that Hector “led me astray from my good sense” (see the translation's note on 20.133). Poseidon's quick sight is Aphrodite's in Book 5, when she saved the same man from Diomedes (εἰ μὴ ἄρʼ ὀξὺ νόησε, 5.312; see [[would-have-had-not]] and [[phrenes]]).
