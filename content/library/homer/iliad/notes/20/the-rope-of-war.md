---
summary: Aeneas says that if a god “were to stretch the outcome of war evenly”, Achilles would not easily win (20.100–102).
refs: [20.100-102]
---

The verb is the rope's: ἶσον τείνειεν πολέμου τέλος, with τέλος, “issue”, where the image of 13.358–360 had πεῖραρ, “rope”, and Walter Leaf sees in it the physical idea of the rope's end passing into the abstract one of the outcome (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 20.101; see the translation's note on 20.101–102 and [[the-bounds-of-victory]]).
