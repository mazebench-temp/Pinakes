summary: Peirous is named as the father of the Thracian Rhigmus, “the noble son of Peirous”, whom Achilles kills (20.484–487). The genitive Πείρεω is found only here, and the poem does not say that Rhigmus' father is the Thracian leader; the index takes him to be.
# Πείρεω (20.484) occurs only here; it belongs here, in the Iliad's scope. The index takes Rhigmus' father for the
# Thracian leader Peirous of 2.844 and Book 4, as Walter Leaf does with Brandreth's Πειρόου: see the body.

{{quote:e2f7bb1b-e15b-5456-add6-a098907669f1}}

μετʼ ἀμύμονα Πείρεω υἱὸν, “after the noble son of Peirous” (20.484; see [[rhigmus]]). Πείρεω is not a Homeric form: Walter Leaf refers it to a nominative Πείρεως, comparing Πείρως, the form the Catalogue's Πείροος takes at 4.520, and thinks Brandreth's correction Πειρόου doubtless right, which makes Rhigmus the son of the Thracian leader (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 20.484; see the translation's note on 20.484). The translation renders the name Peirous, as for the leader.

The index follows him. The name is the same in a different declension, and the son is a Thracian, “who had come from deep-soiled Thrace” (20.485), in the words with which his father was introduced, “who had come from Aenus” (4.520; see [[thrace]] and [[aenus]]). Nothing in the poem tells against it: Peirous fell in the first battle, and the Thracians stood around his body so that the Aetolian Thoas could not strip it (4.527–535; see [[thoas]]). But the Iliad never says that Peirous had a son, and the two men are linked only by the name and the country.

A variant joins father and son in another way. For νηδύϊ, “in his belly”, where Achilles' javelin struck Rhigmus (20.486), many manuscripts read πνεύμονι, “in his lung”, the word of the verse in which Thoas' spear struck Peirous (4.528); Leaf thinks it came in from that passage (on 20.486; see the translation's note on 20.486).
