---
summary: Hector, facing Achilles for the first time, owns that he is the weaker man: “And I know that you are brave, and I am far worse than you. / But these things lie on the knees of the gods, / whether, worse though I am, I shall take your life / hitting you with my spear, since my weapon too is sharp at the point” (20.434–437).
---

Walter Leaf answers the scholiasts, who took 20.434 as irony: Hector's frankness in owning the contest unequal gives his character its dignity (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 20.434). He takes ταῦτα as “these words of thine” (on 20.435), and Achilles' words were a threat: “Come nearer, so that you may the sooner reach the bounds of destruction” (20.429). The gods answer at once: Athena turns Hector's spear back with a breath, and Apollo carries him away in mist (20.438–444; see [[divine-intervention]] and [[gounata]]).
