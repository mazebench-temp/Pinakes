---
summary: The narrator calls Achilles “insatiable of battle” as the Achaeans arm around him (20.2); a variant gives the epithet to the Achaeans.
grc: [ἀκόρητον]
---

“around you, son of Peleus, insatiable of battle, the Achaeans” (20.2; [[apostrophe]]). μάχης ἀκόρητον agrees with σέ, Achilles. The variant ἀκόρητοι, agreeing with Ἀχαιοί, has the commoner rhythm but less support; Walter Leaf prefers it, since it is Achilles' men, not he, who might be thought to have had a surfeit of battle (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 20.2; see the translation's note on 20.2). Either way the phrase is the one Menelaus used of the Trojans, “the Trojans are insatiable of battle” (13.639; [[satiety-of-all-things]]). See [[achilles]].
