---
summary: In the four verses that give the layers of Achilles' shield as metals, two are of tin, set inside (20.271).
---

“two of bronze, and two of tin inside, / and one of gold, and in that the ash spear was stopped” (20.271–272; see [[the-shield-of-achilles]], [[bronze]] and [[gold-and-silver]]). Tin was one of the four metals that Hephaestus cast into the fire when he made the arms, and the greaves were “of soft tin” (18.474, 18.613). Aristarchus athetized 20.269–272, and Walter Leaf thinks them the work of someone who took the five layers of 18.481 for the different metals, whereas they were of hide (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 20.269; see the translation's note on 20.269–272). Aristotle quotes the last half-verse (see [[the-bronze-spear-stopped]]).
