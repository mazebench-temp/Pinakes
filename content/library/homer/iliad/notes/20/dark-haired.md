summary: “the dark-haired god” leads the Achaean party of the gods to the wall of Heracles (20.144); in Aeneas' genealogy the North Wind lies with Erichthonius' mares “in the likeness of a dark-maned stallion” (20.224).
en: [dark-maned]
---

At 20.144 the epithet stands alone for Poseidon's name, as it does once in the Odyssey (*Odyssey* 9.536); elsewhere in the Iliad it goes with Ποσειδάων or with γαιήοχε (13.563, 14.390, 15.174, 15.201). At 20.224 it is the only place in Homer where κυανοχαίτης is said of anything but Poseidon, and the translation renders it “dark-maned” (see its note on 20.224). Walter Leaf sees no special point in it for a horse (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 20.224). See [[poseidon]], [[boreas]] and [[the-mares-of-erichthonius]].
