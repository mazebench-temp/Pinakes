---
summary: Apollo, in Lycaon's likeness, taunts Aeneas, “counselor of the Trojans,” with the boasts he made over his wine (20.83–85).
---

“Aeneas, counselor of the Trojans, where have your threats gone, / which you promised the kings of the Trojans as you drank your wine, / that you would fight face to face with Achilles, son of Peleus?” (20.83–85; [[where-have-our-boasts-gone]]). Deiphobus called Aeneas by the same title when he asked him to defend Alcathous' body (13.463). See [[aeneas]].
