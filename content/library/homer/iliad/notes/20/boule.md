---
summary: Poseidon asks Zeus “his plan” in the assembly of the gods (20.15), and Zeus answers, “You know, shaker of the earth, the plan in my breast, / for which I have gathered you” (20.20–21): the gods may now help either side. The two parties of gods then sit apart, “devising / their plans”, but neither will begin the war (20.153–155).
---

The plan reverses an order. In Book 8 Zeus forbade the gods to help either Trojans or Danaans (8.7–12); now that Achilles is fighting, he fears that the Trojans will not hold him even for a little while, and lets the gods go (20.23–30; see [[plan-of-zeus]], [[assembly]] and [[the-battle-of-the-gods]]). Walter Leaf takes ὧν, “for which”, as neuter, explaining βουλήν, and observes that ὀλλύμενοι, “even as they perish”, might be said by a god of either side but shows the Trojans uppermost in Zeus' thoughts (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 20.21).
