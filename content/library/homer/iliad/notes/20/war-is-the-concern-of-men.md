---
summary: Poseidon checks Hera, who would have the gods turn Aeneas back or stand by Achilles, and proposes that their party sit apart: “But let us go now and sit down / off the path, at a lookout, and war will be the concern of men” (20.136–137).
---

Walter Leaf notes that ἄνδρεσσι is opposed here to the gods, and not, as in Hector's words to Andromache, to women (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 20.137). The proposal holds only while the other side holds back: if Ares or Apollo begins the battle, the gods of Poseidon's party will fight and drive them back to Olympus (20.138–143). Both parties sit apart and neither begins (20.144–155; see [[watching-the-war]] and [[the-battle-of-the-gods]]); but Poseidon himself goes into the battle before the book is over, to save Aeneas (20.318–340).
