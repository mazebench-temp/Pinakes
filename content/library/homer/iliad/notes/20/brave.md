**Hector.** “And I know that you are brave, and I am far worse than you. / But these things lie on the knees of the gods” (20.434–435; [[on-the-knees-of-the-gods]]). Walter Leaf notes that the scholiasts took the line as irony and lost its point: Hector faces, with heroic frankness, a contest he admits is unequal (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 20.434). See [[hector]].

**Aeneas.** Hera, refusing to help him, leaves it to Poseidon whether to let him, “brave as he is, be brought down by Achilles, son of Peleus” (20.311–312). Apollo “put noble might into him” (μένος ἠΰ, 20.80), the phrase used of the might that Zeus breathes into Achilles' horses (17.456).

**The victims.** Iphition is “the brave son of Otrynteus” (20.383), with ἐσθλός before a patronymic as for Atymnius' son and Hypeirochus' son (5.581, 11.673); Demoleon is “a brave defender against battle” (20.396); Demuchus is “tall and brave” (20.457). See [[iphition]], [[demoleon]] and [[demuchus]].
