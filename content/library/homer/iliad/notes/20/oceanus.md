---
summary: When Themis calls the gods to the assembly in the house of Zeus, every river comes except Oceanus.
---

“And not one of the rivers was absent, except Oceanus, / nor any of the nymphs who dwell in the lovely groves / and the springs of rivers and the grassy meadows” (20.7–9; see [[rivers]], [[nymphs]], [[themis-the-goddess]] and [[assembly]]). Oceanus is himself a river, the one from which, as Achilles will say, all rivers and every sea and all springs flow (21.195–197), and he alone stays away. Walter Leaf reports that the scholia give various reasons for his absence, but that Heyne rightly found it less strange than the presence of rivers and nymphs at a council of the gods, and thought 7–9 might have been added to account for the river Scamander among the gods in the battle (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 20.7; see [[scamander]] and the translation's note on 20.7–9). He reports another suggestion, that as Hestia alone stays at home when the gods go out in procession in Plato's *Phaedrus*, so Oceanus is absent because he is the bond that holds the world together. In the *Phaedrus* Hestia “stays alone in the house of the gods” while the others lead their companies through the heavens (247a; see [[plato]]).
