---
summary: When Hector sees his brother Polydorus fall, he can no longer keep back as Apollo told him to, and he comes against Achilles “brandishing his sharp spear, like a flame” (20.419–423): the last time the half-verse is said in the poem.
---

Ἕκτωρ δʼ ὡς ἐνόησε κασίγνητον Πολύδωρον / ἔντερα χερσὶν ἔχοντα λιαζόμενον ποτὶ γαίη, / κάρ ῥά οἱ ὀφθαλμῶν κέχυτʼ ἀχλύς· οὐδʼ ἄρʼ ἔτʼ ἔτλη / δηρὸν ἑκὰς στρωφᾶσθʼ, ἀλλʼ ἀντίος ἦλθʼ Ἀχιλῆϊ / ὀξὺ δόρυ κραδάων φλογὶ εἴκελος: “But when Hector saw his brother Polydorus / holding his bowels in his hands, sinking to the ground, / a mist was poured over his eyes, and he could no longer bear / to range far off for long, but he came against Achilles, / brandishing his sharp spear, like a flame” (20.419–423). The half-verse φλογὶ εἴκελος has followed Hector since Book 13 (13.53, 13.688; 17.88; 18.154), and this is its last use in Homer. The flame comes out of grief. The mist poured over his eyes is the mist of fainting and death, brought here by sorrow (see [[achlys]]), and what brings it is the sight of his youngest brother, whom Priam would not let fight (20.407–418; see [[polydorus-son-of-priam]] and [[hector]]).

The flame breaks a command. Apollo had told Hector not to step out as a champion against Achilles but to wait for him in the crowd, and Hector had gone back among the men, afraid at the god's voice (20.375–380; see [[apollo]]). Before that he had told the Trojans that he would go against Achilles “even if his hands are like fire” (20.371–372; see [[hands-like-fire]]). Now the man like a flame meets the man with hands like fire. Achilles leaps up at the sight and calls him the man who has touched his heart most deeply, who killed his honored companion (20.424–427; see [[achilles]]). Athena turns Hector's spear back with a breath, and Apollo snatches him away in thick mist (20.438–444; see [[divine-rescue]]).

At the end of the book the fire passes to Achilles: he rushes everywhere with his spear, like a god, as a wondrous fire rages through the deep valleys of a dry mountain (20.490–494; see [[forest-fire]]).
