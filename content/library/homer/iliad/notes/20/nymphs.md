---
summary: The nymphs of the groves, the springs and the meadows come with the rivers to the assembly of the gods in the house of Zeus. Later in the book Achilles kills Iphition, whom a Naiad nymph bore to Otrynteus beneath snowy Tmolus.
---

**At the assembly.** When Themis calls the gods to the house of Zeus, not one of the rivers is absent except Oceanus, “nor any of the nymphs who dwell in the lovely groves / and the springs of rivers and the grassy meadows” (20.8–9; see [[themis-the-goddess]], [[rivers]] and [[oceanus]]). It is the only place in the Iliad where the nymphs are among the gods assembled. Walter Leaf compares the *Odyssey*, where Odysseus, woken by the girls' cries, wonders whether they are the nymphs “who hold the steep peaks of the mountains / and the springs of rivers and the grassy meadows” (*Odyssey* 6.123–124), and the *Homeric Hymn to Aphrodite*, where Anchises asks whether the goddess before him is one of the nymphs “who dwell in the lovely groves”, or of those who live on the mountain “and the springs of rivers and the grassy meadows” (*Hymn to Aphrodite* 97–99; *Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 20.8). πίσεα, “meadows”, are water meadows, and Leaf compares the name of Pisa in Elis. He reports Heyne's view that the rivers and nymphs at a council of the gods are stranger than Oceanus' absence, and that 7–9 may have been added (on 20.7; see the translation's note on 20.7–9).

**A Naiad's son.** The first man Achilles kills when he leaps among the Trojans is Iphition, “whom a Naiad nymph bore to Otrynteus, sacker of cities, / beneath snowy Tmolus, in the rich land of Hyde” (20.384–385; see [[iphition]] and [[tmolus]]). The words recall the birth of Satnius, whom “a noble Naiad nymph” bore to Enops by the Satnioeis (ὃν ἄρα νύμφη τέκε νηῒς ἀμύμων, 14.444; see [[satnius]]), and Achilles' boast over him names the lake and the rivers of his home (20.389–392; see [[gygaean-lake]]).
