---
summary: Tithonus is the first named of the sons of Laomedon in Aeneas' genealogy, a brother of Priam (20.237).
---

Λαομέδων δʼ ἄρα Τιθωνὸν τέκετο Πρίαμόν τε, “and Laomedon fathered Tithonus and Priam” (20.237; see [[laomedon]] and [[priam]]). This is the only place where the Iliad names his father; in Book 11 he is only the husband from whose bed Dawn rises (11.1; see [[eos]]). The genealogy gives him no children and goes on only with Priam's line, “and Priam brilliant Hector” (20.240), and with the line of Assaracus, which leads to Anchises and Aeneas (20.239–240). Tithonus and Anchises are thus second cousins, both great-grandsons of Tros (20.231–239; see [[anchises]] and [[tros]]); in the *Homeric Hymn to Aphrodite* the goddess tells Anchises that Tithonus, whom Dawn carried off, was “of your race” (219; see the entry).
