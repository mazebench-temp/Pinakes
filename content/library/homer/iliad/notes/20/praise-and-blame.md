---
summary: Before they fight, Achilles taunts Aeneas with his hopes and his flight (20.178–198), and Aeneas answers that he too knows “how to speak both taunts and evil words” (20.202), and that “there are insults enough for both of us to speak” (20.246). Hector answers Achilles in Aeneas' verses (20.431–433).
refs: [20.178-198, 20.200-202, 20.244-258, 20.431-433]
---

Aeneas ends the exchange with three sayings about speech: the tongue of mortals turns every way, the pasture of words is wide, and as you speak, so you will be spoken to (20.248–250; see [[the-tongue-of-mortals]] and [[such-will-you-hear-back]]). Then he calls for the spears (20.256–258; see [[kertomia]], [[oneidos]] and [[words-and-violence]]).
