---
summary: When the gods join the war, Athena shouts for the Achaeans now from the ditch outside the wall and now from the loud-resounding shores (20.48–50); and the wall of Heracles was built so that he might escape the sea monster when it chased him from the shore toward the plain (20.145–148).
grc: [ἀκτάων, ἠϊόνος]
en: [shores]
---

**Athena's shout.** αὖε δʼ Ἀθήνη / στᾶσʼ ὁτὲ μὲν παρὰ τάφρον ὀρυκτὴν τείχεος ἐκτός, / ἄλλοτʼ ἐπʼ ἀκτάων ἐριδούπων μακρὸν ἀΰτει: “and Athena shouted, / standing now beside the ditch they had dug, outside the wall, / and now on the loud-resounding shores she shouted aloud” (20.48–50). ἀκταί are the shores and headlands where the land meets the sea; Walter Leaf notes that the word means the sea-shores, as always (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 20.50), and the translation keeps “loud-resounding” for ἐρίδουπος (see [[loud-resounding]] and the translation's table). The goddess' cry runs from the Achaean ditch to the sea behind the ships, as Ares' does on the other side from the height of the city to the Simoeis (20.51–53; see [[like-a-black-storm]], [[athena]] and [[the-achaean-wall]]).

**The sea monster.** τεῖχος ἐς ἀμφίχυτον Ἡρακλῆος θείοιο / ὑψηλόν, τό ῥά οἱ Τρῶες καὶ Παλλὰς Ἀθήνη / ποίεον, ὄφρα τὸ κῆτος ὑπεκπροφυγὼν ἀλέαιτο, / ὁππότε μιν σεύαιτο ἀπʼ ἠϊόνος πεδίον δέ: “to the heaped-up wall of divine Heracles, / the high wall, which the Trojans and Pallas Athena / had made for him, so that he might slip away and escape the sea monster / whenever it chased him from the shore toward the plain” (20.145–148; see [[the-wall-of-heracles]], [[sea-beasts]] and [[heracles]]). The monster comes up from the sea onto the beach, ἠϊών, and chases the hero inland. The same word names the beach of the Achaean camp, “the long mouth of the whole shore” where the ships were hauled up in rows (14.35–36), and the deep shore along which the army marched to the assembly in Book 2 (2.92). Poseidon and the gods of his party sit down on this wall, wrapped in an unbreakable cloud (20.149–150).
