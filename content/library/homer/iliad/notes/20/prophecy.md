---
summary: Poseidon foretells Aeneas' future: “it is fated for him to escape”, and “now mighty Aeneas shall be lord over the Trojans, / and his children's children, who will be born hereafter” (20.302–308). He tells Aeneas to keep away from Achilles until “Achilles has met his death and his fate”, “for no other of the Achaeans will slay you” (20.337–339).
refs: [20.302-308, 20.336-339]
---

The prophecy is spoken among the gods, and Aeneas hears only the second part, the warning (see [[poseidon]] and [[aeneas]]). Aphrodite promises Anchises the same in the Hymn to Aphrodite, a son who will rule among the Trojans, and children's children after him (σοὶ δʼ ἔσται φίλος υἱός, ὃς ἐν Τρώεσσιν ἀνάξει / καὶ παῖδες παίδεσσι διαμπερὲς ἐκγεγάοντες, *Homeric Hymn to Aphrodite* 196–197; see the translation's note on 20.306–308). Strabo used the prophecy against the stories of Aeneas' wanderings, and Virgil turned it into Apollo's answer at Delos (see [[the-rule-of-aeneas]], [[strabo-on-the-rule-of-aeneas]] and [[virgil-the-house-of-aeneas]]). See [[moira]] and [[the-short-life-of-achilles]].
