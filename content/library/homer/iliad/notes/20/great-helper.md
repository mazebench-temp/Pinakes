---
summary: Hermes, “the great helper,” goes with the gods of the Achaean side (20.34–35) and stands against Leto (20.72), the first two uses of the title in the Iliad.
---

“and the great helper / Hermes, who excels in his shrewd mind” (20.34–35; [[shrewd-mind]]); “against Leto stood strong Hermes, the great helper” (20.72; [[sokos]], [[leto]]). In the battle of the gods in Book 21 Hermes declines to fight her: it is hard to come to blows with the wives of Zeus, and she may boast among the gods that she has beaten him (21.497–501). See [[hermes]] and [[the-battle-of-the-gods]].
