---
summary: Achilles is swift-footed when Zeus fears that the Trojans will not hold him for a moment (20.26–27), when they tremble at the sight of him (20.44–46), and when Aeneas remembers how he once stood against him on Ida (20.89–93).
---

The epithet comes in the first two passages in the same verse-end, ποδώκεα Πηλεΐωνα (20.27, 20.45; [[son-of-peleus]]). In the third Aeneas recalls fleeing from Achilles' cattle raid, saved by Zeus, who “roused my might and my nimble knees” (20.89–93; [[nimble]], [[the-sack-of-lyrnessus]]). Achilles' other epithet of speed, [[podarkes]], comes three times later in the book. See [[achilles]].
