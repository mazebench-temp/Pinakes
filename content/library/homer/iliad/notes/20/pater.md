---
summary: Zeus is “father Zeus” and “the father of men and gods”, who thunders from on high (20.11, 20.56, 20.192). Achilles taunts the dying Iphition with “your father's domain” by the Gygaean lake (20.391), and Polydorus fights against his father's will: “His father would not let him fight at all” (20.408).
---

The book is full of fathers and sons, from Aeneas' genealogy, a chain of fathers from Zeus to Anchises and Priam (20.215–240), to Priam's youngest son killed by Achilles (20.407–418; see [[polydorus-son-of-priam]], [[fathers-and-sons]] and [[bereaved-parents]]).
