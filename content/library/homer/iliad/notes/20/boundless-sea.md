---
summary: Poseidon shakes “the boundless earth” (20.57–58), with ἀπειρέσιος, a longer form of the same idea, found only here in the Iliad.
grc: ["γαῖαν ἀπειρεσίην"]
---

“and below, Poseidon shook / the boundless earth and the steep peaks of the mountains” (20.57–58; [[earth-shaker]], [[steep]]). The translation renders ἀπειρέσιος as it renders ἀπείρων, “boundless” (see its table of epithets). The Odyssey uses the longer word of number, “countless”: the countless goats of the island that lies off the Cyclopes' land (*Odyssey* 9.116–118), and the countless people of Crete (*Odyssey* 19.172–174). See [[earth]].
