---
summary: Poseidon is the earth-shaker as he comes from the sea to Zeus' assembly (20.13), as he shakes the earth until Hades fears it will break open (20.57–63), as he answers Hera (20.132), as he sees Aeneas' danger, hears Hera's refusal and comes to him (20.291, 20.318, 20.330), and in the simile of the bull, which pleases him (20.405).
---

**The earthquake.** The epithet is taken literally. While Zeus thunders above, “below, Poseidon shook / the boundless earth and the steep peaks of the mountains,” and Aidoneus leaps from his throne, “lest above him / Poseidon the earth-shaker should break the earth open” (20.57–63; [[hades]], [[dank]], [[boundless-sea]]).

**Aeneas.** Four times the formula Ποσειδάων ἐνοσίχθων closes the verse in the story of Aeneas: Poseidon answers Hera's call to turn him back (20.132), is quick to see that he will die (20.291), hears Hera leave the choice to him (20.318), and comes close to him after the rescue (20.330). See [[poseidon]], [[divine-rescue]] and [[would-have-had-not]].

**The bull.** In the simile of Hippodamas' death the bull dragged around the Heliconian lord bellows, “and the earth-shaker is glad of them,” of the young men who drag it (20.403–405; [[heliconian-poseidon]]).
