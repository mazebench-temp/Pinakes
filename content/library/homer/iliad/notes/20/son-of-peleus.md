---
summary: The patronymic runs through the book. The narrator addresses Achilles as “son of Peleus” in its first lines (20.2); Zeus fears that the Trojans will not hold “the swift-footed son of Peleus” (20.27); Aeneas is sent five times “to face the son of Peleus” (ἀντία Πηλεΐωνος); and the last verse begins with the name (20.503).
---

**Facing the son of Peleus.** The phrase ἀντία Πηλεΐωνος marks Aeneas' road to the duel. Apollo rouses him “to face the son of Peleus” (20.80), Aeneas is unwilling “to fight face to face with the high-hearted son of Peleus” (20.88), Hera sees him going “to face the son of Peleus” (20.113, 20.118), and after the rescue Poseidon asks him which god bade him “fight face to face with the high-hearted son of Peleus” (20.333 = 20.88; see [[high-hearted]] and [[aeneas]]).

**In the speeches.** Aeneas and Hector answer Achilles with the same three verses, “Son of Peleus, do not think that with words, as if I were a child, / you will frighten me” (20.200–202 = 20.431–433; see [[kertomia]]). Hector bids the Trojans not to fear the son of Peleus (20.366), and Poseidon and Hera speak of Aeneas brought down by him (20.294, 20.312).

**In the narrative.** The son of Peleus rushes at Aeneas like a lion (20.164), holds his shield away in fear (20.261), and would have killed Aeneas with his sword (20.290); Poseidon pours mist over his eyes (20.322). The book ends on him: “And he was eager to win glory, / the son of Peleus, and his irresistible hands were spattered with gore” (20.502–503; [[irresistible-hands]]). See [[achilles]] and [[swift-footed]].
