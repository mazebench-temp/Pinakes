summary: Poseidon foresees that if Ares or Apollo begins the battle, “there will rise for us too, right there, the quarrel / of the din of battle” (20.140–141). Aeneas asks Achilles “what need is there for strife and quarrels between us two”, “to quarrel with each other face to face, like women” who “go out into the middle of the street and quarrel with each other” (20.251–254).
grc: [νεικεῖν, νεικεῦσʼ]
---

ἔριδας καὶ νείκεα, “strife and quarrels” (20.251), keeps the two words apart (see [[eris]]). Walter Leaf thinks the contracted νεικεῦσι a late form (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 20.251; see the translation's note on 20.251–255). The quarrel Aeneas wants to end is the one of words, and he turns to the bronze (20.256–258; see [[women-quarreling-in-the-street]], [[words-and-violence]] and [[women-and-war]]).
