---
summary: The gods sit in the colonnades that Hephaestus made in the house of Zeus. He goes to the ships with the Achaeans' gods, exulting in his strength and limping, and in the pairs of gods he is set against the river Xanthus, fire against water. The gold of the shield he made for Achilles stops Aeneas' spear: “the gifts of the god”.
# Unnamed: "the gifts of the god" (20.268) and "the crook-footed god" who beat out the layers (20.270).
refs: [20.268, 20.270]
---

**The colonnades.** The gods summoned by Themis “sat down in the polished colonnades, which for father Zeus / Hephaestus had made with his cunning skill” (20.11–12; see [[zeus]] and [[with-cunning-skill]]). He built a house for each of the gods on Olympus (1.606–608), and ἰδυίῃσι πραπίδεσσι, “with his cunning skill”, is said in Homer only of his work. Walter Leaf takes αἴθουσαι as colonnades, not benches, as Zenodotus and others seem to have held (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 20.11).

**To the ships.** “and Hephaestus went with them, exulting in his strength, / limping; and his slender shins moved nimbly beneath him” (20.36–37; see [[exulting-in-strength]] and [[the-battle-of-the-gods]]). The second verse is the one with which he rose from his anvil to greet Thetis (18.411). He is one of the gods whom Poseidon named in Book 15 as his allies against Zeus (15.213–214; see [[poseidon]]).

**Fire against water.** “and against Hephaestus the great deep-eddying river, / whom the gods call Xanthus, and men Scamander” (20.73–74; see [[scamander]]). Leaf sees no principle in the pairing of the gods except here, in the antagonism of fire and water (on 20.67). Theirs is the first of the gods' fights in Book 21. When the river rises against Achilles, Hera sets her son on it: he burns the dead on the plain, dries it, and sets the river's banks and waters on fire, until the river yields and swears never to help the Trojans again, and Hera calls him off, since it is not fitting to strike an immortal god so hard for the sake of mortals (21.328–382).

**The gifts of the god.** Aeneas' spear did not “break the shield; for the gold held it back, the gifts of the god; / but it drove through two layers, and there were still three, / since the crook-footed god had beaten out five layers, / two of bronze, and two of tin inside, / and one of gold, and in that the ash spear was stopped” (20.268–272; see [[the-shield-of-achilles]] and [[crook-footed]]). Aristarchus athetized 269–272, since the layers contradict the making of the shield in Book 18 (18.481) and the gold in the middle could not stop a spear that had pierced two layers (see the translation's note on 20.269–272). Achilles had held the shield away from him in fear, not considering “that the glorious gifts of the gods are not easy / for mortal men to master, nor do they give way” (20.265–266; see [[doron]]).
