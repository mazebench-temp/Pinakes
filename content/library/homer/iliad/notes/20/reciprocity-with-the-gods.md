summary: Poseidon pleads for Aeneas on the ground of the exchange: why should he suffer “when he always gives pleasing / gifts to the gods who hold the wide sky?” (20.298–299). Apollo bids Aeneas pray to the gods, since his mother is the greater goddess (20.104–107).
refs: [20.104-107, 20.297-299]
---

Zeus said of Troy that his altar there “never lacked an equal share of the feast” (4.48), and he will say of Hector that he never failed in pleasing gifts (24.66–70). The god who pleads here is not Aeneas' own patron: Poseidon is on the Achaean side (see [[charis]], [[doron]] and [[sacrifice]]).
