# Λυκάονι (20.81; 21.35, at the river) is both times this Lycaon; Pandarus' father (lycaon) has only Λυκάονος.
grc: [Λυκάονι]
# Unnamed: "in his likeness" (20.82); Aeneas answers the god as "Son of Priam" (20.87).

**The borrowed voice.** εἴσατο φωνήν, “he made his voice like”, and then ἐεισάμενος, “in his likeness” (20.81–82). Apollo has spoken to Hector and Aeneas in other men's likeness before: to Hector as his uncle Asius (16.715–720), as Mentes, the leader of the Cicones (17.71–74), and as Phaenops of Abydos, the dearest of his guest-friends (17.582–585), and to Aeneas as Periphas, his father's herald (17.323–326; see [[apollo]], [[asius-son-of-dymas]], [[mentes]], [[phaenops-son-of-asius]] and [[periphas-son-of-epytus]]). The verse that introduces the god's words is the same for Asius, for Periphas and for Lycaon (16.720 = 17.326 = 20.82). This time he takes a son of Priam, and Aeneas answers him as one: “Son of Priam, why do you bid me do this, though I am unwilling” (20.87; see [[aeneas]]). The poem does not say why the god chose Lycaon.

**A man who has met Achilles.** Lycaon knows Achilles. Achilles caught him by night in Priam's orchard and sold him in Lemnos, and a guest-friend ransomed him; he came home from Lemnos, had eleven days of joy among his friends, and on the twelfth a god put him into Achilles' hands again (21.34–48). That is this same day, the fourth day of battle, which runs on into Book 21 (see [[the-fourth-day-of-battle]]). His mother was Laothoe, a daughter of Altes, king of the Leleges at Pedasus (21.85–88), and the answer that Aeneas gives to Lycaon's voice is the story of the day when Achilles sacked Lyrnessus and Pedasus and Athena bade him “slay the Leleges and the Trojans” (20.89–96; see [[the-sack-of-lyrnessus]] and [[leleges]]). The poem makes nothing of it. Lycaon's full brother Polydorus, the youngest son of Priam, dies at Achilles' hands later in this book (20.407–418; 21.89–91; 22.46–48).
