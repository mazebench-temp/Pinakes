---
summary: Hippodamas, stabbed in the back, “gasped out his life and bellowed, as when a bull / bellows as he is dragged around the Heliconian lord / by young men dragging him; and the earth-shaker is glad of them” (20.403–405).
refs: [20.403-405]
---

The simile shows a sacrifice to Poseidon. Walter Leaf takes ἀμφί literally, of the bull dragged round the god's altar, and reports a scholion that a bull's bellowing at a sacrifice was taken as a sign that the god accepted it, and its silence as a sign of his anger (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 20.404). Strabo reports the same belief of the Ionians, who sacrificed to Heliconian Poseidon at the Panionia in the territory of Priene, with a young man of Priene to oversee the rites, and held the sacrifice favorable when the bull bellowed as it was killed; some took Homer's lines as a reference to that festival (*Geography* 8.7.2; see [[helice-or-the-panionia]], [[heliconian-poseidon]] and [[the-bull-of-the-heliconian-lord]]).
