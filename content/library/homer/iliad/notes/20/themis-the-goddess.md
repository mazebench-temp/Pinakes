# Unnamed: she goes everywhere and bids the gods come (20.5-6), and "the goddess" whom Poseidon heeds (20.14).

{{quote:29d85285-ea2f-5e34-9a53-71e166f1cb31}}

Themis summons the gods for Zeus as the heralds summon the Achaeans to assembly for Agamemnon (2.50–52; see [[assembly]] and [[assembly-type-scene]]). She goes “everywhere, / going to and fro” (πάντῃ φοιτήσασα), and her summons reaches the rivers and the nymphs of the groves, springs and meadows, so that “not one of the rivers was absent, except Oceanus” (see [[rivers]], [[nymphs]] and [[oceanus]]); Poseidon too, “nor did the earth-shaker / fail to heed the goddess, but came up out of the sea to join them” (20.13–14; see [[poseidon]]). θεᾶς, “the goddess” whom he heeds, is Themis. The gods sit in the house of Zeus, and Zeus lets them go down to the war (20.10–30; see [[zeus]] and [[the-battle-of-the-gods]]).

Walter Leaf compares the *Odyssey*, where she is the goddess “who dissolves the assemblies of men and seats them”, and finds the appropriateness of the office obvious (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 20.4; *Odyssey* 2.68–69). In Book 15 she was the first to run to meet Hera when the gods were gathered in the house of Zeus, and Hera bade her begin the gods' equal share of the feast (15.84–95; see the note on Book 15). She is named in the Iliad only in those two passages. Θέμιστα (20.4) is the goddess, and the common word θέμις, “what is right”, has the same letters (θέμιστα, 5.761; see [[themis]] and the translation's note on 20.4).

**In Proclus.** Defending Homer against Plato, Proclus reads this assembly as the gods' turning back to their maker before Zeus sends them to care for the world (see [[proclus-on-the-battle-of-the-gods]]).
