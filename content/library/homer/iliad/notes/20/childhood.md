---
summary: Aeneas and Hector refuse to be frightened by Achilles' words “as if I were a child” (20.200, 20.431), and Aeneas would no longer talk “like children” (20.244). Polydorus, Priam's youngest son, dies through a child's folly, “and then in his childishness, showing off the prowess of his feet” (20.411).
refs: [20.200, 20.211, 20.244, 20.407-412, 20.431]
---

νηπύτιος is the infant, and the heroes use it of what a man in battle must not be (see [[nepios]] and [[children-and-widows]]). Polydorus is the youngest born and the dearest, and his father would not let him fight (20.408–410; see [[polydorus-son-of-priam]] and [[bereaved-parents]]).
