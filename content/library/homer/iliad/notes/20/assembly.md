---
summary: Zeus has Themis call the gods to assembly in his house, where he lets them go down to the war; Poseidon asks why he has called them to assembly again.
passages:
  - 20.4-30 | Zeus calls the gods to assembly
---

“And Zeus bade Themis call the gods to assembly / from the summit of many-folded Olympus” (θεοὺς ἀγορὴν δὲ καλέσσαι, 20.4–5; see [[themis-the-goddess]] and [[zeus]]). The gods gather in the house of Zeus and sit down in its colonnades, and Poseidon asks: “Why, lord of the gleaming thunderbolt, have you called the gods to assembly again?” (20.16; see [[poseidon]]). ἀγορὴν δέ is ἀγορήνδε, “to the assembly”, the suffix of direction written apart, as Οὔλυμπον δέ is “to Olympus” (see the translation's note on 15.133). Walter Leaf compares the *Odyssey*, where Themis is the goddess “who dissolves and seats the assemblies of men” (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 20.4; *Odyssey* 2.68–69).

The assembly reverses the one Zeus held on the morning of the second day of battle, when he forbade the gods to help either side (8.2–27; see the note on Book 8); now he tells them to “help either side, whichever way each of you has a mind” (20.25). In Book 8 the gods fell silent at his word, and only Athena answered him; here Poseidon asks and Zeus answers (20.15–30; see [[assembly-type-scene]] and [[the-battle-of-the-gods]]). The gathering has no dismissal: “So spoke the son of Cronus, and roused relentless war. / And the gods set out to go to war” (20.31–32). Later Poseidon calls the gods' company on Olympus θεῶν … ὁμήγυριν ἄλλων, “the gathering of the other gods” (20.142).
