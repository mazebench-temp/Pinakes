summary: No prayer is spoken in the book; two are bidden. Apollo tells Aeneas, “Come, hero, you too pray to the everlasting gods” (20.104), since his mother is the greater goddess, and Achilles tells Hector that Apollo, who has saved him again, is the god “to whom you must pray when you go into the thud of javelins” (20.451).
# The other forms of εὔχομαι in the book are claims and boasts (euchomai keeps them): Achilles "boasts" that he is all
# of bronze (20.102); Aeneas "claims" his birth and lineage (20.209, 20.241); Achilles thought he "boasted idly" (20.348);
# Achilles "boasting" over Iphition (20.393) and speaking "in triumph" (20.424).
except: [20.102, 20.209, 20.241, 20.348, 20.393, 20.424]
---

Apollo's bidding comes with an argument from birth: Aeneas is the son of Zeus' daughter Aphrodite, Achilles of a lesser goddess, the daughter of the Old Man of the Sea (20.105–107; see [[mothers-and-sons]]). Achilles' words are a taunt in Diomedes' verses of Book 11 (20.449–454 = 11.362–367): the god who saves Hector is the one he prays to (see [[euchomai]] and [[reciprocity-with-the-gods]]).
