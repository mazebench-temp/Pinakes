---
summary: Virgil's Apollo gives Aeneas at Delos Poseidon's prophecy of the rule of his house, and his Neptune recalls snatching Aeneas from Achilles in a hollow cloud. Walter Leaf compares the cave of Cacus laid open, like realms “hateful to the gods”, with Hades' fear for his dwellings, and finds Virgil's reading of the death of Tros in the death of Lausus, whose blood fills his bosom.
---

- Apollo's oracle at Delos, “here the house of Aeneas will rule over all shores, and his children's children”, and Neptune's reminder that he saved Aeneas from the son of Peleus (*Aeneid* 3.94–98, 5.799–811, on 20.302–340): [[virgil-the-house-of-aeneas]].
- **The realms laid bare.** When Hercules tears open the cave of Cacus, its depths are revealed “as if the earth, gaping deep under some force, were to unbar the dwellings below and lay open the pale realms hateful to the gods (*regna … pallida, dis invisa*), and the vast abyss were seen from above, and the shades trembled at the light let in” (*Aeneid* 8.243–246). Walter Leaf compares Aidoneus' fear that his dwellings will be laid bare to mortals and immortals, “terrible and dank, which even the gods hate” (20.64–65), and Virgil's “places rough with mould” (*loca senta situ*, *Aeneid* 6.462) for εὐρώεντα, “dank” (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 20.64): [[hades]] and [[dank]].
- **The bosom filled with blood.** Achilles stabs Tros, son of Alastor, in the liver as he clasps his knees, “and the liver slipped out of him, and the dark blood from it / filled his bosom” (κόλπον ἐνέπλησεν, 20.470–471). Walter Leaf reports Aristarchus' reading ἐνέπρησεν, that the blood “puffed out” the loose fold of the tunic, and remarks that Virgil evidently read our text, for when Aeneas kills Lausus the point “passed through the shield, light arms for one so threatening, and the tunic that his mother had woven with soft gold, and the blood filled his bosom” (*implevitque sinum sanguis*, *Aeneid* 10.817–819; Leaf on 20.471): [[tros-son-of-alastor]] and [[supplication]].
