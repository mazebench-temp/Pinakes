---
summary: When the gods come into the battle, Ares shouts to the Trojans now from the height of the city, now running beside the Simoeis on Callicolone.
grc: [Σιμόεντι]
---

“And Ares on the other side shouted, like a dark storm, / shrilly calling on the Trojans from the topmost height of the city, / and now running beside the Simoeis on Callicolone” (20.51–53; see [[ares]], [[callicolone]] and [[the-battle-of-the-gods]]). Walter Leaf takes θέων, “running”, with the words that follow, ἐπὶ Καλλικολώνῃ, since with πὰρ Σιμόεντι a verb of running would need the accusative, πὰρ Σιμόεντα (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 20.53): Ares runs on Callicolone, and the hill stands beside the river (see the translation's note on 20.53). Strabo, describing the plain after Demetrius of Scepsis, puts Callicolone ten stadia above the village of the Ilians, with the Simoeis flowing past it five stadia off (*Geography* 13.1.35; see [[strabo]]).

The Simoeis is not among the gods at war, as the Scamander is, though Leaf suggests that Σιμοεντ- may be another rendering of the same foreign name (on 20.74; see [[scamander]]). In Book 21 the Scamander calls on it, φίλε κασίγνητε, “dear brother”, to help him hold back Achilles (21.305–311).
