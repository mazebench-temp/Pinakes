Hector has seen his brother Polydorus fall to Achilles' javelin, holding his bowels in his hands, and comes against Achilles “like a flame” (20.419–423; see [[polydorus-son-of-priam]] and [[hector]]). Achilles leaps up and speaks in triumph: “Here near me is the man who has touched my heart most deeply, / who killed my honored companion; no longer now / will we shrink from each other along the bridges of war” (20.425–427; see [[patroclus]]). The verb is the one with which Agamemnon taunted Diomedes, “why do you cower” (πτώσσεις, 4.371). Here it takes an object, ἀλλήλους πτώσσοιμεν, “shrink from each other”, and Walter Leaf notes that this use recurs only in the Odyssey (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 20.426), where birds sweep over the plain “shrinking from the clouds” (νέφεα πτώσσουσαι, *Odyssey* 22.304). In Achilles' mouth the bridges of war are the ground between two men who have kept apart until now. The two meet on it at once, and part again: Athena turns Hector's spear back with a breath, and Apollo snatches Hector away in thick mist (20.438–444; see [[divine-rescue]]).
