---
summary: Achilles mocks Aeneas' hope “that you will rule over the horse-taming Trojans / with the honor of Priam” (20.180–181): Priam will not hand him the prize, “for he has sons, and he is steady, and not light of mind” (20.183). Poseidon foretells that Aeneas and his line will rule the Trojans (20.307–308).
refs: [20.179-186, 20.230, 20.306-308]
---

Walter Leaf notes that Aeneas' claim is explained by his genealogy (20.213–241), and recalls that Aeneas was always angry with Priam, who did not honor him (13.460–461; *Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 20.180). The rule passes by fate, not by Priam's gift: Zeus “has come to hate the lineage of Priam” (20.306; see [[the-rule-of-aeneas]], [[anax]], [[geras]] and [[time]]).
