---
summary: Achilles' javelin strikes Polydorus in the back “where the golden clasps of the belt / held it together, and the corselet met it in a double layer” (20.414–415), the words that told where Pandarus' arrow struck Menelaus (4.132–133).
---

In Book 4 Athena guided the arrow to that place, and it pierced belt, corselet and the guard beneath and only grazed the skin (4.132–140). Here the spear goes straight through, beside the navel (20.416). See [[belt]], [[corselet]] and [[polydorus-son-of-priam]].
