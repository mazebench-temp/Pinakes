**Aeneas' helmet.** Aeneas comes out first, threatening, νευστάζων κόρυθι βριαρῇ, “tossing his heavy helmet” (20.162; see [[heavy-helmet]] and [[aeneas]]). Achilles will come at Hector nodding his shining helmet, κόρυθι δʼ ἐπένευε φαεινῇ (22.314).

**Achilles' helmet.** The stone that Aeneas lifts would have struck Achilles “on helmet or on the shield that had kept grim destruction from him” (20.289; see [[achilles]] and [[the-shield-of-achilles]]): the helmet that Hephaestus made, heavy and fitted to his temples, with a golden crest (18.611–612; see the notes on Books 18 and 19).

**Demoleon.** Achilles stabbed him “in the temple, through the bronze-cheeked helmet. / Nor did the bronze helmet hold it, but right through it / the point, pressing on, broke the bone, and the brain / inside was all spattered” (20.397–400; see [[demoleon]] and [[bronze-cheeked]]). The verses are those of Damasus' death at the hands of Polypoetes (12.183–186), and nearly those of Oileus' at Agamemnon's, where the helmet is “heavy with bronze” (11.95–98; Walter Leaf, *Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 20.397).

**Deucalion.** Achilles “struck his neck with his sword / and sent his head flying far, helmet and all” (20.481–482: τῆλʼ αὐτῇ πήληκι κάρη βάλε; see [[deucalion-the-trojan]] and [[sword]]), as Peneleos dashed Ilioneus' head to the ground, “helmet and all” (αὐτῇ σὺν πήληκι, 14.497–498).
