summary: Words come before blows in this book. Apollo tells Aeneas not to let Achilles “turn you back with wretched words and threats” (20.109), and Aeneas and Hector answer Achilles in the same verses: “do not think that with words, as if I were a child, / you will frighten me” (20.200–201 = 20.431–432). Aeneas knows Achilles' parents from “the tales mortal men have told of old” (20.204), will not part from him with “childish words” (20.211), speaks of “the pasture of words” (20.249) and says, “Whatever kind of word you speak, such will you hear back” (20.250), and ends, “You will not turn me from courage with words” (20.256). Hector could fight “even with the immortals in words” (20.367).
grc: [ἐπέων]
---

πρόκλυτʼ … ἔπεα are words “heard in old times”, or perhaps handed on by tradition, as Walter Leaf explains them (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 20.204; see the translation's note on 20.204): the two heroes know each other's parents by report, not by sight (20.203–205; see [[memory-and-the-muses]]).
