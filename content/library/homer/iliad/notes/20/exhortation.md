---
summary: Two exhortations answer each other. Achilles calls on every Achaean to go man against man, since not even Ares or Athena could manage so great a battle alone (20.353–363); Hector bids the Trojans not to fear him (20.365–372); both end “So he spoke, urging them on” (20.364, 20.373).
---

**Achilles.** “Stand no longer far from the Trojans now, brilliant Achaeans, / but come, let man go against man, and let him be eager to fight” (20.354–355). His reason is the size of the battle: “not even Ares, though he is an immortal god, nor Athena / could deal with the mouth of so great a battle” (20.358–359; [[mighty]], [[war-loving]]).

**Hector.** Hector takes up Achilles' comparison with the gods: “I too could fight even with the immortals in words, / but with the spear it is hard” (20.367–368). He promises to meet Achilles “even if his hands are like fire” (20.371–372; [[hands-like-fire]], [[epanalepsis]], [[tawny]]). Apollo at once forbids it, and Hector goes back into the crowd (20.375–380). See [[hector]] and [[achilles]].
