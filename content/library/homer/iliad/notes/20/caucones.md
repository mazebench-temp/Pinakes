---
summary: Poseidon lifts Aeneas out of his fight with Achilles and sets him down at the far edge of the battle, “where the Caucones were arming themselves for war” (20.329). It is the last time the Iliad names them.
---

Aeneas, “springing from the hand of the god”, flies over many ranks of men and horses “and came to the far edge of the furious war, / where the Caucones were arming themselves for war” (20.327–329; see [[divine-rescue]] and [[aeneas]]). θωρήσσοντο, “were arming themselves”, is the verb of the book's first line, where the Achaeans arm around Achilles (20.1); Walter Leaf takes it here as “were entering the fight” (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 20.329). There Poseidon warns Aeneas to keep away from Achilles (20.330–339; see [[poseidon]]).

Leaf notes that the Caucones are not among the Trojan allies of the Catalogue, and compares the Caucones whom Athena visits from Pylos in the Odyssey, as Pelasgians too are found both in Greece and in Asia Minor (on 20.329; *Odyssey* 3.366; see [[pelasgians]] and the translation's note on 20.329). Dolon named them with the Leleges among the allies camped toward the sea (10.428–429), and the two peoples frame Aeneas' part in this book: the Leleges in his memory of his flight from Achilles, the Caucones where he lands after his second escape (20.96, 20.329; see [[leleges]]).
