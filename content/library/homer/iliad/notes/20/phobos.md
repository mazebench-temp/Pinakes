---
summary: Aeneas has fled from Achilles before: “already once before he put me to flight with his spear / from Ida, when he came upon our cattle” (20.90–91), and Achilles reminds him of it, “Already, I say, once before I put you to flight with my spear”, and “Then you never turned around as you fled” (20.187–190). Before the gods join the armies, the sight of Achilles is enough: “a dread trembling came over the limbs of every Trojan” (20.44).
refs: [20.44-46, 20.89-93, 20.187-194]
---

Aeneas says that Zeus saved him then, rousing his might and his nimble knees (20.92–93), and he answers Achilles' taunt with the maxim that Zeus increases and diminishes the prowess of men (20.242–243; see [[zeus-increases-and-diminishes]] and [[the-sack-of-lyrnessus]]).
