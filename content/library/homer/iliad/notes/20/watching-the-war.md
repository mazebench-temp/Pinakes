summary: Zeus sends the gods into the war and stays to watch: “But I for my part will stay here in a fold of Olympus, / seated, where I shall look on and delight my heart” (20.22–23). Poseidon proposes that his party sit “off the path, at a lookout”, and leave the war to men (20.136–137): they sit on the wall of Heracles wrapped in cloud, and the other party on the brows of Callicolone, while “Zeus, sitting on high, bade them” (20.144–155).
refs: [20.22-23, 20.136-155]
---

Zeus' pleasure in watching sits oddly beside his care for the dying, “I care for them, even as they perish” (20.21), and Walter Leaf notes that the next book develops it to exaggeration, when Zeus laughs to see the gods meet in strife (21.389–390; *Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 20.23). The gods who came down to fight become an audience on two hills; the battle they watch is the duel of Achilles and Aeneas (see [[the-battle-of-the-gods]], [[the-wall-of-heracles]] and [[callicolone]]).
