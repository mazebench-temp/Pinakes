summary: Tros, son of Alastor, comes to clasp Achilles' knees “in the hope that he would take him and spare him and let him go alive, / and not kill him, pitying a man of his own age” (20.464–465); but Achilles “was no sweet-tempered man, nor gentle-hearted, / but in a great fury” (20.467–468). Zeus says of the mortals, “I care for them, even as they perish” (20.21).
refs: [20.21, 20.293, 20.463-469]
---

Pity is what the suppliant asks for, and Achilles has none to give; the narrator's own judgment on him (20.467), which Walter Leaf thinks alien to the epic style, is one of his reasons for suspecting 20.463–468 (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 20.463; see the translation's note on 20.463–468). Among the gods Poseidon has grief for Aeneas and saves him (20.293–308; see [[achos]], [[supplication]] and [[tros-son-of-alastor]]).
