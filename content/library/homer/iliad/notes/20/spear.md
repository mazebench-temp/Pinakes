---
summary: Spears fill the book. Aeneas' heavy spear drives through two layers of Achilles' shield and is stopped in the gold, and Achilles' Pelian ash passes over Aeneas' back into the ground (20.259–283); Hector's spear is turned back by Athena's breath (20.438–441). Achilles kills most of the men he meets with the spear or the javelin, and at the end he rushes everywhere “with his spear, like a god” (20.386–488, 20.493).
---

**Put to flight.** Aeneas tells Apollo that Achilles once “put me to flight with his spear” from Ida (20.90), and Achilles reminds him of it in the same words, “once before I put you to flight with my spear” (20.187; see [[the-sack-of-lyrnessus]]).

**The duel.** Aeneas ends his speech, “let us taste of each other with our bronze-fitted spears” (20.258; see [[bronze-fitted]]). Walter Leaf notes that γεύομαι, “make trial of”, more naturally takes a weapon than a man for its object, as when Achilles says that Lycaon shall taste the point of his spear (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 20.258; 21.60–61). Each man throws once. Aeneas' spear, ὄβριμον ἔγχος, “the heavy spear” (20.259, 20.267; see [[heavy-spear]]), and “the long-shadowed spear” in Achilles' fear (20.262; see [[long-shadowed]]), drives through two layers of the god's shield and is stopped, μείλινον ἔγχος, “the ash spear” (20.272; see [[ash-spear]] and [[the-shield-of-achilles]]); Aristotle quotes the half-verse with “bronze” for “ash” (see [[the-bronze-spear-stopped]]). Achilles' long-shadowed spear, the Pelian ash, passes through the rim of Aeneas' shield and over his back, and stands in the ground (20.273–283; see [[the-pelian-ash]]). Then the sword and the stone take their place (20.283–287).

**Javelins.** ἄκων, “javelin”, is the weapon with which Achilles kills Polydorus, Dryops and Rhigmus (20.413, 20.455, 20.486; see [[polydorus-son-of-priam]], [[dryops]] and [[rhigmus]]), and he tells Hector that Apollo is the god “to whom you must pray when you go into the thud of javelins” (20.451 = 11.364, Diomedes' words to Hector; see [[apollo]]).

**Hector's spear.** Hector would fight even the gods in words, “but with the spear it is hard, since they are far stronger than we are” (20.368). He comes against Achilles “brandishing his sharp spear” (20.423) and throws it, since “my weapon too is sharp at the point” (20.437; see the translation's note on 20.437), but Athena turns it back with a breath and it falls at his own feet (20.438–441; see [[athena]] and [[hector]]).

**The killings.** Iphition he hit “with his spear / full on the head” (20.386–387); through Demoleon's helmet “the point, pressing on, broke the bone” (20.399); Hippodamas “he stabbed … with his spear in the back” (20.402); in Polydorus “the point of the spear went straight through, beside the navel” (20.416); Demuchus he hit “on the knee with his spear” (20.458), and Laogonus, “hitting the one with his spear” (20.462); Mulius “with his spear in the ear; and at once through the other ear went / the bronze point” (20.473–474); Deucalion “there he pierced through the arm / with the bronze point” (20.479–480); and Areithous “with his sharp spear” (20.488; see [[androktasia]] and [[achilles]]). The rampage ends with the simile of the forest fire, “so he rushed everywhere with his spear, like a god” (20.493; see [[forest-fire]]).

The lion of Achilles' simile is roused when a young man “hits him with a spear” (20.168; see [[like-a-lion]]). Poseidon goes “through the battle and through the turmoil of spears” (20.319), in the verse of Aeneas going to find Pandarus (5.167; see [[poseidon]]); Achilles promises that no Trojan “will rejoice, whoever comes near my spear” (20.363), and the Trojans answer Hector's call: “they raised their spears to face them” (20.373).
