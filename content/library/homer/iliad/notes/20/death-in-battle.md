summary: Achilles kills fourteen named men in the last part of the book (20.381–489), and then the rank and file under his horses' hooves (20.490–503). The wounds are told plainly: a head split in two (20.386–387), a brain all spattered within the skull (20.397–400), a man who bellows like a bull as his life goes (20.403–406), bowels held in the hands (20.416–418), a liver slipping out (20.469–471), a spear through both ears (20.472–474), a head sent flying, helmet and all (20.481–483).
refs: [20.381-503]
---

A few of the dead are given a life. Iphition was born to a Naiad under snowy Tmolus, and Achilles' boast tells him that his birthplace is by the Gygaean lake (20.382–392); Polydorus is Priam's youngest son, forbidden to fight (20.407–412); Tros, son of Alastor, is a man of Achilles' own age who begs for his life (20.463–472); Rhigmus had come from deep-soiled Thrace (20.484–485). The formulas of dying close several of the deaths: “and darkness covered his eyes” (20.393, 20.471), “and he fell with a thud” (20.388), “purple death and mighty fate” (20.477; see [[darkness-covered-his-eyes]], [[fell-with-a-thud]] and [[purple-death]]). Iphition's body is torn by the wheels of the Achaean chariots (20.394), and at the end the axle of Achilles' chariot is spattered with blood thrown up by the horses' hooves (20.499–502; see [[androktasia]] and [[the-aristeia]]). The fourteen are [[iphition|Iphition]], [[demoleon|Demoleon]], [[hippodamas|Hippodamas]], [[polydorus-son-of-priam|Polydorus]], [[dryops|Dryops]], [[demuchus|Demuchus]], [[laogonus-son-of-bias|Laogonus]] and [[dardanus-son-of-bias|Dardanus]], the sons of Bias, [[tros-son-of-alastor|Tros]], son of Alastor, [[mulius-killed-by-achilles|Mulius]], [[echeclus-son-of-agenor|Echeclus]], [[deucalion-the-trojan|Deucalion]], [[rhigmus|Rhigmus]] and [[areithous-the-attendant|Areithous]].
