summary: In the duel of Achilles and Aeneas the poem calls Achilles' shield σάκος and Aeneas' ἀσπίς. Aeneas' spear goes through two of the five layers of Achilles' shield and is stopped in the gold (20.259–272); Achilles' spear strikes Aeneas' shield under the outermost rim, where the bronze and the oxhide were thinnest, goes through and parts both its circles (20.274–281), and Poseidon draws it out of the shield (20.322–323). At the end of the book Achilles' horses trample corpses and shields (20.499).
# The layers, rim, hide and circles of the shields in the duel (20.270, 20.275, 20.276, 20.280); each form only here in Homer.
grc: ["πέντε πτύχας", "ἄντυγʼ", "ῥινὸς βοός", κύκλους]
en: [oxhide]
---

**Two shields.** The duel keeps the two words apart. Achilles' shield, the one Hephaestus made, is σάκος (20.259, 20.260, 20.261, 20.268, 20.289), the word for the great shields (see [[the-shield-of-achilles]]); Aeneas holds “his furious shield / before his chest” (ἀσπίδα θοῦριν, 20.162–163; see [[furious]]), and his is ἀσπίς throughout (20.274, 20.277, 20.278, 20.281, 20.323).

**The layers.** Aeneas' spear “drove through two layers, and there were still three, / since the crook-footed god had beaten out five layers” (20.269–270), and four verses, athetized by Aristarchus, make the five layers metals (20.269–272; see the note on [[the-shield-of-achilles]] and the translation's note on 20.269–272). Hector's spear went through six of the seven layers of Ajax's shield, διὰ πτύχας, and was stopped in the seventh hide (7.247–248; see [[the-shield-of-ajax]]).

**The rim and the circles.** Achilles' spear struck Aeneas' shield “under the outermost rim, where the bronze ran thinnest, / and the oxhide on it was thinnest” (20.275–276). Walter Leaf takes πρώτην as the extreme edge of the rim, counted from the circumference inward, as πυμάτη at 6.118 counts it from the center outward; χαλκός is the metal facing, made thinner at the edge, which matters less for defense than the center; and ῥινὸς βοός is the body of the shield, the hide backing the metal, not in front of it (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 20.275–276). The spear “had parted both circles / of the man-covering shield” (20.280–281; see [[man-covering]]): the bronze and the leather, as the A scholia explain and Leaf accepts, which a blow at the edge would tear apart. He allows that κύκλοι might suit the Mycenaean shield shaped like a figure eight, but thinks the poet of so late a passage had the round shield in mind, and that the passage has no bearing on the shape of Achilles' shield in Book 18 (on 20.280).

**Through the shield.** The spear “passing over his back, stood fixed in the ground” (20.279), yet Poseidon draws it out of the shield (20.322–323); Aristarchus athetized 322–324 for the contradiction (see [[the-pelian-ash]] and the translation's note on 20.322–324).

At the end of the book Achilles' horses trample “corpses and shields alike” (20.499 = 11.534; see [[the-horses-of-achilles]]).
