---
summary: Achilles bids Aeneas “draw back / and go into the crowd, and do not stand against me” (20.196–197), and Poseidon, having saved him, tells him, “No, draw back whenever you meet him” (20.335). Apollo tells Hector to wait for Achilles “in the crowd and out of the din”, and Hector goes back “afraid, when he heard the voice of the god” (20.377–380).
refs: [20.195-198, 20.335-339, 20.375-380]
---

Achilles' advice ends in the saying that a fool learns a thing once it is done (20.198; see [[a-fool-learns-once-it-is-done]]); Aristarchus athetized 20.195–198, since Achilles, come for vengeance, should not want his first enemy to escape (Walter Leaf, *Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 20.195). Aeneas is carried out of the fight by the god; Hector takes Apollo's advice until his brother's death brings him out again (20.419–423; see [[divine-intervention]]).
