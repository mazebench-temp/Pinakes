---
summary: Apollo rouses Aeneas in the likeness of Lycaon: “he made his voice like that of Priam's son Lycaon; / in his likeness Apollo, son of Zeus, spoke to him” (20.81–82). In Aeneas' genealogy the North Wind lies with Erichthonius' mares “in the likeness of a dark-maned stallion” (20.224).
---

The god takes a man's voice, as Poseidon took the voices of Calchas and of Thoas to rouse the Achaeans (13.45, 13.216), and Aeneas answers the man he hears, “Son of Priam” (20.87); the poem does not say that he knew the god (see [[seeing-the-gods]] and [[lycaon-son-of-priam]]). The stallion's likeness makes the North Wind the sire of the twelve foals that ran over the ears of grain and the breakers of the sea (20.223–229; see [[the-mares-of-erichthonius]] and [[boreas]]).
