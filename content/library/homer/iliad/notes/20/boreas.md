# Unnamed: "in the likeness of a dark-maned stallion he lay with them" (20.224).
refs: [20.224]
---

“And the North Wind fell in love with them as they grazed, / and in the likeness of a dark-maned stallion he lay with them; / and they conceived and bore twelve foals” (20.223–225; see [[the-mares-of-erichthonius]], [[erichthonius]] and [[aeneas]]). The foals ran “on the very tips of the ripe ears” and “on the very crests of the breakers of the gray sea” (20.227–229). κυανοχαίτῃ, “dark-maned”, is elsewhere the epithet of Poseidon, “dark-haired”, as in this book (20.144), and Walter Leaf sees no special point in it for a horse (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 20.224; see [[dark-haired]] and the translation's note on 20.224).

The North Wind is a god who takes a stallion's likeness, as gods take the likeness of men and birds. So the West Wind fathered Achilles' horses Xanthus and Balius on the Harpy Podarge, “as she grazed in a meadow beside the stream of Oceanus” (16.150–151; see [[zephyrus]], [[podarge]] and [[the-horses-of-achilles]]). Leaf notes that the belief that mares could conceive by the wind was widespread in antiquity, but that here no more need be seen than a mythological form of words for extreme speed (on 20.223).
