---
summary: Apollo stands against Poseidon “with his feathered arrows” (20.68), with ἰά, a plural of ἰός found only here.
grc: ["ἰὰ πτερόεντα"]
---

“For against lord Poseidon / stood Phoebus Apollo with his feathered arrows” (20.67–68). ἔναντα, “against,” and the neuter plural ἰά for ἰούς, “arrows,” occur only here in Homer (Walter Leaf, *Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 20.67; see the translation's note on 20.67–74). See [[apollo]], [[arrows]] and [[winged-words]].
