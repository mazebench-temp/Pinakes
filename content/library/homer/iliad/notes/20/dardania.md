---
summary: Aeneas names Dardania as the first foundation of his line. Dardanus, son of Zeus, founded it when Ilios was not yet built in the plain, and its people dwelt on the slopes of Ida (20.215–218).
---

κτίσσε δὲ Δαρδανίην, “and he founded Dardania” (20.216), is the second verse of the genealogy that Aeneas gives Achilles, after the birth of Dardanus to Zeus (20.215; see [[dardanus]] and [[genealogy-in-battle]]). κτίζω, “found”, occurs only twice in Homer, here and of Amphion and Zethus, “who first founded the seat of seven-gated Thebes” (*Odyssey* 11.263). The next verse gives the reason in a negative, ἐπεὶ οὔ πω Ἴλιος ἱρὴ / ἐν πεδίῳ πεπόλιστο, “since sacred Ilios was not yet / built in the plain” (20.216–217); πολίζω, “build”, is used elsewhere in the Iliad only of the wall that Poseidon and Apollo built for Laomedon (7.453; see [[the-walls-of-troy]]). “Sacred Ilios” ends the verse here as it does in the prophecy of its fall, “a day will come when sacred Ilios will perish” (4.164 = 6.448; see [[troy]]): the genealogy begins with the city not yet built.

The people of Dardania “still dwelt on the slopes of Ida of the many springs” (ὑπωρείας, 20.218), a word found only here in Homer; the formula πολυπίδακος Ἴδης is the one of the earthquake at the start of the book, when “all the feet of Ida of the many springs were shaken” (20.59; see [[ida]]). The house kept its place on the mountain. Aphrodite bore Aeneas to Anchises “on the slopes of Ida” (2.820–821), and Achilles came upon Aeneas among his cattle on Ida and drove him “down the mountains of Ida” (20.90–92, 20.188–190; see [[anchises]] and [[aeneas]]).
