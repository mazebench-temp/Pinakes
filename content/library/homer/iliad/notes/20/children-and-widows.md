summary: Twice in the book a hero tells Achilles not to think he can frighten him with words “as if I were a child” (20.200, 20.431), and Aeneas ends his account of his lineage with Idomeneus' verse, “But come, let us no longer talk like this, like children” (20.244 = 13.292).
grc: ["νηπύτιον ὣς"]
en: [as if I were a child]
---

**As if I were a child.** Πηλεΐδη μὴ δὴ ἐπέεσσί με νηπύτιον ὣς / ἔλπεο δειδίξεσθαι: “Son of Peleus, do not think that with words, as if I were a child, / you will frighten me” (20.200–201). Aeneas answers Achilles' taunts so (see [[aeneas]]), and Hector answers Achilles' threat with the same three verses later in the book (20.431–433 = 20.200–202; see [[hector]]). νηπύτιος, “childish”, is a form of νήπιος (see [[nepios]]), found only in Books 13, 20 and 21. A child is frightened by words, and the speaker says that he is not one: he too knows how to speak taunts. Hector had used the same likeness against Ajax in Book 7, “do not test me as if I were some feeble boy / or a woman, who does not know the works of war” (7.235–236).

**Like children.** At the end of his lineage Aeneas turns the likeness on both of them: ἀλλʼ ἄγε μηκέτι ταῦτα λεγώμεθα νηπύτιοι ὣς / ἑσταότʼ ἐν μέσσῃ ὑσμίνῃ δηϊοτῆτος, “But come, let us no longer talk like this, like children, / standing in the middle of the battle of combat” (20.244–245). The first verse is the one with which Idomeneus broke off his talk with Meriones (13.292), and the second keeps its standing men, ἑσταότες, with the battle in place of the fear of someone's indignation (13.293). Children who talk instead of fighting were Nestor's reproach to the army in Book 2 (2.337–338). A few verses later Aeneas adds women to the children: why should he and Achilles quarrel face to face like women who go out into the middle of the street to abuse each other (20.251–255; see [[women-quarreling-in-the-street]])? The pair, children and women, is the one of Odysseus' rebuke in Book 2 (2.289–290). Aeneas' speech also speaks of “childish words” (ἐπέεσσί γε νηπυτίοισιν, 20.211), the words with which, he says, the two of them will not part.
