summary: As long as the gods stay apart, “the Achaeans exulted greatly”, because Achilles had appeared (20.42). The gods' gifts are “glorious” (20.265), Athena turns Hector's spear back “from glorious Achilles” (20.439), and the book ends with Achilles “eager to win glory” (20.502).
grc: [κύδανον]
en: [exulted]
---

μέγα κύδανον is intransitive, “exulted greatly”; the verb recurs only at 14.73, where it is transitive, “gives glory” (Walter Leaf, *Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 20.42; see the translation's row on κυδάνω). κῦδος ἀρέσθαι, “to win glory”, is the aim of the fighting in many verses (12.407, 16.88, 17.287); at the end of this book Achilles pursues it over the corpses (20.498–503; see [[the-aristeia]]).
