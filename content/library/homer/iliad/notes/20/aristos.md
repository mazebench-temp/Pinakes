---
summary: Hera wants Achilles to know “that the best of the immortals love him” (20.122), and the narrator brings the two champions into the open: “And two men, by far the best, / came together between the two armies, eager to fight, / Aeneas, son of Anchises, and brilliant Achilles” (20.158–160).
---

Walter Leaf compares the meeting of Aeneas and Idomeneus, “two warlike men beyond the others” (13.499–500), and of Glaucus and Diomedes, who “came together between the two armies, eager to fight” (6.120), and notes that this meeting is introduced as if by chance, though Apollo sent Aeneas to it (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 20.158; see [[genealogy-in-battle]]). Poseidon puts the difference between the two best in his warning: Achilles is “at once stronger than you and dearer to the immortals” (20.334; see [[philos]] and [[achilles-and-aeneas]]).
