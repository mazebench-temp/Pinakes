---
summary: Poseidon's earthquake shakes all the feet of Ida of the many springs and its peaks. Aeneas and Achilles both remember the day when Achilles drove Aeneas from his cattle down the mountains of Ida, and in Aeneas' lineage the people of Dardanus live on the slopes of Ida before Ilios is built in the plain.
---

**The earthquake.** When the gods come together, Poseidon shakes “the boundless earth and the steep peaks of the mountains. / And all the feet of Ida of the many springs were shaken, / and its peaks, and the city of the Trojans and the ships of the Achaeans” (20.58–60; see [[poseidon]], [[of-the-many-springs]] and [[the-battle-of-the-gods]]). πόδες, “feet”, are the lowest slopes of the mountain, as Zeleia lies “under the lowest foot of Ida” (2.824), and κορυφαί its peaks, where Zeus has his precinct on Gargarus (8.47–48; see [[gargarus]]). The critic of *On the Sublime* runs 20.60 into his quotation of Poseidon's advance in Book 13 (*On the Sublime* 9.8; see [[longinus-poseidons-advance]]).

**Aeneas' cattle.** Aeneas tells Apollo that Achilles put him to flight once before “from Ida, when he came upon our cattle / and sacked Lyrnessus and Pedasus” (20.91–92; see [[aeneas]] and [[the-sack-of-lyrnessus]]), and Achilles reminds him of it: “Do you not remember when you were alone, and I drove you / away from the cattle, down the mountains of Ida on swift feet, / swiftly?” (20.188–190). Princes of Troy herd on Ida: Aphrodite bore Aeneas to Anchises “on the slopes of Ida” (2.820–821), as he tended his cattle (5.313; see [[anchises]]), and Achilles took two sons of Priam as they tended their flocks on its spurs (11.104–106). Ἰδαίων ὀρέων, “the mountains of Ida”, is the phrase of Book 8, where Zeus “thundered from the mountains of Ida” (8.170; see the note on Book 8).

**The first settlement.** In Aeneas' lineage, Zeus fathered Dardanus, “and he founded Dardania, since sacred Ilios was not yet / built in the plain, a city of mortal men, / but they still dwelt on the slopes of Ida of the many springs” (20.216–218; see [[dardanus]], [[dardania]] and [[troy]]). ὑπωρείας, “slopes”, are the foothills below the mountain (see the translation's note on 20.218). Plato quotes these lines in the *Laws* for the third form of settlement, when men came down from the heights into the plain (*Laws* 681e–682b; see [[plato]]).
