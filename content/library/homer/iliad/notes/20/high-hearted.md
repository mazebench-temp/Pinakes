---
summary: Aeneas is unwilling “to fight face to face with the high-hearted son of Peleus” (20.88), and after the rescue Poseidon asks him which god bade him do it, in the same verse (20.333); Hector calls on the “High-hearted Trojans” not to fear him (20.366).
---

The verse ἀντία Πηλεΐωνος ὑπερθύμοιο μάχεσθαι frames Aeneas' part in the book: he speaks it in reluctance before the duel, and Poseidon gives it back to him as a reproach after it (20.88 = 20.333; see [[son-of-peleus]]). See [[aeneas]] and [[trojans]].
