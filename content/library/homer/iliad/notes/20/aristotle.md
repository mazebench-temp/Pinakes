---
summary: The Poetics answers two problems from Book 20, how Ganymede can “pour wine for Zeus” when the gods drink no wine, and in how many senses Aeneas' spear could be “stopped” in the shield of Achilles. The History of Animals reports that the Scamander was thought to turn sheep yellow, and that this was why Homer called it Xanthus.
---

- Ganymede, who “pours wine for Zeus” though the gods do not drink wine (*Poetics* 25, 1461a29–31, on 20.234): [[aristotle-wine-of-the-gods]] and [[cupbearing]].
- The spear “stopped” in the shield's gold layer (*Poetics* 25, 1461a31–35, on 20.272), quoted with χάλκεον, “bronze”, where the Iliad has μείλινον, “ash”: [[the-bronze-spear-stopped]].
- **Xanthus.** Among the waters that change the color of animals' hair, Aristotle names two rivers of the Antandrian country, one of which makes sheep white and the other black, and adds: “The Scamander river too is thought to make sheep yellow; and that is why they say Homer calls it Xanthus instead of Scamander” (*History of Animals* 3.12). The verse is “whom the gods call Xanthus, and men Scamander” (20.74): [[scamander]] and [[plato-chalcis-and-cymindis]].
