---
summary: Achilles taunts Aeneas with the hope of ruling the Trojans “with the honor of Priam” (20.181), and calls Patroclus “my honored companion” (20.426).
grc: [τετιμένον]
---

The honor is the thing ruled over: Walter Leaf renders “to be master of Priam's dignity among the Trojans”, with the genitive of the thing ruled, as in τιμῆς … ἧς περ ἄνασσες, “the honor you were lord of” (*Odyssey* 24.30), and he calls the article in τιμῆς τῆς Πριάμου a late construction (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 20.180). Aeneas, the poem said in Book 13, was always angry with Priam because Priam did not honor him (13.460–461; see [[kingship-and-authority]], [[geras]] and [[honor-and-dishonor]]).
