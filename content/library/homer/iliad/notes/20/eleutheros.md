---
summary: Achilles reminds Aeneas of Lyrnessus, which he sacked “and took away the day of freedom from the women, and led them off / as captives” (20.193–194).
---

The verse is the one in which Hector, over the dying Patroclus, said what Patroclus had meant to do at Troy, with one word changed: ληϊάδας, “captive”, for Τρωϊάδας, “Trojan” (20.193; 16.831). Hector spoke of a plan that failed, Achilles of a city he took. Briseis was one of the women of Lyrnessus (2.690–691; see [[captive-women]], [[the-sack-of-lyrnessus]] and [[slavery-and-freedom]]).
