---
summary: Poseidon addresses Aeneas “with winged words” when he has set him down at the edge of the battle (20.331), and Achilles, at his fourth charge against the mist, “shouted at him terribly and addressed him with winged words” (20.448).
---

The second verse is the one in which Apollo, at Patroclus' fourth assault on the wall, shouted at him (16.706). Here the hero speaks it, not the god, and Walter Leaf notes that the fourth onset leads to nothing more than Achilles' speech (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 20.444; see [[three-times-and-a-fourth]]).
