summary: Achilles holds his shield away from Aeneas' spear in fear, “the fool, nor did he consider in his mind and in his heart / that the glorious gifts of the gods are not easy / for mortal men to master, nor do they give way” (20.264–266), and the gold of the shield holds the spear back, “the gifts of the god” (20.268).
grc: ["θεῶν ἐρικυδέα δῶρα"]
en: [glorious gifts of the gods]
---

The narrator's maxim joins Paris' half-verse, θεῶν ἐρικυδέα δῶρα (3.65), to Odysseus' words of Achilles' horses, hard “for mortal men to master” (ἀνδράσι γε θνητοῖσι δαμήμεναι, 10.403). The gift here is the shield that Hephaestus made at Thetis' request (18.369–617; see [[the-shield-of-achilles]], [[doron]] and [[nepios]]).
