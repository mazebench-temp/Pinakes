---
summary: Phoebus Apollo goes to the Trojans (20.39), stands against Poseidon (20.68), sets Aeneas on against Achilles (20.118), sits with the Trojan gods on Callicolone (20.152), warns Hector away from Achilles (20.375), and snatches him from Achilles in mist (20.443–450).
---

Poseidon names him with Ares as the gods who might begin the fight among the gods, “But if Ares or Phoebus Apollo begins the battle” (20.138). Achilles' taunt after Hector's rescue, “and now again Phoebus Apollo has saved you, / to whom you must pray when you go into the thud of javelins” (20.450–451), repeats the six verses that Diomedes shouted after Hector in Book 11 (20.449–454 = 11.362–367; Walter Leaf, *Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 20.449).
