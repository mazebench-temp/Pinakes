summary: When the gods join the armies, Athena shouts to the Achaeans by the ditch and on the shore, “And Ares on the other side shouted, like a dark storm” (20.48–53). When Hector has rallied the Trojans, “their might mingled together, and the war cry rose” (20.374).
grc: ["=ἀϋτή", ἀΰτει]
---

Walter Leaf reports that Aristarchus began the main clause at αὖε δʼ Ἀθήνη, “and Athena shouted”, so that her shout answers Ares' (20.48, 20.51; *Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 20.48; see the translation's note on 20.48). The gods' war cry comes from both ends of the field: Athena's from the wall and the shore, Ares' from the height of the city and from Callicolone (see [[athena]], [[ares]] and [[callicolone]]).
