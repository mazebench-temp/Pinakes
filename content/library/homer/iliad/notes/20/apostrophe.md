summary: The narrator turns twice to a character: to Achilles, as the Achaeans arm “around you, son of Peleus” (20.2), and to Apollo, as the gods of the Trojan side sit on Callicolone “around you, archer Phoebus” (20.152).
grc: ["ἀμφὶ σὲ Πηλέος υἱὲ"]
en: [around you]
---

The two addresses are built alike, ἀμφὶ σέ with a vocative, and each puts its addressee at the center of a gathering. The first is the narrator's only address to Achilles, at the moment the army forms around him (20.1–2; see the translation's note on 20.2 and [[insatiable]]). The second comes as the gods sit apart on two heights, the Achaean party on the wall of Heracles and the Trojan party on the brows of Callicolone, “around you, archer Phoebus, and Ares, sacker of cities” (20.149–152; [[ieios]], [[callicolone]], [[the-wall-of-heracles]]). It is the second of the two places where the narrator speaks to Apollo, both with the vocative ἤϊε Φοῖβε (15.365; see the Book 15 note). See [[achilles]] and [[apollo]].
