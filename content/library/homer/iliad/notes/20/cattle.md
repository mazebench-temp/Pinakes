---
summary: Both Aeneas and Achilles remember the day Achilles came upon Aeneas' cattle on Ida and drove him from them (20.90–92, 20.187–190). Aeneas' shield is backed with oxhide (20.276), Hera is ox-eyed (20.309), and the bulls of the threshing simile are βόες, male cattle (20.495–497).
grc: ["βοῶν ἄπο"]
---

**The cattle on Ida.** Aeneas tells Apollo, who has taken the likeness of Lycaon, that this will not be the first time he has faced Achilles: “but already once before he put me to flight with his spear / from Ida, when he came upon our cattle / and sacked Lyrnessus and Pedasus” (20.90–92). Achilles tells it as a taunt: “Do you not remember when you were alone, and I drove you / away from the cattle, down the mountains of Ida on swift feet, / swiftly? Then you never turned around as you fled” (20.188–190). ἡμετέρῃσι, “our”, makes the herd Aeneas' family's (20.91). The house of Anchises kept cattle on Ida: Anchises was tending his cattle when Aphrodite bore Aeneas to him (βουκολέοντι, 5.313; see [[anchises]] and the note on Book 5). Cattle were what men raided and fought for; Achilles told Agamemnon in Book 1 that the Trojans had never driven off his own (1.154; see [[raiding]] and [[the-sack-of-lyrnessus]]). Walter Leaf reports from Didymus that the editions of Rhianus and Aristophanes read βοῶν ἔπι, “by the cattle”, for βοῶν ἄπο, “away from the cattle”, but doubts it, since in that sense ἐπί takes the dative (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 20.188).

**Hide.** Achilles' spear struck Aeneas' shield under the outermost rim, “where the bronze ran thinnest, / and the oxhide on it was thinnest” (20.275–276). ῥινὸς βοός is the hide of an ox; Leaf takes it as the body of the shield, backing the metal facing (on 20.276; see [[shield]]).

**Ox-eyed.** βοῶπις πότνια Ἥρη, “ox-eyed queen Hera”, answers Poseidon when he would save Aeneas (20.309; see [[ox-eyed]] and [[hera]]).

**The bulls of the simile.** At the end of the book Achilles' horses trample the dead as βόες ἄρσενες, male cattle, rendered “bulls”, tread out white barley on a threshing floor (20.495–499; see [[bulls]] and [[bulls-on-the-threshing-floor]]).
