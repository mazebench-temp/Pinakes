---
summary: Lyrnessus is the town to which Aeneas fled when Achilles drove him from his cattle on Ida, and which Achilles sacked after him. Aeneas names it to Apollo, and Achilles to Aeneas, before their duel (20.92, 20.191).
---

Aeneas names it with Pedasus as the towns Achilles sacked when he came upon the cattle on Ida (20.92; see [[pedasus-on-the-satnioeis]]). Achilles names it as the place of Aeneas' refuge: “From there you escaped to Lyrnessus; but I / sacked it, going after you with Athena and father Zeus” (20.191–192). Neither man names Briseis, whom Achilles took “out of Lyrnessus after much toil” (2.690; see [[briseis]]); Achilles speaks only of the women he led away (20.193–194). See [[the-sack-of-lyrnessus]] and [[achilles-and-aeneas]].

Demetrius of Scepsis, as Strabo reports, used Achilles' lines to place the royal seat of Aeneas. He held that it was Scepsis, midway between the country under Aeneas and Lyrnessus, the town to which Aeneas was said to have fled from Achilles (*Geography* 13.1.53; see [[strabo-on-the-rule-of-aeneas]]).
