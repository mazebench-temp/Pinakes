---
summary: The gifts of the book pass between gods and men. Achilles holds his shield away from Aeneas' spear, not knowing “that the glorious gifts of the gods are not easy / for mortal men to master” (20.265–266), and the gold of the shield holds the spear back, “the gifts of the god” (20.268). Poseidon pleads that Aeneas “always gives pleasing / gifts to the gods who hold the wide sky” (20.298–299).
---

θεῶν ἐρικυδέα δῶρα, “the glorious gifts of the gods”, is the half-verse of Paris' answer to Hector (3.65; see [[gifts-of-the-gods]]); here the gifts are Hephaestus' work, and no man can break them (see [[the-shield-of-achilles]]). 20.268 returns at 21.165, and Walter Leaf explains the plural as the gold taken in its parts, standing for the whole metal facing of the shield (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 20.268). Aeneas' gifts go the other way, from a man to the gods (see [[charis]] and [[reciprocity-with-the-gods]]).
