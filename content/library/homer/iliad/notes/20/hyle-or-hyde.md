At 5.708 and 7.221 the text names Hyle, the Boeotian town, and some ancient readers put Hyde in its place. Here the text has Ὕδης, “of Hyde”, the Lydian birthplace of Iphition, whom a Naiad nymph bore to Otrynteus beneath Tmolus (20.382–385; see [[iphition]] and [[hyde]]), and Walter Leaf reports a variant Ὕλης, “of Hyle”, which he calls evidently wrong (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 20.385). He adds that some identified Hyde with the later Sardis, and that Strabo doubted it.

Strabo's discussion is in his account of Lydia (*Geography* 13.4.6). After the Catalogue's three verses on the Maeonians, led by the sons of Talaemenes, “whom the Gygaean lake bore”, and “born beneath Tmolus” (2.864–866), some added this verse as a fourth, “beneath snowy Tmolus, in the rich land of Hyde”. Some made Tychius, “by far the best of leather-workers”, come from there, reading “in Hyde” at 7.221; they added that the place was wooded and struck by lightning, and that the Arimi lived there, and to the verse on Typhoeus' bed among the Arimi (2.783) they joined another made from this one, χώρῳ ἐνὶ δρυόεντι, Ὕδης ἐν πίονι δήμῳ, “in a wooded country, in the rich land of Hyde”. “No Hyde is found among the Lydians”, says Strabo; some gave the name to Sardis, others to its acropolis. In the section before he places the Gygaean lake “named by the poet” forty stades from Sardis, later renamed Coloe, and says that the Hyllus, now called the Phrygius, flows into the Hermus (13.4.5), the lake and the rivers of Achilles' boast over Iphition (20.390–392; see [[gygaean-lake]] and [[tmolus]]).

In his account of Boeotia Strabo turns the verse against the reading Hyde at 5.708: Hyde is in Lydia, “beneath snowy Tmolus, in the rich land of Hyde”, and Oresbius was a Boeotian (*Geography* 9.2.20; see [[oresbius]] and the Book 5 note).
