**Beside Achilles.** When Priam comes into the hut, Achilles' companions sit apart; “only two, / the hero Automedon and Alcimus, offshoot of Ares, / were busy at his side” (24.473–475; see [[alcimedon]], [[offshoot-of-ares]] and [[hut]]). ποίπνυον, “were busy”, is the verb of Hephaestus bustling through the hall of the gods as their cupbearer (1.600) and of his golden handmaids busy around their lord (18.421). Priam comes in “unseen by them” (24.477).

**The attendants.** When Achilles springs out of the door like a lion, he goes “not alone: with him went two attendants, / the hero Automedon and Alcimus, whom above all / of his companions Achilles honored, after Patroclus who was dead” (24.573–575; see [[achilles]] and [[patroclus]]). θεράπων, “attendant”, is Automedon's title when Hector went after him with his spear, “the godlike attendant of the swift-footed grandson of Aeacus” (16.864–865), and what Hermes, in his false tale, has just called himself (24.396; see [[hermes]]). In Book 16 Patroclus honored Automedon most after Achilles (16.146); now Achilles honors him most after Patroclus. The Odyssey says the same of Antilochus, whom Achilles honored above all the rest after the dead Patroclus (*Odyssey* 24.78–79; see [[antilochus]]). The two who yoked Achilles' horses when he armed (19.392–394) now loose Priam's horses and mules from the yoke, bring in the herald and seat him, and take the ransom from the wagon, leaving two mantles and a tunic for the body (24.576–581; see [[boundless-ransom]] and [[idaeus]]).

**The bread.** At the meal that follows, “Automedon took bread and set it out on the table / in beautiful baskets, while Achilles served the meat” (24.625–626; see [[feast-type-scene]]). The verses are those of the night of the embassy, with Automedon's name in the place of Patroclus' (9.216–217); that night Automedon held the meat while Achilles carved (9.209; see [[the-embassy-to-achilles]]).
