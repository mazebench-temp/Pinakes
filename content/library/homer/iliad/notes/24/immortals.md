summary: The word runs through the gods' part in the book: Apollo speaks “among the immortals” (24.32); Peleus was dear “to the hearts of the immortals” (24.61); Thetis is ashamed “to mingle with the immortals” (24.91); a quarrel “has arisen among the immortals” (24.107); Zeus is angry “above all / the immortals” (24.113–114, 24.134–135); Priam would give “the immortals” their due (24.426); Hermes is “an immortal god” (24.460, 24.464); and the Xanthus is the son of “immortal Zeus” (24.693).
---

**The gods' quarrel.** Hera's answer to Apollo sets Achilles, child of a goddess and of a man “dear above all to the hearts of the immortals,” against Hector, who was a mortal (24.56–61; [[mortal-men]]). Thetis has griefs past measure and will not mix with them (24.90–91).

**Hermes.** At Achilles' gate the god tells Priam who he is, “an immortal god, / Hermes,” and why he will not go in: it would be a thing for blame “for an immortal god to welcome mortals so openly” (24.460–464; [[nemesis]]). θεὸς ἄμβροτος (24.460) is the other word for deathless. See [[olympians]] and [[gods-and-mortals]].
