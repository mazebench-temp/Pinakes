---
summary: Deiphobus is named among the nine sons still living whom Priam rebukes as liars and dancers when he makes ready to ransom Hector (24.251).
---

Δηΐφοβόν τε καὶ Ἱππόθοον καὶ δῖον Ἀγαυόν, “and Deiphobus and Hippothous and brilliant Agauus” (24.251; see [[sons-of-priam]]). Hector called him the dearest “of my brothers, the sons whom Hecuba and Priam bore” when Athena came to him in his likeness (22.233–234; see [[hector]] and [[athena]]); now Priam lumps him with the “disgraces” that are left, while the best are dead (24.253–262; see [[priam]]). He is alive at the end of the poem; the Odyssey puts him at the wooden horse and makes his house the scene of Odysseus' hardest fight on the night Troy fell (*Odyssey* 4.274–276, 8.517–520).
