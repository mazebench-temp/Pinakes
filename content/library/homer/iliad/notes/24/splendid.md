---
summary: Gifts are “splendid” three times: the mules “which the Mysians had once given to Priam, splendid gifts” (24.278); the ransom, which Hermes brings in with Priam (24.447); and the gods' gifts to Peleus (24.534).
---

ἀγλαὰ δῶρα joins three kinds of giving: the guest-gift of a people to a king, the ransom a father brings, and the gods' gifts at Peleus' birth, which Achilles sets against the evil the god laid on him too, one son who will die young (24.534–540; [[the-two-jars-of-zeus]]). See [[doron]] and [[mysians]].
