# ἐπὶ κνέφας ἤλυθε (24.351), the second half of the verse of 1.475 (ἐπὶ κνέφας ἦλθε) in another form: only here.
grc: ["ἐπὶ κνέφας ἤλυθε"]
en: [darkness had now come over the earth]
---

The first is the nightfall of Priam's journey: the travelers have passed the mound of Ilus and stop at the river, and in the dark the herald sees Hermes coming (24.349–353; [[the-tomb-of-ilus]], [[scamander]]). The second measures a day of mourning, πρόπαν ἦμαρ ἐς ἠέλιον καταδύντα, as it measured the gods' feast at the end of Book 1 (1.601) and the day of fighting without food that Odysseus says no man can bear (19.162); the day does not run its course, for Priam speaks (24.713–717; [[would-have-had-not]]).
