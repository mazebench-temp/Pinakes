# ἄφρων οὔτʼ ἄσκοπος (24.157, 24.186): two privatives in a row, with οὔτε; only here.
grc: ["ἄφρων οὔτʼ ἄσκοπος"]
en: [not a fool, nor heedless]
---

οὔτε γάρ ἐστʼ ἄφρων οὔτʼ ἄσκοπος οὔτʼ ἀλιτήμων: the first two words are privatives, “without sense” and “without aim,” and the third, “sinner,” completes the triad. Walter Leaf quotes a scholion that the three adjectives cover the three great causes that lead men into cruelty, stupidity, thoughtlessness and malice, and explains ἄσκοπος as “not aiming,” acting on impulse without thought (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 24.157). The verse passes from Zeus to Priam with Iris' message (24.157 = 24.186; [[message-repeated]]), and Achilles bears it out (24.507–570). See [[achilles]].
