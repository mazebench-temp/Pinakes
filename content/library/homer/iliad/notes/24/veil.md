# κάλυμμα occurs in Homer only here (24.93).
grc: [κάλυμμʼ]
---

Iris finds Thetis among the sea goddesses, weeping for the fate of her son (24.83–86), and Thetis goes to Zeus in mourning: “the brilliant goddess took a veil, / dark blue, and no garment was ever blacker than this” (κάλυμμʼ … κυάνεον, 24.93–94; see [[thetis]] and [[iris]]). Walter Leaf takes the κάλυμμα to be the καλύπτρη, the “head covering” that Hecuba flings away (22.406), and the κρήδεμνον of Hera (14.184), and notes that black as a sign of mourning is found only here in Homer, comparing the *Hymn to Demeter*, where the grieving goddess casts a dark blue κάλυμμα over her shoulders; here κυάνεον is used as the same as “black” (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 24.93). Zeus greets her as one who comes “grieved though you are, / with grief not to be forgotten in your heart” (24.104–105).
