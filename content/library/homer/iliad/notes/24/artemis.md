# Unnamed: "those two", who killed all Niobe's children (24.609).

**Niobe's daughters.** Achilles bids Priam eat, as Niobe ate after her twelve children perished in her halls, “six daughters and six sons in their prime” (24.602–604; see [[the-children-of-niobe]] and [[niobe]]):

{{quote:d1ac835e-75e8-5aa5-bcb6-fb0e215cab54}}

The children are divided as Homer divides the deaths the two archers bring, the sons to Apollo and the daughters to Artemis (see [[apollo]]). So in Book 6 Artemis of the showering arrows struck down Andromache's mother “in her father's halls” (6.428), and in her anger killed Laodameia, the daughter of Bellerophon (6.205; see [[of-the-showering-arrows]]). At 24.606 she is ἰοχέαιρα, “of the showering arrows”, the epithet with which she healed Aeneas beside Leto in Apollo's temple on Pergamus (5.447). Her mother is the cause: Niobe “used to liken herself to fair-cheeked Leto” (24.607; see [[leto]]), and the two children whom Leto bore were enough to destroy them all (24.608–609). At the end of the book Hecuba likens Hector to one whom Apollo has killed with his gentle arrows (24.758–759); in the *Odyssey* the same verse is said of Artemis (*Odyssey* 11.172–173, 11.198–199).
