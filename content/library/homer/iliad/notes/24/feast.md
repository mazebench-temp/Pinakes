---
summary: Zeus' altar “never lacked an equal share of the feast” from Hector (24.69); Achilles, with Niobe as his example, makes Priam eat with him (24.601–628); the book ends with the funeral feast “in the house of Priam” (24.802–803).
---

**The gods' share.** Zeus recalls that Hector never failed in the gifts the gods love: “For my altar never lacked an equal share of the feast” (24.69; see [[reciprocity-with-the-gods]] and [[equal-feast]]).

**Achilles and Priam.** The ransom done, Achilles turns his guest to food: “but now let us remember supper”, for even Niobe “remembered food” after her twelve children were killed (24.601–613; see [[niobe]] and [[fasting]]). He slaughters a silver-white sheep, his companions flay and roast it, Automedon sets out bread, Achilles serves the meat, “But when they had put away the desire for drink and food”, Priam marveled at Achilles and Achilles at Priam (24.621–632; see [[table]], [[desire-for-drink-and-food]] and [[achilles]]). Priam says that only now has he tasted food and wine: “before now I had tasted nothing” (24.641–642).

**The funeral feast.** When the mound has been heaped, the Trojans “gathered together and feasted well on a glorious feast / in the house of Priam, the king nurtured by Zeus” (24.802–803; see [[the-funeral-of-hector]] and [[priams-palace]]), as Achilles gave the Myrmidons a funeral feast for Patroclus (23.29).
