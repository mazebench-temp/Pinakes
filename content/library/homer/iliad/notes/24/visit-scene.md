summary: The book is made of visits. Iris finds Thetis among the sea goddesses (24.83–86); Thetis is received on Olympus with a seat beside Zeus and a golden cup (24.97–102); she finds her son groaning while his companions make ready a meal (24.122–127); Iris finds Priam in the dung of his courtyard (24.160–170); and Priam finds Achilles just done with eating, is offered a chair, refuses to sit until he has his son, and is given food and a bed (24.471–676).

**The finding.** Each visitor finds someone at an occupation, and each occupation is grief: Thetis weeping in her cave for her son, Achilles groaning in his hut, Priam rolling in the dung among his weeping sons (24.83–86, 24.123, 24.160–168). Only Achilles, as Priam finds him, has just ended a meal, with Automedon and Alcimus busy beside him and the table still there (24.472–476).

**The reception.** On Olympus the reception is full: Athena makes way for Thetis beside Zeus, Hera puts a golden cup in her hand and comforts her, and Thetis drinks (24.100–102; [[cup]]). In the hut it is turned around. The guest comes in unseen and clasps his host's knees before he is seen (24.477–479; [[supplication-type-scene]]); the host's amazement is the amazement of a household at a killer who comes as a suppliant (24.480–484); and the host raises the guest by the hand and offers him a chair only after both have wept (24.515, 24.522; [[throne]]).

**The guest who will not sit.** Priam refuses the chair, “Do not yet bid me sit on a chair, nurtured by Zeus, while Hector / lies uncared for in the huts” (24.553–554), as Hector would not sit with Helen and Patroclus with Nestor (6.360, 11.648). Once the body is released, the visit runs its full course: a meal, the host's questions, and a bed for the night, with a warning from the host to sleep outside (24.596–676; [[feast-type-scene]], [[xenia]]).
