---
summary: Priam tells the Trojans that Achilles, “as he sent me from the black ships, gave orders” that the Achaeans would do them no harm until the twelfth dawn (24.780–781).
---

The last ships of the poem are the black ships of the Achaean camp, from which Priam brings home the truce for Hector's funeral (24.777–781; [[the-funeral-of-hector]]). μελαινάων ἀπὸ νηῶν has ended the verse before for the Trojans driven from the ships by Patroclus (16.304) and for Nestor's order to his sons to fight on (17.382–383). The other two μέλαιναν of the book are the night, θοὴν διὰ νύκτα μέλαιναν, and belong to [[black-night]] (24.366, 24.653). See [[ships]].
