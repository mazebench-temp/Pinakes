---
summary: Hermes is “the great helper” four times on the night of the ransom: as he comes near to the frightened Priam (24.360), springs onto the chariot (24.440), opens the gate of Achilles' courtyard (24.457), and lies awake while the others sleep (24.679).
---

The title marks each turn of his help. He takes the old man's hand when Priam's mind is “confounded” with fear (24.358–361); he drives the chariot and breathes might into the horses and mules (24.440–442; [[brave]]); he opens the gate that three Achaeans could hardly move (24.453–457; [[hut]]); and while gods and men sleep he ponders how to send Priam home unseen (24.677–681). In the last two he is named with it, Ἑρμείας ἐριούνιος, Ἑρμείαν ἐριούνιον. See [[hermes]] and [[argeiphontes]].
