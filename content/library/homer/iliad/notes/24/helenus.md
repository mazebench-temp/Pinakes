---
summary: Helenus is the first named of the nine sons still living whom Priam rebukes as liars and dancers when he makes ready to ransom Hector (24.249).
---

νεικείων Ἕλενόν τε Πάριν τʼ Ἀγάθωνά τε δῖον, “rebuking Helenus and Paris and brilliant Agathon” (24.249; see [[sons-of-priam]] and [[priam]]). The seer whom the poem called “by far the best of the bird-seers”, who sent Hector into the city to have the women pray to Athena, and who fought with sword and bow until Menelaus pierced his hand (6.76–101, 13.576–600; see [[best-of-bird-seers]]), is no more than one of the “disgraces” that are left, while the best are dead (24.253–262). Ἕλενον, the accusative, is the form of the Achaean Helenus, son of Oenops, whom Hector kills (5.707; see [[helenus-son-of-oenops]]).
