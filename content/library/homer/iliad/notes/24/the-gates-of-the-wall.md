# ὤϊξε πύλας καὶ ἀπῶσεν ὀχῆας (24.446): a phrase, since πύλας and ὀχῆας are claimed only in phrases.
grc: ["ὤϊξε πύλας καὶ ἀπῶσεν ὀχῆας"]
---

“And the guide, the slayer of Argus, poured sleep over them / all, and at once opened the gates and pushed back the bars, / and brought in Priam and the splendid gifts on the wagon” (24.445–447; see [[hermes]], [[night-watch]] and [[the-achaean-wall]]). The gates are shut and barred for the night. ὀχῆες, “bars”, held the gate that Hector broke, two of them set across each other and fastened with a single bolt (12.455–456). Walter Leaf compares the single long bar of fir that held the door of Achilles' courtyard, which three Achaeans drove home and three drew back (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 24.453; 24.453–456; see [[hut]]). Hector needed a stone that Zeus made light for him to break a gate (12.445–462); Hermes opens one at once and leaves the sleeping sentries none the wiser. The poem does not say which gate it was.
