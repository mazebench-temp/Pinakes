summary: Andromache, foreseeing her son's death, says that very many Achaeans “at the hands of Hector bit the boundless ground with their teeth” (24.737–738); and Achilles tells Priam that since the gods brought the war upon him, “there are always battles around your city, and the killing of men” (24.548).
---

The killing scene has no place in the last book, which has no battle; it is remembered. Andromache's verse ends in the formula of the death agony, ὀδὰξ ἕλον … οὖδας, which Nestor used of the Epeians he killed in his youth and Achilles of the Achaeans who died while he sulked (11.749, 19.61; [[untold]]): Hector's killings are the reason why some Achaean whose brother, father or son he killed will throw his son from the tower (24.734–739; [[astyanax]]). Achilles' μάχαι τʼ ἀνδροκτασίαι τε is the word of the scene itself, in Hector's boast of Book 7 (7.237). See [[hector]].
