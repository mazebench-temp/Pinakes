summary: The word is said twice in similes and once in a lie: the eagle's wings are as wide as the door of the chamber “of a wealthy man” (24.317–318); Priam's coming is like that of a killer who comes as an exile “to the house of a wealthy man” (24.480–482); and Hermes says that his father Polyctor “is wealthy, and an old man, just as you are” (24.398).
# ἀφνειοῖο (24.318), only here in the Iliad.
grc: [ἀφνειοῖο]
---

The two similes take their pictures from the rich man's house, its high-roofed chamber with its bolted door and the hall where the stranger arrives and is stared at (24.315–321, 24.480–484; [[high-roofed]]). Hermes' invented father is made to match Priam, rich and old, with many sons (24.397–400; [[polyctor]], [[truth-and-deception]]). See [[priam]].
