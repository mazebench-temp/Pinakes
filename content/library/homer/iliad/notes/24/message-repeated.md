summary: Zeus' charge to Thetis is delivered by her with the person changed, but with her own counsel before it and the ransom added at the end (24.112–116, 24.133–137); Zeus' message to Priam is delivered by Iris almost verse for verse (24.146–158, 24.175–187); and Priam repeats its first lines to Hecuba (24.195–196).

**Thetis.** Zeus says, “tell him the gods are angry with him, and that I above all / the immortals am angry, because in his raging heart / he keeps Hector beside the curved ships and has not released him” (24.113–115), and Thetis says to Achilles, “He says the gods are angry with you, and that he above all / the immortals is angry, because in your raging heart / you keep Hector beside the curved ships and have not released him” (24.134–136; [[curved-ships]]). What she adds frames the message: before it, a mother's counsel to eat, to sleep with a woman, and to remember that his death is near (24.128–132); after it, in place of Zeus' hope that Achilles may fear him (24.116), a plain command, “But come, release him, and accept a ransom for the body” (24.137; [[apoina]]).

**Iris.** Zeus' message to Priam (24.146–158) comes to him in the same verses with the person changed: “to ransom his dear son” becomes “The Olympian bids you ransom brilliant Hector” (24.146, 24.175), and “Let a herald go with him” becomes “Let a herald go with you” (24.149, 24.178). Iris' frame is her own: “Take heart in your mind, Priam,” and a verse of the Dream's in Book 2, that Zeus, “though he is far away, cares greatly for you and pities you” (24.171–174; 2.27; [[pity]]). The Dream's message was false; hers is true.

**Priam.** Priam gives Hecuba the first lines as he heard them, “to ransom my dear son, going to the ships of the Achaeans, / and bring gifts to Achilles that may warm his heart” (24.195–196 = 24.118–119), and leaves out the rest, the herald, the escort and the safety Zeus promised; Hecuba's fear answers what she has not heard (24.200–216).
