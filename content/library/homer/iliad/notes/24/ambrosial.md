# ἀμβρόσια (24.341), of the sandals of the gods: only here in the Iliad.
grc: [ἀμβρόσια]
---

The sandals are the gods' own. The three verses that describe them, “ambrosial, golden, which carried him over the waters / and over the boundless earth as fast as the blasts of the wind” (24.341–342), also send Athena and Hermes on their errands in the Odyssey (*Odyssey* 1.96–98, 5.44–46; [[dressing-scene]], [[sandals]], [[boundless-sea]]). The night is the night of Book 10, where νύκτα διʼ ἀμβροσίην ends a verse in the speeches of Menelaus and of Odysseus (10.41, 10.142); Hermes' question closes with the verse that Nestor put to Agamemnon that night ([[when-other-mortals-are-asleep]]).
