**Twelve days.** The verse that ended the twelve days of the gods' absence in Book 1, “But when the twelfth dawn after that had come” (1.493 = 24.31), ends the twelve days of the dragging and brings Apollo's protest. Hermes counts the same days for Priam (24.413), and Priam asks for eleven more, to be ended by the twelfth dawn of the truce (24.664–667, 24.781). Between them, Achilles' sleepless nights end when “the dawn … appeared over the sea and the shores” (24.12–13), and the dawns of the dragging are “brilliant Dawn” (24.417; [[brilliant]]).

**The day of the return.** Achilles promises the body for the morning, ἅμα δʼ ἠοῖ φαινομένηφιν, the phrase with which he told the envoys in Book 9 that at dawn he and Phoenix would decide whether to go home (9.618; 24.600). The verse of Book 8 brings that morning, as Priam reaches the ford (8.1 = 24.695; [[saffron-robed]]).

**The funeral.** The tenth dawn of the truce comes with the epithet φαεσίμβροτος, “bringing light to mortals,” found only here in the Iliad, in the frame of the tenth-day verse of Book 6 (6.175; 24.785; [[bringing-light-to-mortals]]); the eleventh with the whole dawn line, which the Iliad uses only here and at 1.477 (24.788; [[early-born]], [[rosy-fingered]]).
