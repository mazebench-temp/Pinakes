summary: Purple honors the guest and the dead. Achilles has purple blankets thrown on the beds of Priam and his herald (24.644–645), and Hector's bones are laid in a golden chest covered with soft purple robes (24.795–796).

# accents; πορφυρέοις (24.796) is first met here. The English "purple" of 24.645 stands at the end of 24.644.

**For the guest.** Achilles bids his companions and the slave women “set beds beneath the colonnade, and throw fine purple / blankets on them, and spread rugs over them” (24.644–645; see [[bed]] and [[hut]]). Purple coverings are the honor a house gives its guests, as on the night of the embassy, when Achilles seated the envoys “on chairs with purple rugs” (9.200; see the note on Book 9), and in the Odyssey the same verses make the beds for Telemachus in Sparta and for Odysseus among the Phaeacians (*Odyssey* 4.297–299, 7.336–338; see [[xenia]]).

**For the dead.** At the end of the funeral Hector's brothers and companions put his bones “in a golden chest, / covering them with soft purple robes” (24.795–796; see [[robe]], [[larnax]] and [[the-funeral-of-hector]]). Walter Leaf notes that the short dative πορφυρέοις cannot be removed without an equally un-Homeric shortening in πέπλοισι, and reports Studniczka's view that the covering was spread over the chest rather than wrapped round the bones (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 24.796; see the translation's note on 24.796). Patroclus' body had been covered on its bier with a white cloak (18.352–353), and his bones with a soft linen cloth (23.254); the purple is Hector's.
