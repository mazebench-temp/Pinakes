summary: Poseidon is named once, as one of the three gods who will not have Hector's body stolen. The narrator joins him with Hera and Athena in the hatred of Troy that began with the Judgment of Paris, though Poseidon had a grievance of his own against the Trojans.
# Unnamed: "they" who held to their hatred of Ilios (24.27).
refs: [24.27]
---

**Never Poseidon.** When the gods would have Hermes steal Hector's body, “This pleased all the others, but never Hera, / nor Poseidon, nor the gray-eyed maiden; / but they held to it, as when sacred Ilios first became hateful to them” (24.25–27; see [[the-gods-debate]], [[hera]] and [[athena]]). The reason the narrator gives is “Alexander's blind folly”, his insult to the goddesses in his farmyard (24.28–30; see [[the-judgment-of-paris]]), and Poseidon was no part of that. His own grievance is older: he and Apollo served Laomedon for a year, he building the wall of Troy and Apollo herding the cattle, and Laomedon robbed them of their wage and sent them away with threats (21.441–457; see [[poseidon-and-apollo-serve-laomedon]] and [[laomedon]]). He goes with Hera and Athena to the Achaean side in the battle of the gods (20.33–34; see [[the-battle-of-the-gods]]), yet he saved Aeneas from Achilles in Book 20, so that the race of Dardanus should not perish (20.293–308; see [[aeneas]]). In the last book he does nothing.
