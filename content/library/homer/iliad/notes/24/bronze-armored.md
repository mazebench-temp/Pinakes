---
summary: Priam tells Hecuba that if it is his fate “to die beside the ships of the bronze-armored Achaeans,” he would have it so (24.225–226).
---

The verse-end Ἀχαιῶν χαλκοχιτώνων, the commonest form of the epithet, names the Achaean army here for the last time in the poem: Priam would die there, if Achilles kills him once he has held his son (24.224–227). The armored Achaeans he fears are asleep when he comes, put to sleep by Hermes (24.443–447). See [[achaeans]] and [[priam]].
