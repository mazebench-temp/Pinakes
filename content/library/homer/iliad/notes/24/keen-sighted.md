---
summary: The gods, pitying Hector, keep urging “the keen-sighted slayer of Argus” to steal the body (24.24), and Zeus tells Thetis of it (24.109).
---

Both verses end in ἐΰσκοπον ἀργεϊφόντην, and both are disputed. Aristarchus athetized 24.25–30, and Walter Leaf takes a scholion on 24.109, which calls that verse the source of the interpolation, to show that he rejected 24.24 as well (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 24.23 and 24.109; [[the-judgment-of-paris]]). Zeus will not have the body stolen. Thetis is beside her son night and day, so he sends for her instead (24.71–76; [[argeiphontes]], [[hermes]]).
