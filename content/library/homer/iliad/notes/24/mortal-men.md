summary: Hera says that “Hector is a mortal” (24.58); Priam says that Hector “did not seem / the son of a mortal man, but of a god” (24.258–259); and Achilles tells how the gods gave Peleus, “a mortal,” a goddess as his wife (24.537).
# θνητοῦ (24.259), only here in the Iliad.
grc: [θνητοῦ]
---

Each use sets a mortal against a god. Hera makes the difference between Hector and Achilles one of birth, a mortal suckled at a woman's breast against the son of a goddess (24.56–61). Priam's praise of Hector denies it (24.258–259; [[godlike]]). Achilles' account of his father's marriage makes it the source of Peleus' one sorrow: a mortal with a goddess for his wife, and one son, doomed to an untimely death (24.534–540; [[peleus]]). See [[immortals]] and [[gods-and-mortals]].
