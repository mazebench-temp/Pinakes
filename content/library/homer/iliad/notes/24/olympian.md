---
summary: Achilles will release the body “if the Olympian himself so bids with a ready heart” (24.140); Iris tells Priam that “The Olympian bids you ransom brilliant Hector” (24.175); and Priam tells Hecuba that “an Olympian messenger has come to me from Zeus” (24.194), the only time the epithet is given to Iris.
---

The first two uses are the name of Zeus, “the Olympian,” as the source of the command, once in Achilles' consent and once in Iris' delivery (24.139–140, 24.175). The third is an epithet: Ὀλύμπιος ἄγγελος, “an Olympian messenger,” Priam's word for Iris as he tells his wife why he will go (24.193–196; [[messengers-of-zeus]]). See [[zeus]] and [[iris]].
