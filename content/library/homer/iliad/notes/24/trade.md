---
summary: Hecuba remembers that Achilles “would sell, whomever he took, across the barren sea, / to Samos and to Imbros and to misty Lemnos” the other sons of Priam (24.751–753).
grc: [πέρνασχʼ]
en: [would sell]
---

In her lament Hecuba sets Hector's death against the fate of her other sons: “For my other sons swift-footed Achilles / would sell, whomever he took, across the barren sea, / to Samos and to Imbros and to misty Lemnos” (πέρνασχʼ, 24.751–753; see [[hecuba]], [[sons-of-priam]], [[samothrace]], [[imbros]] and [[lemnos]]). πέρνασκε, the iterative of πέρνημι, is said only here in the Iliad; it gathers what the poem has told of single sales, Lycaon sold into Lemnos and Priam's “selling them off to islands far away” (21.40–41, 22.45; see [[lycaon-son-of-priam]]). Hector, killed, was dragged around a tomb instead (24.754–756; see [[the-dragging-of-hector]]).
