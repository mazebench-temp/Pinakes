summary: Zeus' will goes out by three errands. Iris goes down to the sea and brings Thetis to Olympus (24.74–99); Thetis carries Zeus' word to her son, who consents (24.112–140); and Iris carries Zeus' word to Priam, who tells it to Hecuba (24.143–196).

**The summons.** Zeus asks that one of the gods call Thetis to him, and Iris rises at once, “to carry the message” (24.74–77; [[storm-footed]]). She finds Thetis in her cave among the sea goddesses, weeping, and her message is one verse: “Rise, Thetis; Zeus calls you, whose counsels are imperishable” (24.83–88; [[imperishable]]). Thetis answers that she will go, and the messenger leads her up (24.89–99; [[wind-footed]]).

**Thetis to Achilles.** Zeus gives Thetis her charge in his own words, and she goes down from Olympus to her son's hut, finds him groaning, and delivers it, “I am a messenger to you from Zeus” (24.112–137; [[messengers-of-zeus]], [[visit-scene]]). Achilles' answer is the consent the errand was for: “So be it” (24.139–140).

**Iris to Priam.** “Go, swift Iris” opens Zeus' command (24.144; [[swift-iris]]); Iris finds Priam in the courtyard, sitting in the dung among his mourning sons, stands beside him and speaks softly (24.159–170). She delivers the message in Zeus' words and goes (24.171–188; [[message-repeated]]). The message goes on: Priam tells his wife that “an Olympian messenger has come to me from Zeus” (24.194; [[olympian]]), and Achilles tells Priam that “a messenger came to me from Zeus, / my mother” (24.561–562). See [[iris]], [[thetis]] and [[zeus]].
