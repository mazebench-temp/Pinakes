---
summary: Iris summons Thetis: “Rise, Thetis; Zeus calls you, whose counsels are imperishable” (24.88).
---

ἄφθιτα μήδεα εἰδώς is found only here in Homer. Walter Leaf notes that Hesiod has it three times and the Hymn to Aphrodite once (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 24.88): in the *Theogony* it is Zeus' formula in the story of Prometheus (*Theogony* 545, 550, 561), and in the *Hymn to Aphrodite* it names the father of the gods (43). Leaf also compares the *Hymn to Demeter*, where Iris summons another grieving goddess in nearly the same words. There Zeus sends Iris to call Demeter, dark-robed, from her temple at Eleusis, and Iris says, “Demeter, father Zeus calls you, whose counsels are imperishable,” to come among the gods (*Hymn to Demeter* 314–323). Thetis, in her dark veil, obeys (24.93–94; [[brilliant-among-goddesses]]). See [[zeus]] and [[iris]].
