summary: Thetis and Iris find “the far-seeing son of Cronus” among the assembled gods (24.98); Hecuba doubts that “far-seeing Zeus” will send his messenger (24.296); and Priam and the herald, coming out on the plain, do not escape “the notice of far-seeing Zeus” (24.331).
---

The disputed epithet is set in this book beside seeing. Hecuba asks for a sign Priam may see “with your eyes” (24.294), and Zeus sends it; then, as Priam comes down into the plain, Zeus sees him, “and seeing the old man he pitied him” (24.331–332), and sends Hermes. Hecuba had named him to Priam as the god “who looks down on all of Troy” (24.291). See [[zeus]] and [[pity]].
