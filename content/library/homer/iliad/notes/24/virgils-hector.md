**The picture.** Among the paintings of the war on Juno's temple at Carthage, Aeneas sees that “three times around the walls of Ilium Achilles had dragged Hector, and was selling the lifeless body for gold”, and groans as he sees “the spoils, the chariot, the very body of his friend, and Priam stretching out unarmed hands” (*Aeneid* 1.483–487). The scene is gathered from three books. The three circuits of the walls belong to the chase of Book 22, when the two men “whirled three times around the city of Priam” (22.165), and the dragging behind the chariot begins in that book (22.395–404). The three circuits of the dragging belong to this one: at each dawn Achilles drags Hector “three times around the mound of the dead son of Menoetius” (24.16; see [[the-dragging-of-hector]]).

**The gold.** Priam brings gold among the ransom: from his chests he takes robes, cloaks, rugs, mantles and tunics, two tripods, four cauldrons and a cup, and “he weighed out and brought ten talents of gold in all” (24.228–237; see [[talent]]). In Book 22 Achilles had sworn that he would not give up the body “not even if he should bid them weigh out your own body in gold” (22.351). Virgil's *vendebat*, “was selling”, makes the ransom a sale, as Plato's “price” did (see [[plato-the-price-of-hectors-body]]). In Homer Achilles gives the body back at the bidding of Zeus, and it is Priam who presses the ransom on him: “and accept the ransom, / the many things that we bring you” (24.555–556; see [[the-ransom-of-hector]]).

**The hands.** Virgil's Priam stretches out his hands, unarmed, as a suppliant. Homer's Priam comes into the hut unseen, clasps the knees of Achilles “and kissed his hands, / the terrible, man-slaying hands that had killed many of his sons” (24.478–479), and ends his plea with what he has endured, “to put to my lips the hand of the man who killed my sons” (24.506; see [[supplication]] and [[priam]]). In the second book of the *Aeneid* the dying Priam remembers that Achilles respected the rights of the suppliant (see [[virgils-priam]]).
