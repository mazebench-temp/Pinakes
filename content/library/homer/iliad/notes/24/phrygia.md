---
summary: Achilles names “Phrygia inland” among the bounds of the land over which Priam was once first in wealth and in sons, with Lesbos out to sea and the Hellespont (24.543–546).
---

καθύπερθε, “inland”, literally “higher up”: Walter Leaf explains that Phrygia was part of the high tableland of central Asia Minor, and that to a Greek on the coast a journey either inland or out to sea was “up”, so that ἄνω, of Lesbos, and καθύπερθε, of Phrygia, only seem to contradict each other (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 24.544; see [[lesbos]], [[hellespont]] and the translation's note on 24.544). Strabo quotes the verses to show that Priam ruled the whole coast of the Troad (*Geography* 13.1.7).

Phrygia marks the inland edge of Priam's old wealth, as it marked its loss in Hector's words: once all men spoke of the city of Priam “as rich in gold, rich in bronze”, but now its treasures “have gone to Phrygia and lovely Maeonia, / possessions sold away” to pay for the war (18.288–292; see [[priam]] and [[possessions]]). Priam himself had gone to “vine-rich Phrygia” in his youth, to fight as an ally of the Phrygians on the Sangarius (3.184–189; see [[priam-and-the-amazons]]).
