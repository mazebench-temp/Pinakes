---
summary: Zeus defends Hector with the verses he once spoke of Troy: “For my altar never lacked an equal share of the feast, / libation and the savor of the burnt fat; for that is the prize allotted to us” (24.69–70 = 4.48–49).
---

In Book 4 Zeus said these verses to Hera of the whole city, “sacred Ilios was the most honored in my heart, / and Priam, and the people of Priam” (4.46–47), when she asked him for its destruction; in the last book he says them to Hera again of one man, the Hector whom she would not have honored like Achilles (24.56–70; Walter Leaf, *Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 24.68; see [[hera]], [[time]] and [[doron]]). The γέρας of the gods is their portion of the sacrifice, the libation and the savor, and Priam sees in Hector's gifts the reason for the gods' care of his body (24.422–428; see [[reciprocity-with-the-gods]]).

The dead have a prize of their own, “a tomb and a pillar; for that is the prize of the dead”, as Hera said of Sarpedon, whose brothers and kinsmen would bury him in Lycia (16.456–457). The last book gives it to Hector: his brothers and companions gather the bones, and the Trojans lay them in a trench and heap the mound (24.793–801; see [[tomb]], [[honoring-the-dead]] and [[the-funeral-of-hector]]).
