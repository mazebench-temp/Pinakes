summary: Polites, “good at the war cry”, is named among the nine sons still living whom Priam rebukes as liars and dancers when he makes ready to ransom Hector (24.250).
# Πολίτην only here; kept to the Iliad, since πολίτην is "citizen" in Aristotle (Rhetoric 1398b13).
grc: [Πολίτην]
---

Πάμμονά τʼ Ἀντίφονόν τε βοὴν ἀγαθόν τε Πολίτην, “and Pammon and Antiphonus and Polites, good at the war cry” (24.250; see [[sons-of-priam]], [[pammon]] and [[antiphonus]]). βοὴν ἀγαθός, “good at the war cry”, is the standing epithet of Menelaus and Diomedes (see [[good-at-the-war-cry]]); of the nine sons only Polites is given one besides the “brilliant” of Agathon and Agauus. He was the lookout whose voice Iris took in Book 2, and who led his wounded brother Deiphobus out of the battle in Book 13 (2.791–795, 13.533–539; see [[deiphobus]]).
