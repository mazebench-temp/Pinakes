---
summary: “And now all day long, until the sun went down, / they would have mourned Hector before the gates, shedding tears, / had not the old man spoken among the people from the chariot” (24.713–715).
---

The poem's last turn of the kind is a mortal's, as most of those in the games of Book 23 were. Its closest model is the first of that book, where the sun would have set on the Myrmidons weeping for Patroclus had not Achilles spoken to Agamemnon (23.154–155). Here the mourners are the whole city at the gates, and Priam asks them to make way for the wagon: they will have their fill of weeping when he has brought Hector home (24.716–718; [[sunset-lines]], [[lament]]).
