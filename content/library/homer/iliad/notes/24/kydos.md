---
summary: Zeus settles the gods' quarrel over the body by granting Achilles a κῦδος, the ransom in place of a theft: “but I bestow this glory on Achilles, / keeping your respect and your friendship for the time to come” (24.110–111). Priam prays to Zeus as “most glorious, greatest” (24.308), and the poem's last feast, in Priam's house, is “a glorious feast” (24.802).
---

**The glory of the ransom.** The gods have urged Hermes to steal the body (24.109), and Zeus sets against the theft τόδε κῦδος, “this glory” (24.110): Achilles is to give the body back for gifts (24.116–119; see [[apoina]]). Walter Leaf explains it as a solace to his honor, the receipt of gifts in return for the body instead of having to yield it “unbought, unransomed”, since the whole of Book 9 shows that the heroic point of honor lay in receiving a return; he points to Phoenix's argument, that he would not bid Achilles give up his wrath if Agamemnon were not bringing gifts, and to Meleager, to whom the Aetolians no longer paid the gifts when he had yielded to his own heart (9.515–519, 9.598; *Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 24.110; see [[the-wrath-of-meleager]] and [[time]]). κῦδος προτιάπτω, “bestow glory”, is found only here in Homer, though Leaf notes that κῦδος προσάπτειν is a common phrase in later Greek; Athena “set glory on” Diomedes in the chariot race (ἐπὶ κῦδος ἔθηκε, 23.400; on 24.110). The glory is given for Thetis' sake, keeping her respect and friendship (24.111; see [[aidos]] and [[philotes]]).

**Most glorious.** Priam's prayer opens with the verse of the prayers at the oath and the duels of Books 3 and 7, κύδιστε μέγιστε, “most glorious, greatest” (24.308; 3.276, 3.320, 7.202; see [[most-glorious]] and [[prayer]]). Hermes, in his false tale, has watched Hector many times in “the battle where men win glory” (24.391; see [[where-men-win-glory]]).

**The last feast.** ἐρικυδέα δαῖτα, “a glorious feast” (24.802), is an Odyssean phrase, found four times in the Odyssey and in the Iliad only here (Leaf on 24.802; see [[glorious]] and [[feast]]). The funeral of Hector ends with it, in the house of Priam (24.801–804; see [[the-funeral-of-hector]]).
