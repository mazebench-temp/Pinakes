---
summary: “then Phoebus Apollo spoke among the immortals” (24.32): Apollo begins the protest that brings Hector's body back.
---

The formula Φοῖβος Ἀπόλλων ends the verse for the last time in the poem as Apollo, on the twelfth day of the dragging, rebukes the gods for letting Achilles defile the body (24.31–54). Elsewhere in the book he is Apollo alone, or “god of the silver bow” (24.18, 24.56, 24.605, 24.758; [[god-of-the-silver-bow]]). See [[apollo]].
