---
summary: Thetis and Iris find “the far-seeing son of Cronus” (24.98); “the son of Cronus urged Iris on to sacred Ilios” (24.143); Priam complains that “Zeus, son of Cronus, has given me sorrows” (24.241); Hecuba bids him pray “to the son of Cronus, lord of the dark clouds” (24.290); and in Niobe's story “the son of Cronus had turned the people to stones” (24.611).
---

The patronymic follows Zeus through the book's plan: he receives Thetis, sends Iris, is blamed by Priam for his loss, answers Priam's prayer, and in Achilles' story turns a people to stone so that the dead lie unburied nine days (24.98–119, 24.143–158, 24.241–242, 24.290–321, 24.610–611; [[far-seeing]], [[lord-of-dark-clouds]]).
