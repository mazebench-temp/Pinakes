# ἀγανοῖς ἐπέεσσι (24.772, without the final ν of the entry's form) and ἀγανοφροσύνῃ (24.772): only here in the Iliad.
grc: ["ἀγανοῖς ἐπέεσσι", ἀγανοφροσύνῃ]
en: [gentleness, gentle words]
---

The last speech of the Iliad but one ends on Hector's gentleness: no other in wide Troy is kind to Helen now (24.767–775; [[helen]], [[in-laws]]). The adjective comes thirteen verses earlier in Hecuba's lament, where Apollo's “gentle arrows” bring a death without suffering (24.759; [[fresh-as-dew]]). In the Odyssey Odysseus' mother says that she died not by Artemis' gentle arrows but of longing for her son and for his ἀγανοφροσύνη (*Odyssey* 11.197–203). See [[hector]].
