# Unnamed: "the goddess had borne two", and "those two" (24.608-609).
refs: [24.608-609]
---

**Niobe's boast.** In the story Achilles tells Priam, Apollo and Artemis kill the twelve children of Niobe, “because she used to liken herself to fair-cheeked Leto; / she said that the goddess had borne two, while she herself had borne many; / and so those two, though they were only two, destroyed them all” (24.607–609; see [[the-children-of-niobe]], [[niobe]], [[apollo]] and [[artemis]]). ἰσάσκετο, “used to liken herself”, is found only here, a frequentative verb (Walter Leaf, *Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 24.607): the comparison was a habit, not a single boast. The insult is to Leto, and the vengeance is her children's. Leto is “fair-cheeked” (καλλιπάρῃος), as Themis is when she gives Hera her cup (15.87) and Briseis when she lies beside Achilles at the end of this book (24.676; see [[fair-cheeked]]). Leaf reports a legend that Leto and Niobe had once been close friends, Niobe, like her father Tantalus, being admitted to the company of the immortals, and quotes a verse of Sappho, “Leto and Niobe were very dear companions” (fragment 31 Bergk; on 24.607).
