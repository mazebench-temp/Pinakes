---
summary: Thetis asks her son how long, mourning and grieving, “will you eat out your heart, remembering neither food / nor bed?” (24.129–130), and Achilles has Hector's body washed out of Priam's sight, “lest in the grief of his heart he should not hold back his anger” (24.584).
---

σὴν ἔδεαι κραδίην, “will you eat out your heart” (24.129), sets the heart that grief consumes against the food that Achilles will not eat (see [[fasting]] and [[achos]]). Walter Leaf compares the Odyssey's θυμὸν ἔδων, said of men worn out by hardship and of Odysseus refusing food in Circe's house (*Odyssey* 9.75, 10.143, 10.379), and Bellerophon wandering alone “eating his heart out” (6.202; *Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 24.129; see [[bellerophon]]); he also reports a scholium that cites Pythagoras' rule not to eat the heart.

In the second line the heart is the seat of grief and anger together, ἀχνυμένῃ κραδίῃ χόλον (24.584): Achilles fears what the father's heart might do at the sight of his son, and what his own might do in answer (24.583–586; see [[cholos]] and [[etor]]).
