summary: Hermes, in the likeness of a young Myrmidon, asks Priam “But come, tell me this, and give me an exact account” whether he is sending his treasures away or the Trojans are leaving Ilios (24.380–385); and Achilles uses the same verse to ask Priam how many days he needs for Hector's funeral (24.656–658).
---

The entry tells the formula's use in the Iliad, always at night and across the lines of the war. The last book has two of the four, and one more request of the same kind: Priam, having asked the stranger who he is, asks him in his turn, “come, tell me the whole truth” (πᾶσαν ἀληθείην κατάλεξον), whether Hector still lies by the ships (24.406–409). Hermes' answer to Priam's questions is a false story (24.396–404; [[truth-and-deception]]); Achilles' question leads to the truce (24.656–670). See [[hermes]] and [[achilles]].
