---
summary: Hermes, in the likeness of a Myrmidon, tells Priam that at dawn “the bright-eyed Achaeans will set battle in array around the city” (24.402).
---

The formula ends a verse of Hermes' false tale, which explains why a young follower of Achilles is out on the plain at night: the army is restless at its idleness and the kings cannot hold it back (24.399–404; [[truth-and-deception]]). The battle he announces does not come; Achilles grants a truce of eleven days for Hector's funeral (24.656–670). See [[achaeans]] and [[hermes]].
