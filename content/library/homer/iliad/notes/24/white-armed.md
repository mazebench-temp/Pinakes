---
summary: “Then in anger white-armed Hera said to him” (24.55), answering Apollo; and “white-armed Andromache led the lamentation” over Hector (24.723).
---

The epithet joins the goddess who opposes the return of the body to the wife who receives it. Hera's verse brings in her answer that Hector and Achilles cannot have equal honor (24.55–63); Andromache's opens the first of the three laments in Priam's house, as she holds Hector's head (24.723–724; [[lament]]). Ἀνδρομάχη λευκώλενος was Hector's question in Book 6, where had white-armed Andromache gone (6.377). See [[hera]] and [[andromache]].
