summary: “The guide, the slayer of Argus” six times on Priam's journey: as Hermes obeys Zeus (24.339), in the four verses that bring in his answers to Priam (24.378, 24.389, 24.410, 24.432), and as he pours sleep over the sentries (24.445).
---

The title has its plainest sense in this book. Zeus bids Hermes “go, and lead Priam to the hollow ships of the Achaeans” unseen (24.336–338), and the god leads him there, in the likeness of a young Myrmidon, and in the morning leads him back (24.440–447, 24.689–694). The formula with [[argeiphontes]] ends the verse each time; after 24.445 Hermes is “the great helper” ([[great-helper]]). See [[hermes]].
