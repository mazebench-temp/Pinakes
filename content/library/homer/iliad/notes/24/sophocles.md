---
summary: Sophocles' Antigone and Electra both turn to Niobe, the Phrygian stranger wasting under rain and snow on the heights of Sipylus, and the all-enduring mother who weeps forever in her rocky tomb. Electra answers with her a consolation like Achilles' to Priam.
---

- Antigone's Niobe on Sipylus, “whom the growth of rock, like clinging ivy, subdued” (*Antigone* 823–833), and Electra's, “you who in your rocky tomb weep forever”, set against the chorus' counsel that laments will not raise the dead (*Electra* 137–152, on 24.549–551 and 24.614–617): [[the-weeping-rock-of-sipylus]].
- Walter Leaf compares the κάπετος, the trench of Hector's grave, with the hollow trench dug by hand in the *Ajax* (on 24.795), and sets the Lemnos of the *Philoctetes*, untrodden and uninhabited, which is quoted for the sense “inhospitable”, against Homer's well-built island (on 24.753): [[larnax]], [[tomb]] and [[lemnos]].
