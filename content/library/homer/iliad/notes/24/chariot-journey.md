summary: Priam's night journey is told in the verses of the chariot: the mules are yoked to the wagon and the horses to his chariot (24.265–281), he mounts and drives out with the whip (24.322–327), they halt at the river (24.349–351), Hermes takes the whip and reins (24.440–442), Achilles' companions unyoke (24.576), and before dawn Hermes yokes the teams again and drives them through the camp (24.690–691).

**Yoking.** The yoking of the mule wagon is told verse by verse, from the yoke taken down from its peg to the band tied fast (24.266–274; see the translation's note on 24.268–274; [[wagon]], [[mules]]); for Priam they lead the horses “under the yoke” (24.279). Achilles' own yoking, each morning of the dragging, is told in one verse (24.14; [[swift-footed]]).

**The drive.** Priam mounts, drives out of the doorway and the colonnade (24.322–323; [[loud-resounding]]), and urges the horses “with the whip” through the city, with the herald driving the mules ahead (24.324–327). At the river they halt to water the teams, as darkness falls (24.350–351; [[sunset-lines]]). Hermes takes over the driver's part, “and breathed noble might into the horses and the mules” (24.440–442; [[brave]]), and the unyoking at Achilles' hut is done by his companions (24.576).

**The return.** Hermes yokes the horses and mules and drives through the camp, and no one knows them; at the ford he leaves them, and they drive to the city, the mules carrying the corpse (24.690–697; [[eddying]]). See [[chariot]], [[horses]] and [[hermes]].
