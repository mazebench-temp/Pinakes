---
summary: Cassandra, “like golden Aphrodite,” is the first in Troy to see Priam bringing Hector home (24.699–700).
---

ἰκέλη χρυσῇ Ἀφροδίτῃ was Briseis' half-verse when she saw the body of Patroclus and threw herself on it (19.282). In both places the comparison with the goddess of beauty comes at the moment a woman sees the dead and cries out: Cassandra, from Pergamus, wails aloud and calls the city to come and see Hector (24.699–706; [[cassandra]], [[lament]]). See [[aphrodite]].
