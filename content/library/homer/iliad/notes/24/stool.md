summary: While Achilles' men take the ransom from the wagon, they lead in the herald Idaeus and seat him on a chair (24.577–578); Priam is offered a chair of honor and refuses it while Hector lies uncared for (24.522, 24.553).
# δίφρου (24.578) is here a seat, not the car of a chariot (chariot excepts the line).
grc: ["ἐπὶ δίφρου εἷσαν"]
---

Achilles springs out of the house with Automedon and Alcimus, who loose the horses and mules, “and led in the herald, the old man's crier, / and sat him on a chair” (24.577–578; see [[idaeus]] and [[wagon]]). δίφρος is the same word as the car of the chariot in which Priam drove to the ships (24.322; see [[chariot]]), and here it is the plain seat that a house offers a visitor, as Aphrodite set one for Helen in Paris' chamber and Helen offered one to Hector (3.424–425, 6.354; see the note on Book 6).

The king is offered more. Achilles raises Priam from the ground and bids him “sit down on a chair”, a θρόνος, and Priam refuses: “Do not yet bid me sit on a chair, nurtured by Zeus, while Hector / lies uncared for in the huts” (24.515–522, 24.553–554; see [[throne]] and [[priam]]); Achilles himself goes back to his intricately worked κλισμός by the other wall (24.597–598; see [[hut]]).
