---
summary: Priam twice calls Achilles “nurtured by Zeus”: when he refuses the chair (24.553) and when he asks for a bed (24.635); and the funeral feast is held “in the house of Priam, the king nurtured by Zeus” (24.803).
---

διοτρεφές, the vocative of address to a king, is Priam's courtesy to the man who killed his son, at both of his requests (24.553–558, 24.635–642). The last verse but one of the poem gives the epithet back to Priam himself, as the king in whose house the feast is held (24.802–803; [[glorious]], [[the-funeral-of-hector]]). See [[basileus]].
