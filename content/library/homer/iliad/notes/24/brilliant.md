---
summary: “Brilliant” is Hector seven times: as Achilles defiles him, in Apollo's protest, in Iris' message and Hermes' answer, and as Achilles releases him and asks how long his funeral will be (24.22, 24.50, 24.175, 24.390, 24.593, 24.657, 24.660); it is Achilles five times (24.151, 24.180, 24.513, 24.596, 24.668); and it is Thetis, two of Priam's sons, Dawn and the earth once each, and once Priam himself, whom Achilles calls “brilliant old sir” (24.618).
---

**Hector.** The verse of the defilement ends with the epithet, “So in his rage he defiled brilliant Hector” (24.22; 22.395), and Apollo uses it in his protest, “when he has robbed brilliant Hector of his dear life” (24.50). Zeus' message, through Iris, bids Priam “ransom brilliant Hector” (24.175), and Hermes answers him, “You are testing me, old sir, in asking about brilliant Hector” (24.390). Achilles tells the dead Patroclus that “I have released brilliant Hector / to his dear father” (24.593–594) and asks Priam “for how many days are you minded to give brilliant Hector his funeral rites” (24.657); Priam answers with the same word (24.660; [[the-funeral-of-hector]]).

**Achilles.** In Zeus' message and in Iris' delivery Hector is “the man whom brilliant Achilles killed” (24.151, 24.180; [[message-repeated]]). The epithet comes back as Achilles' grief ends, “But when brilliant Achilles had taken his fill of lament” (24.513), as he goes back into the hut after laying the body on the wagon (24.596), and with [[podarkes]] as he grants the truce (24.668).

**Others.** Achilles' address to Priam, δῖε γεραιέ, “brilliant old sir” (24.618), is found only here. Thetis is the brilliant goddess as she takes her dark veil (24.93; [[brilliant-among-goddesses]]). Among the nine sons Priam rebukes are “brilliant Agathon” and “brilliant Agauus” (24.249, 24.251); Walter Leaf reports that the scholia could not say whether Δῖος or Ἀγαυός is the name in the second, and that Pherecydes knew a son of Priam called Dios (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 24.250; [[agauus]]). Dawn is “brilliant” as Achilles drags the body around the mound at daybreak (24.417; [[dawn-line]]), and the earth is “shining,” χθόνα δῖαν, in the parable of the two jars (24.532), as the upper air is δῖα in Book 16 (16.365).
