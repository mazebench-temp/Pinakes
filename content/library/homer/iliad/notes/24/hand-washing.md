summary: Before his libation and prayer for a safe journey, Priam has the housekeeper pour pure water over his hands from a pitcher into a basin, and then takes the cup from his wife (24.302–305).
# χέρνιβον, πρόχοον (24.304) and νιψάμενος (24.305) are found in the Iliad only here.
grc: [χέρνιβον, πρόχοον, νιψάμενος]
en: [pour pure water over his hands, when he had washed]
passages:
  - 24.302-307 | Priam washes before the libation
---

“He spoke, and the old man urged the housekeeper, a handmaid, / to pour pure water over his hands; and she stood beside him, / the handmaid, holding a basin and a pitcher in her hands. / And when he had washed, he took the cup from his wife” (24.302–305; see [[handmaids]], [[basin-and-pitcher]], [[libation]] and [[priam]]). The washing comes first, as Hector said it must when he refused Hecuba's wine with unwashed hands (6.266–268), and as Achilles washed his own hands before he poured for Patroclus (16.230), in the verses that Priam's libation repeats (24.306–307 ≈ 16.231–232; see [[hecuba]]).

ἀκήρατον, “pure” (24.303), is literally “undefiled, unharmed”, Walter Leaf explains, as of an estate kept intact (15.498); he rejects the sense “unmixed”, which came from confusion with ἄκρητος (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 24.303). χέρνιβον, “basin”, is found nowhere else in Greek, and the line was rejected in antiquity, since Homer elsewhere calls the basin λέβης and the water χέρνιψ (Leaf on 24.304; see the translation's note on 24.304). In the Odyssey a handmaid pours the χέρνιψ from a golden pitcher over a silver basin for guests to wash before the meal (*Odyssey* 1.136–138).
