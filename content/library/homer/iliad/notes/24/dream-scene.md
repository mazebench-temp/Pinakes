---
summary: Hermes wakes Priam in the night: “And he stood above his head and spoke to him: / ‘Old man, you have no thought of any evil, so soundly do you still sleep’” (24.682–683).
---

The god comes to the sleeper in the station of the dream, in the verse with which Agamemnon told his dream in Book 2, with μιν for με (2.59; Walter Leaf, *Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 24.681), and opens with the dream's reproach, εὕδεις: “so soundly do you still sleep” (24.683). But he is not a dream: the god himself stands there, and the sleeper is in danger, among enemy men (24.683–688; [[destructive]]). Priam wakes the herald, Hermes yokes the horses and mules, and they drive out through the camp unseen (24.689–691). See [[hermes]] and [[hypnos]].
