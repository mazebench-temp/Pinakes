---
summary: Priam kisses Achilles' hands, “the terrible, man-slaying hands that had killed many of his sons” (24.478–479); he weeps remembering “man-slaying Hector” (24.509); and Andromache laments “holding in her hands the head of man-slaying Hector” (24.724).
---

The epithet of the killer is shared in this book. Achilles' hands were ἀνδροφόνους when he laid them on Patroclus' breast in the laments of Books 18 and 23 (18.317, 23.18); Priam kisses them (24.478–479; [[supplication]]). Ἕκτορος ἀνδροφόνοιο, Hector's own formula, names him as his father remembers him at Achilles' feet (24.509), and as his wife holds his head in the last laments (24.723–724; [[lament]]). See [[hector]] and [[achilles]].
