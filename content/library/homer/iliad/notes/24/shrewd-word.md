---
summary: Zeus asks for Thetis to be called “so that I may tell her a shrewd word” (24.75), and Andromache laments that Hector did not, as he died, “speak to me some shrewd word” (24.744).
---

The last two of the formula's uses frame the book: the god's word that ends the dragging, and the word that a dying man did not leave his wife, “which I might always / remember night and day, shedding tears” (24.743–745). The translation's note on 24.744 reports Walter Leaf's gloss, “wise word,” here with more than its usual force, as at 24.75. See [[andromache]] and [[epos]].
