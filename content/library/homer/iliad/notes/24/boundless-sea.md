summary: Hermes' sandals carry him “over the boundless earth” (24.342); Achilles reckons Priam's old wealth by Lesbos, Phrygia “and the boundless Hellespont” (24.545); and the last lament ends as “the countless people groaned with her” (24.776).
# ἀπείρων (24.545, 24.776): the nominative, only in this book in Homer.
grc: [ἀπείρων]
en: [countless]
---

The first use is the formula of the earth, ἐπʼ ἀπείρονα γαῖαν, in the verses of the gods' sandals (24.341–342; [[ambrosial]]). The other two are new. Walter Leaf notes that the Hellespont of 24.545 must include the sea along the west coast of the Troad as well as the strait to the north, which could hardly be called boundless (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 24.545; [[hellespont]]). Of the people the word is stranger still. Leaf notes that Homer elsewhere gives ἀπείρων only to the expanse of sea or land, apart from the “boundless bonds” of *Odyssey* 8.340, and that δῆμος, properly a land or a local community, is nowhere else a multitude (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 24.776). The translation renders it “countless.” The *Shield of Heracles* ascribed to Hesiod has the same picture at another burial: “Ceyx buried Cycnus, and the countless people,” λαὸς ἀπείρων (*Shield* 472). Here Helen's lament ends with the whole city groaning (24.776; [[lament]], [[helen]]).
