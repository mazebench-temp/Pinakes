---
summary: Strabo quotes Achilles' words on the bounds of Priam's kingdom as the clearest proof that Priam ruled the whole coast of the Troad, takes Iris' and Hecuba's Samos for Samothrace, and Alcimus for a short form of Alcimedon. He reports that Priam's wealth came from the gold mines near Abydus, and gathers the stories of Sipylus, Tantalus and Niobe.
---

- “And you too, old man, we hear, were prosperous once”: Achilles' words show that Priam ruled all the coast, divided into nine lordships, with Lesbos “the city of Macar” (Μάκαρος πόλις) for the seat of Macar (*Geography* 13.1.7, quoting 24.543–545): [[lesbos]], [[phrygia]] and [[macar]].
- Samos, the poet's name for Samothrace, kept apart from the Ionian island by its neighbors, with Hecuba's πέρνασχʼ ὅν κε λάβοι, “he sold whomever he might take” (*Geography* 10.2.17, quoting 24.753 and 24.78; and 8.3.6, after Apollodorus, with μεσσηγύς τε Σάμοιο): [[samothrace]].
- Ἄλκιμος “instead of Ἀλκιμέδων”, among the short forms cited by those who take Messe for Messene (*Geography* 8.5.3, quoting 24.474): [[alcimedon]].
- Priam's wealth from the gold mines at Astyra near Abydus, and Tantalus' from the mines around Phrygia and Sipylus (*Geography* 14.5.28): [[gold-and-silver]] and [[sipylus]].
- Sipylus overthrown in the reign of Tantalus, and Magnesia below it thrown down by earthquakes in Strabo's own day (*Geography* 1.3.17, 12.8.18, 13.3.5); Tantalus, Pelops and Niobe called Phrygians (12.8.2); the Mother of the Gods called Sipylene (10.3.12); and Aeschylus' *Niobe*, which confuses Ida with Sipylus (12.8.21): [[sipylus]] and [[niobe-play]].
- The other rivers named Achelous, by Dyme in Achaea, also called the Peirus, and near Lamia (*Geography* 10.2.1, 8.3.11, 9.5.10): [[achelous-of-lydia]].
