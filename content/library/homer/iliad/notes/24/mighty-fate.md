---
summary: Thetis tells Achilles that “death and mighty fate stand close beside you” (24.132), and Hecuba says that “mighty Fate” spun Hector's thread at his birth (24.209–210).
---

Thetis' verse is the verse of Patroclus' prophecy to Hector, ἄγχι παρέστηκεν θάνατος καὶ μοῖρα κραταιή (16.853), now said by a mother to her son. Hecuba's Fate is printed as a person, Μοῖρα, and she spins: γιγνομένῳ ἐπένησε λίνῳ, “spun for him with her thread at his birth” (24.209–210). The Odyssey uses the same words for what Odysseus must suffer, what his fate and the Spinners spun for him with their thread at his birth (*Odyssey* 7.196–198). Achilles speaks of the gods spinning grief for mortals (24.525; [[moira]]). See [[thetis]] and [[hecuba]].
