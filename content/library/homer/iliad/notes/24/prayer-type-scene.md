summary: Priam prays before he goes. At Hecuba's bidding he washes his hands, stands in the middle of the enclosure, pours the wine and looks up to the sky (24.302–307); he invokes Zeus and asks to be welcomed and pitied, and for a bird on the right (24.308–313); and Zeus hears him and sends the eagle (24.314–321).
refs: [24.287-298, 24.302-321]
passages:
  - 24.283-321 | Priam's libation and prayer
---

**Setting and gesture.** The prayer is set by Hecuba's counsel, which gives Priam its words before he speaks them (24.287–298), and by the washing of hands that Hector refused in Book 6, so as not to pray to Zeus unwashed (24.302–305; 6.266–268; [[lord-of-dark-clouds]], [[libation]]). Priam stands in the middle of the enclosure, pours out the wine and looks up to the sky (24.306–307).

**Invocation and request.** The invocation is the whole verse of the oaths and prayers of the war, “Father Zeus, who rule from Ida, most glorious, greatest” (24.308; [[father-zeus]]). There is no claim of past service; Priam had given his reason in his answer to Hecuba, “for it is good to lift up one's hands to Zeus” (24.301). The request is twofold, a welcome from Achilles and a sign: the bird is asked for in Hecuba's own words, with the person changed (24.292–295 = 24.310–313; [[swift]], [[of-the-swift-horses]]).

**Answer.** “So he spoke in prayer, and Zeus the counselor heard him” (24.314; [[hear-me]]): the eagle comes on the right, over the city, and the Trojans rejoice (24.315–321; [[surest-of-birds]]). See [[prayer]] and [[priam]].
