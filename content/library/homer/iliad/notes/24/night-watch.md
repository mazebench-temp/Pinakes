**Asleep at supper.** “But when they came to the towers of the ships and the ditch, / the sentries had just begun to busy themselves about their supper; / and the guide, the slayer of Argus, poured sleep over them / all” (24.443–446; see [[hermes]], [[the-achaean-wall]] and [[hypnos]]). The sentries take their supper at their posts, as Nestor's did on the first night of the watch, when they sat between the ditch and the wall and each man made ready his supper by his fire (9.87–88). Walter Leaf notes that here they seem to be at the gate in the wall itself, as if the space between wall and ditch were forgotten, and not out at the ditch, where Books 9 and 10 place them (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 24.443, comparing 9.67 and 10.194). The order of the watch is to stay awake (7.371, 18.299), and Nestor had told the sentries to let no one be taken by sleep (10.192–193). A god undoes it. Leaf takes the wand “with which he bewitches the eyes of men” to be what puts them to sleep (24.343; on 24.339; see [[wand]]). Then he opens the gates and pushes back the bars, and brings in Priam with the ransom (24.446–447; see [[the-gates-of-the-wall]]).

**In Achilles' words.** Achilles knows that a god has brought Priam: “For no mortal would dare to come, not even one in his prime, / into the camp; for he would not escape the sentries, nor would he / easily push back the bar of our doors” (24.565–567; see [[achilles]] and [[seeing-the-gods]]). φυλάκους, “the sentries”, is found only here in Homer as a common noun; Leaf notes that the form is attested on the Arkesilas vase (on 24.566; see the translation's note on 24.566 and its row for φύλακες, φυλακή). The bar is that of Achilles' own courtyard, which three Achaeans drove home (24.453–456; see [[hut]]), and Leaf takes the verses as an allusion to that description (on 24.448).

**The sacred gatekeepers.** Before dawn Hermes, the only one awake among gods and men, ponders “how he might send King Priam / away from the ships unseen by the sacred gatekeepers” (24.680–681). ἱερούς, “sacred”, is said of the watch as in “the sacred company of the sentries” (10.56; Leaf on 24.681; see [[hieros]]). Whether these gatekeepers are the sentries of the evening, Leaf cannot say (on 24.443). The god drives Priam through the camp, “and no one knew” (24.691).
