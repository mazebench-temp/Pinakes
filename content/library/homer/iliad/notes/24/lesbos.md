---
summary: Achilles marks the bounds of the land over which Priam was once first in wealth and in sons by Lesbos, “the seat of Macar”, out to sea, by Phrygia inland and by the Hellespont (24.543–546).
---

{{quote:8b1e4053-e37c-5d18-b9ea-4712432d3ca9}}

Achilles speaks of Priam's past happiness by hearsay (ἀκούομεν, “we hear”, 24.543), and measures it by the land around the Troad: Lesbos to the south, out to sea, Phrygia to the east, inland, and the Hellespont to the north and west (see [[phrygia]], [[hellespont]] and [[priam]]). Walter Leaf explains the two adverbs: ἄνω is “out to sea”, as with ἀνάγειν of putting out, and καθύπερθε “inland”, since Phrygia was part of the high tableland of Asia Minor; to a Greek on the coast a journey either inland or out to sea was “up” (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 24.544; see the translation's note on 24.544). He takes the bounds themselves, Lesbos and Phrygia, to be included in the land where Priam was most blessed, since it would be a small thing to say that he surpassed all men in the Troad, where he was king (on 24.544). Strabo quotes the verses as the clearest proof that Priam ruled the whole coast of the Troad, which was divided into nine lordships under him (*Geography* 13.1.7).

Μάκαρος ἕδος, “the seat of Macar”, names the island by a legendary king (see [[macar]]). The *Homeric Hymn to Apollo* names it in the same words, “holy Lesbos, seat of Macar son of Aeolus” (*Homeric Hymn to Apollo* 37). The island that marks the edge of Priam's old realm is one that Achilles himself sacked before the poem began: from its spoil Agamemnon chose the seven women he offered back to Achilles, and from it came Diomede, who lay beside Achilles in his hut (9.128–130, 9.664–665; see [[achilles-raids]] and [[diomede]]).
