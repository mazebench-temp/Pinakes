---
summary: Achilles raises Priam by the hand and says, “Ah, wretch, truly you have endured many evils in your heart” (24.518); and the gods have spun the thread “for wretched mortals” (24.525).
---

ἆ δείλʼ is said here in pity, as Zeus said it of Hector putting on the armor of Achilles (17.201; see the translation's note on 24.518): others said it in scorn to men about to die (11.441, 16.837), and Achilles says it to the father of the man he killed (24.516–518). δειλοῖσι βροτοῖσι, “wretched mortals,” is the word of the parable that follows, the mortals whose lot the gods spin out in grief (24.525–533; [[the-two-jars-of-zeus]]). See [[achilles]] and [[priam]].
