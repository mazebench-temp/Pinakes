---
summary: Priam asks the young stranger who meets him on the plain, “Who are you, good sir, and of what parents are you” (24.387).
---

This is the third time the half-verse τίς δὲ σύ ἐσσι φέριστε asks a stranger who he is, after Diomedes to Glaucus (6.123) and Hector to Apollo, whom he did not know (15.247). Priam, too, is speaking to a god without knowing it. Glaucus answered with his true descent; Hermes answers with a false one, the seventh son of the Myrmidon Polyctor (24.396–400; [[genealogy-in-battle]], [[truth-and-deception]], [[polyctor]]), and tells the truth only at Achilles' gate (24.460–461).
