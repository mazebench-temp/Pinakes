---
summary: The island is named twice in the last book, each time with its neighbors. Iris plunges into the sea “between Samos and rocky Imbros” to fetch Thetis (24.78–79), and Hecuba laments that Achilles sold her sons “to Samos and to Imbros and to misty Lemnos” (24.751–753).
---

**Iris' plunge.** Zeus sends Iris for Thetis, “and between Samos and rocky Imbros / she leapt into the dark sea; and the water groaned over her” (24.78–79; see [[iris]] and [[thetis]]). Walter Leaf refers to the two places of Book 13 that name the same waters, the peak of Samothrace from which Poseidon watched the battle and his cave “between Tenedos and rocky Imbros” (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 24.78; 13.12, 13.32–33; see [[imbros]] and [[poseidon]]). Ancient commentators took μείλανι, “dark”, for a name, the gulf north of the Thracian Chersonese that was later called the Black Gulf (Μέλας κόλπος); Leaf calls this absurd, and takes the epithet, as usual with the sea, of the surface ruffled by the wind (on 24.79). λίμνη, “the water”, is the open expanse of the sea, set beside πόντος in the same verse (see the translation's note on 24.79).

**Hecuba's lament.** Over Hector's body Hecuba sets his fate against her other sons': Achilles “would sell, whomever he took, across the barren sea, / to Samos and to Imbros and to misty Lemnos” (24.752–753), but Hector he killed and dragged around the mound of Patroclus (24.754–756; see [[hecuba]], [[sons-of-priam]] and [[lemnos]]). The book of Lycaon tells one such sale: Achilles sold him in Lemnos, and a guest-friend, Eetion of Imbros, ransomed him (21.40–43, 21.78–79; see [[lycaon-son-of-priam]] and [[eetion-of-imbros]]).

**In Strabo.** Strabo quotes both verses as evidence that the poet called this island Samos and kept it apart from the other islands of the name by naming it with its neighbors. In his text Iris' verse reads μεσσηγύς τε Σάμοιο καὶ Ἴμβρου παιπαλοέσσης, and Hecuba says πέρνασχʼ ὅν κε λάβοι, “he sold whomever he might take” (*Geography* 8.3.6, citing Apollodorus, and 10.2.17).
