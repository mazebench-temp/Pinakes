summary: Niobe remembered food “when she was worn out with shedding tears” (24.613); the Trojans would have mourned Hector before the gates all day, “shedding tears” (24.714), and they carry him out to the pyre “shedding tears” (24.786); and Andromache wishes for a word she might remember “night and day, shedding tears” (24.745).
# δάκρυ χέοντες (24.714, 24.786): the plural, only here in the Iliad.
grc: ["δάκρυ χέοντες"]
---

The phrase of weeping in the last book belongs to mothers and to the mourning city: Niobe, who still broods over her sorrows though she is stone (24.613–617; [[niobe]]), Andromache, whose husband died without a last word to her (24.743–745), and the people of Troy, at the gates and at the pyre (24.713–714, 24.786; [[lament]]). See [[the-funeral-of-hector]].
