# αἰγίδι occurs in Homer only here (24.20).
grc: [αἰγίδι]
---

The aegis that Apollo carried before Hector in Book 15, shaking it in the faces of the Danaans until they forgot their courage (15.307–322), serves in the last book to protect Hector's body. Apollo “kept all defilement from his flesh, pitying the man, / though he was dead; and he covered him all over with the aegis, / golden, so that he should not tear his skin as he dragged him” (24.19–21; see [[apollo]], [[aeikizo]] and [[the-dragging-of-hector]]). Hermes later tells Priam that the body lies “fresh as dew”, with all its wounds closed (24.419–420; see [[fresh-as-dew]]).
