---
summary: “And when early-born Dawn appeared, the rosy-fingered” (24.788): the dawn of the day of Hector's bones.
---

The whole verse of 1.477 returns at the end of the poem, on the eleventh day of the truce, when the Trojans quench the pyre, gather the bones and heap the mound (24.788–801; [[dawn-line]], [[early-born]]). See [[eos]] and [[the-funeral-of-hector]].
