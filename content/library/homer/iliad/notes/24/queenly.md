summary: Thetis, “his lady mother,” sits close beside her son and strokes him (24.126); and at the gates of Troy “his dear wife and his lady mother” are the first to tear their hair over Hector (24.710).
---

πότνια μήτηρ is the formula of the two mothers of the book. Thetis comes to Achilles in his hut, as she came to him when he groaned for Patroclus (18.70), and tells him to eat, to sleep with a woman and to give up the body (24.126–137). Hecuba, whom Hector called his lady mother when she offered him wine (6.264), is named with Andromache as they rush to the wagon and touch his head (24.710–712; [[lament]]).
