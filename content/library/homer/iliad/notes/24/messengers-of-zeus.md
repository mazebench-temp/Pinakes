---
summary: Thetis tells Achilles, “I am a messenger to you from Zeus” (24.133); Iris stands beside Priam as “the messenger of Zeus” (24.169) and tells him the same (24.173).
---

ἀλλʼ ἐμέθεν ξύνες ὦκα, Διὸς δέ τοι ἄγγελός εἰμι is nearly the verse of the Dream that Zeus sent to Agamemnon (2.26, 2.63). In Book 2 the message was false; in Book 24 both messengers carry Zeus' true will, Thetis to her son and Iris to Priam (24.133–137, 24.169–187; [[messenger-scene]], [[message-repeated]]). Achilles names his mother so when he tells Priam he will obey (24.560–562; [[daughter-of-the-old-man]]), and Priam calls Iris “an Olympian messenger” (24.194; [[olympian]]).
