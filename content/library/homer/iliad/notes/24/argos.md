---
summary: Hermes, in the likeness of a young Myrmidon, offers to escort Priam “even to famous Argos”, in a swift ship or on foot (24.437–438).
---

“But as your escort I would go even to famous Argos, / with great care, in a swift ship or going with you on foot” (24.437–438; see [[hermes]]). κλυτὸν Ἄργος, “famous Argos”, is found only here, and the verse ends as Agamemnon's did when he feared to come home in disgrace, πολυδίψιον Ἄργος ἱκοίμην (4.171; see the note on Book 4). Walter Leaf takes the name, in the mouth of a Myrmidon, for the Pelasgian Argos of Achilles' own country (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 24.437; 2.681; see [[pelasgian-argos]] and [[myrmidons]]). But Achilles, a Myrmidon himself, used it for the homeland of all the Achaeans, set against Troy, when he remembered his hope that he alone would perish “far from horse-pasturing Argos” and Patroclus go back to Phthia (19.329–330; see the note on Book 19), and to come to Argos is the army's phrase for going home (2.115, 4.171, 9.141). Here too it is most simply the homeland across the sea: the god offers to go with the old man as far as Greece, by ship or by land, and no one would scorn such an escort and fight him (24.439).
