---
summary: Apollo says that dragging Hector “is not more honorable for him, nor better” (24.52); and Achilles tells the dead Patroclus that he has released Hector, “since the ransom he gave me is not unseemly” (24.594).
refs: [24.52, 24.594]
---

Both denials say more than they state. Apollo's “not more honorable for him, nor better” is the judgment of the gods on the defilement (24.50–54; [[the-dragging-of-hector]]). Achilles' “not unseemly,” οὔ … ἀεικέα, is his account to Patroclus of a boundless ransom, of which he promises him his share (24.592–595; [[boundless-ransom]], [[apoina]]). The negations of Zeus' message, that Achilles “is not a fool, nor heedless, nor an offender” (24.157), work the same way ([[privative-adjectives]]). See [[achilles]] and [[apollo]].
