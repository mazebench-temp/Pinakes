---
summary: Priam wishes that Achilles may have joy of the ransom and come “to your native land” (24.556–557).
---

The wish is the formula of the return, σὴν ἐς πατρίδα γαῖαν, “to your native land,” spoken to the one man who knows he will not see it. Achilles had said, as he chose to avenge Patroclus and as he cut his hair for the pyre, “since I am not going home to my dear native land” (18.101, 23.150), and in this book he tells Priam that he sits at Troy far from his native land and will not care for his father in old age (24.540–542).
