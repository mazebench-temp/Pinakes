---
summary: Priam, rebuking his nine surviving sons, calls them liars and dancers, “robbers of lambs and kids from their own people” (24.260–262).
---

“Ares has destroyed them, and these disgraces are all that is left, / liars and dancers, best at beating the dancing floor, / robbers of lambs and kids from their own people” (24.260–262). The best of Priam's sons, Mestor, Troilus and Hector, are dead, and he turns on the nine who are left (24.248–264; see [[priam]] and [[sons-of-priam]]). ἀρνῶν ἠδʼ ἐρίφων, “of lambs and kids”, is found only here in the Iliad and three times in the Odyssey, of the young of the Cyclops' flocks in their pens and of offerings (*Odyssey* 9.220, 17.242, 19.398; see [[goats]]). ἁρπακτήρ, “robber”, occurs only here in Homer. Walter Leaf takes ἐπιδήμιοι, “from their own people”, as the emphatic word: they plunder their own folk instead of the enemy (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 24.262). The plunder of lambs and kids is a wolf's work in the similes, where wolves fall on lambs or kids and snatch them from the flocks (16.352–355; see [[like-wolves]]), and the heroes' raids are on the herds of the enemy (11.670–682; see [[raiding]]). Priam's sons steal the lambs of Troy.
