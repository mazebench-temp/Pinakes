---
summary: Hera will not have the gods give “Achilles and Hector equal honor” (24.57), and Zeus agrees that “their honor will not be one and the same” (24.66). Achilles tells Priam that the man to whom Zeus gives only evils “wanders honored neither by gods nor by mortals” (24.533), and Automedon and Alcimus are the companions whom Achilles “honored, after Patroclus who was dead” (24.575).
grc: [τετιμένος]
---

**Equal honor.** Apollo has pleaded for Hector, and Hera turns his plea into a question of honor: Hector is a mortal, suckled at a woman's breast, while Achilles is the son of a goddess whom Hera herself raised and gave to Peleus (24.56–63; see [[hera]] and [[thanatos]]). Walter Leaf takes her answer as a reductio ad absurdum of Apollo's argument, the son of a woman set on a footing with the son of a goddess (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 24.56). Zeus gives her the point, τιμή γε μία, “one and the same”, as ὁμὴν τιμήν is “equal honor” in her question (on 24.66), and then gives Hector his due: he was dearest to the gods for his gifts at their altar (24.66–70; see [[doron]] and [[geras]]). The honor of Achilles is the κῦδος of a ransom accepted (24.110; see [[kydos]] and [[apoina]]).

**Without honor.** In Achilles' parable the man to whom Zeus gives only from the jar of evils is made λωβητός, “a thing of scorn”, which Eustathius, as Leaf reports, glosses as “insulted and without honor” (ἐφύβριστος καὶ ἄτιμος; on 24.531), and he wanders over the earth, honored neither by gods nor by men (24.531–533; see [[the-two-jars-of-zeus]]). τετιμένος, the perfect participle of τίω, is found only here in the Iliad; the Odyssey uses it of the singer Demodocus, honored by the people (*Odyssey* 13.28).

**The honored companions.** Automedon and Alcimus wait on Achilles in the hut and go out with him to take the ransom from the wagon, “whom above all / of his companions Achilles honored, after Patroclus who was dead” (24.473–475, 24.573–579; see [[automedon]], [[alcimedon]] and [[hetairos]]). Leaf compares the Odyssey's words for Antilochus, whom Achilles honored above all his other companions after Patroclus (*Odyssey* 24.78–79; on 24.575).

**A price.** Plato, refusing the story that Achilles gave back Hector's body for payment, calls the ransom τιμή, “a price” (*Republic* 3.390e–391a; see [[plato-the-price-of-hectors-body]] and [[apoina]]).
