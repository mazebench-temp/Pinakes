---
summary: Priam names among the sons he has lost “godlike Mestor” (24.257).
---

“godlike Mestor and Troilus, who fought from his chariot, / and Hector, who was a god among men” (24.257–258) are the three dead sons whom Priam sets against the nine who are left, “liars and dancers” (24.253–262; [[sons-of-priam]]). Mestor appears only here in Homer, as Walter Leaf notes (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 24.257): the epithet is all the poem gives him, as it is for many of the heroes of the past who bear it. See [[mestor]], [[troilus]] and [[hippiocharmes]].
