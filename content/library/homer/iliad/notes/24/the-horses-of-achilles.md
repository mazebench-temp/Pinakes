# Unnamed: the swift horses yoked for the dragging (24.14) and the horses to which Hector is tied (24.51).
refs: [24.14, 24.51]
---

“But whenever he had yoked his swift horses under the chariot, / he would tie Hector behind the chariot to be dragged, / and when he had dragged him three times around the mound of the dead son of Menoetius, / he would rest again in his hut” (24.14–17; see [[the-dragging-of-hector]]). The team is the one that dragged Hector from the walls to the ships, “and the pair flew on not unwilling” (22.400), the immortal horses that wept for Patroclus beside the battle and stood mourning him in the games, their manes trailing on the ground (17.426–440, 23.276–284). Now they drag his killer around his mound. Apollo, in his protest to the gods, gives them a single verse: Achilles “ties him to his horses and drags him around the mound of his dear companion” (24.51; see [[apollo]]). They have no other part in the book; the horses that come to Achilles' hut by night are Priam's (24.279–280, 24.440–442; see [[horses]]).
