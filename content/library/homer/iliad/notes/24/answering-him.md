---
summary: Zeus answers Hera, “Answering her, Zeus the cloud-gatherer said” (24.64); Achilles answers Thetis (24.138); Priam answers Hecuba twice (24.217, 24.299); Zeus speaks to Hermes “face to face” (24.333); Hermes answers Priam four times, “Then the guide, the slayer of Argus, spoke to him in turn” (24.378, 24.389, 24.410, 24.432); and Achilles gives Priam the truce, “Then brilliant swift-footed Achilles spoke to him in turn” (24.668).
---

**Replies.** The three answers with ἀπαμειβόμενος προσέφη are given to the decisions of the book's beginning: Zeus' judgment in the gods' quarrel, Achilles' consent to his mother, and Priam's consent to Hecuba's counsel to pray (24.64–76, 24.138–140, 24.299–301; [[cloud-gatherer]], [[swift-footed]], [[godlike]]).

**In turn.** τὸν δʼ αὖτε προσέειπε fills the meeting on the plain: four times the god answers Priam with it, and Priam's answers are the verses of [[then-answered]] (24.372–432; [[guide]]). Its last use opens Achilles' last speech to Priam (24.668; [[podarkes]]). ἀντίον ηὔδα, the reply “face to face,” is used once of Zeus as he gives Hermes his orders (24.333). See [[then-answered]].
