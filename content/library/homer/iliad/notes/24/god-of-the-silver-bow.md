---
summary: Hera answers Apollo's protest, “Even that word of yours might stand, god of the silver bow” (24.56), and Hecuba says that Hector lies in her halls “like one whom Apollo, god of the silver bow, / has come upon and killed with his gentle arrows” (24.758–759).
---

The vocative ἀργυρότοξε opens Hera's reply, which ends in reproach: Apollo too came to the wedding of Peleus and feasted with his lyre, “friend of the wicked, faithless always” (24.56–63; [[hera]]). In Hecuba's lament the epithet names the god of sudden death without suffering, in a verse that the Iliad uses only here (see the translation's note on 24.759; [[fresh-as-dew]]). In Achilles' story of Niobe the bow itself kills her sons, “with arrows from his silver bow” (24.605; [[the-children-of-niobe]]). See [[apollo]].
