---
summary: “So he spoke in prayer, and Zeus the counselor heard him” (24.314): Zeus answers Priam's prayer with the eagle.
---

The formula of the answered prayer ([[hear-me]]) gives Zeus the epithet of the god whose plans are fulfilled. He sends at once the bird Priam asked for, on the right, and the Trojans rejoice (24.314–321; [[surest-of-birds]], [[eagle]]). The counsel that the eagle confirms is Zeus' own, given to Thetis and to Iris at the beginning of the book (24.112–119, 24.144–158). See [[zeus]] and [[prayer]].
