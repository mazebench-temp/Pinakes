---
summary: The formula follows the last ransom of the poem from the chamber to the hut. It is heaped on the wagon “for Hector's head”, offered in Priam's plea in the words that brought Chryses to the ships, and taken from the wagon by Achilles' companions (24.276, 24.502, 24.579).
---

**For Hector's head.** From the chamber Priam's sons bring the gifts he chose from his chests and heap them on the polished wagon, “the boundless ransom for Hector's head” (24.275–276; 24.228–237; see [[the-ransom-of-hector]] and [[apoina]]). Ἑκτορέης κεφαλῆς joins the adjective of the name, which is said elsewhere of Hector's tunic and of his offerings (2.416, 10.46), to the head that stands for the whole man, as when Achilles mourned the companion he honored ἶσον ἐμῇ κεφαλῇ, “as my own life” (18.82; see [[patroclus]]).

**In the words of Chryses.** Priam ends his plea with the reason he has come: “for his sake I have come now to the ships of the Achaeans / to ransom him from you, and I bring a boundless ransom” (24.501–502). λυσόμενος … φέρω δʼ ἀπερείσιʼ ἄποινα are the words that brought Chryses to the ships, “to win his daughter's freedom, bringing a boundless ransom” (λυσόμενός τε θύγατρα φέρων τʼ ἀπερείσιʼ ἄποινα, 1.13 = 1.372; see [[chryses]] and [[book-1-and-book-24]]). Chryses was driven away; Priam is heard.

**Taken.** Priam begs Achilles to release the body and “accept the ransom, / the many things that we bring you” (24.555–556), and Automedon and Alcimus take from the wagon “the boundless ransom for Hector's head” (24.579 = 24.276), leaving two mantles and a tunic to wrap the body (24.580–581; see [[automedon]] and [[alcimedon]]). Achilles tells the dead Patroclus that the ransom is “not unseemly” and promises him his share of it (24.592–595), and Hermes reckons that Priam alive would cost the sons he has left three times as much (24.685–688; see [[hermes]]).

**The formula's last use.** Of the eleven uses of the formula, the three offers of captives on the battlefield are refused (6.49, 10.380, 11.134), Agamemnon's amends are refused and then paid (9.120, 19.138), and the ransom that Achilles once took for Andromache's mother belongs to the past (6.427; see [[sack-of-thebe]]). Chryses' ransom, at the beginning, is the only other that a father brings, and it is refused (1.13, 1.372). The last three follow one ransom from its loading to its acceptance.
