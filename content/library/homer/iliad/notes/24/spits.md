# ὀβελοῖσιν (24.623) is the entry's.
---

“He spoke, and springing up swift Achilles slaughtered a silver-white sheep, / and his companions flayed it and were busy over it in good order, / and cut it up skillfully and threaded it on spits, / and roasted it carefully, and drew it all off” (24.621–624; see [[achilles]], [[sheep]] and [[feast-type-scene]]). Walter Leaf notes that 24.623–624 are the verses of Agamemnon's feast for Ajax after the duel (7.317–318), and that the next verses, the bread in beautiful baskets and Achilles serving the meat, are those of Achilles' meal for the envoys, where Patroclus set out the bread (24.625–626 = 9.216–217; *Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 24.623; see the note on Book 9). Now Automedon does Patroclus' part (24.625; see [[automedon]]). No portion for the gods is mentioned: the sheep is slaughtered (σφάξʼ) for the meal of host and guest, since even Niobe, in the story Achilles has just told, remembered food (24.602–620; see [[niobe]] and [[xenia]]).
