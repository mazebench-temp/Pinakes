summary: Hermes is to bring Priam “to the son of Peleus” unseen (24.338); Priam asks the stranger whether he is “the attendant of Achilles, son of Peleus” (24.406) and begs to come “to the hut of the son of Peleus” (24.431), and they come there (24.448); Hermes brings in the gifts “for the swift-footed son of Peleus” (24.458) and tells Priam to clasp his knees (24.465); and “the son of Peleus sprang out of the door of the house like a lion” (24.572).
---

**The journey.** From Zeus' order to the threshold, the patronymic names the goal of Priam's night (24.338, 24.406, 24.431, 24.448). The hut of the son of Peleus is described as the travelers reach it (24.448–456; [[hut]]), and Hermes' last advice is to clasp the knees of the son of Peleus and beg him by his father and mother (24.465–467; [[supplication]]).

**The lion.** When Priam has been warned not to provoke him further, Πηλεΐδης δʼ οἴκοιο λέων ὣς ἆλτο θύραζε (24.572): the son of Peleus goes out to bring in the ransom and the body ([[like-a-lion]]). See [[achilles]] and [[peleus]].
