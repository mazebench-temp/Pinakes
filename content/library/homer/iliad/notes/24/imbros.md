---
summary: Imbros is named twice, both times beside Samothrace. Iris leaps into the sea “between Samos and rocky Imbros” on her way to Thetis (24.78–79), and Hecuba laments that Achilles sold her sons “to Samos and to Imbros and to misty Lemnos” (24.752–753).
---

Iris' verse ends as the verse of Poseidon's cave, μεσσηγὺς Τενέδοιο καὶ Ἴμβρου παιπαλοέσσης, “between Tenedos and rocky Imbros” (13.33), with Samothrace in place of Tenedos: μεσσηγὺς δὲ Σάμου τε καὶ Ἴμβρου παιπαλοέσσης (24.78; see [[samothrace]], [[tenedos]] and [[iris]]). Walter Leaf refers the reader to both places of Book 13 (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 24.78). The island lies on the gods' road from Olympus to the Troad: Hera and Sleep leave Lemnos and Imbros behind on their way to Ida (14.281–283; see [[sleep]]), and Iris, sent from Olympus, dives into the sea beside it.

In Hecuba's lament the three islands of the northern Aegean are the places to which Achilles shipped the sons of Priam he took alive (24.751–753; see [[hecuba]], [[lemnos]] and [[sons-of-priam]]). Imbros had its part in the trade: a guest-friend from the island, Eetion, ransomed Lycaon after Achilles had sold him in Lemnos (21.40–43; see [[eetion-of-imbros]] and [[lycaon-son-of-priam]]).
