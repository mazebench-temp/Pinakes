summary: Achilles tells Priam that the messenger who came to him from Zeus was his mother, “the daughter of the Old Man of the Sea”. Iris finds Thetis in her cave among the goddesses of the sea, his daughters.
---

**The daughter of the Old Man.** Warning Priam not to provoke him, Achilles says that he means to release Hector, “for a messenger came to me from Zeus, / my mother, who bore me, the daughter of the Old Man of the Sea” (24.561–562; see [[thetis]] and [[achilles]]). θυγάτηρ ἁλίοιο γέροντος ends the verse as it did when the narrator named Thetis twice in Book 1, as Hera saw that she had been laying plans with Zeus and as Hera taunted him with it (1.538, 1.556), and when Apollo, in Lycaon's likeness, told Aeneas that his mother was a daughter of Zeus and Achilles' a daughter of the Old Man of the Sea (20.105–107; see [[daughter-of-the-old-man]]). Achilles gives his mother her father's title as he gives her message the authority of Zeus. When Iris comes to fetch her, she sits in a hollow cave with the other “goddesses of the sea” around her (ἅλιαι θεαί, 24.83–84; see [[nereids]]), as in Book 18 she sat in the depths of the sea “beside her aged father” with the Nereids around her (18.36–38).
