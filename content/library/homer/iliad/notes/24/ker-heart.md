---
summary: Hera says that Peleus “was dear above all to the hearts of the immortals” (24.61), and Hermes says the same of Hector, “since he was dear above all to their hearts” (24.423). Hermes, in his disguise, will not take a gift behind Achilles' back: “I fear him and feel shame above all in my heart” (24.435). Helen weeps for Hector and for herself, “grieving at heart” (24.773).
---

περὶ κῆρι, “above all” in the heart, is the phrase of the gods' love for men: Hera's for Peleus, to whom she gave Thetis as wife, and Hermes' report of the gods' love for Hector, whose body they keep from harm (24.59–61, 24.422–423; see [[peleus]], [[hector]] and [[philos]]). Hermes uses it again of his own fear and shame before Achilles (24.435–436; see [[aidos]]). Helen's ἀχνυμένη κῆρ, “grieving at heart” (24.773), is the accusative of the part, as in χωόμενος κῆρ, “angry at heart” (1.44; see [[achos]] and [[helen]]).

In the simile of the lead weight Iris goes down to the depths “bringing doom to the fishes” (24.82): that κῆρα is the other word, κήρ “doom” (see [[ker-doom]]). Walter Leaf reports that Plato quotes the passage with πῆμα for κῆρα, and that Didymus defended the variant on the ground that it is absurd to speak of a κήρ for fishes, a reason Leaf himself calls absurd (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 24.80; see the translation's note on 24.80–82).
