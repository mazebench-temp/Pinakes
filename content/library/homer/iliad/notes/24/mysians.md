---
summary: The mules that draw the wagon with Hector's ransom were a gift of the Mysians to Priam (24.277–278).
---

“and yoked the strong-hoofed mules that work in harness, / which the Mysians had once given to Priam, splendid gifts” (24.277–278; see [[mules]], [[strong-hoofed]] and [[priam]]). Walter Leaf reports that according to the scholia the Mysians were famous for their mules, and that the scholia quote a verse of Anacreon on the Mysians as the inventors of the breeding of asses with mares; he adds that they were neighbors of the Eneti, “where the breed of wild mules comes from” (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 24.278; 2.852; see [[eneti]]). The poem does not say when or why the gift was made. The mules are the team of the ransom and of the body: they draw the wagon to Achilles' hut, and at dawn they bring the corpse home (24.324, 24.697; see [[wagon]]).
