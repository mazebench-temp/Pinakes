---
summary: Ilus is named once in the last book, by his grave: at nightfall Priam and his herald drive past “the great mound of Ilus” on their way to Achilles (24.349).
# Ἴλοιο, the old genitive in -οιο, occurs only here; elsewhere the name is Ἴλου (10.415, 11.166, 11.372).
grc: [Ἴλοιο]
---

οἳ δʼ ἐπεὶ οὖν μέγα σῆμα παρὲξ Ἴλοιο ἔλασσαν, “And when they had driven past the great mound of Ilus” (24.349; see [[the-tomb-of-ilus]]). As in Books 10 and 11, Ilus is a landmark of the plain between the city and the ships (10.415, 11.166, 11.372). Beyond his grave lie the river and the ford, where Priam and the herald halt their mules and horses to drink, and where Hermes meets them (24.350–353; see [[scamander]] and [[hermes]]). Walter Leaf finds no excuse for the hiatus after Ἴλοιο and no likely emendation (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 24.349; see the translation's note on 24.349).

Priam passes the grave of his grandfather (20.236–237; see [[priam]]). Five verses later the herald calls him by the title that the poem gave Ilus at his tomb, Δαρδανίδη, “descendant of Dardanus” (24.354; 11.166, 11.372; see [[dardanus]] and [[idaeus]]).
