summary: The blessed gods watch the dragging of Hector with pity and quarrel for nine days over his body; when Thetis comes up to Olympus they sit assembled around Zeus. Hermes tells Priam that the blessed gods care for his son even in death, and Priam that they remember Hector's gifts. Achilles tells Priam that the gods have spun a life of grief for mortals while they themselves are free from care, and that the heavenly gods brought the war upon him; in his story of Niobe the heavenly gods bury her children.

**The gods as audience.** “So in his rage he defiled brilliant Hector; / and the blessed gods, looking on, kept pitying him” (24.22–23; see [[watching-the-war]] and [[pity]]). The gods who watched Achilles chase Hector around the walls (22.166) watch him drag the body, and the pity they feel is the pity Apollo will say Achilles has lost (24.44). All but Hera, Poseidon and Athena would have Hermes steal the body (24.24–26), and Zeus tells Thetis that “for nine days a quarrel has arisen among the immortals” (24.107; see [[the-gods-debate]]). When she comes up to Olympus, she finds the son of Cronus, “and around him all the others, / the blessed gods who live forever, sat assembled” (24.98–99). μάκαρες θεοὶ αἰὲν ἐόντες is found only here in the Iliad and four times in the *Odyssey*, always after Ζεῦ πάτερ ἠδʼ ἄλλοι, “Father Zeus and you other blessed gods who live forever” (*Odyssey* 5.7, 8.306, 12.371, 12.377; Walter Leaf, *Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 24.99; see [[blessed-gods]] and [[gods-who-live-forever]]).

**Care and freedom from care.** The book is full of the gods' care for Hector's body. Hermes tells Priam that the body is fresh as dew and its wounds closed: “So do the blessed gods care for your own son, / corpse though he is, since he was dear above all to their hearts” (24.422–423). Priam answers that his son “never forgot in his halls the gods who hold Olympus; / so they have remembered him, even in the portion of death” (24.427–428; see [[reciprocity-with-the-gods]]), and Hecuba says it again over the body: “and they have cared for you even in the portion of death” (24.750). Achilles gives Priam the other side: “for so the gods have spun the thread for wretched mortals, / to live in grief; but they themselves are free from care” (24.525–526). The words are of one family. The gods care (κήδονται, 24.422; κήδοντο, 24.750) and are free from care (ἀκηδέες, 24.526); Achilles drags Hector “ruthlessly” (ἀκηδέστως, 24.417), and Priam will not sit while Hector “lies uncared for in the huts” (ἀκηδής, 24.554; see the translation's key-word row ἀκηδής).

**The heavenly gods.** Achilles traces Priam's sorrows to them: “But since the heavenly gods brought this bane upon you, / there are always battles around your city, and the killing of men” (24.547–548; see [[heavenly-gods]] and [[the-two-jars-of-zeus]]). In his story the children of Niobe lie nine days unburied, since Zeus had turned the people to stones, “and on the tenth day the heavenly gods buried them” (24.612; see [[the-children-of-niobe]]). θεοὶ Οὐρανίωνες ends the verse also at 1.570, where the gods are troubled at the quarrel of Zeus and Hera, and at 17.195, of the armor the gods gave Peleus. That night “the other gods and the men who fight from chariots / slept all night long”, but not Hermes (24.677–679; see [[hermes]]), as at the start of Book 2 sleep did not hold Zeus (2.1–2).
