---
summary: The man to whom Zeus gives “only from the grievous” jar is made a thing of scorn (24.531); and Andromache's lament has three uses: her son hurled “to grim destruction” (24.735), Hector, who “was not kind in grim battle” (24.739), and the “grievous sorrows” left to her (24.742).
---

τῶν λυγρῶν, “only from the grievous,” is Achilles' word for the jar of evils (24.531; [[the-two-jars-of-zeus]]). In Andromache's lament the adjective runs through her fear for the city, her son and herself: λυγρὸν ὄλεθρον, the formula of “grim destruction” (2.873, 6.16), for the death of Astyanax (24.734–735; [[astyanax]]); δαῒ λυγρῇ, the battle in which Hector was not gentle, the reason why the Achaeans will take their revenge on his son (24.736–739; [[meilichos]]); and ἄλγεα λυγρά, her own share (24.742; [[algos]]). See [[andromache]].
