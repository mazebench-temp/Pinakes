---
summary: Priam's plea “roused the longing for lament for his father” in Achilles (24.507), and when Achilles “had taken his fill of lament, / and the longing for it had gone from his heart and from his limbs” (24.513–514), he rose and raised the old man by the hand.
---

The verse of 24.507, πατρὸς ὑφʼ ἵμερον ὦρσε γόοιο, is the formula of Book 23, where the longing for lament is roused in the Myrmidons by Thetis and by Achilles himself (23.14, 23.108, 23.153), with a father added. Walter Leaf notes that the same verse is spoken in the Odyssey when Menelaus' talk of Odysseus makes Telemachus long to weep for his father (*Odyssey* 4.113; *Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 24.507). Priam, who asked Achilles to remember his father, rouses in him the longing that the son of Odysseus feels for his (see [[old-age-and-memory]], [[fathers-and-sons]] and [[lament]]).

The longing is satisfied like an appetite: Achilles weeps “his fill” (γόοιο τετάρπετο, 24.513; see [[eros]]), and then the ἵμερος leaves him. The second verse, 24.514, was athetized by the Alexandrians as superfluous after 513 and for its γυῖα, since the limbs cannot be the seat of longing; Leaf agrees, and thinks a rhapsodist interpolated it from a line in which fear leaves the limbs (*Odyssey* 6.140; on 24.514; see the translation's note on 24.514).
