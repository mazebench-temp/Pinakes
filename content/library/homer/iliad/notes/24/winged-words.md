---
summary: Thetis and Achilles “spoke many winged words to one another” (24.142), and Achilles raises Priam by the hand, “and he spoke to him, addressing him with winged words” (24.517).
---

**Mother and son.** The verse of the elders on the wall, with πολλά for ἦκα, “many” for “softly” (3.155; 24.142), here sums up a conversation that is not told: the narrator leaves mother and son talking among the ships and turns to Zeus' order to Iris (24.141–143). It is the only use of the formula in the Iliad that is not followed by a speech.

**Achilles and Priam.** The usual whole verse, καί μιν φωνήσας ἔπεα πτερόεντα προσηύδα, brings in Achilles' answer to Priam's supplication, after both have wept: “Ah, wretch, truly you have endured many evils in your heart” (24.516–518; [[wretch]], [[the-two-jars-of-zeus]]).
