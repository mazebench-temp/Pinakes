---
summary: “But when they came to the ford of the fair-flowing river, / the eddying Xanthus” (24.692–693), Hermes leaves Priam and the herald.
---

ἐϋρρεῖος ποταμοῖο, “of the fair-flowing river,” ends the verse in the simile of the stallion that runs to its usual bathing place (6.508, 15.265) and in the two-verse formula of the ford of the Xanthus, here for the third time (14.433, 21.1, 24.692; [[eddying]]). See [[scamander]].
