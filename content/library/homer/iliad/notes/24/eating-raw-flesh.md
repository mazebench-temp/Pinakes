# ὠμηστῇσιν (24.82) and ὠμηστὴς (24.207), forms of ὠμηστής, whose plural ὠμησταί the entry claims at 11.454 and 22.67.
grc: [ὠμηστῇσιν, ὠμηστὴς]
en: [eater of raw flesh]
---

ὠμηστής, “eating raw,” is in the Iliad the word of the birds and dogs that will tear the dead (11.454, 22.67). In Book 24 it is given to the fish of the simile, and then, once in Homer, to a man: Hecuba says that Achilles will not pity or respect Priam, and wishes she could fix her teeth in his liver and eat it (24.205–214; [[hecuba]], [[pity]]). Apollo has called Achilles a lion who goes among the flocks to take his feast (24.41–43; [[like-a-lion]]). See [[achilles]].
