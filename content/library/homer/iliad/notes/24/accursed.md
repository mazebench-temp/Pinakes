---
summary: Andromache, holding Hector's head, tells him that “you have brought accursed lamentation and grief upon your parents” (24.741).
---

The Greek verse is the one Euphorbus spoke to Menelaus (17.37 = 24.741). There the parents were those of the brother Menelaus had killed, and Euphorbus hoped to end their grief with Menelaus' head; here they are Hector's own, and his mother's lament follows Andromache's (24.747–759; [[lament]]). Walter Leaf notes the sudden and harsh change from the third person of the verse before, “and so the people mourn him throughout the city” (24.740), to the second, and compares the narrator's turn to Menelaus at 17.679, where the address is only a figure of speech (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 24.741; [[apostrophe]]).
