---
summary: Iris sinks into the sea like a lead weight “mounted on the horn of an ox of the fields” (24.80–82).
---

Here the words stand in the middle of the verse and in reverse order, ἀγραύλοιο βοὸς κέρας (24.81), where the Odyssey's simile of the fisherman who casts “the horn of an ox of the fields” into the sea ends its verse with βοὸς κέρας ἀγραύλοιο (*Odyssey* 12.251–254). Walter Leaf, comparing that passage, reports two explanations of the horn: the usual one, a little tube of horn over the line above the hook, weighted with lead, which goes back to Aristarchus; and a lure of horn weighted with lead, which he thinks suits the words best (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 24.80; [[fish]]).
