---
summary: Hermes, sent by Zeus to escort Priam, comes swiftly “to Troy and the Hellespont” (24.346), and Achilles names “the boundless Hellespont” among the bounds of Priam's old kingdom (24.545).
---

**Hermes' flight.** Hermes binds on his golden sandals, which carry him over sea and land, takes his wand, and flies; “And quickly he came to Troy and the Hellespont” (24.346; see [[hermes]], [[sandals]] and [[wand]]). The city and the strait stand for the two sides of the plain he must bring Priam across, from Ilios to the ships drawn up by the Hellespont (see [[troy]] and [[ships]]).

**The boundless Hellespont.** In Achilles' words to Priam the Hellespont is the third bound of the old king's realm, with Lesbos out to sea and Phrygia inland: “and Phrygia inland, and the boundless Hellespont” (24.545; see [[lesbos]] and [[phrygia]]). Walter Leaf takes the name here to include the sea off the west coast of the Troad as well as the narrow channel on the north, to which it is now confined, since the strait itself could hardly be called ἀπείρων, “boundless” (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 24.545, referring to 9.360). The verb of the same verse, ἐντὸς ἐέργει, “shuts in” (24.544), is the verb of the Catalogue, where the Thracians are “all those whom the strong-flowing Hellespont encloses” (2.845; see [[thracians]]).
