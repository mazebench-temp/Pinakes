---
summary: Priam says that his son never forgot in his halls “the gods who hold Olympus” (24.427), and so they remembered him even in death.
en: [who hold Olympus]
---

θεῶν οἳ Ὄλυμπον ἔχουσι is Priam's answer to Hermes' report that the body lies unharmed: it is good to give the gods their due, since they have repaid Hector's offerings (24.425–428; [[blessed-gods]]). Apollo and Zeus have said the same at the beginning of the book (24.33–34, 24.66–70). See [[hector]].
