---
summary: Achilles longs for Patroclus, “for his manhood and his noble might” (24.6), and Hermes breathes “noble might” into Priam's horses and mules (24.442); the “many and brave” are Priam's dead sons, mourned by his daughters (24.167) and named by Hecuba and then by Achilles himself, “who has slain many and brave / sons of yours” (24.204–205, 24.520–521).
---

**Noble might.** μένος ἠΰ ends both verses. Patroclus' might is one of the things Achilles cannot sleep for remembering (24.3–9; [[patroclus]]). Hermes' gift to the teams is the gift Zeus gave the horses of Achilles after they wept for Patroclus, “and breathed noble might into the horses” (17.456; 24.440–442; [[hermes]]).

**Many and brave.** πολέας τε καὶ ἐσθλούς is the phrase of the infantry Nestor sets behind his chariots (4.298). In Book 24 it counts the dead: the daughters and daughters-in-law who mourn through Priam's house remember those who, “many and brave,” lay dead at the hands of the Argives (24.166–168). Hecuba's question to Priam, how he can go alone before the eyes of the man who killed so many of his sons, ends “Your heart is iron” (24.203–205); Achilles asks Priam the same question in the same words, in wonder, when he has come (24.519–521).
