---
summary: Niobe likened herself “to fair-cheeked Leto” (24.607), and the last verse of Achilles' night gives him “fair-cheeked Briseis” (24.676).
---

**Leto.** In Achilles' story Niobe's boast against the mother of two is the cause of her loss: she said that Leto had borne two, while she herself had borne many, “and so those two, though they were only two, destroyed them all” (24.607–609; [[niobe]], [[leto]]).

**Briseis.** The epithet that closed the verse of Diomede at the end of the embassy, “the daughter of Phorbas, fair-cheeked Diomede” (9.665), closes the verse of Briseis here, in the same place in the same scene (24.676; [[eupektos]]). She was “fair-cheeked” too when Patroclus led her out of the hut for the heralds (1.346), and this is the last time she is named in the poem. See [[briseis]].
