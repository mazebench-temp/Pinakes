---
summary: Hermes, ordered to guide Priam, “At once he bound under his feet his fine sandals” (24.340), the gods' sandals that carry him over sea and land, and takes up his wand (24.343).
---

The god's version of the scene has only two steps, the sandals and the staff, as when Athena and Hermes set out in the Odyssey in the same verses (24.340–345; *Odyssey* 1.96–98, 5.44–48). The sandal verse keeps the half-line ἐδήσατο καλὰ πέδιλα of the mortal scene (2.44), and the staff is the wand “with which he bewitches the eyes of men” and wakes the sleeping (24.343–344), the instrument of his night's work with the sentries and with Priam (24.445, 24.682–689).
