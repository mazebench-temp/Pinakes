The meal is told in the verses of the feasts that came before it, and the Book 9 note sets out each step. The killing and the roasting are the verses of the feast after the duel of Book 7 (24.623–624 = 7.317–318); the bread and the meat are Achilles' meal for the envoys in the same hut, with Automedon in Patroclus' part (24.625–626 ≈ 9.216–217), and its two closing verses end this meal too (24.627–628 = 9.221–222; [[desire-for-drink-and-food]]). Achilles had urged it with the story of Niobe, who remembered food after her children died (24.601–620; [[mythological-paradigm]]), and Priam says that he has tasted nothing since Hector died (24.641–642).
