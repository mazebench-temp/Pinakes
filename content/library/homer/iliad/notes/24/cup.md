---
summary: Four cups mark the stages of the book. Hera puts a golden cup into Thetis' hand on Olympus (24.101–102); the last treasure of the ransom is the beautiful cup the Thracians gave Priam (24.234–237); Hecuba brings wine in a golden cup for the libation, and Priam prays with it in his hand (24.283–307); and Priam offers the young stranger, who is Hermes, a fine goblet (24.429).
# δέπας (24.101, 24.234), δέπαϊ (24.285), κύπελλον (24.305) and ἄλεισον (24.429) are caught by the entry and its notes
# on Books 4, 11 and 23; δέπας περικαλλές at 24.234 is Priam's cup, which the-cup-of-nestor excepts.
---

**On Olympus.** When Thetis comes to Olympus in her dark veil and sits down beside Zeus, “Hera put a fine golden cup in her hand / and gladdened her with words; and Thetis drank and handed it back” (24.101–102; see [[thetis]] and [[hera]]). The cup is a welcome: so the gods greeted Hera with their cups when she came back from Ida, and she took the cup of Themis (15.84–88; see the note on Book 15). Walter Leaf explains ὤρεξε, “handed it back”, as holding out the cup to return it (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 24.102).

**The Thracian cup.** The last thing Priam takes from his chests is “a beautiful cup, which Thracian men had given him / when he went to them on an embassy” (24.234–235; see [[thracians]] and [[apoina]]). It is a δέπας περικαλλές, the words of Nestor's cup (11.632; see [[the-cup-of-nestor]]), and “not even this / did the old man spare in his halls” (24.235–236).

**The libation.** As Priam and the herald are yoking the horses, Hecuba comes “holding wine sweet to the heart in her right hand / in a golden cup, so that they might pour a libation before they went” (24.284–285), and bids him pray to Zeus for a bird of omen (24.287–298; see [[hecuba]], [[libation]] and [[prayer]]). The verses recur in the Odyssey when Menelaus brings wine in a golden cup to Telemachus and Peisistratus as they leave Sparta, and stands before their horses (*Odyssey* 15.147–150); there the manuscripts read ἐν δέπαϊ χρυσέῳ, which Leaf takes for an adapter's change for the sake of the meter (on 24.284). Priam washes his hands and takes the cup from his wife, “then he prayed, standing in the middle of the enclosure, and poured out the wine, / looking up to the sky” (24.305–307; see [[hand-washing]] and [[wine]]): the verses with which Achilles prayed for Patroclus from the cup he kept in his mother's chest, from which he poured to no god but Zeus (16.231–232; Leaf on 24.306; see the note on Book 16). Zeus heard Achilles and granted half his prayer (16.249–250); Priam he answers at once with the eagle (24.314–321; see [[eagle]]). Hecuba's cup is a δέπας when she brings it (24.285) and a κύπελλον when Priam takes it from her (24.305): the two words name one cup, as they named the cup of Hephaestus in Book 1 (1.584, 1.596).

**The goblet.** On the plain Priam, glad that the young stranger says the gods have kept Hector's body, offers him a gift: “But come, accept from me this fine goblet, / and protect me myself, and with the gods' help escort me” (24.429–430; see [[hermes]] and [[doron]]). Hermes refuses to take gifts behind Achilles' back (24.433–436). ἄλεισον is the word of the golden goblet from which Peleus poured his libation in Nestor's memory (11.774; see the note on Book 11).
