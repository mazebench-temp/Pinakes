summary: Gold covers and carries Hector from first to last. Apollo wraps his body in the golden aegis (24.20–21); Priam weighs out ten talents of gold for him and pours his libation from a golden cup (24.232, 24.285); Hermes binds on golden sandals to bring the old man to Achilles (24.341); and his bones are laid in a golden chest (24.795). Silver is Apollo's bow and Thetis' feet (24.56, 24.89, 24.605, 24.758).

**The gods' gold.** Apollo keeps the dragged body from all harm, “and he covered him all over with the aegis, / golden, so that he should not tear his skin as he dragged him” (24.20–21; see [[apollo]] and [[aegis]]); for the dative αἰγίδι χρυσείῃ Aristarchus read the accusative, αἰγίδα χρυσείην (Walter Leaf, *Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 24.20; see the translation's note on 24.20–21). On Olympus Hera puts “a fine golden cup” into Thetis' hand (24.101; see [[cup]]), and Hermes binds on his sandals, “ambrosial, golden” (24.341; see [[sandals]]). Cassandra is “like golden Aphrodite” (24.699; see [[golden]]).

**Priam's gold.** The ransom has “ten talents of gold in all” (24.232; see [[talent]] and [[apoina]]), and Hecuba brings the wine for the libation “in a golden cup” (24.285). Achilles had heard that Priam was once first in wealth in all the land from Lesbos to the Hellespont (24.543–546; see [[lesbos]]). Strabo reports a story collected by Demetrius of Scepsis from Callisthenes and others, that Priam's wealth came from the gold mines at Astyra near Abydus, of which small remains were still left in his day (*Geography* 14.5.28).

**The golden chest.** Hector's bones are laid “in a golden chest, / covering them with soft purple robes” (24.795–796; see [[larnax]], [[purple]] and [[the-funeral-of-hector]]), as the bones of Patroclus were gathered into a golden bowl, to wait for those of Achilles (23.243–244, 23.253; see the note on Book 23 and [[the-golden-urn]]).

**Silver.** Silver names the gleam of the gods: Apollo is ἀργυρότοξος, “god of the silver bow”, when Hera answers him and when Hecuba likens Hector, fresh in death, to one whom he has killed with his gentle arrows (24.56, 24.758; see [[god-of-the-silver-bow]]), and he killed Niobe's sons with arrows “from his silver bow” (24.605; see [[the-children-of-niobe]]); Thetis is ἀργυρόπεζα, “silver-footed” (24.89, 24.120; see [[silver-footed]]).
