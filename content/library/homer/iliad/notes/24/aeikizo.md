---
summary: Achilles “in his rage … defiled brilliant Hector” at Patroclus' tomb, while Apollo kept “all defilement” from the body (24.19–22); Apollo tells the gods that “in his rage he defiles the senseless earth” (24.54).
---

The verb frames the dragging of the last book. Apollo, pitying the dead man, “kept all defilement from his flesh” and covered him with the golden aegis (πᾶσαν ἀεικείην, 24.18–21; see [[apollo]] and [[aegis]]), and the narrator closes the scene at Patroclus' tomb, “So in his rage he defiled brilliant Hector” (ὣς ὃ μὲν Ἕκτορα δῖον ἀείκιζεν μενεαίνων, 24.22). Apollo's protest to the gods ends on the same verb and the same participle: “for in his rage he defiles the senseless earth” (κωφὴν γὰρ δὴ γαῖαν ἀεικίζει μενεαίνων, 24.54; see [[the-senseless-earth]] and [[nemesis]]). What Hector promised not to do to Achilles (22.256) is what Achilles does to him, and the gods stop it. See [[the-dragging-of-hector]], [[hector]] and [[achilles]].
