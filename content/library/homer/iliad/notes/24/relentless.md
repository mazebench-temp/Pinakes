---
summary: Achilles tells Priam, “Bear up, and do not mourn relentlessly in your heart” (24.549), and Hecuba's lament ends as she “stirred up relentless lamentation” (24.760).
en: [relentlessly]
---

The epithet of unceasing war is given in this book to grief alone, and the two uses answer each other: Achilles bids Priam stop, since grieving will achieve nothing (24.549–551), and in Troy the mother's lament, the second of the three, will not cease (24.747–760; [[lament]], [[hecuba]]).
