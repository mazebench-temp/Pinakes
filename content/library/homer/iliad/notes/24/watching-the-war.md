---
summary: The gods watch Achilles drag Hector: “and the blessed gods, looking on, kept pitying him” (24.23); at the end Cassandra, from Pergamus, is the first to see Priam bringing the body home (24.699–704).
refs: [24.22-23, 24.699-702]
---

The gods' watching ends in pity: “So in his rage he defiled brilliant Hector; / and the blessed gods, looking on, kept pitying him, / and kept urging the keen-sighted slayer of Argus to steal him away” (24.22–24; see [[the-gods-debate]] and [[pity]]). On the last morning the watcher is a Trojan on the citadel: no man or woman saw Priam coming before Cassandra, “who had gone up onto Pergamus”, and “caught sight of her dear father / standing in the chariot” and the body on the bier, and called the city out to see (24.697–706; see [[cassandra]] and [[pergamus]]).
