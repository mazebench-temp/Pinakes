---
summary: Hecuba laments that Achilles sold her other sons “across the barren sea, / to Samos and to Imbros and to misty Lemnos” (24.752–753).
---

The formula of Book 1, παρὰ θῖνʼ ἁλὸς ἀτρυγέτοιο, “along the shore of the barren sea” (1.316, 1.327), here takes another preposition: πέρην ἁλὸς ἀτρυγέτοιο, “across the barren sea,” of the sons Achilles took alive and sold to the islands (24.751–753; [[trade]]). One of them was Lycaon, sold to Lemnos and ransomed by a guest-friend from Imbros, who came home only to meet Achilles again (21.40–44; [[lycaon-son-of-priam]]). See [[hecuba]] and [[lemnos]].
