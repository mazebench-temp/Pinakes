**Helen's lament.** The last lament is a sister-in-law's. Helen calls Hector “by far the dearest to my heart of all my brothers-in-law” (δαέρων, 24.762; see [[helen]] and [[hector]]), and when she remembers those who rebuked her she names the whole household of her marriage: “one of my brothers-in-law or my husband's sisters, or his brothers' well-robed wives, / or my mother-in-law (but my father-in-law was always kind, like a father)” (24.769–770; see [[kind-as-a-father]] and [[priam]]). Hector “would talk them round with words and hold them back” (24.771). Two of the words are those of Hector's own question in Book 6, whether Andromache had gone “to my sisters, or to my brothers' well-robed wives” (6.378), and Helen first called Priam “dear father-in-law” on the wall (3.172). Now, she says, there is no one left in Troy “who is kind to me or a friend, but all shudder at me” (24.775).

**The house.** When Priam goes out to ransom the body, “his daughters and his sons' wives were mourning through the house” (θυγατέρες … ἰδὲ νυοί, 24.166; see [[priams-palace]] and [[trojan-women]]), and his sons and sons-in-law follow him to the edge of the city (παῖδες καὶ γαμβροί, 24.331; see [[sons-of-priam]]).
