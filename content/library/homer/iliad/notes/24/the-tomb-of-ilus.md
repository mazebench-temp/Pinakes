---
summary: The last time the tomb is named, Priam and his herald drive past “the great mound of Ilus” at nightfall on their way to Achilles, and halt beyond it at the river, where Hermes meets them (24.349–353).
grc: ["μέγα σῆμα παρὲξ Ἴλοιο"]
---

“And when they had driven past the great mound of Ilus, / they halted the mules and the horses to drink / at the river; for darkness had now come over the earth” (24.349–351; see [[ilus]] and [[scamander]]). The mound is μέγα, “great”, only here; elsewhere it is σῆμα, “mound” (10.415, 11.166), or τύμβος, “tomb”, with its pillar (11.371; see [[tomb]]). It stands near the river and the ford, where, Walter Leaf supposes, the travelers mean to cross into the enemy's lines (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 24.351; see the translation's note on 24.351). There the herald sees a man coming, and the god takes Priam's hand (24.352–361; see [[hermes]] and [[idaeus]]).

The tomb has marked the ground between the city and the ships for both armies: Hector held his night council beside it, the Trojans fled past it, and Paris shot Diomedes from its pillar (10.414–416, 11.166–168, 11.369–379). Now Ilus' grandson passes it at night with the ransom (see [[the-ransom-of-hector]]). It is one of three mounds in the book: the mound of Patroclus, around which Achilles drags Hector (24.16, 24.51, 24.416, 24.755), the mound of Ilus, and the mound the Trojans heap over Hector's bones at the end (24.799, 24.801; see the translation's row for τύμβος / σῆμα).
