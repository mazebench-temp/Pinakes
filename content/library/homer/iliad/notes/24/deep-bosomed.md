---
summary: Hecuba says that Achilles did not kill Hector as he played the coward, “but as he stood before the Trojans and the deep-bosomed Trojan women” (24.214–215).
---

The last use of the epithet is Hecuba's answer to her son's own words. Hector stayed outside the wall because he felt shame before “the Trojans and the Trojan women with trailing robes” (6.442, 22.105); his mother, wishing she could eat Achilles' liver, says he died standing before the same people, “with no thought of flight or of shelter” (24.212–216).
