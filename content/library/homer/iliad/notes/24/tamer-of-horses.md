---
summary: “So they held the funeral of Hector, tamer of horses” (24.804): the last word of the Iliad.
---

Ἕκτορος ἱπποδάμοιο ends the verse and the poem. The epithet is the Trojans' own, and Hector's among the heroes; the last line names him by it at his grave (24.799–804; [[the-funeral-of-hector]]). Walter Leaf reports a variant of the line in the T scholia, made to join the Iliad to the *Aethiopis*, which followed it in the Epic Cycle (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 24.804). See [[hector]].
