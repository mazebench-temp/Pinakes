---
summary: Achilles weeps for Patroclus through the nights (24.3–11); he and Priam weep together, each for his own (24.507–512); Niobe “remembered food, when she was worn out with shedding tears” (24.613); Troy weeps over the body, and the brothers' tears fall as they gather Hector's bones (24.712–714, 24.786, 24.793–794).
---

**Achilles' nights.** The book opens on sleepless grief: while the others took their fill of sleep, “Achilles / wept, remembering his dear companion” (24.3–4), and “remembering these, he let big tears fall”, lying now on his side, now on his back, now face down (24.9–11; see [[achilles]] and [[patroclus]]).

**Weeping together.** Priam's appeal, “Remember your father, Achilles, like the gods” (24.486), rouses in Achilles “the longing for lament for his father”; the two remember, Priam weeping for Hector, Achilles for his father and again for Patroclus, “and their groaning rose through the house” (24.507–512; see [[priam]], [[peleus]] and [[lament]]). Achilles ends it by raising the old man (24.513–516), and then by the example of Niobe, who “remembered food, when she was worn out with shedding tears” (24.613; see [[niobe]] and [[fasting]]).

**Troy.** When Priam brings the body home, the throng stands around it weeping, and would have mourned Hector before the gates all day, “shedding tears” (24.712–714). Each of the three laments ends “So she spoke, weeping” (24.746, 24.760, 24.776; see [[the-laments-for-hector]]). On the tenth day “they carried out bold Hector, shedding tears” (24.786), and his brothers and companions gathered his bones, “weeping, and the big tears ran down their cheeks” (24.793–794; see [[the-funeral-of-hector]]).
