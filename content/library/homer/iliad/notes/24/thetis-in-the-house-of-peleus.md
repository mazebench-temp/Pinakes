---

The marriage is named twice in the last book, by a goddess and by the son. Hera answers Apollo that Hector and Achilles cannot have the same honor, for Hector “is a mortal and was suckled at a woman's breast”, while Achilles is the offspring of a goddess “whom I myself / nursed and cherished, and gave as wife to a man, / to Peleus, who was dear above all to the hearts of the immortals”; all the gods came to the wedding, Apollo among them with his lyre (24.58–63; see [[hera]], [[apollo]] and [[thetis]]). Achilles tells Priam the other side of the same gift: the gods gave Peleus wealth, rule over the Myrmidons, and “to him, a mortal, they gave a goddess as his wife”, but laid an evil on him too, a single son “doomed to an untimely death”, who sits far away at Troy and will not care for him in his old age (24.534–542; see [[peleus]], [[the-two-jars-of-zeus]] and [[old-age-and-memory]]).
