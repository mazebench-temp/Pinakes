---
summary: Andromache says that many Achaeans, at Hector's hands, “bit the boundless ground with their teeth” (24.738); and for nine days the Trojans bring in “a vast load of wood” for his pyre (24.784).
---

ὀδὰξ ἕλον ἄσπετον οὖδας is Achilles' phrase at the reconciliation: if Briseis had died, so many Achaeans would not have bitten the boundless ground (19.61; [[androktasia]]). Andromache uses it for the men Hector killed, the reason why her son may die at the hands of one of their kinsmen (24.734–738). ἄσπετον ὕλην is the vast wood of the simile in Book 2 and of the pyre of Patroclus (2.455; 23.127), now gathered for Hector's (24.782–784; [[pyres]], [[the-funeral-of-hector]]).
