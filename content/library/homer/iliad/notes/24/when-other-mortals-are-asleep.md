---
summary: Hermes asks Priam where he is driving his horses and mules “through the ambrosial night, when other mortals are asleep?” (24.362–363).
---

The third and last use of the verse. The god asks the question of the night meetings of Book 10 as if he were one of the enemy, and frightens Priam the more (24.362–369; [[ambrosial]]). At the end of the night the contrast that opened Book 2 and Book 10 returns: the others sleep, and Hermes alone is awake (24.677–681; [[chariot-fighters]], [[soft]]). See [[hermes]].
