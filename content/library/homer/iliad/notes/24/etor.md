summary: Hecuba, and after her Achilles, say to Priam, “Your heart is iron” (24.205, 24.521). Apollo tells the gods that Achilles has robbed Hector “of his dear life” (24.50), and Achilles fears that, at the sight of his son, Priam's anger might rise and “Achilles' own heart be stirred” (24.585).

**The iron heart.** Hecuba asks Priam how he can go alone “before the eyes of the man who has slain many and brave / sons of yours” (24.203–205), and Achilles, raising him from the ground, asks the same in the same verses, with one change, the first person ἐξενάριξα for her ἐξενάριξε, so that the killer owns the killing (24.519–521; Walter Leaf, *Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 24.519; see the translation's note on 24.521). The dying Hector had said that Achilles' heart was iron, since he could not be persuaded (22.357); in the last book the iron heart is the suppliant's, that can bear to come (see [[iron]], [[not-stone-or-iron]] and [[thymos]]).

**Life.** φίλον ἦτορ ἀπηύρα is Apollo's phrase for the killing, “when he has robbed brilliant Hector of his dear life” (24.50), as it was the narrator's for Achilles' killing of Asteropaeus (21.201; see [[asteropaeus]]). The ἦτορ that Achilles fears for is his own: if Priam should see his son and fail to hold back his anger, Achilles' heart might be stirred, and he might kill him (ὀρινθείη φίλον ἦτορ, 24.583–586; see [[cholos]] and [[achos]]).
