---
summary: The blessed gods look on at the dragging of Hector and pity him (24.23), sit assembled around Zeus when Thetis comes to Olympus (24.99), and, Hermes tells Priam, care for his son though he is dead (24.422–423).
---

All three are gods who watch and care. “and the blessed gods, looking on, kept pitying him” (24.23) begins the gods' quarrel over the body ([[pity]]); “the blessed gods who live forever” are the assembly that receives Thetis (24.99; [[gods-who-live-forever]]); and “So do the blessed gods care for your own son, / corpse though he is” (24.422–423) is Hermes' answer to Priam's fear that the dogs have eaten him. Against these, Achilles tells Priam that the gods themselves “are free from care,” while mortals live in grief (24.525–526). The “blessed parents” whom Priam ascribes to the stranger are mortals (24.377). See [[olympians]].
