---
summary: Hesiod gives this book's readers their nearest parallels: the three Fates who give mortals good and evil at their birth; the verse on shame that was athetized at 24.45 as taken from the Works and Days; μαχλοσύνη, “lust”, the word that Aristarchus called Hesiod's; Zeus “whose counsels are imperishable”; the Muses' dancing verb; and the wagon of a hundred timbers.
---

- The Fates, Clotho, Lachesis and Atropos, “who give mortals at their birth good and evil to have”, daughters of Night (*Theogony* 217–222) and of Zeus and Themis (904–906), beside the plural Μοῖραι of 24.49: [[fates]].
- “Shame that is no good attends a needy man, shame, which greatly harms men and helps them” (*Works and Days* 317–318); the second verse is 24.45, athetized in antiquity as taken from Hesiod (Leaf on 24.45): [[aidos]], [[savage-as-a-lion]] and [[apollo]].
- μαχλοσύνη, “lust”, used of the daughters of Proetus, who “for their hateful lust” lost the tender bloom of youth (*Catalogue of Women*, fr. 53 Rzach, as Walter Leaf cites it, on 24.23): [[the-judgment-of-paris]] and [[grievous]].
- ἐπερρώσαντο δὲ ποσσίν, of the Muses dancing on Helicon (*Theogony* 8), the verb of the nymphs who dance around the Achelous (24.616): [[nymphs]], [[sipylus]] and [[achelous-of-lydia]].
- Ζεὺς ἄφθιτα μήδεα εἰδώς, “whose counsels are imperishable” (24.88), three times in the story of Prometheus (*Theogony* 545, 550, 561): [[zeus]] and [[imperishable]].
- The sons of Uranus, freed by Zeus, “repaid him the favor of his good deeds” (ἀπεμνήσαντο χάριν) with thunder and the thunderbolt (*Theogony* 501–505), Leaf's parallel for the gods who “remembered” Hector's gifts (24.428): [[doron]] and [[prayer]].
- The wagon of a hundred timbers, its seven-foot axle and its wheel, and the overloaded wagon whose axle breaks (*Works and Days* 424–426, 453–457, 692–693): [[wagon]].
- The countless people, λαὸς ἀπείρων, at the burial of Cycnus (*Shield* 472), as δῆμος ἀπείρων groans with Helen's lament (24.776): [[boundless-sea]].
- The arrows of Heracles feathered with the wings of a dark eagle, μόρφνοιο φλεγύαο (*Shield* 134), and Zeus' μόρφνος (24.316): [[eagle]].
