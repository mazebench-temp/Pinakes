summary: Priam goes to the ships because he heard the goddess and “looked on her face to face” (24.223), but he does not know the god who meets him on the plain in the likeness of a young prince; Hermes names himself only as he leaves, since it would be a thing for blame for a god to welcome mortals openly (24.347–377, 24.460–464). Achilles, who never sees the god, knows that one led Priam to him (24.563–567).
# Unnamed: the goddess seen (24.223), the young prince and the god felt but not known (24.347-348, 24.374-377), the god
# named and his reason for going (24.460-464), and Achilles' knowledge (24.563-564).

**Face to face.** Iris comes to Priam as the messenger of Zeus and says so, and trembling seizes him (24.169–173; see [[iris]]). To Hecuba he gives that as his reason for going: “since I myself heard the goddess and looked on her face to face” (ἐσέδρακον ἄντην, 24.223; see [[truth-and-deception]]).

**A god not known.** The god who comes to escort him takes another shape, “the likeness of a young prince, / with the first down on his lip” (24.347–348; see [[hermes]] and [[eidos]]), the shape in which he meets Odysseus in the Odyssey (*Odyssey* 10.277–279). The herald sees only “a man” and fears for their lives (24.355). Priam feels a god in the meeting without knowing which, or that it is the escort Iris promised (24.182): “But still some god has held his hand over me too, / who sent such a wayfarer to meet me” (24.374–375). He praises the stranger's build and looks and his mind, and says he must come of blessed parents (24.376–377). Walter Leaf notes that Priam does not recognize his guide and that Hermes has to tell his name at the last moment (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 24.181).

**Named and gone.** At Achilles' gate the god says who he is, “Old man, I who have come to you am an immortal god, / Hermes” (24.460–461), and will not come into Achilles' sight: “it would be a thing for blame / for an immortal god to welcome mortals so openly” (24.463–464; see [[nemesis]]). ἄντην, “openly”, face to face, is the word of Priam's sight of Iris (24.223). Leaf takes ἀγαπαζέμεν with the mortals as subject, “to welcome as host”: an immortal cannot let himself become the guest of men (on 24.464; see the translation's note on 24.464).

**Known by his work.** Achilles never sees the god, but he knows what brought Priam: “And I know you too, Priam, in my heart, and you do not escape me: / one of the gods led you to the swift ships of the Achaeans”, for no mortal could have passed the sentries and the bar of his door (24.563–567; see [[achilles]] and [[night-watch]]). γιγνώσκω, “know”, is the verb of recognizing a god, the word of Athena's gift to Diomedes, “so that you may clearly tell god from man” (5.128; see the note on Book 22). When they have eaten, Priam marvels at Achilles, “for he looked like the gods face to face” (24.630; see [[thambos]]).
