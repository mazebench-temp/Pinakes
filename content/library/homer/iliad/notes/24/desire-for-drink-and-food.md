summary: Achilles and Priam eat together, “But when they had put away the desire for drink and food” (24.628), they marvel at each other; and Priam, before he goes, would die gladly “when I have taken my son in my arms and put away my desire for lament” (24.227).
# γόου ἐξ ἔρον εἵην (24.227): the formula's ἐξ ἔρον with lament for food; only here.
grc: ["γόου ἐξ ἔρον εἵην"]
en: [put away my desire for lament]
---

The verse ends the meal in Achilles' hut as it ended his meal for the envoys in Book 9 (9.222 = 24.628; [[feast-type-scene]]). What follows the meal is not song or talk but sight: Priam marvels at Achilles, and Achilles at Priam (24.629–632; [[thambos]]). Priam's own phrase turns the formula to grief: lament is a desire to be satisfied and put away like hunger (24.227), as Achilles has just done, “when brilliant Achilles had taken his fill of lament, / and the longing for it had gone from his heart” (24.513–514; [[the-longing-for-lament]]). See [[priam]] and [[achilles]].
