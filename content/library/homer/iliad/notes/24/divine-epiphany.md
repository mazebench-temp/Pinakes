summary: Iris stands beside Priam and speaks softly, and trembling seizes him (24.169–187); and Hermes, sent by Zeus in the likeness of a young prince, meets Priam on the plain, guides him to Achilles' hut, and there tells him who he is before he goes back to Olympus (24.334–469), returning at night to wake him (24.677–694).

**Iris.** The goddess comes in her own form. Priam trembles (24.170), and she tells him to take heart (24.171); afterward he tells Hecuba that he heard the goddess himself “and looked on her face to face” (24.223), the reason why he will not call it a lie (24.220–224).

**Hermes.** Zeus sends his son in pity, and the god takes the likeness of “a young prince, / with the first down on his lip” (24.331–348). Priam's terror at the sight of him, the hair standing up on his limbs (24.358–360; [[supple]]), takes the place of the mortal's amazement, and the god's disguise is kept through the meeting with a false tale (24.362–439; [[truth-and-deception]]). At the gate he reveals himself, “Old man, I who have come to you am an immortal god, / Hermes,” gives his reason for going no further and his advice, and goes back to Olympus (24.460–469; [[immortals]], [[high-olympus]]). His last coming is by night, standing over the sleeper like a dream ([[dream-scene]]). See [[hermes]], [[iris]] and [[priam]].
