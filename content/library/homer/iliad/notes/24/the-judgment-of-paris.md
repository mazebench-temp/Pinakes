---
summary: The narrator explains why Hera, Poseidon and Athena alone refuse to have Hector's body stolen: they have hated Troy since Alexander insulted the goddesses who came to his farmyard and praised the one who gave him lust. It is the only allusion to the Judgment in Homer, and Aristarchus athetized it.
---

**The allusion.** The gods would have Hermes steal Hector's body, “This pleased all the others, but never Hera, / nor Poseidon, nor the gray-eyed maiden; / but they held to it, as when sacred Ilios first became hateful to them, / and Priam, and the people, for Alexander's blind folly” (24.25–28; see [[the-gods-debate]], [[hera]], [[poseidon]] and [[athena]]). The narrator explains the hatred by its beginning. Ἀλεξάνδρου ἕνεκʼ ἄτης, “for Alexander's blind folly”, is the half-verse with which Helen blamed herself and Paris in Book 6 (6.356), and an ancient reading of Menelaus' words in Book 3 (3.100; see [[arche-or-ate]] and [[ate]]). Only here does the folly have a content.

Each word of the next two verses is strange. νείκεσσε, “insulted” (24.29), is elsewhere in Homer the verb of a rebuke (3.38, 4.336), and cannot mean “judged”; Walter Leaf thinks that no recognized sense of it suits, and guesses that the poet meant something like “humiliated”, of Hera and Athena (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 24.29; see the translation's note on 24.29). μέσσαυλος, “farmyard”, is elsewhere in the Iliad the cattle-yard from which the lion is driven (11.548, 17.112, 17.657), and the phrase ὅτε οἱ μέσσαυλον ἵκοντο ends a verse of the *Odyssey*, of Odysseus' men who came to the Cyclops' yard (*Odyssey* 10.435), where Leaf finds it more in keeping (on 24.29). Herding on Ida was work for princes: Achilles caught two sons of Priam “as they herded sheep” there (11.105–106), and Aphrodite bore Aeneas to Anchises “as he tended his cattle” (5.313). ᾔνησε, “praised”, and μαχλοσύνη, “lust”, occur nowhere else in Homer.

**Aristarchus' athetesis.** Leaf quotes the scholia. Aristarchus athetized 25–30, and perhaps 24 as well (on 24.23, 24.109), on these grounds: Homer does not know the judgment about beauty, or he would have mentioned it often; νείκεσσε means not “judge” but “rebuke” or “quarrel”; μαχλοσύνη is a woman's madness, and Aphrodite gave Paris not that but Helen, the most beautiful woman of her time; and the word is Hesiod's, who first used it of the daughters of Proetus. The Hesiodic verse survives among the fragments of the *Catalogue of Women*: the daughters of Proetus, εἵνεκα μαχλοσύνης στυγερῆς, “for their hateful lust”, lost the tender bloom of their youth (fragment 53 Rzach, as Leaf cites it; see [[hesiod]]). Leaf finds the case decisive against 29–30, “which are thoroughly un-Homeric”, and weaker against the rest: if 24 is kept, 25–28 are not worth expunging (on 24.23). He reports a reading of Aristophanes and of some of the city texts for 30, ἥ οἱ κεχαρισμένα δῶρʼ ὀνόμηνεν, “who named him gifts to his liking”, and takes it for a conjecture made to escape μαχλοσύνη (on 24.30). See the translation's note on 24.23–30.

**The first cause, told last.** Whatever its date, the passage gives the poem's last word on why the gods hate Troy. In Book 4 Zeus asked Hera what Priam and his sons had done to her, that she raged to sack Ilios, and got no answer (4.31–36; see [[the-bargain-of-zeus-and-hera]]). The bracketed verses of Book 8 say that sacred Ilios was hateful to the gods, and Priam, and his people (8.551; see [[the-refused-sacrifice]]). Poseidon's own grievance was Laomedon's fraud, not the Judgment (21.441–457; see [[poseidon-and-apollo-serve-laomedon]]). The gifts of Aphrodite had been named from the beginning, when Hector taunted Paris with “the gifts of Aphrodite, / your hair and your looks” (3.54–55), and Paris answered, “do not throw in my face the lovely gifts of golden Aphrodite” (3.64; see [[paris]] and [[aphrodite]]). The Iliad begins with the quarrel of Achilles and Agamemnon and ends with the story of the war's beginning compressed into six verses, at the moment when Hector's body is still unburied (see [[in-medias-res]] and [[the-abduction-of-helen]]).
