---
summary: Achilles is swift-footed six times: as he answers Thetis (24.138), as Hermes brings in the gifts “for the swift-footed son of Peleus” (24.458), as he looks darkly at Priam (24.559), as he springs up to kill a sheep for the meal (24.621), as he taunts Priam (24.649), and in Hecuba's lament (24.751). Iris is swift-footed twice (24.87, 24.188), and Achilles' horses are “swift horses” (24.14).
---

**Achilles.** The verse of his answers since Book 1, “Answering her, swift-footed Achilles said” (24.138), brings in his consent to his mother; “Looking at him darkly, swift-footed Achilles said” (24.559) brings in his warning to Priam not to provoke him ([[looking-darkly]]), and “Taunting him, swift-footed Achilles said” (24.649) his advice to sleep outside (see the translation's note on 24.649). “swift Achilles” kills the sheep with his own hand, ὠκὺς Ἀχιλλεύς without πόδας (24.621). In Hecuba's mouth the epithet names the man who sold her sons across the sea (24.751–753; [[barren-sea]]).

**Iris and the horses.** πόδας ὠκέα Ἶρις speaks to Thetis and goes away from Priam (24.87, 24.188). The horses that Achilles yokes each dawn to drag Hector are ὠκέας ἵππους, in the half-verse of Book 8, “swift horses under the chariot” (24.14; 8.402, 8.416; [[the-dragging-of-hector]]). ποδώκεϊ Πηλεΐωνι (24.458) is the compound of Book 23 (23.249). See [[achilles]] and [[iris]].
