summary: Hector's pyre. Priam warns Achilles that the wood is far off on the mountain, and when the body is home he sends the Trojans for it; for nine days they bring it in on wagons drawn by oxen and mules, on the tenth they lay Hector on the top of the pyre and set fire to it, and at the next dawn the people gather around it and quench it with wine (24.662–663, 24.777–792).

**The wood.** “For you know how we are penned within the city, and the wood is far / to bring from the mountain, and the Trojans are greatly afraid” (24.662–663; see [[the-funeral-of-hector]]). Walter Leaf reports that Peppmüller rejected these verses, and Düntzer on other grounds, as giving a wrong reason for the days of mourning; he takes them as a touch of pride, and adds Virchow's remark that the wood for Hissarlik and the lower Troad must still be brought by horses from the heights of Ida (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 24.662; see the translation's note on 24.662–663 and [[ida]]). When the body is home, Priam tells the Trojans to “bring wood into the city” without fear of an ambush, since Achilles has promised them twelve days (24.777–781; see [[trojans]]), and they yoke oxen and mules to the wagons and for nine days “brought in a vast load of wood” (24.782–784; see [[mules]] and [[wagon]]). For Patroclus the Achaeans brought oaks from Ida with mules in a morning (23.110–124), and during the truce of Book 7 the Trojans went “for wood” and burned their dead the same day (7.418–432; see [[the-truce-for-the-dead]]).

**On the top of the pyre.** “but when the tenth dawn appeared, bringing light to mortals, / then they carried out bold Hector, shedding tears, / and on the top of the pyre they laid the body, and set fire to it” (24.785–787; see [[bringing-light-to-mortals]] and [[bold]]). ἐν δὲ πυρῇ ὑπάτῃ νεκρὸν θέσαν is the half-verse of Patroclus' pyre (23.165), and the dawn that follows is greeted in the full dawn-line, “And when early-born Dawn appeared, the rosy-fingered” (24.788; see [[dawn-line]]). “then around the pyre of famous Hector the people were astir” (24.789): ἔγρετο, Leaf thinks, is a slip for ἤγρετο, from ἀγείρω, “gather”; the same verb stands at the Achaean pyre of Book 7 (7.434), and the two verses late additions, the second being a verse found three times in the Odyssey (on 24.789; see the translation's note on 24.789–790). The pyre is quenched “as far as the might of the fire had reached” (24.791–792), in the verses of Patroclus' (23.237–238, 23.250–251; see [[cremation]] and [[wine]]).
