# ὕπνου τε γλυκεροῦ (24.3) and ὕπνῳ ὕπο γλυκερῷ (24.636): γλυκερός of sleep, as at 10.4 (ὕπνος … γλυκερός, already claimed).

---

The book begins and ends its sleeplessness with the same phrase. Achilles cannot sleep for grief, while the others take their fill of sleep (24.2–5; [[that-subdues-all]]), and Priam, who has not closed his eyes since Hector died, asks to sleep once he has eaten (24.635–642; [[hypnos]]). His request is a verse of the Odyssey, where Telemachus, a guest at Sparta, asks Menelaus to send him to bed (*Odyssey* 4.294–295). In the night that follows, all sleep but Hermes (24.677–681; [[soft]]). See [[achilles]] and [[priam]].
