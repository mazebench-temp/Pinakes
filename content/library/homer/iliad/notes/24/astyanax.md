summary: The child is not named in the last book. Over Hector's body Andromache foresees his end: he will not reach his youth, for the city will be sacked; he will follow her into slavery, to toil for an implacable master, or some Achaean whose brother, father or son Hector killed will take him by the hand and hurl him from the tower (24.726–739).

**A mere infant.** Apollo's protest names him with Hector's wife and mother, “for his wife to see, and his mother and his child” (24.36; see [[apollo]]). In Andromache's lament he is still πάϊς … νήπιος αὔτως, “a mere infant” (24.726), in the words of her lament in Book 22 (22.484; see [[nepios]]), and she does not think he will reach his youth, ἥβη, for the city will be sacked before it (24.727–729; see [[andromache]] and [[sack-of-troy-foretold]]). Hector was the guardian who “kept the city itself / safe, and kept its devoted wives and little children” (24.729–730), the guarding for which, the poet says, the Trojans named his son Astyanax, ἄστυ + ἄναξ, lord of the city (6.403, 22.506–507).

**Two fates.**

{{quote:5d7681a7-a5e7-5397-abcb-0a3f921b610d}}

The first fate is a slave's: ἔργα ἀεικέα, “shameful tasks”, and ἀμείλιχος, “implacable”, the master he will serve (see [[doulios]] and [[captive-women]]). Hector foresaw such a day for Andromache in Book 6, and prayed that his son would come home from war with bloody spoils and gladden his mother's heart (6.454–458, 6.476–481; see [[hector-and-andromache]]). The second is the death of the children in Priam's picture of the sack, “little children / dashed to the ground in grim combat” (22.63–64), here from the tower, πύργος, to which Andromache ran in Book 6 with the nurse carrying the child (6.386–389; see [[the-tower-of-ilios]]). The reason is his father's own deeds: “For your father was not kind in grim battle” (24.739; see [[meilichos]]).

**The Little Iliad.** Walter Leaf notes that the death of Astyanax was told by the cyclic poets, and quotes from Tzetzes verses of the *Little Iliad* ascribed to Lesches: the son of Achilles led Hector's wife down to the ships, and took the child from the bosom of his nurse and threw him from the tower, seizing him by the foot (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 24.735; see the translation's note on 24.735). Andromache foresees him taken by the hand (χειρὸς ἑλών, 24.735).

For his death at the fall of the city in later poetry, see [[little-iliad]] and [[euripides-astyanax]].
