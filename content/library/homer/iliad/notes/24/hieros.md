---
summary: Ilios is “sacred” three times: in the verse of the gods' old hatred, “as when sacred Ilios first became hateful to them” (24.27), in Zeus' order to Iris (24.143), and in Hermes' question whether the Trojans are all “now leaving sacred Ilios” (24.383); and the Achaean sentries are “the sacred gatekeepers” (24.681).
---

Ἴλιος ἱρή, the formula of the city, keeps its epithet in the mouths of those who wish it destroyed and of the god who is guiding its king (24.27, 24.383). The “sacred gatekeepers” are the Achaean sentries whom Hermes has put to sleep and must pass again (24.443–446, 24.681): Walter Leaf refers to his note on φυλάκων ἱερὸν τέλος, “the sacred company of the sentries” (10.56; *Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 24.681; [[night-watch]]).
