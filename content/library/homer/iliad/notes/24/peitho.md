---
summary: Thetis and Hermes “did not disobey” Zeus (24.120, 24.339). Priam tells Hecuba, “you will not persuade me” (24.219), yet takes her advice to pray, “Lady, I will not disobey you in this” (24.300); Hermes, offered a gift, answers in Priam's words, “but you will not persuade me” (24.433); and when Achilles warns him, “the old man was afraid, and obeyed his word” (24.571).
grc: [ἀπιθήσω, πίσυνος]
---

**The gods obey.** Zeus' will goes down the chain of messengers without resistance: Thetis “did not disobey” (οὐδʼ ἀπίθησε, 24.120), nor did Hermes (24.339), and Achilles accepts the ransom “if the Olympian himself so bids with a ready heart” (24.140; see [[obedience-and-persuasion]], [[thetis]] and [[hermes]]).

**Persuading Priam.** Hecuba cannot move him: οὐδέ με πείσεις, “you will not persuade me” (24.219; see [[hecuba]]). But he yields to her in one thing, the prayer and the omen, “Lady, I will not disobey you in this that you bid me” (24.300; see [[prayer]]), and asks for the bird on which he may rely, “trusting in it” (πίσυνος, 24.295, 24.313; see [[eagle]]). Hermes uses Priam's half-line against him when he refuses the goblet, “but you will not persuade me” (24.433; see [[doron]]).

**Obeying Achilles.** When Achilles warns him not to provoke him, ἔδεισεν δʼ ὃ γέρων καὶ ἐπείθετο μύθῳ, “the old man was afraid, and obeyed his word” (24.571). It is the verse of Chryses, when Agamemnon drove him from the ships in the first book (1.33; see [[chryses]] and [[book-1-and-book-24]]). Chryses obeyed and went away; Priam obeys and stays, and Achilles goes out to bring in the ransom (24.572–579). Hermes' warning before dawn has the same effect: “So he spoke, and the old man was afraid, and roused the herald” (24.689; see [[hypnos]]).
