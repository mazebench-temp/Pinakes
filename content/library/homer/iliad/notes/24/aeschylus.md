---
summary: Aeschylus put the story of this book on the stage in his Phrygians, or the Ransom of Hector, and the story of Niobe in his Niobe. A fragment of the first joins Apollo's two thoughts, that the dead feel nothing and that Nemesis stands above us; Plato quotes the second twice in the pages in which he rejects the jars and the dragging.
---

- The *Phrygians*: the dead who feel neither joy nor pain, and the Nemesis above us; Andromache from Lyrnessus; Hector's weight (frr. 263–272 Nauck, on 24.53–54): [[aeschylus-phrygians]] and [[cicero-the-body-of-hector]].
- Thetis' complaint that Apollo, who sang at her wedding, killed her son, quoted by Plato (*Republic* 2.383a–b), and Hera's taunt to Apollo (24.62–63): [[plato-lying-dream]] and [[apollo]].
- The *Niobe*: the mother brooding on her children's tomb, the god who plants a cause in mortals, and the kin of Tantalus (PSI 1208; fr. 157 Nauck; Plato, *Republic* 2.380a, 3.391e; on 24.602–617): [[niobe-play]].
