---
summary: Apollo opens his protest with “You are hard, you gods, and destructive” (24.33): the only time the word is said of the gods themselves.
---

σχέτλιοί ἐστε θεοί, δηλήμονες: Apollo reproaches the gods, who will not save the body of a man who burned so many thighs of oxen and goats for them, but choose to help “deadly Achilles,” who has lost all pity (24.33–45; [[deadly]], [[pity]]). The word that Priam and Hecuba had used of Achilles (22.41, 22.86) is turned on the gods. See [[apollo]].
