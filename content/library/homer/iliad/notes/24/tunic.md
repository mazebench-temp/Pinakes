---
summary: Twelve tunics go into Priam's ransom (24.231). A well-spun tunic and two mantles are left out of it when it is unloaded at Achilles' hut, and the washed body of Hector is clothed in the tunic and wrapped in a mantle to be carried home (24.580–588).
# χιτῶνας (24.231) and χιτῶνα (24.580, 24.588) are caught by the entry and its note on Book 11.
---

The ransom ends its list of clothing with tunics, “and as many tunics besides” (24.231), after the robes, cloaks, rugs and mantles (24.229–231; see [[robe]], [[cloak]] and [[apoina]]). The same list of a guest's gifts recurs in the Odyssey, twelve cloaks, rugs, mantles and tunics (*Odyssey* 24.276–277; see [[guest-gifts]]).

Not all of it stays with Achilles. “But they left two mantles and a well-spun tunic, / so that he could wrap the corpse and give it to be carried home” (24.580–581), and the slave women, having washed and anointed the body out of Priam's sight, put on it “a fine mantle and a tunic” (24.587–588; see [[bathing]] and [[honoring-the-dead]]). ἐΰννητος, “well-spun”, is the praise of the cloth (see [[well-spun]]). The verses of the washing and the clothing are nearly those of the baths given in the palaces of the Odyssey (*Odyssey* 8.454–455, 17.88–89; Walter Leaf, *Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 24.587). The tunic was the garment next to the skin, and Hector's body goes home dressed as the living are dressed, in a tunic with a mantle over it, wrapped in clothes that were his father's (see [[hector]]).
