---
summary: The Trojans set lookouts around Hector's mound, “lest the well-greaved Achaeans should attack too soon” (24.799–800).
---

One of the commonest epithets of the Achaeans names them for the last time in the poem: the enemy whom the truce holds back while the mound is heaped (24.799–801; [[the-funeral-of-hector]]). See [[achaeans]].
