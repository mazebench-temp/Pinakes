---
summary: Achilles has the slave women “wash and anoint” Hector's body apart, so that Priam should not see his son (24.582–587); the gods had already washed the blood from it (24.419).
---

The last washing in the Iliad is of Hector's body, in Achilles' hut. Achilles “called out the slave women and bade them wash and anoint him, / taking him aside, so that Priam should not see his son”, for fear that the father's anger at the sight would stir his own, and he kill him against the commands of Zeus (24.582–586); “when the slave women had washed him and anointed him with oil”, they dressed him, and Achilles laid him on the bier himself (24.587–589; see [[honoring-the-dead]] and [[achilles]]). The rite is the one he had ordered for Patroclus, whom his companions washed and anointed and laid on a bier (18.343–353). Hermes had told Priam that the body lay fresh, “and the blood is washed from him” (24.419; see [[fresh-as-dew]]).
