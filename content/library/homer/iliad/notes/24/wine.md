summary: Hecuba brings wine sweet to the heart in a golden cup, and Priam, having washed his hands, pours it out to Zeus before he sets out (24.283–307); at Achilles' table he lets sparkling wine go down his throat for the first time since Hector died (24.641–642); and the Trojans quench Hector's pyre with sparkling wine (24.791–792).
# οἶνον (24.284, 24.306), αἴθοπα οἶνον (24.641) and οἴνῳ (24.791) are caught by the entry and its notes on Books 3
# and 11.
---

**The libation.** As the horses are yoked, Hecuba comes “holding wine sweet to the heart in her right hand / in a golden cup, so that they might pour a libation before they went” (24.284–285; see [[hecuba]] and [[cup]]). In Book 6 she offered wine to Hector, to pour to Zeus and then to drink, and he refused it: “and with unwashed hands I shrink from pouring sparkling wine to Zeus” (6.258–268; see the note on Book 6). Now she brings the wine to his father, who has the handmaid pour water over his hands, “then he prayed, standing in the middle of the enclosure, and poured out the wine, / looking up to the sky” (24.302–307; see [[hand-washing]], [[basin-and-pitcher]], [[libation]] and [[prayer]]), and Zeus answers with the eagle (24.314–321; see [[eagle]]).

**At Achilles' table.** When they have eaten, Priam asks to be put to bed, for he has not slept since his son was killed, “Now at last I have tasted food and let sparkling wine / go down my throat; before now I had tasted nothing” (24.641–642; see [[priam]] and [[fasting]]). Walter Leaf notes that καὶ αἴθοπα does not scan, and would read ἠδʼ with Brandreth, as in the Odyssey (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 24.641; see the translation's note on 24.641).

**The pyre.** At the dawn after the burning the people gather around the pyre of Hector, and “first they put out the pyre with sparkling wine, / all of it, as far as the might of the fire had reached” (24.791–792; see [[pyres]], [[cremation]] and [[the-funeral-of-hector]]). It is the verse of the pyre of Patroclus (23.237–238, 23.250–251), and Leaf calls the whole of Hector's funeral almost an abstract of Patroclus' (on 24.791; see [[the-funeral-of-patroclus]] and the note on Book 23).
