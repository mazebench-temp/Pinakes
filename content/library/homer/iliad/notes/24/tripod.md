---
summary: Two gleaming tripods go into Priam's ransom for Hector, with four cauldrons (24.233).
---

“and brought out two gleaming tripods and four cauldrons” (24.233). Tripods and cauldrons are the treasure of the great gift lists: seven tripods and twenty cauldrons head Agamemnon's gifts to Achilles (9.122–123, 19.243–244; see [[the-gifts-of-agamemnon]] and [[cauldron]]), and Achilles brings cauldrons and tripods out of his ships as prizes for the funeral games of Patroclus (23.259; see the note on Book 23). Priam's are fewer, two and four, and they come after the clothes and the gold and before the Thracian cup, the last and dearest of the ransom (24.229–237; see [[apoina]] and [[cup]]). αἴθων, “gleaming”, is the word of Agamemnon's cauldrons (9.123; see [[tawny]]).
