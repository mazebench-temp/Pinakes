summary: Hermes and Achilles warn Priam in the same verse, “if one of them should see you through the swift black night” (24.366, 24.653): first on the plain, and then in the hut where he is to sleep.
---

θοὴν διὰ νύκτα μέλαιναν is the verse-end of the night journeys of Book 10 (10.394, 10.468). In Book 24 the whole verse, τῶν εἴ τίς σε ἴδοιτο θοὴν διὰ νύκτα μέλαιναν, is said twice, and each time the danger is an Achaean who sees the old man. Hermes asks what Priam would do if one of the enemy saw him “bringing so many good things” (24.366–367); Achilles fears that a counselor will see him and tell Agamemnon, “and there might be delay in the ransoming of the body” (24.653–655). The guide who asks the first question takes Priam in unseen, and wakes him to take him out again before Agamemnon can learn of him (24.683–691). See [[swift-night]] and [[hermes]].
