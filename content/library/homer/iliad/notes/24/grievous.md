---
summary: Achilles, lying awake, remembers the sorrows he suffered with Patroclus, “passing through the wars of men and the grievous waves” (24.8); and Alexander praised the goddess “who gave him his grievous lust” (24.30).
---

The first verse is said by Odysseus of himself in the Odyssey, πτολέμους ἀλεγεινά τε κύματα πείρων (*Odyssey* 8.183): here it is the life of two companions, war and the sea, that Achilles cannot stop remembering (24.3–9; [[patroclus]]). The second ends the verses on the judgment of Paris. Walter Leaf reports that Aristarchus objected to μαχλοσύνη, a word for a woman's lust, and called it Hesiodic (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 24.23 and 24.30; [[the-judgment-of-paris]]).
