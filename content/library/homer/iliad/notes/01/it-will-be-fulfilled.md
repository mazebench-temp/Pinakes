Achilles predicts, with the weaker form, that Agamemnon will soon lose his life through his arrogance. He is wrong. Athena answers with the strong form, and she is right: one day Achilles will be offered three times as many splendid gifts because of this outrage, as he is in Book 9. The goddess's certainty corrects the hero's guess.

The language of fulfillment runs through the whole book. The proem says that the plan of Zeus “was being fulfilled” (ἐτελείετο, {{ref:Iliad 1.5}}; [[plan-of-zeus]]). Achilles tells Thetis that Agamemnon's threat “has now been carried out” (τετελεσμένος, {{ref:Iliad 1.388}}). And Zeus promises Thetis that he will see to it, “to bring these things to fulfillment” (ὄφρα τελέσσω, {{ref:Iliad 1.523}}), with a nod that cannot go “unfulfilled” (ἀτελεύτητον, {{ref:Iliad 1.527}}).
