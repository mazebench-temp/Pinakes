---
summary: The sparkling wine Chryses pours on the burning thigh-pieces, the drink of the feast at Chryse, and the root of Achilles' first insult to Agamemnon, “wine-sodden”.
---

**Insult.** Achilles opens his second outburst against Agamemnon with οἰνοβαρές, “heavy with wine”: a drunkard ({{ref:Iliad 1.225}}; see [[wine-sodden]]).

{{quote:feae1f8f-c877-5534-a42c-068656b9245c}}

**Sacrifice.** At Chryse the old priest burns the [[thigh-pieces|thigh-pieces]] and pours sparkling wine over them ({{ref:Iliad 1.462-463}}; see [[libation|libation]]).

**Feast.** After the meal the young men fill the mixing bowls with drink and serve it round, first pouring a little for the gods ({{ref:Iliad 1.470-471}}).

The gods drink [[nectar|nectar]], not wine. Yet the verb for Hephaestus' serving of it, οἰνοχοέω, means “pour wine” ({{ref:Iliad 1.598}}; see [[cupbearing|cupbearing]]).
