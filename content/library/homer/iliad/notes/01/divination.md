---
summary: Calchas, “best of the bird-seers”, reveals why Apollo is angry and what will end the plague, and Agamemnon calls him a “prophet of evils”.
---

Calchas is introduced in full: the best of the bird-seers, who knows what is, what will be and what was before, and who guided the Achaean fleet to Troy by the gift of prophecy Apollo gave him (see [[calchas-guides-the-fleet]] and [[calchas-and-the-muses]]).

{{quote:518116f9-03eb-5055-b165-9116dda34fd9}}

His first words show the seer's danger: he will anger a powerful king, and he asks Achilles to swear to protect him ({{ref:Iliad 1.74-83}}; see [[oath|oath]]). Then he gives the cause of the plague and its cure ({{ref:Iliad 1.93-100}}). Agamemnon turns on him: “Prophet of evils, never yet have you told me anything good”. Readers since antiquity have heard in this an allusion to Aulis, where Calchas demanded the sacrifice of Agamemnon's daughter Iphigenia, a story told in the lost *Cypria* but never in Homer (see [[calchas-evil-prophecies]]).
