Nestor names six heroes in four verses: Peirithous, Dryas ([[shepherd-of-the-people]]), Caeneus, Exadius, Polyphemus ([[antitheos]]), and Theseus son of Aegeus ([[son-of-aegeus]], [[like-the-immortals]]). Most have only a name. The epithets fall on the second, fifth, and sixth, spacing out the list. These are the [[lapiths]] of Thessaly, and the story behind the list is their fight with the Centaurs, the “beasts who lived in the mountains” ([[mountain-dwelling]], [[nestor-and-the-centaurs]]).

The last verse is doubtful. It is missing from many manuscripts and occurs in the same words in the Hesiodic *Shield of Heracles* (182), in a catalogue of Lapiths fighting Centaurs. Without it the list has five names.

The catalogue serves Nestor's argument. The names are his proof: these were the mightiest men ever raised on earth ([[upon-the-earth]], [[polyptoton]]), and they listened to him ([[mythological-paradigm]], [[ring-composition]]).
