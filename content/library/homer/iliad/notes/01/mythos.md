---
summary: μῦθος is almost always the word of the stronger: [[Agamemnon]]'s stern command, [[Athena]]'s word, [[Zeus]]' counsels, and the word that is obeyed or refused.
---

The word keeps company with the verb “obey”.

{{quote:3640bf0c-506f-5c36-8c49-dc270a9d54f6}}

Agamemnon's refusal of Chryses ends with a κρατερὸς μῦθος, “a stern command”. The line recurs when he sends the heralds for Briseis (1.326) and in Achilles' retelling (1.379).

{{quote:a9c48215-7151-5ef4-9d33-f0df529c0e81}}

Chryses obeys the μῦθος out of fear. Achilles “did not disobey the μῦθος of Athena” (1.221). Nestor recalls that the heroes of his youth “obeyed my μῦθος” (1.273). Zeus orders Hera to sit down and obey his μῦθος (1.565), and she does.

In Achilles' retelling, Agamemnon “threatened a μῦθος, which has now been carried out” (1.388). Zeus tells Hera not to hope to know all his μῦθοι, “counsels” (1.545), and she answers, “What a μῦθος you have spoken!” (1.552).

The verb is used twice. Calchas is asked to “explain” (μυθήσασθαι) the wrath of Apollo (1.74), and Agamemnon asks whether the gods have made Achilles a spearman so that insults may rush forward for him to speak (1.291).
