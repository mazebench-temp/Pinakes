summary: In Book 1 anaphora shapes the landing at Chryse, four verses beginning with ἐκ (1.436–439), and Agamemnon's complaint about Achilles (1.287–289).
---

{{quote:8cb93d6b-f2ca-52f2-89d1-d38c3d435361}}

Four consecutive verses begin with ἐκ (“out”): out go the mooring-stones, out go the men, out goes the hecatomb, and out of the seafaring ship steps Chryseis. Each time the preverb stands apart from its verb (ἐκ … ἔβαλον, ἐκ … βαῖνον, ἐκ … βῆσαν, ἐκ … βῆ), so that the word “out” leads every line. The list ends with its goal: the girl is home ([[seafaring-ship]], [[sailing-type-scene]]).

{{quote:5c22e331-9e21-54c7-90a1-2df2accf5bba}}

Agamemnon's complaint about Achilles builds a rising series on πᾶς (“all”): above all others, to have power over all, to be lord over all, to give orders to all. The repetition, which also changes case ([[polyptoton]]), swells to its point: “but there is one, I think, who will not obey him.”
