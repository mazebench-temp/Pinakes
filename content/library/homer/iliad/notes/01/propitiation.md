---
summary: The Achaeans appease Apollo with the return of Chryseis, a hecatomb and the paean; on Olympus, Hephaestus urges Hera to appease Zeus with gentle words.
---

Calchas gives the terms for Apollo: return the girl without ransom and lead a [[hecatomb|hecatomb]] to Chryse, “then we might appease him and win him over” ({{ref:Iliad 1.100}}; see [[wrath-of-apollo]]).

{{quote:a851338d-f3c4-52d7-b72b-a704dd88c7d0}}

Agamemnon sends a leader to offer the sacrifices and appease the Far-Worker ({{ref:Iliad 1.147}}); Achilles reminds Thetis that he was the first to urge it ({{ref:Iliad 1.386}}); and Odysseus announces the purpose at Chryse, “so that we may appease the lord” ({{ref:Iliad 1.444}}). The rite ends as it should: all day the young men seek the god's favor (ἱλάσκοντο) with song and dance, and Apollo is glad as he listens ({{ref:Iliad 1.472-474}}; see [[sacrifice|sacrifice]] and [[paean|paean]]).

The same idea returns on Olympus. Hephaestus urges his mother not to defy Zeus but to approach him with gentle words: “then at once the Olympian will be gracious (ἵλαος) to us” ({{ref:Iliad 1.582-583}}). Men appease the gods with sacrifice and song; the gods appease one another with soft words and a cup of nectar (see [[cupbearing|cupbearing]]).
