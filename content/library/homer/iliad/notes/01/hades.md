Ἄϊδι προΐαψεν: "hurled down to Hades". In Homer "Hades" is always the god; the realm is his "house" (δόμος Ἀΐδαο). The dative here means "to Hades", into his keeping. The proem contrasts the ψυχαί (souls) that go below with αὐτούς ("the men themselves"), the bodies left for dogs and birds. For Homer the self is the body. See [[psyche]].
