---
summary: Chryses' two prayers to Apollo, built alike and asking opposite things, frame the plague (1.35–43, 1.450–457). Achilles' appeal to Thetis (1.351–357) follows the same frame.
---

{{quote:d8c3119c-4499-55ce-888d-a22a1df49a69}}

The first prayer follows a silence. Driven off by Agamemnon, Chryses walks without a word along the shore, goes apart, and prays “long and hard” (πολλά). He calls on Apollo by his cult places and by the title Smintheus ([[smintheus]]). He claims a temple roofed and thigh-pieces burned, and asks for revenge. Apollo hears and comes down ({{ref:Iliad 1.44-52}}).

{{quote:4f241a2b-8fbf-51a9-a40e-1c80f233ca55}}

The second prayer is public, spoken at the altar with lifted hands, in the middle of a sacrifice ([[sacrifice-type-scene]]). The invocation repeats the first ({{ref:Iliad 1.37-38}} = {{ref:Iliad 1.451-452}}), without Smintheus. The claim is the first prayer itself: “once before you heard me”. The request reverses it: ward off the ruin. The closing line is the same ({{ref:Iliad 1.43}} = {{ref:Iliad 1.457}}). The two prayers enclose the plague ([[ring-composition]]; [[wrath-of-apollo]]).

{{quote:4a5b02ac-42a8-51c8-93d2-b39cd413e9b6}}

Achilles' appeal follows Chryses' first prayer. He too has been wronged by Agamemnon. He too withdraws to the shore and prays “long and hard” (πολλὰ … ἠρήσατο, as πολλὰ … ἠρᾶθʼ at {{ref:Iliad 1.35}}). But he names no titles and claims no service. He says only “Mother”, and his claim is the short life she gave him. The close is a prayer's close: “his lady mother heard him” ({{ref:Iliad 1.357}}). Then the goddess comes in person ([[supplication-type-scene]]).
