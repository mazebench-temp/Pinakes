---
summary: The sword [[Achilles]] begins to draw against [[Agamemnon]] and thrusts back into its sheath at [[Athena]]'s word. Book 1 calls it ξίφος and φάσγανον, and names its silver hilt (κώπη).
---

When Agamemnon threatens to take Briseis, Achilles is torn: whether to draw the sharp sword “from beside his thigh”, break up the assembly and kill the son of Atreus, or to check his anger. He has begun to draw his great sword from its [[sheath|sheath]] when [[Athena]] catches him by the [[hair|hair]] (see [[athena-restrains-achilles]]).

{{quote:b485c2e3-8a51-5d8d-815e-3b7920979a60}}

Athena tells him not to draw the sword but to fight with words ({{ref:Iliad 1.210-211}}). He obeys. He holds his heavy hand on the silver hilt (see [[gold-and-silver]]) and pushes the sword back into the sheath.

{{quote:eedaec2f-9e8c-5e95-a6ef-a4fc6856197e}}

The half-drawn sword is the turning point of the quarrel: from here on it is a fight of words (see [[words-and-violence]]).
