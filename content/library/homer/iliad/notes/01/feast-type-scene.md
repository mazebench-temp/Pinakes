---
summary: Book 1 ends with two feast days, the Achaeans' at Chryse and the gods' on Olympus, joined by a repeated line (1.468 = 1.602).
---

{{quote:67088b24-5e40-5999-96a9-458152a4aae0}}

At Chryse the meat of the sacrifice becomes the meal, and the pattern runs in order. The song is a hymn: the young men sing the [[paean|paean]] to Apollo all day, and he listens with pleasure ({{ref:Iliad 1.472-474}}). At sunset they sleep beside the ship ({{ref:Iliad 1.475-476}}).

The gods feast too. Zeus and all the gods are away at a feast with the Ethiopians ({{ref:Iliad 1.423-424}}; [[zeus-among-the-ethiopians]]). On Olympus the quarrel of Zeus and Hera threatens “the good feast” ({{ref:Iliad 1.575-579}}). Hephaestus makes peace and pours the nectar, going round from left to right (ἐνδέξια), and the gods laugh to see him bustle through the hall ([[cupbearing|cupbearing]]; [[laughter-of-the-gods]]).

{{quote:57cd0576-9118-5203-923c-c01942e7b421}}

The line of the human feast returns word for word ({{ref:Iliad 1.602}} = {{ref:Iliad 1.468}}). Then the divine feast adds to it. The verb of lacking takes two more objects: no heart lacked “the beautiful lyre that Apollo held”, nor the Muses who sang ([[lyre|lyre]]; [[muses]]). At Chryse, men sing to Apollo. On Olympus, Apollo plays and the Muses sing. The day ends as it did at Chryse, with sunset and sleep ({{ref:Iliad 1.605-611}}).

Both feasts follow a conflict. The first comes after Apollo's anger has ended, the second after the quarrel of Zeus and Hera.
