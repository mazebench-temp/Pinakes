---
summary: Achilles calls the assembly of Book 1 on the tenth day of the plague (1.54–58). The quarrel breaks its order, and the two enemies dissolve it together (1.304–305).
---

{{quote:0475e6d6-e2a6-56fe-81e7-48b3d0cfae6b}}

The summons comes from Achilles, not the king, because Hera put it in his mind. The gathering line follows, and Achilles is the first to rise. Calchas and Agamemnon rise and sit in turn by the rule ({{ref:Iliad 1.68}}, {{ref:Iliad 1.101}}).

Then the order gives way. The two men answer each other directly. Achilles swears by the scepter in his hand, throws it to the ground, and sits ({{ref:Iliad 1.234-246}}). Nestor does not simply rise; he “springs up” (ἀνόρουσε, {{ref:Iliad 1.248}}).

{{quote:1c14338d-e575-57b0-80bf-68f5b5388194}}

No single speaker closes the meeting. The two enemies “stood up” together (ἀνστήτην, a dual) and dissolved it. The dual recalls the proem, where the same two “stood apart in strife” (διαστήτην, {{ref:Iliad 1.6}}; [[proem]]). From then on Achilles stays away from the assembly, “where men win glory” ({{ref:Iliad 1.490}}; [[where-men-win-glory]]). See [[assembly|assembly]].

The gods meet too. When Zeus enters his house, they rise from their seats ([[rising-for-zeus]]), and Hephaestus “began to speak” among them ({{ref:Iliad 1.571}}).
