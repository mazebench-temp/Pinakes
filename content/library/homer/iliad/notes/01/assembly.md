---
summary: Achilles calls the assembly in which the quarrel breaks out; it breaks up in anger, and he stays away from it thereafter.
---

Book 1 shows an assembly from beginning to end (see [[quarrel-of-achilles-and-agamemnon]]).

**Calling.** On the tenth day of the plague it is [[Achilles]], not the king, who calls the people to assembly, because [[Hera]] put it in his mind ({{ref:Iliad 1.54-55}}).

**Speaking.** When the men have gathered (ἤγερθεν ὁμηγερέες, {{ref:Iliad 1.57}}), each speaker stands to speak and sits when he is done: “He spoke thus and sat down, and among them stood up Calchas” ({{ref:Iliad 1.68}}, and again at {{ref:Iliad 1.101}}). [[Nestor]] is the “clear-voiced orator (ἀγορητής) of the Pylians” ({{ref:Iliad 1.248}}). Achilles holds the [[scepter|scepter]] as he speaks and throws it down when he has finished ({{ref:Iliad 1.234}}, {{ref:Iliad 1.245}}).

{{quote:074d8836-e618-57af-9049-8c0bf04be156}}

**Breaking up.** Achilles considers breaking up the assembly by force and killing Agamemnon ({{ref:Iliad 1.191}}). Instead the two men stand up and dissolve it (λῦσαν δ᾽ ἀγορήν).

{{quote:1c14338d-e575-57b0-80bf-68f5b5388194}}

**Absence.** Afterward Achilles sits by his ships, going neither to the assembly, “where men win glory” (κυδιάνειρα), nor to war ({{ref:Iliad 1.490-491}}; see [[achilles-withdraws]]). Men win honor there with words, as in battle with deeds.

The gods meet in council too: in Zeus's house Hephaestus “began to speak” (ἀγορεύειν) among them ({{ref:Iliad 1.571}}).
