Achilles weeps as he prays; his mother rises and sits by him as he weeps; and she answers him weeping. The same phrase for three moments makes the scene one of shared grief. Homeric heroes weep openly, and in the poem's terms Achilles' tears for the loss of his prize and of Briseis, which begin when he bursts into tears and withdraws from his companions (δακρύσας, {{ref:Iliad 1.349}}), are no sign of weakness.
