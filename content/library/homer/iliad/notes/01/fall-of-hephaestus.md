---
summary: When Hephaestus once tried to defend his mother from Zeus, Zeus seized him by the foot and hurled him from heaven. He fell all day and landed on Lemnos at sunset, barely alive, where the Sintian men took care of him.
---

Like Nestor's story, it is a paradigm, told to make someone yield (see [[mythological-paradigm]]). Hephaestus tells Hera not to provoke Zeus, because he could not protect her: he tried once, and this is what happened. It works. Hera smiles, the gods laugh, and the feast resumes. The same kind of story that failed with Achilles and Agamemnon succeeds on Olympus (see [[hephaestus]] and [[quarrel-on-olympus]]).

The story also explains Hephaestus himself. His fall and his bond with Lemnos are told, but not his lameness: Book 1 does not connect the two, and in Book 18 he tells of another fall, which his lameness caused (18.395–397).
