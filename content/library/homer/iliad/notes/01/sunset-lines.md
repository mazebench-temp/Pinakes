summary: The formulas for sunset and the end of day are used four times in Book 1: at Chryse, in Hephaestus' fall, and twice at the feast of the gods.
---

The sunset lines close the book's two days of feasting, the Achaeans' at Chryse and the gods' on Olympus, and both are followed by sleep. Between them stands a darker use. Hephaestus tells how Zeus threw him from heaven, and he fell “all day long” until sunset, landing on Lemnos with little life left in him ({{ref:Iliad 1.590-594}}; [[fall-of-hephaestus]]). The span of a god's fall is the span of a god's feast: from morning to the setting of the sun. The companion formula for daybreak is [[dawn-line]].
