---
summary: Chryseis must be returned “unbought, unransomed” (1.99), and Thetis wishes her son could sit by the ships “free of tears and free of harm” (1.415).
---

{{quote:8ed20d5f-e256-577a-9a01-a398f5a32a97}}

ἀπριάτην (“unbought,” from πρίαμαι, “buy”) and ἀνάποινον (“unransomed,” from ἄποινα, “ransom”) state Apollo's terms with legal exactness. Agamemnon refused the ransom; now he must give the girl back for nothing.

{{quote:5bf1c082-d60b-58e7-8589-23d9e5bb0fb7}}

ἀδάκρυτος (“tearless,” from δάκρυ, “tear”) and ἀπήμων (“unharmed,” from πῆμα, “harm”) name what Thetis wishes for her son and knows he cannot have ([[swift-fated]]).

Zeus' pledge in the same book uses a series of negations in the same spirit: nothing he confirms with a nod can be taken back, or prove false, or go unfulfilled (οὐ … παλινάγρετον οὐδ’ ἀπατηλὸν οὐδ’ ἀτελεύτητον, {{ref:Iliad 1.526-527}}). Only the last of the three is a privative, but the triple denial has the same force ([[dark-brows]]).
