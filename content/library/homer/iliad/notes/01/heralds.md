---
summary: Agamemnon's heralds Talthybius and Eurybates take Briseis from Achilles, who greets them as “messengers of Zeus and of men”.
---

Agamemnon sends his heralds and attendants, [[Talthybius]] and [[Eurybates]], to take Briseis from Achilles' [[hut|hut]] ({{ref:Iliad 1.320-325}}; see [[taking-of-briseis]]). They go unwillingly, and when they find Achilles they stand in fear and awe and cannot speak. Achilles speaks first and absolves them: the fault is Agamemnon's, not theirs (see [[messengers-of-zeus]]).

{{quote:34ac6799-621d-586c-9840-ac207a71fbdc}}

He then makes them witnesses of his wrong ({{ref:Iliad 1.338}}; see [[oath|oath]]), and they lead Briseis away ({{ref:Iliad 1.347-348}}, {{ref:Iliad 1.391}}). Achilles' greeting states the rule: the heralds carry the king's will, and the anger belongs to the king.
