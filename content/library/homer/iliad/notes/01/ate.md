---
summary: [[Achilles]] wants [[Agamemnon]] to learn “his own blind folly” through the Achaeans' defeat (1.412).
---
{{quote:4dd5afa9-8033-5b4c-9ce6-7a90e33c200d}}

The word occurs once in the book, at the end of Achilles' request to Thetis. He wants Zeus to pen the Achaeans against the sea “so that they may all have joy of their king”, and so that Agamemnon may learn his ἄτη, “in that he paid no honor to the best of the Achaeans”. The recognition is meant to come through suffering. Agamemnon is to learn his folly from the deaths of his men. Patroclus repeats the two lines when he leads the Myrmidons out to battle (16.273–274).

{{quote:12f468d0-42bb-52ff-ae76-a666a61d8a6b}}

The state that ἄτη names has already been described without the word. Agamemnon, Achilles tells the heralds, “rages with a ruinous mind” and cannot look before and after. See [[before-and-after]] and [[foresight]].
