The formulas close eighteen speeches: {{ref:Iliad 1.33}}, 43, 68, 101, 188, 219, 245, 326, 345, 357, 428, 446, 457, 511, 528, 568, 584, 595.

The second half of the verse shows what the words did, and the pattern creates meaning. Two lines far apart share their shape:

{{quote:5b24299e-3fbc-563b-9c53-b39867cf645d}}

{{quote:03fc2f03-04ce-5236-8372-43df0c402283}}

A king threatens; the one threatened is afraid (ἔδεισεν) and obeys. Chryses before Agamemnon and Hera before Zeus are joined by one formula of fear.

The other closings turn to a god's hearing ({{ref:Iliad 1.43}}, {{ref:Iliad 1.457}}; [[hear-me]]), to grief (188), to obedience (345), to tears (357; [[shedding-tears]]), to a silence (511; [[cloud-gatherer]]), and to a smile (595).
