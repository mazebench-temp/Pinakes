---
summary: The arrows of [[Apollo]], which carry the plague through the Achaean camp for nine days. Book 1 calls them by four names: ὀϊστός, ἰός, βέλος and κῆλα.
---

[[Chryses]] prays that the Danaans may pay for his tears “with your arrows” ({{ref:Iliad 1.42}}). Apollo comes down in anger, and the arrows clatter on his shoulders as he moves. He lets fly an arrow (ἰόν), strikes first the [[mules|mules]] and the [[dogs|dogs]], then turns “his piercing shaft” (βέλος ἐχεπευκές) against the men, and the [[pyres|pyres]] of the dead burn without stop. For nine days “the arrows of the god” (κῆλα θεοῖο) range through the army.

{{quote:5fa0cc63-3d21-5929-b2cf-0af39eb523f4}}

Achilles tells the story to Thetis in much the same words ({{ref:Iliad 1.382-384}}).

The arrows are the plague. Homer names the sickness (νοῦσος, {{ref:Iliad 1.10}}; λοιμός, {{ref:Iliad 1.61}}) but shows it only as the god shooting (see [[wrath-of-apollo]]). Elsewhere a sudden death without illness is also the work of the “gentle arrows” of Apollo for men and of Artemis for women (Iliad 24.758–759; Odyssey 11.172–173).
