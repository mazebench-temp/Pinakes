λοιγόν comes five times in the book, always with a verb of warding off, and it links three stories.

{{quote:99e80523-680c-5023-a09e-c6d418bd5974}}

The first ruin is the plague. Achilles hopes that Apollo may be willing to keep it away (1.67). Calchas says the god will not thrust it away until the girl is returned (1.97). At the end, Chryses prays that Apollo “now at last ward off this shameful ruin from the Danaans” (1.456).

{{quote:525753dd-2805-5022-a6c7-447e8e67d3b5}}

The second ruin is still to come. Achilles calls the heralds to witness in case there is ever again need of him “to ward off shameful ruin from the others”. The Achaeans' safety will depend on the man they have dishonored.

{{quote:db6da6f6-9650-5755-bc47-e206904e329a}}

The third is in the past. Thetis once, alone among the immortals, warded off shameful ruin from Zeus, when the other Olympians wanted to bind him. The formula makes mother and son alike: she saved Zeus, and he is needed to save the Achaeans.

The adjective turns the grand word to domestic use. Zeus calls Thetis' request λοίγια ἔργα, “a sorry business”, because it will set him at odds with Hera (1.518), and Hephaestus says the same of his parents' quarrel (1.573).
