Briareus does nothing but sit beside Zeus, "exulting in his glory" (κύδεϊ γαίων), and the plotting gods take fright. His presence alone restores order.

### The language of gods and men

"The gods call him Briareus, but all men call him Aegaeon." This is the first of several places where Homer gives a divine name beside the human one. The others:

- the river Xanthus, called Scamander by men (Iliad 20.74);
- a bird the gods call chalkis and men kymindis (14.291);
- a mound the gods call the tomb of Myrine and men Batieia (2.813–814);
- the herb moly, known by that name to the gods (Odyssey 10.305).

In each case the gods' word seems to be the older or truer one.

### Mightier than his father

ὃ γὰρ αὖτε βίην οὗ πατρὸς ἀμείνων: "for he is mightier even than his father". Homer does not say who the father is. Some ancient readers identified him as Poseidon, which gives the story an edge: Poseidon was one of the plotters, and the champion Thetis brought was stronger than he.
