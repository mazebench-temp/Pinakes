---
summary: The carrion birds that feed on the dead in the poem's opening lines, and the birds whose flight the seer Calchas reads, “by far the best of the bird-seers”.
---

**Carrion.** The wrath made the dead heroes “the spoil of dogs and of every bird” ({{ref:Iliad 1.4-5}}; see [[dogs|dogs]]). The Alexandrian editor Zenodotus is reported to have read “a feast (δαῖτα) for the birds” in place of “every” (πᾶσι; see [[zenodotus-feast-for-birds]]).

**Omens.** [[Calchas]] is “by far the best of the bird-seers” (οἰωνοπόλων, from οἰωνός + πολέω, “be busy with”; {{ref:Iliad 1.69}}; see [[divination|divination]]).
