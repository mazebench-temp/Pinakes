---
summary: Sacrificial victims for Apollo in Book 1, offered with bulls and with lambs.
---

Goats appear in Book 1 only as victims, and always in pairs. Chryses has burned for Apollo the thighs “of bulls and goats” ({{ref:Iliad 1.41}}). Achilles wonders whether the god might accept “the savor of lambs and unblemished goats” ({{ref:Iliad 1.66}}). The army sacrifices hecatombs “of bulls and goats” on the shore ({{ref:Iliad 1.316}}; see [[bulls|bulls]], [[lambs|lambs]] and [[sacrifice|sacrifice]]). The goats of {{ref:Iliad 1.66}} are τέλειοι, “complete, unblemished”: a victim had to be whole and sound.
