---
summary: The noun θάνατος ("death") occurs once in Book 1, when Achilles doubts that the army will even escape it. The verb θνῄσκω ("die") runs through the plague and Achilles' threat, and the adjective θνητός ("mortal") is what separates men from the [[immortals]].
---

The plague is counted in deaths. Hera pities the Danaans "because she saw them dying" (1.56), and Achilles later tells Thetis how the men "died one after another" as Apollo's arrows ranged through the camp (1.383). Achilles opens the first assembly by doubting that the army will even survive the war:

{{quote:4001330b-8bf0-5e31-8dca-2ca592516128}}

In his oath he turns death into a threat against his own side. The Achaeans will long for him when "many fall dying at the hands of man-slaying Hector" (1.242–244; see [[longing-for-achilles]]).

Among the gods, men's deaths become a reason not to quarrel. Hephaestus warns his parents against fighting "for the sake of mortals" (1.574), and the book ends with the gods laughing at a feast. See [[gods-and-mortals]].
