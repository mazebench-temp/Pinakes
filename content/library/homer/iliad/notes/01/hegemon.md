---
summary: ἀρχός appears twice, both times for the commander of the ship that takes Chryseis home.
---

When Agamemnon agrees to return Chryseis, he orders a ship made ready: “let one man of counsel be in command” (εἷς δέ τις ἀρχὸς ἀνὴρ βουληφόρος ἔστω, 1.144). The man is Odysseus, who “went as commander” (ἐν δʼ ἀρχὸς ἔβη, 1.311; see [[return-of-chryseis]]). The shape of Agamemnon's line, “let one man be …”, returns in Book 2, when Odysseus tells the army “let there be one ruler” (εἷς κοίρανος ἔστω, 2.204; see [[one-ruler]]).
