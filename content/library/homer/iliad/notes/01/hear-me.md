summary: Chryses' two prayers to Apollo begin with “hear me” and end with “and Phoebus Apollo heard him” (1.37, 1.43; 1.451, 1.457).

Both of Chryses' prayers open with the same two lines and close with the same line ({{ref:Iliad 1.451-452}}, {{ref:Iliad 1.457}}), so that the whole story of the plague is enclosed by the formula ([[ring-composition]]). The first prayer asks for the Danaans to be punished, the second for their relief, and the god hears both ([[god-of-the-silver-bow]]).

The same close marks Achilles' appeal to his mother: ὣς φάτο δάκρυ χέων, τοῦ δ’ ἔκλυε πότνια μήτηρ, “so he spoke, shedding tears, and his lady mother heard him” ({{ref:Iliad 1.357}}). The son's appeal to Thetis is framed as a prayer, and it is heard.
