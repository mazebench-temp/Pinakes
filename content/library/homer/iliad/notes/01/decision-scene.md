---
summary: Achilles' choice whether to kill Agamemnon (1.188–194) follows the pattern up to the moment of decision, which Athena's arrival cuts off.
---

{{quote:0bb7e262-87ee-5871-8a31-044c537bb731}}

Agamemnon's threat is the shock, and grief (ἄχος) comes over Achilles ([[achos]]). The alternatives are unequal. The first takes two lines and three acts: draw the sword, break up the assembly, kill the son of Atreus. The second takes one line: “or to check his anger and restrain his spirit”.

The resolution never comes in the usual form. The narrator keeps the deliberation going, “while he was pondering this” (ἧος ὃ ταῦθʼ ὥρμαινε), and the hand is already moving: he “was drawing” (ἕλκετο) his sword. The two imperfects run side by side. Then an aorist cuts in: ἦλθε δʼ Ἀθήνη, “Athena came” ([[divine-epiphany]]).

The goddess takes up the second alternative in its own words. Achilles weighed whether to “check” (παύσειεν) his anger; Athena says, “I came to stop (παύσουσα) your fury” ({{ref:Iliad 1.207}}). The choice is still his. He answers that “a man must heed the word of you two, however angry he may be” ({{ref:Iliad 1.216-218}}). See [[athena-restrains-achilles]].
