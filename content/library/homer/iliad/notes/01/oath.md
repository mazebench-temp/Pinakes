---
summary: Achilles swears by Apollo to protect Calchas, and swears his great oath by the scepter that the Achaeans will long for him.
---

**For Calchas.** The seer will speak only if Achilles swears to protect him ({{ref:Iliad 1.76}}). Achilles swears by the god whose will is in question, οὐ μὰ γὰρ Ἀπόλλωνα, “by Apollo”: no one shall lay hands on Calchas while Achilles lives, “not even if you mean Agamemnon” ({{ref:Iliad 1.86-91}}).

**By the scepter.** In the quarrel Achilles swears “a great oath”, ναὶ μὰ τόδε σκῆπτρον, “yes, by this [[scepter|scepter]]”: as surely as the cut staff will never sprout again, the Achaeans will long for him when they are dying at Hector's hands (see [[longing-for-achilles]]). The rest of the Iliad fulfills it.

{{quote:1b73d328-2188-54c3-8c30-379e02d1134c}}

**Witnesses.** When the [[heralds|heralds]] take Briseis, Achilles makes them witnesses (μάρτυροι) before gods, men and the king ({{ref:Iliad 1.338-344}}).

Zeus's nod to Thetis is not an oath, but it binds like one: what he nods to cannot be taken back ({{ref:Iliad 1.524-527}}; see [[tekmor]]).
