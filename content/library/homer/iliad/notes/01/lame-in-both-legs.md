---
summary: The description of Hephaestus at the end of Book 1 (1.607): “the renowned god, lame in both legs” — or, on another reading, “strong in both arms.”
---

When the gods go to their beds at sunset, each goes to the house “where for each of them the renowned god, lame in both legs, / Hephaestus, had built a house with his cunning skill” (1.607–608; [[hephaestus]], [[with-cunning-skill]]). It is the epithet's first use in the poem; see the entry for the day on which the gods laughed at him (1.597–600).
