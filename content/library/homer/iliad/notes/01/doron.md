---
summary: [[Athena]] promises [[Achilles]] three times as many gifts in compensation; Achilles calls his stolen prize a gift; the hecatomb is “gifts” for [[Apollo]].
---
{{quote:0d7d40ff-20aa-51e8-84e9-216e3910c4cc}}

Athena restrains Achilles with a promise of compensation. Someday three times as many splendid gifts will be laid before him because of this outrage (see [[triple-gifts-foretold]]). The promise is kept in the offer of Book 9 (9.121–157), which Achilles refuses, “His gifts are hateful to me” (9.378), and in the gifts he finally receives in Book 19.

{{quote:c1c3c45e-e251-5961-95aa-32a8015d3a2a}}

Achilles calls the prize a gift. It is “far better”, he sneers, to strip the gifts from any man who speaks against you than to arm for battle. A γέρας is the army's gift, and taking it back is a public insult.

{{quote:2d5c10cf-57ae-5e9b-a249-ccd039c264f8}}

In Achilles' retelling, the Achaeans are sending Chryseis home and bringing gifts to the lord Apollo. The hecatomb is a gift to a god.
