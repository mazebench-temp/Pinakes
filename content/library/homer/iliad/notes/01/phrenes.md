---
summary: Hera puts the assembly into [[Achilles]]' mind; [[Agamemnon]]'s φρένες are filled with fury, and Achilles says he rages “with a ruinous mind”.
---
{{quote:407539e1-5dc0-5e69-acb2-57262a80458a}}

The physical picture is clearest here. Agamemnon's φρένες, dark all round, are filled with fury, and his eyes are like blazing fire.

The φρένες receive what the gods put into them. Hera “had put it in his mind” of Achilles to call the assembly (1.55). They are the seat of intelligence: Agamemnon compares Chryseis to his wife in form, stature, mind and handiwork (1.115). They are where a man ponders (1.193), understands (1.333) and stores advice: “lay it to heart” (1.297). Grief reaches them (1.362); pleasure fills them, as when Apollo delights in the paean (1.474); fear lives there (1.555).

{{quote:12f468d0-42bb-52ff-ae76-a666a61d8a6b}}

Achilles' charge against Agamemnon is a charge about his φρένες. A man with ruinous φρένες cannot see what is coming. See [[ate|ἄτη]] and [[foresight]].
