---
summary: Chryses wishes the Achaeans a safe homecoming, [[Achilles]] threatens to sail home to Phthia, and [[Agamemnon]] tells him to go; the only homecoming completed in Book 1 is that of the captive [[Chryseis]].
---
The noun νόστος itself does not occur in Book 1.

{{quote:6a6afeef-58b5-5d8b-bd8e-4e829fdb6667}}

The first speech of the poem wishes the Achaeans a safe return: may the gods grant you to sack Priam's city and come home well.

{{quote:f8109070-d328-5c58-890d-b0885df16866}}

Achilles begins the assembly by imagining a failed homecoming. If war and plague continue, they will be driven back and sail home.

{{quote:68b19839-4949-5478-a95d-f010beaf0b23}}

In the quarrel, going home becomes a threat. Achilles will go to Phthia, since it is far better to go home than to stay dishonored. Agamemnon calls the bluff: go home with your ships and companions and lord it over the Myrmidons (1.179–180). Achilles does not go.

The book's one completed homecoming is not a warrior's. Chryseis, taken far from her native land (1.30), is carried back to Chryse and placed in her father's arms (1.439–447; see [[return-of-chryseis]]). Agamemnon, who meant to take her home to Argos, will have a homecoming of his own: in the Odyssey his ghost tells how Aegisthus and Clytemnestra killed him on his return (*Odyssey* 11.405–434; see [[clytemnestra-and-the-return]]).
