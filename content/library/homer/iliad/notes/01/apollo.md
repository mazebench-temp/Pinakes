---
summary: He answers the prayers of his priest [[Chryses]] twice in Book 1, first to send the plague and then to end it.
---

Apollo starts the action. His priest is refused and prays for revenge. Apollo comes down from Olympus in a rage, and the only simile of his descent makes him dark, not bright:

{{quote:645d889b-e9d0-536a-bbd6-41139541d281}}

He shoots the mules and dogs first, then the men, for nine days (1.50–53). Calchas names the cause: the god is angry "because of his priest" (1.94). When the girl is returned and the hecatomb offered, Chryses prays a second time, in the same words, to reverse the first prayer:

{{quote:91625f1f-64ce-58a9-878f-1be2851091f6}}

The Achaeans spend the day singing the paean, the healing song to Apollo, "and his heart was glad as he listened" (1.474). The next morning he sends them a following wind home (1.479).

He appears last on Olympus, playing the lyre at the gods' feast while the Muses sing (1.603–604). The same god who brought the plague ends the book with music.

The wrath of Apollo comes before the wrath of Achilles and is its model: a slighted honor, a prayer, a withdrawal of help, and death among the Achaeans. See [[wrath-of-apollo]].
