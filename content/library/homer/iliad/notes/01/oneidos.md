---
summary: [[Athena]] licenses [[Achilles]] to reproach Agamemnon with words instead of the sword; Agamemnon complains of Achilles' insults; Zeus fears Hera's abusive words.
---
{{quote:57be2fec-5a6e-5e24-ab28-83fceba92a6e}}

Athena makes the distinction the book depends on. Achilles may not draw his sword, but he may reproach Agamemnon “with words”, telling him how it will be. Insult becomes the licensed substitute for violence.

{{quote:b0be40fd-4d4b-5b65-850e-c4be54ebbff3}}

Achilles uses the license at once: wine-sodden, dog-eyed, deer-hearted, never brave enough to arm for battle or go on ambush.

{{quote:7957db0b-cb98-5b60-a8b6-bc288dea4247}}

Agamemnon asks whether, if the gods made Achilles a spearman, his insults must therefore rush forth to be spoken. The verb προθέουσιν is obscure, and the translation renders it literally (see [[obscure-protheousin]]).

On Olympus Zeus foresees that Hera will provoke him “with abusive words” (ὀνειδείοις ἐπέεσσιν, 1.519).
