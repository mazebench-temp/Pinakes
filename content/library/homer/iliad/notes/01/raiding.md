---
summary: The Achaeans have been sacking the towns of the Troad, and the quarrel is over the spoils; the Trojans, says Achilles, never raided him.
---

**Why Achilles fights.** Achilles asks how anyone can obey Agamemnon's orders “to go on a raid” or to fight ({{ref:Iliad 1.151}}). A raid on one's herds or harvest was a reason for war, and he has none: the Trojans never drove off his [[cattle|cattle]] or [[horses|horses]] or ravaged his harvest in [[Phthia]]. He came to win satisfaction for Menelaus and Agamemnon (see [[war-for-menelaus]]).

{{quote:9c0f84f3-8bab-54d7-b3f3-13215574b17f}}

**Sacked towns.** Most of the prizes in Book 1 come from the towns of the Troad: what the army plundered has all been divided ({{ref:Iliad 1.125}}), and Achilles gets less whenever the Achaeans sack a Trojan town ({{ref:Iliad 1.163-164}}; see [[achilles-raids]]). Chryseis was taken when they went to Thebe, “and sacked it, and brought everything here” ({{ref:Iliad 1.366-367}}; see [[sack-of-thebe]] and [[division-of-spoils|division of spoils]]). Achilles later counts twelve towns sacked from the ships and eleven by land (Iliad 9.328–329).

The same verbs name the war's goal: Chryses wishes the Achaeans to sack the city of Priam ({{ref:Iliad 1.19}}), and Achilles promises repayment when Zeus grants them to sack Troy ({{ref:Iliad 1.128-129}}).
