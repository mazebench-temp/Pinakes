---
summary: Achilles retells the quarrel to Thetis (1.365–392), repeating whole lines from the narrator's account of Chryses' plea (1.12–16, 1.22–25).
---

Achilles begins by asking why he should tell his mother what she already knows ({{ref:Iliad 1.365}}), and then tells her anyway. His account (366–392) covers the sack of Thebe, Chryses' plea, the plague, Calchas' prophecy, and the seizure of Briseis. Where it covers the same ground as the narrator, it repeats him:

{{quote:4222711f-5bae-5ab7-931b-8ca043137aa1}}

Lines 372–375 repeat 13–16 word for word, and 376–379 repeat 22–25. Line 371 reshapes line 12 around an added epithet ([[bronze-armored]]).

The differences are as telling as the repetitions. Achilles begins further back, with the sack of Thebe and the division of its spoils (366–369), and so frames the story as a matter of prizes. He compresses Agamemnon's threat into a single formula ([[stern-command]]) and the plague into a few lines. He adds that Apollo heard Chryses “for he was very dear to him” (381). And he gives himself a part the narrator did not: “at once I was the first to urge that we appease the god” (386). The retelling is the story as Achilles lives it.

Other repetitions in the book work the same way. Thetis restates Achilles' request to Zeus in her own terms, keeping one of his lines intact: “he has taken his prize and keeps it, having seized it himself” ({{ref:Iliad 1.356}}, {{ref:Iliad 1.507}}). Athena repeats the narrator's lines about Hera's sending her ({{ref:Iliad 1.195-196}}, {{ref:Iliad 1.208-209}}; [[white-armed]]), and Hera speaks the narrator's line about Thetis ({{ref:Iliad 1.538}}, {{ref:Iliad 1.556}}; [[silver-footed]]).
