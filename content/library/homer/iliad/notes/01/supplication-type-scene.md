---
summary: Thetis performs the full rite before Zeus, clasping his knees and chin (1.500–516), as Achilles told her to. The book opens with Chryses' plea to the army, the same act without the touch.
---

**Chryses.** The poem opens with a supplication without the touch.

{{quote:da958e60-0546-506c-9375-4f70add0023e}}

The priest brings a boundless ransom ([[apoina]]) and holds Apollo's [[fillets|fillets]] on a golden [[scepter|scepter]]. He begs (λίσσετο) the whole army but touches no one; the god's emblems take the place of the gesture. He asks them to act “in reverence for the son of Zeus” (ἁζόμενοι). The army assents; Agamemnon refuses: “the scepter and the fillet of the god may not protect you” ({{ref:Iliad 1.28}}). The rejected suppliant turns to the god ([[prayer-type-scene]]).

**The instructions.** Achilles tells his mother what to do and on what claim: “if ever in the past you have gladdened the heart of Zeus by word or deed” ({{ref:Iliad 1.394-395}}). He recalls how she freed Zeus from his bonds ([[binding-of-zeus]]) and ends, “Remind him of this now; sit beside him and clasp his knees” ({{ref:Iliad 1.407}}). She promises, “I will clasp his knees” (γουνάσομαι, {{ref:Iliad 1.427}}).

**Thetis and Zeus.** She finds Zeus sitting apart and performs the whole rite.

{{quote:9ddc5d69-31b1-59bc-94fe-c81394392a38}}

{{quote:d7244f28-e947-5002-a343-51566e31a6dc}}

Her claim takes one line, “by word or deed” ([[word-or-deed]]); Zeus knows the story.

{{quote:11854ed9-4cf0-5f6b-9a62-8d5b50785dff}}

Zeus is silent. The narrator takes the usual formula of reply and negates it: τὴν δʼ οὔ τι προσέφη, “said nothing to her” ([[silence]]). The words repeat as the gesture does: “as she had clasped his knees, so she held on” (ὡς ἥψατο … ὣς ἔχετο), “grown fast” to him (ἐμπεφυυῖα). Her second plea is shorter: nod or refuse, “so that I may know well how far I am the most dishonored goddess of all” ({{ref:Iliad 1.514-516}}). She will not let go without an answer. Zeus nods ([[thetis-supplicates-zeus]]). Hera later describes it in the words of Achilles' instruction: “she sat beside you and clasped your knees” ({{ref:Iliad 1.557}}).

**A variation.** Achilles' own appeal to his mother is a prayer. He stretches out his hands toward the sea and complains of his dishonor ({{ref:Iliad 1.351-356}}). The touch comes from the other side: the goddess rises, sits before him, and strokes him with her hand ({{ref:Iliad 1.360-361}}). The half-line “she sat down before him” (καί ῥα πάροιθʼ αὐτοῖο καθέζετο) also begins her supplication of Zeus ({{ref:Iliad 1.500}}). The son's plea to his mother becomes the mother's plea to the father of the gods.
