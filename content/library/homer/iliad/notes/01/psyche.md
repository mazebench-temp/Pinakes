The word occurs once in the book, in the proem. The wrath hurled many ψυχαί to Hades, but made “the men themselves” (αὐτούς) the spoil of dogs and birds. For Homer the self is here the body; what goes to Hades is only a shade. The ψυχαί are called ἴφθιμοι, “mighty”, a strength that belonged to the men while they lived.

Iliad 11.55 has almost the same words with κεφαλάς (“heads”) in place of ψυχάς, and an ancient reading reported in the scholia has κεφαλάς here too. In the next line, Zenodotus is reported to have read “a feast for the birds” (see [[zenodotus-feast-for-birds]]).

The living person's life, in this book, is his [[thymos|θυμός]]: Agamemnon may lose his θυμός (1.205), and Hephaestus fell to Lemnos with little θυμός left in him (1.593).
