---
summary: The plague dead burn on crowded pyres (1.52), and the proem imagines the opposite fate, bodies left for dogs and birds (1.4–5).
---

In Book 1 cremation is seen only in mass. Apollo's arrows strike the camp, “and the pyres of the dead burned on, thick and constant” ({{ref:Iliad 1.52}}). The line gives no name and no mourning, only the fires.

{{quote:2370c7e0-aad2-5a38-82fa-6000b23c58d4}}

The proem imagines the opposite: the wrath made the dead “the spoil of dogs and of every bird” ({{ref:Iliad 1.4-5}}), the fate of the unburied (see [[dogs|dogs]] and [[birds|birds]]).
