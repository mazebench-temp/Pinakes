---
summary: He ends Book 1 by making peace between his parents, recalling how Zeus once threw him from heaven to Lemnos, and pouring nectar amid the gods' unquenchable laughter.
---

The quarrel between Zeus and Hera has frozen the feast. Hephaestus speaks first to the gods, then to his mother:

{{quote:5a2706ca-b8f2-53c2-8513-42e204015bf9}}

He puts a cup in her hand and tells her to endure, because he could not help her if Zeus struck her. He speaks from experience:

{{quote:b2b207ac-383e-519a-93c3-e8c20c7e4877}}

Then he limps around the hall pouring nectar like a cupbearer, and the gods laugh:

{{quote:9de4049e-186b-52fd-a09a-f664fd928840}}

The joke rests on his lameness and on the role he takes. Serving wine was a young, graceful attendant's task, like Hebe's among the gods (4.2–3). The book ends in the houses he built for every god:

{{quote:bd66a6ca-06cb-5f62-a42a-5d07ef699e73}}
