---
summary: ἄλγεα names both the countless sorrows that [[Achilles]]' wrath laid on the Achaeans and the sorrows that [[Apollo]]'s plague brings them.
---
{{quote:9273ec58-af8b-5446-8afb-7a836c58981c}}

The wrath “laid countless ἄλγεα on the Achaeans”. It is the first consequence the poem names.

{{quote:c404ee97-5f02-5ebd-8196-eae5cbadd078}}

Calchas uses the same word for the plague: for the dishonor of his priest, the Far-Shooter “has given ἄλγεα and will give still more”. Agamemnon repeats it back to him (1.110). Apollo's wrath and Achilles' wrath have the same effect, and the proem's word connects them.

Odysseus, returning Chryseis, speaks of the “grievous sorrows” (πολύστονα κήδεα) that Apollo has sent on the Argives (1.445). κῆδος, “care, trouble”, is a near synonym.
