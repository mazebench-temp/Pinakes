{{quote:8fcb2ba6-e97c-5f56-a2c0-932c3112f138}}

Nestor's case for Agamemnon rests on a gift from Zeus. The scepter-bearing king has been given κῦδος, and so a greater share of honor.

{{quote:57820f3f-204d-5568-85ec-bea9f4b2bb23}}

Achilles uses the honorific and spoils it with the next word. Agamemnon is κύδιστε, “most glorious”, and φιλοκτεανώτατε, “most covetous of all men”.

Briareus, summoned by Thetis, sits beside Zeus “exulting in his glory” (κύδεϊ γαίων, 1.405), and the other gods shrink back from him.

{{quote:71e9711c-dc57-5f22-adf4-496eadd25fb2}}

The assembly is called κυδιάνειρα, “where men win glory”, at the moment Achilles stops going to it. His withdrawal cuts him off from the place where glory is won by speech as well as from the battle where it is won by arms.
