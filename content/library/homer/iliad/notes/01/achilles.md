---
summary: Book 1 shows how his wrath began: the assembly he calls, the quarrel with Agamemnon over the prizes, and his withdrawal to the ships.
---

**He calls the assembly.** On the tenth day of the plague, prompted by Hera, Achilles gathers the army and proposes that a seer be asked (1.54–67). When Calchas fears a king's anger, Achilles swears to protect him, "not even if you mean Agamemnon" (1.85–91).

**The quarrel.** Agamemnon agrees to give Chryseis back but demands a new prize. Achilles tells him that everything has been divided already and that he will be repaid when Troy falls (1.122–129). The argument turns to insult. Achilles says he has no quarrel with the Trojans and fights only for Agamemnon's honor, and threatens to sail home:

{{quote:2a3687b1-9562-582c-b239-6a91648e3855}}

**The sword.** When Agamemnon says he will take Briseis, Achilles half draws his sword and is stopped by [[Athena]] (1.188–222). He obeys, but his words grow harsher: "wine-sodden drunk, with the eyes of a dog and the heart of a deer" (1.225).

**The oath.** He swears on the scepter that the Achaeans will long for him when Hector is killing them (1.233–244). Then he flings the scepter to the ground. See [[longing-for-achilles]].

{{quote:32bf3f32-ab12-5dda-bb11-10b5d2fe4f77}}

**The taking of Briseis.** He receives Agamemnon's heralds without blaming them and has Patroclus bring her out. Then he calls them to witness that he may never again defend the Achaeans (1.334–344).

**Thetis.** Alone by the sea, he weeps and calls his mother, tells her the whole story, and asks her to get Zeus to help the Trojans (1.348–412). The request is terrible: he asks for his own side to be slaughtered, "so that they may all have joy of their king" (1.410).

**Withdrawal.** The book leaves him sitting by his ships, wasting away his heart and longing for battle (1.488–492).
