---
summary: Two mothers act in Book 1, both goddesses. Thetis grieves for her son's short life, and Hera is comforted by hers, Hephaestus. Leto is named as Apollo's mother.
---

{{quote:c18fe73f-76d2-5a14-8a2e-e5fcccfe9f61}}

Achilles prays to his "dear mother" and calls her by her title: μῆτερ, "Mother".

{{quote:a6f77150-33f6-5182-89c2-433d10204a2a}}

Nestor's reminder: "a goddess was the mother who bore you".

{{quote:9cd3933c-86e6-5f93-a533-3d3a22feffa8}}

Hephaestus speaks up for "his dear mother, white-armed Hera", and hands her the cup with μῆτερ ἐμή, "my mother" (1.586).

The mothers of Book 1 protect their sons, and the sons protect their mothers. Thetis goes to Zeus for Achilles; Hephaestus once tried to defend Hera and was thrown from heaven for it (1.590–594). See [[mothers-and-sons]].
