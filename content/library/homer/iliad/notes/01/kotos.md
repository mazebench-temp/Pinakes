summary: [[Calchas]] fears the κότος of a king; [[Agamemnon]] says he does not care about [[Achilles]]' resentment.
---

{{quote:50697479-23fc-5582-baed-856434e97255}}

The seer explains why he needs protection before he names the cause of the plague (see [[a-kings-grudge]]). A king who is angry with a lesser man may digest his χόλος for the day, but he keeps κότος afterwards. Calchas is right about the man he fears. Agamemnon greets him as a “prophet of evils” (1.106).

{{quote:72dacd98-c076-57ea-87e1-af753d30b092}}

Agamemnon dismisses Achilles' resentment in two words, οὐδʼ ὄθομαι κοτέοντος, “nor do I mind your resentment”. It is the kind of anger Calchas described, now aimed at the king himself, and the poem will show that he should have minded it.
