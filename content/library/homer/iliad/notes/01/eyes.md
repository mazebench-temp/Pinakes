---
summary: Eyes show anger and shamelessness: Agamemnon's eyes “like blazing fire”, the blazing eyes at Athena's epiphany, and Achilles' taunt, “with the eyes of a dog”.
---

Book 1 has three words for the eyes: ὄσσε (a dual, “the two eyes”), ὄμματα and ὀφθαλμοί.

**Anger.** Agamemnon rises in the assembly full of fury, “and his eyes were like blazing fire” ({{ref:Iliad 1.104}}). When Athena appears to Achilles, “her eyes blazed terribly” ({{ref:Iliad 1.200}}); the Greek does not say whose eyes, and some readers take them to be Achilles' (see [[whose-eyes-blaze]]).

{{quote:e7897fe9-de29-5f2b-9e34-091af596a409}}

**Shamelessness.** Achilles calls Agamemnon a man “with the eyes of a dog and the heart of a deer” ({{ref:Iliad 1.225}}; see [[dog-eyes-deer-heart]]), and earlier “dog-face” (κυνῶπα, {{ref:Iliad 1.159}}; see [[dogs|dogs]]). A dog's look is the look of one who has no shame (see [[anaideia]]).

**Sight.** Hephaestus fears he may see his mother beaten “with my own eyes” ({{ref:Iliad 1.587}}).

Several epithets in Book 1 are built on ὤψ, “eye, face”: gray-eyed Athena (γλαυκῶπις), ox-eyed Hera (βοῶπις), and the bright-eyed Chryseis and Achaeans (ἑλικῶπις, ἑλίκωπες).
