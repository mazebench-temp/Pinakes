---
summary: The lyre [[Apollo]] plays at the gods' feast at the end of Book 1, while the [[Muses]] sing. The god who began the book with his bow ends it with his lyre.
---

At the close of Book 1 the gods feast all day, and no one's heart lacks “the beautiful lyre that Apollo held”, nor the Muses, who sing in turn (see [[feast|feast]]).

{{quote:10140377-58e9-545a-a85d-3caf9c126cf0}}

Apollo entered the book as the archer of the plague ({{ref:Iliad 1.43-52}}; see [[bow|bow]]) and leaves it as the musician of the feast. The Homeric Hymn to Apollo gives him both at birth: “let the lyre and the curved bow be mine” (line 131).
