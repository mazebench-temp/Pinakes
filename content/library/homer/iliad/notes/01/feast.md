---
summary: At Chryse the sacrificial meat becomes a feast where “no one's heart lacked an equal share”; on Olympus the gods' feast survives the quarrel of Zeus and Hera.
---

**At Chryse.** The meat of the [[sacrifice|sacrifice]] becomes the meal: “they feasted, and no one's heart lacked an equal share of the feast” ({{ref:Iliad 1.468}}). The equal feast stands against the quarrel in the camp, which began over a share of the spoils (see [[division-of-spoils|division of spoils]]).

{{quote:222a8840-7bc4-5fd9-9dbe-33c321fe2114}}

**Among the gods.** Zeus and all the gods are away for twelve days at a feast with the blameless [[Ethiopians]] by Oceanus ({{ref:Iliad 1.423-424}}; see [[zeus-among-the-ethiopians]]). On Olympus, Hephaestus warns that the quarrel of Zeus and Hera will spoil “the good feast” ({{ref:Iliad 1.575-579}}). After he has served the nectar, the gods feast all day to Apollo's lyre and the Muses' song, and the line of the feast at Chryse returns: no one's heart lacked an equal share ({{ref:Iliad 1.601-604}}).

{{quote:a4a16f28-c808-5412-9390-986084543dde}}

The poem's opening gives the grim reverse of a feast, the dead as spoil for dogs and birds; Zenodotus read there “a feast for the birds” ({{ref:Iliad 1.5}}; see [[zenodotus-feast-for-birds]]).
