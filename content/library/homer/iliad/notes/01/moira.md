summary: In Book 1 the family appears in “fairly” (κατὰ μοῖραν), in Nestor's share of honor (ἔμμορε), and in [[Achilles]]' fate as ὠκύμορος, “swift-fated”.
---
{{quote:61fe4540-fc21-5739-86ce-b33d8cfe12c3}}

Nestor speaks of honor as a portion. The scepter-bearing king “has had no ordinary share (ἔμμορε) of honor”.

{{quote:3205ba55-4a0c-5367-b288-2c5c2badec4c}}

Agamemnon grants that Nestor has spoken κατὰ μοῖραν, “fair and right”, and then refuses the advice.

{{quote:ee00f60b-70a0-554c-b4a4-d40bcf98aa91}}

{{quote:12f86521-5d0b-5f1a-8902-220a1ef22b34}}

μόρος appears in the compound ὠκύμορος, “swift-fated” (ὠκύς, “swift”, and μόρος). Thetis says it to her son (1.417), and to Zeus she calls him ὠκυμορώτατος, “swifter to his fate than any other man” (1.505). The adjective ὠκύς serves twice for Achilles: he is πόδας ὠκύς, swift-footed, and he is swift to his death.
