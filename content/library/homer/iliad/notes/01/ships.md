---
summary: The black ships of the Achaeans, drawn up on the shore before Troy, are the object named most often in Book 1. They are the camp, the way home, and the place where Achilles prays the Achaeans will be penned in and killed.
---

**The camp.** The Achaeans live beside their beached ships, among their [[hut|huts]] (see [[shore]]). [[Chryses]] comes “to the swift ships” ({{ref:Iliad 1.12}}), Apollo shoots “apart from the ships” ({{ref:Iliad 1.48}}), and the [[assembly|assembly]] breaks up “beside the ships” ({{ref:Iliad 1.305}}).

**The way home.** Achilles threatens to sail home to [[Phthia]] “with my curved ships”, and Agamemnon tells him to go ({{ref:Iliad 1.169-170}}, {{ref:Iliad 1.179}}).

**The ship to Chryse.** One black ship takes [[Chryseis]] and the [[hecatomb|hecatomb]] home, with twenty [[oars|oarsmen]] and Odysseus in command. Its landing and return are told step by step (see [[return-of-chryseis]] and [[sailing-type-scene]]).

{{quote:a2ac1dff-c337-51bc-9afc-7d4e41b9e2f3}}

**The trap.** Achilles asks Thetis to have Zeus pen the Achaeans “among their ships' [[stern|sterns]]” ({{ref:Iliad 1.409}}), and Hera guesses that Zeus has promised to destroy many “beside the ships” ({{ref:Iliad 1.559}}). The army's only way home becomes the place where it is trapped (see [[plan-of-zeus]]).
