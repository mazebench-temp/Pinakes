---
summary: The gods “who live forever” quarrel “for the sake of mortals” and then feast and laugh. Book 1 sets the ease of the Olympians beside the suffering they cause and watch.
---

Book 1 moves between two worlds. On earth men die of plague, quarrel over prizes and weep by the sea. On Olympus the gods quarrel too, but their quarrel ends in a feast.

## Ease

Hera tells Zeus that he always devises what he pleases “quite undisturbed” (εὔκηλος, 1.554). While Achilles waits by the ships, Zeus and all the gods are away at a feast among the blameless Ethiopians by the stream of Oceanus, and will not be back for twelve days (1.423–425; see [[zeus-among-the-ethiopians]]).

{{quote:bf2a637e-9b2f-545e-b3e6-c12458bde8cd}}

Hephaestus puts the gods' view plainly. It is not to be endured that Zeus and Hera should quarrel “for the sake of mortals” and spoil the feast, “since worse things prevail” (see [[worse-things-prevail]]). The fate of the Achaeans, which Zeus has just sealed with a nod, is for Hephaestus a reason not to argue.

## Laughter

{{quote:af42aa99-a0c3-5b6f-a996-5f62ff8cb500}}

The quarrel dissolves in “unquenchable laughter” at Hephaestus bustling about as cupbearer. See [[homeric-laughter-idiom]].

## Feast and sleep

{{quote:a4a16f28-c808-5412-9390-986084543dde}}

The feast on Olympus answers the feast at Chryse. Both end with the same line, “no one's heart lacked an equal share of the feast” (1.468, 1.602), and both have music: the young men of the Achaeans sing the paean to Apollo (1.472–474), and on Olympus Apollo plays the lyre while the Muses sing. Mortals feast to appease a god; the gods feast for pleasure. Then they go to sleep in the houses Hephaestus built them (1.605–611; see [[houses-of-the-gods]]). Only in the next book do we learn that Zeus lay awake, pondering how to honor Achilles and destroy many Achaeans (2.1–4).
