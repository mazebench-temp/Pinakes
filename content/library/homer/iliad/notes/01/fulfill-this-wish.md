It occurs three times: in Chryses' first prayer ({{ref:Iliad 1.41}}), in his second ({{ref:Iliad 1.455}}), and in Thetis' supplication ({{ref:Iliad 1.504}}).

{{quote:7f93b3a8-d1e6-5f70-9e22-082302e66058}}

{{quote:1e128d8c-1859-5afb-84ab-90a5fd13dad9}}

Each time the formula follows a claim of past service: Chryses' offerings (“if ever I roofed a temple … if ever I burned fat thigh-pieces”), Apollo's past favor (“once before you heard me”), and Thetis' past help to Zeus (“if ever I helped you among the immortals by word or deed”; see [[word-or-deed]]). The pattern gives the priest's prayer and the goddess's supplication the same form ([[supplication-type-scene]]). And the wish that Thetis asks Zeus to fulfill, the defeat of the Achaeans, matches the first wish that Chryses asked of Apollo, their punishment.
