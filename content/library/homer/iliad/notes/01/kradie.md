---
summary: [[Achilles]] says [[Agamemnon]] has “the heart of a deer”, and asks whether [[Thetis]] ever gladdened the heart of [[Zeus]].
---

{{quote:19e7913b-3269-5f8d-841b-23bf303ad812}}

Achilles calls Agamemnon a man “with the eyes of a dog and the heart of a deer”. The dog stands for shamelessness, the deer for timidity. Agamemnon himself uses the image of timid fawns to rebuke his men in Book 4 (4.243).

{{quote:3ee679d6-60cc-5bb8-b034-4413cba90a0b}}

Achilles asks Thetis to go to Zeus “if ever you gladdened the heart of Zeus by word or deed”. The line looks forward to her own prayer, “if ever among the immortals I helped you by word or deed” (1.503–504).
