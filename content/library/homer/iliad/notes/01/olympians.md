The gods are named as a body in the book's first speech and at its close:

{{quote:ba7e7dd8-a1d5-5da5-853a-02d3445e5bc0}}

Ὀλύμπια δώματ' ἔχοντες: "who have their homes on Olympus"

{{quote:65c7c133-5754-5a17-a62b-bc69c4b46201}}

θεοὶ αἰὲν ἐόντες: "the gods who live forever". Their defining difference from men is that they do not die.

{{quote:7ae841a3-358c-5b8c-a123-0b4d0f224872}}

Ὀλύμπιοι: the Olympians. Here three of them conspire against their king.

{{quote:f641428b-7b0a-5263-bf43-314449ac461b}}

They stand when their father enters, a gesture of order at the moment the book's second quarrel begins.

{{quote:2311e385-03ef-5479-9d62-5650186bec67}}

Οὐρανίωνες: "the heavenly ones", from οὐρανός, sky

{{quote:98f85422-da83-500b-8166-f3b7a9a378b6}}

μάκαρες θεοί: "the blessed gods". Their laughter is unquenchable. See [[laughter-of-the-gods]].
