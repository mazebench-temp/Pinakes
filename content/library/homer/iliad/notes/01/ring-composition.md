Between them lie the plague, the assembly, the quarrel, and the embassy. The frame is exact and the content reversed: the first prayer asks Apollo to strike, the second to stop; the first rests its claim on the priest's service, the second on the god's past favor ([[prayer-type-scene]], [[fulfill-this-wish]]). Each is followed by the same verse, ὣς ἔφατ’ εὐχόμενος, τοῦ δ’ ἔκλυε Φοῖβος Ἀπόλλων ({{ref:Iliad 1.43}}, {{ref:Iliad 1.457}}; [[hear-me]]).

**Nestor's speech.** Nestor argues from his own past in a ring ([[mythological-paradigm]]):

- A: Obey, for you are both younger than I (πίθεσθ’, 259).
- B: I once kept company (ὡμίλησα) with better men than you (260–261).
- C: The heroes of old and their fight with the mountain beasts (262–268; [[catalogue-of-lapiths]]).
- B′: I kept company (μεθομίλεον) with them; no man now alive could fight them (269–272).
- A′: They heeded my counsel and obeyed (πείθοντο); so you obey too (πίθεσθε, 273–274).

{{quote:c2ca65e6-f432-5d75-a26c-905c18cbf9df}}

The repeated verbs of obedience and of company mark the ring, and the example at the center gives the exhortation its weight.

**The proem.** The first seven lines begin with Achilles (Πηληϊάδεω Ἀχιλῆος) and end with him (δῖος Ἀχιλλεύς), with the wrath and its consequences between ([[proem]]).

**The book as a whole.** Book 1 is built of matched parts. A father's plea for his daughter sets the story going, and a mother's plea for her son carries it to Olympus. The quarrel of Agamemnon and Achilles in the assembly is answered by the quarrel of Zeus and Hera. The divine quarrel echoes the human one in its very phrases ([[no-help-to-you]], [[the-worse-for-you]], [[a-sorry-business]]), and it ends where the human one could not, in a shared feast ([[equal-feast]], [[feast-type-scene]]).

Critics have traced a ring on the scale of the whole poem as well. Books 1 and 24 both turn on a father's plea for his child and on ransom, both count intervals of twelve days, and the verse for the twelfth dawn recurs from one to the other ({{ref:Iliad 1.493}}; Il. 24.31; see [[dawn-line]]).
