---
summary: No prayer in Book 3 opens with “hear me”. Its “hear me” is the plural κέκλυτε, spoken to the armies by Hector, Menelaus and Priam, and at the end by Agamemnon to the Trojans.
grc: [κέκλυτε]
passages:
  - 3.86-94 | Hector to both armies
  - 3.455-460 | Agamemnon to the Trojans
---

κέκλυτε is the reduplicated aorist imperative of κλύω, the verb of the prayer's κλῦθί μευ, in the plural: “hear me”, said to many. In Book 3 it opens speeches to the armies. The whole verse κέκλυτέ μευ Τρῶες καὶ ἐϋκνήμιδες Ἀχαιοί, “Hear me, Trojans and well-greaved Achaeans”, addresses both sides at once, as only a truce allows. Hector opens his announcement of the duel with it (3.86), and Priam his farewell before he drives back to the city (3.304). Menelaus, answering Hector, begins “Hear me now as well” (κέκλυτε νῦν καὶ ἐμεῖο, 3.97).

At the end Agamemnon turns to the enemy alone: κέκλυτέ μευ Τρῶες καὶ Δάρδανοι ἠδʼ ἐπίκουροι, “Hear me, Trojans and Dardanians and allies” (3.456). It is the address with which Trojan speakers open their own assemblies in later books, and the Achaean king uses it to claim the victory and demand Helen back (3.456–460; see [[single-combat]]).

None of the book's prayers to Zeus opens with κλῦθί μευ; they begin with his name and titles (3.276, 3.298, 3.320, 3.351; see [[prayer-type-scene]]).
