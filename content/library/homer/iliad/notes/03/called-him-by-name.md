Elsewhere the half-line follows a gesture of tenderness: Thetis strokes her weeping son (1.361), and Dione her wounded daughter (5.372). Here it follows amazement (θάμβησεν). What comes out of the formula of closeness is not a name but δαιμονίη, “strange one” ([[strange-one]]), and the speech is a rebuke. Helen knows the goddess well enough to accuse her of deceit and to tell her to go and sit beside Paris herself (3.399–412). See [[divine-epiphany]].
