---
summary: Atreus is named only in his sons' patronymic. In Book 3 “the son of Atreus” is Agamemnon at the oath and Menelaus in the duel.
---

The patronymic is used six times of each brother. Agamemnon is “the son of Atreus” when Helen names him (3.178), when Priam blesses him (3.182) and measures Odysseus against him (3.193), when he cuts the lambs' hair and swears the oath (3.271, 3.275), and when he claims the victory (3.461). Menelaus is “the son of Atreus” through the duel (3.347, 3.350, 3.361, 3.364) and in his search for Paris (3.449), and Paris shrinks back “in fear of the son of Atreus” (Ἀτρέος υἱόν, 3.37). Where no name is given, the scene decides which son is meant: the king at the sacrifice, the husband in the duel. See [[son-of-atreus]] and [[atreidae]].
