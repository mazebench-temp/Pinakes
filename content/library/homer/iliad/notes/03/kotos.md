**The grudge of two men.** When the two men have armed and walked into the middle, “They stood close to one another on the measured ground, brandishing their spears, in bitter resentment of each other” (ἀλλήλοισιν κοτέοντε, 3.344–345). The participle is dual: the resentment is mutual. κότος is anger kept until it can be paid back, and Menelaus has kept his for the length of the war. The grudge of the whole war stands in the measured ground in the persons of the two men it concerns. See [[single-combat]].

**Surly.** Antenor, remembering the embassy, says that when Odysseus rose to speak and stood with his eyes on the ground, “you would have said he was some surly fellow (ζάκοτον), and a fool as well” (3.220). ζάκοτος is formed with the intensive prefix ζα-, “very”, and means “very resentful, sullen”. Odysseus looked like a man nursing a grudge, and was about to persuade. See [[phrenes]].
