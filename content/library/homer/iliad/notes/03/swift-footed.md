---
summary: Book 3 gives the feminine formula to Iris as she speaks to Helen, and the plain adjective ὠκύς to the horses of Priam's chariot.
grc: [ὠκέας ἵππους]
en: [swift]
---

Iris comes to Helen in the likeness of Laodice, “and standing near, swift-footed Iris spoke to her” (3.129). The verse is the one that introduced her words to the Trojan assembly in Book 2 ({{ref:Iliad 2.790}}). The goddess of swift errands brings Helen to the wall.

The adjective of the formula, ὠκύς, is also the standing epithet of horses. Priam and Antenor drive “the swift horses” through the Scaean gates to the plain (ὠκέας ἵππους, 3.263). See [[horses]].
