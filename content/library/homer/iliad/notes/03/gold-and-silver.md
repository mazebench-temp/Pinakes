**Golden Aphrodite.** Paris asks Hector not to throw in his face “the lovely gifts of golden Aphrodite” (χρυσέης Ἀφροδίτης, 3.64). Gold is the metal of the gods, and this is Aphrodite's standing epithet (see [[golden]]).

**Golden cups.** For the oath the herald Idaeus carries a shining mixing bowl “and golden cups” (χρύσεια κύπελλα, 3.247–248), from which the wine is drawn and poured out to the gods (3.295–296). The rite that binds the armies is done with precious vessels (see [[cup]] and [[mixing-bowl]]).

**Silver arms.** Paris arms for the duel with greaves “fitted with silver ankle-pieces” (ἀργυρέοισιν ἐπισφυρίοις, 3.331) and slings on his “silver-studded sword” (ξίφος ἀργυρόηλον, 3.334). Menelaus' sword is silver-studded too, and it shatters in three and four pieces on Paris' helmet (3.361–363). The fine metal does not decide the fight. See [[arming-scene]], [[greaves]] and [[silver-studded]].
