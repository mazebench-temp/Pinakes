---
summary: The Trojan elders compare Helen to “the immortal goddesses,” and the prayers at the oath call on Zeus “and the other immortal gods.”
---

The elders on the wall see Helen coming and say to one another that no one can blame the Trojans and Achaeans for suffering so long for such a woman: “she is terribly like the immortal goddesses to look on” (3.156–158; [[no-blame]]). The comparison is the measure of her beauty and the ground of their judgment.

At the oath the immortals are witnesses. The curse over the wine is addressed to “Zeus, most glorious, greatest, and you other immortal gods” (3.298), and Priam, leaving, says that “Zeus knows, I suppose, and the other immortal gods, for which of the two the end of death is fated” (3.308–309). The gods know what the men who swear the oath cannot.
