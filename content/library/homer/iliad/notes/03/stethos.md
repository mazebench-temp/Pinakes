The chest holds the heart and the voice, and armor covers it.

**The mind within.** Paris tells Hector, “so fearless is the mind in your breast” (ἐνὶ στήθεσσιν, 3.63), and Aphrodite's summons “stirred the heart in her breast” in Helen (3.395; see [[thymos]]).

**The voice.** Odysseus stood like a man who knew nothing until “he let the great voice go from his chest” (ὄπα τε μεγάλην ἐκ στήθεος εἵη, 3.221), and his words fell like snowflakes (see [[snowflakes-of-winter]]). Priam had noted that Odysseus was “broader in the shoulders and chest” than Agamemnon (στέρνοισιν, 3.194), a different word for the chest.

**The armor.** Paris arms for the duel: “second, he put on the corselet around his chest” (περὶ στήθεσσιν, 3.332), the corselet of his brother Lycaon (see [[corselet]] and [[arming-scene]]).

**The goddess.** Helen knows Aphrodite in her disguise by “her lovely breasts” (στήθεά θʼ ἱμερόεντα, 3.397), with her neck and her flashing eyes (see [[himeros]] and [[thambos]]).
