---
summary: Iris calls the truce and the coming duel θέσκελα ἔργα, “wondrous doings,” with an adjective related to θεσπέσιος.
grc: [θέσκελα]
---

Iris, in the likeness of Laodice, calls Helen to the wall: “Come here, dear bride, and see the wondrous doings of the horse-taming Trojans and the bronze-armored Achaeans” (3.130–131). θέσκελος is usually explained as θεσ- (“god,” as in θεσπέσιος and θέσφατος) + -κελος (“like,” as in εἴκελος): “godlike, marvelous.” The wonder is a war that has stopped: the men who were bringing tearful Ares on one another now sit in silence, leaning on their shields (3.132–135; [[tearful]]). The Odyssey uses the same phrase, θέσκελα ἔργα, of marvelous deeds and works (Od. 11.374, 11.610).
