---
summary: The patronymic names Zeus once in Book 3, in the narrator's comment that he would not yet fulfill the prayer spoken over the oath.
---

{{quote:77023816-983f-5be9-aff0-dea0c88f39ef}}

The armies pray that whoever first breaks the oath may have his brains poured out on the ground like the wine, his and his children's, and that their wives be subdued by others. The narrator adds that Zeus will not fulfill it yet. “Not yet” (οὐδʼ ἄρα πω) looks ahead: the Trojans will break the oath in Book 4, and Agamemnon will say that if the Olympian does not fulfill it at once, he will fulfill it in the end (Il. 4.160–162). The verse repeats, in the plural, the narrator's comment on Agamemnon's prayer in Book 2: “So he spoke, but the son of Cronus was not yet going to fulfill his prayer” ({{ref:Iliad 2.419}}).
