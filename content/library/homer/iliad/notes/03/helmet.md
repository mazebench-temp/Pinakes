summary: A helmet holds the lots (3.316), and Paris' helmet decides the duel. Menelaus' sword shatters on its ridge, he drags Paris by it until Aphrodite breaks the strap, and the empty helmet is all he carries off (3.361–378).
passages:
  - 3.361-378 | The sword, the helmet and the strap
---

Book 3 uses three of the helmet's names: κυνέη for the helmet that holds the lots and for Paris' as he arms (3.316, 3.336), κόρυς as Menelaus strikes it and seizes it (3.362, 3.369), and τρυφάλεια for the helmet the strap holds and the empty helmet (3.372, 3.376).

**The lots.** Hector and Odysseus shake the lots “in a bronze-fitted helmet” (ἐν κυνέῃ χαλκήρεϊ, 3.316), and it is Hector “of the gleaming helmet” who shakes them (3.324; see [[lots]] and [[of-the-gleaming-helmet]]).

**Paris' helmet.** He sets on his head “a well-made helmet with a horsehair crest, and the plume nodded terribly above it” (3.336–337; see [[arming-scene]]).

**The ridge.** Menelaus draws his sword and strikes “the ridge of his helmet” (κόρυθος φάλον), and the sword shatters in three and four pieces (3.361–363; see [[sword]]).

**The strap.** Then he seizes Paris by the helmet “thick with horsehair” (κόρυθος ἱπποδασείης; see [[horsehair-crested]]) and drags him toward the Achaeans:

{{quote:92727870-6a35-5b2b-bb3c-c468a6b45c27}}

The strap is πολύκεστος, “richly stitched”, from the root of κεστός, “stitched, embroidered”, the word used of Aphrodite's own embroidered band in Book 14 (κεστὸς ἱμάς, 14.214). It is made “from the hide of an ox slain by force” (βοὸς ἶφι κταμένοιο), and Aphrodite breaks it: “the empty helmet came away in his strong hand” (κεινὴ δὲ τρυφάλεια, 3.375–376). Menelaus whirls it round and flings it among the Achaeans, and his companions pick it up (3.377–378). A helmet with no head in it is the only prize of the duel (see [[divine-rescue]]).
