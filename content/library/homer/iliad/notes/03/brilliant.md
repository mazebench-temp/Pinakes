summary: Book 3 gives δῖος to Agamemnon, Odysseus, Alexander and Aphrodite, and to Helen in the formula δῖα γυναικῶν, “brilliant among women.”
---

Helen's formula closes the verse three times. She answers Priam's questions about Agamemnon (3.171) and about Ajax (3.228, with [[long-robed]]), and she goes into Paris' chamber after Aphrodite has threatened her:

{{quote:d5c749fa-1901-54b4-a1f4-8697210ebe9f}}

The Catalogue gave the same formula to Alcestis ({{ref:Iliad 2.714}}).

The masculine is used of Agamemnon (3.120) and of Odysseus, in the line-end formula δῖος Ὀδυσσεύς (3.205, 3.314), and of Paris. Paris is δῖος at the start of his arming (3.329), and he keeps the epithet even in the mouths of those he has wronged. Menelaus prays for vengeance “on the man who first wronged me, brilliant Alexander” (3.352), and Helen tells Aphrodite that Menelaus has beaten “brilliant Alexander” (3.403). Aphrodite herself is δῖα when she speaks to Helen in the likeness of an old woman and when she answers her in anger (3.389, 3.413).
