Paris carries a sword when he steps out as a champion (3.18), and when he arms he slings his “silver-studded sword of bronze” around his shoulders in the verse of the arming scene (3.334–335; see [[arming-scene]] and [[silver-studded]]). It is the verse Agamemnon used when he dressed after the Dream (2.45).

After the spears, the sword. Menelaus “drew his silver-studded sword, raised it and struck the ridge of his helmet; but on it the sword shattered in three and four pieces (τριχθά τε καὶ τετραχθά) and fell from his hand” (3.361–363). “And now my sword has broken in my hands”, he cries to Zeus (ἄγη ξίφος, 3.367). He fights on with his hands, dragging Paris by the helmet (see [[helmet]]).

Agamemnon's knife, which he uses at the oath, “always hung beside the great sheath of his sword” (πὰρ ξίφεος μέγα κουλεόν, 3.272; see [[knife]] and [[sheath]]).
