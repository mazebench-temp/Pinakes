**Rebuke.** Hector “rebuked” Paris (νείκεσσεν) with shaming words (3.38), and Paris answers, “Hector, since you rebuke me (ἐνείκεσας) fairly and not beyond what is fair” (3.59; see [[aisa]]). The line comes again in Book 6, when Hector finds Paris in his chamber polishing his armor and rebukes him once more (6.333). In Book 2 νεικέω was Thersites' verb for abusing a king; here it is a brother's rebuke, accepted as just.

**The quarrel.** Hector presents the duel to both armies as “the word of Alexander, for whose sake the quarrel (νεῖκος) arose” (3.87). The whole war is a νεῖκος between two men, and the duel puts it back in their hands. Menelaus names it with the other word, ἔρις (“my quarrel”, 3.100; see [[eris]]). See [[words-and-violence]].
