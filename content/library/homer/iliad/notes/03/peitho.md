---
summary: Orders in Book 3 are obeyed at once: Talthybius does not disobey Agamemnon, and Priam's companions quickly obey him; Helen obeys Aphrodite only when threatened.
grc: [ἀπίθησʼ, ἐπίθοντο]
passages:
  - 3.413-420 | Helen obeys
---

The book's formulas of obedience are quick and complete. Agamemnon sends Talthybius to the ships for a lamb, “and he did not disobey brilliant Agamemnon” (οὐκ ἀπίθησʼ, 3.120). Priam shudders at the summons to the oath, tells his companions to yoke the horses, “and they quickly obeyed” (ὀτραλέως ἐπίθοντο, 3.260).

The one person who refuses in Book 3 is Helen. She tells Aphrodite that she will not go to Paris' bed (3.410), and she goes only when the goddess threatens her: “Helen, child of Zeus, was afraid, and she went” (3.418–419; see [[deos]]). The narrator does not say that she obeyed or was persuaded; she went in fear, and a goddess led the way (3.420). See [[obedience-and-persuasion]].
