In the Trojan catalogue the Phrygians come to Troy from far-off Ascania (2.862–863). In Book 3 they belong to Priam's memory. On the wall he tells how he once went to "vine-rich Phrygia" ([[phrygia]]) and saw the Phrygian men in great numbers, with their quick horses:

{{quote:6e7db609-0616-5bc0-8eb4-67136c9689a8}}

αἰολόπωλος = αἰόλος ("quick, glancing") + πῶλος ("foal, horse"): "with quick horses" (see [[with-quick-horses]]). The Phrygians were "the people of Otreus and godlike Mygdon", their kings then ([[otreus]], [[mygdon]]), and they were camped on the banks of the [[Sangarius]]. Priam fought among them as an ally (ἐπίκουρος) when the [[Amazons]] came (see [[priam-and-the-amazons]]). The memory serves his praise of the Achaeans: even the Phrygian host was not as large as the army below the wall (3.190).

Helen names their country once more, as a place where Aphrodite might have another favorite to give her to (3.401).
