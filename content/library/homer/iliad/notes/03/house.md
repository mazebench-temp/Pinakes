summary: Book 3 moves between the plain and the house. Helen weaves in the hall of Paris' house, leaves her chamber for the wall, and is brought back to the high-roofed chamber where Aphrodite has set Paris down.

---

Iris finds Helen “in the hall” (ἐν μεγάρῳ, 3.125), and she hurries out “from her chamber” (ἐκ θαλάμοιο, 3.142) to the wall. There she tells Priam that she wishes she had died before she left “my bridal chamber and my kinsmen” (θάλαμον, 3.174) in Sparta, and remembers Idomeneus as a guest “in our house” (οἴκῳ ἐν ἡμετέρῳ, 3.233). Antenor received Menelaus and Odysseus “in my halls” (ἐν μεγάροισι, 3.207). See [[xenia]].

The terms of the duel are about going home. The winner is to “lead them home”, the woman and the possessions (οἴκαδʼ ἀγέσθω, 3.72, 3.93; see [[nostos]]), and Helen fears that Menelaus “wants to take me, hateful as I am, home with him” (3.404). But the home the book ends in is Paris'. Aphrodite sets him down “in his fragrant, perfumed chamber” (ἐν θαλάμῳ εὐώδεϊ κηώεντι, 3.382), calls Helen “to come home” (οἶκον δὲ νέεσθαι, 3.390), and brings her to “the beautiful house of Alexander” (Ἀλεξάνδροιο δόμον περικαλλέʼ, 3.421) and into “the high-roofed chamber” (ὑψόροφον θάλαμον, 3.423). The θάλαμος, the inner room where the bed stands, is where the book leaves them (see [[bed]]).

The prayer at the lots asks that the guilty man “go down into the house of Hades” (δόμον Ἄϊδος, 3.322). He goes into his own chamber instead.
