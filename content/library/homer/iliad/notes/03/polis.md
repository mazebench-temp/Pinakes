Two cities stand behind Book 3, Troy and the city Helen left. Hector tells Paris that his voyage was “a great grief to your father and your city (πόληϊ) and all the people” (3.50), and Iris puts in Helen a longing “for her former husband, and her city (ἄστεος), and her parents” (3.140).

Troy itself is the city (ἄστυ) to which Hector sends heralds for the lambs and Priam (3.116), and through which the heralds carry the offerings for the oath (3.245). Helen, refusing Aphrodite, asks whether the goddess will lead her on still farther, “to one of the well-peopled cities (πολίων) of Phrygia or of lovely Maeonia” (3.400–401; see [[well-peopled]]).
