---
summary: The lion simile has “the swift dogs,” with the plain adjective ταχύς.
grc: [ταχέες τε κύνες]
---

Menelaus rejoices at the sight of Paris like a hungry lion that finds a carcass and devours it, “even though the swift dogs and the young men in their prime rush at him” (3.25–26; [[lion-on-a-carcass]], [[flourishing]]). The adjective is ταχύς, the ordinary word for speed, rather than the ἀργός of the formula κύνες ἀργοί. See [[dogs]].
