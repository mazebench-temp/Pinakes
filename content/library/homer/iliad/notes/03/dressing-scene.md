---
summary: Book 3 has no dressing scene. Its counterpart is the arming of Paris (3.328–338), which shares the sword verse with Agamemnon's dressing in Book 2; and Helen covers herself twice to go out, in a veil of linen and in her robe.
---

The dressing scene's twin is the arming scene, and Book 3 gives the first full arming of the poem (3.328–338; see [[arming-scene]]). The two share a verse. “And around his shoulders he slung his silver-studded sword” is said of Paris arming for the duel (3.334) as it was of Agamemnon dressing after the Dream (2.45). The last item differs with the purpose: Agamemnon took up the scepter of his fathers to go to the council (2.46), Paris “a strong spear that fitted his grip” (3.338).

The women of the book dress only to go out. Helen “veiled herself in white linen” before she left her chamber for the wall (3.141; see [[veil]]), and she goes back “wrapping herself in her shining white robe” (3.419; see [[robe]]). Paris, after the duel, is “shining with beauty and fine clothes” in his chamber (3.392).
