summary: The formula is given to Priam as he leaves the oath to go back to Troy.

{{quote:01991671-c87d-547d-8896-d1949f23cece}}

Priam has come down to the plain for the oath, and he speaks to both armies as “Priam, descendant of Dardanus” (Δαρδανίδης, 3.303; [[dardanus]]). He will not stay for the duel: he cannot bear to see his dear son fight Menelaus, and he leaves the outcome to Zeus and the other gods. Then “the man equal to the gods put the lambs in the chariot” and drove back to Troy with Antenor (3.310–313). The heroic formula is set beside the old man's fear for his son.
