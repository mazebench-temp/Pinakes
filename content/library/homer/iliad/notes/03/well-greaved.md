---
summary: Book 3 names the Achaeans well-greaved in six lines, most of them in speeches that address or describe both armies at once.
grc: [ἐϋκνήμιδας]
---

The whole-line address κέκλυτέ μευ Τρῶες καὶ ἐϋκνήμιδες Ἀχαιοί, “Hear me, Trojans and well-greaved Achaeans,” opens Hector's announcement of the duel (3.86) and Priam's farewell after the oath (3.304). The Trojan elders pair the two peoples in the same way when they see Helen:

{{quote:476eb6c1-ae21-5073-8975-ce9354c33f2e}}

Both armies watch the duel, “the horse-taming Trojans and the well-greaved Achaeans” (3.343; [[tamer-of-horses]]). At its end Menelaus drags Paris “toward the well-greaved Achaeans” (3.370) and flings the empty helmet among them (3.377). The greaves themselves appear in Paris' arming, “fine ones, fitted with silver ankle-pieces” (3.330–331; [[greaves]]). See [[no-blame]].
