**Breathing fury.** The Trojans come on with clamor like cranes, “but the Achaeans came on in silence, breathing fury” (μένεα πνείοντες, 3.8). Their μένος is held in, not shouted (see [[breathing-fury]] and [[silence]]).

**The lambs.** At the oath Agamemnon cuts the lambs' throats and lays them on the ground, gasping, their life failing, “for the bronze had taken away their strength” (ἀπὸ γὰρ μένος εἵλετο χαλκός, 3.294). μένος is the force that keeps a living thing going; the bronze takes it, as it will take it from whoever breaks the oath (3.298–301; see [[thymos]]).

**Eager to kill.** When Aphrodite has broken the helmet strap, Menelaus flings the empty helmet to his companions “but he sprang at him again, eager (μενεαίνων) to kill him with his bronze spear” (3.379–380). The verb μενεαίνω is formed from μένος. The goddess snatches Paris away before the fury can reach him.
