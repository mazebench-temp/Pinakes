---
summary: Hector bears his epithet twice in Book 3: in Agamemnon's order to let him speak, and as he shakes the lots for the duel.
---

Hector has gone into the middle to hold back the Trojans, and the Achaeans are shooting at him. Agamemnon stops them: “Hold, Argives! Do not shoot, young men of the Achaeans! Hector of the gleaming helmet means to say something” (3.82–83). The enemy commander names him by his formula.

At the lots the narrator gives him the fuller form, μέγας κορυθαίολος Ἕκτωρ: “great Hector of the gleaming helmet shook the lots, looking away; and the lot of Paris leapt out at once” (3.324–325; [[lots]]). Hector's helmet is famous, but in Book 3 the helmet that matters is his brother's, by which Menelaus drags him ([[horsehair-crested]], [[helmet]]).
