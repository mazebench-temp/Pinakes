summary: Hector tells Paris that his voyage brought grief to his father, his city and all the people; Helen names Ithaca, where Odysseus was raised, a δῆμος.

passages:
  - 3.46-51 | Hector on Paris' voyage
---

The word is used in both of its senses in Book 3. Paris' voyage was “a great grief to your father and your city and all the people” (πόληΐ τε παντί τε δήμῳ, 3.50): the δῆμος is the community that pays for one man's act. Helen says that Odysseus “was raised in the land of Ithaca, craggy though it is” (ἐν δήμῳ Ἰθάκης, 3.201): the δῆμος is the land a people lives in. The old men on the tower are δημογέροντες, “elders of the people” (3.149; see [[trojan-elders]]).
