---
summary: Book 3 names each brother by his father six times; the patronymic is Agamemnon's at the oath and on the wall, and Menelaus' in the duel.
grc: [Ἀτρέος υἱὸν]
---

The first use belongs to Menelaus. Paris slips back into the crowd, “in fear of the son of Atreus” (Ἀτρέος υἱόν, 3.37). On the wall the son of Atreus is Agamemnon, named by Helen (3.178), blessed by Priam (ὦ μάκαρ Ἀτρεΐδη, 3.182; [[child-of-fortune]]), and measured against Odysseus (3.193). At the oath he draws the knife, cuts the lambs' hair and prays (3.271–275).

In the duel the patronymic passes to Menelaus. Paris' spear strikes “the son of Atreus” on his shield (3.347), and Menelaus answers, “Menelaus, son of Atreus” (3.350). When his sword breaks, “the son of Atreus” groans to the sky (3.361–364; [[wide-sky]]), and when Paris has vanished “the son of Atreus” ranges the throng like a wild beast (3.449; [[like-a-wild-beast]]). The last verse of the book gives the name back to Agamemnon: “So spoke the son of Atreus, and the other Achaeans applauded” (3.461).
