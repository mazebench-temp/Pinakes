The heralds carry through the city two lambs “and wine that cheers the heart, the fruit of the earth, in a goatskin” (οἶνον ἐΰφρονα καρπὸν ἀρούρης / ἀσκῷ ἐν αἰγείῳ, 3.246–247). ἐΰφρων is “cheering, gladdening”, and καρπὸς ἀρούρης, “the fruit of the plowland”, is a phrase for what the earth yields to men. At the oath the heralds mix the wine in the bowl (3.269–270). After the lambs are killed it is drawn out in cups and poured on the ground (3.295–296), and the curse is spoken over it:

{{quote:77023816-983f-5be9-aff0-dea0c88f39ef}}

In Book 1 wine was poured on the burning thigh-pieces and drunk at the feast (1.462–463, 1.470–471). In Book 3 no one drinks. The gladdening wine, the earth's fruit, is poured back on the earth as the likeness of a man's brains. See [[libation]], [[mixing-bowl]] and [[oath]].
