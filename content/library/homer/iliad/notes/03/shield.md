During the truce the armies sit “leaning on their shields” (ἀσπίσι κεκλιμένοι, 3.135). When Paris arms he takes up his shield in the verse of the arming scene, αὐτὰρ ἔπειτα σάκος μέγα τε στιβαρόν τε, “and then his shield, great and heavy” (3.335; see [[arming-scene]]). It is the first σάκος of the poem; Book 2 used only ἀσπίς.

In the duel both shields are described in the same half-line, ἀσπίδα πάντοσε ἴσην, “balanced on every side” (3.347, 3.356; see [[balanced-on-every-side]]). Paris' spear strikes Menelaus' shield, “but the bronze did not break through, and its point was bent back in the strong shield” (ἀσπίδʼ ἐνὶ κρατερῇ, 3.348–349). Menelaus' spear goes “through the shining shield” (διὰ μὲν ἀσπίδος … φαεινῆς) and on through the corselet (3.357–358). The shield Paris took up as a σάκος is struck as an ἀσπίς: the two words name one shield. See [[spear]] and [[corselet]].
