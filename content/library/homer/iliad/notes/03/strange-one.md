---
summary: Helen addresses Aphrodite as δαιμονίη when the goddess, in disguise, summons her to Paris' bed.
---

{{quote:df8d2ca8-fead-5edf-bcf7-78e1a032b790}}

Helen has recognized the goddess by her neck, her breasts and her flashing eyes, and she speaks to her with the vocative of reproach that Zeus used to Hera in Book 1 ({{ref:Iliad 1.561}}). A mortal speaks to a goddess as a husband speaks to a wife, with intimacy and exasperation. She goes on to tell Aphrodite to sit beside Paris herself and give up the paths of the gods (3.406–409). Aphrodite answers with a vocative of her own, σχετλίη, “wretched woman,” and a threat (3.414–417). See [[helen-and-aphrodite]], [[eperopeuo]].
