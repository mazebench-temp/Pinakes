**Terms of homecoming.** The duel is a bargain about going home. Whoever wins is to “take all the possessions and the woman, and lead them home” (οἴκαδʼ ἀγέσθω, 3.72, 3.93). If Paris wins, the Achaeans “go back to horse-pasturing Argos” (νεέσθων, 3.74, 3.257), and Agamemnon's oath says it plainly: “let us go home in our seafaring ships” (νεώμεθα, 3.283). In Book 2 the army nearly went home against fate (2.155); in Book 3 homecoming is the prize of a duel.

**Helen's two homes.** The old men want Helen gone: “let her go home in the ships” (νεέσθω, 3.159). Then Aphrodite comes to her in disguise: “Come here: Alexander calls you to come home” (οἶκον δὲ νέεσθαι, 3.390), meaning Paris' house in Troy. Helen answers that Menelaus has won and wants to take her “home” (οἴκαδʼ ἄγεσθαι, 3.404), meaning Sparta. The same word points to two houses, and the goddess decides which one she goes to (3.418–423). See [[husbands-and-wives]].
