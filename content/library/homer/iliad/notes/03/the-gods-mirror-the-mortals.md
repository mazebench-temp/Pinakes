summary: The bedroom scene of Book 3 is repeated among the gods in Book 14. Paris, beaten, takes Helen to bed with words of desire that Zeus will use to Hera; and in Book 3 Helen tells Aphrodite to live as a mortal man's wife.

**Paris and Zeus.** After the duel Paris invites Helen to bed: never has desire so wrapped his mind, not even on the first night on Cranae, “as I love you now, and sweet longing takes hold of me” (3.441–446). In Book 14 Hera, wearing Aphrodite's band, comes to Zeus on Ida. “Desire wrapped his shrewd mind” (14.294), as when they first lay together in love, and he invites her to bed with a list of his past loves that ends with Paris' line (14.328 = 3.446). The scene of the man who lost a duel is played again by the king of the gods, and each time love takes someone out of the war: Paris in Book 3, and in Book 14 Zeus himself, who falls asleep while Poseidon helps the Achaeans (14.352–360). See [[eros]], [[himeros]] and [[philotes]].

**A goddess as a wife.** Helen turns the mirror the other way. She tells Aphrodite to give up the paths of the gods, never to return to Olympus, and to sit beside Paris and watch over him “until he makes you his wife, or his slave” (3.406–409). She pictures a goddess in a mortal woman's place, her own. The goddess answers with the power that separates them (3.413–417; see [[gods-and-mortals]]).
