**A witness.** Menelaus asks for “a white ram and a black ewe, for Earth and the Sun” (Γῇ τε καὶ Ἠελίῳ, 3.103–104), and in the oath Agamemnon calls on “rivers and earth” (ποταμοὶ καὶ γαῖα, 3.278) with Zeus, the Sun and the powers below. The oath takes the whole world as its witness: the sky, the sun, the rivers, the earth and what lies under it. See [[oath]] and [[helios]].

**The ground.** The armies lay their armor on the ground, “close to one another, and there was little ground between them” (3.114–115). The formula for it is “the bountiful earth” (χθὼν πουλυβότειρα), where arms are laid down and where Priam and Antenor step from the chariot (3.89, 3.195, 3.265; see [[bountiful-earth]]). Odysseus, before he speaks, fixes his eyes “on the ground” (κατὰ χθονός, 3.217). The wine of the oath is “the fruit of the earth” (καρπὸν ἀρούρης, 3.246), and the lambs, their throats cut, are laid on the ground, gasping (ἐπὶ χθονός, 3.293). Helen came to Troy “from a distant land” (ἐξ ἀπίης γαίης, 3.49; see [[distant-land]]).

The earth that is φυσίζοος (“life-giving”) also keeps the dead, and κάτεχεν (“held fast”) is the verb the Catalogue used of Protesilaus: “the black earth held him fast” (2.699). The brothers lie in their “dear native land”, the phrase that in Book 2 meant the home the army longed for (2.140, 2.454). See [[life-giving-earth]], [[castor]] and [[polydeuces]].
