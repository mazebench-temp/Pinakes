Priam measures all three men by head and shoulders. Some are taller than Agamemnon by a head (3.168); Odysseus is shorter by a head, but broader (3.193–194); Ajax towers above the Argives by his head and his broad shoulders. Helen gives him one line and no story, only two epithets of a defender: πελώριος (“huge”; see [[huge]]) and ἕρκος Ἀχαιῶν (“the bulwark of the Achaeans”; see [[bulwark-of-the-achaeans]]). The text does not say which Ajax this is. The size and the title belong to Telamonian Ajax: later the poem calls him “Telamonian Ajax, the bulwark of the Achaeans” (6.5), and gives him Helen's two epithets again when he goes out to fight Hector (7.211).
