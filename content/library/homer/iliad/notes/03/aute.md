---
summary: Agamemnon shouts aloud (ἄϋσεν) to stop the Achaeans shooting at Hector, who has come out between the armies to speak.
grc: [ἄϋσεν]
en: [shouted]
passages:
  - 3.76-85 | Hector holds back the Trojans
---

Hector goes into the middle holding his spear by the middle, and the Trojans sit down; but the Achaeans keep aiming at him with arrows and stones, “but Agamemnon, lord of men, shouted aloud” (μακρὸν ἄϋσεν, 3.81): “Hold, Argives! Do not shoot, young men of the Achaeans! Hector of the gleaming helmet means to say something” (3.82–83). The king's shout stops the fighting so that a word can be heard, and the armies fall silent (3.84–85). The book began with the opposite sound, the Trojans coming on “with clamor and with cries, like birds” (κλαγγῇ τʼ ἐνοπῇ τʼ, 3.2). See [[silence]].
