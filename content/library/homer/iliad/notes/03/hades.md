Agamemnon's witnesses end with the dead: “and you two who below the earth punish dead men, whoever has sworn a false oath” (3.278–279). The verb τίνυσθον is dual, so a pair of powers is meant. They are usually taken to be Hades and Persephone, whom Homer names together as the powers below who fulfill curses (9.456–457, where Hades is “Zeus of the underworld”, and 9.569). In Agamemnon's oath in Book 19 the Erinyes do this work: they punish men under the earth, “whoever has sworn a false oath” (19.259–260). The oath of Book 3 carries the penalty of perjury past death. See [[oath]].

When the lots are put in the helmet, the people of both armies pray:

{{quote:0115f9fd-4466-5ebf-b227-3529b71e8c61}}

δόμον Ἄϊδος εἴσω: “into the house of Hades”. The prayer asks for a death: the guilty man is to go down to the god's house. See [[psyche]].
