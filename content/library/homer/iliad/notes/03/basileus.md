---
summary: From the wall Priam sees that Agamemnon looks like a king, and Helen names him “both a good king and a strong spearman”; at the oath the heralds pour water over the hands of the kings.
passages:
  - 3.166-183 | Helen names Agamemnon
---

Priam, looking at the Achaeans from the tower, picks out one man by his bearing: others are taller by a head, but he has never seen a man so handsome or so stately (γεραρόν), “for he looks like a king” (βασιλῆϊ γὰρ ἀνδρὶ ἔοικε, 3.170). A king can be recognized by sight. Helen names him and gives him both of the excellences that the quarrel of Book 1 divided between two men: “both a good king and a strong spearman” (βασιλεύς τʼ ἀγαθὸς κρατερός τʼ αἰχμητής, 3.179; see [[a-good-king-and-a-strong-spearman]]).

At the oath the word is used of the leaders of both sides alike: the heralds mix the wine and pour water over the hands “of the kings” (βασιλεῦσιν, 3.270), Agamemnon and Priam among them. See [[kingship-and-authority]] and [[agamemnon]].
