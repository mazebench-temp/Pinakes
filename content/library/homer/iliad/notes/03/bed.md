In Aphrodite's summons Paris is “in his chamber, on the inlaid bed” (δινωτοῖσι λέχεσσι, 3.391), shining with beauty. Helen refuses: “I will not go there, it would be a thing for blame, to share his bed” (κείνου πορσανέουσα λέχος, 3.410–411), for the Trojan women would reproach her. The goddess's threat brings her all the same (3.413–420).

Paris asks her to bed, with a memory of the first time, on the island of Cranae, when he “lay with you in love and bed” (ἐμίγην φιλότητι καὶ εὐνῇ, 3.445).

{{quote:12862cd9-6a8b-51d1-ad65-b50138f3a760}}

τρητός, “pierced”, describes the frame bored with holes for the cords that held the bedding (see [[corded]]). The next line turns to Menelaus, ranging through the crowd like a wild beast in search of the man who is in bed with his wife (3.449–450; see [[like-a-wild-beast]]).

Book 1 ended with Zeus going up to his bed with Hera beside him (1.609–611), and Book 3 ends, all but its last lines, with Paris and Helen in theirs. Paris' words return in Book 14, spoken by Zeus to Hera on Mount Ida when she has come to beguile him: “let us go to bed and take our pleasure in love” and “as I love you now, and sweet longing takes hold of me” (3.441, 3.446; 14.314, 14.328).
