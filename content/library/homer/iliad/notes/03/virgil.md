---
summary: The last book of the Aeneid rebuilds the duel and the oath of Iliad 3, and Aeneas' enemies call him a second Paris, with Paris' hair and Paris' softness.
---

- The duel for Lavinia, the treaty sworn before it, and the truce broken (*Aeneid* 12): [[virgil-duel-for-lavinia]].
- **A second Paris.** Iarbas, the African king whom Dido refused, sneers at Aeneas as "that Paris with his half-man retinue", his chin and dripping hair bound up in a Maeonian cap (4.215–217). Turnus, arming, asks his spear to let him lay low "the half-man Phrygian", tear off his corselet, and foul in the dust his hair, curled with a hot iron and wet with myrrh (12.97–100). Both insults recall Hector's taunt in Book 3, that Paris' hair and looks would not help him when he lay in the dust (3.54–55).
- **The snake.** The Greek Androgeos, realizing at night that he has fallen among enemies, starts back like a man who has trodden on a snake in rough briars (2.379–382), as Paris shrank back from Menelaus (3.33–37; see [[man-who-meets-a-serpent]]).
