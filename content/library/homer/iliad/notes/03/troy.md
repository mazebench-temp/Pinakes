**Inside the walls.** Book 2 entered the city for a moment, at the assembly before Priam's doors (2.788). Book 3 spends half its length there. Iris finds Helen weaving in her hall (μέγαρον, 3.125). Helen goes from her chamber to the Scaean gates, where the elders sit on the tower (3.141–153; see [[scaean-gates]]). The heralds carry the offerings for the oaths "through the city" (ἀνὰ ἄστυ, 3.245), and Priam drives out through the gates and back to "windy Ilios" (3.263, 3.305, 3.313). The last scene is set in "the beautiful house of Alexander" (Ἀλεξάνδροιο δόμον περικαλλέα, 3.421), with its high-roofed bedroom (ὑψόροφον θάλαμον, 3.423). Book 6 adds that Paris built the house himself with the best builders in Troy (6.313–317).

**The two names.** Both occur. In the terms of the truce, the Trojans are to go on living in "deep-soiled Troy" (Τροίην ἐριβώλακα, 3.74, 3.257; see [[deep-soiled]]) if the duel settles the war. When Priam leaves the sacrifice he goes back to "windy Ilios" (Ἴλιον ἠνεμόεσσαν, 3.305; see [[windy]]). Hector speaks of the city as Paris' own, to which he brought "a great grief" (3.50).

**The view from the tower.** For two books the Iliad has looked at the war from the Achaean side. In Book 3 the Trojan elders and Helen look out from the wall at the Achaean army on the plain, and Helen names its leaders (3.154–244; see [[the-view-from-the-wall]]). At the end the city takes in the loser of the duel. Aphrodite carries Paris out of the war and into his bedroom, and Troy hides the man whom no Trojan would hide (3.380–382, 3.451–454).
