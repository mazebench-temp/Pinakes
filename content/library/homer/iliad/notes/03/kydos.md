Menelaus seizes Paris by the horsehair crest and drags him toward the Achaeans, choking on his helmet strap. “And now he would have dragged him off and won untold glory” (ἄσπετον ἤρατο κῦδος, 3.373; see [[untold]]), had not Aphrodite seen it and broken the strap. κῦδος is the glory that a god grants for a moment of victory, and here a god takes it away at the moment it is won. Menelaus is left holding an empty helmet, which he flings to his companions (3.376–378). See [[divine-rescue]] and [[aphrodite-rescues-paris]].

The other words of the family are honorific. Paris calls the gifts of the gods ἐρικυδέα, “glorious” (3.65; see [[glorious]] and [[gifts-of-the-gods]]). Agamemnon at the oath and both armies at the lots address Zeus as κύδιστε μέγιστε, “most glorious, greatest” (3.276, 3.298, 3.320; see [[most-glorious]]). The god they call most glorious denies the glory to the man who prays to him (3.351, 3.365).
