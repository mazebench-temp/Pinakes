---
summary: Zeus is invoked twice as "Father Zeus, who rule from Ida": by Agamemnon at the oath sacrifice, and by both armies before the lots are shaken.
grc: [Ἴδηθεν]
---

Ida is not visited in Book 3, but it is named in the prayers. Agamemnon begins the oath with Ζεῦ πάτερ Ἴδηθεν μεδέων κύδιστε μέγιστε, "Father Zeus, who rule from Ida, most glorious, greatest" (3.276), and the Achaeans and Trojans pray in the same words before the lots for the duel (3.320). Ἴδηθεν means "from Ida", and μεδέων "ruling": Zeus is addressed as lord of the land from the mountain above it, where he has his precinct and altar on Gargarus (8.47–48). The Achaean king, and then both armies together, call on Zeus by the mountain of the Troad. The line returns in the prayer for Ajax before his duel with Hector (7.202) and in Priam's prayer before he goes to Achilles (24.308). See [[who-rules-from-ida]] and [[zeus]].
