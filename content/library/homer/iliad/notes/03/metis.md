**Odysseus of many wiles.** Helen names Odysseus for Priam as “the son of Laertes, Odysseus of many wiles (πολύμητις), who was raised in the land of Ithaca, craggy though it is, and knows all kinds of tricks and shrewd counsels” (δόλους καὶ μήδεα πυκνά, 3.200–202). Antenor confirms it from his own table: he came to know the build of both envoys “and their shrewd counsels” (3.208). The epithet returns when Odysseus rises to speak in the Trojan assembly (3.216) and when he stands up at the oath (3.268). His μῆτις is shown in Antenor's account as a kind of disguise: he stood like a fool until he spoke (3.216–224). See [[of-many-wiles]] and [[odysseus]].

**A goddess devises.** Aphrodite threatens Helen that she may hate her and “devise (μητίσομαι) grim hatreds in the midst of both sides, Trojans and Danaans” (3.416). The verb of planning is a threat of war. See [[cholos]].
