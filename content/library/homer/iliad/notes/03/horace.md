---
summary: One of Horace's Odes turns Hector's rebuke of Paris into a prophecy, spoken by the sea god Nereus as Paris carries Helen away.
---

- Nereus' prophecy: the lyre, the combed hair and Venus' protection will not save Paris, whose hair will be fouled with dust (*Odes* 1.15): [[horace-nereus-prophecy]].
- Helen was not the only woman, another ode says, to burn for "an adulterer's combed hair" and the gold on his clothes (*Odes* 4.9.13–16). The Paris of Book 3 is known by the same things: his hair and his looks (3.55), and the fine clothes he shines in when Aphrodite sets him down in his chamber (3.391–392).
