---
summary: The horses stand by in Book 3. They are held in the ranks during the truce, yoked for Priam's drive to the oath, and left beside the armor while the duel is fought.
passages:
  - 3.259-266 | Priam drives to the oath
---

At the truce the men “held their horses in the ranks (ἐπὶ στίχας), and got down themselves” (3.113). Priam tells his companions “to yoke the horses” (ἵππους ζευγνύμεναι), and he and Antenor drive “the swift horses” through the Scaean gates to the plain (3.259–263). When they arrive they step down ἐξ ἵππων, “from the horses”, that is, from the chariot (3.265; see [[chariot]]). Before the duel the armies sit down in their ranks, “where each man's high-stepping horses stood and inlaid armor lay” (3.326–327; see [[high-stepping]]). No horse takes part in the duel.

The book's epithets remember horses. The Trojans are “horse-taming” (ἱππόδαμοι, 3.127, 3.131, 3.251, 3.343), and so is Castor (3.237; see [[tamer-of-horses]]). Argos is “horse-pasturing” (ἱππόβοτον, 3.75, 3.258; see [[horse-pasturing]]), and the Phrygians whom Priam saw in his youth had “quick horses” (αἰολοπώλους, 3.185; see [[with-quick-horses]]). The helmet has a horsehair crest (see [[helmet]]).
