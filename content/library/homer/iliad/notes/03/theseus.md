---
summary: Theseus is not named in Book 3, but Helen's handmaid “Aethra, daughter of Pittheus” (3.144) bears the name of his mother in later myth.
passages:
  - 3.143-144 | Aethra, daughter of Pittheus
---

If the line means Theseus' mother, it alludes to a story told outside Homer. Theseus carried off the young Helen; her brothers took her back and led his mother Aethra away as a captive; Aethra served Helen at Troy until her grandsons found her at the sack (Apollodorus 3.10.7; the *Iliupersis*, in [[proclus|Proclus]]' summary). Homer tells none of it. Plutarch quotes the line as Homer's evidence for Aethra's captivity, and reports that others rejected it (*Theseus* 34).
