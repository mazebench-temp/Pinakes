---
summary: Book 3 calls on Zeus as father at the oath and at the lots, and twice in Menelaus' words during the duel, once in prayer and once in reproach.
grc: [Διὶ πατρί]
---

The full address Ζεῦ πάτερ Ἴδηθεν μεδέων κύδιστε μέγιστε opens Agamemnon's oath (3.276) and the people's prayer at the lots (3.320; [[who-rules-from-ida]]). In the duel Menelaus rushes at Paris “praying to father Zeus” (3.350) and asks for vengeance on the man who wronged a host (3.351–354; [[xenia]]). His prayer begins with another address, Ζεῦ ἄνα, “Lord Zeus” ([[anax]]).

When his sword breaks, the same father is the god he reproaches: “Father Zeus, no other god is more baneful than you” (3.365; [[deadly]], [[wide-sky]]). The address serves both prayer and complaint.
