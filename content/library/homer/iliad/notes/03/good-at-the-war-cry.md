---
summary: The formula introduces Menelaus' one speech to both armies, in which he accepts the duel and asks for the oath.
---

When Hector has announced Paris' offer, both armies fall silent, “and among them spoke also Menelaus, good at the war cry” (3.95–96). The man known for his shout speaks into a silence, and his speech is measured: his grief, his wish that the armies be parted, the lambs for Earth, the Sun and Zeus, and Priam to swear the oath himself (3.97–110). Antenor later remembers Menelaus' speaking in the same terms: few words, but very clear (3.213–215).

The verse has the same shape as the one that introduces Agamemnon's claim of victory at the end of the book: τοῖσι δὲ καὶ μετέειπεν ἄναξ ἀνδρῶν Ἀγαμέμνων (3.455; [[lord-of-men]]). One brother asks for the oath; the other claims what it promised.
