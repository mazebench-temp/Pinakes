---
summary: Hector tells Paris that his lyre and the gifts of Aphrodite, his hair and his looks, would be no help to him when he lay in the dust (3.54–55). In the duel the goddess herself helps him.
grc: ["οὐκ ἄν τοι χραίσμῃ"]
en: [would be no help to you]
---

{{quote:d10cf66b-61cf-5306-8071-257cfecad967}}

The verb is the one of Agamemnon's threat to Chryses and of Zeus' to Hera (1.28, 1.566), but the construction is a potential, οὐκ ἄν … χραίσμῃ, “would be no help”, in Hector's picture of Paris dead at Menelaus' hands. Agamemnon doubted that a god's fillet would protect the priest; Hector is sure that a goddess's gifts will not protect his brother. The book proves him half wrong. The gifts are no help, but the goddess is: when Menelaus is dragging Paris by the helmet, Aphrodite breaks the strap and carries him off (3.373–382; see [[divine-rescue]], [[lyre]] and [[gifts-of-the-gods]]).
