---
summary: In Book 3 Lacedaemon is Helen's home. Her brothers lie buried there, an old wool-comber served her there, and Paris carried her off “from lovely Lacedaemon”.
grc: [Λακεδαίμονος, Λακεδαίμονι]
---

Book 3 names Lacedaemon four times, always as Helen's past. She wonders whether her brothers “did not come with the rest from lovely Lacedaemon” (3.239), but the earth already held them “there in Lacedaemon, in their own dear native land” (3.244). Aphrodite comes to her in the likeness of an old woman who used to comb fine wool for her “when she lived in Lacedaemon” (3.387). Paris remembers carrying her off “from lovely Lacedaemon” (3.443). Helen and Paris end their lines with the same words, Λακεδαίμονος ἐξ ἐρατεινῆς (3.239, 3.443; see [[lovely]]).

The land that Menelaus leads in the Catalogue (2.581–590) is, in Book 3, the home that Helen left. See [[helen]], [[castor]], [[polydeuces]] and [[cranae]].
