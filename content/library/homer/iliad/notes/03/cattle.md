---
summary: In Book 3 cattle appear only as leather and in an epithet: the strap that chokes Paris is made “from the hide of an ox slain by force”, and Helen's handmaid Clymene is “ox-eyed”.
grc: [βοὸς]
---

When Menelaus drags Alexander by the helmet, the richly worked strap under his chin chokes him, until Aphrodite “broke for him the strap, made from the hide of an ox slain by force” (ἱμάντα βοὸς ἶφι κταμένοιο, 3.375). The phrase occurs only here. It is usually explained as the hide of a beast slaughtered in its strength, not one that died of age or sickness, and so the toughest leather; the goddess breaks it with ease. See [[aphrodite-rescues-paris]] and [[helmet]].

One of the two handmaids who go with Helen to the Scaean gates is “ox-eyed Clymene” (Κλυμένη τε βοῶπις, 3.144), with the epithet that Book 1 gave to Hera (see [[ox-eyed]] and [[clymene]]).
