---
summary: The title marks Agamemnon's three public acts in Book 3: stopping his men from shooting at Hector, rising for the oath, and claiming the victory.
---

“Agamemnon, lord of men” shouts aloud to stop the Achaeans shooting at Hector, so that the duel can be proposed (3.81). At the oath he rises, with Odysseus, when Priam and Antenor have come down among the armies (3.267). And the last speech of the book is his: “And among them spoke also Agamemnon, lord of men” (3.455). He calls on the Trojans to give back Argive Helen and the possessions and to pay satisfaction, since victory belongs to Menelaus (3.456–460; [[argive-helen]]).

Between these public acts Agamemnon is seen from the wall, where Helen names him with his fuller title ([[wide-ruling]], 3.178).
