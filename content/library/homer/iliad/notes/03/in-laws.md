---
summary: In Book 3 Helen is spoken of and to in the words of her husbands' families. Hector calls her the bride of a people of spearmen, Iris comes in the likeness of her sister-in-law, Priam hopes she will see her kin by marriage, and she calls Priam her father-in-law and Agamemnon her brother-in-law, “if that was ever so”.
passages:
  - 3.121-130 | Iris in the likeness of Laodice
  - 3.161-180 | Priam and Helen
---

Book 3 gives Helen two families by marriage at once, and the words of kinship show her place between them.

**The Achaean house.** Hector reproaches Paris with bringing back “the bride of a people of spearmen” (νυὸν ἀνδρῶν αἰχμητάων, 3.49). νυός is a daughter-in-law: Helen belonged to a house of warriors, and Paris took her from it. Priam calls her to the wall to see “your former husband, and your kin by marriage (πηούς) and your friends” (3.163). Her kin by marriage are on the plain, among the enemy.

**The Trojan house.** Iris comes to her “in the likeness of her sister-in-law” (γαλόῳ), Laodice, Paris' sister and the wife of Antenor's son (3.121–124), and in that likeness calls her “dear bride” (νύμφα φίλη, 3.130). The goddess takes the shape of Helen's Trojan family and speaks as a young wife's sister-in-law would.

**Father-in-law.** Helen answers Priam as a daughter-in-law.

{{quote:327b71cb-428c-5699-9f2d-72d3c3c29fa7}}

“You are one I respect (αἰδοῖος), dear father-in-law (ἑκυρέ), and one I fear”: the respect and fear due to a husband's father (see [[aidos]]). In the next breath she wishes she had died before she followed his son.

**Brother-in-law.** She names Agamemnon for Priam and ends: “And he was my brother-in-law (δαήρ), dog-faced as I am, if that was ever so” (3.180). The past tense and the doubt (εἴ ποτʼ ἔην γε, “if ever he was”) put the relation in another life that no longer seems real.

The same Helen who calls Priam “father-in-law” names Menelaus “my former husband” to Paris' face (3.429; see [[posis]]). In her lament over Hector in the last book she remembers that her father-in-law was always gentle to her, like a father (24.770). See [[helen]] and [[husbands-and-wives]].
