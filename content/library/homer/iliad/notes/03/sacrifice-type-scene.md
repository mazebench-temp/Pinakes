---
summary: The oath of Book 3 is told in the order and some of the verses of a sacrifice, from the washing of hands to the killing, and then stops. There is no altar, no barley, no burning and no feast, and the victims are carried away.

---

The oath follows the order of a sacrifice and keeps its words where it can (see [[oath]]). Set beside the sacrifice at Chryse (1.447–474):

1. **Preparation.** At Chryse the victims were set around the altar, and the men washed their hands and took up the barley grains (1.448–449). Here the heralds bring the offerings together, mix wine in the bowl, and pour water over the kings' hands (3.268–270). The washing remains; the barley does not, and no altar is named in the whole scene. See [[hand-washing]] and [[barley-grains]].
2. **First hairs.** Agamemnon cuts hair from the lambs' heads (3.273). At Nestor's sacrifice in the Odyssey the first hairs are thrown into the fire as the opening offering (Odyssey 3.445–446). Here there is no fire, and the heralds give the hair to the leaders of both armies (3.274; see [[hair]]).
3. **Prayer.** The verse is Chryses', with the king's name in the priest's place: τοῖσιν δʼ Ἀτρεΐδης μεγάλʼ εὔχετο χεῖρας ἀνασχών, “And the son of Atreus prayed aloud among them, lifting up his hands” (3.275; 1.450). At his sacrifice in Book 2 Agamemnon prayed without the gesture (2.411). The prayer is an oath: it calls witnesses and states terms, where a prayer at a sacrifice claims the god's favor and asks for a gift (see [[prayer-type-scene]]).
4. **Killing.** One half-line does the work of the three verbs at Chryse, “drew back the victims' heads, and cut their throats, and flayed them” (1.459): ἀπὸ στομάχους ἀρνῶν τάμε νηλέϊ χαλκῷ, “cut the lambs' throats with the pitiless bronze” (3.292). The victims are not flayed. They are laid on the ground, still gasping (3.293–294).
5. **The god's portion and the feast.** Nothing. No thigh-bones are cut out and burned, no savor rises to the sky, no one tastes the entrails, and there is no meal.
6. **Wine.** At Chryse the priest poured wine over the burning thigh-pieces (1.462–463). Here the wine is drawn from the bowl in cups and poured on the ground, with a curse (3.295–301; see [[libation]]).
7. **The answer.** As in Book 2, the narrator gives the god's answer: the son of Cronus “was not yet going to fulfill their prayer” (3.302; 2.419).

The scene keeps the frame of a sacrifice and leaves out everything that made a sacrifice an exchange with the gods, the portion that goes up in smoke and the meat that men share. What is left is the death of the victims, and Priam carries the Trojans' lambs back to the city (3.310). The same half-line of killing returns when Agamemnon swears over a boar in Book 19, and the carcass is thrown into the sea (19.266–268). An oath sacrifice is not a gift. It shows the man who swears what will happen to him if he breaks his word. See [[sacrifice]].
