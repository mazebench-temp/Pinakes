summary: In Book 3 the word of heroic excellence is given to looks. Paris is “best in looks” and Laodice the most beautiful of Priam's daughters; Paris challenges all the best of the Argives, and the Achaeans had taken him for a prince.

**The best of the Argives.** Paris steps out before the Trojans and “challenged all the best (ἀρίστους) of the Argives to fight him face to face” (3.19). It is the challenge of a champion, and he withdraws from it as soon as Menelaus appears (3.30–37).

**Best in looks.** Hector turns the word against him: Δύσπαρι, εἶδος ἄριστε, “Evil Paris, best in looks” (3.39; see [[best-in-looks]]). The Achaeans thought “our champion a prince” (ἀριστῆα πρόμον, 3.44) because of his handsome looks, but there is no strength in him. ἀριστεύς, “prince, chief”, is the noun of ἄριστος; the title belongs to a man who is best in battle (see [[eidos]]). The same phrase is used of a woman without irony: Laodice is “most beautiful in form” (εἶδος ἀρίστην) of Priam's daughters (3.124), as Alcestis was of the daughters of Pelias (2.715).

**The best men.** Idaeus tells Priam that “the best men” of both armies call him to the oath (ἄριστοι, 3.250), and the heralds give out the hair of the lambs “to the best of the Trojans and Achaeans” (ἀρίστοις, 3.274). Menelaus wants Priam there because an old man sees before and after, “so that things turn out far the best (ἄριστα) for both sides” (3.110). See [[best-of-the-achaeans]].
