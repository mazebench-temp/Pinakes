Menelaus asks the Trojans for two lambs, “a white ram and a black ewe, for Earth and the Sun” (3.103–104). The colors and the sexes are usually matched to the powers: the white ram for the Sun, the black ewe for Earth. In the oath Agamemnon calls on him second, after Zeus:

{{quote:7ab54449-a0b7-5ed3-8516-543cba486fe4}}

A god who passes over the whole world sees and hears everything, and so he can witness what men swear. The Odyssey gives him the same words (Od. 11.109, 12.323), and Agamemnon calls on him again, with Zeus, Earth and the Erinyes, when he swears in Book 19 (19.258–260).
