summary: Antenor's memory of the embassy moves in four steps, each opening “But when” (ἀλλʼ ὅτε δή): the envoys among the Trojans, their speeches, Odysseus rising, and his great voice (3.209–222).
refs: [3.209, 3.212, 3.216, 3.221]
passages:
  - 3.209-224 | Menelaus and Odysseus in the Trojan assembly
---

ἀλλʼ ὅτε δή, “but when”, opens four clauses of Antenor's speech: when the envoys mingled with the Trojans gathered in assembly (3.209); when they began to weave their words and counsels before all (3.212); when Odysseus of many wiles sprang up (3.216); and when he let the great voice go from his chest (3.221). Each step narrows the scene, from the gathering to the two speakers, to Odysseus alone, to his voice, and the repeated opening holds back the climax until the last: “words like the snowflakes of winter” (3.222; see [[snowflakes-of-winter]] and [[scepter]]).

Book 3 also numbers its sequences. Priam's questions go “next” (δεύτερον, 3.191) and “third” (τὸ τρίτον, 3.225), and Paris puts on his greaves “first” (πρῶτα, 3.330) and his corselet “second” (δεύτερον, 3.332).
