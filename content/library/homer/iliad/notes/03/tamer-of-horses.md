---
summary: Book 3 gives the Trojans their epithet four times, beside the bronze-armored and the well-greaved Achaeans, and names Helen's brother Castor “tamer of horses.”
grc: [ἱππόδαμον, ἱπποδάμους]
---

The pair “of the horse-taming Trojans and the bronze-armored Achaeans” (Τρώων θʼ ἱπποδάμων καὶ Ἀχαιῶν χαλκοχιτώνων) is a whole verse, used three times ([[bronze-armored]]). It describes the trials that Helen weaves into her web (3.127), the spectacle to which Iris calls her (3.131), and the best men of both sides who summon Priam to the oath (3.251). When the duel begins, both armies watch in amazement, “the horse-taming Trojans and the well-greaved Achaeans” (3.343; [[well-greaved]]).

On the wall Helen names her brothers: “Castor, tamer of horses, and Polydeuces, good with his fists” (3.237; [[castor]], [[good-with-his-fists]]). The Trojans' epithet is given to a Greek hero, one of the horsemen among the heroes. The same line recurs in the Odyssey (Od. 11.300).
