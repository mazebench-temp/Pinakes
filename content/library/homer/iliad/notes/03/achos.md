summary: Refusing to go to Paris' bed, Helen tells Aphrodite that she has griefs past measure in her heart.
grc: [ἄχεʼ]
en: [griefs]
passages:
  - 3.406-412 | Go and sit beside him yourself
---

The word comes once in Book 3, at the end of Helen's refusal. She will not go to share Paris' bed; the Trojan women would all blame her hereafter; “and I have griefs past measure in my heart” (ἔχω δʼ ἄχεʼ ἄκριτα θυμῷ, 3.412).

ἄκριτος means “not sorted, not decided”, and so “endless, beyond measure”. Iris used it of the Trojans' “endless words” in Book 2 (ἄκριτοι μῦθοι, 2.796). In Book 1 ἄχος came upon Achilles at an insult to his honor (1.188), and in Book 2 upon Odysseus at the army's flight (2.171). In Book 3 it is a woman's private grief, spoken to the goddess who caused it, and it does not save her from obeying (3.413–420). Menelaus' sorrow (ἄλγος) opens the duel (3.97); Helen's griefs close it. See [[algos]], [[nemesis]] and [[helen]].
