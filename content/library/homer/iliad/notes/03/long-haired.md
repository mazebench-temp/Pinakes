---
summary: Hector imagines the long-haired Achaeans laughing at Paris, and the long-haired Achaeans shoot at Hector until Agamemnon stops them.
---

The formula comes twice, in the same part of the verse. In his rebuke Hector imagines the enemy's scorn: “Surely the long-haired Achaeans are cackling with laughter, who thought our champion a prince because he has handsome looks” (3.43–45). When Hector goes into the middle to hold back the Trojans, the long-haired Achaeans are not laughing but shooting: they aim at him with arrows and pelt him with stones until Agamemnon shouts to them to stop (3.79–83).
