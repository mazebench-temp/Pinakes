**A proposal carried.** Hector rejoices “when he heard his words” (μῦθον, 3.76), stops the Trojan ranks, and announces to both armies “the word (μῦθον) of Alexander, for whose sake the quarrel arose” (3.87). A μῦθος is a word meant to be acted on, and this one is: the armies sit down, and the heralds go for the lambs. Priam's farewell to the armies is a μῦθος too (3.303).

**Weaving words.** Antenor remembers the embassy: “when they began to weave their words and counsels before all” (μύθους καὶ μήδεα πᾶσιν ὕφαινον, 3.212). The image of weaving is used of speech and counsel; in this book it is also Helen's work, weaving the war into her cloth (3.125–128; see [[weaving]]). Menelaus spoke fluently, few words but clear, “since he was not a man of many words” (οὐ πολύμυθος, 3.214). Helen, too, could tell the name of every Achaean she sees (μυθησαίμην, 3.235).

**A rebuke.** Helen, set down by Aphrodite facing Paris, turns her eyes away and “rebuked her husband” (ἠνίπαπε μύθῳ, 3.427). Her μῦθος has the authority the word implies, and Paris answers it with an appeal to bed (3.437–446; see [[eros]]). See [[epos]].
