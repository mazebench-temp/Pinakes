---
summary: Under the terms of the duel, whoever wins, the Achaeans are to go home “to horse-pasturing Argos and Achaea of the lovely women” (3.75, 3.258).
---

Paris proposes the terms (3.71–75), and Idaeus repeats them to Priam (3.255–258). The winner takes the woman and the possessions; the rest make friendship and swear a truce; the Trojans stay in Troy, and the Achaeans go back “to horse-pasturing Argos and Achaea of the lovely women”. The line joins two names for the Achaeans' homeland (see [[argos]]). καλλιγύναιξ (“of the lovely women”) praises a land for its women, and the first to say it is the man who carried one of them off.
