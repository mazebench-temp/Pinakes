---
summary: The duel and the oath are done in bronze. The word stands for the spear and for the knife that kills the lambs, and the helmet that holds the lots is “bronze-fitted”.
grc: [χαλκήρεϊ, χαλκείῳ]
---

χαλκός often stands for the weapon itself. Agamemnon cuts the lambs' throats “with the pitiless bronze” (νηλέϊ χαλκῷ), “for the bronze had taken away their strength” (3.292–294; see [[knife]] and [[pitiless-bronze]]). In the duel Paris' spear strikes Menelaus' shield, “but the bronze did not break through” (3.348), and Menelaus “rushed second with the bronze” (3.349). Paris' spears are “tipped with bronze” (3.18), his sword is “of bronze” (3.335), the lot is for the first throw of the “bronze spear” (χάλκεον ἔγχος, 3.317), and Menelaus springs back to kill Paris “with his bronze spear” (ἔγχεϊ χαλκείῳ, 3.380). The lots are shaken in a “bronze-fitted” helmet (χαλκήρεϊ, 3.316). The Achaeans are “bronze-armored” (χαλκοχίτωνες, 3.127, 3.131, 3.251; see [[bronze-armored]]).
