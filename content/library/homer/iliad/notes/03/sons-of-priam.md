Menelaus asks for Priam himself to come and swear the oaths:

{{quote:f054d837-abd8-59e4-ab3a-b67b9633b0e2}}

ὑπερφίαλοι means "overbearing, arrogant", and ἄπιστοι "not to be trusted". The charge has one son in view above all, Paris, the guest who wronged his host (3.351–354). In Agamemnon's oath the sons are bound with their father: if Alexander falls and "Priam and the sons of Priam" refuse to pay, he will fight on for the penalty (3.288–290).

Three sons of Priam have a part in the book. [[Paris]] fights the duel, and Menelaus' spear strikes "the son of Priam" (Πριαμίδαο, 3.356). [[Hector]], "son of Priam" (Πριάμοιο πάϊς, 3.314), rebukes him, proclaims the truce and shakes the lots. [[lycaon-son-of-priam|Lycaon]] is present only through the corselet that Paris borrows from him (3.333). A daughter also appears: Iris takes the likeness of [[Laodice]], "most beautiful in form of the daughters of Priam" (3.124).
