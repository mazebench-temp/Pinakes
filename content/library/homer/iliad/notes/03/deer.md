---
summary: The deer of Book 3 is a carcass: “an antlered stag or a wild goat” is what the hungry lion finds in the simile for Menelaus (3.23–25).
grc: [ἔλαφον]
en: [stag]
---

In Book 1 the deer was the emblem of cowardice. In Book 3 it is food. When Menelaus sees Alexander, he rejoices “as a lion rejoices when he comes upon a great carcass, finding an antlered stag or a wild goat, when he is hungry” (3.23–25). ἔλαφος κεραός is a stag with its antlers, a large and handsome animal, and σῶμα, “carcass”, is always a dead body in Homer: the stag is already dead, most likely the hunters' kill, and the lion takes it though their dogs and young men rush at him. See [[lion-on-a-carcass]] and [[goats]].

The same line, “an antlered stag or a wild goat”, returns in Book 15, where dogs and country men chase a quarry that escapes them into the rocks and woods, and a bearded lion appears in their path (15.271–276).
