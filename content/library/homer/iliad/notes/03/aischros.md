summary: Hector rebukes Paris “with shaming words”, and Helen imagines her brothers staying away from the battle for fear of the shame (αἴσχεα) that is hers.
passages:
  - 3.38-57 | Hector rebukes Paris
  - 3.236-244 | The earth already held them
---

**Shaming words.** Hector sees Paris shrink back into the crowd and “rebuked him with shaming words” (αἰσχροῖς ἐπέεσσιν, 3.38). The words shame the man they are aimed at, and Hector's list is a list of public disgrace: Paris is a λώβη (“outrage, disgrace”) and ὑπόψιος (“scorned by others”, 3.42), “a joy to your enemies, and a shame (κατηφείη) to yourself” (3.51), and the Achaeans are cackling with laughter at him (3.43). The same line introduces Hector's rebuke in Book 6 (6.325).

**A sister's shame.** Helen cannot find her brothers Castor and Polydeuces among the Achaeans.

{{quote:70e03702-ee22-558f-a91f-bbf93bc38989}}

She supposes that they will not enter the battle “for fear of the shame (αἴσχεα) and the many reproaches that are mine” (3.242). The αἴσχεα are hers, and she imagines them falling on her kin. The narrator corrects her: they are already dead and buried in Lacedaemon. Shame in Homer is what others say, and Helen hears it even where no one is speaking. See [[oneidos]], [[aidos]] and [[castor]].
