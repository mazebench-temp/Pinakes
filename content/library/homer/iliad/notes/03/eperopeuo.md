---
summary: Hector's word for Paris, “seducer”, is Helen's word for what Aphrodite wants to do to her.
passages:
  - 3.38-45 | Hector rebukes Paris
  - 3.399-405 | Helen to Aphrodite
---

The word comes twice in Book 3, at the start and near the end, and both times it names the power that brought Helen to Troy.

Hector opens his rebuke of Paris with four names: “Evil Paris, best in looks, mad for women, seducer” (ἠπεροπευτά, 3.39; see [[evil-paris]]). The seducer is the man who took another's wife by charm.

Helen uses the verb of the goddess. When Aphrodite comes in disguise to take her to Paris' bed, Helen asks: “Strange one, why do you long to deceive me (ἠπεροπεύειν) like this?” (3.399). Will the goddess lead her on to some city of Phrygia or Maeonia, to another mortal she loves? Has she come now “with treachery in your heart” (δολοφρονέουσα, 3.405), because Menelaus has won and wants to take her home? Helen tells her own story as a seduction repeated, and she is the one beguiled. Diomedes will say the same to the wounded goddess in Book 5: it should be enough for her to beguile weak women (5.349).
