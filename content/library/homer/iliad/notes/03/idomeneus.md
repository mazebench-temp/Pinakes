Priam asks about three men; Helen adds a fourth. Idomeneus is the only leader she names unasked, and the only one she remembers as a guest in her own house. θεὸς ὥς (“like a god”) sets him apart among his men, and the leaders of the Cretans gather around him. Her memory is of guest-friendship between Sparta and Crete before the war; later in the book Menelaus prays for vengeance on the guest who wronged his host (3.351–354). In the *Cypria*, as [[proclus|Proclus]] summarizes it, Menelaus was away in Crete when Paris carried Helen off. See [[crete]], [[cretans]] and [[xenia]].
