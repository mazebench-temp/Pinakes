---
summary: Menelaus is ξανθός twice in Book 3, both times as the other party in the contest for Helen: in the terms of Agamemnon's oath and in Helen's warning to Paris.
grc: [ξανθῷ]
---

Agamemnon's oath states the two outcomes of the duel. If Alexander kills Menelaus, he keeps Helen; “but if fair-haired Menelaus kills Alexander,” the Trojans must give her back with all the possessions and pay satisfaction (3.281–287). The formula ξανθὸς Μενέλαος names the man in the clause that would give him back his wife.

Helen uses the dative after she has taunted Paris: she tells him to stop, and not to fight “against fair-haired Menelaus” again, or he may soon be brought down by his spear (3.433–436). The epithet of the husband she has lost comes in her warning to the husband she has.
