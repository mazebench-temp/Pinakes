**Strange one.** Helen recognizes Aphrodite through her disguise and addresses her as δαιμονίη, “strange one” (3.399), the word used between husband and wife on Olympus (1.561) and by Odysseus to the fleeing army (2.190, 2.200). Here a mortal woman uses it to a goddess, and with open reproach: why does she long to deceive her? See [[strange-one]].

**The divinity led the way.** Threatened, Helen gives in and goes, wrapped in her robe, in silence, unseen by the Trojan women, “and the divinity led the way” (ἦρχε δὲ δαίμων, 3.420). The δαίμων is Aphrodite. The narrator does not use her name here but the word for a divine power felt at work, and the line holds both agents: Helen walks, the goddess leads. See [[divine-intervention]] and [[who-is-to-blame]].

**Favored by heaven.** Priam, told that the handsome Achaean is Agamemnon, calls him “blessed son of Atreus, child of fortune, favored by heaven” (ὦ μάκαρ Ἀτρεΐδη μοιρηγενὲς ὀλβιόδαιμον, 3.182). ὀλβιόδαιμον joins ὄλβιος (“prosperous, happy”) and δαίμων: a man whose portion from the gods is wealth and power. See [[child-of-fortune]] and [[moira]].
