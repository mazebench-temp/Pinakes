---
summary: Book 3 is a book of looking. Odysseus fixes his eyes on the ground before he speaks, Helen knows Aphrodite by her flashing eyes, and she turns her own eyes away from Paris.
---

Men look and are looked at throughout the book. Menelaus sees Paris “with his eyes” and rejoices like a lion (3.28). Priam has never seen “with my eyes” a man as handsome as Agamemnon (3.169), and cannot bear to see “with my own eyes” his son fight Menelaus (3.306).

Three gestures of the eyes stand out. Odysseus, before he spoke in the Trojan assembly, “would stand and look down, fixing his eyes on the ground” (κατὰ χθονὸς ὄμματα πήξας, 3.217; see [[scepter]]). Helen knows the goddess disguised as an old woman when she sees “the goddess's beautiful neck and her lovely breasts and her flashing eyes” (ὄμματα μαρμαίροντα, 3.396–397; see [[divine-epiphany]]). And when Aphrodite has seated her facing Paris, Helen sits “turning her eyes away” (ὄσσε πάλιν κλίνασα) and rebukes her husband (3.427; see [[stool]]).
