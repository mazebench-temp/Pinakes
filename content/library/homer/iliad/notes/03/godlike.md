---
summary: θεοειδής is Paris' standing epithet in Book 3, where it names him six times, most pointedly as he shrinks from Menelaus.
grc: [θεοειδέα]
---

Ἀλέξανδρος θεοειδής, “godlike Alexander,” is the Iliad's formula for Paris, and Book 3 tests it at once. He steps out as champion, “godlike Alexander” (3.16), and Menelaus rejoices at the sight of “godlike Alexander” (3.27). Then the formula turns against him:

{{quote:c6707bf8-4db7-5da5-9c28-ecc33e6edbd2}}

The simile of the man who meets a serpent comes between two uses of the formula, and the verse that closes it names him again: “godlike Alexander, in fear of the son of Atreus” (3.37; [[man-who-meets-a-serpent]]). The epithet praises looks, and Hector's rebuke is about exactly that: Paris is “best in looks” and nothing more (3.39, 3.44–45; [[best-in-looks]], [[evil-paris]]).

The formula introduces his reply (3.58), and it ends the book's search for him: Menelaus ranges the field “to see if he could catch sight of godlike Alexander” (3.450). By then a goddess has carried him off ([[aphrodite-rescues-paris]]). Priam's ally Mygdon is “godlike” in another word, ἀντίθεος ([[antitheos]], 3.186).
