---
summary: The god does not appear in Book 3, but his name is a word for war, "tearful Ares", and it lives on in epithets: Menelaus is "dear to Ares", and the old Hicetaon an "offshoot of Ares".
---

In Book 3 Ares is war itself. Helen is weaving the trials that Trojans and Achaeans suffer for her sake "at the hands of Ares" (ὑπʼ Ἄρηος παλαμάων, 3.128), and Iris tells her that the armies who "were bringing tearful Ares on one another" (πολύδακρυν Ἄρηα, 3.132) now sit in silence. In both phrases the name stands for the fighting.

His name also lives in epithets. Menelaus is ἀρηΐφιλος, "dear to Ares", thirteen times in the book (see [[dear-to-ares]]), and once ἀρήϊος, "warlike" (3.339; see [[warlike]]). The old Hicetaon, sitting among the elders who are "done with war now because of their age", is still ὄζος Ἄρηος, "offshoot of Ares" (3.147, 3.150; see [[offshoot-of-ares]] and [[hicetaon]]).
