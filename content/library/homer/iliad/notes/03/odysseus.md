**The ram.** Priam's second question is about a man he cannot place:

{{quote:e21fa1d3-665b-5864-ba36-dbb1b4d62d6c}}

Shorter than Agamemnon but broader, he walks through the ranks while his armor lies on the ground, like a ram (κτίλος, the leader of a flock) going through the sheep. Priam sees him lead before he knows who he is. See [[ram-among-the-sheep]].

**Helen's answer.** “This is the son of Laertes, Odysseus of many wiles, who was raised in the land of Ithaca, craggy though it is, and knows all kinds of tricks and shrewd counsels” (3.200–202). δόλοι (“tricks”) and μήδεα πυκνά (“shrewd counsels”): she names him by what he knows. See [[son-of-laertes]], [[of-many-wiles]] and [[ithaca]].

**Antenor's memory.** Antenor confirms it. Odysseus once came to Troy with Menelaus, on an embassy about Helen, and Antenor received them as guests (3.205–208; see [[the-embassy-to-troy]]). Standing, Menelaus was the taller; seated, Odysseus was the more stately (3.210–211). Menelaus spoke briefly and clearly. Odysseus was different:

{{quote:1400c24c-8075-5e4a-b7ee-66655be59e11}}

He stood with his eyes fixed on the ground and held the scepter stiff, “like a man who knows nothing”, so that you would have taken him for a surly fellow and a fool. Then came the great voice and the words “like the snowflakes of winter”, many and steady, and “no other mortal could rival Odysseus”. In Book 2 the scepter was a weapon in his hand (2.265–269); here he does not even move it. The poem's first description of how Odysseus speaks comes from an enemy's memory. See [[snowflakes-of-winter]] and [[scepter]].

Later critics took Menelaus and Odysseus, with Nestor, as Homer's models of the three styles of oratory: the plain, the middle and the grand (Quintilian 12.10.64; Aulus Gellius 6.14.7; see [[three-styles-of-speech]]).

**At the oath and the lots.** He rises with Agamemnon when the oath is to be sworn (3.267–268). Then he and Hector, one man from each side, measure out the ground for the duel and shake the lots in a bronze helmet to decide who throws first (3.314–317). See [[lots]].
