---
summary: The ships of Book 3 are the voyage that began the war and the voyage that would end it: the seafaring ships in which Paris carried Helen off, and the ships in which the Achaeans would sail home if Paris won.
grc: [νέεσσι, νέεσσʼ]
---

Hector reproaches Paris with the voyage. Was it as such a man, he asks, that “in your seafaring ships you sailed over the sea … and, mingling with foreigners, brought back a lovely woman from a distant land” (ἐν ποντοπόροισι νέεσσι, 3.46–49)? Paris remembers it himself, as the first night of his love: “when first, from lovely Lacedaemon, I snatched you away and sailed off in the seafaring ships” (3.443–444). Helen wonders whether her brothers came to Troy “in their seafaring ships” (3.240).

The ships would also end the war. The old men on the wall want Helen to “go home in the ships” (ἐν νηυσὶ νεέσθω, 3.159), and by the oath, if Paris kills Menelaus, the Achaeans “go home in our seafaring ships” (3.283). Talthybius goes to “the hollow ships”, the Achaean camp, for the lamb of the oath (3.119). See [[seafaring-ship]] and [[the-abduction-of-helen]].
