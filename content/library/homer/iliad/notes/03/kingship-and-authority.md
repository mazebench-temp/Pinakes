summary: Book 3 looks at kings from outside. Menelaus will take an oath only from old Priam, since the king's sons cannot be trusted; from the wall Priam recognizes Agamemnon as a king by his looks, and calls him blessed for the young men subject to him; at the oath the two kings swear for their peoples.

**The king's word.** Menelaus accepts the duel on condition that Priam come and swear “himself, for his sons are overbearing and not to be trusted” (3.105–106). The king's oath binds his people as his sons' word cannot, and an old man sees before and after (3.108–110; see [[before-and-after]]). Priam comes, drives out with Antenor, and stands between the armies while Agamemnon swears (3.259–301). Then he leaves before the fighting, “since I could never bear to see with my own eyes my dear son fighting” (3.306–307). The king who swears for Troy is also the father of the man who caused the war (see [[priam]]).

**A king by sight.** From the wall Priam picks out an Achaean who is not the tallest but is the most handsome and stately he has seen, “for he looks like a king” (βασιλῆϊ γὰρ ἀνδρὶ ἔοικε, 3.170). Helen names him “both a good king and a strong spearman” (3.179; see [[a-good-king-and-a-strong-spearman]]), and Priam calls him blessed, “child of fortune, favored by heaven; truly many young men of the Achaeans have been made subject to you” (3.182–183). A king's standing is seen in his bearing and counted in the men who obey him. Priam, remembering the great Phrygian host, says that it was smaller than Agamemnon's (3.184–190; see [[child-of-fortune]]).

**Two kings at the oath.** The oath is sworn by the two kings for their peoples. The heralds pour water over the hands of the kings (βασιλεῦσιν, 3.270), Agamemnon cuts the lambs' hair and the heralds give it out to the best men of both sides (3.271–274), and Agamemnon prays and names the terms (3.275–291). At the end of the book it is Agamemnon, not Menelaus, who claims the victory and demands Helen and the satisfaction (3.455–460). See [[basileus]], [[agamemnon]] and [[oath]].
