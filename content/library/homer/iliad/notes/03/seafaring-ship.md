---
summary: Book 3 names the seafaring ships four times: the ships of Paris' voyage to Sparta, the ships of Helen's brothers, and the ships that will take the Achaeans home.
grc: [ποντοπόροισιν]
---

The epithet frames the story of the abduction. Hector asks whether it was as a coward that Paris “in your seafaring ships you sailed over the sea” to bring back Helen (3.46–47), and Paris himself recalls the day “I snatched you away and sailed off in the seafaring ships” (3.444; [[the-abduction-of-helen]]). Between them, Helen imagines that her brothers “came here in their seafaring ships” and will not fight for shame of her (3.240), and Agamemnon's oath provides that, if Paris wins, “let us go home in our seafaring ships” (3.283). The ships that brought Helen to Troy are answered by the ships that would take the army home without her. See [[ships]].
