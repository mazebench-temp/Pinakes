{{quote:469d741a-d3e0-5bf1-88a6-a3f1e92361bc}}

κυνῶπις is the feminine of the insult that Achilles threw at Agamemnon (1.159). Helen turns it on herself. The genitive agrees with the “me” implied in ἐμός: “brother-in-law of dog-faced me.” It comes between the praise of Agamemnon and her doubt that the past was ever real (εἴ ποτʼ ἔην γε, “if that was ever so”). She calls herself shameless in the word that men use for the shameless ([[aidos]]).

Helen speaks of herself in this way again. To Hector she calls herself “a dog, an evil-scheming, chilling one” (κυνὸς κακομηχάνου ὀκρυοέσσης, Il. 6.344), and in the Odyssey, at home in Sparta, she speaks of the war fought “for the sake of dog-faced me” (ἐμεῖο κυνώπιδος εἵνεκʼ, Od. 4.145). In Book 3 she also calls herself “hateful” ([[hateful]], 3.404). The narrator's epithets praise her; her own blame her. See [[helen]], [[who-is-to-blame]].
