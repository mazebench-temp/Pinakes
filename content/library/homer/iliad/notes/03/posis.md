---
summary: Helen has two husbands in Book 3. Menelaus is her “former husband” (πρότερος πόσις), whom she longs for and names to Paris' face; Paris arms as “the husband of Helen of the lovely hair”, and she rebukes him as her husband.
passages:
  - 3.161-163 | Your former husband
  - 3.328-329 | The husband of Helen
  - 3.426-436 | Helen rebukes her husband
---

The word comes four times in Book 3, twice for each of Helen's husbands, and one adjective keeps them apart. Menelaus is always the “former” one (πρότερος).

**The former husband.** Iris puts in Helen a longing “for her former husband” (ἀνδρός τε προτέρου, 3.140), and Priam calls her to the wall to see “your former husband” (πρότερόν τε πόσιν, 3.163). The duel is fought to decide whose wife she will be: “you will be called the dear wife of the one who wins” (3.138).

**The husband of Helen.** Paris arms for the duel under his most personal formula.

{{quote:8b86fe54-564c-59c4-8269-af605b8336b8}}

“Brilliant Alexander, the husband of Helen of the lovely hair” (Ἑλένης πόσις ἠϋκόμοιο): the formula names him by his wife as he arms to fight for her (see [[husband-of-helen]]). It comes back when he enters the story later in the poem (for example 7.355, 11.369).

**Two husbands in one line.** After the duel Aphrodite brings Helen to Paris' chamber. She turns her eyes away, “and she rebuked her husband” (πόσιν δʼ ἠνίπαπε μύθῳ, 3.427).

{{quote:37a8b10e-3acb-5079-b6bd-04bc4ed917db}}

She wishes Paris had died, “beaten by a stronger man, who was my former husband” (ὃς ἐμὸς πρότερος πόσις ἦεν). The word “husband” names Paris in the narrator's line and Menelaus in hers. The book ends with Paris taking her to bed, “and his wife followed with him” (ἄκοιτις, 3.447). See [[husbands-and-wives]] and [[in-laws]].
