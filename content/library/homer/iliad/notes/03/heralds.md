**Sent.** Hector sends two heralds to the city “to bring the lambs and to summon Priam” (3.116–117), and Agamemnon sends Talthybius to the ships for a lamb (3.118–120; see [[talthybius]]).

**In the city.** The heralds carry through the city the offerings for the oaths, the lambs and the wine, and “the herald Idaeus” carries the mixing bowl and the golden cups (3.245–248). Standing beside the old king, Idaeus gives him his summons, and passes on the terms of the duel in the words of Paris and Hector (3.249–258; see [[idaeus]], [[messenger-scene]] and [[message-repeated]]).

After Agamemnon has cut the hair from the lambs' heads, “the heralds gave it out to the best of the Trojans and Achaeans” (3.274). The Greek, κήρυκες Τρώων καὶ Ἀχαιῶν νεῖμαν ἀρίστοις, can also be read “the heralds of the Trojans and Achaeans gave it out to the best”. Either way the heralds serve both sides at once: Hector's heralds and Agamemnon's fetched the victims (3.116–120), and at the oath they act as servants of the rite rather than of their own kings. In Book 1 Achilles greeted the heralds as “messengers of Zeus and of men” (1.334); here they serve an oath of Zeus (3.107). They do the same service at other rites. Heralds pour water over the chiefs' hands before the libation in Book 9 (9.174), and in the Odyssey they mix wine and water for the suitors' feast (Odyssey 1.109–110). In Book 7 Idaeus and Talthybius will together part Hector and Ajax at nightfall (7.273–282).
