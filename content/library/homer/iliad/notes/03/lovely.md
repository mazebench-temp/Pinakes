---
summary: Book 3 gives the epithet to Lacedaemon, Helen's home, and to Maeonia, and to the companions of Helen's youth; Paris uses the simpler ἐρατός of Aphrodite's gifts.
grc: [ἐρατεινῆς, ἐρατὰ]
---

The word belongs to the language of desire (ἔραμαι, “love”; [[eros]]), and in Book 3 it is spoken by Helen and Paris. Helen wishes she had died before she left “the lovely companions of my youth” (ὁμηλικίην ἐρατεινήν, 3.175; [[darling]]). She supposes that her brothers did not come “from lovely Lacedaemon” (3.239). And she mocks Aphrodite: will the goddess lead her on to some city “of Phrygia or of lovely Maeonia,” to another man (3.400–402)? Paris uses the epithet of the same place with other feelings: never has desire so seized him, “not even when first, from lovely Lacedaemon, I snatched you away” (3.443–444).

Paris also uses the simple adjective ἐρατός, from which ἐρατεινός is formed, of “the lovely gifts of golden Aphrodite” (δῶρʼ ἐρατά, 3.64; [[golden]]).
