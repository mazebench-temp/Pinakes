---
summary: The Trojans advance “with clamor and with cries, like birds” (3.2), and the birds turn out to be cranes on their way to war with the Pygmies.
grc: [ὄρνιθες]
---

Book 3 names birds once, in its first simile. Τρῶες μὲν κλαγγῇ τʼ ἐνοπῇ τʼ ἴσαν ὄρνιθες ὣς: “the Trojans came on with clamor and with cries, like birds” (3.2). The next line narrows the flock to [[cranes]], whose clamor rises under the face of heaven when they fly south before the winter (3.3–7; see [[cranes-and-pygmies]]).

In Book 2 a flock of birds, “geese or cranes or long-necked swans”, gave the whole Achaean army its noise and its restless motion as it poured onto the plain (2.459–466; see [[birds-on-the-asian-meadow]]). Now the birds are the Trojans alone, and the Achaeans are silent (3.8–9). κλαγγή, the cry of the birds, is also used of the noise of men: of the assembly of Book 2, which had to be brought to order (2.100), and of the uproar of the Trojans when they find the Thracian king Rhesus killed in the night (10.523).
