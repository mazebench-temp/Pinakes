---
summary: Paris steps out as a champion with a curved bow, but fights the duel with the spear; the Achaeans shoot at Hector as he comes forward until Agamemnon stops them.
grc: [τόξα, ἐπετοξάζοντο]
en: [kept shooting at him]
---

Paris comes out in front of the Trojans “with a leopard's skin on his shoulders and his curved bow (καμπύλα τόξα) and a sword”, brandishing two spears (3.17–18). The plural τόξα names one bow, and καμπύλος is “curved”. The bow is the archer's weapon, and when he arms for the duel he leaves it out; he fights Menelaus with spear and sword (3.328–338; see [[arming-scene]] and [[leopard-skin]]). His later deeds in the poem are an archer's. He wounds Diomedes in the foot, and is sneered at for it as an “archer” (11.369–385). Hector, dying, foretells that Paris and Apollo will kill Achilles at the Scaean gates (22.359–360).

When Hector goes out between the armies to announce the duel, “the long-haired Achaeans kept shooting at him (ἐπετοξάζοντο), taking aim with arrows and pelting him with stones”, until Agamemnon shouts, “Hold, Argives! Do not shoot” (3.79–82; see [[arrows]]). The truce begins by stopping the archers. It ends in Book 4 with an archer's shot, when Pandarus wounds Menelaus with the bow that Apollo gave him (see [[the-bow-of-pandarus]]).
