---
summary: Book 3 uses litotes for obedience (“did not disobey”), for the gifts of the gods (“not to be cast aside”), for praise (Menelaus “not a man of many words, nor one who missed the point”), and for a mist that is “no friend to shepherds”.
refs: [3.11, 3.59, 3.65, 3.120, 3.214-215, 3.224]
---

**“Did not disobey.”** Talthybius, sent to the ships for a lamb, “did not disobey brilliant Agamemnon” (οὐκ ἀπίθησʼ Ἀγαμέμνονι δίῳ, 3.120), the formula of prompt obedience that Book 2 used of Athena and of Agamemnon (2.166, 2.441).

**“Not to be cast aside.”** Paris answers Hector's scorn of his looks: οὔ τοι ἀπόβλητʼ ἐστὶ θεῶν ἐρικυδέα δῶρα, “the glorious gifts of the gods are not to be cast aside” (3.65). The adjective occurs in Homer only here and in Nestor's claim for his own counsel (2.361). The denial means that the gifts are to be prized. See [[gifts-of-the-gods]].

**Fair, and not beyond.** Paris grants that Hector has rebuked him “fairly, and not beyond what is fair” (κατʼ αἶσαν … οὐδʼ ὑπὲρ αἶσαν, 3.59). The denial of the opposite doubles the admission.

**Praise by denial.** Antenor says that Menelaus spoke few words but very clear, “since he was not a man of many words nor one who missed the point” (ἐπεὶ οὐ πολύμυθος / οὐδʼ ἀφαμαρτοεπής, 3.214–215). Of Odysseus' great voice he says, “then we did not so much marvel at Odysseus' looks” (3.224): his words made them forget his appearance. See [[three-styles-of-speech]].

**In a simile.** The mist that the South Wind pours on the peaks is “no friend to shepherds, but better than night for a thief” (ποιμέσιν οὔ τι φίλην, 3.11; see [[mist-on-the-mountain]]).
