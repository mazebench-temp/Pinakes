summary: Paris grants that Hector rebukes him κατʼ αἶσαν, “fairly”, and not ὑπὲρ αἶσαν, “beyond what is fair”.

en: [fairly, beyond what is fair]
passages:
  - 3.58-66 | Paris answers Hector
---

After Hector's rebuke, Paris begins his answer with a concession: “Hector, since you rebuke me fairly and not beyond what is fair” (κατʼ αἶσαν … οὐδʼ ὑπὲρ αἶσαν, 3.59). αἶσα is a portion, and κατʼ αἶσαν is “according to the portion”, in due measure; ὑπὲρ αἶσαν is beyond it. Hector's reproach is exactly what Paris has earned, no more.

The concession is not an apology. The sentence breaks off into a simile of Hector's unyielding heart, like an axe in a shipwright's hand (3.60–63; see [[heart-like-an-axe]]), and ends in a defense: do not reproach me with the gifts of Aphrodite (3.64–66; see [[gifts-of-the-gods]]). In Book 6, when Hector rebukes him again, Paris answers with the same line (6.333). In Book 1 the word measured the short life of Achilles (1.416–418); in Book 3 it measures a rebuke. See [[moira]] and [[neikos]].
