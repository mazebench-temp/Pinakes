---
summary: Helen's answers to Priam on the wall are framed by this formula and its close relative, “Helen, brilliant among women, answered him” (3.171, 3.199, 3.228).
grc: ["ἠμείβετʼ ἔπειθʼ", "ἀμείβετο δῖα γυναικῶν"]
---

Priam asks three times who the Achaean is, and Helen answers three times. Twice the verse ends with her title δῖα γυναικῶν, “brilliant among women” ([[brilliant]]), and the verb stands before it: τὸν δʼ Ἑλένη μύθοισιν ἀμείβετο δῖα γυναικῶν (3.171), and, with her other epithet, τὸν δʼ Ἑλένη τανύπεπλος ἀμείβετο δῖα γυναικῶν, “And Helen of the long robes, brilliant among women, answered him” (3.228; see [[long-robed]]). The middle answer has the formula itself, with ἔπειθʼ elided before Helen's name: τὸν δʼ ἠμείβετʼ ἔπειθʼ Ἑλένη Διὸς ἐκγεγαυῖα, “Then Helen, child of Zeus, answered him” (3.199; see [[daughter-of-zeus]]).

In Book 1 the formula brought in the replies of the quarrel and of the gods. In Book 3 it frames a question and answer between a father-in-law and a daughter-in-law, and every answer is a name. See [[helen]] and [[the-view-from-the-wall]].
