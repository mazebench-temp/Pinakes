Paris' two answers frame his part in the book. To Hector's rebuke, “Then godlike Alexander spoke to him in turn” (τὸν δʼ αὖτε προσέειπεν Ἀλέξανδρος θεοειδής, 3.58), the related opening noted under this entry (1.206), and he begins by granting that Hector has rebuked him “fairly” (3.59). To Helen's taunt at the end, “And answering her with these words, Paris said” (τὴν δὲ Πάρις μύθοισιν ἀμειβόμενος προσέειπε, 3.437), and he begins by rejecting her “harsh reproaches” (3.438). The first answer leads to the duel, the second to bed.

Between them Antenor “answered her face to face” (ἀντίον ηὔδα, 3.203), taking up Helen's word about Odysseus to tell of the embassy (3.204–224; see [[the-embassy-to-troy]]).
