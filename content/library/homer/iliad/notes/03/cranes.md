{{quote:5676e478-163f-51ba-8f88-54e6329cc2fe}}

In Book 2 the cranes were one kind of bird among many in the flocks of the Asian meadow, whose noise and restless motion gave the picture of the army pouring onto the plain (2.459–466). Book 3 gives them a simile of their own, and a war. The cry is their mark: κλαγγή (“clamor”) is said three times in four lines (3.2, 3.3, 3.5). They fly south from the winter and its endless rain to the streams of [[oceanus|Oceanus]] at the edge of the world, “bringing slaughter and doom to the Pygmy men” (ἀνδράσι Πυγμαίοισι φόνον καὶ κῆρα φέρουσαι, 3.6; see [[pygmies]]).

The birds are the Trojans, and their noise is set against the silence of the Achaeans, who come on “breathing fury, eager in their hearts to stand by one another” (3.8–9). The words for the cranes' war are the ones Nestor used of the Argives sailing for Troy, “bringing slaughter and doom to the Trojans” (φόνον καὶ κῆρα φέροντες, 2.352).
