---
summary: Helen weeps twice in Book 3, when Iris' longing sends her to the wall and when she tells Priam she wishes she had died; the war itself is “tearful”.
grc: [κλαίουσα]
en: [in tears]
passages:
  - 3.139-145 | Helen goes to the wall
  - 3.171-176 | Helen answers Priam
---

**Helen's tears.** Iris puts in Helen a longing for her former husband, her city and her parents, and at once she veils herself and hurries from her chamber, “letting her tender tears fall” (τέρεν κατὰ δάκρυ χέουσα, 3.142; see [[shedding-tears]]). It is the formula of Thetis weeping for her son in Book 1 (1.413). On the wall she answers Priam with a wish that she had died before she followed Paris, and ends: “But that did not happen; and so I waste away in tears” (κλαίουσα τέτηκα, 3.176). Her tears are for a life lost, and no one answers them; she turns herself to Priam's question (3.177–178).

**A tearful war.** The war itself is πολύδακρυς, “tearful”. Iris says the armies were bringing “tearful Ares” on one another (3.132), and Priam says that the gods roused against him “the tearful war of the Achaeans” (3.165; see [[tearful]]). The men who fight it do not weep in Book 3. The weeping is done by the woman it is fought for. See [[himeros]] and [[helen]].
