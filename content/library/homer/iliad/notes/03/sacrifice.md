---
summary: Book 3 has no sacrifice in the ordinary sense. The lambs of the oath are killed but not offered: there is no altar, no fire, no portion for the gods and no feast.
except: [3.354]
passages:
  - 3.264-313 | The oath sacrifice
---

The only victims killed in Book 3 are the lambs of the oath, and their killing takes from sacrifice only its first steps. The kings' hands are washed, hair is cut from the victims' heads, Agamemnon prays with lifted hands and cuts the lambs' throats, and wine is poured (3.268–301). Then the rite stops. Nothing is burned for the gods, and nothing is eaten; Priam takes the Trojans' lambs back to the city in his chariot (3.310). A sacrifice was an exchange: the god received the savor, the men shared the meat. An oath's victims are given to no one. Their death is the image of what will happen to the man who breaks his word. See [[oath]] and [[sacrifice-type-scene]].

ῥέζω keeps its everyday sense of “do”. Menelaus prays that men to come may shudder “to do evil (κακὰ ῥέξαι) to a host who has shown him friendship” (3.354), as Iris told Hector in Book 2 to “do as I say” (2.802).
