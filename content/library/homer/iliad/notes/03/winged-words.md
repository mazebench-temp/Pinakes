---
summary: The formula occurs once in Book 3, in an unusual form: when the old men on the wall see Helen, “softly they spoke winged words to one another” (3.155).
grc: ["ἔπεα πτερόεντʼ ἀγόρευον"]
---

“And when they saw Helen coming to the tower, softly they spoke winged words to one another” (ἦκα πρὸς ἀλλήλους ἔπεα πτερόεντʼ ἀγόρευον, 3.154–155). The usual verb, προσηύδα, “addressed”, is said of one speaker to one hearer. Here the verb is ἀγόρευον, plural, the speakers are many, and they speak “softly” (ἦκα) “to one another”. The winged words are a murmur that Helen is not meant to hear.

As often, the formula follows a sight. The old men see Helen coming, and what bursts out is admiration and unease together: “There is no blame that Trojans and well-greaved Achaeans should suffer sorrows for so long for such a woman”, and yet “let her go home in the ships” (3.156–160; see [[no-blame]] and [[trojan-elders]]). The other speeches of the book are brought in by plainer verbs and by the formulas of reply.
