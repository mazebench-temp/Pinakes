summary: The only anger in Book 3 is a goddess's. When Helen refuses to go to Paris' bed, Aphrodite answers her in anger and threatens that in her anger she may let her go.

passages:
  - 3.413-420 | Do not provoke me
---

{{quote:79d9e453-2269-5f6e-a419-69b8f47a58ee}}

Helen has told Aphrodite to sit beside Paris herself and give up the paths of the gods (3.406–409). “Brilliant Aphrodite spoke to her in anger” (χολωσαμένη, 3.413): “do not provoke me, wretched woman, or in my anger (χωσαμένη) I may let you go”. The two words are the two ordinary Homeric words for anger, χολόω and χώομαι, side by side. The threat is to withdraw a goddess's favor: to hate Helen as much as she now loves her, to raise hatreds between both armies, and let her perish by an evil fate. Helen is afraid, and goes (3.418–420). The men of Book 3 rebuke, resent and pray; the anger belongs to a god. See [[philos]], [[metis]] and [[aphrodite]].
