---
summary: The herald Idaeus carries a shining mixing bowl from Troy to the oath. The wine is mixed in it, and the men of both armies draw from it for the libation.
grc: [κρητῆρα, κρητῆρι]
---

The bowl comes with the offerings from Troy: “a shining mixing bowl was carried by the herald Idaeus, and golden cups” (κρητῆρα φαεινόν, 3.247–248). At the oath the heralds mix the wine in it (κρητῆρι δὲ οἶνον / μίσγον, 3.269–270), and after the lambs are killed “drawing wine from the mixing bowl in cups they poured it out” (3.295–296). One bowl, brought from Priam's city, serves both sides of the oath. In Book 1 the mixing bowls were filled for a feast of the Achaeans at Chryse (1.470); here the wine in the bowl is for pouring out, not for drinking. See [[libation]], [[cup]] and [[idaeus]].
