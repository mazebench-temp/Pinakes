Before the duel, Hector and Odysseus, one from each side, measure out the ground and then shake the lots “in a bronze-fitted helmet, to see which of the two should first let fly his bronze spear” (3.316–317). While the lots are shaken the people of both armies pray, with lifted hands, that whichever of the two brought these troubles may die (3.318–323). Hector shakes them “looking away” (ἂψ ὁρόων), so that the choice is not his:

The lot gives Paris the first throw, and his throw fails (3.346–349; see [[single-combat]] and [[helmet]]).
