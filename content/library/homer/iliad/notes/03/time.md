---
summary: In Book 3 τιμή is compensation. Agamemnon's oath requires the Trojans to pay the Argives a satisfaction “that will be remembered among men who are yet to come”, and at the end of the book he claims it.
passages:
  - 3.281-291 | The terms of the oath
  - 3.455-461 | Agamemnon claims the victory
---

The word comes three times in Book 3, always in the sense that Achilles gave it when he said the army came to Troy “to win satisfaction (τιμήν) for Menelaus” (1.159): the price paid to make good a wrong.

Agamemnon's oath sets it among the terms. If Menelaus kills Paris, the Trojans are to give back Helen and the possessions, “and pay the Argives such satisfaction (τιμήν) as is fitting, one that will be remembered among men who are yet to come” (3.286–287). If Priam and his sons refuse to pay it, he will fight on “for the sake of the penalty” (εἵνεκα ποινῆς, 3.288–290; see [[poine]]). The satisfaction is more than restitution. It is to be large enough to be remembered, a mark of the Atreidae's honor for men to come (see [[kleos]]).

The terms that Paris proposed and Hector announced mention only Helen and the possessions (3.71–72, 3.92–93). The τιμή is Agamemnon's addition, and he claims it in the same words when Paris has vanished: “give back Argive Helen and the possessions with her, and pay such satisfaction as is fitting” (3.458–460).
