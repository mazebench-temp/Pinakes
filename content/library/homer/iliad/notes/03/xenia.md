The word family comes three times in Book 3, and each time it looks back to the world before the war.

**Antenor's guests.** When Helen names Odysseus, Antenor confirms her word from his own memory.

{{quote:44f89616-789d-51d9-8c87-874d535a7106}}

Before the war Odysseus and Menelaus came to Troy on an embassy about Helen, and Antenor “received them as guests” (ἐξείνισσα) and “welcomed them in my halls” (φίλησα). He came to know their build and their counsels at his own table. The Trojan who hosted the envoys is the one who in Book 7 proposes to give Helen back (7.347–353). The embassy was not received by everyone as Antenor received it. In Book 11 Agamemnon kills two sons of Antimachus, who had urged in the Trojan assembly that Menelaus be killed on the spot when he came as an envoy with Odysseus (11.138–142). See [[antenor]] and [[the-embassy-to-troy]].

**The house in Sparta.** Helen names Idomeneus and adds a memory of home: “Often Menelaus, dear to Ares, entertained him (ξείνισσεν) in our house, whenever he came from Crete” (3.232–233). It is a rare glimpse of her life as Menelaus' wife, and it is a picture of a hospitable house. Paris was once received in that house as a guest.

**The host.** Menelaus prays before his spear-cast.

{{quote:d5f22761-0101-592a-98d1-97e34d0d6970}}

Paris is “the man who first wronged me” (ὅ με πρότερος κάκʼ ἔοργε), and the wrong is named in the last line: “to do evil to a host who has shown him friendship” (ξεινοδόκον κακὰ ῥέξαι, ὅ κεν φιλότητα παράσχῃ). Menelaus asks for more than revenge. He asks for an example, so that “any man of those born hereafter may shudder” at the crime. The prayer is not granted. The spear pierces shield and corselet but does not wound him, the sword shatters on Paris' helmet (3.355–363), and Menelaus complains that no god is more baneful than Zeus (3.365). In Book 13 he makes the same charge again, and names the god he expects to punish it, “Zeus who thunders loud, / god of guests” (13.623–625). See [[philotes]], [[poine]] and [[who-is-to-blame]].
