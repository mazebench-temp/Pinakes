---
summary: Hector wishes that Paris had long ago “put on a tunic of stone”, and in the duel Menelaus' spear cuts through Paris' tunic beside the flank but does not touch the man.
passages:
  - 3.355-360 | Menelaus' throw
---

Hector's rebuke of Paris ends with the tunic of stone: “But the Trojans are great cowards; or else by now you would have put on a tunic of stone (λάϊνον ἕσσο χιτῶνα) for all the evil you have done” (3.56–57). He means that the people would have stoned him to death, and the image dresses Paris in the stones that would have killed him. See [[tunic-of-stone]], [[hector]] and [[paris]].

In the duel the tunic is the last layer. Menelaus' spear goes through the shield and the corselet, “and straight on beside the flank it cut through the tunic” (ἀντικρὺ δὲ παραὶ λαπάρην διάμησε χιτῶνα, 3.359), “but he swerved aside and avoided black doom” (3.360). See [[corselet]] and [[spear]].

Aphrodite's picture of Paris in his bedroom soon after, “shining with beauty and fine clothes” (εἵμασιν, 3.392), shows him dressed for love instead (see [[dance]]).
