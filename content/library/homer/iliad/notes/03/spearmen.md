---
summary: Hector calls Helen's people a people of spearmen, and Helen calls Agamemnon “a strong spearman.”
grc: [αἰχμητής]
---

Hector's rebuke describes Helen as “the bride of a people of spearmen” (νυὸν ἀνδρῶν αἰχμητάων, 3.49). The word explains the danger: Paris took a woman from men who would come with spears to get her back. On the wall Helen describes the leader of those men:

{{quote:469d741a-d3e0-5bf1-88a6-a3f1e92361bc}}

“Both a good king and a strong spearman” (ἀμφότερον βασιλεύς τʼ ἀγαθὸς κρατερός τʼ αἰχμητής, 3.179) names the two sides of a king's worth, rule and war. In Book 1 Agamemnon had used the word to confine Achilles to fighting ({{ref:Iliad 1.290}}). Here the word is praise, and it belongs to Agamemnon himself ([[wide-ruling]]).
