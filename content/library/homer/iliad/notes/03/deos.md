---
summary: Book 3 begins and ends in fear. Paris shrinks back in fear of Menelaus, and Hector calls the Trojans cowards for not stoning him; Helen imagines her brothers afraid of her shame, and at the end she is afraid of Aphrodite and obeys.
passages:
  - 3.30-37 | Paris shrinks back
  - 3.413-420 | Helen is afraid
---

**Paris.** When Paris sees Menelaus among the champions, “his heart was struck with fear” and he drew back into the crowd, like a man who starts back from a serpent in the mountains, trembling seizing his limbs and pallor his cheeks, “godlike Alexander, in fear of the son of Atreus” (δείσας, 3.30–37; see [[man-who-meets-a-serpent]]). The champion in the leopard skin is the book's first coward. Hector adds that the Trojans are “great cowards” (δειδήμονες) for not having stoned him long ago (3.56; see [[tunic-of-stone]]).

**Shame.** Helen supposes that her brothers stay out of the battle “for fear of the shame and the many reproaches that are mine” (αἴσχεα δειδιότες, 3.242). The fear of what others will say is the fear of αἰδώς (see [[aidos]] and [[aischros]]). Her own first words to Priam join respect and fear: he is to her αἰδοῖος and δεινός, “one I respect … and one I fear” (3.172).

**Helen and the goddess.** When Aphrodite threatens to hate her and destroy her, “Helen, child of Zeus, was afraid” (ἔδεισεν, 3.418), and she goes. The line has the form of 1.33, where Chryses is afraid of Agamemnon and obeys, and 1.568, where Hera is afraid of Zeus and sits in silence. In each case the weaker yields to the stronger, and in each the fear is followed by silence: Chryses walks in silence along the shore (1.34), Hera sits in silence (1.569), and Helen goes in silence past the Trojan women (3.419–420). See [[silence]] and [[obedience-and-persuasion]].

Other words of fear fill the book. Priam shudders (ῥίγησεν) at the summons to the oath (3.259), and Menelaus prays that men to come may shudder (ἐρρίγῃσι) at wronging a host (3.353).
