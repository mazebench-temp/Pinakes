κήρ comes four times in Book 3, and three times it follows Paris.

**The cranes.** The Trojans come on with clamor like cranes flying to the streams of Oceanus, “bringing slaughter and doom (κῆρα) to the Pygmy men” (3.6; see [[cranes-and-pygmies]]).

**Avoiding doom.** When Paris sees Menelaus among the champions, his heart is struck with fear “and he drew back into the crowd of his companions, avoiding doom” (κῆρʼ ἀλεείνων, 3.32). In the duel, Menelaus' spear goes through his shield and corselet and cuts his tunic, “but he swerved aside and avoided black doom” (ἀλεύατο κῆρα μέλαιναν, 3.360; see [[black-doom]]). The same verb, ἀλέομαι, “avoid”, twice: the man who runs from the fight and the man who dodges the spear-point are the same, and each time his doom passes him by.

**Like black doom.** At the end of the book, Menelaus ranges the throng looking for him, and no Trojan will show him where Paris is, “for he was hateful to them all, like black doom” (ἶσον … κηρὶ μελαίνῃ, 3.454; see [[like-black-doom]]). The doom Paris escaped in the duel has become what his own people see in him. See [[paris]] and [[thanatos]].
