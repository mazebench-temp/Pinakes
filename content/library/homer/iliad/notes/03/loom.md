---
summary: Iris finds Helen at a great loom in her hall, weaving into a purple cloth the struggles that Trojans and Achaeans suffer for her sake (3.125–128).
passages:
  - 3.125-128 | Helen at the loom
---

“She found her in the hall. She was weaving a great web” (τὴν δʼ εὗρʼ ἐν μεγάρῳ· ἣ δὲ μέγαν ἱστὸν ὕφαινε, 3.125). ἱστός is both the upright loom and the web stretched on it, and the web is “great”, a double-folded purple cloth. In Book 1 the loom stood for the fate of a captive woman, who would walk to and fro at her master's loom in Argos (1.29–31). Helen works at her own, in her husband's house, the mistress of the hall; but what she weaves is the war that is being fought over her. Iris calls her away from it to watch the fighting itself (3.130–138). See [[weaving]] and [[helens-web]].
