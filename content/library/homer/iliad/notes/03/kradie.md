---
summary: Paris tells Hector that his heart is always unyielding, like an axe in a shipwright's hand.
grc: [κραδίη]
passages:
  - 3.59-63 | Paris answers Hector
---

Paris answers Hector's rebuke with a portrait of his brother: “your heart (κραδίη) is always unyielding, like an axe that goes through a beam in the hand of a man who with skill shapes a ship's timber, and it adds to the man's force; so fearless is the mind in your breast” (3.60–63). The κραδίη is the seat of courage, and Hector's is as hard and sharp as bronze. The compliment sets the brothers apart: Hector's heart does not give way, and Paris' was just struck with fear at the sight of Menelaus (φίλον ἦτορ, 3.31). See [[heart-like-an-axe]] and [[noos]].
