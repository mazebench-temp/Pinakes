When Paris sees Menelaus, “he drew back into the crowd of his companions (ἑτάρων), avoiding doom” (3.32). Hector's rebuke recalls the other time Paris led companions: “gathering your trusty companions” (ἑτάρους ἐρίηρας, 3.47), he sailed to a distant land and brought back Helen. The companions who followed him then protect him now.

Menelaus' companions receive his one prize from the duel. He flings Paris' empty helmet among the Achaeans, “and his trusty companions picked it up” (ἐρίηρες ἑταῖροι, 3.378). At the oath Priam tells his companions (ἑταίρους) to yoke the horses for the drive to the plain (3.259–260). See [[paris]] and [[menelaus]].
