summary: A wild goat is the other quarry the hungry lion may find in the simile for Menelaus (3.24), and the wine for the oaths is carried from the city in a goatskin (3.247).

en: [wild goat, goatskin]
---

The goats of Book 1 were victims, and those of Book 2 a working flock. Book 3 has a wild goat and a goat's hide.

**The wild goat.** When Menelaus sees Alexander, he rejoices “as a lion rejoices when he comes upon a great carcass, finding an antlered stag or a wild goat” (ἄγριον αἶγα, 3.23–24). The ἄγριος αἴξ is the goat of the mountains, a hunter's quarry, like the one from whose horns Pandarus made his bow (4.105–111). See [[lion-on-a-carcass]] and [[deer]].

**The goatskin.** The heralds carry through the city “two lambs, and wine that cheers the heart, the fruit of the earth, in a goatskin” (ἀσκῷ ἐν αἰγείῳ, 3.246–247), with the mixing bowl and the golden cups for the oaths. An ἀσκός is a skin bag, and αἴγειος is “of a goat”. The wine in it is poured out at the sacrifice, with the prayer that the oath-breakers' brains may pour out on the ground like it (3.295–301; see [[like-this-wine]] and [[the-oaths-sworn]]).
