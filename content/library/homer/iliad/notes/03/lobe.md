---
summary: Hector wishes that Paris had never been born, rather than be “a disgrace like this, and scorned by others”.
passages:
  - 3.38-45 | Hector rebukes Paris
---

Hector's rebuke begins with a wish that Paris had never been born or had died unmarried, “and it would be far better than for you to be a disgrace (λώβη) like this, and scorned (ὑπόψιον) by others” (3.40–42; see [[never-born]]). In Books 1 and 2 λώβη was an insult one man did to another. Here Paris himself is the outrage: his presence in Troy is an insult to his family and a joke to the enemy (3.43–51). ὑπόψιος, “looked at askance”, is formed from ὑπό and the root of ὄψομαι, “see”: he is watched with contempt. See [[aischros]] and [[oneidos]].
