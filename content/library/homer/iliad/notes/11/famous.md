# !περικλυτὸς ἀμφιγυήεις leaves Hephaestus' title to lame-in-both-legs.
grc: ["κλυτὰ τεύχεʼ", περικλυτός, "!περικλυτὸς ἀμφιγυήεις"]
en: [renowned, glorious armor]
---

Before Iphidamas comes forward, the poet asks the Muses who first faced Agamemnon, “whether of the Trojans themselves or of their famous allies” (11.218–220; [[invocation-of-the-muses]], [[trojan-allies]]). Antiphus, the son of Priam born in wedlock, is περικλυτός, “renowned,” as he stands beside his bastard brother Isus in the chariot (11.102–104; [[antiphus-son-of-priam]], [[isus]]). And Diomedes takes from the sons of Merops “their glorious armor” (κλυτὰ τεύχεα, 11.334; [[the-sons-of-merops]]), the armor he meant to strip from Aeneas in Book 5 (5.435). See [[kleos]].
