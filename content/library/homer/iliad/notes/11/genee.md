**Lineage and age.** Menoetius' words divide the two senses of the word that the article above distinguishes. Elsewhere γενεῇ with a comparative ranks men by order of birth: Podarces was “younger in birth” than his brother (2.707), Bucolion “the eldest by birth” among Laomedon's sons (6.24), and Agamemnon claims to be “the elder by birth” (9.161). Here age is set on the other side of the scale, “but you are the elder” (πρεσβύτερος, 11.787), so γενεῇ … ὑπέρτερος is “higher in lineage” (see the translation's note on 11.786). Achilles is the son of the goddess Thetis and of Peleus, and Peleus is the son of Aeacus, son of Zeus, as Achilles will tell Asteropaeus, claiming to be “of the race of great Zeus” (21.187–191; see [[achilles]], [[thetis]] and [[aeacus]]). The charge gives each friend his rank, lineage and strength to Achilles, age and counsel to Patroclus, and asks the higher to listen to the elder, “to his good” (11.789; see [[menoetius]] and [[the-persuasion-of-a-friend]]).

**Eldest-born.** Coon, who avenges his brother Iphidamas on Agamemnon, is “the eldest-born son of Antenor” (πρεσβυγενὴς Ἀντηνορίδης, 11.249), in a compound found only here (see [[coon]] and [[kasignetos]]). Mulius, the first man Nestor killed, had Augeas' “eldest daughter, fair-haired Agamede” (πρεσβυτάτην, 11.740; see [[agamede]]).
