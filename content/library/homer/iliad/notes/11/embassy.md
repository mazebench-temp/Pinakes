summary: Agamemnon refuses to spare the sons of Antimachus because their father urged the Trojans to kill Menelaus when he came to Troy on an embassy with Odysseus (11.138–142). Nestor recalls another errand, when he and Odysseus went through Achaea gathering the army and came to Peleus' house (11.769–770).

**The embassy to Troy.** The sons of Antimachus beg Agamemnon to take them alive for ransom, and he answers: “If you two are indeed the sons of wise-hearted Antimachus, who once in the Trojans' assembly urged that Menelaus, when he came on an embassy with godlike Odysseus, be killed on the spot, and not be let go back to the Achaeans, now you will pay for your father's shameful insult” (11.138–142). ἀγγελίην ἐλθόντα, “when he came on an embassy”, recalls Antenor's words in Book 3: Odysseus came “on an embassy about you” with Menelaus, and Antenor entertained them in his house (3.205–208; see [[the-embassy-to-troy]]). Only here is it said that the Trojans debated killing the envoys, and that Antimachus, who had taken Alexander's gold, was the man who proposed it (11.123–125, 11.139–141; see [[antimachus]] and [[supplication]]). The envoy was a guest under the gods' protection, and his host's honor was bound up with his safety (see [[xenia]] and [[heralds]]). The same morning Agamemnon kills two sons of Antenor, the host, and two sons of Antimachus, who would have killed the guests (11.122–147, 11.221–263).

**Gathering the army.** Nestor reminds Patroclus of the day when “we had come to the well-appointed house of Peleus, gathering the army throughout the bountiful land of Achaea” (11.769–770). He and Odysseus went from house to house to raise men for the expedition, and at Phthia they found Peleus sacrificing in his courtyard, and took Achilles and Patroclus away with them (11.771–782; see [[peleus]] and [[peleus-sends-achilles-to-troy]]). Nestor had spoken in Book 7 of the joy with which Peleus, in his house, had once asked him the lineage of all the Argives (7.125–128), and Odysseus in Book 9 of Peleus' charge to his son (9.252–259).
