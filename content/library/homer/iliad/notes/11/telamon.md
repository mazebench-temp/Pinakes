summary: Telamon is named in Book 11 only in his son's titles, as Ajax holds one end of the line, comes to the rescue of Odysseus and covers the Achaean retreat (11.7, 11.465, 11.526, 11.542, 11.563, 11.591).
# Τελαμώνιον υἱόν (11.563, 11.591) counts for the father. At 15.462 Τελαμώνιον is Teucer, also his son.
grc: [Τελαμώνιον]
---

Four forms of the father's name serve the son here. The patronymic Τελαμωνιάδης, “son of Telamon”, places Ajax's huts at one end of the camp (11.7 = 8.224) and names the man whom Hector avoids (11.542; see [[ajax]]). Menelaus calls him with the full address, Αἶαν διογενὲς Τελαμώνιε κοίρανε λαῶν, “Telamonian Ajax, sprung from Zeus, lord of the people” (11.465 = 7.234 = 9.644), and Cebriones knows Αἴας … Τελαμώνιος, “Telamonian Ajax”, by his broad shield (11.526; see [[telamonian]]). New in this book is the adjective with “son” spelled out, Αἴαντα μέγαν Τελαμώνιον υἱόν, “great Ajax, the son of Telamon”, used in the donkey simile and in Eurypylus' call to stand around him (11.563, 11.591; see [[eurypylus]]).
