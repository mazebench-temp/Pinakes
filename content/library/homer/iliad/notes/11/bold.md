---
summary: The spears flung at the lion of the simile and at Ajax's shield come “from bold hands.”
grc: [θρασειάων]
---

In the simile, the javelins dart against the lion driven from the farmyard “from bold hands” (11.552–553; [[lion-driven-from-the-farmyard]]), and the same words return in the narrative a few verses later: the spears “from bold hands” stick in Ajax's great shield or stand fixed in the ground, longing to glut themselves on flesh (11.571–574; [[seven-oxhide]]). The formula θρασειάων ἀπὸ χειρῶν passes from the herdsmen of the simile to the Trojans who pursue Ajax. See [[ajax]].
