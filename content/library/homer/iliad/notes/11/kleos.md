summary: Both times the noun comes in Book 11 it is the report that travels: “the great report” of the expedition reaches Cinyras in Cyprus, who sends Agamemnon a corselet, and Iphidamas leaves his bride in Thrace “at the report of the Achaeans” and comes to Troy to die. The Muses are asked whether the first man to face Agamemnon was a Trojan or one of the “famous allies”.
passages:
  - 11.19-23 | Cinyras' gift
  - 11.218-231 | Iphidamas
---

**The report that travels.** κλέος is what is heard (see the article above), and in Book 11 it is heard far from Troy. Agamemnon's corselet was a gift from Cinyras, “for he had heard in Cyprus the great report that the Achaeans were about to sail up to Troy in their ships” (11.21–22; see [[cinyras]], [[cyprus]] and [[corselet]]). The digitized text divides Κύπρον δέ; read as Κύπρονδε, “to Cyprus”, it has the report travel there (see the translation's note on 11.21). The news brings a guest-gift to the king (see [[xenia]]).

The same news brings Iphidamas to his death. Reared in Thrace by Cisseus and married to his daughter, “newly married he came from the bridal chamber at the report of the Achaeans” (μετὰ κλέος, 11.227), with twelve ships that he left at Percote, and he was the first man to face Agamemnon after the invocation (11.218–231; see [[iphidamas]]). The phrase returns in Book 13 for Othryoneus, who “had newly come” to Troy πολέμοιο μετὰ κλέος, “at the report of war”, asking for Cassandra without a bride-price, and is killed by Idomeneus (13.363–382). Both came for the war as young men with a marriage on their minds, and the poem shows each of them only to kill him (see [[death-in-battle]]). In the Odyssey Telemachus goes to Sparta μετὰ σὸν κλέος, “after report of you”, Athena tells Odysseus (*Odyssey* 13.414–415).

**Famous allies.** Iphidamas' entrance is introduced with the verse that opened the Catalogue, “Tell me now, Muses, you who have your homes on Olympus” (11.218 = 2.484), where the poet added that mortals “hear only the report, and know nothing” (2.486; see [[invocation-of-the-muses]] and [[we-hear-only-the-report]]). The Muses are asked who came first against Agamemnon, “whether of the Trojans themselves or of their famous allies” (κλειτῶν ἐπικούρων, 11.220; see [[trojan-allies]]). The answer is both at once: a son of Antenor, raised among the Thracians.
