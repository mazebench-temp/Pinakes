---
summary: War becomes sweeter to the Achaeans than going home to their dear native land; Patroclus laments the leaders who will die far from their native land.
---

Strife's shout makes war “sweeter to them than to go home in the hollow ships to their dear native land” (11.13–14 = 2.453–454; [[hollow-ships]], [[strife]]). At the end of the book Patroclus sees the wounded Eurypylus and laments for the leaders of the Danaans: “so you were destined, far from your friends and your native land, to glut the swift dogs in Troy with your white fat” (11.816–818; [[wretch]], [[swift-dogs]], [[white]]). See [[nostos]].
