---
summary: In Nestor's story the heralds of Pylos cry out at dawn for all to whom Elis owes a debt to come and share in the spoil of the raid (11.685–686).
grc: [ἐλίγαινον]
en: [cried out shrilly]
---

“And the heralds cried out shrilly when dawn appeared for all to come to whom a debt was owed in shining Elis” (κήρυκες δʼ ἐλίγαινον ἅμʼ ἠοῖ φαινομένηφι, 11.685–686). λιγαίνω, “cry out clearly, shrilly”, occurs only here in Homer; it belongs with the clear voice for which heralds are named λιγύφθογγοι, “clear-voiced” (2.50; see [[clear-voiced]]). The heralds call the people together, as they summon the Achaeans to assembly and to battle (2.50–52, 2.442–444), and the leading men of the Pylians gather and divide the spoil (11.687–705; see [[division-of-spoils]] and [[nestor-and-the-epeians]]). The summons at dawn and the division that follows are a small assembly of the Pylian people (see [[assembly]]).
