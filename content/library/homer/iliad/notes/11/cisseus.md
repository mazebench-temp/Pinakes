Book 6 names him only in his daughter's patronymic, Κισσηΐς (6.299); here he is named and acts. He raised the boy “in his house when he was small” and was his μητροπάτωρ, “his mother's father” (11.223–224), a word found only here. When Iphidamas came of age, Cisseus “kept him there, and gave him his daughter” (11.226), in the verse of the Lycian king who kept Bellerophon (6.192; see [[bellerophon]]). The bride-price that Iphidamas paid, a hundred cattle given and a thousand goats and sheep promised from the flocks “pastured for him in untold numbers” (11.244–245), was the price of a wife from his grandfather's house; compare the daughter Agamemnon offers Achilles “without bride-price” (9.146; see [[marriage]]). The bridegroom left her at the report of the Achaeans' coming, and died at Troy (11.227–247; see [[iphidamas]] and [[thrace]]).
