summary: In Nestor's story the Epeians, here once called Eleans, are the enemies of his youth. Haughty after Heracles had weakened Pylos, they owed the Pylians many debts; after Nestor's cattle raid they besieged Thryoessa, and they were routed by the Alpheus and driven back to Buprasium (11.671–761).

**Two names.** The story opens with “a quarrel … between the Eleans and us” (Ἠλείοισι, 11.671), the only time Homer calls them Eleans, the name of the people of Elis in later times. Everywhere else, here as in the Catalogue, they are Epeians (11.688, 11.694, 11.732, 11.737, 11.744; 2.619; see [[elis]]).

**The wrongs.** “The Epeians owed a debt to many” in Pylos (11.688). After Heracles had killed the best of the Pylians, they grew “haughty because of this” and “treated us with outrage and devised reckless deeds” (ὑπερηφανέοντες … ἡμέας ὑβρίζοντες ἀτάσθαλα μηχανόωντο, 11.694–695; see [[heracles-sacks-pylos]]), and their king Augeas kept Neleus' prize horses (11.698–702; see [[augeas]]). The war is told as the Pylians' redress.

**The war.** On the third day after Nestor's raid they “came all together, the men in great numbers and their single-hoofed horses, / with all speed”, the young Moliones arming among them, and besieged Thryoessa, “eager to destroy it utterly” (11.707–713, 11.732–733; see [[the-moliones]] and [[thryum]]). When Nestor killed Mulius, “the leader of the horsemen”, they “fled in fear, this way and that” (11.744–746; see [[mulius]]), and the Pylians pursued them over the plain, “killing them and gathering up their fine armor”, as far as Buprasium (11.753–760; see [[nestor-and-the-epeians]]). Nestor gives his enemies their epithets: they are “bronze-armored” and “great-hearted” (χαλκοχίτωνες 11.694, μεγάθυμοι 11.732, 11.744; see [[bronze-armored]] and [[great-hearted]]).
