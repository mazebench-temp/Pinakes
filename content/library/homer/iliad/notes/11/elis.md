---
summary: In Nestor's story Elis is the land of the Epeians, “shining Elis”, which owed the Pylians many debts: the young Nestor raided its cattle, and its king Augeas had kept Neleus' prize horses (11.671–705).
grc: [Ἤλιδι]
---

Elis is named three times, always in the dative of place. Itymoneus, the first man Nestor killed in the raid, “lived in Elis” (11.673; see [[itymoneus]]). The heralds of Pylos summoned all “to whom a debt was owed in shining Elis” (ἐν Ἤλιδι δίῃ, 11.686; see [[brilliant]]), and to Neleus too “a great debt was owed in shining Elis” (11.698): the four prize horses that had gone there to race for a tripod, and that Augeas kept (11.698–702; see [[augeas]] and [[neleus]]). So the story begins as a matter of debts, and Nestor's raid takes by force what Elis owed (11.672–674; see [[raiding]] and [[nestor-and-the-epeians]]).

Its people are called Eleans once, in the verse that opens the story (Ἠλείοισι, 11.671), and Epeians everywhere else (see [[epeians]]). As in the Catalogue, Elis is a land, not a city. The only town of it named in the story is Buprasium, where the Pylians' pursuit stops, with the Olenian Rock and Alesium, the points that enclose the land of the Epeians in the Catalogue (11.756–758; 2.615–617; see [[buprasium]], [[olenian-rock]] and [[alesium]]).
