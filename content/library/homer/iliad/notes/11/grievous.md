---
summary: A “grievous pain” goes through Diomedes' flesh as he draws out Paris' arrow.
---

Odysseus stands in front of Diomedes, who sits down behind him and pulls the arrow out of his foot, “and a grievous pain went through his flesh” (11.397–398; [[swift-shaft]]). He mounts his chariot and has himself driven to the ships, in the verses used of Agamemnon (11.399–400 = 11.273–274; [[hollow-ships]]). The other adjective rendered “grievous” in this book is ἀργαλέος, of Strife (11.4), of the din of battle (11.278) and of Eurypylus' wound (11.812). See [[diomedes]].
