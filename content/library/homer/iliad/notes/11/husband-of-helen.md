---
summary: “Alexander, the husband of Helen of the lovely hair,” shoots Diomedes and Machaon.
---

The verse names Paris twice, each time as he draws his bow: “But Alexander, the husband of Helen of the lovely hair, was bending his bow at the son of Tydeus” (11.369–370; [[man-made]]), and the Achaeans would have held their ground by the Scamander “had not Alexander, the husband of Helen of the lovely hair, stopped Machaon” (11.505–506; [[three-barbed]]). The title that names him by the cause of the war belongs to the archer whose arrows take three Achaean leaders out of the battle ([[the-wounded-chiefs]]). See [[paris]] and [[lovely-haired]].
