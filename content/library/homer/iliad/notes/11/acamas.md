# The phrase, because Ἀκάμαντʼ is also the Thracian Acamas (6.8; acamas-of-thrace, which excepts 11.60).
grc: ["ἠΐθεόν τʼ Ἀκάμαντʼ"]
passages:
  - 11.56-60 | The Trojan leaders on the rise of the plain
---

ἠΐθεόν τʼ Ἀκάμαντʼ ἐπιείκελον ἀθανάτοισιν, “and young Acamas, like the immortals” (11.60; see [[like-the-immortals]]). ἠΐθεος is a young man not yet married: the word for Simoeisius, “the young man in his prime” (4.474; see [[simoeisius]]), and for the young men who dance with the girls on the shield of Achilles (18.593). The book that calls him young kills his brother Iphidamas, who had left his bride to come to the war (11.221–247; see [[iphidamas]] and [[antenor]]). Acamas does nothing more in Book 11. He goes against the Achaean wall in the company of Aeneas (12.98–100) and avenges his brother Archelochus in Book 14 (14.476–485).
