summary: In Nestor's story the Epeians, haughty after Heracles had left Pylos weak, “treated us with outrage and devised reckless deeds”, and the reckless are routed. The half-verse becomes the Odyssey's formula for the suitors.
grc: [ἀτάσθαλα]
en: [reckless deeds]
passages:
  - 11.689-695 | The outrage of the Epeians
---

Nestor gives the cause of his first war in one verse: the Epeians “treated us with outrage and devised reckless deeds” (ἡμέας ὑβρίζοντες ἀτάσθαλα μηχανόωντο, 11.695; see [[hybris]]). ἀτάσθαλα is the neuter plural of the adjective, “reckless things”, and as the article above describes, the word is a judgment made from the end of the story: the Epeians who devised them lose Mulius, fifty chariots and the field, and are driven to Buprasium (11.737–761; see [[nestor-and-the-epeians]]). In the Odyssey the same half-verse names the suitors, who also die for their recklessness (*Odyssey* 3.207, 17.588, 20.170, 20.370; compare 22.413–416).
