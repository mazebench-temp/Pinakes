---
summary: Dardanus is named in Book 11 only in a title, “descendant of Dardanus”, given twice to Ilus, whose tomb stands in the middle of the Trojan plain (11.166, 11.372).
grc: [Δαρδανίδαο]
---

παρʼ Ἴλου σῆμα παλαιοῦ Δαρδανίδαο, “past the mound of ancient Ilus, descendant of Dardanus” (11.166), and Ἴλου Δαρδανίδαο, παλαιοῦ δημογέροντος, “of Ilus, descendant of Dardanus, an elder of the people long ago” (11.372). Δαρδανίδης is otherwise Priam's title (3.303, 7.366; 5.159), and Ilus is the great-grandson of Dardanus in Aeneas' account of the line (20.215–232; see [[ilus]], [[the-tomb-of-ilus]] and [[priam]]). The people named after Dardanus are in the book too: Hector rallies the “Dardanians who fight hand to hand” (11.286; see [[dardanians]]).
