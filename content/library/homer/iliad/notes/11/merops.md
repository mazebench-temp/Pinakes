The seer exists in the poem only in these verses, which it gives twice (2.831–834 = 11.329–332). In the Catalogue they foretell; in Book 11 they are fulfilled, and the verse that says “the fates of black death were leading them on” is followed by the sons' death at the hands of Diomedes (11.332–334; see [[the-sons-of-merops]] and [[diomedes]]). μαντοσύναι, “the art of prophecy” (11.330), was his above all men, and it did not save his sons (see [[prophecy]] and [[divination]]). Περκωσίου, “of Percote”, ties him to the town on the Hellespont where Iphidamas left his ships a hundred lines before (11.229; see [[percote]]).
