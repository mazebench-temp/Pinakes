---
summary: The South Wind is named once in Book 11, in the simile for Hector's charge, where the West Wind batters the clouds of “the brightening South Wind” (11.305–306).
grc: [Νότοιο]
---

The clouds of the simile are the South Wind's, and the West Wind breaks them with a squall and drives up the sea, as Hector beats down the Achaeans (11.304–309; see [[the-west-wind-and-the-clouds]], [[zephyrus]] and [[hector]]). ἀργεστᾶο Νότοιο, “the brightening South Wind”, returns only when Hera promises a storm of the West Wind and the brightening South Wind against the Trojans (21.334). ἀργεστής is taken as “clearing, brightening” or as “white”, of the clouds or squalls the wind brings; Homer gives it only to the South Wind, Hesiod only to the West Wind (*Theogony* 379, 870; see [[brightening]] and the translation's note on 11.306). Two winds contend again in a simile of Book 16, where the East Wind and the South Wind shake a deep forest in the glens of a mountain, and the battle they stand for is told in two verses of this book (16.765–771; 16.770–771 = 11.70–71).
