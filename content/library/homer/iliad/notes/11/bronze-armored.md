---
summary: Nestor calls the Epeians “bronze-armored” as he tells how they treated the Pylians with outrage.
grc: [χαλκοχίτωνες]
---

“Haughty because of this, the bronze-armored Epeians treated us with outrage and devised reckless deeds” (11.694–695): the Epeians took advantage of Pylos, weakened by Heracles' attack, in which eleven of Neleus' twelve sons were killed (11.689–693; [[heracles-sacks-pylos]], [[epeians]]). The epithet that usually belongs to the Achaeans at Troy is given in Nestor's story to the Epeians of Elis; in the next generation the Epeians fight at Troy among the Achaeans, led by the sons of the Moliones and others (2.615–624; [[the-moliones]]). See [[nestor-and-the-epeians]].
