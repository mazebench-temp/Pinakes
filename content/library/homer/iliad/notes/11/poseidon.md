**The bull.** On the march to Thryoessa the Pylians reach the Alpheus at midday and offer “fine sacrifices to Zeus, supreme in might, / and a bull to Alpheus, and a bull to Poseidon” (11.727–728; see [[alpheus]], [[sacrifice]] and [[nestor-and-the-epeians]]). Poseidon is the god of Neleus' house. In the Odyssey Tyro bears Neleus to him (*Odyssey* 11.235–254), and when Telemachus comes to Pylos the people are sacrificing black bulls to him on the shore (*Odyssey* 3.5–8; see [[neleus]] and [[pylos]]).

**The Moliones.** After Nestor has killed Mulius and taken fifty chariots, the twins are in his way:

{{quote:7301f7e9-f5d0-5d5e-a39f-438ad731e451}}

They are “the two sons of Actor” by name and Poseidon's by birth (see [[the-moliones]], [[actor-of-elis]], [[would-have-had-not]] and [[earth-shaker]]). εὐρὺ κρείων, “wide-ruling”, is otherwise the title of Agamemnon (11.107, 11.238); only here is it given to Poseidon (see [[wide-ruling]]). He saves them as gods save their own at Troy, and as he himself will save Aeneas from Achilles by pouring a mist over Achilles' eyes (20.318–329). The Pylians had sacrificed to him on the eve of the battle, and he saves their enemies; the poem does not comment.
