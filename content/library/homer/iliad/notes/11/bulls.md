summary: In Nestor's story the Pylians, camped by the river Alpheus on the eve of battle, sacrifice to Zeus, offer a bull to the river and a bull to Poseidon, and to Athena a cow from the herd (11.726–729).
grc: [ταῦρον]
en: [bull]
passages:
  - 11.726-730 | Sacrifice at the Alpheus
---

ἔνθα Διὶ ῥέξαντες ὑπερμενεῖ ἱερὰ καλά, / ταῦρον δʼ Ἀλφειῷ, ταῦρον δὲ Ποσειδάωνι, / αὐτὰρ Ἀθηναίη γλαυκώπιδι βοῦν ἀγελαίην: “there we offered fine sacrifices to Zeus, supreme in might, and a bull to Alpheus, and a bull to Poseidon, and to gray-eyed Athena a cow from the herd” (11.727–729). The Pylians waited for dawn by the river Minyeios and reached the Alpheus at midday, and they sacrifice there before the battle with the Epeians, who are besieging Thryoessa on the Alpheus (11.711–733; see [[nestor-and-the-epeians]] and [[alpheus]]). Each god gets the victim that suits: bulls for the river and for the god of the sea, a cow for the goddess. The digitized Greek prints Ἀθηναίη; the sense and the epithet want the dative Ἀθηναίῃ, “to Athena”, as in the same phrase at 9.390 (see the translation's note on 11.729).

The bull is Poseidon's victim. Poseidon is the father of the Moliones, whom he saves in a mist later in the same battle (11.750–752; see [[poseidon]] and [[the-moliones]]), and the Pylians of the Odyssey are found on the shore sacrificing all-black bulls to him (*Odyssey* 3.5–8). A bull to a river is offered at Troy too: Achilles tells the dying Lycaon that the Scamander, to which the Trojans have sacrificed many bulls and into whose eddies they have thrown live horses, will not save them (21.130–132; see [[scamander]] and [[sacrifice]]).
