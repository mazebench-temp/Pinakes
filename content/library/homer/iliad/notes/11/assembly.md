summary: Agamemnon recalls the assembly of the Trojans in which Antimachus urged them to kill Menelaus and Odysseus, who had come as envoys (11.138–142). The Achaeans' own place of assembly and of justice, with the altars of the gods, lies by the ships of Odysseus, where Patroclus meets the wounded Eurypylus (11.806–809).

**The Trojan assembly.** The embassy of Menelaus and Odysseus was heard in the Trojans' assembly, and there Antimachus, who had taken Alexander's gold, urged that Menelaus be killed on the spot and not be let go back to the Achaeans (ἐνὶ Τρώων ἀγορῇ, 11.139–141; see [[embassy]] and [[antimachus]]). Antenor tells of the same meeting in Book 3, where Menelaus spoke briefly and Odysseus' words came like snowflakes in winter (3.209–224; see [[the-embassy-to-troy]]).

**The Achaean place of assembly.** Patroclus, running back to Achilles, comes “to the ships of divine Odysseus, … where their place of assembly and of justice was, where too the altars of the gods had been built for them” (ἀγορή τε θέμις τε, 11.806–808). The ship of Odysseus lies in the middle of the line (11.5–9), and the assembly ground and the altars are there, between the two ends of the camp (see [[themis]] and [[altar]]). Elsewhere the Achaeans hold assembly “beside the stern of Agamemnon's ship” (7.382–383), and the poem does not reconcile the two (see the translation's note on 11.806–808). The meeting place is where Patroclus meets Eurypylus limping from the battle, and where his errand turns into care for a wounded man (11.809–848; see [[healing]]).

**The Pylians gather.** In Nestor's story the heralds call the creditors at dawn, and the leading men of the Pylians gather (συναγρόμενοι) and divide the spoil (11.685–688; see [[heralds]] and [[division-of-spoils]]).
