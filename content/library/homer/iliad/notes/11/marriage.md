summary: Iphidamas, married to the daughter of his mother's father Cisseus, leaves his bride for the war and dies at Agamemnon's hands having had no joy of her, though he gave a hundred cattle for her and promised a thousand goats and sheep (11.221–245). In Nestor's story Mulius, the first man he killed in battle, was the husband of Augeas' eldest daughter Agamede.

**Iphidamas' marriage.** The first man to face Agamemnon after the invocation is Iphidamas, son of Antenor and Theano. Cisseus, Theano's father, reared him in Thrace, “and when he reached the measure of glorious youth, he kept him there, and gave him his daughter” (11.225–226), in the verse with which the Lycian king kept Bellerophon and gave him his daughter (6.192; see [[bellerophon]]). The bride was his mother's sister; the poem does not remark on it (see the translation's note on 11.221–228). “But newly married (γήμας) he came from the bridal chamber at the report of the Achaeans, with twelve curved ships” (11.227–228; see [[iphidamas]], [[cisseus]] and [[theano]]).

**The bride-price.** His death is told with the price of the marriage:

{{quote:4ca3fb72-7bc6-5f3d-a3f6-8cd6c393667b}}

μνηστὴ ἄλοχος is the wife who was wooed, κουριδίη the wedded wife, and χάρις the joy or return that a gift should bring (see [[charis]]). The suitor paid the bride's father in cattle and flocks (ἕδνα, the word used elsewhere: 16.178, 22.472), and Iphidamas had given a hundred cattle and promised a thousand goats and sheep, from his own flocks. Othryoneus, another young man who came to Troy at the report of war, asked for Cassandra without a bride-price, promising to drive the Achaeans from Troy, and is killed by Idomeneus (13.363–382). The dead of Agamemnon's rout lie on the ground “far dearer to the vultures than to their wives” (ἀλόχοισιν, 11.162; see [[husbands-and-wives]]).

**Agamede.** Nestor's first kill in the Epeian battle was Mulius, “the son-in-law of Augeas”, who “had his eldest daughter, fair-haired Agamede, who knew all the drugs that the wide earth nourishes” (11.739–741; see [[mulius]], [[agamede]] and [[in-laws]]).
