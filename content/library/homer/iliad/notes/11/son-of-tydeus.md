---
summary: Diomedes is “son of Tydeus” in Odysseus' call to him, in his fighting, in Paris' aim, and in Nestor's list of the wounded.
---

Odysseus calls to “Diomedes, son of Tydeus”: “Son of Tydeus, what has happened to us, that we have forgotten our furious courage?” (11.312–313; [[furious]], [[odysseus-and-diomedes]]). The son of Tydeus kills the sons of Merops (11.333), wounds Agastrophus (11.338), and goes after his spear when it has struck Hector's helmet (11.357; [[three-layered]]). Paris bends his bow “at the son of Tydeus, shepherd of the people” (11.370; [[shepherd-of-the-people]]). Nestor puts him first among the wounded: “The son of Tydeus, mighty Diomedes, has been shot” (11.660; [[the-wounded-chiefs]]). See [[diomedes]] and [[tydeus]].
