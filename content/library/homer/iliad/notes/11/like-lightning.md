λάμφʼ ὥς τε στεροπὴ πατρὸς Διὸς αἰγιόχοιο: “shone like the lightning of aegis-bearing father Zeus” (11.66). The half-verse is the one used of the spears planted beside Diomedes in the night (10.154), filled out to the end of the line with the epithet αἰγίοχος, “aegis-bearing”. It follows a fuller comparison. Hector, carrying his round shield among the foremost, has just been likened to a baleful star that shows itself out of the clouds and sinks into them again, as he appears now at the front and now at the rear (11.61–65; see [[the-baleful-star]]). The star gives his coming and going; the lightning gives the flash of his bronze. In the night the lightning was the gleam of weapons at rest; at dawn it is a man in armor about to fight.

Lightning is Zeus' own in this book. Later in the morning Zeus sits apart and looks on “the flash of bronze” (χαλκοῦ τε στεροπήν, 11.83), and when Agamemnon nears the wall of Troy Zeus comes down to Ida “with the lightning in his hands” (ἀστεροπήν, 11.184) and sends Iris to promise Hector the day (see [[zeus]] and [[hector]]).
