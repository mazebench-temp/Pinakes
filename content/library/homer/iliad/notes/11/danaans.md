---
summary: The Danaans are named in thirteen verses of Book 11. By their prowess they break the Trojan battalions, and by the end of the day their leaders lie dead or wounded, “wretched leaders and rulers of the Danaans”, as Patroclus calls them (11.90, 11.304, 11.816).
---

At the hour of the woodcutter's meal “the Danaans by their prowess broke the battalions” (11.90; see [[arete]]), and Agamemnon follows the rout, “fiercely calling on the Danaans” (11.165). After that the name marks the army's losses. Hector's nine victims are “the leaders of the Danaans” (11.304; see [[hector]]). Odysseus, left alone, sees that Zeus “has put the other Danaans to rout” (11.406), and Menelaus fears that a great longing for him “may come upon the Danaans” (11.471; see [[odysseus]] and [[menelaus]]). Nestor says that Achilles “does not care for the Danaans or pity them”, and hopes that Patroclus, in Achilles' armor, may become “a light to the Danaans” (11.665, 11.797; see [[nestor]] and [[patroclus]]), as Agamemnon hoped of Teucer's arrows (8.282).
