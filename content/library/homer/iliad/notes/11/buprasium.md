“Until we brought our horses to Buprasium, rich in wheat” (ἐπὶ Βουπρασίου πολυπύρου, 11.756): πολύπυρος, “rich in wheat”, is said also of Argos (15.372) and, in the Odyssey, of Dulichium. With the Olenian Rock and the hill of Alesium it marks the end of the pursuit, where Athena turned the army back, and “the Achaeans drove their swift horses back from Buprasium to Pylos” (11.756–760; see [[olenian-rock]], [[alesium]] and [[nestor-and-the-epeians]]). In the Catalogue these three names belong to the land of the Epeians, Buprasium with “shining Elis”, and the Olenian Rock and Alesium among the points that enclose it (2.615–617; see [[elis]]): the Pylians drove the Epeians back into their own country. Buprasium returns in Nestor's memory of the funeral games of Amarynceus, where the Epeians buried their king (23.630–631; see [[amarynceus]]).
