The city is “Neleian Pylos” (Πύλον Νηλήϊον, 11.682), as in the Odyssey (*Odyssey* 4.639; see [[neleus]]); the spoil of the raid is driven into it “by night, to the city” (11.682–683). “We in Pylos were few and had been ill-used” since Heracles' attack (ἐν Πύλῳ, 11.689; see [[heracles-sacks-pylos]]), a verse that bears on the reading of Dione's “in Pylos among the dead” (5.397; see [[pylos-or-the-gate]]). Thryoessa stands on the Alpheus, “on the border of sandy Pylos” (νεάτη Πύλου ἠμαθόεντος, 11.712; see [[sandy]] and [[thryum]]); Athena gathers the army “throughout Pylos” (Πύλον κάτα, 11.716), and after the battle the victors drive their horses back “to Pylos” (Πύλονδʼ, 11.760; see [[nestor-and-the-epeians]]).

The story puts the border of the Pylians' land on the Alpheus, as the Catalogue puts Thryum at its ford (2.592) and Book 5 has the Alpheus flow “broad through the land of the Pylians” (5.545).

## Outside Homer

From these verses Strabo argued that Nestor's Pylos could be neither the Elean Pylos nor the Messenian Pylos by Coryphasium, since no one could have driven pigs and sheep more than a thousand stades to it while the Epeians reached Thryoessa on the third day, but only the Pylos of Triphylia (*Geography* 8.3.28–29; see [[strabo-on-nestors-pylos]]). The palace excavated at Ano Englianos in Messenia, which most scholars now take for Nestor's, lies far to the south of the Alpheus, and the geography of the story is one of the difficulties of that identification.
