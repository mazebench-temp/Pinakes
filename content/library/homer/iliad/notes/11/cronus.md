---
summary: Cronus is named in Book 11 only in his son's patronymics. Six times Zeus is called “the son of Cronus”, and each time he is shaping the battle.
---

The son of Cronus sets the rainbows in the clouds as a portent for men (Κρονίων, 11.27–28), rouses the evil tumult before the battle (Κρονίδης, 11.53), and is blamed by the gods for meaning to give the Trojans glory (κελαινεφέα Κρονίωνα, “the son of Cronus, lord of the dark clouds”, 11.78). Hector says that his triumph is the gift of “Zeus, son of Cronus” (11.289); the narrator says that “the son of Cronus stretched the battle evenly” (11.336); and Odysseus, alone among the Trojans, says that “the son of Cronus has put the other Danaans to rout” (11.406). Κρονίδης and Κρονίων name Zeus by his father, and the index counts each of them to Cronus as well (see [[son-of-cronus]], [[lord-of-dark-clouds]] and [[zeus]]). Cronus himself, who sits with Iapetus at the lowest limits of earth and sea (8.478–481), is not named.
