summary: Podalirius is named once in Book 11, when the wounded Eurypylus explains why he needs Patroclus: of the army's two healers, Machaon lies wounded in the huts, and Podalirius “on the plain awaits the sharp Ares of the Trojans” (11.833–836).
refs: [11.836]
---

Eurypylus names the brothers by their craft, ἰητροί, “healers” (11.833), as the Catalogue called them “good healers” (2.732; see [[machaon]] and [[healing]]). He speaks with some doubt, and does not say which brother is which: “one, I think, with a wound, in the huts”, the other on the plain (11.834–836). It is the last time Podalirius is named in the Iliad, and the poem never shows him healing or fighting. With both sons of Asclepius out of reach, the wound is left to Patroclus, who learned his drugs from Achilles, and Achilles from Chiron (11.828–832; see [[patroclus]] and [[chiron]]).
