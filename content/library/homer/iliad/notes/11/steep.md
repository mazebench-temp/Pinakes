---
summary: αἰπύς describes the steep wall of Troy and the hill of Thryoessa, and in its figurative sense “sheer destruction” and Achilles' view of the “sheer toil.”
grc: [αἰπὺς]
---

Of places: Agamemnon is about to come beneath the city “and its steep wall” when Zeus intervenes (11.181–182; [[father-of-men-and-gods]]), and in Nestor's story Thryoessa is “a steep hill, far off on the Alpheus” (11.711; [[sandy]], [[thryum]]). The figurative sense, “sheer,” comes three times: a lion falls on cattle at night, “but to one sheer destruction appears” (11.173–174; [[lion]]); Odysseus tells Socus, “Ah, wretch, sheer destruction is surely overtaking you” (11.441; [[wretch]]); and Achilles watches “the sheer toil and the tearful onslaught” from his ship (11.601; [[tearful]], [[watching-the-war]]).
