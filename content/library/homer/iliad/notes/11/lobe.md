**A father's insult.** The sons of Antimachus beg for their lives, and Agamemnon answers with their father's deed: he urged the Trojans in assembly to kill Menelaus when he came as an envoy with Odysseus, “now you will pay for your father's shameful insult” (ἀεικέα τίσετε λώβην, 11.138–142; see [[sons-of-antimachus]], [[the-embassy-to-troy]] and [[apoina]]). The insult is a proposal never carried out, and the payment is the sons' lives: λώβη calls for payment, here with the verb of the blood-price, τίνω (see the article above and [[poine]]). The translation keeps λώβη, “insult”, apart from the Epeians' ὕβρις, “outrage”, later in the book (11.694–695; see [[hybris]]).

**Insulter.** Paris shoots Diomedes through the foot from behind the pillar of Ilus' tomb, and leaps out laughing to boast. Diomedes answers: “Archer, insulter, glorying in your bow, ogler of girls” (τοξότα λωβητὴρ κέρᾳ ἀγλαὲ παρθενοπῖπα, 11.385; see [[paris]], [[bow]] and the translation's note on 11.385). λωβητήρ is the word the army used of Thersites, “this word-slinging slanderer” (2.275), and Hector called his brother a “disgrace” (λώβην, 3.42). In Diomedes' mouth it joins the archer's distance to the boaster's tongue: the man who will not fight face to face insults from afar (11.386–390; see [[euchomai]]).
