---
summary: Patroclus is “the valiant son of Menoetius” three times in the book that begins his part in the war, and Achilles calls him “Brilliant son of Menoetius.”
grc: ["Μενοιτίου ἄλκιμος υἱός"]
---

The verse-end Μενοιτίου ἄλκιμος υἱός, “the valiant son of Menoetius,” is first used here (11.605) and marks Patroclus' three speeches in the book: his first words in the poem, to Achilles (11.605–606; [[the-death-of-patroclus]]), his lament when he sees the wounded Eurypylus (11.814–818), and his answer to Eurypylus (11.837–841; [[strong-spear]]). Achilles addresses him with the patronymic proper: “Brilliant son of Menoetius, dear to my heart” (11.608; [[brilliant]], [[dear-to-my-heart]]). In Nestor's speech Menoetius himself appears, charging his son at Phthia (11.765–789; [[menoetius]]). See [[patroclus]].
