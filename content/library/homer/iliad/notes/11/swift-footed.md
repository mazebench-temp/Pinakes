---
summary: Achilles is “swift-footed” as he brings Isus and Antiphus from Ida and as he answers Patroclus; Iris is “swift-footed” as she comes to Hector; and the horses of the sons of Antimachus and of the Pylians are swift.
---

**Achilles.** Agamemnon recognizes Isus and Antiphus, having seen them by the ships “when swift-footed Achilles brought them from Ida” (11.111–112; [[isus]], [[antiphus-son-of-priam]], [[raiding]]); and “Answering him, swift-footed Achilles said” introduces his words to Patroclus (11.607; [[answering-him]]).

**Iris.** “And standing near, swift-footed Iris spoke to him,” and when she has given Zeus' message, “swift-footed Iris went away” (11.199, 11.210; [[wind-footed]], [[swift-iris]]).

**Horses.** The sons of Antimachus “were driving the swift horses together” when the reins slipped from their hands (11.127; [[glossy]]), and in Nestor's story the Pylians “drove their swift horses back from Buprasium to Pylos” (11.760).
