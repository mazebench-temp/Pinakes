---
summary: Diomedes draws “the swift shaft” of Paris' arrow from his foot, and in a simile “the swift arrow” brings down a stag.
grc: ["ὠκὺς ὀϊστός"]
---

Odysseus stands in front of the wounded Diomedes, who sits down behind him “and pulled the swift shaft out of his foot, and a grievous pain went through his flesh” (11.397–398; [[grievous]]). In the simile of the jackals, the stag a hunter has shot runs while its blood is warm, “but when the swift arrow has beaten it down,” the jackals tear it (11.478–479; [[the-jackals-and-the-stag]], [[eating-raw-flesh]]).
