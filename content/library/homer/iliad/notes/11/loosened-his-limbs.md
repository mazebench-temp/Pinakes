summary: Agamemnon loosens the limbs of the two sons of Antenor, Iphidamas with the sword and Coon with the spear (11.240, 11.260); Coon dies dragging his brother's body, in the verse of Elephenor's death in the first battle. Eurypylus strikes Apisaon in the liver and “loosened his knees” (11.579).

**The sons of Antenor.** Agamemnon wrenches Iphidamas' spear from his hand “and struck him on the neck with his sword, and loosened his limbs” (11.240). Coon, his elder brother, drags the body away by the foot, calling on the best of the Trojans, and Agamemnon “stabbed him with his bronze-fitted spear, and loosened his limbs” beneath the bossed shield (11.257–260). The verse is the one of Elephenor's death in the first battle, when Agenor saw his side bared beneath the shield as he stooped to drag away the body of Echepolus (11.260 = 4.469; see [[coon]] and [[iphidamas]]). Both men die in the act of taking a body, Elephenor to strip it and Coon to save it (see [[fighting-over-the-body]]). The verse with which a son of Antenor killed in Book 4 is now spoken over another son of Antenor (see [[agenor]] and [[antenor]]).

**Apisaon.** Eurypylus' spear strikes Apisaon, son of Phausius, “in the liver beneath the midriff, and at once loosened his knees” (εἶθαρ δʼ ὑπὸ γούνατʼ ἔλυσεν, 11.579; see [[apisaon]]). The verse is used again for the death of another Apisaon, son of Hippasus, killed by Lycomedes in the fight over Patroclus (17.348–349): two men of one name die in the same words.
