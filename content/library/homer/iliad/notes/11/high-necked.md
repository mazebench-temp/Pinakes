---
summary: Horses “with high-arched necks” rattle empty chariots along the lines as Agamemnon kills their drivers.
---

“So under Agamemnon, son of Atreus, fell the heads of the fleeing Trojans, and many horses with high-arched necks rattled empty chariots along the bridges of war, missing their noble charioteers” (11.158–161; [[blameless]]). The drivers lie on the ground, “far dearer to the vultures than to their wives” (11.161–162; [[vultures]]). The proud horses of the epithet run loose without the men who drove them. See [[horses]] and [[the-aristeia-of-agamemnon]].
