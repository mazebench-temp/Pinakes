---
summary: Patroclus uses a knife to cut the arrow from Eurypylus' thigh (11.844), the only time in the Iliad that a knife serves the healer.
grc: [μαχαίρῃ]
---

“There he laid him out, and with a knife cut from his thigh the sharp, piercing arrow” (11.844–845). Eurypylus had asked Patroclus to “cut the arrow out of my thigh” (11.829), as Idomeneus said a healer is worth many men “to cut out arrows” (11.514–515), and the knife does the cutting. Elsewhere in the Iliad the knife is Agamemnon's, for the throats of sacrificial victims (3.271–272, 19.252–253), and an ornament of the dancers on Achilles' shield (18.597–598). Here, in the hands of the man whom Achilles taught Chiron's drugs, it is the healer's tool (see [[healing]], [[arrows]] and [[patroclus]]).
