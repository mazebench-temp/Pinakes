summary: The Dardanians are named only in Hector's rallying cry after Agamemnon leaves the battle, “Trojans and Lycians and Dardanians who fight hand to hand” (11.286), while their leader Aeneas stands among the Trojan leaders on the plain (11.58).
passages:
  - 11.284-290 | Hector rallies the Trojans
---

Δάρδανοι ἀγχιμαχηταί, “Dardanians who fight hand to hand” (11.286), close Hector's cry, as in Book 8 (8.173) and twice more at the ships (15.425, 15.486; see [[hector]], [[trojans]] and [[lycians]]). Their leader Aeneas is among the men around whom the Trojans form up on the rise of the plain (11.58; see [[aeneas]]). The name of their founder sounds in the same book in another form: the tomb the Trojans run past is that of “Ilus, descendant of Dardanus” (11.166, 11.372; see [[dardanus]] and [[ilus]]).
