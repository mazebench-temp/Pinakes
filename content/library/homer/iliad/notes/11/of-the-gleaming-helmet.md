---
summary: Odysseus fears the disgrace “if Hector of the gleaming helmet takes the ships.”
---

Hector is killing the Achaeans by the hundred, and Odysseus calls to Diomedes: “Come here, my friend, and stand by me; for it will be a disgrace if Hector of the gleaming helmet takes the ships” (11.314–315; [[my-dear]], [[elenchos]]). The two stand together until both are wounded ([[odysseus-and-diomedes]]). See [[hector]].
