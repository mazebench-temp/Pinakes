κυάνεοι δὲ δράκοντες ὀρωρέχατο προτὶ δειρὴν / τρεῖς ἑκάτερθʼ: “and dark blue serpents stretched up toward the neck, three on each side” (11.26–27). The serpents are worked in κύανος, the dark blue substance of the ten bands on the corselet that Cinyras sent Agamemnon (11.24; see [[kyanos]], [[corselet]] and [[cinyras]]). ὀρωρέχατο, a perfect of ὀρέγω, “reach, stretch out”, gives them the pose of snakes rearing toward the wearer's throat. They are likened to the rainbows that Zeus sets in the clouds as a portent for men (11.27–28; see [[like-rainbows]]).

The shield has a serpent of its own. From the shield hung a strap of silver, “and on the strap a dark blue serpent writhed, and it had heads, three of them, turned every way, growing from a single neck” (11.38–40; see [[shield]]). Above it on the shield are the grim-faced Gorgon, Terror and Rout (11.36–37; see [[gorgon]]). On a warrior's armor the serpent, with its glare, joins the images of fear that face the enemy. In Book 2 the serpent was a portent from Zeus and in Book 3 a terror met in the mountains (2.308–320, 3.33–37); here it is the king's ornament, made of the same blue enamel as the rest of his gift.
