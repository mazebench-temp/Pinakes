summary: In Nestor's hut the old man and Machaon sit on chairs (κλισμοί) to drink, and Nestor rises from his shining chair (θρόνος) to take Patroclus by the hand and bid him sit, but Patroclus will not (11.623, 11.645–648).
en: [chair]
---

Nestor and Machaon come in from the battle and sit down ἐπὶ κλισμοῖσι, “on chairs” (11.623), in the words in which Hera and Athena, sent back from the battle by Zeus, sat down on golden chairs among the gods (8.436). When Patroclus appears at the door, “the old man rose from his shining chair”, ἀπὸ θρόνου ὦρτο φαεινοῦ, took him by the hand and led him in, and bade him sit down (11.645–646). The θρόνος is the high seat of the master of the house. Rising from it is the courtesy of a host: Achilles rises from his to raise Priam by the hand (24.515), as he sprang up to meet the envoys in Book 9 and Nestor and Odysseus at Peleus' house (9.193–194; 11.777–778; see [[visit-scene]]). Patroclus refuses the seat: “There is no time to sit, old sir, nurtured by Zeus, and you will not persuade me” (11.648; see [[patroclus]] and [[nestor]]).
