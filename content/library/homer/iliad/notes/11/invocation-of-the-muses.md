summary: Before the climax of Agamemnon's aristeia the narrator calls on the Muses, in the verse that opened the Catalogue of Ships, to tell who was the first to come against him (11.218–220). It is the first appeal to the Muses since Book 2, and the first of three in the battle.

The first verse is the one that opens the invocation before the Catalogue of Ships (11.218 = 2.484; see [[catalogue-of-ships]]). The narrator has not called on the Muses since Book 2 (2.484–493, 2.761–762), and he uses the same verse twice more at turns of the battle: to ask who of the Achaeans first took the bloody spoils when Poseidon turned the fight (14.508–510), and how fire first fell on the ships (16.112–113).

The question, “who was the first to come against Agamemnon”, is the one the narrator asks himself without a Muse when Hector charges, “whom first, whom last” (11.299; see [[whom-first-whom-last]]). Here it is answered not with a list but with one man, and with what the Muses know and the singer does not: who Iphidamas was, where he was reared, whom he married, how many ships he brought and where he left them (11.221–230; see [[iphidamas]] and [[genealogy-in-battle]]). The last verse of the answer returns to the question, “he it was who now came against Agamemnon, son of Atreus” (11.231), closing the history in a ring (see [[ring-composition]]).
