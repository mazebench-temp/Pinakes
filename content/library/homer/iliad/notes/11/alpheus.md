Thryoessa stands “far off on the Alpheus” (11.712; see [[thryum]]). After the night by the Minyeios the Pylians march in their armor and reach “the sacred stream of Alpheus” at midday (ἱερὸν ῥόον Ἀλφειοῖο, 11.726; see [[minyeios]]).

{{quote:540e809d-60a7-5915-9c69-b14594545129}}

The river is a god and receives a bull. Achilles reminds the Trojans that they have long sacrificed bulls to the Scamander and thrown horses alive into its eddies (21.130–132), and Peleus vowed his son's hair and fifty rams to the Spercheius (23.144–148; see [[sacrifice]]). In Book 5 the Alpheus, “which flows broad through the land of the Pylians” (5.545), is a god with descendants, the grandfather of Diocles of Pherae (5.544–547). The Pylians sleep “beside the streams of the river” (11.732), and the battle is fought by its banks the next morning; the pursuit runs from there to Buprasium (11.735–760; see [[nestor-and-the-epeians]]).
