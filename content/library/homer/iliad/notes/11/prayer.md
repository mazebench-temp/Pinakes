---
summary: Book 11 has no prayer scene. Diomedes taunts Hector with the god "to whom you must pray" (11.363–364), and in Nestor's tale the Pylians sacrifice at the Alpheus, go into battle praying to Zeus and Athena, and after the victory give thanks to Zeus among the gods and to Nestor among men (11.727–736, 11.761).
except: [11.288, 11.290, 11.379, 11.388, 11.445]
---

**A taunt.** When Hector escapes him, Diomedes calls after him that Phoebus Apollo has saved him again, “to whom you must pray when you go into the thud of javelins” (11.363–364; see [[apollo]]). The prayer is imagined, and the taunt makes it a habit of a man who needs a god to survive.

**Nestor's war.** In Nestor's story the Pylians, come at midday to the Alpheus, offer fine sacrifices to Zeus, a bull to the river, a bull to Poseidon and a cow to Athena (11.727–729; see [[sacrifice]]). At sunrise “we joined in battle, praying to Zeus and Athena” (11.736). After the rout to Buprasium the order of thanks is bold: “and all gave thanks to Zeus among the gods, and to Nestor among men” (11.761). The verb is εὐχετάομαι, the frequentative of εὔχομαι, here thanks and praise, shared by a god and a young man (see [[nestor-and-the-epeians]]).

**Boasts.** The book's other words of the family are boasts, not prayers, and are not counted here: εὖχος, “triumph” (11.288, 11.290, 11.445), εὐχόμενος, Paris leaping from cover “in triumph” (11.379), and εὔχεαι, “you boast” (11.388; see [[euchomai]]).
