---
summary: The jackals that press around a wounded stag, in the simile of the Trojans around Odysseus.
---

“The Trojans pressed, as blood-red jackals in the mountains press around a horned stag that is wounded” (11.474–475; [[antlered]], [[the-jackals-and-the-stag]]). The jackals are the third bearers of the epithet in the poem, after the serpent at Aulis (2.308) and the lion's skin that Agamemnon put on at night (10.23). Whether they are tawny-red or red with blood is not said; they eat raw flesh, and a lion drives them off (11.479–481; [[eating-raw-flesh]], [[ravager]]). See [[jackal]].
