---
summary: Odysseus calls Diomedes “my friend” as he asks him to stand by him, and Nestor so addresses Patroclus as he reminds him of his father's charge.
en: [my friend]
---

Odysseus to Diomedes, when Hector is driving the Achaeans to the ships: “Come here, my friend, and stand by me; for it will be a disgrace if Hector of the gleaming helmet takes the ships” (11.314–315; [[of-the-gleaming-helmet]]). Nestor to Patroclus: “My friend, surely Menoetius charged you thus on that day when he sent you from Phthia to Agamemnon” (11.765–766; [[menoetius]]). The translation renders πέπον “my friend” in both. See [[odysseus-and-diomedes]].
