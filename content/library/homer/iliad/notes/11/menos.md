summary: When Agamemnon's wound dries, “sharp pains came over the might of the son of Atreus”, a verse said twice, before and after the simile of the woman in labor. Hector “urged on the might and spirit of every man”, and the Achaeans, “breathing fury”, are afraid for the wounded Machaon.
passages:
  - 11.264-274 | Agamemnon's pains
---

**The might of the son of Atreus.** Wounded by Coon, Agamemnon fights on “while the blood still welled up warm from his wound” (11.266). When it dries the pain begins, and the narrator gives it in a verse that he says twice, on either side of a simile:

{{quote:945db8eb-1f57-5d97-bc58-edc10011c89f}}

μένος Ἀτρεΐδαο, “the might of the son of Atreus”, names the man by his strength, as Nestor names Heracles by his, βίη Ἡρακληείη, “mighty Heracles” (11.690). The phrase occurs only in these two verses. The pains “sink into” (δῦνον) the very thing that has made Agamemnon terrible all day, and the simile between the two verses gives his pain to a woman in labor, struck by the dart of the Eileithyiai (see [[eileithyia]], [[agamemnon]] and [[the-aristeia-of-agamemnon]]). He leaves the field in the next verses (11.273–283).

**Might in the army.** Hector, seeing Agamemnon go, rallies the Trojans and “urged on the might and spirit of every man” (11.291), the verse that closes Ares' call to the Trojans in Book 5 and Nestor's to the Argives in Book 6 (5.470, 6.72; see [[exhortation]]). When Paris hits Machaon, “the Achaeans breathing fury were terribly afraid” that the Trojans would take him (μένεα πνείοντες, 11.508–509), with the phrase of the Achaeans' silent advance in Book 3 (3.8). Their fury is for the enemy, their fear for the healer (see [[a-healer-is-worth-many]]).
