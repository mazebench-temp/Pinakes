**Nestor's cup.** Beside the table Hecamede sets “a beautiful cup, which the old man had brought from home”, studded with golden nails, with four handles and two golden doves feeding at each, and two bases below; another man could move it from the table only with toil when it was full, but Nestor lifted it without toil (11.632–637). In it she mixes the drink of Pramnian wine, cheese and barley (ἐν τῷ, 11.638). It is the most fully described cup in Homer, and has its own entry (see [[the-cup-of-nestor]], [[kykeon]] and [[studded-with-golden-nails]]). It is a δέπας, the word of the cup Hephaestus hands his mother (1.584), and περικαλλές, “beautiful”, as the translation renders it here.

**Peleus' goblet.** When Nestor and Odysseus came to Peleus' house, the old man was burning the thighs of an ox to Zeus in his courtyard, “and he was holding a golden goblet, pouring sparkling wine over the burning offerings” (11.774–775; see [[libation]] and [[sacrifice]]). ἄλεισον, “goblet”, is a rarer word for a drinking cup, used for libations and as a gift: Priam offers Hermes “a fine goblet” (24.429), and in the Odyssey Peisistratus hands Athena, disguised as Mentor, a golden goblet to pour the libation (Od. 3.50–53).
