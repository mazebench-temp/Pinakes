The armies fight with no god among them but Strife:

{{quote:cd06a2d2-8f77-53c4-82d3-cc1812347e24}}

Zeus' ban of Book 8 still holds: any god who goes to help either side will come back to Olympus “struck in no seemly way” (8.10–12; see [[zeus]]). The gods obey and complain. “They all blamed the son of Cronus, lord of the dark clouds, / because he meant to hold out glory to the Trojans” (11.78–79; see [[kydos]]). ᾐτιόωντο, “blamed”, is the verb of fault-finding, as when Patroclus says that Achilles “would soon blame even one who is blameless” (11.654; see [[aitios]]). Zeus “paid them no heed” (11.80). In Book 4 the gods sat together with Zeus on Olympus, drinking nectar and looking out on Troy (4.1–4); here each sits in his own house, and Zeus sits apart from them all to watch (11.80–83; see [[watching-the-war]] and [[olympus]]). The ban holds until Poseidon, pitying the Achaeans and indignant at Zeus, goes down to them in Book 13 (13.10–38; see [[poseidon]]).

The formula of the homes on Olympus is used of the Muses in this book (11.218), as at the start of the Catalogue, and the index counts that line to them, not to the gods as a body (see [[muses]] and [[homes-on-olympus]]).
