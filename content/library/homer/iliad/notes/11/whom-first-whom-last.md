summary: When Agamemnon has left the field, the narrator asks the question of Hector's charge, “Then whom first, whom last did he slay, Hector, son of Priam, when Zeus gave him glory?”, and answers with nine Achaean leaders in three verses, and then the multitude (11.299–305).

The question is asked in full, both halves, as of Hector and Ares in Book 5 (5.703), and its second verse names the giver of the killing: “when Zeus gave him glory” (ὅτε οἱ Ζεὺς κῦδος ἔδωκεν, 11.300), the fulfillment of the promise that Iris brought him (11.207–209; see [[kydos]] and [[plan-of-zeus]]). The answer takes up its first word, “Asaeus first” (πρῶτα, 11.301), as Teucer's list began “Orsilochus first” (8.274). Nine names follow in three verses, three to a verse, with nothing but a patronymic for one of them, Dolops son of Clytius, and an epithet for the last, Hipponous, “steadfast in battle” (11.301–303; see [[asaeus]], [[autonous]], [[opites]], [[dolops]], [[opheltius-the-achaean]], [[agelaus-the-achaean]], [[aesymnus]], [[orus]] and [[hipponous]]). None of them is heard of before or after.

The list is not of common soldiers. “These were the leaders of the Danaans he killed”, ἡγεμόνας Δαναῶν, “and after them the multitude” (11.304–305), and a simile of the West Wind battering the clouds and whipping up the sea gives the multitude (11.305–309; see [[the-west-wind-and-the-clouds]]). Hector kills nine leaders in three verses; Agamemnon's aristeia had taken some hundred and seventy verses to kill eight men by name (11.91–263; see [[androktasia]] and [[the-aristeia]]). Patroclus' victims will be counted the same way, nine names in three verses, under the same question asked of him directly (16.692–697).
