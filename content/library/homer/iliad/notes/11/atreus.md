---
summary: Atreus is named only in his son's patronymic, and in Book 11 “the son of Atreus” is always Agamemnon, in fifteen verses of his great day and his wounding (11.15–272).
---

The patronymic carries Agamemnon through his aristeia, from the call to arms, “And the son of Atreus shouted” (11.15), to the pains of his wound, which come over “the might of the son of Atreus” (11.268, 11.272; see [[agamemnon]] and [[son-of-atreus]]). It appears in four forms, Ἀτρεΐδης, the genitives Ἀτρεΐδεω and Ἀτρεΐδαο, and the dative Ἀτρεΐδῃ, and once in the full form: the sons of Antimachus beg him, “Take us alive, son of Atreus” (Ἀτρέος υἱέ, 11.131), as Adrastus begged Menelaus in the same words (6.46; see [[sons-of-antimachus]]). There the son of Atreus was the brother who wanted to spare, and Agamemnon talked him out of it; here he is the brother who refuses.
