---
summary: Hades is named three times in Book 11, always as the place where the dead go. Zeus means to hurl many mighty heads down to Hades, the sons of Antenor go down into the house of Hades, and Odysseus promises Socus' soul to “Hades of the famous horses”.
---

**The proem's verse.** Zeus sends down dewdrops wet with blood “because he meant / to hurl down to Hades many mighty heads” (11.54–55; see [[zeus]]). In the proem the wrath “hurled down to Hades many mighty souls” (1.3); here “heads” stand for the men, as when the heads of the fleeing Trojans fall under Agamemnon (11.158). Hector used the same verb of his own death: “for no man will hurl me down to Hades beyond my portion” (6.487; see [[hector]]).

**The house of Hades.** When Agamemnon has killed Iphidamas and Coon, “the sons of Antenor, at the hands of the king, the son of Atreus, / fulfilled their fate and went down into the house of Hades” (11.262–263; see [[iphidamas]] and [[coon]]). δόμον Ἄϊδος εἴσω, “into the house of Hades”, is the phrase of the armies' prayer in Book 3 and of the death Nestor imagines Peleus praying for in Book 7 (3.322, 7.131; see [[psyche]]).

**The famous horses.** Odysseus tells Socus that, beaten down by his spear, he will “give me triumph, and your soul to Hades of the famous horses” (11.445; see [[socus]], [[odysseus]] and [[of-the-famous-horses]]). Sarpedon threatened Tlepolemus with the same verse (5.654), and Meriones will threaten Aeneas with it (16.625). When Socus is dead, Odysseus tells him that his father and mother will not close his eyes, “but the birds / that eat raw flesh will tear at you” (11.453–454; see [[birds]]). The soul goes to Hades and the body is left to the birds, as in the proem (1.3–5).
