**Peleus' version.** The verse is the one Glaucus gave his father Hippolochus (6.208; see the article above), and this is the only time the Iliad gives it to Peleus. The poem remembers Peleus' parting words three times, and each speaker chooses the charge his purpose needs. In Book 9 Odysseus recalled the charge to hold back his proud heart, “for kindliness is better” (9.252–259; see [[kindliness-is-better]]), and Phoenix the charge to make him “a speaker of words and a doer of deeds” (9.438–443; see [[speaker-of-words-doer-of-deeds]]). Nestor, who wants the Achaeans saved, gives Peleus the heroic charge (see [[peleus-sends-achilles-to-troy]] and [[peleus]]).

**Two charges.** Nestor sets it beside the charge Menoetius gave his son, and the two divide the heroic life between two friends. Achilles is to be the best; Patroclus, the elder and the weaker, is to give him counsel and direct him, “and he will obey you, to his good” (11.786–789; see [[menoetius]] and [[the-persuasion-of-a-friend]]). The point of the memory is Patroclus' charge: “So the old man charged you, but you forget” (11.790). Peleus' words come just after Nestor has said that Achilles “will have the profit of his prowess alone” (11.762–764; see [[arete]]), and the verb of the charge, ἀριστεύειν, has run through the book: Machaon was hit “while he was the best in the fighting”, Nestor was “the best of all in counsel”, and Odysseus, alone, bound “whoever would be best in battle” to stand his ground (11.506, 11.627, 11.409; see [[aristos]] and [[the-cowards-leave-the-fighting]]).
