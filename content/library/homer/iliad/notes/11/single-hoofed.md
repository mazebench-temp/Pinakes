---
summary: Hector bids the Trojans drive their “single-hoofed horses” at the Danaans; Idomeneus sends Nestor off with his; in Nestor's story the Epeians come with theirs and he takes Mulius'.
---

Hector, when Agamemnon is gone: “come, drive your single-hoofed horses straight at the mighty Danaans” (11.289–290; [[mighty]]). Idomeneus tells Nestor to take Machaon in his chariot and “drive the single-hoofed horses to the ships as fast as you can” (11.513). In Nestor's story the Epeians come to Thryoessa “all together, the men in great numbers and their single-hoofed horses” (11.708), and Nestor kills Mulius and takes “his single-hoofed horses” (11.738–739; [[mulius]]).
