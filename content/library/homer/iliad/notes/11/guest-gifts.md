summary: Agamemnon's corselet was a guest-gift from Cinyras of Cyprus (11.19–23), and when Nestor and Odysseus came to Peleus' house, Achilles set before them the meal of welcome, “good cheer”, as custom requires for guests (11.779).

en: [guest-gift, good cheer]
---

**Cinyras' corselet.** “Which Cinyras had once given him to be a guest-gift” (δῶκε ξεινήϊον εἶναι, 11.20): the half-verse is the one that tells how the helmet of boar's tusks passed from Amphidamas to Molus (10.269), and the guest-gift is again a piece of armor with a history. Cinyras heard as far as Cyprus that the Achaeans were sailing for Troy, and sent the corselet “to do the king a favor” (χαριζόμενος βασιλῆϊ, 11.23; see [[cinyras]], [[corselet]] and [[charis]]). The poem does not say that Agamemnon had ever been his guest.

**Good cheer.** When Nestor and Odysseus stood in the doorway of Peleus' courtyard, Achilles sprang up, took them by the hand, led them in and bade them sit, “and set good cheer before us, as is the custom with guests” (ξείνιά τʼ εὖ παρέθηκεν, ἅ τε ξείνοις θέμις ἐστίν, 11.777–779). ξείνια here are the food and drink set before visitors, as Charis invites Thetis in “so that I may set good cheer before you” (18.387), and as in the Odyssey hosts set them before their guests (Odyssey 3.490, 15.188). It is the custom, θέμις, of the guest's welcome (see [[themis]], [[xenia]] and [[visit-scene]]).
