---
summary: Patroclus lays a bitter, “pain-killing root” on Eurypylus' wound.
grc: [ὀδυνήφατον]
---

Patroclus cuts the arrow from Eurypylus' thigh, washes the wound, “and put on it a bitter root, rubbing it between his hands, a pain-killing root, which stayed all his pains; and the wound dried, and the blood stopped” (11.846–848; [[bitter]], [[healing]]). The root is not named. The epithet is the one of Paeëon's drugs, with which the physician of the gods healed Hades and Ares (5.401, 5.900). See [[patroclus]] and [[eurypylus]].
