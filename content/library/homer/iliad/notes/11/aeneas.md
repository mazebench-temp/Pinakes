He stands with Hector, Polydamas and the three sons of Antenor, the leaders around whom the Trojans form up for the third day of battle (see [[polydamas]], [[antenor]] and [[trojans]]). θεὸς ὣς τίετο δήμῳ, “was honored by the people like a god”, is a verse-end for kings and priests: Agamemnon among the Argives (10.33), Dolopion, the priest of the Scamander (5.78), and later Thoas (13.218) and Onetor, priest of Zeus of Ida (16.605; see [[honored-like-a-god]]). Only here is it given to Aeneas, and only here does it name the people who honor him, Τρωσί, “the Trojans”. In Book 13 he stands at the back of the throng, angry with Priam, who did not honor him (οὔ τι τίεσκεν, 13.460–461): the same verb, τίω. Book 11 gives him nothing more to do. He returns at the head of the fourth company against the Achaean wall (12.98–100).
