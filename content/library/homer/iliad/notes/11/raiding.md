**Achilles' raids.** Two of Agamemnon's victims, Isus and Antiphus, sons of Priam, had been caught by Achilles “as they herded sheep” on the slopes of Ida; he bound them with willow shoots and let them go for a ransom, and Agamemnon knows them from the day Achilles brought them to the ships (11.104–112; see [[isus]] and [[antiphus-son-of-priam]]). Hecamede was taken “from Tenedos, when Achilles sacked it” (ὅτε πέρσεν Ἀχιλλεύς, 11.625; see [[tenedos]] and [[hecamede]]). These belong to the raids Achilles made in the years before the poem, on the cities of the Troad and the herds on Ida, when he spared men and sold them (9.328–329, 20.89–92, 21.34–42, 21.100–102; see [[achilles-raids]]).

**Nestor's cattle raid.** Nestor's story of his youth begins with a raid: “a quarrel arose between the Eleans and us over a cattle raid (ἀμφὶ βοηλασίῃ), when I killed Itymoneus, the brave son of Hypeirochus, who lived in Elis, as I was driving off cattle in reprisal” (11.671–674). βοηλασίη, “cattle-driving”, occurs only here, and so does ῥύσια, cattle seized in reprisal or as security for what is owed: the Epeians owed a debt to many in Pylos, and the raid took payment by force (11.686–689; see the translation's note on 11.672–674). Itymoneus falls defending his cows, and the country people flee (11.674–676; see [[itymoneus]]).

The plunder is counted in fifties, by kinds of beast, and the horses are set apart at the end, the richest part (see [[cattle]], [[sheep]], [[goats]] and [[horses]]). It is driven into Pylos by night, and Neleus rejoices that so much has fallen to his son on his first venture into war. The raid is also the cause of the war that follows: on the third day the Epeians come in force and besiege Thryoessa (11.706–713; see [[nestor-and-the-epeians]] and [[division-of-spoils]]).

**Debts and reprisals.** The Odyssey tells of the same kind of errand. Odysseus, still a boy, was sent to Messene “for a debt that the whole people owed him”, because Messenians had carried off from Ithaca in their ships “three hundred sheep and their herdsmen” (Odyssey 21.15–21), the half-verse of Neleus' share of the Elean spoil (τριηκόσιʼ ἠδὲ νομῆας, 11.697).
