---
summary: Idomeneus addresses Nestor as “son of Neleus,” and Machaon is brought to “the hut of the son of Neleus” (11.618); the adjective of Neleian Pylos (11.682) belongs to Neleus.
except: [11.682]
---

“Nestor, son of Neleus, great glory of the Achaeans” (11.511; [[great-glory-of-the-achaeans]]) is Idomeneus' address when he asks Nestor to carry Machaon to the ships. “And when they reached the hut of the son of Neleus” (11.618), the two men step down and Eurymedon unyokes the horses ([[bountiful-earth]]). Neleus himself is a figure of Nestor's story: his twelve sons, the debt Augeas owed him, the horses he hid from Nestor (11.692–719; [[neleus]]). The adjective in Πύλον Νηλήϊον, “Neleian Pylos” (11.682), names the father's city, not the son. See [[nestor]].
