**Foot before horse.** At dawn each Achaean orders his charioteer to hold the horses in good order at the ditch, and the men go forward on foot, “armed in their armor”: “They were drawn up at the ditch far ahead of the horsemen, and the horsemen followed a little behind” (11.47–52). It is the reverse of Nestor's arrangement in Book 4, the horsemen with their chariots in front and the foot behind (4.297–300), and μέγα and ὀλίγον, “far” and “a little”, have been taken of time as well as of distance (see the translation's note on 11.47–52). Polydamas will give the Trojans the same advice at the Achaean wall, in the same words: let the attendants hold the horses at the ditch, and let the men follow on foot (12.75–79; see [[polydamas]] and [[chariot]]). ἱππῆες, “horsemen”, are the men who fight from chariots, kept apart from the ἡνίοχοι, the drivers (see [[horses]]).

**The Trojan line.** The Trojans are drawn up on the rise of the plain around Hector, Polydamas, Aeneas and the three sons of Antenor (11.56–60). Hector's place is everywhere: he “now would appear among the foremost, and now among the hindmost, giving orders”, like a baleful star that shows itself and hides again in the clouds (11.62–65; see [[hector]]).

**Level lines.** The two sides cut each other down like reapers working toward one another across a rich man's field, “and neither side thought of deadly rout. The battle held their heads level” (11.67–72): the two lines stood even, as when Zeus later “stretched the battle evenly” (11.336; see [[the-reapers]]). At midday “the Danaans by their prowess broke the battalions, calling to their companions along the ranks” (11.90–91). Where the battalions are thrown into confusion, the battle divides into its two arms: “Foot soldiers killed foot soldiers fleeing under compulsion, and horsemen horsemen” (11.148–151).

**Rallying.** At Zeus' word Hector goes through the army, and the Trojans rally, “and stood facing the Achaeans” (11.211–214, the verses of his rallies in Books 5 and 6); the Argives strengthen their battalions, “and the battle was set in order, and they stood facing each other” (11.215–216). ἀρτύνθη δὲ μάχη occurs only here. Late in the day Eurypylus calls on the Achaean leaders to “turn and stand” around Ajax, and they come and stand close beside him, “leaning their shields against their shoulders and holding up their spears”, and Ajax turns and stands when he reaches them (11.586–595; see [[eurypylus]] and [[the-shield-of-ajax]]).

**The wings.** Hector does not know of Ajax's rout of the Trojans, “since he was fighting on the left of all the battle, beside the banks of the river Scamander”, around Nestor and Idomeneus (11.497–501). Cebriones tells him that the two of them are fighting “at the edge of grim-sounding war”, and drives him across to the center, where Ajax is (11.523–530; see [[cebriones]]).

**The champions.** The πρόμαχοι fight in front of the rest. Zeus' message is to hold back while Agamemnon is “rushing among the champions” (11.188, 11.203). Agastrophus, on foot with his horses far behind, “rushed through the champions, until he lost his dear life” (11.341–342). In Nestor's story the young Nestor, on foot among the Pylian horsemen while “the tribes of foot soldiers streamed in”, kills Mulius, leaps into his chariot and takes his stand “among the champions”, and the Epeians scatter when they see “the leader of the horsemen” fall (11.720–746; see [[nestor-and-the-epeians]]).
