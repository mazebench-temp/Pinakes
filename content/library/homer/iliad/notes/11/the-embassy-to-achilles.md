summary: Book 11 looks back on the embassy twice. Watching the rout, Achilles says that now the Achaeans will stand around his knees beseeching him (11.609–610), as if the envoys had not come; and Nestor asks whether Achilles is waiting until the ships burn (11.666–668), the very term Achilles had set in his last answer to Ajax (9.650–655).

**Beseeching.** From the stern of his ship Achilles sees the Achaeans routed and calls Patroclus: “now I think the Achaeans will stand around my knees / beseeching me; for a need has come that is no longer bearable” (11.609–610). λισσομένους, “beseeching”, is the word of the envoys' errand, for Agamemnon “has sent the best men to entreat you” (9.520). Whether these verses show that the embassy was composed later, or mean that only now will the army come as suppliants in earnest, is an old question, set out above and in the translation's note on 11.609–610. The second verse is Nestor's in the night before, but for one word: there the need sent a king to beg his fellow chiefs (10.117–118; see [[longing-for-achilles]]).

**The term guessed.** Nestor, speaking to Patroclus, asks: “Is he waiting until the swift ships near the sea / burn with consuming fire in spite of the Argives, / and we ourselves are killed one after another?” (11.666–668). Odysseus' report to the kings had given only Achilles' threat to sail home at dawn (9.677–687). The term that Achilles set in his last answer, that he would not think of war until Hector reached the huts and ships of the Myrmidons and burned the ships with fire (9.650–655), is the one Nestor guesses, and the one Achilles keeps (16.61–63; see [[achilles-withdraws]]).

**The first visit.** Nestor's account of the day in Phthia shows the scene that the embassy repeated: visitors at the door, Achilles springing up in amazement, food and drink, and then the visitor's speech (11.769–782; 11.777 = 9.193). In Phthia the young man was eager to come; in Book 9 he refused. The reproach with which Odysseus closed his reminder of Peleus' charge, “So the old man charged you, but you forget”, Nestor now turns on Patroclus (11.790 = 9.259; see [[peleus-sends-achilles-to-troy]]).
