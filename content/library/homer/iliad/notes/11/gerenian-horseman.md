---
summary: Nestor is “the Gerenian horseman” when he takes Machaon into his chariot and when he answers Patroclus, and “the Gerenian” in Patroclus' words to Eurypylus.
---

“So he spoke, and the Gerenian horseman Nestor did not disobey” (11.516): Nestor mounts his chariot with the wounded Machaon and drives to the ships ([[chariot-journey]]). The full formula introduces his answer to Patroclus, the longest speech of the book (11.655–803; [[nestor-and-the-epeians]]). Patroclus, telling Eurypylus that he must take Achilles the word “with which Nestor charged me, the Gerenian, the guardian of the Achaeans,” gives him the title with another epithet (11.839–840; [[guardian-of-the-achaeans]]). The horseman's title suits a book in which Nestor's mares carry him from the field (11.597) and his story turns on the horses his father hid from him (11.717–721). See [[nestor]].
