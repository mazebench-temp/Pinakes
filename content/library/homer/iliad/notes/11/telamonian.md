---
summary: Ajax is “son of Telamon” from the placing of his huts at the end of the line to his retreat; Menelaus calls him “Telamonian Ajax,” and Cebriones knows him by his shield.
grc: [Τελαμώνιον]
---

Strife's shout carries “to the huts of Ajax, son of Telamon,” at one end of the line (11.7; [[balanced-ships]]). Menelaus calls him to Odysseus' rescue as “Telamonian Ajax, sprung from Zeus, lord of the people” (11.465; [[sprung-from-zeus]]), and Cebriones, seeing the Trojans routed, tells Hector, “It is Telamonian Ajax who drives them in confusion; I know him well, for he has the broad shield around his shoulders” (11.526–527; [[seven-oxhide]]). Hector avoids battle “with Ajax, son of Telamon” (11.542; [[aristotle-nemesis]]). In the retreat he is “great Ajax, the son of Telamon,” in the narrator's verse and in Eurypylus' call to stand around him (11.563, 11.591; [[gathered-from-many-lands]]).
