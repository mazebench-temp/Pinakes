---
summary: Euaemon is named twice through his son: “the splendid son of Euaemon” as Eurypylus comes to the help of Ajax (11.575), and Εὐαιμονίδης, “son of Euaemon”, as he limps from the battle and meets Patroclus (11.810).
---

Εὐαίμονος ἀγλαὸς υἱός, “the splendid son of Euaemon”, is the half-verse that has named Eurypylus wherever he acts: in the Catalogue, in the first battle, among the volunteers against Hector and among the leaders who cross the ditch (2.736, 5.79, 7.167, 8.265; see [[splendid]]). Here, for once, it comes before his name, which waits for the next verse (11.575–576; see [[eurypylus]]). The patronymic returns at the end of the book with a new epithet, διογενὴς Εὐαιμονίδης, “the son of Euaemon, sprung from Zeus” (11.810; see [[sprung-from-zeus]]). Homer knows the father only through the son.
