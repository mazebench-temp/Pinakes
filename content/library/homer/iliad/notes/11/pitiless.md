---
summary: Odysseus keeps off “the pitiless day” with his spear, and Eurypylus calls the Achaeans to keep it away from Ajax.
---

Surrounded by the Trojans like a stag among jackals, Odysseus, “darting with his spear, kept off the pitiless day” (11.483–484; [[the-jackals-and-the-stag]]). When Ajax is hard pressed, the wounded Eurypylus calls to the Achaean leaders: “turn and stand, and keep the pitiless day away from Ajax” (11.588–589). νηλεὲς ἦμαρ is the day of death.
