**Zeus' seat.** As Agamemnon nears the wall, “the father of men and gods / sat down on the peaks of Ida rich in springs, / coming down from heaven, with the lightning in his hands” (11.182–184):

{{quote:c6c1ae0c-17d6-5c7d-9f54-56662c199210}}

πιδήεσσα, from πῖδαξ, “spring”, occurs only here; it says what the usual πολυπῖδαξ, “of the many springs”, says (8.47; see [[rich-in-springs]] and [[of-the-many-springs]]). From Ida Iris goes down “from the mountains of Ida to sacred Ilios” with Zeus' message to Hector (11.196; see [[iris]]), and from Ida, “looking down”, Zeus stretches the battle evenly between the two sides (11.336–337; see [[zeus]] and [[watching-the-war]]). He sat there for the second day of battle too (8.47–52).

**The herdsmen's mountain.** Isus and Antiphus were herding sheep “on the slopes of Ida” when Achilles caught them, bound them with willow shoots, and brought them to the ships, until they were ransomed (11.104–112; see [[isus]] and [[antiphus-son-of-priam]]). Ἴδης ἐν κνημοῖσι is the phrase of the place where Aphrodite bore Aeneas to Anchises (2.821), and on the same mountain Achilles chased Aeneas from his cattle (20.188–191).
