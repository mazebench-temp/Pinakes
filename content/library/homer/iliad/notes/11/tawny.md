---
summary: Ajax's retreat is compared to a “tawny lion” driven from a farmyard.
grc: [αἴθωνα]
---

“And as from the farmyard of the cattle a tawny lion is driven off by dogs and country men, … so then Ajax, grieving at heart, went back from the Trojans very unwillingly” (11.548–557; [[lion-driven-from-the-farmyard]], [[bold]]). The simile returns, without the epithet, when Menelaus leaves the body of Patroclus very unwillingly (17.657–666). See [[lion]] and [[ajax]].
