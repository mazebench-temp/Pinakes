---
summary: The “great-hearted” Trojans and Epeians, Arsinous, the father of Hecamede, and Odysseus' “proud heart.”
---

μεγάθυμοι is said twice of the Trojans, as Hector sets them on the Achaeans (11.294; [[the-hunter-and-his-hounds]]) and as they see Odysseus' blood and come at him all together (11.459–460), and twice of the Epeians in Nestor's story, standing around Thryoessa and fleeing when he kills Mulius (11.732–733, 11.744–745; [[epeians]]). Its synonym μεγαλήτωρ praises Arsinous, whose daughter Hecamede was taken at Tenedos (11.626; [[arsinous]]), and is the word of Odysseus' heart when he is left alone: “and deeply troubled he spoke to his own proud heart” (11.403; [[pondering-two-ways]], [[thymos]]). The translation renders that formula, πρὸς ὃν μεγαλήτορα θυμόν, “to his own proud heart.”
