Θρυόεσσα, “rushy”, from θρύον, “rush”, is a fuller form of the Catalogue's Θρύον, “Thryum” (2.592). “There is a city, Thryoessa, a steep hill” (ἔστι δέ τις Θρυόεσσα πόλις αἰπεῖα κολώνη, 11.711) has the frame of the verse that describes Batieia before Troy, ἔστι δέ τις … αἰπεῖα κολώνη (2.811). It lies “on the border of sandy Pylos” (νεάτη Πύλου ἠμαθόεντος), the phrase that places Agamemnon's seven cities (9.153; see [[sandy]] and [[pylos]]). On the third day after Nestor's raid the Epeians laid siege to it, “eager to destroy it utterly” (11.713, 11.733), and the Pylians, roused by Athena in the night, marched to its relief (see [[nestor-and-the-epeians]] and [[alpheus]]).

The Catalogue puts Thryum at “the ford of the Alpheus” (Ἀλφειοῖο πόρον); Nestor puts Thryoessa on a steep hill “far off on the Alpheus”.

## Outside Homer

Strabo took Thryum and Thryoessa for one place: “what he now calls Thryon he elsewhere calls Thryoessa”. The place, he says, was then called Epitalium, in Macistia, and he quotes 11.712 with πυμάτη, “last”, where our text has νεάτη (*Geography* 8.3.24).
