Heracles has a place in Nestor's story only as its cause. “We in Pylos were few and had been ill-used; / for mighty Heracles had come and ill-used us / in the years before, and all our best were killed” (11.689–691): ἐκάκωσε, “ill-used”, answers κεκακωμένοι in the verse before. He is named by his might, βίη Ἡρακληείη, as in the Catalogue (2.658, 2.666; see [[might-of-heracles]]). Of the twelve sons of Neleus, Nestor alone was left (11.692–693; see [[neleus]]), and the Epeians, “haughty because of this”, wronged the weakened Pylians and brought on the war Nestor tells (11.694–695; see [[nestor-and-the-epeians]]).

The Iliad tells this exploit only here, and gives it no cause. In Book 5 Dione says that Heracles shot Hades “in Pylos among the dead”, which may refer to the same war (5.395–397; see [[pylos-or-the-gate]] and [[heracles-wounds-hera-and-hades]]). For the later accounts, in which Heracles also kills Neleus and Nestor survives because he is away among the Gerenians, see [[heracles-sacks-pylos]].
