---
summary: Hector is “son of Priam” in Iris' address and in the verses of his charge; Doryclus, killed by Ajax, is “a bastard son of Priam.”
---

Iris greets Hector: “Hector, son of Priam, the equal of Zeus in counsel” (11.200; [[equal-of-zeus-in-counsel]]). When Agamemnon has left the field, “Hector, son of Priam, the equal of Ares, ruin of mortals” sets the Trojans on the Achaeans (11.295), and the poet asks, “Then whom first, whom last did he slay, Hector, son of Priam, when Zeus gave him glory?” (11.299–300; [[whom-first-whom-last]]). Two other sons of Priam die in the book: Isus and Antiphus at Agamemnon's hands (11.101–109; [[isus]], [[antiphus-son-of-priam]]), and Doryclus, “a bastard son of Priam,” at Ajax's (11.489–490; [[doryclus]], [[nothos]]).
