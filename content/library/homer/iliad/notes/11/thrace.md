---

ὃς τράφη ἐν Θρῄκῃ ἐριβώλακι μητέρι μήλων, “who was reared in deep-soiled Thrace, mother of flocks” (11.222). The two epithets are those of Phthia in Phoenix's speech (9.479; see [[deep-soiled]] and [[mother-of-flocks]]), and they suit the story that follows: the bride-price Iphidamas paid there was counted in flocks, a hundred cattle given and a thousand goats and sheep promised, from herds “pastured for him in untold numbers” (11.244–245; see [[iphidamas]] and [[cisseus]]). He sailed with twelve ships at the report of the Achaeans' coming, left them at Percote on the Asian shore, and came on foot to Ilios (11.227–230; see [[percote]]). The Thracians who fight for Troy came the same way, from across the Hellespont (2.844–845; see [[thracians]]).
