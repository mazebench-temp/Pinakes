---
summary: The Olenian Rock is one of the three places, with Buprasium and the hill of Alesium, where the Pylians' pursuit of the Epeians ended (11.757).
grc: ["πέτρης τʼ Ὠλενίης"]
---

“Until we brought our horses to Buprasium, rich in wheat, / and the Olenian Rock, and where the hill of Alesium / is named” (11.756–758). πέτρης τʼ Ὠλενίης is the Catalogue's πέτρη τʼ Ὠλενίη (2.617) in the genitive, governed by ἐπί of the verse before. In the Catalogue it is one of the points that enclose the land of the Epeians (see [[elis]], [[buprasium]], [[alesium]] and [[nestor-and-the-epeians]]).

## Outside Homer

Strabo quotes these verses and reports the conjecture that the Olenian Rock is the present Scollis, a rocky mountain shared by the people of Dyme, Tritaea and Elis; one can only guess, he says, “since both the places and the names have changed” (*Geography* 8.3.10).
