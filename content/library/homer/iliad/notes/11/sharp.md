---
summary: ὀξύς describes the spears of Agamemnon, Hector and Odysseus, the arrows of the two wounds, Agamemnon's “sharp pains,” and “the sharp Ares of the Trojans.”
grc: [ὀξεῖαι]
---

**Spears.** Agamemnon arms with two spears, “sharp,” whose bronze shines up into the sky (11.43–44; [[tipped-with-bronze]], [[strong-spear]]), and stabs Oileus with his sharp spear (11.95); Hector brandishes his sharp spears as he rallies the Trojans (11.212); Odysseus wounds Deiopites with his (11.421).

**Pains and arrows.** When Agamemnon's wound dries, “sharp pains came over the might of the son of Atreus,” and the verse is repeated after the simile of the sharp dart of labor (11.268–272; [[the-woman-in-labor]], [[bitter]]). Diomedes boasts that his own shaft is sharp, though Paris' is blunt (11.392; [[torn-in-mourning]]), and Patroclus cuts “the sharp, piercing arrow” from Eurypylus' thigh (11.845; [[piercing]]).

**Ares.** Eurypylus says that Podalirius is out on the plain, where he “awaits the sharp Ares of the Trojans” (11.836): ὀξὺς Ἄρης is war at its keenest. The phrase ὀξὺ νόησε, “was quick to see” (11.343), is excluded.
