---
summary: κρατερός marks “mighty Diomedes,” the “fierce battle,” Coon's “strong grief,” and the lion's “strong teeth” in two similes of Agamemnon's rout.
grc: [κρατεροῖσιν]
---

Diomedes is “mighty Diomedes” in the verses that introduce his speeches to Odysseus, to Hector and to Paris (11.316, 11.361, 11.384) and in Nestor's list of the wounded (11.660; [[son-of-tydeus]]). The battle is “fierce” in Zeus' message and Iris' repetition of it (11.190, 11.205; [[destructive]]) and in Menelaus' fear for Odysseus (11.468). When Coon sees his brother fall, “strong grief veiled his eyes” (11.249–250; [[eldest-born]]). Twice in Agamemnon's rout a lion seizes its prey “in his strong teeth” (11.114, 11.175; [[the-lion-and-the-fawns]], [[lion]]).
