grc: [οἴνῳ]
---

**Pramnian wine.** The drink that Hecamede mixes in Nestor's cup is made “with Pramnian wine”, οἴνῳ Πραμνείῳ, with goat's cheese grated over it and white barley meal sprinkled on top (11.638–641; see [[kykeon]] and [[barley-meal]]). Circe's drink in the Odyssey is mixed with Pramnian wine too, with cheese, barley meal and pale honey, and with her drugs (Od. 10.234–236). What “Pramnian” meant was already uncertain to ancient readers: the name of a place, variously located, or of a kind of strong dark wine (see the translation's note on 11.639 and [[pramnian]]). It is the only wine in the Iliad given a name of its own. Wine drunk here is not for a feast but for the restoring of two men just out of the battle, and Plato's Socrates asks whether a doctor or a rhapsode should judge if Homer speaks rightly about it (*Ion* 538b–c; see [[plato-asclepiads]]).

**Wine for the gods.** At Peleus' house the old man holds a golden goblet, “pouring sparkling wine over the burning offerings” (σπένδων αἴθοπα οἶνον, 11.775; see [[libation]], [[sacrifice]] and [[cup]]).
