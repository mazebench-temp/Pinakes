---
summary: Brothers die together through Book 11, and two of them die defending a brother. Coon, Antenor's eldest son, wounds Agamemnon to avenge Iphidamas, “his brother by the same father”, and is killed over his body; Socus comes to defend his brother Charops and is killed by Odysseus.
grc: [ὄπατρον]
passages:
  - 11.248-263 | Iphidamas and Coon
  - 11.426-449 | Charops and Socus
---

**Coon.** When Agamemnon kills Iphidamas, Coon, “the eldest-born son of Antenor”, sees it, and “strong grief veiled his eyes for his fallen brother” (11.248–250). He stabs Agamemnon through the arm from the side, unseen, and is dragging the body away by the foot when Agamemnon kills him over it (11.251–261; see [[coon]], [[iphidamas]] and the article above). κασίγνητον καὶ ὄπατρον, “his brother by the same father” (11.257), joins to the usual word one that names the shared father; the pair returns once more, of Teucer, who walks beside Ajax as his brother “by the same father” (12.371; see [[teucer]]). The two sons of Antenor die together: “There the sons of Antenor, at the hands of the king, the son of Atreus, fulfilled their fate” (11.262–263; see [[antenor]]).

**Socus.** Odysseus wounds Charops, son of Hippasus, “the own brother of wealthy Socus” (αὐτοκασίγνητον, 11.426–427), and Socus comes “to defend him” (11.428). He wounds Odysseus, turns to flee, and is speared in the back; Socus had told Odysseus that he would either “boast over both sons of Hippasus” or lose his life, and the first came true (11.431–433, 11.449–455; see [[socus]] and [[charops]]).

**Brothers in pairs.** The book's other brothers die side by side without defending each other: Isus and Antiphus, Priam's sons, “one a bastard and one born in wedlock”, in one chariot (11.101–109; see [[nothos]]); Peisander and Hippolochus, the sons of Antimachus, in another (11.122–147); and the two sons of Merops (11.328–334). The only brothers who escape are the twins of Nestor's story, the Moliones, hidden in a mist by their father Poseidon (11.750–752; see [[the-moliones]] and [[death-in-battle]]).
