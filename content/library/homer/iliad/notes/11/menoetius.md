summary: Menoetius speaks once in Homer, in Nestor's memory. On the day the army was gathered he was in Peleus' house, and he charged Patroclus, the elder of the two young men, to give Achilles good counsel; Nestor's reminder, “but you forget”, sends Patroclus back to Achilles with the plan that will lead to his death.

**The father's name.** Patroclus is “the valiant son of Menoetius” three times in the book (Μενοιτίου ἄλκιμος υἱός, 11.605, 11.814, 11.837), and Achilles calls him “son of Menoetius” (11.608; see [[son-of-menoetius]] and [[patroclus]]). Nestor gives the father a father of his own: “Menoetius, son of Actor” (11.785; see [[actor-father-of-menoetius]]).

**In Peleus' house.** When Nestor and Odysseus came to Phthia to gather the army, they found “the hero Menoetius within”, with Patroclus and Achilles, while Peleus sacrificed in the courtyard (11.769–775; see [[peleus]] and [[peleus-sends-achilles-to-troy]]). Both young men were willing to go, and “the two old men gave you many charges” (11.782). Peleus told his son always to be the best (11.784; see [[always-to-be-the-best]]). Menoetius' charge is the only speech the poem gives him, and it comes in Nestor's voice, a speech within a speech:

{{quote:e062ce9f-0c28-590a-925b-a8d710d2823f}}

The father ranks the two young men. Achilles is higher “in lineage” (γενεῇ), the son of a goddess, and far the stronger; Patroclus is the elder (πρεσβύτερος), and age gives him the right to advise (see the translation's note on 11.786). Patroclus is to speak “a shrewd word” (πυκινὸν ἔπος), to counsel and to direct, and the charge ends in a promise: “he will obey you, to his good” (11.789; see [[obedience-and-persuasion]]).

**“But you forget.”** Nestor frames the charge in the verses with which Odysseus brought Peleus' charge to Achilles in the embassy: “My friend, surely … charged you” (11.765 ≈ 9.252), “on that day when he sent you from Phthia to Agamemnon” (11.766 = 9.253), and the reproach, “So the old man charged you, but you forget” (11.790 = 9.259). There the father was Peleus, and his son refused. Here the father is Menoetius, and the son goes, his heart stirred (11.804; see [[fathers-and-sons]]).

**Afterward.** Achilles thinks of Menoetius when Patroclus comes to him weeping, and asks whether news has come from Phthia: “they say that Menoetius, son of Actor, is still alive” (16.13–14). After Patroclus' death he groans that he made an empty promise “on that day” to the hero Menoetius in his halls, to bring his son home to Opus with his share of the spoil of Ilios (18.324–327). ἥρωα Μενοίτιον, “the hero Menoetius”, is Nestor's phrase as well (11.771). Patroclus' ghost tells how Menoetius brought him as a small boy from Opus to Peleus' house, after he had killed another boy in anger over knucklebones (23.85–88).
