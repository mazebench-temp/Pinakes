summary: Priam appears in Book 11 only as a father: of Hector, whom Iris finds with Zeus' message, and of three sons who die, Isus and Antiphus, killed by Agamemnon in one chariot, and the bastard Doryclus, killed by Ajax.
passages:
  - 11.101-121 | Agamemnon kills Isus and Antiphus
  - 11.489-490 | Ajax kills Doryclus
---

Every mention of Priam in the book names a son. Isus and Antiphus are “two sons of Priam, one a bastard and one born in wedlock” (11.102; see [[isus]] and [[antiphus-son-of-priam]]), and Doryclus is “a bastard son of Priam” (11.490; see [[doryclus]], [[nothos]] and [[sons-of-priam]]). Hector is the son of Priam three times: when Iris finds “the son of wise-hearted Priam, brilliant Hector” (11.197), a verse used also in Achilles' answer to Ajax in Book 9 and when Apollo finds Hector in Book 15 (9.651, 15.239; see [[wise-hearted]]); in Iris' greeting, “Hector, son of Priam” (11.200); and as Πριαμίδης when he charges (11.295, 11.300; see [[hector]] and [[son-of-priam]]).

The “descendant of Dardanus” whose tomb the Trojans run past is not Priam but his grandfather Ilus, though Priam bears the same title (11.166, 11.372; 3.303; see [[ilus]] and [[dardanus]]).
