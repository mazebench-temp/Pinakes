summary: The leaders fall and the army must fight on without them. Agamemnon and then Eurypylus, wounded, hand the defense of the ships to the other chiefs in the same verse, “Friends, leaders and rulers of the Argives”; Hector kills nine leaders “and after them the multitude”; and in Nestor's story the Epeians run when their leader of the horsemen falls, while Neleus divides the spoil “so that no one might go away cheated of his fair share”.
passages:
  - 11.276-279 | Agamemnon leaves the battle
  - 11.299-309 | The nine leaders killed by Hector
  - 11.586-595 | Eurypylus rallies the Achaeans around Ajax
---

**The leader first.** At dawn Strife puts strength into every Achaean, and Agamemnon calls the Argives to arm (11.10–16). The men go out on foot and form up at the ditch ahead of the horsemen (11.47–52; see [[battle-order]]), and when the Trojan line breaks at the hour of the woodcutter's meal the Danaans go forward “calling to their companions along the ranks; and Agamemnon sprang in first” (11.91–92). Zeus' order to Hector makes the same distinction between the leader and the army from the other side: while Agamemnon rages, Hector is to draw back “and order the rest of the army to fight the enemy” (11.189–190; see [[retreat]]).

**Handing over.** When the leaders are wounded, they hand the army to the other leaders in one verse. Agamemnon, driving from the field, shouts, “Friends, leaders and rulers of the Argives, it is for you now to ward off from the seafaring ships the grievous din of battle” (11.276–278); Eurypylus, shot by Paris, uses the same address to call them to stand around Ajax, and they come and stand close, leaning their shields against their shoulders (11.587–594; see [[the-wounded-chiefs]]). Patroclus, meeting Eurypylus, turns the address into a lament: “Ah, wretched leaders and rulers of the Danaans” (11.816).

**Leaders and multitude.** Hector's charge is told in two parts. First nine names, “these were the leaders of the Danaans he killed”, then “the multitude” (ἡγεμόνας … πληθύν, 11.299–305), battered like the clouds and the sea by the West Wind: “so thick were the heads of the people beaten down by Hector” (11.309; see [[hegemon]], [[laos]] and [[whom-first-whom-last]]). Odysseus, alone, names his fear in the same word, “fearing the multitude” (πληθὺν ταρβήσας, 11.405). The formula “shepherd of the people” is given in the book to great and small alike: Bianor and Apisaon, Diomedes and Eurypylus, Agamemnon twice and the wounded healer Machaon three times (11.92, 11.578, 11.370, 11.842, 11.187, 11.202, 11.506, 11.598, 11.651; see [[shepherd-of-the-people]]).

**An army in Nestor's story.** Nestor's war shows an army and its leaders working together. The leading men of the Pylians divide the spoil among those to whom a debt was owed; Neleus takes his share and gives the rest “to the people to divide, so that no one might go away cheated of his fair share” (11.687–705; see [[division-of-spoils]]). Athena gathers the army by night, “not unwilling, but very eager to fight” (11.714–717); they take supper “throughout the army, by companies” (11.730). When Nestor kills Mulius, “the leader of the horsemen”, the Epeians scatter (11.737–746; see [[nestor-and-the-epeians]]). At the end of the speech Nestor turns to the army that Achilles is failing: he will weep, “when the army has perished” (11.764), and the Myrmidons should follow Patroclus out (11.796–797; see [[myrmidons]]).
