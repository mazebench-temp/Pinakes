---
summary: The boar's “white tusk,” Ajax's “white flesh,” the “white barley meal” of Nestor's drink, and the “white fat” of the dead in Patroclus' lament.
grc: [ἀργέτι, "λευκὸν ὀδόντα", "χρόα λευκὸν", "ἄλφιτα λευκὰ"]
---

λευκός, the common word for white, describes the boar of the simile “whetting the white tusk in his curving jaws” (11.416; [[the-boar-at-bay]], [[supple]]); the spears that fall short of Ajax and stick in the ground “before they could reach his white flesh” (11.573; [[bold]]); and the “white barley meal” that Hecamede sprinkles on the drink (11.640; [[kykeon]], [[barley-meal]]). ἀργής is the white of fat: Patroclus laments that the Achaean leaders are destined “to glut the swift dogs in Troy with your white fat” (11.818; [[swift-dogs]], [[wretch]]).
