---
summary: Menelaus is “fair-haired” in the story of Antimachus; Nestor's spoil includes “tawny” mares; Agamede, Augeas' daughter, is “fair-haired.”
grc: [ξανθὰς, ξανθὴν]
---

Antimachus, bribed with Paris' gold, “would not let Helen be given to fair-haired Menelaus” (11.124–125; [[antimachus]], [[menelaus]]). In Nestor's story ξανθός is the color of horses and of a woman. The spoil of his raid on Elis includes “a hundred and fifty tawny horses, all mares” (11.680–681; [[raiding]]), and his first kill in the battle, Mulius, had married Augeas' eldest daughter, “fair-haired Agamede, who knew all the drugs that the wide earth nourishes” (11.740–741; [[agamede]], [[wide-earth]]). Of horses the translation renders the word “tawny,” as at 9.407 ([[horses]]).
