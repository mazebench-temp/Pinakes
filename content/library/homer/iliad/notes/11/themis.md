**The custom with guests.** When Nestor and Odysseus came to Peleus' house, Achilles led them in, bade them sit, “and set good cheer before us, as is the custom with guests” (ἅ τε ξείνοις θέμις ἐστίν, 11.779; see [[xenia]]). The clause is a form of ἣ θέμις ἐστί, “as is the custom”, with which the poem names what is laid down for a situation: for a king to test the army with words (2.73), for a man to speak against the king in the assembly (9.33), for a man and a woman to share a bed (9.134, 9.276). In the Odyssey Odysseus asks the Cyclops for a guest-gift, “which is the custom for guests” (ἥ τε ξείνων θέμις ἐστίν, *Odyssey* 9.267–268), in nearly the words of Nestor's verse, and reminds him that Zeus avenges guests; the Cyclops answers that the Cyclopes care nothing for Zeus (*Odyssey* 9.269–276).

**The place of justice.** Patroclus, running back along the ships, comes to the ships of Odysseus, “where their place of assembly and of justice was, where too the altars of the gods had been built for them” (ἀγορή τε θέμις τε, 11.806–808). Here θέμις is the place where judgments, θέμιστες, were given, as ἀγορή is the place where the army met (see the translation's note on 11.806–808, [[assembly]] and [[altar]]). The center of the camp is at Odysseus' ships, in the middle of the line, where Strife stood at dawn to shout to both ends (11.5–9). The poem does not reconcile it with the assembly that the herald found in Book 7 “beside the stern of Agamemnon's ship” (7.382–383). There, by the altars and the place of judgment, Patroclus meets the wounded Eurypylus and stops to heal him (11.809–848; see [[eurypylus]] and [[healing]]).
