---
summary: Agamemnon and Iphidamas meet in the verse that brings duelists face to face, after an invocation of the Muses and the story of Iphidamas' life (11.232).
---

“He it was who now came against Agamemnon, son of Atreus. And when they had come close, advancing against each other, the son of Atreus missed, and his spear turned aside” (11.231–233). The meeting is prepared as the great duels are: the narrator calls on the Muses to tell who first came against the king, and gives the man's lineage and history before the two come together (11.218–231; see [[invocation-of-the-muses]] and [[genealogy-in-battle]]). In Book 6 the same verse brought Glaucus and Diomedes together for a question and a friendship (6.121); here it brings an unequal fight. Agamemnon throws first and misses, Iphidamas' thrust bends on his belt, and Agamemnon kills him with the sword (11.233–240; see [[iphidamas]] and [[belt]]).
