---
summary: The sons of Antimachus offer Agamemnon “a boundless ransom” from their father's treasures, and are refused.
---

Peisander and Hippolochus kneel in their chariot: their father's house holds bronze and gold and worked iron, “from these our father would gladly give you a boundless ransom, if he heard that we two were alive at the ships of the Achaeans” (11.131–135; [[worked-with-much-toil]], [[apoina]]). Agamemnon answers that they will pay instead for their father's shameful insult, and kills them both (11.138–147; [[sons-of-antimachus]], [[supplication]]). The same verse-end carried Chryses' ransom for his daughter at the beginning of the poem (1.13). See [[agamemnon]].
