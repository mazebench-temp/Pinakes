---
summary: The huts of Book 11 mark the turn of the story. Patroclus comes out of Achilles' hut at his call, “and this was the beginning of evil for him” (11.603–604); in Nestor's hut Hecamede serves the wounded Machaon, and Patroclus will not sit (11.618–654); and Patroclus takes the wounded Eurypylus into Eurypylus' own hut and tends him there (11.842–848).
---

**The ends of the line.** Strife shouts from Odysseus' ship, in the middle of the line, so that she can be heard at both ends, at the huts of Ajax and of Achilles (11.5–9 = 8.222–226; see [[odysseus]]). The place of assembly and of justice, with the altars of the gods, lies by Odysseus' ships too (11.806–808; see [[assembly]]).

**Achilles' hut.** Achilles watches the rout from the stern of his ship and calls Patroclus, “and he heard it in the hut and came out, the equal of Ares; and this was the beginning of evil for him” (κλισίηθεν, 11.603–604; see [[the-beginning-of-evils]] and [[patroclus]]). His first words in the poem follow (11.606), and his coming out of the hut begins the path to his death (see the translation's note on 11.604). He runs “along the huts and ships of the Achaeans” to Nestor (11.617).

**Nestor's hut.** At Nestor's hut the old man and Machaon step down, Eurymedon unyokes the horses, and the two let the sweat dry from their tunics in the breeze by the shore before they go in and sit down on chairs (11.618–623). Inside, Hecamede, the woman chosen for Nestor from the spoils of Tenedos, sets out the table and mixes the drink (11.624–641; see [[hecamede]], [[table]] and [[kykeon]]). Patroclus stands at the door (θύρῃσιν), and Nestor rises to lead him in (11.644–646; see [[visit-scene]] and [[throne]]).

**Eurypylus' hut.** Eurypylus asks Patroclus to lead him to his black ship, and Patroclus takes him “to the hut”, where an attendant spreads oxhides for him to lie on, and cuts out the arrow (11.828, 11.842–848; see [[healing]]). It is Eurypylus' own: in Book 15 Patroclus is still sitting in the hut of “manly” Eurypylus (ἀγαπήνορος Εὐρυπύλοιο), cheering him with talk and spreading drugs on the wound, until he sees the Trojans rushing at the wall and leaves him to his attendant (15.390–402; see [[eurypylus]]). Machaon, says Eurypylus, lies wounded “in the huts” (ἐνὶ κλισίῃσιν, 11.834), and Nestor's plan is to drive the Trojans “back to the city, away from the ships and huts” (11.803).
