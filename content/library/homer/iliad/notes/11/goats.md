summary: Goats in Book 11 are wealth, prey and food. Iphidamas promised a thousand goats and sheep as his bride-price (11.244–245), Paris says that the Trojans shudder at Diomedes as bleating goats shudder at a lion (11.383), Nestor drove fifty wide herds of goats from Elis (11.679), and Hecamede grates goat's cheese into the mixed drink (11.639).

**Wealth.** Iphidamas, who came from his bridal chamber to die at Troy, had given a hundred cattle for his bride and promised a thousand more beasts, “goats and sheep together, which were pastured for him in untold numbers” (11.244–245; see [[iphidamas]] and [[marriage]]). In Nestor's story the plunder of the cattle raid on Elis included, beside cattle, sheep and pigs, “as many wide-ranging herds of goats”, αἰπόλια πλατέʼ αἰγῶν (11.679; see [[nestor-and-the-epeians]] and [[raiding]]). The phrase is the one of the goatherds who sort out their wide flocks in Book 2 (2.474; see [[goatherds-sorting-their-flocks]]), and the whole verse returns in the Odyssey among the herds of Odysseus (*Odyssey* 14.101).

**Prey.** Paris, mocking Diomedes after shooting him through the foot, says that the Trojans “shudder at you as bleating goats shudder at a lion” (11.383; see [[goats-before-a-lion]]). μηκάδες αἶγες, “bleating goats”, are the goats of the flocks, killed at Patroclus' funeral feast (23.31) and milked by the Cyclops (*Odyssey* 9.244).

**Cheese.** In Nestor's hut Hecamede makes the mixed drink of Pramnian wine for Nestor and Machaon, and grates goat's cheese into it with a bronze grater, αἴγειον … τυρόν, before sprinkling it with white barley (11.638–640; see [[kykeon]] and [[hecamede]]). Circe's potion in the Odyssey is made the same way, with cheese, barley meal and pale honey stirred into Pramnian wine (*Odyssey* 10.234–235).
