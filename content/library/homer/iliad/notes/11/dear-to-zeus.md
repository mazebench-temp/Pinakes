---
summary: Odysseus is “dear to Zeus” twice as the Trojans surround him, and Achilles calls Patroclus so as he sends him to Nestor.
---

The Trojans rush “around Odysseus, dear to Zeus” like hounds and young men around a boar (11.414–420; [[the-boar-at-bay]]), and Menelaus and Ajax find “Odysseus, dear to Zeus” with the Trojans around him like jackals around a wounded stag (11.473–474; [[the-jackals-and-the-stag]]). The epithet is given to him when, as he has said himself, the son of Cronus has put the other Danaans to rout and left him alone (11.401–406). Achilles sends his friend on the errand with the same words: “But go now, Patroclus, dear to Zeus, and ask Nestor” (11.611). See [[odysseus]] and [[patroclus]].
