Ἕκτορι παρβεβαώς, “standing beside Hector in the chariot” (11.522): he has held Hector's reins since Teucer killed Archeptolemus (8.318–319; see [[archeptolemus]] and [[hector]]). He speaks once in the poem, and it is to give his brother counsel:

{{quote:ec912596-a281-565a-b510-706c53281a56}}

He knows Ajax at a distance by his shield, “the broad shield around his shoulders” (11.527), the tower-like shield of seven oxhides (7.219–223; see [[ajax]] and [[the-shield-of-ajax]]). He lashes the horses “with the shrill whip”, and the chariot runs over corpses and shields until the axle and the rails are spattered with blood thrown up by the hooves and the tires (11.531–537; see [[chariot]] and [[horses]]). The same verses are given to Achilles' chariot in Book 20 (20.499–502). The drive brings Hector to the place where the fighting is fiercest, and to the one man there whom he will not face: he ranges through the other ranks, “but he avoided battle with Ajax, son of Telamon” (11.540–542).

The drive into the thick of the fighting is repeated at his death. In Book 16 Hector orders him to whip the horses into the battle, they make straight for Patroclus, and Patroclus' stone kills him (16.727–743; see [[patroclus]]).
