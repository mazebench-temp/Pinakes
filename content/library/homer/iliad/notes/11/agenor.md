Ἀγήνορα δῖον, “brilliant Agenor” (11.59), with his usual epithet (see [[brilliant]]). He stands between his brothers Polybus, who is named nowhere else, and “young Acamas” (11.59–60; see [[polybus]], [[acamas]] and [[antenor]]), among the leaders around whom the Trojans form up for the third day of battle (see [[trojans]]). Later in the book Agamemnon kills two more of his brothers, Iphidamas and Coon (11.221–263; see [[iphidamas]] and [[coon]]). Agenor does nothing more in Book 11. At the Achaean wall he leads the second company with Paris and Alcathous (12.93).
