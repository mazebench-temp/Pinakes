---
summary: Sheep in Book 11 are flocks to be herded and taken. Isus and Antiphus were herding sheep on Ida when Achilles caught them (11.106), Iphidamas promised a thousand goats and sheep for his bride (11.245), and Nestor drove fifty flocks of sheep from Elis, from which Neleus took a great flock for himself (11.678, 11.696).
grc: ["=ὄϊς", πώεα]
passages:
  - 11.104-106 | Isus and Antiphus with their flocks
---

The two sons of Priam whom Agamemnon kills had once been caught by Achilles “as they herded sheep” (ποιμαίνοντʼ ἐπʼ ὄεσσι) on the slopes of Ida, and let go for ransom (11.104–106; see [[achilles-raids]] and [[isus]]). Princes of Troy keep flocks on the mountain, as Anchises kept cattle there (5.313) and Aeneas was driven from his cattle on Ida (20.89–92). Iphidamas' bride-price was to come from flocks “pastured for him in untold numbers”, a thousand “goats and sheep together” (αἶγας ὁμοῦ καὶ ὄϊς, 11.245; see [[iphidamas]]).

In Nestor's story the sheep are spoil. The Pylians drove home from Elis fifty herds of cattle and “as many flocks of sheep” (τόσα πώεα οἰῶν, 11.678), and in the division Neleus took a great flock (πῶϋ μέγʼ οἰῶν) with a herd of cattle, three hundred head and their herdsmen (11.696–697; see [[nestor-and-the-epeians]] and [[cattle]]). πῶυ is a flock of sheep, used in the plural here and in the same words in Eumaeus' count of Odysseus' herds (*Odyssey* 14.100).
