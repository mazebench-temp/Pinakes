summary: The dogs of Book 11 are hunting dogs and farm dogs in four similes and the carrion dogs of Patroclus' lament. Hector sets the Trojans on like a hunter his white-toothed hounds, two boars fall on the hunting dogs, dogs and young men close on a boar at bay, and dogs and country men keep a lion from the cattle; Diomedes calls Hector a dog, and Patroclus mourns the leaders destined to glut the swift dogs of Troy (11.292–293, 11.324–325, 11.362, 11.414–418, 11.548–549, 11.816–818).

**Hounds.** Book 11 is full of hunting dogs. Hector sets the Trojans on the Achaeans as a hunter sets on his κύνας ἀργιόδοντας, “white-toothed hounds”, an epithet elsewhere given to boars (9.539, 10.264, 23.32; 11.292–293; see [[the-hunter-and-his-hounds]]). Diomedes and Odysseus fall on the Trojans as two boars fall on the hunting dogs, ἐν κυσὶ θηρευτῇσι (11.325; see [[two-boars]]), and the Trojans close around Odysseus as dogs and young men close around a boar at bay (11.414–418; see [[the-boar-at-bay]]). The roles change from simile to simile. The Trojans are Hector's hounds, then the dogs that two boars scatter, then the dogs that hold the boar at bay.

**Watchdogs.** The dogs of the farm keep the lion from the cattle yard all night with the country men, and drive it off at dawn; the lion is Ajax, and the dogs the Trojans (11.548–555; see [[lion-driven-from-the-farmyard]]). The scene is the one of the sentries' simile in the night before, dogs keeping watch over the fold (10.183–189; see [[the-watchdogs]]).

**Insult and carrion.** When Hector escapes his spear, Diomedes shouts after him: “Once again you have escaped death now, you dog” (11.362), the verse with which Achilles will shout after him in Book 20 (20.449; see [[hector]]). When Patroclus meets the wounded Eurypylus, he laments the leaders of the Danaans, destined “to glut the swift dogs in Troy with your white fat” (11.816–818), far from their friends and their native land. The dogs that eat the dead are the proem's (1.4–5); Hecuba fears them for Hector (24.211; see [[birds]] and [[patroclus]]).
