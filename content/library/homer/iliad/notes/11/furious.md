---
summary: The “furious shield” of Agamemnon's arming, and four calls to “furious courage.”
---

Agamemnon takes up “the furious shield, man-covering, intricately worked” (11.32; [[man-covering]], [[intricately-worked]]). The adjective of courage is given to a shield again at 20.162 and to the aegis at 15.308. The formula θούριδος ἀλκῆς, “furious courage,” comes four times. Hector calls on the Trojans, Lycians and Dardanians to “remember your furious courage” as Agamemnon leaves the field (11.287; [[hand-to-hand-fighters]]); Odysseus asks Diomedes “what has happened to us, that we have forgotten our furious courage?” (11.313); Ajax in his retreat “would remember his furious courage and turn back again” (11.566–567); and in Nestor's story the Moliones were “still boys, not yet well skilled in furious courage” (11.710; [[the-moliones]]).
