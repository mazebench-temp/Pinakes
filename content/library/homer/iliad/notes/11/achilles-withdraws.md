summary: Achilles watches the battle of Book 11 from the stern of his ship, and for the first time since the embassy he speaks and acts: he calls Patroclus out of the hut and sends him to Nestor to ask about the wounded man in Nestor's chariot (11.599–615).
passages:
  - 11.599-617 | Achilles watches and sends Patroclus
---

**At the end of the line.** The book places Achilles at once. When Strife shouts from Odysseus' ship in the middle of the line, her cry carries to Ajax's huts at one end and to Achilles' at the other, where the two had drawn up their ships, “trusting in their manhood and the strength of their hands” (11.5–9 = 8.222–226). The sentence that places the camp names the absent man.

**The watcher.** When Nestor's mares carry the wounded Machaon past, Achilles sees them:

{{quote:c6473a4b-2cc8-50a2-a990-eb79462d9038}}

The man who withdrew from the fighting has become a spectator of it, like Zeus watching from Ida in the same book (11.80–83, 11.336–337; see [[watching-the-war]]). He speaks for the first time since the embassy, and what he says is what he has waited for: “now I think the Achaeans will stand around my knees / beseeching me” (11.609–610; see [[longing-for-achilles]]). But he does not go out. He sends Patroclus to find out who the wounded man is, since the mares darted past too fast for him to see the man's face (11.611–615).

**The message that comes back.** The errand brings Achilles' withdrawal before Nestor, who judges it: Achilles does not care for the Danaans or pity them, and will have the profit of his prowess alone (11.664–665, 11.762–764). Nestor's plan, that Achilles at least send Patroclus out in his armor, is the answer to the withdrawal that Achilles will accept. In Book 16 he recalls the term he set to Ajax and sends Patroclus out (16.60–100; see [[the-embassy-to-achilles]] and [[the-death-of-patroclus]]).
