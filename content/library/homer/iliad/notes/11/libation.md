---
summary: Peleus pours sparkling wine from a golden goblet over the burning thighs of an ox as he sacrifices to Zeus in his courtyard, on the day he sends Achilles to the war (11.774–775).
grc: [σπένδων]
---

“And he was holding a golden goblet, pouring sparkling wine over the burning offerings” (ἔχε δὲ χρύσειον ἄλεισον σπένδων αἴθοπα οἶνον ἐπʼ αἰθομένοις ἱεροῖσι, 11.774–775). Wine is poured on the fire while the thighs burn, as the old priest Chryses poured it at the sacrifice of Book 1: “The old man burned them on split wood, and over them poured sparkling wine” (1.462–463; see [[sacrifice]] and [[thigh-pieces]]). Here the old man is Peleus, sacrificing in the enclosure of his own courtyard to “Zeus who delights in thunder” when Nestor and Odysseus arrive to take his son to the war (11.769–775; see [[peleus]]). Priam will pour a libation from a golden cup standing in the middle of his courtyard before he sets out for Achilles' hut (24.283–306), and Achilles keeps a cup from which he pours to no god but Zeus (16.225–232; see [[cup]] and [[wine]]).
