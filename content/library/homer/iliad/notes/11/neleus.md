**Neleian.** Νηλήϊος, “of Neleus”, serves as a name for what is his or his son's. Nestor's chariot team are “the Neleian mares” (Νηλήϊαι ἵπποι, 11.597), and the city is “Neleian Pylos” (Πύλον Νηλήϊον, 11.682), as in the Odyssey (*Odyssey* 4.639), where Pylos is also “the well-built city of Neleus” (*Odyssey* 3.4; see [[pylos]]). Nestor is “son of Neleus” twice in the book (Νηληϊάδη 11.511, Νηληϊάδεω 11.618; see [[son-of-neleus]]).

**In Nestor's story.** Neleus is alive and king when his son is young. When the raiders drive the spoil from Elis into Pylos by night, “Neleus was glad at heart / that so much had fallen to me, young as I was when I went to war” (11.683–684). He has lost all his other sons: “we were twelve sons of noble Neleus; / of these I alone was left” (11.692–693; see [[heracles-sacks-pylos]] and [[blameless]]). In the division of the spoil “the old man” (ὁ γέρων) chose a herd of cattle and a great flock of sheep, three hundred with their herdsmen, “for to him too a great debt was owed in shining Elis”: Augeas had kept the four prize-winning horses and the chariot he had sent to race for a tripod (11.696–702; see [[augeas]]). “Angered by these words and deeds”, he took a vast amount, and gave the rest to the people to divide, “so that no one might go away cheated of his fair share” (11.703–705; see [[division-of-spoils]]). When Athena gathered the Pylians by night, “Neleus would not / let me arm, and he hid my horses away; / for he said I knew nothing yet of the works of war” (11.717–719; see [[nestor-and-the-epeians]]). The son fought on foot all the same.

**Fathers.** Neleus guards the one son he has left; in the same speech Peleus and Menoetius send their sons to war with their charges (11.765–790; see [[fathers-and-sons]] and [[peleus-sends-achilles-to-troy]]). The Odyssey names three of his sons by Chloris, Nestor, Chromius and Periclymenus, and a daughter, Pero (*Odyssey* 11.281–287); by the time of the Odyssey he is dead (*Odyssey* 3.409–410).

## Outside Homer

In later accounts Heracles killed Neleus too (Apollodorus, *Library* 2.7.3; see [[heracles-sacks-pylos]]); in the Iliad he outlives the attack and sees his son's first war.
