Iphidamas, son of Antenor, enters “tall and brave” (11.221; [[iphidamas]], [[son-of-antenor]]), with the formula ἠΰς τε μέγας τε that introduces a man of rank about to fight. ἐσθλός, “brave, good,” stands twice in the phrase ἐσθλὸς ἐών, of men whose courage does not help: Menelaus fears that Odysseus, alone among the Trojans, may come to harm, “brave though he is” (11.470–471; [[menelaus]]), and Nestor complains that Achilles, “brave as he is, does not care for the Danaans or pity them” (11.664–665; [[pity]]). In Nestor's story his first victim is Itymoneus, “the brave son of Hypeirochus,” who died defending his cattle (11.672–676; [[itymoneus]]). Hector will use the same phrase over the dying Patroclus: Achilles, brave though he is, did not help him (16.837; [[wretch]]).
