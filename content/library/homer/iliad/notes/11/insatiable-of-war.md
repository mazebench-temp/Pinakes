---
summary: Socus calls Odysseus “insatiable of tricks and toil,” giving him Ares' epithet with other genitives.
grc: ["δόλων ἆτʼ ἠδὲ πόνοιο"]
en: [insatiable of tricks and toil]
---

Socus comes to defend his brother Charops and challenges Odysseus: “Much-praised Odysseus, insatiable of tricks and toil, today either you will boast over both sons of Hippasus, … or struck by my spear you will lose your life” (11.430–433; [[much-praised]]). ἆτʼ is the vocative of ἆτος, the word of Ares' formula, and Socus makes it a taunt at Odysseus' guile and endurance. In the Odyssey Athena greets Odysseus with the same words, δόλων ἆτʼ, when he lies to her on Ithaca (Od. 13.293; [[of-subtle-wiles]]). Socus wounds Odysseus and dies (11.434–449).
