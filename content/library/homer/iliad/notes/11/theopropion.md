---
summary: Nestor guesses that Achilles may be held back by “some oracle”, something from Zeus that his mother has told him, and builds his second plan on the guess: if so, let him send Patroclus out in his armor. In Book 16 Achilles denies that any oracle holds him back.
grc: [θεοπροπίην]
passages:
  - 11.794-803 | Nestor's plan
---

Nestor's advice to Patroclus has two parts. First Patroclus is to persuade Achilles to fight (11.790–793; see [[the-persuasion-of-a-friend]]). Then Nestor allows for a reason he does not know: “But if in his mind he is shunning some oracle, and his lady mother has told him something from Zeus” (εἰ δέ τινα φρεσὶν ᾗσι θεοπροπίην ἀλεείνει, 11.794–795), let him at least send Patroclus out with the Myrmidons and lend him his armor (11.796–803; see [[brief-is-the-breathing-space]]). θεοπροπίη is a pronouncement of the gods' will (see the article above), and Nestor imagines it reaching Achilles as Achilles' knowledge of Zeus' mind usually does, through Thetis, who “often reported to him the mind of great Zeus” (17.408–409; see [[thetis]]).

The guess is half right. Achilles has a prophecy from his mother: he told the envoys that Thetis had spoken of two fates, a short life with imperishable fame at Troy or a long one at home without it (9.410–416; see [[the-two-fates-of-achilles]]). But that is not what keeps him from the battle. When Patroclus repeats Nestor's words to him in Book 16 (16.36–37), Achilles answers that he cares for no oracle he knows of, nor has his mother told him anything from Zeus; what holds him back is the grief of the prize taken from him (16.49–59; see [[achilles]] and [[geras]]). He accepts the second half of Nestor's plan all the same, and sends Patroclus out (16.64–100).

Merops of Percote, whose two sons Diomedes kills in this book, “above all men knew the art of prophecy” (μαντοσύνας, 11.329–330), and could not keep his sons from the war (see [[the-sons-of-merops]] and [[prophecy]]).
