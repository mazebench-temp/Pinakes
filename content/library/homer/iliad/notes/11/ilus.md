summary: Ilus is named twice in Book 11, both times by his grave: the routed Trojans run past “the mound of ancient Ilus, descendant of Dardanus” (11.166), and Paris shoots Diomedes from the pillar on his tomb (11.371–372).
# The phrases give Ilus his title Δαρδανίδαο, which elsewhere is Priam's (5.159; priam excepts 11.166 and 11.372).
grc: ["παλαιοῦ Δαρδανίδαο", "Ἴλου Δαρδανίδαο"]
passages:
  - 11.166-170 | The Trojans flee past the tomb of Ilus
  - 11.369-372 | Paris at the tomb of Ilus
---

In both verses Ilus is old and remote. He is παλαιός, “ancient” (11.166), and at 11.372 παλαιὸς δημογέρων, “an elder of the people long ago”, with the word for the Trojan elders who sit with Priam on the wall (3.149; see [[trojan-elders]]). Both times he is Δαρδανίδης, “descendant of Dardanus”, the title Priam bears (3.303, 7.366), for Ilus is Priam's grandfather, and Dardanus Ilus' great-grandfather (20.215–237; see [[dardanus]] and [[priam]]).

Priam's grandfather lies between the city and the ships, and his grave serves both sides. First the Trojans run past it in rout toward the city; later Paris shoots from it and drives Diomedes from the field (see [[the-tomb-of-ilus]] and [[paris]]).
