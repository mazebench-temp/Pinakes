---
summary: The “bitter pangs” of the Eileithyiai in the simile of Agamemnon's pain, and the “bitter root” with which Patroclus treats Eurypylus.
grc: [πικρὰς, πικρὴν]
---

The Eileithyiai, “the daughters of Hera, who have the bitter pangs in their keeping,” send the sharp dart of labor to which Agamemnon's pain is compared (11.270–271; [[who-brings-hard-birth]], [[the-woman-in-labor]]). At the end of the book Patroclus cuts the arrow from Eurypylus' thigh and lays on the wound “a bitter root,” crushed between his hands, which stays all his pains (11.846–848; [[pain-killing]], [[healing]]). The book has no πικρὸς ὀϊστός, though arrows wound Diomedes, Machaon and Eurypylus ([[arrows]]).
