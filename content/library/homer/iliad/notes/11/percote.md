passages:
  - 11.227-230 | Iphidamas leaves his ships at Percote
---

“These balanced ships he then left at Percote, / but he himself had come on foot to Ilios” (11.229–230). Iphidamas, a son of Antenor brought up in Thrace, crossed with twelve ships when he heard that the Achaeans were coming, and landed at Percote, on the Asian shore of the Hellespont, in the realm of Asius (2.835; see [[iphidamas]], [[thrace]] and [[asius]]). The poem does not say what became of the ships.

A hundred lines later Περκώσιος, “of Percote”, is the title of the seer Merops in verses repeated from the Catalogue (11.329 = 2.831), as Diomedes kills the two sons he had forbidden to go to war (see [[merops]] and [[the-sons-of-merops]]).
