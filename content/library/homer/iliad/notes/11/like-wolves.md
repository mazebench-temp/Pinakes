{{quote:a215431d-7bb6-5c80-af46-502ab15a8407}}

οἳ δὲ λύκοι ὣς / θῦνον: “and they like wolves rushed on” (11.72–73). The short comparison sits at the end of the verse and runs over into the next, as in Book 4, where it is followed by ἀλλήλοις ἐπόρουσαν, “leapt upon one another” (4.471–472). θύνω is to rush or dart (see the translation's note on θύνω and θύω). As in Book 4, there is no prey: both armies are the wolves.

The comparison follows the simile of the reapers, who work toward each other from the two ends of a field (11.67–71; see [[the-reapers]]). The reapers give the steady toll of the killing, the wolves its savagery. Both times Strife is near. In Book 4 she had just gone through the throng, “increasing the groaning of men” (4.440–445); here she alone of the gods is present, and she rejoices to look on (11.73–74; see [[strife]]). The wolves of Book 16 hunt in a pack, the Myrmidons arming like wolves with jaws red from a stag they have killed (16.156–163); the wolves of Books 4 and 11 tear at each other. See [[wolves]].
