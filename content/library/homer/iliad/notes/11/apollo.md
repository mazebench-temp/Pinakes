---
summary: Apollo does not come down to the battle of Book 11, but he is named twice, both times for Hector. Diomedes' spear glances off the helmet that “Phoebus Apollo had given him”, and as Hector escapes, Diomedes shouts after him that “now again Phoebus Apollo has saved you”.
passages:
  - 11.349-367 | Diomedes' spear on Hector's helmet
---

**The helmet.** Diomedes hits Hector on the top of the helmet, “but bronze glanced off bronze, / and did not reach the fair skin, for the helmet held it off, / three-layered, with its socket for the plume, which Phoebus Apollo had given him” (11.351–353; see [[helmet]], [[with-a-socket-for-the-plume]] and [[hector]]). The poem says nothing more of the gift. Apollo gave Pandarus his bow (2.827; see [[pandarus]]); his gift to Hector is armor, and it saves his life. Hector sinks to his knees, “and black night covered his eyes” (11.356), but he comes to himself and escapes in his chariot (11.359–360).

**The taunt.** Diomedes shouts after him: “Once again you have escaped death now, you dog … now again Phoebus Apollo has saved you, / to whom you must pray when you go into the thud of javelins” (11.362–364; see [[diomedes]]). The narrator does not name the god here; he says only that Hector “came to himself” (11.359). Diomedes gives the escape to Apollo, and the poem has given him grounds: Apollo set Hector on his feet after Ajax's stone (7.272) and made Teucer's arrow swerve from him (8.311). Achilles will speak the same three verses when Apollo snatches Hector away from him in thick mist, and that time the narrator says it was the god (20.443–451; 20.449–451 = 11.362–364).
