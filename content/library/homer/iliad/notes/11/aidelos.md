---
summary: Agamemnon's pursuit of the Trojans is compared to a “destroying fire” falling on thick woodland.
---

“And as when destroying fire falls on thick, uncut woodland” (11.155): the whirling wind carries the fire everywhere and the thickets fall uprooted, as the Trojans fall before Agamemnon ([[uncut]], [[forest-fire]]). The same epithet named the fire of the simile in which the gleam of the marching army is compared to a forest ablaze (2.455). See [[agamemnon]] and [[the-aristeia-of-agamemnon]].
