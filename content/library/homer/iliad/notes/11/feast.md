---
summary: Book 11 has no feast of its own. A woodcutter's meal marks the hour of the day when the Achaeans break through (11.86–90), Nestor and Machaon drink in the hut after the battle, and in Nestor's memories the Pylians take their supper by companies on the march, and Achilles sets food and drink before his guests at Peleus' house (11.730, 11.779–780).
grc: ["τάρπημεν ἐδητύος ἠδὲ ποτῆτος"]
en: [taken our fill of food and drink]
---

**The woodcutter's meal.** The fighting goes evenly “as long as it was morning”, but “at the hour when a woodcutter makes ready his meal in the glens of a mountain, when his arms have had their fill of cutting tall trees, and weariness has come into his heart, and longing for sweet food takes hold of his mind, then the Danaans by their prowess broke the battalions” (11.84–91). The comparison is a way of telling the time, not a simile of the action: the δεῖπνον of a man at work, at midday or after, as the army's δεῖπνον before the battle is taken at dawn (8.53; see the translation's note on 11.84–90). The woodcutter's hunger marks a long morning of hard work, and the hour of the Achaeans' breakthrough on the day that began at 11.1 (see [[the-third-day-of-battle]]).

**On the march.** In Nestor's story the Pylians, after the sacrifices at the Alpheus, “took our supper throughout the army, by companies” (δόρπον … κατὰ στρατὸν ἐν τελέεσσι, 11.730), the evening meal of an army in the field; Hector will give the Trojans the same order on the night before Achilles returns (18.298; see [[sacrifice]]).

**At Peleus' house.** Achilles sets “good cheer” before Nestor and Odysseus, “as is the custom with guests”. “But when we had taken our fill of food and drink”, Nestor began to speak (11.779–781; see [[guest-gifts]] and [[visit-scene]]). The line is a variant of the verse that ends every meal, “when they had put away the desire for drink and food” (1.469; see [[desire-for-drink-and-food]]); the Odyssey has it in the third person, of Odysseus and Calypso (Odyssey 5.201).

**In Nestor's hut.** Nestor and Machaon, back from the battle, drink Hecamede's mixture of wine, cheese and barley, “and when the two had drunk and driven off their parching thirst, and were taking pleasure in talk”, Patroclus appears at the door (11.642–644; see [[kykeon]]).
