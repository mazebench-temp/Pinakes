---
summary: Hector is “the equal of Ares, ruin of mortals” as he drives the Trojans on, and Patroclus comes out of the hut “the equal of Ares” to his ruin.
grc: ["ἶσος Ἄρηϊ"]
---

Hector sets the Trojans on the Achaeans as a hunter sets his hounds on a boar: “Hector, son of Priam, the equal of Ares, ruin of mortals” (11.295; [[ruin-of-mortals]], [[son-of-priam]], [[the-hunter-and-his-hounds]]). The verse-end returns when Hector leads the Trojans in Book 13 (13.802). When Achilles calls, Patroclus “came out, the equal of Ares; and this was the beginning of evil for him” (11.604; [[the-death-of-patroclus]]). Here the comparison is made with ἶσος, the word of [[equal-to-the-gods]], instead of the ἀτάλαντος of the standing formula.
