---
summary: Agamemnon is “lord of men” as he leaves his first victims stripped and as he shudders at his wound; in Nestor's story Augeas has the same title.
---

“And Agamemnon, lord of men, left them there with their chests shining bare” (11.99–100), after he has killed Bianor and Oileus ([[heavy-with-bronze]]). When Coon's spear goes through his arm, “Agamemnon, lord of men, shuddered” (11.254), and fought on. In Nestor's story the title belongs to an enemy king: “Augeas, lord of men,” kept the four prize-winning horses that Neleus sent to race in Elis (11.699–702; [[prize-winning]], [[augeas]]).
