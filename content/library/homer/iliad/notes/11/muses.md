---
summary: The poet calls on the Muses once in Book 11, to tell him who was the first to face Agamemnon when the Trojans had rallied; the answer is Iphidamas, son of Antenor.
passages:
  - 11.218-220 | The Muses invoked
---

“Tell me now, Muses, you who have your homes on Olympus, / who was the first to come against Agamemnon, / whether of the Trojans themselves or of their famous allies” (11.218–220). The first verse opened the Catalogue of Ships (2.484), and it returns twice more at turns of the battle (14.508, 16.112; see [[invocation-of-the-muses]] and [[homes-on-olympus]]). Each time after the Catalogue the poet asks for a first: who first faced Agamemnon; which of the Achaeans first took bloody spoils when Poseidon turned the battle (14.509–510); how fire first fell on the ships (16.113). In Book 2 he asked for a whole army, because the Muses “are present, and know all things” while men “hear only the report” (2.485–486; see [[we-hear-only-the-report]]). Here he asks for one man.

The answer follows at once: “Iphidamas, son of Antenor, tall and brave” (11.221; see [[iphidamas]]). The poet tells how he was reared in Thrace by his mother's father, married there, and sailed with twelve ships, which he left at Percote (11.221–231), and then how he died at Agamemnon's hands, far from the bride who gave him no joy (11.232–247).
