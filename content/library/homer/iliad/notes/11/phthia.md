---
summary: Phthia is the place of the day Nestor remembers, when he and Odysseus came to Peleus' house to gather the army and the fathers sent their sons to Troy, Menoetius sending Patroclus “from Phthia to Agamemnon” (11.766).
---

Nestor reminds Patroclus of the charge his father gave him “on that day when he sent you from Phthia to Agamemnon” (11.766). It is the verse with which Odysseus and Phoenix recalled Peleus sending Achilles (9.253 = 9.439; see [[peleus-sends-achilles-to-troy]]); here the father who sends is [[Menoetius]]. Nestor and Odysseus had come to Peleus' house, “gathering the army throughout the bountiful land of Achaea” (11.769–770; see [[peleus]]). Patroclus was not born in Phthia: his ghost tells how Menoetius brought him there as a boy from Opus, and Peleus raised him (23.85–90). In Book 16, when Patroclus comes to him weeping, Achilles asks whether he alone has heard some news from Phthia of their fathers (16.12–16).
