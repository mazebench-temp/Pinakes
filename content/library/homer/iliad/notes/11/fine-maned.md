---
summary: The “fine-maned horses” carry the wounded Agamemnon out of the battle and carry Hector's chariot into it.
---

Agamemnon's charioteer “lashed the fine-maned horses toward the hollow ships,” and the pair flew on not unwilling, their chests flecked with foam (11.280–283; [[hollow-ships]], [[chariot-journey]]). Cebriones lashes Hector's horses with the same words, and they carry the chariot over corpses and shields into the battle (11.531–537; [[shrill]], [[swift]]). The two verses frame the turn of the day: the Achaean king is carried out, and the Trojan driven in. See [[horses]].
