**Three versions of one day.** In Book 9 Odysseus reminded Achilles of what Peleus told him “on that day when he sent you from Phthia to Agamemnon”: Athena and Hera would give him strength, but he must hold back his proud heart, “for kindliness is better”, and leave off strife (9.252–258). Phoenix told of the same day: Peleus sent him with the boy to teach him to be “a speaker of words and a doer of deeds” (9.438–443). Nestor tells it a third time, to Patroclus, and he was there:

{{quote:c8806645-e49f-5fde-9520-9538fa89b6df}}

His version has two fathers and two charges. Peleus told Achilles “always to be the best and to excel above the others” (11.783–784), the charge that Hippolochus gave Glaucus (6.208; see [[always-to-be-the-best]]), and Menoetius told Patroclus, the elder, to advise and guide the stronger man: “speak a shrewd word to him well, and give him counsel, and direct him; and he will obey you, to his good” (11.786–789; see [[menoetius]]). Each speaker remembers the charge that serves him: Odysseus, pleading for an end to anger, the charge of kindliness; Phoenix, pleading as the boy's teacher, the charge to teach; Nestor, who needs Patroclus to persuade Achilles, the charge to counsel. Nestor even closes with Odysseus' verse, “So the old man charged you, but you forget. But even now” (11.790 = 9.259), and the first verse of the day is the same in all three (11.766 = 9.253 = 9.439; see the translation's note on 11.765–790, [[peleus-sends-achilles-to-troy]] and [[mythological-paradigm]]).

**The wounded told again.** Nestor's count of the wounded, “the best men lie among the ships, shot or stabbed”, with the names of Diomedes, Odysseus, Agamemnon and Eurypylus (11.658–662), is repeated by Eurypylus himself (11.825–827) and by Patroclus to Achilles (16.23–27). Nestor names Eurypylus' wound though he left the field before it was given (see the translation's note on 11.662 and [[the-wounded-chiefs]]).

**The plan told again.** Nestor's plan, that Achilles send Patroclus out in his armor (11.794–803), is put to Achilles by Patroclus as his own request (16.36–45), and his words on the persuasion of a friend (11.792–793) are on Patroclus' lips when he leaves Eurypylus' hut in Book 15 (15.403–404; see [[message-repeated]] and [[the-persuasion-of-a-friend]]).
