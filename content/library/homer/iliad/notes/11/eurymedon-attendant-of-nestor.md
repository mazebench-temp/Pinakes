---
summary: When Nestor brings the wounded Machaon back to his hut, his attendant Eurymedon unyokes the horses (11.618–621).
---

“And when they reached the hut of the son of Neleus, / they themselves stepped down onto the bountiful earth, / and Eurymedon, the old man's attendant, unyoked the horses / from the chariot” (11.618–621). The horses are “the Neleian mares” that carried Nestor and Machaon out of the battle, sweating (11.597–598; see [[nestor]] and [[neleus]]). He is θεράπων, “attendant”, the word for the companion who serves a hero, as Patroclus serves Achilles. In Book 8 the same man took charge of Nestor's horses on the field when the old man mounted Diomedes' chariot (8.113–114); here he takes them at the hut. Agamemnon's charioteer, Eurymedon son of Ptolemaeus, is another man (4.228; see [[eurymedon]]).
