---
summary: The “swift hind” of a simile, and the “swift chariot” that Cebriones drives into the battle.
grc: ["ἐλάφοιο ταχείης", "θοὸν ἅρμα"]
---

In the simile of Agamemnon killing Isus and Antiphus, a lion crushes “the helpless young of a swift hind,” and the mother, near as she is, cannot help them (11.113–119; [[the-lion-and-the-fawns]], [[nepios]]). The phrase ἐλάφοιο ταχείης is used of the fawn's mother in the eagle portent of Book 8 as well (8.248). θοός is the speed of Hector's chariot when Cebriones lashes the horses: “they, feeling the stroke, lightly bore the swift chariot among the Trojans and the Achaeans” (11.532–533; [[shrill]], [[fine-maned]]).
