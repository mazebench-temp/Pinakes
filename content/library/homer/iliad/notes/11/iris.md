---
summary: Zeus sends Iris from Ida to Hector with his command and his promise, and she gives them to Hector word for word, adding only an address of her own.
refs: [11.196-197, 11.200-209]
passages:
  - 11.185-210 | Iris brings Zeus' word to Hector
---

**Sent.** On the peaks of Ida, Zeus “urged on golden-winged Iris to carry a message” (11.185), the verse with which he sent her to Hera and Athena in Book 8 (8.398; see [[golden-winged]] and [[zeus]]). His command begins as it does each time he sends her, “Go, swift Iris” (11.186; 8.399, 15.158, 24.144). She goes as “swift Iris, wind-footed” (11.195; see [[wind-footed]]), “down from the mountains of Ida to sacred Ilios” (11.196), as in Book 8 she went from the mountains of Ida to Olympus (8.410).

**The message.** She finds Hector standing among his horses and his chariot and speaks to him “standing near” (11.197–199), in the verse with which she came to the Trojan assembly, to Helen and to Thetis (2.790, 3.129, 24.87). She repeats Zeus' words to Hector as he spoke them (11.202–209 ≈ 11.187–194), turning “he” into “you”, so that his “I will put power in his hands” becomes “he will put power in your hands” (see [[message-repeated]] and [[messenger-scene]]). She adds only an opening of her own: “Hector, son of Priam, the equal of Zeus in counsel, / father Zeus has sent me to tell you this” (11.200–201). Helenus began so when he came to Hector in Book 7 (7.47), and the title “the equal of Zeus in counsel” is otherwise Odysseus' (2.169, 2.407, 2.636, 10.137; see [[equal-of-zeus-in-counsel]] and [[hector]]). In Book 8 she added an insult that Zeus had not given her (8.423–424); here she adds courtesy. “So saying, swift-footed Iris went away” (11.210), the verse that ends her errands also at 8.425, 18.202 and 24.188 (see [[swift-footed]]).

**The rainbow.** The book names the rainbow before the goddess. The serpents on Agamemnon's corselet are “like the rainbows that the son of Cronus / sets in the clouds, a portent for mortal men” (11.27–28; see [[like-rainbows]], [[corselet]] and [[teras]]): ἴρισσιν, the common word that is her name.
