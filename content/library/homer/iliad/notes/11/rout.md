---
summary: Rout and Terror surround the Gorgon on Agamemnon's shield (11.37). In the battle the common word φόβος is the panic flight that falls first on the Trojans and then, by Zeus' doing, on the Achaeans.
---

On Agamemnon's shield “the grim-faced Gorgon was set as a crown, / glaring terribly, and around her were Terror and Rout” (11.36–37; see [[gorgon]], [[terror]] and [[shield]]). On the aegis Rout himself was the crown (5.739; see [[aegis]]); here the Gorgon is, and he stands around her.

The common noun runs through the book. At first “neither side thought of deadly rout” (11.71; the verse returns at 16.771). Then the Trojans flee before Agamemnon: some were “still fleeing in rout through the middle of the plain, like cattle / that a lion has put to flight” (11.172–173; see [[agamemnon]]). When Diomedes has been wounded, “rout had seized them all”, and Odysseus, left alone, says that “the son of Cronus has put the other Danaans to rout” (11.402, 11.406; see [[odysseus]]). Last, “father Zeus, throned on high, roused Ajax to flight” (ἐν φόβον ὦρσε, 11.544; see [[zeus]], [[ajax]] and [[retreat]]). The translation keeps “rout” for the panic of an army and “flight” for one man's retreat (see the translation's table).
