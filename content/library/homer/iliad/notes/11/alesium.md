---
summary: The hill of Alesium is the last point of the Pylians' pursuit of the Epeians, where Athena turned the army back (11.757–758).
grc: [Ἀλησίου]
refs: [11.758]
---

“And the Olenian Rock, and where the hill of Alesium / is named; and from there Athena turned the army back” (καὶ Ἀλησίου ἔνθα κολώνη / κέκληται, 11.757–758). There Nestor killed the last man (11.759; see [[nestor-and-the-epeians]]). The words are oddly ordered. Strabo, who quotes 11.756–758 with μέσφʼ for ὄφρʼ and the spelling Ἀλείσιον, takes them as displaced, “and where the hill of Alesium is called”. He places Alesium on the hill road from Elis to Olympia, in a district then called Alesiaeum around Amphidolis, and adds that some point to a river Aleisius (*Geography* 8.3.10). In the Catalogue Alesium is the last of the points that enclose the land of the Epeians (2.617; see [[elis]], [[buprasium]] and [[olenian-rock]]).
