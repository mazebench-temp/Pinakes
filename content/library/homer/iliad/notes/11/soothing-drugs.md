---
summary: Idomeneus values the healer who can “spread on soothing drugs,” and Eurypylus asks Patroclus to spread them on his wound.
---

Idomeneus sends Nestor to carry the wounded Machaon off: “for a healer is a man worth many others, to cut out arrows and spread on soothing drugs” (11.514–515; [[a-healer-is-worth-many]]). Eurypylus, with no healer free, asks Patroclus to cut out the arrow and wash the wound, “and spread soothing drugs on it, good ones, which they say you have learned from Achilles” (11.829–831; [[most-just]]). The healer of the first verse is Machaon, who spread soothing drugs on Menelaus' wound in Book 4 (4.218; [[plato-asclepiads]]). See [[healing]].
