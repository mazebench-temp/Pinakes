---
summary: While Strife alone attends the battle, the other gods sit at ease in their houses “in the folds of Olympus”; the Muses are invoked as goddesses who have their homes on Olympus, and in Nestor's story Athena comes running from Olympus to rouse the Pylians.
---

“The other gods were not with them, but sat at ease / in their own halls, where for each of them / a fine house had been built in the folds of Olympus” (11.75–77; see [[olympians]] and [[strife]]). The houses are the ones Hephaestus built for each god with his cunning skill (1.606–608; see [[hephaestus]]). κατὰ πτύχας Οὐλύμποιο, “in the folds of Olympus”, is found only here; elsewhere the mountain is πολύπτυχος, “many-folded” (8.411, 20.5; see [[many-folded]]). Zeus is not with them. He sits apart, watching the battle (11.80–83), and later comes down “from heaven” to the peaks of Ida (11.184; see [[zeus]] and [[ida]]).

The Muses are called on as goddesses “who have your homes on Olympus” (11.218, as 2.484; see [[muses]] and [[homes-on-olympus]]). In Nestor's story Athena “came running from Olympus as a messenger” to the Pylians by night (11.715; see [[athena]]), in the verse with which Iris comes to Achilles in Book 18 (18.167).
