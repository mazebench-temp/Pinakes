summary: Agamemnon strips Bianor and Oileus even of their tunics and leaves them “with their chests shining bare” (11.99–100); Nestor and Machaon, back from the battle, let the sweat dry from their tunics in the sea breeze (11.621–622).
grc: [χιτῶνας, χιτώνων]
en: [tunics]
---

**Stripped to the skin.** Agamemnon kills Bianor and his companion Oileus and leaves them “with their chests shining bare, for he had stripped off their tunics” (στήθεσι παμφαίνοντας, ἐπεὶ περίδυσε χιτῶνας, 11.99–100). Elsewhere a stripped man loses his armor; here even the tunic worn beneath the corselet is taken, and the bare skin shines where the bronze shone before (see [[stripping-the-dead]] and [[corselet]]).

**Sweat.** Nestor and Machaon step down at the hut, “and the two let the sweat dry from their tunics, standing in the breeze beside the shore of the sea” (11.621–622; see [[chariot-journey]]). Odysseus and Diomedes washed off their sweat in the sea after their night raid, and then bathed (10.572–576).
