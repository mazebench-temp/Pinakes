---
summary: Peleus burns the fat thigh pieces of an ox to Zeus in the courtyard of his house (11.773).
grc: ["πίονα μηρία"]
en: [fat thigh pieces]
---

“And the old horse-driving Peleus was burning the fat thigh pieces of an ox to Zeus who delights in thunder in the enclosure of the courtyard” (πίονα μηρία καῖε βοὸς Διὶ τερπικεραύνῳ, 11.772–774). πίονα μηρία, “fat thigh pieces”, is the phrase of the god's portion in Chryses' prayer, here without elision (1.40), and Nestor will use it of the sacrifices once offered to Zeus in Argos for a safe return (15.372–374). Nestor and Odysseus, who have come to take Peleus' son to Troy, arrive in the middle of it and are welcomed to the meal (11.776–780; see [[sacrifice]], [[libation]] and [[peleus]]).
