summary: Patroclus is called out of the hut as Achilles' companion and obeys “his dear companion”, and Nestor sends him back with a maxim, “Good is the persuasion of a companion”. In the battle the wounded call to their companions and fall back among them: Odysseus shouts for them, and Eurypylus and Ajax find safety in “the crowd of his companions”.
grc: [ἑταίρου]
passages:

**Achilles' companion.** Seeing the wounded man carried past, Achilles “spoke to his companion Patroclus” (ἑταῖρον ἑὸν Πατροκλῆα, 11.602), and “Patroclus obeyed his dear companion” (φίλῳ … ἑταίρῳ, 11.616 = 9.205): the word runs both ways between them (see [[patroclus]], [[achilles]] and [[philos]]). Nestor gives it a duty. Menoetius told Patroclus that, as the elder, he must counsel and direct Achilles (11.786–789), and Nestor sends him back with the reason it might work: “Good is the persuasion of a companion” (ἑταίρου, 11.793; see [[the-persuasion-of-a-friend]] and [[menoetius]]). A companion can say what envoys and kings could not.

**The crowd of companions.** In the fighting the companions are where a wounded man goes. Odysseus, wounded and surrounded, “gave ground backward, and shouted to his companions” (11.461; see [[retreat]]). Eurypylus, shot by Paris, “drew back into the crowd of his companions, avoiding doom” (11.585), in the verse of Paris' retreat before Menelaus (3.32), and calls the Argives to stand around Ajax; Ajax, falling back step by step, “turned and stood, when he reached the crowd of his companions” (ἔθνος ἑταίρων, 11.595; see [[ajax]] and [[eurypylus]]). When the Danaans break the Trojan line at the hour of the woodcutter's meal, they go forward “calling to their companions along the ranks” (11.91).
