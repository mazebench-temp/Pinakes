---
summary: Agamemnon's sword hangs in a sheath of silver, fitted with golden straps (11.30–31).
grc: [ἀορτήρεσσιν]
en: [golden straps]
---

“And the sheath around it was of silver, fitted with golden straps” (ἀτὰρ περὶ κουλεὸν ἦεν ἀργύρεον χρυσέοισιν ἀορτήρεσσιν ἀρηρός, 11.30–31). The sheath has the long spelling κουλεόν, as when Achilles thrusts his sword back into it (1.220; see [[sword]]). ἀορτῆρες are the straps by which it hangs, from ἀείρω, “lift, hang”: Agamemnon's knife “always hung” (ἄωρτο) beside the great sheath of his sword (3.272; see [[knife]]). The word occurs only here in the Iliad. In the Odyssey the ἀορτήρ is the baldric across Heracles' chest in the underworld, and the twisted cord of the beggar's bag (Od. 11.609–610; 13.438 = 17.198 = 18.109).
