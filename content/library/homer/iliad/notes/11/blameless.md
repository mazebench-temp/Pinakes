---
summary: ἀμύμων, rendered “noble,” marks Polydamas at his first appearance, the charioteers killed in Agamemnon's rout, the Trojan Deiopites, Neleus, and twice the healer.
---

The Trojans are drawn up “around great Hector and noble Polydamas” (11.57), the first time Polydamas is named ([[polydamas]]). The horses of the routed Trojans run loose, “missing their noble charioteers” (11.161; [[high-necked]]). Odysseus' first victim when the Trojans surround him is “noble Deiopites” (11.420; [[deiopites]]). Nestor says that he and his brothers were “twelve sons of noble Neleus” (11.692; [[neleus]]). Twice the word praises a healer: Machaon is “the son of Asclepius, the noble healer” (11.518; [[asclepius]]), and Eurypylus says that Machaon himself is now “in need of a noble healer” (11.835; [[machaon]], [[healing]]).
