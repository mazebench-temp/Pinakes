---
summary: The allies are named twice in Book 11: in the invocation that asks whether the first man to face Agamemnon was one of the Trojans themselves “or of their famous allies” (11.220), and among those who follow Ajax as he gives ground, “gathered from many lands” (11.564).
passages:
  - 11.218-231 | Iphidamas, the first to face Agamemnon
  - 11.563-565 | The Trojans follow Ajax
---

**The invocation.** “Tell me now, Muses … who was the first to come against Agamemnon, / whether of the Trojans themselves or of their famous allies” (11.218–220; see [[invocation-of-the-muses]]). The answer joins the two. Iphidamas is a son of Antenor, but he grew up in Thrace and came to the war with twelve ships of his own, as an ally comes (11.221–230; see [[iphidamas]] and [[thrace]]).

**Gathered from many lands.** As Ajax gives ground, “the high-hearted Trojans and their allies gathered from many lands” follow him, stabbing at his shield (11.564–565; see [[ajax]]). The verse is a formula with one word changed. Elsewhere the allies in it are τηλεκλειτοί, “far-famed” (6.111, 9.233); here they are πολυηγερέες, a word found only here (see [[gathered-from-many-lands]]). The many lands are the subject of the Trojan catalogue (2.816–877; see [[trojan-catalogue]]).
