---
summary: Diomedes, the Paeonian Agastrophus and above all Odysseus are each “famed for his spear.”
---

Diomedes is “famed for his spear” as he kills the sons of Merops (11.333; [[the-sons-of-merops]]), and so is the son of Paeon, Agastrophus, as Diomedes strips him (11.368; [[agastrophus]]). Odysseus has the epithet three times, all in the hours of his wound: when he comes to stand in front of Diomedes while Diomedes draws out Paris' arrow (11.396), when he is left alone (11.401; [[pondering-two-ways]]), and when Nestor counts him among the wounded: “Odysseus, famed for his spear, has been stabbed” (11.661; [[the-wounded-chiefs]]).
