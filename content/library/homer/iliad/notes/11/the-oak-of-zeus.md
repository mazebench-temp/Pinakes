---
summary: The Trojans fleeing from Agamemnon halt at the Scaean gates and the oak and wait for one another (11.170–171).
passages:
  - 11.166-171 | The rout to the Scaean gates
---

Σκαιάς τε πύλας καὶ φηγὸν ἵκοντο, “they came to the Scaean gates and the oak” (11.170): the tree stands with the gates at the edge of the city, where the rout across the plain ends (11.166–171; see [[scaean-gates]], [[the-fig-tree]] and [[the-tomb-of-ilus]]). Hector came in by the same gates and the same oak in Book 6 (6.237), and Achilles named them as the limit beyond which Hector would not come out while he fought (9.352–355).

**Outside Homer.** Strabo, reading this route on the ground, places the oak “a little below the fig tree”, which he describes as a rough place grown with wild figs below the old city, and quotes Achilles' verses about the gates and the oak (13.1.35; see [[strabo]]).
