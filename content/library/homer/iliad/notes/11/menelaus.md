summary: Menelaus is remembered in Book 11 as the envoy whom Antimachus wanted killed at Troy, and he acts once. Hearing Odysseus' three shouts, he brings Telamonian Ajax to the rescue and leads Odysseus out of the throng by the hand (11.138–142, 11.461–488).
refs: [11.140-141, 11.464-473, 11.488]
passages:
  - 11.461-488 | Menelaus and Ajax rescue Odysseus
---

**The envoy.** Menelaus is named twice in the story of Antimachus, the Trojan who took Alexander's gold and “would not let Helen be given to fair-haired Menelaus” (11.124–125; see [[fair-haired]] and [[antimachus]]), and who urged the Trojan assembly that Menelaus, “when he came on an embassy with godlike Odysseus, / be killed on the spot” (11.139–141; see [[the-embassy-to-troy]]). Antenor's memory of that embassy in Book 3 told only of the two envoys' looks and speech (3.205–224). Agamemnon kills Antimachus' sons for it (11.122–147; see [[agamemnon]]).

**Odysseus' cry.** That afternoon the two envoys are together again. Odysseus, wounded and cut off, shouts three times, “as loud as a man's head could hold, / and three times Menelaus, dear to Ares, heard him shouting” (11.462–463; see [[dear-to-ares]] and [[odysseus]]). He does not go alone, but turns to Ajax, who is near:

{{quote:77007192-db54-577e-86c0-d00466402b66}}

He addresses Ajax in the verse with which Hector and Achilles addressed him (11.465 = 7.234 = 9.644; see [[ajax]]). The “longing” (ποθή) he fears the Danaans will feel for Odysseus is the word of Achilles' oath that the Achaeans would one day long for him (11.471; 1.240; see [[pothe]]). Menelaus leads the way, and when Ajax has scattered the Trojans with his tower shield, “warlike Menelaus led him out of the throng, / holding him by the hand, until his attendant drove the horses near” (11.487–488; see [[warlike]]). It is his last act in the book.
