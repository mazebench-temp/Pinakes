summary: Nestor's plan gives the idle Myrmidons a part to play: let Achilles send Patroclus out in Achilles' armor, and “let the rest of the army of the Myrmidons follow with you”, fresh men against men worn out (11.796–803).
refs: [11.796, 11.802-803]
---

The Myrmidons have stayed out of the fighting since Achilles withdrew. In the Catalogue they amuse themselves on the shore with the discus, the javelin and the bow, and wander through the camp “longing for their leader” (2.773–779; see [[achilles]]). In Book 11 Nestor gives them a part in his plan. If Achilles will not fight, let him send Patroclus out, “and let the rest of the army / of the Myrmidons follow with you, in the hope that you may become a light to the Danaans” (11.796–797; see [[patroclus]] and [[nestor]]). Their rest becomes an advantage: “easily would you, who are fresh, drive men worn out by the battle / back to the city” (11.802–803). Patroclus asks for them in the same words (16.38–39), and Achilles arms them and sends them out with him (16.155–220).
