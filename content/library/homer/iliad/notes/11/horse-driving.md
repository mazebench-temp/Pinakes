---
summary: Nestor remembers “the old horse-driving Peleus” sacrificing in his courtyard when he and Odysseus came to gather the army.
---

“And there we found the hero Menoetius within, and you, and beside you Achilles; and the old horse-driving Peleus was burning the fat thigh pieces of an ox to Zeus who delights in thunder” (11.771–773; [[delighting-in-thunder]], [[peleus-sends-achilles-to-troy]]). Nestor used the same title of Peleus in his reproach to the Achaeans before the duel of Book 7 (7.125). Then Peleus charges his son “always to be the best” (11.783–784; [[always-to-be-the-best]]).
