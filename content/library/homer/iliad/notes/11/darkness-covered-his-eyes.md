---
summary: The formula of death is used twice in Book 11 for things short of death: black night covers Hector's eyes when Diomedes' spear strikes his helmet and he faints, as it covered Aeneas' when Diomedes struck him with a stone (11.355–356 ≈ 5.309–310); and grief covers Coon's eyes when he sees his brother fall (11.249–250).
grc: ["κελαινὴ νὺξ ἐκάλυψεν", "ὀφθαλμοὺς ἐκάλυψε"]
en: [veiled his eyes]
---

**Hector's swoon.** Diomedes' spear glances off Hector's helmet, and Hector runs back into the throng, “and he sank to his knees and stayed there, and leaned with his strong hand on the earth; and black night covered his eyes” (11.355–356). The two verses are those of Aeneas' swoon when Diomedes crushed his hip with a stone, and there too a god saved the man: Aphrodite then, Apollo now, by the helmet he gave Hector (5.309–312; 11.352–353, 11.363; see [[helmet]] and [[hector]]). Hector comes to himself and escapes in his chariot (11.359–360), and black night will cover his eyes again when Ajax's stone strikes him in Book 14 (14.438–439).

**Coon's grief.** When Coon sees his brother Iphidamas killed, “strong grief veiled his eyes for his fallen brother” (κρατερόν ῥά ἑ πένθος ὀφθαλμοὺς ἐκάλυψε, 11.249–250). The verb and the eyes of the death formula are given to grief, as later a black cloud of grief covers Hector when he hears of Podes' death and Achilles when he hears of Patroclus' (ἄχεος νεφέλη ἐκάλυψε μέλαινα, 17.591, 18.22; see [[coon]], [[penthos]] and [[achos]]). Coon dies a few verses later (11.260).
