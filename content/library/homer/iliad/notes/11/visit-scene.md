summary: Two visits in Book 11 are told in the same verses of welcome. Patroclus comes to Nestor's hut, and the old man rises, takes him by the hand and bids him sit, but Patroclus refuses: there is no time (11.644–654). And in Nestor's memory he and Odysseus came to Peleus' house, where Achilles sprang up, led them in, seated them and fed them, before they told their errand (11.769–781).

**At Nestor's hut.** Patroclus arrives while Nestor and Machaon, their thirst slaked, are “taking pleasure in talk”, and he stands at the door (11.642–644). The reception follows the pattern: “And seeing him the old man rose from his shining chair, and took him by the hand and led him in, and bade him sit down” (11.645–646; see [[throne]]). The visitor in haste refuses the seat: “There is no time to sit, old sir, nurtured by Zeus, and you will not persuade me” (11.648), and gives his errand at once, with the reason for his hurry, Achilles' temper (11.649–654). The host answers him at length all the same (11.656–803), and the visit ends with Patroclus running back along the ships (11.804–805; see [[messenger-scene]] and [[patroclus]]).

**At Peleus' house.** Inside Nestor's speech is another visit, of the time before the war, when the visitors were Nestor and Odysseus and the young men of the house were Achilles and Patroclus. They came to Peleus' “well-appointed house”, found Menoetius within and the two young men, and Peleus at a sacrifice in his courtyard (11.769–776; see [[sacrifice]] and [[peleus]]). Then: “we two stood in the doorway; and Achilles sprang up in amazement, and took us by the hand and led us in, and bade us sit down, and set good cheer before us, as is the custom with guests” (11.776–779; see [[guest-gifts]]). Only “when we had taken our fill of food and drink” did Nestor begin to say why they had come (11.780–781; see [[feast]]).

**The same verses.** Achilles' welcome is told in the verse with which he sprang up to meet the envoys in Book 9 (11.777 ≈ 9.193), and the verse of the hand and the seat is used both for Nestor's welcome of Patroclus and for Achilles' welcome of Nestor (11.646 = 11.778). The two scenes face each other in the book: in the past, a host who seats his guests and hears their errand after the meal; in the present, a guest who will not sit, because the errand cannot wait (see [[xenia]]).
