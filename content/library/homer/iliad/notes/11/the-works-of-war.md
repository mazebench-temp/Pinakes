The phrase with which Zeus sent Aphrodite away from the battle (5.428) and Hector refused to be treated like a woman (7.235–236) here names what a young man has not yet learned. In Book 2 Nestor told the Achaeans that they talked like “foolish little ones, who care nothing for the works of war” (2.337–338); now he remembers being thought one himself (see the note on Book 7). His father's judgment was a father's protection. Heracles had killed Neleus' other eleven sons, and Nestor was the last (11.690–693; see [[neleus]] and [[fathers-and-sons]]).

The event overturns the judgment: “But even so I stood out among our horsemen, though I was on foot, since so Athena guided the quarrel” (11.720–721). The young man who knew nothing of the works of war kills Mulius, the leader of the horsemen, takes his chariot, and captures fifty more (11.737–749; see [[nestor-and-the-epeians]]). In the same story the battle itself is “the great work of Ares”, which “appeared in front of” the Epeians as they besieged Thryoessa (μέγα ἔργον Ἄρηος, 11.733–734; see [[ares]]). Nestor tells it to Patroclus, a young man who is about to go to war in another man's armor (11.796–801; see [[patroclus]]).
