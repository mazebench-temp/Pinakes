---
summary: The word for the best runs through the book of Agamemnon's aristeia. Hector calls the wounded king “their best man”; the verb ἀριστεύω marks Machaon, Nestor, Mulius and the charge of Peleus; Odysseus binds “whoever would be best” to stand his ground; and by the end of the day “the best men lie among the ships, shot or stabbed”.
grc: [ὤριστος, ἀριστεύῃσι, ἀριστεύεσκεν]
passages:
  - 11.284-290 | Hector: their best man is gone
  - 11.656-664 | Nestor counts the wounded
---

**Their best man.** When Agamemnon drives from the field, Hector shouts to the Trojans: “Their best man is gone, and a great triumph has been given me by Zeus” (οἴχετʼ ἀνὴρ ὤριστος, 11.288–289). ὤριστος is ὁ ἄριστος, “the best”, run together. The title the Achaeans quarreled over in Book 1 is granted to Agamemnon by his enemy, on the day he earns it in the field and is wounded (see [[best-of-the-achaeans]] and [[agamemnon]]).

**To be the best.** The verb ἀριστεύω, “be the best”, is the book's refrain. Machaon is hit “while he was the best in the fighting” (ἀριστεύοντα, 11.506). Hecamede was picked out for Nestor at Tenedos “because he was the best of all in counsel” (βουλῇ ἀριστεύεσκεν ἁπάντων, 11.627; see [[boule]] and [[hecamede]]). Mulius, the first man Nestor killed, was “the leader of the horsemen, who was the best of them in fighting” (11.746; see [[mulius]]), in the half-verse that Hector imagined spoken of himself over Andromache's captivity, ὃς ἀριστεύεσκε μάχεσθαι (6.460). And Peleus charged Achilles “always to be the best and to excel above the others” (11.784; see [[always-to-be-the-best]]). In the middle of the book Odysseus, alone, turns the verb into a duty: “whoever would be best in battle must by all means stand his ground strongly” (ὃς δέ κʼ ἀριστεύῃσι, 11.409–410; see [[the-cowards-leave-the-fighting]]). The book's ancient title, Ἀγαμέμνονος ἀριστεία, “the aristeia of Agamemnon”, is made from the same stem (see [[the-aristeia]] and [[title-of-book-11]]).

**The best laid low.** By the end of the day the word names the wounded. Nestor tells Patroclus that “the best men lie among the ships, shot or stabbed” (οἳ γὰρ ἄριστοι, 11.658–659), and Eurypylus says the same, “all those who were the best before” (11.825–826; see [[the-wounded-chiefs]]). Nestor's story has the same turn in the past: Heracles came to Pylos “in the years before, and all our best were killed”, and Nestor alone was left of Neleus' twelve sons (11.690–693; see [[nestor-and-the-epeians]]).

**Better.** Two judgments of rank in the book use ἀμείνων, the other comparative of ἀγαθός: Zeus was indignant with Hector “whenever he fought a better man” (ἀμείνονι, 11.543; see [[nemesis]]), and Menoetius told his son that Achilles was born higher, “in strength he is far the better” (11.786–787; see [[genee]]).
