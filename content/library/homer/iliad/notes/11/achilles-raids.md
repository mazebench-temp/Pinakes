summary: Book 11 names two more of Achilles' raids. He once caught Isus and Antiphus, sons of Priam, herding sheep on the slopes of Ida, bound them with willow shoots and let them go for ransom (11.104–112), and he sacked Tenedos, from whose spoil the Achaeans picked out Hecamede for Nestor (11.624–627).

**On Ida.** The two sons of Priam whom Agamemnon finds in one chariot had been Achilles' captives before: “These two Achilles once / had bound with pliant willow shoots on the slopes of Ida, / catching them as they herded sheep, and had released them for a ransom” (11.104–106). Agamemnon knows them, for he had seen them by the ships when Achilles brought them from Ida (11.111–112). The raids on Ida return in later books. Aeneas remembers how Achilles drove him from his cattle there and then sacked Lyrnessus and Pedasus (20.89–92, 20.188–191), and Achilles took Lycaon from Priam's orchard by night and sold him on Lemnos (21.34–41). Before Patroclus died, Achilles says, he liked to spare Trojans, and took many alive and sold them (21.100–102). Isus and Antiphus are the first of those spared captives to appear in the poem, and Agamemnon kills both, as a lion crushes a hind's fawns (11.107–121; see [[isus]], [[antiphus-son-of-priam]], [[the-lion-and-the-fawns]] and [[apoina]]).

**Tenedos.** Nestor's woman, Hecamede, was taken in another raid: “whom the old man had won from Tenedos, when Achilles sacked it, / the daughter of great-hearted Arsinous, whom the Achaeans / picked out for him, because he was the best of all in counsel” (11.625–627). Tenedos is the island off the coast of the Troad of which Chryses calls Apollo lord (1.38, 1.452; see [[tenedos]]). The verb ἔξελον, “picked out”, is the one used of Chryseis, whom the Achaeans picked out for Agamemnon from the spoil of Thebe (1.369): Achilles sacks the city, the army shares the spoil, and a chosen woman goes as a prize of honor to a chief who did not take the city (see [[hecamede]], [[division-of-spoils]] and [[geras]]).
