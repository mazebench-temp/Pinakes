---
summary: Agamemnon calls Odysseus “godlike” when he recalls the embassy to Troy, and the narrator gives the epithet to Molion, the attendant of Thymbraeus.
---

Agamemnon refuses to spare the sons of Antimachus, whose father urged the Trojans to kill Menelaus “when he came on an embassy with godlike Odysseus” (11.139–142; [[the-embassy-to-troy]], [[sons-of-antimachus]]). Later Odysseus kills “Molion, the godlike attendant of that lord” (11.322), the attendant of Thymbraeus, whom Diomedes has just killed ([[molion]], [[thymbraeus]]). The epithet is given in the same verse to a man who appears only to die. See [[odysseus]].
