---
summary: Asclepius is named in Book 11 only as Machaon's father, “the son of Asclepius, the noble healer” (11.518), and in Achilles' guess at the wounded man, “the son of Asclepius” (11.614).
---

The verse that brings Machaon onto Nestor's chariot repeats Agamemnon's words in Book 4, with υἱός for υἱόν: Ἀσκληπιοῦ υἱὸς ἀμύμονος ἰητῆρος, “the son of Asclepius, the noble healer” (11.518; 4.194; see [[machaon]] and [[blameless]]). Achilles calls Machaon by the patronymic, τῷ Ἀσκληπιάδῃ (11.614). The father's title comes back at the end of the book without his name: Machaon lies in the huts, “himself in need of a noble healer” (11.835).

Book 11 also gives the poem's other line of healing. Asclepius had his drugs from Chiron, “in friendship” (4.218–219), and Chiron taught Achilles, who taught Patroclus the drugs he lays on Eurypylus' wound (11.830–832; see [[chiron]], [[patroclus]] and [[healing]]). Both lines go back to the Centaur.
