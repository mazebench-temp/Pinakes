---
summary: The dark blood runs from Eurypylus' wound, and Patroclus washes it away.
grc: ["αἷμα μέλαν"]
---

Eurypylus limps out of the battle with the sweat running down, and “from the grievous wound the dark blood gushed” (11.812–813). He asks Patroclus to cut out the arrow “and wash the dark blood from it with warm water” (11.829–830), and Patroclus does so in the same words (11.845–846; [[healing]]). Both of Homer's words for the color are here: μέλαν (11.813) and κελαινόν (11.829, 11.845). See [[eurypylus]] and [[patroclus]].
