---
summary: The Achaeans have built altars to the gods at their place of assembly and justice, by the ships of Odysseus in the middle of the camp (11.806–808).
grc: [βωμοί]
---

“Where their place of assembly and of justice was, where too the altars of the gods had been built for them” (τῇ δὴ καί σφι θεῶν ἐτετεύχατο βωμοί, 11.807–808). The camp has its sacred ground beside its meeting place, by Odysseus' ship in the middle of the line, where Patroclus meets the wounded Eurypylus (see [[assembly]] and [[themis]]). In Nestor's story the sacrifices are made in the open, by the city and on the bank of the Alpheus, and Peleus burns the thighs of an ox in the enclosure of his courtyard, without mention of an altar (11.706–707, 11.727–729, 11.772–775; see [[sacrifice]]).
