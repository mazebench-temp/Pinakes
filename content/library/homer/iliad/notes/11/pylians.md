summary: In Nestor's story the Pylians of his youth are few and weakened by Heracles, yet they divide the spoil of his raid among those the Epeians owed, march at Athena's call to relieve Thryoessa, and rout the Epeians with Zeus' help; at the end they give thanks to Zeus and to Nestor (11.685–761).

“The leading men of the Pylians gathered / and divided the spoil”, for many were owed debts, “since we in Pylos were few and had been ill-used” by Heracles (11.687–691; see [[division-of-spoils]] and [[heracles-sacks-pylos]]). When Athena brought word by night, “the army she gathered throughout Pylos was not unwilling, / but very eager to fight” (11.716–717; see [[athena]]). “We horsemen of the Pylians” waited at the Minyeios for dawn, while “the tribes of foot soldiers streamed in” (ἱππῆες Πυλίων … ἔθνεα πεζῶν, 11.724; see [[minyeios]]); then they sacrificed by the Alpheus, ate by companies and slept in their armor (11.725–731; see [[alpheus]]). They joined battle praying to Zeus and Athena, and “Zeus put great power into the hands of the Pylians” (11.736, 11.753). In the pursuit Nestor calls them “Achaeans” (11.759), and at its end “all gave thanks to Zeus among the gods, and to Nestor among men” (11.761; see [[nestor-and-the-epeians]] and [[nestor]]).
