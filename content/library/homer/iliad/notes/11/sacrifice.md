**Before the battle.** After the spoil of the raid has been divided, the Pylians are “offering sacrifices to the gods” around the city when the Epeians attack (ἕρδομεν ἱρὰ θεοῖς, 11.706–707). On the night march Athena brings them word, and at midday they reach the Alpheus:

{{quote:540e809d-60a7-5915-9c69-b14594545129}}

Each god has a claim. Zeus gives victory: the Pylians go into battle “praying to Zeus and Athena”, Zeus puts great power into their hands, and at the end all give thanks “to Zeus among the gods, and to Nestor among men” (11.736, 11.753, 11.761; see [[zeus]]). Athena has led them and will turn them back (11.714–721, 11.758; see [[athena]]). The river is the place where they camp, and river gods were given bulls: the Trojans, Achilles taunts, have long sacrificed bulls to the Scamander and thrown live horses into its eddies (21.130–132), and Peleus vowed a hecatomb and fifty rams to the Spercheius for his son's return (23.144–148; see [[alpheus]]). Poseidon is the god of Pylos: in the Odyssey Telemachus finds the Pylians on the shore sacrificing black bulls to him (Odyssey 3.4–9), and Neleus was his son by Tyro (Odyssey 11.252–257). He is also the father of the Moliones, whom he hides in mist when Nestor would have killed them (11.750–752; see [[poseidon]] and [[the-moliones]]). The supper and the night in armor follow, before the battle at sunrise (11.730–736; see [[feast]]).

**At Peleus' house.** When Nestor and Odysseus came to Phthia to gather men, they found Peleus at a sacrifice in the courtyard of his house: he “was burning the fat thigh pieces of an ox to Zeus who delights in thunder in the enclosure of the courtyard; and he was holding a golden goblet, pouring sparkling wine over the burning offerings”, while Menoetius, Patroclus and Achilles were busy with the meat (11.772–776; see [[thigh-pieces]], [[libation]] and [[peleus]]). It is a household sacrifice, made by the master of the house at his own altar, as Laertes and Odysseus burned many thighs of oxen at the altar of Zeus of the courtyard in Odysseus' palace (Odyssey 22.334–336). The visitors arrive as the meat is being prepared and are welcomed to the meal that follows (11.777–780; see [[visit-scene]]).
