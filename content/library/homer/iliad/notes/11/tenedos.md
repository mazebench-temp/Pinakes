---
summary: Tenedos is named as the island Achilles sacked, where Nestor won his captive Hecamede, whom the Achaeans chose for him as the best of all in counsel (11.624–627).
---

“Whom the old man had won from Tenedos, when Achilles sacked it” (τὴν ἄρετʼ ἐκ Τενέδοιο γέρων, ὅτε πέρσεν Ἀχιλλεύς, 11.625). It is the only time the Iliad names the sack of Tenedos. Achilles counts twelve cities sacked from the ships and eleven by land without naming them (9.328–329); the poem names a few, among them Lyrnessus, Thebe and Lesbos (see [[achilles-raids]]). The island had been named before only as Apollo's: Chryses prays to the god who stands guard over Chryse and Cilla, “and rule Tenedos with might” (1.38, 1.452). From it came Hecamede, daughter of Arsinous (11.624–627; see [[hecamede]] and [[arsinous]]).
