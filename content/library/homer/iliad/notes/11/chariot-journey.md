summary: Book 11 is a book of chariot journeys out of the battle. Agamemnon and Diomedes mount and order their drivers to the ships in the same two verses, Agamemnon's horses fly “not unwilling”, flecked with foam, and Nestor drives the wounded Machaon to his hut in the verses of Diomedes' ride on the horses of Rhesus, where the horses are unyoked and the men cool off in the sea breeze (11.273–283, 11.399–400, 11.512–520, 11.597–598, 11.618–622).

**Out of the battle.** The journeys of the book go one way, from the battle to the ships, and each carries a wounded man. Agamemnon “sprang up into his chariot and ordered his charioteer to drive to the hollow ships; for his heart was distressed” (11.273–274), and Diomedes leaves in the same two verses (11.399–400). The first half of the second verse took Aeneas' horses to the Achaean ships as spoil (11.274 ≈ 5.327). The drive is told for Agamemnon alone:

{{quote:e136cb61-7574-5b0b-8f5f-30d56e9a4965}}

τὼ δʼ οὐκ ἀέκοντε πετέσθην, “and the pair flew on not unwilling”, is the half-verse of the gods' journeys between Olympus and the earth (5.366, 5.768, 8.45), and the foam and dust are the signs of a hard drive (see [[horses]] and [[chariot]]).

**Nestor and Machaon.** Idomeneus tells Nestor to mount and drive Machaon to the ships with all speed (11.511–513). “At once he mounted his chariot, and beside him Machaon went up”, and the drive is told in two verses from the night before: “and he whipped the horses; and the pair flew on not unwilling toward the hollow ships; for there their hearts were glad to go” (11.517–520; 11.519–520 = 10.530–531), the verses of Diomedes' ride on the horses of Rhesus. The mares are seen again as they pass Achilles' ship, “sweating”, too fast for him to see the wounded man's face (11.597–598, 11.615).

**Arrival.** At the hut the journey ends in its usual steps, without a god's ambrosia or mist: “they themselves stepped down onto the bountiful earth, and Eurymedon, the old man's attendant, unyoked the horses from the chariot; and the two let the sweat dry from their tunics, standing in the breeze beside the shore of the sea” (11.619–622; see [[eurymedon-attendant-of-nestor]]). Odysseus and Diomedes washed off their sweat in the sea at the end of their night's raid (10.572–575); Nestor and Machaon let the wind dry it before they go in to drink (see [[kykeon]]).

**Into the battle.** One drive goes the other way. Cebriones lashes Hector's horses with the shrill whip, and they carry the chariot over corpses and shields into the thick of the fight (11.531–537; see [[cebriones]]).
