---
summary: δῖος honors heroes on both sides, the Achaeans as a people, Dawn, and “shining” Elis in Nestor's story.
grc: ["=δίῃ"]
---

**Heroes.** On the Trojan side, Agenor among the sons of Antenor (11.59) and Hector, whom Iris finds standing by his chariot (11.197) and from whom the Achaeans flee (11.327). On the Achaean side, Agamemnon, as Coon takes his stand “unseen by brilliant Agamemnon” (11.251); Nestor (11.510); Achilles, “brilliant swift-footed Achilles,” watching from his ship (11.599; [[podarkes]]); Odysseus, as he boasts over Socus and in Nestor's memory of the visit to Peleus (11.449, 11.767); and Patroclus, whom Achilles calls “Brilliant son of Menoetius” (11.608; [[son-of-menoetius]], [[dear-to-my-heart]]).

**The Achaeans.** Twice the whole army is δῖοι: in Odysseus' boast, “the brilliant Achaeans will give me burial rites” (11.455), and as they hold their ground by the Scamander until Machaon is wounded (11.504).

**Dawn and Elis.** In Nestor's story the Pylians wait by the Minyeios “for brilliant Dawn” (11.723; [[eos]]), and Elis is δῖα, “shining Elis,” where debts were owed to the Pylians (11.686, 11.698; [[elis]]), as it is in the Catalogue (2.615). Ἠῶ δῖαν belongs here, not to the epithet of the sea ([[shining-sea]]).
