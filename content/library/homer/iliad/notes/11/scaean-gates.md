summary: Agamemnon drives the routed Trojans across the plain to the Scaean gates and the oak, where they halt and wait for one another (11.170–171); he is on the point of reaching the wall when Zeus intervenes.
passages:
  - 11.166-181 | The rout to the Scaean gates
---

“But when they came to the Scaean gates and the oak, / there they halted and waited for one another” (11.170–171). The rout runs past the tomb of Ilus and the wild fig tree, and the gate and the oak mark its end (11.166–171; see [[the-tomb-of-ilus]], [[the-fig-tree]] and [[the-oak-of-zeus]]). The same pair once marked the limit of Hector's daring: as long as Achilles fought, he told Odysseus, Hector “came only as far as the Scaean gates and the oak” (9.352–355; see [[achilles]]). Now Agamemnon drives the Trojans back to that line, and he is “just about to come beneath the city and its steep wall” when Zeus sits down on Ida and sends Iris to Hector (11.181–185; see [[agamemnon]] and [[ida]]).
