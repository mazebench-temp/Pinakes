---
summary: “Godlike Alexander” shoots Eurypylus as he strips Apisaon.
---

“But when godlike Alexander caught sight of him stripping the armor from Apisaon, at once he drew his bow against Eurypylus” (11.581–583; [[apisaon]], [[eurypylus]]). It is the third of Paris' arrows in the book, after those that wound Diomedes and Machaon (11.369–378, 11.505–507; [[three-barbed]]). See [[paris]].
