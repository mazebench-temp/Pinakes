---
summary: Hippolochus, son of Antimachus, and the Achaean Hipponous, killed by Hector, are “steadfast in battle.”
grc: [μενεχάρμην]
---

μενεχάρμης, “abiding the joy of battle,” is a synonym of μενεπτόλεμος with χάρμη in place of πτόλεμος ([[charme]]). It is given twice in the book to men who die: Hippolochus, son of Antimachus, whom Agamemnon kills with his brother Peisander (11.122; [[hippolochus-son-of-antimachus]]), and Hipponous, the last of the nine Achaean leaders Hector kills (11.303; [[hipponous]], [[whom-first-whom-last]]).
