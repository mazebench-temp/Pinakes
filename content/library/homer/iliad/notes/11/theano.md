She is named only to place her son. Her father Cisseus brought up Iphidamas in Thrace, and when he came of age married him to his own daughter, Theano's sister (see [[iphidamas]] and [[cisseus]]). καλλιπάρῃος, “fair-cheeked” (11.224), is her epithet in Book 6 as well (6.298, 6.302; see [[fair-cheeked]]). The priestess who prayed in vain for the city (6.297–311) loses her son in this book. Agamemnon kills Iphidamas and then Coon, “his brother by the same father” (11.257), who dies trying to save the body (see [[coon]]); the poem does not say whether Coon was her son too.
