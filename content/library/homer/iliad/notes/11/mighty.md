---
summary: Zeus means to hurl “many mighty heads” down to Hades; Hector calls on the Trojans to drive at the “mighty Danaans”; Diomedes strips “mighty Agastrophus.”
grc: [ἰφθίμων, ἰφθίμοιο]
---

Zeus sends down dewdrops wet with blood, “because he meant to hurl down to Hades many mighty heads” (11.54–55). The verse recalls the proem, where the wrath “hurled down to Hades many mighty souls” (1.3), with heads in place of souls. Hector, when Agamemnon has left the field, calls on the Trojans to drive their horses “at the mighty Danaans” (11.290; [[single-hoofed]]). Diomedes is taking “the gleaming corselet of mighty Agastrophus” when Paris shoots him (11.373; [[panaiolos]], [[heavy-helmet]]). See [[agastrophus]].
