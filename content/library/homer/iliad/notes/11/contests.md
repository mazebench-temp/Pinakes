summary: The debt that began Nestor's war was a team of four prize-winning horses that Neleus sent to race for a tripod at games in Elis, and that Augeas kept (11.698–702).
grc: ["μετʼ ἄεθλα", θεύσεσθαι]
en: [had gone to the games, to race for a tripod]
---

“For to him too a great debt was owed in shining Elis, four prize-winning horses with their chariot, which had gone to the games; for they were going to race for a tripod; but Augeas, lord of men, kept them there, and sent the driver away, grieving for his horses” (11.698–702). Neleus had sent a team to compete at games held in Elis, the prize a tripod (see [[tripod]]), and the king of the Epeians kept horses and chariot and sent the driver home (see [[augeas]] and [[neleus]]). The theft of the racehorses is one of the debts the Pylians collect when they divide the spoil of Nestor's raid (11.703–705; see [[division-of-spoils]]).

The horses are ἀθλοφόροι, “prize-winning”, the word of the twelve racehorses in Agamemnon's gifts, “which have won prizes with their feet” (9.123–124; see [[prize-winning]]). Games in Elis come back in Nestor's memory in Book 23, the funeral games of Amarynceus at Buprasium, where he won every contest but the chariot race, in which the two sons of Actor beat him by their numbers (23.629–642; see [[amarynceus]] and [[the-moliones]]). They are the twins who, as boys, were among the Epeians besieging Thryoessa (11.709–710, 11.750–752).
