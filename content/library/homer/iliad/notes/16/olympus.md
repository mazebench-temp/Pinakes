---
summary: Olympus is the home from which Achilles fears a god may come against Patroclus, and of the Muses whom the poet calls on; and in a simile a storm cloud comes from Olympus into the sky when Zeus spreads a storm.
---

**The gods' home.** Achilles tells Patroclus not to lead the way on toward Ilios, “lest one of the everlasting gods from Olympus / come in against you; far-working Apollo loves them dearly” (16.93–94; see [[apollo]] and [[olympians]]). Apollo is the god who comes, first on the wall of Troy and then in the battle (16.698–711, 16.788–804). The poet's invocation is addressed to the “Muses, you who have your homes on Olympus” (16.112; see [[muses]] and [[homes-on-olympus]]).

**The mountain of the weather.** When the Trojans flee from the ships, their shouting and rout rise “as when a cloud comes from Olympus into the sky / out of the shining upper air, when Zeus spreads a storm” (16.364–365; see [[the-cloud-from-olympus]], [[sky]] and [[zeus]]). The mountain is the place of the clouds: the Hours who keep the gates of heaven are entrusted with “great heaven and Olympus”, to throw open the thick cloud and to close it (5.749–751 = 8.393–395; see [[hours]]).
