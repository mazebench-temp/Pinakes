---
summary: The Trojans look around “for a way to escape sheer destruction,” Zeus considers whether to increase “the sheer toil” for more men, and Hector asks the dead Patroclus why he prophesies “sheer destruction” for him.
---

“and each man looked around for a way to escape sheer destruction” (16.283), as the Trojans see Patroclus in Achilles' armor; Zeus, over Sarpedon's body, wonders whether Patroclus should die now “or whether he should still increase the sheer toil for more men” (16.651; [[plan-of-zeus]]); and Hector to the dead Patroclus: “Patroclus, why do you prophesy sheer destruction for me?” (16.859; [[death-of-achilles-foretold]], [[hector]]). The adjective of mountains is the book's word for the ruin and the labor of war.
