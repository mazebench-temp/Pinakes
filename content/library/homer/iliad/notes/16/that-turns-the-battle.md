---
summary: Hector, covering himself with his shield, knows “the victory that turns the battle,” and still stands his ground.
---

“Truly he knew the victory that turns the battle; / but even so he stood his ground, and saved his trusty companions” (16.362–363; [[hector]], [[trusty]]). In Books 7 and 8 ἑτεραλκέα νίκην was what a god gives to one side (7.26, 8.171); here Hector reads it in the battle and knows it is not his. See [[plan-of-zeus]].
