---
summary: Patroclus and Achilles speak of Achilles' “lady mother” and what she may have told him from Zeus, and “ox-eyed queen Hera” answers Zeus about Sarpedon.
---

**The mother.** Patroclus guesses that Achilles is held back by an oracle: “and your lady mother has told you something from Zeus” (16.37; [[thetis]]). Achilles answers in the same words, denied: “nor has my lady mother told me anything from Zeus” (16.51). The formula πότνια μήτηρ names Thetis twice in two speeches, once in a guess and once in a denial ([[achilles]], [[patroclus]]).

**Hera.** “Then ox-eyed queen Hera answered him” (16.439; [[ox-eyed]], [[hera]]), with her protest against saving Sarpedon ([[most-dread]]).
