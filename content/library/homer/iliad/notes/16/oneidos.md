summary: The dying Sarpedon warns Glaucus that he will be “a shame and a reproach” to him all his days if the Achaeans strip his body (16.498), and Patroclus tells Meriones that “abusive words” will not make the Trojans give ground from the body (16.628; see [[the-outcome-of-war]]).
grc: [ὄνειδος]
en: [reproach]
---

Sarpedon's warning is the shame of the survivor: a companion who lets his leader's body be stripped carries the reproach for life (16.497–500; see [[stripping-the-dead]] and [[exhortation]]). The pair κατηφείη καὶ ὄνειδος, “a shame and a reproach”, returns in the next book over Patroclus' body: Athena, in the likeness of Phoenix, tells Menelaus that it will be his shame and reproach if dogs drag the companion of Achilles under the wall of Troy, and ends, as Sarpedon did, with “But hold firm, and urge on the whole army” (17.555–559; 16.501).
