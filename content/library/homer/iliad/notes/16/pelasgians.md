---
summary: Achilles calls on the Zeus of Dodona as Πελασγικός, “Pelasgian”, the only time the poem gives the title to a god.
passages:
  - 16.233-235 | Zeus of Dodona
---

“Lord Zeus, of Dodona, Pelasgian, who dwell far away” (16.233; see [[zeus]], [[dodona]] and [[selli]]). Elsewhere in the Iliad the adjective is said only of “Pelasgian Argos”, the first region of Achilles' own kingdom in Thessaly (2.681; see [[pelasgian-argos]] and [[achilles]]), and the Pelasgians are a people among the allies of Troy, from Larisa (2.840–843; 10.429, 17.288; see [[larisa]]). Later Greeks took the title to mean that the god of Dodona was the god of the Pelasgians, whom they counted the oldest people of Greece. [[strabo|Strabo]] cites the verse twice: as proof that Dodona was a Pelasgian foundation, as Ephorus said (*Geography* 7.7.10), and in a list of the places that bore the Pelasgians' name, with Pelasgian Argos and the Pelasgians of Larisa, since “the poet himself calls the Zeus of Dodona Pelasgian” (*Geography* 5.2.4). He also reports a story that the historian Suidas told to please the Thessalians, that the sanctuary had been moved to Dodona from the Pelasgian country around Scotussa in Thessaly, and that Zeus was called Pelasgian from this (*Geography* 7.7.12).
