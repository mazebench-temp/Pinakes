τῆς δὲ τετάρτης ἦρχε γέρων ἱππηλάτα Φοῖνιξ, “The fourth was led by the old horse-driving Phoenix” (16.196): the verse gives him the title with which the narrator introduced his speech to Achilles in Book 9 (9.432; see [[horse-driving]]). Of the five leaders of the Myrmidons he is the only one the poem has shown before, and the only one named without a father (see [[myrmidons]]). In Book 9 he told Achilles that Peleus had made him rich and given him the Dolopians to rule at the far edge of Phthia (9.483–484; see [[dolopians]] and [[the-flight-of-phoenix]]); here he commands a fifth of Achilles' own men. The poem does not say that his company is the Dolopians. Strabo read the Catalogue as joining Phoenix's people to the Myrmidons in a single contingent, and observed that Homer never brings a Dolopian force into the fighting at Troy and does not make Phoenix go out into its dangers, as he does Nestor; Pindar, he adds, made Phoenix lead “a bold throng of Dolopians” (*Geography* 9.5.5; see [[phoenix]]).

So it is in Book 16. The old man who said he could not be parted from Achilles (9.444–445) is named once, in the list, and then stays out of the narrative of the battle; he is not among those who fight over Sarpedon or Patroclus, and in the next book it is Athena, in his likeness, who puts heart into Menelaus (17.553–573).

Walter Leaf, who thought the catalogue of the Myrmidon leaders a later addition, gave Phoenix's place in it as one of his reasons: Phoenix, he held, belongs only to the latest form of the story (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 16.168; see the note on Book 9).
