---
summary: The half-verse marks four falls: Maris, killed by Thrasymedes (16.325), Pronous, the first of Patroclus' victims between the ships and the river (16.401), Bathycles the Myrmidon, killed by Glaucus (16.599), and Patroclus himself, stabbed by Hector: “he fell with a thud, and greatly grieved the army of the Achaeans” (16.822).
---

In Book 16 the second half of the verse is never the clatter of armor. It turns to the darkness (16.325, as 15.578), to the next killing (16.401), or to the grief of the army: of the Achaeans for Bathycles, “and thick grief seized the Achaeans” (16.599; see [[achos]]), and for Patroclus. Patroclus' fall is the only one in the book that grieves a whole army, and he falls without his armor, which Apollo has struck from him (16.793–804).
