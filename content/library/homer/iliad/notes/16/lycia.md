---
summary: Zeus thinks of setting Sarpedon down alive “in the rich land of Lycia”, and instead sends Sleep and Death to carry his body there for burial. Glaucus prays to Apollo as a god who may be in Lycia or in Troy, and tells Hector that Sarpedon “guarded Lycia with his judgments and his strength” (16.542).
---

Λυκίης ἐν πίονι δήμῳ, “in the rich land of Lycia”, is the place of Zeus' wish to save his son, “I should snatch him up and set him down in the rich land of Lycia” (16.437; see [[zeus]] and [[sarpedon]]). Hera turns the wish into a burial: Death and Sleep are to carry him “until they come to the land of wide Lycia, / where his brothers and his kinsmen will give him burial” (16.455–456; see [[hera]] and [[tomb]]), and Zeus repeats the words to Apollo, and the twins “set him down in the rich land of wide Lycia” (16.673, 16.683; see [[sleep]] and [[death]]). Lycia is the land Sarpedon “guarded … with his judgments and his strength” (16.542), where he left his wife and infant son (5.480–481).

Glaucus prays to Apollo, “who are somewhere in the rich land of Lycia / or in Troy” (16.514–515; see [[apollo]] and [[glaucus]]): the god to whom Pandarus prays as “Lycian-born” (4.101, 4.119; see [[lycian-born]]).
