**In address.** Patroclus begins his plea: “Achilles, son of Peleus, by far the strongest of the Achaeans” (16.21). Achilles quotes the Myrmidons' complaint against him: “‘Hard son of Peleus, your mother reared you on anger, / pitiless’” (16.203–204; [[hard]], [[pitiless]], [[cholos]]). The vocative Πηλῆος υἱέ and Πηλέος υἱέ are new to the entry.

**Patroclus' master.** Peisander stood out among the Myrmidons “after the companion of the son of Peleus” (16.195; [[peisander-son-of-maemalus]]); Patroclus calls his men “companions of Achilles, son of Peleus” and bids them fight “so that we may honor the son of Peleus, who is by far the best / of the Argives beside the ships” (16.269–272; [[best-of-the-achaeans]]); the Trojans think “the swift-footed son of Peleus” has come out (16.281). Zeus plans that “the good attendant of Achilles, son of Peleus” drive the Trojans back once more (16.653; [[brave]]), and the narrator says that Patroclus would have escaped his doom “if he had kept the word of the son of Peleus” (16.686; [[black-death]]).
