---
summary: Diomedes lies wounded at the ships. Patroclus names him first among the wounded chiefs, and Achilles measures the Achaeans' weakness by his absence: the spear no longer rages in the hands of Diomedes (16.25, 16.74–75).
refs: [16.75]
---

**Wounded.** “The son of Tydeus, mighty Diomedes, has been shot” (16.25 = 11.660; see [[the-wounded-chiefs]] and [[tydeus]]). Paris shot him through the foot from behind the pillar of Ilus' tomb, and he left the battle (11.369–400; see [[paris]]). He is the first of the wounded whom Patroclus names to Achilles (16.23–27; see [[patroclus]]).

**The spear that rages.** Achilles answers: “For not in the hands of Diomedes, son of Tydeus, / does the spear rage to ward off ruin from the Danaans; / nor have I yet heard the voice of the son of Atreus shouting” (16.74–76; see [[achilles]] and [[agamemnon]]). μαίνεται ἐγχείη, “does the spear rage”, takes up Diomedes' own boast in Book 8, when he took Nestor into his chariot to drive at the Trojans, “so that Hector too / may know whether my spear also rages in my hands” (8.110–111; see [[nestor]]). Achilles, praying for Patroclus a little later, uses the same words: “so that Hector too / may know whether our attendant knows how to fight / even alone, or whether his hands rage irresistible / only when I go into the toil of Ares” (16.242–245; see [[irresistible-hands]]). Achilles names together the two men who have had their days of glory while he stayed away (see [[the-aristeia-of-diomedes]] and [[the-aristeia-of-agamemnon]]): the one's spear is idle, and the other's voice is not heard.
