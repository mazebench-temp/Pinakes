“And Meriones overtook Acamas with his swift feet and stabbed him / on the right shoulder as he was mounting his chariot; / and he fell from the chariot, and a mist was poured over his eyes” (16.342–344; see [[meriones]] and [[achlys]]). He is one of the Trojans whom the Achaean leaders kill one by one as the battle scatters, at the start of Patroclus' counterattack: “These leaders of the Danaans each killed his man” (16.351). In Book 14 Acamas, son of Antenor, killed Promachus over his brother's body and boasted over him, and when Peneleos rushed at him, he did not wait for him (14.476–489; see [[promachus]] and [[peneleos]]). Here Peneleos kills Lycon in the fight just before, and it is Meriones, with his swift feet, who catches an Acamas as he turns to his chariot (16.335–344). The Thracian Acamas was killed by Ajax at the start of Book 6 (6.7–11; see [[acamas-of-thrace]]).
