---
summary: Automedon cuts the dead trace horse loose with “the long-edged sword,” and in the simile of the winds the trees dash together “their long-pointed branches”; the book adds τανυήκεας.
grc: [τανυήκεας]
en: [long-pointed]
---

**The sword.** When Sarpedon's spear kills Pedasus and the reins tangle, Automedon “drew the long-edged sword from beside his stout thigh, / and darted in and cut the trace horse loose, and did not fail” (16.473–474; [[automedon]], [[famed-for-his-spear]], [[pedasus-the-horse]]), with the τανύηκες ἄορ of Book 14 (14.385).

**The branches.** In the forest that the East and South Winds shake, oak, ash and cornel “dash their long-pointed branches against one another / with a wondrous din” (16.768–769; [[smooth-barked]], [[wondrous]]). The adjective of the long blade is given to the branches; τανυήκεας is new to the entry, and the translation renders it “long-pointed.”
