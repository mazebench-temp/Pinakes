# Ζεφύρῳ is always the West Wind in the Iliad: it also catches 23.195, when Achilles prays to the North Wind and the West Wind.

When Automedon yokes the horses for Patroclus, the poem gives their descent: “Xanthus and Balius, who flew as fast as the winds, / whom the Harpy Podarge bore to the West Wind / as she grazed in a meadow beside the stream of Oceanus” (16.149–151; see [[the-horses-of-achilles]], [[podarge]], [[harpies]] and [[oceanus]]). The horses have their father's speed: they “flew as fast as the winds” (ἅμα πνοιῇσι πετέσθην), and in Book 19 Xanthus will tell Achilles that the two of them could run with the blast of the West Wind, the swiftest of all, they say (19.415–416). The North Wind sired horses too. In the likeness of a dark-maned stallion he lay with the mares of Erichthonius as they grazed, and they bore twelve foals that ran over the tips of the standing grain without breaking it and over the breakers of the sea (20.221–229; see [[boreas]]). Podarge, too, is grazing, βοσκομένη, like the mares (βοσκομενάων, 20.223).
