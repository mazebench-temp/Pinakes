summary: Patroclus puts Nestor's plan to Achilles almost word for word, with himself in place of “you” (16.36–45 ~ 11.794–803); Achilles retells the quarrel of Book 1 and quotes the complaints of his own men against him; and Hector, over the dying Patroclus, invents the orders Achilles gave him, the opposite of the orders he did give.
passages:
  - 16.36-45 | Patroclus repeats Nestor's plan
  - 16.200-209 | Achilles quotes the Myrmidons
  - 16.837-842 | Hector imagines Achilles' orders
---

**Nestor's plan.** Nestor had told Patroclus what to ask: if Achilles is shunning an oracle, “let him send you out at least”, with the Myrmidons and his armor, “in the hope that, taking you for him, they may hold off from the war” (11.794–803). Patroclus asks it in the same verses, changed only in person, “then send me out quickly at least … And give me your armor”, and keeps the end, “and brief is the breathing space in war” (16.36–45; see [[brief-is-the-breathing-space]] and [[theopropion]]). He does not say that the plan is Nestor's. Achilles answers the first condition before the request: he heeds no oracle, and his mother has told him nothing from Zeus (16.50–51).

**The quarrel.** Achilles tells the wrong again from his side, in the words he used to the embassy: Agamemnon took back the girl whom the Achaeans had picked out for him, “as if I were some dishonored vagrant” (16.52–59; 16.59 = 9.648; see [[time]] and [[geras]]).

**The Myrmidons.** Before he sends them out, Achilles quotes what each of them said to him all through his wrath: “Hard son of Peleus, your mother reared you on anger, / pitiless, who keep your companions by the ships against their will” (16.203–206; see [[cholos]] and [[exhortation]]). The Greek gives the complaint without marks of quotation, and the English marks it (see the translation's note on 16.203–206).

**Hector's invention.** Over the dying Patroclus Hector imagines what Achilles told him as he went: “Do not come back to me, Patroclus, urger of horses, / to the hollow ships, before you have torn man-slaying Hector's / bloody tunic around his chest” (16.839–841; see [[tunic]]). Achilles' real order was to come back once the ships were safe and not to fight on toward the city without him (16.87–96).
