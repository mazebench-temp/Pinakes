---
summary: Thetis stands behind Achilles in every speech about him: the oracle she might have told him (16.36–37, 16.50–51), the mother Patroclus denies him in anger (16.34), the mother who “reared you on anger” in his men's complaint (16.203), and the mother who packed his chest (16.222–224). Two Myrmidon leaders are sons of mortal mothers by gods, Polydora's by the river Spercheius and Polymele's by Hermes (16.173–192).
---

Achilles' first image for Patroclus' tears is of a mother and a girl (16.7–10; see [[childhood]]), and his first question repeats the verse Thetis spoke to him, “Speak out; do not hide it in your mind” (16.19 = 1.363; see [[speak-out]]). Thetis also receives a suppliant: Epeigeus came to Peleus “and to silver-footed Thetis” (16.574; see [[supplication]]). The book ends with Hector's taunt, naming Achilles “son of Thetis of the lovely hair” (16.860).
