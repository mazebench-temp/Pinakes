summary: Both of the Iliad's words for the vulture are used in Book 16. Sarpedon and Patroclus rush at each other like two vultures, αἰγυπιοί, fighting with loud screams on a high rock (16.428–430), and Hector tells the dying Patroclus that the vultures, γῦπες, will eat him there (16.836).
grc: [αἰγυπιοὶ]
---

**The fighters.** οἳ δʼ ὥς τʼ αἰγυπιοὶ γαμψώνυχες ἀγκυλοχεῖλαι / πέτρῃ ἐφʼ ὑψηλῇ μεγάλα κλάζοντε μάχωνται, “And as two vultures with crooked talons and curved beaks / fight with loud screams on a high rock” (16.428–429; see [[like-vultures]] and [[crooked-talons-and-curved-beaks]]). The αἰγυπιός is the bird of the similes, which fights and hunts; this is the one simile in which two of them fight each other, as two champions meet.

**The eaters of the dead.** σὲ δέ τʼ ἐνθάδε γῦπες ἔδονται, “but here the vultures will eat you” (16.836). The γύψ is the bird of threats and curses, which eats the unburied dead (4.237, 11.162). Hector's boast ends the speech in which he tells Patroclus that Achilles' commands did him no good (16.830–842; see [[hector]]). The threat is not carried out: Patroclus' body is fought over through Book 17 and brought back to the ships (see [[patroclus]]).
