---
summary: Achilles blames the Argives' ruin on their own transgression (16.18); Patroclus' rally blames Agamemnon's blind folly (16.274); the narrator blames Patroclus' folly and, in the same breath, the mind of Zeus (16.685–691); and Patroclus, dying, divides his death among fate, Apollo, Euphorbus and Hector, “the third to slay me” (16.849–850).
---

Patroclus' account is a ranking. Zeus and Apollo “brought me down / easily; for they themselves took the armor from my shoulders”; twenty such men as Hector would have died at his hands; “But deadly fate and the son of Leto have killed me, / and of men Euphorbus; and you are the third to slay me” (16.844–850; see [[moira]], [[stripping-the-dead]] and the translation's note on 16.849–850). The man who will boast of the kill is given the smallest share of it.
