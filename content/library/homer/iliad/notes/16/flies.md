---
summary: The flies of Book 16 are the fighters over Sarpedon's body, who throng around it as flies buzz in a steading around the buckets brimming with milk in spring (16.641–644).
grc: [μυῖαι]
---

ὡς ὅτε μυῖαι / σταθμῷ ἔνι βρομέωσι περιγλαγέας κατὰ πέλλας, “as when flies / buzz in a steading around the buckets brimming with milk” (16.641–642; see [[flies-around-the-milk-pails]]). The swarm of Book 2 was a number, the Achaean army as many as the flies at milking time (2.469–473); here it is a throng drawn to one thing, and the thing is a dead man, so covered with missiles, blood and dust that no one could know him (16.638–640). The fly is the eater of the dead in Book 19, when Achilles fears for the body of Patroclus and Thetis promises to ward off “the savage tribes, / the flies, which feed on men slain in battle” (19.30–31).
