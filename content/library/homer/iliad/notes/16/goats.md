ὡς δὲ λύκοι ἄρνεσσιν ἐπέχραον ἢ ἐρίφοισι, “And as wolves fall upon lambs or kids” (16.352; see [[like-wolves]] and [[lambs]]). ἔριφος is the kid, the young goat. Lambs and kids graze together in Homer's flocks, as in the Cyclops' pens, where the lambs and kids are kept apart by age (*Odyssey* 9.219–222), and they are named together again when Priam reviles his surviving sons as robbers of lambs and kids from their own people (ἀρνῶν ἠδʼ ἐρίφων ἐπιδήμιοι ἁρπακτῆρες, 24.262).
