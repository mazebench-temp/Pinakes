summary: The dying Sarpedon calls on “Glaucus, my friend,” and Patroclus rebukes Meriones, “My friend, the Trojans will not, for abusive words, / give ground.”
---

Sarpedon's last words begin: “Glaucus, my friend, warrior among men, now you must indeed / be a spearman and a bold warrior” (16.492–493; [[glaucus]], [[spearmen]]). Patroclus to Meriones: “My friend, the Trojans will not, for abusive words, / give ground from the body; first the earth will hold many a man” (16.628–629; [[meriones]]). The two uses of πέπον stand on the two sides of the fight for the body: a dying friend's charge and a friend's rebuke. See [[sarpedon]] and [[patroclus]].
