---
summary: The Trojans see Patroclus and Automedon “gleaming in their armor,” and Sarpedon's armor, “bronze, gleaming,” is taken to the ships; the book adds μαρμαίροντας.
grc: [μαρμαίροντας]
---

“And when the Trojans saw the valiant son of Menoetius, / himself and his attendant, gleaming in their armor, / the hearts of all were stirred” (16.278–280; [[son-of-menoetius]], [[automedon]]): the men in Achilles' armor gleam as he would. And after Sarpedon's death “they took the armor from the shoulders of Sarpedon, / bronze, gleaming, and this, to the hollow ships, / the valiant son of Menoetius gave to his companions to carry” (16.663–665; [[hollow-ships]], [[stripping-the-dead]]). The accusative plural μαρμαίροντας, of the men, is new to the entry. See [[sarpedon]].
