---
summary: Achilles' corselet, which Patroclus puts on, is “starry.”
---

“the inlaid corselet, starry, of the swift-footed grandson of Aeacus” (16.134; [[inlaid]], [[corselet]]). The epithet of the starry sky is given to armor that shines like it, as Hector in his bronze was likened to a star in Book 11 (11.62–66) and Achilles' spear point will shine like the evening star (22.317–318). It describes the borrowed armor that makes the Trojans think Achilles has come out ([[swift-footed]]). See [[arming-scene]].
