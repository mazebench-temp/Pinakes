summary: The Myrmidons pour out from the ships behind Patroclus “like wasps by the wayside”, which boys tease and which fly out at any passerby to defend their young (16.259–267). It is the second and last time Homer names the wasp.
grc: [σφήκεσσιν]
---

αὐτίκα δὲ σφήκεσσιν ἐοικότες ἐξεχέοντο / εἰνοδίοις, “At once they poured out like wasps / by the wayside” (16.259–260; see [[wasps-at-the-roadside]]). Homer's two wasps are both in similes, and in both the nest is by a road and the wasps fight for their young: in Book 12 they hold their hollow house against the hunters, as the two Lapiths hold the gate (12.167–172); here, made a common evil by the boys who tease them, they fly out at any passerby who stirs them, as the Myrmidons fall on the Trojans. The wasps of Book 12 have “nimble waists” (μέσον αἰόλοι); these are given no epithet, only their nest by the road and their valiant heart.
