---
summary: Achilles sends Patroclus to fall on the Trojans “so that with blazing fire / they do not burn the ships,” and Patroclus puts out “the blazing fire”; the book adds αἰθόμενον πῦρ.
grc: ["αἰθόμενον πῦρ"]
---

“But even so, Patroclus, to ward off ruin from the ships, / fall on them in force, so that with blazing fire / they do not burn the ships and take away our dear homecoming” (16.80–82; [[nostos]]). The fire that Achilles fears is lit by verse 16.122 ([[tireless]]), and Patroclus' first deed is to put it out: “He drove them from the ships, and put out the blazing fire. / And the half-burned ship was left there” (16.293–294; [[protesilaus]]). The accusative αἰθόμενον πῦρ is new to the entry. See [[ships]].
