---
summary: Hermes, who lay with Polymele in secret, is “Hermes the helper” as he gives her a splendid son, Eudorus.
---

“At once he went up to her upper room and lay with her in secret, / Hermes the helper, and he gave her a splendid son, / Eudorus, surpassingly swift at running, and a fighter” (16.184–186; [[polymele]], [[eudorus]], [[splendid]]). The name and the epithet stand together at the head of the verse after the deed is told, as in the Odyssey, where Hermes leads the souls of the suitors (*Odyssey* 24.10). Three verses earlier the same god is “the mighty slayer of Argus” (16.181; [[argeiphontes]]). See [[hermes]].
