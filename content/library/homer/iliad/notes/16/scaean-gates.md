summary: Driven back to the city, Hector halts his horses at the Scaean gates, unsure whether to fight on or call the army inside the wall, and there Apollo, in the likeness of his uncle Asius, sends him against Patroclus (16.712–725).
refs: [16.713-714]
---

“And Hector held his single-hoofed horses at the Scaean gates; / for he was in doubt whether to drive back into the turmoil and fight, / or to call to the army to crowd within the wall” (16.712–714; see [[hector]] and [[pondering-two-ways]]). Achilles told the embassy that while he fought, Hector would come no farther from the walls than the Scaean gates and the oak (9.352–354), and in Book 22 Hector will wait for Achilles before them and, dying, foretell that Achilles too will die at the Scaean gates (22.5–6, 22.359–360). Here the god who will be with Paris at Achilles' death sends Hector out from them to kill Patroclus (16.715–725; see [[apollo]] and [[asius-son-of-dymas]]).
