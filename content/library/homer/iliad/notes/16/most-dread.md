---
summary: Hera answers Zeus' wish to save Sarpedon with her verse of protest, “Most dread son of Cronus, what a thing you have said!”
---

“Then ox-eyed queen Hera answered him: / ‘Most dread son of Cronus, what a thing you have said! / A man who is mortal, long since ordained to his portion, / do you wish to set free again from grim-sounding death?’” (16.439–442; [[hera]], [[son-of-cronus]], [[grim-sounding]], [[what-a-word]]). It is the fifth of the six times she opens a speech with the verse. As in Book 4, where she would not let Troy be spared, she ends with the warning “Do it; but not all of us other gods approve” (16.443, as 4.29; [[not-all-the-gods-approve]]). Here the protest prevails: Zeus lets Sarpedon die and honors him with a rain of blood (16.458–461; [[bloody]]). See [[zeus]] and [[sarpedon]].
