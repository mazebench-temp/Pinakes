---
summary: Patroclus is “my companion” in Achilles' prayer (16.240), and the book turns on companions: the Myrmidons, “companions of Achilles”, whom Achilles kept idle against their will; Sarpedon's companions, the Lycians, and Glaucus, whom he calls by name as he dies; and Epeigeus, “his fallen companion”, for whom Patroclus' heart is angered.
---

Achilles sends his ἕταρος with the Myrmidons and asks Zeus to bring him back “with all his armor and his companions who fight hand to hand” (16.240–248; see [[prayer]]). The Myrmidons' complaint was that he kept his “companions by the ships against their will” (16.204), and Patroclus rallies them as “companions of Achilles, son of Peleus” (16.269). On the other side Sarpedon sees his companions dying at Patroclus' hands (16.419) and, dying, calls Glaucus “his dear companion” (16.491), and Glaucus prays to fight “to my Lycian companions” (16.524–525). Patroclus' grief for Epeigeus drives his charge: “And grief came upon Patroclus for his fallen companion” (16.581), “and your heart was angered for your companion” (16.585; see [[cholos]]). At the end, struck by Apollo and Euphorbus, he draws back “into the crowd of his companions” (16.817; see [[ker-doom]]).
