---
summary: Twice Patroclus rushes on “for the fourth time … like a god”: at the wall of Troy, where Apollo shouts him back, and in his last charge, where Apollo meets him.
---

“But when for the fourth time he rushed on like a god, / he shouted at him terribly and addressed him with winged words” (16.705–706): at the wall Apollo drives Patroclus back three times and warns him off at the fourth (16.702–711; [[three-times-and-a-fourth]], [[apollo]]). The verse returns at his last charge: “But when for the fourth time he rushed on like a god, / then, Patroclus, the end of your life appeared” (16.786–787; [[apostrophe]]). It is Diomedes' verse when he attacked Apollo over Aeneas (5.438), and it will be Achilles' when Apollo snatches Hector from him (20.447). The man who is like a god is the man whom a god stops ([[equal-of-ares]]). See [[patroclus]].
