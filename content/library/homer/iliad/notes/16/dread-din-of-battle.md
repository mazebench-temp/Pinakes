---
summary: Achilles stands in front of his hut to watch “the dread din of battle,” and Apollo goes down from Ida “into the dread din of battle” to lift Sarpedon's body.
---

“and came and stood in front of the hut; for he still wished in his heart / to watch the dread din of battle between the Trojans and the Achaeans” (16.255–256; [[watching-the-war]], [[achilles]]). The verse-end is the one in which Hera asked Zeus to send Athena to “go into the dread din of battle between the Trojans and the Achaeans” in Book 4 (4.64–65). Later, “he went down from the mountains of Ida into the dread din of battle” (16.677; [[apollo]], [[ida]]), on Zeus' errand for Sarpedon's body. Achilles watches it and the god enters it. See [[sarpedon]].
