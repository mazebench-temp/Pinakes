summary: Agamemnon lies wounded at the ships and does not appear, but the book is full of him. Patroclus names him among the wounded; Achilles recalls how “powerful Agamemnon” took the girl “as if I were some dishonored vagrant”, says that the Trojans would flee if Agamemnon were kind to him, and asks for the girl back with gifts; and Patroclus sends the Myrmidons out so that Agamemnon “may learn his own blind folly” (16.26, 16.52–59, 16.72–77, 16.84–86, 16.273–274).
refs: [16.53-54]
---

**Wounded.** “and Odysseus famed for his spear has been stabbed, and Agamemnon” (16.26). Coon, the eldest son of Antenor, stabbed him through the forearm, and he drove back to the ships (11.248–283; see [[the-wounded-chiefs]] and [[coon]]).

**The quarrel remembered.** Achilles' answer to Patroclus begins with the wrong done to him in Book 1, “whenever a man wishes to rob his equal / and take back his prize, because he is greater in power” (16.53–54; see [[achilles]], [[geras]] and [[quarrel-of-achilles-and-agamemnon]]). The man is not named until the girl is: “powerful Agamemnon took her back out of my hands, / the son of Atreus, as if I were some dishonored vagrant” (16.58–59; see [[powerful]], [[briseis]] and [[like-a-dishonored-vagrant]]). The second verse is the one with which Achilles answered Ajax in the embassy (9.648). Then: the Trojans would soon fill the gullies with their dead, “if powerful Agamemnon / were kind to me” (16.72–73), and the voice he does not hear is “the voice of the son of Atreus shouting / from his hated head” (16.76–77). ἐχθρῆς ἐκ κεφαλῆς, “from his hated head”, occurs only here.

**The gifts.** Patroclus is to win Achilles great honor, “so that you may win me great honor and glory / from all the Danaans, and they send back to me the beautiful girl / and give splendid gifts besides” (16.84–86; see [[time]] and [[kydos]]). Agamemnon offered exactly this in Book 9, the girl returned under oath and seven tripods, twenty cauldrons and the rest (9.121–157, 9.260–299), and Achilles refused it: “Not if he gave me ten times and twenty times as much” (9.379; see [[the-gifts-of-agamemnon]] and [[the-embassy-to-achilles]]). When the gifts are offered again in Book 19, after Patroclus' death, Achilles tells Agamemnon to give them or keep them as he wishes, and to think of battle (19.146–148).

**His blind folly.** Patroclus, leading the Myrmidons out, gives them Achilles' words to Thetis in Book 1: they are to fight “so that we may honor the son of Peleus, who is by far the best / of the Argives beside the ships, he and his attendants who fight hand to hand, / and so that the son of Atreus, wide-ruling Agamemnon, may learn / his own blind folly, in that he paid no honor to the best of the Achaeans” (16.271–274; 16.273–274 = 1.411–412; see [[patroclus]], [[ate]] and [[best-of-the-achaeans]]). In Book 1 Achilles asked for the Trojans to be helped so that Agamemnon might learn it; here the Myrmidons are to teach it by saving the Achaeans. Agamemnon had already confessed it in Book 9: “I was blinded; I do not deny it myself” (9.116).
