---
summary: Ajax's “shining helmet” rings with blows, Patroclus' “shining spear” strikes Pyraechmes, Pronous and Thestor, Sarpedon's misses twice, Zeus never turns away “his shining eyes,” and Apollo thrusts at Patroclus' “shining shield.”
---

**Ajax.** “and around his temples his shining helmet, / as it was struck, rang terribly” (16.104–105; [[ajax]], [[well-made]]).

**The spears.** Patroclus throws first, “his shining spear” (16.284; [[pyraechmes]]); with it he strikes Pronous (16.399; [[pronous]]) and drags Thestor from his chariot “gaping from the chariot on his shining spear” (16.409; [[thestor-son-of-enops]]). Sarpedon twice misses with his: “And Sarpedon missed the man himself with his shining spear” (16.466) and “There again Sarpedon missed with his shining spear” (16.477; [[sarpedon]]). The verse-end δουρὶ φαεινῷ is the book's spear formula, and it belongs to Patroclus' success and Sarpedon's failure.

**Zeus' eyes.** Over Sarpedon's body “nor did Zeus ever / turn his shining eyes away from the fierce battle” (16.644–645; [[krateros]], [[zeus]]).

**The shield.** At the wall “three times Apollo drove him back, / thrusting at the shining shield with his immortal hands” (16.703–704; [[immortals]], [[apollo]], [[three-times-and-a-fourth]]), as he battered back Diomedes' shining shield in Book 5 (5.437).
