---
summary: Book 16 sets the line between gods and men at its sharpest. Zeus cannot save his mortal son without unsettling the order of the gods; Apollo stops Patroclus at the wall, telling him what is not his portion; and a god strips Patroclus before a man kills him. Meriones reminds Aeneas, “for you too are mortal” (16.622).
---

**A god's son.** Sarpedon is “dearest of men” to Zeus (16.433), and Zeus' question is whether a god may take a mortal out of his death. Hera's answer gives the rule: a mortal “long since ordained to his portion” cannot be set free from death without every god wishing the same for his own son (16.441–449; see [[aisa]] and [[not-all-the-gods-approve]]). Zeus yields, and honors his son in the ways open to a god: a rain of blood, a fight stretched out over the body, and a burial carried out by gods (16.459–461, 16.567–568, 16.666–683; see [[bereaved-parents]]).

**The limit.** Patroclus is “like a god” at each fourth attack (16.705, 16.786; see [[daimon]]), and each time a god stops him. At the wall Apollo tells him that Troy is not his to take (16.707–709; see [[three-times-and-a-fourth]]); on the plain Apollo comes unseen and strikes him (16.788–804; see [[divine-intervention]]). The dying Patroclus counts the gods first among his killers (16.844–850).
