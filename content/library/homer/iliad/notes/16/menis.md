---
summary: Achilles speaks of his wrath with its other noun, μηνιθμός: he said he would not end it until the war reached his own ships (16.61–63), the Myrmidons blamed him all through it (16.202), and the Trojans think he has thrown it away (16.282). The noun μῆνις itself is Apollo's, whose wrath Patroclus avoids at the wall (16.711).
grc: [μηνιθμόν]
en: [wrath]
---

μηνιθμός is found only in Book 16, three times, always of Achilles. He grants that he cannot be angry without end, “though I did say / that I would not end my wrath until / the war cry and the war reached my own ships” (16.61–63; see [[cholos]]). He does not wait for it: he grants Patroclus' request while the fighting is still at the ship of Protesilaus (16.64–100; 15.704–706). The Myrmidons' complaints were made “all through my wrath” (16.202; see [[retelling]]). And when the Trojans see Patroclus in the armor, they think “that beside the ships the swift-footed son of Peleus / had thrown away his wrath and chosen friendship” (16.281–282; see [[philotes]]): the wrath is still there, and the friend has come out in its place. Patroclus at the wall draws back “avoiding the wrath of Apollo who shoots from afar” (16.711), the verse that ended Diomedes' attack (5.444; see [[three-times-and-a-fourth]]).
