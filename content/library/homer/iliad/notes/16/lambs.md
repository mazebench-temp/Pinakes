---
summary: The lambs of Book 16 are the prey of wolves: the Danaans fall on the Trojans as wolves fall on lambs or kids that have strayed from the flocks in the mountains through the shepherd's thoughtlessness, and tear them apart (16.352–356).
grc: [ἄρνεσσιν]
---

ὡς δὲ λύκοι ἄρνεσσιν ἐπέχραον ἢ ἐρίφοισι / σίνται ὑπʼ ἐκ μήλων αἱρεύμενοι, “And as wolves fall upon lambs or kids, / ravaging, snatching them out of the flocks” (16.352–353; see [[like-wolves]] and [[wolves]]). The lambs and kids have been scattered in the mountains by the shepherd's carelessness (ποιμένος ἀφραδίῃσι), and the wolves see them and tear them apart at once, “since their hearts have no courage” (ἀνάλκιδα θυμὸν ἐχούσας, 16.354–355). In Book 8 the Trojans would have been penned in Ilios “like lambs” if Zeus had not thundered (8.130–132; see [[penned-like-lambs]]); here they are the lambs and no god stops the wolves. The pair returns in Achilles' answer to Hector, that wolves and lambs have no like-minded heart (22.262–264). See [[goats]] and [[sheep]].
