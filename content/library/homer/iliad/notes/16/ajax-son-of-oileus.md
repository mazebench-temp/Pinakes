---
summary: Ajax, son of Oileus, takes Cleobulus alive in the rout, then kills him on the spot with a sword stroke to the neck (16.330–334). With Telamonian Ajax he is one of “the two Ajaxes” whom Patroclus rouses to fight for Sarpedon's armor (16.555–562).
# Αἴας at 16.330 is this Ajax (ajax excepts the line); the phrase with the victim's name occurs only here.
grc: ["Αἴας δὲ Κλεόβουλον"]
refs: [16.331-334, 16.555-562]
---

**Cleobulus.** “And Ajax, son of Oileus, sprang at Cleobulus / and took him alive, caught in the turmoil; but there and then / he loosed his strength, striking him on the neck with his hilted sword” (16.330–332; see [[cleobulus]] and [[sword]]). ζωὸν ἕλε, “took him alive”, is said elsewhere in the Iliad only of Menelaus taking Adrastus, whose horses had caught in a tamarisk branch; Agamemnon then killed the man as he begged for his life (6.37–65; see [[adrastus-the-trojan]]). Here the same man takes and kills, and the poem gives no word to the captive. The end is told in the verses of the sword: “and the whole sword grew warm with blood; and his eyes / were seized by purple death and mighty fate” (16.333–334; see [[purple-death]] and [[mighty-fate]]); Achilles' sword will kill Echeclus, son of Agenor, in the same two verses (20.476–477). He is sixth in the list of leaders who each kill a man as the Trojans give way (16.306–351; see [[androktasia]]), and he is named, as often, by his patronymic, Ὀϊλιάδης, “son of Oileus” (see [[oileus]]).

**The two Ajaxes.** Over Sarpedon's body Patroclus speaks first to the pair, “who were already eager themselves”: “Ajaxes, now let it be your pleasure to defend yourselves, / such as you were before among men, or even better” (16.555–557; see [[the-two-ajaxes]], [[ajax]] and [[patroclus]]). He asks them to seize the body and strip it, “and they too were eager to defend themselves” (16.558–562).
