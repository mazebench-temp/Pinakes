---
summary: Patroclus tells Achilles that his mind is harsh (16.35); the mind of Zeus overpowers Ajax at the ship (16.103); and the narrator, when Patroclus forgets Achilles' word, gives the rule: “But the mind of Zeus is always stronger than that of men” (16.688; see [[the-mind-of-zeus]]).
---

Achilles' mind is ἀπηνής, “harsh”, and Patroclus finds its source outside his parents: the gray-green sea and the lofty rocks bore him, “since your mind is harsh” (16.33–35; see [[pitiless]]). The mind of Zeus is the force against which men fight in vain: “the mind of Zeus and the noble Trojans were overpowering him” (Ajax, 16.103), and it puts even a valiant man to flight and takes away victory (16.689). The verb νοέω, “notice”, closes the story: Patroclus “did not see” Apollo coming through the turmoil (16.789; see [[seeing-the-gods]]).
