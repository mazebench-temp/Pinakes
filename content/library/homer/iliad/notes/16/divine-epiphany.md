At the wall Apollo shouts at Patroclus as a god, and at the end he comes to meet him wrapped in thick mist, unseen (16.706–709, 16.715–726, 16.788–792).

Hector is in doubt at the Scaean gates, whether to drive back into the fight or call the army inside the wall (16.712–714; see [[decision-scene]]). “And as he pondered this, Phoebus Apollo stood beside him, / in the likeness of a man, young and strong, / Asius, who was the uncle of Hector, tamer of horses” (16.715–717; see [[eidos]] and [[asius-son-of-dymas]]). The disguise is a kinsman's, a mother's brother, and the speech is a kinsman's rebuke: “I wish I were as much stronger than you as I am weaker; / then you would soon hold back from the war to your sorrow” (16.722–723). The god names himself only in the third person, promising that Apollo may give Hector triumph (16.725), and goes back “into the toil of men” (16.726). Hector does not recognize the god, any more than Idomeneus recognized Poseidon speaking in the voice of Thoas (13.215–239); he simply obeys.

Patroclus meets the same god three times, each time less seen. At the wall Apollo speaks to him as a god, by name and with a god's warning (16.706–709). On the plain “he did not see him coming through the turmoil, / for he came to meet him wrapped in thick mist” (16.789–790), and the god strikes him from behind (see [[seeing-the-gods]] and [[divine-intervention]]). The dying man names him all the same: “deadly fate and the son of Leto have killed me” (16.849).
