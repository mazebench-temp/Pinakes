---
summary: The city is the goal the book forbids. Patroclus is to drive the Trojans “back to the city” and no further (16.45, 16.87–96); he pens them between the ships, the river and the wall and will not let them reach it (16.394–398), climbs its wall three times, and is told by Apollo that it is not his portion that “the city of the proud Trojans be sacked by your spear” (16.708). Hector mocks him for having said he would ravage it (16.830).
---

Achilles' own past is a list of cities taken: the “well-walled city” where he won Briseis, and the city of Eetion, from which he brought the horse Pedasus (16.57, 16.153; see [[raiding]] and [[sack-of-thebe]]). His wish is for the greatest of them, that he and Patroclus alone might loose the sacred veil of Troy (16.100; see [[veil]]). The Trojans fight “around the great city of Priam” (16.448), and Hera's warning to Zeus is that many sons of gods fight there (see [[not-all-the-gods-approve]]).
