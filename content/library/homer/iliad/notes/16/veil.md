---
summary: Achilles wishes that he and Patroclus alone might “loose the sacred veil of Troy” (16.100): the city's crown of walls is a woman's κρήδεμνον, and to take the city is to unveil her (see the translation's note on 16.97–100).
grc: [κρήδεμνα]
en: [the sacred veil]
---

λύω is the verb for loosing a κρήδεμνον, used in the Odyssey of the stopper of a wine jar (*Odyssey* 3.392). The image of the veiled city returns there for the sack itself: Odysseus asks Athena to stand by him as when “we loosed the shining veil of Troy” (Τροίης λύομεν λιπαρὰ κρήδεμνα, *Odyssey* 13.388). In the Iliad the veil is also the woman's: when Andromache sees Hector's body dragged behind Achilles' chariot she throws from her head the κρήδεμνον that Aphrodite gave her on her wedding day (22.468–472).
