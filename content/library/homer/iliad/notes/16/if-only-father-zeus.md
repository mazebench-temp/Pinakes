---
summary: Achilles ends his answer to Patroclus with the solemn wish: if only not one of the Trojans or the Argives would escape death, but the two of them alone, “so that we alone might loose the sacred veil of Troy!” (16.97–100). He wishes away both armies; within the day Patroclus is dead.
---

It is Achilles' only use of the line. The veil of Troy is its crown of walls, imagined as a woman's head-veil, κρήδεμνον (see the translation's note on 16.97–100 and [[veil]]). Patroclus comes nearest to loosing it, three times on the angle of the wall, before Apollo tells him that it is not his portion, nor Achilles' (16.698–709; see [[sack-of-troy-foretold]]).
