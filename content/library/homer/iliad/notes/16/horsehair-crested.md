summary: Patroclus arms with a helmet “with a horsehair crest,” the helmets of the Myrmidons touch in their close ranks, Lycon strikes Peneleos' “horsehair-crested helmet,” and Achilles' “horsehair-crested helmet” is stained with dust for the first time; the book adds ἱπποκόμου and ἱππόκομον.
grc: [ἱπποκόμου, ἱππόκομον]
---

**The arming and the ranks.** “and on his mighty head he set a well-made helmet / with a horsehair crest, and the plume nodded terribly above it” (16.137–138; [[arming-scene]], [[well-made]]). As Achilles' speech closes the Myrmidons' ranks, “the helmets with horsehair crests touched with their bright ridges / when the men nodded, so close did they stand to one another” (16.216–217), the verse of the Achaean line in Book 13 (13.132–133; [[bossed]], [[myrmidons]]).

**Peneleos.** “There Lycon / struck the ridge of his horsehair-crested helmet, and at the socket / the sword was shattered” (16.337–339; [[lycon]], [[peneleos]], [[helmet]]). The sword breaks on the helmet, and Peneleos kills Lycon.

**Achilles' helmet.** When Apollo strikes it from Patroclus' head, the horsehair is stained with blood and dust: “before that it had not been lawful / for the horsehair-crested helmet to be stained with dust, / but it guarded the head and the graceful brow of a divine man, / Achilles” (16.796–799; [[with-a-socket-for-the-plume]], [[divine]], [[themis]]). The genitive ἱπποκόμου and the accusative ἱππόκομον are new to the entry.
