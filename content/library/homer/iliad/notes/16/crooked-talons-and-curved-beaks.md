---
summary: Sarpedon and Patroclus rush at each other like “two vultures with crooked talons and curved beaks” fighting on a high rock.
---

{{quote:72dcdc2f-94a1-552f-8b1d-59e9e54830bc}}

Sarpedon and Patroclus leap down from their chariots and come together with a scream, like two vultures fighting on a high rock; the verb κλάζοντε is in the dual, one bird for each man (see the translation's note on 16.428–429). At once Zeus, seeing them, pities them (16.431; [[son-of-cronus]]). See [[sarpedon]], [[patroclus]] and [[vultures]].
