---
summary: Patroclus mocks Cebriones as a diver who would feed many “on the fish-filled sea”; the book adds ἰχθυόεντι.
grc: [ἰχθυόεντι]
---

“If he were ever on the fish-filled sea, / this man would feed many, diving for oysters, / leaping from a ship, even if the sea were rough” (16.746–748; [[dyspemphelos]], [[cebriones]], [[like-a-diver]]). The epithet of the open sea comes in a joke about a man fallen from his chariot. The dative ἰχθυόεντι is new to the entry. See [[sea]].
