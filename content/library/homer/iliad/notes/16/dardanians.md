---
summary: The Dardanians are named once, in Δάρδανος ἀνήρ, “a Dardanian man” (16.807): Euphorbus, son of Panthous, who wounds Patroclus in the back. Their leader Aeneas fights in the battle over Sarpedon's body (16.608–625).
---

Δάρδανος ἀνήρ, “a Dardanian man” (16.807), is how the narrator first names the man who strikes Patroclus from behind, before the next verse calls him “Euphorbus, son of Panthous” (16.808; see [[euphorbus]] and [[panthous]]). Euphorbus is the son of one of the elders who sit with Priam on the wall (3.146), and the word means no more than “Trojan” here, as at 2.701, where “a Dardanian man” kills Protesilaus, the first Achaean to die at Troy (see [[the-death-of-protesilaus]]). The Dardanians of Aeneas are not named in the book; Aeneas himself throws at Meriones and trades taunts with him (16.608–625; see [[aeneas]]).
