---
summary: Achilles bids Patroclus lead “the war-loving Myrmidons” and warns him not to fight on against “the war-loving Trojans”; Hector boasts that he stands out among “the war-loving Trojans.”
---

The word appears here for the first time in the poem, three times. Achilles bids Patroclus “lead the war-loving Myrmidons into battle” (16.65), and warns him: “do not long to fight without me / against the war-loving Trojans; you will make me more dishonored” (16.89–90). At the end of the book Hector boasts over the dying Patroclus: “with the spear I myself / stand out among the war-loving Trojans, I who ward off from them / the day of compulsion” (16.834–836; [[hector]]). Achilles names the enemy so to keep his friend from them, and Hector names his own people so to set himself above them. See [[myrmidons]] and [[trojans]].
