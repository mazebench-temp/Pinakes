summary: Over the body of Sarpedon Patroclus calls first on the two Ajaxes, in the verse with which Poseidon called them at the ships, to strip the man who first leapt inside the Achaean wall (16.555–562).
refs: [16.555-562]
---

When Hector leads the Trojans back to fight for Sarpedon, Patroclus rouses the Achaeans, and “First he spoke to the two Ajaxes, who were already eager themselves” (16.555), the verse of Poseidon's first call in Book 13 (13.46; see [[poseidon]]). He addresses them in the dual, Αἴαντε, “Ajaxes”, and asks them to be “such as you were before among men, or even better” (16.557): the man who lies dead is Sarpedon, who broke the battlement for the Trojans at the wall (12.397–399), and if they could seize and defile him and take his armor, they might bring down some of his companions too (16.558–561; see [[sarpedon]] and [[patroclus]]). “So he spoke, and they too were eager to defend themselves” (16.562), and the fight for the body begins.
