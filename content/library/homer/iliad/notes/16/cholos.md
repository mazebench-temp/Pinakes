---
summary: Achilles' anger is judged by those nearest to him. Patroclus prays that such anger may never take him (16.30), the Myrmidons said his mother reared him on it (16.203), and Achilles himself grants that he cannot be angry without end (16.61). In the battle anger is for the fallen: Maris for his brother, the Myrmidons for the Danaans, Hector for Sarpedon, Patroclus for Epeigeus.
grc: [κεχολωμένοι, κεχόλωσο]
en: [angry for, angered for]
---

**Achilles.** “May such anger never take hold of me as this that you keep” (16.30) is Patroclus' reproach before his request, and the Myrmidons' complaint, as Achilles quotes it, was harsher: “Hard son of Peleus, your mother reared you on anger” (χόλῳ, 16.203), “since such evil anger has fallen on your heart” (16.206; see [[retelling]]). Achilles answers with a concession: “it was not possible after all / to be relentlessly angry in my heart” (16.60–61; see [[menis]]). The anger is not given up but limited: Patroclus will go, Achilles will not.

**For the fallen.** In the battle χόλος is the anger of a man for a dead companion or kinsman, and it moves the fighting. Maris springs at Antilochus “angered for his brother” (16.320; see [[kasignetos]]); Glaucus warns that the Myrmidons will defile Sarpedon's body, “angry for all the Danaans who have perished” (16.546); Hector leads the Trojans “angered for Sarpedon” (16.553); and when Epeigeus falls, Patroclus rushes at the Lycians and Trojans, “and your heart was angered for your companion” (16.585; see [[apostrophe]]). Aeneas is angered when his spear misses Meriones (16.616).
