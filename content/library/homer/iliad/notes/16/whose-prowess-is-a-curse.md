---
summary: Patroclus reproaches Achilles, “you whose prowess is a curse!”, a prowess that does no one any good if it will not save the Argives.
---

The word stands in a run of reproaches. Patroclus has said that “there is no dealing with you, Achilles” (16.29; [[with-many-drugs]]), and he prays that such anger as Achilles keeps may never take hold of him (16.30–31; [[cholos]]); after αἰναρέτη he goes on to call him “Pitiless one” and to deny him his parents: “the gray-green sea bore you, / and the lofty rocks, since your mind is harsh” (16.33–35; [[pitiless]], [[gray-green-sea]], [[lofty]]). Nestor had said the same of Achilles' excellence in Book 11, with the same verb (ὀνίνημι, “have profit of”): Achilles “will have the profit of his prowess alone” (11.762–763; [[nestor]]).
