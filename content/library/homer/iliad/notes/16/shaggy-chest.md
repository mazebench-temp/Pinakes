---
summary: The Achaeans are roused by “Patroclus of the shaggy heart, son of Menoetius.”
---

“But the Achaeans / were roused by Patroclus of the shaggy heart, son of Menoetius” (16.553–554; [[son-of-menoetius]], [[achaeans]]). The phrase λάσιον κῆρ is said of the Paphlagonian Pylaemenes in the Catalogue (2.851), and it was in his shaggy breast that Achilles pondered drawing his sword on Agamemnon (1.189). Here the hairy heart is courage at the moment the Trojans come on, led by Hector in his anger for Sarpedon (16.552–553). See [[patroclus]].
