summary: Patroclus tells Achilles that the gray-green sea bore him, not Thetis; the Argives are pressed back against the shore of the sea; and in the similes and in Patroclus' taunt the sea is the place of the fisherman and the diver.
# πόντοιο is always the sea in the Iliad: it also catches 8.479 ("the lowest limits / of earth and sea"), and 19.375 later.
grc: [πόντοιο]
---

**The gray-green sea.** “Pitiless one, the horseman Peleus was not your father after all, / nor Thetis your mother; but the gray-green sea bore you, / and the lofty rocks, since your mind is harsh” (16.33–35; see [[gray-green-sea]], [[thetis]] and [[achilles]]). The sea and the rocks are the parents of a man without pity; the reproach falls near the truth, for his mother is a goddess of the sea.

**The sea at the camp.** Achilles pictures the Argives “pressed back / against the shore of the sea, holding only a little portion of land still” (16.66–68; see [[achilles]]), as he had asked Thetis to beg of Zeus: to “pen the Achaeans in among their ships' sterns and along the sea” (1.408–410; see [[thetis-supplicates-zeus]]).

**The fisherman and the diver.** In the similes the torrents of Zeus' storm groan as they run “into the purple sea” (16.391; see [[purple]]), and Patroclus drags Thestor from his chariot on his spear as a fisherman sitting on a rock drags a fish “out of the sea onto the shore” (16.407–408; see [[the-fisherman]]). Patroclus mocks the dead Cebriones as a diver: “If he were ever on the fish-filled sea, / this man would feed many, diving for oysters, / leaping from a ship, even if the sea were rough” (16.746–748; see [[fish-filled]], [[dyspemphelos]], [[like-a-diver]] and [[cebriones]]).
