---
summary: Teucer is named once, in a memory: Glaucus' arm is still wearing him down with the wound that Teucer's arrow gave him at the high wall, until Apollo heals it (16.508–512).
---

χειρὶ δʼ ἑλὼν ἐπίεζε βραχίονα· τεῖρε γὰρ αὐτὸν / ἕλκος, ὃ δή μιν Τεῦκρος ἐπεσσύμενον βάλεν ἰῷ / τείχεος ὑψηλοῖο, ἀρὴν ἑτάροισιν ἀμύνων, “And he took his arm in his hand and pressed it; for it was wearing him down, / the wound where Teucer had hit him with an arrow as he rushed on / at the high wall, warding off disaster from his companions” (16.510–512; see [[glaucus]]). Book 12 told the shot: Teucer hit Glaucus “with an arrow as he rushed at the high wall, / where he saw his arm laid bare, and stopped him from battle”, and Glaucus leapt back unseen, so that no Achaean should boast over him (12.387–391; see [[the-achaean-wall]] and [[the-fight-at-the-wall]]). The arrow keeps Glaucus from his dying friend Sarpedon until he prays to Apollo, who stops the pain, dries the blood and puts might into his heart (16.508–531; see [[apollo]], [[sarpedon]] and [[healing]]). ἀρὴν ἑτάροισιν ἀμύνων, “warding off disaster from his companions”, recalls the scene at the wall, where Menestheus sent for the two Ajaxes and Teucer to ward off disaster (12.334; see the translation's note on 12.334).
