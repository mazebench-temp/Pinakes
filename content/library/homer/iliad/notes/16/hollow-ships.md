---
summary: The Argives perish “beside the hollow ships,” the Danaans pour after the Trojans “among the hollow ships,” Sarpedon's armor is carried “to the hollow ships,” and Hector imagines Achilles forbidding Patroclus to come back “to the hollow ships” without Hector's tunic.
---

Achilles asks whether Patroclus weeps for the Argives, “how they are perishing / beside the hollow ships because of their own transgression” (16.17–18). When the fire is out, “the Danaans poured after them / among the hollow ships, and a relentless din arose” (16.295–296; [[relentless]]), the verse that ended Book 12 when Hector broke the gate (12.471). Patroclus sends Sarpedon's armor “to the hollow ships” (16.664; [[marmairon]]), and Hector puts the ships into Achilles' imagined orders: “Do not come back to me, Patroclus, urger of horses, / to the hollow ships” (16.839–840; [[urger-of-horses]]). The ships that Patroclus went out to save are where he was told to return. See [[ships]].
