summary: “Silver-footed Thetis” packed her son's chest for Troy, and Epeigeus came as a suppliant “to Peleus and to silver-footed Thetis”; the book adds the accusative ἀργυρόπεζαν.
grc: [ἀργυρόπεζαν]
---

**The chest.** Achilles opens “a chest, / fine and intricately worked, which silver-footed Thetis / had put on his ship for him to take” (16.221–223; [[chest]], [[intricately-worked]], [[to-keep-off-the-wind]]).

**The suppliant.** Epeigeus, who had killed a cousin, “came as a suppliant to Peleus and to silver-footed Thetis; / and they sent him to follow Achilles, breaker of men” (16.574–575; [[epeigeus]], [[peleus]], [[supplication]], [[breaker-of-men]]). Peleus received other exiles at Phthia, Phoenix (9.480–484) and Patroclus himself (23.85–90); Epeigeus came as a suppliant to the goddess as well as to the king ([[exile-and-refuge]]). The accusative ἀργυρόπεζαν is new to the entry. See [[thetis]].
