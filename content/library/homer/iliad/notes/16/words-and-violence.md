---
summary: Taunts and boasts are answered in the book by fighting and by death. Aeneas and Meriones trade taunts until Patroclus stops them, “there is no need to heap up words, but to fight” (16.617–631); Patroclus mocks the fallen Cebriones as a diver (16.745–750); and Hector boasts over the dying Patroclus and is answered with a prophecy (16.830–854).
---

Patroclus' maxim gives the two kinds of contest their places, “For the outcome of war lies in our hands, and the outcome of words in council” (16.630; see [[the-outcome-of-war]] and [[oneidos]]). His own last speech turns Hector's boast back on him: the gods and Euphorbus brought him down, and Hector is “the third to slay me” (16.844–850; see [[prophecy]]).
