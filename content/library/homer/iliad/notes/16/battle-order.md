---
summary: Achilles sets the Myrmidons in order in five companies under their leaders, and at his words “the ranks closed up more tightly”, helmet on helmet and shield on shield, like the stones of a wall built against the wind (16.168–217). In the battle Patroclus cuts off the foremost battalions (16.394), and both sides strengthen theirs around Sarpedon's body (16.563).
---

The Myrmidon line is described in the verses of the Achaean line in Book 13: “Shield pressed on shield, helmet on helmet, man on man; / and the helmets with horsehair crests touched with their bright ridges / when the men nodded, so close did they stand to one another” (16.215–217 = 13.131–133; see [[polyptoton]] and [[the-stones-of-a-wall]]). The Myrmidons are counted by ships, fifty ships of fifty men under five leaders, a small catalogue within the poem (16.168–197; see [[myrmidons]] and [[hegemon]]). At the end Hector comes at Patroclus “through the ranks” (κατὰ στίχας, 16.820).
