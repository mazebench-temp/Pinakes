---
summary: Apollo takes the likeness of Asius, “the uncle of Hector, tamer of horses.”
---

“in the likeness of a man, young and strong, / Asius, who was the uncle of Hector, tamer of horses, / own brother of Hecuba, and son of Dymas” (16.716–718; [[asius-son-of-dymas]], [[hecuba]], [[dymas]]). The genitive Ἕκτορος ἱπποδάμοιο, with which the poem ends (24.804), appears here for the first time. See [[hector]].
