summary: Achilles likens the weeping Patroclus to a little girl who runs beside her mother, clutching her robe, until she is picked up (16.7–11; see [[like-a-girl]]); the Myrmidons pour out like wasps that boys, “the foolish little ones”, provoke by the road (16.259–265; see [[wasps-at-the-roadside]]); and old Phylas raises his grandson Eudorus “as if he were his own son” (16.191–192).
