**Achilles' honor.** The quarrel is still a matter of honor. Agamemnon took his prize “as if I were some dishonored vagrant” (ἀτίμητον, 16.59 = 9.648; see [[like-a-dishonored-vagrant]]). Patroclus is to win him “great honor and glory / from all the Danaans” (τιμὴν μεγάλην καὶ κῦδος, 16.84–85; see [[kydos]]), and the Achaeans will send back the girl and give splendid gifts besides (16.85–86; see [[geras]] and [[doron]]). The same honor sets the limit of Patroclus' fighting: if he wins glory and fights on toward Troy without Achilles, “you will make me more dishonored” (16.87–90). In the prayer to Zeus, the honor is a fact: “you honored me, and struck the army of the Achaeans hard” (16.237; see [[fulfill-this-wish]]). Patroclus' rally gives honor as the reason to fight, “so that we may honor the son of Peleus”, and Agamemnon may learn that “he paid no honor to the best of the Achaeans” (16.271–274; see [[ate]]).

**Other honors.** Automedon was the man Patroclus “honored most after Achilles” (16.146). Zeus sheds bloody raindrops on the earth, “honoring his dear son” (16.459–460; see [[sarpedon]]). And Onetor, priest of Idaean Zeus, “was honored by the people like a god” (16.604–605; see [[priesthood]]).
