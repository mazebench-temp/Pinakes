summary: The two sons of Amisodarus, Sarpedon's companions, killed by the two sons of Nestor, “went down to Erebus” (16.327).
# Ἔρεβος (16.327) is the only nominative-accusative of the name in the Iliad; the Odyssey has it at 10.528, 11.564,
# 12.81 (and Ἐρεβόσδε, 20.356).
grc: [Ἔρεβος]
---

“So these two, brought down by two brothers, / went down to Erebus, brave companions of Sarpedon, / the spearmen sons of Amisodarus, who reared the Chimaera” (16.326–328; see [[amisodarus]], [[atymnius-son-of-amisodarus]], [[maris]], [[antilochus]], [[thrasymedes]] and [[chimaera]]). Antilochus killed Atymnius, and Thrasymedes his brother Maris as he stood over the body (16.317–325). The dual βήτην, “the two went”, keeps the pairs together to the end. It is the only place in the Iliad where the dead are said to go down to Erebus; elsewhere they go to the house of Hades, or the soul goes to Hades (16.856; see [[hades]] and [[psyche]]). In the Odyssey the souls gather “out of Erebus” and go back “into Erebus” (*Odyssey* 11.37, 11.564).
