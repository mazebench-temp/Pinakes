---
summary: The Myrmidons, arming, are like “wolves / that eat raw flesh, in whose hearts there is boundless courage.”
---

“and they, like wolves / that eat raw flesh, in whose hearts there is boundless courage” (16.156–157; [[wolves]], [[untold]], [[antlered]]). The epithet of the beasts of prey opens the simile in which the Myrmidons, held back so long, go out to the battle gorged with blood, “and the heart in their breasts / is fearless, and their bellies are gorged full” (16.162–163).
