summary: Achilles thinks of Menoetius as still alive in Phthia when Patroclus comes to him weeping (16.14–16). In the book of his son's aristeia and death the father's name is heard eleven times, in Patroclus' titles, “son of Menoetius” and “the valiant son of Menoetius”, in the narrator's voice and in the speeches of Zeus and Hera.
refs: [16.16]
---

**Alive in Phthia.** “Yet they say that Menoetius, son of Actor, still lives, / and Peleus, son of Aeacus, lives among the Myrmidons; / we would grieve greatly for both of them, if they were dead” (16.14–16; see [[actor-father-of-menoetius]], [[peleus]] and [[phthia]]). Achilles can imagine no grief for Patroclus but a father's death. It is Menoetius who loses a son: after Patroclus has fallen, Achilles groans that he made an empty promise “on that day” to the hero Menoetius in his halls, to bring his son home to Opus with his share of the spoil of Ilios (18.324–327; see [[achilles]] and [[patroclus]]).

**The father's name.** The book names Patroclus by his father eleven times. Μενοιτίου ἄλκιμος υἱός, “the valiant son of Menoetius”, the title Book 11 gave him (11.605, 11.814, 11.837), comes three times (16.307, 16.626, 16.665), and twice in the accusative, as the Trojans see him come out in Achilles' armor and as Hector kills him (Μενοιτίου ἄλκιμον υἱόν, 16.278, 16.827; see [[son-of-menoetius]]). Μενοιτιάδης, “son of Menoetius”, is the name Zeus and Hera use for the man who is to kill Sarpedon: “Ah me, that Sarpedon, dearest of men to me, / is fated to be brought down by Patroclus, son of Menoetius!” (16.433–434; also 16.438, 16.452; see [[zeus]], [[hera]] and [[sarpedon]]). The narrator uses it when Sarpedon sees his companions fall, as Patroclus rouses the Achaeans over Sarpedon's body, and as he fights Hector over Cebriones (16.420, 16.554, 16.760).
