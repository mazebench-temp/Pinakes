---
summary: Patroclus sets a helmet “on his mighty head,” Meriones calls Aeneas “mighty though you are,” and “not even the mighty Lycians” stand when Hector flees.
---

“and on his mighty head he set a well-made helmet / with a horsehair crest” (16.137–138; [[well-made]], [[horsehair-crested]], [[arming-scene]]); the verse is the one of Paris' arming and of Teucer's (3.336, 15.480). Meriones answers Aeneas: “Aeneas, it is hard for you, mighty though you are, / to quench the might of all men” (16.620–621; [[aeneas]], [[meriones]]). When Zeus puts a craven heart in Hector, “Then not even the mighty Lycians stood their ground, but fled in rout” (16.659; [[lycians]], [[the-scales-of-zeus]]).
