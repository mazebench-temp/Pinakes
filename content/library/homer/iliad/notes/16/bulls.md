summary: The bull of Book 16 is Sarpedon, who dies under Patroclus like a tawny, great-hearted bull that a lion kills among the herd (16.487–491). Hector covers his shoulders with his shield of bull's hide as the Trojans are driven from the ships (16.358–361).
grc: [ταυρείῃ]
en: [bull's hide]
---

**The bull and the lion.** ἠΰτε ταῦρον ἔπεφνε λέων ἀγέληφι μετελθὼν / αἴθωνα μεγάθυμον ἐν εἰλιπόδεσσι βόεσσι, “As a lion comes upon a herd and kills a bull, / tawny and great-hearted, among the shambling cattle” (16.487–488; see [[lion-among-cattle]]). The bull is the leader of the herd, as in the simile of Agamemnon standing out among the heroes like a bull among the gathered cattle (2.480–483; see [[bull-above-the-herd]]), and the lion takes the leader. It dies groaning under the lion's jaws, as Sarpedon, leader of the Lycians, rages as he dies (16.489–491; see [[sarpedon]]).

**Bull's hide.** ὃ δὲ ἰδρείῃ πολέμοιο / ἀσπίδι ταυρείῃ κεκαλυμμένος εὐρέας ὤμους, “but he, in his skill at war, / covering his broad shoulders with his shield of bull's hide” (16.359–360): Hector watches for arrows and spears, knows that the victory has turned, and still stands his ground to save his companions (16.358–363; see [[hector]] and [[shield]]). ταύρειος, “of bull's hide”, is said of the shield of Deiphobus (13.161–163) and of the leather cap that Diomedes wears on the night raid (10.258).
