summary: Both prayers of the book are heard in the closing verse: “So he spoke in prayer, and Zeus the counselor heard him” (16.249), and Glaucus' prayer ends in the verse of Chryses' prayers, “So he spoke in prayer, and Phoebus Apollo heard him” (16.527 = 1.43, 1.457). Glaucus opens with κλῦθι ἄναξ, “Hear me, lord” (16.514).
grc: ["κλῦθι ἄναξ"]
en: [Hear me, lord]
---

Zeus hears Achilles and grants only half of what he asks (16.249–252; see [[prayer]] and [[neuo]]). Apollo hears Glaucus and grants it all at once: he stops the pains, dries the blood and puts might into his heart (16.527–529; see [[healing]]). Glaucus knows that he has been heard, and rejoices “that the great god had quickly heard him when he prayed” (16.530–531). κλῦθι ἄναξ, with the vocative ἄναξ in place of μευ, is found once more in Homer, when Odysseus, swimming, prays to the river god of Scheria (*Odyssey* 5.445).
