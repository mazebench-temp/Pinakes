---
summary: The Trojans, falling before the Danaans like lambs before wolves, “remembered / the ill-sounding rout, and forgot their furious courage.”
---

It comes at the end of the simile of the wolves among the lambs and kids, which the shepherd has let scatter in the mountains: “so the Danaans fell upon the Trojans; and they remembered / the ill-sounding rout, and forgot their furious courage” (16.356–357; [[wolves]], [[furious]]). To remember flight and forget courage turns upside down the exhortation that Patroclus gave the Myrmidons as they went out, “remember your furious courage” (16.270; [[exhortation]]). See [[phobos]] and [[trojans]].
