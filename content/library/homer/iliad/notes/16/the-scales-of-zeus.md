# Διὸς ἱρὰ τάλαντα occurs only here; τάλαντα alone is also the talents of gold (9.122, 18.507 and later).
grc: ["Διὸς ἱρὰ τάλαντα"]
passages:
  - 16.656-662 | Hector knows the scales of Zeus
---

Over Sarpedon's body Zeus has pondered whether Hector should kill Patroclus at once, and has decided that Patroclus shall first drive the Trojans back to the city (16.644–655; see [[zeus]] and [[plan-of-zeus]]). “And into Hector first of all he put a craven heart; / and he mounted his chariot and turned to flight, and called on the other / Trojans to flee; for he knew the sacred scales of Zeus” (16.656–658; see [[hector]]). No scales are held out, as they were at midday in Book 8, when the fated day of the Achaeans sank and Zeus thundered from Ida (8.68–77). Here the scales are a figure for the turn of the battle, and Hector reads it as men read a sign. In Book 8 the weighing gave him his day of victory; now he knows that the day has turned against him, as Ajax, his spear lopped off, knew “the work of the gods” earlier in the book, with the same verb, γνῶ (16.119–121; see [[ajax]]). ἱρά, “sacred”, is said of the scales because they are the god's. The next time the poem names them, Odysseus speaks of the moment “when he tips the scales”, Zeus, “who is the dispenser of war for men” (19.223–224).
