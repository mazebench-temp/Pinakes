summary: Achilles tells Patroclus to obey his word (16.83), Zeus does not disobey Hera (16.458), and Hector, over the dying Patroclus, imagines how Achilles' orders “persuaded your mind, fool that you were” (16.842). The perfect is trust: Achilles made five leaders “whom he trusted” (16.171), and Meriones speaks of Aeneas “trusting in your hands” (16.624).
grc: [πεῖθε, ἐπεποίθει]
en: [persuaded, whom he trusted]
---

Achilles' instruction begins “But obey, as I put the end of my word in your mind” (16.83), and the end of the word is the order to come back from the ships (16.87–96). Patroclus does not keep it (16.684–687; see [[nepios]] and [[obedience-and-persuasion]]). Hector's version of Achilles' word is the opposite, and he thinks it persuaded Patroclus (16.838–842; see [[retelling]]).
