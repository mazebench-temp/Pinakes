---
summary: Patroclus puts Nestor's plan to Achilles with Nestor's last verse, “and brief is the breathing space in war” (16.43), and the plan works for exactly that long: the Danaans drive the fire from the ships and “drew breath a little, but there was no respite from the war” (16.302).
---

Patroclus asks in Nestor's words, with himself in place of “you” (16.36–45 ~ 11.794–803; see [[retelling]]). The breathing space comes when the Trojans, seeing the armor, think Achilles has given up his wrath and look for a way to escape (16.278–283), and it ends at once: the Trojans give way from the ships only “under compulsion” (16.302–305; see [[exchange-of-armor]]).
