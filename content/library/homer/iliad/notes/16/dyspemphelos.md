---
summary: Patroclus mocks the fallen Cebriones as a diver who would feed many on the fish-filled sea, “even if the sea were rough.”
---

“Ah, truly the man is nimble; how easily he tumbles! / If he were ever on the fish-filled sea, / this man would feed many, diving for oysters, / leaping from a ship, even if the sea were rough, / as now on the plain he tumbles easily from his chariot. / So there are tumblers among the Trojans too.” (16.745–750; [[fish-filled]], [[cebriones]], [[o-popoi]]). Cebriones, struck on the forehead by Patroclus' stone, has pitched from his chariot “like a diver” (16.742), and Patroclus makes a joke of the likeness: a tumbler who would be a fine diver for oysters even in a rough sea.
