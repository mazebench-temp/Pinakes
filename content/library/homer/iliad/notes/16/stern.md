summary: The fire takes the ship of Protesilaus at the stern, where Hector had laid hold of it in the book before: “So the fire played about her stern” (16.124). Patroclus' first spear kills Pyraechmes “beside the stern of the ship of great-hearted Protesilaus” (16.286), and drives the Trojans from it.
grc: ["πρυμνὴν πῦρ", πρυμνῇ]
en: [her stern, beside the stern]
---

πρυμνήν at 16.124 is the stern, though the same form means “at the root” of a tongue cut off (5.292) and of the wood that boars break around them (12.149); hence the pattern “πρυμνὴν πῦρ”. πρυμνῇ in νηῒ πάρα πρυμνῇ, “beside the stern of the ship”, is the stern again, as in the same phrase at 7.383 and 10.35, and at 10.570 and 11.600 (see [[protesilaus]] and [[ships]]).
