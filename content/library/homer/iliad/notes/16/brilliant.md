summary: “Brilliant” names Achilles as he pities Patroclus, Agenor among the Trojan leaders, Epeigeus as he falls, and Sarpedon twice, unrecognizable in the dust and lifted by Apollo; the book adds the upper air, αἰθέρος ἐκ δίης.
grc: ["αἰθέρος ἐκ δίης"]
en: [shining]
---

**Achilles and Agenor.** “And seeing him, brilliant swift-footed Achilles pitied him” (16.5; [[podarkes]]). Glaucus goes to the Trojan leaders, “to Polydamas, son of Panthous, and brilliant Agenor” (16.535; [[agenor]], [[polydamas]]).

**Epeigeus.** The first Myrmidon to fall over Sarpedon's body is “the son of great-hearted Agacles, brilliant Epeigeus” (16.571; [[epeigeus]], [[agacles]], [[great-hearted]]), an exile who had come to Peleus as a suppliant.

**Sarpedon.** Over his body the fight is so thick that “Nor could even a sharp-eyed man any longer have recognized brilliant Sarpedon, / since with missiles and blood and dust / he was wrapped from his head to the tips of his feet” (16.638–640; [[fighting-over-the-body]]). Then Apollo “lifted brilliant Sarpedon out of range of the missiles” (16.678; [[phoebus]]). The same epithet stands for the man made unrecognizable and for the body that the god cleanses ([[sarpedon]]).

**The upper air.** In the simile of the cloud, “as when a cloud comes from Olympus into the sky / out of the shining upper air, when Zeus spreads a storm” (16.364–365), δῖος keeps the sense of its root, “of the bright sky.” The phrase αἰθέρος ἐκ δίης is new to the entry; the translation renders it “shining,” as for the sea ([[shining-sea]]).
