summary: The patronymic names Patroclus through the whole book: the Trojans fear “the valiant son of Menoetius,” Zeus and Hera speak of his killing Sarpedon at the hands of “Patroclus, son of Menoetius,” and Hector kills “the valiant son of Menoetius”; the book adds Μενοιτιάδαο, Μενοιτιάδεω and “Μενοιτίου ἄλκιμον υἱὸν.”

**The valiant son.** The formula Μενοιτίου ἄλκιμος υἱός, “the valiant son of Menoetius,” marks Patroclus at each stage of his day ([[strong-spear]]): the Trojans see him and his attendant gleaming in their armor (16.278; [[marmairon]]); he kills his first man of the fighting among the leaders, Areilycus (16.307; [[areilycus-killed-by-patroclus]]); he rebukes Meriones (16.626); he sends Sarpedon's armor to the ships (16.665); and Hector kills him: “so, though the valiant son of Menoetius had killed many, / Hector, son of Priam, robbed him of his life with the spear at close range” (16.827–828; [[son-of-priam]]).

**Sarpedon's killer.** The patronymic goes with Sarpedon's fate: Sarpedon sees his companions “brought down at the hands of Patroclus, son of Menoetius” (16.420; [[tunics-without-the-band]]), and Zeus laments that his son “is fated to be brought down by Patroclus, son of Menoetius!” (16.434; [[sarpedon]]); the patronymic returns in his question (16.438) and in Hera's answer (16.452; [[hera]]). The Achaeans are roused by “Patroclus of the shaggy heart, son of Menoetius” (16.554; [[shaggy-chest]]), and over Cebriones the two masters of the war cry are “Patroclus, son of Menoetius, and shining Hector” (16.760; [[masters-of-the-war-cry]]).

The genitives Μενοιτιάδαο and Μενοιτιάδεω, and the accusative of the formula, are new to the entry. See [[patroclus]] and [[menoetius]].
