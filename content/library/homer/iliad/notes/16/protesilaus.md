---
summary: The ship of Protesilaus is the first to burn. Patroclus' first spear flies “beside the stern of the ship of great-hearted Protesilaus” and kills Pyraechmes; the Trojans are driven off, the fire is put out, and the half-burned ship is left on the beach (16.122–124, 16.284–294).
refs: [16.1, 16.122-124, 16.293-294]
---

**The first ship to burn.** At the end of Book 15 Hector held the stern of the ship that had brought Protesilaus to Troy and called for fire (15.704–725), and Book 16 begins with the fighting “around the well-benched ship” (16.1; see [[well-benched]]). When Hector has lopped the head from Ajax's spear, “they threw tireless fire / on the swift ship, and at once an unquenchable flame poured over it. / So the fire played about her stern” (16.122–124; see [[ajax]], [[hector]] and [[stern]]). The ship is not named here; the poem names it again only when the fire is put out.

**Patroclus' first spear.** “And Patroclus was the first to throw his shining spear, / straight into the middle, where the most men were thrown into confusion, / beside the stern of the ship of great-hearted Protesilaus” (16.284–286; see [[patroclus]] and [[great-hearted]]). The spear kills Pyraechmes, the leader of the Paeonians, and his men flee (16.287–292; see [[pyraechmes]] and [[paeonians]]): “He drove them from the ships, and put out the blazing fire. / And the half-burned ship was left there” (16.293–294). ἡμιδαής, “half-burned”, occurs only here in Homer. The ship of the first Achaean to die at Troy, which “did not take him back again to his native land” (15.706), is the one that the first Trojan fire reaches and the one that Achilles' companion saves (see [[the-death-of-protesilaus]] and [[the-battle-at-the-ships]]). Achilles had promised in Book 9 that he would not think of war until Hector came to the Myrmidons' huts and ships, killing the Argives, and burned the ships with fire (9.650–653); it is the sight of this fire that makes him send Patroclus out at once (16.124–129; see [[achilles]]).
