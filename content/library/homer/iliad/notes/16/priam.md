---
summary: Priam is named three times: Hera warns Zeus that many sons of gods are fighting “around the great city of Priam” (16.448); Hector's charioteer Cebriones is “the bastard son of famous Priam” (16.738); and “Hector, son of Priam” kills Patroclus (16.828).
---

ἀγακλῆος Πριάμοιο, “famous Priam” (16.738), is Priam's epithet only here; in the same book Ἀγακλῆος is a name, that of Epeigeus' father Agacles (16.571; see [[agacles]]). Cebriones is one of Priam's bastard sons, and he dies as Hector's charioteer (see [[cebriones]], [[nothos]] and [[sons-of-priam]]). Πριαμίδης, “son of Priam”, names Hector at the moment he kills Patroclus (16.828; see [[son-of-priam]]).
