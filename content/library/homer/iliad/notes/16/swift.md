**Sarpedon.** When he sees his Lycians falling before Patroclus, he calls to them: “Shame, Lycians! Where are you fleeing? Now be swift!” (16.422; [[aidos]], [[lycians]]). θοοί may be imperative or a scornful statement, “now you are swift” (see the translation's note on 16.422). Dying, he lays the same word on Glaucus: “now let evil war be your desire, if you are swift” (16.494; [[glaucus]], [[my-dear]]).

**Ares.** Patroclus in his last charge is “the equal of swift Ares” (16.784; [[equal-of-ares]]).

**The escorts.** Zeus bids Apollo send Sarpedon “with swift escorts to be carried, / with Sleep and Death, the twins” (16.671–672), and Apollo does so (16.681–682; [[sleep]], [[death]]). κραιπνός, “swift,” was said of men's feet in Book 6 (6.505); here it is said of the gods who carry the dead. See [[sarpedon]].
