---
summary: The dying Patroclus names Apollo by his mother: “deadly fate and the son of Leto have killed me”.
---

“But deadly fate and the son of Leto have killed me, / and of men Euphorbus; and you are the third to slay me” (16.849–850; see [[apollo]], [[moira]], [[euphorbus]] and [[patroclus]]). Λητοῦς … υἱός, “the son of Leto”, is how the poem first names Apollo, before it names him at all (Λητοῦς καὶ Διὸς υἱός, 1.9). Patroclus ranks his killers from the gods down to Hector (see the translation's note on 16.849–850), and Apollo is named by his mother, as Xanthus will name him when he tells Achilles that it was “the best of the gods, whom lovely-haired Leto bore” who killed Patroclus (19.413–414; see [[the-horses-of-achilles]]).
