---
summary: Achilles sees “the blast of consuming fire” at the ships, the Danaans thrust “the consuming fire” away, and in the simile of the javelin the enemy are δήϊοι.
---

“Up, Patroclus, sprung from Zeus, urger of horses! / I see the blast of consuming fire beside the ships” (16.126–127; [[urger-of-horses]]); and when the fire is out, “so the Danaans, when they had thrust the consuming fire from the ships, / drew breath a little” (16.301–302; [[who-gathers-the-lightning]]). In Book 9 Achilles had told Agamemnon to see for himself how “to ward off the consuming fire from the ships” (9.347, 9.674); here the fire has come, and his friend drives it off. In the simile of the javelin, δήϊοι are “enemies who destroy the spirit” (16.591; [[that-destroys-the-spirit]], [[tanaos]]). See [[ships]].
