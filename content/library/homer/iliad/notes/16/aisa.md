---
summary: Hera reminds Zeus that Sarpedon is “a man who is mortal, long since ordained to his portion” (16.441), the verse Athena will speak of Hector (22.179); Apollo tells Patroclus at the wall, “It is not your portion” to sack Troy (16.707); and in the late afternoon the Achaeans are stronger “beyond their portion” (16.780).
---

Hera's argument rests on the measure of a life: a mortal whose death is long fixed cannot be set free again from death without unsettling the gods (16.441–449; see [[not-all-the-gods-approve]] and [[divine-rescue]]). Apollo's is the measure of a deed: Troy will fall, but not by Patroclus' spear, “nor by Achilles, who is far better than you” (16.707–709; see [[sack-of-troy-foretold]]). ὑπὲρ αἶσαν, “beyond their portion”, is the phrase Hector used to Andromache, “no man will hurl me down to Hades beyond my portion” (6.487); here it says that the Achaeans prevail more than was given them, in the hour before Patroclus dies (16.777–780).
