---
summary: Both deer of Book 16 are already dead in their similes: the great antlered stag that a pack of wolves has torn in the mountains, as the Myrmidons go out to battle (16.156–163), and the slain deer over which two hungry lions fight, as Hector and Patroclus fight over the body of Cebriones (16.756–758).
---

**The wolves' stag.** οἵ τʼ ἔλαφον κεραὸν μέγαν οὔρεσι δῃώσαντες / δάπτουσιν, “that have cut down a great antlered stag in the mountains / and tear at it” (16.158–159; see [[like-wolves]] and [[antlered]]). The stag is the beast of the hunt in other similes too, run down by jackals once a hunter's arrow has struck it (11.474–481; see [[the-jackals-and-the-stag]]) and saved by a rock or a forest when dogs and country men chase it (15.271–276).

**The lions' deer.** ὥ τʼ ὄρεος κορυφῇσι περὶ κταμένης ἐλάφοιο / ἄμφω πεινάοντε μέγα φρονέοντε μάχεσθον, “which on the peaks of a mountain, over a slain deer, / both of them hungry, fight in high spirits” (16.757–758; see [[two-lions]]). The deer is the dead charioteer Cebriones, and the two lions are Hector and Patroclus. In both similes the deer is a carcass, the meat of predators, and the predators are warriors (see [[wolves]] and [[lion]]).
