summary: Achilles hopes to loose “the sacred veil of Troy,” Patroclus drags Thestor from his chariot like a man drawing “a sacred fish” from the sea, and Hector knows “the sacred scales of Zeus”; the book adds ἱερὰ κρήδεμνα and ἱρὰ τάλαντα.
grc: ["ἱερὰ κρήδεμνα", "ἱρὰ τάλαντα"]
---

**The veil.** “so that we alone might loose the sacred veil of Troy!” (16.100; [[father-zeus]]). The κρήδεμνα are the battlements of the city as a woman's head-veil, and to loose them is to take it (see the translation's note on 16.97–100; [[veil]], [[troy]]).

**The fish.** Patroclus drags Thestor from his chariot on his spear, “as when a man / sitting on a jutting rock drags a sacred fish / out of the sea onto the shore with line and gleaming bronze” (16.406–408; [[the-fisherman]], [[gleaming-bronze]], [[thestor-son-of-enops]]). No reason is given for calling the fish sacred; in the Odyssey ἱερός is said of a man's strength (see the translation's note on 16.407).

**The scales.** When Zeus puts a craven heart into Hector, he flees, “for he knew the sacred scales of Zeus” (16.658; [[the-scales-of-zeus]], [[hector]]). The phrases ἱερὰ κρήδεμνα and ἱρὰ τάλαντα are new to the entry.
