---
summary: The choice of Sarpedon's speech at the wall is decided in Book 16. Zeus does not save his son, and Sarpedon dies in the front of the fighting, charging Glaucus, “my friend”, as at the wall, to fight over his body (16.431–461, 16.492–501).
---

At the wall he had said that, since no mortal can escape the fates of death, they must go and either win glory or give it to another (12.322–328). His last speech gives the other side of the same bargain: if the Achaeans strip him, he will be a shame and a reproach to Glaucus all his days (16.498–500; see [[oneidos]] and [[exhortation]]). Glaucus, in his rebuke of Hector, praises him in the terms of the speech, a king “who guarded Lycia with his judgments and his strength” (16.542; see [[kingship-and-authority]]).
