**The attendants.** Patroclus is “the brave attendant of the swift-footed grandson of Aeacus” as the Myrmidons rush on around him (16.165; [[grandson-of-aeacus]]), and Zeus plans that “the good attendant of Achilles, son of Peleus” drive the Trojans back (16.653; [[son-of-peleus]]). Thrasymelus, whom Patroclus kills, was “the good attendant of lord Sarpedon” (16.464; [[thrasymelus]]). ἠῢς θεράπων is said once of each side.

**The fallen.** The sons of Amisodarus are “brave companions of Sarpedon” (16.327; [[amisodarus]]); Sarpedon complains that Patroclus “has loosened the knees of many brave men” (16.425); Epeigeus fled his city “having slain a brave cousin of his” (16.573; [[epeigeus]], [[exile-and-refuge]]); and when Glaucus kills Bathycles, grief seizes the Achaeans “that a brave man had fallen” (16.600; [[bathycles]]).

**The speeches.** Patroclus rebukes Meriones, “why do you, brave as you are, say these things?” (16.627; [[meriones]]), and Hector mocks the dying Patroclus, “not even Achilles, brave as he is, has helped you” (16.837; [[wretch]]). The concessive ἐσθλὸς ἐών is in each case a reproach.
