---
summary: Hera asks Zeus whether he would set Sarpedon free “from grim-sounding death.”
---

“A man who is mortal, long since ordained to his portion, / do you wish to set free again from grim-sounding death? / Do it; but not all of us other gods approve” (16.441–443; [[most-dread]], [[not-all-the-gods-approve]], [[aisa]]). Athena will say the same three verses of Hector (22.179–181). Death is named by its hateful din in the argument against saving a man from it. See [[sarpedon]] and [[hera]].
