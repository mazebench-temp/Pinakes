---
summary: Hector, over the dying Patroclus, says that Patroclus meant to “take away the day of freedom from the Trojan women”, and that he himself wards off from the Trojans “the day of compulsion” (ἦμαρ ἀναγκαῖον, 16.831–836), a phrase found only here.
grc: ["ἦμαρ ἀναγκαῖον"]
en: [the day of compulsion]
---

The second phrase answers the first. The women's day of freedom is lost when their city falls, and the day of compulsion is the day of slavery that follows; Hector's boast is that his spear keeps it off, as he told Andromache that he would rather be dead than see her led away (6.454–465; see [[slavery-and-freedom]] and [[captive-women]]).
