---
summary: Hera bids Zeus “send Death and sweet Sleep” to carry Sarpedon's body to Lycia; the book adds νήδυμον ὕπνον.
grc: ["νήδυμον ὕπνον"]
---

“but when his soul and his life have left him, / send Death and sweet Sleep to carry him / until they come to the land of wide Lycia” (16.453–455; [[sleep]], [[death]], [[wide-lycia]]). Sleep is a person here, the twin of Death (16.672; [[the-death-of-sarpedon]]), and he keeps the epithet of the sleep that falls on men's eyes. The accusative νήδυμον ὕπνον is new to the entry.
