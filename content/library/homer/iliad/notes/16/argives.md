---
summary: Achilles asks whether Patroclus weeps for the Argives perishing at the ships “because of their own transgression”, and wishes that not one of them, nor of the Trojans, might escape death, so that he and Patroclus alone might take Troy; Patroclus, rousing the Myrmidons, calls Achilles “by far the best of the Argives”; Apollo sends turmoil among them as Hector drives at Patroclus (16.17–18, 16.32, 16.68–69, 16.97–100, 16.271–272, 16.729–730).
---

**Their own transgression.** “Or are you grieving for the Argives, how they are perishing / beside the hollow ships because of their own transgression?” (16.17–18; see [[achilles]] and [[patroclus]]). ὑπερβασίη, “transgression”, is the word Menelaus used when he feared that one of Priam's sons might violate the oaths of Zeus (3.105–107); Achilles uses it of his own army. Patroclus answers that the Argives need him: “What good will anyone have of you, even one born hereafter, / if you do not ward off shameful ruin from the Argives?” (16.31–32; see [[shameful-ruin]]). Achilles describes them in their plight, “pressed back / against the shore of the sea, holding only a little portion of land still, / the Argives” (16.67–69).

**Not one of them.** Achilles ends his answer with a wish: “If only, father Zeus and Athena and Apollo, / not one of the Trojans would escape death, of all there are, / nor one of the Argives, but we two might escape destruction, / so that we alone might loose the sacred veil of Troy!” (16.97–100; see [[if-only-father-zeus]]). The army he is sending Patroclus to save is included in the wish for death.

**The best of the Argives.** Patroclus, leading the Myrmidons out, asks them to fight “so that we may honor the son of Peleus, who is by far the best / of the Argives beside the ships” (16.271–272; see [[best-of-the-achaeans]]). In the last act of the book Apollo goes into the throng “and sent evil turmoil among the Argives, / and granted glory to the Trojans and Hector” (16.729–730; see [[apollo]] and [[hector]]).
