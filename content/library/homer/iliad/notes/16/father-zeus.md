---
summary: Achilles wishes, “If only, father Zeus and Athena and Apollo,” that he and Patroclus alone might take Troy, and the cup from which he pours to no god “except father Zeus” serves his libation and prayer before Patroclus goes out.
---

**The wish.** Achilles ends his instructions to Patroclus with a wish in the old form of the invocation: “If only, father Zeus and Athena and Apollo, / not one of the Trojans would escape death, of all there are, / nor one of the Argives, but we two might escape destruction, / so that we alone might loose the sacred veil of Troy!” (16.97–100; [[if-only-father-zeus]], [[athena]], [[apollo]], [[hieros]]). Agamemnon wished in the same verse for ten counselors like Nestor (2.371–372), and Nestor for his youth (7.132–133); Achilles wishes away both armies.

**The cup.** In the chest of Thetis Achilles keeps a cup, “and no other / of men drank the sparkling wine from it, / nor did he pour libations from it to any god, except father Zeus” (16.225–227; [[libation]], [[to-keep-off-the-wind]]). He cleanses it, prays, and puts it away again: “Now when he had poured the libation and prayed to father Zeus, / he went back into the hut, and put the cup away in the chest” (16.253–254). Between the two verses Zeus hears and grants half the prayer (16.249–252; [[counselor]], [[plan-of-zeus]]). See [[zeus]] and [[achilles]].
