summary: Menelaus and Patroclus loosen the limbs of Thoas and Pronous with the same blow, a spear in the chest “where it was bared beside the shield” (16.312 = 16.400), and Patroclus Thrasymelus' with a spear in the lower belly (16.465). The limbs of Lycon are loosened when his head is all but cut off; and Patroclus' own “shining limbs were loosened beneath him” when Apollo has struck him, before he is wounded at all.
grc: ["λῦσε μένος", "ὑπέλυντο δὲ γυῖα", "λύθεν δʼ ὑπὸ φαίδιμα γυῖα"]
en: [loosed his strength, his shining limbs were loosened]
---

The half-line closes three killings (16.312, 16.400, 16.465), and its other forms follow the sword. Ajax, son of Oileus, takes Cleobulus alive and kills him on the spot: “he loosed his strength, striking him on the neck with his hilted sword” (λῦσε μένος, 16.332; see [[menos]]). Peneleos' sword goes so deep into Lycon's neck that only the skin holds, “and the head hung to one side, and his limbs were loosened” (16.340–341). Sarpedon says of the unknown man in Achilles' armor that “he has loosened the knees of many brave men” (16.425), the words Aeneas used of Diomedes (5.176; see [[gounata]]).

The last use is not a death. Apollo has struck Patroclus' back with the flat of his hand and stripped him of his armor, “And blind folly seized his mind, and his shining limbs were loosened beneath him, / and he stood amazed” (16.805–806; see [[ate]]). The formula of the dying man comes before the wound: Euphorbus and Hector have still to strike.
