---
summary: The book is framed by two broken spears. Hector shears the point from Ajax's ash spear, and the fire falls on the ship (16.114–123); Apollo breaks Patroclus' spear in his hands, and he is killed (16.801–802). Between them Patroclus leaves behind the one spear he cannot wield, the Pelian ash, and fights with two of his own.

---

**Ajax's spear.** Hector comes close and strikes Ajax's ash spear “with his great sword, behind the point, at the socket”; Ajax brandishes the docked shaft to no purpose, and the bronze point falls to the ground with a clang (16.114–118). Ajax knows in it “the work of the gods” and draws back, and the Trojans throw fire on the ship (16.119–123; see [[ajax]] and [[ships]]).

**Achilles' spear.** Patroclus takes two spears of his own, and not the spear of the grandson of Aeacus, “heavy, huge, and massive”, which no other Achaean could wield (16.139–142; see [[the-pelian-ash]] and [[arming-scene]]). His own spear is called by the same words when Apollo breaks it: “And the long-shadowed spear was all broken in his hands, / heavy, huge, and massive, tipped with bronze” (16.801–802).

**Throws.** Aeneas' throw at Meriones repeats his throw at Idomeneus in Book 13: Meriones, “looking straight at it, avoided the bronze spear”, and “Aeneas' spear went quivering into the earth, / since it had sped in vain from his strong hand” (16.610, 16.614–615 = 13.503–505; see [[aeneas]]). Euphorbus strikes Patroclus in the back from close by and snatches his ash spear out of the flesh before he runs (16.806–814), and Hector's spear goes through the lowest part of the flank; he draws it out of the dead man with his heel on him (16.820–821, 16.862–863).
