---
summary: When the Trojans give way and the leaders each kill a man, “warlike Menelaus” is the second: he stabs Thoas in the chest where it is bared beside the shield (16.311–312).
refs: [16.312]
---

ἀτὰρ Μενέλαος ἀρήϊος οὖτα Θόαντα / στέρνον γυμνωθέντα παρʼ ἀσπίδα, λῦσε δὲ γυῖα, “And warlike Menelaus stabbed Thoas / in the chest, where it was bared beside the shield, and loosened his limbs” (16.311–312; see [[thoas-killed-by-menelaus]], [[warlike]] and [[loosened-his-limbs]]). He comes second in the list of leaders who “each killed his man” as the battle scatters, after Patroclus and before Meges, Antilochus and Thrasymedes (16.306–351; see [[patroclus]], [[meges]] and [[androktasia]]). The list recalls the first of its kind in the poem, when “each of the leaders took his man” in Book 5 and Menelaus killed Scamandrius the hunter (5.37–38, 5.49–58; see [[scamandrius]]). The second verse returns ninety verses later for Patroclus' first victim between the ships and the wall, Pronous (16.400 = 16.312; see [[pronous]]). Menelaus has no other part in the book; in the next he stands over Patroclus' body (17.1–8).
