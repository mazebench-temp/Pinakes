{{quote:f61bc257-d0e1-5016-846d-64620da820c8}}

Πάτροκλος δʼ Ἀχιλῆϊ παρίστατο ποιμένι λαῶν / δάκρυα θερμὰ χέων ὥς τε κρήνη μελάνυδρος, / ἥ τε κατʼ αἰγίλιπος πέτρης δνοφερὸν χέει ὕδωρ: “and Patroclus came and stood beside Achilles, shepherd of the people, / shedding hot tears like a spring of dark water / that pours its murky water down a sheer rock” (16.2–4). The verse of the rock is Book 9's unchanged (16.4 = 9.15), and so is the spring; only the tears differ. Agamemnon stood up “shedding tears” (δάκρυ χέων), and Patroclus comes “shedding hot tears” (δάκρυα θερμὰ χέων; see [[hot-tears]] and [[shedding-tears]]). Both men then speak “groaning heavily” (βαρὺ στενάχων, 9.16, 16.20; see [[groaning-heavily]]).

Patroclus has come straight from the hut of Eurypylus, where he was tending a wound, after he saw the Trojans rushing on the wall and heard the shouting and rout of the Danaans (15.390–405; see [[eurypylus]]). The book opens on the fight around the ship (16.1), and his tears are for the army: “such grief has overpowered the Achaeans”, he tells Achilles, and names the wounded, Diomedes, Odysseus, Agamemnon and Eurypylus (16.22–27). Achilles pities him and answers with a simile of his own, of a little girl who runs beside her mother in tears and begs to be picked up (16.5–11; see [[like-a-girl]]). The narrator's simile and Achilles' stand side by side, and both are of weeping.

The rock comes back in Patroclus' reproach. A man weeping like water that pours down a rock tells the man who does not weep that he is no son of Peleus and Thetis: “the gray-green sea bore you, / and the lofty rocks, since your mind is harsh” (πέτραι τʼ ἠλίβατοι, 16.34–35; see [[achilles]]). In Book 9 Agamemnon's tears opened the night of the embassy, and Achilles refused the envoys (9.9–28, 9.307–429; see [[the-embassy-to-achilles]]). Patroclus' tears win what the envoys could not: Achilles lets him go out in his armor at the head of the Myrmidons (16.64–100; see [[patroclus]]).

Springs return in the book's similes. The Myrmidons arm like wolves that go in a pack to drink from “a spring of dark water” (κρήνης μελανύδρου, 16.160–161; see [[like-wolves]] and [[of-dark-water]]), and Patroclus dies like a boar that fights a lion for the water of “a little spring” on a mountain (16.823–826; see [[the-lion-and-the-boar]]).
