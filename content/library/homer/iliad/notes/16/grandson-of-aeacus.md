---
summary: The patronymic names Achilles through what is his: the corselet and the spear of the arming, his two attendants Patroclus and Automedon, and, in Patroclus' last words, the man who will kill Hector.
---

**The arming.** Patroclus puts on “the inlaid corselet, starry, of the swift-footed grandson of Aeacus” (16.134), but “Only the spear of the noble grandson of Aeacus he did not take” (16.140; [[the-pelian-ash]], [[blameless]], [[arming-scene]]).

**The attendants.** The Myrmidon leaders rush on “around the brave attendant of the swift-footed grandson of Aeacus” (16.165; [[brave]]), Patroclus; at the end Hector goes after Automedon, “the godlike attendant of the swift-footed grandson of Aeacus” (16.865; [[antitheos]], [[automedon]]).

**The prophecy.** Patroclus foretells Hector's death, “to be brought down at the hands of Achilles, noble grandson of Aeacus” (16.854; [[death-of-achilles-foretold]], [[hector]]). All five uses end the verse, with ποδώκεος or ἀμύμονος before Αἰακίδαο. At 16.15 Αἰακίδης is Peleus himself, Aeacus' son, and belongs to [[aeacus]]. See [[achilles]] and [[swift-footed]].
