---
summary: Achilles hopes for “splendid gifts” from the Achaeans, Hermes gives Polymele “a splendid son,” and Achilles' horses are twice “the splendid gifts that the gods gave to Peleus.”
---

**Gifts.** Achilles tells Patroclus to win him honor so that the Danaans “send back to me the beautiful girl / and give splendid gifts besides” (16.85–86; [[perikalles]], [[briseis]], [[the-gifts-of-agamemnon]]). The gifts were offered in Book 9 and refused.

**A son.** Hermes “gave her a splendid son, / Eudorus, surpassingly swift at running, and a fighter” (16.185–186; [[akaketa]], [[eudorus]]).

**The horses.** “And straight over the ditch leapt the swift horses, / immortal, the splendid gifts that the gods gave to Peleus” (16.380–381), and in the last line of the book they carry Automedon away, in the same words (16.866–867; [[the-horses-of-achilles]], [[swift-footed]]).
