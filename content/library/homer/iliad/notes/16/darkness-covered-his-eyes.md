---
summary: The deaths of Book 16 are told with the whole range of the formulas of darkness. Darkness covers the eyes of Amphiclus and Maris, a mist is poured over Acamas' eyes, the black cloud of death enfolds Erymas, death is poured around Erylaus and Epeigeus, and hateful darkness seizes Laogonus.
grc: ["μέλαν νέφος ἀμφεκάλυψεν"]
en: [the black cloud of death enfolded him]
---

The leaders' killings at the ships end one after another in the formulas (16.306–350; see [[androktasia]]): “and darkness covered his eyes” for Amphiclus, killed by Meges, and for Maris, killed by Thrasymedes (16.316, 16.325); “and a mist was poured over his eyes” for Acamas, killed by Meriones (16.344; see [[achlys]]); and for Erymas, whose teeth Idomeneus' spear shakes out, “and the black cloud of death enfolded him” (θανάτου δὲ μέλαν νέφος ἀμφεκάλυψεν, 16.350). The Odyssey uses the half-verse once, when Menelaus says that nothing would have parted him and Odysseus until the black cloud of death enfolded them (*Odyssey* 4.178–180). Erylaus, killed by Patroclus with a stone, and Epeigeus, killed by Hector with a stone, die in the same verse, “and death that destroys the spirit was poured around him” (16.414 = 16.580; see [[stone-throwing]]); for Laogonus, struck under the jaw by Meriones, “hateful darkness seized him” (16.607).

The great deaths of the book are told otherwise: Sarpedon and Patroclus are covered by “the end of death” (16.502, 16.855; see [[the-end-of-death]]), and Cleobulus' eyes are seized by “purple death” (16.333–334; see [[purple-death]]).
