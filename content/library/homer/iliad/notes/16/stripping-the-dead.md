summary: The armor is the stake of the book's last fights. Sarpedon, dying, fears that the Achaeans will strip him; the Achaeans take his armor, and Cebriones', and the gods strip Patroclus before he is killed. Hector, in the next book, will wear Achilles' armor taken from Patroclus' body.

**Sarpedon.** He warns Glaucus that it will be his shame for life “if the Achaeans / strip me of my armor” (16.499–500; see [[oneidos]]), and Glaucus warns the Trojans that the Myrmidons will take the armor and defile the body (16.545). Patroclus wants exactly that, to take the armor from his shoulders and defile him (16.559–560). Zeus ponders whether Hector should kill Patroclus over the body and take his armor (16.650), and decides otherwise; the Achaeans take Sarpedon's armor, and Patroclus sends it to the ships (16.663–665). The body is not defiled: Apollo carries it away (see [[fighting-over-the-body]]).

**Cebriones and Patroclus.** The Achaeans drag Cebriones out of the missiles “and took the armor from his shoulders” (16.782). The same words end Patroclus' account of his death, with the gods as subject: Zeus and Apollo brought him down easily, “for they themselves took the armor from my shoulders” (16.846; see [[arming-scene]]). He was stripped before he was killed. He ranks his killers, fate and Apollo first, then Euphorbus, “and you are the third to slay me” (ἐξεναρίζεις, 16.850), where the verb of stripping is the verb of killing.
