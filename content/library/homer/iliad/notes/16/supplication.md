summary: Patroclus begs Achilles for his armor, “So he spoke, begging, great fool that he was; for truly he was / begging for his own evil death and doom” (16.46–47). The Myrmidon Epeigeus, who had killed a cousin, came as a suppliant to Peleus and Thetis, and they sent him to Troy with Achilles (16.573–576).
grc: [λισσόμενος, λιτέσθαι, ἱκέτευσε]
en: [begging, came as a suppliant]
---

The two verbs of 16.46–47 are the verbs of supplication, λίσσομαι and λίτομαι, and the narrator's comment turns the entreaty of a friend into one that is granted too well (see [[nepios]]). Epeigeus' story is the exile's: he was lord in Budeum, killed “a brave cousin of his”, and came as a suppliant (ἱκέτευσε) to the house of Peleus, as Phoenix came to Peleus fleeing his father, and Patroclus himself came as a boy after killing a playmate (9.478–484, 23.85–90; see [[exile-and-refuge]]). Peleus' house received them and sent them to Troy with his son.
