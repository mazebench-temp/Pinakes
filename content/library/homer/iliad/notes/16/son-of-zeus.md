---
summary: Glaucus laments “Sarpedon, son of Zeus,” whom his father does not defend; Apollo, “son of Zeus,” rouses Hector in the likeness of Asius and loosens Patroclus' corselet.
---

**Sarpedon.** Glaucus prays: “And the best of men has perished, / Sarpedon, son of Zeus; and he does not defend even his own son” (16.521–522; [[glaucus]], [[sarpedon]]). The title is the reproach: the son of Zeus has died without his father's help. In Book 5 the wounded Sarpedon was named so as he begged Hector not to leave him as prey for the Danaans (5.683–684).

**Apollo.** “in his likeness Apollo, son of Zeus, spoke to him” (16.720; [[asius-son-of-dymas]], [[hector]]), and “And lord Apollo, son of Zeus, loosened his corselet” (16.804; [[corselet]], [[patroclus]]). The half-verse ἄναξ Διὸς υἱὸς Ἀπόλλων ends the stripping of Patroclus, as it introduced Apollo's words to Athena in Book 7 (7.23). See [[apollo]].
