---
summary: At the turn of the battle the poet calls on the Muses for the last time in the Iliad, to tell how fire first fell on the ships of the Achaeans.
passages:
  - 16.112-124 | Fire on the ships
---

“Tell me now, Muses, you who have your homes on Olympus, / how fire first fell upon the ships of the Achaeans” (16.112–113; see [[invocation-of-the-muses]] and [[homes-on-olympus]]). The verse that opened the Catalogue of Ships (2.484) is used here for the fourth and last time in the Iliad, after the invocations of Book 11 and Book 14 (11.218, 14.508), and again the poet asks for a first: who first faced Agamemnon, who first took bloody spoils, how fire first fell on the ships. The answer follows at once. Hector lops off the head of Ajax's spear, Ajax knows the work of the gods and draws back, and the Trojans throw fire on the ship of Protesilaus, “and at once an unquenchable flame poured over it” (16.114–124; see [[hector]], [[ajax]] and [[protesilaus]]). It is the moment Zeus waited for in Book 15, “to see with his eyes the blaze of a burning ship” (15.599–600; see [[zeus]] and [[plan-of-zeus]]), and the moment at which Achilles sends Patroclus out (16.124–129).
