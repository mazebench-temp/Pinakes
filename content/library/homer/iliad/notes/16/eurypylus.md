---
summary: Patroclus names Eurypylus last among the wounded chiefs, “shot in the thigh with an arrow”, in the verse Nestor used in Book 11; he had tended the wound himself until he saw the Trojans storm the wall (16.27).
---

βέβληται δὲ καὶ Εὐρύπυλος κατὰ μηρὸν ὀϊστῷ, “and Eurypylus too has been shot in the thigh with an arrow” (16.27 = 11.662; see [[the-wounded-chiefs]]). Patroclus repeats Nestor's list, but this name he knows at first hand: running back from Nestor's hut he met Eurypylus limping out of the battle, cut the arrow from his thigh, and sat with him, spreading drugs on the wound, until he saw the Trojans storm the wall (11.806–848, 15.390–405; see [[patroclus]] and [[healing]]). “The healers, with their many drugs, are busy over them” (16.28): Patroclus was one of them. This is the last time the Iliad names him.
