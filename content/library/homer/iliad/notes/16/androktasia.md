summary: Patroclus kills twenty-seven men by name in Book 16, and twenty-seven more in his last three charges, “three times he killed nine men”. Around his killings come the leaders' list at the ships, each man killing his man, and single deaths told at length: the sons of Amisodarus, Sarpedon, the Myrmidons Epeigeus and Bathycles, the priest's son Laogonus, Cebriones, and Patroclus himself.

**The leaders' list.** When the Trojans are driven from the ship, “man killed man, as the battle scattered, / among the leaders” (16.306–307; see [[polyptoton]]). Nine Achaean leaders kill nine Trojans in some forty verses, one each: Patroclus, Menelaus, Meges, Antilochus, Thrasymedes, Ajax son of Oileus, Peneleos, Meriones and Idomeneus (16.307–350; see [[menelaus]], [[meges]], [[antilochus]], [[thrasymedes]], [[ajax-son-of-oileus]], [[peneleos]], [[meriones]] and [[idomeneus]]), and the list closes on its own count, “These leaders of the Danaans each killed his man” (16.351). The blows are told with the precision of the type-scene: the spear through the thigh that breaks the bone (16.308–310), the arm torn from the shoulder (16.323–324), the sword that leaves only the skin holding the head (16.339–341), the spear in the mouth that shakes out the teeth and fills the eyes with blood (16.345–350; see [[darkness-covered-his-eyes]]). One pair has a history: Atymnius and Maris, killed by the two sons of Nestor, were Sarpedon's companions and the sons of Amisodarus, “who reared the Chimaera” (16.317–329; see [[kasignetos]]).

**Patroclus' killings.** His kills come in two kinds. Some are told one by one: Pyraechmes, the leader of the Paeonians (16.284–292), Areilycus (16.307–311), Pronous (16.399–401), Thestor, dragged from his chariot on the spear like a fish on a line (16.401–410), Erylaus, with a stone (16.411–414), Thrasymelus, Sarpedon's attendant (16.463–465), Sarpedon (16.477–505), Sthenelaus, with a stone (16.586–587), and Cebriones, with a stone (16.733–743; see [[stone-throwing]]). The rest are names in lists: nine in three verses after Erylaus (16.415–418) and nine under the narrator's question (16.692–697; see [[whom-first-whom-last]]). Then the count without names: “three times he killed nine men” (16.785; see [[three-times-and-a-fourth]]).

**Histories.** The deaths told at length on both sides carry the biographies of the type-scene. The Myrmidon Epeigeus had been lord of Budeum, killed a cousin and came as a suppliant to Peleus and Thetis, who sent him with Achilles to Troy (16.570–580; see [[exile-and-refuge]] and [[supplication]]). Bathycles, son of Chalcon, lived in Hellas and stood out among the Myrmidons for his wealth (16.593–599). Laogonus was the son of Onetor, priest of Idaean Zeus, who was honored by the people like a god (16.603–607; see [[priesthood]]). Cebriones was a bastard son of Priam (16.738; see [[nothos]]). Euphorbus, who wounds Patroclus, is given the history of a victim, though he lives till the next book: he surpassed all of his age, and brought down twenty men from their chariots when he first came (16.808–811).
