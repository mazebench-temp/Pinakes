---
summary: The narrator turns to Patroclus four times with “horseman Patroclus”: as he begins his plea, as he taunts the fallen Cebriones, as Euphorbus strikes him, and as he answers Hector with his last words.
---

The vocative stands at the end of the verse, after the narrator's second person: “Groaning heavily, you said to him, horseman Patroclus” (16.20), as he comes to Achilles in tears ([[groaning-heavily]]); “And taunting him you spoke, horseman Patroclus” (16.744), over the fallen Cebriones ([[cebriones]]); “he was the first to throw a missile at you, horseman Patroclus” (16.812), of Euphorbus ([[euphorbus]]); and “Then you spoke to him feebly, horseman Patroclus” (16.843), as he answers Hector with his last words ([[hector]]). The four addresses run from his plea to his death ([[apostrophe]]). In the same place in the verse he is also [[urger-of-horses]] (ἱπποκέλευθε). See [[patroclus]].
