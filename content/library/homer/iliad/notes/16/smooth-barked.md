---
summary: In the simile of the winds in the forest, “oak and ash and the smooth-barked cornel” dash their branches together as the armies meet over Cebriones.
---

The cornel stands with the oak and the ash in the forest that the East and South Winds shake against each other: “oak and ash and the smooth-barked cornel, / which dash their long-pointed branches against one another / with a wondrous din” (16.767–769; [[wondrous]], [[long-edged]]). So the Trojans and Achaeans fall on each other over the body of Cebriones (16.770–771; [[cebriones]]).
