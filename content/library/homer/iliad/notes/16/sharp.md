---
summary: Sharp spears, bronze and a stone: Patroclus kills Areilycus with his “sharp spear,” Antilochus Atymnius, Patroclus' “sharp stone” strikes Cebriones, “many sharp spears” stand around his body, and Euphorbus and Hector strike Patroclus with “a sharp spear” and “the sharp bronze”; the book adds Glaucus' “sharp pains.”
grc: [ὀξείῃς]
---

**The kills.** Patroclus strikes Areilycus “with his sharp spear, and drove the bronze straight through” (16.309; [[areilycus-killed-by-patroclus]]), with the longer ὀξυόεις; “one stabbed Atymnius with his sharp spear, / Antilochus” (16.317–318; [[atymnius-son-of-amisodarus]], [[antilochus]]); and Meriones threatens Aeneas, “If I too could strike you full in the middle with the sharp bronze” (16.623; [[meriones]]).

**Cebriones.** Patroclus' stone strikes him “on the forehead with the sharp stone” (16.739; [[jagged]], [[cebriones]]), and over his body “many sharp spears were fixed around Cebriones, / and feathered arrows leaping from the bowstrings” (16.772–773; [[feathered]]).

**Patroclus.** Euphorbus strikes him “from behind, in the back, with a sharp spear” (16.806; [[euphorbus]]), and Hector sees him “giving ground, struck by the sharp bronze” (16.819; [[hector]]).

**Glaucus' pains.** Glaucus prays that his arm, “on every side / is pierced with sharp pains” (16.517–518; [[far-shooter]]); ὀξείῃς ὀδύνῃσιν is new to the entry, as the sharp pains of Agamemnon's wound were in Book 11 (11.268–272).
