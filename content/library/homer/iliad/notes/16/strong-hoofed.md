---
summary: Apollo bids Hector “drive your strong-hoofed horses at Patroclus,” and Hector does so.
---

“But come, drive your strong-hoofed horses at Patroclus, / in the hope that you may take him, and Apollo give you triumph” (16.724–725; [[phoebus]], [[asius-son-of-dymas]]); “And Hector let the other Danaans be, and did not slay them, / but he drove his strong-hoofed horses at Patroclus” (16.731–732; [[hector]]). The command and its fulfillment use the same verse-end, with which Sthenelus “drove the strong-hoofed horses after the son of Tydeus” in Book 5 (5.329). See [[horses]].
