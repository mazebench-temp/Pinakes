---
summary: Achilles sends Patroclus out to win him “great honor and glory” (16.84) but forbids him to go on fighting if Zeus grants him glory (16.87–90); he prays Zeus to send glory with him (16.241). The glory goes the other way: Apollo grants it to the Trojans and Hector against Patroclus (16.730).
---

The word marks the limit Achilles sets. Glory for Patroclus is to be glory for Achilles, and only up to the ships: “if / the loud-thundering husband of Hera grants you to win glory, / do not long to fight without me / against the war-loving Trojans; you will make me more dishonored” (16.87–90; see [[time]]). Zeus grants the prayer for glory and refuses the return (16.249–252; see [[prayer]]). When Patroclus has gone past the limit, Apollo goes into the throng, “and sent evil turmoil among the Argives, / and granted glory to the Trojans and Hector” (16.729–730; see [[apollo]]).
