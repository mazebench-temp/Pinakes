summary: Patroclus weeps “like a spring of dark water,” and the wolves of the Myrmidons' simile go in a pack “to a spring of dark water”; the book adds μελανύδρου.
grc: [μελανύδρου]
---

**The tears.** “shedding hot tears like a spring of dark water / that pours its murky water down a sheer rock” (16.3–4; [[hot-tears]], [[dnopheros]], [[sheer]]), the verses of Agamemnon's tears in Book 9 (9.14–15).

**The wolves.** The Myrmidons arming are like wolves that have killed a stag, “and they go in a pack to a spring of dark water / to lap with their thin tongues the dark water / at its surface, belching out the gore of blood” (16.160–162; [[wolves]], [[antlered]], [[eating-raw-flesh]]). The spring of the opening simile, where a friend's tears flow, returns as the place where beasts drink after the kill. The genitive μελανύδρου is new to the entry. See [[myrmidons]].
