summary: The Trojans throw “tireless fire” on the ship, the river god Spercheius is “tireless,” and in the simile of Patroclus' death the lion overpowers “a tireless boar”; the book adds ἀκάμας.
grc: ["Σπερχειῷ ἀκάμαντι", "σῦν ἀκάμαντα"]
---

**The fire.** “And they threw tireless fire / on the swift ship, and at once an unquenchable flame poured over it” (16.122–123; [[swift-ships]], [[blazing-fire]]): the moment that Zeus has waited for since Book 15 (15.599–600; [[counselor]]).

**The river and the boar.** The shorter adjective ἀκάμας, of the same root, comes twice: Polydora bore Menesthius “to tireless Spercheius, a woman who lay with a god” (16.176; [[spercheius]], [[polydora]]), and “As when a lion overpowers a tireless boar in battle” (16.823; [[boar]], [[lion]]), the likeness of Hector killing Patroclus. Both are new to the entry, written as phrases, because the name Ἀκάμας, of the Trojan whom Meriones kills on 16.342, has the same letters ([[acamas]]).
