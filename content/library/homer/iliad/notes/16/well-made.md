summary: Patroclus sets “a well-made helmet” on his head, Ajax's helmet rings on “its well-made plates,” the Lycians and Achaeans clash with “well-made shields of oxhide,” and Cebriones falls “from the well-made chariot”; the book adds εὐποίητος.
grc: ["εὐποίηθʼ", εὐποιητάων]
---

**The helmets.** In the arming, “and on his mighty head he set a well-made helmet / with a horsehair crest” (16.137–138; [[mighty]], [[horsehair-crested]], [[arming-scene]]). Ajax, worn out at the ship, holds on while his helmet “as it was struck, rang terribly, and it was struck again and again / on its well-made plates” (16.105–106; [[phaeinos]], [[ajax]]).

**The shields.** Over Sarpedon's body the thudding rises from the earth, “of bronze and hide and well-made shields of oxhide, / as they stabbed at one another with swords and double-pointed spears” (16.636–637; [[double-pointed]], [[wide-wayed-earth]]).

**The chariot.** Struck on the forehead by Patroclus' stone, Cebriones “like a diver, / fell from the well-made chariot, and his spirit left his bones” (16.742–743; [[cebriones]], [[like-a-diver]]), with εὐεργής, another word of the entry.

εὐποίητος, “well-made,” is new to the entry, in εὐποίηθʼ (16.106) and εὐποιητάων (16.636).
