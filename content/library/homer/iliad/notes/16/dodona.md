# Δωδωναῖε, Δωδώνης, δυσχειμέρου (16.233–234): the only other Homeric forms are Δωδώνην δυσχείμερον (2.750, in the entry)
# and Δωδώνην (Od. 14.327, 19.296).

When Achilles has poured wine from the cup he keeps for Zeus alone, he prays (see [[zeus]], [[cup]] and [[prayer]]):

{{quote:21d392f3-75db-5c4b-87a5-77b034892276}}

He calls on Zeus by his sanctuary in the mountains of the northwest, far from Troy, as the Trojans and Achaeans call on “Father Zeus, who rule from Ida” (3.276; see [[ida]]): Δωδωναῖε, “of Dodona”, and Πελασγικέ, “Pelasgian” (see [[pelasgians]]). The god is “ruling over wintry Dodona” (Δωδώνης μεδέων δυσχειμέρου), the epithet that Dodona has in the Catalogue of Ships (see [[wintry]]), where the Enienes and Perrhaebi under Gouneus “had made their homes around wintry Dodona” (2.750; see [[gouneus]]). δυσχείμερος, “wintry”, is said of Dodona only, in these two verses. Around the god dwell the Selli, his ὑποφῆται, “interpreters”, who go with unwashed feet and sleep on the ground (16.234–235; see [[selli]] and [[with-unwashed-feet]]). The poem does not say why Achilles prays to the Zeus of Dodona rather than to the Zeus of Ida; the Pelasgian title belongs to his own country too, for the first region of his kingdom in the Catalogue is Pelasgian Argos (2.681; see [[pelasgian-argos]]). Strabo takes the address as proof that Dodona was a foundation of the Pelasgians (*Geography* 5.2.4, 7.7.10; see [[strabo]]).
