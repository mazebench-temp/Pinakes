---
summary: “The son of crooked-counseling Cronus” pities Sarpedon and Patroclus as they rush at each other.
---

“And seeing them the son of crooked-counseling Cronus pitied them, / and he spoke to Hera, his sister and wife” (16.431–432; [[son-of-cronus]], [[hera]], [[pity]]). The verse-end Κρόνου πάϊς ἀγκυλομήτεω has elsewhere named the god who gives a scepter, a sign or a burden: the scepter of kingship (2.205, 9.37), the serpent turned to stone at Aulis (2.319), the star sent as a portent (4.75), the stone made light in Hector's hand (12.450). Here the son of the wily god feels pity for his son and for the man who will kill him, and the scene of his choice follows ([[the-death-of-sarpedon]], [[sarpedon]]). See [[cronus]] and [[zeus]].
