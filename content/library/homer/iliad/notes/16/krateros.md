summary: “Mighty Diomedes” is wounded, “the strong might of Echecles” takes Polymele to his house, the battle is “fierce” seven times, Meriones calls Aeneas “strong though you are,” Apollo takes the shape of a man “young and strong,” and Glaucus' wound is “severe”; the book adds καρτερόν.
grc: [καρτερόν]
en: [severe]
---

**Men.** “The son of Tydeus, mighty Diomedes, has been shot” (16.25; [[son-of-tydeus]]). Polymele, after Eudorus' birth, was taken by “the strong might of Echecles, son of Actor” (16.189; [[echecles]], [[bride-price]]). Meriones answers Aeneas: “at once, strong though you are and trusting in your hands, / you would give me triumph” (16.624–625; [[of-the-famous-horses]]). Apollo comes to Hector “in the likeness of a man, young and strong” (16.716; [[asius-son-of-dymas]]).

**The battle.** The fierce battle, κρατερὴ ὑσμίνη, is the book's formula for the fighting over Sarpedon and Patroclus: Hera warns that other gods will want to send their sons “away from the fierce battle” (16.447) and bids Zeus leave Sarpedon “in the fierce battle” (16.451); Zeus stretches deadly night over it (16.567; [[deadly]]) and never turns his eyes from it (16.645, 16.648; [[phaeinos]]); the Trojans and Danaans join in it over Cebriones (16.764), and “Phoebus met you in the fierce battle” (16.788; [[phoebus]]). Zeus “stretched tight the mighty strife” over Sarpedon (16.662; [[son-of-cronus]]).

**The wound.** Glaucus prays to Apollo for “this severe wound” (16.517), and “heal me this severe wound” (16.523; [[far-shooter]], [[healing]]). καρτερός is the same adjective with its letters transposed, and the form καρτερόν is new to the entry.
