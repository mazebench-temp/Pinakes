summary: Polymele, mother of Eudorus, was “beautiful in the dance”, and Hermes fell in love with her when he saw her “among the girls singing and dancing / in the dance of Artemis” (16.180–183). In the battle Aeneas mocks Meriones as a dancer (16.617; see [[not-to-a-dance-but-to-fight]]).
grc: [χορῷ, μελπομένῃσιν, ὀρχηστήν]
en: [in the dance, singing and dancing, dancer]
---

The dance of Artemis is a girls' chorus, in which a god may see a bride, as Hermes sees Polymele (see [[hermes]] and [[the-birth-of-eudorus]]). Aeneas' taunt turns the dance against a fighter: “Meriones, dancer though you are, my spear / would soon have stopped you for good, if I had hit you” (16.617–618). The word comes once more in the Iliad, when Priam calls the sons left to him “liars and dancers” (ψεῦσταί τʼ ὀρχησταί τε, 24.261), and Polydamas had named dancing among the gifts the gods give to men other than warriors (13.731; see [[different-gifts]]).
