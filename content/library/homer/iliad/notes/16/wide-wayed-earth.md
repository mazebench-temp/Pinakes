---
summary: Over Sarpedon's body the din of the fighting rises like the noise of woodcutters, “up from the wide-wayed earth.”
---

“And as the din of woodcutters rises / in the glens of a mountain, and the sound is heard from far away, / so the thudding rose from them, up from the wide-wayed earth, / of bronze and hide and well-made shields of oxhide” (16.633–636; [[well-made]], [[double-pointed]], [[fighting-over-the-body]]). The formula χθονὸς εὐρυοδείης is used here for the only time in the Iliad, as the sound of the fight for the body goes up from the ground. See [[sarpedon]].
