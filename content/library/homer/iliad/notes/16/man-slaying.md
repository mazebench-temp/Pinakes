---
summary: Achilles hears “the voice of man-slaying Hector” calling to the Trojans, and Hector imagines Achilles bidding Patroclus tear “man-slaying Hector's / bloody tunic.”
---

“but the voice of man-slaying Hector, / calling to the Trojans, breaks all around” (16.77–78; [[hector]]): Achilles hears Hector where he no longer hears Agamemnon (16.76; [[son-of-atreus]]). At the end Hector boasts over the dying Patroclus and puts the epithet in Achilles' mouth: “‘Do not come back to me, Patroclus, urger of horses, / to the hollow ships, before you have torn man-slaying Hector's / bloody tunic around his chest’” (16.839–841; [[urger-of-horses]], [[bloody]], [[hollow-ships]]). Hector names himself by his own epithet in the words he invents for his enemy.
