---
summary: Four speeches are introduced with winged words, and they mark the stages of Patroclus' day: Achilles' question to him as he weeps (16.6), Glaucus' rebuke of Hector over Sarpedon (16.537), Apollo's shout at the wall of Troy (16.706), and Hector's boast over the dying Patroclus (16.829).
---

The verse is used whole three times, with the first half-verse naming the manner of the speech. Glaucus speaks “standing near him” (ἀγχοῦ δʼ ἱστάμενος, 16.537), the opening of 13.462 and 14.356. Apollo “shouted at him terribly” (δεινὰ δʼ ὁμοκλήσας, 16.706), the words with which he shouted at Diomedes in the same pattern of three attacks and a fourth (5.439) and with which Achilles shouts after Hector (20.448; see [[three-times-and-a-fourth]]). Hector speaks “boasting over him” (ἐπευχόμενος, 16.829; see [[euchomai]]), and Patroclus answers him without the formula, “feebly” (16.843; see [[apostrophe]]).
