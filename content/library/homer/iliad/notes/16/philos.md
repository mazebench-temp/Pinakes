summary: Zeus calls Sarpedon “dearest of men to me” (16.433), and the word follows the father and son: Hera speaks of every god's “own dear son” (16.447) and asks “if he is dear to you” (16.450), and Zeus sheds blood “honoring his dear son” (16.460) and stretches night over the battle “around his dear son” (16.568).
grc: [φίλτατον]
en: [dearest]
---

Among men the word joins the companions: Sarpedon, dying, “called his dear companion by name” (16.491; see [[hetairos]]), and Glaucus calls the Trojans “friends” to stand by the body (16.544). It is also the word of home: the Trojans must not take “our dear homecoming” (16.82; see [[nostos]]), and Hector imagines Patroclus carrying the Trojan women “to your dear native land” (16.832).
