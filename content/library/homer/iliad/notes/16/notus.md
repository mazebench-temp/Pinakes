---
summary: With the East Wind, the South Wind strives in the glens of a mountain to shake a deep forest, in the simile of the fight over Cebriones.
passages:
  - 16.765-771 | The East Wind and the South Wind in the forest
---

Over the body of Cebriones Hector holds the head and Patroclus the foot, and the armies fight around them “as the East Wind and the South Wind strive with each other / in the glens of a mountain to shake a deep forest” (16.762–766; see [[the-winds-in-the-forest]], [[eurus]], [[cebriones]], [[hector]] and [[patroclus]]). The trees crash together with a wondrous din, and neither side thinks of rout (16.767–771). In Book 2 the two winds blew together on the Icarian sea (2.144–146), and in Book 3 the South Wind poured mist over the mountain peaks (3.10–11); here it is matched against the East Wind, as army against army.
