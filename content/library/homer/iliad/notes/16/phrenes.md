---
summary: The φρένες are the midriff in Sarpedon's death, struck “where the midriff closes around the throbbing heart” and drawn out with the spear (16.481, 16.504), and the mind in Patroclus' end: “blind folly seized his mind” (16.805), and Hector says Achilles' orders “persuaded your mind, fool that you were” (16.842). Thestor is “struck out of his wits” before Patroclus kills him (16.403).
---

The two senses are one word. The φρένες are an organ of the chest, the midriff or the membranes around the heart, and the place where a man thinks: Zeus is divided “as I ponder in my mind” (16.435), and Hera and the dying Patroclus tell their hearers to “lay it to heart”, ἐνὶ φρεσί (16.444, 16.851; see [[lay-it-to-heart]]). In Sarpedon's death the organ is pierced and pulled out, “and the midriff followed with it” (16.504; see [[psyche]]); in Patroclus' it is seized by ἄτη (see [[ate]]).
