summary: Patroclus takes “two strong spears” in the arming; Achilles and the wasps of the simile have “a valiant heart”; Patroclus is “the valiant son of Menoetius” four times; and the mind of Zeus “puts even a valiant man to flight”; the book adds “ἄλκιμον ἄνδρα.”
grc: ["ἄλκιμον ἄνδρα"]
---

**The spears.** “And he took two strong spears that fitted his grip” (16.139; [[arming-scene]]), instead of Achilles' own spear, which no other man could wield (16.140–144; [[the-pelian-ash]]).

**The heart.** Achilles bids his Myrmidons, “There let each man who has a valiant heart fight the Trojans” (16.209), and the wasps of the simile, stirred by a passing traveler, “with valiant hearts, / all fly out and each defends its young” (16.264–265; [[wasps]]).

**The son of Menoetius.** The formula Μενοιτίου ἄλκιμος υἱός recurs through the book, from the Trojans' first sight of Patroclus to his death (16.278, 16.307, 16.626, 16.665, 16.827; [[son-of-menoetius]]).

**The mind of Zeus.** “But the mind of Zeus is always stronger than that of men; / he puts even a valiant man to flight and takes away victory / easily” (16.688–690; [[plan-of-zeus]]). The valiant man is Patroclus, whom Zeus drives on to his death in the next verse (16.691). The phrase ἄλκιμον ἄνδρα is new to the entry.
