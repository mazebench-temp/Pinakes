---
summary: Patroclus comes to Achilles “shedding hot tears like a spring of dark water.”
---

“and Patroclus came and stood beside Achilles, shepherd of the people, / shedding hot tears like a spring of dark water / that pours its murky water down a sheer rock” (16.2–4; [[of-dark-water]], [[dnopheros]], [[sheer]], [[tears]]). The simile is the one in which Agamemnon wept before the assembly in Book 9 (9.14–15), and the man who weeps now weeps for the army. The same opening, δάκρυα θερμὰ χέων, will return for those who weep for Patroclus himself (18.17, 18.235). See [[patroclus]] and [[like-a-girl]].
