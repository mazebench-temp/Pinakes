---
summary: The allies bear the first shock of Patroclus' attack: the Paeonians lose their leader Pyraechmes, and the Lycians lose Sarpedon. Glaucus rebukes Hector for forgetting the allies, “who for your sake, far from their friends and their native land, / are wasting away their lives” (16.539–540), and the Trojans grieve for Sarpedon, “foreigner though he was” (16.550).
---

ἐπικούρων, “allies” (16.538): Glaucus reproaches Hector, “now you have utterly forgotten your allies”, and complains that he does not want to help them (16.538–540; see [[glaucus]] and [[hector]]). Sarpedon made the same complaint in Book 5, that the allies did the fighting while Hector's brothers-in-law and brothers held back (5.472–492). The first man Patroclus kills is an ally, Pyraechmes, leader of the Paeonians (16.284–292; see [[pyraechmes]] and [[paeonians]]), and the greatest of the allies, Sarpedon, dies at his hands (16.462–505; see [[sarpedon]] and [[lycians]]). The Trojans mourn Sarpedon as ἕρμα πόληος, “a prop of their city”, καὶ ἀλλοδαπός περ ἐών, “foreigner though he was” (16.549–550), and Patroclus calls on the Achaeans to defile him (16.559–561). Sarpedon led “the famous allies”, ἀγακλειτῶν ἐπικούρων, against the Achaean wall (12.101), and his attendant Thrasymelus is ἀγακλειτός, “famous”, here (16.463; see [[thrasymelus]]). In Book 17 Hector tells the allies that he did not call them for their numbers, but to defend the Trojans' wives and children (17.220–232).
