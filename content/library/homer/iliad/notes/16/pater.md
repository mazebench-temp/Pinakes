---
summary: Patroclus disowns Achilles' parents in anger, “the horseman Peleus was not your father after all, / nor Thetis your mother” (16.33–34). The father of the book is Zeus, “father of men and gods”, who grants Achilles half his prayer and loses his own son; Apollo obeys “his father” (16.676).
---

Zeus is “father” in Achilles' rite, the only god to whom his cup pours (16.227, 16.253; see [[cup]]), and in his answer, “the father granted him one thing, and refused him the other” (16.250). As Sarpedon's father he yields to Hera and honors his son with a rain of blood (16.458–460; see [[fathers-and-sons]] and [[bereaved-parents]]). Glaucus reproaches him in his prayer to Apollo: Zeus “does not defend even his own son” (16.522).
