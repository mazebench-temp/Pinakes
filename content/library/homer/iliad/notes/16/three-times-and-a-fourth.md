---
summary: The pattern is used twice in Book 16, both times for Patroclus. At the wall of Troy he obeys Apollo's shout and lives; at the end of his last charge there is no shout, and the god strikes him.
passages:
  - 16.698-711 | Patroclus at the wall of Troy
  - 16.784-792 | Patroclus' last charge
---

**At the wall.** Three times Patroclus climbs the angle of the high wall and three times Apollo thrusts him back, “thrusting at the shining shield with his immortal hands” (16.702–704); at the fourth the god shouts that it is not his portion to sack the city, nor even Achilles' (16.705–709; see [[aisa]] and [[sack-of-troy-foretold]]). The scene repeats Diomedes' attack in Book 5 (5.436–444): the same verse of the fourth attack, the same shout, and the same withdrawal, “avoiding the wrath of Apollo who shoots from afar” (16.711 = 5.444; see [[menis]]). Diomedes drew back “a little way”, Patroclus “a long way back” (16.710).

**The last charge.** Three times Patroclus springs at the Trojans, “the equal of swift Ares”, and three times kills nine men (16.784–785). At the fourth there is no warning. The narrator turns to him, “then, Patroclus, the end of your life appeared” (16.787; see [[apostrophe]]), and Apollo, wrapped in mist, strikes him from behind with the flat of his hand (16.788–792). The first time the god speaks and the man obeys; the second time the god does not speak.
