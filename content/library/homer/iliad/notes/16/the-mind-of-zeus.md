summary: The narrator states the rule when Patroclus, forgetting Achilles' word, pursues the Trojans to his death: “But the mind of Zeus is always stronger than that of men; / he puts even a valiant man to flight and takes away victory / easily” (16.688–690). Hector will say the same verses to Glaucus over Patroclus' body (17.176–178).
grc: ["Διὸς κρείσσων νόος"]
en: [the mind of Zeus is always stronger]
passages:
  - 16.684-691 | The mind of Zeus
---

The rule explains a blindness: Patroclus “was greatly blinded, / the fool” (16.685–686; see [[ate]] and [[nepios]]), and yet it was Zeus “who then too let loose the spirit in his breast” (16.691). The verse that balances the two halves is uncertain in the text: the digitized Greek has ὅτε, “and when he himself urges”, where the same half-verse in Book 17 has ὁτέ, “and at another time”, which the translation follows (see the translation's note on 16.690). Earlier in the book the mind of Zeus overpowered Ajax at the ship (16.103; see [[noos]]).
