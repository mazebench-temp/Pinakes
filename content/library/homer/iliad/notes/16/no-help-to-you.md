summary: Hector taunts the dying Patroclus with the verb of help: “Ah, wretch, not even Achilles, brave as he is, has helped you” (16.837). The threat that the protection a man counts on will fail him is spoken here as a thing done.
grc: ["χραίσμησεν Ἀχιλλεύς"]
en: [has helped you]
---

Hector's taunt belongs with the threats of Book 3 and Book 11, “Your lyre would be no help to you then” (3.54) and “your bow would not help you” (11.387), and with the narrator's verdict on Scamandrius, “But Artemis of the showering arrows was no help to him then” (5.53). The help that failed Patroclus is Achilles himself. In Book 1 Achilles had foretold that Agamemnon, for all his grief, would be powerless to help the Achaeans when many of them fell at the hands of man-slaying Hector (1.240–244); the Achaeans have fallen, and now Hector says that Achilles has been powerless to help his own companion. Hector goes on to invent the orders Achilles gave him, to kill Hector before coming back (16.838–842; see [[retelling]]), and the orders were the opposite (16.87–96).
