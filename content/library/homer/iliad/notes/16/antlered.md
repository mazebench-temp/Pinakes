---
summary: The Myrmidons are like wolves “that have cut down a great antlered stag in the mountains / and tear at it.”
---

“and they, like wolves / that eat raw flesh, in whose hearts there is boundless courage, / that have cut down a great antlered stag in the mountains / and tear at it, and the jaws of all of them are red with blood” (16.156–159; [[wolves]], [[eating-raw-flesh]], [[untold]], [[deer]]). In Book 15 the antlered stag escaped the hunters (15.271–276); here the wolves have killed it, and the simile tells of their hunger after the kill. See [[myrmidons]].
