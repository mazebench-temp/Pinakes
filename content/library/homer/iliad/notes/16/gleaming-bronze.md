summary: Patroclus arms himself “in gleaming bronze,” and in the simile of the fisherman the hook is “gleaming bronze”; the book adds ἤνοπι χαλκῷ.
grc: ["ἤνοπι χαλκῷ"]
---

**The arming.** “So he spoke, and Patroclus armed himself in gleaming bronze” (16.130; [[arming-scene]]), with νῶροψ, in the verse with which Ajax armed for his duel with Hector (7.206).

**The fisherman.** Patroclus drags Thestor from his chariot on his spear “as when a man / sitting on a jutting rock drags a sacred fish / out of the sea onto the shore with line and gleaming bronze” (16.406–408; [[thestor-son-of-enops]], [[hieros]], [[the-fisherman]]). Here the epithet is ἦνοψ, and the bronze is a hook; ἤνοπι χαλκῷ, with which the translation gives the same English (see the translation's row on νώροπι, ἤνοπι and αἴθοπι χαλκῷ), is new to the entry. The pattern is lowercase, and Ἤνοπος, the name of Thestor's father on 16.401, belongs to [[enops-father-of-thestor]].
