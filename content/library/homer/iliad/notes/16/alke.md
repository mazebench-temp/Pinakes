summary: Courage is remembered and forgotten. Patroclus tells the Myrmidons to “remember your furious courage” (16.270), and the Trojans, routed, “forgot their furious courage” (16.357). The wolves of the Myrmidons' simile have boundless courage (16.157), the lambs none (16.355); Zeus puts a craven heart in Hector (ἀνάλκιδα, 16.656); and the lion with whom Patroclus is compared is destroyed by “its own courage” (16.753).
