---
summary: Patroclus calls Achilles “Pitiless one,” and Achilles quotes the Myrmidons' complaint, “Hard son of Peleus … pitiless.”
---

**Patroclus.** “Pitiless one, the horseman Peleus was not your father after all, / nor Thetis your mother; but the gray-green sea bore you” (16.33–34; [[gray-green-sea]], [[whose-prowess-is-a-curse]]). νηλεές opens the verse, as an address.

**The Myrmidons.** Achilles reminds his men of what they used to say of him: “‘Hard son of Peleus, your mother reared you on anger, / pitiless, who keep your companions by the ships against their will’” (16.203–204; [[hard]], [[son-of-peleus]], [[myrmidons]]). The word is again the first of a verse. In the embassy Ajax had joined the same two words, σχέτλιος and νηλής, in his last judgment of Achilles: “hard man, and does not regard the friendship of his companions, / with which we honored him beside the ships above all others, / pitiless one” (9.630–632; [[embassy]]). See [[achilles]].
