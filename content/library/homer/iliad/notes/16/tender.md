---
summary: Achilles asks Patroclus why he weeps like a little girl: “Like her, Patroclus, you let your tender tears fall.”
---

“and looks up at her in tears, until she picks her up? / Like her, Patroclus, you let your tender tears fall” (16.10–11; [[like-a-girl]], [[tears]]). The verse closes Achilles' simile of the little girl who clutches her mother's robe. τέρεν κατὰ δάκρυον εἴβει will be said of Peleus at home in Phthia, weeping for his son (19.323).
