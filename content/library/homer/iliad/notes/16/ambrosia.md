---
summary: At Zeus' command Apollo washes Sarpedon's body in the river, anoints it with ambrosia and clothes it in immortal clothing before Sleep and Death carry it home to Lycia.
passages:
  - 16.666-683 | Apollo anoints Sarpedon
---

“Come now, dear Phoebus, go and cleanse the dark blood / from Sarpedon, out of range of the missiles, and then / carry him far away and wash him in the streams of the river, / and anoint him with ambrosia, and put immortal clothing on him” (16.667–670; see [[zeus]], [[apollo]] and [[sarpedon]]). Apollo does each thing in the same words (16.678–680), and gives the body to Sleep and Death (see [[sleep]] and [[death]]). The poem does not say what the ambrosia does for the dead man. Elsewhere the gods use it to keep a body whole: Thetis drips ambrosia and red nectar into the nostrils of the dead Patroclus “so that his flesh might be sound” (ἵνα οἱ χρὼς ἔμπεδος εἴη, 19.38–39), and Aphrodite anoints Hector's body with rose oil, ambrosial, so that Achilles may not tear it as he drags it (23.186–187; see [[nectar]]). The clothing is ἄμβροτα εἵματα, “immortal clothing”: ἄμβροτος is the same formation as ἀμβρόσιος, “not mortal”, and both belong to the gods. So cared for, the body goes home to be buried by his kin “with a tomb and a pillar” (16.674–675; see [[tomb]]).
