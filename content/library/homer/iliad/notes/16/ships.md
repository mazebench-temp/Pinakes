summary: The fire falls on the ships. The narrator asks the Muses “how fire first fell upon the ships of the Achaeans”, Hector shears Ajax's spear, and an unquenchable flame pours over the ship of Protesilaus (16.112–124); at the sight Achilles sends Patroclus out, and Patroclus drives the Trojans away and puts out the fire, leaving the ship half-burned (16.284–296).

**The fire.** Achilles' terms for returning were the ships themselves: he would not end his wrath until the war cry and the war reached his own ships (16.61–63; see [[menis]]), and his fear is the loss of the way home, that the Trojans “burn the ships and take away our dear homecoming” (16.82; see [[nostos]]). When the flame takes hold of the stern (16.124; see [[stern]]), he strikes his thighs and calls Patroclus: “they must not take the ships and leave us no more escape” (16.127–128). The fire is the sign Zeus was waiting for, to turn the battle back from the ships (15.596–602; see [[plan-of-zeus]]).

**Achilles' fleet.** The catalogue of the Myrmidons counts by ships: “Fifty were the swift ships in which Achilles, / dear to Zeus, led his men to Troy; and in each / were fifty men, companions, at the rowing benches” (16.168–170; see [[myrmidons]] and [[catalogue-of-ships]]). In the Catalogue the Myrmidons had fifty ships (2.685).

**Driven back.** Patroclus' first spear kills Pyraechmes beside the stern of Protesilaus' ship; “He drove them from the ships, and put out the blazing fire. / And the half-burned ship was left there” (16.286–294). ἡμιδαής, “half-burned”, is found only here. The Trojans are pushed back “from the ships under compulsion” (16.305), and Patroclus pens them between the ships, the river and the wall (16.396–397). Hector's boast over the dying Patroclus turns the ships to another use: he says Patroclus meant to carry the Trojan women off in them to his native land (16.830–832).
