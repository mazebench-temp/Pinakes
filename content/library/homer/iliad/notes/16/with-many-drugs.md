---
summary: Patroclus tells Achilles that “The healers, with their many drugs,” are busy over the wounded leaders, but that there is no dealing with him.
---

Patroclus counts the wounded, Diomedes, Odysseus, Agamemnon and Eurypylus, whom the healers are tending at the ships (16.23–29; [[diomedes]], [[odysseus]], [[agamemnon]], [[eurypylus]]), and sets their care against Achilles' hardness: “but there is no dealing with you, Achilles” (16.29; [[whose-prowess-is-a-curse]]). The healers have their drugs for the wounds of the others; Achilles is ἀμήχανος, a man with whom there is no dealing. See [[healing]], [[patroclus]] and [[achilles]].
