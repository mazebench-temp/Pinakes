---
summary: Patroclus is “urger of horses” three times, and only in this book: when Achilles sends him to arm, when the narrator speaks to him in his charge at the Lycians, and in the last orders that Hector imagines Achilles gave him.
---

**Achilles.** The fire has caught Protesilaus' ship, and Achilles strikes his thighs: “Up, Patroclus, sprung from Zeus, urger of horses! / I see the blast of consuming fire beside the ships” (16.126–127; [[sprung-from-zeus]], [[destructive]], [[achilles]]). The two vocatives, “sprung from Zeus” and “urger of horses,” fill the verse after the name, and Patroclus arms at once.

**The narrator.** When Hector kills Epeigeus, Patroclus goes through the champions like a hawk, “so straight at the Lycians, Patroclus, urger of horses, / you rushed, and at the Trojans, and your heart was angered for your companion” (16.584–585; [[apostrophe]], [[hawk]], [[epeigeus]]).

**Hector.** Over the dying Patroclus Hector imagines Achilles' parting words: “‘Do not come back to me, Patroclus, urger of horses, / to the hollow ships, before you have torn man-slaying Hector's / bloody tunic around his chest’” (16.839–841; [[hector]], [[man-slaying]], [[bloody]]). Hector puts the epithet in Achilles' mouth, and he is wrong about the orders: Achilles had told Patroclus to come back once he had driven the Trojans from the ships (16.87–96).
