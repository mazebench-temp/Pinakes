summary: Zeus' grief begins with the word: Sarpedon “is fated” (μοῖρʼ) to be brought down by Patroclus (16.434). Patroclus names his killers with it, “deadly fate and the son of Leto have killed me” (16.849), and tells Hector that “death and mighty fate stand close beside you” (16.853). Elsewhere in the book it is a portion of land and the good order of a retreat.
grc: ["μοῖρʼ"]
---

**Fate.** Zeus' first words are a lament: “Ah me, that Sarpedon, dearest of men to me, / is fated to be brought down by Patroclus, son of Menoetius!” (16.433–434; see [[zeus]] and [[the-death-of-sarpedon]]). The father of gods knows his son's μοῖρα and asks only whether to break it; Hera answers in the language of the portion (16.441; see [[aisa]]). Cleobulus' eyes are seized by “purple death and mighty fate” (16.334; see [[purple-death]] and [[mighty-fate]]). Patroclus, dying, puts fate first among his killers, then Apollo, then Euphorbus, and Hector third (16.849–850), and turns the pair of death and fate on Hector (16.853; see [[prophecy]]).

**Share.** The first sense of the word returns in Achilles' picture of the Achaeans pressed against the sea, “holding only a little portion of land still” (16.68), and κατὰ μοῖραν, “in good order”, in the Trojans' retreat over the ditch, which is not one (16.367).
