---
summary: The boar of Book 16 is Patroclus at his death: Hector kills him as a lion overpowers a tireless boar when the two fight over a little spring on a mountain, and the boar gasps hard before it is brought down (16.823–828).
---

ὡς δʼ ὅτε σῦν ἀκάμαντα λέων ἐβιήσατο χάρμῃ, “As when a lion overpowers a tireless boar in battle” (16.823; see [[the-lion-and-the-boar]]). σῦν is the accusative of σῦς, the plain word for the boar. The boar of the similes is the beast that stands its ground and fights, as Idomeneus stood like a boar in the mountains, trusting in its courage, and Odysseus like a boar at bay among dogs and young men (13.471–475, 11.414–420; see [[the-bristling-boar]] and [[the-boar-at-bay]]). Here it is ἀκάμας, “tireless”, and it fights to drink at a spring; it is beaten only when it is gasping (ἀσθμαίνοντα, 16.826), and by a lion's strength. Elsewhere in Homer lion and boar are named together as like beasts, warriors standing “like lions that eat raw flesh or wild boars” (5.782–783, 7.256–257; see [[lions-and-boars]]); this is their only fight. In Book 17 the boar is Ajax, who scatters the Trojans over Patroclus' body as a boar scatters dogs and young men in the mountains (17.281–285). See [[patroclus]] and [[lion]].
