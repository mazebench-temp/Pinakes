---
summary: Achilles asks the weeping Patroclus whether he grieves for the Argives, “how they are perishing / beside the hollow ships because of their own transgression” (ὑπερβασίης, 16.17–18): for Achilles the army's suffering is the price of its own wrong.
grc: [ὑπερβασίης]
---

ὑπερβασίη, “a stepping over”, is the word of Menelaus' fear that the oaths would be violated “by transgression” (3.107). Achilles' σφῆς, “their own”, puts the fault on the whole army and not on Agamemnon alone: the Achaeans gave him his prize and took it back (1.299), and in Book 1 he asked that they be penned in at the sterns “so that they may all have joy of their king” (1.410). The same Achilles sends Patroclus out to save them a few verses later (16.64–82; see [[who-is-to-blame]]).
