---
summary: Achilles pours his libation looking up to the sky; in the similes of the book Zeus moves a cloud from a mountain and the upper air breaks open from the sky, and a storm cloud comes from Olympus into the sky; and the sun stands astride the middle of the sky before the battle turns.
---

**Looking up to the sky.** Achilles prays and pours out the wine “looking up to the sky” (οὐρανὸν εἰσανιδών, 16.232; see [[prayer]], [[libation]] and [[zeus]]), the gesture with which Priam will pray for a sign before he goes to Achilles (24.307). Menelaus, his sword shattered, groaned “looking up into the wide sky” to reproach Zeus (3.364).

**Clouds and the upper air.** When Patroclus has put out the fire, the Danaans breathe again as when Zeus moves a thick cloud from a mountain peak and every lookout and headland stands out, “and from the sky the boundless upper air breaks open” (16.297–300; see [[the-cloud-lifted-from-the-peak]]): the verses of the watch fires in Book 8, which shine like the stars around the moon on a windless night (8.557–558). When the Trojans flee across the ditch, their rout rises from the ships “as when a cloud comes from Olympus into the sky / out of the shining upper air” (16.364–365; see [[the-cloud-from-olympus]] and [[olympus]]), and a storm wind of dust spreads out “under the clouds” (16.374–375).

**The middle of the sky.** “As long as the sun stood astride the middle of the sky” the missiles of both sides struck home (16.777–778; see [[helios]]), the verse of the midday on which Zeus held out his golden scales in Book 8 (8.68).
