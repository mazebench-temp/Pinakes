summary: In the simile of the clearing sky, “Zeus who gathers the lightning” moves a thick cloud from a mountain peak, as the Danaans draw breath when Patroclus has put out the fire.
---

The epithet comes once, in a simile of clearing weather. When Patroclus has put out the fire at Protesilaus' ship, the relief of the Danaans is like a mountain coming out of cloud: “And as when from the high peak of a great mountain / Zeus who gathers the lightning moves away a thick cloud, / and all the lookout places and the high headlands stand out, / and the glens; and from the sky the boundless upper air breaks open” (16.297–300; [[untold]], [[protesilaus]]). The god of storms here opens the sky, and the relief is brief: “but there was no respite from the war” (16.302). See [[zeus]] and [[patroclus]].
