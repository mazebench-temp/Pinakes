---
summary: Achilles asks the weeping Patroclus whether he alone has heard some news from Phthia, where their fathers, Menoetius and Peleus, are said to be still alive (16.13–16).
refs: [16.15]
---

ἦέ τινʼ ἀγγελίην Φθίης ἐξέκλυες οἶος; “or have you alone heard some news from Phthia?” (16.13; see [[achilles]] and [[patroclus]]). The two young men were sent to Troy from Phthia on the same day (9.253, 11.766; see [[peleus-sends-achilles-to-troy]]), and Achilles can think of one grief worth such tears, a death at home: “Yet they say that Menoetius, son of Actor, still lives, / and Peleus, son of Aeacus, lives among the Myrmidons; / we would grieve greatly for both of them, if they were dead” (16.14–16; see [[menoetius]] and [[peleus]]). The news Patroclus brings is of the Achaeans dying at the ships (16.21–29).

The question returns reversed after Patroclus' death. Over his body Achilles says that he could suffer nothing worse, not even if he heard that his father had died, “who now, I think, in Phthia is letting his tender tears fall” for want of such a son, and that he had hoped Patroclus would go home to Phthia without him and show his son Neoptolemus his possessions (19.321–333). The tears he gave the weeping Patroclus in the same words, τέρεν κατὰ δάκρυον εἴβεις, “you let your tender tears fall” (16.11), he now gives his father, and the news from Phthia he imagines is the news that will reach Phthia of his own death (19.334–337; see [[the-death-of-patroclus]]).
