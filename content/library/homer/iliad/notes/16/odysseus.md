---
summary: Odysseus lies wounded at the ships: Patroclus names him among the wounded chiefs, “famed for his spear”, stabbed (16.26).
---

οὔτασται δʼ Ὀδυσεὺς δουρικλυτὸς ἠδʼ Ἀγαμέμνων, “and Odysseus famed for his spear has been stabbed, and Agamemnon” (16.26 ≈ 11.661; see [[the-wounded-chiefs]], [[famed-for-his-spear]] and [[patroclus]]). Socus' spear tore the skin from his ribs, and Menelaus led him out of the battle while Ajax covered them (11.434–488; see [[socus]]). Patroclus names the wounded in two kinds, as Nestor did, those shot with arrows and those stabbed with the spear; Odysseus and Agamemnon were stabbed (βέβληται, οὔτασται, 16.25–27). Odysseus has no other part in the book.
