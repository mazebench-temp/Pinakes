---
summary: Sarpedon is given the honors of the dead by the gods themselves: Apollo washes and anoints his body and clothes it in immortal clothing, and Sleep and Death carry it home to Lycia, where his kin will bury him “with a tomb and a pillar” (16.666–683).
passages:
  - 16.666-683 | Apollo and Sleep and Death care for Sarpedon
---

The care follows a fight in which both sides seek the body, the Achaeans to strip and defile it, the Lycians to save it (16.545, 16.559–561; see [[fighting-over-the-body]]). The enemy takes the armor, and the gods take the body (16.663–665). The order of the rites is the order of Zeus' command, carried out word for word: cleansing the blood, washing in the river, anointing with ambrosia, clothing, and the journey home (16.667–675, 16.678–683; see [[bathing]], [[purification]], [[ambrosia]] and [[the-prize-of-the-dead]]). Patroclus, dying at the end of the book, is left on the field, and his burial waits until Book 23.
