**Hera's counsel.** When Zeus thinks of snatching his son Sarpedon from death, Hera tells him to let him fall at Patroclus' hands, “but when his soul and his life have left him, / send Death and sweet Sleep to carry him / until they come to the land of wide Lycia” (16.453–455; see [[hera]], [[zeus]], [[sarpedon]] and [[the-death-of-sarpedon]]). In Book 14 it was Hera who went to Sleep on Lemnos and won him over to lull Zeus (14.231–291); here she names him to Zeus as a bearer of the dead. νήδυμος, “sweet”, is the epithet of sleep in the formulas, and of the god in Book 14 (14.242, 14.354; see [[sweet-sleep]]).

**The twins.** Zeus gives the order to Apollo, and Apollo carries it out in the same words: he washes and anoints the body, “and he sent him with swift escorts to be carried, / with Sleep and Death, the twins, who quickly / set him down in the rich land of wide Lycia” (16.671–673, 16.681–683; see [[apollo]], [[death]] and [[lycia]]). In Book 14 Sleep was “the brother of Death” (14.231); here the two are twins, διδυμάονες, the word for the twin sons of Diocles and of the nymph Abarbarea (5.548, 6.26). πομποί, “escorts”, are those who see a traveler on his way, as Hermes will escort Priam to Achilles' hut (24.153, 24.182, 24.461; see [[hermes]]). In Book 14 Sleep would not come near Zeus “unless he himself should bid me” (14.247–248); now he comes at Zeus' command, and his errand is a burial. The gods who come for Sarpedon do not kill him: the spear has done that, and the twins carry the body home, where “his brothers and his kinsmen will give him burial / with a tomb and a pillar” (16.674–675; see [[the-prize-of-the-dead]] and [[honoring-the-dead]]).

**The printed text.** Monro–Allen print the twins in lowercase in Book 16 (ὕπνον, ὕπνῳ, θανάτῳ), though they are persons, and the English capitalizes them; at 14.231 the digitized text capitalizes them (see the translation's note on 16.454, 16.672, 16.682 and [[hypnos]]).
