---
summary: The hawk of Book 16 is Patroclus, who makes straight through the champions like a swift hawk that puts jackdaws and starlings to flight, angered by the death of Epeigeus (16.581–585).
---

ἴθυσεν δὲ διὰ προμάχων ἴρηκι ἐοικὼς / ὠκέϊ, ὅς τʼ ἐφόβησε κολοιούς τε ψῆράς τε, “and he made straight through the champions like a hawk, / a swift one, which puts jackdaws and starlings to flight” (16.582–583; see [[like-a-hawk]]). In Book 15 the hawk was Apollo going down from Ida, and its prey was in its epithet, φασσοφόνος, “dove-killer” (15.237–238). Here the prey is a flock of small birds, jackdaws and starlings, which scatter (see [[jackdaws]] and [[starlings]]); the hawk is a man, and the birds are the Lycians and Trojans. In Book 17 the same small birds flee screaming from another hawk, the κίρκος, “which brings death to small birds”, and there they are the Achaeans fleeing before Aeneas and Hector (17.755–759).
