summary: Zeus sheds “bloody raindrops” in honor of Sarpedon, Sarpedon dies “clutching at the bloody dust,” and Hector imagines Achilles bidding Patroclus tear his “bloody tunic”; the book adds αἱματοέσσας.
grc: [αἱματοέσσας]
---

**The rain.** “and he shed bloody raindrops on the earth, / honoring his dear son, whom Patroclus was to / kill in deep-soiled Troy” (16.459–461; [[father-of-men-and-gods]], [[the-death-of-sarpedon]]). Zeus had sent down dewdrops wet with blood at the beginning of the battle in Book 11 (11.53–55). The accusative plural αἱματοέσσας is new to the entry.

**The dust.** Sarpedon falls like a tree and lies “roaring, clutching at the bloody dust” (16.486; [[sarpedon]]), in the verse of the death of Asius (13.393).

**The tunic.** “before you have torn man-slaying Hector's / bloody tunic around his chest” (16.840–841; [[man-slaying]], [[tunic]]), in the orders Hector imagines.
