summary: The narrator asks the question of Patroclus himself, in the second person: “Then whom first, whom last did you slay, / Patroclus, when the gods called you to your death?” Nine names follow in three verses, as for Hector in Book 11, and the rest of the Trojans turn to flight (16.692–697).

---

The second verse of the question names the end of the killer instead of his glory. Of Hector the narrator asked, “when Zeus gave him glory” (11.300); of Patroclus he asks, “when the gods called you to your death” (θάνατον δέ, “to death”, with the suffix of direction printed apart). The answer is built like Hector's list. Its first verse has the same shape and the same second name, “Adrastus first, and Autonous and Echeclus” (16.694), as “Asaeus first, and Autonous and Opites” (11.301): an Achaean Autonous died in Hector's list and a Trojan Autonous dies in Patroclus'. Then six more names in two verses, one with a patronymic, “Perimus, son of Megas” (16.695), and the close, “these he killed; and the others each turned their minds to flight” (16.697; see [[androktasia]] and [[apostrophe]]). The list closes the rout that carries him to the wall of Troy, where Apollo stops him (16.698–711).
