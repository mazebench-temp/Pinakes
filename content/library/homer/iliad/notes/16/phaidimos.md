---
summary: “Shining Hector” kills Epeigeus, gives ground, flees, sends Cebriones into the battle, fights over him, and speaks to the dead Patroclus; Patroclus' “shining limbs” are loosened under Apollo's blow.
---

**Hector.** The formula φαίδιμος Ἕκτωρ follows Hector through the second half of the book: “Him, as he took hold of the corpse, shining Hector struck” (16.577; [[epeigeus]]); “The champions and shining Hector gave ground” (16.588); Zeus considers whether “over godlike Sarpedon, shining Hector / should cut him down too with the bronze” (16.649–650); “and shining Hector bade wise-hearted Cebriones” (16.727; [[wise-hearted]]); “Patroclus, son of Menoetius, and shining Hector” fight over Cebriones (16.760; [[masters-of-the-war-cry]]); and “And to him, though he was dead, shining Hector spoke” (16.858; [[lovely-haired]]).

**The limbs.** When Apollo has struck him, “blind folly seized his mind, and his shining limbs were loosened beneath him” (16.805; [[ate]], [[loosened-his-limbs]]). φαίδιμα γυῖα are the limbs of a man in his strength, here at the moment it leaves him. See [[hector]] and [[patroclus]].
