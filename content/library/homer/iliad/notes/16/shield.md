---
summary: Ajax can no longer hold his shield steady at the ship (16.106–108); Patroclus takes Achilles' “great and heavy” shield (16.136); Hector, knowing the battle has turned, shelters under his shield of bull's hide (16.360–361); and Apollo, thrusting at Patroclus' shield three times at the wall, at the end strikes it from his shoulders, “the full-length shield”, with its strap (16.702–704, 16.803).
---

Two men die in the chest “where it was bared beside the shield” (16.312, 16.400), and Meriones steps forward “under cover of his shield” (ὑπασπίδια, 16.609). The fall of Patroclus' shield is the third stage of his disarming: ἀσπὶς σὺν τελαμῶνι χαμαὶ πέσε τερμιόεσσα, “the full-length shield fell to the ground with its strap” (16.803; see [[arming-scene]]). τερμιόεις, “reaching to the feet”, is used only here in the Iliad (see the translation's note on 16.803).
