summary: Patroclus comes to Achilles weeping “like a spring of dark water / that pours its murky water down a sheer rock” (16.3–4), the simile of Agamemnon's tears in the assembly of Book 9 (9.14–15). Achilles answers with another image of weeping, a little girl in tears who wants her mother to pick her up (16.7–11).
grc: [δεδάκρυσαι, δακρυόεσσα, δάκρυον]
en: [in tears, tender tears]
---

Agamemnon wept for the army when he proposed going home (9.13–15); Patroclus weeps for the same army and proposes going out to fight (16.21–45; see [[the-spring-of-dark-water]] and [[like-a-girl]]). Achilles asks, “Why are you in tears, Patroclus, like a girl”, and ends, “Like her, Patroclus, you let your tender tears fall” (16.7, 16.11; see [[childhood]]). The half-verse τέρεν κατὰ δάκρυον εἴβεις returns when Achilles, mourning Patroclus, thinks of his father in Phthia, who perhaps is weeping for him now (τέρεν κατὰ δάκρυον εἴβει, 19.323). For Zeus the battle itself is “tearful” (16.436; see [[pity]]).
