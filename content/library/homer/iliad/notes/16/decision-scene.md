---
summary: Three decisions, two of them Zeus'. Zeus, torn over Sarpedon, yields to Hera; pondering the death of Patroclus, he decides in the verse with which mortals choose the better way; and Hector, in doubt at the Scaean gates, is decided by Apollo in disguise (16.431–458, 16.644–658, 16.712–725).
passages:
  - 16.431-438 | Zeus torn over Sarpedon
  - 16.644-655 | Zeus decides the death of Patroclus
---

**Sarpedon.** Seeing his son and Patroclus rush at each other, Zeus pities them and divides: “And my heart is divided as I ponder in my mind / whether, while he is still alive, out of the tearful battle / I should snatch him up and set him down in the rich land of Lycia, / or bring him down now at the hands of the son of Menoetius” (16.435–438; see [[divine-rescue]]). The resolution comes from outside: Hera answers, and “the father of men and gods did not disobey” (16.458; see [[not-all-the-gods-approve]]).

**Patroclus.** Over Sarpedon's body Zeus watches and ponders much “about the killing of Patroclus”, ἤ … ἦ: whether Hector should kill him there, over Sarpedon, and take his armor, or whether he should still increase the toil for more men (16.644–651; see [[pondering-two-ways]]). “And as he pondered, this seemed to him the better way” (ὧδε δέ οἱ φρονέοντι δοάσσατο κέρδιον εἶναι, 16.652), the verse of Deiphobus' choice and of Nestor's (13.458, 14.23; see [[kerdos]]): Patroclus should drive the Trojans to the city first and kill many more (16.653–655). The god decides a man's death in the words of a man's choice, and the second course is the one taken; the first is only postponed (see [[plan-of-zeus]]).

**Hector.** At the Scaean gates Hector holds his horses, “for he was in doubt whether to drive back into the turmoil and fight, / or to call to the army to crowd within the wall” (16.713–714). Apollo, in the likeness of Asius, decides for him (16.715–725; see [[divine-epiphany]]).
