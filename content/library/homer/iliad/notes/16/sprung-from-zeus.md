---
summary: Patroclus is “sprung from Zeus” three times: twice in Achilles' mouth, and once in Apollo's, who drives him from the wall.
---

Achilles answers Patroclus' plea, “Ah, Patroclus, sprung from Zeus, what a thing you have said!” (16.49; [[achilles]], [[what-a-word]]), and later sends him to arm, “Up, Patroclus, sprung from Zeus, urger of horses!” (16.126; [[urger-of-horses]]). The third time is Apollo's, at the wall of Troy: “Draw back, Patroclus, sprung from Zeus! It is not your portion / that the city of the proud Trojans be sacked by your spear” (16.707–708; [[apollo]], [[proud]], [[sack-of-troy-foretold]]). The honorific in the god's mouth comes with the command to give way. See [[patroclus]].
