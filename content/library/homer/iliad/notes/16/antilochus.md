summary: One of “the sons of Nestor”, Antilochus stabs Atymnius through the flank; when Atymnius' brother Maris springs at him over the body, his own brother Thrasymedes strikes first, and the two sons of Amisodarus go down to Erebus at the hands of two brothers (16.317–329).
refs: [16.317-318, 16.320, 16.326]
---

Νεστορίδαι δʼ ὃ μὲν οὔτασʼ Ἀτύμνιον ὀξέϊ δουρὶ / Ἀντίλοχος, “And of the sons of Nestor, one stabbed Atymnius with his sharp spear, / Antilochus” (16.317–318; see [[nestor]], [[atymnius-son-of-amisodarus]] and [[sharp]]). He drives the spear through the flank, and Atymnius falls forward (16.318–319). His brother Maris then springs at Antilochus “angered for his brother, / standing in front of the corpse” (16.319–321; see [[maris]]), as Antilochus himself stood over his fallen companion Hypsenor in Book 13 (13.417–420); but Thrasymedes is quicker, and Antilochus is saved by his brother (16.321–325; see [[thrasymedes]]). The narrator sums up the pairing: “So these two, brought down by two brothers, / went down to Erebus, brave companions of Sarpedon, / the spearmen sons of Amisodarus, who reared the Chimaera” (16.326–328; see [[amisodarus]], [[sarpedon]], [[chimaera]] and [[erebus]]).

The brothers fight side by side here. In Book 17 they will be fighting apart, as Nestor had ordered, and will not yet have heard that Patroclus is dead (17.377–383); it is Antilochus whom Menelaus sends to Achilles with the news, and he comes to him “shedding hot tears”, as Patroclus came at the beginning of this book (18.16–21; 16.3; see [[patroclus]] and [[hot-tears]]).
