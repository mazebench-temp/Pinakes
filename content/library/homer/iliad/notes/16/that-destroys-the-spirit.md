summary: Twice “death that destroys the spirit was poured around” a man struck on the head by a stone, and in the simile of the javelin the enemy are “enemies who destroy the spirit”; the book adds θυμοραϊστέων.
grc: [θυμοραϊστέων]
---

**Death.** Of Erylaus, killed by Patroclus: “and he fell face down on the ground, / and death that destroys the spirit was poured around him” (16.413–414; [[erylaus]], [[heavy-helmet]]); of Epeigeus, killed by Hector: “and he fell face down on the body, / and death that destroys the spirit was poured around him” (16.579–580; [[epeigeus]]). The verse is the one of Book 13 (13.544).

**The enemy.** “which a man throws, trying his strength, either in a contest / or in war, under the attack of enemies who destroy the spirit” (16.590–591; [[destructive]], [[tanaos]]). The genitive plural θυμοραϊστέων is new to the entry.
