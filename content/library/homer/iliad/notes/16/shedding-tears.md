summary: Patroclus comes to Achilles “shedding hot tears like a spring of dark water” (δάκρυα θερμὰ χέων, 16.3). The half-verse is used of Patroclus' death in Book 18 as well: Antilochus brings the news “shedding hot tears” (18.17), and Achilles weeps over his body on the bier (18.235).
grc: ["δάκρυα θερμὰ χέων", "δάκρυα θερμὰ χέοντες"]
en: [shedding hot tears]
---

The plural form, δάκρυα θερμά, “hot tears”, was first used of the armies gathering their dead in the truce of Book 7: “and shedding hot tears lifted them onto the wagons” (7.426). In Book 16 it opens the story of Patroclus' death, and in Book 18 it frames the news of it and the mourning (see [[tears]] and [[the-spring-of-dark-water]]).
