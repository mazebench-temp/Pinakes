---
summary: Patroclus and Achilles both name Diomedes, “the son of Tydeus,” among the wounded whose spears no longer guard the ships.
---

Patroclus: “The son of Tydeus, mighty Diomedes, has been shot” (16.25; [[krateros]], [[the-wounded-chiefs]]). Achilles: “For not in the hands of Diomedes, son of Tydeus, / does the spear rage to ward off ruin from the Danaans” (16.74–75). The patronymic that ran through Diomedes' great days in Books 5 and 10 is spoken now of the man who is out of the fight, wounded by Paris' arrow in Book 11 (11.376–378). See [[diomedes]] and [[tydeus]].
