---
summary: The Trojans' “single-hoofed horses” strain back toward the city, and Hector holds “his single-hoofed horses” at the Scaean gates.
---

“and high up a storm wind of dust / spread out under the clouds, and the single-hoofed horses strained / back toward the city, away from the ships and huts” (16.374–376; [[horses]]); and “And Hector held his single-hoofed horses at the Scaean gates” (16.712; [[scaean-gates]], [[hector]]). Apollo, in the likeness of Asius, sends him back from the gates against Patroclus (16.715–725; [[strong-hoofed]]).
