---
summary: Automedon yokes Xanthus and Balius for Patroclus, with the mortal Pedasus in the traces; the immortal pair leap the ditch after the routed Trojans, are thrown into confusion when Sarpedon kills Pedasus, and at the end of the book carry Automedon away from Hector's spear (16.145–154, 16.380–383, 16.466–475, 16.864–867).
refs: [16.145, 16.148, 16.150-151, 16.154, 16.380-381, 16.470-471, 16.475, 16.684, 16.866-867]
---

**The yoking.** Patroclus tells Automedon to yoke the horses, and he leads under the yoke “Xanthus and Balius, who flew as fast as the winds, / whom the Harpy Podarge bore to the West Wind / as she grazed in a meadow beside the stream of Oceanus” (16.148–151; see [[automedon]], [[podarge]], [[zephyrus]] and [[oceanus]]). τὼ ἅμα πνοιῇσι πετέσθην, “who flew together with the breezes”, is said in the dual: the pair are one team, and their speed is their parentage, for their father is a wind. In the traces beside them runs Pedasus, taken at the sack of Eetion's city, “who, mortal though he was, kept pace with the immortal horses” (16.152–154; see [[pedasus-the-horse]]).

**Across the ditch.** When the Trojans flee from the ships, many of their teams break their lords' chariots at the end of the pole in the ditch, but “straight over the ditch leapt the swift horses, / immortal, the splendid gifts that the gods gave to Peleus, / pressing onward” (16.369–382). Patroclus wants to strike Hector, “but his swift horses carried him away” (16.383; see [[hector]]).

**Pedasus killed.** Sarpedon's spear, meant for Patroclus, kills the trace horse. The immortal pair start apart, the yoke creaks, the reins are tangled, until Automedon cuts the dead horse loose, and the two are set straight and pulled taut in the reins (16.466–475). Later Patroclus, calling to his horses and to Automedon, pursues the Trojans and Lycians to his ruin (16.684–687).

**Away from Hector.** When Patroclus is dead, Hector draws his spear from the wound and goes after Automedon, “for he was eager to hit him; but his swift horses carried him away, / immortal, the splendid gifts that the gods gave to Peleus” (16.864–867). The last two verses of the book are verses of the pursuit across the ditch: the verse that saved Hector from Patroclus (16.866 = 16.383) now saves Achilles' charioteer from Hector, and the verse of the gods' gift to Peleus closes the book (16.867 = 16.381). In Book 17 Hector chases the team in vain, Apollo telling him that they are hard for any mortal to master but Achilles, and the horses stand apart from the battle and weep for their charioteer (17.75–78, 17.426–440).
