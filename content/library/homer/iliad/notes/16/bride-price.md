summary: Two of the Myrmidons' mothers are married with great bride-prices after bearing a god's son: Polydora by Borus, “after giving a boundless bride-price” (16.178), and Polymele by Echecles, “after giving a countless bride-price” (16.190), the half-verse of Hector's marriage to Andromache (22.472).
grc: [ἕδνα]
en: [bride-price]
---

The two stories give the woman's family its due in the ordinary way after the extraordinary event: the god leaves, and a mortal husband pays the ἕδνα and takes her home. The sons are raised in the family: Eudorus by his grandfather Phylas, “loving him as if he were his own son” (16.191–192; see [[the-birth-of-eudorus]], [[the-birth-of-menesthius]] and [[marriage]]).
