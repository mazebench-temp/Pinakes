---
summary: Polymele was dancing “in the dance of Artemis of the golden distaff, the clamorous,” when Hermes saw her.
---

“the mighty slayer of Argus / fell in love with her, when he saw her with his eyes among the girls singing and dancing / in the dance of Artemis of the golden distaff, the clamorous” (16.181–183; [[of-the-golden-distaff]], [[polymele]], [[hermes]]). The noisy goddess of the hunt is here the goddess of the girls' dance, and the epithet of din falls on a scene of song: μελπομένῃσιν, “singing and dancing” (16.182). See [[artemis]].
