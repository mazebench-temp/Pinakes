---
summary: Two wolf similes, both for the Achaean side. The leaders of the Myrmidons go out around Patroclus like a pack of wolves with jaws red from a stag they have killed (16.155–167), and the Danaans fall on the Trojans like wolves on lambs and kids that a careless shepherd has let scatter in the mountains (16.352–357).
---

**The pack.** οἳ δὲ λύκοι ὣς / ὠμοφάγοι, “and they, like wolves / that eat raw flesh” (16.156–157; see [[like-wolves]] and [[eating-raw-flesh]]). The wolves have killed a great antlered stag in the mountains and torn it, and they go in a pack (ἀγεληδόν) to drink from a spring of dark water, belching out the gore of blood, their hearts fearless and their bellies full (16.158–163; see [[deer]]). The pack is the band of Myrmidon leaders around Patroclus.

**The flock.** ὡς δὲ λύκοι ἄρνεσσιν ἐπέχραον ἢ ἐρίφοισι / σίνται, “And as wolves fall upon lambs or kids, / ravaging” (16.352–353), when the young beasts have been scattered in the mountains through the shepherd's thoughtlessness and have no courage to resist (16.353–355; see [[lambs]] and [[goats]]). The wolf is the enemy of the flock, and the Trojans are the flock.

In the first battle and on the morning of the third day both armies were wolves and fought each other (4.471–472, 11.72–73). In Book 16 the wolves are one side, the Achaean, with a quarry. Λύκων, the Trojan whom Peneleos beheads all but the skin (16.335–341), has the same letters as λύκων, the genitive plural “of wolves” in Poseidon's list of the beasts that prey on deer (13.103), and is not a wolf (see [[lycon]]).
