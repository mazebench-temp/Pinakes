**The first kill.** Patroclus' first spear is thrown where the Trojans are thickest, “beside the stern of the ship of great-hearted Protesilaus” (16.286), the ship that Hector has just set on fire (16.122–124; see [[protesilaus]] and [[hector]]). It kills the leader of the Paeonians, the westernmost of the allies (see [[paeonians]] and [[trojan-allies]]), and with him dead Patroclus drives the Trojans from the ships and puts out the fire, “And the half-burned ship was left there” (16.293–294).

**The Catalogue's verse.** The verses that name him take up his entry in the Trojan catalogue: there he “led the Paeonians with their curved bows / from far away, from Amydon, from the broad-flowing Axius” (2.848–849); here he “had led the Paeonians who fight from chariots / from Amydon, from the broad-flowing Axius” (16.287–288; see [[amydon]] and [[axius]]). ἱπποκορυστάς, “who fight from chariots”, takes the place of ἀγκυλοτόξους, “with their curved bows”; the Paeonians are ἱπποκορυσταί again when Achilles turns on them after killing Asteropaeus (21.205). ὃς ἀριστεύεσκε μάχεσθαι, “who was always the best in the fighting” (16.292), is said later in the book of Sarpedon, the other allied leader whom Patroclus kills (16.551; see [[sarpedon]]).
