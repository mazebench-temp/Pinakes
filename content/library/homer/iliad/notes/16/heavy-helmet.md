---
summary: The heads of Erylaus and Epeigeus are split in two by stones “in the heavy helmet,” in two verses that repeat each other; the book adds κόρυθι βριαρῇ.
grc: ["κόρυθι βριαρῇ"]
---

Patroclus “struck Erylaus with a stone as he rushed on, / full on the head; and it was all split in two / in the heavy helmet; and he fell face down on the ground, / and death that destroys the spirit was poured around him” (16.411–414; [[erylaus]], [[that-destroys-the-spirit]]). Later in the book Hector kills Epeigeus in the same words: “on the head with a stone; and it was all split in two / in the heavy helmet; and he fell face down on the body” (16.578–579; [[epeigeus]]). The helmet does not protect against a stone. The dative κόρυθι βριαρῇ is new to the entry. See [[helmet]] and [[stone-throwing]].
