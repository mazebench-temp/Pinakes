---
summary: Ajax knows Zeus' hand “in his noble heart,” Patroclus leaves behind the spear of “the noble grandson of Aeacus,” the horse Pedasus is “noble,” Alcimedon is “the noble son of Laerces,” and Patroclus foretells Hector's death at the hands of “Achilles, noble grandson of Aeacus.”
---

**Ajax's heart.** “And Ajax knew in his noble heart, and shuddered, / the work of the gods” (16.119–120; [[ajax]], [[thunders-on-high]]). θυμὸς ἀμύμων is the heart that sees the truth: Zeus has turned against him.

**Achilles.** In the arming, “Only the spear of the noble grandson of Aeacus he did not take” (16.140; [[the-pelian-ash]]), and in Patroclus' prophecy Hector will be “brought down at the hands of Achilles, noble grandson of Aeacus” (16.854; [[grandson-of-aeacus]]).

**A horse and a Myrmidon.** “And in the trace horse's harness he put the noble Pedasus” (16.152; [[pedasus-the-horse]]), a mortal horse beside the immortal pair; and the fifth rank of the Myrmidons is led “by Alcimedon, the noble son of Laerces” (16.197; [[alcimedon]], [[laerces]]). The epithet of heroes is given to a horse, as it is given to things in other books (15.463).
