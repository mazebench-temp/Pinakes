---
summary: Aeneas' spear misses Meriones and quivers in the ground: “and then mighty Ares let its might go.”
---

“for he bent forward, and the long spear behind him / stuck in the ground, and the butt quivered / of the spear; and then mighty Ares let its might go” (16.611–613; [[aeneas]], [[meriones]], [[long-spears]]). The verse-end is the one used when Idomeneus' spear quivered in the beating heart of Alcathous (13.443–444), and it will return in Book 17 (17.529). The god of war is the force in the spear, and here it spends itself in the earth ([[ares]], [[strong-hand]]).
