---
summary: The wolves of the simile have “boundless courage,” and in the simile of the clearing cloud “the boundless upper air breaks open.”
---

“and they, like wolves / that eat raw flesh, in whose hearts there is boundless courage” (16.156–157; [[eating-raw-flesh]], [[wolves]], [[alke]]); and “and from the sky the boundless upper air breaks open” (16.300; [[who-gathers-the-lightning]]). The second verse, with the one before it, is taken from the simile of the stars on the night of the Trojan watch fires (8.557–558), where the glens and headlands stand out under the moon.
