---
summary: Achilles warns Patroclus: if “the loud-thundering husband of Hera” grants him glory, he is not to fight on without Achilles.
---

“When you have driven them from the ships, come back; and if / the loud-thundering husband of Hera grants you to win glory, / do not long to fight without me / against the war-loving Trojans” (16.87–90; [[husband-of-hera]], [[war-loving]], [[kydos]]). The formula ἐρίγδουπος πόσις Ἥρης stands elsewhere in oaths and in the claim that Zeus has roused a man (7.411, 10.329, 13.154); here it is a condition, and the god's gift is what Achilles fears. See [[zeus]] and [[achilles]].
