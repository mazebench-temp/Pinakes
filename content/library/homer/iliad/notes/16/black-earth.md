---
summary: In the simile of the autumn storm, “the whole black earth is weighed down” under the rain that Zeus sends against unjust men; the book adds κελαινὴ … χθών.
grc: ["κελαινὴ βέβριθε χθὼν"]
---

“And as under a storm the whole black earth is weighed down / on a day of late summer, when he pours down the most violent rain, / Zeus, when in resentment he grows angry with men / who by force in the assembly judge crooked ordinances” (16.384–387; [[the-storm-of-late-summer]], [[zeus]], [[themis]]). The adjective is κελαινή, not μέλαινα, and stands apart from χθών; the phrase κελαινὴ βέβριθε χθών is new to the entry. The simile measures the Trojan rout against the flood that the god sends on the land (16.389–393).
