summary: The dying Patroclus prophesies Hector's death at the hands of Achilles, “you yourself will not live long” (16.851–854), and Hector, speaking to the dead man, asks, “why do you prophesy sheer destruction for me? / Who knows if Achilles … may not first be struck by my spear?” (16.859–861). Earlier Achilles denies that he is shunning any oracle (16.50–51).
passages:
  - 16.851-861 | Patroclus foretells Hector's death
---

Patroclus' prophecy is the first of a pair: Hector, dying, will foretell Achilles' death at the Scaean gates, and the same three verses close his life (22.355–363; see [[leaving-manhood-and-youth]] and [[death-of-achilles-foretold]]). Hector's answer is a question, τίς δʼ οἶδʼ, “who knows”, and the poem has already given the answer: Patroclus' words come true in Book 22. The narrator foretells as well, throughout the book: Patroclus was begging for his own death (16.46–47), Zeus refused his safe return (16.250–252), Patroclus “was to / kill” Sarpedon (16.460–461), and the helmet given to Hector brought his destruction near (16.799–800; see [[foresight]]).
