summary: Two full prayer scenes. Achilles' has every part, the longest preparation in the poem and an answer divided in two; Glaucus' has no ritual at all, a claim of need instead of service, and an answer given at once (16.220–256, 16.508–531; see [[prayer]]).
refs: [16.231-252, 16.513-531]
---

In Achilles' scene the setting and gesture take thirteen verses: the chest, the cup, the sulfur, the water, the washed hands, the wine poured, the eyes lifted to the sky (16.220–232). Invocation, claim and request follow the words of Chryses (16.233–238 ~ 1.451–455), and the answer is the closing verse with a second verse that divides it: Zeus “granted him one thing, and refused him the other” (16.249–252). In Glaucus' scene the only gesture is a wounded man gripping his arm (16.510); the invocation tells the god he can hear from anywhere, the claim is the wound and Sarpedon's death, and the answer is the closing verse of Chryses' prayers and the cure (16.514–529).
