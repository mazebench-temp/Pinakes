---
summary: Sarpedon sees his Lycian companions, “who wear their tunics without the band,” brought down by Patroclus, and turns to meet him.
---

The word is said of the Lycians as Sarpedon sees them falling: “And when Sarpedon saw his companions, who wear their tunics without the band, / brought down at the hands of Patroclus, son of Menoetius” (16.419–420; [[son-of-menoetius]]). It comes just after the list of nine men whom Patroclus brings down one after another (16.415–418), and Sarpedon, rebuking his Lycians for their flight, leaps down from his chariot to meet him (16.421–426; [[swift]], [[antitheos]]).
