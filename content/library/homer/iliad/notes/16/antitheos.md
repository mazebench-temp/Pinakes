summary: “Godlike Thrasymedes” kills Maris, Sarpedon calls to “the godlike Lycians,” Zeus ponders over “godlike Sarpedon,” and Hector goes after Automedon, “the godlike attendant”; the book adds the dative plural ἀντιθέοισι.
grc: [ἀντιθέοισι]
---

**Thrasymedes.** Maris springs at Antilochus to avenge his brother, “but godlike Thrasymedes / was first to reach out, before he could stab” (16.321–322; [[thrasymedes]], [[maris]], [[antilochus]]).

**The Lycians and Sarpedon.** Seeing his companions fall, Sarpedon “called out, approaching the godlike Lycians with words” (16.421; [[lycians]], [[swift]]). Over his body, Zeus ponders whether Hector should kill Patroclus “over godlike Sarpedon” (16.649; [[sarpedon]]).

**Automedon.** At the end Hector goes after Automedon, “the godlike attendant of the swift-footed grandson of Aeacus” (16.865; [[automedon]], [[grandson-of-aeacus]]). The dative plural ἀντιθέοισι is new to the entry.
