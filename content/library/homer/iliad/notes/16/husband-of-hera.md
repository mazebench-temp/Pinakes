---
summary: Achilles warns Patroclus not to fight on if “the loud-thundering husband of Hera grants you to win glory.”
---

“and if / the loud-thundering husband of Hera grants you to win glory, / do not long to fight without me / against the war-loving Trojans; you will make me more dishonored” (16.87–90; [[loud-thundering]], [[achilles]], [[patroclus]]). The title comes in a condition rather than an oath, and its gift is the danger: glory won without Achilles would take away from his honor ([[time]]). Later in the book the husband speaks “to Hera, his sister and wife” about Sarpedon (16.432; [[hera]]). See [[zeus]].
