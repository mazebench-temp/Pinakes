---
summary: Idomeneus is the last of the leaders who each kill a man as the Trojans give way: he stabs Erymas in the mouth, and the spear passes up under the brain (16.345–350).
refs: [16.346-350]
---

Ἰδομενεὺς δʼ Ἐρύμαντα κατὰ στόμα νηλέϊ χαλκῷ / νύξε, “And Idomeneus stabbed Erymas in the mouth with the pitiless bronze” (16.345–346; see [[erymas-killed-by-idomeneus]] and [[pitiless-bronze]]). The wound is described in five verses: the spear passes straight through below the brain and splits the white bones, the teeth are shaken out, both eyes fill with blood, the blood spurts through mouth and nostrils as he gapes, “and the black cloud of death enfolded him” (16.346–350). θανάτου δὲ μέλαν νέφος, “the black cloud of death”, occurs nowhere else in the Iliad; the Odyssey uses the verse once (*Odyssey* 4.180). “These leaders of the Danaans each killed his man” (16.351; see [[androktasia]]).

He kills just after his companion Meriones, with whom he fought on the left of the ships in Book 13 (13.246–329; see [[meriones]]). In the first such list, in Book 5, Idomeneus stabbed Phaestus “on the right shoulder as he was mounting his chariot” (5.43–47; see [[phaestus-son-of-borus]]); in this book the verse goes to Meriones and his victim Acamas (16.343 = 5.46).
