---
summary: Patroclus asks for Achilles' armor “to put on my shoulders” and puts on his corselet, “the inlaid corselet, starry” (16.40, 16.133–134); Achilles arms the Myrmidons (16.155); and Apollo, last of all, loosens the corselet from Patroclus (16.804).
grc: [θώρηξεν, θωρηχθῆναι]
en: [armed them, to put on]
---

The verb θωρήσσω, “arm”, runs through the first half of the book: Patroclus asks to arm in Achilles' armor (16.40), Achilles arms the Myrmidons “all of them, throughout the huts” (16.155–156), two men arm in front of them all (16.218), and those who armed with Patroclus march out (16.257). The corselet itself is Achilles' starry one (16.134; see [[starry]] and [[arming-scene]]), and its loosening by Apollo leaves Patroclus “naked” (16.804, 16.815).
