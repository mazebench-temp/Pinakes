---
summary: Ares takes no part in Book 16; his name is war itself. Achilles speaks of “the toil of Ares”, Glaucus says that “bronze Ares” has brought Sarpedon down by Patroclus' spear, Ares lets the might go out of a spent spear, and Patroclus charges “the equal of swift Ares”.
---

Since Athena sat him down on his throne in Book 15 (15.142; see [[athena]]), the god has not come back to the battle, and in Book 16 his name is a word for war and its violence. Achilles prays that Hector may learn whether Patroclus can fight alone, or whether his hands rage only “when I go into the toil of Ares” (μῶλον Ἄρηος, 16.243–245; see [[achilles]]), the phrase of the men who prayed to escape death and the toil of Ares in Book 2 (2.401). Glaucus tells Hector that Sarpedon lies dead: “bronze Ares has brought him down by the spear of Patroclus” (16.541–543; see [[bronze-ares]], [[glaucus]] and [[sarpedon]]). The war god kills with a man's spear. When Aeneas' spear misses Meriones and sticks quivering in the ground, “then mighty Ares let its might go” (16.611–613; see [[obrimos]] and [[aeneas]]), the verse in which Idomeneus' spear, fixed in the heart of Alcathous and shaken by its beating, came to rest in Book 13 (13.442–444; see [[alcathous]]). And when Patroclus charges the Trojans three times, he is “the equal of swift Ares” (16.784; see [[equal-of-ares]] and [[patroclus]]), the title of Meriones in Book 13 (13.295, 13.328, 13.528) and of Hector in Book 8 (8.215). The Achaeans are ἀρηΐφιλοι, “dear to Ares”, that is, warlike (16.303; see [[dear-to-ares]]).
