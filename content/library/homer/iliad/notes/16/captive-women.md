---
summary: Achilles' grievance is still the girl “whom the sons of the Achaeans picked out for me as my prize”, won when he sacked a well-walled city and taken back by Agamemnon (16.56–59), and he sends Patroclus to win her back (16.85–86). Hector imagines Patroclus carrying off the Trojan women in his ships (16.830–832).
---

The two women of the book's speeches belong to the two kinds of the poem's captives: the prize already taken, whom Achilles wants back with gifts (see [[geras]] and [[the-gifts-of-agamemnon]]), and the women of a city not yet taken, whose freedom Hector defends (see [[slavery-and-freedom]]).
