summary: Eileithyia, “who brings hard birth”, brings Eudorus, the son of Hermes and Polymele, out into the light.

  - 16.187-188 | The birth of Eudorus
---

“But when Eileithyia, who brings hard birth, had brought him / out into the light, and he saw the rays of the sun” (16.187–188; see [[who-brings-hard-birth]], [[eudorus]], [[polymele]] and [[the-birth-of-eudorus]]), Polymele was married to Echecles and her father reared the child (16.189–192). Eileithyia is one goddess here and in Zeus' boast in Book 19 that she will that day bring into the light a man who will rule all around him (19.103–105); she is many, the Eileithyiai, in the simile of Book 11, where they are “the daughters of Hera” (11.270–271), and when Hera holds them back from Alcmene (19.119). πρὸ φόως δέ is φόωσδε, “into the light”, the suffix of direction printed apart (see the translation's note on 16.188), as in the same phrase at the birth of Eurystheus (19.118). To see the light of the sun is to be alive: Diomedes' enemy boasts that he “shall not look much longer on the bright light of the sun” (5.120; see [[helios]]).
