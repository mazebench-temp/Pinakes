---
summary: Hera and Zeus send Sarpedon's body “to the land of wide Lycia,” and Sleep and Death set it down “in the rich land of wide Lycia.”
---

Hera's counsel: “send Death and sweet Sleep to carry him / until they come to the land of wide Lycia, / where his brothers and his kinsmen will give him burial” (16.454–456; [[sweet-sleep]], [[the-prize-of-the-dead]]). Zeus repeats it in his orders to Apollo, and the narrator tells it done: Sleep and Death “quickly / set him down in the rich land of wide Lycia” (16.672–673, 16.682–683; [[sleep]], [[death]]). The phrase of Glaucus' story of Bellerophon and his ancestors (6.173, 6.188, 6.210) closes the story of Sarpedon's death. See [[lycia]] and [[the-death-of-sarpedon]].
