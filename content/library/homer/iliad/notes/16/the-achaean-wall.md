summary: The ditch that Apollo bridged for the Trojans in Book 15 now traps them: driven from the ships, they are held back by it, and many chariots break their poles in it, while Hector's horses carry him across and Achilles' immortal horses leap it. Patroclus calls the dead Sarpedon the first to leap inside the wall (16.367–383, 16.510–512, 16.558).
# τείχεος ὑψηλοῖο at 16.512 is this wall (Teucer's arrow, 12.387–388), taken by refs. At 16.397 the "high
# wall" that, with the ships and the river, bounds the ground where Patroclus pens the Trojans is not claimed.
refs: [16.512]
---

**The ditch.** The Trojans go back from the ships in disorder, “and they did not cross back in good order”. Hector's horses carry him out with his armor, “and he left the army / of the Trojans, whom the ditch that had been dug held back against their will. / And many swift chariot-drawing horses in the ditch / broke their lords' chariots at the end of the pole and left them” (16.367–371; see [[hector]], [[dug]] and [[chariot]]). In Book 15 Apollo had kicked the banks into a causeway for the Trojans' chariots and thrown the wall down (15.355–366); now the ditch holds back those he let through. Patroclus drives after them, and “straight over the ditch leapt the swift horses, / immortal, the splendid gifts that the gods gave to Peleus” (16.380–381; see [[patroclus]] and [[the-horses-of-achilles]]). On the first day of fighting after the wall was built Hector had scorned it: “the horses will easily leap over the ditch they have dug” (8.179). It is Achilles' horses that do so.

**The high wall.** Glaucus' arm is still wearing him down with “the wound where Teucer had hit him with an arrow as he rushed on / at the high wall” (16.511–512; see [[glaucus]] and [[teucer]]), in the fight for the wall in Book 12 (12.387–391; see [[the-fight-at-the-wall]]).

**The first inside.** Over Sarpedon's body Patroclus tells the two Ajaxes, “There lies the man who was the first to leap inside the wall of the Achaeans, / Sarpedon” (16.558–559; see [[sarpedon]] and [[the-two-ajaxes]]). In Book 12 the narrator gave the words to Hector, “who was the first to leap inside the wall of the Achaeans” (12.438), though it was Sarpedon who pulled down the battlement and “made a path for many” (12.397–399; see the translation's note on 16.558).
