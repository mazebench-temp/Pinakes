summary: Peneleos and the Trojan Lycon miss each other with their spears and close with swords; Lycon's sword shatters on Peneleos' helmet, and Peneleos' sword sinks into Lycon's neck below the ear until only the skin holds the head (16.335–341).
refs: [16.336-339, 16.341]
---

Πηνέλεως δὲ Λύκων τε συνέδραμον, “And Peneleos and Lycon ran together” (16.335; see [[lycon]]). The duel is told step by step. Both throw and miss; they close with swords; Lycon strikes first, on the ridge of Peneleos' horsehair-crested helmet, “and at the socket / the sword was shattered” (16.337–339; see [[helmet]], [[horsehair-crested]] and [[sword]]); καυλός, “socket”, is the part of the helmet that holds the crest (see the translation's note on 16.338). Then Peneleos strikes: “but he struck him on the neck below the ear, / Peneleos, and the whole sword sank in, and only the skin / held, and the head hung to one side, and his limbs were loosened” (16.339–341). His name is held back to the start of the verse, after the blow (see [[loosened-his-limbs]]).

It is Peneleos' second kill in the poem, and again his sword finishes the work at the neck. In Book 14 he drove his spear through Ilioneus' eye, struck off the head with his sword, and held it up on the spear like a poppy head to taunt the Trojans (14.493–505; see [[ilioneus]] and [[the-poppy]]). He is seventh in the list of leaders who each kill a man as the Trojans give way (16.306–351; see [[androktasia]] and [[boeotians]]).
