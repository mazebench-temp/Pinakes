summary: Hecuba is named once, as the sister of Asius, son of Dymas, whose likeness Apollo takes to send Hector against Patroclus (16.718).
---

αὐτοκασίγνητος Ἑκάβης, “own brother of Hecuba” (16.718): the poem names Hecuba's family only here, when Apollo comes to Hector in the likeness of “Asius, who was the uncle of Hector, tamer of horses” (16.717; see [[asius-son-of-dymas]], [[dymas]] and [[hector]]). The god takes the shape of a kinsman on his mother's side, a Phrygian from the Sangarius (16.719; see [[phrygia]] and [[sangarius]]).
