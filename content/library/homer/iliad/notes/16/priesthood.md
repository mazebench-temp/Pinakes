---
summary: Laogonus, killed by Meriones, was the son of Onetor, “who was the priest of Zeus / of Ida, and was honored by the people like a god” (16.604–605). Around Zeus of Dodona live the Selli, his “interpreters”, with unwashed feet, sleeping on the ground (16.234–235).
---

Two priests' sons died in the same way in Book 5: Phegeus, son of Dares, priest of Hephaestus (5.9–19), and Hypsenor, son of Dolopion, who “had been made priest of the Scamander / and was honored by the people like a god” (5.77–78), in the words used of Onetor (ἐτέτυκτο, θεὸς δʼ ὣς τίετο δήμῳ, 16.605 = 5.78). The priest's honor among his people does not keep his son alive (see [[time]]). The Selli of Dodona are not called priests: they are ὑποφῆται, “interpreters” of the god (see [[selli]] and [[dodona]]).
