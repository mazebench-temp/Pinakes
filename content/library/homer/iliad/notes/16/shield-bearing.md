---
summary: Achilles urges on “the shield-bearing men,” and Sarpedon is three times “the leader of the shield-bearing Lycians,” a title that passes to Glaucus after his death.
---

**The Myrmidons.** “among them stood warlike Achilles, / urging on the horses and the shield-bearing men” (16.166–167; [[warlike]], [[myrmidons]]); ἀσπιδιώτης is the second word of the entry.

**The Lycians.** The formula Λυκίων ἀγὸς ἀσπιστάων names Sarpedon as he dies, “so under Patroclus the leader of the shield-bearing Lycians / raged as he was killed” (16.490–491), and in Glaucus' report, “Sarpedon lies dead, the leader of the shield-bearing Lycians” (16.541; [[bronze-ares]]). Then it is Glaucus' own: “And Glaucus, leader of the shield-bearing Lycians, was the first / to turn” (16.593; [[glaucus]], [[bathycles]]). The title passes from the dead king to his friend within a hundred verses. See [[lycians]] and [[sarpedon]].
