summary: Achilles' trace horse Pedasus is a spoil of Thebe, “whom Achilles once brought back when he took the city of Eetion” (16.153). The mortal horse runs beside the immortal ones into Patroclus' battle, and Sarpedon's spear kills it (16.152–154, 16.466–475).

**A spoil in harness.** When Automedon yokes the immortal horses Xanthus and Balius for Patroclus, he puts a third horse in the trace:

{{quote:63de869a-83d3-5ee3-a487-005fd665b21e}}

τόν ῥά ποτʼ Ἠετίωνος ἑλὼν πόλιν ἤγαγʼ Ἀχιλλεύς, “whom Achilles once brought back when he took the city of Eetion” (16.153): Pedasus is part of the plunder of Thebe, like the lyre that Achilles plays when the envoys find him (“he had taken it from the spoils when he destroyed the city of Eetion”, 9.188; see the note on Book 9) and the lump of iron that Eetion used to throw, which he will set as a prize at Patroclus' funeral games (23.826–829). The two immortal horses were “the splendid gifts that the gods gave to Peleus” (16.381, 16.867; see [[the-horses-of-achilles]]); the mortal horse beside them was won by Achilles' own spear. “Mortal though he was”, he “kept pace with the immortal horses” (16.154; see [[pedasus-the-horse]]). The horse shares its name with a son of Bucolion and with two towns, Pedasus by the Satnioeis and Pedasus in Messenia (6.21, 6.35, 9.152; see [[pedasus-son-of-bucolion]], [[pedasus-on-the-satnioeis]] and [[pedasus-in-messenia]]).

**Its death.** Pedasus dies in the meeting of Patroclus and Sarpedon. Sarpedon's spear misses Patroclus and strikes the horse in the right shoulder; it screams, gasping out its life, and falls in the dust, and the immortal pair start apart and tangle the reins, until Automedon cuts the trace horse loose with his sword (16.466–475; see [[automedon]] and [[the-death-of-sarpedon]]). The verse of its death, κὰδ δʼ ἔπεσʼ ἐν κονίῃσι μακών, ἀπὸ δʼ ἔπτατο θυμός, “and fell down in the dust with a cry, and its spirit flew away” (16.469), is said in the Odyssey of the stag Odysseus kills on Circe's island and of the boar that wounded him on Parnassus (*Odyssey* 10.163, 19.454). Nestor's trace horse, struck in the head by Paris' arrow, threw his team into confusion in the same way, and Nestor too cut it loose with his sword (8.80–91). Of the spoils of Thebe that the poem names, only the horse dies in the fighting.

**The trace horse.** The παρήορος, a third horse harnessed beside the yoked pair, appears in the Iliad only in these two scenes, Nestor's and Achilles'. Walter Leaf reports that some critics wished to remove the Pedasus verses (16.152–154, 16.462–476) because the trace horse is otherwise so rare, but he judged the second passage “particularly vigorous and picturesque”, not to be dispensed with (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 16.152 and 16.467).
