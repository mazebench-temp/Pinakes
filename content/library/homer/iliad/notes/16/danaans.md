---
summary: Patroclus hopes to become “a light to the Danaans”, and Achilles sends him to bring light to the ships and win him honor from all the Danaans. When the fire is out the Danaans draw breath a little, like a mountain cleared of cloud, and fall on the Trojans like wolves on lambs (16.39, 16.84–85, 16.95, 16.295–302, 16.351–356).
---

**A light to the Danaans.** Patroclus asks to be sent out “in the hope that I may become a light to the Danaans” (16.39), in Nestor's words to him (11.797) and in Agamemnon's to Teucer (8.282; see [[patroclus]] and [[nestor]]). Achilles gives the same charge in his own terms: “turn back, once you have brought light to the ships” (16.95), and win him “great honor and glory / from all the Danaans” (16.84–85; see [[achilles]] and [[time]]). After Patroclus' death Achilles will say that he was no light to Patroclus or to his other companions (18.102). φάος, “light”, is the light of rescue (see the translation's note on 15.741).

**A breathing space.** When the fire is put out the Danaans pour after the Trojans among the ships (16.295–296), and the narrator compares the relief to a mountain from which Zeus moves a thick cloud, so that the peaks stand out and the boundless upper air breaks open: “so the Danaans, when they had thrust the consuming fire from the ships, / drew breath a little, but there was no respite from the war” (16.297–302; see [[zeus]]). ἀνέπνευσαν, “drew breath”, is what Patroclus hoped for when he asked for the armor, “and the warlike sons of the Achaeans may draw breath” (16.42), and τυτθόν, “a little”, gives the saying that followed its force: “brief is the breathing space in war” (16.43; see [[brief-is-the-breathing-space]]).

**Like wolves.** “These leaders of the Danaans each killed his man” (16.351; see [[androktasia]]), and the army falls on the Trojans as wolves fall on lambs and kids scattered by a careless shepherd (16.352–356; see [[like-wolves]]). Patroclus follows, “fiercely calling on the Danaans” (16.372). Glaucus, rousing the Trojans to fight for Sarpedon, fears the Myrmidons' revenge, “angry for all the Danaans who have perished, / whom we killed with our spears beside the swift ships” (16.546–547; see [[glaucus]] and [[myrmidons]]). At the end, Hector “let the other Danaans be, and did not slay them”, and drove at Patroclus alone (16.731–732; see [[hector]]).
