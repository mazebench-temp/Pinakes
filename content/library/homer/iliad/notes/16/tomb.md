---
summary: Hera's counsel and Zeus' order give Sarpedon the burial of a king in his own land: his brothers and kinsmen “will give him burial / with a tomb and a pillar; for that is the prize of the dead” (16.456–457 = 16.674–675; see [[the-prize-of-the-dead]]).
grc: [ταρχύσουσι]
en: [give him burial]
---

τύμβος, the mound, and στήλη, the pillar set on it, are the two parts of the grave that the living see; Paris leans on the pillar of Ilus' tomb to shoot at Diomedes (11.371–372). ταρχύω, “give burial”, is used only of Sarpedon and in Hector's challenge in Book 7, where he promises the Achaeans the body of the man he kills, to bury him and heap up a mound by the Hellespont (7.84–91; see [[fame-and-the-tomb]]).
