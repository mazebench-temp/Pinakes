---
summary: Two cleansings with καθαίρω, the verb with which Hera washed the defilements from her body (14.171). Achilles cleanses his cup with sulfur before pouring to Zeus (16.228), and Zeus tells Apollo to cleanse the dark blood from Sarpedon's body before it is anointed and sent home (16.667).
grc: ["ἐκάθηρε θεείῳ", "αἷμα κάθηρον"]
en: [cleansed it with sulfur, cleanse the dark blood]
---

The cup is cleansed “with sulfur / first, and then washed … in fair streams of water” (16.228–229; see [[cup]] and [[libation]]). Sulfur is used once more in Homer to purify: after the killing of the suitors Odysseus calls for sulfur and fire, “the remedy of evils”, to fumigate his hall (*Odyssey* 22.481–482, 22.493–494). In the Iliad sulfur otherwise burns where the thunderbolt strikes (8.135, 14.415; see [[thunderbolt]]). Sarpedon's body is cleansed of its blood, washed in the river, anointed with ambrosia and clothed in immortal clothing, the care of the dead done by a god (16.667–670, 16.678–680; see [[bathing]] and [[honoring-the-dead]]).
