---
summary: “Brilliant swift-footed Achilles” pities Patroclus as he stands weeping beside him.
---

“And seeing him, brilliant swift-footed Achilles pitied him, / and he spoke to him, addressing him with winged words” (16.5–6; [[brilliant]], [[pity]], [[winged-words]]). The formula ποδάρκης δῖος Ἀχιλλεύς introduces Achilles' first speech of the book, with its likeness of Patroclus to a little girl in tears (16.7–11; [[like-a-girl]], [[tender]]). Achilles pities his friend at the beginning of the book, as Zeus pities Sarpedon and Patroclus at its center (16.431; [[son-of-cronus]]). See [[achilles]] and [[patroclus]].
