---
summary: Odysseus “famed for his spear” is among the wounded, Automedon “famed for his spear” cuts the dead trace horse loose, and Meriones “famed for his spear” answers Aeneas.
---

Patroclus names the wounded: “and Odysseus famed for his spear has been stabbed, and Agamemnon” (16.26; [[odysseus]], [[the-wounded-chiefs]]). When Sarpedon's spear kills the trace horse Pedasus, “Automedon, famed for his spear, found a way out of this: / he drew the long-edged sword from beside his stout thigh” (16.472–473; [[automedon]], [[long-edged]], [[pedasus-the-horse]]). And after Aeneas' taunt, “Meriones, famed for his spear, answered him face to face” (16.619; [[meriones]], [[aeneas]]). The epithet of the spearman is given here to a charioteer whose deed is done with a sword.
