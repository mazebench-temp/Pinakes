---
summary: Twice the river's wave falls on Achilles: “so often the great wave of the river fallen from Zeus / beat upon his shoulders from above” (21.268–269), and “the surging wave of the river fallen from Zeus / stood, lifted high, and was bringing the son of Peleus down” (21.326–327).
---

Both times the formula names the wave, κῦμα διιπετέος ποταμοῖο. In Book 17 the shout of the Trojans was likened to a great wave that roars at the mouth of a river fallen from Zeus (17.263–264); here the wave is the river's own, and it is turned against one man. The second wave is “surging” (πορφύρεον, 21.326), as the wave about the ship's keel in Book 1 (1.482; see the translation's table of key words). See [[scamander]] and [[achilles]].
