# Unnamed: the shield struck by Asteropaeus (21.164-165), and the shield the stream falls on (21.241).
refs: [21.164-165, 21.241]
---

**The gold.** Asteropaeus, throwing two spears at once, “hit the shield, but it did not go through / and break the shield; for the gold held it back, the gifts of the god” (21.164–165; see [[asteropaeus]] and [[gold-and-silver]]). The second verse was said when Aeneas' heavy spear struck the same shield (20.268), and Walter Leaf, who takes the gold there to stand for the whole metal facing of the shield, uses the repetition here to show that 20.268 needs no expansion by the four verses that make its five layers metals (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 20.268, 20.269; see the note on Book 20). Agenor's javelin, at the end of the book, is held back by the greave in the same words, θεοῦ δʼ ἠρύκακε δῶρα, “for the gifts of the god held it back” (21.594; see [[greaves]] and [[gifts-of-the-gods]]).

**The stream.** When Achilles leaps into the river, “terribly around Achilles the churning wave rose up, / and the stream, falling on his shield, pushed him back” (21.240–241; see [[scamander]] and [[achilles-and-the-river]]). The shield that stopped the spears of Aeneas and Asteropaeus does not stop the river, and Achilles loses his footing (21.241–242). The river means to bury the armor with him: neither his strength nor his looks will help him, “nor that fine armor, which, I think, deep at the bottom of the lake / will lie covered under the slime” (21.317–318). Leaf takes τά in τὰ τεύχεα καλά as a true pronoun, “those his fair arms” (on 21.317; see [[the-making-of-the-arms]]).
