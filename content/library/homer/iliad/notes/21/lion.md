---
summary: The one lion of Book 21 is Artemis, whom Hera calls “a lion to women”, since Zeus has given her leave to kill whichever of them she wishes (21.483–484).
---

ἐπεὶ σὲ λέοντα γυναιξὶ / Ζεὺς θῆκεν, “since a lion to women / Zeus has made you” (21.483–484; see [[a-lion-to-women]], [[artemis]] and [[hera]]). It is a metaphor, not a simile: the beast of so many similes for warriors on the attack becomes the goddess who kills women with her arrows. Walter Leaf notes that the masculine λέοντα is strange of a goddess, but that the feminine is not found in Homer, and that λέων may be taken as of either gender (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 21.483). In the same book Agenor faces Achilles like a leopard that will not flee from the hunter (21.573–578; see [[leopard]]).
