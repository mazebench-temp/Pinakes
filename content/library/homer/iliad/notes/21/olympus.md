---
summary: Zeus sits on Olympus and laughs as the gods clash. Poseidon tells Apollo that it would be the more shameful to go back to Olympus without a fight; Artemis, beaten, goes up to the bronze-floored house of Zeus, and when the battle is over the other gods go back to Olympus, some angry and some exulting, and sit down beside their father.
---

“And Zeus heard it / as he sat on Olympus” (21.388–389; see [[zeus]] and [[watching-the-war]]), where he had said he would stay and look on (20.22–23). In Book 20 Poseidon expected that if the gods of the Trojan side began the battle, they would soon “go back to Olympus, to the gathering of the other gods, / brought down at our hands under compulsion” (20.142–143); in Book 21 he tells Apollo that it would be the more shameful if the two of them went back to Olympus, “to the bronze-floored house of Zeus”, without a fight (21.437–438; see [[poseidon]], [[apollo]] and [[bronze-floored]]). Οὔλυμπον δέ, “to Olympus” (21.438), is the noun with the suffix of direction printed apart, as Zeus' Οὔλυμπον δέ in Book 8 (8.12; see the translation's note on 21.3).
