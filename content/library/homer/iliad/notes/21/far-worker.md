summary: “far-working lord Apollo” answers Poseidon (21.461); Artemis taunts him, “So you flee, Far-Worker” (21.472), and he says nothing to her (21.478); and “the Far-Worker, in the likeness of Agenor himself in every way,” draws Achilles away from the city (21.600).
---

The god who will not fight Poseidon for the sake of mortals (21.461–467) is silent under his sister's scorn (21.478; [[so-he-spoke]]), and ends the book by deceiving Achilles in a man's likeness (21.599–605; [[truth-and-deception]], [[divine-epiphany]]). See [[apollo]] and [[phoebus]].
