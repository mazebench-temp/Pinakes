summary: In the battle of the gods Athena fights with a stone: she picks up a boundary stone from the plain, strikes Ares on the neck and loosens his limbs.
passages:
  - 21.403-406 | Athena's boundary stone
---

{{quote:e153da5a-ee7f-5431-a928-7ce636a9364e}}

The first two verses are those of Hector in his duel with Ajax, who gave ground and took up a stone “black and jagged and great” (7.264–265; Walter Leaf, *Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 21.403); here it is the goddess who gives ground and picks up the stone, and the god she strikes. Some ancient readers objected to χειρὶ παχείῃ, “strong hand”, of a goddess, and would have read βαρείῃ or Παλλὰς Ἀθήνη; Leaf answers with the same words of Penelope's hand in the *Odyssey* and of Hera's in the *Hymn to Apollo* (*Odyssey* 21.6; *Hymn to Apollo* 340; on 21.403). The stone is a landmark that men of former times had set up (21.405; see [[boundary-stones]]). The blow, κατʼ αὐχένα, “on the neck”, ends with the words of a man's fall in battle, λῦσε δὲ γυῖα, “and loosened his limbs” (21.406; see [[loosened-his-limbs]]), but the god only falls, and covers seven plethra (21.407; see [[ares]], [[athena]] and [[the-battle-of-the-gods]]).
