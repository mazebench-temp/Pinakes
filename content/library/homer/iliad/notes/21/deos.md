summary: Fear of Achilles fills the river, where the Trojans cower like fishes that flee a dolphin “in fear” (21.22–26), and Priam fears “that this baneful man may leap inside the wall” (21.536). But Achilles too runs from the river “in fear” (21.248), Hera shouts, “terribly afraid for Achilles” (21.328), and Oceanus himself “fears the thunderbolt of great Zeus” (21.198).
grc: [δείδοικε, περιδείσασʼ]
en: [fears]
---

Poseidon's encouragement uses the other verbs of fear, “do not tremble too much, nor be afraid” (21.288), and Walter Leaf distinguishes τρέε, to shrink back in alarm, from τάρβει (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 21.288). The leopard of Agenor's simile “neither fears nor flees” (21.575; see [[phobos]] and [[the-dolphin-and-the-fishes]]).
