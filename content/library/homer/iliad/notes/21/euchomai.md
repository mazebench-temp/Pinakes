summary: In this book the verb is all claim and boast. Achilles “spoke in triumph” over Asteropaeus (21.183) and says, “but I claim to be of the lineage of great Zeus” (21.187); Poseidon and Athena promise him “a triumph” (21.297); Athena, “boasting over” Ares, tells him “how much stronger / I claim to be than you” (21.409–411); Artemis taunts Apollo with the triumph he gave Poseidon “for nothing” (21.473) and with his old boasting (21.476); and Hermes tells Leto to “boast that you overcame me by your mighty strength” (21.501).
grc: [ἐπευχομένη]
---

ἐπευχόμενος, “boasting over him”, is the narrator's word for the speech over a fallen enemy. The book gives it to Achilles over Lycaon (21.121), and then twice to Athena, over Ares and over Ares and Aphrodite together (21.409, 21.427): the gods boast over one another as the men do (see [[the-gods-mirror-the-mortals]]). Achilles' claim answers Asteropaeus' recital of his descent from the river Axius (21.153–160), with the verb in which Glaucus and Aeneas claimed their blood (6.211 = 20.241; see [[genee]] and [[genealogy-in-battle]]). No one in the book prays (see [[prayer]]).
