---
summary: Achilles “spoke to his own proud heart” when he saw Lycaon (21.53), as Agenor does when he sees Achilles (21.552); Lycaon wished “in his heart” to escape (21.65). The gods' heart “was blown two ways” when strife fell among them (21.386), Athena “rejoiced in her heart” as she went after Aphrodite (21.423), and Poseidon and Apollo went home from Laomedon “with resentful hearts” (21.456).
---

Walter Leaf takes θυμὸς ἄητο as passive, the heart blown about by gusts of passion, and compares ἀεσίφρων, “light of mind” (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 21.386; see the translation's note on 21.386). Agenor's heart debates with him in the verse of Odysseus and Hector, “But why does my heart debate these things with me?” (21.562; see [[pondering-two-ways]] and [[deeply-troubled]]). The θυμός is also the life that Achilles takes from Asteropaeus and will take from Hector (21.179, 21.296).
