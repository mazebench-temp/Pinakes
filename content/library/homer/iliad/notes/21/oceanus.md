# Unnamed: "from whom all rivers ... flow; / yet even he fears the thunderbolt of great Zeus" (21.196-199).
refs: [21.196-199]
---

{{quote:926be7aa-29dc-5ebb-ad73-c9d63a16740f}}

**The greatest of waters.** Achilles has told the dead Asteropaeus, a grandson of the river Axius, that the lineage of Zeus is mightier than a river's, and that there is no fighting with Zeus, with whom not even lord Achelous can match himself, “nor the great strength of deep-flowing Oceanus, / from whom all rivers and all the sea / and all springs and deep wells flow” (21.195–197; see [[zeus-is-mightier-than-rivers]], [[achelous]] and [[rivers]]). μέγα σθένος Ὠκεανοῖο, “the great strength of … Oceanus”, ends the verse as it ends the verse of the river on the rim of Achilles' shield (18.607; see [[the-shield-of-achilles]]). βαθυρρείτης, “deep-flowing”, is found only here; elsewhere Oceanus is βαθύρροος, “deep-streaming” (7.422, 14.311; see [[deep-streaming]]). That every river, the sea, and every spring and well flow from him is said only here, as only Hera and Sleep call him “the origin of the gods” and “the origin of all” (14.201, 14.246); and Oceanus alone of the rivers stayed away from the assembly of the gods that began this day's battle of the gods (20.7). Later in the book the Scamander calls on the Simoeis as “dear brother” (21.308; see [[simoeis]] and [[kasignetos]]).

**Below Zeus.** “Yet even he fears the thunderbolt of great Zeus / and his dread thunder, when it resounds from the sky” (21.198–199; see [[thunderbolt]] and [[zeus]]). Zenodotus omitted the verse of Oceanus, leaving Achelous as the source of all waters; Aristarchus kept it, because in Homer the parent of rivers is Oceanus, not Achelous, and Walter Leaf calls it “one of the most majestic lines ever written” (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 21.195; see the translation's note on 21.194–197).
