---
summary: Lycaon, begging Achilles for his life, names his mother's father, Altes, “who rules over the war-loving Leleges, / holding steep Pedasus on the Satnioeis” (21.86–87), the poem's one statement of where the Leleges live and who rules them.
---

{{quote:e65b4407-35bb-53dc-a3c2-f5ba791555c9}}

**Their king and their town.** Dolon placed the Leleges only “toward the sea” (10.428–429), and Aeneas named them beside Lyrnessus and Pedasus, the towns that Achilles sacked (20.92–96). Here they have a king, Altes, and a town, steep Pedasus on the river Satnioeis (see [[altes]], [[pedasus-on-the-satnioeis]] and [[satnioeis]]). The king's daughter, Laothoe, is one of Priam's wives, and her sons Lycaon and Polydorus are both killed by Achilles (21.88–91; see [[laothoe]], [[lycaon-son-of-priam]] and [[polydorus-son-of-priam]]). ἀνάσσει, “rules”, is present: Altes still reigns, though Achilles sacked a Pedasus in the years before (see the translation's note on 21.86–87 and [[anax]]).

**War-loving.** φιλοπτόλεμοι, “war-loving” (21.86; see [[war-loving]]), is elsewhere in the Iliad an epithet of Trojans, Achaeans and Myrmidons (16.65, 16.90, 17.224, 20.351); this is the only allied people to be given it. Strabo, who divides the Troad of Priam's time into nine dynasties, makes the Leleges of Altes the third, and quotes this verse (*Geography* 13.1.7; see [[strabo-on-the-city-of-mynes]]).
