---
summary: The Republic will not let the young believe that Achilles was disobedient to the river, though it was a god, and ready to fight it, nor that he slaughtered at the pyre the captives whom he takes alive in this book. The Cratylus names the Scamander as the river that fought in single combat with Hephaestus.
---

- Achilles' disobedience to the river, “though it was a god”, among the things that must not be believed of him, with the slaughter of the captives at the pyre (*Republic* 3.391a–c, on 21.211–234 and 21.26–32): [[plato-achilles-and-the-river]].
- The river at Troy “which fought in single combat with Hephaestus”, whom the gods call Xanthus and men Scamander (*Cratylus* 391e, on 21.328–382): [[plato-chalcis-and-cymindis]].
- All the battles of the gods that Homer composed, which may not be admitted into the city (*Republic* 2.378d), fought at last in this book (21.385–513): [[title-of-book-20]] and [[proclus-on-the-battle-of-the-gods]].
