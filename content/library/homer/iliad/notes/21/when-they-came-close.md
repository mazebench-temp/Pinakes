---
summary: The verse brings Achilles and Asteropaeus face to face, and Achilles speaks first (21.148–149).
---

The two verses are those of the meeting of Glaucus and Diomedes, “And when they had come close, advancing against each other, / Diomedes, good at the war cry, spoke to him first” (6.121–122), with Achilles' name in place of Diomedes', as they were at his meeting with Aeneas (20.176–177). The questions that follow are Diomedes' too: who are you, and the sons of unhappy men meet my might (21.150–151 ~ 6.123, 6.127; [[the-sons-of-unhappy-men]], [[genealogy-in-battle]]). Walter Leaf finds the first verse out of place here, since Achilles has already sprung at Asteropaeus (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 21.148; see the translation's note on 21.148–149).
