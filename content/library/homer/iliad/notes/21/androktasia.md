**Two histories.** The two named deaths of the first half of the book are told at length. Lycaon's history, his capture in his father's orchard, his sale into Lemnos and his ransom, comes before the meeting (21.34–48), and his mother and grandfather in his own plea (21.84–91; [[lycaon-son-of-priam]]). Asteropaeus gives his own lineage, from the river Axius (21.153–160; [[genealogy-in-battle]], [[asteropaeus]]). The blows are the type's: the sword at the collarbone, “and all the way in / the two-edged sword went” (21.117–118; [[two-edged]]); the sword beside the navel, in the verses of Diores' death (21.180–181 ~ 4.525–526; [[darkness-covered-his-eyes]]).

**Stripping.** In Book 20 Achilles took no armor; here he springs on Asteropaeus' chest and strips him before he boasts (21.182–183). Lycaon had no armor to take: he came out of the river “naked, without helmet or shield” (21.50).

**The list.** “There he killed Thersilochus and Mydon and Astypylus / and Mnesus and Thrasius and Aenius and Ophelestes” (21.209–210): seven names and nothing else, the barest form of the type, and the river stops it (21.211–212; [[would-have-had-not]], [[paeonians]], [[thersilochus]]).
