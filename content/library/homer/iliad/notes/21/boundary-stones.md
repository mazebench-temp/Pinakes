# οὖρον ἀρούρης (21.405), found only here.
grc: ["οὖρον ἀρούρης"]
en: [the boundary stone of a field]
passages:
  - 21.403-406 | Athena's boundary stone
---

{{quote:e153da5a-ee7f-5431-a928-7ce636a9364e}}

The first two verses are those of Hector's stone against Ajax (7.264–265; Walter Leaf, *Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 21.403; see [[stone-throwing]]); the third is new, and makes the goddess's weapon a landmark. οὖρον ἀρούρης, “the boundary stone of a field”, has the word of the Book 12 simile, where two men with measuring rods quarrel over the boundary stones of a common field (οὔροισι, 12.421; see [[the-boundary-dispute]]). Leaf compares the turning post of the chariot race, which Nestor thinks may have been set up in the time of earlier men (ἐπὶ προτέρων ἀνθρώπων, 23.332), and his note on the Book 12 simile (on 21.405). ἄνδρες πρότεροι, “men of former times”, set the stone to settle what belonged to whom; the goddess picks it up and strikes the god of war on the neck (see [[athena]], [[ares]] and [[the-battle-of-the-gods]]).
