summary: Lycaon was cutting fig shoots “with the sharp bronze” when Achilles caught him (21.37); he holds Achilles' “sharpened spear” as he begs (21.72); Achilles killed Polydorus “with your sharp spear” (21.91) and draws his sharp sword against Lycaon and Asteropaeus (21.116, 21.173); Agenor hopes that Achilles' flesh “can be pierced by the sharp bronze” (21.568) and throws “the sharp javelin” (21.590).
grc: ["ἔγχος ἀκαχμένον"]
---

The phrase ἔγχος ἀκαχμένον, “the sharpened spear” (21.72), is taken from the arming verse “And he took a strong spear, tipped with sharp bronze” (10.135; [[strong-spear]]); here the spear is Achilles' own, which Lycaon holds in one hand while he clasps his knees with the other (21.71–72; see the translation's table of key words). Agenor's hope, “For surely his flesh too can be pierced by the sharp bronze, / and there is one life in him” (21.568–569), is the hope of a man who knows nothing of a hero who cannot be wounded; Walter Leaf notes that Achilles' invulnerability is post-Homeric (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 21.568). See [[achilles]], [[sword]] and [[spear]].
