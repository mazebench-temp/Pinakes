---
summary: Apollo stands beside Agenor “to ward off the heavy hands of Death” (21.548).
grc: ["θανάτοιο βαρείας χεῖρας"]
---

All the manuscripts read χεῖρας, “hands”; κῆρας, “fates”, is found as early as Eustathius but has no manuscript authority, and Walter Leaf accepts the bold personification, which hardly goes beyond Sleep, “the brother of Death” (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 21.548; 14.231; see the translation's note on 21.548). The English gives Death his capital, as it gives Sleep and Death theirs when they carry Sarpedon home (16.672–683; see [[thanatos]]).
