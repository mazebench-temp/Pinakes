---
summary: Jason is named once in Book 21, in his son's title: when Achilles sold Lycaon into Lemnos, “the son of Jason paid the price” (21.41).
---

υἱὸς Ἰήσονος, “the son of Jason” (21.41), is Euneus, son of Jason and Hypsipyle, who trades with the Achaean camp from Lemnos (7.467–471; see [[euneus]] and [[lemnos]]). Elsewhere the Iliad calls him by the patronymic Ἰησονίδης (7.468, 7.471; 23.747). Walter Leaf reports that Bergk rejected the line as interpolated from the legend of the Argonauts, which the Iliad touches only in late passages (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 21.41; see [[the-lemnian-women]] and [[the-death-of-lycaon]]).
