summary: Under Hephaestus' fire the verb of burning begins three verses: the trees “were burning,” the lotus “was burning,” and “the force of the river was burning” (21.350, 21.351, 21.356); and Achilles' death will come “a morning or an evening or a midday” (21.111).
refs: [21.350, 21.351, 21.356, 21.111]
---

καίοντο … καίετο … καίετο: the fire passes from the trees on the banks to the plants by the water and to the river himself, who then speaks (21.350–356; [[called-him-by-name]]). In Achilles' answer to Lycaon the threefold ἢ … ἢ … ἢ, ἠὼς ἢ δείλη ἢ μέσον ἦμαρ, leaves only the hour of his death uncertain (21.111; see the translation's note on 21.111 and [[mighty-fate]]).
