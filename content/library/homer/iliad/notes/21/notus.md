---
summary: Hera promises to raise “the West Wind and the brightening South Wind” from the sea, a hard storm to carry Hephaestus' fire against the Trojans (21.334–337); she never does.
passages:
  - 21.334-337 | Hera's winds
---

ἀργεστᾶο Νότοιο, “the brightening South Wind” (21.334), the words of the simile of Book 11, where the West Wind batters the South Wind's clouds (11.305–306; see [[brightening]] and [[zephyrus]]). The phrase occurs only in these two places. Hera means to rouse the two winds “out of the sea” (ἐξ ἁλόθεν, 21.335), a hard storm that will carry the evil blaze to burn away the heads and the armor of the Trojans, while Hephaestus burns the river (21.334–338; see [[hera]], [[hephaestus]] and [[fire]]). The South Wind is elsewhere in Homer a wet wind of the sea, which heaps up waves and spreads mist on the mountains (2.145, 3.10; see [[waves-of-the-icarian-sea]]); here it is to drive a fire. Walter Leaf notes that Hera takes no steps to fulfil her promise (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 21.335). See [[achilles-and-the-river]].
