summary: Asteropaeus answers Achilles' question “in turn” (21.152); Achilles answers the river's plea in the formula itself (21.222); and in the battle of the gods Apollo answers Poseidon and Artemis answers Zeus “in turn” (21.461, 21.511).
grc: ["προσεφώνεε φαίδιμος υἱός"]
---

“Then the shining son of Pelegon spoke to him in turn” (21.152) has the line-ending of the Odyssey's replies, προσεφώνεε φαίδιμος υἱός (*Odyssey* 16.308, 24.243; [[phaidimos]]). Achilles' answer to the river, “Answering him, swift-footed Achilles said” (21.222; [[swift-footed]]), grants the plea in words and refuses it in deeds. The two replies of the gods are refusals of another kind: Apollo will not fight his father's brother for the sake of mortals (21.461–467), and Artemis, weeping on her father's knees, lays the blame on Hera (21.511–513; [[well-crowned]], [[clamorous]]).
