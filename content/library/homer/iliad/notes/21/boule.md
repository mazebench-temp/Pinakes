---
summary: Scamander reproaches Apollo: “you have not kept / the plans of the son of Cronus, who charged you many times over / to stand by the Trojans and defend them” (21.229–231).
---

The speech has been condemned, since Apollo is not there and nothing comes of it; Walter Leaf defends it as a reproach, not a cry for help, and notes that nothing is known of such commands of Zeus: an angry partisan takes Zeus' leave as a command (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 21.229; see the translation's note on 21.229–232 and [[plan-of-zeus]]).
