---
summary: Hephaestus burns the bodies on the plain, “and then he turned on the river the flame, gleaming all over” (21.349).
---

φλόγα παμφανόωσαν is the flame that Athena kindled above Achilles' head at the ditch, “a flame, gleaming all over” (18.206). The fire that made him terrible to the Trojans is now turned on the river that is drowning him. See [[hephaestus]] and [[scamander]].
