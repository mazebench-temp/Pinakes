summary: “But among the other gods strife fell, heavy / and grievous” (21.385–386), and Zeus laughs to see “the gods coming together in strife” (21.390). Ares begins with the charge that Athena drives “the gods together in strife” (21.394), and Artemis ends it on her father's knees, blaming Hera, “from whom strife and quarrel have fastened upon the immortals” (21.513). Before them the river begs Hephaestus, “Cease from strife” (21.359), and Achilles tells Asteropaeus that it is hard for him “to strive with the children of the mighty son of Cronus” (21.184).
---

The strife of the gods has come to blows, and Artemis' verb is the one with which Zeus bade the Dream tell Agamemnon that sorrows hang over the Trojans (ἐφῆπται, 2.15); Walter Leaf thinks a reading in which the quarrel has already come to a head more suitable (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 21.513).
