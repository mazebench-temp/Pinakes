---
summary: Lycaon meets Achilles “without helmet or shield” (21.50). Asteropaeus' spear strikes Achilles' shield and is held by the gold (21.164–165), the river's stream falls on the shield and pushes him back (21.241), and Agenor holds his shield before him as he waits for Achilles (21.581).
---

**Unarmed.** Lycaon comes out of the river “naked, without helmet or shield, and he had no spear either”, for he has thrown them all to the ground, worn down by sweat and weariness (21.50–52; see [[lycaon-son-of-priam]]). Walter Leaf takes the long, interrupted sentence to explain how Achilles recognized him: he was disarmed (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 21.50; see the translation's note on 21.49–53).

**Achilles' shield.** Asteropaeus' first spear “hit the shield, but it did not go through / and break the shield; for the gold held it back, the gifts of the god” (21.164–165; 21.165 = 20.268; see [[the-shield-of-achilles]] and [[gold-and-silver]]). The word is σάκος, as in the duel with Aeneas, while the shields of Lycaon and Agenor are ἀσπίς (21.50, 21.581; see the note on Book 20). In the river the stream falls on it: ὤθει δʼ ἐν σάκεϊ πίπτων ῥόος, “and the stream, falling on his shield, pushed him back” (21.241; see [[scamander]]).

**Agenor's shield.** “But he held before him his shield, balanced on every side” (21.581; see [[agenor]] and [[balanced-on-every-side]]), and took aim at Achilles with his spear.
