---
summary: Poseidon addresses Apollo as Phoebus twice in his challenge (21.436, 21.448); “Phoebus Apollo” goes into sacred Ilios to guard its wall (21.515), and sets Agenor on against Achilles (21.545).
---

The last two uses have the name in the reversed order, Ἀπόλλων Φοῖβος, as in Patroclus' assault on the wall, “had not Phoebus Apollo stood on the well-built tower” (16.700), and as here “had not Phoebus Apollo set brilliant Agenor on” (21.545; [[would-have-had-not]]). The scholion of “Ammonios” reads οἶος, “alone,” for Φοῖβος at 21.515, which Walter Leaf finds suits the context but takes to be a conjecture (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 21.515).
