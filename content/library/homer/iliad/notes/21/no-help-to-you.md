summary: The river tells Simoeis that Achilles' strength will not help him, nor his looks, nor his armor (21.316–317); and Achilles tells the dead Asteropaeus that the great river beside him cannot help against Zeus (21.192–193).
grc: [χραισμησέμεν]
en: [will help him]
---

“For I say that neither his strength will help him, nor his looks, / nor that fine armor” (21.316–317): the river's threat is the shape of Hector's to Paris, whose lyre, the gifts of Aphrodite, his hair and his looks would be no help to him in the dust (3.54–55). Achilles' taunt over Asteropaeus turns the same verb on the river: “For here beside you is a great river, if he can do anything / to help; but there is no fighting with Zeus, son of Cronus” (21.192–193; [[son-of-cronus]]).
