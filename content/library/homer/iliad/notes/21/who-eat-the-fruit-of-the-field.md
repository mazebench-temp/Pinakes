---
summary: Apollo: mortals are “wretched creatures, who, like leaves, at one time / blaze with life, eating the fruit of the field, / and at another waste away lifeless” (21.464–466).
grc: ["ἀρούρης καρπὸν ἔδοντες"]
en: [eating the fruit of the field]
---

The participle ἔδοντες, in place of ἔδουσιν, joins the epithet of mortals to Glaucus' image of the leaves (6.146); Walter Leaf finds the mixture of leaves that blaze and eat the fruit of the earth a medley (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 21.464; see the translation's note on 21.464–466).
