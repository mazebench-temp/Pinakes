---
summary: Asteropaeus cannot draw Achilles' spear “out of the bank with his strong hand” (21.175); Athena takes up a stone “in her strong hand” to throw at Ares (21.403), and strikes Aphrodite on the breast “with her strong hand” (21.424).
---

Athena's stone is described in the two verses of the stone that Hector threw at Ajax in Book 7 (21.403–404 = 7.264–265; Walter Leaf, *Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 21.403; [[rugged]], [[stone-throwing]]). Leaf reports from a scholion that some objected to παχείῃ of a goddess's hand and read βαρείῃ, or “Pallas Athena,” in its place; he points to the Odyssey, where Penelope takes the key “in her strong hand” (*Odyssey* 21.6), and to the Hymn to Apollo, where Hera strikes the earth with it (*Homeric Hymn to Apollo* 340).
