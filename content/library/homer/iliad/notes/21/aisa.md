---
summary: Achilles dates the end of his mercy: “for before Patroclus met his fated day” (21.100). Poseidon tells him in the river, “so it is not your fate to be brought down by a river” (21.291), and in the simile of Artemis' flight the dove that flies from the hawk into a cleft escapes, “nor was it her fate to be caught” (21.495).
---

αἴσιμον ἦμαρ, “the fated day”, is the day of death (see [[moira]]). The same adjective marks two escapes, Achilles' from the river and the dove's from the hawk (see [[the-dove-and-the-hawk]]).
