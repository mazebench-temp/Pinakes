---
summary: Antenor is named twice as the father of Agenor, whom Apollo sets against Achilles before the gates: “a man, the son of Antenor, noble and strong” (21.546), and, in the simile of the leopard, “the son of noble Antenor, brilliant Agenor” (21.579).
---

φῶτʼ Ἀντήνορος υἱὸν, “a man, the son of Antenor” (21.546; see [[son-of-antenor]]), and Ἀντήνορος υἱὸς ἀγαυοῦ, “the son of noble Antenor” (21.579; see [[noble]]), name Agenor at the two moments when he is set against Achilles: when Apollo puts daring into him and stands beside him, and when he waits for Achilles like a leopard for the hunter (21.544–549, 21.571–580; see [[agenor]] and [[the-leopard-and-the-hunter]]). In the last book Achilles killed Demoleon, the last of Antenor's sons to die in the poem (20.395–400; see [[demoleon]]). Agenor, whom Apollo snatches away from Achilles, survives (21.595–598; see [[apollo-deceives-achilles]]).
