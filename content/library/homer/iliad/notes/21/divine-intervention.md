summary: The gods act on both sides through the book. Hera spreads a mist before the fleeing Trojans (21.6–7); Xanthus puts might in Asteropaeus (21.145–146); Poseidon and Athena come to Achilles in the river, and Athena puts great strength in him (21.284–304); Hera sends Hephaestus' fire against the river (21.328–382); and Apollo sets Agenor on, saves him in thick mist, and takes his shape to draw Achilles away from the city (21.544–611).
refs: [21.6-7, 21.145-146, 21.284-304, 21.328-382, 21.515-517, 21.538-539, 21.544-611]
---

Each act of a god meets a man's act. Achilles, given strength, goes back into the flood, and the river cannot hold him (21.302–304); Agenor, given daring, still weighs flight before he stands (21.547–580). Walter Leaf notes that Hera's mist is not mentioned again, as so often with supernatural darkness, and that we are not told whom it is meant to check (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 21.6; see the translation's note on 21.6–7).
