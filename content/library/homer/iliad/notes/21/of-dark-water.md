---
summary: In the simile of the river's chase, Achilles is overtaken as a man is outrun by the water he leads “from a spring of dark water” to his garden (21.257).
---

The simile has no parallel elsewhere in Homer (see the translation's note on 21.257–262). The spring of dark water, a place of tears and of beasts drinking in its other uses (9.14, 16.3, 16.160–161), is here the source of a gardener's channel, and the water runs faster than the man who leads it (21.257–264; [[the-channel-and-the-gardener]], [[nimble]]). See [[scamander]].
