---
summary: Achilles once sold Lycaon, son of Priam, into Lemnos, “and the son of Jason paid the price”; a guest-friend ransomed him from there, and he came home from Lemnos only to meet Achilles again twelve days later (21.40–46). Achilles and Lycaon both name the island in their speeches (21.58, 21.79).

**A market for captives.** The island that sent wine to the Achaean camp in Book 7, and took slaves among its payments, also bought the captives of Achilles' raids (7.467–475; see [[euneus]] and [[trade]]). The buyer of Lycaon was the son of Jason, Euneus (21.41; see [[jason]]); the ransom that freed him was paid by Eetion of Imbros, the next island toward the Hellespont (21.42–43; see [[eetion-of-imbros]] and [[imbros]]); and in Book 24 Hecuba says that Achilles sold her other sons across the sea, to Samos, Imbros and Lemnos (24.751–753; see [[slavery-and-freedom]] and [[samothrace]]).

**Its epithets.** The book names the island four times (21.40, 21.46, 21.58, 21.79). The narrator calls it Λῆμνον ἐϋκτιμένην, “well-built Lemnos” (21.40; see [[well-built]]), as the Odyssey calls it “a well-built citadel” (*Odyssey* 8.283). In the speeches it is Λῆμνον ἐς ἠγαθέην, “into sacred Lemnos”, both in Achilles' wonder that the man he sold has come back, “though he was sold into sacred Lemnos” (21.58), and in Lycaon's plea, “into sacred Lemnos, and I fetched you the worth of a hundred oxen” (21.79; see [[egatheos]]); the same epithet is the Catalogue's, where the Achaeans left Philoctetes “in sacred Lemnos” (2.722). The sea did not hold him, says Achilles (21.58–59; see [[lycaon-son-of-priam]] and [[the-death-of-lycaon]]).
