---
summary: The narrator says it once, in the river: “for the gods are stronger than men” (21.264). The river complains that “the gods themselves are always there to defend you” (21.215) and that Achilles “is bent on being the equal of the gods” (21.315); Achilles stands against the wave to learn whether all “the immortals were putting him to flight” (21.266–267); and Hera stops the fire, since it is not fitting “to batter an immortal god like this for the sake of mortals” (21.380).
---

The narrator's maxim is Hector's in Book 20, “but with the spear it is hard, since they are far stronger than we are” (20.368; see [[gods-are-hard-to-face]] and [[gods-and-mortals]]). The river's charge recalls Apollo's warning to Diomedes not to think himself equal to the gods (5.440–442; Walter Leaf, *Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 21.315; see [[gods-wounded-by-mortals]]).
