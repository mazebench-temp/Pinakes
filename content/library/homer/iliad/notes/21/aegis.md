{{quote:a69e6485-396d-5e24-8dde-3c72f9a725b8}}

Athena wears the aegis in the battle of the gods as she put it on to go against Ares in Book 5 (5.738), and Ares' spear is the only weapon that strikes it in the poem (see [[ares]] and [[athena]]). θυσσανόεσσα, “tasseled”, is said only of the aegis (5.738, 15.229, 17.593, 18.204; see [[tasseled]]), from which a hundred golden tassels hang in Book 2 (2.448). For that reason Walter Leaf keeps αἰγίδα here, though most manuscripts read ἀσπίδα, “shield”; he reports the ancient remark that the clause about the thunderbolt is a strange one to use of the aegis, which the poet gives to Zeus as his own weapon (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 21.401; see the translation's note on 21.401 and [[thunderbolt]]). The blow does nothing; Athena gives ground only to pick up a stone (21.403; see [[boundary-stones]]). A few verses later Hera addresses her as “child of aegis-bearing Zeus” (21.420; see [[aegis-bearing]]).
