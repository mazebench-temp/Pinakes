summary: Lycaon's mother Laothoe bore him to be short-lived and will not lament him (21.84–85, 21.123–124); Achilles blames his own mother, “who bewitched me with lies” (21.276). Among the gods Hera sends her son Hephaestus against the river and calls him off, “Hephaestus, hold back, my famous child” (21.379), and Athena tells Ares that he pays “for the Erinyes of your mother” (21.412).
refs: [21.84-85, 21.109, 21.122-124, 21.275-278, 21.330-341, 21.377-380, 21.412-414]

Hera's appeal begins with her son's lameness, “Rise, crook-footed one, my child” (21.331), and the A scholia record an athetesis for it; Walter Leaf calls the line indispensable (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 21.331; see the translation's note on 21.331 and [[hephaestus]]). Achilles reminds Lycaon that “a goddess was the mother who bore me” (21.109; see [[meter]], [[thetis]] and [[laothoe]]).
