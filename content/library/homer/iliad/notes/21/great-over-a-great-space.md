---
summary: Leto gathers up Artemis' bow and the arrows, “fallen here and there in the whirl of dust” (21.503).
grc: ["μετὰ στροφάλιγγι κονίης"]
---

The phrase comes from the death of Cebriones, who “in a whirl of dust / lay, great over a great space” (16.775–776); Walter Leaf calls the borrowing tasteless, since here it can only mean that Artemis raised the dust as she fled (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 21.502; see the translation's note on 21.502–503 and [[curved-bow]]). See [[leto]] and [[artemis]].
