**By night.** Achilles came on Lycaon “coming out by night” (ἐννύχιος προμολών, 21.37), in Priam's own orchard, as he cut young fig shoots for the rails of a chariot (21.35–38; see [[lycaon-son-of-priam]]). The other sons of Priam whom he took alive, Isus and Antiphus, he caught herding sheep on the slopes of Ida, and released them for ransom (11.104–106; see [[isus]] and [[antiphus-son-of-priam]]); on Ida too he drove Aeneas from his cattle before he sacked Lyrnessus and Pedasus (20.89–92, 20.188–191; see [[the-sack-of-lyrnessus]]). Lycaon was not released for ransom but sold across the sea (21.40–41; see [[trade]] and [[lemnos]]).

**Sold or released.** “until then it was somehow dearer to my heart to spare / Trojans, and many I took alive and sold” (21.101–102): Achilles tells Lycaon what he did with his captives before Patroclus died, and that now he will spare none (21.103–105; see [[slavery-and-freedom]] and [[the-death-of-lycaon]]). Hecuba will name the islands to which he sold her sons, Samos, Imbros and Lemnos (24.751–753).
