summary: Six speeches open with winged words: Lycaon's plea (21.73), Achilles' boast over Lycaon (21.121), the river's plea to Hera (21.368), Athena's boast over Ares (21.409), Hera's call to Athena (21.419), and Athena's boast over Ares and Aphrodite (21.427).
grc: ["ἔπεα πτερόεντʼ ἀγόρευεν", "ἔπεα πτερόεντʼ ἀγόρευε"]
---

**Boasts.** Three of the six introduce a boast over the fallen: “and boasting over him he spoke winged words” (21.121), Athena's “and boasting over him she addressed him with winged words” (21.409), and “and she, boasting over them, spoke winged words” (21.427; [[euchomai]]). The god's boast and the hero's are told in the same words.

**Pleas.** Lycaon begins his plea “addressing him with winged words” (21.73); Didymus reports that the verse was not in Aristarchus' editions, but the manuscripts have it, and Walter Leaf finds a speech beginning without it hardly Homeric (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 21.73; see the translation's note on 21.73). The burning river begs Hera “earnestly, with winged words” (21.368). Hera's call to Athena is the couplet with which she sent her against Ares in Book 5 (21.419–420 = 5.713–714; [[o-popoi]]). The singular forms with ἀγόρευε(ν) are new to the entry's patterns. See [[lycaon-son-of-priam]], [[athena]] and [[the-battle-of-the-gods]].
