---
summary: Lycaon thinks that he “must be hateful to father Zeus, / who has given me to you again” (21.83–84); and Achilles, trapped in the river, cries out, “Father Zeus, how no one of the gods has undertaken to save me in my misery / from the river!” (21.273–274).
---

Achilles' cry is framed like Menelaus' complaint when his sword shattered on Paris' helmet: “And the son of Atreus groaned aloud, looking up into the wide sky: / ‘Father Zeus, no other god is more baneful than you’” (3.364–365), as here “And the son of Peleus groaned aloud, looking up into the wide sky” (21.272; [[wide-sky]]). The rest of the speech is Achilles' own: he blames his mother's prophecy, wishes Hector had killed him, and dreads a death like a swineherd boy's in a winter torrent (21.275–283; [[death-of-achilles-foretold]]).
