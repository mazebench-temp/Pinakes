---
summary: “So he spoke, and Leto gathered up the curved bow / and the arrows, fallen here and there in the whirl of dust” (21.502–503).
---

καμπύλα τόξα is the bow, but the arrows are what lie scattered: Walter Leaf calls it a purely mechanical use of a familiar standing epithet, and finds the words “in the whirl of dust” (21.503) borrowed from the death of Cebriones, who lay “in a whirl of dust” (16.775) (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 21.502; see the translation's note on 21.502–503 and [[great-over-a-great-space]]).
