---
summary: Agenor argues the case before Achilles: if he flees with the others, Achilles will catch him and “cut my throat, a coward” (21.555), and if he runs for Ida he may be seen and caught; so he stands, and “would not flee before he had made trial of Achilles” (21.580). Achilles himself runs from the river “in fear” (21.247–256), and Artemis taunts Apollo, who will not fight Poseidon, “So you flee, Far-Worker” (21.472).
refs: [21.247-256, 21.468-477, 21.497-501, 21.552-580, 21.606-611]
---

Hermes gives way to Leto without a blow, and bids her boast that she won (21.498–501). The Trojans' retreat into the city ends the book: they do not dare to wait for one another outside the wall (21.606–611; see [[pondering-two-ways]], [[phobos]] and [[apollo-deceives-achilles]]).
