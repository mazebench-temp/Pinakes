---
summary: Lycaon's mother bore him “to be short-lived” (21.84), and Achilles' mother was a goddess (21.109). Achilles tells the dead Lycaon that his mother will not “lay you on a bier and lament for you” (21.124), and blames his own, “my dear mother, who bewitched me with lies” (21.276). Athena tells Ares that he will pay “for the Erinyes of your mother” (21.412).
---

μινυνθάδιον, “short-lived”, is the word with which Achilles began his first appeal to his own mother (1.352): Lycaon says of himself what Achilles said of himself (see [[the-short-life-of-achilles]], [[mothers-and-sons]] and [[laothoe]]).
