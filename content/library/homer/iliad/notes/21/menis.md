---
summary: The word's one use in the book is in the simile of Achilles' advance on the city: “As when smoke goes up and reaches the wide sky / from a burning city, and the wrath of the gods has sent it up” (21.522–523).
---

Walter Leaf finds the adaptation of 18.207 unsuccessful, and offers two readings: θεῶν δέ ἑ μῆνις ἀνῆκε may be a parenthesis, so that the smoke itself brings toil and sorrow, or the wrath is the subject of the verbs that follow (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 21.522–523; see the translation's note on 21.522–525). Either way the burning city is one that the gods' wrath has struck, and the book has just heard Scamander swear that he will not save Troy even when it burns (21.373–376; see [[smoke-from-a-burning-city]] and [[cholos]]).
