summary: Achilles tells the dead Asteropaeus that “there is no fighting with Zeus, son of Cronus” (21.193); the river reproaches Achilles, “If the son of Cronus has given you all the Trojans to destroy” (21.216), and Apollo, who has not kept “the plans of the son of Cronus” (21.230); Zeus, “the son of Cronus,” takes the weeping Artemis on his knees (21.508); and Agenor knows that Zeus, son of Cronus, gives Achilles glory (21.570).
grc: [Κρονίωνος]
---

**The river's speeches.** The river names Zeus twice by his father: as the god who may have given Achilles the Trojans to destroy (21.216) and as the god whose plans Apollo has neglected (21.229–232; [[boule]]). The genitive Κρονίωνος, in the river's words and in Achilles' boast (21.184, 21.230), is new to the entry. Walter Leaf notes that the river's reproach to Apollo refers to no command of Zeus that we know, and defends it as an angry partisan's outburst (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 21.229; see the translation's note on 21.229–232).

**Agenor.** “but Zeus, son of Cronus, grants him glory” (21.570) is a verse of Book 8 (8.141). It was athetized in antiquity as added by someone who thought a verb was missing, and because it undoes Agenor's hope (Leaf on 21.570; see the translation's note on 21.568–570). See [[zeus]], [[eristhenes]] and [[artemis]].
