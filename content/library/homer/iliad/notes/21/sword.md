---
summary: Achilles leaps into the river “holding only his sword” and kills with it until his hands are weary (21.17–26). He kills Lycaon with the sword at the collarbone (21.116–118) and Asteropaeus “with his sword at close quarters” (21.173–179), and the Paeonians flee when they see their leader brought down “at the hands of the son of Peleus and by his sword” (21.208).
---

**Only his sword.** φάσγανον οἶον ἔχων, “holding only his sword” (21.19): Achilles leaves his spear on the bank (21.17–18; see [[the-pelian-ash]]), and in the river the killing is done with the sword. “and he struck, turning this way and that; and an ugly groaning rose / from the men struck by the sword, and the water grew red with blood” (21.20–21; see [[achilles]]). The verses are those of Diomedes killing the sleeping Thracians, with “killed” and “earth” (10.483–484; see the note on Book 10 and [[the-doloneia]]): the sword kills men who cannot fight back.

**Lycaon.** When Lycaon, his plea refused, sits down with his hands spread, “Achilles, drawing his sharp sword, / struck him on the collarbone beside the neck, and all the way in / the two-edged sword went” (21.116–118; see [[lycaon-son-of-priam]]). ἐρυσσάμενος ξίφος ὀξύ, “drawing his sharp sword”, ends the verse as when he sprang at Aeneas (20.284), and ἄμφηκες, “two-edged”, is said of a sword once more in the Iliad, of the one Thrasymedes gives Diomedes for the night raid (10.256; see [[two-edged]]).

**Asteropaeus.** When his spear has stuck in the bank, “the son of Peleus drew the sharp sword from beside his thigh” (21.173). ἐρυσσάμενος παρὰ μηροῦ, “drawing from beside his thigh”, is said in the Iliad only of Achilles, here and in the quarrel, when he pondered whether to draw his sword on Agamemnon (1.190); in the Odyssey it is Odysseus' verse, seven times (*Odyssey* 9.300, 10.126, 10.294, 10.321, 10.535, 11.24, 11.48). “Achilles took his life with his sword at close quarters” (21.179), as he would have taken Aeneas' had Poseidon not been quick to see (20.290; see [[asteropaeus]]). Seeing their leader “brought down by force at the hands of the son of Peleus and by his sword” (21.208), the Paeonians flee along the river (21.205–208; see [[paeonians]]).
