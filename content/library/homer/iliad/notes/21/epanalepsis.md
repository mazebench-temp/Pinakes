summary: Lycaon names his grandfather twice across the verse-end, “the old man Altes, / Altes, who rules over the war-loving Leleges” (21.85–86), and Asteropaeus his river, “the broad-flowing Axius, / the Axius, who sends the fairest water over the earth” (21.157–158).
refs: [21.86, 21.158]
---

These are the figure with a single name, not the repeated phrase of the entry's three examples. Walter Leaf calls Lycaon's verses an epanalepsis, and argues from it for the old genitive Ἄλταʼ (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 21.86; see the translation's note on 21.86–87). Andromache's father is named in the same way in Book 6, “great-hearted Eetion, / Eetion who lived beneath wooded Placus” (6.395–396), where Leaf also speaks of an epanalepsis (on 6.396). Asteropaeus' verses take up the Catalogue's own epanalepsis of the Axius (2.849–850), from which Leaf takes the second verse to be adapted (on 21.158).
