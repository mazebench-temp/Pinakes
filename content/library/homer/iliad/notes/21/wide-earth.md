---
summary: The gods fall on one another “with a great crash, and the wide earth roared, / and the great sky trumpeted around” (21.387–388).
---

The first half of the verse, σὺν δʼ ἔπεσον μεγάλῳ πατάγῳ, βράχε δʼ …, is the one with which the Trojans fell into the river at the start of the book, ἐν δʼ ἔπεσον μεγάλῳ πατάγῳ, βράχε δʼ αἰπὰ ῥέεθρα, “and they fell in with a great crash, and the sheer streams roared” (21.9; [[steep]]). There the river roared; here the earth roars under the gods, and the sky trumpets, a metaphor Walter Leaf compares with the battle of the Titans in Hesiod (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 21.388; see the translation's note on 21.388).
