---
summary: Lycaon tells Achilles that his mother's father, Altes, king of the Leleges, holds “steep Pedasus on the Satnioeis” (21.87).
---

Πήδασον αἰπήεσσαν ἔχων ἐπὶ Σατνιόεντι, “holding steep Pedasus on the Satnioeis” (21.87; see [[altes]] and [[leleges]]). αἰπήεις, “steep”, is found only here; Walter Leaf takes it as formed from αἰπός as μεσήεις from μέσος, and Book 6 calls the town αἰπεινή, with the same meaning (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 21.87; 6.35; see [[steep]]). ἐπί, “on”, is the reading of Strabo and of Leaf's text, where the manuscripts have ὑπό, “below”; Leaf calls ὑπό a corruption that has invaded all the manuscripts, since the Satnioeis is a river in its other two places (6.34, 14.445), and Strabo says that some write ὑπό wrongly, as if the town lay below a mountain (on 21.87; *Geography* 13.1.50; see the translation's note on 21.86–87 and [[satnioeis]]). The verb of the king is present, ἀνάσσει, “rules” (21.86): the Pedasus that Aeneas names with Lyrnessus among the towns Achilles sacked (20.92) still has its king, and the poem does not say how (see [[the-sack-of-lyrnessus]]). Lycaon's mother, Laothoe, came from there to Priam's house (21.85–88; see [[laothoe]] and [[lycaon-son-of-priam]]).
