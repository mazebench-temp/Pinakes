---
summary: Agenor's javelin rings on Achilles' greave “of newly wrought tin” (21.592), the half-verse that Aristotle quotes in the Poetics.
---

“And around him the greave of newly wrought tin / rang terribly” (21.592–593; see [[greaves]] and [[achilles]]). Hephaestus cast tin into the fire with bronze, gold and silver when he made the arms, and made the greaves “of soft tin” (18.474, 18.613; see [[heanos]] and [[the-making-of-the-arms]]). νεοτεύκτου, “newly wrought”, is found only here in Homer.

Aristotle quotes the half-verse among words used by the custom of the language: mixed wine is still called wine, and so the poet composed “a greave of newly wrought tin” (*Poetics* 25, 1461a27–28; see [[aristotle-wine-of-the-gods]] and [[aristotle]]).
