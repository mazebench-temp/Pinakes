---
summary: “swift Achilles would have killed still more of the Paeonians” had the river not spoken (21.211), and “Answering him, swift-footed Achilles said” (21.222), granting the river's plea and going on killing.
---

The first use has ὠκὺς Ἀχιλλεύς without πόδας, as in Book 19 (19.295). Both stand in the river's protest: the narrator's turn “would have … had not” brings the river in (21.211–212; [[would-have-had-not]]), and the reply formula brings in Achilles' answer, “So it shall be, Scamander, nurtured by Zeus, as you bid” (21.223; [[answering-him]], [[zeus-nurtured]]). See [[achilles]] and [[podarkes]].
