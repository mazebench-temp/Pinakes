---
summary: Achilles, leaping out of the river, darts away “to fly over the plain on his swift feet” (21.247); and Agenor fears that Achilles will “dart after me and catch me with his swift feet” (21.564).
grc: ["ταχέεσσι πόδεσσιν"]
---

The first phrase, ποσὶ κραιπνοῖσι, is the one of Paris hurrying through the city in Book 6 (6.505); here it is the start of the race against the river, in which the wave overtakes Achilles, “nimble though he was” (21.247–264; [[nimble]]). The second, ταχέεσσι πόδεσσιν, is the form with the final ν of the phrase of Book 20, where Achilles chased Aeneas down Ida “on swift feet” (20.189), and it is new to the entry. See [[achilles]], [[agenor]] and [[swift-footed]].
