---
summary: Eetion of Imbros, who ransomed Lycaon from Lemnos, sent him “to shining Arisbe”, and from there Lycaon escaped home to Priam's house (21.43–44).
---

πέμψεν δʼ ἐς δῖαν Ἀρίσβην, “and sent him to shining Arisbe” (21.43), with the town's epithet in the Catalogue, “and shining Arisbe” (2.836). It is a town on the Hellespont, as Walter Leaf notes (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 21.43), the home of Asius, whose horses brought him to Troy from there, and of Axylus, the host by the road whom Diomedes killed (2.836–839, 6.12–19; see [[asius]] and [[axylus]]). From there Lycaon “escaped and came to his father's house” (21.44); Leaf reads in ὑπεκπροφυγών, “escaped”, that he was meant to be kept in custody at Arisbe for his own safety (on 21.44; see [[eetion-of-imbros]] and [[lycaon-son-of-priam]]).
