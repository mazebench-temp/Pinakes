---
summary: Achilles and Agenor each speak “to his own proud heart” before they act (21.53, 21.552); Achilles imagines that “the great-hearted Trojans whom I have killed / will rise up again” (21.55–56); and Asteropaeus answers him, “Great-hearted son of Peleus, why do you ask about my lineage?” (21.153).
---

The first and last uses are the verse of the soliloquy, πρὸς ὃν μεγαλήτορα θυμόν ([[deeply-troubled]]). Asteropaeus' answer is Glaucus' to Diomedes with one name changed: “Great-hearted son of Tydeus, why do you ask about my lineage?” (6.145), as Walter Leaf notes, the dialogue being modeled on that meeting (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 21.153; [[genealogy-in-battle]], [[the-sons-of-unhappy-men]]).
