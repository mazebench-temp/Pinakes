summary: The river's might is the book's adversary. Xanthus puts might into Asteropaeus (21.145–146), “Nor did Scamander abate his fury” (21.305), Hera bids Hephaestus not “stop your fury” (21.340), and at last “the might of Xanthus had been overpowered” (21.383). Among the gods it is the measure of the duel: Athena tells Ares “you match your might with mine” (21.411), Hera says it to Artemis (21.488), and Aphrodite came “to meet my might” (21.431).
grc: [μενέαινε]
---

Achilles' verse to Asteropaeus, “It is the sons of unhappy men who meet my might” (21.151), is Diomedes' to Glaucus (6.127), and Athena turns its end on Aphrodite (ἐμῷ μένει ἀντιόωσα, 21.431; see [[the-sons-of-unhappy-men]]). The verb μενεαίνω marks Achilles' eagerness through the book: he springs at the Trojans “eager to cut men down” (21.33), at Asteropaeus “eager to kill him” (21.140, 21.170), and drives them to the city “eager to win glory” (21.543; see [[kydos]] and [[achilles-and-the-river]]).
