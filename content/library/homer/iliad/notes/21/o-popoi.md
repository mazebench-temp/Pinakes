---
summary: Achilles marvels at Lycaon's return, “Ah, truly this is a great wonder that I see with my eyes” (21.54); the river reproaches Apollo, “Ah, god of the silver bow, child of Zeus” (21.229); and Hera sends Athena after Aphrodite, “Ah, child of aegis-bearing Zeus, Atrytone” (21.420).
---

Achilles' verse is the one he spoke in Book 20 when Aeneas vanished (20.344), and Poseidon and Thoas before him (13.99, 15.286; Walter Leaf, *Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 21.54). Hera's is the verse with which she roused Athena against Ares in Book 5 (21.419–420 = 5.713–714; [[atrytone]], [[child-of-aegis-bearing-zeus]]). The river's is its own: the exclamation of dismay with the titles of Apollo (21.229–232; [[god-of-the-silver-bow]], [[son-of-zeus]]). See [[achilles]], [[scamander]] and [[hera]].
