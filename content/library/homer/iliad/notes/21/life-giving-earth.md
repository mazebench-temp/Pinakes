---
summary: Achilles will try whether Lycaon comes back from the dead as well, “or whether / the life-giving earth will hold him back, which holds back even the strong” (21.62–63).
grc: ["γῆ φυσίζοος"]
---

The phrase here is γῆ φυσίζοος, not the verse-end φυσίζοος αἶα of Book 3 and the Odyssey (3.243; *Odyssey* 11.301). Walter Leaf weighs Brandreth's reading γαῖα φυσίζωος, reports that the T scholia thought the epithet unfitting for the earth that holds the dead and their tombs, and gives Fick's derivation from ζειά, a kind of grain, which would make φυσίζοος mean the same as ζείδωρος, “grain-giving” (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 21.63; see the translation's note on 21.62–63). Achilles' earth holds back the strong, as the gray sea held back many (21.59; [[gray-sea]]). See [[lycaon-son-of-priam]] and [[earth]].
