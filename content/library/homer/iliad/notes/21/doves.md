summary: The dove of Book 21 is Artemis, who, beaten by Hera, flees in tears like a dove that flies from a hawk into a hollow rock, since it was not her fate to be caught (21.493–496).
# πέλεια, the nominative (21.493), only here in Homer.
grc: [πέλεια]
en: [dove]
---

ὥς τε πέλεια, / ἥ ῥά θʼ ὑπʼ ἴρηκος κοίλην εἰσέπτατο πέτρην, “like a dove / that flies from a hawk into a hollow rock” (21.493–494; see [[the-dove-and-the-hawk]] and [[hawk]]). The dove is the hawk's prey here as everywhere in Homer, and it finds its refuge where the rock dove nests, in a cleft of the rock. The goddess whom Hera has just told to hunt wild beasts on the mountains is herself the hunted bird (21.485–486; see [[artemis]] and [[hera]]). Goddesses are like doves once before in the poem, when Hera and Athena go to the battle on foot “like timid doves in their step” (5.778; see [[timid-doves]]).
