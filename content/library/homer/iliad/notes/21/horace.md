---
summary: Horace's Juno dates the ruin of Troy from the day when Laomedon cheated the gods of their agreed wage, and makes Phoebus the builder of its bronze wall.
---

- **Laomedon's wage.** In the third ode of his third book Juno tells the gods in council that Ilion was doomed to dust from the time when Laomedon cheated the gods of their agreed wage (*ex quo destituit deos / mercede pacta Laomedon*, *Odes* 3.3.21–22), and that if its bronze wall rose three times with Phoebus as its builder, three times her Argives would destroy it (3.3.65–68). Walter Leaf quotes the verses on Poseidon's complaint, “then he robbed us by force of our whole wage” (21.451; *Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 21.451). In this book Poseidon built the wall and Apollo herded Laomedon's cattle (21.446–449); in Book 7 Poseidon says that he and Phoebus Apollo built it together (7.452–453): [[poseidon-and-apollo-serve-laomedon]], [[laomedon]] and [[the-walls-of-troy]].
