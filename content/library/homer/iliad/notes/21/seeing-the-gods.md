summary: The river speaks to Achilles “in the likeness of a man” (21.213); Poseidon and Athena come to him “in form like men” and name themselves (21.285–290); Apollo stands beside Agenor hidden “in thick mist” (21.549), and then before Achilles, “in the likeness of Agenor himself in every way” (21.600).
refs: [21.212-213, 21.284-290, 21.549, 21.597-605]
---

Poseidon's naming of himself was athetized, as a god in a man's shape should not name himself; Walter Leaf defends it, and explains the disguise by Hera's words in Book 20, that gods are hard to face in plain sight (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 21.290; 20.131; see [[gods-are-hard-to-face]]). Ares remembers that Athena took the spear “seen by all” against him, where in Book 5 she had made herself invisible (21.397; see [[gods-wounded-by-mortals]]). The narrator shows the listener a god's true size: Ares falls and covers “seven plethra” (21.407), and Leaf compares Tityos, who covers nine (*Odyssey* 11.577; on 21.407). Achilles learns whom he has been chasing only when Apollo tells him (22.8–10; see [[apollo-deceives-achilles]]).
