summary: The gods make war a question. Apollo will not fight Poseidon “for the sake of mortals”, and says, “let them fight it out themselves” (21.463–467); Artemis taunts him for once boasting “that you would fight face to face against Poseidon” (21.477); and Hera tells her, “if you wish to learn of war”, before she beats her (21.487). Agenor calls Achilles “terrible and bold warrior though you are” (21.589), and the Trojans in the city do not wait to learn “who had died in the war” (21.610).
grc: [πτολεμίξω, πολεμιστής]
---

Poseidon calls the war ὁμοίϊος, “common to all” (21.294). Apollo's αὐτοί, “themselves”, leaves the men to fight without the gods' interference (Walter Leaf, *Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 21.467; see [[gods-and-mortals]] and [[the-battle-of-the-gods]]).
