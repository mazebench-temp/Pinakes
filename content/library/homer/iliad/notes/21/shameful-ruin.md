---
summary: The river ponders how to stop Achilles “and ward off ruin from the Trojans” (21.138, 21.250); and Apollo leaps out of the open gates “to ward off ruin from the Trojans” (21.539).
grc: ["λοιγὸν ἀλάλκοι"]
en: [ward off ruin]
---

The river's verse comes twice, as he first plans and then rises against Achilles (21.138 = 21.250; [[brilliant]]). What the river cannot do Apollo does at the gates, and the construction with ἀλάλκοι and the genitive Τρώων is discussed by Walter Leaf, who keeps the reading (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 21.539). See [[scamander]], [[apollo]] and [[the-walls-of-troy]].
