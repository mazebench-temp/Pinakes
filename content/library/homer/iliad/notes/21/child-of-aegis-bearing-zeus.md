---
summary: Hera to Athena, as Aphrodite leads Ares from the field: “Ah, child of aegis-bearing Zeus, Atrytone, / there again that dog-fly is leading Ares, ruin of mortals” (21.420–421).
---

The first verse repeats Hera's call to Athena in Book 5 (21.420 = 5.714; [[o-popoi]], [[atrytone]]). The river's address to Apollo a little earlier, “god of the silver bow, child of Zeus” (21.229), is the one use of Διὸς τέκος in Homer for a god other than Athena ([[son-of-zeus]]). See [[athena]], [[ruin-of-mortals]] and [[dog-fly]].
