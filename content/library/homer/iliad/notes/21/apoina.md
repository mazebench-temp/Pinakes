summary: Achilles refuses Lycaon's offer: “Fool, do not offer me ransom, nor make speeches” (21.99). Lycaon had been ransomed once, by a guest-friend who “ransomed him, and paid much” (21.42), and he tells Achilles what he is worth: when Achilles sold him he fetched “the worth of a hundred oxen”, and “now I have been ransomed, paying three times as much” (21.79–80).
grc: ["μιν ἐλύσατο", λύμην]
en: [ransomed]
---

Before Patroclus died, Achilles took Trojans alive and sold them (21.101–102); now he takes twelve alive only to kill them at the pyre (21.27–28; see [[poine]] and [[human-sacrifice]]). Walter Leaf takes πιφαύσκεο as “tender” (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 21.99), and explains Lycaon's figures: the hundred oxen are the value of the silver bowl that Euneus paid for him (23.741–747), and three times as much is what Eetion of Imbros paid to free him (on 21.79–80; see [[euneus]], [[eetion-of-imbros]] and [[trade]]).
