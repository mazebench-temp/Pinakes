---
summary: The Trojans are “overbearing” three times, in the mouths of their enemies: Achilles will not stop slaying them (21.224), Athena accuses Ares of helping them (21.414), and Poseidon wants Apollo to help bring it about that they perish (21.459).
grc: [ὑπερφιάλους, ὑπερφιάλοισιν]
---

The epithet of blame is the word of the Trojans' enemies, gods and men alike: Achilles answering the river (21.224), Athena boasting over Ares, “you deserted, and give help to the overbearing Trojans” (21.414), and Poseidon reproaching Apollo, “to bring it about that the overbearing Trojans perish / utterly and wretchedly, with their children and their modest wives” (21.459–460). The forms ὑπερφιάλους and ὑπερφιάλοισιν are new to the entry. See [[trojans]] and [[the-battle-of-the-gods]].
