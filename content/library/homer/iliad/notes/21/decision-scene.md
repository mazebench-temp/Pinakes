---
summary: Agenor, waiting for Achilles under the wall, weighs his courses aloud and chooses to stand (21.552–572).
passages:
  - 21.552-572 | Agenor's choice
---

The speech has three conditions, each a course: to flee with the others, and be caught and killed as a coward (21.553–555); to flee by another way across the plain to Ida and come back in the evening (21.556–561); or to go out and meet Achilles before the city (21.567–570). The second is broken off with the verse of Odysseus' and Hector's debates, “But why does my heart debate these things with me?” (21.562 = 11.407 = 22.122), and neither the second nor the third has an apodosis, as Walter Leaf notes (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 21.552, 21.556, 21.567). The simile of the leopard shows the choice made (21.571–580). See [[pondering-two-ways]], [[deeply-troubled]] and [[agenor]].
