---
summary: Lycaon lies face down, “and the dark blood flowed and soaked the earth” (21.119); and Asteropaeus' second spear grazes Achilles' right forearm, “and the dark blood spurted” (21.167).
---

The first verse is Harpalion's death in Book 13 (21.119 = 13.655; Walter Leaf, *Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 21.115). The second blood is Achilles' own: Asteropaeus, throwing with both hands, hits his shield with one spear and grazes his arm with the other (21.162–168; see the translation's note on 21.166–167 and [[ambidextrous]]). κελαινεφές at 21.167 is blood, not Zeus, and [[lord-of-dark-clouds]] excepts the line. See [[achilles]].
