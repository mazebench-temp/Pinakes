---
summary: Agenor measures Achilles by the word before he faces him: “If I flee before mighty Achilles” (21.553), and “for he is far too strong, beyond all men” (21.566). The earth “holds back even the strong” (21.63), Patroclus was “gentle and strong” (21.96), and “a mighty frenzy” held Achilles' heart as he drove the Trojans to the city (21.542–543).
grc: [κρατερῆφι]
---

Agenor himself is “noble and strong” (21.546) when Apollo sets him on, and Hermes tells Leto to boast that she beat him “by your mighty strength” (21.501; see [[euchomai]]). The river tells Achilles the same thing with the verb, “Achilles, beyond all men you are strong” (κρατέεις, 21.214; see [[kratos]]), and Lycaon's verse for Patroclus is nearly the one Zeus spoke of him in Book 17 (17.204; see [[eneeie]]).
