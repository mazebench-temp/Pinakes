---
summary: Asteropaeus tries to draw Achilles' spear out of the bank: “Three times he shook it, eager to draw it out, / and three times he held back from the effort; and the fourth time he wished in his heart / to bend and break the ash spear,” but Achilles killed him first (21.176–179).
grc: ["τρὶς μέν μιν πελέμιξεν"]
en: [Three times he shook it]
---

Here the pattern is turned to a mortal's failure. It is not a god who cuts off the fourth attempt, as with Diomedes, Patroclus and Achilles himself (5.436–442, 16.702–706, 20.445–448), but Achilles' sword. The first verse and half of the second come again in the Odyssey, where Telemachus three times shakes his father's bow, eager to string it, and three times holds back from the effort; the fourth time he would have strung it, had not Odysseus checked him with a nod (*Odyssey* 21.125–129). Bekker restored βίης, “from the effort,” here from that passage, and Walter Leaf follows him (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 21.177; see the translation's notes on 21.176 and 21.177).
