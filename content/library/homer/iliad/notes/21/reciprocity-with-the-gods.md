summary: Achilles tells the Trojans that their river will not protect them, though they have long sacrificed bulls to it and thrown live horses into its eddies: “But even so you will perish by an evil fate” (21.130–133). Poseidon reminds Apollo that Laomedon cheated them of the wage they earned, and reproaches him for doing a favor to Laomedon's people (21.441–460).
refs: [21.130-135, 21.441-460]
---

Walter Leaf compares the bull that Nestor sacrificed to the Alpheus (11.728), and notes that the sacrifice of live horses has no parallel in Homer: Achilles perhaps mentions it in contempt, as a barbarous custom (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 21.131; see the translation's note on 21.130–135 and [[rivers]]). The gods' service to a mortal was a bargain, “for a fixed wage” (21.445), and the mortal broke it (see [[charis]], [[poseidon-and-apollo-serve-laomedon]] and [[laomedon]]).
