---
summary: Herodotus' Magi slaughter white horses at the river Strymon, which Walter Leaf sets beside the horses that the Trojans throw alive into the Scamander, and Cyrus punishes a river that carried off one of his white horses. Herodotus' Paeonians of the Strymon call themselves colonists of the Teucrians from Troy, and Xerxes' army drinks the Scamander dry.
---

- The Scythians' sacrifice of horses, the white horses slaughtered at the Strymon, and Cyrus and the Gyndes (*Histories* 4.61.2, 7.113–114, 1.189–190, on 21.130–132): [[horses-for-the-rivers]].
- **The Paeonians.** The Paeonians whom Achilles kills by the river came, Asteropaeus says, from deep-soiled Paeonia far away (21.154–155, 21.205–211). Herodotus' Paeonians live on the river Strymon, and they told Darius that they were colonists of the Teucrians from Troy (*Histories* 5.13.2); Xerxes' army passed the Paeonians who live north of Mount Pangaeum on its way to the Strymon (7.113.1): [[paeonians]] and [[paeonia]].
- **The Scamander drunk dry.** The river that nearly drowns Achilles in this book was, Herodotus says, the first river on Xerxes' march from Sardis whose stream gave out, not enough for the army and its beasts to drink; Xerxes went up to Priam's Pergamum, and sacrificed a thousand cattle to Athena of Ilium (*Histories* 7.43.1–2): [[scamander]] and [[xerxes]].
