---
summary: The river speaks to Achilles “in the likeness of a man” (21.213), and tells the Simoeis that “neither his strength will help him, nor his looks” (21.316).
---

The river's threat is Hector's to Paris, with the same verb: “Your lyre would be no help to you then, nor the gifts of Aphrodite, / your hair and your looks” (3.54–55). The other gods of the book take human shape with another verb, ἔοικα: Poseidon and Athena come “in form like men” (21.285), and Apollo stands before Achilles “in the likeness of Agenor himself in every way” (21.600; see [[seeing-the-gods]] and [[apollo-deceives-achilles]]).
