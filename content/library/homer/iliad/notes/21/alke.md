---
summary: When Priam sees Achilles from the tower, the Trojans are driven in confusion “and no courage / arose in them” (21.528–529). Agenor fears that if he runs Achilles will “cut my throat, a coward” (21.555), and the leopard of his simile, pierced through, “does not give up / her courage” (21.577–578).
---

ἀνάλκιδα, “a coward”, keeps all the contempt of the word, as when Diomedes scorns Paris' arrow as “the shaft of a weakling, a nobody” (11.390); it does not mean helpless or unarmed (Walter Leaf, *Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 21.555; see the translation's note on 21.555). Agenor chooses the leopard's courage: his “valiant heart” (ἄλκιμον ἦτορ) was eager to fight (21.571–572; see [[etor]] and [[the-leopard-and-the-hunter]]).
