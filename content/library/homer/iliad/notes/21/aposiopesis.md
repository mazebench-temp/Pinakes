summary: Hera to Artemis: “But if you wish to learn of war, so that you may know well / how much stronger I am, since you match your might with mine…” (21.487–488), and she does not finish; she seizes Artemis' hands.
passages:
  - 21.487-489 | Hera breaks off
refs: [21.488]
---

The conditional has no main clause, and the deed supplies it: “She spoke, and caught both her hands at the wrist” (21.489). Walter Leaf reports Nicanor's explanation, that the poet left the speech unfinished on purpose and let the goddess fill out what was lacking with her deeds (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 21.487; see the translation's note on 21.487–489, where the English marks the break). The words of the condition are those with which Glaucus and Aeneas begin their genealogies, “But if you wish to learn …, so that you may know well” (6.150, 20.213), turned from lineage to war ([[genealogy-in-battle]]).
