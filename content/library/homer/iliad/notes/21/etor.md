summary: Zeus watches the gods clash, “and his heart within him laughed / for joy” (21.389–390), and Agenor, resolved to stand, “his valiant heart / within him was eager to make war and to fight” (21.571–572). The verse “knees and dear heart were loosened” is said of Lycaon when Achilles refuses him and of Aphrodite when Athena strikes her (21.114, 21.425), and Achilles leaves Asteropaeus in the sand, “since he had robbed him of his dear life” (21.201).
---

Agenor's ἄλκιμον ἦτορ is the heart of the lion in Achilles' simile in Book 20 (20.169), and the leopard follows at once (21.573–578; see [[alke]] and [[the-leopard-and-the-hunter]]). The verse of the loosened knees and heart is found seven times in the Odyssey and twice in this book, kept apart from “loosened his limbs” (see the translation's row for λύτο γούνατα καὶ φίλον ἦτορ, [[gounata]] and [[loosened-his-limbs]]).
