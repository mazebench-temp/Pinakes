---
summary: Artemis, beaten by Hera, runs to her father: “the maiden sat in tears on her father's knees”, and “her father, / the son of Cronus, drew her to him, and laughing heartily asked her” which god had done it (21.506–508). Laothoe is the “daughter of the old man Altes” (21.85), and Periboea the eldest of the daughters of Acessamenus (21.142–143).
refs: [21.85-88, 21.142-143, 21.504-513]
---

Zeus' question is Dione's to Aphrodite in Book 5, a mother's to her daughter (21.509–510 = 5.373–374), and Walter Leaf notes that 21.510 is omitted here by almost all the manuscripts (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 21.509; see [[the-gods-mirror-the-mortals]] and [[tears]]). Altes gave much with his daughter, Priam will say when he thinks Lycaon and Polydorus may still be ransomed (22.50–51; see [[altes]], [[laothoe]], [[periboea]] and [[acessamenus]]).
