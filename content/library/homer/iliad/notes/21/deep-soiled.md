---
summary: Asteropaeus comes “from deep-soiled Paeonia, which lies far away” (21.154); and the river reminds Apollo of Zeus' command to defend the Trojans until evening “darkens the deep-soiled earth” (21.232).
grc: [ἐριβώλου]
---

The genitive ἐριβώλου is new to the entry. The river's verse, δείελος ὀψὲ δύων, σκιάσῃ δʼ ἐρίβωλον ἄρουραν, sets the evening, perhaps the evening star or the setting sun, against the fertile land (Walter Leaf, *Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 21.232; see the translation's note on 21.229–232). See [[paeonia]] and [[asteropaeus]].
