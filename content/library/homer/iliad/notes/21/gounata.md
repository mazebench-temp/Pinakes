summary: Lycaon clasps Achilles' knees to beg (21.65–71), and when Achilles refuses, “the other's knees and dear heart were loosened where he was” (21.114). The river attacks Achilles at the knees, “and the river was overpowering his knees beneath him” (21.270), until with Athena's strength “his knees leapt high” against the stream (21.302). Artemis sits “on her father's knees” (21.506), and the book ends with the Trojans whose “feet and knees had saved them” (21.611).
