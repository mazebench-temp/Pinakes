---
summary: Achilles binds the hands of the twelve young men “behind them with well-cut straps, / which they themselves used to wear on their plaited tunics” (21.30–31).
---

The straps are the captives' own belts: Walter Leaf takes the ἱμάντες as the leather belts that girded their tunics, and notes that the young men wore no corselets (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 21.31; see the translation's note on 21.31). The same verse-end, ἐϋτμήτοισιν ἱμᾶσι, tied the horses of Rhesus at Diomedes' manger (10.567). See [[human-sacrifice]] and [[son-of-menoetius]].
