summary: Achilles' answer to Lycaon turns on the word: before Patroclus died “it was somehow dearer to my heart to spare / Trojans” (21.101–102), but now “No, friend, you too must die” (21.106). Scamander calls the Simoeis “Dear brother” (21.308), Hera speaks to “her dear son” (21.330, 21.378), and Zeus asks Artemis, “Who of the heavenly gods has done such things to you, dear child” (21.509).
grc: [φίλτερον]
---

The scholia heard mockery in φίλος, as if Achilles answered Lycaon's claim to have eaten at his table; Walter Leaf hears a sudden change to real pity, “a far finer touch” (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 21.106; see the translation's note on 21.106–107 and [[pity]]). Leaf adds that τι, “somehow”, gives a colour of reluctance to Achilles' admission at 21.101 (on 21.101). Lycaon had spent eleven days at home with his friends (21.45), and Agenor will fight “in front of our dear parents and wives and sons” (21.587; see [[patroclus-died-too]]).
