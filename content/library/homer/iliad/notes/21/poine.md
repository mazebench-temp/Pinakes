---
summary: Achilles takes twelve young men alive from the river “as the penalty for Patroclus, son of Menoetius, who had died” (21.28), and tells the Trojans that they will die “until all of you / pay for the slaughter of Patroclus and the ruin of the Achaeans” (21.133–134). Among the gods Ares threatens Athena, “So now I think you will pay for all that you have done” (21.399), and Athena, standing over him, answers, “So you may pay in full for the Erinyes of your mother” (21.412).
grc: [τίσετε, ἀποτισέμεν, ἐξαποτίνοις]
---

The penalty is paid in lives. The twelve were promised at the end of Book 18, “before your pyre I will cut the throats of twelve” (18.336), and they die at the pyre in Book 23 (23.175–176; see [[human-sacrifice]]). Walter Leaf renders ποινή here “blood-price”, as in the quarrel on the shield (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 21.28; 18.498; see the translation's note on 21.28). Ares' threat is answered at once by his fall, and the debt Athena names is to his mother, who curses him for deserting the Achaeans (21.412–414); Leaf takes these Erinyes as hardly more than her curses (on 21.412; see [[erinyes]] and [[hera]]).
