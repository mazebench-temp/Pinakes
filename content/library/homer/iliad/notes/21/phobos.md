---
summary: Flight fills the book from its first lines, where the Trojans flee across the plain “where the Achaeans had been fleeing in terror / the day before” (21.4–5) and pour forward “in panic” (21.6). Achilles himself, as often as he stood against the river, tried to learn whether all “the immortals were putting him to flight” (21.267); at the end the Trojans come “in their rout” into the city (21.606), while Agenor's leopard “neither fears nor flees” (21.575).
refs: [21.3-11, 21.206, 21.247-256, 21.265-267, 21.528-536, 21.553-566, 21.575, 21.606-611]
---

πεφυζότες, “in panic”, the perfect participle of the word for panic flight, is found only in this book (21.6, 21.528, 21.532) and at 22.1 (Walter Leaf, *Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 21.6; see the translation's note on 21.6–7). Achilles flees the river “in fear” (21.248; see [[deos]]), and Artemis taunts Apollo, “So you flee, Far-Worker” (21.472; see [[retreat]]).
