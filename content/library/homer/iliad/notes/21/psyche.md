---
summary: Agenor, deciding to face Achilles, reasons that “his flesh too can be pierced by the sharp bronze, / and there is one life in him, and men say that he / is mortal” (21.568–570).
---

Walter Leaf notes that the invulnerability of Achilles is a purely post-Homeric legend (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 21.568; see the translation's note on 21.568–570). Achilles had said in Book 9 that a man's life cannot be won back once it has passed the barrier of his teeth (9.408–409; see [[the-short-life-of-achilles]] and [[thanatos]]).
