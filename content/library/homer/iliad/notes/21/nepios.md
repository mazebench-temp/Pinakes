summary: Achilles answers Lycaon's plea with “Fool” (21.99). The gods call one another “Little fool”: Athena to the fallen Ares (21.410), Poseidon to Apollo, “Little fool, how senseless is the heart you have!” (21.441), and Artemis to Apollo, “Little fool, why do you carry a bow as empty as the wind?” (21.474). Agenor turns it on Achilles: “you little fool; truly many sorrows will yet be wrought over it” (21.585).
grc: [νηπύτιʼ, νηπύτιε]
---

νηπύτιε as an address is peculiar to this book (Walter Leaf, *Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 21.410; see the translation's note on 21.410). In Book 20 Aeneas and Hector used it of the child they would not be taken for, “as if I were a child” (20.200, 20.431); here it is an insult among equals, said to one who has not understood his own weakness or remembered his own wrongs (21.410–411, 21.441–442). The narrator does not use the word in the book (see [[childhood]] and [[the-gods-mirror-the-mortals]]).
