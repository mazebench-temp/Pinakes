# Ἴμβριος, "of Imbros" (21.43), the adjective of the island, is found only here; the man Imbrius is Ἴμβριον
# (13.171, 13.197).
grc: [Ἴμβριος]
---

Ἴμβριος Ἠετίων, “Eetion of Imbros” (21.43): the adjective tells this Eetion from the father of Andromache, as Walter Leaf notes (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 21.43; see [[eetion-of-imbros]] and [[eetion]]). Imbros lies between Lemnos and the mouth of the Hellespont, and the ransomed Lycaon was sent on to Arisbe, a town on the Hellespont, from where he escaped home (21.42–44; see [[lemnos]] and [[arisbe]]). The Trojan Imbrius, whom Teucer killed, bears the same adjective as his name (13.170–181; see [[imbrius]]). In the last book Hecuba names Imbros, with Samos and Lemnos, among the islands across the sea where Achilles sold her sons (24.751–753; see [[slavery-and-freedom]]).
