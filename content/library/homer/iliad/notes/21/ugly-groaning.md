---
summary: Achilles leaps into the river with his sword and strikes on every side: “and an ugly groaning rose / from the men struck by the sword, and the water grew red with blood” (21.20–21).
---

The two verses are those of Diomedes among the sleeping Thracians, “and an ugly groaning rose” (10.483–484), with “struck” (τύπτε) for “killed” and the water of the river for the earth (see the translation's note on 21.20–21; Walter Leaf, *Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 21.20). The night raid's slaughter of men who cannot fight back is repeated by day, in the river. See [[achilles]], [[sword]] and [[the-aristeia]].
