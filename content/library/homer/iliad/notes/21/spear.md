summary: Achilles leaves his spear on the bank and kills in the river with the sword (21.17–21). The spears of the book are thrown: Achilles' passes over Lycaon's back and stands in the ground (21.67–70), Asteropaeus throws two at once, one from each hand (21.162–168), Achilles' ash sticks in the bank (21.169–178), and Agenor's javelin rings on Achilles' greave (21.590–594). Among the gods, Ares stabs at Athena's aegis with his long spear and reminds her of the spear she drove into him with Diomedes (21.392–402).
# δούρασιν, Asteropaeus' two spears thrown at once (21.162), is found only here in Homer.
grc: [δούρασιν]
---

**Thrown.** Achilles lifts his spear “eager to stab” Lycaon, οὐτάμεναι, but Lycaon “ran in under it”, and the spear passes over his back into the ground (21.67–70). Walter Leaf takes these words to show that the spear is cast, in spite of Aristarchus' rule that οὐτάω is used only of thrusting (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 21.68; see the translation's note on 21.67–70 and [[the-pelian-ash]]). Spears that miss stand in the ground as if alive, “straining to glut itself on the flesh of a man” (21.70) and “longing to glut itself on flesh” (21.168), as at 11.574 and 15.317; Leaf notes the personification (on 21.70).

**Two at once.** Asteropaeus throws “with two spears at once, one from each hand”, “for he could use either hand” (21.162–163): περιδέξιος, “ambidextrous”, is found only here (Leaf on 21.163; see the translation's note on 21.162–163). One spear strikes Achilles' shield and is held by the gold, the other grazes his forearm and is fixed in the ground (21.164–168; see [[the-shield-of-achilles]] and [[asteropaeus]]). He leads the Paeonians “with their long spears” (21.155), though in the Catalogue they are archers (2.848; see [[paeonians]]), and his father Pelegon was “famed for his spear” (21.159).

**Other spears.** Achilles struck down Polydorus “with your sharp spear”, Lycaon reminds him (21.91; see [[polydorus-son-of-priam]]), and he himself will die, he tells Lycaon, “either with a spear or with an arrow from the string” (21.113; see [[death-of-achilles-foretold]]). Fleeing the river, he springs away “as far as the cast of a spear” (21.251; see [[the-cast-of-a-spear]]). Among the gods Ares comes at Athena “holding his bronze spear” and stabs at her aegis “with his long spear” (21.393, 21.402; see [[ares]] and [[aegis]]), reminding her that she once took “the spear seen by all” and drove it into him with Diomedes (21.396–398; 5.855–859; see [[diomedes-wounds-ares]]). The leopard of the simile, pierced with a spear, does not give up her courage (21.577), and Agenor, who stands against Achilles like her, takes aim with his spear and hurls “the sharp javelin” (21.582, 21.590; see [[agenor]] and [[greaves]]).
