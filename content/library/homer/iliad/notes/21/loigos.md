summary: The river and Apollo work for the same end, to “ward off ruin from the Trojans”: twice Scamander ponders how to stop Achilles for it (21.138, 21.250), and Apollo leaps out in front of the gates for it (21.539). Achilles promises the Trojans death until they pay for “the ruin of the Achaeans” (21.134), and Priam, seeing him come, says, “now I think there will be ruin” (21.533).
grc: [λοίγιʼ]
---

The half-verse ends the river's thought twice and Apollo's act once. Priam's λοίγια is the word of Zeus' complaint in Book 1, “a sorry business” (1.518; Walter Leaf, *Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 21.533; see [[scamander]] and [[apollo]]).
