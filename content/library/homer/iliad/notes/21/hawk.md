---
summary: The hawk of Book 21 is the bird of prey from which Artemis, beaten by Hera, flees like a dove into a hollow rock (21.493–494).
---

ἥ ῥά θʼ ὑπʼ ἴρηκος κοίλην εἰσέπτατο πέτρην, “that flies from a hawk into a hollow rock” (21.494; see [[the-dove-and-the-hawk]] and [[doves]]). The hawk answers to Hera, who has seized Artemis by the wrists and beaten her with her own bow (21.489–492; see [[hera]] and [[artemis]]). In Book 15 the hawk was Apollo going down from Ida, “a swift dove-killer, which is the swiftest of winged birds” (15.238; see [[like-a-hawk]]); in this book the swiftest of birds is the eagle, whose swoop Achilles has as he springs away from the river, “at once the strongest and the swiftest of birds” (21.252–253; see [[eagle]]).
