summary: The gold of Achilles' shield holds back Asteropaeus' spear, in the verse said of Aeneas' (21.165 = 20.268). The river of Troy is “silver-eddying” (21.8, 21.130), and the river calls Apollo “god of the silver bow” (21.229).
# ἀργυροδίνης "silver-eddying", of the Xanthus, as ἀργυροδίνῃ of the Peneius (2.753, in the Book 2 note): only these three lines in Homer.
grc: [ἀργυροδίνην, ἀργυροδίνης]
---

**The gold of the shield.** Asteropaeus' spear did not break the shield, “for the gold held it back, the gifts of the god” (21.165; see [[the-shield-of-achilles]] and [[asteropaeus]]). Walter Leaf takes the gold, the most precious part, to stand for the whole metal facing of the shield, and argues from this repetition of 20.268 that the verse needs no expansion by the four verses of Book 20 that make the layers metals, with the gold hidden in the middle (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 20.268, 20.269; see the note on Book 20). Agenor's javelin is held back at the greave by “the gifts of the god” in nearly the same words, without the gold (21.594; see [[greaves]] and [[tin]]).

**Silver.** ἀργυροδίνης, “silver-eddying”, is said of the Peneius in the Catalogue (2.753) and twice in this book of the Xanthus, “the deep-streaming, silver-eddying river” into which the Trojans are penned (21.8) and the river “fair-flowing and silver-eddying though it is” that will not protect them (21.130; see [[silver-eddying]] and [[scamander]]). The river addresses Apollo as ἀργυρότοξε, “god of the silver bow” (21.229; see [[god-of-the-silver-bow]] and [[apollo]]).
