summary: In the battle of the gods Hermes, “the guide, the slayer of Argus”, declines to fight Leto, against whom he was set: it is a hard thing to trade blows with the wives of Zeus, and she may boast among the immortals that she beat him.
# Unnamed: his words to Leto (21.498-501).
refs: [21.498-501]
---

“And the guide, the slayer of Argus, spoke to Leto” (21.497; see [[guide]], [[argeiphontes]] and [[leto]]). Set against her in Book 20 as “strong Hermes, the great helper” (20.72), he is the second god in the battle to refuse a fight, after Apollo (21.461–469; see [[apollo]] and [[the-battle-of-the-gods]]):

{{quote:b4678b65-8e9f-55ee-82cf-884cd13a304c}}

πληκτίζεσθαι, “to trade blows” (21.499), is found only here in Homer; Walter Leaf renders it “to bandy blows”, and takes the plural ἀλόχοισι, “wives”, as a general truth that also glances at Hera's triumph over Artemis just before (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 21.499; see [[zeus]] and [[hera]]). πρόφρασσα, “with a ready heart” (21.500), is the word with which Diomedes, praying to Athena in Book 10, remembers how she stood by his father (10.290; see the translation's row on πρόφρων / πρόφρασσα). εὔχεσθαι, “boast” (21.501), is a boast, not a prayer (see [[euchomai]]). Apollo, on the Trojan side, yielded Poseidon the victory out of contempt for mortals (21.462–467); Hermes, on the Achaean side, yields it to Leto out of regard for a wife of Zeus.
