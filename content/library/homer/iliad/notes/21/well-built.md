summary: Achilles sold Lycaon into “well-built Lemnos” (21.40), having caught him “in the well-built orchard” (21.77); Athena wishes the Trojans' allies were all as weak as Aphrodite, so that the Achaeans would long ago have sacked “the well-built citadel of Ilios” (21.433); and “the wall of the well-built city” is on Apollo's mind (21.516).
grc: [ἐϋδμήτοιο]
---

In Lycaon's verse ἐϋκτιμένῃ ἐν ἀλωῇ is his father's orchard (21.36), not the threshing floor of the same words in Book 20 (20.496; [[lycaon-son-of-priam]]). The last use is the other word of the entry, ἐΰδμητος: Apollo enters the city “for the wall of the well-built city was on his mind, / lest the Danaans sack it against fate that day” (21.516–517), the wall that he and Poseidon built (21.446–447; [[the-walls-of-troy]], [[poseidon-and-apollo-serve-laomedon]]). See [[lemnos]].
