summary: Achilles in the river blames his mother: “No other of the heavenly gods is so much to blame for this / as my dear mother” (21.275–276). Scamander, beaten by fire, pleads that he is “not so much to blame / as all the others who help the Trojans” (21.370–371), and Artemis tells her father that Hera is the one “from whom strife and quarrel have fastened upon the immortals” (21.513).
refs: [21.275-278, 21.369-376, 21.412-414, 21.512-513]
---

Athena tells Ares that his mother's curses are on him because he deserted the Achaeans (21.412–414), and Poseidon lays the gods' grudge against Troy on Laomedon, who cheated them (21.441–460). Each speaker puts the blame on another; Zeus hears Artemis' charge laughing, and the book does not give his answer (21.508–513; see [[aitios]] and [[mothers-and-sons]]).
