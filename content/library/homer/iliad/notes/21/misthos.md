---
summary: Poseidon reminds Apollo that they came from Zeus to proud Laomedon “and served for hire for a year / for a fixed wage” (21.444–445), and that when the seasons brought the term of their wage, “then he robbed us by force of our whole wage” (21.451); they went home “angry over the wage, which he had promised and did not pay” (21.457).
---

Walter Leaf notes that θητεύω recurs in Homer only twice, in the Odyssey, and that Eurymachus' offer shows it means hired service; he thinks it going too far to infer from Achilles' ἐπάρουρος, “bound to the land”, the status of a serf (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 21.444; *Odyssey* 11.489, 18.357–358). Laomedon threatened besides to bind the two gods and sell them to islands far away (21.453–454), as a captive is sold. The story is told also at 7.452–453, where both gods build the wall (see the translation's note on 21.441–457, [[poseidon-and-apollo-serve-laomedon]], [[laomedon]] and [[kotos]]).
