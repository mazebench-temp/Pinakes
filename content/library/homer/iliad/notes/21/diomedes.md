# Διομήδεʼ, the elided accusative (21.396), found only here in Homer.
grc: [Διομήδεʼ]
---

Ares reminds Athena of his wound as he attacks her: “Do you not remember when you set Diomedes, son of Tydeus, on / to stab me” (21.396–397; see [[ares]], [[athena]] and [[diomedes-wounds-ares]]). In Book 5 she released Diomedes from her order not to fight the gods, told him to drive his horses at Ares first, and drove his spear home into the god's flank, wearing the helmet of Hades so that Ares should not see her (5.826–834, 5.844–845, 5.855–859). Ares now says that she took the spear herself, “the spear seen by all” (21.397; see [[seen-by-all]]); Walter Leaf takes πανόψιον as pointed against the goddess who had been invisible, and reports Antimachus' reading ὑπονόσφιον, “stealthy”, since found in a papyrus (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 21.397; see the translation's note on 21.397). The patronymic Τυδεΐδην, “son of Tydeus”, stands beside the name (see [[son-of-tydeus]] and [[tydeus]]). Diomedes himself has been out of the fighting since Paris' arrow went through his foot on the third day of battle (11.369–400).
