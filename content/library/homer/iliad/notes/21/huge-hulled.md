---
summary: The Trojans flee from Achilles into the river “As before a huge-bellied dolphin the other fishes / flee and fill the recesses of a harbor” (21.22–23).
grc: [μεγακήτεος]
en: [huge-bellied]
---

The translation renders the word “huge-bellied” of the dolphin and keeps “huge in the hull” for ships (see its note on 21.22). Walter Leaf refers the word to his note on Odysseus' ship (8.222), and rejects van Leeuwen's ἐλλοί for ἄλλοι, “the other”: the poet counts the dolphin among the fishes (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 21.22). The fishes of the simile become real fishes later in the book, when Achilles throws Lycaon to them and the eels and fishes feed on Asteropaeus (21.122–127, 21.203–204; [[fish]]). See [[achilles]].
