summary: The formula ποδάρκης δῖος Ἀχιλλεύς comes three times: as Achilles catches sight of the unarmed Lycaon (21.49), as he speaks first to Asteropaeus (21.149), and as he sets out again and again against the river (21.265).
---

“As often as brilliant swift-footed Achilles set out / to stand against it” (21.265–266): the epithet of speed is given to the man whom the river outruns, “nimble though he was” (21.264; [[nimble]]). See [[achilles]], [[brilliant]] and [[swift-footed]].
