---
summary: Achilles tries whether all “the immortals were putting him to flight, who hold the wide sky” (21.267); he groans aloud, “looking up into the wide sky” (21.272); and in a simile “smoke goes up and reaches the wide sky” from a burning city (21.522).
---

The second verse is Menelaus' when his sword broke on Paris' helmet, with Achilles' patronymic for his: “And the son of Atreus groaned aloud, looking up into the wide sky” (3.364; [[father-zeus]]). The simile of the smoke is adapted from Book 18, where the smoke of a besieged island town reaches the upper air (18.207; Walter Leaf, *Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 21.522; see the translation's note on 21.522–525). See [[immortals]] and [[achilles]].
