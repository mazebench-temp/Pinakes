---
summary: “Brilliant” is Achilles as he comes upon Lycaon, raises his spear and speaks to Asteropaeus (21.39, 21.49, 21.67, 21.149, 21.161), as the river plots to stop him (21.138, 21.250), as he sets out against the wave (21.265), and in the river's plea to Hephaestus (21.359); and Agenor, whom Apollo sets on against him (21.545, 21.579).
---

**The river's purpose.** The river ponders how to stop “brilliant Achilles” from his toil and ward off ruin from the Trojans, and the verse comes again when the river rises against him (21.138 = 21.250; [[shameful-ruin]]). Walter Leaf thinks the second use interpolated from the first (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 21.249).

**Swift-footed and brilliant.** Three times Achilles is ποδάρκης δῖος Ἀχιλλεύς, the formula of Book 20 (21.49, 21.149, 21.265; [[podarkes]]): when he sees Lycaon, when he speaks first to Asteropaeus, and when he sets out against the river.

**Agenor.** The last uses are Agenor's: “had not Phoebus Apollo set brilliant Agenor on” (21.545) and “so the son of noble Antenor, brilliant Agenor, / would not flee” (21.579–580; [[noble]], [[son-of-antenor]]). δῖαν at 21.43 and 21.219 is “shining,” of Arisbe and of the sea, and is excepted. See [[achilles]] and [[agenor]].
