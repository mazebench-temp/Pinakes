---
summary: Achilles leaps into the river with only his sword, “like a god” (21.18), and after his answer to the river “he rushed on the Trojans like a god” (21.227), just before the river rises against him.
---

The formula marks the hero's attack at its height, where a god often checks him: at the fourth charge against the mist in Book 20 (20.447), and here twice. The first time Achilles fills the river with the dead (21.18–21); the second time, having refused the river's plea to drive the Trojans out of his streams, he rushes on them again, and the river, a god in the likeness of a man (21.213), turns on him (21.233–234).
