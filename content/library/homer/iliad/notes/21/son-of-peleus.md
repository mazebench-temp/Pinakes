summary: The patronymic follows Achilles through the book: against Asteropaeus (21.139, 21.153, 21.173), in the river (21.208, 21.251, 21.272, 21.288, 21.306, 21.327), in Agenor's soliloquy (21.557), and in the last scene, where Apollo draws him away (21.595, 21.599).
grc: ["Πηλέος υἱὸς"]
---

**Asteropaeus.** “Meanwhile the son of Peleus, holding his long-shadowed spear, / leapt at Asteropaeus” (21.139–140; [[long-shadowed]]); Asteropaeus addresses him, “Great-hearted son of Peleus” (21.153; [[great-hearted]]), and “the son of Peleus drew the sharp sword from beside his thigh” (21.173). The nominative Πηλέος υἱός is new to the entry.

**The river.** The Paeonians flee when they see their best man brought down “at the hands of the son of Peleus” (21.208). The son of Peleus springs away from the river “as far as the cast of a spear” (21.251), groans looking up into the wide sky (21.272; [[wide-sky]]), and is told by Poseidon, “Son of Peleus, do not tremble too much, nor be afraid” (21.288). The river is “enraged at the son of Peleus” and its wave “was bringing the son of Peleus down” (21.306, 21.327).

**Agenor and Apollo.** Agenor thinks of letting the others “be driven in confusion / by Achilles, son of Peleus” (21.556–557); “Then the son of Peleus rushed at godlike Agenor” (21.595), and “by a trick he kept the son of Peleus away from the people” (21.599; [[truth-and-deception]]).
