summary: The Trojans are penned into “the deep-streaming, silver-eddying river” (21.8), and Achilles tells them that “the river, fair-flowing and silver-eddying though it is,” will not protect them (21.130–131).
grc: [ἀργυροδίνην, ἀργυροδίνης]
---

The two uses of the book are the epithet's only uses for the Xanthus; its other use in Homer is the Peneius of the Catalogue (2.753). The first comes as half the Trojans fall into the river “with a great crash” (21.8–9), with the epithet that is otherwise said only of Oceanus ([[deep-streaming]]). The second stands in Achilles' taunt over Lycaon beside [[fair-flowing]]: the epithets of a beautiful river are heaped up for the god who will not save his worshippers (21.130–132; [[rivers]]). See [[scamander]] and [[eddying]].
