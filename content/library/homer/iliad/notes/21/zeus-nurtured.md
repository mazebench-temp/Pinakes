---
summary: Lycaon calls Achilles “you who are nurtured by Zeus” as he begs for his life (21.75), and Achilles answers the river, “So it shall be, Scamander, nurtured by Zeus, as you bid” (21.223).
---

The epithet of kings is given here to a river, the only river so addressed in Homer: the Xanthus is the son of Zeus (21.2), and the title fits its birth. Achilles' words grant the river's plea in form and refuse it in fact, for he goes on killing the Trojans until he has penned them in the city (21.223–227; see the translation's note on 21.223 and [[swift-footed]]).
