---
summary: Lycaon wanted above all “to escape evil death and black doom” (21.66), and Agenor sees that if Achilles catches him on the plain, “there will be no more escaping death and the fates” (21.565).
---

At 21.548 κῆρας, “fates”, is read for χεῖρας as early as Eustathius, but Walter Leaf finds no manuscript authority for it and keeps “the heavy hands of Death” (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 21.548; see the translation's note on 21.548 and [[thanatos]]).
