---
summary: Ares calls Athena a dog-fly who drives the gods to strife “with your gale of daring” (21.395); Athena wishes all the Trojans' helpers were “so bold and hardy, as Aphrodite was” (21.430); Apollo “cast daring into” Agenor's heart (21.547), and Agenor calls Achilles “terrible and bold warrior though you are” (21.589).
---

θάρσος ἄητον is doubtful: Walter Leaf suggests “blown about”, from ἄητο a few lines above, and reports “insatiable” as barely possible (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 21.395; see the translation's note on 21.395 and [[dog-fly]]). Athena's “bold and hardy” is mockery: Aphrodite fell at a single blow (21.424–426).
