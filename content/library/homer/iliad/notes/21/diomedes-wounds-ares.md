# Ares' memory of Book 5 (21.394-399).
refs: [21.394-399]
---

{{quote:22c2d59a-76a5-5375-9b6b-a6a5efe4bff4}}

The last words of 398 are those of the wound itself, turned into the second person: διὰ δὲ χρόα καλὸν ἔδαψας, “and tore through my fair skin”, as the narrator said of Diomedes' thrust, διὰ δὲ χρόα καλὸν ἔδαψεν (5.858; Walter Leaf, *Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 21.397). Ares remembers the spear as Athena's, not Diomedes', and as “seen by all” (πανόψιον, 21.397), where in Book 5 she had put on the helmet of Hades so that he should not see her (5.845; see [[helmet-of-hades]]); Leaf takes the word as pointed at that invisible goddess (on 21.397; see [[seen-by-all]], [[diomedes]] and [[athena]]).

His threat, “So now I think you will pay for all that you have done” (21.399), comes to nothing. He stabs at the aegis, and she fells him with a boundary stone and laughs over him (21.400–414; see [[the-battle-of-the-gods]] and [[ares]]). The end of Book 5 is reversed. There the wounded god went up to Olympus, was healed and sat beside Zeus “exulting in his glory” (5.906); here he lies in the dust, and Aphrodite leads him away groaning (21.407, 21.416–417). See [[gods-wounded-by-mortals]].
