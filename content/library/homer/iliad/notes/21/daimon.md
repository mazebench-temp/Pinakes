---
summary: Achilles leaps into the river “like a god” (21.18) and rushes on the Trojans “like a god” (21.227), and Lycaon knows why he has fallen into his hands again: “a god has brought me here” (21.93).
---

δαίμονι ἶσος is the half-verse of a warrior's onslaught (see [[like-a-god]]). Lycaon's god has no name, as the god who threw him into Achilles' hands has none (21.47), and Achilles says the same: no Trojan whom a god throws into his hands will escape (21.103–104; see [[divine-intervention]]).
