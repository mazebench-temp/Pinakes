---
summary: Achilles leaps into the river and “devised evil deeds in his mind” (21.19), and will learn “in my mind” whether Lycaon comes back from below (21.61). Lycaon bids him “lay it to heart” (21.94), the gods' heart is “blown two ways” when strife falls among them (21.386), and Agenor tells Achilles, “Surely you hoped in your mind” to sack Troy that day (21.583).
---

21.94 is Achilles' own verse to Agamemnon in Book 1 (1.297; see [[lay-it-to-heart]]), and the half-verse of 21.19 recurs when Achilles kills the twelve at the pyre (23.176; see [[human-sacrifice]] and [[thymos]]).
