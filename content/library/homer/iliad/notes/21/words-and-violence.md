summary: Words give way to blows throughout the book. Achilles cuts off Lycaon's plea, “Fool, do not offer me ransom, nor make speeches” (21.99), and kills him. Two of the gods' fights open with abuse and end with a blow (21.391–406, 21.479–492). Apollo and Hermes will not fight at all, Hermes because “it is a hard thing / to trade blows with the wives of Zeus the cloud-gatherer” (21.498–499).
refs: [21.74-121, 21.391-414, 21.470-501]
---

Hera fears that the river will turn Hephaestus back “with soothing words and threats” (21.339), and the river does beg, but it is Hera's word that stops the fire (21.367–381). Apollo answers Artemis' abuse with silence (21.478), and Hera breaks off her own threat and finishes it with the bow: Walter Leaf, after Nicanor, takes the deed as the end of the sentence (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 21.487; see the translation's note on 21.487–489). Agenor's taunt before his throw is the book's last challenge, and a god answers it by taking his place (21.583–605; see [[oneidos]], [[neikos]] and [[apollo-deceives-achilles]]).
