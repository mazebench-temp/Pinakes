---
summary: Scamander threatens Achilles with a burial without fame: his armor will lie at the bottom of the lake under the slime, his body will be wrapped in sand and gravel, “nor will the Achaeans know how / to gather his bones”, and “Right there his mound shall be made, and he will have no need / of a heaped-up tomb, when the Achaeans hold his funeral” (21.316–323).
refs: [21.316-323]
---

The river's mound is the silt itself, and the tomb that would keep his name is taken from him. Crates read τυμβοχόης, “of a heaped-up tomb”, Aristarchus τυμβοχοῆσʼ, an infinitive (Walter Leaf, *Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 21.323; see the translation's note on 21.317–323). Achilles' own fear in the river is the same: not death, but a death like a swineherd boy's in a torrent, instead of a death at the hands of the best man bred at Troy (21.279–283; see [[the-short-life-of-achilles]] and [[honoring-the-dead]]).
