---
summary: Words do the work of the book's scenes. Lycaon begs “with words” (21.98); Poseidon and Athena “pledged their faith with words” (21.286); Hera fears that the river will turn Hephaestus back “with soothing words and threats” (21.339); and Hera rebukes Artemis “with abusive words” (21.480). The speeches are framed with the formula of “winged words” (21.73, 21.121, 21.368, 21.409, 21.419, 21.427).
---

Lycaon's words meet “the voice he heard would not be soothed” (21.98), the half-verse of Agamemnon's answer to the sons of Antimachus, who had begged “with soothing words” (11.136–137): the soothing word and the unsoothed answer belong together (see [[supplication]], [[winged-words]] and [[words-and-violence]]).
