---
summary: Lycaon pleads that he is not Hector's full brother, Hector “who killed your companion, gentle and strong” (21.96).
---

The verse is nearly the one Zeus spoke of Patroclus in Book 17 (17.204). Walter Leaf accepts Bayfield's condemnation of 21.94–96, among other reasons because the praise of Patroclus is out of place in an enemy's mouth (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 21.94; see the translation's note on 21.94–96 and [[lycaon-son-of-priam]]).
