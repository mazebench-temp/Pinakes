summary: A Thersilochus is the first of the seven Paeonians whom Achilles kills beside the river after their leader Asteropaeus has fallen (21.209); he may be the ally whom Hector urged on in Book 17.

{{quote:0a06205c-56c9-5222-b5fe-cbf1fdc8aea7}}

**The first of seven.** ἔνθʼ ἕλε Θερσίλοχόν τε, “There he killed Thersilochus” (21.209): when Asteropaeus, the best of the Paeonians, has fallen, Achilles goes after the rest along the river, and Thersilochus heads the list of the seven he kills (see [[asteropaeus]], [[paeonians]] and [[achilles]]). Nothing is told of him but his name.

**The same man?** The name is found only here and in Hector's list of the allied leaders in Book 17, where Thersilochus stands next to Asteropaeus, “Mesthles and Glaucus and Medon and Thersilochus / and Asteropaeus and Deisenor and Hippothous” (17.216–217; see [[hector]] and [[trojan-allies]]). If he is the same man, the ally whom Hector named beside the Paeonian leader dies here among the Paeonians, in the rout that follows that leader's death. The poem does not say so; and two others of the seven, Mydon and Ophelestes, bear the names of men killed earlier in the poem (5.580, 8.274; see [[mydon-the-paeonian]] and [[ophelestes-the-paeonian]]).
