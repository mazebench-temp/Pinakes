---
summary: “Shining Hector raged” on the day before (21.5); Lycaon and Asteropaeus are each “the shining son” of his father (21.97, 21.152); and the two Trojans who stand against Achilles call him “shining Achilles” (21.160, 21.583).
---

**Two sons.** The verse-end φαίδιμος υἱός closes the frame of two speeches: “So the shining son of Priam spoke to him” (21.97), after Lycaon's plea, and “Then the shining son of Pelegon spoke to him in turn” (21.152), before Asteropaeus' answer (see [[lycaon-son-of-priam]], [[asteropaeus]] and [[so-he-spoke]]).

**Shining Achilles.** The vocative φαίδιμʼ Ἀχιλλεῦ is spoken by Phoenix in Book 9 (9.434); in this book it is spoken twice by men about to fight him. Asteropaeus ends his genealogy with it, “Now let us fight, shining Achilles” (21.160), and Agenor begins his challenge with it, “Surely you hoped in your mind, shining Achilles, / that on this day you would sack the city of the proud Trojans” (21.583–584; [[proud]], [[agenor]]).
