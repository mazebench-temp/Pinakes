---
summary: The burning river speaks to Hephaestus: “And the force of the river was burning, and he spoke to him, and called him by name” (21.356).
en: ["spoke to him, and called him by name"]
---

The half-verse of close address, used for a mother's touch and a wife's clinging hand, is said here by a river on fire to the god who is burning him, and the name follows at once: “Hephaestus, no one of the gods can match himself against you” (21.357). ἲς ποταμοῖο, “the force of the river,” is a periphrasis common in the Odyssey and found only here in the Iliad (Walter Leaf, *Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 21.356; see the translation's note on 21.356).
