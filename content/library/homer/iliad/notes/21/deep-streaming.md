---
summary: The Trojans are driven into “the deep-streaming, silver-eddying river” (21.8), the epithet's one use for a river other than Oceanus; and in Achilles' boast over Asteropaeus Oceanus himself is “deep-flowing” (21.195).
grc: [βαθύρροον, βαθυρρείταο]
en: [deep-flowing]
---

βαθύρροος is said elsewhere only of Oceanus, as Walter Leaf notes (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 21.8; see the translation's note on 21.8). Here it is the Xanthus, deep enough to drown men, beside [[silver-eddying]]. The second form, βαθυρρείτης, names Oceanus in the verse that sets him below Zeus: “with whom not even lord Achelous can match himself, / nor the great strength of deep-flowing Oceanus, / from whom all rivers and all the sea / and all springs and deep wells flow” (21.194–197). Zenodotus left out the verse of Oceanus, and Leaf, defending it, calls it “one of the most majestic lines ever written” (on 21.195; see the translation's note on 21.194–197).
