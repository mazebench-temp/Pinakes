---
summary: Poseidon tells Achilles in the river, “for such helpers from among the gods are we two to you, / with the consent of Zeus, I and Pallas Athena” (21.289–290).
---

The verse in which the god names himself was athetized, since a god in a man's shape should not do so; Walter Leaf defends it, since Achilles must know that his help is divine, and a god's disguise is explained by the danger of his full brightness to mortal eyes (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 21.290; see the translation's note on 21.290, [[gods-are-hard-to-face]] and [[seeing-the-gods]]).
