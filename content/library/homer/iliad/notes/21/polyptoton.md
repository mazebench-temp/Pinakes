---
summary: Achilles' boast over Asteropaeus turns on the word “river,” repeated in six forms (21.185–196), and in the river he wishes for the death of “a brave man” at the hands of “a brave man” (21.280).
grc: ["ἀγαθὸς μὲν ἔπεφνʼ"]
refs: [21.185, 21.186, 21.190, 21.191, 21.192, 21.196]
---

**The rivers.** ποταμοῖο, ποταμοῦ, ποταμῶν, ποταμοῖο, ποταμός, ποταμοί (21.185, 21.186, 21.190, 21.191, 21.192, 21.196): the river that fathered Asteropaeus' line is named in every case and set below Zeus each time, “So, as Zeus is mightier than rivers that murmur to the sea, / so the lineage of Zeus is mightier than a river's” (21.190–191; [[zeus-is-mightier-than-rivers]], [[murmuring-to-the-sea]]).

**The brave.** “Would that Hector had killed me, the best man bred here; / then a brave man would have been the killer, and he would have slain a brave man” (21.279–280): ἀγαθὸς μὲν ἔπεφνʼ, ἀγαθὸν δέ κεν ἐξενάριξε. Walter Leaf finds a reminiscence of the verse in a line of Sophocles' *Philoctetes* (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 21.278). See [[achilles]] and [[death-of-achilles-foretold]].
