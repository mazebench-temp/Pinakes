---
summary: Zeus watches the gods' battle from Olympus and laughs, “as he saw the gods coming together in strife” (21.390). Priam watches Achilles from the tower: he “caught sight of huge Achilles; and before him / the Trojans were at once driven in confusion” (21.527–528), and comes down to order the gates opened (21.529–536).
refs: [21.388-390, 21.526-536]
---

In Book 20 Zeus said that he would stay on Olympus to look on and delight his heart (20.22–23), and Walter Leaf saw his laughter here as the exaggeration of that pleasure (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 20.23; see [[laughter-of-the-gods]]). Priam's tower is “divine”, as built by a god (21.526; Leaf on 21.526; see [[the-tower-of-ilios]]). At the end the Trojans do not dare to wait outside the wall “and learn who had escaped / and who had died in the war” (21.609–610).
