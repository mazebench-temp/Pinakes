---
summary: In the first simile of the book the locusts flee to a river from “the tireless fire” (21.13), and Hera tells Hephaestus to hold back “the tireless fire” only when she calls out (21.341).
---

The two uses frame the fight of the river. The simile opens it: “As when, under the rush of fire, locusts flutter up into the air / to flee to a river, and the tireless fire burns, / springing up suddenly, and they cower down into the water” (21.12–14). The Trojans who leap into the Xanthus are the locusts, and the river is their refuge. Hera's order turns the picture round: the tireless fire is now sent against the river itself (21.337–341), and it is “a wondrous fire” as Hephaestus readies it (21.342; [[wondrous]]).
