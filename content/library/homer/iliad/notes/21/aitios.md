---
summary: Two speakers measure blame in the same words. Achilles in the river: “No other of the heavenly gods is so much to blame for this / as my dear mother, who bewitched me with lies” (21.275–276); Scamander to Hera: “I am surely not so much to blame / as all the others who help the Trojans” (21.370–371).
---

Achilles blames his mother for a prophecy that has not yet failed him: she foretold his death under the wall, by Apollo's arrows (21.277–278), and Poseidon assures him at once that it is not his fate to die in the river (21.291). The river shifts its blame to the other gods who help the Trojans, and buys peace with an oath (21.372–376; see [[who-is-to-blame]] and [[mothers-and-sons]]).
