**A father.** In the Catalogue and at the death of Pyraechmes the Axius is the river the Paeonians come from (2.849–850, 16.288). Here it is a god and the head of a family. When Achilles leaps at Asteropaeus in the Scamander, the narrator gives his line from the river: the Axius “of the broad streams” begot Pelegon on Periboea, the eldest of the daughters of Acessamenus, “for with her the deep-eddying river lay” (21.141–143; see [[pelegon]], [[periboea]], [[acessamenus]] and [[deep-eddying]]). εὐρυρέεθρος, “of the broad streams”, is found only here. A river at the head of a line has a parallel in Book 5, where the lineage of Diocles of Pherae is from the Alpheus, “which flows broad through the land of the Pylians, / who begot Ortilochus” (5.544–546; see [[alpheus]] and [[rivers]]).

**In Asteropaeus' mouth.** Asked who he is, Asteropaeus begins from the river, and praises it in the words of the Catalogue: “But my lineage is from the broad-flowing Axius, / the Axius, who sends the fairest water over the earth, / who begot Pelegon, famed for his spear” (21.157–159 ≈ 2.849–850; see [[asteropaeus]] and [[genealogy-in-battle]]). In the Catalogue the water “spreads over the earth” (ἐπικίδναται αἶαν, 2.850); here the river himself “sends” it (ἵησιν, 21.158). Walter Leaf thinks 21.158 a late adaptation of the Catalogue's verse (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 21.158).

**Achilles' answer.** Over Asteropaeus' body Achilles turns the lineage against him in the river's own epithet: “You say that you are of the lineage of a broad-flowing river, / but I claim to be of the lineage of great Zeus” (21.186–187), and as Zeus is mightier than rivers, so is the lineage of Zeus mightier than a river's (21.190–191; see [[zeus-is-mightier-than-rivers]]). εὐρὺ ῥέοντος, “broad-flowing”, comes from Asteropaeus' own verse (21.157). A great river is “here beside you”, Achilles goes on, “if he can do anything / to help” (21.192–193): Leaf notes that πάρα, “beside”, would seem to mean the Scamander, though from the context it should rather be the Axius (on 21.192; see [[scamander]]).
