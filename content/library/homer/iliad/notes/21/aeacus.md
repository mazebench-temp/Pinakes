# Αἰακὸς (21.189), the name itself, is found only here in Homer; elsewhere only the patronymic Αἰακίδης.
grc: [Αἰακὸς]
---

{{quote:fb1d5b93-1341-5a1d-9bad-b4ff6812c63c}}

**The name.** Αἰακός is heard only here in Homer. Everywhere else Aeacus lives in the patronymic Αἰακίδης, which names his son Peleus and his grandson Achilles, and both are in this book (see [[grandson-of-aeacus]]). Asteropaeus, unable to pull Achilles' spear out of the bank, means to bend and break “the ash spear of the grandson of Aeacus” (21.178; see [[the-pelian-ash]] and [[asteropaeus]]); and in Achilles' boast the nominative names his father, Πηλεὺς Αἰακίδης, “Peleus, son of Aeacus”, as at 16.15, in the same verse as the grandfather's own name (21.189; see [[peleus]]).

**Son of Zeus.** ὃ δʼ ἄρʼ Αἰακὸς ἐκ Διὸς ἦεν, “and Aeacus was the son of Zeus” (21.189), is the only place where the poem says it. Achilles says it to answer the dead man's descent from a river: Asteropaeus was the grandson of the Axius, and Achilles, through Peleus and Aeacus, the great-grandson of Zeus, as Idomeneus was through Deucalion and Minos (13.449–452; see [[zeus-is-mightier-than-rivers]], [[genealogy-in-battle]] and [[idomeneus]]). He does not name his mother, the goddess Thetis, whom Aeneas had named with Peleus when he spoke of Achilles' parents in the book before (20.206–207; see [[thetis]]).
