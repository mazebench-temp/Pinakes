---
summary: The Xanthus is the son of “immortal Zeus” (21.2); Achilles, beaten back by the river, wonders whether all the immortals are putting him to flight (21.267); Hera stops Hephaestus, since it is not fitting “to batter an immortal god like this for the sake of mortals” (21.380); and the battle of the gods is fought among the immortals (21.476, 21.500, 21.513).
---

**The river.** “the immortals were putting him to flight, who hold the wide sky” (21.267; [[wide-sky]]): Achilles, fighting a river, sets out again and again to learn whether all the gods are against him, and the wave beats him back (21.265–271). Poseidon and Athena, having promised help, go away “among the immortals” (21.298).

**Immortal and mortal.** The book turns on the difference. Hera calls off Hephaestus, “for it is not fitting / to batter an immortal god like this for the sake of mortals” (21.379–380), and Apollo refuses to fight Poseidon “for the sake of mortals, / wretched creatures” (21.463–464; [[gods-and-men-not-alike]], [[the-generations-of-leaves]]). Among themselves the gods boast: Artemis mocks Apollo's boasts “among the immortal gods” (21.476), Hermes lets Leto boast “among the immortal gods” that she beat him (21.500), and Artemis lays the strife “upon the immortals” at Hera's door (21.513). See [[the-battle-of-the-gods]], [[gods-and-mortals]] and [[mortal-men]].
