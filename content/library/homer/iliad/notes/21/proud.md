summary: Poseidon recalls the year when he and Apollo served “proud Laomedon” for hire (21.443); and Agenor taunts Achilles that he hoped to sack “the city of the proud Trojans” that day (21.584).
grc: ["ἀγήνορι Λαομέδοντι"]
---

**Laomedon.** ἀγήνωρ, the epithet of the headstrong heart, is given to the king who cheated the gods of their wage and threatened to sell them (21.443–457; [[laomedon]], [[poseidon-and-apollo-serve-laomedon]]). The pattern is the phrase, since Ἀγήνορι, the dative of Agenor's name, stands in the same book (21.600).

**The proud Trojans.** Agenor's words echo Apollo's warning to Patroclus at the wall, that it was not fated “that the city of the proud Trojans be sacked by your spear” (16.708): “Surely you hoped in your mind, shining Achilles, / that on this day you would sack the city of the proud Trojans” (21.583–584; [[phaidimos]]). See [[agenor]] and [[trojans]].
