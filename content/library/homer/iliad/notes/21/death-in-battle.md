summary: The river is the book's battlefield. Achilles kills in the water until “the water grew red with blood” (21.21), and the river complains that “my lovely streams are full of corpses” (21.218). Lycaon is struck beside the neck and flung into the river for the fishes (21.116–127); Asteropaeus is struck in the belly, “his bowels poured out on the ground, and darkness covered his eyes / as he gasped” (21.181–182); seven Paeonians are only named (21.209–210).
refs: [21.15-33, 21.114-127, 21.179-183, 21.200-210, 21.218-220, 21.235-239, 21.300-302, 21.343-349]
---

The book denies its dead their rites. Lycaon's mother will not lament him, and the fish will eat his fat (21.122–127); Asteropaeus is left lying in the sand, where the eels and the fishes tear at the fat about his kidneys (21.200–204); the river casts the bodies out onto the dry land and hides the living in its eddies (21.235–239); and Hephaestus' fire burns the dead on the plain (21.343–349). Twelve young men are taken alive to be killed at Patroclus' pyre (21.26–32; see [[human-sacrifice]] and [[honoring-the-dead]]). The seven Paeonians are [[thersilochus|Thersilochus]], [[mydon-the-paeonian|Mydon]], [[astypylus|Astypylus]], [[mnesus|Mnesus]], [[thrasius|Thrasius]], [[aenius|Aenius]] and [[ophelestes-the-paeonian|Ophelestes]] (see [[darkness-covered-his-eyes]], [[androktasia]] and [[the-aristeia]]).
