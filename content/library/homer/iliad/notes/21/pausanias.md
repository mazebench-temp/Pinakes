---
summary: Pausanias says that the Argives once let down bridled horses for Poseidon into Dine, a spring that rises in the sea, which Walter Leaf compares with the horses that the Trojans throw into the Scamander; and at Megara he was shown an altar of Achelous, the river whom Achilles names in his boast.
---

- Horses let down into Dine for Poseidon (*Description of Greece* 8.7.1–2, on 21.132): [[horses-for-the-rivers]].
- **An altar of Achelous.** At Megara, Pausanias says, the local guide led him to a place called Rhus, “Stream”, because water had once flowed there from the mountains above the city; Theagenes, then tyrant, turned the water elsewhere and made an altar to Achelous there (*Description of Greece* 1.41.2). Walter Leaf cites the passage for the wide worship of Achelous (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 21.194), whom Achilles names in his boast over Asteropaeus: there is no fighting with Zeus, “with whom not even lord Achelous can match himself” (21.194): [[achelous]].
