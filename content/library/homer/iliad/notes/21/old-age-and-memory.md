summary: Poseidon claims the elder's right: Apollo should begin, “since I was born first and know more” (21.440), and he reproaches the younger god for forgetting, “You do not even / remember all the evils we suffered around Ilios” (21.441–442), and tells him the story of Laomedon (21.443–460).
refs: [21.436-460]
---

The story fails to move him: Apollo will not fight his father's brother (21.461–469). Ares too begins with memory, “Do you not remember” the day Athena set Diomedes on him (21.396), and Athena fells him with a stone that “men of former times had set there to be the boundary stone of a field” (21.405; see [[genee]], [[boundary-stones]] and [[poseidon-and-apollo-serve-laomedon]]).
