---
summary: Achilles in the river blames “my dear mother, who bewitched me with lies” (21.276), for she had told him that he would die by Apollo's arrows under the wall.
grc: [ψεύδεσσιν]
---

Thetis' word has not failed: Poseidon tells him at once that it is not his fate to die in the river (21.291), and Hector, dying, will foretell his death at the Scaean gates (22.359–360). The verb θέλγω, “bewitch”, returns at the end of the book for Apollo's trick (21.604; see [[truth-and-deception]], [[thetis]] and [[the-short-life-of-achilles]]).
