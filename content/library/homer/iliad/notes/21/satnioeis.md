---
summary: The river is named for the third and last time in Lycaon's plea to Achilles: his grandfather Altes, king of the Leleges, holds “steep Pedasus on the Satnioeis” (21.87).
---

Each of the three men whose story names the Satnioeis dies in battle: Elatus, who lived on its banks (6.34–35; see [[elatus]]), Satnius, born there to a Naiad (14.443–445; see [[satnius]]), and now Lycaon, the grandson of the king who holds Pedasus on the river, who names it while he begs Achilles for his life (21.85–87; see [[lycaon-son-of-priam]], [[altes]] and [[pedasus-on-the-satnioeis]]). ἐπὶ Σατνιόεντι, “on the Satnioeis”, is Walter Leaf's reading and Strabo's for the manuscripts' ὑπό, “below”: the Satnioeis is a river, not a mountain (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 21.87; *Geography* 13.1.50; see the translation's note on 21.86–87).
