summary: Glory is what the gods give and withhold. Achilles, driving the Trojans to the city, “was eager to win glory” (21.543); Agenor, deciding to stand, reminds himself that “Zeus, son of Cronus, grants him glory” (21.570); and Apollo, snatching Agenor away, “no longer allowed him to win glory” (21.596). The gods go back to Olympus, “some of them angry, and some exulting greatly” (21.519).
grc: [κυδιόωντες]
en: [exulting]
---

Agenor's last line is Nestor's of Hector, “For now Zeus, son of Cronus, grants glory to this man” (8.141), and was athetized because it contradicts the hope Agenor has just expressed; Walter Leaf agrees (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 21.570; see the translation's note on 21.568–570). The participle κυδιόων, “exulting”, is said of Agamemnon among his men and of the stallion that runs to the river (2.579; 6.509 = 15.266; see [[agenor]] and [[apollo-deceives-achilles]]).
