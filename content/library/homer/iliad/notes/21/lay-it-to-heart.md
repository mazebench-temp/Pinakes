---
summary: Lycaon introduces his last argument with the formula: “And I will tell you something else, and you lay it to heart: / do not kill me, since I am not of the same womb as Hector” (21.94–95).
---

The verse is Achilles' own in the quarrel of Book 1 (1.297). Walter Leaf reports that Bayfield condemned 21.94–96, and finds his grounds good: two of the verses are borrowed (21.94 = 1.297, 21.96 = 17.204), the verse of warning does not suit a petition, and the plea that Lycaon is not Hector's full brother comes oddly after he has named his full brother Polydorus (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 21.94; see the translation's note on 21.94–96).
