---
summary: Four of the surviving lines of the Latin Odyssey render lines of Book 4, all from Sparta. They are Menelaus' call to turn from weeping to supper, Proteus on the Achaeans who survived, the goddess who saved Agamemnon, and the nymph who holds Odysseus.
refs: [4.213, 4.495, 4.513, 4.557]
---

The lines are given as The Latin Library prints them, with the lines of Book 4 that it sets beside each; the collection does not name their ancient sources.

- ***atque escas habeamus mentionem,*** “and let us make mention of food” (4.213): Menelaus' answer to Peisistratus after the weeping in his hall, “So let us leave off the weeping that came before, / and let us remember supper again, and let them pour water / over our hands” (4.212–214; see [[menelaus]] and [[peisistratus]]). *Escas*, “food”, stands for δόρπου, “supper”, and *habeamus mentionem*, “let us make mention”, for μνησώμεθα, “let us remember”; the fragment has no word for ἐξαῦτις, “again”.
- ***partim errant, nequinont Graeciam redire;*** “some are wandering, they cannot return to Greece” (4.495). Menelaus asks whether all the Achaeans came home unharmed, and Proteus answers, “For many of them were brought down, and many were left” (4.486–495; see [[proteus]] and [[the-returns-of-the-achaeans]]). *Nequinont* is an old form of *nequeunt*, “they cannot”. Where Homer says only that many “were left”, the Latin says what became of them. Proteus goes on to the leaders who perished in their homecoming and to one who, “still alive somewhere, is held back on the wide sea” (4.497–498; see [[odysseus]]).
- ***sancta puer Saturni... regina*** “holy child of Saturn … queen” (4.513): Agamemnon's escape from the sea, “But your brother escaped the dooms and got away from them / in his hollow ships; for queen Hera saved him” (4.512–513; see [[hera]] and [[agamemnon]]). *Regina*, “queen”, answers πότνια (see [[queenly]]). The goddess is named by her father, Saturn, the Roman Cronus, as the Iliad calls Hera “daughter of great Cronus” (*Iliad* 5.721, 8.383; see [[cronus]]). *Puer*, “child”, takes the feminine *sancta*, as *mea puer* is said of Athena in the fragment of 1.64. The Latin Library marks a gap between *Saturni* and *regina*.
- ***apud nimpham Atlantis filiam Calipsonem,*** “at the house of the nymph, the daughter of Atlas, Calypso” (4.557): Proteus' word of Odysseus, “him I saw on an island shedding big tears / in the halls of the nymph Calypso, who by force / holds him there” (4.556–558; see [[calypso]]). *Apud*, “at the house of”, stands for ἐν μεγάροισι, “in the halls of”, and *nimpham* is *nympham*, “nymph”. Homer's line names no father; the Latin adds Atlas, whose daughter she is elsewhere in the poem (1.52, 7.245; see [[atlas]]). Her name takes a Latin accusative, *Calipsonem*.
