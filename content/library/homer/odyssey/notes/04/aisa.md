---
summary: Penelope says that Odysseus never did anything ἐξαίσιον, “excessive”, to anyone, nor said anything excessive among the people (4.690–691).
grc: [ἐξαίσιον]
en: [excessive]
---

ἐξαίσιος is what goes beyond one's αἶσα, beyond what is due: Odysseus did nothing of the kind to any man, “though that is the way of divine kings” (4.690–692; see [[themis]] and [[kingship-and-authority]]). Merry renders “anything unfair”, and joins οὔτε ῥέξας τινὰ ἐξαίσιον, with οὔτε εἰπών added loosely (*Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 4.690); the translation follows, “who never did anything excessive to anyone, nor said anything excessive / among the people” (4.690–691). The Iliad has the word of Thetis' prayer, which Zeus would fulfill, “the excessive prayer of Thetis” (*Iliad* 15.598).
