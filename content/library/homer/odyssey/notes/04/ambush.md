# λόχος (4.441) and λοχάω (λοχησάμενος 4.388, 4.463; λοχήσομαι 4.670; λοχόωντες 4.847), first met here in the Odyssey.

---

**The Horse.** Helen walked “three times” around “the hollow ambush, feeling it all over” (4.277; see [[sinon-and-the-wooden-horse]]); the phrase returns when the Achaeans pour out of the horse (8.515).

**Proteus.** Eidothea tells Menelaus that he must “lie in ambush and catch” her father (4.388), and he asks her, “Devise now yourself an ambush for the divine old man” (4.395; see [[eidothea]] and [[a-god-is-hard-for-a-mortal-to-overpower]]). Lying under the sealskins, “There our ambush would have been most dread”, but for her ambrosia (4.441–446; see [[seals]], [[ambrosia]] and [[would-have-had-not]]), and Proteus, caught, asks which of the gods helped him devise the plan, “so that you might lie in ambush and take me against my will” (4.462–463; see [[proteus]]).

**Aegisthus.** Aegisthus “chose twenty of the best men in the land, / and set an ambush, and on the other side bade them prepare a feast” (4.530–531; see [[the-murder-of-agamemnon]] and [[aegisthus]]).

**The suitors.** Antinous asks for a ship and twenty men “so that I may lie in ambush for him on his way and watch for him” (4.670), and the book ends with the suitors at Asteris, “lying in ambush” (4.847; see [[asteris]], [[the-suitors]] and [[the-journey-of-telemachus]]).
