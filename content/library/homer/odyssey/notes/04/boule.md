βουλή is the plan a man or a god devises. Menelaus has learned the plans and thoughts of many heroes, but never saw such a man as Odysseus (4.267–270; see [[noos]]); Proteus, caught, assumes that a god devised the ambush with him (4.462; see [[a-god-is-hard-for-a-mortal-to-overpower]]). The verb gives the gods' will: a god brought Helen to the Horse who “meant to hold out glory to the Trojans” (4.275; see [[daimon]]), and the gods held Menelaus in Egypt because they “always wished their commands to be remembered” (4.353; see [[reciprocity-with-the-gods]]). In Ithaca the herald Medon hears the suitors' βουλάς from outside the courtyard, “while they inside were weaving their plan” (4.677–678; see [[metis]] and [[medon-the-herald]]).
