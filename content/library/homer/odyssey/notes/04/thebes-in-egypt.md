---
summary: Helen's silver basket and Menelaus' bathtubs, tripods and gold were the gifts of Polybus and his wife Alcandre, who lived in Thebes in Egypt, “where the most treasures lie stored in the houses” (4.125–132).
---

When Helen comes into the hall, her handmaid Phylo brings the silver basket “which had been given her / by Alcandre, the wife of Polybus, who lived in Thebes / in Egypt, where the most treasures lie stored in the houses” (4.125–127; see [[phylo]], [[alcandre]] and [[polybus-of-thebes]]). The last verse is the Iliad's, from Achilles' refusal of Agamemnon's gifts (*Iliad* 9.382), and the city is the one he calls Θῆβαι Αἰγύπτιαι (*Iliad* 9.381; Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 4.126). Polybus gave Menelaus two silver bathtubs, two tripods and ten talents of gold, and his wife gave Helen a golden distaff and the silver basket on wheels, its rims finished with gold (4.128–132; see [[gold-and-silver]] and [[guest-gifts]]). They are the fruit of the wanderings in which Menelaus gathered his wealth among the Egyptians (4.81–93; see [[the-wanderings-of-menelaus]] and [[egypt]]); Strabo supposed that he had gone up to Thebes and from there to the borders of Ethiopia, as a guest of kings (*Geography* 1.2.32; see [[ethiopians]]). The city is not named again in the poem.
