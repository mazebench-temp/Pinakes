---
summary: On Pharos Menelaus' heart “dwindles” (4.374, 4.467) and “was broken” at Proteus' answers (4.481, 4.538); Penelope's “knees and dear heart were loosened” at Medon's news (4.703), the phantom finds her “grieving at heart” (4.804), and her heart is “warmed” by the dream (4.840).
---

The ἦτορ is the heart that holds or fails. Eidothea tells Menelaus that the heart of his companions dwindles, and he says the same of his own (4.374, 4.467); “my dear heart was broken” when Proteus sent him back to Egypt and when he heard of his brother's death (4.481, 4.538; see [[achos]]). In Ithaca Penelope's knees and dear heart are loosened where she stands (4.703, as *Iliad* 21.114; see [[gounata]]), the phantom asks, “Are you asleep, Penelope, grieving at heart?” (4.804), and when she wakes, “her dear heart was warmed, / so clear was the dream” (4.840–841; see [[onar]]).
