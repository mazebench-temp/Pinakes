**The companions at Pylos.** Telemachus asks Menelaus not to keep him long: “But already my companions are growing weary / in sacred Pylos; and you keep me here a long time” (4.598–599; see [[telemachus]] and [[the-journey-of-telemachus]]). The crew stayed with the ship at Pylos while he drove on to Sparta with Peisistratus (3.360–365, 3.481–486; see [[peisistratus]]). For ἠγαθέῃ, “sacred”, Rhianus read a word for “sandy”, as the scholia on 4.702 report (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, critical note on 4.599; see [[egatheos]] and [[sandy]]); both epithets are in the book, “sacred Pylos” at 4.599 and 4.702, “sandy Pylos” at 4.633.

**The news in Ithaca.** The suitors learn of the voyage from Noemon, who asks when Telemachus “will come back from sandy Pylos”, since he needs his ship (4.632–637; see [[noemon-son-of-phronius]] and [[elis]]). They are amazed, “for they did not think / that he had gone to Neleian Pylos, but that he was somewhere there / on the farm” (4.638–640; see [[the-suitors]]): Νηλήιον, “of Neleus”, names the city after Nestor's father, as in the Iliad's Πύλον Νηλήϊον (*Iliad* 11.682; see [[neleus]] and [[nestor]]). Noemon adds that he saw Mentor go on board “for Pylos”, and yet saw him in Ithaca the next morning (4.655–656; see [[mentor]]). The herald Medon tells Penelope that her son has gone “to sacred Pylos and to shining Lacedaemon”, perhaps roused by a god, “to learn / of his father's homecoming, or what fate he has met” (4.702, 4.712–714; see [[medon-the-herald]], [[penelope]] and [[lacedaemon]]). When the suitors speak of Pylos, Telemachus has already left it: the scenes in Ithaca fall on the day of his drive from Pylos to Pherae (see the translation's note on 4.655–656).
