---
summary: Helen sees in Telemachus “the son of great-hearted Odysseus” (4.143), and the phantom comes in the likeness of Iphthime, “daughter of great-hearted Icarius” (4.797).
---

For μεγαλήτορος at 4.143 some read ταλασίφρονος, “stout-hearted”, which Bekker adopted in his second edition (Merry's critical note on 4.143; [[stout-hearted]]). Penelope's father has the epithet as the phantom takes his other daughter's form (4.795–797; [[icarius]], [[iphthime]]).
