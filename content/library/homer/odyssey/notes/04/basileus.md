The strangers marvel at the house “of the king whom Zeus had nurtured” (4.44), and the banqueters come to “the house of the divine king” (4.621; see [[house]]). Menelaus sees in his guests the lineage of “kings whom Zeus has nurtured, / scepter-bearing kings” (4.63–64; see [[kings]] and [[genee]]), and his mixing bowl was the gift of Phaedimus, king of the Sidonians (4.617–618; see [[phaedimus]]). Penelope speaks of “the way of divine kings” (4.691; see [[kingship-and-authority]]). She herself is βασίλεια, “queen”: so Medon addresses her (4.697), and so the suitors call her, “the much-wooed queen” (4.770; see [[much-wooed]] and [[penelope]]).
