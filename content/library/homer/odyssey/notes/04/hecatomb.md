---
summary: The gods held Menelaus on Pharos because he had not offered “perfect hecatombs” (4.352); Proteus sends him back to the river to offer “holy hecatombs” (4.478), and he does, ending the gods' anger (4.581–583).
---

In Egypt “the gods / still held me back, since I had not offered them perfect hecatombs” (4.351–352; see [[the-wanderings-of-menelaus]] and [[reciprocity-with-the-gods]]). Proteus tells him that he will not reach home until he goes back to the river Aegyptus “and offered holy hecatombs / to the immortal gods, who hold the wide sky” (4.478–479; see [[aegyptus-river]]), and Menelaus “offered perfect hecatombs. / And when I had ended the anger of the gods who live forever” (4.582–583; see [[cholos]]) heaped up a tomb for his brother (4.584; see [[tomb]]). τεληέσσας is not "perfect" in the sense of unblemished victims, Merry argues, but "effective", sacrifices that win their accomplishment (τέλος) from the gods (*Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 4.352).
