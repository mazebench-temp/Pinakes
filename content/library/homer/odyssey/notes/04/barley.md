---
summary: White barley is mixed with the spelt thrown before the guests' horses at Sparta (4.41), and broad-eared white barley grows with wheat and spelt on Menelaus' plain (4.604).
---

**Fodder.** At Menelaus' door the attendants feed the strangers' horses spelt, ἀνὰ δὲ κρῖ λευκὸν ἔμιξαν, “and mixed white barley with it” (4.41; see [[spelt]] and [[horses]]), the two grains of the Iliad's horses at their fodder (*Iliad* 5.196, 8.564).

**Broad-eared barley.** On Menelaus' wide plain there are lotus and galingale, “and wheat and spelt and broad-eared white barley” (4.604; see [[wheat]], [[lotus]] and [[galingale]]). εὐρυφυές, “broad-eared”, is found only here. Merry explains that the grains of barley are not set so close round the central stem as those of wheat, and that Virgil's *grandia hordea*, which commentators compare, is only rhetorical (*Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 4.604; Virgil, *Eclogues* 5.36). Most editors read τʼ ἠδʼ εὐρυφυές here. Bekker admits the text's τε ἰδʼ, supposing that ἰδέ once began with a digamma; Merry doubts the digamma but allows the hiatus as a regular one (on 4.604). The plain of grain and fodder is the pasture that Ithaca lacks (4.605–608; see [[ithaca]]).
