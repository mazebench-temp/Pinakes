---
summary: Penelope's prayer to Athena in the order of the type-scene: on Eurycleia's counsel she bathes and puts on clean clothes, goes up with her handmaids, puts barley grains in a basket, calls on the goddess, recalls Odysseus' offerings, asks her to save her son, and raises the cry; “and the goddess heard her prayer” (4.750–767).
refs: [4.750-753, 4.759-767]
passages:
  - 4.759-767 | Penelope prays to Athena
---

The claim is her husband's sacrifices (4.763–764; [[of-many-wiles]]), and the request opens with the words of Telemachus' plea to Menelaus, τῶν νῦν μοι μνῆσαι, “remember these things now for me” (4.765, as 4.331; [[hear-me]], [[atrytone]], [[barley-grains]]). Merry compares the cry, ὀλόλυξε, with the women's cry at Nestor's sacrifice (3.450) and with the Trojan women's prayer to Athena in the Iliad (*Iliad* 6.301; *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 4.767). Menelaus' own prayers on Pharos are told only as he goes, “imploring the gods earnestly” (4.433).
