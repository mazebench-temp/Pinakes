---
summary: Helen recognizes Telemachus as the son of the man who left him newborn, “when for my sake, dog-faced as I am, you Achaeans / came to Troy, bent on bold war” (4.145–146).
---

The verse-end is Menelaus' in the Iliad, when he lies awake for fear for the Argives, “who for his sake had come over the wide waters / to Troy, bent on bold war” (*Iliad* 10.27–28). Here the same words are Helen's, of the war fought for her ([[dog-face]], [[helen]]).
