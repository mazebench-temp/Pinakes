---
summary: Penelope's prayer reminds Athena of the offerings of “Odysseus of many wiles” (4.763).
---

πολύμητις Ὀδυσσεύς names the husband whose sacrifices are the claim of the prayer: “if ever Odysseus of many wiles in his halls / burned to you the fat thigh-pieces of ox or sheep” (4.763–764; [[prayer-type-scene]], [[odysseus]]). Merry compares the prayer of Nisus in Virgil, “if ever my father Hyrtacus brought gifts for me to your altars” (*Aeneid* 9.404–405, quoted by Merry on 4.763).
