summary: In the Horse all the sons of the Achaeans are silent when Helen calls them in their wives' voices, but Anticlus, and Odysseus presses his hands upon his mouth (4.285–288); in Ithaca Antinous bids the suitors carry out their plan “in silence” (4.776), and speechlessness takes Penelope at the news (4.704).
# ἀμφασίη (4.704), "speechlessness", as the Iliad Book 17 note claims it.
grc: [σιγῇ, ἀμφασίη]
---

**Silent in the Horse.** Helen walks three times around the hollow ambush and calls the best of the Danaans by name; Menelaus and Diomedes are eager to answer, but Odysseus holds them back, “Then all the other sons of the Achaeans were silent, / and Anticlus alone wanted to answer you / with words” (4.277–287). Odysseus presses his hands upon his mouth until Athena leads her away, “and saved all the Achaeans” (4.287–289; see [[sinon-and-the-wooden-horse]] and [[anticlus]]).

**The suitors' secret.** When the suitors learn that Telemachus has sailed, Antinous warns them against overbearing talk, lest someone report it inside: “But come, let us rise in silence and accomplish / the plan that has pleased all of us in our minds” (4.774–777; see [[antinous]] and [[truth-and-deception]]). But Medon has already heard their counsels from outside the courtyard (4.677–678; see [[medon-the-herald]]).

**Speechless.** At Medon's news “for a long time speechlessness took” Penelope, and her strong voice was checked (4.704–705; see [[penelope]]).
