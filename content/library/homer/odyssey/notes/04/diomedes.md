# Τυδεΐδης (4.280) is claimed by the entry. Unnamed: the dual of "The two of us were both eager" and "eager though

“But I and the son of Tydeus and brilliant Odysseus / were sitting in the midst and heard how you shouted” (4.280–281; see [[sinon-and-the-wooden-horse]] and [[son-of-tydeus]]). The two who are eager to answer are told in the dual, νῶι μὲν ἀμφοτέρω μενεήναμεν ὁρμηθέντε, “The two of us were both eager to rise up”, and Odysseus checks them ἱεμένω περ, “eager though we were” (4.282–284; see [[menelaus]] and [[odysseus]]). Bekker, with the Harleian and other good manuscripts, reads the plural ὁρμηθέντες (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, critical note on 4.282). In the Iliad Diomedes and Odysseus go together on the night raid (*Iliad* 10; see [[odysseus-and-diomedes]]); here the cooler head is Odysseus', who keeps both kings silent until Athena leads Helen away (4.284–289). In the third book Nestor told how Diomedes came home on the fourth day from Troy (3.180–182).
