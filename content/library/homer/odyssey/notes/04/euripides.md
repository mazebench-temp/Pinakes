---
summary: Euripides' Helen sets the Egypt of this book on the stage. Proteus is a mortal king who lived on Pharos, his daughter Eido has become the prophetess Theonoe, Menelaus arrives shipwrecked after seven years of wandering, and the Dioscuri promise him the island of the blessed, as Proteus promises him the Elysian plain.
---

- Proteus, “dwelling on the island of Pharos, king of Egypt”, and his daughter, called Eido as a child and Theonoe when grown (*Helen* 1–15; on 4.354–355, 4.364–366, 4.384–387); Menelaus' seven years of wandering on the sea, the coasts of Libya and the shipwreck (*Helen* 400–436, 773–776; on 4.81–85, 4.351–352); Theonoe's silence (*Helen* 865–1029); and the island of the blessed promised to “the wanderer Menelaus” (*Helen* 1676–1679; on 4.561–569): [[euripides-helen]], [[proteus]], [[eidothea]] and [[elysium-and-the-isles-of-the-blessed]].
- Helen come “from the house of Proteus”, leaving Egypt, to bury Clytemnestra with Menelaus, newly landed at Nauplia (*Electra* 1278–1281): [[helen-in-egypt]].
