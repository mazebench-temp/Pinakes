“and Phylo brought a silver basket, which had been given her / by Alcandre, the wife of Polybus, who lived in Thebes / in Egypt” (4.125–127; see [[phylo]] and [[polybus-of-thebes]]). After her husband's gifts to Menelaus come hers to Helen: “and apart from these his wife gave Helen beautiful gifts: / she granted her a golden distaff and a basket on wheels / of silver, and its rims were finished with gold” (4.130–132; see [[helen]] and [[gold-and-silver]]). The gifts are the queen's own work things, and Helen comes into the hall “like Artemis of the golden distaff” (4.122; see [[artemis]]): Merry, from the company of epithets the word keeps in the Iliad, would render it “with golden arrows” (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 4.122; see the translation's note on 4.122). The other woman of Egypt who gave Helen a gift is Polydamna, the wife of Thon, from whom she had her drug (4.227–230; see [[polydamna]]).
