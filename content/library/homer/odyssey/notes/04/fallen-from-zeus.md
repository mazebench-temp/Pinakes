---
summary: The river Aegyptus is “the river fallen from Zeus,” in Proteus' command and in Menelaus' return to it (4.477, 4.581).
---

Αἰγύπτοιο, διιπετέος ποταμοῖο, the verse-end of the Iliad's rivers given to the Nile, which Homer calls Aegyptus. Merry reports that Eustathius explains the epithet as “rain-fed”, the best sense for the Nile, which is filled by the heavy summer rains in Ethiopia, “as Aristotle and Eudoxus say” (*Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 4.477; see the translation's note on 4.477, 4.581; [[egypt]]).
