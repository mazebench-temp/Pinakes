---
summary: Telemachus imagines “the court of Olympian Zeus” (4.74); Menelaus names “far-seeing Olympian Zeus” (4.173); and Penelope speaks of “the Olympian” alone, who has given her sorrows beyond all women (4.722).
---

Telemachus' whisper compares Menelaus' house to Zeus' own, and Menelaus, overhearing, corrects him: “truly no mortal could rival Zeus; / for his house and his possessions are immortal” (4.74–79; [[house]], [[immortals]]). Penelope's Ὀλύμπιος without a name begins her lament (4.722; [[zeus]]).
