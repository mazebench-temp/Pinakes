summary: Menelaus would have made Odysseus welcome above all the Argives, and they would have lived side by side, “as we loved and delighted in each other”, until death (4.169–180); Eteoneus asks whether to send the strangers on to someone “who will make them welcome” (4.29). The “dear heart” breaks, is loosened and is warmed (4.481, 4.703, 4.840), and Penelope calls her women “friends” (4.722).
grc: [φιλήσῃ, φιλησέμεν, φιλέοντέ, φιλοίη, φίλους, φίλαι]
en: [welcome, loved, friends]

**To make welcome.** φιλέω of a guest is “make welcome” (see the translation's table of key words). Eteoneus asks whether to unyoke the strangers' horses or send them on “to come to some other man, who will make them welcome” (4.29; see [[xenia]] and [[eteoneus]]), and Menelaus says that he would have welcomed Odysseus above all the Argives and given him a city near his own, so that nothing would have parted them, “as we loved and delighted in each other”, until the black cloud of death enfolded them (4.169–180; see [[odysseus]] and [[kingship-and-authority]]).

**Dear ones.** Proteus tells Menelaus that it is not his fate to see his friends (φίλους) and reach his home until he has made the sacrifices (4.475–476), and Penelope opens her lament, κλῦτε, φίλαι, “Hear me, friends” (4.722; see [[handmaids]]). The way of divine kings is to hate one man and to love another (φιλοίη, 4.692; see [[themis]]).
