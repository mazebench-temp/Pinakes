---
summary: Penelope prays Athena to “ward off the suitors, wickedly overweening” (4.766), and at once “one of the overweening young men” mocks her preparations for a marriage (4.769–771).
grc: [ὑπερηνορέοντας]
---

The prayer and the jeer stand side by side. As Penelope raises the cry (4.766–767; [[prayer-type-scene]]), the suitors raise a din in the halls, and the anonymous voice of one of them, in the verse of 2.324, takes her for a bride preparing her wedding, not knowing that she prays for her son (4.768–771; [[tis-speech]], [[the-suitors]]).
