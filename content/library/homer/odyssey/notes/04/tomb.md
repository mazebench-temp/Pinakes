---
summary: When he has appeased the gods in Egypt, Menelaus “heaped up a tomb for Agamemnon, so that his fame might be unquenchable” (4.584).
---

χεῦʼ Ἀγαμέμνονι τύμβον, ἵνʼ ἄσβεστον κλέος εἴη: “I heaped up a tomb for Agamemnon, so that his fame might be unquenchable” (4.584; see [[agamemnon]], [[the-murder-of-agamemnon]], [[kleos]] and [[honoring-the-dead]]). Agamemnon lay at Mycenae; Menelaus raises his tomb in Egypt, after the hecatombs that ended the gods' anger (4.581–583; see [[hecatomb]]). A scholium says that he made a cenotaph and wrote on a stone Agamemnon's name and how and where he died; Merry answers that this was not an age of inscriptions and that a simple mound sufficed (*Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 4.584, comparing *Iliad* 23.255). The translation keeps τύμβος, "tomb", apart from σῆμα, "mound" (see the general article).
