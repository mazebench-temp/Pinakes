---
summary: Eumelus is named as the husband of Penelope's sister Iphthime, “who had his home in Pherae”, when Athena gives the phantom her likeness (4.797–798).
---

“Iphthime, daughter of great-hearted Icarius, / whom Eumelus had married, who had his home in Pherae” (4.797–798; see [[iphthime]] and [[pherae]]). The poem does not give his father, but the name and the city are those of the Iliad's Eumelus, the son of Admetus, who led the men of Pherae by the lake of Boebeis to Troy (*Iliad* 2.711–715), and Merry takes him for the same man (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 4.798). The marriage makes him the brother-in-law of Odysseus, and puts Penelope's sister far from Ithaca: “since you live very far away”, Penelope tells the phantom (4.811; see [[penelope]]).
