---
summary: Two deliberations in the book end without a choice: Menelaus' whether to let Telemachus speak of his father, which Helen's entrance cuts short (4.116–122), and Penelope's whether her son will escape death, which ends in sleep and a dream (4.787–794).
passages:
  - 4.116-122 | Menelaus ponders, and Helen comes in
  - 4.787-794 | Penelope ponders, and sleep comes
---

Each has the two-way form, ἠέ … ἦ, “whether … or” (4.118–119; ἤ … ἦ, 4.789–790), and each is broken off from outside, as Achilles' deliberation is broken off by Athena in *Iliad* 1 (*Iliad* 1.188–194; [[pondering-two-ways]]).
