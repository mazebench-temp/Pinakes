# Unnamed: "the ready attendant of glorious Menelaus" (4.217).
refs: [4.217]
---

Menelaus ends the weeping for the lost: “So let us leave off the weeping that came before, / and let us remember supper again, and let them pour water / over our hands” (4.212–214). The subject of χευάντων, “let them pour”, is left indefinite, as often (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 4.214), and the poem names the man who does it: “So he spoke, and Asphalion poured water over their hands, / the ready attendant of glorious Menelaus” (4.216–217; see [[menelaus]], [[water-for-the-hands]] and [[ready-attendants]]). The second verse is the one that described Eteoneus at the door (4.23; see [[eteoneus]]). The meal goes on in the formula of the first, “And they put out their hands to the good things lying ready before them” (4.218 = 4.67), and then Helen casts her drug into the wine (4.219–221; see [[helen]]).
