---
summary: Telemachus at the sight of Menelaus' house, “awe holds me as I look at them” (4.75), and Helen at the likeness of the young stranger to Odysseus, “awe holds me as I look” (4.142).
---

**The house.** Telemachus whispers to Peisistratus of the flash of bronze, gold, amber, silver and ivory in the echoing halls: “Surely such is the court of Olympian Zeus within, / so many are these things, untold; awe holds me as I look at them” (4.71–75; see [[house]] and [[gold-and-silver]]). Menelaus overhears and corrects him: no mortal could rival Zeus, whose house and possessions are immortal (4.78–79). Before the meal they had marveled at the gleam of the house, “as of the sun or of the moon” (4.43–46; see [[thambos]]).

**The likeness.** Helen says it of a person, as Nestor did at Pylos: she has never seen anyone so like another, man or woman, “awe holds me as I look, / as this man is like the son of great-hearted Odysseus” (4.141–143; see [[fathers-and-sons]] and [[awe-holds-me]]). On Nestor's words Merry takes the feeling as “amazement” at a resemblance (*Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 3.123).
