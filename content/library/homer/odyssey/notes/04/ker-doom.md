summary: The men in the Horse were “bringing slaughter and doom to the Trojans” (4.273); Ajax, son of Oileus, would have escaped his doom but for his boast (4.502), and Agamemnon “escaped the dooms” at sea only to be killed at home (4.512).
---

In the Horse sat all the best of the Argives, Τρώεσσι φόνον καὶ κῆρα φέροντες, “bringing slaughter and doom to the Trojans” (4.272–273; see [[sinon-and-the-wooden-horse]]). In Proteus' tale of the returns, Ajax “would have escaped his doom, hated though he was by Athena”, had he not boasted (4.502; see [[the-death-of-ajax-son-of-oileus]]), and Agamemnon “escaped the dooms and got away from them / in his hollow ships”, for Hera saved him, only to die at Aegisthus' feast (4.512–537; see [[the-murder-of-agamemnon]]).
