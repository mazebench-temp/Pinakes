# χαλκοῦ, the metal of the house (4.72), first met here in the Odyssey.
grc: [χαλκοῦ]
---

**The house.** Telemachus whispers to Peisistratus to look at “the flash of bronze throughout the echoing halls, / and of gold and amber and silver and ivory” (4.72–73; see [[house]], [[gold-and-silver]], [[amber]] and [[ivory]]). The phrase χαλκοῦ τε στεροπήν, “the flash of bronze”, is the Iliad's, where Zeus looks down on the battle, “the flash of bronze, and the slayers and the slain” (*Iliad* 11.83).

**The weapon.** Helen's drug would keep a man from tears “not even if before his face they cut down his brother or his dear son / with the bronze” (4.225–226; see [[nepenthe]]). In Troy Odysseus, after his spying, killed “many Trojans with the long-edged bronze” (4.257; see [[long-edged]] and [[odysseus-enters-troy-as-a-beggar]]). In Ithaca the suitors “are eager to kill Telemachus with the sharp bronze” (4.700; see [[sharp]]), and Penelope tells Eurycleia, “Dear bride, kill me with the pitiless bronze” (4.743; see [[pitiless-bronze]] and [[eurycleia]]). The leaders of the “bronze-armored Achaeans” who died on the way home were two (4.496; see [[bronze-armored]]).
