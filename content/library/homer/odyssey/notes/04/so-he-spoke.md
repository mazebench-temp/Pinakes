summary: The book's speeches close with the formula some thirty times. Menelaus' tale has its first-person form, “So I spoke” (4.382–554); “So they spoke such words to one another” closes the day at Sparta before the poem returns to Ithaca (4.620); and “So saying” sends the phantom out past the bolt (4.838).
grc: ["ὣς φάθʼ", "ὣς ἐφάμην", "ὣς οἱ μὲν τοιαῦτα πρὸς ἀλλήλους ἀγόρευον", "ὣς εἰπὸν"]
en: [So I spoke, So they spoke such words to one another]
---

ὣς οἱ μὲν τοιαῦτα πρὸς ἀλλήλους ἀγόρευον, the Iliad's verse for closing one scene and turning to another (*Iliad* 5.274, 8.212, 18.368), marks the change from Sparta to Ithaca (4.620–625; see the translation's note on 4.620–624). The narrator's ὣς ἔφατʼ and ὣς φάτο turn each speech to its effect: “So he spoke, and in them all he roused the longing for lament” (4.183; [[the-longing-for-lament]]), “So he spoke, and her knees and dear heart were loosened where she was” (4.703; [[loosened-his-limbs]]). The phantom's ὣς εἰπόν has the neuter participle of the εἴδωλον (4.838; [[eidolon]]).
