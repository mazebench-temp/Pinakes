summary: Eteoneus and Asphalion are each “the ready attendant of glorious Menelaus” (4.23, 4.217), and Eteoneus calls “the others, / the ready attendants,” to unyoke the strangers' horses (4.37–38).

en: [ready attendant]

The whole verse ὀτρηρὸς θεράπων Μενελάου κυδαλίμοιο introduces each of the two men who serve the guests: Eteoneus at the door (4.22–23; [[eteoneus]], [[powerful]]), and Asphalion, who pours the water over their hands at the second meal (4.216–217; [[asphalion]]). In Ithaca the same word describes those who wait on the suitors, “heralds and ready attendants” (1.109), and Penelope uses its adverb when she sends for help: “let someone readily call old Dolius” (ὀτρηρῶς, 4.735).
