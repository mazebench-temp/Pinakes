summary: Odysseus enters Troy disguised as a beggar and brings back “much intelligence” (4.244–258); in Proteus' tale Aegisthus' lookout watches a whole year for Agamemnon's coming, hired for two talents of gold (4.524–528).

**The spy in Troy.** Helen's tale is of a spying: Odysseus marred himself with blows, put rags on, and slipped into the city like a beggar; she alone knew him, and after killing many Trojans “he went back among the Argives, and brought back much intelligence” (4.244–258; see [[odysseus-enters-troy-as-a-beggar]] and [[truth-and-deception]]). Merry takes φρόνιν ἤγαγε as intelligence brought back like booty (*Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 4.258), and the plan he told her as the plan of the Horse (on 4.256).

**The lookout.** When Agamemnon lands, “a lookout saw him from his lookout, whom scheming Aegisthus / had brought and posted there, and promised him as wages / two talents of gold; and he had kept watch for a year, / lest he should pass by unseen” (4.524–527; see [[misthos]] and [[the-murder-of-agamemnon]]). He runs to bring the news to Aegisthus, who sets the ambush (4.528–531; see [[ambush]]).
