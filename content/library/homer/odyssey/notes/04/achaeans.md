---
summary: In Sparta the Achaeans are the army of the war, remembered by Menelaus, Helen and Telemachus: the men who came to Troy for Helen's sake and suffered there, whose plan Odysseus told her, whom he saved in the wooden horse, and of whose homecoming Menelaus asks Proteus (4.106–496). In Ithaca, in the last verse of the book, the Achaeans are the suitors lying in ambush for Telemachus (4.847).
---

**The army of the war.** No one of the Achaeans toiled as much as Odysseus, Menelaus says (4.106; see [[odysseus]]). Helen names the war as hers: “when for my sake, dog-faced as I am, you Achaeans / came to Troy, bent on bold war” (4.145–146; see [[helen]]). “In the land of the Trojans, where you Achaeans suffered woes”, is her phrase and Telemachus' (4.243, 4.330), as it was Telemachus' to Nestor (3.100; see [[trojans]]). In her story Odysseus was “nothing of the kind by the ships of the Achaeans”, a beggar only in disguise, and he told her “the whole plan of the Achaeans” (4.248, 4.256; see [[odysseus-enters-troy-as-a-beggar]]). In Menelaus' story the others in the horse, “the sons of the Achaeans”, were silent, and Odysseus “saved all the Achaeans” when he stopped Anticlus' mouth (4.285, 4.288; see [[sons-of-the-achaeans]] and [[sinon-and-the-wooden-horse]]); on Lesbos “all the Achaeans rejoiced” when he threw Philomeleides (4.344; see [[odysseus-wrestles-philomeleides]]). Menelaus asks Proteus whether all the Achaeans came home unharmed, and hears that “of the leaders of the bronze-armored Achaeans only two” perished on the way (4.487, 4.496; see [[the-returns-of-the-achaeans]] and [[bronze-armored]]).

**The suitors.** The book ends with the ambush: “there the Achaeans waited for him, lying in ambush” (4.847; see [[the-suitors]], [[asteris]] and [[ambush]]). The Achaeans of Ithaca are its nobles and its people, as in the first two books, where Telemachus says that the Achaeans, and most of all the suitors, are delaying his voyage, and Antinous promises that the Achaeans will find him a ship (2.265–266, 2.306–307; see [[ithaca]]).
