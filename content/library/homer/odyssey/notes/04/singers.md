---
summary: At Menelaus' wedding feast a divine singer sings to the lyre while two tumblers whirl in the midst (4.17–19), in the verses of the shield of Achilles.
---

“And among them a divine singer sang / to the lyre; and two tumblers among them, / as he led the song and dance, whirled in the midst” (4.17–19, = *Iliad* 18.604–606; see [[the-wedding-at-sparta]], [[tumblers]], [[lyre]] and [[dance]]). The singular ἐξάρχοντος makes the singer lead the dance, where the shield has the plural, of the tumblers; and Athenaeus, as Merry reports him, says that the singer and the tumblers were brought into the passage from the shield (see the translation's notes on 4.3–19 and 4.19, and [[the-singer-on-the-shield]]).
