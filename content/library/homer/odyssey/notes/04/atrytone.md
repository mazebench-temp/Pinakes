---
summary: Penelope's prayer: “Hear me, child of aegis-bearing Zeus, Atrytone” (4.762).
---

The whole verse of address is the Iliad's, in Diomedes' prayer to Athena (*Iliad* 5.115), and with ὢ πόποι in place of κλῦθί μευ it opens three more speeches there (*Iliad* 2.157, 5.714, 21.420). The Odyssey uses it twice, in Penelope's prayer here and in Odysseus' prayer in Book 6 (6.324; [[hear-me]], [[child-of-aegis-bearing-zeus]]). Merry says that Penelope supplicates Athena in the character in which she generally appears in the Iliad, as the war-goddess; he reports that the epithet is generally taken, with Eustathius, as a lengthened form of ἄτρυτος, “unwearied”, that Bergk refers it to the root of Τριτογένεια, and that others connect it with ὀτρύνω, “rouse”, which would make it like λαοσσόος, “rouser of hosts” (*Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 4.762; [[tritogeneia]], [[prayer-type-scene]], [[athena]]).
