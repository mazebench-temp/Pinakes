summary: “Strong Megapenthes” (4.11); Odysseus, “the strong man,” in the verse with which Helen and Menelaus begin their tales of him (4.242, 4.271), and his “strong hands” on the mouth of Anticlus (4.288); Helen's “mighty oath” (4.253); and the “strong lion” of Menelaus' simile (4.335).
grc: [καρτερὸς, καρτερὸν, κρατεροῖο, κρατερῇσι]
---

The two tales of Odysseus at Troy open with the same verse, Helen's “but what a thing this was that the strong man did and endured” (4.242) and Menelaus' answer, “What a thing this too was that the strong man did and endured” (4.271): καρτερός, the other form of κρατερός, sums up the man who disfigured himself to spy in Troy and who held the others silent in the Horse (4.244–289; [[odysseus]], [[odysseus-enters-troy-as-a-beggar]], [[sinon-and-the-wooden-horse]]). The strong lion of the simile is Odysseus again, coming home to the suitors in his lair (4.333–340; [[lion]]).
