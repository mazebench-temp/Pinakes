summary: The dawn verse three times, “And when early-born Dawn appeared, the rosy-fingered” (4.306, 4.431, 4.576); and Peisistratus' “there will be early-born / Dawn as well” (4.194–195), when he asks not to weep after supper.
---

ἠὼς … ἠριγένεια is Peisistratus' reason for leaving off lament: there will be time at dawn, and Menelaus agrees that there will be talk at dawn too (4.193–215; [[lament]]). The three whole verses open the morning at Sparta and two mornings in Menelaus' tale ([[dawn-line]], [[rosy-fingered]]).
