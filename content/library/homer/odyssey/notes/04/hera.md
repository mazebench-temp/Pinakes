---
summary: In Proteus' account of the returns Hera saves Agamemnon from the sea on his voyage home, only for him to be murdered at Aegisthus' feast.
---

**Queen Hera saved him.** Ajax perished at sea, “But your brother escaped the dooms and got away from them / in his hollow ships; for queen Hera saved him” (4.512–513; see [[agamemnon]] and [[the-murder-of-agamemnon]]). Merry explains σάωσε as saving him from the storm that Athena raised against the Achaeans on their way home (*Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 4.513, comparing 5.108–109; see [[athena]] and [[the-returns-of-the-achaeans]]). πότνια Ἥρη, “queen Hera”, is her Iliad title (see [[queenly]]), and she is the Achaeans' friend there: Argos, Sparta and Mycenae are the three cities dearest to her (*Iliad* 4.51–52), and it is “queen Hera” who puts it in Agamemnon's mind to rally the Achaeans when Hector is at the ships (*Iliad* 8.218–219). Her rescue is the last good fortune of Agamemnon's homecoming. The storm off Malea carries him to the land where Aegisthus lives, a lookout sees him, and he is killed at the feast “as one kills an ox at the manger” (4.514–535; see [[malea]] and [[aegisthus]]). The gods who save a man at sea cannot save him at home: Athena said so in Book 3, that death is common to all, and not even the gods can ward it off from a man they love (3.236–238; see [[not-even-the-gods-can-ward-off-death]]).
