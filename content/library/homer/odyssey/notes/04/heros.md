summary: Telemachus is “the hero Telemachus” at the door and in the porch of Menelaus' house and in Menelaus' greeting (4.21, 4.303, 4.312); Eidothea calls Menelaus “hero” (4.423), Phaedimus is “the hero” (4.617), and Menelaus has known “the plan and the thought of many / heroes” (4.267–268).
---

ἥρως is the title of a man of standing. It is given to the young Telemachus as he arrives (4.21, 4.303) and in Menelaus' first question, “What need has brought you here, hero Telemachus” (4.312; see [[telemachus]]); to Menelaus by the goddess Eidothea (4.423); and to the Sidonian king Phaedimus, “The hero Phaedimus gave it to me” (4.617; see [[phaedimus]]). The heroes whose plans Menelaus has learned are the leaders at Troy (4.267–268; see [[boule]]).
