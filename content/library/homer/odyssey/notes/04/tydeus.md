# Τυδεΐδης (4.280), the patronymic that counts for the father too; the Iliad notes claim Τυδεΐδ* only in the Iliad.
grc: [Τυδεΐδης]
---

Menelaus tells Helen how, when she called the chieftains by name in their wives' voices, “I and the son of Tydeus and brilliant Odysseus / were sitting in the midst and heard how you shouted”, and the two of them were eager to answer until Odysseus held them back (4.280–284; see [[diomedes]], [[son-of-tydeus]] and [[sinon-and-the-wooden-horse]]).
