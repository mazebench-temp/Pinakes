---
summary: The slave women go out from the hall with torches to make up the guests' beds beneath the colonnade (4.300).
---

“And the women went out from the hall with torches in their hands, / and spread the beds” (4.300–301; see [[handmaids]], [[bed]] and [[blankets]]). The verse is Achilles' women making the beds for Priam (*Iliad* 24.647), and it returns in Alcinous' house and in Odysseus' hall (7.339, 22.497). La Roche quotes μεγάρου δᾷδας, “torches from the hall”, as a reading with considerable authority (W. W. Merry's critical note on 4.300). In Book 1 it was Eurycleia who carried the blazing torches before Telemachus to his room (1.428–429).
