---
summary: Menelaus is sending his daughter with horses and chariots to the son of Achilles, “to the renowned city of the Myrmidons, over whom he ruled” (4.5–9).
---

“So he was sending her there with horses and chariots to go / to the renowned city of the Myrmidons, over whom he ruled” (4.8–9; see [[hermione-daughter-of-helen]], [[neoptolemus]] and [[the-wedding-at-sparta]]). The bridegroom is “the son of Achilles, breaker of men”, unnamed, to whom Menelaus had promised her at Troy (4.5–7). ἄνασσεν, “ruled”, makes him king in Phthia (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 4.9; see [[phthia]]): the Myrmidons whom Nestor had heard came home safely under him (3.188–189) are now his people in his father's land. The city is not named; προτὶ ἄστυ περικλυτόν, “to the renowned city”, is a phrase the poem uses again (see the translation's table of formulas).
