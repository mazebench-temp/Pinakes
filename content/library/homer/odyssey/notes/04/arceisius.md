---
summary: Eurycleia, bidding Penelope not to trouble old Laertes, does not think the line of the son of Arceisius altogether hateful to the gods: someone will still be left to hold the house (4.754–757).
---

“And do not afflict an afflicted old man; for I do not think / that to the blessed gods the offspring of the son of Arceisius / is altogether hateful; someone will still be left, I think, to hold / the high-roofed house and the rich fields far away” (4.754–757; see [[laertes]], [[eurycleia]] and [[telemachus]]). The old man is Laertes, “the son of Arceisius”, and his offspring is the line of Odysseus, of which Telemachus is the last; the patronymic is the first mention of the grandfather in the poem.
