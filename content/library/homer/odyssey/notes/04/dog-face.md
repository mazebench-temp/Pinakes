ἐμεῖο κυνώπιδος, “for the sake of me, the dog-faced one”: the word she used of herself on the wall at Troy, when she named her husband's brother to Priam (*Iliad* 3.180). In her own house, at the moment of recognition, she blames herself for the war in the same word ([[helen]], [[ate]]). The Odyssey gives it again to Aphrodite, in Hephaestus' complaint (8.319), and to Clytemnestra, in Agamemnon's account of his death (11.424).
