---
summary: Fire is one of the shapes Proteus will try, as his daughter warns, though when he is seized he becomes four beasts, water and a tree, and no fire (4.417–418, 4.456–458); and Antinous' eyes, at the news that Telemachus has sailed, are “like blazing fire” (4.662).
---

**Wondrous fire.** Eidothea tells Menelaus that her father, once seized, “will make his attempt by becoming all the things that upon the earth / creep, and water, and wondrous fire” (4.417–418; see [[eidothea]], [[proteus]] and [[shape-changing]]). θεσπιδαὲς πῦρ, “wondrous fire”, is the Iliad's verse-end for fire at the wall and at the ships, in the river, in a simile and on the pyre of Patroclus (*Iliad* 12.177, 15.597, 20.490, 21.342, 23.216; see [[wondrous]]). Merry notes that πάντα … γιγνόμενος πειρήσεται means that the old man will try to escape by becoming all these things, not that he will try to become them, for there was no doubt of his power to change his shape (*Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 4.417). In Menelaus' telling of the struggle he becomes a lion, a serpent, a leopard and a great boar, then flowing water and a tree with high foliage (4.456–458; see [[trees]]): the water is there, and the fire is not.

**Eyes like fire.** In Ithaca Antinous hears from Noemon that Telemachus has really sailed, and speaks among the suitors “in distress; and his heart, dark on every side, was filled / with mighty fury, and his eyes were like blazing fire” (4.661–662; see [[antinous]] and [[eyes-like-fire]]). The verses are Agamemnon's in the Iliad, when he rises in anger to answer Calchas (*Iliad* 1.103–104).
