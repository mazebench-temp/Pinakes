---
summary: Golden cups are set beside the guests at Sparta (4.58), and Menelaus promises Telemachus a fine goblet “so that you may pour libations to the gods” (4.591–592).
---

**The golden cups.** After the bread and the meat the carver “set golden cups beside them” (4.58 = 1.142; see [[feast-type-scene]] and [[gold-and-silver]]), though most editors since Wolf reject the two verses of the carver here (see the translation's note on 4.57–58).

**The goblet.** Among the gifts he first offers, Menelaus promises “a fine goblet, so that you may pour libations to the gods, / the immortals, remembering me all your days” (4.591–592; see [[libation]] and [[guest-gifts]]). ἄλεισον is the word of the golden goblet that Peisistratus put into Athena's hands at Pylos (3.50–53). When Menelaus gives his gifts in Book 15, he puts a two-handled cup, δέπας ἀμφικύπελλον, in Telemachus' hand with the silver bowl (15.120–123, a plain rendering; see [[mixing-bowl]]).
