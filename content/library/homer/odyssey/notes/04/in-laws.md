summary: Proteus tells Menelaus that the immortals will send him to the Elysian plain, “because you have Helen as your wife, and in their eyes you are the son-in-law of Zeus” (4.569); Eurycleia addresses Penelope as νύμφα φίλη, “Dear bride” (4.743).
grc: [γαμβρὸς]
en: [son-in-law]
---

Menelaus' end is decided by his marriage: as Helen's husband he is γαμβρὸς Διός, “the son-in-law of Zeus”, and σφιν, “in their eyes”, the gods' (4.569; see [[elysium]], [[husbands-and-wives]] and the translation's note on 4.569). The old nurse calls her mistress νύμφα φίλη, “Dear bride” (4.743; see [[eurycleia]] and [[penelope]]), the words with which Iris, in the likeness of Laodice, calls Helen to the wall in the Iliad (*Iliad* 3.130; see [[helen]]). These are the only two places in Homer where the phrase is used.
