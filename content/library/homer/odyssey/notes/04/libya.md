summary: Libya ends the list of Menelaus' wanderings, and it is the only land in the list that he describes: there the lambs are horned at once, the flocks bear young three times a year, and neither master nor shepherd lacks cheese or meat or milk (4.85–89).

The marvel is plenty. Merry takes τελέθουσι as “are”, not “become”, so that the lambs come to birth with their horns grown or sprouting, as Aristotle read the verse, rather than growing them soon after birth, as Herodotus did; the three lambings are of a piece with it, a sign of the land's fertility, and he compares what was told of the Umbrians, whose flocks bore three times a year (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 4.85; *On Marvellous Things Heard* 80, ascribed to Aristotle; see [[the-horned-lambs-of-libya]], [[sheep]] and [[lambs]]). τελεσφόρον εἰς ἐνιαυτόν, “in the full year's round”, is found in Homer only in this phrase, a fixed epithet of the year that brings all things to their completion (Merry on 4.86). Bekker moved 4.86 after 4.89, to give the reason for the unfailing milk (Merry's critical note on 4.86). With Libya the list ends, and Menelaus turns to what happened meanwhile at home: “While I, gathering much substance in those lands, / was wandering, meanwhile another man killed my brother” (4.90–91; see [[the-wanderings-of-menelaus]] and [[the-murder-of-agamemnon]]).
