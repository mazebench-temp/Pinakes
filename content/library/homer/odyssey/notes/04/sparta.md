---
summary: The city is named once in the book: for his son Megapenthes Menelaus is bringing home “the daughter of Alector of Sparta” (4.10).
---

“And for his son he was bringing the daughter of Alector of Sparta” (4.10; see [[alector]], [[megapenthes]] and [[the-wedding-at-sparta]]). Σπάρτηθεν goes with Ἀλέκτορος, not with ἤγετο, for the wedding is held at Sparta: the bride is a Spartan, and ἤγετο, the word for a bridegroom bringing home his wife, is used here of a father getting a wife for his son (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 4.10; see the translation's note on 4.10). Σπάρτηθεν, “from Sparta”, was the young suitors' word when they jeered that Telemachus might bring helpers “even from Sparta” (2.327). Everywhere else the book names Menelaus' land, Lacedaemon, not the city (4.1, 4.313, 4.702; see [[lacedaemon]]), though Telemachus has come to Sparta as Athena bade him, “to Sparta, to fair-haired Menelaus” (1.285; see [[the-journey-of-telemachus]]).
