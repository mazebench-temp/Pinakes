Menelaus smiles at Telemachus' refusal of the horses and promises to change the gift (4.609–612; see [[menelaus]] and [[telemachus]]): “I will give you a well-wrought mixing bowl; of silver / it is all, and its rims are finished with gold, / the work of Hephaestus. The hero Phaedimus gave it to me, / the king of the Sidonians, when his house sheltered me / as I came there on my way home; and I wish to grant it to you” (4.615–619; see [[mixing-bowl]], [[hephaestus]], [[sidonians]] and [[guest-gifts]]). νοστήσαντα is “as I came there on my homeward voyage”, though Merry doubts that the verb carries any notion of return in itself (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 4.619), and ἀμφεκάλυψε, “sheltered”, is the verb used of the city enclosing the wooden horse (8.511, which Merry compares on 4.618). The Sidonians are among the peoples Menelaus says he came to in his eight years of wandering (4.84; see [[the-wanderings-of-menelaus]]). The bowl is one of the gifts of a host, as Alcandre's basket and Polybus' gold were in Egypt (4.125–132; see [[polybus-of-thebes]]), and it passes from host to guest again; Menelaus repeats the five verses when he gives it in Book 15 (15.115–119).
