summary: Menelaus' island of waiting, “in front of Egypt”, a day's sail from the land, with a harbor where ships draw water before they put out to sea. The gods keep him there twenty days without a wind, until Eidothea finds him and shows him how to take her father on its shore (4.354–461).

---

**The island.** Menelaus begins his tale of Egypt with a description of the place:

{{quote:748637f9-d75a-5b28-9a53-77db31f32514}}

ἔπειτα, “Now”, opens a story, or marks a new stage in one (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 4.354); the description of Asteris at the end of the book begins in the same way, ἔστι δέ τις νῆσος, “There is a rocky island” (4.844; see [[asteris]]). Hayman heard in κικλήσκουσι, “they call it”, the hearsay of sailors, but Merry finds the verb used without any such sense (on 4.355). ἤνυσεν, “covers”, is a gnomic aorist, “as great a way off as a ship makes in a whole day” (on 4.356), and the dark water that the crews draw is water from deep places, where the light does not reach it (on 4.359). A day's run makes Pharos far out at sea, and yet the voyage back to Egypt is “a long and grievous way” to Menelaus when Proteus commands it (4.483; Merry on 4.483; see [[egypt]]).

**The twenty days.** The gods kept him there twenty days without a fair wind, the provisions running out, while his companions wandered about the island fishing with curved hooks, “and hunger wore at their bellies” (4.360–369; see [[fish]]). Eidothea found him walking alone and reproached him: “seeing that you are held back so long on the island” (4.373; see [[eidothea]]), the words he repeats to her father (4.466).

**The shore of the ambush.** The island's shore is the scene of the capture. At midday the Old Man comes out of the sea under the West Wind and lies down to sleep among his seals “in the hollow caves” (4.400–406; see [[proteus]] and [[seals]]); Eidothea scoops out beds in the sand for Menelaus and his three companions and covers them with fresh sealskins (4.435–446; see [[shore]]). The camp of the ships is on the same beach, where they sup and sleep “where the sea breaks upon the shore” (4.426–430, 4.573–575).
