---
summary: Wheat grows with spelt and barley, the lotus and galingale on Menelaus' wide plain, the land for horses that Telemachus sets against Ithaca when he declines the gift of horses (4.602–604).
---

**On Menelaus' plain.** Telemachus leaves Menelaus his horses, for Menelaus is lord of a wide plain “in which there is much lotus, and galingale, / and wheat and spelt and broad-eared white barley”, and Ithaca has no wide courses and no meadow (4.602–608; see [[horses]] and [[ithaca]]). πυροί τε ζειαί τε, “wheat and spelt”, are named after the plants of the meadow, the lotus and galingale, and before the barley (see [[lotus]], [[galingale]], [[spelt]] and [[barley]]): the plain bears grain as well as grass, and all of it can feed horses, as Hector's and Diomedes' horses are fed with wheat in the Iliad (*Iliad* 8.185–189, 10.568–569). This is the only wheat of Odyssey 1–4; it comes again with barley in the land of the Cyclopes and in the land of a just king (9.110, 19.112).
