summary: Menelaus is “son of Atreus” in the narrative and in the addresses of Peisistratus, Telemachus, Helen and Proteus (4.51–594), and Ἀτρέος υἱέ in two of the old man's (4.462, 4.543); once the title is his brother's, when Proteus tells of “the companions of the son of Atreus” killed at Aegisthus' feast (4.536).
grc: ["Ἀτρέος υἱέ"]
---

Ἀτρεΐδης is Menelaus' name at Sparta: his guests sit “beside Menelaus, son of Atreus” (4.51), he weeps with them for Odysseus (4.185), and he sleeps “in the inner part of the high house” (4.304; [[high-roofed]]). Peisistratus, Helen and Telemachus begin their speeches with it (4.156, 4.190, 4.235, 4.291, 4.316, 4.594; [[zeus-nurtured]]), and so does Proteus, with both forms (4.462, 4.492, 4.543). At 4.536 the son of Atreus is Agamemnon, whose companions all died at the feast with Aegisthus' men (4.536–537; [[agamemnon]], [[the-murder-of-agamemnon]]). See [[menelaus]] and [[atreus]].
