# Unnamed: "pressed upon his mouth ... and held him so" (4.287-289).
refs: [4.287-289]
---

“Then all the other sons of the Achaeans were silent, / and Anticlus alone wanted to answer you / with words” (4.285–287; see [[sons-of-the-achaeans]] and [[silence]]). Menelaus and Diomedes had been eager to answer too, and Odysseus held them back (4.282–284); Anticlus he silenced by force, “unceasingly, with his strong hands”, and so “saved all the Achaeans” (4.287–288; see [[odysseus]] and [[sinon-and-the-wooden-horse]]). νωλεμέως, “unceasingly”, is the word of the man who holds on whatever happens, as Odysseus clings under the ram in the Cyclops' cave (9.435).

The five verses that tell of him (4.285–289) were in hardly any copy, the scholia say, and Aristarchus rejected them because the poet never mentions Anticlus in the Iliad (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, critical note on 4.285–289; see the translation's note on 4.285–289). Merry notes that they agree ill with what goes before, since Menelaus and Diomedes, not Anticlus alone, had wished to answer, and he reports a conjecture by which Bekker tried to reconcile the two statements, though Bekker did not accept the verses either (on 4.285).
