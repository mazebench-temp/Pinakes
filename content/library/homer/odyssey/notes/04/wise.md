summary: Telemachus' verse of reply three times (4.290, 4.315, 4.593); Menelaus, “wise beyond all mortals” in Nestor's report (4.190), praises Peisistratus for saying “all that a wise man / would say and do” (4.204–206); and the herald Medon is the man “who had wise understanding” (4.696, 4.711).
grc: ["πεπνυμένα βάζεις", "πεπνυμένα εἰδώς"]
en: [wisely]
---

The word passes from father to son in the exchange with Peisistratus: Nestor used to call Menelaus wise (4.190–191), and Menelaus answers that the son speaks wisely because the father is so: τοίου … πατρός resumes πεπνυμένος, “for from such a sire art thou sprung, because also thy words are wise” (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 4.206; [[fathers-and-sons]], [[nestor]], [[peisistratus]]). In Ithaca the epithet is the herald's: Μέδων πεπνυμένα εἰδώς, a verse-end that returns with him at the end of the poem (4.696, 4.711; 22.361, 24.442; [[medon-the-herald]]).
