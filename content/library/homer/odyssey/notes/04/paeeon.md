---
summary: The Egyptians, from whom Helen had her drug against grief, are every one a healer, skilled beyond all other men, “for they are of the stock of Paeëon”.
---

**The stock of Paeëon.** Helen's drug came from Egypt. Polydamna, the wife of Thon, gave it to her, “a woman of Egypt, where the grain-giving earth bears the most / drugs, many good and many grim, growing intermixed; / and every man there is a healer, skilled beyond all / other men; for they are of the stock of Paeëon” (4.228–232; see [[nepenthe]], [[polydamna]] and [[egypt]]). ἰητρός, “healer”, is the word of the Iliad's praise of Machaon, “a healer is a man worth many others” (*Iliad* 11.514; see the translation's key-word row), and Παιήονος … γενέθλης makes the whole people the descendants of the gods' own physician, who in the Iliad heals Hades and Ares on Olympus (see the general article and [[healing]]). Herodotus, who quotes these verses (2.116.4), found Egypt full of doctors, each of them a healer of one disease and no more, of the eyes, of the head, of the teeth or of the belly (Herodotus 2.84; see [[herodotus]]).

**Paeëon or Apollo.** Merry reports from the scholia that after ἕκαστος Aristarchus wrote “since Apollo granted them to heal”, before “for they are of the stock of Paeëon”, and that the scholiast objected, since Paeëon is not Apollo, as Hesiod also bears witness; Lehrs held that the scholium was wrong to give the reading to Aristarchus, and Merry thinks the words may have been his gloss on the verse rather than a reading (*Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, critical note on 4.231; see [[apollo]]). The verses of Hesiod that the scholium quotes are printed among his fragments: “unless Phoebus Apollo should save him from death, / or Paeëon himself, who knows the drugs for all things” (Hesiod, fr. 2 Evelyn-White, from the scholium on 4.232; see [[hesiod]]). In Homer the two are distinct; the paean is sung to Apollo, but the healer of the gods is Paeëon (see [[paean]]).
