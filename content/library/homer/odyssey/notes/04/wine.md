summary: Helen casts her drug into the wine from which the company is drinking and bids them pour it (4.220–233); the banqueters bring “wine that makes men strong” to the king's house (4.622); and Eurycleia tells how she gave Telemachus bread and sweet wine for his voyage (4.746).
# μέθυ, "wine", first met here in the Odyssey (4.746).
grc: [μέθυ]
---

**The drugged wine.** At the supper after the weeping, Helen “cast a drug into the wine from which they were drinking” (4.220), and when she had cast it in she bade the servants pour (κέλευσέ τε οἰνοχοῆσαι, 4.233; see [[nepenthe]], [[mixing-bowl]] and [[cupbearing]]). The drug was mixed in the bowl with the wine, and only so did it take away grief (4.222).

**Wine that makes men strong.** The banqueters who come to Menelaus' house bring sheep and “wine that makes men strong”, ἐυήνορα οἶνον (4.622; see [[feast]] and the translation's row δαιτυμών, ἐυήνωρ, καλλικρήδεμνος). Later in the poem the same adjective is used of the bronze that the Phaeacians carry to Odysseus' ship (13.19).

**Wine for the voyage.** Eurycleia confesses to Penelope that she knew of her son's journey and gave him “bread and sweet wine”, μέθυ ἡδύ (4.745–746; see [[eurycleia]]): the twelve jars of wine and the barley meal in leather bags that he asked of her in Book 2 (2.349–355, 2.379–380; see [[provisions]] and [[two-handled-jar]]).
