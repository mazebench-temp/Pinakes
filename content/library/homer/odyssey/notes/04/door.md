# The bolt and its strap (4.802) and the bolt in the doorpost (4.838), in phrases, as the Book 1 note's
# "κληῖδʼ ἐτάνυσσεν ἱμάντι".

**The doorway.** Telemachus and Peisistratus stop with their horses “in the doorway of the house” (ἐν προθύροισι δόμων, 4.20), which W. W. Merry takes for the entrance from outside into the court (*Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 4.20; see [[house]] and [[eteoneus]]).

**The bolt.** The phantom needs no door opened: “It went into the chamber past the strap of the bolt, / and it stood above her head” (4.802–803), and when it has spoken, “it slipped away past the bolt of the doorpost / into the blasts of the winds” (4.838–839; see [[eidolon]] and [[iphthime]]). The bolt is the one Eurycleia shuts with its strap in Book 1 (1.441–442), and Merry refers to his note there (on 4.802); at 4.838 he renders “by the side of the bolt that fitted in the doorpost”, and supposes that the phantom slipped through the slit in which the strap worked (on 4.838).
