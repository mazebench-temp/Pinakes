---
summary: Nestor is remembered at Sparta: he sent his son as Telemachus' escort, he used to praise Menelaus' wisdom in his own halls, and Menelaus counts him blessed in his old age and in his sons (4.161–163, 4.190–192, 4.207–211). In his tale Menelaus recalls that he and Nestor left Troy together (4.488).
# Νέστορος, Νέστωρ, Νέστορι and the patronymic Νεστορίδης (4.21, 4.69, 4.71, 4.155, 4.161, 4.186, 4.191, 4.209,
# 4.303, 4.488) are claimed by the entry and its Book 3 note (Νέστορ*). Unnamed: "for he longed to see you" (4.162-163),
# "in his halls" (4.192), his old age and his sons (4.210-211).

**The sender.** Peisistratus explains his coming: “And the Gerenian horseman Nestor sent me forth / to go with him as his escort; for he longed to see you, / so that you might give him counsel in some word or some deed” (4.161–163; see [[gerenian-horseman]], [[peisistratus]] and [[the-journey-of-telemachus]]). Nestor had promised Telemachus a chariot and his sons to take him to Lacedaemon (3.324–326). Peisistratus' tears for Antilochus begin with his father's words: “Son of Atreus, that you were wise beyond all mortals / old Nestor used to say, whenever we made mention of you / in his halls and asked one another about you” (4.190–192; see [[antilochus]] and [[menelaus]]), a verse Aristarchus rejected, but which Merry keeps, taking οἷσιν ἐνὶ μεγάροισι with Nestor's saying, “in his own halls” (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 4.192).

**Blessed in his old age.** Menelaus answers the son with the father's praise:

{{quote:b4bd0b76-fc69-50ee-b543-32f093eb01b5}}

γαμέοντί τε γεινομένῳ τε, “at his marriage and at his birth”, puts the later event first for the sake of the meter (Merry on 4.208; see [[easily-known-is-the-offspring]]), and λιπαρῶς γηρασκέμεν, “to grow old in ease”, is the old age Teiresias foretells for Odysseus, γήραι ὕπο λιπαρῷ (11.136, which Merry compares on 4.210; see [[olbos]] and [[fathers-and-sons]]). The blessing is the one Nestor himself lacks in one thing: his son Antilochus lies dead at Troy (3.111–112; 4.186–202).

**Left behind at Troy.** In the tale of Proteus Menelaus asks whether all the Achaeans came home unharmed, “whom Nestor and I left behind when we went from Troy” (4.488; see [[the-returns-of-the-achaeans]]), the departure Nestor himself told at Pylos, when the two of them sailed together as far as Sunium (3.165–183, 3.276–285; see [[the-departure-of-the-greeks]]).
