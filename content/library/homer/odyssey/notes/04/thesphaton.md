---
summary: Proteus tells Menelaus, “But for you, Menelaus, nurtured by Zeus, it is not decreed / to die and meet your fate in horse-pasturing Argos”: the immortals will send him to the Elysian plain (4.561–569).
---

θέσφατόν ἐστι, “it is decreed”, says that a thing will be because a god has spoken it. Here it is said of what will not be: Menelaus is not to die in Argos, but to be sent by the immortals to the Elysian plain and the limits of the earth, where fair-haired Rhadamanthys is and life is easiest for men, “because you have Helen as your wife, and in their eyes you are the son-in-law of Zeus” (4.561–569; see [[elysium]], [[menelaus]] and [[in-laws]]). The decree comes from Proteus, the unerring Old Man of the Sea, who is himself immortal (4.384–385; see [[proteus]] and [[moira]]).
