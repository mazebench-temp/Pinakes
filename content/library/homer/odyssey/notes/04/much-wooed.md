---
summary: The suitors' jeer: “Surely a marriage for us the much-wooed queen / is preparing, and does not know that slaughter has been made ready for her son” (4.770–771).
---

The first use of the epithet is a mistake: as Penelope prays for her son, the suitors take the cry for the preparation of a wedding (4.767–772; [[tis-speech]], [[the-suitors]], [[penelope]]). The narrator answers at once, “but they did not know how things had been brought about” (4.772).
