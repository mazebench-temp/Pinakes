---
summary: At Sparta Ithaca is remembered and described: Menelaus would have brought Odysseus from it to live near him, Proteus names it as Odysseus' home, and Telemachus, refusing the gift of horses, calls it a land for goats, with no courses for driving and no meadows, and lovelier than horse-pasturing country. The last part of the book is set there, where the suitors plan to ambush him in the strait (4.175, 4.555, 4.601–608, 4.625–847).
passages:
  - 4.601-608 | Ithaca is a land for goats
---

**Remembered at Sparta.** Menelaus would have given Odysseus a city in Argos and brought him “from Ithaca with his possessions and his son / and all his people” (4.174–176; see [[argos]]), and Proteus answers his question about the third of the Achaeans with the hero's home: “It is the son of Laertes, who has his home in Ithaca” (4.555; see [[odysseus]] and [[proteus]]).

**A land for goats.** Menelaus offers three horses and a chariot, and Telemachus asks for a treasure instead:

{{quote:a0c65b44-fcc0-592f-bcd1-79af5ac4ff38}}

Merry finds the asyndeton harsh where αἰγίβοτος, “goat-pasturing”, begins a new clause, and reports Bergk's conjecture that 4.606 should follow 4.608, “and Ithaca, more than all, is a goat-pasturing place yet more lovely than one that pastures horses”; he asks whether ἐπήρατος means lovely in itself or lovely to Telemachus because it is his home, and doubts that the craggy island was admired for its scenery (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 4.606; see [[goats]] and [[horses]]). The islands, ἁλὶ κεκλίαται, “lie upon the sea”, as if they rested on it (Merry on 4.608). Menelaus' own country is the other kind of land, a wide plain of lotus, galingale, wheat, spelt and barley (4.602–604; see [[lacedaemon]]). The answer pleases him: he smiles, strokes Telemachus with his hand, and tells him that he is of good blood (4.609–611). Horace retells the refusal in his *Epistles*: “Not badly Telemachus, son of enduring Ulysses: / ‘Ithaca is not a place fit for horses, since it neither stretches out / in level spaces nor is lavish of much grass; / son of Atreus, I will leave your gifts to you, more fit for you’” (Horace, *Epistles* 1.7.40–43; Merry on 4.605; see [[horace]]).

**In Ithaca.** From 4.625 the book is in Ithaca. Noemon, who has lent his ship, needs it to cross to Elis, where he keeps mares and mules, since Ithaca was no country for breeding them (4.634–637; Merry on 4.635; see [[noemon-son-of-phronius]] and [[elis]]); Antinous asks whether the young men who went with Telemachus were chosen men of Ithaca or his own hired men and slaves (4.642–644; see [[antinous]]); and the suitors plan to lie in wait for him “in the strait between Ithaca and rocky Samos” (4.671; see [[samos]] and [[asteris]]).
