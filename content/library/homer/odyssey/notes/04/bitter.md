---
summary: Telemachus “let a bitter tear fall from beneath his brows” (4.153); the seals breathe out “the bitter smell of the deep sea” (4.406); and if Odysseus met the suitors, “bitter would their wedding be” (4.346).
grc: [πικρόγαμοι]
---

At 4.406 Merry takes πικρόν as adverbial to ἀποπνείουσαι rather than as an adjective of two terminations agreeing with ὀδμήν (*Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 4.406; [[seals]]). πικρόγαμοι, “bitter-wedded”, ends the verse of the suitors' doom, Athena's in Book 1 and Menelaus' here (1.266, 4.346; [[swift-fated]]).
