summary: Helen bathed the disguised Odysseus in Troy “and sworn a mighty oath / not to reveal Odysseus among the Trojans” before he reached the ships (4.253–255); in Ithaca Eurycleia confesses that Telemachus “took a great oath from me / not to tell you before the twelfth day had come” (4.746–747).
grc: [ὤμοσα]
---

**Helen's oath.** Odysseus told Helen the whole plan of the Achaeans only when she had sworn καρτερὸν ὅρκον, “a mighty oath”, not to reveal him among the Trojans before he had reached the ships and the huts (4.252–256; see [[odysseus-enters-troy-as-a-beggar]] and [[truth-and-deception]]). Merry notes that the words do not imply that Helen told the story only after Odysseus' return to the camp: she engaged only not to tell it before (*Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 4.254).

**Eurycleia's oath.** The oath of Book 2 is kept and then confessed: “he took a great oath from me / not to tell you before the twelfth day had come, / or you yourself missed him and heard that he had gone, / so that you might not mar your fair skin with weeping” (4.746–749; 2.373–378; see [[eurycleia]] and [[mothers-and-sons]]).
