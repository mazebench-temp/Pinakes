# σπένδῃσθα (4.591), first met here in the Odyssey.
grc: [σπένδῃσθα]
---

**The goblet of remembrance.** The last of the gifts Menelaus offers is a cup for the gods: “I will give you a fine goblet, so that you may pour libations to the gods, / the immortals, remembering me all your days” (4.591–592; see [[cup]] and [[guest-gifts]]). Every libation poured from it will be a remembrance of the giver. When the gift is changed, the cup is not forgotten: on the morning Telemachus leaves, Menelaus puts a two-handled cup in his hand with the silver bowl, and then comes after the travelers with wine in a golden cup, so that they may pour a libation before they go (15.120–123, 15.147–150, plain renderings; see [[mixing-bowl]]).

**Barley for the goddess.** Penelope, praying for her son, “put barley grains in a basket, and prayed to Athena” (4.761; see [[barley-grains]], [[basket]] and [[prayer]]). Merry rejects Nitzsch's rendering, a smoke-offering of holy barley, and the scholium's view that the basket stood for an altar: the barley was not burned, but poured out from its basket as wine from its cup, and so was rather the equivalent of a libation (*Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 4.761).
