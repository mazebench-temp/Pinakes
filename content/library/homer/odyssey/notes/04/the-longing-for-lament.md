---
summary: Menelaus' words of Odysseus rouse it twice: in Telemachus alone, “and in him he roused the longing for lament for his father” (4.113), and in all of them, in the verse that Aristotle quotes (4.183).
grc: ["ἵμερον ὦρσε γόοιο"]
en: [roused the longing for lament]
---

4.113 is the verse of Priam's appeal to Achilles, when the old man's words about Peleus move Achilles to weep for his own father (*Iliad* 24.507); 4.183 is the verse of Achilles' dream of Patroclus (*Iliad* 23.108), which the *Rhetoric* quotes for the pleasure there is in lamentation, the pleasure of remembering the dead and in a way seeing him (*Rhetoric* 1370b25–29). Merry takes ὑπό as “in the mind”, so that ὑφʼ … ὦρσε is “sent into his mind”, and cites Aristotle's quotation on 4.113 (*Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 4.113); the verse the *Rhetoric* gives is 4.183 (see the translation's note on 4.113).

After the weeping, Helen, Telemachus, Menelaus and Peisistratus all in tears, Peisistratus asks that they not lament after supper, and Helen's drug keeps the tears away for the rest of the night (4.184–232; [[lament]], [[nepenthe]], [[himeros]]).
