summary: Menelaus sends Hermione to the son of Achilles “with horses and chariots” (4.8); his attendants unyoke the strangers' horses and lean their chariot against the gleaming walls (4.39–42); and he offers Telemachus three horses and a well-polished chariot, which Telemachus declines, since Ithaca has no room for driving horses (4.590–608). In his tale Aegisthus goes with horses and chariot to invite Agamemnon to the feast at which he kills him (4.532–535).

**The bride's chariots.** Menelaus is sending Hermione to the son of Achilles: “So he was sending her there with horses and chariots to go / to the renowned city of the Myrmidons” (4.8–9; see [[the-wedding-at-sparta]] and [[hermione-daughter-of-helen]]). W. W. Merry takes ἵπποισι καὶ ἅρμασι as an instrumental dative with πέμπε, as ὄχεσφιν goes with βῆ at 4.533 (*Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 4.533).

**The travelers' chariot.** Telemachus and Peisistratus arrive in Nestor's chariot, and the attendants “unyoked the sweating horses from under the yoke, / and tied them at the horses' mangers, / and threw spelt before them, and mixed white barley with it, / and leaned the chariot against the gleaming walls” (4.39–42; see [[horses]] and [[eteoneus]]). The first and last of these verses are the unyoking of the Trojans' horses and of Hera's chariot in the Iliad (*Iliad* 8.543, 8.435). Merry takes the gleaming walls for the faces of the gateway of the court, and against one of them “the chariot was rested atilt” (on 4.42; see [[house]]).

**The gift declined.** Menelaus offers “three horses and a well-polished chariot” (4.590): a pair and a trace-horse, the scholia explain, like Achilles' team with Pedasus in the traces (Merry on 4.590; *Iliad* 16.152–154; see the translation's note on 4.590). Telemachus answers that he will not take horses to Ithaca, a goat-pasturing land with no wide courses for running, where none of the islands is good for driving horses (4.601–608; see [[ithaca]] and [[guest-gifts]]). Penelope, hearing that he has gone, calls ships “horses of the sea / for men” (4.708–709), and Merry takes ἵπποι there almost as chariots, the team and car together (on 4.708; see [[ships]]).

**Aegisthus' chariot.** In Proteus' account Aegisthus, having set his ambush and ordered the feast, “went to invite Agamemnon, shepherd of the people, / with horses and chariot, pondering shameful things” (4.532–533; see [[the-murder-of-agamemnon]] and [[aegisthus]]): he drives out to fetch his guest in state.
