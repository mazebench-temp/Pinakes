summary: The son of Achilles is sent to the Myrmidons, “over whom he ruled” (4.9); in Libya “neither master nor shepherd” lacks milk (4.87); Menelaus is lord of his possessions with no joy (4.93), of cities round about (4.177) and of a wide plain (4.602).
grc: [ἀνάσσονται]
---

ἄναξ and ἀνάσσω name the lord of men, of a house and of a land. Neoptolemus rules the Myrmidons (4.9; see [[neoptolemus]]), and in Libya the master of the flocks (ἄναξ) and his shepherd have milk and cheese and meat without fail (4.85–89; see [[libya]]). Menelaus is lord of his great possessions, but “with no joy” (4.93; see [[olbos]]), and of the cities about his own, one of which he would have emptied for Odysseus (4.174–177; see [[kingship-and-authority]]); Telemachus leaves him the horses, “for you are lord of a plain / that is wide” (4.602–603; see [[ithaca]]).
