---
summary: The Odyssey names Oceanus first in Proteus' prophecy of the Elysian plain, where he always sends up the breezes of the West Wind to refresh men.
---

**At the limits of the earth.** The Elysian plain lies at “the limits of the earth” (4.563), where the river that runs round the world flows, and the breezes that make life easy there come from it: “but always the breezes of the West Wind, blowing shrill, / Oceanus sends up to refresh men” (4.567–568; see [[elysium]] and [[zephyrus]]). This is the first time the Odyssey names him. In the Iliad the sun rises “out of gently flowing, deep-streaming Oceanus” (*Iliad* 7.422) and the blameless Ethiopians live beside him (*Iliad* 1.423; see [[ethiopians]], whom Menelaus visited on his wanderings, 4.84), and Hera pretends to be going “to see the limits of the all-nourishing earth, / and Oceanus, the origin of the gods, and mother Tethys” (*Iliad* 14.200–201). The ends of the earth, Oceanus and the dwelling of the blessed come together again in Hesiod, whose heroes live “in the Isles of the Blessed beside deep-eddying Oceanus” (*Works and Days* 171; see [[hesiod]]), and Merry compares Pindar's island of the blessed, round which the breezes of Ocean blow (*Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 4.567). Later in the poem Odysseus will cross Oceanus to reach the house of Hades (10.508–511, 11.13).
