**The other Ajax.** Proteus calls him only Αἴας, without his father's name, and the story itself shows which Ajax he means: the son of Telamon lies dead at Troy (3.109; see [[ajax]]), and this one perished at sea on the way home. Merry names him the Locrian Ajax, who was shipwrecked “under the curse of Athena” (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 4.499), and counts him with Agamemnon as the two leaders who did not come home (on 4.497; see [[the-returns-of-the-achaeans]]).

**Hated by Athena.** The poem says that he was ἐχθόμενός … Ἀθήνῃ, “hated … by Athena” (4.502), and does not say why. In the first book Merry explained the grim homecoming that Pallas Athena laid on the Achaeans by the outrage of this Ajax to Cassandra in her temple, noting that here his death is laid to his insolence toward Poseidon (on 1.326; see the translation's note on 1.327, [[athena]] and [[cassandra]]). His end is told under [[the-death-of-ajax-son-of-oileus]]: Poseidon brought him to the rocks of Gyrae and saved him, and when he boasted that he had escaped “against the will of the gods”, split the rock and drowned him (4.500–511; see [[poseidon]] and [[gyrae]]).

**The boaster.** The Iliad's Ajax, son of Oileus, is quick of foot and quick of tongue. At Patroclus' games he abuses Idomeneus until Achilles stops the quarrel, and in the footrace Athena makes him slip in the dung so that Odysseus wins, and he complains that the goddess stands by Odysseus “like a mother” (*Iliad* 23.473–498, 23.768–783; see [[the-funeral-games-of-patroclus]]). In the Odyssey his last words are a boast, ὑπερφίαλον ἔπος, “an overbearing word” (4.503; see [[overbearing]]).
