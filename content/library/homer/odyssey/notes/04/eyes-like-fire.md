---
summary: Antinous, at the news that Telemachus has sailed: “in distress; and his heart, dark on every side, was filled / with mighty fury, and his eyes were like blazing fire” (4.661–662).
grc: ["φρένες ἀμφιμέλαιναι"]
en: [dark on every side]
---

The two verses are Agamemnon's when he rises to answer Calchas (*Iliad* 1.103–104), and a scholium on this place, reported by Merry, says that they were brought over from the Iliad improperly (Merry's critical note on 4.661–662). Merry counts ἀμφιμέλαιναι four times in Homer besides this place (*Iliad* 1.103, 17.83, 17.499, 17.573, where this text prints ἀμφὶ μελαίνας as two words); some, he reports, wrote it so here too, joining ἀμφί to the verb. He takes ἀμφί as “on both sides” and, with Monro, the word as predicative with πίμπλαντʼ, “were filled so as to be black with rage on both sides” (*Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 4.661).
