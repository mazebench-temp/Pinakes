---
summary: Hesiod's Isles of the Blessed, where Zeus settled some of the heroes of Thebes and Troy at the limits of the earth beside Oceanus, answer the Elysian plain that Proteus promises Menelaus; and his Nereus, the Old Man who is unerring and knows just counsels, has the title that this book gives Proteus.
---

- The heroes whom Zeus settled “at the limits of the earth”, in the Isles of the Blessed beside Oceanus, where the grain-giving earth bears fruit three times a year (*Works and Days* 156–173; on 4.561–569): [[elysium-and-the-isles-of-the-blessed]] and [[elysium]].
- Nereus, whom men call the Old Man “because he is unerring (νημερτής) and gentle”, and who knows just counsels (*Theogony* 233–236; on 4.349, 4.384): [[proteus]] and [[old-man-of-the-sea]]. Euripides' Theonoe, the daughter of Proteus, has her justice from Nereus (see [[euripides-helen]]), and Psamathe, her mother in Euripides, is one of Hesiod's Nereids (*Theogony* 260).
