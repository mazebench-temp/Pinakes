---
summary: Peisistratus is “the splendid son of Nestor” at the door and at bedtime (4.21, 4.303), the son of Dawn who killed Antilochus is “the splendid son of shining Dawn” (4.188), and Menelaus promises “splendid gifts” (4.589).
---

Νέστορος ἀγλαὸς υἱός closes the verse that names the two young men, “the hero Telemachus and the splendid son of Nestor”, when they arrive and when they go to sleep (4.21, 4.303; [[peisistratus]]). The son of Dawn is not named; Merry identifies him as [[Memnon]], king of the eastern Aethiopians, whom Achilles killed to avenge Antilochus (*Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 4.188; [[antilochus]], [[phaeinos]]). Menelaus' ἀγλαὰ δῶρα are the horses, the chariot and the goblet he offers (4.589–592; [[guest-gifts]]).
