---

The Iliad's name for the woman the war was fought for is given to her at home, among the guests: in the verse in which “Argive Helen wept, child of Zeus” for Odysseus and for the war (4.184; [[daughter-of-zeus]], [[the-longing-for-lament]]), and as the mistress of the house who orders the beds beneath the colonnade (4.296–301; [[bed]]). Penelope uses the same verse of her at the end of the poem (23.218). See [[helen]].
