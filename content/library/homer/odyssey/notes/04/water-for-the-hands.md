---
summary: The second use of the five verses: when Telemachus and Peisistratus have bathed and sat down beside Menelaus, a handmaid brings them water for the hands, a table, bread and dainties (4.52–56 = 1.136–140).
---

Bathed and dressed, the two young men sit down on chairs beside Menelaus, and the formula serves them exactly as it served the stranger in Telemachus' house (4.51–56 = 1.136–140; see [[xenia]], [[handmaids]] and [[basin-and-pitcher]]). The guests' meal is set apart from the wedding feast going on in the house, as the stranger's was set apart from the suitors' feast in Book 1 (see [[the-wedding-at-sparta]] and [[feast-type-scene]]). The carver's platters and golden cups follow, as in Book 1, but most editors since Wolf reject the two verses here (4.57–58; see the translation's note on 4.57–58). The scholia report a variant χρυσῆν, “golden”, for ξεστήν, “polished”, of the table (W. W. Merry's critical note on 4.54; see [[table]]). The same five verses serve Telemachus and Peisistratus again on the morning they leave Sparta (15.135–139). At the supper after the weeping the washing is told in other words, “let them pour water / over our hands”, and Asphalion pours it (4.213–216; see [[hand-washing]]).
