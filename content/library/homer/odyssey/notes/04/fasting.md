**Penelope's fast.** After her prayer Penelope lies in her upper room ἄσιτος, ἄπαστος ἐδητύος ἠδὲ ποτῆτος, “without food, tasting neither food nor drink” (4.787–788): the second phrase spells out the first without a connective, and Merry compares the verse with Helen's drug, “against grief and anger, that brought forgetfulness of every ill” (*Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 4.221). She ponders whether her son will escape death, and sleep comes upon her (4.789–794; see [[mothers-and-sons]] and [[hypnos]]). Grief takes away a man's appetite too: Menelaus says that the thought of Odysseus makes “sleep and food hateful to me” (4.105).

**Hunger on Pharos.** Held twenty days on the island, Menelaus' men would have used up their provisions and their strength (4.360–364); they wander about the island fishing “with curved hooks, and hunger wore at their bellies” (4.368–369; see [[fish]]).
