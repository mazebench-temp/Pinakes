summary: Menelaus sits beside Telemachus at dawn “and spoke to him, and called him by name” (4.311), and after the refusal of the horses he smiles, “and stroked him with his hand, and spoke to him, and called him by name” (4.610).
grc: ["χειρί τέ μιν κατέρεξεν"]
en: [stroked him with his hand]
---

The whole verse χειρί τέ μιν κατέρεξεν ἔπος τʼ ἔφατʼ ἔκ τʼ ὀνόμαζεν is a goddess's or a mother's gesture in the Iliad, and Merry lists its other places (*Iliad* 1.361, 5.372, 6.485, 24.127; 5.181), with the syncopated participle καρρέζουσα (*Iliad* 5.424); he finds the usual derivation from ῥέζω of little sense and connects κατέρεξεν rather with ὀρέγω, “reach out” (*Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 4.610). Here it is a host's to a young guest, after a smile ([[smiling-at-him]], [[menelaus]], [[telemachus]]).
