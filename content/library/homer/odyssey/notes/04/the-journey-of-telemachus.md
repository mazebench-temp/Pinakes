summary: Sparta, the second stage of the journey, and the ambush laid against the return. Telemachus and Peisistratus come to Menelaus' house on the day of a double wedding; at dawn Telemachus tells his errand and hears what Proteus told Menelaus, that his father is alive on Calypso's island, and asks leave to go back to his companions at Pylos. In Ithaca the suitors learn of the voyage and send Antinous with twenty men to lie in wait for him at Asteris, and Penelope learns of it from Medon (4.1–847).

**Lacedaemon.** “And they came to hollow Lacedaemon with its many ravines, / and drove on to the house of glorious Menelaus” (4.1–2; see [[lacedaemon]] and [[menelaus]]), at nightfall, after the day's drive from Pherae (3.488–497). They find a double wedding in the house (4.3–19; see [[the-wedding-at-sparta]]), and are received, bathed and fed before anyone asks their names (4.20–67; see [[xenia]]). The book takes its ancient title from this stage, Τὰ ἐν Λακεδαίμονι, “the events at Lacedaemon” (see [[the-title-of-odyssey-4]]).

**The news he came for.** At dawn Menelaus asks what need has brought him “to shining Lacedaemon, over the broad back of the sea” (4.312–313), and Telemachus answers with the supplication he made to Nestor (4.316–331, where 4.322–331 = 3.92–101; see [[telemachus]]). The answer is the tale of Proteus, which ends in the news that no one at Pylos could give: Odysseus is alive, held by Calypso on her island without ships or companions (4.555–560; see [[proteus]] and [[calypso]]). It is the news Telemachus will carry home and tell his mother in the same verses (17.142–146).

**Asking leave.** Telemachus would gladly stay a year, but his companions are waiting in sacred Pylos (4.594–599; see [[pylos]]), and he refuses the horses Menelaus offers, for Ithaca has no room for them (4.601–608). Yet by the reckoning of the later books he is still at Sparta a month later, when Athena comes to send him home at the opening of Book 15 (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 4.594; see the translation's note on 4.594).

**The ambush.** The second half of the book goes back in time: the scenes in Ithaca fall on the fourth day, while Telemachus drives from Pylos to Pherae, since Noemon saw Mentor in Ithaca “yesterday at daybreak”, the morning after the ship sailed (4.655–656; see the translation's note on 4.655–656). Noemon's question about his ship tells the suitors that Telemachus has gone, and with whom (4.630–656; see [[noemon-son-of-phronius]] and [[mentor]]). Antinous calls it “a great deed … overbearingly accomplished” and asks for “a swift ship and twenty companions”, the verse of Telemachus' own request in the assembly, to lie in wait for him in the strait between Ithaca and rocky Samos (4.663–672; 4.669 = 2.212; see [[antinous]]). Medon tells Penelope, who did not know her son had gone, and cannot say whether a god roused him or his own heart (4.677–714; see [[medon-the-herald]]); Eurycleia confesses the oath he made her swear, in nearly the words he used (4.745–749, from 2.373–376; see [[eurycleia]]); and the phantom of Iphthime tells Penelope that Athena herself goes with him as his escort (4.825–829; see [[iphthime]] and [[athena]]). The book ends with the suitors' ship at Asteris, “there the Achaeans waited for him, lying in ambush” (4.847; see [[asteris]] and [[the-suitors]]).
