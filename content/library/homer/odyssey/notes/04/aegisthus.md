summary: Menelaus calls him only “another man”, who killed his brother in secret (4.91). In Proteus' account he is “Aegisthus, son of Thyestes”, living where his father had lived; he posts a lookout with a promise of two talents of gold, sets an ambush of twenty men, invites Agamemnon to a feast and kills him there, and none of his own men survives (4.517–537). Proteus leaves his fate open: Menelaus may find him alive, or Orestes may have killed him first (4.546–547).

**Another man.** Menelaus does not name him: “meanwhile another man killed my brother / in secret, unawares, by the guile of his ruinous wife” (4.91–92; see [[agamemnon]]). His name comes in the god's account, with his father's: Θυεστιάδης Αἴγισθος, “Aegisthus, son of Thyestes”, dwelt at the far edge of the land where Thyestes had his dwelling before (4.517–518; see [[thyestes]]). Merry reports the geographer Andron's view that their regular home was on Cythera, though at the time of the murder Aegisthus was at Mycenae, waiting for Agamemnon's return (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 4.514; see [[cythera]]).

**Scheming.** Αἴγισθος δολόμητις, “scheming Aegisthus” (4.525), is his epithet in each book that has named him (1.300; 3.198, 3.250, 3.308; see [[schemer]]). He posted a lookout and promised him two talents of gold as wages, and kept him watching for a year (4.524–527; see [[misthos]] and [[spies-and-scouts]]). When the news came, “at once Aegisthus devised a crafty scheme” (4.529): δολίην … τέχνην is the phrase Menelaus used a little before of Proteus, who did not forget “his crafty art” when they seized him (4.455; see [[proteus]] and [[dolos]]). The lookout goes “to bring the news to the shepherd of the people” (4.528), the title of a king, given here to Aegisthus in a verse that differs by one word from the one in which Eteoneus went to his master Menelaus (4.24; see [[shepherd-of-the-people]]).

**The feast.** He chose twenty of the best men of the land and set them in ambush, had a feast prepared, and drove down to invite Agamemnon “with horses and chariot, pondering shameful things” (4.530–533; see [[ambush]]). ἀεικέα μερμηρίζων, “pondering shameful things”, uses the adjective with which Nestor named the deed Clytemnestra at first refused, ἔργον ἀεικές, “the shameful deed” (3.265). He killed the king at the feast “as one kills an ox at the manger”, and in the fight none of Agamemnon's companions survived, “nor any of Aegisthus' men, but they were killed in the halls” (4.535–537; see [[the-murder-of-agamemnon]]). The words of his choice, ἐείκοσι φῶτας ἀρίστους, “twenty of the best men” (4.530), return for Antinous when the suitors set their ambush for Telemachus (4.778; see [[antinous]] and [[the-suitors]]).

**Alive or dead.** Proteus leaves it open whether he still lives: “For either you will find him alive, or Orestes / will have killed him before you, and you may come in time for the funeral feast” (4.546–547; see [[orestes]]). Nestor has already told the answer: Orestes killed him in the eighth year, and Menelaus came home on the day of the feast (3.306–312; see [[orestes-kills-clytemnestra]]).
