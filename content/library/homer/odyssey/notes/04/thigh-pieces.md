---
summary: Penelope reminds Athena of the thigh-pieces Odysseus burned to her: “if ever Odysseus of many wiles in his halls / burned to you the fat thigh-pieces of ox or sheep, / remember these things now for me, and save my dear son” (4.763–765).
---

Penelope's prayer argues from past offerings, as Chryses' did in the first book of the Iliad, “or if ever I burned for you the fat thigh-pieces / of bulls and goats, fulfill for me this wish” (*Iliad* 1.40–41; see [[chryses]] and [[reciprocity-with-the-gods]]). The sacrifices were Odysseus' own, burned “in his halls” (4.763), and his wife asks the goddess to repay them to his son (see [[penelope]], [[prayer]] and [[athena]]). “So saying, she raised the cry, and the goddess heard her prayer” (4.767).
