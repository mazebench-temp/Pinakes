---
summary: “Prudent Penelope” three times in the night: lying without food in her upper room while the suitors sail (4.787), and answering the phantom of her sister (4.808, 4.830).
---

περίφρων Πηνελόπεια, the Odyssey's verse-end for her, stands apart from ἐχέφρων, “sensible”, which Menelaus gives her when he imagines her mourning (4.111; [[sensible]]), and from Telemachus' πεπνυμένος, “wise” ([[wise]]). It names her as she ponders whether her son will escape death (4.787–793; [[pondering-two-ways]]) and as she speaks with the dim phantom, asking to hear of her husband (4.808–834; [[dream-scene]], [[penelope]]).
