# Unnamed: "for his son" (4.4, 4.10) and "from a slave woman" (4.12).

passages:
  - 4.3-14 | The double wedding
---

The wedding feast Menelaus is giving to his kinsmen is “for his son and his noble daughter” (4.3–4; see [[the-wedding-at-sparta]]). “And for his son he was bringing the daughter of Alector of Sparta, / strong Megapenthes, who was born to him as his darling / from a slave woman; for to Helen the gods brought no more offspring to light, / once she had first borne a lovely child, / Hermione, who had the form of golden Aphrodite” (4.10–14; see [[alector]], [[hermione-daughter-of-helen]] and [[helen]]). ἤγετο is used here of the father getting a wife for his son, where it is commonly the bridegroom who brings home his bride (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 4.10). The tenses, πέμπε and ἤγετο beside ἐξετέλειον, “were bringing to pass”, suggest to Merry that Hermione's departure was still being prepared and that the daughter of Alector was on her way to join Megapenthes; Telemachus comes in at the end of the feasting, for there is no sign of it once he is inside, nor on the next day (on 4.3, on 4.7). Megapenthes takes no part in the book after these lines. He carries the mixing bowl promised in it to Telemachus in Book 15 (15.100–103; see [[phaedimus]]).
