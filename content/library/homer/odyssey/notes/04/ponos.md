---
summary: Penelope fears for a son who knows “nothing well either of toils or of assemblies” (4.818).
grc: [πόνων]
---

Telemachus is νήπιος, a child who has not yet learned the work of men, οὔτε πόνων ἐὺ εἰδὼς οὔτʼ ἀγοράων, the toils and the assemblies of grown men (4.817–818; see [[nepios]], [[assembly]] and [[mothers-and-sons]]). Menelaus' word for Odysseus' labors is another, μογέω: no one of the Achaeans “toiled as much / as Odysseus toiled and endured” (4.106–107).
