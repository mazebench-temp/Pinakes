---
summary: Menelaus imagines Odysseus' family mourning him: “the old man Laertes and sensible Penelope / and Telemachus” (4.111–112), the first time the poem gives her the epithet.
---

Merry notes that the Odyssey uses ἐχέφρων only of Penelope, and once of Odysseus (*Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 4.111; 13.332). In Ithaca, in the same book, the narrator calls her περίφρων, “prudent” (4.787, 4.808, 4.830; [[periphron]], [[penelope]]).
