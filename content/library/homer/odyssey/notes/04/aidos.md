---
summary: The “revered housekeeper” sets out bread for the guests at Sparta (4.55), Peisistratus explains that Telemachus is too modest to speak first (4.158–160), and Telemachus asks Menelaus not to soften anything “out of respect for me, or out of pity” (4.326).
---

The revered housekeeper of the welcome (αἰδοίη ταμίη, 4.55) comes in the verse of Book 1 (1.139; see [[xenia]]). Peisistratus speaks for Telemachus because he is σαόφρων, “modest”, and indignant in his heart at making a show of forward talk before Menelaus on his first visit (4.158–160; see [[nemesis]]); at Pylos Telemachus had told Athena, in Mentor's likeness, that it is shame for a young man to question an older one (3.22–24; see [[shame-for-a-young-man]]). At dawn he asks Menelaus for the whole truth, as he asked Nestor: “And do not soften anything out of respect for me, or out of pity” (4.326, = 3.96; see [[pity]] and [[truth-and-deception]]).
