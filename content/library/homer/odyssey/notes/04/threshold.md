---
summary: Penelope speaks to the herald Medon as he steps over the threshold, and when she has heard him she sinks down on the threshold of her chamber, unable to sit on a chair (4.680, 4.717–718).
---

“And as he stepped over the threshold Penelope spoke to him” (4.680; see [[medon-the-herald]] and [[heralds]]). When she has heard of the plot, she cannot sit on any of the chairs in the house, “but she sat on the threshold of her chamber, worked with much toil, / lamenting piteously”, with her women whimpering round her (4.717–719; see [[throne]], [[handmaids]] and [[penelope]]). W. W. Merry renders: she sank down on the floor of her bower crying piteously (*Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 4.717).
