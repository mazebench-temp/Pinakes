“Alcandre, the wife of Polybus, who lived in Thebes / in Egypt, where the most treasures lie stored in the houses. / He gave Menelaus two silver bathtubs / and two tripods, and ten talents of gold” (4.126–129; see [[alcandre]], [[thebes-in-egypt]], [[menelaus]] and [[guest-gifts]]). The Thebes of Egypt is the city whose wealth Achilles names in the Iliad, when he refuses the gifts of Agamemnon, “all that comes to Thebes / in Egypt, where the most treasures lie stored in the houses” (*Iliad* 9.381–382), and 4.127 is that verse, with the dative Αἰγυπτίῃς for Αἰγυπτίας. His gifts are part of the wealth Menelaus gathered in his wandering, “gathering much substance in those lands” (4.90; see [[the-wanderings-of-menelaus]]), and Helen's basket and distaff come from the same house (4.130–132; see [[helen]] and [[gold-and-silver]]).
