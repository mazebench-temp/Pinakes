---
summary: Telemachus, whispering to Peisistratus, names amber among the splendors of Menelaus' halls: “the flash of bronze throughout the echoing halls, / and of gold and amber and silver and ivory” (4.72–73).
---

The two young men have bathed and eaten, and Telemachus puts his head close to Peisistratus so that the others may not hear (4.68–70; see [[peisistratus]]):

{{quote:1fd864a2-dac3-54c9-a2e6-b5f3716601e7}}

The list runs from the walls' flash of bronze to the precious things of the house, and amber stands between gold and silver. W. W. Merry, who decides for amber in Homer, grants that it is tempting here to take ἤλεκτρον for electrum, the alloy of the two metals it stands between, as Pliny did when he cited this verse (*Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 4.73; see the translation's note on 4.73). Menelaus answers that no mortal could rival Zeus, whose house and possessions are immortal, and that he gathered his own in eight years of wandering (4.78–92; see [[house]], [[possessions]] and [[the-wanderings-of-menelaus]]).
