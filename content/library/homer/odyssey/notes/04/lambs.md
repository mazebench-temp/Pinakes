---
summary: Menelaus' Libya is the land “where the lambs are horned at once”, and where the flocks bear young three times a year, so that neither master nor shepherd lacks cheese, meat or milk (4.85–89).
---

{{quote:994dec0f-3f18-5fe8-8050-4e331123da63}}

**Horned at once.** The last land of Menelaus' eight years of wandering is Libya, and the lambs are its first marvel: ἄρνες ἄφαρ κεραοὶ τελέθουσι (4.85; see [[libya]] and [[the-wanderings-of-menelaus]]). κεραός, “horned”, is in the Iliad the epithet of the stag (*Iliad* 3.24, 11.475, 15.271, 16.158); here it is given to the young of the flock. Whether the lambs grow their horns quickly or are born with them depends on τελέθουσι: Merry takes it as “are”, not “become”, so that they come to birth with horns grown or sprouting (*Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 4.85), and the translation follows him (see the translation's note on 4.85). Herodotus quoted the verse for the quick growth of horns in hot lands, and Aristotle for horned beasts born with their horns in Libya (see [[the-horned-lambs-of-libya]]).

**The wealth of the flocks.** The second marvel follows from the first, as Merry reads the connection of τρὶς γάρ: the flocks bear three times “in the full year's round”, and the milk never fails (4.86–89; on 4.85; see [[sheep]]). The lambs are the land's plenty, and the plenty is what Menelaus gathered there: “While I, gathering much substance in those lands, / was wandering, meanwhile another man killed my brother” (4.90–91; see [[agamemnon]] and [[possessions]]).
