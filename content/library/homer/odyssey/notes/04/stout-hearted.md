---
summary: Helen and Menelaus both name him “stout-hearted Odysseus” as they begin their tales of Troy: “all the trials of stout-hearted Odysseus” (4.241), and “the dear heart of stout-hearted Odysseus” (4.270).
---

Ὀδυσσῆος ταλασίφρονος opens each tale, and the next verse is the same in both, “what a thing this was that the strong man did and endured” (4.242, 4.271; [[krateros]]): the epithet of endurance announces stories of a man who bore disfigurement in Troy and held himself and others still in the Horse ([[odysseus]], [[odysseus-enters-troy-as-a-beggar]], [[sinon-and-the-wooden-horse]]). Some read ταλασίφρονος for μεγαλήτορος at 4.143 as well (see [[great-hearted]]).
