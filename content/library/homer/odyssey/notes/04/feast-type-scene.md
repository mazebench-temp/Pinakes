summary: The guests' meal at Sparta runs through the Odyssey's pattern of welcome, from the bath and the chairs to the water, the bread, the meat and the cups, until they put out their hands and have eaten their fill; then comes talk, not song (4.47–75). After the weeping a second supper is told in three verses, and Helen's drugged wine and her stories take the place of the singer; the guests sleep in the porch, in the verses of Priam's night in Achilles' hut (4.212–239, 4.294–305).

**The guests' meal.** The strangers come in the middle of the wedding feast, but they are served a meal of their own (see [[the-wedding-at-sparta]] and [[xenia]]), and it follows the pattern of the guest's meal in Book 1 step by step:

1. **The bath.** They step into polished bathtubs, the slave women wash and anoint them and put cloaks and tunics on them (4.48–50; see [[bathing]] and [[cloak]]). The first verse is the bath of Odysseus and Diomedes after the night raid (*Iliad* 10.576), and the next two return when Telemachus and Theoclymenus bathe in Ithaca (17.88–89).
2. **The seats.** “they sat down on chairs beside Menelaus, son of Atreus” (4.51; see [[throne]]).
3. **Water, table, bread.** The five verses of the Odyssey's welcome (4.52–56 = 1.136–140; see [[water-for-the-hands]], [[basin-and-pitcher]] and [[table]]).
4. **Meat and cups.** A carver sets out platters of meat and golden cups (4.57–58 = 1.141–142; see [[cup]]). Most editors since Wolf reject the two verses here, which the Harleian and several other manuscripts lack (W. W. Merry's critical note; see the translation's note on 4.57–58).
5. **The host's word.** Menelaus greets them, bids them eat, and promises to ask afterward who they are (4.59–64), as Achilles greeted the embassy, τὼ καὶ δεικνύμενος προσέφη (4.59, as *Iliad* 9.196).
6. **The portion of honor.** He sets before them the chine that had been served to him (4.65–66; see [[feast]]).
7. **Eating.** “And they put out their hands to the good things lying ready before them” (4.67 = 1.149).
8. **Enough.** “But when they had put away the desire for drink and food” (4.68 = 1.150; see [[desire-for-drink-and-food]]).

What follows is not the song of the suitors' feast in Book 1 (1.150–155) but talk: Telemachus whispers his wonder at the house, and Menelaus answers him (4.69–112; see [[house]]).

**Supper again.** After the weeping the meal is taken up in a few verses: Menelaus bids them “remember supper again, and let them pour water / over our hands”, Asphalion pours the water, and the verse of eating comes again (4.213–218 = 4.67; see [[hand-washing]] and [[asphalion]]). There is no singer at this supper. Helen casts her drug into the wine and bids them “take delight in stories” (4.238–239; see [[nepenthe]]), and the stories of Odysseus at Troy are the evening's entertainment (4.240–289).

**Night.** The day ends as Priam's night in Achilles' hut ended: “so that now / we may lie down and take our fill of sweet sleep” (4.294–295, as *Iliad* 24.636), the beds set beneath the colonnade with blankets, rugs and woolly cloaks, the women going out with torches (4.297–300 = *Iliad* 24.644–647), and the guests sleeping “in the porch of the house” (4.302 = *Iliad* 24.673), while the master sleeps in the inner part of the house with his wife beside him (4.304–305, as Nestor in Book 3, 3.402–403; see [[bed]], [[blankets]] and [[torches]]).
