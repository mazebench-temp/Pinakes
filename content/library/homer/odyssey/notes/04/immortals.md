---
summary: In Menelaus' tale the gods are “the immortals, who hold the wide sky” (4.378, 4.479), who fetter him on Pharos (4.380, 4.469), who will send him to the Elysian plain (4.564) and who sent him home (4.586); Proteus himself is “immortal” (4.385); and Menelaus calls Zeus' house and possessions immortal (4.79).
---

ἀθάνατοι, in the verse-end ἀθανάτους, οἳ οὐρανὸν εὐρὺν ἔχουσιν, is Menelaus' word for the powers he has offended and must appease with hecatombs (4.377–380, 4.472–480; [[wide-sky]]). Proteus is ἀθάνατος Πρωτεὺς Αἰγύπτιος, “immortal Proteus of Egypt” (4.385; [[proteus]]), and the immortals' decree for Menelaus is the Elysian plain (4.561–569; [[elysium]]).
