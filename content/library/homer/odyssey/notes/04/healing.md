# ἰητρός (4.231), first met here in the Odyssey.
grc: [ἰητρὸς]
---

{{quote:26e5ad21-3be0-5751-803f-bcab26f849e0}}

The drug Helen cast into the wine was one of these, given to her by Polydamna, the wife of Thon (see [[nepenthe]], [[polydamna]] and [[egypt]]). ἰητρός is the Iliad's word for the healer, as when Idomeneus says of Machaon, “for a healer is a man worth many others” (*Iliad* 11.514; see the translation's row ἰητρός), and Paeëon is the healer of the gods, who cured Hades and Ares in the Iliad (*Iliad* 5.401, 5.899–900; see [[paeeon]]). μεμιγμένα, “intermixed”, means that the wholesome and the harmful grow together (W. W. Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 4.230). Herodotus too found Egypt full of physicians, each for one disease and no more, of the eyes, of the head, of the teeth (Herodotus 2.84; see [[herodotus]]).
