---
summary: Menelaus answers Telemachus' praise of his house: “no mortal could rival Zeus”, but among men there may be one who will rival him in possessions, or none (4.78–81). Odysseus once stood up at Lesbos and wrestled Philomeleides “out of strife” (4.343).
grc: [ἐρίζοι, ἐρίσσεται]
en: [rival]
---

ἐρίζω is to contend with someone, and so to rival him: “Dear children, truly no mortal could rival Zeus; / for his house and his possessions are immortal; / but among men there may be one who will rival me, or there may be none, / in possessions” (4.78–81; see [[olbos]] and [[possessions]]). ἐξ ἔριδος, “out of strife”, is the challenge in which Odysseus wrestled Philomeleides and threw him (4.342–344; see [[odysseus-wrestles-philomeleides]]), the phrase with which Agamemnon warns Menelaus not to fight Hector in the Iliad (*Iliad* 7.111; see the translation's table of key words).
