---
summary: Telemachus' house is full of “the suitors of my mother” (4.321); his father's mother bore him “to misery” (4.325); Helen's drug holds back tears even for a mother dead (4.224).
---

Telemachus tells Menelaus of “the suitors of my mother, overweening in their arrogance” (4.321; see [[hybris]] and [[the-suitors]]), and says of his father, “for beyond all men his mother bore him to misery” (4.325, = 3.95). Helen's drug would keep a man from tears “not even if his mother and his father lay dead” (4.224; see [[nepenthe]]). The book's mother is Penelope, who learns that her son has sailed (4.703–841; see [[mothers-and-sons]]).
