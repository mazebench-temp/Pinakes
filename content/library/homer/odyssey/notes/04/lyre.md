---
summary: At the wedding feast in Menelaus' house a divine singer sings to the lyre while two tumblers whirl among the guests (4.17–19).
---

“and among them a divine singer sang / to the lyre” (φορμίζων, 4.17–18; see [[the-wedding-at-sparta]], [[divine]] and [[tumblers]]), the words of the singer on the shield of Achilles (*Iliad* 18.604–605; see [[the-singer-on-the-shield]]). φορμίζων was the word of Phemius in Book 1, “playing the lyre” for the suitors (1.155; see [[phemius]]). The singer at Sparta is not named, and after the opening verses he is not heard again (see [[feast-type-scene]]).
