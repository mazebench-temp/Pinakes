---
summary: Herodotus quotes this book three times, the drugs that Polydamna gave Helen and the gods who held Menelaus in Egypt, as proof that Homer knew the Egyptian story of Helen, and the lambs of Libya, horned at once, as proof that horns grow quickly in hot lands. His Egypt has a King Proteus at Memphis, a warden of the Nile called Thonis, and a people full of physicians.
---

- **Helen in Egypt.** The drugs of Polydamna, the wife of Thon, and Menelaus held back in Egypt by the gods, quoted to show that Homer knew Helen had been in Egypt (*Histories* 2.116.4–5, quoting 4.227–230 and 4.351–352); Proteus, a man of Memphis and king of Egypt, and the warden of the Canopic mouth, Thonis (*Histories* 2.112–115); and Menelaus held in Egypt by calms, who sacrifices two Egyptian children and flees toward Libya (*Histories* 2.119): [[helen-in-egypt]], [[proteus]] and [[nepenthe]].
- **The lambs of Libya.** “Libya, where the lambs are horned at once”, quoted with ὅθι for ἵνα and called rightly said, since horns come quickly in hot lands and hardly at all in the cold of Scythia (*Histories* 4.29, quoting 4.85): [[the-horned-lambs-of-libya]].
- **A people of physicians.** In Egypt, Herodotus says, each physician treats one disease and no more, and the whole country is full of physicians, for the eyes, the head, the teeth, the belly and the hidden diseases (*Histories* 2.84); the Egyptians purge themselves three days in every month, believing that all diseases come from food, and are the healthiest of men after the Libyans (*Histories* 2.77). Merry sets both beside this book's Egypt, where “every man there is a healer, skilled beyond all / other men” (4.231–232; Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 4.231): [[nepenthe]].
