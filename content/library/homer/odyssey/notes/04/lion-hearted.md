---
summary: Penelope's lament for “my brave, lion-hearted husband,” first among her women and again to the phantom in her dream (4.724, 4.814).
---

The three verses of her loss are repeated whole, from “I who first lost my brave, lion-hearted husband” to his fame “through Hellas and the heart of Argos” (4.724–726 = 4.814–816; [[brave]], [[kleos]]). The epithet of Heracles and Achilles in the Iliad is here Odysseus' ([[odysseus]], [[penelope]]); in the same book Menelaus likens him to a lion coming home to its lair (4.333–340), and Penelope herself, pondering, is a lion encircled by men (4.791–793; [[lion]]).
