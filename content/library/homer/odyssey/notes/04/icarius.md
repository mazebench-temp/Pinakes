# Unnamed: "my father", who gave Penelope her slave Dolius (4.736).
refs: [4.736]
---

**A second daughter.** The phantom Athena sends to Penelope is like “Iphthime, daughter of great-hearted Icarius, / whom Eumelus had married, who had his home in Pherae” (4.797–798; see [[iphthime]], [[great-hearted]] and [[eumelus]]). The book thus gives Icarius two daughters, married into houses far apart, Penelope in Ithaca and her sister in Thessaly (4.810–811; see [[penelope]]). When the dream has gone, Penelope wakes as κούρη Ἰκαρίοιο, “the daughter of Icarius” (4.840; see [[daughter-of-icarius]]). In her lament, Dolius is the slave “whom my father gave me when I came here” (4.736; see [[dolius]]).

**Not at Sparta.** This is the book of Telemachus' visit to Lacedaemon, and the poem never mentions a grandfather there: Menelaus and Helen receive him, and no one speaks of Icarius. Critics who took Icarius for a Spartan found that absurd, and Aristotle answered that Penelope's father may have been Icadius of Cephallenia (see [[the-icarius-problem]] and [[icadius]]).
