When Helen has cast the drug into the wine, the poem tells where it came from: “Such cunning drugs the daughter of Zeus had, / good ones, which Polydamna had given her, the wife of Thon, / a woman of Egypt” (4.227–229; see [[nepenthe]], [[helen]] and [[thon]]). μητιόεντα, “cunning”, Merry would render “helpful”, with Göbel, of drugs that give counsel and help for every human need (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 4.227); for Thon he points to Strabo's town Thonis near Canopus, built by a king Thon, and to Herodotus (on 4.228; see [[thon]]). The gift ties Helen to Egypt, where she and Menelaus stayed on his way home and where Alcandre of Thebes gave her the golden distaff and the basket (4.125–132; see [[alcandre]] and [[egypt]]).
