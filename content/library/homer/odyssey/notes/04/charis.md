---
summary: Penelope ends her rebuke of the suitors, who waste the house of a king who never wronged them: “and there is no thanks afterward for good deeds done” (4.695).
grc: [χάρις]
---

χάρις is the thanks that a service earns, and its absence is a grievance. Penelope's maxim (4.695; see [[no-thanks-for-good-deeds]]) answers the suitors' treatment of Odysseus, who did nothing excessive to any of their fathers (4.687–694; see [[kingship-and-authority]]); Achilles makes the same complaint in the Iliad, “there was, it seems, no thanks / for fighting against the enemy without rest, forever” (*Iliad* 9.316–317), and Leodes repeats Penelope's half-verse as he begs Odysseus for his life (22.319). At the welcome in Sparta the housekeeper sets out bread and many dainties, χαριζομένη παρεόντων, “giving freely of what she had” (4.55–56, as 1.140; see [[xenia]]).
