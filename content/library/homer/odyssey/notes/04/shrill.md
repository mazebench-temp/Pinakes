summary: Pharos lies a day's sail off “when a shrill fair wind blows behind” a ship (4.357); in the Elysian plain the West Wind blows “shrill” (4.567); and the Trojan women “wailed shrilly” when Odysseus had killed many Trojans (4.259).
grc: ["λιγὺ πνείοντος", "λίγʼ ἐκώκυον"]
en: [shrilly]
---

λιγὺς οὖρος (4.357) and Ζεφύροιο λιγὺ πνείοντος (4.567) are the sound of the wind ([[zephyrus]], [[elysium]]); λίγʼ ἐκώκυον is the Iliad's verb of Briseis' wailing for Patroclus (*Iliad* 19.284), here the Trojan women's for their dead (4.259; [[lament]]).
