summary: Phylo brings Helen her silver work basket on wheels, filled with spun yarn, with the golden distaff laid across it, the gifts of Alcandre of Egyptian Thebes (4.125–135).
# Unnamed: none; she is named at both mentions (4.125, 4.133).
passages:
  - 4.120-137 | Helen comes into the hall
---

Helen comes from her fragrant chamber “like Artemis of the golden distaff” (4.122; see [[helen]] and [[artemis]]), and her three women bring the things of her seat and her work: Adreste a chair, Alcippe a rug, “and Phylo brought a silver basket, which had been given her / by Alcandre, the wife of Polybus, who lived in Thebes / in Egypt” (4.123–127; see [[adreste]], [[alcippe]], [[alcandre]] and [[polybus-of-thebes]]). The poem tells the history of the gifts, the bathtubs, tripods and gold that Polybus gave Menelaus, and the golden distaff and the basket on wheels that his wife gave Helen (4.128–132), and comes back to the handmaid: “This the handmaid Phylo brought and set beside her, / filled with finely spun yarn; and across it / lay the distaff, laden with violet-dark wool” (4.133–135). The basket was ὑπόκυκλος, “on wheels”, with castors underneath so that it could be moved about, as Apollonius and Hesychius explain it, against Eustathius' “round” (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 4.131).
