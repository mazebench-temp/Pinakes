summary: The weeping after Menelaus' speech, “Argive Helen wept, child of Zeus, / and Telemachus wept” (4.184–185, κλαῖε μὲν … κλαῖε δέ); the power of Helen's drug, “not even if … not even if” (4.224–225, οὐδʼ εἰ … οὐδʼ εἰ); and Menelaus' promise of the bowl, “I will give you … I will give you” (4.614–615, δώσω … δώσω).
refs: [4.184-185, 4.224-225, 4.614-615]
---

The repeated verb at the start of the verse makes the four mourners one company (4.184–186; [[the-longing-for-lament]]); the repeated condition sets out the two worst griefs a man could see, the deaths of parents and of a brother or son, and the drug outweighs both (4.224–226; [[nepenthe]]); and the repeated promise marks the change of gift, from horses to the finest treasure of the house (4.612–619; [[mixing-bowl]]).
