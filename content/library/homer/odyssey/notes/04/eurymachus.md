summary: Eurymachus sits with Antinous before the hall of Odysseus as one of the two leaders of the suitors when Noemon's question reveals that Telemachus has sailed, and the news angers them both (4.628–629, 4.658).
# Εὐρύμαχος (4.628) is claimed by the entry. Unnamed: "leaders of the suitors" (4.629) and "the proud spirit in both
# of them" (4.658).
refs: [4.629, 4.658]
---

“And Antinous was sitting there, and godlike Eurymachus, / leaders of the suitors, and in excellence they were by far the best” (4.628–629; see [[antinous]], [[godlike]] and [[the-suitors]]). The verse that ranks them comes again at the contest of the bow (21.187). καθῆστο keeps the singular, though it has two subjects (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 4.628). When Noemon has gone home, “the proud spirit in both of them was indignant” (4.658), Antinous and Eurymachus (Merry on 4.658; see [[noemon-son-of-phronius]]); but it is Antinous who speaks and leads the ambush, and Eurymachus is not named again in the book (4.660–672; see [[the-journey-of-telemachus]]).
