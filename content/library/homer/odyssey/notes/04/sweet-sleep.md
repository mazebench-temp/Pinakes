---
summary: Telemachus asks to go to bed, “so that now / we may lie down and take our fill of sweet sleep” (4.294–295); and as Penelope ponders, “sweet sleep came upon her” (4.793).
grc: ["ὕπνῳ ὕπο γλυκερῷ"]
---

Merry takes ὕπνῳ ὕπο … κοιμηθέντες as “lulled beneath the influence of sleep”, as in the metaphor δεδμημένος ὕπνῳ (*Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 4.295). νήδυμος ὕπνος ends Penelope's pondering and brings the dream (4.793–794; [[sleep]], [[dream-scene]]).
