summary: The bedding Helen has made up for the guests beneath the colonnade: fine purple blankets on the beds, rugs spread over them, and woolly cloaks on top (4.297–299 = Iliad 24.644–646). Helen's handmaid Alcippe brings her a rug of soft wool for her chair (4.124).
# τάπης, the woolen rug or coverlet laid on a bed or a chair: τάπητας (4.298), τάπητα (4.124), first met here in the
# Odyssey; the Iliad's τάπης and τάπητας (Il. 9.200, 10.156, 16.224, 24.230, 24.645) are not claimed.
grc: [τάπητας, τάπητα]
en: [rugs]

**The guests' bedding.** Helen bids the slave women “set beds beneath the colonnade, and throw fine purple / blankets on them, and spread rugs over them, / and put woolly cloaks on top for them to wear” (4.297–299; see [[bed]], [[cloak]] and [[purple]]). The three verses are Achilles' order for Priam's bed in the Iliad (*Iliad* 24.644–646), and they return when Arete has a bed made for Odysseus (7.336–338). W. W. Merry describes the layers in his note on Nestor's boast that his house has cloaks and blankets enough for guests: the ῥήγεα were costly blankets, fine, thick and soft; over them, for the sleeper to lie on, a sheet of linen or the still more luxurious woolen coverlet, τάπης; and the χλαῖνα was the counterpane laid over the sleeper (*Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 3.349, citing 4.124, 4.298 and 4.299; see the Book 3 note).

**A rug for a chair.** The τάπης is laid on chairs as well. When Helen comes into the hall, “Alcippe brought a rug of soft wool” for the well-made chair that Adreste set for her (4.123–124; see [[alcippe]], [[adreste]] and [[throne]]), as rugs are laid on the chairs in Achilles' hut (*Iliad* 9.200, as Merry cites it).
