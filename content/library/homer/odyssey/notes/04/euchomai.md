---
summary: Helen asks Menelaus who the strangers “claim to be” (4.139), and Eurycleia sends Penelope up to her room to “pray to Athena” (4.752).
---

The verb has its two senses in the book. Helen asks who the young men εὐχετόωνται, “claim to be” (4.138–139; see [[helen]]), and Eurycleia bids Penelope bathe, go up with her women, and εὔχεʼ Ἀθηναίῃ, “pray to Athena”, who might save her son even from death (4.750–753; see [[eurycleia]]). Penelope's prayer itself is told with ἀράομαι: ἠρᾶτο δʼ Ἀθήνῃ, “and prayed to Athena”, and the goddess “heard her prayer” (ἀρῆς, 4.761–767; see [[prayer]] and [[reciprocity-with-the-gods]]).
