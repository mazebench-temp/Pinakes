---
summary: Menelaus wishes that Odysseus might come among the suitors such as he was once “in well-built Lesbos”, when he stood up to wrestle Philomeleides and threw him, and all the Achaeans rejoiced (4.341–346).
---

{{quote:433cb27a-1645-5c36-b446-9219162ca416}}

Lesbos is the island where, as Nestor told in the last book, the kings met on the voyage home (3.169); when the match was held the poem does not say. Eustathius, Merry reports, says that the more careful authorities made Philomeleides a king of Lesbos who challenged all who came there to wrestle, and so challenged the Achaeans when they put in at the island; ἐξ ἔριδος is “in a match” or “after a challenge” (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 4.343; see [[philomeleides]] and [[odysseus-wrestles-philomeleides]]). ἐυκτιμένῃ, “well-built”, is the Iliad's epithet of the island too (*Iliad* 9.129; see [[well-built]]). Telemachus repeats the whole wish to his mother when he comes home (17.132–135).
