---
summary: Helen is “child of Zeus” as she weeps with the others and as she thinks of the drug (4.184, 4.219), and “the daughter of Zeus” who had such drugs (4.227); Athena is “daughter of aegis-bearing Zeus” when Eurycleia bids Penelope pray to her (4.752).
---

The two titles of Helen come in the scene of the drug. Ἑλένη Διὸς ἐκγεγαυῖα is her verse-end in both poems (4.184, 4.219; 23.218; [[argive-helen]]); Διὸς θυγάτηρ, the Iliad's title of Aphrodite, is given to her as the owner of “cunning drugs”, the gift of an Egyptian woman (4.227–228; [[helen]], [[nepenthe]]). Athena is κούρη Διὸς αἰγιόχοιο in Eurycleia's counsel (4.752; [[aegis-bearing]], [[child-of-aegis-bearing-zeus]]), and Penelope addresses her so in her prayer (4.762).
