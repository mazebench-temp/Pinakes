---
summary: “Noble” three times: Menelaus' daughter at her wedding (4.4), Antilochus remembered by his brother (4.187), and Telemachus in his mother's fear (4.789).
---

ἀμύμων is an epithet of birth and beauty here, as of Aegisthus in Zeus' speech (1.29; see the translation's note on 1.29). At 4.4 Bekker, in his second edition, read ἀμύμονα, since the possessive ὅς always had the initial digamma (Merry's critical note on 4.4). Peisistratus remembers “noble Antilochus” (4.187; [[antilochus]]), and Penelope ponders whether “her noble son would escape death” (4.789; [[telemachus]]).
