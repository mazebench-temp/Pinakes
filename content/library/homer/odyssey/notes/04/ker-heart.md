---
summary: Helen's heart “rejoiced” while the Trojan women wailed (4.259–260); Menelaus never saw “such a one / as was the dear heart of stout-hearted Odysseus” (4.269–270); on the sand his heart “no longer wished to live” (4.539–540).
---

κῆρ is the heart in which one rejoices and grieves. Helen's heart rejoiced while the Trojan women wailed, “since already my heart was turned to going / back home” (4.259–261; see [[ate]] and [[kradie]]). Menelaus' praise of Odysseus names the man by his heart, Ὀδυσσῆος ... φίλον κῆρ (4.270): Merry takes the phrase as a periphrasis for Odysseus himself, like “Pylaemenes of the shaggy heart” in the Iliad (*Iliad* 2.851; *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 4.269). At the news of his brother's death, “my heart / no longer wished to live and see the light of the sun” (4.539–540; see [[tears]]).
