summary: Menelaus ponders whether to let Telemachus speak of his father or “first question him and put every point to the test” (4.119); Proteus “will make his attempt” by becoming every creature (4.417); and Proteus bids Menelaus stop weeping and “try how you may reach your native land” (4.545).
grc: [πειρήσαιτο, πειρήσεται, πείρα]
en: [try]
---

πειράω is to test and to try. Menelaus, seeing the stranger weep, weighs whether to wait or to question him and ἕκαστά τε πειρήσαιτο, “put every point to the test” (4.118–119); Merry takes ἕκαστα as the object, “should test all he said”, against Ameis' “in every particular” (*Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 4.119; see the translation's note on 4.119). Eidothea warns that her father will try every shape to escape, πάντα δὲ γιγνόμενος πειρήσεται, “He will make his attempt by becoming all the things that upon the earth / creep, and water, and wondrous fire” (4.417–418; see [[proteus]] and [[shape-changing]]). When Menelaus has wept his fill, Proteus bids him, ἀλλὰ τάχιστα / πείρα ὅπως κεν δὴ σὴν πατρίδα γαῖαν ἵκηαι, “try how you may reach your native land” (4.544–545; see [[nostos]]).
