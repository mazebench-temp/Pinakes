---
summary: Menelaus, “good at the war cry,” rises at dawn to ask Telemachus why he has come (4.307), and smiles when the young man refuses his horses (4.609).
---

The warrior's epithet comes twice in scenes of peace: in the dressing verses at dawn, which end with Menelaus going out of his chamber “like a god to look upon” (4.306–310; [[dressing-scene]], [[like-a-god-to-look-upon]]), and in the smile with which he takes Telemachus' refusal, “You are of good blood, dear child, by the way you speak” (4.609–611; [[smiling-at-him]], [[called-him-by-name]]).
