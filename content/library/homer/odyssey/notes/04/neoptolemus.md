# Unnamed: "the son of Achilles" (4.5), the promise made at Troy (4.6-7), and the king of the Myrmidons (4.9).
refs: [4.5-9]
---

“The daughter he was sending to the son of Achilles, breaker of men; / for in Troy he had first promised and nodded his assent / that he would give her, and now the gods were bringing their wedding to pass” (4.5–7; see [[hermione-daughter-of-helen]], [[menelaus]], [[achilles]] and [[breaker-of-men]]). The son is not named, and he does not appear: the bride is sent to him with horses and chariots “to the renowned city of the Myrmidons, over whom he ruled” (4.8–9; see [[myrmidons]] and [[the-wedding-at-sparta]]), that is, as king of Phthia (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 4.9). In the previous book Nestor had heard that he brought the Myrmidons home safely (3.188–189), and Odysseus will tell his father's shade that he fetched him from Scyros himself (11.505–509). Later poets gave him Orestes for a rival for Hermione's hand (see [[hermione-daughter-of-helen]] and [[orestes]]).
