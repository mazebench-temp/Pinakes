# The phrase of the singer leading (4.19), where the Shield has the plural, of the tumblers (Il. 18.606, claimed in the
# Iliad Book 18 note).
grc: ["μολπῆς ἐξάρχοντος"]
en: [song and dance]
---

“and two tumblers among them, / as he led the song and dance, whirled in the midst” (4.18–19; see [[the-wedding-at-sparta]] and [[tumblers]]). the genitive is the singer's, who leads the song and dance with his lyre, as Thetis led the lament of the Nereids (ἐξῆρχε γόοιο, *Iliad* 18.51; W. W. Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 4.19); on the Shield the tumblers lead (*Iliad* 18.606; see [[the-singer-on-the-shield]] and the translation's note on 4.19). In Book 1 the suitors turned after their meal to “song and dancing; for these are the accompaniments of a feast” (1.152; see [[feast-type-scene]]).
