---
summary: Hades is named once, in Penelope's question to the phantom of her sister: whether Odysseus still lives and looks on the light of the sun, “or is already dead and in the house of Hades”.
---

**Alive or dead.** When the phantom has told her that Telemachus will come home, Penelope asks it for the other news she wants: “come, tell me also of that wretched man, / whether he still lives somewhere and looks on the light of the sun, / or is already dead and in the house of Hades” (4.832–834; see [[penelope]] and [[eidolon]]). εἰν Ἀίδαο δόμοισι, “in the house of Hades”, is the Iliad's phrase too: Priam says it of his sons Lycaon and Polydorus, “But if they are already dead and in the house of Hades” (*Iliad* 22.52). The pair of verses returns in the Odyssey, with small changes, whenever the absent are in question: the disguised Odysseus asks Eumaeus about the mother and father of Odysseus (15.347–350), Philoetius says it of his absent master (20.205–208), and Odysseus, in his false tale to Laertes, of the guest-friend he claims to have entertained (24.262–264). Its first half is the Iliad's phrase for life itself, “lives and looks on the light of the sun” (*Iliad* 18.61; see [[helios]]); Menelaus on the sands of Pharos “no longer wished to live and see the light of the sun” when he heard of his brother's murder (4.540). The phantom will not answer: “Of him I will not tell you from beginning to end, / whether he is alive or dead” (4.836–837), Menelaus' own words for what he did not know (4.110; see [[anemolios]]).
