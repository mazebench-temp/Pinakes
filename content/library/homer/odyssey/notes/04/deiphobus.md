summary: In Menelaus' tale of the Wooden Horse, “godlike Deiphobus followed” Helen when she came to the Horse and called the chieftains by name in their wives' voices (4.274–279).
# Δηΐφοβος (4.276) is claimed by the entry.
passages:
  - 4.271-289 | Menelaus' tale of the Horse
---

“Then you came there; some god must have bidden you, / a god who meant to hold out glory to the Trojans; / and godlike Deiphobus followed you as you went” (4.274–276; see [[helen]], [[menelaus]] and [[godlike]]). He is the son of Priam whom the Iliad makes the dearest of his brothers to Hector (*Iliad* 12.94, 13.156, 22.233–234). The poem does not say why he goes with Helen; in later legend he married her after the death of Paris, and Merry points to Euripides' *Trojan Women* (959–960) and reports that some ancient critics athetized the verse, doubtless because it seemed to suggest that later form of the story (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 4.276, and his critical note; see [[paris]]). Menelaus says no more of him here. Demodocus' song of the sack of Troy tells how Odysseus and Menelaus went to the house of Deiphobus when the Achaeans poured out of the Horse (8.517–520).
