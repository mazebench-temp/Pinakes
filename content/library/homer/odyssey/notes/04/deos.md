---
summary: Penelope, fasting, ponders like a lion “in fear” when men draw their circle around him (4.791–792); she tells the phantom that she trembles and fears for her son (4.820), and it bids her not be “at all too much afraid” (4.825).
grc: [δείδια, δείδιθι]
---

The simile gives Penelope's fear to the lion, who ponders δείσας, “in fear”, in a throng of hunters (4.791–793; see [[the-lion-encircled]] and [[mothers-and-sons]]). In her dream she says of her son, τοῦ δʼ ἀμφιτρομέω καὶ δείδια, “for him I tremble and fear lest something happen to him” (4.820), and the phantom answers, θάρσει, μηδέ τι πάγχυ μετὰ φρεσὶ δείδιθι λίην, “Take heart, and do not be at all too much afraid in your mind” (4.825; see [[tharsos]] and [[eidolon]]).
