summary: The guests at Sparta wash their hands in the verses of the welcome (4.52–54), and at supper after the weeping Menelaus bids the attendants pour water over their hands, and Asphalion pours it (4.213–217).
# "χερσὶ δʼ ἐφʼ ὕδωρ" (4.213) and "ὕδωρ ἐπὶ χεῖρας ἔχευεν" (4.216), the singular of the Book 1 note's ἔχευαν.
grc: ["χερσὶ δʼ ἐφʼ ὕδωρ", "ὕδωρ ἐπὶ χεῖρας ἔχευεν"]
en: [pour water]
---

**Before the guests' meal.** A handmaid pours the water for the hands over a silver basin, “for them to wash” (4.52–54 = 1.136–138; see [[water-for-the-hands]] and [[basin-and-pitcher]]).

**Before the second supper.** When the weeping for Odysseus and Antilochus is over, Menelaus says: “let us remember supper again, and let them pour water / over our hands” (4.213–214); χευάντων has an indefinite subject, as often, the attendants who serve (W. W. Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 4.214). “So he spoke, and Asphalion poured water over their hands, / the ready attendant of glorious Menelaus” (4.216–217; see [[asphalion]] and [[feast-type-scene]]). In Book 1 the heralds poured the water over the suitors' hands in the plural of the same verse (1.146; *Iliad* 9.174; see [[heralds]]).
