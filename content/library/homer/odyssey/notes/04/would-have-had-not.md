summary: Three times in Menelaus' tale: “And now all our provisions would have been spent, and the strength of the men, / had not one of the gods pitied me and saved me” (4.363–364); “There our ambush would have been most dread” but for Eidothea's ambrosia (4.441–446); and Ajax “would have escaped his doom, hated though he was by Athena, / had he not let fall an overbearing word” (4.502–503).
grc: ["ἔνθα κεν αἰνότατος λόχος ἔπλετο"]
---

The one who turns the story is named each time: Eidothea saves Menelaus' men twice (4.364, 4.444; [[eidothea]]), and Ajax's own word brings Poseidon's trident (4.503–507; [[the-death-of-ajax-son-of-oileus]]). At 4.441 the scholia's lemma read κεῖθι δὴ αἰνότατος, but most copies have ἔνθα κεν, which Bekker restored (see the translation's note on 4.441).
