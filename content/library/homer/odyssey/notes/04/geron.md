summary: The old men of the book: “the old man Laertes” mourning his son (4.111), “an afflicted old man” (4.754); “old Nestor”, granted to grow old in ease in his halls (4.191, 4.210); old Dolius (4.735); and Proteus, the Old Man of the Sea (4.349 and often).
grc: [γηρασκέμεν]
en: [grow old]
---

Laertes is “the old man” who mourns Odysseus (4.111), and Eurycleia begs Penelope not to trouble him: μηδὲ γέροντα κάκου κεκακωμένον, “And do not afflict an afflicted old man” (4.754; see [[laertes]]). Penelope would send to him “old Dolius”, the slave her father gave her (4.735–737; see [[dolius]]). Nestor, “old Nestor”, used to praise Menelaus' wisdom in his halls (4.190–192), and Zeus has granted him to grow old in ease there (4.209–210; see [[easily-known-is-the-offspring]] and [[nestor]]). The book's other old man is a god: Eidothea calls her father “the divine old man” and Menelaus addresses him as γέρον (4.395, 4.465, 4.485; see [[proteus]] and [[unerring-old-man-of-the-sea]]).
