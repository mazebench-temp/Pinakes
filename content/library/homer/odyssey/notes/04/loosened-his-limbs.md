---
summary: At Medon's news “her knees and dear heart were loosened where she was” (4.703), and at the end of her pondering Penelope “slept, leaning back, and all her limbs were loosened” (4.794).
grc: ["λύτο γούνατα καὶ φίλον ἦτορ", "λύθεν δέ οἱ ἅψεα πάντα"]
en: [knees and dear heart were loosened, all her limbs were loosened]
---

The loosening of terror and grief, λύτο γούνατα καὶ φίλον ἦτορ, is Lycaon's verse before Achilles (*Iliad* 21.114); the loosening of sleep, λύθεν δέ οἱ ἅψεα πάντα, is Penelope's here and again in Book 18 (18.189; [[penelope]], [[sweet-sleep]]).
