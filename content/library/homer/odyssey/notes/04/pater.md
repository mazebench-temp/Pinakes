summary: The book is a son's search for news of his father (4.113–119, 4.317, 4.701, 4.714). Menelaus praises Peisistratus as his father's son (4.206), Eidothea tricks her father Proteus (4.387, 4.437), and Penelope reminds the suitors that they heard from their fathers what kind of king Odysseus was (4.687–689).
grc: [πατέρων, πατρῶν]
en: [fathers]
---

Telemachus weeps at his father's name (4.113–116), comes to Sparta for “some report of my father” (4.317), and is said in Ithaca to have gone “in search of news of his father” (4.701; see [[fathers-and-sons]]). Menelaus tells the young men that they must have heard of his own losses “from your fathers, whoever they are” (4.94–95), and praises Peisistratus for speaking as his father does (4.206; see [[easily-known-is-the-offspring]]). Eidothea says of Proteus, “they say that he is my father, who begot me”, and then devises a trick against him (4.387, 4.437; see [[eidothea]]). Penelope tells the suitors, through Medon, that they never heard “from your fathers before, when you were children, / what kind of man Odysseus was among your parents” (4.687–689; see [[kingship-and-authority]]); Merry renders τὸ πρόσθεν “in the days of your childhood” (*Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 4.688).
