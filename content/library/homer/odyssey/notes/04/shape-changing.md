**Changes and likenesses.** The book sets the changes of a god beside the disguises of mortals. Helen tells how Odysseus marred himself with blows, threw rags about his shoulders “like a servant”, and “hiding himself he made himself like another man, / a beggar” to enter Troy (4.244–249; see [[odysseus-enters-troy-as-a-beggar]]), and Menelaus how Helen walked round the Wooden Horse “making your voice like the voices of the wives of all the Argives” (4.279; see [[helen]]). The next morning he tells of the god who can become anything: lion, serpent, leopard and boar, water and a tree (4.455–459; see [[proteus]]). In Ithaca Noemon has seen “Mentor, or a god, who was like him in every way” (4.654; see [[athena]] and [[in-the-likeness-of-mentor]]), and at night Athena makes a phantom “like a woman in form, / Iphthime” (4.796–797; see [[eidolon]]). The mortals change their looks or their voices; the old god of the sea changes his nature, and is held fast until he takes back his own shape (4.420–424, 4.460–461).
