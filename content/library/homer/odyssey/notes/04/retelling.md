summary: Characters repeat their own words: Telemachus' plea to Menelaus is the one he made to Nestor (4.322–331 = 3.92–101); Menelaus asks Proteus in the verses he used to Eidothea (4.468–470 = 4.379–381); and Penelope tells the phantom of her loss in the verses of her lament to her women (4.814–816 = 4.724–726).
refs: [4.322-331, 4.468-470, 4.814-816]

What is kept is the point: Telemachus' request for the truth about his father is the same at Pylos and at Sparta ([[he-will-not-tell-a-lie]]); Menelaus' question about the god who fetters him and his homecoming has to be put to the one who can answer it ([[proteus]], [[fish-filled]]); and Penelope's three verses on her husband are followed each time by the son she fears for (4.727–728, 4.817–818; [[lion-hearted]], [[agapetos]]).
