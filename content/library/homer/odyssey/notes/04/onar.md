---
summary: Penelope, asleep, answers her sister's phantom “slumbering very sweetly at the gates of dreams” (4.809), and wakes with her heart warmed, “so clear was the dream that had rushed upon her in the dead of night” (4.841).
grc: [ὀνειρείῃσι]
en: [dreams]
---

Athena makes a phantom in the likeness of Iphthime and sends it into Penelope's chamber as she sleeps; it stands above her head and speaks (4.795–807; see [[eidolon]], [[iphthime]] and [[dream-scene]]). Merry observes that in Homer the dream is not merely the creation of the sleeper's mind but has a real existence apart from him (*Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 4.796). Penelope answers it ἐν ὀνειρείῃσι πύλῃσιν, “at the gates of dreams” (4.809), for which Merry compares her own gates of horn and ivory in Book 19 (on 4.809; 19.562–567). When it has gone into the winds she wakes, “so clear was the dream” (ἐναργὲς ὄνειρον, 4.841; see [[dream]]): the phantom is ἀμαυρόν, “dim” (4.824, 4.835), but, Merry says, the dream, though dim to the bodily sense, had a clear meaning to the mind (on 4.841).
