summary: Helen's golden distaff and silver basket of finely spun yarn, gifts from Egypt, are set beside her when she comes into the hall (4.125–135). In Ithaca the suitors “were weaving their plan”, and Penelope hopes that Laertes may “weave some plan in his mind” (4.678, 4.739).
---

**Helen's work.** When Helen comes into the hall, Phylo sets beside her the silver basket “filled with finely spun yarn; and across it / lay the distaff, laden with violet-dark wool” (4.133–135; see [[distaff]], [[basket]] and [[helen]]). W. W. Merry notes that Helen was famous as a workwoman (*Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 4.132–135); in the Iliad she was weaving a great web of the battles at Troy (*Iliad* 3.125–128).

**Weaving a plan.** The suitors, plotting the ambush inside the house, “were weaving their plan” (μῆτιν ὕφαινον, 4.678), and Penelope hopes that Laertes “may perhaps weave some plan in his mind” (4.739; see [[metis]] and [[laertes]]). The metaphor is the Iliad's, where Nestor “first of all began to weave a plan” for the kings (*Iliad* 7.324, 9.93); in the Odyssey the weaver of plans is also the weaver of the web (see [[the-web-of-penelope]]).
