---
summary: The θυμός ponders, wishes, endures and is indignant: Menelaus ponders “in his mind and in his heart” (4.117, 4.120), Helen's “heart bids” her speak (4.140), Telemachus is indignant “in his heart” (4.158), Menelaus' men wait “with enduring heart” (4.447, 4.459), and the “proud spirit” of the suitors' leaders is indignant at the news (4.658).
---

The θυμός is where the book's people decide and feel. Menelaus ponders κατὰ φρένα καὶ κατὰ θυμόν whether to question the weeping stranger (4.117, 4.120; see [[phrenes]]); Helen says κέλεται δέ με θυμός, “my heart bids me speak” (4.140); Telemachus is indignant in his heart at forward talk (4.158; see [[nemesis]]); Peisistratus remembers his brother κατὰ θυμόν (4.187); Menelaus and his men wait in the sealskins and hold Proteus τετληότι θυμῷ, “with enduring heart” (4.447, 4.459; see [[enduring]]). In Ithaca the suitors are amazed ἀνὰ θυμόν (4.638), the θυμὸς ἀγήνωρ, “proud spirit”, of Antinous and Eurymachus is indignant (4.658; see [[proud]]), and Penelope sets their θυμός and their shameful deeds against Odysseus' justice (4.694; see [[atasthalie]]). Medon cannot say whether a god roused Telemachus “or whether his own / heart was eager” (4.712–713; see [[who-is-to-blame]]).
