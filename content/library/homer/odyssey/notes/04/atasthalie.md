---
summary: Penelope reminds the suitors through Medon that Odysseus “never did anything at all reckless to a man” (4.693).
grc: [ἀτάσθαλον]
en: [reckless]
---

Odysseus did nothing excessive to anyone among the people, though that is the way of divine kings, to hate one man and love another, “But he never did anything at all reckless to a man” (4.690–693; see [[kingship-and-authority]] and [[themis]]). Merry renders ἀτάσθαλον here “cruel”, parallel to ἐξαίσιόν τι of 4.690 (*Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 4.693; see [[aisa]]). The recklessness is the suitors', whose “spirit and ... shameful deeds / are plain to see” (4.694–695; see [[no-thanks-for-good-deeds]]). In Book 22 Leodes pleads in the same words that he never said or did anything reckless in the house, and that the suitors have met a shameful doom through their own recklessness (ἀτασθαλίῃσιν, 22.313–317).
