---
summary: Menelaus counts the Ethiopians among the peoples he came to in the eight years of his wanderings, after Cyprus, Phoenicia and Egypt (4.83–84).
---

“And I came to the Ethiopians and the Sidonians and the Erembi” (4.84; see [[the-wanderings-of-menelaus]], [[sidonians]] and [[erembi]]). In the first book the Ethiopians were the farthest of men, divided in two, by the risings and the settings of the sun, where Poseidon had gone to feast (1.22–25); here they are a stage on a voyage from Egypt. Strabo saw the difficulty: no Ethiopians live by the Mediterranean, and ships could not pass the cataracts of the Nile, so that some sent Menelaus round Libya by Gades to India, Crates among them, and others through the isthmus by the Arabian Gulf or by a canal (*Geography* 1.2.31). His own answer is that Menelaus, a guest of kings, went up from Thebes to the borders of Ethiopia near Syene and Philae, and was said to have come to the Ethiopians as Odysseus is said to have come to the land of the Cyclopes, by reaching the edge of it (1.2.32; see [[thebes-in-egypt]]). Merry took the Erembi of the same verse for a branch of the Ethiopians (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 4.84).
