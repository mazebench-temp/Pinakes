summary: Noemon asks Antinous when Telemachus will be back from Pylos with the ship he lent him, since he needs it to cross to Elis for his mares; he answers that he gave it willingly, and wonders that he saw Mentor in Ithaca the day after he saw him board the ship. His question tells the suitors that Telemachus has sailed (4.630–657).

**The ship he lent.** While the suitors amuse themselves with the discus and the javelin, “Noemon, son of Phronius, came near them / and questioned Antinous with these words and said” (4.630–631; see [[phronius]] and [[antinous]]): does anyone know when Telemachus will come back from sandy Pylos? “He went off with a ship of mine; and I have need of it / to cross over to Elis with its wide dancing floors, where I have horses, / twelve mares, and at their teats hard-working mules, / unbroken; one of these I would drive off and break in” (4.634–637; see [[elis]], [[horses]] and [[mules]]). It is the swift ship Athena, in Telemachus' likeness, asked of him (2.386–387). Ithaca, as Telemachus has just told Menelaus, has no meadows for horses (4.605–608), and so Noemon keeps a paddock in Elis (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 4.635). The suitors are amazed, for they thought Telemachus was on his farms, with the flocks or the swineherd (4.638–640).

**Willingly given.** To Antinous' questions he answers simply: “I gave it to him myself, willingly. What would anyone else do, / when such a man, with cares in his heart, / asks him? It would be hard to refuse the gift” (4.649–651). The young men who followed are the best in the land after the suitors themselves, “and as their leader I saw going on board / Mentor, or a god, who was like him in every way. / But at this I marvel: I saw brilliant Mentor here / yesterday at daybreak, though at that time he went on board the ship for Pylos” (4.653–656; see [[mentor]] and [[athena]]). Merry paraphrases: Noemon saw the lordly Mentor in Ithaca yesterday at early dawn, though at the time Telemachus started he went on board for Pylos (on 4.655). The god was Athena in Mentor's likeness (2.399–401), and Noemon's “yesterday” dates the Ithaca scenes to the morning after the ship sailed, the fourth day of the poem's action (see the translation's note on 4.655–656). “So he spoke, and went away to his father's house” (4.657), and his innocent question sets Antinous on the plan of the ambush (4.658–672; see [[the-journey-of-telemachus]]).
