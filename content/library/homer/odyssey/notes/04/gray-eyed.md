---
summary: “Then the gray-eyed goddess Athena thought of another thing” (4.795): the verse that brings the goddess back into the story at the end of the book, to send a phantom to Penelope.
---

The epithet comes only here in Book 4, in the verse of her two plans in the night of Telemachus' departure (2.382, 2.393; [[thought-of-another-thing]]). She makes the phantom in the likeness of Penelope's sister and sends it to comfort her (4.795–841; [[eidolon]], [[dream-scene]], [[athena]]).
