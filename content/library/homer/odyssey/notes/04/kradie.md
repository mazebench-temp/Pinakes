summary: Helen's “heart was turned to going / back home” before Troy fell (4.260–261); Telemachus says that nothing kept destruction from his father, “not even if the heart within him had been of iron” (4.293); on Pharos Menelaus' “heart heaved with many thoughts” (4.427, 4.572) and his “heart and proud spirit / were warmed again” (4.548–549).
grc: [κραδίη]
---

κραδίη is the heart that turns, heaves and is warmed. Helen's had turned to home while she was still in Troy (4.260–261; see [[nostos]]); Telemachus imagines a heart of iron, σιδηρέη, that would still not have saved his father (4.292–293; see [[algos]]). Menelaus' heart heaves with many thoughts as he goes back to his ships from Eidothea, and again from Proteus (4.427, 4.572), and at Proteus' counsel “my heart and proud spirit / were warmed again in my breast, grieved though I was” (4.548–549; see [[thymos]] and [[achos]]).
