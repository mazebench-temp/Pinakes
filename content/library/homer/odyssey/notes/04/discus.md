---
summary: The suitors amuse themselves with the discus and the javelin in front of Odysseus' hall when they learn of Telemachus' voyage (4.626).
---

“But the suitors, in front of the hall of Odysseus, / amused themselves throwing the discus and the javelin / on the leveled ground, where they used to, in their arrogance” (4.625–627; see [[the-suitors]] and [[spear]]; again 17.167–169). The middle verse is the Iliad's for the Myrmidons idle by their ships while Achilles keeps from the fighting (*Iliad* 2.774).
