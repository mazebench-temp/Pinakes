---
summary: Telemachus is “terribly / delighted” listening to Menelaus' “stories and … words” (4.597–598); he asks the king to remember any “word or deed” Odysseus fulfilled for him at Troy (4.329), as Nestor sent him to Menelaus for counsel “in some word or some deed” (4.163). Ajax lets fall an overbearing word (4.503), and speechlessness takes Penelope (4.704).
grc: [ἔπεσσι, ἔπεσσιν, ἐπέων]
---

**Word and deed.** ἔπος with ἔργον makes the whole of what a man can do for another: Peisistratus says that Nestor sent him so that Menelaus might give Telemachus counsel “in some word or some deed” (4.163), and Telemachus asks Menelaus to remember for him “if ever my brave father Odysseus / promised you any word or deed and fulfilled it” (4.328–329; see [[reciprocity-with-the-gods]]). Menelaus praises Peisistratus for saying all that a wise man would say and do (4.204–205; see [[easily-known-is-the-offspring]]).

**Words and their weight.** Ajax would have escaped had he not let fall ὑπερφίαλον ἔπος, “an overbearing word” (4.503; see [[ate]] and [[overbearing]]). Menelaus will not conceal a word of what Proteus told him (4.350; see [[truth-and-deception]]). Telemachus is delighted with his μῦθοι and ἔπεα, “stories and ... words” (4.597; see [[mythos]]), and Penelope, at Medon's news, is held a long time by ἀμφασίη ἐπέων, “speechlessness” (4.704), where Merry finds ἐπέων redundant, as βοῶν in βοῶν ἐπιβουκόλος (*Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 4.704; see [[silence]]).
