summary: Helen, seeing Odysseus in the young stranger, asks herself, “Shall I be wrong, or speak the truth? But my heart bids me speak” (4.140), the verse of Nestor in the night in the Iliad.
grc: [ψεύσομαι]
en: [be wrong]
---

ψεύσομαι ἦ ἔτυμον ἐρέω; κέλεται δέ με θυμός (4.140) is the verse with which Nestor, hearing hoofbeats in the night, begins to tell the Achaeans what he hears (*Iliad* 10.532–534). Merry argues from that verse that the choice is between speaking the truth and saying nothing, “Shall I withhold the truth?”, since ψεύδεσθαι is to say what one knows to be false (*Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 4.140; see the translation's note on 4.140). Helen does not hold back; she names the young man as Telemachus before Peisistratus confirms it (4.141–157; see [[fathers-and-sons]] and [[truth-and-deception]]).
