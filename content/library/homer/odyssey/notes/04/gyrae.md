---
summary: In Proteus' tale the Gyrae are the rocks of Ajax's rescue and of his death: Poseidon drives him onto them and saves him from the sea, and when Ajax boasts, the god splits “the Gyraean rock” with his trident, and the piece on which he sits falls into the sea with him (4.499–511).
---

Ajax, son of Oileus, was brought down among his ships, but “First Poseidon drove him onto the Gyrae, / the great rocks, and saved him from the sea” (4.500–501; see [[the-death-of-ajax-son-of-oileus]], [[ajax-son-of-oileus]] and [[poseidon]]). Safe on the rocks, he said that he had escaped the great gulf of the sea against the will of the gods (4.504): φῆ φυγέειν, “said he had escaped”, as one who thought himself secure on the Gyrae (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 4.504). Then Poseidon “took his trident in his strong hands / and struck the Gyraean rock, and split it apart; / and the one part stayed where it was, but the broken piece fell into the sea, / the piece on which Ajax had first sat when he was greatly blinded” (4.506–509). τρύφος, “broken piece”, is found only here in Homer; the rest of the rock stays where it was, and only the piece that bore the boaster falls (Merry on 4.508). The name, from γυρός, “round”, belongs to the rocks themselves; Γυραίην is its adjective (Merry on 4.499). The poem does not say where they were; for the later placings, near Myconos or off Caphereus in Euboea, see the general article.
