---
summary: Antinous and Eurymachus are the leaders of the suitors, “and in excellence they were by far the best” (4.629); Penelope's husband “excelled among the Danaans in every kind of prowess” (4.725, 4.815).
grc: [ἀρετῇ, ἀρετῇσι]
---

The translation keeps “prowess” for a warrior's ἀρετή and “excellence” otherwise (see the translation's table of key words). The suitors' leaders are best ἀρετῇ, “in excellence” (4.629, again 21.187; see [[antinous]], [[eurymachus]] and [[hegemon]]). Penelope's husband excelled παντοίῃς ἀρετῇσι, “in every kind of prowess” (4.725, 4.815; see [[husbands-and-wives]]), as the Iliad says of the son of Copreus, better than his father “in every kind of prowess, both in running and in fighting” (*Iliad* 15.641–642).
