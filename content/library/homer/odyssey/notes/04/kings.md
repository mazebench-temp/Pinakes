---
summary: Menelaus tells his guests that they are “of the lineage of men, of kings whom Zeus has nurtured, / scepter-bearing kings” (4.63–64), and Penelope speaks of “the way of divine kings” (4.691).
---

Menelaus' guess at his guests' birth names the class: “kings whom Zeus has nurtured, / scepter-bearing kings, since base men would not beget such sons as you” (4.63–64; see [[zeus-nurtured]], [[scepter-bearing]] and [[genee]]). Penelope gives the class its habit, which Odysseus did not share: “though that is the way of divine kings: / one man among mortals they hate, another they love” (4.691–692; see [[basileus]] and [[kingship-and-authority]]).
