# Unnamed: "my slave, whom my father gave me" (4.736), "who keeps my garden" and his errand (4.737-738).
refs: [4.736-738]
---

At the end of her lament to her women, Penelope turns to what can be done: “But let someone readily call old Dolius, / my slave, whom my father gave me when I came here, / and who keeps my garden of many trees, so that as quickly as he can / he may sit beside Laertes and tell him all this” (4.735–738; see [[penelope]], [[laertes]] and [[icarius]]). καλέσειε, the optative, has the force of a command (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 4.735), and ἔτι refers back to the time when Penelope left her father's house (on 4.736). παρεζόμενος, “sit beside”, describes the long, quiet talk an old man needs to take in such news (on 4.738), in the hope that Laertes may “come out and mourn before the people, who are eager / to destroy his offspring” (4.740–741), a people Merry supposes to be on the suitors' side (on 4.739). Eurycleia stops it: “And do not afflict an afflicted old man” (4.754; see [[eurycleia]]). The errand is never run, and Dolius is not named again in the book.
