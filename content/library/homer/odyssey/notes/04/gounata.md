summary: Telemachus tells Menelaus, “I come now to your knees” (4.322); on Pharos Menelaus goes along the shore “imploring the gods earnestly” (4.433); in Ithaca Penelope's “knees and dear heart were loosened” at Medon's news (4.703).
grc: [γούνατα, γουνούμενος]
en: [imploring]
---

τὰ σὰ γούναθʼ ἱκάνομαι, “I come now to your knees” (4.322, = 3.92), is the suppliant's formula, spoken to Nestor too: Telemachus asks for news of his father (see [[supplication]]). Menelaus, on the morning of the ambush, walks along the shore πολλὰ θεοὺς γουνούμενος, “imploring the gods earnestly” (4.433). At Medon's news “her knees and dear heart were loosened where she was” (4.703; see [[etor]]), the verse of Lycaon's despair before Achilles in the Iliad (*Iliad* 21.114).
