---
summary: Penelope fears for a son who is “a child, knowing nothing well either of toils or of assemblies” (4.818).
en: [assemblies]
---

ἀγοραί are the assemblies in which men speak, and Telemachus, in his mother's fear, does not yet know them: νήπιος, οὔτε πόνων ἐὺ εἰδὼς οὔτʼ ἀγοράων (4.818; see [[nepios]], [[ponos]] and [[mothers-and-sons]]). He has spoken in one already, the first assembly in Ithaca since Odysseus sailed (2.26–27).
