---
summary: Menelaus' attendants throw spelt before the guests' horses and mix white barley with it (4.41), and spelt grows with wheat and barley on Menelaus' plain (4.604).
---

**Fodder for the guests' horses.** When Menelaus has rebuked Eteoneus, his attendants unyoke the strangers' sweating horses, tie them at the horses' mangers, πὰρ δʼ ἔβαλον ζειάς, ἀνὰ δὲ κρῖ λευκὸν ἔμιξαν, “and threw spelt before them, and mixed white barley with it” (4.39–41; see [[horses]], [[barley]] and [[xenia]]). The guests' beasts are fed as the guests are. In the Iliad horses are fed the same two grains, with ὀλύραι for ζειαί: Pandarus' horses in Lycaon's halls and the Trojan horses waiting for dawn by the watch fires stand “munching white barley and spelt” (*Iliad* 5.195–196, 8.564–565).

**On Menelaus' plain.** The same grain grows on the wide plain that Telemachus names when he declines the gift of horses, πυροί τε ζειαί τε ἰδʼ εὐρυφυὲς κρῖ λευκόν, “and wheat and spelt and broad-eared white barley” (4.604; see [[wheat]], [[lotus]] and [[galingale]]). These two verses are the only places where the Odyssey names spelt. The grain is also in the epithet of the earth, ζείδωρος, “grain-giving”, which comes in this book too, in the land of Egypt that bears the most drugs (4.229; see [[grain-giving]]).
