**Gifts at Sparta.** The house is full of gifts: Polybus of Egyptian Thebes gave Menelaus silver bathtubs, tripods and gold, and his wife gave Helen “beautiful gifts”, the golden distaff and the silver basket (4.125–132; see [[guest-gifts]] and [[gold-and-silver]]). Menelaus promises Telemachus “splendid gifts”, three horses, a chariot and a goblet (4.589–592); Telemachus asks that “whatever gift you would give me, let it be a treasure”, since Ithaca has no room for horses (4.600–608), and Menelaus changes the gift for the finest and most precious of those stored in his house, the mixing bowl of Phaedimus (4.611–619; see [[mixing-bowl]] and [[xenia]]).

**A gift not refused.** Noemon tells Antinous that he gave his ship willingly: “What would anyone else do, / when such a man, with cares in his heart, / asks him? It would be hard to refuse the gift” (4.649–651; see [[noemon-son-of-phronius]]).
