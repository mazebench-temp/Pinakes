en: [pitied, pities, piteously]
---

**Do not spare me.** Telemachus begs Menelaus for the truth about his father: “And do not soften anything out of respect for me, or out of pity, / but tell me in full how you came upon the sight of him” (4.326–327), the verses he spoke to Nestor (3.96–97; see [[aidos]] and [[truth-and-deception]]).

**The pity of the gods.** Menelaus and his men would have starved on Pharos, “had not one of the gods pitied me and saved me, / the daughter of mighty Proteus” (4.364–365; see [[eidothea]]). ὀλοφύρομαι is the lament of one who mourns, and here, of a goddess, the pity that moves her to help. In Ithaca the phantom tells Penelope that Pallas Athena goes with her son, “and she pities you in your mourning” (4.828; see [[athena]] and [[eidolon]]).

**Lamenting piteously.** Penelope sits on the threshold of her chamber, οἴκτρʼ ὀλοφυρομένη, “lamenting piteously” (4.719; see [[lament]]); the same words are said of the companions Odysseus finds at the ship when he comes back from Circe's house (10.408–409), and of the daughters of the Old Man of the Sea around the body of Achilles (24.59).
