summary: Menelaus' “great high-roofed house” (4.15, 4.46), Helen's “fragrant high-roofed chamber” (4.121), “the inner part of the high house” where Menelaus sleeps (4.304); and in Ithaca Eurycleia's hope that someone of Laertes' line will be left “to hold / the high-roofed house” (4.756–757).
grc: [ὑψερεφὲς, ὑψορόφοιο, ὑψερεφέα]
---

ὑψερεφής and ὑψόροφος, the two words of the lofty roof, describe the wealth of Sparta, where the wedding feast fills the house and the strangers marvel at its gleam (4.15–16, 4.43–46; [[house]], [[kydalimos]]), and the queen's chamber, from which Helen comes like Artemis (4.121–122; [[fragrant]]). At 4.476 Bekker read οἶκον ἐς ὑψόροφον for οἶκον ἐυκτίμενον (see [[well-built]]).
