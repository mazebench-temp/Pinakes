**On Pharos.** After Eidothea has left him, Menelaus goes back “to the ships where they stood on the sand” (4.426; see [[pharos]] and [[ships]]); they make their supper and lie down “where the sea breaks upon the shore”, ἐπὶ ῥηγμῖνι θαλάσσης, the line of the breaking waves, as the crew of the Iliad's first book stepped out of their ship (4.429–430; *Iliad* 1.437). At dawn he goes “along the shore of the wide-wayed sea”, imploring the gods (4.432–433; see [[wide-wayed]] and [[prayer]]), as Telemachus went along the shore to pray to Athena (2.260–261). Eidothea makes the ambush in the beach itself: “having scooped out beds in the sands of the sea / she sat waiting” (4.438–439; see [[eidothea]]); then the seals come out of the sea and lie down in order “along the shore of the sea”, and the old man among them (4.448–453; see [[seals]] and [[proteus]]). When Proteus has told him of his brother's death, Menelaus weeps “as I sat on the sand”, and rolls on the ground until he has had his fill of weeping (4.539–541; see [[agamemnon]] and [[lament]]). The last night on the island ends as the first did, with the same verses (4.573–575).

**In Ithaca.** The suitors chosen for the ambush “went to the swift ship and the shore of the sea”, dragged the ship down into deep water, and waited for evening (4.778–786; see [[the-suitors]] and [[ships]]).
