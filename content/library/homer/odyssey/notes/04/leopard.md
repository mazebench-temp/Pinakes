---
summary: The Odyssey's only leopard is the third of the shapes of Proteus, after a bearded lion and a serpent and before a great boar (4.456–457).
---

**Among the changes.** πάρδαλις comes once in the Odyssey, in the verse of the changes: “and then a serpent, and a leopard, and a great boar” (4.457; see [[proteus]], [[serpent]] and [[boar]]). Three of the four beasts, the lion, the leopard and the boar, are the beasts whose might Menelaus himself had set against the pride of the sons of Panthous in the Iliad: “Neither is the leopard's might so great, nor the lion's, / nor the deadly wild boar's” (*Iliad* 17.20–21; see [[lion]] and [[menelaus]]). In his tale he holds all three fast in turn, in the shape of one old man (4.459).

**The spelling.** Merry reports in his critical note that the editions of Aristarchus wrote πάρδαλις with α, while the common text had πόρδαλις, and that a further refinement was to write πόρδαλις for the male and πάρδαλις for the female; he refers to Didymus on *Iliad* 13, the book in which the Iliad first names leopards (*Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 4.457; see [[aristarchus]]).
