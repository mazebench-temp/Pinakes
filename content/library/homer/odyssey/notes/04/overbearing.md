---
summary: Ajax lets fall “an overbearing word” against the gods (4.503); Penelope fears that her son will be killed by “the overbearing suitors” (4.790); and Antinous calls Telemachus' journey a great deed “overbearingly accomplished” (4.663) and bids the others avoid “overbearing talk” (4.774).
---

The word of Ajax's boast, that he had escaped the sea against the will of the gods (4.503–504; [[ajax-son-of-oileus]], [[the-death-of-ajax-son-of-oileus]], [[hybris]]), is turned by Antinous on Telemachus, whose journey he takes as an affront (4.663–664), and on the suitors' own careless talk, lest someone report it inside (4.774–775). The narrator and Penelope keep the word for the suitors (4.790; [[the-suitors]]).
