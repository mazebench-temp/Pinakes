summary: At Sparta μῦθοι are the talk and the tales of the evening: Helen bids the company “take delight in stories” (4.239), and Telemachus would gladly stay a year “listening to your stories and your words” (4.597). In Ithaca the word is the suitors' plan, brooded deep in their minds (4.676, 4.777).
grc: [μύθοις, μυθήσομαι, μυθεόμην]
en: [stories, talk, plans, plan]
---

**Talk and tales.** Menelaus puts off the talk of his guests' errand until morning: “And there will be talk at dawn too / for Telemachus and me to have with one another” (4.214–215). Helen, with her drug in the wine, bids them “take delight in stories; for I will tell you what is fitting”, though she could not tell or name all the trials of Odysseus (4.239–241; see [[zeus-gives-good-and-evil]]). Telemachus is so taken with Menelaus' tale that he would stay a year, “for listening to your stories and your words I am terribly / delighted” (4.597–598; see [[epos]]). Merry takes μυθήσομαι as a subjunctive parallel to ὀνομήνω (*Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 4.240).

**The suitors' plan.** In Ithaca the μῦθοι are the suitors' plans, which they “were brooding deep in their minds” (4.676; see the translation's table of key words on βυσσοδομεύω), and Antinous bids them avoid overbearing talk, all of it alike, and rise in silence to carry out “the plan that has pleased all of us in our minds” (4.774–777; see [[silence]] and [[truth-and-deception]]). Merry takes πάντας ὁμῶς with μύθους (on 4.774; see the translation's note on 4.774–775). Eurycleia, confessing, says she will not hide her μῦθος from Penelope (4.744).
