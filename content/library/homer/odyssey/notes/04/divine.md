summary: The singer at the wedding feast (4.17), the “divine house” of Menelaus (4.43) and Menelaus himself, “the divine king” (4.621); Proteus, “the divine old man” (4.395); “divine Odysseus” in Penelope's words and the narrator's (4.682, 4.799); and “the way of divine kings” (4.691).
grc: ["θείων βασιλήων"]
---

θεῖος ἀοιδός is the Shield's verse, with the singer and the tumblers (4.17–18; [[the-singer-on-the-shield]]). Penelope gives the epithet to kings in general when she sets Odysseus against them: “that is the way of divine kings: / one man among mortals they hate, another they love”, but he never did anything excessive to anyone (4.689–693; [[dike]], [[odysseus]]). Penelope asks whether the suitors have sent Medon “to tell the slave women of divine Odysseus / to cease from their work and prepare a feast for them” (4.682–683), and Athena sends the phantom “to the house of divine Odysseus” (4.799; [[eidolon]]).
