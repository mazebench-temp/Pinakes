---
summary: The last of the four beasts that Proteus becomes is “a great boar” (4.457). The book's other swine are only implied, in the swineherd on Odysseus' farm, with whom the suitors think Telemachus may be staying (4.640).
---

**A great boar.** Proteus, seized on the shore of Pharos, becomes a bearded lion, “and then a serpent, and a leopard, and a great boar”, and after the boar flowing water and a tree (4.456–458; see [[proteus]], [[lion]], [[serpent]], [[leopard]] and [[trees]]). μέγας σῦς is the boar of the wild, as the one that wounded the young Odysseus on Parnassus lay “in a thick lair”, μέγας σῦς again (19.439, a plain rendering). The boar ends the beasts; in the Iliad it is the beast whose spirit is greatest of all (*Iliad* 17.21–22; see [[leopard]]).

**The swineherd.** When Noemon asks for his ship, the suitors learn that Telemachus has really sailed. They had not thought that he had gone to Pylos, “but that he was somewhere there / on the farm, with the flocks or with the swineherd” (4.638–640; see [[the-suitors]] and [[sheep]]). The swineherd is Eumaeus, not yet named (see [[eumaeus]]). Merry takes ἀγρῶν as a genitive of place, explaining αὐτοῦ, “there, on the farm” (*Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 4.639). The suitors expect the boy among his father's herds, whose fat hogs they eat every day (see the Book 2 note).
