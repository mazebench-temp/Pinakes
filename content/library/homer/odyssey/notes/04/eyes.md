summary: The eyes of Book 4: the guests' fill of looking at the house (4.47), the glances by which Menelaus knows Odysseus' son (4.150), Peisistratus' tears (4.186), Antinous' eyes like blazing fire (4.662), Penelope's eyes filled with tears and checked from lament (4.704–705, 4.758), and the seeing with one's own eyes (4.226, 4.269, 4.324).
# ὀφθαλμῶν (4.150) and ὀφθαλμοῖσι (4.324), first met here in the Odyssey.
grc: [ὀφθαλμῶν, ὀφθαλμοῖσι]
---

**Looking.** The strangers gaze at Menelaus' house until “they had taken their fill of looking with their eyes” (4.47; see [[house]]). Menelaus sees the likeness that Helen has named: “for such were his feet, and such his hands, / and the glances of his eyes, and his head and the hair above” (4.149–150; see [[hair]], [[telemachus]] and [[helen]]).

**Tears.** At the name of his brother Antilochus “nor did the son of Nestor keep his eyes free of tears” (4.186; see [[peisistratus]] and [[tears]]). When Penelope hears of the suitors' plot, “for a long time speechlessness took her, and her two eyes / filled with tears” (4.704–705, as *Iliad* 17.695–696; see [[silence]]), and at Eurycleia's words she “lulled her lament, and checked her eyes from lament” (4.758). Antinous' rage is the other way: “his eyes were like blazing fire” (4.662; see [[eyes-like-fire]]).

**With one's own eyes.** The witness of the eyes is the strongest: a man who drank Helen's drug would not weep though his brother or son were cut down “and he saw it with his own eyes” (4.226); Menelaus “never yet” has seen such a man as Odysseus “with my eyes” (4.269); and Telemachus begs him for what he has seen “with your own eyes, or heard word of him from someone else” (4.324, as 3.94).
