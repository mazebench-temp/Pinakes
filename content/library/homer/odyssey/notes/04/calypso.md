summary: Proteus tells Menelaus that he saw Odysseus on an island, shedding big tears in the halls of the nymph Calypso, who holds him there by force; he has no ships and no companions to take him home. It is the first news of where Odysseus is that reaches his family.

**In the halls of the nymph.** Proteus has told of the two leaders who perished on the way home, and of a third “still alive somewhere”, “held back on the wide sea” (4.498), the words in which Athena, as Mentes, spoke of Odysseus to Telemachus (1.197). Menelaus asks who he is, alive or dead (4.551–553), and the old man answers:

{{quote:ed55951d-69f9-5546-b98b-1e2ef70265f6}}

ἀνάγκῃ, “by force”, names what the narrator called her holding him back (ἔρυκε, 1.14), and θαλερὸν κατὰ δάκρυ χέοντα, “shedding big tears”, is the weeping of Odysseus on her shore that Book 5 will show (5.151–158; see [[odysseus]], [[tears]] and [[nymphs]]). The last four verses are said again by Athena at the second council of the gods (5.14–17), and Telemachus repeats them to his mother when he is home, as Menelaus' report of what the unerring Old Man of the Sea told him (17.140–146; see [[telemachus]] and [[penelope]]). So the news comes to Odysseus' house by the road Athena laid out in Book 1, from Sparta and from Menelaus, “for he was the last of the bronze-armored Achaeans to come home” (1.285–286; see [[the-journey-of-telemachus]]). In Book 1 she had told the gods the truth about Calypso (1.48–59) and Telemachus only of savage men on a sea-washed island (1.197–199); Nestor, at Pylos, knew nothing (3.184–185). Menelaus is the first mortal in the poem to name her.
