---
summary: Plato's Socrates makes the Proteus of this book the image of a speaker who slips away from the question, and the Republic forbids the poets to tell lies about him; the Phaedrus quotes Stesichorus' denial of the voyage that Helen remembers here; and the Gorgias sends the just to the Isles of the Blessed, judged by Rhadamanthys, who lives in this book's Elysian plain.
---

- Proteus, “the Egyptian sophist”, and Menelaus, who would not let him go (*Euthydemus* 288b–c); Euthyphro, not to be let go, “like Proteus”, until he tells (*Euthyphro* 15d); Ion, who like Proteus becomes every kind of thing and escapes as a general (*Ion* 541e–542a); and the ban on lies about Proteus and Thetis (*Republic* 2.381d; on 4.384–386, 4.417–422, 4.454–459): [[platos-proteus]] and [[proteus]].
- Stesichorus' palinode, “you did not go on the well-benched ships, / you did not come to the citadel of Troy”, which denies the voyage that Helen remembers in this book (*Phaedrus* 243a–b; on 4.259–264): [[stesichorus-palinode]].
- The Isles of the Blessed, to which the souls of the just are sent, and Rhadamanthys, Aeacus and Minos, the judges of the dead (*Gorgias* 523a–524a, 526b–c; *Republic* 7.519c, 7.540b; on 4.561–569): [[elysium-and-the-isles-of-the-blessed]] and [[rhadamanthus]].
