---
summary: Helen lies down beside Menelaus at the end of the first evening, “Helen of the long robes, brilliant among women” (4.305).
---

Ἑλένη τανύπεπλος … δῖα γυναικῶν is the Iliad's verse-end for her, where she answers Priam on the wall (*Iliad* 3.228; [[brilliant]]). In her husband's house it closes the night of the drug and the two tales of Troy, and the next verse is the dawn (4.304–306; [[helen]], [[menelaus]]). The Odyssey gives her the same words again when she answers Peisistratus before her husband can (15.171).
