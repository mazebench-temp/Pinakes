---
summary: Cyprus heads the list of the lands where Menelaus wandered and gathered his wealth in the eight years before he came home (4.83).
---

“For after much suffering and much wandering / I brought them home in my ships, and came back in the eighth year, / after wandering to Cyprus and Phoenicia and the Egyptians” (4.81–83; see [[the-wanderings-of-menelaus]], [[phoenicians]] and [[egypt]]). The island is the first stage of the voyage east and south that ends in Libya. Strabo, explaining how Menelaus could hope for gifts among these peoples, recalls the Iliad's Cyprus: the Egyptians and their neighbors had heard of the fame of the sons of Atreus, as Cinyras had in Cyprus when he gave Agamemnon his corselet as a guest-gift (*Geography* 1.2.32, quoting *Iliad* 11.20–21; see [[cinyras]] and [[guest-gifts]]).
