summary: Menelaus “noticed” Telemachus' tears (4.116); in Troy Odysseus told Helen “the whole plan of the Achaeans” (4.256); Menelaus has known “the plan and the thought of many / heroes” (4.267–268), and Proteus tells him that he has no need “to learn my mind” (4.493).
grc: [νόησε]
---

νόος is understanding and the plan it forms. Menelaus νόησε, “noticed”, the young man weeping behind his cloak (4.116; see [[tears]]), and agrees with Helen, οὕτω νῦν καὶ ἐγὼ νοέω, “I too see it so now” (4.148). Odysseus, when Helen had sworn not to betray him, told her πάντα νόον ... Ἀχαιῶν, “the whole plan of the Achaeans” (4.256), which Merry takes to be the plan of the Horse (*Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 4.256; see [[odysseus-enters-troy-as-a-beggar]]). Menelaus has known “the plan and the thought of many / heroes”, but none like Odysseus (4.267–270; see [[boule]]), and Proteus warns him that he has no need to know or learn his mind, for he will not be long without tears when he has heard it all (4.492–494; see [[tears]]).
