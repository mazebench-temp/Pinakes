---
summary: Menelaus' grief for Odysseus rouses in Telemachus “the longing for lament for his father” (4.113), and then in them all “the longing for lament” (4.183).
---

ὑφʼ ἵμερον ὦρσε γόοιο, “he roused the longing for lament”, is the verse of the mourning for Patroclus in the Iliad (4.183 = *Iliad* 23.108; see [[the-longing-for-lament]] and [[lament]]). Merry takes ὑπό as “in the mind”, “sent into his mind”, and renders 4.113 “thrilled him with a yearning to bewail his father” (*Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 4.113); Aristotle quotes the verse for the pleasure there is in mourning (*Rhetoric* 1370b28–29, the verse of 4.183; see the translation's note on 4.113). The longing that comes over a man is ἵμερος; Telemachus' “longing for home” at 4.596 is πόθος (see [[pothe]]).
