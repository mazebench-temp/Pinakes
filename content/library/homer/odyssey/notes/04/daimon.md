---
summary: Menelaus lays Helen's coming to the Horse on “some god … / a god who meant to hold out glory to the Trojans” (4.274–275), and Antinous addresses the suitors as δαιμόνιοι, “Strange men” (4.774).
---

**The god at the Horse.** “Then you came there; some god must have bidden you, / a god who meant to hold out glory to the Trojans” (4.274–275; see [[who-is-to-blame]] and [[kydos]]). δαίμων is the god felt but not named; for Merry, Helen came out of curiosity, not to betray the men, and only a god's prompting explains it (*Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 4.274). Eustathius, as Merry reports, remarked that Menelaus here takes a very favorable view of her conduct (on 4.264; see [[husbands-and-wives]]).

**Strange men.** Antinous checks the suitors' boasting, δαιμόνιοι, μύθους μὲν ὑπερφιάλους ἀλέασθε, “Strange men, avoid overbearing talk” (4.774; see [[antinous]], [[strange-one]] and [[silence]]): the address of a speaker who finds his hearers' conduct past understanding, as Hector's δαιμονίη to Andromache (*Iliad* 6.486).
