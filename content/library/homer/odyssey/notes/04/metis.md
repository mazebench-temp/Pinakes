**Weaving a plan.** μῆτιν ὑφαίνειν is the Iliad's phrase for Nestor, who “first of all began to weave a plan” (*Iliad* 7.324, 9.93). Medon hears the suitors' counsels “as he stood outside the courtyard, while they inside were weaving their plan” (4.677–678; see [[medon-the-herald]] and [[boule]]); Penelope would send old Dolius to Laertes, in the hope that he may weave some plan and come out to mourn before the people (4.735–741; see [[laertes]]). She prays to Athena by the thigh-pieces that πολύμητις Ὀδυσσεύς, “Odysseus of many wiles”, once burned to her (4.763–765; see [[of-many-wiles]] and [[reciprocity-with-the-gods]]), and Aegisthus is δολόμητις, “scheming” (4.525; see [[schemer]]).

**Cunning drugs.** Helen's drugs, the gift of the Egyptian Polydamna, are φάρμακα μητιόεντα, “cunning drugs” (4.227; see [[nepenthe]] and [[helen]]). Merry rejects the scholiast's “discovered with understanding” for Göbel's sense, drugs that give counsel and help, as it were, for all human ills, and renders “helpful” (*Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 4.227).
