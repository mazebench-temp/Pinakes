---
summary: Helen called out by name “the best of the Danaans” hidden in the wooden horse (4.278), and Penelope twice remembers her husband as the man who “excelled among the Danaans in every kind of prowess” (4.725, 4.815).
---

The name is the army's, as always (see [[achaeans]] and [[argives]]). In Menelaus' story Helen walked around the horse “and you called out by name the best of the Danaans” (4.278; see [[sinon-and-the-wooden-horse]]); ὀνομακλήδην … ὀνόμαζες is split by a tmesis, ἐκ δʼ ὀνομακλήδην, where the undivided form is ἐξονομακλήδην (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 4.279). In Ithaca Penelope laments the husband she lost first, “who excelled among the Danaans in every kind of prowess, / a brave man, whose fame is wide through Hellas and the heart of Argos” (4.725–726), and says the same verses again to the phantom of her sister (4.815–816; see [[penelope]], [[kleos]] and [[hellas]]).
