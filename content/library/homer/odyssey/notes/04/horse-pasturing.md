---
summary: Menelaus' men died “in wide Troy, far from horse-pasturing Argos” (4.99), and Proteus foretells that he will not die “in horse-pasturing Argos” (4.562); Telemachus turns the epithet against horses: Ithaca is “a goat-pasturing land, and lovelier than a horse-pasturing one” (4.606).
grc: [ἱπποβότῳ]
---

Telemachus refuses the three horses because Ithaca has no wide courses and no meadow; αἰγίβοτος, “goat-pasturing”, is coined against the epithet of Argos, and the next verse adds ἱππήλατος, “good for driving horses”, and ἐυλείμων, “rich in meadows” (4.601–608; see the translation's key words; [[ithaca]], [[horses]]). Merry reports Bergk's conjecture that 4.606 should follow 4.608, so that Ithaca, more than all the islands, is the goat-pasturing land, and asks whether ἐπήρατος means “lovely” or “lovely in my eyes”, because it is home (*Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 4.606).
