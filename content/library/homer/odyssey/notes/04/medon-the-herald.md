**The news.** Penelope was not long without hearing of the suitors' plans, “for the herald Medon told her, who heard their counsels / as he stood outside the courtyard, while they inside were weaving their plan” (4.677–678; see [[the-suitors]] and [[heralds]]). αὐλῆς is a genitive of place, “in the yard, outside” (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 4.678; see the translation's note on 4.678). He goes “through the house to bring the news to Penelope” (4.679), the verse of Eteoneus going to tell Menelaus of the strangers (4.24; see [[eteoneus]]).

**Penelope's mistake.** She takes him for the suitors' messenger: “Herald, why have the noble suitors sent you? / Was it to tell the slave women of divine Odysseus / to cease from their work and prepare a feast for them?” (4.681–683; see [[penelope]]). Her curse on them passes into the second person, “you who gather here constantly and waste much substance” (4.686), and Merry takes the change to include Medon, as the suitors' favorite herald, in her charge (on 4.686). He answers as a man “who had wise understanding” (4.696; see [[wise]]):

When she asks why her son had any need to go on board the swift ships (4.707–710), he answers, “I do not know whether some god roused him, or whether his own / heart was eager to go to Pylos, to learn / of his father's homecoming, or what fate he has met” (4.712–714; see [[telemachus]] and [[the-journey-of-telemachus]]). Both are true: Athena sent him, and he went for news of his father (1.88–95, 2.214–217; see [[athena]]). Then he goes away through the house of Odysseus (4.715).

**Whose man?** In later books Medon is the herald who waits on the suitors (16.252) and the one who pleased them most (17.172–173), yet here he warns Penelope, and at the slaughter he is spared at Telemachus' plea (22.357–377). Merry finds no inconsistency, if he kept up his intimacy with the suitors in order to know their designs against his master's family, which he thinks simpler than to make him play both parties (on 4.677). His warning is recalled when Penelope comes down to rebuke Antinous (16.412 = 4.677).
