---
summary: Menelaus and his companions wait among the seals “with enduring heart,” and hold the old man “steadfastly with enduring heart” (4.447, 4.459).
grc: ["τετληότι θυμῷ"]
---

τετληότι θυμῷ, the perfect participle of the root of τλήμων, is a verse-end of the Odyssey: here of the men who bear the stench of the seals and the old man's changes (4.441–459; [[proteus]], [[seals]]), and later of Odysseus clinging under the Cyclops' ram (9.435) and of Penelope, who waits in the halls with enduring heart (11.181, 16.37).
