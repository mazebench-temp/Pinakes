# κόμην (4.198), the hair cut for the dead, first met here in the Odyssey.
grc: [κόμην]
---

**The likeness.** Telemachus is like his father in his feet and hands, “and the glances of his eyes, and his head and the hair above” (4.149–150; see [[eyes]] and [[telemachus]]).

**For the dead.** Peisistratus is not indignant at weeping for any mortal who dies: “This is the only prize for miserable mortals, / to cut one's hair and let a tear fall from one's cheeks” (4.197–198; see [[the-only-prize-of-miserable-mortals]], [[honoring-the-dead]] and [[the-prize-of-the-dead]]). The hair cut for the dead is the Iliad's rite at Patroclus' pyre (see the Iliad Book 23 note).
