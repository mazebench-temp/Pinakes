---
summary: Helen's only child, Hermione, “had the form of golden Aphrodite” (4.14).
---

χρυσέης Ἀφροδίτης is the measure of a woman's beauty, as in Achilles' refusal of Agamemnon's daughter, “not if she rivaled golden Aphrodite in beauty” (*Iliad* 9.389). Hermione is described in the goddess's likeness on her wedding day (4.3–14; [[hermione-daughter-of-helen]], [[aphrodite]]), and her mother comes out of her chamber a hundred verses later “like Artemis of the golden distaff” (4.122; [[of-the-golden-distaff]]). Penelope will be likened to both goddesses at once (17.37, 19.54).
