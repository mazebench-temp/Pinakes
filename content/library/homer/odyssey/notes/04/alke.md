summary: Menelaus calls the suitors ἀνάλκιδες, “weaklings”, who would lie in a strong man's bed (4.333–334); Aegisthus' lookout watches lest Agamemnon pass by unseen “and remember his furious courage” (4.527).
grc: [ἀνάλκιδες]
en: [weaklings]
---

“Ah, truly it was in the bed of a fierce-hearted man / that they wished to lie, weaklings as they are” (4.333–334): Menelaus' answer to Telemachus' account of the suitors opens the simile of the doe who lays her fawns in a lion's lair (4.335–340; see [[the-hind-in-the-lions-lair]] and [[the-suitors]]). Telemachus repeats it to his mother in Book 17 (17.124–125). θούριδος ἀλκῆς, “furious courage”, is the strength of Agamemnon that the lookout fears (4.527; see [[spies-and-scouts]]).
