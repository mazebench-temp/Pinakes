summary: “Fair-haired Menelaus” eight times, in the verses that bring in his speeches, above all “Answering her, fair-haired Menelaus said” (4.147, 4.168, 4.203, 4.265); and once Rhadamanthys, in the Elysian plain “where fair-haired Rhadamanthys is” (4.564).
grc: ["ξανθὸς Ῥαδάμανθυς"]
---

ξανθὸς Μενέλαος fills the end of the verse whenever Menelaus is about to speak: deeply troubled at Eteoneus' question and at Telemachus' plea (4.30, 4.332; [[deeply-troubled]]), greeting his guests at the meal (4.59), hearing what Telemachus says to Peisistratus of his house (4.76), and four times in the verse of reply (4.147–265; [[answering-him]]). Proteus gives the same epithet to Rhadamanthys, in the place to which Menelaus himself is to be sent (4.563–564; [[rhadamanthus]], [[elysium]]), and the Odyssey gives it to him again in Alcinous' tale (ξανθὸν Ῥαδάμανθυν, 7.323). See [[menelaus]].
