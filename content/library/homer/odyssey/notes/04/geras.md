---
summary: Menelaus sets before his guests the chine “which they had set before himself as his portion of honor” (4.65–66), and Peisistratus names tears and shorn hair “the only prize for miserable mortals” (4.197).
grc: [γέρα]
en: [portion of honor]
---

**The king's portion.** Menelaus lifts the roast chine of an ox in his hands and sets it before the strangers, τά ῥά οἱ γέρα πάρθεσαν αὐτῷ (4.65–66; see [[feast]] and [[xenia]]). Merry gathers the chine as the portion of honor, as when Odysseus sends a cut of it to Demodocus and Eumaeus honors him with it (8.474–476, 14.437), and Ajax is honored with it in the Iliad (*Iliad* 7.321); Menelaus, he says, received it in accordance with a custom that survived for the kings of Sparta, and gives his guests from his own portion; γέρα is a plural of amplification (*Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 4.65–66).

**The prize of the dead.** Peisistratus' maxim makes tears and the cutting of hair the γέρας of mortals in their misery (4.197–198; see [[the-only-prize-of-miserable-mortals]] and [[the-prize-of-the-dead]]).
