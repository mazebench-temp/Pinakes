---
summary: Peisistratus asks Menelaus, “be persuaded by me”, and leave weeping until morning (4.193); Menelaus chose for the ambush the three companions “whom I trusted most in every undertaking” (4.434).
grc: [πίθοιο, πεποίθεα]
en: [be persuaded]
---

“And now, if it is at all possible, be persuaded by me” (4.193): Peisistratus' request, which Menelaus grants, stopping the weeping and turning to supper (4.193–215; see [[the-only-prize-of-miserable-mortals]]). Merry renders εἴ τί που ἔστι “if it is in anywise possible” (*Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 4.193). The perfect πέποιθα is “trust”: on the morning of the ambush Menelaus brings three companions, “whom I trusted most in every undertaking” (4.434; see [[hetairos]]).
