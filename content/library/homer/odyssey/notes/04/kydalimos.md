---
summary: “Glorious Menelaus” five times, always in the genitive at the end of the verse and always of what is his: his house (4.2, 4.46), his neighbors and kinsmen at the wedding feast (4.16), and his ready attendants, Eteoneus and Asphalion (4.23, 4.217).
---

Μενελάου κυδαλίμοιο, the verse-end that names him in both poems, frames the arrival at Sparta: the strangers drive “to the house of glorious Menelaus” (4.2), and when they have looked at it, “there was a gleam as of the sun or of the moon / throughout the high-roofed house of glorious Menelaus” (4.45–46; [[house]], [[high-roofed]]). The men who receive them are each “the ready attendant of glorious Menelaus” (4.23, 4.217; [[ready-attendants]]). See [[menelaus]].
