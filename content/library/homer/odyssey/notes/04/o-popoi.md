---
summary: Menelaus twice, at the news that the son of his friend has come (4.169) and at the suitors' plan to lie in Odysseus' bed (4.333); and Antinous, at the news that Telemachus has sailed (4.663).
---

Each time the cry is followed by ἦ μάλα δή or ἦ μέγα, “Ah, truly”: Menelaus' joy, “Ah, truly the son of a man very dear to me has come to my house” (4.169), his indignation, “Ah, truly it was in the bed of a fierce-hearted man” (4.333; [[fierce-hearted]]), and Antinous' dismay, “Ah, truly a great deed has been overbearingly accomplished” (4.663; [[overbearing]]).
