# "γούναθʼ ἱκάνομαι" (4.322 = 3.92), the suppliant at the knees; γουνούμενος "imploring" (4.433).
grc: [λίσσεαι, "γούναθʼ ἱκάνομαι", γουνούμενος]
---

Telemachus uses the suppliant's words he used to Nestor (4.322–331 = 3.92–101): he comes to Menelaus' knees and begs him by any service his father did him at Troy to tell him the truth (see [[gounata]], [[reciprocity-with-the-gods]] and [[fathers-and-sons]]). Menelaus grants it, “But as for what you ask and beg of me, I would not / swerve aside to speak of other things, nor deceive you” (4.347–348; see [[apate]]), and tells the tale of Proteus (4.351–592). On Pharos Menelaus himself goes along the shore “imploring the gods earnestly” (4.433).
