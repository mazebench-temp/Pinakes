---
summary: Telemachus and Peisistratus reach “hollow Lacedaemon with its many ravines”, in the Catalogue's words for Menelaus' land; Menelaus asks what need has brought Telemachus “to shining Lacedaemon”, and the herald Medon tells Penelope that her son has gone there (4.1, 4.312–313, 4.702).
---

**The arrival.** The book opens with the verse of the Catalogue (*Iliad* 2.581):

{{quote:e57e0e71-6ad9-5eb4-8d50-76a165f55f56}}

Λακεδαίμων is the land, not the town: Buttmann held, and Merry agrees, that here as in the Catalogue it is the district whose chief town was Sparta, since its epithets suit a district and not a town (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 4.1; see [[sparta]]). κοίλην, “hollow”, is the deep valley of the Eurotas between Taygetus and Parnon, which Merry describes from Tozer, and he quotes the lines of Euripides' *Cresphontes* that Strabo cites for the land: much ploughland but hard to work, “for it is hollow, ringed by mountains, and rugged” (*Geography* 8.5.6; Merry on 4.1). κητώεσσαν he derives from κῆτος, “a gulf”, so “cavernous”, of the many rifts and fissures of the ground; Zenodotus read καιετάεσσαν, which the scholia explain as “full of calamint” (Merry's critical note on 4.1; see the translation's note on 4.1, and the general article for Strabo's account of both words). The book's ancient title names the place, “the events at Lacedaemon” (see [[the-title-of-odyssey-4]]).

**Shining Lacedaemon.** The next morning Menelaus asks, “What need has brought you here, hero Telemachus, / to shining Lacedaemon, over the broad back of the sea?” (4.312–313; see [[menelaus]] and [[telemachus]]), and in Ithaca Medon tells Penelope that her son has gone “to sacred Pylos and to shining Lacedaemon” (4.702; see [[medon-the-herald]] and [[pylos]]). Λακεδαίμονα δῖαν is Nestor's phrase in the last book (3.326; see [[brilliant]]).

**Menelaus' plain.** Telemachus refuses horses because Ithaca has no room for them, while Menelaus is “lord of a plain / that is wide, in which there is much lotus, and galingale, / and wheat and spelt and broad-eared white barley” (4.602–604; see [[ithaca]] and [[lotus]]). Merry takes the plain either for the flat land of Messenia, whose eastern part belonged to Menelaus' realm, or for the valley of the Eurotas itself, a level plain some fifteen miles long and four wide (on 4.602).
