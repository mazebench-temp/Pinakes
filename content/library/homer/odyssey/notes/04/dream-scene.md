summary: Athena makes a phantom in the likeness of Penelope's sister Iphthime and sends it to her; it comes into the chamber past the strap of the bolt, “stood above her head,” asks “Are you asleep, Penelope?”, comforts her, will not say whether Odysseus lives, and slips away into the blasts of the winds; and she wakes comforted (4.795–841).

The pattern is that of Agamemnon's dream in *Iliad* 2: a figure sent by a god in the likeness of someone the sleeper knows stands above the head and begins with the sleeper's sleep (*Iliad* 2.16–23). But the opening is a question, “Are you asleep”, where the dreams of the Iliad say “You are asleep” (*Iliad* 2.23, 23.69; see the translation's key words), and Penelope answers and asks in turn. The dream is ἐναργές, “clear” (4.841; [[eidolon]], [[iphthime]], [[penelope]]).
