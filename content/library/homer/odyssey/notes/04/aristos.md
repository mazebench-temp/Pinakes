summary: The Horse holds “all the best / of the Argives” (4.272–273); Menelaus chooses the three best of his companions for the ambush on Pharos (4.408–409); in Ithaca the best young men of the land sail with Telemachus (4.652), Antinous and Eurymachus are “by far the best” of the suitors (4.629), and Antinous chooses twenty of the best for the ambush (4.778).
grc: [ἀρίστους, ἀριστεύουσι]
---

The best men are chosen for the dangerous work: “all the best / of the Argives” sit in the Horse, and Helen calls “the best of the Danaans” by name (4.272–278; see [[sinon-and-the-wooden-horse]]); Eidothea bids Menelaus choose three companions, “who are the best you have beside your well-benched ships” (4.408–409); Aegisthus sets an ambush of twenty of the best men in the land (4.530–531). Nestor's sons are “the best with the spear” (4.211; see [[easily-known-is-the-offspring]]). In Ithaca Noemon says that the young men who are the best in the land after the suitors themselves went with Telemachus (4.652), Antinous complains that Telemachus has gone off choosing “the best men in the land” (4.666), and he chooses twenty of the best for his own ambush (4.778; see [[the-suitors]]).
