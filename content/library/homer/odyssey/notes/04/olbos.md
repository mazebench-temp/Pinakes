---
summary: Menelaus finds the son of a fortunate man easy to know, and Nestor is his example, granted “to grow old in ease in his halls”, with sons of good sense who are the best with the spear (4.207–211). His own great wealth brings him no joy (4.93–99).
---

**Nestor's prosperity.** The son of Cronus spins ὄλβος for a man at his marriage and at his birth, and his offspring are easily known (4.207–208; see [[easily-known-is-the-offspring]]); Nestor has it all his days, a comfortable old age in his halls and sons who are wise and the best with the spear (4.209–211; see [[nestor]] and [[geron]]). At Pylos Telemachus denied such prosperity to himself and his father (3.208–209).

**Wealth without joy.** Menelaus has gathered great wealth in eight years of wandering, and his house is like the court of Zeus (4.71–92); but he is lord of it with no joy, since his brother was killed meanwhile, and he would live with a third part of it if the men who died at Troy were safe (4.93–99; see [[possessions]] and [[menelaus]]). His good fortune is to come at the end: the gods will send him to the Elysian plain, “where life is easiest for men” (4.563–565; see [[elysium]]).
