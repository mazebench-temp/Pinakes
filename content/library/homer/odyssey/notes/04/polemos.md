---
summary: The Achaeans came to Troy “bent on bold war” for Helen's sake (4.146); Menelaus asks Proteus whether any of the leaders perished at home among their friends, “when he had wound out the war” (4.490), and Proteus answers that Menelaus was himself at “the battle” (4.497).
---

Helen speaks of the war as the Achaeans' coming for her sake, πόλεμον θρασὺν ὁρμαίνοντες, “bent on bold war” (4.145–146; see [[bold-war]] and [[dog-face]]). Menelaus asks Proteus about those who died at sea or “in the arms of his friends, when he had wound out the war” (4.489–490), ἐπεὶ πόλεμον τολύπευσεν, the image of wool wound off into a ball; Proteus will not dwell on those who fell at Troy, for “at the battle you were there yourself” (4.497; see [[the-returns-of-the-achaeans]]).
