---
summary: The title of three men: Menelaus, to whom Eteoneus goes “to bring the news to the shepherd of the people” (4.24); Aegisthus, to whom his lookout goes in the same words when Agamemnon lands (4.528); and Agamemnon, “shepherd of the people,” whom Aegisthus invites to the feast (4.532).
---

The verse βῆ δʼ ἴμεν ἀγγελέων … ποιμένι λαῶν brings news to a master twice in the book: to Menelaus, of two strangers at his door (4.24), and to Aegisthus, of the king whose return he has paid a man to watch for (4.524–528; [[messenger-scene]]). Proteus' tale gives the shepherd's title to the usurper and, four verses later, to the king he kills (4.532–535; [[aegisthus]], [[agamemnon]], [[the-murder-of-agamemnon]]).
