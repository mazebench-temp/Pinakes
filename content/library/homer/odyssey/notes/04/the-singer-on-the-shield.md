**The two texts.** Of the five verses, the last three are the Shield's (4.17–19 = *Iliad* 18.604–606), and they differ in one word: μολπῆς ἐξάρχοντος, “as he led the song and dance”, singular, of the singer, where the printed Iliad has ἐξάρχοντες, of the tumblers (*Iliad* 18.606; see [[tumblers]], [[lyre]] and the translation's notes on 4.19 and on *Iliad* 18.604–606).

**Athenaeus' charge.** W. W. Merry reports the criticism in Athenaeus (5.180): Diodorus, a follower of Aristophanes, struck out the whole wedding (4.3–19), and the dancing and tumbling seemed out of place in the house of Menelaus; the lines, Athenaeus suggests, were added by Aristarchus' school, who did not see that the wedding feast was over, and were transferred from the Hoplopoiia with the very error of diction, since it was not the tumblers who led but the singer (*Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 4.3–19 and 4.15–19; see [[athenaeus]] and [[aristarchus]]). A scholium on the passage says that the five verses are not Homer's but Aristarchus' (Merry's critical note on 4.15–19). Merry himself, discussing the scene, takes the five verses to be an interpolation (on 4.3), and answers the larger excision that without 4.3–19 the passage from 4.2 to 4.20 is very awkward (on 4.3–19).
