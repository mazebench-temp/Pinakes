summary: The guests sit on chairs beside Menelaus after their bath (4.51); Adreste sets a well-made chair for Helen, who sits in it with a footstool under her feet (4.123, 4.136); and Penelope, hearing of the plot against her son, cannot bear to sit on a chair, though there are many in the house (4.716–718).
# The chair as κλισίη (4.123, "a well-made chair", where the Iliad's κλισίη is a hut: hut excepts the line), κλισμός
# (κλισμῷ, 4.136) and δίφρος (4.717), first met here in the Odyssey.
grc: ["κλισίην εὔτυκτον", κλισμῷ, "δίφρῳ ἐφέζεσθαι"]
---

**The guests' seats.** Bathed and dressed, the strangers “sat down on chairs beside Menelaus, son of Atreus” (ἔς ῥα θρόνους, 4.51; see [[feast-type-scene]] and [[xenia]]).

**Helen's chair.** “And with her came Adreste and set for her a well-made chair” (4.123; see [[adreste]]), κλισίη, a word the Iliad uses of the huts of the camp (see the translation's row κλισίη, κλισμός). Bekker, following a reading of the Harleian manuscript, printed εὔπτυκτον, “folding”, an alteration W. W. Merry thinks needless (critical note on 4.123). Alcippe lays a rug of soft wool on it (4.124; see [[blankets]]), and Helen “sat down in the chair, and there was a footstool under her feet” (4.136; see [[footstool]]).

**Penelope's chair.** When Medon has told her of the plot, grief is poured about Penelope, “and she could no longer bear / to sit on a chair, though there were many chairs in the house, / but she sat on the threshold of her chamber” (4.716–718; see [[threshold]] and [[penelope]]). Merry renders: she had no longer the heart to sit on a seat, though there were many in the chamber, but sank down on the floor of her bower crying piteously (on 4.717). δίφρος here is a chair, as it is in later books, not the car of a chariot (see [[chariot]]).
