summary: Menelaus asks what brought Telemachus “over the broad back of the sea” (4.313); the winds are “the escorts / of ships over the broad back of the sea” (4.361–362); Odysseus has no ships to take him over it (4.560); and Eidothea plunges into “the broad bosom of the sea” (4.435).
grc: ["θαλάσσης εὐρέα κόλπον"]
en: [broad bosom]
---

ἐπʼ εὐρέα νῶτα θαλάσσης ends the three verses. The fourth is the verse of Odysseus on Calypso's island, which the poem uses again when the gods send Hermes (4.557–560 = 5.14–17; [[calypso]]). Thetis sends her sisters home with the Iliad's phrase, “Now go down into the broad bosom of the sea” (*Iliad* 18.140); here it is the sea goddess Eidothea who goes down into it, to bring the sealskins (4.435–436; [[eidothea]]).
