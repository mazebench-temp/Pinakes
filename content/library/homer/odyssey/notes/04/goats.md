# αἰγίβοτος (4.606; again of Ithaca, 13.246), claimed here, since no epithet entry has it.
grc: [αἰγίβοτος]
---

**A land for goats.** Declining Menelaus' gift of horses, Telemachus says that in Ithaca “there are no wide courses for running, nor any meadow; / it is a goat-pasturing land, and lovelier than a horse-pasturing one” (4.605–606; see [[ithaca]], [[horses]] and [[telemachus]]). αἰγίβοτος is formed from αἴξ, “goat”, and βόσκω, “feed, pasture”, as ἱππόβοτος, “horse-pasturing”, the epithet of Argos in this book (4.99, 4.562), is formed from ἵππος (see [[horse-pasturing]]). The word comes once more in Homer, again of Ithaca, when Athena describes the island to Odysseus, who does not yet know where he has landed: it is good for goats and good for cattle (αἰγίβοτος δʼ ἀγαθὴ καὶ βούβοτος, 13.246, a plain rendering). The goats themselves are a large part of Odysseus' wealth: eleven wide flocks of them graze at the far end of the island (14.103–104, a plain rendering; see the general article).
