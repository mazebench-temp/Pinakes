---
summary: Odysseus is “brilliant” in the Horse (4.280), Helen “brilliant among women” (4.305), and Mentor “brilliant” in Noemon's puzzle (4.655); and Lacedaemon is “shining” in the words of Menelaus and of the herald Medon (4.313, 4.702).
---

δῖος Ὀδυσσεύς sits in the Horse beside Menelaus and the son of Tydeus (4.280; [[son-of-tydeus]]). Noemon saw “brilliant Mentor” in Ithaca on the morning after he saw a Mentor board the ship for Pylos, “Mentor, or a god” (4.653–656; [[mentor]], [[in-the-likeness-of-mentor]]). Λακεδαίμονα δῖαν, the verse-end of 3.326, comes twice: in Menelaus' question, “What need has brought you here, hero Telemachus, / to shining Lacedaemon” (4.312–313), and in Medon's news to Penelope that her son has gone “to sacred Pylos and to shining Lacedaemon” (4.702; [[egatheos]], [[lacedaemon]]). The δῖαν of the sea belongs to [[shining-sea]] (4.577).
