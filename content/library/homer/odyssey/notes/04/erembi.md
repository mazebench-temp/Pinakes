---
summary: Named once, in the list of the peoples Menelaus came to while he gathered his wealth: “and I came to the Ethiopians and the Sidonians and the Erembi” (4.84).
---

The Erembi stand between the Sidonians and Libya in Menelaus' list, “after wandering to Cyprus and Phoenicia and the Egyptians, / and I came to the Ethiopians and the Sidonians and the Erembi / and to Libya” (4.83–85; see [[the-wanderings-of-menelaus]], [[ethiopians]], [[sidonians]] and [[libya]]). The poem says nothing more of them, and the name is found nowhere else in Homer. Aristarchus read Ἐρεμβούς, Crates Ἐρεμνούς, “dark” (see the translation's note on 4.84), and Merry, who connects the name with ἐρεμνός, takes them for a branch of the Ethiopians on the coast opposite Cyprus (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 4.84; see [[cyprus]]). Strabo found the order of the list exact: Homer, he says, names the far peoples in their order, as Hipparchus also observed, where the poets who send Dionysus or Triptolemus over the earth join places far apart and part those that lie together (*Geography* 1.2.20); for him the Erembi were Arabs of the shore of the Arabian Gulf toward Egypt (1.2.34).
