summary: Telemachus weeps holding his purple cloak before his eyes (4.115, 4.154), and Helen has fine purple blankets thrown on the guests' beds (4.297–298).
# The purple blankets, "πορφύρεʼ ἐμβαλέειν" (4.298), whose English "purple" stands on the line before (4.297);
# the same phrase is claimed in the Iliad's Book 24 note (Il. 24.645).
grc: ["πορφύρεʼ ἐμβαλέειν"]
refs: [4.297]
---

Telemachus “let a tear fall from his eyelids to the ground when he heard of his father, / holding up his purple cloak before his eyes / with both his hands” (4.114–116), and Menelaus repeats the words when he tells Helen of it (4.153–154; see [[cloak]] and [[telemachus]]). Purple is the color of fine cloth: Helen has the slave women “throw fine purple / blankets” on the guests' beds, in the verses of Priam's bed in Achilles' hut (4.297–298 = *Iliad* 24.644–645; see [[blankets]] and [[bed]]). Odysseus will hide his tears among the Phaeacians behind a great purple mantle (8.84–85, a plain rendering).
