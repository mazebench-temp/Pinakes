---
summary: Menelaus is “nurtured by Zeus” in the address of everyone who speaks to him, Eteoneus, Helen, Peisistratus, Telemachus, Eidothea and Proteus (4.26–561); the narrator calls him “the king whom Zeus had nurtured” as the strangers marvel at his house (4.44), and he finds in his guests the lineage “of kings whom Zeus has nurtured” (4.63).
grc: [διοτρεφές, διοτρεφέος]
---

The vocative διοτρεφές opens or closes the addresses: Eteoneus' question, “There are two strangers here, Menelaus, nurtured by Zeus” (4.26); Helen's, “Do we know, Menelaus, nurtured by Zeus, who these men / claim to be” (4.138–139), and again before her tale (4.235); the verse of Peisistratus and Telemachus, “Menelaus, son of Atreus, nurtured by Zeus, leader of the people” (4.156, 4.291, 4.316; [[son-of-atreus]], [[leader-of-men]]); Eidothea's promise (4.391); and Proteus' prophecy, “But for you, Menelaus, nurtured by Zeus, it is not decreed / to die” (4.561–562; [[elysium]]).

Eteoneus' first words of the strangers, that they are “like the lineage of great Zeus” (4.27), Merry takes to imply royalty, kings being generally called διογενεῖς and διοτρεφεῖς (*Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 4.27). Menelaus says the same after the meal: “base men would not beget such sons as you” (4.64; [[scepter-bearing]], [[basileus]]).
