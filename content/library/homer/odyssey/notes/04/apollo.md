summary: Apollo is named once, in Menelaus' wish that Odysseus might come upon the suitors as he was when he wrestled Philomeleides on Lesbos: “If only, father Zeus and Athena and Apollo”.
---

**The formula of the wish.** When Telemachus has told of the suitors in his house, Menelaus likens them to the fawns that a hind lays in a strong lion's lair, and prays: “If only, father Zeus and Athena and Apollo, / such as he was once in well-built Lesbos, / when he stood up and wrestled Philomeleides out of strife, / … if only Odysseus, such as that, would engage the suitors” (4.341–345; see [[if-only-father-zeus]], [[odysseus-wrestles-philomeleides]] and [[the-suitors]]). The verse is the Iliad's opening of a wish, four times there (*Iliad* 2.371, 4.288, 7.132, 16.97), and five times in the Odyssey, here for the first time. Merry quotes Gladstone: Pallas and Apollo alone are invoked with Zeus in this formula, which is used not of any rite or function of theirs but when the speaker would give a peculiar solemnity to some paramount desire; Nitzsch would limit it to wishes beyond the speaker's hope (*Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 4.341; see [[zeus]] and [[athena]]). Menelaus' wish is of that kind. Odysseus is far away on Calypso's island, as Menelaus will report from Proteus later in the same speech (4.555–560), but the wish will be granted in the hall in Book 22.
