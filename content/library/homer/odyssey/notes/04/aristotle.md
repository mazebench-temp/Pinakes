---
summary: Aristotle quotes this book twice in the Rhetoric, the verse of the longing for lament and Menelaus' praise of a speech as long as a wise man's; the History of Animals corrects the horned lambs of Libya; the Problems ascribed to him quote the West Wind of the Elysian plain; and fragments of his Homeric Problems measure the day's sail to Pharos and cite the gods' commands.
---

- The verse that follows Menelaus' memory of Odysseus, “So he spoke, and in them all he roused the longing for lament”, quoted for the pleasure there is in mourning, which lies in remembering the dead and in a way seeing him, what he did and what he was like (*Rhetoric* 1.11, 1370b25–29, quoting 4.183 = *Iliad* 23.108). Merry refers the quotation to 4.113, where the half-line is said of Telemachus alone (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 4.113; see the translation's note on 4.113): [[the-longing-for-lament]].
- Menelaus' answer to Peisistratus, “My friend, since you have said all that a wise man / would say and do” (4.204–205), quoted as far as “wise man” for the limit to the number of enthymemes: Homer says “as much as”, not “such things as” (*Rhetoric* 3.17, 1418a6–9): [[enthymema]].
- Libya, where the horned beasts are born with their horns, “not only the lambs, as Homer says, but the others too” (*History of Animals* 8.28; on 4.85): [[the-horned-lambs-of-libya]].
- The breezes of the West Wind in the Elysian plain, quoted in another form, ἀλλʼ αἰεὶ ζεφύροιο διαπνείουσιν ἀῆται, with the question why the west wind is the sweetest of the winds (*Problems* 26.31; on 4.567): [[elysium]] and [[elysium-and-the-isles-of-the-blessed]].
- The scholia on 4.356 measure the day's sail to Pharos from Naucratis, “where the trading station of Egypt then was, as Aristotle says”, the Nile reaching only so far at that time (*Homeric Problems* fr. 392 Gigon).
- Explaining why Circe is called αὐδήεσσα, Aristotle says that she speaks in human fashion, for the gods speak through signs, birds, victims and dreams, and their utterances are commands, quoting “and the gods always wished their commands to be remembered” (4.353), the verse that Zenodotus rejected (*Homeric Problems* fr. 394 Gigon, from the scholia on *Odyssey* 5.334 and 10.136; see the translation's note on 4.353).
