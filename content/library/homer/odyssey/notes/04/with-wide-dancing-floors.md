---
summary: Noemon needs his ship “to cross over to Elis with its wide dancing floors,” where he keeps twelve mares (4.635–636).
---

Ἤλιδʼ ἐς εὐρύχορον: Merry explains Noemon's paddock in Elis by the poverty of Ithaca in pasture for horses, which Telemachus has just described to Menelaus (*Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 4.635; 4.601–608; [[elis]], [[noemon-son-of-phronius]], [[horse-pasturing]]).
