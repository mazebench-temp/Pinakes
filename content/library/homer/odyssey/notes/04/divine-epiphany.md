---
summary: Two divine figures appear to mortals in need: Eidothea, who meets Menelaus alone on Pharos, is taken for a goddess, teaches him how to catch her father and plunges into the sea (4.364–425); and Athena's phantom, in the likeness of Penelope's sister, which comes to her as she sleeps and is asked whether it is a god (4.795–841). In Ithaca Noemon tells of a Mentor who was perhaps a god (4.653–656).
refs: [4.364-370, 4.376, 4.425, 4.653-656, 4.795-803, 4.831, 4.838-839]

  - 4.795-841 | The phantom comes to Penelope
---

Menelaus knows at once that she is a goddess, “whoever you are of the goddesses” (4.376; [[eidothea]]); Penelope, still asleep, answers “If you are a god and have heard a god's voice” (4.831), and the phantom will not answer her about Odysseus (4.836–837; [[eidolon]], [[dream-scene]]). Noemon saw “Mentor, or a god, who was like him in every way” go on board, and Mentor himself in Ithaca the next morning (4.653–656; [[in-the-likeness-of-mentor]], [[noemon-son-of-phronius]]).
