---
summary: Eurycleia bids Penelope bathe and go up to her upper room with her handmaids to pray, and she does; there she lies without food, pondering whether her son will escape death, until sleep comes upon her and Athena sends the phantom (4.750–841).
---

**The prayer.** “But bathe, and put clean clothes on your body, / and go up to your upper room with your handmaids / and pray to Athena”, Eurycleia tells Penelope, and she does so in the same words (4.750–752, 4.759–761; see [[eurycleia]], [[bathing]] and [[prayer]]). The upper room is the women's place: in Book 1 Penelope heard Phemius' song from it, and went back up to it with her handmaids and wept for Odysseus until Athena cast sweet sleep upon her eyelids (1.328, 1.362–364).

**The night.** While the suitors launch their ship, “up in her upper room prudent Penelope / lay without food, tasting neither food nor drink” (4.787–788; see [[penelope]] and [[fasting]]), pondering like a lion encircled by men, until sweet sleep came upon her (4.789–794). There Athena's phantom comes to her in the likeness of her sister Iphthime (4.795–841; see [[iphthime]] and [[eidolon]]).
