**The host at a distance.** Nestor is named first as a father, in the patronymic of the son who lies asleep beside Telemachus at Sparta (15.4–6, 15.44–48; see [[peisistratus]]). At the parting Menelaus sends him a greeting: “Farewell, young men, and give my greeting to Nestor, shepherd of the people; / for truly he was kind to me like a father, / while we sons of the Achaeans were fighting in Troy” (15.151–153; see [[menelaus]], [[kind-as-a-father]] and [[shepherd-of-the-people]]). At Pylos, Telemachus asks Peisistratus not to take him past the ship, since they are guest-friends “from our fathers' friendship” (15.197), “lest the old man keep me in his house against my will / in his eagerness to make me welcome; but I must get home quickly” (15.200–201; see [[telemachus]] and [[xenia]]). It is the hospitality Nestor pressed on him at their first parting, when he would not let the son of Odysseus sleep on the deck of his ship “while I / am alive, and after me my sons are left in the halls / to entertain the guests” (3.352–355). The city lay some way inland, as Strabo points out, so that the ship could be reached without passing Nestor's door (D. B. Monro, *Homer's Odyssey, Books XIII–XXIV*, 1901, on 15.199; see [[pylos]] and [[strabo-on-nestors-pylos]]).

**His son's account of him.** Peisistratus bids Telemachus embark before he himself reaches home and tells the old man: “For I know this well in my mind and in my heart: / such is his overweening spirit, he will not let you go, / but he will come here himself to call you, and I say that he / will not go back empty-handed; for he will be very angry, for certain” (15.211–214; see [[hyperbios]] and [[cholos]]). The first verse is the Iliad's verse of Agamemnon and of Hector, each foreseeing that sacred Ilios will fall (*Iliad* 4.163, 6.447), and the second is Polydamas' fear of Achilles' spirit (*Iliad* 18.262): here, Monro writes, the words are “only half-serious” (Monro on 15.212; see the translation's note on 15.212). Peisistratus drives back to the city of the Pylians, “and quickly reached the house” (15.216), and the poem says nothing of the old man's anger.
