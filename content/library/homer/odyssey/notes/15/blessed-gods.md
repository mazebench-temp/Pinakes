---
summary: Eumaeus: “But now I lack these things; yet for me myself / the blessed gods make the work grow at which I stay; / from it I have eaten and drunk and given to those I respect” (15.371–373).
---

The swineherd said it before of a god, without the epithet: a good master gives his servant a house and an allotment “when he has toiled much for him, and a god makes his work grow, / as this work of mine grows, at which I stay” (14.65–66). Now he lacks the kindness of Anticleia, and the gods still make the work grow: from its fruits he eats and drinks and gives to the αἰδοῖοι, those who have a claim on his regard, members of the family, guests and suppliants (D. B. Monro, *Homer's Odyssey, Books XIII–XXIV*, 1901, on 15.373; see the translation's note on 15.373, [[aidos]], [[eumaeus]] and [[anticleia]]).
