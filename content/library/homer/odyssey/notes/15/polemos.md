---
summary: Menelaus' farewell to Nestor, “for truly he was kind to me like a father, / while we sons of the Achaeans were fighting in Troy” (15.152–153).
---

The war of the Iliad is in the book only as a memory. Menelaus pledges the two young men at parting and sends his greeting to Nestor, “for truly he was kind to me like a father, / while we sons of the Achaeans were fighting in Troy” (ἧος ἐνὶ Τροίῃ πολεμίζομεν υἷες Ἀχαιῶν, 15.152–153; see [[kind-as-a-father]], [[nestor]], [[sons-of-the-achaeans]] and [[troy]]). The half-verse is the one with which the false Cretan of Book 14 counted his own war, “There we sons of the Achaeans fought for nine years” (14.240; see the Book 14 note), and Nestor's son stands before him as his guest (see [[peisistratus]]).
