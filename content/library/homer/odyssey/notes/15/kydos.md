---
summary: Glory in small things: a meal before a journey brings “glory and splendor and a blessing too” (15.78), and Hermes “grants / grace and glory to the works of all men” (15.319–320), even to a servant's.
---

**The glory of a meal.** Menelaus keeps his guests for one more meal: “It brings both, glory and splendor and a blessing too, / to dine before going over the wide, boundless earth” (ἀμφότερον, κῦδός τε καὶ ἀγλαΐη καὶ ὄνειαρ, 15.78–79; see [[feast]] and [[xenia]]). ἀμφότερον is adverbial: a feast is both an honor (κῦδος καὶ ἀγλαΐη) and a benefit (ὄνειαρ) (D. B. Monro, *Homer's Odyssey, Books XIII–XXIV*, 1901, on 15.78). The host's glory is in sending his guest away fed.

**The glory of work.** In the hut the beggar boasts of his skill in service, “by the favor of Hermes the guide, who grants / grace and glory to the works of all men, / in service no other mortal could rival me, / in heaping up a fire well and splitting dry wood” (χάριν καὶ κῦδος ὀπάζει, 15.319–322; see [[hermes]], [[charis]] and [[peira]]). κῦδος, which in the Iliad the gods give to the side that wins, is given here to a fire well laid, meat well carved and roasted and wine well poured, “such services as the lesser do for their betters” (15.322–324; see [[cupbearing]]): the beggar's claim, made by the man who sacked Troy.
