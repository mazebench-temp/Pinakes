---
summary: Peisistratus on his father: “For I know this well in my mind and in my heart: / such is his overweening spirit, he will not let you go” (15.211–212).
---

The verse is Polydamas' of Achilles, “such is his overweening spirit, he will not be willing” (*Iliad* 18.262); Monro renders ὑπέρβιος “overbearing,” “masterful,” and calls the words here only half-serious (D. B. Monro, *Homer's Odyssey, Books XIII–XXIV*, 1901, on 15.212; see the translation's note on 15.212). The old host's overweening spirit is his eagerness to keep a guest, the fault that Menelaus has just named (15.68–74, 15.199–201; see [[what-is-due-is-better]], [[nestor]] and [[peisistratus]]).
