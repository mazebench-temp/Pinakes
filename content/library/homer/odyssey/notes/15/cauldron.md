# λεβήτων (15.84), the only genitive plural in Homer, first met here; it applies in the Odyssey only.
grc: [λεβήτων]
en: [cauldron]
---

“no one will send us / away just as we came, but each will give us at least one thing to take with us, / either a tripod of good bronze or a cauldron, / or two mules or a golden goblet” (15.82–85; see [[tripod]], [[guest-gifts]] and [[cup]]). The pair is the one that each Phaeacian lord gave Odysseus, “a great tripod and a cauldron” (13.13), where Eustathius knew the reading ἠέ, “or”, the alternative of this verse (see the Book 13 note). In the meal that follows the λέβης is the other vessel of its name, the silver basin over which a handmaid pours water for the hands (15.135–136; see [[basin-and-pitcher]] and [[water-for-the-hands]]).
