---
summary: At Pherae, as on the way out: “And when early-born Dawn appeared, the rosy-fingered” (15.189).
---

15.184–191 are the verses of the night at Pherae and the morning departure in Book 3 (3.486–493; see the Book 3 note and [[chariot-journey]]); Monro counts this as the morning of the 37th day (D. B. Monro, *Homer's Odyssey, Books XIII–XXIV*, 1901, on 15.189). This time the drive is short: they come quickly to Pylos, and Telemachus asks to be left at his ship (15.193–201; see [[dawn-line]], [[rosy-fingered]] and [[eos]]).
