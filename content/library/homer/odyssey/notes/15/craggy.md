---
summary: Theoclymenus at the landing: “And where shall I go, dear child? To whose house shall I come / of the men who rule over craggy Ithaca?” (15.509–510).
---

The seer takes up the verse-end of Telemachus' complaint to Athena, “and all who rule over craggy Ithaca,” the suitors of the islands and of Ithaca itself (1.247; again 16.124, 21.346; see the Book 1 note). The men who rule are the suitors' houses: Telemachus cannot take him to his own, where his mother keeps away from the suitors at her loom, and names Eurymachus instead (15.512–524; see [[eurymachus]], [[the-suitors]] and [[ithaca]]).
