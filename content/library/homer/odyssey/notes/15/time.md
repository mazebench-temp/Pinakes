summary: Worth and honor: Menelaus gives the gift “that is finest and most precious” (15.114); Anticleia, rearing the slave child with her daughter, “honored me little less” (15.365); and Telemachus bids Peiraeus “welcome him with care and honor him” (15.543).
# τιμηέστατον (15.114; again 4.614, the same verse) and τιέμεν (15.543; again 17.56, the same verse), first met here among
# the Odyssey's notes. τιέμεν is from τίω "honor", not τίνω "pay".
grc: [τιμηέστατον, τιέμεν]
---

**Most precious.** Menelaus chooses from the treasures of his house “the one that is finest and most precious” (ὃ κάλλιστον καὶ τιμηέστατόν ἐστι, 15.114 = 4.614; see [[mixing-bowl]] and [[doron]]): τιμή is worth that can be counted in goods, and the gift's value is the measure of the guest's.

**Honored.** Eumaeus remembers Anticleia's kindness: “because she herself reared me together with Ctimene of the long robes, / her strong daughter, whom she bore the youngest of her children; / with her I was reared, and she honored me little less” (ὀλίγον δέ τί μʼ ἧσσον ἐτίμα, 15.363–365; see [[anticleia]], [[ctimene]] and [[slavery-and-freedom]]). The bought child was honored almost as the daughter of the house, until the daughter was married and he was sent to the farm (15.366–370).

**Honor a guest.** Telemachus gives the seer into Peiraeus' keeping: “now too take this stranger for me to your house, / and welcome him with care and honor him, until I come” (ἐνδυκέως φιλέειν καὶ τιέμεν, 15.542–543; see [[xenia]], [[philos]] and [[peiraeus-son-of-clytius]]), the words he repeats when he tells his mother where the stranger is (17.55–56).
