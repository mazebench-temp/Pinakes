**The gifts and the sons.** Amphiaraus “perished at Thebes, for the sake of gifts to a woman. / His sons were Alcmaeon and Amphilochus” (15.247–248; see [[amphiaraus]], [[alcmaeon]] and [[amphilochus]]). The gifts are the bribe with which Eriphyle sent her husband to Thebes: Amphiaraus “was forced to take part in the war of Thebes, in which he fell, because of the necklace given to his wife Eriphyle”, as Monro explains the verse (D. B. Monro, *Homer's Odyssey, Books XIII–XXIV*, 1901, Appendix III §6; see [[the-seven-against-thebes]]). The woman is not named here; the eleventh book named her as the one “who took precious gold as the price of her own husband” (11.326–327; see [[eriphyle]] and the Book 11 note). The verse that follows names the son who avenged his father, and says nothing more of him: the Odyssey knows Alcmaeon only as a name in the seer's genealogy, beside his brother, and tells neither the vengeance nor the war of the sons against Thebes (see [[the-epigonoi]]). The house goes on as a house of seers: Apollo made Polypheides, of the other branch, the best of seers “after Amphiaraus died” (15.252–253; see [[polypheides]] and [[melampus-and-the-cattle-of-iphiclus]]).
