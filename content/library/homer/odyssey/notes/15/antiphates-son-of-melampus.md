The pedigree of the seer Theoclymenus turns at Argos, where Melampus, “ruling over many Argives”, married a wife and built a high-roofed house, “and begot Antiphates and Mantius, two mighty sons” (15.240–242; see [[melampus]], [[theoclymenus]] and [[mantius]]). υἷε κραταιώ, “two mighty sons”, ends the verse as it does at *Iliad* 13.345, of Zeus and Poseidon, “the two mighty sons of Cronus” (see the translation's key words). The poet follows Antiphates' line first, three generations in three verses: “Antiphates begot great-hearted Oicles, / and Oicles Amphiaraus, who rouses armies, … His sons were Alcmaeon and Amphilochus” (15.243–248; see [[oicles]], [[amphiaraus]], [[alcmaeon]], [[amphilochus]] and [[great-hearted]]). Then he comes back to Mantius, whose line leads to the man standing by Telemachus' ship (15.249–256).
