---
summary: Three companies: Telemachus' crew, whom he orders aboard at Pylos and sends on to the city at Ithaca (15.37, 15.217–218, 15.287, 15.496, 15.503), with Peiraeus, “his trusted companion” (15.539); the swineherd's men in the hut, “all you other companions” (15.307); and the Phoenician crew in Eumaeus' story (15.441).
---

**Telemachus' crew.** Athena's orders begin with the companions: on reaching Ithaca he is to “send the ship and all your companions on to the city” (15.37), and he does so on landing (15.503). At Pylos Peisistratus bids him “Go aboard now in haste, and bid all your companions do the same” (15.209), and “Telemachus urged on his companions and gave them orders: / ‘Put the arms in order, companions, in the black ship’” (15.217–218; see [[sailing-type-scene]]), where τὰ τεύχεα are the arms, not the rigging, which is taken in hand later (D. B. Monro, *Homer's Odyssey, Books XIII–XXIV*, 1901, on 15.218). When the seer is aboard, “Telemachus urged on his companions and bade them / lay hold of the tackle” (15.287–288), and at dawn off Ithaca “the companions of Telemachus loosed the sails, and took down the mast” (15.496). He had told the seer that he went to Pylos with “companions and a black ship” (15.269; see [[to-learn-of-his-fathers-homecoming]]). The seer, who had begged him “by your own head and those of the companions who follow you” (15.262; see [[supplication]]), calls him “apart from his companions” to read the falcon (15.529; see [[the-omen-of-the-falcon]]). Of the crew Telemachus picks out Peiraeus, “his trusted companion” (πιστὸν ἑταῖρον, 15.539; see [[trusted-companion]] and [[peiraeus-son-of-clytius]]), the one who obeys him most “of my companions who followed me to Pylos” (15.540–541; see [[peitho]]), and who takes command of the ship when Telemachus goes to the swineherd (Monro on 15.547).

**The swineherd's men.** In the hut the beggar addresses the company as on the night before, “Hear me now, Eumaeus, and all you other companions” (15.307 = 14.462), and offers to go “so that I may not wear you and your companions out” (15.309); Eumaeus answers that no one is vexed by his presence, “neither I nor any other of the companions who are with me” (15.336; see [[xenia]]).

**The Phoenician crew.** In Eumaeus' story the nurse makes the traders' crew keep the secret: “let none of your companions speak to me, / meeting me either in the street / or perhaps at the spring” (15.440–442; see [[silence]], [[the-kidnapping-of-eumaeus]] and [[the-phoenician-nurse]]).
