---
summary: The book opens as Athena goes to “Lacedaemon with its wide dancing floors” to remind Telemachus of his homecoming, and its first part is spent there, in the house of Menelaus (15.1–181).
---

“But Pallas Athena went to Lacedaemon with its wide dancing floors, / to the shining son of great-hearted Odysseus, / to remind him of his homecoming and to urge him to return” (1–3; see [[athena]], [[telemachus]] and [[with-wide-dancing-floors]]). The verse takes up the end of Book 13, where she “went to shining Lacedaemon, after the son of Odysseus” (13.440; see the Book 13 note): “The scene changes to Sparta, whither we follow Athene”, and since Book 14 ended with the coming on of night in the hut, the story begins on the next day, the 36th, though “the chronology is not quite clear” (D. B. Monro, *Homer's Odyssey, Books XIII–XXIV*, 1901, on 15.1). εὐρύχορον is the epithet Athena used for the land when she told Odysseus where his son had gone (13.414). Lacedaemon is named only in this verse of the book, which is spent until verse 181 in Menelaus' house, at the leave-taking, the gifts and [[the-omen-of-the-eagle-and-the-goose|the omen of the eagle]]; then the chariot speeds “through the city toward the plain” and reaches Pherae at sunset (182–186; see [[menelaus]], [[helen]] and [[pherae-in-messenia]]).
