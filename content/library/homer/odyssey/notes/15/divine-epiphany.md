---
summary: Athena comes to Sparta to send Telemachus home: she finds him awake among the sleepers, “Standing near him, gray-eyed Athena spoke to him” (15.9), gives her warning and her instructions, and “So saying, she went away to high Olympus” (15.43).
refs: [15.1-9, 15.43]
---

The goddess comes in her own name, with no likeness given, to a man who is awake. “Standing near him” is the verse with which she came to Odysseus by the ships in *Iliad* 2, after the same finding, εὗρεν, of a man troubled in mind: Odysseus standing grieved by his ship, Telemachus kept awake by cares for his father (15.4–9; *Iliad* 2.169–172). There Odysseus “knew the voice of the goddess as she spoke” and ran (*Iliad* 2.182); here Telemachus says nothing of her, but wakes Peisistratus at once and asks for the horses (15.44–47). It is not a dream: in the dream-scene the figure stands above the sleeper's head (4.803, 6.21; see [[dream-scene]]), as Athena will stand over Odysseus when he lies awake in Book 20, “like a woman in form” (20.30–33, a plain rendering).
