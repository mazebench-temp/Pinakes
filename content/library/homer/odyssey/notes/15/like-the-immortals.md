---
summary: Eumaeus: “and over both of them my father was king, / Ctesius, son of Ormenus, like the immortals” (15.413–414).
---

The verse is built like the Iliad's “Maeon son of Haemon, like the immortals” (*Iliad* 4.394), name, patronymic and epithet, and like the verse of Iphitus, son of Eurytus, in Book 21 (21.14). ἐπιείκελος ἀθανάτοισιν, the nominative, is new to the entry in the Odyssey. The king of the blessed island is Eumaeus' father, and the swineherd a king's son (see [[ctesius]], [[ormenus-father-of-ctesius]], [[syrie]] and [[eumaeus]]).
