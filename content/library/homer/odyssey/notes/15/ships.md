summary: Two ships carry the book's two threads. Telemachus' ship waits at Pylos, takes the seer Theoclymenus on board, sails home by night past the suitors' ambush and lands on the coast of Ithaca, where Peiraeus takes it on to the city (15.33–37, 15.199–300, 15.495–553). In Eumaeus' story a Phoenician ship trades on Syrie for a year and carries off the child and his nurse, who dies on the voyage and falls into the bilge (15.415–483).
# The phrase "ὅπλων ἅπτεσθαι", "lay hold of the tackle" (15.288; earlier 2.423), first claimed here; it applies in the
# Odyssey only, since ὅπλων alone is also the arms of the Poetics and the Rhetoric. ἄντλῳ (15.479) is the entry's.

**Athena's orders.** “But keep your well-made ship far from the islands, / and sail by night as well”, and at the first shore of Ithaca “send the ship and all your companions on to the city”, while Telemachus himself goes to the swineherd (15.33–38; see [[athena]], [[well-made]] and [[the-journey-of-telemachus]]). The islands are Dulichium, Same and Zacynthus, to be given a wide berth (see the translation's note on 15.33–34).

**At Pylos.** “Do not take me past the ship”, Telemachus asks Peisistratus, who turns the horses “to the swift ship and the shore of the sea” and puts the gifts in its stern (15.199–207; see [[peisistratus]] and [[stern]]). Telemachus bids the crew “Put the arms in order, companions, in the black ship” (15.218; see [[black-ships]]): τὰ τεύχεα are the arms, like the shields and spears with which the suitors' ship was laden (16.474), not the rigging, which is handled later (D. B. Monro, *Homer's Odyssey, Books XIII–XXIV*, 1901, on 15.218; see [[spear]]). The seer Theoclymenus finds him praying and sacrificing to Athena beside the ship and begs, “take me on board the ship” (15.222–277; see [[theoclymenus]] and [[supplication]]), and Telemachus will not drive him “from the balanced ship”: he lays the stranger's bronze spear on the deck “of the curving ship”, goes on board “the seafaring ship” himself and seats the seer beside him in the stern (15.280–286; see [[balanced-ships]], [[curving]], [[seafaring-ship]], [[deck]] and [[stern]]).

**The voyage.** “And Telemachus urged on his companions and bade them / lay hold of the tackle” (15.287–288), the verses of the departure from Ithaca (2.422–423): ὅπλα, the ship's tackle, are claimed here in the phrase, since the word alone is also arms (see the Book 10 note). They raise the mast and hoist the sails (15.289–291; see [[mast]], [[mast-box]], [[forestays]] and [[sails]]), and Athena sends a wind “so that as quickly as possible / the ship, running, might cross the salt water of the sea” (15.292–294; see [[sailing-type-scene]]); ὕδωρ goes with ἀνύσειε, or perhaps rather with ἀνύσειε θέουσα taken together, which is equivalent to διαπρήξειε (Monro on 15.294). The ship passes Elis by night and makes for the islands, Telemachus “pondering whether he would escape death or be caught” (15.295–300; see [[the-suitors]] and [[ambush]]).

**The Phoenician ship.** The beggar asks whether hostile men took Eumaeus “in their ships” (15.386–388), and the swineherd's story is of one ship. The Phoenicians came to [[syrie|Syrie]], “bringing countless baubles in a black ship” (15.415–416; see [[phoenicians]] and [[trade]]); one of them lay with the nurse “by the hollow ship” (15.420; see [[the-phoenician-nurse]]); she bade them send word to the house “when the ship is full of substance”, and promised to bring the child on board (15.446–452). They stayed a year, “and got much substance by trading for their hollow ship”, and when “the hollow ship was laden for their going” they sent a messenger (15.455–458; see [[hollow-ships]]). By night the nurse brought the child to the harbor, “where the swift-sailing ship of the Phoenician men was” (15.472–473; see [[swift-sailing-ships]] and [[harbor]]), and they sailed six days; on the seventh Artemis struck the woman, “and she fell into the bilge with a splash, like a sea bird” (15.476–479; see [[artemis]] and [[like-a-sea-bird]]). ἄντλος is the bilge, the hold of the ship (see the entry); the crew threw her overboard, “to become a spoil for seals and fish”, and the wind and the water brought them to Ithaca (15.480–482; see [[seals]] and [[the-kidnapping-of-eumaeus]]).

**The landing.** At dawn the crew loose the sails, take down the mast, bring the ship in with oars, cast out the mooring-stones and make fast the stern-cables, ending in the verses of the landing at Chryse (15.495–499; 15.497–499 ~ *Iliad* 1.435–437; see [[oars]], [[mooring-stones]] and [[stern-cables]]). “You now row the black ship on to the city”, Telemachus tells them (15.503), and the omen of the falcon falls “between the ship and Telemachus himself” (15.528; see [[the-omen-of-the-falcon]]). Peiraeus goes on board and takes command, carrying out Telemachus' orders (15.547; Monro on 15.547; see [[peiraeus-son-of-clytius]]); the crew loose the stern-cables, sit down at the benches, and “They pushed off and sailed to the city” (15.548–553; see [[rowing-benches]]), ἀνώσαντες, “pushing off”, out to sea (Monro on 15.553).
