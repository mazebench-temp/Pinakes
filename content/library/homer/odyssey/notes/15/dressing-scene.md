---
summary: Telemachus dresses in haste to meet Menelaus: “in haste he put his glossy tunic about his body, / and threw a great mantle over his massive shoulders” (15.60–61); and on Ithaca he sets out for the swineherd's hut: “And Telemachus bound under his feet his fine sandals, / and he took a strong spear, tipped with sharp bronze, / from the deck of the ship” (15.550–552).
refs: [15.60-62, 15.550-552]
---

**At Sparta.** The tunic and then the great mantle, a garment to a verse, as Agamemnon dresses at the beginning of *Iliad* 2, “He sat up and put on his soft tunic, / fine and new-made, and threw his great cloak around him” (*Iliad* 2.42–43); here “in haste,” σπερχόμενος, as Menelaus comes up from his bed (15.57–62; see [[glossy]], [[tunic]] and [[cloak]]). The verse that names Telemachus at the end of the scene, 15.63, is omitted in many manuscripts (see the translation's note on 15.63, 15.74, 15.139).

**On Ithaca.** The sandals and the spear, in the two verses with which Athena set out from Olympus in Book 1 (1.96, 1.99), without the divine sandals that carried her over the sea (see the note on Book 1, and [[sandals]], [[strong-spear]] and [[arming-scene]]). He binds on his sandals again when he goes from the hut to the city (17.2).
