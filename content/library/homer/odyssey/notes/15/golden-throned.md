---
summary: “So he spoke, and at once Dawn of the golden throne came” (15.56), at the end of Peisistratus' speech; and in the line of Melampus, “but Cleitus was carried off by Dawn of the golden throne / for the sake of his beauty, so that he might dwell among the immortals” (15.250–251).
---

The verse of 15.56 is the Odyssey's dawn after a speech (10.541, 12.142; see the general article); here it answers Peisistratus' “and soon it will be dawn” (15.50). At 15.250 the same epithet belongs to Dawn as the lover of a mortal, who took Cleitus for his beauty as she took Orion (5.121; see [[cleitus-son-of-mantius]], [[dawn-and-orion]] and [[eos]]); for 15.251 see [[immortals]].
