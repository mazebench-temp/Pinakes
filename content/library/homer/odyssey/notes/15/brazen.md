---
summary: The nurse to the Phoenician: “I claim to be from Sidon, rich in bronze, / and I am the daughter of Arybas, whose wealth came in a flood” (15.425–426).
---

Σιδῶνος πολυχάλκου, the genitive, is new to the entry: the city is rich in bronze as Troy once was in men's mouths (*Iliad* 18.289; see the general article). The wealth is a trader's: Monro takes ῥυδόν, “in a stream,” for a piece of mercantile argot, and Ἀρύβας for a Phoenician name, perhaps Hasdrubal (D. B. Monro, *Homer's Odyssey, Books XIII–XXIV*, 1901, on 15.426; see the translation's note on 15.426, [[sidon]], [[arybas]] and [[phoenicians]]). The sky of the book is not of bronze but of iron, the sky that the suitors' arrogance reaches (15.329; see [[sky]]).
