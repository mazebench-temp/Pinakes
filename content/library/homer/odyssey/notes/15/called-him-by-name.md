---
summary: Helen stands beside Telemachus “holding the robe in her hands, and spoke to him, and called him by name” (15.124); and Theoclymenus takes him apart, “and he clung to his hand, and spoke to him, and called him by name” (15.530).
---

At 15.124 the gesture before the half-verse is the gift held out, as when Aphrodite puts the strap into Hera's hands (*Iliad* 14.218); Helen's first words are “dear child” (15.125; see [[robe]] and [[helen]]). 15.530 is the whole verse of Antinous' mocking clasp in the assembly (2.302); here the stranger clings to Telemachus' hand to tell him the meaning of the falcon (15.529–534; see [[theoclymenus]] and [[the-omen-of-the-falcon]]).
