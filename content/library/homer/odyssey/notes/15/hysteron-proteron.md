---
summary: Peiraeus “bade his companions / to go on board themselves and loose the stern-cables” (15.547–548), though the cables are loosed before they embark; and Menelaus offers, “so that I myself may go with you, and I will yoke the horses for you” (15.81).
refs: [15.81, 15.548]
---

15.548 is the verse of Odysseus' orders to his crews (9.178, 9.562, 11.637, 12.145), which D. B. Monro calls a prothysteron, “since they must have unfastened the cables before embarking. The embarkation is put first as being the main action” (*Homer's Odyssey, Books XIII–XXIV*, 1901, on 15.548, comparing 13.274; see [[sailing-type-scene]] and [[stern-cables]]). At 15.81 the yoking is named after the going because “The clause ὑποζεύξω κτλ. is subordinate in sense to ἕπωμαι: hence the prothysteron” (Monro on 15.81, comparing 13.274 and 14.209; see the translation's note on 15.78–85). The English keeps the Greek order in both.
