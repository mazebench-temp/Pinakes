summary: In Eumaeus' story the Phoenician nurse will go with the traders only under oath: “if you sailors would be willing / to pledge me with an oath to bring me home unharmed”; they all swear, and when they have completed the oath she gives them her orders (15.435–444). She never comes home: on the seventh day at sea Artemis strikes her down (15.477–481).

**The nurse's terms.** The Phoenician who has lain with her by the ship asks whether she would come home with them, to see the high-roofed house of her father and mother, “For truly they are still alive and are reputed rich” (15.430–433; see [[the-kidnapping-of-eumaeus]], [[phoenicians]] and [[the-phoenician-nurse]]). Her answer names the condition: “That might well be, if you sailors would be willing / to pledge me with an oath to bring me home unharmed” (15.435–436). ὅρκῳ πιστωθῆναι is to give a pledge by oath (see the translation's table of key words). “So she spoke, and they all swore as she bade them. / But when they had sworn and completed the oath” (15.437–438; see [[oath-formulas]]). The verses are those of the oath of Odysseus' companions not to touch the cattle of the Sun, and of the suitors' oath not to strike the beggar for Irus, with one word changed: there they “swore that they would not”, ἀπώμνυον, a promise to refrain; here the sailors swear to do a thing, ἐπώμνυον (12.303–304; 18.58–59, a plain rendering).

**The oath and the bargain.** Sworn, they receive her orders: silence in the street and at the spring, lest the old man suspect and bind her, then word at the house when the ship is laden; she will bring gold and the king's child as her passage money (15.439–453; see [[silence]] and [[trade]]). The oath is the only thing she asks for herself, and it is not put to the test. On the seventh day of the voyage Artemis strikes her, and the sailors throw her overboard as a spoil for seals and fish (15.477–481; see [[artemis]] and [[like-a-sea-bird]]). The oath that brought her aboard binds them to nothing more, and the child she brought is sold in Ithaca (15.482–483; see [[oaths-and-their-breaking]] and [[slavery-and-freedom]]).
