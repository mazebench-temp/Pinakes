---
summary: The suppliant seer begs Telemachus “by the burnt offerings and by the god” to whom he is sacrificing (15.261).
en: [god]
---

Theoclymenus finds Telemachus sacrificing to Athena at the stern, and supplicates him by what he sees: “My friend, since I find you making sacrifice in this place, / I beg you by the burnt offerings and by the god, and then / by your own head and those of the companions who follow you, / tell me truly, as I ask, and do not hide it” (λίσσομʼ ὑπὲρ θυέων καὶ δαίμονος, 15.260–263; see [[supplication]], [[sacrifice]] and [[theoclymenus]]). The god is Athena (15.222), whom the stranger does not name: he begs by the power to whom the offerings are made (see the entry). θύεα are “burnt offerings”, as in the offering the Trojan women are to bring to Athena's temple (*Iliad* 6.270; see the translation's table of key words).
