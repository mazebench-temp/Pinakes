---
summary: Peisistratus lays Menelaus' gifts “in the wicker body of the chariot” (15.131), the πεῖρινς that the Iliad binds on Priam's wagon.
---

“And the hero Peisistratus laid them in the wicker body of the chariot / when he had received them, and gazed at them all in his heart” (15.131–132; see [[peisistratus]], [[chariot]] and [[guest-gifts]]). The πεῖρινς is the body bound on the frame of a wagon, in which Priam's ransom for Hector was loaded (*Iliad* 24.190, 24.267; see the entry and the translation's row πεῖρινς); here it is fitted to a chariot, and holds a guest's gifts. The Suda explains πείρινθα as the upper part of the wagon, into which the load is put (π 1459). θηήσατο θυμῷ is “Gazed upon them with his heart”, that is, “with all his thoughts centred upon them” (D. B. Monro, *Homer's Odyssey, Books XIII–XXIV*, 1901, on 15.132).
