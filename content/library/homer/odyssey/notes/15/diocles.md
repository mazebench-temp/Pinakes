“And the sun set, and all the streets grew dark; / and they came to Pherae, to the house of Diocles, / son of Ortilochus, whom Alpheus begot as his son. / There they spent the night, and he set good cheer before them” (15.185–188; see [[pherae-in-messenia]], [[ortilochus]], [[alpheus]] and [[guest-gifts]]). The four verses are the night of the outward journey (3.487–490), and with the dawn line and the departure that follow they make the whole stage again, from Sparta to Pherae and from Pherae to Pylos (15.184–193 ≈ 3.486–494; D. B. Monro, *Homer's Odyssey, Books XIII–XXIV*, 1901, on 15.184–192; see [[chariot-journey]] and [[sunset-lines]]). ξείνια are the “good cheer” of a host's table, as at 3.490 (see the translation's key words). The host who receives the son of Odysseus on both nights is the father of the twins whom Aeneas killed at Troy (*Iliad* 5.541–560; see [[crethon]] and [[orsilochus-son-of-diocles]]), but the Odyssey does not say so.
