**The farmstead.** After supper in the hut Odysseus speaks, “testing the swineherd, / whether he would still welcome him with care and bid him stay / there at the farmstead, or would urge him on to the city” (15.304–306; see [[peira]], [[eumaeus]] and [[hut]]), and Eumaeus bids him stay until Telemachus comes (15.335–339; see [[cloak]]).

**Telemachus at the fold.** Athena had told Telemachus to go first of all to the swineherd and to spend the night there (15.38–40; see [[athena]]). He lands at a point not far from the farmstead (D. B. Monro, *Homer's Odyssey, Books XIII–XXIV*, 1901, on 15.545), sends the ship on to the city, and the book ends with his walk: “and his feet carried him quickly on as he strode, until he came to the fold, / where his countless swine were, among which the swineherd, / being good, used to sleep, kind to his masters” (15.555–557; see [[telemachus]], [[boar]] and [[the-title-of-odyssey-15]]). The swineherd sleeps among the swine, as he went out to lie beside the boars on the night of the storm (14.524–533; see the Book 14 note). For “as he strode” Murray prints Aristarchus' προβιβάντα, where the manuscripts have προβιβῶντα (Monro on 15.555; see the translation's note on 15.555).
