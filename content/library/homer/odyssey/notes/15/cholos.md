---
summary: Two angers: Nestor's, which Peisistratus foresees if Telemachus is let go without a visit, “for he will be very angry, for certain” (15.214); and Polypheides', who “moved away to Hyperesia, angry with his father” (15.254).
---

**A host's anger.** Peisistratus agrees to leave Telemachus at the ship and warns him to hurry: Nestor will not let him go, “but he will come here himself to call you, and I say that he / will not go back empty-handed; for he will be very angry, for certain” (μάλα γὰρ κεχολώσεται ἔμπης, 15.213–214; see [[nestor]] and [[xenia]]). ἔμπης has here a general affirmative force, “for certain” (D. B. Monro, *Homer's Odyssey, Books XIII–XXIV*, 1901, on 15.214), and the warning, with Nestor's “overweening spirit” just before it, is only half-serious (Monro on 15.212; see [[thymos]]): the anger is that of a host whose kindness is refused.

**A son's anger.** In the line of Melampus, Polypheides, made by Apollo the best of seers, “moved away to Hyperesia, angry with his father, / and living there he prophesied for all mortals” (ἀπενάσσατο πατρὶ χολωθείς, 15.254–255; see [[polypheides]], [[mantius]], [[hyperesia]] and [[pater]]). The seer's family is a family of exiles: Melampus fled Pylos (15.228), Polypheides left his father, and his son [[theoclymenus|Theoclymenus]] flees Argos for a killing (15.224, 15.272–276; see [[exile-and-refuge]]).
