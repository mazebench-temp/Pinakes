**In Athena's warning.** Athena urges Telemachus home “so that you may still find your noble mother at home” (15.15; see [[blameless]]), and gives her reason: “For already her father and her brothers are bidding her / marry Eurymachus” (15.16–17; see [[icarius]] and [[eurymachus]]). Only here does the poem say that her brothers press her, and it never names them; Penelope herself tells the stranger that “my parents urge me on hard / to marry” (19.158–159, a plain rendering), while the suitors would not go to her father's house so that Icarius might settle the bride-price (2.52–54). Strabo quotes 15.16–17 with 2.52–53 to show that her father and brothers were alive and lived not in Lacedaemon, where Telemachus would have lodged with them, but in Acarnania (*Geography* 10.2.24; see [[the-icarius-problem]] and [[strabo]]). Athena goes on: “beware lest against your will she carry some possession from the house. / For you know what kind of heart is in a woman's breast” (15.19–20; see [[a-womans-heart]]). Aristophanes rejected 19 for the meanness it appeals to, but it is needed for the next sentence, and “The ancient critics were apt to judge Homeric sentiment from their own standard” (D. B. Monro, *Homer's Odyssey, Books XIII–XXIV*, 1901, on 15.19; see the translation's note on 15.19, 15.24–26, 15.91). The goddess means to send Telemachus home quickly, and her picture of his mother is not the poem's own.

**News for her.** Telemachus is to send the swineherd into the city “to bring the news to prudent Penelope / that you are safe and have come back from Pylos” (15.40–42; see [[periphron]] and [[eumaeus]]), and the disguised Odysseus, testing his host, proposes to go to the house of Odysseus and “tell my news to prudent Penelope” (15.313–314; see [[odysseus]] and [[peira]]). Helen's robe is to wait for Telemachus' bride in her keeping: “let it lie with your dear mother / in the hall” (15.127–128; see [[helen]] and [[robe]]).

**The mistress.** Eumaeus, who loved her mother-in-law, has no kindness from her: “But from my mistress it is not possible to hear anything kindly, / neither word nor deed, since evil has fallen on the house, / overbearing men; and the slaves greatly long / to speak face to face with their mistress and learn of everything” (15.374–377; see [[anticleia]], [[the-suitors]] and [[slavery-and-freedom]]).

**At her loom.** At the landing [[theoclymenus|Theoclymenus]] asks whether he should go straight to Telemachus' mother and his house; Telemachus answers that she would not see him, “for she does not often show herself to the suitors in the house, / but away from them she weaves at her loom in the upper room” (15.511–517; see [[weaving]], [[loom]] and [[upper-room]]). ἀπὸ τῶν, “away from them”, is the article as an unemphatic pronoun, a use hardly found except with prepositions (Monro on 15.517). In the same speech he names the suitor who is most eager “to marry my mother” (15.521–524; see [[eurymachus]]).
