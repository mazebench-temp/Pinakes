---
summary: “And when the dear son of Odysseus caught sight of him” (15.59), Telemachus dresses and goes out to Menelaus; he is “Telemachus, the dear son of divine Odysseus” as he speaks (15.63) and as his ship sails on to the city (15.554); and Eumaeus repeats his promise: “But when the dear son of Odysseus comes, / he will clothe you in a cloak and a tunic as clothing” (15.337–338).
---

The whole verse Τηλέμαχος, φίλος υἱὸς Ὀδυσσῆος θείοιο, which the entry claims from this book, is first used here and returns in the later books (17.3, 20.283, 21.432). 15.63 is missing in most manuscripts, and Monro brackets it (D. B. Monro, *Homer's Odyssey, Books XIII–XXIV*, 1901, critical note on 15.63; see the translation's note on 15.63, 15.74, 15.139). 15.337–339 are nearly the verses of the first night (14.515–517; see the Book 14 note), and this time the son comes: the next book brings him to the hut (16.4–16; see [[telemachus]] and [[divine]]).
