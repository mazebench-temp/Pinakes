---
summary: Athena: “But urge Menelaus, good at the war cry, as quickly as you can / to send you on” (15.14–15); and four times “Menelaus, good at the war cry” at the leave-taking: he comes from his bed to the young men (15.57), answers Telemachus (15.67), hears his wish to go (15.92), and bids Eteoneus kindle a fire (15.97).
---

The accusative βοὴν ἀγαθὸν Μενέλαον, the Iliad's (*Iliad* 4.220, 13.581, 17.237), is used only here in the Odyssey, and the entry claims it from this book. The nominative ends the verse four times in a scene of peace, the hospitality of the morning of departure, as in Book 4 (see the Book 4 note, [[menelaus]] and [[eteoneus]]).
