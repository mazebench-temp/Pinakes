---
summary: “And Odysseus, sprung from Zeus, answered him” (15.485), when Eumaeus has told his story.
---

The narrator gives Odysseus the bare title, without the name of his father, three times: as Athena sends the North Wind to bring him to the Phaeacians (5.382–387), here, and in the night of tales with Penelope, when “Odysseus, sprung from Zeus, all the sorrows that he had brought / upon men … all this he told” (23.306–308, a plain rendering). This is its only frame of reply (see [[answering-him]]). The answer turns the sorrows of the swineherd's story to the good that Zeus has set beside the evil (15.486–492; see [[zeus-gives-good-and-evil]] and [[the-kidnapping-of-eumaeus]]).
