---
summary: Athena's second warning, “And I will tell you something else” (15.27); the formulas of address, winged words (15.208, 15.259) and the word with which a speaker “called him by name” (15.124, 15.530); the mistress from whom the slaves hear no kindly “word nor deed” (15.375); the nurse who will let no sailor speak to her (15.440); and Telemachus' answer to the seer, “Would that this word, stranger, might be fulfilled” (15.536).
---

**Words of warning and address.** Athena turns from Penelope's marriage to the ambush with “And I will tell you something else, and you take heed of it in your heart” (ἄλλο δέ τοί τι ἔπος ἐρέω, 15.27; see [[ambush]] and [[thymos]]). The book's speeches are introduced by the formulas: Peisistratus and [[theoclymenus|Theoclymenus]] address Telemachus “with winged words” (15.208, 15.259; see [[winged-words]]), and Helen, giving the robe, and Theoclymenus, taking his hand, “spoke to him, and called him by name” (ἔπος τʼ ἔφατʼ ἔκ τʼ ὀνόμαζε, 15.124, 15.530; see [[called-him-by-name]]).

**Word and deed.** In the hut Eumaeus complains of his mistress since the suitors came: “But from my mistress it is not possible to hear anything kindly, / neither word nor deed, since evil has fallen on the house” (οὔτʼ ἔπος οὔτε τι ἔργον, 15.374–375; see [[penelope]] and [[the-suitors]]). The pair is a zeugma, for “to hear” suits only the word (D. B. Monro, *Homer's Odyssey, Books XIII–XXIV*, 1901, on 15.375). In his story the nurse demands silence of the Phoenician crew: “Silence now, and let none of your companions speak to me, / meeting me either in the street / or perhaps at the spring” (μή τίς με προσαυδάτω ἐπέεσσιν, 15.440–442; see [[silence]], [[the-phoenician-nurse]] and [[the-kidnapping-of-eumaeus]]).

**A word fulfilled.** When [[theoclymenus|Theoclymenus]] has read the falcon, Telemachus answers with a wish: “Would that this word, stranger, might be fulfilled; / then you would quickly know friendship and many gifts / from me” (αἲ γὰρ τοῦτο, ξεῖνε, ἔπος τετελεσμένον εἴη, 15.536–538; see [[it-will-be-fulfilled]] and [[the-omen-of-the-falcon]]). Penelope will say the same three verses to the seer and to the beggar (17.163–165, 19.309–311).
