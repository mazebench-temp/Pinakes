---
summary: “Odysseus of many wiles” asks the swineherd how he came to be sold (15.380).
---

When Eumaeus has told of Laertes, Anticleia and [[ctimene|Ctimene]], “Answering him, Odysseus of many wiles said” (τὸν δʼ ἀπαμειβόμενος προσέφη πολύμητις Ὀδυσσεύς, 15.380; see [[of-many-wiles]] and [[odysseus]]), and asks how the swineherd was taken from his parents (15.381–388). The epithet stands in the formula of answer, but it falls on a man in disguise who learns, by asking, the history of the servant he is testing (see [[peira]] and [[slavery-and-freedom]]).
