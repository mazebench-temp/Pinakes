---
summary: In Eumaeus' story the king's counselors have gone out “to the session and the talk of the people” when the nurse steals the cups from the porch (15.466–469). φῆμις, elsewhere the talk itself, is here, Monro thinks, the place where the people talk.
---

**The place of talk.** The nurse leads the child out through the porch, where the cups and the tables of the banqueters had been left, for the men “who attended on my father” were away: “they had gone out to the session and the talk of the people” (15.466–468; see [[the-kidnapping-of-eumaeus]], [[the-phoenician-nurse]] and [[feast]]). The banqueters are the king's counselors, like the twelve elders of Phaeacia, and θῶκος is their sitting-place or tribunal; φῆμις, “which elsewhere means the ‘talk’ of the people, is here (= ἀγορή) the place of talking” (D. B. Monro, *Homer's Odyssey, Books XIII–XXIV*, 1901, on 15.467–468; see the translation's note on 15.466–468, and [[assembly]] and [[elders]]). The translation keeps “the talk of the people”, the English of δήμοιό τε φῆμιν where Penelope weighs her husband's bed and “the talk of the people” (16.75 = 19.527, plain renderings). On [[syrie|Syrie]] the talk is the business of the men who govern, and while they are at it the house is left to the women and the child.
