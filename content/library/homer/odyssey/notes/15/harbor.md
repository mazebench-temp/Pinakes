---
summary: In Eumaeus' story the nurse leads the child at nightfall to “the famous harbor” where the Phoenician ship lay (15.471–473); and at dawn Telemachus' companions row their ship in “to the anchorage” on the coast of Ithaca, in the verses of the landing at Chryse in the Iliad (15.495–500).
---

**The famous harbor.** “And the sun set, and all the streets grew dark; / and we went quickly and came to the famous harbor, / where the swift-sailing ship of the Phoenician men was” (471–473; see [[syrie]], [[phoenicians]] and [[the-kidnapping-of-eumaeus]]). λιμένα κλυτόν, “the famous harbor”, is said only here and of the harbor of the Laestrygonians, “When we had come into the famous harbor, around which a rock” runs on both sides (10.87; see [[laestrygonians]] and [[famous]]).

**To the anchorage.** At dawn of the 38th day the ship from Pylos comes in to the coast of Ithaca: “And by the shore / the companions of Telemachus loosed the sails, and took down the mast / quickly, and brought her forward to the anchorage with oars; / they cast out the mooring-stones and made fast the stern-cables, / and they themselves stepped out where the sea breaks upon the shore, / and made ready their meal and mixed the sparkling wine” (495–500; see [[sailing-type-scene]], [[mooring-stones]], [[stern-cables]] and [[shore]]). Verses 497–499 are, with one word changed, the landing of Odysseus' ship at Chryse (*Iliad* 1.435–437), and ὅρμος here is the anchorage, the place where a ship is moored, as in the “measure of anchorage” of the harbor of Phorcys (13.101; see the Book 13 note). For προέρυσσαν, “brought her forward”, Aristarchus read προέρεσσαν, “rowed her forward”, as in the Iliad, which D. B. Monro prints; ἐπὶ χέρσου means that they had now reached the land, not that they had landed, and the mast is taken down before the ship is run aground (*Homer's Odyssey, Books XIII–XXIV*, 1901, on 15.495, and his critical note on 15.497; see the translation's note on 15.495–497). The poem does not name the place: from it the ship goes on to the city, and Telemachus walks to the swineherd's fold (503–557; see [[ithaca]] and [[fold]]).
