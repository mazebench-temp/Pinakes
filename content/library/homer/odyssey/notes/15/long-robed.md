summary: “But Helen of the long robes was before him, and said” (15.171); and Eumaeus: “because she herself reared me together with Ctimene of the long robes, / her strong daughter, whom she bore the youngest of her children” (15.363–364).
---

τὸν δʼ Ἑλένη τανύπεπλος opens the verse as in her answer to Priam on the wall (*Iliad* 3.228; see the Book 4 note), here when she reads the omen before her husband can (15.169–178; see [[helen]] and [[the-omen-of-the-eagle-and-the-goose]]). τανυπέπλῳ, new to the entry, is the epithet of Ctimene, Odysseus' sister, who is named only here (15.363–367; see [[ctimene]] and [[mighty]]).
