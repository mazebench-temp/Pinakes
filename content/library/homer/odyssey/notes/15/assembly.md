summary: Book 15 has no assembly; ἀγορεύω in it is speaking (15.155, 15.266, 15.352, 15.493). The one gathering it names is on Syrie, in Eumaeus' story, where the king's counselors have gone out “to the session and the talk of the people”, the council's seat and the place where the people talk (15.468).

# "So they spoke such words to one another": speaking, not an assembly. θῶκον (15.468), "the session", only here.

**Talk, not assembly.** The book is spent at Sparta, on the road, at sea and in the swineherd's hut, and ἀγορεύω is the verb of its conversations: Telemachus answers Menelaus “as you say” (15.155), he and Eumaeus each promise a stranger “I will tell you very exactly” (15.266 = 15.352), and the talk in the hut ends, “So they spoke such words to one another” (15.493 = 14.409).

**On Syrie.** In Eumaeus' story the cups and tables of the feast lie unwatched in the porch because the king's men “had gone out to the session and the talk of the people” (15.466–468; see [[the-talk-of-the-people]], [[the-kidnapping-of-eumaeus]] and [[syrie]]). The banqueters who attended on the king are his counselors, like the twelve elders of the Phaeacians; θῶκος is their sitting-place or tribunal, and φῆμις is here the ἀγορή, “the place of talking” (D. B. Monro, *Homer's Odyssey, Books XIII–XXIV*, 1901, on 15.467–468; see the translation's note on 15.466–468, and [[elders]]). Ithaca had the same pair, and neither had met since Odysseus sailed: “Never has there been an assembly of ours or a session” (2.26; see [[boule]] and [[the-assembly-of-the-ithacans]]). On the blessed island the council sits and the people talk, and it is while the men are away at it that the child is taken.
