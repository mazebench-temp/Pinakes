---
summary: Peisistratus is “the splendid son of Nestor” when Athena finds the young men sleeping and when they yoke the horses to go (15.4, 15.144); the nurse of Eumaeus' story is “skilled in splendid handiwork” (15.418); and Telemachus names “Eurymachus, the splendid son of wise-hearted Polybus” (15.519).
---

Νέστορος ἀγλαὸς υἱός closes the verse that names the two young men at their arrival in Sparta and as they go to sleep (4.21, 4.303), and here when Athena finds them in the porch and as they drive away (15.4, 15.144; see [[peisistratus]]). The nurse is described in the verse of Athena's disguise, “beautiful and tall and skilled in splendid handiwork” (15.418; 13.289; see [[weaving]], [[the-kidnapping-of-eumaeus]] and [[the-phoenician-nurse]]). ἀγλαὸν υἱόν praises Eurymachus as Telemachus sends the stranger to him, before the falcon changes his mind (15.518–543; see [[wise-hearted]], [[eurymachus]] and [[the-omen-of-the-falcon]]).
