---
summary: Eumaeus: “These nights are endless; there is time to sleep, / and time for those who take delight in it to listen” (15.392–393).
grc: [ἀθέσφατοι]
---

νύκτες ἀθέσφατοι, as Alcinous' “This night is very long, endless,” when he bids Odysseus go on with his tale (11.373; see the Book 11 note and the Odyssey translation's key words): the long night gives time for a story, and the swineherd tells his own (see [[a-time-for-many-words]], [[much-sleep-is-a-vexation]] and [[the-kidnapping-of-eumaeus]]). The plural comes again of the herds of Odysseus, which “are becoming endless” (20.211, a plain rendering).
