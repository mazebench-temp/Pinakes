**The king of Syrie.** The disguised Odysseus asks the swineherd how he came to be a slave: was the city of his father and his lady mother sacked, or did enemies carry him off alone with the flocks (15.381–388)? Eumaeus begins with the island, “There is an island called Syrie, if perhaps you have heard of it, / above Ortygia, where the turnings of the sun are” (15.403–404; see [[syrie]], [[ortygia]], [[the-turnings-of-the-sun]] and [[the-kidnapping-of-eumaeus]]), a land where no famine or sickness comes and old men die by the gentle arrows of Apollo and Artemis (15.405–411; see [[gentle-arrows]]), with two cities, “and over both of them my father was king, / Ctesius, son of Ormenus, like the immortals” (15.412–414; see [[ormenus-father-of-ctesius]], [[like-the-immortals]] and [[eumaeus]]). The son of the king is a slave, as Odysseus, a king, sits in the hut as a beggar.

**His house.** The Phoenician woman who stole the child was a slave in his house: ἔσκε δὲ πατρὸς ἐμοῖο γυνὴ Φοίνισσʼ ἐνὶ οἴκῳ, where πατρός goes with οἴκῳ and not with γυνή, as Ameis took it (15.417; D. B. Monro, *Homer's Odyssey, Books XIII–XXIV*, 1901, on 15.417; see [[the-phoenician-nurse]] and [[the-kidnapping-of-eumaeus]]). When the trader who seduced her asked who she was, “she at once pointed out the high-roofed house of my father” (15.424). πατρός here “must surely mean ‘my father’”, Monro writes, with Pierron and Hayman, and not “her father”, as it is generally taken, though the same verse means “her father” in the tale of the Laestrygonians (10.111): she showed the roof of the house she belonged to, and went on to say that she had been brought from Sidon “to this man's house” (15.428–429), τοῦδε being, in Monro's words, “this that I am showing you” (Monro on 15.424; see the translation's note on 15.417, 15.424). She fears that someone may tell “the old man”, who would bind her in a grievous bond (15.442–444), and the child she offers the traders is “the child of a good man” (15.450). It is a rich house: when the trader came with his necklace, “the slave women and my lady mother” handled it (15.459–463; see [[necklace]]), and the banqueters “who attended on my father”, his counsellors, as Monro takes them, had gone out to the session, leaving the cups and tables in the porch, so that the woman carried off three goblets with the child (15.466–470; Monro on 15.466, 15.467; see [[cup]] and [[the-talk-of-the-people]]). Of the father after the theft, and of the mother, the swineherd says nothing more.
