---
summary: In Menelaus' treasure chamber Helen stands beside the chests where the robes she made herself are kept (15.104–105).
---

“and Helen stood beside the chests, / where her robes were, richly embroidered, which she had made herself” (15.104–105; see [[helen]], [[robe]] and [[possessions]]). φωριαμός is found elsewhere in Homer only of the chests from which Priam takes the robes, cloaks and tunics of Hector's ransom (*Iliad* 24.228–231; see the entry and [[the-ransom-of-hector]]): in both places they hold woven things laid up as treasure in a chamber that the poet calls fragrant, and Menelaus goes down to it in the verse in which Priam went down to his (15.99, as *Iliad* 24.191; see [[house]] and [[fragrant]]). The Suda explains the word as κιβωτός, “box” (φ 668).
