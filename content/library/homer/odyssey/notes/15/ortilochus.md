summary: Named in the verse that gives Diocles of Pherae his descent from the river, when Telemachus and Peisistratus lodge with him on their way home: “son of Ortilochus, whom Alpheus begot as his son” (15.187 = 3.489).
# Ὀρτίλοχ* (in the entry) claims Ὀρτιλόχοιο (15.187).
---

The verse belongs to the house of Diocles, where the young men spend the night at Pherae going and coming: “and they came to Pherae, to the house of Diocles, / son of Ortilochus, whom Alpheus begot as his son” (15.186–187 = 3.488–489; see [[diocles]], [[alpheus]] and [[pherae-in-messenia]]). τὸν refers to Ortilochus, the son of the river, as in the Iliad's fuller pedigree of Diocles, whose lineage “was from the river / Alpheus, … / who begot Ortilochus, lord over many men; / and Ortilochus begot great-hearted Diocles” (*Iliad* 5.544–547). Strabo uses the verses to show that Pherae, in Messenia, was “the house of Ortilochus”, where Odysseus met Iphitus (*Geography* 8.5.8; see [[strabo]]).
