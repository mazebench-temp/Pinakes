The younger line of Melampus has two brothers, “Polypheides and Cleitus” (15.249; see [[mantius]] and [[polypheides]]), and the poet disposes of the second at once, “but Cleitus was carried off by Dawn of the golden throne / for the sake of his beauty, so that he might dwell among the immortals” (15.250–251; see [[eos]], [[golden-throned]] and [[immortality]]), before he comes to the seer and his son (15.252–256; see [[theoclymenus]]). The second verse is the Iliad's verse of Ganymede (*Iliad* 20.235; see [[ganymede]]), and Aristarchus obelized it here (D. B. Monro, *Homer's Odyssey, Books XIII–XXIV*, 1901, critical note on 15.251; see the translation's note on 15.225–255). For Dawn's other mortal loves, Tithonus and Orion, see [[dawn-and-orion]]; the book has given her the same epithet when she comes on the morning of the leave-taking, “and at once Dawn of the golden throne came” (15.56).
