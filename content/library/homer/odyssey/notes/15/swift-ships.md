---
summary: Peisistratus “turned the horses to the swift ship and the shore of the sea” (15.205), and Theoclymenus finds Telemachus “pouring libations and praying beside the swift black ship” (15.258).
---

ἐπὶ νῆα θοὴν καὶ θῖνα θαλάσσης is the Odyssey's verse-end of the way down to a ship (4.779, 10.154, 13.65), here driven by chariot; θοῇ παρὰ νηῒ μελαίνῃ is the verse-end of Achilles' warning, “of the other things that I have beside my swift black ship” (*Iliad* 1.300; see [[ships]] and [[black-ships]]). θοῇσιν of the islands at 15.299 is another matter: there “the meaning ‘swift’ cannot be seriously thought of,” and D. B. Monro leaves the word unexplained (*Homer's Odyssey, Books XIII–XXIV*, 1901, on 15.299–300; the English has “the pointed islands”: see the translation's note on 15.295–300, and [[the-pointed-islands]]).
