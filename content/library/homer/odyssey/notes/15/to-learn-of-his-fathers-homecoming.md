---
summary: Telemachus tells the seer why he sailed: “Therefore I have taken companions and a black ship / and come to learn of my father, who has long been gone” (15.269–270).
---

The verse of Athena's command in Book 1, “and go to learn of your father, who has long been gone” (1.281), now with ἦλθον, “I have come,” for ἔρχεο, “go,” and after a verse that has given up hope: “but now he has perished by a grim destruction” (15.268; see [[such-was-i-if-ever]]). In the assembly he gave the purpose with νόστον, “the homecoming” (2.215). He answers the stranger's questions in their order, his country, his father, his errand (15.264–270; see [[who-are-you-among-men]] and [[theoclymenus]]).
