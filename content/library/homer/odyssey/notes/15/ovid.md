---
summary: Ovid's Penelope writes of the father who chides her delays, of a Sparta that does not know the truth, of the ambush laid for her son and of an old Laertes who holds off death for his son's return, all things that this book tells from the other side. His Themis foretells the end of the house of Amphiaraus, the seer whom the earth swallows alive and the son who avenges his father on his mother, and his Melampus, “the son of Amythaon”, cures the daughters of Proetus.
---

- **Penelope's letter.** Her father Icarius “compels me to leave my widowed bed” (*Heroides* 1.81–82), the pressure that Athena reports at Sparta, “For already her father and her brothers are bidding her / marry Eurymachus” (15.16–17); “Sparta too does not know the truth” (*Heroides* 1.65), though at Sparta Helen reads the eagle as a sign that Odysseus will come home, “or else he is already / at home” (15.177–178); Telemachus “was almost taken from me lately by an ambush” (*Heroides* 1.99), the ambush he escapes in this book (15.28–34, 15.295–300); and Laertes “holds off the last day of fate” so that his son may close his eyes (*Heroides* 1.113–114), where the swineherd says that the old man “prays to Zeus always / that his spirit may perish from his limbs in his own halls” (15.353–354; plain renderings of the *Heroides*): [[ovid-penelopes-letter]], [[icarius]], [[the-omen-of-the-eagle-and-the-goose]] and [[laertes]].
- **The house of Amphiaraus.** When Hebe is about to swear that she will give no other man back his youth, Themis stops her: civil war is coming at Thebes, “and, the earth drawn from under him, the seer will see his own shades while still alive; and the son, avenging parent on parent, will be in the same deed both pious and wicked”, driven from his mind and home by the Furies and his mother's ghost, until his wife demands the fatal gold and the sword of Phegeus' house is plunged into a kinsman's side (*Metamorphoses* 9.403–412, plain renderings). The poem says of the seer only that he “perished at Thebes, for the sake of gifts to a woman”, and names his son with his brother, “His sons were Alcmaeon and Amphilochus” (15.247–248): [[amphiaraus]], [[alcmaeon]], [[amphilochus]], [[eriphyle]] and [[alcmaeon-kills-eriphyle]].
- **Melampus' cure.** Whoever drinks from the spring of Clitor shuns wine, either because of some power in the water, or because, as the people of the place say, “the son of Amythaon”, after rescuing the maddened daughters of Proetus from their frenzy by song and herbs, threw the cleansings of the mind into those waters (*Metamorphoses* 15.322–328, plain renderings). The Melampus of this book is a seer who came from Pylos to rule in Argos, and the ancestor of a line of seers (15.225–256): [[melampus]].
