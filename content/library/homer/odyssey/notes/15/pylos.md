---
summary: On the way home from Sparta Telemachus comes to “the steep citadel of Pylos” and asks Peisistratus to leave him at the ship rather than take him to Nestor's house (15.193–216), a passage Strabo used to show that Nestor's city lay inland; Pylos is also the home that Melampus left, and the place from which the seer Theoclymenus sails with Telemachus (15.226–236, 15.541).
---

**Back from Pylos.** Athena bids Telemachus send Eumaeus to Penelope with the news “that you are safe and have come back from Pylos” (42; see [[athena]] and [[penelope]]); in the next book he gives the swineherd the message in the same words (16.131). The journey home is Book 3's journey run backward: “And quickly then they came to the steep citadel of Pylos” (193), where the chariot left “the steep citadel of Pylos behind” on the way out (3.485; see [[steep]] and [[the-journey-of-telemachus]]).

**Past the city.** Telemachus asks a favor: “Do not take me past the ship, you who are nurtured by Zeus, but leave me there, / lest the old man keep me in his house against my will / in his eagerness to make me welcome; but I must get home quickly” (199–201; see [[peisistratus]], [[nestor]] and [[xenia]]). Peisistratus turns the horses “to the swift ship and the shore of the sea”, loads the gifts into the stern and sends Telemachus aboard before Nestor can hear of it, and then drives “back to the city of the Pylians” alone (205–216; see [[pylians]] and [[shore]]). The ship and the city lie apart: “This shows, as Strabo points out, that the city of Pylos was some way inland” (D. B. Monro, *Homer's Odyssey, Books XIII–XXIV*, 1901, on 15.199; see the translation's note on 15.199). Strabo set this passage beside the messenger whom Nestor had sent to Telemachus' crew at the ship (3.423–424): Telemachus does not let Peisistratus drive to the city but turns aside to the ship, “as the way to the city and to the anchorage is not the same”; for him Nestor's Pylos was the Triphylian one, more than thirty stades from the sea, while the other places named Pylos lie on the shore (*Geography* 8.3.26; see [[strabo-on-nestors-pylos]] and [[strabo]]). He drew a second argument from the voyage that follows, past Cruni and Chalcis (*Geography* 8.3.26–27; see [[cruni]] and [[chalcis-in-elis]]). W. W. Merry, who held for the Messenian Pylos, granted that this passage puts the ship at some distance from the town, but suggested that the town may have stood somewhat inland of the promontory of Coryphasium, with only the port on the promontory, a view “which would solve the difficulties stated by Strabo” (*Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 3.4; see the Book 3 note).

**Mother of flocks.** The seer [[theoclymenus|Theoclymenus]] is a descendant of Melampus, “who once lived in Pylos, mother of flocks, / a rich man among the Pylians, living in a house far above the rest”; the poem tells in brief how he won Neleus' daughter for his brother, driving “the loud-bellowing cattle / to Pylos from Phylace”, and left for Argos (225–240; see [[melampus]], [[melampus-and-the-cattle-of-iphiclus]], [[neleus]] and [[phylace]]). μητέρι μήλων, “mother of flocks”, is said in the Iliad of Iton, of Phthia and of Thrace (*Iliad* 2.696, 9.479, 11.222; see [[mother-of-flocks]]); only here is it said of Pylos.

**The companions of the voyage.** At the landing Telemachus hands the stranger to Peiraeus, “of my companions who followed me to Pylos” (540–541; see [[peiraeus-son-of-clytius]] and [[theoclymenus]]).
