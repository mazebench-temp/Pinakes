---
summary: Menelaus would have his guests eat before they go: “It brings both, glory and splendor and a blessing too, / to dine before going over the wide, boundless earth” (15.78–79).
---

ἐπʼ ἀπείρονα γαῖαν is the earth of Poseidon's complaint and of the sandals of Hermes and of Athena (*Iliad* 7.446, 24.342; 1.98; see the Odyssey translation's key words); with πολλήν, “over the wide, boundless earth,” it is the long road home that lies before the guests. A feast, Monro says, is both an honor and a benefit (D. B. Monro, *Homer's Odyssey, Books XIII–XXIV*, 1901, on 15.78). Aristarchus obelized 15.78–85, the meal and Menelaus' offer to take Telemachus through Hellas and Argos (see the translation's note on 15.78–85, [[feast]], [[guest-gifts]] and [[hellas]]).
