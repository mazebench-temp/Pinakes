---
summary: On Eumaeus' island of Syrie no famine comes, “nor does any / hateful sickness besides fall upon wretched mortals”; the old die by the gentle arrows of Apollo and Artemis (15.407–411).
---

Eumaeus begins his story with a blessed island: “Famine never comes upon its people, nor does any / hateful sickness besides fall upon wretched mortals; / but when the tribes of men grow old throughout the city, / Apollo, god of the silver bow, comes with Artemis / and comes upon them with his gentle arrows and kills them” (οὐδέ τις ἄλλη / νοῦσος ἐπὶ στυγερὴ πέλεται δειλοῖσι βροτοῖσιν, 15.407–411; see [[syrie]], [[gentle-arrows]], [[apollo]] and [[artemis]]). ἄλλη νοῦσος is “disease as well”, by the familiar idiom: famine is not counted a disease (D. B. Monro, *Homer's Odyssey, Books XIII–XXIV*, 1901, on 15.407; see the translation's note on 15.407). The two kinds of death that Odysseus distinguished when he asked his mother how she died, “a long sickness” or Artemis “with her gentle arrows” (11.171–173; see the Book 11 note), are here divided between the lands of men and the blessed island: on Syrie there is no sickness, and the arrows come only with old age (see [[geron]]). The archer who brought the plague on the army in *Iliad* 1 is here the giver of a painless end.
