---
summary: Telemachus to the stranger: “My line is from Ithaca, and my father is Odysseus, / if that was ever so; but now he has perished by a grim destruction” (15.267–268).
---

λυγρῷ ὀλέθρῳ is the dative of the manuscripts, which Murray prints, as in Nestor's “we have heard where each one perished by a grim destruction” (3.87); Aristarchus read the accusative λυγρὸν ὄλεθρον, with the copies that Didymus called the more refined (αἱ χαριέστεραι), and Monro prints it (D. B. Monro, *Homer's Odyssey, Books XIII–XXIV*, 1901, critical note on 15.268; see the translation's note on 15.268). Monro takes εἴ ποτʼ ἔην as an assurance, not a doubt, “as surely as there was a Ulysses” (Monro on 15.268; see [[such-was-i-if-ever]], [[odysseus]] and [[theoclymenus]]).
