summary: Named once, as the master of the house where the seer Melampus lay a year in bonds for the sake of Neleus' daughter: “in the halls of Phylacus” (15.231).
# Φυλάκοιο (15.231) is claimed by the entry, and with it the English "Phylacus", which phylacus-the-trojan would
# otherwise take by its English alone.
---

The poem's only use of his own name: Melampus' goods were held by Neleus for a year, “And all that time, in the halls of Phylacus, / he lay bound in a grievous bond, suffering bitter pains / for the sake of the daughter of Neleus” (15.231–233; see [[melampus]], [[neleus]] and [[pero]]), and then he drove the cattle “to Pylos from Phylace” (15.236; see [[phylace]] and [[cattle]]). In the catalogue of heroines the cattle are “the cattle of mighty Iphiclus”, and it is Iphiclus who sets the seer free (11.289–296; see [[iphiclus]]); here the bonds are in his father's house. The Iliad names Phylacus only in his son's patronymic, Φυλακίδης (*Iliad* 2.705, 13.698).
