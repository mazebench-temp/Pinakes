---
summary: Fifteen closes. The goddess leaves Telemachus, “So saying, she went away to high Olympus” (15.43), and in Eumaeus' story the nurse leaves the traders in the same words, “So saying, she went away to the fine house” (15.454); at the leave-taking the closes turn each speech to an act, the gifts handed over, the whip laid on, the horses turned and driven off (15.120, 15.130, 15.182, 15.202–205, 15.215); and the night's talk ends, “So they spoke such words to one another” (15.493).
---

ἡ μὲν ἄρʼ ὣς εἰποῦσʼ ἀπέβη is elsewhere in Homer the half-verse of goddesses leaving, Athena and Iris in the Iliad (*Iliad* 5.133, 8.425), Athena in the Odyssey (1.319, 6.41); at 15.454 it is given to a mortal woman, who goes back to the house she means to rob (see [[high-olympus]] and [[the-kidnapping-of-eumaeus]]). ὣς οἱ μὲν τοιαῦτα πρὸς ἀλλήλους ἀγόρευον ends a talk as the story moves on (see the note on Book 14): here the night of the swineherd's story, which ends in a short sleep and the dawn of the landing (15.493–495; see [[of-the-fair-throne]]). ἦ καί, “He spoke,” closes Telemachus' answer to Helen before the whip (15.182) and his answer to the seer before he calls [[peiraeus-son-of-clytius|Peiraeus]] (15.539).
