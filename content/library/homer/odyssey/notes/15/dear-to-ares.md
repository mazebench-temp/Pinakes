summary: After the omen of the eagle: “So he spoke, and Menelaus, dear to Ares, pondered / how he might read it to him fair and right, with understanding” (15.169–170).
---

ἀρηΐφιλος Μενέλαος, his verse-end in the duel of *Iliad* 3 (*Iliad* 3.21, 3.136, 3.253), is the only use of the word in the Odyssey, and the warrior's epithet stands at a moment of hesitation: while he ponders, Helen reads the sign before him (15.171–178; see [[helen]], [[menelaus]] and [[the-omen-of-the-eagle-and-the-goose]]). The form ἀρηΐφιλος is new to the entry in the Odyssey.
