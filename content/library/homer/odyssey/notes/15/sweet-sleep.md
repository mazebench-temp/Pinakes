---
summary: At Sparta “sweet sleep did not hold Telemachus, but in his heart / through the ambrosial night cares for his father kept him awake” (15.7–8); and after Athena's warning “he woke the son of Nestor from sweet sleep, / stirring him with his heel” (15.44–45).
---

Τηλέμαχον δʼ οὐχ ὕπνος ἔχε γλυκύς is Agamemnon's sleepless night at the opening of *Iliad* 10, “was not held by sweet sleep, as he pondered many things in his mind” (*Iliad* 10.3–4), with γλυκύς for γλυκερός. The night goes on as the Iliad's does, with a wakeful man rousing his companion “stirring him with his heel,” λὰξ ποδὶ κινήσας (15.45; *Iliad* 10.157–158; see [[soft]] and the translation's note on 15.45). ἡδέος ὕπνου has the adjective of the sleep that Athena casts on Penelope's eyelids (1.363–364; see [[ambrosial]], [[telemachus]] and [[peisistratus]]).
