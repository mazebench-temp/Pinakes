summary: Menelaus brings the parting wine in a golden cup (15.148); in the hut Eumaeus warns the beggar of the suitors' tables laden with bread and meat and wine (15.334), bids him drink his wine as he listens through the long night (15.391), and tells of the island of his birth, “full of wine” (15.406); and at the landing the crew mix the sparkling wine, and Telemachus promises them a feast of wine sweet to drink (15.500, 15.507).
# Forms first met here; they apply in the Odyssey only: οἰνοπληθής, "full of wine" (15.406, only here in Homer), and
# ἡδυπότοιο, "sweet to drink" (15.507; earlier 2.340 and 3.391, the old wine of Odysseus' storeroom and of Nestor's
# house), always of wine.

**The parting wine.** Menelaus follows the chariot “holding wine sweet to the heart in his right hand / in a golden cup, so that they might pour a libation before they went” (15.148–149; see [[cup]], [[libation]] and [[honey-sweet]]), as Hecuba brought Priam wine before he drove out to Achilles (*Iliad* 24.284–285). At the meal before it Megapenthes had poured the wine (15.141; see [[cupbearing]] and [[megapenthes]]).

**In the hut.** The beggar offers to serve the suitors, good at “pouring wine” among other tasks (15.323; see [[cupbearing]]), and Eumaeus answers that their servants are young men in fine clothes, and “the well-polished tables / are laden with bread and meat and wine” (15.333–334; see [[table]] and [[the-suitors]]). When the beggar asks for his story, the swineherd bids him “listen now in silence and take your delight, and drink your wine / as you sit. These nights are endless” (15.391–392; see [[silence]], [[a-time-for-many-words]], [[much-sleep-is-a-vexation]] and [[delight-in-remembered-sorrows]]), and begins with the island where he was born, “good for cattle, good for sheep, full of wine, rich in wheat” (15.406; see [[syrie]] and [[rich-in-wheat]]). οἰνοπληθής is found only here in Homer (see the translation's row for 15.405–406).

**At the landing.** At dawn on Ithaca the crew “made ready their meal and mixed the sparkling wine” (15.500; see [[sailing-type-scene]]), and Telemachus, sending them on to the city, promises them for the morning a reward for the voyage, “a good feast of meat and of wine sweet to drink” (15.506–507; see [[feast]]). ἡδύποτος is said of the old wine in the jars of Odysseus' storeroom and of the wine that Nestor opened in its eleventh year (2.340, 3.391; see [[the-storeroom-of-odysseus]]).
