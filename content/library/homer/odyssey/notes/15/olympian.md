---
summary: Telemachus leaves the suitors' end to the god: “But Zeus the Olympian, who dwells in the upper air, knows this, / whether before the marriage he will bring about an evil day for them” (15.523–524).
---

Ζεὺς … Ὀλύμπιος, the name and the title on either side of the verb, as in Nausicaa's “it is Zeus himself, the Olympian, who deals out prosperity to men” (6.188); the second half of the verse is told under [[dwelling-in-the-upper-air]]. Telemachus has just named Eurymachus as the man the stranger should go to, the likeliest to marry his mother, and he leaves the outcome to Zeus (15.518–524; see [[eurymachus]], [[evil-day]] and [[zeus]]).
