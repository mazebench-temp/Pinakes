---
summary: The crew sailing from Pylos raise the mast “within the hollow mast-box”, in the verse of the departure from Ithaca (15.289 = 2.424).
---

“The mast of fir, within the hollow mast-box, / they raised and set up, and made it fast with forestays” (15.289–290; see [[mast]], [[forestays]] and [[sailing-type-scene]]). The verses with which the ship put out from Ithaca on the night of Telemachus' departure (2.424–425; see the Book 2 note) return for the voyage home, on which he carries the seer Theoclymenus and must escape the suitors' ambush (15.285–300; see [[theoclymenus]] and [[the-journey-of-telemachus]]). κοίλης, “hollow”, is the box's epithet here, not the ship's (see [[hollow-ships]], which excepts the line), and these are the only two places in Homer where μεσόδμη names a part of a ship (see the entry).
