---
summary: In the line of Melampus, the seer Polypheides, father of Theoclymenus, “moved away to Hyperesia, angry with his father, / and living there he prophesied for all mortals” (15.254–255).
---

“But Apollo made high-hearted Polypheides a seer, / by far the best of mortals, after Amphiaraus died; / he moved away to Hyperesia, angry with his father, / and living there he prophesied for all mortals” (252–255; see [[polypheides]], [[mantius]], [[amphiaraus]] and [[divination]]). The verse of the move is the one in which the Catalogue tells of Phyleus, the father of Meges, “who once went off to live in Dulichium, angry with his father” (*Iliad* 2.629; see [[phyleus]] and [[dulichium]]), with Ὑπερησίηνδʼ, “to Hyperesia”, in place of Δουλίχιον δʼ. The quarrel is not told in either poem. The seer of the next verse who comes up to Telemachus at Pylos is Polypheides' son: τοῦ, “his”, is Polypheides (D. B. Monro, *Homer's Odyssey, Books XIII–XXIV*, 1901, on 15.256; see [[theoclymenus]]).

The family had lived at Argos, where Melampus settled and his sons were born (238–242; see [[argos]] and [[melampus]]). Hyperesia stands on the southern shore of the Gulf of Corinth, among the towns of Agamemnon's contingent in the Catalogue (*Iliad* 2.573), in the coastal land that Strabo says belonged in his day to the Achaeans (*Geography* 8.6.25; see [[strabo]]).
