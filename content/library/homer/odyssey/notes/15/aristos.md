**The best of the suitors.** Athena warns Telemachus: “The best of the suitors lie in ambush for you of set purpose / in the strait between Ithaca and rocky Samos, / eager to kill you before you reach your native land” (μνηστήρων … ἀριστῆες λοχόωσιν, 15.28–30; see [[ambush]], [[the-suitors]] and [[asteris]]). They are the twenty men Antinous chose for it in Book 4, “twenty of the best men” (4.778), and an ambush is where the best are tried (14.217–218; see the Book 14 note). Before it, she bids him go home and entrust each thing “to whichever of the slave women seems to you the best, / until the gods show you a glorious wife” (15.24–26; see [[handmaids]] and [[marriage]]), verses that some ancient critics rejected because they lead to nothing in the story (D. B. Monro, *Homer's Odyssey, Books XIII–XXIV*, 1901, on 15.24–26; see the translation's note on 15.19, 15.24–26, 15.91).

**The best of seers.** After Amphiaraus died, Apollo made Polypheides “a seer, / by far the best of mortals” (μάντιν … βροτῶν ὄχʼ ἄριστον, 15.252–253; see [[polypheides]], [[best-of-bird-seers]] and [[divination]]): the seer who boards Telemachus' ship is the son of the best.

**The best man.** On Ithaca Telemachus names Eurymachus: “for he is by far the best man, and most of all he is eager / to marry my mother and to hold the prize of honor of Odysseus” (πολλὸν ἄριστος ἀνήρ, 15.521–522; see [[eurymachus]] and [[geras]]). The best of the suitors is the one most dangerous to the house, and the omen that follows answers him (15.525–534; see [[the-omen-of-the-falcon]] and [[basileus]]).
