---
summary: Eumaeus' story: “And there came Phoenicians, men famed for ships, / greedy rogues, bringing countless baubles in a black ship” (15.415–416).
---

ναυσίκλυτος, the Phaeacians' epithet (7.39, 8.191, 8.369, 13.166; see the Book 7 note), is given here once to another people, and the verse ends in ἄνδρες as the Phaeacians' “men famed for ships” do (8.191). The praise is undone at the start of the next verse, τρῶκται, “greedy rogues” (see the Odyssey translation's key words, [[phoenicians]], [[trade]] and [[the-kidnapping-of-eumaeus]]).
