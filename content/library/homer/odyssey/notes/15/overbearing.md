---
summary: Athena warns Telemachus of the “men in your house / so overbearing” (15.11–12); the stranger offers to “mingle with the overbearing suitors” (15.315); and Eumaeus says that evil has fallen on the house, “overbearing men” (15.375–376).
---

The first is Nestor's warning again, in the same verses (15.10–13 = 3.313–316; see the note on Book 3). μνηστήρεσσιν ὑπερφιάλοισι is the suitors' formula with the longer dative, in the stranger's offer to serve them (15.313–324), and the swineherd answers with their arrogance, “whose arrogance and violence reach the iron sky” (15.329; see [[hybris]]). At 15.376 the epithet stands at the beginning of the verse, in apposition to the evil itself, ἐπεὶ κακὸν ἔμπεσεν οἴκῳ, / ἄνδρες ὑπερφίαλοι (see [[the-suitors]] and [[eumaeus]]).
