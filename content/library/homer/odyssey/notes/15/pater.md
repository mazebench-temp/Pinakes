---
summary: Fathers: the father Telemachus lies awake for, seeks and names (15.8, 15.90, 15.267–270); Penelope's father, who urges her to remarry (15.16), Nestor, “kind to me like a father” (15.152), and the seer's grandfather, from whom Polypheides moved away in anger (15.254); and Eumaeus' father, the king of Syrie, whose house the Phoenician nurse pointed out (15.413–467).
---

**Telemachus' father.** The book begins with a sleepless son: “through the ambrosial night cares for his father kept him awake” (μελεδήματα πατρὸς ἔγειρεν, 15.8; see [[hypnos]]), his anxious thoughts about his father (D. B. Monro, *Homer's Odyssey, Books XIII–XXIV*, 1901, on 15.8). He wants to go home, “lest while I seek my godlike father I perish myself” (15.90; see [[antitheos]]), and he answers the seer at Pylos, “My line is from Ithaca, and my father is Odysseus, / if that was ever so; but now he has perished by a grim destruction. / Therefore I have taken companions and a black ship / and come to learn of my father, who has long been gone” (15.267–270; see [[genee]] and [[to-learn-of-his-fathers-homecoming]]). He claims Peisistratus as a guest-friend “from our fathers' friendship” (15.197; see [[xenia]]).

**Other fathers.** Athena warns that Penelope's “father and her brothers are bidding her / marry Eurymachus” (15.16–17; see [[icarius]], [[kasignetos]] and [[eurymachus]]). Menelaus sends his greeting to Nestor, “for truly he was kind to me like a father, / while we sons of the Achaeans were fighting in Troy” (15.152–153; see [[kind-as-a-father]] and [[nestor]]). In the seer's line Polypheides “moved away to Hyperesia, angry with his father” (πατρὶ χολωθείς, 15.254; see [[polypheides]], [[mantius]] and [[cholos]]). In the hut Odysseus asks after his own father, “whom he left on the threshold of old age when he went” (15.348; see [[laertes]] and [[geron]]), and blesses his host in the name of “father Zeus” (15.341; see [[father-zeus]]).

**Eumaeus' father.** Odysseus asks whether the city “in which your father and lady mother lived” was sacked (15.384–385), and the swineherd answers with his father's name: “over both of them my father was king, / Ctesius, son of Ormenus, like the immortals” (15.413–414; see [[ctesius]], [[ormenus-father-of-ctesius]] and [[basileus]]). “And there was in my father's house a Phoenician woman” (15.417; see [[the-phoenician-nurse]]), where πατρός goes with οἴκῳ, not with γυνή, as Ameis takes it (Monro on 15.417). When the Phoenician asked her who she was, “she at once pointed out the high-roofed house of my father” (15.424): πατρός “must surely mean ‘my father,’” not “her father”, as it is generally taken (Monro on 15.424; see the translation's note on 15.417, 15.424), though the same verse is the Laestrygonian girl's “house of her father” at 10.111. The sailor's answer turns the phrase on her, “so that you may see the high-roofed house of your father and mother” (15.432): “You have shown us the ‘great house’ where you are kept in slavery: will you return to the ‘great house’ of your father and mother?” (Monro on 15.424). The messenger came “to my father's house” with the necklace (15.459), and the cups the nurse stole stood on the tables of the banqueters “who attended on my father” (15.466–467), his counsellors, like the twelve elders in Phaeacia (Monro on 15.467; see [[the-kidnapping-of-eumaeus]]).
