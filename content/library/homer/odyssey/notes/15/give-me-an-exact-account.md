summary: Telemachus answers the seer's question, “Well then, stranger, I will tell you very exactly” (15.266), and so does the swineherd the stranger's, about the parents of Odysseus (15.352); and Odysseus asks for the swineherd's own story: “But come, tell me this, and give me an exact account” (15.383).
---

The answer verse is Telemachus' to Athena in Book 1 (1.214) and Eidothea's to Menelaus in his tale (4.383, 4.399); in the hut it is the swineherd's here and Telemachus' to his disguised father in the next book (16.113), and the stranger began his Cretan tale with its other form (14.192). The question verse turns the talk to the host: the formula for questioning a stranger is put to the swineherd (15.383–388; see the general article, [[who-are-you-among-men]] and [[the-kidnapping-of-eumaeus]]).
