**The desire put away.** Three meals of the book end in the same verse: “But when they had put away the desire for drink and food” (αὐτὰρ ἐπεὶ πόσιος καὶ ἐδητύος ἐξ ἔρον ἕντο), at Menelaus' table before the parting (15.143), in the hut before Odysseus tests the swineherd (15.303), and on the shore of Ithaca before Telemachus sends his crew on to the city (15.501; see [[desire-for-drink-and-food]] and [[feast-type-scene]]). The verse marks the moment for speech: the meal is over, and the business of each scene can begin.

**Lovely.** πολυήρατος is what is much desired. Helen gives Telemachus her robe as “a remembrance of Helen's hands, for the time of your lovely marriage, / for your wife to wear” (πολυηράτου ἐς γάμου ὥρην, 15.126–127; see [[robe]], [[marriage]] and [[helen]]), and Eumaeus tells how he was reared with Ctimene until “we both came to the lovely prime of youth” (ἥβην πολυήρατον, 15.366; see [[ctimene]]), when she was married to Same and he sent to the farm (15.367–370). Odysseus will use the word of the bed he and Penelope come back to at last, “our lovely bed” (23.354, a plain rendering).
