---
summary: Eumaeus' Phoenician nurse tells her lover that she comes “from Sidon, rich in bronze”, the daughter of a rich man, carried off by Taphian pirates and sold to the house of Eumaeus' father (15.425–429).
---

**From Sidon.** When the Phoenician sailor who has lain with her by the ship asks who she is and where she comes from, the nurse points out her master's high-roofed house and answers: “I claim to be from Sidon, rich in bronze, / and I am the daughter of Arybas, whose wealth came in a flood; / but Taphian men, pirates, seized me / as I was coming from the fields, and brought me here and sold me / to this man's house; and he paid a worthy price for me” (423–429; see [[arybas]], [[taphians]], [[euchomai]], [[the-kidnapping-of-eumaeus]] and [[the-phoenician-nurse]]). She is “a Phoenician woman” (417; see [[phoenicians]]) and a Sidonian by birth, of the people whose women made the robes of Hecuba and whose king gave Menelaus the silver bowl that Menelaus gives Telemachus at the start of the book (*Iliad* 6.289–292; 15.113–119; see [[sidonians]] and [[mixing-bowl]]); she too is “skilled in splendid handiwork” (418).

**Rich in bronze.** Σιδῶνος πολυχάλκου, “Sidon, rich in bronze”, is the only place in Homer where the city is called by its own name: the Iliad has Σιδονίηθεν, “from Sidon” (*Iliad* 6.291), and Book 13 “well-peopled Sidon”, Σιδονίην, the land of Sidon, to which the honest Phoenicians of the false tale sailed home (13.285; see the Book 13 note). πολύχαλκος is said in the Iliad of rich men, “rich in gold, rich in bronze” (*Iliad* 10.315, 18.289), and in both poems of the sky (*Iliad* 5.504; 3.2); only here is it said of a city (see [[brazen]]). Her father's riches are described in a trader's words: ῥυδόν, “in a stream”, sounds to D. B. Monro “like a piece of mercantile argot”, and Ἀρύβας “seems to be a Phoenician name—perhaps Hasdrubal” (*Homer's Odyssey, Books XIII–XXIV*, 1901, on 15.426; see the translation's note on 15.426, and [[arybas]]).

**Back to Sidon.** The sailor offers to take her home, “so that you may see the high-roofed house of your father and mother / and themselves” (431–433), and she goes with the traders, taking the child and her master's goblets; but on the seventh day at sea Artemis strikes her down, and she never comes home (477–481; see [[artemis]] and [[like-a-sea-bird]]).
