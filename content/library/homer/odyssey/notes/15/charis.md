---
summary: Hermes, by whose favor the beggar claims his skill in service, “grants / grace and glory to the works of all men” (15.319–320).
en: [grace]
---

The beggar who would serve the suitors names his patron: “by the favor of Hermes the guide, who grants / grace and glory to the works of all men, / in service no other mortal could rival me” (Ἑρμείαο ἕκητι διακτόρου, ὅς ῥά τε πάντων / ἀνθρώπων ἔργοισι χάριν καὶ κῦδος ὀπάζει, 15.319–321; see [[hermes]], [[kydos]] and [[peira]]). χάρις here is the grace that makes work pleasing, the charm a god gives to what men do, as Athena poured grace over Odysseus himself before Nausicaa and before the Phaeacians (6.235, 8.19; see the Book 6 and Book 8 notes). The works in question are a servant's: laying a fire, splitting wood, carving, roasting and pouring the wine (15.322–324; see [[cupbearing]]).
