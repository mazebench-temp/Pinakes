---
summary: At sunset on the voyage home “the ship made for Pheae, driven on by the wind of Zeus” (15.297), the Pheia of Nestor's tale in the Iliad, a headland on the coast of Elis, where most manuscripts read “Pherae”.
---

“And the sun set, and all the streets grew dark; / and the ship made for Pheae, driven on by the wind of Zeus, / and past shining Elis, where the Epeians rule” (296–298; see [[the-journey-of-telemachus]], [[cruni]] and [[elis]]). ἐπέβαλλεν, “made for”, is apparently a sailor's word, “stood for” or “ran for” a point in view (D. B. Monro, *Homer's Odyssey, Books XIII–XXIV*, 1901, on 15.295–298; see the translation's key-word row). The wind is the one that Athena sent at the start (292; see [[the-winds]]), here “the wind of Zeus”, as in Eumaeus' story it is Zeus who sends the Phoenicians a fair wind (475; see [[zeus]] and [[the-kidnapping-of-eumaeus]]).

**Pheae or Pherae.** Nearly all the manuscripts give Φεράς, “Pherae”, and so does the *Hymn to Apollo* in the same verse (*Hymn to Apollo* 427); Aristarchus and Strabo read Φεάς. Monro adopts it and identifies the place with Pheia, the town on the Iardanus by whose walls Nestor fought in his youth (*Iliad* 7.135), and with the Pheia of Thucydides (2.25, as Monro cites him; see [[iardanus]]): “A ship going northwards from Pylos would steer for Pheia. The headland near Pheia, the ancient Ichthys, now Katakolo, must have been familiar as a land-mark. On the other hand there is no place of the name of Pherae in this part of the Peloponnesus.” He thinks it not improbable that Φεράς was suggested by the Achaean town of Pharae, Φαραί (Monro on 15.295–298; see the translation's note on 15.295–300). The Pherae of the journey by land, where Telemachus spent a night on the way to Sparta and again on the way back, is the Messenian town of Diocles (186; see [[pherae-in-messenia]]).

**The order of the verses.** Strabo quotes the verse after the verse of sunset, with ἀγαλλομένη, “exulting”, for ἐπειγομένη, “driven on”, as the Hymn has it (*Geography* 8.3.26; see [[strabo]]). For Monro its place is “almost fixed by the geography”: Pheae, “being to the south of Elis, naturally comes before it in this narrative”, and so he prints it before the verse of Cruni and Chalcis (Monro on 15.295–298; see [[cruni]] and [[chalcis-in-elis]]). Strabo, describing the coast, puts Cape Pheia north of the mouth of the Alpheus, with a small island and a harbor before it, from which Olympia is a hundred and twenty stadia inland (*Geography* 8.3.12; see [[alpheus]] and [[olympia]]).
