---
summary: Theoclymenus: “Avoiding death and black doom at their hands, / I am an exile, since it is my lot to wander among men” (15.275–276).
---

θάνατον καὶ κῆρα μέλαιναν is the pair of Athena's warning against the suitors and of Telemachus' despair for his father (2.283, 3.242; see the Book 2 and Book 3 notes). Here it is the death that a killer flees at the hands of the dead man's many brothers and kinsmen, who “rule with great power over the Achaeans” (15.272–274). Editors generally take τῶν with θάνατον, “their death,” that is, death at their hands; Monro takes it with the ὑπ- of ὑπαλευάμενος, “under stress of” (D. B. Monro, *Homer's Odyssey, Books XIII–XXIV*, 1901, on 15.275; see the translation's note on 15.275, [[theoclymenus]], [[exile-and-refuge]] and [[the-killer-in-exile]]). [[black-ships]] excepts the line.
