---
summary: Eumaeus warns the stranger against the throng of the suitors, “whose arrogance and violence reach the iron sky” (15.329).
---

“Surely you long to perish there altogether, / if you wish to slip into the throng of the suitors, / whose arrogance and violence reach the iron sky” (327–329; see [[the-suitors]], [[hybris]] and [[eumaeus]]). οὐρανὸν ἵκει, “reaches the sky”, is said of what goes up from men and is heard or known everywhere: the shout of the army running to the ships, the savor of sacrifice, the fame of Nestor's shield and of Odysseus himself, “and my fame reaches the sky” (*Iliad* 2.153, 1.317, 8.192; 9.20; see [[kleos]]); here it is the suitors' violence that reaches it. σιδήρεον, “iron”, is the sky's epithet only in this verse, which the stranger repeats in Book 17 (17.565); the Iliad has a sky of bronze, χάλκεον, to which “the iron din” of battle goes up (*Iliad* 17.424–425; see [[iron]] and [[brazen]]).
