---
summary: Zeus, “the loud-thundering husband of Hera”, in the prayers for Telemachus' homecoming and for the fulfillment of Helen's omen (15.112, 15.180).
# πόσιν at 15.490 is "drink", βρῶσίν τε πόσιν τε "food and drink", as at 1.191, 10.176, 12.320.
except: [15.490]
---

The only husband named by the word in the book is a god's. Menelaus gives Telemachus the bowl with a prayer, “as for your homecoming, as you have it in mind, / may Zeus bring it to pass for you, the loud-thundering husband of Hera” (ἐρίγδουπος πόσις Ἥρης, 15.111–112; see [[husband-of-hera]], [[loud-thundering]] and [[nostos]]), and Telemachus answers Helen's reading of the eagle with the same title, “So may Zeus now grant it, the loud-thundering husband of Hera” (15.180; see [[zeus]] and [[the-omen-of-the-eagle-and-the-goose]]), the verse with which his father had taken leave of Nausicaa (8.465). The other husbands of the book have other words: the “dear wedded husband” whom, Athena says, a woman no longer remembers once he is dead (κουριδίοιο φίλοιο, 15.22; see [[a-womans-heart]]), and “the man who marries her” (15.21).
