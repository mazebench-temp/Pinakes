summary: In Eumaeus' story the Phoenician who brings the necklace “nodded to her in silence”, the signal to the nurse that the ship is ready (15.463–464).
# καννεύσας (15.464), "when he had nodded", the shortened κατανεύσας, only here in Homer.
grc: [καννεύσας]
---

The traders' messenger comes to the king's house with a golden necklace, and while the queen and her women handle it and bargain, he gives the signal: “offering a price; and he nodded to her in silence. / And when he had nodded, he went off to the hollow ship, / and she took me by the hand and led me out of the house” (ὁ δὲ τῇ κατένευσε σιωπῇ. / ἦ τοι ὁ καννεύσας, 15.463–465; see [[silence]], [[necklace]] and [[the-kidnapping-of-eumaeus]]). The nod is the sign of a promise kept: the nurse had asked for a message when the ship was full (15.446–447; see [[the-phoenician-nurse]]), and the message is given without a word. κατανεύω is the verb of Zeus' nod that confirms (see the entry); here it is the nod of a conspirator.
