summary: The seer of the younger line of Melampus, made the best of seers by Apollo after Amphiaraus died, who moved to Hyperesia in anger with his father and prophesied there; his son is the seer Theoclymenus (15.249–256).
# Πολυφείδεα (15.249, 15.252) is claimed by the entry. Unnamed: "by far the best of mortals", Hyperesia, and "His son"
# (15.253-256; τοῦ is Polypheides, Monro on 15.256).
refs: [15.253-256]
---

The pedigree of Theoclymenus reaches his father through his grandfather: “And Mantius begot Polypheides and Cleitus” (15.249; see [[mantius]] and [[cleitus-son-of-mantius]]). One brother Dawn carries off, and the other Apollo makes a seer, “by far the best of mortals, after Amphiaraus died” (15.252–253; see [[apollo]] and [[amphiaraus]]): the words imply that the best seer before him had been Amphiaraus, of the elder line, whom Zeus and Apollo loved (15.244–245). “He moved away to Hyperesia, angry with his father, / and living there he prophesied for all mortals” (15.254–255; see [[hyperesia]] and [[divination]]). ἀπενάσσατο πατρὶ χολωθείς is the verse-end of the Catalogue's Phyleus, the father of Meges, “who once went off to live in Dulichium, angry with his father” (*Iliad* 2.629; see [[phyleus]]); in neither place does the poem say why. Then the poet comes back to the man by the ship: “His son it was who came up, and Theoclymenus was his name” (15.256), τοῦ being Polypheides (D. B. Monro, *Homer's Odyssey, Books XIII–XXIV*, 1901, on 15.256; see [[theoclymenus]]).
