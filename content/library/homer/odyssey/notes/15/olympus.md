---
summary: When she has warned Telemachus at Sparta, Athena “went away to high Olympus” (15.43).
---

“So saying, she went away to high Olympus; / but he woke the son of Nestor from sweet sleep” (43–44; see [[athena]], [[telemachus]] and [[high-olympus]]). The verse ends a god's visit to a mortal as Hermes' visits end, when he has guided Priam to Achilles' hut and when he has given Odysseus the moly: “then Hermes went away to high Olympus” (*Iliad* 24.468, 24.694; 10.307; see [[hermes]]). She had promised him a fair wind from “whoever of the immortals guards and protects you” (34–35), and at Pylos she sends it herself (292; see [[the-winds]]). The book's other Olympian is Zeus, in Telemachus' “But Zeus the Olympian, who dwells in the upper air, knows this” (523; see [[olympian]], [[zeus]] and [[upper-air]]).
