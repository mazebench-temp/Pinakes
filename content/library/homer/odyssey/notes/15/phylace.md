---
summary: In the line of Melampus, the seer who was held a year in bonds “in the halls of Phylacus” escapes his doom and drives the loud-bellowing cattle “to Pylos from Phylace” (15.231–236).
---

“And all that time, in the halls of Phylacus, / he lay bound in a grievous bond, suffering bitter pains / for the sake of the daughter of Neleus and the heavy blind folly / which the goddess, the house-smiting Erinys, had put in his mind. / But he escaped his doom and drove the loud-bellowing cattle / to Pylos from Phylace, and took vengeance for the shameful deed / on godlike Neleus” (231–237; see [[melampus]], [[melampus-and-the-cattle-of-iphiclus]], [[phylacus]], [[neleus]] and [[pero]]). Book 11 told how Neleus would give his daughter only to the man who drove the cattle of Iphiclus “out of Phylace”, and how the seer who promised it was held there in bonds for a year (11.287–297; see [[iphiclus]] and the Book 11 note). This book names the house he lay in, the halls of Phylacus, father of Iphiclus, and finishes the story: ἐς Πύλον ἐκ Φυλάκης, “to Pylos from Phylace”, the cattle went south across Greece, from Thessaly to Nestor's country (see [[pylos]]). Φυλάκης is told from φυλακῆς, “of the watch”, only by its accent.
