summary: Parting from the two young men with a cup of wine, Menelaus sends his greeting to Nestor, “for truly he was kind to me like a father, / while we sons of the Achaeans were fighting in Troy” (15.151–153).
# The English of the half-verse here (15.152), without the δέ of 2.47 and 2.234; the Greek is the entry's.
en: [kind to me like a father]
---

**The parting cup.** Menelaus has followed Telemachus and Peisistratus out to the chariot, “holding wine sweet to the heart in his right hand / in a golden cup, so that they might pour a libation before they went” (15.148–149; see [[libation]] and [[cup]]). “He stood before the horses, and pledging them he spoke” (15.150): “Farewell, young men, and give my greeting to Nestor, shepherd of the people; / for truly he was kind to me like a father, / while we sons of the Achaeans were fighting in Troy” (15.151–153; see [[menelaus]] and [[nestor]]). The verses of the cup are those with which Hecuba brought Priam wine before he set out for the hut of Achilles, and stood before his horses (*Iliad* 24.283–286; see the translation's row οἶνον ἔχων ἐν χειρὶ μελίφρονα). χαίρετον, “Farewell”, is a dual, to the two young men together (see the translation's row χαίρετον).

**Like a father.** πατὴρ ὣς ἤπιος ἦεν is the half-verse with which the Odyssey praises Odysseus as king, πατὴρ δʼ ὣς ἤπιος ἦεν, “and he was kind, like a father” (2.47, 2.234 = 5.12; see the Book 2 note), here without the δέ and with ἐμοί, “to me”: Menelaus speaks of what Nestor was to him. There it is a king's kindness to his people; here it is an old man's to a younger king, at war far from home. The second verse is the one in which Odysseus, on the shore of Ithaca, reminds Athena of the kindness she showed him at Troy: “But this I know well, that before now you were kind to me, / while we sons of the Achaeans were fighting in Troy” (13.314–315 = 15.153; see [[athena]]). Nestor's own account of the voyage home tells how Menelaus, coming late, overtook him and Diomedes at Lesbos (3.165–169), and how the two kings sailed on together as far as Sunium, “with friendly hearts toward one another” (φίλα εἰδότες ἀλλήλοισιν, 3.276–278; see [[the-returns-of-the-achaeans]]). Telemachus answers that they will tell Nestor all of it, and wishes that he could tell his father so on finding him at home in Ithaca (15.155–159; see the translation's note on 15.156–158).

**Kindness in the book.** ἤπιος, “kind”, runs through Book 15. Eumaeus is kind to his masters (ἤπια οἶδεν, 15.39 = 13.405; ἀνάκτεσιν ἤπια εἰδώς, 15.557; see [[eumaeus]]), and Odysseus tells him that Zeus set good beside his evil when he came, a stolen child, to the house of a man “who is kind, who gives you food and drink / with care” (15.489–491; see [[zeus-gives-good-and-evil]] and [[the-kidnapping-of-eumaeus]]). The father whose kindness Menelaus remembers is himself too kind for a guest in a hurry: Telemachus asks Peisistratus not to take him past the ship, “lest the old man keep me in his house against my will / in his eagerness to make me welcome” (15.200–201), soon after Menelaus has said that it is as bad to hold back a guest who is eager to go as to urge on one who wishes to stay (15.72–73; see [[what-is-due-is-better]]).
