---
summary: Menelaus is “the son of Atreus” six times at the leave-taking: in Peisistratus' words, “the hero, the son of Atreus, Menelaus famed for his spear” (15.52), in Telemachus' address (15.64, 15.87), and as he takes the cup among his treasures, gives it, and follows the chariot (15.102, 15.121, 15.147).
---

Agamemnon is not named in the book, and the patronymic is his brother's throughout, as it was at Sparta in Book 4. ἥρως Ἀτρεΐδης opens the verse in Peisistratus' words and in the narrator's at the giving of the cup (15.52, 15.121), and Ἀτρεΐδης … ξανθὸς Μενέλαος fills the verse that brings him after the chariot with the parting cup (15.147; see [[fair-haired]] and [[libation]]). Telemachus' address is the whole verse told under [[zeus-nurtured]] (15.64, 15.87). At 15.52 the text prints Ἀτρείδης without the diaeresis, a slip left as printed (see the translation's note on 15.29, 15.52, 15.116, 15.240, and [[menelaus]]).
