The verses of the way out bring the young men back, so that the night at Pherae is told in the same words both ways (see the note on Book 3, and [[pherae-in-messenia]] and [[diocles]]). The verse of the whip, “and the pair flew on not unwilling,” leads on to the arrival in the words of Iris' drive with Aphrodite to Olympus, “And quickly then they came” (15.192–193; *Iliad* 5.366–367; see [[steep]] and [[litotes]]). The text prints οὐκ ἄκοντε, contracted, where the verse elsewhere has ἀέκοντε, a slip that the meter allows (see the translation's note on 15.192). The journey does not end, as it began, at Nestor's house: Peisistratus turns the horses to the ship, puts the gifts in the stern, and drives back to the city alone (15.202–216; see [[swift-ships]], [[fine-maned]] and [[peisistratus]]).
