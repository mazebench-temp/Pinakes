---
summary: Peisistratus puts Menelaus' gifts in the stern of Telemachus' ship (15.206); Telemachus sacrifices to Athena beside the stern (15.222–223); and when the seer has come on board he sits down in the stern and seats Theoclymenus beside him (15.285–286).
---

**The gifts.** Peisistratus “took out the beautiful gifts and put them in the stern of the ship, / the clothing and the gold, which Menelaus had given him” (15.206–207; see [[guest-gifts]], [[clothing]] and [[peisistratus]]). ἐξαίνυτο is used pregnantly, “took out (and placed)” (D. B. Monro, *Homer's Odyssey, Books XIII–XXIV*, 1901, on 15.206, comparing 13.274).

**The sacrifice.** While the crew go on board, Telemachus “was busy with these things, and was praying and sacrificing to Athena / beside the stern of the ship” (15.222–223; see [[athena]], [[prayer]] and [[sacrifice]]), and there the seer Theoclymenus comes up to him as a suppliant (15.223–278; see [[theoclymenus]] and [[supplication]]).

**The seats of honor.** When Telemachus has taken the stranger's spear, “Then he sat down in the stern, and beside him / he seated Theoclymenus” (15.285–286; see [[deck]], [[stern-cables]] and [[theoclymenus]]). On the voyage out Athena, in Mentor's likeness, sat down in the stern with Telemachus beside her (2.416–418; see the Book 2 note); now Telemachus is the leader of the voyage, and the guest has the place beside him.
