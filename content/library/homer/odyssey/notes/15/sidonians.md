---
summary: Menelaus gives Telemachus the silver mixing bowl, “the work of Hephaestus”, that Phaedimus, “the king of the Sidonians”, gave him on his way home (15.113–119, repeating 4.613–619).
---

“I will give you a well-wrought mixing bowl; of silver / it is all, and its rims are finished with gold, / the work of Hephaestus. The hero Phaedimus gave it to me, / the king of the Sidonians, when his house sheltered me / as I came there on my way home; and I wish to grant it to you” (115–119; see [[phaedimus]], [[mixing-bowl]], [[menelaus]] and [[guest-gifts]]). The verses are those of Menelaus' promise in Book 4 (4.613–619), where Strabo found the Sidonians' craftsmanship and Menelaus' stay among them (see the Book 4 note); here the gift is made, and Megapenthes sets the bowl before Telemachus (121–123; see [[megapenthes]]). Two manuscripts, P and H, omit the seven verses, and D. B. Monro brackets them (*Homer's Odyssey, Books XIII–XXIV*, 1901, critical note on 15.113–119); Murray prints them (see the translation's note on 15.113–119).

The Sidonians come into the book once more, by birth: the Phoenician nurse who carried off the child Eumaeus says that she is “from Sidon, rich in bronze” (425; see [[sidon]], [[phoenicians]], [[the-phoenician-nurse]] and [[the-kidnapping-of-eumaeus]]).
