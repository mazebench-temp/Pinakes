---
summary: Love from the heart: Zeus and Apollo “loved with all their hearts” the seer Amphiaraus (15.245), and Anticleia, who reared Eumaeus, “loved me dearly in her heart” (15.370).
en: [with all their hearts]
---

**With all their hearts.** In the line of Melampus, Amphiaraus is the man “whom aegis-bearing Zeus and Apollo loved with all their hearts / with every kind of love” (ὃν περὶ κῆρι φίλει, 15.245–246; see [[amphiaraus]], [[philos]] and [[philotes]]). περὶ κῆρι is the phrase of an honor or a love beyond others: Zeus foretells that the Phaeacians “will honor him with all their hearts like a god” (5.36), and Arete “has been honored with all their hearts” by her children and her people (7.69). The love of the gods did not keep Amphiaraus from dying young, before “the threshold of old age” (15.246; see [[geron]]).

**Dearly in her heart.** Eumaeus remembers Odysseus' mother, who reared him with her daughter Ctimene, clothed him and sent him to the farm, “and she loved me dearly in her heart” (φίλει δέ με κηρόθι μᾶλλον, 15.370; see [[anticleia]] and [[ctimene]]). κηρόθι μᾶλλον is “a fixed phrase, in which the comparative has no application to the present context”: properly “rather much (than little)”, and so, by a litotes, “right well”; it does not mean “more than before”, as Ameis takes it (D. B. Monro, *Homer's Odyssey, Books XIII–XXIV*, 1901, on 15.370). The translation renders it here “dearly in her heart”, where elsewhere the phrase is “all the more … in his heart”, of anger (5.284, 9.480; see the translation's note on 15.370).
