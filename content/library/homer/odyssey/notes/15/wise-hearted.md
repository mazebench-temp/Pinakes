---
summary: Laertes mourns “his wise-hearted wedded wife” (15.356); and Telemachus names “Eurymachus, the splendid son of wise-hearted Polybus” (15.519).
---

Anticleia is the only woman in Homer called δαΐφρων, a use that suits the sense “wise” rather than “warlike” (see the general article and [[anticleia]]). Πολύβοιο δαΐφρονος ἀγλαὸν υἱόν names a son through his father, as the Iliad's υἱὲ δαΐφρονος does (*Iliad* 2.23, 4.370); elsewhere Eurymachus is “son of Polybus,” Πολύβου πάϊς, without the epithet (1.399, 2.177), and the Phaeacian Polybus who made the purple ball had it too (8.373; see [[polybus-father-of-eurymachus]], [[eurymachus]] and [[splendid]]).
