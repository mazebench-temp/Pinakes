---
summary: Menelaus sends his greeting to Nestor, who “was kind to me like a father, / while we sons of the Achaeans were fighting in Troy” (15.152–153).
---

At the parting Menelaus pledges the young men in a golden cup and sends a message to Nestor: “Farewell, young men, and give my greeting to Nestor, shepherd of the people; / for truly he was kind to me like a father, / while we sons of the Achaeans were fighting in Troy” (151–153; see [[menelaus]], [[nestor]] and [[kind-as-a-father]]). The last verse is the one in which Odysseus, in the book before last, reminded Athena that she had been kind to him at Troy (13.314–315; see [[sons-of-the-achaeans]] and [[achaeans]]). The war is far off in this book: Troy is named only here, in a host's remembrance of his years there.
