summary: Telemachus at Pylos: “Put the arms in order, companions, in the black ship” (15.218), and the seer finds him “beside the swift black ship” (15.258); “Therefore I have taken companions and a black ship / and come to learn of my father” (15.269–270); the Phoenicians, “bringing countless baubles in a black ship” (15.416); and at the landing, “You now row the black ship on to the city” (15.503).
---

The ship of Telemachus' voyage is black at each of its turns, the departure from Pylos, the seer's plea and the landing in Ithaca, and so is the traders' ship of Eumaeus' story (see [[ships]], [[phoenicians]] and [[the-kidnapping-of-eumaeus]]). At 15.258 it has both its epithets, θοῇ παρὰ νηῒ μελαίνῃ (see [[swift-ships]]). The entry excepts 15.275, where μέλαιναν is the black doom (see [[black-doom]]).
