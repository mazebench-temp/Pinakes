# The invitation that the maxim closes (15.391-393).
refs: [15.391-393]
---

**The night of the story.** Eumaeus' invitation turns the end of supper into a second evening. The stranger is to drink “as you sit”, the other herdsmen may go out to sleep, and the two of them will stay up “drinking and feasting in the hut” (15.391–398; see [[feast]] and [[eumaeus]]). The scene in the hut, Monro observes, carries on the idyl of Odysseus in the swineherd's hut and fills the otherwise blank space of Telemachus' night voyage (D. B. Monro, *Homer's Odyssey, Books XIII–XXIV*, 1901, on 15.301–495; see [[the-journey-of-telemachus]]). The stranger had wished for such a night the evening before, food and sweet wine “for a long time” in the hut, since a whole year would not be enough to tell his sorrows (14.193–198); now he listens, and the sorrows told are the swineherd's (see [[the-kidnapping-of-eumaeus]]).

**A short sleep.** The maxim proves true in its own way. When the story and Odysseus' answer are done, “they slept, not for long, but a little while; / for soon came Dawn of the fair throne” (15.494–495; see [[hypnos]] and [[dawn-line]]), and the poem turns to the shore where Telemachus' ship comes in at the same dawn (Monro on 15.495).
