---
summary: Seeing and understanding: Telemachus “caught sight of” Menelaus (15.59); Menelaus pondered how to read the omen “with understanding” (15.170); and Eumaeus asks why “this thought” of begging among the suitors has come into the stranger's mind (15.326).
en: [caught sight of, with understanding]
---

νοέω is first to notice: “And when the dear son of Odysseus caught sight of him” (ὡς οὖν ἐνόησεν, 15.59), Telemachus dresses in haste and goes out to Menelaus (15.60–62; see [[dressing-scene]]). It is also to understand: Menelaus ponders how he might read the omen “fair and right, with understanding” (κατὰ μοῖραν ὑποκρίναιτο νοήσας, 15.170; see [[moira]] and [[the-omen-of-the-eagle-and-the-goose]]). And the νόημα is the thought or purpose formed in the mind: when the beggar says he will go to the city and serve the suitors, Eumaeus answers, “Ah me, stranger, why has this thought come / into your mind? Surely you long to perish there altogether” (τίη τοι ἐνὶ φρεσὶ τοῦτο νόημα / ἔπλετο, 15.326–327; see [[phrenes]] and [[peira]]). αὐτόθι is “on the spot, without escape” (D. B. Monro, *Homer's Odyssey, Books XIII–XXIV*, 1901, on 15.327; see the translation's note on 15.327–328).
