---
summary: The clause of Aristotle's outline that this book brings to its crisis is the plot against the son. Athena warns Telemachus at Sparta of the ambush, and he sails home past it by night; the journey itself, which the outline leaves among the episodes, ends here, at the hut to which the father has already come, and the son is the first to whom the father makes himself known in the next book.
refs: [15.28-35, 15.295-300]
---

**His son plotted against.** “… things at home are such that his property is being wasted by suitors and his son plotted against” (τὸν υἱὸν ἐπιβουλεύεσθαι, *Poetics* 1455b19–21; see the entry above). The plot laid in Book 4 is told to the son in this book: “The best of the suitors lie in ambush for you of set purpose / in the strait between Ithaca and rocky Samos, / eager to kill you before you reach your native land” (15.28–30; see [[the-suitors]]). Athena tells him how to pass it, keeping his ship far from the islands and sailing by night, and where to land, at the first shore of Ithaca, sending the ship on to the city and going himself to the swineherd (15.31–42). The escape is told in two lines: from Elis the ship is sent on toward the pointed islands, Telemachus “pondering whether he would escape death or be caught” (15.299–300; see [[the-journey-of-telemachus]] and [[the-pointed-islands]]).

**Episode and story.** The journey to Pylos and Sparta is not in Aristotle's outline; by his account it is episode. Its end joins the story. The son comes to the place where the father has already arrived, ἀφικνεῖται in Aristotle's verb (1455b21), and the heading of this book gives the son's coming the noun of that verb, “the arrival of Telemachus at Eumaeus' hut” (see [[the-title-of-odyssey-15]]). In the next book the father makes himself known to him, the first of the “certain people” of the outline (*Poetics* 1455b21–22; see [[anagnorisis]]). Asked why Odysseus revealed himself to his young son and to the herdsmen but not to Penelope, Aristotle answered that he had to tell those who would share the danger, and told each of them privately and at the right time, his son first, then the nurse, then the cowherd and the swineherd (*Homeric Problems* fr. 399 Gigon, paraphrased; see [[aristotle]]).
