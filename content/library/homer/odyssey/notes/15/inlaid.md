summary: Helen's robes, “where her robes were, richly embroidered, which she had made herself” (15.105); and twice the young men “yoked the horses and mounted the inlaid chariot,” at Sparta and at Pherae (15.145, 15.190).
grc: [παμποίκιλοι]
en: [richly embroidered]
---

παμποίκιλοι is new to the entry in the Odyssey, in a verse that takes up Hecuba's robes, “richly embroidered, the work of women / of the Sidonians” (*Iliad* 6.289–290): Helen's are the work of her own hands (see [[robe]], [[helen]] and [[sidonians]]). 15.145 and 15.190 are the verse of the chariot journey of Book 3 (3.492; see the Book 3 note and [[chariot-journey]]).
