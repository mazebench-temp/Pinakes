summary: Peisistratus' decision, “this seemed to him the better way” (15.204), and the nurse's offer to the Phoenicians of her master's son, “a crafty one indeed” (15.451), who will fetch a price beyond counting.
en: [crafty]
---

**The better way.** Asked to leave Telemachus at the ship, Peisistratus deliberates: “And as he pondered, this seemed to him the better way: / he turned the horses to the swift ship and the shore of the sea” (δοάσσατο κέρδιον εἶναι, 15.204–205; see [[decision-scene]] and [[phrenes]]). κέρδιον, “more to one's advantage”, is the comparative of the formula in which Homer's men choose between two courses (5.474, 6.145, 10.153).

**A crafty child.** In Eumaeus' story the nurse offers the traders more than gold for her passage: “For I am rearing in the halls the child of a good man, / a crafty one indeed, who runs along with me out of doors; / him I would bring on board, and he would fetch you a price beyond counting, / wherever you sell him among men of another tongue” (κερδαλέον δὴ τοῖον, 15.450–453; see [[the-kidnapping-of-eumaeus]], [[the-phoenician-nurse]] and [[trade]]). τοῖον after an adjective is itself adjectival, and means “not, as usually given, ‘so very —,’ but ‘quite,’ ‘just’”: the child may properly be called κερδαλέος (D. B. Monro, *Homer's Odyssey, Books XIII–XXIV*, 1901, on 15.451; see the translation's note on 15.451). The word with which Athena praised the craft of Odysseus (13.291) is here a slave dealer's reckoning of a child's worth, and the child is the swineherd telling the story.
