summary: Named once, with his brother Amphilochus, as a son of Amphiaraus, at the end of the elder line of Melampus in the pedigree of the seer Theoclymenus (15.248).
# Ἀλκμαίων (15.248) is claimed by the entry, exactly; Ἀλκμάονα, the son of Thestor (Il. 12.394), is alcmaon.
---

“His sons were Alcmaeon and Amphilochus” (15.248; see [[amphilochus]] and [[amphiaraus]]): the verse closes the line of [[antiphates-son-of-melampus|Antiphates]], after the father's death at Thebes “for the sake of gifts to a woman” (15.247; see [[eriphyle]] and [[the-seven-against-thebes]]). The poem does not tell what the sons did, the second war against Thebes and Alcmaeon's vengeance on his mother for his father's death, though both stories were well known later (see [[the-epigonoi]] and [[alcmaeon-kills-eriphyle]]). The Homeric spelling, Ἀλκμαίων, is not the Ἀλκμέων of Aristotle's *Poetics* (1453a20, 1453b24, 1453b33), and Homer's other Alcmaon, the Achaean son of Thestor whom Sarpedon kills, is Ἀλκμάων (*Iliad* 12.394; see [[alcmaon]]).
