summary: The gifts of Sparta are gold and silver: the golden goblet a host would give on the tour Menelaus offers, the silver mixing bowl with rims of gold, the golden pitcher over a silver basin, the golden cup of the parting libation, and “the clothing and the gold” stowed at Pylos (15.85–149, 15.207). In Eumaeus' story the nurse promises the traders gold, and their agent brings a golden necklace strung with amber (15.448, 15.460). Dawn is “of the golden throne”, and Apollo of the silver bow brings death to the old on Syrie (15.56, 15.250, 15.410).

**At Sparta.** The tour that Menelaus offers would bring a gift from every city, among them “a golden goblet” (15.85; see [[cup]] and [[guest-gifts]]). The gift he gives is silver and gold together: “I will give you a well-wrought mixing bowl; of silver / it is all, and its rims are finished with gold, / the work of Hephaestus” (15.115–117 = 4.615–617; see [[mixing-bowl]] and [[hephaestus]]). “The lips are wrought above, and finished, with gold” (D. B. Monro, *Homer's Odyssey, Books XIII–XXIV*, 1901, on 15.116): the same verse-end finished Helen's silver work basket from Egyptian Thebes (4.131–132). At the meal a handmaid pours water from a pitcher, “a fine golden one, over a silver basin” (15.136; see [[basin-and-pitcher]]), and at the chariot Menelaus brings wine “in a golden cup, so that they might pour a libation before they went” (15.149; see [[libation]]). At Pylos Peisistratus puts in the ship's stern “the clothing and the gold, which Menelaus had given him” (15.207).

**The traders' gold.** In Eumaeus' story gold is the nurse's passage money: “for I will bring gold too, whatever comes to my hand” (15.448; see [[trade]], [[the-kidnapping-of-eumaeus]] and [[the-phoenician-nurse]]), and when she leaves she hides three goblets in her bosom (15.469; see [[cup]]). The traders' agent brings to the king's house “a golden necklace, strung here and there with amber” (15.460; see [[necklace]] and [[amber]]): μετά is “at intervals”, and ἠλέκτροισιν are pieces of amber, not the alloy of gold and silver (Monro on 15.460; see the translation's note on 15.460).

**The gods' metals.** Dawn is “of the golden throne” when she comes at Peisistratus' word and when she carries off Cleitus (15.56, 15.250; see [[golden-throned]], [[eos]] and [[cleitus-son-of-mantius]]), and on [[syrie|Syrie]] “Apollo, god of the silver bow, comes with Artemis” to bring the old a gentle death (15.410–411; see [[god-of-the-silver-bow]] and [[gentle-arrows]]).
