---
summary: At Pylos Telemachus bids the crew put the arms in order in the ship (15.218) and takes the bronze spear of the suppliant Theoclymenus from him (15.282); on the coast of Ithaca he takes up a strong spear from the deck before he goes to the swineherd (15.551).
---

**The arms.** “Put the arms in order, companions, in the black ship” (15.218; see [[ships]]): τὰ τεύχεα are the arms, such as the shields and spears with which the suitors' ship was laden (16.474), not the rigging, which is taken in hand later (15.287–291; D. B. Monro, *Homer's Odyssey, Books XIII–XXIV*, 1901, on 15.218; see the translation's note on 15.218).

**The guest's spear.** When Telemachus has agreed to take the suppliant on board, “So he spoke, and took the bronze spear from him, / and laid it on the deck of the curving ship” (15.282–283; see [[theoclymenus]], [[deck]] and [[supplication]]), as he received the bronze spear of his first guest in Book 1 and stood it in the spear-rack (1.121, 1.127–129; see [[spear-rack]] and [[xenia]]). The verse returns when Eumaeus takes Telemachus' own spear at the threshold of the hut (16.40).

**Telemachus' spear.** Before he goes ashore, “he took a strong spear, tipped with sharp bronze, / from the deck of the ship” (15.551–552; see [[sharp]], [[strong-spear]] and [[sandals]]), the verse in which Athena took up her spear to go to Ithaca (1.99), and in the Iliad Nestor, roused in the night and again when he goes out to see the battle, and Teucer, when his bowstring breaks (*Iliad* 10.135, 14.12, 15.482). It is not the spear of [[theoclymenus|Theoclymenus]], as Ameis supposed: the spear that Telemachus himself usually carried, as he did to the assembly (2.10), would also lie on the deck (Monro on 15.551).
