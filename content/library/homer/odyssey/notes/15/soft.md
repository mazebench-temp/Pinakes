---
summary: Athena finds the two young men sleeping in the porch, “the son of Nestor overcome by soft sleep; / but sweet sleep did not hold Telemachus” (15.6–7).
---

μαλακῷ δεδμημένον ὕπνῳ, the verse-end of the sleeping army in the Iliad, here in the singular: the one who sleeps is set against the one who wakes, as the general article describes (see [[sweet-sleep]] and [[peisistratus]]).
