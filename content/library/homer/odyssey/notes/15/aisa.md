# αἴσιμον (15.239; again 16.280), first met here among the Odyssey's notes.
grc: [αἴσιμον]
en: [what is due, his lot]
---

**What is due.** Menelaus blames the host who loves and hates beyond measure: “what is due is better in all things” (ἀμείνω δʼ αἴσιμα πάντα, 15.71; see [[what-is-due-is-better]] and [[xenia]]), the half-verse with which Alcinous answered Odysseus' fear of his anger (7.310). αἴσιμα are the things that accord with αἶσα, the due share: here the measure of a host's kindness.

**A lot to dwell, a lot to wander.** In the seer's genealogy Melampus, driven from Pylos, found his place: “And he himself came to the land of other men, / to horse-pasturing Argos; for there it was his lot / to dwell, ruling over many Argives” (τόθι γάρ νύ οἱ αἴσιμον ἦεν / ναιέμεναι, 15.238–240; see [[melampus]] and [[argos]]). His great-grandson leaves the same Argos with the opposite lot: “Avoiding death and black doom at their hands, / I am an exile, since it is my lot to wander among men” (ἐπεί νύ μοι αἶσα κατʼ ἀνθρώπους ἀλάλησθαι, 15.275–276; see [[theoclymenus]] and [[exile-and-refuge]]). The verb of his wandering is the one with which Athena opened the book, telling Telemachus that it is no longer good for him “to wander far from your home” (τῆλʼ ἀλάλησαι, 15.10; see [[nostos]]).
