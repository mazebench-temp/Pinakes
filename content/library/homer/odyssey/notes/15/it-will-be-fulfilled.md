---
summary: Helen's prophecy, “as the immortals / put it in my heart, and as I think it will be fulfilled” (15.172–173); and Telemachus' answer to the seer's reading of the falcon, “Would that this word, stranger, might be fulfilled” (15.536).
---

The first is the introduction of Athena's prophecy as Mentes, with its weaker “I think” (1.200–201; see the note on Book 1), now in the mouth of a woman who reads an omen (see [[the-omen-of-the-eagle-and-the-goose]]). The second is a wish, αἲ γὰρ τοῦτο … ἔπος τετελεσμένον εἴη, in three verses that promise friendship and gifts to the bringer of good news; Penelope answers with them the seer, when he repeats his reading to her, and the beggar, when he swears that Odysseus will come (15.536–538 = 17.163–165 = 19.309–311; see [[the-omen-of-the-falcon]] and [[euangelion]]).
