summary: Birth and line: the eagle of Helen's omen came “from the mountain, where he was born and bred” (15.175); the seer is “by birth … a descendant of Melampus” (15.225), whose line the poem then traces; Telemachus answers him, “My line is from Ithaca” (15.267); and the seer, reading the falcon, tells him, “There is no other race more kingly than yours” (15.533).
# ἔκγονος (15.225), "descendant", is claimed by an earlier note.
en: [born and bred, descendant, line]
---

**Born and bred.** Helen reads the eagle that snatched the tame goose: “As this eagle snatched the goose that was being reared in the house, / coming from the mountain, where he was born and bred, / so Odysseus … will come home and take vengeance” (ὅθι οἱ γενεή τε τόκος τε, 15.174–177; see [[the-omen-of-the-eagle-and-the-goose]] and [[eagle]]). The pair is a hendiadys of nearly synonymous words, like κραδίη θυμός τε and ἀνείρεαι ἠδὲ μεταλλᾷς (15.339, 15.390): “The two meanings are fused, as it were, into a single more complete conception” (D. B. Monro, *Homer's Odyssey, Books XIII–XXIV*, 1901, on 15.175; see the translation's note on 15.175). The wild bird from the mountain is set against the tame goose reared in the house (ἀτιταλλομένην ἐνὶ οἴκῳ, 15.174), as the master coming home against the suitors in his house (15.177–178; see [[the-suitors]]).

**A descendant of Melampus.** The seer who comes to the ship at Pylos is introduced by his line: “a seer; and by birth he was a descendant of Melampus” (γενεήν γε Μελάμποδος ἔκγονος, 15.225; see [[theoclymenus]] and [[melampus]]). ἔκγονος is the great-grandson (Monro on 15.225): the narrator goes on to trace the line from Melampus through Mantius and Polypheides to the man at the ship, with the other branch through Antiphates and Oicles to Amphiaraus and his sons (15.225–256; see [[mantius]], [[polypheides]], [[antiphates-son-of-melampus]], [[oicles]], [[amphiaraus]] and the translation's note on 15.225–255). The seer then asks the young man the questions of birth, and Telemachus answers with his own line: “My line is from Ithaca, and my father is Odysseus, / if that was ever so” (ἐξ Ἰθάκης γένος εἰμί, 15.267–268; see [[who-are-you-among-men]] and [[such-was-i-if-ever]]), εἴ ποτʼ ἔην being an assurance, not a doubt (Monro on 15.268).

**The kingly race.** At Ithaca the seer reads the falcon for the young man's house: “There is no other race more kingly than yours / in the land of Ithaca, but you are always powerful” (ὑμετέρου δʼ οὐκ ἔστι γένος βασιλεύτερον ἄλλο, 15.533–534; see [[basileus]], [[the-omen-of-the-falcon]] and [[arceisius]]). Most manuscripts read the genitive γένευς; Monro prints γένος, the reading of a corrector of the manuscript H (Monro's critical note on 15.533). The prophecy answers Telemachus' last words before the omen, which had named Eurymachus as the man most eager “to marry my mother and to hold the prize of honor of Odysseus” (15.521–522; see [[geras]]).
