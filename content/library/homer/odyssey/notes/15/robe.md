summary: The robes that Helen made herself lie in the chests of Menelaus' treasure chamber; she gives Telemachus the largest and most beautiful, “a remembrance of Helen's hands”, for his bride to wear at their marriage, and until then to lie with his mother (15.104–130).

**In the chests.** Helen stands “beside the chests, / where her robes were, richly embroidered, which she had made herself” (15.104–105; see [[chest]], [[helen]] and [[weaving]]), and takes the most beautiful and the largest, which “shone like a star” and lay beneath all the others (15.106–108; see [[like-a-star]]). The verses are those of Hecuba's robe for Athena, chosen from robes that Sidonian women had made (*Iliad* 6.288–295; see [[the-robe-for-athena]]); here the maker is Helen herself, and the robe goes not to a goddess but to a guest.

**A bride's robe.** “I too give you this gift, dear child, / a remembrance of Helen's hands, for the time of your lovely marriage, / for your wife to wear” (15.125–127; see [[guest-gifts]], [[marriage]] and [[telemachus]]). Fine clothes belong to a wedding: Athena sent Nausicaa to the washing because at her marriage she must wear fine clothes herself and give others to those who lead her home (6.27–28; see the Book 6 note), and among the suitors' gifts to Penelope the first is a great robe from Antinous (18.292–294, a plain rendering). Until the wedding the robe is to lie in the hall with Telemachus' mother (15.127–128; see [[penelope]]), who weaves at her own loom in the upper room (15.517; see [[loom]]). μνῆμα, “remembrance”, keeps the giver in mind: the bow of Iphitus lay in Odysseus' halls “as a remembrance of a dear guest-friend” (21.40, a plain rendering), and Achilles gave Nestor a bowl from the prizes “to be a memorial of the funeral of Patroclus” (*Iliad* 23.619; see [[phiale]] and the translation's row μνῆμα).

**Carried home.** “So saying, she put it in his hands, and he received it with joy” (15.130), and Peisistratus lays it with the other gifts in the chariot (15.131; see [[wagon]] and [[chariot]]); at Pylos the gifts, “the clothing and the gold”, go into the stern of the ship (15.206–207; see [[clothing]] and [[stern]]).
