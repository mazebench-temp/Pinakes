---
summary: Melampus once lived at Pylos, “a rich man among the Pylians, living in a house far above the rest” (15.227); in Eumaeus' story the nurse is “the daughter of Arybas, whose wealth came in a flood” (15.426), and the trader promises her that her parents “are still alive and are reputed rich” (15.433).
en: [whose wealth came in a flood]
---

Πυλίοισι, “among the Pylians,” is a locatival dative with μέγʼ ἔξοχα δώματα ναίων (D. B. Monro, *Homer's Odyssey, Books XIII–XXIV*, 1901, on 15.227; see [[melampus]] and [[pylians]]). In the nurse's story Monro hears a trader's words. ῥυδόν, “in a stream,” in ῥυδὸν ἀφνειοῖο, “one to whom riches came in a flood,” is “an appropriate word for a trader's wealth” and “sounds like a piece of mercantile argot”; and καλέονται in ἀφνειοὶ καλέονται, “are reputed,” “have the credit of being,” “also looks like a trader's way of speaking” (Monro on 15.426 and 15.433; see the translation's notes on 15.426 and on 15.433, 15.445, and [[arybas]], [[phoenicians]], [[the-kidnapping-of-eumaeus]] and [[the-phoenician-nurse]]).
