# πτόλις (15.384), πόλιες (15.412) and ἄστεʼ (15.492; again 9.128, 19.170, 23.267), first met here among the Odyssey's notes.
grc: [πτόλις, πόλιες, "ἄστεʼ"]
---

**The cities of the road.** Telemachus drives out of Sparta, and the horses “sped through the city toward the plain” (15.183); he comes to “the steep citadel of Pylos” (15.193; see [[pylos]] and [[steep]]), but asks not to be taken past the ship (15.199), which shows, as Strabo points out, that the city lay some way inland (D. B. Monro, *Homer's Odyssey, Books XIII–XXIV*, 1901, on 15.199); Peisistratus drives back alone “to the city of the Pylians” (15.216). Menelaus had offered to lead him “to the cities of men” for gifts (15.82). On Ithaca the city is where the suitors are: Athena tells him to send the ship and his companions “on to the city” while he goes to the swineherd (15.37), and to send the swineherd “into the city” with the news (15.40); on landing he does so, “They pushed off and sailed to the city” (15.553), and he says he will come down to the city at evening (15.505).

**To the city to beg.** In the hut the beggar's test turns on the same place: he will go “at dawn … off to the city / to beg” and wander “through the city by myself” (15.308–311), and the test is whether Eumaeus would keep him or “urge him on to the city” (15.306; see [[peira]]).

**A man's city.** The stranger's question at Pylos is “Where are your city and your parents?” (15.264; see [[who-are-you-among-men]]). Odysseus asks the swineherd how he came to be sold: “was the wide-streeted city of men sacked, / in which your father and lady mother lived” (ἠὲ διεπράθετο πτόλις ἀνδρῶν εὐρυάγυια, 15.384–385; see [[wide-streeted]] and [[slavery-and-freedom]]), the first of the two ways in which a free child became a slave. Eumaeus' answer is an island of two cities: “There are two cities, and everything is divided between them; / and over both of them my father was king” (ἔνθα δύω πόλιες, 15.412–413; see [[syrie]], [[basileus]] and [[the-kidnapping-of-eumaeus]]), where men grow old “throughout the city” untouched by sickness (15.409). The guest, for his part, has come “after wandering through many cities of mortals” (πολλὰ βροτῶν ἐπὶ ἄστεʼ ἀλώμενος, 15.492), the wanderer of the proem, who “saw the cities of many men” (1.3).
