---
summary: Odysseus asks whether the city was sacked “in which your father and lady mother lived” (15.385); and in Eumaeus' story “the slave women and my lady mother” handle the trader's necklace (15.461).
---

πατὴρ καὶ πότνια μήτηρ is the verse-end of the two speeches of Book 6 that look to Nausicaa's marriage (6.30, 6.154), here of the parents from whom the swineherd was taken as a child (15.381–388). In his answer the lady mother is the queen of [[syrie|Syrie]], offering a price for the necklace while the trader nods to the nurse in silence (15.459–465; see [[necklace]], [[eumaeus]], [[the-kidnapping-of-eumaeus]] and [[the-phoenician-nurse]]).
