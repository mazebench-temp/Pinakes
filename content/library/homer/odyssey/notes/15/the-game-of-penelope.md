summary: Apion's champion at the game is the favorite of this book. Athena names Eurymachus as the suitor whom Penelope's father and brothers urge her to marry, because he surpasses the others in gifts, and Telemachus names him at the landing as “by far the best man”, the man the Ithacans look upon as a god, just before the falcon's omen sets the house of Odysseus above him.
refs: [15.16-18, 15.519-524]
---

In Apion's account Eurymachus had won the game most often, and was hopeful of the marriage (see the entry above). This book twice names him as the likeliest husband. At Sparta Athena warns Telemachus that “her father and her brothers are bidding her / marry Eurymachus; for he surpasses all / the suitors in his gifts, and has increased his bride-price greatly” (15.16–18; see [[eurymachus]], [[icarius]] and [[bride-price]]). At the landing Telemachus, who cannot take the seer Theoclymenus to his own house, names another man for him: “Eurymachus, the splendid son of wise-hearted Polybus, / whom the men of Ithaca now look upon as a god; / for he is by far the best man, and most of all he is eager / to marry my mother and to hold the prize of honor of Odysseus” (15.519–522; see [[polybus-father-of-eurymachus]] and [[geras]]). Zeus knows, he adds, “whether before the marriage he will bring about an evil day for them” (15.524), and as he speaks a falcon flies by on the right (15.525–528). Monro finds that the naming of Eurymachus “seems at first sight to have no sufficient motive”, and is “merely intended to give occasion for the prophecy of Theoclymenus”, who declares the line of Odysseus the most kingly in Ithaca: “In consequence of this prophecy the resort to Eurymachus is tacitly given up” (D. B. Monro, *Homer's Odyssey, Books XIII–XXIV*, 1901, on 15.519; see [[the-omen-of-the-falcon]] and [[theoclymenus]]). The leader of Apion's game is the leader in this book too, and the omen that follows his name goes against him.
