summary: Brothers who press, win and avenge: Penelope's “father and her brothers” urge her to marry Eurymachus (15.16); Melampus wins the daughter of Neleus “for his brother” (15.237); and the man Theoclymenus killed has “many brothers and kinsmen” in Argos, from whom the seer flees (15.273).
# ἔται (15.273; again 4.16), "kinsmen", as in the Iliad's notes (ἔτας, ἔτῃσι in Book 6).
grc: [ἔται]
---

**Her brothers.** Athena hurries Telemachus home with news of his mother's kin: “For already her father and her brothers are bidding her / marry Eurymachus; for he surpasses all / the suitors in his gifts, and has increased his bride-price greatly” (ἤδη γάρ ῥα πατήρ τε κασίγνητοί τε κέλονται, 15.16–18; see [[icarius]], [[penelope]], [[eurymachus]] and [[bride-price]]). The brothers are not named in Homer; Penelope's sister Iphthime is the only sibling the poem names (4.797; see [[iphthime]]).

**For his brother.** Melampus, freed from the bonds of Phylacus, “drove the loud-bellowing cattle / to Pylos from Phylace, and took vengeance for the shameful deed / on godlike Neleus, and for his brother he brought the woman / home” (κασιγνήτῳ δὲ γυναῖκα / ἠγάγετο, 15.235–238; see [[melampus]], [[melampus-and-the-cattle-of-iphiclus]] and [[pero]]). The poem does not name the brother (see [[bias]]).

**Brothers and kinsmen.** The seer tells Telemachus why he flees: “So I too am far from my native land, having killed a man / of my own tribe; and he has many brothers and kinsmen / in horse-pasturing Argos, and they rule with great power over the Achaeans” (κασίγνητοί τε ἔται τε, 15.272–274; see [[theoclymenus]], [[exile-and-refuge]], [[the-killer-in-exile]] and [[poine]]). The clause carries on, in paratactic form, the description of the man: “one who has many brothers and comrades” (D. B. Monro, *Homer's Odyssey, Books XIII–XXIV*, 1901, on 15.273). The brothers and kinsmen are those who owe the dead man burial, as they will give Sarpedon his (*Iliad* 16.456, 16.674), and vengeance.
