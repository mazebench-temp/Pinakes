---
summary: The crew loose the stern-cables at Pylos when Theoclymenus has been seated (15.286); on the coast of Ithaca they make them fast in the verse of the landing at Chryse (15.498); and when Telemachus goes ashore, Peiraeus bids the crew go on board and loose them again for the city (15.547–552).
---

**At Pylos.** When Telemachus has seated the seer beside him in the stern, “they loosed the stern-cables” (15.286; see [[stern]] and [[theoclymenus]]), the half-verse of the departure from Ithaca (2.418; see the Book 2 note).

**On Ithaca.** At dawn the crew bring the ship in: “they cast out the mooring-stones and made fast the stern-cables, / and they themselves stepped out where the sea breaks upon the shore” (15.498–499 = *Iliad* 1.436–437; see [[mooring-stones]] and [[shore]]). ῥηγμίς seems always to mean the “broken water” or surf, not the beach on which it breaks (D. B. Monro, *Homer's Odyssey, Books XIII–XXIV*, 1901, on 15.499).

**To the city.** Peiraeus, taking charge of the ship, “bade his companions / to go on board themselves and loose the stern-cables” (15.547–548; see [[peiraeus-son-of-clytius]]), the verse of Odysseus' departures in his tale (9.178, 9.562, 11.637, 12.145). It is a hysteron proteron, since the cables must have been loosed before the crew went on board; the embarkation comes first as the main action (Monro on 15.548; see [[hysteron-proteron]]). Telemachus takes his spear from the deck, “and they loosed the stern-cables” (15.552; see [[deck]] and [[spear]]).
