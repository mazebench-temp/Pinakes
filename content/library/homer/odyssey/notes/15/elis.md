---
summary: On the voyage home Telemachus' ship runs by night “past shining Elis, where the Epeians rule” (15.298), the verse of Book 13; then it is sent on toward the islands.
---

“And the sun set, and all the streets grew dark; / and the ship made for Pheae, driven on by the wind of Zeus, / and past shining Elis, where the Epeians rule” (296–298; see [[the-journey-of-telemachus]], [[pheia]] and [[epeians]]). The verse is the one in which the Cretan of the false tale asked the Phoenicians to set him down “at shining Elis, where the Epeians rule” (13.275; see the Book 13 note), and it returns once more, when the suitors' kin fear that Odysseus may get away “to shining Elis” (24.431, a plain rendering). Elis is the last mainland the ship passes before it is sent on toward the islands (299; see [[the-pointed-islands]]), as in the *Hymn to Apollo* the Cretan ship passes “shining Elis, where the Epeians rule” and then sees Ithaca and the islands (*Hymn to Apollo* 426–429; see [[cruni]]).

**A harsh construction.** In the text as Strabo and Murray give it, παρʼ Ἤλιδα δῖαν, “past shining Elis”, has to be construed with ἐπέβαλλεν, “made for”, in the verse before: “a ‘zeugma’ of more than ordinary harshness”, D. B. Monro calls it, and one reason why he moves the verse of Cruni and Chalcis, “they went past …”, to stand just before it, as the Hymn has it (*Homer's Odyssey, Books XIII–XXIV*, 1901, on 15.295–298; see [[cruni]] and [[chalcis-in-elis]]).

**After sunset.** Strabo argued from this verse against those who made Nestor's Pylos the one in Elis: a ship sailing north from there toward Ithaca would have had to pass Elis itself, and before sunset, “but he says after sunset” (*Geography* 8.3.27; see [[strabo-on-nestors-pylos]] and [[pylos]]). Of the islands “toward horse-pasturing Elis” in Book 21 (21.347, a plain rendering) Monro writes that “the description ‘towards Elis’ applies very well to Zante, and partly to Cephallonia”, and that in the poet's conception Telemachus, coming from Elis, would pass the islands more or less closely (Monro on 15.299–300; see [[zacynthus]] and [[samos]]).
