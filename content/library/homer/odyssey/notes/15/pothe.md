---
summary: ποθή as “lack”: in Telemachus' house “there is no lack of good cheer for guests” (15.514), and with Peiraeus the stranger “shall not lack good cheer” (15.546).
en: [lack]
---

The word that is elsewhere “longing” for one who is absent is here the want of what a guest needs. Telemachus tells the seer that he would bid him come to his house, “for there is no lack of good cheer for guests” (οὐ γάρ τι ξενίων ποθή, 15.514; see [[xenia]]), but sends him elsewhere because he will be away and his mother keeps to her upper room (15.515–517); and Peiraeus, taking the stranger, answers in the same words, “he shall not lack good cheer” (ξενίων δέ οἱ οὐ ποθὴ ἔσται, 15.546; see [[peiraeus-son-of-clytius]]). The translation keeps “lack” for these two places, an exception to “longing” (see the translation's table of key words, ποθή), as in the longing for Achilles that will come upon the Achaeans (*Iliad* 1.240) or the suitors' longing for Penelope's substance (2.126).
