**The richest suitor.** Athena's warning at Sparta begins with him: “For already her father and her brothers are bidding her / marry Eurymachus; for he surpasses all / the suitors in his gifts, and has increased his bride-price greatly” (15.16–18; see [[icarius]], [[penelope]] and [[bride-price]]). περιβάλλει, “surpasses”, is Achilles' word for his horses, which “surpass the rest in prowess” (*Iliad* 23.276), and ἐξώφελλεν ἔεδνα, “has increased his bride-price”, the gifts with which a suitor bids for a bride (see the translation's key words). In the last books he gives Penelope a golden necklace strung with amber (18.295–296).

**The man to go to.** At the landing in Ithaca, when the seer [[theoclymenus|Theoclymenus]] asks where he is to go, Telemachus, who has no house to offer him, names another man:

{{quote:0b0b6624-89b8-5657-929e-5a86ad05134b}}

ἶσα θεῷ, “as a god”, is the honor of a ruler (see [[honored-like-a-god]]), and the marriage would give him γέρας, “the prize of honor” of Odysseus, his place in the land (see [[geras]] and [[olympian]]). The name, D. B. Monro observes, “seems at first sight to have no sufficient motive, and to lead to nothing in the sequel”: it is there to give occasion for the seer's prophecy, one of the steps that prepare the end. Telemachus “confesses that he cannot receive a stranger in his house”, and sends him to the man who seems likely to be the future lord of Ithaca; when the falcon flies on the right, Theoclymenus proclaims that the line of Odysseus will always be the most kingly, and “In consequence of this prophecy the resort to Eurymachus is tacitly given up” (*Homer's Odyssey, Books XIII–XXIV*, 1901, on 15.519; see [[theoclymenus]] and [[the-omen-of-the-falcon]]). For εἴ κε … τελευτήσει (15.524), the only instance in Homer of εἴ κε with the future in an object clause, Monro would read τελευτήσῃ (Monro on 15.524; see the translation's note on 15.524). The seer will meet Eurymachus in the hall, and it is Eurymachus who calls him mad when he sees the suitors' doom (20.359–362).
