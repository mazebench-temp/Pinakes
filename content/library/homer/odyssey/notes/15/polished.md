---
summary: At Sparta a handmaid “drew up a polished table” for the meal before the parting (15.137); and in the swineherd's picture of the suitors' feasts “the well-polished tables / are laden with bread and meat and wine” (15.333–334).
---

ξεστὴν … τράπεζαν is in the verses of the guest's meal (15.135–139; see [[water-for-the-hands]]). ἐΰξεστοι, the compound of the Cretan's javelins in the last book (14.225; see the note on Book 14), describes tables only here, those that young servants in fine cloaks and tunics, their heads shining with oil, keep laden for the suitors (15.330–334; see [[table]] and [[the-suitors]]). D. B. Monro doubts that the spondaic verse that follows was meant to express the weight of the laden tables, and thinks its original form was more dactylic (*Homer's Odyssey, Books XIII–XXIV*, 1901, on 15.334).
