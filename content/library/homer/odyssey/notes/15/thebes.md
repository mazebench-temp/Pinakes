---
summary: In the line of the seer Theoclymenus, Amphiaraus, loved by Zeus and Apollo, did not reach old age, “but perished at Thebes, for the sake of gifts to a woman” (15.244–247).
---

Oicles begot “Amphiaraus, who rouses armies, / whom aegis-bearing Zeus and Apollo loved with all their hearts / with every kind of love; but he did not reach the threshold of old age, / but perished at Thebes, for the sake of gifts to a woman” (244–247; see [[amphiaraus]], [[oicles]], [[the-seven-against-thebes]] and [[eriphyle]]). The war is not told: Thebes is named for the place of his death, as in the Iliad Diomedes names it for his father's grave, “Tydeus, whom the heaped earth covers at Thebes” (*Iliad* 14.114; see [[tydeus]]), and the gifts to a woman are left to the hearer. γυναίων εἵνεκα δώρων is the half-verse with which Odysseus told of the Ceteians who died around Eurypylus (11.521; D. B. Monro, *Homer's Odyssey, Books XIII–XXIV*, 1901, on 15.247; see the translation's key-word row). The threshold of old age is not its beginning: he never fairly set foot in old age (Monro on 15.246). His sons, Alcmaeon and Amphilochus, are named in the next verse (248; see [[alcmaeon]], [[amphilochus]] and [[the-epigonoi]]).
