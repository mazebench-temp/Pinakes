---
summary: The beggar: “by the favor of Hermes the guide, who grants / grace and glory to the works of all men, / in service no other mortal could rival me” (15.319–321).
---

Ἑρμείαο διακτόρου, the genitive of Calypso's source (12.390; see the Book 12 note), here names the god of a servant's skills: heaping up a fire, splitting wood, carving, roasting and pouring wine (15.322–324; see the translation's note on 15.322 and [[hermes]]). The beggar offers to serve the suitors; Eumaeus answers that their servants are young men well clad in cloaks and tunics, not such as he (15.326–336; see [[the-suitors]] and [[eumaeus]]).
