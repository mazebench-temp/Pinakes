# ἔμφυλον (15.273), "of my own tribe", only here in Homer.
grc: [ἔμφυλον]
en: [of my own tribe]
---

**Of my own tribe.** Theoclymenus tells Telemachus why he is an exile: “So I too am far from my native land, having killed a man / of my own tribe; and he has many brothers and kinsmen / in horse-pasturing Argos” (ἄνδρα κατακτὰς / ἔμφυλον, 15.272–274; see [[theoclymenus]], [[exile-and-refuge]], [[the-killer-in-exile]] and [[kasignetos]]). ἔμφυλος, one within the φῦλον, is found only here in Homer (see the translation's table of key words): the dead man was of the seer's own people, and his kin are the ones he flees.

**The tribes of men.** On Syrie, where no sickness comes, “when the tribes of men grow old throughout the city, / Apollo, god of the silver bow, comes with Artemis” and kills them with his gentle arrows (φῦλʼ ἀνθρώπων, 15.409–411; see [[syrie]], [[nousos]] and [[geron]]).
