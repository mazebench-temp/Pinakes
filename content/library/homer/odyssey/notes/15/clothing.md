summary: The gifts that Peisistratus puts in the stern of Telemachus' ship are “the clothing and the gold” of Menelaus (15.207); in the hut Eumaeus promises the beggar a cloak and a tunic “as clothing” from Telemachus (15.338), and remembers the fine clothes Anticleia gave him (15.368).
---

**The gifts.** At Pylos Peisistratus puts the gifts in the stern of Telemachus' ship, “the clothing and the gold, which Menelaus had given him” (15.206–207; see [[guest-gifts]], [[stern]] and [[gold-and-silver]]), nearly the verse of the gifts of the Phaeacians, which Arete laid in a chest for Odysseus (8.438–440; see the Book 8 note and [[the-gifts-of-the-phaeacians]]). Of the gifts from Sparta the only garment the book names is the robe that Helen made (15.104–130; see [[robe]]).

**The guest's clothing.** In the hut Eumaeus promises that when Telemachus comes, “he will clothe you in a cloak and a tunic as clothing, / and will send you wherever your heart and spirit bid you” (15.338–339 = 14.516–517; see [[cloak]], [[tunic]] and [[xenia]]); and he remembers how Anticleia, when her daughter was married, gave him the same dress: “me she clothed in a cloak and a tunic, clothes / very fine” (15.368–369; see [[anticleia]] and [[eumaeus]]).
