---
summary: At dawn off Ithaca the crew take down the mast and bring the ship in to the anchorage with oars (15.496–497), in the verse with which the Iliad's crew rowed in at Chryse.
---

“and took down the mast / quickly, and brought her forward to the anchorage with oars” (15.496–497; see [[mast]], [[harbor]] and [[ships]]). The verse is the Iliad's landing at Chryse, “and rowed her forward to the anchorage with oars” (*Iliad* 1.435), and Aristarchus read the Iliad's προέρεσσαν, “rowed forward”, here too, as Monro prints; the manuscripts and Murray have προέρυσσαν, which the translation renders “brought her forward” (D. B. Monro, *Homer's Odyssey, Books XIII–XXIV*, 1901, critical note on 15.497; see the translation's note on 15.495–497). Under sail the ship came from Pylos; with the oars it comes to land, and with them the crew are to take it on: “You now row the black ship on to the city”, Telemachus tells them (15.503; see [[black-ships]]).
