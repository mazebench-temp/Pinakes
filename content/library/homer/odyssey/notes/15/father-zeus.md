---
summary: Odysseus to Eumaeus: “Would that you, Eumaeus, might be as dear to father Zeus / as you are to me, since you have given me rest from wandering and dread misery” (15.341–342).
---

The first verse is the beggar's thanks of the first night, when Eumaeus honored him with the chine of the boar (14.437–441; see the Book 14 note). Here the reason is the rest from wandering, and the saying that follows is “There is nothing worse for mortals than roaming” (15.343; see [[nothing-worse-than-wandering]], [[nothing-worse-than-the-sea]] and [[eumaeus]]).
