---
summary: Eurymachus, Telemachus tells the seer, is most eager “to marry my mother and to hold the prize of honor of Odysseus” (15.522).
---

Telemachus names Eurymachus as the man who could receive the stranger: “for he is by far the best man, and most of all he is eager / to marry my mother and to hold the prize of honor of Odysseus” (μητέρʼ ἐμὴν γαμέειν καὶ Ὀδυσσῆος γέρας ἕξειν, 15.521–522; see [[eurymachus]], [[marriage]] and [[aristos]]). The prize of honor is the king's: in the underworld Odysseus asked his mother “whether my prize of honor is still with them, or some other man / already holds it” (11.175–176; see the Book 11 note and [[basileus]]), and the son now says that the suitor who marries his mother will take it. The answer comes at once in the falcon, and in the seer's reading that no race in Ithaca is “more kingly” than Telemachus' own (15.525–534; see [[the-omen-of-the-falcon]] and [[genee]]).
