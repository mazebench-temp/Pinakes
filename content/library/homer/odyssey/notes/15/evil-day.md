---
summary: Telemachus names Eurymachus to the seer and leaves the end to Zeus, “whether before the marriage he will bring about an evil day for them” (15.524).
en: [evil day]
---

Here κακὸν ἦμαρ is not warded off but brought to pass, τελευτήσει, and on the suitors (see the general article). Monro calls εἴ κε with the future in an object clause the only instance in Homer, and would read the subjunctive τελευτήσῃ (D. B. Monro, *Homer's Odyssey, Books XIII–XXIV*, 1901, on 15.524; see the translation's note on 15.524). As Telemachus speaks, the falcon flies by on the right (15.525–534; see [[the-omen-of-the-falcon]], [[dwelling-in-the-upper-air]] and [[the-suitors]]).
