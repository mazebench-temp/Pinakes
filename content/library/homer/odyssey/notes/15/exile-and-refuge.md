---
summary: The seer Theoclymenus comes to Telemachus at Pylos as a fugitive killer: he has killed a man of his own tribe, whose brothers and kinsmen are powerful in Argos, and he is an exile, “since it is my lot to wander among men”; he asks to be taken aboard, and Telemachus takes him (15.223–225, 15.271–286). His ancestor Melampus fled his native land and came to rule in Argos (15.228–240).
# Unnamed: the fugitive (15.223-225) and his plea (15.272-278).

**The killer in flight.** The poem introduces him by his flight before his name: “a man came up close to him, / from a far land, fleeing from Argos, having killed a man, / a seer” (15.223–225; see [[theoclymenus]]). τηλεδαπός, “from a far land”, and φεύγω, “flee, be an exile”, are the words of the homicide (see the translation's table of key words), and he gives his own account in the same terms: he has killed a man ἔμφυλον, “of my own tribe”, a word found only here. πολλοὶ δέ carries on the description of the dead man in paratactic form, “one who has many brothers and comrades” (D. B. Monro, *Homer's Odyssey, Books XIII–XXIV*, 1901, on 15.273), and they are men who “rule with great power over the Achaeans” in Argos. So he must flee, “Avoiding death and black doom at their hands”, where τῶν goes with the ὑπό of ὑπαλευάμενος, “under stress of” (Monro on 15.275; see [[black-doom]]). Odysseus will state the rule in the last books: a man who has killed even one man, with few helpers behind him, is an exile, leaving behind his kin by marriage and his native land (23.118–120, a plain rendering).

**The lot to wander.** “I am an exile, since it is my lot to wander among men” (15.276; see [[aisa]] and [[nothing-worse-than-wandering]]). The plea that follows is a suppliant's, and Telemachus grants it at once, takes his spear and seats him beside himself in the stern (15.277–286; see [[supplication]]). The Cretan of Odysseus' first false tale, a killer who begged Phoenicians to take him aboard, told on Ithaca what [[theoclymenus|Theoclymenus]] does in fact at Pylos (13.258–275; see the Book 13 note and [[the-first-cretan-tale]]).

**The seer's ancestors.** Exile runs in the line. Melampus, “who once lived in Pylos”, came “to the land of other men, fleeing his native land / and great-hearted Neleus”, who had held his goods by force; he was bound for a year in the halls of Phylacus, drove the cattle to Pylos, took vengeance on Neleus, and came to Argos, where it was his lot to dwell, ruling over many Argives (15.226–240; see [[melampus]] and [[melampus-and-the-cattle-of-iphiclus]]). ἄλλων δῆμον, “the land of other men”, is Argos, as the words are taken up again at 15.238 (Monro on 15.228). The fugitive found a kingdom, as Phoenix found one with Peleus (*Iliad* 9.480–484). And Polypheides, the seer's father, “moved away to Hyperesia, angry with his father” (15.254; see [[polypheides]] and [[hyperesia]]): the other road into exile, a quarrel with a father, which was Phoenix's (*Iliad* 9.447–457). The descendant flees Argos, where his ancestor found refuge.
