---
summary: As Telemachus speaks, a bird flies by on the right, “a falcon, the swift messenger of Apollo” (15.526).
---

ταχὺς ἄγγελος is the phrase of Priam's prayer for an omen, “send a bird of omen, your swift messenger,” answered by the eagle that Zeus sends on the right (*Iliad* 24.310–321). Here the messenger is Apollo's, the falcon that the Odyssey calls “the nimblest of winged birds” (13.86–87), and the seer knows it at once for “a bird of omen” (15.531–532; see [[falcon]], [[birds]], [[apollo]] and [[the-omen-of-the-falcon]]).
