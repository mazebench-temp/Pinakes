---
summary: While Telemachus sails home by night, the poem goes back to the swineherd's hut, where Odysseus and Eumaeus have supper with the herdsmen (15.301–302) and then sit up drinking and talking of their sorrows (15.398–400).
---

“And the two of them in the hut, Odysseus and the brilliant swineherd, / were having supper; and the other men were having supper beside them” (15.301–302; see [[eumaeus]], [[odysseus]] and [[brilliant]]); the first verse will open the next book, when Telemachus comes to the hut (16.1). The scene in the hut carries on the idyll of Odysseus at the swineherd's, and fills the night of Telemachus' voyage (D. B. Monro, *Homer's Odyssey, Books XIII–XXIV*, 1901, on 15.301–495; see [[the-journey-of-telemachus]]). There the guest tests his host once more (15.304–339; see [[peira]]), and Eumaeus, when the others have gone out to sleep, keeps him up for the night of stories: “But let us two, drinking and feasting in the hut, / take delight in each other's wretched sorrows / as we remember them” (15.398–400; see [[delight-in-remembered-sorrows]], [[a-time-for-many-words]] and [[the-kidnapping-of-eumaeus]]). They sleep only a little while before dawn (15.493–494).
