---
summary: Menelaus' answer to Telemachus' request to go home (15.68–71): he will not keep him long, for he is indignant with a host who loves beyond measure and hates beyond measure, “what is due is better in all things”. The next verses explain the excess (15.72–74).
# 15.68-70, the sentence that the half-verse closes.
refs: [15.68-70]
---

{{quote:aa1325d3-fac9-54a4-8ff9-fadaf049ea16}}

**The host's measure.** Telemachus asks at dawn to be sent home, “for already my heart yearns to come home” (15.64–66; see [[telemachus]] and [[nostos]]). Menelaus grants it before anything else, and gives the reason as a judgment on hosts: he blames another host, “who loves beyond measure / and hates beyond measure”. φιλέω and ἐχθαίρω keep “love” and “hate” here for the antithesis, where φιλέω of a host is otherwise “make welcome” (see the translation's table of key words, ἔξοχα φιλέω, and its note on 15.70–74). The opposition is repeated and explained in the next two verses: too much kindness, shown in keeping a guest against his will, is as bad as sending him away before he wishes to go (D. B. Monro, *Homer's Odyssey, Books XIII–XXIV*, 1901, on 15.70; see [[welcome-the-guest-send-him-on]]). The half-verse is the one with which Alcinous answered Odysseus' fear of his anger (7.310), and both times the host who speaks it is letting a guest go home (see [[alcinous]] and [[pompe]]).

**The measure kept.** Menelaus keeps his own word, with one excess of courtesy. He asks Telemachus to wait for the gifts and a meal, for a feast before the road is both an honor and a benefit (15.75–79; Monro on 15.78; see [[feast]] and [[guest-gifts]]), and then offers to go with him through Hellas and the heart of Argos to collect a gift from every city (15.80–85; see [[hellas]]). Telemachus declines, for he left no one to watch over his possessions (15.86–91), and Menelaus does not press him (15.92–94; see [[menelaus]]).
