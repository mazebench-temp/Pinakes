---
summary: Telemachus wakes Peisistratus: “Wake up, Peisistratus, son of Nestor; bring the single-hoofed horses / and yoke them under the chariot, so that we may make our way” (15.46–47).
---

μώνυχας ἵππους, the Iliad's commonest epithet of horses at the verse-end, comes in the Odyssey only here, of the horses of a journey and not of battle (see the general article, [[horses]] and [[chariot-journey]]). Peisistratus will not drive “through the dark night,” and bids him wait for the gifts (15.49–55; see [[dnopheros]]).
