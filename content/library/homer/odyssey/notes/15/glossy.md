---
summary: Telemachus dresses to meet Menelaus: “in haste he put his glossy tunic about his body, / and threw a great mantle over his massive shoulders” (15.60–61).
---

χιτῶνα περὶ χροῒ σιγαλόεντα returns when the beggar describes to Penelope the tunic that Odysseus wore as his guest in Crete: “And I noticed the tunic about his body, glossy” (19.232, a plain rendering; see [[tunic]] and [[dressing-scene]]). The great mantle, μέγα φᾶρος, is the one Odysseus draws over his head at the Phaeacians' feast (8.84; see [[cloak]]).
