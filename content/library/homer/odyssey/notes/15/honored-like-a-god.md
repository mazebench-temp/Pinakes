---
summary: Telemachus names Eurymachus to Theoclymenus, “the splendid son of wise-hearted Polybus, / whom the men of Ithaca now look upon as a god” (15.519–520).
grc: ["ἶσα θεῷ"]
en: [look upon as a god]
---

τὸν νῦν ἶσα θεῷ Ἰθακήσιοι εἰσορόωσι: the people's honor of the formula, given in their eyes rather than by their gifts, as the Phaeacians look on Arete and on the man of graceful speech “as a god” (7.71, 8.173; see the general article). Here it is said of the suitor of whom Telemachus goes on, “for he is by far the best man, and most of all he is eager / to marry my mother and to hold the prize of honor of Odysseus” (15.521–522), and the omen of the falcon follows at once (15.525–534; see [[eurymachus]], [[polybus-father-of-eurymachus]] and [[the-omen-of-the-falcon]]).
