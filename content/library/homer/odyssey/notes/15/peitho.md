summary: Obedience on Telemachus' ship: the crew “listened closely to him and obeyed” at Pylos (15.220) and “eagerly obeyed” when he bade them hoist the mast (15.288); and Peiraeus is the companion who, “in other things most of all”, obeys him (15.540–541).
# πείθῃ (15.541), only here in Homer.
grc: [πείθῃ]
---

Telemachus' first voyage was made with a crew that answered him, and the book shows it again. At Pylos he bids them put the arms in order and go aboard, “and they listened closely to him and obeyed” (κλύον ἠδʼ ἐπίθοντο, 15.220); with the seer aboard he bids them “lay hold of the tackle; and they eagerly obeyed” (ἐσσυμένως ἐπίθοντο, 15.288; see [[sailing-type-scene]] and [[hetairos]]). On Ithaca he trusts the stranger to the most obedient of them: “Peiraeus, son of Clytius, you in other things most of all / obey me, of my companions who followed me to Pylos; / now too take this stranger for me to your house” (πείθῃ ἐμῶν ἑτάρων, 15.540–542; see [[peiraeus-son-of-clytius]], [[clytius-father-of-peiraeus]], [[trusted-companion]] and [[xenia]]). The son whom the suitors would not obey in the assembly of Book 2 is obeyed by his own men.
