---
summary: Vengeance foretold and vengeance told: Helen reads the omen, “so Odysseus … will come home and take vengeance” (15.176–177); and Melampus “took vengeance for the shameful deed / on godlike Neleus” (15.236–237).
---

**Vengeance foretold.** Helen reads the eagle that snatched the goose from the court: “so Odysseus, after much suffering and much wandering, / will come home and take vengeance; or else he is already / at home, and is planting evil for all the suitors” (οἴκαδε νοστήσει καὶ τίσεται, 15.176–178; see [[the-omen-of-the-eagle-and-the-goose]], [[nostos]] and [[the-suitors]]). The verb in the middle is to make another pay: the vengeance of the master on the men who devour his house.

**Vengeance told.** In the seer's genealogy Melampus, who had lain a year in Phylacus' bonds for the sake of Neleus' daughter, “escaped his doom and drove the loud-bellowing cattle / to Pylos from Phylace, and took vengeance for the shameful deed / on godlike Neleus” (ἐτίσατο ἔργον ἀεικὲς / ἀντίθεον Νηλῆα, 15.235–237; see [[melampus]], [[neleus]] and [[melampus-and-the-cattle-of-iphiclus]]): Neleus had held his goods by force for a full year (15.229–231). The seer descended from him is himself in flight from the vengeance of a dead man's kin (15.272–278; see [[kasignetos]] and [[exile-and-refuge]]).
