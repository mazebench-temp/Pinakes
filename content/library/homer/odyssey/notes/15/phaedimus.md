At Telemachus' departure Menelaus gives the bowl he promised in Book 4, in the same verses: “I will give you a well-wrought mixing bowl; of silver / it is all, and its rims are finished with gold, / the work of Hephaestus. The hero Phaedimus gave it to me, / the king of the Sidonians, when his house sheltered me / as I came there on my way home; and I wish to grant it to you” (15.115–119 = 4.615–619; see [[mixing-bowl]], [[sidonians]], [[menelaus]] and [[the-wanderings-of-menelaus]]). The bowl passes from a Sidonian king to Menelaus and from Menelaus to Telemachus, a guest-gift given twice (see [[guest-gifts]]). The verses 113–119 are missing in two manuscripts, and Monro brackets them here (D. B. Monro, *Homer's Odyssey, Books XIII–XXIV*, 1901, critical note on 15.113–119; see the translation's note on 15.113–119). Whether φαίδιμος is here a name or the epithet “shining”, which the scholia doubted on Book 4, the text prints it with a capital (see [[phaidimos]]).
