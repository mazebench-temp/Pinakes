---
summary: Odysseus asks Eumaeus how he came to Ithaca: “or were you alone with the sheep or the cattle / when hostile men took you in their ships and sold you / to this man's house” (15.386–388).
---

The beggar's two guesses, a city sacked or a boy caught alone with the herds by raiders (15.383–388), are the ways of war and of raiding by sea (see [[piracy]] and [[slavery-and-freedom]]). The answer is neither: Eumaeus was carried off by Phoenician traders, to whom his own nurse betrayed him, and the verse of his sale, “to this man's house, and he paid a worthy price,” is the one in which the nurse tells of her own (15.388 = 15.429; see [[the-kidnapping-of-eumaeus]], [[the-phoenician-nurse]] and [[trade]]).
