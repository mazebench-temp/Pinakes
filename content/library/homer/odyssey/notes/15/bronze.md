---
summary: The bronze of the book is the bronze of spears, the spear of Theoclymenus and Telemachus' spear “tipped with sharp bronze” (15.282, 15.551); the tripods of Menelaus' promise are “of good bronze”, and the nurse's Sidon is “rich in bronze” (15.84, 15.425).
---

Telemachus takes “the bronze spear” of the suppliant seer and lays it on the deck (15.282; see [[spear]] and [[theoclymenus]]), and before he goes ashore he takes up his own, “a strong spear, tipped with sharp bronze” (15.551; see [[sharp]]). The metal also names the worth of a gift and of a city: the tripods that the hosts of Menelaus' promised journey would give are “of good bronze”, εὐχάλκων (15.84; see [[well-bronzed]] and [[tripod]]), and the Phoenician nurse claims to come “from Sidon, rich in bronze”, Σιδῶνος πολυχάλκου (15.425; see [[brazen]], [[sidon]] and [[the-phoenician-nurse]]), the city of the king who gave Menelaus his silver bowl (15.117–118; see [[sidonians]] and [[mixing-bowl]]).
