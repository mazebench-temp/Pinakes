---
summary: Offering to take Telemachus through Hellas and Argos, Menelaus promises that no host will send them away without a gift: “either a tripod of good bronze or a cauldron, / or two mules or a golden goblet” (15.80–85).
---

“And if you wish to turn aside through Hellas and the heart of Argos, / so that I myself may go with you, and I will yoke the horses for you / and lead you to the cities of men; and no one will send us / away just as we came, but each will give us at least one thing to take with us, / either a tripod of good bronze or a cauldron, / or two mules or a golden goblet” (15.80–85; see [[hellas]], [[argos]], [[well-bronzed]], [[cauldron]], [[mules]] and [[cup]]). αὔτως is “as we came”, that is, without any present (D. B. Monro, *Homer's Odyssey, Books XIII–XXIV*, 1901, on 15.83). The tripod heads the list, as it heads the gifts of kings: Polybus of Egyptian Thebes gave Menelaus two tripods (4.128–129; see the Book 4 note), and each of the Phaeacian lords gave Odysseus a great tripod and a cauldron for his voyage home (13.13–14; see the Book 13 note and [[the-gifts-of-the-phaeacians]]).

Aristarchus obelized 78–85. Monro grants that Telemachus was not likely to accept, but holds that ancient manners may have required some such speech from the host, and he rejects Bury's view that Hellas here is Achaia and the journey a short détour through Argolis and Achaia (on 15.80–85; see the translation's note on 15.78–85 and [[xenia]]). Telemachus declines: he left no one at home to watch over his possessions (15.86–91; see [[possessions]] and [[telemachus]]).
