summary: When Telemachus asks to leave, Eteoneus, who lives near Menelaus, comes from his bed, kindles a fire and roasts meat at his master's bidding, and at the parting meal carves and gives out the portions (15.95–98, 15.140).
# Ἐτεωνεύς and Βοηθοΐδης (15.95; Βοηθοΐδης alone 15.140) are claimed by the entry. Unnamed: "since he lived not far from
# him", Menelaus' order and his obedience (15.96-98).

The ready attendant who doubted at the door whether to take the strangers in (4.20–36) serves at their going. “And Eteoneus, son of Boethous, came near him, / having risen from his bed, since he lived not far from him; / Menelaus, good at the war cry, bade him kindle a fire / and roast meat; and he heard and did not disobey” (15.95–98; see [[menelaus]], [[boethous]] and [[fire]]). He rises from his bed as Menelaus had risen from his (15.58), and W. W. Merry points to these verses for his dwelling near the king (*Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 4.22). “He heard and did not disobey” is the verse-end of men who obey an order at once (see [[peitho]]). At the meal, after the handmaid's water and the housekeeper's bread, “beside them the son of Boethous divided the meat and served out the portions; / and the son of glorious Menelaus poured the wine” (15.140–141; see [[feast-type-scene]] and [[megapenthes]]): the patronymic stands alone for him, his last appearance in the poem.
