---
summary: Athena: “But you should go yourself and entrust each thing / to whichever of the slave women seems to you the best, / until the gods show you a glorious wife” (15.24–26).
---

Διὸς κυδρὴ παράκοιτις is the title of Hera and of Leto (*Iliad* 18.184; 11.580; see the Book 11 note); here κυδρὴν παράκοιτιν is the wife the gods may give Telemachus, the only use of the formula without Zeus (see the Odyssey translation's key words, κυδρὴ παράκοιτις). Some ancient critics rejected 15.24–26, on the ground that they lead to nothing in the story that follows (D. B. Monro, *Homer's Odyssey, Books XIII–XXIV*, 1901, on 15.24–26; see the translation's note on 15.19, 15.24–26, 15.91, [[telemachus]] and [[marriage]]).
