summary: Menelaus' son brings the silver mixing bowl, “strong Megapenthes” (15.122); Melampus lay bound at Phylace, “suffering bitter pains” (15.232), and in Argos “begot Antiphates and Mantius, two mighty sons” (15.242); and Theoclymenus to Telemachus: “There is no other race more kingly than yours / in the land of Ithaca, but you are always powerful” (15.533–534).
grc: [καρτεροὶ]
en: [powerful]
---

κρατερὸς Μεγαπένθης is the verse-end of Book 4 (4.11; see the Odyssey translation's key words), and κρατέρʼ ἄλγεα πάσχων the Iliad's for Philoctetes on Lemnos (*Iliad* 2.721). υἷε κραταιώ, which the entry claims from this book, is the Iliad's pair of “the two mighty sons of Cronus” (*Iliad* 13.345): κραταιός is formed from κράτος, like κρατερός (see the general article and [[mighty-fate]]); here the sons are the ancestors of Amphiaraus and of Theoclymenus (15.242–256; see [[antiphates-son-of-melampus]] and [[mantius]]). καρτεροί, new to the entry, is the seer's word for the house of Odysseus after the falcon's sign. Monro renders it “powerful”: the word is generally used of physical strength, but compare the common use of κρατέω, “bear rule” (D. B. Monro, *Homer's Odyssey, Books XIII–XXIV*, 1901, on 15.534; see the Odyssey translation's key words, [[the-omen-of-the-falcon]] and [[theoclymenus]]).
