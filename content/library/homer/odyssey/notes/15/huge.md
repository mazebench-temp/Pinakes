---
summary: The omen at the farewell: “As he spoke, a bird flew by on the right, / an eagle, carrying in its talons a huge white goose, / a tame one from the court” (15.160–162).
---

πέλωρος, the word of the Cyclops, is here the size of a fattened goose, and ἥμερος, “tame,” is used only here in Homer (see the Odyssey translation's key words, ἀργὴ χήν). In Helen's reading the eagle from the mountain is Odysseus, and the goose reared in the house is taken as the suitors will be (15.172–178; see [[the-omen-of-the-eagle-and-the-goose]], [[eagle]], [[geese]] and [[white]]).
