---
summary: The events at Lacedaemon end in this book, whose first 181 lines are the last night and morning of Telemachus' stay. Menelaus answers in the words of Telemachus' plea in Book 4, gives the bowl that he promised there in the verses of the promise, and lets him go; the two young men drive out through the city at line 182.
refs: [15.1-5, 15.182-183]
---

**The end of the stay.** The book begins with the place-name of the old title, “But Pallas Athena went to Lacedaemon with its wide dancing floors” (15.1), and finds Telemachus and Peisistratus “sleeping in the porch of glorious Menelaus” (15.5; see [[lacedaemon]] and [[the-title-of-odyssey-15]]). In Book 4 Telemachus had asked, “Son of Atreus, do not keep me here a long time” (4.594); Menelaus now answers him in the same words, “Telemachus, I will not keep you here for long / when you are eager for your homecoming” (πολὺν χρόνον ἐνθάδʼ ἐρύξω, 15.68–69; see [[what-is-due-is-better]]). The gift is the one he promised then, when Telemachus asked for a treasure he could take home instead of horses: the silver mixing bowl with its rims finished with gold, the work of Hephaestus, given in the verses of the promise (15.113–119 = 4.613–619; see [[mixing-bowl]]). Helen adds a robe of her own making, and as the chariot is about to set off she reads the omen of the eagle (15.123–130, 15.160–178; see [[helen]] and [[the-omen-of-the-eagle-and-the-goose]]). Then “very quickly / they sped through the city toward the plain” (15.182–183). The title of *Odyssey* 4 fits these lines as well as its own. Monro, who finds from the movements of Odysseus in the same days that Telemachus “must have spent twenty days in Sparta”, observes that his visit “is always represented as a hurried one” (D. B. Monro, *Homer's Odyssey, Books XIII–XXIV*, 1901, Appendix I §10).
