summary: The swine of Book 15 are the herd of Odysseus that Eumaeus guards. Athena sends Telemachus first to the swineherd, “who is the guardian over your swine” (15.38–39); Eumaeus bids his men sleep and follow “the master's swine” at dawn (15.396–397); and at the end of the book Telemachus comes on foot to the fold, “where his countless swine were, among which the swineherd, / being good, used to sleep” (15.555–557).

**The guardian of the swine.** Athena sends Telemachus home from Sparta with the verses with which she sent his father to the swineherd: “but you yourself go first of all to the swineherd, / who is the guardian over your swine, and is kind to you alike” (15.38–39 = 13.404–405; see [[athena]], [[eumaeus]] and the Book 13 note). The swine are Telemachus' own, τοι, as they were his father's when the same words were said to Odysseus; D. B. Monro refers on this verse to his note on 13.405, where he takes ὁμῶς, “alike”, of Eumaeus' loyalty, at one with his master (*Homer's Odyssey, Books XIII–XXIV*, 1901, on 15.39). Telemachus is to spend the night there and send the swineherd to the city with the news of his return (15.40–42).

**The master's swine.** The night in the hut is long, and Eumaeus keeps the stranger up to hear his story, but he sends the others to bed: “And of the rest, whoever's heart and spirit bids him, / let him go out and sleep; and when dawn appears / let him take his meal and follow the master's swine” (15.395–397). ὕεσσιν ἀνακτορίῃσιν, “the master's swine”, is found only here in Homer (see the translation's row ὕες ἀνακτόριαι); the herd is the property of the absent king, and the men who follow it are his servants (see [[possessions]]). In the morning the herdsmen are sent out “with the herded swine” (16.3, a plain rendering).

**The fold.** When the ship has gone on to the city, Telemachus walks to the farm: “and his feet carried him quickly on as he strode, until he came to the fold, / where his countless swine were, among which the swineherd, / being good, used to sleep, kind to his masters” (15.555–557; see [[fold]] and [[telemachus]]). The swine are sows, as in the sties of the fold, μυρίαι and ᾗσι being feminine (see the Book 13 and Book 14 notes). ἐνίαυεν, “used to sleep”, is said once more in the Odyssey, of the Cyclops: “There a huge man used to sleep, who herded his flocks / alone, far off” (9.187–188); Eumaeus, who in the last book went out on a stormy night to sleep beside the boars under a hollow rock (14.524–533), sleeps among the swine as their keeper. ἀνάκτεσιν ἤπια εἰδώς, “kind to his masters”, closes the book with the words of 15.39, ἤπια οἶδεν: the man who guards the swine is the man who is kind to the house (see [[kind-as-a-father]]). The next book opens in the hut at dawn, with the dogs fawning on Telemachus as he comes to the door (16.1–12).
