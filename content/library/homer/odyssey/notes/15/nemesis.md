---
summary: Menelaus' indignation at the host who keeps or hurries his guest: “I am indignant with another / man, a host, who loves beyond measure / and hates beyond measure” (15.69–71).
---

Menelaus grants Telemachus' request to go with a rule of hospitality: “Telemachus, I will not keep you here for long / when you are eager for your homecoming; I am indignant with another / man, a host, who loves beyond measure / and hates beyond measure; what is due is better in all things” (νεμεσσῶμαι δὲ καὶ ἄλλῳ / ἀνδρὶ ξεινοδόκῳ, 15.68–71; see [[xenia]], [[what-is-due-is-better]] and [[philos]]). νέμεσις is the indignation at what is not fitting (see the entry), and the unfitting thing is excess on either side: “Too much kindness, shown in keeping a guest against his will, is as bad as sending him away before he wishes to go” (D. B. Monro, *Homer's Odyssey, Books XIII–XXIV*, 1901, on 15.70). By saying that he would blame another host for it, Menelaus clears himself of the fault he names.
