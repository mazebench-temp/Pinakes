---
summary: Eumaeus' island is “not so very populous, but a good one, / good for cattle, good for sheep, full of wine, rich in wheat” (15.405–406).
---

A verse of four epithets of plenty, three of them found only here in Homer: εὔβοτος, εὔμηλος and οἰνοπληθής (see the translation's key words). πολύπυρος was the epithet of Dulichium in the false tale of the night before (14.335). The plenty is the first mark of an island where no famine or sickness comes (15.407–411; see [[syrie]] and [[wretch]]).
