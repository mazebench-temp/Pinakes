---
summary: Athena bids Telemachus hurry home, “so that you may still find your noble mother at home” (15.15).
---

Of persons ἀμύμων stays “noble” (see the Odyssey translation's key words). The goddess praises Penelope in the verse before her warning: her father and brothers bid her marry Eurymachus, and she may carry some possession from the house, “For you know what kind of heart is in a woman's breast” (15.16–23; see [[a-womans-heart]], [[penelope]] and [[eurymachus]]). The shade of Agamemnon, who warned that there is no trusting women, gives her the same epithet in the last book, praising the good mind of “noble Penelope” (24.194, a plain rendering; see [[there-is-no-trusting-women]]).
