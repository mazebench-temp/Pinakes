---
summary: Theoclymenus asks to whose house he should go, “of the men who rule over craggy Ithaca” (15.510).
en: [rule over]
---

On the shore of Ithaca the seer asks where he is to go: “And where shall I go, dear child? To whose house shall I come / of the men who rule over craggy Ithaca? / Or shall I go straight to your mother and your house?” (ἀνδρῶν οἳ κραναὴν Ἰθάκην κάτα κοιρανέουσιν, 15.509–511; see [[theoclymenus]], [[craggy]] and [[ithaca]]). The words are those with which Telemachus counted the suitors, “all who rule over craggy Ithaca” (1.247; see [[the-suitors]]), and the stranger, without knowing it, puts the question of the book: who rules in Ithaca. Telemachus answers by naming Eurymachus (15.518–522), and the falcon by naming no other race “more kingly” than his own (15.533; see [[basileus]] and [[the-omen-of-the-falcon]]).
