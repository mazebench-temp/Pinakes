---
summary: Two wishes: the woman's, as Athena tells it, who “wishes to increase the house of the man who marries her” (15.21), and Telemachus', “I wish to go back now to my own” (15.88).
en: [she wishes, I wish]
---

βούλομαι is to want one thing rather than another, and both wishes of the book are about a house. Athena warns Telemachus of a remarried woman: “she wishes to increase the house of the man who marries her, / and her former children and her dear wedded husband / she no longer remembers” (κείνου βούλεται οἶκον ὀφέλλειν ὅς κεν ὀπυίῃ, 15.21–23; see [[a-womans-heart]] and [[penelope]]). The use of ὅς κεν in a general statement is unusual, but may be defended on the ground that βούλεται ὀφέλλειν is felt as a future (D. B. Monro, *Homer's Odyssey, Books XIII–XXIV*, 1901, on 15.21). Telemachus answers Menelaus' offer of a tour with the wish to guard his own: “I wish to go back now to my own; for when I left I did not leave behind / anyone to watch over my possessions” (βούλομαι ἤδη νεῖσθαι ἐφʼ ἡμέτερʼ, 15.88–89; see [[nostos]] and [[possessions]]).
