---
summary: Statius' Thebaid tells at length the end of the house of Amphiaraus that this book gives in a line. His Amphiaraus reads the birds with an aged Melampus, whom this book makes his great-grandfather, and sees seven eagles fall; the necklace of Harmonia, a work of the Lemnian smith, brings the “fatal gold” into his house; and at Thebes the earth takes him down alive, commending his deceived house to Apollo.
---

- **Two seers and the birds.** Before the war the seers take the omens: Amphiaraus, and with him Melampus, son of Amythaon, “now older, but green in mind and in Phoebus”. The victims forbid the war, and they climb a mountain to read the empty sky; Melampus sees no bird of good omen, only vultures and hawks exulting in their prey, and Amphiaraus sees countless swans, which he reads as Thebes, attacked by seven eagles, the Argive kings, who fall one by one; “that one who falls, revered Melampus, I recognize” (*Thebaid* 3.450–551, plain renderings). In this book Melampus is Amphiaraus' great-grandfather, “Antiphates begot great-hearted Oicles, / and Oicles Amphiaraus, who rouses armies” (15.242–244), and two birds are read: an eagle that carries off a white goose, which Helen reads as the return and vengeance of Odysseus, and a falcon that plucks a dove, which the seer of Melampus' line reads for the house of Odysseus (15.160–178, 15.525–534): [[melampus]], [[antiphates-son-of-melampus]], [[oicles]], [[amphiaraus]], [[the-omen-of-the-eagle-and-the-goose]] and [[the-omen-of-the-falcon]].
- **A work of the Lemnian.** The necklace that Argia wears is Harmonia's, made by the Lemnian smith for her wedding, with the Cyclopes and the Telchines helping; Grief and Wrath and Pain and Discord pressed it with their hands, and the wife of the doomed seer saw it and brooded in secret envy (*Thebaid* 2.265–305, plain renderings). Argia gives it up willingly to bring Amphiaraus to the war, “and so the fatal gold burst into the house of Eriphyle” (*Thebaid* 4.187–213, plain renderings). In this book the smith's work is a gift of friendship, Menelaus' silver bowl, “the work of Hephaestus” (15.117), and Amphiaraus “perished at Thebes, for the sake of gifts to a woman” (15.247): [[amphiaraus]], [[eriphyle]], [[necklace]] and [[mixing-bowl]].
- **Taken down alive.** At the end of his last battle Amphiaraus commends to Apollo “my deceived house, and the punishment of my wicked wife, and the noble madness of my son”; the god leaps down grieving and turns away his tears, the ground opens, and the huge chasm swallows him with his horses, and “as he was, he carries his upright chariot down to Tartarus, and as he fell he looked back at the sky” (*Thebaid* 7.785–823, plain renderings). In this book Zeus and Apollo loved him “with all their hearts / with every kind of love”, and of his end the poem says only that “he did not reach the threshold of old age, / but perished at Thebes” (15.245–247): [[amphiaraus]] and [[the-seven-against-thebes]].
