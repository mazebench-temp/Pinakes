---
summary: Menelaus answers Telemachus' request to go home, “Then Menelaus, good at the war cry, answered him” (15.67); and Odysseus the swineherd's plea to stay, “Then much-enduring brilliant Odysseus answered him” (15.340).
---

15.67 is the verse of Menelaus' answer to Agamemnon in the night of *Iliad* 10 (*Iliad* 10.60), the book whose sleepless leader and wakened companion the opening of this one has followed (15.4–8, 15.44–45; see [[sweet-sleep]] and [[good-at-the-war-cry]]); the answer is the host's maxim, “what is due is better in all things” (15.68–74; see [[what-is-due-is-better]]). 15.340, the verse of the stranger's answer to the plea to stay, returns twice in the books that follow (16.186, 17.280; see [[much-enduring]]).
