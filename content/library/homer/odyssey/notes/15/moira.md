---
summary: The portions served at the last meal in Menelaus' house (15.140), and κατὰ μοῖραν, “fair and right”, twice of a man deliberating: Menelaus, how to read the omen (15.170), and Peisistratus, how to grant Telemachus' request (15.203).
---

**Portions.** At the farewell meal “the son of Boethous divided the meat and served out the portions” (κρέα δαίετο καὶ νέμε μοίρας, 15.140; see [[eteoneus]] and [[feast]]), the share of each guest, which is the first sense of the word (see the entry).

**Fair and right.** Two men in the book ponder how to act κατὰ μοῖραν, in due measure. When the eagle flies by, “Menelaus, dear to Ares, pondered / how he might read it to him fair and right, with understanding” (ὅππως οἱ κατὰ μοῖραν ὑποκρίναιτο νοήσας, 15.169–170; see [[the-omen-of-the-eagle-and-the-goose]] and [[noos]]), ὑποκρίναιτο being “should expound”, “read the sign” (D. B. Monro, *Homer's Odyssey, Books XIII–XXIV*, 1901, on 15.170; see [[divination]]); but Helen is before him with the reading (15.171–178; see [[helen]]). At Pylos “the son of Nestor took counsel with his heart / how he might promise and fulfill it fair and right” (ὅππως οἱ κατὰ μοῖραν ὑποσχόμενος τελέσειεν, 15.202–203; see [[peisistratus]] and [[mythos]]). The two verses differ only in the participle and the verb, and are found only here.
