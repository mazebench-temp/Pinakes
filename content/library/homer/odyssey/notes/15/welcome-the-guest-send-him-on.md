---
summary: Menelaus' rule for hosts (15.72–74) is the measure of the book's hospitality. Nestor is the host who would keep a guest in his eagerness to welcome him, and Telemachus avoids him (15.199–201); in the hut Odysseus tests Eumaeus on the two halves of the rule, whether he will welcome him and bid him stay or urge him on to the city, and the swineherd answers with both (15.304–339).
# 15.73, the second half of the argument; 15.72 and 15.74 are the entry's forms.
refs: [15.73]
---

**At Sparta.** Menelaus speaks the rule at dawn, when Telemachus asks to be sent home, and keeps it: he will not hold the young man long, but asks him to wait for the gifts and a meal (15.68–79; see [[guest-gifts]] and [[feast]]). His first words answer Telemachus' plea of the earlier visit, “do not keep me here a long time” (μὴ δή με πολὺν χρόνον ἐνθάδʼ ἔρυκε, 4.594), with the same words turned to a promise, “I will not keep you here for long” (οὔ τί σʼ ἐγώ γε πολὺν χρόνον ἐνθάδʼ ἐρύξω, 15.68; see [[menelaus]]). ξεῖνον ἐποτρύνει, “urges on a guest” (15.73), is the fault of the host who hates beyond measure, and κατερύκει, “holds back”, the fault of the one who loves beyond measure (D. B. Monro, *Homer's Odyssey, Books XIII–XXIV*, 1901, on 15.70; see [[what-is-due-is-better]]).

**At Pylos.** The book shows the second fault at once. Telemachus will not go back to Nestor's house, “lest the old man keep me in his house against my will / in his eagerness to make me welcome”, ἱέμενος φιλέειν, Menelaus' word for the welcome turned into a danger (15.199–201; see [[nestor]]). Peisistratus agrees: “such is his overweening spirit, he will not let you go, / but he will come here himself to call you” (15.212–213), words half-serious, repeated from Achilles' temper in the Iliad (Monro on 15.212; *Iliad* 18.262).

**In the hut.** Odysseus' test of the swineherd is the rule put as a question: he speaks “testing the swineherd, / whether he would still welcome him with care and bid him stay / there at the farmstead, or would urge him on to the city” (15.304–306; see [[peira]] and [[eumaeus]]). φιλέοι and ὀτρύνειε are Menelaus' two verbs. Eumaeus answers with both halves of the rule. “But stay; for no one is vexed by your presence”, and when Telemachus comes, “he will clothe you in a cloak and a tunic as clothing, / and will send you wherever your heart and spirit bid you” (15.335–339; 14.515–517; see [[pompe]]). The test is passed, and the stranger stays the night.
