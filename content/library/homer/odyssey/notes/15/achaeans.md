---
summary: The Achaeans are the army in which Menelaus fought beside Nestor, “while we sons of the Achaeans were fighting in Troy” (15.153), and the people of Argos over whom the kin of the man Theoclymenus killed “rule with great power” (15.274).
---

**The sons of the Achaeans.** Menelaus remembers Nestor's kindness “while we sons of the Achaeans were fighting in Troy” (153; see [[nestor]], [[troy]] and [[sons-of-the-achaeans]]), the verse in which Odysseus remembered Athena's (13.315).

**The Achaeans of Argos.** Theoclymenus is in flight from the kin of the man he killed: “he has many brothers and kinsmen / in horse-pasturing Argos, and they rule with great power over the Achaeans” (273–274; see [[theoclymenus]], [[argos]] and [[the-killer-in-exile]]). Here the Achaeans are not the army at Troy but the people of one land, as the people of Ithaca are “the long-haired Achaeans” whom Telemachus summons to the assembly (2.7; see [[ithacans]]).
