---
summary: Telemachus puts on his glossy tunic in haste to meet Menelaus (15.60); in the hut the tunic is named with the cloak as the guest's dress, worn by the suitors' servants, promised to the beggar, and given by Anticleia to the young Eumaeus (15.331, 15.338, 15.368).
---

“in haste he put his glossy tunic about his body” (15.60; see [[glossy]], [[cloak]] and [[dressing-scene]]): the disguised Odysseus will describe the tunic he saw on Odysseus in the same words, χιτῶνʼ … περὶ χροῒ σιγαλόεντα (19.232). With the cloak the tunic makes the guest's dress, and the hut names it three times: on the suitors' servants, “well clad in cloaks and tunics” (15.331; see [[the-suitors]]), in Eumaeus' promise that Telemachus will clothe the beggar “in a cloak and a tunic as clothing” (15.338; see [[clothing]]), and in the gift of Anticleia to the boy she reared with her daughter (15.368; see [[anticleia]] and [[eumaeus]]).
