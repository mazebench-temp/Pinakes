---
summary: “and fair-cheeked Helen stood beside him, / holding the robe in her hands” (15.123–124).
---

The epithet and the robe stand together as in *Iliad* 6, where “fair-cheeked Theano took the robe” that Hecuba had chosen and laid it on the knees of Athena (*Iliad* 6.302); the robe that Helen chose, in the verses of Hecuba's choice, is for a bride, Telemachus' wife (15.104–108, 15.125–128; see [[robe]], [[helen]] and [[inlaid]]). καλλιπάρῃος is new to the entry in the Odyssey, and only here in Homer is it Helen's.
