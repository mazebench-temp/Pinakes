---
summary: Melampus came “to horse-pasturing Argos; for there it was his lot / to dwell, ruling over many Argives” (15.239–240); and the man Theoclymenus killed has many kinsmen “in horse-pasturing Argos, and they rule with great power over the Achaeans” (15.274).
---

Ἄργος ἐς ἱππόβοτον opens the verse as in the Iliad's “to horse-pasturing Argos and Achaea of the lovely women” (*Iliad* 3.75, 3.258), a line that Monro calls of a traditional type (D. B. Monro, *Homer's Odyssey, Books XIII–XXIV*, 1901, on 15.80–85). ἱππόβοτον, the accusative, is new to the entry in the Odyssey. Argos is where Melampus' line settled and the land from which Theoclymenus flees (15.224, 15.238–240, 15.272–274; see [[argos]], [[melampus]] and [[theoclymenus]]).
