**The bowl of the Sidonian king.** Menelaus gives Telemachus “a well-wrought mixing bowl”, “the work of Hephaestus. The hero Phaedimus gave it to me, / the king of the Sidonians, when his house sheltered me / as I came there on my way home” (15.115–119; see [[phaedimus]], [[sidonians]] and [[mixing-bowl]]). The gift promised in the fourth book is given in the same seven verses (15.113–119 = 4.613–619), and with it a piece of the wealth of the wanderings (see the Book 4 note). Two manuscripts omit the verses here, and Monro brackets them; Murray prints them (see the translation's note on 15.113–119).

**Much wandering.** Helen reads the omen of the eagle in the phrase in which Menelaus told of his own eight years abroad: as he brought his wealth home “after much suffering and much wandering”, so Odysseus, “after much suffering and much wandering, / will come home and take vengeance” (15.176–177; 4.81; see [[the-omen-of-the-eagle-and-the-goose]] and [[odysseus]]).
