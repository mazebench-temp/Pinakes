---
summary: Sailing from Pylos, the crew hoist the white sails “with well-twisted ox-hide ropes” (15.291), and at dawn off the coast of Ithaca they loose them and take down the mast (15.496).
---

“and hoisted the white sails with well-twisted ox-hide ropes” (15.291; see [[white]], [[mast]] and [[sailing-type-scene]]): the verse of the departure from Ithaca (2.426; see the Book 2 note), and the only other place in Homer where βοεύς, “ox-hide rope”, and ἐΰστρεπτος, “well-twisted”, are found. Then Athena sends them “a following wind, / blowing hard and wild through the upper air” (15.292–293; see [[athena]] and [[upper-air]]). At dawn by the shore of Ithaca “the companions of Telemachus loosed the sails, and took down the mast / quickly” (15.496–497; see [[mast]]); at Chryse the crew “furled the sails and stowed them in the black ship” before they lowered the mast and rowed in (*Iliad* 1.433).
