# Ἀμφίλοχ* (in the entry) claims Ἀμφίλοχος (15.248).
---

“His sons were Alcmaeon and Amphilochus” (15.248) closes the elder line of Melampus, after the verses on the father, whom Zeus and Apollo loved, and who “did not reach the threshold of old age, / but perished at Thebes, for the sake of gifts to a woman” (15.245–247; see [[amphiaraus]], [[alcmaeon]] and [[the-seven-against-thebes]]). The verse names the sons and tells nothing of them: their war against Thebes and their mother's death are left out (see [[the-epigonoi]] and [[alcmaeon-kills-eriphyle]]). The poet turns back to the younger line, which ends in the seer standing beside Telemachus' ship (15.249–256; see [[theoclymenus]] and [[mantius]]).
