summary: Old men and old age: Nestor, “the old man” whose welcome Telemachus would escape (15.200, 15.210); Amphiaraus, who “did not reach the threshold of old age” (15.246), and Laertes, left “on the threshold of old age” and brought by grief “to old age before its time” (15.348, 15.357); the people of Syrie, whom Apollo and Artemis kill when they “grow old” (15.409); and Eumaeus' father, the old king whom the nurse fears (15.442).
# γήραος (15.246, 15.348; again 23.212) and γηράσκωσι (15.409), first met here among the Odyssey's notes.
grc: [γήραος, γηράσκωσι]
---

**The old man.** At Pylos Nestor is “the old man” whose hospitality Telemachus fears: “lest the old man keep me in his house against my will / in his eagerness to make me welcome” (15.200–201; see [[nestor]] and [[xenia]]), and Peisistratus bids him hurry aboard “before I reach home and bring word to the old man” (15.210).

**The threshold of old age.** Of Amphiaraus, loved by Zeus and Apollo, the poet says, “but he did not reach the threshold of old age” (οὐδʼ ἵκετο γήραος οὐδόν, 15.246; see [[amphiaraus]]): the threshold is not the beginning of old age, but he never fairly set foot in it (D. B. Monro, *Homer's Odyssey, Books XIII–XXIV*, 1901, on 15.246, comparing *Iliad* 22.60). In the hut Odysseus asks after his father, “whom he left on the threshold of old age when he went” (ἐπὶ γήραος οὐδῷ, 15.348; see [[laertes]]), the words in which Priam foresees his own end (*Iliad* 22.60), and Eumaeus answers that his wife's death “brought him to old age before its time” (ἐν ὠμῷ γήραϊ θῆκεν, 15.357; see [[anticleia]] and [[achos]]). ὠμός is “unripe”, “premature”; the meaning of ὠμογέρων, “in green old age” (*Iliad* 23.791), is curiously different (Monro on 15.357; see the translation's note on 15.357). Penelope will say that the gods begrudged her and Odysseus to stay by each other and “come to the threshold of old age” (23.210–212, a plain rendering).

**Growing old on Syrie.** On Eumaeus' island no famine or sickness comes, “but when the tribes of men grow old throughout the city, / Apollo, god of the silver bow, comes with Artemis / and comes upon them with his gentle arrows and kills them” (15.409–411; see [[syrie]], [[nousos]] and [[gentle-arrows]]): on Syrie death waits for old age. Its old king is Eumaeus' father, and the nurse fears that someone may “go to the house and tell / the old man” of her plot (γέροντι, 15.442–443; see [[ctesius]] and [[the-phoenician-nurse]]).
