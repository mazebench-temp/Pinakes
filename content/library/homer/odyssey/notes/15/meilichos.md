---
summary: Eumaeus: “But from my mistress it is not possible to hear anything kindly, / neither word nor deed, since evil has fallen on the house, / overbearing men” (15.374–376).
grc: [μείλιχον]
en: [kindly]
---

μείλιχον, new to the entry in the Odyssey, is the word of Briseis' lament for Patroclus, “who were always kind” (*Iliad* 19.300; see the general article). The mistress is Penelope: since the suitors came, the slaves long in vain to speak with her face to face and to take something back with them to the farm (15.375–379; see [[penelope]] and [[the-suitors]]). Monro notes that ἀκοῦσαι, “to hear,” suits only the word, not the deed (D. B. Monro, *Homer's Odyssey, Books XIII–XXIV*, 1901, on 15.375).
