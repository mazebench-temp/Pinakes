---
summary: Theoclymenus begs Telemachus “by the burnt offerings and by the god, and then / by your own head,” to “tell me truly, as I ask, and do not hide it: / Who are you among men, and from where? Where are your city and your parents?” (15.261–264).
---

The question of hosts to strangers is asked by a stranger of the man he will beg to take him on board, with a verse of its own before it (15.263; see the Odyssey translation's formulas, and [[supplication]] and [[theoclymenus]]). Telemachus answers in the verse of his first answer to Athena, “Well then, stranger, I will tell you very exactly” (15.266 = 1.214; see [[give-me-an-exact-account]]). He does not put the question to the stranger in turn: the seer tells his own story unasked, a killer fleeing the kinsmen of the man he killed (15.271–278; see [[the-killer-in-exile]] and [[exile-and-refuge]]).
