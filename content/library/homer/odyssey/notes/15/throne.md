---
summary: Dawn of the golden throne comes as Peisistratus ends his counsel (15.56), and in the line of Melampus she carries off Cleitus for his beauty (15.250); at Menelaus' last meal the young men sit “on the chairs and the seats” (15.134).
---

**Dawn's throne.** “So he spoke, and at once Dawn of the golden throne came” (15.56; see [[golden-throned]] and [[eos]]): the verse that ends both of Circe's speeches of instruction (10.541, 12.142; see the Book 10 note), here the end of Peisistratus' counsel to wait for Menelaus and his gifts (15.49–55; see [[peisistratus]]). In the line of Melampus the goddess is a lover: “but Cleitus was carried off by Dawn of the golden throne / for the sake of his beauty, so that he might dwell among the immortals” (15.250–251; see [[cleitus-son-of-mantius]] and [[dawn-and-orion]]). The dawn after the night in the hut is ἐΰθρονος, “of the fair throne” (15.495; see [[of-the-fair-throne]]).

**Chairs and seats.** At the meal before the leave-taking, “they sat down on the chairs and the seats” (15.134; see [[feast-type-scene]]), κατὰ κλισμούς τε θρόνους τε, the pair of seats of the suitors in Odysseus' hall, of Nestor's kin at Pylos and of Circe's guests (1.145, 3.389, 10.233; see the translation's row κλισμός, θρόνος).
