---
summary: The sign of the eagle and the goose: Peisistratus asks Menelaus “whether the god has shown this sign to us two, or to you yourself” (15.168).
---

As Telemachus takes his leave, “a bird flew by on the right, / an eagle, carrying in its talons a huge white goose, / a tame one from the court” (15.160–162; see [[eagle]], [[the-omen-of-the-eagle-and-the-goose]] and [[divination]]). δεξιός is “to the right”, crossing the path from left to right, the lucky direction (D. B. Monro, *Homer's Odyssey, Books XIII–XXIV*, 1901, on 15.160; see the translation's note on 15.160, 15.164). Peisistratus asks for a reading: “Consider now, Menelaus, nurtured by Zeus, leader of the people, / whether the god has shown this sign to us two, or to you yourself” (ἢ νῶϊν τόδʼ ἔφηνε θεὸς τέρας ἦε σοὶ αὐτῷ, 15.167–168; see [[theos]]). “Show a sign” is the phrase of the omen at Aulis, “To us Zeus the counselor has shown this great sign” (*Iliad* 2.324), and of the sign that Nestor's men asked of the god on the way home from Troy (3.173). Menelaus ponders, and Helen reads it (15.169–178; see [[prophecy]] and [[helen]]). The second omen of the book, the falcon with the dove, is not called a τέρας: [[theoclymenus|Theoclymenus]] knows it for “a bird of omen”, οἰωνός (15.532; see [[birds]], [[falcon]] and [[the-omen-of-the-falcon]]).
