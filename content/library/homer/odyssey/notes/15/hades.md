summary: Hades is named once, in the disguised Odysseus' question about his own parents, “whether they still live somewhere under the rays of the sun, / or are already dead and in the house of Hades” (15.349–350).
# The entry's Ἀΐδαο claims 15.350.
---

**Alive or dead.** When Eumaeus bids him stay, the stranger asks after “the mother of divine Odysseus, / and of his father, whom he left on the threshold of old age when he went, / whether they still live somewhere under the rays of the sun, / or are already dead and in the house of Hades” (15.347–350; see [[anticleia]], [[laertes]] and the Book 15 note on [[helios]]). It is Penelope's question to the phantom about her husband, “or is already dead and in the house of Hades” (4.834; see the Book 4 note), here in the plural, like Priam's of his two sons, “But if they are already dead and in the house of Hades” (*Iliad* 22.52). The questioner knows half the answer. He has been to the house of Hades himself and seen his mother there, and she told him that his father lived on in the country, grieving and longing for his son's return, and that longing for him had robbed her of her life (11.187–203; see [[odysseus-in-the-land-of-the-dead]]). Eumaeus tells him the same in his own words: Laertes still lives, but prays to Zeus for death, and his wife “died of grief for her glorious son, / by a wretched death” (15.353–359; see the Book 15 note on [[zeus]]).
