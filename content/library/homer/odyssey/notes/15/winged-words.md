---
summary: Peisistratus urges Telemachus aboard, “and urging him on he addressed him with winged words” (15.208); and Theoclymenus comes up to him at the stern, “and he spoke to him, addressing him with winged words” (15.259).
---

ἐποτρύνων ἔπεα πτερόεντα προσηύδα is the Iliad's verse, in the plural, of a god or a leader urging men to battle (*Iliad* 13.94, 13.480, 17.219), here of a friend hurrying a friend away before his father can stop him (15.209–214; see [[peisistratus]]). 15.259 is the verse with which Telemachus first spoke to Athena at his door (1.122) and Odysseus to her on the shore of Ithaca (13.227), here for the stranger at the ship (see [[theoclymenus]]).
