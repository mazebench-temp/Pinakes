---
summary: “Then much-enduring brilliant Odysseus answered him” (15.340), when Eumaeus has bidden him stay.
---

The verse brings in the beggar's thanks to the swineherd, the wish of father Zeus and the saying “There is nothing worse for mortals than roaming” (15.341–345; see [[father-zeus]], [[nothing-worse-than-wandering]] and [[nothing-worse-than-the-sea]]). His other speeches in the hut are brought in plainly, “Odysseus spoke among them, testing the swineherd” (15.304), and with other epithets, “Odysseus of many wiles” (15.380) and “Odysseus, sprung from Zeus” (15.485; see [[of-many-wiles]] and [[sprung-from-zeus]]).
