---
summary: The child Eumaeus, when the Phoenician woman has been thrown overboard, is “left behind, grieving at heart” (15.481).
---

At sea, on the seventh day, Artemis strikes the nurse, and the traders throw her overboard “to become a spoil for seals and fish”: “but I was left behind, grieving at heart; / and the wind and the water carried them and drove them to Ithaca” (λιπόμην ἀκαχήμενος ἦτορ, 15.477–482; see [[achos]], [[artemis]], [[the-kidnapping-of-eumaeus]] and [[the-phoenician-nurse]]). The half-verse is the one with which the false Cretan ended his tale to Athena, left on the shore of Ithaca by Phoenician sailors (13.286; see the Book 13 note): the swineherd's true story of the Phoenicians ends as his master's lie did.
