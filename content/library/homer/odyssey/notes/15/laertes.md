**The question.** Odysseus, who has heard in the hut that his son is away and that his house is overrun, asks the swineherd about his parents: “come, tell me of the mother of divine Odysseus, / and of his father, whom he left on the threshold of old age when he went, / whether they still live somewhere under the rays of the sun, / or are already dead and in the house of Hades” (15.347–350; see [[odysseus]] and [[anticleia]]). The threshold of old age is the phrase that the same book uses of Amphiaraus, who never reached it (15.246; see [[amphiaraus]]), and the Iliad of Priam (*Iliad* 22.60).

**The answer.** Eumaeus answers of the father first:

{{quote:8605b620-748d-5ec1-8f41-bce80f949668}}

ὠμός is “unripe”, “premature”, and the old age is ὠμὸν γῆρας, old age before its time; “The meaning of ὠμογέρων is curiously different” (D. B. Monro, *Homer's Odyssey, Books XIII–XXIV*, 1901, on 15.357; *Iliad* 23.791; see the translation's note on 15.357). The Suda sets the half-verse ἐν ὠμῷ γήραϊ θῆκε beside “for in misfortune mortals grow old quickly” (19.360) to explain a line of Aristophanes, “I come away more than a year older” (Suda π 1733). He lives as Athena, in the likeness of Mentes, described him to Telemachus, no longer coming to the city but suffering woes far away in the country with an old handmaid (1.189–193), and as his wife's shade told her son in the underworld (11.187–196). Eumaeus goes on to the wife's death and to his own childhood in her house (15.358–370; see [[anticleia]] and [[ctimene]]).

**The buyer.** At the end of his story, when the Phoenicians' ship has been carried to Ithaca, “Laertes bought me with his own possessions” (15.483; see [[eumaeus]] and [[the-kidnapping-of-eumaeus]]): the verse-end of the purchase of Eurycleia, “whom Laertes once bought with his own possessions” (1.430; see [[eurycleia]]), and of Eumaeus' own purchase of Mesaulius (14.452; see [[mesaulius]] and [[slavery-and-freedom]]). The king's son from [[syrie|Syrie]] was bought by the king of Ithaca and reared with his daughter.
