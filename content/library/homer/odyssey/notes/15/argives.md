---
summary: Melampus, the ancestor of the seer Theoclymenus, came to horse-pasturing Argos, where it was his lot “to dwell, ruling over many Argives” (15.239–240).
---

“And he himself came to the land of other men, / to horse-pasturing Argos; for there it was his lot / to dwell, ruling over many Argives” (238–240; see [[melampus]], [[argos]] and [[theoclymenus]]). The Argives are here the people of Argos and its land, not the army at Troy. πολλοῖσιν ἀνάσσοντʼ Ἀργείοισιν: ἀνάσσοντα is an accusative agreeing with the understood subject of ναιέμεναι, “to dwell … ruling” (D. B. Monro, *Homer's Odyssey, Books XIII–XXIV*, 1901, on 15.240). Herodotus tells how Melampus won his share of that rule: the Argives, whose women had gone mad, sent for him to cure them, and he asked half the kingship for himself and a third for his brother Bias (*Histories* 9.34; see [[herodotus]] and [[bias]]).
