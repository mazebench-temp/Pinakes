---
summary: Athena warns Telemachus that the suitors lie in ambush for him “in the strait between Ithaca and rocky Samos” (15.29), and Eumaeus tells how Odysseus' sister Ctimene was given in marriage to Same (15.367); both verses served ancient scholars arguing over the names of the island and its city.
---

**The strait.** Athena, standing near Telemachus at Sparta, warns him: “The best of the suitors lie in ambush for you of set purpose / in the strait between Ithaca and rocky Samos, / eager to kill you before you reach your native land” (28–30; see [[athena]], [[the-suitors]] and [[ambush]]). The second verse is the one in which Antinous laid the plan (4.671), and the suitors' ship is waiting at Asteris, the island in that strait (4.844–847; see [[asteris]] and the Book 4 note). Athena bids him keep his ship far from the islands and sail by night, and he comes home by another way (33–34, 299–300; see [[the-pointed-islands]]). Strabo cites the verse to show that by Samos Homer means the island later called Cephallenia: with the epithet “rocky” he marks off the island from the city of the same name, one of the island's four cities, which was called both Samos and Same (*Geography* 10.2.10; see [[strabo]] and [[rocky]]).

**Given in marriage to Same.** Anticleia reared Eumaeus with her youngest child, “Ctimene of the long robes, / her strong daughter”, and when both came of age, “they gave her in marriage to Same, and got countless gifts” (363–367; see [[ctimene]], [[anticleia]] and [[bride-price]]). Σάμηνδʼ ἔδοσαν, “gave … to Same”, is a pregnant construction, given in marriage and sent there, as the Phoenician of the false tale “put me on board a seafaring ship bound for Libya” (14.295), and as in the Iliad Autolycus gave the helmet “at Scandeia” to Amphidamas, Σκάνδειάνδʼ ἄρα δῶκε as Monro quotes it (D. B. Monro, *Homer's Odyssey, Books XIII–XXIV*, 1901, on 15.367; *Iliad* 10.268; see the translation's note on 15.367). Apollodorus, as Strabo reports him, took this verse with Telemachus' count of the suitors, “from Same there are twenty-four men” (16.249, a plain rendering), as proof that the city was called Same, while the island was Samos only. Strabo grants that the arguments have some reason, for the poet does not set out clearly either Cephallenia or Ithaca and the other places near them, so that commentators and historians disagree (*Geography* 10.2.10; see the Book 1 note).
