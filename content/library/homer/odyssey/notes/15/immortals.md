---
summary: Athena: “and he will send you a fair wind behind you, / whoever of the immortals guards and protects you” (15.34–35); Helen: “I will prophesy, as the immortals / put it in my heart” (15.172–173); and Cleitus, carried off by Dawn “so that he might dwell among the immortals” (15.251).
---

The goddess who will send the wind speaks of herself as one of the immortals, and when the ship sails it is “gray-eyed Athena” who sends it (15.292; see [[gray-eyed]] and [[athena]]). 15.251 is the Iliad's verse of Ganymede, whom the gods carried off “for the sake of his beauty, so that he might dwell among the immortals” (*Iliad* 20.235); Aristarchus obelized it here (D. B. Monro, *Homer's Odyssey, Books XIII–XXIV*, 1901, critical note on 15.251; see the translation's note on 15.225–255, [[cleitus-son-of-mantius]], [[ganymede]] and [[immortality]]). [[ctesius|Ctesius]] is “like the immortals” (15.414; see [[like-the-immortals]]).
