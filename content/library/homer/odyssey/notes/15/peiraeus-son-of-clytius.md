**The stranger given to him.** At the landing Telemachus has no house to offer the seer who came aboard at Pylos, and has named Eurymachus, until the falcon's omen turns him back to his own friends (15.508–538; see [[theoclymenus]], [[eurymachus]] and [[the-omen-of-the-falcon]]). “He spoke, and called to Peiraeus, his trusted companion: / ‘Peiraeus, son of Clytius, you in other things most of all / obey me, of my companions who followed me to Pylos; / now too take this stranger for me to your house, / and welcome him with care and honor him, until I come’” (15.539–543; see [[trusted-companion]], [[clytius-father-of-peiraeus]] and [[xenia]]). The request is the host's duty handed to a friend, in the words of welcome, ἐνδυκέως φιλέειν καὶ τιέμεν, which Telemachus will repeat when he tells his mother where the stranger is (15.543 = 17.56).

**His answer.** “Telemachus, why, if you were to stay here a long time, / I will take care of this man, and he shall not lack good cheer” (15.545–546). εἰ γάρ κεν is “why, if”, with γάρ as a kind of interjection, and ἐνθάδε, “here”, shows that Telemachus has landed not far from the swineherd's homestead (D. B. Monro, *Homer's Odyssey, Books XIII–XXIV*, 1901, on 15.545; see the translation's note on 15.545). ξενίων δέ οἱ οὐ ποθὴ ἔσται, “he shall not lack good cheer”, takes up Telemachus' own words of a moment before, οὐ γάρ τι ξενίων ποθή, “for there is no lack of good cheer for guests” (15.514; see the translation's key words, ποθή). He is δουρικλυτός, “famed for his spear”, the Iliad's epithet of fighters, which the Odyssey gives to him alone (15.544; see [[famed-for-his-spear]]).

**The ship.** “So saying, he went on board, and bade his companions / to go on board themselves and loose the stern-cables” (15.547–548): Peiraeus “now takes the command of the ship, and proceeds to carry out the directions of Telemachus”, who had bidden the crew row on to the city (Monro on 15.547; 15.503), and the order of the verbs, embarking before unmooring, puts the main action first (Monro on 15.548; see [[hysteron-proteron]] and [[stern-cables]]). The crew push off for the city, while Telemachus takes his spear from the deck and goes on foot to the fold (15.549–557; see [[telemachus]]).
