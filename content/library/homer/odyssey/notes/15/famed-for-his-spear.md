---
summary: Peisistratus: “But wait until he brings gifts and sets them in the chariot, / the hero, the son of Atreus, Menelaus famed for his spear” (15.51–52); and at the landing, “And Peiraeus, famed for his spear, answered him face to face” (15.544).
---

The compounds δουρικλειτός and δουρικλυτός, new to the entry in the Odyssey, share the English, as in the Iliad (see the Odyssey translation's key words, ναυσίκλυτος). δουρικλειτὸς Μενέλαος is the Iliad's verse-end (*Iliad* 5.55, 5.578, 10.230) and returns twice in Book 17 (17.116, 17.147); δουρικλυτός, in the Iliad the word of Idomeneus, Odysseus, Automedon, Meriones and Achilles, is in the Odyssey Peiraeus' alone (15.544; 17.71; see [[menelaus]] and [[peiraeus-son-of-clytius]]).
