summary: When Telemachus leaves Sparta, Megapenthes goes down with his father and Helen to the treasure chamber, carries the silver mixing bowl and sets it before the guest, and pours the wine at the parting meal (15.100–103, 15.121–122, 15.141).
# Μεγαπένθ* (in the entry) claims Μεγαπένθης (15.100, 15.122) and Μεγαπένθεʼ (15.103). Unnamed: "the son of glorious
# Menelaus" (15.141).
refs: [15.141]
---

The son of Menelaus by a slave woman, whose wedding Telemachus found being celebrated at Sparta (4.10–12), serves at the guest's departure. Menelaus goes down to the fragrant treasure chamber, “not alone: with him went Helen and Megapenthes” (15.100; see [[menelaus]] and [[helen]]), takes a two-handled cup himself, “and bade his son Megapenthes carry a mixing bowl / of silver” (15.103–104; see [[mixing-bowl]]). When Menelaus has offered the bowl, “the work of Hephaestus”, the gift of Phaedimus (15.113–119 = 4.613–619; see [[phaedimus]]), “the shining mixing bowl / strong Megapenthes brought and set before him, / the silver one” (15.121–123; see [[phaeinos]] and [[krateros]]): κρατερὸς Μεγαπένθης, “strong Megapenthes”, is his epithet at both his appearances (4.11; see the translation's key words). At the meal that follows, Eteoneus carves, “and the son of glorious Menelaus poured the wine” (15.140–141; see [[eteoneus]] and [[cupbearing]]).
