summary: The son's homecoming and the father's. Athena comes “to remind him of his homecoming and to urge him to return” (15.3), Telemachus' heart “yearns to come home” (15.66), and Menelaus prays that Zeus may bring his homecoming to pass (15.111–112); Telemachus hopes to find Odysseus at home (15.157), and Helen reads the omen, “so Odysseus … will come home and take vengeance” (15.176–177). In Eumaeus' story the Phoenician offers the nurse a homecoming, and she asks for an oath “to bring me home unharmed” (15.431–436).
# νεῖσθαι (15.88), only here in Homer.
grc: [νεῖσθαι]
en: [go back, their going]
---

**The son's return.** The book opens with the goddess at Sparta, “to remind him of his homecoming and to urge him to return” (νόστου ὑπομνήσουσα καὶ ὀτρυνέουσα νέεσθαι, 15.3; see [[athena]] and [[the-journey-of-telemachus]]). At dawn Telemachus asks to be sent away, “for already my heart yearns to come home” (15.66; see [[pompe]] and [[thymos]]), and again, “I wish to go back now to my own; for when I left I did not leave behind / anyone to watch over my possessions” (15.88–89; see [[possessions]]). Menelaus will not keep him “when you are eager for your homecoming” (15.69; see [[xenia]]), and gives him the bowl with a prayer: “as for your homecoming, as you have it in mind, / may Zeus bring it to pass for you, the loud-thundering husband of Hera” (15.111–112; see [[zeus]]). The bowl was itself a gift of Menelaus' own return, given by the king of the Sidonians “as I came there on my way home” (15.118–119 = 4.618–619; see [[phaedimus]], [[mixing-bowl]] and [[the-wanderings-of-menelaus]]).

**The father's return.** Every homecoming in the book points to the one that is already made. Telemachus wishes that, “having come home to Ithaca and finding Odysseus in the house”, he might tell him of Menelaus' kindness (15.156–158; see [[philotes]]); Helen reads the eagle and the goose, “so Odysseus, after much suffering and much wandering, / will come home and take vengeance; or else he is already / at home, and is planting evil for all the suitors” (οἴκαδε νοστήσει καὶ τίσεται, 15.176–178; see [[the-omen-of-the-eagle-and-the-goose]] and [[poine]]). The second guess is the truth: Odysseus is already on Ithaca, at supper in the swineherd's hut (15.301–302).

**A homecoming offered.** In Eumaeus' story the word is the bait. The Phoenician who has lain with the nurse asks her, “Would you then come home again with us, / so that you may see the high-roofed house of your father and mother / and themselves?” (15.431–433; see [[the-phoenician-nurse]]), and she makes the sailors swear “to bring me home unharmed” (15.436; see [[oath]]); when the ship is laden “for their going” (15.457), she takes the child of the house with her as the price of her passage (15.449–453, 15.465), and dies at sea (15.477–481; see [[the-kidnapping-of-eumaeus]] and [[artemis]]). The homecoming of the nurse is the beginning of the swineherd's exile: “So it was that I came to see this land with my eyes” (15.484).
