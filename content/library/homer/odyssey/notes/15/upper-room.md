---
summary: Penelope, Telemachus tells Theoclymenus, keeps away from the suitors in the upper room, weaving at her loom (15.516–517).
---

“for she does not often show herself to the suitors in the house, / but away from them she weaves at her loom in the upper room” (15.516–517; see [[penelope]], [[loom]] and [[the-suitors]]). It is the reason Telemachus gives the seer for not taking him home: he himself will be away, and his mother will not see a guest (15.513–517; see [[theoclymenus]] and [[xenia]]). The upper room is her refuge from the first book, where she goes up with her women to weep for Odysseus until Athena casts sleep on her eyelids (1.362–364; see the Book 1 note).
