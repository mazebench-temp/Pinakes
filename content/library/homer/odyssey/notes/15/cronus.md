# The entry's Κρονίων* claims Κρονίων (15.477).
---

**The son of Cronus.** The Phoenicians sail six days with the nurse and the boy, “but when Zeus, son of Cronus, added the seventh day, / then Artemis of the showering arrows struck the woman” (15.477–478; see [[zeus]], [[son-of-cronus]], [[artemis]], [[the-phoenician-nurse]] and [[the-kidnapping-of-eumaeus]]). The first verse is that of the seventh day on Thrinacia, when the companions had feasted six days on the cattle of the Sun (12.399; see the Book 12 note), and the patronymic fills the end of it, Ζεὺς … Κρονίων. One manuscript brings the son of Cronus into the book a second time: at Telemachus' answer to the seer, “Would that this word, stranger, might be fulfilled” (15.536), G reads τελέσειε Κρονίων, “might the son of Cronus fulfill”, for τετελεσμένον εἴη (D. B. Monro, *Homer's Odyssey, Books XIII–XXIV*, 1901, critical note on 15.536), the ending of the verse when the cowherd Philoetius says it to the beggar, “Would that the son of Cronus might fulfill this word, stranger” (20.236, a plain rendering), and of the half-verse in which Medon told Penelope of the suitors' plot to kill Telemachus on his way home, “which may the son of Cronus not bring to pass” (4.699; see [[it-will-be-fulfilled]] and [[ambush]]).
