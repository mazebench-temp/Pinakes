---
summary: Before Menelaus' last meal for his guests a handmaid pours water for the hands from a golden pitcher over a silver basin (15.135–136).
---

“And a handmaid brought water for the hands in a pitcher and poured it, / a fine golden one, over a silver basin, / for them to wash” (15.135–137; see [[water-for-the-hands]], [[hand-washing]], [[handmaids]] and [[gold-and-silver]]). The same verses brought the pitcher and the basin to Athena in Odysseus' hall, to Telemachus and Peisistratus on their first evening at Sparta, and to Odysseus in the houses of Alcinous and of Circe (1.136–137, 4.52–53, 7.172–173, 10.368–369), and they will bring them to Telemachus and [[theoclymenus|Theoclymenus]] in the house of Odysseus (17.91–92). The meal of leave-taking is served as the meal of welcome was (15.135–139 = 4.52–56; see [[feast-type-scene]] and [[table]]).
