summary: Penelope's marriage hangs over the book. Her father and brothers bid her marry Eurymachus, and a woman who remarries, Athena warns, forgets her wedded husband (15.16–23); Telemachus, sending a stranger to Eurymachus, calls him most eager to marry his mother, and leaves it to Zeus whether an evil day will come before the marriage (15.519–524). Telemachus is to wait until the gods show him a glorious wife, and Helen gives him a robe for his bride (15.26, 15.126–128). Melampus married at Argos (15.241), Ctimene was given in marriage to Same (15.367), and Laertes mourns his wedded wife (15.356).

**Penelope's marriage.** Athena's first reason for sending Telemachus home is his mother's marriage: her father and brothers are bidding her “marry Eurymachus”, and “she wishes to increase the house of the man who marries her”, forgetting “her dear wedded husband” once he is dead (15.16–23; see [[a-womans-heart]], [[penelope]] and [[bride-price]]). At the end of the book Telemachus, unable to receive [[theoclymenus|Theoclymenus]] in a house where his mother keeps to her loom away from the suitors, sends him to Eurymachus, “for he is by far the best man, and most of all he is eager / to marry my mother and to hold the prize of honor of Odysseus” (15.513–522; see [[eurymachus]] and [[geras]]). The marriage would carry the king's prize with it. He leaves the end to Zeus, “whether before the marriage he will bring about an evil day for them”, and the falcon flies at once (15.523–525; see [[evil-day]] and [[the-omen-of-the-falcon]]). Monro would read τελευτήσῃ, since εἴ κε with the future in such a clause is found only here (D. B. Monro, *Homer's Odyssey, Books XIII–XXIV*, 1901, on 15.524).

**Telemachus' bride.** Athena bids him entrust the house to the best of the slave women “until the gods show you a glorious wife” (15.24–26), and Helen gives him the robe she made “for the time of your lovely marriage, / for your wife to wear”, to lie meanwhile with his mother (15.125–128; see [[robe]] and [[helen]]). His own marriage is spoken of only as a gift laid up for it.

**Other marriages.** Melampus, exiled from Pylos, came to Argos, “and there he married a wife and built a high-roofed house” (15.241; see [[melampus]]); for his brother he had won the daughter of Neleus, by driving the cattle from Phylace to Pylos (15.235–238; see [[melampus-and-the-cattle-of-iphiclus]] and [[pero]]). Laertes mourns for “his wise-hearted wedded wife” (15.356; see [[anticleia]]), and his daughter Ctimene “they gave … in marriage to Same, and got countless gifts” (15.367): Σάμηνδʼ ἔδοσαν is a pregnant construction, given in marriage and sent to Same (Monro on 15.367; see the translation's note on 15.367, and [[ctimene]] and [[samos]]).
