---
summary: At dawn Menelaus bids Eteoneus kindle a fire and roast meat for his guests' last meal (15.97–98); and the beggar boasts that no man could rival him in service, in heaping up a fire and splitting dry wood (15.321–322).
---

**Eteoneus' fire.** “Menelaus, good at the war cry, bade him kindle a fire / and roast meat; and he heard and did not disobey” (15.97–98; see [[eteoneus]] and [[feast-type-scene]]). Monro would perhaps read ὀπτῆσαι κρεάων, “wherewith to roast some of the flesh” (D. B. Monro, *Homer's Odyssey, Books XIII–XXIV*, 1901, on 15.98).

**The beggar's service.** Testing the swineherd, Odysseus offers to serve the suitors, by the favor of Hermes, who grants grace and glory to the works of all men: “in service no other mortal could rival me, / in heaping up a fire well and splitting dry wood, / in carving and roasting meat and pouring wine” (15.319–323; see [[hermes]], [[cupbearing]] and [[peira]]). The infinitives depend on ἐρίσσειε and spell out δρηστοσύνῃ: “no one would contend with me in respect of service, in piling up a fire”, and so on (Monro on 15.322; see the translation's row δρηστοσύνη). δανά, “dry”, is the reading of some manuscripts and of Eustathius; others have πολλά, “many” (Monro's critical note on 15.322; see the translation's note on 15.322).
