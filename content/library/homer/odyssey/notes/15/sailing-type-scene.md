summary: The voyage home in the steps of the scene: the crew embark at Pylos (15.217–221), and the ship is loosed with the seer on board (15.282–286); the mast, the sails and the wind, in the verses of the voyage out (15.287–294); the run by night past Elis toward the islands (15.295–300); the landing at dawn (15.495–500); and Peiraeus taking the ship on to the city (15.547–553). In Eumaeus' story the Phoenicians sail six days and a seventh (15.473–482).

**Embarking.** Telemachus bids the crew “Put the arms in order” and go aboard (15.217–219). τὰ τεύχεα are “the arms”; the word “does not include the rigging (ὅπλα), which is taken in hand later” (D. B. Monro, *Homer's Odyssey, Books XIII–XXIV*, 1901, on 15.218, comparing 16.474). Before they sail he sacrifices at the stern and takes the suppliant on board (15.222–281; see [[theoclymenus]]). He sits in the stern, where Athena sat on the voyage out, and seats the stranger beside him, and the crew loose the stern-cables (15.285–286; 2.416–418; see [[stern]] and [[stern-cables]]).

**Under sail.** The verses of the launch at Ithaca (2.420–426) in another order: first the mast in its box, the forestays and the sails, then the wind that Athena sends, “blowing hard and wild through the upper air,” in the Iliad's phrase for the west wind on the standing grain (15.287–294; *Iliad* 2.148; see [[mast]], [[mast-box]], [[forestays]], [[sails]] and [[white]]). The run goes on through the night of the hut, past [[cruni|Cruni]] and [[chalcis-in-elis|Chalcis]], Pheae and Elis, toward the islands where the suitors wait (15.295–300; see the translation's note on 15.295–300, and [[sunset-lines]], [[the-journey-of-telemachus]] and [[the-pointed-islands]]).

**The landing.** At dawn the companions loose the sails and take down the mast, bring the ship to the anchorage with oars, cast out the mooring-stones, make fast the stern-cables and step out into the surf, in the verses of the landing at Chryse (15.495–499; *Iliad* 1.433–437; see [[mooring-stones]]). ἐπὶ χέρσου means that they had reached land, not that they had landed, since the mast is taken down before the ship is run aground; and ῥηγμίς is “the ‘broken water’ or ‘surf’ … not the beach on which it breaks” (Monro on 15.495, 15.499). Aristarchus read προέρεσσαν, “rowed,” as in the Iliad, where the text has προέρυσσαν (see the translation's note on 15.495–497).

**The ship sails on.** [[peiraeus-son-of-clytius|Peiraeus]] goes aboard and takes the command (15.547; Monro on 15.547), and the crew embark and loose the stern-cables in the verses of Odysseus' ships (15.548–549 = 9.178–179; see [[hysteron-proteron]]); they push off, ἀνώσαντες, where ἀνά is “out to sea,” not the direction of the voyage, and sail for the city (15.552–553; Monro on 15.553).

**Eumaeus' voyage.** The Phoenicians embark, “having taken us two on board,” sail six days and nights with a fair wind from Zeus, and on the seventh the nurse dies; “the wind and the water carried them and drove them to Ithaca” (15.473–482; see [[watery-ways]], [[son-of-cronus]], [[the-kidnapping-of-eumaeus]] and [[the-phoenician-nurse]]).
