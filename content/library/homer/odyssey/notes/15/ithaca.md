---
summary: Ithaca is the goal of Telemachus' voyage: Athena warns him of the ambush “in the strait between Ithaca and rocky Samos” and bids him land at “the first shore of Ithaca” and go to the swineherd (15.29, 15.36–38), and at dawn his ship comes in to that shore (15.495–500). Ithaca is also where the wind and the water brought the child Eumaeus to be sold to Laertes (15.482–484), and where, in Theoclymenus' reading of the omen, there is “no other race more kingly” than the house of Odysseus (15.533–534).
---

**The first shore of Ithaca.** Athena's warning at Sparta places the ambush “in the strait between Ithaca and rocky Samos” (29; see [[samos]], [[asteris]] and [[ambush]]) and gives the landing: “But when you reach the first shore of Ithaca, / send the ship and all your companions on to the city, / but you yourself go first of all to the swineherd” (36–38; see [[eumaeus]] and [[athena]]). πρώτην ἀκτήν is the first part of the island's shore that he reaches, short of the city to which the ship is to go on. At dawn on the 38th day the companions loose the sails by the shore, row the ship in to the anchorage and make their meal, and Telemachus sends the ship on to the city and goes to the farmstead on foot (495–557; see [[harbor]] and [[shore]]); D. B. Monro calls it “the landing of Telemachus on the neighbouring coast” (*Homer's Odyssey, Books XIII–XXIV*, 1901, on 15.495). Strabo, following the course of the ship, thought that it landed on the far side of the island from the strait the suitors guarded (*Geography* 8.3.26; see [[the-pointed-islands]]).

**From Ithaca.** At Sparta Telemachus wishes he might come home to Ithaca and find his father there: “would that, / having come home to Ithaca and finding Odysseus in the house, / I might tell him so” (156–158; see the translation's note on 15.156–158). To the seer at Pylos he names his country first: “My line is from Ithaca, and my father is Odysseus, / if that was ever so” (267–268; see [[such-was-i-if-ever]] and [[theoclymenus]]). At the landing Theoclymenus asks, “To whose house shall I come / of the men who rule over craggy Ithaca?” (509–510; see [[craggy]]), the verse in which Telemachus had named the suitors of the island in the first book (1.247; see [[the-suitors]]).

**Brought to Ithaca.** Eumaeus ends his story with his arrival on the island: “and the wind and the water carried them and drove them to Ithaca, / where Laertes bought me with his own possessions. / So it was that I came to see this land with my eyes” (482–484; see [[laertes]] and [[the-kidnapping-of-eumaeus]]). ἐπέλασσε φέρων ἄνεμός τε καὶ ὕδωρ, “the wind and the water carried and drove”, ends the verses in which Menelaus' five ships were brought to Egypt and Odysseus to Scheria (3.300, 7.277).

**The land of Ithaca.** [[theoclymenus|Theoclymenus]] reads the falcon for Telemachus: “There is no other race more kingly than yours / in the land of Ithaca, but you are always powerful” (533–534; see [[the-omen-of-the-falcon]] and [[ithacans]]). ἐν δήμῳ Ἰθάκης, “in the land of Ithaca”, is the phrase of Helen's description of Odysseus in the Iliad, “raised in the land of Ithaca, craggy though it is” (*Iliad* 3.201), and of the harbor of Phorcys (13.97). καρτεροί is “powerful”, a word generally used of strength of body, though κρατέω is common in the sense of bearing rule (Monro on 15.534; see the translation's key-word row).
