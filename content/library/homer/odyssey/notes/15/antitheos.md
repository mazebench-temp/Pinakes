---
summary: Telemachus asks leave to go home, “lest while I seek my godlike father I perish myself” (15.90); and Melampus, freed, “took vengeance for the shameful deed / on godlike Neleus” (15.236–237).
---

πατέρʼ ἀντίθεον is the household's word for its absent master, as in Penelope's “the offspring of godlike Odysseus” (4.741) and Eumaeus' “my godlike master” (14.40). Neleus is godlike in the verse that tells his punishment: he had held Melampus' goods by force for a full year, and the seer, escaping his doom at Phylace, drove the cattle to Pylos and brought Neleus' daughter home as a wife for his brother (15.228–238; see [[neleus]], [[melampus]] and [[melampus-and-the-cattle-of-iphiclus]]). The honorific passes no judgment on the man, any more than ἀμύμων on Aegisthus (1.29; see the general article and [[blameless]]).
