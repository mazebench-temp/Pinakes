summary: Odysseus asks the swineherd: “was the wide-streeted city of men sacked, / in which your father and lady mother lived” (15.384–385).
grc: [εὐρυάγυια]
---

The Iliad's epithet of the city that is taken, above all Troy (see the general article), in the first of the stranger's two guesses; the second, a boy caught with the flocks and sold over the sea, is told under [[hostile-men]]. In the Iliad Achilles caught two of Priam's sons as they herded sheep on Ida and released them for a ransom, and sold Lycaon into Lemnos, “taking him there in his ships, and the son of Jason paid the price” (*Iliad* 11.104–106, 21.40–41; compare 15.387–388; see [[slavery-and-freedom]] and [[the-kidnapping-of-eumaeus]]).
