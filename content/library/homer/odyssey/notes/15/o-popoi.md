---
summary: Odysseus hears that the swineherd was reared beside Ctimene: “Ah, so you were still small, swineherd Eumaeus, / when you were driven so far from your native land and your parents” (15.381–382).
---

The cry with ἄρα of a thing newly understood, as in Odysseus' cry on waking on Ithaca (13.209; see the note on Book 13), here of pity: the stranger infers the swineherd's childhood from his account of Anticleia, and asks for his story (15.363–388; see [[eumaeus]] and [[the-kidnapping-of-eumaeus]]). Εὔμαιε συβῶτα, the vocative with which the poet addresses the swineherd, is here in the stranger's own mouth (see [[apostrophe]]).
