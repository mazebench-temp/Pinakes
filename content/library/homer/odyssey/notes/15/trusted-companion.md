---
summary: “He spoke, and called to Peiraeus, his trusted companion: / ‘Peiraeus, son of Clytius, you in other things most of all / obey me, of my companions who followed me to Pylos’” (15.539–541).
---

πιστὸν ἑταῖρον at the verse-end after a verb of address, as when Automedon calls Alcimedon to his side (*Iliad* 17.500): the Iliad's formula of the friend in battle, for the only time in the Odyssey. Telemachus' next words gloss it, since πιστός belongs to the family of πείθω (see the general article): Peiraeus is the companion who “most of all” obeys, πείθῃ (15.540–541), and he is asked to keep the stranger “until I come” (15.542–543). He does so, and is asked to keep Menelaus' gifts as well (17.52–56, 17.71–83; see [[peiraeus-son-of-clytius]], [[theoclymenus]], [[peitho]] and [[hetairos]]).
