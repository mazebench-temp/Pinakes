summary: Peisistratus: “But wait until he brings gifts and sets them in the chariot, / the hero, the son of Atreus, Menelaus famed for his spear, / and sends us off, speaking comfortingly with gentle words” (15.51–53).
grc: ["μύθοις ἀγανοῖσι"]
---

μύθοις ἀγανοῖσι, with μῦθοι for the Iliad's ἔπεα, is used only here. Monro sees these promised words in Menelaus' offer to take Telemachus through Hellas and Argos: granting that the guest was not likely to accept, ancient manners may have required some such speech from the host (D. B. Monro, *Homer's Odyssey, Books XIII–XXIV*, 1901, on 15.80–85; see [[guest-gifts]], [[a-guest-remembers-his-host]] and [[menelaus]]).
