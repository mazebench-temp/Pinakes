---
summary: Athena finds the young men “sleeping in the porch of glorious Menelaus” (15.5), and at the meal “the son of glorious Menelaus poured the wine” (15.141); and Eumaeus tells of Odysseus' mother: “And she died of grief for her glorious son” (15.358).
---

Μενελάου κυδαλίμοιο, the verse-end of the arrival at Sparta (4.2, 4.46; see the Book 4 note), frames the departure too; the son who pours the wine is Megapenthes (see [[megapenthes]] and [[cupbearing]]). At 15.358 the glorious son is Odysseus, in Eumaeus' words to the stranger who is that son (15.355–360; see [[anticleia]] and [[odysseus]]).
