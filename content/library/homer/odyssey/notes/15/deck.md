---
summary: Telemachus lays Theoclymenus' bronze spear on the deck when the seer comes on board at Pylos (15.282–283), and on the coast of Ithaca he takes a strong spear from the deck before he goes ashore (15.551–552).
---

“So he spoke, and took the bronze spear from him, / and laid it on the deck of the curving ship” (15.282–283; see [[theoclymenus]], [[spear]] and [[curving]]): the host takes his guest's spear, and the deck of the ship serves for the spear-rack of a house (see [[spear-rack]]). On Ithaca, before he goes to the swineherd, Telemachus “took a strong spear, tipped with sharp bronze, / from the deck of the ship” (15.551–552). It is not the spear of Theoclymenus, as Ameis supposed: the spear that Telemachus himself usually carried (2.10) would also lie on the deck (D. B. Monro, *Homer's Odyssey, Books XIII–XXIV*, 1901, on 15.551). The passengers' place was in the stern, where the deck was raised (15.285; see [[stern]] and the entry).
