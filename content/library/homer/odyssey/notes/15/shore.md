summary: Athena bids Telemachus land at “the first shore of Ithaca” (15.36); at Pylos Peisistratus drives him “to the swift ship and the shore of the sea” (15.205); and at dawn his companions step out on Ithaca “where the sea breaks upon the shore” (15.499).
# πρώτην ἀκτὴν, "the first shore" (15.36), as a phrase: ἀκτή alone is also meal, "the meal of barley" (ἀλφίτου
# ἀκτῇ, 14.429; 2.355).
grc: ["πρώτην ἀκτὴν"]
---

**The first shore of Ithaca.** “But when you reach the first shore of Ithaca, / send the ship and all your companions on to the city, / but you yourself go first of all to the swineherd” (36–38; see [[athena]], [[ithaca]] and [[eumaeus]]). ἀκτή is the shore, as where Calypso found Odysseus weeping on the beach of her island (5.151); the same word is meal in “the meal of barley”, ἀλφίτου ἀκτή (2.355, 14.429).

**The shore of the sea.** At Pylos Peisistratus does as Telemachus asks: “he turned the horses to the swift ship and the shore of the sea” (205; see [[pylos]] and [[peisistratus]]). ἐπὶ νῆα θοὴν καὶ θῖνα θαλάσσης is the verse-end of many a going down to a ship in the Odyssey, from the suitors' setting out in Book 4 to the herald who leads Odysseus to the Phaeacians' ship (4.779, 10.154, 10.402, 10.407, 10.569, 12.367, 13.65; see the Book 13 note).

**Where the sea breaks upon the shore.** At dawn on Ithaca “And by the shore / the companions of Telemachus loosed the sails” (495–496); ἐπὶ χέρσου means that they had now reached land, not that they had landed (D. B. Monro, *Homer's Odyssey, Books XIII–XXIV*, 1901, on 15.495; see [[harbor]]). Then “they themselves stepped out where the sea breaks upon the shore” (499 = *Iliad* 1.437). ῥηγμίς, Monro holds, “seems always to mean the ‘broken water’ or ‘surf’ … not the beach on which it breaks” (Monro on 15.499, comparing 4.430).
