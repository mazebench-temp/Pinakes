summary: Death feared and death told: the dead husband a remarried wife forgets (15.23); the seer who flees “death and black doom” (15.275) and Telemachus at sea, “pondering whether he would escape death or be caught” (15.300); Amphiaraus, after whose death Polypheides was the best of seers (15.253); and Anticleia, who died of grief “by a wretched death—may no one die so” (15.359).
# τεθνηκότος (15.23), θάνεν (15.253), τεθνᾶσι (15.350) and θάνοι (15.359), forms found only here in the Odyssey.
grc: [τεθνηκότος, θάνεν, τεθνᾶσι, θάνοι]
en: [dead, die]
---

**Once he is dead.** Athena warns Telemachus of a woman's heart: she favors the man who marries her, “and her former children and her dear wedded husband / she no longer remembers, once he is dead, nor asks about them” (τεθνηκότος, 15.22–23; see [[a-womans-heart]] and [[penelope]]). The text prints τεθνηκότος, the only such form in the Homeric texts here; Monro prints τεθνηότος (see the translation's note on 15.23).

**Death escaped.** In the seer's line Melampus “escaped his doom” (15.235; see [[ker-doom]] and [[melampus]]), and the seer himself is in flight from the kin of the man he killed: “Avoiding death and black doom at their hands, / I am an exile” (τῶν ὑπαλευάμενος θάνατον καὶ κῆρα μέλαιναν, 15.275–276; see [[black-doom]] and [[exile-and-refuge]]), where τῶν is governed by the ὑπό of ὑπαλευάμενος, “under stress of” (D. B. Monro, *Homer's Odyssey, Books XIII–XXIV*, 1901, on 15.275). Telemachus, who has taken him aboard, sails past the ambush by night, “pondering whether he would escape death or be caught” (15.300; see [[ambush]]). Of the seer's kin one did not escape: [[polypheides|Polypheides]] became the best of seers “after Amphiaraus died” (15.253), who “perished at Thebes, for the sake of gifts to a woman” (15.247; see [[amphiaraus]]).

**A wretched death.** In the hut Odysseus asks whether his parents “still live somewhere under the rays of the sun, / or are already dead and in the house of Hades” (15.349–350; see [[hades]]), and Eumaeus tells him of his mother: “And she died of grief for her glorious son, / by a wretched death—may no one die so who / lives here as my friend and does me kindness” (λευγαλέῳ θανάτῳ, ὡς μὴ θάνοι, 15.358–360; see [[anticleia]] and [[achos]]). The son had heard from her shade how she died (11.197–203).
