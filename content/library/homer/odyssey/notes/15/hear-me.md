---
summary: Helen begins her reading of the omen, “Hear me, and I will prophesy, as the immortals / put it in my heart” (15.172–173); and the stranger opens his test of the swineherd, “Hear me now, Eumaeus, and all you other companions” (15.307).
grc: ["κέκλυθι νῦν"]
---

κλῦτέ μευ is the address to a company, as Nausicaa's to her handmaids (6.239); αὐτάρ marks “the slight contrast between the listeners (κλῦτέ μευ) and the speaker: ‘do you hear, and I &c.’” (D. B. Monro, *Homer's Odyssey, Books XIII–XXIV*, 1901, on 15.172). The rest is Athena's prophecy as Mentes, “But now I will prophesy to you, as the immortals / put it in my heart” (1.200–201; see [[it-will-be-fulfilled]] and [[the-omen-of-the-eagle-and-the-goose]]). 15.307 is the verse with which the stranger began the tale of the cloak the night before (14.462), the singular κέκλυθι to Eumaeus with the others added (see [[peira]] and [[the-tale-of-the-cloak]]).
