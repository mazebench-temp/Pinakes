**Anticleia's gift.** “me she clothed in a cloak and a tunic, clothes / very fine, and gave me sandals for my feet, / and sent me to the farm” (15.368–370; see [[anticleia]], [[cloak]] and [[eumaeus]]). ὑποδήματα, “things bound under”, is another word than the πέδιλα of the dressing verse; it comes again when Eurymachus offers the beggar work on a farm, with clothes and sandals for his feet (18.361).

**Telemachus goes ashore.** “And Telemachus bound under his feet his fine sandals, / and he took a strong spear, tipped with sharp bronze, / from the deck of the ship” (15.550–552; see [[telemachus]], [[spear]], [[deck]] and [[dressing-scene]]). The first half-verse, ὑπὸ ποσσὶν ἐδήσατο καλὰ πέδιλα, is the one in which Athena bound on her golden sandals when she set out for Ithaca in Book 1, and Hermes his when he went to Calypso (1.96, 5.44; *Iliad* 24.340; see the entry), and the second verse is the one in which Athena took up her spear (1.99): the verses that brought the goddess to Telemachus' house send him to the swineherd's farmstead. He will bind on his sandals again at dawn to go to the city (17.2).
