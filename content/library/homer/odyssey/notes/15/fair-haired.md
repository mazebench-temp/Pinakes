---
summary: “Fair-haired Menelaus” three times at the leave-taking: he speaks to Telemachus over the gifts (15.110), “and fair-haired Menelaus led them to the house” (15.133), and “fair-haired Menelaus, son of Atreus, went after them” with the wine of the parting libation (15.147).
---

ξανθὸς Μενέλαος, the verse-end of his speeches in Book 4 (see the Book 4 note), here also of his movements. At 15.133 the hair is named, κάρη ξανθός, “fair of head,” a phrase used only here in Homer (see [[menelaus]], [[libation]] and [[guest-gifts]]).
