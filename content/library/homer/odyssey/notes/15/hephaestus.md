summary: Hephaestus is named once, as the maker of the silver mixing bowl with rims of gold that Menelaus gives Telemachus at parting, “the work of Hephaestus”, the gift of Phaedimus, king of the Sidonians (15.115–119 = 4.615–619).
# The entry's Ἥφαιστ* claims Ἡφαίστοιο (15.117).
---

**The work of Hephaestus.** In Book 4 Menelaus offered Telemachus, in place of the horses, the finest and most precious of his treasures, a mixing bowl “the work of Hephaestus” (4.613–619; see the Book 4 note). At parting he gives it in the same verses: “I will give you a well-wrought mixing bowl; of silver / it is all, and its rims are finished with gold, / the work of Hephaestus. The hero Phaedimus gave it to me, / the king of the Sidonians” (15.115–118; see [[mixing-bowl]], [[phaedimus]], [[sidonians]] and [[guest-gifts]]), and “the shining mixing bowl / strong Megapenthes brought and set before him” (15.121–122; see [[megapenthes]]). Two manuscripts omit the seven verses of the promise here, and Monro brackets them (D. B. Monro, *Homer's Odyssey, Books XIII–XXIV*, 1901, critical note on 15.113–119; see the translation's note on 15.113–119). Gold laid on silver is the craft that Hephaestus teaches men with Athena: the craftsman who “pours gold around silver” is “a skilled man whom Hephaestus and Pallas Athena have taught / every kind of craft” (6.232–234; see [[athena]]). Strabo, quoting the verses, took “the work of Hephaestus” for praise of the Sidonians' craft (Strabo 1.2.33; see [[mixing-bowl]]).
