---
summary: The book opens with the goddess on her way: “But Pallas Athena went to Lacedaemon with its wide dancing floors, / to the shining son of great-hearted Odysseus” (15.1–2).
---

The double name takes up the thread where Book 13 left her, setting out for Lacedaemon after the son of Odysseus (13.439–440): the scene changes to Sparta, “whither we follow Athene” (D. B. Monro, *Homer's Odyssey, Books XIII–XXIV*, 1901, on 15.1). In the rest of the book she is gray-eyed Athena, standing by the wakeful Telemachus and sending the wind for his voyage (15.9, 15.292; see [[gray-eyed]], [[with-wide-dancing-floors]] and [[athena]]).
