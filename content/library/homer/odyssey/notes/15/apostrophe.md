---
summary: The poet speaks to the swineherd once in the book, as he answers the stranger's plan to go begging in the city: “Then, deeply troubled, you said to him, swineherd Eumaeus” (15.325).
---

The sixth of the Odyssey's addresses to Eumaeus, and the only one with μέγʼ ὀχθήσας in place of ἀπαμειβόμενος (see [[deeply-troubled]] and the note on Book 14). His other speeches in the book are brought in by his office, in the third person: “Then the swineherd, leader of men, spoke to him in turn” (15.351, 15.389; see [[answering-him]] and [[eumaeus]]). The stranger uses the same vocative, Εὔμαιε συβῶτα, in his own speech (15.381; see [[o-popoi]]).
