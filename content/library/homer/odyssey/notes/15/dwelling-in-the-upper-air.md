---
summary: Telemachus on Eurymachus: “But Zeus the Olympian, who dwells in the upper air, knows this, / whether before the marriage he will bring about an evil day for them” (15.523–524).
---

The third and last use of the phrase in Homer, after Agamemnon's prayer and his threat that Zeus will shake the aegis over the oath-breakers (*Iliad* 2.412, 4.166). Here the answer comes at once: as Telemachus speaks, a bird flies by on the right, “a falcon, the swift messenger of Apollo” (15.525–526; see [[the-omen-of-the-falcon]], [[olympian]], [[evil-day]] and [[zeus]]).
