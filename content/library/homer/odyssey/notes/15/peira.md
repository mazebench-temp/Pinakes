---
summary: On the second night in the hut Odysseus speaks of going to beg in the city, “testing the swineherd, / whether he would still welcome him with care and bid him stay / there at the farmstead, or would urge him on to the city” (15.304–306); Eumaeus bids him stay (15.335).
---

The verse that opened the test of the cloak on the night before opens a second test: “Odysseus spoke among them, testing the swineherd, / whether he would still welcome him with care and bid him stay / there at the farmstead, or would urge him on to the city” (συβώτεω πειρητίζων, 15.304–306; the first verse is 14.459; see the Book 14 note, [[eumaeus]] and [[xenia]]). This time the trial is of a host's patience. The guest offers to leave “so that I may not wear you and your companions out”, to beg in the city, to bring his news to Penelope and to serve the suitors, at whose work no mortal could rival him “by the favor of Hermes the guide” (15.307–324; see [[hermes]], [[penelope]] and [[the-suitors]]). Eumaeus, “deeply troubled”, answers that he would perish among men “whose arrogance and violence reach the iron sky”, and keeps him: “But stay; for no one is vexed by your presence”, until Telemachus comes, who will clothe him and “send you wherever your heart and spirit bid you” (15.325–339; see [[hybris]], [[pompe]] and [[telemachus]]), the promise he had made at the end of the first test (14.515–517). The swineherd passes again, and the guest blesses him for having given him “rest from wandering and dread misery” (15.341–342; see [[gaster]]). Telemachus will warn his father against going about “merely testing each man” on the farms while the suitors devour his goods (16.313, a plain rendering), and the last trial is of Laertes (24.216, 24.221).
