---
summary: Peisistratus, having left Telemachus at the ship, drives “back to the city of the Pylians” (15.216); and Melampus, the seer's ancestor, was once “a rich man among the Pylians” (15.227).
---

**The city of the Pylians.** When he has put Telemachus and the gifts aboard, Peisistratus “drove the fine-maned horses / back to the city of the Pylians, and quickly reached the house” (215–216; see [[peisistratus]], [[pylos]] and [[fine-maned]]), knowing that the old man would have come to the ship himself to fetch his guest, and will be very angry (211–214; see [[nestor]]). Peisistratus is the last of Nestor's people that the poem shows.

**Among the Pylians.** Melampus “once lived in Pylos, mother of flocks, / a rich man among the Pylians, living in a house far above the rest” (226–227; see [[melampus]]). Πυλίοισι is a locatival dative, “among the Pylians”, to be taken with μέγʼ ἔξοχα δώματα ναίων, as in Polyphemus, “whose power is the greatest / among all the Cyclopes” (1.70–71), the goats “which stand out by far among all the herds” (21.266, a plain rendering), and the Iliad's ox that “in a herd stands out far above all the rest” (*Iliad* 2.480; D. B. Monro, *Homer's Odyssey, Books XIII–XXIV*, 1901, on 15.227). The Pylians are the people from whom he fled, with their king Neleus (228–229; see [[neleus]] and [[melampus-and-the-cattle-of-iphiclus]]).
