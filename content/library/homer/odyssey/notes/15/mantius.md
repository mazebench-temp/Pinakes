The second of the “two mighty sons” of Melampus (15.242; see [[melampus]] and [[antiphates-son-of-melampus]]). After the elder line has run to Amphiaraus and his sons, the poet turns back with αὖ, “and”, to the younger: “And Mantius begot Polypheides and Cleitus” (15.249; see [[polypheides]] and [[cleitus-son-of-mantius]]). Of his sons, one was carried off by Dawn and the other “moved away to Hyperesia, angry with his father” (15.250–254; see [[hyperesia]]). The poem does not say why (see [[polypheides]]). The name of Mantius, with its stem μαντ-, “seer”, stands between the two seers of the house, his father and his son (15.225, 15.252–255; see [[divination]]).
