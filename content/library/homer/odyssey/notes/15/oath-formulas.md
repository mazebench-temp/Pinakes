---
summary: The Phoenicians swear to the nurse: “So she spoke, and they all swore as she bade them. / But when they had sworn and completed the oath” (15.437–438).
---

The couplet of the oaths of Eurycleia, Circe and the companions (2.377–378, 10.345–346, 12.303–304; again 18.58–59), with ἐπώμνυον, “swore,” where the others have ἀπώμνυον, “swore that they would not” (see [[oath]]). ἐπώμνυον is the reading of Aristarchus and the vulgate; G, F, X and other manuscripts give ἀπώμνυον here too (D. B. Monro, *Homer's Odyssey, Books XIII–XXIV*, 1901, critical note on 15.437; see [[the-kidnapping-of-eumaeus]]).
