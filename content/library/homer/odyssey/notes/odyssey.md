## The plan of Book 1

When Zeus agrees that Odysseus shall come home, Athena proposes two things (1.81–95; see [[the-council-of-the-gods]]). Hermes is to go to the island of Ogygia, “so that with all speed / he may tell the nymph of the lovely tresses our unerring counsel, / the homecoming of stout-hearted Odysseus, that he may return” (1.85–87); she herself will go to Ithaca, to rouse his son to call the Achaeans to an assembly and speak out to the suitors, and to send him to Sparta and Pylos to learn of his father's homecoming, “and so that noble fame may be his among men” (1.88–95).

The poem carries out her part first. She goes down to Ithaca at once in the likeness of Mentes (1.96–105; see [[athena-visits-telemachus]]); Telemachus calls the assembly at dawn in Book 2 (2.6–9) and comes to Pylos in Book 3 and to Lacedaemon in Book 4 (3.4–5, 4.1–2; see [[the-journey-of-telemachus]]). Hermes' errand waits until the start of Book 5, where Zeus sends him with the words of her proposal, νύμφῃ ἐυπλοκάμῳ … νημερτέα βουλήν, / νόστον Ὀδυσσῆος ταλασίφρονος, ὥς κε νέηται (5.30–31, as 1.86–87). The two stories meet in Eumaeus' hut, when Odysseus tells his son, “I am your father” (16.188; see [[the-return-of-odysseus]]).

## Book 2 and the last book

The assembly of Book 2 carries out the first part of Athena's plan, and the last book answers it with a second assembly of the Ithacans, after the killing of the suitors. Each is opened by an old father weeping for a lost son, in the same verse, “Shedding tears for him, he addressed them and spoke” (2.24 = 24.425): Aegyptius, whose son the Cyclops ate, and Eupeithes, whose son Antinous was the first of the suitors to die. In each Halitherses son of Mastor rises with the same address, “Hear me now, men of Ithaca, in what I say” (2.161 = 24.454), and in the second he reminds the people that they would not listen to him or to Mentor (24.455–457; see [[the-assembly-of-the-ithacans]] and [[halitherses]]). The likeness of Mentor, which Athena takes for the first time on the shore after the first assembly (2.267–268), is the form in which she ends the war that follows the second (24.502–503, 24.546–548; see [[mentor]]).

## The books

- **1–4.** The council of the gods, Athena's visit and Telemachus' first stand against the suitors (Book 1); the assembly of the Ithacans and Telemachus' departure (Book 2); Nestor at Pylos (Book 3); Menelaus and Helen at Lacedaemon, and the suitors' plan to ambush Telemachus on his way home (Book 4; 4.669–672).
- **5–8.** Hermes at Calypso's cave, the raft and the storm (Book 5); Odysseus among the Phaeacians, from Athena's visit to Nausicaa to the songs of Demodocus at Alcinous' feast (Books 6–8; 6.15–17, 7.14–17).
- **9–12.** Odysseus' tale to the Phaeacians, from his name (9.19) to the cattle of the Sun and his arrival, alone, on Calypso's island (12.447–453).
- **13–16.** His return to Ithaca asleep (13.187–190), Eumaeus' hut (14.1–4), Athena's journey to Sparta to bring Telemachus home (15.1–3), and the meeting of father and son (16.188).
- **17–20.** The beggar in his own house (17.336–337), and Eurycleia's washing of his feet (19.386–388).
- **21–24.** The contest of the bow (21.1–4), the killing of the suitors (22.1–2), Penelope's recognition and the bed (23.296), and the last book: Hermes leads down the suitors' souls (24.1–2), Odysseus goes out to Laertes' farm (24.205–206), and Athena, in the likeness of Mentor, makes the peace (24.546–548).

The poem ends as its first book began, with Athena in a mortal's likeness: Mentes of Taphos in Book 1, Mentor of Ithaca, whose form she first takes in Book 2, in the last lines (see [[athena-as-mentor-in-later-writers]]).
