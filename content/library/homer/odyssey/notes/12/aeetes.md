# Αἰήταο (12.70) is claimed by the entry.
---

Circe, his own sister (10.137), names him once more in her counsel to Odysseus: by the way of the Wanderers no ship of men has escaped but one, “the Argo, in the thoughts of all, sailing from Aeetes”, and Hera sent her past “since Jason was dear to her” (12.69–72; see [[circe]], [[the-argo]], [[the-wanderers]], [[jason]] and [[hera]]). παρʼ Αἰήταο πλέουσα places the ship on her homeward voyage from Colchis, W. W. Merry notes, though he takes the passage for a later addition (*Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 12.70; see the translation's note on 12.69–72). The poem names neither his land nor his daughter, nor the fleece that the Argo went to fetch (see the entry and [[medea]]).
