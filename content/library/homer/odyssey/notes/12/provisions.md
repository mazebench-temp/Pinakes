summary: Circe's handmaids bring bread, meat and wine to the shore of Aeaea (12.18–19). On Thrinacia, held by the South Wind for a month, the companions live on the ship's bread and wine, and when all the provisions are spent they hunt fish and birds with curved hooks, until Eurylochus persuades them to kill the cattle (12.320–343).
# The provisions, "ἤια πάντα" (12.329; again 4.363, the provisions of Menelaus on Pharos), as a phrase: ἤια alone is
# also ἤια "I went" (4.427 and later). It applies in the Odyssey only.

**Circe's gift.** Circe's handmaids bring “bread and meat in plenty and sparkling red wine” to the shore (12.19; see [[handmaids]] and [[wine]]), and on Thrinacia Odysseus bids the companions eat “the food that immortal Circe gave” and keep from the cattle: “Friends, there is food and drink in the swift ship” (12.302, 12.320; see [[circe]] and [[oath]]).

**Spent.** “And for a whole month the South Wind blew without ceasing” (12.325; see [[notus]]), and the ship cannot leave. “And as long as they had bread and red wine, / they kept away from the cattle, longing for life. / But when all the provisions in the ship were spent, / and they were roaming about in quest of game, as they had to, / fish and birds, whatever came to their hands, / with curved hooks, and hunger wore at their bellies” (12.327–332; see [[fish]] and [[birds]]), Odysseus goes up the island to pray, and Eurylochus persuades the others that “to die of hunger and meet one's fate is the most pitiable” (12.333–342; see [[eurylochus]] and [[the-cattle-of-helios]]). καὶ δή is still within the clause of ὅτε, and the main clause begins with δὴ τότε at 333 (W. W. Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 12.330; see the translation's note on 12.328–330). The verse of the hooks is the verse of Menelaus' companions on Pharos, kept there twenty days by the gods without a wind, when “all our provisions would have been spent, and the strength of the men”, had not Eidothea pitied him (4.360–369; see [[menelaus]]). Bekker and other editors bracket it here as interpolated from that passage, though Athenaeus quotes it in this place; it is not customary, Merry remarks, though possible, as Eustathius says, to catch sea birds with a hook and line (Merry's critical note on 12.332; see the translation's note on 12.332).
