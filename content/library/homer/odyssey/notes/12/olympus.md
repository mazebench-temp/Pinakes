---
summary: On Thrinacia Odysseus goes up the island alone and prays “to all the gods who hold Olympus”, and they pour sweet sleep upon his eyelids (12.337–338).
---

θεοῖς οἳ Ὄλυμπον ἔχουσιν, “the gods who hold Olympus”, is a verse-end for the gods together in both poems (*Iliad* 5.890; 6.240, 8.331, and in later books 14.394, 18.180, 19.43; see [[homes-on-olympus]] and [[olympians]]). The prayer is answered with sleep, and while he sleeps the companions kill the cattle; waking, Odysseus cries out to “Father Zeus, and you other blessed gods who live forever” that they lulled him to his blind folly (12.338–372; see [[prayer]], [[thrinacia]] and [[the-cattle-of-helios]]).
