summary: The tale ends (12.447–453): nine days adrift on the mast and keel, the tenth night at Ogygia, Calypso's love and care, and the break-off, since he told all this the day before “to you and to your strong wife” (12.452).
---

The last verses of the book are the last of Odysseus' tale, begun at the start of Book 9 and broken only by the pause in the hall (11.333–384; see [[the-tale-to-alcinous]] and [[a-time-for-many-words]]). He brings it down to the moment where his answer to Arete had begun, and says so: “For I already told it to you yesterday in your house, / to you and to your strong wife” (12.451–452; see [[alcinous]], [[arete-queen-of-the-phaeacians]] and [[marriage]]). So the tale that began with the question of what to tell first and what last (9.14) ends with a refusal to tell anything twice. The Phaeacians' answer comes at the start of Book 13, in the verses of the pause, silent and held by a spell (13.1–2 = 11.333–334; see [[thelgo]] and [[silence]]).
