summary: Elpenor's funeral, as his soul asked it: logs are cut where the headland juts out farthest, the body is burned with its armor, and a tomb is heaped over it with a pillar and his oar (12.11–15).
# "νεκρός τʼ ἐκάη" (12.13), only here; ἐκάη alone is also the burning of the thigh-pieces (12.364).
grc: ["νεκρός τʼ ἐκάη"]
en: [was burned]
---

The funeral is told in five verses, in the order of the rite. “And quickly we cut logs, and where the headland jutted out farthest / we buried him, grieving and shedding big tears. / But when the body was burned, and the armor of the body, / we heaped up a tomb, and dragged a pillar up onto it, / and on the top of the tomb we fixed the well-fitted oar” (12.11–15; see [[elpenor]] and [[the-death-of-elpenor]]). θάπτομεν, “we buried him”, names the whole funeral, burning included, before the burning is told (see [[weep-for-a-day-and-bury]]). The armor goes into the fire with him, as he asked, “with my armor, whatever I have” (11.74), and as Achilles burned Eetion “with his intricate armor” (*Iliad* 6.418). Nothing is said of gathering the bones, as at the funerals of Patroclus and Hector (*Iliad* 23.238–257, 24.791–804): the tomb is heaped as soon as the burning is done (see [[tomb]] and [[honoring-the-dead]]). It is the only funeral among the companions; the six whom Scylla takes and the men drowned in the wreck have none (12.245–259, 12.417–419).
