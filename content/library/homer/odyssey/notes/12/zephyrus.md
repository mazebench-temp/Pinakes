---
summary: The West Wind of Book 12 is the storm wind. Eurylochus names “the stormy West Wind” with the South Wind as the winds that most of all shatter ships (12.289); when the ship leaves Thrinacia, “the screaming West Wind, raging with a great storm” breaks the forestays and brings down the mast (12.408–410); and it drops again when the South Wind takes Odysseus back to Charybdis (12.426).
# The entry claims Ζεφύροιο (12.289) and Ζέφυρος (12.408, 12.426).
---

**The stormy West Wind.** Eurylochus fears a storm “of the South Wind or the stormy West Wind, which most of all / shatter ships” (12.289–290; see [[notus]], [[stormy]] and [[eurylochus]]). The stormy West Wind, Ζέφυρος δυσαής, is the West Wind of the Iliad's banquet of the winds, “in the house of the stormy West Wind” (*Iliad* 23.200), and of Poseidon's storm on the raft (5.295; see the Book 5 note). It is not the West Wind of Book 10, the fair wind that Aeolus left free to carry the ships home (10.25–26; see the Book 10 note), nor the gentle breeze of the Elysian plain, to which W. W. Merry refers on this verse (*Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 12.290 and 4.567; see the general article).

**The screaming West Wind.** When the ship has left the island and Zeus has set a dark cloud above it, “at once there came / the screaming West Wind, raging with a great storm, / and the storm of wind broke the forestays of the mast, / both of them; and the mast fell backward” (12.407–410; see [[zeus]], [[forestays]], [[mast]] and [[the-shipwreck-of-odysseus]]). κεκληγώς, “screaming”, is said of a wind only here; it is the cry of birds and of men, and of the companions in Scylla's jaws, “and there at her doors she devoured them, screaming” (12.256; see [[scylla]]). λαίλαπι θύων, “raging with its storm”, ends three verses of the book: the wind that drops before the ship sails (12.400), the West Wind that comes (12.408), and the West Wind that drops again, “Then the West Wind ceased raging with its storm, / and the South Wind came quickly, bringing sorrows to my heart” (12.426–427; see [[the-storm-wind-on-the-sea]]).
