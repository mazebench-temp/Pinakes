---
summary: Odysseus tells Circe all that happened in the land of the dead “fair and right” (κατὰ μοῖραν, 12.35), and Eurylochus fears to die of hunger “and meet one's fate” (πότμον ἐπισπεῖν, 12.342).
---

**Fair and right.** On the night of the return to Aeaea Circe questions Odysseus about each thing, “and I told her all of it fair and right” (αὐτὰρ ἐγὼ τῇ πάντα κατὰ μοῖραν κατέλεξα, 12.35; see [[circe]]): κατὰ μοῖραν is “duly, as is fitting”, each thing in its share. Her answer begins “All these things have been brought to an end”, ταῦτα taking in all the circumstances of the voyage to Hades (12.37; W. W. Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 12.37), and goes on to what is still to come.

**Fate.** “but to die of hunger and meet one's fate is the most pitiable” (λιμῷ δʼ οἴκτιστον θανέειν καὶ πότμον ἐπισπεῖν, 12.342; see [[all-deaths-are-hateful]]). πότμος is the lot that falls to a man, his death: Menelaus is not fated to meet it in Argos (4.562), and Odysseus in the storm wished he had met it at Troy (5.308), in the same words. Eurylochus' counsel hastens it: the companions meet their fate in the wreck (12.415–419; see [[the-cattle-of-helios]]).
