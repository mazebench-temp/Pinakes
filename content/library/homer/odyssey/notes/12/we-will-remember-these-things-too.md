---
summary: The verse ends the first half of Odysseus' speech in the strait (12.212), before his orders to the rowers and the helmsman; a few verses later Scylla takes six of the men it was spoken to, “the most pitiable thing I have seen with my own eyes” (12.258).
---

Odysseus tells the Phaeacians this promise, and the telling is its fulfillment: he does remember these things, at Alcinous' table (see [[the-tale-to-alcinous]]). What he remembers is not a rescue only. The companions he encouraged see Charybdis and look on her “fearing destruction”, and meanwhile Scylla seizes six of them, who call on him by name “then for the last time” (12.244–250); he calls it the most pitiable thing he saw “of all that I have toiled through, searching out the paths of the sea” (12.258–259; see [[pity]] and [[odysseus-passes-scylla]]). The remembering of the saying is his alone, for none of those who heard it come home (12.417–419).
