summary: Odysseus to Circe: “Come now, goddess, tell me this truly, / whether I might somehow escape from deadly Charybdis / and ward off the other, when she would harm my companions” (12.112–114).
---

νημερτὲς ἐνίσπες, the verse-end of Telemachus' requests to Nestor and Menelaus (3.101, 4.331) and of Menelaus' question to him (4.314), with the opening εἰ δʼ ἄγε δή μοι, which Penelope uses again to question her nurse: “Come now, dear nurse, tell me truly” (23.35, a plain rendering). Circe's answer is a rebuke: “Hard man, once again your mind is on the works of war / and toil” (12.116–117; see [[hard]], [[charybdis]] and [[scylla]]).
