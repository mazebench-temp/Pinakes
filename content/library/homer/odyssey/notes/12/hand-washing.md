# "χεῖρας νιψάμενος" (12.336), the singular of the Book 10 phrase; the same words at 2.261, where Telemachus washes his
# hands in the gray sea before praying to Athena.
grc: ["χεῖρας νιψάμενος"]
en: [washed my hands, washed his hands]
---

Odysseus prepares for prayer as Telemachus did on the shore of Ithaca: “and washed his hands in the gray sea, and prayed to Athena” (χεῖρας νιψάμενος πολιῆς ἁλὸς εὔχετʼ Ἀθήνῃ, 2.261), “I washed my hands, where there was shelter from the wind, / and prayed to all the gods who hold Olympus” (12.336–337; see [[prayer]]). Telemachus washes in the sea itself; Odysseus, alone on the island, looks for a sheltered place, and nothing is said of the water. The washing is the only part of the ritual told, and the prayer is made alone, apart from companions whose loyalty he now doubts (W. W. Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 12.333). The gods answer with sleep (12.338; see [[hypnos]]).
