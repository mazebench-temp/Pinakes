summary: Oceanus opens the book as it closed the last: “And when the ship had left the stream of the river Oceanus / and had come to the wave of the wide-wayed sea, / and to the island of Aeaea” (12.1–3), the island that lies where the Sun rises.
# Ὠκεανοῖο (12.1) is claimed by the entry's stem.
---

**Out of the river.** Book 11 ended with the ship carried down the river Oceanus (11.639–640; see the Book 11 note), and Book 12 begins with its coming out: “And when the ship had left the stream of the river Oceanus / and had come to the wave of the wide-wayed sea, / and to the island of Aeaea, where are the house of early-born Dawn / and her dancing floors, and the risings of the Sun, / there we came and ran the ship ashore on the sand” (12.1–5; see [[rivers]], [[aeaea]], [[eos]] and [[helios]]). ἀπὸ δʼ ἵκετο is ἀφίκετο δέ, still part of the protasis, and the apodosis begins with the landing at 12.5 (W. W. Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 12.2; see the translation's note on 12.1–4). The river and the sea are two: the ship leaves the stream, ῥόον, of the river at the edge of the world, and comes to the wave of the open sea. ποταμοῖο … Ὠκεανοῖο, “the river Oceanus”, is the river of the shield of Achilles, where Hephaestus set “the great strength of the river Oceanus” along the outermost rim (*Iliad* 18.607–608), and the stream beside which the men had walked to the place of the dead (11.21). The sun rises out of it (*Iliad* 7.421–423; see the general article), and the island to which the ship returns lies at the risings of the Sun, a placing that Merry explains by Homer's joining of the farthest east and west (on 12.3; see the Book 12 note on [[helios]]).
