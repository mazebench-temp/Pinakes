---
summary: Bound to the mast and longing to hear the Sirens, Odysseus bids his companions set him free, “nodding with my brows”, but they bend forward and row (12.192–194).
---

“So they spoke, sending out their beautiful voice; and my heart / wished to listen, and I bade my companions set me free, / nodding with my brows; but they bent forward and rowed” (12.192–194; see [[sirens]], [[odysseus-and-the-sirens]] and [[mast]]). Their ears are stopped and his hands are bound: “It was no good to speak”, W. W. Merry notes, and quotes Ovid's half-verse, “for wax was smeared on his companions” (*Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 12.194; see [[wax]]). The nod with the brows is the gesture with which, after the Cyclops, he signed no to each of his weeping companions (9.468; see the Book 9 note), and the half-verse of the answer, οἱ δὲ προπεσόντες ἔρεσσον, is the one with which the companions obeyed his nod as they rowed away from the Cyclops' rocks, κρατὶ κατανεύων, “nodding with my head” (9.490; see [[oars]]). Here they keep his own earlier order against his nod, “And if I beg you and bid you set me free, / then you must press me with still more bonds” (12.163–164), and Perimedes and Eurylochus bind him tighter (12.195–196; see [[perimedes-the-companion]] and [[eurylochus]]).
