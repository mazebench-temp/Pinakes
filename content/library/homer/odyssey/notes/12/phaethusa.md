---
summary: Circe names Phaethusa first of the two goddesses who herd the Sun's cattle and sheep on Thrinacia, “nymphs of the lovely tresses, Phaethusa and Lampetie, / whom brilliant Neaera bore to Hyperion the Sun” (12.132–133). She has no part in the story that follows.
---

**The herders.** Circe describes the herds before their keepers: seven herds of cattle and as many flocks of sheep, fifty in each, which bear no young and never die. “And goddesses are their herders, / nymphs of the lovely tresses, Phaethusa and Lampetie, / whom brilliant Neaera bore to Hyperion the Sun” (12.129–133; see [[the-cattle-of-helios]], [[thrinacia]], [[lampetie]], [[neaera]] and [[helios]]). ἐπιποιμένες, “herders”, is Aristarchus' reading, against ἐπὶ ποιμένες, and W. W. Merry compares ἐπιβουκόλος, the word of “the herdsman of the cattle” (3.422; *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, critical note on 12.131; see the translation's note on 12.131). Their mother “settled them on the island of Thrinacia, to dwell far off / and guard their father's sheep and his cattle with curving horns” (12.135–136). Merry takes τηλόθι, “far off”, with ἀπῴκισε, “far from” the place of their birth, or as a way of saying that the island lay in the far west, unless it goes with ναίειν, “to dwell far off” (Merry on 12.135).

**Her name.** Merry calls the two nymphs “Bright” and “Shining”, and their mother “Younger”, “fit wife and daughters for the Sun” (on 12.132): Φαέθουσα is the participle of φαέθω, “shine”, and her father is the shining Sun (see the general article). Only Lampetie appears again, when she brings her father the news that the companions have killed his cattle (12.374–375; see the Book 12 note on [[lampetie]]). In the cave where the ship is anchored the companions find “the fair dancing floors and seats of the nymphs” (12.318; see [[nymphs]]), but the tale does not say that they saw the herders.
