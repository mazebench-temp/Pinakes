---
summary: Odysseus to the rowers: “You keep striking the deep breakers of the sea with your oars” (12.214).
---

βαθεῖαν of the surf, ἁλὸς ῥηγμῖνα βαθεῖαν. Merry describes ῥηγμίς as the wave breaking on the land, and cites this verse for its epithet (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 4.430; see the translation's key words, ῥηγμὶς βαθεῖα). The same breakers make the shore where the men stepped out on Aeaea, “where the sea breaks upon the shore” (12.6; see [[shore]] and [[oars]]).
