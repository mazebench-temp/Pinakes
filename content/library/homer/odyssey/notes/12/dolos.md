---
summary: In the simile of Scylla's fishing the fisherman casts down bait “as a snare for the little fishes” (12.252).
---

“As when on a jutting rock a fisherman with a very long rod / casts down bait as a snare for the little fishes, / and lets the horn of an ox of the fields down into the sea, / and then catches one and flings it out, gasping, / so they were lifted, gasping, toward the rocks” (12.251–255; see [[the-fisherman]] and [[odysseus-passes-scylla]]). δόλον κατὰ εἴδατα βάλλων is the bait as a trap, the food that hides the hook: ῥάβδος is a fishing rod here, not a fishing spear, which would not be used for little fishes nor with bait, and εἴδατα is Aristarchus' reading, against Callistratus' δείλατα (W. W. Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 12.251 and his critical note on 12.252; see the Odyssey translation's note on 12.251–253). Scylla needs no trick, and the snare is the fisherman's; in the simile the six men are the little fishes, caught as easily.
