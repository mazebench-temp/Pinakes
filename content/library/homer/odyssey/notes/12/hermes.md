---
summary: Hermes is named once, as the god from whom Calypso learned what the Sun and Zeus said in heaven: “and she said she had heard it herself from Hermes the guide” (12.390).
---

**The messenger's report.** The tale has one scene in heaven, the Sun's complaint and Zeus' promise of the thunderbolt (12.374–388; see [[helios]] and [[zeus]]), and Odysseus names his source for it: “This I heard from Calypso of the lovely hair; / and she said she had heard it herself from Hermes the guide” (12.389–390; see [[calypso]], [[guide]] and [[the-tale-to-alcinous]]). Ἑρμείαο διακτόρου, “Hermes the guide”, joins the god's name to his old title διάκτορος, traditionally “guide” or “messenger”, whose formation is disputed (see the general article). He is the gods' messenger in the Odyssey: Zeus sends him to Calypso with the words “Hermes, since you for your part are our messenger in all else” (5.29), and she knew him at once when he came, “for the gods are not unknown to one another” (5.79; see the Book 5 note). A scholion therefore called Odysseus' words a lie, since Calypso had not yet seen Hermes; W. W. Merry answers that the falsehood rather rests with Calypso (*Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, critical note on 12.389–390; see the Book 12 note on [[calypso]] and the translation's note on 12.389–390). On Aeaea Hermes had met Odysseus himself, with the drug that kept him safe from Circe (10.277–306; see the Book 10 note and [[moly]]).
