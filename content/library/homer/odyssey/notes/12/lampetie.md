---
summary: Lampetie is named twice. With her sister Phaethusa she herds the Sun's cattle and sheep on Thrinacia (12.132), and when the companions have killed the cattle she carries the news to her father: “And swiftly to Hyperion the Sun came a messenger, / Lampetie of the long robes, to tell him that we had killed his cattle” (12.374–375).
---

**The herder.** In Circe's warning the herds of the Sun have two goddesses for herders, “nymphs of the lovely tresses, Phaethusa and Lampetie, / whom brilliant Neaera bore to Hyperion the Sun” (12.132–133; see [[neaera]], [[thrinacia]] and the Book 12 note on [[phaethusa]]). W. W. Merry calls the sisters “Bright” and “Shining” (*Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 12.132; see the general article).

**The messenger.** The companions sacrifice the best of the cattle while Odysseus sleeps, and he wakes to the smell of the fat (12.353–373; see [[the-cattle-of-helios]]). “And swiftly to Hyperion the Sun came a messenger, / Lampetie of the long robes, to tell him that we had killed his cattle” (12.374–375; see [[helios]], [[hyperion]] and [[long-robed]]). ἄγγελος ἦλθε, “came a messenger”, is said of the Sun himself in Demodocus' song, when he came to tell Hephaestus of Ares and Aphrodite (8.270; see the Book 8 note on [[helios]]). The Sun who brought news there receives it here, and at once he speaks among the immortals and demands that the companions be punished (12.376–383). ἔκταμεν ἡμεῖς, “we had killed”, is the reading of Aristarchus' copies; the manuscripts have ἔκταν ἑταῖροι, “my companions had killed”, which Merry thinks came from a wish to exonerate Odysseus from the blame (Merry's critical note on 12.375 and note on 12.375; see the translation's note on 12.374–390). The verses of the scene in heaven, 12.374–390, carry the obelos in one manuscript and were probably rejected by Aristarchus, because the Sun, who “sees all things and hears all things” (*Iliad* 3.277; 12.323), should have known without a messenger; for the problem and Aristotle's answers to it see the Book 12 note on [[helios]].
