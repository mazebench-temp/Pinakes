---
summary: Odysseus warns his companions on Thrinacia: “for these are the cattle and fat sheep of a dread god, / of the Sun, who sees all things and hears all things” (12.322–323).
---

Teiresias' verse (11.109; see the Book 11 note). The god who sees all things learns of the slaughter from a messenger (12.374–375), and the scholia rejected those verses for that reason: “this is contrary to ‘and Sun, who see all things and hear all things’ (*Iliad* 3.277); for he ought to have known of himself” (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, critical note on 12.374–390; see the translation's note on 12.374–390, and [[messenger-scene]] and [[helios]]).
