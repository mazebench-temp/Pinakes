summary: At the Wanderers “the storms of deadly fire” sweep away planks and men (12.68); Odysseus asks whether he might escape “deadly Charybdis” (12.113), and the South Wind brings him back “that I might measure out again the way to deadly Charybdis” (12.428); and on the keel and mast he is “borne by deadly winds” (12.425).
grc: [ὀλοὴν]
---

τὴν ὀλοὴν … Χάρυβδιν, twice, is new to the entry; in Circe's description the whirlpool is “brilliant” (12.104; see [[brilliant]] and [[charybdis]]). πυρὸς ὀλοοῖο θύελλαι, “the storms of deadly fire” (see the translation's key words): Merry takes the smoke that Odysseus sees after the Sirens as these fires, seen by day (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 12.202). ὀλοοῖς ἀνέμοισιν is the verse-end of the winds that carried the ships from Malea, “for nine days I was borne by deadly winds” (9.82; see [[the-winds]]). At 12.428 Apollonius the Sophist read θάλασσαν for Χάρυβδιν (see the translation's note on 12.428).
