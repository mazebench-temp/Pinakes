---
summary: “And the father of men and gods no longer let Scylla / see me; for then I would not have escaped sheer destruction” (12.445–446).
---

The scholia rejected the two verses, since Scylla cannot leave her cave; Merry brackets them and suggests ἐμέ as the subject of εἰσιδέειν, “suffered him not to set eyes on Scylla again” (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, critical note and note on 12.445–446). The English follows the scholia's construal, with Scylla as the one who sees (see the translation's note on 12.445–446, and [[would-have-had-not]] and [[scylla]]).
