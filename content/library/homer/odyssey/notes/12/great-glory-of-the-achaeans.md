---
summary: The Sirens: “Come here, much-praised Odysseus, great glory of the Achaeans” (12.184).
---

The second half of Agamemnon's and Nestor's verse of praise (*Iliad* 9.673, 10.544), with δεῦρʼ ἄγʼ ἰών, “Come here,” for “Come, tell me” (see [[much-praised]]). The Sirens know the war at Troy (12.189–190) and greet Odysseus with the title the Achaeans gave him there; Cicero renders it *O decus Argolicum* (*De Finibus* 5.49; see the translation's note on 12.184–191, and [[sirens]]).
