---
summary: The loss of the companions, which the proem lays to their own recklessness (1.7–9), is told with every cause the poem knows: a god devising evil when Eurylochus carries the landing (12.295), the oath the companions break (12.298–304), Eurylochus' evil counsel (12.339), the gods' sleep, which Odysseus blames (12.371–373), and the Sun's anger and Zeus' thunderbolt (12.374–419). The manuscripts even change a verse to clear Odysseus (12.375).
refs: [12.295, 12.339, 12.371-373, 12.375]
---

**A god, a man, the men.** When the companions applaud Eurylochus' speech, Odysseus says, “And then indeed I knew that a god was devising evil” (12.295; see [[daimon]] and [[eurylochus]]), the verse with which Nestor told how he fled from Troy, knowing that a god was devising evil (3.166). He yields the landing, but binds the men with an oath, so that whatever follows is theirs: “that no one, in evil recklessness, / will kill a cow or a sheep” (12.300–301; see [[oath]] and [[atasthalie]]). Eurylochus “led the way in evil counsel among my companions” (12.339; see [[boule]] and [[all-deaths-are-hateful]]), and the men followed him.

**Odysseus and the gods.** Odysseus was asleep, as he was when the bag of the winds was opened, and he blames the gods for it: “truly to my blind folly you lulled me in pitiless sleep, / and my companions, staying behind, have devised a great deed” (12.372–373; see [[ate]] and [[hypnos]]). The deed is theirs, the folly his, and the sleep the gods'. In the next verses Lampetie tells the Sun “that we had killed his cattle” (ὅ οἱ βόας ἔκταμεν ἡμεῖς, 12.375): so read the copies of Aristarchus, while the manuscripts have ἔκταν ἑταῖροι, “my companions killed”, “from a wish to exonerate Odysseus from the blame” (W. W. Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, his critical note on 12.375; see the Odyssey translation's note on 12.374–390). Zeus' answer in the first council of the gods, that mortals have sorrows beyond their share through their own recklessness and then blame the gods (1.32–34), is borne out by the companions and contested by Odysseus in the same scene.
