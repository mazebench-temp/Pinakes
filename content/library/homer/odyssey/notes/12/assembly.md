summary: On Thrinacia, when the storm has driven the ship into a cave, Odysseus calls an assembly and warns the companions to keep away from the cattle (12.319–323); and the simile of the judge marks the hour when the timbers come up from Charybdis, “when a man rises from the assembly for his supper” (12.439–441).
# ἀγορεύσω (12.56) is Circe's "I will tell", not the assembly.
except: [12.56]
---

**On the island.** “And then I called an assembly and spoke among them” (καὶ τότʼ ἐγὼν ἀγορὴν θέμενος μετὰ μῦθον ἔειπον, 12.319), the verse with which Odysseus called his men together on the goat island and on Aeaea, there “among them all” (9.171, 10.188; see W. W. Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, his critical note on 12.319, for the variant πᾶσιν here). He tells them there is food and drink in the ship, and that the cattle and sheep belong to “a dread god, / of the Sun, who sees all things and hears all things” (12.320–323; see [[feast]] and [[helios]]), and their proud spirit is persuaded (12.324; see [[peitho]]). The earlier debate, held on board as they came to the island, was no formal assembly, and there Eurylochus carried the landing against him with the companions' applause (12.264–294; see [[eurylochus]]).

**The judge's hour.** The last assembly of the book is a simile. Odysseus clings to the fig tree over Charybdis until the mast and keel come up again, late, “at the hour when a man rises from the assembly for his supper, / one who judges the many quarrels of young men pleading their cases” (12.439–440; see [[the-judge-rising-for-supper]] and [[neikos]]). The time is toward evening, when a judge may be supposed to have got through his cases and the market-place begins to empty; ἀνέστη is an aorist of custom (Merry on 12.439–441). The assembly as a place of judgment is the scene on the Shield of Achilles, where the people gather in the place of assembly and two men quarrel over a blood-price before the elders (*Iliad* 18.497–508; see [[the-trial-on-the-shield]]).
