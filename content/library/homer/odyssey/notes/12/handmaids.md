---
summary: Circe's handmaids come down with her to the shore of Aeaea, bringing “bread and meat in plenty and sparkling red wine” for the men back from the dead (12.18–19).
---

The handmaids of Circe's house (10.348–359; see the Book 10 note) serve her once more, on the beach where the companions have buried Elpenor: “very quickly / she came, having made herself ready; and her handmaids brought with her / bread and meat in plenty and sparkling red wine” (12.17–19; see [[circe]] and [[aeaea]]). On this food the men feast all day, as Circe bids them (12.23–30). Bread and red wine are what the ship carries away from Aeaea: on Thrinacia the companions are to eat “the food that immortal Circe gave”, and they keep away from the cattle “as long as they had bread and red wine” (12.302, 12.327–328; see [[provisions]] and [[thrinacia]]).
