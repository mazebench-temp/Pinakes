---
summary: Eurylochus' reproach to Odysseus on Thrinacia: “You are a hard man, Odysseus; your might is beyond measure, and your limbs / never grow weary” (12.279–280).
---

“You are a hard man, Odysseus; your might is beyond measure, and your limbs / never grow weary; truly you are made all of iron” (σχέτλιός εἰς, Ὀδυσεῦ· περί τοι μένος, 12.279–280; see [[eurylochus]], [[hard]] and [[iron]]). W. W. Merry renders “a hard man art thou, beyond all measure is thy might”, and notes that the scholium explains σχέτλιος well by καρτερικός, “enduring” (*Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 12.279). The μένος that is praised is the reproach: a man whose strength does not tire will not let tired men set foot on land (12.281–285; see [[hypnos]]). Circe had called him σχέτλιε for wanting to fight Scylla (12.116; see [[polemos]]), and the companions use his strength against him to win their supper and the landing (12.291–294; see [[yield-to-night]]).
