summary: Against Circe's word Odysseus arms himself and goes up onto the deck at the prow, where he expects Scylla to appear first (12.228–231); in the storm after Thrinacia the helmsman, struck by the falling mast, falls from the deck like a diver (12.413–414).
# πρῴρης, "at the prow" (12.230, the only place in Homer), first met here; it applies in the Odyssey only, since in
# later prose πρῷρα is simply the prow.
grc: [πρῴρης]
en: [prow]
---

**The deck at the prow.** Circe had warned him not to linger arming himself beside the rock (12.121–123, 12.226–227), but “I put on my glorious armor, and taking two long spears / in my hands I went onto the deck of the ship / at the prow; for from there I expected that she would first appear, / Scylla of the rock, who brought a bane on my companions” (12.228–231; see [[spear]], [[scylla]] and [[odysseus-passes-scylla]]). πρῴρης, like πρύμνη, “stern”, is properly an adjective agreeing with the ship (W. W. Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 12.230), and Merry takes the verse as evidence that the ship had a small deck at the bow, as another at the stern, with the waist open between them (Appendix I §5; see [[stern]]). From there Odysseus watches in vain, and “my eyes grew weary / as I glanced all around toward the misty rock” (12.232–233; see [[eyes]]).

**The helmsman's fall.** When the mast falls back in the storm, it crushes the helmsman's head on the stern, “and he, like a diver, / fell from the deck, and his proud spirit left his bones” (12.413–414; see [[steering-oar]] and [[like-a-diver]]): he was at his place by the steering oar on the raised deck at the stern (Merry, Appendix I §5). In the Iliad's verses the diver falls “from the high tower” (*Iliad* 12.386).
