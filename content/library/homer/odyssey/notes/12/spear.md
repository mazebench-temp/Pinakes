---
summary: Forgetting Circe's command, Odysseus puts on his armor and takes two long spears up onto the deck at the prow to meet Scylla (12.226–231), but never sees her until she has taken six of his men (12.232–250).
---

Circe had rebuked him for the thought of fighting: “Hard man, once again your mind is on the works of war / and toil” (12.116–117; see [[circe]] and [[scylla]]). At the strait, “then I let the grievous command of Circe / slip from my mind, since she had told me not to arm myself; / but I put on my glorious armor, and taking two long spears / in my hands I went onto the deck of the ship / at the prow” (12.226–230; see [[deck]] and [[odysseus-passes-scylla]]). ἀλεγεινῆς is “hard”, W. W. Merry explains, because a fighting man chafes at passive endurance, λανθανόμην is “I let myself forget”, and καταδύς is used, as in the Iliad, of putting on one's arms (*Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 12.226–228; see the translation's note on 12.226–227). Two spears are a warrior's arms, as Athena in the likeness of Mentes imagined Odysseus standing at his doors “with helmet and shield and two spears” (1.256; see the Book 1 note). These are never thrown: he cannot catch sight of Scylla anywhere, and she takes six men while all eyes are on Charybdis (12.232–246; see [[eyes]] and [[charybdis]]).
