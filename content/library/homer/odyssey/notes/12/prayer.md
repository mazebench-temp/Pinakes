summary: On Thrinacia Odysseus goes up the island alone, washes his hands and prays to all the gods for a way home, and they pour sleep on him (12.333–338); the companions pray over the stolen cattle (12.356, 12.359); and Odysseus, waking to the smell of the fat, cries out to Zeus and the gods that they lulled him to his ruin (12.370–373).

**Alone.** “then I went away up the island, so that I might pray / to the gods, in case one of them might show me a way to go home” (12.333–334; see [[nostos]]). He goes apart from his companions because he was more likely to hold communion with the gods alone, as Eidothea met him when he went his way “alone, apart from my companions” (4.367) and Hermes met him on the way to Circe's house (10.277); and besides, W. W. Merry adds, the trust between him and his companions was broken, and he could not utter his misgivings about them to the gods in their presence (*Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 12.333). He washes his hands “where there was shelter from the wind”, and prays “to all the gods who hold Olympus” (12.336–337; see [[hand-washing]] and [[olympians]]). The answer is sleep: “and they poured sweet sleep upon my eyelids” (12.338; see [[hypnos]] and [[sweet-sleep]]).

**Over the cattle.** While he sleeps the companions drive off the cattle, “and they stood around them and prayed to the gods” (12.356), and the verse of the rite begins, “And when they had prayed” (12.359; see [[sacrifice]]). What they prayed is not told; the prayer is part of the rite, made over victims the gods did not give.

**The cry.** Waking, Odysseus smells the fat and calls out to the immortal gods (12.370; see the Odyssey translation's note on 12.369–370): “Father Zeus, and you other blessed gods who live forever, / truly to my blind folly you lulled me in pitiless sleep” (12.371–372; see [[ate]] and [[who-is-to-blame]]). The address is the verse with which Athena opens the council of the gods and Hephaestus calls them to see Ares and Aphrodite (5.7, 8.306), and the Sun uses it in the next speech to ask for vengeance (12.377; see [[helios]]). Odysseus' words are a complaint, not a request: the prayer he made before sleeping has been answered against him.
