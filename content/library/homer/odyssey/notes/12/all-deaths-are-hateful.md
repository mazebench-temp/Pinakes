---
summary: Eurylochus' second speech on the island of the Sun (12.339–351), made while Odysseus sleeps up the island: the maxim, the plan to drive off the best of the cattle and sacrifice them, the vow of a temple, and the choice of drowning over hunger. The companions applaud, as they did at his first speech (12.352 = 12.294).
---

**Hunger.** The saying answers what the book has just told: the month of the South Wind, the provisions spent, the men roaming the island for fish and birds, “whatever came to their hands”, while “hunger wore at their bellies” (12.325–332; see [[gaster]] and [[fasting]]). As long as they had bread and wine they kept away from the cattle, λιλαιόμενοι βιότοιο, “longing for life”, which W. W. Merry renders *vitae servandae studiosi*, “eager to save their lives”, against those who take it as “seeking food”, since they still had bread and wine (*Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 12.328; see the translation's note on 12.328–330).

**The counsel.** “And Eurylochus led the way in evil counsel among my companions” (12.339; see [[eurylochus]] and [[boule]]). Merry sets the sweet sleep of the verse before, γλυκὺν ὕπνον, against the evil counsel, κακῆς βουλῆς, and recalls that it was during Odysseus' sleep that the companions had opened the bag of the winds (Merry on 12.338; 10.31; see [[hypnos]]). Eurylochus opens with the verse with which Odysseus had told them the prophecies of Teiresias and Circe, “Hear my words, my companions, though you are suffering evils” (12.340 = 12.271), and makes the hunger a reason for sacrifice: ῥέξομεν, “let us sacrifice”, and the sacrifice implies a meal for the worshipers (12.343–344; Merry on 12.344; see [[sacrifice]]). The vow of a temple follows (12.345–347; see [[reciprocity-with-the-gods]]), and then the quick death by drowning, if the Sun should still be angry and the other gods follow his lead (12.348–351; see [[better-to-perish-once-and-for-all]]). “So spoke Eurylochus, and the other companions applauded” (12.352): the verse that closed his first speech, when he persuaded them to land for supper (12.294; see [[yield-to-night]]). The first speech brought them to the island; the second brings them to the cattle.
