---
summary: Two unnamed gods: before the Sirens “a god lulled the waves to sleep” (12.169), and when the companions applaud Eurylochus on Thrinacia Odysseus knows “that a god was devising evil” (12.295).
---

δαίμων names a power felt but not identified. At the Sirens' island the wind ceases, “and there was a calm, / a windless calm, and a god lulled the waves to sleep” (12.168–169; see [[hypnos]] and [[odysseus-and-the-sirens]]): the calm is the Sirens' weather, and no god is named for it. Off Thrinacia, when the companions side with Eurylochus, “And then indeed I knew that a god was devising evil” (12.295; see [[eurylochus]] and [[who-is-to-blame]]), the verse of Nestor's flight from Troy (3.166). Odysseus sees a god in his men's decision before any god has acted; the gods who later act are named, the Sun, who asks for vengeance, and Zeus, who takes it (12.374–419; see [[helios]] and [[zeus]]).
