# "μέθυ λεῖψαι" (12.362), σπένδοντες (12.363), each only here.
grc: ["μέθυ λεῖψαι", σπένδοντες]
en: [to pour, poured libations]
---

In the sacrifice of the Iliad the old priest burns the thigh-pieces on split wood, “and over them poured / sparkling wine” (*Iliad* 1.462–463; see [[sacrifice-type-scene]]). On Thrinacia the wine is gone, as the bread is, with the provisions of Circe's island (12.327–329): “And they had no wine to pour over the burning offerings, / but poured libations with water as they roasted all the entrails” (12.362–363; see [[sacrifice]] and [[wine]]). λεῖψαι and σπένδοντες are the two verbs of the libation, “pour” and “pour out a drink offering”, and the poem sets them in the place of the Iliad's λεῖβε; water stands for wine as the oak leaves stand for barley (12.357–358; see [[barley]]). The substitutes mark a rite performed with what the island gives, from victims that were not theirs to give.
