# Κύκλωψ (12.209) belongs to polyphemus-the-cyclops. Unnamed: the cave and the escape remembered (12.209-212).
refs: [12.209-212]
---

**The speech in the strait.** When the men see the smoke and the great wave and let the oars fall, Odysseus goes through the ship and speaks to each man in turn (12.201–207; see [[odysseus-passes-scylla]]):

{{quote:3c546328-62a5-5b3f-a4fc-8b49207a6254}}

εἴλει is “penned us”, of the Cyclops shutting them in his cave (12.210; W. W. Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 12.210; see the translation's key-word row for εἰλέω and [[polyphemus-the-cyclops]]). The text reads ἕπει, “besets”, where Merry prints ἔπι, “is upon us”, and takes the other readings for attempts to escape an unexampled lengthening of its last vowel (12.209; Merry's critical note and on 12.209; see the translation's note on 12.209). τῶνδε, “these things”, are the dangers before them, not the sufferings already past (12.212; Merry on 12.212, after Ameis; see the translation's note on 12.212).

**Prowess, counsel and mind.** In the cave Odysseus saved his men by guile, the false name and the wine and the stake (9.345–414; see [[nobody]] and [[metis]]); here he claims the escape for himself, ἐμῇ ἀρετῇ, βουλῇ τε νόῳ τε, “by my prowess and my counsel and my mind” (12.211). Eurylochus had told the companions on Aeaea that the men eaten by the Cyclops perished through their leader's recklessness (10.435–437); in the strait Odysseus remembers the escape, and he does not tell the men that Scylla will take six of them, as the Cyclops took six (12.223–225; 9.289, 9.311, 9.344). In his own house, in the twentieth book, he will bid his heart endure in the same memory, “you endured a more shameless thing once” (20.18–21, a plain rendering; see the general article).

**Virgil.** Aeneas speaks the same words to his men after the storm off Libya, and names both dangers that Odysseus' men had faced, Scylla and the Cyclops (*Aeneid* 1.198–203; Merry on 12.208; see [[odysseus-passes-scylla]] and [[virgil]]).
