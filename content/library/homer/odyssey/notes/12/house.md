**The house of Dawn.** The ship comes back “to the island of Aeaea, where are the house of early-born Dawn / and her dancing floors, and the risings of the Sun” (12.3–4; see [[aeaea]], [[eos]] and [[helios]]). The χοροί are “dancing lawns”, W. W. Merry explains, for Eos must be supposed to have a train of nymphs, symbolizing perhaps the quick-glancing rays of light, and the plural ἀντολαί reflects the sun's rising at different points of the horizon as the seasons change (*Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 12.4). That Aeaea should lie at the sunrise, when the course of Book 10 carried the ship to the northwest, he explains by Homer's sense that the extreme West and the extreme East are almost the same point (on 12.3; see the translation's note on 12.1–4).

**Circe's house.** “Then I sent some of my companions to the house of Circe / to bring the body of Elpenor, who had died” (12.9–10; see [[circe]] and [[elpenor]]), the house from whose roof he fell (10.552–560; see the Book 10 note). Circe's talk with Odysseus that night is not in her house: they are away from the companions, Merry thinks probably in some grotto, since she goes back up the island at dawn (on 12.33; 12.143).

**The house of Alcinous.** The tale ends where it is told: “But why do I tell you this tale? / For I already told it to you yesterday in your house, / to you and to your strong wife” (12.450–452; see [[alcinous]], [[arete-queen-of-the-phaeacians]] and [[the-tale-to-alcinous]]). Here Odysseus turns and speaks to Alcinous directly (Merry on 12.450), and “yesterday in your house” points back to the evening when he told Arete how he came from Calypso (Merry on 12.451; 7.241–297).
