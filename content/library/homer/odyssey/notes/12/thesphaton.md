---
summary: Odysseus calls Circe's instructions “the oracles that Circe told me”, and shares them with his companions, since it is not right that only one or two should know them (12.154–157).
---

“Friends, it is not right that only one or two should know / the oracles that Circe told me, brilliant among goddesses; / but I will tell them, so that knowing them we may either die / or else avoid death and doom and escape” (θέσφαθʼ ἅ μοι Κίρκη μυθήσατο, 12.154–157; see [[circe]], [[ker-doom]] and [[prophecy]]). θέσφατα are things spoken by a god, and so fixed: Circe's directions have the authority of the prophecies of Teiresias, whose warning about the cattle they repeat (12.137–141 = 11.110–114; see [[the-prophecy-of-teiresias]]). Odysseus does not tell them all of it. He tells the Sirens and the binding, and nothing yet of the strait; of Scylla he will say nothing at all (12.158–164, 12.223–225; see [[silence]] and [[odysseus-and-the-sirens]]).
