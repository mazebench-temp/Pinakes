# ἐμητίσαντο (12.373), only here.
grc: [ἐμητίσαντο]
en: [devised]
---

The verb of μῆτις, the planning in which Odysseus excels, is given to the companions at the moment they have planned without him: “truly to my blind folly you lulled me in pitiless sleep, / and my companions, staying behind, have devised a great deed” (οἱ δʼ ἕταροι μέγα ἔργον ἐμητίσαντο μένοντες, 12.372–373; see [[ate]] and [[hetairos]]). μέγα ἔργον is a monstrous deed, as of Epicaste's marriage (11.272; W. W. Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 11.272). In the strait he had claimed the escape from the Cyclops for “my prowess and my counsel and my mind” (12.211; see [[boule]], [[noos]] and [[not-without-knowledge-of-evils]]); on Thrinacia his men devise for themselves, under Eurylochus' evil counsel (12.339), and he sleeps (12.338; see [[hypnos]]).
