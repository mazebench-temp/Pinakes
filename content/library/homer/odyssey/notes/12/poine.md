---
summary: The Sun asks Zeus and the gods to punish the companions who killed his cattle, and threatens to go down to Hades and shine among the dead if they do not pay him fitting recompense (12.377–383).
# τῖσαι "punish" (12.378), τίσουσι "pay" (12.382), each only here.
grc: [τῖσαι, τίσουσι]
en: [punish]
---

The Sun's speech uses the verb of the penalty in both its voices. “Father Zeus, and you other blessed gods who live forever, / punish the companions of Odysseus, son of Laertes, / who overweeningly have killed my cattle” (12.377–379): τῖσαι, the aorist of τίνω, “make them pay”, is the gods' part (see [[helios]] and [[zeus]]). Then the companions' part: “And if they do not pay me fitting recompense for the cattle, / I will go down into the house of Hades and shine among the dead” (12.382–383), οὐ τίσουσι to be taken closely together, “refuse to pay”, as οὔ φησι is “deny” (W. W. Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 12.382; see [[reciprocity-with-the-gods]]). ὑπέρβιον, “overweeningly”, is used as an adverb, as in Book 14 (Merry on 12.379; 14.92, 14.95). The penalty is paid as Zeus promises, with the ship and the men (12.385–388, 12.415–419; see [[thunderbolt]] and [[the-cattle-of-helios]]), and Odysseus, who did not kill the cattle, pays with the loss of all his companions, as the prophecy said (12.139–141).
