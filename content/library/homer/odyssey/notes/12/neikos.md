summary: Odysseus rebukes his companions one by one when he finds the cattle dead (12.392), and the judge of the simile settles “the many quarrels of young men pleading their cases” (12.440).
# νείκεον (12.392), first met here in the Odyssey.
grc: [νείκεον]
---

**The rebuke.** “And when I had come down to the ship and the sea, / I rebuked them, going up to each in turn, but we could find / no remedy, for the cattle were already dead” (νείκεον ἄλλοθεν ἄλλον ἐπισταδόν, 12.391–393; see [[hetairos]] and [[the-cattle-of-helios]]). ἐπισταδόν is “walking up to each one and then standing near him”, like παρασταδόν, “standing beside each man”, when he encouraged them in the strait, but including the coming toward each, which suits ἄλλοθεν (W. W. Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 12.392; 12.207). The rebuke comes too late to change anything, and the gods' signs follow it at once (12.394–396; see [[teras]]).

**The quarrels.** The judge in the simile of the hour rises from the assembly “one who judges the many quarrels of young men pleading their cases” (κρίνων νείκεα πολλὰ δικαζομένων αἰζηῶν, 12.440; see [[the-judge-rising-for-supper]] and [[assembly]]): νεῖκος here is a dispute brought for judgment, as on the Shield of Achilles, where two men quarrel over a blood-price before the people (*Iliad* 18.497–508; see [[the-trial-on-the-shield]]).
