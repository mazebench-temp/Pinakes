---
summary: The companions weep for the six, “and as they wept, sweet sleep came upon them” (12.311); on Thrinacia the gods “poured sweet sleep upon my eyelids” (12.338); and “then sweet sleep rushed from my eyelids” (12.366).
---

Merry: it was during his sleep that the companions had brought trouble on him before, by opening the skin of the winds (10.31), and γλυκύν makes a fine contrast to the evil counsel of the next verse (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 12.338). When he wakes, the sweet sleep is “pitiless” (12.372; see [[pitiless]] and [[hypnos]]).
