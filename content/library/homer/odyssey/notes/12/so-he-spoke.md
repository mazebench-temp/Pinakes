---
summary: Eleven speeches close with the formula in the book: three of Circe's (12.28, 12.111, 12.142); five of Odysseus' own, “So I spoke” (12.115, 12.222, 12.277, 12.303, 12.324); the Sirens' “So they spoke, sending out their beautiful voice” (12.192); and Eurylochus' two, “So spoke Eurylochus, and the other companions applauded” (12.294, 12.352).
---

Eurylochus' close is the Iliad's verse of Agamemnon, “So spoke the son of Atreus, and the other Achaeans applauded” (*Iliad* 3.461), with the companions for the Achaeans; both times the others side with him against Odysseus (12.294–297, 12.352–353; see [[eurylochus]]). ὣς φάσαν (as 10.67) closes the only song in the book, and names the voice that sang it (see [[honeyed-voice]] and [[sirens]]). Some closes take other forms: “So I was telling each thing and making it known to my companions” (12.165), and the replies on Olympus, which follow without a close (12.384, 12.389). The tale itself has no close in the book; it comes in the next, in the verse of the pause at 11.333: “So he spoke, and they all fell hushed in silence” (13.1; see [[the-tale-to-alcinous]]).
