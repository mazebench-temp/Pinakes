---
summary: Odysseus arms against Circe's word: “but I put on my glorious armor, and taking two long spears / in my hands I went onto the deck of the ship / at the prow” (12.228–230).
refs: [12.226-231]
---

The arming is a verse and a half, with no pieces named: καταδύς, “putting on,” of arms, as in the Iliad (*Iliad* 4.222, 6.504; Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 12.228), and the two spears of a warrior. Circe had warned him, “if you linger, arming yourself beside the rock, / I fear that she will rush at you again” (12.121–122), and he says that he let her “grievous command” slip from his mind (12.226–227; see the translation's note on 12.226–227, and [[grievous]] and [[famous]]). The armor is no help: he cannot catch sight of Scylla, and while all eyes are on Charybdis she takes six men from the ship (12.232–247; Merry on 12.247; see [[odysseus-passes-scylla]] and [[scylla]]).
