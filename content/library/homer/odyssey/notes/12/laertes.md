# Λαέρτ* (in the entry) claims Λαερτιάδεω (12.378).
---

The Sun, told by Lampetie that his cattle have been killed, complains to the gods: “Father Zeus, and you other blessed gods who live forever, / punish the companions of Odysseus, son of Laertes, / who overweeningly have killed my cattle” (12.377–379; see [[helios]], [[son-of-laertes]] and [[odysseus]]). The genitive Λαερτιάδεω Ὀδυσῆος, “of Odysseus, son of Laertes”, which fills the second half of the verse, is found here for the first time in Homer (the Iliad has only Λαερτιάδη and Λαερτιάδης); the later books use it often, above all in the verse of address ὦ γύναι αἰδοίη Λαερτιάδεω Ὀδυσῆος, “Revered wife of Odysseus, son of Laertes” (17.152; 19.165 and later, a plain rendering).
