# ἀλγήσετε (12.27), only here.
grc: [ἀλγήσετε]
en: [suffer pain]
---

Circe promises to show the way and point out each thing, “so that you may not, through some grievous mischief, / either on sea or on land, suffer pain and meet with bane” (ἀλγήσετε πῆμα παθόντες, 12.26–27; see [[circe]]). κακορραφίη, “mischief”, is used in the plural at 2.236, and κακὰ ῥάπτομεν, “we stitched evils”, is the same metaphor (W. W. Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 12.26); ἀλγήσετε is a subjunctive with the short vowel, and ἁλός a local genitive, “on the sea” (Merry on 12.27). Her directions are followed and the pain comes all the same. After the wreck the West Wind drops, “and the South Wind came quickly, bringing sorrows to my heart, / that I might measure out again the way to deadly Charybdis” (12.427–428; see [[notus]] and [[thymos]]): the words φέρων ἐμῷ ἄλγεα θυμῷ are a parenthesis, and ὄφρα follows on ἦλθε (Merry on 12.428; see the Odyssey translation's note on 12.428).
