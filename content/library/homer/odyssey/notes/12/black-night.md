---
summary: Eurylochus: “But now let us yield to black night / and make ready our supper, staying beside the swift ship” (12.291–292).
---

The verse of Hector's and Nestor's counsel in the Iliad (*Iliad* 8.502, 9.65), spoken against Odysseus' order to drive past the island (12.276). Merry: nightfall suggests supper-time, so that “to act upon the suggestion of night” is here to make supper ready (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 12.291). Eurylochus has just named the danger that the night brings to ships, “Out of the nights come harsh winds, the ruin of ships” (12.286), and the storm comes in the night (12.312–315; see [[yield-to-night]], [[swift-night]] and [[eurylochus]]).
