---
summary: Eurylochus: “but you bid us wander on just as we are through the swift night” (12.284).
grc: ["διὰ νύκτα θοὴν"]
---

διὰ νύκτα θοήν, the Iliad's θοὴν διὰ νύκτα in the other order, is new to the entry; Merry renders αὔτως “just as we are,” tired, sleepy and supperless (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 12.284). The night he fears brings the storm (12.286–290, 12.312–315; see [[black-night]] and [[eurylochus]]).
