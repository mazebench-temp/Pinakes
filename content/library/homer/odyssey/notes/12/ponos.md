# πονησάμενοι (12.151 = 11.9), the participle of πονέομαι, which the entry's stems do not catch.
grc: [πονησάμενοι]
en: [busied ourselves]
---

**War and toil.** “Hard man, once again your mind is on the works of war / and toil” (καὶ πόνος, 12.116–117; see [[polemos]]): πόνος here is the Iliad's labor of battle, which Odysseus would take up against Scylla, and Circe sets the pair against the gods, “will you not give way even to the immortal gods?” (12.117; see [[alke]]).

**The ship's work.** The verb is used of the sailors' daily work: “At once, when we had busied ourselves with all the tackle throughout the ship, / we sat; and the wind and the helmsman steered her” (12.151–152, as 11.9–10; see [[ships]]). It is the verse with which they left Aeaea for the land of the dead; now they leave it for the Sirens, with the following wind that Circe sends (12.148–150; see [[circe]]).
