---
summary: Circe names the Wanderers, “as the blessed gods call them” (12.61); and Odysseus and the Sun both appeal to “Father Zeus, and you other blessed gods who live forever” (12.371, 12.377).
grc: ["θεοὶ μάκαρες"]
---

θεοὶ μάκαρες, in the reverse order, is new to the entry (as *Iliad* 4.127; again 14.83, 18.134; see [[language-of-gods-and-men]]). The address is the verse of Athena at the council of the gods and of Hephaestus calling them to see Ares and Aphrodite (5.7, 8.306; see the Odyssey translation's formulas), here spoken first by the man who wakes to the smell of the sacrifice and then by the god whose cattle it is (12.370–373, 12.376–383; see [[father-zeus]] and [[gods-who-live-forever]]).
