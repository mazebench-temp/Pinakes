summary: When the cattle of the Sun are killed, the gods show signs to the companions: the hides crawl, the meat lows on the spits, both roast and raw, and there is a sound as of cattle (12.394–396).
# τέραα (12.394), the open plural, only here in Homer.
grc: [τέραα]
---

The portent comes after the deed. Odysseus has come down to the ship and rebuked the men one by one, but the cattle are already dead (12.391–393; see [[neikos]]), and then “at once the gods showed signs to them: / the hides crawled, and the meat on the spits lowed, / both roast and raw, and there was a sound as of cattle” (12.394–396; see [[sacrifice]] and [[spits]]). εἷρπον is “crawled on the ground” (W. W. Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 12.395). The text prints προύφαινον without the coronis (see the Odyssey translation's note on 12.394).

The Iliad's portents call for an interpretation, and men act on them, trusting “the portents of the gods” (θεῶν τεράεσσι, *Iliad* 4.398, 4.408, 6.183). This one needs no seer and changes nothing. The companions feast on for six days (12.397–398; see [[feast]]); the sign is the gods' anger made visible, and its fulfillment is the thunderbolt on the seventh day (12.399–419; see [[thunderbolt]] and [[the-cattle-of-helios]]).
