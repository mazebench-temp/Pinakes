---
summary: Circe greets the men back from Hades, “Hard men, who have gone down alive into the house of Hades” (12.21), and rebukes Odysseus, “Hard man, once again your mind is on the works of war” (12.116); and Eurylochus: “You are a hard man, Odysseus” (12.279).
---

Merry renders the word “something like ‘irrepressible’” in Circe's rebuke, and in Eurylochus' approves the scholion's καρτερικός, “enduring”: “a hard man art thou, beyond all measure is thy might” (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 12.116, 12.279). Eurylochus explains it himself: “truly you are made all of iron” (12.280; see [[iron]] and [[eurylochus]]). The men who went down alive are “twice dying, when other men die once” (12.22; see the translation's note on 12.22).
