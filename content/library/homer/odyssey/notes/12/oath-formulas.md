summary: Odysseus to his companions: “But come now, all of you, swear me a mighty oath” (12.298); “So I spoke, and at once they swore that they would not, as I bade them. / But when they had sworn and completed the oath” (12.303–304).
grc: [ἀπώμνυον, "ὄμοσάν τε τελεύτησάν τε τὸν ὅρκον"]
---

The verses of Circe's oath in Book 10, in the plural (10.345–346; see the Odyssey translation's formulas). For ἀπώμνυον Merry refers to his note on Eurycleia's oath (2.377), where he takes the verb in its later sense, “swore that she would not,” against Ameis' “swore unreservedly,” and reports Nitzsch's view that ὄμοσεν names the substance of the oath and τελεύτησεν the form in which it was couched (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 12.303 and 2.377). The plural forms are new to the entry, and the second verse comes again in the oaths of Books 15 and 18 (15.438, 18.59). What the companions swear is that no one, “in evil recklessness, / will kill a cow or a sheep” (12.300–301); they keep it while the food lasts (12.327–328), and break it when Eurylochus leads them (12.339–353; see [[oath]], [[krateros]] and [[the-cattle-of-helios]]).
