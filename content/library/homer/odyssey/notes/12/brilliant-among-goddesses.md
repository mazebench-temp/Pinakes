---
summary: Circe four times: “And standing in our midst she spoke, brilliant among goddesses” (12.20); “So I spoke, and at once she answered, brilliant among goddesses” (12.115); “She then went away up the island, brilliant among goddesses” (12.143); and in Odysseus' words to his companions, “the oracles that Circe told me, brilliant among goddesses” (12.155).
---

The first verse is found only here (see the Odyssey translation's formulas), the second is Eidothea's (4.382, 4.398; 10.487, 10.503). These are the formula's last uses for Circe: she goes away up the island and is not seen again (12.143). Merry gathers from that verse that the night's talk of 12.33–34 was not in her house but probably in some grotto or bower near the shore (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 12.33, 12.143; see [[circe]] and [[then-answered]]).
