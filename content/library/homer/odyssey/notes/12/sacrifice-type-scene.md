summary: The companions sacrifice the cattle of the Sun in the verses of the sacrifice at Chryse, without barley and without wine: they pray “having stripped tender leaves from a high-leafed oak; / for they had no white barley on board the well-benched ship” (12.357–358), and “poured libations with water as they roasted all the entrails” (12.363).
refs: [12.343-347, 12.353-365, 12.395-398]
passages:
  - 12.353-365 | The sacrifice of the cattle of the Sun
---

Eurylochus proposes the rite as the way to take the cattle: “let us drive off the best of the cattle of the Sun / and sacrifice to the immortals” (12.343–344), and Merry notes that a sacrifice implies a meal for the worshippers (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 12.344). The steps of the pattern follow, each with a gap.

1. **Preparation and prayer.** “and they stood around them and prayed to the gods” (12.356) takes up the verse of Agamemnon's sacrifice, where the men “took up the barley grains” (*Iliad* 2.410); here leaves stand in for the barley (12.357–358), as Merry explains, comparing Nestor's sacrifice (3.441–447; Merry on 12.356; see [[barley-grains]], [[high-leafed]] and [[tender]]).
2. **Killing.** “And when they had prayed and slaughtered and flayed them” (12.359): the verse of *Iliad* 1.458 without the barley, and of the three verbs of the killing only the last two, without the drawing back of the victims' heads (*Iliad* 1.459). Merry's note on the verse lists the earlier places of the verses that follow (Merry on 12.359).
3. **The god's portion.** The thigh-bones wrapped in fat in a double fold, and raw flesh laid on them (12.360–361 = *Iliad* 1.460–461; see [[thigh-pieces]]). Where the old man at Chryse pours sparkling wine over the burning pieces (*Iliad* 1.462–463), “they had no wine to pour over the burning offerings, / but poured libations with water” (12.362–363; see [[libation]] and [[wine]]).
4. **The men's portion.** The thigh-pieces burned, the entrails tasted, the rest cut up and threaded on spits (12.364–365 = 3.461–462; see [[spits]]).
5. **The meal.** It is not told at once: Odysseus wakes and smells the fat (12.366–369), and the feast is told only after the portents, “For six days then my trusty companions / feasted” (12.397–398), while the hides crawl and the meat lows on the spits (12.395–396; see [[feast-type-scene]] and [[the-cattle-of-helios]]).

The ritual itself is in [[sacrifice]].
