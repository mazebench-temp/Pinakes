---
summary: When Odysseus bids the companions drive the ship past the island of the Sun, “their dear heart was broken” (12.277).
---

“But drive the black ship on past the island.’ / So I spoke, and their dear heart was broken” (τοῖσιν δὲ κατεκλάσθη φίλον ἦτορ, 12.276–277; see [[hetairos]] and [[the-cattle-of-helios]]). It is the verse of their despair on Aeaea, when Odysseus told them of the smoke he had seen on the island, which made them think of the Laestrygonians and the Cyclops, and when he told them they must go to the house of Hades (10.196–200, 10.564–566). Here what breaks their heart is an order to row on through the night, and it turns at once to Eurylochus' reproach and the landing (12.278–294; see [[eurylochus]] and [[yield-to-night]]).
