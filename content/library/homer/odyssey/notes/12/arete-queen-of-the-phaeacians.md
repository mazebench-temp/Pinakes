# Unnamed: "your strong wife" (12.452).
refs: [12.452]
---

“But why do I tell you this tale? / For I already told it to you yesterday in your house, / to you and to your strong wife” (12.450–452; see [[alcinous]] and [[odysseus]]). His first account of himself, on the night of his coming, was his answer to her: she knew the clothes he wore, which she had made with her handmaids, and asked who he was, and from where, and who had given them to him (7.233–239). He told her then of Ogygia and Calypso, of the thunderbolt that shattered his ship and the nine days on the keel, and of Nausicaa, who gave him the clothes (7.241–297; see [[calypso]] and [[nausicaa]]). The long tale ends where that answer began: on Ogygia, with Calypso, “who loved me and looked after me” (12.450). ἰφθίμῃ ἀλόχῳ, “strong wife”, is said in the Iliad of the wife of Diomedes and of the wife of Sthenelus, son of Perseus (*Iliad* 5.415, 19.116; see [[mighty]]).
