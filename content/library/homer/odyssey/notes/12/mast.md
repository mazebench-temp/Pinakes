summary: If Odysseus would hear the Sirens, Circe tells him, he must be bound upright in the mast-step with the rope-ends fastened to the mast, and bound with more bonds if he begs to be freed; and so it is done (12.50–54, 12.160–164, 12.178–179, 12.193–200). After Thrinacia the mast is set up again; the storm breaks the forestays, the falling mast kills the helmsman, and when the surge has snapped it off at the keel Odysseus lashes it to the keel and rides on the two (12.402–444).

**The mast-step.** Circe's words, which Odysseus repeats to his companions and they carry out, are “let them bind you in the swift ship hand and foot, / upright in the mast-step, and let the rope-ends be fastened to the mast itself” (12.50–51; the second verse again at 12.162, and with ἀνῆπτον, “fastened”, at 12.179; see [[sirens]] and [[odysseus-and-the-sirens]]). W. W. Merry translates, “let them bind thee hand and foot standing upright in the mast-step, and from the mast itself let rope-ends be fastened”, the mast, ἱστοῦ, being understood from ἱστοπέδη (*Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 12.50). The word is found only in these three verses, and its sense was uncertain in antiquity. Apollonius' Homeric lexicon, Merry reports, explains it as an upright timber rising from the keel, to which the mast is bound, and so does the Suda (ι 695); Eustathius and Hesychius take it for the hollow place in the middle of the ship into which the mast is set, deriving the word from πούς, “foot” (Merry, Appendix I §12, who counts the Suda with them, though its entry gives the first sense). Merry regards ἱστοπέδη and μεσόδμη as virtually the same thing, μεσόδμη describing the structure with reference to the whole ship, ἱστοπέδη with closer reference to the mast, and renders them “step” and “mast-box” (Appendix I §12; see [[mast-box]], [[keel]] and the translation's row ἱστοπέδη; πείρατα).

**The rope-ends.** Merry, with Liddell and Scott, brings every use of πείρατα under one sense, “ends”: the bounds of the earth and of Ocean, the end that a thing reaches, the rope at whose two ends Zeus and Poseidon pull (*Iliad* 13.358–360; see [[the-rope-of-war]]). Here, where Odysseus is lashed to the mast, the word means exactly the rope-ends, not the whole rope, as the Etymologicum Magnum explains it, and as ἀρχή later names the end of a rope: “the free ends of the cords with which Odysseus is tied are secured to the mast” (Merry on 12.51; see the translation's note on 12.51). When he begs to be set free, “nodding with my brows”, Perimedes and Eurylochus stand up “and bound me with more bonds and pressed me tighter” (12.194–196; see [[brows]], [[perimedes-the-companion]] and [[eurylochus]]), as Circe had said, and only when the song can no longer be heard do the companions free him from his bonds (12.197–200; see [[wax]]). Aristophanes athetized 53–54, asking why one already bound should be bound again (Merry's critical note; see the translation's note on 12.53–54).

**The mast in the storm.** On the seventh day after the cattle were killed the companions put out from Thrinacia, “setting up the mast and hoisting the white sails” (12.402; see [[sails]] and [[white]]), nearly the verse with which the fleet put out again after the storm of Book 9 (9.77). The West Wind breaks both forestays, “and the mast fell backward, and all the tackle / was spilled into the bilge. And on the stern of the ship / it struck the helmsman's head, and smashed the bones / of the head all together” (12.410–413; see [[forestays]], [[steering-oar]] and [[like-a-diver]]). ὁ δʼ ἄρα at 411 is the mast, and the ὅπλα are all the cordage of mast and yard, which fell into the hold when the mast fell aft (Merry on 12.410–411; see [[ships]]). After the thunderbolt the surge snaps the mast off at the keel, into whose solid wood it was stepped (Appendix I §12), and Odysseus lashes the two together with the backstay and rides on them; Charybdis swallows them and at evening gives back “the mast and keel” (12.422–425, 12.437–441; see [[keel]] and [[charybdis]]). The mast of the raft that Calypso helps him build is broken in the middle by the first storm (5.316).
