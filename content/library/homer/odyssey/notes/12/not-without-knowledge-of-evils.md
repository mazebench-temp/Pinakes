---
summary: The saying opens Odysseus' speech in the strait (12.208–221), between the Sirens and Scylla: the companions have dropped their oars at the sight of the smoke and the sound of the roar, and he reminds them of the Cyclops' cave before he gives his orders and keeps silent about Scylla.
---

The speech has two parts, the memory and the orders. The memory is the Cyclops: “surely this evil that besets us is no greater than when the Cyclops / penned us in his hollow cave with his mighty strength” (12.209–210; see [[odysseus-and-the-cyclops]] and [[polyphemus-the-cyclops]]). εἴλει is “penned us” (W. W. Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 12.210), and ἕπει, “besets”, is the reading of the text, where Merry prints ἔπι, “is upon us” (his note and critical note on 12.209; see the translation's note on 12.209). The orders follow, introduced by “But now come, let us all do as I say” (12.213), the verse with which Agamemnon and others call the Iliad's army to a decision (*Iliad* 2.139, 9.26; see [[peitho]]): the rowers are to keep striking the deep breakers, and the helmsman to keep the ship away from the smoke and wave and make for the crag (12.214–221). The companions obey (12.222). The saying works; the knowledge it claims is incomplete, for Odysseus keeps back what he knows of Scylla (12.223–225; see [[silence]] and [[we-will-remember-these-things-too]]).
