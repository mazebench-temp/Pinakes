---
summary: Neaera settles her daughters on Thrinacia “When their lady mother had borne them and reared them” (12.134), θρέψασα τεκοῦσά τε, “having reared and borne them” in the Greek order.
grc: ["θρέψασα τεκοῦσά τε"]
en: [had borne them and reared them]
---

Merry calls it a prothysteron and refers to his note on 4.208, where he lists it with τράφεν ἠδʼ ἐγένοντο (*Iliad* 1.251) under γαμέοντί τε γεινομένῳ τε, “at bridal and birth” (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 12.134 and 4.208). As at 4.723, the English gives the natural order (see [[neaera]], [[queenly]] and [[thrinacia]]).
