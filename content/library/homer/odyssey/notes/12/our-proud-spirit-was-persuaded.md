---
summary: Circe persuades the men to feast a day before they sail: “So she spoke, and our proud spirit was persuaded” (12.28); and Odysseus persuades them to keep from the cattle of the Sun: “So I spoke, and their proud spirit was persuaded” (12.324).
---

12.28 is the verse of Antinous' story of the web (2.103 = 10.466), and 12.324 the verse with which the companions obeyed Odysseus on the morning they left Aeaea (10.550; see the Odyssey translation's formulas). The second persuasion holds only while the food lasts: “And as long as they had bread and red wine, / they kept away from the cattle, longing for life” (12.327–328; see [[proud]], [[peitho]] and [[the-cattle-of-helios]]).
