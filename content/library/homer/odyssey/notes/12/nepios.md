---
summary: The man who hears the Sirens never comes home to “his wife and little children” (νήπια τέκνα, 12.42).
---

“Whoever draws near in his ignorance and hears the voice / of the Sirens, for him his wife and little children / do not stand beside him when he comes home, nor are they glad” (12.41–43; see [[sirens]] and [[nostos]]). νήπια τέκνα are “little children”, the helpless young; the man they wait for in vain is the one who comes near “in his ignorance” (ἀιδρείῃ, 12.41), the condition that the narrator elsewhere marks with νήπιος, “the fool”. The singular παρίσταται goes with the wife alone, and the children join her as subjects of γάνυνται (W. W. Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 12.42). The proem called the companions νήπιοι for eating the cattle of the Sun (1.8; see [[the-cattle-of-helios]]), and in this book they do it.
