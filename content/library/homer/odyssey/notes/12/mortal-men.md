summary: The crag of Scylla: “No mortal man could climb it or set foot on its top, / not even if he had twenty hands and feet” (12.77–78); and Zeus to the Sun: “and to mortal men upon the grain-giving earth” (12.386).
grc: ["βροτὸς ἀνὴρ"]
---

βροτὸς ἀνήρ, new to the entry, is always set against the gods in Homer: the mortal man whose likeness Ares takes (*Iliad* 5.604), the god a mortal man cannot overcome (4.397), and here the rock that no mortal could climb, where Scylla lives, “not even if a god should meet her” (12.88; see [[scylla]]). θνητοῖσι βροτοῖσιν is the verse of the sunrise in Book 3 (12.386 = 3.3), set against the Sun's threat to go down and shine among the dead (12.383; see [[grain-giving]] and [[helios]]).
