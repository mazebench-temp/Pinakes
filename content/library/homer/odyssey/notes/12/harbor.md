---
summary: On Thrinacia, after the oath, the companions moor the ship “in the hollow harbor / near sweet water” for the night (12.305–306); after the storm they drag it into a hollow cave, where the nymphs have their dancing floors and seats (12.317–318).
---

**The hollow harbor.** Eurylochus has asked only for a supper on land and a night beside the ship, “and at dawn we will go on board and put out into the wide sea” (12.291–293; see [[eurylochus]] and [[thrinacia]]). When the companions have sworn not to touch the cattle, “we moored the well-made ship in the hollow harbor / near sweet water, and my companions went out / of the ship” (12.305–307; see [[oath]] and [[well-made]]). λιμένι γλαφυρῷ is found only here; γλαφυρός, “hollow”, is the common epithet of ships and caves (see [[hollow-ships]] and [[hollow-caves]]). The ship is left in the water. For a short stay, W. W. Merry explains, a ship was not drawn up on shore but moored out in the water, and so ἐνήσομεν, Eurylochus' word, is “will put out” rather than “launch” (*Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 12.293, comparing 4.785); on Aeaea, where they stayed a year, Circe had bidden them first of all drag the ship up onto the land (10.403–404; see [[aeaea]]).

**The cave.** In the third part of the night Zeus sends the storm, and at dawn “we anchored the ship, dragging it into a hollow cave. / There were the fair dancing floors and seats of the nymphs” (12.317–318; see [[nymphs]] and [[dance]]). The harbor of the island of goats had water and a cave too, “at the head of the harbor flows bright water, / a spring from under a cave” (9.140–141; see [[the-island-of-goats]]), and so will the harbor of Phorcys on Ithaca, near whose head is “a lovely cave, misty, / sacred to the nymphs who are called Naiads”, with “ever-flowing waters” in it (13.102–109, a plain rendering; see [[ithaca]]).
