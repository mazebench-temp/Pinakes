summary: Named once in the Odyssey, when Circe tells of the Wanderers, past which no ship has come but the Argo, sailing home from Aeetes; and even she came through only because Hera sent her past, “since Jason was dear to her” (12.69–72).
# Ἰήσων (12.72), the only nominative of his name in Homer, is claimed by the entry.
passages:
  - 12.69-72 | The Argo passes the Wanderers
---

Circe warns Odysseus that by the way of the Wanderers “no ship of men that comes there has ever yet escaped”, for the waves and the storms of fire sweep away the planks of ships and the bodies of men (12.66–68; see [[the-wanderers]], [[circe]] and [[odysseus]]), save one:

{{quote:49a41337-1d13-56c0-a99e-b259316a0809}}

The Argo is on her homeward voyage from Colchis, W. W. Merry notes (*Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 12.70; see [[the-argo]]), coming “from Aeetes” (12.70), the brother of Circe herself (10.137; see [[aeetes]]); and she is saved by Hera's love for Jason (see [[hera]]). The subject of βάλεν, “would have dashed”, is the waves, and not a word is said of the rocks closing in on the ship (Merry on 12.71), the danger of the Symplegades, the Clashing Rocks, with which the Wanderers were early identified: here it is the surf and the storms of fire that destroy ships (Merry on 12.61; see the translation's note on 12.61). πᾶσι μέλουσα, “in the thoughts of all”, is what Odysseus says of himself when he names himself to the Phaeacians, “who for tricks of every kind / am in the thoughts of men” (9.19–20; Merry on 12.70). Aristarchus is said to have written it as one word, πασιμέλουσα (Merry's critical note on 12.70), and Merry takes the four verses for a later addition (on 12.70; see the translation's note on 12.69–72).

It is the Odyssey's only mention of Jason. The Iliad names him only as the father of Euneus, whom Hypsipyle bore him on Lemnos (*Iliad* 7.468–471, 21.41, 23.747; see [[euneus]] and [[hypsipyle]]), and the catalogue of heroines names his kin, though not him: Tyro, who bore Aeson and Pelias; and Pelias “lived in Iolcus” (11.254–259; see [[tyro]], [[aeson]] and [[pelias]]).
