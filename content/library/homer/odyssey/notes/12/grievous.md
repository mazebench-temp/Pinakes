---
summary: Circe will show the way “so that you may not, through some grievous mischief, / either on sea or on land, suffer pain” (12.26–27); and Odysseus “let the grievous command of Circe / slip from my mind” (12.226–227).
---

Merry takes ἀλεγεινῆς at 12.226 as “hard,” because a fighting man chafes at passive endurance (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 12.226; see the translation's note on 12.226–227). κακορραφίῃ ἀλεγεινῇ is a phrase of this book; the noun is in the plural at 2.236 (Merry on 12.26). The grievous command is Circe's word against arming (12.116–123; see [[arming-scene]] and [[circe]]).
