---
summary: The mouth of Scylla's cave is so high on the crag that not even a man in his prime, shooting an arrow with his bow from the ship, could reach it (12.83–84).
---

“Not even a man in his prime, from the hollow ship, / shooting an arrow with his bow, could reach the hollow cave” (12.83–84; see [[scylla]], [[arrows]] and [[hollow-ships]]): the cavern's mouth is so high up in the cliff that the strongest bowman could not reach it with his arrow, W. W. Merry explains (*Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 12.83). The bow is Circe's measure of the crags: the two are so near each other that “you could even shoot an arrow across” (12.102). When Scylla comes, Odysseus, who boasted of his archery among the Phaeacians (8.215–228; see the Book 8 note), arms himself with two long spears, not a bow (12.228–229; see [[spear]]).
