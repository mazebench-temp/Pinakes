# γοόωντες (12.234), first met here in the Odyssey.
grc: [γοόωντες]
---

“We sailed up the narrow strait, lamenting” (ἡμεῖς μὲν στεινωπὸν ἀνεπλέομεν γοόωντες, 12.234; see [[odysseus-passes-scylla]]). The verb of the lament for the dead is used of the living, who row between Scylla on one side and Charybdis on the other, and lament before anyone has died. Odysseus has told them nothing of Scylla, but they see Charybdis suck down the salt water and spew it up, “and pale fear took hold of them” (12.235–243; see [[deos]] and [[silence]]). The dead of the strait, the six whom Scylla takes, get no lament but the companions' tears that night (12.309–311; see [[tears]]).
