summary: The shore of Book 12 is the beach of Aeaea, where the ship is run ashore on the sand on its return from the dead (12.5–7), and the headland on which the companions bury Elpenor (12.11–15). On Thrinacia, waking from the sleep that the gods poured on him, Odysseus goes back “to the swift ship and the shore of the sea” (12.366–367).
# πρόεχʼ ἀκτή (12.11), "where the headland jutted out farthest", as a phrase: bare ἀκτή is also the grain of Demeter
# (ἀκτήν, Il. 13.322, 21.76) and barley meal (ἀκτῆς, Od. 2.355).
grc: ["πρόεχʼ ἀκτή"]
en: [headland]
---

**The beach of Aeaea.** Back from Oceanus, “there we came and ran the ship ashore on the sand, / and we ourselves too stepped out where the sea breaks upon the shore. / There we fell asleep and waited for brilliant Dawn” (12.5–7; see [[aeaea]]): the three verses with which the ship came back to the island of goats after the Cyclops (9.546–547, 9.150–151; see the Book 9 note), as on the first landing on Aeaea the ship was brought in “to the shore in silence” (10.140; see the Book 10 note).

**The headland.** At dawn the companions bring Elpenor's body from Circe's house, and “where the headland jutted out farthest / we buried him” (12.11–12; see [[elpenor]] and [[the-death-of-elpenor]]). W. W. Merry renders it “where the beach ran out to its furthest point”, and compares the mound of Achilles, which the host of the Argives heaped up “on a jutting headland”, ἀκτῇ ἔπι προὐχούσῃ, by the broad Hellespont, so that it might be seen far off from the sea (24.80–84, a plain rendering), and the jutting beaches, ἠιόνας προὐχούσας, over which Nausicaa's maids flee (6.138; *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 12.12). Elpenor had asked for his mound “on the shore of the gray sea”, a mound for men to come to hear of (11.75–76; see [[tomb]] and [[gray-sea]]). That night the others sleep on the beach “beside the stern-cables of the ship” (12.32; see [[stern-cables]]).

**The ship and the shore.** On Thrinacia, when the gods have poured sleep on him and the companions have killed the cattle, Odysseus wakes and “went to the swift ship and the shore of the sea” (12.366–367), the verse with which he went down from Circe's house to the ship in Book 10 (10.407; see the Book 10 note), and coming down “to the ship and the sea” he finds the cattle already dead (12.391–393; see [[thrinacia]] and [[the-cattle-of-helios]]).
