---
summary: Two silences in the passage of the strait: Odysseus, bound to the mast with his companions' ears stopped, can only nod with his brows (12.192–194), and before Scylla he says nothing of her, “lest my companions in fear should stop / rowing” (12.223–225).
refs: [12.192-194, 12.223-225]
---

**Signs.** At the Sirens speech is useless: the companions' ears are stopped with wax, and Odysseus, wishing to listen, bids them set him free “nodding with my brows” (12.192–194); it was no use to speak, since they could not hear him (W. W. Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 12.194; see [[neuo]] and [[odysseus-and-the-sirens]]). The escape from the Cyclops was also made in signs, when no one dared speak (9.468–469, 9.490).

**Silence kept.** In the strait he tells the companions of the smoke and the crag, “But of Scylla I said nothing more, a vexation past remedy, / lest my companions in fear should stop / rowing, and huddle themselves together inside the ship” (12.223–225; see [[deos]] and [[odysseus-passes-scylla]]). He dared not reveal the details of the danger, Merry explains, because he knew from Circe's prophecy that he must lose at least six men, and he contented himself with the vague mention of the crag (on 12.223). The man who had told his companions the oracles so that they might die knowing or escape (12.156–157) keeps back the one danger that knowledge cannot help (see [[thesphaton]] and [[not-without-knowledge-of-evils]]).
