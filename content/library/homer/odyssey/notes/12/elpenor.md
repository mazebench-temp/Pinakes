summary: The burial that his soul begged for. Back on Aeaea from the land of the dead, Odysseus sends companions at dawn to bring the body from Circe's house; where the headland juts out farthest they burn it with its armor, weeping, heap up a tomb, drag a pillar onto it and fix his oar on the top (12.8–15).

**The promise kept.** At the pit Elpenor's soul had begged Odysseus not to leave him behind “unwept, unburied”, but to burn him with his armor, heap up a mound for him on the shore of the gray sea, and fix on it the oar with which he rowed among his companions (11.72–78); Odysseus answered, “All this, unhappy man, I will accomplish and do for you” (11.80; see [[odysseus]], [[unwept-and-unburied]] and the Book 11 note). On the first morning after the ship comes back to Aeaea he keeps the promise to the letter:

{{quote:3bbd0cf7-c67f-5482-87b4-82a98bcb5885}}

He is buried with tears, as he asked, and the half-verse in which they weep, ἀχνύμενοι θαλερὸν κατὰ δάκρυ χέοντες, “grieving and shedding big tears” (12.12), is the one in which they set out for the land of the dead (11.5; see [[tears]]); the body is burned “and the armor of the body” (12.13; see [[cremation]]); and the tomb is heaped on the shore, “where the headland jutted out farthest” (12.11, 12.14; see [[tomb]] and [[honoring-the-dead]]). θάπτομεν is imperfect, “we buried him where the beach ran out to its furthest point”, as W. W. Merry renders it, comparing the tomb of Achilles and Patroclus, which the Achaeans heaped “on a jutting headland” by the Hellespont (24.80–82, a plain rendering; *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 12.12); that tomb was to be seen from far off, from the sea (24.83, a plain rendering). Merry joins ἐπὶ … ἐρύσαντες, “having dragged up a stone pillar thereon”, on the mound, and compares ῥυτοῖσιν λάεσσι, the “hauled stones” of the Phaeacians' place of assembly (6.267; Merry on 12.14). The στήλη is named only here in the Odyssey; in the Iliad the tomb and the pillar are the due of the dead, which Sarpedon's kinsmen will give him, “with a tomb and a pillar; for that is the prize of the dead” (*Iliad* 16.457, 16.675; see [[sarpedon]]).

**The oar.** “and on the top of the tomb we fixed the well-fitted oar” (12.15; see [[oars]]): πήξαμεν … ἐυῆρες ἐρετμόν are the words of Teiresias' charge to Odysseus to fix his own oar in the ground, far from the sea (11.129; see [[the-prophecy-of-teiresias]] and [[the-death-of-elpenor]]). For the end of the verse Zenodotus wrote ἵνα σῆμα πέλοιτο, “so that it might be a mark” (Merry's critical note on 12.15).

**The last of him.** Circe does not fail to notice their return while they are busy with these rites, “We were arranging all these things” (12.16; see [[circe]]), and the poem does not name Elpenor again: he is named in four lines only, at his fall, at the pit and at his burial (10.552; 11.51, 11.57; 12.10).
