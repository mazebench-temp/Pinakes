summary: Circe's warning of the Sun's cattle ends in the verses of the seer's prophecy (12.137–141, as 11.110–114), and off Thrinacia Odysseus remembers “the word / of the blind seer, Theban Teiresias, / and of Circe of Aeaea”, and tells his men “the prophecies of Teiresias” (12.264–276). The book sees the prophecy fulfilled: the companions kill the cattle and perish, and Odysseus alone escapes (12.339–446).

**Circe repeats the seer.** Circe ends her counsel with the island of Thrinacia and the Sun's cattle, and her last verses are those in which Teiresias had warned Odysseus at the pit: “If you leave them unharmed and think of your homecoming, / you may still reach Ithaca, though suffering evils; / but if you harm them, then I foretell destruction for you, / for your ship and your companions. And even if you yourself escape, / you will come home late and in evil plight, having lost all your companions” (12.137–141, as 11.110–114; see [[circe]], [[thrinacia]] and [[the-prophecy-of-teiresias]]). The last two verses are omitted, or written in the margin, in the majority of the manuscripts, and La Roche brackets them; Murray prints them (W. W. Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, critical note on 12.140–141; see the translation's note on 12.140–141).

**The word of the blind seer.** Still at sea off the island, Odysseus hears the cattle lowing as they are stalled for the night, “and there fell upon my heart the word / of the blind seer, Theban Teiresias, / and of Circe of Aeaea, who charged me again and again / to avoid the island of the Sun, who gladdens mortals” (12.266–269; see [[odysseus]], [[the-blind-seer]] and [[thebes]]). The manuscripts read μάντιος for μάντηος, as at 10.493 (Merry's critical note on 12.267; see the Book 10 note). Grieving at heart, he tells his men “the prophecies of Teiresias / and of Circe of Aeaea” (12.270–273; see [[prophecy]]), and the verbs that follow, “who charged me” and “she said”, are singular in the best manuscripts, of Circe alone (Merry's critical notes on 12.268, 12.273 and 12.275; see the translation's note on 12.265–275): “for there, she said, was the most dread evil for us. / But drive the black ship on past the island” (12.275–276). Eurylochus and the crew overrule him (12.277–303; see [[eurylochus]]).

**The prophecy fulfilled.** What the seer foretold comes about in this book. The companions kill the cattle while Odysseus sleeps (12.339–365; see [[the-cattle-of-helios]]), Zeus strikes the ship with his thunderbolt, and all the companions are lost (12.415–419; see [[the-shipwreck-of-odysseus]]); Odysseus alone escapes, “having lost all your companions” (11.114 = 12.141), to come home late and in evil plight “on someone else's ship” (11.115).
