---
summary: Scylla devours the six companions “at her doors”, the mouth of her cave, as they scream and stretch out their hands to Odysseus (12.256–257).
---

“And there at her doors she devoured them, screaming, / stretching out their hands to me in grim combat” (12.256–257; see [[scylla]], [[grim-combat]] and [[odysseus-passes-scylla]]). Scylla's doors are the mouth of her cave in the middle of the crag, so high that no bowman could reach it from a ship (12.80–84; see [[bow]]), where she sits sunk to her middle and holds her heads out to fish (12.93–95). In the simile just before, the fisherman flings the gasping fish θύραζε, “out”, onto the rock (12.254; see [[the-fisherman]]). ἐν αἰνῇ δηιοτῆτι is more graphic than a word for death or pain, W. W. Merry writes, since it implies an unavailing struggle with an overmastering power, “as we speak of ‘death-struggle’” (*Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 12.257; see the translation's note on 12.256–257).
