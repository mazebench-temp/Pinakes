---
summary: The Sun is “Hyperion the Sun” four times in Book 12, when Circe names the mother of his daughters (12.133), when Odysseus sees his sheep (12.263), in Eurylochus' vow of a temple (12.346) and when Lampetie brings him the news (12.374); and once the wax grows warm in “the ray of the lord Sun, son of Hyperion” (12.176), the only time Homer calls him by the patronymic.
---

**Hyperion the Sun.** The name stands beside Ἠέλιος at each of the Sun's moments in the book: the nymphs “whom brilliant Neaera bore to Hyperion the Sun” (12.133; see [[neaera]], [[phaethusa]] and [[lampetie]]), “many fat sheep of Hyperion the Sun” on the island (12.263; see [[thrinacia]]), Eurylochus' promise, “we will at once build a rich temple to Hyperion the Sun” (12.346; see [[eurylochus]]), and the messenger, “And swiftly to Hyperion the Sun came a messenger” (12.374; see [[helios]] and [[the-cattle-of-helios]]). Ὑπερίονος Ἠελίοιο at the end of 12.263 is the verse-end of the proem's “the cattle of Hyperion the Sun” (1.8; see the Book 1 note) and of the Iliad's Titans, who “have no joy in the rays of Hyperion the Sun” (*Iliad* 8.480; see [[cronus]]); in the other three verses the dative Ἠελίῳ Ὑπερίονι follows the first foot, the same words in the same place each time.

**Son of Hyperion.** At the Sirens' island Odysseus warms the wax in the sun: “and at once the wax grew warm, since the great strength bade it / and the ray of the lord Sun, son of Hyperion” (12.175–176; see the Book 12 note on [[helios]]). Ὑπεριονίδης is found only here in Homer. Nitzsch held it un-Homeric and the verse spurious (W. W. Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 12.175), and Merry, discussing the name on 1.8, denies that it proves Ὑπερίων a shortened patronymic, since a form in -ίδης need not be one (see the Book 1 note and the general article). The patronymic is in the other early poems: Hesiod's Circe is “daughter of the Sun, son of Hyperion”, Ἠελίου θυγάτηρ Ὑπεριονίδαο, with the name and the patronymic in one verse, as here (*Theogony* 1011; see [[circe]]), and in the *Hymn to Demeter* it is the Sun's name when he answers Demeter, τὴν δʼ Ὑπεριονίδης ἠμείβετο μύθῳ (*Hymn to Demeter* 74; see [[hesiod]]). Where the Iliad's verse has “the rays of Hyperion the Sun” (*Iliad* 8.480), αὐγῇς Ὑπερίονος Ἠελίοιο, the Odyssey's has the same noun with the patronymic, Ἠελίου τʼ αὐγὴ Ὑπεριονίδαο ἄνακτος.
