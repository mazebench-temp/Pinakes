---
summary: Past the Sirens, “at once my trusty companions took away the wax” (12.199); and on Thrinacia, “For six days then my trusty companions / feasted, having driven off the best of the cattle of the Sun” (12.397–398).
---

Merry finds the epithet “certainly inappropriate” at 12.397, a mere epic formula, which need not be read as ironical (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 12.397). The same companions are trusty when they free Odysseus from the mast and when they eat the forbidden meat (see [[hetairos]] and [[the-cattle-of-helios]]).
