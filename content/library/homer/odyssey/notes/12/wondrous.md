summary: Odysseus to his companions: “First, of the wondrous Sirens, she bids us avoid / the voice and the flowering meadow” (12.158–159); and Zeus roused a wind against them “with a heaven-sent storm” (12.314).
grc: [θεσπεσιάων]
---

Σειρήνων … θεσπεσιάων is new to the entry. The scholion explains it as “uttering divine things,” but Merry takes it of the wonderful charm of their song, not of the revelations they profess to make (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 12.158; see [[sirens]] and [[flowering]]). λαίλαπι θεσπεσίῃ is the verse of the storm of Book 9 (12.314 = 9.68; see the Odyssey translation's formulas).
