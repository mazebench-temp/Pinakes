---
summary: Circe: “anoint the ears of your companions, / kneading honey-sweet wax, so that none of the others / may hear” (12.47–49).
---

κηρὸν … μελιηδέα, the wax of the bees, honey-sweet against the Sirens' “honeyed voice” (12.187; see [[honeyed-voice]], [[wax]] and [[sirens]]). Merry takes παρεξελάαν and ἀλεῖψαι as imperatives, and reports Nitzsch's citation of a line of Alcman in which Circe anoints the ears of Odysseus' companions, the earliest testimony extant to a particular scene of the Odyssey (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 12.47; see the translation's note on 12.47–50).
