---
summary: The Sirens greet Odysseus with the Iliad's verse of praise, “Come here, much-praised Odysseus, great glory of the Achaeans” (12.184).
---

“Come here, much-praised Odysseus, great glory of the Achaeans, / stop your ship, so that you may hear the voice of us two” (μέγα κῦδος Ἀχαιῶν, 12.184–185; see [[great-glory-of-the-achaeans]], [[much-praised]] and [[sirens]]). The address is the one with which Agamemnon and Nestor greeted Odysseus in the Iliad, when he came back from the embassy to Achilles and from the night raid (*Iliad* 9.673, 10.544; see [[do-not-praise-me-too-much]]). The Sirens know whom they are singing to, and begin with his glory: the song that promises knowledge of Troy opens with the fame he won there (12.189–191; see [[song-and-fame]] and [[odysseus-and-the-sirens]]).
