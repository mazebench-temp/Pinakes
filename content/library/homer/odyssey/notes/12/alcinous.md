# Unnamed: "you", addressed at the end of the tale (12.450-453).
refs: [12.450-453]
---

The whole book is spoken to the king and the Phaeacians in his hall (see [[the-tale-to-alcinous]] and [[phaeacians]]), but only in its last verses does Odysseus turn and address Alcinous directly (W. W. Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 12.450; see [[odysseus]]): “But why do I tell you this tale? / For I already told it to you yesterday in your house, / to you and to your strong wife; and it is hateful to me / to tell over again a tale that has been plainly told” (12.450–453). “Yesterday” is the night of his coming, when he sat in the hall with the king and the queen after the other guests had gone, and answered Arete's question with the story of Ogygia, the thunderbolt and the nine days on the keel (7.230–297; see [[arete-queen-of-the-phaeacians]] and [[calypso]]). The tale that Alcinous asked for when his guest wept at Demodocus' song, “where you were driven in your wanderings, and to what lands / of men you came” (8.572–586; see [[odysseus-weeps-at-the-feast]]), is done, and the book ends with it. The next begins with the hall silent, “held by a spell throughout the shadowy halls”, as it was at the pause (13.1–2, a plain rendering; 11.333–334), and with the king's answer: since his guest has come to his house, he will not be beaten back on his way home (13.3–6, a plain rendering; see [[pompe]]).
