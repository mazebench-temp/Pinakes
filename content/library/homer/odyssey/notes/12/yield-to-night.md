# "πειθώμεθα νυκτὶ μελαίνῃ" (12.291), the verse of Iliad 8.502 and 9.65, whose phrase the Iliad notes claim there.
grc: ["πειθώμεθα νυκτὶ μελαίνῃ"]
en: [let us yield to black night]
---

The verse has come from the Iliad's war to the sea. Hector, when darkness has saved the Achaean ships, and Nestor in the assembly of the Achaeans bid their men yield to night and take their supper (*Iliad* 8.502–503, 9.65–66; see the entry). Eurylochus uses it against Odysseus, who would have them row on past the island: men worn out by weariness and sleep cannot sail through the swift night, when harsh winds rise out of the dark, so “But now let us yield to black night / and make ready our supper, staying beside the swift ship, / and at dawn we will go on board and put out into the wide sea” (12.279–293; see [[eurylochus]], [[hypnos]] and [[peitho]]). Nightfall suggests supper-time, and so to act upon the suggestion of night is here to make supper ready (W. W. Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 12.291). In the Iliad the night parts armies and the men who obey it fight again at dawn; here the men who obey it never put out at dawn, for the storm Zeus raises in the night holds them on the island for a month (12.312–326; see [[the-cattle-of-helios]]).
