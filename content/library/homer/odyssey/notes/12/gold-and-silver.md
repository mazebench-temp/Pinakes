---
summary: The only gold of the book is the throne of Dawn, who comes “of the golden throne” at the end of Circe's instructions (12.142).
---

“So she spoke, and at once Dawn of the golden throne came” (ὣς ἔφατʼ, αὐτίκα δὲ χρυσόθρονος ἤλυθεν Ἠώς, 12.142 = 10.541; see [[eos]] and [[golden-throned]]). The book has no treasure, no gift and no ransom; its riches are the Sun's cattle and the temple full of fine offerings that Eurylochus promises the god (12.346–347; see [[reciprocity-with-the-gods]]), and the gold is a goddess's. The verse that ends Circe's night of instructions ended her night with Odysseus before the voyage to the dead (10.541), and the dawn it brings is the dawn of departure (12.143–146; see [[circe]]).
