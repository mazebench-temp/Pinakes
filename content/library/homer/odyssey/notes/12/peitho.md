summary: Persuasion and obedience in the book: the companions' proud spirit is persuaded by Circe and by Odysseus (12.28, 12.324); they obey his orders in the strait, “let us all do as I say” (12.213, 12.222); and Eurylochus persuades them to land, “let us yield to black night” (12.291).
# πειθώμεθα (12.213, 12.291), first met here in the Odyssey.
grc: [πειθώμεθα]
---

**Persuaded.** “So she spoke, and our proud spirit was persuaded” (12.28), when Circe bids them feast all day and sail at dawn, and “So I spoke, and their proud spirit was persuaded” (12.324), when Odysseus warns them in the assembly on Thrinacia to keep away from the cattle (see [[our-proud-spirit-was-persuaded]] and [[assembly]]).

**Obeying.** In the strait he calls for obedience in the Iliad's verse of decision, “But now come, let us all do as I say” (νῦν δʼ ἄγεθʼ, ὡς ἂν ἐγὼ εἴπω, πειθώμεθα πάντες, 12.213; as *Iliad* 2.139, 9.26), and gives his orders; “So I spoke, and they quickly obeyed my words” (12.222; see [[not-without-knowledge-of-evils]]). They obey because they do not know about Scylla (12.223–225; see [[silence]]).

**Yielding.** On the evening at Thrinacia the same verb serves the other side. Eurylochus: “But now let us yield to black night / and make ready our supper, staying beside the swift ship” (ἀλλʼ ἦ τοι νῦν μὲν πειθώμεθα νυκτὶ μελαίνῃ, 12.291–292; see [[yield-to-night]] and [[eurylochus]]), and the companions applaud (12.294). Odysseus, outvoted, says “truly you are pressing me hard, one man alone” (12.297), and the men he could command in the strait he can now only bind with an oath (12.298–304; see [[oath]]).
