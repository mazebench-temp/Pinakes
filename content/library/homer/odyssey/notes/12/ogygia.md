summary: The tale ends where the poem found Odysseus: borne nine days from Charybdis on the mast and keel, “on the tenth night / the gods brought me to the island of Ogygia, where Calypso / lives” (12.447–449).
---

**The end of the tale.** After Charybdis Odysseus is carried nine days on the wreckage, and on the tenth night the gods bring him to Ogygia, to Calypso, “who loved me and looked after me” (12.447–450; see [[calypso]], [[charybdis]] and [[the-shipwreck-of-odysseus]]). He had told Arete of his coming there in nearly the same verses, “and was borne along for nine days; and on the tenth black night / the gods brought me to the island of Ogygia” (7.253–254; see the Book 7 note), and the scholia report that eight verses of that book were athetized because the story is told again here (see the translation's note on 7.251–258). So he breaks off: “But why do I tell you this tale? / For I already told it to you yesterday in your house” (12.450–451), turning, as W. W. Merry notes, to address Alcinous directly (*Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 12.450; see [[alcinous]] and [[the-tale-to-alcinous]]). The island where his story of the wanderings stops is the one on which the poem first showed him, held by Calypso (1.13–15, 1.85; see the Book 1 note).
