summary: Odysseus to his companions in sight of Thrinacia, “Hear my words, my companions, though you are suffering evils” (12.271), and Eurylochus to the companions, with the same verse, while Odysseus sleeps (12.340).
---

The verse of the speech on Aeaea (10.189; see the Odyssey translation's formulas) opens the two speeches that decide the end of the voyage: Odysseus' warning to drive past the island (12.271–276) and Eurylochus' counsel to drive off the best of the cattle and sacrifice them (12.340–351). The second speaker takes up the leader's own opening while the leader is away and asleep (12.333–339; see [[eurylochus]], [[sweet-sleep]] and [[the-cattle-of-helios]]).
