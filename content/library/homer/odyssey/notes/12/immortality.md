---
summary: Two immortal things that mortals cannot fight or touch: Scylla, “not mortal, but an immortal evil” (12.118), and the cattle of the Sun, which neither breed nor die (12.130–131).
refs: [12.118, 12.130-131]
---

**Scylla.** Circe's answer to Odysseus' wish to fight is a statement of kind: “She is not mortal, but an immortal evil, / dread and grievous and savage, not to be fought” (12.118–119; see [[scylla]] and [[alke]]). κακόν may almost be personified into “fiend” (W. W. Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 12.118). An immortal cannot be killed, so courage is useless and flight the only course (12.120), and Scylla is still in her cave when Odysseus passes again (12.430, 12.445–446; see [[odysseus-passes-scylla]]).

**The cattle.** The Sun's herds share the condition of their master: “And no young are born of them, / nor do they ever die” (12.130–131; see [[genee]] and [[the-cattle-of-helios]]). Their number never changes, and it is the companions who break the order of the deathless herds by killing them; the meat lows on the spits, “both roast and raw”, as if the cattle were still alive (12.395–396; see [[teras]]). Mortals who touch what belongs to the immortals pay with their mortality (12.417–419; see [[thanatos]]).
