summary: For Elpenor's burning the companions cut logs where the headland juts out farthest, and burn his body with his armor before they heap up the mound (12.11–13).
# φιτροὺς, the logs (12.11, the only place in the Odyssey), first met here; it applies in the Odyssey only, since in
# the Iliad φιτρῶν are also the logs of the Achaean wall (12.29, 21.314).
grc: [φιτροὺς]
en: [logs]
---

The pyre is not named. “And quickly we cut logs, and where the headland jutted out farthest / we buried him, grieving and shedding big tears. / But when the body was burned, and the armor of the body, / we heaped up a tomb” (12.11–14; see [[cremation]], [[tomb]] and [[elpenor]]). φιτροί are the logs that the woodcutters carry for the pyre of Patroclus (*Iliad* 23.123), and the armor burned with the body is what Elpenor asked for: “but burn me with my armor, whatever I have” (11.74; see the Book 11 note and [[the-death-of-elpenor]]). The pyres of the two books before are vowed, not built: the pyre heaped with good things that Odysseus is to give the dead when he comes home (10.523, 11.31).
