---
summary: Scylla's teeth, in three rows, are “thick and close-set, full of black death” (12.92).
---

μέλανος θανάτοιο ends the verse of the teeth of the creature that Circe calls “not mortal, but an immortal evil” (12.118; see [[scylla]] and [[immortals]]). The death in the teeth comes later in the book: “and there at her doors she devoured them, screaming” (12.256; see [[odysseus-passes-scylla]]).
