---
summary: The Sirens: “For no one has ever yet rowed past here in his black ship / before he has heard the honeyed voice from our lips; / but he goes home delighted, and knowing more” (12.186–188).
---

The promise is the one Circe had turned round: whoever hears their voice does not come home to wife and children (12.41–43; see [[sirens]], [[shrill]] and [[honey-sweet]]). Merry joins ὄπα with ἀπὸ στομάτων ἡμέων, as the English takes it (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 12.187).
