**Hera and Jason.** “Only one seafaring ship has sailed past there, / the Argo, in the thoughts of all, sailing from Aeetes; / and even her the waves would quickly have dashed there against the great rocks, / but Hera sent her past, since Jason was dear to her” (12.69–72; see [[the-argo]], [[jason]], [[aeetes]] and [[the-wanderers]]). It is the only place in Homer where Hera's care for Jason is told, and the only place where the Argo is named; the ship is already “in the thoughts of all” (12.70), as Odysseus, by his own account, is “in the thoughts of men” for his tricks (9.19–20). W. W. Merry, who draws the comparison, refers to Apollonius' account of the passage (*Argonautica* 4.924 and following), notes that Homer places it on the homeward voyage from Colchis, and thinks the four verses probably a later addition (*Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 12.70; see the translation's note on 12.69–72). Not a word is said, he adds, of the rocks closing in upon the ship (on 12.71): the danger is the waves and the storms of fire (12.67–68). παρέπεμψεν, “sent her past”, makes the goddess the ship's escort, as πομπή is the escort that gods and kings give travelers in the Odyssey (see [[pompe]]). In the Iliad Hera is the friend of the Achaeans before Troy (see the general article); here she saves a hero of the generation before them.
