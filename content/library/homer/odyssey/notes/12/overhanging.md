summary: Circe: “On the one side are overhanging rocks, and against them / roars the great wave of dark-eyed Amphitrite” (12.59–60).

πέτραι ἐπηρεφέες, the rocks of the Wanderers (12.61), the third use in Homer after the banks of the Achaean ditch and the cliffs of the Laestrygonians' harbor (see the general article, the translation's key words, ἐπηρεφής, and [[the-wanderers]] and [[dark-eyed]]).
