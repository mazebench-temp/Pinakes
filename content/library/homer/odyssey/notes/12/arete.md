---
summary: In the strait Odysseus reminds his companions that his prowess, with his counsel and his mind, brought them out of the Cyclops' cave (12.211).
---

“yet even from there, by my prowess and my counsel and my mind, / we escaped, and some day, I think, we will remember these things too” (ἐμῇ ἀρετῇ, βουλῇ τε νόῳ τε, 12.211–212; see [[boule]], [[noos]] and [[we-will-remember-these-things-too]]). ἀρετή is set first, before counsel and mind, and it is his own, ἐμῇ: the claim is made to men who have just dropped their oars in terror, to give them confidence in their leader (12.203–207; see [[deos]] and [[not-without-knowledge-of-evils]]). In the next scene the prowess he trusts fails him. He arms against Scylla in his glorious armor with two long spears, and cannot even see her (12.226–233; see [[ephemosyne]] and [[odysseus-passes-scylla]]).
