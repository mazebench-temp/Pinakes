---
summary: Circe's Wanderers, which only the Argo has passed, on its way home from Aeetes, are where the Odyssey touches the Argonauts' story. Merry refers to the Argonautica for the passage, and sets beside Homer's doves the dove that Euphemus sends through the Clashing Rocks.
---

- **The Argo.** The only ship that has sailed past the Wanderers is “the Argo, in the thoughts of all, sailing from Aeetes”; the waves would have dashed her against the great rocks, “but Hera sent her past, since Jason was dear to her” (12.69–72). Merry refers to the *Argonautica* for the description of this passage, notes that the Odyssey has the ship on its homeward voyage from Colchis, and thinks the four verses probably a later addition; Homer, he observes, says not a word of rocks threatening to close in upon the ship (W. W. Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 12.70 and 12.71, citing *Argonautica* 4.924 and following; the *Argonautica* is not read here): [[the-argo]], [[the-wanderers]], [[jason]] and [[aeetes]].
- **The dove.** Homer's doves that carry ambrosia to Zeus only “pass by” the Wanderers, and the sheer rock takes one of them each time (12.62–65). In the *Argonautica*, Merry notes, the dove is given new features: as Noah sent out a dove, so Euphemus sends one ahead to see whether the passage through the Clashing Rocks can be made; the rocks clash together and cut off the tips of its tail feathers, but it darts away unharmed (*Argonautica* 2.561 and following, as Merry quotes it, a plain rendering; Merry on 12.63): [[doves]] and [[the-wanderers]].
