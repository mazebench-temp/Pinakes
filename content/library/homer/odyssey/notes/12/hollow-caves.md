summary: Odysseus to his companions: “surely this evil that besets us is no greater than when the Cyclops / penned us in his hollow cave with his mighty strength” (12.209–210); and with κοῖλος, Scylla's “hollow cave” (12.84, 12.93) and the cave into which the ship is dragged on Thrinacia (12.317).
grc: ["κοῖλον σπέος", "σπείους κοίλοιο"]
---

ἐνὶ σπῆι γλαφυρῷ κρατερῆφι βίηφιν is the half-verse of the Cyclops' cave (9.476), here the evil the men escaped (12.211–212; see [[the-cave-of-polyphemus]] and [[krateros]]). κοῖλον σπέος and σπείους κοίλοιο are new to the entry: κοῖλος, the other word for the hollow ship, describes Scylla's cave, so high that no arrow could reach it, and the cave of the nymphs' dancing floors on Thrinacia (12.83–84, 12.93, 12.317–318; see [[scylla]], [[nymphs]] and [[hollow-ships]]). The harbor of Thrinacia is γλαφυρός too (12.305; see [[harbor]]).
