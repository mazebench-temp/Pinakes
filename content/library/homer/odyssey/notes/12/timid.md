summary: Not even “the timid doves / that carry ambrosia to father Zeus” can pass the Wanderers (12.62–63).
grc: [τρήρωνες]
refs: [12.62]
---

τρήρωνες, the nominative plural, is new to the entry. Merry: from its connection with τρεῖν the word implies the timidity that shows itself in rapid flight; Homer speaks only of the danger of passing by, and says nothing of rocks closing on the doves, which later legend develops (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 12.63; see [[doves]], [[father-zeus]] and [[the-wanderers]]).
