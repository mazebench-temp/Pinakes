When Odysseus sees smoke and a great wave and hears a roar, and the oars fly from the terrified companions' hands (12.201–205), he goes through the ship and reminds them of the cave: “surely this evil that besets us is no greater than when the Cyclops / penned us in his hollow cave with his mighty strength; / yet even from there, by my prowess and my counsel and my mind, / we escaped” (12.209–212; see [[polyphemus-the-cyclops]] and [[odysseus-and-the-cyclops]]). The words take up the taunt he shouted at the Cyclops from his ship, that it was not a weakling's companions he was “to eat in your hollow cave with your mighty strength” (9.475–476; see [[hollow-caves]]). εἴλει, “penned us”, is the verb of the Trojans penning the Achaeans in among the sterns of their ships, and of the Trojans who hemmed Odysseus in among them (*Iliad* 18.447, 11.413; W. W. Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 12.210; see the translation's key words).
