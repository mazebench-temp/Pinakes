---
summary: No arrow shot from a ship could reach Scylla's cave (12.83–84), and the two crags are so near each other that “you could even shoot an arrow across” (12.102).
# διοϊστεύσειας, "you could even shoot an arrow across" (12.102), first met here (the verb again at 19.578 and in Book
# 21, of the shot through the axes); it applies in the Odyssey only.
grc: [διοϊστεύσειας]
en: [shoot an arrow across]
---

**Out of reach.** “Not even a man in his prime, from the hollow ship, / shooting an arrow with his bow, could reach the hollow cave” (12.83–84; see [[bow]] and [[scylla]]).

**Across.** “The other crag, lower, you will see, Odysseus. / They are near one another; you could even shoot an arrow across” (12.101–102; see [[charybdis]] and [[the-fig-tree-of-charybdis]]). πλησίον stands with εἰσί understood, “they are near each other”, and καί κεν διοϊστεύσειας measures the actual gap between the crags, as W. W. Merry explains (*Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 12.102; see the translation's note on 12.101–102). διοϊστεύω, “shoot an arrow through”, is the verb of the contest that Penelope will set, to shoot through all twelve axes (19.578, a plain rendering; see [[iron]]).
