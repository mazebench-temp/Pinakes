---
summary: Odysseus credits the escape from the Cyclops to “my prowess and my counsel and my mind” (12.211), and in the strait he sees the six companions already lifted on high in Scylla's jaws (ἐνόησα, 12.248).
---

**Mind.** “yet even from there, by my prowess and my counsel and my mind, / we escaped” (ἐμῇ ἀρετῇ, βουλῇ τε νόῳ τε, 12.211–212; see [[arete]], [[boule]] and [[not-without-knowledge-of-evils]]). The three nouns sum up how he brought the companions out of the Cyclops' cave, with the stake, the name Nobody and the rams (9.318–463; see [[odysseus-and-the-cyclops]] and [[nobody]]).

**Seeing.** The verb νοέω is perception. Odysseus is looking at Charybdis when he turns toward the ship and the companions, “I saw already their feet and their hands above / as they were lifted on high” (ἤδη τῶν ἐνόησα πόδας καὶ χεῖρας ὕπερθεν, 12.248–249; see [[odysseus-passes-scylla]]). All eyes had been on Charybdis; he hears a cry, turns, and catches sight of the men swinging aloft in Scylla's clutches (W. W. Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 12.247). The seeing comes too late, as it must: he had been told six would be lost (12.109–110).
