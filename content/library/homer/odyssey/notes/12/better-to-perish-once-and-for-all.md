**The choice at sea.** Eurylochus has proposed to kill the best of the Sun's cattle, sacrifice them and vow the god a temple in Ithaca; “But if, angry over his straight-horned cattle, / he wishes to destroy our ship, and the other gods follow his lead, / I would rather lose my life once and for all with one gulp of the wave / than be slowly drained away on a desolate island” (12.348–351; see [[all-deaths-are-hateful]] and [[eurylochus]]). βούλομαι … ἤ is “I would rather … than” (W. W. Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 12.350; see [[boule]]). ἅπαξ takes the place of Ajax's ἕνα χρόνον, “at one time”, and πρὸς κῦμα χανών, “with one gasp open-mouthed at the wave”, names the death that Ajax left open: drowning, as Proteus told Menelaus that the lesser Ajax perished “when he had drunk the salt water” (4.511; Merry on 12.350). The second verse begins as Ajax's did, ἢ δηθὰ στρεύγεσθαι (*Iliad* 15.512): στρεύγεσθαι, from the stem of στράγξ, “a drop”, is perhaps to be squeezed out drop by drop, “to be drained of strength”, and the scholia explain the variant στρέγγεσθαι in the same way, “to perish drop by drop” (Merry on 12.351 and his critical note; see the Odyssey translation's table of key words).

**Granted.** Ajax chose between a quick end and survival in battle, against men he despised; Eurylochus chooses between two deaths, and the gods grant the one he prefers. The companions drown when Zeus wrecks the ship, carried on the waves “like sea crows around the black ship” (12.403–419; see [[the-cattle-of-helios]]), and Odysseus, who would not choose, is left to drift for nine days on the mast and keel (12.420–448).
