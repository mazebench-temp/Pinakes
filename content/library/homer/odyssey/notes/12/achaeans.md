---
summary: The Achaeans are named once, when the Sirens hail Odysseus as “great glory of the Achaeans” (12.184).
---

The Sirens call to him: “Come here, much-praised Odysseus, great glory of the Achaeans” (12.184; see [[sirens]]). πολύαινʼ Ὀδυσεῦ, μέγα κῦδος Ἀχαιῶν is the half-verse in which Agamemnon and Nestor ask him for his news on his return from the embassy and from the night raid (*Iliad* 9.673, 10.544; see [[great-glory-of-the-achaeans]] and [[much-praised]]): the Sirens speak to the hero of the war whose song they offer (12.189–190; see [[troy]]). Cicero renders the title “O decus Argolicum”, the Argive glory (*De Finibus* 5.49; see [[cicero]]).
