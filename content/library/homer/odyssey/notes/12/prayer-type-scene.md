---
summary: Odysseus goes up the island alone to pray: “I washed my hands, where there was shelter from the wind, / and prayed to all the gods who hold Olympus; / and they poured sweet sleep upon my eyelids” (12.336–338).
refs: [12.333-338, 12.356]
---

The prayer is told in its frame only: the washing of the hands, the gods invoked as a body, and the purpose, “in case one of them might show me a way to go home” (12.334); the answer is sleep, while Eurylochus speaks (12.338–339; see [[hand-washing]], [[homes-on-olympus]] and [[sweet-sleep]]). Merry: Odysseus withdraws because he was more likely to hold communion with the gods alone, and because the trust between him and his companions was broken, so that he could not voice his misgivings to the gods in their presence (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 12.333). The companions pray before the slaughter, with leaves for barley (12.356–357; see [[sacrifice-type-scene]]), and Eurylochus makes a vow of a temple to the Sun (12.345–347), which Nitzsch called the only vow of its kind in Homer (Merry on 12.347).
