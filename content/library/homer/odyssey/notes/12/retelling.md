---
summary: Odysseus tells his companions the oracles of Circe, but not all of them: the Sirens and the bonds of the mast, in her words turned to his own (12.154–164); “But of Scylla I said nothing more” (12.223); and on Thrinacia the warnings of Teiresias and Circe in the verses with which he had remembered them at sea (12.271–276).
refs: [12.154-164, 12.223-225, 12.271-276, 12.450-453]
---

Circe's instructions (12.39–54) come back with the changes a speaker makes: “if you yourself are willing to listen, / let them bind you” becomes “She bade me alone to hear their voice; but you must bind me” (12.49–50, 12.160), the meadow becomes “the flowering meadow” and the heap of bones is left out (12.45–46, 12.159; see [[flowering]]), and the verse of the mast-step is kept whole (12.51 = 12.162). The Wanderers and the choice of ways are not told, nor is Scylla, “a vexation past remedy,” lest the men stop rowing; Merry: he knew from Circe that he must lose six men (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 12.223). The warnings of Teiresias and Circe are summed up in the verses he remembered at sea (12.273–274 = 12.268–269) and turned into an order: “But drive the black ship on past the island” (12.276). At the end Odysseus breaks off rather than tell again what he told Arete the day before (12.450–453 ~ 7.244–297, with 12.448 = 7.254; see [[the-tale-to-alcinous]], [[sirens]] and [[scylla]]).
