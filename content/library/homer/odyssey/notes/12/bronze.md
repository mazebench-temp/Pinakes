---
summary: Odysseus cuts a great round of wax into small pieces “with the sharp bronze” (12.173–174).
---

“But I with the sharp bronze cut a great round of wax / into small pieces, and pressed them with my strong hands” (12.173–174; see [[wax]] and [[sharp]]). χαλκός alone is the blade, as in the Iliad, and ὀξέι χαλκῷ is the phrase with which Teiresias foretold that the suitors would die “openly with the sharp bronze” (11.120; see the Book 11 note). Here the blade cuts wax, and W. W. Merry finds a conscious touch of humor in the grand words that follow, the great strength and “the ray of the lord Sun, son of Hyperion”, that warm the wax (*Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 12.175; 12.175–176). It is the only bronze of the book: the arms Odysseus puts on against Scylla are only “my glorious armor” and “two long spears” (12.228; see [[spear]]).
