---
summary: On Thrinacia, after their supper, the companions “put away the desire for drink and food”, and then weep for the men Scylla took (12.308–309).
---

“But when they had put away the desire for drink and food, / then they remembered and wept for their dear companions” (αὐτὰρ ἐπεὶ πόσιος καὶ ἐδητύος ἐξ ἔρον ἕντο, 12.308–309; see [[desire-for-drink-and-food]], [[feast]] and [[tears]]). ἔρος here is the appetite, in the formula that closes the meals of both poems (*Iliad* 1.469; 1.150, 3.67, 4.68), and here the meal is followed by grief and the grief by sleep (12.311; see [[hypnos]]). A month later, when the bread and wine are gone, the same appetite drives them to the cattle (12.329–365; see [[gaster]] and [[all-deaths-are-hateful]]).
