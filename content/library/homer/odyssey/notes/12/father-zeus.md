---
summary: The doves “that carry ambrosia to father Zeus” (12.63); and twice “Father Zeus, and you other blessed gods who live forever” (12.371, 12.377).
---

Διὶ πατρί, in the account of the doves of which the rock always takes one, “and the father sends in another to make up the number” (12.62–65). Merry rejects any connection with the later story of doves feeding the infant Zeus in Crete, and finds an allusion to the Pleiads (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 12.62; see the translation's note on 12.62–65, and [[doves]], [[timid]] and [[pleiades]]). The address is Odysseus' cry when he smells the meat and the Sun's demand on Olympus (12.370–383; see [[blessed-gods]]).
