# ἔκλαιον (12.309), κλαιόντεσσι (12.311), each first met here in the Odyssey.
grc: [ἔκλαιον, κλαιόντεσσι]
---

**At the tomb.** Elpenor is buried as he asked, not “unwept” (11.72): the companions burn him “grieving and shedding big tears” (ἀχνύμενοι θαλερὸν κατὰ δάκρυ χέοντες, 12.12), the half-verse in which they went on board for the land of the dead (11.5; see [[elpenor]], [[achos]] and [[honoring-the-dead]]).

**After supper.** The men Scylla took get no funeral, only tears. On the island of the Sun the companions make ready their supper, “But when they had put away the desire for drink and food, / then they remembered and wept for their dear companions / whom Scylla had seized from the hollow ship and devoured; / and as they wept, sweet sleep came upon them” (12.308–311; see [[desire-for-drink-and-food]], [[hypnos]] and [[odysseus-passes-scylla]]). The meal comes first and the grief after, in the formula's order, and the weeping ends in sleep. It is the state of the survivors after the Cyclops and the Laestrygonians, “glad to have escaped death, though we had lost our dear companions” (9.566 = 10.134). Odysseus had seen the six taken, calling on him by name, and called it the most pitiable thing he had seen (12.248–259; see [[pity]]).
