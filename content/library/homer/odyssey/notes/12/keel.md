**The bare keel.** “But I kept pacing through the ship, until her sides / were loosed from the keel by the surge, and the wave carried it along bare, / and snapped the mast off at the keel; but over it / the backstay had been flung, made of oxhide; / with it I lashed the two together, the keel and the mast, / and sitting on them I was borne by deadly winds” (12.420–425; see [[the-shipwreck-of-odysseus]], [[mast]] and [[forestays]]). W. W. Merry renders, “till the surge loosened the sides from the keel, and a wave carried her along all dismantled, and snapped off her mast close at the keel” (*Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 12.420), and τὴν δὲ ψιλήν is the keel left bare, “the sides of the ship having been torn away from it” (Appendix I §2; see the translation's note on 12.420–423). The mast breaks “at the keel” because it was stepped into the keel's solid wood (Appendix I §12).

**Through Charybdis.** The South Wind carries Odysseus back by night to Charybdis, which sucks the timbers down; he springs up to the fig tree and clings there like a bat, “until she should vomit up again / the mast and keel; and as I yearned for them they came, / late” (12.426–439; see [[charybdis]], [[like-a-bat]] and [[the-fig-tree-of-charybdis]]). At the hour when a judge rises from the assembly for his supper “the timbers came to light out of Charybdis”, and he lets himself fall beside them, sits on them and rows with his hands (12.439–444; see [[the-judge-rising-for-supper]] and [[oars]]). δοῦρα, “timbers”, are the keel and the mast, as δόρυ is a ship's timber elsewhere (see [[spear]]); for nine days they carry him to Ogygia (12.447–448; see [[ogygia]]). This is the keel that Calypso remembered him astride, and that he told Arete he held in his arms (5.130, 7.252–253; see [[calypso]]).
