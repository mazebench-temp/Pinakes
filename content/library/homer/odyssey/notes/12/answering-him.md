---
summary: Odysseus tells his answer to Circe with “So she spoke, and I answered her and said” (12.111); Eurylochus “answered me with a hateful word” (12.278); and on Olympus, “Answering him, Zeus the cloud-gatherer said” (12.384).
---

ἀμειβόμενος προσέειπον, the first-person verse of the tale (as 10.336; see the Odyssey translation's formulas), introduces his question about Charybdis and Scylla (12.112–114). Two manuscripts note a variant ἀτυζόμενος, “distraught,” in the margin, which Buttmann thought very good, since Odysseus breaks in on Circe's speech (see the translation's note on 12.111). στυγερῷ μʼ ἠμείβετο μύθῳ is the protest of Eurylochus against sailing on past Thrinacia (12.278–293; see [[hateful]] and [[eurylochus]]). The third verse is the Iliad's frame of Zeus' replies (12.384 = *Iliad* 20.19; see [[cloud-gatherer]]), and the answer, which promises the thunderbolt, is one that Odysseus could only have heard at second hand (12.389–390; see [[messenger-scene]]).
