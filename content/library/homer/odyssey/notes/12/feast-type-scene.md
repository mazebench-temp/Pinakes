summary: The meals of the book: Circe's handmaids bring “bread and meat in plenty and sparkling red wine” to the shore, and the men feast till sunset (12.18–30); on Thrinacia the supper that Eurylochus asks for (12.283, 12.292) and the meal after the landing (12.306–311); and six days of feasting on the cattle of the Sun (12.397–398).
refs: [12.18-30, 12.292, 12.306-311, 12.397-398]
---

Circe's invitation is a verse of Book 10, “But come, eat food and drink wine” (12.23 = 10.460), and the day ends in the verses of the feasts of Books 9 and 10 (12.29–30; see [[sunset-lines]], [[untold]] and [[red]]). On Thrinacia the supper ends with the desire for food and drink put away and the eaters weeping for their companions (12.307–311; see [[desire-for-drink-and-food]]). The last feast is the forbidden one: “For six days then my trusty companions / feasted, having driven off the best of the cattle of the Sun” (12.397–398), after the hides had crawled and the meat had lowed on the spits (12.395–396; see [[sacrifice-type-scene]], [[trusty]] and [[the-cattle-of-helios]]).
