---
summary: At the sacrifice of the Sun's cattle the companions strip tender leaves from an oak, “for they had no white barley on board the well-benched ship” (12.357–358).
---

“And they stood around them and prayed to the gods, / having stripped tender leaves from a high-leafed oak; / for they had no white barley on board the well-benched ship” (12.356–358; see [[the-cattle-of-helios]], [[prayer]] and [[well-benched]]). κρῖ λευκόν, “white barley”, is the fixed phrase of the entry. In the Iliad's verse the men stand around the ox and take up the barley grains (*Iliad* 2.410); here they have none, W. W. Merry notes, and must use leaves stripped from an oak in their place, as the grains are scattered at Nestor's sacrifice (*Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 12.356; 3.441–447; see [[barley-grains]] and [[sacrifice-type-scene]]). Their provisions are spent (12.329; see [[provisions]]), and wine too is wanting, so that they pour water (12.362–363; see [[wine]]).
