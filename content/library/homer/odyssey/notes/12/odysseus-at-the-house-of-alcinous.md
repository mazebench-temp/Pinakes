**Yesterday.** On the evening of his arrival, when the leaders had gone home, Arete asked the stranger who he was and who had given him his clothes, and he told her and Alcinous how he came from Calypso's island to their shore (7.237–297). That was the night before the feast at which he now ends his tale (see [[the-tale-to-alcinous]]), and he ends it where that account began: “From there for nine days I was borne along, and on the tenth night / the gods brought me to the island of Ogygia, where Calypso / lives, she of the lovely tresses, a dread goddess with human voice” (12.447–449), nearly the verses he spoke to the queen (7.253–255). With “But why do I tell you this tale?” he turns to Alcinous and speaks to him directly (12.450; W. W. Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 12.450), naming the queen beside him, “to you and to your strong wife” (12.452; see [[alcinous]] and [[arete-queen-of-the-phaeacians]]). For “yesterday” Merry refers to his notes on that first account (on 12.451), where the scholia report that eight verses were athetized because the story is told later, in this book (Merry's critical note on 7.251–258; see the translation's note on 7.251–258 and [[the-shipwreck-of-odysseus]]).
