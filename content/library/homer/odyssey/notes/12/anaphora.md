summary: The Sirens' promise: “For we know all the things that in wide Troy / the Argives and Trojans toiled through by the will of the gods, / and we know all that comes to pass upon the bountiful earth” (12.189–191); and Charybdis: “For three times a day she sends it up, and three times she sucks it down” (12.105).
refs: [12.105, 12.189-191]
---

ἴδμεν γάρ … ἴδμεν δʼ: two clauses for the two kinds of knowledge the Sirens offer, the war at Troy and all that happens on earth (see [[sirens]] and [[bountiful-earth]]). τρὶς μέν … τρὶς δʼ, the rhythm of the whirlpool. Merry's critical note cites Polybius for δίς, “twice” (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, critical note on 12.105): as Strabo reports him, Polybius took the “three times” for a scribe's or a historian's mistake for “twice,” the two tides of the strait (Strabo, *Geography* 1.2.16), and Strabo defends it as a common kind of exaggeration (*Geography* 1.2.36; see the translation's note on 12.104–105, and [[charybdis]]).
