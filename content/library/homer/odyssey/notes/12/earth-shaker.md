---
summary: Charybdis: “for not even the earth-shaker could save you from the evil” (12.107).
---

Poseidon is named in the book only by this epithet, and only as a measure: not even the god of the sea could save a man from the whirlpool. Circe gives Scylla the same kind of measure, “not even if a god should meet her” (12.88), where Merry quotes the scholion that the poet always takes the divine as the bound of hyperbole (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 12.88; see [[poseidon]] and [[charybdis]]).
