summary: The voyages of the book in the steps of the scene: the ship run ashore on the sand at Aeaea and the men stepping out “where the sea breaks upon the shore” (12.5–7); the boarding, and the wind that Circe sends (12.144–152); the calm before the Sirens' island, the sails furled and the rowing (12.168–172, 12.180); the landing on Thrinacia, “we moored the well-made ship in the hollow harbor / near sweet water” (12.305–306), and after the storm the ship dragged into a cave (12.317); and the last departure, “setting up the mast and hoisting the white sails” (12.401–402).

The landing on Aeaea is told in the verses of the island of goats (12.5–7 = 9.546, 9.150, 9.151), and the boarding in those of the departures of Book 9 (12.145–147 = 9.178–180; again 11.637–638). 12.147, the rowing, is wanting in some manuscripts and has been bracketed by almost all editors since Wolf, since the oars were not needed with a favoring wind (see the translation's note on 12.147). The wind and the helmsman steer the ship as on the voyage to the dead (12.148–152 = 11.6–10, with 12.151 nearly the same). In the calm the companions stow the sails in the ship, so that μηρύσαντο cannot mean only “brailed up” (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 12.170). On Thrinacia the ship is first moored, not drawn up, as for a short stay, and Merry takes ἐνήσομεν as “we will put out,” not “launch” (Merry on 12.293, citing 4.785 and 2.295); after the storm the men drag it into a hollow cave (12.317; see [[hollow-caves]]). The last departure is near the verse of 9.77 (12.402), and the verses that follow, “But when we were leaving the island, and no other / land appeared, but only sky and sea,” return, with Crete for the island, in the [[the-tale-to-eumaeus|Cretan tale]] (12.403–404 ~ 14.301–302; see [[sails]], [[mast]], [[stern-cables]], [[rowing-benches]] and [[ships]]).
