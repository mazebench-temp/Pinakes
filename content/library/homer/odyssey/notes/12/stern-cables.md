---
summary: Back on Aeaea the companions lie down to sleep beside the stern-cables while Circe takes Odysseus apart (12.31–33), and at dawn, when she has gone, he bids them go on board and loose the cables (12.144–145).
---

**Asleep by the cables.** “When the sun went down and darkness came on, / the others lay down to sleep beside the stern-cables of the ship, / but she took me by the hand, away from my dear companions” (12.31–33; see [[circe]] and [[sunset-lines]]). The second verse is nearly that of the crew who sleep beside their stern-cables at Chryse after the sacrifice and the paean (*Iliad* 1.476; see the entry); here the ship is beached on the sand of Aeaea (12.5), and Circe and Odysseus are apart, probably in some grotto, Merry thinks, since she goes back up the island afterward (W. W. Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 12.33; 12.143).

**Loosed.** At dawn “I went to the ship and urged on my companions / to go on board themselves and loose the stern-cables” (12.144–145; see [[ships]] and [[rowing-benches]]), the order of departure in Book 9 and at the limits of Oceanus (9.178, 9.562, 11.637; see the Book 11 note). The ship is not moored again until the harbor of Thrinacia (12.305).
