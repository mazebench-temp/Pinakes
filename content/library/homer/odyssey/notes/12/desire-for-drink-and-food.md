---
summary: On Thrinacia, “But when they had put away the desire for drink and food, / then they remembered and wept for their dear companions / whom Scylla had seized from the hollow ship and devoured” (12.308–310).
---

The verse that closes the meals at Ithaca, Pylos and Sparta (1.150, 3.67, 3.473, 4.68) here closes the first supper after the strait, and the eating gives way to grief for the six whom Scylla took, until sleep comes upon the weeping men (12.306–311; see [[shedding-tears]], [[sweet-sleep]] and [[feast-type-scene]]).
