---
summary: The first epithet of Calypso, “the lady nymph Calypso, brilliant among goddesses” (1.14).
---

The proem leaves Odysseus in the keeping of “the lady nymph Calypso, brilliant among goddesses” (1.14; [[queenly]]), who holds him in her hollow caves (1.15; [[hollow-caves]]). Odysseus uses the same verse-end of her when he tells the Phaeacians who he is (9.29). See [[calypso]].
