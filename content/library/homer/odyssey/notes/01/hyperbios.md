---
summary: Telemachus' first words to the suitors as master of the house: “Suitors of my mother, overweening in your arrogance” (1.368).
---

The address names the suitors by their relation to his mother and by their ὕβρις together (μνηστῆρες ὑπέρβιον ὕβριν ἔχοντες; [[hybris]]). Telemachus repeats the verse word for word at Sparta, when he tells Menelaus what is happening in his house, after the verse of the slaughtered flocks that Athena spoke in the council (4.320–321 = 1.92, 1.368); and in Book 16 Penelope resolves to show herself to the suitors, “who have overweening arrogance” (16.409–410).
