---
summary: Book 1 names Penelope's father twice and places him nowhere. Athena tells Telemachus to send his mother, if she wishes to marry, back to the hall of her father, “a man of great power”, and the narrator calls her “the daughter of Icarius” when she first appears. Where the hall was is the question that Aristotle and Strabo answer in different ways.
refs: [1.275-278, 1.329]
---

**The two lines.** Athena's counsel to Telemachus begins with the suitors and his mother: “and your mother, if her heart is eager to marry, / let her go back to the hall of her father, a man of great power; / and they will arrange a wedding and make ready the bride-price” (1.275–277; see [[athena-visits-telemachus]]). Merry notes that where Icarius dwelt was an old question, and gives Aristotle's answer and Strabo's (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 1.276). Whether “they” who arrange the wedding are the bride's family, as Nitzsch held after Eustathius, or the suitors is disputed (Merry on 1.277; see the translation's note on 1.275–278). The name itself comes when Penelope hears Phemius' song from her upper room: “the daughter of Icarius, prudent Penelope” (1.329; see [[daughter-of-icarius]] and [[icarius]]).

**Two answers.** The critics whom Aristotle answers took Icarius for a Laconian, and found it absurd that Telemachus did not meet him when he went to Lacedaemon; Aristotle answers with the Cephallenians' story that Odysseus married among them and that her father was Icadius (*Poetics* 25, 1461b4–9; see the entry above). Strabo argues from the same silence to another place. Some Lacedaemonians, he says, settled in Acarnania with Icarius, Penelope's father, and the Odyssey presents him and her brothers as living, since the suitors shrink from going to his house and her father and brothers urge her to marry Eurymachus; it is not plausible that they lived in Lacedaemon, “for Telemachus would not have lodged with Menelaus when he came there”, and no other home of theirs is recorded. The story is that Tyndareus and Icarius, driven from home by Hippocoon, went to Thestius, ruler of the Pleuronians, and helped him win much of the land beyond the Achelous; Tyndareus went home, having married Thestius' daughter Leda, while Icarius stayed on with a part of Acarnania, and there had Penelope by Polycaste, daughter of Lygaeus, and her brothers (*Geography* 10.2.24, quoting *Odyssey* 2.52–53 and 15.16–17; see [[strabo]]).
