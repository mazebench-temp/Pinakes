summary: The sea is the place of Odysseus' sufferings and of the others' escape; he is held on an island “where the navel of the sea is”, and Telemachus fears that the wave rolls his bones. Atlas knows its depths and Phorcys rules over it (1.4, 1.12, 1.50–53, 1.72, 1.162, 1.183, 1.197).
# πόντῳ (1.4, 1.197), as in the Iliad's Book 15 note, and ἁλὶ, "in the sea" (1.162).
grc: [πόντῳ, ἁλὶ]
---

**The sea of the wanderings.** The man of many turns “on the sea … suffered many sorrows in his heart” (1.4); the others who came home escaped “both the war and the sea” (1.12). Odysseus is held back “on the wide sea, / on a sea-washed island” (1.197–198), “where the navel of the sea is” (1.50; see [[ogygia]]). Telemachus imagines his father's white bones rotting in the rain on the mainland, “or the wave rolls them in the sea” (1.161–162); the bones, Merry notes, are the subject of the first verb and become the object of the second, where the sentence would naturally run ἢ κύματι κυλίνδεται, “or are rolled by the wave” (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 1.162).

**Its powers.** Atlas “knows the depths / of the whole sea” (1.52–53; see [[atlas]]), and Phorcys, the grandfather of Polyphemus, “rules over the barren sea” (1.72; see [[phorcys-the-sea-god]] and [[barren-sea]]). Poseidon, the earth-shaker, keeps Odysseus wandering (1.68–75; see [[poseidon]]).

**The trader's sea.** The stranger has come “sailing over the wine-dark sea to men of another tongue” (1.183; see [[wine-dark]] and [[temese]]).
