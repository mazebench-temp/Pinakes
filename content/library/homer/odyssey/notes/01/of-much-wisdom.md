---
summary: Athena asks the gods to let “Odysseus of much wisdom” return to his own house (1.83).
---

The epithet comes in the plan that sets the poem moving: if the blessed gods wish Odysseus to return, let Hermes go to Calypso and tell her their unerring counsel (1.82–87; [[blessed-gods]]). In the same book the stranger calls him “a man of many devices” ([[of-many-devices]]), and the proem “the man of many turns” ([[of-many-turns]]). See [[odysseus]].
