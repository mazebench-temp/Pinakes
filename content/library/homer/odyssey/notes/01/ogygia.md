---
summary: In the council of the gods Athena pleads for Odysseus, held on a sea-washed island “where the navel of the sea is” by the daughter of Atlas, and proposes that Hermes be sent “to the island of Ogygia” to tell the nymph the gods' counsel (1.48–59, 1.84–87).
---

**The island unnamed.** Athena describes the island before she names it. Odysseus suffers woes far from his friends (1.48–49):

{{quote:13bb7b8c-8fef-53b6-b7fe-cb98e9816b53}}

The τε of ὅθι τε, Merry explains, marks a lasting feature of the place, one that belongs to it alone (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 1.50), and the sentence starts afresh with νῆσος, “It is an island”, after νήσῳ, a break in the construction that he compares with the verses on Eetion at *Iliad* 6.395–396 (on 1.51). The goddess who lives there is the daughter of “baleful Atlas”, who knows the depths of the whole sea and holds the pillars that keep earth and heaven apart (1.52–54; see [[atlas]] and [[calypso]]). She keeps Odysseus with gentle and wheedling talk, so that he may forget Ithaca, while he longs to see even the smoke leaping up from his own land (1.55–59). The narrator has already said where: “in her hollow caves” (1.14–15).

**The name.** When Zeus has agreed, Athena proposes that Hermes go “to the island of Ogygia” and tell the nymph the gods' “unerring counsel, / the homecoming of stout-hearted Odysseus” (1.84–87; see [[hermes]] and [[the-council-of-the-gods]]). The errand waits until Book 5, where Zeus sends Hermes with the same words (5.29–31); in this book Athena goes first to Ithaca (1.88–89).

**In the stranger's tale.** Disguised as Mentes, Athena gives Telemachus another picture: his father is alive, “held back on the wide sea, / on a sea-washed island, and harsh men hold him, / savage men, who keep him back against his will” (1.197–199). The half-verse of the council returns (1.50), but the nymph has become a band of savage men (see [[mentes-the-taphian]] and [[sea-washed]]).
