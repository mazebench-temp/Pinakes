summary: Athena, in the likeness of a stranger, comes down from Olympus and stands at the doorway of Odysseus' house, on the threshold of the courtyard, until Telemachus sees her (1.103–104).
# οὐδοῦ (1.104): οὐδός, the threshold of a house; the Iliad's forms are in its notes.
grc: [οὐδοῦ]
---

Athena “stood in the land of Ithaca at the doorway of Odysseus, / on the threshold of the courtyard” (1.103–104), οὐδοῦ ἐπʼ αὐλείου, the threshold of the gate into the courtyard, “in the outer porch at the threshold of the court”, as W. W. Merry renders the two phrases (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 1.103; see [[door]] and [[house]]). There a stranger waits to be received, and Telemachus is indignant that one should stand long at the doors (1.119–120; see [[xenia]]). The house has other thresholds: the stone threshold of the hall, which Telemachus crosses when he comes home (*Odyssey* 17.30), the threshold of ash wood inside the doors where Odysseus sits as a beggar (*Odyssey* 17.339), and the great threshold onto which he leaps, stripped of his rags, to shoot the suitors (*Odyssey* 22.2).
