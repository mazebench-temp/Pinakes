{{quote:30dcb255-4540-5f2b-93da-83f4fd03fe53}}

Mentes' tale gives his ship a place on the island. ἥδε, “over there”, is said with a gesture, as Merry explains, and ἐπʼ ἀγροῦ, “by the fields”, leaves open whether the ship is drawn up on land or lies afloat beside the farmland: ἀγρός is tilled land, not the beach. Merry sets beside it the ship of Telemachus' companions, drawn up “on the mainland” inside the harbor (ἐπʼ ἠπείροιο, 16.324–325), and Antinous' plan to waylay Telemachus ἐπʼ ἀγροῦ νόσφι πόληος, “in the fields, away from the city” (16.383), and he supposes that the ship lay on the margin of a creek that served as a harbor, well within the coast line, an idea that the name Rheithron, “stream”, seems to bear out (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 1.185; see the translation's note on 1.185). The same words, ἐπʼ ἀγροῦ, place Laertes on his farm a few verses later, “far away in the country” (1.189–190; see [[laertes]]). See [[neion]], [[ithaca]] and [[mentes-the-taphian]].
