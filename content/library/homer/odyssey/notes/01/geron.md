---
summary: Laertes is “the old man” who no longer comes to the city (1.188–189), and Telemachus wishes he had been the son of a man “whom old age overtook among his own possessions” (1.218).
grc: [γέροντʼ]
---

Old age at home among one's possessions is the blessing Telemachus wishes for a father, set against the vanished Odysseus (1.217–220; see [[fathers-and-sons]] and [[old-age-and-memory]]). Laertes has the old age without the blessing: far away in the country he “suffers woes / with an old handmaid”, who brings him food and drink when weariness takes him on the slope of his vineyard (1.190–193; see [[laertes]]). Merry takes ἐπʼ ἀγροῦ πήματα πάσχειν as “lives a hard life on his farm”, not of grief for his son (*Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 1.190).
