summary: Iron is the cargo Mentes carries to Temese to trade for bronze, and the image of the bonds that will not keep Odysseus from coming home (1.184, 1.204).
# σιδήρεα (1.204); σίδηρον (1.184) is caught by the entry.
grc: [σιδήρεα]
---

**A cargo of iron.** Mentes sails to Temese “for bronze”, and carries “gleaming iron” to pay for it (1.184; see [[temese]] and [[trade]]). Iron here is goods for exchange, as when the Achaeans buy their wine from Lemnos “some with bronze, and some with gleaming iron” (*Iliad* 7.473).

**Bonds of iron.** The stranger foretells that Odysseus “will not be away much longer from his dear native land, / not even if bonds of iron hold him” (1.203–204; see [[divination]]). Iron is the image of what holds fast, as a heart of iron in the Iliad is one that does not yield (*Iliad* 22.357, 24.205, 24.521; see [[not-stone-or-iron]]). δέσματα is the subject of ἔχῃσι, Merry notes, and Cobet, missing an object, would write εἴ πέρ ϝε, with ϝε, “him” (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 1.204).
