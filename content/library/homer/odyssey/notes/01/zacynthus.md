---
summary: “Wooded Zacynthus” is the last of the three islands whose best men woo Penelope (1.246).
---

Telemachus names the suitors' islands “Dulichium and Same and wooded Zacynthus” (1.246; see [[the-suitors]]). The adjective ὑλήεντι, “wooded”, has here the same form for the feminine as for the masculine, one of the adjectives that Homer uses with two endings, where Book 9 has the feminine, ὑλήεσσα Ζάκυνθος (9.24; Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 1.420). Merry also notes that the final vowel of ὑλήεντι stays short before the Ζ, as before Ζέλειαν and Ζελείης in the Iliad (on 1.246; *Iliad* 2.824, 4.103). See [[wooded]].
