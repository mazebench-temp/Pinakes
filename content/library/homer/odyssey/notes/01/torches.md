---
summary: Eurycleia carries the blazing torches before Telemachus as he goes to his chamber at night; the verse is said twice, before and after the story of her purchase (1.428, 1.434).
---

“And with him, carrying blazing torches, went true-hearted / Eurycleia” (1.428–429), and after the narrator has told how Laertes bought her, the verse returns: “she carried the blazing torches with him” (1.434; see [[eurycleia]]). The repetition closes the story of her history and brings the narrative back to the night. τῷ … φέρε is “carried for him” (W. W. Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 1.428). Telemachus goes to bed by the same light on the night before the killing of the suitors, “under blazing torches” (*Odyssey* 19.47–48; see [[bed]]).
