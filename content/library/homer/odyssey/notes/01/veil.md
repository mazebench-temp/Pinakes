# The phrase σχομένη λιπαρὰ κρήδεμνα (1.334; again 16.416, 18.210, 21.65, always Penelope). A bare κρήδεμνα would also
# catch the "shining battlements" of Troy (Τροίης λιπαρὰ κρήδεμνα, Od. 13.388).
grc: ["σχομένη λιπαρὰ κρήδεμνα"]
en: [veil]
---

When Penelope comes to the suitors, she stands by the doorpost of the hall, “holding her shining veil before her cheeks” (1.334; see [[the-song-of-the-returns]] and [[door]]), with a handmaid on either side (1.335; see [[handmaids]]). The κρήδεμνον (κάρα, “head”, and δέω, “bind”) was probably, W. W. Merry suggests, a broad piece of fine linen tied round the head with a ribbon, with two broad bands hanging down that could be drawn across to veil the eyes and cheeks; σχομένη is “after she had drawn” it (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 1.334). He compares Helen, who “veiled herself in white linen” before she went out to the wall (*Iliad* 3.141). The verse comes back each time Penelope shows herself to the suitors (*Odyssey* 16.416, 18.210, 21.65).
