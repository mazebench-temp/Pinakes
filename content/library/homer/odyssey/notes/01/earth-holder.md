---
summary: Zeus: “But Poseidon the earth-holder is stubbornly forever / angry because of the Cyclops” (1.68–69).
grc: [γαιήοχος]
---

The name and epithet open Zeus' explanation of why Odysseus cannot come home: the god's anger for the blinding of his son Polyphemus (1.68–75; [[polyphemus-the-cyclops]]). Six verses later the same god is “Poseidon the earth-shaker” ([[earth-shaker]]). Merry reports three explanations of γαιήοχος: “earth-surrounding,” as the older commentators had it, though it is doubtful that ἔχειν can mean this; “earth-supporting,” the earth resting on the sea; and Autenrieth's “lord of earth” (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 1.68; see the translation's note on 1.68). See [[poseidon]].
