# οἰνοχοεύων (1.143) is first met here in the Odyssey.
grc: [οἰνοχοεύων]
en: [pouring wine]
---

At the guest's meal “a herald went to and fro, pouring wine for them” (1.143; see [[heralds]]): αὐτοῖσιν, “for them”, goes with οἰνοχοεύων, and means the guest and Telemachus, who sat at meat with him (W. W. Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 1.143). In the Iliad the cupbearer is young, the young men at Chryse, Hebe or Ganymede among the gods; here the pourer is a herald, the servant of the house (see [[feast-type-scene]]). For the suitors the young men do the work of Chryse: “and the young men filled the mixing bowls brim-full with drink” (1.148; *Iliad* 1.470; see [[mixing-bowl]]).
