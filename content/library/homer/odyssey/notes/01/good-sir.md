---
summary: Eurymachus to Telemachus: “But I want to ask you, good sir, about the stranger” (1.405).
---

Eurymachus answers more smoothly than Antinous: he leaves the kingship to the gods, wishes Telemachus his possessions, and then, with the courteous address, asks about the visitor who has just left so quickly (1.400–411). In the *Iliad* φέριστε is said three times to a stranger who is, or may be, a god (*Iliad* 6.123, 15.247, 24.387); here it is said to Telemachus about a stranger who was a goddess, though Eurymachus does not know it. Telemachus does: he answers with the stranger's own story, “but in his heart he knew the immortal goddess” (1.412–420). Compare the stranger's question ([[who-are-you-among-men]]).
