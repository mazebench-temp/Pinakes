# ἠλακάτην (1.357) and the phrase ἔργον ἐποίχεσθαι (1.358), claimed for the Iliad by its notes.
grc: [ἠλακάτην, "ἔργον ἐποίχεσθαι"]
en: [the distaff, to go about their work]
---

“But go into the house and attend to your own work, / the loom and the distaff, and tell your handmaids / to go about their work” (1.356–358; see [[loom]], [[handmaids]] and [[war-is-the-concern-of-men]]). The distaff, ἠλακάτη, holds the wool for spinning, and the loom is for weaving: the two are the women's work of the house, from the queen to her slaves, as in Hector's verses to Andromache that Telemachus repeats (*Iliad* 6.490–492). ἐποίχεσθαι, “to go about” the work, is properly to move to and fro, as the weaver walks along the upright loom (W. W. Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 1.358). Penelope is often seen at this work in the Odyssey: sitting with her distaff by the doorpost of the hall (*Odyssey* 17.96–97), and weaving the shroud for Laertes that she unwove by night (*Odyssey* 2.93–110; see [[penelope]]).
