---
summary: “Brilliant Odysseus” three times, once alive in the stranger's words and twice dead in Telemachus' (1.196, 1.396, 1.398); and brilliant Nestor (1.284), brilliant Orestes (1.298), and Penelope, “brilliant among women” (1.332).
grc: ["=δῖον", "δῖα γυναικῶν"]
---

The verse-end δῖος Ὀδυσσεύς says two opposite things in this book. The stranger assures Telemachus, “For brilliant Odysseus has not yet died upon the earth” (1.196); at the end of the day Telemachus tells Antinous that the kingship may go to another “since brilliant Odysseus is dead” (1.396), and that he will be lord of the slaves “whom brilliant Odysseus took as spoil for me” (1.398).

Athena sends Telemachus to “brilliant Nestor” at Pylos (1.284; [[nestor]]) and holds up the fame of “brilliant Orestes” (1.298; [[orestes]]). Penelope, coming down to the hall, is δῖα γυναικῶν, “brilliant among women” (1.332), the formula that the *Iliad* gives to Helen (*Iliad* 3.171, 3.228, 3.423) and once to Alcestis (*Iliad* 2.714). Calypso is “brilliant among goddesses” in the verse-end of 1.14 ([[brilliant-among-goddesses]]).
