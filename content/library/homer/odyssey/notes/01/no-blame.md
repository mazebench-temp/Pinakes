---
summary: Telemachus tells Penelope, “There is no blame on this man for singing the evil fate of the Danaans” (1.350), in the words of the Trojan elders: no one may blame the singer for the sorrows of the war, as no one could blame Trojans and Achaeans for suffering them.
---

οὐ νέμεσις stands at these two places only in Homer (*Iliad* 3.156; *Odyssey* 1.350), and both are about the war at Troy: the elders excuse the suffering for Helen, Telemachus the song that tells it (see [[nemesis]] and [[the-song-of-the-returns]]). Merry renders the phrase “no ill-will can be felt” (*Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 1.350).
