Between the goddess who wants him and the wife who mourns him, the book sets the question of a new husband for Penelope: Athena bids Telemachus, if his father is dead, “give your mother to a husband” (ἀνέρι, 1.292; see [[marriage]] and [[the-suitors]]). Its first example is the other marriage of the war's end: Aegisthus married the wife of the son of Atreus and killed her husband on his return (1.35–36; see [[the-murder-of-agamemnon]]).
