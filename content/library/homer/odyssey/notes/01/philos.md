---
summary: Telemachus greets the stranger, “Here with us you will be treated as a friend” (φιλήσεαι, 1.123), and the goddess calls him “my friend” (1.301); he thanks her for speaking “in friendship, / as a father to his son” (1.307–308). Anchialus loved Odysseus “terribly” (1.264), and Eurycleia loved Telemachus “most / of the slave women” (1.434–435).
grc: [φίλοισι, φίλων, φίλου, φίλʼ, φίλης, φίλοι, φιλέεσκε, φιλήσεαι]
---

Merry notes that φιλέω is first the showing of friendship, of which hospitality is the most definite form, without excluding the feeling (*Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 1.123; see [[xenia]]).
