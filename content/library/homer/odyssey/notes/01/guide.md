---
summary: Athena proposes to send Hermes, “the guide, the slayer of Argus,” to Ogygia (1.84).
grc: [διάκτορον]
---

The epithet comes in the plan that sends the gods' messenger to Calypso with their unerring counsel, the homecoming of Odysseus (1.84–87; [[argeiphontes]], [[messenger-scene]]). Merry reports the ancient explanations: Eustathius takes διάκτορος as the one who carries messages through, from διάγω; Buttmann refers it to διώκω, “run,” as a runner, comparing διάκονος; and Nitzsch keeps διάγω in the sense of “conduct”; Merry compares Hermes guiding Heracles on his quest for Cerberus (11.625; Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 1.84). See [[hermes]].
