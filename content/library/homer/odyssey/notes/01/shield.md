---
summary: The stranger wishes that Odysseus would come home and stand at the outer doors “with helmet and shield and two spears”, such as he was when Mentes first saw him (1.255–257).
---

Athena, as Mentes, pictures the hero armed against the suitors: “If only he would come now and stand at the outer doors of the house, / with helmet and shield and two spears” (1.255–256; see [[helmet]], [[spear]] and [[odysseus-seeks-poison]]). πήληξ, ἀσπίς and δύο δοῦρε are the arms of the Iliad's warriors, who often carry two spears (*Iliad* 3.18, 11.43, 12.298); beside Nestor's bed lie his shield, two spears and shining helmet (*Iliad* 10.76). The wish looks ahead to the end of the poem, when Odysseus stands on the threshold of his hall with the bow and then with shield, helmet and two spears (22.122–125; see [[odysseus]] and [[the-suitors]]).
