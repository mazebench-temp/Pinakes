---
summary: “Godlike Telemachus” sees the stranger first, as he sits among the suitors grieving at heart (1.113).
grc: [θεοειδής]
---

The epithet introduces Telemachus into the poem, and the verses that follow show him as he is before the goddess comes: sitting among the suitors, seeing his father in his mind, imagining him come home to scatter them (1.113–117). When she leaves he will be “a man equal to the gods” (1.324; [[equal-to-the-gods]]). See [[telemachus]].
