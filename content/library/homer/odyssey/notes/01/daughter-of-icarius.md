---
summary: Penelope first appears in the poem under the full verse, “the daughter of Icarius, prudent Penelope,” as she hears Phemius' song from her upper room (1.328–329).
---

Penelope has been named before, by the stranger (1.223), and her father's hall in Athena's counsel to Telemachus: if her heart is eager to marry, “let her go back to the hall of her father, a man of great power” (1.275–276; [[icarius]]). When she comes into the story herself, the narrator gives her the whole formula:

{{quote:32d4fb43-1f8c-591a-8bdb-ade2c47a733d}}

Her father's name and her own epithet frame her entrance: she comes down from the upper room with two handmaids, stands by the doorpost holding her veil before her cheeks, and asks Phemius to stop his song (1.330–344; [[periphron]], [[the-song-of-the-returns]]). The formula comes back twelve more times in the poem (see the entry). See [[penelope]].
