---
summary: Telemachus sets “an inlaid chair” for himself beside the stranger, apart from the suitors (1.132).
grc: [ποικίλον]
---

The host's own seat is a κλισμός, set beside the stranger's chair so that the two may talk apart from the din of the suitors (1.130–135; [[throne]], [[intricately-worked]]). Merry, after a scholium, distinguishes the κλισμός, with a sloping back, from the θρόνος, which stood higher and was the chair of state (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 1.132; see the translation's note on 1.132).
