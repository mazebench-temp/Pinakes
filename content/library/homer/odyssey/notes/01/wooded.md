---
summary: Mount Neion, under which the stranger's ship lies, is “wooded” (1.186), and so is Zacynthus, among the islands whose nobles woo Penelope (1.246).
grc: [ὑλήεντι]
---

The translation keeps “wooded” for ὑλήεις, as in the Iliad, and “full of trees” for δενδρήεις ([[full-of-trees]]). Merry notes ὑλήεντι Ζακύνθῳ, the masculine form with a feminine noun, beside ὑλήεσσα Ζάκυνθος elsewhere, as one of the adjectives that Homer can use with two endings (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 1.420). See [[neion]], [[rheithron]] and [[zacynthus]].
