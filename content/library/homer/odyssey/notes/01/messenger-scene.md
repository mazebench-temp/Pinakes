summary: A message reported in the council: the gods sent Hermes to warn Aegisthus, and Zeus quotes the warning as Hermes gave it, “So Hermes spoke” (1.37–43); and Athena plans to send Hermes to Calypso (1.84–87).

---

Zeus tells the whole errand in a few verses: the gods sent Hermes, the message was neither to kill Agamemnon nor to woo his wife, and its reason is given in Hermes' own words, “for from Orestes vengeance will come for the son of Atreus, / when he comes of age and longs for his own land” (1.37–41); then “So Hermes spoke, but the mind of Aegisthus / he did not persuade” (1.42–43; [[so-he-spoke]], [[keen-sighted]]). Merry notes the change from reported to direct speech at 1.40 (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 1.40). The message was delivered, and refused. Athena's plan sets up another errand for the same messenger, to tell Calypso the gods' unerring counsel (1.84–87), which is carried out in Book 5. See [[hermes]] and [[aegisthus]].
