# ὥς τε πατὴρ ᾧ παιδί (1.308).
grc: ["ὥς τε πατὴρ ᾧ παιδί"]
en: [as a father to his son]
---

“Stranger, truly you say these things in friendship, / as a father to his son, and I will never forget them” (1.307–308; see [[telemachus]] and [[athena]]). φίλα φρονέων, “in friendship”, is literally “minding kind things”, and the same words are in the long simile of Book 16, where Eumaeus welcomes Telemachus home from Pylos as a father with a loving heart (φίλα φρονέων) welcomes his only son, come back from a distant land in the tenth year (16.17–19). Here, as there and as in Phoenix's story (*Iliad* 9.480–482), the one who loves as a father is not the father. The stranger has just given a father's counsel: to call the assembly, send the suitors home and his mother back to her father's house, sail for news of Odysseus, and, if he is dead, raise his mound, give his mother to a husband and kill the suitors, as Orestes killed his father's murderer (1.269–302; see [[orestes]]). Telemachus has no father at hand to give it. He has told the stranger, “My mother says that I am his; but I for my part / do not know” (1.215–216), and the stranger has answered that he is “terribly like him” in his head and fine eyes (1.208–209; see [[fathers-and-sons]]).

The comparison comes back in scorn. When Antinous rebukes the swineherd for bringing the beggar who is Odysseus into the hall, Telemachus tells him that he cares for him finely, as a father for his son, πατὴρ ὣς κήδεαι υἷος, since he bids him drive the stranger from the hall with a word of compulsion (17.374–399). Odysseus himself is remembered on Ithaca as a king who was kind to his people as a father (2.46–47, 2.230–234; see [[kind-as-a-father]]).
