summary: Phemius sings to the suitors “the homecoming of the Achaeans” (1.326), and Penelope, who knows the deeds “which singers make famous” (1.338), asks him to sing another. Telemachus defends him: “men praise that song the more / which comes newest to those who listen” (1.351–352).
refs: [1.325-327, 1.337-338, 1.346-352]
---

The song of Book 1 is the matter of the poem itself, sung before those who are living it: the Trojan war, Merry notes, had become ten years after “matter of national history” (*Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 1.326; see [[the-song-of-the-returns]] and [[phemius]]). Penelope hears it from her upper room and comes down weeping, since the singer's subject is her own grief (1.328–344; see [[penthos]]). Phemius sings among the suitors “under compulsion” (1.154), and Telemachus defends his freedom to give delight “in whatever way his mind is roused” (1.347; see [[noos]]), and the praise that men give the newest song (1.351–352), which Plato quotes, as Merry reports, with variants (on 1.351; see the translation's note on 1.351–352, [[plato-the-newest-song]] and [[kleos]]).
