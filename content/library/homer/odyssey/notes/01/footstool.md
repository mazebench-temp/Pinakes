summary: Telemachus seats the stranger on a fine chair of honor with a footstool under her feet, in the verse with which Charis seats Thetis in the house of Hephaestus (1.130–131).
# θρῆνυς (1.131), claimed in the Iliad only by the Book 18 note.
grc: [θρῆνυς]
---

Telemachus leads the stranger in and seats her “on a chair—spreading a cloth beneath her— / fine and intricately worked; and there was a footstool under her feet” (1.130–131; see [[throne]]). The second verse is the Iliad's, where Charis seats Thetis on a silver-studded throne in the house of Hephaestus (*Iliad* 18.389–390; see the translation's note on 1.130), and the Odyssey repeats it when Helen sits down to hear her guests and when Circe seats Odysseus (*Odyssey* 4.136, 10.315, 10.367). καλὸν δαιδάλεον, “fine and intricately worked”, belongs to the chair, not to the cloth, if the words about the cloth are taken as a parenthesis, as W. W. Merry advises (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 1.130). The footstool marks the seat of honor: Telemachus' own chair, set beside the guest's, is a lower κλισμός, and no footstool is named for it (1.132; see [[xenia]]).
