---
summary: Athena pleads for Odysseus by his sacrifices: “Did not Odysseus / please you beside the ships of the Argives, offering sacrifices / in wide Troy? Why are you so angry with him, Zeus?” (1.60–62). Zeus answers that he could not forget a man “first in sacrifices / given to the immortal gods” (1.66–67).
refs: [1.60-62, 1.65-67]
---

The plea has the shape of a prayer's claim on a god, the recalling of past gifts (see [[prayer]]), and Zeus accepts it: the gifts are remembered, and what stands in the way is another god's anger (1.68–79; see [[cholos]]). χαρίζετο is the verb of a favor that earns a return (see [[charis]]); Merry notes that the elided τοι of 1.60 is the dative it governs (*Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 1.60), and renders Zeus' answer “beyond [all others] gave sacrifices to the gods” (on 1.66). The god who stands in the way is himself away receiving sacrifice, gone to the Ethiopians “to share in a hecatomb of bulls and rams” (1.22–26; see [[hecatomb]] and [[poseidon]]).
