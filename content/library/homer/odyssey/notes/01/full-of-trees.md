---
summary: Athena describes Calypso's island to Zeus: “It is an island full of trees, and a goddess has her dwelling there” (1.51).
---

The sentence starts again in the middle of Athena's speech. She has put Odysseus “on a sea-washed island” in the dative (1.50; [[sea-washed]]), and the next verse takes the island up anew in the nominative, νῆσος δενδρήεσσα; Merry calls it an anacoluthon and compares the way Andromache's father is named and then taken up again at the head of the next verse, “the daughter of great-hearted Eetion, / Eetion who lived beneath wooded Placus” (*Iliad* 6.395–396; Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 1.51). The two epithets, the sea around the island and the trees on it, lead to the goddess and to her father, Atlas (1.51–54; [[calypso]], [[ogygia]], [[oloophron]]).
