---
summary: Eurycleia hangs Telemachus' tunic on a peg “beside the corded bed” (1.440), in the last scene of the book.
---

The bed is λέχεα in the plural, τρητοῖσι λέχεσσι. Merry takes the epithet of the bedstead, not the bedding, and explains it as “morticed”: the frame was put together of many pieces, the supports fitting into holes in the cross-pieces, which is why the plural is used; he cites Plato's distinction between things joined by boring (τρητά) and things bound together without it (*Statesman* 279e), against the view that the holes took the cords or straps on which the bedding lay (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 1.440; see the translation's note on 1.440). The translation keeps the Iliad's rendering, “the corded bed” (*Iliad* 3.448). See [[bed]].
