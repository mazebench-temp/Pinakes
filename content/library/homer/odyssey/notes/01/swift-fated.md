---
summary: Athena wishes that Odysseus might come and face the suitors: “they would all be swift-fated, and bitter would their wedding be” (1.266).
grc: [ὠκύμοροι]
---

The verse closes the stranger's wish that Odysseus would stand at the doors with helmet, shield and two spears, as she once saw him (1.255–266). Its two verses, “if only Odysseus, such as that, would engage the suitors: / they would all be swift-fated, and bitter would their wedding be” (1.265–266), are Menelaus' wish at Sparta (4.345–346), which Telemachus repeats to his mother (17.136–137). The Iliad's word for Achilles' short life is turned on the suitors, and paired with πικρόγαμοι, “bitter in their marriage,” which Merry reports Nitzsch took for one of the words coined in anger for the occasion (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 1.266).
