summary: In the tale Athena tells as Mentes, the Taphian is sailing “to men of another tongue, / to Temese, for bronze”, with a cargo of “gleaming iron” (1.183–184).
---

**Iron for bronze.** Telemachus has asked the stranger on what ship he came and how sailors brought him to Ithaca (1.170–173). The answer is a trader's errand:

{{quote:44edc3cd-ece1-54f5-b152-433c94fa6614}}

The voyage is an exchange of metals. χαλκός is the metal copper as well as its alloy, bronze, and Merry, who places Temese at the copper mines of Tamassus in Cyprus, understands copper (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 1.184); the translation keeps “bronze”, as everywhere (see the translation's note on 1.184, [[bronze]] and [[trade]]). “Gleaming iron”, αἴθων σίδηρος, is the iron of the Iliad's world of work and trade: a chariot-maker fells a poplar “with the gleaming iron” (*Iliad* 4.485), and the Achaeans buy their wine from Lemnos “some with bronze, and some with gleaming iron” (*Iliad* 7.473; see [[iron]]). The people of Temese are ἀλλόθροοι, “of another tongue”, a word the Odyssey uses again of the foreigners among whom Menelaus and Odysseus wander and to whom slaves are sold (3.302, 14.43, 15.453). Where Temese was the poem does not say: Strabo looked for it in Bruttium, at Tempsa, and Merry in Cyprus, at Tamassus.
