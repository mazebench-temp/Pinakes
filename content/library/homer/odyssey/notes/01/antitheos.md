---
summary: The epithet that comes with Odysseus' name the first time the poem speaks it, “godlike Odysseus” (1.21), and that Zeus gives to the Cyclops whom Odysseus blinded, “godlike Polyphemus” (1.70).
grc: [ἀντιθέῳ]
---

The proem calls its hero only “the man of many turns” ([[of-many-turns]]), and his name first comes twenty verses later, with this epithet: Poseidon “raged relentlessly / against godlike Odysseus, until he reached his own land” (1.20–21). The same verse returns in Book 6, when Athena keeps out of his sight out of respect for her father's brother (6.329–331). Fifty lines on Zeus gives the epithet to Poseidon's son, the Cyclops, “godlike Polyphemus, whose power is the greatest / among all the Cyclopes” (1.70–71; [[polyphemus-the-cyclops]]). The word measures rank and strength, not goodness: the hero and the monster who will be his enemy are both a match for the gods. See [[odysseus]].
