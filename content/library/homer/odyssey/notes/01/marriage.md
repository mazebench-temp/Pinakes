---
summary: Laertes bought Eurycleia and “honored her in his halls as he honored his devoted wife, / but he never lay with her in bed, for he shunned his wife's anger” (1.432–433); Athena counsels that Penelope go back to her father's house to be married, with a bride-price (1.275–278).
---

The book sets two marriages in the house of Odysseus side by side. Laertes kept his marriage whole: he honored the slave woman he had bought as he honored his wife, but did not share her bed, “for he shunned his wife's anger” (1.430–433; see [[eurycleia]], [[laertes]] and [[concubines]]). Penelope's second marriage is the suitors' aim: Athena tells Telemachus that if her heart is eager to marry, she should go back to her father's house, where they will arrange a wedding and make ready the bride-price (1.275–278; see [[penelope]], [[the-suitors]] and the translation's note on 1.275–278).
