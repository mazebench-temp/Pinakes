---
summary: Athena twice puts Odysseus “on a sea-washed island”: to Zeus, where she names Calypso (1.50), and to Telemachus in the likeness of Mentes, where she speaks only of harsh men (1.198).
---

Both uses are Athena's, and both open the verse with the same words, νήσῳ ἐν ἀμφιρύτῃ. In the council of the gods she tells Zeus where Odysseus suffers:

{{quote:13bb7b8c-8fef-53b6-b7fe-cb98e9816b53}}

and she goes on to the goddess who keeps him, the daughter of Atlas (1.51–55; [[calypso]], [[ogygia]]). In the likeness of Mentes she gives Telemachus the same half-verse, but no goddess: “on a sea-washed island, and harsh men hold him, / savage men, who keep him back against his will” (1.198–199). The stranger knows what the goddess knows, and tells it in a form a mortal could have heard. See [[full-of-trees]], the epithet of the next verse.
