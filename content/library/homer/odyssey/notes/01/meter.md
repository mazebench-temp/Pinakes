summary: Telemachus' mother is in nearly every speech of the book: “My mother says that I am his” (1.215); the suitors “woo my mother” (1.248); Athena would send her back to her father, if “her heart is eager to marry” (1.275); and Telemachus answers her, “My mother, why do you begrudge the trusty singer” (1.346), and addresses the suitors as “Suitors of my mother” (1.368).
grc: [μητέρα, μητρὸς]
---

μητέρα δʼ (1.275) begins a sentence that ends in an anacoluthon. Merry notes that the mother, set against the suitors whom Telemachus is to send away, needs a verb such as “send off”, and that the sentence turns instead to the softer “let her go back” (*Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 1.275; see the translation's note on 1.275–278).
