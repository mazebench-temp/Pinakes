summary: A book of fathers. Zeus is “the father of men and gods” (1.28) and Athena's “Our father” (1.45, 1.81); Odysseus is Telemachus' “absent father” (1.135), whom he sees in his mind (1.115) and is sent to learn of (1.94, 1.281); Penelope may go back to her father's hall (1.276). The adjective πατρώιος names what comes from the fathers: a guest-friendship between two houses (1.175–176, 1.187, 1.417) and the kingship of Ithaca (1.387).
grc: [πατέρα, πατρώιος, πατρώιοι, πατρώιόν]
