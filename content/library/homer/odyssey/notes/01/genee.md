summary: Telemachus says that no one has known “his own parentage” (γόνον, 1.216), but the goddess tells him that the gods “have not made your lineage nameless for the time to come” (1.222). Antinous grants that the kingship of Ithaca is his “by birth from your father” (γενεῇ, 1.387), and Eurymachus asks the vanished stranger's “birthplace” (γενεή, 1.407).
grc: [γόνον]
en: [parentage, birthplace]
---

Merry takes ἑὸν γόνον as “his parentage”, in the sense of γονή (*Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 1.216), and νώνυμνον ὀπίσσω as “inglorious for the time to come”: the Greeks, he notes, pictured the future as what came up behind them (on 1.222). The book's questions of identity ask for a γενεή: “Who are you among men, and from where? Where are your city and your parents?” (1.170; see [[fathers-and-sons]] and [[euchomai]]).
