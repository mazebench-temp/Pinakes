summary: Telemachus offers the departing stranger a gift to take to his ship, “a precious one, very beautiful, which will be a treasure for you / from me, such as dear guest-friends give to guest-friends” (1.312–313); the goddess asks for it on her way back, “and it will bring you its worth in return” (1.318).
en: [treasure]
refs: [1.311-313, 1.316-318]
---

The gift is a κειμήλιον, a treasure to be laid up in the guest's house, as guest-gifts were kept as proof of the bond (see [[xenia]] and [[doron]]). Telemachus offers it at parting, with the bath that a host gives (1.309–313). The goddess, eager for her journey, asks him to give it “on my way back, to carry home, / taking out a very beautiful one” (1.317–318). Merry takes ἑλών as “having taken”, from the store-room, not “having chosen” (*Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 1.318; see the translation's note on 1.318), and her last words as a pledge that she will repay the gift handsomely, since Telemachus might fear that a guest who put off his gift could not return it (on 1.318; see [[time]]). When Menelaus offers him gifts at Sparta, Telemachus asks in turn that whatever he gives be a κειμήλιον (4.600).
