summary: Odysseus once went to Ephyra “seeking a man-slaying drug, so that he might have it / to smear his bronze-fitted arrows” (1.261–262).
grc: [ἀνδροφόνον]

The Iliad's epithet of Hector and of Achilles' hands is given to a poison. Ilus, son of Mermerus, refused it for fear of the gods, and the stranger's father gave it, out of love for Odysseus (1.259–264; [[odysseus-seeks-poison]], [[bronze-fitted]]).
