---
summary: Mermerus is named once, in the patronymic of his son Ilus, from whom Odysseus sought a man-slaying drug at Ephyra (1.259).
---

παρʼ Ἴλου Μερμερίδαο, “from Ilus, son of Mermerus” (1.259; see [[ilus-son-of-mermerus]] and [[odysseus-seeks-poison]]). Μερμερίδαο is the old genitive in -αο of the patronymic, like Ἀτρεΐδαο, “of the son of Atreus” (1.35, 1.40), and Πεισηνορίδαο, “son of Peisenor” (1.429), the book's other patronymics in the genitive (see [[son-of-atreus]] and [[peisenor-father-of-ops]]). Nothing else is said of the father. He is not the Trojan Mermerus whom Antilochus kills in the Iliad (*Iliad* 14.513; see [[mermerus]]).
