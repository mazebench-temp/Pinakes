**The Muse of the Odyssey.** The poem's first words are addressed to her: “Tell me, Muse, of the man of many turns” (1.1; see [[invocation-of-the-muses]], [[proem]] and [[of-many-turns]]). The Iliad calls on θεά, “goddess” (*Iliad* 1.1); the Odyssey names her μοῦσα in its first line and keeps θεά for the last line of the proem, “Of these things, goddess, daughter of Zeus, beginning where you will, tell us too” (1.10; see [[zeus]]). Merry derives Μοῦσα from the root μεν, “think”, and for her father compares the Iliad's “Olympian Muses, daughters of aegis-bearing Zeus” (*Iliad* 2.491); he notes that the Odyssey makes the Muses nine, at the funeral of Achilles (24.60), but that Hesiod is the first to give their names (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 1.1). μοι, “me”, in the first line is the unemphatic enclitic, “prithee”, set against the emphatic καὶ ἡμῖν, “us too”, of the last (on 1.1).

**Beginning where you will.** τῶν ἁμόθεν γε … εἰπὲ καὶ ἡμῖν: Merry takes ἁμόθεν to leave it to the Muse to decide at what point the singer shall take up the story, “from any point thou wilt”, as Demodocus takes up the tale of the wooden horse “from where” the Argives sailed away (8.500). καὶ ἡμῖν is either “that we too, the poet and his audience, may know as well as you”, for the Muses are goddesses and know all things (*Iliad* 2.485), or “tell us too, as you have told others”; Aristarchus thought καί superfluous (on 1.10; see the translation's note on 1.10). The next verse begins the story at the point chosen, with all the others at home and Odysseus alone held on Calypso's island (1.11–15; see [[calypso]] and [[in-medias-res]]).
