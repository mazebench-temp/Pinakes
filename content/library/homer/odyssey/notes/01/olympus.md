---
summary: While Poseidon is away the gods meet in the halls of Olympian Zeus, and Athena goes darting down from the peaks of Olympus to Ithaca (1.26–27, 1.102).
---

The council of the gods meets “in the halls of Olympian Zeus” (1.27; see [[the-council-of-the-gods]] and [[olympian]]), where Athena calls her father “Olympian” (1.60). When it is over she binds on her golden sandals, takes her spear, and “went darting down from the peaks of Olympus” to the doorway of Odysseus (1.96–103; see [[sandals]] and [[athena-visits-telemachus]]). The verse is the Iliad's verse for the gods' descents, used of Athena going to the Achaean ships and to Pandarus (*Iliad* 2.167, 4.74), and the poem uses it once more, in its last book (24.488).
