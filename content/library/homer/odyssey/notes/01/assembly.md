---
summary: Athena's plan is to rouse Telemachus “to call the long-haired Achaeans to an assembly” (1.90); as Mentes she tells him, “Tomorrow call the Achaean heroes to an assembly / and declare your word to all” (1.272–273), and he tells the suitors, “at dawn let us all go and sit in the assembly” (1.372).
---

The first book prepares the assembly of the second. Athena names it in the council of the gods as the way to put might in Telemachus' heart (1.88–91; see [[the-council-of-the-gods]] and [[athena]]), gives it to him as her counsel, with the gods as witnesses (1.272–274; see [[athena-visits-telemachus]] and [[oath]]), and Telemachus announces it to the suitors, so that he may speak out his word to them “without regard for anyone” and bid them leave his halls (1.372–374; see [[telemachus-and-the-suitors]] and [[the-suitors]]). The other uses of ἀγορεύω in the book are speaking, not an assembly (see [[assembly-type-scene]]).
