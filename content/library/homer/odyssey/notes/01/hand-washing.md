# χέρνιβα (1.136), νίψασθαι (1.138) and the verse of the heralds' water (1.146), first met here in the Odyssey.
grc: [χέρνιβα, νίψασθαι, "ὕδωρ ἐπὶ χεῖρας ἔχευαν"]
en: [for them to wash, poured water over their hands]
---

**Before the meal.** In the Iliad the hands are washed before a prayer, a libation, an oath or a sacrifice; in the Odyssey they are washed before every meal as well. For the guest and Telemachus a handmaid brings the χέρνιψ, “water for the hands”, in a golden pitcher and pours it over a silver basin, “for them to wash” (1.136–138; see [[water-for-the-hands]], [[basin-and-pitcher]] and [[handmaids]]). W. W. Merry describes the manner of it: the guest holds his hands over the basin while the water is poured on them. νίπτω is to wash a part of the body, where λούω is to bathe the whole; it is to pour water on a thing, where βάπτω, used once in Homer, is to dip it in; and πλύνω is used only of things (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 1.138; see [[bathing]]).

**The suitors' washing.** For the suitors “the heralds poured water over their hands” (1.146; see [[heralds]] and [[feast-type-scene]]). The verse goes before drinking and libation in both poems: the heralds pour the water over the kings' hands at the oath of Book 3 and before the libation of the embassy (*Iliad* 3.270, 9.174), and in the Odyssey before the libation to Poseidon at Pylos and before the suitors' libation on the day of the bow (*Odyssey* 3.338, 21.270). At Pylos too the water comes before the sacrifice, brought in a flowered basin, and Nestor begins the rite “with the water for the hands and the barley grains” (*Odyssey* 3.440–445; see [[barley-grains]]).
