summary: Calypso keeps Odysseus “with gentle and wheedling talk” (1.56), and Telemachus, going to bed, takes off “his soft tunic” (1.437).
grc: ["μαλακοῖσι καὶ αἱμυλίοισι λόγοισιν", "μαλακὸν δʼ ἔκδυνε χιτῶνα"]
en: [gentle]
---

Of words μαλακός is “gentle,” as μαλακοῖς ἐπέεσσιν, “with gentle words,” in the *Iliad* (*Iliad* 1.582, 6.337), and here it is paired with αἱμύλιος, “wheedling,” for the talk with which Calypso bewitches Odysseus so that he may forget Ithaca (1.56–57; see the translation's key words). Of things it is “soft”: at the end of the book Telemachus sits on his bed and takes off his soft tunic, in the dressing verse turned about (1.437; [[dressing-scene]], [[tunic]]).
