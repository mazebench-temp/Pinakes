summary: Telemachus, having fed the stranger first, asks the goddess in the likeness of Mentes the stranger's question, “Who are you among men, and from where? Where are your city and your parents?” (1.170), and she answers with the tale of Mentes.
passages:
  - 1.169-177 | Telemachus questions the stranger
---

The question comes in the order the welcome requires. At the door Telemachus promised the stranger that he would tell what he needed when he had tasted the meal (1.123–124); only when they have eaten, and Phemius has begun to sing, does he put his head close and ask (1.156–157, 1.169–177). The questions come one after another: “give me an exact account” (1.169; [[give-me-an-exact-account]]), the verse of the formula (1.170), the ship and the sailors who brought him (1.171–173), “tell me this truly” (1.174; [[tell-me-this-truly]]), and whether he is newly come or a guest-friend of the family (1.175–177). Merry hears “a touch of naïve humour, in the mouth of an islander” in the verse about the ship, “For I do not think that you came here on foot” (1.173; Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 1.173).

{{quote:509af42a-1893-51c5-91d1-9fd33a49c93d}}

The goddess answers point by point with a false identity: Mentes, son of Anchialus, lord of the Taphians, his ship in the harbor of Rheithron, a guest-friend of the house from of old (1.179–193). The questions return at the end of the book, indirect and in a suitor's mouth, when Eurymachus asks Telemachus “where this man comes from, from what land he claims to be, / and where are his birthplace and his native fields” (1.406–407; Merry on 1.406 compares 1.171).
