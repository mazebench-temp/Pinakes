---
summary: The Republic quotes Telemachus' two lines on the newest song, with two words changed, for its warning against new manners of music; the Ion names Phemius as a rhapsode of Ithaca.
---

- The song “which comes newest to those who listen”, quoted as the song which comes newest “about the singers”, and the fear that the poet may be taken to praise a new manner of song (*Republic* 4.424b–c, quoting 1.351–352 with variants): [[plato-the-newest-song]].
- Socrates asks Ion whether he has ever seen a man clever at explaining Olympus, Thamyras, Orpheus or “Phemius the rhapsode of Ithaca”, but at a loss over Ion of Ephesus (*Ion* 533b–c, on the singer of this book, 1.154, 1.337): [[phemius]].
- The “corded bed” of Telemachus' chamber, τρητοῖσι λέχεσσι (1.440), for which Merry takes τρητός as “morticed”, of the frame, citing the *Statesman*'s division of things put together into those that are bored and those that are bound without boring (*Statesman* 279e; Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 1.440; see the translation's note on 1.440 and [[bed]]).
