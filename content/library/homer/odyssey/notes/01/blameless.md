---
summary: “Noble Aegisthus”: Zeus remembers the murderer of Agamemnon with the epithet (1.29); and Telemachus says that his house was once likely to be “rich and flawless” (1.232).
grc: [ἀμύμονος]
en: [flawless]
---

The first council of the gods opens with the name of a murderer and an epithet of praise: Zeus “remembered noble Aegisthus, / whom the son of Agamemnon, far-famed Orestes, had killed” (1.29–30), and goes on to blame him for his own ruin (1.32–43). Merry (*Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 1.29; see the translation's note on 1.29) explains that ἀμύμων refers to some excellence, such as gentle birth or personal beauty, with no necessary moral sense; it is “almost titular, like our epithet ‘gallant,’” and he compares δῖα Κλυταιμνήστρη at 3.266, “brilliant Clytemnestra.” The word is used of things too: Telemachus tells the stranger that “this house was once likely to be rich and flawless, / while that man was still at home” (1.232–233; [[wealthy]]).
