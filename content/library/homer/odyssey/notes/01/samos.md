---
summary: Under the name Same, the island is one of the three whose best men woo Penelope (1.246).
---

Telemachus names the islands of the suitors “Dulichium and Same and wooded Zacynthus” (1.246; see [[the-suitors]]). This is the first time the Odyssey uses the name Same, Σάμη, for the island the Iliad calls Samos (*Iliad* 2.634); Book 4 has Samos again (4.671). Strabo takes the verse as proof that the poet, counting the islands, called the island Same that he had earlier called Samos; Apollodorus, he reports, wanted to read Σάμῳ τε for Σάμῃ τε here, holding that the city was called both Same and Samos but the island only Samos (*Geography* 10.2.10). Merry suggests that Same and Dulichium may be the two parts of Cephallenia (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 1.246; see [[dulichium]]).
