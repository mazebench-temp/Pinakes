Athena tells Telemachus that if his mother's heart is eager to marry, she should go back “to the hall of her father, a man of great power”, where a wedding would be arranged and a great bride-price made ready (1.275–278). The poem does not say where the hall is. When Penelope first appears, coming down from her upper room to stop Phemius' song, the narrator names her by her father, “the daughter of Icarius, prudent Penelope” (1.329; see [[daughter-of-icarius]] and [[penelope]]). Ancient critics asked why Telemachus, going to Lacedaemon, never met a grandfather they took for a Spartan; Aristotle and Strabo answered by placing Icarius elsewhere (see [[the-icarius-problem]]).
