---
summary: Telemachus would not grieve so if his father had fallen at Troy: “Then all the Achaeans would have made a tomb for him, / and he would have won great fame for his son too in days to come” (1.239–240). Athena tells him that if he hears his father is dead, he is to come home and “heap up a mound for him” (1.291).
refs: [1.239-241, 1.291]
---

Merry takes the κλέος of 1.240 as the glory of which the tomb would be the lasting memorial (*Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 1.240; see [[kleos]] and [[tomb]]), and the σῆμα of 1.291 not as a grave, since the body would not be there, but as a monument (on 1.291). The tomb at Troy would have given fame; the man the Harpies snatched away “without report” has none (1.241).
