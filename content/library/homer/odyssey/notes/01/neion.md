---
summary: Mentes' ship lies “in the harbor of Rheithron, under wooded Neion” (1.186), the one verse of the poem that names the mountain.
---

Neion is the stranger's landmark for the harbor where his ship waits (1.185–186; see [[rheithron]]). ὑλήεις, “wooded”, is the epithet that the same book gives Zacynthus, ὑλήεντι Ζακύνθῳ (1.246; see [[wooded]] and [[zacynthus]]). The mountain belongs to the stranger's tale, which gives the ship a real place on the island, a harbor and a mountain above it (see [[mentes-the-taphian]]). Telemachus describes his home in the same way when he tells Nestor that he comes from Ithaca ὑπονηίου, “under Neion” (3.81).
