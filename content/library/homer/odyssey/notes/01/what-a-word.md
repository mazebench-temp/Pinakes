---
summary: Zeus rebukes Athena's reproach: “My child, what a word has escaped the barrier of your teeth!” (1.64).
grc: ["ποῖόν σε ἔπος φύγεν ἕρκος ὀδόντων"]
en: [what a word has escaped the barrier of your teeth]
---

Athena has asked Zeus why he is so angry with Odysseus (1.62), and he answers that he could never forget him; it is Poseidon who is angry (1.64–75; [[cloud-gatherer]]). He answers her with the same two verses in the second council (5.21–22). The verse comes six times in the Odyssey: Athena, as Mentor, uses it to Telemachus at Pylos (3.230), Eurycleia twice (19.492, 23.70), and Antinous to Leiodes (21.168). Merry takes ἕρκος ὀδόντων, against the ancient commentators who understood the lips, of the teeth themselves, which look like a fence when the lips open to speak, and cites Solon for the same understanding in classical times (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 1.64; see the translation's note on 1.64). See [[zeus]] and [[athena]].
