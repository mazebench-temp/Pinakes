summary: In Book 1 εὔχομαι is always “claim”, and always of who one is. Telemachus asks who the sailors “claim to be” (1.172); the goddess answers, “I claim to be Mentes, son of wise-hearted Anchialus” (1.180), and claims an old guest-friendship between the houses (1.187); Eurymachus asks from what land the stranger “claims to be” (1.406), and Telemachus repeats the claim (1.418).
en: [claim]
---

The verb is the one with which Glaucus ends the account of his lineage, “This is the lineage and the blood I claim” (*Iliad* 6.211; see [[genee]] and [[fathers-and-sons]]), and the questions of Book 1 are of that kind: city, parents, ship (1.170–173). The goddess's answer is the most solemn of them, and false: she is not Mentes (1.180–181; see [[mentes-the-taphian]] and [[eidos]]). Merry notes that εὐχετόωντο (1.172) is past, limited to the time when the stranger was on shipboard with the crew (*Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 1.172). The verb of prayer in the book is ἀράομαι: the suitors would all “pray to be nimbler of foot” if Odysseus came home, and they “prayed to lie beside her in bed” (1.164, 1.366; see [[prayer]]).
