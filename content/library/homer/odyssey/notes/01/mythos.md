---
summary: The book follows Telemachus' μῦθος, the word that commands. Athena bids him “declare your word to all” in the assembly (1.273); he tells his mother that “speech will be the concern of men” (μῦθος, 1.358), and she lays “her son's wise word to heart” (1.361); and he tells the suitors that at dawn he will “speak out my word to you without regard for anyone” (1.373).
grc: [μῦθος, μύθων, μυθήσεαι]
en: [speech, words, speak, tell]
---

**A word to be obeyed.** Merry renders μῦθον πέφραδε (1.273) “make known your say”, that is, announce your will (*Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 1.273), and joins ἀπειπέμεν, Athena's word for what Telemachus is to say to the suitors (1.91), with μῦθον ἀπηλεγέως ἀποείπω (1.373), “to speak out his mind”, against Nitzsch's “renounce” (on 1.91; see the translation's note on 1.91). In the hall Telemachus takes the first word as Zeus does on Olympus: τοῖσι δὲ μύθων ἦρχε (1.28) and ἤρχετο μύθων (1.367), “began to speak”.

**Speech for war.** Telemachus sends his mother to her work in Hector's verses, with μῦθος where Hector had πόλεμος: “speech will be the concern of men, / of all of them, and mine most of all” (1.358–359; *Iliad* 6.492–493; see [[war-is-the-concern-of-men]]). Penelope goes, “for she laid her son's wise word to heart” (1.361), and the suitors, “biting their lips with their teeth”, marvel “that he spoke so boldly” (1.381–382; see [[biting-their-lips]], [[tharsos]] and [[thambos]]).
