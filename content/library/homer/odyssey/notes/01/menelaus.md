# Unnamed: "for he was the last of the bronze-armored Achaeans to come home" (1.286).
refs: [1.286]
---

κεῖθεν δὲ Σπάρτηνδε παρὰ ξανθὸν Μενέλαον· / ὃς γὰρ δεύτατος ἦλθεν Ἀχαιῶν χαλκοχιτώνων (1.285–286; see [[sparta]], [[fair-haired]], [[bronze-armored]] and [[the-journey-of-telemachus]]). ὅς is demonstrative, and δεύτατος, the superlative of δεύτερος, “later”, means “last”, an illogical but natural sense, Merry says (*Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 1.286). Athena's reason for the visit is the lateness of his return: the last to come home may have the latest news. So it proves, for Menelaus has heard from Proteus that Odysseus is alive, held on Calypso's island (4.555–560; see [[the-returns-of-the-achaeans]] and [[calypso]]). He came home in the eighth year, he says, on the day Orestes gave the funeral feast for Aegisthus (4.82; 3.311–312); Merry reckons it the ninth year after the fall of Troy (on 1.16). He is also the brother of the king whose murder Zeus remembers in the council (1.35–43; see [[agamemnon]] and [[the-murder-of-agamemnon]]). The renderings of other books are plain renderings, not the translation.
