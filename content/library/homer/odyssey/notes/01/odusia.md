---
summary: Eight of the surviving lines of the Latin Odyssey render lines of Book 1: the first line, Athena's address to Zeus and his answer, the basin and pitcher of the guest's washing, Telemachus' question to the stranger, the stranger's question about the feast, and the suitors of his mother.
refs: [1.1, 1.45, 1.64-65, 1.136-137, 1.169, 1.225-226, 1.248]
---

The lines are given here as The Latin Library prints them, with the lines of Book 1 that it sets beside each. The first is quoted by Gellius (see the entry above); for the others this note relies on that collection, which does not name their ancient sources.

- ***Virum mihi, Camena, insece versutum.*** “Tell me, Camena, of the versatile man” (1.1): the opening of the poem, “Tell me, Muse, of the man of many turns” (see [[proem]] and [[of-many-turns]]).
- ***Pater noster, Saturni filie.*** “Our father, son of Saturn” (1.45): Athena's address to Zeus, “Our father, son of Cronus, highest of lords”. Cronus becomes the Roman Saturn, and the fragment has no words for “highest of lords” (see [[cronus]]).
- ***Mea puer, quid verbi ex tuo ore supra fugit?*** “My child, what word has fled from your mouth?” (1.64): Zeus' answer, “My child, what a word has escaped the barrier of your teeth!” *Puer*, “child”, here takes the feminine *mea*; for Homer's barrier of the teeth the Latin has the mouth (*ore*).
- ***Neque enim te oblitus sum, Laertie noster.*** “Nor indeed have I forgotten you, our son of Laertes” (1.65). Homer's Zeus asks a question about Odysseus in the third person, “How then could I forget divine Odysseus”; in the fragment he speaks to Odysseus himself, and names him by his father (see [[laertes]]).
- ***argenteo polybro, aureo eglutro.*** “with a silver basin, a golden pitcher” (1.136–137): the guest's washing, “And a handmaid brought water for the hands in a pitcher and poured it, / a fine golden one, over a silver basin”. The Latin keeps the metals, gold for the pitcher and silver for the basin, and names the basin first (see [[basin-and-pitcher]] and [[hand-washing]]).
- ***tuque mihi narrato omnia disertim.*** “and you, tell me everything plainly” (1.169): Telemachus to the stranger, “But come, tell me this, and give me an exact account”, with *disertim* for ἀτρεκέως, “exactly” (see [[give-me-an-exact-account]]).
- ***Quae haec daps est? Qui festus dies?*** “What feast is this? What holiday?” (1.225–226): the stranger's question, “What feast, what throng is this? What need do you have of it? / A banquet, or a wedding?” *Daps* answers δαίς, “feast”; where Athena asks whether this is a banquet or a wedding, the Latin asks what festival day it is.
- ***… matrem procitum plurimi venerunt.*** “… very many have come to woo my mother” (1.248): Telemachus' complaint, “so many woo my mother and wear down my house”. *Procitum*, “to woo”, belongs with *proci*, “suitors”, the word of Cicero's “feasts of the suitors” (*in procorum epulis*, *Brutus* 71; see [[the-suitors]]).
