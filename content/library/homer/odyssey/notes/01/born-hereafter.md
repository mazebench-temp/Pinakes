---
summary: Athena ends the example of Orestes with the hope that some one “of those born hereafter may speak well” of Telemachus (1.302).
---

The stranger has told Telemachus of the fame that Orestes won among all men when he killed his father's slayer (1.298–300), and turns the example on him: “be valiant, so that some one of those born hereafter may speak well of you” (1.301–302). Fame among men to come is the reward held out to the young man, as the Iliad's heroes measure their deeds by what later men will say. See [[kleos]], [[mythological-paradigm]] and [[strong-spear]].
