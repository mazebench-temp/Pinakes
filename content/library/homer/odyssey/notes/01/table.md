# τραπέζας (1.111) is first met here.
grc: [τραπέζας]
---

**The tables of the hall.** When Athena comes, the suitors' servants are making the hall ready: “others again with sponges full of holes were washing the tables / and setting them out” (1.111–112; see [[heralds]] and [[feast-type-scene]]). The tables are set before the seats, each diner, or each pair, with a table of his own: W. W. Merry compares Tacitus on the Germans, “separate seats for each, and a table for each”, and notes that the practice was not universal, for one table serves all the guests in Achilles' hut (*Iliad* 9.216; Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 1.112). The same sponges “full of holes” clean the tables and the chairs of the hall after the suitors are killed (*Odyssey* 22.437–439, 22.452–453), and Eurycleia has the slave women wipe the tables with sponges on the morning of their last feast (*Odyssey* 20.151–152).

**The guest's table.** For Telemachus and the stranger the handmaid “drew up a polished table beside them” (1.138), one table for the two of them, set apart from the suitors (see [[hand-washing]] and [[xenia]]). παρὰ … ἐτάνυσσε is “drew up to the seat” (Merry on 1.138). On it the housekeeper sets the bread and the carver the platters of meat, “and set golden cups beside them” (1.139–142; see [[cup]]).
