---
summary: The epithet's first use of a house: when Penelope has gone up, “the suitors raised a din throughout the shadowy halls” (1.365).
---

In the Odyssey σκιόεις describes the halls of a house as well as mountains and clouds, in two verse-ends, ἀνὰ μέγαρα σκιόεντα and κατὰ μέγαρα σκιόεντα. The verse of the suitors' uproar comes three times (1.365, 4.768, 18.399), and with κατά the halls are where men fall silent or lie down to sleep (10.479, 11.334, 13.2, 23.299). Here the din follows Penelope's departure, and the suitors all pray to lie beside her (1.365–366). Merry explains the epithet as the gloom inseparable from large rooms lit only through narrow openings in the roof, all the darker against the bright light outside; of mountains he takes it as the gray misty color of distant hills (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 1.365).
