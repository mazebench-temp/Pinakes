---
summary: Three times in the book: “a devoted handmaid” stands on either side of Penelope (1.335), Eurycleia is “true-hearted” (1.428), and Laertes honored her as he honored “his devoted wife” (1.432).
---

The epithet first comes in the verses of Penelope's entrance, which return when she shows herself to the suitors in Books 18 and 21: she stands by the doorpost of the hall, holding her veil before her cheeks, “and a devoted handmaid stood on either side of her” (1.333–335; [[handmaids]]). At the end of the book the narrator gives it twice in the history of Eurycleia: she carries the torches for Telemachus, “true-hearted” (κεδνὰ ἰδυῖα, 1.428), and Laertes, who bought her, “honored her in his halls as he honored his devoted wife,” but never lay with her (1.432–433; [[eurycleia]], [[laertes]]).
