summary: In the proem the Sun is the owner of the cattle that Odysseus' companions ate, and the god who took away the day of their homecoming.
# Unnamed: "and he took away from them the day of their homecoming" (1.9).
refs: [1.9]
---

**The god of the cattle.** The proem names the Sun by both his names, “Hyperion the Sun”, Ὑπερίονος Ἠελίοιο (1.8; see [[hyperion]]), and gives him the last act in the companions' story: αὐτὰρ ὁ τοῖσιν ἀφείλετο νόστιμον ἦμαρ, “and he took away from them the day of their homecoming” (1.9; see [[the-cattle-of-helios]] and [[day-of-homecoming]]). The proem makes the Sun the cause of the companions' loss, as Zeus will make Poseidon the cause of their leader's wanderings (1.20–21, 1.68–75; see [[poseidon]]). When the story is told in full, Teiresias and then Odysseus himself warn of the cattle of Helios, “who sees all things and hears all things” (11.108–109, 12.322–323; see [[who-sees-all-things]]), the god whom Agamemnon calls to witness the oaths of the Iliad (*Iliad* 3.277).
