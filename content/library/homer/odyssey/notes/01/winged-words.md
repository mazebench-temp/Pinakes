---
summary: Telemachus' first words to the stranger at the door: “and he spoke to her, addressing her with winged words” (1.122).
---

The whole verse, καί μιν φωνήσας ἔπεα πτερόεντα προσηύδα, introduces the welcome, “Welcome, stranger! Here with us you will be treated as a friend” (1.122–124; [[xenia]], [[visit-scene]]). Merry reports that Classen counted forty-four instances of the verse and found that it marks the first beginning of a speech, the taking up of one that was broken off, or a peculiar solemnity or emphasis in the words that follow (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 1.122). Here it is the first word spoken in the house of Odysseus. See [[telemachus]].
