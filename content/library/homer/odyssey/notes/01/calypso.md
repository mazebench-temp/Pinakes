**Him alone.** The tale proper begins with the one man the war and the sea have not let go. All the others who escaped are at home (1.11–12; see [[the-return-of-odysseus]]):

{{quote:777bdccc-2a28-5f9c-96b8-fd4ebf390b0a}}

The moment is the end of the seventh year on her island, and the year in which the gods have spun his return has come (1.16–17; Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 1.11 and 1.16; see the translation's note on 1.11). ἔρυκε, “held back”, is the word of her part in the story, and it returns in Athena's κατερύκει (1.55) and in the stranger's κατερύκεται (1.197). λιλαιομένη πόσιν εἶναι, “longing for him to be her husband”, is said of her again in Odysseus' tale to the Phaeacians and in the account of what he told Penelope (9.30, 23.334), and of Circe (9.32; see [[circe]]). νύμφη πότνια, “the lady nymph”, and δῖα θεάων, “brilliant among goddesses”, are her first epithets (see [[nymphs]] and [[brilliant-among-goddesses]]).

**Athena's account.** In the council of the gods Athena tells where Odysseus suffers: on the sea-washed island “where the navel of the sea is”, full of trees, where “a goddess has her dwelling”, the daughter of baleful Atlas (1.50–54; see [[ogygia]], [[atlas]] and [[the-council-of-the-gods]]). Then she says what the goddess does to him:

{{quote:2e726887-2a82-50a7-a803-7dc6ef136da9}}

μαλακοῖσι καὶ αἱμυλίοισι λόγοισιν, “with gentle and wheedling talk”: αἱμύλιος, “wheedling”, is found nowhere else in Homer, and λόγος only here and once in the Iliad, where Patroclus cheers the wounded Eurypylus with talk (*Iliad* 15.393). Merry finds a sample of such talk in her last plea, that if Odysseus knew the sorrows still in store for him he would stay and be immortal (on 1.56; 5.206–213). θέλγει, “she bewitches”, is the verb of the Sirens' song (12.40, 12.44) and of Hermes' wand (5.47), and the verb of Aegisthus, who beguiled Agamemnon's wife with words (3.264; see [[aegisthus]]). Athena sets the goddess's design against the man's longing: she would have him forget Ithaca, and he longs to die if only he may see the smoke of his own land.

**The message.** When Zeus has agreed, Athena proposes that Hermes go to Ogygia, “so that with all speed / he may tell the nymph of the lovely tresses our unerring counsel, / the homecoming of stout-hearted Odysseus, that he may return” (1.85–87; see [[hermes]] and [[with-lovely-tresses]]). νημερτέα βουλήν, “unerring counsel”, is “that which is infallibly purposed”, the return itself (Merry on 1.86). The errand waits until Book 5, where Zeus gives Hermes the order in nearly the same words (5.29–31).

**Not named to Telemachus.** In the likeness of Mentes, Athena tells Telemachus only that his father is “held back on the wide sea, / on a sea-washed island, and harsh men hold him, / savage men, who keep him back against his will” (1.197–199; see [[mentes-the-taphian]]). The goddess of the council has become a band of savage men.
