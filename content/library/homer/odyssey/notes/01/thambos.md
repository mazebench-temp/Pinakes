summary: When the goddess has gone, Telemachus “was amazed in his heart; for he thought that it was a god” (1.323); Penelope, “amazed”, goes back into the house at her son's words (1.360); and the suitors “marveled at Telemachus, that he spoke so boldly” (1.382).
grc: [θαύμαζον]
en: [marveled]
---

Merry marks the aorists of 1.322–323 as the entry of fresh points of action: “He noticed it—he was amazed—for the idea struck him that it was a god” (*Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 1.323; see [[seeing-the-gods]] and [[like-a-bird]]). When Athena leaves Nestor at Pylos in a bird's likeness, amazement holds all who see it (3.371–372). Penelope's amazement is at her son: Merry takes it as the cause of her withdrawal, and a reason to doubt Aristarchus' rejection of 1.356–359 (on 1.360; see [[war-is-the-concern-of-men]]). θαυμάζω, “marvel”, is θάμβος' companion word (see [[thaumaston]]).
