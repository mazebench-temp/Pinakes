---
summary: The mixing bowls of Odysseus' hall serve the suitors: heralds and attendants mix the wine and water in them before the meal, and the young men fill them brim-full for the drinking, in the verse of the feasts of the Iliad (1.110, 1.148).
# κρητῆρσι (1.110) is first met here.
grc: [κρητῆρσι]
---

When Athena arrives, “heralds and ready attendants were busy for them, / some mixing wine and water in the mixing bowls” (1.109–110; see [[heralds]] and [[wine]]). At the meal “the young men filled the mixing bowls brim-full with drink” (1.148; see [[cupbearing]] and [[feast-type-scene]]), the verse of the drinking at Chryse and before the embassy (*Iliad* 1.470, 9.175). ἐπεστέψαντο, “filled brim-full”, is literally “crowned”: W. W. Merry, after Curtius, refers στέφω to the root of the Latin *stipare*, “to pack close”, and renders “they brimmed the bowls with drink”; the genitive ποτοῖο follows the verbs of filling, as in κρητῆρας ἐπιστεφέας οἴνοιο, “mixing bowls brim-full of wine” (*Odyssey* 2.431; Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 1.148). The bowls are the hall's: Eurycleia has them cleaned for the suitors' last feast, and Phemius lays his lyre down between the mixing bowl and a chair when he goes to supplicate Odysseus (*Odyssey* 20.152, 22.340–341; see [[phemius]]).
