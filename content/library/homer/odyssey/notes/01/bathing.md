---
summary: Telemachus presses the stranger to stay, bathe and take a gift home, as a host should; the goddess will not be held back (1.309–318).
---

When the stranger has given her counsel and makes to leave, Telemachus begs her to wait, “so that when you have bathed and satisfied your dear heart, / you may go to your ship with a gift, rejoicing in your heart” (1.310–311; see [[xenia]] and [[guest-gifts]]). The bath is part of the welcome he owes a guest of his father's house, with the meal and the gift; in the Odyssey a guest is usually bathed before he is fed, as Telemachus and Peisistratus are on their arrival at Sparta and Odysseus in Circe's house (*Odyssey* 4.48–51, 10.358–365), but this guest has been fed and counseled already, and the bath is offered last, before the parting gift. The goddess refuses: “Do not hold me back now any longer, longing as I am for my journey” (1.315; see [[athena-visits-telemachus]]).
