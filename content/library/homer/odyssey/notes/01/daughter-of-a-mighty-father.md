---
summary: Athena arms for her journey to Ithaca with the spear “with which she overpowers the ranks of men, / of heroes, against whom she, the daughter of the mighty father, bears resentment” (1.100–101).
---

The verses of Athena's spear are the verses of her arming in the *Iliad*, where she sets out with Hera to join the battle (*Iliad* 8.390–391, and with one word changed *Iliad* 5.746–747). Here the goddess of war takes up the heavy spear to visit a young man in his father's house, and goes in the likeness of a guest (1.102–105; [[heavy-spear]], [[arming-scene]]). Merry notes that some editors, Bekker among them, write ὀμβριμο- for ὀβριμο- on the authority of later manuscripts, and connects the word with βρίθω (“be heavy”) and βαρύς (“heavy”), with a prefixed ὀ- (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 1.101). See [[athena]].
