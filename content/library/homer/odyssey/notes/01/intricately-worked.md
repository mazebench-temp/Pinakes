---
summary: Telemachus seats the stranger on a chair “fine and intricately worked; and there was a footstool under her feet” (1.131).
grc: [δαιδάλεον]
---

The verse is the *Iliad*'s, from the welcome Charis gives Thetis in the house of Hephaestus (*Iliad* 18.390), and the Odyssey uses it again when Circe seats Odysseus (10.367). Merry takes the words “spreading a cloth beneath her” as a parenthesis, so that καλὸν δαιδάλεον goes with the chair (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 1.130; see the translation's note on 1.130).
