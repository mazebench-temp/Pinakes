---
summary: Odysseus' sheep are μῆλʼ ἁδινά, “thronging sheep,” in Athena's account of the suitors (1.92).
grc: ["μῆλʼ ἁδινὰ"]
en: [thronging]
---

The translation renders ἁδινά “thronging” of sheep, where the Iliad's ἁδινός is “swarming” of bees and flies (see the translation's note on 1.92). Merry prefers “thick-thronging” as a descriptive epithet of sheep and goats, keeping closer to Buttmann's own sense of the root, compactness, than Buttmann's view that the word describes these flocks only, always driven in in numbers (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 1.92). Telemachus repeats the verse at Sparta (4.320). See [[sheep]] and [[shambling]].
