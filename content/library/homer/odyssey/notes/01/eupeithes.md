---
summary: Named only in his son's patronymic, in the verse that brings in Antinous' answer to Telemachus' warning, “Then Antinous, son of Eupeithes, spoke to him in turn” (1.383).
---

The book gives him no more than his name. His part comes later: Penelope recalls how Odysseus saved him from the people of Ithaca, who were angry because he had joined pirates of the Taphians, the people whom Athena as Mentes claims to rule (1.181; see [[taphians]]), in raiding the Thesprotians (16.424–430); and after his son's death he leads the suitors' kin against Odysseus (24.422–471; see [[antinous]]).
