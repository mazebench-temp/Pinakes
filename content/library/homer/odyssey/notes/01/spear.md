# ἔγχος (1.99, 1.104, 1.121, 1.127), ἔγχεʼ (1.129) and δοῦρε (1.256), claimed in the Iliad by its notes.

**Athena's spear.** Before she goes down to Ithaca the goddess arms herself:

{{quote:1f433411-216f-590b-b4fe-399e7cd1965d}}

The second and third verses are the Iliad's, when Athena arms for battle (*Iliad* 5.746–747, 8.390–391); here she takes her spear to visit a house as a guest (see [[strong-spear]] and [[athena]]). She stands at the door holding “the bronze spear” (1.104), and Telemachus takes her right hand and receives it from her (1.121), as a host takes the guest's spear.

**The spear-rack.** He carries the spear into the house and stands it against a tall pillar, “inside a polished spear-rack, where many other / spears of stout-hearted Odysseus stood” (1.127–129; see [[spear-rack]] and [[house]]). The guest's spear stands among the absent master's.

**Two spears.** The stranger wishes that Odysseus would come and stand at the doors “with helmet and shield and two spears” (1.256; see [[shield]]).
