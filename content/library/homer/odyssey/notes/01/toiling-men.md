---
summary: Telemachus: it is not the singers who are to blame, but Zeus, “who gives / to toiling men, to each, as he wills” (1.348–349).
---

Telemachus defends Phemius' song of the Achaeans' return with a saying about the gods and men: the singers are not to blame, Zeus is, who gives to each as he wills (1.346–349; [[who-is-to-blame]], [[the-song-of-the-returns]]). The epithet sets men who labor against the god who gives. Merry renders the passage “who dispenses to enterprising men severally as he will,” that is, good or bad fortune, and quotes Eustathius' explanation, that the singers are not to blame for men's misfortunes, since things did not fall out so because they sing of them, but they sing because things fell out so (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 1.347; see the translation's note on 1.349).
