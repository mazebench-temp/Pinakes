---
summary: Eurycleia, “whom Laertes once bought with his own possessions / when she was still in her first youth” (1.430–431).
grc: [πρωθήβην]
en: [in her first youth]
---

The epithet belongs to the short history of the old nurse that the narrator gives as she lights Telemachus to bed: bought young, for the worth of twenty oxen, honored like Laertes' wife, and the one who reared Telemachus (1.428–435; [[eurycleia]]).
