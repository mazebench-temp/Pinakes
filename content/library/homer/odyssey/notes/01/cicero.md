---
summary: Cicero finds in the songs sung at the suitors' feasts proof that there were poets before Homer, judges the Latin Odyssey as primitive as a work of Daedalus, and twice gives the answer of Scipio, who had greeted the death of Tiberius Gracchus with a verse of this book.
---

- There were poets before Homer, as the songs sung in his poem “at the feasts of the Phaeacians and of the suitors” show (*in Phaeacum et in procorum epulis*, *Brutus* 71, on 1.153–155 and 1.325–327): [[phemius]] and [[the-song-of-the-returns]].
- The Latin Odyssey of Livius Andronicus, “like some work of Daedalus” (*Brutus* 71–73): [[odusia]].
- Scipio's answer in the assembly, that Tiberius Gracchus “seemed to have been justly killed”, after he had recited Athena's verse on Aegisthus at the news (*Pro Milone* 8; *On the Orator* 2.106; on 1.47): [[scipio-and-tiberius-gracchus]].
- Homer called not Ajax or Achilles but Ulysses the sacker of cities (*Letters to Friends* 10.13.2, which Merry cites on 1.2, the sack of Troy in the proem): [[sacker-of-cities]] and [[proem]].
