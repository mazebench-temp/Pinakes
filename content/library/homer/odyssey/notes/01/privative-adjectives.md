---
summary: Telemachus on his father: “he is gone, out of sight, unheard of” (1.242), οἴχετʼ ἄιστος ἄπυστος, after “without report” (ἀκλειῶς, 1.241).
grc: ["ἄιστος ἄπυστος"]
en:
  - "out of sight, unheard of"
---

Two privative adjectives side by side, with no conjunction, close the lament that the Harpies have snatched Odysseus away “without report” (1.241), a privative adverb of the same kind. Merry explains the pair as “he has passed out of sight, out of hearing”: no one has seen him, and no one has heard of him from one who saw him, and he quotes Eustathius, who takes the words as explaining ἀκλειῶς (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 1.242; see the translation's note on 1.241). The same Telemachus speaks of his father as made “out of sight beyond all other men” by the gods a few verses before (1.235–236), and the stranger has told him that the gods have not made his lineage “nameless,” νώνυμνον (1.222).
