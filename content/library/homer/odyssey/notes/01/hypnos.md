summary: Athena gives Penelope sleep: she weeps for her husband “until sweet / sleep was cast upon her eyelids by gray-eyed Athena” (1.363–364). The suitors go home to bed (1.424), but Telemachus lies awake “all night long”, planning his journey (1.443–444).
grc: [ὕπνον]
# 1.364: the English "sleep" stands on this line, the Greek ὕπνον on the line before.
refs: [1.364]
---

The two verses of Penelope's sleep return each time she goes up to weep for Odysseus (16.450–451, 19.603–604, 21.357–358; see [[penelope]]). The suitors' going to bed is the end of the verse with which the gods end their day in the Iliad, “they went to lie down, each to his own house” (1.424, as *Iliad* 1.606), and Merry reports Monro's view that κακκείοντες has a desiderative ending (*Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 1.424). The book ends with a sleepless night, παννύχιος (1.443; see [[the-journey-of-telemachus]]).
