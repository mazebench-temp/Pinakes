---
summary: Telemachus defends Phemius' song of the “evil fate of the Danaans”, their grim homecoming from Troy (1.350).
---

When Penelope asks Phemius to stop his song of the Achaeans' grim homecoming, Telemachus answers that the singers are not to blame: “There is no blame on this man for singing the evil fate of the Danaans; / for men praise that song the more / which comes newest to those who listen” (1.350–352; see [[nemesis]], [[the-song-of-the-returns]] and [[the-returns-of-the-achaeans]]). κακὸν οἶτον, “the evil fate”, is the homecoming that Athena laid on them (1.326–327). The Danaans are named only here in the book; elsewhere it calls the same men Achaeans and Argives (see [[achaeans]] and [[argives]]).
