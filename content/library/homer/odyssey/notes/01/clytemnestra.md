---
summary: Unnamed in the first book, she is the wife whom Aegisthus married “against fate”, though Hermes had warned him “neither to kill the man himself nor to woo his wife” (1.35–39).
refs: [1.35-36, 1.39]
---

Zeus' example in the council of the gods is told without her name: Aegisthus “married the wife of the son of Atreus, / his wedded wife, and killed him on his return” (1.35–36), although the gods had sent Hermes to warn him “neither to kill the man himself nor to woo his wife” (1.39; see [[aegisthus]], [[the-murder-of-agamemnon]] and [[the-council-of-the-gods]]). ἄλοχον μνηστήν, “his wedded wife”, is literally a wife who was wooed, from μνάομαι, the verb of Aegisthus' wooing in the warning (μνάασθαι, 1.39) and of the suitors who now woo Penelope (μνῶνται, 1.248; see [[penelope]] and [[the-suitors]]).
