# θείη in the Odyssey is always the verb, the optative or subjunctive of τίθημι (1.116, 8.465, 15.180, 16.198, 20.225, 23.186).
grc: [θεῖον, "!θείη"]
---

Zeus answers Athena's reproach with the epithet: “How then could I forget divine Odysseus, / who is first among mortals in mind, and first in sacrifices / given to the immortal gods” (1.65–67). The singer Phemius is θεῖος ἀοιδός, “the divine singer,” when Penelope asks him to stop his song (1.336; [[phemius]]); in the Odyssey the epithet goes with singers, as with Demodocus (8.43, 8.47).

The entry's form θείη, the feminine of the adjective, is in this poem always a verb: the optative or subjunctive of τίθημι, “put, make,” as when Telemachus imagines his father coming home to “make a scattering of the suitors here through the house” (σκέδασιν κατὰ δώματα θείη, 1.116; again 20.225), or “so may Zeus grant it” (8.465, 15.180).
