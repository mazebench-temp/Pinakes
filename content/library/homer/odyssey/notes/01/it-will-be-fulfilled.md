---
summary: The stranger's prophecy: “now I will prophesy to you, as the immortals / put it in my heart, and as I think it will be fulfilled” (1.200–201).
grc: ["τελέεσθαι ὀίω"]
---

Athena, in the likeness of Mentes, prophesies that Odysseus will not be away much longer, though she disclaims a seer's art, “though I am no seer and have no clear knowledge of birds” (1.200–205; [[divination]]). The two verses of the introduction come again when Helen interprets the omen of the eagle and the goose at Sparta (15.172–173), and the half-verse τελέεσθαι ὀίω twice more in the poem (3.226, 22.215). Speaking as a mortal, the goddess uses the weaker form of the formula, with “I think,” not the certainty of τετελεσμένον ἔσται. See [[athena]].
