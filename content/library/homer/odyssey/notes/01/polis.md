summary: The proem's hero sacked “the sacred citadel of Troy” and “saw the cities of many men and learned their mind” (1.2–3). The stranger is asked his city with his parents (1.170); his ship lies “away from the city” (1.185), and Laertes “no longer comes to the city” (1.189).
grc: [ἄστεα, πόλις, πόληος, πόλινδε]
---

Merry quotes Cicero's remark that Homer called not Ajax or Achilles but Ulysses πτολιπόρθιος, “sacker of cities”, and explains ἔπερσεν by Athena's words to Odysseus near the end of the poem, that the city of Priam was taken by his counsel (*Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 1.2; 22.230; see [[odysseus]]). ἄστεα (1.3) are cities as places where men live; the scholiast, Merry reports, explains the mind Odysseus learned there by his question whether a people are savage or kind to strangers (on 1.3; see [[noos]]).
