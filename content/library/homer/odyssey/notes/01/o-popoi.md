---
summary: Twice the cry opens a speech: Zeus, “Ah, how mortals now blame the gods!” (1.32), and Athena, “Ah, truly you are in great need of absent Odysseus” (1.253).
---

Both speakers are moved to indignation by what mortals do. Zeus opens the council with ὢ πόποι and an exclamation, οἷον, which Merry renders “to think how!” (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 1.32), and the speech that follows is his answer to men who blame the gods for their own recklessness (1.32–43; [[who-is-to-blame]], [[atasthalie]]). Athena, in the likeness of Mentes, opens with the cry and ἦ δή when Telemachus has told her of the suitors, and wishes that Odysseus would come and stand at the doors with helmet, shield and two spears (1.253–266).
