# μῆλʼ ἁδινὰ (1.92; again 4.320), the flocks: μῆλα are sheep and goats together (9.184), and μῆλον is also an apple (7.120). οἰὸς ἀώτῳ (1.443), "sheep's wool", as Iliad 13.599.

**The slaughtered flocks.** Athena means to rouse Telemachus to speak out to the suitors, “who are forever / slaughtering his thronging sheep and shambling cattle with curving horns” (1.91–92; see [[the-suitors]] and [[cattle]]). μῆλα are the small livestock, sheep and goats together, as Odysseus says of the Cyclops' flocks, μῆλʼ, ὄιές τε καὶ αἶγες, “flocks, sheep and goats” (9.184; see [[goats]]). ἁδινός, “thronging”, Merry takes as “thick-thronging”, a descriptive epithet of sheep and goats, rather than with Buttmann as an epithet for this case, of beasts that were always driven to the suitors in throngs (*Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 1.92; see the translation's note on 1.92); in the Iliad the word is said of swarming bees and flies (*Iliad* 2.87, 2.469). σφάζουσι, “slaughtering”, is from σφάζω, “cut the throat”, the verb of the sacrifice (ἔσφαξαν, *Iliad* 1.459; see [[sacrifice]]), here of beasts killed for the suitors' feasts. The swineherd later tells how they are supplied: of the eleven flocks of goats that graze at the far end of Ithaca, each goatherd drives one to the suitors every day, the best of the well-fed goats (14.103–106).

**Sheep's wool.** In the last verses of the book Telemachus, alone in his chamber, lies “all night long, covered with sheep's wool” (κεκαλυμμένος οἰὸς ἀώτῳ), planning the journey that Athena has shown him (1.443–444; see [[telemachus]]). οἰός is the genitive of ὄις, “sheep”, not οἶος, “alone”. ἄωτος (or ἄωτον) is the finest of the wool, the fleece, here a woolen coverlet; Merry (on 1.443) refers the word, with Buttmann, to ἄημι, “blow”, as Latin *floccus* is referred to *flo*. In the Iliad the same words are a sling of twisted wool (*Iliad* 13.599, 13.716).
