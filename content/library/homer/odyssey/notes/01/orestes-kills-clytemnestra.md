# Unnamed: the vengeance foretold and paid (1.40-43), and Orestes' deed (1.298-300).
refs: [1.29-30, 1.40-43, 1.298-300]
---

The vengeance is told twice in the first book, from the gods' side and from the son's. Zeus remembers Aegisthus, “whom the son of Agamemnon, far-famed Orestes, had killed” (1.29–30), and Hermes' warning had named the avenger, “for from Orestes vengeance will come for the son of Atreus, / when he comes of age and longs for his own land” (1.40–41; see [[hermes]] and [[poine]]). Athena, as Mentes, makes it an example for Telemachus: “have you not heard what fame brilliant Orestes won / among all men, when he killed his father's slayer, / scheming Aegisthus” (1.298–300; see [[orestes]], [[aegisthus]] and [[fathers-and-sons]]).

Only Aegisthus' death is told. The first book does not name Clytemnestra, and in the whole poem her death is touched on once: Orestes gave the Argives a funeral feast “for his hateful mother and for the coward Aegisthus” (3.309–310; see [[clytemnestra]]). The tragedians made the matricide the center of the story (see [[libation-bearers]]).
