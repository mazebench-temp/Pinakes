---
summary: The first epithet of the poem: the Muse is to tell of “the man of many turns” (1.1), and his name is held back until 1.21.
---

The proem gives the man an epithet and a story before it gives him a name. πολύτροπον is unfolded at once by the clause that follows it, “who was driven / far astray, after he had sacked the sacred citadel of Troy” (1.1–2), and Merry notes Homer's habit of explaining a word in the clause after it, as πατροφονῆα, “his father's slayer,” is explained by “who had killed his famous father” (1.299–300; Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 1.1). The name comes at 1.21, with another epithet, “godlike Odysseus” ([[antitheos]]).

The book gathers other epithets of the same family around him. Athena asks the gods to let “Odysseus of much wisdom” return (1.83; [[of-much-wisdom]]), and in the likeness of Mentes she tells Telemachus that his father will devise a way home, “since he is a man of many devices” (1.205; [[of-many-devices]]). See [[odysseus]] and [[proem]].
