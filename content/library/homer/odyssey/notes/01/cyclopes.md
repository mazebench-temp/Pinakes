---
summary: The Cyclopes are named in Zeus' answer to Athena: Poseidon is angry “because of the Cyclops, whom he blinded of his eye, / godlike Polyphemus, whose power is the greatest / among all the Cyclopes” (1.69–71).
---

Κύκλωπος κεχόλωται, “angry because of the Cyclops” (1.69): the singular is Polyphemus, named in the next verse (see [[polyphemus-the-cyclops]]), and the race is named only to measure his strength, πᾶσιν Κυκλώπεσσι, “among all the Cyclopes” (1.71). Merry weighs two senses of the dative, “in the eyes of all the Cyclopes” or “among” them, and compares κρατέω with the dative, as when Achilles' shade is told μέγα κρατέεις νεκύεσσιν, “you have great power among the dead” (11.485); however the dative is explained, he adds, it is not the genitive that may follow a superlative in a comparison (*Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 1.71). The English takes “among”.

The book says nothing else of them. Their land and their ways are described when Odysseus tells his story to the Phaeacians (9.105–130), and the blinding of the one whose power was greatest is the cause of all that Poseidon does to Odysseus (1.20–21, 1.68–75; see [[poseidon]]). The renderings of other books are plain renderings, not the translation.
