---
summary: The first use of the formula: Telemachus' chamber, built high “in a place with a view all round,” where the book ends (1.425–426).
---

When the suitors have gone home to sleep, the narrator follows Telemachus to his own room, “where his chamber in the beautiful courtyard / had been built high, in a place with a view all round” (1.425–426). Merry places it at a corner of the portico, where it might have a view in two or more directions, and takes the phrase of a clear view rather than of high ground (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 1.425). There Eurycleia lights him to bed, and there he lies awake all night planning the journey that Athena has shown him (1.427–444). See [[house]] and [[perikalles]].
