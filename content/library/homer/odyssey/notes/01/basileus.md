summary: Antinous prays that Zeus may never make Telemachus “king in sea-girt Ithaca” (1.386), and Telemachus answers that “it is no bad thing to be a king”, but that there are “other kings of the Achaeans, / many in sea-girt Ithaca, young and old” (1.392–395). Eurymachus leaves it on the knees of the gods “who will be king of the Achaeans in sea-girt Ithaca” (1.400–401).
grc: [βασιλευέμεν, βασιλῆες, βασιλεύσει]
---

**What a king has.** Telemachus answers the sneer with what kingship brings: “at once his house / grows rich, and he himself is more honored” (1.392–393; see [[time]]). Merry takes his question, “Do you think this is the worst thing among men?” (1.391), as a taunt: Telemachus pretends to see in Antinous' words a kindly wish to spare him the troubles of rule, though Antinous is eager to be king himself (*Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 1.391); and he hears in Antinous' σέ γε either that emphasis, “not you”, or a sneer at so young a fire-eater (on 1.386). Antinous concedes that the kingship is Telemachus' “by birth from your father” (γενεῇ πατρώιον, 1.387; see [[genee]]).

**Many kings.** Merry notes that the title is given to many at once: Antinous is βασιλεύς (24.179), Antinous and Eurymachus βασιλῆες (18.64), and among the Phaeacians twelve βασιλῆες rule beside Alcinous (on 1.394; 8.390–391). In Ithaca it names the island's nobles, “young and old” (1.395), and Telemachus yields it to them and keeps his own house (1.396–398; see [[anax]] and [[kingship-and-authority]]).
