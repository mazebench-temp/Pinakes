summary: Athena bids Telemachus “consider in your mind and in your heart / how you may kill the suitors in your halls, / whether by guile or openly” (1.294–296); the book ends with him “pondering many things in his mind” (1.427) and planning the journey (1.444).
grc: ["φρεσὶ μερμηρίζων"]
en: [consider in your mind and in your heart, pondering many things in his mind]

---

The deliberation is given to Telemachus as a task. The formula of the divided mind, κατὰ φρένα καὶ κατὰ θυμόν, “in your mind and in your heart,” comes in Athena's command, and the two courses follow as a double question, ἠὲ δόλῳ ἢ ἀμφαδόν, “whether by guile or openly” (1.294–296), as in the scenes where a man is torn two ways. At night he lies awake “pondering many things in his mind” (1.427), and the last verse of the book gives the result of his thinking, “he planned in his mind the journey that Athena had shown him” (1.444).
