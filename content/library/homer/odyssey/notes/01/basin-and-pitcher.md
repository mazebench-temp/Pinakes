---
summary: The first pitcher and basin of the Odyssey are a golden pitcher and a silver basin, from which a handmaid pours water over the hands of Telemachus and his guest (1.136–138).
# προχόῳ (1.136), and the basin in its phrase, ἀργυρέοιο λέβητος (1.137): a bare λέβητος would also catch the
# cauldrons given as gifts in later books.
grc: [προχόῳ, "ἀργυρέοιο λέβητος"]
---

“And a handmaid brought water for the hands in a pitcher and poured it, / a fine golden one, over a silver basin, / for them to wash” (1.136–138; see [[water-for-the-hands]] and [[hand-washing]]). W. W. Merry joins the words as “bringing it in a pitcher” (προχόῳ … φέρουσα) and “poured the water (over their hands)” (χέρνιβα ἐπέχευε), and the guest holds his hands over the basin while the water is poured (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 1.136 and 1.138). The vessels are a rich house's: the pitcher is of gold and the basin of silver (see [[gold-and-silver]]). The Iliad names a basin and pitcher only once, at Priam's libation, where the basin is χέρνιβον, a word found nowhere else; the Odyssey's basin is a λέβης, the word of the cauldron (*Iliad* 24.302–304; see [[cauldron]] and the translation's row χέρνιψ / πρόχοος / λέβης). The service is for the guest; the suitors have the water poured over their hands by the heralds (1.146; see [[heralds]]).
