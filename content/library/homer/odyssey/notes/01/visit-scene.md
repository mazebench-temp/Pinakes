Book 1 gives the Odyssey's first and fullest reception of a stranger, and every part of the pattern is there. **Arrival:** the goddess darts down from Olympus and stands at the doorway of Odysseus, on the threshold of the courtyard, spear in hand, in the likeness of Mentes (1.102–105). **Finding:** she finds (εὗρε) the suitors delighting their hearts with game pieces before the doors, sitting on the hides of the oxen they had killed, while heralds and attendants mix wine and set out tables (1.106–112; [[game-pieces]]). **Reception:** Telemachus sees her first, goes straight to the doorway, indignant that a stranger should stand long at the doors, takes her right hand, receives the bronze spear and speaks: “Welcome, stranger! Here with us you will be treated as a friend; and then, / when you have tasted the meal, you will tell us what you need” (1.113–124; [[winged-words]]). **Seat and meal:** he stands her spear in the spear-rack, seats her on a chair with a footstool, apart from the suitors, and the five verses of the meal follow (1.125–143; [[water-for-the-hands]], [[throne]], [[footstool]]). **Questions:** only after the meal does he ask who she is (1.169–177; [[who-are-you-among-men]]). **Errand:** the stranger tells her false name and her true counsel (1.178–305).

The last part is the gift. Telemachus asks her to stay, bathe and take a gift, as guest-friends give to guest-friends (1.309–313); she refuses in haste, “longing as I am for my journey,” and asks him to keep the gift until she comes back (1.314–318; [[guest-gifts]]), as the visitor in haste refuses the seat in the *Iliad*. Then she goes as a god goes, like a bird (1.319–323; [[divine-epiphany]]). Merry takes the promise “you will be treated as a friend” of the welcome shown in deeds, since “in primitive society,” he writes, “the showing of friendliness precedes the feeling of friendliness” (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 1.123).
