# The English "Phorcys" is claimed here, within the Odyssey only: in the Iliad the name is the Phrygian's (phorcys).
en: [Phorcys]
---

**Grandfather of the Cyclops.** In Zeus' answer to Athena the nymph Thoosa, mother of Polyphemus, is “the daughter of Phorcys, who rules over the barren sea” (1.71–72; see [[thoosa]], [[polyphemus-the-cyclops]] and [[poseidon]]). ἁλὸς ἀτρυγέτοιο, “the barren sea”, is a phrase of both poems (*Iliad* 1.316, 1.327; see [[barren-sea]]), but the singular μέδων, “ruler”, is used in Homer only of him; the plural stands in the formula ἡγήτορες ἠδὲ μέδοντες, “leaders and rulers”, and the word lives on in names such as Laomedon and Automedon. The Phrygian Phorcys of the Iliad is another man (see [[phorcys]]). In Book 13 the sea god gives his name to the harbor of Ithaca where the Phaeacians set Odysseus ashore, “the harbor of Phorcys, the old man of the sea” (13.96, 13.345).
