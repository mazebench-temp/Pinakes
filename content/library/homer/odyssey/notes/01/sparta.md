---
summary: Athena plans to send Telemachus “to Sparta and to sandy Pylos” for news of his father, and tells him to go from Pylos to Sparta, to Menelaus, who came home last of the Achaeans (1.93–95, 1.284–286).
---

**The plan.** In the council of the gods Athena names the goals of the journey in one verse: “And I will send him to Sparta and to sandy Pylos / to learn of his dear father's homecoming” (1.93–94; see [[pylos]] and [[the-journey-of-telemachus]]). Telemachus repeats the verse in the assembly and to Eurycleia (2.214, 2.359).

**The order of the journey.** In her counsel to Telemachus, Athena reverses the order: “First go to Pylos and ask brilliant Nestor, / and from there to Sparta, to fair-haired Menelaus; / for he was the last of the bronze-armored Achaeans to come home” (1.284–286). Σπάρτηνδε, “to Sparta”, is found only here in Homer. δεύτατος, “the last”, Merry explains, is the superlative of δεύτερος in its sense of “later”, as at *Iliad* 10.368 (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 1.286). Menelaus, who came home last, is the likeliest to have news; Telemachus will find him in Lacedaemon (4.1; see [[menelaus]] and [[lacedaemon]]).
