summary: Poseidon “is stubbornly forever / angry because of the Cyclops” (1.68–69), and Zeus promises that he “will let go / his anger” (1.77–78). Laertes kept Eurycleia from his bed, “for he shunned his wife's anger” (1.433).
grc: [κεχόλωται]

Athena's question, “Why are you so angry with him, Zeus?” (ὠδύσαο, 1.62), uses another verb of anger, ὀδύσσομαι, and Merry hears in it a play on the name of Odysseus, “a man of wrath”, who both endures the wrath of the gods and deals wrath to his enemies (*Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 1.62, comparing 19.275 and 19.407 ff.; see the translation's note on 1.62 and [[odysseus]]). Zeus answers that the anger is Poseidon's, for the blinding of his son, and that against the will of all the other gods he will not be able “to strive alone” (1.68–79; see [[poseidon]] and [[polyphemus-the-cyclops]]).
