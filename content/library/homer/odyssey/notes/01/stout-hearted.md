---
summary: Odysseus is “stout-hearted” twice: in Athena's plan for his homecoming (1.87) and in the spear-rack where his spears still stand (1.129).
grc: [ταλασίφρονος]
---

The genitive formula Ὀδυσσῆος ταλασίφρονος, frequent in the Odyssey, comes first in the message Hermes is to carry, “the homecoming of stout-hearted Odysseus” (1.87), and then in his house, where Telemachus stands the stranger's spear among the “spears of stout-hearted Odysseus” (1.128–129).
