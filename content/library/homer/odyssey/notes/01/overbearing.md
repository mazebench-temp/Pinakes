---
summary: The suitors are “overbearing men” to Telemachus, who seats his guest apart from them (1.134), and to the stranger they seem to feast “so overbearingly, like men committing outrage” (1.227).
grc: [ὑπερφιάλοισι, ὑπερφιάλως]
en: [overbearingly]
---

The word of blame enters the poem with the suitors and stays with them. Telemachus does not want his guest to loathe his meal “coming among overbearing men” (1.133–134), and the guest, seeing the feast, asks what it is: “so overbearingly, like men committing outrage, they seem to me / to feast through the house” (1.227–228; [[hybris]]). Merry explains ὑπερφίαλος, like ὑπερφυής, as “overgrown,” from the root φυ-, a sense that easily passes into “overweening” (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 1.134). See [[the-suitors]].
