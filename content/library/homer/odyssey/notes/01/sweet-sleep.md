---
summary: Penelope weeps for Odysseus in her upper room “until sweet / sleep was cast upon her eyelids by gray-eyed Athena” (1.363–364).
grc: ["ἡδὺν ἐπὶ βλεφάροισι"]
refs: [1.363]
---

The Odyssey has its own epithet for this sleep, ἡδύς (“sweet”), in two verses that end four of Penelope's scenes: ὄφρα οἱ ὕπνον / ἡδὺν ἐπὶ βλεφάροισι βάλε γλαυκῶπις Ἀθήνη (1.363–364, 16.450–451, 19.603–604, 21.357–358). The sleep is the goddess' gift, as it is when sleep is ambrosial ([[ambrosial]]). See [[penelope]] and [[hypnos]].
