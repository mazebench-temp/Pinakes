**The mother of the Cyclops.** Answering Athena, Zeus traces Poseidon's anger to its cause through three generations: Polyphemus, “godlike”, the strongest of the Cyclopes; his mother Thoosa, a nymph; and her father Phorcys, “who rules over the barren sea” (1.68–73; see [[poseidon]], [[polyphemus-the-cyclops]], [[phorcys-the-sea-god]] and [[cyclopes]]). The genealogy makes the point of the speech: the Cyclops is Poseidon's own son, and the god's anger is a father's (1.68–75). Thoosa lay with him ἐν σπέσσι γλαφυροῖσι, “in the hollow caves” (1.73), the words used earlier of the caves where Calypso holds Odysseus (1.15; see [[calypso]] and [[hollow-caves]]). Homer names her nowhere else.
