---
summary: Pallas Athena three times: following Telemachus into the house (1.125), chafing at his account of the suitors (1.252), and in Phemius' song, the goddess who laid on the Achaeans their grim homecoming (1.327).
---

Twice the double name comes while the goddess is Mentes to Telemachus, “So saying, he led the way, and Pallas Athena followed” (1.125), and “And chafing at this, Pallas Athena spoke to him” (1.252). The third time the name is in the song the suitors hear after she has gone: Phemius sings “the grim homecoming that Pallas Athena laid on them from Troy” (1.327). The goddess who has just helped Telemachus is the goddess whose anger the song remembers. Merry explains the anger by the outrage of Ajax, son of Oileus, to Cassandra in Athena's temple, and notes that at 4.499 Ajax's death is laid to his insolence against Poseidon (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 1.326; see the translation's note on 1.327).
