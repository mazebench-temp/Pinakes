---
summary: Penelope hears Phemius' song from her upper room, comes down the stairway to the hall, and when Telemachus sends her away goes back up to it with her handmaids and weeps for Odysseus until Athena sends her sleep (1.328–330, 1.362–364).
---

“And from her upper room she understood in her mind the wondrous song” (1.328; see [[the-song-of-the-returns]]). ὑπερωιόθεν, “from her upper room”, is found only here. W. W. Merry observes that the poet might have said ὑπερωίῳ, “in her upper room”, as of Penelope fasting there in Book 4 (*Odyssey* 4.787), but that “from” adds that she hears the singing at that distance, as the Fury “heard from Erebus” (ἔκλυεν ἐξ Ἐρέβεσφιν, *Iliad* 9.572; Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 1.328). She comes down “the high stairway of her house” with two handmaids (1.330–331; see [[handmaids]]). When Telemachus has sent her back to her work, she goes up again: “And going up to her upper room with her handmaids, / she wept then for Odysseus, her dear husband, until sweet / sleep was cast upon her eyelids by gray-eyed Athena” (1.362–364; see [[penelope]] and [[sweet-sleep]]). The three verses are her refrain in the Odyssey: they return when she withdraws after talking with the stranger, and when Telemachus sends her away from the contest of the bow (*Odyssey* 19.602–604, 21.356–358).
