---
summary: Telemachus sends Penelope from the hall in Hector's verses, with μῦθος, “speech”, in place of war, and a new close: “for mine is the power in this house” (1.356–359).
---

{{quote:44074f0e-5ddb-578c-a671-2dc98a0d8bab}}

**The athetesis.** Aristarchus rejected the four lines. Merry doubts the rejection, since something in Telemachus' words must account for Penelope's amazement and her withdrawal: “And she, amazed, went back into the house; / for she laid her son's wise word to heart” (1.360–361; *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 1.360; see the translation's note on 1.356–359 and [[thambos]]).

**The new close.** Where Hector ended with all the men born in Ilios, Telemachus claims κράτος, the power in the house (see [[kratos]] and [[mythos]]). Merry takes τοῦ as deictic, as if he laid his hand on his breast, and reports the view that it stands for the ἄναξ of the house, as Telemachus calls himself later that evening (on 1.359; 1.397; see [[anax]]). Merry takes ἔργον ἐποίχεσθαι, “to go about their work”, of work at the loom, where the weaver walked from side to side following her shuttle (on 1.358; see [[loom]] and [[weaving]]).
