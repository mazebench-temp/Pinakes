---
summary: Heaven is held apart from the earth by the tall pillars of Atlas, and the gods are those “who hold the wide sky” (1.53–54, 1.67).
---

Athena describes Atlas as the one who “himself holds the tall pillars / that hold earth and heaven apart” (1.53–54; see [[atlas]] and [[earth]]). ἀμφὶς ἔχουσιν may mean that the pillars hold up heaven at either side, east and west, or, as Merry finds simpler and the translation follows, that they keep the two asunder, the earth below and heaven above; Merry cites Hesiod, in whom Atlas holds up the wide sky with his head and his tireless hands (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 1.53; *Theogony* 517–520, 746–748; see the translation's note on 1.52–54). In Zeus' answer the gods are the immortals “who hold the wide sky” (1.67; see [[wide-sky]]).
