summary: Athena's visit to Telemachus is an epiphany at full length. She comes down from Olympus, stands at the door in the likeness of a man, is seen first by Telemachus, speaks with him as a guest, and is known only as she leaves, flying off like a bird; he is amazed and thinks it was a god.

**Arrival in a man's likeness.** The goddess “went darting down from the peaks of Olympus” and stood at the doorway of Odysseus “in the likeness of a stranger, the leader of the Taphians, Mentes” (1.102–105; see [[athena]] and [[mentes-the-taphian]]). Of the ways in which a god appears, plainly to one man or in a mortal's shape, this is the second (see [[seeing-the-gods]]). Telemachus “was by far the first to see her” (1.113), but what he sees is a stranger kept waiting at the door, and he takes her hand and her spear and makes her his guest (1.119–143; see [[xenia]] and [[athena-visits-telemachus]]). In the Iliad's first epiphany Athena is seen by Achilles alone and known at once: “Achilles was astonished, and turned around, and at once he knew / Pallas Athena” (*Iliad* 1.199–200; see [[athena-restrains-achilles]]).

**Known at the parting.** Here the recognition waits for the end of the visit:

{{quote:f1981b7f-6fc0-5b6c-aaca-71eee703010f}}

θάμβησεν, “he was amazed”, is the verb of Achilles' recognition, and of Helen's when she knows Aphrodite (*Iliad* 1.199, 3.398; see [[thambos]]); but Telemachus does not name the god, he only “thought that it was a god”. The manner of her going gives her away, as at Pylos, where she leaves “in the likeness of a sea-eagle”, amazement seizes all who see it, and Nestor names her as the daughter of Zeus (3.371–379; see [[like-a-bird]]). Telemachus keeps the knowledge to himself: to the suitors he calls the stranger Mentes, “but in his heart he knew the immortal goddess” (1.417–420).
