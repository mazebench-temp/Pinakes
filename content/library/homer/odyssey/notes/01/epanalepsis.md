---
summary: The Ethiopians are named and named again at the head of the next verse: “But he had gone to the Ethiopians, who are far away, / the Ethiopians, who are divided in two, the farthest of men” (1.22–23).
refs: [1.22-23]
---

The word Αἰθίοπας is taken up at the beginning of the next verse, and the second naming adds what the first left out: the people are divided in two, some where the sun sets and some where it rises (1.22–24). The figure gives the absent god's whereabouts the weight of a description, as the council of the gods begins without him (1.26–27; [[ethiopians]], [[poseidon]]). Compare the whole-phrase repetitions of the *Iliad* that the entry describes.
