---
summary: The narrator calls the companions who ate the cattle of the Sun “fools” (1.8), and Athena tells Telemachus that he must give up “childish ways, since you are no longer of that age” (1.297).
---

νήπιοι is the narrator's word for men who act without knowing what will follow (see [[atasthalie]] and [[the-cattle-of-helios]]). The second use belongs to a coming of age, as Orestes' vengeance was to come “when he comes of age” (1.41; see [[orestes]]). Merry explains νηπιάας, beside νηπιέη, as a form produced by assimilation, and ὀχέειν, “to practise”, as a settled, deliberate behavior, like ὕβριν ἔχοντες (1.368; *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 1.297; see [[childhood]] and [[hybris]]).
