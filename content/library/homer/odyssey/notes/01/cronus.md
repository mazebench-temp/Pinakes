---
summary: Cronus is named only in his son's patronymics. Athena twice addresses Zeus as “son of Cronus”, Κρονίδη, in the council of the gods, and Antinous wishes that “the son of Cronus”, Κρονίων, may never make Telemachus king.
---

**Κρονίδης.** “Our father, son of Cronus, highest of lords” (1.45, 1.81) is the verse with which Athena, and only Athena in Homer, addresses Zeus: in the Iliad when he has forbidden the gods the war (*Iliad* 8.31), and in the last book of the Odyssey (24.473; see [[athena]], [[zeus]] and [[son-of-cronus]]).

**Κρονίων.** The other patronymic is a suitor's. When Telemachus has spoken boldly, Antinous answers: “May the son of Cronus never make you king in sea-girt Ithaca, / which is yours by birth from your father” (1.386–387; see [[antinous]] and [[kingship-and-authority]]).
