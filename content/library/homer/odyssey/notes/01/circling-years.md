---
summary: The proem dates the action: “But when, as the years circled, the year had come / in which the gods had spun the thread for him to return home” (1.16–17).
grc: ["περιπλομένων ἐνιαυτῶν"]
en: [as the years circled]
---

The participle is περιπλόμενος, “revolving,” in place of the Iliad's περιτελλόμενος, and the phrase sets the turning years against the one year that is fated. Merry distinguishes the two words: ἐνιαυτός is a twelvemonth, one like another, while ἔτος is a particular year in a series; and he reckons the ten years from the fall of Troy to the return (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 1.16; see the translation's note on 1.11).
