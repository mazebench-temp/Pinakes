summary: Bronze is the metal of Athena's spear, of the poisoned arrows Odysseus wanted, and of the Achaeans' armor, and the goal of Mentes' voyage to Temese (1.99, 1.104, 1.121, 1.184, 1.262, 1.286).
# χαλκῷ (1.99), χάλκεον (1.104, 1.121), χαλκόν (1.184), χαλκήρεας (1.262); χαλκοχιτώνων (1.286) is caught by the entry.
grc: [χαλκῷ, χάλκεον, χαλκόν, χαλκήρεας]
---

**Arms.** Athena takes up “a strong spear, tipped with sharp bronze” (1.99; see [[sharp]] and [[spear]]), and holds “the bronze spear” at Odysseus' door until Telemachus receives it from her (1.104, 1.121). Odysseus once sailed to Ephyra for a drug to smear on his “bronze-fitted arrows”, χαλκήρεας (1.261–262; see [[arrows]] and [[odysseus-seeks-poison]]); the Iliad's χαλκήρης is “bronze-fitted” too (see the translation's key words). Menelaus was the last “of the bronze-armored Achaeans” to come home (1.286; see [[bronze-armored]]).

**Trade.** Mentes sails “to Temese, for bronze”, with a cargo of iron (1.184; see [[temese]] and [[iron]]). χαλκός is the metal copper as well as its alloy; Merry, who places Temese in Cyprus, understands copper there (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 1.184), but the translation keeps “bronze”, as everywhere (see the translation's note on 1.184).
