**Far-famed.** τόν ῥʼ Ἀγαμεμνονίδης τηλεκλυτὸς ἔκτανʼ Ὀρέστης, “whom the son of Agamemnon, far-famed Orestes, had killed” (1.30; see [[agamemnon]] and [[famous]]): the epithet is used by prolepsis, Merry says, since the vengeance he would take was to be the ground of his fame (*Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 1.30). The deed is already done when the poem begins.

**The prophecy.** Hermes' warning to Aegisthus named him: “for from Orestes vengeance will come for the son of Atreus, / when he comes of age and longs for his own land” (1.40–41; see [[hermes]] and [[poine]]). He was a boy then, and away from home; Nestor will say that he came back from Athens in the eighth year of Aegisthus' rule (3.305–307). The words ἧς ἱμείρεται αἴης, “longs for his own land”, return eighteen verses later of Odysseus, who longs to see the smoke leaping up from his own land, ἧς γαίης, and longs to die (1.57–59; see [[calypso]]). The renderings of other books are plain renderings, not the translation.

Athena tells the gods' story again, now to a son (see [[the-murder-of-agamemnon]] and [[fathers-and-sons]]). If Odysseus is dead, Telemachus is to heap up a mound for him, give his mother to a husband, and then consider how to kill the suitors, “whether by guile or openly” (1.289–296; see [[telemachus]] and [[the-suitors]]); the fame Orestes won, κλέος (1.298), is the fame she promised the son in the council (1.95; see [[kleos]]). Nestor will use almost the same verses (3.195–200). Merry reports that Nauck marked 1.293–302 as spurious, as Hentze and Ameis did after him, because the passage does not agree with the advice of 1.274 and the lines that follow (on 1.293; see the translation's note on 1.293–302).

**Brilliant Orestes.** δῖος Ὀρέστης (1.298; see [[brilliant]]). Athena, as Mentes, assures Telemachus that “brilliant Odysseus has not yet died upon the earth” (1.196), in the verse with which Agamemnon's shade asks Odysseus for news of his son: οὐ γάρ πω τέθνηκεν ἐπὶ χθονὶ δῖος Ὀρέστης, “for brilliant Orestes has not yet died upon the earth” (11.461).
