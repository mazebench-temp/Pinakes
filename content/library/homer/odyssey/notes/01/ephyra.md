---
summary: Mentes first saw Odysseus on his way back from Ephyra, where Ilus son of Mermerus had refused him a man-slaying drug for his arrows (1.257–264). Whether the Ephyra of Thesprotia or of Elis is meant was disputed.
---

**The drug from Ephyra.** Athena, as Mentes, wishes that Odysseus would come home armed, such as he was when Mentes first saw him in his father's house, “on his way back from Ephyra, from Ilus, son of Mermerus” (1.257–259). He had gone there on his swift ship for a man-slaying drug to smear on his arrows; Ilus would not give it, fearing the indignation of the gods, but Mentes' father gave it (1.260–264; see [[odysseus-seeks-poison]], [[ilus-son-of-mermerus]] and [[anchialus-father-of-mentes]]). In the next book the suitors fear that Telemachus will go to Ephyra, “rich land”, for poisons to throw into their mixing bowl (2.328–330).

**Which Ephyra.** The poem does not say (see the translation's note on 1.259). Merry derives the name from a word for a lookout, a name that suits many places, and counts three or four Ephyras in Homer: the city later called Corinth (*Iliad* 6.152), which is not meant here; a town in Thessaly, later Crannon (*Iliad* 13.301, as Strabo explains it); a town in Thesprotia, later Cichyrus (*Iliad* 2.659); and an old town in Elis, on the river Selleis. For the Odyssey only the last two are in question. Nitzsch argued for Thesprotia, since Mentes has Odysseus stop at Taphos on his way home, and Taphos lies between Thesprotia and Ithaca. Merry answers that a ship sailing round the headland of Leucas, which in Homer's day, he holds, was not yet an island but the ἀκτὴ ἠπείροιο, “headland of the mainland”, of 24.378, would miss Taphos altogether, and that if the Taphian islands are placed among the Echinades, as a scholiast on Apollonius places them, the same argument favors Elis. For Elis he adds Agamede, the daughter of Augeas, king of Elis, who knew all the drugs that the wide earth grows (*Iliad* 11.739–741); the suitors' words, which name Ephyra with Pylos and Sparta as if all three were in the Peloponnese (2.326–329); and the corselet that Phyleus brought from Ephyra on the river Selleis (*Iliad* 15.530–531). He reports that the scholiast, on the authority of Apollodorus, makes Ilus son of Mermerus a great-grandson of Jason and Medea and king of Thesprotia, and that Eustathius tells of Medea living for a while in Elis; each story, he thinks, was invented or taken up by the champions of one Ephyra (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 1.259). Strabo placed the Ephyra of the drugs in Elis (*Geography* 8.3.5; see [[taphos]] and [[selleis-ephyra]]).
