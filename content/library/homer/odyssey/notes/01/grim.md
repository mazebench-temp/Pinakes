---
summary: Phemius sings “the grim homecoming that Pallas Athena laid on them from Troy” (1.327), and Penelope asks him to cease from “this grim song” (1.341).
grc: [λυγρῆς]
---

The narrator calls the song's subject grim, and Penelope takes up the word for the song itself: “cease from this song, / this grim song, which always wears down my dear heart in my breast” (1.340–342). Merry sets the two relative clauses side by side: the homecoming that Athena laid on them (ὃν) is a single fact, while the song that always wears down her heart (ἥ τε) is a thing that happens again and again (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 1.50).
