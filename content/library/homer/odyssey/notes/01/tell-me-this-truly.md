---
summary: The first use of the verse: Telemachus asks the stranger to tell him truly whether he is newly come or a guest-friend of the house from his fathers' time (1.174–177).
---

Telemachus' last question to the stranger is the one that matters to him, whether the visitor knew his father: “And tell me this truly, so that I may know it well, / whether you are newly come, or are a guest-friend / from our fathers' time” (1.174–176). The goddess answers, “I will tell you all this very exactly” (1.179; [[give-me-an-exact-account]]), and what she tells is the life of Mentes, which is not hers, down to the guest-friendship of the two houses, for which she sends him to ask old Laertes (1.180–193). The verse comes back in the Odyssey's other meetings with strangers, and once the other way round, when Odysseus asks it of Athena in disguise (13.232). See [[who-are-you-among-men]] and [[xenia]].
