summary: Athena's epithet eight times in the book: in her answers to Zeus (1.44, 1.80), in her exchanges with Telemachus while she is Mentes to him (1.156, 1.178, 1.221, 1.314), at her departure (1.319), and when she sends sleep to Penelope (1.364).
grc: [γλαυκῶπιν]
---

The book opens with the gods' council, and Athena answers Zeus twice in the verse “Then the gray-eyed goddess Athena answered him” (1.44, 1.80), the verse with which she answers Diomedes in the *Iliad* (*Iliad* 5.825; [[then-answered]]). When she goes to Ithaca in the likeness of Mentes, the narrator keeps her name and her epithet: Telemachus speaks to “gray-eyed Athena” (1.156), and she answers him as “the gray-eyed goddess Athena” (1.178, 1.221, 1.314), while he sees a Taphian guest. “So saying, gray-eyed Athena went away” (1.319), and at the end of Penelope's scene it is she who casts sweet sleep on her eyelids (1.363–364; [[sweet-sleep]]).

Merry holds that the epithet means brightness and flashing splendor rather than a color, and compares the terrible light of the goddess' eyes when Achilles turns and knows her (*Iliad* 1.200; Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 1.44). See [[athena]].
