---
summary: Athena twice addresses Zeus, “Our father, son of Cronus, highest of lords” (1.45, 1.81), and Antinous prays that “the son of Cronus” may never make Telemachus king (1.386).
---

Athena's address to Zeus is a whole verse of the *Iliad* (*Iliad* 8.31), and she uses it twice in the council, first to agree with Zeus about Aegisthus and turn to Odysseus, then to propose the plan of the poem (1.45–62, 1.81–95; [[father-zeus]], [[most-high]]). Antinous uses the patronymic in mockery: “May the son of Cronus never make you king in sea-girt Ithaca” (1.386; [[sea-girt]]). See [[zeus]].
