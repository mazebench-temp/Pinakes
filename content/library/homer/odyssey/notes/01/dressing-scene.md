---
summary: Athena makes ready for Ithaca with the sandal verse and the spear (1.96–101); and the book ends with the dressing scene reversed, when Telemachus takes off his soft tunic and Eurycleia hangs it on a peg (1.436–442).
---

The goddess' preparations are made of two kinds of verse. The sandals are those of the gods' journeys, “ambrosial, golden,” which carry her over the waters and the boundless earth, in the verses that carry Hermes to Priam in the *Iliad* and to Calypso in Book 5 (1.96–98; *Iliad* 24.340–342; 5.44–46; [[ambrosial]], [[boundless-sea]]); the spear is the warrior's, taken up with the verse of the arming (1.99–101; [[arming-scene]]). Telemachus will bind on his sandals and take up his spear in the same words when he leaves the ship for Eumaeus' hut and on the morning of the contest of the bow (15.550–551, 20.126–127).

At the end of the day the order runs backward. Telemachus opens the doors of his chamber, sits on the bed and takes off his soft tunic, μαλακὸν δʼ ἔκδυνε χιτῶνα, the dressing verse with ἔκδυνε, “took off,” for ἔνδυνε, “put on”; Eurycleia folds and smooths it and hangs it on a peg beside the corded bed (1.436–440; [[soft]], [[tunic]], [[corded]]).
