summary: The goal of the return in three places: Poseidon drives Odysseus “from his native land” (1.75); the stranger promises that he “will not be away much longer from his dear native land” (1.203); and Athena tells Telemachus, if his father is dead, to “come home to your dear native land” (1.290).
grc: ["πατρίδος αἴης"]
en: [native land]
---

Zeus' account of Poseidon's anger ends with the land Odysseus cannot reach: the god does not kill him, “but drives him wandering from his native land” (1.75). In the likeness of Mentes, Athena prophesies the opposite (1.200–205; [[divination]]). The formula then turns to Telemachus, who is to sail to Pylos and Sparta and, if he hears that his father is dead, to “come home to your dear native land” and heap up a mound for him (1.290–291).
