summary: Agamemnon is not named in the first book except in his son's patronymic. He is “the son of Atreus”, whose wife Aegisthus married and whom he killed on his return (1.35–40), and the “famous father” whom Orestes avenged (1.299–300); his homecoming is the first that the poem tells.
# Unnamed: "killed him on his return" (1.36), "neither to kill the man himself" (1.39), "his father's slayer ...
# who had killed his famous father" (1.299-300).

**Unnamed.** The book names him only in his son's title, Ἀγαμεμνονίδης, “the son of Agamemnon” (1.30; see [[orestes]]). Zeus calls him “the son of Atreus” (1.35, 1.40; see [[son-of-atreus]] and [[atreus]]), and Athena, as Mentes, “his famous father”, πατέρα κλυτόν (1.300; see [[famous]]). His wife is not named either (see [[clytemnestra]]).

**The homecoming murdered.** The first homecoming the poem tells is his, and it ends at the hands of the man who had married his wife: τὸν δʼ ἔκτανε νοστήσαντα, “and killed him on his return” (1.36; see [[the-murder-of-agamemnon]], [[aegisthus]] and [[nostos]]). The gods' warning to Aegisthus had spoken of him, “neither to kill the man himself nor to woo his wife” (1.39; see [[hermes]]), and his son took the vengeance that Hermes foretold, τίσις Ἀτρεΐδαο, “vengeance … for the son of Atreus” (1.40; see [[poine]]). In the council the story is Zeus' example of men who bring sorrow on themselves (1.32–43; see [[who-is-to-blame]]); in the house of Odysseus it becomes Athena's example for the son (1.298–302; see [[orestes-kills-clytemnestra]]).

**His brother.** Athena sends Telemachus to Agamemnon's brother, “to fair-haired Menelaus; / for he was the last of the bronze-armored Achaeans to come home” (1.285–286; see [[menelaus]] and [[the-returns-of-the-achaeans]]). From Menelaus he will learn how Agamemnon died (4.512–537). The rendering of the fourth book is a plain rendering, not the translation.
