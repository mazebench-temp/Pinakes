---
summary: Zeus tells how the gods sent Aegisthus a warning, “sending Hermes, the keen-sighted slayer of Argus” (1.38).
---

The warning was explicit: neither to kill Agamemnon nor to woo his wife, for vengeance would come from Orestes (1.37–41). The keen-sighted messenger was not heeded (1.42–43; [[messenger-scene]]). Merry reports a view that makes Hermes a wind-god driving his fleecy flock of clouds, and ἀργεϊφόντης “sky-clearing,” and finds that this picture of a careful shepherd “gives a new point to” ἐύσκοπον (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 1.38; [[argeiphontes]]). See [[hermes]].
