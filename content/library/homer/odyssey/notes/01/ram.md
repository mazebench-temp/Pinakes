# ἀρνειῶν (1.25), found only here in Homer; ἀρνεῖται "refuses" (1.249) is another word.
grc: [ἀρνειῶν]
en: [rams]
---

Poseidon has gone to the Ethiopians “to share in a hecatomb of bulls and rams” (ταύρων τε καὶ ἀρνειῶν ἑκατόμβης, 1.25; see [[hecatomb]], [[bulls]] and [[poseidon]]). The genitive plural ἀρνειῶν occurs only here in Homer. Bull and ram are offered together in the Iliad, when the young men of Athens seek the favor of Erechtheus “with bulls and rams” (*Iliad* 2.550), and Teiresias tells Odysseus that when he has killed the suitors he must carry an oar inland, to men who do not know the sea, and there offer Poseidon a ram, a bull and a boar (11.119–131; told again to Penelope, 23.267–278).
