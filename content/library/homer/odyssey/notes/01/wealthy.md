summary: Wealth in Telemachus' words: the suitors would pray to be nimbler of foot “rather than richer in gold and clothing” if Odysseus came home (1.164–165); the house was once likely to be “rich and flawless” (1.232); and a king's house “grows rich” (1.393).
grc: [ἀφνειότεροι, ἀφνειὸς]
en: [rich, richer]
---

Each time the word measures the house of Odysseus. The suitors who live on its wealth would rather run than be rich if its master returned (1.163–165); the house that would have been rich and flawless is being eaten away (1.232–233, 1.248–251; [[blameless]]); and kingship is no bad thing, Telemachus answers Antinous, since at once a king's house grows rich and he himself is more honored (1.392–393; [[kingship-and-authority]]).
