---
summary: The suitors are ἀγήνορες, “proud,” as the stranger finds them at their game pieces (1.106) and as they come in to the feast (1.144).
grc: [ἀγήνορας, ἀγήνορες]
---

In the Odyssey the epithet becomes the suitors' own, μνηστῆρες ἀγήνορες, “the proud suitors,” and its first two uses frame the stranger's welcome: Athena finds them before the doors (1.106), and when she has been seated and fed apart, “the proud suitors came in” (1.144).
