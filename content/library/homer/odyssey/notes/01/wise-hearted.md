---
summary: “Wise-hearted Odysseus,” for whom Athena's heart is torn (1.48), and Anchialus, “wise-hearted,” in the verse with which the stranger names herself and Telemachus repeats it (1.180, 1.418).
grc: [δαΐφρονι]
---

Athena gives the epithet to Odysseus as she turns the council from Aegisthus to him: “But my heart is torn for wise-hearted Odysseus” (1.48), with a play of sound in δαΐφρονι δαίεται. Merry rejects the view, which he gives to Buttmann, that δαΐφρων means “warlike” (from δαΐς, “battle”) in the Iliad and “prudent” (from δαῆναι, “learn”) in the Odyssey, since the Iliad too gives it to Odysseus; he derives it from δαῆναι alone, the skill being that of war or of peace as the person requires (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 1.48; see the translation's note on 1.48). The father of Mentes has the same epithet in the stranger's false name, “Mentes, son of wise-hearted Anchialus” (1.180, 1.418; [[anchialus-father-of-mentes]], [[lovers-of-the-oar]]).
