---
summary: Athena means to “put might in his heart” (1.89), and as she leaves she puts in it “might and daring” (1.321). Telemachus speaks “so boldly” that the suitors marvel, and Antinous says that “the gods themselves are teaching you / to be a man of lofty speech and to speak boldly” (1.382–385).
---

μένος καὶ θάρσος is what Athena gave Diomedes at the beginning of his great day, “might and daring, so that he would stand out among all” (*Iliad* 5.1–3; see [[menos]]). Here it makes a son bold enough to face his mother and the suitors (1.345–380). Antinous' sneer states what the narrator has shown: the boldness is a god's gift (1.320–322). Merry renders it “It must be the gods themselves that are thy teachers” (*Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 1.384).
