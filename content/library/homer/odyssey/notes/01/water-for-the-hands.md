summary: The first use of the five verses: Telemachus has the stranger served apart from the suitors, with water for the hands, a polished table, bread and dainties (1.136–140).
---

Telemachus seats the stranger on a chair with a footstool and sets his own chair beside her, away from the suitors and their din (1.130–135), and the formula serves the two of them:

{{quote:3970370e-3224-5868-aae1-c7a9eef2f27d}}

The carver's platters and the golden cups follow, as at Menelaus' house (1.141–142; *Odyssey* 4.57–58), and only then do the suitors come in, to be served in their own way by heralds, slave women and young men (1.144–148). Merry reports an objection to 1.140 here: the verse elsewhere describes what can be got ready quickly for an unexpected guest, while in this house a whole banquet is about to be served. He answers that the separate provision Telemachus makes for his guest points to the unfriendly relations between the young prince and the suitors (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 1.140; see the translation's note on 1.140).
