---
summary: Had Odysseus died at Troy, “all the Achaeans would have made a tomb for him” (1.239); if Telemachus hears that his father is dead, he is to “heap up a mound for him, and give him funeral rites” (1.291).
---

The tomb is what the absent man lacks. Telemachus wishes his father had died at Troy, for then “all the Achaeans would have made a tomb for him, / and he would have won great fame for his son too in days to come” (1.239–240), where now the Harpies have snatched him away without report (1.241; see [[fame-and-the-tomb]] and [[harpies]]). Athena's counsel makes the mound the first duty if he is dead: “heap up a mound for him, and give him funeral rites, / very many, as many as are fitting” (1.291–292; see [[honoring-the-dead]] and [[athena-visits-telemachus]]).
