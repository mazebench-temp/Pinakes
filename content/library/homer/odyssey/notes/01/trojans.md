---
summary: Telemachus would not grieve so if his father had fallen “in the land of the Trojans”, for then he would have had a tomb and left great fame to his son (1.236–240).
---

The Trojans are named once, in Telemachus' bitter answer to the stranger. A death at Troy would have been bearable: “for I would not grieve so for him if he were dead, / if he had been brought down among his companions in the land of the Trojans, / or in the arms of his friends, when he had wound out the war” (1.236–238). Then all the Achaeans would have made a tomb for him, and he would have won great fame for his son (1.239–240; see [[troy]], [[achaeans]] and [[kleos]]). Instead the Harpies have snatched him away without report (1.241). Τρώων ἐνὶ δήμῳ, “in the land of the Trojans”, is the war's place, as Ἰθάκης ἐνὶ δήμῳ, “in the land of Ithaca”, is the island's (1.103).
