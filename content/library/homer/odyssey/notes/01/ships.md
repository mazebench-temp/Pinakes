summary: The stranger has come by ship, with his companions, and his ship waits by the fields; Odysseus sailed to Troy and to Ephyra; and Telemachus is to fit out the best ship there is, with twenty oarsmen, and sail for news of his father (1.61, 1.171–173, 1.182–186, 1.211, 1.260, 1.280, 1.303–311).
# νηῦς (1.185) and νῆʼ (1.280); the other forms of the book are caught by the entry.
grc: [νηῦς, νῆʼ]
---

**The stranger's ship.** After asking who the stranger is, Telemachus asks how he came: “And on what sort of ship did you come? And how did sailors / bring you to Ithaca?”, for no one comes to an island on foot (1.171–173; see [[ithaca]]). The stranger answers with his ship and companions, a trader's crew (1.182–184), and points to where the ship lies: νηῦς δέ μοι ἥδʼ ἕστηκεν, “My ship stands over there” (1.185; see [[rheithron]] and [[taphians]]). When he leaves, he says that he is going down “to my swift ship / and my companions” (1.303–304), and Telemachus would have him go “to your ship with a gift” (1.311; see [[swift-ships]] and [[guest-gifts]]).

**Odysseus' ships.** At Troy Odysseus sacrificed to Zeus “beside the ships of the Argives” (1.61), and he went there with the best of the Argives “in their hollow ships” (1.211; see [[hollow-ships]]); once, before the war, he had gone to Ephyra “on his swift ship” for a drug for his arrows (1.260; see [[ephyra]]).

**The ship for Telemachus.** Athena's counsel begins his journey: “fit out with twenty oarsmen the best ship there is” (1.280; see [[the-journey-of-telemachus]]). Twenty oarsmen is the crew that Agamemnon chose for the ship that took Chryseis home in the Iliad, with Odysseus in command (*Iliad* 1.308–311).
