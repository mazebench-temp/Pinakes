---
summary: Before she leaves Olympus, Athena binds on her golden sandals, which carry her over the waters and over the boundless earth as fast as the blasts of the wind (1.96–98).
---

{{quote:1869f932-0d04-51bd-87fd-e7707ddd935f}}

The verses are those in which Hermes binds on his sandals when Zeus sends him to guide Priam (*Iliad* 24.340–342), and again when Zeus sends him to Calypso (5.44–46). Here they are Athena's, whose errand to Ithaca the council has set beside his to Ogygia (1.84–89; see [[hermes]], [[ogygia]] and [[athena-visits-telemachus]]). ὑγρή, “the waters”, is one of the feminine adjectives used as nouns, Merry notes, with ἀμβροσίη and σχεδίη (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 1.97). See [[ambrosial]] and [[gold-and-silver]].
