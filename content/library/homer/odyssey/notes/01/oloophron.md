---
summary: Calypso is “the daughter of baleful Atlas, who knows the depths / of the whole sea, and himself holds the tall pillars” (1.52–53).
en: [baleful]
---

The translation renders the word “baleful” here, and “deadly” of beasts in the *Iliad* (see the translation's note on 1.52–54). Merry gives “malign,” literally “of mischievous intent,” and finds in it the fear that surrounds uncanny knowledge: the word is given to Aeetes, Circe's brother (10.137), and to Minos (11.322), and to Atlas because he knows the depths of the whole sea, for such knowledge makes its possessor suspected of the will as well as the power to use it for harm; he reports other explanations, Welcker's of a rebellion of the Titans that Atlas led, and Nägelsbach's of Phoenician traders who kept their western routes secret (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 1.52).
