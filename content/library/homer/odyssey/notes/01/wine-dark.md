---
summary: The stranger says she is “sailing over the wine-dark sea to men of another tongue” (1.183).
---

The epithet belongs to Mentes' cover story of a trading voyage to Temese, for bronze, with a cargo of iron (1.182–184; [[temese]]). Merry reports that οἶνοψ may mean “glittering” or “gleaming,” like αἶθοψ, the epithet of wine, but that the older commentators agree in rendering it “dark,” as Eustathius does, who held that wine was anciently dark in color (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 1.183).
