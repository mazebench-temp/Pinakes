grc: ["ὣς ἔφαθʼ", "ὣς εἰποῦσʼ"]
en: [So Hermes spoke, So Telemachus spoke]
# ἦ καί at 1.158 and 1.389 is interrogative, and ἦ at 1.175 is the second member of a double question: not “he spoke and.”
except: [1.158, 1.175, 1.389]
---

The first “so he spoke” of the poem closes a message inside a speech: Zeus quotes what Hermes told Aegisthus and goes on, “So Hermes spoke, but the mind of Aegisthus / he did not persuade” (1.42–43; [[messenger-scene]]). Athena's two departures close with the same participle: from the council, as she binds on her sandals (1.96; [[dressing-scene]]), and from the house of Odysseus, in a whole verse of the *Iliad*, “So saying, gray-eyed Athena went away” (1.319; *Iliad* 5.133). At the end of the day the formula leads into the suitors' silence, “So he spoke, and they all, biting their lips with their teeth” (1.381; [[biting-their-lips]]), and into the knowledge Telemachus keeps to himself, “So Telemachus spoke, but in his heart he knew the immortal goddess” (1.420).

The words ἦ καί that elsewhere mean “he spoke, and” open questions in this book. Telemachus' “will you be indignant with me for what I say?” begins with an interrogative ἦ καί, to the stranger and to Antinous (1.158, 1.389), and at 1.175 ἦ is the second member of a double question, “whether you are newly come, or are a guest-friend,” which the ancient grammarians accented with the circumflex (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 1.175).
