---
summary: Strabo reads this book as a geographer. He takes its third line for the poets' praise of travel, argues over the two Ethiopians, Same and Samos, Ephyra of the drugs, the Taphians and the Argos of “Hellas and the heart of Argos”, and places Penelope's father in Acarnania.
---

- The man who “saw the cities of many men and learned their mind” as the poets' model of practical wisdom, given by Homer to Odysseus above all (*Geography* 1.1.16, 1.2.4, on 1.3): [[proem]].
- The Ethiopians “divided in two”, and the readings of Aristarchus and Crates (*Geography* 1.2.24–26, on 1.23–24): [[ethiopians]].
- “Dulichium and Same and wooded Zacynthus”, where Homer counting the islands calls Same the island that he elsewhere calls Samos (*Geography* 10.2.10, on 1.246): [[samos]].
- The Ephyra in Elis, on the Selleis, from which came the man-slaying drugs that Odysseus went to seek (*Geography* 8.3.5, on 1.259–262): [[ephyra]].
- The Taphians as friends of the Ithacans, guest-friends of Odysseus' house (*Geography* 10.2.24, on 1.180–188, 1.417): [[taphians]].
- “Hellas and the heart of Argos” as Homer's proof, against Thucydides, that the Hellenes were already one people, and μέσον Ἄργος among the places where Argos means the Peloponnese (*Geography* 14.2.28, 8.6.9, on 1.344): [[hellas]] and [[argos]].
- Icarius and Penelope's brothers in Acarnania, since Telemachus would not have lodged with Menelaus if they had lived in Lacedaemon (*Geography* 10.2.24, on 1.276 and 1.329): [[the-icarius-problem]] and [[icarius]].
