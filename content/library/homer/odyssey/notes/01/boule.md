---
summary: The gods' βουλή is Odysseus' homecoming: Hermes is to tell Calypso “our unerring counsel, / the homecoming of stout-hearted Odysseus” (1.86–87). For Telemachus the gods “have willed otherwise” (1.234), and the book ends with his own plan: “he planned in his mind the journey that Athena had shown him” (1.444).
grc: [ἐβόλοντο, βούλευε]
en: [willed, planned]
---

Merry takes νημερτέα βουλὴν νόστον as “that which is infallibly purposed, viz. that Odysseus shall return” (*Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 1.86; see [[nostos]] and [[plan-of-zeus]]). ἐβόλοντο stands for ἐβούλοντο, as βόλεσθε for βούλεσθε (16.387); Eustathius, Merry reports, read ἐβάλοντο, as if of a throw of the dice (on 1.234). The last verse of the book gives Telemachus the verb of the gods' counsel, βουλεύω, for the journey the goddess has shown him (see [[the-journey-of-telemachus]]).
