summary: Telemachus tells Eurymachus that the stranger is “my guest-friend from our fathers' time, from Taphos”, though in his heart he knows the goddess (1.417–420).
---

**From Taphos.** When Eurymachus asks where the stranger came from and whether he brought news of Odysseus (1.405–411), Telemachus repeats what the goddess told him and keeps to himself what he has understood:

{{quote:9d789e55-c791-5943-9dfc-72b90ca8b2b0}}

The island is named only here in Homer. As Mentes, Athena had said that she was lord over the Taphians, and that the two houses were guest-friends from their fathers' time, “from of old” (1.180–181, 1.187–188; see [[taphians]], [[mentes-the-taphian]] and [[xenia]]). Telemachus answers with the bond, πατρώιος ξεῖνος, “guest-friend … from our fathers' time”, and with the island.

**On the way from Ephyra.** Mentes first saw Odysseus “in our house”, that is, at Taphos, when he was on his way home from Ephyra with the drug for his arrows that Mentes' father gave him (1.257–264; see [[odysseus-seeks-poison]] and [[ephyra]]). Nitzsch argued from this that the Ephyra of the drug was the one in Thesprotia, since Taphos lies between Thesprotia and Ithaca; Merry answers that a ship sailing round the headland of Leucas to Ithaca would miss Taphos altogether, and that if the Taphian islands are placed among the Echinades, as a scholiast on Apollonius places them, the same reasoning favors the Ephyra in Elis (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 1.259).
