---
summary: Telemachus imagines his father dead: “a man whose white bones are rotting somewhere in the rain, / lying on the mainland, or the wave rolls them in the sea” (1.161–162).
grc: ["λεύκʼ ὀστέα"]
---

The white bones of the dead return twice in the Odyssey: in Anticleia's account of what happens when the spirit leaves the white bones (11.218–222), and in the shade of Agamemnon's account of the urn where Achilles' white bones lie (24.76). Telemachus' bones are imagined, and the stranger has just come to tell him that his father is alive (1.196–199). See [[odysseus]].
