---
summary: Hermes twice in the council, with two of his epithets: “the keen-sighted slayer of Argus,” sent to warn Aegisthus (1.38), and “the guide, the slayer of Argus,” to be sent to Calypso (1.84).
grc: [ἀργεϊφόντην]
---

The title closes the verse both times, with a different epithet before it each time: ἐύσκοπον ἀργεϊφόντην ([[keen-sighted]]) when Zeus tells of the warning Aegisthus did not heed (1.37–43), and διάκτορον ἀργεϊφόντην ([[guide]]) in Athena's plan (1.84–87). Merry reports several explanations of the title: he thinks the -ο- of -φόντης an Aeolic change for -α-, which would fit an old sense of brightness, “quickly and clearly showing,” as a scholium has it; he notes that Alcman calls a white cheese ἀργιφόντας and that the *Etymologicum Magnum* makes the word an epithet of the Sun; he gives the view of Hermes as a wind-god, “sky-clearing”; and he takes “slayer of Argus,” Io's many-eyed guardian, for a later etymology invented when the old sense had been lost (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 1.38).
