---
summary: Athena plans to send Telemachus to Sparta and “sandy Pylos” for news of his father, and bids him go first to Pylos and ask brilliant Nestor (1.93, 1.284).
---

Pylos is named twice, both times as a goal of the journey Athena plans for Telemachus. In the council she pairs it with Sparta, “to Sparta and to sandy Pylos” (1.93), with the city's standing epithet, ἠμαθόεις, “sandy”, as in the Iliad (*Iliad* 2.77; see [[sandy]] and [[sparta]]). In her counsel on Ithaca it comes first: “First go to Pylos and ask brilliant Nestor” (1.284; see [[nestor]] and [[the-journey-of-telemachus]]). The young man is to ask the oldest of the Achaean kings, and then the one who came home last (1.285–286). The voyage takes him to Pylos, “the well-built citadel of Neleus”, at the beginning of Book 3 (3.4).
