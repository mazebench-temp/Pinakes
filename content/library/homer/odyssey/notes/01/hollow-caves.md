---
summary: The two caves of the opening: Calypso holds Odysseus “in her hollow caves” (1.15), and in “the hollow caves” the nymph Thoosa lay with Poseidon and bore Polyphemus (1.73).
---

Both caves belong to nymphs, and in both a nymph unites, or wishes to unite, with a man or a god. The proem leaves Odysseus with the first: “the lady nymph Calypso, brilliant among goddesses, held back / in her hollow caves, longing for him to be her husband” (1.14–15; [[calypso]], [[brilliant-among-goddesses]]). Zeus names the second when he explains Poseidon's anger: the Cyclops whom Odysseus blinded was born to the nymph Thoosa, daughter of Phorcys, “having lain with Poseidon in the hollow caves” (1.71–73; [[thoosa]], [[polyphemus-the-cyclops]]). The one cave keeps Odysseus from home; from the other came the son whose blinding keeps him wandering (1.68–75). Odysseus himself uses the half-verse of 1.15 when he tells the Phaeacians of Calypso (9.29–30).
