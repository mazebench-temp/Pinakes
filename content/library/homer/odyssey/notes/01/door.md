---
summary: The book moves through the doors of Odysseus' house: Athena waits at the doorway, the suitors play in front of the doors, Telemachus wishes his father would stand at the outer doors armed, Penelope stands by the doorpost of the hall, and Eurycleia shuts Telemachus' chamber at night with the handle and the bolt (1.103–120, 1.255, 1.333, 1.436–442).
---

**The doorway.** Athena stands “at the doorway of Odysseus, / on the threshold of the courtyard” (1.103–104; see [[threshold]]), where a stranger waits to be received, and the suitors are delighting their hearts with game pieces “in front of the doors” (1.107; see [[game-pieces]]). Telemachus sees her first, “He went straight to the doorway, and he was indignant in his heart / that a stranger should stand long at the doors” (1.119–120; see [[xenia]] and [[nemesis]]).

**At the outer doors.** The goddess wishes that Odysseus would come “and stand at the outer doors of the house, / with helmet and shield and two spears” (1.255–256; see [[athena-visits-telemachus]]): the master of the house, armed, at his own door.

**The doorpost of the hall.** Penelope comes down among the suitors and “stood by the doorpost of the strongly made hall, / holding her shining veil before her cheeks” (1.333–334; see [[the-song-of-the-returns]] and [[veil]]), at the edge of the men's hall, in a verse that returns whenever she appears to them (*Odyssey* 16.415, 18.209, 21.64).

**The chamber door.** At night Telemachus “opened the doors of the strongly made chamber” (1.436), and when Eurycleia has hung up his tunic she “went out of the chamber, and pulled the door to by the handle / of silver, and drew home the bolt with its strap” (1.441–442; see [[eurycleia]]). W. W. Merry explains the fastening: the bolt slides on the inside of the door into the doorpost, and a strap tied to it runs out through a slit, so that one who has pulled the door shut by the handle can shoot the bolt home from outside (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 1.441; see the translation's note on 1.441–442).
