---
summary: The first use of the two verses: the suitors bite their lips and marvel at Telemachus' boldness after his first speech to them (1.381–382).
---

Telemachus has just spoken to the suitors as master of the house for the first time: let them feast now without shouting, but at dawn he will call an assembly and tell them to leave his halls, and if they go on eating his substance, Zeus may grant deeds of requital (1.368–380). The narrator gives their silence before he gives their answer:

{{quote:04669fff-3ec6-5ecc-be79-2cc5266956ad}}

Antinous then takes up the narrator's words in mockery: “surely the gods themselves are teaching you / to be a man of lofty speech and to speak boldly” (1.384–385), θαρσαλέως ἀγορεύειν answering θαρσαλέως ἀγόρευεν. The pair of verses comes back twice in the poem, each time after Telemachus has spoken out to the suitors again (see the entry). See [[telemachus-and-the-suitors]], [[antinous]] and [[the-suitors]].
