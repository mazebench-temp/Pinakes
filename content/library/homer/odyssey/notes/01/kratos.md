summary: Poseidon's son is “godlike Polyphemus, whose power is the greatest / among all the Cyclopes” (1.70–71), and Telemachus claims the power in the house, “for mine is the power in this house” (1.359). The suitors are the best men “who hold command over the islands” (ἐπικρατέουσιν, 1.245).
grc: [ἐπικρατέουσιν]
---

Merry weighs whether Κυκλώπεσσι means “in the eyes of” or “among” all the Cyclopes, and reports Ellendt's view that such datives are the object of the verb of power (*Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 1.71). On Telemachus' claim see [[war-is-the-concern-of-men]] and [[anax]].
