---
summary: “Then the gray-eyed goddess Athena answered him,” three times: twice to Zeus in the council (1.44, 1.80), and once to Telemachus, when she declines his gift (1.314).
---

The verse is the one with which Athena answers Diomedes in the *Iliad* (*Iliad* 5.825), and in the Odyssey it comes again three times in her meeting with Odysseus on Ithaca (13.329, 13.392, 13.420). In this book she answers Zeus with it, first agreeing about Aegisthus and turning to Odysseus (1.44–62), then proposing the plan (1.80–95), and last, in the likeness of Mentes, she uses it to refuse Telemachus' offer of a bath and a gift: “Do not hold me back now any longer” (1.314–318; [[guest-gifts]]).
