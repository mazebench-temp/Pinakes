---
summary: Telemachus sits among the suitors imagining his father home again, to “have his honor and rule over his own house” (1.117). He offers the stranger “a precious one” of his treasures (1.312), and tells Antinous that a king “himself is more honored” (1.393); Laertes “honored” Eurycleia in his halls as he honored his wife (1.432).
grc: [τιμῆεν, τιμηέστερος, τίεν]
en: [precious]
---

Merry gives Nitzsch's list of the prerogatives that make up a Homeric king's τιμή: the royal estate (τέμενος), tribute and fees, special gifts, daily maintenance at the public cost, and invitations to feasts; he compares Sarpedon's account of the honors of the Lycian kings, “a seat of honor, with meat, and with full cups” (*Iliad* 12.311), and the domain they hold (*Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 1.117; see [[kingship-and-authority]] and [[honor-and-dishonor]]). The τιμή Telemachus imagines for his father is to rule in his own house, where the suitors feast on another man's substance (1.160; see [[anax]] and [[basileus]]). The gift he offers his guest is τιμῆεν, “precious”, a thing of honor and worth (see [[guest-gifts]]).
