---
summary: Atreus is named only in his son's patronymic, Ἀτρεΐδαο, “of the son of Atreus”, twice in Zeus' story of Aegisthus (1.35, 1.40); the son is Agamemnon.
---

Ἀτρεΐδαο, the old genitive in -αο of Ἀτρεΐδης (1.35, 1.40; see [[son-of-atreus]] and [[agamemnon]]): Aegisthus married the wife “of the son of Atreus”, and “from Orestes vengeance will come for the son of Atreus” (see [[the-murder-of-agamemnon]]). In the second verse Merry rejects joining Ὀρέσταο Ἀτρεΐδαο, “Orestes, grandson of Atreus”, since it is against Homeric custom to use the grandfather's name for a patronymic except in the case of Achilles (*Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 1.40; see [[poine]] and the translation's note on 1.40). The book tells nothing of Atreus himself.
