# Unnamed: "but he would not / give it to him, since he feared the indignation of the gods who live forever" (1.262-263).
refs: [1.262-263]
---

ἐξ Ἐφύρης ἀνιόντα παρʼ Ἴλου Μερμερίδαο, “on his way back from Ephyra, from Ilus, son of Mermerus” (1.259; see [[ephyra]] and [[mermerus-father-of-ilus]]). Odysseus had sailed there for a drug to smear on his arrows: ἀλλʼ ὁ μὲν οὔ οἱ / δῶκεν, ἐπεί ῥα θεοὺς νεμεσίζετο αἰὲν ἐόντας, “but he would not / give it to him, since he feared the indignation of the gods who live forever” (1.262–263; see [[odysseus-seeks-poison]]). νεμεσίζομαι takes an accusative only here; Merry renders it “to stand in awe of”, literally to be angry with oneself, or ashamed of oneself, before another, as αἰδώς and νέμεσις go together at *Iliad* 13.122 (*Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 1.263; see [[nemesis]], [[aidos]] and the translation's note on 1.263). In the book that opens with Zeus' complaint that men bring sorrows on themselves by their own recklessness (1.32–43; see [[atasthalie]]), Ilus is a man who holds back from a deed for fear of the gods. Anchialus, Mentes' father, gave the drug for love of Odysseus (1.264; see [[anchialus-father-of-mentes]]).

He is not the Ilus of Troy, son of Tros, whom the Iliad remembers by his grave mound in the plain (see [[ilus]] and the translation's note on 1.259). The phrase Ἴλου Μερμερίδαο occurs only here.
