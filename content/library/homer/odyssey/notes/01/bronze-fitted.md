---
summary: Odysseus once sought a man-slaying drug at Ephyra “to smear his bronze-fitted arrows” (1.261–262).
grc: [χαλκήρεας]
---

The epithet belongs to the story the stranger tells of Odysseus' visit to Ilus, son of Mermerus, who would not give him the poison, “since he feared the indignation of the gods who live forever” (1.259–264; [[odysseus-seeks-poison]], [[man-slaying]]). Merry observes that poisoned arrows are not mentioned in the *Iliad*, and that the Odyssey alludes to them in words that show a strong feeling against the practice as barbarous or impious (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 1.263; see the translation's note on 1.263).
