---
summary: Athena's arming is a single act: “and she took a strong spear, tipped with sharp bronze, / heavy, huge, and massive” (1.99–100), in verses of the Iliad's armings.
grc: ["εἵλετο δʼ ἄλκιμον ἔγχος"]
---

The goddess puts on no armor. After the sandals of her journey she takes only the spear, with the last verse of an arming, εἵλετο δʼ ἄλκιμον ἔγχος, which in the *Iliad* closes the armings of Paris (*Iliad* 3.338), of Nestor (*Iliad* 10.135, 14.12) and of Teucer (*Iliad* 15.482), and with the verses of her own spear in the *Iliad* (*Iliad* 8.390–391; [[heavy-spear]], [[daughter-of-a-mighty-father]]). The weapon goes with her to Ithaca and is the first thing Telemachus takes from her at the door (1.104, 1.121; [[spear]], [[visit-scene]]). See [[dressing-scene]] and [[strong-spear]].
