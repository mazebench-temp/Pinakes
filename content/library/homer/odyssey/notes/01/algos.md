---
summary: The proem's hero “suffered many sorrows in his heart” on the sea (1.4), and in Zeus' speech mortals “through their own recklessness have sorrows against fate” (1.34).
---

Both poems name ἄλγεα in their opening lines: the wrath “laid countless sorrows on the Achaeans” (*Iliad* 1.2), and Odysseus suffered them on the sea (1.4). Telemachus' own griefs have other names, “pains and lamentations” (ὀδύνας τε γόους τε, 1.242) and “other evil sorrows” (κήδεʼ, 1.244; see [[penthos]] and [[who-is-to-blame]]).
