---
summary: The proem's hero “saw the cities of many men and learned their mind” (1.3), and Zeus calls him “first among mortals in mind” (1.66). Telemachus defends the singer's freedom to give delight “in whatever way his mind is roused” (1.347).
grc: [νόον, νόος, ἐνόησα, νοήσας]
en: [to see, first saw, perceiving]
---

**The minds of men.** νόον ἔγνω is in Merry's words “learned what their spirit was like”, and the scholiast, he reports, compares Odysseus' question on the shore of the Phaeacians, whether the people are violent, savage and unjust, or kind to strangers, with a god-fearing mind (*Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 1.3; 6.119–121; see [[polis]] and [[xenia]]).

**First in mind.** Zeus could not forget a man “who is first among mortals in mind, and first in sacrifices / given to the immortal gods” (1.66–67). Merry renders “beyond mortals in wisdom”, and compares Nestor's “first among the Danaans in counsel, and first in battle” (*Iliad* 1.258; on 1.66; see [[reciprocity-with-the-gods]]).

**The singer's mind.** Telemachus asks why his mother begrudges the singer “giving delight in whatever way his mind is roused” (1.346–347; see [[phemius]] and [[song-and-fame]]).

**Seeing.** The verb νοέω is “see, perceive”. Odysseus longs “to see even the smoke leaping up / from his own land” (1.58–59), which Merry glosses “were it but the smoke” (on 1.58); the stranger recalls when she first saw him (1.257); and Telemachus, “perceiving it in his mind”, knows that a god has been with him (1.322–323; see [[thambos]]).
