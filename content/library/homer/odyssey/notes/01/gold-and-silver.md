**The gods' gold.** Athena's sandals are “ambrosial, golden” (1.97; see [[sandals]]), as Hermes' are in the Iliad (*Iliad* 24.341).

**The house's gold and silver.** The stranger is welcomed with the vessels of a rich house: a handmaid pours the water for the hands from a pitcher, “a fine golden one, over a silver basin” (1.136–137; see [[basin-and-pitcher]] and [[hand-washing]]), and a carver sets out “golden cups” (1.142; see [[cup]]). Telemachus' chamber door is closed “by the handle / of silver” (1.441–442; see [[house]]). The suitors, Telemachus says, would pray to be nimbler of foot rather than “richer in gold and clothing” if Odysseus came home (1.164–165; see [[possessions]]).
