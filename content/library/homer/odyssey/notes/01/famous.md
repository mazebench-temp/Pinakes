# περικλυτὸς ἀμφιγυήεις, Hephaestus' title (Od. 8.300 and often), belongs to lame-in-both-legs.
grc: [τηλεκλυτὸς, "πατέρα κλυτὸν", περικλυτός, "!περικλυτὸς ἀμφιγυήεις"]
en: [far-famed, renowned]
---

Orestes is far-famed when Zeus first names him, as the son of Agamemnon who killed Aegisthus (1.29–30); Merry calls the epithet a prolepsis, since the vengeance he would take was to be the ground of his fame (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 1.30). Athena takes the same story as an example of fame for Telemachus: “Or have you not heard what fame brilliant Orestes won / among all men” (1.298–299), who killed the slayer of his famous father (1.300; [[orestes]], [[kleos]]). The singer, whose songs make men famous (κλείουσιν, 1.338), is himself περικλυτός (1.325; [[phemius]]). In the Odyssey περικλυτός is also part of Hephaestus' title, περικλυτὸς ἀμφιγυήεις ([[lame-in-both-legs]]).
