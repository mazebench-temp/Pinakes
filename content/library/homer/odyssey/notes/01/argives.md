---
summary: The Argives are the army at Troy, beside whose ships Odysseus sacrificed to Zeus, and whose best men went to Troy in their hollow ships (1.61, 1.211).
---

Both times the name looks back to the war. Athena asks Zeus whether Odysseus did not please him “beside the ships of the Argives, offering sacrifices / in wide Troy” (1.61–62; see [[troy]] and [[zeus]]). In the stranger's tale, Mentes last saw Odysseus before he embarked for Troy, “where the others too, / the best of the Argives, went in their hollow ships” (1.210–211; see [[hollow-ships]] and [[mentes-the-taphian]]). Achaeans and Danaans are the army's other names (see [[achaeans]] and [[danaans]]).
