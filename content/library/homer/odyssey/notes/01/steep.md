---
summary: “Sheer destruction” twice in the first verses of the poem: the returned Achaeans had escaped it (1.11), and Aegisthus knew it was coming (1.37).
grc: ["αἰπὺν ὄλεθρον"]
en: [sheer]
---

The phrase αἰπὺν ὄλεθρον ends both verses. The proem uses it of the Achaeans who are home, “as many as had escaped sheer destruction” (1.11), and Zeus of Aegisthus, who married Agamemnon's wife and killed him on his return, “knowing it was sheer destruction” (1.36–37; [[aegisthus]]). Merry reports Nitzsch's view that αἰπύς here means any form of death that comes suddenly, and finds it more graphic to render it “headlong” or “sheer,” violent death being thought of as a plunge from a height (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 1.11); on 1.37 he renders “though he knew it would be his utter destruction,” and notes that the gods' ἡμεῖς is emphatic: they were not the cause (on 1.37).
