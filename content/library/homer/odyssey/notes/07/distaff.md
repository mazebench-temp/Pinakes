---
summary: The slave women of Alcinous who are not at the mills weave at the looms and turn the yarn as they sit (7.105–106).
---

“and others weave at the looms and turn the yarn / as they sit, like the leaves of a tall poplar” (7.105–106; see [[loom]], [[weaving]] and [[the-house-of-alcinous]]). ἠλάκατα στρωφῶσιν, “turn the yarn”, is the work that Nausicaa found her mother doing at the hearth at dawn, “turning the sea-purple yarn” (6.53, 6.306; see the Book 6 note and [[arete-queen-of-the-phaeacians]]): the queen and her slave women spin alike. The Suda glosses ἠλάκατα as “fine threads” (η 190). The simile of the leaves catches the constant movement of the women at their task, W. W. Merry thinks, rather than their sitting close together as leaves grow, as Nitzsch took it (*Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 7.106; see the translation's note on 7.106 and [[like-the-leaves-of-a-poplar]]).
