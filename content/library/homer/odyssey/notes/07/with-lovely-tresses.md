---
summary: Athena, “of the lovely tresses, the dread goddess, would not allow it” (7.41), and Calypso twice in Odysseus' story, “she of the lovely tresses, the dread goddess” (7.246, 7.255).
---

Merry notes that the epithet is given to Athena only here, though the Iliad calls her ἠύκομος, “of the lovely hair” (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 7.41, citing *Iliad* 6.92, 6.273, 6.303; [[lovely-haired]]). Elsewhere in the Odyssey the phrase belongs to the goddesses who keep Odysseus, Calypso and Circe (see the Odyssey translation's formulas and [[dread-goddess]]).
