---
summary: In his tale to Arete Odysseus tells of two shores of Scheria: the rocky coast where the wave would have dashed him against great rocks, “a joyless place” (7.278–279), and the shore by the river where the next day he saw Nausicaa's handmaids playing, and the girl among them, “like the goddesses” (7.290–291).
---

**The joyless rocks.** “There, as I came out onto the land, the wave would have overpowered me, / dashing me against great rocks, a joyless place” (7.278–279; see [[sea]] and [[the-shipwreck-of-odysseus]]). W. W. Merry renders κέ μʼ … βιήσατο “would have crushed me as I climbed out upon the shore”, ἐκβαίνειν being used as in Book 5 of a man coming out of the sea onto the land (5.415), and takes καί as explaining, “against the huge rocks, that ugly spot” (*Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 7.278, 7.279). He compares the rock to which Prometheus is bound, ἀτερπῆ τήνδε … πέτραν, “this joyless rock” (Aeschylus, *Prometheus Bound* 31). It is the coast of Book 5, all reefs and sheer rock, with no beach where the waves strike slanting (5.400–440; see the Book 5 note). The place he reached instead was the river's mouth, “smooth, free of rocks” (7.282 = 5.443; see [[rivers]]).

**The shore of the playing girls.** “And I noticed your daughter's handmaids on the shore / playing, and she herself was among them, like the goddesses” (7.290–291; see [[handmaids]], [[nausicaa]] and [[like-the-goddesses]]). θίς is the strand by the sea, where the clothes had been spread to dry and the girls played at ball, and from which they fled over the jutting beaches when he came out of the bushes (6.94–117, 6.138; see the Book 6 note). Of the ball, the cry that woke him and the flight he says nothing: in his telling the girls are playing, and the king's daughter is among them.
