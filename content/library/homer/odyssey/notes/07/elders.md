---
summary: Alcinous will call “more of the elders” at dawn to entertain the stranger and consider his escort (7.189–191); the leaders who feast in his hall are men of the same council, and the old Echeneus, “one of the older Phaeacian men”, is the first to speak for the suppliant (7.155–166).
---

**More of the elders.** When the feast is over, Alcinous sends the leaders home: “and at dawn we will call more of the elders / and entertain the stranger in the halls, and to the gods / offer fine sacrifices, and then we will remember his escort too” (7.189–191; see [[xenia]] and [[assembly]]). ἐπί goes with καλέσαντες, “bidding them gather to the meeting” (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 7.189; see the translation's note on 7.189). The “leaders and rulers of the Phaeacians” whom he is addressing (7.186) are of their number, since he will call “more” of them; in the eighth book he counts twelve kings who rule in the land, and himself the thirteenth (8.390–391, Greek only; see [[phaeacians]] and [[kings]]).

**The old man speaks first.** While Odysseus sits in the ashes and the company is silent, “the old hero Echeneus” speaks, “who was one of the older Phaeacian men, / and excelled in speaking, knowing many things of old” (7.155–157; see [[echeneus]] and [[geron]]). προγενέστερος does not govern Φαιήκων, a partitive genitive, but stands alone as a qualifying comparative, like our “elderly” (Merry on 7.156, comparing γεραίτερος, 3.362). So in the assembly of Ithaca it is an old man who speaks first, the hero Aegyptius, who “was bent with age and knew countless things” (2.15–16; see [[aegyptius]]).
