---
summary: “Much-enduring brilliant Odysseus” closes six verses of the book, from the first, “So he prayed there, much-enduring brilliant Odysseus” (7.1), to “So he lay sleeping there, much-enduring brilliant Odysseus” (7.344); between them he stands and gazes at the house and garden (7.133), goes through the house in the mist (7.139), drinks and eats (7.177), and is glad at the promise of his escort (7.329).
---

The two verses that frame the book are built on the first verse of Book 6, which 7.344 repeats (6.1; see the Odyssey translation's formulas). 7.177 is the verse of his meal by the river, “Then much-enduring brilliant Odysseus drank and ate” (6.249), and 7.329, “So he spoke, and much-enduring brilliant Odysseus was glad,” is said again in Books 8, 13 and 18 (8.199, 13.250, 18.281).
