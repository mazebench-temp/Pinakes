summary: The house of Alcinous is a house of gold and silver: golden doors on silver doorposts with a silver lintel and a golden handle, gold and silver dogs on either side, and golden youths holding torches in the hall (7.88–101). The stranger washes from a golden pitcher over a silver basin and is seated on a silver-studded chair (7.162, 7.172–173); Rhexenor was struck down by Apollo of the silver bow (7.64).
# Forms first met here (they apply in the Odyssey only): χρύσειαι (7.88; later 18.294), χρύσειοι (7.91, 7.100) and
# ἀργύρεοι (7.89, 7.91). χρυσέη (7.90) is caught by the Book 6 note's χρυσέῃ, χρυσείῃ (7.173) by the Book 1 note.

**A house of gold and silver.** After the bronze walls and their coping, the house is gold and silver:

{{quote:6353f4a3-bba1-5667-b398-241f72ec9eb0}}

“And on either side were golden and silver dogs” (7.91; see [[the-gold-and-silver-dogs]]), and within, “golden young men on well-built pedestals / stood, holding blazing torches in their hands” (7.100–101; see [[torches]] and [[the-house-of-alcinous]]). The metals are those of the gods' houses and of Menelaus' halls, where Telemachus saw “the flash of bronze throughout the echoing halls, / and of gold and amber and silver and ivory” (4.72–73; see the Book 4 note and [[bronze]]). Gold and silver figures are a craftsman's art in the Iliad too: on the shield of Achilles “golden herdsmen walked in line with the cattle” (*Iliad* 18.577; see [[the-shield-of-achilles]]), and W. W. Merry takes the golden youths and the gold and silver dogs of Alcinous for images of the same kind (*Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 7.91).

**For the guest.** Echeneus bids Alcinous seat the stranger “on a silver-studded chair” (7.162; see [[silver-studded]] and [[throne]]), and a handmaid brings water for his hands “in a pitcher and poured it, / a fine golden one, over a silver basin” (7.172–173), the verses of every guest's welcome (1.136–137; see [[basin-and-pitcher]] and [[water-for-the-hands]]).

**The silver bow.** Rhexenor, Arete's father, was struck down by “Apollo, god of the silver bow” (7.64; see [[apollo]], [[god-of-the-silver-bow]] and [[rhexenor]]).
