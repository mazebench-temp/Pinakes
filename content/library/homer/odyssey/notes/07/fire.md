---
summary: When Nausicaa comes home in the evening, her old chamber-woman lights a fire for her in her chamber (7.7, 7.13); and Odysseus, when he has clasped Arete's knees, sits down at the hearth in the ashes, beside the fire (7.153–154).
---

**Nausicaa's fire.** “And she herself went to her own chamber; and a fire was kindled for her / by an old woman from Apeire, her chamber-woman Eurymedusa” (7.7–8; see [[eurymedusa]] and [[nausicaa]]); “She lit the fire for her, and made ready her supper within” (7.13). A scholium takes the fire as a sign that the season was winter, and the older interpreters put this part of the wanderings late in the year, as the stormy night of rain and west wind in Eumaeus' hut may suggest (14.457–458); W. W. Merry finds the evening reason enough, the fire serving for light or warmth or both, as the slave women of Odysseus' house heap wood on the braziers, φόως ἔμεν ἠδὲ θέρεσθαι, to give light and warmth (19.64, a plain rendering; *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 7.7; see the translation's note on 7.7). Zenodotus athetized 7.13, since 7.7 has already said that the fire was lit (Merry's critical note; see the translation's note on 7.13).

**The fire at the hearth.** Nausicaa had told Odysseus that he would find her mother sitting “at the hearth in the light of the fire” (6.305; see the Book 6 note). He finds the queen, clasps her knees, makes his plea for an escort, and “He spoke thus and sat down at the hearth in the ashes / beside the fire; and they all fell hushed in silence” (7.153–154; see [[hearth]], [[supplication]] and [[silence]]). From the hearth Alcinous raises him by the hand, ἀπʼ ἐσχαρόφιν, and seats him on a shining chair (7.167–169; see [[throne]]).
