# πεποιθότες (7.34; again 9.107, of the Cyclopes, "trusting in the immortal gods"). ἔπειθεν (7.258) is in the Book 2
# note.
grc: [πεποιθότες]
en: [trusting]
---

The perfect πέποιθα is “trust”, and the book gives it to the Phaeacians in Athena's warning: they put up with no strangers, “Trusting in the speed of their swift ships, / they cross the great gulf, since the earth-shaker granted it to them” (7.34–35; see [[phaeacians]] and [[swift-ships]]). θοός is the conventional epithet of ships and ὠκύς the one that means something here, “the speed of their swift ships” (W. W. Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 7.34; see the translation's note on 7.34). The Cyclopes, in Book 9, trust in the gods in the same participle and do not plow or plant (9.106–108). The active is persuasion, and the book has it once, at the turning point of the stranger's story: the goddess said that she would make him immortal and ageless, “but she never persuaded the heart in my breast” (7.257–258; see [[immortality]] and [[calypso]]). He will say it again to Penelope (23.337).
