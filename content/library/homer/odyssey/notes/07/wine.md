---
summary: Wine is mixed for the libation to Zeus, the god of suppliants, when the stranger has been raised from the hearth (7.163–165, 7.179–183). In his story Odysseus tells of the sweet wine that Calypso gave him for the raft and the sparkling wine that Nausicaa gave him by the river (7.265, 7.295).
---

**For Zeus of the suppliants.** Echeneus bids Alcinous have the heralds “mix the wine, so that to Zeus who delights in thunder / we may pour libations too, who attends on revered suppliants” (7.164–165; see [[echeneus]] and [[a-suppliant-is-respected-by-the-gods]]), and the king gives the order to Pontonous: “mix a bowl and serve out wine / to all throughout the hall” (7.179–180; see [[mixing-bowl]] and [[pontonous]]). “So he spoke, and Pontonous mixed the wine sweet to the heart” (7.182; see [[libation]] and [[cup]]). μελίφρων is “honey-hearted”, in Tennyson's phrase, which gives the wine a kind of personality, W. W. Merry notes, like the English “generous wine” or the Odyssey's “wine that makes men strong” (4.622); the adjective is also said of wheat and of sleep in the Iliad and of bread in the Odyssey (*Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 7.182; *Iliad* 8.188, 2.34; 24.489). The Suda glosses it “sweet, sweetening the mind” (μ 530).

**In the story.** Calypso sent Odysseus off with “bread and sweet wine” (7.265), as she had loaded the raft with a skin of dark wine (5.265; see [[provisions]] and [[calypso]]), and Nausicaa “gave me bread in plenty and sparkling wine” (7.295; see [[nausicaa]]), the wine of her own provisions (6.77–78). αἴθοψ, said in Homer of wine, of bronze and of smoke, probably means “fiery-looking”, Merry thinks, whether of the bright color of wine, the flashing surface of metal or the gleam of fire through smoke (on 7.295; see the entry).
