summary: The supplication of Arete: the stranger comes through the hall unseen, “And Odysseus threw his arms around the knees of Arete” (7.142); his plea (7.146–152); he sits in the ashes of the hearth until Echeneus speaks (7.153–166); and Alcinous raises him by the hand and seats him in his son's chair (7.167–171).
refs: [7.139-171]
---

**Approach and gesture.** Nausicaa had told him to pass her father by and clasp her mother's knees (6.310–315); he does so, and the mist that hid him flows back at the touch (7.139–145; [[achlys]], [[gounata]]).

**Appeal.** “to your husband and to your knees I have come, after toiling much” (7.147): the verse of his prayer to the river, with the husband in place of the stream (5.449). He blesses the banqueters, that each may hand on to his children his possessions and the prize the people gave him, and asks for an escort home (7.148–152).

**The wait.** He sits in the ashes “beside the fire,” and all fall silent (7.153–154; [[sat-down-stood-up]], [[hearth]]); the answer comes from the old Echeneus, who tells the king to raise him and to pour a libation to Zeus, “who attends on revered suppliants” (7.155–166; [[with-good-intent]]).

**Acceptance.** Alcinous takes him by the hand and seats him on a shining chair (7.167–171; [[phaeinos]], [[manly]]), and the libation is poured (7.179–183). Odysseus later says that he came to Nausicaa as a suppliant, and Alcinous that “it was to her first that you came as a suppliant” (7.292, 7.301).
