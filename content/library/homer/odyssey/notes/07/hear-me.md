---
summary: Alcinous to the Phaeacian lords after the libation: “Hear me, leaders and rulers of the Phaeacians, / so that I may say what the heart in my breast commands me” (7.186–187).
grc: ["κέκλυτε Φαιήκων ἡγήτορες ἠδὲ μέδοντες", "ὄφρʼ εἴπω τά με θυμὸς"]
en: [so that I may say what the heart in my breast commands me]
---

The first verse is the Phaeacian form of the address, which Alcinous uses four times more in Book 8 (8.26, 8.97, 8.387, 8.536); the second is the Iliad's (*Iliad* 7.68, 7.349, 7.369, 8.6; see the Odyssey translation's formulas). The speech that follows sends the lords home to bed and promises the stranger his escort (7.188–206; [[alcinous]], [[hegemon]], [[pompe]]).
