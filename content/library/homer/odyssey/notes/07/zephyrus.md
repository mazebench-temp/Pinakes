# Ζεφυρίη (7.119), the feminine adjective of the wind's name used as a noun, "the West Wind", is found only here in
# Homer; it is claimed here rather than in the entry, whose patterns are the noun's own forms.
grc: [Ζεφυρίη]
---

**The breeze of the orchard.** Outside the court of Alcinous lies an orchard of pears, pomegranates, apples, figs and olives, and “Of these the fruit never perishes or fails, / in winter or in summer, lasting all the year; but always / the West Wind, blowing, brings some to growth and ripens others” (7.117–119; see [[the-garden-of-alcinous]] and [[trees]]). The wind is the reason the orchard bears at every season: pear grows old on pear and apple on apple (7.120–121). Ζεφυρίη is the adjective of Ζέφυρος used as a noun, one of a number of such feminines, with ὑγρή, “the waters” (1.97), ἀμβροσίη, “ambrosia” (4.445), and σχεδίη, “raft” (5.33), in the list Merry takes from Ameis (*Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 7.119 and on 1.97; see the translation's key-word row).

**Elysium and Scheria.** The verse is shaped like the Old Man of the Sea's description of the Elysian plain, where there is no snow or storm or rain, “but always the breezes of the West Wind, blowing shrill, / Oceanus sends up to refresh men” (4.567–568; see [[elysium]]): ἀλλʼ αἰεί there, ἀλλὰ μάλʼ αἰεί here, and the participle of πνέω in both. In the Iliad and in the storms of the Odyssey the West Wind is a wind of squalls and high seas (5.295, 12.289, 12.408; see the general article); in these two places it is a constant mild breeze.
