---
summary: “and white-armed Arete called to her handmaids / to set beds beneath the colonnade, and throw fine purple / blankets on them, and spread rugs over them, / and put woolly cloaks on top for them to wear” (7.335–338); the women go out with torches, make the bed, and call the stranger to it (7.339–342).
---

The verses are those of the guests' beds at Sparta and in Achilles' hut (4.297–300; *Iliad* 24.644–647). Merry notes that στόρεσαν, “spread,” sums up the work told in the verses before, that λέχος here is the bedstead, firmly framed, and that ἐγκονέουσαι, “in haste,” is found only in the feminine participle, here, at 23.291 and in *Iliad* 24.648 (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 7.340). The guest sleeps beneath the colonnade and the king in the inner part of the high house beside the queen (7.344–347), as at Pylos and at Sparta (3.399–403, 4.302–305; [[corded]], [[bed]], [[handmaids]], [[arete-queen-of-the-phaeacians]]).
