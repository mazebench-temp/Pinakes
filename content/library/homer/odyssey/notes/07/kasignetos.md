summary: When Nausicaa comes home, “her brothers stood / around her, like the immortals”, unyoked the mules and carried the clothing inside (7.4–6).
# κασίγνητοι (7.4) is in the entry.
---

The brothers of Book 6 were a reason for clean clothes, five sons of Alcinous who want freshly washed garments for the dance (6.62–65; see the note there). Here they receive the washing: they stand around their sister at the doorway, ἀθανάτοις ἐναλίγκιοι, “like the immortals”, take the mules from the wagon and carry the clothing in (7.4–6; see [[like-the-immortals]], [[mules]] and [[wagon]]). στῆσεν, “she halted”, has the mules for its object (W. W. Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 7.4). One of them is named later in the evening: Laodamas, the son whom Alcinous loved most of all, who gives up his chair beside his father to the stranger (7.170–171; see [[laodamas-son-of-alcinous]]).
