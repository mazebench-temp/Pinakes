---
summary: Alcinous promises that the stranger will reach his home under the Phaeacians' escort “without toil and vexation” (ἄνευθε πόνου καὶ ἀνίης, 7.192), as their crew once went to Euboea and back in a day “without weariness” (7.325).
# πόνου (7.192) is in the entry.
---

The escort is a promise of ease after toil. The stranger has come “after toiling much” (πολλὰ μογήσας, 7.147) and could tell of all that he “toiled through by the will of the gods” (7.214; see [[algos]] and [[supplication]]); the king answers that he will go home ἄνευθε πόνου καὶ ἀνίης, “without toil and vexation” (7.192; see [[pompe]]). ἀνίη, “vexation”, is found only here in Homer as a noun (see the translation's table of key words). The Phaeacians' own voyages are like that: when they took Rhadamanthys to Euboea, “they went there, and accomplished it without weariness / on the same day, and made their way back home” (ἄτερ καμάτοιο, 7.325–326; see [[the-voyage-of-rhadamanthus]]).
