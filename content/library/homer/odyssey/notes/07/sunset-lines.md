---
summary: “And the sun was declining, and sweet sleep let me go” (7.289).
grc: ["δείλετό τʼ ἠέλιος"]
en: [the sun was declining]
---

δείλετο, “was declining,” is Aristarchus' reading, as Eustathius reports, where others read δύσετο, “set,” the half-verse of the Odyssey's sunsets (2.388, 6.321). Merry prints δύσετο and, rather than force the time of day, allows that epic poetry uses formulas “with a certain carelessness” once they have become formulas (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 7.289; see the translation's note on 7.289). Odysseus had slept “all night long, and into the dawn and to midday” (7.288; [[sweet-sleep]], [[helios]]).
