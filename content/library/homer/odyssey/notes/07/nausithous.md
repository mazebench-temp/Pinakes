# Ναυσίθο* (in the Book 6 note) claims Ναυσίθοον (7.56, 7.62) and Ναυσίθοος (7.63). Unnamed: "begot a son" (7.61).
refs: [7.61]
---

**The son of Poseidon.** Book 6 told of the founder who led the Phaeacians to Scheria and built their city (6.4–12); this book names his parents. To explain who the queen is, Athena goes back to him: “Nausithous first was born to Poseidon the earth-shaker / and Periboea, most beautiful in form of women, / the youngest daughter of great-hearted Eurymedon”, and after the fate of Eurymedon and his Giants, “And Poseidon lay with her and begot a son, / great-hearted Nausithous, who was lord among the Phaeacians; / and Nausithous begot Rhexenor and Alcinous” (7.56–63; see [[poseidon]], [[periboea-mother-of-nausithous]], [[eurymedon-king-of-the-giants]] and [[giants]]). His sons are the king and the queen's father, so that Athena can say that Arete comes “of the same / parents who bore King Alcinous” (7.54–55; see [[rhexenor]], [[alcinous]] and [[arete-queen-of-the-phaeacians]]). The earth-shaker, who, as Athena says, granted the Phaeacians to cross the great gulf in their swift ships, is their founder's father (7.34–35; see [[phaeacians]]).

**His epithet.** μεγάθυμος, “great-hearted” (7.62; see [[great-hearted]]); in Book 6 he is θεοειδής, “godlike” (6.7). His prophecy of Poseidon's anger against the Phaeacians, which Alcinous will remember twice, is not told in this book (8.564–571, 13.172–178, Greek only; see the entry and [[the-migration-of-the-phaeacians]]).
