---
summary: The dogs stand on either side at the house of Alcinous, among the wonders that Odysseus sees before he crosses the bronze threshold (7.91–94).
---

The dogs come in the description that explains Odysseus' long pause before the threshold (7.82–94; see [[the-house-of-alcinous]] and [[threshold]]): after the bronze walls with their coping of dark blue enamel, the golden doors, the silver doorposts and lintel and the golden handle, “on either side were golden and silver dogs” (7.91; see [[kyanos]], [[door]] and [[gold-and-silver]]). The verse of their task ends δῶμα φυλασσέμεναι μεγαλήτορος Ἀλκινόοιο, “to guard the house of great-hearted Alcinous” (7.93), as the verse that began the description ended δῶμα καθʼ ὑψερεφὲς μεγαλήτορος Ἀλκινόοιο, “throughout the high-roofed house of great-hearted Alcinous” (7.85).

Later in the same night Odysseus tells Arete that Calypso promised to make him “immortal and ageless all my days”, “but she never persuaded the heart in my breast” (7.257–258; see [[calypso]] and [[immortal-and-ageless]]). The dogs of the house have from Hephaestus what its guest refused from a goddess (7.94; see [[hephaestus]]).
