summary: The feast in the hall of Alcinous: the lords “feasting at their feast” (7.49–50) on the chairs along the walls, “drinking and eating” (7.98–99), and the last libation of the night (7.136–138); the stranger served and fed (7.172–177); the wine mixed and the libation to Zeus, “But when they had poured libations and drunk as much as their hearts desired” (7.179–184); and the same verse again before the guests go home and the handmaids clear away the gear of the feast (7.228–232).
refs: [7.49-50, 7.98-99, 7.136-138, 7.172-184, 7.228-232]
---

The libation verse is the Iliad's (*Iliad* 9.177) and Nestor's at Pylos (3.342, 3.395). Merry notes that it has been used already at 7.184, and suggests that during the conversation the guests have kept their cups in their hands, against Nitzsch's view that the first may be an interpolation (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 7.228).
