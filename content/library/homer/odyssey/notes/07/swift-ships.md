summary: Athena of the Phaeacians: “Trusting in the speed of their swift ships, / they cross the great gulf” (7.34–35), and “their ships are swift as a wing or a thought” (7.36); they are skilled “in driving a swift ship on the sea” (7.109); and Odysseus tells of “my swift ship,” shattered by Zeus' thunderbolt (7.249–250).
grc: ["νηυσὶ θοῇσιν", "πεποιθότες ὠκείῃσι", "νέες ὠκεῖαι"]
---

At 7.34 θοῇσιν is the conventional epithet and ὠκείῃσι the special one, and Merry finds that even “trusting in the speed of their swift ships” does not subdue “swift” enough, so emptied of meaning has it been by use as a class epithet; he cites an imitation in Sophocles' *Ajax*, θοῶν ὠκυάλων νεῶν (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 7.34; see the translation's note on 7.34). The speed of the ships is the point of the simile that follows (7.36; [[like-wings]], [[the-travelers-thought]], [[phaeacians]], [[ships]]).
