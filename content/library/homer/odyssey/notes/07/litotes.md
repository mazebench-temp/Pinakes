---
summary: Athena: “For these men do not much put up with strangers” (7.32); Arete “is in no way lacking in good sense” (7.73); Echeneus: “this is not more honorable” (7.159); Alcinous: the gods “do not hide themselves at all” (7.205); Odysseus of Nausicaa: “she did not fall short of good sense” (7.292); and Alcinous of his daughter: “this, at least, she did not devise rightly” (7.299).
refs: [7.32, 7.73, 7.159, 7.205, 7.292, 7.299]
---

Merry calls the first a strong litotes, “not over and above fond of,” meaning “not at all fond of” (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 7.32), and takes κάλλιον at 7.159 as “not more honourable” than the Phaeacians' ordinary custom of welcoming a stranger (Merry on 7.159; see the translation's note on 7.159). Mother and daughter are praised in the same negative form, for their good sense (7.73, 7.292; [[arete-queen-of-the-phaeacians]], [[nausicaa]], [[noos]]).
