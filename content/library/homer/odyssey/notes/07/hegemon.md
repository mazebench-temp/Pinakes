summary: The Phaeacian lords are “the leaders and rulers of the Phaeacians” (ἡγήτορες ἠδὲ μέδοντες): they sit along the walls of Alcinous' hall drinking and eating (7.98–99), Odysseus finds them pouring the last libation to Hermes (7.136–138), and Alcinous addresses them so when he sends them home (7.186). Athena, in the likeness of a girl, is the stranger's guide: “I will lead the way” (7.30).
# ἡγήτορας, μέδοντας (7.136). ἡγήτορες (7.98, 7.186) and μέδοντες (7.186) are in the entry.
grc: [ἡγήτορας, μέδοντας]
---

ἡγήτωρ, “leader”, and μέδων, “ruler”, are the words of the Iliad's rallying verse, ὦ φίλοι, Ἀργείων ἡγήτορες ἠδὲ μέδοντες, “Friends, leaders and rulers of the Argives” (see the entry). In Scheria they name the king's peers at his table: “There the leaders of the Phaeacians used to sit, / drinking and eating; for they had unfailing store” (7.98–99; see [[the-house-of-alcinous]]), and “he found the leaders and rulers of the Phaeacians / pouring libations from their cups to the keen-sighted slayer of Argus” (7.136–137; see [[hermes]] and [[libation]]). Alcinous addresses them with the verse, “Hear me, leaders and rulers of the Phaeacians” (7.186), which he will use four times more in Book 8 (8.26, 8.97, 8.387, 8.536; see the translation's table of formulas and [[kings]]). The word's root is the verb of going before, ἡγέομαι, and the book's first guide is a goddess: Odysseus asks the girl with the water jar, “will you not lead me to the house of the man / Alcinous” (7.22–23), and she answers, “I will lead the way” (ἡγεμονεύσω, 7.30), and “Pallas Athena led the way / swiftly” (7.37–38; see [[athena]] and [[followed-in-the-footprints]]).
