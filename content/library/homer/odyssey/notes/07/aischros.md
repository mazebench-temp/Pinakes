---
summary: Odysseus tells Alcinous that he would not follow Nausicaa into the town “out of fear and shame” (δείσας αἰσχυνόμενός τε, 7.305).
# αἰσχυνόμενος (7.305) is in the entry.
---

The active αἰσχύνω is “disgrace, disfigure”: Glaucus' father told him not to shame the race of his fathers (*Iliad* 6.209), and Hermes tells Priam that Achilles does not disfigure Hector's body (*Iliad* 24.418). The middle, “be ashamed”, is a word of the Odyssey, and this is its first use: the stranger's shame before the king, which he gives as the reason why he did not come to the house with the girl and her handmaids (7.303–306; see [[quick-to-anger]] and [[deos]]). The present participle, beside the aorist δείσας, denotes the abiding condition of modesty, where the aorist is the sudden fear (W. W. Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 7.305; see [[aidos]]). Later the same participle names the suitors' shame before “the talk of men and women”, the fear that Nausicaa had felt (21.323, a plain rendering; see [[the-talk-of-the-people]]), and Irus, bidding the old beggar get away from the doorway before he is dragged out by the foot, says that he is ashamed to do it all the same (18.10–12).
