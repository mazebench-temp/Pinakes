---
summary: Odysseus to Alcinous: “for there is nothing more shameless than the hateful belly” (7.216).
grc: [στυγερῇ]
---

Merry takes ἐπί with the dative in its sense “beyond,” a turn of comparison that he finds also in Herodotus and Thucydides (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 7.216, citing Herodotus 4.118 and Thucydides 3.45). The hateful belly bids a man remember it “even one sorely worn down and with grief in his heart” (7.218), and the epithet of war and death is turned on the most ordinary of needs (7.215–221; [[gaster]], [[penthos]]).
