---
summary: In his story to Arete Odysseus tells the loss of his last ship in one verse: “There all my other noble companions perished” (7.251).
# ἑταῖροι (7.251) is in the entry.
---

The verse is the one with which Hermes and Calypso told the same wreck in Book 5, in the third person, “There all his other noble companions perished” (5.110, 5.133; see the Book 5 note). Here it is his own, after Zeus struck and shattered his swift ship with the thunderbolt in the middle of the sea (7.249–250; see [[thunderbolt]] and [[the-shipwreck-of-odysseus]]), and he passes at once to his nine days on the keel. The companions are not named, and their fault, the cattle of the Sun, is not told; that comes in his tale in Book 12 (12.260–419). The scholia report that the eight verses 7.251–258 were athetized because the story is told later, and Merry finds 7.254–255 the most suspicious of them (W. W. Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, critical note on 7.251–258; see the translation's note there).
