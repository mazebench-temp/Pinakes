---
summary: Alcinous: the gods do not hide themselves from the Phaeacians, “since we are near to them, / as are the Cyclopes and the savage tribes of the Giants” (7.205–206).
refs: [7.205]
---

Merry reports that ἐγγύθεν is generally taken here, like ἀγχίθεοι (5.35, 19.279), as “near-related,” and that Welcker took it in its usual sense, “neighbouring to,” as though the Phaeacians lived in a celestial country like the Hyperboreans; this suits Nitzsch's remark that the peoples at the edge of the world are the gods' favorites, as the Ethiopians whom Poseidon visits, “the farthest of men” (1.23), and Herodotus' saying that the ends of the inhabited earth have the finest things (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 7.205; Herodotus 3.106.1). The English “near to them” keeps both senses (see the translation's note on 7.205; [[phaeacians]], [[cyclopes]], [[giants]], [[seeing-the-gods]]).
