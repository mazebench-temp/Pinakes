---
summary: The Phaeacians are heroes too: Odysseus marvels at “the places of assembly of the heroes themselves” (7.44); the old Echeneus is “the old hero” (7.155); and Odysseus addresses the king as ἥρως, “Hero”, when he defends Nausicaa (7.303).
# ἡρώων (7.44) and ἥρως (7.155, 7.303) are in the entry and its Odyssey notes.
---

ἥρως is a title of respect, and the Phaeacians have it, though they care nothing for bow or quiver (6.270). The stranger's wonder passes from the harbors, the ships and the walls to the men themselves, αὐτῶν θʼ ἡρώων ἀγοράς (7.44): αὐτῶν marks the turn from the city to its people (W. W. Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 7.44; see [[the-city-of-the-phaeacians]] and [[assembly]]). γέρων ἥρως Ἐχένηος, “the old hero Echeneus” (7.155), is said as of the old men of the assembly of Ithaca, “the hero Aegyptius” and “the old hero Halitherses” (2.15, 2.157; see [[echeneus]] and [[geron]]). In address the word is courteous: Menelaus' “hero Telemachus” (4.312), and here the stranger's “Hero, do not rebuke the noble girl for that” (7.303), when he takes the king's blame of his daughter on himself (see [[quick-to-anger]] and [[alcinous]]).
