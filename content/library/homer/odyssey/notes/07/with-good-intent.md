---
summary: “At last the old hero Echeneus spoke among them, / who was one of the older Phaeacian men, / and excelled in speaking, knowing many things of old. / With good intent he addressed them and spoke” (7.155–158).
---

The counselor's verse, which follows an account of the speaker's age, wisdom or eloquence, as of Calchas, Nestor and Odysseus in the Iliad (*Iliad* 1.73, 1.253, 2.283) and of Mentor in Book 2 (2.228); here it introduces the old man who breaks the silence of the hall and speaks for the suppliant (7.154–166; [[echeneus]], [[elders]], [[supplication-type-scene]]).
