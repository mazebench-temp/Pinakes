---
summary: Arete: “Stranger, I myself will be the first to ask you this: / Who are you among men, and from where? Who gave you these clothes?” (7.237–238).
---

The queen joins to the first half of the formula a question of her own, for she knows the mantle and tunic that she made with her handmaids (7.233–235; [[clothing]], [[arete-queen-of-the-phaeacians]]). Merry takes 7.237 as “I will begin (the conversation) by asking thee this,” the queen taking the initiative, and notes that Penelope speaks the same verse when she is left alone with the stranger, before the whole of the formula (19.104–105; Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 7.237). Odysseus answers the second question and not the first; he tells his name only in Book 9 (7.241–297; 9.19).
