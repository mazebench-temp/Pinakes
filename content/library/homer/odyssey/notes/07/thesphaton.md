summary: When Odysseus throws his arms around Arete's knees, “then the god-sent mist flowed back from the man himself” (θέσφατος ἀήρ, 7.143).
# θέσφατος (7.143) is in the entry.
en: [god-sent]
---

Elsewhere θέσφατος is what a god has spoken, an oracle or a thing decreed (see the entry). Of the mist it can only mean that the thing comes from a god: the mist that Athena poured around the stranger in the street (7.14–17, 7.41–42) is lifted when it has done its work, at the moment of the supplication, and the whole hall sees him (7.143–145; see [[achlys]], [[athena]] and [[supplication]]). The translation renders it “the god-sent mist”, beside θεσπέσιος, “heaven-sent”, of the same mist a hundred lines before (7.42; see the translation's table of key words). αὐτοῖο is not merely “from him” but “from the man himself”, the hero now visible (W. W. Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 7.143). The negative compound comes later in the book, when Poseidon “stirred up the endless sea” (θάλασσαν ἀθέσφατον, 7.273; see [[endless]]): Apollonius' lexicon explains it as so great that not even a god could tell it, and Düntzer took the α for an intensive prefix, making ἀθέσφατος the same as θεσπέσιος (Merry on 7.273).
