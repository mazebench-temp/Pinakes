summary: Two kinds of tribes: Alcinous says that the gods do not hide themselves from the Phaeacians, who are near to them, “as are the Cyclopes and the savage tribes of the Giants” (7.206), and Odysseus excuses himself with a maxim, “for we tribes of men on the earth are quick to anger” (7.307).
# φῦλʼ ἀνθρώπων (7.307; again 3.282, 15.409). φῦλα (7.206) is in the entry.
grc: ["φῦλʼ ἀνθρώπων"]
---

φῦλον is a stock or kind. The king's “savage tribes of the Giants” (ἄγρια φῦλα Γιγάντων, 7.206) are a race set beside the Cyclopes and the Phaeacians as peoples near to the gods (see [[giants]], [[cyclopes]] and [[seeing-the-gods]]). The stranger's φῦλʼ ἀνθρώπων, “the tribes of men”, is mankind, as Phrontis “surpassed the tribes of men” in steering a ship (3.282): with it the masculine δύσζηλοι agrees with the men the phrase means and not with the neuter φῦλα, a construction according to the sense (W. W. Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 7.307; see [[quick-to-anger]]).
