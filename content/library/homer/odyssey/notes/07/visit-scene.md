summary: Odysseus at the house of Alcinous: he stands pondering before the bronze threshold (7.82–83), gazes at the house and garden (7.133–134), steps over the threshold (7.135), finds the lords at their last libation (7.136–138), and goes through the hall to the queen (7.139–141); the seat and the meal come only after his supplication and Echeneus' rebuke (7.167–177).
refs: [7.82-83, 7.133-141, 7.167-177]
---

The two verses of his gazing are Hermes' at Calypso's cave, with his own name (5.75–76; see the Odyssey translation's formulas). The visit departs from the pattern: no one meets the stranger at the door or takes him by the hand, since Athena's mist hides him until he clasps Arete's knees (7.139–143), and the host's question who he is comes from the queen, late in the evening (7.237–239; [[supplication-type-scene]], [[who-are-you-among-men]], [[xenia]]).
