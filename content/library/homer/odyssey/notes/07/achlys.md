summary: Athena “poured a mist / over him, a heaven-sent mist, in friendship in her heart”, so that the Phaeacians did not notice him as he went through their city (7.39–42).
# ἀχλύν (7.41) is in the entry.
---

ἀχλὺν / θεσπεσίην, “a heaven-sent mist” (7.41–42), is the phrase of the mist that Poseidon scatters from Achilles' eyes when he has carried Aeneas out of the battle (*Iliad* 20.341–342; see the translation's table of key words). There the mist was over the eyes of the man who could not see; here it is around the man who must not be seen, and it does what the mist of the Iliad's rescues does for a hero in danger (see [[divine-rescue]] and [[athena]]). The book names the same mist with a second word: Athena poured πολλὴν ἠέρα, “a thick mist”, around him as he set out (7.15), he goes through the hall “wrapped in the thick mist” (7.140), and at the queen's knees “the god-sent mist”, θέσφατος ἀήρ, flows back from him (7.143; see [[thesphaton]]). Zenodotus read ἥ σφισιν at 7.41, as if the Phaeacians were covered and not the stranger, and the scholia rejected it on the evidence of 7.143 (see the translation's note on 7.41). In the Phaeacians' own ships the mist is a standing condition: they cross the sea “hidden in mist and cloud” (ἠέρι καὶ νεφέλῃ κεκαλυμμέναι, 8.562, a plain rendering; see [[pompe]]).
