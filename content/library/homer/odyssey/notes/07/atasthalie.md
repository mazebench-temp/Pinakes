---
summary: Eurymedon, king of the Giants, “destroyed his reckless people, and perished himself” (λαὸν ἀτάσθαλον, 7.60).
# ἀτάσθαλον (7.60) is in the entry and its Odyssey notes.
---

The Giants are “high-hearted” (ὑπερθύμοισι, 7.59) and their people “reckless”, and the two epithets are Merry's reason for thinking that king and people perished in some foolhardy expedition against another tribe, rather than in a revolt or for the king's own guilt (W. W. Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 7.59; see [[giants]], [[eurymedon-king-of-the-giants]] and [[basileus]]). The word is the one with which the poem began, of the companions who “perished through their own recklessness” (1.7; see the entry). In the two verses on Periboea's father the ruin is the people's and the king's together: “he destroyed his reckless people, and perished himself” (7.60).
