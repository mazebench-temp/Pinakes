---
summary: ἀνάσσω is the verb of Phaeacian rule: Alcinous “over all / the Phaeacians … was lord, and the people listened to him as to a god” (7.10–11); Odysseus asks the girl with the water jar for the house of Alcinous, “who is lord among these people” (7.23); and his father Nausithous “was lord among the Phaeacians” (7.62).
# ἄνασσε (7.11, 7.62) and ἀνάσσει (7.23) are in the entry.
---

The book uses ἀνάσσω, “be lord”, of the two kings of the Phaeacians whom it names, the founder Nausithous and his son (7.62, 7.10–11; see [[nausithous]] and [[alcinous]]). θεοῦ δʼ ὣς δῆμος ἄκουεν, “the people listened to him as to a god” (7.11), Merry renders “hearkened to him” (W. W. Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 7.11; see [[honored-like-a-god]]); the people will look on his queen in the same way (7.71; see [[time]]). The stranger's question uses the verb too, ὃς τοῖσδε μετʼ ἀνθρώποισι ἀνάσσει, “who is lord among these people” (7.23). βασιλεύω, “be king”, is kept for an older and darker king, Eurymedon of the Giants, who destroyed his people (7.59–60; see [[basileus]]).
