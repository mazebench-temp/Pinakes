summary: Nine speeches end with the formula, from the end of Odysseus' prayer to Athena, “So he prayed there, much-enduring brilliant Odysseus” (7.1), to the handmaids' call to bed, “So they spoke, and it seemed welcome to him to lie down to sleep” (7.343); among them the suppliant's plea, “He spoke thus and sat down at the hearth in the ashes” (7.153), and his speech of the belly, “So he spoke, and they all approved and bade / send the stranger on his way” (7.226–227).
grc: ["ὧς ὁ μὲν ἔνθʼ ἠρᾶτο"]
en: [So he prayed there]
---

7.1 recasts the first verse of Book 6, “So he lay sleeping there,” for the prayer that closed that book (6.1, 6.323–327); the digitized text prints ὧς for ὣς (see the translation's note on 7.1). 7.226 is the verse of the suitors' approval of Antinous' ambush (4.673), here a host's decision to send his guest home (see the Odyssey translation's formulas), and “So they spoke such words to one another” (7.334 = 4.620) closes the talk of host and guest.
