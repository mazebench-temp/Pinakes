---
summary: Odysseus names Atlas once, as the father of “wily Calypso”, when he tells Arete of Ogygia (7.244–246).
---

“There is an island, Ogygia, lying far off in the sea; / there the daughter of Atlas, wily Calypso, / lives” (7.244–246; see [[calypso]] and [[ogygia]]). Atlas is named only twice in Homer, here and in Athena's plea at the first council of the gods, both times in the genitive and as Calypso's father. Athena gives him the epithet “baleful” and his two marks, that he knows the depths of the whole sea and holds the pillars that keep earth and heaven apart (1.52–54); Odysseus gives the name alone.
