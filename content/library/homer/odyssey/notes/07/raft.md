---
summary: Odysseus tells Arete that Calypso sent him off on a raft of many bindings, with bread and wine, immortal garments and a fair wind; on the eighteenth day, in sight of land, the storm that Poseidon raised would not let him ride on it and scattered it, and he swam to land (7.264–277).
---

**Sent on a raft.** Odysseus tells the queen of the raft without saying that he built it: “And she sent me on a raft of many bindings, and gave me much, / bread and sweet wine, and put immortal garments on me, / and she sent forth a fair wind, harmless and warm” (7.264–266; see [[calypso]], [[of-many-bindings]], [[provisions]] and [[clothing]]). In Book 5 Calypso gives him the tools and shows him the trees, and he builds it alone in four days (5.228–262; see [[the-building-of-the-raft]]). The verses of the fair wind and of the voyage are those of Book 5, “For seventeen days I sailed, crossing the sea, / and on the eighteenth there appeared the shadowy mountains” (7.267–268), with πλέον, “I sailed”, for πλέεν, “he sailed” (5.268, 5.278–279; see the translation's rows for those verses).

**Scattered.** Poseidon “roused the winds against me and bound up my path, / and stirred up the endless sea, nor would the wave / let me be borne on the raft as I groaned without ceasing. / Then the storm scattered it; but I / swam and cut through this gulf” (7.272–276; see [[poseidon]] and [[the-shipwreck-of-odysseus]]). διεσκέδασε, “scattered”, is the verb of Book 5, where the wave scatters the long beams of the raft as a wind scatters a heap of husks (5.370; see [[the-heap-of-husks]]). For κέλευθον, “my path”, W. W. Merry reads κέλευθα (*Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 7.272 and his critical note; see the translation's note on 7.272), and the whole account is shorter than the story of Book 5: no mast breaks, no goddess gives him her veil, and the raft is gone in a verse (see the Book 5 note).
