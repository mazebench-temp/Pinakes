**The house of Erechtheus.** Athena “came to Marathon and to wide-streeted Athens, / and entered the well-built house of Erechtheus” (7.80–81; see [[athena]], [[marathon]] and [[athens]]). Merry takes the house for the temple on the Acropolis in which Athena and Erechtheus were worshiped together, as Leto and Artemis share Apollo's temple in Pergamus (*Iliad* 5.445–448), and compares the Catalogue, where Athena raised Erechtheus and “set him down in Athens, in her own rich temple” (*Iliad* 2.547–549; Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 7.81; see [[athena-and-erechtheus]]). Merry thinks that Herodotus refers to these verses without naming them when he writes that on the Acropolis there is a temple of Erechtheus, “who is said to be earth-born”, with the olive and the salt water that Athena and Poseidon left as witnesses of their strife for the land (Herodotus 8.55.1; see [[herodotus]]). The temple is the goddess's, but the poem calls it his house; πυκινὸν δόμον, “well-built house”, is said again seven verses later of the house of Alcinous, which the golden doors shut in (7.88; see [[the-house-of-alcinous]]). The scholia report that Chaeris suspected the verses on Athens (Merry's critical note on 7.79–80; see the translation's note on 7.79–80).
