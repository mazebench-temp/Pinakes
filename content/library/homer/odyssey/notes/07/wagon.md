---
summary: Nausicaa's brothers unyoke the mules from under the wagon at the doorway of the house and carry the clothing inside (7.4–6).
---

“and her brothers stood / around her, like the immortals, and they from under the wagon / unyoked the mules and carried the clothing inside” (7.4–6; see [[mules]], [[clothing]] and [[nausicaa]]). The wagon, ἀπήνη, is the one that her father had his slaves make ready for her in the morning, “high, with good wheels, fitted with a frame above” (6.69–72; see the Book 6 note). At the house the sons of the house unload it; at the river the girls had taken the clothes from it themselves and carried them into the water (6.90–91; see [[washing-troughs]]).
