summary: Athena leaves Scheria and comes “to Marathon and to wide-streeted Athens”, and enters “the well-built house of Erechtheus” (7.80–81): the city's name in the singular, Ἀθήνη, the only time in Homer.
# "εὐρυάγυιαν Ἀθήνην" (7.80) is claimed by the entry; athena excepts the line.
---

**Wide-streeted Athens.** When she has brought Odysseus to the king's house, Athena leaves the island: she “went away / over the barren sea, and left lovely Scheria, / and came to Marathon and to wide-streeted Athens, / and entered the well-built house of Erechtheus” (7.78–81; see [[athena]], [[scheria]], [[marathon]] and [[erechtheus]]). Elsewhere Homer names the city in the plural, Ἀθῆναι (*Iliad* 2.546, 2.549; 3.278, 3.307, 11.323); only here is it Ἀθήνη, the goddess's own name, as Mycenae is both Μυκήνη and Μυκῆναι (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 3.307). The index catches the city by the phrase εὐρυάγυιαν Ἀθήνην, since Ἀθήνην alone is the goddess everywhere else. εὐρυάγυια, “wide-streeted”, is the epithet of Troy in the Iliad and of Mycenae (*Iliad* 2.12, 4.52; see [[wide-streeted]]).

**The house of Erechtheus.** Athens is, as in the Catalogue, the city of Erechtheus, whom Athena set down “in Athens, in her own rich temple” (*Iliad* 2.547–549; see [[athena-and-erechtheus]]): Merry takes his “well-built house” for the temple on the Acropolis where Athena and Erechtheus were worshiped together (on 7.81).

**A suspected passage.** The scholia report that Chaeris suspected the verses, and Merry thinks they were suspected as an addition by a later rhapsode to please Athenian feeling (Merry's critical note on 7.79–80; see the translation's note on 7.79–80). The Catalogue's Athenian entry, which also names Erechtheus, was suspected in antiquity in the same way (see the general article and [[solon-and-salamis]]).
