---
summary: Bronze is the metal of the house of Alcinous, its threshold and its walls (7.83, 7.86, 7.89).
# Forms first met here in the Odyssey: χάλκεοι (7.86) and χαλκέῳ (7.89); χάλκεον (7.83) is in the Book 1 note.
grc: [χάλκεοι, χαλκέῳ]
---

Odysseus ponders “before he reached the bronze threshold” (7.83; see [[threshold]]); “walls of bronze were drawn out this way and that” around the court (7.86), and the silver doorposts stand “in the bronze threshold” (7.89; see [[door]], [[house]] and [[the-house-of-alcinous]]). Bronze here is the frame of the house, and gold and silver its ornament (see [[gold-and-silver]]). It is the metal of the divine world: Hephaestus built his own house of bronze (*Iliad* 18.369–371), the house of Zeus is “bronze-floored” (*Iliad* 1.426; see [[bronze-floored]]), and Tartarus has “the threshold of bronze” (*Iliad* 8.15). At Sparta Telemachus had marveled at “the flash of bronze throughout the echoing halls” of Menelaus (4.72; see the Book 4 note), and Alcinous will call his own house “bronze-floored” (13.4, a plain rendering).
