**At the doorway.** Nausicaa “halted them in the doorway”, ἐν προθύροισι, and her brothers unyoked the mules and carried the clothing inside (7.4–6; see [[mules]], [[wagon]] and [[clothing]]).

**The golden doors.** The doors of the house of Alcinous are the first thing in it after the walls of the court:

{{quote:6353f4a3-bba1-5667-b398-241f72ec9eb0}}

The σταθμοί are the posts on either side of a door, as the Suda glosses them (σ 996). ὑπερθύριον, the beam “above the door”, is found nowhere else in Homer, and κορώνη is the curved handle by which a door is pulled to, as Telemachus pulls his chamber door to “by the handle / of silver” (1.441–442; see the entry and the translation's row ὑπερθύριον, κορώνη). The handle of Telemachus' door is silver; here the doors and the handle are gold, the doorposts and the lintel silver, and the threshold bronze (see [[gold-and-silver]], [[bronze]] and [[threshold]]), and on either side stand the gold and silver dogs of Hephaestus (7.91–94; see [[the-gold-and-silver-dogs]] and [[the-house-of-alcinous]]). The golden doors “shut in the well-built house”, πυκινὸν δόμον ἐντὸς ἔεργον (7.88), the epithet that Athena's house of Erechtheus has in the verse before the description begins (7.81; see [[house]]).

**Near the doors.** The orchard lies “outside the court, near the doors” (7.112; see [[the-garden-of-alcinous]]).
