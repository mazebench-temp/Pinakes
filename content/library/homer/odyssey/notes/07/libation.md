summary: The Phaeacian leaders, found pouring the last libation of the evening to Hermes (7.136–138), mix a bowl for Zeus “who attends on revered suppliants” at Echeneus' word and the king's (7.163–165, 7.179–183), and pour again before they go home (7.184, 7.228).
refs: [7.163-165, 7.179-183]
---

**The last libation.** Odysseus finds the leaders and rulers of the Phaeacians “pouring libations from their cups to the keen-sighted slayer of Argus, / to whom they would pour the last libation, whenever they thought of bed” (7.137–138; see [[hermes]] and [[hypnos]]).

**For the suppliant.** Echeneus bids the king seat the stranger and “bid the heralds / mix the wine, so that to Zeus who delights in thunder / we may pour libations too, who attends on revered suppliants” (7.163–165); Alcinous gives the order to Pontonous in the same verses (7.179–181), and the herald serves the wine out to all, “first pouring drops for the libation into the cups” (7.183; see [[a-suppliant-is-respected-by-the-gods]], [[mixing-bowl]], [[pontonous]] and [[supplication]]). Twice the evening closes with the formula “But when they had poured libations and drunk as much as their hearts desired” (7.184 = 7.228, as 3.342 and *Iliad* 9.177; see [[feast-type-scene]]).
