---
summary: Odysseus ends his story to Arete, “This is the truth I have told you, grieved though I am” (ἀχνύμενός περ, 7.297).
# ἀχνύμενός (7.297) is in the entry.
---

The concessive phrase closes the story as a like one opened his request to the king, ἐμὲ μὲν δορπῆσαι ἐάσατε κηδόμενόν περ, “let me eat my supper, grieved though I am” (7.215; see [[gaster]] and [[penthos]]): the grief is acknowledged and set aside, first for the belly and then for the queen's question. ἀληθείην is predicative to ταῦτα, “these things, as the truth” (W. W. Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 7.297), and the story he calls the truth leaves out his name (see [[xenia]] and [[arete-queen-of-the-phaeacians]]).
