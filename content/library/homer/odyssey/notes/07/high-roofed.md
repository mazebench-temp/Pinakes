summary: Athena: “your high-roofed house and your native land” (7.77); the gleam “throughout the high-roofed house of great-hearted Alcinous” (7.85); the spring that runs “toward the high house” (7.131); Odysseus: “my wealth and my slaves and my great high-roofed house” (7.225); and Alcinous lies down “in the inner part of the high house” (7.346).
grc: ["δόμον ὑψηλόν"]
---

7.77 has ὑψόροφον where the same verses in Book 6 have “well-built” (6.315; see the Odyssey translation's formulas). 7.225 is Achilles' verse in his lament for Patroclus, the house he had hoped his friend would show his son (*Iliad* 19.331–333): Odysseus would be content to die once he has seen his own (7.224–225; [[house]], [[the-return-of-odysseus]]).
