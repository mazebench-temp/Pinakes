**Two prayers.** “So he prayed there, much-enduring brilliant Odysseus” (7.1) closes the prayer of the last book, which Athena heard but did not answer face to face (6.321–331; see [[seeing-the-gods]]); in this book she comes to him at once, in the likeness of a young girl (7.19–20; see [[athena]]). At the end of the day, glad at the king's promise of an escort, “he spoke in prayer, and spoke to him, and called him by name” (7.330): “Father Zeus, would that he might accomplish all that he has said, / Alcinous! Then his fame over the grain-giving earth / would be unquenchable, and I would reach my native land” (7.331–333; see [[zeus]], [[kleos]] and [[nostos]]). The prayer asks in one breath for the king's fame and for the suppliant's homecoming.
