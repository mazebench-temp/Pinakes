---
summary: A story in Athenaeus of a fuller who would not give back a dress until he was brought oil suggests to Merry that the oil dripping from the linen of Alcinous' house was oil used in dressing the cloth.
---

- **The fuller's oil.** The courtesan Glycerium sends her maid to the fuller for her dress; he says it is not ready and that she cannot have it unless she brings him oil, for his own is used up (Athenaeus 13.582, as Merry reports it). From this Merry suggests that oil was actually used, and freely, in fulling and dressing clothes, a modification of Nitzsch's view that the oil running off showed the closeness of the web; he adds that most commentators take the oil of this verse, of the tunics of the dancers on the shield of Achilles (*Iliad* 18.595–596) and of Nestor's polished stones (3.408) for the look of oil, a gloss (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 7.107; see the translation's note on 7.107): [[weaving]] and [[plutarch]].
