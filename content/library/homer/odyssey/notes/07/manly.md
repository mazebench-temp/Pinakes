---
summary: Alcinous seats the suppliant on a shining chair, “making his son, manly Laodamas, stand up, / who sat beside him, and whom he loved most of all” (7.170–171).
grc: [ἀγαπήνορα]
---

The Iliad gives the epithet to warriors and to their attendants (*Iliad* 8.114, 13.756, 15.392, 23.113); here it is a king's son who gives up his place to a stranger. The father is the subject of φιλέεσκε, “he loved,” in a clause that Merry compares for its form with *Iliad* 3.386–388 (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 7.171). Laodamas wins the boxing at the games of Book 8 and is the first to invite the stranger to compete (8.117, 8.130–133). See [[laodamas-son-of-alcinous]] and [[throne]].
