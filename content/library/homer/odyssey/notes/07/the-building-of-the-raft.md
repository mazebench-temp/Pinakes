# Unnamed: the raft and Calypso's gifts in Odysseus' account (7.264-266).
refs: [7.264-266]
---

The four days of felling, hewing, boring and rigging (5.228–262) drop out of the story Odysseus tells the queen. In his account Calypso is the one who acts: “And she sent me on a raft of many bindings, and gave me much, / bread and sweet wine, and put immortal garments on me, / and she sent forth a fair wind, harmless and warm” (7.264–266; see [[calypso]] and [[raft]]). The last verse is the fifth book's (7.266 = 5.268), and “on a raft of many bindings” is the phrase of Zeus' decree, that Odysseus should go home “on a raft of many bindings, suffering woes” (5.33; again 5.338, where Ino sits on the raft; see [[of-many-bindings]] and [[the-council-of-the-gods]]). The raft is mentioned twice more in his account: the wave would not let him be borne on it, and the storm scattered it (7.274–275; see [[the-shipwreck-of-odysseus]]).
