---
summary: Odysseus to Athena in the likeness of a girl: “For I have come here, a much-tried stranger, / far away, from a distant land” (7.24–25).
---

The scholia on this verse give the two ancient senses of ταλαπείριος: Aristarchus took it for ταλαίπωρος, “wretched,” and others for a stranger who has come from far away (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 6.193, quoting the scholia on 7.24; see the translation's note on 6.193). The stranger names himself by Nausicaa's word for him (6.193), and the girl answers him as the young answer an older man, “stranger, father” (7.28; see the Odyssey translation's key words; [[xenia]], [[distant-land]]).
