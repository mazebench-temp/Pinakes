**The farthest land.** Alcinous ordains the stranger's escort for the next day and measures it by the longest voyage his people know:

{{quote:f835f064-df01-5638-8e2a-e9bb9aeb6f77}}

The island off the coast of central Greece, “holy Euboea” of the Catalogue (*Iliad* 2.535), is for the Phaeacians the farthest of lands, and only “those who saw it” can say how far it is (see [[phaeacians]] and [[scheria]]). τέλεσσαν and ἀπήνυσαν mean almost the same; τελεῖν perhaps regards the accomplishment of the journey, and ἀπανύειν the arrival at the goal, as οἴκαδε, “home”, suggests; ἤματι τῷ αὐτῷ is “on that very day”, nearly the Attic use of ὁ αὐτός (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 7.325, 7.326). The speed is the point: a land farther than the farthest is no obstacle to ships that went there and back in a day (see [[ships]]).

**Tityus in Euboea.** Why Rhadamanthys went to see Tityus, and why in Euboea, the poem does not say, and Merry finds no clue to the legend (on 7.323; see [[the-voyage-of-rhadamanthus]], [[rhadamanthus]] and [[tityus]]). Strabo, quoting the verse, adds that a cave called Elarium, after Elara, the mother of Tityus, was shown in the island, with a hero-shrine of Tityus, and that certain honors were paid to him there; the stories of Tityus, he says, were told at Panopeus in Phocis (*Geography* 9.3.14; see [[strabo]] and [[panopeus]]).
