# ὀπηδεῖ (9.271).
grc: ["ἅμʼ αἰδοίοισιν ὀπηδεῖ"]
en: [who attends on revered suppliants]
passages:

**The libation.** In Book 5 Odysseus, swimming for his life, claimed the respect that even the gods owe a wanderer (5.447–450; see the entry). In Alcinous' hall the claim is granted in the form of a rite. When the stranger has thrown his arms around the queen's knees and sat down in the ashes, Echeneus tells the king to raise him, “and bid the heralds / mix the wine, so that to Zeus who delights in thunder / we may pour libations too, who attends on revered suppliants” (7.163–165; see [[echeneus]], [[supplication]] and [[libation]]). Alcinous raises him, and when he has eaten gives the order to Pontonous in the same two verses (7.179–181; see [[pontonous]]). The libation is poured to the god of the suppliant before the stranger has said who he is (7.182–184; see [[xenia]]).

**Revered.** αἰδοῖος is the one to whom αἰδώς, respect, is owed (see [[aidos]]): in Book 5 the wanderer is αἰδοῖος even to the gods (5.447), and here Zeus “attends on”, ὀπηδεῖ, the suppliants who are owed it, following them as a companion does (see [[zeus]] and [[delighting-in-thunder]]). Odysseus will use the same verse-end of strangers when he warns the Cyclops to respect the gods: “we are your suppliants; and Zeus is the avenger of suppliants and strangers, the god of guests, who attends on revered strangers” (9.269–271, a plain rendering; see [[polyphemus-the-cyclops]]). When he wakes on Ithaca and thinks that the Phaeacians have put him ashore in a strange land, he calls on the same god against them: “may Zeus of suppliants punish them” (Ζεὺς … ἱκετήσιος, 13.213, a plain rendering). When the Phaeacians send him home, Alcinous bids Pontonous mix the bowl again, but the libation is then a prayer to father Zeus as they send the stranger to his native land (13.49–52).
