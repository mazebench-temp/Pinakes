---
summary: “Answering him, Odysseus of many wiles said” introduces his three answers in the book: to Alcinous, who wonders whether he is a god (7.207); to Arete, who asks who he is and who gave him his clothes, “Answering her, Odysseus of many wiles said” (7.240); and to Alcinous' blame of his daughter (7.302).
---

The verse is the Iliad's for Odysseus' replies (*Iliad* 10.382, 10.423, 10.554, 19.154) and the Odyssey's from his answer to Calypso on (5.214). Each of the three speeches turns a question aside with tact: he is no god but a man of sorrows (7.208–225), he tells the truth of the clothes and not his name (7.241–297), and he takes the blame for Nausicaa's choice on himself (7.303–307).
