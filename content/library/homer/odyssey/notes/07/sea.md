---
summary: In Book 7 the sea is the Phaeacians' road, which they cross in their swift ships “since the earth-shaker granted it to them” (7.34–36, 7.108–109, 7.317–328), and the sea of Odysseus' tale to Arete, from Ogygia “far off in the sea” to the storm that Poseidon raised and the gulf he swam through to Scheria (7.239, 7.244–282). Athena leaves the island over the barren sea (7.79).
---

**The Phaeacians' sea.** Athena tells Odysseus that the Phaeacians, “Trusting in the speed of their swift ships, / … cross the great gulf, since the earth-shaker granted it to them” (7.34–35; see [[phaeacians]], [[poseidon]] and [[the-travelers-thought]]). λαῖτμα, “gulf”, is the depth of the open sea (see the Book 5 note and the translation's table of key words). The men's skill is the sea's as the women's is the loom's: “As much as the Phaeacians are skilled beyond all men / in driving a swift ship on the sea, so are the women / skilled at the loom” (7.108–110; see [[like-the-leaves-of-a-poplar]] and [[weaving]]). At the end of the evening Alcinous promises Odysseus their skill: he will sleep, “and they will row over the calm, until you come / to your native land and your house” (7.318–320), and he will see “how far the best / are my ships, and my young men at tossing up the sea with the oar-blade” (7.327–328; see [[oars]]). γαλήνη, “the calm”, is the calm that the god of the river of Scheria made before him (5.452; see the translation's table of key words); πηδόν, “the oar-blade”, may be named from πηδάω, “leap”, since the blade seems to spring from the water at the end of the stroke (W. W. Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 7.328).

**Athena's road.** When she has told him of Arete, the goddess “went away / over the barren sea, and left lovely Scheria” for Marathon and Athens (7.78–80; see [[barren-sea]], [[athena]] and [[athens]]).

**The sea of the tale.** Arete asks the stranger who gave him his clothes: “Did you not say that you came here wandering over the sea?” (7.238–239), recalling his plea for an escort home (7.151–152; Merry on 7.239). His answer is the sea of Book 5 told again in brief (see [[the-shipwreck-of-odysseus]] and [[retelling]]). “There is an island, Ogygia, lying far off in the sea” (7.244; see [[ogygia]]); he came there when Zeus struck and shattered his swift ship “in the middle of the wine-dark sea” (7.249–250, after 5.131–132; see [[wine-dark]] and [[thunderbolt]]). On the raft he sailed seventeen days, “crossing the sea” (7.267), until Poseidon “roused the winds against me and bound up my path, / and stirred up the endless sea” (7.272–273; see [[endless]]). The raft broken, “I / swam and cut through this gulf, until to your land / the wind and the water carried and drove me” (7.275–277): τόδε λαῖτμα is “yonder gulf”, as he points toward the sea (Merry on 7.276), the λαῖτμα he had said he crossed when he first saw the coast (5.409). At the shore the wave would have dashed him against the great rocks, and he swam out again until he came to the river (7.278–281; see [[shore]] and [[rivers]]). He leaves out Ino and her veil, and the two nights and two days that he drifted on the swell (5.333–390; see [[leucothea]]).
