summary: “Athena / of the lovely tresses, the dread goddess” (7.40–41), and Calypso, “she of the lovely tresses, the dread goddess,” as Odysseus tells of her twice (7.246, 7.255).
---

The phrase that the Odyssey keeps for the goddesses who hold Odysseus is given once to the goddess who guides him, as she hides him in mist from the Phaeacians (7.40–42; [[achlys]], [[athena]]). Its second use in his story, 7.255, is in the verses that may have been athetized in antiquity, and Merry finds 7.254–255, a repetition of what Odysseus has just said, the most suspicious of them (see the translation's note on 7.251–258; [[with-lovely-tresses]], [[calypso]]).
