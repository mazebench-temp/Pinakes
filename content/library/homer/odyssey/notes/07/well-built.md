summary: Athena “entered the well-built house of Erechtheus” (7.81); at Alcinous' house “golden doors shut in the well-built house” (7.88), and the golden young men stand “on well-built pedestals” (7.100).
grc: [ἐυδμήτων, "Ἐρεχθῆος πυκινὸν δόμον", "θύραι πυκινὸν δόμον"]
---

The translation's “well-built” renders here two words besides the entry's ἐϋκτίμενος: ἐΰδμητος, of the pedestals (7.100), as the Iliad says it of the Achaean wall and of the towers of Troy (*Iliad* 12.36, 22.195), and πυκινός, “close, compact,” in πυκινὸν δόμον, as of the house of Amyntor that Autolycus broke into (*Iliad* 10.267); Merry renders the house of Erechtheus “the strong house” (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 7.81). Where Athena's verses of Book 6 have “well-built” (6.315), 7.77 has “high-roofed” ([[high-roofed]], [[the-house-of-alcinous]], [[erechtheus]]).
