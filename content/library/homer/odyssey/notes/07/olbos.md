summary: At the queen's knees the suppliant blesses the banqueters: “may the gods grant them prosperity / in their lives, and may each of them hand on to his children / the possessions in his halls and the prize the people gave him” (ὄλβια, 7.148–150).
# ὄλβια (7.148; again 8.413, 13.42, 24.402), which the entry's comment foresaw.
grc: [ὄλβια]
---

A suppliant has nothing to give but a blessing, and this one names the goods of a house: prosperity, possessions, and a prize that passes to the sons (see [[supplication]], [[geras]] and [[possessions]]). The words ὄλβια δοῖεν, “may they grant prosperity”, stand by themselves in a later blessing, when Odysseus answers the apology of Euryalus, “and may the gods grant you prosperity” (8.413, a plain rendering); so ζωέμεναι, “in their lives”, is probably an addition that explains them, “prosperity, for them to live in”, rather than an infinitive governed by δοῖεν (W. W. Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 7.148). The same half-verse ends old Dolius' greeting to Odysseus on Laertes' farm (24.402), and at the end of his stay Odysseus prays that the gods may make the Phaeacians' gifts to him ὄλβια, “blessed” (13.41–42; see [[pompe]]).
