---
summary: Odysseus comes into the hall as the leaders of the Phaeacians are “pouring libations from their cups to the keen-sighted slayer of Argus” (7.137), the last libation before bed (7.136–138).
---

Merry reports the scholium that the last libation went to Hermes as the giver of sleep, and Nitzsch's view that it was offered to him as the guardian of the house for the night (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 7.138). See [[hermes]], [[libation]] and [[argeiphontes]].
