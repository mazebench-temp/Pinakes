---
summary: On Ogygia, Odysseus tells Arete, “always the garments, / immortal, that Calypso gave me, I kept wet with my tears” (7.259–260).
# δάκρυσι (7.260) is in the entry and its Odyssey notes.
---

The seven years on the island are summed up in one image, the goddess's immortal clothing soaked by a mortal's tears (7.259–260; see [[immortality]], [[calypso]] and [[clothing]]). Book 5 showed the weeping from outside, the man sitting on the shore “racking his heart with tears and groans and sorrows” and looking out over the barren sea, his eyes never dry of tears (5.82–84, 5.151–153; see the Book 5 note). δεύεσκον, “I kept wet”, is an iterative, the habit of every day, and the verb comes again in Book 8, when the song of Troy makes the stranger melt and his tears wet his cheeks under his eyelids (δάκρυ δʼ ἔδευεν, 8.521–522, a plain rendering). The tears of this book are told, not shed: the stranger gives no sign of grief in Alcinous' hall but his words, “grieved though I am” (7.215, 7.297; see [[penthos]] and [[achos]]).
