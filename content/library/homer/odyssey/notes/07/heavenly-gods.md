---
summary: Odysseus to Arete: “It is hard, queen, to tell from beginning to end / my sorrows, since the heavenly gods have given me many” (7.241–242).
---

Merry takes the ending of Οὐρανίωνες, after Curtius, as merely amplifying, and the word here as “unmistakably” the gods of heaven, though in *Iliad* 5.898 it may name the Titans as sons of Uranus; he reports Aristarchus' distinction between the Οὐρανίωνες and the Olympians (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 7.242, citing Aristarchus on *Iliad* 15.225). The verse is said again when Odysseus begins his tale to the Phaeacians (9.15; [[olympians]], [[algos]]).
