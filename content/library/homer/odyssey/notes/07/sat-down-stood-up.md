---
summary: Odysseus ends his plea to Arete and Alcinous: “He spoke thus and sat down at the hearth in the ashes / beside the fire; and they all fell hushed in silence” (7.153–154).
grc: ["ὣς εἰπὼν κατʼ ἄρʼ ἕζετʼ"]
---

The first half of the assembly's verse, in which one speaker sits and the next rises (*Iliad* 1.68; 2.224), seats the suppliant in the ashes, “in the posture of a suppliant” (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 7.153); no one rises to answer until the old Echeneus speaks (7.155–166; [[hearth]], [[supplication-type-scene]], [[silence]]).
