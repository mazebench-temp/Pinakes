---
summary: Odysseus to the Phaeacian lords: “But you, make haste when dawn appears, / so that you may set me, unhappy man, on my native land” (7.222–223).
---

ἅμʼ ἠοῖ φαινομένηφιν, the half-verse of the next morning's business; Alcinous had already set the escort for the coming day, “at dawn we will call more of the elders” (7.189), and fixes it at the end of the evening, “for tomorrow” (7.317–318; [[dear-native-land]]).
