# "So he prayed there" (7.1) and "Odysseus rose to go to the city" (7.14): the grove, which the book does not name.
refs: [7.1, 7.14]
---

The sixth book ended with Odysseus' prayer in the grove of Athena, and the seventh begins in the same place: “So he prayed there, much-enduring brilliant Odysseus; / and the might of the two mules carried the girl to the city” (7.1–2; see [[athena]] and [[nausicaa]]). The verse joins the books as the sixth was joined to the fifth, ὣς ὁ μὲν ἔνθα, “So he … there”, with the hero's formula (6.1; see the translation's note on 7.1). Nausicaa had told him to sit in the grove and wait until she and her handmaids had come to the city and reached her father's house (6.295–296); and when the narrator has brought her home, “then Odysseus rose to go to the city” (7.14; see [[the-city-of-the-phaeacians]]). The grove is not named again.
