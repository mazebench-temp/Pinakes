---
summary: “So he spoke, and Pontonous mixed the wine sweet to the heart” (7.182).
grc: [μελίφρονα]
en: [sweet to the heart]
---

Merry quotes the rendering “honey-hearted” from Tennyson, and finds that it gives the wine a kind of personality, like “generous wine” (εὐήνωρ οἶνος, 4.622); he lists μελίφρων of wheat (*Iliad* 8.188), of food (24.489) and of sleep (*Iliad* 2.34) (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 7.182). The verse returns when the wine is mixed for the stranger's last libation in Book 13 (13.53; [[wine]], [[pontonous]]).
