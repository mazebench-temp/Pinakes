summary: Ameis, as Merry reports, set beside the Phaeacian ships “swift as a wing or a thought” Cicero's sentence that nothing is swifter than the mind.
---

- **Swift as thought.** “Nothing is swifter than the mind; there is no speed that can contend with the speed of the mind” (*nihil est animo velocius, nulla est celeritas quae possit cum animi celeritate contendere*, *Tusculan Disputations* 1.43, a plain rendering), said of the soul that breaks through the air when it leaves the body. Ameis quoted the words, with a verse of Theognis and Thales' saying that mind is swiftest, for it runs through everything, beside Athena's “their ships are swift as a wing or a thought” (7.36; Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 7.36, who cites the passage by chapter as 1.19): [[the-travelers-thought]].
