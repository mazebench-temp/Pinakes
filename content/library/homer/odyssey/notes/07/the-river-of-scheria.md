summary: In his story to Arete, Odysseus tells how, thrown back from the rocks, he swam on to the river, where the place seemed best (7.280–282), slept among the bushes apart from “the river fallen from Zeus” (7.283–288), and was bathed in the river by Nausicaa's command (7.296); he says nothing of the river god who took him in.
# The river of his landing, unnamed: rivers claims ποταμός (7.281, 7.284, 7.296). He comes out of its water (7.283)
# and climbs from its ravine (7.285).
refs: [7.281-285, 7.296]
---

**The landing.** Odysseus tells the queen the end of his swim in the words of the fifth book: thrown back from the rocks of the coast, “I drew back and swam out again, until I came / to the river, where the place seemed to me the best, / smooth, free of rocks, and there was shelter from the wind” (7.280–282; the last verse is 5.443; see [[scheria]] and [[the-shipwreck-of-odysseus]]). ἐκ δʼ ἔπεσον, “I fell out of the water”, is a pregnant phrase: coming out of the water he sank down, gathering his spirit, which must mean, Merry thinks, with deep gasps for breath, and the fifth book tells the result, when his spirit came back to him (7.283; 5.458; Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 7.283). Then the river has its epithet, “apart from the river fallen from Zeus” (διιπετέος ποταμοῖο, 7.284; see [[fallen-from-zeus]] and [[rivers]]), and ἐκβάς, “came out”, is out of the ravine in which the river ran, as in the fifth book he turned away from the river, ἐκ ποταμοῖο λιασθείς, to lie down in the rushes (7.285; 5.462; Merry on 7.285). There he slept among the bushes, under the leaves, all night and into the next day (7.285–289).

**What he leaves out.** The prayer to the river and the god's answer, who stopped his stream and brought him safe into his mouth (5.444–453), are not in the story he tells; to Arete the river is only the best place on the coast (see [[a-suppliant-is-respected-by-the-gods]]). Nor is the bath quite as it was: Nausicaa “bathed me in the river, and gave me these clothes” (7.296), where in the sixth book she bade her handmaids bathe him and he washed himself, apart from them (6.209–224; see [[nausicaa]] and [[bathing]]).
