περὶ κῆρι is “with all the heart”, said here of the honor in which her husband, her children and her people hold Arete, as in Book 5 the Phaeacians will honor the stranger “with all their hearts like a god” (7.69, 5.36; W. W. Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 7.69; see [[time]] and [[arete-queen-of-the-phaeacians]]). The heart ponders at the threshold: πολλὰ δέ οἱ κῆρ / ὥρμαινʼ ἱσταμένῳ, “his heart pondered much / as he stood there”, his pace showing the wonder of his mind as he stopped again and again before the glories of the house (7.82–83; Merry on 7.83; see [[the-house-of-alcinous]] and [[thambos]]). And the heart is the seat of the anger that the king disclaims: οὔ μοι τοιοῦτον ἐνὶ στήθεσσι φίλον κῆρ, / μαψιδίως κεχολῶσθαι (7.309–310; see [[cholos]] and [[what-is-due-is-better]]).
