---
summary: Eurymedon “once was king over the high-hearted Giants. / But he destroyed his reckless people, and perished himself” (7.59–60).
grc: [ὑπερθύμοισι]
---

The Iliad's word of praise stands here beside ἀτάσθαλον, “reckless.” Merry finds three possible readings of 7.60: the Giants and their king were destroyed in some foolhardy expedition against another tribe, or a rising of the people against their king ended in ruin for both, or the king's guilt destroyed him and his people; he thinks the first the likeliest “on the strength of the epithets” ὑπερθύμοισι and ἀτάσθαλον (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 7.59).
