---
summary: The girl with the water jar is “the gray-eyed goddess Athena” when she meets Odysseus at the edge of the city (7.19), answers him (7.27) and begins to speak at the palace (7.47); and “So she spoke, and gray-eyed Athena went away / over the barren sea” (7.78–79).
---

All four are whole-verse formulas: 7.27 is the verse of her answers in Books 1 and 3 (1.178, 1.221, 3.25, 3.229), 7.47 the Iliad's verse for her speech to Hera and Zeus (*Iliad* 5.420), and 7.78 her departure from Pylos (3.371). Merry notes that τοῖσι, “among them,” is said here of a conversation between two (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 7.47), and he counts such verses among the formulas that epic poetry uses “with a certain carelessness” once they have become formulas (Merry on 7.289).
