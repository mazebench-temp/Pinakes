---
summary: Odysseus clasps the queen's knees and names her husband first: “Arete, daughter of godlike Rhexenor, / to your husband and to your knees I have come, after toiling much” (7.146–147).
# πόσιν (7.147) is in the entry.
---

The verse is the one he spoke to the god of the river, with πόσιν, “husband”, in the place of ῥόον, “stream” (7.147 after 5.449; see [[supplication]]). The suppliant has done as Nausicaa told him, passing her father by to clasp her mother's knees (6.310–311), and the plea brings the king in at once: the queen is addressed, but the claim is made on the husband and the knees together, and on the banqueters after them (7.146–148; see [[arete-queen-of-the-phaeacians]] and [[alcinous]]). It is the husband who answers, raising him from the hearth (7.167–171). Their marriage is told by Athena: Alcinous made his brother's daughter his wife and honored her as no other woman on earth is honored (7.63–68; see [[rhexenor]], [[husbands-and-wives]] and [[time]]), and the book ends with “the lady his wife” sharing his bed (7.347).
