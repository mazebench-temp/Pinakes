---
summary: In his tale to Arete the river of Scheria is the end of the swim: driven back from the rocks, Odysseus swam on “until I came / to the river, where the place seemed to me the best” (7.280–282), came out of the water and slept apart from “the river fallen from Zeus” (7.283–285), and the next day Nausicaa had him bathed in it (7.296).
---

**The river's mouth.** At the coast the wave would have crushed him against the great rocks, “but I drew back and swam out again, until I came / to the river, where the place seemed to me the best, / smooth, free of rocks, and there was shelter from the wind” (7.280–282; see [[the-river-of-scheria]] and [[shore]]). The last verse is the narrator's verse of Book 5 (5.443). There the river was a god, whom Odysseus knew as he flowed forth and prayed to as a suppliant, and who stopped his stream and brought him safe into his mouth (5.444–453; see the Book 5 note); to Arete he says only that the wind and the water carried him to the land, and that he came to the river (7.276–281). ἐκ δʼ ἔπεσον θυμηγερέων, “And I fell out of the water, gathering my spirit” (7.283), is in W. W. Merry's words a pregnant phrase: coming out of the water he sank down, rallying his spirit, doubtless with deep gasps for breath; the result is told in Book 5, when “his spirit was gathered back into his heart” (5.458; Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 7.283). θυμηγερέων is found only here (see the translation's table of key words).

**Fallen from Zeus.** “And I, apart from the river fallen from Zeus, / came out and slept among the bushes” (7.284–285; see [[fallen-from-zeus]] and [[trees]]). διιπετής is the Iliad's epithet of rivers fed by the rain of Zeus, and here, in his own telling, Odysseus gives it to the river that saved him. ἐκβάς is “came out”, up from the ravine in which the river ran, as he had withdrawn from the river into the rushes in Book 5 (Merry on 7.285, comparing 5.462).

**The bath.** Nausicaa, he says, “gave me bread in plenty and sparkling wine, / and bathed me in the river, and gave me these clothes” (7.295–296; see [[nausicaa]] and [[bathing]]). In Book 6 she bade her handmaids bathe him in the river, “where there is shelter from the wind”, and he washed himself, standing apart from them (6.209–224; see the Book 6 note). He tells the queen of the clothes because they are what she has asked about (7.238).
