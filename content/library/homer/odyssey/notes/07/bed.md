summary: The Phaeacian leaders pour the last libation to Hermes whenever they think of bed (7.138). At the end of the evening Arete has her handmaids set a bed for the guest beneath the colonnade, in the verses of the beds at Sparta and in Achilles' hut; they spread it in haste and bid him go to bed, and he sleeps on a corded bed while Alcinous lies down in the inner part of the house beside the queen (7.335–347).
# κοίτου, "bed" (7.138; also 2.358; later 16.481), first met here; κοίτοιο is in the Book 3 note.
grc: [κοίτου]
---

**Thinking of bed.** Odysseus finds the leaders of the Phaeacians pouring libations to Hermes, “to whom they would pour the last libation, whenever they thought of bed” (7.137–138; see [[hermes]], [[libation]] and [[hypnos]]).

**The guest's bed.** When Odysseus has told his story and Alcinous has promised him his escort, “white-armed Arete called to her handmaids / to set beds beneath the colonnade, and throw fine purple / blankets on them, and spread rugs over them, / and put woolly cloaks on top for them to wear” (7.335–338; see [[arete-queen-of-the-phaeacians]], [[handmaids]], [[blankets]], [[purple]] and [[cloak]]). The verses are Helen's order at Sparta and Achilles' in his hut for Priam and his herald (4.297–299; *Iliad* 24.644–646; see the Book 4 note), and as there the women go out with torches and spread the bed (7.339–340; *Iliad* 24.647–648; see [[torches]]). στόρεσαν takes up the whole process described in the verses before, and λέχος is the bedstead, firmly framed together; ἐγκονέουσαι, “in haste”, is found only in the feminine participle, here, in Achilles' hut and on the night of the recognition in Ithaca (W. W. Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 7.340; *Iliad* 24.648; 23.291). The handmaids' words are brief: “Rise and go to bed, stranger; your bed has been made” (7.342), and “it seemed welcome to him to lie down to sleep” (7.343; see [[hypnos]]).

**The corded bed and the inner part.** “So he lay sleeping there, much-enduring brilliant Odysseus, / on a corded bed beneath the loud-resounding colonnade, / and Alcinous lay down in the inner part of the high house, / and beside him the lady his wife shared his bed and couch” (7.344–347; see [[corded]], [[loud-resounding]] and [[house]]). The verses are those of Telemachus' night at Pylos, where the guest sleeps in the colonnade and Nestor's wife shares his bed (3.399, 3.402–403; see the Book 3 note), and of Menelaus and Helen at Sparta (4.304–305). For πόρσυνε, “shared”, Aristarchus' copies read πόρσαινε (the scholia, in Merry's critical note on 7.347; see the translation's note on 7.347). The first of these verses is also the first verse of Book 6, so that Book 6 opens and Book 7 closes on Odysseus asleep (7.344 = 6.1).
