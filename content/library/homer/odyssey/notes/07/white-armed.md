summary: Mother and daughter share the epithet: Eurymedusa “had reared white-armed Nausicaa in the halls” (7.12), and “white-armed Arete” begins to speak to the stranger (7.233) and calls her handmaids to make his bed (7.335).
grc: [λευκώλενον]
---

7.233, “And among them white-armed Arete began to speak,” is the frame of Nausicaa's song and dance and of Andromache's lament (6.101; *Iliad* 24.723), and it returns when Arete speaks among the Phaeacians in Book 11 (11.335). See [[nausicaa]], [[arete-queen-of-the-phaeacians]] and [[eurymedusa]].
