summary: Odysseus tells Alcinous that he would not follow Nausicaa into the town, “out of fear and shame, / lest your heart too should be indignant when you saw it” (δείσας, 7.305–306).
# δείσας (7.305) is in the entry.
---

δείσας αἰσχυνόμενός τε joins an aorist participle to a present: the first is the sudden fear that came over him, the second the abiding condition of modesty, as the heralds come to Achilles ταρβήσαντε καὶ αἰδομένω, “in fear and awe of the king” (*Iliad* 1.331). The second participle stands almost in parenthesis, since the clause μή … ἐπισκύσσαιτο, “lest your heart … should be indignant”, follows directly on δείσας (W. W. Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 7.305; see [[aischros]] and [[cholos]]). The fear is a guest's fear of his host's anger, and the story is told to cover the girl (see [[quick-to-anger]]). At the beginning of the evening Athena had told him the opposite, μηδέ τι θυμῷ / τάρβει, “do not in your heart / be afraid at all” (7.50–51; see [[tharsos]] and [[a-bold-man-is-better]]).
