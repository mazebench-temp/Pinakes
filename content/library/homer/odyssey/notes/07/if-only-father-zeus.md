---
summary: Alcinous: “If only, father Zeus and Athena and Apollo, / being such a man as you are, and thinking as I do, / you might have my daughter and be called my son-in-law” (7.311–313).
---

Merry finds here a mixture of the two forms that follow αἲ γάρ: the wish, with the optative, as in Menelaus' wish for Odysseus (4.341–346), and the prayer, with the infinitive, as in the Achaeans' prayer for the lot (*Iliad* 7.179); here, as in Laertes' wish (24.376–381), “the wish becomes the prayer under the influence of vehement emotion” (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 7.311). Aristarchus doubted whether the six verses were Homer's (see the translation's note on 7.311–316; [[nausicaas-wish]], [[father-zeus]]).
