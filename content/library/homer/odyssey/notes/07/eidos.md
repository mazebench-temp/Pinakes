summary: Periboea, the mother of Nausithous, was “most beautiful in form of women” (εἶδος, 7.57); Odysseus tells Alcinous that he is not like the immortals “in form or stature, but like mortal men” (7.209–210); and things “seemed” to him, the place at the river mouth the best (7.281) and the bed in the colonnade welcome (7.343).
# εἶδος (7.57) is in the entry; ἐείσατο (7.281, 7.343) is in the Book 5 note.
---

**Looks.** γυναικῶν εἶδος ἀρίστη, “most beautiful in form of women” (7.57), is said of Periboea, the youngest daughter of Eurymedon, who bore Nausithous to Poseidon (see [[periboea-mother-of-nausithous]] and [[best-in-looks]]). The stranger's looks are the question of the evening. Athena had made him “taller to look upon and stouter” at the river (6.229–235), and the king wonders whether he is a god (7.199–206); he answers that he is not like the immortals οὐ δέμας οὐδὲ φυήν, “in form or stature” (7.209–210; see [[immortality]]). φυή and εἶδος measured Nausicaa against the immortals in Book 6, “like the immortals in stature and in looks” (6.16; see the note there).

**Seeming.** ἐείσατο is “seemed” (see the Book 5 note). At the river mouth “the place seemed to me the best, / smooth, free of rocks” (7.281–282), the verse of the landing in Book 5 (5.442–443; see [[the-river-of-scheria]]), and at the end of the evening “it seemed welcome to him to lie down to sleep” (7.343; see [[hypnos]]).
