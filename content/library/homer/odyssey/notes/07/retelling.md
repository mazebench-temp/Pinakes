summary: Odysseus tells Arete the story of Book 5, from Ogygia to the river and the leaves (7.244–297), in many of the narrator's verses and of Calypso's: the thunderbolt and the wreck (7.249–251, after 5.131–133), the promise of immortality (7.257 = 5.136), the fair wind (7.266 = 5.268), the eighteen days (7.267–268, after 5.278–279), and the landing place by the river (7.282 = 5.443).
refs: [7.244-297]
---

What he leaves out is the gods' part: the council and Hermes' message, which he gives only as a guess at Calypso's reasons, “at a message from Zeus, or perhaps her own mind had turned” (7.263), Ino and her veil, and Athena's help on the rocks: at 7.278–280 his account has no “had not” ([[would-have-had-not]]). He adds what Book 5 did not tell, the seven years on Calypso's island (7.259), and ends with Nausicaa's kindness, the answer to the queen's question about the clothes (7.290–297; see the translation's notes on 7.250, 7.251–258, 7.272–273 and 7.289; [[the-shipwreck-of-odysseus]], [[calypso]]).
