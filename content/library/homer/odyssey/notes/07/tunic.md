---
summary: Arete knows the tunic that Odysseus is wearing, with the mantle, for she made them herself with her handmaids (7.234–235).
---

“for she knew the mantle and the tunic when she saw the clothes, / fine ones, which she herself had made with her handmaids” (7.234–235; see [[arete-queen-of-the-phaeacians]], [[cloak]] and [[clothing]]). They are the mantle and tunic that Nausicaa's handmaids laid beside him at the river (6.214), and that he put on after his bath (6.228; see the Book 6 note). The Suda explains the χιτών as the light garment worn under the cloak, named from its being poured round the limbs (χ 335). What the queen sees on the stranger is her own household's weaving (see [[weaving]]), and her question about the clothes is answered only at the end of his story: Nausicaa “bathed me in the river, and gave me these clothes” (7.296; see [[nausicaa]]).
