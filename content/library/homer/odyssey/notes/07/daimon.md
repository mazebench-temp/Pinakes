---
summary: Odysseus tells Arete that after the wreck “a god brought” him, alone, to Calypso's hearth (7.248).
---

ἀλλʼ ἐμὲ τὸν δύστηνον ἐφέστιον ἤγαγε δαίμων, “But me, unhappy man, a god brought to her hearth, / alone” (7.248–249; see [[calypso]] and [[ogygia]]). ἐφέστιον goes with ἤγαγε, “brought me to be her guest” (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 7.248; see the translation's key-word row), as Eurycleia tells Penelope that Odysseus has come home alive to his own hearth, ζωὸς ἐφέστιος (23.55). The god is a power felt and not named, as in Book 6, where “some god has cast me ashore here” (6.172; see the Book 6 note). A few verses later Odysseus says the same in the plural, “the gods brought me to the island of Ogygia” (7.254), and at the river “a god poured boundless sleep over me” (7.286), where the narrator of Book 5 had named the god: it was Athena who poured sleep on his eyes (5.491–493; see [[athena]], [[hypnos]] and [[theos]]).
