# "ἔντεα δαιτός", the gear of the feast that is cleared away (7.232; only here); δαιτός is also the feast's, which keeps
# the English "feast".
grc: ["ἔντεα δαιτός"]
en: [gear]
---

**The guest's table.** “and she drew up a polished table beside them” (7.174), the verse that follows the water for the hands at every welcome (1.138, 4.54; see [[basin-and-pitcher]], [[water-for-the-hands]] and [[polished]]). The table is a small one, drawn up beside the guest (see the entry).

**Clearing away.** When the Phaeacian leaders have gone home, Odysseus is left in the hall with Arete and Alcinous, “and the handmaids cleared away the gear of the feast” (7.232; see [[feast]] and [[handmaids]]). ἔντεα is used here of implements or apparatus, W. W. Merry notes, as in the Hymn to Apollo it is the tackle of a ship (*Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 7.232; *Homeric Hymn* 3.489; see the translation's row ἔντεα δαιτός), and ἀπεκόσμεον, “cleared away”, answers ἐκόσμει, “made ready”, of the supper that the old woman who reared Nausicaa makes ready for her at the beginning of the book (7.13; Merry on 7.12). A scholium with the words of 7.174 as its lemma records that the verse was rejected as out of keeping with Homer's usage, since he never has the tables carried away while the banqueters are still present; Merry assigns the scholium to 7.232, where the tables are cleared with the guest still in the hall (Merry's critical notes on 7.174, 7.232; see the translation's note on 7.174).
