**The wine for Zeus.** Echeneus had told the king to bid the heralds mix the wine, so that they might pour a libation to Zeus, who attends on revered suppliants (7.163–165; see [[echeneus]]). When Odysseus has washed and eaten (7.172–177), Alcinous gives the order to his herald by name:

{{quote:9326e539-20b2-5a13-a025-035e4c651901}}

The order repeats Echeneus' verses (7.180–181, as 7.164–165; see [[zeus]], [[a-suppliant-is-respected-by-the-gods]] and [[mixing-bowl]]). μελίφρονα, “sweet to the heart”, has been rendered “honey-hearted”, which gives the wine a kind of personality, like “generous wine” (W. W. Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 7.182, quoting Tennyson; see [[wine]]). The last verse is the Iliad's verse of the libation, as when the young men serve the wine after the sacrifice at Chryse (*Iliad* 1.471; 3.340): the wine-pourer, as he came round to each man, poured into his cup a first drop, to be poured out in libation, and then the full draught (Merry on 3.340; see [[libation]] and [[cupbearing]]). A scholium marks a short pause after πᾶσιν, “to all” (Merry's critical note on 7.183). “But when they had poured libations and drunk as much as their hearts desired” (7.184), Alcinous speaks to the leaders of the escort (7.185–206; see [[alcinous]]).
