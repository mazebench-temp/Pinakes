---
summary: Athena: “you will find the kings whom Zeus has nurtured / feasting at their feast” (7.49–50).
---

The Phaeacian lords, βασιλῆες like the princes of the Iliad, have the kings' epithet, which Poseidon had given to the whole people (5.378; [[kings]], [[phaeacians]]). Merry compares Tydeus, who came to Thebes as a stranger, found the Cadmeians feasting in the house of Eteocles, and was not afraid (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 7.49, citing *Iliad* 4.385–388; [[tharsos]]).
