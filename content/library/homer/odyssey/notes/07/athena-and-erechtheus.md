# Unnamed: the house of Erechtheus (7.80-81).
refs: [7.80-81]
---

**The house of Erechtheus.** Athena leaves Odysseus at the door of the palace, “So she spoke, and gray-eyed Athena went away / over the barren sea, and left lovely Scheria, / and came to Marathon and to wide-streeted Athens, / and entered the well-built house of Erechtheus” (7.78–81; see [[athena]], [[marathon]] and [[athens]]). In the Catalogue she raised the earth-born Erechtheus and set him down “in Athens, in her own rich temple” (*Iliad* 2.549); here the house is his, and she enters it. Merry takes it to be the temple on the Acropolis dedicated to the joint worship of Athena and Erechtheus, as in the Iliad Leto and Artemis share Apollo's temple at Pergamus (*Iliad* 5.445–448), and thinks Herodotus refers to this passage, without naming it, when he speaks of the temple of Erechtheus on the Acropolis, with the olive and the sea water inside it (Herodotus 8.55; W. W. Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 7.81; see [[erechtheus]] and [[herodotus]]). The words πυκινὸν δόμον, “well-built house”, are used of the house of Alcinous a few verses later, which golden doors shut in (7.88).

**An Athenian addition?** The scholia report that the passage was suspected, as Chaeris too says in his notes on the text; probably, Merry thinks, on the ground that a later rhapsode may have added it to gratify Athenian feeling (Merry's critical note on 7.79–80; see the translation's note on 7.79–80 and [[solon-and-salamis]]).
