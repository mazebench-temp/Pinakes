---
summary: The Phaeacians are “great-hearted” (7.16), and so are their kings and the king of the Giants: “great-hearted Eurymedon” (7.58), “great-hearted Nausithous” (7.62), and twice the house “of great-hearted Alcinous” (7.85, 7.93).
grc: [μεγαθύμων, μεγάθυμον]
---

The book has both words of the entry, μεγάθυμος (7.16, 7.62) and μεγαλήτωρ (7.58, 7.85, 7.93); μεγαλήτορος Ἀλκινόοιο closes the verse as in the formula of his daughter, “the daughter of great-hearted Alcinous” (6.17, 6.196, 6.213). Athena hides Odysseus in mist lest any of the great-hearted Phaeacians taunt him (7.14–17; [[phaeacians]], [[eurymedon-king-of-the-giants]], [[nausithous]], [[alcinous]]).
