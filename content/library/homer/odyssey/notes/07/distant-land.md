---
summary: Odysseus to the girl at the edge of the city: “For I have come here, a much-tried stranger, / far away, from a distant land” (7.24–25).
---

Merry takes the phrase as the explanation of τηλόθεν, “from afar, from a distant land,” with Eustathius; he reports Buttmann's view that the Ἀπία γῆ of later Greek, the Peloponnese, is a different word, but suggests from the accent a root meaning “water,” so that the phrase would mean “from a land over the water” (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 7.25). The verse-opening is Nestor's (*Iliad* 1.270; [[much-tried]]).
