---
summary: The book begins at the end of a prayer, “So he prayed there, much-enduring brilliant Odysseus” (7.1), and ends the evening with another: Odysseus answers Alcinous' promise, “Father Zeus, would that he might accomplish all that he has said, / Alcinous!” (7.331–332).
refs: [7.330-333]
---

The second prayer has the invocation and the wish but no claim on the god, and the poem tells no answer; it asks for the king's fame and the guest's homecoming together: “Then his fame over the grain-giving earth / would be unquenchable, and I would reach my native land” (7.332–333; [[father-zeus]], [[called-him-by-name]], [[kleos]]). The first was heard by Athena, who did not yet show herself (6.323–331).
