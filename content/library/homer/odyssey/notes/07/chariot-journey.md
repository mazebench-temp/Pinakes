summary: The end of Nausicaa's drive: “and the might of the two mules carried the girl to the city” (7.2), and “she halted them in the doorway, and her brothers stood / around her, like the immortals, and they from under the wagon / unyoked the mules and carried the clothing inside” (7.4–6).
refs: [7.2-6]
---

Merry supplies ἡμιόνους with στῆσεν, “halted,” the verb of the Iliad's chariot journeys (ἵππους στήσασα, *Iliad* 5.755; Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 7.4). The unyoking that ends the type-scene is done by the king's sons (7.5–6; [[mules]], [[wagon]], [[like-the-immortals]]).
