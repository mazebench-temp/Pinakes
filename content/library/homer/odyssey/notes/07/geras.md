---
summary: Two prizes of a king. The old Eurymedusa, Nausicaa's chamber-woman, was brought by ship from Apeire, and the Phaeacians “had picked her out as a prize for Alcinous, because over all / the Phaeacians he was lord” (7.9–11); and Odysseus, at the queen's knees, prays that each of the Phaeacian leaders may hand on to his children “the possessions in his halls and the prize the people gave him” (7.148–150).
# γέρας (7.10, 7.150) is in the entry.
---

**A captive.** γέρας ἔξελον, “picked out as a prize”, is the language of the Iliad's division of the spoils: the girl whom the sons of the Achaeans picked out for Achilles as his prize (*Iliad* 16.56, 18.444; see the translation's table of key words). The closest parallel is Nestor's woman, Hecamede, taken when Achilles sacked Tenedos, “whom the Achaeans / picked out for him, because he was the best of all in counsel” (*Iliad* 11.625–627). The reason is the same kind here, “because over all / the Phaeacians he was lord, and the people listened to him as to a god” (7.10–11; see [[eurymedusa]], [[alcinous]] and [[division-of-spoils]]). αὐτήν is emphatic, “her”, picked out from among all the other captives (W. W. Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 7.10, comparing 9.160 and *Iliad* 11.626). The ships that brought her from Apeire brought her as a captive (7.8–9; see [[apeire]]).

**A domain.** At the queen's knees the suppliant blesses the banqueters: may the gods grant them prosperity in their lives, “and may each of them hand on to his children / the possessions in his halls and the prize the people gave him” (7.148–150; see [[supplication]] and [[olbos]]). The γέρας given by the people is the royal domain, such as the τέμενος of Alcinous that Nausicaa pointed out near the grove (6.293), and the prayer is that their possessions may be enjoyed after them by their children and not by strangers, so that παισίν is emphatic (Merry on 7.150; see [[possessions]]). Sarpedon's great domain by the Xanthus, which the Lycians gave their kings, is an honor of the same kind (*Iliad* 12.313–314; see [[sarpedons-speech]]).
