---
summary: At Echeneus' word Alcinous “took wise-hearted Odysseus of subtle wiles by the hand / and roused him from the hearth” (7.168–169).
---

The Iliad has the verse-end once, of Odysseus alone and wounded among the Trojans (*Iliad* 11.482); here it names the suppliant in the ashes as the king raises him to his son's chair (7.167–171; [[supplication-type-scene]], [[wise-hearted]]). The Odyssey has it three times more, in the fight with the suitors (22.115, 22.202, 22.281).
