summary: At the door of the palace Athena tells Odysseus to go in, “and do not in your heart / be afraid at all; for a bold man is better in all / deeds, even if he comes from some other place” (7.50–52).
# θαρσαλέος (7.51) is in the entry.
---

In Book 6 Athena put daring into Nausicaa's mind and took the fear from her limbs, so that she stood her ground before the naked stranger (6.139–140; see the note there). Here the goddess gives the stranger the same thing as advice, in a maxim (7.51–52; see [[a-bold-man-is-better]] and [[athena]]). τάρβει is the verb of Tydeus' fearlessness when he came, a stranger and alone, among the feasting Cadmeians, which Merry compares (*Iliad* 4.385–388; W. W. Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 7.49; see [[tydeus]]). Odysseus does as he is bidden: he walks through the hall to the queen and clasps her knees before all the leaders of the Phaeacians (7.139–142; see [[supplication]]). At the end of the evening he describes himself otherwise. He would not follow Nausicaa into the town, he tells her father, “out of fear and shame” (δείσας αἰσχυνόμενός τε, 7.305; see [[deos]] and [[quick-to-anger]]).
