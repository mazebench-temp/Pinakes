summary: At the palace door Athena tells Odysseus that he will find “the kings whom Zeus has nurtured / feasting at their feast” (7.49–50): the leaders of the Phaeacians, who sit in Alcinous' hall drinking and eating (7.95–99), and pour the last libation to Hermes when they think of bed (7.136–138).
# βασιλῆας (7.49) and βασιλῆα (7.55, 7.141, King Alcinous, one of them) are in the entry.
---

The kings of Scheria are the chief men of the land, whom Nausicaa saw her father going to join at the council in Book 6 (6.54–55; see the note there). Athena names them with their Iliad epithet, διοτρεφέας βασιλῆας, “the kings whom Zeus has nurtured” (7.49; see [[zeus-nurtured]]), and the narrator, inside the hall, with the words of command: “There the leaders of the Phaeacians used to sit, / drinking and eating; for they had unfailing store” (7.98–99), “the leaders and rulers of the Phaeacians” (7.136; see [[hegemon]] and [[the-house-of-alcinous]]). Merry sets Athena's “do not … be afraid” beside Tydeus, who was not afraid when he found the Cadmeians feasting in the house of Eteocles (W. W. Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 7.49; *Iliad* 4.385–388; see [[a-bold-man-is-better]]). Alcinous is the king among them, Ἀλκίνοον βασιλῆα, “King Alcinous” (7.55, 7.141; see [[alcinous]] and [[basileus]]); in *Odyssey* 8 he counts them, twelve kings who rule among the people, and he the thirteenth (8.390–391, a plain rendering). They hear the suppliant's plea and his blessing on their houses, wait in silence for the king's word, and go home to sleep when he has spoken (7.146–154, 7.161, 7.185–188, 7.229; see [[supplication]] and [[geras]]).
