# keeps the nominative.

**The Phaeacians' passenger.** Alcinous' measure of his ships is a voyage they once made: Euboea, “which those who saw it say is the farthest away, / of our people, when they carried fair-haired Rhadamanthys / to visit Tityus, the son of Earth”, and they went there and came back home the same day without weariness (7.321–326; see [[the-voyage-of-rhadamanthus]], [[euboea]], [[tityus]] and [[phaeacians]]). ξανθὸν Ῥαδάμανθυν, “fair-haired Rhadamanthys”, is the epithet he has in Proteus' prophecy, ὅθι ξανθὸς Ῥαδάμανθυς (4.564; see [[fair-haired]]); the Aeolic form of the name was Βραδάμανθυς (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 7.323).

**From Elysium?** In Proteus' prophecy Rhadamanthys is already in the Elysian plain at the limits of the earth (4.563–564; see [[elysium]]). Merry, who finds no clue to the legend of the voyage, thinks it just possible that Scheria was imagined as not far from Elysium, so that the Phaeacian sailors were at hand to carry Rhadamanthys from there; the purpose of the visit he finds equally obscure (on 7.323; see the translation's note on 7.323–324 and [[scheria]]). He had already joined the two places in his note on Scheria: the Elysian plain and the garden of Alcinous both have the West Wind always blowing, and Rhadamanthus, who lives in Elysium, is the Phaeacians' passenger (4.567, 7.118–119; 4.564, 7.323; Merry on 5.34; see [[zephyrus]] and [[the-garden-of-alcinous]]).
