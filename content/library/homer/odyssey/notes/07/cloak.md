---
summary: Arete knows the mantle and the tunic that Odysseus is wearing, for she made them herself with her handmaids (7.233–235); at night woolly cloaks are laid on top of his bed (7.338).
---

**The mantle that Arete knew.** When the guests have gone, “white-armed Arete began to speak; / for she knew the mantle and the tunic when she saw the clothes, / fine ones, which she herself had made with her handmaids” (7.233–235; see [[arete-queen-of-the-phaeacians]], [[tunic]] and [[clothing]]). The words φᾶρός τε χιτῶνά τε εἵματʼ are those with which the handmaids laid the clothes beside him at the river (6.214; see the Book 6 note and [[nausicaa]]): the queen recognizes her own weaving on the stranger, and asks “Who gave you these clothes?” (7.238; see [[who-are-you-among-men]]). The φᾶρος, the larger mantle of fine cloth, is worn by kings and goddesses (see the entry); the Suda distinguishes the χλαῖνα, the thick winter cloak, named from χλιαίνω, “warm”, from the χιτών, the light garment worn beneath it (χ 335).

**Woolly cloaks.** Arete has her handmaids “put woolly cloaks on top for them to wear” on the bed in the colonnade (7.338 = 4.299, *Iliad* 24.646; see [[bed]], [[blankets]] and [[thick-and-woolly]]); the χλαῖνα served as a counterpane laid over the sleeper, W. W. Merry notes, citing the same verse at Sparta (*Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 3.349).
