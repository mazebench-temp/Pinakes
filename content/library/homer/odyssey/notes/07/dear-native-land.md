summary: The book's word for home: Athena's promise of “your high-roofed house and your native land” (7.77); the suppliant's plea, “speed my escort, so that I may reach my native land / the sooner” (7.151–152); Alcinous' promise that he will reach it “under our escort” (7.193) and be rowed “to your native land and your house” (7.320); and Odysseus' words, “set me, unhappy man, on my native land” (7.223) and “I would reach my native land” (7.333).

Every speech of the guest and his hosts turns on the native land, from the supplication to the prayer that ends the day (7.151, 7.333; [[nostos]], [[pompe]], [[the-return-of-odysseus]]). Alcinous' verse returns when the house of Aeolus asks the returning Odysseus why he has not reached home after their careful sending (10.64–66).
