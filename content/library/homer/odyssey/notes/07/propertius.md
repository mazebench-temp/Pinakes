---
summary: Propertius' orchards do not equal the Phaeacian woods; and Nitzsch, as Merry reports, set a couplet of his beside the construction of Athena's words on Arete's honor.
---

- **The Phaeacian woods.** “Nor do my orchards equal the Phaeacian woods” (*nec mea Phaeacas aequant pomaria silvas*, *Elegies* 3.2.13, a plain rendering), among the riches the poet lacks (on 7.112–132): [[the-garden-of-alcinous-in-later-writers]] and [[the-garden-of-alcinous]].
- **A verb understood.** “No less will this fame of my tomb become known than the bloody tomb of the Phthian hero was” (*nec minus haec nostri notescet fama sepulcri, / quam fuerant Phthii busta cruenta viri*, *Elegies* 2.13.37–38, a plain rendering): as “known” is understood with *fuerant*, so Nitzsch would understand “honored” with ἔστιν in “So she has been honored with all their hearts, and still is” (7.69; Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 7.69, who cites the couplet as 2.13.38): [[arete-queen-of-the-phaeacians]] and [[plato]].
