**The places of assembly.** Among the sights of the city are αὐτῶν θʼ ἡρώων ἀγοράς, “the places of assembly of the heroes themselves” (7.44; see [[the-city-of-the-phaeacians]]). Nausicaa had spoken of one place of assembly, around the precinct of Poseidon (6.266–267), and the plural returns when the Phaeacians gather in the morning, and “quickly the places of assembly and the seats were filled with mortals” (8.16). The word has the double meaning of public speaking and of the place where it is done, and Merry reports that some compare φῆμις, which the scholia explain as ἐκκλησία, συνέδριον, an assembly or a council (15.468; Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 7.44); the English takes the places, which Odysseus can see.

**The gathering at dawn.** When the leaders have poured their libations, Alcinous sends them home: “Now that you have feasted, go home and lie down; / and at dawn we will call more of the elders / and entertain the stranger in the halls, and to the gods / offer fine sacrifices, and then we will remember his escort too” (7.188–192; see [[elders]], [[xenia]] and [[sacrifice]]). ἐπί goes with καλέσαντες, “bidding them gather to the meeting” (Merry on 7.189; see the translation's note on 7.189). In the eighth book it is held in the place of assembly beside the ships, where the Phaeacians sit on the polished stones, and Athena, in the likeness of Alcinous' herald, goes through the city summoning “the leaders and rulers of the Phaeacians” to hear about the stranger; there Alcinous proposes the escort, and asks the scepter-bearing kings to entertain the stranger in his house (8.4–45; see [[heralds]] and [[the-return-of-odysseus]]). Alcinous addresses them in the hall with the verse with which he will open that assembly, “Hear me, leaders and rulers of the Phaeacians” (7.186 = 8.26).

**Speaking, not assembling.** At the feast ἀγορεύω is only “speak”: Echeneus and Alcinous “addressed them”, ἀγορήσατο (7.158, 7.185), Odysseus finds it hard “to tell” his sorrows, ἀγορεῦσαι (7.241), and they spoke such words to one another, ἀγόρευον (7.334). The entry excepts these lines.
