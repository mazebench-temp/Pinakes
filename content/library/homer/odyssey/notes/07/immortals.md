---
summary: Nausicaa's brothers stand around her “like the immortals” (7.5); Hephaestus' dogs are “immortal and ageless all their days” (7.94); Alcinous: “But if he is one of the immortals come down from heaven” (7.199); Odysseus: “am not like the immortals, who hold the wide sky” (7.209); and Calypso's offer, “that she would make me immortal and ageless all my days” (7.257).
---

7.199 is Diomedes' question to Glaucus (*Iliad* 6.128, with “you are”); Alcinous asks it of a stranger in his house, and Odysseus answers with the other half of the Iliad's division of the world, “mortal men” (7.209–210; [[like-the-immortals]], [[mortal-men]], [[ageless]], [[immortal-and-ageless]]).
