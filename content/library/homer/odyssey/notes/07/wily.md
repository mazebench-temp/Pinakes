---
summary: Odysseus to Arete: “There is an island, Ogygia, lying far off in the sea; / there the daughter of Atlas, wily Calypso, / lives” (7.244–246).
---

Merry finds in the epithet Odysseus' own experience of Calypso, who schemed to keep him as her husband, rather than the character the poem has given her (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 7.245; see the Odyssey translation's key words). In the same speech she is “she of the lovely tresses, the dread goddess,” who loved him and offered to make him immortal, but “never persuaded the heart in my breast” (7.255–258); she let him go after seven years, “at a message from Zeus, or perhaps her own mind had turned” (7.259–263; [[calypso]], [[with-lovely-tresses]], [[immortal-and-ageless]]).
