---
summary: The house of Alcinous gleams “as of the sun or of the moon” (7.84), in the verse that described the house of Menelaus (4.45).
---

“For there was a gleam as of the sun or of the moon / throughout the high-roofed house of great-hearted Alcinous” (7.84–85; see [[helios]] and [[the-house-of-alcinous]]). The verse is the one in which Telemachus and Peisistratus marveled at the house of Menelaus at Sparta (4.45–46). The sun and the moon stand together as on the Shield of Achilles, where Hephaestus made “the tireless sun, and the moon at the full” (*Iliad* 18.484; see [[the-shield-of-achilles]]); here they are the measure of the bronze, gold and silver of a king's house, as the moon is the measure of the blaze from Achilles' new shield (*Iliad* 19.374).
