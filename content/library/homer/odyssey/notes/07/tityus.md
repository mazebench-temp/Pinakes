summary: Alcinous names Tityus, “the son of Earth”, as the one whom fair-haired Rhadamanthys went to visit, when the Phaeacians carried him to Euboea and came back the same day (7.321–326).
# Τιτυὸν and Γαιήιον (7.324) are in the entry. "And they went there" (7.325): to Euboea, where Tityus was.
refs: [7.325]
---

**The one they went to see.** Euboea is the farthest of lands, Alcinous says, as those of his people say who saw it, “when they carried fair-haired Rhadamanthys / to visit Tityus, the son of Earth” (7.321–324; see [[the-voyage-of-rhadamanthus]], [[rhadamanthus]], [[euboea]] and [[phaeacians]]). Tityus is named only for the sake of the voyage, and the poem does not say why he was visited. ἐποψόμενον, “to visit”, is a future participle of purpose; Strabo, quoting the verse, has ὀψόμενον, “to see” (*Geography* 9.3.14). Γαιήιον υἱόν, “the son of Earth”, names his mother in an adjective, as Ποιάντιον υἱόν names Philoctetes, son of Poias (3.190; Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 7.324; see [[earth]]).

**Tityus in Euboea.** Here Tityus is in Euboea (Merry on 11.576). Strabo reports that a cave named after his mother Elara was shown in the island, with a hero-shrine of Tityus at which he received honors, though the stories of him, he says, were told at Panopeus in Phocis (*Geography* 9.3.14; see [[strabo]] and [[panopeus]]). In the eleventh book Odysseus sees him stretched on the ground in the underworld, with two vultures at his liver, because he laid hands on Leto as she went to Pytho through Panopeus (11.576–581, Greek only; see [[leto]]). Merry, setting the two places side by side, can find no clue to the legend of the visit (on 7.323).
