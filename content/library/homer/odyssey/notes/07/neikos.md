# νείκεα (7.74; again 12.440, of the judge who settles the quarrels of young men), νείκεε (7.303).
grc: [νείκεα, νείκεε]
en: [quarrels, rebuke]
---

**Settling quarrels.** Athena's praise of the queen ends with her wisdom in disputes: ᾗσι τʼ ἐὺ φρονέῃσι καὶ ἀνδράσι νείκεα λύει, “for the women she favors, and for men as well, she settles quarrels” (7.74; see [[arete-queen-of-the-phaeacians]] and [[noos]]). The text has the feminine; Merry prints οἷσιν, “for those to whom she shows favour, be they even men”, and rejects the rendering “gives wise counsel” for εὖ φρονεῖν (W. W. Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 7.74; see the translation's note on 7.74). To settle quarrels is a judge's work: in Book 12 the hour of supper is the hour when a man rises from the assembly after judging the many quarrels of young men who go to law (12.439–440, a plain rendering; see [[themis]]).

**Rebuke.** At the end of the evening the verb is the king's blame of his daughter, which the stranger turns aside: “Hero, do not rebuke the noble girl for that” (7.303; see [[quick-to-anger]] and [[nausicaa]]). νεικέω is the word of spoken abuse and reproof, here a father's mild fault-finding (7.299–301).
