# The phrase "μυελὸν ἀνδρῶν" (2.290; again 20.108, of the meal ground in Odysseus' house).
grc: ["μυελὸν ἀνδρῶν"]
en: [the marrow of men, mill-ground barley]
---

Athena's orders name the meal with the wine: “wine in two-handled jars, and barley meal, the marrow of men, / in thick skins” (2.290–291; see [[provisions]] and [[wine]]). μυελός is marrow, and the same words are said of the barley and wheat meal that twelve women make at the mills of Odysseus' house, where one of them speaks the word of omen that Odysseus prays for (20.105–108; a plain rendering). Telemachus asks Eurycleia to “pour me barley meal into well-sewn leather bags”, and adds, “let there be twenty measures of the meal of mill-ground barley” (2.354–355; see [[eurycleia]]): μυληφάτος, “mill-ground”, from μύλη, “mill”, is found only here in Homer, and ἀκτή is “meal”, as in “the meal of holy barley” in Nestor's hut (*Iliad* 11.631). When she has sworn, Eurycleia “poured barley meal for him into well-sewn leather bags” (2.380), the verse of his order put into the narrative.
