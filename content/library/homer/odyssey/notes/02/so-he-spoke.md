summary: The book's speeches close with the formula fourteen times, from “So he spoke” after Aegyptius' wish (2.35) to “So he spoke, and led the way” on the shore (2.413); among them “So Telemachus spoke” before the eagles (2.146), Leiocritus' “So he spoke, and quickly dissolved the assembly” (2.257), and “So they spoke” after the suitors' jeers (2.337).

# ἦ καὶ at 2.60 is "truly, even" (ἦ καὶ ἔπειτα), not "he spoke, and".
except: [2.60]
---

**The effect of the speech.** As the entry describes, the rest of the verse turns to what the words do. Aegyptius' wish for the man who called the assembly is taken by Telemachus as a word of omen: “So he spoke, and the dear son of Odysseus rejoiced at the word of omen” (2.35; [[dear-son-of-odysseus]]). Telemachus' own speech ends in anger and in tears (2.80–81; [[shedding-tears]]), and his second in a sign from Zeus: “So Telemachus spoke, and for him far-seeing Zeus sent two eagles” (2.146; [[far-seeing]]). His prayer closes with ὣς ἔφατʼ εὐχόμενος, and Athena comes (2.267; [[prayer-type-scene]]); when she has spoken as Mentor the narrator names her, “So spoke Athena, daughter of Zeus” (2.296; [[daughter-of-zeus]]).

**Variants.** ὣς ἄρʼ ἐφώνησεν, λῦσεν δʼ ἀγορὴν αἰψηρήν is the verse in which Achilles dissolves the assembly of reconciliation (2.257 = *Iliad* 19.276; [[assembly-type-scene]]). ἦ ῥα, καί, “He spoke, and,” closes Telemachus' refusal of Antinous (2.321), and ὣς φάν, “So they spoke,” the suitors' jeers (2.337; [[tis-speech]]). At the end of the book the same half-verse serves goddess and man: “So she spoke, and Pallas Athena led the way” to the ship, and on the shore “So he spoke, and led the way, and they followed with him” (2.405, 2.413; [[followed-in-the-footprints]]). At 2.60 ἦ καί is “truly, even,” in Telemachus' “truly, even then / we shall be wretched” (2.60–61), not the closing formula.
