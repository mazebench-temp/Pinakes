---
summary: Penelope's shroud is for Laertes, “against the time when / the deadly fate of death that lays men low takes him down” (2.99–100).
---

The verse-end τανηλεγέος θανάτοιο is joined here to μοῖρʼ ὀλοή, “the deadly fate,” in the words with which, as Antinous tells it, Penelope set up her loom (2.96–102; [[deadly]], [[the-web-of-penelope]]). The whole verse comes again in Penelope's own account of the web and in the shade of Amphimedon's (19.145, 24.135). See [[laertes]], [[shroud]] and [[thanatos]].
