---
summary: The house of Odysseus is the matter of the assembly. Telemachus tells the people that an evil far greater than his father's loss will soon shatter his whole house, Mentor is the man to whom Odysseus entrusted it, and the suitors jeer that they would divide its possessions and leave the house to Penelope's next husband. The second half of the book moves through it at night, from the storeroom where the provisions are made ready to the hall where Athena pours sleep on the suitors.

**A house destroyed.** Telemachus has called the assembly for “a business of my own, since evil has fallen upon my house, / twofold” (2.45–46): his father is lost, “and now an evil far greater still, which will soon shatter my whole house / utterly, and destroy my substance altogether” (2.48–49; see [[the-suitors]] and [[possessions]]). The suitors “haunt our house every day”, slaughtering its oxen, sheep and goats and drinking its wine, “For there is no man here / such as Odysseus was, to ward off disaster from the house” (2.55–59), and “no longer fairly / has my house been destroyed” (2.63–64; see [[nemesis]]). He will not thrust his mother out of the house (2.130–137; see [[mothers-and-sons]]), and repeats to the suitors the warning of the first book: “leave my halls, and provide other feasts, / eating your own possessions, taking turns from house to house”, or else, “unavenged, you would perish inside my house” (2.139–145, as 1.374–380; see [[telemachus-and-the-suitors]] and the translation's note on 2.141).

**Mentor and the house.** Mentor is the companion to whom Odysseus, “when he went in the ships, he had entrusted all his house” (2.226; see [[mentor]] and the translation's note on 2.227). He tells the people that the suitors “stake their own heads when they violently devour / the house of Odysseus” (2.237–238), and Leiocritus answers that if Odysseus himself came and found the suitors “feasting in his house” and tried “to drive them out of the hall”, he would meet a shameful doom (2.246–251; see [[leiocritus-son-of-euenor]]). The assembly breaks up, “And they scattered, each to his own house, / and the suitors went to the house of divine Odysseus” (2.258–259).

**The house at night.** Telemachus finds the suitors “in the halls”, flaying goats and singeing hogs “in the courtyard” (2.299–300), and they jeer that if he perished at sea “we would divide all his possessions, and the house / we would give to his mother to keep, and to whoever married her” (2.335–336). He goes down to his father's storeroom, where the wealth of the house is kept (2.337–347; see [[the-storeroom-of-odysseus]]), and his mother is to know nothing until she has “gone up to her upper room” for the night (2.358; see [[upper-room]]). Athena goes “to the house of divine Odysseus” and pours sleep on the suitors, who go to sleep “through the city”, and she calls Telemachus “out of the well-appointed halls” (2.394–400; see [[hypnos]]). The provisions are “together in the hall” (2.411; see [[provisions]]).
