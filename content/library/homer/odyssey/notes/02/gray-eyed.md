summary: Athena's epithet five times in the night of the departure: twice in the verse “Then the gray-eyed goddess Athena thought of another thing” (2.382, 2.393), when she calls Telemachus out to the ship (2.399), when she sends the wind (2.420), and in the libation, “most of all to the gray-eyed daughter of Zeus” (2.433).
grc: [γλαυκώπιδι]
---

The epithet comes with each of the goddess' acts in the second half of the book: she gathers the crew and borrows the ship (2.382–387), puts the suitors to sleep (2.393–398), calls Telemachus out of the halls in the likeness of Mentor (2.399–401), and sends the following wind (2.420–421; [[thought-of-another-thing]], [[in-the-likeness-of-mentor]], [[sailing-type-scene]]). The crew pour libations to all the gods, but most of all to her, Διὸς γλαυκώπιδι κούρῃ (2.433; [[daughter-of-zeus]], [[libation]]). See [[athena]].
