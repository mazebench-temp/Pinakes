---
summary: Penelope's shroud is for the day when “the deadly fate of death that lays men low takes him down” (2.100); Leiocritus tells Mentor that “what you have said is not fair and right” (2.251); and Telemachus calls his father “that ill-fated man” (2.351).
grc: [μοῖρʼ, κάμμορον]
---

κατὰ μοῖραν, “fair and right”, is speech that gives each his due share (see the entry); μοῖρʼ ὀλοή, “the deadly fate”, is the share of death (2.100; see [[that-lays-men-low]]), and κάμμορος, “ill-fated”, from κατά and μόρος, is rendered as δύσμορος (1.49; see the translation's table).
