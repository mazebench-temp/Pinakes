summary: The book of Telemachus' first public acts. He calls the assembly and sits in his father's seat, tells the people of his twofold evil and flings down the scepter in tears, refuses to send his mother away, and asks for a ship; when the assembly fails him he prays on the shore, and Athena, in the likeness of Mentor, promises him a ship. He refuses the suitors' feast, has Eurycleia make ready the provisions and swear silence, and sails by night with the goddess beside him.

**In his father's seat.** At dawn he dresses with the verses of the heroes, sword and sandals, and goes out “like a god to look upon” (2.1–5; see [[dressing-scene]]); ἄντην is “in presence”, literally “if looked at in the face” (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 2.5). He goes to the assembly with a bronze spear and two swift dogs, and Athena pours a wondrous grace upon him (2.10–13; see [[dogs]], [[charis]] and [[athena]]); nearly the same verses bring him to the place of assembly when he has come back from Pylos (17.61–64). He sits in his father's seat and the elders make way, as for the son of the king (2.14; Merry on 2.14; see [[elders]] and [[the-assembly-of-the-ithacans]]).

**The appeal.** He rejoices at the old man's word of omen and stands up in the middle, and the herald Peisenor puts the scepter in his hand (2.35–38; see [[aegyptius]], [[peisenor-the-herald]] and [[scepter]]): the staff is the badge of public office, and the herald who places it in a speaker's hand invests him with that office for the time (Merry on 2.37). It is his own business, he says:

{{quote:12b6de7c-d3d4-53ac-814d-a65f2795363e}}

The text reads κακά with Aristophanes, so that ὅ is “in that” and the evils fallen on the house are two; Aristarchus read κακόν, with δοιά in the sense “in two ways”, as a scholion reports (Merry on 2.45). He calls on the people to be indignant, to feel shame before their neighbors and to fear the wrath of the gods (2.64–67; see [[nemesis]], [[aidos]] and [[menis]]), begs them by Zeus and Themis (2.68–69; see [[themis-the-goddess]]), and asks, bitterly, whether his brave father did the Achaeans some harm for which they now take vengeance on the son (2.71–74). It would be better for him if they ate up his goods themselves, for then there would be recompense (2.74–79; see [[poine]]). Then he flings the scepter to the ground, as Achilles does in the Iliad's first assembly, “bursting into tears”, as old Phoenix does before he pleads with Achilles (2.80–81; *Iliad* 1.245, 9.433; see [[shedding-tears]] and [[pity]]).

**The answers.** To Antinous, who would have him send his mother back to her father, he answers that he cannot thrust out “the woman who bore me, who raised me” (2.130–137; see [[mothers-and-sons]]), and he repeats before the people the warning he gave the suitors the night before (2.139–145 = 1.374–380; see [[telemachus-and-the-suitors]]); Zeus answers his last words with two eagles (2.146; see [[the-omen-of-the-eagles]] and [[zeus]]). Eurymachus scorns him as a young man set on by the old seer, and the suitors fear no one, “not even Telemachus, for all his many words” (2.188–191, 2.200): πολύμυθος is the word Antenor denies of Menelaus, “since he was not a man of many words” (*Iliad* 3.214; see [[eurymachus]]). He will plead no more. He asks for a swift ship and twenty companions, as Athena told him to, and gives her plan in her own words, turned into the first person: if his father lives he will endure another year; if he is dead, he will come home, heap up a mound and give his mother to a husband (2.209–223, where 2.218–223 answer 1.287–292, and the twenty companions 1.280; see [[the-journey-of-telemachus]]).

**The prayer.** When the assembly is dissolved he goes apart along the shore, washes his hands in the gray sea, and prays to the god who came to the house yesterday (2.260–266; see [[prayer]] and [[prayer-type-scene]]). θεός is a predicate: he knew that the stranger was a god (see the translation's note on 2.262). Athena comes in the likeness of Mentor and tells him that he will be no coward and no fool, “if indeed your father's noble might has been instilled in you, / such a man as he was to accomplish both deed and word” (2.270–272), that few sons are like their father, and that the counsel of Odysseus has not wholly failed him (2.276–280; see [[few-sons-are-like-their-father]], [[fathers-and-sons]], [[metis]] and [[mentor]]). He does not stay long, “once he had heard the voice of the goddess” (2.297).

**In the house.** He refuses to feast with the suitors as before, “now that I am grown”, and draws his hand from Antinous' hand (2.298–322; see [[telemachus-and-the-suitors]]). Then he goes down into his father's storeroom, has Eurycleia draw off twelve jars of wine and pour twenty measures of barley meal into leather bags, and answers her lament: “Take heart, nurse, since this plan is not without a god” (2.337–372; see [[the-storeroom-of-odysseus]], [[provisions]], [[eurycleia]] and [[not-without-a-god]]). She swears not to tell his mother until the eleventh or twelfth day, “so that she may not mar her fair skin with weeping” (2.373–377; see [[oath]] and [[penelope]]).

**The departure.** Athena, now in his own likeness, gathers the crew, and at nightfall, as Mentor again, calls him out of the halls (2.382–404). On the shore “the sacred strength of Telemachus” speaks to his companions (2.409; see [[sacred-strength-of-telemachus]]), a phrase the poem gives him seven times, and he leads them, has the provisions stowed, and sits beside the goddess in the stern (2.409–418); then he bids them lay hold of the tackle, and they raise the mast (2.422–426; see [[the-journey-of-telemachus]] and [[sailing-type-scene]]).

**Wise Telemachus.** Four of his answers are brought in by the verse “And wise Telemachus answered him face to face” (2.129, 2.208, 2.309), with “her” to Eurycleia (2.371; see [[wise]]), and the narrator calls him “the dear son of Odysseus” when he rises, when he rejoices and when his orders are carried out (2.2, 2.35, 2.415; see [[dear-son-of-odysseus]]). To Antinous he is “Telemachus, lofty in speech, uncontrollable in your fury” (2.85, 2.303; see [[antinous]]).
