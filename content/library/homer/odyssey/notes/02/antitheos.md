---
summary: Aegyptius' dear son “had gone with godlike Odysseus / to Ilios of the fine horses in the hollow ships” (2.17–18).
---

The epithet that came with Odysseus' name the first time the poem spoke it (1.21) is used again in the dative, ἀντιθέῳ Ὀδυσῆι, in the old man's memory of the son whom the Cyclops ate (2.17–20; [[aegyptius]], [[antiphus-son-of-aegyptius]]). See [[odysseus]].
