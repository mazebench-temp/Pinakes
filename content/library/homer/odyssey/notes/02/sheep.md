# sensitive, since the dative plural οἷς "their own" (1.237 and often) has the same letters.

“but they haunt our house every day, / sacrificing oxen and sheep and fat goats” (2.55–56; see [[the-suitors]], [[cattle]] and [[goats]]). ὄις is the accusative plural, in form the same as the nominative singular (Attic οἶς), and the text prints it with and without the diaeresis: ὄις here and in the Cyclops' cave, where he sits and milks “the ewes and the bleating goats” (9.244, 9.341), ὄϊς when Penelope repeats this verse (17.535) and when the suitors slaughter “great sheep and fat goats” for their meals (17.180, 20.250; plain renderings). In the first book the sheep went with the cattle, “his thronging sheep and shambling cattle with curving horns”, which the suitors are forever slaughtering (1.92; see the Book 1 note).
