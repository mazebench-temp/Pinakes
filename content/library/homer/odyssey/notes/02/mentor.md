summary: Mentor, to whom Odysseus entrusted his house when he sailed, rises after Telemachus has asked for a ship. Let no king be kind again, he says, since no one remembers Odysseus; he does not blame the suitors, who stake their own heads, but the people, who sit silent though they are many. Leiocritus calls him crazed and dissolves the assembly, and then Athena takes his likeness, promises Telemachus a ship, and at nightfall leads him to it.

**The keeper of the house.** He stands up when Telemachus has sat down:

{{quote:6aebd152-adab-525f-9883-d9d5f437f527}}

Neither infinitive of 2.227 has a subject in the Greek, and the old man whom the house is to obey may be Mentor himself or Laertes (see the translation's note on 2.227 and [[laertes]]). He brings in his speech with the verse used for Halitherses (see [[halitherses]]), “With good intent he addressed them and spoke” (2.228, as 2.160; see [[with-good-intent]]), and begins with the address of every speaker in this assembly, “Hear me now, men of Ithaca, in what I say” (2.229, as 2.25 and 2.161; see [[hear-me-now-men-of-ithaca]] and [[ithacans]]).

**Let no king be kind.** His rebuke is a reproach to the people for their forgetfulness:

{{quote:bf271d97-16f8-52f7-ae38-b1ff766a75bb}}

The last half-verse is the one with which Telemachus began, “and was kind, like a father” (2.47; see [[kind-as-a-father]], [[basileus]] and [[kingship-and-authority]]), and Athena repeats the five verses to the gods when they meet again (5.8–12, the second council; see [[the-council-of-the-gods]]). He does not begrudge the proud suitors their violence, “for they stake their own heads” in devouring the house of Odysseus (2.235–238; see [[the-suitors]]); his indignation is for “the rest of the people”, who sit silent and do not stop the suitors, “few as they are, though you are many” (2.239–241; see [[nemesis]], [[demos]] and [[laos]]).

**Leiocritus' answer.** “Harsh Mentor, crazed in your mind” (2.243): ἀταρτηρός, “harsh”, is said once in the Iliad, of the words with which Achilles answers Agamemnon (*Iliad* 1.223), and φρένας ἠλεέ is Athena's “crazed in your mind” to Ares (*Iliad* 15.128). Leiocritus leaves the journey to Mentor and Halitherses in scorn (see [[halitherses]]), “who are his father's companions from of old” (2.253–254; see [[leiocritus-son-of-euenor]] and [[the-assembly-of-the-ithacans]]).

**In his likeness.** On the shore Athena comes to Telemachus “in the likeness of Mentor, both in form and in voice” (2.267–268) and speaks as his father's old friend: “such a companion of your father's am I to you / that I will fit out a swift ship for you and go with you myself” (2.286–287; see [[athena]] and [[divine-epiphany]]). At nightfall, when she has poured sleep on the suitors, she calls him out of the halls with the same verse, “in the likeness of Mentor, both in form and in voice” (2.399–401), and leads the way to the ship (2.405–406; see [[the-journey-of-telemachus]]). The goddess takes the shape of the man who has just spoken for Telemachus in the assembly, as in the first book she took the shape of a guest-friend of his house (see [[mentes-the-taphian]]).
