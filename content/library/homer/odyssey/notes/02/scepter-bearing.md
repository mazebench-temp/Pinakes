---
summary: Mentor: “Never again let any man with a ready heart be gentle and kind, / a scepter-bearing king,” since no one remembers divine Odysseus (2.230–234).
---

Mentor turns the formula of the king against the people who have forgotten theirs: let no scepter-bearing king be gentle and kind any more, “but let him always be harsh and do evil” (2.230–232; [[kind-as-a-father]], [[kings]]). Athena speaks the same five verses among the gods at the beginning of Book 5 (5.8–12). Telemachus has held the scepter in the assembly and flung it to the ground (2.37, 2.80; [[scepter]]). See [[mentor]] and [[basileus]].
