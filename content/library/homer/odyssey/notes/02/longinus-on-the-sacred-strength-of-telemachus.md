---
summary: The phrase that Longinus set beside Plato's is used first in this book, when Telemachus takes command of his crew on the shore, on the day on which Athena hoped that his father's noble might was in him.
refs: [2.409]
---

The phrase comes when Telemachus and the goddess reach the shore and find the long-haired companions there: “And among them spoke also the sacred strength of Telemachus” (2.409). What he says is a command, “Come here, friends, let us bring the provisions” (2.410); he leads the way, and they stow everything on the ship “as the dear son of Odysseus bade them” (2.413–415), and on board he urges them to lay hold of the tackle (2.422–423; see [[the-journey-of-telemachus]]). The strength that the formula gives him is the strength that Athena, in Mentor's likeness, hoped to find in him earlier that day: “if indeed your father's noble might has been instilled in you, / such a man as he was to accomplish both deed and word” (2.271–272; see [[fathers-and-sons]]). In Longinus' comparison the phrase is only a way of naming a man; in this book it comes as the son takes command of men of his own.
