summary: The verse of Eurycleia's lineage, “Eurycleia, daughter of Ops, son of Peisenor”, returns to name the keeper of Odysseus' storeroom (2.347).
---

Εὐρύκλειʼ, Ὦπος θυγάτηρ Πεισηνορίδαο, “Eurycleia, daughter of Ops, son of Peisenor” (2.347). In the first book the verse began the story of her purchase by Laertes (1.429–433); here it closes the description of the storeroom, naming the woman who keeps it night and day (2.345–347; see [[eurycleia]] and [[the-storeroom-of-odysseus]]). Ops is named only to name his daughter, and the book says nothing more of him (see [[peisenor-father-of-ops]]).
