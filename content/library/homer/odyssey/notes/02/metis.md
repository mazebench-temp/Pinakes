summary: Halitherses remembers “Odysseus of many wiles” sailing for Ilios (2.173), and Athena, in the likeness of Mentor, tells Telemachus that “the counsel of Odysseus has not wholly failed you” (2.279).
grc: [μῆτις]
en: [counsel]
---

**The father's counsel in the son.** Athena puts the inheritance in two words of the father's: μένος, if “your father's noble might has been instilled in you” (2.271; see [[menos]]), and μῆτις, “the counsel of Odysseus”, which “has not wholly failed you” (2.279). Between them stands the rule that few sons are like their father (2.276–277; see [[few-sons-are-like-their-father]] and [[fathers-and-sons]]). The translation renders μῆτις here “counsel”, the planning a voyage needs; Odysseus' own μῆτις is the trick that takes him out of the Cyclops' cave, when his name and his μῆτις deceived the giant, ὡς ὄνομʼ ἐξαπάτησεν ἐμὸν καὶ μῆτις ἀμύμων (9.414), with a play on μή τις, “no one” (9.405–410).

**The man of many wiles.** Halitherses gives Odysseus his epithet as he remembers the day “when they were embarking for Ilios, / the Argives, and among them went Odysseus of many wiles” (2.172–173; see [[of-many-wiles]] and [[halitherses]]). His wife's share of the same gifts is named by the suitors with other words, κέρδεα, “crafty ways”, and νοήματα, “devices” (2.88, 2.118, 2.121; see [[kerdos]] and [[noos]]).
