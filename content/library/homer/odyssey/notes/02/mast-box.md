---
summary: As Telemachus' ship puts out from Ithaca, the crew raise the mast of fir and set it up “within the hollow mast-box” (2.424).
---

Once Athena has sent the West Wind and Telemachus has bidden his companions lay hold of the tackle, they raise the mast and stand it in its box: “The mast of fir, within the hollow mast-box, / they raised and set up” (2.424–425; see [[mast]] and [[sailing-type-scene]]). κοίλης, “hollow”, is here the box's epithet, not the ship's, though the same word is the standing epithet of ships (see [[hollow-ships]]). The three verses of the mast, the forestays and the sails return word for word when Telemachus sails home from Pylos (15.289–291 = 2.424–426), and the word is found nowhere else in Homer of a ship (see the translation's table of key words, μεσόδμη “mast-box”).
