---
summary: “Brilliant Odysseus” twice: there has been no assembly “since brilliant Odysseus went away in the hollow ships” (2.27), and Penelope, in Antinous' account of the web, speaks of him as dead (2.96).
---

At 2.27 the epithet follows the name, Ὀδυσσεὺς δῖος, in the middle of the verse; at 2.96 it is the verse-end formula δῖος Ὀδυσσεύς, with which Telemachus spoke of his father as dead in Book 1 (1.396). Penelope's words, as the suitor quotes them, begin her trick: “Young men, my suitors, since brilliant Odysseus is dead, / wait” (2.96–97; [[the-web-of-penelope]]). See [[odysseus]] and [[aegyptius]].
