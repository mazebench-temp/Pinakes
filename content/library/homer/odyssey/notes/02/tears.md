---
summary: Aegyptius speaks “Shedding tears” for his lost son (2.24), and Telemachus flings down the scepter “bursting into tears” (2.80–81); he asks Eurycleia to keep his voyage secret, so that his mother “may not mar her fair skin with weeping” (2.376).
grc: [κλαίουσα]
en: [weeping]
---

In the assembly the old man weeps as he begins to speak, and the young man when he has spoken; pity follows, and silence (2.81–82; see [[pity]] and [[silence]]). In the last assembly of the poem Eupeithes speaks shedding tears for his son, in the verse of Aegyptius (24.425, as 2.24; see [[assembly]]).
