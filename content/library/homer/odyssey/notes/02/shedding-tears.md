---
summary: Aegyptius speaks “shedding tears” for his lost son (2.24), and Telemachus flings the scepter to the ground, “bursting into tears” (2.80–81).
grc: ["δάκρυʼ ἀναπρήσας"]
en: [bursting into tears]
---

The old man's tears are for Antiphus, whom the Cyclops ate, though his speech does not name his grief; Merry takes τοῦ as “for him” (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 2.24; [[aegyptius]]). Telemachus' tears end his own speech: “So he spoke in anger, and flung the scepter to the ground, / bursting into tears; and pity seized all the people” (2.80–81). The gesture is Achilles' in the quarrel of *Iliad* 1 (ποτὶ δὲ σκῆπτρον βάλε γαίῃ, *Iliad* 1.245), and the participle is Phoenix's before his answer to Achilles (*Iliad* 9.433). See [[scepter]] and [[telemachus]].
