summary: The absent king of the assembly. His son and Mentor remember a king who was “kind, like a father”, and no one has called the people together since he sailed; the book is divided over whether he lives, the suitors and the old nurse sure that he is dead, the eagles and Halitherses that he is near. His companions keep his house, his storeroom keeps wine against his return, and the goddess finds his might and counsel in his son.

**The king who was kind.** Telemachus begins his appeal with the loss of his father, “who once among you / here was king, and was kind, like a father” (2.46–47), and Mentor ends his rebuke with the same words: “since no one remembers divine Odysseus / among the people he was lord over, and he was kind, like a father” (2.233–234; see [[kind-as-a-father]], [[basileus]] and [[mentor]]). In the Iliad the comparison is Helen's, of Priam (*Iliad* 24.770), and Athena repeats Mentor's verses to the gods (5.8–12). There has been no assembly since he went away in the ships (2.26–27; see [[the-assembly-of-the-ithacans]]), “For there is no man here / such as Odysseus was, to ward off disaster from the house” (2.58–59).

**Alive or dead.** His son does not know: “my father is elsewhere on earth, / alive or dead” (2.131–132), and he asks for a ship to learn which (2.215–223; see [[the-journey-of-telemachus]]). The suitors are sure, and “say that he will come home no more” (2.238); for Eurymachus he “has perished far away” (2.182–183), and Eurycleia weeps that he “has perished far from his native land, / Odysseus, sprung from Zeus, among a strange people” (2.365–366; see [[eurycleia]]). Against them stand the eagles of Zeus and the old reader of birds: Odysseus will not be long away, “but is already somewhere / near, and plants slaughter and doom for these men”, as was foretold when he sailed, that he would come home unknown to all in the twentieth year (2.163–176; see [[the-omen-of-the-eagles]], [[the-prophecy-of-halitherses]] and [[the-return-of-odysseus]]). Leiocritus scorns the thought: if he came, he would die fighting so many in his own hall (2.246–251; see [[leiocritus-son-of-euenor]]).

**His companions.** He left his whole house in Mentor's charge (2.225–227), and Mentor and Halitherses are his “companions from of old” (2.254; see [[mentor]], [[halitherses]] and [[hetairos]]). The son of Aegyptius, Antiphus, sailed with him to Ilios and was eaten by the Cyclops (2.17–20; see [[antiphus-son-of-aegyptius]] and [[polyphemus-the-cyclops]]), and the prophecy of his return foretold that he would lose all his companions (2.174).

**In his son.** Athena, as Mentor, makes the father the measure of the son: “if indeed your father's noble might has been instilled in you, / such a man as he was to accomplish both deed and word” (2.271–272), and “the counsel of Odysseus has not wholly failed you” (μῆτις Ὀδυσσῆος, 2.279; see [[metis]], [[fathers-and-sons]] and [[telemachus]]).

**His storeroom.** In the high-roofed chamber of the house stand jars of old wine, “in case Odysseus ever / came home, even after toiling through many sorrows” (2.337–343; see [[the-storeroom-of-odysseus]]), and Telemachus asks for the sweetest after the one Eurycleia keeps for “that ill-fated man” (2.349–352; see [[wine]]).

**His epithets.** δῖος, “brilliant” (2.27, 2.96); ἀντίθεος, “godlike” (2.17); πολύμητις, “of many wiles” (2.173); θεῖος, “divine” (2.233, 2.259, 2.394); ἀμύμων, “noble” (2.225); διογενής, “sprung from Zeus” (2.352, 2.366); Ἰθακήσιος, “of Ithaca” (2.246); ἐσθλός, “brave” (2.46, 2.71); and three times his son is “the dear son of Odysseus” (2.2, 2.35, 2.415).
