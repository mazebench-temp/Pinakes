---
summary: Antinous mocks Telemachus with a promise of “a ship and choice oarsmen” (2.307); he has “no ship of my own, nor oarsmen” (2.319); and at nightfall his companions “sit at the oars”, waiting for him (2.403).
---

The twenty companions Telemachus asks the assembly for are the crew of his ship (2.212), as Athena had told him to fit out the best ship there is “with twenty oarsmen” (1.280; see [[ships]]). Antinous answers with mockery: the Achaeans will see to “a ship and choice oarsmen” (2.307), ἐξαίτους ἐρέτας, the word that elsewhere picks out choice wine and choice hecatombs (*Iliad* 12.320; 5.102). Telemachus will go as a passenger, “for I have no ship of my own, nor oarsmen” (2.319).

The oarsmen come from Athena. When she has gathered them, she calls Telemachus out: “already your well-greaved companions / sit at the oars, waiting for you to set out” (2.402–403; see [[ithacans]]). ἐπήρετμοι, “at the oars”, is said elsewhere of ships, in the verse that tells what Odysseus lacks on Calypso's island, “he has no ships with oars and no companions” to carry him home (4.559, 5.16, 5.141, 17.145; see [[calypso]]). The companions sit down at the benches (2.419; see [[rowing-benches]]), but the poem does not have them row: Athena's West Wind carries the ship (2.420–421; see [[zephyrus]]).
