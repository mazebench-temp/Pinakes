---
summary: Eurymachus answers Halitherses with the formula of prophecy, “But I will speak out to you, and it will surely come to pass” (2.187), and what will come to pass is a fine laid on the old man.
---

The suitor takes up the seer's language for his threat: if Halitherses urges Telemachus to anger, it will be the worse for the young man, “and on you, old man, we will lay a fine” (2.187–193; [[divination]]). The seer had just said of his own prophecy, “and now it is all being fulfilled” (τὰ δὲ δὴ νῦν πάντα τελεῖται, 2.176; [[the-prophecy-of-halitherses]]). In Book 1 the goddess, speaking as a mortal, used the weaker form, “as I think it will be fulfilled” (1.201). See [[eurymachus]] and [[halitherses]].
