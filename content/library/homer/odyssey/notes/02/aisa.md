---
summary: Halitherses excels “in speaking words of fate” (ἐναίσιμα, 2.159), and Eurymachus answers that not all birds are “birds of fate” (ἐναίσιμοι, 2.182). Antinous says that Penelope did not devise her delay “rightly” (ἐναίσιμον, 2.122), and Mentor's good king knows “in his mind what is due” (αἴσιμα, 2.231).
grc: [ἐναίσιμον, ἐναίσιμα, ἐναίσιμοι, αἴσιμα]
en: [of fate, rightly, what is due]
---

ἐναίσιμος, “in accordance with αἶσα”, is what is fated and what is fitting, and the book uses both: the seer's words and the birds that carry fate (see the translation's table and [[many-birds-not-all-of-fate]]), a course rightly devised (2.122), and the king's sense of what is due (2.231; see [[basileus]]). In the Iliad it is a good thing “when a messenger knows what is due” (αἴσιμα, *Iliad* 15.207; see [[a-messenger-who-knows-what-is-due]]).
