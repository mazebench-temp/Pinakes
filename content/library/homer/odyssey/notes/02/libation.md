**Libations at sea.** The last verses of the book are a libation under sail:

{{quote:7f9de893-f58b-5eae-8acb-f4a5ceac7543}}

The wine is poured once the ship is under way, the mast stepped and the sail full of Athena's wind (2.420–429), and the bowls are set up on board (see [[mixing-bowl]], [[brim-full]] and [[sailing-type-scene]]). λείβω, “pour in drops”, is the verb of the libation; the drink offering is poured to all the gods, and above all to the goddess who has sent the wind and sits in the stern in the likeness of Mentor, with Telemachus beside her (2.416–418, 2.420–421; see [[athena]]). On the shore, when she promised him the ship, Telemachus “had heard the voice of the goddess” (2.297).

**Libations for a journey.** In the Iliad, too, wine is poured before men set out: the envoys pour libations and drink before they go to Achilles (*Iliad* 9.174–177), and Priam pours one, praying to Zeus for a sign, before he drives to Achilles' hut (*Iliad* 24.302–313; see [[wings-as-wide-as-a-door]]). The journey of Telemachus ends as it begins, with libations: when he leaves Sparta, Menelaus comes out with wine in a golden cup, so that they may pour a libation before they go (15.147–150), and when he makes ready to sail home from Pylos he is praying and sacrificing to Athena by the stern of his ship, and pouring libations, when the seer Theoclymenus finds him (15.222–223, 15.257–258; plain renderings; see [[the-journey-of-telemachus]]).
