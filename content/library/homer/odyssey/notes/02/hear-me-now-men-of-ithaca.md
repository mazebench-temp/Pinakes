---
summary: Three speakers open with the verse in the assembly: Aegyptius, who asks who has called it (2.25), Halitherses, who reads the eagles (2.161), and Mentor, who rebukes the silent people (2.229).
---

Each speaker turns the address to his own purpose. Aegyptius asks the men of Ithaca who has gathered them, in the first assembly since brilliant Odysseus sailed (2.25–34; [[aegyptius]], [[the-assembly-of-the-ithacans]]). Halitherses speaks to them all, “and it is above all to the suitors that I declare these things” (2.161–162; [[halitherses]], [[the-omen-of-the-eagles]]). Mentor speaks to the people who sit silent and do not stop the suitors, “few as they are, though you are many” (2.229, 2.239–241; [[mentor]], [[ithacans]]). Telemachus does not use the verse: he answers the old man who asked, “Old man, that man is not far off” (2.40). The suitors who answer address one man each, “Telemachus” (2.85), “Old man” (2.178), “Harsh Mentor” (2.243).
