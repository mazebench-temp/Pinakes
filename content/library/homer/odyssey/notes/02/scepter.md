---
summary: Telemachus speaks with the scepter the herald Peisenor puts in his hand (2.37–38), and flings it to the ground in tears (2.80).
---

The assembly's scepter passes to whoever speaks: “the scepter was put in his hand / by the herald Peisenor, who knew wise counsels” (2.37–38; see [[peisenor-the-herald]] and [[heralds]]). When he has spoken, Telemachus “flung the scepter to the ground, / bursting into tears” (2.80–81), in the words with which Achilles flings it down in the quarrel, “So spoke the son of Peleus, and flung the scepter to the ground” (*Iliad* 1.245; see [[the-assembly-of-the-ithacans]]). Mentor names the king by it: let no “scepter-bearing king” be gentle and kind any longer (2.231; see [[scepter-bearing]]).
