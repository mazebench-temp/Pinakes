# Unnamed: the jibe about Ephyra and its drugs (2.328-330).
refs: [2.328-330]
---

{{quote:dfed349e-45b8-533d-8266-eb7052908486}}

**The jibe.** Telemachus has refused Antinous' hand and the feast, and the suitors taunt him; this is what one of “the overweening young men” would say (2.321–325; see [[telemachus-and-the-suitors]]). Surely, he says, Telemachus is pondering slaughter for them, and he gives him three ways to bring it: helpers from sandy Pylos, or from Sparta, or drugs from Ephyra (2.325–330; see [[ephyra]], [[pylos]] and [[sparta]]). He did not hear the stranger's tale in the first book: Telemachus sat with the stranger apart from the suitors and spoke with his head close, “so that the others might not hear” (1.132–135, 1.156–157; see [[athena-visits-telemachus]]). The suitor knows Ephyra as a place of drugs without it, and the poem names again the place where Odysseus sought a drug for his arrows (1.259–264).

**Drugs that destroy life.** θυμοφθόρα φάρμακʼ, “drugs that destroy life” (2.329): the first book's drug was ἀνδροφόνον, “man-slaying”, for smearing arrows (1.261–262; see [[man-slaying]] and [[ephyra]]); this one is for the wine, “and put them in the mixing bowl, and destroy us all” (2.330; see [[mixing-bowl]]). So Helen casts a drug into the wine at Sparta, one that banishes grief (4.219–221; see [[helen]]), and Circe mixes hers with the food and wine she gives Odysseus' men (10.234–236; see [[circe]]). When Odysseus turns on the suitors at their feast he uses the bow, and nothing is said of poison (22.1–21; see [[bow]]). The renderings of other books are plain renderings, not the translation.
