---
summary: The young suitors jeer that Telemachus may go to Ephyra, “that rich plowland”, for drugs to put in their mixing bowl (2.328–330), the place where Odysseus once sought a man-slaying drug (1.259–263).
---

After Pylos and Sparta, Ephyra comes last in the suitors' mockery, as the place of poisons:

{{quote:dfed349e-45b8-533d-8266-eb7052908486}}

The drug of Book 1 was for arrows, a φάρμακον ἀνδροφόνον, “a man-slaying drug” (1.261); these are θυμοφθόρα φάρμακα, “drugs that destroy life”, for the mixing bowl. The adjective is used in the Iliad of the deadly signs that Proetus scratched on a folded tablet for Bellerophon to carry (*Iliad* 6.169; see [[bellerophon]]). πίειραν ἄρουραν, “rich plowland”, is said of a place only here and of the land of the Lotus-eaters (23.311); on the shield of Achilles it is the fallow field being plowed (*Iliad* 18.541).

The suitors do not say which Ephyra they mean (see the translation's note on 1.259). Merry, arguing for the Ephyra of Elis, sets both passages of drugs beside Agamede, the daughter of Augeas, king of Elis, “who knew all the drugs that the wide earth nourishes” (*Iliad* 11.740–741; see [[agamede]]), and counts the suitors' naming of Ephyra with Pylos and Sparta, as if all three lay in the Peloponnese, among his reasons (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 1.259).
