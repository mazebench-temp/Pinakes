---
summary: “And when they had gathered and were all assembled” (2.9): the men of Ithaca gather for the first assembly since Odysseus sailed.
grc: ["ἤγερθεν ὁμηγερέες τʼ ἐγένοντο"]
---

The verse follows the heralds' summons (2.6–8; [[clear-voiced]]), and in this form it is said in the *Iliad* of the Trojans gathered at Hector's pyre (*Iliad* 24.790). Merry holds that the line is no tautology: the first clause is the gathering of the people toward the place of meeting, the second the assembly fully formed (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 2.9; see the translation's note on 2.9). The Odyssey uses it again for the assembly of the Phaeacians and for the last assembly of Ithaca, after the killing of the suitors (8.24, 24.421). See [[assembly-type-scene]].
