# Unnamed: "Fill twelve, and fit them all with lids" (2.353).
refs: [2.353]
---

The jars come first in Athena's orders: “make ready provisions and fit them all into vessels, / wine in two-handled jars, and barley meal, the marrow of men” (2.289–290; see [[provisions]] and [[athena]]). In the storeroom Telemachus repeats them to his nurse: “Nurse, come, draw me off wine in two-handled jars”, “Fill twelve, and fit them all with lids” (2.349, 2.353; see [[eurycleia]] and [[the-storeroom-of-odysseus]]). πῶμα is a lid, as of a quiver or a chest (*Iliad* 4.116, 16.221; see [[chest]]). Twelve jars of wine are also the gift of Maron to Odysseus, drawn off with the same verb, ἀφύσσας (9.204). When she has sworn, Eurycleia “drew off wine for him in two-handled jars” (2.379), and at night the crew carry the jars and the bags of meal to the ship (2.410–415).
