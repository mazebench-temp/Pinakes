---
summary: Telemachus' prayer on the shore opens “Hear me, you god who came yesterday to our house” (2.262), and it is answered by the goddess' coming (2.267–268).
---

The prayer names no god; Telemachus calls on the god who came to the house the day before (2.262–264; see the translation's note on 2.262). It closes with the usual verse, ὣς ἔφατʼ εὐχόμενος, but where the Iliad goes on “and (the god) heard him” (τοῦ δʼ ἔκλυε), Athena “came near to him / in the likeness of Mentor” (2.267–268; [[prayer-type-scene]], [[in-the-likeness-of-mentor]]). The speakers of the assembly open with another imperative of the same verb, κέκλυτε δὴ νῦν μευ, “Hear me now” ([[hear-me-now-men-of-ithaca]]).
