---
summary: “And they were amazed at the birds, when they saw them with their eyes, / and pondered in their hearts what was to be fulfilled” (2.155–156): the Ithacans' amazement at the eagles of Zeus.
---

The amazement is the people's first answer to the omen, before anyone reads it: they see the two eagles wheel over the assembly, tear each other and dart away on the right, and they ponder what is to come (2.146–156; see [[the-omen-of-the-eagles]] and [[eagle]]). Halitherses then speaks, the only man of his age who knew how to read birds (2.157–159; see [[halitherses]] and [[divination]]).
