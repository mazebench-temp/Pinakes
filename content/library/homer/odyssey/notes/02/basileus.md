**The good king and the bad.** Mentor's wish is ironic, and it describes a good king by his opposite. The one he would no longer have is πρόφρων, “with a ready heart”, “gentle and kind” (ἀγανὸς καὶ ἤπιος), and knows “in his mind what is due” (αἴσιμα, 2.230–231; see [[aisa]]); the one he wishes on the people is “always … harsh” and does αἴσυλα, “evil” (2.232). Odysseus was the first kind, and the people have forgotten him (2.233–234; see [[kind-as-a-father]] and [[laos]]). Athena speaks the same five verses to Zeus and the gods on Olympus at the beginning of Book 5, and the council sends Hermes to Calypso (5.7–12; see [[athena]]).

**A king in the past tense.** Telemachus speaks of his father's reign as over: “who once among you / here was king” (βασίλευε, 2.46–47). In the assembly he holds the scepter only as a speaker, put into his hand by the herald when he rises (2.37–38; see [[scepter]]). Merry explains that the staff was the badge of public office, carried by kings, priests, prophets, heralds and judges, and that the herald, placing it in a speaker's hand, invested him for the time being with a public office (*Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 2.37). Telemachus flings it to the ground (2.80). Who is king in Ithaca is the question Antinous and Telemachus raised the evening before (1.384–402; see [[kings]] and [[kingship-and-authority]]); in the assembly no one asks it.
