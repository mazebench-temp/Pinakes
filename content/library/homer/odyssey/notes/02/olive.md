# The phrase "ἐυῶδες ἔλαιον" (2.339), only here in Homer; ἔλαιον alone is left to bathing (see the entry).
grc: ["ἐυῶδες ἔλαιον"]
en: [oil]
---

The storeroom holds “clothing in chests, and fragrant oil in plenty” beside the gold and the bronze (2.338–339; see [[the-storeroom-of-odysseus]] and [[chest]]). ἔλαιον is the oil of the olive, and in Homer it is for anointing, the living after the bath and the dead before burial (see [[bathing]]); εὐώδης, “fragrant”, is said of oil only here, and elsewhere in Homer of a chamber at Troy and of the cypress on Calypso's island (*Iliad* 3.382; 5.64). The oil is part of the wealth that the house keeps, with its clothing and its wine.
