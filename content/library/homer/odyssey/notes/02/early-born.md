---
summary: “Early-born Dawn” begins the verse of the first dawn in the Odyssey, the morning of the assembly (2.1).
---

ἠριγένεια opens the verse of daybreak and ῥοδοδάκτυλος closes it, with φάνη, “appeared,” between them ([[dawn-line]], [[rosy-fingered]]). Merry gives Ἠώς, Aeolic αὔως, as the same word as Latin *Aurora*, from a root meaning “to give heat, or light” (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 2.1). See [[eos]].
