**From the hall to the storeroom.** The suitors have jeered that Telemachus will fetch helpers from Pylos or Sparta, or drugs from Ephyra, or else perish at sea like his father and leave them his possessions to divide (2.325–336; see [[the-suitors]] and [[odysseus-seeks-poison]]). “So they spoke; but he went down into the high-roofed chamber of his father” (2.337). The scene passes from the hall, where the suitors drink the sparkling wine of the house (2.57), to the locked room where its wealth is kept and the old wine stands waiting for Odysseus' homecoming (2.340–345; see [[storage-jar]] and [[wine]]).

**The keeper.** The description ends with the woman who guards everything there “with the great knowledge of her mind” (2.346), and the narrator names her in the verse of her lineage, “Eurycleia, daughter of Ops, son of Peisenor” (2.347, as 1.429; see [[eurycleia]], [[ops]] and [[handmaids]]). Telemachus calls her “to the chamber” (θαλαμόνδε, 2.348) and asks her for twelve two-handled jars of wine, “the sweetest after the one you are keeping”, and twenty measures of barley meal in leather bags, all to be made ready together for him to take in the evening (2.349–358; see [[two-handled-jar]], [[barley-meal]] and [[provisions]]). “You alone must know of it” (2.356): the provisions leave the room in secret, under her oath (2.373–380), and that night only one woman of the house has heard of the voyage (2.411–412; see [[the-journey-of-telemachus]]).
