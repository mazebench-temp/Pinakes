# φᾶρος is "mantle" in the translation (2.97, as Iliad 24.588); the Iliad's "mantle" is in its Book 24 note.
en: [mantle]
---

“wait, eager though you are for my marriage, until I have this mantle / finished” (2.97–98; see [[the-web-of-penelope]] and [[shroud]]). The φᾶρος is the great mantle of kings and goddesses, and the cloth in which the dead are wrapped: Hector is given back in a mantle and a tunic (*Iliad* 24.588), and Penelope's mantle is to be ταφήιον, “a shroud”, for Laertes (2.99). In her own telling of the trick a god first breathed the φᾶρος into her mind (19.138), and in the shade of Amphimedon's she washed it when it was finished and showed it, shining like the sun or the moon (24.147–148; plain renderings).
