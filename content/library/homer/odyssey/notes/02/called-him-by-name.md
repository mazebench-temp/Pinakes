---
summary: Antinous, with a laugh, “clung to his hand, and spoke to him, and called him by name” (2.302).
---

The whole verse, ἔν τʼ ἄρα οἱ φῦ χειρί, ἔπος τʼ ἔφατʼ ἔκ τʼ ὀνόμαζε, is the one with which Hecuba and Andromache take Hector's hand in *Iliad* 6 (*Iliad* 6.253, 6.406). Here the gesture of closeness is a suitor's, and the name is followed by the mocking epithets of the assembly, “Telemachus, lofty in speech, uncontrollable in your fury” (2.301–303; [[lofty-in-speech]]). When he has refused, Telemachus draws his hand from the hand of Antinous “easily” (2.321–322). See [[antinous]] and [[telemachus-and-the-suitors]].
