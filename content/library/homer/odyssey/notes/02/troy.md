---
summary: Ilios is named twice, both times as the place the fleet sailed for twenty years before: Aegyptius' son Antiphus went there with Odysseus, and Halitherses prophesied when the Argives were embarking for it (2.17–18, 2.172–173). Of the war itself the book says nothing.
---

**Where they sailed.** “For his dear son too had gone with godlike Odysseus / to Ilios of the fine horses in the hollow ships” (2.17–18; see [[aegyptius]], [[antiphus-son-of-aegyptius]] and [[hollow-ships]]). Ἴλιον εἰς ἐύπωλον, “to Ilios of the fine horses” (see [[of-the-fine-horses]]), is a formula of both poems for those who went to the war. In the Iliad it brings in men who followed a leader to Troy and died there, Crethon and Orsilochus, who “followed with the Argives to Ilios of the fine horses”, and Epeigeus, whom Peleus and Thetis sent with Achilles “to Ilios of the fine horses, to fight the Trojans” (*Iliad* 5.541–551, 16.570–577). In the Odyssey Odysseus says the same of himself to his mother's shade, and Eumaeus of him (11.168–169, 14.70–71). Here the verse brings in Antiphus, who followed Odysseus and did not come back: the Cyclops ate him on the way home (2.19–20; see [[polyphemus-the-cyclops]]).

**When they sailed.** Halitherses' prophecy was made “when they were embarking for Ilios, / the Argives, and among them went Odysseus of many wiles” (2.172–173; see [[argives]], [[of-many-wiles]] and [[the-prophecy-of-halitherses]]). εἰσανέβαινον is “were embarking”, as ἀναβήμεναι, “embarked”, in Mentes' memory of Odysseus before he “embarked for Troy” (1.210), where the Iliad's Ἴλιον εἰσανέβησαν is “gone up into Ilios” (*Iliad* 6.74; see the translation's note on 2.172). The book's other departures do not name the city: Odysseus “went away in the hollow ships” (2.27), and “when he went in the ships” he entrusted all his house to Mentor (2.226; see [[mentor]]). Twenty years have passed since the fleet sailed (2.175), and nothing is said of the war or of the city's fall.
