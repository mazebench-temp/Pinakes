**The women of old.** Antinous complains that Penelope keeps the suitors waiting with crafty ways such as they “have never heard of in any woman, not even of old”, and he names three of the women of the old stories, “Tyro and Alcmene and well-crowned Mycene” (2.115–122; see [[penelope]], [[kerdos]], [[achaean-women]], [[tyro]] and [[mycene]]). Alcmene is the only one of the three whom the Iliad names: Zeus counts her among his loves, and Agamemnon tells how Hera held back her labor so that Eurystheus was born before Heracles (*Iliad* 14.323–324, 19.95–124; see [[the-birth-of-heracles]]). In the underworld Odysseus sees her after Tyro and Antiope, “the wife of Amphitryon, who bore Heracles, bold in endurance and lion-hearted, after she had lain in the arms of great Zeus” (11.260–268; see [[amphitryon]] and [[heracles]]). The renderings of other books are plain renderings, not the translation.
