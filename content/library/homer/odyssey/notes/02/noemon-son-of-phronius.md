---
summary: Athena, in the likeness of Telemachus, asks Noemon for a swift ship, and he gladly promises it (2.386–387).
---

Telemachus has told the suitors that he will go as a passenger, “for I have no ship of my own, nor oarsmen” (2.319), and Athena, in the likeness of Mentor, has promised him the best of the many ships of Ithaca, new and old (2.292–294; see [[ships]]). The ship she finds is Noemon's. After gathering the crew in the likeness of Telemachus, “she asked Noemon, the shining son of Phronius, / for a swift ship; and he gladly promised it to her” (2.386–387; see [[phronius]] and [[swift-ships]]). Then, at sunset, she drags it down to the sea herself and moors it at the far edge of the harbor (2.388–391; see [[harbor]]). Noemon is named only here in the book; he returns in Book 4, where his question about the ship he lent tells the suitors that Telemachus has sailed (4.630–656).
