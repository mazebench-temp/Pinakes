---
summary: Athena, in Mentor's likeness, tells Telemachus that his father was a man “to accomplish both deed and word” (2.272), and Antinous bids him let no other “evil be a care in your breast, either deed or word” (2.303–304). Mentor blames the people, who “do not approach them with words” (2.240), and the suitors meet Telemachus' plans as he goes, “And they insulted him and taunted him with words” (2.323).
---

**Deed and word.** ἔργον τε ἔπος τε joins what a man does to what he says. Athena uses the pair of Odysseus: if his might is in his son, the son's journey “will not be in vain or unfulfilled”, since his father was “such a man as he was to accomplish both deed and word” (2.271–273; see [[fathers-and-sons]] and [[menos]]). It is what Peleus sent Phoenix to make of Achilles, “a speaker of words and a doer of deeds” (*Iliad* 9.443; see [[speaker-of-words-doer-of-deeds]]). Antinous turns the pair to the feast: let no evil of deed or word trouble him, “but eat and drink with me, just as before” (2.303–305; see [[antinous]]).

**Words against deeds.** The people's fault is a want of words: they “sit silent, and do not approach them with words / and stop the suitors” (καθαπτόμενοι ἐπέεσσι, 2.240–241; see [[silence]] and [[nemesis]]). Merry explains καθάπτομαι as earnest address, not necessarily rough, the word with which Telemachus turns to Aegyptius (2.39), and compares this line (*Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 2.39). Eurymachus accuses Halitherses of “beguiling” Telemachus “with your words” (παρφάμενος ἐπέεσσιν, 2.189), and the suitors answer his plans with words alone (ἐκερτόμεον ἐπέεσσιν, 2.323; see [[kertomia]]).
