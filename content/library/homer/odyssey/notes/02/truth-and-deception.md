---
summary: Penelope deceives the suitors with promises and with the web, until one of her women “who knew it clearly” tells them (2.91–110); Athena goes about Ithaca in the likeness of Mentor and of Telemachus (2.268, 2.383, 2.401); and Telemachus keeps his voyage from his mother, while the suitors, his nurse fears, will plot that he “may perish by guile” (2.367–376).
refs: [2.91-110, 2.267-268, 2.367-368, 2.373-376, 2.383, 2.401]
---

**The deceiver deceived.** Antinous makes Penelope the deceiver: she gives hope to all and sends them messages, “but her mind has other things in view” (2.91–92; see [[noos]]), and her web is a trick (δόλος) that kept the Achaeans waiting three years (2.93–106; see [[dolos]] and [[the-web-of-penelope]]). Truth comes out through a servant, “one of her women told us, who knew it clearly” (2.108). The poem later gives Penelope's side of the same words: Athena tells Odysseus that his wife's mind has other things in view because she mourns for him (13.379–381).

**Gods in disguise.** Athena deceives for Telemachus, not against him. She comes to him in Mentor's likeness, goes through the city in his own likeness to gather a crew, and calls him to the ship as Mentor again (2.267–268, 2.383–387, 2.399–401; see [[athena]], [[seeing-the-gods]] and [[eidos]]). He took her for a god the day before (1.323, 1.420) and says only that his plan is “not without a god” (2.372; see [[not-without-a-god]]).

**Secrets.** Telemachus keeps the voyage from his mother and binds his nurse by oath (2.373–378; see [[oath]]), and Eurycleia's fear is of a trick against him (2.367–368). The suitors, deceived for three years, imagine plots of their own: Telemachus, they jest, will fetch helpers from Pylos or poison from Ephyra (2.325–330; see [[odysseus-seeks-poison]]). Meanwhile the ship is made ready, and the suitors sleep while it sails (2.393–398; see [[hypnos]]).
