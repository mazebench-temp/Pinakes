---
summary: Athena “poured sweet sleep upon the suitors”, made the cups fall from their hands, and sent them through the city to bed, “since sleep was falling on their eyelids” (2.395–398).
grc: [εὕδειν]
---

The sleep is Athena's device for the departure. Having gathered the crew and the ship, she goes to the house, pours sleep on the suitors and confuses them as they drink (πλάζε δὲ πίνοντας, 2.396), so that the hall is empty when she calls Telemachus out to the ship (2.393–404; see [[athena]] and [[the-journey-of-telemachus]]). The night before she had cast sleep on Penelope's eyelids, after her weeping for her husband (1.363–364). The suitors go to their own houses to sleep, οἱ δʼ εὕδειν ὤρνυντο κατὰ πτόλιν (2.397), as they did the evening before (1.424); see [[sweet-sleep]] and, for the god, [[sleep]].
