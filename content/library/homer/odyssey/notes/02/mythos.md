grc: [μύθοισιν, ἐμυθεόμην, μυθέαι]
en: [words, told, pronounce, tell]
---

**Speaking out.** The evening before, Telemachus told his mother that “speech will be the concern of men” (1.358; see [[war-is-the-concern-of-men]]), and the suitors that he would “speak out my word to you without regard for anyone” (1.373). In the assembly he speaks it, and refuses the word they ask of him, to send his mother back to her father: “So I will never speak that word” (2.137; see [[mothers-and-sons]]). The people could press the suitors “with words” for repayment (2.77; see [[poine]]), but no one dares “to answer Telemachus with harsh words” except Antinous (2.82–84; see [[silence]]). He ends his last speech to the suitors claiming what he has gained, that he now learns by “hearing the words of others” (2.314; see [[nepios]]).

**Words of fate.** μυθέομαι is also the seer's verb. Halitherses surpasses his age “in speaking words of fate” (ἐναίσιμα μυθήσασθαι, 2.159; see [[aisa]]) and recalls what he foretold to Odysseus, “as I told him” (ἐμυθεόμην, 2.172); Eurymachus answers, “nor do we heed the oracle that you, old sir, / pronounce, never to be fulfilled” (μυθέαι ἀκράαντον, 2.201–202; see [[divination]] and [[halitherses]]).

**The secret word.** In the house the μῦθος is to be kept. Telemachus makes Eurycleia swear “to tell none of this to my dear mother” (2.373; see [[oath]]); Athena, in his likeness, goes through the city, and “standing beside each man she spoke her word” (2.384); and at the ship he tells the crew that his mother has learned nothing, “only one has heard the word” (2.411–412; see [[eurycleia]]).
