---
summary: In the storeroom of Odysseus jars of old wine, sweet to drink, stand in a row against the wall, kept in case he comes home (2.340–343).
---

The jars of the storeroom hold “old wine, sweet to drink”, and stand “fitted in order against the wall, in case Odysseus ever / came home” (2.340–343; see [[the-storeroom-of-odysseus]] and [[nostos]]). ἡδύποτος, “sweet to drink”, is said again of old wine at Pylos, where Nestor's housekeeper opens a wine in its eleventh year and he mixes a bowl of it to pour to Athena (3.390–394), and the wine in the jars is ἄκρητος, “unmixed”, as it was kept before it was mixed with water in the bowl (2.341; see [[wine]] and [[mixing-bowl]]). ἀρηρότες, “fitted”, is from ἀραρίσκω, the verb of the doors of the room, “closely fitted” (πυκινῶς ἀραρυῖαι, 2.344; see [[closely-fitted]]). Telemachus does not ask for this wine: he asks Eurycleia for “the sweetest after the one you are keeping”, and she draws it off for him into two-handled jars (2.349–350, 2.379; see [[two-handled-jar]] and [[eurycleia]]).
