summary: Telemachus prays to the “god who came yesterday to our house” (2.262), and goes home “once he had heard the voice of the goddess” (2.297); to his nurse he says that “this plan is not without a god” (2.372). He bids the people fear “the wrath of the gods” (2.66), and Antinous grants that the gods put Penelope's purpose in her breast (2.124–125).
grc: [θεοῦ]
---

θεός at 2.262 is a predicate: Telemachus prays to the stranger of the day before as a god, without a name, since he knew her for one when she left (1.323, 1.420; see the translation's note on 2.262 and [[seeing-the-gods]]). See also [[menis]] and [[not-without-a-god]].
