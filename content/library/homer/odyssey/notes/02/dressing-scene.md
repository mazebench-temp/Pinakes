**The pattern in the Odyssey's words.** The scene keeps the order of the entry in fewer verses, and in verses of the Odyssey. Telemachus rises from his bed (ὤρνυτʼ ἄρʼ ἐξ εὐνῆφιν, 2.2); puts on his clothes in a single half-verse, εἵματα ἑσσάμενος, where the Iliad names tunic and cloak one to a verse (2.3); sets his sword about his shoulder (2.3; [[sharp]]); binds on his sandals with the whole-line formula (2.4; [[sleek]], [[sandals]]); and goes out of his chamber, θεῷ ἐναλίγκιος ἄντην, “like a god to look upon” (2.5; [[like-a-god-to-look-upon]]). Merry notes that it was the custom to sit up and put on at least part of one's clothing before leaving the bed, and compares Agamemnon's dressing in *Iliad* 2 and 10 (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 2.3; *Iliad* 2.42, 10.21; see the translation's note on 2.3). The last item, the spear, comes when he has gathered the people: “in his hand he held the bronze spear, / not alone: with him went two swift dogs” (2.10–11; [[swift-dogs]], [[spear]]).

**The same verses elsewhere.** The five verses, from the dawn verse on, return when Menelaus rises at Sparta on the morning after Telemachus' arrival, with βοὴν ἀγαθὸς Μενέλαος in the place of Ὀδυσσῆος φίλος υἱός (4.306–310; [[dear-son-of-odysseus]]), and Nestor and Alcinous rise from their beds in the first of them (3.405, 8.2). Telemachus dresses again with the clothes, sword and sandal verses on the morning of the contest of the bow (20.124–126). Book 1 had ended with the scene reversed, as he took off his soft tunic in his chamber and lay planning the journey all night (1.436–444); Book 2 begins with him dressing and going out of the same chamber.
