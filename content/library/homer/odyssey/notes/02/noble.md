---
summary: “The noble suitors”: Telemachus addresses them so (2.209), and Leiocritus imagines Odysseus finding them feasting in his house (2.247); and Antinous speaks of Telemachus' “noble father” (2.308).
grc: [ἀγαυούς, ἀγαυοῦ]
---

In the Odyssey ἀγαυός is one of the suitors' epithets, μνηστῆρες ἀγαυοί, and Telemachus uses it as he turns from Eurymachus to all of them: “Eurymachus, and you others, all the noble suitors” (2.209). Leiocritus uses it of himself and his fellows when he says that Odysseus himself, if he came home, would meet a shameful doom fighting them (2.246–251; [[leiocritus-son-of-euenor]]). Antinous gives it to the absent father as he mocks the son's journey, “in search of news of your noble father” (2.308). See [[the-suitors]] and [[proud]].
