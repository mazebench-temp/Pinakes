summary: Antiphus went with Odysseus “to Ilios of the fine horses in the hollow ships” (2.18), and there has been no assembly “since brilliant Odysseus went away in the hollow ships” (2.27); the suitors imagine Telemachus “going on the hollow ship” and perishing far from his friends (2.332–333).
# κοίλης at 2.424 is the hollow mast-box (κοίλης ἔντοσθε μεσόδμης), not a ship.
except: [2.424]
---

The phrase κοίλῃς ἐνὶ νηυσί ends two verses of Aegyptius' speech, and both look back to the sailing of the fleet for Troy: his son's departure and the last assembly held in Ithaca (2.17–18, 2.26–27; [[aegyptius]]). The suitors' second jeer turns the epithet on the son: he too may go “on the hollow ship” and perish “wandering like Odysseus” (2.332–333; [[tis-speech]]). The κοίλης of 2.424 is the hollow box that holds the mast ([[mast-box]]). See [[ships]].
