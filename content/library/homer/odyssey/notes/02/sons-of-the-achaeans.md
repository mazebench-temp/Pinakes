---
summary: In Ithaca “the sons of the Achaeans” are the suitors: Antinous warns that Penelope may go on “vexing the sons of the Achaeans for a long time” (2.115), and Eurymachus that “the sons of the Achaeans will not cease / from their grievous wooing” (2.198–199).
---

The Iliad's periphrasis for the army before Troy is given to the young nobles who woo Penelope, and both times in their own mouths. Antinous uses it at the end of his account of the web, and Eurymachus after his threat to Halitherses (2.115–117, 2.197–199; [[the-suitors]], [[antinous]], [[eurymachus]]). See [[achaeans]].
