---
summary: The formula names the men of Ithaca summoned to the assembly, “the long-haired Achaeans” (2.7), as Athena had planned (1.90), and at the end of the book the crew, “their long-haired companions” (2.408).
---

The summons carries out the goddess' plan in the council of the gods, that Telemachus should call “the long-haired Achaeans to an assembly” (1.88–90; [[clear-voiced]], [[assembly-type-scene]]). The Iliad's formula for the host before Troy is given to the men of a single island. At night it goes with the crew that Athena has gathered for the voyage: “they found on the shore their long-haired companions” (κάρη κομόωντας ἑταίρους, 2.407–408; [[sailing-type-scene]]).
