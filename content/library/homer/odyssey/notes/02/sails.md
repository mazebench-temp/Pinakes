---
summary: The crew hoist the white sails “with well-twisted ox-hide ropes”, and the wind blows full into the sail (2.426–427).
---

ἕλκον δʼ ἱστία λευκὰ ἐυστρέπτοισι βοεῦσιν, “and hoisted the white sails with well-twisted ox-hide ropes” (2.426; see [[white]]). Where the Iliad's crew “spread the white sails” (*Iliad* 1.480), this crew haul them up with ropes of twisted ox-hide; βοεύς, “ox-hide rope”, and ἐΰστρεπτος, “well-twisted”, are found in Homer only in this verse and in its repetition when Telemachus sails from Pylos (15.291). Then “the wind blew full into the sail” (2.427), the West Wind that Athena has sent (2.420–421; see [[zephyrus]]), in the words of the Iliad's voyage home from Chryse (*Iliad* 1.481; see [[mast]] and [[cutwater]]).
