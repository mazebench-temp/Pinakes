---
summary: Telemachus asks the assembly for a swift ship and twenty companions (2.212); the suitors give him none, and Athena, as Mentor and as Telemachus, finds the ship, borrows it from Noemon, launches it at nightfall and sails in it with him (2.287–295, 2.386–434).
---

**A ship and twenty companions.** Telemachus asks the assembly: “But come, give me a swift ship and twenty companions, / who will accomplish the way for me there and back” (2.212–213), as Athena had told him to “fit out with twenty oarsmen the best ship there is” (1.280; see [[swift-ships]]). In Book 4 Antinous asks for a ship to lie in wait for him in the same verse (4.669 = 2.212). Antinous mocks the request: the Achaeans will see to “a ship and choice oarsmen” (2.307). Telemachus answers that he will go all the same, as ἔμπορος, “a passenger”, one who sails in another man's ship, “for I have no ship of my own, nor oarsmen: / that, I suppose, is what seemed to you the better way” (2.319–320; see the translation's table of key words). The young men add that he may perish “going on the hollow ship” far from his friends, like his father (2.332–333; see [[hollow-ships]]).

**Athena's ship.** In the likeness of Mentor the goddess promises to “fit out a swift ship for you and go with you myself” (2.287): of the many ships of Ithaca, new and old, she will pick the best, “and we will quickly make her ready and launch her on the wide sea” (2.292–295; see [[ithaca]]). In the likeness of Telemachus she asks Noemon for a swift ship (2.386–387; see [[noemon-son-of-phronius]]), drags it down to the sea at sunset, puts in “all the tackle that well-benched ships carry”, and moors it at the far edge of the harbor (2.389–391; see [[well-benched]] and [[harbor]]).

**The voyage.** The companions bring the provisions “to the well-benched ship” and stow them (2.414–415; see [[provisions]]). Athena goes on board first and sits in the stern, with Telemachus beside her (2.416–418; see [[stern]]); the crew loose the stern-cables and sit down at the benches (2.418–419; see [[stern-cables]] and [[rowing-benches]]); they set up the mast in its box and hoist the sails (2.424–426; see [[mast]], [[mast-box]], [[forestays]] and [[sails]]); and the wave roars about the cutwater as the ship runs on through the night (2.427–434; see [[cutwater]], [[black-ships]] and [[sailing-type-scene]]).
