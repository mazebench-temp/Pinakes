**The last of the three.** In Antinous' complaint what keeps the suitors waiting is Penelope's mind: Athena has given her skill in beautiful handiwork, good wits and crafty ways beyond any woman of the old stories (2.115–122; see [[penelope]] and [[kerdos]]). The women of old are “the Achaean women with lovely tresses” (2.119; see [[achaean-women]] and [[with-lovely-tresses]]), and Mycene is the last of the three he names. Homer names her only here, and she is not among the heroines whom Odysseus sees in the underworld, where Tyro and Alcmene are (11.235, 11.266; see [[tyro]] and [[alcmene]]). Her epithet, ἐυστέφανος, “well-crowned”, is in the Iliad the epithet of a city, Thebes, and of the goddess Artemis (*Iliad* 19.99, 21.511; see [[well-crowned]]), and her name is the singular name of Mycenae, whose eponym later tradition made her (see [[mycenae]]).
