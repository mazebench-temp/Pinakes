---
summary: Telemachus will “call upon the gods who live forever” against the suitors (2.143), as he warned them the day before; and at sea the crew pour libations “to the immortal, everlasting gods” (2.432).
grc: ["θεοῖς αἰειγενέτῃσιν"]
en: [everlasting gods]
---

The verses 2.141–145 repeat Telemachus' warning of 1.376–380: if the suitors think it better to destroy one man's substance without payment, he will call on the gods who live forever, and they would perish unavenged in his house (see the translation's note on 2.141; [[poine]]). Zeus answers at once with two eagles (2.146; [[far-seeing]]). At the end of the book the other formula of the gods' permanence closes the libation, λεῖβον δʼ ἀθανάτοισι θεοῖς αἰειγενέτῃσιν, as the armies pray to the everlasting gods over the wine of the oath in *Iliad* 3 (2.432; *Iliad* 3.295–296; [[immortals]], [[libation]]).
