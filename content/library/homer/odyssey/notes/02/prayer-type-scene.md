---
summary: Telemachus' prayer on the shore follows the steps of the pattern but leaves two of them out: he goes apart and washes his hands in the gray sea (2.260–261), calls on an unnamed god with “Hear me” (2.262), recalls the god's command instead of his own offerings, and asks nothing (2.263–266); the god answers by coming to him in the likeness of Mentor (2.267–268).
refs: [2.260-268]
passages:
  - 2.260-268 | Telemachus prays on the shore
---

1. **Setting and gesture.** Τηλέμαχος δʼ ἀπάνευθε κιὼν ἐπὶ θῖνα θαλάσσης, “But Telemachus went apart along the shore of the sea” (2.260): ἀπάνευθε κιών are the words of Chryses' first prayer in the *Iliad*, made by the shore of the sea (*Iliad* 1.34–35). He washes his hands in the sea itself (2.261; [[gray-sea]], [[hand-washing]]).
2. **Invocation.** κλῦθί μευ, without a name: “Hear me, you god who came yesterday to our house” (2.262; [[hear-me]]).
3. **Claim.** In place of a service done, the god's own command: “and bade me go in a ship over the misty sea / to learn of the homecoming of my father, who has long been gone” (2.263–264; [[misty]], [[to-learn-of-his-fathers-homecoming]]).
4. **Request.** None is spoken. The prayer ends with a complaint, that the Achaeans and “most of all the suitors, wickedly overweening” are delaying it all (2.265–266; [[overweening]]).
5. **Answer.** The closing verse begins as in the Iliad, ὣς ἔφατʼ εὐχόμενος, and ends with the goddess' coming instead of her hearing: “and Athena came near to him / in the likeness of Mentor, both in form and in voice” (2.267–268; [[in-the-likeness-of-mentor]]).
