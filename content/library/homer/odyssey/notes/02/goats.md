Penelope says the same five verses to Eumaeus (17.534–538 = 2.55–59). πίονας αἶγας, “fat goats”, come back with the suitors' feasts in the later books: the goatherd Melanthius drives in the best goats of all the flocks for their meal, and they slaughter “great sheep and fat goats” (17.180, 17.212–214, 20.173–175, 20.250, plain renderings). ἱερεύω, “sacrifice”, is also the word for slaughtering beasts for a meal (see [[sacrifice]] and [[feast]]).

**Flaying goats.** Telemachus comes home from his prayer by the sea and “found the proud suitors in the halls, / flaying goats and singeing fat hogs in the courtyard” (2.299–300; see [[boar]]). ἀνιεμένους, “flaying”, is the middle of ἀνίημι, “let go, loosen”: they loosen the hides from the carcasses. The suitors do the work themselves, as the heroes of the Iliad do. Achilles laid “the back of a sheep and of a fat goat, / and the chine of a fat hog” on the chopping block for the envoys, and carved them himself (*Iliad* 9.206–209; see [[feast-type-scene]]): the same three beasts, sheep, goat and hog, that the suitors are eating in Odysseus' house.
