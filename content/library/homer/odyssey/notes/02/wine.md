**The suitors' wine.** The suitors haunt the house, “sacrificing oxen and sheep and fat goats, / and hold banquets and drink the sparkling wine, / wantonly” (2.56–58; see [[the-suitors]] and [[feast]]). Antinous bids Telemachus “eat and drink with me, just as before” (2.305), and that night Athena makes the cups fall from their hands as they drink (2.396; see [[cup]]).

**The old wine.** In the storeroom “jars of old wine, sweet to drink, / stood holding within them the unmixed, divine drink”, kept “in case Odysseus ever / came home” (2.340–343; see [[storage-jar]] and [[the-storeroom-of-odysseus]]). θεῖον ποτόν is said again of the wine of Maron, the priest of Apollo at Ismarus, the sweet unmixed wine that Odysseus took to the Cyclops' cave (9.203–205; see [[divine]]). Telemachus asks his nurse for “sweet wine, the sweetest after the one you are keeping, / thinking of that ill-fated man”, in twelve two-handled jars (2.349–353; see [[two-handled-jar]] and [[eurycleia]]), with the barley meal that goes with wine in Athena's orders (2.290; see [[barley-meal]] and [[provisions]]).

**At sea.** When the ship is under way, “they set up mixing bowls brim-full of wine, / and poured libations to the immortal, everlasting gods, / and most of all to the gray-eyed daughter of Zeus” (2.431–433; see [[libation]], [[mixing-bowl]] and [[brim-full]]).
