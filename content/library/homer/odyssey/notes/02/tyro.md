---
summary: Antinous names Tyro first of the Achaean women of old whose crafty ways could not match Penelope's: “Tyro and Alcmene and well-crowned Mycene” (2.120).
passages:
  - 2.115-122 | The women of old
---

**First of the women of old.** In Antinous' complaint what keeps the suitors waiting is Penelope's mind. Athena has given her skill in beautiful handiwork, good wits and crafty ways, such as the suitors “have never heard of in any woman, not even of old”, among “the Achaean women with lovely tresses” of the past (2.116–119; see [[penelope]], [[kerdos]], [[achaean-women]] and [[with-lovely-tresses]]). He names three, “Tyro and Alcmene and well-crowned Mycene”, and Tyro first (2.120; see [[alcmene]] and [[mycene]]). They are women known from the old stories, and he does not say what they were known for.

**In the underworld.** Tyro is also the first of the heroines whom Odysseus sees among the dead, ἔνθʼ ἦ τοι πρώτην Τυρὼ ἴδον εὐπατέρειαν, “There first I saw Tyro, daughter of a noble father” (11.235), and her story is told there: the daughter of Salmoneus and wife of Cretheus loved the river Enipeus, and Poseidon lay with her in the river's likeness and told her that she would bear glorious children; she bore Pelias and Neleus (11.235–257; see [[pelias]] and [[neleus]]). The deceit in her story is the god's. Alcmene comes after her in that list too, with Antiope between them (11.260–268); Mycene is not among them. The renderings of other books are plain renderings, not the translation.
