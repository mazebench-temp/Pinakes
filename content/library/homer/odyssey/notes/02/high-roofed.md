---
summary: Telemachus goes down “into the high-roofed chamber of his father, / a wide one, where gold and bronze lay heaped” (2.337–338).
---

The storeroom is a θάλαμος, as the entry describes, like Priam's high-roofed storeroom, into which he goes down for the ransom of Hector (*Iliad* 24.191–192). Here it holds gold and bronze, clothing, oil and the old wine kept for Odysseus' return, behind locked doors and in Eurycleia's care (2.337–347; [[the-storeroom-of-odysseus]], [[closely-fitted]], [[eurycleia]]).
