summary: In the assembly every speaker lays the blame on someone else. Telemachus blames the suitors and the people who let them be (2.63–79); Antinous answers, “it is not the Achaean suitors who are to blame toward you: / it is your dear mother” (2.87–88); Eurymachus blames the seer (2.178–193), and Mentor the silent people (2.239–241).
refs: [2.70-79, 2.85-88, 2.178-193, 2.239-241]
---

**The charges.** Telemachus asks the people to hold back, unless his father did them some harm that they are avenging on his son (2.70–74; see [[poine]]). Antinous lays the blame on Penelope and her web (2.85–110; see [[aitios]] and [[dolos]]), and grants in passing that the gods put her purpose in her breast (2.124–125). Telemachus will not drive her out, for fear of her father, her Erinyes and the blame of men (2.130–137; see [[nemesis]]). Eurymachus charges Halitherses with setting Telemachus on for a gift (2.178–193; see [[doron]]), and Mentor turns on the people, who sit silent though they are many (2.239–241; see [[silence]]).

**The answer.** The poem gives its verdict in the last assembly, through Halitherses: ὑμετέρῃ κακότητι, φίλοι, τάδε ἔργα γένοντο, it was through their own wickedness that these things came about, since they would not obey him or Mentor and restrain their sons (24.454–462; see [[assembly]] and [[halitherses]]). It is the answer Zeus gave in the council of Book 1, that mortals suffer beyond their share by their own recklessness (1.32–34).
