---
summary: Athena, in the likeness of Mentor, “spoke to him, addressing him with winged words” (2.269); and Eurycleia “lamenting addressed him with winged words” (2.362).
---

Both speeches answer a moment of feeling: the goddess' comes at once upon Telemachus' prayer, the nurse's upon his order to fill the jars, when she has “wailed aloud” (2.267–269, 2.361–362; [[eurycleia]]). The first is the whole-line formula, καί μιν φωνήσασʼ ἔπεα πτερόεντα προσηύδα; the second puts the participle of lament in the place of φωνήσασα, καί ῥʼ ὀλοφυρομένη. See [[athena]].
