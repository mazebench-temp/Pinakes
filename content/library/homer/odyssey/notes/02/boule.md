summary: Athena, in the likeness of Mentor, bids Telemachus “let be the plan and the thought of the suitors” (2.281), and he tells his nurse, “this plan is not without a god” (2.372). Aegyptius' “an assembly of ours or a session” (2.26) names the council beside the gathering of the people.

**Plan against plan.** βουλή and [[noos|νόος]] go together for the suitors' designs, which Athena tells Telemachus to disregard: they are “senseless men, since they are in no way discerning or just”, and know nothing of the death that is near them (2.281–284; see [[ker-doom]]). Odysseus claims the same pair for himself when he reminds his men that they escaped from the Cyclops' cave ἐμῇ ἀρετῇ, βουλῇ τε νόῳ τε, by his excellence, his counsel and his mind (12.209–212). Telemachus' own plan, the voyage, has a god behind it (2.372; see [[not-without-a-god]]), and Athena has already promised him the ship (2.285–295).

**The council.** No assembly has met since Odysseus sailed, “an assembly of ours or a session” (ἀγορὴ … οὔτε θόωκος, 2.26). Merry explains that the council of kings and chiefs was called βουλή or θῶκος, the chiefs (γέροντες) being the recognized heads of the noblest families, whether aged or not, while the people attended the ἀγορή and expressed their assent or dissent; he compares the council that Agamemnon holds before the assembly of the army (*Iliad* 2.53, 2.93), and Aristotle's account of Crete, where all share in the assembly, but it has authority only to ratify what the elders and the magistrates have resolved (*Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 2.7; Aristotle, *Politics* 2.10, 1272a10–12). θόωκος he takes as the βουλὴ γερόντων, comparing Nestor's pair of assembly and council, εἰν ἀγορῇ … οὔτʼ ἐνὶ βουλῇ (3.127; Merry on 2.26). See the translation's note on 2.7, 2.26, and [[elders]] and [[assembly]].
