---
summary: Without a man like Odysseus the house has no defense: “we shall be wretched, and unskilled in courage” (2.61).
---

ἀλκή here is the strength to ward off, and the verb ἀμύνω, “ward off”, comes three times in four lines (2.59–62): no man is left “such as Odysseus was, to ward off disaster from the house”, and Telemachus would do it himself, “if I had the strength”. οὐ δεδαηκότες ἀλκήν makes courage a thing that must be learned (see [[nepios]]); Athena later promises that he will be “no coward” (2.270; see [[fathers-and-sons]]).
