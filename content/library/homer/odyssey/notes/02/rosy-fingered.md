---
summary: “The rosy-fingered” closes the verse of the poem's first dawn (2.1).
---

Merry suggests that the epithet, if it is more than a part of the description of Dawn's feminine beauty, may point to the spreading rays of rosy light, like the fingers of an open hand, that are often seen just before sunrise; he quotes Eustathius, for whom Dawn's fingers are, by allegory, the rays of the sun (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 2.1; see the translation's note on 2.1).
