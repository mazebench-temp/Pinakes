---
summary: Telemachus' verse of reply, “And wise Telemachus answered him face to face,” four times (2.129, 2.208, 2.309, 2.371); and the herald Peisenor, “who knew wise counsels” (2.38).
grc: ["πεπνυμένα μήδεα"]
---

In the assembly the verse brings in Telemachus' answers to Antinous and Eurymachus (2.129, 2.208), in the house his refusal of Antinous' invitation (2.309), and in the storeroom his answer to Eurycleia's lament, “Take heart, nurse, since this plan is not without a god” (2.371–372; [[answering-him]]). The herald who puts the scepter in his hand has the verse of the Trojan herald Idaeus, with his own name, κῆρυξ Πεισήνωρ πεπνυμένα μήδεα εἰδώς (2.38): in the *Iliad* it is κῆρυξ Ἰδαῖος πεπνυμένα μήδεα εἰδώς, “by the herald Idaeus, who knew wise counsels” (*Iliad* 7.278; [[peisenor-the-herald]], [[heralds]]). Merry notes that the scepter was the badge of public office, carried by kings, priests, prophets, heralds and judges, and that the herald who put it in a speaker's hand invested him with that office for the time (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 2.37; [[scepter]]). See [[telemachus]].
