---
summary: The poem's first dawn opens Book 2 with the whole verse, “And when early-born Dawn appeared, the rosy-fingered” (2.1), and the next verse gives the first act of the day, “the dear son of Odysseus rose from his bed” (2.2).
---

Book 1 ended at night, with Telemachus planning in his mind the journey that Athena had shown him (1.443–444). The verse of daybreak brings the morning of the assembly he had announced to the suitors (1.372). The Odyssey uses the whole verse twenty times, and this is the first; the Iliad has it only for its first dawn and its last (*Iliad* 1.477, 24.788). As the entry describes, the next verse begins the day's action, here the dressing scene of the young man going to the assembly (2.2–5; [[dressing-scene]], [[dear-son-of-odysseus]]). The same five verses, with Menelaus in Telemachus' place, open the morning at Sparta (4.306–310). See [[early-born]], [[rosy-fingered]] and [[eos]].
