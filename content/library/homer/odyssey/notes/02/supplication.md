---
summary: Telemachus begs the people “by Olympian Zeus and by Themis” (2.68), and after the omen he will no longer beg the suitors (2.210).
---

λίσσομαι takes the gods by whom one begs in the genitive: “I beg you by Olympian Zeus and by Themis, / who dissolves the assemblies of men and seats them” (2.68–69; see [[themis-the-goddess]] and [[zeus]]). To the suitors he gives it up: “I will no longer beg you or speak to you about these things” (2.210), and asks for a ship instead (see [[the-journey-of-telemachus]]).
