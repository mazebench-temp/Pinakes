“For I will take it in the evening, when / my mother has gone up to her upper room and is thinking of bed” (2.357–358; see [[provisions]] and [[eurycleia]]). The upper room is where Penelope goes at night, as at the end of the first book, where she goes up to it with her handmaids and weeps for Odysseus until Athena casts sleep on her eyelids (1.362–364; see [[penelope]]). Telemachus' plan rests on it: the stores are to be taken while she is upstairs, and she is not to learn of his going until the eleventh or twelfth day (2.373–376). That night he tells the crew, “My mother has learned nothing of it” (2.411).
