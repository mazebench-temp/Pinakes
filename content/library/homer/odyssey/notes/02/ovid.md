---
summary: Ovid's Penelope, writing to the absent Ulysses, takes up the cares of this book: the suitors devouring the house, the father who chides her delays, the old Laertes, Telemachus' journey to Pylos, and the strength she lacks to drive the enemies out, in Telemachus' own words to the assembly.
---

- Penelope's letter to Ulysses: the suitors, Icarius, Laertes and the boy Telemachus, almost killed in an ambush “while he made ready to go to Pylos against the will of all”, and “Nor have I the strength to drive the enemies from the house” (*Heroides* 1.81–110, on 2.52–54, 2.60–62, 2.99–100, 2.113–114, 2.373–376): [[ovid-penelopes-letter]].
- The hanging web that wearies her widowed hands as she seeks to beguile the long night (*Heroides* 1.9–10), where Homer's Penelope unraveled her web by night (2.104–105): [[penelopes-web-in-later-writers]].
