summary: Telemachus and Penelope again. The suitors demand that the son send his mother back to her father; he answers that he cannot thrust out the woman who bore him and raised him, for she would call down the Erinyes on him. Athena measures him as the son of both his parents; he keeps his voyage from his mother so that she may not weep, and the old nurse who raised him laments him as an only and beloved son.

**The woman who bore me.** The suitors' answer is that the son should send his mother away to marry (2.113–114, 2.195; see [[the-suitors]]). He answers Antinous:

{{quote:ca492944-4834-5cd6-b956-bc623f1efd1c}}

ἥ μʼ ἔτεχʼ, ἥ μʼ ἔθρεψε, “who bore me, who raised me” (2.131): he will give her to a husband if his father is dead, as Athena told him (2.223, as 1.292), but he will not drive her out. A mother's curse is a power in both poems. In the Iliad Althaea prays to the gods below for her son's death, “and the Erinys who walks in darkness / heard her out of Erebus” (*Iliad* 9.566–572; see [[althaea]] and [[meleager]]), and Athena taunts Ares, “So you may pay in full for the Erinyes of your mother” (*Iliad* 21.412); in the Odyssey the Erinyes of a mother fulfill the woes of Oedipus (11.279–280). See [[erinyes]] and [[nemesis]].

**His parents' son.** Athena, in the likeness of Mentor, names both parents: “But if you are not his offspring and Penelope's, / then I do not expect you to accomplish what you have in mind” (2.274–275), and goes on to the saying that few sons are like their father (2.276–277; see [[few-sons-are-like-their-father]], [[fathers-and-sons]] and [[penelope]]).

**Spared her tears.** He makes Eurycleia swear not to tell his mother of the voyage until the eleventh or twelfth day, or until she misses him, “so that she may not mar her fair skin with weeping” (2.373–376), and on the shore he tells the crew, “My mother has learned nothing of it” (2.411).

**The nurse.** The woman who weeps for him is the old nurse who reared him (1.435), φίλη τροφός, “the dear nurse” (2.361), whom he calls μαῖα, “nurse” (2.349, 2.372): “Why, dear child, has this thought come / into your mind?”, she asks him, “you, an only and beloved son” (2.363–365; see [[eurycleia]]). He is the only son of an only son (16.117–120; see [[telemachus]]).
