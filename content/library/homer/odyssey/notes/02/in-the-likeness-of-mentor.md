---
summary: Athena comes to Telemachus “in the likeness of Mentor, both in form and in voice,” after his prayer on the shore (2.268) and at night to call him to the ship (2.401); between the two she goes through the city in the likeness of Telemachus himself (2.383).
---

The disguise follows Mentor's own speech in the assembly and Leiocritus' scorn that Mentor and Halitherses, “who are his father's companions from of old,” would speed Telemachus on his way (2.253–254). The goddess makes the scorn come true in Mentor's shape: she promises to fit out a swift ship and to go with him herself, “such a companion of your father's am I to you” (2.286–287), and at night she leads him to it (2.399–406; [[followed-in-the-footprints]]). Between the two appearances she takes the likeness of Telemachus to gather the crew and borrow the ship (2.382–387; [[thought-of-another-thing]]). See [[athena]] and [[mentor]].
