---
summary: The verse vouches for the two men who speak for the house of Odysseus in the assembly: Halitherses, the reader of birds (2.160), and Mentor, Odysseus' companion (2.228).
grc: ["ἐὺ φρονέων ἀγορήσατο καὶ μετέειπε"]
---

Each time, as the entry describes, the verse follows the speaker's credentials: Halitherses alone surpassed the men of his age “in knowing birds and in speaking words of fate” (2.158–159), and Mentor was the companion to whom Odysseus entrusted all his house when he sailed (2.225–227). Both speak against the suitors, and both are answered with contempt, Halitherses by Eurymachus and Mentor by Leiocritus (2.177–207, 2.242–256). In the assembly of the last book Halitherses is brought in again by the same verses, the second of them changed, and with ἐϋφρονέων written as one word (24.451–453).
