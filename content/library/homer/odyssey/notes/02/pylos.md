---
summary: Telemachus names Sparta and sandy Pylos as the goals of his journey, in the verse of Athena's plan (2.214, 2.359); Antinous mockingly promises him a ship to “sacred Pylos” (2.308), and the young suitors jeer that he will bring helpers from sandy Pylos (2.326).
---

**The goal.** Telemachus asks the assembly for a swift ship and twenty companions: “For I will go to Sparta and to sandy Pylos / to learn of the homecoming of my father, who has long been gone” (2.214–215; see [[sparta]] and [[sandy]]). The first verse is the verse of Athena's plan in the council, with εἶμι, “I will go”, for πέμψω, “I will send” (1.93), and he says it again to Eurycleia when he asks her for the provisions (2.359). Athena's counsel had put Pylos first, “First go to Pylos and ask brilliant Nestor” (1.284; see [[nestor]]), and the ship that sails at the end of the book reaches Pylos at sunrise in the next (3.1–5).

**Sacred Pylos.** Antinous laughs and takes his hand: let him eat and drink as before, and the Achaeans will see to “a ship and choice oarsmen, so that you may come the sooner / to sacred Pylos, in search of news of your noble father” (2.307–308). ἠγάθεος, “sacred”, is the epithet of Pylos in the first book of the Iliad, where Nestor ruled “in sacred Pylos” (*Iliad* 1.252; see [[egatheos]]). Telemachus refuses: he will try to let loose the evil fates on them, “whether I go to Pylos or stay here in this land” (2.316–317). Πύλονδε, “to Pylos”, is the form of *Iliad* 11.760.

**Helpers from Pylos.** One of the young men jeers that Telemachus is pondering slaughter for them: “Either he will bring some helpers from sandy Pylos, / or even from Sparta” (2.326–327), or he will fetch drugs from Ephyra (2.328–330; see [[ephyra]] and [[odysseus-seeks-poison]]).
