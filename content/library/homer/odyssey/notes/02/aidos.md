---
summary: Telemachus asks the men of Ithaca to “feel shame before others, the men who dwell around, / who live about us” (2.65–66), between their own indignation and the wrath of the gods.
grc: [αἰδέσθητε]
en: [feel shame]
---

The aorist imperative αἰδέσθητε is the middle term of three: indignation of their own (νεμεσσήθητε, 2.64), shame before the neighboring peoples (2.65), and fear of the gods (θεῶν δʼ ὑποδείσατε μῆνιν, 2.66; see [[nemesis]] and [[menis]]). The περικτίονες are the people who live round about, whose regard the island would lose if it let the house of its king be destroyed (2.63–64). No one answers the appeal but Antinous, who blames Telemachus' mother (2.84–88; see [[aitios]]).
