---
summary: “And all the people gazed at him as he came” (2.13): the λαοί of Ithaca gather at Telemachus' summons (2.41), are seized with pity at his tears (2.81), and are sent home by a suitor, “But come, people, scatter, each to his work” (2.252). Mentor complains that no one remembers Odysseus “among the people he was lord over” (2.234).
---

In this book λαός is not an army but the people of the island in assembly. They gaze at Telemachus as he comes, with the grace Athena has poured on him (2.12–13; see [[charis]]), the two verses that return when he goes to the place of assembly in Book 17 (17.63–64). He tells Aegyptius that it was he who gathered them (2.40–41; see [[assembly]]). When he flings down the scepter, “pity seized all the people” (2.81; see [[pity]]), but “all the others were silent” (2.82; see [[silence]]), and Mentor blames them for it (2.239–241; see [[demos]] and [[nemesis]]). Their king was a father to them, “and he was kind, like a father” (2.233–234; see [[basileus]] and [[kind-as-a-father]]); now Leiocritus, one of the suitors, dismisses them to their work (2.252; see [[leiocritus-son-of-euenor]]). In the assembly that follows the killing of the suitors, Halitherses reminds the people that they would not obey him, nor Mentor, “shepherd of the people” (Μέντορι ποιμένι λαῶν), when the two of them urged them to restrain their sons (24.454–457).
