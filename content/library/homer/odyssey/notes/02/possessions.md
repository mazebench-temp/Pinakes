---
summary: The possessions of Odysseus' house are what the suitors eat up (2.123, 2.312–313) and would divide (2.335), and what Telemachus bids them spare by eating their own (2.140).
---

“For so long will they eat up your substance and your possessions” (2.123), Antinous tells Telemachus; Telemachus answers with the verses of the first book, bidding them feast “eating your own possessions, taking turns from house to house” (2.140, as 1.375). He reproaches them that “in the past you ate up many fine / possessions of mine, you suitors, while I was still a child” (2.312–313), and the young men jest that if he perishes at sea “we would divide all his possessions” (2.335; see [[the-suitors]] and [[telemachus-and-the-suitors]]).
