---
summary: Odysseus as king of Ithaca “was kind, like a father”: Telemachus says it of the father he has lost (2.47), and Mentor of the king whom no one remembers (2.234).
grc: ["πατὴρ δʼ ὣς ἤπιος"]
en: [was kind, like a father]
---

The half-verse closes a description of the king in both speeches. Telemachus has lost his brave father, “who once among you / here was king, and was kind, like a father” (2.46–47; [[brave]]). Mentor wishes that no scepter-bearing king may ever again be gentle and kind, since no one remembers divine Odysseus “among the people he was lord over, and he was kind, like a father” (2.230–234; [[scepter-bearing]], [[mentor]]). The Greek has δʼ between πατήρ and ὡς, πατὴρ δʼ ὣς ἤπιος ἦεν, where Helen's words for Priam have πατὴρ ὣς ἤπιος αἰεί (*Iliad* 24.770). See [[kings]] and [[odysseus]].
