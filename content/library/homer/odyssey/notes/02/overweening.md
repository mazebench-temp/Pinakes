---
summary: Telemachus' prayer ends with the suitors “wickedly overweening” (2.266), and their jeers come in as what “one of the overweening young men would say” (2.324, 2.331).
grc: [ὑπερηνορέοντες]
---

The word is first Telemachus' own, in his prayer on the shore: all this the Achaeans are delaying, “and most of all the suitors, wickedly overweening” (2.265–266; [[prayer-type-scene]]). Then the narrator uses it in the two verses that bring in the suitors' jeers, ὧδε δέ τις εἴπεσκε νέων ὑπερηνορεόντων and ἄλλος δʼ αὖτʼ εἴπεσκε νέων ὑπερηνορεόντων (2.324, 2.331; [[tis-speech]]), verses that come again when the suitors mock in later books (4.769, 17.482, 20.375, 21.361, 21.401). See [[the-suitors]].
