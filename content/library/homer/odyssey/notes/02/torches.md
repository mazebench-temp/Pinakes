---
summary: Penelope unraveled her web by night, “when she had set torches beside her” (2.105).
---

“Then by day she would weave the great web, / but by night she would unravel it, when she had set torches beside her” (2.104–105; see [[the-web-of-penelope]] and [[weaving]]). The torches light the night's work of undoing, as in the first book they lit Eurycleia and Telemachus to his chamber (1.428, 1.434; see [[eurycleia]]). The optative παραθεῖτο after ἐπεί marks a thing done again and again, “whenever she had set them beside her”, with the iteratives ὑφαίνεσκεν and ἀλλύεσκεν. The verse returns in Penelope's own telling, in the first person, “whenever I had set torches beside me”, and in the shade of Amphimedon's (19.150, 24.140; the rendering is a plain one).
