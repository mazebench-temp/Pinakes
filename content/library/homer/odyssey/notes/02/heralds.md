**The summons.** The three verses are the Iliad's. With them Agamemnon has the heralds call the army to the assembly after his dream (*Iliad* 2.50–52, where the second verse has ἀγορὴν δέ for ἀγορήνδε), and again to war when the assembly is over (*Iliad* 2.442–444, with αὐτίκα for αἶψα and πόλεμον δέ, “to war”; see [[clear-voiced]] and [[long-haired]]). Telemachus gives the order in Agamemnon's words, and it calls the first assembly held in Ithaca since Odysseus sailed (2.25–27; see [[telemachus]], [[assembly]] and [[the-assembly-of-the-ithacans]]). W. W. Merry distinguishes the council of the chiefs, the βουλή or θῶκος, from the ἀγορή, the gathering of the people, who expressed their assent or dissent on the measures of the council (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 2.7; see [[boule]] and the translation's note on 2.7).

**The scepter.** When Telemachus stands up in the middle of the assembly, “the scepter was put in his hand / by the herald Peisenor, who knew wise counsels” (2.37–38; see [[peisenor-the-herald]] and [[scepter]]), as a herald puts it into the hand of Menelaus at the funeral games of Patroclus (*Iliad* 23.567–568). By placing the scepter in the speaker's hand, Merry explains, the herald invested him for the time being with a public office (Merry on 2.37).
