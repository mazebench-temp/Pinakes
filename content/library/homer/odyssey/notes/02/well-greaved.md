---
summary: Telemachus speaks of “the well-greaved Achaeans” whom his father might have harmed (2.72), and Athena, in Mentor's likeness, of his “well-greaved companions” waiting at the oars (2.402).
grc: [ἐυκνήμιδας]
---

The Iliad's commonest epithet of the army is used twice in the book, once of the Achaeans of Ithaca and once of the crew of one ship. Telemachus supposes that the suitors may be taking vengeance on him for some harm his father did “in ill will” to the well-greaved Achaeans (2.71–73). At night the goddess tells him that his “well-greaved companions / sit at the oars, waiting for you to set out” (2.402–403; [[in-the-likeness-of-mentor]], [[sailing-type-scene]]). See [[achaeans]].
