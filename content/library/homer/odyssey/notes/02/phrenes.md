summary: Athena gave Penelope “good wits” with her skill at the loom and her crafty ways (2.117), and Leiocritus calls Mentor “crazed in your mind” (2.243). Penelope “pondered in her mind this further trick” (2.93), and Eurycleia asks why “this thought” has come into the boy's mind (2.363).

**Good wits and crazed wits.** φρένας ἐσθλάς, “good wits” (2.117), are one of Athena's gifts to Penelope (see [[kerdos]]), and the shade of Agamemnon praises them at the end of the poem: ὡς ἀγαθαὶ φρένες ἦσαν ἀμύμονι Πηνελοπείῃ (24.194; see [[penelope]]). Leiocritus' φρένας ἠλεέ (2.243) is Athena's insult to Ares in the Iliad, “Madman, crazed in your mind” (φρένας ἠλέ, *Iliad* 15.128; see [[leiocritus-son-of-euenor]]). Halitherses and Mentor speak “With good intent” (ἐὺ φρονέων, 2.160, 2.228; see [[with-good-intent]]).
