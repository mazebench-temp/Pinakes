summary: After Telemachus' speech “all the others were silent, and no one dared / to answer Telemachus with harsh words” (2.82–83), and Mentor blames the people because “you all / sit silent” (2.239–240). Telemachus tells the suitors that he cannot “feast in silence” among them (2.311).

**The silent people.** Silence is the people's answer in the assembly. They pity Telemachus and say nothing, and the only man who answers him is a suitor (2.81–84; see [[pity]]). Mentor ends the debate by naming it: “you all / sit silent, and do not approach them with words / and stop the suitors, few as they are, though you are many” (2.239–241; see [[nemesis]] and [[epos]]). Again no one of the people answers, and a suitor dissolves the assembly (2.242–257; see [[assembly]]). The half-line ἔνθʼ ἄλλοι μὲν πάντες ἀκὴν ἔσαν returns of the Achaeans hidden in the wooden horse (4.285).

**A silent feast.** Antinous invites Telemachus to eat and drink with them as before (2.303–305); he answers that there is no way for him among such men “to feast in silence and make merry at my ease” (2.310–311; see [[telemachus-and-the-suitors]]).
