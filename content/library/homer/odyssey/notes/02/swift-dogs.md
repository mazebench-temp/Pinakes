---
summary: Telemachus goes to the assembly “not alone: with him went two swift dogs” (2.11).
grc: ["κύνες ἀργοὶ"]
---

The verse comes again, with ἔγχος ἔχων, “holding his spear,” at its head, when Telemachus goes out to the place of assembly in Books 17 and 20 (17.62, 20.145). Merry holds that ἀργός refers to swiftness and not to color, against the older interpreters who rendered it “white” (Eustathius' λευκόποδες), and compares the name of the storm-swift Harpy Podarge (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 2.11; *Iliad* 16.150; see the translation's note on 2.11). He reports the variant κύνες πόδας ἀργοί. See [[dogs]].
