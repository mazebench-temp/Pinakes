---
summary: Telemachus sits in his father's seat (2.14) and mourns “my brave father, who once among you / here was king” (2.46–47), and the old Aegyptius, who opens the assembly, mourns his lost son (2.17–24). Athena, in Mentor's likeness, measures the son against the father: “Few sons are like their father; / most are worse, and few are better than their father” (2.276–277).

**The father's seat.** Telemachus takes his place in the assembly “in his father's seat” (2.14; see [[elders]]) and begins his complaint with the father he has lost, a king who “was kind, like a father” (2.46–47; see [[basileus]]). If the people are wronging him, he says, it can only be in vengeance on the son for some harm the father did them (2.71–74; see [[poine]]).

**A father's grief.** The first speaker is a father who mourns. Aegyptius' son Antiphus sailed with Odysseus and was eaten by the Cyclops; of his three other sons one, Eurynomus, keeps company with the suitors, and two work their father's fields; “But even so he did not forget that one, mourning and grieving” (2.17–24; see [[aegyptius]]). In the poem's last assembly another father speaks first, shedding tears for his son, Eupeithes for Antinous (24.422–425; see [[assembly]]).

**Like his father.** Athena makes the son's journey depend on the father: if “your father's noble might has been instilled in you”, it will not be in vain; “if you are not his offspring and Penelope's”, she does not expect it (2.271–275; see [[menos]]). Between the two promises, that he will be no coward and no fool and that his father's counsel has not failed him (2.270, 2.278–279; see [[noos]] and [[metis]]), she states the rule (see [[few-sons-are-like-their-father]]). She offers herself as “such a companion of your father's” (2.286; see [[hetairos]] and [[mentor]]), and the suitors measure him against his father with a jeer: he may perish at sea, “wandering like Odysseus” (2.332–333).

**Grown.** Telemachus tells the suitors that the boy they plundered has grown: “But now that I am grown, and hearing the words of others / I learn, and the spirit within me grows” (2.314–315; see [[nepios]]). He goes down to his father's storeroom (2.337) and takes the provisions for his voyage (2.348–360; see [[the-journey-of-telemachus]]).
