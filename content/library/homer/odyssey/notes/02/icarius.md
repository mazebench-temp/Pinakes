summary: Penelope's father is named twice by Telemachus: the suitors shrink from going to his house, where he would settle the bride-price and give her to the man he wished (2.52–54), and Telemachus will not send his mother back to him, for he would have to pay back much to Icarius and would suffer evil from her father (2.132–134). The suitors' demand is that she go back to her father to marry.
# Unnamed: her father's house (2.52); the father who bids her marry (2.113-114); the evil from her father (2.134);
# her father's house and the bride-price for a dear daughter (2.195-197).
refs: [2.52, 2.113-114, 2.134, 2.195-197]
---

**The bride's father.** The suitors “shudder at going to the house of her father, / Icarius, so that he himself might settle the bride-price for his daughter / and give her to whom he wished” (2.52–54). ἐεδνώσαιτο, “settle the bride-price”, is the father's part in a marriage, as ἔεδνα, “bride-price”, are the gifts of the suitor (see the translation's table of key words, [[bride-price]] and [[marriage]]). Antinous would have Telemachus send her away to marry “whichever man her father bids, and who pleases her” (2.113–114), and Eurymachus that she go back to her father's house, where the wedding and the bride-price will be made ready (2.195–197, where 2.196–197 = 1.277–278; see [[antinous]] and [[eurymachus]]). The book does not say where the house is (see [[the-icarius-problem]]).

**What the son would owe.** “And it would be hard for me to pay back much / to Icarius, if of my own will I send my mother away. / For I shall suffer evil from her father” (2.132–134): to send her home against her will would cost Telemachus a payment to her father, and evil from him besides, as well as her Erinyes and the blame of men (2.134–137; see [[mothers-and-sons]] and [[penelope]]).
