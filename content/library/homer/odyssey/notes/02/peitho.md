---
summary: Penelope's web worked by persuasion: “So she spoke, and our proud spirit was persuaded” (2.103), and for three years she “persuaded the Achaeans” (2.106). Odysseus left his house to Mentor, “that it should obey the old man” (2.227).
grc: [ἐπεπείθετο, ἔπειθεν]
en: [persuaded]
---

πείθομαι is to let oneself be persuaded, and so to obey (see the entry): the suitors were persuaded by the woman they meant to marry (see [[dolos]]), and the household was to obey Mentor (see the translation's note on 2.227 and [[mentor]]). Telemachus' πείσομαι at 2.134, “I shall suffer”, is the future of πάσχω, not of πείθω.
