summary: “Divine Odysseus”: no one remembers him (2.233); the suitors go to his house when the assembly breaks up (2.259), and Athena goes there to put them to sleep (2.394); and his storeroom holds “the unmixed, divine drink” (2.341).
grc: [θείου]
---

Ὀδυσσῆος θείοιο closes the verse at 2.233 and 2.394, as it closes the verse in which the Argives “praised the words of divine Odysseus” in *Iliad* 2 (*Iliad* 2.335); at 2.259 the shorter genitive stands before the name, θείου Ὀδυσῆος. The epithet goes with the house of the absent king: the suitors go to it when the assembly is dissolved (2.258–259), and the goddess goes to it to pour sleep on them (2.394–395; [[sweet-sleep]]). In the storeroom the old wine is ἄκρητον θεῖον ποτόν, “the unmixed, divine drink,” kept in case Odysseus comes home (2.340–343; [[wine]], [[the-storeroom-of-odysseus]]).
