---
summary: Telemachus asks the Ithacans to leave him alone “with grim grief / to be worn down” (2.70–71).
---

The epithet goes with πένθος, “grief,” in the plea that follows his appeal by Olympian Zeus and by Themis: σχέσθε, φίλοι, καί μʼ οἶον ἐάσατε πένθεϊ λυγρῷ / τείρεσθʼ (2.68–71; [[penthos]], [[themis-the-goddess]]). In Book 1 it was the grim homecoming of the Achaeans and Phemius' grim song of it (1.327, 1.341). See [[telemachus]].
