---
summary: The formula names Telemachus three times: as he rises from his bed on the morning of the assembly (2.2), as he rejoices at Aegyptius' word of omen (2.35), and as the crew stow the provisions on the ship at his bidding (2.415).
---

The three uses mark the beginning, the middle and the end of his day. He rises to call the assembly (2.1–5; [[dressing-scene]]); he hears, without being named, the old man's wish that Zeus may bring the man who called it some good, and takes it as a word of omen (2.33–35; [[aegyptius]]); and at night his orders are carried out at the ship, “as the dear son of Odysseus bade them” (2.415; [[sailing-type-scene]]). Merry explains the φήμη of 2.35 as a chance utterance whose full bearing the speaker does not know: Aegyptius does not know that he is blessing Telemachus, who declares himself the man who called the assembly only at 2.40 (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 2.35).
