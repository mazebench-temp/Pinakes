---
summary: Athena, as Mentor, says that Odysseus was the man “to accomplish both deed and word” (2.272), and Antinous bids Telemachus let no other evil “be a care in your breast, either deed or word” (2.304).
grc: ["ἔργον τε ἔπος τε"]
en: [both deed and word, either deed or word]
---

The pair ἔργον τε ἔπος τε closes the verse both times. The goddess uses it of the father in whose likeness she hopes the son will prove himself (2.270–273; [[fathers-and-sons]]); a few verses later the suitor uses it of what Telemachus should not plan, inviting him to eat and drink as before (2.303–305). In the *Iliad* Zeus says that he himself will devise “both deed and word” to let the Achaeans breathe again (*Iliad* 15.234).
