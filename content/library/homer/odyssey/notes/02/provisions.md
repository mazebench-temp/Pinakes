**Athena's orders.** In the likeness of Mentor, Athena divides the work: she will fit out a swift ship and gather willing companions, and he is to go home, keep company with the suitors, and make ready the provisions (2.285–295; see [[athena]], [[mentor]] and [[the-journey-of-telemachus]]). ὅπλισσον, “make ready”, is the verb of a meal prepared, as when the Cyclops “made ready … the last of his suppers” (ὡπλίσσατο δόρπον, 2.20), and ἄρσον, “fit”, the verb of fitting the jars with their lids (2.353; see [[two-handled-jar]]).

**In the storeroom.** Telemachus gives the orders to Eurycleia: wine in twelve two-handled jars, and barley meal in “well-sewn leather bags”, twenty measures of it, “and let it all be got ready together” (2.349–356; see [[eurycleia]], [[barley-meal]] and [[the-storeroom-of-odysseus]]). He will take it in the evening, when his mother has gone up to bed (2.357–358; see [[upper-room]]). When Eurycleia has sworn to keep silent, she draws off the wine and pours the meal (2.379–380).

**To the ship.** At nightfall, when Athena has brought him to the crew on the shore, Telemachus leads them to fetch the stores: “Come here, friends, let us bring the provisions; for they are all / together in the hall now” (2.410–411), “And they brought everything to the well-benched ship / and stowed it, as the dear son of Odysseus bade them” (2.414–415; see [[well-benched]] and [[sailing-type-scene]]).
