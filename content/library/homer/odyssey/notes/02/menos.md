---
summary: Antinous twice greets Telemachus as “lofty in speech, uncontrollable in your fury” (μένος ἄσχετε, 2.85, 2.303), and Athena hopes that his “father's noble might has been instilled in you” (μένος ἠύ, 2.271).
---

The same word gives two judgments of the boy. In Antinous' mouth μένος is an unruly temper, ἄσχετος, “uncontrollable”, as Zeus tells Ares, “You have your mother's fury, uncontrollable and unyielding” (*Iliad* 5.892; see the translation's table). In Athena's it is the father's strength instilled in the son (ἐνέστακται, 2.271), the μένος she put in his heart the day before (1.89, 1.321; see [[fathers-and-sons]] and [[tharsos]]).
