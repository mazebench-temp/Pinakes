# Μαστορίδης (2.158; again 24.452): the patronymic counts for the father and for the son (halitherses). At
# Iliad 15.438 it is Lycophron, so it is claimed here, within the Odyssey only.
grc: [Μαστορίδης]
---

Μαστορίδης, “son of Mastor” (2.158; see [[halitherses]]), opens the verse after the name that ends the verse before. The Iliad, too, names a seer's father as it introduces him: Calchas is Θεστορίδης, “son of Thestor”, in the verse that calls him “by far the best of the bird-seers” (*Iliad* 1.69; see [[calchas]] and [[best-of-bird-seers]]). The same two verses bring Halitherses into the assembly of the last book, with a new second half to the second (24.451–452). Nothing else is said of the father. He is not the Iliad's Mastor, whose son Lycophron is also Μαστορίδης (*Iliad* 15.438; see [[mastor]]).
