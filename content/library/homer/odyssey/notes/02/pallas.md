summary: “So she spoke, and Pallas Athena led the way / swiftly; and he followed in the footprints of the goddess” (2.405–406).

The two names come together as the goddess, still in Mentor's likeness, leads Telemachus from the house to the ship, in a couplet that the Odyssey uses again at Pylos and in the town of the Phaeacians (3.29–30, 7.37–38; [[followed-in-the-footprints]]). In Book 1 the double name came twice while she was Mentes to him (1.125, 1.252). See [[athena]].
