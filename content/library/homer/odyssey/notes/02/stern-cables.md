---
summary: With Athena and Telemachus seated in the stern, the crew loose the stern-cables and go on board (2.418–419).
---

τοὶ δὲ πρυμνήσιʼ ἔλυσαν, “And they loosed the stern-cables” (2.418): the ship has lain moored at the edge of the harbor since nightfall (2.391; see [[harbor]]), and once the cables are loosed the crew go on board and sit down at the benches (2.419; see [[rowing-benches]]). The half-verse comes again in Book 15, after Telemachus has seated Theoclymenus beside him in the stern for the voyage home from Pylos (15.285–286), and when he sends the ship on to the city from the coast of Ithaca (15.552). At Chryse in the Iliad the crew make the cables fast on landing and sleep beside them before they sail (*Iliad* 1.436, 1.476; see [[stern]]).
