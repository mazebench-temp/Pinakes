---
summary: Antinous grants Penelope fame by her delays: “Great fame she makes / for herself, but for you a longing for much substance” (2.125–126). Telemachus will sail in the hope of “a rumor / from Zeus, which most of all brings report to men” (2.216–217).
---

**Fame and report.** The book uses the word in the two ways of Book 1. With an epithet it is “fame”, μέγα κλέος (2.125); bare, it is the report that travels, which a rumor from Zeus carries (2.217), in the words of Athena's charge to Telemachus the day before (1.282–283; see [[ossa]]). Merry holds that κλέος in Homer means “report”, and that the sense of “fame” lies in an epithet such as μέγα (*Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 1.241; see the translation's note on 1.241).

**Her fame, his substance.** Antinous sets the one against the other: while the gods keep Penelope to her purpose, she makes great fame for herself, and her son loses his goods (2.123–128; see [[penelope]] and [[possessions]]). The poem's last word on her fame is spoken among the dead: the shade of Agamemnon says that the fame of her excellence will never perish (τῷ οἱ κλέος οὔ ποτʼ ὀλεῖται / ἧς ἀρετῆς, 24.196–197; see [[arete]]).

**News of the father.** Telemachus will go to Sparta and to sandy Pylos “in the hope that some mortal may tell me, or I may hear a rumor / from Zeus, which most of all brings report to men” (2.216–217; see [[the-journey-of-telemachus]] and [[nostos]]). Antinous, mocking, calls the voyage a search for news, μετʼ ἀγαυοῦ πατρὸς ἀκουήν, “in search of news of your noble father” (2.308), with ἀκουή, the noun of ἀκούω, “hear”, as κλέος is akin to κλύω.
