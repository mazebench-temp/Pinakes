summary: Athena comes to Telemachus twice in the likeness of Mentor, “both in form and in voice”, and between those visits goes through the city in the likeness of Telemachus himself. The narrator names her each time; Telemachus obeys “the voice of the goddess”, and in Book 4 Noemon, who lent the ship, wonders whether he saw Mentor or a god.

**In the likeness of Mentor.** Telemachus prays on the shore, “and Athena came near to him / in the likeness of Mentor, both in form and in voice” (2.267–268; see [[prayer]], [[athena]] and [[mentor]]). The Iliad's gods take a man's shape in the same way, “in form and in tireless voice”: Poseidon as Calchas, Athena as Phoenix and as Deiphobus (*Iliad* 13.45, 17.555, 22.227; see [[seeing-the-gods]]). In the Odyssey the verse belongs to Athena as Mentor: twice here (2.268, 2.401), in the fight in the hall (22.206), and twice at the end of the poem (24.503, 24.548). In Book 1 she came as Mentes, a stranger from Taphos (1.105; see [[mentes-the-taphian]]); Mentor is a man of Ithaca, the old companion to whom Odysseus entrusted his house (2.225–227), and he has just spoken in the assembly (2.229–241). The god speaks as the companion of the father: “such a companion of your father's am I to you” (2.286).

**The voice of the goddess.** Telemachus does not name her. He prayed to “you god who came yesterday to our house” (2.262), and when she has spoken he goes, “once he had heard the voice of the goddess” (2.297): the narrator's words, which do not say how much he knew. At Pylos he still calls her Mentor (3.22, 3.240), until she leaves in the likeness of a sea eagle and Nestor names her as the daughter of Zeus (3.371–379; see [[like-a-bird]]).

**In the likeness of Telemachus.** Between her two visits as Mentor the goddess takes the shape of the young man himself, and goes through the city doing in his name what he had asked of the people in vain, a swift ship and companions (2.383–387; see [[telemachus]]). She makes her rounds as she will in Scheria in the likeness of Alcinous' herald, “standing beside each man” (2.384 = 8.10). Noemon, who lends her the ship, tells Antinous in Book 4 that he gave it to Telemachus willingly, since it is hard to refuse such a man; the leader he saw go aboard was Mentor, or a god who was like him in every way (Μέντορα, ἠὲ θεόν), and what Noemon wonders at is that he saw Mentor himself in Ithaca the morning before (4.649–656; see [[noemon-son-of-phronius]]).

**Following the goddess.** As Mentor again she calls him from the hall (2.399–404), and “he followed in the footprints of the goddess” (2.406). The verse leads him behind her again at Pylos (3.30), and Odysseus behind Calypso and behind Athena in the likeness of a young girl in Scheria (5.193, 7.38).
