---
summary: Telemachus washes his hands in the gray sea and prays to the god who bade him go over the misty sea (2.260–263); Eurycleia begs him not to suffer evils on the barren sea (2.370); and at nightfall the ship goes down to the sea and runs before a West Wind “sounding over the wine-dark sea” (2.389, 2.421).
---

**The prayer.** After the assembly, “Telemachus went apart along the shore of the sea, / and washed his hands in the gray sea, and prayed to Athena” (2.260–261; see [[shore]], [[gray-sea]] and [[prayer]]): the god who came to his house had bidden him go “in a ship over the misty sea” (2.263; see [[misty]]). The sea is where the journey begins and where the danger lies.

**The fear of the sea.** Athena, in the likeness of Mentor, will make a ship ready “and launch her on the wide sea” (2.295). The suitors jeer that he may “perish far from his friends, wandering like Odysseus” (2.333), and Eurycleia begs him to stay: “you have no need / to suffer evils on the barren sea or to wander” (2.369–370; see [[barren-sea]]). Both speak of the sea of the father's wanderings, which the first book had set at the head of the poem (1.4).

**The voyage.** At nightfall Athena “dragged the swift ship down to the sea” (2.389): ἅλαδε, “to the sea”, the word of the Iliad's talk of dragging the ships down to sail home (*Iliad* 9.683, 14.97). Telemachus and the goddess come “down to the ship and the sea” (2.407), she sends “a fresh West Wind, sounding over the wine-dark sea” (2.421; see [[wine-dark]] and [[zephyrus]]), and the ship “ran over the waves, accomplishing her way” all night (2.429, 2.434; see [[cutwater]] and [[sailing-type-scene]]).
