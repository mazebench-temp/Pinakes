summary: Mentor is “the companion of noble Odysseus” (2.225), and with Halitherses one of Telemachus' “father's companions from of old” (2.254); Athena, in his likeness, is “such a companion of your father's” (2.286). Telemachus asks the people for “a swift ship and twenty companions” (2.212), and the goddess gathers a crew of volunteers (2.291–292, 2.391–392, 2.402–408).

**The father's companions.** The old companions of Odysseus in the book are the men who stayed at home: Mentor, to whom he entrusted his house (2.225–227), and Halitherses, whom Leiocritus names with him as the men to speed Telemachus on his way (2.253–254; see [[mentor]] and [[halitherses]]). Those who sailed with him are lost. Halitherses foretold that he would come home “after suffering many evils, and losing all his companions” (2.174; see [[nostos]]), and the son of Aegyptius was the last of them that the Cyclops ate (2.17–20; see the translation's note on 2.20). When Telemachus next comes to the place of assembly, he sits with Mentor, Antiphus and Halitherses, his father's companions from of old, in the words of 2.254 (17.67–70).

**The son's companions.** The young men are his own: the goddess gathers them through the land as volunteers (ἐθελοντῆρας, 2.291–292), they assemble at the ship (2.391–392), “already your well-greaved companions / sit at the oars” (2.402–403), and he urges them on as they raise the mast (2.422; see [[the-journey-of-telemachus]]).
