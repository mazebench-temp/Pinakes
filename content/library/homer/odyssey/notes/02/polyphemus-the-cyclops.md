**The Cyclops in Ithaca.** The verses that bring in the old hero Aegyptius, who opens the first assembly since Odysseus sailed, tell what became of his son Antiphus, who went with Odysseus to Ilios (2.15–24; see [[aegyptius]], [[antiphus-son-of-aegyptius]] and [[troy]]). The narrator tells what no one in Ithaca can know, since none of the companions who sailed with Odysseus came home (1.5–9), and what Odysseus himself will tell the Phaeacians in the ninth book, where none of the six men the Cyclops ate is named (9.287–344). The first book named the Cyclops as the cause of Poseidon's anger (1.68–75); the second gives one of his victims a name and a father.

**The last of his suppers.** ὡπλίσσατο δόρπον (2.20) is the Cyclops' own formula, “he made ready his supper” (9.291, 9.344). There he seizes two of Odysseus' twelve companions at a time and makes his meal of them three times, at evening, at dawn and at evening again (9.195, 9.289–291, 9.311, 9.344, with δεῖπνον for the morning meal); the last of his suppers is the second evening's, after which Odysseus gives him the wine (9.344–347). Merry takes πύματον to mean that Antiphus was the last of the six, comparing the Cyclops' promise to eat “Nobody” last (9.369), and rejects Eustathius' “last of all men”, which needs an un-Homeric story in which the Cyclops lost his life with his sight; perhaps, he adds, the stress falls on ὡπλίσσατο, since once blinded the Cyclops could no longer wait on himself (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 2.20; see the translation's note on 2.20). In the ninth book he survives the blinding, prays to his father and hurls rocks after the ship (9.526–542).

**The savage in his cave.** ἄγριος, “savage” (2.19), is the word of Odysseus' foreboding before he met him and of his companions' warning when he taunts him from the ship (9.215, 9.494). ἐν σπῆι γλαφυρῷ, “in his hollow cave” (2.20; see [[hollow-caves]]), comes back in that taunt, “Cyclops, it was not a feeble man's companions, after all, that you were to eat / in your hollow cave by brute force” (9.475–476), and in Odysseus' memory of the day the Cyclops penned them in (12.209–210). The renderings of other books are plain renderings, not the translation.
