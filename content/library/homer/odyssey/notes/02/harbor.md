---
summary: At nightfall Athena drags the borrowed ship down to the sea and moors it at the far edge of the harbor, where the companions gather (2.389–392).
---

When the sun has set, Athena drags the swift ship down to the sea, puts in all its tackle, and “moored her at the far edge of the harbor, and around her the brave companions / gathered all together” (2.389–392; see [[ships]] and [[noemon-son-of-phronius]]). ἐπʼ ἐσχατιῇ, “at the far edge”, is where Odysseus, alone of his fleet, keeps his ship outside the harbor of the Laestrygonians, tied to a rock (10.95–96). The harbor is not named. In Book 1 the stranger's ship lay in the harbor of Rheithron, “by the fields, away from the city” (1.185–186; see [[rheithron]]); here Athena gathers the crew in the city, and she and Telemachus go from the house down to the ship and the sea, where they find the companions on the shore (2.383–385, 2.394–408; see [[shore]]).
