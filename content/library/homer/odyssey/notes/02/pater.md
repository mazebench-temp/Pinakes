summary: Telemachus sits in his “father's seat” (2.14) and sails “to learn of the homecoming of my father” (2.215); the suitors would have Penelope go back to her father's house (2.52–54, 2.113–114, 2.195), and Mentor and Halitherses are Telemachus' “father's companions from of old” (2.254). Athena measures the son against the father (2.270–280).
grc: [πατρώια]
---

A book of fathers, as Book 1 was: Odysseus, the king who was “kind, like a father” to his people (2.47, 2.234; see [[kind-as-a-father]]); Icarius, to whom Penelope might be sent back (see [[icarius]]); Aegyptius, mourning one son while two others keep to their “father's fields” (πατρώια ἔργα, 2.22; see [[aegyptius]]). See [[fathers-and-sons]], [[few-sons-are-like-their-father]] and [[hetairos]].
