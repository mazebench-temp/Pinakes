---
summary: “But it is not the Achaean suitors who are to blame toward you: / it is your dear mother, who knows crafty ways beyond all others” (2.87–88): Antinous moves the blame from the suitors to Penelope.
---

As most often in Homer, the word is spoken to deny guilt and to lay it on another (see the note on Book 1). Antinous' proof is the web, the trick by which Penelope kept the suitors waiting (2.89–110; see [[dolos]] and [[kerdos]]), and his remedy is that Telemachus send her back to her father (2.113–114). See [[who-is-to-blame]].
