summary: Antinous says that Penelope “knows crafty ways beyond all others” (2.88), the gift of Athena, “such as we have never heard of in any woman, not even of old” (2.118). Telemachus twice uses the comparative: it would be “better” for him if the people themselves ate up his goods (2.74), and the suitors' refusal of a ship “is what seemed to you the better way” (2.320).
grc: [κέρδεα]
en: [crafty ways]
---

**Penelope's crafty ways.** Antinous puts the blame for the suitors' long stay on the woman “who knows crafty ways beyond all others” (ἥ τοι πέρι κέρδεα οἶδεν, 2.88; see [[aitios]]). He names the gifts of Athena that make her a match for them: “to be skilled in beautiful handiwork, and good wits, / and crafty ways” (2.117–118), the skill at the loom, the wits to devise the web, and the craft to keep it going for three years (2.93–110; see [[phrenes]], [[weaving]] and [[dolos]]). The same words are said of her husband. The stranger tells Penelope that Odysseus knows crafty ways beyond all mortal men, ὣς περὶ κέρδεα πολλὰ καταθνητῶν ἀνθρώπων / οἶδʼ Ὀδυσεύς (19.285–286), and Athena tells Odysseus that both of them know κέρδεα, he among mortals and she among the gods (13.296–299; see [[metis]]).

**Better.** The comparative κέρδιον is Telemachus' word for a choice. “For me it would be better / if you yourselves ate up my treasures and my herds” (2.74–75), since then there would be recompense (2.76; see [[poine]]). In his last words to the suitors it is bitter: they will not give him a ship, so he will go as a passenger, “that, I suppose, is what seemed to you the better way” (2.319–320).
