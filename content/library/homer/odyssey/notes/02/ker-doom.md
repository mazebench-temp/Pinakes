summary: Halitherses says that Odysseus, near at hand, “plants slaughter and doom” for the suitors (2.165); Athena says that they know nothing “of death and black doom, / which is near them now” (2.283–284); Telemachus will try “to let loose the evil fates” upon them (2.316), and hopes that his father may come home “escaping death and the fates” (2.352).
grc: [κῆρα, κῆρας]
en: [doom, fates]
---

The suitors' κήρ is named three times, by Halitherses, by Athena and by Telemachus, and the suitors mock the two warnings they hear. Halitherses reads it in the eagles (φόνον καὶ κῆρα φυτεύει, 2.165; see [[the-omen-of-the-eagles]] and [[halitherses]]), and in Book 17 Telemachus uses the words of himself: if he plants slaughter and doom for the suitors, Peiraeus is to bring him his gifts (17.78–83). Athena in Mentor's likeness says it is near (2.283–284), in the phrase θάνατον καὶ κῆρα μέλαιναν, with the epithet of [[black-doom]]. Telemachus, who has just come of age, will try to bring the κῆρες on them, whether he goes to Pylos or stays at home (2.316–317; see [[nepios]]). For his father he speaks of the doom escaped: Odysseus may come home θάνατον καὶ κῆρας ἀλύξας (2.352), the verse that ends Athena's calming of the storm in Book 5, so that he may reach the Phaeacians (5.385–387). Eurycleia at once answers that he “has perished far from his native land” (2.365; see [[nostos]]).
