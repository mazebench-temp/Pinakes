---
summary: Halitherses foresees evil for many “who live in clear-seen Ithaca” (2.167), Leiocritus scoffs that Telemachus will sit and hear news in Ithaca and never make his journey (2.255–256), and Athena promises him the best of the many ships “in sea-girt Ithaca, new and old” (2.292–294). The book shows the town for the first time, from the place of assembly to the harbor.
---

**Clear-seen Ithaca.** Halitherses, reading the eagles, foretells the suitors' doom and adds: “for many others too there will be evil, / of us who live in clear-seen Ithaca” (2.166–167). The epithet, ἐυδείελος, is the Odyssey's for the island, and recurs in Odysseus' own description of it, ναιετάω δʼ Ἰθάκην ἐυδείελον, “I live in clear-seen Ithaca” (9.21), and four times more of Ithaca (13.212, 13.325, 14.344, 19.132); once it is said of an island Odysseus does not know (13.234). The rendering is provisional: δείελος, “evening” (*Iliad* 21.232), lies behind an old alternative, “lying toward the west” (see [[clear-seen]] and the translation's note on 2.167). In Book 1 the island was κραναή, “craggy” (1.247; see [[craggy]]).

**Odysseus of Ithaca.** Leiocritus calls him Ὀδυσεὺς Ἰθακήσιος, “Odysseus of Ithaca” (2.246), the title the Iliad gives his herald, “Eurybates of Ithaca” (*Iliad* 2.184; see [[eurybates-of-ithaca]]), and the people are Ἰθακήσιοι, “men of Ithaca” (2.25, 2.161, 2.229; see [[ithacans]]). Leiocritus mocks the journey: “But I think he will sit here a long time, and hear / news in Ithaca, and never accomplish this journey” (2.255–256; see [[the-journey-of-telemachus]]). Athena, in the likeness of Mentor, answers the need: “There are many ships / in sea-girt Ithaca, new and old; / of these I will pick out for you the best there is” (2.292–294; see [[sea-girt]] and [[ships]]).

**The town.** The assembly gives the first view of Ithaca's town: the place of assembly, where the elders make way for Telemachus to sit in his father's seat (2.14; see [[assembly]]); the houses and the city across which the eagles dart (2.154); the shore of the sea, where Telemachus goes apart to pray (2.260; see [[shore]]); the streets that grow dark at sunset (2.388); and the harbor where Athena moors the ship (2.391; see [[harbor]]). The people go home, “each to his own house” (2.258), and the suitors, when Athena pours sleep on them, “rose to go to sleep through the city” (2.397): they live in the town, and “haunt our house every day” (2.55).
