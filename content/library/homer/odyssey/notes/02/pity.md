---
summary: When Telemachus flings down the scepter in tears, “pity seized all the people” (2.81), but no one speaks for him; Eurycleia, “lamenting”, begs him not to sail (2.362).
grc: [οἶκτος]
en: [pity, lamenting]
---

οἶκτος, “pity”, occurs only twice in Homer: here, and of the Ithacans again in the last assembly, when Eupeithes weeps for his son (24.438; see [[assembly]]). Here pity is followed by silence (2.82; see [[silence]] and [[tears]]).
