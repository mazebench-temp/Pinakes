---
summary: “Then the gray-eyed goddess Athena thought of another thing” twice in the night of the departure: she goes through the city in the likeness of Telemachus, gathers the crew and borrows a ship (2.382–387), and then goes to the house of Odysseus and pours sleep on the suitors (2.393–398).
---

Telemachus has done what the goddess told him: the provisions are ready and he has gone back to the suitors (2.381; 2.288–291). The verse turns from him to her, and each time a new part of her plan follows, first the ship and its crew, then the sleep that frees him from the suitors (2.382–398; [[sailing-type-scene]], [[sweet-sleep]]). Then she calls him out in Mentor's likeness (2.399–401; [[in-the-likeness-of-mentor]]). See [[athena]] and [[gray-eyed]].
