summary: Penelope told the suitors “since brilliant Odysseus is dead” (2.96); Telemachus does not know whether his father is “alive or dead” (2.132), and will raise a mound if he hears “that he is dead” (2.220). Athena says that the suitors know nothing “of death and black doom” (2.283), and Telemachus hopes that his father may come home “escaping death and the fates” (2.352).
grc: [θάνε, θανάτοιο, τέθνηκε, τεθνηῶτος]
en: [dead]
---

Odysseus' death is the question of the book. Penelope's pretext assumes it (2.96), Eurymachus asserts it (2.182–183), and Eurycleia fears it (2.365–366); Halitherses denies it (2.163–176), and Telemachus will sail to learn which is true (2.215–223; see [[nostos]] and [[ker-doom]]). The suitors' own death, which they do not know, is near (2.283–284).
