summary: Aegyptius asks whether the assembly has been called for news of the army or for “some other public matter” (2.32), and Telemachus answers that it is “a business of my own” (2.44–45). grc: [δήμῳ, δήμιον]
en: [public]
---

**Public and private.** δήμιον, “public”, is the adjective of δῆμος, and it gives the assembly its business. Aegyptius names the two things that might call the people together, news of an army coming and “some other public matter” (2.30–32), and Telemachus, repeating his words, has neither: what has fallen on his house is ἐμὸν αὐτοῦ χρεῖος, “a business of my own” (2.42–45; see [[assembly]] and [[the-city-and-the-household]]). He makes it the people's all the same, asking them to feel indignation and shame (2.63–67; see [[nemesis]]). At Pylos he tells Nestor that his errand is private, not public (πρῆξις δʼ ἥδʼ ἰδίη, οὐ δήμιος, 3.82), and Menelaus at Sparta asks him the same question, δήμιον ἦ ἴδιον (4.312–314); in Book 21 the young Odysseus is sent to Messene for a debt that the whole people was owed (21.16–21).

**The people of a land.** Mentor turns his anger on “the rest of the people” (ἄλλῳ δήμῳ, 2.239), the community apart from the suitors, who sit silent though they are many (2.239–241; see [[laos]] and [[silence]]). Elsewhere in the book δῆμος is the land with its people: the Achaean women “in the land” (2.101), Athena gathering a crew “through the land” (2.291), Telemachus' threat “whether I go to Pylos or stay here in this land” (2.317), and Eurycleia's fear that Odysseus has died far away, ἀλλογνώτῳ ἐνὶ δήμῳ, “among a strange people” (2.366).
