summary: Telemachus' voyage is told step by step: the goddess gathers the crew and borrows a ship (2.382–387), drags it down to the sea at sunset and fits it out (2.388–392); Telemachus brings the provisions aboard, the crew loose the stern-cables, she sends a following wind, and they raise the mast, hoist the sails and pour libations as the ship runs all night (2.413–434).

**Launching.** In the Iliad the commander has the ship dragged down to the sea and chooses the oarsmen (*Iliad* 1.141–142, 1.308–309). Here the goddess does it all, in the likeness of Telemachus: she goes through the city and bids the men gather at the ship in the evening, asks Noemon for it, and when the sun has set drags it down to the sea herself, puts in all its tackle, and moors it at the far edge of the harbor (2.382–392; [[thought-of-another-thing]], [[noemon-son-of-phronius]], [[sunset-lines]], [[well-benched]]).

**Embarking.** Telemachus leads the crew to the house for the provisions and stows them in the ship; Athena sits in the stern with Telemachus beside her, the crew loose the stern-cables and sit down at the rowing benches (2.410–419; [[provisions]], [[stern-cables]], [[rowing-benches]]).

**Under sail.** Athena sends a following wind in the verse with which Apollo sends one to the ship from Chryse, with her name in place of his, τοῖσιν δʼ ἴκμενον οὖρον ἵει γλαυκῶπις Ἀθήνη (2.420; *Iliad* 1.479). The crew raise the mast of fir in its box, make it fast with forestays and hoist the white sails, in three verses where the Iliad has one, and the three verses of the wind in the sail, the wave at the cutwater and the running ship follow as in the Iliad (2.424–429; *Iliad* 1.480–483; [[mast]], [[mast-box]], [[forestays]], [[sails]], [[white]], [[cutwater]]):

**Libation.** Then they set up mixing bowls brim-full of wine and pour libations to all the gods, and most of all to Athena (2.430–433; [[brim-full]], [[libation]]), and the ship runs all night and through the dawn (2.434). The arrival at Pylos opens Book 3 (3.1–5).
