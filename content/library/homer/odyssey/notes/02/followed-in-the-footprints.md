---
summary: Athena, in the likeness of Mentor, leads Telemachus from the house to the ship: “So she spoke, and Pallas Athena led the way / swiftly; and he followed in the footprints of the goddess” (2.405–406).
---

The couplet's first use in the poem comes as Telemachus leaves his father's house by night, called out of the halls by the goddess he sees as Mentor (2.399–404; [[in-the-likeness-of-mentor]]). At the shore he leads in his turn, with the first half of the same verse: “So he spoke, and led the way, and they followed with him” (2.413). See [[pallas]] and [[sailing-type-scene]].
