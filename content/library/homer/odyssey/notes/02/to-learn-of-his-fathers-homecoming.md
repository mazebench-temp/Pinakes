---
summary: Telemachus gives the purpose of his journey to the assembly and in his prayer on the shore, “to learn of the homecoming of my father, who has long been gone” (2.215, 2.264), and to Eurycleia, “to learn of my dear father's homecoming” (2.360).
---

He takes the verse from Athena's counsel of the day before (1.281), and with it the plan that follows: if he hears that his father is alive he will endure another year, and if dead he will come home and heap up a mound for him (2.218–223; 1.287–292; [[dear-native-land]]). In the prayer the verse is the god's own command, recalled to the god (2.262–264; [[prayer-type-scene]]). To Eurycleia he gives the variant of Athena's first plan, “if I may hear of it somewhere” (2.360; 1.94). See [[the-journey-of-telemachus]] and [[sandy]].
