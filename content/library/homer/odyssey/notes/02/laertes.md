**The shroud.** Λαέρτῃ ἥρωι ταφήιον, “a shroud for the hero Laertes” (2.99), is Penelope's reason for the web, and the old man for whom it is woven is still alive: she will finish it “against the time when / the deadly fate of death that lays men low takes him down” (2.99–100; see [[the-web-of-penelope]], [[shroud]], [[that-lays-men-low]] and [[heros]]). The same verses return when Penelope and the shade of Amphimedon tell the story (19.144–147, 24.134–137), and at the end of the poem the old man is alive on his farm (24.205–231).

**The old man of the house.** When Odysseus sailed he entrusted his house to Mentor, “that it should obey the old man” (2.227): the Greek does not say whether the old man is Mentor or Laertes (see the translation's note on 2.227 and [[mentor]]).
