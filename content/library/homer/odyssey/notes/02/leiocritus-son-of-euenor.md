summary: Leiocritus answers Mentor. It is hard to fight with men who outnumber you over a feast: even Odysseus, if he came home, would meet a shameful doom in his own house. Let the people scatter to their work and Mentor and Halitherses speed Telemachus on his way, for he will sit in Ithaca and never make the journey. With that he dissolves the assembly.
# Unnamed: his speech (2.243-256) and the dissolving of the assembly (2.257).

**The third suitor.** After Antinous and Eurymachus, a third suitor answers, brought in by his father's name like Eurymachus, “Then Leiocritus, son of Euenor, answered him face to face” (2.242, shaped like 2.177; see [[euenor]] and [[answering-him]]). He mocks Mentor, “Harsh Mentor, crazed in your mind” (2.243; see [[mentor]]), and turns Mentor's reproach, that the many do nothing against the few (2.241), into a threat: “It is a hard thing / to fight over a feast with men who are more numerous, at that” (2.244–245). Whether “more numerous” goes with the fight or with the men for whom it is hard is uncertain (see the translation's note on 2.245).

**Even if Odysseus came.** His boast is the one the poem will answer:

{{quote:9818f65c-e6bf-54c3-99eb-fd9c80098052}}

Odysseus does come home, finds the suitors feasting in his hall and fights them, few against many, and Telemachus kills Leiocritus with his spear (22.294–296; see [[odysseus]] and [[the-return-of-odysseus]]).

**The assembly dissolved.** “But come, people, scatter, each to his work” (2.252): he sends the people home and leaves the voyage to Telemachus' father's old companions, Mentor and Halitherses, who will speed him on his way (see [[halitherses]]), though he thinks that the young man will sit in Ithaca hearing news and never make the journey (2.253–256; see [[the-journey-of-telemachus]]). “So he spoke, and quickly dissolved the assembly” (2.257) is the verse with which Achilles dissolves the assembly of the Achaeans (*Iliad* 19.276), and the next verse has “each to his own house” (2.258) where the Iliad has “each to his own ship” (*Iliad* 19.277). Here the assembly is dissolved not by the man who called it but by one of the suitors (see [[the-assembly-of-the-ithacans]] and [[assembly-type-scene]]).
