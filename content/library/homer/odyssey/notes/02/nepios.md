---
summary: “Is it not enough that in the past you ate up many fine / possessions of mine, you suitors, while I was still a child?” (2.312–313): Telemachus tells the suitors that the child they plundered has grown.
---

νήπιος has here its first sense, a small child, set against μέγας, “grown” (2.314). The growth Telemachus claims is of the mind as well as the body: “But now that I am grown, and hearing the words of others / I learn, and the spirit within me grows” (2.314–315; see [[thymos]]). He answers Athena's charge of the day before, that he should not keep up childish ways, νηπιάας ὀχέειν, “since you are no longer of that age” (1.296–297), and her promise in Mentor's likeness that he will be “no coward and no fool” (2.270; see [[noos]] and [[fathers-and-sons]]). His nurse still calls him “dear child” and “an only and beloved son”, and would keep him at home (2.363–370; see [[eurycleia]] and [[childhood]]).
