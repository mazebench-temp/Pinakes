# The English "cutwater" stands in 2.427, the Greek στείρῃ in 2.428, as in Il. 1.481–482 (the entry's refs).
refs: [2.427]
---

{{quote:ee978674-b7f1-548d-801b-2c01eeed579a}}

The verses are those of the Iliad's one narrated voyage, the ship's run home from Chryse with Apollo's wind in the sail (*Iliad* 1.481–483; see [[sails]]); only the first half of the first verse differs, ἔπρησεν δʼ ἄνεμος for ἐν δʼ ἄνεμος πρῆσεν. στείρη is the forward edge of the keel, where the bow cuts the water, and the wave is πορφύρεον, rendered “surging” (see [[purple]]). Here the ship runs all night, “and through the dawn” (2.434; see [[sailing-type-scene]]).
