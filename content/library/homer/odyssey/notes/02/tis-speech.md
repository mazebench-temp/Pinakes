---
summary: The suitors' jeers at Telemachus come as what “one of the overweening young men would say” (2.324) and what another “would say in turn” (2.331).
grc: ["τις εἴπεσκε", "ἄλλος δʼ αὖτʼ εἴπεσκε"]
en: [would say]
---

The two nameless speeches give the crowd's verdict, as in the Iliad, but the crowd is the suitors, insulting and taunting Telemachus as they make ready their feast (2.322–323; [[overweening]], [[the-suitors]]). The first has him bringing helpers from Pylos or Sparta, or drugs from Ephyra to put in their mixing bowl (2.325–330; [[odysseus-seeks-poison]]); the second has him perishing far from his friends like his father, which would leave them to divide his possessions (2.332–336). The second is brought in with ἄλλος δʼ αὖτʼ, “another … in turn,” in place of ὧδε δέ τις.
