summary: Telemachus makes his nurse swear “to tell none of this to my dear mother / until the eleventh or the twelfth day has come” (2.373–374), and “the old woman swore a great oath by the gods not to tell” (2.377).
grc: [ὄμοσον, ὄμοσεν, ἀπώμνυ]
en: [swear, swore, sworn]
---

**An oath of silence.** The oath binds Eurycleia to keep the voyage from Penelope for eleven or twelve days, or until she misses her son and hears that he has gone, “so that she may not mar her fair skin with weeping” (2.373–376; see [[eurycleia]] and [[mothers-and-sons]]). ἀπώμνυ is “swore not to” (see the translation's note on 2.377), and the verse that closes the scene, “But when she had sworn and completed the oath” (2.378), is the verse of Hera's oath to Sleep (*Iliad* 14.280) and of Circe's to Odysseus (10.346), and, in the plural, of the oaths sworn to Odysseus by his companions and by the suitors (12.304, 18.59) and to the Phoenician woman by the sailors (15.438). See [[oath-formulas]].

**Kept.** The oath holds. When Penelope learns from the herald Medon that her son has sailed, Eurycleia tells her that she knew everything and that Telemachus took a great oath from her not to tell before the twelfth day, in the words of his own request (4.743–749).
