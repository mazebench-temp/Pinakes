---
summary: Calypso, “the lady nymph,” goes to Odysseus when she has heard the messages of Zeus (5.149–150), and he begins his answer to her offer of immortality “Lady goddess” (5.215).
grc: ["πότνα θεά"]
---

πότνια νύμφη is the proem's title for Calypso (1.14). πότνα is a shorter form found only in the address πότνα θεά: Odysseus' to Calypso here, his to Athena in Ithaca (13.391), and Penelope's to Artemis (20.61). He uses it to soften a refusal: “do not be angry with me for this” (5.215–220; [[calypso]], [[penelope]]).
