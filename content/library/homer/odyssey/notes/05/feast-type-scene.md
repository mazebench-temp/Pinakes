summary: Two meals in Calypso's cave, both in the verses of the welcome. Hermes is seated, a table is set with ambrosia and nectar is mixed, and he eats and drinks before he speaks (5.86–96); on the last evening Odysseus eats the food of mortals and Calypso ambrosia, they put out their hands to the good things and take their fill, and talk follows, not song (5.194–202).

**The god's meal.** The welcome of Hermes keeps the order of a guest's meal, with the gods' own food (see [[xenia]]):

1. **The seat.** Calypso seats him on a shining, glossy throne (5.86; see [[throne]]).
2. **Table and drink.** “So she spoke, and the goddess set a table beside him, / filling it with ambrosia, and mixed red nectar” (5.92–93; see [[table]], [[ambrosia]] and [[nectar]]). κέρασσε, “mixed”, is the verb of mixing wine (see [[mixing-bowl]]).
3. **Eating.** “And the guide, the slayer of Argus, drank and ate” (5.94).
4. **Enough.** “But when he had dined and satisfied his heart with food” (5.95), a verse that returns when Odysseus has eaten in Eumaeus' hut (14.111). ἤραρε θυμὸν ἐδωδῇ is “comforted his heart with food”: W. W. Merry compares Abraham's words to his three guests, “I will fetch a morsel of bread, and comfort ye your hearts” (*Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 5.95, citing Genesis 18:5).
5. **Speech.** “then at last he answered her with words and said” (5.96): the errand comes after the meal, as the questions come after the meal in the welcomes of Books 1, 3 and 4 (1.123–124, 3.69–70, 4.60–62).

**The last meal.** Back in the cave on the evening before the raft, the meal is told in the verses of a feast in a house:

1. **The seats.** Odysseus sits “on the throne from which had risen / Hermes”, and Calypso sits facing him (5.195–198).
2. **Two foods.** “and the nymph set before him all kinds of food, / to eat and to drink, such as mortal men eat”, while the slave women set ambrosia and nectar beside her (5.196–199; see [[handmaids]]). The neuter plural οἷα covers the various kinds of food under πᾶσαν ἐδωδήν; though Calypso sets mortal food before Odysseus, she uses nectar and ambrosia herself (Merry on 5.197).
3. **Eating.** “And they put out their hands to the good things lying ready before them” (5.200), the verse of meals in a house throughout the Odyssey (1.149, 4.67).
4. **Enough.** “But when they had taken their fill of food and drink” (5.201), the verse of Nestor's welcome in Peleus' house in the Iliad (*Iliad* 11.780).
5. **Talk.** “Calypso, brilliant among goddesses, began to speak among them” (5.202): the ordinary opening of an address has become a formula, and τοῖς stands though only Odysseus is there to hear, Hermes having gone (Merry on 5.202); Aristonicus marked the verse because one speaks to one (the scholia, in Merry's critical note; see the translation's note on 5.202).

There is no singer and no dance. The talk is Calypso's last offer of immortality and Odysseus' choice of home (5.203–224), and the evening ends not with sleep but with love in the inner part of the cave (5.225–227; see [[calypsos-cave]] and [[philotes]]).
