---
summary: Swimming along the cliffs of Scheria, Odysseus fears that “a god may even send some great sea monster against me / out of the sea, such as famous Amphitrite breeds in great numbers” (5.421–422).
---

The fear is the last of three. If he tries to land on the rocks, a great wave may dash him against them; if he swims on along the shore, the storm may snatch him back out to sea, “or a god may even send some great sea monster against me / out of the sea, such as famous Amphitrite breeds in great numbers; / for I know how the famous shaker of the earth is angry with me” (5.415–423; see [[shore]], [[amphitrite]], [[daimon]] and [[poseidon]]). The translation renders κῆτος “sea monster” here, as for the Iliad's monster against which the wall of Heracles was built (*Iliad* 20.147), and “beasts” for the seals of Proteus (4.443–452; see the translation's table of key words and [[seals]]).

ἐξ ἁλός, “out of the sea”, is the text; Aristarchus wrote εἰν ἁλί, “in the sea” (the scholia, in Merry's critical note on 5.422). With ἐξ ἁλός, W. W. Merry suggests, ἅλς may be taken as the water by the shore, as distinct from the open sea, πέλαγος, so that the monsters are imagined haunting the rocks and caves of the coast; but he grants that the distinction is not carefully kept, for the poem has ἁλὸς ἐν πελάγεσσι, “in the open sea”, of Leucothea's home in this very book (*Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 5.422; 5.335; see the translation's note on 5.422 and [[sea]]). Amphitrite, who breeds them, is κλυτός, “famous”, with the masculine ending (see [[famous]]). On 5.422 Merry refers to his note on the third book, where he holds that so far as Amphitrite is personified in Homer she is the sea itself rather than a goddess, and quotes Dissen: the waves are called Amphitrite's, not Poseidon's, and it is she, not Poseidon, who is said to rear the κήτεα of the sea (Merry on 3.91). Scylla in her cave fishes for dolphins and dogfish, and for any greater κῆτος that she can catch, of the thousands that Amphitrite breeds (12.95–97, a plain rendering).
