summary: Fourteen speeches end with the formula, from “He spoke, and said to Hermes, his dear son, face to face” (5.28) to Odysseus' prayer, “So he spoke, and at once the god stopped his stream” (5.451); among them Odysseus' wish for death at Troy ends “As he spoke, a great wave struck him from above” (5.313).
grc: ["ὣς ἄρα φωνήσασα", "ὣς ἄρα μιν εἰπόντʼ"]
en: [As he spoke]
---

ὣς ἄρα μιν εἰπόντα is the half-verse with which the Iliad ends the speeches of the dying, “the end of death covered him” (*Iliad* 16.502, 16.855, 22.361); here, after Odysseus has wished that he had died at Troy, it brings the wave, and a few verses later he sits on the raft “avoiding the end of death” (5.313–326; [[the-end-of-death]]). At 5.92 the digitized text prints ὥς ἄρα with an acute, a slip recorded in the translation's notes. The speech-closings turn each speech to its effect: Hermes obeys at once (5.43–44), Calypso and Odysseus shudder at what they hear (5.116, 5.171), Calypso smiles (5.180), the sun goes down on the last evening (5.225), and Poseidon gathers the clouds (5.291).
