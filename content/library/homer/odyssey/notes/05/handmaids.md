---
summary: Calypso's slave women set ambrosia and nectar beside her at the last meal with Odysseus (5.199).
---

“and the slave women set ambrosia and nectar beside her” (5.199; see [[ambrosia]], [[nectar]] and [[feast-type-scene]]). δμῳαί, the word for the slave women of a house, is used of a goddess' servants, who are not otherwise named or counted in the book. Circe's house has four handmaids, “born of the springs and the groves / and the sacred rivers that flow to the sea”, who prepare her guest's seat, table, wine and bath (10.348–365, a plain rendering; see [[circe]]).
