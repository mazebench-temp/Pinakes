---
summary: Calypso: Demeter lay with Iasion “in a thrice-plowed fallow field” (5.126–127).
grc: [τριπόλῳ]
en: [thrice-plowed]
---

Merry explains τρίπολος by the three plowings of the year, at the end of autumn, in spring and in summer, the field so freshened being the νειός, and refers to Hesiod, who bids the farmer plow in spring, turn the fallow again in summer, and sow it while the soil is still light (*Works and Days* 462–463); the scholia quote, with disapproval, a reading τριπύλῳ (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 5.127 and his critical note; see the translation's note on 5.125–128). See [[demeter]] and [[iasion]].
