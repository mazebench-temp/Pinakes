---
summary: Hermes: “You, a goddess, ask me, a god, why I have come” (5.97), and Odysseus at night in the cave, “unwilling beside the willing” (5.155).
grc: ["θεὰ θεόν", "οὐκ ἐθέλων ἐθελούσῃ"]
en: ["a goddess, ask me, a god", unwilling beside the willing]
---

Merry takes both as the favorite usage of setting contrasted words side by side, against the order of grammar, and compares Aegisthus leading Clytemnestra home, “willing, he led her, willing” (3.272; Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 5.155, 5.97). See [[calypso]] and [[hermes]].
