---
summary: Troy is the war's past in the book: the land from which Odysseus might have carried home his share of the spoil (5.39), the city of Priam that the Achaeans fought around for nine years and sacked in the tenth (5.106–107), and “wide Troy”, where the Danaans who perished were blessed (5.307).
# Unnamed: "the city of Priam" and "sacked the city" (5.106-107).
refs: [5.106-107]
---

**From Troy.** Zeus promises that the Phaeacians will give Odysseus more “than Odysseus would ever have carried off from Troy” (5.39). Τροίης is the land of Troy, as when his companions say that he is bringing many treasures home from Troy (ἐκ Τροίης, 10.40; Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 5.39); a variant made it an adjective with the spoil (see the translation's note on 5.39 and [[the-fall-of-troy]]).

**The city.** Hermes does not name it: the Achaeans fought ἄστυ πέρι Πριάμοιο, “around the city of Priam”, and in the tenth year πόλιν πέρσαντες, “sacked the city” (5.106–107; see [[priam]] and [[the-returns-of-the-achaeans]]).

**Wide Troy.** Τροίῃ ἐν εὐρείῃ, “in wide Troy” (5.307), opens the verse in Odysseus' praise of the Danaans who died there (see [[danaans]]). The words οἳ τότʼ ὄλοντο / Τροίῃ ἐν εὐρείῃ, “who perished then / in wide Troy”, are Menelaus' of the men for whom he would give two thirds of his wealth (4.98–99), and the verse-opening is Athena's when she asks Zeus whether Odysseus did not please him with sacrifices “in wide Troy” (1.61–62), and Priam's, of his sons, in the Iliad (*Iliad* 24.256, 24.494).
