---
summary: Athena's speech to the council is made of earlier verses: Mentor's (5.8–12 = 2.230–234), Proteus' (5.14–17 = 4.557–560) and Medon's (5.19–20 = 4.701–702); and Calypso repeats Hermes' account of the wreck in her own (5.110–111 = 5.133–134).
refs: [5.8-20, 5.130-134]
---

Merry allows that Athena's cento may be an intentional recapitulation in words the hearers knew, against Hermann's view that Homer repeats verses only in formulas and messages (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 5.7; see the translation's note on 5.7–20). Calypso, retelling the wreck, gives it to Zeus' thunderbolt (5.130–132; [[the-shipwreck-of-odysseus]]); Odysseus will tell Arete the whole story again, from the wreck to the river (7.244–297).
