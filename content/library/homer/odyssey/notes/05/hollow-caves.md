summary: Calypso's cave: Odysseus sleeps beside her at night “in the hollow caves,” unwilling beside the willing (5.154–155), and the singular names the cave three times, where the vine grows round it, where she leads him to supper, and where they spend the last night (5.68, 5.194, 5.226).
grc: ["σπείους γλαφυροῖο", "σπεῖος γλαφυρὸν"]
---

The plural phrase of the proem (1.15) returns for the nights of his captivity (5.154–155; [[calypso]]). The genitive σπείους γλαφυροῖο and the accusative σπεῖος γλαφυρόν are the same cave: a garden vine is stretched “about the hollow cave” (5.68–69), the goddess and the man come “to the hollow cave” to eat (5.194), and “in the inner part of the hollow cave” they take their pleasure in love on the eve of the building (5.226–227). See [[calypsos-cave]].
