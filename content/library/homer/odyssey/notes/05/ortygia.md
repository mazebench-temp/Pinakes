---
summary: In Calypso's protest against the jealous gods, Ortygia is where Artemis killed Orion, the mortal whom Dawn had taken for herself (5.121–124).
---

**Where Orion died.** Calypso's first example of the gods' jealousy is Dawn's: the gods begrudged her Orion “until in Ortygia chaste Artemis of the golden throne / came upon him with her gentle arrows and killed him” (5.123–124; see [[artemis]], [[orion]], [[eos]] and [[calypso]]). The place is named and nothing more is said of it. Merry takes it for Delos, where, by the account he cites from Apollodorus, Artemis killed Orion, and he sets aside Völcker's view that the Syracusan Ortygia is meant (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 5.121, 5.123; see the translation's note on 5.121–124 and the general article).

Some ancient critics rejected 5.123–124, because in Homer Artemis never kills men (the scholia, in Merry's critical note). Merry answers that the death of Orion does break the rule by which Apollo brings death to men and Artemis to women, but that she kills him here not as a goddess of death but out of jealousy, and that the arrows are still ἀγανά, “gentle”, of the painless death, though shot in anger (Merry on 5.124; see [[gentle-arrows]]). The same verse of the gentle arrows returns in Eumaeus' account of his island, which lies “above Ortygia” (15.404–411).
