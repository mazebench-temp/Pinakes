---
summary: The goal of the book, named ten times: Odysseus cannot reach his native land (5.15), the Phaeacians will send him there (5.37), it is his fate to reach it (5.42, 5.115), Calypso will send him “so that you may reach your native land quite unharmed” (5.168), and asks whether he would go “home to your dear native land” (5.204).
---

Two of the verses answer each other across the book. Calypso warns him of the sorrows he must fill up “before you reach your native land” (5.206–207), and in the storm he remembers it: “I fear that everything the goddess said was true, / when she said that on the sea, before I reached my native land, / I would fill up the measure of sorrows” (5.300–302). The verse “so that he may reach his native land quite unharmed” is said of Telemachus by Zeus and of Odysseus by Calypso (5.26, 5.144, 5.168). See [[nostos]].
