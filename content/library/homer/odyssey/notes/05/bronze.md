---
summary: The bronze of the book is the bronze of the carpenter's tools: Calypso bids Odysseus cut beams “with the bronze”, gives him a bronze axe, and he hews the trees with the bronze (5.162, 5.235, 5.244). The Phaeacians will give him bronze and gold in plenty (5.38).
---

χαλκός stands for the tool made of it. Calypso bids Odysseus “cut long beams with the bronze and fit together / a broad raft” (5.162–163), the axe she gives him is “of bronze, sharpened on both sides” (5.235), and he fells twenty trees “and hewed them with the bronze” (5.244; see [[axe]], [[adze]] and [[the-building-of-the-raft]]). The metal of the heroes' weapons and armor (see the entry) is here the metal of the shipwright's tools. As a gift it goes with gold and clothing in the welcome that Zeus foretells: the Phaeacians will send Odysseus home “giving him bronze and gold in plenty, and clothing” (5.38; see [[gold-and-silver]], [[guest-gifts]] and [[phaeacians]]).
