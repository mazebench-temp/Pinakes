**Those men.** The book takes up the suitors where Book 4 left them, lying in wait for Telemachus at Asteris (4.842–847; see [[asteris]] and [[the-journey-of-telemachus]]). Athena tells the gods, in the herald Medon's words, “And now they are eager to kill his beloved son / as he comes home” (5.18–19, as 4.700–701; see [[telemachus]]), and Zeus answers with a question: “Was it not you yourself who devised this plan, / that Odysseus should come and take vengeance on those men?” (5.23–24). Athena had not spoken of vengeance at the first council, where she spoke only of his return; the thought is first Nestor's (3.216), and Teiresias will repeat it (11.118; Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 5.23; see the translation's note on 5.23–24). The two verses come back at the end of the poem, when Zeus answers Athena again after the suitors are dead (24.479–480).

**Back again.** Zeus bids Athena bring the young man home, “and the suitors in their ship may go back again” (5.27). παλιμπετές is a neuter adverb, “back again”, the reversal of a course, from which comes the sense “in vain” (Merry on 5.27); the raised dot that the digitized Greek prints after νηί is a slip (see the translation's note on 5.27).
