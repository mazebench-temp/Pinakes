---
summary: At the second council of the gods Athena repeats Mentor's words to the assembly of Ithaca: no one remembers divine Odysseus “among the people he was lord over, and he was kind, like a father” (5.11–12 = 2.233–234).
---

Athena opens her plea to Zeus with five verses that Mentor spoke in Book 2: let no scepter-bearing king be gentle and kind any more, but always harsh and doing evil, “since no one remembers divine Odysseus / among the people he was lord over, and he was kind, like a father” (5.8–12 = 2.230–234; see [[athena]], [[mentor]], [[scepter-bearing]] and [[the-council-of-the-gods]]). Her speech is made of earlier verses: 5.14–17 are Proteus' words of Odysseus held by Calypso (4.557–560), and 5.19–20 Medon's to Penelope (4.701–702). W. W. Merry allows that the repetition may be an intentional recapitulation, against Hermann's rule that Homer repeats only formulas and messages (*Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 5.7; see the translation's note on 5.7–20 and [[retelling]]). In Ithaca Mentor said it to rebuke the people, who sit silent and do not stop the suitors, few as they are (2.239–241); among the gods Athena says it as the first of the sorrows of Odysseus that she tells them (5.5–6).
