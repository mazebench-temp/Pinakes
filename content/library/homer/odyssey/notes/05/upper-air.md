# αἰθέρος (5.50), first met in the Odyssey here; again 15.293, the fair wind that rushes through the upper air.
grc: [αἰθέρος]
---

“Stepping on Pieria, he fell from the upper air upon the sea” (5.50; see [[hermes]], [[pieria]] and [[the-gull]]). W. W. Merry (*Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 5.50) joins ἐξ αἰθέρος with ἔμπεσε, “he fell from the upper air”, and takes Πιερίην ἐπιβάς of the god's passage over the peaks of the Pierian range, as Hera, going from Olympus to Lemnos, stepped on Pieria and sped over the snowy mountains of Thrace (*Iliad* 14.225–229). If Pieria is instead the plain, a stage between Olympus and the sea, ἐξ αἰθέρος goes with ἐπιβάς, for the αἰθήρ is the clear air of the mountain heights, distinguished from the lower air, ἀήρ; and Merry recalls that Zeus on Olympus is αἰθέρι ναίων, “dwelling in the upper air” (15.523). Either way the god comes down from the heights to the sea, and crosses it at the height of a sea bird (5.51–54).
