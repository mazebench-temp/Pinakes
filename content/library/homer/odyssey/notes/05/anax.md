---
summary: Odysseus addresses the god of the river of Scheria as ἄναξ, “lord”: “Hear me, lord, whoever you are” and “But pity me, lord” (5.445, 5.450). Athena recalls that Odysseus was lord over his people (5.12).
---

ἄναξ is the title of a god in prayer, and Odysseus gives it to a god he cannot name: κλῦθι, ἄναξ, ὅτις ἐσσί, “Hear me, lord, whoever you are” (5.445), as Glaucus calls on Apollo, κλῦθι ἄναξ (*Iliad* 16.514), and ἀλλʼ ἐλέαιρε, ἄναξ, “But pity me, lord” (5.450; see [[prayer]], [[the-river-of-scheria]] and [[a-suppliant-is-respected-by-the-gods]]). At the council Athena recalls the king in the verb of the same word: no one remembers Odysseus “among the people he was lord over” (λαῶν οἷσιν ἄνασσε, 5.12 = 2.234; see [[basileus]]).
