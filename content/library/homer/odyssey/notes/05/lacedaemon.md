summary: Athena tells the gods that Telemachus has gone “to sacred Pylos and to shining Lacedaemon” and that the suitors lie in wait for him (5.18–20), and Zeus bids her bring him home unharmed (5.25–27); he is still in Menelaus' land, where she will come for him in the fifteenth book.
---

**Shining Lacedaemon.** Athena's plea at the second council ends with Telemachus' journey, in the verse with which Medon told Penelope of it: “to sacred Pylos and to shining Lacedaemon” (5.20 = 4.702; see [[pylos]], [[telemachus]] and [[medon-the-herald]]). Λακεδαίμονα δῖαν is Nestor's phrase for Menelaus' land (3.326) and Menelaus' own (4.313; see [[brilliant]] and the Book 4 note). Zeus answers that she is able to bring him safe to his native land, and the suitors back again in their ship (5.25–27; see [[the-council-of-the-gods]] and [[the-suitors]]). The poem does not follow her there yet. She goes “to shining Lacedaemon after the son of Odysseus” only when she has left Odysseus in Ithaca (13.439–440, a plain rendering), and finds Telemachus lying awake in the forecourt of Menelaus' house, kept from sleep by thoughts of his father (15.1–8; see [[menelaus]] and [[the-journey-of-telemachus]]).
