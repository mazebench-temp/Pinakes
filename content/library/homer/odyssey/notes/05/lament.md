---
summary: On Calypso's shore Odysseus' life ebbs away “as he mourned for his homecoming” (5.153), and Calypso's first words to him are “do not mourn here any longer, I beg you” (5.160).
en: [mourned, mourn]
---

ὀδύρομαι, which the translation renders “mourn”, apart from γοάω, “lament”, is here a man's mourning for something lost to him alive. Odysseus sits weeping on the rocks and the beaches, “and his sweet life was ebbing away / as he mourned for his homecoming, since the nymph no longer pleased him” (νόστον ὀδυρομένῳ, 5.152–153; see [[nostos]] and [[tears]]); in Ithaca, Athena will tell him, his wife has been doing the same, “always mourning in her heart for your homecoming” (νόστον ὀδυρομένη, 13.379; see [[penelope]]). Calypso answers his mourning with her promise: “Ill-fated man, do not mourn here any longer, I beg you, nor let your life / waste away; for now with a ready heart I will send you away” (5.160–161; see [[calypso]] and [[moira]]). The groans that go with his tears, στοναχαί (5.83, 5.157), are the sound of the same grief (see [[algos]]).
