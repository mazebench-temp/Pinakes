summary: In Calypso's cave the θρόνος is a goddess' seat of honor: she seats Hermes on “a shining, glossy throne”, and on the last evening Odysseus sits on the throne from which the god had risen (5.86, 5.195). Artemis, in Calypso's protest, is “of the golden throne” (5.123).
# θρόνῳ (5.86), first met in the Odyssey here (the Iliad's θρόνῳ is claimed in its Book 8 note); θρόνου (5.195) and
# χρυσόθρονος (5.123) are entry forms.

**The guest's seat.** “And Calypso, brilliant among goddesses, questioned Hermes, / when she had seated him on a shining, glossy throne” (5.85–86; see [[xenia]] and [[visit-scene]]). ἱδρύω, “seat”, is the verb of Athena seating Ares on his throne on Olympus (*Iliad* 15.142), and Charis seats Thetis on a silver-studded throne in the house of Hephaestus before she offers her good cheer (*Iliad* 18.389–390). σιγαλόεις, “glossy”, is usually referred to σίαλος, “fat”, as if “oiled”; others derive it from a root meaning “shine” (W. W. Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 5.86; see [[glossy]] and [[phaeinos]]). In the houses of men the Odyssey's θρόνος is a “chair” (1.130); here, in a goddess' house, it is the gods' seat, and the translation renders it “throne” (see its row θρόνος).

**The mortal on the god's seat.** On the last evening Odysseus “sat down there on the throne from which had risen / Hermes”, and Calypso sat facing him (5.195–198; see [[feast-type-scene]] and [[calypsos-cave]]). The guest sits where the god sat, but he is given the food of mortals (5.196–197; see [[ambrosia]]).

**Of the golden throne.** In Calypso's protest, Orion was killed by “chaste Artemis of the golden throne” (5.123; see [[golden-throned]] and [[artemis]]), the epithet that the Iliad gives to Hera and to Artemis herself (*Iliad* 1.611, 9.533), and the Odyssey later to Dawn (10.541).
