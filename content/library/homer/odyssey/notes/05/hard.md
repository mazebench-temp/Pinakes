---
summary: Calypso: “You are hard, you gods, and jealous beyond all others” (5.118).
---

The verse begins as Apollo's reproach of the gods over Hector's body, σχέτλιοί ἐστε, θεοί (*Iliad* 24.33), and some read his δηλήμονες, “destructive,” here too; Merry keeps ζηλήμονες, “jealous,” which the next verse explains, since the gods begrudge goddesses their mortal lovers (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 5.118; see the translation's note on 5.118). See [[the-jealousy-of-the-gods]] and [[calypso]].
