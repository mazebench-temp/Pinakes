summary: Zeus sends Hermes to tell Calypso “our unerring counsel” (5.30), and reminds Athena that she herself devised the plan of vengeance (5.23). Odysseus asks Calypso to swear that she will not plan some other evil bane against him (5.179, 5.187), and Poseidon sees that “the gods have changed their purpose” about Odysseus (5.286).
grc: [ἐβούλευσας, βουλευσέμεν, μετεβούλευσαν]
en: [devised, changed their purpose]
---

**The counsel of the gods.** Zeus answers Athena with a question, “Was it not you yourself who devised this plan” (οὐ γὰρ δὴ τοῦτον μὲν ἐβούλευσας νόον αὐτή, 5.23; see [[noos]] and [[poine]]), and sends Hermes with the verses she proposed at the first council: “tell the nymph of the lovely tresses our unerring counsel, / the homecoming of stout-hearted Odysseus” (νημερτέα βουλήν, 5.30–31 = 1.86–87; see [[nostos]] and [[the-council-of-the-gods]]). Merry takes νόστον in apposition with βουλήν, with ὥς κε νέηται spelling it out (*Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 5.31). Hermes adds that no other god can evade the mind of Zeus (5.103–104; see [[the-mind-of-zeus]] and [[plan-of-zeus]]).

**Plans against him.** Odysseus suspects Calypso's offer of a raft and asks her to swear “that you will not plan some other evil bane against me myself” (μή τί μοι αὐτῷ πῆμα κακὸν βουλευσέμεν ἄλλο, 5.179), and she swears it in his words (5.187; see [[oath]] and [[dolos]]). Poseidon, coming back from the Ethiopians, finds that the gods have taken a new counsel in his absence: “Ah, truly the gods have changed their purpose / about Odysseus, while I was among the Ethiopians” (μετεβούλευσαν, 5.286–287; see [[poseidon]] and [[o-popoi]]). The compound is found only here in Homer.
