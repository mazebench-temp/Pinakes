summary: Calypso sets her looks against Penelope's, “not in form or stature, since it is in no way fitting / for mortal women to vie with immortals in form and looks” (5.212–213), and Odysseus grants that his wife “is less than you in looks and stature to behold” (5.217). From the raft the land of the Phaeacians “looked like a shield on the misty sea” (5.281).
grc: [ἐείσατο]
en: [looked like, came into his sight, appear, seemed]
---

**Looks.** On the last evening Calypso rests her offer on her form: “Surely I claim to be in no way inferior to her, / not in form or stature, since it is in no way fitting / for mortal women to vie with immortals in form and looks” (δέμας καὶ εἶδος ἐρίζειν, 5.211–213; see [[immortality]] and [[beauty-and-its-price]]). The words are those in which Agamemnon set Chryseis above his wife, “since she is in no way inferior to her, / not in form or stature, not in mind or in handiwork” (*Iliad* 1.114–115); Calypso leaves out the mind and the handiwork. Odysseus grants her the whole comparison: “prudent Penelope / is less than you in looks and stature to behold; / for she is mortal, and you are immortal and ageless” (5.216–218; see [[penelope]]). ἀκιδνοτέρη is a word of uncertain meaning, “weaker” or “meaner” in the scholia (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 5.217; see the translation's note on 5.216–217), and Merry notes that where Calypso spoke of beauty Odysseus speaks of his wife's wisdom (on 5.216). He will use the same half-verse of himself to Alcinous: he is not like the immortals “in form or stature” (7.210).

**Seeming.** The aorist of εἴδομαι is the way things look from the sea. The land of the Phaeacians “looked like a shield on the misty sea” (εἴσατο δʼ ὡς ὅτε ῥινόν, 5.281; see [[scheria]] and the translation's note on 5.281); Poseidon sees Odysseus from far away, “for he came into his sight / sailing over the sea” (5.283–284; see [[poseidon]]); “so welcome did the land and the woods appear to Odysseus” as a father's life to his children (ἀσπαστὸν ἐείσατο, 5.398; see [[the-fathers-recovery]]); and at the river's mouth “the place seemed to him the best” (5.442; see [[the-river-of-scheria]]).
