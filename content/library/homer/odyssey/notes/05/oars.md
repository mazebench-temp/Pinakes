---
summary: Odysseus has “no ships with oars and no companions”, and Calypso has none to give him (5.16, 5.141); the raft he builds has a steering oar but no oars, and the Phaeacians, to whom Athena drives him, are “lovers of the oar” (5.255, 5.386).
---

ἐπήρετμος, “with oars”, goes with the companions who pull them: “for he has no ships with oars and no companions / who might take him over the broad back of the sea” (5.16–17 = 4.559–560), and Calypso answers Hermes in the same verses, “for I have no ships with oars and no companions” (5.141–142; see [[ships]] and [[calypso]]). In Book 2 it is said of the companions themselves, who “sit at the oars, waiting for you to set out” (2.403). The raft that Odysseus builds alone has a mast, a sail and a steering oar, and no oars are named (5.254–260; see [[raft]] and [[steering-oar]]). In the storm Athena drives him toward the Phaeacians, “lovers of the oar” (φιληρέτμοισι, 5.386; see [[lovers-of-the-oar]] and [[phaeacians]]).
