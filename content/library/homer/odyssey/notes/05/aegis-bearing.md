---
summary: Hermes, and Calypso after him: “there is no way that the mind of aegis-bearing Zeus / can be evaded by any other god, or made vain” (5.103–104, 5.137–138).
---

Calypso yields in the messenger's words, turning his statement into her reason, “But since there is no way” (5.137–139; [[the-mind-of-zeus]], [[hermes]], [[calypso]]).
