---
summary: The Achaeans are the army that would have honored Odysseus had he died at Troy: “Then I would have had my funeral rites, and the Achaeans would have spread my fame” (5.311). In Hermes' account, unnamed, they are the men who fought around the city of Priam and sinned against Athena on their homecoming (5.106–109).
# Unnamed: "the men who fought around the city of Priam" and went home (5.106-108).
refs: [5.106-108]
---

**Rites and fame.** τῷ κʼ ἔλαχον κτερέων, καί μευ κλέος ἦγον Ἀχαιοί (5.311; see [[the-fight-over-the-body-of-achilles]] and [[kleos]]). κλέος ἦγον is “would have spread my fame”, carrying on the force of κε, or, as others take it, “would have brought back tidings of me” (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 5.311; see the translation's note on 5.311). It is what Telemachus said the Achaeans would have done for his father had he fallen at Troy: “Then all the Achaeans would have made a tomb for him, / and he would have won great fame for his son too in days to come” (1.239–240; see [[fame-and-the-tomb]] and [[honoring-the-dead]]). The Danaans of 5.306 and the Achaeans of 5.311 are the same army, named twice in six verses (see [[danaans]]).

**The army that went home.** Hermes does not name them: they are “the men who fought around the city of Priam / for nine years, and in the tenth sacked the city and went / home”, and sinned against Athena on their homecoming (5.106–109; see [[the-returns-of-the-achaeans]] and [[the-fall-of-troy]]).
