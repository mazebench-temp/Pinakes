---
summary: Calypso tells Hermes that she meant to make Odysseus “immortal and ageless all his days” (5.136), and Odysseus, refusing her last offer, grants that she is “immortal and ageless” and his wife is not (5.218).
grc: ["ἀθάνατον καὶ ἀγήραον"]
---

The formula that Hector used for the impossible (*Iliad* 8.538–541) is here first an offer and then a concession. Calypso, protesting to Hermes that the gods begrudge her a mortal husband, says that she loved and cared for Odysseus, “and I said / that I would make him immortal and ageless all his days” (5.135–136); Aristarchus read ἀγήρων for ἀγήραον (Merry's critical note; see the translation's note on 5.136). On the last evening Odysseus gives her the words back: “for she is mortal, and you are immortal and ageless” (5.218), and still he wishes to go home (5.219–220; see [[immortality]] and [[nostos]]). Hector wished to be what Calypso is; Odysseus is offered it and will not take it. The verse of the offer comes back twice in the poem, when Odysseus tells Arete of Calypso and when he tells Penelope his wanderings (7.257, 23.336).
