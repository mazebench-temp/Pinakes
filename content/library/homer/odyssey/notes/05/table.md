---
summary: Calypso sets a table beside Hermes with her own hands and fills it with ambrosia (5.92–93).
---

“So she spoke, and the goddess set a table beside him, / filling it with ambrosia, and mixed red nectar” (5.92–93; see [[ambrosia]], [[nectar]] and [[xenia]]). In the houses of men a handmaid draws up the table beside the guest, in the verse παρὰ δὲ ξεστὴν ἐτάνυσσε τράπεζαν, “and she drew up a polished table beside them” (1.138, 4.54), and bread and meat are set on it (1.139–142); the goddess serves the god herself, and the table holds the gods' food. The Homeric table is small, one for each diner (see the entry): Hermes eats alone at his, and on the last evening Calypso sets the food before Odysseus without a table being named (5.196–199; see [[feast-type-scene]]).
