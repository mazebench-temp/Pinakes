# ἐπίκριον, the yard (5.254, 5.318), found only in these two verses; ἱστόν at 5.62 is the loom, excepted in the entry.
grc: [ἐπίκριον]
en: [yard]
---

**The mast and the yard.** After the hull and the deck, “he made a mast in it, and a yard fitted to it” (5.254; see [[the-building-of-the-raft]] and [[raft]]). The mast of a ship was usually of fir, as Telemachus' is (2.424), and was stepped into the solid wood of the keel, so that when the mast of Odysseus' ship is snapped off at its foot the wave breaks it “against the keel” (12.422; W. W. Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, Appendix I §12). The ἐπίκριον is the yard from which the square sail hung. Eustathius explained the name as “being on the ἴκρια”, the deck, which does not express its usual position; Merry would rather derive it from ἄκρον, “top” (Appendix I §14; see [[deck]] and [[sails]]). ἄρμενον αὐτῷ, “fitted to it”, is the participle that fits the axe to Odysseus' hands (5.234; see [[axe]]).

**Broken.** In the storm a great wave whirls the raft round and throws Odysseus from it, “and his mast was broken in the middle / by a dread storm of winds clashing together that came upon it, / and far off the sail and the yard fell into the sea” (5.316–318; see [[steering-oar]]). The wreck of his ship after Thrinacia was told otherwise: there the storm broke both forestays and the mast fell back, its tackle spilling into the hold, and later a wave snapped it off at the keel, and Odysseus lashed mast and keel together with the backstay and rode on them (12.409–425, a plain rendering; see [[forestays]] and [[the-cattle-of-helios]]). The raft's mast is gone after the first wave, and the hull holds until the second (5.366–370).

**Loom and mast.** ἱστός, “the upright”, is the loom at which Calypso weaves earlier in the book (5.62; see [[loom]]).
