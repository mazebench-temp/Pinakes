---
summary: Ino, “who was once a mortal with human voice, / but now in the open sea has a share of honor from the gods” (5.334–335).
grc: [αὐδήεσσα]
en: [with human voice]
---

The ancients doubted the word. Merry reports Eustathius' explanations, “renowned” or “famous,” and Aristophanes', “speaking with human voice,” which he prefers, and a variant οὐδήεσσα, “earthly” (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 5.334); the translation's note on 5.334 adds that the scholia give that variant to Aristotle. In later books the same word ends the verse of Circe and of Calypso, “a dread goddess with human voice” (10.136, 12.449). See [[leucothea]].
