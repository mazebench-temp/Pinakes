summary: Athena tells the gods “the many sorrows of Odysseus” (κήδεα, 5.5): he lies on an island “suffering bitter sorrows” (5.13), and on Calypso's shore racks his heart “with tears and groans and sorrows” (5.83, 5.157). Calypso warns him of the sorrows it is his lot to fill up before he reaches home, and in the storm he fears that her words are being fulfilled (5.206–207, 5.300–302). Ino pities him “suffering sorrows” (5.336), and the father of the simile lies in sickness “suffering bitter pains” (5.395).

**The sorrows of Odysseus.** The second council begins with them: “And Athena told them of the many sorrows of Odysseus, / remembering them” (5.5–6; see [[the-council-of-the-gods]]). The word there is κήδεα, the cares and griefs of κῆδος, which the translation renders “sorrows” as it renders ἄλγεα, and which is counted here with them (so too at 1.244 and 4.108). “But he lies on an island, suffering bitter sorrows”, she goes on (κρατέρʼ ἄλγεα πάσχων, 5.13), in the verse that told of Philoctetes left on Lemnos, “But he lay on an island, suffering bitter pains” (*Iliad* 2.721; see [[philoctetes]]).

**On the shore.** Twice the narrator shows him on Calypso's shore in the same two verses, “racking his heart with tears and groans and sorrows. / He would look out over the barren sea, letting tears fall” (5.83–84 = 5.157–158; see [[tears]] and [[lament]]). ἐρέχθω, “rack”, is in the Iliad the buffeting of a ship by the winds (*Iliad* 23.317; see the translation's table of key words and [[cunning-not-strength]]).

**The measure.** Calypso tells him that if he knew in his mind “what a measure of / sorrows it is your lot to fill up before you reach your native land”, he would stay with her (κήδεʼ ἀναπλῆσαι, 5.206–207; see [[aisa]] and [[immortality]]). In the storm he remembers her words with ἄλγεʼ for κήδεʼ: “I fear that everything the goddess said was true, / when she said that on the sea, before I reached my native land, / I would fill up the measure of sorrows; and now it is all being fulfilled” (5.300–302; see [[three-times-blessed]]). ἀναπίμπλημι is to fill a measure to the brim, as the Achaeans “will fill up the measure of an evil fate and perish” (*Iliad* 8.34).

**Suffering.** Ino sees him “as he wandered, suffering sorrows”, and pities him (5.336; see [[pity]] and [[leucothea]]); on the raft he resolves to stay “and endure, suffering sorrows”, as long as the beams hold (5.361–362; see [[much-enduring]]). When he sees the land, the simile gives his sorrows to a father: the land is as welcome to him as a father's life to his children, when he “lies in sickness, suffering bitter pains, / long wasting away, and a hateful god has attacked him” (5.394–397; see [[the-fathers-recovery]] and [[daimon]]). The verse is Athena's verse of Odysseus on the island, with νούσῳ, “sickness”, for νήσῳ, “island” (5.13, 5.395).
