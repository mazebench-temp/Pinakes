---
summary: Calypso's formula eleven times: “Calypso, brilliant among goddesses” in nine verses, from her welcome of Hermes (5.78, 5.85) to her command to keep the Bear on his left hand (5.276), and “brilliant among goddesses” alone when she speaks to Odysseus on the shore and leads him to the cave (5.159, 5.192).
---

Καλυψώ, δῖα θεάων fills the second half of the verse at each turn of her part: she knows Hermes when she sees him and questions him (5.78, 5.85), shudders at the command of Zeus (5.116), smiles at Odysseus' demand for an oath (5.180), begins to speak at their last supper (5.202), goes home while he cuts the trees, and brings him the augers and the cloth for the sails (5.242, 5.246, 5.258). At 5.192 the formula takes the place of Pallas Athena in the couplet of a goddess who leads the way (2.405; [[followed-in-the-footprints]]); 5.202 is the Iliad's verse of Nestor, “began to speak among them” (*Iliad* 2.433), with the plural kept though only Odysseus is there (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 5.202). When she sends him off, and in the verse of the garments she gave him, she is “brilliant Calypso” (5.263, 5.321, 5.372; [[brilliant]]). See [[calypso]].
