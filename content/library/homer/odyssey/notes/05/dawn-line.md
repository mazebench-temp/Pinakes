---
summary: Three dawns: the dawn of the council, “Now Dawn rose from her bed beside noble Tithonus” (5.1 = Iliad 11.1), the whole verse of the morning on which the building begins (5.228), and the day-count verse of the third day in the sea, “But when Dawn of the lovely tresses brought the third day” (5.390); and the cold breeze from the river “in the early dawn” (5.469).
grc: ["Ἠὼς δʼ ἐκ λεχέων", "τρίτον ἦμαρ ἐυπλόκαμος τέλεσʼ Ἠώς", "ἠῶθι πρό"]
en: [Dawn rose from her bed, brought the third day, in the early dawn]
---

5.1 is, as Merry says, a poetical way of saying “the next morning”; the poem does not say which morning (see the translation's note on 5.1). At 5.390 Merry takes τέλεσε not as “ending” but as “bringing into full and perfect action”: when daylight has fully come, dawn may be said to be over (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 5.390). The day-count verse returns in Odysseus' tales (9.76, 10.144). See [[eos]], [[early-born]] and [[rosy-fingered]].
