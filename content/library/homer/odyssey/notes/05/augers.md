---
summary: Calypso brings Odysseus augers while he hews the timbers, and he bores every piece and fits them to one another before he pegs them together (5.246–248).
---

“Meanwhile Calypso, brilliant among goddesses, brought him augers; / and he bored them all and fitted them to one another, / and hammered it together with pegs and fastenings” (5.246–248; see [[calypso]], [[the-building-of-the-raft]] and [[raft]]). τόφρα, “meanwhile”, marks her coming while he works, as she will come again with the cloth for the sails (5.258). He bores the pieces so that the hole in the upper one matches the one in the lower, and with the holes in line he drives home the pegs (W. W. Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 5.247–248). The grammarian Aristophanes thought that the two verses say the same thing (the scholia, in Merry's critical note on 5.247–248).
