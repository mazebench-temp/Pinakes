---
summary: The Danaans who died at Troy perished “doing a favor to the sons of Atreus” (5.307), and Calypso's mantle is “fine and lovely” (χαρίεν, 5.231).
grc: [χαρίεν]
en: [doing a favor, lovely]
---

χάρις is the favor a service does. In the storm Odysseus calls the Danaans who died at Troy blessed: they perished there χάριν Ἀτρεΐδῃσι φέροντες, “doing a favor to the sons of Atreus” (5.306–307; see [[three-times-blessed]] and [[atreidae]]). It is the phrase with which Achilles bids Phoenix not to trouble his heart “to do a favor to the hero son of Atreus” (*Iliad* 9.612–613), and with which Pandarus says that he led the Trojans to Ilios “to do a favor to brilliant Hector” (*Iliad* 5.210–211). The war was a favor done for the sons of Atreus, and the men who died in it had what the drowning man has not, a death in war. The adjective is the word of a thing's charm: on the morning of his departure the nymph puts on a great silver-white mantle, λεπτὸν καὶ χαρίεν, “fine and lovely” (5.230–231 = 10.543–544, of Circe; see [[cloak]] and [[dressing-scene]]).
