---
summary: Poseidon twice: seeing Odysseus on his raft near the land of the Phaeacians, “he shook his head and spoke to his own heart” (5.285), and again when the raft is broken and Odysseus leaps into the sea (5.376).
---

The first soliloquy is anger that the gods have changed their purpose while he was among the Ethiopians, and a promise to give Odysseus his fill of misery (5.286–290); the second sends him to wander until he comes among the Phaeacians (5.377–379). Merry takes the threat as sarcasm, as if nothing short of the extremity of suffering would satisfy Odysseus (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 5.379).
