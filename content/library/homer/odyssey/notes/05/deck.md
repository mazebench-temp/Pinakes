---
summary: Calypso bids Odysseus fix a deck on his raft “high up”, and he sets it up on close-set ribs before he finishes the hull with long side planks (5.163–164, 5.252–253).
---

**High up.** Calypso's order is to “fit together / a broad raft; and fix a deck upon it / high up, so that it may carry you over the misty sea” (5.162–164; see [[raft]] and [[calypso]]). ὑψοῦ is added because the decks are the highest part of the hull, and εὐρεῖαν and ὑψοῦ stand first in their verses for emphasis: the vessel is to be broad in the beam and to have plenty of freeboard. Brieger would put a comma after αὐτῆς and join ὑψοῦ with φέρῃσιν, “carry you high and dry” (W. W. Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 5.164).

**On the ribs.** When the beams are bored and pegged together, “setting up the deck, fitting it to the close-set ribs, / he worked on; and he finished with long side planks” (5.252–253; see [[the-building-of-the-raft]]). Merry translates “And he worked away, setting up the decks by fitting them to the close-set ribs, and he finished off with the long side-planking” (on 5.252): ἀραρών is subordinate to στήσας and shows how the decks could be set up, by pinning their planks to the ribs (Appendix I §5). The ancient interpreters were divided. Eustathius explained ἴκρια as the deck at the stern where the helmsman sits and the deck that follows it, but, puzzled by ἵστημι, “set up”, which seems to call for upright timbers, he offered the curved ribs as another reading; some scholia take ἴκρια here as the uprights to which the decks are fixed, and Brieger and Hentze made the ἴκρια the ribs and the σταμῖνες their stays (Merry, Appendix I §5). The translation follows Merry, with “deck” for the ἴκρια (see its note on 5.252–253).

**The order of the work.** In Merry's account the deck is the last part of the hull, after the keel and the ribs: “He begins the σχεδίη by laying the keel, and finishes off with the ἴκρια” (on 5.164). The book names no keel for the raft; the keel of Odysseus' own ship, which he bestrode after Zeus' thunderbolt, is the one Calypso remembers (5.130).
