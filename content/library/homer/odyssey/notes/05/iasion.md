Calypso tells the story to Hermes as proof that the gods begrudge goddesses their mortal husbands: “So too when Demeter of the lovely tresses, with Iasion, / yielding to her own heart, lay with him in love and bed / in a thrice-plowed fallow field; and not long without hearing of it / was Zeus, who killed him, striking him with a gleaming thunderbolt” (5.125–128; see [[demeter-and-iasion]], [[demeter]] and [[zeus]]). Orion, the first example, was killed by Artemis (5.121–124; see [[dawn-and-orion]]); Iasion is killed by Zeus himself, the god who now orders Calypso to give up Odysseus (see [[the-jealousy-of-the-gods]] and [[calypso]]). ᾧ θυμῷ εἴξασα marks the goddess's own choice, not compulsion (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 5.126), and the thrice-plowed field is the νειός, the fallow that had been plowed three times in the year (Merry on 5.127; see [[thrice-plowed]]).
