---
summary: Merry finds the figure twice in the book: Odysseus “put on a cloak and a tunic” (5.229), and Calypso sends him off “when she had clothed him in fragrant garments and bathed him” (5.264).
refs: [5.229, 5.264]
---

Merry explains the first as natural, since the cloak, which strikes the eye, is named before the tunic that is put on first, and refers the second to it (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 5.229, 5.264). See [[dressing-scene]] and [[bathing]].
