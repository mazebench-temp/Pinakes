---
summary: Odysseus makes a steering oar for his raft, steers with it skillfully, sitting, through seventeen sleepless nights, and lets it fly from his hands when the first wave strikes (5.255, 5.270–271, 5.315–316).
---

**Made and used.** After the mast and the yard Odysseus makes the steering oar: “and he made a steering oar besides, to steer with” (5.255; see [[the-building-of-the-raft]] and [[raft]]). When Calypso has sent him off, “Glad of the wind, brilliant Odysseus spread his sails” (5.269), and he sails alone and steers by the stars:

{{quote:c270d5ba-7478-547c-b59c-e32a86badbbd}}

ἥμενος, “sitting there”, shows the helmsman seated at his oar (W. W. Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, Appendix I §17), and the Bear is to be kept ἐπʼ ἀριστερὰ χειρός, “on his left hand” (5.277), a phrase found only here in Homer with χειρός, which Merry thinks may refer picturesquely to the hand of the steersman, the center from which the directions seem to diverge (on 5.277; see [[the-bear]] and [[pleiades]]). τεχνηέντως, “with skill”, answers the skill with which he made the raft and its sails (εὖ τεχνήσατο, 5.259).

**Lost.** When Poseidon's wave whirls the raft round, “He himself fell far from the raft, and the steering oar / he let fly from his hands” (5.315–316). προέηκε, “tossed it from his hand”, describes the suddenness of the act more graphically than μεθέηκε, “let it go”, would (Merry on 5.316). The mast breaks in the same moment (5.316–318; see [[mast]]).
