---
summary: Odysseus supplicates the river in words, “as I now / come to your stream and to your knees, after toiling much. / But pity me, lord; I claim to be your suppliant” (5.448–450).
refs: [5.445-450]
---

The approach is the swim to the river's mouth, the gesture is in the words, “to your knees,” since the god is the stream itself, and the answer is the calm that brings him safe ashore (5.441–453; [[supplication]], [[prayer-type-scene]], [[the-river-of-scheria]]).
