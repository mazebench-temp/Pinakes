---
summary: The island where Calypso holds Odysseus is not named in this book. Hermes comes to it “far away”, over boundless salt water with no city of men near (5.55, 5.100–102), and Odysseus leaves it on his raft on the fifth day, sailing east with the Bear on his left hand (5.263–277).
# The island is only νῆσος in Book 5 (5.13, 5.55, 5.60, 5.238, 5.263); its name is in 1.85, 6.172, 7.244, 7.254,
# 12.448 and 23.333.
refs: [5.13, 5.55, 5.60, 5.238, 5.263]
---

**The errand at last.** In the first council Athena proposed two things: that Hermes be sent “to the island of Ogygia” to tell the nymph the gods' counsel, and that she herself go to Ithaca and rouse Telemachus (1.81–95). Her own part has been done, and six days have passed, Merry reckons, without Hermes' being sent (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, introductory note to Book 5; see [[the-council-of-the-gods]] and [[the-journey-of-telemachus]]). Now Zeus gives the command in the words of Book 1, “tell the nymph of the lovely tresses our unerring counsel, / the homecoming of stout-hearted Odysseus” (5.30–31, as 1.86–87; see [[hermes]] and [[calypso]]), but the island's name is not repeated. In this book it is only “an island”, first in Athena's plea, which takes up Proteus' words of Odysseus in the halls of Calypso (5.13–17; 4.556–560).

**Far from men.** Hermes “came to the island, which lay far away” (5.55), and stepping out of the violet sea “onto the land”, ἤπειρόνδε, went on to the cave (5.56–57; see [[calypsos-cave]]). ἤπειρος is land as against sea, here the land of an island (Merry on 5.56), as the Phaeacian crew step out ἤπειρόνδε on Ithaca (13.116). Hermes complains to Calypso of the road: “who of his own will would speed across so much salt water, / boundless? Nor is there any city of mortals near, men who to the gods / offer sacrifices and choice hecatombs” (5.100–102). οὐδέ τις … πόλις is a demonstrative clause standing for a relative one, “where there is no city” (Merry on 5.101). Nor is there any way off the island: Calypso has “no ships with oars and no companions” to send him (5.141–142, in Athena's words of 5.16–17), and the timber for his raft comes from “the far edge of the island, where tall trees grew” (5.238; see [[the-building-of-the-raft]] and [[trees]]).

**The way to Scheria.** On the fifth day Calypso sends him “from the island” (5.263). She had bidden him keep the Bear on his left hand as he crossed the sea (5.276–277; see [[the-bear]]); with the northern stars on his left he sails east, and the mountains of the Phaeacians' land rise on the eighteenth day (5.278–281; see [[scheria]]). Odysseus gives Arete the same reckoning, seventeen days at sea and the shadowy mountains on the eighteenth (7.267–268).
