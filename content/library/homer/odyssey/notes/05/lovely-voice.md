---
summary: Hermes finds Calypso within, “singing with a lovely voice” as she walks to and fro at the loom (5.61–62).
---

ἀοιδιάουσʼ ὀπὶ καλῇ: the goddess sings at her weaving, as Circe does when Odysseus' companions come to her house (10.221). Merry quotes Bothe's remark that the verb ἀοιδιάω seems by its very sound to express the sweetness of the nymph's song (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 5.61). See [[calypso]] and [[loom]].
