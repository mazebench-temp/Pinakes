---
summary: Immortality runs through Calypso's part: the meadows where “even an immortal” would gaze (5.73), “the gods are not unknown to one another, / the immortals” (5.79–80), her offer to make Odysseus immortal (5.136, 5.209), and his answer, “for she is mortal, and you are immortal and ageless” (5.218).
---

The word frames the book's choice: Calypso would have him “be immortal” if he stayed (5.208–209), and Odysseus sets the mortal wife against the immortal goddess (5.211–220). Elsewhere it is the gods: the dawn that brings light “to the immortals and to mortals” (5.2), the immortal he fears is weaving a trap for him (5.356–357), and the gods to whom a wanderer is to be respected (5.447).
