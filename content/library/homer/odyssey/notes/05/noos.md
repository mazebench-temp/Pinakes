summary: Zeus reminds Athena that the plan of vengeance was her own (“Was it not you yourself who devised this plan”, 5.23), and Hermes and Calypso say that the mind of Zeus cannot be evaded (5.103, 5.137). Calypso owns that the gods are stronger than she “both to devise and to fulfill” (5.170), and swears that she is “thinking of” only his good, “for my mind too is right” (5.188–190). On the raft Odysseus finds “nothing better to think of beforehand” (5.364), and Athena “thought of another thing” (5.382).
grc: [νοέω, ἐνόησεν, προνοῆσαι]
en: [this plan, thinking of, think of beforehand]
---

**The plan of the gods.** At the second council Zeus answers Athena's plea with a question: “Was it not you yourself who devised this plan, / that Odysseus should come and take vengeance on those men?” (5.23–24; see [[the-council-of-the-gods]] and [[poine]]). νόος is here the plan the mind forms. Merry notes that Athena had not expressed this νόος at the first council, where she spoke only of his return: the thought is first Nestor's, in his words of comfort to Telemachus, and comes back in Teiresias' answer to Odysseus (*Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 5.23–24; 3.216, 11.118; see the translation's note on 5.23–24). Zeus will ask her the same question in the same verses at the end of the poem (24.479–480). The mind of Zeus is the plan that no other god can evade, as Hermes tells Calypso and she repeats (5.103, 5.137; see [[the-mind-of-zeus]]).

**Calypso's mind.** She sends Odysseus off “if the gods are willing, who hold the wide sky, / who are stronger than I am both to devise and to fulfill” (νοῆσαί τε κρῆναί τε, 5.169–170; see [[calypso]]), and when he asks for an oath she swears that she plots no harm: “No, I am thinking of and will devise just what I would / plan for myself, if so great a need should come upon me; / for my mind too is right” (5.188–190; see [[oath]] and [[aisa]]). Merry gives the sense, “but I am thinking of and will devise for you just that plan which I should contrive for mine own self, whenever the need might come so sore on me” (on 5.188).

**In the storm.** Odysseus weighs Ino's counsel against his own and decides to stay on the raft while it holds, and then to swim, “since there is nothing better to think of beforehand” (προνοῆσαι, 5.364; see [[dolos]]). Athena, when Poseidon has gone home, “thought of another thing” (ἄλλʼ ἐνόησεν, 5.382; see [[thought-of-another-thing]] and [[athena]]): she binds the other winds and rouses the North Wind to carry him to the Phaeacians (5.383–387).
