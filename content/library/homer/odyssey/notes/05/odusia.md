---
summary: One of the surviving lines of the Latin Odyssey renders a line of Book 5, the verse in which Odysseus' knees and heart are loosened when Poseidon's storm breaks over the raft: “Igitur demum Ulixi cor frixit prae pavore.”
refs: [5.297]
---

The line is given as The Latin Library prints it, with the line of Book 5 that it sets beside it; the collection does not name its ancient source.

- ***Igitur demum Ulixi cor frixit prae pavore.*** “Then at last the heart of Ulixes froze for fear” (5.297): “And then Odysseus' knees and dear heart were loosened” (see [[loosened-his-limbs]]). *Igitur demum*, “then at last”, answers καὶ τότε; *Ulixi* is the Latin name of Odysseus, Ulixes (see [[odysseus]]); *cor* is the dear heart, ἦτορ, and the knees are left out. Where Homer's verb is λύτο, “were loosened”, the Latin has *frixit*, “froze”, and gives the cause, *prae pavore*, “for fear”. The verse returns when Odysseus sees the reefs of Scheria (5.406). Virgil, telling his own storm after this one, keeps Homer's verb and gives it the cold: Aeneas' limbs are “loosened with cold”, *solvuntur frigore membra* (*Aeneid* 1.92; see [[three-times-blessed]]).
