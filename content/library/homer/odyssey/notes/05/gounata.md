summary: Odysseus' knees and dear heart are loosened when the storm breaks and when he sees the reefs of Scheria (5.297, 5.406); he comes “to your stream and to your knees” as the river's suppliant (5.449), and when it has brought him in “he let both his knees sink, / and his strong hands” (5.453–454).
grc: ["γούνατʼ"]
---

The knees carry a man's strength, and his fear loosens them: twice “Odysseus' knees and dear heart were loosened”, when the storm breaks over the raft and when he finds no landing on the reefs of Scheria (5.297 = 5.406; see [[loosened-his-limbs]] and [[deos]]). As a suppliant he comes to the knees of the god of the river, which he can only name (5.449; see [[supplication]]). And at the river's mouth, the sea's work done, “he let both his knees sink, / and his strong hands; for his dear heart was overcome by the sea” (5.453–454). The picture is of an exhausted man, his arms dropping at his sides and his knees bending under him, as in the common phrase λύτο γούνατα, not of the rest after battle that γόνυ κάμψαι means in the Iliad (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 5.453, comparing *Iliad* 7.118; see the translation's note on 5.453–454).
