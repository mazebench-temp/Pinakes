---
summary: Zeus' message to Calypso (5.29–42) is delivered by Hermes with changes (5.97–115), and Calypso passes it on to Odysseus as her own kindness (5.160–170).
passages:
  - 5.29-42 | Zeus' message
  - 5.97-115 | Hermes delivers it
---

Only the last two verses are delivered as given, the fate “to see his friends and to reach / his high-roofed house” (5.41–42 = 5.114–115, with ἀλλʼ ἔτι). Hermes leaves out the raft, the Phaeacians and the gifts, and adds his own unwillingness and the story of the Achaeans' return (5.99–111; see the translation's note on 5.105–111). Calypso tells Odysseus nothing of Zeus: “for now with a ready heart I will send you away” (5.161), with only a hint of the gods' will (5.169–170), and he suspects a trap (5.173–179). Merry reports Hermann's rule that messages are among the few things Homer repeats word for word (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 5.7; [[messenger-scene]], [[hermes]]).
