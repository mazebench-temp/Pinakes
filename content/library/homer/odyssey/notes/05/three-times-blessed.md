---
summary: When the storm breaks over the raft, Odysseus cries that the Danaans who perished in wide Troy were three and four times blessed, and wishes he had died there on the day the Trojans hurled their spears at him around the dead son of Peleus: “but now it was fated that I be caught by a wretched death” (5.299–312).
---

The cry is the saying itself, and the article tells its scene: the storm Poseidon raises (5.282–298), the fear that Calypso's words are coming true (5.300–302), and the reckoning of a death at Troy, with its funeral rites and its fame, against a death at sea, which has neither (5.306–312; see [[kleos]] and [[fame-and-the-tomb]]). The wave breaks on him as he speaks (5.313), and in the next book, ashore, he will use the felicitation of the living (6.154–159).
