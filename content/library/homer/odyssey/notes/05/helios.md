---
summary: The sun sets on the last evening in Calypso's cave, and in the thicket where Odysseus sleeps on Scheria the shining sun never strikes with its rays.
---

**The last evening.** Odysseus has told Calypso that he will endure whatever a god sends on the sea: “So he spoke, and the sun went down and darkness came on” (5.225; see [[sunset-lines]] and [[calypsos-cave]]), and that night is their last together.

**The thicket.** The two bushes under which he sleeps grow so close that “neither the wet might of the blowing winds could pass, / nor did the shining sun ever strike them with its rays” (5.478–479; see [[olive]]). The verses, with the rain of 5.480, return nearly the same of the boar's lair on Parnassus (19.440–442; see [[boar]]).
