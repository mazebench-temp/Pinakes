---
summary: Hermes binds on his sandals for the journey (5.44–46), and on the morning the building begins Odysseus puts on a cloak and a tunic, and Calypso her mantle, girdle and head covering (5.228–232).
---

Merry notes the order “a cloak and a tunic,” though the tunic is put on first, and explains it as natural: the cloak, which strikes the eye, is named first ([[hysteron-proteron]]; Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 5.229). The goddess's dressing is Circe's in the same verses (5.230–232 = 10.543–545). See [[sandals]], [[cloak]], [[tunic]], [[girdle]] and [[veil]].
