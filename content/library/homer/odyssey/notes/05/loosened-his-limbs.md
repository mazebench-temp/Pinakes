summary: “And then Odysseus' knees and dear heart were loosened” twice: when the four winds fall on the raft (5.297), and when he sees the cliffs and reefs before the land (5.406).
---

The verse of terror, after Lycaon's before Achilles (*Iliad* 21.114) and Penelope's at Medon's news (4.703), returns in the hall when Odysseus sees the suitors armed (22.147). Each time here it is followed by a soliloquy ([[deeply-troubled]]).
