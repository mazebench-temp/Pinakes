---
summary: Hermes binds on his golden, ambrosial sandals to carry Zeus' command to Calypso, in the verses of his journey to Priam in the Iliad and of Athena's to Ithaca in Book 1 (5.44–46).
---

“At once he bound under his feet his fine sandals, / ambrosial, golden, which carried him over the waters / and over the boundless earth as fast as the blasts of the wind” (5.44–46; see [[hermes]], [[ambrosial]] and [[dressing-scene]]). The verses are those of Hermes' journey to Priam (*Iliad* 24.340–342), and of Athena's when she goes to Ithaca in the first book (1.96–98). A scholium, as W. W. Merry reports it, holds that the verses were wrongly carried from here to Athena's journey and to the Iliad, and another records the objection that the wand which follows is of no use here (*Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, critical note on 5.43–49; see [[wand]]). The sandals carry the god over sea and land; Homer does not say that they have wings, and Hermes' passage is told in other words: he flies, steps on Pieria, falls from the upper air upon the sea and skims the waves like a gull (5.49–54; see [[pieria]] and [[the-gull]]). πέτετο, “flew”, is used of any rapid movement, not only of birds (Merry on 5.49).
