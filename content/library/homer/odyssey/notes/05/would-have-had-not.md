---
summary: Twice on the rocks of Scheria: “There his skin would have been stripped off and his bones crushed together, / had not the gray-eyed goddess Athena put it in his mind” to seize the rock (5.426–427), and “Then unhappy Odysseus would have perished against fate, / had not gray-eyed Athena given him presence of mind” (5.436–437).
grc: ["ἔνθα κʼ ἀπὸ ῥινοὺς", "ἔνθα κε δὴ δύστηνος"]
---

The second names what is prevented, a death ὑπὲρ μόρον, “against fate,” as in the Iliad's first “had not,” when the Argives “would have had their homecoming against fate” (ὑπέρμορα, *Iliad* 2.155; [[moira]]). Each time Athena saves him through his own mind, with a thought and with presence of mind; Merry notes that the indicative λάβε, “he seized,” shows that the thought was acted on at once (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 5.427; [[gray-eyed]], [[athena]]).
