---
summary: At dawn “the nymph herself put on a great silver-white mantle” (5.230).
---

The robing of the goddess, ἀργύφεον φᾶρος μέγα, with a golden girdle and a head covering, is told in the same verses of Circe (5.230–232 = 10.543–545; [[dressing-scene]], [[cloak]], [[girdle]], [[veil]]). Merry gives the other form of the word, ἄργυφος (10.85), and reports Benfey's view that the ending comes from a root implying likeness, the one that forms the suffix of some diminutives (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 5.230). See [[calypso]].
