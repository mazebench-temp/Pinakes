---
summary: Calypso bathes Odysseus and clothes him in fragrant garments before she sends him from the island (5.264); on the voyage he steers by the Bear, which alone has no share in “the baths of Oceanus” (5.275).
---

**The bath before the voyage.** “and on the fifth brilliant Calypso sent him from the island, / when she had clothed him in fragrant garments and bathed him” (5.263–264; see [[calypso]] and [[fragrant]]). The verse names the clothing before the bath, the reversed order in which the cloak is named before the tunic (W. W. Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 5.264, referring to his note on 5.229; see [[cloak]]). In the Odyssey the bath belongs to the welcome of a guest (see the entry); here it is the farewell, with clothes, food and a fair wind (5.264–268; see [[provisions]] and [[xenia]]). The fragrant garments will weigh him down in the sea (5.321), and in the next book, on Scheria, he will wash the brine from his back and shoulders in the river himself (6.224–227).

**The baths of Oceanus.** The Bear, by which Calypso bade him steer, “turns in its place and watches Orion, / and alone has no share in the baths of Oceanus” (5.274–275 = *Iliad* 18.488–489; see [[the-bear]], [[alone-without-a-share]] and [[oceanus]]). The stars bathe when they set into the stream of Oceanus, and the Bear never sets (see the note on *Iliad* 18).
