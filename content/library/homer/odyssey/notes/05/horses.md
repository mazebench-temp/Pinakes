---
summary: When the raft is broken, Poseidon lashes his fine-maned horses and drives home to Aegae (5.380–381); and Odysseus, astride a single beam of the raft, sits it “as though riding a horse” (5.371).
---

Poseidon has watched Odysseus plunge into the sea with Ino's veil, and has taunted him: “So now, after suffering many evils, wander over the sea” (5.375–379). Then he “lashed the fine-maned horses, / and came to Aegae, where his glorious house is” (5.380–381; see [[poseidon]], [[fine-maned]] and [[aegae]]). The god's chariot over the sea is the one of the Iliad, where he yokes his bronze-hoofed horses at Aegae and drives over the waves to the Achaean ships while the sea beasts play around him (*Iliad* 13.21–31; see [[chariot-journey]]). Here the drive is the other way, home to Aegae, and the god leaves the scene of the book. A few verses earlier the horse is a likeness: Odysseus, his raft scattered by the wave, “bestrode a single beam, as though riding a horse” (5.371), one of the two places in Homer, W. W. Merry notes, that allude to riding on horseback (*Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 5.371; see [[like-a-rider]] and [[the-rider-of-four-horses]]).
