---
summary: Odysseus answers Calypso: “I know it myself, / all of it, that prudent Penelope / is less than you in looks and stature to behold” (5.215–217).
---

Merry notes that Calypso lays all the stress on Penelope's beauty, while Odysseus has the tact to speak only of his wife's wisdom, and in telling his longing speaks of his home, saying no more of Penelope (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 5.216; see the translation's note on 5.216–217).
