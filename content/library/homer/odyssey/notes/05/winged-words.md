---
summary: Calypso answers Hermes' message “with winged words” (5.117), and Odysseus answers her bidding to build a raft in the same verse (5.172).
---

Both times the verse follows a shudder, ὣς φάτο, ῥίγησεν δὲ … (5.116, 5.171), as Hera shudders at Zeus' threat in the Iliad (*Iliad* 15.34), and both speeches are protests: the goddess against the jealousy of the gods, the man against a goddess he suspects (5.118–144, 5.173–179; [[the-jealousy-of-the-gods]], [[oath-formulas]]).
