---
summary: The gods know one another at sight: Calypso knows Hermes the moment she looks at him, “for the gods are not unknown to one another”. A man sees less. Odysseus takes Poseidon's storm for the work of Zeus, mistrusts the goddess who appears to him on the raft, knows the river's god only in its current, and never sees Athena, who saves him twice.
# Unnamed: Calypso's recognition of Hermes (5.77-80); Odysseus' suspicion of "one of the immortals" (5.356-357);
# "he knew the god as he flowed forth" (5.444).
refs: [5.77-80, 5.356-357, 5.444]
---

**Gods among gods.** Hermes comes into the cave, “and when she looked him in the face / Calypso, brilliant among goddesses, did not fail to know him; / for the gods are not unknown to one another, / the immortals, not even if one lives in a house far away” (5.77–80; see [[hermes]] and [[calypso]]). The scholia say she knew him by a divine power, not because she had seen him before, and Merry takes the last words for Calypso's own case, on her distant island (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 5.77, 5.79; see the translation's notes on 5.77–78 and 5.80). Among men the poem's rule is the opposite: gods are hard to know, and are known late (see the general article). Hermes, for his part, is a god looking at what gods enjoy: the cave and its garden are a sight at which “even an immortal, if he came, / would gaze”, and he stands and gazes (5.73–76).

**A goddess on the raft.** Ino comes to Odysseus in the open, rising from the sea like a diving bird and sitting on his raft (5.337–338; see [[leucothea]] and [[like-a-diving-bird]]). He knows her for “one of the immortals”, and fears a trap because she bids him leave the raft: “may it not be that someone is weaving a trap for me again” (5.356–357), as he had feared some other plan in Calypso's bidding (5.173–179).

**A god in the current.** At the river's mouth “he knew the god as he flowed forth” (5.444), and prays to him without knowing who he is, “whoever you are” (5.445; see [[the-river-of-scheria]]). Merry: “he recognised him (sc. for a god) as he flowed forth” (on 5.444).

**Gods unseen.** Poseidon sees Odysseus from the mountains of the Solymi (5.282–284), but Odysseus does not see Poseidon, and blames the storm on Zeus (5.303–305; see [[poseidon]] and [[zeus]]). Athena acts for him unseen, in the winds, in his mind and in his sleep (5.382–387, 5.427, 5.437, 5.491–493; see [[athena]]). In the next book the narrator gives the reason she stays hidden: she does not yet appear to him face to face, out of respect for her father's brother, who rages against him until he reaches his own land (6.329–331).
