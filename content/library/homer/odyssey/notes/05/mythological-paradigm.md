---
summary: Calypso argues from two stories that the gods begrudge goddesses their mortal husbands: Dawn's Orion, whom Artemis killed in Ortygia, and Demeter's Iasion, whom Zeus struck with his thunderbolt (5.118–129).
passages:
  - 5.118-129 | Calypso's two examples
---

The examples are framed by the charge and its application, “You are hard, you gods, and jealous” and “And so now again you begrudge me, you gods” (5.118, 5.129; [[ring-composition]]). They are told not to persuade but to accuse: each ends with a god killing the mortal lover, and Calypso yields at once (5.137–144). See [[dawn-and-orion]], [[demeter-and-iasion]] and [[the-jealousy-of-the-gods]].
