# σχοίνῳ (5.463), the only σχοῖνος in Homer; the town Σχοῖνον (Iliad 2.497) is schoenus'.
grc: [σχοίνῳ]
---

“And he, withdrawing from the river, / sank down among the rushes, and kissed the grain-giving earth” (5.462–463; see [[the-river-of-scheria]] and [[grain-giving]]). σχοῖνος, the rush, is named only here in Homer, in a collective singular. The rushes are his first rest after two nights and two days in the sea, but he weighs the frost and the dew of the river at dawn against the wild beasts of the forest, and goes to the forest, which he finds near the water (5.464–477; see [[pondering-two-ways]] and [[olive]]).
