---
summary: The second council opens with the gods in their seats, “and among them / Zeus who thunders on high, whose power is the greatest” (5.3–4).
---

The verse-end οὗ τε κράτος ἐστὶ μέγιστον is the Iliad's for Zeus, in Agamemnon's mouth, “for his power is the greatest” (*Iliad* 2.118 = 9.25), and here it is the narrator's, at the head of the council that will send Odysseus home (5.3–4; see [[the-council-of-the-gods]], [[zeus]] and [[thunders-on-high]]). In Book 1 the same words gave Poseidon's son his power among the Cyclopes (1.70). The gathering is the gods' ordinary daily session in the hall of Zeus, not an assembly he calls (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 5.3; see the translation's note on 5.3).
