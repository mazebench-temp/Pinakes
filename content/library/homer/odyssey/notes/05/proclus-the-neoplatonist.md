---
summary: Proclus reads Hermes' flight “like a bird, a gull” as a change of form: as Athena is likened to Mentor, so Hermes is likened to the gull, a god descending through the orders below him.
---

- When one and the same god proceeds through the orders below it down to the last of them, the myths say that it changes into the form of the order it has reached: “for they say that Athena is likened to Mentor, Hermes to the gull and Apollo to the hawk” (*On the Republic* I 113 Kroll, on 5.51 and *Iliad* 15.237). A scholium on 5.51 takes the likeness otherwise, as one of the swoop and not of the form (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 5.54): [[athena-as-mentor-in-later-writers]], [[the-gull]] and [[hermes]].
