---
summary: On the shore of Scheria, weighing the river bank against the forest, Odysseus decides: “And as he pondered, this seemed to him the better way” (κέρδιον, 5.474).
---

Come ashore at night, Odysseus sets one danger against another: by the river the frost and the dew may overcome him in his weakness, and in the thickets up the slope, if he sleeps, he may become the prey of wild beasts (5.465–473; see [[hypnos]]). The recurring verse of deliberation ends the debate, ὣς ἄρα οἱ φρονέοντι δοάσσατο κέρδιον εἶναι (5.474, as *Iliad* 13.458; see [[decision-scene]]), and he goes to the forest. κέρδιον is the course that gains more, the advantage a man weighs when he must choose. The verse comes again at once in the next book, when he decides to entreat Nausicaa from a distance with gentle words rather than clasp her knees, lest she be angry (6.145–147; see [[supplication]]).
