# Unnamed: "lay with him in love and bed" (5.126).
refs: [5.126]
relations:
  - lover: iasion (5.125-126)
---

Calypso's second example of the gods' jealousy is a goddess like herself: “So too when Demeter of the lovely tresses, with Iasion, / yielding to her own heart, lay with him in love and bed / in a thrice-plowed fallow field” (5.125–127; see [[demeter-and-iasion]], [[iasion]] and [[the-jealousy-of-the-gods]]). ἐυπλόκαμος, “of the lovely tresses”, is the epithet Calypso has herself (5.58) and Dawn at 5.390 (see [[with-lovely-tresses]]); Zeus in the Iliad calls Demeter καλλιπλόκαμος, “of the lovely tresses, the queen” (*Iliad* 14.326). ᾧ θυμῷ εἴξασα is “yielding to her passion”, not compelled (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 5.126), and the place is a fallow field plowed three times in the year (Merry on 5.127; see [[thrice-plowed]]). Zeus, who once loved her himself (*Iliad* 14.326), kills the mortal with his thunderbolt (5.128; see [[zeus]]). In Hesiod the child of the union is Plutus, Wealth, born in Crete (*Theogony* 969–974; see the general article).
