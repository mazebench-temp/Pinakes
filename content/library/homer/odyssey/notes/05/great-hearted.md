summary: Three times on Calypso's island he is “great-hearted Odysseus”: Hermes does not find him within (5.81), the lady nymph goes to him (5.149), and she plans his escort (5.233); and four times in the storm the verse of his soliloquies, “and deeply troubled he spoke to his own proud heart” (5.298, 5.355, 5.407, 5.464).
grc: [μεγαλήτορα, μεγαλήτορι]
en: [proud]
---

Hermes found Calypso within, but not, as one might have thought, Odysseus too: he was weeping on the shore (5.81–84; Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 5.81). θυμὸς μεγαλήτωρ, “the proud heart,” is the heart of the Iliad's soliloquies, to which a man alone speaks when he must decide (*Iliad* 11.403; [[deeply-troubled]], [[thymos]]).
