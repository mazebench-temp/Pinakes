---
summary: In the simile of the father's recovery “a hateful god has attacked him” with sickness (5.396), and in the sea Odysseus fears that “a god may even send some great sea monster against me” (5.421).
---

δαίμων is a god whose work is felt but not named. The father of the simile lies in sickness, long wasting away, “and a hateful god has attacked him”, until the gods free him from his misery (5.395–397; see [[the-fathers-recovery]] and [[algos]]). ἔχραε, “assails”, is a gnomic aorist beside the present κεῖται, of the verb with which Telemachus says the suitors “have fallen upon” his mother (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 5.396; 2.50), and the sickness is of the kind the ancients thought beyond the reach of human skill, as the Cyclopes tell Polyphemus that there is no way to escape a sickness sent by great Zeus (Merry on 5.394; 9.411; see [[nousos]]). In the sea Odysseus fears for himself what the simile tells of the sick man: a god may send a great monster against him out of the sea, such as famous Amphitrite breeds in great numbers (5.421–422; see [[sea-beasts]] and [[amphitrite]]). He does not know which god is at work in the storm, and lays it on Zeus (5.303–304; see [[who-is-to-blame]]); in the next verse he names the god he fears, “for I know how the famous shaker of the earth is angry with me” (5.423; see [[poseidon]] and [[cholos]]).
