---
summary: Athena opens the second council with Mentor's verses: let no scepter-bearing king ever again be kind and gentle, since no one remembers Odysseus among the people he was lord over, “and he was kind, like a father” (5.8–12 = 2.230–234).
---

Athena's plea is made of verses already heard, and the first five are Mentor's to the men of Ithaca, when he rebuked them for sitting silent while the suitors wasted the house (5.8–12 = 2.230–234; see [[the-council-of-the-gods]] and the translation's note on 5.7–20). In the assembly they were a reproach to the people; before the gods they are a complaint about the reward of a good king. The σκηπτοῦχος βασιλεύς, “scepter-bearing king” (5.9; see [[scepter-bearing]]), should be gentle and kind and know in his mind what is due (see [[aisa]]); Odysseus was such a king over his people (ἄνασσε, 5.12; see [[anax]] and [[laos]]), “and he was kind, like a father” (see [[kind-as-a-father]] and [[kingship-and-authority]]), and no one remembers him. Merry allows that the borrowing may be an intentional recapitulation, in words the hearers of the earlier books knew, against Hermann's canon that Homer repeats only formulas and messages (*Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 5.7).
