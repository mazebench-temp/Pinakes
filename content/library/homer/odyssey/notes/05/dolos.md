---
summary: When Ino bids him leave the raft, Odysseus fears that “someone is weaving a trap for me again, / one of the immortals” (5.356–357), as he had suspected Calypso of planning “some other evil bane” against him (5.173–179).
---

Twice in the book a goddess offers Odysseus a way home, and twice he suspects a trick. To Calypso, who bids him build a raft, he answers that she is planning something else and not his escort, and asks for an oath (5.173–179; see [[oath]] and [[calypso]]); she laughs at him for a rogue, and Merry explains that, full of craft himself, he suspects a crafty intention in everyone else (*Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 5.182; see [[metis]]). To Ino, who bids him strip and swim with her veil, he says, “Ah me, may it not be that someone is weaving a trap for me again, / one of the immortals, since she bids me leave the raft” (μή τίς μοι ὑφαίνῃσιν δόλον αὖτε, 5.356–357; see [[leucothea]]). A δόλος is woven like a web or a plan: the king of Lycia “wove another cunning trap” for Bellerophon (*Iliad* 6.187), and in the Cyclops' cave Odysseus will weave “all kinds of tricks and plans” himself (9.422). This time he obeys only in part: he stays on the raft as long as its beams hold together, and takes to the water with the veil when the wave has broken it (5.358–375; see [[noos]]).
