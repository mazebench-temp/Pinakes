summary: "There is no way that the mind of aegis-bearing Zeus / can be evaded by any other god, or made vain", says Hermes to Calypso, and she gives his words back to him (5.103–104 = 5.137–138).
---

Hermes tells Calypso that he came at Zeus' bidding against his will: "But there is no way that the mind of aegis-bearing Zeus / can be evaded by any other god, or made vain" (5.103–104). She answers with the same verses, "But since there is no way that the mind of aegis-bearing Zeus / can be evaded by any other god, or made vain, / let him go to his ruin" (5.137–139; see [[aegis-bearing]], [[the-mind-of-zeus]], [[hermes]] and [[calypso]]).
