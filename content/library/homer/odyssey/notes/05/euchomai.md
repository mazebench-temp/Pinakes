---
summary: Calypso says, “Surely I claim to be in no way inferior to her” (5.211); Odysseus prays to the god of the river in his heart (5.444) and ends, “I claim to be your suppliant” (5.450).
---

The verb's senses stand side by side in the book. Calypso uses it of a claim: οὐ μέν θην κείνης γε χερείων εὔχομαι εἶναι, “Surely I claim to be in no way inferior to her, / not in form or stature” (5.211–212; see [[eidos]] and [[immortality]]). At the river's mouth Odysseus prays, εὔξατο ὃν κατὰ θυμόν, “and prayed to him in his heart” (5.444; see [[prayer]]), and in the same speech he claims: ἱκέτης δέ τοι εὔχομαι εἶναι, “I claim to be your suppliant” (5.450; see [[supplication]]), the claim that Eumaeus will make for him before Telemachus (16.67). εὔχομαι εἶναι is “claim to be”, as when Glaucus claims his lineage in the Iliad (*Iliad* 6.211; see the translation's table of key words), and the prayer entry does not count those two lines.
