summary: The Odyssey's standing name for its hero appears for the first time in the poem: “much-enduring brilliant Odysseus” shudders at Calypso's bidding to cross the sea on a raft (5.171), ponders Ino's advice (5.354), and rejoices at the bed of leaves (5.486).
grc: [πολύτλας]
---

Before Book 5 the poem gives him other titles, stout-hearted, divine, of many wiles; πολύτλας δῖος Ὀδυσσεύς, which the Iliad gives him five times (*Iliad* 8.97 and later), comes in with his ordeal at sea, and marks three turns of it: fear of the raft, doubt of the goddess who would save him, and rest. The first verse, “So she spoke, and much-enduring brilliant Odysseus shuddered,” is built like Hera's shudder at Zeus' threat (5.171; *Iliad* 15.34), and the last returns in the last book (5.486 = 24.504).
