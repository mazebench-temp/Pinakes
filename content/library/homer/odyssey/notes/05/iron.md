---
summary: Calypso ends her oath, “for my mind too is right, and my own heart / in my breast is not of iron, but full of pity” (5.190–191).
grc: [σιδήρεος]
---

The heart of iron is the Iliad's image for one that no prayer moves: the dying Hector finds one in Achilles, “for truly the heart in your breast is iron” (σιδήρεος ἐν φρεσὶ θυμός, *Iliad* 22.357), and Hecuba and Achilles find one in Priam, who dares to go to his sons' killer (*Iliad* 24.205, 24.521; see [[not-stone-or-iron]]). Telemachus used it at Sparta of his father, whom nothing would have saved “not even if the heart within him had been of iron” (4.293; see [[kradie]]). Calypso turns it to her own defense: her heart is not iron but ἐλεήμων, “full of pity” (5.190–191; see [[pity]] and [[oath]]), and Merry hears her setting her temper against that of the gods who have spitefully ordered Odysseus' departure (*Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 5.190). Penelope's heart will be called iron in her turn, when she will not believe that the stranger is her husband (23.172).
