summary: Three of Odysseus' soliloquies in the storm weigh his courses: to stay on the raft as long as it holds (5.356–364), to weigh landing on the rocks against swimming on along the shore, which the wave decides for him (5.408–425), and at the river to climb to the wood rather than lie by the water: “And as he pondered, this seemed to him the better way” (5.465–475).
grc: ["δοάσσατο κέρδιον εἶναι"]
passages:
  - 5.354-364 | Odysseus decides to stay on the raft
  - 5.464-475 | Odysseus chooses the wood
---

The last is built on the two-way form, εἰ μέν … εἰ δέ (5.466, 5.470), each course with its fear, and closes with the verse of the choice, as in the Iliad (*Iliad* 13.458), which the Odyssey uses six more times (6.145 and after). The first is a refusal: Ino has bidden him leave the raft, and he decides not to obey yet, “and it seems to me best” (5.356–364). In the second the choice is taken from him; Athena puts it in his mind to seize the rock (5.427; [[would-have-had-not]]). See [[pondering-two-ways]], [[deeply-troubled]] and [[kerdos]].
