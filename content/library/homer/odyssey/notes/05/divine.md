---
summary: Odysseus is “divine” twice: Athena complains that “no one remembers divine Odysseus” (5.11), and Calypso sits facing him at supper (5.198).
---

5.11 is Mentor's verse in the assembly, which Athena takes up with the four before it (5.8–12 = 2.230–234; see the translation's note on 5.7–20). At supper the man called divine eats such food as mortal men eat, and the slave women set ambrosia and nectar beside the goddess (5.196–200; [[ambrosia]], [[nectar]]).
