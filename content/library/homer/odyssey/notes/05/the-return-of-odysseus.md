summary: The return begins. At the second council Zeus fixes its course: Odysseus is to go without escort of gods or men, on a raft, to Scheria, and the Phaeacians will send him home with more than he would have brought from Troy. Calypso lets him go, he builds the raft and sails, and Poseidon's storm brings him, naked and alone, to the Phaeacians' shore.

**The decree.** In the first book the gods resolved that Odysseus should come home (1.76–87); in the fifth Zeus gives the way. Hermes is to tell Calypso "the homecoming of stout-hearted Odysseus, that he may return / with the escort neither of gods nor of mortal men; / but on a raft of many bindings, suffering woes, / on the twentieth day he may reach deep-soiled Scheria" (5.31–34), where the Phaeacians will honor him like a god and send him home in a ship, "giving him bronze and gold in plenty, and clothing, / a great store, more than Odysseus would ever have carried off from Troy" (5.38–39; see [[the-council-of-the-gods]], [[raft]], [[scheria]] and [[phaeacians]]). The prophecy is fulfilled in the thirteenth book, where the same gifts are counted on the shore of Ithaca (13.135–138).

**The longing.** Odysseus has been held seven years (7.259). Calypso finds him on the shore, his eyes never dry of tears, "as he mourned for his homecoming, since the nymph no longer pleased him" (5.153), and he answers her offer of immortality: "But even so I wish and yearn all my days / to come home and see the day of my homecoming" (5.219–220; see [[calypso]], [[immortality]] and [[day-of-homecoming]]).

**The voyage.** On the fifth day after he begins the raft Calypso sends him from the island (5.263; see [[the-building-of-the-raft]]), and he sails seventeen days by the stars until the mountains of the Phaeacians' land appear (5.270–281). Poseidon, seeing him so near "the great bounds of the misery that has come upon him" (5.289), wrecks the raft, and he reaches the land by swimming, with Ino's veil and the river's mercy (5.333–463; see [[poseidon]], [[leucothea]] and [[the-river-of-scheria]]). He comes to Scheria with nothing, as Zeus had said he would come to it "suffering woes" (5.33).
