---
summary: After the axe, Calypso gives Odysseus “a polished adze”, and with it he smooths the felled trees straight to the chalk line (5.237, 5.245).
---

“And then she gave him a polished adze” (5.237; see [[calypso]] and [[the-building-of-the-raft]]). ἐύξοον, “polished”, cannot be active, “smoothing”: it means that the adze had as good and smooth a handle as the axe, unless it refers to the high polish of the blade (W. W. Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 5.237; see [[polished]]). The adze is for squaring and smoothing, and its work begins with ξέσσε: “and smoothed them skillfully and made them straight to the chalk line” (5.245; Merry on 5.237 and 5.244; see [[the-chalk-line]] and [[axe]]).
