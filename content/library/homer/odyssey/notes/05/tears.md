---
summary: Hermes finds Odysseus not in the cave but weeping on the shore, racking his heart “with tears and groans and sorrows” and looking out over the barren sea, “letting tears fall” (5.81–84); Calypso finds him there again, his eyes “never / dry of tears” (5.151–158).
grc: [δάκρυσι, δακρυόφιν]
---

Odysseus' first appearance in the narrative of the poem is in tears. Hermes, in the cave, does not find him within: “he sat weeping on the shore, as he had before” (5.81–82), and the narrator gives two verses of him that return word for word when Calypso goes to find him, “racking his heart with tears and groans and sorrows. / He would look out over the barren sea, letting tears fall” (5.83–84 = 5.157–158; see [[algos]], [[shedding-tears]] and [[barren-sea]]). The scholia called the second verse superfluous in its first place, and Merry's text brackets it there (Merry's critical note; see the translation's note on 5.83–84). The second time the narrator says more: “his eyes were never / dry of tears, and his sweet life was ebbing away / as he mourned for his homecoming, since the nymph no longer pleased him”; by night he slept beside her under compulsion, “unwilling beside the willing”, and by day he sat on the rocks and the beaches (5.151–156; see [[nostos]], [[lament]] and [[with-an-unwilling-heart]]). It is the picture Proteus gave Menelaus, of a man “on an island shedding big tears / in the halls of the nymph Calypso” (4.556–557). Calypso's first words end it, “do not mourn here any longer” (5.160). When he tells Arete of these years, he says that he wet with tears the immortal clothes Calypso gave him (7.259–260), and the half-verse δάκρυα λείβων, “letting tears fall”, returns when he hides his face in his mantle at the Phaeacians' feast, weeping at the singer's song of Troy (8.83–86).
