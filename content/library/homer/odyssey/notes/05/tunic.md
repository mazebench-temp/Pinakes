---
summary: Odysseus puts on a cloak and a tunic on the morning he begins his raft, the tunic named second though it is put on first (5.229).
---

“At once Odysseus put on a cloak and a tunic” (5.229; see [[cloak]] and [[dressing-scene]]). The tunic goes on first, under the cloak, but the cloak is named first because it strikes the eye more than the undergarment (W. W. Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 5.229), as when Polycaste puts “a fine mantle and a tunic” on Telemachus after his bath (3.467). The garments that Calypso gives him for the voyage, and that weigh him down in the sea, are εἵματα, clothes in general (5.264, 5.321, 5.372; see [[cloak]]).
