summary: Calypso swears by “Earth … and the wide Heaven above” (5.184), and the gods are those “who hold the wide sky” (5.169); in the storm the sky is the source of the dark: Poseidon covers land and sea with clouds, “and night arose from the sky”, and Odysseus sees the wide sky wreathed in clouds (5.293–294, 5.303).
# οὐρανὸς (5.184), the nominative, first met in the Odyssey here; again 11.316, 12.404, 14.302.
grc: [οὐρανὸς]
---

**Heaven as witness.** Calypso's oath is the oath of Hera in the Iliad: “Let Earth now be witness to this, and the wide Heaven above, / and the down-flowing water of Styx” (5.184–185 = *Iliad* 15.36–37; see [[earth]], [[styx]] and [[oath]]). The digitized Iliad capitalizes Earth and Heaven there as powers called on by name; this text has the common nouns, and the translation renders “Earth” and “Heaven” here as there. Calypso has just told Odysseus that the gods “who hold the wide sky” are stronger than she is both to devise and to fulfill (5.169–170; see [[wide-sky]]).

**The sky in the storm.** When Poseidon has gathered the clouds, “with clouds he covered / land and sea alike; and night arose from the sky” (5.293–294; see [[poseidon]] and [[sea]]); W. W. Merry hears in the language and the rhythm the sudden coming of the dark (*Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 5.294). Odysseus, who does not know whose storm it is, sees it as Zeus' work: “With what clouds he is wreathing the wide sky, / Zeus” (5.303–304; Merry on 5.304; see [[zeus]]).
