---
summary: At the second council Athena tells the gods that the suitors mean to kill Telemachus on his way home, “for he has gone in search of news of his father / to sacred Pylos and to shining Lacedaemon” (5.18–20), the words in which Medon told Penelope of the journey (4.701–702).
---

Athena's plea ends with the danger to Telemachus: “And now they are eager to kill his beloved son / as he comes home; for he has gone in search of news of his father / to sacred Pylos and to shining Lacedaemon” (5.18–20; see [[telemachus]], [[the-suitors]] and [[lacedaemon]]). The last two verses are those of the herald Medon to Penelope (4.701–702; see [[medon-the-herald]]), one of the borrowings that make the speech a cento of earlier verses (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 5.7; see the translation's note on 5.7–20). For ἠγαθέην, “sacred”, Rhianus read ἠμαθίην, “sandy” (Merry's critical note on 5.20; see the translation's note on 5.20, [[egatheos]] and [[sandy]]), as he had read a word for “sandy” at 4.599 (see the Book 4 note). Telemachus has already left Pylos: he is in Lacedaemon, where the poem leaves him until Athena comes to rouse him for home (15.1–3; see [[the-journey-of-telemachus]]).
