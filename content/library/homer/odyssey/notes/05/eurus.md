---
summary: The East Wind is one of the four winds that clash together in Poseidon's storm (5.295), and when the raft is broken it plays with the wreck among them, yielding it to the West Wind to chase (5.332).
---

The four winds are named together only in this book of Homer, here and when they toss the broken raft (5.331–332): “The East Wind and the South Wind clashed together, and the stormy West Wind / and the North Wind, born of the clear sky, rolling up a great wave” (5.295–296; see [[poseidon]], [[notus]], [[zephyrus]] and [[boreas]]). The East Wind is paired with the South Wind, as in the similes of the Iliad, where the two raise the waves of the Icarian sea or strive in a mountain forest (*Iliad* 2.144–146, 16.765–769). When the raft has lost its mast, the winds toss it over the open sea like thistles in late summer, “and now again the East Wind would yield it to the West Wind to chase” (5.332; see [[thistles-in-the-north-wind]]).
