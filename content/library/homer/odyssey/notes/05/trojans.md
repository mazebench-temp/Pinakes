---
summary: The Trojans come back to Odysseus in the storm as the enemy of one day of the war: on the day of the fight over the dead Achilles they hurled their spears at him “in their greatest numbers” (5.309–310).
---

“on that day when, in their greatest numbers, bronze-fitted spears / the Trojans hurled at me around the dead son of Peleus” (5.309–310; see [[the-fight-over-the-body-of-achilles]] and [[achilles]]). ἐπέρριψαν, “hurled at”, is found only here in Homer. Odysseus wishes he had died under those spears, with funeral rites and fame, rather than drown (5.311–312; see [[odysseus]]). In the last book the shade of Agamemnon tells how the best sons of the Trojans and the Achaeans were killed around the body, fighting the whole day (24.37–42, a plain rendering), and in the underworld Odysseus remembers that the sons of the Trojans, with Pallas Athena, judged the contest for Achilles' arms (11.547; see [[the-judgment-of-arms]]).
