---
summary: Hermes warns Calypso to have regard for the wrath of Zeus, “lest in time to come he grow angry with you in resentment” (κοτεσσάμενος, 5.147).
grc: [κοτεσσάμενος]
---

κότος is anger kept for later, and Hermes' warning is about later: μετόπισθε, “in time to come” (5.146–147; see [[menis]] and [[hermes]]). The half-verse κοτεσσάμενος χαλεπήνῃ is the one in which the Iliad's simile of the autumn storm shows Zeus, “when in resentment he grows angry with men / who by force in the assembly judge crooked ordinances” (*Iliad* 16.386–387; see [[the-storm-of-late-summer]] and [[zeus]]); χαλεπήνῃ is intransitive, as of the winter season at 5.485 (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 5.147). Calypso has already yielded (5.137–144; see [[the-mind-of-zeus]]): the warning names what would follow if she had not.
