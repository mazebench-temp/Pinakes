# ζώνην (5.231; again 10.544 and Tyro's girdle, 11.245), the girdle in the Odyssey; in the Iliad ζώνην is a man's
# waist (2.479, 11.234), so the form stays out of the entry.
grc: [ζώνην]
---

“and the nymph herself put on a great silver-white mantle, / fine and lovely, and around her waist she cast a girdle, / fine and golden, and over her head a head covering” (5.230–232; see [[cloak]], [[veil]] and [[calypso]]). περὶ … βάλετʼ, “cast around”, is the verb in tmesis, and it governs the head covering too: κεφαλῇ goes with it, ἐφύπερθε being a mere adverb, as in Hera's dressing, κρηδέμνῳ δʼ ἐφύπερθε καλύψατο (W. W. Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 5.232; *Iliad* 14.184). ἰξύς, “waist”, is a word of the Odyssey only, here and in the same verse of Circe's dressing (10.544). The goddess dresses as Hera did for Zeus, with a girdle and a veil (*Iliad* 14.181–184; see [[the-adornment-of-a-goddess]]), but on this morning she is dressing to give Odysseus the tools for his raft (5.233–237; see [[the-building-of-the-raft]]).
