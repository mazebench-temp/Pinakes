**The oath asked.** Calypso tells Odysseus to build a raft and promises him provisions and a fair wind (5.160–170), but she says nothing of Hermes or of Zeus' command, and he suspects a trick: “Something else, goddess, is what you are planning here, and not my escort” (5.173; see [[dolos]] and [[calypso]]). Merry finds him cautious to a fault, since she had promised to send him away with a ready heart (*Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 5.177, referring to 5.161). He will not board the raft against her will, “unless you would bring yourself, goddess, to swear me a great oath / that you will not plan some other evil bane against me myself” (5.178–179), the verses in which he will ask Circe for the same oath, and Circe swears it at once (10.343–346; see [[circe]]).

**The oath sworn.** She smiles, strokes him with her hand and calls him “a rogue, and no fool” (5.180–183), and swears:

{{quote:b0d30078-c674-5e77-b1ec-f0eeda0b7bf9}}

Her witnesses are Earth, the wide Heaven above and the water of Styx (5.184–186), the three verses with which Hera swears to Zeus that it is not through her will that Poseidon harms the Trojans (*Iliad* 15.36–42; see [[styx]] and [[oath-formulas]]). The content of the oath is the content of the request, with τοι for μοι (5.187, after 5.179), and Merry supplies ἐμέ as the subject of βουλευσέμεν, “that I will not” (on 5.187). Then she goes beyond the oath, to the thought behind it: what she means for him is just what she would plan for herself in such need, “for my mind too is right, and my own heart / in my breast is not of iron, but full of pity” (5.188–191; see [[noos]], [[aisa]] and [[pity]]). In Hesiod, Zeus made Styx herself “the great oath of the gods” (*Theogony* 397–400), and a god who swears falsely by her water lies breathless and voiceless for a year, and for nine years more is kept from the councils and feasts of the gods (*Theogony* 782–804).
