---
summary: At the council of the gods Athena repeats Mentor's words in the assembly of Ithaca: let no "scepter-bearing king" be gentle and kind again, since no one remembers Odysseus (5.8–12 = 2.230–234).
---

Athena opens her plea with five verses of Mentor's speech to the Ithacans: "Never again let any man with a ready heart be gentle and kind, / a scepter-bearing king, nor know in his mind what is due" (5.8–9 = 2.230–231; see [[scepter-bearing]], [[mentor]] and [[the-council-of-the-gods]]). Merry calls her speech a cento of earlier verses (*Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 5.7; see the translation's note on 5.7–20).
