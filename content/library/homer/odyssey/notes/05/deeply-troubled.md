summary: Four times in the storm Odysseus speaks, “deeply troubled,” “to his own proud heart”: when the four winds fall on the raft (5.298), when Ino bids him leave it (5.355), when he sees the cliffs of Scheria (5.407), and when he has come ashore at the river (5.464).
grc: ["ὀχθήσας δʼ ἄρα εἶπε"]
---

The verse of the Iliad's soliloquies, said there seven times of men alone and troubled by what they see (*Iliad* 11.403), is used here four times in one book. Twice it follows “And then Odysseus' knees and dear heart were loosened” (5.297, 5.406; [[loosened-his-limbs]]); between his soliloquies the god speaks his own, “he shook his head and spoke to his own heart” (5.285, 5.376; [[shook-his-head]]). The last three weigh one course against another ([[decision-scene]], [[pondering-two-ways]]). See [[great-hearted]] and [[thymos]].
