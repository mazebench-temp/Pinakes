---
summary: Calypso mixes red nectar for Hermes with his ambrosia, and on the last evening her slave women set nectar beside her while Odysseus drinks the drink of mortals (5.93, 5.199).
---

“So she spoke, and the goddess set a table beside him, / filling it with ambrosia, and mixed red nectar” (5.92–93; see [[ambrosia]], [[table]] and [[xenia]]). κέρασσε, “mixed”, is the verb of mixing wine with water in the bowl, and the gods' drink is served as men's is (see [[mixing-bowl]] and [[wine]]). νέκταρ ἐρυθρόν, “red nectar”, is the nectar that Thetis drips into the nostrils of Patroclus in the Iliad (*Iliad* 19.38; see [[red]]); in the Odyssey it is drunk. On the last evening the slave women set ambrosia and nectar beside Calypso, while Odysseus is given food and drink “such as mortal men eat” (5.196–199; see [[feast-type-scene]]). Odysseus will offer the Cyclops a drink that he calls a piece of ambrosia and nectar, the wine of Maron (9.359).
