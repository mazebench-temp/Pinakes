summary: Odysseus' prayer to the river of Scheria in the order of the type-scene: he knows the god as he flows forth and prays to him in his heart (5.444), calls on him, “Hear me, lord, whoever you are” (5.445), makes his claim, that a wanderer is to be respected even by the gods (5.447–449), asks for pity (5.450), and is answered at once (5.451–453).
passages:
  - 5.441-453 | Odysseus prays to the river
---

Merry takes κατὰ θυμόν as a prayer in the heart, not because an exhausted swimmer had no breath for words, as a scholium suggests, but as Odysseus prays to Athena in the footrace (*Iliad* 23.769; Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 5.444). The claim is a suppliant's, not a sacrificer's ([[supplication-type-scene]], [[hear-me]], [[the-river-of-scheria]]).
