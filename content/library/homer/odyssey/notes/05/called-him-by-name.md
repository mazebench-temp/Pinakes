---
summary: Calypso smiles at Odysseus' demand for an oath, “and stroked him with her hand, and spoke to him, and called him by name” (5.180–181).
---

The goddess's gesture of the Iliad, and Menelaus' to Telemachus (4.610), comes before a teasing reproach: “Truly you are a rogue, and no fool” (5.182), playful, as Merry takes it (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 5.182). See [[smiling-at-him]] and [[calypso]].
