---
summary: The Bear, which Odysseus keeps on his left as he steers from Calypso's island, "alone has no share in the baths of Oceanus" (5.275 = *Iliad* 18.489).
---

Steering by night, Odysseus watches the Pleiades, Boötes, "and the Bear, which they also call by the name of the Wagon, / which turns in its place and watches Orion, / and alone has no share in the baths of Oceanus" (5.273–275 = *Iliad* 18.487–489; see [[the-bear]], [[pleiades]] and [[bootes]]). The stars set in Oceanus and rise from him, as the sun does, and the Bear, never setting, is the one Calypso tells him to keep on his left hand (5.276–277). Aristotle cites οἴη δʼ ἄμμορος as a metaphor, the best known put for the only one, since other stars also never set (*Poetics* 1461a20–21; see [[alone-without-a-share]] and the translation's note on 5.273–275).
