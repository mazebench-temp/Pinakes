---
summary: Priam is named once, in Hermes' words for the Achaeans' war, “the men who fought around the city of Priam / for nine years” (5.106–107).
---

Troy is Priam's city here as in Nestor's memories: ἄστυ πέρι Πριάμοιο, “around the city of Priam” (5.106), where πέρι follows its noun and throws its accent back (see [[troy]] and [[the-fall-of-troy]]). Nestor spoke of “the great city of lord Priam” and of the day “when we had sacked the steep city of Priam” (3.107, 3.130), and the Cretan stranger of Odysseus' tale to Eumaeus says that in the tenth year they sacked the city of Priam and went home (14.241). The phrase sets Odysseus among the heroes of that war, the most wretched of them all (5.105–106; Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 5.106; see [[the-returns-of-the-achaeans]]).
