summary: The second council of the gods takes up the journey where Book 4 left it, with the suitors lying in wait. Athena tells the gods that they mean to kill Telemachus on his way home from Pylos and Lacedaemon, and Zeus bids her bring him home unharmed (5.18–27); then the poem turns to Odysseus, and does not come back to Telemachus until Book 15.

**Between the two councils.** Six days have passed since Athena first asked Zeus for Odysseus' return, as the notes of time in the books between show (2.1; 3.1, 3.404, 3.491; 4.306), and Hermes has not yet been sent to Calypso: at the opening of Book 5 she renews her request (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, at the head of his notes on Book 5; see [[the-council-of-the-gods]]). By the count of the structure file the council falls on the seventh day of the poem, the morning after the last day told at Sparta (4.306–624), or two days earlier if it follows the night that ends Book 4 in Ithaca (see the translation's note on 5.1). Athena ends her plea with the news the herald Medon brought Penelope, in the same words, “for he has gone in search of news of his father / to sacred Pylos and to shining Lacedaemon” (5.19–20 = 4.701–702; see [[medon-the-herald]] and [[telemachus]]). Zeus bids her bring the young man home: “escort him skillfully, for you are able, / so that he may reach his native land quite unharmed, / and the suitors in their ship may go back again” (5.25–27; see [[athena]] and [[the-suitors]]).

**The close of a volume.** Those who find two poems in the Odyssey, a Telemachy and a “Return of Odysseus”, pieced together, take the opening of Book 5 as an attempt to cover a gap between them: the journey ends with Book 4 and is taken up again in Books 15 and 16, and the second council is an awkward necessity, caused by inserting the Telemachy after the first, where the despatch of Hermes ought to have followed at once (1.87 to 5.30; Köchly, Schmitt and Hennings, as Merry reports them on 5.7). Merry answers that the break at the end of Book 4, with Telemachus waylaid by the suitors, is “the end of one volume of the novel, which closes with an exciting situation”, and that the repeated verses bring the hearer's mind back to the story of the principal hero; but he grants that “the main difficulty remains untouched”: in the second council Athena says nothing of her proposal to send Hermes, and speaks as if nothing were intended for Odysseus (on 5.7; see the translation's note on 5.7–20). The poem does not come back to Telemachus until Athena goes to Sparta to remind him of his homecoming (15.1–3), a month later by the reckoning of the following books (Merry on 4.594; see the translation's note on 4.594).
