---
summary: Calypso tells of two strokes of Zeus' gleaming thunderbolt, the one that killed Iasion when Demeter lay with him and the one that shattered Odysseus' swift ship in the middle of the sea (5.125–132).
---

In her protest against the gods' jealousy Calypso names the thunderbolt twice, and the two verses end alike, ἀργῆτι κεραυνῷ, “with a gleaming thunderbolt” (see [[of-the-gleaming-thunderbolt]]). Demeter lay with Iasion in a thrice-plowed fallow field, “and not long without hearing of it / was Zeus, who killed him, striking him with a gleaming thunderbolt” (5.127–128; see [[demeter-and-iasion]], [[iasion]] and [[the-jealousy-of-the-gods]]). Odysseus she saved “when he was astride the keel, / alone, when his swift ship, with a gleaming thunderbolt, / Zeus struck and shattered in the middle of the wine-dark sea” (5.130–132; see [[the-shipwreck-of-odysseus]] and [[ships]]). The god who struck the goddess' lover struck the ship of the man she now loves. ἔλσας, “struck”, is from εἴλω, “squeeze”, here of a crushing blow, though others take it as “driving down” into the sea (W. W. Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 5.132); Zenodotus read ἐλάσας (Merry's critical note; see the translation's note on 5.132).
