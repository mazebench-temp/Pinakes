---
summary: Hawks roost with owls and long-tongued crows of the sea in the wood around Calypso's cave (5.65–67).
---

“And there long-winged birds would roost, / owls and hawks and long-tongued crows / of the sea” (5.65–67; see [[birds]] and [[calypsos-cave]]). The hawk of the Iliad is the swiftest of birds and the likeness of gods going down from the heights and of heroes in their onset (*Iliad* 13.62–65, 15.237–238, 16.582–583; see [[like-a-hawk]]). On Calypso's island it is only one of the birds that roost in the wood, εὐνάζοντο, a picture of rest; the god who comes to the island is likened not to a hawk but to a gull that fishes over the sea (5.51–54; see [[the-gull]]).
