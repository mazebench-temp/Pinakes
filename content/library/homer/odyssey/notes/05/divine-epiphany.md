---
summary: Ino appears to Odysseus in the storm: she sees and pities him, rises from the sea like a diving bird, sits on the raft, gives him her veil with her counsel, and plunges back (5.333–353); he doubts her, fearing a trap of the immortals (5.356–364); and at the river mouth he “knew the god as he flowed forth” (5.444).
passages:
  - 5.333-353 | Ino gives Odysseus her veil
---

The goddess comes unbidden, rising from the sea like a diving bird, like it in the sudden plunge, not in shape, as Merry takes it (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 5.353), and she bids him turn away when he throws the veil back (5.350), as those must who may not watch a divine power at work (Merry on 5.350). Merry compares, for the river god known by his flowing, Sophocles' “he knew the god by touching him” (Merry on 5.444). See [[leucothea]], [[veil]] and [[the-river-of-scheria]].
