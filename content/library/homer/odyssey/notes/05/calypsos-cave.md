---
summary: The cave is the scene of the book's first part. Hermes stops to gaze at it before he goes in, Calypso receives him there and hears the command of Zeus, and there she and Odysseus eat their last meal together and spend their last night before the raft is built (5.55–148, 5.192–227).
---

**Hermes at the cave.** The god who has crossed so much salt water stands still at the cave: “There the guide, the slayer of Argus, stood and gazed. / But when he had gazed at everything in his heart, / at once he went into the wide cave” (5.75–77; see [[hermes]]). Calypso, looking him in the face, knows him at once, “for the gods are not unknown to one another, / the immortals, not even if one lives in a house far away”, words that W. W. Merry takes to describe her own case, living on her distant island (*Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 5.79; see [[calypso]]). Odysseus is not within: he sits weeping on the shore, as he had before (5.81–84). She seats the god on a shining throne, sets a table with ambrosia and mixes red nectar (5.86–93; see [[xenia]], [[throne]] and [[feast-type-scene]]), and in the cave he gives her the message of Zeus and hears her protest (5.97–147).

**The last evening.** When she has sworn her oath on the shore, Calypso leads the way back, and Odysseus follows in her footprints: “And they came to the hollow cave, the goddess and the man” (5.192–194; see [[hollow-caves]]). He sits on the throne from which Hermes had risen, she sets before him the food of mortal men, and her slave women set ambrosia and nectar beside her (5.195–199; see [[ambrosia]]). After the meal and the last talk, in which she offers him immortality and he chooses his home (5.202–224), the sun goes down: “and the two of them went in, and in the inner part of the hollow cave / took their pleasure in love, staying by each other” (5.226–227). μυχῷ goes with τερπέσθην: “having gone their way, they took their pleasure in the recess of the cave” (Merry on 5.226).

**Leaving the cave.** At dawn she gives him the axe and the adze and leads him away to the far edge of the island, where the tall trees grow, and goes back to her house (5.228–242; see [[the-building-of-the-raft]]). The cave is not named again in the book; on the fifth day she bathes and clothes him and sends him from the island (5.263–264).
