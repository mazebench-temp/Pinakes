summary: Gold is the metal of the gods in Book 5: Hermes' golden sandals and golden wand (5.44–45, 5.87), Calypso's golden shuttle and golden girdle, with a silver-white mantle (5.62, 5.230–232), and Artemis “of the golden throne” (5.123). Among men it is the gift of the Phaeacians, who will give Odysseus “bronze and gold in plenty, and clothing” (5.38).
grc: [χρυσείην]
---

The metals of the book belong to the gods. Hermes binds on his “fine sandals, / ambrosial, golden, which carried him over the waters” (5.44–45 = *Iliad* 24.340–341, as Athena's at 1.96–97; see [[sandals]] and [[hermes]]), and Calypso greets him as “Hermes of the golden wand” (5.87; see [[of-the-golden-wand]] and [[wand]]). Calypso weaves “with a golden shuttle” (5.62; see [[loom]]), and on the morning of the parting she puts on a great silver-white mantle and “around her waist she cast a girdle, / fine and golden” (5.230–232 = 10.543–545, of Circe; see [[girdle]] and [[silver-white]]). Artemis, in Calypso's tale, is χρυσόθρονος, “of the golden throne” (5.123; see [[golden-throned]] and [[artemis]]). Gold comes to men as a gift: the Phaeacians will give Odysseus “bronze and gold in plenty, and clothing, / a great store, more than Odysseus would ever have carried off from Troy” (5.38–39; see [[phaeacians]] and [[guest-gifts]]), verses that Poseidon throws back at Zeus when the Phaeacians have set him ashore in Ithaca with such gifts (13.134–138).
