---
summary: Hermes complains that there is no city of mortals near Calypso's island, “men who to the gods / offer sacrifices and choice hecatombs” (5.101–102).
---

The messenger of the gods explains his unwilling errand by the remoteness of the island: “who of his own will would speed across so much salt water, / boundless? Nor is there any city of mortals near, men who to the gods / offer sacrifices and choice hecatombs” (5.100–102; see [[hermes]] and [[ogygia]]). Where there are no men there are no sacrifices, and a god has no reason to go (see [[reciprocity-with-the-gods]] and [[sacrifice]]). The god whose absence let the council send Odysseus home was away for the same reason: Poseidon had gone to the Ethiopians “to share in a hecatomb of bulls and rams” (1.22–26), and he is coming back from them when he sees the raft (5.282; Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 5.282; see [[ethiopians]] and [[poseidon]]).
