---
summary: The Aeneid draws three times on this book. Mercury flies to Carthage with Hermes' golden sandals and wand and swoops to the sea like a bird; the storm of the first book follows Poseidon's storm, down to Aeneas' cry that those who died at Troy were three and four times blessed; and the helmsman Palinurus keeps his eyes on the stars, falls with the tiller, and sees land from the top of a wave. In the Georgics the Bears fear to be dipped in Ocean.
---

- Mercury sent to bid Aeneas leave Carthage, with the sandals, the wand, the halt on Atlas and the bird skimming the sea (*Aeneid* 4.219–278, after 5.28–54; and his first flight to Carthage, *Aeneid* 1.297–304), which Merry compares with Hermes' flight (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 5.43–49): [[virgils-mercury]] and [[the-gull]].
- The storm, Aeneas' limbs loosened with cold and his cry *O terque quaterque beati* (*Aeneid* 1.81–123, after 5.291–318), and the landing in Libya (*Aeneid* 1.157–173): [[virgils-storm]], [[virgils-diomedes]] and [[three-times-blessed]].
- Palinurus, asleep at the tiller under the stars, and his swim to Italy (*Aeneid* 5.833–871, 6.337–371, after 5.270–277, 5.388–393 and 5.425–473; Merry on 5.393): [[virgils-palinurus]].
- The two Bears “that fear to be dipped in the water of Ocean” (*Georgics* 1.244–246, after 5.275): [[alone-without-a-share]].
