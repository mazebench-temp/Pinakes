---
summary: Poseidon, returning from the Ethiopians, sees Odysseus on his raft “from far away, from the mountains of the Solymi” (5.282–284), the Odyssey's only mention of the people whom Bellerophon fought in the Iliad.
---

**The mountains of the Solymi.** τηλόθεν ἐκ Σολύμων ὀρέων ἴδεν, “saw him from far away, from the mountains of the Solymi” (5.283): the god on his way back from the Ethiopians looks out over the sea from their heights and sees the raft (see [[poseidon]] and [[ethiopians]]). In the Iliad the Solymi are a people of warriors whom Bellerophon fought for the Lycian king, and in fighting whom his son Isander was killed (*Iliad* 6.184–185, 6.203–204; see [[bellerophon]] and [[isander]]); here only their mountains are named.

Merry reports Strabo's placing of the Solymi on the heights of the Taurus range in Lycia and Pisidia, and notes that Homer does not seem to make them one with the Lycians, as Herodotus does (Herodotus 1.173): the Iliad makes the two peoples enemies (*Iliad* 6.168–186). Perhaps, he suggests, a colony from Crete settled in the Milyas and drove the old inhabitants back into their mountain strongholds (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 5.283; see the translation's note on 5.283 and [[lycians]]).

Strabo counts the verse among the poet's transfers of real places to the Ocean, as he moved there the things of the Pontus: the Solymi held the highest peaks of Taurus, from Lycia to Pisidia, which showed their summits from the south to all who lived within the Taurus, and above all to those about the Pontus, and so by some likeness they too were set beside the Ocean, “for he says of the man sailing on the raft, ‘But the powerful earth-shaker, returning from the Ethiopians, / saw him from far away, from the mountains of the Solymi’” (*Geography* 1.2.10). Elsewhere he takes the phrase as meaning no more than “from the southern regions”, of namesakes of the Pisidian Solymi invented by the poet (*Geography* 1.2.28; see [[strabo]]).
