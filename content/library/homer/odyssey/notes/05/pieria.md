---
summary: Hermes, sent to Calypso, flies down from Olympus, steps on Pieria and drops from the upper air to the sea (5.50), the road Hera took to Lemnos in the Iliad.
---

**The step to the sea.** Hermes binds on his sandals and takes his wand in the verses of his flight to Priam (5.43–49 = *Iliad* 24.339–345; see [[hermes]] and the translation's note on 5.43–49). In the Iliad he comes at once to Troy and the Hellespont (*Iliad* 24.346); here the poem gives his road: “Stepping on Pieria, he fell from the upper air upon the sea” (5.50), Πιερίην δʼ ἐπιβὰς ἐξ αἰθέρος ἔμπεσε πόντῳ, and then he skims the waves like a gull (5.51–54). Merry compares Hera's flight from the peak of Olympus, “stepping on Pieria and lovely Emathia”, over the snowy mountains of the Thracians to Athos and the sea (*Iliad* 14.225–229; see [[emathia]] and [[athos]]), and takes Πιερίην δʼ ἐπιβάς of the god's passage over the peaks of the Pierian range, which runs northwest from Olympus (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 5.50; see [[olympus]]). πέτετο, “flew” (5.49), is said of any swift movement, not only of birds (Merry on 5.49). The opening words are Hera's, with the masculine participle for the feminine; for both gods Pieria is the first land below Olympus on the way to the sea.
