summary: The eyes of Book 5: Hermes' wand bewitches the eyes of men (5.47), Odysseus' eyes are never dry of tears on Calypso's shore (5.151–152), he saw the land far off with his own eyes (5.358–359), and Athena pours sleep on his eyes under the olives (5.492).
# ὄμματα (5.47; 1.208, 16.179, 24.3) and ὄμμασι (5.492), always "eyes" in the Odyssey.
grc: [ὄμματα, ὄμμασι]
---

**The wand.** Hermes takes "the wand with which he bewitches the eyes of men, / of whomever he will, and again wakes others who are sleeping" (5.47–48 = *Iliad* 24.343–344; see [[wand]] and [[hermes]]); he holds the same wand when he calls out the souls of the suitors (24.1–4).

**Tears.** Calypso finds Odysseus sitting on the shore, "and his eyes were never / dry of tears, and his sweet life was ebbing away / as he mourned for his homecoming" (5.151–153; see [[tears]] and [[nostos]]).

**Seeing for himself.** When Ino bids him leave the raft, he trusts his own sight before a goddess' word: "But I will certainly not obey yet, since far off, with my eyes, / I saw the land where she said there was a refuge for me" (5.358–359; see [[leucothea]]).

**Sleep.** At the end of the book "Athena / poured sleep on his eyes, so that it might free him as quickly as possible / from his grievous weariness, enfolding his dear eyelids" (5.491–493; see [[hypnos]] and [[athena]]).
