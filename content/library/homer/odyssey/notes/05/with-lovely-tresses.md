---
summary: Calypso is “the nymph of the lovely tresses” in Zeus' command and in the cave where Hermes finds her (5.30, 5.57–58); the epithet is given to Demeter in Calypso's story of Iasion (5.125), and to Dawn on the morning Odysseus sees land (5.390).
grc: [ἐυπλόκαμος]
---

Zeus' command repeats Athena's plan at the first council, “tell the nymph of the lovely tresses our unerring counsel” (5.30; 1.86, with εἴπῃ). The nominative ἐυπλόκαμος, which Murray prints without the diaeresis, closes the phrase for each goddess: νύμφη … ἐυπλόκαμος (5.57–58), ἐυπλόκαμος Δημήτηρ (5.125), and the day-count verse, “But when Dawn of the lovely tresses brought the third day” (5.390; again 9.76, 10.144; [[dawn-line]]). In later books it is Calypso's and Circe's in the verse-end ἐυπλόκαμος, δεινὴ θεός, “of the lovely tresses, a dread goddess” (7.246, 10.136, 12.449). See [[calypso]], [[demeter]] and [[eos]].
