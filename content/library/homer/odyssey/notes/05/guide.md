---
summary: Hermes is “the guide, the slayer of Argus” four times, at each step of his errand: he does not disobey Zeus (5.43), stands and gazes at the island (5.75), eats and drinks at Calypso's table (5.94), and answers her protest (5.145).
grc: [διάκτορος]
---

The verse-end διάκτορος ἀργεϊφόντης is his on his journey to Priam in the Iliad, and the verses of his flight are the same (5.43–49 = *Iliad* 24.339–345; [[argeiphontes]]). In the narrative he is named by his titles, in address by his name: Zeus calls him “Hermes,” and Calypso “Hermes of the golden wand” (5.29, 5.87; [[of-the-golden-wand]]). See [[hermes]] and [[messenger-scene]].
