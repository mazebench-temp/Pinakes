summary: Aeolus' household are “amazed in their hearts” when Odysseus comes back (10.63); the companions on Aeaea gaze at the great stag (10.180); Eurylochus' return is met with questions “in amazement” (10.249); and Circe, her drug defeated, says “Wonder holds me” (10.326).
# θηήσαντʼ (10.180), ἀγασσάμεθʼ (10.249) and "θαῦμά μʼ ἔχει" (10.326), each only here in the Odyssey.
grc: [θηήσαντʼ, ἀγασσάμεθʼ, "θαῦμά μʼ ἔχει"]
en: [gazed at]
---

Aeolus' household, seeing the man they had sent home sitting on their threshold, “were amazed in their hearts and questioned us” (ἀνὰ θυμὸν ἐθάμβεον, 10.63; see [[aeolus]]). On Aeaea, uncovering their heads, the companions “gazed at the stag; for it was a very great beast” (θηήσαντʼ ἔλαφον, 10.179–180), θηήσαντο being “stared at”, with the notion of amazement, from the root of θαυμάζειν (W. W. Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 10.180; see [[deer]]). When Eurylochus comes back unable to speak, “we had all questioned him in amazement” (ἀγασσάμεθʼ ἐξερέοντες, 10.249), literally “showed surprise in our questioning”, the finite verb playing the part of an adverb, as in σπεῦσε πονησάμενος (9.250; Merry on 10.249). And Circe, when Odysseus has drunk her drugs and is not bewitched, says “Wonder holds me, how you drank these drugs and were not bewitched at all” (θαῦμά μʼ ἔχει ὡς, 10.326), where ὡς suggests the manner as well as the fact, and Bekker's πῶς is not needed (Merry on 10.326; see the translation's note on 10.326 and [[thelgo]]).
