---
summary: Eurylochus: “for we may still escape the evil day” (10.269); and Hermes gives Odysseus the good drug, “and it will ward off the evil day from your head” (10.288).
---

Merry renders κακὸν ἦμαρ “the day of evil,” like νόστιμον, δούλιον and ἀναγκαῖον ἦμαρ, and κρατός “from thy head,” comparing Achilles' “there is no one who will keep the dogs from your head” (*Iliad* 22.348; Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 10.269, 10.288). Eurylochus would escape it by flight, Odysseus by the god's drug (10.266–269, 10.286–292; [[eurylochus]], [[moly]]).
