---
summary: Pausanias, as Merry quotes him, took the Laestrygonians, “not like men, but like the Giants”, for proof that Homer's Giants were mortal men, and thought that Homer took the scenery of his underworld from the Acherusian lake of Thesprotia, with its rivers Acheron and Cocytus. Merry cites him too for the oracles of the dead, for the ram sacrificed into a pit before the descent into the cave of Trophonius, and for the tomb of Teiresias.
---

- **Like the Giants.** Homer, Pausanias says, made no mention of the Giants in the Iliad, but in the Odyssey he wrote that the Laestrygonians came against the ships of Odysseus likened not to men but to Giants, and made the king of the Phaeacians say that the Phaeacians are near to the gods, as are the Cyclopes and the race of the Giants: in these verses he shows that the Giants are mortal and not a race of gods (Pausanias 8.29.2, as Merry quotes it; Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 7.59; 10.119–120 and 7.205–206): [[giants]] and [[laestrygonians]].
- **The rivers of Thesprotia.** Pausanias thinks that the scenery of the Homeric Nekyia was taken from the Acherusian lake in Thesprotia, with its rivers Acheron and Cocytus; the truth, Merry answers, may lie the other way, and the rivers may have got their names from the poetry, though the natural scenery of Greece is plainly reproduced in the description of Hades (Merry on 10.513, citing Pausanias 1.17): [[acheron]] and [[cocytus]].
- **The pit and the ram.** Among the oracles of the dead Merry names the one near the Acherusian lake in Thesprotia and one at Phigalea in Arcadia, citing Pausanias, and he quotes Pausanias on the rites before the descent into the cave of Trophonius at Lebadea, which recall Circe's directions: on the night on which each man goes down, they sacrifice a ram into a pit (Merry on 10.517, citing Pausanias 1.17.5 and 3.17.8 for the oracles and Pausanias 9.39 for the ram; 10.516–527): [[the-offering-to-the-dead]] and [[herodotus]].
- **The tomb of Teiresias.** After the war of the Epigoni, Merry notes, Teiresias was said to have died near Haliartus, and his tomb was shown there in Pausanias' time (Merry on 10.492, citing Pausanias 7.3.1 and Pausanias 9.18.4); Strabo places it by the spring Tilphossa (*Geography* 9.2.27): [[teiresias]] and [[strabo]].
