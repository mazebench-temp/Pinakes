# The spring before its name: "the fair-flowing spring" (10.107).
refs: [10.107]
---

**At the spring.** The scouts have followed the wagon road down to the city:

{{quote:3db2074e-ccaf-5d9f-93ce-fc53ce315bab}}

ὑδρευούσῃ, “drawing water”, is the task of a king's daughter here, as of the daughters of Celeus in the *Hymn to Demeter* (98–110; W. W. Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 10.105; see the entry). The spring is named with the reason for her coming, ἔνθεν γὰρ ὕδωρ προτὶ ἄστυ φέρεσκον, “for from there they used to carry water to the city” (10.108), φέρεσκον being the iterative of a custom. The scouts' question is double, asking the name both of the king and of his people (Merry on 10.110), and the girl answers it at once by pointing out her father's high-roofed house (10.111; see [[high-roofed]] and [[antiphates-the-laestrygonian]]), where they find her mother, “as huge as a mountain peak” (10.113).
