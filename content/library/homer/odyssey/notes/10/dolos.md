summary: Eurylochus stays outside Circe's door, “for he suspected a trick” (10.232, 10.258). Odysseus charges Circe with “treachery in your heart” (10.339), and at the table she asks him, “Do you suspect some other trick?” (10.380).
# δολοφρονέουσα (10.339), only here in the Odyssey; δολοφρονέων of Odysseus (18.51, 21.274).
grc: [δολοφρονέουσα]
en: [treachery]
---

Eurylochus alone does not follow Circe in: “but Eurylochus stayed behind, for he suspected a trick” (ὀισάμενος δόλον εἶναι, 10.232), and in his report to Odysseus he says it of himself in the same words (10.258; see [[eurylochus]]). The trick was the drug in the food and the wand (10.233–243; see [[kykeon]] and [[wand]]), what Hermes calls “the baneful thoughts of Circe” (ὀλοφώια δήνεα, 10.289; see the translation's table of key words, δήνεα). When Circe, her drug defeated, bids Odysseus to her bed, he answers that she does it “with treachery in your heart” (δολοφρονέουσα, 10.339; see [[with-treachery-in-her-heart]]), the word of Helen's reproach to Aphrodite, who has come and stood by her “with treachery in your heart” (*Iliad* 3.405), and of Hera deceiving Zeus (*Iliad* 14.197, 19.106). At the table, when he will not eat, Circe asks in Eurylochus' words, “Do you suspect some other trick? You have no need / to fear; for I have already sworn you a mighty oath” (ἦ τινά που δόλον ἄλλον ὀίεαι, 10.380–381; see the translation's note on 10.380 and [[oath]]).
