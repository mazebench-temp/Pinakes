summary: Aeolus' sons and daughters feast all day with their parents (10.8–10, 10.61). The Laestrygonians make meals of Odysseus' men (10.116, 10.124); on Aeaea the companions feast all day on the stag, and later for a whole year in Circe's house (10.155–186, 10.452, 10.467–477).
# δαίνυνται (10.9; also 7.203, the gods who feast with the Phaeacians) and δαινύμενον (10.61), first met here; they
# apply in the Odyssey only.

**Aeolus' table.** The twelve children of Aeolus, “always beside their dear father and devoted mother, / feast, and countless good things lie before them, / and the house, full of the savor of meat, resounds round about in the court / by day” (10.8–11; see [[aeolus]] and [[house]]). When Odysseus comes back to beg a second escort he finds Aeolus “feasting beside his wife and his children” (10.61).

**The meals of the Laestrygonians.** Antiphates “seized one of my companions and made ready his meal” (10.116; see [[antiphates-the-laestrygonian]]), and his people, spearing the men in the water like fish, “carried them off to a joyless meal” (10.124; see [[laestrygonians]]): ἀτερπέα δαῖτα, a “gruesome meal”, in W. W. Merry's rendering, whose critical note records a variant δαῖτα πένοντο (*Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 10.124; see the translation's note on 10.124). The word of the shared meal is turned to the eating of men, in the verse of the Cyclops, who “seized two men together and made ready his meal” (9.311; see [[polyphemus-the-cyclops]]).

**The feast of the stag.** On Aeaea, after two days of grief, Odysseus brings the stag to the ship and rouses his companions: “let us think of eating, and not be worn down by hunger” (10.177; see [[deer]]). When they have gazed at the stag, they “washed their hands and made ready a glorious feast. / So all day long, until the sun went down, / we sat feasting on untold meat and sweet wine” (10.182–184; see [[hand-washing]], [[untold]] and [[sunset-lines]]).

**The year in Circe's house.** Odysseus finds the companions whom Circe had bathed “all feasting well in the halls” (10.452), and they stay a year, “every day, for a full year's round” feasting on untold meat and sweet wine (10.467–468; see [[circe]] and [[xenia]]), until the companions call him out and bid him think of home (10.471–474); on that last day they feast again till sunset (10.476–477).
