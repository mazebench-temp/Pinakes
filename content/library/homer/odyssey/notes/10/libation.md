# "χοὴν χεῖσθαι" (10.518; χοὴν χεόμην, 11.26): the drink offering to the dead, χοή, which Eustathius keeps apart from λοιβή and σπονδή.
grc: ["χοὴν χεῖσθαι"]
en: [drink offering]
refs: [10.519-520]
---

Around the pit Odysseus is to “pour a drink offering to all the dead, / first with honey and milk, then with sweet wine, / and the third time with water; and sprinkle white barley meal over it” (10.518–520; see [[the-offering-to-the-dead]], [[honey]], [[milk]], [[wine]] and [[barley-meal]]). χοὴν χεῖσθαι is the proper term for a drink offering to the dead, and Eustathius, as Merry reports him, calls the χοή proper to the dead, and not the σπονδή or the λοιβή, the words of libation (W. W. Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 10.518). The offering gives the dead, in Merry's words, “the ordinary supports of life” (on 10.519). The Iliad has one pouring for a dead man: all night Achilles drew wine from a golden mixing bowl “and poured it on the ground, and soaked the earth, / calling on the soul of poor Patroclus” (*Iliad* 23.218–221; see [[psyche]]).
