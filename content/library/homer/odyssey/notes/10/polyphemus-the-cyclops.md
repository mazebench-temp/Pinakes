summary: Remembered twice in the tenth book. At Aeaea the companions weep as they remember "the mighty, great-hearted Cyclops, the eater of men" (10.200), and Eurylochus, refusing to go to Circe's house, recalls how the Cyclops penned the companions in and blames Odysseus' recklessness for their death (10.435–437).
# The entry claims Κύκλωπος (10.200) and Κύκλωψ (10.435).
---

**At Aeaea.** When Odysseus tells his men of the smoke rising from Circe's island, “their dear heart was broken / as they remembered the deeds of Antiphates the Laestrygonian / and of the mighty, great-hearted Cyclops, the eater of men” (10.198–200; see [[antiphates-the-laestrygonian]] and [[odysseus-and-the-cyclops]]). ἀνδροφάγος, “the eater of men”, recalls the meals of the cave, two men at a time (9.288–293, 9.311, 9.344).

**Eurylochus' charge.** Eurylochus fears that Circe will make them all swine or wolves or lions, “as the Cyclops penned them in, when to his farmyard came / our companions, and with them went this bold Odysseus; / for it was through his recklessness that they too perished” (10.435–437; see [[eurylochus]] and [[atasthalie]]). The last verse turns against Odysseus the words of the proem, where the companions “perished through their own recklessness” (1.7).
