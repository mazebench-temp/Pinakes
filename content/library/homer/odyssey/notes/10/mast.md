---
summary: Circe tells Odysseus that he needs no guide on the voyage to the house of Hades: he is to set up the mast, spread the sails and sit, and the North Wind will carry the ship (10.505–507).
# ἱστόν at 10.222, 10.226 and 10.254 is Circe's loom, and is excepted in the entry.
---

When Odysseus asks who will guide them to the house of Hades, “No one has ever yet come to the house of Hades in a black ship” (10.501–502), Circe answers: “let no longing for a guide trouble you beside your ship, / but set up your mast and spread the white sails, / and sit; and the breath of the North Wind will carry her” (10.505–507; see [[sails]], [[boreas]] and [[circe]]). παρὰ νηί goes best with μελέσθω, W. W. Merry suggests, as though Odysseus might be hanging about the ship waiting for a pilot (*Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 10.505). The ship that needs no pilot is the Phaeacians' way, whose ships have neither helmsmen nor steering oars (8.557–558; see [[phaeacians]]). In this book the same word, ἱστός, is also Circe's loom (10.222; see [[loom]]).
