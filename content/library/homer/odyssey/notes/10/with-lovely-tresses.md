---
summary: Circe at her first appearance in the tale, “Circe of the lovely tresses, a dread goddess with human voice” (10.136), and the third day on her island, “But when Dawn of the lovely tresses brought the third day” (10.144).
---

The first is the verse-end formula that the Odyssey keeps for the goddesses who hold Odysseus; it ends the verse again when Circe sends a wind behind his ship (11.8, 12.150), and once of Calypso (12.449; see the Odyssey translation's formulas; [[dread-goddess]], [[with-human-voice]], [[circe]]). The second ends two days and two nights that the men lay on the shore of Aeaea, “eating our hearts out with weariness and sorrows together” (10.142–144), in the verses of the shore after the storm in Book 9 (9.74–76; [[dawn-line]], [[eos]]).
