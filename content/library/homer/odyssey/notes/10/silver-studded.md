---
summary: Odysseus slings on “my silver-studded sword, / great and made of bronze” to go after his companions (10.261–262), and Circe twice seats him “on a silver-studded chair, / fine and intricately worked” (10.314–315 = 10.366–367).
---

The sword is the one he will draw on her (10.321). The chair is the seat of an honored guest, as Alcinous seats him on one (7.162), and the verse of seating comes before the drugged drink and again after the bath, before the meal he will not eat (10.314–317, 10.366–374; see the Odyssey translation's formulas; [[throne]], [[sword]], [[intricately-worked]]).
