---
summary: As Strabo reports him, Polybius read this book's Aeolus as history: a king honored for telling sailors when to put out of the strait, whose title of dispenser of the winds hints at the forecasts that the Liparaean islanders made from their volcanoes; and he rejected Eratosthenes' jest about the cobbler who stitched the skin of the winds.
---

- **The dispenser of the winds.** Aeolus, Polybius says, who told sailors in advance when to put out in the region of the strait, where the currents run both ways and make sailing out difficult, was called the dispenser of the winds and held to be a king, as Danaus was honored for showing the Argives their water and Atreus for showing the sun's course opposite to the heavens; and so Polybius will not let Aeolus, or the whole of Odysseus' wandering, be heard as a myth (Strabo, *Geography* 1.2.15; 10.21–22): [[aeolus]], [[dispenser-of-the-winds]] and [[strabo]].
- **The forecasts.** Of the three craters on Hiera, the island between Lipara and Sicily, the largest has a lip five stades round; when the south wind is coming, a misty haze settles round the island so that Sicily cannot be seen, and when the north wind, pure flames rise from the crater and the roaring grows louder; from the differences of the roars and from where the blasts, the flames and the smoke begin, the wind that will blow on the third day can be foretold, and when the weather kept men from sailing at Lipara some foretold it, Polybius says, without error. So the most fabulous of the poet's sayings, that Aeolus is the dispenser of the winds, was not said idly but hints at the truth (Strabo, *Geography* 6.2.10): [[aeolus]], [[aeolia]] and [[the-winds]].
- **The cobbler.** Nor does Polybius approve Eratosthenes' pronouncement that one will find where Odysseus wandered when one finds the cobbler who stitched the skin of the winds (Strabo, *Geography* 1.2.15): [[skin-of-the-winds]].
