---
summary: On Aeaea Odysseus divides his men into two bands, and they shake the lots in a helmet to decide which band goes toward the smoke; the lot of Eurylochus leaps out (10.203–209).
---

“But I counted off all my well-greaved companions / into two bands, and gave each band a leader; / one I led myself, and godlike Eurylochus the other. / And quickly we shook the lots in a bronze-fitted helmet, / and out leapt the lot of great-hearted Eurylochus” (κλήρους δʼ ἐν κυνέῃ χαλκήρεϊ πάλλομεν ὦκα, 10.203–207; see [[eurylochus]], [[helmet]] and [[bronze-fitted]]). The lots were pebbles shaken in a helmet, and the one that leapt out first decided the matter, as the lot leapt out for the duel of Paris and Menelaus and for the chariot race (ἔθορε, as *Iliad* 23.353; ὄρουσεν, *Iliad* 3.325; W. W. Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 10.207). Merry quotes Sophocles' Teucer on the lot that Ajax cast into the helmet, no runaway clod of wet earth but one that would be the first to leap out of the plumed helmet (*Ajax* 1285–1287, as Merry quotes it on 10.207). Eurylochus' band, twenty-two men, set out weeping, and leave the others lamenting (10.208–209; see [[tears]] and [[lament]]). In the Cyclops' cave the lot chose the four men who would hold the stake (9.331–335); here it chooses which half of the crew will meet Circe (see [[hetairos]]).
