---
summary: The seer of the book is dead: Circe sends Odysseus to consult “the blind seer, whose wits are steadfast”, Teiresias, in the house of Hades (10.490–495), who “will come to you at once” to the pit and tell him the way and his homecoming (10.538–540).
---

The seer whom Odysseus must consult is among the dead. Circe sends him “to consult the soul of Theban Teiresias, / the blind seer, whose wits are steadfast” (μάντηος ἀλαοῦ, 10.492–493; see [[teiresias]] and [[the-blind-seer]]); μάντηος is the reading of one Venice manuscript, followed by almost all modern editors, where the others have μάντιος (W. W. Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, his critical note on 10.493; see the translation's note on 10.493). The consultation is asked as of an oracle, with the verb used of Agamemnon at Pytho (χρησομένους, 10.492, 10.565; χρησόμενος, 8.81), and made by a rite: the pit, the drink offering, the vows and the blood that brings the souls (10.516–537; see [[the-offering-to-the-dead]]). Oracles of the dead were known in later Greece, Merry notes, where the ground seemed to open a way to the world below (on 10.517), and there was an oracle of Teiresias at Orchomenus (on 10.492).
