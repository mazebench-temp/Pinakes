summary: Aeolus' children feast “beside their dear father and devoted mother” (10.8); Circe and Aeetes are children of the Sun “and of their mother Perse, whom Oceanus begot as his child” (10.139).
---

The book's mothers are a goddess's and a household's. Aeolus' six sons and six daughters, married to one another, feast every day “beside their dear father and devoted mother” (παρὰ πατρὶ φίλῳ καὶ μητέρι κεδνῇ, 10.8; see [[aeolus]], [[pater]] and [[devoted]]). Circe and her brother were born of the Sun “and of their mother Perse, whom Oceanus begot as his child” (μητρός τʼ ἐκ Πέρσης, 10.139; see [[perse]] and [[oceanus]]). At the pit in Book 11 Odysseus will meet the soul of his own mother (11.84–87).
