---
summary: Odysseus goes up alone “to the great house of Circe, skilled in many drugs” (10.276).
grc: [πολυφαρμάκου]
en: [skilled in many drugs]
---

The epithet that the Iliad gives once to the healers at the ships (*Iliad* 16.28) comes once in the Odyssey, as Hermes meets Odysseus with a drug against hers (10.275–306). The book is full of her φάρμακα: the evil drugs with which she bewitched the beasts round her house (10.213), the grim drugs in the companions' food (10.236), the drug in Odysseus' own cup (10.317, 10.326–327), and “the ruinous drug” that made the bristles grow, which she undoes with another (10.392–394); against them stands Hermes' “good drug” (10.287, 10.292). The translation renders the epithet “skilled in many drugs” here and “with their many drugs” of the healers (see the Odyssey translation's key words; [[drugs]], [[circe]], [[healing]]).
