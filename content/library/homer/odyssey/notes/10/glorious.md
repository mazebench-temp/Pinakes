---
summary: “they washed their hands and made ready a glorious feast” (10.182).
---

The feast is the stag that Odysseus has carried down to the ship (10.158–184). Merry takes the washing of hands as properly an act of ritual, since every meal was regarded as a sacrificial feast in which the gods were the first sharers (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 10.182; [[deer]], [[feast-type-scene]]).
