---
summary: Eurylochus “took hold of my knees with both hands and begged me” (10.264); Circe “ran in under it and clasped my knees” (10.323); and Odysseus “entreated her by her knees” (10.481).
refs: [10.62-69, 10.264-269, 10.323-335, 10.480-486]
---

Three clasps of the knees, and one plea from the threshold. Aeolus refuses the plea (10.62–75); Eurylochus is allowed to stay at the ship (10.271–273); Circe is spared on the condition of the oath (10.336–347); and Odysseus is granted his return, by way of the house of Hades (10.483–495). Merry renders γούνων “by her knees,” comparing the genitive of Zeus' name with λίσσομαι at 2.68 (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 10.481; [[supplication]], [[circe]], [[eurylochus]]).
