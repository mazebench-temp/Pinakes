# ἄασαν (10.68), only here.
grc: [ἄασαν]
---

Odysseus' answer to Aeolus names two causes of the disaster: “My evil companions led me into blind folly, and with them sleep, / cruel sleep. But make it good, my friends; for you have the strength” (ἄασάν μʼ ἕταροί τε κακοὶ πρὸς τοῖσί τε ὕπνος / σχέτλιος, 10.68–69; see the translation's table of key words, ἄτη and ἀάω, and [[hypnos]], [[hetairos]] and [[hard]]). Merry sets the form beside the other aorists of ἀάω, ἀάσατο (*Iliad* 11.340), ἄασας (*Iliad* 8.237) and ἀασάμην (*Iliad* 9.119), and the contracted ἆσε and ἄσατο (11.61; *Iliad* 19.95) (W. W. Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 10.68). In his tale the folly is the whole crew's, “we were lost through our own folly” (ἀφραδίῃσιν, 10.27), and “through our own folly” again when the men must row (ματίῃ, 10.79; see [[who-is-to-blame]]). In Book 11 Elpenor's soul explains his fall with the same verb: ἆσέ με δαίμονος αἶσα κακὴ καὶ ἀθέσφατος οἶνος, “an evil fate from a god led me into blind folly, and endless wine” (11.61; see [[elpenor]]).
