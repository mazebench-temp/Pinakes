---
summary: Circe finds in Odysseus “a mind in your breast that cannot be bewitched” (10.329), and bids the companions eat “until you take heart again in your breasts” (10.461).
refs: [10.461]
---

The breast holds the mind and the heart. Circe, her drug defeated, says “But there is a mind in your breast that cannot be bewitched” (σοὶ δέ τις ἐν στήθεσσιν ἀκήλητος νόος ἐστίν, 10.329; see [[noos]] and [[thelgo]]), and she bids the companions, withered and spiritless after their wanderings, eat and drink “until you take heart again in your breasts” (θυμὸν ἐνὶ στήθεσσι λάβητε, 10.461; see [[thymos]]).
