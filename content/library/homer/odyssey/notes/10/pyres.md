# πυρήν (10.523; again 11.31, 24.69), first met in the Odyssey here; it applies in the Odyssey only (the Iliad's πυρήν is
# in its notes).
grc: [πυρήν]
en: [pyre]
---

At the pit Odysseus is to implore the strengthless heads of the dead, “vowing that, when you come to Ithaca, a barren heifer, the best you have, / you will sacrifice in your halls, and heap the pyre with good things” (10.522–523; see [[the-offering-to-the-dead]], [[the-strengthless-heads]] and [[ithaca]]). The pyre is an offering for the dead, not a funeral. Among the “good things” Eustathius, as W. W. Merry reports him, counts honey, flowers, cloaks and arms, such as custom laid on the pyres of the dead, and Merry points to the pyre of Patroclus (*Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 10.523), where Achilles set two-handled jars of honey and of ointment against the bier (*Iliad* 23.170).
