---
summary: Aeolus answers Odysseus' plea with a word of banishment (10.71); Odysseus opens his speech on Aeaea, “Hear my words, my companions” (10.189); Polites is the first to speak at Circe's door (10.224); and Odysseus tells his men where Circe is sending them (10.561).
---

The noun introduces speeches of weight. Aeolus' household falls silent, “but the father answered me” (ἠμείβετο μύθῳ, 10.71) with the sentence of banishment (see [[themis]]). On Aeaea Odysseus calls an assembly and begins, “Hear my words, my companions, though you are suffering evils” (κέκλυτέ μευ μύθων, 10.189); its double opening, κέκλυτε and then ὦ φίλοι, is quite contrary to Homeric custom, and Callistratus thought the verse had been put in by someone who did not know that Homer opens a speech with γάρ (W. W. Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 10.189 and his critical note; see the translation's note on 10.189 and [[assembly]]). At 10.188 Rhianus read μετὰ μῦθον ἔειπον for μετὰ πᾶσιν ἔειπον (Merry's critical note on 10.188), the half-verse that introduces Odysseus' last words to the companions in the book (10.561). At Circe's door “Polites, leader of men, began to speak among them” (μύθων ἦρχε, 10.224; see [[polites-the-companion]]).
