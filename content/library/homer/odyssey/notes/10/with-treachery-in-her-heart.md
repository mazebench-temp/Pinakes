---
summary: Odysseus to Circe: “and keeping me myself here, with treachery in your heart, you bid me / go into your chamber and go up into your bed” (10.339–340).
---

The participle that introduces Hera's speeches of deceit in the Iliad (*Iliad* 14.197, 14.300, 14.329, 19.106) is here the word of a suspicious man for the goddess who invites him to her bed, and he answers her with the oath that Hermes taught him (10.299–301, 10.342–345; [[oath-formulas]], [[circe]], [[dolos]]). Later in the poem Odysseus himself, disguised as a beggar, speaks δολοφρονέων to the suitors (18.51, 21.274).
