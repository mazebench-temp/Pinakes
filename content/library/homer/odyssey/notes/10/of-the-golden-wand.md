The nominative of the title with which Calypso greeted Hermes (5.87). Merry notes that Hermes' ῥάβδος is still a plain wand in Homer, and that the caduceus with two serpents winding round it is a later invention (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 10.277). Circe has a wand too, with which she strikes the companions into the sties and Odysseus in vain (10.238, 10.293, 10.319, 10.389); the god of the golden wand brings the drug that defeats hers (10.287–306; [[wand]], [[moly]], [[argeiphontes]], [[hermes]]).
