summary: “gifts from Aeolus, great-hearted son of Hippotes” (10.36); the companions remember “the mighty, great-hearted Cyclops, the eater of men” (10.200); and “out leapt the lot of great-hearted Eurylochus” (10.207).
---

μεγαλήτορος three times, of a host, of a monster and of a companion. Merry, on the Cyclops, allows it as a standing epithet or as “stout-hearted,” but notes that μεγαλήτωρ may also mean “arrogant, haughty,” as of Eurymedon, king of the Giants (7.58), and of the headstrong temper of Agamemnon (θυμὸς μεγαλήτωρ, *Iliad* 9.109; Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 10.200).
