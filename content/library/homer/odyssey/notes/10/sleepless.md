---
summary: Among the Laestrygonians “a man who took no sleep could have earned two wages” (10.84).
grc: [ἄυπνος]
en: [who took no sleep]
---

ἄυπνος ἀνήρ, the man who would do without sleep in a land where herdsman hails herdsman, for the paths of night and of day are close together (10.82–86). The explanations of the double wages, the scholia's gadflies, Crates' short nights of the north and Merry's evening twilight that runs into the dawn, are given in the translation's note on 10.81–86 ([[telepylus]], [[laestrygonians]]).
