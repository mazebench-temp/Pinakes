---
summary: The men whom the Laestrygonians spear like fish are carried off “to a joyless meal” (10.124), and leave nothing to bury. Elpenor dies on Circe's roof as the ship is leaving, and is left behind unburied (10.551–560). At the pit Odysseus is to vow the dead a barren heifer and a pyre heaped with good things when he comes home (10.521–525).
refs: [10.124, 10.523, 10.560]
---

The dead of the book get none of their due. Antiphates makes his meal of the first scout he seizes (10.116), and his people spear the men in the harbor “like fish” and carry them off “to a joyless meal” (10.124; see [[laestrygonians]] and [[antiphates-the-laestrygonian]]), as the Cyclops ate the companions in Book 9 and left nothing (see the Book 9 note). Elpenor, who falls from Circe's roof and breaks his neck, is not missed in the haste of departure (10.551–560; see [[elpenor]] and [[the-death-of-elpenor]]). In Book 11 his soul is the first to come to the pit, “for he had not yet been buried beneath the wide-wayed earth; / for we had left his body behind in the hall of Circe, / unwept and unburied, since another toil was pressing us” (11.52–54, a plain rendering), and in Book 12 the companions go back to Aeaea, burn his body with his arms, heap up his mound and set his oar on it (12.8–15).

The rite at the pit gives the dead what can still be given them: the drink offering, the prayers, and the vow that Odysseus will sacrifice a barren heifer in his halls “and heap the pyre with good things” (10.518–525; see [[the-offering-to-the-dead]]). Merry, on the vow, refers to the pyre of Patroclus and reports Eustathius' list of what custom put on the pyres of the dead, honey, flowers, cloaks and arms (W. W. Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 10.523; *Iliad* 23.165–177; see [[cremation]]).
