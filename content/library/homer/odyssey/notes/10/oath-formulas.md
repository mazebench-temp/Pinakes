summary: Hermes bids Odysseus make Circe “swear a great oath by the blessed gods” (10.299); Odysseus asks it of her (10.343–344); “So I spoke, and at once she swore that she would not, as I bade her. / But when she had sworn and completed the oath” (10.345–346); and later, “for I have already sworn you a mighty oath” (10.381).
grc: [ἀπώμνυεν]
en: [swore]
---

10.343–344 are the verses of Odysseus' demand of Calypso (5.178–179), and 10.345–346 those of Eurycleia's oath to Telemachus (2.377–378), with ἀπώμνυεν, the oath of denial, “swore that she would not” (see the Odyssey translation's formulas). The oath is the price of the bed: Circe had asked him to her bed “so that, having lain together / in bed and love, we may trust one another,” and he will not go until she has sworn (10.334–347; [[oath]], [[circe]], [[with-treachery-in-her-heart]]).
