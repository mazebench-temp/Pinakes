---
summary: Circe dresses on the morning of the departure and sets a head covering on her head (10.545).
---

Circe puts on her mantle and her golden girdle, “and on her head she set a head covering” (10.545; see [[girdle]], [[cloak]] and [[circe]]). The verse is Calypso's but for one word: there Murray's text has κεφαλῇ δʼ ἐφύπερθε καλύπτρην, “and over her head a head covering” (5.232), the reading of Aristarchus' copies, and here ἐπέθηκε, the reading of the commoner ones; Aristarchus read ἐφύπερθε here too (W. W. Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, critical notes on 5.232 and 10.545; see the translation's notes on 5.232 and 10.545).
