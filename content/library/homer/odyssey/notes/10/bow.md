---
summary: Odysseus slings his bow about his shoulders with his sword before he goes alone to Circe's house (10.261–262).
---

When Eurylochus has told of the companions' disappearance, “I slung about my shoulders my silver-studded sword, / great and made of bronze, and my bow about them” (10.261–262; see [[sword]] and [[odysseus]]). ἀμφὶ δὲ τόξα has no verb of its own: βαλόμην is to be supplied again, so that the bow too is slung about the shoulders (W. W. Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 10.262; see the translation's note on 10.262). He never uses it in the book: against Circe it is the sword and Hermes' drug that serve (see [[drugs]]).
