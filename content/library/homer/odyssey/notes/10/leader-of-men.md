---
summary: “Then Polites, leader of men, began to speak among them, / who was the nearest and dearest to me of my companions” (10.224–225), and Circe's “leader of the people” to Odysseus (10.538).
---

ὄρχαμος ἀνδρῶν, Peisistratus' title at Pylos (3.400, 3.454, 3.482), is here a companion's; Merry renders the two superlatives κήδιστος … κεδνότατός τε “nearest and dearest” (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 10.225; [[devoted]]). The address ὄρχαμε λαῶν, which closes the verse to Menelaus in Books 4 and 15, is said to Odysseus once, as Circe promises that the seer will come at once (10.538; [[the-blind-seer]]).
