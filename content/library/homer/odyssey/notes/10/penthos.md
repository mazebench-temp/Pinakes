---
summary: At Circe's table Odysseus will not eat, “held by strong grief” for his companions in the sties (10.375–376).
---

When the bath and the meal have been set before him, Odysseus sits “with my thoughts elsewhere, and my heart foreboded evil” (10.374), and Circe sees him “not putting out / my hands to the food, but held by strong grief” (κρατερὸν δέ με πένθος ἔχοντα, 10.375–376; see [[fasting]] and [[krateros]]). For κρατερόν a variant reads στυγερόν, “hateful”, which a scholium calls better (W. W. Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, his critical note on 10.376; see the translation's note on 10.374–378). The grief is for the companions penned in the sties, and he will not eat until he has seen them free (10.383–387; see [[hetairos]]).
