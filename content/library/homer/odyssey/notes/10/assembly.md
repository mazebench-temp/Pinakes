**The Laestrygonians' assembly.** The scouts find the wife of Antiphates, as huge as a mountain peak, “And at once she called from the place of assembly famous Antiphates, / her husband” (10.114–115; see [[antiphates-the-laestrygonian]]): the Laestrygonians, W. W. Merry notes, having a king and an ἀγορή, are more advanced in the externals of civilization than the Cyclopes, who have neither assemblies nor laws (*Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 10.114; 9.112; see [[laestrygonians]] and [[cyclopes]]).

**Odysseus' assembly.** On the third morning on Aeaea, “then I called an assembly and spoke among them all” (10.188), and he tells his companions that they do not know where the darkness is nor the dawn, that he has seen smoke in the middle of the island, and that he does not think there is any plan left (10.189–197; see [[hetairos]]). Rhianus, Merry reports from the scholia, read μετὰ μῦθον ἔειπον for μετὰ πᾶσιν ἔειπον (critical note on 10.188).
