Eurylochus' band stand in the doorway, “and they heard Circe within singing with a lovely voice / as she walked to and fro at a great immortal loom, such as are / the fine and lovely and splendid works of goddesses” (ἱστὸν ἐποιχομένης μέγαν ἄμβροτον, 10.221–223; see [[circe]], [[loom]] and the translation's table of key words, ἱστὸν ἐποιχομένη). So Hermes found Calypso in her cave, “singing with a lovely voice”, as she “walked to and fro at the loom and wove with a golden shuttle” (5.61–62; see [[calypso]]), and so Agamemnon foresaw Chryseis in his house, walking to and fro at the loom (*Iliad* 1.31). Polites tells the others what they hear: “someone within, walking to and fro at a great loom, / is singing beautifully, and the whole floor rings all round with it, / either a goddess or a woman” (10.226–228; see [[polites-the-companion]] and [[seeing-the-gods]]), and Eurylochus repeats it in his report (10.254–255). What she is they learn when they have drunk her drink (10.229–243; see [[thelgo]]).
