The book counts few days: nine of sailing and six of rowing (10.28, 10.80), and then the dawns of Aeaea, the third day on the shore (10.142–144), the morning after the feast of the stag (10.187), and, after a year whose dawns are not told, the morning of the departure for the house of Hades, which comes at once at the end of Circe's speech (10.541). The last is a verse of the Odyssey's own (again 12.142, 15.56, 20.91; see the Odyssey translation's formulas; [[with-lovely-tresses]], [[early-born]], [[rosy-fingered]], [[golden-throned]], [[eos]]).
