# "χεῖρας νιψάμενοι" (10.182), the plural of the verse in which Telemachus washes his hands before praying (2.261; later
# 12.336), first met here; it applies in the Odyssey only.
grc: ["χεῖρας νιψάμενοι"]
en: [for me to wash]
---

**Before the feast of the stag.** When the companions have gazed their fill at the great stag that Odysseus has brought to the ship, “they washed their hands and made ready a glorious feast” (10.182; see [[deer]] and [[feast]]). χεῖρας νιψάμενοι, W. W. Merry explains, is properly an act of ritual, and it is used here because every meal was in truth a sacrificial feast in which the gods were the first sharers (*Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 10.182; see [[sacrifice]]). The same words, in the singular, are Telemachus' washing before his prayer to Athena (2.261; see [[prayer]]).

**In Circe's house.** After his bath Odysseus is seated, and “a handmaid brought water for the hands in a pitcher and poured it, / a fine golden one, over a silver basin, / for me to wash” (10.368–370; see [[water-for-the-hands]] and [[basin-and-pitcher]]), the verses of every guest's meal in the Odyssey; but he will not eat until his companions are free (10.373–387; see [[odysseus]]).
