summary: Odysseus goes “through the sacred glens” toward Circe's house (10.275); her handmaids are born “of the sacred rivers” (10.351); and her house is “the sacred house of Circe” (10.426, 10.445, 10.554).

Merry takes ἱεράς of the glens as belonging to Circe, and so, he notes, her house is called sacred (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 10.275). The rivers are sacred in the verses that make the handmaids nymphs, born of springs, groves and rivers (10.350–351; [[circe]], [[nymphs]]).
