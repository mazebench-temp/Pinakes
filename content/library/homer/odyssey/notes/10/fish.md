summary: The Laestrygonians, stoning Odysseus' ships in their harbor, spear the men “like fish” and carry them off to a joyless meal (10.124).
# ἰχθῦς (10.124), the accusative plural; in the Odyssey it is fish wherever it occurs (5.53, 12.331, 19.113). The
# Iliad's ἰχθύς (21.127) and ἰχθὺς (23.692) are in its Book 21 note.
grc: [ἰχθῦς]
---

“and spearing them like fish they carried them off to a joyless meal” (10.124; see [[like-fish]] and [[laestrygonians]]). ἰχθῦς is the accusative plural. W. W. Merry pictures the men thrown into the water when the stones have broken the ships, and speared as they float, “Harpooning them like fish” (*Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 10.124); Aristophanes read εἴροντες, which the scholion explains as threading them together into strings (Merry's critical note on 10.124; see the translation's note on 10.124). The men are the Laestrygonians' catch and their food, as their king had already seized one of the scouts and “made ready his meal” (10.116; see [[antiphates-the-laestrygonian]]). The sea of Odysseus' wanderings is the “fish-filled sea” in Circe's words, who knows “all the sorrows you suffered on the fish-filled sea” (10.458), and says that Teiresias will tell him “how you shall go over the fish-filled sea” (10.540; see [[fish-filled]]).
