---
summary: The companions envy the fine treasures Odysseus brings home from Troy (10.40–42), and Circe bids him put the ship's possessions and tackle away in caves before he brings his companions to her house (10.403–405, 10.423–424).
---

**The treasures from Troy.** As the ships near Ithaca the companions say to one another: “Many fine treasures he is bringing from Troy / out of the spoil, while we, who have gone the same road to its end, / are going home, all of us, with empty hands” (10.40–42; see [[hetairos]] and [[skin-of-the-winds]]). κειμήλια, “treasures”, are things laid up, and ληίδος is a partitive genitive with it, “from the spoil”: Aristarchus took Τροίης with ληίδος, “Trojan spoil”, while Merry takes it as “from the land of Troy” (W. W. Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 10.40; see the translation's note on 10.40). They suspect that Aeolus' skin holds more of the same, gold and silver (10.43–45; see [[gold-and-silver]]).

**In the caves.** Circe sends Odysseus back to the shore: “First of all drag the ship up onto the land, / and put your possessions and all the tackle away in caves, / and come back yourself and bring your trusty companions” (10.403–405; see [[ships]] and [[circe]]), and he gives the order in the same words (10.423–424). ὅπλα are the ship's gear, ropes and sails (see [[ships]]).
