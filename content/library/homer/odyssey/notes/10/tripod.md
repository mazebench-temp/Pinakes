---
summary: The fourth of Circe's handmaids brings water and kindles a great fire under a great tripod, and when the water boils she bathes Odysseus from it, mixing it to suit his heart (10.358–363).
---

“And the fourth brought water and kindled a great fire / under a great tripod; and the water grew warm. / But when the water had boiled in the gleaming bronze, / she set me in a bathtub and bathed me from the great tripod” (10.358–361; see [[bathing]], [[handmaids]], [[fire]] and [[bronze]]). The tripod is the cauldron of the bath, as in the Iliad's and the Odyssey's verses of heating water for a bath, “to set a great tripod over the fire” (*Iliad* 18.344, 22.443, 23.40; 8.434). λόε, W. W. Merry explains, is “she washed me out of a great cauldron, over head and shoulders, after she had mixed it to my taste”, not quite boiling, that is, but well tempered (*Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 10.361, 10.362; see the translation's row θυμῆρες). ἤνοπι, “gleaming”, said of the bronze, is of uncertain derivation: the ancients compounded it of the negative ἀν- and ὤψ, “sight”, to make it “dazzling”, and modern scholars refer it to a root meaning “shine” or “burn” (Merry on 10.360; see [[gleaming-bronze]]).
