summary: Odysseus' companions hear Circe within, singing with a lovely voice as she walks to and fro at a great immortal loom (10.221–223); Polites and then Eurylochus tell of it in the same words (10.226–227, 10.254).
# ἱστόν at 10.506 is the mast, set up for the voyage to the house of Hades, and is excepted in the entry.
---

At the doorway of the house in the glens the companions “heard Circe within singing with a lovely voice / as she walked to and fro at a great immortal loom, such as are / the fine and lovely and splendid works of goddesses” (10.221–223; see [[circe]], [[weaving]] and [[house]]). So Calypso sang at her loom when Hermes came to her cave, “and wove with a golden shuttle” (5.61–62; see [[calypso]]), and the Iliad's captive Chryseis was to walk to and fro at the loom in Agamemnon's house (*Iliad* 1.31; see the translation's row ἱστὸν ἐποιχομένη). Polites, who speaks first, hears only “someone within, walking to and fro at a great loom”, “either a goddess or a woman” (10.226–228; see [[polites-the-companion]]), and Eurylochus repeats the words in his report to Odysseus (10.254–255; see [[eurylochus]]).
