---
summary: Merry draws on Herodotus three times for this book: the floating island of Chemmis, which Herodotus never saw move, for the floating island of Aeolus; Aea, the Colchis of Medea's father, beside Circe's Aeaea; and the oracle of the dead on the river Acheron in Thesprotia, among the places where the dead were consulted.
---

- **A floating island.** The Egyptians say that Chemmis, an island in a deep, broad lake beside the temple at Buto, floats; “I myself saw it neither floating nor moving”, Herodotus says, “and I marvel to hear it, whether an island truly floats” (*Histories* 2.156.2, a plain rendering). Merry quotes the passage to show the plain sense of πλωτή, “floating”, in the verse on Aeolus' island, the sense that Aristarchus gave the word, and finds that Herodotus' doubts only bring out the meaning more strongly (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 10.3; see the translation's note on 10.1–3): [[aeolia]].
- **Aea.** The Greeks sailed in a long ship to Aea in Colchis and the river Phasis and carried off the king's daughter Medea (*Histories* 1.2.2); the Argo was sailing to Aea for the fleece when Heracles was left behind at Aphetae (*Histories* 7.193.2); and Cytissorus son of Phrixus came from Aea in Colchis to save his grandfather Athamas (*Histories* 7.197.3). Merry cites these chapters for Aea, the old name of Colchis, whose king Aeetes is Circe's brother in the Odyssey, beside the eastern form of Circe's story (Merry on 10.135; 10.137): [[aeetes]], [[aeaea]] and [[circe]].
- **The oracle of the dead.** Periander, tyrant of Corinth, sent messengers to the [[thesprotians|Thesprotians]] on the river Acheron, to the oracle of the dead, to ask about a deposit left by a guest; his dead wife Melissa appeared and would not tell, for she was cold and naked, since the clothes buried with her had not been burned (*Histories* 5.92η.2). Merry cites it, with Pausanias, for one of the oldest oracles of the dead, near the Acherusian lake in Thesprotia, in his note on the pit that Circe bids Odysseus dig (Merry on 10.517): [[acheron]], [[the-offering-to-the-dead]] and [[pausanias]].
