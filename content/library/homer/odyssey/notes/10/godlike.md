---
summary: Odysseus divides the companions: “one I led myself, and godlike Eurylochus the other” (10.205).
---

θεοειδής, “godlike in form,” is the first epithet Eurylochus has in the poem; in the next verses the lot falls to him, and he is “great-hearted Eurylochus” (10.206–207; [[great-hearted]], [[lots]], [[eurylochus]]).
