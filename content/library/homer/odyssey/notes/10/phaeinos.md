---
summary: Aeolus binds the skin in the ship “with a shining cord / of silver” (10.23–24), and three times Circe “came out and opened the shining doors” (10.230, 10.256, 10.312).
grc: [φαεινὰς]
---

Merry takes the cord, μέρμις, as silver drawn into a wire fine enough to plait into a cord that could tie the neck of the skin so tight that not a breath of wind could slip past, and finds in it an advanced stage of metal-working (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 10.23; see the Odyssey translation's key words, μέρμις). The doors are in the verse of Circe's welcome, told by the narrator, by Eurylochus and of Odysseus himself (10.230–231 = 10.256–257, 10.312–313; [[door]], [[circe]]).
