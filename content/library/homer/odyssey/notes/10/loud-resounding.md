---
summary: Where Odysseus must go, “there is a rock, and the meeting of the two loud-resounding rivers” (10.515).
---

The epithet of the sea's shores and of the echoing colonnade is given once to rivers, Pyriphlegethon and Cocytus, which flow into Acheron (10.513–515). Merry takes ἐριδούπων to point to the noise of a cataract: the two streams run higher than the Acheron and plunge over a ledge of rock where they meet, and the rock may part their falls; he wonders whether the Leucadian rock that the souls of the suitors pass on their way below alludes to it (24.11; Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 10.515; [[rivers]], [[styx]]).
