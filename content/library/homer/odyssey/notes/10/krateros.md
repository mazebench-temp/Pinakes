---
summary: Odysseus to Eurylochus, “for harsh necessity has come upon me” (10.273); Circe sees him “held by strong grief” (10.376); and she reassures him, “for I have already sworn you a mighty oath” (10.381).
en: [harsh]
---

κρατερὴ ἀνάγκη is the Iliad's “harsh necessity,” as in Hector's words to Andromache (*Iliad* 6.458). At 10.376 a variant reads στυγερόν for κρατερόν, which a scholion calls better (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, critical note on 10.376; see the translation's note on 10.374–378). The mighty oath is the one she swore at Hermes' bidding (10.343–345; [[oath-formulas]]).
