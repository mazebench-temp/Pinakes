---
summary: On Aeaea, after the feast of the stag: “then I called an assembly and spoke among them all” (10.188), and Odysseus tells his companions of the island and the smoke (10.189–197).
refs: [10.188-202]
---

The verse of Book 9 (9.171), and again an assembly of one speaker: no one answers, and the men weep, remembering Antiphates and the Cyclops (10.198–202). Merry finds the double opening, κέκλυτε … ὦ φίλοι, contrary to Homeric custom, and reports Callistratus' view that the first verse was put in front by someone who did not know Homer's way of opening a speech with γάρ (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 10.189 and his critical note; see the translation's note on 10.189; [[assembly]], [[hear-me]]).
