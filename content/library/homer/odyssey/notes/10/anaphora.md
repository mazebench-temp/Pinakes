---
summary: Odysseus' bewilderment on Aeaea: “we do not know where the darkness is, nor where the dawn, / nor where the sun, who brings light to mortals, goes beneath the earth, / nor where he rises” (10.190–192).
refs: [10.190-192]
---

ὅπῃ … οὐδʼ ὅπῃ … οὐδʼ ὅπῃ … οὐδʼ ὅπῃ: four clauses for the two pairs of the sun's course. Merry takes ζόφος and ἠώς as an exhaustive division of the world, so that Odysseus means only that he has no idea where they are (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 10.190; see the translation's note on 10.190; [[bringing-light-to-mortals]]).
