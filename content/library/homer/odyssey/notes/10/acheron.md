---
summary: Acheron is the last landmark of Circe's directions: beyond Oceanus, at the house of Hades, Pyriphlegethon and Cocytus flow into it by a rock, and there Odysseus is to draw close and dig the pit for the dead (10.513–517).
---

**The last landmark.** Circe's directions go step by step: the North Wind will carry the ship; across Oceanus there is a shore of good soil with the groves of Persephone; there the ship is to be beached, and Odysseus is to go on alone into “the dank house of Hades” (10.505–512; see [[boreas]], [[the-groves-of-persephone]] and [[hades]]). Then come the rivers: “There into Acheron flow Pyriphlegethon / and Cocytus, which is a branch of the water of Styx, / and there is a rock, and the meeting of the two loud-resounding rivers” (10.513–515; see the entry). ἔνθα, “there”, must mean at the entrance of the house of Hades, as W. W. Merry takes it (*Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 10.513), and the next verse begins with the same word: ἔνθα δʼ ἔπειθʼ, ἥρως, χριμφθεὶς πέλας, “there then, hero, draw close” (10.516), and dig the pit for the offerings to the dead (10.517–537). The rivers mark the place where the living man is to stop. When the voyage is told in the next book, Odysseus says only that they went along the stream of Oceanus “until we came to the place that Circe had pointed out” (11.21–22).
