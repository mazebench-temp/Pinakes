---
summary: Circe seats Odysseus on a silver-studded chair with a footstool under his feet, before the drugged drink and again before the meal (10.314–315 = 10.366–367).
---

“She led me in and seated me on a silver-studded chair, / fine and intricately worked; and there was a footstool under my feet” (10.314–315; see [[throne]] and [[circe]]). The verse of the footstool is the Iliad's, where Charis seats Thetis on a silver-studded throne (*Iliad* 18.389–390), and the Odyssey's for Telemachus' guest and for Helen (1.131, 4.136). Circe gives it twice, first as the seat of a guest whom she means to bewitch with her drink (10.316–319; see [[kykeon]]), then, when she has sworn her oath and he has bathed, as the seat of an honored guest at a meal (10.366–367; see [[xenia]] and [[bathing]]).
