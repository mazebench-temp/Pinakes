summary: Odysseus “pondered in my noble heart” in the storm (10.50), “pondered in my mind and in my heart” on the lookout (10.151), and “pondered in my mind” whether to kill Eurylochus (10.438); and going to Circe's house, “my heart heaved with many thoughts as I went” (10.309).
grc: ["μερμήριξα δʼ ἔπειτα", "κατὰ θυμὸν ἀμύμονα μερμήριξα", "μετὰ φρεσὶ μερμήριξα"]
---

μερμηρίζω in the first person, the narrator's own deliberations, each followed by a choice ([[decision-scene]]). Merry connects the verb with μέρμερος and with the root of “care” and “thought,” as in Latin *memor*, not with μέρος, “part,” from which the idea of a divided mind had been drawn (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 10.151). 10.309 is Menelaus' verse on Pharos (4.427, 4.572; see the Odyssey translation's formulas; [[blameless]]).
