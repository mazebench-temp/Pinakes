---
summary: Odysseus rouses his companions “with soothing words, standing beside each man” (10.173 = 10.547), and the companions hold him back from Eurylochus “with soothing words, one from one side and one from another” (10.442).
---

The first verse comes again in Book 12 (12.207); Merry takes ἄνδρα ἕκαστον as the collective ἑταίρους in distributive form (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 10.173). 10.441–442 are the verses of the companions' plea after the first taunt to the Cyclops (9.492–493), used now to save a companion from Odysseus' sword (see the Odyssey translation's formulas; [[eurylochus]], [[soft]]).
