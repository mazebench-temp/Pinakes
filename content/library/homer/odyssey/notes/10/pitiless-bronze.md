---
summary: The companions are to flay and burn “the sheep that lie there slaughtered with the pitiless bronze” (10.532).
---

μῆλα, τὰ δὴ κατάκειτʼ ἐσφαγμένα νηλέι χαλκῷ: the blade is the one that has cut the victims' throats over the pit (10.527–532). Merry calls the present κατάκειτʼ, “lie,” quite necessary: κατέκειτʼ, “lay,” is right in the next book, where the verse is told as done (11.45), and has been carried over from there (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 10.532 and his critical note; see the translation's note on 10.532; [[sacrifice]]).
