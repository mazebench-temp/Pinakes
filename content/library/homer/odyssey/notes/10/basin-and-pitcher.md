---
summary: After his bath in Circe's house a handmaid pours water for Odysseus' hands from a golden pitcher over a silver basin (10.368–370).
---

“And a handmaid brought water for the hands in a pitcher and poured it, / a fine golden one, over a silver basin, / for me to wash” (10.368–370 = 1.136–138, 4.52–54, 7.172–174; see [[water-for-the-hands]], [[hand-washing]] and [[handmaids]]). The pitcher of gold and the basin of silver belong to the welcome of every guest in the Odyssey; in Circe's house the verses come after four handmaids have already spread the chairs, set the tables, mixed the wine and bathed the guest, and Köchly, as W. W. Merry quotes him, judged them inserted most ineptly here (*Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, critical note on 10.348; see the translation's note on 10.368–372). La Roche, Merry adds, thought them no older than the thirteenth century, since Eustathius and the scholia do not know them (critical note on 10.368–372).
