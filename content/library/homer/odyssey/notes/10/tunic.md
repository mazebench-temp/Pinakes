---
summary: Odysseus and his companions are given tunics with their cloaks after their baths in Circe's house (10.365, 10.451), and Circe dresses Odysseus in cloak and tunic on the morning of the departure (10.542).
# "tunics" (10.451; first at 4.50, where the slave women dress the guests in Menelaus' house), the plural of the entry's
# English; it applies in the Odyssey only.
en: [tunics]
---

The tunic is the garment next to the skin, and in the verses of the welcome it goes on with the cloak after the bath: the handmaid “put a fine cloak and a tunic around me” (10.365; see [[bathing]] and [[cloak]]), and Circe puts “woolly cloaks and tunics” around the companions (10.451). On the morning of the departure “she put a cloak and a tunic about me as clothing” (10.542; see [[clothing]] and [[circe]]), as on the morning Odysseus begins his raft on Calypso's island he puts on cloak and tunic himself (5.229).
