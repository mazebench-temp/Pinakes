---
summary: Circe and Aeetes “were born of the Sun, who brings light to mortals” (10.138), and Odysseus, lost on her island, does not know “where the sun, who brings light to mortals, goes beneath the earth” (10.191).
grc: [φαεσιμβρότου]
en: [who brings light to mortals]
---

Merry takes φαεσίμβροτος, like φθισίμβροτος (22.297) and the later κλεόμβροτος and ἄμβροτος, as showing how βροτός was formed: from a root *mor-*, μροτός became μβροτός by the insertion of β, and βροτός when the initial μ fell away (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 10.138). The second use is in Odysseus' speech of bewilderment, “we do not know where the darkness is, nor where the dawn” (10.190–192), which Merry takes as a general way of saying that he has no idea where they are (Merry on 10.190; see the translation's note on 10.190; [[helios]], [[eos]]).
