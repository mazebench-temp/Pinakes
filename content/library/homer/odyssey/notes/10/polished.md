---
summary: Circe's house, “built / of polished stones” (10.210–211, 10.252–253), and the “polished table” that the handmaid draws up for the meal (10.370).
grc: [ξεστοῖσιν]
---

ξεστοῖσιν λάεσσι, of dressed stone, as the stones on which Nestor sits before his doors (3.406) and the seats of the Phaeacians' place of assembly (8.6); Merry joins λάεσσι with τετυγμένα, “built of polished stones” (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 10.210). The table is the verse of the meal set before a guest (10.370 = 1.138; [[water-for-the-hands]], [[house]]).
