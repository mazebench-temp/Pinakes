---
summary: Odysseus' men feast on meat and sweet wine after the stag is killed, and for a year in Circe's house (10.184, 10.468, 10.477). Circe stirs her drugged mixture into Pramnian wine (10.234–236), her handmaid mixes sweet wine for Odysseus in a silver bowl (10.356–357), and Circe bids the companions eat and drink wine until their spirit returns (10.460–465). Wine is the second of the drink offerings to the dead (10.519), and Elpenor falls from Circe's roof sodden with wine (10.555).
---

**The feasts.** The skin that Aeolus gives Odysseus holds not wine but the winds, though ἀσκός is the word of the wineskin (10.19; see [[skin-of-the-winds]]). On Circe's island, after the stag is carried to the ship, “all day long, until the sun went down, / we sat feasting on untold meat and sweet wine” (10.183–184; see [[feast]], [[untold]] and [[deer]]), and the same verses tell the year in Circe's house, “every day, for a full year's round” (10.467–468), and its last day (10.476–477; see [[sunset-lines]]).

**Circe's wine.** For the companions Circe stirs cheese, barley meal and pale honey into Pramnian wine, with her drugs in the food (10.234–236; see [[kykeon]], [[pramnian]] and [[drugs]]). When she has sworn her oath and the handmaids are preparing the house, “the third mixed wine sweet to the heart in a mixing bowl, / sweet wine in a silver bowl, and served out golden cups” (10.356–357; see [[mixing-bowl]], [[cup]], [[honey-sweet]] and [[handmaids]]). To the restored companions, withered with their hard wandering, she says: “But come, eat food and drink wine, / until you take heart again in your breasts” (10.460–461; see [[hetairos]]).

**The offering and Elpenor.** At the pit by the house of Hades Odysseus is to pour a drink offering to all the dead, “first with honey and milk, then with sweet wine, / and the third time with water” (10.519–520; see [[honey]], [[milk]] and [[libation]]). On the morning of the departure the youngest of the companions, Elpenor, who had lain down apart on the roof of Circe's house, “longing for the cool air, had lain down to sleep, sodden with wine”, and falls to his death (10.552–560; see [[elpenor]], [[house]] and [[wine-sodden]]), as the Cyclops had fallen asleep, sodden with Odysseus' wine (9.374).
