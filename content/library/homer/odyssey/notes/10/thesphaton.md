---
summary: After the year on Aeaea the companions call Odysseus to his homecoming, “if it is decreed for you to be saved and to reach / your high-roofed house and your native land” (10.473–474).
---

“Strange one, think now at last of your native land, / if it is decreed for you to be saved and to reach / your high-roofed house and your native land” (εἴ τοι θέσφατόν ἐστι, 10.472–474; see [[hetairos]], [[strange-one]] and [[nostos]]). The decree they speak of is the one Zeus gave in Book 5 in nearly the same words, “For so it is his fate to see his friends and to reach / his high-roofed house and his native land” (5.41–42), and the one the Cyclops prayed against, “But if it is his fate to see his friends and reach / his well-built house and his native land” (9.532–533; see [[curses]]); Merry's text reads οἶκον ἐυκτίμενον here as there (see the translation's note on 10.474). The man who knows the decree is Teiresias, to whom Circe sends him to learn “the way and the measures of your path / and your homecoming” (10.538–540; see [[teiresias]] and [[prophecy]]). At 10.549 two Vienna manuscripts read θέσφατα, “what is decreed”, for πότνια, which Bekker would adopt (W. W. Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, his critical note on 10.549; see the translation's note on 10.549).
