# ἱμερόεις (10.398), ἱμείρετε (10.431), ἱμείρων (10.555), each only here.
grc: [ἱμερόεις, ἱμείρετε, ἱμείρων]
en: [longing, long for]
---

Three longings run through the book. When Circe has restored the companions and they know Odysseus, “on them all came the longing for lament, and all round the house / rang terribly” (πᾶσιν δʼ ἱμερόεις ὑπέδυ γόος, 10.398–399; see [[lament]]): ἱμερόεις, elsewhere “lovely”, here describes a lament they long for, in a variation on the verse “in them all he roused the longing for lament” (4.183 = *Iliad* 23.108; see [[the-longing-for-lament]]). Eurylochus turns the word into reproach: “Ah, wretched men, where are we going? Why do you long for these evils, / to go down to the hall of Circe” (τί κακῶν ἱμείρετε τούτων, 10.431–432; see [[eurylochus]] and [[wretch]]). And Elpenor, “longing for the cool air”, lies down to sleep on the roof, sodden with wine (ψύχεος ἱμείρων, 10.555; see [[elpenor]] and the translation's table of key words, ψῦχος).
