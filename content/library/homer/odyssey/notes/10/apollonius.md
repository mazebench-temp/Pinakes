---
summary: Merry sets beside Circe's tame beasts the beasts of the Argonautica, which follow her like sheep following the shepherd; and a scholiast says that Apollonius, following Hesiod, brought Circe on the Sun's chariot to the western island off Tyrrhenia.
---

- **The beasts.** Circe's mountain wolves and lions, bewitched by her drugs, fawn on the men like dogs on their master (10.212–219). In the *Argonautica*, Merry notes, the beasts follow Circe “as sheep come in throngs from the folds following the shepherd” (*Argonautica* 4.672, as Merry quotes it, a plain rendering; Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 10.212; the *Argonautica* is not read here): [[circe]] and [[the-dogs-fawning-on-their-master]].
- **Circe in the West.** A scholiast reports that Apollonius, following Hesiod, said that Circe came on the chariot of the Sun to the island lying off Tyrrhenia, and called it Hesperian because it lies toward the sunset (*Catalogue of Women* fr. 46 Evelyn-White): [[circe]], [[aeaea]] and [[hesiod]].
