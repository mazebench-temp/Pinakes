---
summary: When the winds have driven him back to the Aeolian island, Odysseus with a herald and one companion sits down on the threshold of Aeolus' house, beside the doorposts (10.59–63).
---

“Then I took a herald with me and one companion / and went to the glorious house of Aeolus; and I found him / feasting beside his wife and his children. / And coming into the house, beside the doorposts, on the threshold / we sat down” (10.59–63; see [[aeolus]], [[door]] and [[heralds]]). So Odysseus will sit on the threshold of his own hall as a beggar (17.339, Greek only): it is a humbler place, W. W. Merry notes, than the hearth to which he walked up the hall in Alcinous' house (7.153), though Nitzsch observed that the hearth was not regarded as a sanctuary until later times (*Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 10.62; see [[supplication]]). The humble place does not save him: Aeolus sends him away as a man hated by the gods (10.72–76; see [[xenia]]).
