---
summary: From the lookout of Aeaea “smoke appeared to me rising from the wide-wayed earth” (10.149).
---

Climbing a rugged lookout on Aeaea, Odysseus sees the first sign of Circe's house: “and smoke appeared to me rising from the wide-wayed earth, / in the halls of Circe, through the thick brush and the woods” (καί μοι ἐείσατο καπνός, 10.149–150; see [[spies-and-scouts]] and [[wide-wayed-earth]]). The book's likenesses use the other verb, ἔοικα: the Laestrygonians “not like men, but like the Giants” (10.120), Hermes “in the likeness of a young man” (10.278) and the swine driven out of the sty “looking like fat hogs nine years old” (10.390; see [[shape-changing]]).
