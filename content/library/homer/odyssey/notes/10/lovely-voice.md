---
summary: The companions hear “Circe within singing with a lovely voice / as she walked to and fro at a great immortal loom” (10.221–222).
---

ὀπὶ καλῇ, the half-verse of Calypso at her loom (5.61), is the first sign of the goddess: Polites hears someone “singing beautifully, and the whole floor rings all round with it, / either a goddess or a woman” (10.227–228), and Eurylochus, telling it at the ship, says only that she “was singing in a clear voice” (λίγʼ ἄειδεν, 10.254; see the Odyssey translation's key words, ἱστὸν ἐποιχομένη; [[loom]], [[circe]], [[calypso]]).
