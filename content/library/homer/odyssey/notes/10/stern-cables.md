summary: In the harbor of the Laestrygonians Odysseus alone keeps his ship outside, making its cables fast to a rock, and when the stoning begins he cuts them with his sword and escapes (10.95–96, 10.126–127). On Aeaea he plaits a cable of willow shoots to bind the feet of the stag (10.166–168).
# Forms first met here (they apply in the Odyssey only): πείσματʼ (10.127), and πεῖσμα (10.167, the cable of willow
# shoots; later 13.77, 22.465). The Book 6 note has πείσματα, the Book 9 note πείσματος.
grc: [πείσματʼ, πεῖσμα]
---

**Outside the harbor.** The other ships are moored close together within the hollow harbor, where no wave ever swells, “but I alone kept my black ship outside, / there at the far edge, making the cables fast to a rock” (10.95–96; see [[harbor]], [[ships]] and [[laestrygonians]]): W. W. Merry renders, “having made fast my hawser from a rock”, joining ἐκ to δήσας (*Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 10.95). When the Laestrygonians stone the ships in the harbor, “I drew the sharp sword from beside my thigh / and with it cut the cables of my dark-prowed ship” (10.126–127; see [[sword]] and [[dark-prowed]]), and his ship alone escapes to the open sea (10.128–132). There is no time to loose the cables, the verse of departure in the earlier books (9.178, 9.562): they are cut.

**The cable of willow shoots.** On Aeaea Odysseus kills the stag and binds its feet with a cable he plaits himself: “then I plucked brushwood and willow shoots, / and plaiting a cable, about a fathom's length, well twisted from both ends, / I bound together the feet of the dread monster” (10.166–168; see [[deer]], [[willow]] and [[spear]]). πεῖσμα is the word of the ship's cables (see the translation's row ὅσον τʼ ὄργυιαν). ἀμφοτέρωθεν may describe the whole length of the rope, but Merry prefers to join it with πλεξάμενος, of a rope of two strands passed across and across in the plaiting, “till it was well twisted” (on 10.167).
