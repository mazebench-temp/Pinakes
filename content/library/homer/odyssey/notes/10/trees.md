summary: The trees of Book 10 are the thick brush and woods of Aeaea, through which Odysseus sees the smoke of Circe's house (10.150, 10.197); the ilex and the oak, whose acorns, with the fruit of the cornel, Circe throws to the companions she has made swine (10.242); and the tall poplars and fruit-losing willows of Persephone's groves beyond Oceanus (10.509–510).

**The woods of Aeaea.** From the lookout Odysseus sees smoke rising “in the halls of Circe, through the thick brush and the woods” (10.150, again 10.197; see [[aeaea]] and [[circe]]). The stag that he kills is coming down to the river “from its pasture in the woods” (10.159; see [[deer]]), Circe's house stands “in the glens” (10.210, 10.252; see the translation's row βῆσσα), and Hermes leaves Odysseus and goes back to Olympus “across the wooded island” (10.308; see [[wooded]]).

**Acorns and cornel.** “and before them Circe / threw ilex acorns and oak acorns and the fruit of the cornel / to eat, such as swine that sleep on the ground always eat” (10.241–243; see [[boar]] and the translation's row ἄκυλος, βάλανος, κράνεια). ἄκυλος is the acorn of the ilex, the holm oak: the Suda calls it the fruit of the πρῖνος (Suda α 1045), and W. W. Merry quotes Pliny, who says that Homer called the ilex's acorn *acylos* and so distinguished it from the acorn proper (*Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 10.242, quoting Pliny, *Natural History* 16.8 in his numbering). βάλανος is the fruit of the oak, the φηγός (Merry on 10.242), the acorn on which Eumaeus' swine will be found feeding (13.409). The cornel, κράνεια, is one of the trees of the Iliad's forest that the winds dash together, the oak, the ash and the smooth-barked cornel (*Iliad* 16.765–769; see [[the-winds-in-the-forest]] and [[smooth-barked]]). All three were food for swine: Columella names the oak, the holm oak and the cornel among the trees whose fruits, ripening at different seasons, feed a herd of swine through almost the whole year (*On Agriculture* 7.9.6), as Merry notes of the cornel (on 10.242).

**Poplars and willows.** The groves of Persephone beyond Oceanus are “tall poplars and willows that lose their fruit” (10.510; see [[willow]] and [[persephone]]). The Odyssey's poplar grows by water, around Calypso's cave, in Athena's grove beside a spring on Scheria, and here by the stream of Oceanus (5.64, 6.291–292; see the Book 9 note). Merry renders the verse “both tall aspens, and willows that shed their fruit” (on 10.508).
