---
summary: Pyriphlegethon is named once, in Circe's directions: it flows with Cocytus into Acheron, at the entrance to the house of Hades (10.513–515).
---

**One verb for two rivers.** Ἀχέροντα Πυριφλεγέθων τε ῥέουσιν / Κώκυτός θʼ (10.513–514): the plural verb stands between two subjects in the singular, a figure the grammarians called the σχῆμα Ἀλκμανικόν, “Alcman's figure”, because, Eustathius says, Alcman used it to excess; W. W. Merry quotes a line of Alcman that names Castor and Polydeuces in the same way (*Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 10.513). The two rivers that flow into Acheron are named together and meet at the rock, “the meeting of the two loud-resounding rivers” (10.515; see [[acheron]], [[cocytus]] and [[loud-resounding]]); Pyriphlegethon is given no epithet of its own, and the fire is only in its name.
