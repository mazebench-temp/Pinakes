summary: Odysseus' twelve ships sail from Aeolus' island with the winds bound in a skin and are driven back when it is opened (10.23–55). In the harbor of the Laestrygonians eleven are stoned and shattered, and only Odysseus' ship, moored outside, escapes (10.87–132). On Aeaea it is dragged ashore and its tackle stored in caves for the year with Circe (10.403–404, 10.423–424), and Circe sends it to the house of Hades with the North Wind and no pilot (10.501–511).
# The ship's tackle, "ὅπλα τε πάντα" (10.404, 10.424; later 12.410), as a phrase: in the Iliad the same words are
# Hephaestus' tools (18.409, 18.412), and ὅπλα alone is also arms. It applies in the Odyssey only.

**From Aeolus' island.** Aeolus binds the skin of the winds “in the hollow ship” and sends the West Wind “to carry the ships and ourselves” (10.23–26; see [[skin-of-the-winds]] and [[hollow-ships]]); when the skin is opened, “the ships were borne by the evil storm of wind / back to the Aeolian island” (10.54–55). There were several ships together, as αἱ δέ shows, with Odysseus leading in the one he steered (W. W. Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 10.56).

**Eleven ships lost.** In the harbor of Telepylus, enclosed by sheer cliffs with a narrow mouth, the others “all steered their curving ships inside” and moored them close together, “but I alone kept my black ship outside” (10.87–96; see [[harbor]], [[curving]] and [[black-ships]]). The Laestrygonians pelt the ships from the cliffs, “and at once an evil din rose up throughout the ships, / of men being killed and of ships being shattered together” (10.122–123; see [[laestrygonians]] and [[stone-throwing]]): the sounds of κακός, κόναβος and κατά in quick succession are meant, Merry thinks, to echo the crushing volley (on 10.122). Odysseus cuts his cables, “but the others perished there in a body” (10.132; see [[stern-cables]] and [[oars]]). From here on the tale has one ship.

**The ship on Aeaea.** The ship is brought to shore in silence, “into a harbor where ships can lie, and some god guided us” (10.140–141; see [[aeaea]]). When Circe has given back the companions, she bids Odysseus “drag the ship up onto the land, / and put your possessions and all the tackle away in caves” (10.403–404; see [[possessions]]), and he gives the order in the same words (10.423–424). The companions offer to leave Eurylochus “to stay there beside the ship and guard the ship” (10.444; see [[eurylochus]]): ἔρυσθαι is “to guard”, and in later times, Merry notes, ships were sometimes enclosed in a stockade (on 10.444).

**To the house of Hades.** “No one has ever yet come to the house of Hades in a black ship” (10.502), Odysseus objects; Circe answers that he needs no guide: he is to set up the mast and spread the sails, let the North Wind carry the ship across Oceanus, and beach it there (10.505–511; see [[mast]], [[sails]], [[boreas]] and [[oceanus]]).
