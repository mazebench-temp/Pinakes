---
summary: Odysseus pleads with Aeolus, “approaching them with gentle words” (10.70), and speaks to the companions at the ship “with gentle words” (10.422).
grc: ["μαλακοῖσι καθαπτόμενος", "μαλακοῖς ἐπέεσσι"]
en: [gentle words]
---

μαλακός of words is “gentle” in the translation (see the Odyssey translation's key words, μαλακός). At 10.70 Zenodotus read μαλακοῖσιν ἀμειβόμενος, which a scholion prefers, since Odysseus does not reproach Aeolus but supplicates him (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, critical note on 10.70; see the translation's note on 10.70); καθαπτόμενος is earnest address, not necessarily rough (see the translation's note on 2.39). Aeolus answers, “Off with you from the island, quickly” (10.72; [[aeolus]], [[soothing-words]]).
