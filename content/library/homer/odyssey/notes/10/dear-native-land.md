summary: The native land in sight and lost: “and on the tenth our native fields were already coming into sight” (10.29), until the storm carries the companions “weeping, away from their native land” (10.49); Circe's drugs are meant to make men “utterly forget their native land” (10.236); and after a year the companions bid Odysseus “think now at last of your native land” (10.472).

The phrase runs through the book eleven times. Aeolus' household had sent Odysseus off to reach “your native land and your house” (10.65–66); the companions at Circe's ship welcome him as if they had reached their native land (10.416, 10.420); and Odysseus tells them, “You think, no doubt, that you are going home to your dear native land” (10.562), the whole formula for a journey that leads instead to the dead ([[nostos]], [[ithaca]], [[lotus-eaters]]).
