---
summary: Lamus is named once, in Odysseus' description of the Laestrygonians' city, “the steep citadel of Lamus” (10.81); the tale does not say who he was.
---

**The citadel of Lamus.** “and on the seventh we came to the steep citadel of Lamus, / Telepylus of the Laestrygonians” (10.81–82; see [[telepylus]] and [[laestrygonians]]). When the scouts ask the king's daughter “who was king of these people and over whom he ruled”, she points out the house of her father, Antiphates (10.106–111; see [[antiphates-the-laestrygonian]]). The tale names Lamus for the city and Antiphates for the king. That leaves room both for Fäsi's view, which W. W. Merry reports, that the two are names of one murderous king (*Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 10.81), and for the Latin poets' view, that Lamus founded the city and Antiphates ruled it later (see the entry).
