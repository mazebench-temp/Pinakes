# πηῷ (10.441), only here.
grc: [πηῷ]
en: [kinsman by marriage]
---

Eurylochus has told the companions that Odysseus' recklessness destroyed the men in the Cyclops' cave, and Odysseus ponders whether to draw his sword “and strike off his head with it and bring it to the ground, / kinsman by marriage though he was, and very near” (καὶ πηῷ περ ἐόντι μάλα σχεδόν, 10.440–441; see [[eurylochus]] and [[atasthalie]]). πηός is probably a connection by marriage, like the Latin *affinis*, and μάλα σχεδόν intensifies it; a later story, Merry reports, made Eurylochus the husband of Odysseus' sister Ctimene, who is named in Book 15 (W. W. Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 10.441; 15.363). Alcinous counts such kin among the dearest, a son-in-law or a father-in-law, “who are / the dearest after one's own blood and kin” (8.582–583). The companions hold Odysseus back (10.441–445; see [[hetairos]]).
