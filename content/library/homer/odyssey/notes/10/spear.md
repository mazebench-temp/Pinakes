**To the lookout.** On the third day on Aeaea, “then I took my spear and my sharp sword / and went up quickly from beside the ship to a lookout” (10.145–146; see [[sword]]).

**The stag.** On his way back one of the gods sends a great high-horned stag into his path as it comes down to the river to drink (10.157–160; see [[deer]]). “And as it came out, I struck it on the spine, in the middle of the back, / and the bronze spear passed straight through, / and it fell down in the dust with a cry, and its spirit flew away” (10.161–163). The verses are the Iliad's: the spear that passes straight through is Idomeneus' (*Iliad* 16.346), and the fall with a cry is that of Pedasus, Achilles' trace horse (*Iliad* 16.469; W. W. Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 10.163; see [[pedasus-the-horse]]). “And setting my foot on it I drew the bronze spear out of the wound” (10.164), as Hector draws his spear from the dead Patroclus, setting his heel on him (*Iliad* 16.862–863): Odysseus steps on the body to pull the harder, and lays the spear on the ground to have his hands free (Merry on 10.164). He plaits a cable of willow shoots, binds the stag's feet, and carries it on his neck, “leaning on my spear, since there was no way to carry it on the shoulder / with one hand; for it was a very great beast” (10.170–171; see [[stern-cables]] and the translation's row ἄκνηστις; καταλοφάδεια).
