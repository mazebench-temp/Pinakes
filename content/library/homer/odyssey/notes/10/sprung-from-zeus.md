---
summary: Circe's verse of address (10.401, 10.488, 10.504), and the companions' plea as Odysseus thinks of killing Eurylochus: “Sprung from Zeus, we will leave this man, if you bid it, / to stay there beside the ship and guard the ship” (10.443–444).
---

διογενές alone, in the companions' mouth, holds back the drawn sword (10.438–445); Merry renders εἰ σὺ κελεύεις “if thou wilt consent to that” (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 10.443). Eurylochus himself had begged Odysseus as one “nurtured by Zeus” (10.266; [[zeus-nurtured]], [[son-of-laertes]], [[of-many-devices]]).
