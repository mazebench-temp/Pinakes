summary: The companions turned into swine keep their minds, “steadfast, as before” (10.240); Circe finds in Odysseus “a mind in your breast that cannot be bewitched” (10.329); and Persephone has given Teiresias a mind even in death, “that he alone should be wise” (10.494–495).
# νοῦς (10.240), the only contracted form of the word in Homer.
grc: [νοῦς]
---

**The minds of the swine.** Circe's drug changes the body and leaves the man inside it: “And they had the heads of swine, and the voice, and the bristles, / and the form, but their minds were steadfast, as before” (αὐτὰρ νοῦς ἦν ἔμπεδος, ὡς τὸ πάρος περ, 10.239–240; see [[shape-changing]] and [[circe]]). νοῦς is found contracted only here in Homer, and Merry would read νόος; Zenodotus read καὶ πόδας, “and the feet”, for καὶ δέμας (W. W. Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 10.240 and his critical note; see the translation's note on 10.240).

**A mind that cannot be bewitched.** When the drug has failed on Odysseus, Circe gives the reason: “But there is a mind in your breast that cannot be bewitched” (ἀκήλητος νόος, 10.329; see [[thelgo]] and [[stethos]]). The scholia objected that the companions too had kept their minds, so that the verse said nothing of Odysseus alone; Merry answers that she means the strong and stubborn mind that was generally proof against sorcery, since she does not know that he carries an amulet (Merry's critical note on 10.329; see [[moly]]).

**A mind among the dead.** In the house of Hades νόος is what the dead lack. Teiresias alone keeps it: “to him even in death Persephone has given a mind, / that he alone should be wise” (τῷ καὶ τεθνηῶτι νόον πόρε Περσεφόνεια, 10.494–495; see [[teiresias]], [[persephone]] and [[psyche]]).

**Noticing.** The verb is Circe's watching at the table: “when Circe saw me sitting there, and not putting out / my hands to the food” (ἐνόησεν, 10.375–376; see [[fasting]]).
