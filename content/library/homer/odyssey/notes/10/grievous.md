---
summary: “And the men's spirit was worn down by the painful rowing / through our own folly, since no escort showed itself any longer” (10.78–79).
---

εἰρεσίης ἀλεγεινῆς: with the wind gone, the ships must be rowed. Merry follows the scholion that takes ἡμετέρῃ, “our,” as said in sympathy, Odysseus counting himself in because his sleep gave the crew their chance, and takes πομπή of the wind, the means of the journey (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 10.79; see the Odyssey translation's key words, ἀφραδίη, ματίη; [[pompe]], [[aeolus]]).
