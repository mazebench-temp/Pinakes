---
summary: Cronus is named in Book 10 only in his son's patronymic: Aeolus binds up the winds, “for the son of Cronus had made him dispenser of the winds” (10.21).
---

Aeolus gives Odysseus the skin of an ox in which he has bound up the paths of the blustering winds, “for the son of Cronus had made him dispenser of the winds, / both to still and to rouse whichever he wishes” (10.21–22; see [[zeus]], [[son-of-cronus]], [[aeolus]] and [[dispenser-of-the-winds]]). Κρονίων is Zeus as the master who shares out the world's powers, the name under which Odysseus sacrificed the Cyclops' ram “to Zeus, lord of the dark clouds, son of Cronus, who rules over all” (9.552; see the Book 9 note). The winds are not Aeolus' own: in Homer he is only their manager, by the permission of Zeus (W. W. Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 10.21; see [[the-winds]]).
