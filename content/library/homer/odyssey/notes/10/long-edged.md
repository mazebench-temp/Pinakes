---
summary: Odysseus ponders “whether to draw the long-edged sword from beside my stout thigh” and strike off Eurylochus' head (10.439–440).
grc: [τανύηκες]
---

ἄορ τανύηκες, the form of the compound that the Iliad gives to Poseidon's sword and to the sword with which Automedon cuts loose the trace horse (*Iliad* 14.385, 16.473); Odysseus draws it again in the next book, among the dead (11.231). Here the companions hold him back (10.441–445; [[eurylochus]], [[sharp]], [[soothing-words]]).
