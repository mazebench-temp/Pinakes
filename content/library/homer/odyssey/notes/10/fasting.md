---
summary: On Aeaea Odysseus rouses his starving men to eat (10.174–177). At Circe's table, after the bath, he will not eat: he sits “eating your heart, and do not touch food or drink”, until she has freed his companions (10.373–387).
refs: [10.373-387]
---

**Hunger refused.** Odysseus' first care on Aeaea is to feed his men. He brings down the stag and bids them eat, “as long as there is food and drink in the swift ship, / let us think of eating, and not be worn down by hunger” (10.176–177; see [[deer]] and [[hetairos]]).

**Food refused.** At Circe's table, bathed and seated, he will not eat (see [[like-a-man-without-speech]], [[penthos]], [[phrenes]], [[silence]] and [[thymos]]):

{{quote:3239be21-9621-54bb-bc8e-2065eb5ea5ef}}

He answers that no fair-minded man would taste food or drink “before he had set his companions free and seen them with his eyes” (10.383–387; see [[aisa]]). In the Iliad it was Odysseus who argued against Achilles' fast that men must eat, “for in that is might and courage” (*Iliad* 19.160–163), and Achilles who would let neither drink nor food go down his throat while his companion lay dead (οὐ πόσις οὐδὲ βρῶσις, *Iliad* 19.209–210; see the entry). On Aeaea the man who refuses food for his companions is Odysseus; when they are freed, the whole company feasts with Circe for a year (10.467–468; see [[feast]]).
