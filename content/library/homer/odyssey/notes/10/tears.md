summary: The book is full of weeping: the companions swept from Ithaca by the winds (10.49), the lament for the men the Laestrygonians and the Cyclops ate (10.201–202), the men turned into swine (10.241), Eurylochus' tears (10.247–248), the companions' welcome of Odysseus at the ship (10.409–415), and his own tears on Circe's bed when he learns that he must go to the house of Hades (10.496–499), in the verses of Menelaus' despair on Pharos.
# δακρυόεντες (10.415), μυρομένοισιν (10.202, 10.568): only here in the Odyssey.
grc: [δακρυόεντες, μυρομένοισιν]
---

**Weeping companions.** The storm carries the companions out to sea “weeping, away from their native land” (10.48–49). On Aeaea, when Odysseus tells them of the smoke, they remember Antiphates and the Cyclops: “And they wept loudly, shedding big tears; / but there was no profit for them in their weeping” (θαλερὸν κατὰ δάκρυ χέοντες, 10.201–202; see [[prexis]]). Eurylochus' band goes off “weeping” (10.209), the men penned as swine weep in the sties (10.241; see [[shape-changing]]), and Eurylochus comes back with his eyes “filled with tears” (10.247–248; see [[lament]]). When Odysseus returns from Circe's house, he finds the others at the ship “lamenting piteously, shedding big tears” (10.409), and they “poured around me in tears” (δακρυόεντες ἔχυντο, 10.415), as calves run round their mothers coming home from pasture (10.410–414). At the end of the book the two verses come again, when the companions hear where they must go and walk down to the ship “grieving, shedding big tears” (10.566–570).

**Odysseus' tears.** When Circe tells him that he must go to the house of Hades, Odysseus weeps on her bed: “So she spoke, and my dear heart was broken, / and I wept as I sat on the bed, and my heart / no longer wished to live and see the light of the sun. / But when I had had my fill of weeping and rolling about” (10.496–499; see [[ker-heart]] and [[etor]]). The verses are those of Menelaus on the sand of Pharos, when Proteus told him that Agamemnon had been killed, with the bed in place of the sand (4.538–541; see [[menelaus]]).
