summary: Odysseus, waking to the storm, chooses to “endure in silence and remain still among the living” (10.52); Aeolus' household falls silent at his plea (10.71); the one ship left comes in to Aeaea “in silence” (10.140); and at Circe's table Odysseus sits “like a man without speech” (10.378).
# σιωπῇ (10.140; also 1.325, 1.339, 7.154, 8.234 and later books, always "in silence"); ἀναύδῳ (10.378), only here.
grc: [σιωπῇ, ἀναύδῳ]
en: [without speech]
---

**Endurance.** Waking to find the winds loose, Odysseus ponders “whether I should fall from the ship and perish in the sea, / or endure in silence and remain still among the living. / But I endured and stayed, and covering my head I lay / in the ship” (ἀκέων τλαίην, 10.51–54; see [[thymos]] and [[enduring]]). Covering the head is a sign of abandonment to grief, as when he hid his face at Demodocus' song (8.92; W. W. Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 10.53; see [[odysseus-weeps-at-the-feast]]).

**Refusal.** At his plea Aeolus' household “fell silent” (ἄνεῳ ἐγένοντο, 10.71) before the father answered him: “Off with you from the island” (10.72; see [[themis]] and [[aeolus]]).

**Landing.** After the Laestrygonians the one ship left comes in to the shore of Aeaea “in silence, / into a harbor where ships can lie, and some god guided us” (σιωπῇ, 10.140–141; see [[harbor]] and [[aeaea]]).

**Grief.** Eurylochus comes back from Circe's house and “could not speak out a word, though he longed to” (10.246; see [[lament]]). At Circe's table, with his companions still in the sties, Odysseus will not eat or speak, and Circe asks: “Why do you sit like this, Odysseus, like a man without speech, / eating your heart, and do not touch food or drink?” (ἶσος ἀναύδῳ, 10.378–379; see [[fasting]] and the translation's table of key words, ἶσος ἀναύδῳ). Buttmann and Ahrens denied that there was a present ἕζομαι and would read ἕζεο, the aorist (Merry on 10.378; see the translation's note on 10.374–378).
