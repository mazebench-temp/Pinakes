---
summary: Odysseus draws “the sharp sword from beside my thigh” to cut the cables (10.126), takes “my spear and my sharp sword” to the lookout (10.145), is told by Hermes to draw it on Circe and does so (10.294, 10.321), and is told by Circe to draw it at the pit (10.535).
---

ξίφος ὀξὺ ἐρυσσάμενος παρὰ μηροῦ is the verse of Achilles' sword against Agamemnon (*Iliad* 1.190, with φάσγανον) and of Odysseus' first thought in the Cyclops' cave (9.300); it comes four times here, with ἄορ at 10.321, and twice more in the next book (11.24, 11.48). At 10.439 the sword is “long-edged,” to be drawn “from beside my stout thigh” against Eurylochus ([[long-edged]], [[sword]]).
