**The homecoming of the Achaeans.** “And for a whole month he made me welcome and questioned me about each thing, / Ilios and the ships of the Argives and the homecoming of the Achaeans” (10.14–15; see [[aeolus]], [[troy]] and [[argives]]). νόστον Ἀχαιῶν, “the homecoming of the Achaeans”, is the theme of Phemius' song in the first book, “the grim homecoming that Pallas Athena laid on them from Troy” (1.326–327; see [[nostos]] and [[the-returns-of-the-achaeans]]), which Penelope cannot bear to hear. Aeolus, far from Greece, wants the whole story, and Odysseus tells it “fair and right” (10.16); his own homecoming is then in Aeolus' gift, bound in the skin of the winds (10.17–26), and it is his companions who lose it.
