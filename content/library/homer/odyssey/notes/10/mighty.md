en: [strong]

ἰφθίμη of a woman, “strong,” as the Iliad uses the word of wives of rank (*Iliad* 5.415, 19.116), is here the giant king's daughter drawing water at the spring; the name of Penelope's sister, Ἰφθίμη, is another word, which this entry leaves to her (4.797; see the translation's note on 10.106; [[iphthime]]). The Laestrygonians are ἴφθιμοι in the verse that brings them running from all sides, “countless, not like men, but like the Giants” (10.119–120; [[laestrygonians]], [[giants]]). ἰφθίμῳ τʼ Ἀίδῃ καὶ ἐπαινῇ Περσεφονείῃ is Hesiod's pair too (*Theogony* 768; [[hades]], [[dread]]).
