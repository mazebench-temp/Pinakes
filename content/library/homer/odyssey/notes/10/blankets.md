**Aeolus' house.** The married sons and daughters of Aeolus feast all day with their father and mother, “and at night again, beside their modest wives, / they sleep on rugs and on corded beds” (10.11–12; see [[aeolus]], [[bed]] and [[corded]]). The τάπης is a woolen coverlet, the rug spread for a guest (4.124; see the Book 4 note).

**Circe's chairs.** While Circe and Odysseus are in her chamber, the first of her four handmaids makes ready the seats: “One of them threw fine blankets over the chairs, / purple ones on top, and spread linen cloths beneath them” (10.352–353; see [[throne]], [[purple]] and [[handmaids]]). λῖτα, W. W. Merry notes, is to be explained as at 1.130, where a cloth is spread on the guest's chair (*Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 10.353; see the Book 1 note of [[throne]]); some take it as a neuter plural (see the translation's note on 10.353). The purple blankets lie on top, καθύπερθε, and the linen beneath, ὑπένερθε.
