---
summary: Barley meal goes into Circe's drugged mixture for the companions (10.234), and white barley meal is to be sprinkled over the drink offering to the dead at the pit (10.520).
---

**In Circe's drink.** “And for them she made a mixture of cheese and barley meal and pale honey / with Pramnian wine” (10.234–235; see [[kykeon]], [[cheese]] and [[honey]]), as Hecamede sprinkled white barley meal over Nestor's drink (*Iliad* 11.640).

**At the pit.** Circe's instructions for the offering to the dead end the drink offering of honey and milk, wine and water: “and sprinkle white barley meal over it” (10.520; see [[the-offering-to-the-dead]] and [[white]]). The barley that is sprinkled on a victim for the gods above is here the food of the dead, part of what W. W. Merry calls an offering of the ordinary supports of life (*Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 10.519).
