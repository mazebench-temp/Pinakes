---
summary: Aeolus is the father of twelve children who feast always “beside their dear father” (10.5–8), and it is “the father” who answers Odysseus' plea (10.71); the Laestrygonian girl shows the scouts “the high-roofed house of her father” (10.111).
---

The two fathers of the book are kings. Aeolus' children feast “beside their dear father and devoted mother” (10.8; see [[aeolus]] and [[meter]]), and when Odysseus asks for a second escort, his household falls silent, “but the father answered me” (πατὴρ δʼ ἠμείβετο μύθῳ, 10.71) with the sentence of banishment (10.72–75; see [[themis]]). The strong daughter of Antiphates, met at the spring, “at once pointed out the high-roofed house of her father” (πατρὸς … ὑψερεφὲς δῶ, 10.111), where her father ate one of the men she had guided (10.105–116; see [[antiphates-the-laestrygonian]]).
