summary: Olympus is named once in Book 10: when Hermes has given Odysseus the moly and his instructions, he goes away “to high Olympus / across the wooded island” (10.307–308).
---

**Hermes goes back.** “Then Hermes went away to high Olympus / across the wooded island, and I to the house of Circe / went my way” (10.307–309; see [[hermes]], [[high-olympus]] and [[aeaea]]). The first verse is the Iliad's for Hermes' departure from Priam at Achilles' hut, when he has woken the old man and guided him out of the Achaean camp (*Iliad* 24.694; again, with the god's name in the next verse, 24.468–469). In both poems Hermes comes down in the likeness of a young man to help a mortal who must go alone into a dangerous house (10.277–279, as *Iliad* 24.347–348), and in both, his work done, he goes back to Olympus before the mortal goes in. On Aeaea his way back lies νῆσον ἀνʼ ὑλήεσσαν, “across the wooded island”; ὑλήεσσα, “wooded”, is the one epithet the book gives the island.
