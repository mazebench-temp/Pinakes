---
summary: Among the Laestrygonians a man could earn two wages, “one by herding cattle, the other by pasturing silver-white sheep” (10.85); and Circe puts on “a great silver-white mantle” (10.543).
grc: [ἄργυφα]
---

ἄργυφα μῆλα, with the short form of the adjective, as of the sheep that Achilles kills for Priam (*Iliad* 24.621), in the land where “the paths of night and of day are close together” (10.84–86; see the translation's note on 10.81–86). The mantle is the verse of Calypso's robing (5.230 = 10.543; [[telepylus]], [[clothing]]).
