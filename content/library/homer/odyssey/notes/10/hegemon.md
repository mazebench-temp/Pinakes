---
summary: On Aeaea Odysseus divides his men into two bands “and gave each band a leader” (10.204). When he asks Circe who will guide him to the house of Hades, she answers, “let no longing for a guide trouble you” (10.501–505).
---

Odysseus counts his companions off “into two bands, and gave each band a leader; / one I led myself, and godlike Eurylochus the other” (ἀρχὸν δὲ μετʼ ἀμφοτέροισιν ὄπασσα, 10.204–205; see [[lots]] and [[eurylochus]]). For the journey to the house of Hades he asks for a guide, “Circe, who will guide us on this journey? / No one has ever yet come to the house of Hades in a black ship” (10.501–502), and she answers that he needs none: “let no longing for a guide trouble you beside your ship” (ἡγεμόνος, 10.505), for the North Wind will carry the ship (see [[pothe]], [[pompe]] and [[boreas]]). The god who “guided” the ship into the harbor of Aeaea (ἡγεμόνευεν, 10.141) is not named (see [[divine-intervention]]).
