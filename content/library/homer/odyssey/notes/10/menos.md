---
summary: The stag comes down from the woods to drink, “for the might of the sun had hold of it” (10.160).
---

The book's one μένος is the sun's: the great stag comes down from its pasture in the woods to the river “to drink; for the might of the sun had hold of it” (δὴ γάρ μιν ἔχεν μένος ἠελίοιο, 10.159–160; see [[deer]] and [[helios]]), and Odysseus spears it as it comes out. Zenodotus read δὴν γάρ, “for long” (W. W. Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, his critical note on 10.160).
