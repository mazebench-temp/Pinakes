---
summary: A herald goes with Odysseus and one companion to the house of Aeolus when the storm has driven the ships back (10.59), and a herald goes as the third with the two men sent to find out who lives in the land of the Laestrygonians, in the verse that sent the scouts to the Lotus-eaters (10.102 = 9.90).
# κήρυκά (10.59) and κήρυχʼ (10.102) are claimed by the notes of earlier books, which apply to the whole Odyssey.
---

**To the house of Aeolus.** “then I took a herald with me and one companion / and went to the glorious house of Aeolus” (10.59–60; see [[aeolus]]): ὀπασσάμενος is “having taken as my companion” (W. W. Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 10.59). They sit on the threshold as suppliants, and Aeolus drives them away (10.62–76; see [[supplication]] and [[odysseus]]).

**To the Laestrygonians.** At Telepylus Odysseus sends men to find out “what men they might be who eat bread upon the earth, / choosing two men, and sending a herald with them as the third” (10.101–102; see [[spies-and-scouts]] and [[who-eat-the-fruit-of-the-field]]), the verses with which he sent the scouts to the Lotus-eaters (9.89–90; see the Book 9 note and the translation's note on 9.89–90). At the king's house Antiphates seizes one of the three for his meal, and the other two escape to the ships; the poem does not say whether the herald was taken or spared (10.112–117; see [[antiphates-the-laestrygonian]]).
