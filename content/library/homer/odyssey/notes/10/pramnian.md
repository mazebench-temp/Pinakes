---
summary: Circe makes the companions “a mixture of cheese and barley meal and pale honey / with Pramnian wine” (10.234–235).
---

The ingredients are those of Hecamede's mixed drink for Nestor and Machaon, Pramnian wine with cheese and white barley meal (*Iliad* 11.638–640); Merry notes that she leaves out the honey, which is an ingredient here, and takes the mixture for a kind of stimulant for those who need strength, not an ordinary food (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 10.234). Of the name he reports derivations from a Mount Pramnon or Pramne in Icaria and from the country near Ephesus or Smyrna, quotations in Athenaeus that seem to make it a local name, Galen's description of a dark, harsh wine, and Eustathius' derivation from παραμένειν, “to keep”; he supposes a local name that came to mean a kind of vine (Merry on 10.234). Circe adds her grim drugs (10.236; [[kykeon]], [[wine]], [[drugs]]).
