summary: Aeolus entertains Odysseus for a month and sends him off with an escort, but when the winds bring him back he drives him from the house as a man hated by the gods (10.14–75). The Laestrygonian king makes a meal of the first stranger he finds (10.105–116). Circe receives the companions with a drugged drink, and Odysseus with the bath and the table only when she has sworn the oath (10.229–243, 10.348–387).
# "φίλει με" (10.14), the host's welcome, as φιλέω in the Book 4 note.
grc: ["φίλει με"]
en: [made me welcome]
---

**Aeolus.** “And for a whole month he made me welcome and questioned me about each thing” (10.14). The family of Aeolus have received him into “their city and their fine house”, but Aeolus, as head of the household, is the only subject of φίλει, “entertained me” (W. W. Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 10.13; see [[aeolus]] and [[odysseus-and-aeolus]]). When Odysseus asks to go, the host gives what a guest asks for, an escort (10.17–18; see [[pompe]]), and a gift, the skin with the winds bound in it, which the companions take for “gifts from Aeolus, great-hearted son of Hippotes” (10.35–36; see [[doron]] and [[skin-of-the-winds]]).

**The welcome refused.** Driven back by the storm, Odysseus does not walk up the hall but sits on the threshold, “beside the doorposts” (10.62–63), a more modest posture than at the hearth of Alcinous, and the one he will take as a beggar in his own house (7.153; 17.339; Merry on 10.62). He asks “with gentle words” (10.70), as a suppliant rather than an accuser, in the view of the scholium (Schol. H.) that prefers Zenodotus' reading μαλακοῖσιν ἀμειβόμενος (Merry's critical note on 10.70; see the translation's note on 10.70 and [[supplication]]). Aeolus answers with the law of his house: “Off with you from the island, quickly, most despised of the living; / for it is not lawful for me to care for or to send on his way / a man who is hated by the blessed gods” (10.72–74; see [[themis]] and [[elenchos]]). The failure of the voyage is read as the gods' hatred, and the stranger is no longer “from Zeus”, as Nausicaa said all strangers and beggars are (6.207–208; see [[strangers-and-beggars-are-from-zeus]]).

**The Laestrygonians.** The scouts are shown the king's high-roofed house by his daughter at the spring (10.105–111); inside, the king at once “seized one of my companions and made ready his meal” (10.116; see [[antiphates-the-laestrygonian]]), the welcome that the Cyclops gave in Book 9 (9.288–291; see [[odysseus-and-the-cyclops]]).

**Circe's table.** Circe opens the shining doors and calls the companions in, seats them on chairs and seats, and makes them a mixed drink with drugs in it (10.230–236; see [[kykeon]]); with Odysseus she does the same, seating him on “a silver-studded chair, / fine and intricately worked; and there was a footstool under my feet” (10.314–317; see [[throne]] and [[footstool]]). The true welcome comes after the oath. The handmaids spread the chairs with blankets, set out silver tables and golden baskets, mix the wine and heat the bath (10.348–365; see [[handmaids]] and [[bathing]]), and the meal is served in the verses of the Odyssey's welcomes (10.368–372 = 1.136–140; see [[water-for-the-hands]] and [[feast-type-scene]]), which Köchly thought borrowed and out of place in Circe's house (Merry's critical note on 10.368–372; see the translation's note on 10.368–372). Odysseus will not eat until his companions are freed (10.373–387; see [[fasting]]); then the whole company feasts with her for a year (10.467–468).
