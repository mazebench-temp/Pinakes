summary: After the bath Odysseus is served with the five verses, from “And a handmaid brought water for the hands in a pitcher and poured it” to “adding many dainties, giving freely of what she had” (10.368–372).
---

The verses of the welcome in Ithaca, at Sparta and among the Phaeacians (1.136–140, 4.52–56, 7.172–176), in Circe's house, where Odysseus will not eat until his companions are freed (10.373–387). Köchly called them borrowed and out of place here, and La Roche thought them no older than the thirteenth century, unknown to Eustathius and the scholia (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, critical note on 10.368–372; see the translation's note on 10.368–372; [[feast-type-scene]], [[polished]]).
