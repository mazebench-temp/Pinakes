---
summary: “but there was no profit for them in their weeping” closes the companions' two laments on Aeaea: for the men whom the Laestrygonians and the Cyclops ate (10.198–202), and at the news of the journey to the house of Hades (10.566–568).
---

The verse is the narrator's judgment on tears. When Odysseus tells of the smoke on the island, the companions remember Antiphates and the Cyclops: “And they wept loudly, shedding big tears; / but there was no profit for them in their weeping” (οὐ γάρ τις πρῆξις ἐγίγνετο μυρομένοισιν, 10.201–202). When he tells them that they must go to the house of Hades, “they sat down there and lamented, and tore their hair; / but there was no profit for them in their weeping” (10.567–568; see [[tears]] and [[lament]]). It is Achilles' word to Priam put into narrative: “for there is no profit in chilling lament” (οὐ γάρ τις πρῆξις πέλεται κρυεροῖο γόοιο, *Iliad* 24.524; see [[the-two-jars-of-zeus]] and the translation's table of key words, πρῆξις). In the Iliad it was Odysseus who said that the dead must be buried “having a pitiless heart, after weeping for him a single day” (*Iliad* 19.228–229; see [[weep-for-a-day-and-bury]]); on Aeaea the weeping is followed each time by action, the casting of lots and the walk down to the ship (10.203, 10.569).
