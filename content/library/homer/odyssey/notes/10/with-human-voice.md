---
summary: Circe, “a dread goddess with human voice” (10.136).
---

αὐδήεσσα, the word that described Ino (5.334), ends the verse that introduces Circe, as it will when she sends the wind behind Odysseus' ship (11.8, 12.150), and once of Calypso (12.449). The scholion on the verse, as Dindorf corrected its “Aristarchus,” gives Aristotle a reading οὐδήεσσα; Merry supposes from the scholia on 5.334 that Aristotle wrote αὐλήεσσα (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, critical note on 10.136; see the translation's note on 10.135). Polites, hearing her sing within, cannot tell whether the singer is “either a goddess or a woman” (10.228; [[dread-goddess]], [[circe]]).
