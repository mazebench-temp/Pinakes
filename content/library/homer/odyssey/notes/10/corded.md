---
summary: Aeolus' sons, married to his daughters, “sleep on rugs and on corded beds” beside their wives (10.12).
---

τρητοῖσι λέχεσσι, the beds of a rich house, in the picture of the island where the twelve children feast for ever by day beside their father and mother and sleep at night beside their modest wives (10.5–12; [[aeolus]], [[bed]], [[marriage]]).
