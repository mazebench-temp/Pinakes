---
summary: The scouts ask the Laestrygonian girl “who was king of these people” (10.110); she shows them her father's house, and the king is called from the place of assembly (10.111–114).
---

The Laestrygonians, who eat men as the Cyclopes do, have a king, and the scouts' first question is for him: “who was king of these people and over whom he ruled” (ὅς τις τῶνδʼ εἴη βασιλεύς, 10.110; see [[anax]]). The girl points out “the high-roofed house of her father”, and his wife calls “famous Antiphates” from the place of assembly (10.111–114; see [[antiphates-the-laestrygonian]] and [[assembly]]). Having a king and an ἀγορή, the Laestrygonians are, in Merry's words, “more advanced in the externals of civilisation than the Cyclopes” (W. W. Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 10.114; see [[laestrygonians]] and [[themis]]).
