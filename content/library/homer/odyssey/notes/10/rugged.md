summary: The companions welcome Odysseus “as if they had come to their native land and to the very city / of rugged Ithaca” (10.416–417), and Circe speaks of “your native land / of rugged Ithaca” (10.462–463).
grc: [τρηχείης]
---

τρηχείης Ἰθάκης: Odysseus called his island so to the Phaeacians, “rugged, but a good nurse of young men” (9.27). Both times the island is the measure of a homecoming that has not yet come (10.415–420, 10.461–465; [[ithaca]], [[dear-native-land]]).
