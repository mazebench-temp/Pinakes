summary: Circe prescribes the sacrifices of the offering to the dead: the vow of a barren heifer to the dead and of an all-black sheep to Teiresias, to be paid in Ithaca (10.521–525), and a ram and a black ewe slaughtered at the pit, turned toward Erebus, and burned while the companions pray to Hades and Persephone (10.526–534). Circe leaves the victims by the ship (10.571–572).

**The vow.** A sacrifice is promised to the dead, to be made at home: Odysseus is to vow “that, when you come to Ithaca, a barren heifer, the best you have, / you will sacrifice in your halls, and heap the pyre with good things, / and that to Teiresias apart you will sacrifice a sheep, to him alone, / all black, that stands out among your flocks” (10.522–525; see [[teiresias]], [[cattle]] and [[pyres]]). The heifer is barren, a scholium explains, because the things of the dead are barren and childless (W. W. Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 10.522); the sheep is black, because white victims were offered to the gods above and black to the gods below (Merry on 10.525), and it is to be sacrificed apart, so that the offering in his honor should not be confused with the general offering to the dead (Merry on 10.524; see [[the-offering-to-the-dead]]).

**At the pit.** “then sacrifice a ram and a black ewe, / turning them toward Erebus” (10.527–528; see [[ram]] and [[erebus]]): their necks are bent down, where in sacrifice to the gods of heaven the victim's head is drawn back so that it looks up to the sky (*Iliad* 1.459; 3.453; Merry on 10.528). The blood is for the souls who come to drink it (10.529–537; see [[psyche]]). The companions are to “flay the sheep that lie there slaughtered with the pitiless bronze, / and burn them, and pray to the gods, / to mighty Hades and dread Persephone” (10.532–534; see [[pitiless-bronze]] and [[prayer]]); nothing is said of a meal.

**The victims.** The animals are Circe's gift: as the men go down to the shore she ties “a ram and a black ewe” beside the ship, slipping past them unseen (10.571–574; see [[sheep]] and [[seeing-the-gods]]).
