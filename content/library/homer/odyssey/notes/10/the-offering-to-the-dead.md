summary: Circe prescribes the rite when Odysseus asks who will guide him to the house of Hades (10.516–537): the pit, the drink offering of honey and milk, wine and water, the barley meal, the vows of a barren heifer to the dead and a black sheep to Teiresias, the prayers, the ram and the black ewe turned toward Erebus, and the sword that keeps the dead from the blood. At dawn she leaves the ram and the ewe by the ship unseen (10.571–572).

**The way and the rite.** Odysseus has wept at the news that he must go to the house of Hades, and asks Circe who will guide him there, for “No one has ever yet come to the house of Hades in a black ship” (10.496–502). She answers with the way, the place and the rite: the North Wind will carry the ship across Oceanus to the groves of Persephone, and there, where Pyriphlegethon and Cocytus flow into Acheron, he is to draw close and dig the pit (10.505–517; see [[circe]], [[boreas]] and [[rivers]]). ἔνθα, “there”, must mean at the entrance to the house of Hades (W. W. Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 10.513). The pit is to be a cubit long and a cubit wide, “this way and that” (Merry on 10.517).

**The vows.** γουνοῦσθαι, “implore”, is the entreaty, and the verse after it gives the very words of the vow that the entreaty implies (10.521–522; Merry on 10.522; see [[the-strengthless-heads]] and [[supplication]]). The heifer is to be barren, because, as a scholium that Merry quotes explains, the things of the dead are childless and barren (ἄγονα γὰρ καὶ στεῖρα τὰ τῶν νεκρῶν, Merry on 10.522); the pyre is to be heaped with good things (10.523; see [[cremation]]). The sheep for Teiresias is to be sacrificed apart (ἀπάνευθεν), so that the offering in his honor should not be confused with the general offering to the dead, and to be all black, the color of victims for the powers below (Merry on 10.524, 10.525; see [[teiresias]]).

**The prayers and the sacrifice.** Then Odysseus is to beg “the glorious tribes of the dead with prayers” (10.526), and κλυτά is more than an ornament here, since most of those he is about to see are heroes and famous women (Merry on 10.526; see [[the-tribes-of-the-dead]] and [[prayer]]). The ram and the black ewe are to be slaughtered “turning them toward Erebus” (10.527–528): their necks are bent down, as in sacrifice to the gods below, where in sacrifice to the gods of heaven the victim's head is drawn back so that it looks up to the sky and bares its throat to the knife (αὐέρυσαν, *Iliad* 1.459; ἀνελόντες, 3.453; Merry on 10.528; see [[sacrifice]] and [[erebus]]). Odysseus himself is to turn away, as though he were not to witness what Merry calls “the mysterious coming” to the blood, as Ino bade him turn away when he threw her veil back into the sea (5.350; Merry on 10.528; see the translation's note on 5.350). ἱέμενος ποταμοῖο ῥοάων, “making toward the streams of the river”, turns him from the western darkness toward the stream of Oceanus, and so toward the world of the living (Merry on 10.529). The souls will come, “the dead who have died”, a pleonasm like νεκύεσσι καταφθιμένοισι of Book 11 (10.530; Merry on 10.530; see [[the-dead-who-have-died]] and [[psyche]]).

**The sword.** The companions are to flay and burn the sheep “that lie there slaughtered”, κατάκειτʼ, which Merry calls quite necessary; the past tense κατέκειτʼ is right in Book 11, where the sheep have already been killed, and came into this verse from there (10.532; Merry on 10.532 and his critical note; see the translation's note on 10.532). They are to pray to “mighty Hades and dread Persephone” (10.533–534; see [[hades]] and [[persephone]]), while Odysseus sits with drawn sword and lets none of the dead near the blood before Teiresias, who “will tell you the way and the measures of your path / and your homecoming” (10.535–540; see [[sword]] and [[nostos]]).

**The victims.** The companions lament when they hear where they are going (10.561–568). When they come down to the shore, Circe has already been there and tied up beside the black ship “a ram and a black ewe”, slipping past them unseen (10.569–574; see [[ram]], [[sheep]] and [[seeing-the-gods]]).
