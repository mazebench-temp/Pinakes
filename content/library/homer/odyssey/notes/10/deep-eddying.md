---
summary: “beach your ship there by deep-eddying Oceanus” (10.511).
---

The Iliad's epithet of the Scamander, and once of the Axius, is given in the Odyssey only to Oceanus, beyond which lie the groves of Persephone (10.508–511; [[oceanus]], [[rivers]]). Merry takes ἐπʼ Ὠκεανῷ as explaining αὐτοῦ, “there,” and notes that in the next book, when Circe's orders are carried out, the men beach the ship and walk some way along the stream before they reach the place she named (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 10.511; 11.20–22).
