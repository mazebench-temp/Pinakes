summary: On Aeolus' island the companions take their meal “beside the swift ships” (10.57); on Circe's, the verse-end “to the swift ship and the shore of the sea” comes four times (10.154, 10.402, 10.407, 10.569), and Circe knows Odysseus as one “returning from Troy in your swift black ship” (10.332).
---

10.56–58 repeat the meal at the land of the Lotus-eaters (9.85–87). After the Laestrygonians the swift ship is the only one left (10.131–132), and Odysseus goes back and forth between it and Circe's house through the book (also 10.176, 10.244, 10.408; [[ships]], [[from-there-we-sailed-on]]).
