---
summary: The Laestrygonians pelt Odysseus' ships in their harbor from the cliffs with stones, each a load for a man, and shatter all but his own (10.121–132).
---

When Antiphates raises the cry, the Laestrygonians come, “countless, not like men, but like the Giants. / From the cliffs they pelted us with stones, each a load for a man” (10.120–121; see [[laestrygonians]] and [[giants]]). ἀπὸ πετράων is from the tops of the cliffs that command the harbor, and the stones were each a load for an ordinary man, as Eustathius explains ἀνδραχθής, “such as would be a burden for other men to carry” (W. W. Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 10.121; see the translation's row ἀνδραχθής; κόναβος). The measure is the Iliad's kind for the stones of heroes, which two men such as mortals are now could not carry (see the entry). The stones shatter the ships with an evil din, and only Odysseus' ship, moored outside the harbor, escapes (10.122–132; see [[ships]]).
