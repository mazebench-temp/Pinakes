---
summary: Circe is “own sister of baleful Aeetes” (10.137).
---

αὐτοκασιγνήτη ὀλοόφρονος Αἰήταο: like Atlas, the father of Calypso (1.52), the kinsman of the goddess who keeps Odysseus is named with the epithet of a malign mind, and both children are of the Sun and of Perse (10.138–139). Merry takes the word, of Aeetes as of Minos (11.322), to denote the qualities for which a wizard is feared, and compares ὀλοφώια δήνεα Κίρκης, “the baneful thoughts of Circe” (10.289); in that note he calls Aeetes Circe's father, though the verse makes her his own sister (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 1.52; see the Odyssey translation's key words, αὐτοκασιγνήτη). The Argo's voyage home from Aeetes, παρʼ Αἰήταο, is named in Circe's warning about the Wandering Rocks (12.70; [[aeetes]], [[circe]]).
