---
summary: Circe seats Odysseus' companions on chairs and seats before she gives them her drugged drink (10.233), and Odysseus twice on a silver-studded chair with a footstool, once for the drink and once for the meal (10.314–315 = 10.366–367); her handmaids spread blankets on the chairs and draw up tables before them (10.352–354). Dawn is “of the golden throne” (10.541).
# θρόνοις (10.352) and θρόνων (10.354), first met here (they apply in the Odyssey only; later 17.32, 20.150, 22.23).
grc: [θρόνοις, θρόνων]
---

**The seats of Circe's house.** “She led them in and seated them on chairs and seats” (10.233; see [[circe]] and [[kykeon]]), in the pair of words of the suitors' seats in Odysseus' hall, κατὰ κλισμούς τε θρόνους τε (1.145; see the translation's row κλισμός, θρόνος). For Odysseus she brings the chair of honor: “She led me in and seated me on a silver-studded chair, / fine and intricately worked; and there was a footstool under my feet” (10.314–315; see [[silver-studded]] and [[footstool]]), the second verse that seats Thetis in Hephaestus' house and Telemachus' guest in Book 1 (*Iliad* 18.390; 1.131). The same two verses seat him again after his bath, for the meal he will not eat (10.366–367); with 10.366, W. W. Merry notes, the main clause begins, after the clause of the bath and the clothing (*Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 10.366; see the translation's note on 10.365–366).

**The handmaids' work.** “One of them threw fine blankets over the chairs, / purple ones on top, and spread linen cloths beneath them; / another drew up tables before the chairs” (10.352–354; see [[blankets]], [[table]] and [[handmaids]]): a table to each chair, as Merry explains, not one table for meat and another for drink, as Eustathius took it (on 10.354).

**The golden throne.** At the end of Circe's instructions “at once Dawn of the golden throne came” (10.541; see [[golden-throned]] and [[eos]]).
