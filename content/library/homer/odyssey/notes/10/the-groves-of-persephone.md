# The place without its name: "beach your ship there by deep-eddying Oceanus" (10.511).
refs: [10.511]
---

**Across Oceanus.** The groves are the landfall of the voyage to the dead. Oceanus, as W. W. Merry explains it, is not a sea but a river round the earth, which a ship could cross from the sea to the shore on its farther side; he renders Circe's words “where there is a rough-grown coast, and groves of Persephone, both tall aspens, and willows that shed their fruit—there, on the edge of the swirling ocean, beach thy ship” (*Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 10.508; see [[oceanus]] and [[deep-eddying]]). The translation keeps λάχεια, “of good soil”, as at 9.116 (see the translation's note on 10.509). The ship stays at the groves; Odysseus is to go on himself “into the dank house of Hades”, to the rock where the rivers meet, and dig the pit there (10.512–517; see [[hades]] and [[acheron]]). The North Wind that brings him (10.507) is the one wind of the voyage, as the West Wind was of Aeolus' gift (10.25; see [[boreas]] and [[zephyrus]]).
