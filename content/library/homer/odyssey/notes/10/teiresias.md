**Another journey.** When Odysseus begs Circe to send him home, she answers: “But first you must accomplish another journey and come / to the house of Hades and of dread Persephone, / to consult the soul of Theban Teiresias” (10.490–492; see [[hades]], [[persephone]] and [[thebes]]), and she tells what sets him apart from the rest of the dead: Persephone has given him a mind, “that he alone should be wise; but the others flit as shadows” (10.493–495; see the entry, [[the-blind-seer]] and [[psyche]]). μάντηος, “seer”, is the reading of one Venice manuscript, followed by almost all modern editors, where the others have μάντιος; Plato, quoting the last verse, reads ταὶ δέ for τοὶ δέ (W. W. Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, critical notes on 10.493, 10.495; see the translation's notes on 10.493 and 10.495, and [[plato-the-terrors-of-hades]]). Odysseus weeps and asks who will guide them, since no one has yet come to Hades in a black ship (10.496–502; see [[odysseus]]).

**The black sheep.** At the pit he is to implore the dead, vowing that when he comes to Ithaca he will sacrifice a barren heifer in his halls, “and that to Teiresias apart you will sacrifice a sheep, to him alone, / all black, that stands out among your flocks” (10.521–525; see [[the-offering-to-the-dead]], [[sacrifice]] and [[sheep]]). ἀπάνευθεν, “apart”, means at another spot, so that the offering in his honor should not be confused with the offering to the rest of the dead, and the victim is black because black victims were offered to the gods of the world below, as white to the gods above (Merry on 10.524, 10.525, comparing *Aeneid* 6.251).

**Until you have questioned Teiresias.** He is to sit with his sword drawn and not let “the strengthless heads of the dead / come near the blood until you have questioned Teiresias” (10.536–537; see [[the-strengthless-heads]] and [[sword]]):

The last two verses are Eidothea's promise to Menelaus that her father Proteus will tell him the way home (4.389–390; see [[proteus]]); here the seer is a dead man (see [[prophecy]] and [[divination]]).

**To the companions.** Odysseus gives his men the news in Circe's own verses, “to the house of Hades and of dread Persephone, / to consult the soul of Theban Teiresias” (10.564–565 = 10.491–492), “and their dear heart was broken” (10.566). In Book 11 he does as she bids, and the seer comes and prophesies (11.25–33, 11.90–151; see the entry).
