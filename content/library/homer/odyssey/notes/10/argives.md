summary: The Argives are named once in Book 10, in Aeolus' questions about the war: Ilios, “the ships of the Argives” and the homecoming of the Achaeans (10.14–16).
---

**The ships of the Argives.** Aeolus questions Odysseus for a month about “Ilios and the ships of the Argives and the homecoming of the Achaeans” (10.15; see [[aeolus]], [[troy]] and [[achaeans]]). The verse names the army by two of its names, as Alcinous' question in Book 8 joined two of them, “the fate of the Argive Danaans and of Ilios” (8.578; see the Book 8 note and [[danaans]]). Ἀργείων … νέας are the ships that went to Troy and sailed home from it, the fleet whose scattering is the story of the returns (see [[the-returns-of-the-achaeans]]); of Odysseus' own twelve ships, one is left after the Laestrygonians (10.132; see [[laestrygonians]]).
