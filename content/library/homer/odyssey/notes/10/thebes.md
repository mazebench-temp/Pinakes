**Theban Teiresias.** Circe tells Odysseus that he must first go “to the house of Hades and of dread Persephone, / to consult the soul of Theban Teiresias, / the blind seer, whose wits are steadfast” (10.491–493; see [[teiresias]]), and he gives his companions her words (10.563–565). Θηβαῖος is an adjective here, “Theban”, as the lexicon parses it. The verse ψυχῇ χρησομένους Θηβαίου Τειρεσίαο, “to consult the soul of Theban Teiresias”, is said twice in this book, and with the participle changed once more in the next, when Odysseus tells his mother why he has come (11.165), and again in the summary of his wanderings (23.323); and Θηβαίου Τειρεσίαο ends the verses in which the soul comes to the pit (11.90) and in which Odysseus remembers the seer's warning (12.267). The city itself is not named in the book. Teiresias belongs, W. W. Merry notes, to the Theban cycle of legend (*Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 10.492), and the next book, in which his soul comes to the pit, brings other Thebans among the souls: Antiope, whose sons Amphion and Zethus founded and walled seven-gated Thebes, and Epicaste, the mother of Oedipus (11.260–280; see [[seven-gated]]).
