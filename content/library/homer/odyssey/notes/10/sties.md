summary: Circe strikes the drugged companions with her wand and pens them in her sties, where they weep and eat acorns and cornel (10.237–243); Hermes tells Odysseus that they “keep to close-packed sties” (10.283), Circe bids Odysseus himself go to the sty (10.320), and at his demand she opens its doors and drives them out (10.388–390).
# "So they were penned there weeping" (10.241), with the verb of the pens and no word for them, is a ref.
refs: [10.241]
---

**The penning.** When the companions have drunk Circe's drink, “then at once / she struck them with her wand and penned them in the sties” (10.237–238; see [[wand]], [[drugs]] and [[circe]]). κατά and ἐέργνυ go together, συφεοῖσιν being a purely local dative, and the form ἐέργνυ is found only here (W. W. Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 10.238). In the sties they have the heads, the voice, the bristles and the form of swine, but keep their minds (10.239–240; see [[shape-changing]] and [[boar]]), and they weep while Circe throws them the food of swine, ilex acorns and oak acorns and the fruit of the cornel (10.241–243; see [[trees]] and the translation's row ἄκυλος, βάλανος, κράνεια). Aristarchus, as Merry reports the scholia, did not know verse 242, and Callistratus wrote another in its place (Merry's critical note; see the translation's note on 10.242).

**Hermes' word.** Hermes tells Odysseus that his companions “are penned like swine, and keep to close-packed sties” (10.283; see [[hermes]]): ἔρχαται is the perfect of the verb of shutting in, ἔργω, with the last consonant of its stem aspirated before the ending (Merry on 10.283). Eurylochus uses the same verb when he likens Circe to the Cyclops who “penned them in” (10.435; Merry on 10.435; see [[eurylochus]] and the translation's note on 10.435).

**The sty for Odysseus.** When her drug has failed, Circe strikes Odysseus with her wand and says, “Go now to the sty, and lie down with the rest of your companions” (10.320), two imperatives without a connective (Merry on 10.320); he draws his sword instead (see [[sword]]). At his demand she goes out through the hall “with her wand in her hand, and opened the doors of the sty, / and drove them out, looking like fat hogs nine years old” (10.389–390; see [[door]]), and anoints each with the drug that makes them men again (see [[drugs]]). On ἐννεώροισιν, “nine years old”, see Merry on 10.390 and 10.19, and [[skin-of-the-winds]].
