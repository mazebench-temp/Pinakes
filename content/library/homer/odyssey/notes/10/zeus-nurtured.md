---
summary: Eurylochus begs Odysseus, “Do not lead me there against my will, you who are nurtured by Zeus, but leave me here” (10.266), and the companions welcome him back: “At your return, you who are nurtured by Zeus, we are as glad / as if we had come to Ithaca, our native land” (10.419–420).
---

The kings' epithet is given to Odysseus by his own men, once as he sets out for Circe's house and once when he comes back from it (10.263–269, 10.418–421). The second half of 10.266 comes again when Telemachus asks Peisistratus to leave him at his ship rather than take him to Nestor's house (15.199; [[eurylochus]], [[hetairos]]).
