**Odysseus' bath.** When Circe has sworn her oath, her handmaids make ready the house, and the fourth heats the bath water (10.358–360; see [[tripod]], [[fire]] and [[handmaids]]):

{{quote:2517bfad-82df-5b33-82da-77a268fa20aa}}

θυμῆρες is said predicatively with κεράσασα, “having mixed it to suit my heart”, and is probably a form of its own from θυμός, not the same as θυμαρής (W. W. Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 10.362; see the translation's row θυμῆρες). κάματον θυμοφθόρον, “the weariness that destroys life”, uses the word of the “drugs that destroy life” that the suitors feared (2.329; see the translation's row θυμοφθόρος). The bath ends in the verses of the welcome: dressed in cloak and tunic, Odysseus is seated on a silver-studded chair, and the main clause begins only with the seating, after the bath and the clothing (10.366–367; Merry on 10.366; see [[cloak]], [[tunic]] and [[throne]]).

**The companions' bath.** While Odysseus fetches the rest of his men from the ship, “Meanwhile in her house Circe had bathed the rest of my companions / with care and anointed them richly with olive oil, / and put woolly cloaks and tunics around them” (10.449–451; see [[circe]] and [[hetairos]]), and he finds them feasting in the halls (10.452). The goddess who turned the first band into swine now gives the second the care of a host (see [[xenia]]).
