---
summary: Circe stirs pale honey into her drugged mixture for the companions (10.234), and honey and milk is the first of the drink offerings that Odysseus is to pour for the dead (10.519).
# μελικρήτῳ, "with honey and milk" (10.519 = 11.27), the only instances in Homer; it applies in the Odyssey only, and milk
# claims it too.
grc: [μελικρήτῳ]
---

**In Circe's drink.** “And for them she made a mixture of cheese and barley meal and pale honey / with Pramnian wine” (10.234–235; see [[kykeon]], [[cheese]] and [[barley-meal]]). μέλι χλωρόν, “pale honey”, is the honey that Hecamede set beside Nestor's drink (*Iliad* 11.631); in Circe's it is mixed in, W. W. Merry observes, where Hecamede left it out (*Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 10.234).

**Honey and milk.** At the pit Odysseus is to pour a drink offering to all the dead, “first with honey and milk, then with sweet wine, / and the third time with water” (10.519–520; see [[the-offering-to-the-dead]], [[milk]] and [[wine]]). μελίκρητον is a mixture of honey and milk, as in the μελίκρατʼ … γάλακτος that Helen sends to Clytemnestra's tomb in Euripides, and the whole offering gives the dead the ordinary supports of life (Merry on 10.519; *Orestes* 115; see the translation's row βόθρος, πυγούσιον; χοή; μελίκρητον).
