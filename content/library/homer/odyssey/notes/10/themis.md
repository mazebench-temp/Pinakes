---
summary: Aeolus refuses a second escort: “it is not lawful for me to care for or to send on his way / a man who is hated by the blessed gods” (10.73–74). The Laestrygonians, who eat men like the Cyclopes, have what the Cyclopes lacked, a king and a place of assembly (10.110–114).
---

**Not lawful.** When the storm brings Odysseus back, Aeolus' household asks first what god is against him (10.64; see [[daimon]]), and Aeolus' answer follows from it: “Off with you from the island, quickly, most despised of the living; / for it is not lawful for me to care for or to send on his way / a man who is hated by the blessed gods; / off with you, since it is as one hated by the gods that you come here” (οὐ γάρ μοι θέμις ἐστί, 10.72–75; see [[aeolus]], [[pompe]] and [[xenia]]). θέμις is here what custom and right allow, as in Odysseus' appeal to the Cyclops for a guest-gift, “as is the custom with strangers” (9.268), and the man whom Aeolus received as a guest is now one whom the gods hate (see [[elenchos]] and [[theos]]). ἔρρε is “begone to your ruin” (μετὰ φθορᾶς ἀναχώρει) in the scholium Merry quotes, and θᾶσσον “as speedily as possible” (W. W. Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 10.72).

**A king and an assembly.** The Laestrygonians eat men as the Cyclopes do, but they have a king, of whom the scouts ask “who was king of these people and over whom he ruled”, and a place of assembly, from which his wife calls him (10.110–114; see [[antiphates-the-laestrygonian]], [[basileus]] and [[assembly]]). Having a king and an ἀγορή, they are, in Merry's words, “more advanced in the externals of civilisation than the Cyclopes” (on 10.114; see [[laestrygonians]] and [[cyclopes]]), who “have no assemblies that bring counsel, and no ordinances” (9.112).
