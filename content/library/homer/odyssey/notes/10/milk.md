**The flower of moly.** Hermes pulls the drug from the ground and shows Odysseus its nature: “At the root it was black, but its flower was like milk; / moly the gods call it” (10.304–305; see [[moly]], [[drugs]] and [[white]]). The milk is the measure of whiteness, as the Iliad's “white milk” (*Iliad* 4.434, 5.902).

**Honey and milk.** The first of the drink offerings at the pit is μελίκρητον, honey mixed with milk, before the sweet wine and the water (10.518–520; see [[honey]] and [[the-offering-to-the-dead]]): W. W. Merry compares the offerings of Euripides' plays, where Helen sends honey and milk and wine to Clytemnestra's tomb and Iphigenia pours milk, wine and honey for the brother she believes dead (*Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 10.519; Euripides, *Orestes* 114–115, *Iphigenia in Tauris* 159–166).
