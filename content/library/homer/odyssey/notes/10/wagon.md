---
summary: Odysseus' scouts in the land of the Laestrygonians follow a smooth road by which the wagons brought wood down to the city from the high mountains (10.103–104).
---

“They went ashore and took a smooth road, by which the wagons / brought wood down to the city from the high mountains” (10.103–104; see [[laestrygonians]] and [[telepylus]]). λείη ὁδός, W. W. Merry explains, is a road cleared for use, by felling trees and levelling, as Apollo promises to level the way for the Trojan horses (κέλευθον … λειανέω, *Iliad* 15.261), so as to make it a wagon road, ἁμαξιτός, like the one under the walls of Troy (*Iliad* 22.146; *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 10.103). A variant gave ᾗ κεν ἄμαξαι for ᾗ περ ἄμαξαι (Merry's critical note; see the translation's note on 10.103). The road and its wagons are the first sign of a city on a coast where, from the lookout, no works of oxen or of men could be seen, only smoke (10.97–99).
