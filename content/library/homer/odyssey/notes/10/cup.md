---
summary: Circe makes Odysseus her drugged mixed drink in a golden cup (10.316), and when she has sworn her oath a handmaid serves out golden cups for the wine mixed in a silver bowl (10.357).
---

**The golden cup.** Circe seats Odysseus on a silver-studded chair, “and she made me the mixed drink in a golden cup, for me to drink, / and put the drug in it, meaning evil in her heart” (10.316–317; see [[kykeon]], [[drugs]] and [[gold-and-silver]]). La Roche writes δέπαι, not δέπᾳ, W. W. Merry reports, on the ground that the iota subscript is never written in Homer after a short alpha, as in γήραι and σέλαι (*Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, critical note on 10.316). The cup that should have made him a beast is the one he drinks off unharmed (10.318–319; see [[moly]]).

**The golden cups.** When Circe has sworn not to harm him, her handmaids make ready the house: “the third mixed wine sweet to the heart in a mixing bowl, / sweet wine in a silver bowl, and served out golden cups” (10.356–357; see [[mixing-bowl]], [[wine]] and [[handmaids]]), the gold and silver of a goddess's house, as in the house of Menelaus (4.71–75).
