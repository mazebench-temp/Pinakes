---
summary: Circe, when her drug has failed: “Surely you are Odysseus of many turns; he always told me / you would come, the slayer of Argus of the golden wand, / returning from Troy in your swift black ship” (10.330–332).
grc: [πολύτροπος]
---

The second and last use of the word that opened the poem (1.1); Merry, glossing it “of many devices,” notes that it is found again only here (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 1.1; see the translation's note on 1.1). Circe puts the stranger's question, “Who are you among men, and from where?”, and answers it herself from a prophecy (10.325–332; [[who-are-you-among-men]], [[of-the-golden-wand]]). The verse before gives her reason, “there is a mind in your breast that cannot be bewitched” (10.329): Merry finds the ancient objection to the line more apparent than real, since she means the strong and stubborn mind that is generally proof against sorcery, not knowing that he carries an amulet (Merry's critical note on 10.329; [[odysseus]], [[moly]]).
