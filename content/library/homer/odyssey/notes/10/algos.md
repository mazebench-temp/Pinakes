The book's ἄλγεα are the voyage's. Landing on Aeaea after the Laestrygonians, the men lie two days and two nights by the ship, “eating our hearts out with weariness and sorrows together” (καμάτῳ τε καὶ ἄλγεσι θυμὸν ἔδοντες, 10.142–143; see [[thymos]]). When the companions restored from the sties have met the others in her house and wept, Circe stops the lament: “I myself know too / both all the sorrows you suffered on the fish-filled sea, / and all the harm that merciless men did you on land” (10.457–459; see [[pity]], [[lament]] and [[fish-filled]]).
