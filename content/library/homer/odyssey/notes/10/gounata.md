---
summary: Eurylochus takes Odysseus by the knees and begs not to be led back to Circe's house (10.264); Circe, her drug defeated, clasps Odysseus' knees (10.323); and Odysseus entreats Circe by her knees to send him home (10.481).
---

Three times in the book the knees are the suppliant's hold. Eurylochus “took hold of my knees with both hands and begged me” (ἀμφοτέρῃσι λαβὼν ἐλλίσσετο γούνων, 10.264); Circe, under Odysseus' sword, “ran in under it and clasped my knees” (λάβε γούνων, 10.323); and Odysseus in Circe's bed “entreated her by her knees” (γούνων ἐλλιτάνευσα, 10.481), γούνων being “by her knees”, as one begs a man by Zeus (W. W. Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 10.481; see [[supplication]]).
