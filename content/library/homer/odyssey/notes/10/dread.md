---
summary: Circe sends Odysseus “to the house of Hades and of dread Persephone” (10.491 = 10.564), and he is to pray at the pit “to mighty Hades and dread Persephone” (10.534).
grc: [ἐπαινῆς]
---

Merry argues for the sense “dread.” Some take ἐπαινή as “praiseworthy,” another form of ἐπαινετή or a verbal adjective from ἔπαινος with a change of accent; but Persephone, always named with Hades, is likely to have a more terrible title, and he derives the word from ἐπί and αἰνός, “fearful,” or writes with Buttmann καὶ ἐπʼ αἰνή, “and besides, awful”; in later Greek, he reports, Tryphiodorus used it wrongly for “praiseworthy” (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 10.491; see the translation's note on 10.491). In the Iliad she has the epithet only in curses (*Iliad* 9.457, 9.569); here she is the goal of a journey and a goddess of the rite at the pit, and the prayer is made in the next book (11.47; [[persephone]], [[hades]], [[mighty]]).
