---
summary: Odysseus' companions stand “in the doorway of the goddess of the lovely tresses” (10.220), and Odysseus after them “at the doors of the goddess of the lovely tresses” (10.310).
---

θεᾶς καλλιπλοκάμοιο ends both verses: the epithet that the Iliad gives to Demeter, Thetis and Ariadne is given in the Odyssey to Circe alone. The verses differ in the place where the visitors stand. ἐν προθύροισι, “in the doorway,” is Aristarchus' reading at 10.220, adopted since Wolf, where nearly all the manuscripts have εἰνὶ θύρῃσι, the words of 10.310 (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, critical note on 10.220; see the translation's note on 10.220), and Merry takes the πρόθυρα as the door of the courtyard (Merry on 10.220). Both times the goddess comes out at once and opens the shining doors (10.230, 10.312; [[circe]], [[door]], [[with-lovely-tresses]]).
