summary: Aeolus: “for it is not lawful for me to care for or to send on his way / a man who is hated by the blessed gods” (10.73–74); and Hermes bids Odysseus make Circe “swear a great oath by the blessed gods” (10.299).
grc: ["θεοῖσιν ἀπέχθηται μακάρεσσιν", "μακάρων μέγαν ὅρκον"]
---

Aeolus reads the storm that brought Odysseus back as the gods' judgment, “since it is as one hated by the gods that you come here” (10.75; [[themis]], [[aeolus]]). The oath by the blessed gods is the one Circe swears before Odysseus goes to her bed (10.343–346; [[oath-formulas]]).
