---
summary: Merry draws on Athenaeus' quotations of comic poets for Pramnian wine, the wine in which Circe mixed her drugs.
---

- **Pramnian wine.** Circe mixes cheese, barley meal and pale honey “with Pramnian wine”, and into the food her drugs (10.234–236). Some ancient authorities, Merry notes, named the wine from a mountain Pramnon or Pramne on the island of Icaria, others from the country about Ephesus or Smyrna; Athenaeus quotes the comic poet Ephippus, who speaks of loving Pramnian Lesbian wine, and Demetrius of Troezen, whose speaker will not let a man drink Pramnian or Chian or Thasian or Peparethian, which seems to settle a local meaning. Merry thinks that the name, local at first, came to mean a kind of vine, and reports Galen's description of it as dark and harsh (Athenaeus 1.28–30, as Merry quotes it; Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 10.234): [[pramnian]], [[wine]] and [[kykeon]].
