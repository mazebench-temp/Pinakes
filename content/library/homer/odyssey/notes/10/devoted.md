---
summary: Aeolus' children feast “always beside their dear father and devoted mother” (10.8), and Polites was “the nearest and dearest to me of my companions” (10.225).
en: [dearest]
---

Merry calls κήδιστος … κεδνότατός τε a sort of play on words, renders it “nearest and dearest,” and compares κήδιστοί τʼ ἔμεναι καὶ φίλτατοι, where the Iliad's translation has the same English (*Iliad* 9.642; Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 10.225; see the Odyssey translation's key words, κήδιστος … κεδνότατος). Polites speaks once, at Circe's door, and is not named again (10.224–229; [[leader-of-men]]).
