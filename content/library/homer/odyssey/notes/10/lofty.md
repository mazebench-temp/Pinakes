---
summary: The Laestrygonians' harbor, “around which a rock / stands lofty and unbroken on both sides” (10.87–88).
---

Merry takes τετύχηκε as “stands,” like τέτυκται, and διαμπερές as showing that the wall of cliff ran unbroken from one side to the other (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 10.88; see the translation's note on 10.87–88). The lofty rock was the Cyclops' door-stone in Book 9 (9.243); here it is the cliff that closes the harbor ([[harbor]], [[overhanging]]).
