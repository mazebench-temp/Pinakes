---
summary: Eurylochus' charge against Odysseus: “and with them went this bold Odysseus; / for it was through his recklessness that they too perished” (10.436–437).
grc: [θρασὺς]
---

The epithet that the Iliad gives above all to Hector is here a reproach, and Merry takes the next verse as its explanation: “Foolhardy, I say, for it was through his infatuation” (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 10.437). Eurylochus sets the Cyclops' farmyard beside Circe's house (10.431–437), and Odysseus' answer is to think of drawing his sword (10.438–441; [[atasthalie]], [[eurylochus]], [[odysseus-and-the-cyclops]]).
