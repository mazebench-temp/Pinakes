summary: Twice Odysseus climbs “a rugged lookout” to see what land he has come to (10.97–99, 10.146–152, 10.194–197), and twice he sends men to find out: two companions and a herald to the Laestrygonians, where the king eats one of them (10.100–117), and on Aeaea half the crew under Eurylochus, who stays outside Circe's house and keeps watch (10.153–155, 10.203–260).
# "σκοπιὴν ἐς παιπαλόεσσαν" (10.97, 10.148, 10.194), "ἐς περιωπήν" (10.146) and ἐσκοπίαζον (10.260), only here in the
# Odyssey (σκοπιήν alone is also the Sun's watch, 8.302). The scouts by refs: 10.100-102 = 9.88-90, and 10.155.
grc: ["σκοπιὴν ἐς παιπαλόεσσαν", "ἐς περιωπήν", ἐσκοπίαζον]
en: [kept watch, find out]

**The lookout.** At the Laestrygonians' harbor Odysseus keeps his ship outside, “and I climbed to a rugged lookout and stood there”; he sees no works of oxen or of men, “only smoke” (10.95–99). The Laestrygonians, like the Cyclopes, were only graziers, Merry notes, so that there would be no plowed land, the works of oxen, and no vineyards or gardens, the works of men (W. W. Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 10.97). On Aeaea he climbs a lookout again, “in the hope that I might see the works of mortals and hear their voices”, and sees smoke rising from Circe's halls (10.146–152; see the translation's table of key words, ἐνοπή); in the morning he tells the men what he saw from it, an island ringed by the boundless sea, and smoke in the middle of it (10.194–197; see [[aeaea]]).

**The scouts.** To the Laestrygonians he sends “some of my companions to go and find out / what men they might be who eat bread upon the earth, / choosing two men, and sending a herald with them as the third” (10.100–102 = 9.88–90; see [[heralds]] and [[who-eat-the-fruit-of-the-field]]). They meet the king's daughter at the spring, and the king seizes one of them for his meal; the other two escape to the ships (10.103–117; see [[antiphates-the-laestrygonian]] and [[artacia]]).

**On Aeaea.** This time he thinks it better “first to go to the swift ship and the shore of the sea, / and give my companions their meal, and send some of them to find out” (10.153–155), and the lot sends Eurylochus with twenty-two men (10.203–209; see [[lots]]). Eurylochus stays outside Circe's door, suspecting a trick, and when the others vanish, “I sat a long while and kept watch” (ἐσκοπίαζον, 10.258–260; see [[eurylochus]] and [[dolos]]); then he runs back to report (10.244–260).
