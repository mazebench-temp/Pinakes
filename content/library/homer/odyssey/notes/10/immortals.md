---
summary: Aeolus is “dear to the immortal gods” (10.2), and Circe walks to and fro “at a great immortal loom” (10.222).
grc: ["μέγαν ἄμβροτον"]
---

ἄμβροτος, the word of the immortal oil of Book 8 (8.365), describes the goddess's loom, whose works are “the fine and lovely and splendid works of goddesses” (10.222–223; [[loom]], [[splendid]]). Aeolus' favor with the gods is the first thing said of him, and he sends Odysseus away as “a man who is hated by the blessed gods” (10.2, 10.73–75; [[aeolus]]).
