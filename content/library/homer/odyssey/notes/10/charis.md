# χαριεστάτη (10.279 = Il. 24.348), only here in the Odyssey.
grc: [χαριεστάτη]
en: [most graceful]
---

The words of χάρις run in the book from the favor that a gift is to the grace that is seen. The companions grudge Odysseus the gifts that Aeolus gave him, “a favor done in friendship” (χαριζόμενος φιλότητι, 10.43; see [[philotes]] and [[doron]]). Hermes takes the likeness he took for Priam, “a young man / with the first down on his lip, when youth is at its most graceful” (10.278–279 = *Iliad* 24.347–348; see [[hermes]] and [[eidos]]). Circe sings at a great loom, “such as are / the fine and lovely and splendid works of goddesses” (λεπτά τε καὶ χαρίεντα, 10.222–223; see [[weaving]] and [[loom]]), and at dawn she puts on “a great silver-white mantle, / fine and lovely” (λεπτὸν καὶ χαρίεν, 10.543–544; see [[clothing]]), in the verses of Calypso's dressing on the morning she gives Odysseus the axe for his raft (5.230–231; see [[calypso]]).
