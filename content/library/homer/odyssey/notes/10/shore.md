summary: The shore of Book 10 is the beach of Aeaea, where the one ship comes in silently, the men lie two days in grief and feast on the stag until they sleep “where the sea breaks upon the shore” (10.140–186), and to which Odysseus goes back and forth between the ship and Circe's house, “to the swift ship and the shore of the sea” (10.154, 10.402, 10.407, 10.569). Beyond Oceanus there is “a shore of good soil” with the groves of Persephone (10.509).
# ἀκτή τε λάχεια (10.509), the shore beyond Oceanus, as a phrase: the groves of Persephone claim the rest of the verse.
grc: ["ἀκτή τε λάχεια"]
---

**The beach of Aeaea.** “There we brought our ship in to the shore in silence” (10.140; see [[aeaea]] and [[harbor]]), and for two days and nights the men lie there, eating their hearts out with weariness and sorrow (10.142–143). When Odysseus brings the stag, they gaze at it “along the shore of the barren sea” (10.179; see [[barren-sea]] and [[deer]]), feast all day, and sleep “where the sea breaks upon the shore” (10.183–186), the verses of the island of goats (9.161–162, 9.168–169; see the Book 9 note). ἐπὶ νῆα θοὴν καὶ θῖνα θαλάσσης, “to the swift ship and the shore of the sea”, measures the distance between the beach and Circe's house: Odysseus plans to go back there first from the lookout (10.154), Circe sends him there for his companions (10.402), he goes (10.407), and the whole company comes down to it at last to leave (10.569). The verse is the Odyssey's for going down to a ship (4.779).

**Another shore.** Beyond Oceanus, Circe says, “there is a shore of good soil and the groves of Persephone” (10.509; see [[the-groves-of-persephone]]). ἀκτή τε λάχεια: λάχεια is the word for the island of goats, “of good soil” (9.116), and the scholia insist that it be read so, not ἐλάχεια, “very small”, since Persephone's precinct would not be called that (W. W. Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, critical note on 10.509; see the translation's note on 10.509).
