---
summary: Circe's drink and wand turn the companions into swine that keep the minds of men (10.237–243), and her anointing turns them back, “younger than they had been before, / and much handsomer and taller to look upon” (10.388–396); Eurylochus fears that she will make them all “either swine or wolves or lions” (10.433). Hermes meets Odysseus “in the likeness of a young man” (10.278).
refs: [10.213, 10.238-240, 10.278, 10.338, 10.390-396, 10.433]
---

**Men into swine.** Circe gives the companions her mixed drink with the drugs in it, strikes them with her wand and pens them in the sties (see [[wand]], [[kykeon]], [[drugs]], [[sties]], [[noos]] and [[boar]]):

{{quote:37aa1a94-d3d6-500d-ac11-7d962bcf6675}}

For καὶ δέμας, “and the form”, Zenodotus read καὶ πόδας, “and the feet” (W. W. Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, his critical note on 10.240; see the translation's note on 10.240). Odysseus sums up her deed, “you who turned my companions into swine in your halls” (10.338), and Eurylochus fears that she will make them all “either swine or wolves or lions, / to guard her great house for her even under compulsion” (10.433–434), taking the beasts at her door for men she had changed (10.212–213; Merry on 10.213; see [[thelgo]], [[wolves]], [[lion]] and [[the-dogs-fawning-on-their-master]]).

**Back into men.** At Odysseus' demand Circe drives them out of the sty, “looking like fat hogs nine years old” (10.390), and anoints each of them with another drug (10.391–392; see [[circe]] and [[hetairos]]):

{{quote:180cfc6a-0da3-5f35-8fc2-f92bae4ee05a}}

**A god's likeness.** Hermes meets Odysseus on the way to Circe's house “in the likeness of a young man / with the first down on his lip, when youth is at its most graceful” (10.278–279), the likeness he took when he came to guide Priam to Achilles (*Iliad* 24.347–348; see [[hermes]] and [[seeing-the-gods]]).
