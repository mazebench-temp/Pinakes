---
summary: The companions take the skin of the winds for a guest's gift of treasure, “gold and silver, / gifts from Aeolus, great-hearted son of Hippotes” (10.35–36).
---

The companions see only a gift: Odysseus, they say, “was bringing home gold and silver, / gifts from Aeolus, great-hearted son of Hippotes” (δῶρα παρʼ Αἰόλου, 10.35–36), “and now these things have been given him, a favor done in friendship, / by Aeolus” (10.43–44; see [[philotes]], [[charis]] and [[guest-gifts]]). Their grudge is that he brings “many fine treasures … from Troy / out of the spoil”, while they go home with empty hands (10.40–42; see [[gold-and-silver]]). ἐκ Τροίης is “from the land of Troy”: Aristarchus took Τροΐης as an adjective with ληίδος, “Trojan spoil” (W. W. Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 10.40; see the translation's note on 10.40). The gift was the winds (10.47; see [[skin-of-the-winds]] and [[hetairos]]).
