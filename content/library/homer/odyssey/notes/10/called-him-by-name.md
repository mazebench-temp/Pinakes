---
summary: Hermes “clung to my hand, and spoke to me, and called me by name” (10.280), and Circe “struck me with her wand, and spoke to me, and called me by name” (10.319).
---

The half-verse of close address follows a touch (see the general article): the god's hand that stops Odysseus on his way, and the goddess's wand that should have turned him into a swine; the first touch saves him and the second fails (10.280–281, 10.318–320; [[wand]], [[hermes]], [[circe]]).
