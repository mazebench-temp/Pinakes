---
summary: Circe's wand completes the work of her drugs: she strikes the companions with it and pens them in the sties (10.238), Hermes warns Odysseus of “her very long wand” (10.293), she strikes him with it when her drug has failed (10.319), and she goes to free the companions with it in her hand (10.389). Hermes, who gives Odysseus the drug against her, is himself “of the golden wand” (10.277, 10.331).
---

**Circe's wand.** The stroke follows the drink. When the companions have drunk Circe's mixture, “then at once / she struck them with her wand and penned them in the sties” (10.237–238; see [[sties]], [[kykeon]] and [[drugs]]); πεπληγυῖα, “having struck”, is a perfect participle of the kind of τεθνηυῖα and τετρηχυῖα (W. W. Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 10.238). Hermes tells Odysseus what to do: “When Circe strikes you with her very long wand, / then draw the sharp sword from beside your thigh / and spring at Circe as though eager to kill her” (10.293–295; see [[sword]] and [[hermes]]). It happens so. When the drink has not bewitched him, “she struck me with her wand, and spoke to me, and called me by name: / ‘Go now to the sty, and lie down with the rest of your companions’” (10.319–320), and he draws his sword (10.321–322). At last, at his demand, Circe goes out through the hall “with her wand in her hand, and opened the doors of the sty” (10.389), but it is with another drug, not with the wand, that she makes the swine men again (10.391–396; see [[drugs]] and [[shape-changing]]).

**Hermes' wand.** The god who meets Odysseus on the way to the house is “Hermes of the golden wand” (10.277), and Circe remembers that “the slayer of Argus of the golden wand” always told her that Odysseus would come (10.330–331; see [[of-the-golden-wand]] and [[argeiphontes]]). In Homer, Merry notes, the wand of Hermes is only a plain wand; the caduceus with two serpents winding round it is a later invention (on 10.277). The god whose wand bewitches the eyes of men (*Iliad* 24.343; 5.47) gives Odysseus the drug that keeps Circe's wand from changing him (10.287–292, 10.302–306; see [[moly]]).
