summary: Book 10 has two harbors. The Laestrygonians' “famous harbor”, ringed by a sheer rock with headlands at its narrow mouth and a white calm within, holds the other ships, moored close together, while Odysseus keeps his outside; there they are destroyed (10.87–132). On Aeaea some god guides the one ship “into a harbor where ships can lie” (10.140–141).

**The famous harbor.** At Telepylus the harbor is described before the scouts go up to the city:

{{quote:59e4a1aa-0844-564d-a2f7-5cdd951682f6}}

The cliff τετύχηκε, “stands”, like τέτυκται, and διαμπερές shows that the wall of rock ran unbroken from one side to the other (W. W. Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 10.88; see the translation's note on 10.87–88); headlands jut out facing one another at the mouth, the ἀκταὶ προβλῆτες of the coast of Scheria (5.405), and the entrance is ἀραιή, “narrow” (10.89–90). ἔνθʼ οἵ γʼ … ἔχον, “there they all steered”, is the main clause to which the long description has been leading (Merry on 10.91; see the translation's note on 10.91). It is a harbor such as a sailor would choose: the water within is always calm, λευκὴ … γαλήνη, “a white calm” (10.94; see the translation's key words), as in the harbor of Phorcys on Ithaca, whose two headlands keep out the swell so that ships lie there without a cable (13.96–101; see [[ithaca]]). Odysseus alone keeps his ship outside, “there at the far edge, making the cables fast to a rock” (10.95–96; see [[stern-cables]]); αὐτοῦ ἐπʼ ἐσχατιῇ is “there, at its outermost edge” (Merry on 10.95–96).

**The trap.** The harbor that seemed safest is the trap. The Laestrygonians stone the ships from the cliffs that command it, and kill the men “within the deep harbor”, λιμένος πολυβενθέος ἐντός (10.121–125; see [[deep-harbor]] and [[laestrygonians]]), the verse-end of the Iliad's harbor of Chryse (*Iliad* 1.432). Eustathius, Merry reports, explains the great loss by the narrowness of the entrance, through which the ships could not get out (on 10.90). Odysseus cuts his cables and his men row out to the open sea, away from ἐπηρεφέας … πέτρας, “the overhanging rocks”, the cliffs at the entrance (10.126–132; Merry on 10.131; see [[overhanging]]). In the harbor of the island of goats no cable was needed at all (9.136–139); here the one ship tied outside to a rock is the one that survives.

**A harbor where ships can lie.** On Aeaea “we brought our ship in to the shore in silence, / into a harbor where ships can lie, and some god guided us” (10.140–141; see [[aeaea]]). ναύλοχος is the word for the harbors of the islet of Asteris, with an entrance on either side, where the suitors lie in wait for Telemachus (4.846–847; see the translation's key words), and, as at the island of goats, a god brings the ships in (9.142). On Aeaea the ship is later dragged up on the land and the tackle stored in caves (10.403–404, 10.423–424).
