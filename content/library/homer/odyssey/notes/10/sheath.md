summary: When Odysseus has drawn his sword on her, Circe bids him put it in its sheath and go up with her into her bed (10.333–335).
# κολεῷ (10.333; the Iliad's, 7.304 and 23.825, is in its Book 7 note), first met in the Odyssey here; it applies in the
# Odyssey only.
grc: [κολεῷ]
---

Circe, who has clasped Odysseus' knees under his drawn sword and named him as the man of many turns whom Hermes foretold, says: “But come, put your sword in its sheath, and then let us two / go up into our bed, so that, having lain together / in bed and love, we may trust one another” (10.333–335; see [[sword]], [[bed]] and [[circe]]). He sheathes it only after she has sworn the great oath not to harm him (10.336–347; see [[oath]]).
