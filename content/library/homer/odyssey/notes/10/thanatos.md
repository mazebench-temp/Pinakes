---
summary: The one ship escapes the Laestrygonians, “glad to have escaped death, though we had lost our dear companions” (10.134); Hermes says that moly is hard to dig up “for mortal men; but the gods can do all things” (10.305–306).
---

The refrain of the escapes counts death escaped and companions lost: “From there we sailed on, grieving at heart, / glad to have escaped death, though we had lost our dear companions” (ἄσμενοι ἐκ θανάτοιο, 10.133–134 = 9.565–566; see [[from-there-we-sailed-on]] and [[hetairos]]). θνητός, “mortal”, sets men against the gods in Hermes' words on the moly: “and it is hard to dig up / for mortal men; but the gods can do all things” (ἀνδράσι γε θνητοῖσι, 10.305–306; see [[the-gods-can-do-all-things]] and [[mortal-men]]). The death of the book is Elpenor's, whose soul goes down to Hades (10.560; see [[psyche]] and [[elpenor]]).
