---
summary: In Circe's directions the ram and the black ewe are to be sacrificed “turning them toward Erebus”, while Odysseus turns away toward the river and the souls of the dead come (10.527–530).
---

**Toward Erebus.** “then sacrifice a ram and a black ewe, / turning them toward Erebus, and turn yourself away, / making toward the streams of the river; and there many / souls of the dead who have died will come” (10.527–530; see [[ram]] and [[sacrifice]]). εἰς Ἔρεβος στρέψας, W. W. Merry explains, means bending the victims' necks downward, the custom in sacrifice to the gods below, where in sacrifice to the gods of heaven the head is drawn back to look up at the sky and bare the throat to the knife, as in the Iliad's αὐέρυσαν (*Iliad* 1.459; *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 10.528). Odysseus himself is to turn away, as if he must not watch the coming of the dead to the blood, toward the stream of Oceanus: standing on the farther shore and facing the western darkness, he turns back to face the living world (Merry on 10.528, 10.529; see [[oceanus]] and [[rivers]]). Erebus is the direction of the dead: in the next book the souls come up “out of Erebus” to the blood, and Ajax goes back “into Erebus” without a word (11.37, 11.564).
