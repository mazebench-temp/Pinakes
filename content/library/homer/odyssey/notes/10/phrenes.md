---
summary: Teiresias is “the blind seer, whose wits are steadfast” (10.493); Elpenor was “not very / valiant in war nor sound in his wits”, and “forgot in his mind” the ladder (10.552–553, 10.557). Odysseus ponders “in my mind and in my heart” whether to go toward the smoke (10.151–152), and “in my mind” whether to strike off Eurylochus' head (10.438–441).
---

**Wits that last.** Teiresias is “the blind seer, whose wits are steadfast” (τοῦ τε φρένες ἔμπεδοί εἰσι, 10.493; see [[teiresias]]). To keep even the φρένες in death was a special privilege (W. W. Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 10.495): the soul of Patroclus is “soul and phantom, but there is no mind in it at all” (ἀτὰρ φρένες οὐκ ἔνι πάμπαν, *Iliad* 23.104; see [[psyche]] and [[noos]]).

**Elpenor's wits.** The youngest companion is introduced by what he lacked: “not very / valiant in war nor sound in his wits” (οὔτε φρεσὶν ᾗσιν ἀρηρώς, 10.552–553), “steady in mind”, like the later φρενήρης (Merry on 10.553; see the translation's table of key words). Woken by the noise of the companions, “he sprang up suddenly, and forgot in his mind / to come back down by going to the long ladder” (ἐκλάθετο φρεσὶν ᾗσιν, 10.557–558; see [[elpenor]]).

**Pondering.** Odysseus ponders twice with his φρένες. On the lookout, “then I pondered in my mind and in my heart / whether to go and find out, since I had seen the fiery smoke” (κατὰ φρένα καὶ κατὰ θυμόν, 10.151–152), and when Eurylochus calls him reckless, “I pondered in my mind / whether to draw the long-edged sword” and strike off his head (μετὰ φρεσί, 10.438–441; see [[eurylochus]]). In both places μερμηρίζω takes an infinitive only, without the usual ὡς, ὅπως or ἢ … ἢ (Merry on 10.151; see [[decision-scene]], [[pondering-two-ways]] and [[thymos]]).

**Thoughts elsewhere.** At Circe's table Odysseus “sat with my thoughts elsewhere, and my heart foreboded evil” (ἀλλοφρονέων, 10.374), the word used of Euryalus after the boxing, led away “his wits wandering” (*Iliad* 23.698; Merry on 10.374; see the translation's table of key words, ἀλλοφρονέων, and [[fasting]]).
