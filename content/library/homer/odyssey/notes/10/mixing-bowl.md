# 10.357, "sweet wine in a silver bowl" (ἐν ἀργυρέῳ, the mixing bowl of the line before), is a ref.
refs: [10.357]
---

“The third mixed wine sweet to the heart in a mixing bowl, / sweet wine in a silver bowl, and served out golden cups” (10.356–357; see [[handmaids]], [[wine]], [[cup]] and [[honey-sweet]]). The bowl is of silver, like Maron's gift (9.203) and the bowl of Sidonian work that Achilles sets as a prize (*Iliad* 23.741–743; see [[gold-and-silver]]). It is the third of the handmaids' tasks, between the tables and the bath (10.352–359), and the scholia, as W. W. Merry reports them, made Circe the year and her four handmaids the seasons; following the idea, Merry gives the wine to Autumn (*Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 10.350–351; see the translation's note on 10.348–351). Circe's drugs were not in the bowl but in the food (10.235–236; see [[kykeon]] and [[drugs]]), as the suitors had feared a drug in the mixing bowl and Helen cast hers into the wine (2.329–330; 4.220–222; see [[nepenthe]]).
