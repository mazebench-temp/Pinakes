---
summary: Odysseus and Eurylochus shake the lots in a bronze-fitted helmet to decide which band will go to the smoke on Aeaea, and Eurylochus' lot leaps out (10.206–207).
---

Odysseus divides his companions into two bands, one under himself and one under Eurylochus, “And quickly we shook the lots in a bronze-fitted helmet, / and out leapt the lot of great-hearted Eurylochus” (10.206–207; see [[lots]] and [[eurylochus]]). The verse is the Iliad's for the lots of the duel of Paris and Menelaus and of the archery at Patroclus' games, with πάλλομεν, “we shook”, for πάλλον ἑλόντες (*Iliad* 3.316, 23.861), and the lot that leaps out of the helmet is the lot of Ajax in *Iliad* 7, κυνέης, “out of the helmet”, of Agamemnon (*Iliad* 7.175–182; see [[bronze-fitted]]). The lots, W. W. Merry explains, were pebbles shaken in a helmet, and the one that leapt out first decided the lot, as in the lines he quotes from Sophocles' *Ajax* (1285–1287; *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 10.207).
