---
summary: “and there many / souls of the dead who have died will come” (10.529–530).
---

The souls come when the victims are turned toward Erebus and Odysseus himself turns away toward the streams of the river: Merry places him on the shore beyond Oceanus, facing the western darkness, and turning back toward the stream (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 10.529; see the translation's note on 5.350). The Iliad's phrase for corpses names souls here for the first time ([[psyche]], [[erebus]], [[the-strengthless-heads]]).
