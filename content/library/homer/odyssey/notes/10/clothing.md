---
summary: On the morning of the departure Circe puts a cloak and a tunic about Odysseus “as clothing” (10.542).
---

“And she put a cloak and a tunic about me as clothing” (10.542; see [[cloak]], [[tunic]] and [[circe]]): εἵματα stands in apposition to the two garments, “for clothing” (W. W. Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 10.542). The clothes are a host's gift to a departing guest, as Calypso gave Odysseus fragrant clothes when she sent him from her island (5.264; see [[xenia]]).
