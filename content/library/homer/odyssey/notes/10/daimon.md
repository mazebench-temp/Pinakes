---
summary: Aeolus' household greets the returning Odysseus: “How have you come, Odysseus? What evil god has attacked you?” (10.64). After the year on Aeaea the companions call him “Strange one” (δαιμόνιʼ, 10.472).
---

The unnamed god is the power that turned back a voyage begun with every advantage: “How have you come, Odysseus? What evil god has attacked you?” (τίς τοι κακὸς ἔχραε δαίμων; 10.64). ἔχραε, “attacked”, is a thematic aorist from the stem χραF- (W. W. Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 10.64). Aeolus draws the conclusion that the man is “hated by the blessed gods” (10.73–75; see [[themis]] and [[xenia]]). At the end of the year the companions address Odysseus as δαιμόνιε, “Strange one, think now at last of your native land” (10.472), the address that always implies, Merry says, that a man is under some superhuman influence, good or bad, and is used when his behavior, his intentions or his powers excite wonder (on 10.472; see [[strange-one]] and [[nostos]]).
