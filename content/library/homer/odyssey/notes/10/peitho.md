summary: The companions obey Odysseus at once on Aeaea (10.178, 10.428); four times the verse of consent, “my proud spirit was persuaded”, closes a speech (10.406, 10.466, 10.475, 10.550); and Circe would have Odysseus share her bed so that “we may trust one another” (10.335).
# πίθοντο (10.178, 10.428; again 12.222), πεποίθομεν (10.335), only here.
grc: [πίθοντο, πεποίθομεν]
en: [obeyed, trust one another]
---

**Obedience.** On Aeaea the companions do what Odysseus says: “So I spoke, and they quickly obeyed my words” (ἐμοῖς ἐπέεσσι πίθοντο, 10.178, 10.428), where a scholium objected that ὦκα, “quickly”, did not suit men so disheartened (W. W. Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, his critical note on 10.178; see [[hetairos]]). Only Eurylochus holds back, and follows at last “for he was afraid of my terrible rebuke” (10.448; see [[eurylochus]] and [[deos]]).

**Consent.** Four times a speech ends with the verse of a heart persuaded: Circe bids Odysseus bring his companions, “So she spoke, and my proud spirit was persuaded” (10.406); she bids them all stop lamenting and eat, “So she spoke, and our proud spirit was persuaded” (10.466); the companions bid him think of home, “So they spoke, and my proud spirit was persuaded” (10.475); and he bids them rise and go, “So I spoke, and their proud spirit was persuaded” (10.550; see [[our-proud-spirit-was-persuaded]], [[proud]] and [[thymos]]). The verse is the one with which Antinous told how Penelope's request about the web persuaded the suitors (10.466 = 2.103; see [[the-web-of-penelope]]).

**Trust.** Circe asks Odysseus to her bed, “so that, having lain together / in bed and love, we may trust one another” (πεποίθομεν ἀλλήλοισιν, 10.334–335), πεποίθομεν being a subjunctive of the perfect πέποιθα (Merry on 10.335; see [[philotes]]); he will trust her only on oath (10.336–347; see [[oath]]).
