---
summary: “And as when the calves of the fields, around the cows of the herd” (10.410), the simile of the companions running to meet Odysseus.
---

Merry renders ἄγραυλοι “in the fold-yard,” as Eumaeus uses ἀγρός of the homestead in the country (16.27), and takes the cows as having been out in a drove to pasture (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 10.410, 10.412). The calves are the companions at the ship, and Odysseus, coming back from Circe's house, the returning cow (10.410–417; [[cattle]]).
