# αἰδοίῃς (10.11), only here in the Odyssey.
grc: [αἰδοίῃς]
en: [modest]
---

Two women of the book's households are αἰδοῖαι. The sons of Aeolus, who have married their sisters, sleep at night “beside their modest wives” (παρʼ αἰδοίῃς ἀλόχοισιν, 10.11; see [[aeolus]] and [[marriage]]), the half-verse of Priam's palace, where the sons-in-law of Priam slept beside their modest wives in twelve roofed chambers of polished stone (*Iliad* 6.248–250). At Circe's table “the revered housekeeper brought bread and set it before me” (αἰδοίη ταμίη, 10.371), in the verses of every welcome in the poem (10.368–372 = 1.136–140; see [[xenia]] and [[handmaids]]).
