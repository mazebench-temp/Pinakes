---
summary: “for the son of Cronus had made him dispenser of the winds, / both to still and to rouse whichever he wishes” (10.21–22).
---

The explanation comes in the middle of the gift, between the skin in which the paths of the winds are bound up and the silver cord that ties it fast in the ship (10.19–24). Merry sets the phrase beside Zeus' “dispenser of war” (*Iliad* 4.84; Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 10.21), and the translation keeps the English of that title (see the Odyssey translation's key words, ταμίης ἀνέμων; [[aeolus]], [[son-of-cronus]]).
