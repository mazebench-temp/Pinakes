---
summary: Odysseus alone keeps “my black ship outside” the Laestrygonians' harbor (10.95); he carries the stag “to the black ship” (10.169); Eurylochus comes back “to the swift black ship” (10.244); “No one has ever yet come to the house of Hades in a black ship” (10.502); and Circe ties the ram and the ewe “beside the black ship” (10.571).
---

The ship is black seven times in the book (also 10.272, 10.332). Twice μέλαιναν is the black ewe, θῆλύν τε μέλαιναν (10.527, 10.572), and the entry excepts those lines: the victims for the dead are black, as the sheep for Teiresias is “all black” (10.524–525), and Merry notes that white victims were offered to the gods above and black to the gods below (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 10.525; [[sheep]], [[ram]], [[ships]]).
