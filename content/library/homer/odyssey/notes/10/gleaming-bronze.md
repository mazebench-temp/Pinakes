en: [gleaming]

ἦνοψ, the epithet of the fisherman's hook in the Iliad (*Iliad* 16.408), here of the bronze of the tripod in which the water for Odysseus' bath is heated (10.358–361). Merry calls the word of most uncertain derivation: the ancients joined it with ὄψ to make it mean “dazzling” or “blinding,” and he compares the eyes that are blinded “by the gleam of bronze from the shining helmets” (*Iliad* 13.340–341); modern scholars suggest a root meaning “shine” or “burn” (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 10.360; [[tripod]], [[bathing]], [[bronze]]).
