summary: “So they spoke, and the evil counsel of my companions prevailed” (10.46). Hermes bids Odysseus make Circe swear that she will not plan some other evil bane against him (10.299–301), and Odysseus demands it in the same words (10.343–344).
---

The companions' talk at the sheet becomes a decision: “So they spoke, and the evil counsel of my companions prevailed; / they loosed the skin, and all the winds rushed out” (βουλὴ δὲ κακὴ νίκησεν ἑταίρων, 10.46–47), νίκησεν being “carried the day”, as when the worse prevails (18.404; W. W. Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 10.46; see [[nike]], [[hetairos]] and [[skin-of-the-winds]]). The verb is Circe's. Hermes tells Odysseus to bid her swear “that she will not plan some other evil bane against you yourself” (βουλευσέμεν, 10.299–300), and he asks it of her in the same words (10.343–344), the verses with which he made Calypso swear before he built his raft (5.178–179; see [[oath]] and [[calypso]]).
