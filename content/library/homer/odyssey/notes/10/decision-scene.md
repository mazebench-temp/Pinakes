---
summary: Three decisions: Odysseus wakes to the storm and ponders “whether I should fall from the ship and perish in the sea, / or endure in silence” (10.50–53); on Aeaea he ponders whether to go and find out where the smoke comes from, “And as I pondered, this seemed to me the better way” (10.151–155); and he ponders whether to cut off Eurylochus' head, until the companions hold him back (10.438–445).
refs: [10.50-53, 10.151-155, 10.438-445]
---

Merry notes that μερμηρίζω usually takes ὡς, ὅπως or ἤ … ἤ, but here and at 10.438 the infinitive alone (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 10.151; [[pondering-two-ways]]). The first choice is made by the man himself, “But I endured and stayed” (10.53); the second by the better counsel, in the verse of Odysseus' choices in the sea (5.474); the third by his companions (10.441–445; [[soothing-words]]).
