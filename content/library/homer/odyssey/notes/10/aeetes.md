summary: Named once, as Circe's brother, in the verses that bring her in: “own sister of baleful Aeetes; / both were born of the Sun, who brings light to mortals, / and of their mother Perse, whom Oceanus begot as his child” (10.137–139).
# Αἰήταο (10.137) is claimed by the entry. Unnamed: "both" (10.138).
refs: [10.138]
---

Circe is αὐτοκασιγνήτη ὀλοόφρονος Αἰήταο, “own sister of baleful Aeetes” (10.137; see [[circe]], [[oloophron]] and the translation's key words), and the two are children of the Sun and of Perse, daughter of Oceanus (10.138–139; see [[helios]], [[perse]] and [[oceanus]]). W. W. Merry notes that the ancient name of Colchis was Aea and its king Aeetes, whose daughter was Medea, the famous sorceress, and finds in the Odyssey two forms of the story of Circe, one linking her with the East and belonging to the Argonautic legend, which this genealogy follows, the other placing her home in the West (*Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 10.135; see [[aeaea]], [[medea]] and [[jason]]). The poem names him once more, when Circe tells of the Argo passing the Wandering Rocks on its way home “from Aeetes” (12.70, a plain rendering).
