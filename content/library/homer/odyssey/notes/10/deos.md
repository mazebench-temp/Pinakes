summary: The men at the oars flee the Laestrygonians' harbor “fearing destruction” (10.130); Eurylochus' band “were afraid when they saw the dread monsters” at Circe's door (10.219); Circe yields to Odysseus “in fear of you” (10.296) and bids him not to fear at her table (10.380–381); and Eurylochus follows the others “for he was afraid of my terrible rebuke” (10.448).
# ἔδεισαν (10.219; again 13.184), ὑποδείσασα (10.296).
grc: [ἔδεισαν, ὑποδείσασα]
---

**In the harbor.** At the Laestrygonians' harbor Odysseus cuts his cable, and his men toss up the sea with their oars, “fearing destruction” (δείσαντες ὄλεθρον, 10.130; see [[odysseus-among-the-laestrygonians]]).

**At Circe's door.** The wolves and lions stand up on their hind legs and fawn on the men, “and they were afraid when they saw the dread monsters” (τοὶ δʼ ἔδεισαν, ἐπεὶ ἴδον αἰνὰ πέλωρα, 10.219; see [[wolves]] and [[lion]]). ἔδεισαν, the reading of Eustathius and probably of Aristarchus, is better than the common ἔδδεισαν, the ε being lengthened before δϝ, the original form of the root (W. W. Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 10.219 and his critical note; see the translation's note on 10.215–219).

**Circe's fear.** Hermes foretells that Circe, “in fear of you”, will bid Odysseus lie with her (ὑποδείσασα, 10.296), and she cries out and clasps his knees when he springs at her (10.322–324; see [[supplication]]). At the table, when he will not eat, she reassures him: “You have no need / to fear; for I have already sworn you a mighty oath” (δειδίμεν, 10.380–381; see [[oath]]).

**Eurylochus.** Eurylochus, who would not go back to Circe's house and called it the Cyclops' cave again, follows the others in the end, “for he was afraid of my terrible rebuke” (ἔδεισεν γὰρ ἐμὴν ἔκπαγλον ἐνιπήν, 10.447–448; see [[eurylochus]]).
