---
summary: Circe sends Odysseus “to consult the soul of Theban Teiresias, / the blind seer, whose wits are steadfast” (10.492–493).
---

The blindness of the seer is named once in the book and at once set against his mind: his wits are steadfast, φρένες ἔμπεδοι, and “to him even in death Persephone has given a mind, / that he alone should be wise; but the others flit as shadows” (10.493–495). Merry renders “to whom even after his death did Persephone grant sense, for him alone of all men to have wisdom” (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 10.494; [[teiresias]], [[wise]], [[psyche]]).
