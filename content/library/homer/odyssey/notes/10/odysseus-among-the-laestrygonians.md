**Six days.** The voyage from Aeolus is told in the verse of the voyage toward Ithaca, with six days for nine (10.80, as 10.28), but now with no wind: the men row, “since no escort showed itself any longer” (10.78–79; see [[odysseus-and-aeolus]]). On the seventh day they reach “the steep citadel of Lamus”, which Merry takes to be named after the king, so that Lamus would be another name of the murderous Antiphates, and Telepylus, which he explains as “big-gated”, gates on the scale of a people “not like men, but like the Giants” (10.81–82, 10.120; W. W. Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 10.81; see [[lamus]], [[telepylus]] and [[antiphates-the-laestrygonian]]). For the herdsmen who meet in the gateway and the short nights of the land, see [[telepylus]] and the translation's note on 10.81–86.

**The harbor.** τετύχηκε, “stands”, of the cliff, and διαμπερὲς ἀμφοτέρωθεν, “unbroken on both sides”: the wall of cliff ran without a break from one side to the other (10.88; Merry on 10.88; see the translation's note on 10.87–88 and [[harbor]]). ἔνθʼ οἵ γʼ εἴσω πάντες ἔχον, “there they all steered … inside”, is the main clause to “When we had come into the famous harbor” (10.87–91; Merry on 10.91), and the moored ships lay πλησίαι, “close together” (10.93). Against them stands αὐτὰρ ἐγών: “but I alone kept my black ship outside, / there at the far edge, making the cables fast to a rock”, ἐπʼ ἐσχατιῇ telling more nearly where (10.95–96; Merry on 10.95–96; see [[stern-cables]]). The verse of the lookout returns on Aeaea (10.97 = 10.148; again 10.194), and Merry compares Aeneas climbing a rock to look out over the sea (on 10.97, citing *Aeneid* 1.180). No works of oxen or of men were to be seen (10.98): the Laestrygonians, like the Cyclopes, were graziers only, Merry explains, with no plowed land for oxen and no vineyards or gardens, but the smoke showed that the land was inhabited (on 10.97).

**The scouts and the king.** The scouts are sent in the verses of the land of the Lotus-eaters (10.100–102 = 9.88–90; see [[the-lotus-eaters]], [[spies-and-scouts]] and [[heralds]]). λείη ὁδός is a road cleared for use, and ἐκβάντες keeps its sense, “after they had left the ships” (10.103; Merry on 10.103; see the translation's note on 10.103 and [[wagon]]). They ask the girl “who was king of these people and over whom he ruled”: with οἷσιν, Merry explains, the question is double, asking the names of both king and people (10.110; Merry on 10.110 and his critical note). In the queen's house they are aghast, κατὰ δʼ ἔστυγον (10.113): στυγεῖν is the word for the horror of the gods at the dank dwellings of the dead, “which even the gods hate” (*Iliad* 20.65; Merry on 10.113). She calls her husband from the place of assembly (10.114): the Laestrygonians, having a king and an assembly, are more advanced in the externals of civilization than the Cyclopes (Merry on 10.114; see [[assembly]] and [[cyclopes]]). The king seizes one man and “made ready his meal”, μάρψας ὡπλίσσατο δεῖπνον (10.116), in the words of the Cyclops' second meal, “again he seized two men together and made ready his meal” (9.311; see [[odysseus-and-the-cyclops]]); in the Iliad the same words tell the hour “when a woodcutter makes ready his meal” (*Iliad* 11.86).

**The ships destroyed.** τεῦχε βοήν, “he raised a hue and cry” (10.118; Merry on 10.118), and the Laestrygonians come from all sides (10.119–120; see [[laestrygonians]] and [[giants]]). They throw from the tops of the cliffs that command the harbor, stones each a load for an ordinary man (10.121; Merry on 10.121; see the translation's key-word row for ἀνδραχθής). In κακὸς κόναβος κατὰ νῆας the snapping sounds κα, κο, κα in close sequence, repeated in the next verse, are meant to render the crushing volley (10.122–123; Merry on 10.122). The ships were broken up and the men thrown into the water, where the Laestrygonians speared them as they floated, “with tridents or other sharpened tools”, as Eustathius puts it, and carried them off to their meal (10.124; Merry on 10.124). Aristophanes read εἴροντες, “stringing them” like fish, for πείροντες, and the scholia and Apollonius the Sophist have other readings (Merry's critical note on 10.124; see the translation's note on 10.124, [[like-fish]] and [[fish]]).

**One ship.** ὄφρα … τόφρα δέ, “While … I”: while the Laestrygonians were killing the men inside the harbor, Odysseus drew his sword and cut his cables (10.125–127; Merry on 10.126; see [[sword]]). The crew “tossed up the sea with their oars”, ἅλα πάντες ἀνέρριψαν, the reading of Callistratus and Rhianus; most manuscripts have ἅμα, and Merry prefers ἅλα for the formula ἀναρρίπτειν ἅλα πηδῷ (10.130; 7.328, 13.78; Merry on 10.130 and his critical note; see the translation's note on 10.130). The overhanging rocks that the ship flees are the cliffs at the entrance of the harbor (10.131; Merry on 10.131), and “the others perished there in a body” (10.132). The two verses of departure follow in full, as after the Cicones and the Cyclops (10.133–134 = 9.62–63, 9.565–566; see [[from-there-we-sailed-on]] and [[the-tale-to-alcinous]]).
