summary: Three arrivals at a house: Odysseus at Aeolus' a second time, where he and his men sit on the threshold (10.59–66); the scouts at the Laestrygonian king's (10.111–116); and the companions and then Odysseus at Circe's, where the goddess comes out, opens the shining doors and seats them (10.210–243, 10.308–347).
refs: [10.59-75, 10.111-116, 10.210-243, 10.308-347]
---

Each visit turns the pattern. At Aeolus' house the guest of a month comes back as a suppliant and sits on the threshold, a humbler place than the hearth, as Odysseus the beggar will sit in his own house (17.339; Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 10.62), and he is sent away (10.72–75). At the Laestrygonians' the host eats a visitor (10.116). At Circe's every step is in order, the welcome at the door, the seats, the food and drink, but the drink holds drugs, and the guests become swine (10.230–243); Odysseus, protected by the moly, goes through the same steps to another end (10.312–347; [[xenia]], [[circe]], [[aeolus]]).
