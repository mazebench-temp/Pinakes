---
summary: In the simile of the companions' welcome, the calves of a farm run out to meet the cows coming home from pasture, “and the pens no longer / hold them” (10.410–414).
---

When Odysseus comes back to the ship, his companions pour around him in tears, “as when the calves of the fields, around the cows of the herd / as they come back to the dung-yard when they have had their fill of pasture, / all skip together to meet them, and the pens no longer / hold them, but lowing without ceasing they run around / their mothers” (10.410–414; see [[the-calves-and-the-cows]], [[hetairos]] and [[cattle]]). The cows have been out at pasture in a drove, W. W. Merry explains, and when they come back to the farmyard, κόπρος, literally the dung heap, the calves that have stayed penned at the farm greet them frisking and lowing (*Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 10.412; see the translation's note on 10.410–414). ἄγραυλοι is “in the fold-yard”, the ἀγρός being the homestead as against the town, as when Eumaeus complains that Telemachus seldom comes out to the farm (Merry on 10.410; 16.27). κόπρος is the yard of the Iliad's herd on the Shield, which goes out lowing ἀπὸ κόπρου to its pasture (*Iliad* 18.575; see [[the-herd-and-the-lions]]).
