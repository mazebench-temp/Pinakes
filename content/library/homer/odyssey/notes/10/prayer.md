summary: The prayers of the book are to the dead and the gods below. At the pit Odysseus is to beg “the glorious tribes of the dead with prayers” (10.526), and his companions are to “pray to the gods, / to mighty Hades and dread Persephone” as they burn the victims (10.533–534).
# ἐπεύξασθαι (10.533; again 11.46).
grc: [ἐπεύξασθαι]
---

The book has no prayer to the Olympians; its prayers belong to the offering to the dead that Circe prescribes (see [[the-offering-to-the-dead]]). Odysseus is first to implore the dead, vowing them sacrifices when he comes home (10.521–525; see [[supplication]] and [[sacrifice]]): the vow is the gift that the prayer promises, as in the exchange of prayer and offering with the gods (see [[reciprocity-with-the-gods]]). Then, “when you have begged the glorious tribes of the dead with prayers” (εὐχῇσι λίσῃ, 10.526), he is to sacrifice the ram and the ewe; κλυτά is said with meaning, since most of those who will come are heroes or famous women (W. W. Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 10.526). While the victims burn, the companions are to “pray to the gods, / to mighty Hades and dread Persephone” (10.533–534; see [[hades]] and [[persephone]]). In Book 11 Odysseus does all of it, and begs the tribes of the dead “with vows and prayers” (εὐχωλῇσι λιτῇσί τε, 11.34, a plain rendering).
