---
summary: The spring is named once, in Athena's directions to the swineherd's herd, which feeds “by the Rock of Corax and at the spring Arethusa”, eating acorns and drinking “the dark water” (13.407–410).
---

Athena tells Odysseus that he will find the swineherd sitting beside his swine, which feed by the rock and drink at the spring:

{{quote:990a9eba-5610-5e4f-8f4c-ba4b27332950}}

The spring gives the swine μέλαν ὕδωρ, “the dark water”, as the wolves of the Iliad's simile “go in a pack to a spring of dark water / to lap with their thin tongues the dark water” (*Iliad* 16.160–161; see [[the-spring-of-dark-water]]), and the acorns and the water together “nourish the rich lard of swine”, τεθαλυῖαν ἀλοιφήν, with the adjective of the rich dew of Athena's praise of Ithaca (13.245; see the translation's key-word row and [[ithaca]]). The other spring on Ithaca that the poem describes, the built spring near the town with the altar of the nymphs above it, has no name, though its makers have: “which Ithacus made, and Neritus and Polyctor” (17.205–207, a plain rendering; see [[the-rock-of-corax]] and [[eumaeus]]).
