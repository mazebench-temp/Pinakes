---
summary: A single line survives from Aeschylus' Penelope, in which a speaker claims to be a Cretan of the oldest stock, as Odysseus does in his false tales; and in the Suppliants the daughters of Danaus warn of the heavy anger of Zeus of suppliants, the title that Odysseus gives Zeus when he wakes in this book.
---

- **A Cretan.** Of the *Penelope* one line is quoted: ἐγὼ γένος μέν εἰμι Κρὴς ἀρχέστατον, “By birth I am a Cretan, of the oldest stock” (*Penelope* fr. 187 Nauck, a plain rendering). The speaker is not named. In this book Odysseus first makes himself a Cretan, to the disguised Athena: “I heard of Ithaca, yes, even in wide Crete” (13.256–286): [[the-first-cretan-tale]] and [[crete]].
- **Zeus of suppliants.** The daughters of Danaus, suppliants at the altars of Argos, warn King Pelasgus that “heavy is the anger of Zeus of suppliants” (βαρύς γε μέντοι Ζηνὸς ἱκεσίου κότος, *Suppliants* 347), bid him look to “the watcher on high”, the guardian of toiling mortals who sit as suppliants at their neighbors' knees and do not get lawful justice, for “the anger of Zeus of suppliants waits” (*Suppliants* 381–386), and their father reports that the king won over the Argives by warning them of that great anger (*Suppliants* 615–617; plain renderings). Odysseus, believing that the Phaeacians have betrayed him, prays: “May Zeus, the god of suppliants, punish them, who watches over / other men as well, and punishes whoever does wrong” (13.213–214): [[god-of-suppliants]], [[zeus]] and [[supplication]].
