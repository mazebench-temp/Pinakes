---
summary: Odysseus promises the Naiads gifts again, “if the daughter of Zeus, driver of spoil, readily grants / that I myself may live, and makes my dear son grow” (13.359–360).
---

Διὸς θυγάτηρ ἀγελείη is the Iliad's half-verse for Athena turning the arrow aside from Menelaus (*Iliad* 4.128), and this is the Odyssey's only nominative, beside the genitive Ἀθηναίης ἀγελείης (16.207; see the general article). The goddess of spoil is named in a prayer for life and for a son's growing up, and she stands beside him as he prays: she answers at once, “Take heart” (13.361–362; see [[prayer]], [[nymphs]] and [[athena]]). Monro finds the place of με unusual and suggests that the original reading was πρόφρασσα, the proper feminine of πρόφρων (D. B. Monro, *Homer's Odyssey, Books XIII–XXIV*, 1901, critical note on 13.359), the form Odysseus uses of her a little later, “if you would help me with a ready heart” (πρόφρασσʼ ἐπαρήγοις, 13.391).
