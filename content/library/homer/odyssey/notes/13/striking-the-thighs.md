---
summary: Odysseus wakes and does not know his land: “And he sprang up and stood, and looked at his native land; / he groaned aloud then, and struck both his thighs / with the flat of his hands, and lamenting he spoke a word” (13.197–199).
---

The two verses of Patroclus when he saw the Trojans over the wall (*Iliad* 15.397–398); the text prints ἄρ without its elision mark before ἔπειτα (see the translation's note on 13.198). He has looked at his native land and does not know it, and the speech that follows is the question of a man in an unknown country (13.200–216; see [[to-the-land-of-what-mortals]] and [[odysseus-set-ashore-on-ithaca]]).
