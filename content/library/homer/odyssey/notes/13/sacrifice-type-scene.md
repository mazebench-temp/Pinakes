summary: Alcinous' ox for Zeus on the day of the departure, “and when they had burned the thigh-pieces they feasted on a glorious feast” (13.24–26); and the twelve picked bulls for Poseidon, made ready while the lords of the Phaeacians pray “standing around the altar” (13.181–187).
refs: [13.24-26, 13.181-187]
---

The first sacrifice is told in three verses, the victim and the god, then the thigh-pieces burned and the feast (13.24–26; see [[son-of-cronus]], [[sacred-might-of-alcinous]] and [[feast-type-scene]]). The second stops at its first step: the bulls are made ready and the lords pray around the altar, and in the same verse the poem turns to Odysseus waking on Ithaca; the narrative does not return to Scheria (13.184–187; see [[hecatomb]], [[prayer]] and [[the-ship-turned-to-stone]]). The ritual is in [[sacrifice]].
