---
summary: Poseidon answers Zeus, “Then Poseidon the earth-shaker answered him” (13.146), and Athena answers Odysseus three times, “Then the gray-eyed goddess Athena answered him” (13.329, 13.392, 13.420).
---

Poseidon's verse is his answer to Idomeneus in the Iliad (*Iliad* 13.231; see [[earth-shaker]]). Athena's is her answer to Zeus in the first council and to Telemachus at the end of her visit (1.44, 1.80, 1.314), and to Diomedes in the Iliad (*Iliad* 5.825; see [[gray-eyed]]). On Ithaca her three answers are the turns of the talk: that she never doubted his return, and the land made known (13.330–351); her promise to stand beside him, and the beggar's disguise (13.393–415); and her answer about Telemachus' journey (13.421–428; see [[answering-him]]).
