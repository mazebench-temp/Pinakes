# πόθει (13.219), first met here among the Odyssey's notes.
grc: [πόθει]
en: [missed]
---

“So saying, he counted the beautiful tripods and cauldrons / and the gold and the fine woven clothes. / Of these he missed nothing; but he mourned for his native land, / dragging himself along the shore of the loud-roaring sea” (τῶν μὲν ἄρʼ οὔ τι πόθει, 13.217–220; see [[possessions]], [[tripod]], [[cauldron]] and [[dear-native-land]]). ποθέω is to miss what is absent (see the entry), with the genitive of what is found wanting. Of the goods he feared the Phaeacians had stolen (13.215–216) nothing is missing; what he longs for is the land he is standing on, hidden from him by Athena's mist (13.189–196; see [[eidos]] and [[nothing-sweeter-than-ones-own-land]]).
