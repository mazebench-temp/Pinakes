# ἄκοντα, "javelin" (13.225; again 14.531, 21.340), first met here; it applies in the Odyssey only. The Odyssey's
# ἄκοντε (15.192) is the contracted ἀέκοντε, "unwilling", and is not caught.

**The herdsman's javelin.** Athena comes “in form like a young man, a herdsman of sheep”, with a cape about her shoulders, “and under her shining feet she had sandals, and in her hands a javelin” (13.222–225; see [[athena]], [[cloak]], [[sandals]] and [[shape-changing]]). A herdsman carries a javelin, as Eumaeus, going out to his swine for the night, takes “a sharp javelin, a defense against dogs and men” (14.531, a plain rendering; see [[eumaeus]]).

**The Cretan's spear.** In the tale that he tells her, Odysseus killed the son of Idomeneus: “Him I struck with my bronze-fitted spear as he came down / from the country, lying in ambush near the road with one companion. / A very dark night covered the sky, and no one / of men noticed us, and I took his life unseen” (13.267–270; see [[the-first-cretan-tale]], [[orsilochus-son-of-idomeneus]], [[bronze-fitted]], [[ambush]] and [[bronze]]). ἀγρόθεν goes with κατιόντα, “as he came down from the country” (D. B. Monro, *Homer's Odyssey, Books XIII–XXIV*, 1901, on 13.268, comparing 15.428).
