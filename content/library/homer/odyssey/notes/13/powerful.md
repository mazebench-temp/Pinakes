---
summary: Odysseus' farewell to the king begins with the verse of address: “Powerful Alcinous, preeminent among all the people, / send me on my way unharmed, when you have poured libations, and farewell to you” (13.38–39).
---

The sixth and last use of the verse, with which Odysseus praised the dancers, began his tale and answered the king in the pause (8.382, 9.2, 11.355, 11.378), and Euryalus made amends (8.401). This time it asks for the escort, at sunset, and the speech ends with farewell to the Phaeacians, their wives and their children (13.38–46; see [[preeminent]], [[alcinous]] and [[pompe]]).
