---
summary: Athena's promise and its fulfillment: “I will wither the fair skin on your supple limbs” (13.398), and “She withered the fair skin on his supple limbs” (13.430).
---

ἐνὶ γναμπτοῖσι μέλεσσι, the verse-end of a man's strength, kept or lost (11.394), here of the skin that the goddess withers to make him an old beggar: the verse is said first as a promise, κάρψω, and then as the deed, κάρψεν (see the Odyssey translation's key words, and [[shape-changing]]). At 13.430 the manuscripts have μέν οἱ; the text follows Bekker's κάρψεν μέν, and Hermann proposed κάρψεν οἱ (D. B. Monro, *Homer's Odyssey, Books XIII–XXIV*, 1901, critical note on 13.430; see the translation's note on 13.430).
