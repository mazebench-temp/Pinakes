---
summary: The last libation in Alcinous' hall: “and they poured libations / to the blessed gods, who hold the wide sky, / from where they sat” (13.54–56).
grc: ["ἔσπεισαν μακάρεσσι"]
---

θεοῖσιν / ἔσπεισαν μακάρεσσι: the noun ends one verse and its epithet begins the next, followed by the verse-end of Teiresias' and Proteus' instructions for sacrifice, τοὶ οὐρανὸν εὐρὺν ἔχουσιν (4.479, 11.133; see [[wide-sky]] and the Book 12 note on [[immortals]]). Monro renders αὐτόθεν “in their places,” “even as they sat” (D. B. Monro, *Homer's Odyssey, Books XIII–XXIV*, 1901, on 13.56). Alcinous had asked for a prayer to father Zeus (13.51; see [[father-zeus]], [[libation]] and [[honey-sweet]]).
