# κερτομέουσαν (13.326), first met here among the Odyssey's notes.
grc: [κερτομέουσαν]
en: [taunting]
---

“But now I implore you by your father—for I do not think / that I have come to clear-seen Ithaca, but I am roaming some other / land; and I think that you are taunting me / in saying these things, to deceive my mind— / tell me if it is true that I have come to my dear native land” (σὲ δὲ κερτομέουσαν ὀΐω, 13.324–328; see [[eperopeuo]], [[phrenes]] and [[dear-native-land]]). κερτομέουσαν is “seeking to vex” (D. B. Monro, *Homer's Odyssey, Books XIII–XXIV*, 1901, on 13.326). In Scheria Athena had poured a mist around him so that no Phaeacian, meeting him, “should taunt him with words and ask him who he was” (7.16–17); now, in a mist of her making, he suspects the goddess herself of taunting him. He had taunted the Cyclops with cutting words from his ship (9.473–479; see the Book 9 note); now he fears that he is being mocked.
