**A bed on the deck.** When the escorts have stowed the food and drink, “for Odysseus they spread a rug and a linen sheet / on the deck of the hollow ship, so that he might sleep unwaking, / at the stern” (13.73–75; see [[deck]], [[stern]], [[sleep]] and [[odysseus-set-ashore-on-ithaca]]). ῥῆγός τε λίνον τε, “a rug and a linen sheet”, is a guest's bedding, as Achilles' companions spread Phoenix's bed with “fleeces and a rug and the fine nap of linen” (*Iliad* 9.661; see the entry and the translation's row ῥῆγος). Nestor would not let the son of Odysseus “lie down on the deck of a ship” while he had a house to lodge him (3.352–355; see [[xenia]]); the Phaeacians make the guest's bed on the deck itself, and he sleeps there through the voyage (13.79–92).

**Lifted ashore.** At the harbor of Phorcys the sailors step out of the ship, “and first they lifted Odysseus out of the hollow ship, / linen sheet and glossy rug and all, / and laid him down on the sand, overcome by sleep” (13.117–119; see [[glossy]] and [[the-harbor-of-phorcys]]). αὐτῷ σύν τε λίνῳ is “with the linen cloth as it was” (D. B. Monro, *Homer's Odyssey, Books XIII–XXIV*, 1901, on 13.118): they lift man and bedding together, and he does not wake until they have gone (13.187).
