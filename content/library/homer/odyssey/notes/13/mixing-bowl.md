summary: Alcinous bids the herald Pontonous mix a bowl for the libation before the stranger is sent home, in the words of his first welcome (13.49–54); in the cave of the Naiads on Ithaca there are mixing bowls and two-handled jars of stone (13.105–106).
# κρητῆρες (13.105), first met here and found only here in the Odyssey; it applies in the Odyssey only.
grc: [κρητῆρες]
---

**The last bowl.** “And then the might of Alcinous spoke to the herald: / ‘Pontonous, mix a bowl and serve out wine / to all throughout the hall, so that when we have prayed to father Zeus / we may send the stranger on his way to his native land’” (13.49–52; see [[pontonous]], [[heralds]], [[alcinous]] and [[wine]]). The first two verses are those with which Alcinous welcomed the suppliant on the night he came (7.179–180; see the Book 7 note), and the bowl that was then mixed so that they might pour libations to Zeus “who attends on revered suppliants” (7.180–181) is now mixed for the prayer that sends him home. “So he spoke, and Pontonous mixed the wine sweet to the heart, / and served it out to all, going up to each in turn” (13.53–54; see [[libation]] and [[cup]]): the verses of 7.182–183, except that the herald now goes up to each in turn, ἐπισταδόν, where before he first poured drops for the libation into the cups.

**Bowls of stone.** In the cave of the Naiads by the harbor of Phorcys, “In it are mixing bowls and two-handled jars / of stone; and there the bees store up honey” (13.105–106; see [[two-handled-jar]], [[bees]], [[honey]], [[nymphs]], [[the-cave-of-the-naiads]] and [[the-harbor-of-phorcys]]). The vessels of the nymphs' cave are of stone, as their looms are (13.107; see [[loom]]).
