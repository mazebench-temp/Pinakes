---
summary: Poseidon “did not forget the threats which he had made against godlike Odysseus / at first” (13.126–127); and Athena tells of the suitors, “wooing your godlike wife and giving bride-price” (13.378).
---

ἀντιθέῳ Ὀδυσῆϊ is the verse-end of Poseidon's anger in the proem, when he “raged relentlessly / against godlike Odysseus, until he reached his own land” (1.20–21; again 6.330–331). Now Odysseus has reached it, asleep, and the god turns his threats on the Phaeacians who brought him (13.125–164; see [[poseidon]] and [[the-migration-of-the-phaeacians]]). 13.378 is Teiresias' verse for the suitors (11.117; see the Book 11 note), spoken now by Athena on Ithaca (see [[penelope]], [[the-suitors]] and [[bride-price]]).
