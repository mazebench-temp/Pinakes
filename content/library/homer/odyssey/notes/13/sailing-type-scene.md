summary: The last voyage of the Phaeacians in the steps of the scene: the stores stowed and a bed spread for Odysseus “on the deck of the hollow ship” (13.70–75); the rowers at the benches and the cable loosed “from the pierced stone” (13.76–78); the run by night and the arrival at dawn (13.81–95); the landing, when the ship “ran up on the land as much as half her length” (13.113–115); the crew stepping out and lifting out the sleeper and his gifts (13.116–124); and the ship's return, ended by Poseidon in sight of the city (13.161–164).

The verses are the book's own, not those of the earlier voyages, and the steps are made to fit a passenger who sleeps: the bed is spread on the deck at the stern, “so that he might sleep unwaking” (13.73–75), and the men take their places at the benches “in good order” (13.76–77; see [[deck]], [[blankets]], [[rowing-benches]] and [[stern-cables]]). No sail or wind is named; the ship is driven by the oars, “so hard was she driven by the hands of the rowers” (13.115), faster than a falcon (13.86–88; see [[the-four-stallions]]). At the end, in place of the ship drawn up and the crew asleep beside her, the rowers lift out the sleeping passenger with his bedding, set his gifts by the olive and go home (13.116–125; see [[odysseus-set-ashore-on-ithaca]], where the voyage is told). The false tale has a voyage of its own: a Phoenician ship bound for Pylos or Elis, a wind that drives it off its course, a landing by night and a departure for Sidon (13.272–286; see [[the-first-cretan-tale]]).
