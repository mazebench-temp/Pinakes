---
summary: “and when early-born Dawn appeared, the rosy-fingered” (13.18), on the day of the departure; and at the end of the voyage the brightest star “comes to announce the light of early-born Dawn” (13.94).
---

The book has two dawns. The first is the dawn line, on the morning of the thirty-fourth day of the action (D. B. Monro, *Homer's Odyssey, Books XIII–XXIV*, 1901, on 13.18; see [[dawn-line]] and [[rosy-fingered]]); the second is announced by the morning star as the ship comes to Ithaca, at the dawn of the thirty-fifth (Monro on 13.93; see the translation's note on 13.93). Ἠοῦς ἠριγενείης is the genitive of 12.3 (see the Book 12 note and [[eos]]).
