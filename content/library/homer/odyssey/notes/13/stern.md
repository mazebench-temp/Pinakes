summary: Odysseus' bed is laid on the deck at the stern of the Phaeacian ship (13.74–75), and as the ship runs like four stallions under the whip, “her stern lifted” and the wave seethed behind her (13.81–85).
# πρυμνῆς (13.75), first met in the Odyssey here and found only here in it; it applies in the Odyssey only (the Iliad's
# πρυμνῆς, 15.435 and 15.704, is claimed by its Book 15 note).
grc: [πρυμνῆς]
---

**The sleeper's place.** The rug and the linen sheet are spread for Odysseus “on the deck of the hollow ship, so that he might sleep unwaking, / at the stern” (13.74–75; see [[deck]] and [[blankets]]). πρυμνῆς, like πρῴρης “at the prow”, is properly an adjective agreeing with the ship (W. W. Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 12.230), and Merry takes this verse for evidence of a small deck at the stern (Appendix I §5). The stern is the leader's place, where Athena sat with Telemachus beside her on the voyage to Pylos (2.416–418; see the Book 2 note); here it is given to the passenger, who sleeps.

**The stern lifted.** “And she, as four stallions yoked together on the plain, / all rushing on together under the blows of the whip, / lift themselves high as they swiftly make their way, / so her stern lifted, and behind her / the great surging wave of the loud-roaring sea seethed” (13.81–85; see [[the-four-stallions]], [[ships]] and [[loud-roaring-sea]]). For πρύμνη some read πρῴρη, “prow”, as giving a truer picture; but, Monro asks, how could πρύμνη have crept into the text? (D. B. Monro, *Homer's Odyssey, Books XIII–XXIV*, 1901, on 13.84; see the translation's note on 13.81–84). The sentence begun with ἡ δʼ is taken up again with ὣς ἄρα τῆς, the anacoluthon softened by the nominative ἵπποι of the simile (Monro on 13.81).
