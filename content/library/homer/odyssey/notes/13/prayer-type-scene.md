summary: Odysseus prays to the Naiads: “And at once he prayed to the nymphs, lifting up his hands” (13.355–360); and the Phaeacians pray to Zeus over the libation and to Poseidon around the altar (13.51–55, 13.185–187).
refs: [13.51-55, 13.185-187, 13.355-360]
---

The prayer to the Naiads has all the steps of the scene in six verses: the gesture, the hands lifted (13.355); the invocation, by name and descent, “Naiad nymphs, daughters of Zeus” (13.356); the claim, the gifts given “as before” (13.358); and the request, put as a condition on Athena, “if the daughter of Zeus, driver of spoil, readily grants / that I myself may live, and makes my dear son grow” (13.359–360; see [[driver-of-spoil]]). No answer is told; the goddess beside him answers in her own way, “Take heart” (13.362). The Phaeacians' prayers are told without their words (see [[prayer]], where the prayers are discussed, and [[libation]]).
