---
summary: Odysseus prays to the Naiads: “never did I / think to see you again; but now with my gentle vows / rejoice” (13.356–358).
en: [gentle]
---

εὐχωλῇς ἀγανῇσι are the “gentle vows” of Phoenix's speech, with which men turn even the gods aside (*Iliad* 9.499); here they are the greeting of a man come home to the goddesses of his harbor (see [[the-harbor-of-phorcys]]). Monro renders εὐχωλῇς “with my prayers,” “now that you again hear my prayers,” and finds χαίρετε hardly more than a form of greeting, construed with the dative as though it meant “be gladdened” (D. B. Monro, *Homer's Odyssey, Books XIII–XXIV*, 1901, on 13.357). See [[prayer]], [[euchomai]] and [[nymphs]].
