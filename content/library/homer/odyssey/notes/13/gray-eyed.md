---
summary: Seven times “the gray-eyed goddess Athena” speaks, smiles or answers on Ithaca (13.236, 13.287, 13.329, 13.361, 13.374, 13.392, 13.420); and Odysseus calls her “gray-eyed one” when he asks her to stand beside him (13.389).
---

θεὰ γλαυκῶπις Ἀθήνη closes the whole-verse formulas of her answers (see the Book 7 note), and once the verse of the smile, “So he spoke, and the gray-eyed goddess Athena smiled” (13.287), when she has heard his lie (see [[smiling-at-him]] and [[the-first-cretan-tale]]). The vocative γλαυκῶπι is found once in the Iliad, in Zeus' threat to her, delivered by Iris: “so that you may know, gray-eyed one, what it is to fight your father” (*Iliad* 8.420). Here it is the hero's appeal: “If you would stand beside me, gray-eyed one, as eager as that, / I would fight even with three hundred men” (13.389–390; see [[athena]]).
