---
summary: The swift ship of the escort: the herald leads Odysseus “to the swift ship and the shore of the sea” (13.65); Poseidon complains that the Phaeacians “carried him asleep in a swift ship over the sea” (13.134); Zeus advises turning her to stone “like a swift ship, so that all men may marvel” (13.157); and the Phaeacians ask, “who has fettered the swift ship on the sea / as she was driven home?” (13.168–169).
---

ἐπὶ νῆα θοὴν καὶ θῖνα θαλάσσης, the verse-end of the walks down to the ship (4.779; 10.154, 10.407, 10.569; 12.367). The epithet stays with the ship when she is stone: νηῒ θοῇ ἴκελον, “like a swift ship,” is all that is left of the speed that outran the falcon (13.157; 13.86–88), and ἐπέδησε, “fettered,” is the word of the seer fettered by fate (11.292; see the Odyssey translation's key words).
