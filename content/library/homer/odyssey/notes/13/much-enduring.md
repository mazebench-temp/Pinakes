---
summary: Twice “much-enduring brilliant Odysseus was glad, / rejoicing in his own land”: when Athena names Ithaca (13.250–251), and when she scatters the mist and shows it to him (13.353–354).
---

13.250 is the verse of 7.329 and 8.199 (see the Book 8 note). The second verse of each pair ends differently: the first time he rejoices in his land “as she told him,” and answers her with a lie (13.251–255); the second time he sees it, kisses the earth and prays to the nymphs (13.352–360; see [[grain-giving]], [[dear-native-land]], [[brilliant]] and [[odysseus]]).
