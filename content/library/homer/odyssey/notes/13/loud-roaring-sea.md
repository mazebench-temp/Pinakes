---
summary: The ship runs like four stallions, “so her stern lifted, and behind her / the great surging wave of the loud-roaring sea seethed” (13.84–85); and Odysseus, not knowing his own land, mourns for it, “dragging himself along the shore of the loud-roaring sea, / lamenting much” (13.220–221).
---

The Odyssey has the epithet only in this book. The second verse-end, παρὰ θῖνα πολυφλοίσβοιο θαλάσσης, is the Iliad's for Chryses walking away in silence and for Odysseus and Ajax on their way to Achilles (*Iliad* 1.34, 9.182), and Achilles lies groaning “on the shore of the loud-roaring sea” after Patroclus' death (*Iliad* 23.59; see the general article). Here it is the hero of the Odyssey on his own shore (13.219–221; see [[shore]] and [[dear-native-land]]). The wave of 13.85 is πορφύρεον, “surging” (see the Odyssey translation's formulas, πορφύρεον μέγα θῦε πολυφλοίσβοιο θαλάσσης, and [[purple]]).
