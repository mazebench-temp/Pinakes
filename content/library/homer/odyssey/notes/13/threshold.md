---
summary: When he has bidden Arete farewell, Odysseus steps over the threshold of the house of Alcinous for the last time, and a herald leads him to the ship (13.63–65).
---

“So saying, brilliant Odysseus stepped over the threshold, / and with him the might of Alcinous sent a herald / to lead him to the swift ship and the shore of the sea” (13.63–65; see [[the-house-of-alcinous]], [[heralds]] and [[odysseus-at-the-house-of-alcinous]]). ὑπὲρ οὐδὸν ἐβήσετο is the half-verse with which he first stepped over the bronze threshold into the house, after standing before it and gazing at everything (7.135; see the Book 7 note): the stay among the Phaeacians begins and ends at the threshold. His farewell to the queen comes just before it (13.59–62; see [[cup]] and [[arete-queen-of-the-phaeacians]]).
