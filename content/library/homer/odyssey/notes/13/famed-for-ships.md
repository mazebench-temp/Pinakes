---
summary: “And they spoke winged words to one another, / the Phaeacians of the long oars, men famed for ships” (13.165–166), as they watch their ship turned to stone.
---

The verse of the discus and of the song of Ares and Aphrodite (8.191 = 8.369; see the Book 8 note) comes a third and last time when the seafarers' fame is struck: the ship that was “already in plain sight” is rooted in the sea (13.163–169), and Alcinous bids them stop escorting mortals (13.180–181; see [[long-oared]], [[phaeacians]] and [[pompe]]). Odysseus will name them so once more, to his son: Φαίηκές μʼ ἄγαγον ναυσίκλυτοι, “The Phaeacians, famed for ships, brought me” (16.227, a plain rendering).
