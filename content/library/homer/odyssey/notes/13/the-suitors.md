---
summary: The suitors are the object of the book's last part. Athena hides Odysseus so that no one may know him “before the suitors had paid for all their transgression” (13.192–193); she tells him how they “for three years now have been lording it in your hall, / wooing your godlike wife and giving bride-price” (13.377–378); the two plan their destruction under the sacred olive (13.372–373); and she makes him unsightly to them and foretells that the floor will be spattered with their blood (13.393–403, 13.425–428).
---

**Before the suitors had paid.** The mist that keeps Odysseus from knowing his land is also meant to keep him unknown, “lest his wife should know him first, and his townsmen and friends, / before the suitors had paid for all their transgression” (13.192–193; see [[athena]]). The second verse comes again in the hall, when Odysseus refuses Eurymachus' offer of amends: he would not stop his hands from slaughter “before the suitors had paid for all their transgression” (22.63–64, a plain rendering).

**Lording it in the hall.** When the gifts are hidden, the two sit down beside the sacred olive “and planned destruction for the overbearing suitors” (13.372–373; see [[olive]] and [[overbearing]]), and Athena sets out the case:

The suitors are ἀναιδέες, “shameless” (see [[anaideia]]). κοιρανέουσι is said ironically, “are lording it” (D. B. Monro, *Homer's Odyssey, Books XIII–XXIV*, 1901, on 13.377; see the translation's key-word row and [[koiranos]]). τρίετες, “for three years”, is elsewhere always the time during which Penelope deceived them with her web (2.106, 19.151, 24.141; see [[the-web-of-penelope]]), and the giving of ἕδνα is the suitors' side of the wooing (see [[bride-price]]). The last two verses are Antinous' complaint of Penelope in the assembly, there in his mouth and here in the goddess's (2.91–92; see [[penelope]]); ἀγγελίαι are “messages” (Monro on 13.381).

**Three hundred men.** Odysseus answers that, with Athena beside him, “I would fight even with three hundred men” (13.390); Telemachus will count a hundred and eight of them, fifty-two from Dulichium, twenty-four from Same, twenty from Zacynthus and twelve from Ithaca, with servants besides (16.245–253, a plain rendering; see the general article). Athena promises that some of them “will spatter the boundless floor with blood and brains / among the suitors, who are devouring your substance” (13.395–396; see [[earth]]), “who are devouring your substance” being Teiresias' word for them (11.116), and she repeats the verse of the ambush they have laid for Telemachus: “before that the earth will hold fast some / of the suitors, who are devouring your substance” (13.427–428; see [[ambush]] and [[the-journey-of-telemachus]]). One manuscript and Eustathius omit the second of these (Monro's critical note on 13.428).

**Unsightly to the suitors.** She will make him unknown to all mortals, “so that you may seem unsightly to all the suitors / and to your wife and your son” (13.397–403; see [[odysseus-changed-into-a-beggar]] and [[shape-changing]]). ἀεικέλιος, “unsightly”, is said of looks, as of the shipwrecked Odysseus in Nausicaa's eyes (6.242; see the translation's key-word row).
