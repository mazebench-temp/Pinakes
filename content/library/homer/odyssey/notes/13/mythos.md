summary: Words true and false: Alcinous' word pleases the Phaeacians (13.16); Athena means to “tell him each thing” (13.191); Odysseus, about to answer her, “did not speak the truth, but took back his word” (13.254); and she calls him the best of mortals “in counsel and in words”, though his “thievish words” are dear to him (13.295–298).
# μυθήσαιτο (13.191; again 3.114), first met here among the Odyssey's notes.
grc: [μυθήσαιτο]
en: [thievish words, in words, tell him each thing]
---

**Words that are obeyed.** Alcinous asks each of the kings for a tripod and a cauldron, to be repaid by the people, “So spoke Alcinous, and his word pleased them” (τοῖσιν δʼ ἐπιήνδανε μῦθος, 13.16; see [[alcinous]] and [[charis]]): the king's word is the kind of speech that is acted on. Athena's purpose in hiding the land from Odysseus is speech of another kind, to make him unknown “and tell him each thing” (ἕκαστά τε μυθήσαιτο, 13.191), the counsel that comes before the change of his looks (D. B. Monro, *Homer's Odyssey, Books XIII–XXIV*, 1901, on 13.191; see [[eidos]]).

**A word taken back.** Glad to hear the name of Ithaca, Odysseus answers the young herdsman, “yet he did not speak the truth, but took back his word, / always turning a very crafty mind in his breast” (πάλιν δʼ ὅ γε λάζετο μῦθον, 13.254–255; see [[noos]], [[kerdos]] and [[the-first-cretan-tale]]): he left unsaid what he would have said if he had told the truth (Monro on 13.254). In the Iliad the half-verse is Agamemnon's, who takes back his rebuke when he sees that Odysseus is angry (*Iliad* 4.357); here Odysseus takes back the truth itself.

**Thievish words.** Athena answers the lie with praise and mockery: he was not to leave off, even in his own land, “your deceits / and thievish words, which are dear to you from the ground up” (μύθων τε κλοπίων, 13.294–295; see [[apate]] and [[dolos]]), and yet he is “by far the best of all mortals / in counsel and in words” (βουλῇ καὶ μύθοισιν, 13.297–298; see [[aristos]] and [[boule]]). When the gifts are hidden, “the gray-eyed goddess Athena began to speak” (μύθων ἦρχε, 13.374), and the plan against the suitors begins.
