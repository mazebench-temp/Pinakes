---
summary: Athena tells Odysseus of his son, “who went to Lacedaemon with its wide dancing floors, to Menelaus, / to learn the report of you, whether you were still alive anywhere” (13.414–415).
grc: ["πευσόμενος μετὰ σὸν κλέος"]
en: [to learn the report of you]
---

The purpose of the journey in the goddess's own words to the father, with σὸν κλέος, “the report of you,” in the place of νόστον … πατρός; D. B. Monro renders μετὰ σὸν κλέος “after thy story” (*Homer's Odyssey, Books XIII–XXIV*, 1901, on 13.415). When Odysseus asks why she did not simply tell his son, she answers that she sent him herself, “so that he might win noble fame” (13.417–422): the son who went to learn of his father's fame is to win his own, as she said in the first council (1.95; see [[kleos]], [[the-journey-of-telemachus]] and [[telemachus]]).
