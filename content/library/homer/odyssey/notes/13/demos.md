summary: The land and the people: the gifts are to be gathered “among the people” (13.14), the harbor of Phorcys lies “in the land of Ithaca” (13.97), the leaders “of the people of the Phaeacians” pray to Poseidon (13.186), and Odysseus asks the disguised Athena “what land is this, what people, what men are born here?” (13.233).
# δῆμος (13.233; again 7.11, 7.150, 16.114, 21.17), accent-sensitive, as in the Book 11 note.
grc: ["=δῆμος"]
---

**The land.** The harbor of Phorcys is “in the land of Ithaca” (ἐν δήμῳ Ἰθάκης, 13.97; see [[the-harbor-of-phorcys]] and [[ithaca]]); the false Cretan would not serve Idomeneus “in the land of the Trojans” (13.266); and Odysseus reminds Athena that she encouraged him “in the rich land of the Phaeacian men” (13.322).

**The people.** The kings who give the stranger a tripod and a cauldron each will recover the cost, “and we in turn will gather it among the people / and repay ourselves” (κατὰ δῆμον, 13.14–15; see [[charis]]), and Odysseus' farewell ends with the wish, “and may there be no evil among the people” (μεταδήμιον, 13.46; see [[arete]]). When the ship has been turned to stone, “the leaders and rulers of the people of the Phaeacians” stand around Poseidon's altar (13.186; see [[hegemon]]).

**The question.** Waking in a land he does not know, Odysseus asks the young shepherd, “And tell me this truly, so that I may know it well, / what land is this, what people, what men are born here?” (τίς γῆ, τίς δῆμος, τίνες ἀνέρες ἐγγεγάασιν, 13.232–233; see [[tell-me-this-truly]] and [[to-the-land-of-what-mortals]]). In the *Homeric Hymn to Apollo* the Cretan sailors whom the god has carried to Crisa against their will put the same question, in nearly the same verse, to Apollo, who has come to them in the likeness of a young man: τίς δῆμος; τίς γαῖα; τίνες βροτοὶ ἐγγεγάασιν; (*Homeric Hymn to Apollo* 449–450, 467–468).
