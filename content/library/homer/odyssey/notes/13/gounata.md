summary: The plowman of the simile goes home at sunset, “and his knees are hindered as he goes” (13.34); on Ithaca Odysseus comes to the young herdsman's “dear knees” (13.231), and implores the revealed goddess “by your father” (13.324).
# γουνάζομαι (13.324; again 11.66), first met here among the Odyssey's notes.
grc: [γουνάζομαι]
en: [implore]
---

**The plowman's knees.** Odysseus watches the sun all day, longing for it to set, “as when a man longs for his supper, for whom all day long / two wine-dark oxen drag the jointed plow through the fallow field, / and gladly for him the light of the sun sets, / so that he may go about his supper, and his knees are hindered as he goes” (βλάβεται δέ τε γούνατʼ ἰόντι, 13.31–34; see [[the-plowman-and-his-supper]]). The half-verse is the Iliad's, of the man who fights all day without food, whom thirst and hunger overtake (*Iliad* 19.165–166). For the anomalous present βλάβεται, Monro notes, the aorist βλάβετο could always be read (D. B. Monro, *Homer's Odyssey, Books XIII–XXIV*, 1901, his critical note on 13.34).

**The suppliant's knees.** On the shore Odysseus greets the young herdsman with the words of supplication: “for to you / I pray as to a god, and I come to your dear knees” (καί σευ φίλα γούναθʼ ἱκάνω, 13.230–231; see [[supplication]] and [[euchomai]]). When the herdsman has become a goddess and told him that he is in Ithaca, he supplicates again, with the verb: “But now I implore you by your father” (νῦν δέ σε πρὸς πατρὸς γουνάζομαι, 13.324; see [[pater]] and [[eperopeuo]]), as Elpenor's shade had implored him “by those you left behind” (11.66).
