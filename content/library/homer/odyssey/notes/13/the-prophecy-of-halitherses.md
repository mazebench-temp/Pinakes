# Unnamed: the return foretold, in the prophecy's words (13.131-132, 13.340, 13.397).
refs: [13.131-132, 13.340, 13.397]
---

**The words of the prophecy.** When the Argives embarked for Ilios, Halitherses foretold that Odysseus, “after suffering many evils, and losing all his companions, / unknown to all, in the twentieth year / he would come home” (2.174–176). Three of its terms are spoken again in the thirteenth book, on the day of the homecoming, and by gods. Poseidon, complaining to Zeus, says that he had meant Odysseus to come home only after many sufferings, Ὀδυσῆʼ ἐφάμην κακὰ πολλὰ παθόντα / οἴκαδʼ ἐλεύσεσθαι, “I said that Odysseus, after suffering many evils, / would come home” (13.131–132; see [[poseidon]] and [[the-ship-turned-to-stone]]), nearly the seer's φῆν κακὰ πολλὰ παθόντʼ … οἴκαδʼ ἐλεύσεσθαι. Athena tells Odysseus that she never doubted that he would come home ὀλέσας ἄπο πάντας ἑταίρους, “having lost all your companions” (13.340; see [[athena]]), as Halitherses said ὀλέσαντʼ ἄπο πάντας ἑταίρους (2.174), and the Cyclops and Teiresias before her (9.534, 11.114; see [[the-return-of-odysseus]]). Then she makes him ἄγνωστον, “unknown”: “But come, I will make you unknown to all mortals” (13.397; also 13.191), the ἄγνωστον πάντεσσιν of the prophecy (2.175; see [[odysseus-changed-into-a-beggar]]).

**Fulfilled.** Halitherses ended, τὰ δὲ δὴ νῦν πάντα τελεῖται, “and now it is all being fulfilled” (2.176). The same half-verse closes another remembered prophecy in this book, when Alcinous sees the Phaeacian ship turned to stone and recalls his father's words: “So the old man used to say; and now it is all being fulfilled” (13.178; see [[prophecy]] and [[the-migration-of-the-phaeacians]]). Halitherses also said that Odysseus was already near, planting death and doom for the suitors (2.163–167); on Ithaca he and the goddess sit by the sacred olive and plan destruction for them (13.372–373; see [[the-suitors]]).
