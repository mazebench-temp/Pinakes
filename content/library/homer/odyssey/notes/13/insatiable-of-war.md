---
summary: Athena, when Odysseus has lied to her: “Hard man, of subtle wiles, insatiable of tricks” (13.293).
en: [insatiable of tricks]
---

δόλων ἆτʼ are the words of Socus' challenge to Odysseus in the Iliad, “insatiable of tricks and toil” (*Iliad* 11.430; see the note on *Iliad* 11), with the vocative of ἆτος, the word of Ares' formula. The Trojan's taunt becomes the goddess's fond reproach, after σχέτλιε and ποικιλομῆτα, of the man who has just told her a false tale (13.287–295; see [[hard]], [[of-subtle-wiles]], [[dolos]] and [[the-first-cretan-tale]]).
