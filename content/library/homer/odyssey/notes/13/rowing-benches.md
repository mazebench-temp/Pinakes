summary: Alcinous stows the gifts under the thwarts of the Phaeacian ship (13.20–22); the crew sit down at the rowing benches, each man in good order (13.76–77); and at the harbor of Phorcys they step out of “the well-thwarted ship” (13.116).
# ἐϋζύγου, "well-thwarted" (13.116; ἐΰζυγοι again at 17.288, to add with its book), the epithet of a ship with good
# thwarts, ζυγά, first met here; it applies in the Odyssey only. "ὑπὸ ζυγὰ" (13.21) is claimed by the Book 9 note.
grc: [ἐϋζύγου]
en: [well-thwarted]
---

**Under the thwarts.** “And the sacred might of Alcinous stowed it well, / under the thwarts, going himself through the ship, so that it might not / hinder any of the companions as they rowed” (13.20–22; see [[alcinous]], [[oars]] and [[tripod]]). ὑπὸ ζυγά goes with κατέθηκε: the bronze is stowed under the thwarts (D. B. Monro, *Homer's Odyssey, Books XIII–XXIV*, 1901, on 13.21). The ζυγά are the cross-beams of the hull, under which Odysseus bound the companions who had eaten the lotus (9.99; see the Book 9 note).

**At the benches.** When Odysseus has lain down on the deck, “they sat down at the rowing benches, each man / in good order, and loosed the cable from the pierced stone” (13.76–77; see [[stern-cables]] and [[deck]]). κόσμῳ, “in good order”, says of the Phaeacians what ἑξῆς, “in order”, says of Odysseus' crews in the Odyssey's verse of rowing (9.104; see the entry).

**The well-thwarted ship.** At Ithaca “they stepped out of the well-thwarted ship onto the land” (13.116; see [[ships]]). ἐΰζυγος, “well-thwarted”, is formed from ζυγά, the thwarts, and is found in Homer only here and at 17.288 (see the translation's row ἐΰζυγος).
