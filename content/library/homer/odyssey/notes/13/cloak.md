summary: A slave woman of Arete carries a freshly washed mantle and a tunic to the ship for Odysseus (13.67); the nymphs of the cave weave sea-purple webs, φάρεα, on their looms of stone (13.107–108); Athena comes to him as a young herdsman with a cape folded double around her shoulders (13.222–224); and the beggar she makes of him has a deerskin for a cloak (13.436–437).

# φάρεʼ alone is also the cloth for the sails, which the sails' Book 5 note claims ("φάρεʼ ἔνεικε", 5.258); and λώπην,
# the herdsman's cape (13.224, the only place in Homer).

**The mantle for the voyage.** “Arete sent slave women with him, / one carrying a freshly washed mantle and a tunic” (13.66–67; see [[arete-queen-of-the-phaeacians]], [[tunic]] and [[washing-troughs]]): φᾶρος ἐϋπλυνὲς ἠδὲ χιτῶνα, the gift that each of the kings brought and that the queen added of her own (8.392, 8.425; see the Book 8 note and [[the-gifts-of-the-phaeacians]]). The words return when Athena gives him back his own looks in the swineherd's hut: “First a freshly washed mantle and a tunic / she put about his chest” (16.173–174, a plain rendering).

**Webs.** In the cave of the Naiads “the nymphs / weave sea-purple webs, a wonder to see” (13.107–108; see [[the-cave-of-the-naiads]], [[nymphs]], [[loom]], [[purple]] and [[weaving]]). φάρεα are “webs” (D. B. Monro, *Homer's Odyssey, Books XIII–XXIV*, 1901, on 13.108): Monro compares 19.138, where the φᾶρος that a god put into Penelope's mind is the web she weaves on her great loom (see the translation's row ἱστοὶ λίθεοι; φάρεα ἁλιπόρφυρα, and [[the-web-of-penelope]]). The word names the cloth on the loom as well as the mantle made from it.

**The herdsman's cape.** Athena comes to Odysseus “in form like a young man, a herdsman of sheep, / very delicate, such as are the sons of lords, / with a well-made cape folded double around her shoulders” (13.222–224; see [[athena]], [[sandals]], [[spear]] and [[shape-changing]]). λώπη, found only here in Homer, is a cloak or wrap: the Suda explains it as ἱμάτιον, περίβλημα (λ 731; see the translation's row ἐπιβώτωρ μήλων; παναπάλος; λώπη). δίπτυχος, “folded double”, is the way a χλαῖνα could be worn, double (διπλῆ) rather than single (see the entry).

**A deerskin for the beggar.** When Athena makes him an old beggar she puts a rag and a tunic on him, “and around him she put the great skin of a swift deer, / worn bare” (13.434–437; see [[clothing]], [[deer]], [[pouch]] and [[odysseus-changed-into-a-beggar]]): ψιλόν is “bare, the wool worn off” (Monro on 13.437). The skin takes the place of a cloak, as the skin of a great lion does for Agamemnon when he cannot sleep (*Iliad* 10.21–24; see [[lion-skin]]). In the swineherd's hut he will tell a tale to win a cloak for the night, and Eumaeus throws over him the one he kept to change into, “thick and large” (14.459–461, 14.520–522, a plain rendering; see [[eumaeus]]).
