---
summary: The rock is named once, in Athena's directions: Odysseus will find the swineherd sitting beside his swine, which are feeding “by the Rock of Corax and at the spring Arethusa” (13.407–410).
---

When Athena has told Odysseus that she will make him unknown to all mortals, she sends him first to the swineherd and tells him where to find him (13.397–406; see [[eumaeus]]):

{{quote:990a9eba-5610-5e4f-8f4c-ba4b27332950}}

The rock and the spring mark the place of the herd: the swine are feeding, νέμονται, beside the one and drinking from the other, eating acorns, βάλανον, and the dark water, which together “nourish the rich lard of swine” (see [[arethusa]], [[boar]] and the translation's key-word row for βάλανος and τεθαλυῖα ἀλοιφή). παρήμενον, “sitting beside”, is “abiding with” (D. B. Monro, *Homer's Odyssey, Books XIII–XXIV*, 1901, on 13.407). In the next book Odysseus goes up from the harbor “by the rugged path / through the wooded country, among the heights, to where Athena / had shown him the brilliant swineherd” (14.1–3, a plain rendering; see [[the-harbor-of-phorcys]]), and finds him sitting in the porch of his high-walled fold (14.5–6).
