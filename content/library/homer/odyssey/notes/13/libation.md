summary: Odysseus asks the Phaeacians to pour libations and send him on his way (13.39); Pontonous serves the wine, they pour to the blessed gods from where they sit, and Odysseus puts the cup in Arete's hand and bids her farewell (13.50–62).
# ἔσπεισαν (13.55), only here.
grc: [ἔσπεισαν]
---

**The parting libation.** When the sun has set, Odysseus speaks first: “Powerful Alcinous, preeminent among all the people, / send me on my way unharmed, when you have poured libations, and farewell to you” (13.38–39; see [[alcinous]] and [[pompe]]). The king gives the order to Pontonous in the verses of the night the stranger came (13.49–50 = 7.178–179; see [[heralds]]), but for a new purpose: then the libation was for Zeus “who attends on revered suppliants” (7.180–181; see the Book 7 note), now it is “so that when we have prayed to father Zeus / we may send the stranger on his way to his native land” (13.51–52; see [[prayer]]). Pontonous serves it out, “and they poured libations / to the blessed gods, who hold the wide sky, / from where they sat” (13.54–56). αὐτόθεν ἐξ ἑδρέων is “in their places”, “even as they sat”, as Agamemnon speaks “from where he sat, and not standing up in their midst” (D. B. Monro, *Homer's Odyssey, Books XIII–XXIV*, 1901, on 13.56, comparing *Iliad* 19.77).

**The cup for the queen.** “But brilliant Odysseus stood up, / and placed the two-handled cup in the hand of Arete” (13.56–57; see [[arete-queen-of-the-phaeacians]] and [[cup]]), and with it he speaks his farewell: “Farewell to you, queen, continually, until old age / and death come, which are the lot of men” (13.59–60). It was to Arete that he made his first plea in the house, at her knees (7.142–152), and he takes his leave of the house through her. The gesture is the one with which Peisistratus gave the cup first to the disguised Athena at Pylos, “So saying, he put the cup of sweet wine in her hand” (3.51), when Nestor's sons received the strangers at the sacrifice (see [[the-sacrifice-at-pylos]]).
