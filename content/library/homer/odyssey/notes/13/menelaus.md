summary: Athena tells Odysseus that Telemachus has gone to Lacedaemon, to Menelaus, for news of his father, and that he sits at his ease in the house of the son of Atreus with untold plenty beside him (13.412–424).
# Μενελά* (in the entry) claims Μενέλαον (13.414). Unnamed: "in the house of the son of Atreus" (13.424), where
# Ἀτρεΐδαο is Menelaus (agamemnon's Book 13 note excepts the line).
refs: [13.424]
---

Athena will go “to Sparta of the lovely women / to call Telemachus, your dear son, Odysseus, / who went to Lacedaemon with its wide dancing floors, to Menelaus, / to learn the report of you, whether you were still alive anywhere” (13.412–415; see [[sparta]], [[lacedaemon]], [[telemachus]] and [[the-journey-of-telemachus]]). For πὰρ Μενέλαον, the reading of the text, the vulgate has πρός (D. B. Monro, *Homer's Odyssey, Books XIII–XXIV*, 1901, critical note on 13.414). When Odysseus asks why she let his son wander, she answers that Telemachus “has no toil, but sits at his ease / in the house of the son of Atreus, and untold plenty lies beside him” (13.423–424): the house that Telemachus thought like the court of Olympian Zeus, “so many are these things, untold” (4.74–75, the same ἄσπετα), and the wealth that Menelaus gathered in eight years of wandering (4.78–92; see [[the-wanderings-of-menelaus]]). Book 15, which goes back to Telemachus, takes up Athena where this book leaves her, on her way to Lacedaemon “to remind him of his homecoming and to urge him to return” (15.1–3, a plain rendering).
