---
summary: Two dawns: “And when early-born Dawn appeared, the rosy-fingered, / they hurried to the ship, and brought the bronze” (13.18–19), the last morning on Scheria; and at the end of the voyage, “When that brightest star rose up which most of all / comes to announce the light of early-born Dawn, / then the seafaring ship drew near to the island” (13.93–95).
refs: [13.93-95]
---

D. B. Monro counts the first the morning of the thirty-fourth day of the action of the poem and the second the dawn of the thirty-fifth (*Homer's Odyssey, Books XIII–XXIV*, 1901, on 13.18 and 13.93). The day between is a day of waiting: Odysseus keeps turning his head toward the sun, “in haste for it to set” (13.29–35; see [[sunset-lines]]), and the voyage is made by night. The second dawn is told without the verse, by the star that announces it, which the poet names only as “that brightest star”; ὑπερέσχε is intransitive, “rises” (Monro on 13.93; see the translation's note on 13.93, and [[the-morning-star]], [[eos]], [[early-born]] and [[seafaring-ship]]).
