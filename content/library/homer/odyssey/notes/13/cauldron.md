# λέβητα (13.13), first met in the Odyssey here and found only here in it; it applies in the Odyssey only (the Iliad's
# λέβητα, 23.267, is in its Book 23 note). λέβητας (13.217) is the entry's.
grc: [λέβητα]

“But come, let us give him a great tripod and a cauldron, / man by man” (13.13–14; see [[tripod]], [[alcinous]] and [[the-gifts-of-the-phaeacians]]). The tripod and the cauldron go together as the Iliad's treasure goes, the seven tripods and twenty cauldrons at the head of Agamemnon's gifts and the prizes that Achilles brings out for the games (*Iliad* 9.122–123, 23.259; see [[the-gifts-of-agamemnon]]); in the Odyssey the λέβης is more often the basin of the guest's washing (1.137, 3.440; see [[basin-and-pitcher]]) or the cauldron of the bath (8.426; see the Book 8 note). Eustathius read ἠέ for ἠδέ, “a tripod or a cauldron” (D. B. Monro, *Homer's Odyssey, Books XIII–XXIV*, 1901, critical note on 13.13), the alternative of Menelaus' promise to Telemachus that no host will send them away empty-handed, but each will give “either a tripod of good bronze or a cauldron” (15.82–84). On the shore of Ithaca Odysseus “counted the beautiful tripods and cauldrons / and the gold and the fine woven clothes” (13.217–218), and missed nothing (see [[possessions]]).
