---
summary: To make him unknown, Athena dims Odysseus' eyes, “that were so beautiful before”: she says that she will (13.401), and with her wand she does (13.433).
---

Athena tells Odysseus what she will make of him: she will wither his skin and destroy his fair hair, “and I will dim your eyes, that were so beautiful before, / so that you may seem unsightly to all the suitors / and to your wife and your son, whom you left in your halls” (13.398–403; see [[athena]], [[shape-changing]], [[odysseus-changed-into-a-beggar]] and [[hair]]). When she has touched him with her wand, the deed follows in the words of the promise, “and dimmed his eyes, that were so beautiful before” (13.429–433; see [[wand]]). κνυζόω, “dim”, is found in Homer only in these two verses (see the translation's row κάρπτω; κνυζόω), and the Suda explains the future of 13.401, κνυζώσω, as ἀπρεπεῖς ποιήσω, “I will make unseemly” (κ 1891). Skin, hair and eyes are changed together, and the rags, the deerskin, the staff and the pouch complete the beggar (13.434–438; see [[clothing]] and [[pouch]]).
