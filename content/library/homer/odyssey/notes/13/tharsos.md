---
summary: Odysseus reminds Athena that in Scheria “you encouraged me with words” (13.323), in a verse many critics reject; she answers his prayer to the nymphs with “Take heart” (13.362); and he asks her for “a might full of daring” (13.387).
---

**Encouraged in Scheria.** After the sack of Troy, Odysseus tells Athena, he never saw her on his ship, “until the time when, in the rich land of the Phaeacian men, / you encouraged me with words and led me into the city yourself” (θάρσυνάς τε ἔπεσσι, 13.322–323; see [[odysseus-at-the-house-of-alcinous]]). There she had met him “like a young girl, a maiden, carrying a water jar”, led him to the palace, and sent him in with an encouragement, “for a bold man is better in all / deeds” (7.19–52; see [[athena]] and [[shape-changing]]). The ancients argued that Odysseus could not know she was there, since she did not reveal herself to him; Monro thinks the objection supposes more accuracy than a poem allows, but grants that the two verses have something of the air of an insertion meant to reconcile this speech with the Phaeacian books (D. B. Monro, *Homer's Odyssey, Books XIII–XXIV*, 1901, on 13.320–323; see the Odyssey translation's note on 13.320–323).

**Take heart.** When he has prayed to the Naiads to let him live and see his son grow, she answers with the ordinary word of encouragement: “Take heart; do not let these things trouble your mind” (θάρσει, 13.362; see [[phrenes]] and [[nymphs]]), Hephaestus' verse to Thetis in the Iliad (*Iliad* 18.463). Told of the suitors, he asks her for the gift of daring itself: “putting in me a might full of daring, / as when we loosed the shining veil of Troy” (13.387–388; see [[menos]] and [[full-of-daring]]).
