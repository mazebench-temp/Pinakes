---
summary: Athena, in the likeness of a young herdsman, answers Odysseus' question about the land: “You are a fool, stranger, or you have come from far away” (13.237), the verse with which the Cyclops answered him.
---

Odysseus has asked what land this is, whether an island or a shore of the mainland (13.232–235), and the herdsman answers: “You are a fool, stranger, or you have come from far away, / if indeed you ask about this land. It is not so very / nameless; very many know it” (νήπιός εἰς, ὦ ξεῖνʼ, 13.237–239; see [[athena]], [[ithaca]] and [[xenia]]). The verse is the one in which the Cyclops answered Odysseus' appeal to the gods of suppliants and strangers (9.273; see [[polyphemus-the-cyclops]]). There it was contempt; here it is the goddess's play, and it is true: the man who asks the name of the land is the one who should know it best, and does not, because she has hidden it from him (13.189–196; see [[eidos]]). νήπιος is the word with which the narrator marks a man who does not know what is happening to him (see the entry); the goddess uses it of the hero whom she is about to enlighten. In 13.238 the text prints the vulgate τήνδε τε, which Monro calls impossible; one of the best manuscripts has τήνδε γε, with the stress on *this* land (D. B. Monro, *Homer's Odyssey, Books XIII–XXIV*, 1901, on 13.238).
