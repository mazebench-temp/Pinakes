---
summary: Under the sacred olive Athena begins the plan: “Son of Laertes, sprung from Zeus, Odysseus of many devices, / consider how you will lay hands on the shameless suitors” (13.375–376).
---

The goddess who is “famed for cunning and crafty ways” among the gods (13.299) gives him his epithet of contrivance as she asks him to devise the suitors' death, and he answers by asking her to devise it with him: “But come, weave a plan, how I may take vengeance on them” (13.386; see [[son-of-laertes]], [[metis]] and [[the-suitors]]).
