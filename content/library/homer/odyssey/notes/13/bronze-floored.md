---
summary: Alcinous: “Odysseus, since you have come to my bronze-floored house, / high-roofed, so I think that you will not be beaten back / and sail for home again” (13.4–6).
---

In the Iliad χαλκοβατὲς δῶ is the house of Zeus (*Iliad* 1.426, 14.173, 21.438, 21.505), and in Book 8 the house of Hephaestus (8.321; see the Book 8 note); only here does a mortal give it to his own house, the palace whose walls and threshold are of bronze (7.86–89; see [[the-house-of-alcinous]] and [[high-roofed]]). The house is the ground of the king's assurance: the guest who has reached it will not be beaten back (see the translation's note on 13.5–6).
