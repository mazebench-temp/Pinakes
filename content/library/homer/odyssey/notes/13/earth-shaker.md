summary: “But the earth-shaker / did not forget the threats which he had made against godlike Odysseus” (13.125–126); “Then Poseidon the earth-shaker answered him” (13.146); “But when Poseidon the earth-shaker heard this, / he went to Scheria” (13.159–160); and “the earth-shaker came near her, / and turned her to stone” (13.162–163).
---

The scene follows the pattern of *Iliad* 7. There Poseidon complained to Zeus that the Achaeans had built their wall without giving hecatombs to the gods, and that its fame would make men forget the wall he and Apollo built for Laomedon; Zeus answered with the verse he uses here and let him sweep the wall into the sea and cover the beach with sand (*Iliad* 7.445–463; see the general article). Here he complains that mortals do not honor him, and he would shatter the Phaeacians' ship and cover their city with a great mountain (13.128–152; see [[shaker-of-the-earth]] and [[cloud-gatherer]]). The god of the earthquake roots the ship in the sea “with a blow of the flat of his hand” (13.164), and the Phaeacians make ready twelve bulls for him and pray that he may not cover their city with the mountain (13.172–187; see [[the-migration-of-the-phaeacians]], [[hecatomb]] and [[poseidon]]).
