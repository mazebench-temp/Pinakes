**The last libation.** At sunset Odysseus asks the king to pour libations and send him on his way, and the Phaeacians approve (13.38–48; see [[odysseus]] and [[pompe]]). “And then the might of Alcinous spoke to the herald: / ‘Pontonous, mix a bowl and serve out wine / to all throughout the hall, so that when we have prayed to father Zeus / we may send the stranger on his way to his native land’” (13.49–52; see [[alcinous]], [[mixing-bowl]], [[libation]] and [[zeus]]). The first two verses are the king's order on the night the stranger came, when the wine was poured to Zeus “who attends on revered suppliants” (7.178–181); now it is poured for his going. “So he spoke, and Pontonous mixed the wine sweet to the heart, / and served it out to all, going up to each in turn” (13.53–54): the first verse is 7.182, but where in Book 7 he first poured drops for the libation into the cups (7.183), here he goes up to each man in turn, ἐπισταδόν, as Odysseus went from man to man on Thrinacia, and as the suitors' herald Mulius serves the wine for their last libation of an evening in Odysseus' hall (D. B. Monro, *Homer's Odyssey, Books XIII–XXIV*, 1901, on 13.54, comparing 12.392 and 18.425; see [[heralds]]). Then “they poured libations / to the blessed gods, who hold the wide sky, / from where they sat” (13.54–56), and Odysseus gives his cup to Arete (13.56–57; see [[arete-queen-of-the-phaeacians]]).

**The other herald.** The herald whom the king sends to lead Odysseus down to the ship (13.64–65) is not named, and the poem does not say that he is Pontonous. Pontonous is named for the last time here: the herald who mixed the wine for the first libation of the stranger's stay mixes it for the last.
