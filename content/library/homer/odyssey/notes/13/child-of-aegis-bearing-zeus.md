---
summary: “Pallas Athena, daughter of aegis-bearing Zeus” (13.252, 13.371).
---

κούρη Διὸς αἰγιόχοιο closes the verse when Odysseus rejoices at the name of his land, as the goddess told it him (13.250–252), and again when she has laid away his goods in the cave and set a stone against the doors (13.366–371; see [[the-cave-of-the-naiads]]). Between the two she names herself with the shorter title, “Pallas Athena, daughter of Zeus” (13.300; see [[daughter-of-zeus]] and [[aegis-bearing]]). At 13.252 the text prints a comma after Διός (see the translation's note on 13.252). The Naiads, whom Odysseus calls “daughters of Zeus” in his prayer (13.356), have only the shorter title too (see [[nymphs]] and [[athena]]).
