---
summary: The Phaeacians “sat down at the rowing benches, each man / in good order, and loosed the cable from the pierced stone” (13.76–77).
grc: ["τρητοῖο λίθοιο"]
en: [pierced]
---

τρητός, “bored,” which of a bed is rendered “corded” (see the general article and the translation's note on 1.440), is said here of a mooring stone bored to take the cable, the only stone so called in Homer (see the Odyssey translation's key words, πεῖσμα … τρητοῖο λίθοιο, and [[stern-cables]] and [[sailing-type-scene]]). In the harbor of Phorcys, at the end of the voyage, ships stay “without a mooring rope” (13.100; see [[the-harbor-of-phorcys]]).
