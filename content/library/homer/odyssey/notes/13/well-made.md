---
summary: Athena comes as a young herdsman “with a well-made cape folded double around her shoulders” (13.224); and Odysseus carries into the cave “the gold and the unyielding bronze / and the well-made clothes, which the Phaeacians had given him” (13.368–369).
---

εὐεργέα λώπην, the herdsman's cape, a word found only here in Homer (see the Odyssey translation's key words), with the adjective of the Odyssey's “well-made ship” (8.567; see [[perikalles]] and [[dressing-scene]]). εὐποίητα, of the clothes among the gifts, is new to the entry (see [[unyielding]], [[clothing]] and [[possessions]]).
