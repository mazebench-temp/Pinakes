---
summary: The Odyssey names the Epeians only at the end of one verse: Elis is the land “where the Epeians rule” (13.275), the second goal of the Cretan in Odysseus' false tale; the verse returns in Books 15 and 24.
---

The people of Elis, enemies of Pylos in Nestor's tales and a contingent of forty ships in the Catalogue (*Iliad* 11.670–761, 2.615–624), are in the Odyssey no more than the end of a verse: “or at shining Elis, where the Epeians rule” (13.275; see [[elis]] and [[the-first-cretan-tale]]). Ἐπειοί stands in the same place when the verse returns, of Telemachus' voyage home and of the suitors' kin, who fear that Odysseus may escape to Pylos or to Elis (15.298, 24.431). Strabo, setting out the old divisions of the land that the Eleans later held, takes the verse for Homer's name for the Epeians' share, Elis, beside Nestor's Pylos (*Geography* 8.3.1; see [[strabo]] and [[pylos]]).
