---
summary: The maxim ends Alcinous' speech after the tale (13.4–15): the stranger will not be beaten back on his way home, and each of the lords is to add a great tripod and a cauldron to his gifts, to be repaid from the people. The next morning the bronze is brought to the ship (13.18–22).
---

Alcinous answers the end of the tale with a promise and a proposal. The promise is in the words of Achilles' first speech in the Iliad, turned to a new use: “so I think that you will not be beaten back / and sail for home again, even though you have suffered very much” (13.5–6; D. B. Monro, *Homer's Odyssey, Books XIII–XXIV*, 1901, on 13.5–6, comparing *Iliad* 1.59–60; see the translation's note on 13.5–6). The proposal adds to the clothes and gold already in the chest a great tripod and a cauldron from each man, the cost to be gathered among the people, “for it is hard for one man to give as a free gift” (13.10–15; see [[gold-and-silver]] and [[guest-gifts]]). The lords approve and go to bed, and at dawn they hurry to the ship with “the bronze that makes men strong”, which the king stows under the thwarts himself (13.16–22; see [[alcinous]]). Odysseus counts the tripods and cauldrons on the shore of Ithaca, and Athena hides them in the cave of the nymphs (13.217–218, 13.363–371; see [[tripod]], [[cauldron]] and [[the-cave-of-the-naiads]]).
