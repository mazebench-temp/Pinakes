---
summary: The ship reaches Ithaca at dawn, “then the seafaring ship drew near to the island” (13.95); and on its way back to Scheria, “the seafaring ship came very near, / coursing swiftly along” (13.161–162), before Poseidon turns it to stone.
---

ποντοπόρος νηῦς at the verse-end, the nominative of 12.69. The epithet marks the end of the voyage out and the ship's last moment as a ship: Zeus has just advised turning it to stone “like a swift ship” (13.157; see [[swift-ships]] and [[the-ship-turned-to-stone]]). D. B. Monro takes διωκομένη as “coursing along,” as in the phrase ἅρμα διώκειν (*Homer's Odyssey, Books XIII–XXIV*, 1901, on 13.162; see the translation's note on 13.162).
