---
summary: “And they spoke winged words to one another, / the Phaeacians of the long oars, men famed for ships” (13.165–166), when Poseidon has turned their ship to stone.
---

The whole verse of the discus and of the song (8.191 = 8.369; see the Book 8 note) is said for the third and last time, of men who watch their own ship rooted in the sea as it comes home (13.161–170; see [[famed-for-ships]], [[phaeacians]] and [[the-migration-of-the-phaeacians]]). It had run so hard under their oars that it beached half its length on Ithaca (13.113–115).
