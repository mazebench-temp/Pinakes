summary: War remembered and war to come: the sleeper on the Phaeacian ship has passed “through the wars of men and the grievous waves” (13.91), as the false Cretan claims to have done (13.264); Odysseus recalls the days “while we sons of the Achaeans were fighting in Troy” (13.315); Athena would not fight with Poseidon (13.341); and Odysseus would fight three hundred men with her beside him (13.390).
# πολεμίζομεν (13.315; again 14.240, 15.153), first met here among the Odyssey's notes.
grc: [πολεμίζομεν]
en: [were fighting]
---

**The wars of men.** The narrator sums up the sleeping Odysseus as a man “who before had suffered very many sorrows in his heart, / passing through the wars of men and the grievous waves” (ἀνδρῶν τε πτολέμους ἀλεγεινά τε κύματα πείρων, 13.90–91; see [[algos]]); πτολέμους carries on the construction of πολλὰ πάθʼ ἄλγεα, while πείρων belongs only to the waves (D. B. Monro, *Homer's Odyssey, Books XIII–XXIV*, 1901, on 13.91). The false Cretan claims the same verse for his spoil from Troy (13.263–264; see [[the-first-cretan-tale]]).

**Troy.** Odysseus reminds Athena that she was kind to him “while we sons of the Achaeans were fighting in Troy” (πολεμίζομεν, 13.315; see [[athena]] and [[sons-of-the-achaeans]]), but that once “we had sacked the steep city of Priam” he never saw her again (13.316–318; see [[the-fall-of-troy]]). The half-verse returns twice in the later books, in the [[the-tale-to-eumaeus|false tale to Eumaeus]] and in Menelaus' farewell to Telemachus and the son of Nestor (14.240, 15.153).

**Fighting gods and men.** Athena explains her absence: “but I did not wish to fight with Poseidon, / my father's brother” (μάχεσθαι, 13.341–342; see [[poseidon]] and [[kasignetos]]); in Scheria, too, she had not yet shown herself, out of respect for him (6.329–331). Odysseus, told of the suitors, asks her to stand beside him as at the sack of Troy: “I would fight even with three hundred men / with you, lady goddess, if you would help me with a ready heart” (13.390–391; see [[menos]] and [[the-suitors]]).
