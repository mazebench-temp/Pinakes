summary: The Phaeacians stow the food and drink “in the hollow ship” (13.71), spread his bedding “on the deck of the hollow ship” (13.74) and lift him “out of the hollow ship” (13.117); Odysseus counts his goods, “to see whether they have gone off with anything in the hollow ship” (13.216); and in the false tale the Phoenicians “took my goods out of the hollow ship” (13.283).
grc: ["ἰκριόφιν γλαφυρῆς"]
---

The landing is told twice, once by the poet and once in the lie: the Phaeacians set the sleeper and his goods on the sand (13.116–124), and in the tale the Phoenicians set down the goods “where I myself lay on the sands” (13.283–284; see [[phoenicians]], [[possessions]] and [[the-first-cretan-tale]]). The ship is γλαφυρή four times and κοίλη once; νηὸς ἐπʼ ἰκριόφιν γλαφυρῆς, with the deck between the noun and its epithet, is new to the entry (see the Odyssey translation's key words, ἴκρια, and [[deck]]).
