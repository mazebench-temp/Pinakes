---
summary: Athena's mist makes everything look strange to Odysseus: “the long paths and the harbors fit for mooring, / and the lofty rocks and the flourishing trees” (13.195–196).
---

πέτραι ἠλίβατοι are the rocks that, Patroclus says, must have borne Achilles, so harsh is his mind (*Iliad* 16.34–35; see the Odyssey translation's key words, πέτραι ἠλίβατοι); here they are the rocks of home made strange (13.189–196; see [[ithaca]]). The plural is new to the entry in the Odyssey. In the tale the lofty rock was the Cyclops' door-stone and the cliff of the Laestrygonians' harbor (9.243, 10.88; see the Book 9 and Book 10 notes).
