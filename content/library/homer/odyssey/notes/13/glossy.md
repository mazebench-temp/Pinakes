---
summary: The Phaeacians lift Odysseus out of the ship asleep, “linen sheet and glossy rug and all” (13.118).
---

ῥήγεϊ σιγαλόεντι, the rug they had spread for him on the deck with a linen sheet, “so that he might sleep unwaking” (13.73–74; see the Odyssey translation's key words, ῥῆγος). Monro renders αὐτῷ σύν τε λίνῳ “with the linen cloth as it was” (D. B. Monro, *Homer's Odyssey, Books XIII–XXIV*, 1901, on 13.118): they set him down on the sand in his bedding, overcome by sleep (13.117–119; see [[blankets]] and [[odysseus-set-ashore-on-ithaca]]). Elsewhere the Odyssey's glossy rugs are in the plural, ῥήγεα σιγαλόεντα (6.38, 11.189).
