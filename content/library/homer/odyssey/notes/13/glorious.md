---
summary: The last feast in Alcinous' house: “and when they had burned the thigh-pieces they feasted on a glorious feast” (13.26).
---

δαίνυντʼ ἐρικυδέα δαῖτα is a verse-end of the feast at Pylos (3.66) and of Hector's funeral feast at the end of the Iliad (*Iliad* 24.802); on Aeaea the companions “made ready a glorious feast” of the stag (10.182). Here it follows the sacrifice of an ox to Zeus and comes before the song of Demodocus (13.24–28; see [[feast]], [[lord-of-dark-clouds]] and [[divine]]).
