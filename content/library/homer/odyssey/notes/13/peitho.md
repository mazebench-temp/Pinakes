---
summary: Alcinous ends his speech on the prophecy with the Iliad's verse of decision, “Come then, let us all do as I say” (13.179), and Athena shows Odysseus the harbor, the cave and the mountain “so that you may be certain” (13.344).
---

**Obeying.** When the ship has been turned to stone, Alcinous tells the Phaeacians what his father foretold and what they must now do: “Come then, let us all do as I say: / stop escorting mortals, whenever anyone comes / to our city; and to Poseidon twelve picked bulls / let us sacrifice” (πειθώμεθα πάντες, 13.179–182; see [[alcinous]], [[pompe]] and [[hecatomb]]). The verse is the one with which leaders in the Iliad turn to their proposal (*Iliad* 2.139, 12.75, 14.370, 18.297), and Odysseus used it in the strait (12.213). The Phaeacians obey at once, in fear (13.184; see [[deos]]).

**Trusting.** Odysseus will not believe that he is in Ithaca, and Athena gives him proof: “But come, I will show you the seat of Ithaca, so that you may be certain” (ὄφρα πεποίθῃς, 13.344; see [[ithaca]]), and she points out the harbor of Phorcys, the olive, the cave and Mount Neritum (13.345–351; see [[the-harbor-of-phorcys]], [[the-cave-of-the-naiads]] and [[neritum]]). The perfect πέποιθα, “trust”, is won by the sight of the places he knew, and when the mist is scattered he kisses the earth (13.352–354; see [[nothing-sweeter-than-ones-own-land]]).
