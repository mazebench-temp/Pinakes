---
summary: The cave at the head of the harbor is “sacred to the nymphs who are called Naiads” (13.104 = 13.348); Odysseus and Athena sit “beside the trunk of the sacred olive” to plan the suitors' death (13.372); and twice “the sacred might of Alcinous” at the departure (13.20, 13.24).
---

ἱρὸν νυμφάων names the goddesses to whom the cave belongs, as the grove in Scheria was “sacred to Athena” (6.321–322; see the Book 6 note): Odysseus used to offer the nymphs hecatombs there (13.349–350; see [[the-cave-of-the-naiads]], [[nymphs]] and [[hecatomb]]). The olive by the cave, “long-leaved” when the harbor is described (13.102, 13.346; see [[the-harbor-of-phorcys]]), is ἱερή when the two sit under it to plan (13.372–373; see [[olive]]). Both phrases are new to the entry. ἱερὸν μένος Ἀλκινόοιο is a formula of its own (see [[sacred-might-of-alcinous]]).
