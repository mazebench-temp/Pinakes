---
summary: Athena answers the false tale: “Hard man, of subtle wiles, insatiable of tricks, you were not, then, / even in your own land, to leave off your deceits / and thievish words, which are dear to you from the ground up” (13.293–295).
---

ποικιλομῆτα, the only vocative of the epithet in Homer, is new to the entry. It stands between σχέτλιε, “hard,” which D. B. Monro takes as said here “in a friendly and admiring tone,” comparing *Iliad* 22.41 and 22.86 (*Homer's Odyssey, Books XIII–XXIV*, 1901, on 13.293), and δόλων ἆτʼ, the words of Socus' taunt (*Iliad* 11.430; see [[hard]] and [[insatiable-of-war]]). The goddess who says it claims the same gift for herself: “and I among all the gods / am famed for cunning and crafty ways” (13.298–299; see [[metis]], [[dolos]] and [[kerdos]]). For πεδόθεν, “from the ground up,” see the translation's note on 13.295.
