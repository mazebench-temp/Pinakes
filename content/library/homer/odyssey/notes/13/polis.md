summary: The city of the Phaeacians under threat: Poseidon wishes “to cover their city round about with a great mountain” (13.152), Zeus repeats the words (13.158), Alcinous recognizes them in his father's prophecy (13.177) and prays that the god may not do it (13.183); and on Ithaca Odysseus remembers “the steep city of Priam” (13.316).
# πόλει (13.152, 13.158, 13.177, 13.183; again 6.9, 8.569), first met here among the Odyssey's notes.
grc: [πόλει]
---

**The mountain over the city.** Four times in the book the same words threaten the Phaeacians' city: “and to cover their city round about with a great mountain” (μέγα δέ σφιν ὄρος πόλει ἀμφικαλύψαι). Poseidon says them, wishing to shatter the ship as it comes back from its escort (13.149–152; see [[poseidon]] and [[pompe]]); Zeus repeats them in his answer, after advising him to turn the ship to stone in sight of the people “from the city” (13.155–158; see [[laos]]); Alcinous recognizes them in the prophecy of his father, that Poseidon “would cover our city round about with a great mountain” (13.175–177; 8.564–569; see [[nausithous]] and [[thesphaton]]); and he prays that the god may not “cover our city round about with a towering mountain” (13.183; see [[the-migration-of-the-phaeacians]]). ἀμφικαλύψαι is rather far from the verb that governs it (ἐθέλω, 13.149), and the scholia seem to know an ancient variant, probably the future indicative ἀμφικαλύψω (D. B. Monro, *Homer's Odyssey, Books XIII–XXIV*, 1901, on 13.152). At 13.158 Aristophanes read μηδέ for μέγα δέ, so that Zeus forbids the mountain, no doubt because of the Phaeacians' prayers, though the prayers need not affect what Zeus says now (Monro on 13.158; see the Odyssey translation's note on 13.158). The poem does not say whether the mountain fell; the Phaeacians are last seen praying for their city (13.185–187). Alcinous' ἄστυ, “our city”, is the same place (13.181).

**Troy and Scheria.** On Ithaca Odysseus remembers Athena's kindness before Troy and her absence “when we had sacked the steep city of Priam / and gone aboard the ships, and a god scattered the Achaeans” (13.316–317 = 3.130–131; see [[the-fall-of-troy]] and [[steep]]), and, in verses that are generally condemned, her help in Scheria, when she “led me into the city yourself” (13.323; Monro on 13.320–323; see [[odysseus-at-the-house-of-alcinous]]).
