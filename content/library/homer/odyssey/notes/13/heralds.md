---
summary: For the libation before the escort Alcinous bids his herald Pontonous mix a bowl and serve the wine to all (13.49–54), and he sends a herald to lead Odysseus down to the swift ship and the shore of the sea (13.64–65).
---

**Pontonous.** “And then the might of Alcinous spoke to the herald: / ‘Pontonous, mix a bowl and serve out wine / to all throughout the hall, so that when we have prayed to father Zeus / we may send the stranger on his way to his native land’” (13.49–52; see [[pontonous]], [[mixing-bowl]] and [[libation]]). The king gave the same order, in the same first verses, on the night the stranger came, so that they might pour libations to Zeus “who attends on revered suppliants” (7.178–181); the wine that received the suppliant now sends him home. “So he spoke, and Pontonous mixed the wine sweet to the heart, / and served it out to all, going up to each in turn” (13.53–54; the first verse is 7.182, and the second ends otherwise than 7.183). D. B. Monro compares ἐπισταδόν with 12.392 and 18.425 (*Homer's Odyssey, Books XIII–XXIV*, 1901, on 13.54); the second is the same verse, of the herald Mulius serving the suitors' last libation of an evening in Odysseus' hall (18.423–426, a plain rendering: “And for them the hero Mulius mixed a bowl, / a herald from Dulichium”).

**The herald to the ship.** “So saying, brilliant Odysseus stepped over the threshold, / and with him the might of Alcinous sent a herald / to lead him to the swift ship and the shore of the sea” (13.63–65), as a herald led the blind Demodocus in and out of the hall in Book 8 (8.62, 8.105–107, 8.471–473; see the Book 8 note). This herald is not named. Arete sends slave women with him to carry the mantle and tunic, the chest and the bread and wine (13.66–69; see [[arete-queen-of-the-phaeacians]] and [[handmaids]]).
