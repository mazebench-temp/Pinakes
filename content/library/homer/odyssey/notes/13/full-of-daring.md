---
summary: Odysseus to Athena: “and stand beside me yourself, putting in me a might full of daring, / as when we loosed the shining veil of Troy” (13.387–388).
---

μένος πολυθαρσές, which Thetis put into Achilles before he called the Achaeans to assembly (*Iliad* 19.37), is asked of the goddess for the fight against the suitors, with the memory of the sack of Troy. κρήδεμνα is “the diadem of towers,” from *Iliad* 16.100, a refinement on calling the battlements of a town its head (D. B. Monro, *Homer's Odyssey, Books XIII–XXIV*, 1901, on 13.388; see the translation's note on 13.388, [[veil]] and [[the-fall-of-troy]]). With her beside him he would fight three hundred men (13.389–391; see [[menos]] and [[athena]]).
