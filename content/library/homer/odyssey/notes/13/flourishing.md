---
summary: Athena: the swine feed by the spring, “eating acorns to satisfy their hearts and the dark water / drinking, which nourish the rich lard of swine” (13.409–410).
grc: ["τεθαλυῖαν ἀλοιφήν"]
---

τεθαλυῖα ἀλοιφή has the participle of θάλλω, the verb from which θαλερός is formed, as the chine “rich with lard” at Achilles' supper for the envoys (*Iliad* 9.208), and the Odyssey's θαλερὴ ἀλοιφή is the fat of the chine that Odysseus gave Demodocus (8.476; see the Book 8 note). The swine are Eumaeus' herd, to which Athena sends Odysseus first (13.404–411; see [[eumaeus]] and [[boar]]). The translation's key words set the phrase beside τεθαλυῖα of the dew of Ithaca, “the rich dew” (13.245).
