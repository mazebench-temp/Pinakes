summary: The poet of the sleeping man: “who before had suffered very many sorrows in his heart, / passing through the wars of men and the grievous waves” (13.90–91); and Odysseus in the false tale, of his spoil from Troy, “for which I suffered sorrows in my heart, / passing through the wars of men and the grievous waves” (13.263–264).
---

ἀνδρῶν τε πτολέμους ἀλεγεινά τε κύματα πείρων is Achilles' verse for all he endured with Patroclus (*Iliad* 24.8) and Odysseus' at the games (8.183; see the Book 8 note). The Odyssey says it twice in this book, first of the man asleep, “forgetful of all he had suffered” (13.92), and then in his own mouth, in a lie (see [[the-first-cretan-tale]] and [[algos]]). Monro takes πτολέμους and κύματα as carrying on the construction of πολλὰ πάθʼ ἄλγεα, with πείρων, “passing through,” belonging only to κύματα, so that it is not properly a zeugma (D. B. Monro, *Homer's Odyssey, Books XIII–XXIV*, 1901, on 13.91; see the translation's note on 13.91).
