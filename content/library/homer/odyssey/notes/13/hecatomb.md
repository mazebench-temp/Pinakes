---
summary: Showing Odysseus his own island, Athena points to the cave of the Naiads, “where you used to offer / many perfect hecatombs to the nymphs” (13.349–350); on Scheria the Phaeacians, to whom the gods used to come at their hecatombs, make twelve picked bulls ready for Poseidon in fear (13.180–187).
---

**Hecatombs to the nymphs.** The proof that the land is Ithaca is a place of worship that Odysseus knows: “and this is the vaulted cave where you used to offer / many perfect hecatombs to the nymphs” (13.349–350; see [[nymphs]] and [[the-cave-of-the-naiads]]). τεληέσσας ἑκατόμβας, “perfect hecatombs”, is a formula of both poems (*Iliad* 1.315, 2.306; 4.352, 4.582), and ἔρδεσκες is an iterative, “you used to offer”: the hecatombs were the habit of his life before the war. In his prayer to the Naiads he promises them more, “and we will give you gifts as well, as before” (13.358; see [[prayer]]).

**Twelve bulls for Poseidon.** The Phaeacians were a people to whom the gods used to appear in plain sight “whenever we offer famous hecatombs”, and who feasted beside them (7.201–203). When Poseidon has turned their ship to stone, Alcinous proposes, “and to Poseidon twelve picked bulls / let us sacrifice, in the hope that he may have pity / and not cover our city round about with a towering mountain” (13.181–183; see [[the-ship-turned-to-stone]] and [[poseidon]]). The offering is not called a hecatomb, but it is the great offering of a people in danger, and its words are those of the Trojan women's vow to Athena of twelve yearling cows, “if she will pity / the city and the wives of the Trojans and their little children” (*Iliad* 6.93–95, 6.308–310; see [[pity]] and [[propitiation]]). Athena refused the Trojans: “Pallas Athena shook her head” (*Iliad* 6.311). The poem does not say whether Poseidon accepted the bulls. It leaves the Phaeacians at their prayers, “the leaders and rulers of the people of the Phaeacians, / standing around the altar” (13.186–187), and goes back to Odysseus waking in Ithaca.
