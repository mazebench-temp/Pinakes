---
summary: In the cave of the Naiads the nymphs weave “sea-purple webs, a wonder to see” on their looms of stone (13.107–108), in the color of the yarn that the queen of the Phaeacians spins.
---

“And in it are towering looms of stone, where the nymphs / weave sea-purple webs, a wonder to see” (13.107–108; see [[loom]], [[cloak]], [[weaving]], [[nymphs]] and [[the-cave-of-the-naiads]]). ἁλιπόρφυρα is found in Homer only here and of the yarn that Arete spins at the hearth (6.53, 6.306; see the Book 6 note): the work of the goddesses in the cave on Ithaca has the color of the queen's work on Scheria, which Odysseus has just left. W. W. Merry reports that a scholium on this verse and Eustathius took the word for purple like the sea when it shows purple, against the common interpretation, “dyed with the purple of the sea” (*Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 6.53).
