summary: The cave of the Naiads, furnished in three clauses: “In it are mixing bowls and two-handled jars / of stone … And in it are towering looms of stone … and in it are ever-flowing waters” (13.105–109); and Odysseus' plea to the young herdsman, “but save these things, and save me,” and his question, “what land is this, what people, what men are born here?” (13.230, 13.233).
refs: [13.105-109, 13.230, 13.233]

ἐν δέ … ἐν δʼ … ἐν δʼ, the anaphora of a description, as the verses of the landing at Chryse begin with ἐκ (*Iliad* 1.436–439; see [[seafaring-ship]]); the doors of the cave follow in a new sentence (13.109–112; see [[the-cave-of-the-naiads]]). σάω μὲν ταῦτα, σάω δʼ ἐμέ puts the goods first and the man second; D. B. Monro takes σάω as pointing to a contracted non-thematic form, unless it is a mistake for σάου (*Homer's Odyssey, Books XIII–XXIV*, 1901, on 13.230). τίς γῆ, τίς δῆμος, τίνες ἀνέρες asks three times for the one thing he does not know (see [[tell-me-this-truly]]).
