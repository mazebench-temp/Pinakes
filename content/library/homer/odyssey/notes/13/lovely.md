---
summary: “And at the head of the harbor is a long-leaved olive, / and near it a lovely cave, misty” (13.102–103); and Athena shows it to Odysseus in the same words (13.346–347).
---

ἄντρον ἐπήρατον ἠεροειδές gives the cave two epithets, the lovely and the dim (see [[the-cave-of-the-naiads]], [[misty]] and [[nymphs]]). ἐπήρατος is the Iliad's word for a citadel and its wealth (*Iliad* 18.512, 22.121), and Telemachus called Ithaca, a land for goats, “lovelier than a horse-pasturing one” (4.606; see the Book 8 note and [[ithaca]]). The neuter ἐπήρατον is new to the entry in the Odyssey.
