---
summary: Neritum is the last of the places Athena shows Odysseus before she scatters the mist: “and this is Mount Neritum, clothed in forest” (13.351).
---

Athena proves to Odysseus that he is home by pointing to the places one by one, the harbor of Phorcys, the olive, the cave of the Naiads, and last the mountain: τοῦτο δὲ Νήριτόν ἐστιν ὄρος καταειμένον ὕλῃ, “and this is Mount Neritum, clothed in forest” (13.344–351; see [[ithaca]], [[the-harbor-of-phorcys]] and [[the-cave-of-the-naiads]]). Then she scatters the mist, “and the land came into view” (13.352). καταειμένον ὕλῃ, “clothed in forest”, is said once more in the poem, of the steep mountain, Parnassus, on which the young Odysseus was wounded by the boar (19.431, a plain rendering; see [[the-boar-hunt-on-parnassus]]), and Athena has just praised Ithaca for “forest / of every kind” (13.246–247). In Book 9 Odysseus gave the mountain the Iliad's epithet, “Neritum with its quivering leaves, conspicuous” (9.22; see [[with-quivering-leaves]] and the Book 9 note). W. W. Merry, gathering the poem's picture of the island, sets the two descriptions together: “the striking mass (ἀριπρεπές 9.22) of Mt. Neriton, clothed in forest (καταειμένον ὕλῃ 13.351; εἰνοσίφυλλον 9.22)” (*Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, Appendix on Ithaca, p. 556).
