---
summary: With the beggar's rags Athena gives Odysseus a staff and a shabby pouch (13.437): the σκῆπτρον of kings and heralds is here a beggar's stick.
---

“and she gave him a staff and a shabby pouch, / rent in many places; and on it was a twisted cord to sling it by” (13.437–438; see [[pouch]], [[athena]], [[odysseus-changed-into-a-beggar]] and [[clothing]]). The word is σκῆπτρον, the scepter that a king holds and a herald puts into the hand of a speaker (see the entry); the translation renders it “staff” when it is a beggar's (see its row σκῆπτρον, of a beggar; πήρη; στρόφος ἀορτήρ). It belongs with σκήπτομαι, “lean on”, and the beggar leans on it: when the swineherd's dogs rush at him he sits down, by craft, “and the staff fell from his hand” (14.31), and Eumaeus later gives him “a staff to his heart's liking” for the road to the city, where he walks “leaning on a staff” (17.199, 17.203, a plain rendering; see [[eumaeus]]). In the Odyssey's first assembly the scepter was put into Telemachus' hand by the herald (2.37); in the house of Odysseus the beggar will put a staff into the hand of the beaten Irus, in the same words (18.103).
