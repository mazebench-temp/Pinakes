# Unnamed: the scattering of the Achaeans (13.317-319).
refs: [13.317-319]
---

**“A god scattered the Achaeans.”** Odysseus reminds Athena that she was kind to him at Troy, “But when we had sacked the steep city of Priam / and gone aboard the ships, and a god scattered the Achaeans, / after that I did not see you, daughter of Zeus, nor did I notice you / boarding my ship, to ward off some sorrow from me” (13.316–319; see [[athena]] and [[the-fall-of-troy]]). The first two verses are the ones in which Nestor began the returns for Telemachus (3.130–131), and the Cretan of the next book ends a verse with the same words, “and a god scattered the Achaeans”, when he tells Eumaeus how he left Troy (14.242, a plain rendering; see [[the-first-cretan-tale]]). In Nestor's account the god who scattered them is named in the verses that follow: Zeus devised a grim homecoming for the Argives, “since in no way discerning or just / were they all”, and many of them met an evil fate “through the deadly wrath of the gray-eyed daughter of the mighty father, / who set strife between the two sons of Atreus” (3.132–136). In the thirteenth book the goddess gives Odysseus another reason for her absence, that she would not fight with Poseidon, her father's brother (13.341–343; see [[poseidon]]); of her wrath against the Achaeans she says nothing. Nestor's words for their fault come back in this book in Odysseus' mouth, when he thinks the Phaeacians have cheated him: they were “not altogether discerning or just” (13.209; 3.133; see [[phaeacians]]).
