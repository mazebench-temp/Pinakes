---
summary: Athena explains why she did not stand by Odysseus on his wanderings: she would not fight Poseidon, “angry because you blinded his dear son” (13.343).
---

“But I never doubted it, but in my heart / I knew that you would come home, having lost all your companions; / but I did not wish to fight with Poseidon, / my father's brother, who has laid up a grudge against you in his heart, / angry because you blinded his dear son” (χωόμενος, 13.339–343; see [[kotos]], [[poseidon]], [[kasignetos]] and [[polyphemus-the-cyclops]]). The last verse and the half-verse before it are Teiresias' words in the land of the dead (11.102–103; see [[the-prophecy-of-teiresias]]): the anger he foretold is the reason the goddess gives for her absence. The manuscripts are divided at 13.342 between κότον, “grudge”, and χόλον, “anger” (D. B. Monro, *Homer's Odyssey, Books XIII–XXIV*, 1901, his critical note on 13.342), the two words that Calchas distinguishes in *Iliad* 1 (*Iliad* 1.80–83; see the entry). The text keeps κότον, the anger that is stored up until it is fulfilled, and gives its cause with χωόμενος: the blinding, and the Cyclops' prayer to his father that followed it (9.526–536; see [[curses]]).
