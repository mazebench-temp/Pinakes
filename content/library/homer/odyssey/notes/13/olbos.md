---
summary: Odysseus prays that the heavenly gods may “make them prosper for me”, the escort and the gifts of the Phaeacians (13.41–42).
---

At his farewell Odysseus names what he has received and asks the gods to bless it: “for now all is accomplished that my dear heart desired, / an escort and gifts of friendship; and may the heavenly gods / make them prosper for me” (τά μοι θεοὶ Οὐρανίωνες / ὄλβια ποιήσειαν, 13.40–42; see [[doron]], [[pompe]] and [[heavenly-gods]]). ὄλβος is the prosperity that the gods give and take away (see the entry), and the prayer asks that the gifts become it, that he may bring them home and keep them; the next verses name the rest of what he hopes for, his wife at home and his dear ones unhurt (13.42–43). The gifts are kept safe: Athena hides them in the cave of the nymphs (13.361–371; see [[possessions]] and [[the-cave-of-the-naiads]]). The escort that brought them costs the Phaeacians their ship (13.159–164; see [[the-migration-of-the-phaeacians]]).
