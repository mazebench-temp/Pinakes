**The nymphs' looms.** The cave at the head of the harbor has its furniture of stone: “In it are mixing bowls and two-handled jars / of stone; and there the bees store up honey. / And in it are towering looms of stone, where the nymphs / weave sea-purple webs, a wonder to see” (13.105–108; see [[the-cave-of-the-naiads]], [[the-harbor-of-phorcys]], [[nymphs]] and [[loom]]). φάρεα are “webs”, the cloth on the loom (D. B. Monro, *Homer's Odyssey, Books XIII–XXIV*, 1901, on 13.108, comparing 19.138). ἁλιπόρφυρα, “sea-purple”, is otherwise the color of Arete's yarn, which Nausicaa found her turning by the hearth, “a wonder to see” (6.53, 6.306; see [[arete-queen-of-the-phaeacians]] and [[purple]]). The goddesses of the Odyssey weave in their dwellings, Calypso “with a golden shuttle” (5.61–62) and Circe at “a great immortal loom” (10.222); the nymphs of Ithaca have looms of stone in a cave.

**Skilled in handiwork.** When Athena puts off the shepherd's likeness “she was like a woman in form, / beautiful and tall and skilled in splendid handiwork” (13.288–289; see [[shape-changing]] and [[athena]]), the verses of her coming to the swineherd's hut (16.157–158). Skill at the loom is the mark of a woman of rank and the gift of Athena herself: the Phaeacian women are skilled at the loom, “for Athena has given them above all others / to be skilled in beautiful handiwork, and good wits” (7.110–111), and Achilles would not marry Agamemnon's daughter even if she “equaled gray-eyed Athena in handiwork” (*Iliad* 9.390).

**The woven gifts.** Among the gifts that Poseidon begrudges are “bronze and gold in plenty, and woven clothing” (13.136), and on waking Odysseus counts “the gold and the fine woven clothes” (13.218; see [[clothing]] and [[possessions]]). The book also uses the craft as a figure: Athena has come “to weave a plan” with him, and he asks her, “But come, weave a plan” (13.303, 13.386; see [[metis]]).
