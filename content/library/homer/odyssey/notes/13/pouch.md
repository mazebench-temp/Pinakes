When Athena has touched Odysseus with her wand, withered his skin, destroyed his hair, dimmed his eyes and put a [[rags|rag]] and a tunic on him, she finishes the beggar:

{{quote:d0a582f3-f9a2-5fb9-b21c-eafa14ffd72a}}

The pouch comes last, after the deerskin and with the staff (see [[deer]], [[cloak]], [[scepter]], [[clothing]] and [[odysseus-changed-into-a-beggar]]). ψιλόν, “worn bare”, is said of the deerskin, “the wool worn off” (D. B. Monro, *Homer's Odyssey, Books XIII–XXIV*, 1901, on 13.437); the pouch is ἀεικής, “shabby”, and πυκνὰ ῥωγαλέη, “rent in many places”, as the [[rags|rag]] and the tunic are ῥωγαλέα, “rent” (13.435; see the translation's rows σκῆπτρον, of a beggar; πήρη; στρόφος ἀορτήρ and ῥωγαλέος; ῥυπόων). With this outfit he is to go to the swineherd (13.404–411; see [[eumaeus]]), and the two verses of the pouch return, with βάλλετο, “slung”, when he sets out for the city and when he has beaten the beggar Irus in his own hall (17.197–198, 18.108–109; see the entry).
