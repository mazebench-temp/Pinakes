---
summary: In the false tale, Idomeneus' son Orsilochus is a runner who “outran toiling men with his swift feet” in Crete (13.261).
---

The Cretan of Odysseus' lie killed “the dear son of Idomeneus, / swift-footed Orsilochus, who in wide Crete / outran toiling men with his swift feet” (ἀνέρας ἀλφηστὰς νίκα ταχέεσσι πόδεσσιν, 13.259–261; see [[orsilochus-son-of-idomeneus]], [[swift-footed]], [[toiling-men]] and [[the-first-cretan-tale]]). νικάω is to surpass in any contest (see the entry), here in running, and the imperfect νίκα, “outran”, is a habit, the reputation of the fastest man in Crete. The speed is told only to be made useless: the Cretan lay in ambush by the road at night and struck him with a spear as he came down from the country (13.267–270; see [[ambush]]).
