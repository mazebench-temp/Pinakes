---
summary: Odysseus' farewell to the Phaeacians: “and may I find my noble wife at home / when I return, and my dear ones unhurt” (13.42–43).
---

ἀμύμονα … ἄκοιτιν: of persons ἀμύμων stays “noble” in the Odyssey's English (see the Odyssey translation's key words, ἀμύμων). The word is said of Penelope once more, when the soul of Agamemnon praises her among the dead: “So good was the mind of noble Penelope” (24.194, a plain rendering; see [[penelope]]). Athena will tell him how his wife has waited, in tears, for his return (13.336–338, 13.379–381).
