---
summary: Telemachus “went to Lacedaemon with its wide dancing floors, to Menelaus”, to learn news of his father (13.414–415), and the book ends as Athena “went to shining Lacedaemon, after the son of Odysseus” (13.440).
---

**With its wide dancing floors.** Athena tells Odysseus that his son “went to Lacedaemon with its wide dancing floors, to Menelaus, / to learn the report of you, whether you were still alive anywhere” (13.414–415; see [[telemachus]], [[menelaus]] and [[to-learn-of-his-fathers-homecoming]]). εὐρύχορον, “with its wide dancing floors”, is said of Lacedaemon only here and at the opening of Book 15, where Athena reaches it (15.1); Noemon gave the same epithet to Elis (4.635; see [[with-wide-dancing-floors]] and [[elis]]). For πάρ, “to”, the vulgate reads πρός, and some manuscripts γάρ (D. B. Monro, *Homer's Odyssey, Books XIII–XXIV*, 1901, critical note on 13.414). Two verses before, Athena named the same goal by the city, “Sparta of the lovely women” (13.412; see [[sparta]]).

**Shining Lacedaemon.** “When the two had made their plans, they parted. She then / went to shining Lacedaemon, after the son of Odysseus” (13.439–440; see [[athena]]). Λακεδαίμονα δῖαν, “shining Lacedaemon”, is the land's phrase in the Odyssey, from Nestor's advice to Telemachus to Telemachus' own account of his journey (3.326, 4.313, 4.702, 5.20; 17.121; see [[brilliant]]). The book ends in the middle of a sentence, ἡ μὲν … “she went to Sparta, while he &c.” (Monro on 13.440): the next book takes it up with Odysseus, “But he went up from the harbor by the rugged path” (14.1; see [[the-harbor-of-phorcys]]), and Athena's arrival in Lacedaemon waits for the beginning of Book 15.
