---
summary: Athena, when Odysseus has lied to her: “Hard man, of subtle wiles, insatiable of tricks, you were not, then, / even in your own land, to leave off your deceits” (13.293–294).
---

Monro hears σχέτλιε “said in a friendly and admiring tone” (D. B. Monro, *Homer's Odyssey, Books XIII–XXIV*, 1901, on 13.293), as Diomedes says it to Nestor, who never ceases from toil (*Iliad* 10.164; see the general article). The goddess's reproach is half praise, and she says so at once: they both know crafty ways, he the best of mortals in counsel and words, she famed among the gods for cunning (13.296–299; see [[of-subtle-wiles]], [[insatiable-of-war]], [[kerdos]] and [[metis]]). Circe had called him hard for wanting to fight Scylla, and Eurylochus for his endurance (12.116, 12.279; see the Book 12 note).
