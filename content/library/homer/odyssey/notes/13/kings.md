---
summary: Waking in a land he does not know, Odysseus wishes that the Phaeacians had kept his goods, and that he had come instead “to some other of the mighty kings, / who would have welcomed me and sent me on my way home” (13.204–206).
---

“Would that they had stayed there among the Phaeacians / where they were; and I would have come to some other of the mighty kings, / who would have welcomed me and sent me on my way home” (13.204–206; see [[phaeacians]] and [[xenia]]). ὄφελον has the goods, χρήματα, for its subject: it is they that should have stayed (D. B. Monro, *Homer's Odyssey, Books XIII–XXIV*, 1901, on 13.204). For κεν in the next verse some read τινʼ, which Monro thinks perhaps defensible, the aorist indicative carrying on the unfulfilled wish (on 13.205). ὑπερμενέων βασιλήων, “of the mighty kings”, is the phrase of Agamemnon's reproach to Zeus in the Iliad, “Father Zeus, have you ever before struck any of the mighty kings / with such blind folly” (*Iliad* 8.236–237), and it returns when the cowherd says that he would long ago have fled to another of the mighty kings (20.222–223). Odysseus has just left the house of one, “King Alcinous” (13.62; see [[alcinous]] and [[basileus]]).
