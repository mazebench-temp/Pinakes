---
summary: Poseidon wishes to shatter “the beautiful ship of the Phaeacians” (13.149), as Alcinous' father foretold (13.175); Odysseus counts “the beautiful tripods and cauldrons” (13.217); and Athena dims his eyes, “that were so beautiful before” (13.401, 13.433).
---

περικαλλέα νῆα in Poseidon's request and in the prophecy as Alcinous now recalls it (13.149, 13.175), where he had first told it with ἐυεργέα νῆα, “a well-made ship” (8.567), and three manuscripts read ἐυεργέα here too (D. B. Monro, *Homer's Odyssey, Books XIII–XXIV*, 1901, critical note on 13.175; see [[well-made]] and [[the-migration-of-the-phaeacians]]); the text prints a comma between the adjective and its noun at 13.175 (see the translation's note on 13.175). περικαλλέας, of the tripods, is new in the Odyssey: they are the gifts that Alcinous asked of each of the lords (13.13; see [[tripod]] and [[possessions]]). The eyes are the last of the beauty that the goddess takes away for the beggar's disguise, in her promise and in the deed (13.401, 13.433; see [[shape-changing]]); the general article tells the opposite case, a goddess known by the beauty that her disguise could not hide (*Iliad* 3.396–398).
