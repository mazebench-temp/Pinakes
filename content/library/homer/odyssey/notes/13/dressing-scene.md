---
summary: Athena comes as a young herdsman, “with a well-made cape folded double around her shoulders; / and under her shining feet she had sandals, and in her hands a javelin” (13.224–225); and she dresses Odysseus as a beggar, “a vile rag and a tunic,” “the great skin of a swift deer, / worn bare,” and last “a staff and a shabby pouch” (13.434–438).
refs: [13.224-225, 13.434-438]
---

The herdsman is described in the items of the scene, cloak, sandals and spear, with the opening of the sandal verse (13.224–225; see [[sleek]] and [[well-made]]). The beggar's dress follows the same order, the garments first, a [[rags|rag]], a tunic and a deerskin, and last the staff with which a man goes out: σκῆπτρον, the word of the scepter that Agamemnon takes up to go to the assembly (*Iliad* 2.46), is here a beggar's staff, given with a pouch “rent in many places” (13.437–438; see the Odyssey translation's key words, and [[scepter]], [[swift]] and [[shape-changing]]). The verses of the pouch and its cord return when he sets out for the city and after the fight with Irus (17.197–198, 18.108–109).
