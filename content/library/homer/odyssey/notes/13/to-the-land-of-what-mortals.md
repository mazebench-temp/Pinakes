---
summary: Odysseus wakes in his own land and does not know it: “Ah me, to the land of what mortals have I come this time? / Are they arrogant and savage and unjust, / or kind to strangers, with a mind that fears the gods?” (13.200–202).
---

The three verses of his waking on Scheria (6.119–121), now on Ithaca, where Athena has poured a mist around him so that everything appears “in another shape” (13.189–196; see the translation's note on 13.194). D. B. Monro takes ἦ … ἦε as a double question, “are they savage or hospitable?”, like the question about the land at 13.234 (*Homer's Odyssey, Books XIII–XXIV*, 1901, on 13.201–202). This time the question goes on with the cares of a man with treasure, “Where am I taking all these goods?” (13.203), and with a curse on the Phaeacians, who, he thinks, have cheated him (13.209–214; see [[o-popoi]] and [[supreme-in-might]]). The answer comes from the goddess in the likeness of a young herdsman, in the Cyclops' verse, “You are a fool, stranger, or you have come from far away” (13.237 = 9.273), and then with the name, “the name of Ithaca has reached even to Troy” (13.248). On Scheria the answer was the girl's, among the Cyclopes the monster's; here it is the goddess's, and the land is his own (see [[odysseus-set-ashore-on-ithaca]], [[divine-epiphany]] and [[ithaca]]).
