---
summary: Telemachus has gone to Lacedaemon “to learn the report of you” (13.415), and Athena says that she sent him “so that he might win noble fame” (13.422): the two senses of the word, the son's search for his father's κλέος and his own.
---

Athena tells Odysseus that she will go to Sparta to call his son home, “who went to Lacedaemon with its wide dancing floors, to Menelaus, / to learn the report of you, whether you were still alive anywhere” (πευσόμενος μετὰ σὸν κλέος, 13.414–415; see [[telemachus]], [[the-journey-of-telemachus]] and [[to-learn-of-his-fathers-homecoming]]); Monro renders μετὰ σὸν κλέος “after thy story” (D. B. Monro, *Homer's Odyssey, Books XIII–XXIV*, 1901, on 13.415). Odysseus asks why she did not tell the boy what she knew, so that he too must wander and suffer (13.417–419), and she answers with the other sense of the word: “I myself escorted him, so that he might win noble fame / by going there” (ἵνα κλέος ἐσθλὸν ἄροιτο, 13.422–423; see [[pompe]]). It was the reason she gave the gods when she planned the journey, “and so that noble fame may be his among men” (1.95), and the narrator gave it again when the young man first spoke to Nestor (3.76–78). The report of the father and the fame of the son are one word, and the journey in search of the first wins the second.
