---
summary: Athena tells the stranger of the land: “Truly it is rugged and not good for driving horses, / but it is not very poor, though it is not broad” (13.242–243).
grc: [τρηχεῖα]
---

τρηχεῖα, the nominative, is new to the entry. Before she names Ithaca, the goddess describes it in the word that Odysseus used of it to Alcinous, “rugged, but a good nurse of young men” (9.27), and with the reason Telemachus gave Menelaus for refusing his horses: “none of the islands is good for driving horses” (4.607; see the Odyssey translation's key words). The praise that follows is told by denials (13.243; see [[litotes]] and [[ithaca]]).
