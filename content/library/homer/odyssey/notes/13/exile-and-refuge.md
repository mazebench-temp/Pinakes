summary: In his false tale Odysseus makes himself a Cretan exile: he killed the son of Idomeneus, who would have robbed him of his spoil, and fled with his goods, begging Phoenician sailors to take him on board (13.256–286). In Book 15 the fugitive Theoclymenus will do in fact what the Cretan only claims.
# Unnamed: the exile's tale (13.256-286).
refs: [13.256-286]
---

**A killer in flight.** The stranger on the shore of Ithaca explains himself as an exile: “and leaving as much again to my children, / I am an exile, since I killed the dear son of Idomeneus, / swift-footed Orsilochus” (13.258–260; see [[the-first-cretan-tale]], [[idomeneus]] and [[orsilochus-son-of-idomeneus]]). φεύγω is “be an exile”, the verb of the man who flees the kin of the dead (see the translation's key-word row on φεύγω), and ἔτι τοσαῦτα is “as much more” (D. B. Monro, *Homer's Odyssey, Books XIII–XXIV*, 1901, on 13.258): he has brought half his wealth and left half to his sons. The tale has the parts of the pattern. There is a killing, done from ambush by night when the king's son was coming home (13.267–270; see [[ambush]]); there is a powerful house to flee, for the dead man was Idomeneus' son; and there is a plea to strangers for refuge, as the Cretan “begged” the Phoenicians and paid them with spoil to take him on board and set him down at Pylos or Elis (13.272–275; see [[supplication]] and [[phoenicians]]).

**The tale come true.** ἐφέσσαι, “to put me on board”, Monro compares with Theoclymenus' plea in Book 15, νηὸς ἔφεσσαι (on 13.274). The seer, who has killed a kinsman in Argos and is fleeing the dead man's brothers and kinsmen, comes to Telemachus as he sacrifices beside his ship at Pylos and says, as the Cretan said, “I am an exile”, and “take me on board the ship, since I have come to you as a suppliant in my flight” (φεύγω, 15.272–278, a plain rendering; see [[the-killer-in-exile]]). Telemachus takes him. The exile that Odysseus invents to hide his name is the true story of the man who will sail to Ithaca with his son.
