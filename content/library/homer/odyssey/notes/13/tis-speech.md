---
summary: The Phaeacians see the ship turned to stone: “And this is what a man would say, looking at his neighbor: / ‘Ah me, who has fettered the swift ship on the sea / as she was driven home? And she was already in plain sight.’ / So a man would say; but they did not know how things had been brought about” (13.167–170).
---

The crowd's verdict is a question, and the closing verse, which in Book 4 follows the suitors' guess about Penelope's marriage (4.772), says that the speakers are wrong: they do not know what the god has done (see [[the-ship-turned-to-stone]] and [[swift-ships]]). The answer comes in the next speech, from Alcinous, who remembers his father's prophecy (13.171–183; see [[o-popoi]]).
