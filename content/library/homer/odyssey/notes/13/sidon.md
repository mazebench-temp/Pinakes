---
summary: At the end of Odysseus' false tale the Phoenicians, having set him and his goods ashore, “went aboard for well-peopled Sidon / and were gone” (13.285–286).
---

The tale ends with the Phoenicians going home: “And they went aboard for well-peopled Sidon / and were gone; but I was left behind, grieving at heart” (13.285–286; see [[phoenicians]] and [[the-first-cretan-tale]]). Σιδονίην is “the land of Sidon”, found only here in Homer; the Iliad has Σιδονίηθεν, “from Sidon” (*Iliad* 6.291), and Book 15 will have the city's own name, Σιδῶνος πολυχάλκου, “Sidon, rich in bronze” (15.425). εὖ ναιομένην, “well-peopled”, is the Iliad's word for cities and citadels, as “the well-peopled citadel of Ilios” (εὖ ναιόμενον πτολίεθρον, *Iliad* 2.133; see [[well-peopled]] and the translation's formula row for 13.285). Sidon is where the Phoenicians of the tale belong: Strabo notes that men disputed whether Sidon or Tyre should be called the mother city of the Phoenicians, and that Homer names only Sidon (*Geography* 16.2.22; see [[strabo]]).
