summary: The Phaeacian crew loose the cable from the pierced stone as the voyage begins (13.77); in the harbor of Phorcys, sheltered by its headlands, ships stay without a mooring rope (13.99–101).
# The mooring rope of ships, "ἄνευ δεσμοῖο" (13.100), as a phrase, since δεσμός is any bond (the-bonds-of-hephaestus
# excepts the line); first met here, it applies in the Odyssey only. πεῖσμα (13.77) is claimed by the Book 10 note.
grc: ["ἄνευ δεσμοῖο"]
en: [mooring rope]
---

**Loosed from the stone.** When Odysseus has lain down on the deck, the crew “sat down at the rowing benches, each man / in good order, and loosed the cable from the pierced stone” (13.76–77; see [[rowing-benches]] and [[ships]]). The πεῖσμα is the cable that made the ship fast to the shore, passed through a stone pierced for it (see the translation's row πεῖσμα … τρητοῖο λίθοιο); the verse is the Phaeacians' own way of casting off, where Odysseus' crews “loose the stern-cables” (9.178, 12.145; see the entry).

**Without a mooring rope.** The harbor of Phorcys is closed by two sheer headlands that keep off the great wave of the winds, “but within, without a mooring rope, there stay / the well-benched ships, once they have reached the measure of anchorage” (13.99–101; see [[the-harbor-of-phorcys]], [[harbor]] and [[well-benched]]). ὅρμου μέτρον is “the measure of anchorage”, the distance at which ships are fastened by a cable to the shore, if fastening is needed (D. B. Monro, *Homer's Odyssey, Books XIII–XXIV*, 1901, on 13.101; see the translation's row ὅρμου μέτρον). So in the harbor of the island off the land of the Cyclopes “there is no need of a cable, / neither to cast out mooring-stones nor to make fast stern-cables” (9.136–137; see [[mooring-stones]] and the Book 9 note).
