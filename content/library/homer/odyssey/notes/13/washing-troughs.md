---
summary: The mantle that a slave woman of Arete carries to the ship for Odysseus is “freshly washed”, ἐϋπλυνές, like the mantles of the Phaeacians' gifts (13.67).
---

“one carrying a freshly washed mantle and a tunic” (13.67; see [[cloak]], [[tunic]] and [[arete-queen-of-the-phaeacians]]). φᾶρος ἐϋπλυνὲς ἠδὲ χιτῶνα is the gift that each of the kings brought and that the queen added of her own (8.392, 8.425; see the Book 8 note), and the words return when Athena gives Odysseus back his looks in the swineherd's hut and puts “a freshly washed mantle and a tunic” about him (16.173, a plain rendering). On Scheria the clothes of the king's house were washed at the troughs by the river, where Nausicaa and her handmaids trod them (6.85–98; see the Book 6 note and [[clothing]]).
