summary: Athena pours a mist around Odysseus “so that she might make him / unknown, and tell him each thing” (13.190–191); and in the false tale, “I bade them take me on board and set me down at Pylos” (13.274), Πύλονδε καταστῆσαι καὶ ἐφέσσαι, “set me down at Pylos and take me on board” in the Greek order.
refs: [13.191, 13.274]
---

D. B. Monro finds a prothysteron at 13.191: the talk comes before the change of his looks, but the more definite act is put first; ἄγνωστον τεύξειεν refers to the same change as ἄγνωστον τεύξω at 13.397, which the goddess makes with her wand at the end of the book (*Homer's Odyssey, Books XIII–XXIV*, 1901, on 13.191; see the translation's note on 13.189–191, and [[shape-changing]]). At 13.274, as at 4.723 and 12.134, the English gives the natural order (see [[the-first-cretan-tale]]).
