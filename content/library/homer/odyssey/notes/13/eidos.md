# ἀλλοειδέα (13.194), only here in Homer.
grc: [ἀλλοειδέα]
en: [in another shape]
---

**In another shape.** Odysseus wakes in his own land and does not know it, for Athena has poured a mist around him, “so that she might make him / unknown, and tell him each thing” (13.189–191): “Therefore all things appeared in another shape to their lord, / the long paths and the harbors fit for mooring, / and the lofty rocks and the flourishing trees” (ἀλλοειδέα φαινέσκετο πάντα ἄνακτι, 13.194–196; see [[anax]] and [[athena]]). ἀλλοειδής, “of another look”, is a compound of εἶδος found only here in Homer. The manuscripts are divided between φαίνετο and φαινέσκετο: the second makes ἀλλοειδέα three long syllables, which Buttmann rejected, and its frequentative sense is out of place, so Monro prints ἀλλοειδέʼ ἐφαίνετο (D. B. Monro, *Homer's Odyssey, Books XIII–XXIV*, 1901, on 13.194; see the Odyssey translation's note on 13.194). The mist was not meant to make Odysseus invisible: Athena wished to prepare him by consultation and by changing his appearance, for otherwise he would have gone straight to his palace and all would have been lost (Monro on 13.191).

**Came into view.** When she has shown him the harbor, the olive, the cave and the mountain, “the goddess scattered the mist, and the land came into view” (εἴσατο δὲ χθών, 13.352; see [[the-harbor-of-phorcys]], [[the-cave-of-the-naiads]] and [[nothing-sweeter-than-ones-own-land]]): εἴσατο is the aorist of εἴδομαι, “appear”, said here of the land, where it is so often said of a god in a mortal's likeness. Athena's own likenesses in the book are told with δέμας, “form”: she comes “in form like a young man, a herdsman of sheep” (δέμας … εἰκυῖα, 13.222), and then is “like a woman in form, / beautiful and tall and skilled in splendid handiwork” (13.288–289; see [[shape-changing]]). Odysseus names the difficulty: “for you make yourself like anything” (σὲ γὰρ αὐτὴν παντὶ ἐΐσκεις, 13.313; see [[the-gods-are-not-unknown-to-one-another]]).
