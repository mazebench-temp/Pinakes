summary: The book's crafty ways. Odysseus answers the disguised Athena with a lie, “always turning a very crafty mind in his breast” (13.255); she answers that only a crafty man and a cheat could get past him (13.291), and that they both know “crafty ways”, he the best of mortals and she famed among the gods “for cunning and crafty ways” (13.296–299).
# πολυκερδέα (13.255) and κερδαλέος (13.291), each only here in Homer; κέρδεʼ (13.297; again 18.216) and κέρδεσιν (13.299),
# first met here among the Odyssey's notes.
grc: [πολυκερδέα, κερδαλέος, "κέρδεʼ", κέρδεσιν]
en: [crafty ways, crafty]

**A very crafty mind.** Glad to hear the name of Ithaca, Odysseus answers the young herdsman, “yet he did not speak the truth, but took back his word, / always turning a very crafty mind in his breast” (νόον πολυκερδέα νωμῶν, 13.254–255; see [[noos]], [[mythos]] and [[the-first-cretan-tale]]). πολυκερδής, “very cunning”, is found in Homer only here (D. B. Monro, *Homer's Odyssey, Books XIII–XXIV*, 1901, on 13.255); the noun πολυκερδείῃσιν, “in his great craftiness”, is given to the same man in the last book (24.167, a plain rendering).

**Crafty, and a cheat.** Athena smiles, strokes him with her hand, takes the likeness of a woman, and answers the lie: “Crafty would he be, and a cheat, who should get past you / in tricks of every kind, even if it were a god who met you” (κερδαλέος κʼ εἴη καὶ ἐπίκλοπος, 13.291–292; see [[dolos]] and [[athena]]). κερδαλέος, “crafty”, goes with πολυκερδής and with κέρδεα (Monro on 13.291); ἐπίκλοπος, “a cheat”, is what Alcinous had said the Phaeacians did not take him for (11.363–364; see [[eperopeuo]]).

**Both know crafty ways.** “But come, let us no longer talk like this, since we both know / crafty ways; for you are by far the best of all mortals / in counsel and in words, and I among all the gods / am famed for cunning and crafty ways” (εἰδότες ἄμφω / κέρδεʼ … μήτι τε κλέομαι καὶ κέρδεσιν, 13.296–299; see [[metis]] and [[aristos]]). To know κέρδεα is the Iliad's phrase for the craft that wins with lesser means: Nestor tells Antilochus that “he who knows crafty ways” can win with worse horses, and Odysseus, “who knew crafty ways”, rises to wrestle Ajax (*Iliad* 23.322, 23.709). The goddess claims the same knowledge for herself, and pairs it with μῆτις, the cunning for which she is famed.
