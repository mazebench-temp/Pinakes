---
summary: The plowman of the simile, “for whom all day long / two wine-dark oxen drag the jointed plow through the fallow field” (13.31–32).
---

βόε οἴνοπε, the dual, is new to the entry: the oxen of the Iliad's simile of the two Ajaxes, “two wine-dark oxen” with “the jointed plow” in the fallow (*Iliad* 13.703), the only other place where the epithet describes anything but the sea. The plowman longs for the sunset that will send him to his supper, as Odysseus, eager to go home, longs for the sun to set (13.29–35; see [[the-plowman-and-his-supper]], [[oxen-at-the-plow]], [[jointed]] and [[sunset-lines]]).
