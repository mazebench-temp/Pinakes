---
summary: Odysseus goes to meet the young herdsman and greets him as a suppliant, “for to you / I pray as to a god, and I come to your dear knees” (13.226–235); and he implores Athena “by your father” to tell him whether he is home (13.324–328).
refs: [13.226-235, 13.324-328]
---

The approach and the appeal without the clasp: the knees are named, not touched (13.231), and the plea is for the goods and the man, “save these things, and save me,” and for the name of the land (13.230–235; see [[anaphora]] and [[tell-me-this-truly]]). The answer is the land's name, and then, after the second plea, the land itself shown (13.237–249, 13.344–352). See [[supplication]].
