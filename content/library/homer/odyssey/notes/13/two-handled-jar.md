---
summary: In the cave of the Naiads by the harbor of Phorcys there are mixing bowls and two-handled jars of stone, and there the bees store up honey (13.105–106).
---

“In it are mixing bowls and two-handled jars / of stone; and there the bees store up honey” (13.105–106; see [[mixing-bowl]], [[bees]], [[honey]], [[nymphs]] and [[the-cave-of-the-naiads]]). Elsewhere in the Odyssey the jars hold wine, taken on a voyage or given as a gift (2.290, 2.349–353, 9.164, 9.204; see the entry); here they are of stone, λάϊνοι, like the looms beside them (13.107; see [[loom]]), and the bees use them. W. W. Merry, summing up the poem's picture of Ithaca, describes the grotto as containing bowls, jars and looms of stone, “where the Naiades wove their sea-purple webs, while the bees stored honey for them in the hollows of the rock” (*Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, Appendix III §1).
