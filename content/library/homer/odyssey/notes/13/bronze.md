---
summary: Bronze is the treasure of the Phaeacians' gifts, “the bronze that makes men strong” that the lords bring to the ship (13.19), the “bronze and gold in plenty” of Poseidon's complaint (13.136) and “the unyielding bronze” that Odysseus carries into the cave (13.368); in his false tale it is the sharp bronze with which he killed Orsilochus (13.271). The house of Alcinous is bronze-floored (13.4).
---

**Treasure.** At dawn the lords “hurried to the ship, and brought the bronze that makes men strong” (13.19; see [[tripod]] and [[cauldron]]): the tripods and cauldrons that Alcinous asked of them the night before (13.13–14). εὐήνωρ, “that makes men strong”, is said of wine at 4.622 (see the translation's row εὐήνωρ). Poseidon names the gifts “bronze and gold in plenty, and woven clothing” (13.136; see [[poseidon]] and [[the-gifts-of-the-phaeacians]]), the words of Zeus' promise (5.38), and Odysseus carries into the cave “the gold and the unyielding bronze / and the well-made clothes” (13.368–369; see [[gold-and-silver]], [[unyielding]] and [[the-cave-of-the-naiads]]). Gold and bronze are the stored wealth of a house, as in the storeroom of Odysseus, “where gold and bronze lay heaped” (2.338; see [[the-storeroom-of-odysseus]]).

**Weapon.** In the tale he tells the disguised Athena, the Cretan exile struck Orsilochus “with my bronze-fitted spear” and “killed him with the sharp bronze” (13.267, 13.271; see [[the-first-cretan-tale]], [[spear]], [[bronze-fitted]], [[sharp]] and [[orsilochus-son-of-idomeneus]]).

**The bronze-floored house.** Alcinous begins: “Odysseus, since you have come to my bronze-floored house, / high-roofed” (13.4–5; see [[bronze-floored]], [[high-roofed]], [[the-house-of-alcinous]] and [[house]]).
