summary: Athena comes to Odysseus in the likeness of a young herdsman, with sandals under her shining feet and a javelin in her hands (13.225), in the words of the Odyssey's verse of dressing.
# πέδιλʼ (13.225), the elided form, first met here and found only here in the Odyssey; it applies in the Odyssey only.
grc: [πέδιλʼ]
---

Athena comes to the shore “in form like a young man, a herdsman of sheep”, with a cape folded double around her shoulders, “and under her shining feet she had sandals, and in her hands a javelin” (13.222–225; see [[athena]], [[cloak]], [[spear]] and [[shape-changing]]). ποσσὶ δʼ ὑπὸ λιπαροῖσι is the beginning of the verse with which a man rises and dresses, “and under his shining feet he bound his fine sandals” (2.4, 4.309; see [[sleek]] and the Book 2 and Book 4 notes); here the verse describes the herdsman's dress instead of the act. The goddess who wears these sandals had bound on her own, golden and ambrosial, when she went down to Ithaca in Book 1, sandals “which carried her over the waters / and over the boundless earth as fast as the blasts of the wind” (1.96–98; see the Book 1 note). When Odysseus reaches the swineherd in the next book, Eumaeus is fitting sandals to his feet, cutting them from an oxhide (14.23–24; see [[eumaeus]]).
