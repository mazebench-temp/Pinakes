---
summary: Alcinous: “Odysseus, since you have come to my bronze-floored house, / high-roofed, so I think that you will not be beaten back / and sail for home again” (13.4–6).
---

ὑψερεφές, held over to the start of the verse, adds the Odyssey's word for great houses to χαλκοβατὲς δῶ, the Iliad's for the house of Zeus (see [[bronze-floored]]). Book 7 gave the same epithet to the palace, “the high-roofed house of great-hearted Alcinous” (7.85; see the Book 7 note and [[the-house-of-alcinous]]). D. B. Monro finds the next words taken from Achilles' in *Iliad* 1.59–60, “but with a change of application which has made them somewhat obscure” (*Homer's Odyssey, Books XIII–XXIV*, 1901, on 13.5–6; see the translation's note on 13.5–6).
