summary: The minds of the book: the strangers' “mind that fears the gods” (13.202) and the Phaeacians whom Odysseus thinks “not altogether discerning” (13.209); his own “very crafty mind” (13.255) and “thought” (13.330), which Athena praises as “quick-witted” (13.332); her “counsel and my mind” (13.305); and Penelope, whose “mind has other things in view” (13.381).
# ἀγχίνοος (13.332), only here in Homer.
grc: [ἀγχίνοος]
en: [quick-witted]
---

**Minds that are judged.** Waking in a land he does not know, Odysseus asks whether its people are “kind to strangers, with a mind that fears the gods” (νόος … θεουδής, 13.202; see [[hybris]] and [[xenia]]), and decides that the Phaeacians “were not altogether discerning or just” (νοήμονες, 13.209; see [[hegemon]] and [[themis]]), since they seem to have left him in a strange land. To the young herdsman he prays, “may you not meet me with evil intent” (κακῷ νόῳ, 13.229; see [[supplication]]).

**His mind.** Before the lie the narrator shows the mind at work: “always turning a very crafty mind in his breast” (νόον πολυκερδέα νωμῶν, 13.255; see [[kerdos]] and [[the-first-cretan-tale]]), νωμῶν being “turning about”, “revolving” (D. B. Monro, *Homer's Odyssey, Books XIII–XXIV*, 1901, on 13.255). In the lie, darkness hides the ambush, “and no one / of men noticed us” (ἐνόησε, 13.269–270), and the verb returns in his reproach to Athena, “nor did I notice you / boarding my ship” (ἐνόησα, 13.318–319). Her answer is the praise of his mind: “Always such is the thought in your breast; / therefore I cannot abandon you, unhappy as you are, / because you are courteous, and quick-witted, and sensible” (νόημα … ἀγχίνοος, 13.330–332; see [[sensible]]). ἀγχίνοος, “quick of mind”, from ἄγχι, “near”, and νόος, is found in Homer only here; ἐπητής is “charming”, “polite” (Monro on 13.332).

**Her mind and Penelope's.** Athena claims the gifts for her own “counsel and my mind” (βουλῇ τε νόῳ τε, 13.305; see [[boule]]). Of Penelope, always mourning for his homecoming, she says that she “gives hope to all, and makes promises to each man, / sending out messages, but her mind has other things in view” (13.379–381; see [[penelope]] and [[the-suitors]]), the verses in which Antinous complained of her in the assembly (2.91–92). The suitors' grievance is told to her husband as the proof of her faithfulness.
