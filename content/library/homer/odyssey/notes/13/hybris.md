---
summary: Waking in a land he does not know, Odysseus asks the question he asked in Scheria and before the Cyclopes: “Are they arrogant and savage and unjust, / or kind to strangers, with a mind that fears the gods?” (13.201–202).
---

“Ah me, to the land of what mortals have I come this time? / Are they arrogant and savage and unjust, / or kind to strangers, with a mind that fears the gods?” (ἦ ῥʼ οἵ γʼ ὑβρισταί τε καὶ ἄγριοι οὐδὲ δίκαιοι, 13.200–202; see [[to-the-land-of-what-mortals]], [[xenia]] and [[themis]]). The verses are those of his waking on the shore of Scheria (6.119–121), and the second and third are those with which he set out to make trial of the Cyclopes (9.175–176; see [[peira]]); ἦ … ἦε is a double question (D. B. Monro, *Homer's Odyssey, Books XIII–XXIV*, 1901, on 13.201–202). This time he is home, and the answer is in his own house: the arrogant men are the suitors, whom Athena will describe to him before the book ends (13.375–381; see [[the-suitors]]). In the last book, when Odysseus asks his father whether it is truly Ithaca that he has come to (24.258–259), Laertes, not knowing that the suitors are dead, answers, “Stranger, truly you have come to the land you ask about, / but arrogant and reckless men hold it” (24.281–282, a plain rendering).
