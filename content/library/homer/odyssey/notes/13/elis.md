---
summary: “or at shining Elis, where the Epeians rule” (13.275): Elis is the second place to which the Cretan of Odysseus' false tale asked the Phoenicians to take him, in a verse that returns in Books 15 and 24.
---

The Cretan of the tale asked the Phoenicians to set him down at Pylos, “or at shining Elis, where the Epeians rule” (13.274–275; see [[the-first-cretan-tale]], [[pylos]] and [[phoenicians]]). Ἤλιδα δῖαν, “shining Elis”, is the Catalogue's phrase for the land (*Iliad* 2.615; see [[brilliant]]), and ὅθι κρατέουσιν Ἐπειοί, “where the Epeians rule”, gives it its people (see [[epeians]]). The verse comes back twice: when Telemachus' ship sails home along the coast, “past shining Elis, where the Epeians rule” (15.298), and when the suitors' kin fear that Odysseus may get away “either to Pylos / or to shining Elis” (24.430–431, a plain rendering; see the translation's formula row for 13.275). The *Homeric Hymn to Apollo* has the verse of Book 15 entire, and the verse before it nearly so, in the course of another voyage up that coast, the voyage of the Cretan ship that the god steers past “shining Elis, where the Epeians rule” toward Pherae (*Hymn to Apollo* 426–427; see [[crete]]), and Strabo quotes the verse in the same form to show that Homer called the land of the Epeians Elis, as he called Nestor's land Pylos (*Geography* 8.3.1; see [[strabo]]).
