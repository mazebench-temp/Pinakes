---
summary: On the shore of Ithaca Athena tells Odysseus where his son is: she will go to Sparta to call him home (13.411–415). To his reproach that she let the boy wander, she answers that she sent him to win noble fame, and that the suitors' ambush will fail (13.416–428). At the end of the book she goes to Lacedaemon after him (13.439–440), and the fifteenth book takes up the journey there.
# Unnamed: Telemachus at Sparta (13.412-415); the reproach and the answer (13.417-424); the ambush (13.425-428);
# Athena's departure (13.439-440). Telemachus is named at 13.413.

---

**The errand.** Odysseus is to wait with the swineherd “until I go to Sparta of the lovely women / to call Telemachus, your dear son, Odysseus, / who went to Lacedaemon with its wide dancing floors, to Menelaus, / to learn the report of you, whether you were still alive anywhere” (13.412–415; see [[telemachus]], [[sparta]], [[of-the-lovely-women]] and [[with-wide-dancing-floors]]). The purpose is the one Athena gave in the first council of the gods, “to learn of his dear father's homecoming, if he may hear of it somewhere” (1.94; see [[to-learn-of-his-fathers-homecoming]]); μετὰ σὸν κλέος is “after thy story”, in search of the report of him (D. B. Monro, *Homer's Odyssey, Books XIII–XXIV*, 1901, on 13.415; see [[kleos]]). For πὰρ Μενέλαον the vulgate has πρός, and some manuscripts have ἤν for εἰ (Monro's critical notes on 13.414–415).

**The reproach.** This is the first that Odysseus has heard of his son's journey, and he answers with a reproach: “Why then did you not tell him, you who know all things in your mind? / Was it so that he too, wandering, might suffer sorrows / over the barren sea, while others devour his substance?” (13.417–419; see [[barren-sea]]). βίοτον δέ οἱ ἄλλοι ἔδουσι is “while others devour” (Monro on 13.419). Three manuscripts and Eustathius have the subjunctive ἔδωσι (Monro's critical note on 13.419).

**Noble fame.** “Do not let him be too much on your mind. / I myself escorted him, so that he might win noble fame / by going there” (13.421–423). It is the second purpose of the plan she put before the gods, “so that noble fame may be his among men” (1.95); she went with him herself in the likeness of Mentor as far as Pylos, and left him there in the likeness of a sea eagle (2.399–434, 3.371–372; see [[athena]] and [[mentor]]). He suffers no toil, she says, but sits at his ease “in the house of the son of Atreus, and untold plenty lies beside him” (13.423–424; see [[menelaus]] and [[son-of-atreus]]).

**The ambush.** “Truly young men in a black ship lie in ambush for him, / eager to kill him before he reaches his native land; / but I do not think it will be so” (13.425–427; see [[ambush]]). The ambush is the one Antinous asked for and laid with twenty men at the little island of Asteris, in the strait between Ithaca and Samos (4.669–672, 4.842–847; see [[antinous]] and [[asteris]]). Before Telemachus can be killed, she adds, the earth will hold fast some of the suitors, “who are devouring your substance” (13.427–428), the words that end her prophecy of blood on the floor (13.396). One manuscript and Eustathius omit 13.428 (Monro's critical note). When she reaches Sparta she will warn Telemachus of the ambush in the verse she speaks here, “eager to kill you before you reach your native land” (15.30, a plain rendering; 13.426).

**To Lacedaemon.** “When the two had made their plans, they parted. She then / went to shining Lacedaemon, after the son of Odysseus” (13.439–440; see [[lacedaemon]]). The book ends there, in the middle of a sentence whose second half is the next book, given to Odysseus at the swineherd's hut (Monro on 13.440). The fifteenth book takes up the goddess where she went: “But Pallas Athena went to Lacedaemon with its wide dancing floors, / to the shining son of great-hearted Odysseus, / to remind him of his homecoming and to urge him to return” (15.1–3, a plain rendering).
