---
summary: In the false tale the exile gives the Phoenicians “spoil to satisfy their hearts” (13.273), and Athena tells of the swineherd's swine at the spring, “eating acorns to satisfy their hearts” (13.409).
en: [to satisfy their hearts]
---

μενοεικέα ληΐδα is the Cretan's fare for his passage, paid out of the spoil from Troy over which, he says, he killed Orsilochus (13.262–273; see [[the-first-cretan-tale]]); in the next Cretan tale it is the share of his spoil that he picks out for himself (14.232). βάλανον μενοεικέα feeds the swine of Eumaeus, with “the dark water,” both of which “nourish the rich lard of swine” (13.409–410; see [[eumaeus]] and [[boar]]).
