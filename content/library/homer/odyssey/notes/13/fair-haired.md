---
summary: Athena: “I will wither the fair skin on your supple limbs, / and destroy the fair hair from your head” (13.398–399); and she “destroyed the fair hair from his head” (13.431).
---

ξανθὰς … τρίχας: here Odysseus is fair-haired, and the poem says otherwise elsewhere. In Book 6 Athena made his thick hair fall “like the hyacinth flower” (6.231; see [[hyacinth]]), and when she gives him back his own looks in Book 16 “the beard grew dark about his chin” (16.176, a plain rendering). D. B. Monro notes the contradiction on both passages: κυάνεαι “must mean ‘dark.’ The poet forgets that Ulysses had ‘yellow’ hair before” (*Homer's Odyssey, Books XIII–XXIV*, 1901, on 13.431 and 16.176). In his account of Ridgeway's view that the Achaeans were a fair-haired people, he adds that the statements about Odysseus are contradictory (Monro, Appendix V §8).
