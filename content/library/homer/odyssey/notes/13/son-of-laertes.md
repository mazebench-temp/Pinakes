---
summary: Seated beside him under the sacred olive, Athena begins the plan: “Son of Laertes, sprung from Zeus, Odysseus of many devices, / consider how you will lay hands on the shameless suitors” (13.375–376).
---

The verse with which the goddess sent him through the fleeing army in the Iliad (*Iliad* 2.173), and her first use of it in the Odyssey (again 16.167, 24.542), after Calypso, Circe and five of the dead (5.203; 10.401, 10.488, 10.504; 11.60, 11.92, 11.405, 11.473, 11.617). A few verses before, she has called him “Hard man, of subtle wiles, insatiable of tricks” (13.293); now she addresses him with the full formality of the verse as the plan against the suitors begins (13.372–381; see [[of-subtle-wiles]], [[sprung-from-zeus]], [[of-many-devices]], [[laertes]] and [[the-suitors]]).
