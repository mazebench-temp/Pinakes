---
summary: “The leaders and rulers of the people of the Phaeacians” pray to Poseidon around his altar (13.186), while Odysseus, waking in Ithaca, accuses “the leaders and rulers of the Phaeacians” of leaving him in a strange land (13.210).
---

The phrase with which Alcinous used to address his council, “Hear me, leaders and rulers of the Phaeacians” (7.186, 8.26, 8.536), is used twice in the book, of the same men, at the same moment. The narrator leaves them at Poseidon's altar: “So they prayed to lord Poseidon, / the leaders and rulers of the people of the Phaeacians, / standing around the altar” (δήμου Φαιήκων ἡγήτορες ἠδὲ μέδοντες, 13.185–187; see [[phaeacians]] and [[prayer]]). In the next verse Odysseus wakes in Ithaca, and blames them: “Ah, then they were not altogether discerning or just, / the leaders and rulers of the Phaeacians, / who have brought me away to another land; truly they told me / they would bring me to clear-seen Ithaca, and they have not done it” (13.209–212; see [[themis]] and [[noos]]). οὐκ ἄρα ἦσαν is “they are not, as I thought they were” (D. B. Monro, *Homer's Odyssey, Books XIII–XXIV*, 1901, on 13.209). The men he accuses of breaking their word are praying for their city because they kept it (see [[the-migration-of-the-phaeacians]]).
