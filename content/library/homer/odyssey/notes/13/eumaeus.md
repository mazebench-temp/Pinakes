**First of all to the swineherd.** When Athena has made Odysseus unknown, she sends him first, not to his house, but to his swineherd:

{{quote:acb6d4f1-1afd-5faa-a37b-3db67ae7abf9}}

The first two verses come again when Athena gives Telemachus the same instructions at Sparta (15.38–39; see [[telemachus]]). τοι goes with ἤπια οἶδε, “he is of friendly mind to thee”; ὁμῶς is disputed. Ameis refers it to ὑῶν ἐπίουρος, so that Eumaeus is as devoted to Odysseus as to his possessions, the swine; others to the next verse, “even as he is towards thy son and Penelope”, which will not suit 15.39, where that verse does not follow. Monro finds in the word a blend of loyalty with agreement: Eumaeus was “at one with Ulysses in the loyalty of his heart” (D. B. Monro, *Homer's Odyssey, Books XIII–XXIV*, 1901, on 13.405; see the translation's note on 13.405). ἐπίουρος, “guardian”, is the word of Minos, “a guardian over Crete” (*Iliad* 13.450; see [[minos]]).

**Where to find him.** “You will find him sitting beside the swine; and they are feeding / by the Rock of Corax and at the spring Arethusa, / eating acorns to satisfy their hearts and the dark water / drinking, which nourish the rich lard of swine. / Stay there, and sitting beside him ask him about everything” (13.407–411; see [[the-rock-of-corax]], [[arethusa]] and [[boar]]). παρήμενον is “abiding with” (Monro on 13.407). The swineherd is to be his first host on Ithaca and his source of news, while Athena brings Telemachus home (13.412–415; see [[xenia]]). In the next book Odysseus goes up from the harbor “by the rugged path” to the swineherd's farm (14.1–4; see [[the-harbor-of-phorcys]]), and there the poem names him, speaking to him in the second person: “Answering him, you said, swineherd Eumaeus” (14.55).
