---
summary: The last words of Odysseus' false tale on the shore of Ithaca: the Phoenicians sailed away for Sidon, “but I was left behind, grieving at heart” (13.286).
---

The Cretan of the lying tale ends his story where Odysseus himself is standing. The Phoenicians, he says, set his goods down on the sand while he slept, “And they went aboard for well-peopled Sidon / and were gone; but I was left behind, grieving at heart” (ἀκαχήμενος ἦτορ, 13.285–286; see [[the-first-cretan-tale]], [[etor]], [[phoenicians]] and [[sidon]]). The grief has a true model. The landing he tells is his own, asleep on the sand with his goods set down beside him (13.116–124, 13.282–284; see [[odysseus-set-ashore-on-ithaca]]), and before Athena came he had dragged himself along the shore mourning for his native land, “lamenting much” (13.219–221; see [[lament]]). The participle ἀκαχήμενος belongs to ἀκαχίζω, “grieve”, of the same root as ἄχος.
