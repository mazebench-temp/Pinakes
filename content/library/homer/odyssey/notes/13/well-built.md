δόμοις ἔνι ποιητοῖσι(ν), the verse-end of the “well-built house” of the Iliad (*Iliad* 5.198). D. B. Monro, explaining πηκτόν, the “jointed” plow of 13.32, sets ποιητός of a house beside it: neither need be translated “well made,” though that may be implied, for the point is that the thing is a work of skill (*Homer's Odyssey, Books XIII–XXIV*, 1901, on 13.32; see [[jointed]]). The well-built house is the one where the suitors are feasting (13.376–381; see [[house]] and [[the-suitors]]).
