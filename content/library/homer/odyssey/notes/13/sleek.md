summary: Athena comes as a young herdsman, “and under her shining feet she had sandals, and in her hands a javelin” (13.225); and Odysseus asks her to stand beside him “as when we loosed the shining veil of Troy” (13.388).
grc: [λιπαροῖσι, "λιπαρὰ κρήδεμνα"]
---

ποσσὶ δʼ ὑπὸ λιπαροῖσι, the opening of the sandal verse of the [[dressing-scene]] (2.4, 4.309), with πέδιλʼ ἔχε, “she had sandals,” in place of the binding on: the disguise is told in the items of a man dressed to go out (see [[divine-epiphany]]). λιπαρὰ κρήδεμνα is the phrase of Penelope's veil (1.334); of Troy, D. B. Monro takes it as “the diadem of towers,” after *Iliad* 16.100, where the Iliad has ἱερὰ κρήδεμνα, “the sacred veil” (*Homer's Odyssey, Books XIII–XXIV*, 1901, on 13.388; see the translation's note on 13.388, and [[veil]] and [[troy]]).
