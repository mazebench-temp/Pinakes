---
summary: Athena smiles at the false tale: “So he spoke, and the gray-eyed goddess Athena smiled, / and stroked him with her hand” (13.287–288).
---

The smile of Calypso at Odysseus' suspicion (5.180) and of Menelaus at Telemachus' frankness (4.609), here of a goddess who has been told a lie to her face, and admires it: her answer begins “Crafty would he be, and a cheat, who should get past you / in tricks of every kind” (13.291–292). It is the smile of the stronger speaker, who knows how the scene will end (see the general article, [[called-him-by-name]], [[of-subtle-wiles]] and [[the-first-cretan-tale]]).
