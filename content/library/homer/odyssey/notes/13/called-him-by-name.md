---
summary: Athena answers the false tale with a smile and a touch: “So he spoke, and the gray-eyed goddess Athena smiled, / and stroked him with her hand; and she was like a woman in form” (13.287–288).
grc: ["χειρί τέ μιν κατέρεξε"]
en: [stroked him with her hand]
---

The gesture of the whole verse χειρί τέ μιν κατέρεξεν ἔπος τʼ ἔφατʼ ἔκ τʼ ὀνόμαζε, after a smile, as with Menelaus and Calypso (4.609–610, 5.180–181; see [[smiling-at-him]]); but where the verse goes on to the naming, here the half-verse turns to her change of form, and the name that follows in her speech is her own, “Pallas Athena, daughter of Zeus” (13.300; see [[pallas]] and [[divine-epiphany]]).
