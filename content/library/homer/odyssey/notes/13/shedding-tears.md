---
summary: Athena of Penelope: “and always in misery for her / the nights and the days waste away, as she sheds tears” (13.337–338).
---

Anticleia's words to her son among the dead (11.182–183), now in the goddess's mouth, who says that she never doubted his return (13.339–340); the swineherd will say the second verse to Telemachus (16.39). Aristarchus rejected 13.333–338, the verses on the testing of Penelope (D. B. Monro, *Homer's Odyssey, Books XIII–XXIV*, 1901, critical note on 13.333–338). See [[penelope]], [[tears]] and [[peira]].
