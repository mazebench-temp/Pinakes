---
summary: Athena leaves Odysseus with the swineherd while she goes “to Sparta of the lovely women / to call Telemachus” home (13.411–415).
---

When she has sent Odysseus to the swineherd, Athena tells him to stay there “until I go to Sparta of the lovely women / to call Telemachus, your dear son, Odysseus, / who went to Lacedaemon with its wide dancing floors, to Menelaus, / to learn the report of you, whether you were still alive anywhere” (13.411–415; see [[telemachus]], [[menelaus]] and [[the-journey-of-telemachus]]). καλλιγύναικα, “of the lovely women”, is the Iliad's epithet for Hellas and for the Achaean land (*Iliad* 2.683, 9.447; 3.75, 3.258), given to Sparta only here (see [[of-the-lovely-women]]); the Suda explains it as “having beautiful women” (Suda κ 217). The city and the land stand side by side for the same journey, Sparta in one verse and Lacedaemon two verses later (see [[lacedaemon]]). Strabo noticed that Homer uses Lacedaemon of both the land and the city, and argued that Menelaus' house must have been in Sparta itself, or Telemachus would not have said, “For I will go to Sparta and to sandy Pylos” (2.214; *Geography* 8.5.8; see [[strabo]]). Odysseus asks why she did not simply tell his son, and she answers that Telemachus “sits at his ease / in the house of the son of Atreus” (13.416–424).
