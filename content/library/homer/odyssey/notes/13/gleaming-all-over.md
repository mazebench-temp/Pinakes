---
summary: At the farewell feast Odysseus “kept turning his head toward the all-gleaming sun, / in haste for it to set; for he was eager to go home” (13.29–30).
en: [all-gleaming]
---

Only here in Homer is the sun παμφανόων; elsewhere the word is said of armor, walls, a chariot and a cauldron, and the translation renders it “all-gleaming” (see the Odyssey translation's key words, παμφανόων). Monro takes δῦναι ἐπειγόμενος as “in haste, eager for (his) setting” (D. B. Monro, *Homer's Odyssey, Books XIII–XXIV*, 1901, on 13.30). The ship sails by night, and the simile that follows is the plowman's longing for the sunset and his supper (13.31–35; see [[jointed]] and [[helios]]).
