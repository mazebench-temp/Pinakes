---
summary: The cave's second door, “toward the South Wind, is for gods; by that one / men do not enter, but it is the way of the immortals” (13.111–112); and Poseidon: “Father Zeus, no longer shall I among the immortal gods / be held in honor” (13.128–129).
---

θεώτεραι is not “more divine” but “divine,” in contrast to the human door (D. B. Monro, *Homer's Odyssey, Books XIII–XXIV*, 1901, on 13.111; see the translation's note on 13.111): one door is for men going down, the other the way of the immortals (13.109–112; see [[nymphs]] and [[boreas]]). Poseidon measures his honor among the immortals by the honor mortals pay him, and Zeus answers that the gods do not dishonor him, and that if any man does, vengeance is his (13.141–144; see [[time]] and [[poseidon]]).
