summary: Athena is “Pallas Athena, daughter of Zeus” when she pours the mist around Odysseus (13.190), and “daughter of aegis-bearing Zeus” (13.252, 13.371); she names herself “Pallas Athena, daughter of Zeus” (13.300); Odysseus reproaches her, “after that I did not see you, daughter of Zeus” (13.318), and prays to the Naiads, “daughters of Zeus,” that “the daughter of Zeus, driver of spoil,” may let him live (13.356–360).

en: [daughters]

The narrator's title for the goddess is the one she gives herself when she reveals herself to him: “yet you did not know / Pallas Athena, daughter of Zeus, who always / stand by you in all your toils and guard you” (13.299–301). Odysseus takes up the title in his answer, asking where she was from the sack of Troy until Scheria (13.316–323; see the translation's note on 13.320–323, and [[pallas]] and [[athena]]). Διὸς θυγάτηρ with ἀγελείη is a half-verse of the Iliad (*Iliad* 4.128; see [[driver-of-spoil]]). The Naiads are κοῦραι Διός, as the nymphs with Artemis and the nymphs who roused the goats on the island are “daughters of aegis-bearing Zeus” (6.105, 9.154; see [[child-of-aegis-bearing-zeus]] and [[nymphs]]); νηϊάδες κοῦραι Διός is new to the entry.
