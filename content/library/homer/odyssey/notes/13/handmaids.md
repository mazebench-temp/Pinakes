---
summary: When Odysseus leaves the house of Alcinous, Arete sends three slave women down to the ship with him, one with a freshly washed mantle and a tunic, one with the chest, and one with bread and red wine (13.66–69).
---

“And Arete sent slave women with him, / one carrying a freshly washed mantle and a tunic, / and another she granted him, to carry the close-fitted chest along; / and a third brought bread and red wine” (13.66–69; see [[arete-queen-of-the-phaeacians]] and [[the-house-of-alcinous]]). δμῳαί, “slave women”, carry the queen's last provision for the voyage: fresh clothes, and the chest that she herself had filled with his gifts and that he had tied with the knot Circe taught him (8.438–448; see [[chest]], [[cloak]] and [[tunic]]), and the escorts take the things from them at the ship and stow them, “all the drink and food” (13.70–72; see [[provisions]] and [[wine]]).
