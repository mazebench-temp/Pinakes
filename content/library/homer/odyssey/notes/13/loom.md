“And in it are towering looms of stone, where the nymphs / weave sea-purple webs, a wonder to see” (13.107–108; see [[nymphs]], [[weaving]], [[cloak]], [[purple]] and [[the-cave-of-the-naiads]]). The goddesses of the Odyssey are weavers: Calypso walks to and fro at her loom singing, and so does Circe (5.61–62, 10.221–223; see the Book 5 and Book 10 notes), and in their cave on Ithaca the Naiads weave too (see the translation's row ἱστοὶ λίθεοι; φάρεα ἁλιπόρφυρα). Their looms are of stone, as are the mixing bowls and the two-handled jars beside them (13.105–106; see [[mixing-bowl]] and [[two-handled-jar]]); the webs are φάρεα, “webs” (D. B. Monro, *Homer's Odyssey, Books XIII–XXIV*, 1901, on 13.108), the word of the mantle, and sea-purple, the color of the yarn that Arete spins (6.53).
