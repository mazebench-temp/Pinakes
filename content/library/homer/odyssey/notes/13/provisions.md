Of the three slave women whom Arete sends to the ship with Odysseus, one carries the mantle and the tunic, another the chest, “and a third brought bread and red wine. / And when they had come down to the ship and the sea, / at once in the hollow ship the noble escorts / took these and stowed them, all the drink and food” (13.69–72; see [[arete-queen-of-the-phaeacians]], [[handmaids]], [[wine]] and [[chest]]). Bread and red wine are a ship's stores, as Calypso promised to put them on the raft and Circe brought them to the companions back from Hades (5.165, 12.19), and as long as they lasted on Thrinacia the companions kept from the cattle of the Sun (12.327–328; see the Book 12 note and the entry). A host provides them for the guest he sends home (see [[xenia]]); the voyage is a single night, and Odysseus sleeps through it (13.79–92).
