---
summary: In Odysseus' false tale the Cretan exile bids the Phoenicians “take me on board and set me down at Pylos, / or at shining Elis” (13.274–275), the two lands of the western Peloponnese.
---

The Cretan of the tale names Pylos first among the places to which the Phoenicians were to take him: “I bade them take me on board and set me down at Pylos, / or at shining Elis, where the Epeians rule” (13.274–275; see [[the-first-cretan-tale]], [[phoenicians]] and [[elis]]). Πύλονδε καταστῆσαι is a pregnant construction, “to bring to Pylos and set down there”, as Odysseus will say of the Phoenician who put him on board a ship “bound for Libya” (14.295, a plain rendering; D. B. Monro, *Homer's Odyssey, Books XIII–XXIV*, 1901, on 13.274). Pylos and Elis are named together as the mainland powers across the sea from the islands, Nestor's kingdom and the land of the Epeians, the division that Strabo found in Homer's account of the old Peloponnese (*Geography* 8.3.1; see [[strabo]] and [[epeians]]); the suitors' kin will fear in the last book that Odysseus may escape them “either to Pylos / or to shining Elis, where the Epeians rule” (24.430–431, a plain rendering). In the *Homeric Hymn to Apollo* the Cretans whom the god carries off to be his ministers at Pytho were bound “to Pylos from Crete” when he turned their ship aside (*Hymn to Apollo* 388–399, 470; see [[crete]]).
