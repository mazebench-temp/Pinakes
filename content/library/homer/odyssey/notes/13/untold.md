---
summary: Poseidon complains that the Phaeacians gave Odysseus “untold gifts” (13.135); Athena foretells that one or another of the suitors “will spatter the boundless floor with blood and brains” (13.395), and says that Telemachus sits at his ease in Menelaus' house, “and untold plenty lies beside him” (13.424).
---

The wealth of the gifts, the plenty of the house in Sparta, and the floor of the hall where the suitors will die. ἄσπετον οὖδας is the Iliad's verse-end of men who “bit the boundless ground with their teeth” as they fell (*Iliad* 24.738; 19.61); Athena's prophecy brings it into the hall, and the death of the suitors fulfills it in the old words, “These then, all together, bit the boundless ground with their teeth” (22.269, a plain rendering).
