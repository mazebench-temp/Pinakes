---
summary: The cave of the Naiads has two doors, one toward the North Wind, by which men go down, and one toward the South Wind, “the way of the immortals” (13.109–112); when the gifts have been laid away in the cave, Athena sets a stone against the doors (13.370).
---

**The two doors.** “And it has two doors, / one toward the North Wind, by which men go down, / and the other, toward the South Wind, is for gods; by that one / men do not enter, but it is the way of the immortals” (13.109–112; see [[the-cave-of-the-naiads]], [[nymphs]], [[boreas]] and [[notus]]). θεώτεραι is not “more divine” but “divine”, in contrast to the human door (D. B. Monro, *Homer's Odyssey, Books XIII–XXIV*, 1901, on 13.111; see the translation's row καταιβάτης; θεώτερος): Monro compares θηλυτέρῃσι γυναιξί, “the womenfolk” (15.422), the comparative that sets one kind against the other. The poem does not say by which door Odysseus and Athena go in when they hide the gifts; the goddess “went into the misty cave” (13.366).

**The stone.** Odysseus brings the gold, the bronze and the clothes, “And she laid them away well, and set a stone against the doors, / Pallas Athena, daughter of aegis-bearing Zeus” (13.370–371; see [[athena]], [[possessions]] and [[the-gifts-of-the-phaeacians]]). The Cyclops shut his cave with a great stone too, to keep his flock in (9.240–243; see [[door-stone]]); this one keeps a treasure, which Odysseus will tell Telemachus lies “in caves by the will of the gods” (16.232, a plain rendering; see [[the-cave-of-the-naiads]]).

**Outside Homer.** Strabo quotes the doors, in a shortened verse, αἱ μὲν πρὸς βορέαν, αἱ δʼ αὖ πρὸς νότον, among the places where Homer shows his learning in the zones of the earth (κλίματα) and the winds, naming them as he describes a place (*Geography* 1.2.20; see [[strabo]]). The philosopher Numenius, as Proclus reports him, called the poem to witness for the two chasms in the heaven of Plato's myth of Er (*Republic* 614c), which he took for the tropic signs, Cancer the chasm of the soul's descent into becoming and Capricorn of its ascent: the doors toward the North Wind are the ways down for men, and those toward the South Wind are the ways of the immortals only, since Capricorn, leading souls up, frees them from their life among men (Proclus, *On the Republic* II 128–129 Kroll, where the text is corrupt in the clause on Cancer; see [[proclus-the-neoplatonist]] and [[plato]]).
