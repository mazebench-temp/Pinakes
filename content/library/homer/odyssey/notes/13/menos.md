---
summary: “The might of Alcinous” names the king four times in the farewell (13.20, 13.24, 13.49, 13.64), and Odysseus asks Athena to stand beside him, “putting in me a might full of daring, / as when we loosed the shining veil of Troy” (13.387–388).
---

**The might of Alcinous.** The periphrasis that names the king by his might, “the sacred might of Alcinous”, runs through the day of the farewell: it stows the gifts under the thwarts, sacrifices the ox, calls the herald and sends him with Odysseus (μένος Ἀλκινόοιο, 13.20, 13.24, 13.49, 13.64; see [[sacred-might-of-alcinous]] and [[alcinous]]). “And for them the sacred might of Alcinous sacrificed an ox” (τοῖσι δὲ βοῦν ἱέρευσʼ ἱερὸν μένος Ἀλκινόοιο, 13.24) is the Iliad's verse of Agamemnon's sacrifice, with the king of the Phaeacians in place of the “lord of men” (*Iliad* 7.314), and Monro notes the play of language, ἱέρευσʼ ἱερόν, referring to the play of τίει and τίσις at 13.144 (D. B. Monro, *Homer's Odyssey, Books XIII–XXIV*, 1901, on 13.24 and 13.144; see [[poine]]).

**A might full of daring.** When Athena has told him how the suitors live in his house, Odysseus asks her for what the gods give in battle: “and stand beside me yourself, putting in me a might full of daring, / as when we loosed the shining veil of Troy. / If you would stand beside me, gray-eyed one, as eager as that, / I would fight even with three hundred men” (μένος πολυθαρσὲς ἐνεῖσα, 13.387–390; see [[full-of-daring]], [[tharsos]], [[veil]] and [[the-fall-of-troy]]). The phrase is the Iliad's: Thetis, when she has brought him the new armor, “put in him a might full of daring” (*Iliad* 19.37; see [[achilles]]). κρήδεμνα, “the shining veil”, is the diadem of towers, from *Iliad* 16.100 (Monro on 13.388; see the Odyssey translation's note on 13.388).
