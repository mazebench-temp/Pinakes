---
summary: Athena gives the epithet to Odysseus, “because you are courteous, and quick-witted, and sensible” (13.332), and in its usual place to Penelope, whom the swineherd loves, “and sensible Penelope” (13.406).
---

The three adjectives of 13.332 give the reason why the goddess cannot abandon him (13.331): ἐπητής, which D. B. Monro renders “charming,” “polite,” comparing ἐπητύς, “civility” (21.306), ἀγχίνοος, “quick-witted,” and ἐχέφρων (*Homer's Odyssey, Books XIII–XXIV*, 1901, on 13.332; see the Odyssey translation's key words). It is the only time that Homer gives the word to Odysseus, and it is the epithet of the wife he is to test before he makes himself known to her (13.335–336; see [[penelope]] and [[peira]]). ἐχέφρονα Πηνελόπειαν (13.406) is new to the entry.
