---
summary: Agamemnon is named with the patronymic in Odysseus' answer to Athena, “Ah, truly, like Agamemnon, son of Atreus, / I was about to perish by an evil fate in my halls” (13.383–384), and Menelaus by the patronymic alone in hers: Telemachus “sits at his ease / in the house of the son of Atreus” (13.423–424).
---

Ἀγαμέμνονος Ἀτρεΐδαο, the genitive formula of *Iliad* 1.203, names the king whose murder in his own hall Odysseus heard from his shade (11.405–434; see [[agamemnon]] and [[o-popoi]]). Some forty verses later the same genitive is the other brother, in whose house in Sparta Telemachus is a guest (13.412–424; see [[menelaus]] and [[sparta]]).
