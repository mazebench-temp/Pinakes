---
summary: The ship runs on, “bearing a man whose counsels were like the gods', / who before had suffered very many sorrows in his heart” (13.89–90).
grc: ["θεοῖς ἐναλίγκια μήδεʼ"]
en: [like the gods]
---

θεοῖς ἐναλίγκια is a brachylogy, “like the (counsels of the) gods,” as κόμαι Χαρίτεσσιν ὁμοῖαι, hair like the Graces' (*Iliad* 17.51; D. B. Monro, *Homer's Odyssey, Books XIII–XXIV*, 1901, on 13.89; see the translation's note on 13.89). The likeness is of the mind, not of looks: the Iliad's Odysseus is “the equal of Zeus in counsel” (see [[equal-of-zeus-in-counsel]]), and the poet's praise of the sleeping man sets his counsels beside his sufferings and his sleep, “forgetful of all he had suffered” (13.89–92; see [[odysseus]], [[grievous]] and [[metis]]).
