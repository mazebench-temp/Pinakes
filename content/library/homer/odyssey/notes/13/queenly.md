---
summary: “I would fight even with three hundred men / with you, lady goddess, if you would help me with a ready heart” (13.390–391).
---

πότνα θεά, the address with which Odysseus softened his refusal of Calypso's offer (5.215), ends his plea to Athena for her help against the suitors: she is to stand beside him “as when we loosed the shining veil of Troy” (13.387–391; see [[athena]], [[the-suitors]] and [[sleek]]). In the same speech he calls her γλαυκῶπι, “gray-eyed one” (13.389; see [[gray-eyed]]).
