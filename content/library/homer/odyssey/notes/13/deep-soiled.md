---
summary: Odysseus asks the young herdsman: “Is it one of the clear-seen islands, or some shore / of the deep-soiled mainland that lies upon the sea?” (13.234–235).
---

Monro joins ἀκτὴ ἠπείροιο, “a shore of the mainland,” and compares ἁλὶ κεκλιμένη with 4.608 (D. B. Monro, *Homer's Odyssey, Books XIII–XXIV*, 1901, on 13.235), where Telemachus says that none of the islands “that lie upon the sea” is good for driving horses or rich in meadows, “and Ithaca above them all” (4.605–608). The mainland is the land of rich soil, against the islands (see [[clear-seen]]); Athena's answer gives the rugged island a richness of its own, grain and wine, rain and dew, pasture and forest (13.242–247; see [[ithaca]]). ἐριβώλακος, the Iliad's genitive (as *Iliad* 5.44), is new to the entry in the Odyssey.
