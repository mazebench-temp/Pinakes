---
summary: Poseidon complains that the Phaeacians, “who after all are sprung from my own stock”, do not honor him (13.130).
---

“Father Zeus, no longer shall I among the immortal gods / be held in honor, since mortals do not honor me at all, / the Phaeacians, who after all are sprung from my own stock” (τοί πέρ τοι ἐμῆς ἔξ εἰσι γενέθλης, 13.128–130; see [[poseidon]], [[phaeacians]] and [[time]]). The royal line of the Phaeacians comes from the god: Nausithous, Alcinous' father, was born to Poseidon and Periboea (7.56–63; see [[nausithous]]). τοι gives the sense “after all” (D. B. Monro, *Homer's Odyssey, Books XIII–XXIV*, 1901, on 13.130): his own descendants are the ones who slight him. Alcinous, when the ship is turned to stone, finds the cause where his father did, in the escorts that Poseidon begrudged them (13.172–178; see [[pompe]] and [[thesphaton]]).
