---
summary: Alcinous bids Pontonous serve the wine, “so that when we have prayed to father Zeus / we may send the stranger on his way to his native land” (13.51–52); and Poseidon begins his complaint, “Father Zeus, no longer shall I among the immortal gods / be held in honor” (13.128–129).
---

13.50–51 is Alcinous' order to Pontonous in Book 7, where the libation went to “Zeus who delights in thunder, … who attends on revered suppliants” (7.179–181); now that the suppliant is a departing guest, the verse asks a prayer to father Zeus for his escort (see the Book 7 note on [[delighting-in-thunder]], and [[pontonous]] and [[libation]]). Poseidon calls his brother father, as he did when he complained of the Achaean wall: “Father Zeus, is there any mortal on the boundless earth” (*Iliad* 7.446; see [[earth-shaker]] and [[poseidon]]). The address is one of respect, not kinship (see the general article).
