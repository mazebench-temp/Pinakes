---
summary: Alcinous sacrifices an ox “to Zeus, lord of the dark clouds, son of Cronus, who rules over all” (13.25); and Poseidon answers Zeus, “I would do it at once, lord of the dark clouds, as you say” (13.147).
grc: ["ἔρξαιμι κελαινεφές"]
---

13.25 is the verse of Odysseus' sacrifice of the Cyclops' ram, which Zeus did not heed (9.552–555; see the Book 9 note and [[son-of-cronus]]); now, on the day of the escort, the god will grant Poseidon the Phaeacians' ship (13.139–158). The vocative κελαινεφές, the address of Agamemnon's prayer and of Hera's and Athena's words to Zeus in the Iliad (*Iliad* 2.412, 15.46, 22.178), is new to the entry in the Odyssey, where the word is otherwise said of blood (11.36, 11.153; see [[dark-blood]]); the note claims only Poseidon's phrase (see [[poseidon]] and [[zeus]]).
