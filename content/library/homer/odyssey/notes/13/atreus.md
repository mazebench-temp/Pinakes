summary: Named only in the patronymic of his two sons: Agamemnon, whose death in his halls Odysseus would have shared but for Athena (13.383), and Menelaus, in whose house at Sparta Telemachus sits at his ease (13.424).
# Ἀτρεΐδ* (in the entry) claims Ἀτρεΐδαο (13.383, 13.424). At 13.383 the son is Agamemnon; at 13.424 he is Menelaus,
# and agamemnon's Book 13 note excepts the line, which menelaus takes by refs.
---

Ἀγαμέμνονος Ἀτρεΐδαο, “Agamemnon, son of Atreus” (13.383), names the elder son in the verse with which Odysseus sets his own danger beside the murder of Agamemnon (see [[agamemnon]] and [[the-murder-of-agamemnon]]). Forty lines later Athena tells him that Telemachus “sits at his ease / in the house of the son of Atreus” (13.423–424), and there the son of Atreus is Menelaus, named ten verses before (13.414; see [[menelaus]] and [[telemachus]]). The patronymic counts for the father both times (see [[son-of-atreus]]).
