---
summary: Athena's account of Ithaca, before she names it: “It is not so very / nameless; very many know it” (13.238–239), and “but it is not very poor, though it is not broad” (13.243).
refs: [13.238-239, 13.243]
---

οὐδέ τι λίην / οὕτω νώνυμος and οὐδὲ λίην λυπρή: the goddess praises the land by denying its faults, as the island off the land of the Cyclopes was praised, “For it is not a poor place at all” (9.131), and then names its goods, grain and wine, rain and dew, pasture, forest and water (13.244–247), and last its name, which “has reached even to Troy” (13.248; see [[rugged]] and [[ithaca]]).
