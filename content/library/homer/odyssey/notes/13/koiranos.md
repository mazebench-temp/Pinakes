---
summary: Athena describes the suitors to Odysseus as men “who for three years now have been lording it in your hall” (13.377).
---

“Consider how you will lay hands on the shameless suitors, / who for three years now have been lording it in your hall, / wooing your godlike wife and giving bride-price” (οἳ δή τοι τρίετες μέγαρον κάτα κοιρανέουσι, 13.376–378; see [[the-suitors]], [[anaideia]] and [[bride-price]]). κοιρανέουσι is said ironically, “are lording it” (D. B. Monro, *Homer's Odyssey, Books XIII–XXIV*, 1901, on 13.377; see the Odyssey translation's table of key words, κοιρανέω (of the suitors)): the verb of a commander's rightful authority, used in the Iliad of Odysseus himself as he goes through the army “like a master” to stop its flight (*Iliad* 2.207; see [[one-ruler]]), is given to the men who have taken over his house. The three years are Antinous' own count, “For it is now the third year, and soon the fourth will pass” (2.89; see [[the-web-of-penelope]]).
