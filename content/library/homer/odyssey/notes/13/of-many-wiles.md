---
summary: Three answers to Athena on Ithaca: “Answering her, Odysseus of many wiles said” (13.311, 13.382, 13.416).
---

The verse of his answers to Calypso, Arete and Nausicaa (5.214, 7.240, 8.463), in the narrator's voice again after the four books of the tale, in which the epithet came only in the pause (11.354, 11.377). Each time he answers the goddess who has just claimed cunning for herself among the gods (13.298–299): with his doubt that the land is Ithaca (13.312–328), with his request that she weave a plan and stand beside him (13.383–391), and with his question why she did not tell his son (13.417–419; see [[answering-him]], [[metis]] and [[athena]]).
