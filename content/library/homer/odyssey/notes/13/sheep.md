summary: Athena meets Odysseus on the shore of Ithaca “in form like a young man, a herdsman of sheep, / very delicate, such as are the sons of lords” (13.221–225).
passages:
  - 13.221-235 | Athena as a young herdsman
---

**A herdsman of sheep.** “And Athena came near to him, / in form like a young man, a herdsman of sheep, / very delicate, such as are the sons of lords, / with a well-made cape folded double around her shoulders; / and under her shining feet she had sandals, and in her hands a javelin” (13.221–225; see [[athena]], [[shape-changing]] and [[odysseus-set-ashore-on-ithaca]]). ἐπιβώτωρ μήλων, “a herdsman of sheep”, is found only here. μῆλα are the flocks, sheep and goats together (see the Book 9 note), and βώτορες are herdsmen, as Odysseus' own herdsmen who graze his flocks on the mainland (14.102) and the herdsmen of the Iliad's lion simile, who keep watch over their sheep “with dogs and spears” (*Iliad* 12.302–303). The disguise has the herdsman's gear: a cape for the weather, sandals, and a javelin, ἄκων, the weapon that Eumaeus takes up at night when he goes out to sleep by his swine, “a defense against dogs and men” (14.531; see [[cloak]], [[sandals]] and [[spear]]).

**The sons of lords.** παναπάλῳ, “very delicate”, is found only here, and the herdsman is delicate “such as are the sons of lords”, οἷοί τε ἀνάκτων παῖδες ἔασι (13.223; see [[anax]]). In Homer kings' sons tend the flocks and herds. Achilles took Priam's sons Isus and Antiphus on the slopes of Ida “as they herded sheep”, and killed Andromache's seven brothers “among their shambling cattle and white sheep” (*Iliad* 11.104–106, 6.421–424); Aphrodite bore Aeneas to Anchises as he tended his cattle, and Achilles once chased Aeneas himself away from his cattle on Ida (*Iliad* 5.313, 20.188–190). The disguise suits the island too, a land of flocks, “good goat-pasturing and cattle-pasturing land”, as the herdsman tells the stranger (13.246; see the Book 13 notes on [[goats]] and [[cattle]]). Hermes met Odysseus on Circe's island in the likeness of a young man, “with the first down on his lip, when youth is at its most graceful” (10.277–279; see [[divine-epiphany]]); Athena's young man is a shepherd, and Odysseus, who does not know her, supplicates him as a god (13.228–235; see [[supplication]]).
