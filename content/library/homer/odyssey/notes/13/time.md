summary: Poseidon complains to Zeus that he will no longer “be held in honor” among the gods, since the Phaeacians, his own stock, “do not honor me at all” (13.128–130); Zeus answers that the gods do not dishonor him, and that if any man does, “vengeance is always yours” (13.141–144). Demodocus is “honored by the people” (13.28).

**The honor of a god.** Poseidon has not forgotten his threats against Odysseus, and he brings them to Zeus as a matter of honor: “Father Zeus, no longer shall I among the immortal gods / be held in honor, since mortals do not honor me at all, / the Phaeacians, who after all are sprung from my own stock” (τιμήεις ἔσομαι, ὅτε με βροτοὶ οὔ τι τίουσιν, 13.128–130; see [[poseidon]] and [[genee]]). Zeus answers: “The gods do not dishonor you at all; it would be hard / to assail the eldest and the best with acts of dishonor. / But if any man, yielding to his own force and strength, / does not honor you at all, vengeance is always yours, even hereafter” (13.141–144; see [[aristos]], [[kratos]] and [[poine]]). ἀτιμίῃσιν ἰάλλειν is “to fling at, assail, with acts of dishonour” (D. B. Monro, *Homer's Odyssey, Books XIII–XXIV*, 1901, on 13.142), and between τίει, “pays honor”, and τίσις, “payment” in the sense of vengeance, there is a play of words (Monro on 13.144): the honor that a mortal withholds from a god is exacted from him as payment.

**The wall and the ship.** The exchange repeats one in the Iliad. There Poseidon complains that the Achaeans have built their wall without giving the gods hecatombs, and that its fame will make men forget the wall he built for Laomedon; Zeus answers with the verse he uses here, “Ah, shaker of the earth, wide in strength, what a thing you have said!”, and lets him sweep the wall into the sea when the Achaeans are gone (*Iliad* 7.446–463; 13.140; see [[the-achaean-wall]]). In both the god of the sea defends his honor against a work of men, and is given leave to destroy it: the wall, and here the ship, turned to stone in sight of the city (13.154–164; see [[the-migration-of-the-phaeacians]]).

**Honored by the people.** At the farewell feast the singer is “Demodocus, honored by the people” (λαοῖσι τετιμένος, 13.28; see [[demodocus]] and [[laos]]).
