---
summary: When Athena scatters the mist, Odysseus is glad, “rejoicing in his own land, and kissed the grain-giving earth” (13.354).
---

κύσε δὲ ζείδωρον ἄρουραν is the half-verse of his landing on Scheria, when he had crawled out of the river (5.463); now it is his own island. Agamemnon too “touched his native land and kissed it” when he came home to his death (4.522; see [[nothing-sweeter-than-ones-own-land]], [[earth]] and [[ithaca]]). Then Odysseus lifts his hands and prays to the nymphs (13.355–360; see [[nymphs]]).
