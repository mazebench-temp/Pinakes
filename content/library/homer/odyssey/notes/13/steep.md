---
summary: “But when we had sacked the steep city of Priam / and gone aboard the ships, and a god scattered the Achaeans” (13.316–317).
---

Nestor's two verses on the return from Troy (3.130–131), in Odysseus' mouth; the first is also his account of Neoptolemus to Achilles, with ἀλλʼ ὅτε δή (11.533). Nestor went on to the grim homecoming that Zeus devised and to the wrath of Athena, “who set strife between the two sons of Atreus” (3.132–136); Odysseus goes on to the goddess whom he did not see again “after that” (13.318–319; see [[troy]], [[priam]] and [[the-returns-of-the-achaeans]]).
