---
summary: The Phaeacians lift out “the possessions which the noble Phaeacians / had given him as he went home, through great-hearted Athena” (13.120–121).
---

διὰ μεγάθυμον Ἀθήνην is the half-verse of Demodocus' song of the fall of Troy, where Odysseus “conquered in the end, through great-hearted Athena” (8.520; see the Book 8 note): the goddess who gave him the victory gave him the gifts. Monro renders διά “by the agency of” (D. B. Monro, *Homer's Odyssey, Books XIII–XXIV*, 1901, on 13.121), and Athena claims the gifts as her work: “all that the noble Phaeacians / gave you as you went home, by my counsel and my mind” (13.304–305). When the gifts are laid in the cave, some manuscripts have the words of 13.120–121, ending διὰ μεγάθυμον Ἀθήνην, in place of τά οἱ Φαίηκες ἔδωκαν, “which the Phaeacians had given him” (Monro's critical note on 13.369; see [[the-cave-of-the-naiads]], [[possessions]] and [[athena]]).
