---
summary: Alcinous calls on the lords who always drink “the sparkling wine of the elders” in his halls (13.8–9); Pontonous mixes the wine for the farewell libation (13.50–54); a slave woman of Arete brings bread and red wine for the voyage (13.69); and on Ithaca Athena tells the stranger that wine grows there (13.244–245).
---

**The wine of the elders.** Alcinous gives his last order to “all who in my halls drink the sparkling wine of the elders / always, and listen to the singer” (13.8–9; see [[alcinous]], [[demodocus]] and [[geron]]). γερούσιος οἶνος is the wine of the chiefs who drink at the king's table, as in the Iliad, where Agamemnon tells Idomeneus that his cup always stands full when “the sparkling wine of the elders / is mixed in the mixing bowl by the best of the Argives” (*Iliad* 4.259–263; see the translation's row γερούσιος οἶνος and [[idomeneus]]). Those who drink it are those who must now give the stranger a tripod and a cauldron each (13.13–15; see [[tripod]]).

**For the libation.** At Alcinous' bidding Pontonous mixes a bowl and serves the wine, μέθυ, to all in the hall, “and Pontonous mixed the wine sweet to the heart” (13.50–53; see [[pontonous]], [[mixing-bowl]], [[honey-sweet]] and [[libation]]), the verses of the libation on the night the stranger came (7.179–183).

**For the voyage.** “and a third brought bread and red wine” (13.69; see [[provisions]], [[red]] and [[arete-queen-of-the-phaeacians]]). Bread and red wine are what Calypso promised to put on the raft and what Circe brought to the companions back from Hades (5.165, 12.19), and as long as the companions had them on Thrinacia they kept away from the cattle of the Sun (12.327–328; see [[the-cattle-of-helios]]).

**On Ithaca.** Athena, still in the form of a young herdsman, tells the stranger of the island: “For in it grows endless grain, and wine / grows too; and there is always rain and the rich dew” (13.244–245; see [[ithaca]] and [[athena]]).
