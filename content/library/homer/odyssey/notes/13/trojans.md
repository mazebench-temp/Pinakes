---
summary: The Cretan of Odysseus' false tale fell out with Idomeneus because he would not serve under him “in the land of the Trojans, but led other companions of my own” (13.265–266).
---

“Because I would not court his father's favor by serving as his attendant / in the land of the Trojans, but led other companions of my own” (13.265–266; see [[the-first-cretan-tale]] and [[idomeneus]]). δήμῳ ἔνι Τρώων, “in the land of the Trojans”, has δῆμος in its first sense of a land and its people, as the harbor of Phorcys lies ἐν δήμῳ Ἰθάκης, “in the land of Ithaca” (13.97; see [[the-harbor-of-phorcys]] and [[demos]]). θεράπευον is “served as θεράπων”, an attendant, the place that Patroclus held beside Achilles and Meriones beside Idomeneus in the Iliad (*Iliad* 16.165, 23.113), and the negative covers χαριζόμενος, “courting his favor”, as well (D. B. Monro, *Homer's Odyssey, Books XIII–XXIV*, 1901, on 13.265; see the translation's note on 13.265 and [[meriones]]).
