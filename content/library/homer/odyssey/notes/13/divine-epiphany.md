The steps of arrival, recognition, speech and departure fill the second half of the book, and the recognition is double, of the goddess and of the land. Odysseus does not know the herdsman, and the goddess reproaches him, “yet you did not know / Pallas Athena, daughter of Zeus” (13.299–300); his answer states the difficulty of every epiphany, “It is hard, goddess, for a mortal who meets you to know you, / even one who knows a great deal; for you make yourself like anything” (13.312–313). The land is known only when she scatters the mist (13.344–352). She leaves without a farewell, in the verse in which Thetis and Zeus part in the Iliad, “When the two had made their plans, they parted” (13.439 = *Iliad* 1.531).
