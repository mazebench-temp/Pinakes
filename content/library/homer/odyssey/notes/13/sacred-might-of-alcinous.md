---
summary: The king's acts on the day of the departure: “And the sacred might of Alcinous stowed it well” (13.20), “And for them the sacred might of Alcinous sacrificed an ox / to Zeus” (13.24–25); and without the epithet, “And then the might of Alcinous spoke to the herald” (13.49) and “with him the might of Alcinous sent a herald” (13.64).
---

The bronze, εὐήνορα χαλκόν, “the bronze that makes men strong” (13.19), is stowed “under the thwarts” by the king himself going through the ship, so that it will not hinder the rowers; D. B. Monro joins ὑπὸ ζυγά with κατέθηκε (*Homer's Odyssey, Books XIII–XXIV*, 1901, on 13.21; see [[rowing-benches]] and [[possessions]]). At 13.24 Monro points to the play of sound in βοῦν ἱέρευσʼ ἱερὸν μένος, “sacrificed an ox … the sacred might,” and refers among other places to 13.144, where τίει, “honors,” and τίσις, “vengeance,” stand side by side (Monro on 13.24 and 13.144; see [[sacrifice-type-scene]]). 13.49 repeats 7.178, the order to Pontonous to mix the wine for the libation, and Pontonous obeys in the verse of 7.182 (13.53); at 13.64 the king sends the herald to lead the guest to the ship (see [[pontonous]], [[heralds]] and [[libation]]).
