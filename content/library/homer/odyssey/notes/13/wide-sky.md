---
summary: The libation before the departure: “and they poured libations / to the blessed gods, who hold the wide sky, / from where they sat” (13.54–56).
---

τοὶ οὐρανὸν εὐρὺν ἔχουσιν, the verse-end of the gods who live in heaven, as in Eurylochus' counsel to “sacrifice to the immortals, who hold the wide sky” the cattle of the Sun (12.343–344; see [[immortals]]). D. B. Monro renders αὐτόθεν ἐξ ἑδρέων “in their places,” “even as they sat,” comparing *Iliad* 19.77 (*Homer's Odyssey, Books XIII–XXIV*, 1901, on 13.56; see [[libation]]).
