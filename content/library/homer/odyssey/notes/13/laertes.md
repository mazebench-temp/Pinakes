# Λαέρτ* (in the entry) claims Λαερτιάδη (13.375).
---

διογενὲς Λαερτιάδη, πολυμήχανʼ Ὀδυσσεῦ, “Son of Laertes, sprung from Zeus, Odysseus of many devices” (13.375; see [[son-of-laertes]], [[sprung-from-zeus]], [[of-many-devices]] and [[odysseus]]), is the verse with which Athena first speaks to him in the Iliad, when she sends him to stop the flight to the ships (*Iliad* 2.173), and with which she will speak to him again when she bids him make himself known to his son and when she ends the fighting on Ithaca (16.167, 24.542). Here she begins with it the planning of the suitors' death (13.374–381; see [[athena]] and [[the-suitors]]). Laertes himself has no part in the book: Athena tells Odysseus of his wife and his son, not of his father (13.336–338, 13.379–381, 13.412–415).
