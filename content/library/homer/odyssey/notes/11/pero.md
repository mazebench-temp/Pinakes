summary: The daughter of Neleus and Chloris, “strong Pero, a marvel to mortals”, wooed by all who dwelt around; Neleus would give her only to the man who drove off the cattle of Iphiclus from Phylace, and a noble seer undertook it (11.287–297).
passages:
  - 11.287-297 | Pero and the cattle of Iphiclus
---

“And besides these she bore strong Pero, a marvel to mortals, / whom all who dwelt around were wooing” (11.287–288; see [[chloris]] and [[mighty]]): τοῖσι … ἐπί is “besides these”, her three brothers (W. W. Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 11.287). Neleus set a bride-price that was a feat: “but Neleus / would give her to no one who did not drive the broad-browed cattle with curving horns / out of Phylace, the cattle of mighty Iphiclus, / hard to drive” (11.288–291; see [[neleus]], [[iphiclus]], [[phylace]], [[broad-browed]] and [[with-curving-horns]]). The cattle were “hard to drive”, Merry explains, because they were so well guarded (on 11.291). The seer who promised to drive them is the subject of the rest of the passage (11.291–297; see [[melampus]] and [[melampus-and-the-cattle-of-iphiclus]]), and Pero is not named again; Book 15 says only that he brought “the woman” home for his brother (15.237–238).
