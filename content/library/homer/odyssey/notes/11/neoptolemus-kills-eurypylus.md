# Unnamed: the killing and the Ceteians (11.519-521).
refs: [11.519-521]
passages:
  - 11.517-522 | Eurypylus son of Telephus
---

**One name among many.** Odysseus cannot name all the men that Neoptolemus killed, in the verse with which he broke off his catalogue of women (11.517 = 11.328), “but what a man he slew with the bronze, the son of Telephus, / the hero Eurypylus, and many of his companions around him, / the Ceteians, were killed, for the sake of gifts to a woman” (11.519–521; see [[eurypylus-son-of-telephus]], [[telephus]] and [[ceteians]]). οἷον is an exclamation, “what a man” (see the translation's note on 11.519). He was the handsomest man Odysseus saw, after Memnon (11.522; see [[memnon]]).

**The gifts.** Eurypylus, Merry explains, was the son of Telephus, king of the Mysians, by Astyoche, a sister or daughter of Priam, and the last of the allies to come to the help of Troy: his mother was persuaded to send him by Priam's gift of a golden vine (W. W. Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 11.520). The same words, γυναίων εἵνεκα δώρων, “for the sake of gifts to a woman”, are said of the seer Amphiaraus, who died at Thebes because his wife was bribed (on 11.521; 15.247, Greek only; see [[alcmaeon-kills-eriphyle]] and the translation's key-word row for Κήτειοι). For the Ceteians and the doubts about their name, see [[ceteians]].
