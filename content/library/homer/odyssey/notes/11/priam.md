summary: Named twice in the heroes' scenes: Cassandra is “Priam's daughter”, whose dying voice Agamemnon heard (11.421), and Troy is “the steep city of Priam” that the Achaeans sacked (11.533).
# Πριαμ* (in the entry) claims Πριάμοιο (11.421, 11.533).
---

οἰκτροτάτην δʼ ἤκουσα ὄπα Πριάμοιο θυγατρός, “And the most pitiable voice I heard was that of Priam's daughter” (11.421; see [[cassandra]] and [[agamemnon]]): the king's daughter dies far from Troy, beside the king of the army that sacked it. And when Odysseus tells Achilles of his son, the fall of the city is Priam's: “But when we had sacked the steep city of Priam, / with his share and a noble prize he went on board his ship” (11.533–534; see [[troy]], [[steep]] and [[neoptolemus]]). Priam's death at the altar, which later poets gave to Neoptolemus, the Odyssey does not tell (see the entry).
