---
summary: Heracles “among the immortal gods / takes his delight in their feasts, and has Hebe of the lovely ankles” (11.602–603).
grc: [καλλίσφυρον]
---

καλλίσφυρον Ἥβην, in one of the three verses that were rejected in antiquity (see [[of-the-golden-sandals]]). A scholion, as Merry reports it, took Ἥβην here for Heracles' own youthful strength, not for the goddess who pours the wine, and another explained καλλίσφυρον in that sense, “with strong ankles” (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, critical notes on 11.602–603; see [[hebe]]). Hesiod gives the epithet to Alcmene in the same story (*Theogony* 950).
