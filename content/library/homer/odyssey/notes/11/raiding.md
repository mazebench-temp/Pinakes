---
summary: Odysseus guesses at Agamemnon's death: was it at sea, or did merciless men kill him on land “as you were cutting off their cattle and fine flocks of sheep, / or fighting for a city and its women?” (11.399–403); Agamemnon answers in the same words that it was neither (11.406–408). Neoptolemus came away from the sack of Troy with his share and a noble prize (11.533–534).
# διεπέρσαμεν (11.533; again 3.130, 13.316).
grc: [διεπέρσαμεν]
en: [sacked]
---

**The deaths of raiders.** The question names the two ordinary ends of a man of war, the sea and the raid: “Or did merciless men do you harm on land / as you were cutting off their cattle and fine flocks of sheep, / or fighting for a city and its women?” (11.401–403; see [[ker-doom]]). περιταμνόμενον is “cutting off”, intercepting the herds as one drives them off (W. W. Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 11.402), and for the last verse Merry prefers “fighting to take a city and carry off its women”, as he takes *Iliad* 18.265, since the foray on the cattle commends it (Merry on 11.403; see the Odyssey translation's note on 11.399–403, verses that Aristophanes athetized as carried over from Agamemnon's answer). The answer denies both: he was killed at home, at a feast (11.406–411; see [[feast]] and [[the-murder-of-agamemnon]]).

**Troy.** The greatest raid of the poem ends the story of Neoptolemus: “when we had sacked the steep city of Priam, / with his share and a noble prize he went on board his ship / unharmed” (11.533–535; see [[neoptolemus]], [[moira]] and [[geras]]), in the verse Nestor and Odysseus use of the sack (3.130; 13.316).
