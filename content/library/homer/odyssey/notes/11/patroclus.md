# Πατρόκλ* (in the entry) claims Πατροκλῆος (11.468).
---

“and there came the soul of Achilles, son of Peleus, / and of Patroclus, and of noble Antilochus” (11.467–468; see [[achilles]] and [[psyche]]). He does not speak. The friendship of these heroes, W. W. Merry notes, is resumed in the underworld, Patroclus being the dearest comrade of Achilles (*Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 11.467). In the Iliad his soul had come to Achilles in a dream to ask for burial, so that he might pass the gates of Hades (*Iliad* 23.65–92; see [[the-ghost-of-patroclus]]), and the Odyssey's last book tells that his bones lie mixed with those of Achilles in one golden urn (24.76–77, Greek only).
