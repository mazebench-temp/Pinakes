# ἐτίομεν (11.484), ἀτιμάζουσιν (11.496).
grc: [ἐτίομεν, ἀτιμάζουσιν]
en: [we honored you, dishonor him]
---

**Honor like the gods.** The sons of Leda are held alive beneath the earth, “and even beneath the earth, having honor from Zeus, / now they live on alternate days, and now again / they are dead; and they have been allotted honor like the gods” (11.301–304; see [[castor]], [[polydeuces]] and [[immortality]]). Odysseus gives the same measure to Achilles: “while you lived, we honored you equally with the gods, / we Argives, and now again you are mighty among the dead” (ἐτίομεν ἶσα θεοῖσιν, 11.484–485), and Achilles refuses it (see [[rather-a-hired-laborer]] and [[achilles]]).

**Honor kept or lost.** Achilles' first questions are about honor. He asks whether Peleus “still has honor among the many Myrmidons, / or whether they dishonor him throughout Hellas and Phthia, / because old age holds him fast in his hands and feet”, and wishes he could come back even for a little while to make his might hateful to those “who do him violence and keep him from his honor” (11.494–503; see [[peleus]] and [[geron]]). Odysseus too has asked his mother whether his “prize of honor” is still with his father and son, or whether another man holds it (11.174–176, 11.184–185; see [[geras]]). Among the living, the guest's honor is shared: Arete tells the Phaeacians that each of them “has his share of honor” (ἔμμορε τιμῆς, 11.338; see [[xenia]]).
