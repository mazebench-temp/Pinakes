summary: Agamemnon complains that his wife turned away and would not close his eyes as he went to the house of Hades (11.425–426), and that she did not let him have his fill of his son with his eyes (11.452–453); Odysseus never saw Neoptolemus weep in the horse (11.528), and Heracles knows him when he sees him with his eyes (11.615).
# ὀφθαλμοὺς (11.426), first met in the Odyssey here (the Iliad's are in its notes; again 24.296); it applies in the
# Odyssey only.
grc: [ὀφθαλμοὺς]

**The closing of the eyes.** “But she, the dog-faced one, / turned away, and did not bring herself, though I was going to the house of Hades, / to close my eyes with her hands or to shut my mouth” (11.424–426; see [[clytemnestra]], [[agamemnon]] and [[dog-face]]). To close the eyes of the dead was the last office of the nearest kin: in Book 24 Laertes, believing his son dead far from home, laments that his wife did not wail over him on his bier, “closing his eyes; for that is the due of the dead” (24.295–296, a plain rendering). Clytemnestra refused it, and Agamemnon adds that she did not let him “have my fill of my son / with my eyes” (11.452–453; see [[orestes]]).

**Seeing.** Odysseus never saw Neoptolemus with his eyes “either turning pale in his fair skin or wiping / a tear from his cheeks” in the horse (11.528–530; see [[neoptolemus]] and [[the-wooden-horse]]), and the phantom of Heracles knew him “when he saw me with his eyes” (11.615; see [[heracles]]).
