# φιλοτήσια (11.246).
grc: [φιλοτήσια]
en: [the works of love]
---

The love of a god and a mortal woman is the subject of much of the catalogue (see [[the-loves-of-zeus]]). Poseidon, in the likeness of the river Enipeus, lies with Tyro, and “when the god had finished the works of love” (φιλοτήσια ἔργα, 11.246; the Odyssey translation's table of key words), he clasps her hand and blesses her: “Rejoice in this love, lady; and as the year circles round / you will bear splendid children, for not in vain are the embraces / of the immortals” (χαῖρε, γύναι, φιλότητι, 11.248–250), χαῖρε being meant as a blessing, “be happy in this love” (W. W. Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 11.248; see [[tyro]] and [[poseidon]]).
