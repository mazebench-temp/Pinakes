summary: The murderer in Agamemnon's own account: the souls that come with the king are those who died with him “in the house of Aegisthus”, and “Aegisthus, contriving death and fate for me, / killed me, with my ruinous wife, when he had called me to his house / and feasted me” (11.389, 11.409–411).
# Αἴγισθ* (in the entry) claims Αἰγίσθοιο (11.389) and Αἴγισθος (11.409).
---

The dead king names his murderer twice: his companions' souls are those of “all who with him / had died in the house of Aegisthus and met their fate” (11.388–389), and “Aegisthus, contriving death and fate for me, / killed me, with my ruinous wife, when he had called me to his house / and feasted me, as one kills an ox at the manger” (11.409–411; see [[agamemnon]] and [[clytemnestra]]). The Homeric version, W. W. Merry notes, makes Aegisthus the murderer and a banquet the scene, where Aeschylus and Euripides have Clytemnestra kill her husband in the bath; but even here she is his accomplice (*Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 11.409; see [[the-murder-of-agamemnon]] and [[the-murder-of-agamemnon-in-tragedy]]). Proteus had told Menelaus of the same feast, with the ambush of twenty men that Aegisthus set beside it, and the verse of the ox at the manger is his (4.529–537; 11.411 = 4.535). Agamemnon says nothing of Aegisthus' end; he asks only whether Orestes is still alive (11.457–461; see [[orestes]]).
