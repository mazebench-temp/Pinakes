summary: “there came the soul of Achilles, son of Peleus” (11.467); Ajax was best in looks “after the noble son of Peleus” (11.470, 11.551); Odysseus greets him, “Achilles, son of Peleus, by far the strongest of the Achaeans” (11.478); and the Achaeans grieve for Ajax “as much as for the head of Achilles, son of Peleus” (11.557).
grc: ["Πηλῆος υἱέ", Πηληϊάδαο]
---

Πηλῆος υἱέ, the vocative of the Iliad's address (11.478, as *Iliad* 16.21, 19.216), and Πηληϊάδαο are new to the entry. Merry reads Πηλέος at 11.478, scanned as a spondee, where Bekker and Ameis read Πηλῆος, as Murray does; with Πηλῆος the first syllable of υἱέ must be short (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 11.478 and his critical note; see the translation's note on 11.476, 11.478, 11.483). Achilles asks for news of his father, whom no one now protects (11.494–503; see [[peleus]] and [[achilles]]).
