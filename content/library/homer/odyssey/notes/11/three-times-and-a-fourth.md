---
summary: Odysseus tries to clasp the soul of his mother: “Three times I sprang toward her, and my heart bade me clasp her, / and three times she flew from my hands like a shadow or a dream” (11.206–207).
grc: ["τρὶς μὲν ἐφωρμήθην", "τρὶς δέ μοι ἐκ χειρῶν"]
en: [Three times I sprang toward her, three times she flew from my hands]
---

The rhythm of the Iliad's three attempts, without the fourth: the third failure is the last, and it brings no god's shout, only “sharp grief” (11.208). Merry notes that the iterative γενέσκετο suits τρίς, each failure bringing its own sorrow, and sets beside the scene Achilles' wish to embrace the soul of Patroclus, where they should “take our fill” of lament as here (*Iliad* 23.97–98; 11.211–212), and Virgil's three embraces of Aeneas and Creusa (*Aeneid* 2.792–794, as he quotes it; Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 11.206, 11.208, 11.211; see [[meeting-with-the-dead]] and [[psyche]]).
