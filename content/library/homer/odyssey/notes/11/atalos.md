---
summary: Among the souls at the pit come “tender maidens with hearts new to sorrow” (11.39).
grc: [ἀταλαὶ]
---

παρθενικαί τʼ ἀταλαί: παρθενική is used here, as in the vintage scene of the Iliad (*Iliad* 18.567), for παρθένος, “maiden.” Merry calls the etymology of ἀταλαί very uncertain, and connects it with ἀτάλλω, “to leap or skip like a young animal” (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 11.39; see [[new-to-sorrow]] and [[who-had-borne-much]]).
