---
summary: Named twice in the catalogue of heroines, as the husband of Alcmene, “the wife of Amphitryon” (11.266), and in the title of Heracles, “the son of Amphitryon”, whose wife was Megara (11.270).
---

Ἀλκμήνην … Ἀμφιτρύωνος ἄκοιτιν, “Alcmene, the wife of Amphitryon” (11.266; see [[alcmene]]), and τὴν ἔχεν Ἀμφιτρύωνος υἱὸς μένος αἰὲν ἀτειρής, “whom the son of Amphitryon, ever unyielding in his might, had as wife” (11.270; see [[megara-wife-of-heracles]], [[heracles]] and [[unyielding]]). The same three lines that call Heracles the son of Amphitryon say that Alcmene bore him “in the arms of great Zeus” (11.268), as the Iliad gives him both fathers in one speech (*Iliad* 5.392, 5.396).
