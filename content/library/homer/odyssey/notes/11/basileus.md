---
summary: Tyro is “the queen among women” (11.258), Chloris “was queen in Pylos” (11.285), and Echeneus calls Arete “our prudent queen” (11.345).
---

Three queens. Tyro, who bore sons to Poseidon and to Cretheus, is βασίλεια γυναικῶν, “the queen among women” (11.258; see [[tyro]] and the Odyssey translation's table of key words). Chloris became queen by her marriage to Neleus: ἡ δὲ Πύλου βασίλευε, “she was queen in Pylos”, Πύλου being a genitive of place, since βασιλεύω is regularly intransitive (11.285; W. W. Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 11.285; see [[chloris]] and [[pylos]]). And in the pause of the tale Echeneus tells the Phaeacians that “our prudent queen” has spoken well, though deed and word depend on Alcinous (11.344–346; see [[arete-queen-of-the-phaeacians]] and [[echeneus]]).
