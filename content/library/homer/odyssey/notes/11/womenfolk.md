summary: The heroines are “the souls of the womenfolk” scattered by Persephone (11.385–386); and Agamemnon says that Clytemnestra has poured shame on herself and “on those to come hereafter, / the womenfolk, even on one who does good” (11.433–434).
grc: [θηλυτεράων, θηλυτέρῃσι]
---

γυναικῶν θηλυτεράων and θηλυτέρῃσι γυναιξί are new to the entry; the second verse comes again in the Odyssey (15.422, 24.202). Merry, after Lobeck, finds no quality like “fair” or “tender” in the word: it marks off the one sex from the other with a naive emphasis, the ending -τερος helping the contrast, and means nothing but “female” (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 11.386; see [[hagnos]] and [[clytemnestra]]).
