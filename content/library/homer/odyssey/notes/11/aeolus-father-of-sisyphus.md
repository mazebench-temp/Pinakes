summary: Named in the patronymic of Tyro's husband, “Cretheus, son of Aeolus” (11.237), the Aeolus of the genealogies, not the keeper of the winds of Book 10.
# Αἰολίδαο (11.237), the patronymic of Cretheus, counts for the father as well; it applies in the Odyssey only, since
# in the Iliad "son of Aeolus" is Sisyphus (Il. 6.154, Αἰολίδης, claimed by the entry). cretheus claims it too.
grc: [Αἰολίδαο]

φῆ δὲ Κρηθῆος γυνὴ ἔμμεναι Αἰολίδαο, “and said she was the wife of Cretheus, son of Aeolus” (11.237; see [[tyro]] and [[cretheus]]). The patronymic that the Iliad gives Sisyphus, Αἰολίδης (*Iliad* 6.154; see [[sisyphus]]), here marks Cretheus as his brother, and Tyro, the daughter of Salmoneus, as the wife of her father's brother, if Salmoneus is a son of the same Aeolus, as the Hesiodic *Catalogue* makes him (see [[salmoneus]]). The keeper of the winds of Book 10, Aeolus son of Hippotes, is another figure (see [[aeolus]]); Sisyphus himself appears later in the book, heaving his stone (11.593–600).
