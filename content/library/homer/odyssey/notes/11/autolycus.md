# Αὐτόλυκ* (in the entry) claims Αὐτολύκου (11.85).
---

Αὐτολύκου θυγάτηρ μεγαλήτορος Ἀντίκλεια, “Anticleia, daughter of great-hearted Autolycus” (11.85; see [[anticleia]] and [[great-hearted]]): the verse names the mother of Odysseus by her father, his grandfather, and it is the first time the Odyssey names either. W. W. Merry's note gathers what the poem says of him later: he lived on Parnassus with his sons, surpassed all men in thievery and the oath by the gift of Hermes, and gave his grandson his name (19.394–409, Greek only); later writers, he adds, made him a son of Hermes by Chione or Philonis (*Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 11.85; see [[odysseus]] and [[hermes]]). The Iliad names him once, as the thief who broke into Amyntor's close-built house (*Iliad* 10.266–267; see [[the-boars-tusk-helmet]]).
