---
summary: Named last of the three sons whom Tyro bore to Cretheus, “Amythaon, who fought from his chariot” (11.259), his only mention in Homer.
---

ἱππιοχάρμης, “who fought from his chariot”, is given to Troilus in the Iliad (*Iliad* 24.257; see [[hippiocharmes]]). Thirty lines later the catalogue tells of the seer who undertook to drive the cattle of Iphiclus for Neleus' daughter (11.291–297), whom *Odyssey* 15 names as Melampus and later writers call Amythaon's son (see [[melampus]]).
