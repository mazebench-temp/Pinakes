summary: Persephone is “noble” three times: Odysseus asks whether the soul of his mother is “some phantom that noble Persephone / has urged on toward me” (11.213–214); the women come, “for noble Persephone urged them on” (11.226); and he fears the Gorgon's head that “noble Persephone should send against me out of Hades” (11.635). Achilles asks for word “of my noble son” (11.492).
grc: [ἀγαυὴ]
---

ἀγαυὴ Περσεφόνεια is a formula of this book alone in Homer, and its form is new to the entry; in the Iliad the same word is the name of a Nereid (*Iliad* 18.42), which the entry does not claim. The queen of the dead is the one who sends: a phantom, as Odysseus fears, the souls of the women, and the Gorgon's head (see [[persephone]], [[eidolon]] and [[gorgon]]); she is also “dread” and “holy” in the book (11.47, 11.386; see [[dread]] and [[hagnos]]). τοῦ παιδὸς ἀγαυοῦ is Achilles' son Neoptolemus, “that noble son of mine,” as Merry renders it (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 11.492; see [[neoptolemus]]).
