---
summary: Teiresias: the suitors are “wooing your godlike wife” (11.117); the mother of the Aloadae bore “godlike Otus and far-famed Ephialtes” (11.308); Alcinous asks whether Odysseus saw “any of your godlike companions” (11.371); and Odysseus tells Achilles that “godlike Nestor and I alone surpassed” Neoptolemus in counsel (11.512).
---

ἀντιθέην, of Penelope, and ἀντιθέων, of the companions who died at Troy, are new to the entry (see [[penelope]], [[otus]] and [[nestor]]). The word is the praise of heroes remembered, and the companions about whom Alcinous asks are the dead of Troy (11.371–372; see [[the-tale-to-alcinous]]).
