---
summary: At the pit Odysseus pours the drink offering to all the dead and sprinkles white barley meal over it, as Circe bade him (11.26–28).
---

“And around it poured a drink offering to all the dead, / first with honey and milk, then with sweet wine, / and the third time with water; and I sprinkled white barley meal over it” (11.26–28; see [[the-offering-to-the-dead]], [[honey]], [[milk]], [[wine]] and [[white]]). The verses carry out Circe's instructions almost word for word (10.518–520): ἄλφιτα λευκὰ πάλυνον answers her παλύνειν. The same words in Book 14 are Eumaeus' sprinkling of barley meal over the meat he sets before his guest, and he sprinkles it over the first portions he throws into the fire for the gods (14.77, 14.429); here it is given to the dead.
