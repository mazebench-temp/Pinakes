---
summary: Odysseus begs the dead as Circe bade him, “And when with vows and prayers the tribes of the dead / I had begged” (11.34–35), and at the end of his tale “the countless tribes of the dead gathered / with a wondrous din” (11.632–633).
grc: ["ἔθνεʼ ἀγείρετο μυρία νεκρῶν"]
---

The phrase opens the rite and closes the tale. At the beginning the tribes are begged “with vows and prayers,” εὐχωλῇσι λιτῇσί τε (11.34; see [[prayer]] and [[the-strengthless-heads]]); at the end they come of themselves, μυρία, “countless,” before Odysseus can see Theseus and Peirithous, and pale fear takes him lest Persephone send the Gorgon's head against him (11.630–635; see [[pale-fear]], [[wondrous]] and [[gorgon]]). Merry renders “but, ere that might be, up gathered the countless tribes of dead with awful cry,” taking ἐπὶ … ἀγείρετο together (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 11.632).
