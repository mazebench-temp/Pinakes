---
summary: Leto is named twice in Book 11: as the mother of the son of Zeus who killed the sons of Aloeus, “whom Leto of the lovely hair bore” (11.318), and as the goddess whom Tityus assaulted, “Leto, the glorious wife of Zeus, / as she went to Pytho through Panopeus with its lovely dancing floors” (11.580–581).
---

**The mother of Apollo.** The giant sons of Aloeus would have piled the mountains on Olympus, “but the son of Zeus, whom Leto of the lovely hair bore, destroyed / them both” (11.318–319; see [[apollo]], [[otus]], [[ephialtes]] and [[lovely-haired]]). The half-verse is the Iliad's, with τόν for ὅν, of the god to whom Chryses prays, “to lord Apollo, whom Leto of the lovely hair bore” (*Iliad* 1.36).

**Tityus' crime.** Odysseus sees Tityus, “the son of glorious Earth”, stretched over nine plethra while two vultures tear his liver, and he gives the reason: “for he had dragged off Leto, the glorious wife of Zeus, / as she went to Pytho through Panopeus with its lovely dancing floors” (11.576–581; see [[tityus]], [[pytho]] and [[panopeus]]). Merry renders ἕλκησε “laid violent hands on”, comparing the slave women dragged shamefully about the house (16.108) and the dragging away of Andromache and of Priam's daughters in the Iliad (*Iliad* 6.465, 22.62; Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 11.580). κυδρὴν παράκοιτιν, “the glorious wife”, struck him as somewhat inappropriate to Leto, since it is the distinctive title of Hera, whom Iris names so in the Iliad, “Hera sent me, the glorious wife of Zeus” (*Iliad* 18.184; Merry on 11.581; see [[kydros]] and [[hera]]). Zeus himself counts “glorious Leto” among the loves he names to Hera (*Iliad* 14.327; see [[zeus]] and [[the-loves-of-zeus]]).
