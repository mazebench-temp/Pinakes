---
summary: Named in his grandson's patronymic, “the swift-footed grandson of Aeacus”, when the soul of Achilles knows Odysseus and when it strides away glad (11.471, 11.538).
# Αἰακίδαο (11.471, 11.538) is claimed by the entry; achilles claims it in its Book 11 note.
---

ἔγνω δὲ ψυχή με ποδώκεος Αἰακίδαο, “And the soul of the swift-footed grandson of Aeacus knew me” (11.471), and ψυχὴ δὲ ποδώκεος Αἰακίδαο / φοίτα μακρὰ βιβᾶσα, “the soul of the swift-footed grandson of Aeacus / went ranging with long strides” (11.538–539; see [[achilles]], [[swift-footed]] and [[grandson-of-aeacus]]). The formula, which the Iliad uses often, frames the meeting of Odysseus and Achilles in the land of the dead; these are the only two places where the Odyssey names Aeacus, in the patronymic of his grandson (see [[peleus]]).
