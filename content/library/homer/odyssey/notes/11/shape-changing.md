---
summary: Poseidon lies with Tyro “in his likeness”, the likeness of the river Enipeus whom she loved, and only afterward names himself (11.241–252).
refs: [11.241, 11.252]
---

Tyro loved the river Enipeus and walked often by its streams; “in his likeness the earth-holder, the shaker of the earth, / lay with her at the mouth of the eddying river”, and a wave arched over them like a mountain and hid the god and the mortal woman (τῷ δʼ ἄρα εἰσάμενος, 11.238–244; see [[tyro]], [[enipeus]] and [[poseidon]]). The god takes the form of the river god, as Merry explains τῷ (W. W. Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 11.241), and reveals himself only when the union is over: “but I, you must know, am Poseidon the earth-shaker” (11.252). The sleep he sheds on her may have been meant to give him time to resume his own form (Merry on 11.245, reporting Hayman; see [[hypnos]] and [[eidos]]).
