summary: The souls gather at the pit “with a wondrous cry” and at the end “with a wondrous din” (11.43, 11.633); in the pause Alcinous asks Odysseus to “go on telling me of wondrous doings” (11.374); and on Heracles' baldric “wondrous works were wrought” (11.610).
grc: [θέσκελα]
---

θεσπεσίῃ ἰαχῇ and ἠχῇ θεσπεσίῃ are two nouns for the noise of the dead, kept apart in the translation as “cry” and “din”; the second is the din with which the Trojans and Hector pour their missiles on Nestor and Diomedes in the Iliad (*Iliad* 8.158–159; see the Odyssey translation's formulas, and [[pale-fear]]). θέσκελα ἔργα, the “wondrous doings” that Iris calls Helen to see on the plain (*Iliad* 3.130), are what the king wants to hear more of from his guest, and then the works of the craftsman on the strap of Heracles (see [[the-tale-to-alcinous]] and [[heracles]]).
