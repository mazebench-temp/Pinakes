---
summary: In the pause of the tale Arete claims the stranger as her own guest, “though each of you has his share of honor” (11.338), and asks for more gifts; Alcinous keeps “our guest” one more day to complete his present (11.350–352).
---

Arete's “he is my guest” (ξεῖνος δʼ αὖτʼ ἐμός ἐστιν, 11.338) claims him as hers especially, because he made his first appeal to her, and grants that each of the Phaeacians has a share in the honor of entertaining him and treating him well (W. W. Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 11.338; see [[arete-queen-of-the-phaeacians]] and [[supplication]]); Nausicaa had sent him to her mother (6.310–315; see [[quick-to-anger]]). So the Phaeacians are not to hurry him away nor cut short the gifts for one in such need, since they have many possessions “by the will of the gods” (11.339–341): κολούετε means limiting the gifts to fewer than they would give if he stayed, not withdrawing those already promised (Merry on 11.339–340; see [[doron]] and [[possessions]]). Alcinous makes the guest bear one more day, “much as he is in need of his homecoming”, until he completes the whole present (11.350–352; see [[pompe]] and [[nostos]]).
