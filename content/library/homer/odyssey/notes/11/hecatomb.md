---
summary: Teiresias bids Odysseus, when he has appeased Poseidon far inland, go home and offer “holy hecatombs / to the immortal gods, who hold the wide sky; / to all of them in order” (11.132–134).
---

The hecatombs end the prophecy of Teiresias: after the ram, the bull and the boar for Poseidon (11.130–131; see [[sacrifice]] and [[poseidon]]), Odysseus is to honor the other gods at home, “to all of them in order” (πᾶσι μάλʼ ἑξείης, 11.132–134; see [[the-prophecy-of-teiresias]]). 11.133 is the verse of Proteus' command to Menelaus, who must go back to the river Aegyptus and offer holy hecatombs to the immortal gods before they will grant him his journey home (4.477–480; 11.133 = 4.479; see [[menelaus]]). The text prints ἱερᾶς, a genitive singular, where the same verse has the accusative plural ἱερὰς at 23.279, as at 3.144 and 4.478: a slip, left as printed (see the Odyssey translation's note on 11.132).
