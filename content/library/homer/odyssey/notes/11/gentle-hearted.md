summary: Anticleia: “but longing for you, and for your counsels, shining Odysseus, / and for your gentleness, robbed me of my honey-sweet life” (11.202–203).
grc: [ἀγανοφροσύνη]
en: [gentleness]
---

σή τʼ ἀγανοφροσύνη, the noun of the entry's adjective, which in the Iliad is the gentleness that Helen remembers in Hector (*Iliad* 24.772). Merry renders “longing for thee, and [for] thy counsels and [for] thy tenderness robbed me of sweet life,” taking σός, σή as standing for the objective genitive, “longing for thee” (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 11.202; see the translation's note on 11.202–203, and [[pothe]] and [[gentle-arrows]]).
