---
summary: The blood of the victims runs into the pit, “and the dark blood flowed” (11.36), and the souls must drink it before they can speak with Odysseus: Teiresias drinks it before he prophesies (11.98), his mother “came and drank the dark blood; and at once she knew me” (11.153), the women gather “about the dark blood” and drink one by one (11.228–234), and Agamemnon “knew me at once, when he had drunk the dark blood” (11.390).
---

αἷμα κελαινεφές twice (11.36, 11.153) and αἷμα κελαινόν four times (11.98, 11.228, 11.232, 11.390): the book uses both of the blood at the pit. Teiresias gives the rule: “Whichever of the dead who have died you let / come near the blood will tell you truly; / but whoever you begrudge it to will go back again” (11.147–149). The tale does not keep it strictly. Elpenor speaks without the blood, and Teiresias knows Odysseus and speaks to him before he drinks (11.91); Merry concludes that the drinking gave the seer neither the power to recognize nor the power to speak, and that the blood was craved as a drink offering to the dead, as Eustathius says that Teiresias gives his prophecy in return for the drink; he compares Neoptolemus' prayer that his dead father come and drink the black blood of Polyxena (Euripides, *Hecuba* 534–537; Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 11.96). Of Achilles the tale says only that he knew Odysseus, and Heracles knows him by sight (11.471, 11.615; see [[meeting-with-the-dead]], [[the-offering-to-the-dead]] and [[psyche]]).
