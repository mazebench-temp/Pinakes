---
summary: Teiresias' first words: “You seek a honey-sweet homecoming, shining Odysseus” (11.100); and Anticleia's last: longing for her son “robbed me of my honey-sweet life” (11.203).
---

νόστος μελιηδής is a phrase of this book (see the Odyssey translation's key words), and the god who will make the homecoming “grievous” follows at once (11.101; see [[nostos]]). μελιηδέα θυμὸν ἀπηύρα is the Iliad's verse-end of killing in battle, of Rhesus, the thirteenth man whom Diomedes killed in the Thracian camp (*Iliad* 10.495); here it is longing that takes the life away (see [[pothe]]).
