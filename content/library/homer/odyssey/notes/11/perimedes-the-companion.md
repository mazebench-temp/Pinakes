# Περιμήδης (11.23) and the English "Perimedes" are claimed by the entry.
---

“There Perimedes and Eurylochus held / the victims; and I drew the sharp sword from beside my thigh” (11.23–24; see [[eurylochus]], [[odysseus]] and [[the-offering-to-the-dead]]). ἔσχον is “set” or “held fast”, the victims that had been brought being made ready for the sacrifice (W. W. Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 11.24): the ram and the black ewe that Circe had tied by the ship (10.571–572; see [[ram]] and [[sheep]]). Perimedes and Eurylochus, Merry notes, are the two most important of Odysseus' companions, and the poem names them together again at the Sirens (12.195, Greek only); Eurylochus had led the second band on Aeaea (10.205; Merry on 11.23). He never speaks in the poem.
