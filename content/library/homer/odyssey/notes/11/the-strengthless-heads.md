---
summary: Odysseus tells how he did as Circe bade him: “And I earnestly implored the strengthless heads of the dead” (11.29), and, sword in hand, “did not let the strengthless heads of the dead / come near the blood until I had questioned Teiresias” (11.49–50).
---

Circe's instructions come back in the first person (10.521–525, 10.535–537). With the first verse goes the vow of a barren heifer for all the dead when he comes home to Ithaca, and of a black sheep for Teiresias alone (11.29–33); the second is spoken as he sits at the pit with the sharp sword drawn, and its last verse returns when his mother comes and he will not let her near the blood before the seer (11.48–50, 11.88–89; see [[sharp]], [[teiresias]] and [[the-offering-to-the-dead]]).
