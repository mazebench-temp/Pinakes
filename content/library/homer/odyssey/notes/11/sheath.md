summary: At Teiresias' word Odysseus draws back from the pit and thrusts his silver-studded sword into its sheath, so that the seer may drink of the blood (11.95–98).
# κουλεῷ (11.98), first met in the Odyssey here; it applies in the Odyssey only. The Book 10 note has κολεῷ.
grc: [κουλεῷ]
---

“So he spoke, and I drew back and thrust my silver-studded sword / into its sheath” (11.97–98; see [[sword]], [[silver-studded]] and [[teiresias]]). κουλεός is the lengthened form of κολεός that the verse needs, as in Achilles' sheathing of his sword in *Iliad* 1 (*Iliad* 1.220). In Book 10 it was Circe who bade him sheathe it (10.333).
