summary: “Far-famed Ephialtes” is the second of Iphimedeia's two giant sons, who with his brother Otus threatened the gods with war, meant to pile Ossa on Olympus and Pelion on Ossa, and was killed by the son of Zeus and Leto before his beard grew (11.305–320).

---

“Godlike Otus and far-famed Ephialtes” (11.308): the Iliad named him κρατερός, “mighty” (*Iliad* 5.385), and the Odyssey gives him τηλεκλειτός, the epithet of the Trojans' allies in the Iliad (*Iliad* 6.111; see [[famous]]) and of Icarius, Penelope's father, in a later book (19.546). Nothing is told of him apart from his brother: the two are the tallest and handsomest men the earth ever reared, they threaten the gods and mean to scale the sky, and the son of Zeus and Leto kills them both before the down covers their cheeks (11.309–320; see [[the-aloadae-threaten-olympus]], the Book 11 notes on [[otus]] and [[iphimedeia]], and [[apollo]]). W. W. Merry explains the name from the threshing floor, as the one who tosses the grain for the wind to winnow, or from the leaping on the grapes in the wine-press (*Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 11.307; see the general article).
