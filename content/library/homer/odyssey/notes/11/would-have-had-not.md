summary: The sons of Aloeus “would have accomplished it, if they had reached the measure of youth; / but the son of Zeus … destroyed / them both” (11.317–319); the angry Ajax “would still have spoken to me, or I to him; / but the heart in my own breast wished / to see the souls of the others” (11.565–567); and Odysseus “would have seen still more men of earlier times, … / but before that the countless tribes of the dead gathered” (11.630–632).
grc: ["ἔνθα χʼ ὅμως προσέφη"]
---

The book has the turn three times, and only the first is the Iliad's kind, in which a god stops what would have happened: Apollo kills Otus and Ephialtes before they can pile Ossa on Olympus and Pelion on Ossa (11.313–320; see [[son-of-zeus]]). In the other two the narrator turns back his own tale: his wish to see more of the dead cuts off the talk with Ajax, and the throng of the dead cuts off his wish (see [[the-tribes-of-the-dead]]). The lines on Ajax (11.565–567) were suspected, since ὅμως is found only here in the Odyssey (see the translation's note on 11.565–567).
