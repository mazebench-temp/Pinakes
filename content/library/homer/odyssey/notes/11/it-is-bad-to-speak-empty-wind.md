---
summary: Odysseus gives the soul of Agamemnon the phantom's answer: he does not know whether Orestes is alive or dead, “and it is a bad thing to speak empty wind” (11.463–464).
---

Agamemnon has asked whether his son lives, at Orchomenus, at Pylos or at Sparta, “for brilliant Orestes has not yet died upon the earth” (11.457–461; see [[orestes]] and [[agamemnon]]); a verse athetized “for its silliness”, since if he were sure Orestes lived he would not ask (Merry's critical note on 11.461). Odysseus answers: “Son of Atreus, why do you ask me this? I do not know at all / whether he is alive or dead; and it is a bad thing to speak empty wind” (11.463–464; see [[anemolios]]). All he means, Merry says, is that he has no certain knowledge, and so will not waste time in talk (W. W. Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 11.464). The father does not learn that his son has avenged him; the hearers of the tale know it from Books 1, 3 and 4 (see [[the-murder-of-agamemnon]]).
