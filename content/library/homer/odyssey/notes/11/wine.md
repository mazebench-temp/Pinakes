---
summary: Sweet wine is the second of the drink offerings poured for the dead (11.27), and Elpenor's soul blames “an evil fate from a god … and endless wine” for his fall from Circe's roof (11.61).
---

**At the pit.** “First with honey and milk, then with sweet wine, / and the third time with water” (11.27–28; see [[the-offering-to-the-dead]], [[honey]] and [[milk]]).

**Elpenor's wine.** The first of the dead to come is the companion who fell from Circe's roof, and he gives two causes: ἆσέ με δαίμονος αἶσα κακὴ καὶ ἀθέσφατος οἶνος, “an evil fate from a god led me into blind folly, and endless wine” (11.61; see [[elpenor]], [[aisa]] and [[ate]]). ἀθέσφατος is “endless”, as of the Iliad's rain (*Iliad* 3.4), and δαίμονος is “some evil power”, W. W. Merry notes, said quite vaguely and not of Circe (*Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 11.61; see the translation's row δαίμονος αἶσα κακή). Book 10 had told how he lay down on the roof “sodden with wine” (10.555; see [[wine-sodden]]); here he joins the god's fate and the wine, as Agamemnon blames “Zeus and Fate and the Erinys who walks in darkness” for the blind folly they cast into his mind (*Iliad* 19.86–88).
