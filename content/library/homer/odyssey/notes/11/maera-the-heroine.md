**The last verse.** Μαῖράν τε Κλυμένην τε ἴδον στυγερήν τʼ Ἐριφύλην (11.326; see [[clymene-the-heroine]], [[eriphyle]] and [[catalogue-of-heroines]]). Maera and Clymene have only their names; Eriphyle has an epithet and a verse of her own. Then Odysseus breaks off: “But all of them I could not tell or name, / all the wives and daughters of heroes that I saw; / for before that even the immortal night would wane” (11.328–330). W. W. Merry notes that with Maera, Clymene and Eriphyle the catalogue returns to the Boeotian legends from which the Attic names of Phaedra, Procris and Ariadne had turned it, and he quotes Pherecydes for a story that makes her the mother of Locrus, a founder of Thebes with Amphion and Zethus (*Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 11.326; see the general article and [[thebes]]). The pair of names has a twin in the Iliad's catalogue of the Nereids, where a Clymene and a Maera stand in successive verses (*Iliad* 18.47–48; see [[maera]] and [[clymene-the-nereid]]).
