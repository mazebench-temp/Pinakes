summary: Poseidon is “angry because you blinded his dear son” (11.103); Ajax's soul stands apart, “still angry because of the victory” over the arms of Achilles (11.544), and Odysseus asks whether even in death he will not forget his anger (11.553–555); “angry as he was”, he would still have spoken (11.565).
# κεχολωμένη (11.544), χόλου (11.554).
grc: [κεχολωμένη, χόλου]
en: [angry, anger]
---

**Poseidon.** Teiresias explains the god's grudge: “you will escape the shaker of the earth, since he has laid up a grudge against you in his heart, / angry because you blinded his dear son” (χωόμενος, 11.102–103; see [[poseidon]], [[kotos]] and [[odysseus-and-the-cyclops]]).

**Ajax.** The anger of the dead does not end. The soul of Ajax “stood apart, still angry because of the victory / that I won over him” (κεχολωμένη, 11.543–545; see [[ajax]] and [[the-judgment-of-arms]]), and Odysseus asks, “were you not then / even in death to forget your anger against me over the armor, / the ruinous armor?” (χόλου εἵνεκα τευχέων, 11.553–555), and begs him to tame his might and his proud spirit (11.562; see [[menos]]). Ajax answers nothing (11.563). The next verse, “There, angry as he was, he would still have spoken to me, or I to him” (κεχολωμένος, 11.565), is suspect: ὅμως, “still”, is found only here in the Odyssey, and Merry takes the interpolation that follows to begin with it (W. W. Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 11.565; see the Odyssey translation's note on 11.565–567).
