---
summary: εὐχωλή is the vow that goes with the prayers to the dead (11.34), and εὔχομαι is a woman's claim: Antiope “claimed that she had slept even in the arms of Zeus” (11.261).
---

**The vow.** Odysseus begs the tribes of the dead “with vows and prayers” (εὐχωλῇσι λιτῇσί τε, 11.34): the vows are the ones he has just made, a barren heifer for the dead and a black sheep for Teiresias (11.29–33; see [[prayer]] and [[the-offering-to-the-dead]]).

**The claim.** In the catalogue of women each speaks of her own birth and her lovers, and the verbs are verbs of saying: Tyro “said” she was the offspring of Salmoneus and the wife of Cretheus (φάτο, φῆ, 11.236–237), Iphimedeia “declared” that she had lain with Poseidon (φάσκε, 11.306), and Antiope “claimed that she had slept even in the arms of Zeus” (Διὸς εὔχετʼ ἐν ἀγκοίνῃσιν ἰαῦσαι, 11.261). Her boast, as Merry puts it, was that she had a lover higher than Poseidon himself, even Zeus, and he compares Hera, of whom Aphrodite says, “for you sleep in the arms of Zeus, the greatest of all” (*Iliad* 14.213; W. W. Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 11.261 and 11.305; see [[antiope]], [[tyro]], [[iphimedeia]] and [[the-loves-of-zeus]]).
