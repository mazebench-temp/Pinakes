---
summary: The words of the book: the dead and the living speak “with winged words” (11.56, 11.154, 11.209, 11.396, 11.472, 11.616) and sit or stand “exchanging hateful words” (11.81, 11.465); Teiresias gives “an easy word” (11.146); Alcinous finds in Odysseus “beauty of words” (11.367); and Odysseus speaks to Ajax “with soothing words” and gets no answer (11.552–563).
---

**At the pit.** The meetings are named by their manner of speech. Odysseus addresses Elpenor, his mother and Agamemnon “with winged words”, and his mother, Achilles and Heracles answer “lamenting” with winged words (11.56, 11.209, 11.396; 11.154, 11.472, 11.616; see [[winged-words]] and [[pity]]). With Elpenor and with Agamemnon he sits or stands “exchanging hateful words” (ἐπέεσσιν … στυγεροῖσιν, 11.81, 11.465): Merry renders στυγεροῖσιν “lamentable” here, and the translation keeps “hateful” as everywhere (W. W. Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 11.81; see the Odyssey translation's note on 11.81–82 and its table of formulas). Teiresias' “easy word”, ῥηΐδιον ἔπος, is a simple rule for holding or refusing communication with the dead (11.146; Merry on 11.146; see [[the-offering-to-the-dead]]). To Ajax Odysseus speaks “with soothing words”, and asks him to come and hear “my word / and speech” (11.552, 11.561–562; see [[soothing-words]] and [[ajax]]); he answers nothing (11.563; see [[silence]]).

**Among the Phaeacians.** Echeneus reminds the Phaeacians that on Alcinous “depend both deed and word” (11.346; see [[word-or-deed]]), and Alcinous confirms that “This word shall indeed be so” (11.348). His praise of the teller sets words against lies: “but you have beauty of words, and within you are good wits” (σοὶ δʼ ἔπι μὲν μορφὴ ἐπέων, 11.367; see [[mythos]], [[eperopeuo]] and [[the-tale-to-alcinous]]). Poseidon, when he has lain with Tyro, “spoke to her, and called her by name” (11.247; see [[called-him-by-name]]).
