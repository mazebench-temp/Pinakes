# Εὐρυλοχ* (in the entry) claims Εὐρύλοχος (11.23).
---

“There Perimedes and Eurylochus held / the victims; and I drew the sharp sword from beside my thigh” (11.23–24; see [[perimedes-the-companion]], [[odysseus]] and [[the-offering-to-the-dead]]). The man who stayed outside Circe's house and tried to hold the crew back from it (10.231–273, 10.429–448) is now one of the two who serve at the rite, and Perimedes and Eurylochus, W. W. Merry notes, are the two most important of Odysseus' companions (*Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 11.23). The rest of the rite falls to the companions as a body, whom Odysseus bids flay and burn the sheep and pray to Hades and Persephone (11.44–47), and whom he orders back on board at the end (11.636–638; see [[hetairos]]).
