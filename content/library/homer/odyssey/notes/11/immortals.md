summary: Hecatombs “to the immortal gods, who hold the wide sky” (11.133); Poseidon to Tyro, “for not in vain are the embraces / of the immortals” (11.249–250); the sons of Aloeus “even threatened the immortals on Olympus” (11.313); Heracles “among the immortal gods / takes his delight in their feasts” (11.602–603); and the tale is longer than “the immortal night” (11.330).
grc: ["φθῖτʼ ἄμβροτος"]
---

νὺξ ἄμβροτος, “the immortal night,” is kept apart from the ambrosial night (ἀμβροσίη νύξ) in the translation (see the Odyssey translation's key words, and [[ambrosial]]): Odysseus breaks off the catalogue of the heroines, since even the night would come to an end before he could name them all (11.328–330). ἀθανάτοισι θεοῖσι, τοὶ οὐρανὸν εὐρὺν ἔχουσι is a verse of Proteus' advice to Menelaus (11.133 = 4.479; see [[wide-sky]]).
