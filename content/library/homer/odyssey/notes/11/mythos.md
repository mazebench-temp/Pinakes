---
summary: Alcinous praises the tale: “you have told your tale skillfully, as a singer does” (11.368), and asks to hear more (11.370–376); Odysseus answers that “there is a time for many words, and a time also for sleep” (11.379); Agamemnon counsels him not to tell his wife every word (11.442); Achilles asks for word of his son, who in council “did not miss the right words” (11.492, 11.511).
---

**The tale and the teller.** The noun names Odysseus' own tale, the matter of the book. Alcinous answers it: “you have told your tale skillfully, as a singer does, / the grievous sorrows of all the Argives and of yourself” (μῦθον δʼ ὡς ὅτʼ ἀοιδὸς ἐπισταμένως κατέλεξας, 11.368–369), the short comparison implying “as a singer tells it” (W. W. Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 11.368; see [[singers]], [[the-tale-to-alcinous]] and [[algos]]). Odysseus himself breaks off with “But all of them I could not tell or name” (μυθήσομαι, 11.328), the verse he uses again of Neoptolemus' victims (11.517), and takes it up again after granting that there is a time for many words (11.379; see [[a-time-for-many-words]]). Arete “began to speak” among the Phaeacians (μύθων ἤρχετο, 11.335; see [[arete-queen-of-the-phaeacians]]).

**Words kept and given.** Agamemnon's counsel is not to tell a wife “every word that you know well”, μῦθον meaning all that is in one's mind, as Zeus tells Hera not to hope to know all his counsels (*Iliad* 1.545; Merry on 11.442; see [[there-is-no-trusting-women]]). Achilles asks Odysseus to “tell me word of my noble son” (11.492), and hears that Neoptolemus always spoke first in council “and did not miss the right words”, οὐχ ἡμάρτανε μύθων, that is, did not say the wrong ones (11.511; Merry on 11.511; see [[neoptolemus]]); Odysseus will tell him “the whole truth” (11.507). Ajax will not hear Odysseus' “word / and speech” (11.561–563; see [[ajax]]).
