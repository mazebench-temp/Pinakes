---
summary: Antiope bore Amphion and Zethus, “who first founded the seat of seven-gated Thebes / and fenced it with towers, since without towers they could not / dwell in Thebes with its wide dancing floors, mighty though they were” (11.263–265).
---

Θήβης ἕδος, “the seat of Thebes,” as “the seat of Ithaca” (13.344, a plain rendering) and “the seat of Olympus” (*Iliad* 24.144); Merry explains the towers as a defense against the wild tribes around the city (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 11.263). The Iliad gives the epithet to the Thebes of the Seven (*Iliad* 4.406); here it goes back to the founding (see [[thebes]], [[with-wide-dancing-floors]] and [[krateros]]).
