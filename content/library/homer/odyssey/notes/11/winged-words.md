---
summary: Odysseus addresses Elpenor, his mother and Agamemnon “with winged words” (11.56, 11.209, 11.396), and his mother, Achilles and Heracles address him “lamenting … with winged words” (11.154, 11.472, 11.616).
---

The two verses divide the book's six uses between them. καί μιν φωνήσας ἔπεα πτερόεντα προσηύδων, Odysseus' own verse of the tale (as 10.482), comes each time after he has wept and pitied the dead or failed to embrace them (11.55–56, 11.208–209, 11.395–396); καί μʼ (καί ῥʼ) ὀλοφυρομένη (ὀλοφυρόμενος) ἔπεα πτερόεντα προσηύδα is the dead speaking to him, as Circe had spoken lamenting at his knees (10.324; see [[meeting-with-the-dead]] and [[lament]]).
