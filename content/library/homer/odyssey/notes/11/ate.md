# ἆσε (11.61), only here.
grc: [ἆσε]
en: [led me into blind folly]
---

Elpenor explains his fall with the verb Odysseus used of his companions and his sleep in Book 10 (ἄασαν, 10.68): ἆσέ με δαίμονος αἶσα κακὴ καὶ ἀθέσφατος οἶνος, “an evil fate from a god led me into blind folly, and endless wine” (11.61; see [[elpenor]], [[aisa]] and [[the-death-of-elpenor]]). ἆσε is the contracted form of the aorist, as ἄσατο in Agamemnon's tale of Ate and Zeus (*Iliad* 19.95; W. W. Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 10.68). The folly is a stupor, not a crime: drunk and asleep on Circe's roof, he did not think to come down by the ladder (11.62–64; see [[noos]]).
