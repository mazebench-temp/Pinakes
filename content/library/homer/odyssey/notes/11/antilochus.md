# Ἀντίλοχ* (in the entry) claims Ἀντιλόχοιο (11.468).
---

“and there came the soul of Achilles, son of Peleus, / and of Patroclus, and of noble Antilochus” (11.467–468; see [[achilles]], [[patroclus]] and [[blameless]]). He does not speak. W. W. Merry recalls Achilles' smile for him at the games, “rejoicing in Antilochus, since he was his dear companion” (*Iliad* 23.555–556; *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 11.467). He was killed at Troy by “the splendid son of shining Dawn” (4.187–188), whom Odysseus names a little later, “brilliant Memnon” (11.522; see [[memnon]]), and in the last book the shades come again in the same company (24.15–18, Greek only).
