summary: Odysseus to his mother's soul: “why do you not stay for me when I am eager to clasp you, / so that even in the house of Hades, throwing our arms around each other, / we two might take our fill of chilling lament?” (11.210–212).
grc: ["κρυεροῖο τεταρπώμεσθα γόοιο"]
---

Merry sets beside the verse Achilles' words to the soul of Patroclus, “let us put our arms around each other … / and take our fill of deadly lament” (*Iliad* 23.97–98), where ὀλοοῖο stands in place of κρυεροῖο (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 11.211). κρυερὸς γόος is the lament of Achilles' words to Priam (*Iliad* 24.524) and of Menelaus' (4.103; see [[lament]], [[three-times-and-a-fourth]] and [[meeting-with-the-dead]]).
