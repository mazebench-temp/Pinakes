---
summary: The half-verse of the Iliad's proem ends the story of the seer who drove off the cattle of Iphiclus: when the year came round Iphiclus set him free, “when he had told all the oracles; and the plan of Zeus was being fulfilled” (11.297).
---

**A small plan.** The seer who promised Neleus the cattle of Iphiclus was fettered by “a hard fate of a god”, the bonds and the herdsmen being, as Merry puts it, the form that fate took (11.292–293; W. W. Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 11.293; see [[moira]]). A year later Iphiclus released him, “when he had told all the oracles; and the plan of Zeus was being fulfilled” (11.296–297; see [[melampus-and-the-cattle-of-iphiclus]]). The phrase, Merry notes, is the Iliad's (on 11.297; *Iliad* 1.5): there it hangs over the wrath of Achilles and the deaths of the Achaeans, here over a seer's year in bonds, the cattle driven to Pylos, and a bride won for his brother. The imperfect ἐτελείετο, “was being fulfilled”, keeps its force: the plan was working itself out through the year of bonds (see [[boule]]). The book speaks of the gods' plans twice more, “the deadly plans of the gods” through which Oedipus suffered (11.276) and the “plans of women” through which Zeus worked his hatred of the house of Atreus (11.437; see [[oedipus-kills-laius]] and [[the-murder-of-agamemnon]]).
