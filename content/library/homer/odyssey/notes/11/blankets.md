---
summary: Laertes has no “beds and cloaks and glossy blankets” to sleep in, but sleeps among the slaves in winter and on fallen leaves in summer (11.188–194).
---

οὐδέ οἱ εὐναὶ / δέμνια καὶ χλαῖναι καὶ ῥήγεα σιγαλόεντα, “Nor has he, to sleep in, / beds and cloaks and glossy blankets” (11.188–189; see [[laertes]], [[bed]], [[cloak]] and [[glossy]]). W. W. Merry renders the line “couches and cloaks and bright-coloured rugs” (*Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 11.188), and the ῥήγεα σιγαλόεντα are the bedding of a well-furnished house, the glossy blankets that Nausicaa takes to the washing (6.38). Their lack is the mark of Laertes' grief, as the disguised Odysseus will refuse the cloaks and blankets that Penelope offers him (19.317–318, 19.336–340).
