summary: Tityus assaulted Leto “as she went to Pytho through Panopeus with its lovely dancing floors” (11.580–581).
---

**Leto's road.** The giant Tityus lies stretched over nine plethra among the dead, with vultures tearing at his liver, “for he had dragged off Leto, the glorious wife of Zeus, / as she went to Pytho through Panopeus with its lovely dancing floors” (11.580–581; see [[tityus]], [[leto]] and [[panopeus]]). Πυθώδʼ, “to Pytho”, with the suffix of direction, is the only time Homer names the place in this form; W. W. Merry refers to his note on the oracle in the eighth book, where Pytho is “the oldest name of the place in Phocis where Apollo's oracle was established”, and to the Catalogue, where it is “rocky Pytho” (*Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 11.581 and 8.80; *Iliad* 2.519; see the Book 8 note and [[rocky]]). Pytho is the sanctuary of her son Apollo, and Panopeus lay on the way to it from Boeotia, near the Cephisus (Merry on 11.581; see [[apollo]]).
