---
summary: Neleus would give his daughter Pero only to the man who drove the cattle of mighty Iphiclus “out of Phylace”; the seer who promised to do it was held there in bonds for a year, until Iphiclus released him (11.287–297).
---

**The cattle of Phylace.** Chloris bore Neleus a daughter, Pero, whom all who dwelt around were wooing, “but Neleus / would give her to no one who did not drive the broad-browed cattle with curving horns / out of Phylace, the cattle of mighty Iphiclus, / hard to drive” (11.288–291; see [[pero]], [[neleus]] and [[iphiclus]]). ἐλάσειε, “drive”, carries the condition laid down, “unless he had driven them off” (W. W. Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 11.290), and the cattle were ἀργαλέας, “hard to drive”, because they were so well guarded (on 11.291; see [[cattle]] and [[broad-browed]]). The noble seer alone promised to drive them, but a hard fate from a god fettered him, “and grievous bonds, and the country herdsmen” (11.291–293), and only when the year had come round did Iphiclus release him, “when he had told all the oracles” (11.294–297). The seer is not named here; he is Melampus, whose story Book 15 tells, when he is held in the house of Phylacus and at last drives the cattle “to Pylos from Phylace” (15.231–237; Merry on 11.281; see [[melampus]] and [[phylacus]]).
