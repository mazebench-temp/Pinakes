---
summary: “or did Artemis of the showering arrows / come upon you with her gentle arrows and kill you?” (11.172–173); and Anticleia answers in the same verse that this was not her death (11.198–199).
---

The sudden death of a woman, set against the long wasting sickness (11.172, 11.200–201; see [[hateful]]). Neither took her: “longing for you, and for your counsels, shining Odysseus, / and for your gentleness, robbed me of my honey-sweet life” (11.202–203; see [[gentle-hearted]] and [[honey-sweet]]). Merry, on the phrase in Book 3, cites this passage for the gentle arrows set against a long sickness (see the general article).
