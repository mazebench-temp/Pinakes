---
summary: Agamemnon of his wife: “but she, the dog-faced one, / turned away, and did not bring herself, though I was going to the house of Hades, / to close my eyes with her hands or to shut my mouth” (11.424–426).
---

ἡ δὲ κυνῶπις, the nominative of the word that Helen used of herself (4.145; *Iliad* 3.180), is Agamemnon's name for Clytemnestra, who would not perform for him the last rite of closing the eyes and mouth (see [[clytemnestra]] and [[honoring-the-dead]]). The dog is the image of shamelessness, and the next verse says it in plain words: “there is nothing more dreadful and more shameless than a woman” (κύντερον, 11.427; see [[anaideia]]). Merry follows the reading in which Agamemnon, dying, lets his hands fall to the ground (see the translation's note on 11.423–424).
