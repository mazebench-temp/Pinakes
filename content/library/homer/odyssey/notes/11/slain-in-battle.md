---
summary: Among the souls that gather to the pit come “many stabbed by bronze-fitted spears, / men slain in battle, wearing their armor clotted with blood” (11.40–41).
grc: [ἀρηίφατοι]
---

Murray prints the word without the diaeresis of the Iliad's ἀρηϊφάτους. βεβροτωμένα, “clotted with blood,” is from βρότος, the clotted blood of the dead on the plain (*Iliad* 7.425; see the Odyssey translation's key words). Zenodotus and Aristophanes rejected the six lines 11.38–43, as Merry reports the scholia, because in what follows the souls come one at a time and not mixed together, and because wounds are not seen on phantoms, which is why Odysseus must ask Agamemnon how he died (11.398); those who defended the lines answered that the dead keep the look they had in life, as Orion still hunts with his club and Agamemnon comes with the men who fell around him (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 11.38–43; see the translation's note on 11.38–43, and [[bronze-fitted]] and [[that-lays-men-low]]).
