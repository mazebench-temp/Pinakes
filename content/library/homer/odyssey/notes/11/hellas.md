---
summary: Achilles fears that Peleus, now old, is dishonored “throughout Hellas and Phthia” (11.496).
---

**Hellas and Phthia.** The soul of Achilles asks whether his old father still has honor among the Myrmidons, “or whether they dishonor him throughout Hellas and Phthia” (11.495–496; see [[peleus]] and [[phthia]]). ἀνʼ Ἑλλάδα τε Φθίην τε is the Iliad's pair, the two parts of Achilles' own kingdom in Thessaly, where he says there are many Achaean women “throughout Hellas and Phthia” (*Iliad* 9.395). W. W. Merry refers to his note on the first book, where Hellas with the heart of Argos stands for all Greece (*Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 11.496; 1.344); here, with Phthia, it keeps its narrower, Iliadic sense (see [[myrmidons]]).
