---
summary: The word is “noble” throughout the book: “the noble seer” Teiresias (11.99) and the unnamed seer who undertook to drive the cattle of Iphiclus (11.291), “noble Salmoneus” (11.236), “noble Antilochus” (11.468), “the noble son of Peleus” (11.470, 11.551), “noble Peleus” (11.494, 11.505), and Ajax, “son of noble Telamon” (11.553).
---

The translation renders the word “noble” of persons, as in the Iliad (see the Odyssey translation's principles). μάντις ἀμύμων is the title of the seer at the moment he begins to prophesy, in a verse built like the one with which Calchas at last speaks out in the Iliad (11.99 ~ *Iliad* 1.92), and of the seer who undertook to drive the cattle of Iphiclus (11.291; see [[teiresias]] and [[the-blind-seer]]). At 11.236 some read ἀτασθάλου, “reckless,” for the father of Tyro, and Merry refers to his note on “noble Aegisthus” (1.29), where he takes ἀμύμων for an almost titular word of praise with no necessary moral sense (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, critical note on 11.236; see the translation's note on 11.233, 11.236). μετʼ ἀμύμονα Πηλεΐωνα, the end of the verse that ranks Ajax after Achilles, is the Iliad's (11.470 = 11.551 = *Iliad* 17.280; see [[best-in-looks]]).
