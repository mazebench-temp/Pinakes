---
summary: The birds of Book 11 are in likenesses of the dead: the souls around the phantom of Heracles raise “a clamor of the dead, as of birds / scattering in terror on every side” (11.605–606), and the soul of Odysseus' mother flies from his hands, and flits away like a dream, with the verbs of a bird's flight (11.207–208, 11.222).
---

**A clamor as of birds.** “And around him rose a clamor of the dead, as of birds / scattering in terror on every side” (11.605–606; see [[a-clamor-as-of-birds]] and [[heracles]]). οἰωνοί are the large birds, birds of prey and of omen (see the entry), and κλαγγή is the Iliad's word for the cry of cranes and of the Trojans who come on like birds (*Iliad* 3.2–5; see [[cranes-and-pygmies]]).

**The soul's flight.** The dead fly as birds fly. Anticleia's soul ἔπτατο, “flew”, from her son's hands, “like a shadow or a dream” (11.207–208), and when the body has been burned “the soul, like a dream, flits away and hovers”, ἀποπταμένη πεπότηται (11.222; see [[like-a-shadow-or-a-dream]] and [[psyche]]); πεποτήαται is the word of the bees on the wing in the Iliad's simile (*Iliad* 2.90). In the last book the souls of the suitors follow Hermes gibbering, as bats fly gibbering in a cave (24.5–9, a plain rendering).
