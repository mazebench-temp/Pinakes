---
summary: When Odysseus takes up his tale again after the pause, the heroines are dismissed by Persephone: “But when the souls had been scattered this way and that / by holy Persephone, the souls of the womenfolk” (11.385–386).
---

She had sent them up, “for noble Persephone urged them on” (11.226), and now she scatters them, and the soul of Agamemnon comes (11.387; see [[womenfolk]] and [[persephone]]). In this book the queen of the dead has three epithets: “dread” in the prayer at the pit (11.47), “noble” three times (11.213, 11.226, 11.635), and “holy” here (see [[dread]] and [[noble]]). Merry notes that the Odyssey otherwise gives ἁγνή only to Artemis and to a feast, and thinks it possible that the word here is a later change, from a time when Persephone was regarded in a new light; Eustathius reports copies that read αἰνὴ Περσεφόνη, “dread Persephone” (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 11.386 and his critical note; see the translation's note on 11.385–386).
