summary: A bull is the second victim of the sacrifice to Poseidon that Teiresias bids Odysseus make far inland, “a ram and a bull and a boar that mounts the sows” (11.130–131).
# ταῦρον (11.131; again 23.278, the same verse), first met in the Odyssey here; the Iliad's are in its Book 11 and
# later notes.
grc: [ταῦρον]
---

“And make fine sacrifices to lord Poseidon, / a ram and a bull and a boar that mounts the sows” (11.130–131; see [[poseidon]], [[ram]] and [[boar]]). The bull is Poseidon's victim: Nestor's people sacrifice “bulls all black” to him on the shore at Pylos, and the Phaeacians make ready twelve bulls for him when he turns their ship to stone (3.6; 13.181–184; see the Book 3 note). W. W. Merry takes the threefold offering of Teiresias for something special, since the usual victim of the sea god was the black bull (*Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 11.131; see the Book 11 note on [[ram]]). In the Iliad Nestor remembers offering “a bull to Alpheus, and a bull to Poseidon” (*Iliad* 11.728), and Hippodamas bellows as he dies like a bull dragged by young men around the Heliconian lord, while the earth-shaker is glad of them (*Iliad* 20.403–405).
