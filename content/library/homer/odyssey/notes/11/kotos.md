---
summary: Teiresias tells Odysseus that he will not escape Poseidon, “since he has laid up a grudge against you in his heart, / angry because you blinded his dear son” (11.102–103).
---

The grudge is Poseidon's, kept since the blinding of Polyphemus (11.101–103; see [[poseidon]], [[polyphemus-the-cyclops]] and [[odysseus-and-the-cyclops]]). ὅ is the conjunction, “in that he hath stored up wrath in his heart against thee” (W. W. Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 11.102), where Athena's repetition of the verse in Book 13 has the relative ὅς (13.342; see the Odyssey translation's note on 11.102). Merry compares Agamemnon's fear that the other Achaeans too “are laying up anger against me in their hearts, just as Achilles is” (*Iliad* 14.50). Teiresias' prophecy ends with the means of laying it to rest: an oar planted far inland and a sacrifice to Poseidon (11.121–131; see [[sacrifice]] and [[the-prophecy-of-teiresias]]).
