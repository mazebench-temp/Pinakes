---
summary: Odysseus' heart “in my own breast” wishes to see the other souls (11.566), and around Heracles' chest is the terrible baldric with its wrought figures (11.609).
refs: [11.566]
---

The breast holds the heart, θυμός, as in the earlier books: after Ajax has gone without a word, “the heart in my own breast wished / to see the souls of the others who have died” (11.566–567; see [[thymos]]). On the phantom of Heracles the breast carries a sight: “And terrible was the baldric around his chest, / a golden strap, on which wondrous works were wrought” (ἀμφὶ περὶ στήθεσσιν ἀορτήρ, 11.609–610; see [[the-baldric-of-heracles]] and [[heracles]]).
