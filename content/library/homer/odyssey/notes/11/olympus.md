**War on Olympus.** Otus and Ephialtes, the sons of Iphimedeia, “even threatened the immortals on Olympus / that they would raise the din of furious war” (11.313–314; see [[otus]], [[ephialtes]] and [[immortals]]), and then “were eager to set Ossa on Olympus, and on Ossa / Pelion with its quivering leaves, so that heaven might be scaled” (11.315–316; see [[ossa-mountain]] and [[pelion]]). The passage puts Olympus to two uses, as the home of the gods to be attacked and as the first step of a stair to reach them in the sky above, and the clash was felt in antiquity: Aristarchus, who held that Ὄλυμπος in Homer is always the mountain, rejected the second pair of verses, while Eustathius could take Olympus there for the sky (W. W. Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 11.315–316; see [[ossa-mountain]], [[sky]] and the translation's note on 11.315–316). The son of Leto killed the brothers before they could do it (11.318–320; see [[apollo]]).
