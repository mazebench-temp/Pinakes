---
summary: Epicaste, who married her son, hanged herself and left Oedipus “sorrows behind, / very many, all that the Erinyes of a mother bring to pass” (11.279–280).
refs: [11.279-280]
---

No curse is spoken in the book, but one is implied. Epicaste, “held fast by her own grief”, hanged herself from the high roof beam, “but to him she left sorrows behind, / very many, all that the Erinyes of a mother bring to pass” (11.277–280; see [[jocasta]], [[oedipus-kills-laius]] and [[erinyes]]). The Erinyes are the powers that fulfill a parent's curse, and the Odyssey has used them so before: Telemachus will not send his mother from the house, for she “will call down the hateful Erinyes” as she goes (2.134–136; W. W. Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 11.280, referring to 2.135). μητρὸς Ἐρινύες is the phrase of Athena's taunt to Ares in the battle of the gods, that he may pay in full for his mother's Erinyes (*Iliad* 21.412; see the Odyssey translation's table of key words, μητρὸς Ἐρινύες).
