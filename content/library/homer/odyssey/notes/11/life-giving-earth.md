---
summary: Castor and Polydeuces, “both of whom the life-giving earth holds fast, alive though they are; / and even beneath the earth, having honor from Zeus, / now they live on alternate days, and now again / they are dead” (11.301–304).
---

The Iliad's verse-end of their burial (*Iliad* 3.243) is changed by one word, ζωούς, “alive.” Merry finds it hard to reconcile the phrase, which means “dead and buried,” with that word, unless as a kind of oxymoron, limited by the lines that follow (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 11.301; see the translation's note on 11.298–304, and [[the-death-of-the-dioscuri]], [[immortality]] and [[time]]).
