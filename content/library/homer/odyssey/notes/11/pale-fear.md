---
summary: The formula frames the tale of the dead: when the souls first gather “with a wondrous cry; and pale fear took hold of me” (11.43), and when the countless tribes gather at the end “with a wondrous din; and pale fear took hold of me / lest the Gorgon's head … noble Persephone should send against me out of Hades” (11.633–635).
---

χλωρὸν δέος ᾕρει, the end of the verse of the Achaeans' terror at the thunder of Zeus (*Iliad* 7.479), is spoken by Odysseus of himself, ἐμὲ δέ, at both ends of his account. Merry sees no reason for the first fear but the weird sound of the cries the shades raise; at the second there is good ground for terror (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 11.43). The first fear does not stop him: he orders the companions to burn the victims and sits with his sword over the blood (11.44–50); the second sends him back to the ship (11.636; see [[wondrous]], [[the-tribes-of-the-dead]] and [[gorgon]]).
