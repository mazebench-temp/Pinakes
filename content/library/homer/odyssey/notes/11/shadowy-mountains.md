summary: At the pause in the tale the Phaeacians “were held by a spell throughout the shadowy halls” (11.334); and the wind flings the fruit away from Tantalus “toward the shadowy clouds” (11.592).
---

κατὰ μέγαρα σκιόεντα, the halls of Alcinous, in the verse that will come back when the tale is over (11.334 = 13.2; see [[thelgo]] and [[the-tale-to-alcinous]]); Merry calls κηληθμός the “glamour” of the story (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 11.334). νέφεα σκιόεντα are the clouds of the Iliad's mountains (see the general article), here above the trees of the lake in which Tantalus stands (11.588–592; see [[tantalus]] and [[with-high-foliage]]).
