summary: At the end of his tale Odysseus would have waited to see the heroes of an earlier time, “Theseus and Peirithous, glorious children of the gods”, had the countless tribes of the dead not gathered around him with a wondrous din (11.628–635).
# Πειρίθοόν (11.631) is claimed by the entry.
passages:
  - 11.628-632 | The heroes Odysseus did not see
---

**Men of earlier times.** “And I would have seen still more men of earlier times, whom I wished to see, / Theseus and Peirithous, glorious children of the gods; / but before that the countless tribes of the dead gathered / with a wondrous din; and pale fear took hold of me” (11.630–633; see [[theseus]], [[glorious]], [[the-tribes-of-the-dead]] and [[pale-fear]]). προτέρους ἀνέρας, “men of earlier times”, takes up “the heroes who had perished in former days” of the verse before (11.629; see [[heros]]), and the pair belong to the generation of Nestor's youth, when Peirithous was the first of the mightiest men Nestor ever saw (*Iliad* 1.260–265; see [[nestor-and-the-centaurs]]). “Children of the gods” fits Peirithous, whom the Iliad makes a son of Zeus (*Iliad* 2.741, 14.317–318); the Iliad's Theseus is the son of Aegeus (*Iliad* 1.265; see [[aegeus]]). Virgil keeps the phrase: his Charon calls Theseus and Pirithous *dis geniti*, “born of gods” (*Aeneid* 6.393–394).

**The verse suspected.** Hereas of Megara, as Plutarch reports him, said that Peisistratus inserted this verse into Homer's Nekyia to please the Athenians, quoting it with ἀριδείκετα, “conspicuous”, for ἐρικυδέα; W. W. Merry brackets it (*Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, critical note on 11.631, quoting Plutarch, *Theseus* 20; see the translation's note on 11.631, [[theseus-line]] and [[pisistratus|Peisistratus]]).

**In Hades.** The poem does not say why the two should be among the dead. In Virgil they went down together to carry off the queen of the underworld from the chamber of Dis, and Pirithous lies with Ixion and the Lapiths under a black rock that is ever about to fall (*Aeneid* 6.392–397, 6.601–603, plain renderings; see [[virgil-the-punished-in-tartarus]], [[ixion]] and [[lapiths]]).
