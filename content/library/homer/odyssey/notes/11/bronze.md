---
summary: The bronze of the book is the blade that kills: the victims at the pit are slaughtered “with the pitiless bronze” (11.45), the suitors are to die “openly with the sharp bronze” (11.120), and Neoptolemus killed Eurypylus “with the bronze” and came home “neither struck by the sharp bronze / nor stabbed hand to hand” (11.519, 11.535–536). Orion's club is “all of bronze” (11.575).
---

χαλκός alone is the blade, as in the Iliad. At the pit the sheep “lay there slaughtered with the pitiless bronze” (11.45; see [[pitiless-bronze]] and [[the-offering-to-the-dead]]), and the dead come “stabbed by bronze-fitted spears” (11.40; see [[bronze-fitted]] and [[spear]]). Teiresias foretells that Odysseus will kill the suitors “whether by guile or openly with the sharp bronze” (11.120; see [[the-suitors]] and [[sharp]]). In Odysseus' account of Neoptolemus the word comes four times: “whenever we fought with the bronze on the plain of the Trojans” (11.513), the son of Telephus “slew with the bronze” (11.519), “his spear heavy with bronze” (11.532; see [[heavy-with-bronze]]), and his homecoming unhurt, “neither struck by the sharp bronze / nor stabbed hand to hand” (11.535–536; see [[neoptolemus]]). Orion's club, παγχάλκεον, “all of bronze” (11.575; see [[mace]] and [[orion]]), is the only bronze that is not a blade.
