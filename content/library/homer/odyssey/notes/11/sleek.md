---
summary: Teiresias foretells a gentle death that “will kill you / worn out by an old age of ease” (11.135–136).
grc: [λιπαρῷ]
en: [of ease]
---

γήραι ὕπο λιπαρῷ, the comfortable old age that Menelaus praised in Nestor's lot, “to grow old in ease in his halls” (4.210), and that Odysseus will repeat to Penelope in the same words (23.283; see the translation's key words, γῆρας λιπαρόν). The text prints γήραι, where the dative is γήραϊ everywhere else (see the translation's note on 11.136).
