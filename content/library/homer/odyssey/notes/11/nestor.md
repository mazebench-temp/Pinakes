summary: Named among the children Chloris bore to Neleus in Pylos (11.285–286), and in Odysseus' praise of Neoptolemus in council at Troy: “godlike Nestor and I alone surpassed him” (11.512).
# Νέστωρ, Νέστορα (in the entry) claim Νέστορά (11.286) and Νέστωρ (11.512).
---

**His mother.** Among the heroines Odysseus sees Chloris, the wife of Neleus and queen in Pylos, who “bore him splendid children, / Nestor and Chromius and proud Periclymenus”, and a daughter, Pero (11.285–287; see [[chloris]], [[neleus]], [[chromius-son-of-neleus]], [[periclymenus]] and [[pero]]). It is the only place where Homer names Nestor's mother; his father he names often, in the patronymic (see [[son-of-neleus]]).

**In council.** Of Neoptolemus at Troy: “Truly, whenever we took counsel about the city of Troy, / he always spoke first, and did not miss the right words; / godlike Nestor and I alone surpassed him” (11.510–512; see [[neoptolemus]], [[odysseus]] and [[godlike]]): “Nestor and I alone were his masters” in speaking, as W. W. Merry renders it, the variant νεικέσκομεν, “we found fault with him”, standing beside νικάσκομεν (*Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 11.511 and critical note on 11.512). The two counselors of the Iliad are the measure of the young man's speech.
