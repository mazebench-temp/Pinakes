# ἕδνα (11.117, 11.282; again 13.378, 19.529).
grc: [ἕδνα]
en: [bride-price]
---

**The suitors' gifts.** Among the evils Odysseus will find at home are “overbearing men, who are devouring your substance, / wooing your godlike wife and giving bride-price” (11.116–117; see [[the-suitors]] and [[penelope]]), a verse Athena will repeat in Ithaca (13.378). The suitors' outrages, Merry observes, had not yet begun when Teiresias spoke, unless the present tense is taken as prophetic (W. W. Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 11.115; see the Odyssey translation's note on 11.115–116).

**Neleus and Pero.** In the catalogue of women Neleus married Chloris “for her beauty, after giving a countless bride-price” (ἐπεὶ πόρε μυρία ἕδνα, 11.281–282), the Iliad's formula for a marriage made (*Iliad* 16.190, 22.472; see [[chloris]] and [[neleus]]). When their daughter Pero was wooed by all who dwelt around, he set the price as a feat: he would give her to no one who did not drive the cattle of Iphiclus out of Phylace, ὃς μή … ἐλάσειε being a condition, “who did not drive” (11.287–291; Merry on 11.290; see [[pero]], [[iphiclus]] and [[melampus]]). The seer who undertook it paid with a year in bonds (11.291–297).
