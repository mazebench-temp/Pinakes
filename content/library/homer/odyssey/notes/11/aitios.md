---
summary: Odysseus tells the silent soul of Ajax that no one else is to blame for his death, “but Zeus, who hated the army of the Danaan spearmen / terribly, and set your fate upon you” (11.558–560).
---

The denial of blame and its transfer to a god are the word's usual work, and here they are an apology: “no one else / is to blame, but Zeus” (οὐδέ τις ἄλλος / αἴτιος, ἀλλὰ Ζεύς, 11.558–559; see [[ajax]], [[zeus]] and [[who-is-to-blame]]). The logical form would be “no one else is to blame, but Zeus is to blame, because in his hatred he set your fate upon you”; Merry compares Hephaestus, who says that no one else is to blame for his lameness but his two parents (8.311–312; W. W. Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 11.559). Ajax answers nothing (11.563).
