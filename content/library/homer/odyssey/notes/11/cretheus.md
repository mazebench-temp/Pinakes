summary: Tyro says that she was “the wife of Cretheus, son of Aeolus” (11.237), and after her sons by Poseidon she bore him Aeson, Pheres and Amythaon (11.258–259).
# Αἰολίδαο (11.237), his patronymic, applies in the Odyssey only; aeolus-father-of-sisyphus claims it too.
grc: [Αἰολίδαο]
---

Cretheus is named twice, both times in Tyro's account of herself, as her husband and as the father of her younger sons: “And her other sons she, the queen among women, bore to Cretheus, / Aeson and Pheres and Amythaon, who fought from his chariot” (11.258–259; see [[tyro]], [[aeson]], [[pheres]] and [[amythaon]]). The poem sets her marriage before Poseidon's embrace, and W. W. Merry notes that the scholion on 11.245 speaks of her as already the wife of Cretheus, so that the “maiden girdle” would be out of place (*Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 11.245; see [[girdle]]).
