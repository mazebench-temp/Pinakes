summary: Of Tyro's two sons by Poseidon, “Pelias lived in Iolcus with its wide dancing floors, / rich in flocks”, and Neleus in sandy Pylos (11.254–257).
---

**The city of Pelias.** Tyro bore Pelias and Neleus to Poseidon, “mighty attendants of great Zeus, / both of them; Pelias lived in Iolcus with its wide dancing floors, / rich in flocks, and the other in sandy Pylos” (11.255–257; see [[tyro]], [[pelias]], [[neleus]] and [[pylos]]). θεράποντε Διός, “attendants of Zeus”, means kings, as θεράποντες Ἄρηος are warriors (W. W. Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 11.255), and the two kingdoms are set side by side, one in Thessaly and one in the Peloponnese. εὐρύχορος, “with its wide dancing floors”, is said of Thebes in the same passage (11.265) and of Hellas in the Iliad (*Iliad* 9.478; see [[with-wide-dancing-floors]]); πολύρρηνος, “rich in flocks”, goes with Pelias rather than with the city, and Merry reports πολύρηνος, with a single ρ, as the reading of Aristarchus, after La Roche (his critical note on 11.257; see [[rich-in-flocks]]). Merry also calls Tyro's husband Cretheus the founder of Iolcus (on 11.235), but the poem says only that she bore him three more sons, Aeson, Pheres and Amythaon (11.258–259).
