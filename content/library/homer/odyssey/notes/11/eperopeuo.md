---
summary: Alcinous tells Odysseus that the Phaeacians do not take him for “a deceiver and a cheat”, such as the many whom the earth feeds, “fashioning lies out of things that no one could even see” (11.363–366).
---

The praise is a judgment on the truth of the tale. “Odysseus, this we do not judge you to be when we look at you, / a deceiver and a cheat” (ἠπεροπῆά τʼ ἔμεν καὶ ἐπίκλοπον, 11.363–364; the Odyssey translation's table of key words, ἠπεροπεύς, ἐπίκλοπος), like the many who fashion lies “out of things that no one could even see” (11.365–366; see [[pseudos]] and [[truth-and-deception]]). Merry takes ὅθεν κέ τις οὐδὲ ἴδοιτο of lies that no one can bring to the proof of sight: Odysseus has just entertained his hosts with marvels from the land of Hades, where no mortal could go and test them, like the man in Herodotus who derived the Nile from Oceanus and, carrying his story back into the unseen, “has no refutation” (Herodotus 2.23); Alcinous does not discredit the tale, but gently reminds him of the doubtful truth of travelers' stories (W. W. Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 11.363, 11.366). Curtius derived ἠπεροπεύς from a Sanskrit word for “otherwise” and a root meaning “speak” (Merry on 11.366). Against the deceiver Alcinous sets what he hears: “but you have beauty of words, and within you are good wits” (11.367; see [[mythos]]).
