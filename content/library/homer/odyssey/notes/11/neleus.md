summary: Born to Tyro and Poseidon with Pelias, Neleus ruled in sandy Pylos (11.254–257); he married Chloris for her beauty with a countless bride-price, and would give their daughter Pero to no man who did not drive off the cattle of Iphiclus (11.281–291).
# Νηλῆα (11.254; again 15.237), the accusative; Νηλεύς and Νηλῆος are claimed in the Book 3 note.
grc: [Νηλῆα]
---

**Tyro's son.** “And she conceived and bore Pelias and Neleus, / who became mighty attendants of great Zeus, / both of them; Pelias lived in Iolcus …, and the other in sandy Pylos” (11.254–257; see [[tyro]], [[poseidon]], [[pelias]] and [[pylos]]). Book 3 had shown his seat before Nestor's doors (3.406–410); here his birth is told.

**Chloris and Pero.** Neleus is also the husband of a later heroine: “And I saw beautiful Chloris, whom Neleus once / married for her beauty, after giving a countless bride-price” (11.281–282; see [[chloris]] and [[bride-price]]): ἐπεὶ πόρε μυρία ἕδνα ends the verse in the Iliad of Echecles taking Polymele home and of Hector taking Andromache from the house of Eetion (*Iliad* 16.190, 22.472). Their daughter Pero was wooed by all the neighbors, “but Neleus / would give her to no one who did not drive the broad-browed cattle with curving horns / out of Phylace, the cattle of mighty Iphiclus” (11.288–290; see [[pero]] and [[iphiclus]]). οὐδʼ … ἐδίδου is “was not for giving”, W. W. Merry explains, and ἐλάσειε, “who did not drive”, is a condition, not ability (*Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 11.289–290; see the translation's note on 11.289–290). In Book 15 the seer who won her takes vengeance on Neleus, who had held his possessions for a year (15.229–237; see [[melampus]]).
