---
summary: The companions go aboard “grieving, shedding big tears” (11.5), and Odysseus and Agamemnon stand “grieving, shedding big tears” (11.466); Penelope's nights and days waste away “as she sheds tears” (11.183); and Agamemnon “wept loudly, letting big tears fall” (11.391).
grc: ["δάκρυ χέοντες", "δάκρυ χεούσῃ", "κατὰ δάκρυον εἴβων"]
en: [shedding big tears, as she sheds tears, letting big tears fall]
---

The verse-end of the companions' grief (see [[flourishing]]); Penelope's verse comes again in the Odyssey (13.338, 16.39); and δάκρυον εἴβων is the soul of Agamemnon weeping as it stretches out its hands (11.391–392; see [[tears]] and [[meeting-with-the-dead]]).
