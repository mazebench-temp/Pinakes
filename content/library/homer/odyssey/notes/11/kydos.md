---
summary: Odysseus greets the soul of Agamemnon with the title of the living king, “Most glorious son of Atreus, Agamemnon, lord of men” (11.397).
---

The address is the Iliad's verse for the commander of the army (*Iliad* 2.434; see the Odyssey translation's table of formulas), spoken to a soul that has just told how he was killed at a feast. κύδιστε, “most glorious”, belongs to the king's power, and the question that follows, “what fate of death … brought you down?”, measures how far he has fallen (11.397–398; see [[agamemnon]], [[lord-of-men]] and [[ker-doom]]).
