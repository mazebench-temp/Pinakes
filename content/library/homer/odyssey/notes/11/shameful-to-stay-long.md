---
summary: Odysseus tells Alcinous that he would stay even a year if he could go home with more gifts, “with a fuller hand”, more respected and dearer to all who saw him return (11.355–361).
refs: [11.356-361]
---

Odysseus' answer to Alcinous turns the thought of the Iliad's maxim around: there, a long stay that ends empty-handed is a disgrace; here, a longer stay that ends with a fuller hand would be “far better”, and would make him “more respected and dearer to men, / by all who should see me come home to Ithaca” (11.356–361; see [[kerdos]], [[doron]] and [[nostos]]). It was a bad thing, Merry notes, to return empty-handed, as the companions said before they opened the skin of the winds (10.42; W. W. Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 11.358).
