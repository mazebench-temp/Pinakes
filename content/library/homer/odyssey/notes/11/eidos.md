summary: Arete asks how the stranger seems to the Phaeacians “in looks and stature and the well-balanced mind within” (11.337); Ajax was the best “in looks and form” after Achilles (11.469, 11.550); and Poseidon lies with Tyro “in his likeness”, the likeness of the river Enipeus (11.241).
# εἰσάμενος (11.241).
grc: [εἰσάμενος]
en: [in his likeness]
---

**Looks.** Arete presents the guest by his εἶδος, his μέγεθος and his φρένες: “Phaeacians, how does this man seem to you to be / in looks and stature and the well-balanced mind within?” (11.336–337; see [[arete-queen-of-the-phaeacians]] and [[phrenes]]); πῶς … εἶναι is used like the later πῶς ἔχειν, “how seems he to stand in your eyes?” (W. W. Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 11.336). Among the dead Ajax is ranked by his looks: “the best in looks and form / of all the Danaans, after the noble son of Peleus” (11.469–470), “who in looks and in deeds stood out above the rest” (11.550–551; see [[best-in-looks]] and [[ajax]]).

**Likeness.** The god takes a shape: “in his likeness the earth-holder, the shaker of the earth, / lay with her at the mouth of the eddying river” (τῷ δʼ ἄρα εἰσάμενος, 11.241–242), τῷ being the river god Enipeus, whom Tyro loved (Merry on 11.241; see [[enipeus]], [[poseidon]] and [[shape-changing]]).
