summary: The Achaeans are named in the speeches among the dead: Odysseus fears that “whoever is best of the Achaeans” has married his wife (11.179), hails Achilles as “by far the strongest of the Achaeans” (11.478), brought Neoptolemus “to join the well-greaved Achaeans” (11.509), and tells Ajax that “we Achaeans” grieve for him as for Achilles (11.556).
---

**The best of the Achaeans.** Odysseus asks his mother whether Penelope still stays beside her son, “or whether whoever is best of the Achaeans has already married her” (11.179; see [[penelope]] and [[the-suitors]]): here the Achaeans are the nobles of Ithaca and the islands, as in the first books (see the Book 1 and Book 2 notes). Among the dead the name is the army's again. Odysseus greets Achilles as μέγα φέρτατʼ Ἀχαιῶν, “by far the strongest of the Achaeans” (11.478; see [[achilles]]), in the verse with which Patroclus and Odysseus himself addressed him at Troy (*Iliad* 16.21, 19.216), and tells him that he brought his son from Scyros “to join the well-greaved Achaeans” (11.509; see [[neoptolemus]] and [[well-greaved]]). To the silent Ajax he says that “for you we Achaeans / grieve as much as for the head of Achilles, son of Peleus” (11.556–558; see [[ajax]]). The war's three names come together in these speeches, Achaeans, Argives and Danaans, as they do in the Iliad (see [[argives]] and [[danaans]]).
