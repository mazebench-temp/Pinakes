---
summary: “And I saw Phaedra and Procris and beautiful Ariadne, / the daughter of baleful Minos” (11.321–322).
---

Merry renders ὀλοόφρων “evil-hearted,” perhaps in memory of the cruel tribute that Minos laid on Athens (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 11.322). The same Minos is the judge of the dead later in the book (11.568–571; see [[minos]] and [[ariadne]]).
