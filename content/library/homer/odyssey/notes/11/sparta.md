---
summary: Agamemnon's soul asks whether Orestes may be living “perhaps with Menelaus in broad Sparta” (11.460).
---

**With Menelaus in broad Sparta.** The dead king does not know whether his son is alive, and names the places where he might have found refuge: “perhaps in Orchomenus, or in sandy Pylos, / or perhaps with Menelaus in broad Sparta; / for brilliant Orestes has not yet died upon the earth” (11.459–461; see [[orestes]], [[menelaus]], [[orchomenus]] and [[pylos]]). Sparta is the house of his brother, and in W. W. Merry's reading all three places are chosen only as refuges at a good distance from Mycenae (*Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 11.458). Σπάρτῃ εὐρείῃ, “broad Sparta”, gives the city the epithet of wide lands, as Λυκίῃ εὐρείῃ is “wide Lycia” in the Iliad (*Iliad* 6.210; see [[wide-lycia]]); Troy is εὐρεῖα too, “wide Troy”, in this book (11.499; see [[troy]]). The last verse was athetized “for its silliness”, as Merry reports the scholium: if Agamemnon was sure that his son had not died, why does he ask whether he is still alive? (his critical note on 11.461; see the translation's note on 11.461). The audience knows what Agamemnon does not: Orestes came back from Athens and avenged him (3.306–310; 1.298–300). Odysseus cannot tell him, and will not “speak empty wind” (11.463–464; see [[it-is-bad-to-speak-empty-wind]]).
