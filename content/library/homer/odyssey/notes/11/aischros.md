---
summary: Clytemnestra “has poured shame on herself and on those to come hereafter, / the womenfolk, even on one who does good” (11.432–434).
---

Agamemnon's soul makes the shame of one woman a stain on all: ἡ δʼ ἔξοχα λυγρὰ ἰδυῖα / οἷ τε κατʼ αἶσχος ἔχευε, “she, with her mind set on grim things beyond all others, / has poured shame on herself”, οἷ being the reflexive pronoun with its full force (11.432–433; W. W. Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 11.432, 11.433; see [[clytemnestra]] and [[there-is-no-trusting-women]]). The deed itself is ἔργον ἀεικές, “the shameful deed” (11.429; see the Odyssey translation's table of key words, ἀεικής).
