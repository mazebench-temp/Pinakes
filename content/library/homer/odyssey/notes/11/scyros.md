summary: Odysseus tells the soul of Achilles that he himself brought Neoptolemus “in the hollow, balanced ship / from Scyros to join the well-greaved Achaeans” (11.506–509).
---

**From Scyros.** Achilles asks for news of his son, and Odysseus begins with his own part: “for it was I myself who brought him in the hollow, balanced ship / from Scyros to join the well-greaved Achaeans” (11.508–509; see [[neoptolemus]], [[achilles]], [[hollow-ships]] and [[well-greaved]]). The Iliad knows Scyros as the place where the son is being reared while his father fights at Troy (*Iliad* 19.326–327, 19.331–332), and the Odyssey has spoken of him before, when Menelaus sends his daughter to be the bride of the son of Achilles, who rules the Myrmidons (4.5–9; see [[myrmidons]]). In the legend as W. W. Merry tells it, Neoptolemus had been brought up on Scyros by his grandfather Lycomedes, and Odysseus fetched him to Troy because of an oracle of Helenus that without him Troy could never be taken, as in Sophocles' *Philoctetes* (*Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 11.508; see [[helenus]]). Odysseus' tale takes the young man from the island to the councils, the battles and the wooden horse, and home unharmed with his prize (11.510–537; see [[neoptolemus-comes-to-troy]]).
