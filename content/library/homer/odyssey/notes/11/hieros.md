summary: Teiresias bids Odysseus “go home and offer holy hecatombs / to the immortal gods, who hold the wide sky; / to all of them in order” (11.132–134); his mother is the one “whom I had left alive when I went to sacred Ilios” (11.86); and Theseus was bringing Ariadne “from Crete to the hill of sacred Athens” (11.323).
grc: ["Ἴλιον ἱρὴν", "Ἀθηνάων ἱεράων"]
en: [sacred]
---

The text prints ἱερᾶς, a genitive singular, where the same verse has ἱερὰς at 23.279 (see the translation's note on 11.132). The hecatombs to all the gods follow the sacrifice to Poseidon alone, far inland (11.130–131; see [[hecatomb]] and [[poseidon]]). Ἴλιον ἱρήν is the Iliad's verse-end for Troy (see [[troy]]), and ἱεράων the only epithet that the tale gives Athens (see [[athens]]).
