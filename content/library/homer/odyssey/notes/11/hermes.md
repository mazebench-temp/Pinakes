---
summary: Hermes is named once in Book 11, at the end of the phantom of Heracles' story: he brought the hound up out of Hades, “and Hermes guided me, and gray-eyed Athena” (11.625–626).
---

**The guide.** Eurystheus sent Heracles to bring the hound from the house of Hades, the hardest labor he could think of; “and I brought it up and led it out of Hades, / and Hermes guided me, and gray-eyed Athena” (11.625–626; see [[heracles]], [[heracles-and-cerberus]], [[athena]] and [[hades]]). ἔπεμψεν is “conducted me”, as in Nausicaa's words, “so that I may send you” (6.255; Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 11.626). It is the office that the Odyssey gives Hermes at its end, when he calls out the souls of the suitors and leads them down to the meadow of asphodel (24.1–14, a plain rendering; see [[the-meadow-of-asphodel]]), and that the Iliad gives him with the living, when Zeus sends him to guide Priam through the Achaean camp, “as your escort” (*Iliad* 24.437; see [[pompe]]). The Iliad tells the same labor without Hermes: Athena says that Zeus sent her down to help Heracles whenever the labors of Eurystheus wore him out, and that, had she known how Zeus would repay her, Heracles would never have escaped the streams of Styx when he was sent to the house of Hades for the hound (*Iliad* 8.362–369).
