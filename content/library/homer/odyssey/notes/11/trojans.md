---
summary: The Trojans are the enemy of the tale's past: Odysseus followed Agamemnon “to fight the Trojans” (11.169), his companions “escaped the groaning war cry of the Trojans” only to perish on the way home (11.383), Neoptolemus fought “on the plain of the Trojans” (11.513), and “the sons of the Trojans” judged the arms of Achilles (11.547).
---

**The war remembered.** Odysseus tells his mother that he has been wandering ever since he followed Agamemnon to Ilios “to fight the Trojans” (11.169; see [[troy]]), and Alcinous that his companions “escaped the groaning war cry of the Trojans, / but in their homecoming perished by the will of an evil woman” (11.383–384; see [[the-returns-of-the-achaeans]] and [[clytemnestra]]). To Achilles he tells how Neoptolemus fought “on the plain of the Trojans” and in the wooden horse “had evil in mind for the Trojans” (11.513, 11.532; see [[neoptolemus]] and [[the-wooden-horse]]).

**The judges of the arms.** The anger of Ajax goes back to the contest for the arms of Achilles, which Thetis set as a prize, “and the sons of the Trojans were the judges, and Pallas Athena” (11.546–547; see [[the-judgment-of-arms]], [[ajax]] and [[athena]]). Aristarchus rejected the verse, and W. W. Merry gives Nitzsch's reasons for doubting it: Trojan captives would be the most unlikely umpires in a dispute between Achaean chieftains; Homer does not say παῖδες Τρώων, but κοῦροι or υἷες; the verse is superfluous; and Odysseus would hardly make Athena the author of so disastrous a decision (*Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 11.547 and his critical note; see the translation's note on 11.547). A scholium says that the story comes from the Cyclic poets (Merry's critical note on 11.547). In the *Aethiopis*, as Merry tells it, Agamemnon and the chieftains could not decide, and on Nestor's advice the question was put to the Trojan captives, which of the two heroes had done the Trojans more harm, and they answered “Odysseus”; in the *Little Iliad* spies were sent to listen at the walls of Troy, and heard two Trojan girls disputing whether Ajax or Odysseus deserved the prize (on 11.543).
