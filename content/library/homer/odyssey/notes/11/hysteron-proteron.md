---
summary: Odysseus asks his mother how she died, then about his father and his son, and last about his wife (11.170–179); she answers in the reverse order, of his wife, his son, his father, and last of her own death (11.180–203).
refs: [11.170-203]
---

The questions run from the dead to the living: “What fate of death that lays men low brought you down?” (11.171), “And tell me of my father and of my son” (11.174), “And tell me of my wedded wife” (11.177). The answers begin with the last: Penelope stays “with enduring heart” (11.181), Telemachus holds the domains (11.184–187), his father stays in the country (11.187–196), and she herself died of longing for him (11.197–203; see [[enduring]], [[wedded]] and [[that-lays-men-low]]).
