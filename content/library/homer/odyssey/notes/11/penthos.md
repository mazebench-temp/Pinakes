---
summary: Laertes lies in the country and “nurses a great sorrow in his heart, / longing for your homecoming” (11.195–196); among the dead are “tender maidens with hearts new to sorrow” (11.39).
---

Anticleia's account of Laertes ends in mourning: “There he lies grieving, and nurses a great sorrow in his heart, / longing for your homecoming, and hard old age comes upon him besides” (μέγα δὲ φρεσὶ πένθος ἀέξει, 11.195–196; see [[laertes]], [[pothe]] and [[nostos]]). ἀέξει, “increases”, is used of cherishing or fostering sorrow, as of Telemachus when the stranger is struck (17.489), and μέγα is predicative: he makes his sorrow great (W. W. Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 11.195). Among the souls that gather at the pit are maidens νεοπενθέα θυμὸν ἔχουσαι, “with hearts new to sorrow” (11.39; see the Odyssey translation's table of key words, νεοπενθής, and [[psyche]]).
