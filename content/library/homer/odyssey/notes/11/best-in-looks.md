summary: “and of Ajax, who was the best in looks and form / of all the Danaans, after the noble son of Peleus” (11.469–470); and again, “Ajax, who in looks and in deeds stood out above the rest / of all the Danaans, after the noble son of Peleus” (11.550–551).
grc: ["πέρι μὲν εἶδος"]
en: [in looks and in deeds]
---

The first is the verse of Euryalus among the Phaeacians (8.116–117), the second the Iliad's verse of Ajax (*Iliad* 17.279–280); Merry renders it “was superior in form and in achievements to the other Greeks” (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 11.550). Both rank Ajax after Achilles, whose arms Odysseus won from him (see [[ajax]], [[the-judgment-of-arms]] and [[blameless]]).
