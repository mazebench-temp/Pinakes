summary: Alcinous claims the escort for himself, “for mine is the power among the people” (11.353), and Odysseus tells Achilles that “now again you are mighty among the dead” (11.485).
# κρατέεις (11.485).
grc: [κρατέεις]
en: [you are mighty]
---

Alcinous ends his answer in the half-line of Telemachus to his mother, with the people in place of the house: his guest's escort will be the concern of all men, “and mine most of all; for mine is the power among the people” (11.352–353; see [[war-is-the-concern-of-men]], [[pompe]] and [[alcinous]]). Among the dead the power is Achilles': “we honored you equally with the gods, / we Argives, and now again you are mighty among the dead” (μέγα κρατέεις νεκύεσσιν, 11.484–485). κρατέω usually takes the genitive; the dative is found only here and once more in the Odyssey, and νεκύεσσιν may be a local dative, “among the dead”, or an ethical one, “mighty in the eyes of the dead” (W. W. Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 11.485; see [[achilles]] and [[rather-a-hired-laborer]]).
