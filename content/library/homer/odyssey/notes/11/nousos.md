# νοῦσος (11.172, 11.200; again 15.408).
grc: [νοῦσος]
en: [sickness]
---

The two ordinary deaths of women at home are a long sickness and the sudden death sent by Artemis' gentle arrows (11.171–173; see [[ker-doom]], [[artemis]] and [[gentle-arrows]]). Anticleia denies both in turn, in the same order: it was not the goddess, “nor did any sickness come upon me, such as most often / takes the spirit from the limbs with hateful wasting” (11.198–201), ἐξείλετο being a gnomic aorist of what commonly happens (W. W. Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 11.201; τηκεδών, “wasting”, the Odyssey translation's table of key words). Her death was longing (11.202–203; see [[pothe]] and [[anticleia]]). Eumaeus' island of Syrie knows the same two deaths: no hateful sickness comes upon its people, but when men grow old Apollo and Artemis come and kill them with their gentle arrows (15.407–411).
