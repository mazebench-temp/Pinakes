---
summary: Agamemnon tells how he and his companions were killed at Aegisthus' feast, “around the mixing bowl and the laden tables”, while the whole floor seethed with blood (11.419–420).
---

“But you would have grieved most in your heart, had you seen that sight, / how around the mixing bowl and the laden tables / we lay in the hall, and the whole floor seethed with blood” (11.418–420; see [[agamemnon]], [[the-murder-of-agamemnon]], [[table]] and [[house]]). The bowl and the tables, the furniture of a feast, mark the murder as a crime against the meal itself: Aegisthus killed him “when he had called me to his house / and feasted me” (11.410–411; see [[feast]] and [[xenia]]). θῦεν is “seethed”: the floor swam or bubbled with blood, W. W. Merry explains, the verb of the violence of winds and waves (*Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 11.420; see the translation's note on 11.420).
