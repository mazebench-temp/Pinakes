---
summary: Elpenor was left “unwept and unburied, since another toil was pressing us” (11.54).
---

The toil was the journey to the house of Hades, which Circe had laid on Odysseus at once (10.488–495; see [[elpenor]] and [[the-death-of-elpenor]]): the companions left the body in Circe's hall in their haste, and Merry notes that Odysseus thus knew the manner of Elpenor's death, which makes his question “how did you come beneath the misty darkness?” the harder to explain (W. W. Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 11.51; see [[honoring-the-dead]]). Heracles' labors are ἄεθλοι, not πόνοι (11.622–624; see [[heracles]]).
