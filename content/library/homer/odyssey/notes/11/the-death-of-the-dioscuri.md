# Unnamed: the brothers held by the earth, alive and dead by turns (11.301-304).
refs: [11.301-304]
passages:
  - 11.298-304 | Leda and her sons
---

**After the Iliad.** “Castor, tamer of horses, and Polydeuces, good with his fists” is the verse in which Helen names her brothers from the wall of Troy (11.300 = *Iliad* 3.237; see [[castor]] and [[polydeuces]]). The narrator of the Iliad ended her search with their burial, the life-giving earth already holding them in their own country (*Iliad* 3.243–244); the next verse here keeps the words, φυσίζοος αἶα and κατέχει, and turns them: the earth holds them fast “alive though they are” (11.301; see [[life-giving-earth]]). Neither poem says how the brothers died; the Odyssey adds what the Iliad's narrator does not, that “even beneath the earth, having honor from Zeus, / now they live on alternate days, and now again / they are dead; and they have been allotted honor like the gods” (11.302–304; see [[immortality]] and [[time]]). The verses give no reason for the shared life, which later poets explain by Polydeuces' gift to his brother (see the general article). Merry doubted the passage, and found the Iliad's form of the story reconcilable with it only by straining the words; his notes are given under [[castor]] (see also the translation's note on 11.298–304).
