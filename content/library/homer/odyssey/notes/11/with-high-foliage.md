---
summary: Over Tantalus' head, “trees with high foliage let their fruit hang down” (11.588), until the wind flings it away.
grc: [ὑψιπέτηλα]
---

δένδρεα δʼ ὑψιπέτηλα, the plural of the tall tree of the Iliad's simile (*Iliad* 13.437) and of Proteus' last shape (4.458; see the Odyssey translation's key words). The fruit is that of Alcinous' garden: “pears and pomegranates and apple trees with splendid fruit, / and sweet figs and flourishing olives” (11.589–590 = 7.115–116; see [[tantalus]], [[the-garden-of-alcinous]] and [[shadowy-mountains]]).
