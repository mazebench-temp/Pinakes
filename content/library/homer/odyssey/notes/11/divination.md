---
summary: The seers of the book are Teiresias, “the noble seer” who prophesies at the pit (11.99), and the unnamed “noble seer” of Pylos, who alone promised to drive off the cattle of Iphiclus (11.291).
---

The same epithet is given to both. When Teiresias has drunk the blood, “the noble seer spoke to me with words” (μάντις ἀμύμων, 11.99; see [[teiresias]], [[blameless]] and [[the-prophecy-of-teiresias]]). He holds a golden scepter, the badge of office carried by kings, by priests and prophets, as by Chryses (*Iliad* 1.15), and by judges (11.91; W. W. Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 11.90; see [[scepter]]). The other μάντις ἀμύμων is the seer who promised Neleus to drive the cattle of Iphiclus from Phylace and was held in bonds for a year (11.291–297; see [[melampus]]); the poem names him only in Book 15, as an ancestor of the seer Theoclymenus (15.225–242), and later writers told how he understood the speech of birds and of the worms in his prison's beam (Merry on 11.297). Elpenor too seems to know more than he should: he knows that Odysseus will return to Aeaea, and Eustathius says that he speaks prophetically (11.69–70; Merry on 11.51; see [[elpenor]] and [[prophecy]]).
