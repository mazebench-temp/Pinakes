---
summary: Pylos is the kingdom of Neleus, Tyro's son by Poseidon, who lived “in sandy Pylos” (11.257), and of Chloris, who “was queen in Pylos” and bore him Nestor (11.285–286); Agamemnon's soul wonders whether Orestes may be living there (11.459).
---

**The house of Neleus.** Of Tyro's two sons by Poseidon, Pelias lived in Iolcus, “and the other in sandy Pylos” (11.257; see [[neleus]], [[iolcus]] and [[sandy]]). Neleus married Chloris of Minyan Orchomenus, “and she was queen in Pylos, and bore him splendid children, / Nestor and Chromius and proud Periclymenus”, and a daughter, Pero (11.285–287; see [[chloris]], [[nestor]], [[chromius-son-of-neleus]], [[periclymenus]] and [[pero]]). Πύλου is a local genitive, “queen in Pylos”, not governed by the verb, as βασιλεύειν is regularly used without an object; it was her marriage with Neleus that made her queen (W. W. Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 11.285; see the translation's note on 11.284–285). Neleus had twelve sons in all, Merry adds, but all except Nestor were killed by Heracles (on 11.285; *Iliad* 11.690–693; see [[heracles-sacks-pylos]]). The seer who went to Phylace for the cattle of Iphiclus, to win Pero for his brother, had once lived in Pylos himself, as the fifteenth book tells (15.225–238; see [[melampus]] and [[phylace]]).

**A refuge for Orestes.** Agamemnon asks Odysseus whether he has heard of his son as still living, “perhaps in Orchomenus, or in sandy Pylos, / or perhaps with Menelaus in broad Sparta” (11.459–460; see [[orestes]], [[orchomenus]] and [[sparta]]): places, as Merry sees it, chosen only as refuges at a good distance from Mycenae (on 11.458). Telemachus had gone to the same two, Pylos and Sparta, for news of his own father (Books 3 and 4; see [[the-journey-of-telemachus]]).
