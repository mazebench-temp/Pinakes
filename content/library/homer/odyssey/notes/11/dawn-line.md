---
summary: Alcinous in the pause: “I could hold out even till brilliant Dawn, if you / would bring yourself to tell in the hall of your sorrows” (11.375–376).
---

Merry renders καὶ … ἐς ἠῶ, “even till morn,” and takes ἀνασχοίμην absolutely, “I could hold on” (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 11.375). The dawn is the measure of the night that Alcinous has just called “very long, endless” (11.373; see [[endless]]); it comes only after the tale is done, when the Phaeacians have gone to sleep and “early-born Dawn appeared, the rosy-fingered” (13.17–18; see [[brilliant]]).
