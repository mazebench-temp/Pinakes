---
summary: Odysseus at the pause: it is time to sleep, “either when I have gone to the swift ship and my companions, / or here” (11.331–332).
---

ἐπὶ νῆα θοήν, the ship of the Phaeacians made ready for his escort; Merry takes the companions for the sailors whom Alcinous had sent down to the harbor to sail on the morrow (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 11.331, citing 7.317 and 8.50). Alcinous answers that the night is long and asks for more of the tale (11.373–376; see [[ships]] and [[the-tale-to-alcinous]]).
