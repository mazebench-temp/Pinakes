---
summary: Odysseus greets the soul of his companion, “Elpenor, how did you come beneath the misty darkness?” (11.57), and his mother greets him with the same words, “My child, how did you come beneath the misty darkness / while you are alive?” (11.155–156).
grc: [ἠερόεντα]
---

ζόφος ἠερόεις is the darkness that fell to Hades as his share of the world (*Iliad* 15.191), into which the dead go down (*Iliad* 23.51). The same question is put first by the living to the dead and then by the dead to the living. Elpenor, who fell from Circe's roof, has come “faster on foot than I in my black ship” (11.58; [[elpenor]]); Anticleia wonders that her son has come alive (“It is hard for the living to see these things”), with great rivers between, and Oceanus, “which no one can cross / on foot, unless he has a well-made ship” (11.156–159; [[oceanus]], [[well-made]]). Ancient critics found the first question strange after Odysseus has said how Elpenor died and lay unburied (11.51–54; see [[unwept-and-unburied]]).
