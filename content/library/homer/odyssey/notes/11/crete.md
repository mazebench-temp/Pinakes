summary: Crete is the home of Ariadne, “the daughter of baleful Minos”, whom Theseus “was bringing from Crete to the hill of sacred Athens” when Artemis killed her on Dia (11.321–325).
---

**From Crete to Athens.** Among the women sent by Persephone, Odysseus saw “Phaedra and Procris and beautiful Ariadne, / the daughter of baleful Minos, whom Theseus once / was bringing from Crete to the hill of sacred Athens” (11.321–323; see [[ariadne]], [[minos]], [[theseus]] and [[athens]]). The voyage ended on Dia, the little island off the north coast of Crete that, in W. W. Merry's view, Theseus would pass in sailing for Athens (*Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 11.322; see [[dia]]). Merry calls the three names Attic legend, breaking the run of Thessalian and Boeotian heroines, and thinks the five verses a later interpolation (on 11.321). Phaedra too, he notes, was a daughter of the Cretan Minos, and became the wife of Theseus (on 11.321; see [[phaedra]]). Minos himself appears later among the dead, holding a golden scepter and giving judgment (11.568–571); he is a son of Zeus and was king in Crete, and his grandson Idomeneus leads the Cretans at Troy (*Iliad* 13.449–453; Merry on 11.568; see [[idomeneus]]).
