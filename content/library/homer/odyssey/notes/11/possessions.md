---
summary: Arete urges the Phaeacians not to cut short their gifts to the stranger, “for many / possessions lie in your halls by the will of the gods” (11.340–341).
---

“So do not hurry to send him away, and the gifts / for one in such need do not cut short; for many / possessions lie in your halls by the will of the gods” (11.339–341; see [[arete-queen-of-the-phaeacians]], [[doron]] and [[xenia]]). Odysseus answers Alcinous that he would stay a year for such gifts, since it would be better “to come to my dear native land with a fuller hand” (11.358–359; see [[odysseus]]): the possessions of the Phaeacians are to become his, as the goods of his own house are being eaten by the suitors (11.116; see [[the-suitors]]).
