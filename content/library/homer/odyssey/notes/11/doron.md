---
summary: In the pause of the tale Arete asks the Phaeacians not to cut short the gifts for their guest (11.339–341), Alcinous keeps him a day to complete “the whole present” (11.351–352), and Odysseus would stay a year for “splendid gifts” (11.357). Among the dead of Troy are the Ceteians, killed “for the sake of gifts to a woman” (11.521).
---

**The Phaeacians' gifts.** The gifts were promised in Book 8 (8.389–395, 8.438–442; W. W. Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 11.339). Arete, claiming the guest as hers, asks the Phaeacians not to send him away in haste, “and the gifts / for one in such need do not cut short; for many / possessions lie in your halls by the will of the gods” (11.339–341; see [[xenia]] and [[possessions]]): κολούετε means giving fewer than they would give if he stayed a little longer, not taking back what has been promised (Merry on 11.340). Alcinous keeps him until tomorrow, “until I complete / the whole present” (δωτίνην, 11.351–352), and Odysseus answers that if they kept him a year, “and should speed my escort and give splendid gifts”, he would want it, for it would be far better to come home “with a fuller hand” (11.356–359; see [[pompe]] and [[shameful-to-stay-long]]).

**Gifts to a woman.** Neoptolemus killed Eurypylus son of Telephus, and around him “many of his companions around him, / the Ceteians, were killed, for the sake of gifts to a woman” (11.520–521; see [[eurypylus-son-of-telephus]] and [[ceteians]]). The words return in Book 15 of Amphiaraus, whom his wife betrayed for gifts (15.247), as Eriphyle in this book “took precious gold as the price of her own husband” (11.327; see [[eriphyle]]). Merry tells the story behind the Ceteians' deaths: Eurypylus' mother Astyoche, a sister or daughter of Priam, was persuaded to send him to Troy by the present of a golden vine (Merry on 11.520, 11.521); Strabo found the verses a riddle rather than a clear statement: no one knew whom to understand by the Ceteians, nor what the gifts to a woman were, and the grammarians invented little myths rather than solving the question (*Geography* 13.1.69; Merry on 11.521).
