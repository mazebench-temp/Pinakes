**The verse among the dead.** Nestor's complaint, “my strength / is not such as it was before in my supple limbs” (*Iliad* 11.668–669), is an old man's; here the same words are said of a dead man. The soul of Agamemnon knows Odysseus at once when it has drunk the dark blood, weeps, and stretches out its hands, eager to reach him, but it has no strength to do it (11.390–394; see [[agamemnon]], [[dark-blood]] and [[the-strengthless-heads]]). W. W. Merry supplies the thought that the verse leaves out, “but [he did not reach me], for …” (*Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 11.393), and takes γναμπτοῖσι μέλεσσι, “supple limbs”, as “contrasting the limbs of the living with the stiffness and starkness of the dead” (on 11.394). Odysseus had tried three times to clasp his mother, and three times she flew from his hands like a shadow (11.204–208); now it is the dead man who reaches out, and cannot.

**Both nouns.** The verse here has both of the words that its two other forms divide: ἲς ἔμπεδος, “steadfast strength”, as in Nestor's verse, and κῖκυς, “vigor”, the word of the Hymn to Aphrodite, where Tithonus, made immortal but not young, has a voice that flows on endlessly, “but there is no vigor, / such as there was before in his supple limbs” (*Homeric Hymn to Aphrodite* 237–238, a plain rendering; see [[tithonus]]). κῖκυς is found in Homer only here, and Merry connects it with κίω and κινέω, so that it joins the ideas of movement and of power (on 11.393; see the translation's note on 11.393). Homer has the adjective ἄκικυς, “feeble”, of the Cyclops' estimate of the man who blinded him and of Telemachus' fear for himself (9.515, 21.131). The word comes again in a fragment of Aeschylus' *Sisyphus*, “but there is no vigor (κῖκυς) in you, nor blood-flowing veins”, beside another which says that “in the sinews of the dead there is no moisture” (frs. 229–230 Nauck, plain renderings; see [[aeschylus]]), and the sinews, ἶνες, are what Anticleia says no longer “hold the flesh and the bones together” in the dead (11.219; see [[the-way-of-mortals]]).
