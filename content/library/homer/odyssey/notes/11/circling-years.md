---
summary: Poseidon to Tyro: “and as the year circles round / you will bear splendid children” (11.248–249); and the seer was released “when the months and the days were being fulfilled / as the year circled round again, and the seasons came round” (11.294–295).
grc: ["περιπλομένου δʼ ἐνιαυτοῦ", "περιτελλομένου ἔτεος"]
en: [as the year circles round, as the year circled round]
---

Two participles of the turning year in one catalogue. ἂψ περιτελλομένου ἔτεος is the Iliad's word of the circling years (*Iliad* 8.404; see the translation's note on 11.295). περιπλομένου ἐνιαυτοῦ was a puzzle, since a child is not carried for a year: Eustathius took it for a year in the course of completion, not yet complete, and Aulus Gellius reports Favorinus' explanation that the year is drawing to its close, not finished (*Attic Nights* 3.16; Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 11.248; see [[tyro]]).
