summary: Odysseus sees “the mother of Oedipus, fair Epicaste”, who married her son unknowing; the gods made it known at once, she hanged herself, and Oedipus went on ruling the Cadmeians in Thebes, suffering all the sorrows that the Erinyes of a mother bring to pass (11.271–280).

**The oldest form of the story.** The catalogue tells it in ten lines (11.271–280; see [[jocasta]], where they are quoted). Oedipus “had slain his own father” and “married her” (11.273–274), but the poem names neither Laius nor the oracle, the exposure on Cithaeron or the Sphinx, and says nothing of the blinding or of an exile. W. W. Merry calls it the story in its simplest and probably earliest form: the incest, the parricide and Epicaste's suicide are the only events recorded, and ἄφαρ, “at once”, means that the truth came out as soon as the marriage was made (*Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 11.271, 11.274; see the translation's note on 11.273–274). Oedipus lives on as king: “But he, suffering sorrows in lovely Thebes / through the deadly plans of the gods, ruled the Cadmeans” (11.275–276; see [[cadmeians]]), where Merry joins θεῶν διὰ βουλάς with ἄλγεα πάσχων and suspects a contrast between the loveliness of the city and the misery of its king (on 11.275); the Iliad knows of his funeral games at Thebes (*Iliad* 23.679–680). His mother left him “very many” sorrows, “all that the Erinyes of a mother bring to pass” (11.279–280; see [[erinyes]] and [[curses]]).

**The name and the later versions.** Merry notes the form Οἰδιπόδαο, as in the Iliad (*Iliad* 23.679), and doubts the story of the swollen feet, which Homer does not mention; Schneidewin, he reports, referred the name to οἶδα, “know”, comparing Sophocles' “Oedipus who knew nothing” (*Oedipus the King* 397, as Merry quotes it). He quotes Grote on the old epic *Oedipodia*, in which Oedipus' four children were born to a second wife, Euryganeia, after Epicaste's death (on 11.271; see [[oedipus-kills-laius]] and [[the-seven-against-thebes]]).
