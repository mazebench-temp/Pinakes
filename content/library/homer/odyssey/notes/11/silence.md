summary: Odysseus breaks off his tale, “So he spoke, and they all fell hushed in silence, / and they were held by a spell throughout the shadowy halls” (11.333–334); his mother “sits in silence near the blood” until she has drunk (11.142); and the soul of Ajax “answered me nothing” (11.563).
grc: [ἀκέουσʼ]
---

The verse of the silence after a challenge in the Iliad and after the stranger's boast at the games (8.234) is here the silence of listeners held by the tale, and it returns when the tale is over (13.1–2; see [[shadowy-mountains]], [[thelgo]] and [[the-tale-to-alcinous]]). The silence of the dead is of two kinds: Anticleia “has not brought herself / to look on her own son face to face or to speak to him” until she drinks the blood (11.142–143, 11.153; see [[dark-blood]]), and Ajax will not speak, though he has heard Odysseus' soothing words (11.552–564; see [[meeting-with-the-dead]] and [[soothing-words]]).
