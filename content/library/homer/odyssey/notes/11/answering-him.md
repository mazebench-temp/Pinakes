summary: Odysseus tells his answers to the dead with “So he spoke, and I answered him and said” (11.79, 11.138, 11.435, 11.462, 11.477, 11.504; to his mother “So she spoke, and I answered her and said,” 11.163), and their answers with “So I spoke, and at once he answered me and said” (11.145, 11.404, 11.440, 11.487); his mother answers with “So I spoke, and at once my lady mother answered” (11.180, 11.215). In the pause Alcinous and Odysseus answer each other in the verses of Book 7 (11.347, 11.354, 11.362, 11.377); and Ajax “answered me nothing” (11.563).
grc: ["αὐτίκʼ ἀμείβετο πότνια μήτηρ", "οὐδὲν ἀμείβετο"]
en: [answered me nothing]
---

ἀμειβόμενος προσέειπον, the first-person verse of the tale (see the Odyssey translation's formulas), is the frame of the conversations with Teiresias, with his mother, with Agamemnon and with Achilles, and its third-person form gives the dead their replies (see [[so-he-spoke]] and [[meeting-with-the-dead]]). The pause in the hall uses the verses of the court of Alcinous: “Then Alcinous answered him in turn and said” (11.347, 11.362 = 7.298) and “Answering him, Odysseus of many wiles said” (11.354, 11.377 = 7.207; see [[of-many-wiles]]). The formula of answer is turned at the end into its denial, when the soul of Ajax will not speak: “So I spoke, and he answered me nothing” (11.563; see [[ajax]]).
