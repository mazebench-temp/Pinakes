summary: The sails are set at the launching, Circe sends “a following wind that filled the sail”, and all day long the sails are stretched as the ship crosses the sea to the limits of Oceanus (11.3, 11.7, 11.11).
# πλησίστιον, "that filled the sail" (11.7; again 12.149), the compound of ἱστίον, first met here; it applies in the
# Odyssey only.
grc: [πλησίστιον]
---

The ship sails without a pilot, as Circe promised (10.505–507): “And behind our dark-prowed ship / she sent a following wind that filled the sail, a good companion, / Circe of the lovely tresses, a dread goddess with human voice” (11.6–8; see [[circe]], [[the-winds]] and [[with-human-voice]]). πλησίστιος, “filling the sail”, from πλήθω and ἱστίον, and ἐσθλὸν ἑταῖρον, “a good companion”, make the wind a member of the crew (see the translation's row ἴκμενος οὖρος; πλησίστιος). “And all day long her sails were stretched as she crossed the sea. / And the sun set, and all the streets grew dark” (11.11–12; see [[sunset-lines]]).
