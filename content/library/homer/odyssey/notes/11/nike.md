---
summary: Neoptolemus in council was surpassed only by Nestor and Odysseus (11.512); Ajax's soul stands apart, still angry at the victory that Odysseus won over him for the arms of Achilles, and Odysseus wishes he had never won it (11.543–548).
---

**In counsel.** Neoptolemus always spoke first and did not miss the right words: “godlike Nestor and I alone surpassed him” (νικάσκομεν, 11.511–512; see [[neoptolemus]] and [[nestor]]), where one ancient reading gave νεικέσκομεν, “we argued with him” (Merry's critical note on 11.512).

**The victory over Ajax.** The soul of Ajax stood apart, “still angry because of the victory / that I won over him when I pleaded my case beside the ships / for the armor of Achilles” (κεχολωμένη εἵνεκα νίκης, τήν μιν ἐγὼ νίκησα, 11.544–546; see [[ajax]] and [[the-judgment-of-arms]]). Thetis set the armor as a prize, ἔθηκε, as prizes are set for a contest (*Iliad* 23.262–265; W. W. Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 11.546), and the sons of the Trojans and Athena judged (11.547). Odysseus regrets the victory itself: “Would that I had never been the victor for such a prize!”, ἀέθλῳ being the prize, as ἕνεκʼ αὐτῶν, “because of it”, refers back to the armor (11.548–549; Merry on 11.548; see [[cholos]]).
