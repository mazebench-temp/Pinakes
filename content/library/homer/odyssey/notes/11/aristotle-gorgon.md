**The problem.** A fragment of Aristotle's *Homeric Problems* sets this verse beside the verses of the aegis (*Iliad* 5.738–742): why does Homer say in one place that the Gorgon's head is in Hades and in another that Athena carries it on the aegis, with Strife and Onslaught? Aristotle's answer is that perhaps she did not carry the Gorgon's actual head on the aegis, any more than Strife itself, but the fright that the Gorgon produces in those who look at it (*Homeric Problems* fr. 377 Gigon, paraphrased; see [[gorgon]], [[aegis]] and [[aristotle]]). The solution keeps the head where this book puts it, in the house of Hades, and makes the Iliad's head an image of terror.

**Merry's answer.** W. W. Merry removes the difficulty from the other side. Odysseus, he thinks, uses the phrase for any horrible spectre, not for the Gorgon's head that the Iliad puts on Athena's aegis and on Agamemnon's shield and in Hector's eyes, and he translates “a Gorgon-head of some terrible monster” (*Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 11.632–634; see the translation's note on 11.632–634). He takes δεινοῖο πελώρου as in apposition to the Gorgon implied in the adjective Γοργείην, as κυνώπιδος, “dog-faced”, agrees with the speaker implied in ἐμός (*Iliad* 3.180), and Πυλοιγενέος βασιλῆος, “the king born in Pylos”, with the man implied in Νεστορέῃ, “of Nestor” (*Iliad* 2.54; Merry on 11.634). The translation keeps the Gorgon, “the Gorgon's head, the head of the dread monster”.

**Persephone and the edge of the world.** It is Persephone who would send the head (11.635; see [[persephone]] and [[noble]]), and the fear closes the book's visit to the dead: Odysseus goes at once to the ship (11.636–640; see [[pale-fear]]). Hesiod sets the Gorgons themselves “beyond glorious Oceanus, at the edge, toward Night”, where the clear-voiced Hesperides live, and tells how Perseus cut off Medusa's head (*Theogony* 274–281, a plain rendering; see [[hesiod]]): the same far side of Oceanus to which Odysseus sailed to call up the dead (11.13–22; see [[oceanus]] and [[cimmerians]]).
