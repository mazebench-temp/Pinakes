---
summary: Odysseus asks Teiresias how his mother may know him (11.140–144) and asks his mother how she died (11.170–173); Alcinous asks in the pause whether he saw any of his companions who died at Troy (11.370–372); and Agamemnon asks whether he has heard of Orestes alive (11.457–461).
---

The book has the verse four times. Twice Odysseus uses it of the dead, and twice it is used to him, by his host and by the soul of Agamemnon. Alcinous' question, like the Iliad's, is asked at night: “This night is very long, endless” (11.373; see [[endless]] and [[the-tale-to-alcinous]]). Two of the questions offer a choice: “Was it a long sickness, or did Artemis …” (11.172), “perhaps in Orchomenus, or in sandy Pylos, / or perhaps with Menelaus in broad Sparta” (11.459–460; see [[sandy]]).
