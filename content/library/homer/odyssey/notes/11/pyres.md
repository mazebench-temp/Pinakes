---
summary: At the pit Odysseus vows to the dead that when he comes home to Ithaca he will sacrifice a barren heifer and heap the pyre with good things (11.30–31).
---

“Vowing that, when I came to Ithaca, a barren heifer, the best I had, / I would sacrifice in my halls, and heap the pyre with good things” (11.30–31; see [[the-offering-to-the-dead]], [[sacrifice]] and [[euchomai]]), the vow that Circe dictated (10.521–523). The pyre here is not a funeral pyre but the fire of an offering to the dead, on which, as on Patroclus' pyre, precious things are burned (*Iliad* 23.166–177). Elpenor asks for the other kind: “but burn me with my armor, whatever I have” (11.74; see [[cremation]] and [[elpenor]]).
