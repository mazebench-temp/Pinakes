---
summary: “Anticleia, daughter of great-hearted Autolycus, / whom I had left alive when I went to sacred Ilios” (11.85–86).
---

μεγαλήτορος, of Odysseus' grandfather, the man who, the poem says later, excelled all men in thieving and in oaths (19.395–396, a plain rendering; see [[autolycus]]). Merry recalls that Autolycus gave Odysseus his name (19.403–409) and stole the helmet of Amyntor in the Iliad (*Iliad* 10.266–267; Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 11.85).
