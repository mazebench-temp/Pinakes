---
summary: Poseidon, in the likeness of the river Enipeus, lies with Tyro, “and he clung to her hand, and spoke to her, and called her by name” (11.247).
---

The verse is Antinous' to Telemachus in the assembly (2.302), and here the god's at parting from a mortal woman (see [[tyro]] and [[poseidon]]). He calls her only γύναι, “lady,” and tells her to keep silent and not to name him, before he names himself: “but I, you must know, am Poseidon the earth-shaker” (11.248–252; see [[earth-shaker]]).
