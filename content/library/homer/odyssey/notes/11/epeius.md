summary: Named as the maker of the wooden horse, in Odysseus' account to Achilles of his son's courage: “And when we went down into the horse that Epeius made” (11.523).
# Ἐπειός (11.523) is claimed by the entry.
---

αὐτὰρ ὅτʼ εἰς ἵππον κατεβαίνομεν, ὃν κάμʼ Ἐπειός, “And when we went down into the horse that Epeius made, / we, the best of the Argives” (11.523–524; see [[the-wooden-horse]], [[sinon-and-the-wooden-horse]] and [[neoptolemus]]). κάμε, “made”, is the verb of a craftsman's labor. In the house of Alcinous Odysseus had asked Demodocus to sing of the same horse, “which Epeius made with Athena's help” (8.492–493), and this second mention is the last time the poem names its maker (see [[athena]] and [[demodocus]]).
