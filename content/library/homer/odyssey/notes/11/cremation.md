summary: Elpenor asks to be burned with his armor and given a mound with his oar on it (11.74–78), and Anticleia explains why the dead cannot be held: once the spirit has left the bones, “the strong might of the blazing fire” overcomes flesh and bones, and the soul flits away like a dream (11.219–222). Odysseus vows the dead a pyre heaped with good things (11.31).
# κακκῆαι (11.74, only here); "πυρήν τʼ ἐμπλησέμεν" (10.523, 11.31); "πυρὸς κρατερὸν μένος" (11.220).
grc: [κακκῆαι, "πυρήν τʼ ἐμπλησέμεν", "πυρὸς κρατερὸν μένος"]
en: [burn me, heap the pyre, the strong might of the blazing fire]
---

**Elpenor's pyre.** The soul of Elpenor asks Odysseus to burn him “with my armor, whatever I have” (11.74; see [[elpenor]] and [[honoring-the-dead]]). Merry compares Ajax in Sophocles, who would have the rest of his armor buried with him (*Ajax* 577, as Merry quotes it), Achilles' burning of Eetion “with his intricate armor” (*Iliad* 6.418), and the treasures laid on the pyre of Patroclus, as if the dead could have the use of them in another world (*Iliad* 23.171 and after; W. W. Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 11.74). The verb is κακκῆαι, Aristarchus' reading against κακκεῖαι (Merry's critical note; see the Odyssey translation's note on 11.74). In Book 12 it is done: “But when the body was burned, and the armor of the body, / we heaped up a tomb” (12.13–14; see [[tomb]]).

**What the fire does.** Anticleia gives the reason the dead cannot be embraced in terms of the pyre: the sinews no longer hold flesh and bones together, “but the strong might of the blazing fire / overcomes them, as soon as the spirit has left the white bones” (11.219–221; see [[the-way-of-mortals]]). The fire is the end of the body and the condition of the soul's going, as the soul of Patroclus says that it will never come back from Hades once it has been given its portion of fire (*Iliad* 23.75–76).

**The vow.** At the pit Odysseus vows the dead a barren heifer in his halls, and to “heap the pyre with good things” (πυρήν τʼ ἐμπλησέμεν ἐσθλῶν, 11.31), the words of Circe's instruction (10.523; see the Book 10 note on [[honoring-the-dead]] and [[the-offering-to-the-dead]]).
