**Peleus in Phthia.** After his son, Achilles asks for news of his father: “And tell me of noble Peleus, if you have heard anything, / whether he still has honor among the many Myrmidons, / or whether they dishonor him throughout Hellas and Phthia, / because old age holds him fast in his hands and feet” (11.494–497; see [[peleus]], [[myrmidons]] and [[hellas]]). Phthia, W. W. Merry notes, must include the country round as well as the town, as in the Catalogue, where the Myrmidons, Hellenes and Achaeans come from Phthia and Hellas (*Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 11.496; *Iliad* 2.683–684). In the Iliad Phthia was the home Achilles threatened to sail back to and never did (*Iliad* 1.169–171, 9.356–363); now he would come back to his father's house, “even for a little while”, as strong as he was at Troy, to make his might hateful to any who wrong the old man (11.498–503; see [[time]]). Odysseus has no news of Peleus to give (11.505).
