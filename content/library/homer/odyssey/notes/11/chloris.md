summary: Odysseus sees beautiful Chloris, the youngest daughter of Amphion son of Iasus, whom Neleus married for her beauty with a countless bride-price; queen in Pylos, she bore him Nestor, Chromius and Periclymenus, and strong Pero (11.281–287).
passages:
  - 11.281-297 | Chloris and her children
---

**Queen in Pylos.** Neleus married her “for her beauty, after giving a countless bride-price” (11.282; see [[neleus]] and [[bride-price]]), the words in which the Iliad tells of Hector winning Andromache (*Iliad* 22.472). ἡ δὲ Πύλου βασίλευε is “she was queen in Pylos”: Πύλου is a local genitive, W. W. Merry explains, and it was her marriage that made her queen, so that οἱ, “to him”, refers to the king implied in βασίλευε (*Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 11.285; see [[pylos]] and the translation's note on 11.284–285). Her father, “Amphion, son of Iasus, / who once ruled with might in Minyan Orchomenus” (11.283–284; see [[amphion-son-of-iasus]] and [[orchomenus]]), is not the Theban Amphion of twenty lines before (11.262).

**Nestor's mother.** The first of her sons is the old king whose house Telemachus visited in Book 3 (see [[nestor]]); Merry adds that Neleus had twelve sons in all, but all except Nestor were slain by Heracles (on 11.285; *Iliad* 11.690–693; see [[chromius-son-of-neleus]] and [[periclymenus]]). The rest of her story is her daughter's: the bride-price of cattle that Neleus asked for Pero, and the seer who paid it (11.287–297; see [[pero]] and [[melampus]]). Merry gives the whole family in a table, from Salmoneus and Iasus down through Pero and Bias to Amphiaraus and [[theoclymenus|Theoclymenus]] (on 11.281).
