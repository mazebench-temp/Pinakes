summary: Chloris is “the youngest daughter of Amphion, son of Iasus, / who once ruled with might in Minyan Orchomenus” (11.283–284), and Agamemnon's soul asks whether Orestes may be living in Orchomenus, or in Pylos, or in Sparta (11.458–460).
---

**Minyan Orchomenus.** Neleus married Chloris for her beauty, after giving a countless bride-price, “the youngest daughter of Amphion, son of Iasus, / who once ruled with might in Minyan Orchomenus” (11.281–284; see [[neleus]] and [[bride-price]]). This ancient Boeotian city, W. W. Merry explains, at the inflow of the Cephisus into Lake Copais, was the capital of the kingdom of the Minyae, named after their king Minyas, who built the famous treasure house there; the legends also speak of a Minyan Orchomenus in Thessaly, and Nitzsch preferred to take this one for the Thessalian, comparing *Iliad* 2.511 and 9.381. Merry thinks the question makes little difference to the story, since the Minyae were said to have come from Thessaly into Boeotia, and two Minyan cities called Orchomenus would have had the same people (*Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 11.284). Μινυείῳ is La Roche's reading, after Bekker, for the common Μινυηίῳ, since the shortening of a long vowel before ἶφι, which once began with a digamma, would be without parallel in Homer (Merry's critical note on 11.284; see the translation's note on 11.284–285). The adjective is the Iliad's Μινύειον, of the same city (*Iliad* 2.511; see [[minyeios]] for the river of the same name in Elis). From Minyan Orchomenus Chloris went to be queen in Pylos (11.285; see [[pylos]]).

**Orchomenus, Pylos or Sparta.** Agamemnon, who knows nothing of his son since his death, asks Odysseus whether he has heard of Orestes as still living somewhere, “perhaps in Orchomenus, or in sandy Pylos, / or perhaps with Menelaus in broad Sparta” (11.458–460; see [[orestes]], [[sparta]] and [[pylos]]). Merry sees no particular reason for these places, except that each would be a refuge at a good distance from Mycenae, and notes that no clue is given whether the Boeotian or the Arcadian Orchomenus is meant (on 11.458; see [[orchomenus-arcadia]]); here it is taken for the Minyan city of the earlier verse. He finds it surprising that neither Phocis nor Athens is named, Athens being the city from which Orestes came back to take vengeance in the third book (3.307; Merry on 11.458; see [[athens]]).
