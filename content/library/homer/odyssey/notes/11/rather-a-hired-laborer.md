---
summary: The soul of Achilles, told by Odysseus that he is mighty among the dead and should not grieve, answers that he would rather serve a landless man for hire on earth than rule all the dead (11.482–491), and turns at once to ask about his son and his father.
passages:
  - 11.473-491 | Achilles and Odysseus
---

The exchange begins with Achilles' own question, how Odysseus dared to come down to Hades, “where the dead / dwell without sense, the phantoms of dead mortals” (11.473–476; see [[achilles]] and [[the-land-of-the-dead]]). Odysseus answers with his own misfortune and Achilles' fortune: none more blessed, honored like the gods in life, mighty among the dead (11.478–486; see [[three-times-blessed]] and [[time]]). The refusal is the answer of a man who knows what the dead are, and it is followed at once by what he still cares for among the living: “But come, tell me word of my noble son” and of Peleus, whom he can no longer defend (11.492–503; see [[neoptolemus]], [[peleus]] and [[bereaved-parents]]). Odysseus' account of Neoptolemus sends him away glad across the meadow of asphodel (11.505–540; see [[the-meadow-of-asphodel]]).
