summary: The blinding of the Cyclops shapes the prophecy of the dead. Teiresias names first the anger of Poseidon, “angry because you blinded his dear son” (11.101–103), and foretells the homecoming in the two verses of the Cyclops' curse (11.114–115).
# Unnamed: Poseidon's anger for his son (11.101-103); the curse in the seer's mouth (11.114-115).

**The god's grudge.** Teiresias begins with the obstacle: “but a god will make it grievous for you; for I do not think / you will escape the shaker of the earth, since he has laid up a grudge against you in his heart, / angry because you blinded his dear son” (11.101–103; see [[poseidon]] and [[polyphemus-the-cyclops]]). The god was heard: when the Cyclops prayed, “the dark-haired god heard him” (9.536), and Zeus said at the first council of the gods that Poseidon was angry because of the Cyclops, whom Odysseus blinded of his eye (1.68–75; see [[odysseus-and-the-cyclops]]). ὅ is “in that”, and the verse of the grudge comes back when Athena tells Odysseus why she would not fight her uncle Poseidon (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 11.102; 13.341–343; see the translation's note on 11.102). Even so, the seer adds, they may still come home, though suffering evils (11.104).

**The curse in the seer's mouth.** The Cyclops prayed that, if Odysseus was fated to reach home, he might “come late and in evil plight, having lost all his companions, / on someone else's ship, and find bane in his house” (9.534–535). Teiresias foretells it in the same verses, now in the second person and on a condition: if the cattle of the Sun are harmed, “you will come home late and in evil plight, having lost all your companions, / on someone else's ship, and find bane in your house” (11.114–115; see [[the-cattle-of-helios]] and [[the-prophecy-of-teiresias]]). The curse of a son, heard by his father, and the warning of a seer about another god's cattle end in the same homecoming.

**The dead of the cave.** None of the companions whom the Cyclops ate come to the pit; the scholia's answer, that they had a burial of a sort, is discussed under [[elpenor]].
