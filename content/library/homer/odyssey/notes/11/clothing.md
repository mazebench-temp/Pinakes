---
summary: In winter Laertes sleeps where the slaves sleep, in the dust near the fire, “and wears poor clothes on his body” (11.191).
---

κακὰ δὲ χροῒ εἵματα εἷται, “and wears poor clothes on his body” (11.191; see [[laertes]], [[fire]] and [[bed]]): the clothes of a slave or a beggar, which Odysseus himself will wear on Ithaca. Merry renders it “and sorry clothes has he got on his back” (*Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 11.188). When Odysseus finds him at last in Book 24, he is digging round a plant in his orchard in a dirty, patched tunic, “nursing his sorrow” (24.226–231, a plain rendering).
