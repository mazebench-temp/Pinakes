# πατέρος (11.501).
grc: [πατέρος]
en: [father's]
---

Fathers are among the bonds the dead invoke and ask about. Elpenor implores Odysseus “by your wife and your father, who reared you when you were small, / and by Telemachus” (11.67–68; see [[supplication]]), and Odysseus asks his mother first of all about his father and son (11.174). Anticleia's answer is the fullest picture of Laertes before Book 24: “your father stays there / in the country, and does not come down to the city”, sleeping in winter in the dust by the fire, in summer on beds of leaves on the slope of his vineyard, “longing for your homecoming” (11.187–196; see [[laertes]] and [[bereaved-parents]]). Agamemnon contrasts his own fate with Odysseus': “his dear father will see him when he comes, / and he will greet his father, as is the custom” (11.450–451; see [[telemachus]] and [[fathers-and-sons]]). Oedipus “had slain his own father” before he married his mother (11.273; see [[oedipus-kills-laius]]). And Achilles, asking for news of Peleus, wishes he could come “even for a little while, to my father's house”, as the man he was at Troy (11.501; see [[peleus]]).
