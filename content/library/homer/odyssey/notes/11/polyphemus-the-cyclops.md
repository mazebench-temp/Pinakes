# Unnamed: "his dear son" (11.103).
refs: [11.103]
---

**His dear son.** Teiresias tells Odysseus that he will not escape the shaker of the earth, “since he has laid up a grudge against you in his heart, / angry because you blinded his dear son” (11.102–103; see [[poseidon]] and [[odysseus-and-the-cyclops]]). The seer does not name the son. The prophecy that follows grants the end of the Cyclops' prayer to his father, that Odysseus should come home late and in evil plight, having lost all his companions, on someone else's ship, and find bane in his house (11.114–115 after 9.534–535; see [[curses]] and the Book 9 note).

**The men he ate.** None of the companions whom the Cyclops ate comes to the blood. The scholia asked why neither they nor the men killed by the Laestrygonians came to meet their captain, and answered that, unlike Elpenor, they were not waiting about the gates of Hades, since they had been buried after a fashion, though not with due rites. Merry does not think the answer absurd: the great disadvantage of lying unburied was to be left visible in the upper world after death, and to have been eaten by the Cyclops at least hid them from sight (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 11.58; see [[elpenor]] and [[honoring-the-dead]]).
