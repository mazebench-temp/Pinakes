# The river without the name: "in his likeness" (11.241) and "the mouth of the eddying river" (11.242).
refs: [11.241-242]
---

**The fairest of the rivers.** Tyro, daughter of Salmoneus and wife of Cretheus, comes first among the women sent by Persephone, and the first thing told of her is her love:

{{quote:7de8bfdf-3850-5a78-a51b-ee68c26f6e2a}}

The river is θεῖος, “divine”, a god as well as a stream, and it is praised above all rivers. ἵησι, “runs”, is intransitive, as of the spring in the garden of Alcinous (W. W. Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 7.130 and 11.239; see [[the-garden-of-alcinous]]). πωλέσκετο, “used to go often”, is an iterative: she haunted the river's banks (see [[tyro]]).

**In his likeness.** τῷ δʼ ἄρα εἰσάμενος, “and in his likeness”, is the Earthshaker “having made himself like unto him”, the river god (Merry on 11.241), and the god lay with her ἐν προχοῇς ποταμοῦ … δινήεντος, “at the mouth of the eddying river” (11.241–242; see [[poseidon]], [[earth-holder]] and [[eddying]]). Merry notes that the words can only mean the outfall or mouth of the river, and that they are used loosely, since to admit Poseidon the river should flow straight into the sea, whereas the Thessalian Enipeus falls into the Peneius (on 11.242); the god goes back “beneath the billowing sea” when he leaves her (11.253). Poseidon names himself only at the end, when he bids her keep silent (11.251–252): until then the lover was the river she loved.
