# Unnamed: "a man far worse than I", who laid the labors on Heracles and sent him for the hound (11.621-624).
refs: [11.621-624]
---

“I was the son of Zeus, son of Cronus, yet I had misery / boundless; for I was made subject to a man far worse than I, / and he laid hard labors upon me. / And once he sent me here to bring the hound; for he could think / of no other labor that would be harder for me than this” (11.620–624; see [[heracles]] and [[heracles-and-cerberus]]). χείρονι φωτί is Eurystheus, son of Sthenelus, to whose service Heracles was bound through the spiteful intrigues of Hera, as Agamemnon tells in the Iliad (*Iliad* 19.95–133; W. W. Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 11.621). The Iliad's Athena, too, remembers sending Heracles to Hades for the hound at Eurystheus' bidding (*Iliad* 8.362–369). Homer does not list the labors, nor name the hound (Merry on 11.623).
