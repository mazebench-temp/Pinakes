summary: The book shows the dead coming up to the blood at the edge of Erebus: the gathering of the souls (11.36–43), the meetings one at a time, which the blood makes possible (11.51–567), and then, as if from within, the judge, the hunter and the punished (11.568–631), until the countless tribes of the dead drive Odysseus back to the ship (11.632–640).

**The place.** The land is described by those who belong to it. Teiresias asks why Odysseus has left the light of the sun “to see the dead and this joyless place” (ἀτερπέα χῶρον, 11.93–94; see the Odyssey translation's table of key words, ἀτερπὴς χῶρος); his mother marvels that he has come “beneath the misty darkness / while you are alive”, since “it is hard for the living to see these things”, and great rivers lie between, Oceanus first of all (11.155–159; see [[oceanus]]), verses the Alexandrian critics rejected (W. W. Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, his critical note on 11.157–159). Achilles asks how he dared to come down to Hades, “where the dead / dwell without sense, the phantoms of dead mortals” (11.475–476; see [[phrenes]] and [[eidolon]]). The souls come “out of Erebus” and go back into it (11.37, 11.564; see [[erebus]]), and go into “the house of Hades” (11.150, 11.627; see [[hades]]); Achilles and Orion range over “the meadow of asphodel” (11.539, 11.573; see [[the-meadow-of-asphodel]]).

**The order of the dead.** The dead come by kinds: a crowd at first, then the unburied companion, the mother, the seer, the women one at a time, the companions of the war, each with those who died with him (11.36–567; see [[odysseus-in-the-land-of-the-dead]]). The blood keeps the order: Odysseus' sword lets one drink at a time (11.48–50, 11.228–234; see [[the-offering-to-the-dead]]). With Minos, Orion, Tityus, Tantalus, Sisyphus and Heracles the scene changes: Odysseus no longer questions souls at the pit but sees, as an eyewitness, what goes on in the depths of Erebus, and Merry takes the passage for a later addition (Merry on 11.565; see [[minos]], [[orion]], [[tityus]], [[tantalus]], [[sisyphus]] and [[heracles]]).
