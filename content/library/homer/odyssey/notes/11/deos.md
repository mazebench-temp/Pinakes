---
summary: “Pale fear took hold of me”, Odysseus says twice: when the souls first gather with a wondrous cry (11.43), and at the end, when the countless tribes of the dead come with a wondrous din and he fears that Persephone will send the Gorgon's head (11.633–635).
---

The verse frames the book (ἐμὲ δὲ χλωρὸν δέος ᾕρει, 11.43, 11.633; see [[pale-fear]]). The first time, Merry sees no reason for panic except the weird sound of the cries raised by the souls; the second time there is good ground for terror, the Gorgon's head (W. W. Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 11.43; see [[gorgon]] and [[persephone]]). Fear sends Odysseus back to the ship at once (11.636–640). Fear of another kind shows in the wooden horse, where the limbs of each leader “trembled beneath him”, and not Neoptolemus' (11.527; see [[the-brave-man-in-ambush]]).
