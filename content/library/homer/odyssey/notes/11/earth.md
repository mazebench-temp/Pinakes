---
summary: In the book of the dead the earth is what holds the dead: Elpenor not yet buried beneath it (11.52), the Dioscuri held fast by it though alive (11.301–304), Ajax's head that it holds fast because of the arms (11.549). Earth is also a mother, “glorious Earth”, whose son Tityus lies among the punished (11.576).
---

**The earth that holds the dead.** Elpenor's soul comes first to the pit, “for he had not yet been buried beneath the wide-wayed earth” (11.52; see [[elpenor]] and [[wide-wayed-earth]]). Castor and Polydeuces are held by it in a stranger way: “both of whom the life-giving earth holds fast, alive though they are; / and even beneath the earth, having honor from Zeus, / now they live on alternate days, and now again / they are dead” (11.301–304; see [[castor]], [[polydeuces]] and [[life-giving-earth]]); the Iliad had said of them only that the life-giving earth already held them fast (*Iliad* 3.243), and W. W. Merry finds ζωούς, “alive”, hard to reconcile with that verse, where κατέχει has its regular sense of the dead and buried: only by straining the interpretation, he says, can the two forms of the story be made to agree (*Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 11.301; see the translation's note on 11.298–304). Odysseus says of Ajax that “because of it the earth has held fast such a head” (11.549; see [[ajax]]), and Agamemnon's soul is sure that Orestes “has not yet died upon the earth” (11.461; see [[upon-the-earth]]).

**Glorious Earth.** In this book Earth is named as a person once, as the mother of the giant whom Odysseus sees stretched over nine plethra: “And I saw Tityus, the son of glorious Earth” (11.576; see [[tityus]] and [[glorious]]). The seventh book calls him Τιτυὸν Γαιήιον υἱόν, “Tityus, the son of Earth”, whom the Phaeacians carried Rhadamanthys to visit (7.324), and Strabo, quoting that verse, says that the stories of Tityus were told at Panopeus, and that a cave named after his mother Elara was shown in Euboea (*Geography* 9.3.14; see [[panopeus]] and [[strabo]]). Elsewhere Earth is a power called to witness oaths (*Iliad* 3.104, 3.276–280, 19.258–260), and in the Catalogue the grain-giving earth is the mother of Erechtheus (*Iliad* 2.548; see [[erechtheus]] and [[oath]]).

**The earth that grows.** The other earth of the book is the ground that feeds: “the grain-giving earth” reared the tallest men of all, Otus and Ephialtes (11.309; see [[grain-giving]]); “the black earth feeds” many deceivers and cheats (11.365; see [[black-earth]]); and around the feet of Tantalus, when the water is swallowed up, “the black earth appeared” (11.587; see [[tantalus]]). Teiresias tells Odysseus to fix his oar in the ground in the land of men who do not know the sea (11.129; see [[teiresias]] and [[winnowing-fan]]), and the native land, πατρὶς γαῖα, is where Agamemnon bids him bring his ship secretly (11.455; see [[dear-native-land]]).
