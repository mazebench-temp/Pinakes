passages:
  - 11.321-325 | Phaedra, Procris and Ariadne
---

“And I saw Phaedra and Procris and beautiful Ariadne” (11.321; see [[procris]], [[ariadne]] and [[catalogue-of-heroines]]). Phaedra and Procris have no epithet, no father and no story; only Ariadne, the third, is told in the four verses that follow (11.322–325). W. W. Merry notes that the three names belong to Attic legend and break the continuity of the Thessalian and Boeotian stories, and thinks 11.321–325 probably a later interpolation, perhaps of the time of the Peisistratean recension (*Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 11.321; see the translation's note on 11.321–325). He supplies what the verse leaves out: Phaedra was the daughter of the Cretan Minos, the wife of Theseus after the death of Hippolyte, and her fate was caused by her guilty love for her stepson Hippolytus (on 11.321; see [[minos]] and [[theseus]]). In that story Phaedra and Ariadne are sisters, and Phaedra dies as Epicaste dies earlier in the catalogue, who “fastened a noose hanging sheer from the high roof beam” (11.277–278; Euripides, *Hippolytus* 802; see [[jocasta]] and the general article).
