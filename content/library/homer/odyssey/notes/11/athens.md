---
summary: Theseus “was bringing from Crete to the hill of sacred Athens” the daughter of Minos, Ariadne, when Artemis killed her on Dia (11.321–325).
---

**The hill of sacred Athens.** The only mention of the city in the book is in the story of Ariadne, whom Theseus was carrying off from Crete ἐς γουνὸν Ἀθηνάων ἱεράων, “to the hill of sacred Athens”, “but he had no joy of her” (11.323–324; see [[ariadne]], [[theseus]] and [[crete]]). The plural Ἀθῆναι is the name of the city here, as in Nestor's story of Orestes, who came “back from Athens” (3.307), while the seventh book has the singular, Ἀθήνη (7.80; see the Book 3 and Book 7 notes); with Θήβη and Θῆβαι, Μυκήνη and Μυκῆναι it is one of the names that have both forms (W. W. Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 11.263; see [[thebes]] and [[mycenae]]). γουνός is a rising ground, as in “the slope of his vineyard”, where Laertes sleeps in summer (11.193); Merry renders the phrase “the soil of sacred Athens” (on 11.322). ἱεράων, “sacred”, is the epithet of cities in Homer (see [[hieros]]).

**Athenian names in the Nekyia.** Phaedra, Procris and Ariadne belong to Attic legend, and Merry thinks the five verses on them a later interpolation, which, like other passages that touch on Athenian legend, may belong to the time of the Peisistratean recension (on 11.321). The same suspicion fell on the verse near the end of the book in which Odysseus would have seen “Theseus and Peirithous, glorious children of the gods” (11.631): Hereas of Megara, as Plutarch reports him, said that Peisistratus put it into Homer's Nekyia to please the Athenians (Merry's critical note on 11.631; see the translation's note on 11.631 and [[theseus-line]]).
