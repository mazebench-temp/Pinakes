---
summary: The black ship is launched with the sheep aboard and runs all day before Circe's wind to the limits of Oceanus, where it is beached (11.1–22), and at the end it is boarded again and carried down the stream of Oceanus (11.636–640). In the speeches the ship is the measure of the journey: Elpenor came faster on foot, no one crosses Oceanus without a well-made ship, and Odysseus will come home “on someone else's ship” (11.58, 11.158–159, 11.114–115).
# The ship's tackle, "ὅπλα" (11.9, ὅπλα ἕκαστα), is linked through the English "tackle" of the Book 10 note.
passages:

  - 11.636-640 | Back on board
---

**The voyage out.** The book begins with a launching: “first of all we dragged the ship down into the shining sea, / and we set the mast and the sails in the black ship, / and we took the sheep and put them on board” (11.2–4; see [[mast]], [[sails]] and [[sheep]]). τὰ μῆλα are “those sheep”, W. W. Merry notes, the ram and the black ewe that Circe had tied up by the ship (*Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 11.4; 10.571–572). “Behind our dark-prowed ship” Circe sends a following wind; the men busy themselves with the tackle, “and the wind and the helmsman steered her”, and “all day long her sails were stretched as she crossed the sea” (11.6–11; see [[dark-prowed]] and [[sailing-type-scene]]), as Circe had promised that the North Wind would carry the ship without a guide (10.505–507). At the limits of Oceanus “we came and ran the ship ashore, and took the sheep / out of it” (11.20–21; see [[oceanus]]).

**The ship in the speeches.** Elpenor, who died on Aeaea, has reached the dead before them: “You have come faster on foot than I in my black ship” (11.58; see [[elpenor]] and [[black-ships]]). Anticleia knows that the living cannot cross Oceanus “on foot, unless he has a well-made ship” (11.158–159; see [[anticleia]] and [[well-made]]). Teiresias foretells the island where “you bring your well-made ship” (11.106), the destruction “for your ship and your companions” if they harm the Sun's cattle, and a homecoming “on someone else's ship” (11.112–115; see [[teiresias]], [[the-cattle-of-helios]] and [[thrinacia]]): the Phaeacians' ship, on which the tale is being told. The men inland who will take an oar for a winnowing fan do not know “ships with crimson prows” (11.124; see [[winnowing-fan]]). Agamemnon is asked whether Poseidon overcame him “among the ships” (11.399, 11.406; see [[agamemnon]]), and counsels Odysseus to bring his ship to shore in secret (11.455–456). Odysseus himself brought Neoptolemus from Scyros “in the hollow, balanced ship” (11.508; see [[neoptolemus]], [[hollow-ships]] and [[balanced-ships]]).

**Back on board.** When the dead throng about him, he goes back to the ship: “At once then I went to the ship and ordered my companions / to go on board themselves and loose the stern-cables. / And they went on board at once and sat down at the rowing benches. / And the wave of the stream carried the ship down the river Oceanus, / first with rowing, and afterward a fair wind” (11.636–640; see [[stern-cables]], [[rowing-benches]] and [[oars]]).
