summary: Odysseus answers Agamemnon's account of his death: “Ah, truly far-seeing Zeus has hated the offspring of Atreus / terribly, through the plans of women, / from of old” (11.436–438).
---

ὢ πόποι, ἦ μάλα δή, the opening of the Cyclops' cry when he remembers the oracle of his blinding (9.507). The house of Atreus is the example: Helen, for whose sake many perished, and Clytemnestra, who prepared the snare (11.438–439; see [[far-seeing]], [[atreus]] and [[clytemnestra]]). Aristophanes rejected 11.435–440 (see the translation's note on 11.435–440).
