summary: Named once in Homer, in his son's patronymic: “the son of Telephus, / the hero Eurypylus”, whom Neoptolemus killed at Troy (11.519–520).
# Τήλεφ* (in the entry) claims Τηλεφίδην (11.519), the only time Homer names him; eurypylus-son-of-telephus claims it too.
---

ἀλλʼ οἷον τὸν Τηλεφίδην κατενήρατο χαλκῷ, / ἥρωʼ Εὐρύπυλον, “but what a man he slew with the bronze, the son of Telephus, / the hero Eurypylus” (11.519–520; see [[eurypylus-son-of-telephus]] and [[neoptolemus]]). Τηλεφίδης, “son of Telephus”, is the only place in Homer where Telephus is named. W. W. Merry makes him king of the Mysians and the husband of Astyoche, a sister or a daughter of Priam (*Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 11.520), and Strabo, quoting these verses, says that the poet mentions no more of the story of Teuthras and Telephus, the kings of the country around Teuthrania and the Caicus, setting a riddle rather than saying anything clear (*Geography* 13.1.69; see [[ceteians]]).
