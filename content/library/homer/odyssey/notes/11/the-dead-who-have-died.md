---
summary: The souls “of the dead who have died” gather out of Erebus to the blood (11.36–37); Teiresias tells Odysseus that whichever of them he lets come near the blood will speak truly (11.147–148); they stand grieving after Achilles has gone (11.541–542), and Ajax goes after them into Erebus without a word (11.563–564). Achilles would rather serve another for hire “than be lord over all the dead who have perished” (11.491).
grc: ["νεκύεσσι καταφθιμένοισιν", "ἰδέειν κατατεθνηώτων"]
en: [the dead who have perished, who have died]
---

The book has the verse-end four times, as Circe had foretold it (10.530), and twice more the dead are named in the same doubled way: Achilles' νεκύεσσι καταφθιμένοισιν, “the dead who have perished,” the pleonasm that Merry sets beside this one (11.491; Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 10.530; see [[rather-a-hired-laborer]]), and, after Ajax has gone, Odysseus' wish “to see the souls of the others who have died” (11.567). The first gathering brings the whole throng at once, brides, young men, old men, maidens and warriors (11.38–41; see [[who-had-borne-much]], [[new-to-sorrow]] and [[slain-in-battle]]); the later ones are the souls that stay when the great dead have spoken (see [[psyche]], [[erebus]] and [[meeting-with-the-dead]]).
