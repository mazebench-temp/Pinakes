---
summary: After his mother had hanged herself, Oedipus, “suffering sorrows in lovely Thebes / through the deadly plans of the gods, ruled the Cadmeans” (11.275–276).
---

**Oedipus' people.** The Cadmeans are named once in the Odyssey, in Odysseus' account of Epicaste, the mother of Oedipus, who married her own son in the ignorance of her mind; the gods made it known at once, and while she went to the house of Hades, he went on ruling: Καδμείων ἤνασσε, “ruled the Cadmeans” (11.271–280; see [[jocasta]], [[oedipus]] and [[thebes]]). In the Iliad the name belongs to the generation before Troy, the Thebans whom Tydeus beat at their own feast (*Iliad* 4.385–398); here it is the people of Oedipus, who is king in the Odyssey's story even after the truth is out. This, W. W. Merry notes, is the story in its simplest and probably earliest form: the parricide, the incest and Epicaste's death are the only events recorded, and ἄφαρ, “at once”, means that the truth came out as soon as the marriage had taken place, so that she did not live with him for years and bear him children (*Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 11.271, 11.274; see the translation's note on 11.273–274). Cadmus himself, after whom the people is named, appears in the Odyssey only as the father of Ino (5.333).
