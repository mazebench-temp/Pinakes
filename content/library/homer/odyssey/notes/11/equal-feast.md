---
summary: Anticleia tells her son that no one yet holds his prize of honor: “Telemachus holds the domains, and feasts at equal feasts, / such as it is fitting for a man who gives judgment to attend; / for all men invite him” (11.184–187).
grc: ["δαῖτας ἐίσας"]
en: [equal feasts]
---

The equal feasts are those of a king, to which the people invite him. Merry counts the invitation among the royal privileges summed up in Sarpedon's words to Glaucus, the seat of honor, the meat and the full cups, and the domain (*Iliad* 12.310–314), and notes that if Telemachus could then have been no more than fourteen, he must be sharing in the honors rather than ruling; her account shows that she died before the suitors came (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 11.184; see the translation's note on 11.184–187, and [[telemachus]] and [[feast]]).
