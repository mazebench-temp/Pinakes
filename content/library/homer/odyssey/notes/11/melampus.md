summary: The unnamed “noble seer” who alone promised to drive the cattle of Iphiclus out of Phylace for Pero's hand; a hard fate of a god fettered him with grievous bonds and the country herdsmen, until after a year Iphiclus released him, when he had told all the oracles (11.291–297).
# He is not named in this book: the lines that tell of him are refs (Μελάμποδος is Book 15's, 15.225).
refs: [11.291-297]

{{quote:e50c0b23-ca29-5a29-b4f5-2a256baf0cf6}}

**The bonds.** μάντις ἀμύμων, “the noble seer” (11.291), is the phrase used of Teiresias earlier in the book (11.99; see [[teiresias]] and [[divination]]). His failure is told as a fate laid on him by a god, and δεσμοί and βουκόλοι, the bonds and the herdsmen, describe and define that fate, W. W. Merry explains; ἀγροιῶται, “of the country”, is a form like ἄγραυλοι (*Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 11.293; see [[moira]]). Then the year runs its course, “as the year circled round again, and the seasons came round” (11.295; see the translation's note on 11.295).

**The oracles.** θέσφατα πάντʼ εἰπόντα, “when he had told all the oracles” (11.297; see [[thesphaton]]), was read two ways in antiquity, as Merry reports: Eustathius took it of a prophecy that the childless Iphiclus would have children, which won the seer his release; a scholion of his foreknowledge that he would be caught and held for a year (on 11.297). The close, Διὸς δʼ ἐτελείετο βουλή, “and the plan of Zeus was being fulfilled”, ends the fifth verse of the Iliad (*Iliad* 1.5; see [[plan-of-zeus]]). Book 15 names him, as the ancestor of [[theoclymenus|Theoclymenus]], and tells the rest: Neleus held his goods by force for the year, and when he had escaped and driven the cattle to Pylos, he took vengeance on Neleus, brought the woman home for his brother, and went to rule in Argos (15.225–242; see [[neleus]], [[pero]] and [[melampus-and-the-cattle-of-iphiclus]]).
