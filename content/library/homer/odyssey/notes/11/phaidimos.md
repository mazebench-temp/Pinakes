---
summary: Teiresias, Anticleia and Achilles address Odysseus as “shining Odysseus” (11.100, 11.202, 11.488); and the wayfarer will say that he carries a winnowing fan “on your shining shoulder” (11.128).
grc: [φαιδίμῳ]
---

φαίδιμʼ Ὀδυσσεῦ is each time the address to the living man in a speech of the dead: the opening of the prophecy, “You seek a honey-sweet homecoming, shining Odysseus” (11.100; see [[honey-sweet]]); Anticleia's last words, “but longing for you, and for your counsels, shining Odysseus” (11.202); and Achilles' refusal, “Do not speak comfortingly to me of death, shining Odysseus” (11.488; see [[rather-a-hired-laborer]]). φαιδίμῳ ὤμῳ is the shoulder of the body (as in the Iliad's φαίδιμα γυῖα), on which the oar will be taken for a winnowing fan (11.127–128, again 23.275; see [[oars]]).
