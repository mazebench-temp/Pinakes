**A great deed.** μέγα ἔργον, “a great deed” (11.272), is said of a crime, as of Aegisthus' deed (3.261); W. W. Merry renders it “monstrous”, and sees in the word the Greek instinct that joins moderation to propriety (*Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 11.272; see the translation's note on 11.272). ἀιδρείῃσι νόοιο is “in the ignorance of her mind” (see the translation's row ἀιδρείῃσιν). ἀνάπυστα, “known”, is the adjective of ἀναπυνθάνομαι, “inquire into”: the gods made no special revelation, Merry thinks, but set men's curiosity on the right track (on 11.274).

**Her death.** “But she went to the house of Hades, the strong gatekeeper, / when she had fastened a noose hanging sheer from the high roof beam, / held fast by her own grief” (11.277–279; see [[hades]], [[gatekeeper]] and [[house]]). αἰπύν is predicative, “having fastened high a noose from the lofty beam”, and the μέλαθρον is the long rafter that ran from wall to wall and carried the roof, as Merry explains; ᾧ ἄχεϊ σχομένη is “held fast by, absorbed in” her sorrow, as the Phaeacians are held by a spell (on 11.278–279; 11.334; see [[achos]]). Her curse outlives her: she left Oedipus “very many” sorrows, “all that the Erinyes of a mother bring to pass” (11.280; see [[erinyes]] and [[oedipus]]).
