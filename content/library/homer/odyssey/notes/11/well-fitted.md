---
summary: Teiresias bids Odysseus “take a well-fitted oar and go” inland until he meets men who do not know “well-fitted oars, which are wings to ships”, and there “fix your well-fitted oar in the ground” (11.121–129).
---

The epithet comes three times in the prophecy (11.121, 11.125, 11.129), each time of the oar he is to carry inland; Elpenor's oar on his mound is fixed with the same verse-end in Book 12 (12.15; see [[the-prophecy-of-teiresias]], [[oars]] and [[elpenor]]).
