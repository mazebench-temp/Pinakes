summary: Procris is named between Phaedra and Ariadne, “And I saw Phaedra and Procris and beautiful Ariadne” (11.321), with no word of her family or her fate.
passages:
  - 11.321-325 | Phaedra, Procris and Ariadne
---

“And I saw Phaedra and Procris and beautiful Ariadne” (11.321; see [[phaedra]] and [[ariadne]]). Procris has only her name, between two daughters of Minos. W. W. Merry counts the three among the names of Attic legend that break the run of Thessalian and Boeotian stories, and thinks 11.321–325 probably a later interpolation (*Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 11.321; see the translation's note on 11.321–325). He identifies her as the daughter of Erechtheus, king of Athens, and the wife of Cephalus, and tells the later story of the javelin that never missed and of her death by it at her husband's hand (on 11.321; see [[erechtheus]] and the general article). Homer's catalogue does not say how any of the three women died except Ariadne, whom Artemis killed (11.324–325); in the later story Procris, who had joined Artemis in the hunt, dies by the javelin that she herself gave her husband.
