**The old hero again.** “And among them spoke also the old hero Echeneus, / who was one of the older Phaeacian men” (11.342–343): the first verse is that of the old seer of Ithaca, “And among them spoke also the old hero Halitherses”, with the name changed (2.157; see [[halitherses]], [[geron]] and [[heros]]), and the second is the verse that described him when he spoke for the suppliant at the hearth (11.343 = 7.156). The second verse is missing from some of the best manuscripts, though others and Eustathius have it, and the Augsburg manuscript adds the verse that followed it in Book 7, “and excelled in speaking, knowing many things of old” (7.157; W. W. Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, critical note on 11.343; see the translation's note on 11.343, 11.407, 11.428).

**His words.** He speaks to the Phaeacians as φίλοι, “Friends” (see [[philos]]):

{{quote:b9b15c08-b325-5de1-9723-39b2cb976e04}}

In Homer σκοπός is generally a watcher or a spy, but at 22.6 it is the mark at which an archer shoots, and that is its sense here, “wide of the mark”, as in Plato, where Socrates grants that Theaetetus has spoken “not wide of the mark” (οὐκ ἀπὸ σκοποῦ, *Theaetetus* 179c), and in Xenophon (*Symposium* 2.10, as Merry cites it; Merry on 11.344). ἀπὸ δόξης the scholia take as “not at variance with our own ideas” of how strangers should be treated, Eustathius as “not at variance with our expectation” of what she would do; Merry thinks the second settled by Dolon's promise, “and I will not be a spy for you in vain, nor disappoint your hopes” (οὐδʼ ἀπὸ δόξης, *Iliad* 10.324; see [[dolon]]), and in later Greek the phrase reappears as παρὰ δόξαν (Merry on 11.344; see the translation's key words and its note on 11.344). The last verse means that both promise and performance depend on the king (Merry on 11.346; see [[word-or-deed]]). The queen is βασίλεια περίφρων, “our prudent queen” (11.345; see [[arete-queen-of-the-phaeacians]] and [[periphron]]), and Alcinous answers him in turn, “Then Alcinous answered him in turn and said” (11.347), confirming her proposal (11.348–353; see [[alcinous]]).
