---
summary: φίλος names one's own: Polyphemus is Poseidon's “dear son” (11.103), Odysseus wishes to throw his arms around his mother in a loving embrace (11.211), Eriphyle sold “her own husband” (11.327), Odysseus would come home “to my dear native land” more respected and “dearer to men” (11.359–360), and Neoptolemus is Achilles' “dear son” (11.506).
# φίλας (11.211), φίλτερος (11.360), φίλοιο (11.506).
grc: [φίλας, φίλτερος, φίλοιο]
en: [dear, dearer]
---

The book's φίλοι are kin. Poseidon is angry “because you blinded his dear son” (11.103; see [[polyphemus-the-cyclops]]), and Neoptolemus is “your dear son” to Achilles (11.506; see [[neoptolemus]]); Telemachus will be seen by “his dear father” (11.450; see [[fathers-and-sons]]). φίλας περὶ χεῖρε βαλόντε is “with a loving embrace”, the arms thrown around each other in mourning (11.211; W. W. Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 11.211; see [[lament]]), and Eriphyle took gold “as the price of her own husband” (φίλου ἀνδρός, 11.327; see [[eriphyle]]). The native land is “dear” (11.359, 11.455; see [[dear-native-land]]), and Odysseus would be “more respected and dearer to men” coming home rich (11.360; see [[aidos]]). Echeneus begins his advice to the Phaeacians with ὦ φίλοι, “Friends” (11.344), and Odysseus' heart “in my own breast” wishes to see the other souls (11.566).
