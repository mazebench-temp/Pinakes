# Unnamed: the horse and the ambush (11.523-532).
refs: [11.523-532]
passages:
  - 11.523-532 | Neoptolemus in the horse
---

**From inside.** Menelaus told how Helen walked around the horse, calling the leaders by name in the voices of their wives, and how Odysseus held back the men who wanted to answer (4.271–289); Demodocus sang, at Odysseus' own request, of the horse “which Epeius made with Athena's help” and of the Trojans' debate over it (8.492–520; see [[demodocus]]). Odysseus' own account, to the soul of Achilles, is told for the sake of Achilles' son: “And when we went down into the horse that Epeius made, / we, the best of the Argives, and to me had been given the whole command” (11.523–524; see [[the-wooden-horse]] and [[epeius]]). The others wept and trembled, but Neoptolemus never paled or wiped a tear from his cheeks; he kept begging Odysseus to let him out, feeling for the hilt of his sword and his spear, with evil in mind for the Trojans (11.526–532; see [[neoptolemus]]).

**The command.** The verse that gives Odysseus the command “both to throw open the close ambush and to close it” (11.525) is found in all the manuscripts, but Aristarchus did not know it, and Merry thinks it borrowed from a verse of the gates of heaven in the Iliad (W. W. Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 11.525; see the translation's note on 11.525). In both tellings Odysseus keeps the ambush shut: in Menelaus' he holds back Menelaus, Diomedes and Anticlus (4.280–289), and in his own Neoptolemus begs him again and again to let him out (11.530–531). Sinon, whom the later story left behind to deceive the Trojans, is not named in Homer (see [[sinon]]).
