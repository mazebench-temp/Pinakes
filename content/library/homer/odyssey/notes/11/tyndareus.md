---
summary: Tyndareus is named twice in two verses, as the husband of Leda and as the father to whom she bore Castor and Polydeuces (11.298–299).
---

“And I saw Leda, the wife of Tyndareus, / who bore to Tyndareus two fierce-hearted sons, / Castor, tamer of horses, and Polydeuces, good with his fists” (11.298–300; see [[leda]], [[castor]] and [[polydeuces]]). The poem says nothing else of him here: his name gives the woman her place and her sons their father. W. W. Merry, who doubts the whole passage, finds a sign of its late origin in the first mention, τὴν Τυνδαρέου παράκοιτιν, where τήν does the work of the later article (*Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 11.304; see the Book 11 note on [[leda]]). ὑπὸ Τυνδαρέῳ, “to Tyndareus”, makes him the father, where the heroines around Leda name Zeus or Poseidon (11.241–254, 11.260–262, 11.266–268, 11.305–306).
