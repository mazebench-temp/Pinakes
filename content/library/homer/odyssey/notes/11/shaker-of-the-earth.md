---
summary: Teiresias warns that Odysseus will not escape “the shaker of the earth, since he has laid up a grudge against you in his heart, / angry because you blinded his dear son” (11.102–103); and Poseidon lies with Tyro in the likeness of the river Enipeus, “the earth-holder, the shaker of the earth” (11.241).
---

The first is the god of the Cyclops' curse (9.528–535; see [[odysseus-and-the-cyclops]] and [[poseidon]]); Merry takes ὅ as the conjunction, “in that he hath stored up wrath” (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 11.102; see the translation's note on 11.102). The second is the formula γαιήοχος ἐννοσίγαιος in the story of Tyro (see [[earth-holder]], [[earth-shaker]] and [[tyro]]).
