# ὄλβιοι (11.137; again 23.284), ὄλβιος (11.450).
grc: [ὄλβιοι, ὄλβιος]
en: [prosperous, happy one]
---

The prophecy of Teiresias ends in prosperity, the people of a good king flourishing around him: ἀμφὶ δὲ λαοὶ / ὄλβιοι ἔσσονται (11.136–137; see [[laos]], [[thanatos]] and [[the-prophecy-of-teiresias]]), the words Odysseus will repeat to Penelope (23.283–284). Agamemnon uses the adjective of a son: Telemachus, an infant when they sailed, now sits among men, ὄλβιος, “happy one!”, for his father will see him when he comes home (11.449–450), where the translation keeps “happy” for the address, an exception to “prosperous” (the Odyssey translation's table of key words, ὄλβιος; see [[telemachus]] and [[fathers-and-sons]]). His own son he was not allowed to see (11.452–453).
