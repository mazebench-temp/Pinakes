# δικασπόλον (11.186), θεμιστεύοντα (11.569).
grc: [δικασπόλον, θεμιστεύοντα]
en: [the way of mortals, a man who gives judgment, as is the custom, giving the law, judgment]
---

**The way of mortals.** δίκη is the way a kind of being is: “this is the way of mortals, when one of them dies” (11.218; W. W. Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 11.218; see [[the-way-of-mortals]]), as Penelope spoke of “the way of divine kings” (4.691). θέμις is what is customary: Agamemnon says that Telemachus will greet his returning father “as is the custom” (ἣ θέμις ἐστίν, 11.450–451; see [[fathers-and-sons]]).

**Judgment.** Telemachus, Anticleia says, holds the domains undisturbed and feasts at equal feasts, “such as it is fitting for a man who gives judgment to attend” (δικασπόλον, 11.185–186): the king is the giver of judgment, as the head of the council, and the boy is admitted to the royal honors (Merry on 11.184 and 11.186, who notes that Telemachus could then have been at most fourteen; see [[telemachus]] and the Odyssey translation's note on 11.184–187). Among the dead Minos holds a golden scepter, θεμιστεύοντα νέκυσσιν, “giving the law to the dead”, and they ask him for judgment, δίκας, sitting and standing (11.568–571). θεμιστεύω here takes the dative, “giving law to”, where the Cyclopes' θεμιστεύει with the genitive means rather to be master of children and wives (9.114; Merry on 11.569). He gives judgment among the dead as he did in life; he is not the judge of the dead of later legend (Merry on 11.568; see [[minos]] and [[the-land-of-the-dead]]).
