---
summary: Chloris bore “Nestor and Chromius and proud Periclymenus” (11.286); and Odysseus bids the soul of Ajax “tame your might and your proud spirit” (11.562).
grc: [ἀγέρωχον, "ἀγήνορα θυμόν"]
---

ἀγέρωχος, given to warlike peoples in the Iliad, is here a single man's, Nestor's brother. Merry reports that the word has exercised the etymologists: Aristonicus and the older commentators took it for “holding honor” (γεράοχος), and Buttmann and Lobeck despaired of finding a derivation (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 11.286). ἀγήνορα θυμόν is the proud heart of the Iliad's heroes, which Odysseus asks Ajax to tame, as Phoenix asked Achilles to tame his great heart (*Iliad* 9.496); Ajax answers nothing (11.563; see [[ajax]] and [[meeting-with-the-dead]]).
