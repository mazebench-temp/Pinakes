# θητευέμεν (11.489; again 18.357).
grc: [θητευέμεν]
en: [serve another for hire]
---

θητεύω is to work for hire as a θής, and ἐπάρουρος makes the hired man a serf bound to another man's land; the θῆτες are named beside the slaves in the Odyssey (4.644), working for hire being what marks the difference, and in the Solonian constitution they were the hired laborers (W. W. Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 11.489). Achilles chooses the lowest of these lives on earth, and for a master who has no land of his own and no great livelihood, over kingship among the dead (11.488–491; see [[rather-a-hired-laborer]], [[achilles]] and [[slavery-and-freedom]]). The Iliad's one hired service is the gods' year under Laomedon (*Iliad* 21.444–445).
