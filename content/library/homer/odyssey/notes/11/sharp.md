---
summary: Odysseus “drew the sharp sword from beside my thigh” to dig the pit and to guard the blood (11.24, 11.48), and Teiresias bids him “hold away your sharp sword” (11.95); Teiresias foretells the killing of the suitors “whether by guile or openly with the sharp bronze” (11.120); Odysseus' failure to clasp his mother brings “sharp grief” (11.208); and Neoptolemus came out of Troy “neither struck by the sharp bronze / nor stabbed hand to hand” (11.535–536).
---

The verse of the drawn sword, four times in the last book, comes twice more here, the second time as Circe had bidden (11.24, 11.48 = 10.535; see [[sword]] and [[the-offering-to-the-dead]]). At 11.97 the sword is put back into its sheath, “my silver-studded sword” (see [[silver-studded]]), and at 11.231 drawn again, “the long-edged sword,” to keep the women from the blood (see [[long-edged]]).
