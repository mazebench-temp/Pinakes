# Ἀμφίονος and Ἰασίδαο (11.283) are claimed by the entry.
---

ὁπλοτάτην κούρην Ἀμφίονος Ἰασίδαο, / ὅς ποτʼ ἐν Ὀρχομενῷ Μινυείῳ ἶφι ἄνασσεν, “the youngest daughter of Amphion, son of Iasus, / who once ruled with might in Minyan Orchomenus” (11.283–284; see [[chloris]], [[orchomenus]] and [[amphion-of-thebes]]). This Amphion, W. W. Merry notes, is distinct from the Theban Amphion, though a later form of the story identified the two (*Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 11.283). La Roche, with Bekker, prints Μινυείῳ for the common Μινυηΐῳ (Merry's critical note on 11.284; see the translation's note on 11.284–285).
