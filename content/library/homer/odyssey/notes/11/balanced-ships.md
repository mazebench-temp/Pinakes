---
summary: Arete asks how the stranger seems to the Phaeacians, “in looks and stature and the well-balanced mind within” (11.337), and Odysseus tells Achilles that he himself brought Neoptolemus “in the hollow, balanced ship / from Scyros” (11.508–509).
except: [11.185]
---

Merry gives two derivations of ἐΐσας at 11.337: from the root of “seem,” which would make it “good, seemly,” or from ἶσος, which would make the mind “well-balanced” like the ships “on even keel”; the scholiast's “proportioned to the body” he rejects (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 11.337). The translation takes the second (see the Odyssey translation's key words). The feasts of 11.185 are another phrase (see [[equal-feast]]). κοίλης ἐπὶ νηὸς ἐίσης joins the two epithets of the ship in one verse-end (see [[hollow-ships]], [[neoptolemus]] and [[scyros]]).
