# Πανοπῆος (11.581) is the town here; in the Iliad the same form is the man, Epeius' father (Il. 23.665;
# panopeus-father-of-epeius, which excepts this line), so the form is claimed in this note, for the Odyssey only.
grc: [Πανοπῆος]
---

**On the road to Pytho.** The crime of Tityus is placed on a road: he laid hands on Leto “as she went to Pytho through Panopeus with its lovely dancing floors” (11.580–581; see [[tityus]], [[leto]] and [[pytho]]). Panopeus “with its fair lawns”, W. W. Merry notes, lay near the Cephisus on the borders of Boeotia; in Strabo's time it was called Phanoteus, and some ruins of its walls still remained (*Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 11.581). Strabo adds that the stories of Tityus were told there (*Geography* 9.3.14; see [[strabo]]). καλλίχορος, “with its lovely dancing floors”, is the epithet of a place where people gather to dance, as εὐρύχορος, “with its wide dancing floors”, is given to Iolcus and to Thebes in this book (11.256, 11.265; see [[with-wide-dancing-floors]]). In the Iliad the same name is the father of Epeius, “the son of Panopeus” (*Iliad* 23.665; see [[panopeus-father-of-epeius]]), and Epeius, whose wooden horse Odysseus recalls in this book (11.523), was a man of Panopeus by Strabo's account (9.3.14; see [[epeius]]).
