summary: Laertes sleeps in winter in the dust near the fire, among the slaves (11.190–191), and Anticleia tells her son that when a man dies “the strong might of the blazing fire” consumes his flesh and bones (11.219–221).
---

**Laertes by the fire.** “But in the winter he sleeps where the slaves sleep in the house, / in the dust near the fire, and wears poor clothes on his body” (11.190–191; see [[laertes]], [[house]] and [[clothing]]): the old king has left his bed for the hearth of the servants' quarters.

**The fire of the dead.** Anticleia explains why her son cannot embrace her: “the sinews no longer hold the flesh and the bones together, / but the strong might of the blazing fire / overcomes them, as soon as the spirit has left the white bones, / and the soul, like a dream, flits away and hovers” (11.219–222; see [[anticleia]], [[the-way-of-mortals]], [[cremation]] and [[menos]]). πυρὸς κρατερὸν μένος αἰθομένοιο, “the strong might of the blazing fire”, gives the fire the μένος of a living thing, as the Chimaera breathes out “the terrible might of blazing fire” in the Iliad (*Iliad* 6.182; see [[chimaera]]).
