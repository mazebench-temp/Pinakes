---
summary: Alcinous: Odysseus has told “the grievous sorrows of all the Argives and of yourself” (11.369); and Agamemnon: Clytemnestra, “with her mind set on grim things beyond all others, / has poured shame on herself” (11.432–433).
---

κήδεα λυγρά, the sorrows of the tale, as the king sums them up (see [[the-tale-to-alcinous]]); λυγρὰ ἰδυῖα, “knowing grim things,” of the murderess, the phrase of a mind given to harm (see [[clytemnestra]]).
