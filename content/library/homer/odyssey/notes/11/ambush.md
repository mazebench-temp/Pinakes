---
summary: The wooden horse is an ambush: Odysseus had the command, “both to throw open the close ambush and to close it”, and inside it the other leaders wept and trembled while Neoptolemus begged to be let out (11.523–532).
---

Odysseus tells Achilles how his son behaved in the horse: “And when we went down into the horse that Epeius made, / we, the best of the Argives, and to me had been given the whole command, / both to throw open the close ambush and to close it” (πυκινὸν λόχον, 11.523–525; see [[the-wooden-horse]], [[epeius]] and [[sinon-and-the-wooden-horse]]). Aristarchus did not know 11.525, though it is in all the manuscripts; Merry thinks it borrowed from the Iliad's verse on the gates of heaven, “both to throw open the thick cloud and to close it” (*Iliad* 5.751), with words that suit a cloud but are forced with an ambush, and brackets it (W. W. Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 11.525 and his critical note; see the Odyssey translation's note on 11.525). The ambush tests the men inside as Idomeneus said it would: the other leaders wipe away tears and tremble, and Neoptolemus neither pales nor weeps but keeps begging to go out against the Trojans (11.526–532; see [[the-brave-man-in-ambush]] and [[neoptolemus]]).
