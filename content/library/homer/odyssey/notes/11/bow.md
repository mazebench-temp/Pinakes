summary: The phantom of Heracles stands among the scattering dead “like dark night, / holding his naked bow, with an arrow on the string, / glancing terribly around, like one ever about to shoot” (11.606–608).
# νευρῆφιν, "on the string" (11.607), first met in the Odyssey here (the Iliad's are in its notes); it applies in the
# Odyssey only.
grc: [νευρῆφιν]
en: [string]
---

ὁ δʼ ἐρεμνῇ νυκτὶ ἐοικώς, / γυμνὸν τόξον ἔχων καὶ ἐπὶ νευρῆφιν ὀιστόν, / δεινὸν παπταίνων, αἰεὶ βαλέοντι ἐοικώς (11.606–608; see [[heracles]], [[like-night]] and [[arrows]]). γυμνόν is “uncased”, out of its bow-case, W. W. Merry explains, comparing the case of Odysseus' bow (*Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 11.607; 21.54), and παπταίνων, “glancing terribly around”, always suggests anxiety in the look, here caution blended with keen expectation (on 11.608). The sentence has four participles and no finite verb, and the English supplies “was” (see the translation's note on 11.606–608). Heracles is an archer in Homer, as in the Iliad, where he shot Hera and Hades (*Iliad* 5.392–397; see [[heracles-wounds-hera-and-hades]]); the club and the lion skin of later art are not his here (see [[lion-skin]]). Artemis' arrows, which might have killed Anticleia, are named earlier in the book (11.172–173, 11.198–199; see [[gentle-arrows]] and [[artemis]]).
