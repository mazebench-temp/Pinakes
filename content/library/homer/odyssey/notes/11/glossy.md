---
summary: Laertes has not, “to sleep in, / beds and cloaks and glossy blankets” (11.188–189).
---

The bedding of a house, which the old man has given up: in winter he sleeps where the slaves sleep, in the dust near the fire, and in summer on fallen leaves on the slope of his vineyard (11.187–196; see [[laertes]], [[blankets]] and [[bed]]). Merry takes εὐναί as predicative, “nor has he for bedding couches and cloaks and bright-coloured rugs” (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 11.188; see the translation's note on 11.188–189).
