---
summary: “Castor, tamer of horses, and Polydeuces, good with his fists” (11.300).
---

The Iliad's verse of Helen's brothers (*Iliad* 3.237), followed here, as there, by the life-giving earth that holds them; but here they are alive and dead on alternate days (11.301–304; see [[life-giving-earth]] and [[polydeuces]]). Merry derives Castor's name, like Epicaste's, from the root καδ (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 11.300).
