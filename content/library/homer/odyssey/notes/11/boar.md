summary: The swine of Book 11 are the boar of the sacrifice to Poseidon, “a boar that mounts the sows” (11.131); the white-tusked hogs, slaughtered for a rich man's feast, to which Agamemnon likens his companions killed around him (11.413–415); and the wild boars wrought with bears and lions on the baldric of Heracles (11.611).
# κάπρον, "boar" (11.131; again 23.278, the same verse), first met as this form in the Odyssey here; the Iliad's

**The boar of the sacrifice.** Teiresias bids Odysseus sacrifice to Poseidon “a ram and a bull and a boar that mounts the sows”, συῶν τʼ ἐπιβήτορα κάπρον (11.131; see [[ram]], [[bulls]] and the translation's row ἐπιβήτωρ). κάπρος is the boar, the male of the herd, and the phrase makes him a sire, ἐπιβήτωρ συῶν, as the ram and the bull are the males of their kinds (W. W. Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 11.131). The Iliad's boar of sacrifice is the one that Talthybius holds when Agamemnon swears his oath before returning Briseis, and whose throat Agamemnon cuts (*Iliad* 19.196–197, 19.251–268; see [[oath]]).

**Hogs for a feast.** Agamemnon's companions “were killed unceasingly, like white-tusked hogs / that are slaughtered in the house of a rich man of great power / for a wedding, or a shared feast, or a rich banquet” (11.412–415; see [[like-white-tusked-hogs]] and [[the-ox-at-the-manger]]). These are the swine of the feast, as at the funeral feast of Patroclus and at Alcinous' banquet (*Iliad* 23.32; 8.60; see [[white-tusked]]).

**Wild boars.** On the baldric of Heracles are wrought “bears and wild boars and lions with flashing eyes” (11.611; see [[the-baldric-of-heracles]], [[bears]] and [[lion]]). ἀγρότεροι σύες are the wild boars of the hunt and of the Iliad's similes, where the hunter sets his dogs “on a wild boar or a lion” (*Iliad* 11.293); the Odyssey's other boars of this kind are those that Artemis delights in on the mountains, in the simile of Nausicaa (6.104), and the boar that wounded the young Odysseus on Parnassus (19.439–454, a plain rendering; see [[the-scar-of-odysseus]]).
