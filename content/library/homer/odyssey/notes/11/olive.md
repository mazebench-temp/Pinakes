---
summary: Olives are the last of the fruit trees that hang over Tantalus, “sweet figs and flourishing olives” (11.590), the verse of Alcinous' orchard, whose fruit the wind flings away whenever he reaches for it.
passages:
  - 11.582-592 | Tantalus in the lake
---

“And trees with high foliage let their fruit hang down over his head, / pears and pomegranates and apple trees with splendid fruit, / and sweet figs and flourishing olives” (11.588–590; see [[tantalus]] and [[trees]]). The second and third verses are those of the orchard outside the court of Alcinous, where the fruit never fails, winter or summer (7.115–118; see [[the-garden-of-alcinous]] and the Book 7 note); in the land of the dead the same olives are always out of reach: “whenever the old man made straight for them, to grasp them with his hands, / the wind would fling them toward the shadowy clouds” (11.591–592). τηλεθόωσαι, “flourishing”, is the word of the olive sapling that a man rears in a lonely, well-watered place in the Iliad's simile, ἔρνος … τηλεθάον, “a fine, thriving tree” (*Iliad* 17.53–55; see [[the-olive-sapling]] and [[flourishing]]).
