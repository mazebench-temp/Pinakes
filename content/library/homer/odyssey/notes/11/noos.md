---
summary: Elpenor, waking on Circe's roof, “did not think” to go back by the ladder (11.62); Odysseus asks his mother for Penelope's “plan and her thought” (11.177); and Epicaste married her son “in the ignorance of her mind” (11.272).
---

Three uses of the book show the word's range of noticing, intending and understanding. Elpenor, lying in Circe's hall, “did not think / to come back down by going to the long ladder”, and fell from the roof (οὐκ ἐνόησα, 11.62–64; see [[elpenor]] and [[the-death-of-elpenor]]). Odysseus asks his mother to tell him of “my wedded wife, her plan and her thought” (βουλήν τε νόον τε, 11.177), whether she stays and keeps all things safe or has married another (see [[penelope]] and [[boule]]). And Epicaste “did a great deed in the ignorance of her mind” (ἀιδρείῃσι νόοιο, 11.272), marrying her son without knowing it (see [[jocasta]], [[oedipus-kills-laius]] and the Odyssey translation's table of key words, ἀιδρείῃσιν).
