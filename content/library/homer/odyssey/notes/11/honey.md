---
summary: The first of the drink offerings that Odysseus pours around the pit for all the dead is μελίκρητον, honey mixed with milk (11.27).
---

“First with honey and milk, then with sweet wine, / and the third time with water” (11.27–28; see [[the-offering-to-the-dead]], [[milk]], [[wine]] and [[barley-meal]]), as Circe told him (10.519–520). The word of the honey is used twice more in the book, of the homecoming that Odysseus seeks and of the life that longing took from his mother: “a honey-sweet homecoming” (11.100) and “my honey-sweet life” (11.203; see [[honey-sweet]]).
