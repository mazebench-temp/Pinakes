---
summary: Agamemnon stretches out his hands to Odysseus, “but no longer was there steadfast strength in him, or any vigor, / such as there was before in his supple limbs” (11.393–394).
---

The verse-end of strength lost, as in Nestor's lament for his youth (*Iliad* 11.669; see the general article). Merry takes γναμπτοῖσι μέλεσσι for the supple limbs of the living, set against the stiffness of the dead (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 11.394). κῖκυς, “vigor,” is found only here in Homer (see the Odyssey translation's key words, and [[meeting-with-the-dead]]).
