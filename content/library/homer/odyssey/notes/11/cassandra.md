# Κασσανδρ* (in the entry) claims Κασσάνδρης (11.422). Unnamed: "Priam's daughter" (11.421); "whom" (11.422-423).
refs: [11.421, 11.423]
---

“And the most pitiable voice I heard was that of Priam's daughter, / Cassandra, whom scheming Clytemnestra killed / beside me” (11.421–423; see [[agamemnon]], [[clytemnestra]] and [[priam]]). οἰκτροτάτην is predicative: “but saddest of all that I heard was the voice of Priam's daughter” (W. W. Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 11.421). ἀμφʼ ἐμοί, “beside me”, Eustathius explains either as “because of me” or “over me”; the first, Merry thinks, may have been suggested by the notion that she was not present at the banquet, but there is no difficulty in supposing that she came in with Agamemnon and sat at his side, her hated presence adding a sting of jealousy to Clytemnestra's bitterness; he renders “close by me”, or more graphically, “as she clung about me” (Merry on 11.423; see the translation's note on 11.423–424). Agamemnon, dying, raised his hands, to protect her as far as he could or to beg his wife for her, in the reading Seiler took from Königinhoff, as Merry reports it, and let them fall (11.423–424). The poem says nothing of how she came to Mycenae, nor that she was a seer; the Iliad knew her as the most beautiful of Priam's daughters (*Iliad* 13.365), and the story of her captivity is later (see the entry).
