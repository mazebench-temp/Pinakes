**Honor among the Myrmidons.** In the land of the dead Achilles' first thought for the living is for his son, his second for his father: he asks “whether he still has honor among the many Myrmidons, / or whether they dishonor him throughout Hellas and Phthia, / because old age holds him fast in his hands and feet” (11.494–497; see [[peleus]], [[phthia]] and [[time]]). The Myrmidons were the men Achilles led at Troy (*Iliad* 2.684); at home they are the people over whom his father's honor rests, and over whom his son now rules (4.9; see [[neoptolemus]]). Achilles imagines coming back among them as he was at Troy and making his hands hateful “to any / of those who do him violence and keep him from his honor” (11.502–503).
