“And I saw Maera and Clymene and hateful Eriphyle, / who took precious gold as the price of her own husband” (11.326–327; see [[maera-the-heroine]] and [[eriphyle]]). Clymene has only her name. W. W. Merry, who hears in the last three names a return to Boeotian legend, makes her the daughter of Minyas, the wife of Phylacus and the mother of Iphiclus (*Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 11.326), as the Hesiodic *Catalogue of Women* did, which the scholion on this verse quotes (fr. 84 Evelyn-White; see the general article). In that family she would join the women of the catalogue who come before her: her son is the Iphiclus of Phylace whose cattle Neleus demanded for Pero, and who released the seer who tried to drive them off (11.288–297; see [[iphiclus]], [[pero]], [[melampus]] and [[phylace]]), and Chloris, Pero's mother, was the daughter of a king of Minyan Orchomenus (11.281–284; see [[chloris]] and [[orchomenus]]).
