καὶ Μεγάρην, Κρείοντος ὑπερθύμοιο θύγατρα, “and Megara, the daughter of high-hearted Creon” (11.269; see [[megara-wife-of-heracles]] and [[high-hearted]]). The poem gives him no city and no father; W. W. Merry, telling the later story in which Creon, king of Thebes, gave Megara to Heracles for his victory over Erginus of Orchomenus, holds him distinct from the brother-in-law of Oedipus (*Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 11.269; see the translation's note on 11.269). The catalogue goes straight on to the mother of Oedipus, Epicaste, and names no brother of hers (11.271–280; see [[jocasta]]). On the identification, which Euripides makes, see the general article.
