---
summary: Agamemnon's soul, having told how his wife and Aegisthus killed him, counsels Odysseus to keep part of his mind hidden from his wife and to bring his ship home secretly, “for there is no trusting women any more” (11.441–456), though he has said that Penelope is wise.
passages:
  - 11.441-456 | Agamemnon's counsel
---

The counsel answers Odysseus' own words, that Zeus has hated the offspring of Atreus “through the plans of women”, Helen's and Clytemnestra's (11.436–439; see [[genee]] and [[helen]]). Agamemnon gives it in two parts, with Penelope's praise and Telemachus between them: never be kind even to your wife, tell her one part and hide another (11.441–443); death will not come to you from her, for she is wise, and your son, an infant when we sailed, will greet his father, as Agamemnon's wife did not let him see his own (11.444–453; see [[penelope]] and [[telemachus]]); and then, “something else, and you lay it to heart”, the secret landing and the maxim (11.454–456; see [[lay-it-to-heart]]). The book has already given the other side: Anticleia told her son that Penelope stays in his halls with enduring heart, and wastes her nights and days in tears (11.181–183). Agamemnon goes on at once to ask after his own son (11.457–461; see [[orestes]]).
