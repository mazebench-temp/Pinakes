  - 11.254-257 | Pelias and Neleus

“And she conceived and bore Pelias and Neleus, / who became mighty attendants of great Zeus, / both of them; Pelias lived in Iolcus with its wide dancing floors, / rich in flocks, and the other in sandy Pylos” (11.254–257; see [[tyro]], [[neleus]], [[iolcus]], [[with-wide-dancing-floors]] and [[rich-in-flocks]]). θεράποντε Διός, “attendants of great Zeus”, are kings, W. W. Merry explains, as the θεράποντες of Ares are warriors and those of the Muses singers (*Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 11.255). The two brothers rule far apart, each named with his city's epithet: Pelias in Iolcus, a town of Thessalian Magnesia that Merry calls the capital of the Aeolids and the meeting place of the Argonauts, and Neleus in Pylos (on 11.256). Merry tells the later story of Pelias' seizure of Iolcus and his sending of Jason after the fleece, on 11.259 (see [[jason]]).
