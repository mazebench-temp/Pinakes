---
summary: Elpenor's soul blames his fall on Circe's roof on “an evil fate from a god … and endless wine” (11.61).
---

Asked how he came beneath the misty darkness faster than the ship, Elpenor answers: “an evil fate from a god led me into blind folly, and endless wine” (ἆσέ με δαίμονος αἶσα κακὴ καὶ ἀθέσφατος οἶνος, 11.61; see [[elpenor]], [[ate]] and [[wine]]). δαίμονος is quite vague, “some evil power”, with no allusion to Circe (W. W. Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 11.61; see [[daimon]]). The fate is named first and the wine second, as Odysseus blamed “my evil companions … and with them sleep” for the loss of the winds (10.68–69; see the Odyssey translation's table of key words, δαίμονος αἶσα κακή, and [[who-is-to-blame]]).
