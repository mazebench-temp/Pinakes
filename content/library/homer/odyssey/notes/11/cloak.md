---
summary: Among the comforts that Laertes has given up are cloaks to sleep under: “Nor has he, to sleep in, / beds and cloaks and glossy blankets” (11.188–189).
---

The χλαῖνα, the heavy woolen cloak that served as a blanket at night, is part of the bedding that Laertes, grieving in the country, no longer uses (11.188–189; see [[laertes]], [[bed]] and [[blankets]]); he sleeps in winter in the dust by the fire, in poor clothes (11.190–191; see [[clothing]]).
