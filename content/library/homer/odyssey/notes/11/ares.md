summary: Ares is named once in Book 11, in Odysseus' praise of Neoptolemus, who came out of Troy unharmed, “neither struck by the sharp bronze / nor stabbed hand to hand, as often / happens in war; for Ares rages without distinction” (11.535–537).
# Ἄρης (11.537) is claimed by the Odyssey's Book 8 note.
---

**Ares rages without distinction.** Odysseus tells the soul of Achilles that his son went on board after the sack of Troy “unharmed, neither struck by the sharp bronze / nor stabbed hand to hand, as often / happens in war; for Ares rages without distinction” (11.535–537; see [[neoptolemus]] and [[achilles]]). The god is war itself, as often in the Iliad (see the general article). ἐπιμίξ is “indiscriminately”: Merry renders “the war-god wreaks his rage indiscriminately”, and compares the Trojans “in turmoil, all mingled together” (*Iliad* 11.525) and Hector's “Enyalius is impartial, and he kills the man who would kill” (*Iliad* 18.309; see [[enyalius]]), like the *Mars communis* of the Latin writers (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 11.537; see the translation's key-word row). Cicero, writing of the civil war, has it so: “for since in every war Mars is common to both sides, and the outcome of battles is always uncertain” (*nam cum omnis belli Mars communis et cum semper incerti exitus proeliorum sunt*, *Letters to Friends* 6.4.1; see [[cicero]]). Neoptolemus came through it untouched, and the praise pleases his father: the soul goes off “glad because I said that his son was preeminent” (11.540).
