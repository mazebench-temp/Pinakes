summary: The book of the souls. They gather “out of Erebus” to the blood (11.37); the soul of Elpenor, which “went down to Hades” when he fell (11.51, 11.65), the soul of Anticleia (11.84, 11.141, 11.205) and the soul of Teiresias (11.90, 11.150, 11.165) come one by one; the soul flies from the dead body “like a dream” (11.222); and after the pause come the souls of Agamemnon, Achilles and Ajax, and the souls of the others who have died (11.387, 11.467, 11.543, 11.567).

  - 11.36-43 | The souls gather
---

**The souls.** The word runs through the book as the name of each person who comes: “the soul of my companion Elpenor”, “the soul of my dead mother”, “the soul of Theban Teiresias”, “the soul of Agamemnon”, “the soul of Achilles”, “the soul of Ajax” (11.51, 11.84, 11.90, 11.387, 11.467, 11.543), the verse ἦλθε δʼ ἐπὶ ψυχή, “And there came the soul”, introducing each in turn (11.84, 11.90, 11.387, 11.467; see the Odyssey translation's table of formulas). The grammar follows the person: the soul of Teiresias comes “holding” (ἔχων, masculine) a golden scepter, a construction by sense like τούς … ἔθνεα νεκρῶν at 11.34 (W. W. Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 11.90; see [[teiresias]] and [[scepter]]). Odysseus says that he came “to consult the soul of Theban Teiresias” (ψυχῇ χρησόμενον, 11.165), the purpose Circe gave him (10.492).

**What the soul is.** Elpenor tells his own death in the words of Book 10, “and my soul went down to Hades” (11.65; see [[elpenor]]), and Anticleia tells what happens to the soul at death: when the spirit has left the white bones and the fire has consumed them, “the soul, like a dream, flits away and hovers” (11.221–222; see [[the-way-of-mortals]], [[thymos]] and [[dream]]). Merry, on the pair θυμός and ψυχή, connects ψυχή with a root meaning “to blow”, which makes it the equivalent of *anima* and *spiritus*, as θυμός is connected with the root of smoke (Merry on 11.221). The soul cannot be held: Odysseus' arms close on nothing three times (11.204–208; see [[eidolon]]).

**The many.** The souls come as a crowd and are sent away as one: “the souls of the dead who have died” gather out of Erebus (11.37; see [[the-dead-who-have-died]] and [[erebus]]), Persephone scatters the souls of the women (11.385–386; see [[persephone]]), the other souls stand grieving after Achilles leaves (11.541–542), and Ajax goes off without a word “after the other / souls of the dead who have died, into Erebus” (11.563–564). Odysseus stays because his heart wished “to see the souls of the others who have died” (11.566–567; see [[the-land-of-the-dead]]).
