---
summary: “Pelias lived in Iolcus with its wide dancing floors, / rich in flocks” (11.256–257).
grc: [πολύρρηνος]
---

πολύρρηνος, of a man, is the epic form of πολύρρην, which the Iliad has of men in the seven towns that Agamemnon offers Achilles (*Iliad* 9.154). Merry derives it from a root meaning “sheep” (the ἀρν- of ἀρνός, “lamb”), and reports, after La Roche, that Aristarchus read πολύρηνος with a single ρ (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 11.257 and his critical note; see the translation's note on 11.257, 11.264, 11.288, and [[pelias]]). The epithet of wealth in flocks stands beside the brother's cattle: Neleus would give his daughter only for the cattle of Iphiclus (11.288–290; see [[broad-browed]]).
