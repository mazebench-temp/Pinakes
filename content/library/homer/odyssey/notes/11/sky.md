---
summary: The sky is the sun's road, which never brings his light to the Cimmerians (11.17–18), the home of the immortals “who hold the wide sky” (11.133), and the height the sons of Aloeus meant to scale (11.316).
---

**The sun's road.** The sun never looks down on the Cimmerians, “neither when he climbs toward the starry sky, / nor when he turns back again from the sky toward the earth” (11.17–18; see [[cimmerians]], [[helios]] and [[starry]]): the rising and the setting of his daily course, from the earth up to the highest point of the sky and back (W. W. Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 11.18). The Sun himself speaks of his course in nearly the same words in the next book, of the cattle in which he took delight as he went up into the starry sky and when he turned back from the sky toward the earth (12.379–381; see [[thrinacia]]).

**The gods of the wide sky.** Teiresias bids Odysseus, when he has appeased Poseidon, go home and offer hecatombs “to the immortal gods, who hold the wide sky; / to all of them in order” (11.133–134; see [[immortals]], [[wide-sky]] and [[hecatomb]]). The verse is the Odyssey's formula for the gods (4.479).

**Heaven scaled.** The sons of Aloeus piled the mountains in their thought, Ossa on Olympus and Pelion on Ossa, ἵνʼ οὐρανὸς ἀμβατὸς εἴη, “so that heaven might be scaled” (11.316; see [[ossa-mountain]] and [[olympus]]).
