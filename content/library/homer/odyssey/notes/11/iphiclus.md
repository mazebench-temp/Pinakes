**Mighty Iphiclus.** βίης Ἰφικληείης (11.290) and βίη Ἰφικληείη (11.296) are “mighty Iphiclus”, literally “the might of Iphiclus”, as βίη Ἡρακληείη is “mighty Heracles” (11.601; *Iliad* 2.658; see [[might-of-heracles]] and the translation's row βίη Ἰφικληείη). The Iliad uses the same periphrasis of Priam and of Eteocles (see [[might-of-priam]] and [[might-of-eteocles]]).

**The cattle of Phylace.** Neleus would give Pero only to the man who drove “the broad-browed cattle with curving horns / out of Phylace, the cattle of mighty Iphiclus” (11.289–290; see [[pero]], [[neleus]] and [[phylace]]). W. W. Merry makes Iphiclus the son of Phylacus, living at Phylace on Mount Othrys in Thessalian Phthiotis; Phylacus, he says, seems to have stolen the cattle from Tyro, Neleus' mother, and Iphiclus kept them guarded by savage herdsmen and, as later legends added, by a fierce watchdog; the story of the seer's imprisonment may have grown from the names Φύλακος and Φυλάκη, “guard” (*Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 11.290). The herdsmen and the bonds held the seer for a year, “then at last mighty Iphiclus released him, / when he had told all the oracles” (11.296–297; see [[melampus]] and [[melampus-and-the-cattle-of-iphiclus]]). In Book 15 the seer is bound “in the halls of Phylacus”, Iphiclus' father (15.231).
