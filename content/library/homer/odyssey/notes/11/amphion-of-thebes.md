passages:
  - 11.262-265 | The founders of Thebes
---

“And she bore two sons, Amphion and Zethus, / who first founded the seat of seven-gated Thebes / and fenced it with towers, since without towers they could not / dwell in Thebes with its wide dancing floors, mighty though they were” (11.262–265; see [[antiope]], [[zethus]], [[thebes]] and [[with-wide-dancing-floors]]). The brothers are named together and act together, and Homer says nothing of the lyre with which later poets had Amphion raise the walls (see the general article). πύργωσαν, “fenced it with towers”, and ἀπύργωτον, “without towers”, make the reason a play on one word: strong as they were, they needed walls. Twenty lines later the catalogue names another Amphion, son of Iasus and king of Minyan Orchomenus, whom W. W. Merry and Grote keep distinct from this one (11.283; *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 11.260, 11.283; see [[amphion-son-of-iasus]]).
