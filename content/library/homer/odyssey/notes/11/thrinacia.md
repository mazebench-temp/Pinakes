---
summary: Teiresias names the island first, as the place where the companions will find the cattle and fat sheep of the Sun, and where their homecoming will be decided (11.104–115).
---

**The first naming.** The seer's prophecy turns on the island before Odysseus has heard of it:

{{quote:0bd77c01-1513-54b2-bbaf-a5613a7d30be}}

εὕρητε, “and find”, still depends on ὁππότε κε πρῶτον, “when first”: “You may see your homes again if you can restrain yourselves, when you land on Thrinacia and when you find the grazing herds” (W. W. Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 11.108). His note on the island itself is on Circe's description in the next book (on 11.107, referring to 12.127). προφυγὼν ἰοειδέα πόντον, “escaping the violet sea”, makes the island a landfall after the dangers of the sea (see [[violet-sea]]), but the test is on the land: if the herds are left unharmed, the companions may still reach Ithaca, though suffering evils; if they are harmed, Teiresias foretells destruction for the ship and the companions, and for Odysseus a late homecoming, alone, on someone else's ship (11.110–115; see [[the-cattle-of-helios]] and [[ithaca]]). Circe repeats the warning in the same verses when she names the island again (12.127–141), and when the ship comes there Odysseus hears the cattle lowing and remembers “the word / of the blind seer, Theban Teiresias” (12.266–267; see [[the-blind-seer]]).
