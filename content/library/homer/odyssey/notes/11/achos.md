summary: The book's grief: the companions sail “grieving” for the land of the dead (11.5); Odysseus keeps his mother from the blood “though deeply grieved” (11.88), and sharp grief rises in him when she slips from his arms (11.208); Laertes lies grieving on his vineyard (11.195); Epicaste hangs herself “held fast by her own grief” (11.279); the souls of the dead stand grieving (11.388, 11.542); and the Achaeans grieve for Ajax as for Achilles (11.556–558).
# ἀχέων (11.195), ἀχνυμένη (11.388; again 24.21), ἀκαχίζευ (11.486), ἀχνύμεναι (11.542), ἀχνύμεθα (11.558).
grc: [ἀχέων, ἀχνυμένη, ἀκαχίζευ, ἀχνύμεναι, ἀχνύμεθα]
en: [grieving, grieve]
---

**The living.** The companions go aboard “grieving, shedding big tears” for the dreadful journey ahead (11.5; W. W. Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 11.5; see [[tears]]). Odysseus will not let his mother near the blood, “though deeply grieved” (πυκινόν περ ἀχεύων, 11.88; see [[pity]]), and when her soul flies from his arms “sharp grief rose in my heart all the more” (ἄχος ὀξύ, 11.208; see [[anticleia]]). At home Laertes “lies grieving, and nurses a great sorrow in his heart” (ἀχέων, 11.195; see [[penthos]] and [[laertes]]).

**The dead.** Grief goes down with them. Epicaste fastened the noose “held fast by her own grief” (ᾧ ἄχεϊ σχομένη, 11.279), the middle aorist with a passive sense, “absorbed in” her sorrow, as the Phaeacians are “held by a spell” (11.334; Merry on 11.279; see [[jocasta]] and [[thelgo]]). The soul of Agamemnon comes “grieving” (11.387–388), the two stand “grieving, shedding big tears” (11.466), and after Achilles goes, the other souls “stood grieving, and each one asked about its own sorrows” (11.541–542; see [[the-land-of-the-dead]]). Odysseus tells Achilles not to grieve at all that he is dead (μή τι θανὼν ἀκαχίζευ, 11.486; see [[rather-a-hired-laborer]]), and tells Ajax that the Achaeans grieve for him “as much as for the head of Achilles … without end” (11.556–558; see [[ajax]]).
