summary: Elpenor asks for a mound on the shore of the gray sea, “the mound of an unhappy man, even for men to come to hear of”, with his oar fixed on it (11.75–78); the oar that Teiresias bids Odysseus plant in the ground far inland is another such sign (11.126–129).
refs: [11.75-78]
---

**A mound for men to come.** Elpenor's request is the Odyssey's version of the tomb that speaks: “heap up a mound for me on the shore of the gray sea, / the mound of an unhappy man, even for men to come to hear of. / Do this for me, and fix on the tomb the oar / with which I rowed when I was alive, among my companions” (11.75–78; see [[elpenor]], [[tomb]] and [[oars]]). ἀνδρὸς δυστήνοιο stands in the genitive as if it depended on σῆμα, “the mound of an unhappy man”, instead of agreeing with μοι (W. W. Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 11.76). καὶ ἐσσομένοισι πυθέσθαι, “even for men to come to hear of”, is the phrase of report to later men: Hector resolves to die “having done some great thing for men to come to hear of” (*Iliad* 22.305), and Telemachus says that the Achaeans will carry the fame of Orestes wide “even for men to come to hear of” (3.204). On the shore, Merry notes, the mound would be seen by all who passed, though they must have been few off the lonely coasts of Aeaea (on 11.74).

**The oar as a sign.** The oar marks the man by his work. Nitzsch, as Merry reports, quoted an epitaph ascribed to Sappho on the fisherman Pelagon, on whose tomb his father set a creel and an oar as a memorial of his hard life (*Palatine Anthology* 7.505; Merry on 11.74), and Aristotle tells that the Iberians, a warlike people, fix around a man's tomb as many spits as the enemies he has killed (*Politics* 7, 1324b19–20). Teiresias' prophecy repeats the gesture: Odysseus is to plant his well-fitted oar in the ground where men take it for a winnowing fan, and Merry recalls Elpenor's request there (11.126–129; Merry on 11.129; see [[the-prophecy-of-teiresias]]). The mound is heaped and the oar set up in Book 12 (12.14–15, a plain rendering).
