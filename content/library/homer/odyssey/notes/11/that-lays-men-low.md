---
summary: Odysseus asks his mother and Agamemnon the same question: “What fate of death that lays men low brought you down?” (11.171, 11.398).
---

Merry takes κὴρ … θανάτοιο for the particular form of death that comes to each man, set against the fate that is common to all (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 11.171; see [[ker-doom]] and [[thanatos]]). Each time the question offers a choice: a long sickness or the arrows of Artemis (11.172–173), the sea or a raid or a fight for a city (11.399–403), and each answer is neither (11.197–203, 11.405–411). The scholia, as Merry reports them, explain the question to Agamemnon from the fact that phantoms show no wounds (see [[slain-in-battle]]).
