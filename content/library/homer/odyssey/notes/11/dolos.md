---
summary: Teiresias foretells that Odysseus will kill the suitors “whether by guile or openly with the sharp bronze” (11.120), and Odysseus tells Agamemnon that Clytemnestra was “preparing a snare” for him while he was far away (11.439).
---

Two snares, one to come and one past. Teiresias' “whether by guile or openly” (ἠὲ δόλῳ ἢ ἀμφαδόν, 11.120) repeats Athena's words to Telemachus in the first book (1.296), and the poem will choose guile (see [[the-suitors]] and [[the-prophecy-of-teiresias]]). Odysseus' answer to Agamemnon's tale names the other: “for Helen's sake many of us perished, / and against you Clytemnestra was preparing a snare while you were far away” (δόλον ἤρτυε, 11.438–439; see [[clytemnestra]], [[schemer]] and [[the-murder-of-agamemnon]]); Agamemnon draws from it his counsel that Odysseus should come home in secret (11.454–456; see [[there-is-no-trusting-women]]).
