---
summary: As in Book 10, the prayers are to the dead and to the gods below. Odysseus begs the tribes of the dead “with vows and prayers” (11.34), and his companions, as they burn the victims, “pray to the gods, / to mighty Hades and dread Persephone” (11.46–47).
---

Odysseus does what Circe prescribed (10.521–534; see the Book 10 note). He implores the strengthless heads of the dead with the vow of a barren heifer and a black sheep (11.29–33; see [[supplication]] and [[sacrifice]]); when he has begged them “with vows and prayers” (εὐχωλῇσι λιτῇσί τε, 11.34; see [[euchomai]] and [[litai]]), he cuts the victims' throats over the pit; and the companions pray to Hades and Persephone while they burn the sheep (ἐπεύξασθαι, 11.46–47; see [[hades]], [[persephone]] and [[the-offering-to-the-dead]]). At 11.261 εὔχετʼ is “claimed”, of Antiope's boast, not a prayer (see [[euchomai]]).
