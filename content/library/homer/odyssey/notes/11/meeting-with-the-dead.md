---
summary: Odysseus' tale of the dead is a chain of such meetings, each told with some of the fixed verses and each varying them: Elpenor (11.51–83), his mother (11.84–89, 11.152–224), Teiresias (11.90–151), Agamemnon (11.387–466), Achilles (11.467–540), Ajax (11.543–564) and Heracles (11.601–627).
refs: [11.51-83, 11.84-89, 11.90-151, 11.152-224, 11.387-466, 11.467-540, 11.543-564, 11.601-627]
---

“And there came the soul” brings his mother, Teiresias, Agamemnon and Achilles (11.84, 11.90, 11.387, 11.467); Elpenor comes “first” of all, because he is not yet buried (11.51–52; see [[unwept-and-unburied]]). Odysseus weeps and pities Elpenor, his mother and Agamemnon in the same verse (11.55, 11.87, 11.395).

The blood divides the meetings. Elpenor speaks without it, and Merry notes that he neither asks for it nor shows any need of it; Teiresias knows Odysseus and speaks to him before he drinks, and drinks before he prophesies; his mother sits in silence near the blood and does not know her son until she has drunk, and then “at once she knew me” (11.141–144, 11.153; Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 11.96). Agamemnon too knows him “at once, when he had drunk the dark blood” (11.390); of Achilles the tale says only that he knew him (11.471), and Heracles knows him “when he saw me with his eyes” (11.615; see [[dark-blood]]).

Three souls address him “lamenting,” his mother, Achilles and Heracles (11.154, 11.472, 11.616), and five of the dead greet him with the same verse, “Son of Laertes, sprung from Zeus, Odysseus of many devices”: Elpenor, Teiresias, Agamemnon, Achilles and Heracles (11.60, 11.92, 11.405, 11.473, 11.617; see [[son-of-laertes]]). The embrace fails twice: three times his mother flies from his hands (11.206–208), and Agamemnon stretches out his hands to him, “eager to reach me,” with no strength left in his limbs (11.392–394; see [[supple]]). Elpenor's meeting and Agamemnon's close with the same verse, “So we two sat exchanging hateful words” and “So we two stood exchanging hateful words” (11.81, 11.465), his mother's with “So we two were exchanging words” (11.225). Teiresias and Heracles go back into the house of Hades, and twice the verse that follows is the same, “but I stayed there steadfastly” (11.150–152, 11.627–628).

Ajax breaks the pattern. His soul alone stands apart, and to Odysseus' soothing words “he answered me nothing, but went after the other / souls of the dead who have died, into Erebus” (11.543–544, 11.563–564; see [[soothing-words]] and [[the-dead-who-have-died]]). The heroines and the last sights are told in another manner, as a catalogue of what Odysseus saw: “And after her I saw,” “And I saw,” “And after him I caught sight of” (11.260, 11.266, 11.298, 11.572, 11.601; see the Odyssey translation's formulas).
