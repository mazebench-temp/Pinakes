---
summary: In the wooden horse “the other leaders and rulers of the Danaans / were wiping away their tears” (11.526–527).
---

ἡγήτορες ἠδὲ μέδοντες, “leaders and rulers”, is the Iliad's phrase for the chieftains of the army, and here they are the men inside the horse, set beside Neoptolemus: they wept and trembled, he did not (11.526–530; see [[the-wooden-horse]], [[neoptolemus]] and [[the-brave-man-in-ambush]]). The command was Odysseus' own, “to me had been given the whole command” (11.524).
