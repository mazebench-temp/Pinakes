summary: Odysseus followed “brilliant Agamemnon / to Ilios of the fine horses” (11.168–169); Alcinous could listen “even till brilliant Dawn” (11.375); Agamemnon says that “brilliant Orestes has not yet died upon the earth” (11.461); and Eurypylus was the handsomest man Odysseus saw “after brilliant Memnon” (11.522).
grc: ["=δίῳ"]
---

Ἀγαμέμνονι δίῳ is new to the entry. The father and the son are both δῖος in the book, the dead king in Odysseus' words to his mother and the living son in the dead king's own (11.168, 11.461; see [[agamemnon]] and [[orestes]]). Line 11.461 was rejected “for its silliness,” since Agamemnon has just asked whether his son is still alive (11.458; see the translation's note on 11.461). Memnon is named only here in Homer (see [[memnon]]).
