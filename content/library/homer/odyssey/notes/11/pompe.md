---
summary: In the pause of the tale the escort is discussed: Odysseus says that his escort will be “the care of the gods and of you” (11.332); Alcinous, keeping him until tomorrow, says that “his escort will be the concern of men, / of all of them, and mine most of all” (11.352–353); and Odysseus would stay a year if they would “speed my escort and give splendid gifts” (11.357).
---

The escort was fixed for the morrow in Book 7, and the ship and its crew made ready in Book 8 (7.317–318; 8.34–55); Merry takes the ἑταῖροι to whom Odysseus might go to sleep at the ship (11.331) as the sailors Alcinous had sent down to the harbor (W. W. Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 11.331; see [[phaeacians]]). Breaking off his tale, Odysseus leaves the escort to “the care of the gods and of you” (11.332). Arete asks the Phaeacians not to hurry to send him away (ἀποπέμπετε) or cut short the gifts (11.338–341; see [[xenia]] and [[arete-queen-of-the-phaeacians]]), and Alcinous answers with the verses of Telemachus to his mother, with the escort in place of speech: “and his escort will be the concern of men, / of all of them, and mine most of all; for mine is the power among the people” (11.352–353; see [[war-is-the-concern-of-men]], [[kratos]] and [[alcinous]]). Odysseus' answer joins the escort to the gifts: if they bid him stay a year, “and should speed my escort and give splendid gifts”, he would come home with a fuller hand (11.356–359; see [[doron]]).
