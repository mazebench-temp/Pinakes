summary: In the land of the dead Ithaca is the goal of every question: the vow of a heifer when Odysseus comes home (11.30), Teiresias' promise that he may still reach it (11.111), his mother's question whether he has not yet come to it (11.162), his own wish to come home to it with a fuller hand (11.361), and the way “to rocky Ithaca” that he came to ask of the seer (11.480).
# Ithaca without the name: "our own land" (11.167, 11.482), "my dear native land" (11.359), "your dear native land"
# (11.455).

**The vow and the prophecy.** At the pit Odysseus vows to the dead, as Circe told him, a barren heifer, the best he has, to be sacrificed “when I came to Ithaca” in his halls (11.30–31, after 10.521–523; see [[the-offering-to-the-dead]] and the Book 10 note). Teiresias makes the homecoming conditional: if the cattle of the Sun are left unharmed, “you may still reach Ithaca, though suffering evils” (11.111; see [[thrinacia]] and [[the-cattle-of-helios]]); if not, Odysseus will come home late and alone, and find trouble in his house (11.112–117; see [[the-suitors]]).

**Not yet home.** His mother asks, “And have you not yet come / to Ithaca, nor seen your wife in your halls?” (11.161–162; see [[anticleia]]), and he answers that he has not yet come near the Achaean land, “nor yet set foot / on our own land” (11.166–167; see [[achaea]]); he gives Achilles the same answer, that he came to consult Teiresias “how I might come to rocky Ithaca” (11.480–482; see [[rocky]]). παιπαλόεσσα, “rocky”, is said of Ithaca only here; elsewhere in the Odyssey it is the epithet of Samos and Chios, and of a lookout and a path (4.671, 3.170, 10.97, 17.204), while Ithaca is “craggy” and “rugged” (1.247, 9.27; see [[craggy]] and [[rugged]]). Anticleia's answer brings the house itself into the land of the dead: Penelope waiting in the halls, Telemachus holding the domains, Laertes in the country, sleeping in the dust by the fire with the slaves in winter and on fallen leaves in summer (11.181–196; see [[penelope]], [[telemachus]] and [[laertes]]).

**Home with a fuller hand.** In the pause in the hall, Odysseus tells Alcinous that he would gladly stay a year for the gifts, since to come home with a fuller hand would be far better, “and I would be more respected and dearer to men, / by all who should see me come home to Ithaca” (11.360–361; see [[alcinous]] and [[doron]]). And Agamemnon, murdered on his homecoming, advises him to bring his ship to his dear native land in secret, “for there is no trusting women any more” (11.455–456; see [[agamemnon]] and [[dear-native-land]]).
