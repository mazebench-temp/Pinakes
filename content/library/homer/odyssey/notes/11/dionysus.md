---
summary: Dionysus is named once in Book 11, in the story of Ariadne, whom Artemis slew “in sea-washed Dia, on the testimony of Dionysus” (11.325). The poem does not say what he testified.
# Διονύσου (11.325) is claimed by the entry.
---

**The testimony of Dionysus.** Theseus was bringing Ariadne from Crete to Athens, “but he had no joy of her; for before that Artemis slew her / in sea-washed Dia, on the testimony of Dionysus” (11.324–325; see [[ariadne]], [[theseus]], [[artemis]] and [[dia]]). Διονύσου μαρτυρίῃσιν is all the poem says. Merry reports two explanations: that Dionysus told Artemis that Theseus had lain with Ariadne in her sacred grove in Dia, or that Ariadne had been promised to Dionysus and had given herself to a mortal lover. The common account, he notes, is different: Theseus deserted Ariadne in Dia, which was taken for Naxos, and Dionysus made her his wife (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 11.322). Hesiod knows the marriage: “golden-haired Dionysus made blond Ariadne, / the daughter of Minos, his flourishing wife, / and the son of Cronus made her deathless and ageless for him” (*Theogony* 947–949; see [[hesiod]]). The Dia of this verse Merry takes for the islet off Heracleion on the north coast of Crete, which Theseus would pass on the way to Athens (on 11.322; see the translation's note on 11.321–325).

**The form of the name.** Διονύσου, with a short ο, occurs only here in Homer; the regular form in the Iliad and the Odyssey is Διώνυσος (*Iliad* 6.132, 6.135, 14.325; 24.74), while the hymns commonly have Διόνυσος (Merry on 11.325; see the general article). Merry thinks the whole passage of Phaedra, Procris and Ariadne, 11.321–325, a later interpolation which, like other passages that allude to Athenian legend, may belong to the date of the Peisistratean recension (on 11.321).
