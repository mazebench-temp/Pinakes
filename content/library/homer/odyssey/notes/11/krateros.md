summary: “the strong might of the blazing fire” that burns the bodies of the dead (11.220); Pelias and Neleus, “mighty attendants of great Zeus” (11.255); Amphion and Zethus, “mighty though they were” (11.265); “Hades, the strong gatekeeper” (11.277); “the fierce battle” (11.417); and Tantalus and Sisyphus “suffering bitter pains” (11.582, 11.593).
---

κρατερὼ θεράποντε Διός: Merry takes “attendants of Zeus” for kings, as the attendants of Ares are warriors and those of the Muses singers (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 11.255; see [[pelias]] and [[neleus]]). κρατέρʼ ἄλγεʼ ἔχοντα, “suffering bitter pains,” ends the same verse for the two sinners among the dead (11.582, 11.593; see [[tantalus]] and [[sisyphus]]), with the entry's English “bitter.” Elsewhere the word is the might of fire, of the god of the dead (see [[gatekeeper]]) and of the battles Odysseus has seen, which Agamemnon sets beside the slaughter at the feast (11.416–420).
