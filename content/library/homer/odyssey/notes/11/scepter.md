---
summary: Teiresias comes to the blood “holding a golden scepter” (11.91), and Minos sits among the dead “holding a golden scepter, giving the law to the dead” (11.568–569).
---

**The seer's scepter.** ἦλθε δʼ ἐπὶ ψυχὴ Θηβαίου Τειρεσίαο / χρύσεον σκῆπτρον ἔχων, “And there came the soul of Theban Teiresias, / holding a golden scepter” (11.90–91; see [[teiresias]] and [[gold-and-silver]]). The participle agrees with the man, not with ψυχή, a construction by sense, and such a gold-studded staff, W. W. Merry notes, was the badge of office, carried by kings, by priests and prophets, as by Chryses (*Iliad* 1.15) and Teiresias here, by heralds and by judges (*Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 11.90; see [[chryses]] and [[divination]]). Among the dead the seer keeps the staff of his office, as he alone of the dead keeps his mind (10.493–495).

**The judge's scepter.** Near the end of the book Odysseus sees Minos, the splendid son of Zeus, “holding a golden scepter, giving the law to the dead, / seated” (11.569–570; see [[minos]] and [[themis]]), in nearly the same words as Teiresias. The elders of the Iliad's shield hold heralds' scepters as they give judgment (*Iliad* 18.505).
