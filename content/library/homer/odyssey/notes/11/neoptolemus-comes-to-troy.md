summary: Odysseus tells the soul of Achilles that it was he who brought Neoptolemus in his ship from Scyros to the Achaeans at Troy (11.506–509), the only place in Homer that says how the son of Achilles came to the war; the rest of his account tells what the young man did there (11.510–537).
# Unnamed: the voyage from Scyros (11.508-509).
refs: [11.508-509]
passages:
  - 11.505-512 | Neoptolemus brought from Scyros
---

**Brought by Odysseus.** Achilles asks first for his son: “whether he followed to the war to be a champion, or not” (11.492–493; see [[achilles]]). The answer begins with his coming: “for it was I myself who brought him in the hollow, balanced ship / from Scyros to join the well-greaved Achaeans” (11.508–509; see [[neoptolemus]] and [[scyros]]). In the Iliad Achilles speaks of “my dear son, who is being reared for me in Scyros, / if indeed godlike Neoptolemus is still alive” (*Iliad* 19.326–327), and the Odyssey has the boy at Troy, among the leaders in the horse (11.523–532), and coming home safe with the Myrmidons, “whom the shining son of great-hearted Achilles led” (3.188–189). Merry explains the voyage from the later story: the young man had been brought up in Scyros by his grandfather Lycomedes, and Odysseus fetched him because the seer Helenus had declared that Troy could never be taken without him (W. W. Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 11.508; see [[helenus]]).

**Proved.** What follows in Odysseus' answer is the proof that the boy was a champion: first in counsel after Nestor and Odysseus, far ahead of the rest in battle, the killer of Eurypylus, steady in the horse, and unwounded at the end (11.510–537; see [[neoptolemus-kills-eurypylus]] and [[sinon-and-the-wooden-horse]]). For Merry's notes on the passage, see [[neoptolemus]].
