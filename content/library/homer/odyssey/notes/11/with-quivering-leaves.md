---
summary: “They were eager to set Ossa on Olympus, and on Ossa / Pelion with its quivering leaves, so that heaven might be scaled” (11.315–316).
---

The epithet of Neritum on Ithaca (9.22) is given to the mountain at the top of the giants' stair (see [[pelion]] and [[ossa-mountain]]). The two verses were rejected as impossible; Merry gives Aristarchus' objection, that at 11.313 Olympus is the place of the fight and here only the first step to the gods, and thinks the lines an interpolation from a Gigantomachy (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 11.315–316; see the translation's note on 11.315–316).
