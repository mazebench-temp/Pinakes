summary: Over the Cimmerians “deadly night is spread over those wretched mortals” (11.19); and Heracles greets Odysseus, “ah, wretch, truly you too drag out some evil fate, / such as I bore under the rays of the sun” (11.618–619).
grc: ["δειλοῖσι βροτοῖσι*"]
---

δειλοῖσι βροτοῖσι, the Iliad's verse-end of mortals in their misery (*Iliad* 22.31, 24.525), is said of the Cimmerians (see [[cimmerians]] and [[deadly]]). ἆ δείλʼ is said in pity by the dead hero to the living man; Merry renders “Wretched man, an evil sort of fate thou too dost endure,” and the translation takes the verse as a statement, not a question (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 11.618; see the translation's note on 11.618–619, and [[heracles]]).
