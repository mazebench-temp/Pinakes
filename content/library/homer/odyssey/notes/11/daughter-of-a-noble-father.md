---
summary: “There the first I saw was Tyro, daughter of a noble father, / who said that she was the offspring of noble Salmoneus” (11.235–236).
---

The first of the heroines is named by her father before anything else is told of her, and the next verse names him (see [[tyro]] and [[blameless]]). Merry reports the scholiasts' explanation of her name, from τυρός, “curd-white,” for her fairness (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 11.235). In the Iliad and later in the Odyssey the epithet is Helen's (see the general article).
