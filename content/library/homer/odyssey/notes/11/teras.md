---
summary: Teiresias gives Odysseus a sign, “very easy to see”, of the land where he must plant his oar: a wayfarer who takes it for a winnowing fan (11.126–128).
---

The σῆμα here is a token to be recognized, not a portent sent by a god: “And I will tell you a sign, very easy to see, and it will not escape you” (σῆμα δέ τοι ἐρέω μάλʼ ἀριφραδές, 11.126). When another wayfarer says that Odysseus carries a winnowing fan on his shoulder, he will know that he has come among men who do not know the sea; the proof would be unmistakable, and the mistake not absurd, since both oar and winnowing shovel may be called a blade (W. W. Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 11.128; see [[winnowing-fan]], [[oars]] and [[the-prophecy-of-teiresias]]). At 11.75 σῆμα is Elpenor's mound, and belongs to [[tomb]].
