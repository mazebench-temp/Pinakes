# μήνιμα (11.73), as Il. 22.358.
grc: [μήνιμα]
en: [a cause of the gods' wrath]
---

μήνιμα is the thing that brings on μῆνις, the wrath of the gods (see the Odyssey translation's table of key words, θεῶν μήνιμα). Elpenor's soul warns Odysseus not to leave him unwept and unburied, “lest I become a cause of the gods' wrath against you” (11.72–73; see [[elpenor]] and [[honoring-the-dead]]), the verse with which the dying Hector, refused the return of his body, warned Achilles (*Iliad* 22.358). Merry calls Elpenor's claim a post-Homeric refinement: the gods are not shown taking offense at the sight of an unburied corpse, and Hector's verse refers not to the denial of burial but to the outrages done to his body (W. W. Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 11.51).
