# Κρονίονος (11.620) is claimed by the entry.
---

**The son of Cronus.** Heracles' phantom begins the story of his labors with his descent: Ζηνὸς μὲν πάϊς ἦα Κρονίονος, “I was the son of Zeus, son of Cronus, yet I had misery / boundless; for I was made subject to a man far worse than I” (11.620–622; see [[heracles]], [[zeus]], [[son-of-cronus]] and [[eurystheus]]). Κρονίονος, the genitive of Κρονίων with a short ο, is found only here and in Sleep's refusal in the Iliad, “but I would not come near Zeus, the son of Cronus” (*Iliad* 14.247; see the Iliad's Book 14 note). The full title of the father sets off the son's misery, as Achilles set Heracles' death against his father's love: “not even mighty Heracles escaped his doom, / who was the dearest of all to lord Zeus, son of Cronus” (*Iliad* 18.117–118).

**At the limits.** The book does not name Cronus' own place. Merry, explaining the “limits of deep-streaming Oceanus” to which the ship comes (11.13), quotes the Iliad's “lowest limits / of earth and sea, where Iapetus and Cronus / sit and have no joy in the rays of Hyperion the Sun / or in the winds, and deep Tartarus is all around them” (*Iliad* 8.478–481; Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 11.13; see [[oceanus]] and [[titans]]). The land of the Cimmerians beside those limits is likewise one that “the shining sun” never looks on (11.15–19; see [[helios]]).
