summary: Odysseus promises to tell of the companions “who escaped the groaning war cry of the Trojans, / but in their homecoming perished by the will of an evil woman” (11.383–384).
# ἀυτήν (11.383), accent-sensitive against the pronoun αὐτήν.
grc: ["=ἀυτήν"]
en: [war cry]
---

ἀυτή, the war cry, stands for the battle itself, a use more common in the Iliad than in the Odyssey (W. W. Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 11.383). The men who survived the war died at home, and the woman is Clytemnestra, as the tale that follows shows (Merry on 11.384; see [[the-murder-of-agamemnon]] and [[nostos]]).
