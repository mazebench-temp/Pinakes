---
summary: Odysseus offers to tell of his companions, “who escaped the groaning war cry of the Trojans, / but in their homecoming perished by the will of an evil woman” (11.383–384).
grc: [στονόεσσαν]
---

στονόεσσαν ἀυτήν: Merry notes that ἀυτή is used for “battle” more often in the Iliad than in the Odyssey, like φύλοπις (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 11.383; see [[polyaix]]). The evil woman is Clytemnestra, whose victims Odysseus goes on to tell (Merry on 11.384; see [[clytemnestra]]).
