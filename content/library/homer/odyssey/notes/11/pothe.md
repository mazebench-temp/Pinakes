# ποθέων (11.196).
grc: [ποθέων]
en: [longing]
---

**A mother's death.** Asked whether a sickness or Artemis killed her, Anticleia answers that it was neither: “but longing for you, and for your counsels, shining Odysseus, / and for your gentleness, robbed me of my honey-sweet life” (11.202–203; see [[anticleia]], [[nousos]] and [[thymos]]). σός πόθος is the longing for him, and its objects are three, “for thee, and for thy counsels and for thy tenderness” (W. W. Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 11.202); one Venice manuscript reads κήδεα, “cares”, for μήδεα (Merry's critical note; see the Odyssey translation's note on 11.202–203). Eumaeus will tell the stranger the same story: she wasted away with grief for her glorious son, by a wretched death (15.358–360).

**A father's longing.** Laertes lies on the leaves of his vineyard “longing for your homecoming” (σὸν νόστον ποθέων, 11.196; see [[laertes]] and [[nostos]]), the reading of the “more elegant” copies, as a scholium calls them, against σὸν πότμον γοόων, “lamenting your fate”, in most manuscripts (Merry's critical note on 11.196).
