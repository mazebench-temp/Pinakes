---
summary: Eumaeus says that if his master had died at Troy or among his friends, all the Achaeans would have made a tomb for him; now the Harpies have snatched him away without report (14.367–371).
---

“since they did not bring him down among the Trojans / or in the arms of his friends, when he had wound out the war. / Then all the Achaeans would have made a tomb for him, / and he would have won great fame for his son too in days to come. / But now the Harpies have snatched him away without report” (14.367–371; see [[fame-and-the-tomb]], [[harpies]], [[kleos]] and [[eumaeus]]). The swineherd says of his master what Telemachus said to the stranger in Book 1 (1.237–241), and the shade of Achilles will say it of Agamemnon (24.30–33): a tomb raised by the army would have kept his name and his son's. Several manuscripts and Eustathius omit 369–370, which repeat 1.239–240 (D. B. Monro, *Homer's Odyssey, Books XIII–XXIV*, 1901, critical note on 14.369–370).
