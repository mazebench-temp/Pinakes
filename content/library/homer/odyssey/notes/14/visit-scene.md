  - 14.1-54 | The arrival at the swineherd's hut

**Arrival and finding.** Athena had shown him the way, and he goes up “by the rugged path / through the wooded country” (14.1–4; see [[rugged]] and [[wooded]]). “He found him sitting in the porch” (τὸν δʼ ἄρʼ ἐνὶ προδόμῳ εὗρʼ ἥμενον, 14.5), and before the host's occupation is told the poem describes the place, the fold, the twelve sties, the boars and the four dogs (14.5–22; see [[fold]], [[sties]] and [[in-a-place-with-a-view]]). Then the occupation, as Hermes found Calypso singing at her loom (5.61–62): “And he himself was fitting sandals about his feet, / cutting a good-colored oxhide” (14.23–24; see [[sandals]]).

**The dogs.** The welcome is nearly prevented. The dogs rush at the stranger, who sits down “by craft,” and the staff falls from his hand; the swineherd runs out, the leather falls from his, and he drives them off with stones (14.29–36; see [[dogs]] and [[would-have-had-not]]). When Telemachus comes to the hut, the dogs fawn on him without barking, and the vessels with which the swineherd is mixing wine fall from his hands (16.4–13).

**Welcome, seat and meal.** The host's first speech ends in an invitation with the meal before the questions: “But follow me, let us go to the hut, old man, so that you too, / when you have had your heart's fill of bread and wine, / may tell me where you are from and how many sorrows you have endured” (14.45–47; see [[feast]] and [[xenia]]). The seat is a herdsman's: thick brushwood strewn and “the hide of a shaggy wild goat, / his own bedding” (14.49–51), as he will make it again for Telemachus, “green brushwood beneath and a fleece on top” (16.47, a plain rendering). The meal follows at once (14.72–79; see [[sacrifice-type-scene]]), and the questions come only in the evening, after the guest has asked about the master and offered his oath (14.185–190; see [[who-are-you-among-men]]).
