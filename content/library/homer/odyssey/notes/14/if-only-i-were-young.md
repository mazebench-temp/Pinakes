---
summary: The stranger begins the tale of the cloak with Nestor's verse, “If only I were young like that, and my strength were steadfast, / as when we prepared an ambush beneath Troy and led it” (14.468–469), and ends it with the same wish for the present: “If only I were young like that now, and my strength were steadfast” (14.503).
---

14.468 is the verse of Nestor's tale of the games of Amarynceus (*Iliad* 23.629), spoken by a man whom Athena has given “the skin / of an aged old man” (13.431–432; see [[odysseus-changed-into-a-beggar]]). It introduces a story of Troy, the night ambush that Odysseus and Menelaus led, with the speaker third in command (14.469–471; see [[the-tale-of-the-cloak]] and [[ambush]]), and the wish comes back at the end, with νῦν, “now,” in place of εἴθʼ, as Nestor's story of Ereuthalion ends with the verse (*Iliad* 7.157). It leads to the point: “then someone in the farmstead of the swineherds would give me a cloak” (14.504). D. B. Monro finds the formula “less appropriate here, where the story turns upon cunning rather than prowess” (*Homer's Odyssey, Books XIII–XXIV*, 1901, Additional Notes and Corrections, p. 287, on 14.468). He reports that ancient critics rejected 14.504–506, and thinks them weak, since they spoil a story whose point is that “it hints at the need of a cloak without directly asking for one” (Monro on 14.504–506). The stranger announces the wish before he speaks it: εὐξάμενός τι ἔπος ἐρέω, “I will speak a word with a wish” (14.463; see [[euchomai]] and [[the-mad-wine]]).
