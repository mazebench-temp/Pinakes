summary: In the wreck of the Cretan tale: “But for me, Zeus himself, though I had sorrows in my heart, / put the mast of vast length of the dark-prowed ship / into my hands, so that I might still escape bane” (14.310–312).
---

ἱστὸν … νηὸς κυανοπρῴροιο: the mast of a dark-prowed ship was the target set up for the archers at the funeral games of Patroclus (*Iliad* 23.852; see the general article). In Book 12 Odysseus lashed the mast to the keel himself and rode on the two (12.420–425); in the [[the-tale-to-eumaeus|tale]] Zeus puts the mast into his hands (see [[mast]], [[irresistible]] and [[the-shipwreck-of-odysseus]]).
