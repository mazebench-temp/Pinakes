---
summary: Two answers to Eumaeus, “Answering him, Odysseus of many wiles said” (14.191, 14.390), and the thanks for the chine, “and Odysseus of many wiles spoke to him and said” (14.439).
---

The verse of his answers to Athena that morning (13.311, 13.382, 13.416) opens the Cretan tale and the wager (14.191–192, 14.390–400; see [[answering-him]], [[the-tale-to-eumaeus]] and [[rhetre]]). 14.439, καί μιν φωνήσας προσέφη πολύμητις Ὀδυσσεύς, is found only here, and the speech is a blessing on the host: “Would that you, Eumaeus, might be as dear to father Zeus / as you are to me” (14.440–441; see [[eumaeus]] and [[prayer]]).
