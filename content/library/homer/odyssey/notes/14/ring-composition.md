summary: The book begins and ends with the swineherd's care for his master's goods: “the brilliant swineherd, who cared most for his substance / of all the servants that brilliant Odysseus had acquired” (14.3–4), and “Odysseus rejoiced / that he cared so much for his substance while he was far away” (14.526–527); and the tale of the cloak begins and ends with the wish “If only I were young like that” (14.468, 14.503).
refs: [14.3-4, 14.526-527, 14.468, 14.503]

βιότοιο μάλιστα κήδετο at the start, βιότου περικήδετο at the end: the first time the poet says it, and the second time Odysseus sees it, as the swineherd goes out to sleep beside the boars in the storm (see [[eumaeus]] and [[possessions]]). D. B. Monro takes ἐόντος in 14.527 as a possessive genitive with βιότου, “his substance while he was far away” (*Homer's Odyssey, Books XIII–XXIV*, 1901, on 14.527). A pair of verses marks the two things the swineherd acquired by himself, the fold and the slave Mesaulius, “while his master was away, / without his mistress and old Laertes” (14.8–9 = 14.450–451; see [[fold]] and [[mesaulius]]).
