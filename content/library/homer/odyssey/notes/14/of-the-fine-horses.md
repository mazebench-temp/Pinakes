---
summary: Eumaeus of his master: “for he too went, to win satisfaction for Agamemnon, / to Ilios of the fine horses, to fight the Trojans” (14.70–71).
---

14.71 is the verse that brings the Myrmidon Epeigeus to Troy in the Iliad (*Iliad* 16.576), and Odysseus told his mother among the dead why he had gone in the same words in the first person, “to fight the Trojans” (11.168–169; see the general article). The swineherd says it of his master as of a man who will not come back: “but he has perished” (14.68–71; see [[troy]] and [[agamemnon]]).
