# διεμοιρᾶτο (14.434) and μοίρῃ (14.448; again 22.54), first met here among the Odyssey's notes.
grc: [διεμοιρᾶτο, μοίρῃ]
en: [portion, amiss]
---

**Fate.** “—would that I had died and met my fate / there in Egypt; for a bane was still waiting for me—” (ὡς ὄφελον θανέειν καὶ πότμον ἐπισπεῖν, 14.274–275; see [[thanatos]] and [[the-raid-on-egypt]]): the half-verse of Odysseus' own wish on the raft, “Would that I too had died and met my fate” (5.308).

**Portions.** At the evening sacrifice “the swineherd / stood up to carve; for in his mind he knew well what was due. / And cutting it, he divided it all into seven portions; / one he set aside for the nymphs and for Hermes, son of Maia, / with a prayer, and the others he gave to each” (ἕπταχα πάντα διεμοιρᾶτο δαΐζων, 14.432–436; see [[sacrifice]], [[nymphs]], [[hermes]] and [[maia]]). Monro quotes J. L. Myres on the custom as it survived in Greece: the host stands and picks over the whole dish, “putting fair equivalents towards each of the guests” (D. B. Monro, *Homer's Odyssey, Books XIII–XXIV*, 1901, on 14.432). After the libation Eumaeus puts the cup into the stranger's hands, “and he sat down by his own portion” (ὁ δʼ ἕζετο ᾗ παρὰ μοίρῃ, 14.448).

**Due measure.** “Old man, the tale you have told is flawless, / and you have not yet spoken any word amiss or profitless” (οὐδέ τί πω παρὰ μοῖραν ἔπος, 14.508–509; see [[ainos]], [[epos]] and [[the-tale-of-the-cloak]]): παρὰ μοῖραν, beside or beyond the portion, is the opposite of κατὰ μοῖραν, “as is due” (see the entry).
