---
summary: Eumaeus will not name his master: “Him, stranger, even though he is not here, I feel shame / to name; for he loved me greatly and cared for me in his heart; / but I call him dear master, even though he is far away” (14.145–147).
grc: [ἠθεῖον]
en: [dear master]
---

The only time Homer uses ἠθεῖος of someone who is not addressed. Monro takes it that the swineherd does not name Odysseus in speaking of him, but uses ἠθεῖος, “which is properly a form of respectful address,” as titles are used now; elsewhere there is only the vocative (D. B. Monro, *Homer's Odyssey, Books XIII–XXIV*, 1901, on 14.145–147). The translation renders it “dear master,” where the Iliad's vocative is “dear brother” (see the Odyssey translation's key words). He has just named him once, “but longing for Odysseus, who is gone, takes hold of me” (14.144; see [[aidos]] and [[eumaeus]]).
