---
summary: Euripides' Odysseus tells the Cyclops the law that the swineherd keeps in this book, to receive suppliants wrecked at sea, give them gifts and help them with clothing, and calls on Zeus of strangers; the poor farmer of the Electra, reckoning what he can give two noble strangers, has the swineherd's means and his thought.
---

- **The law of strangers.** In the *Cyclops* Odysseus pleads with Polyphemus: “it is a law for mortals” to receive suppliants wrecked at sea, to give them gifts of hospitality and to help them with clothing, not to spit their limbs and fill one's belly and jaws with them (*Cyclops* 299–303, plain renderings), and when the Cyclops will not listen he prays, “Zeus of strangers, see this” (Ζεῦ ξένιʼ, ὅρα τάδʼ, *Cyclops* 354). The swineherd keeps the law without being asked: “Stranger, it is not lawful for me, not even if one more wretched than you should come, / to dishonor a stranger; for they are all from Zeus, / strangers and beggars” (14.56–58), and the talk of the book keeps returning to clothing, “a cloak and a tunic” (14.132, 14.154, 14.396, 14.516), the gift that the [[thesprotians|Thesprotian]] prince had given the castaway in the [[the-tale-to-eumaeus|tale]] (14.320): [[strangers-and-beggars-are-from-zeus]], [[god-of-guests]], [[euripides-cyclops]] and [[cloak]].
- **A poor man's welcome.** In the *Electra* the farmer to whom Electra has been married comes home to find two noble strangers at his house and reckons what he has: there is still enough in the house to fill them with food for one day; at such times, he says, he sees how great a power wealth has, to give to strangers and to save a body that has fallen sick, “but daily food comes to little; for every man, when he is filled, rich or poor, has the same” (*Electra* 424–431, plain renderings). The swineherd serves his guest “what slaves have here”, porkers, because the fat hogs go to the suitors (14.80–81), and calls his gift “small but dear” (14.58). Euripides does not name Homer, and the likeness is one of kind: [[eumaeus]] and [[xenia]].
