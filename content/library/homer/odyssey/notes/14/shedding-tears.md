---
summary: The king of Egypt takes the Cretan suppliant home: “and seating me in his chariot he took me home as I shed tears” (14.280).
---

δάκρυ χέοντα, the participle of the Iliad's pitied men, of Agamemnon and of Ajax as Zeus pities them (*Iliad* 8.245, 17.648), here after the pity of the man supplicated: “and he protected me and pitied me” (14.279; see [[supplication-type-scene]] and [[pity]]). Earlier in the book the tears are Penelope's, shed at every vagrant's tale: “and as she mourns, the tears fall from her eyelids” (14.129; see [[vagrants-tell-lies]], [[tears]] and [[penelope]]).
