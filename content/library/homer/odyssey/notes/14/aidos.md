summary: Eumaeus feels “shame / to name” his absent master (14.145–146); the Cretan was once “feared and respected among the Cretans” (14.234); Eumaeus will “respect” the stranger for Zeus' sake, not for his lies (14.388); and the beggar's story ends with the “respect for a good man” that would have given him a cloak (14.505).
# αἰδέσσομαι (14.388), first met here among the Odyssey's notes.
grc: [αἰδέσσομαι]
---

**Shame to name him.** “Him, stranger, even though he is not here, I feel shame / to name; for he loved me greatly and cared for me in his heart; / but I call him dear master, even though he is far away” (καὶ οὐ παρεόντʼ ὀνομάζειν / αἰδέομαι, 14.145–147; see [[dear-brother]] and [[philos]]). The swineherd does not name Odysseus in speaking of him, but uses ἠθεῖος, properly a form of respectful address; “The modern use of titles shows many instances of the same kind” (D. B. Monro, *Homer's Odyssey, Books XIII–XXIV*, 1901, on 14.145–147). He has just named him (14.144).

**Feared and respected.** The Cretan's raids made him great at home: “and quickly my house grew, and then / I was feared and respected among the Cretans” (δεινός τʼ αἰδοῖός τε, 14.233–234; see [[cretans]] and [[piracy]]), the pair of words that Athena wished for Odysseus among the Phaeacians (8.22).

**Respect for a guest.** Eumaeus states the ground of his welcome: “for it is not for that reason that I shall respect you or make you welcome, / but in fear of Zeus, the god of guests, and in pity for you yourself” (σʼ αἰδέσσομαι, 14.388–389; see [[god-of-guests]], [[xenia]] and [[deos]]). The beggar's story turns the same words into a request: someone would give him a cloak, if he were young, “for both reasons, out of friendship and respect for a good man” (φιλότητι καὶ αἰδοῖ φωτὸς ἑῆος, 14.505; see [[the-tale-of-the-cloak]], [[philotes]] and [[ainos]]).
