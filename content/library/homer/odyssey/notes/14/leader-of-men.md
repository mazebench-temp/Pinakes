---
summary: The dogs, “four of them, which the swineherd, leader of men, had reared” (14.22); and “Then the swineherd, leader of men, answered him” (14.121).
---

The title of Pisistratus and of Polites is given to the swineherd (see the general article and the Odyssey translation's key words, δῖος ὑφορβός). He has four men under him (14.24–26) and a slave of his own (14.449–452; see [[eumaeus]] and [[mesaulius]]). Callistratus suspected 14.22 “for the counting of the dogs and the epithet,” as the scholia report; Monro finds the epithet not unusual, but thinks it singular that the dogs are as many as the swineherd's men, and suggests that the line first spoke of the men (D. B. Monro, *Homer's Odyssey, Books XIII–XXIV*, 1901, critical note on 14.22). The formula comes again with Eumaeus in the next books, and with the cowherd Philoetius (15.351, 15.389, 16.36, 17.184; 20.185, 20.254).
