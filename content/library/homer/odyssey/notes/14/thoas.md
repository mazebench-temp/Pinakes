**The messenger.** In the tale that the stranger tells Eumaeus on the stormy night, Odysseus, to get a cloak for his shivering comrade, tells the men in the ambush of a dream and asks for someone to go to Agamemnon for more men from the ships (14.490–498; see [[the-tale-of-the-cloak]] and [[odysseus]]). τόνδε, “this”, is the device of sending Thoas (D. B. Monro, *Homer's Odyssey, Books XIII–XXIV*, 1901, on 14.490). The device works:

{{quote:38ce9d41-3dee-5806-8607-5f6874f9b454}}

The Thoas of the [[the-tale-of-the-cloak|tale]] is the Iliad's, the leader of the Aetolians, “skilled with the javelin / and good in close fighting”, one of the young men who contend in speeches in the assembly (*Iliad* 15.281–284) and who carry Agamemnon's gifts to Achilles (*Iliad* 19.238–239; see [[aetolians]] and [[andraemon]]). His cloak is φοινικόεσσα, “crimson”, as was Nestor's when he went out on the night of the Doloneia (*Iliad* 10.133; see [[crimson]] and [[the-doloneia-question]]), and he lays it aside to run, as Odysseus threw off his cloak and ran through the camp to stop the flight to the ships (*Iliad* 2.183; see [[cloak]]). For θέτο, the reading of G, D, U and others, F, P, H and X have βάλε, the verb of that verse (Monro's critical note on 14.500). Telemachus lays aside his crimson cloak in the same half-verse when he sets up the axes for the contest of the bow (21.118).
