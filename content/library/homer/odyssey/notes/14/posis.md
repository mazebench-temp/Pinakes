---
summary: Penelope weeps at every false report, “as is the custom of a woman, when her husband has perished far away” (14.130).
---

Every wanderer who comes to Ithaca goes to Penelope with deceits, “and she receives him well and makes him welcome and questions him about each thing, / and as she mourns, the tears fall from her eyelids, / as is the custom of a woman, when her husband has perished far away” (ἐπὴν πόσις ἄλλοθʼ ὄληται, 14.128–130; see [[penelope]], [[themis]] and [[tears]]). Eumaeus speaks of her husband as dead, and the husband is listening. Two manuscripts read ἐπεί, “since”, for ἐπήν, “when” (D. B. Monro, *Homer's Odyssey, Books XIII–XXIV*, 1901, critical note on 14.130).
