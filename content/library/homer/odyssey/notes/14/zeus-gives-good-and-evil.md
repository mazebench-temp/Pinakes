# "δύναται γὰρ ἅπαντα" (14.445 = 4.237), Helen's half-verse, which the entry's phrase does not reach here.
grc: ["δύναται γὰρ ἅπαντα"]
en: [for he can do all things]
refs: [14.444]
---

The stranger has thanked his host for the long chine with a wish, “Would that you, Eumaeus, might be as dear to father Zeus / as you are to me, since you honor me with good things, such as I am” (14.440–441; see [[sacrifice]] and [[prayer]]). Eumaeus turns the thanks aside: “Eat, strange guest, and take your delight in these things, / such as they are; and the god will give one thing and let another be, / whatever he wishes in his heart; for he can do all things” (14.443–445). The last half-verse is Helen's at Sparta, where it closes the maxim that Zeus gives good and evil, now to one and now to another (4.236–237); Eumaeus' version has no Zeus by name and no evil, only the god who gives one thing and withholds another as he pleases. It is spoken by a man who has lost his master and serves his enemies, to a guest he believes to be one of many beggars, and the guest is the master whom the god is about to give back (see [[eumaeus]] and [[the-gods-can-do-all-things]]).
