summary: The beggar's tale: “But when far-seeing Zeus devised that hateful journey, / which loosed the knees of many men” (14.235–236).
---

τήν γε στυγερὴν ὁδόν, the journey to Troy, with an article that Monro takes to express aversion (D. B. Monro, *Homer's Odyssey, Books XIII–XXIV*, 1901, on 14.235). The second verse ends as Eumaeus' wish against the race of Helen, “since she loosed the knees of many men” (14.69): the swineherd blames the woman, the Cretan the journey and the god who devised it (see [[helen]], [[far-seeing]] and [[at-whom-men-shudder]]).
