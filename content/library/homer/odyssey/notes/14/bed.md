summary: The beds of the swineherd's hut. Eumaeus seats his guest on his own bedding, brushwood under a goat's hide (14.49–51); after supper the men hasten to bed (14.455), and he makes the guest a bed by the fire, with skins of sheep and goats and a cloak over him (14.518–522), but will not sleep there himself (14.524–525).
# Forms first met here; they apply in the Odyssey only: ἐνεύναιον, "bedding" (14.51; ἐνευναίων 16.35), κοῖτον (14.455,
# only here) and the phrases "αὐτόθι κοῖτος" (14.525; κοῖτος is also "rest", 19.515, and a bird's roost, 22.470) and
# "εὐνήν ἐν δʼ ὀΐων" (14.519), since εὐνήν alone is also a lion's lair (4.338).

**His own bedding.** Eumaeus “brought him in and seated him, and strewed thick brushwood beneath him, / and spread on it the hide of a shaggy wild goat, / his own bedding, great and thick” (14.49–51; see [[hut]], [[goats]] and [[xenia]]): αὐτοῦ ἐνεύναιον, “his own very bed-covering” (D. B. Monro, *Homer's Odyssey, Books XIII–XXIV*, 1901, on 14.51).

**The bed by the fire.** After supper “Mesaulius took away their bread, and they hastened to bed” (14.455; see [[mesaulius]]). When the [[the-tale-of-the-cloak|tale of the cloak]] has won its point, the swineherd “set for him near the fire / a bed, and threw on it the skins of sheep and goats. / There Odysseus lay down; and he threw over him a cloak” (14.518–520; see [[fire]], [[sheep]] and [[cloak]]), and the young men slept beside him; “but the swineherd / did not like to sleep there, away from the boars”, and went out to sleep by them under a hollow rock (14.524–533; see [[eumaeus]] and [[fold]]).
