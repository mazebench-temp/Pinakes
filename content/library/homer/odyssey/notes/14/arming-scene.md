summary: In the raid on Egypt the Cretan gives himself up: “at once I took the well-made helmet from my head / and the shield from my shoulders, and threw the spear from my hand” (14.276–277); and in the tale of the cloak he followed the ambush “with only my shield and my shining kilt” (14.482).
refs: [14.276-277, 14.482]
---

The helmet verse of the arming scene, κρατὶ δʼ ἐπʼ ἰφθίμῳ κυνέην εὔτυκτον ἔθηκεν, “and on his mighty head he set a well-made helmet” (*Iliad* 3.336), is turned round: ἀπὸ κρατὸς κυνέην εὔτυκτον ἔθηκα, and helmet, shield and spear are put off in turn before the man goes unarmed to the king's horses (14.276–279; see [[well-made]] and [[supplication-type-scene]]). At Troy he went out to the night ambush without his cloak, and D. B. Monro notes that he has a tunic on, which “is always taken for granted,” as Hector is called γυμνός when he puts off his shield and helmet (*Homer's Odyssey, Books XIII–XXIV*, 1901, on 14.482; *Iliad* 22.124; see [[the-tale-of-the-cloak]], [[kilt]] and [[phaeinos]]). Eumaeus' gear for his night with the boars is in [[dressing-scene]].
