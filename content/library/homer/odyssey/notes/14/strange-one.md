---
summary: Eumaeus gives the stranger the chine: “Eat, strange guest, and take your delight in these things, / such as they are” (14.443–444).
grc: [δαιμόνιε]
en: [strange guest]
---

δαιμόνιε ξείνων, with the genitive of the class, as the swineherd's ἆ δειλὲ ξείνων, “Ah, wretched stranger” (14.361; see [[wretch]]): the address of wonder at a man's words, here at the guest's wish that the host might be dear to Zeus, “since you honor me with good things, such as I am” (14.440–441). The swineherd answers that the god gives one thing and lets another be (14.444–445; see [[zeus-gives-good-and-evil]] and [[the-gods-can-do-all-things]]).
