---
summary: The voyages of the Cretan tale: nine ships fitted out and the crews feasted for six days, then “on the seventh day we went aboard and from wide Crete / we sailed with a fresh and fair North Wind, / easily, as if downstream,” and in five days came to the river Aegyptus (14.246–258); the Phoenician's ship bound for Libya (14.295–300); and the Thesprotian ship bound for Dulichium, which puts in at Ithaca in the evening (14.334–347).
refs: [14.246-258, 14.295-300, 14.334-347]
---

The voyage to Egypt has the steps of the scene in the Cretan's own verses: the ships fitted out (στέλλω, 14.247–248; see the Odyssey translation's key words and [[the-raid-on-egypt]]), the embarking, the wind, the run, and the halt, “and in the river Aegyptus I brought my curving ships to a halt” (14.258; see [[curving]] and [[aegyptus-river]]). The verse “we sat, and the wind and the helmsmen steered the ships” (14.256) is that of Odysseus' own voyages in his tale to the Phaeacians, in the plural as at 9.78 and in the singular at 11.10 and 12.152. The Phoenician's ship runs “before a fresh and fair North Wind” (14.299) until Zeus wrecks it in the verses of the wreck of Odysseus' last ship (14.301–309; 12.403–419; see [[the-shipwreck-of-odysseus]] and [[retelling]]). The [[thesprotians|Thesprotian]] ship ends its voyage with the steps of a landing told from the side of a captive: the crew go ashore at evening and take their supper on the beach, leaving him bound in the ship (14.344–347; see [[well-benched]] and [[the-tale-to-eumaeus]]).
