---
summary: The Cretan tale opens with the formula's answer: “Well then, I will tell you all this very exactly” (14.192).
---

The verse with which the goddess, as Mentes, began her false account of herself (1.179), and here the beggar begins his. The exactness is put off at once: “May there now be, for us two, for a long time, both food / and sweet wine,” for a whole year would not be enough to tell his sorrows (14.193–198; see [[a-time-for-many-words]]), and the tale that follows is invented (see [[the-tale-to-eumaeus]] and [[pseudos]]). With ξεῖνε in place of ταῦτα the verse is the answer of Telemachus to Mentes and of Eidothea to Menelaus (1.214; 4.383, 4.399), and in the next books it is the answer of the swineherd and of Telemachus to the stranger (15.352, 16.113).
