---
summary: Zeus, the god of guests, “who most of all is indignant at evil deeds” (14.284).
---

In the Cretan tale the king of Egypt spares the suppliant because he “had regard for the wrath of Zeus, / the god of guests, who most of all is indignant at evil deeds” (ὅς τε μάλιστα νεμεσσᾶται κακὰ ἔργα, 14.283–284; see [[the-raid-on-egypt]], [[god-of-guests]], [[menis]] and [[xenia]]). νέμεσις is most often the indignation of men at what is improper (see the entry); here it is the god's, and the evil deeds are those done to a suppliant and stranger, whom Zeus protects as “the avenger of suppliants and strangers” (9.270). Eumaeus has said the same of all the gods: “The blessed gods do not love cruel deeds, / but honor justice” (14.83–84; see [[the-gods-honor-justice]]).
