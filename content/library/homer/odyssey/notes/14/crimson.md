---
summary: In the tale of the cloak: “So he spoke, and then Thoas, son of Andraemon, rose / quickly, and laid aside his crimson cloak, / and set off running to the ships” (14.499–501).
---

The [[the-tale-of-the-cloak|tale]] is set on a freezing night at Troy, like the night of *Iliad* 10, when Nestor fastens on “a crimson cloak” to go out (*Iliad* 10.133; see the general article). Thoas lays it aside to run, as Odysseus threw off his cloak and ran at Athena's word (*Iliad* 2.183), and the teller lies warm in it until dawn (14.501–502; see [[thoas]], [[cloak]] and [[golden-throned]]). χλαῖναν θέτο φοινικόεσσαν closes the verse again when Telemachus lays aside his cloak before the contest of the bow (21.118).
