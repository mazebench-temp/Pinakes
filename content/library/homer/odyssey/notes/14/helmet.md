---
summary: In Egypt the Cretan of the beggar's tale takes off his well-made helmet and his shield and throws away his spear, to go as a suppliant to the king (14.276–277).
---

“at once I took the well-made helmet from my head / and the shield from my shoulders, and threw the spear from my hand” (14.276–277; see [[shield]], [[spear]], [[supplication]] and [[the-raid-on-egypt]]). κυνέη, the helmet of the Iliad's warriors, here εὔτυκτος, “well-made” (see [[well-made]]), is the first piece of armor he lays down: disarmed, he goes to meet the king's horses, clasps his knees, and is spared (14.278–284; see [[egypt]]).
