---
summary: The Cretan claims his line from Crete and from Castor, son of Hylax (14.199, 14.204); and Pheidon's store of Odysseus' treasure “would feed another man to the tenth generation” (14.325).
---

**Line.** Asked “Who are you among men, and from where?” (14.187; see [[who-are-you-among-men]]), the beggar answers with a lineage: “I claim that my line is from wide Crete, / the son of a rich man” (ἐκ μὲν Κρητάων γένος εὔχομαι εὐρειάων, 14.199–200; see [[the-tale-to-eumaeus]], [[crete]] and [[euchomai]]), and “Castor, son of Hylax, of whose line I claim to be” (τοῦ ἐγὼ γένος εὔχομαι εἶναι, 14.204; see [[castor-son-of-hylax]] and [[hylax]]), in the words with which Glaucus ends his genealogy (*Iliad* 6.211). The line is invented, and it includes a bought mother (14.202–203; see [[nothos]]).

**Generation.** Pheidon showed the stranger the treasure that Odysseus had gathered: “It would feed another man to the tenth generation, / so many treasures of his lay stored in the halls of the lord” (καί νύ κεν ἐς δεκάτην γενεὴν ἕτερόν γʼ ἔτι βόσκοι, 14.325–326; see [[possessions]] and [[pheidon]]), that is, would feed one owner and then another for ten generations (D. B. Monro, *Homer's Odyssey, Books XIII–XXIV*, 1901, on 14.325). The verse returns in the tale to Penelope (19.294).
