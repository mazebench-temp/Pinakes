# βουλεύσας (14.296) and βουλευέμεν (14.491), first met here among the Odyssey's notes.
grc: [βουλεύσας, βουλευέμεν]
en: [the will, planned, planning]
---

**The will of Zeus.** In [[thesprotians|Thesprotia]] the king “said that he had gone to Dodona, so that from the god's / high-leafed oak he might hear the will of Zeus, / how he should return to the rich land of Ithaca” (Διὸς βουλὴν ἐπακούσαι, 14.327–329; see [[dodona]], [[the-oak-of-zeus]] and [[nostos]]). The βουλή of the Iliad's proem, the plan of Zeus (see the entry), is here something a man goes to ask of an oracle.

**Plans of the tale.** The Phoenician put the Cretan on a ship for Libya, “having planned lies, so that I might take a cargo there with him, / but there, in truth, so that he might sell me” (ψεύδεα βουλεύσας, 14.296–297; see [[the-tale-to-eumaeus]], [[pseudos]] and [[phoenicians]]); and the [[thesprotians|Thesprotian]] crew, whom [[pheidon|Pheidon]] had bidden to take him to [[acastus-king-of-dulichium|Acastus]] “with care”, had their own plan: “but an evil counsel pleased their minds / concerning me, so that I might yet come utterly to a bane of misery” (κακὴ φρεσὶν ἥνδανε βουλή, 14.337–338; see [[doulios]] and [[phrenes]]). ἐνδυκέως, “with all kindness”, is set against the crew's κακὴ βουλή (D. B. Monro, *Homer's Odyssey, Books XIII–XXIV*, 1901, on 14.337).

**Planning and fighting.** At Troy Odysseus “had this thought in his heart, / such a man was he at planning and at fighting” (βουλευέμεν ἠδὲ μάχεσθαι, 14.490–491; see [[polemos]] and [[the-tale-of-the-cloak]]), the two excellences of a leader, counsel and battle.
