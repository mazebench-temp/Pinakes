---
summary: Eumaeus bought his servant Mesaulius “from the Taphians with his own possessions”, by himself, while his master was away (14.449–452).
---

At the evening meal “Mesaulius served them bread, whom the swineherd / had acquired by himself alone, while his master was away, / without his mistress and old Laertes; / and he had bought him from the Taphians with his own possessions” (449–452; see [[mesaulius]] and [[eumaeus]]). The Taphians are slave dealers here, as in the next book Taphian pirates carry off the Phoenician woman who will steal the child Eumaeus and sell her to his father's house (15.425–429; see [[piracy]], [[trade]], [[the-phoenician-nurse]] and [[the-kidnapping-of-eumaeus]]). πρίατο κτεάτεσσιν ἑοῖσιν, “bought with his own possessions”, ends the verse in which Laertes bought Eurycleia (1.430; see [[eurycleia]]), the verse in which Odysseus asks Eumaeus who bought him (115), and the one in which Eumaeus tells how Laertes bought him from the Phoenicians (15.483): the slave who was bought has bought a slave. The two verses between echo what the poet said of the fold that Eumaeus built for the swine, “while his master was away, / without his mistress and old Laertes” (8–9; see [[fold]] and [[slavery-and-freedom]]).
