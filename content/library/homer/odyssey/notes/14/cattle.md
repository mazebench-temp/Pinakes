**The twelve herds.** “Twelve herds of cattle on the mainland; as many flocks of sheep, / as many droves of pigs, as many wide-ranging herds of goats, / which strangers and his own herdsmen graze” (14.100–102; see [[possessions]] and the Book 14 notes on [[sheep]], [[goats]] and [[boar]]). Cattle stand first, as the measure of wealth (see the entry). The count is made in the verses with which Nestor told of the herds he drove off from Elis, “fifty herds of cattle, as many flocks of sheep” (*Iliad* 11.678; see [[nestor-and-the-epeians]]), and in the pattern of the Sun's herds on Thrinacia, “seven herds of cattle, and as many fine flocks of sheep” (12.129; see [[the-cattle-of-helios]]). Here ἀγέλαι stands alone, and the cattle are understood. The herds of the mainland come into the poem with the cowherd Philoetius, who brings “a barren heifer for the suitors” across the strait with the ferrymen (20.185–188, a plain rendering).

**Oxhide.** When the stranger arrives, Eumaeus is sitting in the porch, “fitting sandals about his feet, / cutting a good-colored oxhide” (14.23–24; see [[sandals]] and the translation's row ἐϋχροής; ὑλακόμωροι; κεκλήγοντες), and when he runs out to the dogs “the leather fell from his hand” (14.34; see [[dogs]]). ἐϋχροής, “good-colored”, is found only here in Homer. Hesiod tells the farmer to bind about his feet in winter sandals of the hide of an ox killed by force, fitted and lined with felt (*Works and Days* 541–542).
