summary: Book 14 has no assembly. ἀγορεύω is the verb of speaking in the swineherd's hut, and the only gathering of the people the book names is the one in the false tale, whose harsh talk sent the Cretan and Idomeneus to Troy (14.237–239).
# ἀγορεύεις (14.116), "as you say"; ἀγορεύω (14.160, 14.398), "as I say", the beggar's oath and wager; ἀγόρευσον
# (14.186), "tell me this truly"; ἀγορεύσω (14.192), "I will tell you"; ἀγόρευον (14.409), "So they spoke such words to
# one another": speaking, not an assembly.
except: [14.116, 14.160, 14.186, 14.192, 14.398, 14.409]
---

Eumaeus lives apart among the swine and goes to the city only when Penelope sends for him because news has come (14.372–374), and the book's talk is the talk of two men in a hut. In the [[the-tale-to-eumaeus|Cretan tale]] the people are heard once, not in an assembly but as a pressure no man could resist: when Zeus devised the war, “they bade me and far-famed Idomeneus / lead the ships to Ilios; and there was no means / to refuse, for the harsh talk of the people held us” (14.237–239; see [[the-talk-of-the-people]] and [[idomeneus]]).
