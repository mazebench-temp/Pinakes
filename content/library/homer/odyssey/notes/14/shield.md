**Laid down.** In Egypt, when Zeus has cast panic among his men, the Cretan of the tale “took the well-made helmet from my head / and the shield from my shoulders, and threw the spear from my hand” and went to clasp the king's knees (14.276–279; see [[helmet]], [[spear]], [[supplication]] and [[the-raid-on-egypt]]).

**Shields for blankets.** At the ambush beneath Troy “an evil night came on, when the North Wind fell, / freezing; and from above came snow like hoarfrost, / cold, and ice grew thick on our shields. / Then all the others had cloaks and tunics, / and slept at ease, their shoulders wrapped in their shields” (14.475–479; see [[the-tale-of-the-cloak]], [[snow-like-hoarfrost]], [[cloak]] and [[boreas]]). The large oval “Mycenaean” shield was swung round to the back, and served as a shelter, “almost as an outer garment” (D. B. Monro, *Homer's Odyssey, Books XIII–XXIV*, 1901, on 14.479, after Reichel). The teller had followed “with only my shield and my shining kilt” (14.482; see [[kilt]]), and the shield was not enough.
