---
summary: Eumaeus prays at the sacrifice “that Odysseus of much wisdom should return to his own house” (14.423–424); and the stranger blames the wine, “which sets even a man of much wisdom to sing” (14.464).
---

Both of the uses that the general article describes come in this book. 14.424 is the verse of Athena's plea in the first council of the gods (1.83), and the poem says it of the swineherd's prayers again in Books 20 and 21 (20.238–239, 21.203–204; see [[prayer]] and [[eumaeus]]). 14.464 adapts Achilles' verse on anger, “which sets even a man of much wisdom to rage” (*Iliad* 18.108); D. B. Monro holds the Iliad's to be the original, since it “gives a better sense—since singing at a feast is hardly a sign of madness,” and notes “the otiose μάλα” (*Homer's Odyssey, Books XIII–XXIV*, 1901, Additional Notes and Corrections, p. 287, on 14.464; see [[the-mad-wine]] and [[sweeter-than-dripping-honey]]).
