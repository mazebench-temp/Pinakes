---
summary: The Cretan praises himself by denials, “since I was no worthless man / nor one who fled from war” (14.212–213), and confesses his nature by another, “but work was never dear to me” (14.222); and Eumaeus praises the tale of the cloak: “and you have not yet spoken any word amiss or profitless” (14.509).
refs: [14.212-213, 14.222, 14.509]
---

ἀποφώλιος, “worthless,” is the word of Poseidon's promise to Tyro, “for not in vain are the embraces / of the immortals” (11.249–250); here the denial claims the excellence for which the bastard son won a wife from people of many allotments (14.211–213; see [[arete]] and [[the-tale-to-eumaeus]]). The denial “work was never dear to me” means that he hated it, as the next verses show, since ships, wars and arrows were dear to him instead (14.222–228; see [[different-gifts]]). The swineherd's two negatives praise the tale by denying its faults, and they are earned: the tale has asked for a cloak without asking (14.508–511; see [[profitless]] and [[ainos]]).
