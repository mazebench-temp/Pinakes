---
summary: Eumaeus sprinkles white barley meal on the roast he sets before his guest (14.77), and at the evening sacrifice he sprinkles “the meal of barley” on the first offerings that he throws into the fire (14.429).
---

**On the roast.** The swineherd sets the porkers before his guest “hot, on the spits as they were; and he sprinkled white barley meal on it” (14.77; see [[spits]], [[white]] and [[eumaeus]]): ἄλφιτα λευκά, the white barley meal that Hecamede sprinkles over Nestor's drink, the women of the Shield for the reapers' dinner, and Odysseus over the offerings to the dead (*Iliad* 11.640, 18.560; 10.520, 11.28; see the entry).

**On the offerings.** At the evening sacrifice “the swineherd laid raw flesh, / taking the first offerings from all the limbs, in the rich fat, / and these he threw into the fire, sprinkling them with the meal of barley” (14.427–429; see [[sacrifice]] and [[fire]]): ἀλφίτου ἀκτή, “the meal of barley”, as in the Iliad's “meal of holy barley” (*Iliad* 11.631). The pieces of raw meat laid in the fat are the first offerings from each of the limbs, properly the gods' share of the sacrifice (D. B. Monro, *Homer's Odyssey, Books XIII–XXIV*, 1901, on 14.428).
