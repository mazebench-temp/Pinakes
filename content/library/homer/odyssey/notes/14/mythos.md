---
summary: The tellers of the book: vagrants who “do not wish to tell the truth” (14.125); the beggar who “will not speak idly, but with an oath” (14.151); the Aetolian who “beguiled me with a tale” (14.379); and in the tale of the cloak, Odysseus' low word in the night (14.492–494).
en: [tell the truth, speak idly, tale]
---

**Telling the truth.** Eumaeus distrusts all wanderers, “but vagrants wanting keep tell lies at random, / and do not wish to tell the truth” (οὐδʼ ἐθέλουσιν ἀληθέα μυθήσασθαι, 14.124–125; see [[vagrants-tell-lies]], [[pseudos]] and [[truth-and-deception]]), and the beggar answers, “yet I will not speak idly, but with an oath, / that Odysseus is coming home” (ἀλλʼ ἐγὼ οὐκ αὔτως μυθήσομαι, ἀλλὰ σὺν ὅρκῳ, 14.151–152; see [[oath]]).

**A tale.** Eumaeus' distrust has a history: “ever since an Aetolian man beguiled me with a tale” (ἐξήπαφε μύθῳ, 14.379; see [[apate]] and [[aetolians]]), the false report that Odysseus was mending his ships in Crete (14.382–385). In the tale of the cloak the μῦθος is Odysseus' word at Troy, spoken low: “and speaking in a low voice he said to me: / ‘Be silent now, so that no other man of the Achaeans hears you’” (14.492–493; see [[silence]]), and then to all, propped on his elbow, the dream that sent Thoas to the ships (14.494–495; see [[dream]] and [[the-tale-of-the-cloak]]).
