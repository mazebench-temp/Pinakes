---
summary: The Cretan of the beggar's tale counts arrows among the things that were dear to him, “grim things, which to others are things to shudder at” (14.224–226).
---

“but ships with oars were always dear to me, / and wars, and well-polished javelins, and arrows, / grim things, which to others are things to shudder at. / But to me those things were dear which, I suppose, a god put in my mind” (14.224–227; see [[spear]], [[ships]], [[different-gifts]] and [[the-tale-to-eumaeus]]). The arrows go with the javelins as the weapons of a raider who loved ships and wars, but not work or “the care of a household” (14.222–223; see [[piracy]]).
