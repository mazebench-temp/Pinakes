---
summary: For the evening sacrifice, “they brought in a very fat boar, five years old” (14.419).
---

πίονα πενταέτηρον ends the verse as for the ox that Agamemnon sacrificed to Zeus before the first battle (*Iliad* 2.402–403). Here it is the best of the swine, which Eumaeus kills “for the stranger / from a far land” (14.414–415), and the only pig to which Homer gives the epithet (see the general article, [[boar]] and [[sacrifice]]). Monro counts the line among the passages in which the Odyssey borrows from the Iliad: “five years old” is right for an ox, but not for a pig, “which at that age is too old for use” (D. B. Monro, *Homer's Odyssey, Books XIII–XXIV*, 1901, on 14.419, after Pierron, and Appendix II §2). The verses of the roasting are those of the Iliad (14.430–431 = *Iliad* 1.465–466).
