---
summary: Eumaeus on the suitors: “at their ease / they devour his possessions overweeningly, and there is no sparing” (14.91–92), and “they waste the wine, drawing it off overweeningly” (14.95).
---

The neuter is an adverb in both lines (D. B. Monro, *Homer's Odyssey, Books XIII–XXIV*, 1901, on 14.95), as in Menelaus' prayer (*Iliad* 17.19; see the general article). It is the Sun's word for the companions who killed his cattle (12.379; see the Book 12 note), and now the suitors eat the herds of Odysseus as the companions ate the cattle of the Sun (14.93–108; see [[the-suitors]] and [[possessions]]). The two verses come again, nearly, when Telemachus speaks to his father (16.314–315).
