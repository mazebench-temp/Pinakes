---
summary: In the tale of the cloak the ambush comes to Troy by night: “But when we came to the city and the steep wall” (14.472).
---

πτόλιν αἰπύ τε τεῖχος, Troy and its wall in Hector's rebuke of Paris, “The people are dying around the city and the steep wall” (*Iliad* 6.327), and in the verse that brings Agamemnon beneath the wall in his pursuit of the Trojans (*Iliad* 11.181); here the men of the ambush lie before it in the reeds and the marsh, crouching under their armor (14.473–475; see [[the-walls-of-troy]] and [[the-tale-of-the-cloak]]). D. B. Monro notes that περὶ ἄστυ is used, “with some vagueness,” of besiegers (*Homer's Odyssey, Books XIII–XXIV*, 1901, on 14.473).
