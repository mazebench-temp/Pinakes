---
summary: After the evening sacrifice the swineherd pours a libation and puts the cup “to Odysseus, sacker of cities,” into his hands (14.447–448).
---

The narrator's title for the man who sits as a beggar at the swineherd's supper; the dative Ὀδυσσῆϊ πτολιπόρθῳ is found only here in the Odyssey (see [[libation]] and [[feast-type-scene]]). The guest has just told the swineherd that he is “such as I am” (14.441), and the next speech he makes is the tale of a night at Troy (14.462–506; see [[the-tale-of-the-cloak]]).
