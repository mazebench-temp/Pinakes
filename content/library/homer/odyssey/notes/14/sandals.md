summary: When Odysseus reaches the farmstead, Eumaeus is sitting in the porch fitting sandals to his feet, cutting them from an oxhide (14.5, 14.23–24); when the dogs run at the stranger, the leather falls from his hand (14.34).
# σκῦτος, "the leather" (14.34, the only σκῦτος in Homer; the Iliad has σκυτοτόμος, "leather-worker", 7.221), first met
# here; it applies in the Odyssey only.
grc: [σκῦτος]
en: [leather]

“And he himself was fitting sandals about his feet, / cutting a good-colored oxhide” (14.23–24; see [[eumaeus]] and [[fold]]), while three of his men are out with the swine and the fourth has taken a hog to the suitors in the city (14.24–28). The verse of dressing binds fine sandals on the shining feet of kings and gods (see the entry and the Book 13 note); here a swineherd makes his own, sitting in his porch. When the dogs run at the beggar, Eumaeus “rushed out through the gateway, and the leather fell from his hand” (14.34; see [[door]] and [[dogs]]): σκῦτος is “the leather”, that is, the leather “which he was cutting into sandals” (D. B. Monro, *Homer's Odyssey, Books XIII–XXIV*, 1901, on 14.34; see the translation's row μετασπών; σκῦτος; λιθάδες).
