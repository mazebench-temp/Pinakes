---
summary: After a year in Phoenicia, the Phoenician of Odysseus' tale puts him on a ship “bound for Libya”, pretending that they will trade there and meaning to sell him (14.295–297); the ship never arrives, for Zeus wrecks it off Crete.
---

When the year in Phoenicia was over, the Phoenician “put me on board a seafaring ship bound for Libya, / having planned lies, so that I might take a cargo there with him, / but there, in truth, so that he might sell me and get an untold price” (295–297; see [[phoenicians]] and [[seafaring-ship]]). The Phoenician gives one reason and has another. ἐς Λιβύην … ἐέσσατο is a pregnant use, “put me on board (to take me) to Libya”, and ἵνα is local, “that I should be his partner in taking a cargo there”; the emphatic κεῖθι, “there”, and the change from ἵνα to ὡς mark the second purpose as the one not avowed but real (D. B. Monro, *Homer's Odyssey, Books XIII–XXIV*, 1901, on 14.295–297). The Cretan follows him on board “suspecting it, but under compulsion” (298). ἄσπετον ὦνον is “an untold price” (see the translation's key-word row).

The tale moves through the lands of Menelaus' wanderings, which ran through Cyprus and Phoenicia and the Egyptians to Libya, “where the lambs are horned at once” (4.83–85; see [[the-wanderings-of-menelaus]]): Egypt, Phoenicia, and the voyage toward Libya. The Cretan never comes there. The ship “ran before a fresh and fair North Wind, / on the mid-sea course past Crete; and Zeus devised destruction for them” (299–300; see [[crete]] and [[the-shipwreck-of-odysseus]]).

In the story of the oracles that Herodotus heard from the priests of Theban Zeus, Phoenicians carried off two priestesses from Egypt and sold one into Libya and the other into Greece, among the Thesprotians, as Herodotus thought, where she founded the oracle of Dodona (*Histories* 2.54.1, 2.56.1–3; see [[herodotus]], [[thesprotians]] and [[dodona]]). Phoenicians who sell people, Libya, the Thesprotians and the oracle of Dodona: these are the peoples and places of the second half of this tale.
