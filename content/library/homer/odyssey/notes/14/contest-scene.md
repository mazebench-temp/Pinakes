---
summary: In the tale of the cloak Thoas rises at Odysseus' call for a messenger in the verse of the contestants' rising: “So he spoke, and then Thoas, son of Andraemon, rose / quickly, and laid aside his crimson cloak” (14.499–500).
---

ὣς ἔφατʼ, ὦρτο δʼ ἔπειτα is the verse with which the competitors rise at Achilles' call in the games of Patroclus (*Iliad* 23.708, 23.811, 23.836, 23.859), and Homer has it nowhere else. Here the call is a wish, “but would that someone / would go and tell Agamemnon” (14.496–497), and the man who rises is named with his father, as the contestants are named when they rise (see the general article, step 3). His prize is the errand: he lays aside his cloak and runs to the ships, and the speaker lies in it till dawn (14.500–502; see [[thoas]], [[crimson]] and [[the-tale-of-the-cloak]]).
