---
summary: Eumaeus honors the beggar with the long chine, and the beggar answers: “Would that you, Eumaeus, might be as dear to father Zeus / as you are to me, since you honor me with good things, such as I am” (14.440–441).
---

The guest answers the portion of honor, the long chine of the boar (14.437–438; see [[geras]] and [[plato-chine-of-ajax]]), with a wish in the name of father Zeus, and Eumaeus replies that the god will give one thing and let another be (14.443–445; see [[zeus-gives-good-and-evil]]). The beggar says the first verse again in Book 15, with another reason: “as you are to me, since you have given me rest from wandering and dread misery” (15.341–342).
