heading: From the harbor to the swineherd
summary: The book begins where the thirteenth ended, on the same day: Odysseus goes up from the harbor through the wooded hills to the place where Athena had shown him the swineherd (14.1–4); in his false tale that afternoon he gives his landing on Ithaca another shape (14.344–359).
# Unnamed: the way up from the harbor (14.1-4).
refs: [14.1-4]
---

**The way up.** “But he went up from the harbor by the rugged path / through the wooded country, among the heights, to where Athena / had shown him the brilliant swineherd” (14.1–3; see [[the-harbor-of-phorcys]], [[rugged]] and [[eumaeus]]). The harbor is the harbor of Phorcys, where the Phaeacians set him down asleep at dawn, and the day is still that of his landing (see the Book 13 note and [[the-return-of-odysseus]]). διʼ ἄκριας is “‘through’ or ‘among the hill-tops’”, and πέφραδε, “had shown”, is “‘showed’ (the way to)”, as at 15.424 (D. B. Monro, *Homer's Odyssey, Books XIII–XXIV*, 1901, on 14.2, 14.3). Athena had told him where to go and what he would find: “You will find him sitting beside the swine”, by the Rock of Corax and the spring Arethusa (13.404–411; see [[the-rock-of-corax]] and [[arethusa]]). He finds the swineherd sitting in the porch of his farmstead, and the welcome follows (14.5–54; see [[visit-scene]] and [[odysseus-changed-into-a-beggar]]).

**The landing retold.** That afternoon, in the tale he tells the swineherd, Odysseus lands on Ithaca a second time. In the tale the [[thesprotians|Thesprotian]] sailors bring him in the evening to “the tilled fields of clear-seen Ithaca”, bound in the ship, and go ashore for supper; the gods undo his bonds, he swims ashore and hides in a thicket, and “the gods themselves hid me / easily, and they brought me and set me at the farmstead / of a man of understanding” (14.344–359; see [[the-tale-to-eumaeus]] and [[clear-seen]]). In truth he came at dawn, asleep, in a Phaeacian ship whose crew left him on the sand with his gifts (13.113–125); the goddess poured a mist around him “so that she might make him / unknown” (13.189–191), and she, not the gods together, sent him to the farmstead (13.404–411).
