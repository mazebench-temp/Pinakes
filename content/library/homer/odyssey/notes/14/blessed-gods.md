---
summary: Eumaeus on the suitors: “The blessed gods do not love cruel deeds, / but honor justice and the righteous deeds of men” (14.83–84).
---

The swineherd's maxim is spoken against the suitors, who eat the fat hogs “with no thought in their minds of the gods' regard, nor any pity” (14.82; see [[the-gods-honor-justice]]), and it opens his argument: even raiders who carry off spoil by Zeus' gift fear the gods' regard, but the suitors, who he thinks have heard from a god that his master is dead, waste his goods without fear (14.85–95; see [[hard]], [[hostile-men]] and [[the-suitors]]).
