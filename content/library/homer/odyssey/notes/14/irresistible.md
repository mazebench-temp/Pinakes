---
summary: In the wreck of the Cretan tale: “But for me, Zeus himself, though I had sorrows in my heart, / put the mast of vast length of the dark-prowed ship / into my hands” (14.310–312).
grc: [ἀμαιμάκετον]
en: [of vast length]
---

The third use of the word in Homer and the only one that is not of the Chimaera (see the general article). Monro takes ἀμαιμάκετον here as “of vast length,” probably formed by intensive reduplication from the root μακ-; the senses that come from a verb “to rage,” “invincible” or “raging,” hardly suit a mast, though such an epithet might be given to one “in a spirit of burlesque, such as we occasionally trace in the Odyssey” (D. B. Monro, *Homer's Odyssey, Books XIII–XXIV*, 1901, on 14.311). The translation follows him (see the translation's note on 14.311). In Book 12 the mast is only a mast, lashed to the keel (12.422–425; see [[mast]], [[dark-prowed]] and [[the-shipwreck-of-odysseus]]).
