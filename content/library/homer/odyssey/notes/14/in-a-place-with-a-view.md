---
summary: The swineherd's fold “had been built high, in a place with a view all round, / beautiful and great, with a clear space all around” (14.5–7).
---

The last of the four uses of the verse-end in the Odyssey, and the only one that is not of a house: after Telemachus' chamber and Circe's house, the fold that the swineherd built for his master's swine (14.5–10; see [[fold]] and [[eumaeus]]). περίδρομος, with which Merry explained περίσκεπτος (see the general article), D. B. Monro takes as “with an open space round it,” serving as the glacis of the fortress, to guard against surprise (*Homer's Odyssey, Books XIII–XXIV*, 1901, on 14.7). The guard is needed at once: the dogs rush at the stranger as he comes (14.29–30; see [[visit-scene]]).
