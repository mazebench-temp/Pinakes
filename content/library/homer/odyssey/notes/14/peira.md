summary: On the night of rain and wind Odysseus tells his story of the cloak at Troy “testing the swineherd”, to see whether he would give him his own cloak or bid one of his men do so (14.459–461).
# πειρητίζων (14.459; again 15.304, 16.313, 24.221), first met here among the Odyssey's notes.
grc: [πειρητίζων]
---

“And Odysseus spoke among them, testing the swineherd, / whether he would take off his cloak and give it to him, or urge one of his companions / to do so, since he cared for him greatly” (συβώτεω πειρητίζων, 14.459–461; see [[eumaeus]] and [[cloak]]). The test is of a host's care, and the master who sets it is the guest. Its form is a tale, the night at Troy when Odysseus found him a cloak (14.462–506; see [[the-tale-of-the-cloak]] and [[ainos]]), and Eumaeus passes it, giving the guest the spare cloak he keeps for storms (14.520–522). The same verse opens a second test the next evening, when Odysseus speaks of going to the town to beg (15.304), and he will make trial of Laertes in the same word (24.221).
