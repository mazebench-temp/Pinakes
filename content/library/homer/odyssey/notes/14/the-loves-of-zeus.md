---
summary: The portion Eumaeus sets aside for the nymphs and for “Hermes, son of Maia” (14.435) is the one place where Homer names Hermes' mother.
refs: [14.435]
---

When Eumaeus divides the meat of the evening sacrifice, “one he set aside for the nymphs and for Hermes, son of Maia, / with a prayer” (14.435–436; see [[sacrifice]], [[hermes]] and [[maia]]). Μαιάδος υἱεῖ is the only mention of Maia in Homer. Elsewhere Hermes is simply the son of Zeus: Apollo hails him “Hermes, son of Zeus” (8.335), and Zeus speaks to “his dear son” (*Iliad* 24.333). Hesiod names the mother in his list of the women who bore children to Zeus, Maia, daughter of Atlas, who “bore glorious Hermes to Zeus, / herald of the immortals” (*Theogony* 938–939), and the hymns call the god “son of Zeus and Maia” (*Homeric Hymns* 4.1, 18.10, 29.7; see [[hesiod]]).
