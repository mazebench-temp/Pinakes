---
summary: Eumaeus to the stranger: “And you too, old man of many sorrows, since a god has brought you to me, / do not try to please me with lies, nor bewitch me with them” (14.386–387).
---

πολυπενθές, the vocative, is new to the entry. The swineherd pities the man in the beggar's [[rags|rags]] and will not believe his news of Odysseus, since an Aetolian deceived him once (14.379–389; see [[thelgo]] and [[pseudos]]); the man he calls “of many sorrows” is the master whose return he has just denied.
