---
summary: The beggar's tale: “But for me, wretched man, Zeus the counselor devised evils” (14.243).
---

The first use of the epithet in the Odyssey. κακὰ μήδετο μητίετα Ζεύς is the half-verse of the night after the Achaeans built their wall, when “all night long Zeus the counselor devised evil” for the feasting armies (*Iliad* 7.476–478; see the general article). In the [[the-tale-to-eumaeus|tale]] Zeus devises every turn: the hateful journey to Troy (14.235), the evils after it (14.243), and the destruction of the Phoenician's ship (14.300; see [[far-seeing]], [[hateful]] and [[zeus]]).
