summary: Two meals at the hut: the stranger's, the two porkers roasted on the spits with barley meal and wine in an ivy-wood bowl (14.72–113), and the supper of the boar, “But now it is time for supper” (14.407–456).
refs: [14.72-79, 14.109-113, 14.407-408, 14.453-456]
---

The first meal is the guest's alone. The host serves it and sits facing him, “and he himself sat facing him,” in the half-verse of Achilles serving the envoys in his hut (14.79; *Iliad* 9.218), and its end is told in the verse of Hermes' meal at Calypso's table, “But when he had dined and satisfied his heart with food” (14.111 = 5.95), before the host gives him his own bowl full of wine (14.112–113). The supper has all the steps but song: the sacrifice and the carving into seven portions (14.414–438; see [[sacrifice-type-scene]]), the libation (14.446–448), the meal and its end in the verses of the first meal of the poem (14.453–454 = 1.149–150; see [[desire-for-drink-and-food]]), and sleep (14.455–456). In place of the singer the stranger speaks “a word with a wish,” for the wine “sets even a man of much wisdom to sing” (14.463–464; see [[the-mad-wine]] and [[feast]]).
