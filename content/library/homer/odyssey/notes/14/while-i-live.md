---
summary: Eumaeus of his master: “while he, perhaps yearning for food, / wanders over the land and the city of men of another tongue, / if he still lives somewhere and looks on the light of the sun” (14.42–44).
---

εἴ που ἔτι ζώει καὶ ὁρᾷ φάος ἠελίοιο, the half-verse with which Penelope asked the phantom of her sister whether her husband lived (4.833, with ἤ), is said here by the swineherd and in Book 20 by the cowherd Philoetius, each of his absent master (20.207). The sight of the sun is set beside the picture of a man in want of food among strangers (14.42–43; see [[nostos]] and [[eumaeus]]), and the man is sitting in front of him.
