summary: The gods honor “the righteous deeds of men” (14.84); the beggar ends his tale, “for it is still my lot to live” (14.359); and Eumaeus carves the boar, “for in his mind he knew well what was due” (14.433).
en: [righteous deeds, my lot, what was due]
---

αἴσιμα are the things that accord with one's αἶσα, the due share. Eumaeus sets them against the suitors' feasting: “The blessed gods do not love cruel deeds, / but honor justice and the righteous deeds of men” (δίκην τίουσι καὶ αἴσιμα ἔργʼ ἀνθρώπων, 14.83–84; see [[themis]] and [[the-gods-honor-justice]]); and the narrator gives the word to Eumaeus' carving of the boar for seven: “for in his mind he knew well what was due” (περὶ γὰρ φρεσὶν αἴσιμα ᾔδη, 14.433; see [[moira]] and [[sacrifice]]). The beggar ends the Cretan tale where he sits, at “the farmstead / of a man of understanding; for it is still my lot to live” (ἔτι γάρ νύ μοι αἶσα βιῶναι, 14.358–359; see [[the-tale-to-eumaeus]]): his αἶσα is the measure of his life, and it is not yet spent.
