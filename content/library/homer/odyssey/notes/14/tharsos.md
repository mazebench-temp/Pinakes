---
summary: The Cretan's boast: “Truly Ares and Athena gave me daring / and the strength that breaks men” (14.216–217).
---

“Truly Ares and Athena gave me daring / and the strength that breaks men; whenever I picked for an ambush / the best men, planting evils for the enemy, / never did my proud spirit foresee death” (ἦ μὲν δὴ θάρσος μοι Ἄρης τʼ ἔδοσαν καὶ Ἀθήνη / καὶ ῥηξηνορίην, 14.216–219; see [[ares]], [[athena]], [[breaker-of-men]] and [[the-brave-man-in-ambush]]). θάρσος is nearly always a god's gift in Homer (see the entry), and the Cretan claims it from two at once, the god of war and the goddess who is Odysseus' own patron. It shows in the ambush, the test of courage where the coward is known (*Iliad* 13.275–286; see [[ambush]]). The boast stands beside the [[rags|rags]]: “but now all that has left me; / but even so, looking at the stubble, I think you can / tell what it was” (14.213–215; see [[the-stubble]]).
