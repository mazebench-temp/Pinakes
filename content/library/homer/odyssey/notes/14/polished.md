summary: The Cretan's delights: “and wars, and well-polished javelins, and arrows” (14.225); and his escape from the Thesprotian ship: “I slid down the polished lading plank and brought to the sea / my chest” (14.350–351).
grc: [ἐΰξεστοι, ξεστὸν]
en: [well-polished]
---

ἐΰξεστος, “well-polished,” the compound of ξεστός with εὖ, said in the Iliad of the doors of Troy's gates, “long, well polished, bolted fast” (*Iliad* 18.276), here of javelins, among the things dear to a man who did not love work (14.224–228; see [[spear]] and [[different-gifts]]). ξεστὸν ἐφόλκαιον is the plank on which cargo is drawn aboard, which D. B. Monro explains as a “lading plank,” to take in cargo (*Homer's Odyssey, Books XIII–XXIV*, 1901, on 14.350; see the Odyssey translation's key words, and [[the-tale-to-eumaeus]]).
