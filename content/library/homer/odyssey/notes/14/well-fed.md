---
summary: The swineherd sends the suitors “always the best of all the well-fed fat hogs” (14.19), and each of the goatherds sends them “whichever of the well-fed goats seems the best” (14.106).
---

Two of the entry's four places, both of the best beasts of the master's herds going to the suitors' table every day (14.17–19, 14.105–106; see [[the-suitors]], [[boar]] and [[possessions]]). σίαλος, “fat hog,” is the word of the hog whose chine Achilles serves the envoys in his hut (*Iliad* 9.208; see the Odyssey translation's key words). At the end of the book the swineherd takes “the fleece of a large, well-fed goat” to sleep in, ἐϋτρεφέος, another word for the same thing (14.530; see [[dressing-scene]]).
