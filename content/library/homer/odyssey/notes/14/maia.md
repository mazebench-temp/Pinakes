summary: Maia is named once in Homer, in the swineherd's evening sacrifice: of the seven portions Eumaeus sets one aside, with a prayer, “for the nymphs and for Hermes, son of Maia” (14.435–436).
# The entry's Μαιάδος claims the line (14.435).
---

**Son of Maia.** “And cutting it, he divided it all into seven portions; / one he set aside for the nymphs and for Hermes, son of Maia, / with a prayer, and the others he gave to each” (14.434–436; see [[hermes]], [[nymphs]], [[sacrifice]] and [[eumaeus]]). The mother's name stands in the place of the patronymic, and it is the only time Homer gives Hermes his mother: Apollo greets him in Demodocus' song as “Hermes, son of Zeus” (8.335). The Hymns join the two names, Διὸς καὶ Μαιάδος υἱός, “son of Zeus and Maia” (*Hymn to Hermes* 1), and use Μαιάδος υἱός alone as a formula, as here (see the general article). D. B. Monro doubted that the two verses are genuine, because Homer's usual form of the god's name is Ἑρμείας and not Ἑρμῇ (*Homer's Odyssey, Books XIII–XXIV*, 1901, on 14.435; see the Book 14 note on [[hermes]]).
