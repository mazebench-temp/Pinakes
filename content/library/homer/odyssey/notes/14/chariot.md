summary: In the beggar's tale the king of Egypt, driving out with his army, spares the Cretan who clasps his knees and takes him home in his chariot (14.278–280).
# The phrase "ἐς δίφρον δέ" (14.280), first met here; it applies in the Odyssey only, since δίφρος in this poem is also a
# chair or stool (4.717, 17.330 and often).

“and I went to meet the horses of the king / and clasped his knees and kissed them; and he protected me and pitied me, / and seating me in his chariot he took me home as I shed tears” (14.278–280; see [[supplication]], [[horses]], [[egypt]] and [[the-raid-on-egypt]]). ἐρύσατο καί μʼ ἐλέησεν, “protected me and pitied me”, is a hysteron proteron of the common type (D. B. Monro, *Homer's Odyssey, Books XIII–XXIV*, 1901, on 14.279; see [[hysteron-proteron]]). The raider who came in his ships to plunder goes home in the king's chariot, a guest under the protection of Zeus, the god of guests (14.283–284; see [[god-of-guests]] and [[xenia]]).
