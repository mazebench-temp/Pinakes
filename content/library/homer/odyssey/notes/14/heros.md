---
summary: No one of the heroes, on the mainland or in Ithaca, had such wealth as Odysseus (14.96–97); and Pheidon, “the hero”, took care of the shipwrecked Cretan without price (14.317).
---

“For truly his livelihood was untold; no one of the heroes / had so much, neither on the dark mainland / nor in Ithaca itself; not even twenty men together / have so much wealth” (ἀνδρῶν ἡρώων, 14.96–99; see [[possessions]] and [[ithaca]]): the heroes are the noblemen of the islands and the mainland, the class from which the suitors come (see the entry); ἠπείροιο is a genitive of the space within which (D. B. Monro, *Homer's Odyssey, Books XIII–XXIV*, 1901, on 14.97). In the [[the-tale-to-eumaeus|Cretan tale]] the title goes to a king: “There the king of the Thesprotians, Pheidon, took care of me, / the hero, without price” (14.316–317; see [[pheidon]], [[thesprotians]] and [[basileus]]).
