---
summary: Eumaeus mixes wine for his guest in an ivy-wood bowl (14.78), and after the meal he fills and gives him his own bowl, the σκύφος from which he drinks himself (14.112).
---

**The ivy-wood bowl.** “and in an ivy-wood bowl he mixed honey-sweet wine” (14.78; see [[wine]] and [[eumaeus]]): the κισσύβιον in which Odysseus brought the Cyclops his wine (9.346; see the Book 9 note), and in which the swineherd will mix wine again, in the same verse, for Telemachus (16.52, a plain rendering). The Suda explains κισσύβιον as “properly a drinking cup of ivy wood” (κ 1682; see the translation's note on 9.346).

**His own bowl.** “then he filled the bowl from which he himself drank and gave it to him, / full of wine” (14.112–113; see [[xenia]]). The clause begins the main sentence, and the swineherd is not named, the act “being necessarily that of the host” (D. B. Monro, *Homer's Odyssey, Books XIII–XXIV*, 1901, on 14.112). σκύφος, “bowl”, is found only here in Homer (see the translation's row σκύφος): Aristarchus read σκύφον, Aristophanes the neuter σκύφος, as Athenaeus reports (Monro's critical note on 14.112).
