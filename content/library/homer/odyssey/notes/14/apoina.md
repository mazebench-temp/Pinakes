summary: Pheidon, king of the Thesprotians, took care of the shipwrecked Cretan “without price” (14.317), the word of the Iliad for Chryseis, to be given back to her father unbought.
en: [without price]
---

“There the king of the Thesprotians, Pheidon, took care of me, / the hero, without price; for his dear son came upon me / overcome by cold and weariness, and led me home” (ἥρως ἀπριάτην, 14.316–318; see [[pheidon]], [[thesprotians]] and [[xenia]]). ἀπριάτην, from πρίαμαι, “buy”, is the word of Calchas' demand that Chryseis be returned to her father “unbought, unransomed” (*Iliad* 1.99; see the entry): there no price is to be taken for a captive, here none is asked for a guest. The other dealers of the tale think only of price, the Phoenician who meant to sell the Cretan “and get an untold price” (14.297; see [[the-tale-to-eumaeus]] and [[trade]]) and the sailors who plotted his slavery (14.340; see [[doulios]]).
