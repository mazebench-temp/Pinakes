summary: In the tale of the cloak, Menelaus, son of Atreus, leads the ambush beneath the wall of Troy with Odysseus, and the stranger is third in command (14.469–471).
# Μενελά* (in the entry) claims Μενέλαος (14.470); Ἀτρεΐδης in the same verse is his (agamemnon's Book 14 note
# excepts the line).
---

“If only I were young like that, and my strength were steadfast, / as when we prepared an ambush beneath Troy and led it. / Odysseus and Menelaus, son of Atreus, were the leaders, / and with them I was third in command; for they themselves bade me” (14.468–471; see [[if-only-i-were-young]], [[the-tale-of-the-cloak]], [[ambush]] and [[odysseus]]). The tale puts Menelaus beside Odysseus, as the poems do elsewhere: the two went to Troy together as envoys before the war (*Iliad* 3.205–224), Odysseus held back Menelaus and Diomedes in the wooden horse when Helen called to them in the voices of their wives (4.271–289; see [[the-wooden-horse]]), and Demodocus sang how Odysseus went with Menelaus to the house of Deiphobus at the sack (8.517–520). Meanwhile the real Menelaus is entertaining Telemachus at Sparta (13.412–424; see [[telemachus]]).
