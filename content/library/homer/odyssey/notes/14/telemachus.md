**The son in the swineherd's talk.** Telemachus is at Sparta, where Athena has gone to call him home (13.412–415, 13.440; see [[sparta]] and [[the-journey-of-telemachus]]), but the swineherd speaks of him to his father. No wanderer's news can persuade “his wife and his dear son” (14.123), and the stranger swears that Odysseus will punish whoever “here dishonors his wife and his shining son” (14.164; see [[phaidimos]]). Eumaeus wishes the master home for the sake of all who long for him, “the old man Laertes, and godlike Telemachus” (14.173; see [[godlike]]), and then mourns the son:

{{quote:698cd743-fe84-5cc7-9c15-4fb22c729f3b}}

ἀγητόν, “admirable”, is masculine, in apposition to οὔ τι χέρεια, and τὸν δέ begins the apodosis to ἐπεί (D. B. Monro, *Homer's Odyssey, Books XIII–XXIV*, 1901, on 14.177, 14.178); δέμας is the reading of L, W and Eustathius, where the vulgate has φρένας (Monro's critical note on 14.177). μετὰ πατρὸς ἀκουήν, “in search of news of his father”, is like μετὰ σὸν κλέος, Athena's words for the same journey, “to learn the report of you” (13.415; Monro on 14.179). The journey to Pylos is the one Telemachus made in Books 2–4, and the ambush is the suitors' ship at Asteris, laid at the end of Book 4 (4.842–847; see [[ambush]] and [[telemachus-and-the-suitors]]). “But let us leave him alone, whether he is caught / or escapes, and the son of Cronus holds his hand over him” (14.183–184). The manuscript M marks 174–184 with the obelus, “perhaps because Eumaeus could not know of the ambush” (Monro's critical note on 14.174–184). See also [[like-a-sapling]] and [[arceisius]].

**The promise.** When the stranger has won a cloak for the night with his [[the-tale-of-the-cloak|tale]], Eumaeus tells him that he must wear his own [[rags|rags]] again at dawn: “But when the dear son of Odysseus comes, / he himself will give you a cloak and a tunic as clothing, / and will send you wherever your heart and spirit bid you” (14.515–517; see [[cloak]]). The three lines are missing in several manuscripts, and stand again where Eumaeus says them in the next book (15.337–339; Monro's critical note on 14.515–517; see the translation's note on 14.515–517). Telemachus will come to the hut and meet his father there in Book 16.
