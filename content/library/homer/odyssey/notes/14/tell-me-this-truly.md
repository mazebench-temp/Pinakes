---
summary: Eumaeus turns from his grief to his guest: “But come, old sir, tell me of your own sorrows, / and tell me this truly, so that I may know it well” (14.185–186).
---

The verse with which Telemachus questioned the goddess as Mentes (1.174), and Odysseus the goddess as a young herdsman the same morning (13.232), is asked here of Odysseus in disguise. In Book 1 it follows the questions about the stranger and his ship (1.170–174); here it comes first, after the request for his sorrows, κήδεʼ ἐνίσπες, and the questions follow (14.187–190; see [[who-are-you-among-men]]). The stranger takes up the sorrows: a whole year would not be enough to tell “the sorrows of my heart” (14.196–198; see [[a-time-for-many-words]]).
