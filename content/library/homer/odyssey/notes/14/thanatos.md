---
summary: The Cretan's father was carried off by “the fates of death” (14.207); the Cretan himself never foresaw death in an ambush (14.219), and in Egypt wished he had died (14.274).
---

“But the fates of death came and carried him away / to the house of Hades” (τὸν κῆρες ἔβαν θανάτοιο φέρουσαι, 14.207–208; see [[ker-doom]], [[hades]] and [[olbos]]): the death of [[castor-son-of-hylax|Castor, son of Hylax]], which ended his prosperity and the bastard's place in his house. The son's boast is of a warrior who did not think of death: “whenever I picked for an ambush / the best men, planting evils for the enemy, / never did my proud spirit foresee death” (14.217–219; see [[thymos]] and [[the-brave-man-in-ambush]]); and his wish in Egypt, beaten and surrounded, is for the death he escaped: “would that I had died and met my fate / there in Egypt” (14.274–275; see [[the-raid-on-egypt]] and [[moira]]).
