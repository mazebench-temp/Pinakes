---
summary: In the raid on Egypt: “For six days then my trusty companions / feasted” (14.249–250), and “Then indeed I bade my trusty companions / stay there beside the ships and guard the ships” (14.259–260).
---

Both are verses of Odysseus' tale to the Phaeacians: the companions who feasted for six days on the cattle of the Sun (12.397–398), and the order at the Cyclops' island to stay and guard the ship, there in the singular (9.193–194). In the false tale the six days' feast is the leader's gift before the voyage, and the order is broken: the men give way to arrogance and plunder the Egyptians' fields (14.249–251, 14.259–265; see [[hybris]], [[the-raid-on-egypt]] and [[retelling]]). 14.259–260 return when the raid is told to Antinous (17.428–429).
