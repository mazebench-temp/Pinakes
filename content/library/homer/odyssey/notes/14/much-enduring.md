---
summary: When Eumaeus will not believe that his master is alive, “Then much-enduring brilliant Odysseus spoke to him in turn” (14.148), and swears that Odysseus is coming home (14.149–164).
---

The formula introduces the beggar only here in the book; three other speeches begin with “Odysseus of many wiles” (14.191, 14.390, 14.439; see [[of-many-wiles]] and [[brilliant]]). He speaks of himself in the third person, and swears that Odysseus will come “in this very year” (14.161; see [[oath]]).
