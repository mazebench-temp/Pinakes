---
summary: “So he spoke, and split wood with the pitiless bronze” (14.418): the swineherd makes the fire for the evening sacrifice.
---

The formula of the blade that kills splits firewood here, as at 8.507 it was the bronze with which the Trojans thought “to split the hollow timber” of the horse; elsewhere in the Odyssey it slaughters the sheep at the pit of the dead (10.532 = 11.45) and cuts off noses and ears (18.86, 21.300, 22.475). The billet of oak with which Eumaeus strikes the boar is a piece he kept back from this splitting (14.425; see [[sacrifice-type-scene]] and [[bronze]]).
