summary: The Cretan of Odysseus' tale measures his father and himself by the Cretans: Castor son of Hylax “was honored then among the Cretans by the people like a god” (14.205), and his bastard son, after nine raids, “was feared and respected among the Cretans” (14.234), until the people sent him to Troy with Idomeneus. Eumaeus has heard an earlier lie, that Odysseus was seen “among the Cretans, with Idomeneus” (14.382).
# Unnamed: the Cretans who bade the speaker and Idomeneus lead the ships to Ilios (14.237-239).
refs: [14.237-239]
---

**Among the Cretans.** The father of the persona is “Castor, son of Hylax, of whose line I claim to be, / who was honored then among the Cretans by the people like a god / for his prosperity and wealth and his glorious sons” (204–206; see [[castor-son-of-hylax]] and [[the-tale-to-eumaeus]]). θεὸς ὣς τίετο δήμῳ is said in the Iliad of Agamemnon, of Aeneas among the Trojans, of Thoas, lord of the Aetolians, and of two priests (*Iliad* 10.33, 11.58, 13.218; 5.78, 16.605; see [[honored-like-a-god]]). The son, born of a bought mother, wins the same place by war: nine times before the war he led ships against foreign men, “and quickly my house grew, and then / I was feared and respected among the Cretans” (229–234; see [[raiding]] and [[concubines]]). δεινός τʼ αἰδοῖός τε, “feared and respected”, is what Athena made Odysseus himself among the Phaeacians (8.21–22).

**Sent to Troy.** “But when far-seeing Zeus devised that hateful journey, / which loosed the knees of many men, / then they bade me and far-famed Idomeneus / lead the ships to Ilios; and there was no means / to refuse, for the harsh talk of the people held us” (235–239; see [[idomeneus]] and [[the-talk-of-the-people]]). The subject of ἤνωγον, “they bade”, is the Cretans (D. B. Monro, *Homer's Odyssey, Books XIII–XXIV*, 1901, on 14.237). In the Iliad the Cretans come to Troy under Idomeneus and Meriones (*Iliad* 2.645–652); the [[the-tale-to-eumaeus|tale]] gives Idomeneus a fellow leader who is not in the Catalogue.

**The Aetolian's lie.** Eumaeus will not believe the stranger, because an Aetolian once deceived him: “He said he had seen him among the Cretans, with Idomeneus, / mending his ships, which the storm winds had shattered” (382–383; see [[aetolians]]). The lie sets Odysseus among the same Cretans and the same king as the stranger's own tale. In Book 19 Odysseus, again a Cretan, tells Penelope that he entertained Odysseus in Crete when the wind drove him there on his way to Troy, and that Odysseus asked for Idomeneus, his guest-friend (19.172–202).
