summary: The swineherd's herd: “who have endured misery / for long, suffering because of the white-tusked hogs” (14.415–416); the hairs of “the white-tusked boar” at the sacrifice and its chine for the guest (14.423, 14.438); and the last verses of the book, where the swineherd goes “to lie down where the white-tusked hogs / slept beneath a hollow rock” (14.532–533).
en: [white-tusked]
---

The standing epithet of swine runs through the book of the swineherd, from his complaint that others eat the herdsmen's labor (14.415–417) to his night beside the boars (see [[boar]] and [[eumaeus]]). ἀργιοδόντων is new to the entry. The Odyssey's formulas render ἀργιόδοντες ὕες “white-tusked hogs,” as the Iliad does (*Iliad* 23.32; see the Odyssey translation's formulas). The chine of the white-tusked boar was the portion of honor that Odysseus sent to Demodocus at the Phaeacians' feast (8.474–476); here it is his own (14.437–438; see [[plato-chine-of-ajax]] and [[sacrifice-type-scene]]).
