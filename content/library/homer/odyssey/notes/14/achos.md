# ἄχνυται (14.170) and ἄχνυνται (14.376), first met here among the Odyssey's notes.
grc: [ἄχνυται, ἄχνυνται]
en: [grieves]
---

Eumaeus' first speech sets his grief beside his work: “for, mourning and grieving for my godlike master, / I sit here, and rear fat hogs for others / to eat” (ὀδυρόμενος καὶ ἀχεύων, 14.40–42; see [[anax]] and [[the-suitors]]). He cannot bear to hear his master named: “do not remind me / of these; for truly the heart in my breast / grieves whenever anyone reminds me of my devoted master” (θυμὸς … ἄχνυται, 14.168–170; see [[thymos]] and [[stethos]]). And when a wanderer brings news, Penelope's household divides into two: “both those who grieve for their master, who has long been gone, / and those who rejoice as they eat his substance without payment” (14.376–377; see [[poine]]).
