summary: Athena is named twice and does not appear. The book opens with Odysseus going up to the swineherd “to where Athena / had shown him” (14.2–3), as she told him at the end of Book 13, and in the Cretan tale “Ares and Athena gave me daring / and the strength that breaks men” (14.216–217). She herself has gone to Lacedaemon for Telemachus.
# The entry claims Ἀθήνη (14.2, 14.216).
---

**Where Athena had shown him.** “But he went up from the harbor by the rugged path / through the wooded country, among the heights, to where Athena / had shown him the brilliant swineherd” (14.1–3; see [[eumaeus]] and [[rugged]]). πέφραδε is “showed” (the way to), as the Phoenician nurse in Eumaeus' story “pointed out the high-roofed house” of his father (15.424, a plain rendering; D. B. Monro, *Homer's Odyssey, Books XIII–XXIV*, 1901, on 14.3, compares the verse). The book carries out her instructions: “But you yourself go first of all to the swineherd, / who is the guardian over your swine, and is kind to you alike, / and loves your son and sensible Penelope. / You will find him sitting beside the swine” (13.404–407; see the Book 13 note). Odysseus comes in the likeness she gave him, an old man in [[rags|rags]], with the staff she gave him, which falls from his hand when the dogs rush at him (13.429–438, 14.29–31; see [[odysseus-changed-into-a-beggar]] and [[scepter]]). She herself is gone: “When the two had made their plans, they parted. She then / went to shining Lacedaemon, after the son of Odysseus” (13.439–440), and Book 15 begins with her arrival there (15.1–3).

**Daring.** In the [[the-tale-to-eumaeus|Cretan tale]] the stranger boasts of his courage: “Truly Ares and Athena gave me daring / and the strength that breaks men” (14.216–217; see [[ares]], [[tharsos]] and [[breaker-of-men]]). Daring is Athena's gift in the poems. She gave Diomedes “might and daring” (*Iliad* 5.1–2), in the Odyssey she put “might and daring” in Telemachus' heart and “daring” in his mind at Pylos (1.320–321, 3.76), and she “put daring” in Nausicaa's mind when the others fled from the naked stranger (6.139–140). On the shield of Achilles she leads out the sortie from the besieged city with Ares, “and Ares led them and Pallas Athena, / both of gold” (*Iliad* 18.516–517).
