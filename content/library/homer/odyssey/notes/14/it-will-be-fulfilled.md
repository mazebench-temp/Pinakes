---
summary: The stranger's oath ends in the language of fulfillment: “truly all these things will be fulfilled as I say” (14.160).
grc: ["τάδε πάντα τελείεται"]
en: [will be fulfilled]
---

τελείεται, the future of τελέω, sworn with ἦ μέν (see [[oath-formulas]] and [[let-zeus-be-witness]]), where Halitherses and Alcinous used the present of a prophecy coming true, “and now it is all being fulfilled” (2.176, 13.178). The swineherd answers with a denial: “nor will Odysseus ever come home again” (14.167). The verse returns in the oath to Penelope (19.305), who answers with the word of the formula, αἲ γὰρ τοῦτο, ξεῖνε, ἔπος τετελεσμένον εἴη, “Would that this word, stranger, might be fulfilled” (19.309, a plain rendering).
