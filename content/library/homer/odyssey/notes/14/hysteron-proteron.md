summary: The Cretan's half-brothers “were born and reared in his halls” (14.201), τράφεν ἠδʼ ἐγένοντο, “were reared and born” in the Greek order; and in D. B. Monro's reading two more: the king of Egypt “protected me and pitied me” (14.279), and Eumaeus “went outside and made himself ready” (14.526).
refs: [14.279, 14.526]
---

At 14.201 the half-verse of Nestor's contemporaries and of Penelope's (see the general article) is given in the natural order in the English, as at 12.134 (see [[nothos]] and [[the-tale-to-eumaeus]]); D. B. Monro would read τράφον for τράφεν (*Homer's Odyssey, Books XIII–XXIV*, 1901, on 14.201). He calls ἐρύσατο καί μʼ ἐλέησεν “a prothysteron of the common type,” comparing 13.274: the pity that moved the king is named after the protection it led to (Monro on 14.279; see [[supplication-type-scene]]). At 14.526 the going out properly comes after the furnishing, ὡπλίζετο, “but is put first as being the main action” (Monro on 14.526; see [[dressing-scene]]). In both the English keeps the Greek order.
