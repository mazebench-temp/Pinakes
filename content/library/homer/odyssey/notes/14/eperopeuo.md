---
summary: The beggar stakes his life on his news: if Odysseus does not come home, let Eumaeus throw him from a rock, “so that another beggar too may avoid deceiving” (14.400).
en: [deceiving]
---

The verb closes the compact that the beggar proposes when even his oath has not persuaded Eumaeus: “but if your master does not come as I say, / set your slaves on me and throw me down from a great rock, / so that another beggar too may avoid deceiving” (ὄφρα καὶ ἄλλος πτωχὸς ἀλεύεται ἠπεροπεύειν, 14.398–400; see [[rhetre]]). The penalty answers Eumaeus' charge that wanderers lie to Penelope for their keep (14.124–127; see [[pseudos]] and [[apate]]): the beggar offers to make an example of himself to all beggars. It is the deceiver of Book 13 who speaks, the man who feared that Athena was taunting him “to deceive my mind” (13.327; see the Book 13 note), and his news is true.
