---
summary: In the beggar's tale the flash of bronze fills the plain of Egypt and the Egyptians kill the raiders “with the sharp bronze” (14.268–271), and bronze is the first of Odysseus' treasures in the house of the Thesprotian king (14.324); at home Eumaeus splits firewood “with the pitiless bronze” (14.418).
---

**In the tale.** At dawn the Egyptians came, “and the whole plain was filled with foot soldiers and horses / and the flash of bronze” (14.267–268; again 17.436–437; see the translation's formula row and [[the-raid-on-egypt]]); “There they killed many of us with the sharp bronze” (14.271; see [[sharp]]). In the house of the king of the [[thesprotians|Thesprotians]] the beggar saw the possessions Odysseus had gathered, “bronze and gold and iron worked with much toil” (14.324; see [[possessions]], [[iron]] and [[gold-and-silver]]): the treasure that a captured man offers for his life in the Iliad (*Iliad* 6.48, 10.379, 11.133) and that lies in Odysseus' storeroom (21.10).

**The swineherd's axe.** “So he spoke, and split wood with the pitiless bronze” (14.418; see [[pitiless-bronze]], [[sacrifice]] and [[eumaeus]]): the bronze is the axe, as the word alone can name a blade (see the entry). With a billet of oak that he kept back from the splitting he strikes the boar (14.425; see [[trees]]).
