summary: Before he swears that Odysseus will come home, the disguised Odysseus says Achilles' line of the man he hates, now of the man “who, giving way to poverty, speaks deceits” (14.156–157); then he tells the swineherd a long false tale.
# "ὁμῶς Ἀΐδαο πύλῃσι" (14.156), without the ν of the entry's Iliad phrase (Il. 9.312).
grc: ["ὁμῶς Ἀΐδαο πύλῃσι"]
---

**The beggar's line.** Eumaeus has told the stranger that every wanderer who comes to Ithaca with news of Odysseus lies to get his keep, and that he too would fashion a tale for a cloak and a tunic (14.122–132; see [[vagrants-tell-lies]]). The stranger answers that he will speak with an oath, that he will take no reward before the master is home, and that he hates the very kind of man the swineherd means: “For hateful to me as the gates of Hades is that man / who, giving way to poverty, speaks deceits” (14.156–157; see [[oath]], [[euangelion]] and [[apate]]). In the Iliad the hated man hides one thing in his mind and says another (*Iliad* 9.312–313); here he is the poor man who lies for gain, and the speaker is a beggar who has been lying since he landed and is about to tell the long tale of a Cretan who never existed (14.192–359; see [[the-tale-to-eumaeus]] and [[truth-and-deception]]).

**A weak addition.** The Odyssey has the line with a verb. In *Iliad* 9.312 the verb is left unexpressed, to the advantage of the sense; in the Odyssey γίγνεται is a weak addition, and Monro counts the verse among the Iliad's lines borrowed or imitated in the Odyssey (D. B. Monro, *Homer's Odyssey, Books XIII–XXIV*, 1901, Appendix II §2).
