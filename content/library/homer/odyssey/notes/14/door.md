---
summary: When the dogs run at the beggar, the swineherd rushes out through the gateway of his farmstead, and the leather he was cutting falls from his hand (14.33–34).
---

“but the swineherd, following quickly on swift feet, / rushed out through the gateway, and the leather fell from his hand” (14.33–34; see [[eumaeus]], [[dogs]] and [[sandals]]). πρόθυρον is the “gateway”, that is, of the αὐλή, and σκῦτος “the leather” that Eumaeus was cutting into sandals (D. B. Monro, *Homer's Odyssey, Books XIII–XXIV*, 1901, on 14.34; see [[fold]]). Elsewhere the πρόθυρον is the gateway of a court, where a stranger stands to be received (see the entry and [[xenia]]); here the beggar has sat down outside it, and the staff has fallen from his hand in the same half-verse, ἔκπεσε χειρός (14.31; see [[scepter]]). μετασπών, rendered “following”, is “taking in hand” for Monro, from a root sep, and so a different word from ἕπομαι (on 14.33; see the translation's note on 14.33).
