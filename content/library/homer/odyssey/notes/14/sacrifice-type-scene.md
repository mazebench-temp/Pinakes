**The first meal.** “From there he chose two and brought them, and sacrificed them both, / and singed them and cut them up and threaded them on spits” (14.74–75): the men's portion of the scene without the god's, in a verse near Achilles' as he cuts up the meat for his guests in his hut, “And he cut them up well and threaded them on spits” (*Iliad* 9.210).

**The evening sacrifice** keeps the order of the scene (see the general article) and changes its matter.

1. **Preparation.** The best of the swine is brought in, “a very fat boar, five years old,” and set by the hearth, not at an altar (14.414–420); D. B. Monro calls the verse an imitation, or a parody, of Agamemnon's ox for Zeus (*Iliad* 2.402–403), and adds after Pierron that “The epithet ‘five years old’ is proper for beef, but not for pork” (*Homer's Odyssey, Books XIII–XXIV*, 1901, on 14.419; see [[five-years-old]] and [[hearth]]). There is no water for the hands and no barley grains.
2. **Prayer.** The swineherd begins with the first offering of hairs from the head, as Nestor did (3.446), and prays to all the gods for his master's return (14.422–424; see [[prayer]]); ἀπαρχόμενος is ἀποτέμνων ὡς ἄργμα, cutting off as a firstling, and so governs τρίχας, as in Agamemnon's oath over the boar (Monro on 14.422; *Iliad* 19.254).
3. **Killing.** He strikes with a billet of oak, “and its soul left it” (14.425–426), the half-verse of Sarpedon's swoon (*Iliad* 5.696); then they cut its throat and singe it, as a hog is singed, not flayed (14.426; see [[boar]]).
4. **The god's portion.** In place of the thigh-pieces wrapped in fat, the swineherd lays raw flesh “taking the first offerings from all the limbs, in the rich fat” and throws it into the fire sprinkled with barley meal (14.427–429), with ὠμοθετέω, the verb of the Iliad's verse (*Iliad* 1.461; see [[thigh-pieces]] and [[barley-meal]]). Monro: “This, properly speaking, represented the share of the gods in the sacrifice: but in the division among the guests one share was reserved for the host's especial patrons,” the nymphs and Hermes (Monro on 14.428). He reports that Aristarchus read πάντων in 14.428, the manuscripts πάντοθεν (his critical note).
5. **The men's portion.** The rest is cut up, spitted, roasted and drawn off in the two verses of Chryse (14.430–431 = *Iliad* 1.465–466; see [[spits]]); no entrails are tasted, and no wine is poured on the fire.
6. **The meal.** The swineherd carves and divides the meat into seven portions, one set aside for the nymphs and Hermes, and honors his guest with the chine (14.432–438; see [[feast]] and [[sacrifice]]). The firstling pieces for the gods and the libation come after his speech, before the guest is given the cup (14.446–448; see [[libation]] and [[feast-type-scene]]).
