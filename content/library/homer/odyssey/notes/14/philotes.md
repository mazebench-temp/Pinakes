---
summary: The beggar's story of the cloak ends with the wish that someone would give him a cloak “for both reasons, out of friendship and respect for a good man” (14.505).
---

“If only I were young like that now, and my strength were steadfast; / then someone in the farmstead of the swineherds would give me a cloak, / for both reasons, out of friendship and respect for a good man” (ἀμφότερον, φιλότητι καὶ αἰδοῖ φωτὸς ἑῆος, 14.503–505; see [[if-only-i-were-young]], [[aidos]], [[ainos]] and [[cloak]]): φιλότης, the bond of host and guest (see the entry), and the respect owed to a worthy man, together. Ancient critics rejected these last verses of the story; Monro agrees that they are weak and spoil its point, which is to hint at the need of a cloak without asking for one (D. B. Monro, *Homer's Odyssey, Books XIII–XXIV*, 1901, on 14.504–506).
