---
summary: Eumaeus on the suitors: “But these men know something—they have heard some voice of a god— / of that man's grim destruction” (14.89–90); and the beggar's “but ships with oars were always dear to me, / and wars, and well-polished javelins, and arrows, / grim things, which to others are things to shudder at” (14.224–226).
---

λυγρὸν ὄλεθρον, the Iliad's destruction (*Iliad* 2.873, 6.16; see the general article), is here the death of Odysseus, which the suitors seem to know of (see the translation's note on 14.89–90). λυγρά are the wars and weapons that the raider loved, as others love work and the care of a household (14.222–228; see [[different-gifts]] and [[at-whom-men-shudder]]).
