summary: The ships of the beggar's tales: the Cretan's nine raids and his nine ships for Egypt, brought to a halt in the river (14.229–260); the Phoenician's ship, struck by the thunderbolt (14.295–311); the Thesprotian ship that should have taken him to Dulichium, on which the sailors bound him (14.332–357); and at Troy the ships far from the ambush (14.496–501). Eumaeus asks the stranger on what ship he came (14.188).

**The host's questions.** “And on what sort of ship did you come? And how did sailors / bring you to Ithaca?” (14.188–189; see [[who-are-you-among-men]]), Eumaeus asks, in the words of Telemachus' questions to his guest in Book 1 (1.170–173). Even raiders, he has said, “fill their ships and set out to go home” with fear of the gods' regard on their minds (14.85–88; see [[the-gods-honor-justice]], [[piracy]] and [[the-suitors]]).

**The Cretan's ships.** “ships with oars were always dear to me” (14.224; see [[oars]]); “nine times I led men and swift-sailing ships / against foreign men” (14.230–231; see [[swift-sailing-ships]]), and he and Idomeneus were bidden to “lead the ships to Ilios” (14.237–238; see [[idomeneus]]). For Egypt “Nine ships I fitted out”, and before a fresh North Wind “not one of my / ships came to harm”; “in the river Aegyptus I brought my curving ships to a halt”, and bade his companions “stay there beside the ships and guard the ships” (14.248–260; see [[curving]], [[aegyptus-river]] and [[the-raid-on-egypt]]).

**The Phoenician's ship.** The Phoenician “put me on board a seafaring ship bound for Libya” (14.295; see [[seafaring-ship]] and [[phoenicians]]). Past Crete the son of Cronus set a dark cloud “above the hollow ship”, Zeus “hurled his thunderbolt at the ship”, “and all fell out of the ship”, “like sea crows around the black ship” (14.303–308; see [[thunderbolt]], [[hollow-ships]], [[black-ships]] and [[like-sea-crows]]), in the verses of the wreck of Book 12 (12.403–419; see [[the-shipwreck-of-odysseus]]). Only the mast saves him (14.311; see [[mast]]).

**The Thesprotian ship.** The king of the Thesprotians swore “that the ship had been drawn down and the companions were ready” to take Odysseus home (14.332–333; see [[thesprotians]]), but sent the beggar off first, since “a ship happened to be going / of Thesprotian men to Dulichium, rich in wheat” (14.334–335; see [[dulichium]]). Out at sea the crew plotted his slavery, “bound me fast in the well-benched ship” off Ithaca and went ashore to eat (14.339–347; see [[well-benched]] and [[doulios]]), and when the gods had undone his bonds, “I slid down the polished lading plank” into the sea (14.350). ἐφόλκαιον is probably a lading plank, to take in (ἐφέλκεσθαι) cargo; J. L. Myres, whom Monro quotes, had seen such planks on sailing boats and steamers in the Mediterranean, and one used as a gangway in shoal water (D. B. Monro, *Homer's Odyssey, Books XIII–XXIV*, 1901, on 14.350; see the translation's row ὅπλον ἐϋστρεφές; ἐφόλκαιον). The crew went back “on board the hollow ship” without him (14.357).

**Other ships.** An Aetolian vagrant had told Eumaeus that he saw Odysseus in Crete “mending his ships, which the storm winds had shattered” (14.382–383; see [[aetolians]]). In the tale of the cloak the men of the ambush “have come very far from the ships”, and Thoas runs to the ships with the message (14.496–501; see [[the-tale-of-the-cloak]] and [[thoas]]).
