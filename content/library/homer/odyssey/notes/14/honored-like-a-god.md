summary: The beggar's false father: “Castor, son of Hylax, of whose line I claim to be, / who was honored then among the Cretans by the people like a god / for his prosperity and wealth and his glorious sons” (14.204–206).
en: ["by the people like a god"]
---

θεὸς ὣς τίετο δήμῳ, the Iliad's verse-end of kings and priests, is used here for the last time in Homer. The verse is built like that of Aeneas, ὃς Τρωσὶ θεὸς ὣς τίετο δήμῳ, with the Cretans in place of the Trojans (*Iliad* 11.58; see the general article). The honor rests on wealth and sons, and when Castor dies both go to the lawful sons (14.207–210; see [[castor-son-of-hylax]], [[kydalimos]] and [[high-hearted]]).
