summary: Minds good and bad: the suitors, “with no thought in their minds of the gods' regard” (14.82); Telemachus, whose “well-balanced mind” a god or a man has harmed (14.178); the Phoenician who led the Cretan away “by his cunning” (14.290) and the sailors whose minds an evil counsel pleased (14.337); and Eumaeus, who did not forget the gods, “for his mind was good” (14.421), and carved, “for in his mind he knew well what was due” (14.433).
# φρονέοντες (14.82), first met here among the Odyssey's notes.
grc: [φρονέοντες]
en: [minds, cunning]
---

**The suitors.** Eumaeus gives his guest the porkers, “but the fat hogs the suitors eat, / with no thought in their minds of the gods' regard, nor any pity” (οὐκ ὄπιδα φρονέοντες ἐνὶ φρεσὶν οὐδʼ ἐλεητύν, 14.81–82; see [[the-gods-honor-justice]], [[themis]] and [[the-suitors]]), the same as the Iliad's θεῶν ὄπιν οὐκ ἀλέγοντες (D. B. Monro, *Homer's Odyssey, Books XIII–XXIV*, 1901, on 14.82), “caring nothing for the regard of the gods” (*Iliad* 16.388). Even raiders feel “a mighty fear of the gods' regard” falling on their minds (14.88; see [[deos]]).

**Telemachus.** Eumaeus fears for the young man: “then one of the immortals harmed the well-balanced mind within him, / or one of men; and he has gone in search of news of his father / to sacred Pylos” (βλάψε φρένας ἔνδον ἐΐσας, 14.178–180; see [[telemachus]] and [[the-journey-of-telemachus]]): the phrase of 11.337, said there of Odysseus himself. To Eumaeus the journey looks like madness.

**The tale.** The Cretan's love of war was what “a god put in my mind” (14.227; see [[the-tale-to-eumaeus]] and [[different-gifts]]), and in Egypt “Zeus himself put this thought in my mind” (14.273; see [[noos]]). The Phoenician “took me away, winning me over by his cunning” (ᾗσι φρεσίν, 14.290; see [[peitho]]), “by his wit” or “cunning” (Monro on 14.290); the [[thesprotians|Thesprotian]] crew, told to take him to [[acastus-king-of-dulichium|Acastus]] with care, had other plans: “but an evil counsel pleased their minds / concerning me” (τοῖσιν δὲ κακὴ φρεσὶν ἥνδανε βουλή, 14.337–338; see [[boule]]).

**Eumaeus' mind.** At the sacrifice “nor did the swineherd / forget the immortals; for his mind was good” (φρεσὶ γὰρ κέχρητʼ ἀγαθῇσιν, 14.420–421), and at the meal “the swineherd / stood up to carve; for in his mind he knew well what was due” (περὶ γὰρ φρεσὶν αἴσιμα ᾔδη, 14.432–433; see [[aisa]] and [[sacrifice]]).
