---
summary: The end of the evening meal in the hut, in the formula: “But when they had put away the desire for drink and food” (14.454).
---

“And they put out their hands to the good things lying ready before them. / But when they had put away the desire for drink and food, / Mesaulius took away their bread, and they hastened to bed” (πόσιος καὶ ἐδητύος ἐξ ἔρον ἕντο, 14.453–455; see [[desire-for-drink-and-food]] and [[mesaulius]]). ἔρος is appetite, “put away” when it is satisfied; the two verses are those of the suitors' meal in the first book (1.149–150), and the second ends every feast of the Iliad (*Iliad* 1.469).
