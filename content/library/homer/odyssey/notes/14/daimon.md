# δαιμόνιε (14.443), first met here among the Odyssey's notes.
grc: [δαιμόνιε]
en: [strange guest, a god]
---

**A god.** δαίμων names a power that is felt and not named (see the entry). Eumaeus ends his answer to the [[the-tale-to-eumaeus|Cretan tale]]: “And you too, old man of many sorrows, since a god has brought you to me, / do not try to please me with lies, nor bewitch me with them” (ἐπεί σέ μοι ἤγαγε δαίμων, 14.386–387; see [[thelgo]]). The beggar, in the tale of the cloak, blames the cold night on one: “for I have no cloak; a god beguiled me / into wearing only a tunic; and now there is no more escape” (παρά μʼ ἤπαφε δαίμων, 14.488–489; see [[apate]] and [[the-tale-of-the-cloak]]).

**Strange guest.** At the meal Odysseus has thanked his host for the chine, and Eumaeus answers, “Eat, strange guest, and take your delight in these things, / such as they are” (ἔσθιε, δαιμόνιε ξείνων, 14.443–444; see [[xenia]]). δαιμόνιε is said to someone whose words or acts are past understanding; here it is gentle, a host's word to a guest who praises a poor meal, built like ἆ δειλὲ ξείνων, “Ah, wretched stranger” (14.361).
