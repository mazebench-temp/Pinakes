# ἦρξα (14.230), ἡγείσθην (14.470) and the phrase νήεσσʼ ἡγήσασθαι (14.238), verbs of command first met here among the
# Odyssey's notes; ἄρχον (14.471), the verb, "I was third in command", is caught by the entry's ἀρχόν.
grc: [ἦρξα, ἡγείσθην, "νήεσσʼ ἡγήσασθαι"]
en: [led men, lead]
---

The verbs of the entry's nouns name the commands of the Cretan's false life. Before the war, “nine times I led men and swift-sailing ships / against foreign men, and very much fell to my lot” (εἰνάκις ἀνδράσιν ἦρξα, 14.230–231; see [[the-tale-to-eumaeus]], [[piracy]] and [[raiding]]). When the war came, “then they bade me and far-famed Idomeneus / lead the ships to Ilios; and there was no means / to refuse, for the harsh talk of the people held us” (νήεσσʼ ἡγήσασθαι, 14.237–239; see [[idomeneus]], [[cretans]] and [[the-talk-of-the-people]]), where those who bade them are the Cretans (D. B. Monro, *Homer's Odyssey, Books XIII–XXIV*, 1901, on 14.237). In the tale told to Athena the Cretan exile would not serve Idomeneus at Troy, but “led other companions of my own” (13.265–266; see [[the-first-cretan-tale]]); here he shares the command with Idomeneus. In the tale of the cloak the ambush at Troy has three commanders: “Odysseus and Menelaus, son of Atreus, were the leaders, / and with them I was third in command; for they themselves bade me” (ἡγείσθην … τρίτος ἄρχον ἐγών, 14.470–471; see [[ambush]], [[menelaus]] and [[the-tale-of-the-cloak]]).
