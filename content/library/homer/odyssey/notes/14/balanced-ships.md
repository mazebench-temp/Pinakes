---
summary: Eumaeus on Telemachus: “then one of the immortals harmed the well-balanced mind within him, / or one of men” (14.178–179).
---

φρένας ἔνδον ἐΐσας is the phrase of Arete's question about the stranger, and it keeps its English (11.337; see the Book 11 note and the Odyssey translation's key words). Eumaeus has just praised the boy whom the gods reared like a sapling, no worse than his father in form and looks (14.175–177; see [[like-a-sapling]]); the journey to Pylos seems to him a harm done to that mind, and the suitors now lie in ambush for him on his way home (14.179–182; see [[the-journey-of-telemachus]] and [[telemachus-and-the-suitors]]).
