---
summary: The swineherd's hut, the first κλισίη of the Odyssey that is not a hut of the camp at Troy. Eumaeus leads the beggar to it and seats him on brushwood under a goat's hide (14.45–51); inside it the beggar tells his tale (14.194), and there they make their supper (14.404–408).
---

**The welcome.** “But follow me, let us go to the hut, old man”, says Eumaeus when he has driven off the dogs (14.45; see [[eumaeus]] and [[dogs]]), and “the brilliant swineherd led the way to the hut, / and brought him in and seated him, and strewed thick brushwood beneath him, / and spread on it the hide of a shaggy wild goat, / his own bedding, great and thick” (14.48–51; see [[brilliant]], [[bed]] and [[xenia]]): αὐτοῦ ἐνεύναιον is “his own very bed-covering” (D. B. Monro, *Homer's Odyssey, Books XIII–XXIV*, 1901, on 14.51). The hut stands in his farmstead, by the fold he built himself (14.5–13; see [[fold]]). Until now the poem's huts have been those of the Achaean camp, remembered in Helen's tale and in Demodocus' song (4.255, 8.501; see the Book 4 and Book 8 notes); from here on the κλισίη is the swineherd's dwelling, where Telemachus will find the two of them at dawn, making ready their morning meal (16.1–2, a plain rendering), and where the seat of brushwood and fleece is made again for him (16.47).

**Inside the hut.** The beggar wishes that the two of them had food and sweet wine for a whole year, “as we stay inside the hut”, and still he could not finish telling his sorrows (14.193–198; see [[wine]] and [[the-tale-to-eumaeus]]). When he offers the wager, Eumaeus answers that he would have good fame indeed, “if, after I had brought you into my hut and given you good cheer, / I should then kill you” (14.404–405; see [[rhetre]], [[xenia]] and [[guest-gifts]]), and calls for his men to come in “so that we may make ready a pleasant supper in the hut” (14.407–408; see [[feast]] and [[sacrifice]]). The sacrifice, the supper and the night pass there, by the hearth and the fire, until the swineherd goes outside to sleep with the boars (14.418–533; see [[hearth]] and [[fire]]).
