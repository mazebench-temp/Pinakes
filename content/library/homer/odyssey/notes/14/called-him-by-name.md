---
summary: Odysseus thanks his host: “And Odysseus rejoiced / that he received him so, and he spoke to him, and called him by name” (14.51–52), and his words are a blessing, “May Zeus grant you, stranger, and the other immortal gods, / what you most desire” (14.53–54).
---

The half-verse without a gesture before it: the guest's joy at the welcome stands in the gesture's place (14.51–52). The name he calls is ξεῖνε, “stranger,” the word for guest and host alike, and the host answers with it, “Stranger, it is not lawful for me … to dishonor a stranger” (14.53, 14.56–57; see [[xenia]] and [[strangers-and-beggars-are-from-zeus]]). The blessing is the guest's first prayer for his host (see [[prayer]]).
