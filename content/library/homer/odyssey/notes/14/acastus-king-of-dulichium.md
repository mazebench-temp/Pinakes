“But he sent me off first; for a ship happened to be going / of Thesprotian men to Dulichium, rich in wheat. / There he bade them take me to King Acastus / with care; but an evil counsel pleased their minds / concerning me, so that I might yet come utterly to a bane of misery” (14.334–338; see [[pheidon]], [[thesprotians]], [[dulichium]] and [[rich-in-wheat]]). King Acastus is named only here. The crew strip the stranger of the cloak and tunic that Pheidon's son gave him, dress him in a [[rags|rag]], and bind him in the ship off Ithaca, and he escapes them on shore (14.339–359; see [[doulios]] and [[the-tale-to-eumaeus]]). Dulichium is still where he wishes to go: in the wager he offers Eumaeus, if his master comes home, he is to be clothed and sent “to go to Dulichium, where my heart was glad to go” (14.396–397; see [[rhetre]]).
