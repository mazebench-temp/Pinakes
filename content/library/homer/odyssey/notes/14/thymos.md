# εὔθυμος (14.63), only here in Homer.
grc: [εὔθυμος]
en: [kind-hearted]
---

**Food and wine.** The meals of the hut are told in the formulas of the heart's satisfaction: the suitors' boar is sent to them “so that they might sacrifice it and have their hearts' fill of meat” (κορεσαίατο θυμόν, 14.28); Eumaeus invites the stranger in “so that you too, / when you have had your heart's fill of bread and wine, / may tell me where you are from” (14.45–47; see [[visit-scene]]); “But when he had dined and satisfied his heart with food” (14.111, as 5.95), he takes the bowl, “and rejoiced in his heart” (14.113; see [[feast]]).

**Grief and doubt.** Eumaeus' heart is the seat of his grief and of his disbelief. He asks the stranger, “do not remind me / of these; for truly the heart in my breast / grieves whenever anyone reminds me of my devoted master” (14.168–170; see [[achos]] and [[stethos]]); “Ah, wretched stranger, truly you have stirred my heart greatly” (14.361). The beggar twice names the fault: “your heart is always unbelieving” (θυμὸς δέ τοι αἰὲν ἄπιστος, 14.150) and “Truly the heart in your breast is very unbelieving” (14.391; see [[peitho]]). The master he grieves for would have rewarded him as “a kind-hearted master gives to his servant” (ἄναξ εὔθυμος, 14.63; see [[anax]]), the adjective's only use in Homer.

**The Cretan's heart.** The false life is driven by the heart: “never did my proud spirit foresee death” in an ambush (θυμὸς ἀγήνωρ, 14.219); a month after Troy “my heart bade me sail to Egypt” (14.246; see [[the-raid-on-egypt]]); and Dulichium is the place “where my heart was glad to go” (14.397). At Troy Odysseus, nudged awake, “had this thought in his heart” (14.490; see [[noos]]).

**Gladdened.** Eumaeus honors the stranger with the chine, “and gladdened the heart of his master” (κύδαινε δὲ θυμὸν ἄνακτος, 14.438); the god gives “whatever he wishes in his heart” (14.445; see [[theos]]); and Telemachus, Eumaeus promises, will send the stranger “wherever your heart and spirit bid you” (κραδίη θυμός τε, 14.517; see [[kradie]]).
