summary: Eumaeus would have Odysseus come home, “as I wish it, and as Penelope does, / and the old man Laertes, and godlike Telemachus” (14.172–173).
---

Τηλέμαχος θεοειδής, a verse-end of the Odyssey from Book 1 on (1.113; see the Book 1 note). The verse names the household that waits for the master, his wife, his father and his son, and Eumaeus' next thought is for the son (14.174–184; see [[penelope]], [[laertes]] and [[telemachus]]).
