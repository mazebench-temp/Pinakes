---
summary: In the Cretan's tale Odysseus has gone to Dodona to hear from the oak of Zeus “how he should return to the rich land of Ithaca, / after being away so long, openly or in secret” (14.329–330).
---

The formula's first place for Ithaca, which it names again when Autolycus comes for the birth of his grandson (19.399). When the stranger tells Penelope the same story, the verse has “to his dear native land” in its place (19.298, a plain rendering; see [[dear-native-land]]), and D. B. Monro notes that there the metre requires νοστήσειε, which Murray prints here as νοστήσειʼ where the manuscripts have νοστήσῃ (*Homer's Odyssey, Books XIII–XXIV*, 1901, critical note on 14.329; see [[dodona]] and [[ithaca]]).
