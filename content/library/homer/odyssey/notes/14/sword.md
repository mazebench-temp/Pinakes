---
summary: Going out to guard the boars through the night, Eumaeus slings his sharp sword about his shoulders (14.528).
---

“First he slung his sharp sword about his massive shoulders, / and put on a cloak that kept off the wind, very thick” (14.528–529; see [[eumaeus]], [[cloak]] and [[spear]]): he arms against dogs and men, with a javelin in his hand (14.531). The words recall the verse of dressing, περὶ δὲ ξίφος ὀξὺ θέτʼ ὤμῳ, “and about his shoulder set his sharp sword” (2.3, 4.308, 20.125). For στιβαροῖς … ὤμοις, “massive shoulders”, one manuscript has the accusative, perhaps rightly, since the dative plural in -οις is probably not Homeric; or the true reading may be the singular, στιβαρῷ … ὤμῳ, as in that verse (D. B. Monro, *Homer's Odyssey, Books XIII–XXIV*, 1901, critical note on 14.528).
