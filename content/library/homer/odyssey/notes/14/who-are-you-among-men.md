---
summary: After the oath Eumaeus asks the host's questions: “Who are you among men, and from where? Where are your city and your parents? / And on what sort of ship did you come? And how did sailors / bring you to Ithaca? Who did they claim to be? / For I do not think that you came here on foot” (14.187–190).
---

The four verses of Telemachus' questions to the goddess in the likeness of Mentes (1.170–173), here asked of the stranger long after the meal, at the end of the talk about the master and the oath (14.45–47, 14.185–186; see [[visit-scene]] and [[tell-me-this-truly]]). D. B. Monro thinks 1.171–173 probably interpolated in Book 1, and cites a scholion which says that the verses are more fitting in the mouth of Eumaeus, and that this is why some copies lacked them there (Monro, Appendix I §10, in a note). εὐχετόωντο is “claim,” as in the stranger's answer, “I claim that my line is from wide Crete” (14.189, 14.199; see [[euchomai]]). The answer is the Cretan tale (14.192–359; see [[give-me-an-exact-account]] and [[the-tale-to-eumaeus]]). Telemachus asks the questions about the ship twice in Book 16, of the swineherd about his guest and of his father once he knows him (16.57–59, 16.222–224).
