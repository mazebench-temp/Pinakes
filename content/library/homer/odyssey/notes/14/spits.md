---
summary: Eumaeus threads the two porkers for his guest on spits and serves the meat hot on the spits (14.75–77); at the evening sacrifice the boar's meat is threaded on spits in the verses of the Iliad's sacrifices (14.430–431).
---

**The first meal.** The swineherd “singed them and cut them up and threaded them on spits. / And when he had roasted it all, he brought it and set it before Odysseus, / hot, on the spits as they were” (14.75–77; see [[eumaeus]], [[sties]] and [[barley-meal]]): αὐτοῖς ὀβελοῖσιν is “with the spits as they were”, without drawing them out of the meat (D. B. Monro, *Homer's Odyssey, Books XIII–XXIV*, 1901, on 14.77; see the translation's row αὐτοῖς ὀβελοῖσιν).

**The sacrifice.** At the evening sacrifice of the boar “they cut up the rest and threaded it on spits, / and roasted it carefully, and drew it all off” (14.430–431; see [[sacrifice]] and [[feast]]): the two verses of the sacrifice at Chryse (*Iliad* 1.465–466; again *Iliad* 2.428–429), the first of which the Odyssey has already used at Pylos and on Thrinacia (3.462, 12.365). This time the meat is not served on the spits: it goes on platters, and the swineherd stands up to carve it (14.432–434).
