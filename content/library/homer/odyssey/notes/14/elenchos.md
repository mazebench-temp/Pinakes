---
summary: Eumaeus to the stranger whom his dogs nearly tore to pieces: “and you would have poured disgrace on me” (14.38).
en: [disgrace]
---

“Old man, truly the dogs came near to tearing you to pieces / all of a sudden, and you would have poured disgrace on me” (καί κέν μοι ἐλεγχείην κατέχευας, 14.37–38; see [[dogs]] and [[xenia]]). A host whose dogs maul a stranger at his gate is shamed by it, and the disgrace is “poured” on him, as Antilochus tells his horses to hurry, “so that she does not pour disgrace on you two”, the mare Aethe (*Iliad* 23.408). The stranger who would have brought the disgrace is the master of the dogs (see [[the-dogs-fawning-on-their-master]]).
