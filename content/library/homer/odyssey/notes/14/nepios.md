---
summary: The Cretan's men in Egypt carried off “the women and the little children” (14.264).
---

In Egypt the Cretan's men would not obey him: “but they, giving way to arrogance, following their own might, / at once the beautiful fields of the Egyptian men / they laid waste, and carried off the women and the little children, / and killed the men” (ἐκ δὲ γυναῖκας ἄγον καὶ νήπια τέκνα, 14.262–265; see [[the-raid-on-egypt]], [[captive-women]] and [[piracy]]): the prey of raiders, as of the sack of a city, when “strangers lead away the children and the deep-girdled women” (*Iliad* 9.594). The verses return in the tale to Antinous (17.433).
