summary: Words in a book of tales: Eumaeus' suspicion that the stranger too “would fashion a tale” for a cloak (14.131); the beggar's “word with a wish”, which the wine brings out, “a word that is better left unsaid” (14.463–466); and Eumaeus' verdict, “you have not yet spoken any word amiss or profitless” (14.509).
en: [tale]
---

**A tale fashioned.** “Quickly you too, old sir, would fashion a tale, / if someone would give you a cloak and a tunic as clothing” (αἶψά κε καὶ σύ, γεραιέ, ἔπος παρατεκτήναιο, 14.131–132; see [[pseudos]] and [[cloak]]). παρατεκταίνομαι is a builder's word, to build otherwise, and so to alter or to make up; in the Iliad Nestor says that not even Zeus could have fashioned otherwise what has been done (*Iliad* 14.53–54).

**Words that wine brings out.** The beggar excuses the [[the-tale-of-the-cloak|tale of the cloak]]: “I will speak a word with a wish; for the wine bids me, / the mad wine, which sets even a man of much wisdom to sing / and to laugh softly, and stirs him up to dance, / and brings out a word that is better left unsaid” (εὐξάμενός τι ἔπος ἐρέω … καί τι ἔπος προέηκεν ὅ περ τʼ ἄρρητον ἄμεινον, 14.463–466; see [[the-mad-wine]] and [[euchomai]]).

**No word amiss.** Eumaeus answers the [[the-tale-of-the-cloak|tale]]: “Old man, the tale you have told is flawless, / and you have not yet spoken any word amiss or profitless” (οὐδέ τί πω παρὰ μοῖραν ἔπος νηκερδὲς ἔειπες, 14.508–509; see [[ainos]], [[moira]] and [[profitless]]). The first meeting had its formulas of address: Odysseus “spoke to him, and called him by name” (14.52; see [[called-him-by-name]]), and over the cup “addressing him with winged words” (14.114; see [[winged-words]]).
