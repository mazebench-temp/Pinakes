---
summary: Athena “had shown him the brilliant swineherd, who cared most for his substance / of all the servants that brilliant Odysseus had acquired” (14.3–4); “So saying, the brilliant swineherd led the way to the hut” (14.48); “Then much-enduring brilliant Odysseus spoke to him in turn” (14.148); and twice more “the brilliant swineherd” answers and calls to his men (14.401, 14.413).
---

δῖος ὑφορβός, “the brilliant swineherd,” closes the verse where δῖος Ὀδυσσεύς stands for his master, and the first lines of the book set the two side by side (14.3–4; see the Odyssey translation's key words, δῖος ὑφορβός). The formula is used eighteen times in the poem, all from this book on (14.3 to 22.162): the servant who keeps his master's substance has his master's epithet (see the general article, [[eumaeus]] and [[leader-of-men]]).
