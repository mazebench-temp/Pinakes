---
summary: Telemachus, who Eumaeus thought would be “admirable in form and looks” (14.177).
---

Eumaeus remembers his hopes for Telemachus: “and I thought that he would be among men in no way inferior / to his dear father, admirable in form and looks, / then one of the immortals harmed the well-balanced mind within him” (δέμας καὶ εἶδος ἀγητόν, 14.176–178; see [[telemachus]], [[pater]] and [[phrenes]]): form and looks are what make a youth his father's equal, and his mind is what the god or man has harmed. ἀγητόν is masculine, in apposition to “in no way inferior”, and δέμας is the reading of two manuscripts and Eustathius, where most have φρένας (D. B. Monro, *Homer's Odyssey, Books XIII–XXIV*, 1901, on 14.177 and critical note on 14.177).
