---
summary: The book's first verses: “But he went up from the harbor by the rugged path / through the wooded country, among the heights” (14.1–2).
---

The island is as the goddess described it that morning, “Truly it is rugged and not good for driving horses” (13.242), and the way to the swineherd's farmstead goes up from the harbor through woods and over the heights, διʼ ἄκριας, “through” or “among the hill-tops” (D. B. Monro, *Homer's Odyssey, Books XIII–XXIV*, 1901, on 14.2; see [[wooded]], [[the-harbor-of-phorcys]] and [[visit-scene]]). τρηχεῖαν ἀταρπόν is the only rugged path in Homer.
