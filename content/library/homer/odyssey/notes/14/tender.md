---
summary: The stranger blames the wine, which sets even a man of much wisdom to sing “and to laugh softly, and stirs him up to dance” (14.464–465).
grc: ["ἁπαλὸν γελάσαι"]
en: [laugh softly]
---

ἁπαλόν, the neuter as an adverb, of laughter, the one place where Homer gives the word of the body to a sound (see the general article, and the Odyssey translation's key words). It is the second of the wine's effects, after song and before the dance and the word better left unsaid (14.463–466; see [[the-mad-wine]] and [[anaphora]]).
