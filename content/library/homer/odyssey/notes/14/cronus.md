summary: Cronus is named only in his son's patronymic, three times. Eumaeus leaves Telemachus to his fortune, “whether he is caught / or escapes, and the son of Cronus holds his hand over him” (14.183–184); in the beggar's storm “the son of Cronus set a dark cloud / above the hollow ship” (14.303–304); and Eumaeus would “pray to Zeus, son of Cronus” after killing his guest (14.406).
# The entry's Κρονίων* claims Κρονίων (14.184, 14.303) and Κρονίωνα (14.406).
---

**The son of Cronus.** Eumaeus ends his grief for Telemachus, for whom the suitors lie in wait: “But let us leave him alone, whether he is caught / or escapes, and the son of Cronus holds his hand over him” (14.183–184; see [[zeus]], [[son-of-cronus]] and [[telemachus-and-the-suitors]]). ὑπέρσχῃ χεῖρα Κρονίων is the end of Agamemnon's taunt to the Argives who hang back, “to see if the son of Cronus will hold his hand over you?” (*Iliad* 4.249), and Achilles says that far-seeing Zeus “has held / his hand over” Ilios (*Iliad* 9.419–420): the hand held over a man or a city protects it. In the [[the-tale-to-eumaeus|beggar's tale]] the storm begins as it did in Book 12: “then the son of Cronus set a dark cloud / above the hollow ship” (14.303–304 = 12.405–406; see the Book 12 note). And Eumaeus refuses the wager with irony: “readily, after that, would I pray to Zeus, son of Cronus!” (14.406; see [[rhetre]] and the Book 14 note on [[zeus]]). Δία Κρονίωνα with a verb of entreaty is the phrase of Thetis, who “in supplication … spoke to lord Zeus, son of Cronus”, and of the Prayers, who “go to Zeus, son of Cronus, and pray” against the man who refuses them (*Iliad* 1.502, 9.511; see [[litai]]).
