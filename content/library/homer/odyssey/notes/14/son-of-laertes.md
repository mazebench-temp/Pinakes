---
summary: In the tale of the cloak the speaker wakes Odysseus at the ambush: “Son of Laertes, sprung from Zeus, Odysseus of many devices, / I shall no longer be among the living, but the cold / is bringing me down” (14.486–488).
---

The whole-line address that gods and the dead have spoken to Odysseus in the Odyssey (5.203; 10.401, 10.488, 10.504; 11.60, 11.92, 11.405, 11.473, 11.617; 13.375), and that the kings speak to him in the Iliad (*Iliad* 4.358, 8.93, 9.308, 9.624, 10.144, 23.723), here in the mouth of a Cretan, the third man of the ambush, as he tells it to Odysseus himself (see [[sprung-from-zeus]], [[of-many-devices]] and [[the-tale-of-the-cloak]]). The formality of the verse sits beside the complaint that follows: he has no cloak, and a god beguiled him into wearing only a tunic (14.487–489).
