---
summary: Two mothers: the mother of Eumaeus, in whose house he was born and reared (14.140–141), and the “bought mother” of the Cretan tale, a concubine (14.202–203).
---

Eumaeus speaks of “my father's and mother's / house, where I was first born, and they themselves reared me” (14.140–141; see [[pater]]); how he lost them is told in the next book (15.403–484; see [[eumaeus]]). In the [[the-tale-to-eumaeus|Cretan tale]] the teller's mother is a slave bought for a price: “but me a bought mother bore, / a concubine” (ἐμὲ δʼ ὠνητὴ τέκε μήτηρ / παλλακίς, 14.202–203; see [[concubines]] and [[nothos]]).
