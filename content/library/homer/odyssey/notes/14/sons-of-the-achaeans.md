---
summary: The Cretan's war: “For before the sons of the Achaeans set foot on Troy, / nine times I led men and swift-sailing ships / against foreign men” (14.229–231), and “There we sons of the Achaeans fought for nine years” (14.240).
---

The line-end formula of the army in a raider's account of himself, the second time in the first person, with the speaker one of them (see [[achaeans]] and [[the-tale-to-eumaeus]]). The nine years of fighting and the sack in the tenth are the count of Calchas' prophecy at Aulis, and the next verse begins as his does, τῷ δεκάτῳ δὲ πόλιν, “and in the tenth … the city” (14.240–241; *Iliad* 2.328–329).
