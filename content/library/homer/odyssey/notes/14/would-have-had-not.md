# καί νύ κεν at 14.325 (again 19.294) is a potential, "it would feed another man to the tenth generation".
except: [14.325]
---

The narrator's turn, with ἔνθα κεν in the first verse and ἀλλά in place of εἰ μή, as in Circe's story of the Argo (12.71–72); the danger is a beggar's, and the one who turns it back is a man (see [[dogs]], [[swift]] and [[visit-scene]]). The swineherd's first words take it up in his own voice: “Old man, truly the dogs came near to tearing you to pieces / all of a sudden, and you would have poured disgrace on me” (14.37–38; see [[elenchos]]). καί νύ κεν at 14.325 is not the turn but a potential, “It would feed another man to the tenth generation,” of the treasure that the king of the [[thesprotians|Thesprotians]] showed: “one owner and then another to ten generations,” as D. B. Monro explains it (*Homer's Odyssey, Books XIII–XXIV*, 1901, on 14.325; see [[possessions]]).
