**Twelve sties.** “and within the fold he had made twelve sties / close to one another, beds for the swine; and in each / fifty swine that sleep on the ground were penned, / breeding sows; but the boars lay outside, / far fewer; for these kept dwindling, eaten / by the godlike suitors” (14.13–18; see [[fold]], [[boar]] and [[the-suitors]]). The numbers and the words are those of Priam's palace, where fifty chambers of polished stone stood “close to one another” for his sons, and twelve for his daughters, πλησίον ἀλλήλων (*Iliad* 6.244–249; see [[priam]]). The lines are “almost a parody” of that description (D. B. Monro, *Homer's Odyssey, Books XIII–XXIV*, 1901, on 14.13–16), one of the places where a lofty epic formula is used of a subject unworthy of it, as the dogs' epithet ὑλακόμωροι, “barking”, parodies the heroic ἐγχεσίμωροι (Monro, Appendix II §2; 14.29; see [[dogs]]). The boars outside numbered three hundred and sixty (14.20), and the herds of the master that Eumaeus goes on to count are reckoned in the same way (14.100–108; see [[possessions]] and [[numbers-and-counting]]).

**Two porkers.** To feast his guest the swineherd “went to the sties, where the herds of porkers were penned. / From there he chose two and brought them, and sacrificed them both” (14.73–74; see [[spits]] and [[feast-type-scene]]). They are χοῖροι, the young pigs that slaves eat; the fattened hogs go to the suitors (14.80–81; see [[boar]]).

**At nightfall.** When the swineherds come home with the herd, “They penned the sows in their haunts to sleep, / and an untold clamor arose from the swine as they were stalled for the night” (14.411–412; see [[fold]]). ἔρξαν is the verb of shutting in, which Eurylochus uses of the Cyclops who “penned them in” (10.435; see the translation's note on 10.435 and the Book 10 note).
