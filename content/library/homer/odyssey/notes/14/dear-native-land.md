---
summary: Eumaeus no longer mourns so much for his parents, “eager though I am / to see them with my eyes, being in my native land” (14.142–143); and Pheidon told the beggar that he had entertained Odysseus “on his way to his native land” (14.322), and that the ship and companions were ready “who would take him to his dear native land” (14.333).
---

Eumaeus speaks of the house where he was born (14.140–141), and his native land is not Ithaca: in Book 15 he tells how he was carried off as a child from the island of [[syrie|Syrie]], and Laertes bought him (15.403–484; see [[eumaeus]] and [[the-kidnapping-of-eumaeus]]). What the beggar tells of Pheidon he tells again to Penelope, nearly in the same verses (19.287–299; see [[pheidon]] and [[thesprotians]]).
