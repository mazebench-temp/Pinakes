---
summary: In the tale of the cloak the ambush comes by night “to the city and the steep wall” of Troy and lies hidden in the reeds and the marsh around it (14.472–475).
---

“But when we came to the city and the steep wall, / As for us, around the city in the thick brushwood, / among the reeds and the marsh, crouching under our armor, / we lay” (472–475; see [[the-tale-of-the-cloak]] and [[ambush]]). πτόλιν αἰπύ τε τεῖχος, “the city and the steep wall”, is the Iliad's phrase for Troy under siege, where the people are dying “around the city and the steep wall” (*Iliad* 6.327; also 11.181). The men of the ambush lie close under the wall through a freezing night, when the North Wind had dropped and snow fell like hoarfrost and ice grew on their shields (475–477; see [[snow-like-hoarfrost]] and [[boreas]]). περὶ ἄστυ, “around the city”, is used with some vagueness of besiegers (D. B. Monro, *Homer's Odyssey, Books XIII–XXIV*, 1901, on 14.473; see [[troy]]).
