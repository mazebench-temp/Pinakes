summary: As the dogs rush at the stranger, “the swineherd, following quickly on swift feet, / rushed out through the gateway” (14.33–34).
grc: ["ποσὶ κραιπνοῖσι"]
---

ποσὶ κραιπνοῖσι, the swift feet of Paris and Achilles in the Iliad (*Iliad* 6.505, 22.138), and the verse-end of Hector going after his companions, ὦκα … ποσὶ κραιπνοῖσι μετασπών (*Iliad* 17.190), here of a herdsman running to save his guest (see [[would-have-had-not]] and [[dogs]]). D. B. Monro takes μετασπών as “taking in hand,” from a root distinct from ἕπομαι, “follow”; the English keeps “following” (*Homer's Odyssey, Books XIII–XXIV*, 1901, on 14.33; see the translation's note on 14.33).
