summary: Odysseus, set on by the dogs, “sat down, by craft, and the staff fell from his hand” (14.31); and the Thesprotian sailors gave up the search for him, since “it seemed to them no better / to search further” (14.355–356).
en: [by craft]
---

“Suddenly the barking dogs caught sight of Odysseus. / And they ran at him, baying; but Odysseus / sat down, by craft, and the staff fell from his hand” (ἕζετο κερδοσύνῃ, 14.29–31; see [[dogs]] and [[scepter]]). κερδοσύνη is the craft of a man who knows what to do, the word with which Helen told how Odysseus evaded her questions when he entered Troy in disguise, “but by craft he evaded me” (4.251; see [[odysseus-enters-troy-as-a-beggar]]). In the Cretan tale the sailors who meant to sell him search for him in the woods, “but since it seemed to them no better / to search further, they went back again / on board the hollow ship” (κέρδιον, 14.355–357; see [[the-tale-to-eumaeus]]).
