---
summary: The false tale is told in true verses. The wreck of Odysseus' last ship, told to the Phaeacians, becomes the wreck of the Phoenician's (14.301–309, from 12.403–419); the drift of nine days “and on the tenth black night” is his drift to Calypso's island (14.314 = 7.253); the Thesprotian prince clothes him in the verse in which Circe clothed him (14.320 = 10.542); and “all that I have toiled through by the will of the gods” is what he told Alcinous (14.198 = 7.214).
refs: [14.198, 14.301-309, 14.314, 14.320]
---

The verses are kept and their places changed. In the tale to the Phaeacians Zeus strikes the ship for the killing of the cattle of the Sun, and Odysseus alone escapes on keel and mast (12.403–425); here he strikes a Phoenician ship on which the speaker was being taken to be sold, and where “my companions fell out of the ship,” now “all fell out of the ship” (12.417, 14.307); Zeus himself puts the mast into his hands (14.310–313; see [[the-shipwreck-of-odysseus]], [[like-sea-crows]] and [[mast]]). In later books the tale is told again, in part: the raid on Egypt to Antinous in the same verses, 14.258–272 at 17.427–441, with another ending, and the news of Odysseus in [[thesprotians|Thesprotia]] to Penelope, 14.321–335 at 19.287–299 (see [[the-raid-on-egypt]] and [[the-tale-to-eumaeus]]). Eumaeus' verses on the tomb that the Achaeans would have made for his master are Telemachus' (14.369–371 = 1.239–241); D. B. Monro thinks that 14.369–370, which most good manuscripts lack here, come from Book 1, so that 14.371 is the only true repetition, “probably an epic commonplace” (*Homer's Odyssey, Books XIII–XXIV*, 1901, Additional Notes and Corrections, p. 286, on 14.368–371; see [[fame-and-the-tomb]]).
