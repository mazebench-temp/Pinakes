---
summary: In the beggar's tale, when Zeus has struck the ship with his thunderbolt, he puts “the mast of vast length” into the Cretan's hands, and the man is borne on it for nine days to the land of the Thesprotians (14.310–315).
---

“But for me, Zeus himself, though I had sorrows in my heart, / put the mast of vast length of the dark-prowed ship / into my hands, so that I might still escape bane. / Clinging to it I was borne along by the deadly winds” (14.310–313; see [[the-shipwreck-of-odysseus]], [[zeus]], [[dark-prowed]] and [[thesprotians]]). The storm and the stroke of the thunderbolt are told in the verses of the wreck of Book 12 (14.301–309; 12.403–419; see [[thunderbolt]]), where Odysseus lashed the mast to the keel and sat on them (12.422–425; see the Book 12 note). ἀμαιμάκετον is “of vast length”, probably formed by intensive reduplication from the root μακ-; taken from μαιμάσσω, “rage”, it would be “invincible” or “furious”, senses that hardly suit a mast, though “Such an epithet might however be applied to a mast in a spirit of burlesque, such as we occasionally trace in the Odyssey” (D. B. Monro, *Homer's Odyssey, Books XIII–XXIV*, 1901, on 14.311; see the translation's note on 14.311 and [[irresistible]]).
