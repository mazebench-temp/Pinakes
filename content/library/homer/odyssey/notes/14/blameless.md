---
summary: The beggar's oath: “Now let Zeus be witness first of the gods, and the table of hospitality, / and the hearth of noble Odysseus, to which I have come” (14.158–159); and Eumaeus' answer to the tale of the cloak: “Old man, the tale you have told is flawless” (14.508).
---

Of persons ἀμύμων stays “noble,” and of a thing well made or well done it is “flawless,” as when Alcinous says that the Phaeacians are not flawless boxers (8.246; see the Odyssey translation's key words). The oath returns three times with the same hearth of noble Odysseus (17.155–156, 19.303–304, 20.230–231; see [[let-zeus-be-witness]], [[oath]], [[hearth]] and [[table]]). Eumaeus' αἶνος is the tale itself, not praise (see the translation's note on 14.508, [[ainos]] and [[the-tale-of-the-cloak]]); he calls it flawless because he has understood what it asks, and gives the beggar a cloak for the night (14.509–522; see [[cloak]]).
