---
summary: The Thesprotian sailors bring the beggar to Ithaca as a slave: “and in the evening they came to the tilled fields of clear-seen Ithaca” (14.344).
---

Ἰθάκης εὐδειέλου ἔργα, “the tilled fields of clear-seen Ithaca.” On waking in his own land Odysseus could not believe that he had come to clear-seen Ithaca (13.212, 13.325; see the Book 13 note); now, on the island, he tells of a man brought there against his will, who slipped his bonds and came to the swineherd's farmstead (14.334–359; see [[ithaca]], [[thesprotians]] and [[the-tale-to-eumaeus]]).
