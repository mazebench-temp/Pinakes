---
summary: Castor, son of Hylax, the false father of the Cretan tale, was honored among the Cretans like a god “for his prosperity and wealth and his glorious sons” (14.206), until death carried him off.
---

“Castor, son of Hylax, of whose line I claim to be, / who was honored then among the Cretans by the people like a god / for his prosperity and wealth and his glorious sons. / But the fates of death came and carried him away / to the house of Hades” (ὄλβῳ τε πλούτῳ τε καὶ υἱάσι κυδαλίμοισιν, 14.204–208; see [[castor-son-of-hylax]], [[time]] and [[thanatos]]). Prosperity, wealth, sons and the honor of the people make up the ὄλβος of the entry, complete, and death ends it at a stroke: the sons divide the livelihood by lot and leave the bastard very little (14.208–210; see [[nothos]] and [[lots]]).
