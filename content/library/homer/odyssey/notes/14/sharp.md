summary: In the raid on Egypt, “There they killed many of us with the sharp bronze” (14.271); and Eumaeus goes out for the night armed: “First he slung his sharp sword about his massive shoulders,” and “took a sharp javelin, a defense against dogs and men” (14.528, 14.531).
---

ὀξέϊ χαλκῷ, the Iliad's formula of the killing blow, in the lie as in the first Cretan tale (13.271), and again when the raid is told to Antinous (17.440; see [[the-raid-on-egypt]]). ξίφος ὀξύ and ὀξὺν ἄκοντα arm the herdsman for his night beside the boars, the sword in its verse of the dressing and arming scenes and the javelin against “dogs and men” (see [[dressing-scene]], [[sword]] and [[spear]]).
