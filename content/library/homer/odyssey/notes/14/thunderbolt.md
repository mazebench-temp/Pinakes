---
summary: In the beggar's tale Zeus thunders and hurls his thunderbolt at the Phoenician's ship as it leaves Crete behind, and the ship is filled with sulfur (14.305–307), in the verses of the wreck of Book 12.
---

“And at the same moment Zeus thundered and hurled his thunderbolt at the ship; / and she shook all over, struck by the thunderbolt of Zeus, / and was filled with sulfur; and all fell out of the ship” (14.305–307; see [[zeus]], [[ships]] and [[the-tale-to-eumaeus]]). The verses are those of the wreck that followed the eating of the Sun's cattle (12.415–417; see [[the-shipwreck-of-odysseus]] and the Book 12 note), with “all”, ἅπαντες, for “my companions”, ἑταῖροι: the true story lends its words to the false one, and in it Zeus destroys the crew of the Phoenician who meant to sell the teller (14.295–300; see [[phoenicians]]). The stroke comes when the ship has left Crete and “no other / land appeared, but only sky and sea” (14.301–302).
