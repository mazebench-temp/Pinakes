summary: Three things beyond telling: the master's livelihood, “For truly his livelihood was untold” (14.96); the price the Phoenician hoped to get for the Cretan, “an untold price” (14.297); and the noise of the swine penned for the night, “an untold clamor” (14.412).
grc: [ἄσπετος]
---

ἄσπετος, the nominative, is new to the Odyssey's forms of the entry (the Iliad's are in its notes). The livelihood is counted at once, herd by herd (14.96–108; see [[possessions]] and [[anaphora]]). κλαγγὴ δʼ ἄσπετος ὦρτο has the words of the uproar that rises among the Trojans when they find the men of Rhesus killed in the night, κλαγγή τε καὶ ἄσπετος ὦρτο κυδοιμός, “a clamor and an untold tumult” (*Iliad* 10.523); here it is the noise of the swine as they are penned for the night (14.411–412; see [[boar]] and [[the-doloneia]]).
