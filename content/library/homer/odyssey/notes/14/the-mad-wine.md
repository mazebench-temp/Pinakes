---
summary: On a cold, wet night without a moon, after the supper, the stranger tests whether the swineherd will give him a cloak, and begins with an apology for the wine (14.457–467); the tale of the cloak follows, and Eumaeus answers that the tale is flawless (14.468–517).
---

**The setting.** The meat has been shared and the men are full, when “an evil night came on, a night without a moon, and Zeus rained / all night long, and a great West Wind blew, always wet” (14.457–458; see [[zephyrus]]). σκοτομήνιος, “without a moon”, is a night in the dark part of the month; it was then four days to the new moon (D. B. Monro, *Homer's Odyssey, Books XIII–XXIV*, 1901, on 14.457). The stranger speaks “testing the swineherd, / whether he would take off his cloak and give it to him, or urge one of his companions / to do so, since he cared for him greatly” (14.459–461; see [[cloak]] and [[eumaeus]]). He addresses them all, “Hear me now, Eumaeus, and all you other companions” (14.462), and lays what he is about to say on the wine (14.463–467).

**What the wine brings out.** The word better left unsaid is a story of his youth, an ambush beneath Troy on a night of snow, when he had left his cloak behind and Odysseus got him one by a trick (14.468–502; see [[the-tale-of-the-cloak]], [[snow-like-hoarfrost]] and [[ambush]]). Its point is that it hints at the need of a cloak without directly asking for one, and the three lines that state the need more plainly (14.504–506) were rejected by ancient critics; they are weak in themselves and spoil the story (Monro on 14.504–506). The swineherd takes the hint: “Old man, the tale you have told is flawless, / and you have not yet spoken any word amiss or profitless; / so you shall not lack clothing” (14.508–510; see [[ainos]] and [[profitless]]). The wine has said nothing the speaker did not mean to say.
