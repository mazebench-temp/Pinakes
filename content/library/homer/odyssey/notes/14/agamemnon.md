**For Agamemnon's honor.** Eumaeus ends his account of the master who would have rewarded him with the cause of his death: “for he too went, to win satisfaction for Agamemnon, / to Ilios of the fine horses, to fight the Trojans” (14.70–71; see [[odysseus]], [[eumaeus]] and [[troy]]). Ἀγαμέμνονος εἵνεκα τιμῆς is the cause that Achilles threw in Agamemnon's face in the first quarrel, “to win satisfaction for Menelaus—and for you, dog-face” (*Iliad* 1.158–159; see [[time]]). The stranger takes it up in his question: “And you say that he perished to win satisfaction for Agamemnon” (14.117). The two verses are the only ones in Homer with the phrase.

**In the tale of the cloak.** At the ambush beneath Troy, Odysseus tells the men of a dream and asks for someone to go to the ships: “but would that someone / would go and tell Agamemnon, son of Atreus, shepherd of the people, / whether he might urge more men to come from the ships” (14.496–498; see [[the-tale-of-the-cloak]], [[shepherd-of-the-people]] and [[son-of-atreus]]). The commander's name is the excuse that sends Thoas running and leaves his cloak behind (14.499–502; see [[thoas]]).
