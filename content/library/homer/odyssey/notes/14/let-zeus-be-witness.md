---
summary: The beggar offers his oath that Odysseus will come home: “Now let Zeus be witness first of the gods, and the table of hospitality, / and the hearth of noble Odysseus, to which I have come: / truly all these things will be fulfilled as I say” (14.158–160).
---

The first oath of the Odyssey in the verse, sworn by the man whose return it promises (see [[odysseus]]). The table is the swineherd's, where the stranger has just eaten (14.72–113), but the hearth is not his: D. B. Monro notes that in Homer the hearth is a sacred object but not yet a goddess, like the later Hestia, that ἱστίη occurs only in this form of oath, the ordinary word being ἐσχάρη, and that the house of Eumaeus was not “the hearth of Ulysses,” so that the words apply only to his own palace (*Homer's Odyssey, Books XIII–XXIV*, 1901, on 14.159; 17.156, 19.304; see [[hearth]] and [[table]]).

The five verses 158–162 recur in the oath to Penelope (19.303–307), where, Monro notes, the last of them, “as the old month wanes and the new begins,” fixes the day: the one called at Athens ἔνη καὶ νέα, which was the day after their talk. He thinks it more probable that 161–162, “or perhaps, as Kirchhoff held, the seven lines 158–164 that contain the oath,” belong to Book 19 and were brought in here wrongly (Monro on 14.158–162; see the translation's note on 14.158–164, and [[it-will-be-fulfilled]]). Eumaeus does not take the oath: “But let us leave the oath alone” (14.171), and the stranger turns it into a wager with the gods as witnesses (14.391–400; see [[oath-formulas]] and [[rhetre]]).
