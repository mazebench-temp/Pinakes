---
summary: Unable to persuade Eumaeus even with an oath, the beggar proposes “a compact” with the gods of Olympus as witnesses: clothing and passage to Dulichium if the master comes home, death from a great rock if he does not (14.391–400); Eumaeus declines it (14.402–406).
---

**The compact.** “Truly the heart in your breast is very unbelieving, / seeing that not even with an oath have I won you over, nor do I persuade you. / But come now, let us make a compact; and hereafter / let the gods who hold Olympus be witnesses for us both” (ῥήτρην ποιησόμεθʼ … μάρτυροι ἀμφοτέροισι θεοί, 14.391–394; see [[oath]], [[peitho]] and [[olympus]]). The terms bind both sides: “If your master comes home to this house, / clothe me in a cloak and a tunic as clothing, and send me / to go to Dulichium, where my heart was glad to go; / but if your master does not come as I say, / set your slaves on me and throw me down from a great rock, / so that another beggar too may avoid deceiving” (14.395–400; see [[dulichium]], [[cloak]], [[doulios]] and [[eperopeuo]]). The first condition is the reward for good news that Eumaeus had refused (14.152–154, 14.166; see [[euangelion]]); the second makes the teller's life the pledge of his [[the-tale-to-eumaeus|tale]], and turns Eumaeus' charge against vagrants who lie for their keep into a penalty for lying (14.124–127; see [[vagrants-tell-lies]] and [[pseudos]]).

**Declined.** Eumaeus answers with irony: “Stranger, so indeed would I have good fame and prosperity / among men, both now and hereafter, / if, after I had brought you into my hut and given you good cheer, / I should then kill you and take away your dear life; / readily, after that, would I pray to Zeus, son of Cronus!” (14.402–406; see [[xenia]], [[kleos]] and [[arete]]). Then he turns to supper (14.407–408). The compact is never made, but its terms come true: Odysseus comes home within the year the beggar swore to (14.161), and Telemachus promises the stranger in this very hut “a cloak and a tunic, fine clothes” (16.79, a plain rendering).
