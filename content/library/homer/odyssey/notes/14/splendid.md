---
summary: The Cretan's confession: “but work was never dear to me, / nor the care of a household, which rears splendid children” (14.222–223).
---

ἀγλαὰ τέκνα, the children that Poseidon promised Tyro and that Chloris bore to Neleus (11.249, 11.285), here the fruit of the household life that the raider did not love; ships, wars, javelins and arrows were dear to him instead (14.224–228; see [[different-gifts]] and [[the-tale-to-eumaeus]]).
