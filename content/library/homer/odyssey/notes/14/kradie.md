---
summary: Telemachus, Eumaeus promises, will send the stranger “wherever your heart and spirit bid you” (14.517).
---

“But when the dear son of Odysseus comes, / he himself will give you a cloak and a tunic as clothing, / and will send you wherever your heart and spirit bid you” (πέμψει δʼ ὅππῃ σε κραδίη θυμός τε κελεύει, 14.515–517; see [[thymos]] and [[telemachus]]). The three verses are missing in many manuscripts, and Eumaeus says them again the next evening, when the stranger speaks of going to the city to beg (15.337–339; D. B. Monro, *Homer's Odyssey, Books XIII–XXIV*, 1901, critical note on 14.515–517; see the translation's note on 14.515–517).
