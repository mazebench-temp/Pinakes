summary: After the meal is shared out, “He spoke, and made an offering of the firstling pieces to the everlasting gods, / and when he had poured a libation of sparkling wine, to Odysseus, sacker of cities, / he put it into his hands” (14.446–448).
grc: ["θεοῖς αἰειγενέτῃσι"]
---

θεοῖς αἰειγενέτῃσι closes the verse as in Hector's hope of setting up the mixing bowl of freedom for the gods (*Iliad* 6.527), and as at the libation on Telemachus' ship, where the form has ν (2.432; see the Book 2 note). The firstling pieces, ἄργματα, are the first pieces cut from the limbs (14.427–428; D. B. Monro, *Homer's Odyssey, Books XIII–XXIV*, 1901, on 14.446; see [[sacrifice]] and [[libation]]).
