# δειλῷ is "wretched" here; in Aristotle the same form is "the coward" (deos), so it stays in the note.
grc: ["=δειλῷ"]
---

ἆ δειλὲ ξείνων, the address of pity, with the genitive of the class, “wretched one among strangers,” as Eumaeus later calls the beggar δαιμόνιε ξείνων, “strange guest” (14.443; see [[strange-one]]); Antinous will use the same words in scorn when the beggar asks to try the bow, “Ah, wretched stranger, there are no wits in you” (21.288, a plain rendering). The two uses are the two ends of the address (see the general article). ἐμοὶ δειλῷ is the Cretan's pity for himself, in the verse that turns his tale from the war to his misfortunes (14.243–245; see [[counselor]] and [[the-tale-to-eumaeus]]).
