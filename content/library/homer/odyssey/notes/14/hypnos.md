summary: The night in the hut: the sows penned “to sleep” (14.411); Odysseus asleep by the fire under his host's spare cloak, and the young men beside him (14.523–524); and Eumaeus, who “did not like to sleep there, away from the boars” (14.525), and went out to lie down where the hogs “slept beneath a hollow rock” (14.532–533). In the tale of the cloak the others “slept at ease” in the snow (14.479).
# κοιμήσατο (14.523), first met here among the Odyssey's notes.
grc: [κοιμήσατο]
---

The book ends with sleep. The herdsmen come home with the swine, and “They penned the sows in their haunts to sleep” (14.411; see [[sties]]). After the tale of the cloak, Eumaeus makes the stranger a bed by the fire and covers him with his own spare cloak (14.518–522; see [[cloak]]): “So Odysseus slept there, and beside him / the young men slept; but the swineherd / did not like to sleep there, away from the boars, / but he went outside and made himself ready; and Odysseus rejoiced / that he cared so much for his substance while he was far away” (14.523–527; see [[eumaeus]]). Armed against dogs and men, he “went to lie down where the white-tusked hogs / slept beneath a hollow rock, sheltered from the North Wind” (14.532–533; see [[white-tusked]] and [[boreas]]); κείων, “to lie down”, is a future participle, always construed, as the Homeric rule requires, with a verb of motion (D. B. Monro, *Homer's Odyssey, Books XIII–XXIV*, 1901, on 14.532). In the tale the narrator's companions at Troy “had cloaks and tunics, / and slept at ease, their shoulders wrapped in their shields” (14.478–479; see [[the-tale-of-the-cloak]]), while he lay awake with cold.
