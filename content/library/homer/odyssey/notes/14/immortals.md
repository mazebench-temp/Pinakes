summary: The beggar's “May Zeus grant you, stranger, and the other immortal gods, / what you most desire” (14.53–54) and “Zeus knows, I suppose, and the other immortal gods” (14.119); Eumaeus' “then one of the immortals harmed the well-balanced mind within him” (14.178); and at the evening sacrifice, “nor did the swineherd / forget the immortals; for his mind was good” (14.420–421).
---

The beggar twice sets Zeus and the other immortals at the end of one verse, Ζεύς … καὶ ἀθάνατοι θεοὶ ἄλλοι, in his first words in the hut and in his first question (14.53, 14.119; see [[zeus]]). The swineherd remembers the immortals before he kills the boar, with the first offering of hairs from its head and a prayer for his master's return (14.420–424; see [[sacrifice]] and [[prayer]]).
