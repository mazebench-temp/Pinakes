---
summary: Two ships of the Cretan tale: the Phoenician “put me on board a seafaring ship bound for Libya” (14.295), and the Thesprotian crew turned on him “when the seafaring ship had sailed far from the land” (14.339).
---

Both ships are of men who mean to sell him, and the epithet of the crossing names the place where he is helpless: the open sea (see [[phoenicians]], [[thesprotians]] and [[the-tale-to-eumaeus]]). D. B. Monro takes ἐς Λιβύην ἐέσσατο as “a pregnant use,” “put me on board (to take me) to Libya” (*Homer's Odyssey, Books XIII–XXIV*, 1901, on 14.295; see [[libya]]). ποντοπόρος νηῦς ends the verse as it did for the Phaeacian ship that brought Odysseus home (13.95, 13.161).
