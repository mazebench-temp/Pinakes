---
summary: Eumaeus at the end of the day's talk: “But now it is time for supper; may my companions be inside / very soon, so that we may make ready a pleasant supper in the hut” (14.407–408).
---

λαρὸν τετυκοίμεθα δόρπον is the half-verse of Eurylochus on Thrinacia (12.283; see the Book 12 note). There the supper was the companions' first step toward the cattle of the Sun; here it is the boar that Eumaeus sacrifices for the stranger (14.414–456; see [[sacrifice]] and [[hut]]).
