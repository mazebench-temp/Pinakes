# Ἑλένης (in the entry) claims Ἑλένης (14.68).
---

Telling the stranger of the master who would have rewarded him had he grown old at home, Eumaeus breaks off: “but he has perished—would that the race of Helen had perished utterly, / brought to its knees, since she loosed the knees of many men; / for he too went, to win satisfaction for Agamemnon, / to Ilios of the fine horses, to fight the Trojans” (14.68–71; see [[eumaeus]], [[agamemnon]] and [[troy]]). πρόχνυ is literally “on the knees”, used of utter downfall, “but here with a play on the literal sense: ‘may the race of Helen fall and be brought to its knees, even as she has loosed the knees of many men’” (D. B. Monro, *Homer's Odyssey, Books XIII–XXIV*, 1901, on 14.69; see [[gounata]]). φῦλον, “the race”, is her kind or family (see [[phylon]]). The same half-verse, “which loosed the knees of many men”, is said a little later of the war itself, the “hateful journey” that Zeus devised for the Achaeans (14.235–236). The curse is the swineherd's; the Odyssey's Helen is at home in Sparta, entertaining Telemachus (Book 4; 15.1–181).

**Outside Homer.** The chorus of Aeschylus' *Agamemnon* laments her in like words: ἰὼ ⟨ἰὼ⟩ παράνους Ἑλένα, / μία τὰς πολλάς, τὰς πάνυ πολλὰς / ψυχὰς ὀλέσασʼ ὑπὸ Τροίᾳ, “ah, mad Helen, who alone destroyed the many, the very many lives under Troy” (*Agamemnon* 1455–1457; see [[aeschylus]]).
