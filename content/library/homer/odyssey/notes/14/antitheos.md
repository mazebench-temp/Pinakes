summary: The suitors are “godlike” as they eat the swineherd's boars (14.17–18); Eumaeus mourns “my godlike master” (14.40) and fears for “the race / of godlike Arceisius” (14.181–182); and the companions of the tales are twice godlike at the verse-end, the raider's “with my godlike companions” (14.247) and the Aetolian's “with his godlike companions” (14.385).
grc: [ἀντίθεοι, ἀντιθέου]
---

ἀντίθεοι and ἀντιθέου are new to the entry. Only here are the suitors themselves ἀντίθεοι, in the narrator's verse that tells how they eat the boars, “since the swineherd sent them / always the best of all the well-fed fat hogs” (14.18–19; see [[the-suitors]] and [[well-fed]]). Eumaeus gives the word to his master in his first speech (14.40), and to Odysseus' grandfather when he fears that the suitors' ambush will end the line (14.180–182; see [[arceisius]] and [[telemachus-and-the-suitors]]). σὺν ἀντιθέοις ἑτάροισιν, the verse-end of Menelaus' companions (4.571; see the Book 4 note), names the men whom the beggar led to Egypt (14.247; see [[the-raid-on-egypt]]) and the companions with whom, the Aetolian said, Odysseus would come home (14.385; see [[aetolians]]).
