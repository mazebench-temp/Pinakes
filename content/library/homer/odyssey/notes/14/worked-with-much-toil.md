---
summary: The king of the Thesprotians shows the Cretan the treasure Odysseus had gathered, “bronze and gold and iron worked with much toil” (14.323–324).
---

πολύκμητον, the accusative, is new to the entry. The verse of the Iliad's captives' ransom (see the general article) here counts the wealth of a man said to be on his way home, “It would feed another man to the tenth generation” (14.325; see [[possessions]] and [[pheidon]]). When the stranger tells Penelope the same story the verse is left out, and the possessions go straight to the tenth generation (19.293–294).
