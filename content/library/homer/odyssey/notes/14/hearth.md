---
summary: The stranger swears by “the hearth of noble Odysseus, to which I have come” (14.159), though he is in the swineherd's hut; at the evening sacrifice the boar is set by the hearth (14.420).
---

**The hearth of the oath.** “Now let Zeus be witness first of the gods, and the table of hospitality, / and the hearth of noble Odysseus, to which I have come: / truly all these things will be fulfilled as I say” (14.158–160; see [[let-zeus-be-witness]], [[oath]], [[table]], [[zeus]] and [[xenia]]). ἱστίη, in later Greek ἑστία, is in Homer “a sacred object, but is not a goddess, like the later Ἑστία”; it occurs only in this form of oath, and the ordinary word for the hearth is ἐσχάρη (D. B. Monro, *Homer's Odyssey, Books XIII–XXIV*, 1901, on 14.159). “The house of Eumaeus was not the ‘hearth of Ulysses’”: the words apply only to his own palace, where the stranger will swear the same oath to Penelope (19.303–304) and the seer Theoclymenus swears it too (17.155–156; Monro on 14.159). Monro holds that the lines 161–162, or perhaps, as Kirchhoff held, all seven lines of the oath, 158–164, belong to Book 19 and have been brought in here wrongly (on 14.158–164; see the translation's note on 14.158–164). Eumaeus declines it: “But let us leave the oath alone” (14.171).

**The hearth of the sacrifice.** For the evening sacrifice his men bring in a fat five-year-old boar, and “They set it by the hearth” (14.419–420; see [[boar]], [[five-years-old]] and [[sacrifice]]); the swineherd throws hairs from its head into the fire as the first offering (14.422; see [[fire]]). At bedtime he sets a bed for his guest “near the fire” (14.518; see [[bed]]).
