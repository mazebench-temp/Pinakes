---
summary: Ships “with oars” were always dear to the Cretan of the beggar's tale (14.224); escaping from the Thesprotian ship, he rows through the sea with his hands (14.351).
---

“but ships with oars were always dear to me, / and wars, and well-polished javelins, and arrows” (14.224–225; see [[ships]], [[spear]], [[arrows]] and [[different-gifts]]). νῆες ἐπήρετμοι, “ships with oars”, is elsewhere the phrase of Odysseus' plight on Calypso's island, where “he has no ships with oars and no companions” to take him home (4.559, 5.16, 5.141; later 17.145; see [[calypso]]); the raider of the [[the-tale-to-eumaeus|tale]] had always had them. When the gods have undone his bonds on the [[thesprotians|Thesprotian]] ship, he slips into the sea, “and then I rowed through it with both my hands, / swimming” (14.351–352; see [[rags]]): διερέσσω, the oarsman's verb, is used of the hands, as when Odysseus, sitting on the mast and keel after Charybdis, “rowed with my hands” (12.444).
