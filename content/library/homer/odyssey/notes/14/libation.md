# σπείσας (14.447; again 18.151), first met here in the Odyssey; ἀποσπένδων (14.331) is in the Book 3 note.
grc: [σπείσας]
en: [pouring libations, poured a libation]
---

**With an oath.** The Cretan says that he heard of Odysseus at the court of the [[thesprotians|Thesprotian]] king: “And he swore to me myself, pouring libations in his house, / that the ship had been drawn down and the companions were ready / who would take him to his dear native land” (14.331–333; see [[pheidon]] and [[oath]]). ἀποσπένδω, “pour out”, is the verb of Nestor's libation to Athena at Pylos (3.394), and the drink offering makes the oath solemn, as the wine poured from the mixing bowl seals the oaths before the duel in *Iliad* 3 (*Iliad* 3.295–296; see [[like-this-wine]]). The king's word is part of the [[the-tale-to-eumaeus|lie]], and it will come back with the same verses in the tale to Penelope (19.288–290).

**Before the cup.** In the hut the libation comes at the end of the sacrifice: Eumaeus “made an offering of the firstling pieces to the everlasting gods, / and when he had poured a libation of sparkling wine, to Odysseus, sacker of cities, / he put it into his hands” (14.446–448; see [[sacrifice]] and [[wine]]). The host pours first and then gives the cup, as at Pylos Peisistratus bids the disguised Athena pour and pray before she passes the cup to Telemachus (3.40–47). No libation is told at the first meal of the day (14.72–113).
