---
summary: Eumaeus praises the tale of the cloak: “Old man, the tale you have told is flawless, / and you have not yet spoken any word amiss or profitless” (14.508–509).
---

νηκερδές is new to the entry: the word that brings no gain, said of the tale that has brought its teller a cloak for the night (14.510–522; see [[ainos]], [[litotes]] and [[the-tale-of-the-cloak]]). Two manuscripts have ἔνισπες for ἔειπες (D. B. Monro, *Homer's Odyssey, Books XIII–XXIV*, 1901, critical note on 14.509).
