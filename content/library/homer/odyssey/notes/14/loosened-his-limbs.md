summary: Eumaeus' curse on the race of Helen, “since she loosed the knees of many men” (14.69), and the Cretan's “hateful journey, / which loosed the knees of many men” (14.235–236).
grc: ["ὑπὸ γούνατʼ ἔλυσε"]
en: [loosed the knees]
---

πολλῶν … ὑπὸ γούνατʼ ἔλυσε is the killer's phrase of the Iliad, said of Diomedes and of Patroclus, of Hector and of Ares (*Iliad* 5.176, 16.425, 15.291, 24.498; see the general article); here the killers are Helen and the war that Zeus devised (see [[helen]] and [[zeus]]). D. B. Monro hears a play on the knees in the swineherd's curse: πρόχνυ, used of utter downfall, keeps its literal sense here, “may the race of Helen fall and be brought to its knees, even as she has loosed the knees of many men” (*Homer's Odyssey, Books XIII–XXIV*, 1901, on 14.69; see [[gounata]] and [[phylon]]). In his additional notes he reports Brugmann's view that πρόχνυ is from the root of κναίω, “gnaw, rub away,” so that it meant first “rubbed away,” and so “utterly” (Monro, Additional Notes and Corrections, p. 286).
