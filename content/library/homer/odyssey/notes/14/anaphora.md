summary: Eumaeus counts his master's herds on the mainland: “Twelve herds of cattle on the mainland; as many flocks of sheep, / as many droves of pigs, as many wide-ranging herds of goats” (14.100–101); and the stranger tells what wine does: it sets a man to sing, “and to laugh softly, and stirs him up to dance, / and brings out a word that is better left unsaid” (14.464–466).
refs: [14.100-101, 14.464-466]
---

τόσα … τόσσα … τόσʼ: 14.101 is the verse of Nestor's booty from the raid on the Eleans, “fifty herds of cattle, as many flocks of sheep, / as many droves of pigs, as many wide-ranging herds of goats” (*Iliad* 11.678–679; see [[nestor-and-the-epeians]]), here used to count the wealth of a master, twelve of each kind (see [[possessions]] and [[numbers-and-counting]]). καί … καί … καί carries the wine from song and laughter to the word “that is better left unsaid,” and the stranger goes on to speak it (14.464–467; see [[the-mad-wine]]).
