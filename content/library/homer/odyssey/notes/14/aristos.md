# ἀριστῆας (14.218; again 21.153, 21.170), first met here among the Odyssey's notes.
grc: [ἀριστῆας]
---

The best of each herd is the suitors' tribute. The boars dwindle, “since the swineherd sent them / always the best of all the well-fed fat hogs” (14.18–19; see [[the-suitors]] and [[sties]]); the goatherds bring them daily “whichever of the well-fed goats seems the best” (14.106); and Eumaeus: “and I pick out the best of the swine with care and send it to them” (14.108). For the stranger the order is reversed: “Bring the best of the swine, so that I may sacrifice it for the stranger / from a far land” (14.414–415; see [[sacrifice]] and [[xenia]]). In the [[the-tale-to-eumaeus|Cretan tale]] the best are the men chosen for an ambush, where courage is shown: “whenever I picked for an ambush / the best men, planting evils for the enemy” (ἄνδρας ἀριστῆας, 14.217–218; see [[the-brave-man-in-ambush]] and [[ambush]]).
