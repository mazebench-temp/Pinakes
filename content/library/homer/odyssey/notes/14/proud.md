---
summary: The Cretan of himself in war: “never did my proud spirit foresee death, / but leaping out far the first I would kill with my spear / whatever man of the enemy gave way before me with his feet” (14.219–221).
---

θυμὸς ἀγήνωρ with a verb of foreboding, as when Odysseus' proud spirit foreboded the Cyclops, “that a man would come upon us, clothed in great courage” (9.213–214); here it is the spirit that never foresaw death. The verse belongs to the raider's portrait of himself, the man picked for ambushes and first in the attack (14.216–221; see [[the-brave-man-in-ambush]], [[ambush]] and [[the-tale-to-eumaeus]]).
