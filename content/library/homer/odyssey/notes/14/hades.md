---
summary: Hades is named twice, each time in a verse of the Iliad. The beggar swears that the man who lies from poverty is “hateful to me as the gates of Hades” (14.156), the words of Achilles to Odysseus, and in his tale his Cretan father is carried off by “the fates of death … to the house of Hades” (14.207–208).
# The entry's Ἀΐδαο claims both lines (14.156, 14.208).
---

**The gates of Hades.** Before his oath the stranger refuses any reward until Odysseus comes home: “For hateful to me as the gates of Hades is that man / who, giving way to poverty, speaks deceits” (14.156–157; see [[euangelion]], [[hateful-as-the-gates-of-hades]] and [[oath]]). The first verse is Achilles' in the embassy, spoken to Odysseus himself: “For hateful to me as the gates of Hades is that man / who hides one thing in his mind and says another” (*Iliad* 9.312–313). In the swineherd's hut the speaker himself hides who he is, and yet what he swears is true, while Eumaeus takes it for a lie (14.149–164, 14.363–365; see [[truth-and-deception]]). The gates of Hades are the threshold that the dead pass, as Tlepolemus tells Sarpedon that he “will pass the gates of Hades”, and the ghost of Patroclus asks to be buried “so that I may pass the gates of Hades” (*Iliad* 5.646, 23.71).

**The house of Hades.** In the [[the-tale-to-eumaeus|Cretan tale]] the speaker's father, Castor son of Hylax, honored among the Cretans like a god, died and left his sons to divide his livelihood: “But the fates of death came and carried him away / to the house of Hades” (14.207–208; see [[ker-doom]] and [[castor-son-of-hylax]]). κῆρες ἔβαν θανάτοιο φέρουσαι is said in the Iliad by Odysseus, of the witnesses of the portent at Aulis, “all whom the fates of death have not carried away” (*Iliad* 2.302), and εἰς Ἀΐδαο δόμους is the destination of the journey that Circe gives Odysseus in Book 10, “to the house of Hades and of dread Persephone” (10.491, 10.564; see [[persephone]]).
