---
summary: The stranger asks who bought the swineherd, “so very rich and mighty as you say?” (14.116); and in the false tale he is “the son of a rich man” of Crete (14.200).
grc: [ἀφνειοῖο]
---

ἀφνειοῖο is new to the Odyssey's forms of the entry (the Iliad has it in its Book 24 note). ἀνέρος ἀφνειοῖο, the phrase of the rich man's chamber door in the simile of Zeus' eagle sent to Priam (*Iliad* 24.317–318), names the Cretan's father, Castor son of Hylax, whose wealth the true-born sons divided by lot, leaving the bastard little (14.199–210; see [[castor-son-of-hylax]], [[nothos]] and [[the-tale-to-eumaeus]]). The master whose wealth Eumaeus has just counted is the stranger himself (14.96–108; see [[possessions]]).
