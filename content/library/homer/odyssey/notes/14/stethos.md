---
summary: The breast that holds the heart, Eumaeus' grieving (14.169) and “very unbelieving” (14.391); and the Cretan's chest, which he lowered into the sea to swim away from his captors (14.350–351).
---

The chest holds the heart: “for truly the heart in my breast / grieves whenever anyone reminds me of my devoted master” (θυμὸς ἐνὶ στήθεσσιν ἐμοῖσιν / ἄχνυται, 14.169–170; see [[thymos]] and [[achos]]), and the beggar's reproach, “Truly the heart in your breast is very unbelieving” (14.391; see [[peitho]]). In the Cretan tale it is the swimmer's body: “I slid down the polished lading plank and brought to the sea / my chest, and then I rowed through it with both my hands, / swimming” (14.350–352; see [[the-tale-to-eumaeus]]), ἐφόλκαιον being probably a “lading plank”, to take in cargo (D. B. Monro, *Homer's Odyssey, Books XIII–XXIV*, 1901, on 14.350).
