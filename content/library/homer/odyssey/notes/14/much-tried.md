---
summary: Eumaeus, when he has heard the tale of the cloak: “so you shall not lack clothing, nor anything else / that is fitting for a much-tried suppliant who has come our way” (14.510–511).
---

The two verses are Nausicaa's promise to the shipwrecked stranger (6.192–193; see the Book 6 note and the translation's note on 6.193), with a limit added: “for now; but at dawn you will knock about in your own rags” (14.512; see [[rags]]). For the night the swineherd throws over him the thick cloak he keeps for storms (14.520–522; see [[cloak]] and [[supplication]]).
