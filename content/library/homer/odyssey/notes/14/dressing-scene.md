summary: Eumaeus goes out to sleep beside the boars: “First he slung his sharp sword about his massive shoulders, / and put on a cloak that kept off the wind, very thick, / and took up the fleece of a large, well-fed goat, / and took a sharp javelin, a defense against dogs and men” (14.528–531).

The steps of the scene, an item to a verse, for a herdsman going out into a stormy night: the sword slung about the shoulders, the cloak, a goat's fleece for his bed, and last the javelin taken up, εἵλετο, the spear with which a man goes out (see the general article, and [[sword]], [[cloak]], [[sharp]] and [[spear]]). The order is changed, the sword coming first. ὡπλίζετο, “made himself ready,” D. B. Monro takes as “furnished himself,” “took what he needed for his bivouac,” and the going out, told first, as the main action put before it (*Homer's Odyssey, Books XIII–XXIV*, 1901, on 14.526; see [[hysteron-proteron]]). The cloak is his own; the spare one, “which he kept by him to change into,” he has thrown over the stranger (14.520–522). Earlier in the day he “quickly drew his tunic tight with his belt” before going to the sties for the stranger's meal (14.72).
