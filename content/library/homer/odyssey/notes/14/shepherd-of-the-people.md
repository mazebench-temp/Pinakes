---
summary: In the tale of the cloak Odysseus asks for a messenger: “but would that someone / would go and tell Agamemnon, son of Atreus, shepherd of the people, / whether he might urge more men to come from the ships” (14.496–498).
---

Ἀτρεΐδῃ Ἀγαμέμνονι, ποιμένι λαῶν is the half-verse with which Odysseus rebuked Thersites for railing at the king (*Iliad* 2.254), and the title of the king to whom any man who saw something would go and tell it, as Achilles warns Priam (*Iliad* 24.654). Here it belongs to the past of the tale, a night of the war at Troy (see [[agamemnon]], [[son-of-atreus]] and [[the-tale-of-the-cloak]]).
