---
summary: Eumaeus on the master who would have rewarded him: “such as a kind-hearted master gives to his servant, / a house and an allotment and a much-wooed wife” (14.63–64).
---

The only time the word is not said of Penelope (see the general article). A wife whom many would woo is part of a good servant's reward, with a house and land, “when he has toiled much for him” (14.65; see [[eumaeus]] and [[slavery-and-freedom]]). The translation keeps “much-wooed,” as for “the much-wooed queen” (4.770; see the Odyssey translation's key words).
