---
summary: Prayer goes with the sacrifices of Book 3. Athena, in the likeness of Mentor, prays to Poseidon for Nestor and the Pylians and for her own and Telemachus' return, and brings it all to pass herself; Nestor prays to Athena when he knows her, vowing a heifer, and again at the libation and at the sacrifice.
refs: [3.43-64, 3.380-385, 3.393-394, 3.445-447]
---

**Athena's prayer.** Peisistratus gives the cup first to the elder stranger: “Pray now, stranger, to lord Poseidon” (3.43; see [[peisistratus]]). Athena “prayed earnestly to lord Poseidon” (3.54): glory for Nestor and his sons, a gracious return for the Pylians, a homecoming for Telemachus and herself (3.55–61; see [[poseidon]] and [[the-sacrifice-at-pylos]]). “So she prayed, and she herself was bringing it all to pass” (3.62): the god who prays is the god who answers (see [[athena]]). Telemachus prays in the same way after her (3.64).

**Nestor's prayer.** When the goddess leaves in the likeness of a sea eagle, Nestor prays to her: “But, queen, be gracious, and grant me noble fame, / to me and my children and my revered wife” (3.380–381), and vows her the heifer of Diomedes' vow (3.382–384 = *Iliad* 10.292–294); “So he spoke in prayer, and Pallas Athena heard him” (3.385 = *Iliad* 5.121; see [[gold-poured-around-her-horns]] and [[the-sacrifice-to-athena]]). At home “he prayed as he poured the libation” (3.394), and at the sacrifice “he prayed as he made the first offering” (3.446).
