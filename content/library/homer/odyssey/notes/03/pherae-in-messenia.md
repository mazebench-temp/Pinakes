Pherae is the halfway house of the journey, a day's drive from Pylos and a day from Lacedaemon (3.484–497; see [[pylos]], [[lacedaemon]] and [[the-journey-of-telemachus]]). The host is the Diocles of the Iliad, whose twin sons Aeneas killed at Troy, and the genealogy is the Iliad's: Alpheus, “which flows broad through the land of the Pylians”, begot Ortilochus, and Ortilochus great-hearted Diocles (*Iliad* 5.541–547; see [[diocles]], [[ortilochus]] and [[alpheus]]). Merry gathers the town's Homeric epithets, “well-built” (*Iliad* 5.543) and “holy” (*Iliad* 9.151, among the seven towns Agamemnon offers Achilles), places it on the left bank of the Nedon at the northeast of the Messenian Gulf, on the site of modern Kalamata, and finds its position in Messene settled by the house of Ortilochus there, where Odysseus met Iphitus (21.15–16; Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 3.488). For him the night at Pherae is the strongest argument that Nestor's Pylos was the Messenian one (Merry on 3.4; see [[strabo-on-nestors-pylos]]). Diocles “set good cheer before them”, ξείνια, the entertainment owed to guests (3.490; see [[xenia]]), and they stop at his house again on the way home (15.186–188).
