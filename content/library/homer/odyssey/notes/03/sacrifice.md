summary: Book 3 is framed by sacrifices: the Pylians sacrifice black bulls to Poseidon on the shore when Telemachus lands, Nestor recalls the offerings at Tenedos and Geraestus on the way home from Troy, Aegisthus burns thigh-pieces in thanks for his success, and Nestor sacrifices a heifer with gilded horns to Athena.
# ἱερὰ ῥέζον (3.5), "were offering sacrifice", as ἱερὰ ῥέζων (1.61); ἱρὰ θεοῖσιν (3.159) is the Book 1 note's.
grc: ["ἱερὰ ῥέζον"]
refs: [3.5-9, 3.144-145, 3.159, 3.178-179, 3.273-274, 3.418-463]
---

**At Pylos.** Telemachus comes ashore at a sacrifice: “There on the shore of the sea the people were offering sacrifice, / bulls all black, to the dark-haired earth-shaker” (3.5–6; see [[the-sacrifice-at-pylos]] and [[poseidon]]). ἱερὰ ῥέζειν, “to do sacred things”, is the phrase of Athena's plea for Odysseus in the first book (1.61).

**On the way home.** Agamemnon wished “to hold back the army and offer holy hecatombs, / so that he might heal the terrible anger of Athena” (3.144–145; see [[the-departure-of-the-greeks]]). At Tenedos “we offered sacrifice to the gods” (3.159), and at Geraestus, after the crossing of the open sea, “for Poseidon, of bulls, / we laid many thigh-pieces on the altar” (3.178–179; see [[geraestus]] and [[thigh-pieces]]).

**Aegisthus.** Having won Agamemnon's wife, “he burned many thigh-pieces on the holy altars of the gods, / and hung up many offerings, woven things and gold” (3.273–274; see [[aegisthus]] and [[the-murder-of-agamemnon]]): he thanks the gods for the deed they had sent Hermes to forbid (1.37–39).

**Nestor's sacrifice.** The heifer vowed to Athena is sacrificed in the morning, partly in the verses of the sacrifice at Chryse (3.447 = *Iliad* 1.458; 3.458–462 = *Iliad* 1.461–465; see [[the-sacrifice-to-athena]] and [[sacrifice-type-scene]]).
