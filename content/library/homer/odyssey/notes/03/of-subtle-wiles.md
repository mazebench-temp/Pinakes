summary: At Tenedos some turned back “with lord Odysseus, wise-hearted, of subtle wiles” (3.163).
---

ἀμφʼ Ὀδυσῆα ἄνακτα δαΐφρονα, ποικιλομήτην is the Iliad's verse-end ἀμφʼ Ὀδυσῆα δαΐφρονα ποικιλομήτην (*Iliad* 11.482) with ἄνακτα, “lord,” added ([[wise-hearted]]). Odysseus' cunning is the theme of Nestor's praise a few verses earlier: “brilliant Odysseus far surpassed them / in tricks of every kind” (3.121–122; [[metis]], [[dolos]], [[odysseus]]).
