---
summary: Nestor and Menelaus sail together from Troy until “holy Sunium, the cape of Athens”, where Apollo kills Menelaus' helmsman Phrontis and Menelaus stays behind to bury him (3.276–285).
---

Sunium is where Nestor and Menelaus part company. They had sailed together from Troy, “the son of Atreus and I, with friendly hearts toward one another; / but when we came to holy Sunium, the cape of Athens”, Phoebus Apollo killed Menelaus' helmsman with his gentle arrows as he held the steering oar of the speeding ship (3.276–281; see [[phrontis-son-of-onetor]], [[apollo]] and [[menelaus]]). Menelaus is held back there to bury his companion, and the delay sends him on alone toward Malea and the storm that will carry him to Egypt (3.284–300; see [[malea]] and [[the-wanderings-of-menelaus]]). The cape lies across the strait from Geraestus, where the ships had put in after crossing from Lesbos (3.177–179; see [[geraestus]]).

Merry explains ἱρόν, “holy”, by the temple on the cape, which he took for Athena's, or by the cult that sailors paid there to Poseidon (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 3.278; see [[hieros]]). ἄκρον Ἀθηνέων, “the cape of Athens”, is one of the Odyssey's two mentions of the city in this book; the other brings Orestes home “back from Athens” (3.307; see [[athens]]).
