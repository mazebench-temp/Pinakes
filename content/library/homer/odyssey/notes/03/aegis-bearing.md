---
summary: Athena is twice the daughter of aegis-bearing Zeus: when Peisistratus gives her the cup first (3.42), and when Nestor pours the night's libation to her (3.394).
---

κούρην Διὸς αἰγιόχοιο (3.42) and κούρῃ Διὸς αἰγιόχοιο (3.394) close the verse each time a mortal honors the goddess with wine: first unknowingly, when Peisistratus hands the golden cup to the stranger in Mentor's likeness, and then after she has shown herself, when Nestor mixes the wine eleven years old and prays to her as he pours (3.41–42, 3.390–394; [[daughter-of-zeus]], [[libation]]).
