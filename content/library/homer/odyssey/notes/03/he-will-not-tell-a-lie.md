---
summary: Athena tells Telemachus that Nestor “will not tell a lie, for he is very wise” (3.20), and Nestor says the same of Menelaus as he sends him on to Sparta (3.328).
---

The couplet brings Telemachus to Nestor and sends him on from him. He has said that he is not yet practiced in shrewd words and that there is shame for a young man in questioning an older one (3.22–24; [[shame-for-a-young-man]], [[shrewd-word]]); the goddess answers that he must ask for himself, and that the old king will tell him the truth. Nestor tells what he knows and what he has heard, “as is the custom” (3.184–187), and sends the young man on to Menelaus, for “he has lately come home from abroad” (3.317–318), in the same words of truthfulness (3.327–328; [[the-journey-of-telemachus]], [[nestor]], [[menelaus]], [[wise]]).
