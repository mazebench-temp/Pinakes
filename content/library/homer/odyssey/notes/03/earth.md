**The earth of men.** The book opens with the sun leaving the water “into the brazen sky, to give light to the immortals / and to mortal men upon the grain-giving earth” (3.2–3; see [[sky]], [[grain-giving]] and [[mortal-men]]), the words with which Zeus will bid Helios shine on, among the immortals and mortal men, when he threatens to go down to the dead (12.385–386).

**The earth of the dead.** Athena tells Telemachus that he has sailed to learn of his father, “where the earth hid him and what fate he has met” (3.16). κύθε γαῖα is the burial, as Nitzsch saw: the aorist names a moment, “he was buried”, and the two clauses come in reverse order, how he died and where earth received his body; the worst is supposed (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 3.16; see [[tomb]]). Nestor's tale of Aegisthus turns on the same rite denied: had Menelaus found him alive, “they would not have heaped up the loose earth over him even in death” (3.258; see [[heaped-earth]] and [[achaean-women]]). Telemachus' own “native land”, πατρίδα γαῖαν, is where Nestor says he would go home, weary, before he had heard all the Achaeans' sufferings (3.117; see [[dear-native-land]]).

**The ground of the sacrifice.** At the sacrifice to Athena the heifer's head is lifted “from the wide-wayed earth” to bare the throat (3.453; see [[wide-wayed-earth]] and [[the-sacrifice-to-athena]]).
