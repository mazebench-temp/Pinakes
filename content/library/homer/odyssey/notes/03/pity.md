---
summary: Telemachus asks Nestor not to soften his account “out of respect for me, or out of pity” (3.96).
---

The verse, which he says again to Menelaus (4.326), asks for the truth without the pity that would hide it (see [[aidos]] and [[truth-and-deception]]): Merry takes με as the object of both participles and μειλίσσεο as “soften thy words” (*Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 3.96). What Telemachus wants to hear is his father's death, if Nestor saw it or heard of it from another (3.92–95): the young man who feared to question an older one now asks him not to spare him (3.24; see [[shame-for-a-young-man]]).
