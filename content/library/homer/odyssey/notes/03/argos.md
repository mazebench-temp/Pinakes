---
summary: Argos is Diomedes' city, where his ships reach home on the fourth day from Troy (3.180–182); in Telemachus' question “Was he not in Achaean Argos?” and in Nestor's “in a corner of horse-pasturing Argos” it is the wider land, Agamemnon's realm, where Aegisthus sat at ease while the army was at Troy (3.251, 3.263).
---

**Diomedes' city.** Nestor's account of the voyage home ends its first part at Argos: “It was the fourth day when in Argos the balanced ships / of the companions of Diomedes, son of Tydeus, tamer of horses, / were moored; but I held on for Pylos” (3.180–182; see [[diomedes]], [[balanced-ships]] and [[pylos]]). This is the city of Diomedes in the Catalogue (*Iliad* 2.559), reached a day after the night landing at Geraestus (3.177–178; see [[geraestus]]).

**Achaean Argos.** Telemachus asks where Menelaus was when his brother was killed: “Was he not in Achaean Argos, but somewhere else / wandering among men” (3.251–252; see [[menelaus]] and [[the-murder-of-agamemnon]]). Ἄργεος is a local genitive, which places less exactly than the dative, as in Telemachus' praise of his mother before the contest of the bow, οὔτε Πύλου ἱερῆς οὔτʼ Ἄργεος οὔτε Μυκήνης, “neither in sacred Pylos nor in Argos nor in Mycenae” (21.108, a plain rendering); the epithet Ἀχαιικόν belongs to Argos as the chief place of the Peloponnese, or marks it off from the Pelasgian Argos of Thessaly (*Iliad* 2.681; Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 3.251; see [[pelasgian-argos]] and [[achaeans]]). The Iliad has the phrase in Agamemnon's offer, “if we come to Achaean Argos” (*Iliad* 9.141, 9.283).

**A corner of Argos.** While the Achaeans toiled at Troy, Aegisthus, “undisturbed, in a corner of horse-pasturing Argos, / kept bewitching the wife of Agamemnon with many words” (3.263–264; see [[aegisthus]], [[clytemnestra]] and [[horse-pasturing]]). The phrase μυχῷ Ἄργεος ἱπποβότοιο is used in the Iliad of Ephyra (*Iliad* 6.152; see [[ephyra-in-argos]]). If Argos means the whole Peloponnese, Merry says, the words mean no more than “within Argos”; but Ernst Curtius, whom he quotes, took them of Mycenae, which lies at the innermost corner of the plain of the Inachus, so that Argos is Agamemnon's kingdom, and the epithet “horse-pasturing”, apt for the fertile plain rather than for “thirsty Argos” as a whole (*Iliad* 4.171), supports him (Merry on 3.263; see [[mycenae]]). A few verses earlier Merry reports a reading ἑκὰς Ἄργεος, “far from Argos”, for ἑκὰς ἄστεος, “far from the city”, where Aegisthus' body would have lain unburied; he keeps “the city”, Mycenae, since the city of Argos was Diomedes' and the whole kingdom will not suit the passage (3.260; Merry on 3.260).
