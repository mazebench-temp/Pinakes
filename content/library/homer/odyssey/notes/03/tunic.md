---
summary: After the bath Polycaste puts a fine mantle and a tunic around Telemachus (3.467).
---

“And when she had bathed him and anointed him richly with olive oil, / and put a fine mantle and a tunic around him” (3.466–467; see [[bathing]], [[cloak]] and [[polycaste]]). The verse belongs to the bath of a guest or a master: Odysseus is dressed in the same words after his bath on the day of the recognition, and with a χλαῖνα for the φᾶρος after his baths in Circe's house and among the Phaeacians (23.155; 10.365, 8.455), and the Iliad uses it of Hector's body, washed and anointed by the slave women before it is given back to Priam (*Iliad* 24.587–588). Clean clothes after the bath complete the welcome: the guest comes to the feast washed, anointed and newly dressed (3.468–469; see [[xenia]]).
