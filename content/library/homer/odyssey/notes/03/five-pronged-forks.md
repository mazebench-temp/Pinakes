---
summary: At the sacrifice to Athena young men stand beside Nestor holding five-pronged forks while he burns the thigh-pieces, in the verse of the sacrifice at Chryse (3.459–460 = *Iliad* 1.462–463).
---

“The old man burned them on split wood, and over them poured / sparkling wine; and young men beside him held five-pronged forks in their hands” (3.459–460; see [[the-sacrifice-to-athena]] and [[sacrifice-type-scene]]). The two verses are those of the sacrifice at Chryse, where the old man is the priest Chryses (*Iliad* 1.462–463); at Pylos he is Nestor, who performs the sacrifice of his own house, and the young men are not named. παρʼ αὐτὸν ἔχον is “came to his side and held” (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 3.460). The forks occur only in these two verses in Homer.
