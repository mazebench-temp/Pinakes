summary: Nestor will not let his guest lie down on the deck of a ship, as if from a poor man with no cloaks and blankets “for himself or his guests to sleep softly in” (3.346–355); Telemachus sleeps on a corded bed beneath the colonnade with Peisistratus beside him, and Nestor “in the inner part of the high house” (3.396–403).
grc: [ἐνεύδειν, εὕδῃ, κοίμησε, καθεῦδε]
---

Where a guest sleeps is a matter of the host's honor: Nestor would be like a man “altogether without clothing, and poor”, if Telemachus went to sleep on the ship (3.346–353; see [[xenia]] and [[bed]]), and Athena grants that he should sleep in Nestor's halls (3.359–360). Merry explains the beds: ῥήγεα, the costly blankets laid on the bed over a fleece, and χλαῖναι, the cloaks that served as counterpanes (*Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 3.349). Nestor sleeps in the inner part of the house with “the lady his wife” (3.402–403), and the sons and sons-in-law go to their own houses (3.396; see [[in-laws]]).
