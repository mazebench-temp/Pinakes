---
summary: Nestor remembers Achilles as the leader of the Achaeans' raids by sea and among the best who lie dead at Troy (3.105–109); his son brought the Myrmidons home safely (3.188–189).
---

The war Nestor remembers was fought on the sea as well as round the city: “both all that we bore with our ships over the misty sea, / roaming after plunder wherever Achilles led” (3.105–106). ὅπῃ ἄρξειεν, wherever he led on each occasion, is an optative of indefinite frequency (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 3.106): the raids were many, as in the Iliad Achilles counts twelve cities sacked with his ships and eleven on foot (*Iliad* 9.328–329; see [[the-sack-of-lyrnessus]]). Then Nestor names the dead: “There lies warlike Ajax, there Achilles, / there Patroclus, a counselor equal to the gods” (3.109–110; see [[ajax]] and [[patroclus]]). In the last book the shade of Agamemnon tells how Achilles' bones were laid with those of Patroclus in a golden urn, and those of Antilochus apart (24.71–79, a plain rendering; see [[antilochus]]). The Myrmidons came home under “the shining son of great-hearted Achilles” (3.189; see [[neoptolemus]], [[myrmidons]] and [[great-hearted]]).
