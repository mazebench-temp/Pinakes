---
summary: Telemachus' verse of reply, “And wise Telemachus answered her face to face” or “answered him,” five times (3.21, 3.75, 3.201, 3.225, 3.239); Nestor and Menelaus, each of whom “will not tell a lie, for he is very wise” (3.20, 3.328); and Peisistratus, in whom Athena rejoiced as “the wise and just man” (3.52).
---

The couplet of 3.19–20 and 3.327–328 is the book's own formula ([[he-will-not-tell-a-lie]]). Merry renders δικαίῳ of Peisistratus “proper,” one who observes the custom of men, and draws the same link between knowledge and propriety in πεπνυμένῳ ἀνδρὶ δικαίῳ as in the couplet on Nestor (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 3.52).
