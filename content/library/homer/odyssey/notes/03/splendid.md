---
summary: Philoctetes is “the splendid son of Poias” (3.190), and the slave women are to bring “bright water” for the feast (3.429).
---

Ποιάντιον ἀγλαὸν υἱόν: the patronymic adjective, which Merry counts among the Homeric Aeolisms (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 3.190), with the epithet of the sons of noble fathers ([[philoctetes]], [[poias]]). ἀγλαὸν … ὕδωρ is the water for the feast of Athena (3.427–429).
