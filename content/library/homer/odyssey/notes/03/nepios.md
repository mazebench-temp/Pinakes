---
summary: Agamemnon, who stayed behind to appease Athena with hecatombs, is “the fool, and he did not know that she would not be persuaded” (3.146).
---

The verse is the Iliad's for Tros, son of Alastor, who came to clasp Achilles' knees in the hope that he would spare him (*Iliad* 20.463–466); here the one who will not be persuaded is the goddess (see the translation's note on 3.146). The narrator's νήπιος is spoken by Nestor, who knows the end: Agamemnon came home to die (3.193–194, 3.234–235; see [[the-mind-of-the-gods-is-not-quickly-turned]], [[agamemnon]] and [[peitho]]).
