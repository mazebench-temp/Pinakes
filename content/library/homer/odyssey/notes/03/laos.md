summary: At Troy the λαός is the army that the sons of Atreus gathered at sunset (3.140), that Agamemnon would hold back to sacrifice (3.144), and that divided in half (3.155). At home it is the people, who may hate a king's son (3.214–215), and who were made subject to Aegisthus in Mycenae (3.304).
grc: [λαὸς]
en: [army]
---

**The army.** In Nestor's tale of the quarrel λαός is the army: the brothers called it to an assembly “idly and against all order, at the setting of the sun”, and “told them the word for which they had gathered the army” (3.138–140); Agamemnon wished “to hold back the army and offer holy hecatombs” (3.144); and at dawn “half of the army halted and stayed / there beside Agamemnon, son of Atreus, shepherd of the people” (3.155–156; see [[the-army-and-its-leaders]] and [[shepherd-of-the-people]]). Merry finds the unseemliness not in the hour alone but in calling the men at that hour, when they had come from a banquet heated with wine (*Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 3.138; see [[assembly]]).

**The people.** At home it is the people of the land. Nestor asks Telemachus whether he submits willingly, “or do the people / throughout the land hate you, following the voice of a god?” (3.214–215; see [[demos]]). Merry finds in the god's voice no oracle or omen but the unspoken, contagious feeling of a multitude, a hatred that stifles loyalty without breaking out into rebellion (on 3.214). In Mycenae, after the murder, δέδμητο δὲ λαὸς ὑπʼ αὐτῷ, “the people were made subject to him” (3.304), and Aegisthus ruled seven years (3.305; see [[aegisthus]] and [[kingship-and-authority]]).
