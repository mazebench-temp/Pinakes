summary: Had Menelaus found Aegisthus alive, “they would not have heaped up the loose earth over him even in death, / but the dogs and the birds would have devoured him” (3.258–259); Menelaus stops at Sunium “to bury his companion and give him funeral rites” (3.285); and Orestes gives the Argives a funeral feast for his mother and Aegisthus (3.309–310).
grc: [κτερίσειεν]
refs: [3.258-261, 3.309-310]
---

**The unburied.** Nestor's answer to “how did the son of Atreus die?” begins with the death Aegisthus escaped: had Menelaus come home first, the murderer would have had no mound even in death, but would have lain on the plain far from the city for the dogs and the birds, unwept by any Achaean woman (3.255–261; see [[aegisthus]], [[dogs]] and [[birds]]). It is the fate the Iliad's first lines hold over the dead, to be “the spoil of dogs and of every bird” (*Iliad* 1.4–5), and Merry takes the city as Mycenae (*Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 3.260). The singer who guarded Clytemnestra was left on a desert island “to become the prey and spoil of birds” (3.271; see [[the-singer-of-clytemnestra]]).

**Rites at Sunium.** Menelaus, “eager though he was for his journey”, is held back at Sunium to bury his helmsman Phrontis, struck down by Apollo, ὄφρʼ ἕταρον θάπτοι καὶ ἐπὶ κτέρεα κτερίσειεν (3.284–285; see [[phrontis-son-of-onetor]] and [[hetairos]]), the phrase with which Athena in Book 1 told Telemachus to “give him funeral rites” if he heard that his father was dead (κτέρεα κτερεΐξαι, 1.291; see [[fame-and-the-tomb]]).

**The funeral feast.** When Orestes had killed Aegisthus, δαίνυ τάφον Ἀργείοισιν, he gave the Argives a funeral feast for his mother and for Aegisthus (3.309–310), as Achilles “gave them a funeral feast to satisfy the heart” for Patroclus (*Iliad* 23.29; Merry on 3.309), and the Trojans feasted in Priam's house when they had heaped Hector's mound (*Iliad* 24.801–803; see [[orestes-kills-clytemnestra]] and the translation's note on 3.309–310). Menelaus came home the same day (3.311–312; see [[the-wanderings-of-menelaus]]).
