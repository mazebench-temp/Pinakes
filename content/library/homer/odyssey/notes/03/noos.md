---
summary: “for the mind of the gods who live forever is not quickly turned” (3.147); Nestor and Odysseus took counsel “with mind and thoughtful counsel” (3.128), and the Argives were “in no way discerning or just” (3.133).
---

νόος is the god's purpose in the maxim (3.147; see [[the-mind-of-the-gods-is-not-quickly-turned]]) and the shared thought of the two counselors at Troy, νόῳ καὶ ἐπίφρονι βουλῇ (3.128), where Merry takes ἐπίφρων as the exact opposite of ἄφρων (*Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 3.128; see [[boule]] and [[metis]]). οὔ τι νοήμονες οὐδὲ δίκαιοι (3.133), Nestor's reason for the grim homecoming, is Athena's verdict on the suitors in Book 2 (2.282; see [[themis]] and [[who-is-to-blame]]). On the shore Athena tells Telemachus that some things he will think of, νοήσεις, himself (3.26; see [[some-things-you-will-think-of-yourself]]).
