---
summary: The formula names Telemachus twice: when he prays after the goddess, “and in the same way the dear son of Odysseus prayed” (3.64), and in Nestor's protest that “the dear son of this man Odysseus” will not sleep on the deck of a ship (3.352–353).
---

At 3.352 τοῦδʼ ἀνδρός stands before the name, “this man,” and Merry notes that ὅδε ἀνήρ in Homer always stands for someone present to the senses or, as here, to the thought, never for the speaker (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 3.352; [[xenia]]). See [[telemachus]].
