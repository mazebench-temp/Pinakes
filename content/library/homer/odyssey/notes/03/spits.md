# ὀβελοὺς (3.463), first met in the Odyssey here. Unnamed: "threading more on spits" (ἄλλα τʼ ἔπειρον, 3.33).
grc: [ὀβελοὺς]
refs: [3.33]
---

**On the shore.** The Pylians are “making ready the feast, were roasting meat and threading more on spits” (3.33; see [[the-sacrifice-at-pylos]] and [[feast]]): W. W. Merry takes ἄλλα with both verbs, “Some meats there were roasting, and others they were piercing with the spit” (*Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 3.33).

**At the sacrifice.** When the thigh-pieces have burned and the entrails have been tasted, “they cut up the rest and threaded it on spits, / and roasted it, holding the sharp-pointed spits in their hands” (3.462–463; see [[the-sacrifice-to-athena]] and [[sacrifice-type-scene]]). The first verse is the Iliad's at Chryse (*Iliad* 1.465), where ἀμφʼ ὀβελοῖσιν ἔπειραν means that the spits ran right through the meat and stood out on either side, with ἀμφί an adverb, as Merry reads it from the plainer πεῖράν τʼ ὀβελοῖσι of a later book (19.422; Merry on 3.462). The second verse is found only here, and ἀκρόπορος, “sharp-pointed”, only in this verse; the Iliad's sacrifice has instead “and roasted it carefully, and drew it all off” (*Iliad* 1.466). The meat comes off the spits for the feast: “And when they had roasted the outer flesh and drawn it off” (3.470; see [[feast-type-scene]]).
