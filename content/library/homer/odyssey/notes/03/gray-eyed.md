---
summary: Athena's epithet eight times: in the verses that bring in her speeches to Telemachus and Nestor (3.13, 3.25, 3.229, 3.330, 3.356), in Nestor's wish that she would love Telemachus (3.218), in his tale of her wrath, “the gray-eyed daughter of the mighty father” (3.135), and at her departure, “So she spoke, and gray-eyed Athena went away” (3.371).
---

The book adds the genitive γλαυκώπιδος, in the phrase of the wrath (3.135; [[daughter-of-a-mighty-father]], [[deadly]]). The verse of her answers in Book 1 brings them in again, “Then the gray-eyed goddess Athena spoke to him in turn” (3.25, 3.229, 3.356; [[answering-him]]); at 3.13 she speaks first ([[spoke-first]]), and at 3.330 “And among them spoke also the gray-eyed goddess Athena.” Nestor's wish puts the epithet in a mortal's mouth: “If only gray-eyed Athena were willing to love you / as she cared then for glorious Odysseus” (3.218–219). See [[athena]].
