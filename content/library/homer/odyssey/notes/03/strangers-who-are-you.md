---
summary: When the strangers have eaten, Nestor asks them who they are, and whether they sail on business or wander the sea like pirates (3.69–74).
---

The question waits for the end of the meal, as the welcome of a guest requires (3.67–70; [[desire-for-drink-and-food]], [[visit-scene]]). Telemachus, with the daring that Athena has put in his mind, answers its parts: who they are, “We have come from Ithaca under Neion,” and why, “this business I speak of is private, not public” (3.76–82; [[prexis]], [[tharsos]]). The Cyclops asks the same four verses before he eats Odysseus' men (9.252–255; [[piracy]], [[thucydides-and-the-pirates]]).
