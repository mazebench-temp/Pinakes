summary: Two marriages in Nestor's house and tale. Agamemnon perished “by the guile of Aegisthus and of his own wife” (3.235), who at first refused “the shameful deed” and then went, willing, with Aegisthus (3.265–272). Nestor prays for fame for himself, his children and his “revered wife” (3.381), who shares his bed that night (3.403) and raises the cry at the sacrifice (3.451–452).
refs: [3.234-235, 3.263-275, 3.381, 3.403, 3.451-452]
---

**The wife who yielded.** Aegisthus spent the years of the war “in a corner of horse-pasturing Argos”, bewitching the wife of Agamemnon with words, while a singer guarded her at her husband's charge (3.263–268; see [[aegisthus]], [[the-singer-of-clytemnestra]] and [[truth-and-deception]]). She refused at first, “for her mind was good”; then “willing, he led her, willing, to his own house” (3.265–272; see [[clytemnestra]] and [[phrenes]]). Athena sets that homecoming against the one she would choose: better to toil through many sorrows and come home late than “come home and perish at my own hearth, as Agamemnon / perished by the guile of Aegisthus and of his own wife” (3.232–235; see [[a-god-can-save-a-man-from-afar]] and [[the-murder-of-agamemnon]]). Telemachus, who asks how it happened, has a mother at home with suitors in the house (3.212–213, 3.248–252; see [[penelope]]).

**The revered wife.** Nestor's own marriage is the household's center. The lady his wife prepares his bed and shares it in the inner part of the house (3.402–403), where Merry notes that πόρσυνε is used always of the wife who shares the bed (*Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 3.403); she is his “revered wife”, for whom he asks Athena's favor (3.381; see [[aidos]]), and at the sacrifice she raises the cry with their daughters and their sons' wives, “Eurydice, eldest of the daughters of Clymenus” (3.450–452; see [[eurydice]] and [[ololyge]]).
