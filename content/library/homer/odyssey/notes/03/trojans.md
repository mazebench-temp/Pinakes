---
summary: The Trojans are named only as the enemy of the past war: Odysseus “sacked the city of the Trojans” at Nestor's side, and “the land of the Trojans” is where the Achaeans suffered (3.84–87, 3.100, 3.220).
---

Telemachus asks Nestor for news of the father who, “they say, / fighting at your side sacked the city of the Trojans”, since of all the others “as many as fought against the Trojans” he has heard where each one perished (3.84–87; see [[odysseus]] and [[troy]]). Τρώων πόλιν, “the city of the Trojans”, is the city named by its people, as Nestor names it by its king, “the great city of lord Priam” (3.107; see [[priam]]). δήμῳ ἔνι Τρώων, “in the land of the Trojans”, is the war's place in both speakers' mouths: Odysseus kept his promises there (3.99–100), and Athena stood openly beside him there (3.219–222; see [[athena]] and [[achaeans]]), as Telemachus in Book 1 would have had his father fall “in the land of the Trojans” rather than vanish (1.237).
