**Beside his father.** Peisistratus seats the strangers “beside his brother Thrasymedes and his father” (3.39; see [[peisistratus]] and [[kasignetos]]). From his place next to his father Merry supposes that he was the eldest of the sons, and compares *Iliad* 9.81, where he is named first of the captains of the watch (*Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 3.36).

**The blow.** At dawn he is among the six sons around Nestor, ἀντίθεος, “godlike” (3.414; see [[godlike]]). At the sacrifice, “Thrasymedes, steadfast in battle, / stood by, holding a sharp axe in his hand, to strike the heifer down” (3.442–443; see [[steadfast-in-battle]] and [[axe]]), the epithet the Iliad gives him when he arms Diomedes for the night raid (*Iliad* 10.255). When they have prayed and scattered the barley grains, “at once the son of Nestor, high-hearted Thrasymedes, / standing close, struck; and the axe cut through the sinews / of the neck, and loosed the strength of the heifer” (3.448–450; see [[high-hearted]] and [[the-sacrifice-to-athena]]). The blow only stuns the heifer; she dies when Peisistratus cuts her throat (3.454–455; Merry on 3.450).
