---
summary: At Tenedos Zeus “roused evil strife against us once more, a second time,” and Nestor calls him “the hard god” (3.160–161).
---

σχέτλιος stands first in the verse, in apposition to Ζεύς, the reproach of Agamemnon's complaint in the Iliad, here in Nestor's tale of the second strife ([[the-departure-of-the-greeks]]). Merry, against the scholia, derives it from σχέσθαι, “holding out,” the very opposite of self-sparing, like the Latin *improbus*, and renders “hard” (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 3.161). See [[zeus]].
