---
summary: Ovid's Nestor, telling the tale of Caeneus at a feast before Troy, counts his years: he has lived two hundred and is living his third age, the three generations of men over which, in this book, he has been king.
---

- *vixi / annos bis centum; nunc tertia vivitur aetas*, “I have lived two hundred years; now my third age is being lived” (*Metamorphoses* 12.187–188, on 3.245): [[nestors-three-generations]] and [[nestor-and-the-centaurs]].
