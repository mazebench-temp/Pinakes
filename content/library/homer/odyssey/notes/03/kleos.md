---
summary: Athena put daring in Telemachus “so that noble fame may be his among men” (3.78); he is “in quest of the wide report” of his father (3.83); the Achaeans will carry Orestes' fame wide, “even for men to come to hear of” (3.204); and Nestor, knowing the goddess, prays, “grant me noble fame, / to me and my children and my revered wife” (3.380–381).
---

**Fame and report.** The book uses the word in the two ways of Books 1 and 2. With an epithet it is fame, κλέος ἐσθλόν, “noble fame” (3.78, 3.380); with the genitive of the man spoken of it is the report that travels: πατρὸς ἐμοῦ κλέος εὐρὺ μετέρχομαι, “I am in quest of the wide report of my father, if I may hear of it somewhere” (3.83). Merry renders “I am in quest of news, if I can anywhere hear it, of my father”, compares σὸν κλέος (13.415), and takes εὐρύ as “far-spread”, as of Odysseus' fame through Hellas (1.344) and of Orestes' here (3.204) (*Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 3.83; see the translation's note on 1.241). The report is what Nestor cannot give: he came home ἀπευθής, “without tidings” (3.184), and knows only what men say (3.186–200).

**Fame for the son.** The narrator gives Athena's gift of daring the purpose she gave the voyage in Book 1, in the same verse, “and so that noble fame may be his among men” (3.78 = 1.95; see [[tharsos]]). Nestor holds up Orestes, and Telemachus grants his fame: “yes, truly that son took vengeance, and the Achaeans / will carry his fame wide, even for men to come to hear of” (3.203–204; see [[orestes]], [[poine]] and [[so-good-a-thing-is-a-son]]). Wolf took καὶ ἐσσομένοισι πυθέσθαι from Eustathius; the manuscripts have καὶ ἐσσομένοισιν ἀοιδήν, “a song even for men to come”, and Merry, against Nitzsch's preference for the infinitive, suggests that κλέος may be the praise of the present generation and ἀοιδή the praise of posterity (on 3.204; see the translation's note on 3.204 and [[song-and-fame]]).

**Nestor's prayer.** When the goddess leaves in the likeness of a sea eagle, Nestor knows her and prays for fame for his whole house, with a heifer vowed in return (3.380–384; see [[athena]], [[reciprocity-with-the-gods]] and [[the-sacrifice-to-athena]]). At the feast on the shore Athena had prayed to Poseidon for the same house, “To Nestor first of all and to his sons grant glory” (3.57; see [[kydos]]).
