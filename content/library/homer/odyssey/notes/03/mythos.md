---
summary: Telemachus is “not yet practiced at all in shrewd words” (3.23), but Nestor finds that “your words are like his”, his father's (3.124–125); the sons of Atreus told the drunken assembly “the word for which they had gathered the army” (3.140).
grc: [μύθοισι, μῦθοί]
---

**The young man's words.** οὐδέ τί πω μύθοισι πεπείρημαι πυκινοῖσιν (3.23): Merry renders “I have not practised myself in wisely-worded address” (*Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 3.23; see [[shame-for-a-young-man]]). Nestor's verdict answers it: ἦ τοι γὰρ μῦθοί γε ἐοικότες, “For truly your words are like his, and you would not say / that a younger man would speak so like him” (3.124–125). Merry argues at length that ἐοικότες and ἐοικότα mean “like his”, not “like what they should be” (Nitzsch), from the ὧδε of the next line and from the parallel words of Helen at Sparta (4.141–143) and of Eurycleia (19.380–381) (on 3.124; see the translation's note on 3.124–125, [[sebas]] and [[fathers-and-sons]]).

**The word of the kings.** μῦθον μυθείσθην (3.140) is the figure grammarians called σχῆμα ἐτυμολογικόν, a verb with its own noun (Merry on 3.140), for the word of the sons of Atreus to their drunken army (see [[the-army-and-its-leaders]] and [[eris]]).
