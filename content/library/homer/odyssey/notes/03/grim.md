summary: The grim things of the war and the returns: the “grim destruction” by which the others perished and of which Telemachus asks to hear for his father (3.87, 3.93), the “grim homecoming” that Zeus devised (3.132), and the “grim destruction” and the “grim things” that Aegisthus devised for Agamemnon (3.194, 3.303).
---

λυγρὸν νόστον (3.132) is the phrase of Phemius' song in Book 1, “the grim homecoming that Pallas Athena laid on them from Troy” (1.327), now told by a man who lived it ([[nostos]], [[the-returns-of-the-achaeans]]). The verb with Aegisthus' deed is μήδομαι, “devise,” both times (3.194, 3.303; [[the-murder-of-agamemnon]]); on the order of 3.303–305 see the translation's note on 3.303–305.
