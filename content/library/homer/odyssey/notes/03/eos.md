---
summary: Dawn, in the verse that opened Book 2, begins the fourth and the fifth days of the poem: the morning of the sacrifice to Athena at Nestor's house, and the morning of the drive on from Diocles' house at Pherae.
# Not counted: ἠῶθεν "at dawn" (3.153, 3.366), the time of day, as in the Iliad's notes.
---

**Two dawns.** The third day of the poem began with the sun, not with Dawn: the ship had sailed all night and through the dawn (2.434), and Book 3 opens with the sun springing up from the water as it comes to Pylos (3.1–4; see [[helios]]). The goddess brings the next two days, with ἦμος δʼ ἠριγένεια φάνη ῥοδοδάκτυλος Ἠώς, “And when early-born Dawn appeared, the rosy-fingered” (3.404, 3.491; see [[dawn-line]], [[early-born]] and [[rosy-fingered]]). At the first, Nestor rises from his bed and sits on the polished stones before his doors to give his orders for the sacrifice to Athena (3.404–429; see [[nestor]]); at the second, Telemachus and Peisistratus yoke the horses at Pherae and drive on across the plain toward Sparta (3.491–497; see [[chariot-journey]] and [[the-journey-of-telemachus]]).

**At dawn.** ἠῶθεν, “at dawn”, marks two departures in the book: half the army drags its ships down to the sea at dawn after the drunken assembly (3.153; see [[the-returns-of-the-achaeans]]), and Athena, in the likeness of Mentor, says that she will go to the Caucones at dawn (3.366; see [[caucones]]). As in the Iliad's notes, the hour is not counted to the goddess.
