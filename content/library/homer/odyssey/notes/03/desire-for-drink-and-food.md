---
summary: The formula ends both feasts of the book, and each time Nestor begins to speak: to question the strangers on the shore (3.67–70), and to send Telemachus on with horses and chariot (3.473–476).
---

The verse marks the moment for speech. Nestor says that now “it is more fitting to question and to ask / the strangers who they are, now that they have taken their fill of food” (3.69–70): the guest is fed before he is questioned ([[visit-scene]], [[xenia]], [[strangers-who-are-you]]). Merry takes κάλλιον as “more proper” than it would have been to question them before (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 3.69). See [[feast-type-scene]].
