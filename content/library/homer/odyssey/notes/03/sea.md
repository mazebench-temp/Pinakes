---
summary: The sea of Book 3 is the sea of the returns: smoothed by a god and measured in a single run on Nestor's swift voyage, and the sea that wrecked Menelaus' ships, so great that not even the birds come back from it within the year (3.105, 3.142, 3.153–179, 3.192, 3.286–300, 3.321–322). Telemachus has sailed over it to ask whether his father died on land or at sea (3.15, 3.90–91).
---

**The sea of the question.** Athena reminds Telemachus that this is why he has sailed over the sea, to learn of his father (3.15–16). He asks Nestor whether Odysseus “was brought down on the mainland by hostile men / or on the open sea among the waves of Amphitrite” (3.90–91; see [[amphitrite]] and [[hostile-men]]), and Nestor's own question to the strangers is whether they wander at random “over the sea, like pirates” (3.73; see [[prexis]]).

**The sea of the returns.** Nestor's war was fought from the ships that roamed “over the misty sea” (3.105; see [[misty]]), and Menelaus' counsel was to think of the homecoming “over the broad back of the sea” (3.142; see [[broad-back-of-the-sea]]). The ships were dragged down “into the shining sea” (3.153; see [[shining-sea]]), and “a god made smooth the vast-gulfed sea” (3.158): μεγακήτεα, which Merry renders “gulfy”, full of deep hollows, from κῆτος (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 3.158); the Iliad gives the same adjective to ships and to the dolphin (see the translation's table of key words). From Lesbos the god's sign sent the ships across the open sea, πέλαγος, a word the Iliad has only once (*Iliad* 14.16): “cut across the middle of the open sea to Euboea”, over “the fish-filled ways”, and they landed at Geraestus, “having measured the great open sea” (3.174–179; see [[fish-filled]], [[euboea]] and [[geraestus]]). Merry, on the fish-filled ways, recalls that fish were no part of the heroes' food except under the pressure of hunger (on 3.177).

**The sea that takes men.** Idomeneus brought all his men home, “and the sea took none of them from him” (3.192; see [[idomeneus]]): πόντος, the open sea, as a power that robs. Menelaus went “over the wine-dark sea” to Malea, where the waves swelled huge, like mountains, and broke his ships on the reefs of Crete (3.286–299; see [[wine-dark]], [[malea]] and [[crete]]), and Nestor sends Telemachus to him as to a man come home from a sea “so great and terrible” that not even the birds come back from it within the year (3.321–322; see [[egypt]]).
