---
summary: “Telemachus, what a word has escaped the barrier of your teeth!” (3.230): Athena's rebuke answers Telemachus' “I do not think this word will ever be fulfilled” (3.226). Aegisthus bewitched Agamemnon's wife “with many words” (3.264).
---

ἔπος is the word that may or may not come true. Telemachus will not believe Nestor's hope, οὔ πω τοῦτο ἔπος τελέεσθαι ὀίω (3.226; see [[it-will-be-fulfilled]]), and the goddess rebukes the word he has let escape (3.230; see [[what-a-word]] and [[a-god-can-save-a-man-from-afar]]). In the appeal to Nestor the phrase ἢ ἔπος ἠέ τι ἔργον, “any word or deed”, is in Merry's view a hendiadys, the word-foreshadowed act (*Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 3.99; see [[word-or-deed]]). Words also beguile and quarrel: Aegisthus θέλγεσκʼ ἐπέεσσιν, “kept bewitching” Clytemnestra “with many words” (3.264; see [[clytemnestra]]), and the sons of Atreus stood answering each other “with harsh words” (3.148).
