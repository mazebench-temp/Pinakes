---
summary: Nestor tells how for nine years the Achaeans stitched evils for the Trojans “with tricks of every kind”, and how Odysseus far surpassed all “in tricks of every kind” (3.118–122); Athena says that Agamemnon perished “by the guile of Aegisthus and of his own wife” (3.235).
---

**The war of tricks.** Nestor makes the nine years at Troy a war of δόλοι: εἰνάετες γάρ σφιν κακὰ ῥάπτομεν ἀμφιέποντες / παντοίοισι δόλοισι (3.118–119). Merry renders κακὰ ῥάπτομεν “devised mischief”, reports Eustathius' remark that the phrase carries no blame, though its compound κακορραφίη does, and compares ὑφαίνειν δόλον, “weave a trick” (*Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 3.118). The phrase returns two lines on for Odysseus, who “far surpassed them / in tricks of every kind” (3.121–122; see [[nike]]), so that no man would match himself with him “in counsel” (3.120; see [[metis]] and [[odysseus]]). The trick that ended the war is the wooden horse, the δόλος that Odysseus brought into the citadel filled with men, as he says himself when he asks Demodocus for the song (8.492–495).

**The guile of Aegisthus.** The other δόλος is the murder at home: Agamemnon perished ὑπʼ Αἰγίσθοιο δόλῳ καὶ ἧς ἀλόχοιο (3.235), the preposition governing both genitives and δόλῳ a circumstantial dative (Merry on 3.235). Athena's line makes the wife a partner in the guile, as Nestor's tale will show her yielding to Aegisthus' words (3.263–272; see [[clytemnestra]] and [[the-murder-of-agamemnon]]). Aegisthus' epithet in the book is δολόμητις, “scheming” (3.198, 3.250, 3.308; see [[aegisthus]]).
