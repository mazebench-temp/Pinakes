---
summary: Athena, in the likeness of Mentor, approves Nestor's offer to keep Telemachus in his house: “it is fitting / that Telemachus should obey you, since it is much better so.”
refs: [3.357-358]
---

Nestor will not have the son of Odysseus sleep on the deck of a ship while he lives and his sons are left in the halls “to entertain the guests, whoever comes to my house” (3.352–355; see [[xenia]] and [[nestor]]). Athena, as Mentor, agrees: “You have spoken well in this, dear old man; and it is fitting / that Telemachus should obey you, since it is much better so” (3.357–358; see [[mentor]]). The clause has the shape of Nestor's own counsel to Agamemnon and Achilles, “So you too obey, since it is better to obey” (ἐπεὶ πείθεσθαι ἄμεινον, *Iliad* 1.274; see [[obedience-and-persuasion]]); here it is said to the old man, of the young one.
