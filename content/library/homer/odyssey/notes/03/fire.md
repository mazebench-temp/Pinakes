---
summary: Fire carries the offerings of the book's two sacrifices: at sunset on the shore the tongues of the victims are thrown into the fire and libations poured over them (3.341), and at the sacrifice to Athena Nestor throws hairs from the heifer's head into the fire and burns the thigh-pieces on split wood (3.446, 3.459).
---

**The tongues.** At the end of the day on the shore, “they threw the tongues into the fire, and standing up poured libations over them” (3.341; see [[the-sacrifice-at-pylos]] and [[libation]]). The company rose, W. W. Merry suggests, and went to the hearth to pour the wine on the burning offerings (*Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 3.341).

**The hair and the thigh-pieces.** At the sacrifice to Athena Nestor prays “as he made the first offering, throwing hairs from the victim's head into the fire” (3.446; see [[the-sacrifice-to-athena]]), the first share of the victim given to the god before it is struck. When the heifer has been killed, the old man burns the thigh-pieces “on split wood” and pours wine over them (3.459–460; see [[thigh-pieces]] and [[sacrifice-type-scene]]); the slave women had been told to bring wood (ξύλα, 3.429; see [[handmaids]]). On the shore the day before the burning was already under way when the strangers arrived (3.9).
