---
summary: εὐρυμέτωπον, “broad of brow”, describes the heifer of Nestor's vow to Athena (3.382), as it describes Diomedes' heifer in the Iliad.
---

The vow gives the heifer three epithets in two verses, βοῦν ἦνιν εὐρυμέτωπον, / ἀδμήτην, “a yearling heifer, broad of brow, / unbroken” (3.382–383; see [[yearling]], [[unbroken]] and [[cattle]], where the vow is quoted), the verses of Diomedes' vow (*Iliad* 10.292–293). It is the only use of the word in this book, and the heifer it describes is fetched from the plain and sacrificed the next morning (3.421–455). In the later books of the Odyssey it describes the herds of Iphiclus, the cattle of the Sun and the cattle that Philoetius keeps for Odysseus (11.289, 12.262, 12.355, 20.212; see the entry).
