---
summary: Athena, as Mentor, would rather suffer many sorrows and “come home and see the day of my homecoming” than perish at the hearth, as Agamemnon did (3.232–235).
---

The verse is the one Odysseus speaks to Calypso and to Nausicaa (5.220, 8.466), here in the mouth of the goddess in Mentor's likeness, set against Agamemnon, who “perished by the guile of Aegisthus and of his own wife” (3.235; [[the-murder-of-agamemnon]], [[nostos]]).
