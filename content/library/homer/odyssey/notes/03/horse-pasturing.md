---
summary: Aegisthus sat “undisturbed, in a corner of horse-pasturing Argos” (3.263).
---

μυχῷ Ἄργεος ἱπποβότοιο: Merry discusses whether Argos here is the whole Peloponnese, or, with Ernst Curtius, Agamemnon's kingdom, Mycenae standing at the innermost corner of the plain of Inachus, a view which he finds the epithet supports, since the plain of Inachus is fertile while Argos at large is πολυδίψιον, “thirsty” (*Iliad* 4.171; Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 3.263; [[argos]], [[mycenae]], [[aegisthus]]).
