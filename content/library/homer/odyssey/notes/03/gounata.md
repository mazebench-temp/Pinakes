---
summary: “That is why I come now to your knees” (3.92): Telemachus' appeal to Nestor for news of his father.
grc: [γούναθʼ]
---

The words of a suppliant without the gesture: Telemachus does not clasp the old man's knees, he speaks of coming to them (see [[supplication]]), and goes on with the suppliant's appeal to past favors, “I beg you, if ever my brave father Odysseus / promised you any word or deed and fulfilled it” (3.98–99; see [[word-or-deed]]). Merry compares the formula of adjuration with Virgil's “si qua tuis unquam” (*Aeneid* 9.404) (*Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 3.98). Telemachus says the whole appeal again to Menelaus (4.322–331).
