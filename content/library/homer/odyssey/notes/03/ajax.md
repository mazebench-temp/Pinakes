---
summary: Nestor names him first among the best of the Achaeans who lie dead at Troy, “There lies warlike Ajax, there Achilles” (3.109).
---

ἔνθα μὲν Αἴας κεῖται ἀρήιος, “There lies warlike Ajax” (3.109; see [[warlike]]). Αἴας without a patronymic always means the son of Telamon, Merry notes (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 3.109), and he is the Ajax who died at Troy: the son of Oileus perished at sea on the way home, as Menelaus will tell (4.499–511, a plain rendering; see [[ajax-son-of-oileus]]). Nestor gives no cause of his death. In the underworld the shade of Ajax stands apart, still angry over the arms of Achilles (11.543–564; see [[achilles]] and [[the-returns-of-the-achaeans]]).
