---
summary: Athena sends Telemachus to learn from Nestor “what counsel he keeps hidden in his breast” (3.18), and Nestor says that at Troy “no man ever wished to match himself with him in counsel to his face”, since Odysseus far surpassed all in tricks (3.120–122). Odysseus is ποικιλομήτης, “of subtle wiles” (3.163).
grc: [μῆτιν]
---

**Counsel hidden and shown.** Athena's charge is εἴδομεν ἥν τινα μῆτιν ἐνὶ στήθεσσι κέκευθε (3.18), where Merry takes the subjunctive εἴδομεν as one of will, “let us learn” (*Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 3.18). The old man's counsel is what Telemachus came for (see [[nestor]] and [[old-age-and-memory]]), but the μῆτις he speaks of is another's: “There no man ever wished to match himself with him in counsel to his face, / since brilliant Odysseus far surpassed them / in tricks of every kind” (3.120–122; see [[dolos]]). Merry notes that ἤθελε here implies a decision, “no one chose to match himself” (on 3.120). The two men, Nestor says, “never spoke at odds, in the assembly or in the council, / but with one heart, with mind and thoughtful counsel, / we considered for the Argives how all might turn out best by far” (3.127–129; see [[boule]] and [[noos]]).

**Of subtle wiles.** At Tenedos Odysseus turns back to Agamemnon, ἀμφʼ Ὀδυσῆα ἄνακτα δαΐφρονα, ποικιλομήτην, “with lord Odysseus, wise-hearted, of subtle wiles” (3.163; see [[of-subtle-wiles]] and [[wise-hearted]]). Aegisthus' δολόμητις, “scheming” (3.198, 3.250, 3.308), joins μῆτις to the δόλος of the murder (see [[aegisthus]]).
