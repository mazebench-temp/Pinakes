---
summary: The housekeeper puts bread and wine in Telemachus' chariot, “and meats, such as kings whom Zeus has nurtured eat” (3.479–480).
---

The διοτρεφέες βασιλῆες of the Iliad (see [[zeus-nurtured]]) are named here by their fare: ὄψα, “meats”, which Merry glosses “flesh-meat”, properly what is cooked, as against the bread, σῖτον (*Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 3.480; see the translation's table of key words). The kings of the book are the chiefs of Nestor's tale (3.130–198) and the two hosts of Telemachus' journey, Nestor and Menelaus (see [[basileus]] and [[kingship-and-authority]]).
