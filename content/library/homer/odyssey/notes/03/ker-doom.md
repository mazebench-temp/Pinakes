summary: Telemachus says that “the immortals have devised for him death and black doom” (3.242), and Neleus, who sat on the polished stones before Nestor, “had already been overcome by his doom and gone to the house of Hades” (3.410).
grc: ["κηρὶ δαμεὶς"]
---

θάνατον καὶ κῆρα μέλαιναν is the phrase of Athena's warning to the suitors in Book 2 (2.283; see [[black-doom]]), turned now by Telemachus on his father, as his answer to Athena's maxim that not even the gods can ward off death (3.236–242; see [[not-even-the-gods-can-ward-off-death]] and [[thanatos]]). The second κήρ is the doom of an old king: ἀλλʼ ὁ μὲν ἤδη κηρὶ δαμεὶς Ἄϊδόσδε βεβήκει (3.410), of Neleus, whose seat Nestor now takes (see [[neleus]] and [[hades]]), the verse that tells of Nausithous, the father of Alcinous, in Book 6 (6.11).
