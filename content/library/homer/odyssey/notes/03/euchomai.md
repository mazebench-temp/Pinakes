---
summary: The book's prayers: Peisistratus bids the stranger pray to Poseidon (3.43–48), Athena “prayed earnestly to lord Poseidon” (3.54–62), and Nestor prays to Athena with a vow (3.380–385), at the libation (3.393–394) and at the sacrifice (3.445–447). In Mentor's likeness Athena says of Telemachus' companions, “I claim to be the only older man among them” (3.362).
---

**Pray.** Merry takes εὔχεσθαι at 3.48 as “a worshipper”: Peisistratus thinks that the younger stranger also prays to the immortals, as all men do (*Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 3.48; see [[all-men-need-the-gods]] and [[prayer]]). The goddess prays in disguise and answers her own prayer (3.55–62; see [[athena]]); Nestor's prayer, with the vow of a heifer with gilded horns, ends “So he spoke in prayer, and Pallas Athena heard him” (3.385, as *Iliad* 5.121; see [[reciprocity-with-the-gods]]); and at the sacrifice he prays as he makes the first offering (3.445–446; see [[sacrifice-type-scene]]).

**Claim.** οἶος γὰρ μετὰ τοῖσι γεραίτερος εὔχομαι εἶναι (3.362) is the other sense, “claim to be”, which the prayer entry leaves aside (see the note on Book 1 and [[geron]]).
