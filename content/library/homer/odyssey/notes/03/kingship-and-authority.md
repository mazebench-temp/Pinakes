summary: Pylos has the king that Ithaca lacks: Nestor “has been king for three generations of men” (3.245) and sits at dawn on the polished stones of Neleus, “holding the scepter”, with his sons around him (3.405–415). He asks whether the people “throughout the land” hate Telemachus (3.214–215); and in Mycenae the people were made subject to Aegisthus, who ruled seven years until Orestes came back (3.304–306).
refs: [3.214-217, 3.245, 3.304-306, 3.405-415]
---

**The old king.** Nestor sacrifices with his people in their nine companies (3.4–8; see [[the-sacrifice-at-pylos]]), has ruled through three generations (3.245; see [[genee]] and [[anax]]), and in the morning sits in his father's place before his doors with the scepter, “the Gerenian, the guardian of the Achaeans” (3.405–412; see [[scepter]], [[neleus]] and [[nestor]]). His sons gather round him and carry out his orders (3.412–416, 3.430–454).

**The king's son and the people.** Nestor's question to Telemachus is the question of Ithaca: “tell me, do you submit willingly, or do the people / throughout the land hate you, following the voice of a god?” (3.214–215; see [[laos]] and [[demos]]). Merry rejects the view of Nitzsch and Eustathius that the voice of a god means an oracle or omen for setting a king aside, a practice for which there is scanty evidence: dislike of a king is not deposing him, and would not wait for a divine sanction. The voice is the unspoken, contagious, unanimous feeling of a multitude, hatred of a prince stifling all loyalty without breaking into rebellion (*Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 3.214).

**The usurper.** Mycenae shows the other kingship: Aegisthus killed the son of Atreus, “and the people were made subject to him; / and for seven years he ruled over Mycenae rich in gold” (3.304–305), until Orestes came back in the eighth (3.306; see [[aegisthus]], [[orestes]] and the translation's note on 3.303–305).
