summary: The thigh-pieces are burned for the god at every sacrifice in the book: on the shore at Pylos, at Geraestus, by Aegisthus after his success, and at Nestor's sacrifice to Athena, where they are cut out, wrapped in fat and burned with wine.
# The phrases of Book 3 (a bare μηρ* would catch the thighs of men, μηρούς, Iliad 10.573); κατὰ μῆρʼ ἐκάη again 12.364.
grc: ["μηρίʼ ἔκαιον", "μῆρʼ ἔθεμεν", "μηρίʼ ἔκηε", "μηρία τάμνον", "κατὰ μῆρʼ ἐκάη"]

On the shore the Pylians had tasted the entrails “and were burning the thigh-pieces on the altar for the god” (3.9; see [[the-sacrifice-at-pylos]]); at Geraestus Nestor's men “laid many thigh-pieces on the altar” for Poseidon (3.179; see [[geraestus]]); and Aegisthus “burned many thigh-pieces on the holy altars of the gods” (3.273; see [[aegisthus]]). At Nestor's sacrifice they “cut out the thigh-pieces / all in good order, and wrapped them in fat, / making a double fold, and laid raw flesh upon them” (3.456–458), as at Chryse (*Iliad* 1.460–461), and only “when the thigh-pieces were burned up and they had tasted the entrails” do they cut up the rest for the spits (3.461–462; see [[the-sacrifice-to-athena]] and [[sacrifice-type-scene]]).
