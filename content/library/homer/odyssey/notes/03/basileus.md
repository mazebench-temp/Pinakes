summary: The only βασιλῆες of the book are the “kings whom Zeus has nurtured”, whose fare the housekeeper puts in Telemachus' chariot (3.480). Nestor's rule is told with another word, ἀνάσσω: he “has been king for three generations of men” (3.245).
---

The noun names a class (see [[kings]] and [[zeus-nurtured]]); the rule of particular men is told with ἀνάσσω, Nestor's over three generations (3.245; see [[anax]] and [[genee]]) and Aegisthus' over Mycenae for seven years (3.305; see [[kingship-and-authority]]). Nestor sits in his king's place in the morning, on the polished stones before his doors, “holding the scepter” (3.405–412; see [[scepter]] and [[nestor]]).
