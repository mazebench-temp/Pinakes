---
summary: Hesiod's poems fill in what Book 3 leaves untold. The Catalogue of Women gives Telemachus and Nestor's daughter Polycaste a son, Persepolis, and tells how Nestor alone of Neleus' sons escaped Heracles, a guest among the Gerenians; the Theogony makes Amphitrite, whose waves the book names, a daughter of Nereus, and gives Cottus, promising his help to Zeus, the “mind and thoughtful counsel” that Nestor claims for himself and Odysseus.
---

- **Polycaste.** The *Catalogue of Women* made Nestor's youngest daughter, who bathes Telemachus in this book (3.464–465), the mother of his son: “And to Telemachus well-girdled Polycaste, / the youngest daughter of Nestor, son of Neleus, / bore Persepolis, joined with him through golden Aphrodite” (fr. 12 Evelyn-White, quoted by Eustathius on Homer, p. 1796.39; a plain rendering). The second verse is the Odyssey's (3.465), with κούρη, “girl”, for θυγάτηρ, “daughter”. The son's name, from πέρθω, “sack”, and πόλις, “city”, is the sense of the epithet of his grandfather Odysseus, πτολίπορθος, “sacker of cities”. See [[polycaste]], [[telemachus]] and [[sacker-of-cities]].
- **The Gerenian.** Nestor is “the Gerenian horseman” throughout this book (3.68, 3.102, 3.386 and often), and “the Gerenian” alone as he sits on the stones of Neleus (3.411). The *Catalogue* told how Heracles “killed the noble sons of steadfast Neleus, / eleven of them; the twelfth, Gerenian Nestor the horseman, / happened to be a guest among the horse-taming Gerenians”, and how “Nestor alone escaped, in flowery Gerenus” (fr. 11 Evelyn-White, quoted by Stephanus of Byzantium under Gerenia; plain renderings). Merry reports that Nestor was said to have been brought up at Gerenia, on the Messenian gulf, or to have taken refuge there when Heracles ravaged Pylos (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 3.68). See [[gerenian-horseman]], [[neleus]] and [[heracles-sacks-pylos]].
- **Amphitrite.** Telemachus cannot tell whether his father perished on the mainland or “on the open sea among the waves of Amphitrite” (3.90–91). Hesiod names Amphitrite among the daughters of Nereus and Doris, and makes her, by Poseidon, the mother of Triton (*Theogony* 243, 930–933). See [[amphitrite]].
- **Thoughtful counsel.** Nestor says that he and Odysseus at Troy were “with one heart, with mind and thoughtful counsel” (νόῳ καὶ ἐπίφρονι βουλῇ, 3.128), a phrase found nowhere else in Homer. In the *Theogony* the hundred-handed Cottus promises Zeus that “with unbending mind and thoughtful counsel” (ἀτενεῖ τε νόῳ καὶ ἐπίφρονι βουλῇ) the three brothers will defend his power against the Titans (*Theogony* 661–663). See [[boule]] and [[platos-nestor]].
