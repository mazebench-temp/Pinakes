summary: Idomeneus brought all his companions home to Crete, and the sea took none of them (3.191–192); Menelaus' fleet, split by the storm off Malea, lost some of its ships on the rocks of the island's south coast, at the edge of Gortyn toward Phaestus, though the men escaped (3.291–300).

**Idomeneus' return.** Among the homecomings that Nestor has heard of in his halls is that of the Cretan king: “And Idomeneus brought all his companions back to Crete, / all who had escaped from the war, and the sea took none of them from him” (3.191–192; see [[idomeneus]], [[cretans]] and [[the-returns-of-the-achaeans]]). It is told with those of the Myrmidons and of Philoctetes, the safe returns, before Nestor comes to Agamemnon (3.188–194).

**The wreck.** The storm that broke on Menelaus off Malea split his fleet (see [[malea]]):

The passage moves from the Cydonians and their river to Gortyn and Phaestus on the south coast (see [[cydonians]], [[iardanus-in-crete]], [[gortyn]] and [[phaestus]]), and the commentators have had to fit the wreck to the map. Merry rejects two answers, redistributing the peoples of Crete, or making the sailors put to sea again after entering the Cydonian bay, and supposes instead that the ships neared Crete at the western end of the south coast, at the promontory called Κριοῦ μέτωπον, “the Ram's Forehead”, and drifted along that coast until they were wrecked on the cliffs where it turns south (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 3.292, 3.293). The men escape σπουδῇ, “with difficulty”, “with much ado” in Merry's words (3.297; Merry on 3.297), but the ships are lost; the five that the wind carries to Egypt take Menelaus on to his years of wandering (see [[egypt]] and [[the-wanderings-of-menelaus]]).
