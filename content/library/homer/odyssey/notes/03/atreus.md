---
summary: Atreus is named only in his sons' patronymic: Ἀτρεΐδης is Agamemnon (3.193, 3.248, 3.268, 3.304) and Menelaus (3.257, 3.277), and Ἀτρεΐδῃσι the two together (3.136).
---

Ἀτρεΐδης, “the son of Atreus”, is used of both brothers in this book, and only the context tells which: Agamemnon, whose death Telemachus asks about and who charged the singer to guard his wife (3.193, 3.248, 3.268, 3.304), and Menelaus, who would have killed Aegisthus “coming from Troy” and who sailed with Nestor (3.257, 3.277; see [[agamemnon]], [[menelaus]] and [[son-of-atreus]]). Athena's wrath “set strife between the two sons of Atreus”, Ἀτρεΐδῃσι μετʼ ἀμφοτέροισιν (3.136; see [[atreidae]] and [[the-departure-of-the-greeks]]). The book tells nothing of Atreus himself.
