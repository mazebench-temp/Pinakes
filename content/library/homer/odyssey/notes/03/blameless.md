---
summary: Nestor remembers his son among the dead at Troy: “my own dear son, at once strong and noble, / Antilochus” (3.111–112).
---

ἅμα κρατερὸς καὶ ἀμύμων joins strength to excellence ([[krateros]]), and the next verse adds his speed and his fighting, “surpassingly swift at running, and a fighter” (3.112; [[swift]]). The translation keeps “noble” for ἀμύμων of persons, as in the Iliad. See [[antilochus]].
