---
summary: Telemachus says of his father that “beyond all men his mother bore him to misery” (3.95); the suitors devise evils “for your mother's sake” (3.212); and Orestes gave the Argives a funeral feast “for his hateful mother and for the coward Aegisthus” (3.310).
grc: [μητέρος]
---

Two mothers stand behind the book's two sons. Telemachus' is the cause of the suitors' presence in his house (3.212–213; see [[penelope]] and [[the-suitors]]); Orestes' shares the funeral feast with her lover, μητρός τε στυγερῆς, “his hateful mother” (3.310; see [[orestes-kills-clytemnestra]] and [[clytemnestra]]). The scholia, as Merry reports, said that 3.309–310 were missing in some editions, and Aristarchus held that the lines hint that Clytemnestra died with Aegisthus, though whether by Orestes' hand is not clear (*Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 3.309; see the translation's note on 3.309–310 and [[mothers-and-sons]]).
