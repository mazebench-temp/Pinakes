summary: The companions of the book: Nestor's companions roast meat at the feast (3.32–33); Diomedes rouses his and they moor at Argos (3.167, 3.180–182); Idomeneus brings all his back to Crete (3.191–192); Menelaus stays at Sunium to bury his helmsman, “his companion” (3.285); and Telemachus' companions wait at the ship, where Athena goes to encourage them (3.323, 3.361, 3.424, 3.432).
grc: [ἕταροι, ἕταρον]
---

The returns are counted by companions: Idomeneus lost none to the sea (3.191–192), and Menelaus, eager though he is for his journey, stops at Sunium to bury his helmsman Phrontis (3.284–285; see [[phrontis-son-of-onetor]] and [[honoring-the-dead]]). Telemachus' crew are “younger men who follow out of friendship” with the goddess in Mentor's likeness, “all of an age with great-hearted Telemachus” (3.363–364; see [[mentor]] and [[geron]]); Nestor sends for them to share in the sacrifice, all but two (3.423–424, 3.431–432).
