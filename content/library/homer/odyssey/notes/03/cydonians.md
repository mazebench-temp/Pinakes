---
summary: Some of Menelaus' ships are driven to Crete, “where the Cydonians lived about the streams of the Iardanus” (3.292).
---

The Cydonians are named as the people of the coast where the storm brings the ships, by their river, the Iardanus (3.291–292; see [[iardanus-in-crete]] and [[crete]]). Merry, who places their city and the river on the north coast, reconciles the place with the wreck on the south coast, at the edge of the land of Gortyn, by giving the people the whole breadth of the west of the island, as far as the southern cape; Homer, he notes, does not name their city (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 3.292; see [[gortyn]]). Strabo's report that Staphylus gave the west of Crete to the Cydonians agrees with him (*Geography* 10.4.6). In the island's catalogue of peoples, later in the poem, they come third, after the Achaeans and the Eteocretans (19.175–177).
