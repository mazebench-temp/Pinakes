---
summary: Cato's reason why Nestor may praise his own virtues, that “he was seeing the third generation of men”, is the reason Telemachus gives in this book for putting his question to the old king, who “has been king for three generations of men”.
refs: [3.244-245]
---

In the passage on Nestor's honeyed speech, Cicero's Cato explains why the old man could speak of his own virtues without seeming arrogant or talkative: “for he was seeing the third generation of men” (*tertiam enim aetatem hominum videbat*, *On Old Age* 31). The Iliad's verses that introduce Nestor make him king among the third generation (*Iliad* 1.250–252). In this book Telemachus turns to him, “since beyond all others he knows judgments and wisdom; / for they say he has been king for three generations of men” (3.244–245; see [[nestors-three-generations]]).
