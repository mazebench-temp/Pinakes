# The basin in its phrase (3.440), as the Book 1 note's "ἀργυρέοιο λέβητος"; flowering claims ἀνθεμόεντι.
grc: ["ἀνθεμόεντι λέβητι"]
---

“And Aretus brought them water for the hands in a flowered basin, / coming from the chamber” (3.440–441; see [[aretus-son-of-nestor]], [[hand-washing]] and [[the-sacrifice-to-athena]]). The basin is a λέβης, “damasked with flowers”, as the scholiast explains ἀνθεμόεντι, and it had been kept in a storeroom and was brought out for the occasion, W. W. Merry notes (*Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 3.440; see [[flowering]]). At the meal in the first book a handmaid poured the water from a golden pitcher over a silver basin (1.136–138).
