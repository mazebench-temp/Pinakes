---
summary: The Laws quotes Athena's counsel to Telemachus on the shore of Pylos for the young citizens; the Phaedrus jokes about the handbooks of speech that Nestor and Odysseus, the two counselors of this book, wrote in their leisure at Troy; and in the Hippias Major the sophist's Nestor advises the young Neoptolemus after the fall of Troy, as this book's Nestor advises Telemachus.
---

- “Telemachus, some things you will think of yourself in your own mind, / and in others a god too will give you counsel”: the young must likewise think that what has been said is enough, and that the god and the daemon will suggest the rest (*Laws* 7.803e–804a, quoting 3.26–28): [[athena-as-mentor-in-later-writers]] and [[some-things-you-will-think-of-yourself]].
- The “arts of speech of Nestor and Odysseus, which the two of them composed in their leisure at Troy” (*Phaedrus* 261b–c), the pair who, as Nestor says here, never spoke at odds in the assembly or in the council (3.126–129): [[platos-nestor]].
- Nestor, who surpassed all men in the strength of his speech and still more in self-control (*Laws* 4.711d–e), and Hippias' speech in which Nestor, after the fall of Troy, tells Neoptolemus how a young man may win the greatest fame (*Hippias Major* 286a–b; on 3.188–200): [[platos-nestor]].
