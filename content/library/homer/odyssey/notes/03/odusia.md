---
summary: One of the surviving lines of the Latin Odyssey renders a line of Book 3, Patroclus among the dead whom Nestor names at Troy. Gellius quotes it for the word adprimus, “by far the first”, which stands where Homer calls Patroclus “a counselor equal to the gods”.
refs: [3.110]
---

The line is given as The Latin Library prints it, with the line of Book 3 that it sets beside it. Its source is Gellius. The poet Annianus held that the prefix *ad-* takes the acute accent when it strengthens a word, as in *admodum*; Gellius answers that in *adpotus*, *adprimus* and *adprime* it strengthens the word without the acute, and adds that Livius in his *Odyssia* uses *adprimus* for “by far the first” (*longe primum*), in this verse (*Attic Nights* 6.7.5–11).

- ***ibidemque vir summus adprimus Patroclus.*** “and there too the man highest and first of all, Patroclus” (3.110): Nestor's roll of the best of the Achaeans who were killed at Troy, “There lies warlike Ajax, there Achilles, / there Patroclus, a counselor equal to the gods” (3.109–110; see [[patroclus]]). *Ibidem*, “in the same place”, answers Homer's ἔνθα, “there”, the word with which each of Nestor's three verses on the dead begins (3.109–111). Where Homer has θεόφιν μήστωρ ἀτάλαντος, “a counselor equal to the gods”, the Latin has two superlatives, *summus*, “highest”, and *adprimus*, and leaves out both the counsel and the gods (see [[counselor-equal-to-the-gods]]).
