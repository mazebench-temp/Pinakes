---
summary: Among the dead who lie at Troy Nestor names “Patroclus, a counselor equal to the gods” (3.110).
---

ἔνθα δὲ Πάτροκλος, θεόφιν μήστωρ ἀτάλαντος, “there Patroclus, a counselor equal to the gods” (3.110; see [[counselor-equal-to-the-gods]]). The half-verse is the Iliad's for Patroclus in Automedon's words after his death, “except Patroclus, a counselor equal to the gods, / while he lived” (*Iliad* 17.477–478), and for Priam and Peirithous (*Iliad* 7.366, 14.318); this book gives it again to Neleus, who sat on the polished stones before Nestor (3.409; see [[neleus]]). He lies at Troy with Ajax and Achilles (3.109; see [[achilles]] and [[ajax]]).
