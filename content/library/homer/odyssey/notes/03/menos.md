---
summary: Nestor calls the Achaeans at Troy “uncontrollable in our might” (μένος ἄσχετοι, 3.104), and Thrasymedes' axe “loosed the strength of the heifer” (3.450).
---

μένος ἄσχετοι is the phrase of Antinous' taunt to Telemachus, μένος ἄσχετε, “uncontrollable in your fury” (2.85, 2.303; see the translation's table of key words); Nestor uses it of the army that suffered at Troy (3.103–104). At the sacrifice the blow of the axe loosens the heifer's μένος, which Merry takes of stunning, not killing: the heifer dies only when Peisistratus cuts her throat (3.453–455; *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 3.450; see [[ololyge]]).
