---
summary: Telemachus sleeps “beneath the loud-resounding colonnade” of Nestor's house (3.399), and at dawn the two young men drive out from the doorway and “the loud-resounding colonnade” of Diocles' (3.493).
---

αἰθούσῃ ἐριδούπῳ (3.399) is in the verse of the guest's bed, used again of Odysseus at the house of Alcinous (7.345; [[corded]]), and αἰθούσης ἐριδούπου (3.493) in the verse of the departure, which comes again when the young men leave Sparta and Pherae on their way home (15.146, 15.191; [[chariot-journey]]). The colonnade is where a guest sleeps ([[house]], [[xenia]]).
