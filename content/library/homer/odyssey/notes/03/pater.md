summary: Telemachus has sailed to learn “of your father” (3.15–16) and asks Nestor for news of “my brave father Odysseus” (3.98); Orestes took vengeance on the man who “had killed his famous father” (3.198, 3.308). Peisistratus seats the strangers beside his brother “and his father” (3.39), and Athena honored Telemachus' “brave father among the Argives” (3.379).
grc: [πατέρι]
---

The father is the object of the voyage and the measure of the son: “your father, if truly / you are his offspring” (3.122–123; see [[fathers-and-sons]] and [[genee]]). Telemachus' answer to the example of Orestes includes his father in his misfortune, “for my father and for me” (3.209; see [[olbos]]), and the father-slayer, πατροφονῆα (3.197, 3.307), is the word of Athena's example in Book 1 (1.299; see [[orestes]] and [[aegisthus]]). At Pylos the sons gather round their father in the morning, as he sits on the polished stones where his own father Neleus used to sit (3.405–416; see [[neleus]] and [[nestor]]).
