---
summary: The two dawns of the book open with the whole verse, “And when early-born Dawn appeared, the rosy-fingered” (3.404, 3.491): the morning of the sacrifice at Pylos and the morning at Pherae.
---

As the entry describes, the next verse begins the day's action: the Gerenian horseman Nestor rises from his bed and goes out to the polished stones (3.405–406), and the two young men yoke the horses (3.492; [[chariot-journey]]). The book's first day began without the verse, with the sun rising from the Ocean stream (3.1–3; [[helios]]). See [[early-born]] and [[rosy-fingered]].
