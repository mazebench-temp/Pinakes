# Unnamed: "the youngest daughter of Nestor" and the bath (3.465-467).
grc: [Πολυκάστη]
refs: [3.465-467]
passages:
  - 3.464-469 | Polycaste bathes Telemachus
---

“Meanwhile lovely Polycaste bathed Telemachus, / the youngest daughter of Nestor, son of Neleus” (3.464–465; see [[telemachus]], [[nestor]] and [[bathing]]). λοῦσεν has the force of a pluperfect: while the others were roasting the meat, she had bathed him (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 3.464). She anoints him richly with olive oil and puts a fine mantle and a tunic around him, and “he stepped out of the bathtub, like the immortals in form; / and he went and sat down beside Nestor, shepherd of the people” (3.466–469; see [[cloak]] and [[tunic]]). On the part women take in the bathing of men in the Odyssey, from Helen's account of Odysseus to Eurycleia's washing of his feet, Merry gathers the passages and the discussion of Gladstone (on 3.465). ὁπλοτάτη is “youngest”, perhaps connected with ἁπαλός, “tender” (Merry on 3.465).
