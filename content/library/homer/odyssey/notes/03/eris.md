---
summary: Athena “set strife between the two sons of Atreus” (3.136), and at Tenedos Zeus “roused evil strife against us once more, a second time” (3.161).
grc: [ἔριν]
---

The strife of the returns comes from the gods twice. Athena's wrath sets it between the brothers (3.136), μετʼ Ἀτρεΐδῃσι, which Merry renders “between the sons of Atreus” (*Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 3.136; see [[the-departure-of-the-greeks]], [[menis]] and [[the-mind-of-the-gods-is-not-quickly-turned]]), and the army divides; at Tenedos Zeus, σχέτλιος, “the hard god”, rouses it again among those who sailed, and Odysseus turns back to Agamemnon (3.159–164; see [[zeus]], [[hard]] and [[the-returns-of-the-achaeans]]). Merry takes ἔπι as an adverb with ὦρσε, “sent upon us disastrous strife”, and σχέτλιος as from σχέσθαι, “holding with a firm grip”, like Latin *improbus* (on 3.161). As in the proem of the Iliad, where the son of Atreus and Achilles “stood apart in strife” and a god brought them together to fight (*Iliad* 1.6–8), the strife between kings is a god's doing.
