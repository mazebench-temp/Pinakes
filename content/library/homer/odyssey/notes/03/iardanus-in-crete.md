summary: The storm off Malea drives some of Menelaus' ships to Crete, “where the Cydonians lived about the streams of the Iardanus” (3.291–292).
# Ἰαρδάνου (3.292), in this poem only: the Iliad's Ἰαρδάνου (Il. 7.135) is the river of Elis (iardanus, which
# excepts Od. 3.292).
grc: [Ἰαρδάνου]
en: [Iardanus]
---

The river places the Cydonians, and through them the part of Crete to which the storm drives the ships: ἧχι, “where”, narrows Κρήτῃ, “to Crete”, to the part of the island where the Cydonians lived (3.291–292; Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 3.292; see [[cydonians]]). Merry puts the river and the city of the Cydonians on the north coast, while the wreck happens on the south coast, “at the far edge of Gortyn” (3.294): the Cydonians, he explains, held the breadth of the island and lay along the south coast as well (Merry on 3.292; see [[gortyn]] and [[crete]]). The half-verse Ἰαρδάνου ἀμφὶ ῥέεθρα is the one that ends Nestor's account of the battle of his youth by the Iardanus of Elis, “about the streams of the Iardanus” (*Iliad* 7.135; see [[iardanus]] and [[nestor-and-ereuthalion]]).
