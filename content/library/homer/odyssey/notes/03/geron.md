summary: Nestor is “the old man” of the book. Telemachus and Athena address him “Old man” (3.226, 3.331, 3.357); he marvels at the goddess's departure (3.373), mixes the wine (3.390, 3.393) and leads the sacrifice (3.436, 3.444, 3.459). The young man fears to question “an older one” (3.24), and Athena claims to be “the only older man” among Telemachus' companions (3.362).
grc: [γεραίτερον, γεραίτερος]
en: [older]
---

**The old man.** γέρων is Nestor's title in Book 3, in address and in the narrative. Telemachus calls him ὦ γέρον when he will not believe that the gods can help him (3.226), and Athena when she praises his tale and his care for his guest, γέρον φίλε, “dear old man” (3.331, 3.357). The narrator calls him ὁ γέρων at the mixing bowl and at the altar, and γέρων ἱππηλάτα Νέστωρ, “the old horse-driving Nestor” (3.390–393, 3.436, 3.444; see [[nestor]] and [[horse-driving]]); at the altar the verse is the Iliad's for the old priest at Chryse, καῖε δʼ ἐπὶ σχίζῃς ὁ γέρων (3.459 = *Iliad* 1.462; see [[sacrifice-type-scene]]).

**Old and young.** Age orders the book's speech and manners. The young man is ashamed to question an older one (3.24; see [[shame-for-a-young-man]]); Peisistratus gives the cup first to the stranger who looks older (3.49–50; see [[all-men-need-the-gods]]); and Athena, leaving for the ship, claims the elder's place among the young companions, “all of an age with great-hearted Telemachus” (3.362–364; see [[mentor]] and [[hetairos]]). Nestor's age is his authority: he has been king for three generations of men, and to Telemachus “he appears … like an immortal to look upon” (3.245–246; see [[genee]] and [[old-age-and-memory]]).
