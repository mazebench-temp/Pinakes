---
summary: The Achaeans “suffered sorrows” in the land of the Trojans (3.220), and Athena would rather come home “even after toiling through many sorrows” than die at her own hearth (3.232).
---

ἄλγεα πολλὰ μογήσας is the Odyssey's phrase for the man who comes home late: the old wine in Odysseus' storeroom waits for the day he “came home, even after toiling through many sorrows” (2.343), and Athena makes it her own choice (3.232–233; see [[a-god-can-save-a-man-from-afar]] and [[nostos]]). The sorrows of Troy are Nestor's subject, ὀιζύς and πήματα (3.100–103, 3.220; see [[the-returns-of-the-achaeans]]).
