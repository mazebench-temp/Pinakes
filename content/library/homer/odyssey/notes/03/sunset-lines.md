summary: Three sunsets: the assembly called “at the setting of the sun” (3.138), the end of Nestor's tale, “So he spoke, and the sun went down and darkness came on” (3.329), and the two days of the chariot journey, “And the sun set, and all the streets grew dark” (3.487, 3.497).
en: [the setting of the sun]
---

ἐς ἠέλιον καταδύντα: Merry takes the unseemliness to lie not in the hour, since morning was the usual time for assemblies, but in calling the men at that hour when they had been feasting and were heavy with wine (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 3.138; [[the-departure-of-the-greeks]]). The second sunset has the Iliad's half-verse ἐπὶ κνέφας ἦλθε after ἠέλιος δʼ ἄρʼ ἔδυ, in the verse that ends Odysseus' answer to Calypso in Book 5 (5.225), and Athena takes it as the signal for the libations and for bed: “For already the light has gone down under the darkness” (3.335; [[libation]]). The third is the Odyssey's sunset verse of 2.388, twice on the road (3.487, 3.497; [[chariot-journey]]).
