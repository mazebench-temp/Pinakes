# Unnamed: "my revered wife" in Nestor's prayer (3.381); "the lady his wife" (3.403); "the revered wife" (3.451).
grc: [Εὐρυδίκη]
refs: [3.381, 3.403, 3.451]
---

**The wife of the house.** Nestor prays to Athena for noble fame “to me and my children and my revered wife” (αἰδοίῃ παρακοίτι, 3.381), where παρακοίτι is accented as a contraction of παρακοίτιι (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 3.381). At night he sleeps “in the inner part of the high house, / and the lady his wife shared his bed and couch” (3.402–403; see [[bed]] and [[house]]). πόρσυνε is used always of the wife who shares the bed, Merry notes, since no one but the wife came freely into the husband's chamber, which lay at the back of the house (on 3.403).

**The cry.** When Thrasymedes' axe strikes the heifer, “the women raised the cry, / his daughters and his sons' wives and the revered wife / of Nestor, Eurydice, eldest of the daughters of Clymenus” (3.450–452; see [[clymenus]] and [[the-sacrifice-to-athena]]). The cry, ὀλόλυξαν, is not a shriek of horror but a religious shout of joy at the moment of the sacrifice (Merry on 3.450; see [[ololyge]]).
