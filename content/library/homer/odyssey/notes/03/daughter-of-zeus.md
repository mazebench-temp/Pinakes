---
summary: Athena is the daughter of Zeus four times: as Peisistratus addresses her (3.42), at the end of her speech at sunset, “So spoke the daughter of Zeus” (3.337), in Nestor's recognition, “but the daughter of Zeus, most glorious Tritogeneia” (3.378), and in his libation at night (3.394).
---

κούρην Διὸς αἰγιόχοιο (3.42) and κούρῃ Διὸς αἰγιόχοιο (3.394) frame her visit ([[aegis-bearing]]). Διὸς θυγάτηρ closes her last speech as Mentor, ἦ ῥα Διὸς θυγάτηρ (3.337; [[so-he-spoke]]), and opens Nestor's naming of her, in the words with which the Iliad names her when she rouses the Achaeans, Διὸς θυγάτηρ κυδίστη Τριτογένεια (3.378; *Iliad* 4.515; [[tritogeneia]], [[most-glorious]], [[seeing-the-gods]]).
