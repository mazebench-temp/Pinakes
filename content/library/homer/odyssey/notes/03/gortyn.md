---
summary: Some of Menelaus' ships are wrecked on “a smooth rock, steep to the sea, / at the far edge of Gortyn, in the misty sea” (3.293–294).
---

The place of the wreck is given by the land it borders: ἐσχατιῇ Γόρτυνος, “at the far edge of Gortyn” (3.294). Merry renders it “on the verge of the territory of Gortyn”, and compares ἐσχατιή to the Anglo-Saxon “mark” or “marches”, the waste land that bounded the divisions of a country (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 3.294). Gortyn itself lay inland, on the fertile plain bounded by Ida, and the cliff is the coast below it, between Phaestus at the head of the bay and the southern cape (Merry on 3.292; see [[phaestus]] and [[crete]]). The sea is the “misty” sea of the formula, ἠεροειδέι πόντῳ (3.294; see [[misty]] and [[sea]]).

Whether λισσή, “smooth”, is an epithet or a proper name was later in doubt, as Eustathius and Stephanus of Byzantium report, Merry says, and the name lies behind a reading in Strabo (on 3.293); Strabo's text names a Lissen in the territory of Phaestus (*Geography* 10.4.14). Merry also reports a tradition that the rock was called Maleon, kept in a reading ascribed to Zenodotus (on 3.293). The Catalogue had named Gortyn for its walls (*Iliad* 2.646; see [[walled]]); the Odyssey names it only for the edge of its land, where the sea meets the cliffs.
