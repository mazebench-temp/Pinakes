summary: Nestor's house has cloaks and fine blankets for its guests, and he will not have Telemachus sleep on his ship as if from a poor man's house (3.348–351); after the bath Polycaste puts a fine mantle and a tunic around Telemachus (3.467).
# χλαῖναι (3.349, 3.351), first met in the Odyssey here; φᾶρος (3.467) is the entry's, and "mantle" the Book 2 note's.
grc: [χλαῖναι]
en: [cloaks]
---

**Cloaks for the guests.** Nestor will not let his guests go from his house “as from a man altogether without clothing, and poor, / who has no cloaks and blankets in plenty in his house / for himself or his guests to sleep softly in. / But I have cloaks and fine blankets” (3.348–351; see [[blankets]] and [[xenia]]). The χλαῖνα here is bedding: W. W. Merry explains that it served as a counterpane laid over the sleeper, and was also thrown over chairs, besides being worn over the tunic in place of the φᾶρος (*Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 3.349; see [[bed]]). ἀνείμονος, “without clothing”, is the man who has no changes of raiment (Merry on 3.348).

**The mantle after the bath.** When Polycaste has bathed and anointed Telemachus, she puts “a fine mantle and a tunic around him” (3.467; see [[bathing]] and [[tunic]]). The φᾶρος is the great mantle of kings, and the same verse dresses Odysseus after his bath on the day of the recognition (23.155); at Circe's house and among the Phaeacians the bathed Odysseus is given a χλαῖνα and a tunic in its place (10.365, 8.455).
