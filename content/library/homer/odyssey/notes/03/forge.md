# ῥαιστήρ (18.477). ἄκμονα and πυράγρην (3.434) are the entry's.

The smith comes to the sacrifice “holding in his hands his smith's tools, the instruments of his craft, / anvil and hammer and well-made tongs, / with which he worked the gold” (3.433–435; see [[laerces-the-goldsmith]] and [[the-sacrifice-to-athena]]). They are the tools of Hephaestus' forge in the Iliad, the anvil, the hammer and the tongs (*Iliad* 18.476–477), without the bellows and the crucibles. The tools show, W. W. Merry argues, that the gold was not poured molten but spread round the horns as leaf or foil (*Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 3.384 and 3.437; see the translation's note on 3.384 and 3.437). The hammer has a word of its own here, σφῦρα, where the Iliad's is ῥαιστήρ. χαλκήια, “smith's tools”, take their name from χαλκεύς, “smith”, which is what the narrator calls the goldsmith (3.432): the worker in gold is named from the metal most commonly worked (Merry on 3.425). πείρατα τέχνης, “the instruments of his craft”, Merry renders “wherein lie the issues of art”, the tools on which the craft depends for its accomplishment (on 3.433).
