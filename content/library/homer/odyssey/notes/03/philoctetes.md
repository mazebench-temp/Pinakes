# Ποιάντιον (3.190): the patronymic adjective counts for the son as well as the father (poias). It is claimed here,
# within the Odyssey, because later Greek has a "Poiantian plain" (Suda π 3072) that is not the son's.
grc: [Ποιάντιον]

εὖ δὲ Φιλοκτήτην, Ποιάντιον ἀγλαὸν υἱόν, “and safely Philoctetes, the splendid son of Poias” (3.190; see [[poias]] and [[splendid]]). Ποιάντιος is a patronymic adjective, which Merry counts among Homer's Aeolisms (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 3.190). He comes home among the men Nestor has heard of (3.186–192; see [[the-returns-of-the-achaeans]]), and the poem says no more of his return; in the eighth book Odysseus remembers him as the one archer who surpassed him at Troy (8.219–220, a plain rendering).
