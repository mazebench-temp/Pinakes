---
summary: When Athena departs “in the likeness of a sea eagle”, “amazement seized all who saw it. / And the old man marveled, when he saw it with his eyes” (3.372–373). Telemachus, told that Athena might love him as she loved his father, says, “amazement holds me” (3.227).
grc: [θαύμαζεν, "=ἄγη"]
---

**The sea eagle.** The amazement is at a god seen going: θάμβος δʼ ἕλε πάντας ἰδόντας (3.372), rendered as the Iliad's θάμβος δʼ ἔχεν εἰσορόωντας, “and amazement held those who watched” (*Iliad* 3.342; see the translation's table of key words). Nestor, marveling, takes Telemachus by the hand and names the goddess, “most glorious Tritogeneia” (3.373–378; see [[athena]], [[seeing-the-gods]] and [[phene]]), as in Book 1 Telemachus “was amazed in his heart” when the stranger flew away (1.320–323). Merry sends the reader to that departure, ὄρνις δʼ ὣς ἀνόπαια διέπτατο (1.320), and takes εἰδομένη of actual bodily likeness (*Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 3.372; see [[eidos]] and the translation's note on 3.372).

**Amazement holds me.** ἄγη μʼ ἔχει is the young man's answer to Nestor's wish that Athena might love him as she cared for his father: “for you have said too great a thing; amazement holds me” (3.227), which Merry renders “for thou hast spoken of a very hard thing: I am all amaze at it” (on 3.227). Telemachus says the half-verse again in Book 16, when his father speaks of the two of them facing the suitors alone (16.236–243). The book's third word of wonder is σέβας, “awe” (3.123; see [[sebas]]).
