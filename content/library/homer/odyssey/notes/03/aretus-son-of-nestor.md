# Ἄρητος and the English "Aretus" stay in the Odyssey: Ἄρητος is the Trojan of Il. 17.494 (aretus).
# Unnamed: the basket in his other hand (3.441-442).

“And Aretus brought them water for the hands in a flowered basin, / coming from the chamber, and in his other hand he held the barley grains / in a basket” (3.440–442; see [[basin-and-pitcher]], [[barley-grains]] and [[the-sacrifice-to-athena]]). The water is for washing the hands, not for sprinkling; the scholiast takes the basin to be worked with flowers, and Merry supposes it kept in the chamber and brought out for the occasion (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 3.440). ἑτέρῃ, “in his other hand”, is the left (Merry on 3.441). With the water and the grains Nestor then begins the rite (3.445; see [[nestor]]).
