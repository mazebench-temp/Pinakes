summary: The welcome at Pylos follows the pattern on the shore: the strangers come to the gathering of the Pylians at their feast, are welcomed with the hand, seated on fleeces and given the entrails and the cup, and only when they have eaten does Nestor ask who they are (3.31–74).

passages:
  - 3.31-74 | The welcome at Pylos
---

**Arrival:** they come to “the gathering and the seats of the Pylian men” (3.31; see the translation's key words on ἄγυρις). **Finding:** Nestor sits with his sons, while his companions make ready the feast, roasting meat and threading more on spits (3.32–33; [[feast-type-scene]]). **Reception:** they all come together and welcome the strangers with their hands; Peisistratus comes first, takes them both by the hand and seats them “on soft fleeces on the sands of the sea” beside his brother Thrasymedes and his father (3.34–39; [[soft]], [[peisistratus]], [[thrasymedes]]). **Meal:** he gives them portions of the entrails and the golden cup, and bids the elder stranger pray to the god whose feast it is (3.40–66; [[libation]], [[cup]], [[prayer-type-scene]]). **Questions:** only then, “now that they have taken their fill of food,” does Nestor ask who they are (3.69–74; [[desire-for-drink-and-food]], [[strangers-who-are-you]]). **Errand:** Telemachus tells why he has come (3.79–101).

The departure turns the pattern round. When the goddess and Telemachus would go back to the ship at nightfall, Nestor holds them back: no guest of his will sleep on a ship's deck while he lives (3.343–355; [[xenia]]). The goddess goes as a god goes, in the likeness of a sea eagle (3.371–372; [[divine-epiphany]], [[phene]]), and Telemachus stays as a guest of the house, with a bed in the colonnade, a bath, and a chariot for the next stage of his journey (3.397–399, 3.464–469, 3.475–485; [[corded]], [[bathing]], [[chariot-journey]]).
