Water and barley open the rite at Pylos, as at Chryse. Aretus comes from the chamber with the water for the hands in a flowered basin, “and in his other hand he held the barley grains / in a basket” (3.440–442; see [[aretus-son-of-nestor]] and [[hand-washing]]); ἑτέρῃ is the left hand, the right being the hand par excellence (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 3.441). Then “the old horse-driving Nestor / began the rite with the water for the hands and the barley grains” (3.444–445), and “when they had prayed and scattered the barley grains” the heifer is struck (3.447, as *Iliad* 1.458; see [[the-sacrifice-to-athena]] and [[sacrifice-type-scene]]). The barley is named three times in three steps: carried in (οὐλαί), taken up for the beginning of the rite, and thrown (προβάλοντο). Merry takes the οὐλαί as coarsely ground barley meal, the Attic ὀλαί, and reports two etymologies, Max Müller's from the root of Latin *mola*, “meal”, and Curtius' from the root of εἰλύω, “roll” (on 3.441). He calls οὐλοχύτας at 3.445 proleptic, since the meal is not yet scattered but only lifted from the basket; the scattering is προβάλοντο (on 3.445). The basket, κάνεον, holds the barley again when Penelope puts barley grains in a basket and prays to Athena for her son, and ends her prayer with the women's cry (4.759–767; see [[ololyge]]).
