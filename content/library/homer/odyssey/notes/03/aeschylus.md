---
summary: Aeschylus' Oresteia retells the story that Nestor tells in this book, and changes it: the queen plans and strikes, Aegisthus is the “cowardly lion” who kept house while the men fought, Orestes comes home from Phocis and kills his mother, and goes to Athens only after the deed, to be judged there.
---

- The watchman's “woman's heart that plans like a man” and Clytemnestra's net and blows (*Agamemnon* 11, 1379–1387); Aegisthus, “a cowardly lion … keeping house” (*Agamemnon* 1224–1225, 1625–1627; on 3.262–264 and 3.310); Orestes reared by Strophius the Phocian and come home as “a Daulian from Phocis” (*Agamemnon* 877–881; *Libation Bearers* 674; on 3.307); and Athens as the place of his trial (*Eumenides* 79–84, 752–753): [[the-murder-of-agamemnon-in-tragedy]].
