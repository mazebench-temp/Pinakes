summary: Athens is named twice: Sunium is “the cape of Athens”, where Apollo killed Menelaus' helmsman (3.278), and Orestes came home “back from Athens” to kill Aegisthus (3.307), where tragedy brings him from Phocis.
# Ἀθηνέων (3.278) and Ἀθηνάων (3.307) are claimed by the entry; athena excludes them.
---

**The cape of Athens.** “But when we came to holy Sunium, the cape of Athens” (3.278; see [[sunium]]): the city's name stands for its land, as it does in the Catalogue, where Athens is the only town named in Attica (*Iliad* 2.546–556). Ἀθηνέων is the Ionic genitive of the plural Ἀθῆναι.

**Back from Athens.** Orestes came in the eighth year “back from Athens, and killed his father's slayer” (3.307; see [[orestes]] and [[orestes-kills-clytemnestra]]). Homer says nothing more of his years away. In tragedy he comes home from Phocis, where Strophius has raised him: Clytemnestra tells Agamemnon that the boy is in the care of Strophius the Phocian (Aeschylus, *Agamemnon* 877–881), and Orestes comes to the palace as a stranger from Daulis in Phocis (*Libation Bearers* 674–679; see [[strophius]] and [[phocians]]); he goes to Athens only after the deed, sent by Apollo to Athena's city to be judged (*Eumenides* 79–80). Merry notes the difference (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 3.307; see the translation's note on 3.307), and adds that the Odyssey has the city's name in the singular, Ἀθήνη (7.80), as well as in the plural.
