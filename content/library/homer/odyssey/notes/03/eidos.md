---
summary: Athena departs φήνῃ εἰδομένη, “in the likeness of a sea eagle” (3.372).
---

Merry, after Seiler, notes that εἰδόμενος and εἰδομένη are used in Homer not of a likeness of movement or action but of an actual bodily resemblance, so that the goddess is seen to change into the bird (*Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 3.372; see the translation's note on 3.372 and [[phene]]). It is the participle of her disguise in Book 2, “in the likeness of Mentor, both in form and in voice” (2.268, 2.401; see [[in-the-likeness-of-mentor]]). Telemachus addresses her as Mentor through the book (3.22, 3.240; see [[mentor]]), and Nestor knows her for a goddess only by the change (3.371–379; see [[seeing-the-gods]] and [[thambos]]).
