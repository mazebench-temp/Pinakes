---
summary: Nestor vows Athena a yearling heifer, broad of brow and unbroken, “with gold poured around her horns” (3.382–384), and the next morning the goldsmith spreads the gold on the horns (3.425–438).
refs: [3.426, 3.437]
---

The verses are Diomedes' (*Iliad* 10.292–294), and here they are kept. Nestor bids one of his sons call the goldsmith Laerces “so that he may pour gold around the heifer's horns” (3.425–426); the old man gives the gold, and the smith spreads it, “working it with skill, so that the goddess might rejoice to see the offering” (3.437–438; [[laerces-the-goldsmith]], [[the-sacrifice-to-athena]]). Merry notes that the smith's tools, anvil, hammer and tongs, show that περίχευεν means only spreading the gold in leaf or foil around the horns (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 3.437; [[well-made]]).
