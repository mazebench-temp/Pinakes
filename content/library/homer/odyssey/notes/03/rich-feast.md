---
summary: Athena came to Nestor “in plain sight at the rich feast of the god” (3.420).
---

θεοῦ ἐς δαῖτα θάλειαν: the feast is Poseidon's on the shore, where the goddess came as a guest (3.43–44). Merry renders ἐναργής “in visible presence,” comparing the Iliad's verse on the gods who appear in plain sight (*Iliad* 20.131), and notes that θάλεια exists only in the feminine, from the root of θάλλω, “flourish” (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 3.420; [[divine-epiphany]], [[seeing-the-gods]]).
