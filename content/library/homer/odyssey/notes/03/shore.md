# ψαμάθοις ἁλίῃσιν, "the sands of the sea" (3.38), as a phrase: ψάμαθος alone is sand of any kind, the beach at
# Il. 1.486 but the bank of a river at Il. 21.202 and the sand of a simile at Il. 2.800.
grc: ["ψαμάθοις ἁλίῃσιν"]
en: [sands of the sea]

The book opens on another shore than the one where the last ended (2.408): “There on the shore of the sea the people were offering sacrifice” (3.5; see [[the-sacrifice-at-pylos]] and [[pylians]]). ἐπὶ θινὶ θαλάσσης, “on the shore of the sea”, has θίς, the strand at the water's edge, and the whole feast of the first day is held there, in the open, by the sea whose god receives the bulls (see [[poseidon]]). Peisistratus “seated them at the feast / on soft fleeces on the sands of the sea, / beside his brother Thrasymedes and his father” (3.37–39; see [[peisistratus]], [[thrasymedes]] and [[nestor]]). ἁλίῃσιν, “of the sea”, gives the sand its place, as in the next book, where Eidothea scoops out beds “in the sands of the sea”, ἐν ψαμάθοισι … ἁλίῃσιν, for Menelaus and his men to lie in wait for Proteus (4.438, a plain rendering). At nightfall the company leaves the shore for Nestor's house (3.386–388; see [[house]]).
