summary: The sack is remembered twice: Telemachus has heard that Odysseus fought at Nestor's side and sacked the city of the Trojans, and Nestor begins the returns where the war ended, “when we had sacked the steep city of Priam” (3.84–85, 3.118–119, 3.130).
# Unnamed: "sacked the city of the Trojans" (3.85), "the son of Cronus barely brought it to an end" (3.118-119), and
# "when we had sacked the steep city of Priam" (3.130).
refs: [3.85, 3.118-119, 3.130]
---

Telemachus asks for news of the man “who once, they say, / fighting at your side sacked the city of the Trojans” (3.84–85; see [[odysseus]]). Nestor's war lasted nine years of contriving evils for the Trojans, “and the son of Cronus barely brought it to an end” (3.118–119); μόγις, “barely”, is only after much ado, and Merry notes the change of tense from the imperfect ῥάπτομεν, “we stitched”, to the aorist ἐτέλεσσε, “brought it to an end” (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 3.119; see [[zeus]]). The sack itself is told in one clause, and the returns follow at once: “But when we had sacked the steep city of Priam / and gone aboard the ships, and a god scattered the Achaeans” (3.130–131; see [[priam]], [[the-returns-of-the-achaeans]] and [[the-departure-of-the-greeks]]). Demodocus will sing the wooden horse and the sack among the Phaeacians (8.499–520).
