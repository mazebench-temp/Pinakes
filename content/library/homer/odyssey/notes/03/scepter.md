---
summary: At dawn Nestor sits on the polished stones before his doors, where Neleus sat before him, “holding the scepter”, as his sons gather round him (3.411–412).
---

“and now Nestor in his turn sat on them, the Gerenian, the guardian of the Achaeans, / holding the scepter. And around him his sons gathered in a body” (3.411–412; see [[nestor]], [[neleus]] and [[guardian-of-the-achaeans]]). The king holds the scepter in the place where his father sat before him, and gives his orders for the day from it (3.417–429; see [[the-sacrifice-to-athena]]). In the assembly of Book 2 the scepter passed from hand to hand to whoever rose to speak (2.37–38); here it is the king's own, as Agamemnon's is in the Iliad, and Nestor is not speaking in an assembly but sitting in state before his house, among his sons (see [[kingship]] and [[house]]).
