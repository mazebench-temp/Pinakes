---
summary: The patronymic nine times, for both brothers: the two sons of Atreus between whom Athena set strife (3.136); Agamemnon at Troy (3.156, 3.164), as he left for Troy (3.268) and in his death (3.193, 3.248, 3.304); Menelaus coming home too late and sailing with Nestor (3.257, 3.277).
---

Ἀτρεΐδῃσι μετʼ ἀμφοτέροισιν, “between the two sons of Atreus,” as Merry takes μετά (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 3.136), begins the quarrel ([[the-departure-of-the-greeks]]); Ἀτρεΐδης εὐρὺ κρείων Ἀγαμέμνων is the Iliad's verse-end (3.248; [[wide-ruling]]). The patronymic without a name is Agamemnon at 3.193, 3.268 and 3.304, and Menelaus at 3.277; at 3.257 Nestor adds the name, “fair-haired Menelaus” ([[fair-haired]]). See [[agamemnon]], [[menelaus]] and [[atreus]].
