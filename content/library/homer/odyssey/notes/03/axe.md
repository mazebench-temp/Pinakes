---
summary: At the sacrifice to Athena Thrasymedes stands by with a sharp axe, and when the barley has been scattered he strikes, and the axe cuts through the sinews of the heifer's neck (3.442–450).
---

“and Thrasymedes, steadfast in battle, / stood by, holding a sharp axe in his hand, to strike the heifer down” (3.442–443; see [[thrasymedes]], [[steadfast-in-battle]] and [[sharp]]), and when Nestor has prayed and the barley grains have been scattered, “at once the son of Nestor, high-hearted Thrasymedes, / standing close, struck; and the axe cut through the sinews / of the neck, and loosed the strength of the heifer” (3.448–450; see [[the-sacrifice-to-athena]]). The blow fells the heifer but does not kill her: it severs the muscles of the neck and stuns her, and she dies when Peisistratus cuts her throat (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 3.450; see [[peisistratus]]). No sacrifice in the Iliad has an axe (see [[sacrifice-type-scene]]). The stroke appears there only in a simile, when the Trojan Aretus, struck by Automedon's spear, leaps forward and falls as an ox of the fields falls when a man in his prime strikes it behind the horns with a sharp axe and cuts through the sinew (*Iliad* 17.520–523; see [[aretus]]).
