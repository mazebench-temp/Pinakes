summary: Athena's visit to Pylos completes the epiphany begun in Book 2. As Mentor she lands with Telemachus, counsels him, prays and rebukes him as a man would, and takes her leave; then she departs in the likeness of a sea eagle, all who see it are amazed, and Nestor names her and prays to her. The next morning she comes, unseen, to the sacrifice that pays his vow.

**In the likeness of Mentor.** In Book 2 she came to Telemachus “in the likeness of Mentor, both in form and in voice” (2.268, 2.401). At Pylos the likeness is not described again: the narrator names her Athena throughout, and Telemachus answers her as “Mentor” (3.22, 3.240; see [[mentor]] and [[athena]]). Her part is a man's. She steps ashore before him and tells him to go to Nestor (3.12–20), takes the cup as the elder guest and prays to Poseidon as a worshiper (3.41–62), and counsels and rebukes the young man as an older companion would (3.26–28, 3.230–238). The arrival and the speech of the pattern are stretched over a whole day.

**Departure and recognition.** The end of the pattern comes at sunset, when she has taken her leave of Nestor (3.357–370): “So she spoke, and gray-eyed Athena went away / in the likeness of a sea eagle; and amazement seized all who saw it” (3.371–372; see [[phene]] and [[thambos]]). The first verse returns when she leaves Scheria for Athens (7.78). In Book 1 the recognition was Telemachus' alone, and unspoken (1.320–323, 1.420; see the Book 1 note); here everyone sees the departure, and Nestor speaks the recognition. He takes Telemachus by the hand and names her, “the daughter of Zeus, most glorious Tritogeneia” (3.374–379; see [[seeing-the-gods]] and [[tritogeneia]]), and prays and vows her a heifer (3.380–384). “So he spoke in prayer, and Pallas Athena heard him” (3.385).

**The escort of the gods.** Nestor's first words are for the young man: “I do not think you will be a coward and a weakling, / if, young as you are, the gods go with you as your escorts” (3.375–376). πομπῆες, “escorts”, is the word he had used of his own sons, who would escort Telemachus to Lacedaemon (3.325).

**After the epiphany.** The appearance of the god turns into worship: Nestor's libation to her that night (3.393–394), his orders at dawn, “so that first of all the gods I may propitiate Athena, / who came to me in plain sight at the rich feast of the god” (3.419–420), and the sacrifice, to which “Athena came / to share” (3.435–436; see [[the-sacrifice-to-athena]]).
