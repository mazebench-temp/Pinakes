---
summary: Cicero's letters to Atticus quote this book four times: Telemachus' “Mentor, how then shall I go?” and Athena's answer, as Caesar approached, and Nestor's “long voyage” and “windy Mimas”, for a voyage and a road of his own. In On Old Age he gives Nestor “the third generation of men” as his license to praise himself.
---

- “Here I could wish I had that Minerva of Homer's, disguised as Mentor”, to whom he might say “Mentor, how then shall I go?” (*Letters to Atticus* 9.8.2, quoting 3.22): [[athena-as-mentor-in-later-writers]].
- Caesar came so suddenly that Cicero had to act unprepared, “but still, ἄλλα μὲν αὐτός, as he says, ἄλλα δὲ καὶ δαίμων ὑποθήσεται” (*Letters to Atticus* 9.15.4, quoting 3.26–27): [[athena-as-mentor-in-later-writers]] and [[some-things-you-will-think-of-yourself]].
- The “long voyage” (δολιχὸν πλόον) that Nestor and Diomedes pondered at Lesbos, for his own crossing from Regium to Greece, and the road from Sinuessa, where Atticus urged him “past windy Mimas”, that is, keeping the Appian Way “on the left” (*Letters to Atticus* 16.6.1, 16.13.1–2, quoting 3.169, 3.171–172): [[cicero-the-long-voyage]].
- Nestor might proclaim his own virtues without seeming arrogant or talkative, “for he was seeing the third generation of men” (*On Old Age* 31; on 3.245): [[nestors-three-generations]] and [[cicero-nestor-honey]].
