---
summary: Telemachus answers Nestor “taking heart; for Athena herself had put daring in his mind” (3.76); Aegisthus “took heart and killed” Agamemnon while Menelaus was away (3.252); and Athena goes to the ship “to encourage the companions” (3.361).
---

**The goddess's gift.** ἐνὶ φρεσὶ θάρσος … θῆχʼ is the gift of the Iliad's Athena to Diomedes at the beginning of his great day (*Iliad* 5.1–3), and of the Odyssey's to Telemachus in Book 1 (1.320–321; see the translation's table of key words). Here it answers the young man's shame (3.14, 3.24; see [[aidos]] and [[shame-for-a-young-man]]), and Merry marks the tense of θαρσήσας, “having plucked up courage” (*Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 3.76). It serves Athena's double purpose, that he may ask for news of his father and that noble fame may be his (3.77–78; see [[kleos]]).

**The other daring.** θαρσήσας returns for Aegisthus: Telemachus asks whether Menelaus was away, “so that the other took heart and killed him” (3.251–252), and Merry takes the clause, set in parataxis, as giving the result of Menelaus' absence (on 3.252; see [[aegisthus]] and [[the-murder-of-agamemnon]]). The same participle serves the son who speaks to an old man and the coward who kills a king in his absence (ἄναλκις, 3.310).

**Encouragement.** At nightfall the goddess goes to the black ship “to encourage the companions and tell them each thing” (θαρσύνω, 3.361; see [[hetairos]]), and leaves in the likeness of a sea eagle (3.371–372).
