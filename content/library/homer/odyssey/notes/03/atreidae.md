---
summary: The two sons of Atreus are set at strife by Athena's wrath: they call the army to an assembly at sunset and stand answering each other with harsh words, and the fleet is divided between them (3.136–156).
# Unnamed: the two brothers in the dual, "the two of them called" (3.137), "the two told them the word" (3.140),
# "the two of them, answering each other" (3.148).
refs: [3.137, 3.140, 3.148]
---

ἥ τʼ ἔριν Ἀτρεΐδῃσι μετʼ ἀμφοτέροισιν ἔθηκε, “who set strife between the two sons of Atreus” (3.136; see [[athena]] and [[eris]]): μετά with the dative is “between”, Merry notes (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 3.136). The quarrel is told in the dual, as the Iliad named the brothers in the dual when Chryses begged them (*Iliad* 1.16): τὼ δὲ καλεσσαμένω, “the two of them called” (3.137), μῦθον μυθείσθην, “the two told them the word” (3.140), and τὼ μὲν χαλεποῖσιν ἀμειβομένω ἐπέεσσιν, “the two of them, answering each other with harsh words” (3.148). Menelaus urges the army home and Agamemnon would keep it (3.141–147; see [[menelaus]], [[agamemnon]] and [[the-departure-of-the-greeks]]). The two are never together again in the poem: one was murdered on his return, and the other came home on the day of the funeral feast for his murderer (3.193–198, 3.311–312; see [[the-murder-of-agamemnon]] and [[the-wanderings-of-menelaus]]).
