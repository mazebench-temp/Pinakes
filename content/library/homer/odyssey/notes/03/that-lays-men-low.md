---
summary: Athena, as Mentor, on the death that not even the gods can ward off: “whenever / the deadly fate of death that lays men low takes him down” (3.237–238).
---

μοῖρʼ ὀλοὴ καθέλῃσι τανηλεγέος θανάτοιο is the verse of Penelope's shroud for Laertes in Book 2 (2.100), now of every man's death ([[deadly]], [[thanatos]], [[not-even-the-gods-can-ward-off-death]]).
