summary: The one place in the poem that touches on Clytemnestra's death: when Orestes had killed Aegisthus, “he gave the Argives a funeral feast / for his hateful mother and for the coward Aegisthus” (3.309–310). How she died is not said; Aristarchus, as the scholia report, took the lines to hint that she perished with Aegisthus, and thought it unclear whether by Orestes' hand.

**The funeral feast.** δαίνυ τάφον, “a funeral feast” (3.309), is a banquet for the dead, as Achilles gives the Myrmidons a funeral feast for Patroclus, αὐτὰρ ὁ τοῖσι τάφον μενοεικέα δαίνυ, “and he gave them a funeral feast to satisfy the heart” (*Iliad* 23.29; Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 3.309; see [[feast]]). The guests are the Argives, and Merry draws the consequence: Orestes is shown justified in the eyes of his own people, and nothing is said of the Erinyes (on 3.309; see [[erinyes]]).

**Her death unspoken.** The scholia, Merry reports, say that 3.309–310 were missing in some editions, and give Aristarchus' view that the lines hint that Clytemnestra perished together with Aegisthus, though whether by Orestes too is unclear. Nitzsch remarked that here alone, if anywhere, does Homer lay her death to her son; Merry thinks that Homer's legend most probably did, and that the poet leaves it unspoken (on 3.309; see the translation's note on 3.309–310). The verse of Orestes' deed names only Aegisthus, “and killed his father's slayer, / scheming Aegisthus, who had killed his famous father” (3.307–308; see [[aegisthus]]); his mother enters only as the dead for whom the feast is given, στυγερή, “hateful” (3.310; see [[clytemnestra]]). As in the first book, the example held up to Telemachus is the vengeance on Aegisthus (3.195–200; 1.298–302; see [[orestes]]).

**On the same day.** Menelaus came home that same day, too late for the vengeance and in time for the feast (3.311–312; see [[the-wanderings-of-menelaus]]).
