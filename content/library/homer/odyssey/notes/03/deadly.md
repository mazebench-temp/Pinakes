---
summary: Two deadly things: “the deadly wrath of the gray-eyed daughter of the mighty father” that brought many of the Achaeans an evil fate (3.135), and “the deadly fate of death that lays men low” (3.238).
---

μήνιος ἐξ ὀλοῆς (3.135) adds the genitive to the entry's forms: the wrath of Athena after the sack, which Merry takes as directed first against the Locrian Ajax for his outrage upon Cassandra, and then against the chiefs who left it unpunished (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 3.135; [[menis]], [[daughter-of-a-mighty-father]]). μοῖρʼ ὀλοή (3.238) is the verse-end of Laertes' death in Book 2 (2.100), here of every man's ([[that-lays-men-low]], [[moira]]).
