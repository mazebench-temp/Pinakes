---
summary: “Godlike Telemachus” twice: as he and Athena are eager to go back to the ship (3.343), and as Nestor's sons seat him among them at dawn (3.416).
---

θεοειδής (3.343) and θεοείκελος (3.416), the two forms of the epithet. In the same scene at dawn Thrasymedes is ἀντίθεος, also “godlike” (3.414; [[antitheos]]), and after the sacrifice Telemachus steps from the bath “like the immortals in form” (3.468; [[like-the-immortals]]). See [[telemachus]].
