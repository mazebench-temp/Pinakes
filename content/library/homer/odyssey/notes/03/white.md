---
summary: The stones before Nestor's doors are “white, shining with oil” (3.408).
---

λευκοί, ἀποστίλβοντες ἀλείφατος. Merry reports the scholiast's explanation, that oil makes what it is rubbed on glisten like marble, and takes the phrase as a polish or varnish laid on the stone, explaining how the stones were made smooth; Eustathius and the scholia generally read it “glistening as if with oil” (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 3.408; [[polished]], [[neleus]]).
