---
summary: Telemachus despairs of his father: “already / the immortals have devised for him death and black doom” (3.241–242).
---

θάνατον καὶ κῆρα μέλαιναν is the pair of 2.283, where Athena, as Mentor, said that the suitors know nothing of “death and black doom”; now Telemachus says it of his father, in answer to the goddess' own words on death, which not even the gods can ward off from a man they love (3.236–238; [[not-even-the-gods-can-ward-off-death]], [[ker-doom]], [[thanatos]]). The book's other μέλαιναν is the black ship ([[black-ships]]).
