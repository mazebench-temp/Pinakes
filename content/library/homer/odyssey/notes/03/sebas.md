---
summary: “awe holds me as I look at you” (3.123): Nestor's wonder at the likeness of Telemachus' words to his father's, the first σέβας of the Odyssey.
grc: [σέβας]
en: [awe]
---

σέβας μʼ ἔχει εἰσορόωντα breaks into Nestor's praise of Odysseus' tricks: “your father, if truly / you are his offspring; awe holds me as I look at you. / For truly your words are like his” (3.122–124; see [[fathers-and-sons]] and [[genee]]). Merry takes the feeling as “amazement”, at the resemblance traced in the following lines, and notes that the phrase recurs at 4.75 and 6.161, and that in the second passage it is taken up a few lines on by ἐτεθήπεα θυμῷ, “I was amazed in my heart” (6.166) (*Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 3.123; see the translation's table of key words and [[thambos]]). At Sparta Helen says it of the same likeness: no one, she says, man or woman, was ever so like another, σέβας μʼ ἔχει εἰσορόωσαν, as this young man is like the son of Odysseus (4.141–143). Merry uses Helen's words to decide that Nestor's ἐοικότες means “like his”, not “fitting” (on 3.124; see the translation's note on 3.124–125).
