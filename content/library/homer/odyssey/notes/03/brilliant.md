summary: Odysseus is “brilliant” in Telemachus' plea and twice in Nestor's praise of his cunning (3.84, 3.121, 3.126); so are the Achaeans at Troy (3.116), Clytemnestra before her fall (3.266), Orestes on his return (3.306), Nestor's son Echephron at the sacrifice (3.439), and “shining Lacedaemon” (3.326).
grc: ["=δίου", "=δῖοι", "δῖα Κλυταιμνήστρη", "Λακεδαίμονα δῖαν"]
en: [shining]
---

The book adds four forms to the nominative δῖος: the genitive, δίου Ὀδυσσῆος ταλασίφρονος (3.84; [[stout-hearted]]); the plural, δῖοι Ἀχαιοί (3.116); the feminine of a queen, δῖα Κλυταιμνήστρη (3.266); and the accusative of a land, Λακεδαίμονα δῖαν (3.326), a verse-end of the Odyssey that comes again when others speak of Telemachus' journey (4.702, 5.20). Merry renders δῖα of Clytemnestra “queenly,” of position or birth only, as διογενής and διοτρεφής are ordinary epithets of kings: the epithet stands in the verse that says she refused Aegisthus at first, “for her mind was good” (3.265–266; Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 3.266). The sea's δῖαν belongs to [[shining-sea]] (3.153). See [[odysseus]], [[clytemnestra]], [[orestes]], [[echephron]] and [[lacedaemon]].
