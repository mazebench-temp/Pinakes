summary: The Pylians offer Poseidon a hecatomb, and Athena prays that he give them “a gracious return” for it (3.58–59); Agamemnon's hecatombs cannot win back Athena's favor (3.143–147); Aegisthus, having won Clytemnestra, burned thigh-pieces and hung up offerings (3.273–275); and Nestor vows Athena a heifer with gilded horns in return for noble fame, and pays it the next morning (3.380–384, 3.418–463).

---

**Gift and return.** Athena's prayer at the sacrifice on the shore asks for the return: glory for Nestor and his sons, “a gracious return” for the Pylians “for this famous hecatomb”, and a safe homecoming for herself and Telemachus (3.55–61; see [[charis]], [[kydos]] and [[the-sacrifice-at-pylos]]). Nestor's vow puts the gift after the request, in the verses of Diomedes' vow in the night raid (3.382–384 = *Iliad* 10.292–294): grant noble fame to me and my house, “and I in turn will sacrifice to you a yearling heifer” (3.380–384; see [[kleos]]). At dawn he pays it, “so that first of all the gods I may propitiate Athena, / who came to me in plain sight at the rich feast of the god” (3.419–420; see [[the-sacrifice-to-athena]]).

**When the gift fails.** Agamemnon's hecatombs to heal Athena's anger were offered in vain, since the mind of the gods is not quickly turned (3.143–147; see [[the-mind-of-the-gods-is-not-quickly-turned]]). At Tenedos those who sailed sacrificed “eager to go home”, and Zeus roused strife again (3.159–161), though at Geraestus they laid many thigh-pieces on Poseidon's altar after crossing the open sea, and the wind held to Pylos (3.177–183). Aegisthus too was generous to the gods: having led Agamemnon's wife home, he burned many thigh-pieces on their altars and hung up offerings, woven things and gold, “having accomplished a great deed, which he had never expected in his heart” (3.273–275; see [[aegisthus]]); Ameis, as Merry reports, took the offerings for a sign of how hard the conquest had been, so that Aegisthus was surprised at his own success (*Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 3.269). He paid for it miserably all the same (3.195; see [[poine]]).
