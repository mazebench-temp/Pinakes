---
summary: Nestor, having seen the goddess go, “took Telemachus by the hand, and spoke to him, and called him by name” (3.374).
---

Τηλεμάχου δʼ ἕλε χεῖρα, ἔπος τʼ ἔφατʼ ἔκ τʼ ὀνόμαζεν: the gesture and the half-line of close address, as when Agamemnon takes Menelaus by the right hand in the Iliad (*Iliad* 7.108). The words that follow are the first a mortal speaks of the goddess' presence: “My friend, I do not think you will be a coward and a weakling, / if, young as you are, the gods go with you as your escorts” (3.375–376; [[seeing-the-gods]], [[nestor]]).
