Dogs and birds feeding on the unburied dead are the fate that the Iliad's proem names (*Iliad* 1.4–5; see [[dogs]] and [[birds]]). The women's lament is part of the burial that is denied him, with the mound of earth (see [[tomb]] and [[lament]]). Ἀχαιιάδων is the form of Book 2, where Penelope fears the indignation of “one of the Achaean women in the land” (2.101; see [[nemesis]]): there their blame, here their tears, are the judgment of the women of the Achaean lands on a man's life. μέγα ἔργον, “great … deed”, is here “audacious”, “shameless”, as Merry classes the phrase, one of its three senses in Homer (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 3.261).
