---
summary: At Troy, at dawn after the quarrel, half of the army “put our possessions on board, and the deep-girdled women” (3.154).
---

βαθυζώνους τε γυναῖκας: the captive women of the sacked city, loaded with the plunder ([[the-departure-of-the-greeks]]). Merry explains the girdle resting on the hips, with the folds of the robe falling over it, and reports Damm's argument that the word, given in Homer to Trojan and Dardanian women, does not mark barbarians (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 3.154).
