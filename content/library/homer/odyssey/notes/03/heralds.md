---
summary: At sunset on the shore of Pylos the heralds pour water over the hands of the company before the libation to Poseidon (3.338).
---

“And the heralds poured water over their hands” (3.338; see [[hand-washing]] and [[the-sacrifice-at-pylos]]): Nestor's heralds serve at the sacrifice of the Pylians, as heralds serve at the oaths and libations of the Iliad, in the same words (*Iliad* 3.270, 9.174). The young men fill the bowls and serve the wine (3.339–340; see [[cupbearing]]). The tongues of the victims, burned at the end of the day (3.332, 3.341), were in later Attic usage the herald's portion: W. W. Merry quotes Aristophanes' “the tongue is cut apart for the herald” and a scholiast's note that the first portions of the tongue were given to the herald, though he refers the offering here to Poseidon (*Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 3.332). These are the only heralds of the book.
