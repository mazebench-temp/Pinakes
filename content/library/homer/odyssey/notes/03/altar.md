summary: The Pylians are burning the thigh-pieces on the altar for Poseidon when the strangers land (3.9); at Geraestus Nestor's men lay thigh-pieces of bulls on the altar for the same god (3.178–179); and Aegisthus, when he has won Clytemnestra, burns thigh-pieces on the holy altars of the gods (3.273).
# βωμοῖς (3.273), first met here. The altar is not named at 3.9 and 3.179, where the English "on the altar" renders
# ἐπί.

**On the shore and at Geraestus.** The Greek of the sacrifices on the shore and at Geraestus does not name the altar: θεῷ δʼ ἐπὶ μηρίʼ ἔκαιον, “and were burning the thigh-pieces on the altar for the god” (3.9), and ἐπὶ μῆρʼ ἔθεμεν, “we laid many thigh-pieces on the altar” (3.179). Only ἐπί, “on”, points to it, and the translation supplies the altar, as W. W. Merry does in rendering the first, “burning the thigh-slices on the altar in the god's honour” (*Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 3.9; see [[the-sacrifice-at-pylos]], [[geraestus]] and [[thigh-pieces]]). Both are sacrifices to Poseidon by the sea.

**Aegisthus' offerings.** When Aegisthus had led Clytemnestra to his house, “he burned many thigh-pieces on the holy altars of the gods, / and hung up many offerings, woven things and gold, / having accomplished a great deed, which he had never expected in his heart” (3.273–275; see [[aegisthus]] and [[the-murder-of-agamemnon]]). The altars are θεῶν ἱεροῖς ἐπὶ βωμοῖς, “the holy altars of the gods” (see [[hieros]]).

**At Nestor's house.** No altar is named at the sacrifice to Athena. Merry takes the seats and wood that the slave women are told to bring as set on either side of the altar, which stood in the middle (3.429; Merry on 3.429; see [[the-sacrifice-to-athena]]).
