summary: Athena, in Mentor's likeness, prays to Poseidon on the shore with libation, invocation and request, and the narrator answers that she herself was bringing it all to pass (3.43–62); Nestor, when she has gone, prays to her for noble fame and vows a heifer (3.380–385).

1. **Setting and gesture.** Peisistratus gives the stranger the golden cup and bids her pray to lord Poseidon, “for it is his feast you have come upon in coming here,” and then pass the cup to the younger man (3.40–50; [[libation]], [[cup]], [[all-men-need-the-gods]]).
2. **Invocation.** “Hear me, Poseidon, earth-holder, and do not begrudge” (3.55; [[hear-me]], [[earth-holder]]).
3. **Claim.** None is spoken: the hecatomb of the Pylians is before the god (3.59; [[the-sacrifice-at-pylos]]).
4. **Request.** Glory for Nestor and his sons, a gracious return to the Pylians for their hecatomb, and for Telemachus and herself a return when they have done the business they came for (3.57–61; [[prexis]]).
5. **Answer.** In place of the god's hearing, the narrator says: “So she prayed, and she herself was bringing it all to pass” (3.62; [[so-he-spoke]]).

Telemachus prays in the same way (3.64). Nestor's prayer, when he has seen the goddess go, asks her to be gracious and to grant noble fame to him, his children and his wife, and vows a heifer in the verses of Diomedes (3.380–384; [[gold-poured-around-her-horns]]); it is heard in the verse of Diomedes' prayer, “So he spoke in prayer, and Pallas Athena heard him” (3.385; *Iliad* 5.121). At the sacrifice the next morning he prays to her again as he begins the rite (3.445–446; [[sacrifice-type-scene]]). See [[prayer]].
