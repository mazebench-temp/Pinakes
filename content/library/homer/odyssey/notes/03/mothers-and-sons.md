---
summary: Telemachus says of his father, “beyond all men his mother bore him to misery” (3.95); Nestor tells of the suitors who woo “for your mother's sake” (3.212); and Orestes, who avenged his father, gave the funeral feast “for his hateful mother and for the coward Aegisthus” (3.310).
refs: [3.95, 3.212-213, 3.309-310]
---

**Orestes' mother.** The book tells Clytemnestra's death only through the funeral feast: when Orestes had killed Aegisthus, “he gave the Argives a funeral feast / for his hateful mother and for the coward Aegisthus” (3.309–310; see [[orestes-kills-clytemnestra]] and [[clytemnestra]]). The scholia, Merry reports, say that the lines were missing in some editions, and Aristarchus found in them a hint that she died with Aegisthus, though not plainly by Orestes' hand; Merry thinks that Homer's legend probably laid the mother's death to the son as well and that the poet passes over it, while Orestes is justified before the people to whom he gives the feast and no Erinyes pursue him (*Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 3.309; see the translation's note on 3.309–310). Nestor tells the tale of the son who avenged his father to a son whose mother is wooed in his own house (3.195–200, 3.211–213; see [[fathers-and-sons]] and [[penelope]]).

**Born to misery.** πέρι γάρ μιν ὀιζυρὸν τέκε μήτηρ (3.95) makes the mother's bearing the beginning of the son's suffering, as Thetis says to Achilles, “Ah me, my child, why did I raise you, when I bore you to such sorrow?” (*Iliad* 1.414; see [[why-did-i-raise-you]]).
