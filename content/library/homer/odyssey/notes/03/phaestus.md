---
summary: At the edge of Gortyn the South Wind drives the great wave against the left headland, “toward Phaestus, and a small rock holds back the great wave” (3.295–296).
---

The last verses of the description bring the wave inland: “there the South Wind drives the great wave against the left headland, / toward Phaestus, and a small rock holds back the great wave” (3.295–296; see [[notus]] and [[gortyn]]). Merry pictures the swell rolling from the southwest against the cliff and beating northward toward Phaestus, which Strabo puts sixty stadia from Gortyn, twenty from the sea and forty from its port, Matalum (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 3.292; *Geography* 10.4.14). σκαιὸν ῥίον, “the left headland”, is for him the abrupt western end of the mountain chain that runs along the south of the island; others take it for a little cape that served as a breakwater for the port of Phaestus, and the scholia explain the small rock in the same way: the wave raised by the South Wind would have left Phaestus without a harbor, had not the rock in front of it broken the waves (Merry on 3.293). Merry thinks “a small rock” would suit an isolated rock better than the long cliff, one that sheltered the men while the ships were broken (on 3.293; see [[crete]]).
