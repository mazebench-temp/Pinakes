---
summary: Athena tells Telemachus that he has “no more need of shame, not even a little” (3.14), and he answers that “there is shame for a young man in questioning an older one” (3.24); he asks Nestor to soften nothing “out of respect for me, or out of pity” (3.96). Nestor's wife is his “revered wife” (3.381, 3.451).
grc: [αἰδοῦς, αἰδὼς, αἰδόμενος]
en: [shame, revered]
---

**Shame and daring.** The goddess's first words at Pylos take away the young man's αἰδώς, and Merry glosses αἰδοῦς as “shyness” and οὐδʼ ἠβαιόν as “not one whit” (*Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 3.14). Telemachus names the shame he feels: the respect a young man owes an older one, whom he is to question (3.22–24; see [[shame-for-a-young-man]] and [[geron]]). It gives way to daring, as Athena means it to. He answers Nestor “taking heart; for Athena herself had put daring in his mind” (3.76; see [[tharsos]]), and the old man finds his words like his father's (3.123–125; see [[sebas]]). Later in the poem he sends the same counsel to the beggar in his hall, αἰδὼς δʼ οὐκ ἀγαθὴ κεχρημένῳ ἀνδρὶ παρεῖναι, “shame is no good companion for a man in need” (17.347, 17.352).

**Out of respect.** Telemachus asks the old man not to spare him: μηδέ τί μʼ αἰδόμενος μειλίσσεο μηδʼ ἐλεαίρων (3.96), a verse he says again to Menelaus (4.326). Merry takes με with both participles and μειλίσσεο as “soften thy words” (on 3.96; see [[pity]] and [[truth-and-deception]]). The respect he refuses is a host's for a grieving guest: he wants the truth whatever it is, his father's death if Nestor saw it (3.92–95).

**Revered.** αἰδοῖος is the respect a person commands. Nestor prays to Athena for noble fame for himself, his children and his “revered wife” (3.381), and she raises the cry at the sacrifice with his daughters and his sons' wives (3.451–452; see [[eurydice]] and [[ololyge]]), as the housekeeper of Odysseus' house is αἰδοίη in Book 1 (1.139).
