---
summary: Athena, as Mentor: “But death, which is common to all, not even the gods / can ward off from a man they love” (3.236–237).
---

θάνατον μὲν ὁμοίιον: Merry renders the epithet “common to all,” as it is used of old age, of strife and of war in the Iliad (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 3.236, comparing *Iliad* 4.315, 4.444, 9.440), and the translation keeps the Iliad's sense, where old age is “the common lot” (*Iliad* 4.315).
