# Περσεύς and the English "Perseus" stay in the Odyssey: elsewhere Perseus is the son of Danae (perseus).
grc: [Περσεύς]
en: [Perseus]
---

Περσεὺς δʼ ἀμνίον εἶχε, “And Perseus held the bowl for the blood” (3.444; see [[the-sacrifice-to-athena]]). ἀμνίον, the vessel in which the blood of the victim is caught, occurs only here in Homer (see the translation's table of key words); Merry thinks it probably connected with αἷμα, “blood”, and reports that Curtius suggested a connection with ἀμίς (*Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 3.444). Each son has his part in the rite: Stratius and Echephron lead the heifer, Aretus brings the water and the barley grains, Thrasymedes strikes with the axe and Peisistratus cuts the throat (3.439–454; see [[nestor]] and [[peisistratus]]).
