# Unnamed: "my own dear son" (3.111).
refs: [3.111]
---

ἔνθα μὲν Αἴας κεῖται ἀρήιος, ἔνθα δʼ Ἀχιλλεύς, / ἔνθα δὲ Πάτροκλος … ἔνθα δʼ ἐμὸς φίλος υἱός: “There lies warlike Ajax, there Achilles, / there Patroclus, … and there my own dear son” (3.109–111). The father names his son last, after Ajax, Achilles and Patroclus, and gives him a verse and a half of his own: ἅμα κρατερὸς καὶ ἀμύμων, “at once strong and noble”, and πέρι μὲν θείειν ταχὺς ἠδὲ μαχητής, “swift beyond others in running, and a fighter” (3.111–112; see [[ajax]], [[achilles]], [[patroclus]] and [[blameless]]). Merry compares ταχὺς ἔσκε θέειν, “was swift to run”, of the dog Argos (17.308), and the swift-footed Achilles (*Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 3.112). The half-verse comes again in the accusative when his brother Peisistratus, who never saw him, weeps for him at Sparta (4.202; see [[peisistratus]], [[nestor]] and [[fathers-and-sons]]).
