summary: Athena sends Telemachus to Nestor, who “will not tell a lie, for he is very wise” (3.20), and Nestor sends him on to Menelaus with the same words (3.328); Telemachus asks for the truth, and Nestor answers, “I will tell you all the truth” (3.254). The goddess goes among them as Mentor until she leaves in a bird's likeness, and Aegisthus wins Clytemnestra by “bewitching” her “with many words” (3.264).
refs: [3.19-20, 3.96-101, 3.187, 3.247, 3.254, 3.264, 3.327-328, 3.371-379]
---

**Truth asked and told.** The book's speakers ask for the truth and promise it: “tell me truly” (νημερτές, 3.101), “tell me the truth” (ἀληθές, 3.247), and “Well then, my child, I will tell you all the truth” (3.254, again 16.61), with the old man's promise to tell what he has heard, “as is the custom, and I will not hide it from you” (3.187; see [[pseudos]] and [[themis]]). Telemachus asks Nestor not to soften anything out of respect or pity (3.96; see [[pity]]). The trust is placed in the speaker: Nestor “will not tell a lie, for he is very wise” (3.20), and Menelaus too (3.327–328).

**The goddess in disguise.** Athena deceives for Telemachus, not against him. He calls her Mentor (3.22, 3.240), she claims the older man's place among his companions (3.362), and only her departure as a sea eagle shows Nestor who she is (3.371–379; see [[athena]], [[seeing-the-gods]] and [[in-the-likeness-of-mentor]]).

**Bewitching words.** Aegisthus, in a corner of Argos, πόλλʼ Ἀγαμεμνονέην ἄλοχον θέλγεσκʼ ἐπέεσσιν, “kept bewitching the wife of Agamemnon with many words” (3.264; see [[aegisthus]] and [[clytemnestra]]), with the verb of Calypso's “gentle and wheedling talk”, with which she bewitches Odysseus so that he may forget Ithaca (1.56–57; see [[calypso]]).
