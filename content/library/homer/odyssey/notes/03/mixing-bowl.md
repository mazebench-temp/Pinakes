summary: At sunset the young men fill the mixing bowls brim-full for the libation to Poseidon and the other gods (3.332, 3.339), and in his house Nestor mixes a bowl of his eleven-year-old wine and pours a libation to Athena (3.390–394).
# κρητῆρα (3.390, 3.393), first met in the Odyssey here; its English there is "a bowl" ("the old man mixed a bowl").
grc: [κρητῆρα]
en: [bowl]
---

**At sunset.** Athena bids the company “mix the wine” (κεράασθε, 3.332), the verb from which κρητήρ, the mixing bowl, takes its name, and “the young men filled the mixing bowls brim-full with drink” (3.339; see [[brim-full]] and [[cupbearing]]). The verse is the one that fills the bowls at Chryse and before the embassy in the Iliad and for the suitors in Odysseus' hall (*Iliad* 1.470, 9.175; 1.148, 21.271; see [[feast-type-scene]]).

**In Nestor's house.** For the guests who have come home with him, “the old man mixed a bowl / of wine sweet to drink, which in its eleventh year / the housekeeper opened” (3.390–392), and “of this the old man mixed a bowl” to pour a libation to Athena (3.393–394; see [[wine]] and [[libation]]). Elsewhere a herald mixes the bowl at his lord's bidding, as Alcinous bids Pontonous mix one and serve the wine to all (7.179–181, 13.50–52), and the herald Mulius mixes for the suitors (18.423); here the narrator gives the mixing to the old king himself. Fäsi took the verb to mean that he had the bowl mixed (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 3.390).
