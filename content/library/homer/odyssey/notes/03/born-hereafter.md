---
summary: Nestor ends the example of Orestes as Athena did in Book 1: “be valiant, so that some one of those born hereafter may speak well of you” (3.200).
---

The couplet of 3.199–200 is that of 1.301–302: the old king, who does not know the goddess beside him, gives Telemachus the advice she gave him as Mentes, with the same example ([[orestes]], [[strong-spear]]). Telemachus answers with the fame that posterity will hear of: the Achaeans “will carry his fame wide, even for men to come to hear of” (3.204; [[kleos]]).
