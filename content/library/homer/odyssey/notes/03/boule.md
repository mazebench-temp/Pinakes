---
summary: Nestor and Odysseus never spoke at odds “in the assembly or in the council”, but took counsel “with one heart, with mind and thoughtful counsel” (3.127–128); after the drunken assembly the Achaeans “were divided in their counsel” (3.150). Agamemnon “wished” to hold back the army (3.143), and Athena “would rather” come home late than die at her hearth (3.232).
grc: [βούλετο, βουλοίμην]
en: [wished]
---

**Council at Troy.** οὔτε ποτʼ εἰν ἀγορῇ δίχʼ ἐβάζομεν οὔτʼ ἐνὶ βουλῇ (3.127) sets the two bodies side by side, the assembly of the people and the council of the chiefs (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 3.127, referring to his note on 2.7; see [[assembly]]). Nestor's counsel with Odysseus is the opposite of the quarrel that follows: δίχα δέ σφισιν ἥνδανε βουλή, “and they were divided in their counsel” (3.150), when the brothers called the army together at sunset (see [[the-mind-of-the-gods-is-not-quickly-turned]] and [[eris]]). For δίχʼ ἐβάζομεν Nitzsch compared Herodotus' generals whose opinions were divided before Marathon, as Merry reports (on 3.127).

**Wish and preference.** βούλομαι, the verb of the noun, names Agamemnon's wish to stay and sacrifice (3.143) and Athena's preference, βουλοίμην δʼ ἂν ἐγώ γε, for a late homecoming over a death at the hearth (3.232–235; see [[a-god-can-save-a-man-from-afar]]); Merry lists the uses of βούλεσθαι with ἤ, “rather than”, in both poems (on 3.232).
