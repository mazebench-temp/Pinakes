summary: δαίμων is twice a god whom the speaker does not name. Athena, in the likeness of Mentor, tells Telemachus that “a god too will give you counsel” (3.27), and Nestor fled from Tenedos “since I knew that a god was devising evil” (3.166).
# "god" renders δαίμων at 2.134, 3.27 and 3.166, the lines the entry's form claims in the Odyssey.
en: [god]
---

**A god will give you counsel.** Telemachus fears that he has no practice in speech, and the goddess, in a man's likeness, answers that some things he will think of himself, “and in others a god too will give you counsel; for I do not think / that you were born and bred against the will of the gods” (3.27–28; see [[athena]] and [[some-things-you-will-think-of-yourself]]). The speaker is herself the god who will counsel him, and she does not say so. The narrator names her from the first: as Telemachus steps ashore, ἦρχε δʼ Ἀθήνη, “and Athena went before him” (3.12), where the Iliad, of Aphrodite leading Helen to Paris, says only ἦρχε δὲ δαίμων, “and the divinity led the way” (*Iliad* 3.420; see the Iliad Book 3 note).

**A god devising evil.** After the second strife at Tenedos, Nestor sailed off with all his ships, “since I knew that a god was devising evil” (3.165–166; see [[the-departure-of-the-greeks]] and [[the-returns-of-the-achaeans]]). In his tale of the voyage Nestor names Zeus as the author of the grim homecoming and of the second strife (3.132, 3.152, 3.160), Athena's wrath as its cause (3.135, 3.145), and Poseidon as the god to whom he sacrificed at Geraestus (3.178); but the god who scattered the Achaeans, smoothed the sea, showed the sign at Lesbos and sent the fair wind is only θεός, “a god” (3.131, 3.158, 3.173, 3.183; see [[theos]], [[zeus]] and [[teras]]). The δαίμων of 3.166 is of the same kind, a power felt at work and not identified. In Book 2 Telemachus said that “a god will give” him other evils if he sent his mother away against her will (2.134–135; see [[erinyes]]).
