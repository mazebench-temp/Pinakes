---
summary: Menelaus overtakes Nestor and Diomedes in Lesbos, the second day's stage from Troy, as they ponder the long voyage home, above Chios or below it; the god's sign sends them straight across the open sea to Euboea (3.168–175).
---

{{quote:3bba9f04-c264-5afd-bcfc-360d41795291}}

Lesbos is the second day's stage: Tenedos was the first, and Geraestus, after the crossing, the third (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 3.169; see [[tenedos]] and [[geraestus]]). ὀψέ, “late”, means here after a long interval, not late in the day, and μετὰ νῶι, “after us two”, is “to join us”, Nestor and Diomedes (Merry on 3.168; see [[menelaus]], [[diomedes]] and [[nestor]]). δολιχὸν πλόον, “the long voyage”, is the passage straight across the Aegean, shorter than the way round by the islands but a longer run without a port; the question was whether to take it or not, by the outer course above Chios, by Psyria, or by the inner course below it, past Mimas (Merry on 3.169; see [[chios]], [[psyria]] and [[mimas]]). They ask the god to show them a sign, and he bids them cut across the open sea; a shrill fair wind carries them over by night, and at Geraestus they sacrifice to Poseidon (3.173–179; see [[teras]] and [[poseidon]]).

Lesbos is otherwise a place of the Iliad's raids and of Odysseus' prowess: Achilles took “well-built Lesbos” (*Iliad* 9.129), and in the next book Menelaus remembers how Odysseus once rose in a contest there and threw Philomeleides in wrestling (4.342–344).
