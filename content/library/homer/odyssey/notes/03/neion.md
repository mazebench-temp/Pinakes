---
summary: Telemachus tells Nestor that he comes “from Ithaca under Neion” (3.81), the mountain's second and last mention in the poem.
---

ἐξ Ἰθάκης ὑπονηίου, “from Ithaca under Neion” (3.81): the adjective ὑπονήιος, found only here, makes the mountain a landmark of the town beneath it, as Mentes' ship lay “under wooded Neion” in Book 1 (1.186; see [[rheithron]] and [[ithaca]]). The scholia compare Θήβη ὑποπλακίη, “Thebe under Placus” (*Iliad* 6.397), and Merry takes the epithet of the town, not of the whole island (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 3.81; see [[thebe]]). Strabo read the words apart, ὑπὸ Νηίου, “under Neion”, and found it unclear whether Neion was the same as Neritum or another mountain or place (*Geography* 10.2.11; see [[neritum]]).
