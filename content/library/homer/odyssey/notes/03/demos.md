---
summary: Telemachus tells Nestor that his errand is “private, not public” (3.82); the Achaeans suffered “in the land of the Trojans” (3.100, 3.220), and Nestor asks whether the people “throughout the land” hate him (3.215).
grc: [δήμιος]
---

**Private and public.** πρῆξις δʼ ἥδʼ ἰδίη, οὐ δήμιος (3.82) is the distinction Telemachus drew in the assembly the day before, when he said that he brought no public matter but “a business of my own” (2.44–45; see [[prexis]] and [[the-city-and-the-household]]); at Sparta Menelaus will ask him the same question, δήμιον ἦ ἴδιον (4.314).

**The land.** δῆμος is a land with its people: δήμῳ ἔνι Τρώων, “in the land of the Trojans”, where the Achaeans suffered woes and sorrows (3.100, 3.220; and 3.104), and ἀνὰ δῆμον, “throughout the land” (3.215), where the people may hate a king's son (see [[laos]]).
