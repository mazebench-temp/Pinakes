---
summary: Telemachus would take vengeance on the suitors “for their grievous transgression”, men “who treat me with outrage and devise reckless deeds” (3.206–207).
grc: [ὑπερβασίης]
en: [transgression]
---

ὑβρίζοντες ἀτάσθαλα μηχανόωνται is the verse the poem gives the suitors again and again (3.207, 17.588, 20.170, 20.370; see [[atasthalie]] and [[the-suitors]]). In the Iliad it is Nestor's, for the Epeians who “treated us with outrage and devised reckless deeds” when Heracles had weakened Pylos (*Iliad* 11.689–695; see [[nestor]]), and Telemachus now says it to Nestor of the men in his own house. Here Telemachus names their offense ὑπερβασίη, “transgression”, a going beyond (ὑπερβαίνω) what is due. The word is Antilochus' for a young man's offense, “You know what the transgressions of a young man are like” (*Iliad* 23.589), and its verb is Phoenix's for the man who “oversteps and does wrong” (*Iliad* 9.501). The suitors will be made to pay for the whole of it, πρὶν πᾶσαν μνηστῆρας ὑπερβασίην ἀποτῖσαι (22.64; see [[poine]]). Nestor's word for the same men is ὑπερφίαλοι, “overbearing” (3.315; see [[overbearing]]).
