---
summary: The Pylians sacrifice “bulls all black, to the dark-haired earth-shaker” (3.6).
---

ἐνοσίχθονι κυανοχαίτῃ: the two epithets stand for Poseidon's name at the head of the book ([[earth-shaker]]). Merry takes κυανοχαίτης as the color Tennyson calls “blue-black hair,” notes that the Hymn to Demeter gives it to Hades and the Iliad to a horse, and explains the black victims, offered elsewhere to the powers below or to Earth, as emblems of the dark sea, or, after Nitzsch, of the god's terrible power (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 3.6; [[the-sacrifice-at-pylos]], [[bulls]]). See [[poseidon]].
