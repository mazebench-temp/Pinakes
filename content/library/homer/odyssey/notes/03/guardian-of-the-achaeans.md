---
summary: Nestor takes his father's seat on the polished stones, “the Gerenian, the guardian of the Achaeans” (3.411), the Odyssey's only use of the title.
---

Νέστωρ αὖ τότʼ ἐφῖζε Γερήνιος, οὖρος Ἀχαιῶν: the second half of the verse is the Iliad's (*Iliad* 8.80, 11.840, 15.370, 15.659; [[gerenian-horseman]]). Merry derives οὖρος, “warder,” from the root of ὁράω, as in ἐπίουρος and φρουρός, and compares the Latin *tueri* for the link between guarding and seeing (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 3.411). The guardian now holds the scepter among his sons, where Neleus, “a counselor equal to the gods,” used to sit (3.408–412; [[counselor-equal-to-the-gods]], [[scepter]], [[nestor]]).
