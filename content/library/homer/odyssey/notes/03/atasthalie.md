---
summary: The suitors are men “who treat me with outrage and devise reckless deeds” (3.207).
grc: [ἀτάσθαλα]
en: [reckless deeds]
---

Telemachus' line is the poem's formula for the suitors (17.588, 20.170, 20.370; see [[hybris]] and [[the-suitors]]), and ἀτάσθαλα is the neuter plural of ἀτάσθαλος, the adjective of the proem's ἀτασθαλίῃσιν (1.7). The book's other wrongdoers are named with other words: the Argives, “in no way discerning or just” (3.133; see [[noos]] and [[themis]]), and Aegisthus, whose μέγα ἔργον, “very great … deed” (3.261), Merry takes in a bad sense, “audacious, shameless, impious” (*Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 3.261; see [[aegisthus]]).
