---
summary: Tenedos is the first day's run from Troy for the half of the army that sailed at once. There they sacrificed to the gods, eager to go home, and Zeus roused a second strife: Odysseus and his men turned back to Agamemnon, while Nestor and Diomedes fled on (3.159–167).
---

“And when we came to Tenedos we offered sacrifice to the gods, / eager to go home; but Zeus was not yet devising our homecoming, / the hard god, who roused evil strife against us once more, a second time” (3.159–161; see [[zeus]] and [[the-departure-of-the-greeks]]). Tenedos, opposite Troy, is the first day's journey (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 3.159), and the sacrifice there is made, as Merry reads οἴκαδε ἱέμενοι, “eager to go home”, to win the gods' favor for the voyage (on 3.160; see [[sacrifice]]). σχέτλιος, “the hard god”, is one who holds on with a firm grip, “the very opposite of ‘self-sparing’”, like the Latin *improbus* (Merry on 3.161; see [[hard]]).

The second quarrel divides those who had sailed. “Some turned back and went off in their curving ships / with lord Odysseus, wise-hearted, of subtle wiles, / to do a kindness once more to Agamemnon, son of Atreus” (3.162–164; see [[odysseus]], [[agamemnon]] and [[curving]]), and so Tenedos is the last place where Nestor saw Odysseus, and the end of what he can tell Telemachus of his father (3.184–185; see [[the-returns-of-the-achaeans]]). Nestor fled with his ships, and Diomedes with his companions; Menelaus came after them, late, and overtook them at Lesbos (3.165–169; see [[diomedes]], [[menelaus]] and [[lesbos]]). In the Iliad Tenedos is Apollo's island and the island Achilles sacked, from which Nestor had his captive Hecamede (*Iliad* 1.38, 11.624–627; see [[hecamede]]).
