---
summary: The storm off Malea carries five of Menelaus' ships to Egypt, where he gathers much substance and gold and wanders among men of another tongue while Aegisthus rules Mycenae (3.299–305); Nestor calls the sea he came home from so great that not even the birds come back from it within the year (3.318–322). It is the Odyssey's first mention of Egypt.
---

**Five ships to Egypt.** When the storm split the fleet, “the five dark-prowed ships / the wind and the water carried and drove to Egypt” (3.299–300; see [[malea]] and [[crete]]). κυανοπρῳρείους, “dark-prowed”, is a lengthened form, for the meter, of the adjective of the phrase νεὸς κυανοπρῴροιο, “of the dark-prowed ship”, which Homer has thirteen times (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 3.299; see [[dark-prowed]]). For a ship carried from Malea to Africa Merry compares Jason, whom Herodotus has driven to Libya by the North Wind (Herodotus 4.179; Merry on 3.299).

**Wealth among strangers.** “So he, gathering much substance and gold there, / wandered with his ships among men of another tongue” (3.301–302; see [[menelaus]] and [[the-wanderings-of-menelaus]]). ἀγείρων, “gathering”, points to gifts received, as Merry and Nitzsch read it: in the next book Menelaus' house is full of the presents of Polybus of Egyptian Thebes and his wife (4.125–132; Merry on 3.301; see [[thebes-in-egypt]] and [[guest-gifts]]). ἀλλόθροοι, “of another tongue”, is the word of Mentes' voyage to Temese (1.183; see [[temese]]). Meanwhile Aegisthus did his work at home and ruled Mycenae for seven years (3.303–305; see [[the-murder-of-agamemnon]]).

**A sea so great.** Nestor sends Telemachus to Menelaus as to a man who has come home from lands that no one would expect to return from, once the storms have driven him “into a sea so great, from which not even the birds / come back within the year” (3.317–322; see [[birds]], [[sea]] and [[the-journey-of-telemachus]]). The scholia call it hyperbole, and Merry sets beside it the different account of Odysseus' false tale, in which a ship runs from Crete to Egypt in five days before a fair North Wind (14.252–257; Merry on 3.321).
