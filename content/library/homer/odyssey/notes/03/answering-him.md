summary: The verses of reply in the dialogue at Pylos: Telemachus' “And wise Telemachus answered her face to face” or “answered him,” five times (3.21, 3.75, 3.201, 3.225, 3.239), and Athena's “Then the gray-eyed goddess Athena spoke to him in turn,” three times (3.25, 3.229, 3.356).
---

Nestor's replies have a verse of their own, “Then the Gerenian horseman Nestor answered him” (3.102, 3.210, 3.253; [[then-answered]], [[gerenian-horseman]]), and the goddess speaks first when they land (3.13; [[spoke-first]]). The exchanges between Telemachus and the goddess frame his doubts: his shame before an older man (3.21–24), and his despair of his father (3.225–228, 3.239–242), which she answers each time (3.25–28, 3.229–238). At 3.75 the verse runs on into the next, θαρσήσας, “taking heart; for Athena herself had put daring in his mind” (3.75–76; [[tharsos]]). See [[telemachus]] and [[wise]].
