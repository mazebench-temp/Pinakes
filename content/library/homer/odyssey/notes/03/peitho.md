---
summary: Athena “would not be persuaded” by Agamemnon's hecatombs (3.146); she tells Nestor that “it is fitting / that Telemachus should obey you” (3.357–358); and Nestor's sons “listened closely to him and obeyed” (3.477).
grc: [ἐπίθοντο]
---

The middle is both “be persuaded” and “obey”. The goddess cannot be persuaded quickly (3.146–147; see [[the-mind-of-the-gods-is-not-quickly-turned]]); the young man is bidden to obey the old one, ἐπεὶ πολὺ κάλλιον οὕτως, “since it is much better so” (3.358), where Merry joins σοὶ πείθεσθαι (*Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 3.357), as Nestor in the Iliad told Agamemnon and Achilles, “it is better to obey” (*Iliad* 1.274; see [[better-to-obey]] and [[obedience-and-persuasion]]). His sons obey him at once when he bids them yoke the horses (3.475–478).
