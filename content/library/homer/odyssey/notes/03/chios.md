---
summary: At Lesbos the kings ponder whether to go home “above rocky Chios” or “below Chios”, the only verses in which Homer names the island (3.170–172).
---

Chios is named twice in three verses, as the landmark of the two courses home from Lesbos: “whether we should go home above rocky Chios, / by the island of Psyria, keeping it on our left, / or below Chios, past windy Mimas” (3.170–172; see [[lesbos]], [[psyria]] and [[mimas]]). καθύπερθε, “above”, is to seaward of the island, on its north and west, where the straight course across the Aegean runs, and ὑπένερθε, “below”, inside it, through the channel between Chios and the mainland of Asia (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 3.169). παιπαλόεσσα, “rocky”, describes for Merry the rugged lines of upheaved rock on the Chian coast (on 3.170; see [[rocky]]). The island that later claimed Homer is thus seen from the sea, by a fleet that does not touch it.
