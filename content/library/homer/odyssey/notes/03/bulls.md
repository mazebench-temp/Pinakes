# ταύρους (3.6, 3.8), first met in the Odyssey here; again of the twelve bulls the Phaeacians make ready for Poseidon
# (13.181, 13.184).
grc: [ταύρους]
---

**On the shore.** ταύρους παμμέλανας, ἐνοσίχθονι κυανοχαίτῃ: “bulls all black, to the dark-haired earth-shaker” (3.6; see [[the-sacrifice-at-pylos]], [[poseidon]], [[dark-haired]] and [[earth-shaker]]). Each of the nine companies “held nine bulls before them” (3.8): προύχοντο, of the victims set out before the sacrificers, ready for the rite (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 3.8, comparing the spears held out before them at *Iliad* 17.355; see the translation's note on 3.8). That makes eighty-one bulls, and Athena calls the sacrifice a “famous hecatomb” (3.59). Merry notes that it is no hundred, and that the word is used loosely of any great offering, even of one with no oxen in it (on 3.58, citing *Iliad* 23.146; see [[hecatomb]]).

**All black.** παμμέλας, “all black”, is given in the Odyssey only to these bulls and to the ram that Odysseus is to sacrifice to Teiresias alone, the best of the flock (10.524–525, 11.32–33). Black victims, Merry observes, were generally offered to the powers below or to Earth, as the black ewe is brought for Earth in the oath of *Iliad* 3 (*Iliad* 3.103–104; see [[sheep]]). He takes the color here as an emblem of the dark sea, or, with Nitzsch, as a sign of the god's terrible power, which set him beside the gods below (on 3.6). In the verse itself the color of the victims answers the color the god is given, κυανοχαίτης, “dark-haired”.

**At Geraestus.** On the voyage home from Troy, when the god's sign had sent Nestor's ships straight across the open sea from Lesbos, they put in at Geraestus by night, “and for Poseidon, of bulls, / we laid many thigh-pieces on the altar, having measured the great open sea” (3.178–179; see [[geraestus]], [[thigh-pieces]] and [[divination]]). The bulls are offered to the sea god when the crossing is made. The bull is the Odyssey's victim for Poseidon throughout: with rams at the hecatomb of the Ethiopians (1.25), with a ram and a boar in the sacrifice Teiresias prescribes (11.130–131), and twelve chosen bulls when the Phaeacians fear his anger (13.181–184; see the Book 1 note). In the Iliad, Nestor remembers offering him a bull by the Alpheus in his youth (*Iliad* 11.728).

**Later.** In the Hesiodic *Shield* Poseidon is ταύρεος Ἐννοσίγαιος, the Earth-shaker “of the bulls” (*Shield* 104), a title that Merry takes to refer to the sacrifice of bulls to him. He also notes that in Pindar and Virgil the bull sacrificed to Poseidon is white (on 3.6, citing Pindar, *Olympian* 13.69, and *Aeneid* 5.237).
