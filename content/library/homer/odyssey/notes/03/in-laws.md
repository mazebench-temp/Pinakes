summary: Nestor leads “his sons and his sons-in-law” to his house (3.387), and at the sacrifice the women raise the cry, “his daughters and his sons' wives” with Nestor's wife (3.451).
grc: [γαμβροῖσιν, νυοί]
en: [sons-in-law, sons' wives]
---

Nestor's house is a household like Priam's, where the married sons and daughters live with their husbands and wives in chambers about the court (*Iliad* 6.242–250; see [[nestor]] and [[priam]]). After the libation the sons and sons-in-law go to lie down, “each to his own house”, and only the unmarried Peisistratus sleeps beside Telemachus (3.396–401); in the morning they come to Nestor “from their chambers” (3.413). Merry takes these θάλαμοι as detached buildings, as the chambers of Priam's sons-in-law are, since their occupants were all married (*Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 3.396; see [[peisistratus]] and [[house]]). At the sacrifice the daughters-in-law raise the cry at the sacrifice with the king's daughters and his wife (3.450–452; see [[ololyge]] and [[eurydice]]).
