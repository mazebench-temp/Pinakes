---
summary: Nestor, hearing Telemachus speak, says “awe holds me as I look at you. / For truly your words are like his” (3.123–124).
---

Merry takes the amazement as at the likeness that the next verses trace, and argues at length, against Nitzsch, that ἐοικότες … ἐοικότα means “like his,” not “fitting”: a young man would not speak so like an older one unless the two were father and son (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 3.123, 3.124; see the translation's note on 3.124–125). In Sparta Helen will say the same words of his looks (4.141–144). See [[fathers-and-sons]], [[telemachus]] and [[odysseus]].
