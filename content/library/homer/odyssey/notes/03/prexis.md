---
summary: Athena prays to Poseidon that she and Telemachus may return “when we have done the business” for which they came (3.60); Nestor asks the strangers whether they sail “on some business” or at random, like pirates (3.72), and Telemachus answers, “this business I speak of is private, not public” (3.82).
grc: [πρήξαντα, πρήσσῃσιν]
en: [done the business, make his way]
---

**The business of the voyage.** The goddess names it first, in her prayer at the sacrifice on the shore, without saying what it is: δὸς δʼ ἔτι Τηλέμαχον καὶ ἐμὲ πρήξαντα νέεσθαι (3.60; see [[nostos]] and [[athena]]). Nestor's question to the strangers sets the word against wandering, κατὰ πρῆξιν ἦ μαψιδίως, “on some business, or … at random” (3.72; see [[piracy]] and the translation's table of key words), and Merry takes the business of such a voyage to be trade (*Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 3.72). Telemachus' answer makes it a matter of his house: πρῆξις δʼ ἥδʼ ἰδίη, οὐ δήμιος, “this business I speak of is private, not public” (3.82), the distinction he drew in the assembly the day before, when he told Aegyptius that he brought no public matter but “a business of my own” (ἐμὸν αὐτοῦ χρεῖος, 2.44–45; see [[demos]]). The business is news of his father (3.83; see [[kleos]]).

**On the road.** The verb returns when the voyage becomes a journey by land: Nestor bids his sons yoke the horses for Telemachus, ἵνα πρήσσῃσιν ὁδοῖο, “so that he may make his way” (3.476), with ὁδοῖο a partitive genitive (Merry on 3.476; see [[the-journey-of-telemachus]]).
