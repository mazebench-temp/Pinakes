---
summary: When Menelaus, sailing on after Sunium, reaches the steep mountain of Malea, Zeus pours the breath of shrill winds on him and splits his fleet, driving some ships to Crete and five to Egypt (3.286–300).
---

{{quote:d0b366b0-604f-56a9-a9d1-fee6e53bacec}}

καὶ κεῖνος, “he too”: Menelaus sets sail as Nestor had done, once he has buried his helmsman at Sunium (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 3.286; see [[sunium]] and [[phrontis-son-of-onetor]]). Nestor's own ships came home to Pylos with a fair wind that never died down (3.182–183); for Menelaus, Malea is where Zeus devises “a hateful journey” (3.288; see [[zeus]] and [[far-seeing]]). Μαλειάων ὄρος αἰπύ, “the steep mountain of Malea”, ends the verse here and again in Book 4, where Agamemnon, nearing the cape, is caught up by a storm (4.514; see [[steep]]). Merry gives the other spellings of the name and the saying that Eustathius quotes, “when you round Malea, forget the things at home” (Merry on 3.287).

The waves “swelled huge”: τροφέοντο, as the text here prints it, where Merry's text reads τροφόεντα, “big”, a second object of “poured” (see the translation's note on 3.290). διατμήξας, “having parted the fleet in twain”, divides the ships into the two groups that follow, those driven to Crete (τὰς μέν, 3.291) and the five carried to Egypt (τὰς πέντε, 3.299; Merry on 3.291; see [[crete]] and [[egypt]]). The storm begins the years of wandering that keep Menelaus away while Aegisthus rules at Mycenae (3.301–305; see [[the-wanderings-of-menelaus]] and [[mycenae]]).
