**The citadel of Neleus.** The book's first landfall names him: οἱ δὲ Πύλον, Νηλῆος ἐυκτίμενον πτολίεθρον, “and they came to Pylos, the well-built citadel of Neleus” (3.4; see [[pylos]] and [[well-built]]), as in Book 4 Pylos is Πύλον … Νηλήιον, “Neleian Pylos” (4.639).

**The patronymic.** Telemachus addresses Nestor three times as Νέστορ Νηληϊάδη, “Nestor, son of Neleus” (3.79, 3.202, 3.247; see [[son-of-neleus]] and [[great-glory-of-the-achaeans]]), and Polycaste is “the youngest daughter of Nestor, son of Neleus” (3.465; see [[polycaste]]). The Iliad has both Νηληϊάδης and Νηλεΐδης (*Iliad* 23.652): the endings are -ιάδης and -ίδης, and the stem has a doubtful vowel, as in Νηλῆος beside Νηλέος (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 3.79).

**The seat.** At dawn Nestor goes out to the stones before his doors:

{{quote:9727a012-20a8-58fc-b175-19e5973bd575}}

θεόφιν μήστωρ ἀτάλαντος, “a counselor equal to the gods”, ends the verse here as it does for Patroclus in Nestor's tale (3.110; see [[counselor-equal-to-the-gods]]). The verse of his death, ἀλλʼ ὁ μὲν ἤδη κηρὶ δαμεὶς Ἄϊδόσδε βεβήκει, “but he had already been overcome by his doom and gone to the house of Hades” (3.410; see [[ker-doom]] and [[hades]]), comes again in Book 6 of Nausithous, the founder of the Phaeacian city, and there too his son rules after him: “and Alcinous was ruling then” (6.11–12, a plain rendering; Alcinous is his son, 7.63). The seat has passed from father to son, and Nestor sits on it “holding the scepter” (3.411–412; see [[nestor]], [[scepter]] and [[fathers-and-sons]]). In the Iliad Neleus is alive and king when his son is young (*Iliad* 11.683–684, 11.717–719); here he is dead.
