summary: When Telemachus sets out from Pylos for Sparta, the housekeeper puts bread and wine in the chariot, and meats such as kings whom Zeus has nurtured eat (3.479–480).
refs: [3.479-480]
---

“And a woman, the housekeeper, put bread and wine in it, / and meats, such as kings whom Zeus has nurtured eat” (3.479–480; see [[handmaids]], [[zeus-nurtured]] and [[the-journey-of-telemachus]]). The voyage from Ithaca was stocked from the storeroom of Odysseus (2.349–356); the journey by land is stocked by the host. ὄψα, W. W. Merry notes, is flesh-meat, properly what is cooked (ὀπτός), while ἤια, the word for provisions, is bread, here called σῖτος (*Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 3.480).
