---
summary: Peisistratus bids the stranger pour libation and pray “as is the custom” (3.45), and Nestor promises to tell all he has heard, “as is the custom” (3.187). Athena rejoices in “the wise and just man” (3.52); the Argives were “in no way discerning or just” (3.133); and Nestor “knows judgments and wisdom” beyond all others (3.244).
grc: [θέμις, δίκας, δικαίῳ, δίκαιοι]
en: [custom, just, judgments]
---

**As is the custom.** ἣ θέμις ἐστί marks what is done because it is right: the libation and prayer of a stranger who comes upon a sacrifice (3.45; see [[all-men-need-the-gods]] and [[prayer]]), and an old man's telling of what he knows to the young man who asks (3.187; see [[nestor]]). Merry takes ἥ as the relative pronoun assimilated to its noun, against the old grammarians who made it an adverb equal to ὡς, and lists the phrase's uses in both poems (*Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 3.45).

**Just men.** δίκαιος is the adjective of δίκη, and in the Odyssey it is a man who keeps the ways of men. Merry renders δικαίῳ “proper”: as δίκη is the common custom or usage of men, the δίκαιος is one who observes it, and the thought joins knowledge to propriety, as in πεπνυμένῳ ἀνδρὶ δικαίῳ (on 3.52; see [[wise]]). Peisistratus, who gives the cup first to the elder stranger, is such a man (3.52); the Argives, on whom Zeus laid a grim homecoming, were not, οὔ τι νοήμονες οὐδὲ δίκαιοι (3.133), the words Athena used of the suitors in Book 2 (2.282; see [[noos]] and [[nostos]]).

**Judgments.** Telemachus turns to Nestor ἐπεὶ περὶ οἶδε δίκας ἠδὲ φρόνιν ἄλλων (3.244). Merry takes the plural δίκας as perhaps “rules of right”, so that Nestor would be the best man to consult on the dealings of man with man, and joins περίοιδε … ἄλλων, “knows better than any other men”; others join φρόνιν ἄλλων, “what other men think” (on 3.244; see the translation's note on 3.244–245). The next verse gives the ground of his knowledge, three generations of rule (3.245; see [[genee]] and [[old-age-and-memory]]).
