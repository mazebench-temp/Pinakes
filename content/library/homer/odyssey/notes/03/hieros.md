---
summary: “Holy” three times in the tales of the returns: the “holy hecatombs” that Agamemnon wished to offer (3.144), the “holy altars” on which Aegisthus burned thigh-pieces (3.273), and “holy Sunium, the cape of Athens” (3.278).
---

ἱερὰς ἑκατόμβας (3.144), ἱεροῖς ἐπὶ βωμοῖς (3.273), Σούνιον ἱρόν (3.278). Merry refers the epithet of Sunium to the temple of Athena whose Doric columns gave the cape its modern name, Colonna, or to the worship that seafaring men paid there to Poseidon, and reports Damm's view that Zeus Sounieus was worshipped there (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 3.278; [[sunium]]). The neuter plural ἱερά, ἱρά, “sacrifices,” is used in the book for the offerings themselves (3.5, 3.159, 3.436; [[sacrifice]]).
