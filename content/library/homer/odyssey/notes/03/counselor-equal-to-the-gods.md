---
summary: Two men are “a counselor equal to the gods”: Patroclus among the dead at Troy (3.110), and Neleus, who used to sit on the polished stones before his son Nestor (3.409).
---

θεόφιν μήστωρ ἀτάλαντος ends the verse both times. Patroclus has it in Nestor's list of the best who lie at Troy, Ajax, Achilles, Patroclus and Antilochus (3.109–112; [[patroclus]]); Neleus in the verses of the seat before the doors, which his son takes now that he “had already been overcome by his doom and gone to the house of Hades” (3.408–411; [[neleus]], [[polished]]).
