---
summary: The river is named as an ancestor: Diocles of Pherae, Telemachus' host for the night, is the son of Ortilochus, “whom Alpheus begot as his son” (3.488–489).
---

Diocles is “son of Ortilochus, whom Alpheus begot as his son” (3.489; see [[diocles]], [[ortilochus]] and [[pherae-in-messenia]]). The river is a god and the father of a royal house, as in the Iliad's genealogy of the same family: Alpheus, “which flows broad through the land of the Pylians, / who begot Ortilochus, lord over many men” (*Iliad* 5.545–546). τὸν … παῖδα, “whom … as his son”, is Ortilochus, not Diocles. The verse returns when Telemachus spends the night at Pherae again on the way home (15.187). Nestor's Pylians, in the tale of his youth, sacrificed “a bull to Alpheus, and a bull to Poseidon” by its stream (*Iliad* 11.726–728; see [[pylians]]).
