**Landing.** The crew put straight in, haul up and furl the sails, anchor the ship and step out (3.10–11). Merry contrasts the landing at Chryse, where the sail is lowered and stowed and the mast let down into its rest: here the sail is brailed up to the yard without lowering, so that it can be shaken out at a moment's notice (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 3.11, comparing *Iliad* 1.432–437; see the translation's note on 3.11).

**Nestor's voyage** keeps the steps of the pattern: the ships dragged down into the shining sea and loaded with possessions and captive women (3.153–154; [[shining-sea]], [[deep-girdled]]), embarking and rowing away (3.157), a calm sent by a god (3.158; [[huge-hulled]]), a sacrifice at Tenedos (3.159), the deliberation at Lesbos and the god's sign (3.169–175; [[teras]], [[divination]]), a shrill fair wind over the fish-filled ways (3.176–177; [[shrill]], [[fish-filled]]), and landfall by night at Geraestus, with thigh-pieces of bulls for Poseidon (3.177–179; [[geraestus]]). Merry counts the stages, Tenedos the first day, Lesbos the second and Geraestus the third, as Diomedes' ships reach Argos on the fourth (3.180; Merry on 3.169; [[the-returns-of-the-achaeans]]).

**Menelaus' voyage** goes wrong at every step: Apollo kills his helmsman at Sunium (3.278–285; [[phrontis-son-of-onetor]]), Zeus sends the storm off Malea that splits the fleet (3.286–290; [[malea]]), and the ships are wrecked on the reefs of Crete or carried to Egypt (3.291–300; [[the-wanderings-of-menelaus]]).
