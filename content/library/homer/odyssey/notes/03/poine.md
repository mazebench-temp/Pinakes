---
summary: Vengeance is the book's answer to the murder of Agamemnon: Aegisthus “paid for it miserably”, since Orestes “took vengeance on his father's slayer” (3.195–197, 3.203). Telemachus wishes for strength “to take vengeance on the suitors for their grievous transgression” (3.206), and Nestor asks, “Who knows if he will come someday and take vengeance on them for their violence” (3.216).
---

**Orestes.** ἀπέτισεν, “paid for it” (3.195), is the killer's payment, the word Zeus used of Aegisthus in the council of the gods, “and now he has paid back all at once” (1.43); ἐτίσατο, “took vengeance”, is the son's (3.197, 3.203; see [[orestes]], [[aegisthus]] and [[so-good-a-thing-is-a-son]]). The book tells the payment later, when Orestes comes back from Athens in the eighth year, kills his father's slayer, and gives the Argives a funeral feast (3.306–310; see [[orestes-kills-clytemnestra]] and [[honoring-the-dead]]).

**Telemachus' wish.** He takes the example for himself, as a wish: αἲ γὰρ ἐμοὶ τοσσήνδε θεοὶ δύναμιν περιθεῖεν, / τίσασθαι μνηστῆρας ὑπερβασίης ἀλεγεινῆς (3.205–206). Merry renders “invest me with power as great as his”, takes τοσσήνδε as “so great now, and in my person”, with τίσασθαι epexegetic of δύναμιν, and notes that τίσασθαί τινά τινος, “punish someone for something”, occurs again only in Menelaus' “Truly I thought I would pay back Alexander for his wickedness” (*Iliad* 3.366) (*Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 3.205, 3.206; see [[hybris]]). The gods, he answers himself, have spun him no such fortune (3.208–209; see [[olbos]]).

**Who knows.** Nestor turns the wish into a question: “Who knows if he will come someday and take vengeance on them for their violence, / either he alone or all the Achaeans together?” (3.216–217), the vengeance Athena, as Mentes, left on the knees of the gods, “whether he will return and take vengeance, or not” (1.267–268; see [[on-the-knees-of-the-gods]] and [[the-return-of-odysseus]]). Merry takes βίας as an accusative plural, “violence”, the plural reversing the sense of the singular βίη, “might” (on 3.216).
