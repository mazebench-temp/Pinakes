---
summary: Hephaestus: “how Aphrodite … / always dishonors me, and loves destructive Ares, / because he is handsome and sound of foot” (8.308–310).
---

Merry takes ἀΐδηλος in Homer as “annihilating,” “making unseen,” as distinct from the later sense “unseen,” and reports derivations that would make it “fiercely burning” or “that on which one cannot look” (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 8.309; see the translation's note on 8.309). Zeus has called Ares so in the Iliad, “destructive as you are” (*Iliad* 5.897); the husband now uses the father's word of the lover ([[ares]], [[hephaestus]]).
