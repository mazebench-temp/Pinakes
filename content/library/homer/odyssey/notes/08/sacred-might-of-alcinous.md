---
summary: The periphrasis names the king five times in the book: he rises at dawn and leads the Phaeacians to the place of assembly (8.2, 8.4), rejoices at the stranger's praise of the dancers (8.385), leads the lords back to his house with the gifts (8.421), and, as “the might of Alcinous,” speaks to Arete (8.423).
---

The same verse, “And the sacred might of Alcinous led the way for them” (8.4, 8.421), leads the people out to the place of assembly in the morning and the kings home to the palace in the evening. Each time the king acts as king: he rises to give a stranger his escort, accepts the stranger's praise, and orders the gifts stored and the bath heated (8.385–395, 8.423–432). The song of the book has the same turn of speech for a god, μένος Ἡφαίστοιο, “the might of Hephaestus loosed the bonds” (8.359), found only there in Homer ([[alcinous]], [[hieros]], [[arete-queen-of-the-phaeacians]], [[hephaestus]]).
