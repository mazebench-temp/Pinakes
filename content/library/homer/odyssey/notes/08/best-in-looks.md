---
summary: Euryalus, the son of Naubolus, “who was the best in looks and form / of all the Phaeacians after noble Laodamas” (8.116–117).
grc: ["ἄριστος ἔην εἶδός τε δέμας τε"]
---

The two verses are said of Ajax in the Underworld, the best of the Danaans in looks and form after the son of Peleus (11.469–470, 24.17–18), and the Iliad has the same second verse for Nireus, “the most handsome man who came to Ilios / of all the Danaans, after the noble son of Peleus” (*Iliad* 2.673–674), and for Ajax (*Iliad* 17.279–280). Odysseus answers Euryalus' taunt by granting him his looks and denying him a mind: “your looks are outstanding … but in mind you are worthless” (8.176–177; see the translation's note on 8.116; [[euryalus-the-phaeacian]], [[laodamas-son-of-alcinous]], [[eidos]], [[different-gifts]]).
