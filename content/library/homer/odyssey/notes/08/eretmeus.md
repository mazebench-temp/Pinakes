---
summary: One of the young men who rise for the games (8.112); he wins none of the contests and is not named again.
---

He closes the second verse of the list (8.112; see [[nauteus]] and [[the-games-of-the-phaeacians]]). The oar of his name is in the book's first scene, when the young men chosen for the escort make the oars fast “at the rowing benches” and fit them “in their leather straps” (8.37, 8.53; see [[oars]] and [[rowing-benches]]). He wins none of the five contests (8.120–130).
