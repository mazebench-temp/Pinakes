---
summary: In the song of the horse Odysseus, “like Ares”, goes with Menelaus to the house of Deiphobus, and “there, he said, he dared the most dread fight, / and conquered in the end, through great-hearted Athena” (8.517–520).
---

νικῆσαι καὶ ἔπειτα is “did after all conquer”: καὶ ἔπειτα resumes αἰνότατον πόλεμον τολμήσαντα, and is not a displaced ἔπειτα καί (W. W. Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 8.520). The victory of the last night of Troy is Odysseus', with Athena's help, at the house of Deiphobus (8.517–520; see [[deiphobus]], [[athena]] and [[the-fall-of-troy]]); the man who won it weeps to hear it sung (8.521–531; see [[tears]]).
