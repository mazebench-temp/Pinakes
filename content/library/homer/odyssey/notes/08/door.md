---
summary: In Demodocus' song Hephaestus stands in the doorway of his house and calls the gods, and the gods stand in the doorway to see the lovers in their bonds (8.304, 8.325). In the house of Alcinous Nausicaa stands by the doorpost of the hall to take leave of the stranger (8.458).
---

**In the doorway.** Hephaestus comes home “and stood in the doorway, and a savage anger gripped him; / and he shouted terribly, and called out to all the gods” (8.304–305; see [[hephaestus]] and [[cholos]]). The gods come, “And the gods, the givers of blessings, stood in the doorway; / and unquenchable laughter rose among the blessed gods” (8.325–326; see [[olympians]], [[givers-of-blessings]] and [[laughter-of-the-gods]]): ἐν προθύροισι, the place where a visitor stands before he is received (see the entry), is where the gods stand to look in at the bed (see [[bed]] and [[the-bonds-of-hephaestus]]). The goddesses stay at home (8.324).

**By the doorpost.** After the bath, “Nausicaa, who had her beauty from the gods, / stood by the doorpost of the strongly made hall” (8.457–458; see [[nausicaa]] and the translation's row πύκα ποιητός). The verse is Penelope's when she comes down to the suitors and stands by the doorpost of her hall (1.333; again 16.415, 18.209, 21.64).
