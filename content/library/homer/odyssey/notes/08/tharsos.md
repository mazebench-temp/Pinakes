---
summary: Athena, in form like a man, marks the stranger's throw and tells him, “Take heart about this contest at least: / none of the Phaeacians will reach this or throw beyond it” (8.197–198).
---

θάρσει is Homer's word of encouragement, and here a god gives it at the games. ἄεθλον is an accusative of reference with θάρσει, “be of good heart as to this trial of strength at any rate” (W. W. Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 8.197). τόδε γʼ is Aristarchus' reading; others had τόν γʼ or τόνδʼ (Merry's critical note and on 8.198; see the translation's note on 8.198). The stranger is glad, and speaks more lightly (8.199–201; see [[athena]] and [[eneeie]]).
