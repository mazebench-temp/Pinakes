# μητιόωσα (8.9 = 6.14).
grc: [μητιόωσα]
en: [devising]
---

The verse of Athena's purpose, νόστον Ὀδυσσῆι μεγαλήτορι μητιόωσα, “devising a homecoming for great-hearted Odysseus” (8.9), is the verse with which she went to Nausicaa's bedroom in Book 6 (6.14; see [[athena]] and [[nostos]]): the goddess's cunning is spent on his return. His own is in his epithet, πολύμητις, “of many wiles”, in six of the formulas that introduce his speeches in the book (8.152, 8.165, 8.412, 8.463, 8.474, 8.486; see [[of-many-wiles]] and [[odysseus]]). The book does not show his wiles at work; it hears of them, in the song of the horse that he brought into the citadel as a snare (8.492–495; see [[dolos]]), and Alcinous asks him not to hide anything with crafty thoughts (8.548; see [[kerdos]]). The craft that wins in the song is Hephaestus' (τέχνῃσι, 8.332; see [[evil-deeds-do-not-prosper]]).
