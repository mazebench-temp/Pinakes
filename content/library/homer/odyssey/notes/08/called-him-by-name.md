---
summary: Athena, in form like a man, marks the stranger's throw “and spoke to him, and called him by name” (8.194); and in the song Ares comes to Aphrodite in Hephaestus' house, “and he clung to her hand, and spoke to her, and called her by name” (8.291).
---

8.291 is the verse with which, in the Iliad, Charis and then Hephaestus himself take Thetis by the hand when she comes to his house (*Iliad* 18.384, 18.423); in the song the god's house receives a lover. Athena's address follows no touch of the hand, and the stranger does not know her (8.193–200; [[divine-epiphany]], [[the-song-of-ares-and-aphrodite]]).
