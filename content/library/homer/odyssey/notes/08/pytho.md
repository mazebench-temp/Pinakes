---
summary: In Demodocus' first song Apollo had foretold the quarrel of the best of the Achaeans to Agamemnon “in sacred Pytho, when he crossed the stone threshold / to consult the oracle” (8.79–81).
---

**The oracle.** Agamemnon rejoiced at the quarrel of Odysseus and Achilles: “For so Phoebus Apollo had told him in prophecy / in sacred Pytho, when he crossed the stone threshold / to consult the oracle” (8.79–81; see [[apollo]], [[agamemnon]] and [[the-quarrel-of-odysseus-and-achilles]]). It is the only time Homer speaks of the oracle. χρείων is “giving response”, as the newborn god in the *Hymn to Apollo* says “I will declare to men the unerring will of Zeus”, χρήσω (*Hymn* 3.132; Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 8.79), and the Hymn has χρησόμενοι, “to consult the oracle”, of the men who come to the god's temple from all Greece (*Hymn* 3.292). τότε, “then”, is the time of the consultation, when the war was only beginning and “the beginning of bane was rolling upon / Trojans and Danaans” (8.81–82); the oracle was given before the war (Merry on 8.75, 8.81; see [[the-beginning-of-evils]]). Merry reports another form of the story, from Demetrius of Phalerum as the scholia on 3.267 quote him: Menelaus went to Delphi with Odysseus and asked the god about the coming expedition to Ilios (on 8.81).

**Sacred Pytho and its threshold.** Πυθοῖ ἐν ἠγαθέῃ, “in sacred Pytho”: ἠγάθεος is said in the Iliad of Pylos, Lemnos and the Nysean mount, and here of Pytho, which the Iliad calls “rocky” (*Iliad* 2.519, 9.405; see [[egatheos]] and [[rocky]]); the Homeric *Hymn to Hestia* has the Odyssey's words, Πυθοῖ ἐν ἠγαθέῃ, of the house of Apollo that Hestia tends (*Hymn* 24.2). The stone threshold, λάινον οὐδόν, is the threshold of the temple, which in the Iliad shuts in the god's treasure (*Iliad* 9.404; see the translation's note on 8.80 and [[threshold]]). In the *Hymn to Apollo* Trophonius and Agamedes lay it on the foundations that Apollo marked out, and the peoples of men build the temple around it with polished stones (*Hymn* 3.294–299). Merry reports that O. Müller explained the stone threshold of a treasure house under the ground, and that Nitzsch dissented (on 8.80).

**The name.** Πυθώ, Merry notes, was the oldest name of the place in Phocis where Apollo's oracle was founded (on 8.80). The *Hymn to Apollo* derives it from πύθεσθαι, “rot”, because the body of the serpent that the god killed rotted there, “and from this it is now called Pytho” (*Hymn* 3.363–374); others derived it from πυθέσθαι, “inquire”, where the quantity of the vowel is a difficulty. Δελφοί was properly the name of the people, and Merry finds the word first in a Homeric hymn, where Artemis goes to her brother's house “in the rich land of the Delphians” (on 8.80; *Hymn* 27.14); Homer never uses it.
