summary: Hera is named only in Zeus' title, “the loud-thundering husband of Hera”, when Odysseus answers Nausicaa's farewell (8.465). In the song of Ares and Aphrodite she is one of the “two parents” whom Hephaestus blames for his lameness (8.312).
# Ἥρης (8.465) is claimed by the entry. Unnamed: Hephaestus' "two parents" (8.312).

**The husband of Hera.** Nausicaa bids the stranger remember her in his native land, and he answers, “Nausicaa, daughter of great-hearted Alcinous, / so may Zeus now grant it, the loud-thundering husband of Hera, / that I may come home and see the day of my homecoming” (8.464–466; see [[nausicaa]], [[zeus]], [[husband-of-hera]] and [[loud-thundering]]). ἐρίγδουπος πόσις Ἥρης is a phrase of oaths and wishes: in the Iliad it ends Agamemnon's oath at the truce for the dead and Hector's oath to Dolon (*Iliad* 7.411, 10.329), and it comes in Achilles' charge to Patroclus, if Zeus grants him glory (*Iliad* 16.88); in the Odyssey Menelaus prays in it for Telemachus' homecoming, and Telemachus answers Helen's reading of an omen with this verse (15.112, 15.180).

**Hephaestus' mother.** In his complaint to the gods Hephaestus says that no one else is to blame for his lameness “but my two parents, who I wish had never begotten me” (8.312; see [[hephaestus]]). In the Iliad his mother is Hera, who wished to hide him away because he was lame (*Iliad* 18.395–397), and his father Zeus (*Iliad* 1.577–578); Hesiod makes Hera bear him alone, without union with Zeus (*Theogony* 927–929).
