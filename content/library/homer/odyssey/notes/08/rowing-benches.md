summary: Alcinous bids the young men of the escort make the oars fast ἐπὶ κληῖσιν, “at the rowing benches”, and step out of the ship (8.37–38), the verse on which many commentators take the κληΐδες for the pins to which the oars were tied.
---

“And when you have all made the oars fast at the rowing benches, / step out again” (8.37–38; see [[oars]], [[ships]] and [[alcinous]]). Elsewhere the crew sit down ἐπὶ κληῖσι, at the benches (2.419, 4.579); here the oars are bound there, δησάμενοι, and the men leave the ship. W. W. Merry, discussing whether the κληΐδες are the same as the ζυγά, the thwarts that served as benches, notes that on the strength of this verse many commentators take them for the thole-pins on which the oars worked, the oars being tied to them by the leather strap, τροπός, of 4.782 and 8.53; on that view an oar dropped from the rowers' hands in a panic would hang in its loop and trail in the water (12.203–205), and ἐπὶ κληῖσι καθίζειν would be “sit at the rowlocks”, the κληΐδες being the later σκαλμοί (*Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, Appendix I §§8–9). The translation keeps “rowing benches” (see the entry). The merchant ship of Euryalus' taunt is πολυκλήις, “many-benched” (8.161; see [[many-benched]]).
