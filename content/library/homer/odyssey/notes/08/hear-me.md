---
summary: Alcinous addresses the Phaeacian lords four times with “Hear me, leaders and rulers of the Phaeacians”: to propose the escort and the feast (8.26), the games (8.97), the gifts (8.387), and, last, to stop the song and question the stranger (8.536).
---

8.26–27 repeat 7.186–187. Athena, as his herald, uses the same vocative with another verb, “Come now, leaders and rulers of the Phaeacians” (8.11; see the Odyssey translation's formulas, and Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 8.11, on δεῦτʼ ἄγε with the plural; [[alcinous]], [[assembly-type-scene]]).
