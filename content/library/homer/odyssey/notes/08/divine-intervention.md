summary: Athena works the stranger's day among the Phaeacians: she gathers the people, pours grace and stature on him “so that he might be dear to all the Phaeacians” (8.7–23), and at the games marks his throw and gives him heart (8.193–198). The singer begins his last song “stirred by the god” (8.499), and Alcinous ends the day with the prophecy of what Poseidon may do (8.564–571).
# Unnamed: Athena's plan (8.9), the grace (8.18-23), the mark (8.193-198), the singer stirred by the god (8.499), the horse made with Athena's help (8.493), the victory through Athena (8.520).

The goddess's plan runs through the day, “devising a homecoming for great-hearted Odysseus” (8.9; see [[athena]] and [[nostos]]). She fills the place of assembly, and pours grace on the stranger and makes him taller and stouter, “so that he might be dear to all the Phaeacians, / and feared and respected, and might accomplish the many contests” (8.18–23; see [[charis]] and [[aidos]]). At the games, in a man's form, she marks his throw and tells him to take heart (8.193–198; see [[tharsos]] and [[seeing-the-gods]]). In the songs she is the helper of his old deeds: the horse that Epeius made “with Athena's help”, and the victory at the house of Deiphobus “through great-hearted Athena” (8.493, 8.520; see [[sinon-and-the-wooden-horse]] and [[nike]]). The singer begins “stirred by the god” (ὁρμηθεὶς θεοῦ, 8.499; see [[memory-and-the-muses]]). The Phaeacians know their own god's hand too: Nausithous foretold that Poseidon would one day strike their ship, “and these things the god may bring to pass, / or they may be left unfulfilled” (8.564–571; see [[prophecy]] and [[poseidon]]).
