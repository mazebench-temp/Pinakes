summary: Alcinous rises from his bed at dawn (8.2), and beds close his list of the Phaeacians' pleasures (8.249). In Demodocus' song the bed of Hephaestus is the scene: Ares shames it, Hephaestus pours his bonds around its posts and from the roof beam, and the lovers are caught in it, as Hermes says he would gladly be (8.269–342).

**Rising and resting.** The day begins as “the sacred might of Alcinous rose from his bed” (8.2; see [[sacred-might-of-alcinous]] and [[dawn-line]]), and when Alcinous names what is always dear to his people, the list ends with “warm baths and beds” (8.249; see [[phaeacians]], [[bathing]] and [[clothing]]).

**The bed of Hephaestus.** Demodocus' second song is a story of a bed. Ares and Aphrodite “first lay together in the house of Hephaestus / in secret; and he gave her many gifts, and shamed the bed and couch / of lord Hephaestus” (8.268–270; see [[ares]], [[aphrodite]] and [[the-song-of-ares-and-aphrodite]]). W. W. Merry takes the gifts as Ares' presents to win her consent (*Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 8.269). Hephaestus forges his bonds and goes “to the chamber where his bed lay, / and all around the bedposts he poured the bonds in a circle on every side, / and many too were poured down from above, from the roof beam” (8.277–279; see [[the-bonds-of-hephaestus]] and [[house]]). ἕρματα are properly “supports”, here the bedposts (Merry on 8.278). Ares comes to Aphrodite: “Come here, my dear, let us go to bed and take our pleasure, lying together” (8.292; see the translation's note on 8.292). “And the two went to the bed and slept; and around them the bonds” of Hephaestus poured (8.296–297). Hephaestus shows the gods “where the two of them sleep in love, / having gone into my bed” (8.313–314), and Apollo asks Hermes whether he would be willing, held in strong bonds, “to sleep in bed beside golden Aphrodite” (8.336–337; see [[hermes]] and [[apollo]]). The bed is the marriage bed that the adulterer shames, as the bride-price that Hephaestus claims back is the price of the marriage (8.318–319; see [[bride-price]] and [[marriage]]).
