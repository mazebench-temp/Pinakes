---
summary: Hephaestus forges “bonds / unbreakable, not to be loosed, so that they might stay there steadfast” (8.274–275).
grc: ["ἀρρήκτους ἀλύτους"]
en: [unbreakable, not to be loosed]
---

8.275 is the verse of the golden hobbles that Poseidon casts round his horses' feet in the Iliad, “unbreakable, not to be loosed, so that they might stay there steadfast” (*Iliad* 13.36–37), and the Iliad joins the two words again in the knot of war, ἄρρηκτόν τʼ ἄλυτόν τε (*Iliad* 13.360; [[the-rope-of-war]], [[the-song-of-ares-and-aphrodite]]).
