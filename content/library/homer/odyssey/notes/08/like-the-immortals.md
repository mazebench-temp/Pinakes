summary: Athena calls the leaders to see the stranger, “like the immortals in form” (8.14); and Odysseus tells Euryalus of the man who “in looks is like the immortals, / but no grace is set as a crown around his words” (8.174–175).
grc: ["ἀλίγκιος ἀθανάτοισιν"]
---

The goddess makes her word true at once, pouring grace on him and making him taller and stouter to look upon (8.18–20; [[gold-poured-around-silver]]). The second likeness is Odysseus' portrait of the young man who has taunted him: “so with you too, your looks are outstanding” (8.176; [[different-gifts]], [[immortals]]).
