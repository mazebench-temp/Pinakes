summary: The book opens with the assembly that Alcinous announced the night before: the Phaeacians sit on the polished stones of their place of assembly by the ships, and Alcinous proposes the escort and the feast (8.4–45). The games are held at the same place (8.109–110), and in Demodocus' last song the men in the wooden horse sit in the place of assembly of the Trojans (8.503).
# ἀγόρευον, the gods' talk to one another (8.333), and ἀγόρευʼ, "so the old man used to say" (8.570), are speaking,
# not assembling, and are excepted in the entry.
---

**The assembly by the ships.** At dawn “the sacred might of Alcinous led the way for them / to the place of assembly of the Phaeacians, which was built for them beside the ships. / And they came and sat down on the polished stones / near together” (8.4–7; see [[alcinous]], [[phaeacians]] and [[the-city-of-the-phaeacians]]). Merry compares the elders of the trial scene on the shield of Achilles, who “sat on polished stones in the sacred circle” (*Iliad* 18.503–504), and finds the fixing of such solid stone seats described in Nausicaa's account of the place, “fitted with hauled stones set deep in the earth” (6.267; Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 8.6); πλησίον is “near together” (Merry on 8.7). Athena, in the likeness of Alcinous' herald, calls the leaders and rulers of the Phaeacians to “go to the place of assembly” (8.11–12; see [[athena]] and [[heralds]]), and quickly “the places of assembly and the seats were filled with mortals / as they gathered” (8.16–17). The plural, here as in ἕδραι, points to the different parts into which the place of assembly was divided, and Merry would join βροτῶν, “of mortals”, with the places and the seats, as in ἀνδρῶν ἀγοράς, “the assemblies of men” (2.69), rather than with “were filled” (Merry on 8.16; see the translation's note on 8.16). “And when they had gathered and were all assembled” (8.24, as 2.9), Alcinous addresses them (8.25–45): the stranger begs an escort, so let them drag a ship down for him, choose fifty-two young men to row it, and come, the scepter-bearing kings, to entertain him in the palace (see [[pompe]], [[kings]] and [[ships]]). It is the gathering of more of the elders that he announced the night before (7.189–191).

**The games.** After the feast the Phaeacians go out again “to the place of assembly, and a great throng followed with them, / countless” (8.109–110), and the games are held there (see [[the-games-of-the-phaeacians]] and [[contests]]). The ring of spectators is the ἀγών, “gathering” (8.200, 8.238, 8.259–260, 8.380), which the stewards widen around the leveled dancing floor (8.260; Merry on 8.260; see the key-word row of the translation and [[dance]]). Odysseus answers Laodamas that he sits “in the midst of your assembly, in need of my homecoming, … begging the king and all the people” (8.156–157; see [[laodamas-son-of-alcinous]]); μετά with the dative is used here with a collective noun, where it generally takes a plural (Merry on 8.156). In his answer to Euryalus, ἀγορητύς, “eloquence”, is the third of the gifts that the gods do not give to all men alike (8.168), and the man to whom a god gives it “speaks surely, / with soothing modesty, and stands out among those gathered” (8.171–172; see [[different-gifts]] and [[euryalus-the-phaeacian]]).

**The assembly of the Trojans.** In the song of the wooden horse the men inside sit “in the place of assembly of the Trojans, hidden in the horse; / for the Trojans themselves had dragged it into the citadel” (8.503–504; see [[sinon-and-the-wooden-horse]], [[trojans]] and [[troy]]). ἀγορή is not the market place but the assembly, as the next verse shows (Merry on 8.503): it is held on the citadel, as in the Iliad the Trojans hold an assembly “on the height of the city of Ilios, … beside Priam's doors” (*Iliad* 7.345–346). There the Trojans “talked on and on, confusedly”, divided three ways in their counsel (8.505–506); ἄκριτα, Merry thinks, is the contradiction between the views of different speakers (on 8.505; see the translation's note on 8.505).
