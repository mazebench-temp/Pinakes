# ἐχολώσατε (8.205), χολωσάμενος (8.227; again 12.348, 18.25), κεχολωμένος (8.276; again 11.565, 19.324, 22.369).
grc: [ἐχολώσατε, χολωσάμενος, κεχολωμένος]
en: [angered, in anger, anger]
---

The book's anger is a guest's, a god's and a husband's. Euryalus' taunt stirs the stranger, and after his throw he challenges them all, “since you have angered me greatly” (8.205), though he has already checked his warmth with an apology to the company (8.178; see [[strife-with-ones-host]]); Alcinous names the anger, “angry because this man stood beside you in the gathering / and rebuked you” (χωόμενος, 8.238), and turns it aside (see [[arete]] and [[neikos]]). The god's anger is Apollo's, who killed Eurytus for challenging him with the bow (8.227–228; see [[who-fights-the-gods]]). The husband's is Hephaestus': he forges the bonds “brooding evil deep in his mind”, makes the snare “in his anger at Ares”, and when he comes back to his door “a savage anger gripped him” (8.273–276, 8.304; see [[the-song-of-ares-and-aphrodite]]). In the end he takes Poseidon's pledge and loosens the bonds (8.358–359; see [[pledges-for-the-worthless]]).
