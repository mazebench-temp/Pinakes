---
summary: Athena, in the likeness of Alcinous' herald, calls the Phaeacian leaders to the place of assembly to learn about the stranger: “So saying, she urged on the might and spirit of every man” (8.15).
grc: ["ὤτρυνε μένος καὶ θυμὸν ἑκάστου"]
en: [urged on the might and spirit of every man]
---

The verse closes exhortations to battle ten times in the Iliad, once after a goddess's speech, Hera's in the likeness of Stentor (*Iliad* 5.792); Ameis notes that it is found only here in the Odyssey (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 8.15; see the translation's note on 8.15). Here it closes a summons to an assembly, spoken to each man in turn (8.10–14; [[divine-epiphany]], [[assembly-type-scene]], [[menos]]).
