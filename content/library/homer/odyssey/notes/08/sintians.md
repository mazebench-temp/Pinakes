---
summary: Ares tells Aphrodite that Hephaestus has gone to Lemnos, “to the Sintians of savage speech” (8.294), the people who took care of him when he fell from heaven.
---

Ares tells Aphrodite that Hephaestus “is no longer at home, but has gone already, I think, / to Lemnos, to the Sintians of savage speech” (8.293–294; see [[ares]], [[hephaestus]] and [[lemnos]]). The Sintians are the people of Hephaestus' island, who took care of him when Zeus threw him from heaven and he fell on Lemnos (*Iliad* 1.590–594; see [[fall-of-hephaestus]]), and this is the only other time Homer names them. ἀγριόφωνος, “of savage speech”, is found only here in Homer (see the key-word row of the translation). Merry takes it as equivalent to βαρβαρόφωνος, “of barbarous speech”, the Iliad's word for the Carians (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 8.294; *Iliad* 2.867; see [[barbarophonos]] and [[carians]]): the Sintians speak a foreign tongue. Bekker, as Merry's critical note reports him, records that Porson referred to this line the gloss ἀκριτόφωνος, “of confused speech”, in the lexicons of Apollonius and Hesychius (see the translation's note on 8.294). On the Sintians as a Thracian people, Merry refers to Strabo (on 8.283; *Geography* 12.3.20; see [[strabo]]).
