---
summary: In the footrace of the Phaeacians the νύσσα is not a turning post but the line from which the runners start: “For them the running was strained to the full from the post” (8.121), in the words of the footrace at Patroclus' games.
---

The young men try themselves first in running: “For them the running was strained to the full from the post; and all together / they flew swiftly on, raising the dust of the plain” (8.121–122; see [[the-games-of-the-phaeacians]] and [[contests]]). The first words are those of the footrace at Patroclus' games, τοῖσι δʼ ἀπὸ νύσσης τέτατο δρόμος (*Iliad* 23.758). ἀπὸ νύσσης is “from the starting line”, as the scholia gloss it, ἀπὸ τῆς ἀφετηρίας, exactly our “scratch”, W. W. Merry says, comparing νύσσω, “prick”; and δρόμος is not the course but the running, kept up at full stretch, as a battle or a fight is “strained” in the Iliad (*Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 8.121; *Iliad* 12.436; see the translation's note on 8.121–125). Clytoneus wins by “the furrow-length of a pair of mules in the fallow” (8.123–125; see [[clytoneus]] and [[mules]]). The marks of the discus throw, τέρματα (8.193), are claimed for the turning post only in the Iliad (see [[discus]]).
