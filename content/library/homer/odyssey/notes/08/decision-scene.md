summary: The Trojans sit around the horse in their place of assembly, “and they were divided three ways in their counsel: / either to split the hollow timber with the pitiless bronze, / or to drag it to the top of the height and throw it down the rocks, / or to let it stand as a great offering to appease the gods; / and that is how it was to be accomplished in the end” (8.506–510).

---

The pattern's double question becomes threefold, ἠὲ … ἢ … ἤ, and the ponderer is a crowd that “talked on and on, confusedly” (8.505; see the translation's note on 8.505). The half-verse of the division has τρίχα, “three ways,” for the δίχα, “two ways,” of Nestor's Achaeans quarreling after the sack (3.150) and of the besiegers on the shield (*Iliad* 18.510). The choice is made by fate, not by the better counsel: “for it was fated that the city should perish once it had enfolded / the great wooden horse” (8.511–512; see the translation's notes on 8.507 and 8.509; [[pitiless-bronze]], [[sinon-and-the-wooden-horse]], [[the-fall-of-troy]]).
