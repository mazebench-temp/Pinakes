summary: “And the two went to the bed and slept; and around them the bonds / of Hephaestus of much wisdom poured, skillfully made” (8.296–297), and the gods laugh “as they saw the craft of Hephaestus of much wisdom” (8.327).
grc: [πολύφρονος]
---

The Iliad gives the god the epithet once, when his fire boils the river, Ἡφαίστοιο βίηφι πολύφρονος (*Iliad* 21.367; see the Odyssey translation's formulas). In the song it praises the wit of the snare, τέχναι, “the craft” (8.327), and the gods draw the lesson that the slow has caught the swift, “lame as he is, by his craft” (8.329–332; [[famed-for-his-craft]], [[hephaestus]]).
