---
summary: “the Phaeacians of the long oars, men famed for ships” (8.191 = 8.369): they cower to the earth as the stranger's discus hums over them, and they delight with him in the song of Ares and Aphrodite.
grc: [δολιχήρετμοι]
en: [of the long oars]
---

Merry finds an intentional sarcasm in the high-sounding epithets set beside the Phaeacians' terror at the whiz of the quoit (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 8.191). The whole verse comes once more, when the Phaeacians watch their ship turned to stone (13.166; see the Odyssey translation's formulas; [[famed-for-ships]], [[phaeacians]], [[discus]]).
