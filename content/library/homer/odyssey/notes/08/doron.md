# δώρῳ (8.397).
grc: [δώρῳ]
en: [gift, gifts]
---

δῶρα are given for many reasons, and the book shows three. The kings give guest-gifts, “as is fitting” (8.389–399; see [[guest-gifts]]); Euryalus must make amends “with words / and with a gift” (8.396–397; see [[amends-and-reconciliation]]); and Alcinous gives his golden goblet so that the stranger may remember him (8.430–432; see [[memory-and-the-muses]]). The heralds bring the gifts to the house, the king's sons set “the beautiful gifts” beside their mother, and Arete lays them in a chest (8.417–420, 8.438–441; see [[chest]]). At supper the king names the gifts and the escort together, πομπὴ καὶ φίλα δῶρα (8.545; see [[pompe]]). In the song Ares too gives gifts, to win Aphrodite (πολλὰ δʼ ἔδωκε, 8.269; W. W. Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 8.269).
