# ἄλοχον (8.410; again 1.36, 3.264, 5.210 and later).
grc: [ἄλοχον]
en: [wife]
---

The stranger has said nothing of a wife, but the Phaeacians' good wishes assume one. Alcinous asks him to tell the Phaeacians' excellence “to another of the heroes too, when in your own halls / you feast beside your wife and your children” (8.242–243; see [[arete]]), and Euryalus, making amends, blesses him: “may the gods grant you to see your wife and to reach your native land, / since long now you suffer woes far from your friends” (8.410–411; see [[amends-and-reconciliation]] and [[nostos]]). It is the longing Calypso had named, to see his wife, whom he desires all his days (5.209–210).

Demodocus' second song turns on a marriage of the gods. Ares “shamed the bed and couch / of lord Hephaestus” (8.269–270; see [[aischros]]); the husband catches the lovers in his bonds and asks Zeus for the whole bride-price he gave for the girl, “because his daughter is beautiful, but she has no self-control” (8.317–320; see [[bride-price]], [[husbands-and-wives]] and [[the-song-of-ares-and-aphrodite]]). In the last song's simile a wife flings herself on her dying husband before his city and is led away into slavery (8.523–530; see [[posis]]).
