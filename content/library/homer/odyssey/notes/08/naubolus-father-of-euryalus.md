**His son's name.** “And up rose Euryalus too, the equal of Ares, ruin of mortals, / the son of Naubolus, who was the best in looks and form / of all the Phaeacians after noble Laodamas” (8.115–117; see [[euryalus-the-phaeacian]]). With the common reading Ναυβολίδης θʼ, “the son of Naubolus” would be another young man, named only by his father's name; this text omits θʼ, with La Roche, Bekker and Nauck, and La Roche argued that Homer calls by the father's name alone only men known to the hearers from elsewhere (W. W. Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, critical note on 8.116; see the translation's note on 8.116). Merry would derive the name from βολίς, a plummet for sounding (on 8.111), one of the many Phaeacian names drawn from seafaring (see [[phaeacians]]).
