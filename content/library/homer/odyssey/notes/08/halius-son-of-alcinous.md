**At the games.** “And up stood the three sons of noble Alcinous, / Laodamas and Halius and godlike Clytoneus” (8.118–119; see [[laodamas-son-of-alcinous]] and [[clytoneus]]). Of the five contests the poem names a winner for each, and he wins none: Clytoneus the running, Laodamas the boxing (8.120–130; see [[the-games-of-the-phaeacians]]).

**The ball dance.** “And Alcinous bade Halius and Laodamas / dance alone, since no one vied with them” (8.370–371; see [[alcinous]]). With the purple ball that wise-hearted Polybus had made for them, one bending back flings it toward the shadowy clouds, the other leaps high and catches it before his feet touch the ground; then they dance on the earth, changing places again and again, while the other young men beat time (8.372–380; see [[ball]], [[dance]] and [[polybus-the-phaeacian]]). The poem does not say which of the brothers throws and which catches. On the game and the dance, see the note on Laodamas. He is not named again in the poem.
