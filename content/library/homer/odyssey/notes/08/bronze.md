# παγχάλκεον, "all of bronze" (8.403; later 11.575, Orion's club; πάγχαλκος, πάγχαλκον of helmets in 18.378, 22.102 are
# other forms), first met here; the Iliad's παγχάλκεος (20.102) is not claimed.

Euryalus gives the stranger “this sword, all of bronze, which has a hilt / of silver” (8.403–404; see [[sword]] and [[gold-and-silver]]), and the bath is heated in a cauldron that the Greek calls simply “bronze”, χαλκόν (8.426; see [[cauldron]]). In the song the gods gather “to the bronze-floored house” (8.321; see [[bronze-floored]] and [[house]]), and in the song of the horse the Trojans debate whether “to split the hollow timber with the pitiless bronze” (8.507; see [[pitiless-bronze]] and [[the-wooden-horse]]). Zeus had foretold that the Phaeacians would give Odysseus “bronze and gold in plenty, and clothing” (5.38): the bronze of their gifts, tripods and cauldrons, is added in Book 13 (13.13–15, Greek only).
