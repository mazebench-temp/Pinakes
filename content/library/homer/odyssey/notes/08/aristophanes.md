---
summary: Two notes in Merry's commentary on this book touch Aristophanes, both given as he reports them: a line of the Acharnians that Nitzsch compared for the imperative with a plural address, and a scholium on the Peace that records Apion's defense of the song of Ares and Aphrodite.
---

- **“Come now”, to many.** Athena summons the Phaeacian leaders with δεῦτʼ ἄγε, “Come now” (8.11), the formula ἄγε with a plural; Nitzsch, Merry reports, compared a line of the *Acharnians* in which “tell me”, a singular imperative, is addressed to many, the demesmen (*Acharnians* 319, as Merry quotes it; Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 8.11): [[athena]] and [[assembly]].
- **Apion and the song.** The grammarian Apion, Merry reports from the scholium on Aristophanes' *Peace* 788, defended the song of Ares and Aphrodite against those who athetized it (Merry on 8.267): [[the-song-of-ares-and-aphrodite]] and [[the-song-of-ares-and-aphrodite-in-later-writers]].
