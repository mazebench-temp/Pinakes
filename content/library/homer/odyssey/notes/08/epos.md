summary: Book 8 is a book about words. The gods do not give eloquence to every man, and a god crowns some men's words with beauty (8.167–175); Euryalus' word “bites the heart” and was not “fair and right” (8.185, 8.397), and he asks the storm winds to carry it away (8.408–409); and the Phaeacians delight in the singer's words (8.91).
# ἔπεσι (8.170).
grc: [ἔπεσι]
en: [words, word]
---

The book weighs words. The stranger tells Euryalus that a god crowns one man's words with beauty, μορφὴν ἔπεσι στέφει, while another has no grace set as a crown around his words (8.170, 8.175; see [[different-gifts]] and [[charis]]). κατὰ μοῖραν is the measure of a word: Euryalus approves Laodamas' word, “fair and right” (8.141), Alcinous condemns Euryalus' own, “by no means fair and right” (8.397; see [[moira]]), and the stranger asks the singer to tell the horse “fair and right” (8.496). Euryalus' word “bites the heart” (θυμοδακὴς … μῦθος, 8.185), and in his apology he wishes that any terrible word may be carried off by the storm winds (8.408–409; see [[amends-and-reconciliation]]). The singer's words are a pleasure: the Phaeacians urge him on, “since they delighted in his words” (8.91; see [[singers]]).
