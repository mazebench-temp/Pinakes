---
summary: In Demodocus' song Hephaestus makes as if to go to Lemnos, “the well-built citadel, / which is by far the dearest to him of all lands” (8.283–284); Ares tells Aphrodite that he has gone there, “to the Sintians of savage speech” (8.294), but he turns back before he reaches it (8.301).
---

**The dearest of lands.** When the snare is laid around the bed, Hephaestus “made as if to go to Lemnos, the well-built citadel, / which is by far the dearest to him of all lands” (8.283–284; see [[hephaestus]] and [[the-song-of-ares-and-aphrodite]]). εἴσατʼ ἴμεν is “made show of going”, and Merry compares Ovid's words in his own telling of the story, “fingit iter Lemnon”, “he feigns a journey to Lemnos” (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 8.283; *Ars amatoria* 2.579; see [[ovid]]). The island is the god's because he fell there when Zeus threw him from heaven, and the Sintians took care of him (*Iliad* 1.590–594; see [[fall-of-hephaestus]]). Merry prints ἔσκεν, “was”, for ἐστίν, “is”, in 8.284; the manuscripts vary, and the scholia record the verse-end ἔπλετο πασέων (Merry's critical note; see the translation's note on 8.284).

**The citadel.** ἐυκτίμενον πτολίεθρον, “the well-built citadel” (see [[well-built]]), makes Lemnos a city, and Merry infers from the Iliad, where Hera comes to “Lemnos, the city of divine Thoas” and leaves with Sleep “the city of Lemnos and Imbros”, that Lemnos was the name of the city as well as of the island (on 8.283; *Iliad* 14.230, 14.281; see [[thoas-of-lemnos]] and [[imbros]]).

**The Sintians.** Ares, seeing Hephaestus go, tells Aphrodite that he is away: “for Hephaestus is no longer at home, but has gone already, I think, / to Lemnos, to the Sintians of savage speech” (8.293–294; see [[ares]], [[aphrodite]] and [[sintians]]). Merry, after Strabo, takes them for a Thracian people, and suggests that, as their connection with Hephaestus would suggest, they were smiths by trade, and that metal came into the island: in the Iliad the Achaeans buy the wine that Euneus sends from Lemnos with bronze and with gleaming iron, among other things (on 8.283; *Iliad* 7.467–475; see [[euneus]]).

**Before he reached it.** But the Sun had seen the lovers and told him, and he came back “having turned back before he reached the land of Lemnos” (8.300–302; see [[helios]]). The verse names the island, Λήμνου γαῖαν, “the land of Lemnos”, where 8.283 named the citadel.
