---
summary: Alcinous ranks a man's attachments: kin by marriage are “the dearest after one's own blood and kin” (μεθʼ αἷμά τε καὶ γένος αὐτῶν, 8.583).
---

γένος is here a man's own stock, joined with αἷμα, “blood”: the relations of birth, set before the connections of marriage, son-in-law and father-in-law, and before a companion, however dear (8.581–586; W. W. Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 8.583; see [[in-laws]] and [[a-companion-no-worse-than-a-brother]]). The same evening the king asks the stranger the name his parents gave him, since parents name every child at birth (8.550–554; see [[no-one-is-nameless]]).
