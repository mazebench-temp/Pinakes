# δήμιοι (8.259).
grc: [δήμιοι]
en: [officers of the people]
---

δῆμος is a people and its land. The young men for the escort are picked κατὰ δῆμον, “throughout the people” (8.36; see [[pompe]]), and the stranger sits in the assembly “begging the king and all the people” (8.157; see [[supplication]]). The stewards of the games, αἰσυμνῆται, are δήμιοι, public officers and not servants of the king's household, distinguished from other public officers, such as the heralds who are called δημιοεργοί (19.135), by their office at the gatherings (W. W. Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 8.258; see [[dance]]). The twelve kings and Alcinous rule κατὰ δῆμον, “throughout the land” (8.390; see [[kings]]). The land is foreign in the maxim, δήμῳ ἐν ἀλλοδαπῷ, “in a foreign land” (8.211; see [[strife-with-ones-host]]), and in the stranger's boast it is δήμῳ ἔνι Τρώων, “in the land of the Trojans” (8.220). Last, Alcinous asks him to name “your land and your people and your city” (8.555; see [[no-one-is-nameless]]).
