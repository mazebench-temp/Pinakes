---
summary: In the first song the beginning of bane rolls upon Trojans and Danaans “through the counsels of great Zeus” (8.82); in the third the Trojans, around the horse, “were divided three ways in their counsel” (8.506).
---

The two counsels of the book are a god's and a people's. Zeus' counsels set the war rolling (Διὸς μεγάλου διὰ βουλάς, 8.82; see [[the-beginning-of-evils]] and [[plan-of-zeus]]). The Trojans' counsel at the end of it is split three ways: to cut the hollow timber open with the bronze, to drag it to the height and throw it down the rocks, or to let it stand “as a great offering to appease the gods”, and the last prevails, since fate had set it so (τρίχα δέ σφισιν ἥνδανε βουλή, 8.505–511; see [[aisa]] and [[sinon-and-the-wooden-horse]]). ἄκριτα, of their talk, may be “endless”, “undecided” or “indiscriminate”, and Merry prefers the last (W. W. Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 8.505; see the translation's note on 8.505).
