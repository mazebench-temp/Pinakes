---
summary: The stranger tells Euryalus that he was among the foremost in contests “as long as I trusted in my youth and my hands” (8.181).
---

ὄφρʼ ἥβῃ τε πεποίθεα χερσί τʼ ἐμῇσι: as πεποίθεα is a pluperfect, ἔμμεναι before it must be a past tense too, “I was for prowess among the first, when I was sure of my mettle and strength of hand” (W. W. Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 8.179, 8.181; see the translation's note on 8.180–181). The trust in youth and hands is past; the stranger speaks of himself as broken by the sea and by war, and then throws the discus beyond them all (8.182–198; see [[nothing-worse-than-the-sea]]).
