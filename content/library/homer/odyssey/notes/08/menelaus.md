---
summary: In Demodocus' song of the sack of Troy, “godlike Menelaus” goes with Odysseus to the house of Deiphobus (8.517–518).
---

“but how Odysseus went to the house of Deiphobus / like Ares, together with godlike Menelaus” (8.517–518; see [[deiphobus]], [[the-fall-of-troy]] and [[antitheos]]). The song gives the fight at the house to Odysseus, and Menelaus is named only as his companion. The two had sat together in the horse with Diomedes, as Menelaus told Telemachus at Sparta, when Helen walked around it and Odysseus held them back from answering her (4.280–284; see [[sinon-and-the-wooden-horse]] and [[helen]]). The Odyssey does not tell how Menelaus recovered Helen; it shows them at home in Sparta, reconciled (4.120–305).
