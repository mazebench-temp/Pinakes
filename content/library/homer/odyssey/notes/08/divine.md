---
summary: The singer is “divine”: Alcinous bids them “call the divine singer, / Demodocus” (8.43–44), and a herald goes “to fetch the divine singer” (8.47); Odysseus uncovers his head each time “the divine singer stopped singing” (8.87); and Alcinous stops the song: “Ever since we have been at supper and the divine singer was roused to sing, / from then on he has never ceased from miserable lamentation, / the stranger” (8.539–541). The young men of the dance strike “the divine dancing floor with their feet” (8.264).
---

θεῖος ἀοιδός is Phemius' formula in Book 1 and Demodocus' here (1.336; see the Odyssey translation's formulas). Alcinous gives the reason with the name: “for to him above all the god has given song” (8.44). The other word for the gift of song, θέσπις, is “wondrous” (8.498; see the Odyssey translation's key words, θέσπις, and [[wondrous]]; [[demodocus]], [[singers]], [[trusty]], [[famous]], [[dance]]).
