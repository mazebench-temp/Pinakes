---
summary: For the feast in his house Alcinous sacrifices twelve sheep, eight white-tusked hogs and two shambling cattle (8.59–61).
---

**The sacrifice.** “And for them Alcinous sacrificed twelve sheep, / and eight white-tusked hogs, and two shambling cattle; / these they flayed and dressed, and made ready a lovely feast” (8.59–61; see [[sacrifice]], [[feast]], [[sheep]], [[boar]] and [[shambling]]). ἀμφί θʼ ἕπον, “dressed”, is the work between the flaying and the table, the verb used of the meat and of an ox in the Iliad (W. W. Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 8.61; *Iliad* 11.776, 18.559).

**Mules and oxen.** The oxen of the Iliad's furrow-length are left out of the Odyssey's: there mules are “better than oxen / at dragging the jointed plow through the deep fallow” (*Iliad* 10.352–353), and the footrace measures Clytoneus' lead by the mules alone (8.124; see [[mules]]).
