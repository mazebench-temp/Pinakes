---
summary: “And the herald came near, leading the trusty singer” (8.62 = 8.471), at the morning feast and again at supper.
---

Merry notes that this form of the word, ἐρίηρον, occurs only here, at 8.471 and of Phemius (1.346), while its common form is the plural of companions, ἐρίηρες ἑταῖροι; he renders it “worthy” (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 8.62; see the Odyssey translation's formulas). The second verse goes on with the singer's title, “Demodocus, honored by the people” (8.472; [[honored-like-a-god]], [[demodocus]], [[heralds]]).
