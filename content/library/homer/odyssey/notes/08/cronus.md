---
summary: Cronus is named in Book 8 only in his son's patronymic: when Ares comes to the house of Hephaestus, Aphrodite “had just come from her father, the mighty son of Cronus” (8.289).
---

In Demodocus' second song Ares finds Aphrodite alone: “She had just come from her father, the mighty son of Cronus, / and had sat down” (8.289–290; see [[zeus]], [[aphrodite]] and [[son-of-cronus]]). ἐρισθενής, “mighty”, is said in Homer only of Zeus (*Iliad* 13.54, 19.355, 21.184; see [[eristhenes]]), and ἐρισθενέος Κρονίωνος is the verse-end of Achilles' taunt over the fallen Asteropaeus, “It is hard for you to strive with the children of the mighty son of Cronus” (*Iliad* 21.184). Aphrodite comes from her father as Athena, in the Iliad, goes back from Achilles “to the well-built house of her mighty father”, πρὸς πατρὸς ἐρισθενέος πυκινὸν δῶ (*Iliad* 19.355).
