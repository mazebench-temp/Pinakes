---
summary: Named as the father of Amphialus, and himself as the son of Tecton, when the young men rise for the games (8.114).
---

“And Amphialus, son of Polyneus, the son of Tecton” (8.114; see [[amphialus]], [[tecton]] and [[the-games-of-the-phaeacians]]). Τεκτονίδαο, “the son of Tecton”, stands in apposition to Πολυνήου, a patronymic added to the father's name, as in the Iliad's τέκτονος υἱὸν / Ἁρμονίδεω, “son of the builder, / the son of Harmon” (*Iliad* 5.59–60; see [[harmon]]). Polyneus himself takes no part in the games.
