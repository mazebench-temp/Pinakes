summary: Laodamas of the stranger: “In build at least he is no mean man” (8.134); Odysseus to Euryalus: “Stranger, you have not spoken well” (8.166), “I am not unskilled in contests” (8.179), and of himself, “For I am no mean man at all the contests there are among men” (8.214); Alcinous: “since what you say among us is not ungracious” (8.236) and Euryalus' word “was by no means fair and right” (8.397); and his last question, “Since in no way inferior to a brother / is a companion who has wise understanding” (8.585–586).
refs: [8.134, 8.166, 8.179, 8.187, 8.214, 8.236, 8.397, 8.585]

Merry calls οὐ κακός at 8.214 a litotes to express positive excellence, “thoroughly well skilled am I” (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 8.214; see the translation's note on 8.214). The figure runs through the quarrel of words at the games: the young men understate the stranger's build, he answers the taunt in kind and throws a discus “heavier by no little” (8.187), and the king grants the justice of his anger in the same form (8.134–139, 8.166–185, 8.236–240; [[the-games-of-the-phaeacians]], [[euryalus-the-phaeacian]]).
