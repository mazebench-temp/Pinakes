---
summary: Odysseus to Nausicaa: “that I may come home and see the day of my homecoming; / then even there I would pray to you as to a god” (8.466–467).
---

The verse is the one with which he told Calypso that he longed for home all his days (5.220), and Athena's, in Mentor's likeness, at Pylos (3.233; see the Odyssey translation's formulas). It answers Nausicaa's wish, “when you are in your native land” (8.461; [[dear-native-land]], [[nostos]], [[nausicaa]]).
