summary: Told by the Sun, Hephaestus forges the bonds, pours them invisibly around his bed and from the roof beam, and feigns a journey to Lemnos; Ares and Aphrodite lie down and are caught, unable to move a limb. The gods come to look and laugh, Hermes says he would gladly lie in three times as many, and Hephaestus looses them when Poseidon promises that Ares will pay (8.272–360).

**The making.** The Sun tells Hephaestus of Ares and Aphrodite, and he goes to his smithy “brooding evil deep in his mind” (8.270–273; see [[helios]], [[forge]] and [[the-song-of-ares-and-aphrodite]]):

{{quote:64d7e7fb-8be1-5135-9a30-7bb05b8106a8}}

αὖθι μένοιεν, “might stay there steadfast”, may have the lovers or the bonds for its subject (W. W. Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 8.275). ἑρμῖσιν are the bedposts, properly “supports” (Merry on 8.278; see [[bed]]), and μελαθρόφιν, “from the roof beam”, makes καθύπερθε, “from above”, more exact (Merry on 8.279; see [[house]]). The spider's webs are the measure of their fineness: no one could see them, “not even one of the blessed gods” (8.280–281; see [[blessed-gods]]). Then he makes as if to go to Lemnos (8.283–284; see [[lemnos]]).

**The lovers caught.** Ares comes as soon as he sees him go, and the two lie down: “and around them the bonds / of Hephaestus of much wisdom poured, skillfully made, / and they could not move or lift any of their limbs. / And then indeed they knew that there was no more escape” (8.296–299; see [[ares]], [[aphrodite]] and [[of-much-wisdom]]). Hephaestus, warned by the Sun, comes home and calls the gods to see: “the snare and the bonds will hold them / until her father gives me back the whole bride-price” (8.317–318; see [[bride-price]], [[dolos]] and [[door]]).

**The gods look on.** The gods laugh at “the craft of Hephaestus of much wisdom” (8.327; see [[laughter-of-the-gods]]), and say that the slow has caught the swift, “lame as he is, by his craft” (8.330–332). Apollo asks Hermes whether he would be willing, “held fast in strong bonds, / to sleep in bed beside golden Aphrodite”, and Hermes answers: “Let three times as many boundless bonds hold me fast all around, / and you gods look on, and all the goddesses too, / but let me sleep beside golden Aphrodite” (8.336–342; see [[apollo]] and [[hermes]]). Some copies lacked 8.333–342, and some athetized them, for their impropriety (see the translation's note on 8.266–366).

**The release.** Poseidon does not laugh, and begs Hephaestus to set Ares free, promising that he will pay what is due (8.344–348; see [[poseidon]]). Hephaestus answers that he could not hold the surety: “How could I bind you among the immortal gods, / if Ares should go off, escaping both the debt and the bonds?” (8.352–353; see the translation's notes on 8.351 and 8.352–353). When Poseidon makes himself the surety, “the might of Hephaestus loosed the bonds. / And the two, when they were loosed from the bonds, strong as they were, / sprang up at once” and are gone, he to Thrace and she to Cyprus (8.359–362; see [[thrace]] and [[cyprus]]). The chest that Odysseus ties for the voyage later in the book is fastened with a δεσμός too, a knot that Circe taught him (8.443–448; see [[chest]]).
