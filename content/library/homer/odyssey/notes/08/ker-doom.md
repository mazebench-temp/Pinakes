---
summary: In the song of the horse the best of the Argives sit inside it, “bringing slaughter and doom to the Trojans” (Τρώεσσι φόνον καὶ κῆρα φέροντες, 8.513).
---

The verse ends the account of the Trojans' three counsels about the horse and the fate that settled it: “for it was fated that the city should perish once it had enfolded / the great wooden horse, where they were sitting, all the best / of the Argives, bringing slaughter and doom to the Trojans” (8.511–513, after 4.272–273; see [[aisa]], [[sinon-and-the-wooden-horse]] and [[the-fall-of-troy]]). The mood of ἀμφικαλύψῃ, where the optative might be expected, keeps as nearly as possible the wording of the oracle (W. W. Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 8.511).
