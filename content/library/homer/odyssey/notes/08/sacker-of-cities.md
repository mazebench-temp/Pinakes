---
summary: “and up rose Odysseus, sprung from Zeus, sacker of cities” (8.3): the first time the Odyssey gives him the title.
---

Merry notes that in the Odyssey the epithet is used only of Odysseus, with special reference to the craft by which he enabled the Greeks to take Ilium, while the Iliad gives it also to Achilles, Ares, Enyo, Oileus and Otrynteus (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 8.3). The book that opens with it closes with that craft: Odysseus asks Demodocus for the song of the wooden horse, “which once brilliant Odysseus brought up into the citadel as a snare, / having filled it with the men who sacked Ilios” (8.494–495), and the singer tells how the sons of the Achaeans sacked the city (8.514–520; [[the-fall-of-troy]], [[sinon-and-the-wooden-horse]], [[odysseus]]).
