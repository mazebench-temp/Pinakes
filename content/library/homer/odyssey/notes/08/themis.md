---
summary: Alcinous asks the stranger to tell of the peoples he has met, “both those who are harsh and savage and unjust, / and those who are kind to strangers, with a mind that fears the gods” (8.575–576).
---

The question is the stranger's own, the one he asked when he woke on Scheria, and will ask again setting out for the Cyclopes and on waking in Ithaca (6.119–121; 9.175–176; 13.200–202): justice and the welcome of strangers are named together, and both together with the fear of the gods (see [[xenia]], [[to-the-land-of-what-mortals]] and [[the-tale-to-alcinous]]). ἠμὲν … δίκαιοι forms the contrast to οἵ τε φιλόξεινοι (W. W. Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 8.575). The tale that answers it, in Books 9–12, begins with the Cicones, the Lotus-eaters and the Cyclopes.
