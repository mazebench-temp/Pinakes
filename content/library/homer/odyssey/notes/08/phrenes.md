# φρένʼ (8.131; again 17.174).
grc: ["φρένʼ"]
---

The book gives φρένες to ships. Alcinous asks the stranger's land “so that our ships may carry you there, aiming with their minds” (τιτυσκόμεναι φρεσί, 8.556), for “the ships themselves know the thoughts and minds of men” (νοήματα καὶ φρένας ἀνδρῶν, 8.559): these marvelous ships are endowed with human sense, anticipating the will of their masters (W. W. Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 8.556; see [[pompe]] and [[noos]]). Among men, φρένες are one of the gifts the gods do not give to all, beside stature and eloquence (8.168; see [[different-gifts]]); the young men delight their hearts with the contests (ἐτέρφθησαν φρένʼ ἀέθλοις, 8.131); and Alcinous sees that grief has beset the stranger's mind (ἄχος φρένας ἀμφιβέβηκεν, 8.541; see [[achos]]).
