summary: At the first song Odysseus covers his face, “letting tears fall from beneath his brows” (8.86), and escapes the notice of all but Alcinous “as he let tears fall” (8.93); at the third he “was melting, and tears wet his cheeks beneath his eyelids” (8.522), “so Odysseus let a pitiful tear fall from beneath his brows” (8.531), and again Alcinous alone notices (8.532).
grc: ["δάκρυ δʼ ἔδευεν", "ὑπʼ ὀφρύσι δάκρυον"]
en: [tears wet his cheeks, let a pitiful tear fall, let a bitter tear fall]
---

δάκρυα λείβων, “letting tears fall,” is the phrase of Odysseus on Calypso's shore (5.84, 5.158). 8.93–95 and 8.532–534 are the same three verses, and the tears of the third song are given a simile, the woman who weeps over her husband fallen before his city (8.523–530); the verse that closes it is said again of Odysseus and his son at their meeting (16.219), and Telemachus wept so at the name of his father (4.153; see the Odyssey translation's formulas; [[odysseus-weeps-at-the-feast]], [[groaning-heavily]], [[tears]]).
