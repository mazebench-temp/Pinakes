---
summary: Agamemnon rejoiced “in his mind”, secretly, at the quarrel of the best of the Achaeans (8.78); Alcinous alone “noticed” the stranger's tears, twice (8.94, 8.533); “in mind you are worthless”, the stranger tells Euryalus (8.177); the king asks him not to hide anything “with crafty thoughts” (8.548); the Phaeacian ships “know the thoughts and minds of men” (8.559); and the peoples kind to strangers have “a mind that fears the gods” (8.576).
grc: [νοήμασι]
en: [thoughts]
---

**Noticing.** νοέω is to see and understand. The Sun “had seen them lying together in love” (ἐνόησε, 8.271; see [[helios]]). At the first song and at the third the stranger hides his tears from all the others, “but Alcinous alone noticed him and observed him” (ἐπεφράσατʼ ἠδʼ ἐνόησεν, 8.94 = 8.533; see [[alcinous]] and [[odysseus-weeps-at-the-feast]]).

**The mind and its looks.** Agamemnon “rejoiced in his mind” (χαῖρε νόῳ, 8.78), that is, secretly (W. W. Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 8.78), when the best of the Achaeans quarreled (see [[the-beginning-of-evils]]). The stranger sets Euryalus' looks against his mind: “your looks are outstanding … but in mind you are worthless” (νόον δʼ ἀποφώλιός ἐσσι, 8.176–177; see [[different-gifts]] and [[eidos]]).

**Thoughts.** Alcinous, treating the stranger as a brother, asks him not to hide “with crafty thoughts” (νοήμασι κερδαλέοισιν, 8.548; see [[kerdos]] and [[as-good-as-a-brother]]) what he is asked. His ships need no helmsmen: “the ships themselves know the thoughts and minds of men” (νοήματα καὶ φρένας ἀνδρῶν, 8.559; see [[phrenes]] and [[pompe]]). And the peoples the stranger is to describe are those who are savage and unjust, and those who are kind to strangers, “with a mind that fears the gods” (νόος … θεουδής, 8.575–576; see [[xenia]]).
