---
summary: Athenaeus is in Merry's notes on this book for his collection of the ball games of the ancients, beside the ball dance of Halius and Laodamas; he also preserves Aristotle's remark that Homer has Odysseus bathe before the feast of the Phaeacians.
---

- **Ball games.** On the purple ball that Halius and Laodamas toss toward the clouds and catch in mid-leap (8.372–376), Merry notes that much about the ball play of the ancients is collected in Athenaeus' first book, and more in Pollux, and that Eustathius gives this game a name of its own (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 8.372; Athenaeus is not read here): [[ball]] and [[halius-son-of-alcinous]].
- **The bath before the feast.** Athenaeus quotes Aristotle's *Symposium* for Homer's care that men bathe before they dine: the housekeeper bids Odysseus bathe before the feast of the Phaeacians (8.449), for it was not fitting to come to a symposium covered in sweat and dust (fr. 50 Gigon, from Athenaeus): [[aristotle]] and [[bathing]].
- **Dancing to song.** Athenaeus' use of the young men who dance while Demodocus plays (8.262–264) for Homer's knowledge of dancing to song is given under [[title-of-book-18]].
