---
summary: The man whom a god has given eloquence “speaks surely, / with soothing modesty, and stands out among those gathered, / and as he goes through the city they look on him as a god” (8.171–173).
grc: [μειλιχίῃ]
---

Hesiod has the same verses, nearly word for word, in his portrait of the king whom the Muses honor: he speaks surely, ὁ δʼ ἀσφαλέως ἀγορεύων, and as he goes through the gathering the people propitiate him as a god, αἰδοῖ μειλιχίῃ, μετὰ δὲ πρέπει ἀγρομένοισιν (*Theogony* 81–92; 8.172 = *Theogony* 92). The translation joins αἰδοῖ μειλιχίῃ to the speaker; Merry, after Hesiod, joins it to the onlookers, who look on him with submissive reverence (see the translation's note on 8.171–172; [[different-gifts]], [[aidos]]).
