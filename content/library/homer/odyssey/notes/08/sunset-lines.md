---
summary: “And the sun set, and the glorious gifts were there for him” (8.417).
---

By sunset all his presents had come in, as Merry explains; the day that closes is the one that began with the first verse of the book, and the stranger's stories keep the household up until the time given in Book 13 (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 8.417; 13.17–18). δύσετό τʼ ἠέλιος opens the verse as at 2.388 and 6.321 (see [[dawn-line]] and [[guest-gifts]]).
