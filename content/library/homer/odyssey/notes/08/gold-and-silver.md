**The gold of the gifts.** Zeus had foretold that the Phaeacians would send Odysseus home “giving him bronze and gold in plenty, and clothing” (5.38; see [[zeus]] and [[guest-gifts]]), and the gifts of this book are that. Each of the thirteen kings gives “a talent of precious gold” (8.393; see [[talent]] and [[kings]]); Euryalus gives a sword “all of bronze, which has a hilt / of silver”, in a sheath of ivory (8.403–405; see [[sword]], [[sheath]], [[ivory]] and [[bronze]]); and Alcinous adds “this beautiful goblet of mine, / of gold”, for the libations of a lifetime (8.430–432; see [[cup]]). Arete stows in the chest “the clothing and the gold, which the Phaeacians had given him” (8.440; see [[chest]] and [[clothing]]).

**Epithets.** The silver of the singer's chair and of the sword, ἀργυρόηλος, “silver-studded” (8.65, 8.406, 8.416), and the gold of Ares' reins and of “golden Aphrodite” (8.285, 8.337, 8.342) belong to the epithets (see [[silver-studded]], [[of-the-golden-reins]] and [[golden]]).
