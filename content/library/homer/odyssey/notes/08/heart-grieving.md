---
summary: “And when Hephaestus heard the heart-grieving tale, / he went to his smithy, brooding evil deep in his mind” (8.272–273).
---

The tale is the Sun's, who has seen Ares and Aphrodite together (8.270–271). In the Iliad it is said of anger, words and outrage (see the general article); here it is news that wounds a husband, and it turns at once to the forging of the bonds (8.273–275; [[helios]], [[hephaestus]]).
