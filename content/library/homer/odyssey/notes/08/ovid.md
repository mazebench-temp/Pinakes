---
summary: Ovid told the song of Ares and Aphrodite twice, in the Art of Love as a lesson for lovers who would trap a rival and in the Metamorphoses as the first of the loves of the Sun, and from exile he named it as proof that Homer too sang of love.
---

- **The Art of Love.** “A story is told, the best known in all heaven, of Mars and Venus caught by the snares of Mulciber” (*Art of Love* 2.561–562): Venus mocks her husband's lameness before Mars, the Sun informs on the lovers, Vulcan sets his invisible snares and *fingit iter Lemnon*, “feigns a journey to Lemnos” (*Art of Love* 2.579), the phrase Merry sets beside Homer's εἴσατʼ ἴμεν ἐς Λῆμνον, “he made as if to go to Lemnos” (8.283; Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 8.283). An anonymous god asks for the chains as Hermes does, Neptune obtains the release, and Ovid adds that the lovers did openly afterwards what they had hidden before, and that Vulcan repented of his art (*Art of Love* 2.561–600; plain renderings): [[the-song-of-ares-and-aphrodite-in-later-writers]], [[the-song-of-ares-and-aphrodite]] and [[lemnos]].
- **The Metamorphoses.** Leuconoe, one of the daughters of Minyas, begins the loves of the Sun with his informing: “this god sees all things first” (*Metamorphoses* 4.172). Vulcan's chains are finer than the finest threads “or the spider's web that hangs from the top of the beam” (*Metamorphoses* 4.178–179), as Homer's bonds are “fine as spider's webs” and poured “from the roof beam” (8.279–280), and the story was long “the best known in all heaven” (*Metamorphoses* 4.189), until Venus took her revenge on the informer (*Metamorphoses* 4.169–192; plain renderings): [[the-song-of-ares-and-aphrodite-in-later-writers]], [[helios]] and [[who-sees-all-things]].
- **The Tristia.** Defending himself to Augustus, Ovid asks, “Who but Maeonides tells of Venus and Mars bound, their bodies caught on a shameful bed?” (*Tristia* 2.377–378, a plain rendering), with the Iliad's adulteress and the Odyssey's wooed wife: [[the-song-of-ares-and-aphrodite-in-later-writers]] and [[homer]].
