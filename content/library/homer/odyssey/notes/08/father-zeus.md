---
summary: Hephaestus calls the gods from his doorway: “Father Zeus, and you other blessed gods who live forever, / come here, so that you may see deeds that are laughable, and hard to bear” (8.306–307).
---

The verse opens Athena's complaint in the council of Book 5 (5.7), and in Book 12 Odysseus cries out with it, and the Sun demands vengeance for his cattle (12.371, 12.377; see the Odyssey translation's formulas). Hephaestus calls the gods as witnesses of a wrong, and they come to laugh (8.321–327; [[blessed-gods]], [[gods-who-live-forever]], [[zeus]]).
