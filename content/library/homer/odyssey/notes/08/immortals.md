summary: The stranger is “like the immortals in form” (8.14), and the man without grace of speech “in looks is like the immortals” (8.174); Eurytus and Heracles “vied even with the immortals in archery” (8.225); and in the song “laughter rose among the immortal gods” (8.343), Poseidon promises that Ares “will pay all that is due among the immortal gods” (8.348), Hephaestus asks “How could I bind you among the immortal gods” (8.352), and the Graces anoint Aphrodite “with oil / immortal” (8.364–365).
grc: [ἀμβρότῳ]
---

The oil is ἄμβροτος, the word from which ambrosia takes its name, “such as is upon the gods who live forever” (8.365; see the translation's note on 8.365; [[like-the-immortals]], [[the-adornment-of-a-goddess]], [[ambrosial]], [[who-fights-the-gods]]).
