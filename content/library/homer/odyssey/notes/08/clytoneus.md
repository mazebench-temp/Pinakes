**The footrace.** “And up stood the three sons of noble Alcinous, / Laodamas and Halius and godlike Clytoneus” (8.118–119; see [[laodamas-son-of-alcinous]] and [[halius-son-of-alcinous]]). The running comes first, and he wins it:

{{quote:91e7977e-5cad-5368-abdb-b51b620ef7e6}}

ἀπὸ νύσσης is “from the starting line”, and τέτατο δρόμος, “the running was kept up at full speed” (W. W. Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 8.121; see [[turning-post]] and the translation's note on 8.121–125). The measure of his lead is the one by which Odysseus and Diomedes let Dolon get ahead of them in the night, “as far off as the furrow-length / of mules” (*Iliad* 10.351–353; Merry on 8.124; see [[mules]] and [[jointed]]), and ὑπεκπροθέων is “slipping forward in advance” (Merry on 8.125). The Phaeacians count running among the arts they excel in (8.103, 8.247, 8.253), and it is the one contest in which the stranger fears that one of them may outstrip him (8.230–233; see [[the-games-of-the-phaeacians]]).

**His epithets.** ἀντίθεος, “godlike” (8.119; see [[antitheos]]); ἀμύμων, “noble” (8.123; see [[blameless]]). He is not named again in the poem.
