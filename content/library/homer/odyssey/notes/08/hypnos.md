# εὕδοιμι (8.342), εὕδῃσθα (8.445).
grc: [εὕδοιμι, εὕδῃσθα]
en: [sleep]
---

Sleep in the song is the lovers' undoing. Ares bids Aphrodite come to bed, and “it seemed welcome to her to lie down to sleep”; they lie down and sleep, and the bonds close around them (8.292–298; see [[the-song-of-ares-and-aphrodite]]). Hephaestus expects that they will soon lose their wish to sleep (8.315–317), and Apollo asks Hermes whether he would be willing, held fast in strong bonds, “to sleep in bed beside golden Aphrodite” (8.335–337); Hermes would, under three times as many bonds (8.339–342; see [[hermes]] and [[laughter-of-the-gods]]). The other sleep of the book is the stranger's to come. Arete bids him tie the lid of his chest, “so that no one may do you harm on the way, when hereafter / you sleep sweet sleep as you go in the black ship” (8.443–445; see [[chest]] and [[sweet-sleep]]): Alcinous had promised in Book 7 that he would sleep while the Phaeacians rowed him home (7.318–319; see [[pompe]]).
