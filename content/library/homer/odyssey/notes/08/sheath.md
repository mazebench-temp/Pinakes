# κολεόν (8.404), found only here in Homer; the entry has κολεοῖο and κουλεόν, the Iliad's spelling, which a scholium
# and a lemma give here too (Merry's critical note on 8.404).
grc: [κολεὸν]

Euryalus' sword has “a hilt / of silver, and a sheath of newly sawn ivory / is wound around it” (8.403–405; see [[sword]], [[ivory]] and [[euryalus-the-phaeacian]]). κολεόν is the subject of ἀμφιδεδίνηται, “is wound around”, “encloses” the blade, as Agamemnon's sheath is “of silver” around his sword (W. W. Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 8.404; *Iliad* 11.29–31). A scholium and the lemma of another give κουλεός here, the spelling the Iliad uses where the verse needs a long first syllable (Merry's critical note on 8.404; see the entry). An ivory sheath is found nowhere else in Homer.
