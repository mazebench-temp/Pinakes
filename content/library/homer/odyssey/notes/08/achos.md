# ἄχεϊ (8.530), ἀκάχημαι (8.314; again 19.95).
grc: [ἄχεϊ, ἀκάχημαι]
en: [grief, grieved]
---

The book's grief is the stranger's, and he speaks of it only once: when he sends Demodocus the portion of chine, “I will greet him, for all my grief” (καί μιν προσπτύξομαι ἀχνύμενός περ, 8.478), where προσπτύξομαι is still under ὄφρα, “that I may do homage to him, though sad at heart”, and the grief is chiefly for the tears the first song drew from him, and perhaps for the delay of his departure (W. W. Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 8.477; see the translation's note on 8.477–478 and [[time]]). Alcinous reads it in his tears: “surely grief has beset his mind” (μάλα πού μιν ἄχος φρένας ἀμφιβέβηκεν, 8.541; the scholia record μέγα for μάλα: see the translation's note on 8.541 and [[tears]]). In the simile it is the captive woman's: “her cheeks waste away with most pitiful grief” (ἐλεεινοτάτῳ ἄχεϊ, 8.530; see [[pity]] and [[the-woman-weeping-over-her-husband]]). In the song the grief is Hephaestus', who calls the gods to see the lovers in his bed, “and I am grieved to see it” (ἀκάχημαι, 8.314).
