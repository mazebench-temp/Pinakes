---
summary: In the song Hephaestus lays his lameness on his parents: “Yet no one else is to blame for this, / but my two parents, who I wish had never begotten me” (8.311–312).
refs: [8.312]
---

The word is used, as usual in Homer, to move the blame: Aphrodite dishonors her lame husband and loves Ares, “handsome and sound of foot”, but the lameness is not his fault, ἀτὰρ οὔ τί μοι αἴτιος ἄλλος, / ἀλλὰ τοκῆε δύω (8.308–312; see [[hephaestus]] and [[who-is-to-blame]]). τὼ μὴ γείνασθαι ὄφελλον, “who I wish had never begotten me”, is in Merry's Latin *qui utinam me nunquam genuissent* (W. W. Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 8.312), a wish for a birth undone, as Hector wishes that Paris had never been born (*Iliad* 3.40; see [[never-born]]). The Iliad tells why he might blame them: his mother wished to hide him away because he was lame (*Iliad* 18.395–397).
