# βωμός (8.363; later 17.210, the altar of the Nymphs), first met in the Odyssey here; the Iliad's is in its Book 4
# note.
grc: [βωμός]
---

Freed from the bonds, laughter-loving Aphrodite goes to Cyprus, “to Paphos, where she has a precinct and a fragrant altar” (8.362–363; see [[aphrodite]], [[paphos]], [[cyprus]] and [[fragrant]]). The second verse ends as the Iliad's verse of Zeus' precinct and altar on Gargarus, ἔνθά τέ οἱ τέμενος βωμός τε θυήεις (*Iliad* 8.47–48; see [[gargarus]]), and, with ὅθι τοι, the verse of the precinct and altar of the river Spercheius at its springs, to which Peleus vowed Achilles' hair (*Iliad* 23.144–148; see the translation's row ἔνθα δέ οἱ τέμενος βωμός τε θυήεις). The *Homeric Hymn to Aphrodite* has the goddess's precinct and fragrant altar at Paphos in the same words, with θυώδης for θυήεις (*Homeric Hymn to Aphrodite* 59).
