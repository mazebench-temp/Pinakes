---
summary: Alcinous is “the sacred might of Alcinous”, ἱερὸν μένος Ἀλκινόοιο, four times in the book (8.2, 8.4, 8.385, 8.421; μένος Ἀλκινόοιο 8.423); Athena urges on “the might and spirit of every man” (8.15); and in the song “the might of Hephaestus” loosens the bonds (8.359).
---

μένος stands for the man or the god in a periphrasis of honor: ἱερὸν μένος Ἀλκινόοιο leads the Phaeacians to the assembly and home again (8.2, 8.4, 8.385, 8.421, 8.423; see [[sacred-might-of-alcinous]] and [[alcinous]]), and μένος Ἡφαίστοιο loosens the bonds when Poseidon has pledged himself (8.359; see [[hephaestus]] and [[pledges-for-the-worthless]]). Athena, going through the city in the herald's likeness, “urged on the might and spirit of every man” (ὤτρυνε μένος καὶ θυμὸν ἑκάστου, 8.15), the formula that Ameis notes ten times in the Iliad and only here in the Odyssey (W. W. Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 8.15; see the translation's note on 8.15).
