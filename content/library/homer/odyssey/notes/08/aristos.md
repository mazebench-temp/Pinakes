---
summary: “The best” is the measure of the games and of the songs. The first song is the quarrel of “the best of the Achaeans” (8.78); the young men chosen for the escort are “the best” (8.36), the best of the Phaeacians urge on the singer and go to the games (8.91, 8.108), and each event has its best; the Phaeacians are “the best with ships” (8.247), their dancers “the best” (8.250, 8.383); and in the horse sit “all the best / of the Argives” (8.512–513).
---

The first song turns on the word: Agamemnon rejoiced “that the best of the Achaeans were contending” (ὅ τʼ ἄριστοι Ἀχαιῶν δηριόωντο, 8.78), and the scholia's story of the oracle is that Troy would fall when the best of the Greeks quarreled (W. W. Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 8.75; see [[the-beginning-of-evils]] and [[the-quarrel-of-odysseus-and-achilles]]). Among the Phaeacians it is the measure of the games. Euryalus is the best in looks after Laodamas (8.116–117); Clytoneus is “by far the best at running”, Euryalus surpasses all the best at wrestling (8.123, 8.127; see [[the-games-of-the-phaeacians]]). Alcinous claims for his people that they are “the best with ships” and calls for the dancers “who are the best”, and the stranger grants that they are (8.247, 8.250, 8.383; see [[different-gifts]] and [[dance]]). The last use is the Iliad's sense again: “all the best / of the Argives” sit in the wooden horse (8.512–513; see [[sinon-and-the-wooden-horse]] and [[best-of-the-achaeans]]).
