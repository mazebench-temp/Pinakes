---
summary: Demodocus sings how Odysseus and Achilles “once … contended at a rich feast of the gods” (8.76), and Alcinous calls the lyre “the companion of the rich feast” (8.99).
grc: ["δαιτὶ θαλείῃ"]
refs: [8.99]
---

At 8.99 the noun and its epithet stand apart, ἣ δαιτὶ συνήορός ἐστι θαλείῃ; some wrote ἑταίρῃ for θαλείῃ, probably a gloss on συνήορος from 17.271, where Odysseus calls the lyre the companion that the gods made for the feast (see the translation's note on 8.99). Nitzsch placed the quarrel at the Greeks' first landing on Tenedos, when they were keeping festival in prospect of success, and Merry, reporting him, points to this phrase (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 8.75; [[feast]], [[lyre]], [[the-beginning-of-evils]]).
