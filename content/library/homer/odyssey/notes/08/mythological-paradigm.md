summary: Odysseus to the Phaeacians: “But with men of former days I will not wish to vie, / neither with Heracles nor with Eurytus of Oechalia, / who vied even with the immortals in archery. / That is why great Eurytus died early, and did not reach old age / in his halls; for Apollo in anger / killed him, because he challenged him to shoot with the bow” (8.223–228).
refs: [8.223-228]
---

The example is set against the speaker's own boast: he is the best bowman now living, after Philoctetes at Troy, but he will not vie with the men of old, and the death of Eurytus gives the reason (8.215–228). Merry compares Diomedes' example of Lycurgus, who did not live long either, since he strove with the gods of heaven (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 8.226, comparing *Iliad* 6.130–131), and notes that of the two men only Eurytus contended with the gods (Merry on 8.225; see the translation's note on 8.225). The bow that Odysseus will string in his own house was Eurytus', given him by Eurytus' son Iphitus (21.31–33; [[who-fights-the-gods]], [[eurytus]], [[heracles]], [[men-of-today]]).
