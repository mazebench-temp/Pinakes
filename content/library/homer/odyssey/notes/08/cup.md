summary: A cup of wine is set beside the blind singer (8.70); Odysseus, weeping at the first song, takes the two-handled cup and pours a libation at every pause (8.89); and Alcinous gives him his own golden goblet, so that he may remember him whenever he pours libations at home (8.430–432).
passages:
  - 8.430-432 | The golden goblet of Alcinous
---

**The singer's cup.** Beside Demodocus' table the herald sets “a cup of wine, to drink whenever his heart bade him” (8.70; see [[demodocus]], [[table]] and [[wine]]).

**Libations at the pauses.** As Demodocus sings the quarrel of Odysseus and Achilles, the stranger covers his head with his mantle, and “each time the divine singer stopped singing, / he would wipe away his tears and take the mantle from his head, / and take the two-handled cup and pour libations to the gods” (8.87–89; see [[cloak]], [[libation]] and [[odysseus-weeps-at-the-feast]]). The optatives are iterative, “each time he stopped” (see the translation's note on 8.87–92), and W. W. Merry takes the libation at every pause in the story as a thank-offering to the gods, in remembrance of their constant care of him (*Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 8.87). δέπας ἀμφικύπελλον is the Iliad's formula for the cup of gods and men (*Iliad* 1.584; see the entry).

**The golden goblet.** Among the gifts Alcinous names his own: “And I will grant him this beautiful goblet of mine, / of gold, so that remembering me all his days / he may pour libations in his hall to Zeus and the other gods” (8.430–432; see [[alcinous]], [[gold-and-silver]] and [[guest-gifts]]). A goblet is a gift that keeps a giver in mind at every libation: Menelaus promises Telemachus “a fine goblet, so that you may pour libations to the gods, / the immortals, remembering me all your days” (4.591–592). The golden goblet, χρύσειον ἄλεισον, is in Peleus' hand at his sacrifice in the Iliad, and at Pylos Peisistratus hands it first to the disguised Athena (*Iliad* 11.774; 3.50–53; see the Book 3 note).
