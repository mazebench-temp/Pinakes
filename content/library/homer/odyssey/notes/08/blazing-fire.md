---
summary: Arete has the slave women set a great tripod over the fire, “And they set the tripod for the bath on the blazing fire” (8.435).
grc: ["πυρὶ κηλέῳ"]
---

8.434–437 are the verses of the bath that Achilles orders heated for Patroclus' body (*Iliad* 18.344–348; 8.437 = *Iliad* 18.348); here the water warms for a living guest, who has not been cared for so since he left Calypso (8.449–453). The phrase comes again in Book 9, of the fire in which the stake is hardened for the Cyclops' eye (9.328; [[tripod]], [[bathing]]).
