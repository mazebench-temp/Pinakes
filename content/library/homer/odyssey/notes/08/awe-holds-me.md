---
summary: Odysseus to Alcinous after the ball dance: “you boasted that your dancers were the best, / and indeed it has proved true; awe holds me as I look” (8.383–384).
---

The verse-end is the one with which he owned his wonder at Nausicaa's beauty (6.161); now he says it of the dancing of her brothers (8.370–380). Merry renders ἠδʼ ἄρʼ ἑτοῖμα τέτυκτο “and lo! thy words are fulfilled” (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 8.384; see the Odyssey translation's key words, σέβας; [[dance]], [[alcinous]]).
