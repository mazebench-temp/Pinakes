---
summary: Odysseus ties a knot on his chest, “a complicated one, which lady Circe had once taught him” (8.448).
---

πότνια Κίρκη, “lady Circe” (again 10.394, 10.549, 12.36; see the Odyssey translation's formulas). The goddess is named here for the first time in the poem, before the stranger tells her story (see Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 8.448, on the knot; [[circe]], [[chest]]).
