---
summary: “And at once he spoke among the Phaeacians, lovers of the oar” (8.96, 8.386, 8.535): Alcinous calls for the games, for the guest-gifts, and for the end of the song.
---

Each time the king speaks after watching his guest: his tears at the first song (8.93–103), his praise of the dancers (8.381–397), and his tears at the third (8.532–586). The verse comes once more, when the ship is ready in Book 13 (13.36; see the Odyssey translation's formulas; [[phaeacians]], [[alcinous]]).
