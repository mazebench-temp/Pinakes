---
summary: “yet Agamemnon, lord of men, / rejoiced in his mind that the best of the Achaeans were contending” (8.77–78).
grc: ["ἄναξ δʼ ἀνδρῶν"]
---

The particle divides the Iliad's title, ἄναξ δʼ ἀνδρῶν Ἀγαμέμνων. Merry takes the fierce words as dismaying the bystanders, “but Agamemnon rejoiced,” and νόῳ as “secretly” (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 8.77 and 8.78), for Apollo had foretold the quarrel to him at Pytho (8.79–82); the scholia, as Merry reports them, say that the oracle set the fall of Ilium at the time when the best of the Greeks should quarrel (Merry on 8.75; see the translation's note on 8.74–82; [[agamemnon]], [[the-beginning-of-evils]]).
