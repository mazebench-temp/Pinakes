---
summary: The mantles of the Phaeacians' gifts are ἐυπλυνές, “freshly washed”: each king is to bring “a freshly washed mantle and a tunic”, and Arete adds one of her own to the chest (8.392, 8.425).
---

Alcinous bids each of the kings bring the stranger “a freshly washed mantle and a tunic / and a talent of precious gold” (8.392–393; see [[cloak]], [[tunic]] and [[guest-gifts]]), and in the same words bids Arete put a mantle and tunic of her own in the chest (8.425; see [[chest]]). ἐυπλυνής, from πλύνω, “wash”, is the word of the washing that Nausicaa and her handmaids did at these troughs the day before (6.31, 6.93; see the Book 6 note).
