summary: Demodocus' first song is “the quarrel of Odysseus and Achilles” (νεῖκος, 8.75). At the games Euryalus “rebuked him to his face” (8.158), and Alcinous answers the stranger's anger at the man who “rebuked” him (8.239).
# νεῖκος (8.75; again 16.98, 18.264 and later), νείκεσέ (8.158), νείκεσεν (8.239).
grc: [νεῖκος, νείκεσέ, νείκεσεν]
en: [quarrel, rebuked]
---

**The quarrel.** The lay whose fame reached the sky is “the quarrel of Odysseus and Achilles, son of Peleus, / how once they contended at a rich feast of the gods / with terrible words” (8.75–77; see [[the-quarrel-of-odysseus-and-achilles]] and [[oime]]). νεῖκος stands in apposition with κλέα, “the famous deeds of men” (W. W. Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 8.75): a quarrel of words between the best of the Achaeans, which Agamemnon was glad to see (see [[the-beginning-of-evils]]). ἐκπάγλοις, “terrible”, are words that dismayed the bystanders by their fierceness (Merry on 8.77).

**The rebuke.** At the games νεικέω is the verb of Euryalus' taunt: “Then Euryalus answered him in turn and rebuked him to his face” (νείκεσέ τʼ ἄντην, 8.158; ἄντην, “to the face”: Merry on 8.158; see [[euryalus-the-phaeacian]]). A scholium records φώνησέν τε for the second half of the verse (see the translation's note on 8.158). Alcinous answers the stranger's anger, “because this man stood beside you in the gathering / and rebuked you” (8.238–239; see [[arete]]), and before the day is out the young man makes amends (8.396–415; see [[amends-and-reconciliation]]).
