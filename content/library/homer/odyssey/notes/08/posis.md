---
summary: Zeus is “the loud-thundering husband of Hera” in the stranger's answer to Nausicaa (8.465), and in the simile of the third song a woman weeps “flinging herself upon her dear husband, / who has fallen before his city and his people” (8.523–524).
---

The stranger prays to Zeus under his title as husband, ἐρίγδουπος πόσις Ἥρης, “the loud-thundering husband of Hera” (8.465; see [[husband-of-hera]] and [[loud-thundering]]), when he answers the girl who once wished for such a husband herself (6.244–245). In the simile of the third song the husband is the fallen defender of a city, and his wife throws herself on him as he dies, while the enemy drive her away into slavery; so Odysseus weeps at the song of the city he sacked (8.523–531; see [[odysseus-weeps-at-the-feast]] and [[tears]]). The word does not occur in Demodocus' song of Ares and Aphrodite, where Hephaestus is the wronged husband (see [[marriage]]).
