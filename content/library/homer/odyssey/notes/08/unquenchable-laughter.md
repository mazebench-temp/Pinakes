---
summary: “and unquenchable laughter rose among the blessed gods / as they saw the craft of Hephaestus of much wisdom” (8.326–327).
---

The verse is the Iliad's for the gods' laughter at Hephaestus pouring the wine, “And unquenchable laughter rose among the blessed gods / as they saw Hephaestus bustling through the halls” (*Iliad* 1.599–600). There they laughed at the lame god; here they laugh at the lovers caught by his craft, and after Hermes' answer “laughter rose among the immortal gods” again, all but Poseidon (8.343–344; [[laughter-of-the-gods]], [[homeric-laughter-idiom]], [[of-much-wisdom]]).
