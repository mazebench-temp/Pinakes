---
summary: The Achaeans are the army of the war in Demodocus' songs and in Odysseus' words: “the best of the Achaeans”, whose quarrel Agamemnon welcomed (8.78), the archers at Troy among whom Odysseus counts himself (8.220), the army whose fate the singer tells as if he had been there (8.489–490), and “the sons of the Achaeans” who sacked the city (8.514).
---

**The best of the Achaeans.** In the first song Agamemnon “rejoiced in his mind that the best of the Achaeans were contending” (8.78), Odysseus and Achilles (see [[the-quarrel-of-odysseus-and-achilles]], [[odysseus]] and [[achilles]]). The verse-end ὅ τʼ ἄριστοι Ἀχαιῶν is shaped like the Iliad's ὅ τʼ ἄριστον Ἀχαιῶν οὐδὲν ἔτισας, Achilles' complaint that Agamemnon did not honor “the best of the Achaeans” (*Iliad* 1.244; again 1.412, 16.274; see [[best-of-the-achaeans]]).

**“We Achaeans.”** Odysseus counts himself among them when he speaks of the war: Philoctetes alone surpassed him with the bow “in the land of the Trojans, when we Achaeans shot with the bow” (8.220; see [[philoctetes]] and [[trojans]]).

**The fate of the Achaeans.** He praises Demodocus: “for all too well, in due order, you sing the fate of the Achaeans, / all that they did and suffered, and all that the Achaeans toiled through, / as if perhaps you had been present yourself, or heard it from another” (8.489–491; see [[demodocus]] and [[we-hear-only-the-report]]). The second verse describes the οἶτος, “fate”, that the first names, a word generally referred to οἴσω, “bear”, as Latin *fors* to *fero*, though others derive it from the cry οἶ; Bekker rejected the verse without the authority of the manuscripts, and Düntzer suspected ἔρξαν, “did”, beside “suffered” and “toiled”, and the repeated Ἀχαιοί (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 8.489; see the translation's note on 8.489–490). In the song of the horse that follows, “the sons of the Achaeans sacked the city, / pouring out from the horse” (8.514–515; see [[sons-of-the-achaeans]] and [[the-fall-of-troy]]), and Alcinous asks why the stranger weeps at the same fate under the army's other names, “the fate of the Argive Danaans and of Ilios” (8.578; see [[argives]] and [[danaans]]).
