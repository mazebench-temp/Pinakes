summary: Singers “have their share of honor and respect” among all men, because the Muse has taught them their lays, and Odysseus honors Demodocus with a portion of the chine (8.474–481); the singer is “honored by the people” (8.472). In the song Hephaestus complains that Aphrodite “always dishonors me” (8.309).
# τετιμένον (8.472), ἀτιμάζει (8.309; again 6.283, 14.164, 23.116).
grc: [τετιμένον, ἀτιμάζει]
en: [honored by the people, dishonors]
---

**The singer's honor.** The herald leads in “Demodocus, honored by the people” (λαοῖσι τετιμένον, 8.472; see [[demodocus]] and [[laos]]), and the stranger cuts a portion from the chine of the boar that had been served to him as a mark of honor, and sends it to the singer (8.474–483; W. W. Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 8.475; see [[feast]] and [[plato-chine-of-ajax]]). He gives the reason in a maxim: “For among all men upon the earth singers / have their share of honor and respect, because / the Muse has taught them their lays, and loves the tribe of singers” (τιμῆς ἔμμοροί εἰσι καὶ αἰδοῦς, 8.479–481; see [[singers]], [[aidos]] and [[oime]]). ἔμμοροι is “having a share”: honor is a portion, as the meat is.

**A husband dishonored.** In the song the wronged husband speaks of his own dishonor: “how Aphrodite, the daughter of Zeus, because I am lame, / always dishonors me, and loves destructive Ares” (8.308–309; see [[hephaestus]] and [[the-song-of-ares-and-aphrodite]]). The gods laugh at the remedy he finds (see [[evil-deeds-do-not-prosper]]).
