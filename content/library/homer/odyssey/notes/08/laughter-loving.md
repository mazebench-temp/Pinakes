summary: “and she, laughter-loving Aphrodite, came to Cyprus, / to Paphos, where she has a precinct and a fragrant altar” (8.362–363).
grc: [φιλομμειδὴς]
---

Merry explains the double μ as the trace of the lost σ of the root of “smile,” φιλο-σμειδής, and reports Hesiod's derivation from μήδεα, which he follows Gaisford in rejecting (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 8.362; Hesiod, *Theogony* 200; see the general article). The goddess just loosed from the bonds goes to her own sanctuary with her standing epithet, and the Graces bathe and clothe her there (8.360–366; [[aphrodite]], [[paphos]], [[the-adornment-of-a-goddess]]).
