summary: The day of the games begins and ends in the house of Alcinous. The stranger “has newly come to the house of wise-hearted Alcinous”, Athena tells the Phaeacians; the king summons the young men and the kings to a feast there, and its colonnades, courts and rooms fill (8.13, 8.28–58). The lyre is fetched from it for the dance (8.254–257), and at sunset the gifts are carried to it, where the stranger is bathed, takes leave of Nausicaa, and hears the song of the horse (8.417–586).

**Summoned to the house.** Athena, in the likeness of the king's herald, tells the leaders of the Phaeacians to come and learn about the stranger “who has newly come to the house of wise-hearted Alcinous” (8.13; see [[athena]] and [[heralds]]). In the assembly Alcinous says that the stranger “has come to my house in his wanderings”, and that no other man “who comes to my house” stays long for want of an escort (8.28–33; see [[alcinous]] and [[pompe]]). The young men of the crew are to come “to our house” for a quick feast, and the scepter-bearing kings “to my beautiful house”, so that they may make the stranger welcome in the halls (8.38–42; see [[feast]] and [[kings]]).

**The feast and the lyre.** The young men go “to the great house of wise-hearted Alcinous”, and its colonnades, courts and rooms fill with men (8.56–58; on the parts of the house, see the Book 8 note of [[house]]). From the games Alcinous sends for the clear-toned lyre “that lies somewhere in our house”, and the herald fetches “the hollow lyre from the king's house” (8.254–257; see [[lyre]]).

**The evening.** At sunset the gifts are complete, “And the noble heralds carried them to the house of Alcinous; / and the sons of noble Alcinous received them / and set the beautiful gifts beside their revered mother”, and the king leads the guests to the high chairs (8.417–422; see [[guest-gifts]], [[arete-queen-of-the-phaeacians]] and [[throne]]). The rest of the book passes inside: Arete packs the chest brought from her chamber, the stranger is bathed, Nausicaa takes her leave of him by the doorpost of the hall, and at the supper Demodocus sings the wooden horse until the king stops him and asks the stranger who he is (8.423–586; see [[chest]], [[bathing]], [[door]] and [[odysseus-weeps-at-the-feast]]). There, in the next four books, Odysseus tells his tale (see [[the-tale-to-alcinous]]).
