**The ball.** After the song of Ares and Aphrodite, Alcinous bids his sons Halius and Laodamas dance alone, “since no one vied with them. / And when they had taken in their hands the beautiful ball, / a purple one, which wise-hearted Polybus had made for them” (8.370–373; see [[halius-son-of-alcinous]], [[laodamas-son-of-alcinous]], [[ball]] and [[purple]]), one would fling it toward the shadowy clouds and the other leap from the ground to catch it, and then they danced (8.374–380; see [[dance]]). The maker is named for his work and given one epithet, δαΐφρων, “wise-hearted” (see [[wise-hearted]]); nothing else is told of him.

**Makers named beside their work.** The poems name a few such makers where the thing is used: the shield of Ajax, “which Tychius had toiled to make for him, / by far the best of leather-workers” (*Iliad* 7.220–221; see [[tychius]]); the chair of Penelope, inlaid with ivory and silver, which the craftsman Icmalius once made (*Odyssey* 19.55–57, a plain rendering); and in this book the wooden horse, “which Epeius made with Athena's help” (8.493; see [[epeius]]). The verb is the same: τήν σφιν Πόλυβος ποίησε δαΐφρων, “which wise-hearted Polybus had made for them” (8.373), as ὅς οἱ ἐποίησεν σάκος αἰόλον, “who made him the flashing shield” (*Iliad* 7.222), and τὸν Ἐπειὸς ἐποίησεν, “which Epeius made” (8.493).
