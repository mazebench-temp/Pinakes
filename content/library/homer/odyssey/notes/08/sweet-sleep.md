---
summary: Arete bids the stranger tie a knot on his chest, so that no one may do him harm on the way, “when hereafter / you sleep sweet sleep as you go in the black ship” (8.444–445).
---

Her word foresees the voyage of Book 13, when sleep falls on him in the Phaeacian ship, νήδυμος, unwaking, the sweetest, most like death (13.79–80; [[chest]], [[hypnos]]).
