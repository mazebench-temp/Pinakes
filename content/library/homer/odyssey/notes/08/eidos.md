# εἴσατʼ "made as if", of Hephaestus' feigned journey (8.283), only here in the Odyssey.
grc: ["εἴσατʼ ἴμεν"]
en: [made as if to go]
---

**Likenesses.** Athena summons the Phaeacians to the assembly “in the likeness of the herald of wise-hearted Alcinous” (εἰδομένη κήρυκι, 8.8; see [[athena]] and [[heralds]]), and at the games she marks the stranger's throw “in form like a man” (ἀνδρὶ δέμας ἐικυῖα, 8.194), with the verb of likeness, ἔοικα. The stranger is glad to see “a gentle companion in the gathering” (8.199–200; see [[eneeie]]).

**Looks and worth.** Euryalus is introduced as “the best in looks and form / of all the Phaeacians after noble Laodamas” (εἶδός τε δέμας τε, 8.116–117; see [[euryalus-the-phaeacian]]). When he taunts the stranger, the answer uses his own advantage against him: “For one man is less in looks, / but a god crowns his words with beauty … Another again in looks is like the immortals, / but no grace is set as a crown around his words; / so with you too, your looks are outstanding … but in mind you are worthless” (8.169–177; see [[different-gifts]] and [[beauty-and-its-price]]). The young Phaeacians judge by the body: Laodamas finds the stranger “no mean man” in build, φυή (8.134), and Euryalus says he is “not like an athlete” (8.164).

**Seeming.** In the song the verb is used of pretense and of wish. Hephaestus εἴσατʼ ἴμεν ἐς Λῆμνον, “made as if to go to Lemnos” (8.283), “made show of going”, as Ovid says of the same story, *fingit iter Lemnon* (*Ars amatoria* 2.579, as W. W. Merry quotes it, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 8.283; see [[lemnos]] and [[hephaestus]]); and to Aphrodite “it seemed welcome … to lie down to sleep” (ἀσπαστὸν ἐείσατο, 8.295).
