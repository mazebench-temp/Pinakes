---
summary: Achilles is named once, in Demodocus' first song: the quarrel of Odysseus and “Achilles, son of Peleus”, the two best of the Achaeans, at a rich feast of the gods (8.75–78).
---

“the quarrel of Odysseus and Achilles, son of Peleus, / how once they contended at a rich feast of the gods / with terrible words” (8.75–77; see [[the-quarrel-of-odysseus-and-achilles]] and [[peleus]]). The two are together ἄριστοι Ἀχαιῶν, “the best of the Achaeans” (8.78), the title over which Achilles quarrels with Agamemnon in the Iliad, when he says that the king has paid no honor to the best of the Achaeans (*Iliad* 1.244, 1.412; see [[quarrel-of-achilles-and-agamemnon]]). The poem does not tell what the quarrel was about. The scholia, as W. W. Merry reports them, made it a dispute after the death of Hector in which Achilles praised courage and Odysseus intelligence (*Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 8.75), the contrast that Odysseus draws himself in the Iliad, when he grants that Achilles is the stronger with the spear and claims to surpass him in understanding (*Iliad* 19.216–219).

Odysseus hears the song of himself and Achilles without being known, and weeps (8.83–92; see [[odysseus-weeps-at-the-feast]]). In the eleventh book he will meet Achilles' shade among the dead (11.467–540, Greek only).
