---
summary: Odysseus fears only the footrace: “for I have been too shamefully overcome / among many waves, since on board the ship my keep was not / unfailing; and so my limbs are loosened” (8.231–233).
grc: ["γυῖα λέλυνται"]
en: [limbs are loosened]
---

The half-verse is the Iliad's for rowers worn out at their oars, “and their limbs are loosened with weariness” (*Iliad* 7.6), and Telemachus says it of Irus after his beating (18.242). The loosening that is death in battle is here the weariness of the sea (see the translation's note on 8.232–233; [[sea]], [[odysseus]]).
