# πρηκτῆρες (8.162) and πρήσσεσκον (8.259), only here in Homer.
grc: [πρηκτῆρες, πρήσσεσκον]
en: [traders, used to manage]
---

**Traders.** Euryalus likens the stranger to “a leader of sailors who are traders” (ἀρχὸς ναυτάων οἵ τε πρηκτῆρες ἔασιν, 8.162; see [[trade]]). For the word Merry compares Nestor's question to his guests, whether they sail “on some business” (κατὰ πρῆξιν, 3.72), and finds no need for Cobet's conjecture (W. W. Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 8.162–164). The Iliad has πρηκτήρ once, of the finished hero, “a speaker of words and a doer of deeds” (*Iliad* 9.443; see [[speaker-of-words-doer-of-deeds]]): the same noun names the noble doer and the man who does business at sea.

**Managing.** The verb is used of the nine stewards of the games, “officers of the people, who used to manage everything well at the gatherings” (8.258–259). The tense of πρήσσεσκον shows that their regular duties are meant, and not only what they did that day (Merry on 8.258; see [[dance]] and the translation's table of key words).
