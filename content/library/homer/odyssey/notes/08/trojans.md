summary: The Trojans are the other side of the war in Demodocus' songs and in Odysseus' boast: the people upon whom, with the Danaans, the beginning of bane was rolling (8.82), the land where Philoctetes alone outshot Odysseus (8.220), and the people who dragged the wooden horse into their citadel, debated its fate three ways, and let it stand, to their ruin (8.503–513).
# Unnamed: the Trojans who talked on and on, sitting around the horse, and their three counsels (8.505-510).
refs: [8.505-510]
---

**The war's two sides.** In the first song the quarrel foretold at Pytho came when “the beginning of bane was rolling upon / Trojans and Danaans” (8.81–82; see [[danaans]] and [[pytho]]), and in Odysseus' boast the war is the time when he and the other Achaeans shot with the bow “in the land of the Trojans” (8.220; see [[achaeans]] and [[troy]]).

**The horse in the citadel.** In the third song the men in the horse sit “in the place of assembly of the Trojans”, “for the Trojans themselves had dragged it into the citadel” (8.503–504; see [[assembly]] and [[sinon-and-the-wooden-horse]]). There “they talked on and on, confusedly, / sitting around it; and they were divided three ways in their counsel”: to split the hollow timber with the bronze, to throw it down the rocks from the height, or to let it stand as an offering to appease the gods (8.505–509). ἄκριτα may be “endless”, “undecided” or “indiscriminate”, the last either of one man contradicting himself or of many contradicting each other; Merry takes it of the clash of different speakers' views, and finds two of the senses joined in Virgil's line on the same debate, “scinditur incertum studia in contraria vulgus” (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 8.505; *Aeneid* 2.39; see [[virgil]] and the translation's note on 8.505). ἥνδανε is “was finding favor”: only one of the three was decided on in the end (Merry on 8.506), and the city was fated to perish once it had taken the horse in (8.510–512). Düntzer, Merry reports, quotes from the epitome of Arctinus that some said the horse ought to be dedicated to Athena (on 8.509; see [[athena]]). The men inside were “bringing slaughter and doom to the Trojans” (8.513, as 4.273; see [[argives]]).
