---
summary: Ares sees “Hephaestus, famed for his craft, going away” (8.286), and Poseidon keeps begging “Hephaestus, famed for his work, to set Ares free” (8.345).
grc: [κλυτοτέχνην, κλυτοεργὸν]
en: [famed for his work]
---

κλυτοεργός, “famed for his work,” is found only here in Homer, later in the same song as κλυτοτέχνης (see the Odyssey translation's key words, κλυτοεργός). The work they praise is the snare itself, bonds forged at the anvil and poured around the bed, fine as spider's webs and invisible even to the gods (8.273–282), and the gods' laughter is at “the craft of Hephaestus of much wisdom” (8.327; [[of-much-wisdom]], [[lame-in-both-legs]], [[hephaestus]]).
