---
summary: Eurytus, who challenged Apollo, “did not reach old age / in his halls” (8.226–227); and Alcinous tells his father's prophecy, “So the old man used to say” (8.570).
---

Old age is the measure of a full life, and Eurytus did not reach it, for Apollo killed him early (οὐδʼ ἐπὶ γῆρας / ἵκετʼ ἐνὶ μεγάροισι, 8.226–227; see [[who-fights-the-gods]]). The old man of the book is absent: Nausithous, the king's father, whose prophecy of Poseidon's anger Alcinous repeats, ὣς ἀγόρευʼ ὁ γέρων, “So the old man used to say” (8.564–570; see [[nausithous]] and [[pompe]]). In the hall young and old gather for the feast (νέοι ἠδὲ παλαιοί, 8.58).
