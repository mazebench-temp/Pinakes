---
summary: Named only in his son's title, when the Phaeacians gather at the assembly and gaze at “the wise-hearted son of Laertes”, on whom Athena has poured a wondrous grace (8.17–19).
---

**His son's name.** The Phaeacians do not yet know the stranger's name, but the narrator gives it through his father's: “and many gazed when they saw / the wise-hearted son of Laertes; for upon him Athena / poured a wondrous grace, upon his head and shoulders” (8.17–19; see [[odysseus]], [[athena]] and [[gold-poured-around-silver]]). υἱὸν Λαέρταο δαΐφρονα is a phrase, not the patronymic Λαερτιάδης of the formula of address (see [[son-of-laertes]] and [[wise-hearted]]); the Phaeacians will hear the name of his father from his own lips when he tells them who he is: “I am Odysseus, son of Laertes” (9.19, Greek only, a plain rendering).
