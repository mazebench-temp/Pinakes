---
summary: Alcinous bids the young men of the escort make the oars fast at the rowing benches (8.37), and they fit them in their leather straps when they launch the ship (8.53). The Phaeacians themselves are “lovers of the oar” and “of the long oars” (8.96, 8.191).
---

Alcinous gives the crew their orders: “And when you have all made the oars fast at the rowing benches, / step out again” (8.37–38; see [[rowing-benches]] and [[ships]]). At the launching they “fitted the oars in their leather straps” (8.53), as the suitors did (4.782; see the Book 4 note and the translation's row τροπός). The τροπός, W. W. Merry explains, is the old Latin *struppus* and our “strap”, the leather loop fixed at the gunwale in which the oar works (*Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 4.782); on what the oars were made fast to, see the Book 8 note of [[rowing-benches]].

The oar is in the people's epithets, φιλήρετμοι, “lovers of the oar” (8.96, 8.386, 8.535), and δολιχήρετμοι, “of the long oars” (8.191, 8.369; see [[lovers-of-the-oar]], [[long-oared]] and [[phaeacians]]), and in the name of one of the young men who rise for the games, Ἐρετμεύς, from ἐρετμόν, “oar” (8.112; see [[eretmeus]] and the translation's note on 8.111–119). Their ships, Alcinous says, need no helmsmen or steering oars (8.557–558; see [[steering-oar]]).
