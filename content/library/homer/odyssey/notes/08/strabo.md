---
summary: Strabo quotes this book three times. Demodocus' first song is his evidence for the age of the oracle at Delphi, Odysseus' boast of his spear for the throwing spear, and Alcinous' call to the dancers for the Corybants; and he names the Sintians of the song, to whom Hephaestus pretends to go, a Thracian people of Lemnos.
---

- **The oracle at Delphi.** Delphi is famous for the temple of Pythian Apollo and for its oracle, which is ancient, “if indeed Agamemnon is said by the poet to have consulted it; for the lyre-singer is brought in singing” (a plain rendering) the quarrel of Odysseus and Achilles, and how Agamemnon rejoiced, “For so Phoebus Apollo had told him in prophecy” at Pytho (*Geography* 9.3.2, quoting 8.75–80 with omissions). Like Aristotle, who calls him “the lyre player” (*Poetics* 1455a3), Strabo names Demodocus by his art, ὁ κιθαρῳδός: [[pytho]], [[the-quarrel-of-odysseus-and-achilles]] and [[demodocus]].
- **The throwing spear.** The Euboeans fought hand to hand with thrusting spears, but there were also spears for throwing, such as the Pelian ash, and the man who said, δουρὶ δʼ ἀκοντίζω, ὅσον οὐκ ἄλλος τις ὀιστῷ, “And I throw the spear as far as no other man shoots an arrow” (8.229), meant the javelin (*Geography* 10.1.13): [[the-pelian-ash]] and [[spear]].
- **The dancers.** The Corybants, Strabo says, are named from walking with a butting motion in the dance, “and the poet calls them βητάρμονες”, quoting Alcinous' call to the Phaeacian dancers (*Geography* 10.3.21, a plain rendering). His verse begins δεῦτʼ ἄγε, the opening of Athena's summons to the Phaeacian leaders (8.11), where the text has ἀλλʼ ἄγε (8.250): [[dance]] and [[curetes]].
- **The Sintians.** Hephaestus pretends to go to Lemnos, “to the Sintians of savage speech” (8.294). Merry, calling them a Thracian people later known as Sapaeans, cites Strabo (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 8.283), who says that some of the Thracians were called Sintians, then Sinti, then Saii, among whom Archilochus threw away his shield, and are now called Sapaeans, all living around Abdera and the islands near Lemnos (*Geography* 12.3.20); elsewhere he writes that the Sinti, a Thracian people, lived on Lemnos, “and so Homer calls them Sintians” (*Geography* 7 fr. 46; plain renderings): [[sintians]] and [[lemnos]].
