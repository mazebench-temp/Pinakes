---
summary: The wooden horse is the “hollow ambush” from which the sons of the Achaeans pour out to sack Troy, in Demodocus' last song (8.515).
---

“And he sang how the sons of the Achaeans sacked the city, / pouring out from the horse, leaving the hollow ambush” (8.514–515; see [[the-fall-of-troy]] and [[the-wooden-horse]]). κοῖλον λόχον, “the hollow ambush”, is the phrase of Menelaus' story of the horse, when Helen walked around it three times (4.277), and the singer's words for the men inside it, “where they were sitting, all the best / of the Argives” (8.512–513), are Menelaus' too (4.272–273). The Achaeans' last stratagem at Troy is an ambush that the Trojans themselves let stand “as a great offering to appease the gods” (8.509–510).
