# λαοῖσι τετιμένος (13.28) to be added with Book 13.
grc: ["λαοῖσι τετιμένον"]
en: [honored by the people]
---

The singer's own verse-end, said of him here and in Book 13 (13.28). Merry takes the words as an interpretation of the name Demodocus, which he renders *populo acceptus* (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 8.472 and 8.44). The Iliad's formula for those whom the people honor is a king's or a priest's, θεὸς δʼ ὣς τίετο δήμῳ (see the general article); the singer's honor comes from the people, and at the same supper the guest honors him with a portion of the chine, for “among all men upon the earth singers / have their share of honor and respect” (8.474–481; [[demodocus]], [[trusty]], [[time]], [[singers]]).
