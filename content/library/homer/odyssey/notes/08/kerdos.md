summary: Euryalus taunts the stranger with looking like a merchant captain mindful of his freight and “his greedily snatched gains” (κερδέων θʼ ἁρπαλέων, 8.163–164); Alcinous asks him not to hide what he is asked “with crafty thoughts” (νοήμασι κερδαλέοισιν, 8.548).
# κερδέων (8.164) and κερδαλέοισιν (8.548), only here in Homer.
grc: [κερδέων, κερδαλέοισιν]
en: [gains, crafty thoughts]
---

**Gains.** Euryalus does not liken the stranger to a man skilled in contests, “but to one who goes to and fro with a many-benched ship, / a leader of sailors who are traders, / and who is mindful of his freight, or watches over his homeward cargo / and his greedily snatched gains” (8.159–164; see [[trade]], [[prexis]] and [[euryalus-the-phaeacian]]). Merry renders “a home-cargo of greedily gotten gains”, the τε … καί being disjunctive (W. W. Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 8.161, 8.162–164; see the translation's note on 8.161–164). The insult is that the stranger lives for profit; κέρδος is the gain itself, the advantage a man stands to win (see the entry).

**Crafty thoughts.** The adjective turns the word from profit to cunning. When Alcinous asks the stranger his name, he bids him not hide anything “with crafty thoughts” (8.548; see [[noos]] and [[as-good-as-a-brother]]): κερδαλέος is the word of shrewd planning, praise or reproach as the speaker pleases, and the king, who has seen the stranger keep back his name for a night and a day, asks him to put it aside (see [[metis]]).
