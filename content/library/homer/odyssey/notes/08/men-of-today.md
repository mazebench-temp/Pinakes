summary: Odysseus claims to be far the best archer “of all the mortals who are now on the earth and eat bread”, but “with men of former days I will not wish to vie”, with Heracles or Eurytus of Oechalia, who vied even with the immortals (8.221–225).
# The Odyssey's form of the half-verse, ὅσσοι νῦν βροτοί εἰσιν (8.222).
grc: ["νῦν βροτοί εἰσιν"]
en: [all the mortals who are now on the earth]
refs: [8.223-225]
---

The stranger's boast measures himself as the Iliad's heroes do, against the living and against the dead. Among the living he is first with the bow, after Philoctetes at Troy, “of all the mortals who are now on the earth and eat bread” (8.219–222; see [[philoctetes]]). σῖτον ἔδοντες marks men as men, apart from the gods, who feed on ambrosia, from beasts, and perhaps from ogres and the like who plant no corn (W. W. Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 8.222; see [[ichor]]), as Diomedes speaks of “the mortals who eat the fruit of the field” (*Iliad* 6.142). With the men of former days he will not vie: “I would not care” (Merry on 8.223), since Heracles and Eurytus vied even with the immortals in archery, and Eurytus died young for it (8.223–228; see [[heracles]], [[eurytus]] and [[who-fights-the-gods]]). Nestor in the Iliad says the same of the men of his youth: “against those men no one / of the mortals now living on earth could fight” (*Iliad* 1.271–272; see the entry).
