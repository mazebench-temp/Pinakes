**A gift of amends.** Alcinous bids Euryalus make amends to the stranger “with words / and with a gift, since the word he spoke was by no means fair and right” (8.396–397; see [[euryalus-the-phaeacian]] and [[alcinous]]), and the young man answers:

{{quote:ab902eee-247c-5e56-9792-58aa4721297a}}

The offer is made in the words with which Achilles, at Patroclus' games, gives Eumelus the corselet of Asteropaeus to console him, “of bronze, around which a casting of shining tin / runs in a circle; it will be worth much to him” (*Iliad* 23.560–562): there the casting of tin, here the ivory sheath, ἀμφιδεδίνηται, “is wound around it” (W. W. Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 8.404; see [[sheath]] and [[ivory]]). Whether πολέος ἄξιον means simply a thing of price, or a gift that will make up for much, for the injury done, Merry leaves open (on 8.405; see the translation's note on 8.405). ἄορ, ξίφος and the hilt, κώπη, are all here (see the entry), and the sword is “silver-studded”, ἀργυρόηλον, as Hector's sword is when he gives it to Ajax after their duel (8.406, 8.416; *Iliad* 7.303–304; see [[silver-studded]] and [[gold-and-silver]]).

**The farewell.** “Farewell, stranger, father; and if any word has been spoken / that was terrible, may the storm winds at once snatch it up and carry it away” (8.408–409), and Odysseus answers the gift with a blessing: “And may no longing for this sword ever come upon you hereafter, / which you have given me, making amends with your words” (8.414–415; see [[guest-gifts]] and [[olbos]]). “He spoke, and around his shoulders he slung the silver-studded sword” (8.416), ending as the verse of Agamemnon's arming ends (*Iliad* 2.45).
