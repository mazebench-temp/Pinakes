---
summary: ἄναξ is the title of Agamemnon, “lord of men” (8.77), and in the song of Hephaestus and Apollo, “lord Hephaestus” (8.270), “far-working lord Apollo” (8.323), “lord Apollo, son of Zeus” (8.334), “lord Apollo who shoots from afar” (8.339).
---

The title belongs to men and gods alike. In the first song Agamemnon, “lord of men”, rejoices at the quarrel of the best of the Achaeans (ἄναξ ἀνδρῶν, 8.77; see [[lord-of-men]] and [[agamemnon]]). In the song of Ares and Aphrodite it is Hephaestus whose bed is shamed, “of lord Hephaestus” (8.270; see [[hephaestus]]), and Apollo, who asks Hermes his question and is answered by name (8.323, 8.334, 8.339; see [[apollo]], [[far-worker]] and [[far-shooter]]).
