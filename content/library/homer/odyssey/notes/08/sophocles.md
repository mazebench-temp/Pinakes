---
summary: Merry finds the most trustworthy evidence for the quarrel of Demodocus' first song in a satyr play of Sophocles, the Gathering of the Achaeans or Diners, and calls a verse of the Ajax an imitation of the bath-water warmed for Odysseus. Neither text is read here; both are given as Merry reports them.
---

- **The quarrel at the feast.** The scholia set the quarrel of Odysseus and Achilles (8.75–82) after the death of Hector, a dispute of courage against wit; Merry, with Nitzsch, sets it at the feast on Tenedos before the Iliad, and finds “the most trustworthy evidence” in the fragments of Sophocles' satyr play, the *Gathering of the Achaeans* or *Diners*. To it he refers Aristotle's example of Achilles angry at Tenedos because he was not invited to dinner (*Rhetoric* 1401b16–19), and Plutarch's remark that the Odysseus of Sophocles, provoking Achilles, says not that he is angry because of the dinner but that now he sees the seats of Troy he is afraid (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 8.75): [[the-quarrel-of-odysseus-and-achilles]], [[aristotle]] and [[plutarch]].
- **The tripod over the fire.** For Alcinous' “warm a cauldron on the fire for him” and the great tripod that the slave women set over the fire for the stranger's bath (8.426, 8.434–437), Merry cites as “the imitation” a verse of the *Ajax* that bids men set a tall tripod over the fire (*Ajax* 1404, as Merry quotes it; Merry on 8.426): [[tripod]], [[cauldron]] and [[bathing]].
