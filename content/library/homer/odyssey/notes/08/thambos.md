summary: The Phaeacians go out “to marvel at the games” (8.108); Odysseus “marveled in his heart” at the flashing feet of the dancers (8.265); and Nausicaa, at the doorpost, “marveled at Odysseus as she looked at him with her eyes” (8.459).
# θαυμανέοντες (8.108), θαύμαζε (8.265; again 24.370).
grc: [θαυμανέοντες, θαύμαζε]
en: [marvel, marveled]
---

The book is a spectacle, and its wonder is named three times. The best of the Phaeacians go to the place of assembly “to marvel at the games” (ἀέθλια θαυμανέοντες, 8.108), the tests of prowess (W. W. Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 8.108; see [[the-games-of-the-phaeacians]]). The stranger watches the young dancers strike the divine dancing floor with their feet, “And Odysseus / gazed at the flashing of their feet, and marveled in his heart” (8.264–265; see [[dance]]), and afterward tells the king, “awe holds me as I look” (8.384; see [[sebas]]). At evening Nausicaa, who had her beauty from the gods, stands by the doorpost and marvels at him (θαύμαζεν, 8.457–459; see [[nausicaa]] and [[beauty-and-its-price]]). The garments the Graces put on Aphrodite at Paphos are “a wonder to see” (θαῦμα ἰδέσθαι, 8.366).
