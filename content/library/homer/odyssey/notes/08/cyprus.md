---
summary: Freed from Hephaestus' bonds, Aphrodite goes to Cyprus, to her precinct and altar at Paphos, while Ares goes to Thrace (8.361–363).
---

When the bonds are loosed, Ares goes off to Thrace, “and she, laughter-loving Aphrodite, came to Cyprus, / to Paphos, where she has a precinct and a fragrant altar” (8.361–363; see [[aphrodite]], [[thrace]] and [[paphos]]). The poem names the island first and then the town of the sanctuary within it, as the *Hymn to Aphrodite* does in the same words (*Hymn* 5.58–59). Each lover goes to a land of his or her own: the goddess to the island whose name she bears in the Iliad as Κύπρις, “the Cyprian” (*Iliad* 5.330; see [[cypris]]). Cyprus was a stage of Menelaus' wanderings in the fourth book, the first of the lands where he gathered his wealth (4.83); here it is the goddess's own.
