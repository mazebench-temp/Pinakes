---
summary: The book opens with the dawn verse, “And when early-born Dawn appeared, the rosy-fingered, / the sacred might of Alcinous rose from his bed” (8.1–2), on the day of the assembly, the games and the gifts.
---

The two verses open Book 2 and the mornings at Pylos and at Sparta in the same way, each with a man of the house rising from his bed, Telemachus, Nestor and Menelaus (2.1–2, 3.404–405, 4.306–307); here the king rises, and his guest with him (8.3). The day ends at the sunset of 8.417, when the gifts have been brought in, and the rest of the book is its evening (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 8.417; [[sunset-lines]], [[early-born]], [[rosy-fingered]], [[sacred-might-of-alcinous]]).
