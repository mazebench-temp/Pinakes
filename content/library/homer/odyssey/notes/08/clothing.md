---
summary: Alcinous counts “changes of clothing” among the Phaeacians' pleasures (8.249); in Demodocus' song the Graces clothe Aphrodite in lovely garments at Paphos (8.366); and Arete lays the clothing and the gold of the Phaeacians' gifts in the stranger's chest (8.440).
---

**Changes of clothing.** “And always dear to us are the feast and the lyre and the dances / and changes of clothing and warm baths and beds” (8.248–249; see [[phaeacians]], [[feast]], [[dance]], [[bathing]] and [[bed]]). εἵματα ἐξημοιβά are clothes worn one after another, W. W. Merry explains, as the swineherd's hut has no store of cloaks and ἐπημοιβοὶ χιτῶνες, tunics to change into (*Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 8.249; 14.513, Greek only). Merry reports Nitzsch's view that the list paints a people devoted to peace, as the scholia take it, and adds that it has a color of luxury too (on 8.244; see [[different-gifts]]).

**The garments of the goddess.** At the end of Demodocus' second song the Graces bathe Aphrodite at Paphos, anoint her “with oil / immortal, such as is upon the gods who live forever, / and they clothed her in lovely garments, a wonder to see” (8.364–366; see [[graces]], [[aphrodite]], [[the-adornment-of-a-goddess]] and [[the-song-of-ares-and-aphrodite]]). A hymn tells the same bath and anointing at Paphos in the same verses, but there the goddess puts on her lovely clothes herself (*Homeric Hymn to Aphrodite* 58–64).

**The gifts.** Arete brings out the chest and puts in it “the beautiful gifts, / the clothing and the gold, which the Phaeacians had given him” (8.439–440; see [[chest]], [[gold-and-silver]] and [[guest-gifts]]), with a mantle and a tunic of her own (8.441; see [[cloak]] and [[tunic]]).
