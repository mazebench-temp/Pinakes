---
summary: Two purple things: the great purple mantle that Odysseus draws over his head to hide his tears at Demodocus' first song (8.84–85), and the purple ball that Polybus made for the sons of Alcinous (8.372–373).
---

**The purple mantle.** As Demodocus sings the quarrel of Odysseus and Achilles, Odysseus “took his great purple mantle in his strong hands / and drew it down over his head, and covered his handsome face” (8.84–85; see [[cloak]] and [[odysseus-weeps-at-the-feast]]). πορφύρεον μέγα φᾶρος are the words of Agamemnon's “great purple cloak”, which he holds up in his hand to rally the Achaeans at the ships (*Iliad* 8.221).

**The purple ball.** For the dance of Halius and Laodamas the two young men take “the beautiful ball, / a purple one, which wise-hearted Polybus had made for them” (8.372–373; see [[ball]] and [[polybus-the-phaeacian]]).
