# οἰνοποτῆρας, "men drinking wine" (8.456), found only here in Homer.
grc: [οἰνοποτῆρας]
---

Beside Demodocus' table the herald sets “a cup of wine, to drink whenever his heart bade him” (8.70; see [[cup]] and [[demodocus]]). Odysseus steps out of the bath and goes “among the men / drinking wine” (8.456–457), οἰνοποτῆρες, a word found only here, and the evening meal begins as “they were already serving out portions and mixing the wine” (8.470; see [[feast]] and [[mixing-bowl]]). The libations of the book are poured from the cup: Odysseus pours one at every pause in the first song (8.89), and Alcinous gives him a golden goblet for his libations at home (8.430–432; see [[libation]]).
