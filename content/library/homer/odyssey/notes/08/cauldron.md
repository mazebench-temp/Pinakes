summary: Alcinous bids his household warm a cauldron on the fire for the stranger's bath, χαλκόν, “the bronze” (8.426); the women heat the water in a great tripod (8.434–437).
# "πυρὶ χαλκὸν" (8.426), the cauldron named by its metal, claimed by the phrase (it applies in the Odyssey only): χαλκόν
# alone is bronze everywhere, and the same two words are the riddle's "bronze glued on with fire" in the Poetics and the
# Rhetoric (1458a29, 1405b1).
grc: ["πυρὶ χαλκὸν"]
en: [cauldron]
---

“And warm a cauldron on the fire for him, and heat water” (8.426; see [[bathing]] and [[alcinous]]). The Greek says only χαλκόν, “bronze”: the vessel is named by its metal (see [[bronze]]). W. W. Merry joins ἀμφί with πυρί, “warm for him a cauldron over the fire”, the legs of the tripod bestriding the fire, and notes that here the tripod is stand and cauldron in one, as the verses that follow show (*Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 8.426; see [[tripod]]): Arete's women set “a great tripod over the fire”, pour in the water, and “the fire played around the belly of the tripod” (8.434–437).
