summary: The lyre of Demodocus is “the clear-toned lyre”: Pontonous hangs it on its peg above the singer (8.67, 8.105), Alcinous sends for it to the palace (8.254) and the herald brings it to the dancing floor (8.261), and at the end Alcinous bids the singer “now stop his clear-toned lyre” (8.537).
grc: ["φόρμιγγα λίγειαν"]
---

The phrase is the Iliad's φόρμιγγι λιγείῃ in another case (*Iliad* 9.186; see the Odyssey translation's key words). Once the book calls the same lyre “hollow,” φόρμιγγα γλαφυρήν (8.257), and once the instrument of the Phaeacians' pleasures κίθαρις, “the lyre” (8.248; [[lyre]], [[demodocus]], [[pontonous]]).
