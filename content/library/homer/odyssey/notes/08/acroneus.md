---
summary: When Alcinous leads the Phaeacians out to the contests, Acroneus is the first of the young men named as they rise (8.111). He wins none of the five contests (8.120–130).
---

**First of the list.** “And young men stood up, many and noble. / There rose up Acroneus and Ocyalus and Elatreus” (8.110–111; see [[the-games-of-the-phaeacians]]). ὦρτο, “rose up”, opens the list as it opens the Iliad's lists of men who rise to a challenge or a contest: “First by far rose Agamemnon, lord of men”, of the nine who stand up to meet Hector, and “First by far rose Eumelus, lord of men”, of the drivers in the chariot race (*Iliad* 7.162, 23.288). Twelve young men are named in four verses (8.111–114), then Euryalus and the three sons of Alcinous (8.115–119; see [[euryalus-the-phaeacian]] and [[alcinous]]). The poem names the winner of each contest, and Acroneus is not among them (8.120–130).
