---
summary: “But when they had put away the desire for drink and food, / the Muse set the singer on to sing the famous deeds of men” (8.72–73), and at supper, “then Odysseus of many wiles spoke to Demodocus” (8.485–486).
---

The verse is the Odyssey's end of a meal, and in Ithaca, too, it is followed by song (1.150–152). Here both songs come after it: the quarrel of Odysseus and Achilles, at the Muse's prompting, and the wooden horse, at the guest's request (8.73–82, 8.487–498; [[feast-type-scene]], [[demodocus]]).
