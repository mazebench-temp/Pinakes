---
summary: Demodocus sings “about the love of Ares and well-crowned Aphrodite” (8.267): the Sun sees them “lying together in love” (8.271), Ares comes “eager for the love of well-crowned Cytherea” (8.288), and Hephaestus shows the gods “where the two of them sleep in love” (8.313).
---

φιλότης is love as union, the word of the song's title and of each turn of its story (8.267, 8.271, 8.288, 8.313; see [[the-song-of-ares-and-aphrodite]], [[ares]] and [[aphrodite]]). ἰσχανόων is “eager for”, literally “clinging to” (W. W. Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 8.288). The husband's word for it is bitter: they sleep in love in his own bed, “and I am grieved to see it” (8.313–314; see [[achos]]), and his remedy is the gods' laughter and a fine (see [[evil-deeds-do-not-prosper]]).
