---
summary: Alcinous answers the stranger's boasts: he wishes “to show the excellence that attends you”, angry at the man who rebuked him, so that no mortal would make light of his excellence; and he asks him to tell the heroes at home of the Phaeacians, “remembering our excellence”, the deeds Zeus lays on them from their fathers' time (8.236–249).
---

**The stranger's excellence.** After the discus and the challenge, when all have fallen silent, Alcinous alone answers: “Stranger, since what you say among us is not ungracious, / but you wish to show the excellence that attends you, / angry because this man stood beside you in the gathering / and rebuked you, in such a way as no mortal would make light of your excellence / who knew in his mind how to speak fitting words” (8.236–240; see [[charis]] and [[euryalus-the-phaeacian]]). The excellence is the strength and skill the stranger has just shown and claimed: the discus thrown beyond all the marks, the bow, the spear (8.186–229; see [[the-games-of-the-phaeacians]]). Merry makes two clauses depend on ἐθέλεις, one giving the reason, the anger at Euryalus, and the other a consequence, “in such manner that no one would think slightingly of thee”, since the willingness had already passed into act (W. W. Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 8.237); the translation takes the words, with Bothe and Eustathius, of the manner of the rebuke (see the translation's note on 8.237–239).

**The Phaeacians' excellence.** The king asks in return that the stranger tell another of the heroes, when he feasts at home with his wife and children, of “our excellence, the kind of deeds that on us too / Zeus lays, continually, from the days of our fathers” (8.241–245; see [[memory-and-the-muses]]). It is not the excellence of fighters: “we are not flawless boxers, nor wrestlers, / but we run swiftly with our feet, and are the best with ships”, and dear to them are the feast, the lyre, the dances, changes of clothing, warm baths and beds (8.246–249; see [[different-gifts]] and [[phaeacians]]). Each people has its own excellence, given by Zeus, as each man has his own gifts.

**Prospering.** The verb of the same family, ἀρετάω, “prosper”, is used in the gods' saying in Demodocus' song: “Evil deeds do not prosper” (οὐκ ἀρετᾷ κακὰ ἔργα, 8.329; Merry on 8.329; see [[evil-deeds-do-not-prosper]]).
