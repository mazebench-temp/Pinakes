---
summary: “But the goddesses, being female, stayed each at home for shame” (8.324).
en: [being female]
---

Merry finds no quality such as “fair” or “tender” in θηλύτερος: it marks off one sex from the other with a naive emphasis, the ending -τερος suggesting the contrast, and means nothing but “female” (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 11.386, to which his note on 8.324 refers; see the Odyssey translation's key words, θηλύτεραι). Here the contrast is of goddesses with gods: the gods come to see and laugh, and the goddesses keep away (8.321–327; [[olympians]], [[aidos]]).
