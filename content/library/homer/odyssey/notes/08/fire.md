---
summary: The fire under the tripod heats the stranger's bath (8.426–437), and in Demodocus' last song the Argives set fire to their huts and sail away, leaving the best of them in the horse (8.500–503).
---

**The fire of the bath.** Alcinous bids his household warm a cauldron “on the fire” for the stranger, and the women set the tripod “on the blazing fire”, ἐν πυρὶ κηλέῳ, and “the fire played around the belly of the tripod” (8.426, 8.434–437, after *Iliad* 18.344–348; see [[tripod]], [[cauldron]], [[bathing]] and [[blazing-fire]]).

**The fire in the huts.** Demodocus begins the song of the horse “from where they had gone on board their well-benched ships / and were sailing away, having thrown fire on their huts, / the Argives” (8.500–502; see [[hut]], [[the-wooden-horse]] and [[the-fall-of-troy]]). They were sailing away, W. W. Merry notes, but only as far as Tenedos; the huts were of wood, as in the Iliad (*Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 8.501; *Iliad* 24.449–450). In the Iliad it is the Trojans who carry fire against the Achaeans' ships; here the Achaeans burn their own camp as they sail away, leaving the horse behind.
