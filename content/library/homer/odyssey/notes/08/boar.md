summary: Eight white-tusked hogs are among the victims of the feast that Alcinous gives in the morning (8.60), and at supper Odysseus cuts a portion of the chine of a white-tusked boar for Demodocus (8.474–476).
# ὕας (8.60; again 16.341, 17.604, Eumaeus' swine) and ὑός (8.476; again 14.423, 14.438), first met in the Odyssey
# here; in this poem both are always pigs. The Iliad's ὑὸς (10.264) is not reached by this note.

“And for them Alcinous sacrificed twelve sheep, / and eight white-tusked hogs, and two shambling cattle” (8.59–60; see [[sacrifice]], [[white-tusked]], [[sheep]] and [[cattle]]). At supper Odysseus speaks to the herald, “having cut off a portion of the chine (and yet more was left over) / of a white-tusked boar, and there was rich lard on either side”, and sends it to Demodocus (8.474–483; see [[demodocus]] and [[heralds]]). ὗς, “pig”, is the word of the Odyssey's tame swine as well as of the wild boar (see the entry).
