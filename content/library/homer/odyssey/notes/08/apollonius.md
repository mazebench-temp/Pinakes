---
summary: Merry sets beside Euryalus' wish that the storm winds carry off his harsh word a phrase of Apollonius, who gives a fault to the winds.
---

- **To the winds.** Euryalus makes amends with a sword and a prayer: if any word was spoken that was terrible, “may the storm winds at once snatch it up and carry it away” (8.408–409). Merry compares Apollonius' “let us give our fault to the winds” (*Argonautica* 1.1334, as Merry quotes it), with Horace's cares handed to the winds and a verse of Theocritus (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 8.409; the *Argonautica* is not read here): [[euryalus-the-phaeacian]] and [[horace]].
