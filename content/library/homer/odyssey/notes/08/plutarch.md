---
summary: Two passages of Plutarch stand in Merry's notes on this book, both given as he reports them: the Odysseus of Sophocles' Diners provoking Achilles, and the knot of Gordius, which Plutarch calls a δεσμός like the knot on Odysseus' chest.
---

- **Odysseus provokes Achilles.** For the quarrel of Demodocus' first song (8.75–82), Merry cites Plutarch's remark that the Odysseus of Sophocles, provoking Achilles, does not say that he is angry because of the dinner, but that now he sees the seats of Troy he is afraid (*Moralia* 74a, as Merry quotes it; Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 8.75): [[the-quarrel-of-odysseus-and-achilles]] and [[sophocles]].
- **The knot.** For δεσμός, the “knot” that Odysseus ties on his chest of gifts (8.443, 8.447), Merry notes that Plutarch always calls the knot of Gordius, which Alexander cut with his sword, a δεσμός (*Life of Alexander* 18, as Merry cites it; Merry on 8.443): [[chest]] and [[the-bonds-of-hephaestus]].
