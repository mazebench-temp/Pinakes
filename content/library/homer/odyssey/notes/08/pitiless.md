---
summary: In the simile of the third song the woman weeps over her husband, “who has fallen before his city and his people, / keeping off the pitiless day from his town and his children” (8.524–525).
---

ἀμύνων νηλεὲς ἦμαρ is the Iliad's half-verse of the fighter who wards off the day of death, from himself or from others (*Iliad* 11.484, 11.588, 13.514, 17.511); here the day comes, and the woman is led into slavery (8.526–530; [[pitiless-bronze]], [[odysseus-weeps-at-the-feast]]).
