**The spear cast.** Odysseus ends the boast of his skill at the bow with the spear: “And I throw the spear as far as no other man shoots an arrow” (8.229; see [[bow]] and [[arrows]]). Strabo quotes the line as proof that the speaker means the throwing spear, as against the thrusting spears with which the Euboeans fought hand to hand (*Geography* 10.1.13; see [[the-pelian-ash]] and [[strabo]]).

**The spears of the enemy.** Odysseus weeps at the song of the sack of Troy as a wife weeps over her husband, fallen before his city, “but they, behind her, / beating her back and shoulders with their spears, / lead her away into slavery” (8.527–529; see [[odysseus-weeps-at-the-feast]], [[the-fall-of-troy]] and the translation's note on 8.529).
