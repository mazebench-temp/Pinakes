---
summary: “And Pontonous set for him a silver-studded chair” (8.65), the singer's seat by the pillar; and Euryalus gives the stranger his sword, “So saying, he put the silver-studded sword in his hands” (8.406), which Odysseus slings around his shoulders (8.416).
---

The chair is the seat of honor of Book 7 (7.162) and of Thetis in Hephaestus' house (*Iliad* 18.389). The sword is a gift of reconciliation after a quarrel, as Hector gives Ajax “a silver-studded sword” after their duel, so that men may say that they fought and parted in friendship (*Iliad* 7.299–303; [[throne]], [[sword]], [[euryalus-the-phaeacian]], [[guest-gifts]]).
