---
summary: Alcinous sacrifices for the feast in his house: “twelve sheep, / and eight white-tusked hogs, and two shambling cattle; / these they flayed and dressed, and made ready a lovely feast” (8.59–61).
refs: [8.59-61]
---

The sacrifice is told in three verses, with no prayer, barley or burning of the thighs: the victims, the flaying, and the feast. The half-verse of the flaying, τοὺς δέρον ἀμφί θʼ ἕπον, is the one with which Agamemnon's ox is dressed after Ajax's duel with Hector, “They flayed it and dressed it” (*Iliad* 7.316), the feast at which Ajax is honored with the chine (*Iliad* 7.321; [[white-tusked]], [[feast]]).
