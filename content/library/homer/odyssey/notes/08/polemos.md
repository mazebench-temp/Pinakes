# πτολέμους (8.183; again 13.91, 13.264).
grc: [πτολέμους]
en: [wars]
---

ἀνδρῶν τε πτολέμους ἀλεγεινά τε κύματα πείρων, “passing through the wars of men and the grievous waves” (8.183): the participle belongs properly to the waves, as one “cuts through” the sea, and is extended by zeugma to the wars (W. W. Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 8.183). The verse sums up the stranger's life: the narrator says it of him as he sleeps on the Phaeacian ship (13.91), and he says it of himself again in the false tale he tells Athena on Ithaca (13.264; see [[algos]] and [[nothing-worse-than-the-sea]]). The war comes back in the third song, where he goes to the house of Deiphobus “like Ares” and dares the most dread fight (αἰνότατον πόλεμον, 8.517–520; see [[nike]] and [[the-fall-of-troy]]).
