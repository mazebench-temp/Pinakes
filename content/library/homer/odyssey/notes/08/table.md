---
summary: The herald sets a basket and a beautiful table beside the blind singer, and a cup of wine beside it, so that he may eat and drink whenever he wishes (8.69–70).
---

When Pontonous has seated Demodocus and hung his lyre above his head, “beside him he set a basket and a beautiful table, / and beside it a cup of wine, to drink whenever his heart bade him” (8.69–70; see [[pontonous]], [[demodocus]], [[basket]] and [[cup]]). The singer has his own table, as every guest of the Odyssey has one drawn up beside him (1.138; see the entry), set where a blind man can reach it, as the herald shows him where to take the lyre (8.68; see [[lyre]]). The meal follows at once, in the verse of the feast: “And they put out their hands to the good things lying ready before them” (8.71; see [[feast]] and [[feast-type-scene]]).
