summary: Three times “This the renowned singer sang; but Odysseus” (8.83, 8.367, 8.521), after each of the songs; Ares goes “to the house of renowned Hephaestus” (8.287); at sunset “the glorious gifts were there for him” (8.417); and in the song of the horse the Argives sit hidden “around famous Odysseus” (8.502).
grc: ["περικλυτοῦ Ἡφαίστοιο", "κλυτὰ δῶρα", ἀγακλυτὸν]
---

The verse of the singer is the Odyssey's for Phemius (1.325) and for Demodocus here, and each time it turns from the song to its hearer: Odysseus weeps after the first and the third, and after the second he is delighted with the Phaeacians (8.83–92, 8.367–369, 8.521–534; see the Odyssey translation's formulas). περικλυτός is also Hephaestus' title in the phrase of his lameness, which belongs to [[lame-in-both-legs]] (8.300, 8.349, 8.357), and the book has its compounds in κλυτοτέχνης and κλυτοεργός of the same god (8.286, 8.345; [[famed-for-his-craft]]) and ναυσίκλυτος of the Phaeacians (8.191, 8.369; [[famed-for-ships]], [[demodocus]], [[guest-gifts]]).
