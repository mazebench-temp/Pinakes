summary: In the song Ares goes “to the house of renowned Hephaestus, / eager for the love of well-crowned Cytherea,” finds her just come from her father and seated, “and he came into the house, / and he clung to her hand, and spoke to her, and called her by name” (8.287–291).
refs: [8.287-291]
---

The house is the one to which Thetis comes in *Iliad* 18, where Hephaestus' wife comes forward to meet the visitor, “and she clung to her hand, and spoke to her, and called her by name,” and the god himself greets her with the same verse (*Iliad* 18.381–385, 18.423). In the song the husband is away, the visitor takes the hand himself, and in place of a seat and a meal he offers the bed (8.292–296; see the general article; [[called-him-by-name]], [[the-song-of-ares-and-aphrodite]]).
