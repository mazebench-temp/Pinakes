---
summary: Merry, on Demodocus' blindness, is reminded of Milton's “blind Thamyris and blind Maeonides”.
---

- **Blind Thamyris and blind Maeonides.** On the Muse who robbed Demodocus of his eyes but gave him sweet song (8.63–64), Merry writes that “these words remind us of ‘blind Thamyris and blind Maeonides,’ and of our own poet who in these words parallels their lot with his own” (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 8.64). The poet is Milton, whom Merry does not name, setting his own blindness beside that of Thamyris and of Homer: [[demodocus-and-the-blind-homer]], [[thamyris]] and [[homer]].
