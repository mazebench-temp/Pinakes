summary: In Demodocus' song of the sack of Troy, Odysseus goes “like Ares” to the house of Deiphobus with Menelaus, and there dares the most dread fight and conquers through Athena (8.516–520).
# Δηιφόβοιο (8.517) is claimed by the entry's stem.
---

“He sang how each in his own place ravaged the steep city, / but how Odysseus went to the house of Deiphobus / like Ares, together with godlike Menelaus. / There, he said, he dared the most dread fight, / and conquered in the end, through great-hearted Athena” (8.516–520; see [[the-fall-of-troy]], [[menelaus]] and [[athena]]). Of all the houses of Troy the song names only this one, and the poem does not say why the two went there. Deiphobus has already been named once in the Odyssey at the end of Troy, following Helen when she walked around the horse and called the Achaean leaders by name (4.274–279; see [[helen]] and [[sinon-and-the-wooden-horse]]). In later legend Deiphobus married Helen after the death of Paris, and some ancient critics rejected 4.276, doubtless, W. W. Merry thinks, because it seemed to point to that story (*Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 4.276 and his critical note); the song says only that Menelaus was there with Odysseus, and that the fight was “the most dread” (8.519).

καὶ ἔπειτα, “in the end”, takes up the fight he dared: “did after all conquer”; διά with the accusative, “through great-hearted Athena”, gives the cause, and a scholium rationalizes the goddess into Odysseus' own intelligence and cunning (Merry on 8.520).
