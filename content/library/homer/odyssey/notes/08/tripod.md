---
summary: Arete bids her slave women set a great tripod over the fire to heat the stranger's bath, and the heating is told in the verses of the Iliad's washing of Patroclus' body (8.433–437, after Iliad 18.344–348).
passages:
  - 8.433-437 | The tripod for the bath
---

“So he spoke, and Arete told her slave women / to set a great tripod over the fire with all speed. / And they set the tripod for the bath on the blazing fire, / and poured water into it, and took wood and kindled it beneath. / The fire played around the belly of the tripod, and the water grew warm” (8.433–437; see [[arete-queen-of-the-phaeacians]], [[handmaids]], [[bathing]] and [[fire]]). The verses are those with which Achilles' companions heat water to wash the blood from Patroclus' body (*Iliad* 18.344–348), with the slave women in place of the men; the bath of the dead and the bath of the guest are heated alike. λοετροχόος is “for filling the bath” (W. W. Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 8.435), and the tripod is stand and cauldron in one, as γάστρη, “the belly”, shows (Merry on 8.426; see [[cauldron]]).
