---
summary: The stranger's last words to Nausicaa: if Zeus grants him his homecoming, “then even there I would pray to you as to a god / always, all my days” (8.467–468).
---

εὐχετάομαι is the frequentative of εὔχομαι, and the optative with κε makes it a promise for a whole life: “then even there I would pray to you as to a god / always, all my days; for you gave me my life, maiden” (8.467–468; see [[zoagria]] and [[nausicaa]]). τῷ is “in that case”, should he come home (W. W. Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 8.467). To pray to a mortal as to a god is the honor of a saviour: the Trojans “prayed as to a god” to Hector (*Iliad* 22.394), and the Pylians thanked Zeus among the gods and Nestor among men (*Iliad* 11.761). Telemachus will say the same two verses to Helen when she reads an omen of his father's return (15.180–181 = 8.465, 8.467). The prayer is for his homecoming, “so may Zeus now grant it” (8.465; see [[prayer]] and [[nostos]]).
