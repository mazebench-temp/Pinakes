---
summary: The first of Demodocus' three songs, sung at the morning feast in the house of Alcinous and told in ten verses of reported speech (8.72–82). Odysseus weeps at it, and in the evening asks the singer to change his theme to the wooden horse (8.83–92, 8.492).
# Unnamed: the song (8.73-82); "change your theme", from the first lay, as Merry takes it (8.492).
refs: [8.73-82, 8.492]
passages:
  - 8.72-82 | The first song of Demodocus
---

**The lay.** μοῦσʼ ἄρʼ ἀοιδὸν ἀνῆκεν ἀειδέμεναι κλέα ἀνδρῶν, “the Muse set the singer on to sing the famous deeds of men” (8.73; see [[muses]]); a variant has ἐνῆκεν (see the translation's note on 8.73). οἴμη is the “strain”, literally the “way” of song (8.74; W. W. Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 8.74; see [[oime]]), and its fame “had at that time reached the wide sky” (see [[wide-sky]]). νεῖκος, “the quarrel”, stands in apposition with κλέα, as the particular deed sung (Merry on 8.75; see [[neikos]]).

**The words of the quarrel.** ὥς ποτε δηρίσαντο, “how once they contended” (8.76), at θεῶν ἐν δαιτὶ θαλείῃ, “a rich feast of the gods” (see [[rich-feast]]). ἐκπάγλοις ἐπέεσσιν, “with terrible words”, were words whose fierceness dismayed the bystanders, “but Agamemnon rejoiced” (8.77; Merry on 8.77). χαῖρε νόῳ is “rejoiced in his mind”, that is, secretly (8.78; Merry on 8.78). The text prints ὅ τʼ, “that”, with La Roche, where Merry prints ὅτʼ, “when” (see the translation's note on 8.78). ἄριστοι Ἀχαιῶν, “the best of the Achaeans”, is plural: both men are the best (see [[achilles]] and [[odysseus]]).

**The oracle.** χρείων, “giving response”, is the word of an oracle, as in Apollo's “I will proclaim to humankind the unerring will of Zeus” (8.79; *Hymn* 3.132; Merry on 8.79; see [[apollo]] and [[phoebus]]). Πυθοῖ ἐν ἠγαθέῃ, “in sacred Pytho” (see [[pytho]] and [[egatheos]]), is the place's oldest name, which the Hymn to Apollo derives from the rotting of the serpent's body there (Merry on 8.80), and λάινον οὐδόν, “the stone threshold”, is the threshold of the temple, as in Achilles' “all that is shut within the stone threshold of the Archer, / Phoebus Apollo, in rocky Pytho” (*Iliad* 9.404–405; Merry on 8.80; see [[threshold]] and [[aphetor]]). τότε, “then”, is the time of the consultation: the war was just beginning, and κυλίνδετο πήματος ἀρχή, “the beginning of bane was rolling”, is a wave of evils just rolling on, soon to sweep many lives away (8.81; Merry on 8.81; see [[the-beginning-of-evils]]). Διὸς μεγάλου διὰ βουλάς, “through the counsels of great Zeus” (8.82; see [[plan-of-zeus]] and [[boule]]). The scholia report that the last two verses were missing in some editions and were athetized for that reason; Merry finds the notice incomplete, since it gives no sufficient reason for rejecting them (Merry's critical note on 8.81–82; see the translation's note on 8.74–82).

**The singer's close.** ταῦτʼ ἄρʼ ἀοιδὸς ἄειδε περικλυτός, “This the renowned singer sang” (8.83), closes all three songs (8.367, 8.521), and the words ἀοιδὸς ἄειδε περικλυτός bring in Phemius' song of the returns in Ithaca (1.325; see [[the-song-of-the-returns]]). Here what follows it is Odysseus' mantle drawn over his head (8.83–86; see [[odysseus-weeps-at-the-feast]]).

**Change your theme.** “Demodocus, I praise you far above all mortals”, Odysseus says at supper: the Muse or Apollo has taught him, “for all too well, in due order, you sing the fate of the Achaeans, / all that they did and suffered, and all that the Achaeans toiled through, / as if perhaps you had been present yourself, or heard it from another” (8.487–491; see [[demodocus]] and [[we-hear-only-the-report]]). μετάβηθι, “change your theme” (8.492), turns from the lay of the morning, which he has just praised, and not from the song of Ares and Aphrodite (Merry on 8.492; see the translation's note on 8.492). He praises a song about himself without saying so, and asks for another.
