---
summary: Odysseus praises the Phaeacian dancers: “you boasted that your dancers were the best, / and indeed it has proved true; awe holds me as I look” (8.383–384).
---

The verse-end σέβας μʼ ἔχει εἰσορόωντα, “awe holds me as I look”, is the Odyssey's word for the wonder at a sight beyond the ordinary: at Telemachus' likeness to his father, at Menelaus' palace, at Nausicaa (3.123, 4.75, 4.142, 6.161; see the translation's table of key words and [[awe-holds-me]]). Here it is said of the ball dance of Halius and Laodamas and the beat of the young men's feet (8.370–380; see [[dance]] and [[ball]]). ἀπείλησας is “you boasted”, as a scholium explains it, and ἑτοῖμα τέτυκτο, “and lo! thy words are fulfilled” (W. W. Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 8.383, 8.384). The king rejoices, and proposes the guest-gift (8.385–389; see [[guest-gifts]]).
