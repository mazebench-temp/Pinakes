summary: Alcinous and his sons are “noble”: Euryalus is the best in looks “after noble Laodamas” (8.117), “And up stood the three sons of noble Alcinous” (8.118), “noble Clytoneus was by far the best at running” (8.123), and “the sons of noble Alcinous” receive the gifts (8.419); and Alcinous says of his people, “For we are not flawless boxers, nor wrestlers” (8.246), and calls them “the noble Phaeacians” (8.428).
grc: [ἀμύμονες]
---

ἀμύμων of persons keeps the Iliad's “noble”; of men at a skill it is “flawless,” as ἀμύμονος ὀρχηθμοῖο, “flawless dancing” (*Iliad* 13.637; see the Odyssey translation's key words, ἀμύμων). In this sense Alcinous' praise of his people begins with a denial: not the best boxers and wrestlers, but swift runners and the best with ships (8.246–247; [[different-gifts]], [[phaeacians]], [[alcinous]]).
