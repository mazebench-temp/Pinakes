summary: Demodocus' song of the horse tells what Menelaus told at Sparta and ends with two of his verses, “the great wooden horse, where they were sitting, all the best / of the Argives, bringing slaughter and doom to the Trojans” (8.512–513, after 4.272–273); and Alcinous tells again the prophecy he heard from his father Nausithous (8.564–569).
refs: [8.499-520, 8.564-571]
---

Menelaus told of Odysseus' endurance inside the horse, when Helen came and called the chieftains in their wives' voices (4.266–289); the singer tells the Argives' departure, the Trojans' counsels, the sack and Odysseus' fight at the house of Deiphobus, and Odysseus himself will tell Achilles' shade of his son's courage in the horse (11.523–532). Alcinous gives his father's words in reported speech and leaves their fulfillment to the god (8.570–571); when the ship is turned to stone he says them again, with περικαλλέα for ἐυεργέα, and ends that all is now being fulfilled (13.172–178; [[sinon-and-the-wooden-horse]], [[the-migration-of-the-phaeacians]], [[so-he-spoke]]).
