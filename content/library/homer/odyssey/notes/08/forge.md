# χαλκεῶνα, "smithy" (8.273), found only here in Homer.
grc: [χαλκεῶνα]
en: [smithy]
---

“And when Hephaestus heard the heart-grieving tale, / he went to his smithy, brooding evil deep in his mind, / and set the great anvil on the anvil block, and forged bonds / unbreakable, not to be loosed, so that they might stay there steadfast” (8.272–275; see [[hephaestus]], [[heart-grieving]] and [[the-song-of-ares-and-aphrodite]]). χαλκεών, the bronze-smith's workshop, is named only here in Homer. κόπτε δὲ δεσμούς, “and forged bonds”, ends a verse of the Iliad too, where Hephaestus, at work in his house when Thetis comes, is “forging the rivets” for the handles of his self-moving tripods (*Iliad* 18.379; see [[the-bonds-of-hephaestus]]). The same tools, anvil, hammer and tongs, are the goldsmith's at Pylos (3.432–435; see the Book 3 note).
