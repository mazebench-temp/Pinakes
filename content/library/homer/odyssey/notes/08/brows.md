---
summary: Odysseus lets tears fall “from beneath his brows” at Demodocus' first song and again at the third (8.86, 8.531).
---

At the quarrel of Odysseus and Achilles “he was ashamed before the Phaeacians, letting tears fall from beneath his brows” (8.86), and at the song of the horse “Odysseus let a pitiful tear fall from beneath his brows” (8.531; see [[tears]] and [[odysseus-weeps-at-the-feast]]). The second verse is the son's at Sparta, with ἐλεεινόν, “pitiful”, for πικρόν, “bitter” (4.153; see the Book 4 note and the translation's row ἐλεεινὸν ὑπʼ ὀφρύσι δάκρυον εἶβεν); father and son weep alike at the story of the father.
