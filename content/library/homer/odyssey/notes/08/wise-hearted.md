---
summary: δαΐφρων is the book's epithet of Alcinous, “the herald of wise-hearted Alcinous” (8.8) and “the house of wise-hearted Alcinous” (8.13, 8.56); of Odysseus, “the wise-hearted son of Laertes” (8.18); and of the craftsman Polybus, who made the purple ball (8.373).
---

The translation takes the word from δαῆναι, “learn,” not from δαΐς, “battle” (see the translation's note on 1.48). The maker of the ball is praised by it for his skill, “a purple one, which wise-hearted Polybus had made for them” (8.373; [[alcinous]], [[odysseus]], [[ball]]).
