---
summary: Odysseus cuts a portion of the chine “of a white-tusked boar, and there was rich lard on either side” (8.476).
en: [rich lard]
---

Merry takes θαλερὴ … ἀλοιφή as showing the excellence of the chine in fatness, as the clause before it shows its size (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 8.475). The Iliad's swine for Patroclus' funeral feast are “rich with lard,” θαλέθοντες ἀλοιφῇ, with the verb of the same root (*Iliad* 23.32; [[white-tusked]], [[demodocus]]).
