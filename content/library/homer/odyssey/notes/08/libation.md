# σπείσασκε (8.89), σπένδῃ (8.432).
grc: [σπείσασκε, σπένδῃ]
en: [pour libations]
---

The libations of the book are a guest's. While Demodocus sings of the quarrel, the stranger hides his face and weeps; “Each time the divine singer stopped singing, / he would wipe away his tears and take the mantle from his head, / and take the two-handled cup and pour libations to the gods” (8.87–89; see [[tears]] and [[cup]]). The iterative σπείσασκε marks a libation at every pause, a thank-offering to the gods in remembrance of their constant care of him (W. W. Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 8.87; see the translation's note on 8.87–92). The king's last gift makes the libation a memorial: his own golden goblet, so that the stranger may pour libations at home to Zeus and the other gods, remembering him (8.430–432; see [[guest-gifts]], [[memory-and-the-muses]] and [[prayer]]).
