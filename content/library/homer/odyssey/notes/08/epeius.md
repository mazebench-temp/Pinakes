---
summary: Odysseus asks Demodocus to sing of the wooden horse “which Epeius made with Athena's help”, and which he himself brought into the citadel of Troy as a snare (8.492–495).
---

“sing of the fashioning of the horse / of wood, which Epeius made with Athena's help, / which once brilliant Odysseus brought up into the citadel as a snare” (8.492–494; see [[the-wooden-horse]], [[sinon-and-the-wooden-horse]] and [[athena]]). Epeius is named only as the maker, and the verses divide the work: the making is his, with the goddess, the bringing in is Odysseus'. A scholium draws the moral that Odysseus too is a sacker of cities, since the device was Epeius' and Athena's and the undertaking Odysseus' (W. W. Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 8.494; see [[sacker-of-cities]]).

The Iliad knows him only as an athlete, the boxer who owns that he falls short in battle and wins the boxing at the funeral games of Patroclus, and who throws the lump of iron so that all the Achaeans laugh (*Iliad* 23.664–699, 23.838–840). The Odyssey names him only as the maker of the horse, here and when Odysseus tells the shade of Achilles of the day the best of the Argives went down into it (11.523, Greek only).
