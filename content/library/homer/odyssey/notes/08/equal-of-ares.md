---
summary: “and up rose Euryalus too, the equal of Ares, ruin of mortals” (8.115).
grc: ["ἶσος Ἄρηϊ"]
---

The likeness is the Iliad's for Hector and for Patroclus (*Iliad* 11.295, 11.604); here it is given to a young man at the games. At the end of the book the singer gives Odysseus the god's likeness itself, “like Ares,” as he goes with Menelaus to the house of Deiphobus (8.517–518; [[ruin-of-mortals]], [[ares-and-rout]], [[euryalus-the-phaeacian]]).
