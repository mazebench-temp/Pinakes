---
summary: Odysseus boasts that he would be the first to shoot and strike his man in a throng of enemies, and that he throws the spear as far as no other man shoots an arrow (8.216, 8.229).
# ὀιστεύσας "shoot" (8.216; later 12.84) and ὀιστῷ "an arrow" (8.229), the Iliad's ὀϊστεύσας and ὀϊστῷ (in its notes),
# printed without the diaeresis; first met in the Odyssey here.
grc: [ὀιστεύσας, ὀιστῷ]
---

In his challenge Odysseus claims the archer's first shot: “I would be the first to shoot and strike my man in a throng / of hostile men” (8.216–217; see [[bow]] and [[hostile-men]]), and ends with a measure that sets the spear against the arrow, “And I throw the spear as far as no other man shoots an arrow” (8.229; see [[spear]]). ἀκοντίζω, “throw the spear”, is extended to ὀιστῷ by zeugma (W. W. Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 8.229).
