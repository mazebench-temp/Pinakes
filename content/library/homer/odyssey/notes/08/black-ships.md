---
summary: Alcinous: “But come, let us drag a black ship down to the shining sea / on her first voyage” (8.34–35); “they dragged the black ship down into deep water, / and set the mast and the sails in the black ship” (8.51–52); and Arete bids the stranger tie a knot on his chest so that no one may harm it “when hereafter / you sleep sweet sleep as you go in the black ship” (8.444–445).
---

8.34 is Agamemnon's verse for the ship that takes Chryseis home (*Iliad* 1.141), with a new word in the next line, πρωτόπλοον, “on her first voyage,” found only here in Homer; 8.52 is the verse of the suitors' ship (4.781; [[sailing-type-scene]], [[ships]]).
