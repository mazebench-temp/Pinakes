# Unnamed: "but all that time his keep had been steady, as for a god" (8.453).
refs: [8.453]
---

**Cared for as a god.** The housekeeper bids him bathe, “and he was glad in his heart to see / the warm bath, since he had not often been cared for so / since he left the house of Calypso of the lovely hair; / but all that time his keep had been steady, as for a god” (8.450–453; see [[bathing]] and [[lovely-haired]]). ἠύκομος, “of the lovely hair”, is her epithet again when Odysseus says that he heard from her what the Sun and Zeus had said of his companions (12.389–390; see the general article). τόφρα δέ is “but all that time”, the whole of his stay with Calypso, and θεῷ ὥς accords with the way she had treated him, since she promised to make him immortal and ageless all his days (5.135–136; see [[immortal-and-ageless]]); for the uninterrupted comfort of the gods Merry compares Alcinous in his chair, who “sits and drinks his wine like an immortal” (6.309), and he notes that a scholium makes the same reference to Calypso's promise. ἔμπεδος is “constant”, as we say “regular” (W. W. Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 8.452–453). κομιδή, “keep”, is the word of the voyage in Odysseus' answer to Euryalus: “since on board the ship my keep was not / unfailing” (8.232–233; see the translation's key-word row). The last bath she gave him was on the day of his going, when she bathed him and clothed him in fragrant garments (5.264).
