---
summary: γάστρη is the belly of the tripod in which the water for the stranger's bath is heated: “The fire played around the belly of the tripod, and the water grew warm” (8.437 = *Iliad* 18.348).
---

The bath is prepared in the verses with which the Iliad heats the water to wash the body of Patroclus (8.434–437, after *Iliad* 18.344–348; see [[bathing]] and [[tripod]]). γάστρη, the swelling belly of a vessel, is a word of the γαστήρ family (see the entry). The bath is the first warmth the stranger has had since he left Calypso's house (8.450–453).
