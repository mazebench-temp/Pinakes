---
summary: Athena and Alcinous call the Phaeacian lords “leaders and rulers of the Phaeacians” (8.11, 8.26, 8.97, 8.387, 8.536), and twelve kings “rule as leaders” with Alcinous the thirteenth (8.391); Euryalus likens the stranger to “a leader of sailors who are traders” (8.162).
---

The address κέκλυτε, Φαιήκων ἡγήτορες ἠδὲ μέδοντες, “Hear me, leaders and rulers of the Phaeacians”, opens four of Alcinous' speeches to his lords (8.26, 8.97, 8.387, 8.536; see [[hear-me]] and [[phaeacians]]), and Athena, in the herald's likeness, summons them by the same title (8.11). The lords are twelve kings, “distinguished kings throughout the land” who “rule as leaders” (ἀρχοὶ κραίνουσι), with Alcinous the thirteenth (8.390–391; W. W. Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 8.390; see [[kings]] and [[basileus]]). The word ἀρχός is turned to insult by Euryalus, who likens the stranger to “a leader of sailors who are traders” (ἀρχὸς ναυτάων, 8.162; see [[trade]]).
