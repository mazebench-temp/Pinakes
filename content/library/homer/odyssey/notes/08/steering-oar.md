---
summary: Alcinous tells the stranger that the Phaeacians have no helmsmen and their ships no steering oars, such as other ships have, since the ships themselves know the thoughts and minds of men and the cities of all men (8.555–563).
---

Alcinous asks the stranger his land, “so that our ships may carry you there, aiming with their minds; / for the Phaeacians have no helmsmen, / nor any steering oars, such as other ships have; / but the ships themselves know the thoughts and minds of men” (8.556–559; see [[ships]], [[phaeacians]] and [[alcinous]]). κυβερνητῆρες and πηδάλια are “helmsmen” and “steering oars”, as πηδάλιον is the steering oar in Nestor's tale of Phrontis (see the translation's row κυβερνητῆρες, πηδάλια; 3.281). τιτυσκόμεναι is “aiming at it with their minds”, the verb of a man aiming a spear or a cast, and the ships, W. W. Merry writes, are endued with human sense, anticipating the will of their masters (*Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 8.556; see the translation's note on 8.556–563). The stranger himself had made a steering oar for his raft and steered with it, until the storm threw him from the raft and he let it go from his hands (5.255, 5.270, 5.315–316; see [[raft]]).
