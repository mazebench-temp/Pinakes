Alcinous gives the orders in the steps of the pattern: the ship dragged down to the sea, the crew chosen, the oars made fast at the rowing benches (8.34–37). The launching is told in the verses of Telemachus' voyage and of the suitors' ship: 8.50 = 2.407, and 8.51–55 follow 4.780–785, where the suitors fit out their ship for the ambush (see the Book 4 note). The young men then step out again and leave the ship at her mooring, ready for the voyage (8.38; Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 8.38; see the translation's note on 8.38). It sails in Book 13, from the same verse, αὐτὰρ ἐπεί ῥʼ ἐπὶ νῆα κατήλυθον ἠδὲ θάλασσαν (13.70), when the gifts are stowed and the rowers loose the cable from the pierced stone (13.70–78; [[ships]], [[mast]], [[sails]], [[oars]], [[rowing-benches]], [[pompe]]).
