# πηός (8.581; πηῷ again 10.441) and πενθερός (8.582; the Iliad's πενθερῷ, 6.170, is in its Book 6 note).
grc: [πηὸς, πενθερός]
en: [kinsman of yours by marriage]
refs: [8.583]
---

The king guesses at the reason for the stranger's tears in the order of a man's attachments, and kin by marriage come first: “Did some kinsman of yours by marriage perish before Ilios, / a good man, a son-in-law or a father-in-law, who are / the dearest after one's own blood and kin?” (8.581–583; see [[genee]]). πηός seems limited to connections by marriage, who are nearest to a man after his own blood relations; γαμβρός goes with γαμεῖν, “marry”, and πενθερός perhaps with a root meaning “bind” (W. W. Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 8.581, 8.582; see the translation's table of key words). αὐτῶν, “their own”, refers generally to all who know the difference between connections and relations (Merry on 8.583). κήδιστοι, “dearest”, is the word with which Ajax tells Achilles that his comrades are eager “to be the nearest and dearest to you, of all the Achaeans” (*Iliad* 9.641–642). Alcinous himself had wished in Book 7 that the stranger might stay and be his son-in-law (7.311–315; see [[nausicaas-wish]]). The second guess, a companion, is the subject of the maxim that ends the book (8.584–586; see [[a-companion-no-worse-than-a-brother]] and [[hetairos]]).
