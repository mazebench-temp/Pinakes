---
summary: A tunic goes with the freshly washed mantle and the talent of gold that each of the thirteen kings gives the stranger (8.392, 8.425, 8.441), and after his bath the slave women put a fine cloak and a tunic around him (8.455).
---

Alcinous bids each king bring “a freshly washed mantle and a tunic / and a talent of precious gold” (8.392–393; see [[cloak]], [[talent]], [[kings]] and [[guest-gifts]]), and Arete puts her own mantle and tunic in the chest with the others' gifts (8.425, 8.441; see [[chest]] and [[arete-queen-of-the-phaeacians]]). After the bath “the slave women had washed him and anointed him with oil, / and put a fine cloak and a tunic around him” (8.454–455; see [[bathing]]). The pair, mantle or cloak and tunic, is the whole dress of a man in the Odyssey, the gift of a host to a guest from Nausicaa's river onward (6.214; see the entry).
