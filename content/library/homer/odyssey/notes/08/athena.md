---
summary: Athena prepares Odysseus' day among the Phaeacians. In the likeness of Alcinous' herald she goes through the city calling the leaders to the place of assembly to see the stranger, and pours a wondrous grace on him, making him taller and stouter; at the games, in form like a man, she marks the fall of his discus and bids him take heart. In Demodocus' third song she is the helper with whom Epeius made the wooden horse, and through her Odysseus conquered at the house of Deiphobus.

**In the likeness of the herald.** The day begins with Alcinous leading the Phaeacians to the place of assembly by the ships; while they sit there, the goddess goes through the city:

{{quote:dc1028f9-5c33-5b8e-bbbd-4c9b3e9dac44}}

In the Iliad she took the same disguise for the same man: when Odysseus rose in the assembly with the scepter, “beside him gray-eyed Athena, / in the likeness of a herald, ordered the people to be silent” (*Iliad* 2.279–280), εἰδομένη κήρυκι, the words of 8.8, which are found nowhere else in Homer (see [[heralds]] and [[assembly]]). “devising a homecoming for great-hearted Odysseus” (8.9) is the verse with which, in Book 6, she went to the house of Alcinous to send Nausicaa her dream (6.14; see [[nostos]] and [[great-hearted]]). Her summons calls the stranger “like the immortals in form” (8.14; see [[like-the-immortals]]), and the verse that closes it, “So saying, she urged on the might and spirit of every man” (8.15), is a formula of the Iliad, where it comes ten times, found only here in the Odyssey (W. W. Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 8.15, after Ameis; see the translation's note on 8.15).

**The grace.** When the places of assembly are full, “many gazed when they saw / the wise-hearted son of Laertes; for upon him Athena / poured a wondrous grace, upon his head and shoulders, / and made him taller and stouter to look upon, / so that he might be dear to all the Phaeacians, / and feared and respected, and might accomplish the many contests / in which the Phaeacians made trial of Odysseus” (8.17–23; see [[gold-poured-around-silver]], [[charis]] and [[wondrous]]). She has done it for him before, after his bath in the river (6.229–235), and will again after his bath in his own house (23.156–162); in the same words she makes Penelope taller and stouter for the suitors (18.195) and Laertes after his bath (24.369), and pours a wondrous grace on Telemachus, so that all the people gaze at him as he comes (2.12–13, 17.63–64). Zenodotus athetized 8.23, since Odysseus did not accomplish many contests in Phaeacia but only threw the discus; Merry, after Eustathius, takes the one contest as his discharge in full of all those to which he might else have been challenged, “and for all these the care of Athena qualified him” (on 8.22; see the translation's note on 8.22–23).

**The marker.** Odysseus' discus flies beyond the marks of all, “And Athena set the mark, / in form like a man, and spoke to him, and called him by name” (8.193–194; see [[discus]] and [[the-games-of-the-phaeacians]]):

{{quote:70d4b14b-380f-5182-994e-acf992da2507}}

Merry explains ἔθηκε by the Iliad's σήμηνε δὲ τέρματʼ Ἀχιλλεύς (*Iliad* 23.358): the τέρματα are the distances at which each discus fell, and the σήματα the marks that register them (on 8.193; see the translation's note on 8.192–193). ἀνδρὶ δέμας ἐικυῖα, “in form like a man”, is said of her again on Ithaca, where she comes to Odysseus in the form of a young man, a herdsman of sheep (13.221–222). “So she spoke, and much-enduring brilliant Odysseus was glad, / rejoicing that he saw a gentle companion in the gathering” (8.199–200): ἐνηής is always used of close friends, as of Patroclus, “his companion, gentle and strong” (Merry on 8.200; *Iliad* 17.204). The poem does not say that Odysseus knew her. On Ithaca, when she has made herself known, he tells her that after Troy he never saw her until in the rich land of the Phaeacians she encouraged him with words and led him into the city herself (13.316–323); her words to him in this book bid him take heart, θάρσει (8.197; see [[tharsos]]).

**In the third song.** Odysseus asks Demodocus to sing “of the fashioning of the horse / of wood, which Epeius made with Athena's help” (8.492–493; see [[epeius]], [[sinon-and-the-wooden-horse]] and [[the-fall-of-troy]]). Merry quotes the scholium on the next verse: the construction was the work of Epeius and Athena, the enterprise that of Odysseus, and so he is the sacker of cities (on 8.494; see [[sacker-of-cities]]). The song ends with Odysseus at the house of Deiphobus, who “dared the most dread fight, / and conquered in the end, through great-hearted Athena” (8.519–520; see [[great-hearted]]). μεγάθυμος is said of her only here and in Book 13, where the Phaeacians set down on Ithaca the gifts they gave Odysseus “through great-hearted Athena” (13.121); Ἀθήνην at 8.520 is the goddess, not the city of 7.80.

**Her names.** Παλλὰς Ἀθήνη (8.7); Ἀθήνη (8.18, 8.193); σὺν Ἀθήνῃ, “with Athena's help” (8.493); μεγάθυμον Ἀθήνην (8.520). See [[pallas]].
