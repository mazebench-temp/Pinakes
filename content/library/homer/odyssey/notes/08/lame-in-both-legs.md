---
summary: “And the renowned god, lame in both legs, came near them” (8.300), and he answers Poseidon twice: “Then the renowned god, lame in both legs, spoke to him in turn” (8.349) and “answered him” (8.357).
---

Merry gives both senses of ἀμφιγυήεις, “lame of both feet” and “of the two stout arms,” and notes that the word is generally found with κλυτός or περικλυτός (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 8.300; see the translation's note on 8.300). The song makes the lameness its point: Aphrodite dishonors him “because I am lame” and loves Ares “because he is handsome and sound of foot” (8.308–310), and the gods' verdict is that the slow has caught the swift, “lame as he is, by his craft” (8.329–332). 8.357 is the Iliad's verse for his answers to Thetis, with “her” (*Iliad* 18.393, 18.462; [[hephaestus]], [[famed-for-his-craft]], [[litai]]).
