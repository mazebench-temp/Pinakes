---
summary: Hephaestus will hold the lovers in the bonds until her father gives back the bride-price, “all that I put into his hands for the sake of the dog-faced girl” (8.319).
---

In the Iliad Hephaestus uses the word of his mother, “my dog-faced mother, who wished / to hide me away because I was lame” (*Iliad* 18.396–397); here it is his wife, who scorns him for the same lameness (8.308–311). Helen used it of herself before Menelaus (4.145; [[aphrodite]], [[bride-price]], [[hephaestus]]).
