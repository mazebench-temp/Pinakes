---
summary: The Argives are the army of the song of the wooden horse, those who burned their huts and sailed away (8.500–502) and “all the best / of the Argives” hidden in the horse (8.512–513); and Alcinous asks why the stranger weeps at “the fate of the Argive Danaans” (8.578).
---

**The army divided.** Demodocus takes up his song “from where they had gone on board their well-benched ships / and were sailing away, having thrown fire on their huts, / the Argives”, while the others sat around Odysseus in the place of assembly of the Trojans, hidden in the horse (8.500–503; see [[well-benched]], [[hut]] and [[sinon-and-the-wooden-horse]]). ἀπέπλειον, “they were sailing away”, but only as far as Tenedos; the huts were of wood, as in the Iliad, canvas tents being a later invention (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 8.501; see [[tenedos]]). The men in the horse are “all the best / of the Argives, bringing slaughter and doom to the Trojans” (8.512–513), the words of Menelaus' story of the horse in the fourth book (4.272–273; see [[trojans]]).

**“The Argive Danaans.”** Alcinous asks the stranger why he weeps “as you hear the fate of the Argive Danaans and of Ilios” (8.578; see [[danaans]] and [[troy]]). Ἀργείων Δαναῶν joins two of the army's three names. Merry takes Ἀργείων as an epithet referring to the home of the Danaans, as in Ἀργείη Ἑλένη, “Argive Helen” (4.184; see [[argive-helen]] and [[argos]]); Gladstone would make Δαναοί the epithet and Ἀργεῖοι the noun, and Bekker preferred the reading ἡρώων Δαναῶν, “of the Danaan heroes” (Merry on 8.578). The translation follows Merry.
