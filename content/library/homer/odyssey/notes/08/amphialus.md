---
summary: He rises for the games, named with his father and grandfather (8.114), and wins the jumping, “the foremost of all” (8.128).
---

**The jumping.** “In jumping Amphialus was the foremost of all” (8.128; see [[the-games-of-the-phaeacians]]). ἅλμα, “jumping”, is one of the four contests that Alcinous names when he proposes the games, “in boxing and in wrestling and in jumping and in running” (8.103; see the translation's table of key words), and the jump is told in a single verse, as are the discus and the boxing after it (8.128–130; see [[elatreus]] and [[laodamas-son-of-alcinous]]). The funeral games of the Iliad have no jumping (*Iliad* 23.257–897; see [[the-funeral-games-of-patroclus]]), and when Odysseus challenges the Phaeacians he offers them boxing, wrestling or running, not the jump (8.206).

**His family.** Of the twelve young men named first, he alone is given a father and a grandfather: “and Amphialus, son of Polyneus, the son of Tecton” (8.114; see [[polyneus]] and [[tecton]]).
