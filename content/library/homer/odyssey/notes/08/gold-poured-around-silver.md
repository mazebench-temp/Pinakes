**The grace before the games.**

{{quote:a458fcb4-b12f-5cb6-b6e4-34c7954eb8a0}}

The work is the work of Book 6 in a shorter form and another order: there Athena first made Odysseus taller and stouter and sent down his thick hair like the hyacinth, and then poured grace on his head and shoulders as a craftsman pours gold around silver (6.229–235); here she pours the grace first, θεσπεσίην κατέχευε χάριν κεφαλῇ τε καὶ ὤμοις, “poured a wondrous grace, upon his head and shoulders” (8.19; see [[charis]] and [[wondrous]]), and then makes him “taller and stouter to look upon” (8.20), with no simile and no hyacinth (see [[athena]] and [[odysseus]]). The verse of stature returns twice in later books, of Penelope, whom Athena makes taller and stouter before she shows herself to the suitors (18.195), and of the old Laertes after his bath (24.369).

The purpose differs too. In Book 6 the grace was for Nausicaa's eyes (6.236–245; see [[nausicaa]]); here it is for a people: “so that he might be dear to all the Phaeacians, / and feared and respected, and might accomplish the many contests” (8.21–22; see [[phaeacians]], [[aidos]] and [[the-games-of-the-phaeacians]]). ὥς κεν … γένοιτο suggests not only the purpose but the accomplishment of the result (W. W. Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 8.21), and the gathering sees it at once: “many gazed when they saw / the wise-hearted son of Laertes” (8.17–18; see [[son-of-laertes]] and [[wise-hearted]]). On the “many contests” and Zenodotus' athetesis of 8.23, see the translation's note on 8.22–23.
