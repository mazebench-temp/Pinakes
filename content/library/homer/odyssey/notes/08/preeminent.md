summary: Odysseus to Euryalus: “so with you too, your looks are outstanding” (8.176); Alcinous names “twelve distinguished kings throughout the land” (8.390) and bids Arete bring “a splendid chest, the best there is” (8.424); and the stranger and Euryalus address the king as “Powerful Alcinous, preeminent among all the people” (8.382, 8.401).
grc: [ἀριπρεπές, ἀριπρεπέες, ἀριπρεπέʼ, "πάντων ἀριδείκετε λαῶν"]
en: [outstanding, distinguished, splendid]
---

The book has ἀριπρεπής in three senses, of looks, of rank and of a thing, and the translation gives each its own word (see the Odyssey translation's key words, ἀριπρεπής). “Preeminent” in the address is ἀριδείκετος, the word of Coon and of Perseus in the Iliad (*Iliad* 11.248, 14.320); the whole verse comes four times more in Books 9–13, the first time to open the stranger's tale (9.2, 11.355, 11.378, 13.38; see the Odyssey translation's formulas; [[powerful]], [[alcinous]], [[kings]], [[chest]]).
