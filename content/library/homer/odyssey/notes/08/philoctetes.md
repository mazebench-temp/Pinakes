---
summary: In his challenge to the Phaeacians Odysseus names Philoctetes as the one archer who surpassed him at Troy (8.219–220).
---

“Philoctetes alone surpassed me with the bow / in the land of the Trojans, when we Achaeans shot with the bow” (8.219–220; see [[odysseus]] and [[the-games-of-the-phaeacians]]). Odysseus is boasting of his archery, a skill that is not among the Phaeacians' contests, and the boast has its measure: he would be the first to strike his man in a throng of enemies, he is far the best of all the mortals now on earth, and only the men of former days, Heracles and Eurytus, he will not challenge (8.215–228; see [[heracles]], [[eurytus]] and [[men-of-today]]). Nitzsch, as W. W. Merry reports him, explains the concession: the bow was Philoctetes' weapon of battle, while Odysseus used it only in contests of skill or, now and then, in the fight, so that Philoctetes might well be expected to surpass him (*Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 8.219). The Iliad calls him “well skilled with the bow” and leaves him on Lemnos, where the Argives would soon remember him (*Iliad* 2.718–725; see [[philoctetes-on-lemnos]]); Odysseus' words assume that he came to Troy (see [[philoctetes-brought-from-lemnos]]), and Nestor has heard that he came home safely (3.190).
