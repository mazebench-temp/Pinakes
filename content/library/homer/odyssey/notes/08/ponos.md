---
summary: In the simile of the third song the enemy lead the captive wife “away into slavery, to have toil and misery” (πόνον τʼ ἐχέμεν καὶ ὀιζύν, 8.529).
---

The toil is a slave's, the lot of the women of a sacked city: εἴρερον, “slavery”, is found only here in Homer (see the translation's table of key words and its note on 8.529), a word that Merry connects with εἴρω and σειρά, “rope”, comparing Latin *servus* (W. W. Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 8.529; see [[the-woman-weeping-over-her-husband]]). The man who weeps like her had brought the same lot on the women of Troy (8.514–531; see [[odysseus-weeps-at-the-feast]] and [[captive-women]]).
