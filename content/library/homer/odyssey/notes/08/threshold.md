# "λάινον οὐδὸν" (8.80; later 16.41, 17.30, 20.258, 23.88, always a threshold), first met in the Odyssey here; the
# Iliad's λάϊνος οὐδός (9.404) is in its Book 9 note.
grc: ["λάινον οὐδὸν"]
---

“For so Phoebus Apollo had told him in prophecy / in sacred Pytho, when he crossed the stone threshold / to consult the oracle” (8.79–81; see [[apollo]], [[pytho]], [[agamemnon]] and [[the-quarrel-of-odysseus-and-achilles]]). λάινος οὐδός is the threshold of Apollo's temple at Pytho, as in Achilles' words in the Iliad, that his life is worth more than “all that is shut within the stone threshold of the Archer, / Phoebus Apollo, in rocky Pytho” (*Iliad* 9.404–405; W. W. Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 8.80; see the translation's note on 8.80). Merry reports O. Müller's view that the stone threshold was a treasure house underground, from which Nitzsch dissents (on 8.80). Later in the poem the same words are the stone threshold of a house that Telemachus and Penelope step over (16.41, 17.30, 23.88, Greek only).
