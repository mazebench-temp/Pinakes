The song ends with three steps of the pattern, the goddess's own place, the bath and oil, and the dress (see the general article), but the order of the story is reversed: she is adorned not before she goes to win a lover but after her shame, and the scene closes the song instead of opening a seduction. The *Hymn to Aphrodite* brings her to the same place in the same words, “to Paphos, where she has a precinct and a fragrant altar,” with θυώδης for θυήεις, and goes on to the shining doors, the bath and oil of the Graces in this book's two verses (*Hymn to Aphrodite* 61–62 = 8.364–365), and her journey to Anchises (*Hymn to Aphrodite* 58–68; see the translation's note on 8.365; [[graces]], [[aphrodite]], [[immortals]], [[lovely]], [[fragrant]]).
