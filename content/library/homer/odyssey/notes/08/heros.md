# ἥρῳ (8.483), accent-sensitive, so as not to catch ἠρῶ "you prayed" (18.176).
grc: ["=ἥρῳ"]
en: [the hero]
---

ἥρως in the Odyssey is a title of respect for a man of standing, not only for a warrior. Alcinous imagines the stranger at home telling another of the heroes, ἡρώων, of the Phaeacians' excellence (8.241–245; see [[arete]]), and the singer who receives the stranger's gift of meat is “the hero Demodocus” (8.482–483; see [[demodocus]] and [[singers]]), as the Ithacan elder who opens the assembly of Book 2 is “the hero Aegyptius” (2.15; see [[aegyptius]]).
