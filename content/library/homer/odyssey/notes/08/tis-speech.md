---
summary: “And this is what a man would say, looking at his neighbor: / ‘Evil deeds do not prosper; the slow overtakes the swift’” (8.328–329).
---

The verse brings in the crowd's verdict in the Iliad, on Thersites' beating and over Hector's body (*Iliad* 2.271, 22.372); here the crowd is the gods, and its verdict a maxim on the case, that Hephaestus, slow and lame, has caught the swiftest of the gods by his craft and is owed the adulterer's fine (8.329–332; [[evil-deeds-do-not-prosper]], [[the-song-of-ares-and-aphrodite]]).
