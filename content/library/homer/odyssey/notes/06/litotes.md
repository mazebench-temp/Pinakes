summary: Alcinous: “I do not grudge you the mules, my child, nor anything else” (6.68); Nausicaa: the stranger seems “like neither a base man nor a witless one” (6.187), “Not against the will of all the gods who hold Olympus / does this man mingle with the godlike Phaeacians” (6.240–241), and “you do not seem to me to be senseless” (6.258).
refs: [6.68, 6.187, 6.240, 6.258]
---

Merry joins οὐ … ἀέκητι, as in Book 3 (3.28), and compares Virgil's “non sine numine divum” (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 6.240, citing *Aeneid* 2.777). 6.258 is Ino's verse to Odysseus in the storm (5.342; see the Odyssey translation's formulas).
