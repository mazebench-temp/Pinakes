**Nausicaa's directions.** After the description of the city and the gossip she fears, Nausicaa tells Odysseus how to win “escort and a homecoming” from her father (6.289–290; see [[nausicaa]] and [[the-city-of-the-phaeacians]]):

{{quote:1e500a02-6d6f-52e1-b2e5-687293e1de63}}

ἄλσος Ἀθήνης … αἰγείρων is “a grove of Athena, of poplars”: the second genitive gives what the grove is made of, as in ἄλσος ὑδατοτρεφέων αἰγείρων, the grove of water-nourished poplars by the spring outside the town of Ithaca (17.208), and need not be explained as a double genitive (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 6.291; see [[trees]]). The τέμενος of Alcinous is “the royal park” (Merry on 6.293), a king's domain, as the Lycians marked out a domain of orchard and plowland for Bellerophon (*Iliad* 6.194–195; see the translation's note on 6.293 and [[alcinous]]). The grove lies “as far from the city as a man can make himself heard with a shout” (6.294). The same measure is the distance at which Odysseus, swimming in, heard the surf on the reefs of Scheria (5.400), and in his later tales the distance from which he taunted the Cyclops and at which the Sirens saw his ship (9.473, 12.181). There he is to sit and wait until the girls have reached the house, and only then go into the city (6.295–299): she would not be seen coming into it with a stranger (6.273–288; see [[phaeacians]]).

**The prayer in the grove.** Nausicaa drives so that the handmaids and Odysseus can keep up on foot, “And the sun set, and they came to the famous grove, / sacred to Athena, where brilliant Odysseus sat down” (6.321–322; see [[famous]] and [[hieros]]). At once he prays to Athena: “listen to me now at least, since before you never listened / to me when I was being wrecked” (6.325–326), and asks to come to the Phaeacians “as one welcomed and pitied” (6.327; see [[welcomed-and-pitied]]). She hears him, but does not yet appear to him face to face, out of respect for her father's brother, who was still raging against Odysseus (6.328–331; see [[athena]], [[poseidon]] and [[seeing-the-gods]]). In her own grove the goddess does not show herself.
