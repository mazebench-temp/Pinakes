---
summary: Athena goes to the Phaeacians “devising a homecoming for great-hearted Odysseus” (6.14); Nausicaa is “the daughter of great-hearted Alcinous” (6.17, 6.196, 6.213), and she sends the stranger to “the house of my father, great-hearted Alcinous” (6.299).
---

The verse of 6.14 returns when the goddess goes through the town in the likeness of Alcinous' herald (8.9). θυγάτηρ μεγαλήτορος Ἀλκινόοιο is the Iliad's half-verse for Andromache, “the daughter of great-hearted Eetion” (*Iliad* 6.395, 8.187), with the father's name changed; the narrator uses it as he names the girl (6.17, 6.213), and she uses it when she names herself to the stranger (6.196).
