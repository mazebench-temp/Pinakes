---
summary: “Your glossy clothes lie uncared for,” Athena tells Nausicaa (6.26); the wagon is to carry “the glossy blankets” (6.38), and she takes “the whip and the glossy reins” (6.81).
---

Merry joins κεῖται ἀκηδέα, “lie uncared for,” and takes σιγαλόεντα as a fixed epithet, so bound to its noun that no contradiction is felt in clothes that are glossy and dirty at once; so too the shining clothing she brings out to wash (6.74), the comrades who ruined Odysseus, still called “trusty” (12.397), and Antilochus' horses, “swift-footed” though the slowest in the race (*Iliad* 23.304, 23.310; Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 6.26; [[phaeinos]], [[trusty]]). The whip and the glossy reins are the Iliad's verse-end for a driver (*Iliad* 5.226, 17.479); here a girl takes them to drive the mule wagon ([[chariot-journey]], [[wagon]], [[blankets]]).
