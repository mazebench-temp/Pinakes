---
summary: Athena is “gray-eyed” five times in the night of the dream and at the ball: she goes to Alcinous' house (6.13), speaks to Nausicaa in a friend's likeness (6.24), goes away to Olympus (6.41, 6.47), and “thought of another thing” (6.112).
---

At 6.47 the epithet stands alone for her name, “There the gray-eyed one went away,” as Zeus uses it of her in the Iliad (*Iliad* 8.406) and Odysseus in address (13.389). The verse of the disguise, “In her likeness gray-eyed Athena spoke to her” (6.24), is built like the Iliad's verse for Aphrodite in the likeness of the old wool-comber, “in her likeness brilliant Aphrodite spoke to her” (*Iliad* 3.389).
