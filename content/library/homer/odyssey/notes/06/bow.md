# βιός, the bow (6.270; later 22.246); the same letters without accents are βίος "life" in Aristotle, so the form stays
# in the Odyssey note.
grc: [βιὸς]
---

“For the Phaeacians care nothing for bow or quiver, / but for masts and the oars of ships and balanced ships, / in which they glory as they cross the gray sea” (6.270–272; see [[phaeacians]], [[quiver]] and [[ships]]). βιός is the rarer word for the bow (see the entry). The Phaeacians are a people of ships, not of war: no man will ever come to their land bringing combat, Nausicaa has said, for they live far apart in the sea (6.201–205). Their king says as much at the games: they are not flawless boxers or wrestlers, but swift runners and the best with ships (8.246–247). The stranger they receive is an archer; at those games he boasts that he knows how to handle the polished bow, and that only Philoctetes surpassed him at Troy (8.215–221; see [[odysseus]] and [[philoctetes]]).
