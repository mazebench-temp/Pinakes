---
summary: Odysseus went to Delos on the way to Troy, “and many people followed me” (6.164); Nausicaa promises to tell him “the name of the people” (6.194).
---

λαός is the people as a body of followers, and in Odysseus' mouth it is his army: “for I went there too, and many people followed me, / on that journey on which I was to have evil sorrows” (6.164–165). The visit to Delos would belong to the voyage from Aulis to Troy through the Cyclades, as in the route Nestor describes for the return (W. W. Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 6.162; see [[the-palm-on-delos]] and [[algos]]). The words hint at who the stranger is, a leader of men; Nausicaa does not take the hint up. Her λαοί are the people of the land: “I will show you the city, and tell you the name of the people. / The Phaeacians hold this city and land” (6.194–195; see [[phaeacians]] and [[demos]]).
