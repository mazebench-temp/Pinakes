summary: Odysseus' blessing, “three times blessed are your father and your lady mother, / and three times blessed your brothers” (6.154–155); the seat of the gods, “not shaken by winds, nor ever / wet with rain, nor does snow come near it” (6.43–44); and the mules, “And they ran well, and well they stepped out with their feet” (6.318).
refs: [6.43-44, 6.154-155, 6.318]
---

τρὶς μάκαρες … τρὶς μάκαρες opens two verses and leads to a third blessing, μακάρτατος, “most blessed,” of the man who will marry her (6.154–159; [[three-times-blessed]]); οὔτʼ … οὔτε … οὔτε denies the weathers of the earth to Olympus (6.43–45; [[olympus]]); and ἐὺ μὲν … ἐὺ δέ gives the mules' going in two verbs (see the translation's note on 6.318).
