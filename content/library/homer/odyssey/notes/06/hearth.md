---
summary: Nausicaa finds her mother at the hearth among her handmaids, turning the sea-purple yarn, when she goes to her parents at dawn (6.52–53); at the end of her instructions she tells Odysseus that the queen sits at the hearth in the light of the fire, and that it is her knees he must clasp (6.305–315).
---

**At dawn.** Nausicaa goes through the house to tell her parents of her dream and finds them within: “Her mother was sitting at the hearth with her handmaids, / turning the sea-purple yarn” (6.52–53; see [[distaff]], [[purple]] and [[handmaids]]), while her father was going out of doors to the council (6.53–55; see [[alcinous]]). The queen is not named in this book; she is Arete (7.54; see [[arete-queen-of-the-phaeacians]]).

**The queen at the hearth.** At the end of her directions Nausicaa tells the stranger how to find her mother when he has passed through the court and the hall:

{{quote:b9ae062b-f2d2-58c8-8bb7-96d4a27a55bb}}

The queen is seated, her chair set against the pillar, and the king's chair leans against hers, a little nearer the door (W. W. Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 6.307–308; see the translation's note on 6.307–309, [[house]] and [[throne]]). The stranger is to pass the king by and throw his arms around the queen's knees, “so that you may see the day of your homecoming” (6.310–311; see [[supplication]] and [[day-of-homecoming]]). The hearth, with its fire, is the place of the suppliant: in the next book Odysseus clasps Arete's knees and then sits down at the hearth in the ashes, beside the fire, until Alcinous raises him from it (7.139–171; see [[fire]]).
