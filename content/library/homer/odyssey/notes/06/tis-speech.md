---
summary: Nausicaa shuns the talk of the town, “and this is what some baser man might say, if he met us” (6.275); she gives his words (6.276–284), and closes them, “So they will say, and this would be a reproach to me” (6.285).
grc: ["τις ὧδʼ εἴπῃσι", "ὣς ἐρέουσιν"]
en: [this is what some baser man might say, So they will say]
passages:
  - 6.273-285 | What a baser man might say
---

The imagined speech has the frame of Hector's: τις ὧδʼ εἴπῃσι κακώτερος, “some baser man might say” (6.275), as Hector fears that “another man, baser than I,” will say that he destroyed the army (*Iliad* 22.106–107), and the same close, ὣς ἐρέουσιν, “So they will say” (*Iliad* 22.108). The scholia report that the fourteen verses from 6.275 were athetized as unfitting to the speaker, and Merry reads 6.282–284 as bitter sarcasm (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 6.282; see the translation's note on 6.275–288).
