---
summary: Athena tells Nausicaa that her suitors are the best men “of all the Phaeacians, where you yourself have your birth” (6.35).
---

ὅθι τοι γένος ἐστὶ καὶ αὐτῇ (6.35): with ἐστί, “where you have your own family ties”, the better reading, against ἐσσί, “to whom you yourself belong by birth”. A scholium takes ὅθι with ἀριστῆες, the best men, among whom her own family stands; the order of the words makes it likelier, Merry thinks, that ὅθι takes up δῆμον, so that Athena reminds her that she is wooed by the noblest men of her own people (W. W. Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 6.35; see the translation's note on 6.35, [[aristos]] and [[marriage]]). The stranger's birth is not asked in the book: Nausicaa tells him her own and her father's name (6.196–197; see [[xenia]]).
