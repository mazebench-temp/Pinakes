summary: The shore of Book 6 is the beach at the river's mouth on Scheria: the clothes are spread to dry along it where the sea washes the pebbles clean (6.94–95), the girls flee over its “jutting beaches” when Odysseus appears (6.138), and after his bath he sits apart on it, shining with beauty and grace (6.236–237).
# ἠιόνας προὐχούσας, "the jutting beaches" (6.138), as a phrase, found only here: ἠιόνας alone is also the Argive town
# Ἠϊόνας (Il. 2.561), and the Book 5 note claims ἠιόνας only in its own phrase (5.418, 5.440).
grc: ["ἠιόνας προὐχούσας"]
en: [jutting beaches]

**The pebbles.** The washing done, the girls spread the clothes “in a row along the shore of the sea, just where / the sea, beating on the land, used to wash the pebbles clean” (6.94–95; see [[sea]] and [[washing-troughs]]), and wait for them to dry in the rays of the sun (6.98; see [[helios]]). The beach is the θίς, the strand by the sea, here of λάιγγες, “pebbles”, the stones that cling to the suckers of the octopus in the simile of the day before (5.433; see [[the-octopus]]). W. W. Merry takes the place, where the sea always scours the stones, as almost a fixed point of the beach, since the Mediterranean has little tide (*Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 6.95; see the translation's note on 6.95).

**The jutting beaches.** When Odysseus comes out of the bushes, “they fled in fear this way and that over the jutting beaches” (6.138; see [[the-hungry-lion]] and [[handmaids]]). ἠιόνας προὐχούσας are, in Merry's rendering, “jutting spits”: the scene is laid near the mouth of a river, so that the low sandy beaches common in such places are meant (on 6.138). They are the kind of shore Odysseus looked for in vain along the cliffs the day before, “beaches where the waves strike slanting” (5.418, 5.440), which Merry takes for the low horns of shore found especially at the mouths of rivers (on 5.418; see the Book 5 note).

**Sitting apart.** After his bath and Athena's gift of grace, Odysseus “went apart along the shore of the sea and sat down, / shining with beauty and grace; and the girl gazed at him” (6.236–237; see [[gold-poured-around-silver]] and [[nausicaa]]). On Calypso's island he sat on the shore and wept, looking out over the barren sea (5.82–84, 5.151–158); on Scheria he sits on the shore and is looked at.
