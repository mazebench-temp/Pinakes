---
summary: “So he lay sleeping there, much-enduring brilliant Odysseus” (6.1), and “Then much-enduring brilliant Odysseus drank and ate / greedily; for he had long tasted no food” (6.249–250).
---

The book opens with the hero “worn out by sleep and weariness” (6.2), and the epithet returns when he eats at last (6.249–250; [[fasting]]). The first verse is said again when he goes to sleep in Alcinous' house (7.344; see the Odyssey translation's formulas). See [[brilliant]] and [[odysseus]].
