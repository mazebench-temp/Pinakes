summary: Nausithous settled the Phaeacians in Scheria, “far from toiling men” (6.8), and the whole book is spent on its shore and on the road to its city. Nausicaa tells the stranger whose land it is (6.195), and her handmaids that her people live “far apart in the sea of many waves” (6.204).
# The land spoken of without its name: "the land and the city" (6.3), the land of unknown mortals (6.119), "this city
# and land" (6.177, 6.191, 6.195), "the land of the Phaeacian men" (6.202), the fields on the way to the city (6.259).
refs: [6.3, 6.119, 6.177, 6.191, 6.195, 6.202, 6.259]
---

**Far from toiling men.** Nausithous “settled them in Scheria, far from toiling men” (6.8; see [[nausithous]], [[toiling-men]] and [[the-migration-of-the-phaeacians]]). δὲ Σχερίῃ is Aristarchus' reading; others read δʼ ἐν Σχερίῃ, and Apollonius ἐς Σχερίην (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, critical note on 6.8; see the translation's note on 6.8). Scheria is named only here in the book; elsewhere it is the land, γαῖα, or the city and the land together.

**Whose land?** Odysseus wakes not knowing where he is: “to the land of what mortals have I come this time?” (6.119; see [[to-the-land-of-what-mortals]]). He tells Nausicaa that he knows no one “of the people who hold this city and land” (6.176–177), and she answers him in his own words: “The Phaeacians hold this city and land” (6.195; see [[phaeacians]] and [[nausicaa]]). To her handmaids she adds that no enemy will ever come to “the land of the Phaeacian men”, for “we live far apart in the sea of many waves, / the farthest of men” (6.202–205). ἐνὶ πόντῳ does not settle whether Scheria is an island; it means only that the Phaeacian land lay far across the sea (Merry on 6.204; see [[of-many-waves]]). Strabo quoted the same verses, with those on Ogygia, as plain signs that the poet set these places in the Atlantic, as fictions (*Geography* 1.2.18; see [[strabo]]).

**The shore and the road.** The book's places are those of the coast where Odysseus came ashore: the river and its washing troughs, the shore where the clothes are spread to dry, the thicket where he slept (6.85–98, 6.127–128; see [[the-river-of-scheria]], [[washing-troughs]] and [[shore]]). From there the road leads through “the fields and the works of men” (6.259) to the grove of Athena, beside Alcinous' domain, and on to the city with its rampart and a harbor on either side (6.262–267; see [[the-grove-of-athena]] and [[the-city-of-the-phaeacians]]). Odysseus reached the land on the twentieth day from Ogygia, as Zeus had said he would (6.170–172; 5.34; see [[ogygia]]).
