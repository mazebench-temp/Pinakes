---
summary: Nausicaa names her father to the stranger: “I am the daughter of great-hearted Alcinous, / on whom the strength and the force of the Phaeacians depend” (6.196–197).
---

τοῦ δʼ ἐκ Φαιήκων ἔχεται κάρτος τε βίη τε (6.197): the strength and force of the people “hang from” the king. Merry compares the construction with Miltiades' word to Callimachus before Marathon, that all now rests with him and “depends on you” (ἐκ σέο ἤρτηται, Herodotus 6.109.6), and with Nestor's to Agamemnon, σέο δʼ ἕξεται ὅττι κεν ἄρχῃ, “whatever he begins will rest with you” (*Iliad* 9.102; W. W. Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 6.197). The pair κάρτος τε βίη τε is Eidothea's word to Menelaus for the strength he must use on Proteus (4.415). Nausicaa says it after the name of her people and before her own (6.195–197; see [[alcinous]], [[phaeacians]] and [[kingship-and-authority]]).
