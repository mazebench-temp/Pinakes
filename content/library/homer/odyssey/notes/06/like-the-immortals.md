---
summary: Nausicaa sleeps “like the immortals in stature and in looks” (6.16), and her father sits on his chair and “drinks his wine like an immortal” (6.309).
grc: ["ἀθανάτῃσι φυὴν καὶ εἶδος ὁμοίη", "ἀθάνατος ὥς"]
en: [like an immortal]
---

For 6.309 Merry compares the gods seated beside Zeus while Hebe pours them nectar (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 6.309, comparing *Iliad* 4.1–3). The book is full of such likenesses: Odysseus takes the girl for a goddess and likens her to Artemis (6.149–152), and she says of him after his bath that he is like the gods (6.243; [[wide-sky]], [[immortals]], [[divine-epiphany]]).
