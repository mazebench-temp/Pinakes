---
summary: “And he went on like a mountain-bred lion, trusting in his courage” (6.130).
---

The verse opens the simile of the lion driven by hunger, whose last verse is the Iliad's (6.134 = *Iliad* 12.301); the Odyssey uses λέων ὀρεσίτροφος once more, of the Cyclops eating Odysseus' men (9.292). Merry notes that the dative ἀλκί, in ἀλκὶ πεποιθώς, is found four times in the Iliad and only here in the Odyssey, and that the point of the comparison is that both lion and man are driven by hunger to an unusual boldness, “for need had come upon him” (6.136; Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 6.130, 6.135).
