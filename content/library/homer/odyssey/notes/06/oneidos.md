---
summary: Nausicaa ends the speech she imagines in the mouth of some baser Phaeacian, “So they will say, and this would be a reproach to me” (6.285).
---

The reproach is the end of the talk: ὣς ἐρέουσιν, ἐμοὶ δέ κʼ ὀνείδεα ταῦτα γένοιτο, “So they will say, and this would be a reproach to me” (6.285; see [[the-talk-of-the-people]] and [[nemesis]]). Hector closes the words he fears from a baser man with the same ὣς ἐρέουσιν (*Iliad* 22.108), and the suitors will close theirs, as Eurymachus imagines them, with nearly the same verse, ὣς ἐρέουσʼ, ἡμῖν δʼ ἂν ἐλέγχεα ταῦτα γένοιτο, “So they will say, and this would be a disgrace to us” (21.329, a plain rendering). The plural ὀνείδεα is the many reproaches of men that a god set in Phoenix's heart beside “the talk of the people” (δήμου … φάτιν καὶ ὀνείδεα, *Iliad* 9.460; see [[phoenix-patricide-lines]]). Nausicaa's reproach would fall on her, not on the stranger, and it is enough to send him into the city alone (6.289–299; see [[aidos]]).
