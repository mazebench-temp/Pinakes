---
summary: The Phaeacians once lived near “the Cyclopes, overweening men, / who used to plunder them” (6.5–6).
---

In the Odyssey the participle is above all the suitors' (2.324, 4.769); Merry notes that the Iliad uses it only twice, of the Trojans and of Deiphobus, and that the adjective ὑπερήνωρ is found in Homer only as a name (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 6.5). Here it names the neighbors whose strength drove the Phaeacians to Scheria ([[cyclopes]], [[the-migration-of-the-phaeacians]]).
