---
summary: At dawn “well-robed Nausicaa” wakes from Athena's dream (6.48–49).
---

The epithet comes with her waking, the morning after the goddess has told her that her clothes lie unwashed and her marriage is near (6.25–28; [[dream-scene]], [[of-the-fair-throne]]). The robes, πέπλοι, are among the clothes she is to take to the river (6.38; [[robe]]). The Iliad gives the epithet to women in groups and to a maidservant; Nausicaa is the only woman named with it in Homer.
