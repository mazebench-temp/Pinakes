---
summary: “Am I perhaps somewhere near men with human voice?” Odysseus asks on waking (6.125).
---

The Odyssey's other uses of the epithet are of goddesses and of Ino, who was once a mortal (5.334, 10.136); here it marks men, as against the nymphs whose voices he thinks he hears (6.122–125; see the translation's note on 6.122–125; [[to-the-land-of-what-mortals]]).
