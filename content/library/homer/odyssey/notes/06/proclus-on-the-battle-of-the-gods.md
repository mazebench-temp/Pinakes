---
summary: Proclus takes the verse that ends the book's picture of Olympus, “in it the blessed gods take their delight all their days”, for the poet's own proof that there is no faction among the gods.
refs: [6.46]
---

The verse closes the description of Olympus to which Athena returns from Nausicaa's dream (6.41–47; see [[olympus]] and [[blessed-gods]]). Proclus, beginning his defense of the battle of the gods, sets it against Plato's charge: there is neither faction nor any mortal division among the gods, but peace and a life without harm, as the poet himself shows when he speaks of Olympus as spread beneath the gods, who have every gladness and sights of unspeakable beauty (*On the Republic* I 87 Kroll; see the entry above and [[the-seat-of-the-gods-in-later-writers]]).
