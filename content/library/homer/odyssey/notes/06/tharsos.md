---
summary: When the handmaids flee from the naked stranger, “only the daughter of Alcinous stood her ground; for Athena / put daring in her mind and took the fear from her limbs” (6.139–140).
---

The girls see a man “terrible … afflicted by the brine”, and “fled in fear this way and that over the jutting beaches” (6.137–138); τρέσσαν is “fled scared”, and Merry notes Aristarchus' rule that τρεῖν always carries the notion of flight, though the sense is not strongly marked everywhere (W. W. Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 6.138). Nausicaa alone stays, and the narrator gives the reason: θάρσος ἐνὶ φρεσὶ θῆκε καὶ ἐκ δέος εἵλετο γυίων, “put daring in her mind and took the fear from her limbs” (6.140; see [[athena]] and [[deos]]). It is the gift Athena gave Telemachus at Pylos, “for Athena herself had put daring in his mind” (3.76), and Diomedes in the Iliad (*Iliad* 5.1–2; see the translation's table of key words). “She checked herself and stood facing him” (6.141): σχομένη is “halting”, having checked herself from flight, as Eustathius explains it (Merry on 6.141). The daring is the condition of the whole meeting: Athena has planned that Odysseus should wake and see the girl who would lead him to the city (6.112–114; see [[thought-of-another-thing]]), and the god's gift lets her stand while the man, who came on “like a mountain-bred lion, trusting in his courage” (6.130; see [[the-hungry-lion]]), keeps his distance (6.141–147; see [[supplication]]).
