---
summary: Nausicaa calls her people “the godlike Phaeacians” (6.241), and the book ends with Poseidon raging “against godlike Odysseus, until he reached his own land” (6.330–331).
---

The last verse of the book is the proem's (1.21): there Poseidon “raged relentlessly” (1.20), here “furiously,” ἐπιζαφελῶς (6.330; see the Odyssey translation's key words), and the anger that opened the poem closes the book in which Odysseus reaches the Phaeacians (6.329–331; [[poseidon]], [[phaeacians]]).
