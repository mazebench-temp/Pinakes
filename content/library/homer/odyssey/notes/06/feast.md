**The meal by the river.** The food comes from the house: Nausicaa's mother put in a box “food to satisfy the heart, / of every kind”, and meats, and wine in a goatskin (6.76–78; see [[provisions]] and [[wine]]). When the girls have washed the clothes and bathed, “they took their meal then beside the banks of the river, / and waited for the clothes to dry in the rays of the sun. / But when they had taken their fill of food, the slave women and she herself, / they played with a ball” (6.97–100; see [[washing-troughs]], [[bathing]] and [[ball]]). δεῖπνον, “meal”, is the word of a guest's welcome too: “when you have tasted the meal, we will ask who you are”, Menelaus tells the strangers at Sparta (4.61; see [[feast-type-scene]]).

**Food and drink for the stranger.** The first thing that Nausicaa orders for the suppliant is a meal: “But, handmaids, give the stranger food and drink, / and bathe him in the river” (6.209–210; see [[xenia]] and [[handmaids]]), and she repeats the order when he has bathed (6.246). “and set food and drink beside Odysseus. / Then much-enduring brilliant Odysseus drank and ate / greedily; for he had long tasted no food” (6.248–250; see [[fasting]] and [[much-enduring]]). When the storm broke up his raft he was two nights and two days in the sea, and he has slept since he came ashore (5.388–389, 5.491–493, 6.1–2; see [[the-shipwreck-of-odysseus]]). βρῶσίν τε πόσιν τε is the food and drink that Laertes' old handmaid sets before him in the country (1.191).
