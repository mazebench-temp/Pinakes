# κάπροισι (6.104), found only here in Homer; the Iliad's κάπροισιν (Il. 5.783) is in the Book 5 note.

**Artemis' boars.** τερπομένη κάπροισι καὶ ὠκείῃς ἐλάφοισι, “delighting in boars and swift deer” (6.104; see [[artemis-among-her-nymphs]], [[artemis]] and [[deer]]). κάπρος is the wild boar, the beast of the mountains, as in the Iliad's “wild boars”, συσὶ κάπροισιν (*Iliad* 5.783). W. W. Merry notes that Erymanthus, named in the verse before, was the fabled haunt of the great boar slain by Heracles, so that the boars are peculiarly fitting here (*Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 6.102; see [[erymanthus]]). In the Iliad Artemis, angry because Oeneus had made her no harvest offering, roused against him the wild boar that ravaged his orchard (*Iliad* 9.533–540; see [[the-calydonian-boar]]); here she delights in hunting boars. The boar that matters most to Odysseus is still to come, the boar of Parnassus that gave him his scar (19.428–458; see [[the-scar-of-odysseus]]).
