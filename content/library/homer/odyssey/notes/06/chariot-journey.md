**The drive.** “And she took the whip and the glossy reins, / and whipped them to a run” (6.81–82): the driver's verses of the Iliad, but where the horses fly on not unwilling (3.484; *Iliad* 5.366), the mules clatter and strain on without end, at a steady pace, since the handmaids follow on foot (6.82–84; see the translation's note on 6.83; [[glossy]], [[mules]]).

**The unyoking.** In ὑπεκπροέλυσαν (6.88) Merry finds the three prepositions at work: they took the mules from under the yoke, detached them from the cart, and turned them out to graze (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 6.86; [[eddying]], [[honey-sweet]]).

**The drive back.** The girl yokes the mules herself, lashes them with the shining whip, and drives so that the handmaids and Odysseus can follow on foot (6.252–253, 6.316–320; see the translation's notes on 6.318 and 6.319–320; [[strong-hoofed]], [[phaeinos]], [[wagon]]).
