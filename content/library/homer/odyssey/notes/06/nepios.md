---
summary: Nausicaa tells the stranger that her father's house is easy to find: “even a child could lead you, / a little one” (6.300–301).
en: [a little one]
---

καὶ ἂν πάϊς ἡγήσαιτο / νήπιος (6.300–301): νήπιος here is the plain sense of the word, a small child, “even a child would show thee the way” (W. W. Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 6.300), not the “fool” of the narrator's judgments elsewhere. The house of Alcinous is so unlike the other houses of the Phaeacians that a little child could point it out (6.300–303; see [[house]] and [[alcinous]]). Merry finds the clause οἷος δόμος Ἀλκινόοιο an awkward explanation of ἐοικότα τοῖσι (on 6.301; see the translation's note on 6.301–303).
