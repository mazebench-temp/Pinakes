---
summary: Nausicaa's companions are “girls of the lovely tresses” (6.135, 6.222) and “her handmaids of the lovely tresses” (6.198, 6.238).
grc: [ἐυπλοκάμοισι, ἐυπλοκάμοισιν]
---

The Odyssey gives the epithet above all to goddesses, to Calypso, Demeter, Dawn and Circe (1.86, 5.125, 5.390, 10.136); in this book it is four times the dative plural of mortal girls (see the Odyssey translation's formulas). 6.238 is built like Andromache's verse to her slave women, δμῳῇσιν ἐϋπλοκάμοισι μετηύδα (*Iliad* 22.449). Twice it names the girls among whom Odysseus must come naked, in the narrator's simile and in his own words (6.135, 6.222; [[handmaids]]).
