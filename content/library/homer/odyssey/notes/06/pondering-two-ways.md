summary: Woken by the cry of the girls, “sitting up he pondered in his mind and in his heart” (6.118); and when Nausicaa stands her ground, “Odysseus pondered / whether he should clasp the knees of the fair-faced girl and beg her” (6.141–142).
grc: ["δὲ μερμήριξεν Ὀδυσσεύς"]
---

The first pondering is a question with no course in it, what land he has come to ([[to-the-land-of-what-mortals]]); the second sets out two courses and ends in a choice ([[decision-scene]]). ἑζόμενος, “sitting up,” takes the place of ἧος ὁ ταῦθʼ, “While he was pondering this,” of the verse in Books 4 and 5 (4.120, 5.365, 5.424).
