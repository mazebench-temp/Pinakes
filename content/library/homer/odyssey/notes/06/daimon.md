---
summary: Odysseus tells Nausicaa that after twenty days at sea “now some god has cast me ashore here, / so that, I suppose, I may yet suffer evil here too” (6.172–173).
---

νῦν δʼ ἐνθάδε κάββαλε δαίμων (6.172): δαίμων is the god whose hand is felt and not named. Odysseus knows who wrecked him, and names him in his prayer at the end of the book, “the famous shaker of the earth” (6.326; see [[poseidon]]), but the power that has cast him on this shore is “some god”, and he expects more evil from it, “for I do not think / it will cease, but the gods will yet accomplish much before that” (6.173–174; see [[algos]]). κάββαλε is “cast me ashore”, not a safe landing but a casting out by the violence of the winds, as the scholium says (W. W. Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 6.172); the Harleian manuscript reads κάμβαλε, which Bekker, La Roche and Ameis adopt (Merry's critical note; see the translation's note on 6.172). πάροιθεν is “before that”, before the respite comes; the scholium's “hereafter” cannot be right, since Homer says ὄπιθεν or ὀπίσσω for that (Merry on 6.174; see the translation's note on 6.174). The divine hand on this shore is in fact Athena's, who has planned his waking and the girl who will lead him to the city (6.112–117; see [[athena]] and [[divine-intervention]]).
