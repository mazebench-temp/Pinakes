# πείσματα, the cables by which a ship is made fast to the shore (6.269; later 10.96, 10.127; πείσματος 9.136).
grc: [πείσματα]
en: [cables]
---

“There they take care of the tackle of the black ships, / cables and sails, and they taper the oars” (6.268–269; see [[ships]], [[sails]] and [[oars]]). The πείσματα are the cables that make a ship fast to the land, as the stern-cables do: in the later books Odysseus moors his ship outside the harbor of the Laestrygonians by binding cables to a rock, and cuts them with his sword to escape (10.95–96, 10.126–127), and the harbor of the island off the land of the Cyclopes needs no cable, no mooring stones and no stern-cables (9.136–137; plain renderings; see [[mooring-stones]]). Eustathius knew a variant σπείρας, “warping cables”, for the sails of the same verse (see the translation's note on 6.269).
