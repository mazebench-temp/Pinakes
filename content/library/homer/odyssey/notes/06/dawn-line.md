---
summary: Athena bids Nausicaa go to wash “when dawn appears” and urge her father “in the early dawn” (6.31, 6.36); and “at once Dawn of the fair throne came, and she woke her” (6.48).
grc: ["ἠοῖ φαινομένηφι", "Ἠὼς ἦλθεν ἐύθρονος"]
---

ἅμʼ ἠοῖ φαινομένηφι is the Iliad's phrase for what is to be done at daybreak (*Iliad* 9.682, 11.685), and ἠῶθι πρό is the early dawn of a battle cry in the Iliad (*Iliad* 11.50) and of the cold breeze from the river in Book 5 (5.469). The dawn that wakes Nausicaa is the one the goddess named (6.48; [[of-the-fair-throne]], [[eos]]); the same half-verse brings the dawn on which Telemachus lands on Ithaca (15.495).
