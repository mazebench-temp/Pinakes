summary: Athena bids Nausicaa urge “your famous father” (6.36); Alcinous goes “to join the famous kings” at the council (6.54); Nausicaa will take “the glorious clothes” to the river (6.58); at sunset they come “to the famous grove” of Athena (6.321); and Odysseus complains that the goddess did not hear him “when the famous shaker of the earth wrecked me” (6.326).

πατέρα κλυτόν is said of Agamemnon in the verse of Aegisthus' crime, “scheming Aegisthus, who had killed his famous father” (1.300, 3.198, 3.308); here it is the father of a girl soon to be married. Of things κλυτός is “glorious,” as the house of Poseidon is (5.381), or “famous,” as the grove is (6.321; see the Odyssey translation's key words). The famous kings are the Phaeacian lords who call their king to council (6.54–55; [[noble]], [[phaeacians]], [[boule]]). See [[the-grove-of-athena]] and [[shaker-of-the-earth]].
