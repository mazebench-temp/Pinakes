**The founder.** As Athena comes to the city, the poet tells who its people are: “From there godlike Nausithous had moved them and led them away, / and settled them in Scheria, far from toiling men; / and he drove a wall around the city, and built houses, / and made temples of the gods, and divided the fields” (6.7–10; see [[the-migration-of-the-phaeacians]], [[hypereia-of-the-phaeacians]], [[cyclopes]] and [[scheria]]). He is named once in the book, with his epithet θεοειδής, “godlike” (see [[godlike]]), and he is already dead: “But he had already been overcome by his doom and gone to the house of Hades, / and Alcinous was ruling then, who knew counsels from the gods” (6.11–12), the verse that tells at Pylos of Nestor's father Neleus (3.409–410; see [[alcinous]], [[neleus]] and [[hades]]).

**His city.** What Nausicaa shows Odysseus later in the book is the founder's work: the fields and the works of men through which the wagon goes, the high rampart around the city, the place of assembly around the precinct of Poseidon, and the houses of the Phaeacians, among which the house of Alcinous stands out (6.259–267, 6.301–303; see [[the-city-of-the-phaeacians]] and [[nausicaa]]). That he was a son of Poseidon, whose precinct stands in that place of assembly, the poem tells only in the next book (7.56–62; see [[poseidon]]).
