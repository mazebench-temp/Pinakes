summary: At the end of the simile of Artemis, “so she stood out among her handmaids, a maiden unwed” (6.109); and Odysseus puts on “the clothes that the maiden unwed had given him” (6.228).
en: [unwed]
---

ἀδμής is the word of the heifer never yoked and of mules not yet broken (3.383, 4.637); of a girl it means unwed, for the same verb, δάμνημι, is used of giving a woman to a husband, as Thetis says Zeus “subdued me to a man” (*Iliad* 18.432). In the dream Athena had told her that she would not be a maiden much longer (6.33; [[marriage]], [[nausicaa]]).
