summary: Odysseus' prayer in the grove in the order of the type-scene: he sits down in the sacred grove (6.321–322), prays at once to the daughter of great Zeus (6.323), calls on her, “Hear me, child of aegis-bearing Zeus, Atrytone” (6.324), makes his claim, a reproach that she did not hear him in the storm (6.325–326), asks, “Grant that I come to the Phaeacians as one welcomed and pitied” (6.327), and is heard (6.328), but she does not yet appear (6.329–331).
passages:
  - 6.321-331 | Odysseus prays to Athena in her grove
---

The claim of a prayer is usually a past service or a past answer, “once before you heard me when I prayed” (*Iliad* 1.453, 16.236); Odysseus turns it around: “listen to me now at least, since before you never listened / to me when I was being wrecked” (6.325–326). The request is the verse the *Rhetoric* quotes for the two aims of a speaker, to be liked and to be pitied (*Rhetoric* 1415b26–27; see the translation's note on 6.327 and [[welcomed-and-pitied]]). The answer is divided: the goddess hears, but out of regard for her father's brother she does not appear to him face to face (6.329–331; see the translation's note on 6.329–331; [[hear-me]], [[poseidon]], [[seeing-the-gods]]).
