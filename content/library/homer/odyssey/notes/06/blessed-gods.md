---
summary: The blessed gods appear once, at home: on Olympus, which no wind or rain or snow comes near, “the blessed gods take their delight all their days” (6.46).
---

The verse ends the picture of the seat of the gods to which Athena returns from Nausicaa's dream (6.41–47; see [[olympus]] and [[olympians]]). μάκαρες θεοί, “the blessed gods”, are here what the word says, beings whose happiness nothing disturbs; the same book uses the word of mortals in the language of compliment, when Odysseus calls Nausicaa's parents and brothers “three times blessed” and her future husband “the most blessed in his heart, beyond all others” (6.154–158; see [[three-times-blessed]]).
