---
summary: Eustathius records that Sophocles, the most skillful ball player among private men, wrote a satyr play, the Washerwomen, and himself played Nausicaa in it.
---

- Eustathius, on the game of ball in this book (6.100–117), says that the Lacedaemonians excelled all others at ball play, that Alexander was the most expert of kings, and that among private men the most skillful was Sophocles the tragedian, who wrote a satyr play called the *Washerwomen* (Πλύντριαι) and himself took the part of Nausicaa in it (as Merry reports him on 6.115): [[ball]], [[nausicaa]] and [[the-title-of-odyssey-6]].
