---
summary: The Stranger of Plato's Statesman names among the gifts of the gods the arts that came from Hephaestus and his fellow craftswoman, the pair who in this book's simile teach the goldsmith every kind of craft.
---

- When men were first left without the gods' care, the gifts of which the old stories tell were given to them with the teaching they needed: “fire from Prometheus, the arts from Hephaestus and his fellow craftswoman, and seeds and plants from others” (*Statesman* 274c–d, a plain rendering). Nitzsch, as Merry reports him, set the passage beside “a skilled man whom Hephaestus and Pallas Athena have taught / every kind of craft” (6.233–234), and Merry adds the *Hymn to Hephaestus*, in which Hephaestus with gray-eyed Athena taught glorious works to men on earth, who before lived in caves in the mountains like beasts (*Homeric Hymn* 20.2–4; Merry on 6.233): [[gold-poured-around-silver]], [[hephaestus]] and [[athena]].
