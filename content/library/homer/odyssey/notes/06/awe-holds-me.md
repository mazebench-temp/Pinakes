---
summary: “For never yet have I seen with my eyes such a mortal, / neither man nor woman; awe holds me as I look” (6.160–161).
---

The verse is Helen's when she sees the likeness of Odysseus in Telemachus (4.142), with the participle made masculine; here it is the awe of a stranger before a girl he has likened to Artemis (6.149–152). It returns as ἐτεθήπεα, “I was astounded,” in the comparison with the palm on Delos (6.166–168; [[sebas]], [[the-palm-on-delos]]).
