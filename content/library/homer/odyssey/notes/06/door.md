summary: Nausicaa's two handmaids sleep by the doorposts of her chamber, and its shining doors are closed, when Athena comes to her in the night (6.18–20).
# θύραι (6.19; later 7.88, 13.109, 17.267), always doors, and σταθμοῖιν, the dual "by the doorposts" (6.19; only
# here): σταθμός alone is also a steading. θύραζε, "out of doors" (6.53), is not the door.
grc: [θύραι, σταθμοῖιν]
---

“and beside her two handmaids, who had their beauty from the Graces, / one on either side, by the doorposts; and the shining doors were closed” (6.18–19; see [[handmaids]] and [[house]]). W. W. Merry supposes that the two slept on mattresses on the floor, so near the door that it could not be opened without waking them (*Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 6.18). ἐπέκειντο, “were closed”, is “lay to” on their posts, the verb with which the Hours who keep the gates of heaven close the thick cloud, ἐπιθεῖναι (Merry on 6.19; *Iliad* 5.749–751; see [[hours]]). The goddess does not need it opened: “she rushed like a breath of wind to the girl's bed” (6.20; see [[athena]] and [[bed]]), as the phantom that she sends to Penelope goes into the chamber past the strap of the bolt (4.802; see the Book 4 note and [[eidolon]]). In the next book the doors of the house of Alcinous are golden and the doorposts silver, set on a threshold of bronze (7.88–89, a plain rendering).
