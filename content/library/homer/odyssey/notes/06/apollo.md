---
summary: Apollo is named once, by his altar on Delos, where Odysseus once saw a young palm springing up and wondered at it as he wonders now at Nausicaa (6.162–168).
---

**The altar on Delos.** Odysseus, wondering at the girl, remembers a sight: “Once in Delos, beside the altar of Apollo, such a thing / I saw, a young sapling of a palm tree springing up; / for I went there too, and many people followed me, / on that journey on which I was to have evil sorrows” (6.162–165; see [[the-palm-on-delos]], [[palm]] and [[delos]]). The visit would belong to the voyage from Aulis to Troy through the Cyclades, and Merry reports Voss's view, through Nitzsch, that Delos was in Agamemnon's time the regular oracle for seafaring men; there seems to have been a palm always kept in the god's precinct, like the sacred olives of the Academy at Athens (*Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 6.162; see the translation's note on 6.162–163). Homer says nothing more of Apollo on Delos. The *Hymn to Apollo* makes the island his birthplace, where Leto bore him “nearest the palm” (*Hymn to Apollo* 16–18, 117–119; see [[leto]]), and the scholia referred Odysseus' palm to that first palm (Merry on 6.162). The god of the altar is the archer of the Iliad; in the next book he is the god who shot Rhexenor, Alcinous' brother, in his hall, leaving one daughter, Arete (7.64–66).
