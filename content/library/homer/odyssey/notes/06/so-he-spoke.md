summary: Ten of the book's eleven speeches end with the formula, from “So saying, gray-eyed Athena went away” (6.41) to “So he spoke in prayer, and Pallas Athena heard him” (6.328); Nausicaa's answer to the stranger ends with ἦ ῥα καί, “She spoke, and gave orders to her handmaids” (6.198).
en: [She spoke]
---

The closings turn each speech to its effect. Four tell of obedience: the slaves obey the king (6.71), and the handmaids stand still and call to one another (6.211), go apart (6.223), and “listened closely to her and obeyed” (6.247), a verse of the Iliad's councils (*Iliad* 7.379). One gives the speaker's reason instead: “So she spoke; for she was ashamed to name her blooming marriage” (6.66; [[aidos]], [[flourishing]]). 6.41 is the verse of Athena's departure in Book 1 (1.319). Only Odysseus' supplication has no closing verse: Nausicaa answers at once (6.185–186; [[answering-him]]).
