summary: Odysseus calls Nausicaa's brothers “three times blessed” in their sister (6.155), five sons of Alcinous, “two married, and three young unmarried men in their bloom” (6.62–63); and Poseidon is Athena's “father's brother” (6.330).
# πατροκασίγνητον (6.330).
grc: [πατροκασίγνητον]
en: [brothers]
---

Nausicaa's brothers are named only in later books; here they are a reason for clean clothes, “five dear sons are born to you in the halls, / two married, and three young unmarried men in their bloom; / and they always want to have freshly washed clothes / when they go to the dance” (6.62–65; see [[marriage]] and [[dance]]), and a third of Odysseus' felicitation, τρὶς μάκαρες δὲ κασίγνητοι, “and three times blessed your brothers” (6.155; see [[three-times-blessed]]). At the end of the book the word is a god's kinship. Athena does not yet appear to Odysseus, “for she respected / her father's brother” (πατροκασίγνητον, 6.329–330), Poseidon, Zeus' brother (see [[poseidon]] and [[aidos]]); Apollo will not fight his uncle in the Iliad for the same reason, πατροκασιγνήτοιο (*Iliad* 21.468–469).
