---
summary: Athena in the dream says that from fine clothes comes good talk, “and your father and lady mother rejoice” (6.30); Odysseus says “three times blessed are your father and your lady mother” (6.154).
---

The verse-end πατὴρ καὶ πότνια μήτηρ comes in both speeches that look to Nausicaa's marriage, the goddess' and the stranger's (6.27–30, 6.154–159; [[three-times-blessed]], [[marriage]]). The mother is not named in this book (6.51–53, 6.305–312; [[arete-queen-of-the-phaeacians]]).
