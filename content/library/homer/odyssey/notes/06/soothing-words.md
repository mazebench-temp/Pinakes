---
summary: Odysseus decides to beg Nausicaa “with soothing words, standing apart” (6.143, 6.146), and “At once he spoke a soothing and shrewd word” (6.148).
grc: [μειλιχίοισι, μειλίχιον]
en: [soothing]
---

Through the sense of gain-getting, Merry says, κερδαλέος takes almost any coloring, from the wise counsel of a goddess to the craftiness of Sisyphus (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 6.148); the soothing word is also a shrewd one ([[kerdos]], [[decision-scene]], [[supplication-type-scene]]).
