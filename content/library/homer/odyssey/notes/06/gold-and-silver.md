**The golden flask.** “And she gave her liquid olive oil in a golden flask” (6.79), and the handmaids give the stranger the same oil in the same flask (6.215; see [[oil-flask]] and [[bathing]]). The vessels of the houses of kings are of gold (see the Book 4 note).

**Gold poured around silver.** When Odysseus has bathed, Athena makes him taller and stouter and pours grace on him: “And as when a man pours gold around silver, / a skilled man whom Hephaestus and Pallas Athena have taught / every kind of craft, and he accomplishes lovely works, / so she poured grace upon his head and shoulders” (6.232–235; see [[gold-poured-around-silver]], [[hephaestus]] and [[athena]]). The silver is the ground on which the gold is laid, ἀργύρῳ being a locative, like κεφαλῇ τε καὶ ὤμοις, “upon his head and shoulders”, in the verse that answers it; the verb is the one used of the gold “poured around” the horns of a heifer for a sacrifice (W. W. Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 6.232; 3.384 = *Iliad* 10.294; 3.437; see the Book 3 note and [[laerces-the-goldsmith]]). Merry saw in the picture a fair forehead framed by auburn hair (on 6.232). For the teachers of the craftsman Nitzsch, as Merry reports, quoted Plato's list of the gods' gifts to men, fire from Prometheus and the arts from Hephaestus and his fellow-craftsman (on 6.233; Plato, *Statesman* 274c–d). The same verses return when Athena pours grace on Odysseus after his bath in his own house (6.230–234 = 23.157–161).
