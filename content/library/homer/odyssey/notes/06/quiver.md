---
summary: The Phaeacians care nothing for bow or quiver, Nausicaa says, but for ships (6.270).
---

“For the Phaeacians care nothing for bow or quiver, / but for masts and the oars of ships and balanced ships” (6.270–271; see [[bow]], [[phaeacians]] and [[ships]]). The quiver goes with the bow as the weapons of the archer, Apollo's “bow on his shoulders and his close-covered quiver” in the first book of the Iliad (*Iliad* 1.45; see the entry). The bow of Iphitus and its quiver, full of arrows, wait in the storeroom of Odysseus until the day of the contest (21.11–12, 21.59–60, a plain rendering; see [[the-storeroom-of-odysseus]]).
