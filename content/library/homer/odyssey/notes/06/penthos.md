---
summary: Odysseus ends the praise of Nausicaa with his own state: “and hard grief has come upon me” (6.169).
---

χαλεπὸν δέ με πένθος ἱκάνει (6.169): the half-verse turns the speech from wonder to the plea, and what follows is the cause of the grief, twenty days at sea and a god who has cast him ashore to suffer more (6.170–174; see [[algos]] and [[daimon]]). πένθος in the Odyssey is often the grief that comes upon a person, as on Penelope when she hears the song of the returns, a grief that will not be forgotten (1.342), and here it is joined to the fear of clasping the girl's knees (6.168–169; see [[deos]] and [[supplication]]).
