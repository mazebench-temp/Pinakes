summary: The girls' meal by the river, told in short: “they took their meal then beside the banks of the river” (6.97), and “when they had taken their fill of food, the slave women and she herself” (6.99), the game and the song and dance follow (6.100–101); and the stranger is given food and drink and eats greedily (6.246–250).
refs: [6.97-101, 6.246-250]
---

6.99 is the verse of the meal's end in a new form: αὐτὰρ ἐπεί opens it as it opens the common verse, “But when they had put away the desire for drink and food” (1.150), and τάρφθεν, “had their fill,” is a form of the verb of Calypso's meal (5.201). As song and dance follow the suitors' feast, “the accompaniments of a feast” (1.150–152), so μολπή, “the song and dance,” follows the meal by the river (6.101; [[ball]], [[feast]]). The stranger's meal is a guest's: Nausicaa bids her handmaids give him food and drink before he is led to the city (6.209, 6.246–248; [[xenia]], [[fasting]]).
