---
summary: The two handmaids who sleep by Nausicaa's door “had their beauty from the Graces” (6.18).
---

**Beauty from the Graces.** Athena finds Nausicaa asleep in her chamber, “and beside her two handmaids, who had their beauty from the Graces, / one on either side, by the doorposts” (6.18–19; see [[handmaids]] and [[nausicaa]]). The Graces are the measure of beauty, as Euphorbus' hair was “like the Graces'” (*Iliad* 17.51), and they give it: Χαρίτων ἄπο κάλλος ἔχουσαι is said of the maids, and when Nausicaa takes her leave of Odysseus in Book 8 she is θεῶν ἄπο κάλλος ἔχουσα, having her beauty from the gods (8.457). Grace itself, χάρις, comes later in the book, when Athena pours it on Odysseus after his bath and he sits on the shore “shining with beauty and grace” (6.235–237; see [[charis]] and [[athena]]).
