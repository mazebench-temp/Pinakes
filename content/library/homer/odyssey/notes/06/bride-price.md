---
summary: Odysseus, praising Nausicaa, calls most blessed of all the suitor “who prevails with his bride-price and leads you to his home” (6.158–159).
grc: [ἐέδνοισι]
en: [bride-price]
---

The felicitation of Nausicaa's parents and brothers rises to the man who will marry her: “But he in turn is the most blessed in his heart, beyond all others, / who prevails with his bride-price and leads you to his home” (6.158–159; see [[three-times-blessed]] and [[marriage]]). ἐέδνοισι βρίσας is “having prevailed by his gifts”: σέ is governed by ἀγάγηται, not by βρίσας, since βρίθω is always intransitive in Homer, and Eustathius explains, “prevailing over the other suitors by the number of his bride-gifts” (W. W. Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 6.159; see the translation's table of key words). The picture is the one the suitors of Penelope will be asked to accept, each wooing her from his own house with gifts and she marrying the man who gives the most (16.390–392). Nausicaa has such suitors, the best of the Phaeacians (6.34–35), “many and noble” (6.284; see [[the-talk-of-the-people]]). Her father will make a different offer to the stranger, his daughter with a house and possessions from himself, if he will stay (7.311–315), as Agamemnon offered Achilles a daughter without bride-price and gifts besides (*Iliad* 9.146–148).
