summary: Athena herself comes to Nausicaa's bed “like a breath of wind,” “stood above her head and spoke to her, / in the likeness of the daughter of Dymas, famed for ships,” rebukes her for her slackness, bids her wash her clothes at dawn, and goes away to Olympus; at dawn the girl wakes, and “at once she marveled at the dream” (6.13–49).

  - 6.13-50 | Athena's dream for Nausicaa
---

The parts of the type-scene are all here: the sleeper in her chamber, with the doors closed (6.15–19); the coming, “like a breath of wind” (6.20); the station above the head (6.21); the likeness of a friend of her own age (6.22–24); the speech, which opens with a reproach, as the dreams of the Iliad open with “You are asleep” (*Iliad* 2.23): “Nausicaa, how did your mother bear you so slack?” (6.25); the departure (6.41–47); and the waking (6.48–50). Unlike the phantom of Book 4, this dream is the goddess herself (4.795–841), and it is the dawn that wakes the girl (6.48). Merry renders ἀπεθαύμασε “was lost in wonder at” (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 6.49). See [[dream]], [[athena]], [[famed-for-ships]] and [[nausicaa]].
