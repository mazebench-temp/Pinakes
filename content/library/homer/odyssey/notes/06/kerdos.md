# κερδαλέον (6.148; again 15.451).
grc: [κερδαλέον]
---

**The better way.** The verse that ended Odysseus' deliberation on the shore the night before ends this one, ὣς ἄρα οἱ φρονέοντι δοάσσατο κέρδιον εἶναι (6.145 = 5.474; see [[decision-scene]]). κέρδιον is the course that gains more: to beg with soothing words, standing apart, rather than clasp the girl's knees and anger her (6.141–147; see [[supplication]]).

**A shrewd word.** αὐτίκα μειλίχιον καὶ κερδαλέον φάτο μῦθον (6.148). Through the sense of gain-getting, Merry notes, the derivatives of κέρδος take almost any coloring, from the wise counsel of a goddess, as Athena led Hector on κερδοσύνῃ, “by craft” (*Iliad* 22.247), to the selfishness of Agamemnon, κερδαλεόφρων, “with your mind set on gain” (*Iliad* 1.149), and the craft of Sisyphus, “the craftiest of men” (*Iliad* 6.153); the fox is κερδαλέη and κερδώ in Pindar and Archilochus (W. W. Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 6.148). Here the word is praise: the speech that follows wins what he needs, the city and the clothes, by praise of the girl, wonder, and an account of his sufferings that asks for pity (6.149–185; see [[pity]] and [[three-times-blessed]]).
