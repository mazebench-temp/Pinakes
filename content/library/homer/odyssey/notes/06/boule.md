summary: Nausicaa meets her father going out “to join the famous kings / at the council, to which the noble Phaeacians had called him” (6.54–55), and asks for the wagon, since it is fitting for him, among the foremost, “to take counsel with clean clothes on your body” (6.60–61).
# βουλεύειν (6.61; again 12.58).
grc: [βουλεύειν]
en: [council]
---

The Phaeacian council meets at dawn, and the king goes out to it as his daughter comes to ask her favor: ἐς βουλήν, ἵνα μιν κάλεον Φαίηκες ἀγαυοί (6.55; see [[kings]] and [[phaeacians]]). She makes the council part of her reason: “And for you yourself it is fitting, when you are among the foremost, / to take counsel with clean clothes on your body” (6.60–61; see [[alcinous]] and [[wagon]]). βουλὰς βουλεύειν is the cognate accusative, which names the sphere in which the verb's action works rather than an object it passes over to, as “sleep one's sleep”: a usage chiefly of poetry, which avoids common verbs and gains emphasis by it (W. W. Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 6.61). The manuscripts divide between the accusatives ἐόντα, ἔχοντα and the datives ἐόντι, ἔχοντι (see the translation's note on 6.60–61).
