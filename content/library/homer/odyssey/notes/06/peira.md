# πειρήσοντα (6.134).
grc: [πειρήσοντα]
en: [make trial, make an attempt]
---

The soliloquy ends in a decision to find out: ἀλλʼ ἄγʼ ἐγὼν αὐτὸς πειρήσομαι ἠδὲ ἴδωμαι, “But come, I will make trial myself and see” (6.126). πειρήσομαι may be the aorist subjunctive, parallel to ἴδωμαι, or more likely the future, a plain statement of what he is going to do, followed by the mood of intention (W. W. Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 6.126). The narrator answers with a simile in which the trial is a lion's: rained on and blown by the wind, he goes after the cattle or the sheep, “and his belly bids him / make an attempt on the sheep, and go even into the close-built fold” (μήλων πειρήσοντα, 6.130–134; see [[the-hungry-lion]] and [[lion]]), as Odysseus, naked, was about to come among the girls, “for need had come upon him” (6.135–136). Merry renders “to make an attack on the sheep”, and the point of the comparison, he says, is that both are pushed by hunger into an act of unusual boldness (on 6.133, 6.135; see [[fasting]]).
