---
summary: Odysseus addresses Nausicaa as ἄνασσα, “queen”: “I implore you, queen: are you some goddess, or a mortal?” (6.149) and “But pity me, queen” (6.175).
grc: [ἄνασσα]
---

γουνοῦμαί σε, ἄνασσα (6.149): the title is a goddess's. Merry notes that Homer uses it elsewhere only of Demeter (*Iliad* 14.326) and of Athena, whom Nestor prays to as “queen” at Pylos (3.380), and sees a special compliment in its use of a woman (W. W. Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 6.149; see the translation's table of key words). It suits the question that follows, whether she is a goddess or a mortal (6.149–153; see [[divine-epiphany]] and [[artemis]]), and it comes back with the plea, ἀλλά, ἄνασσʼ, ἐλέαιρε, “But pity me, queen” (6.175), the words he spoke to the river god with ἄναξ, “lord” (5.450; see [[pity]] and [[supplication]]). He also calls her γύναι, “lady” (6.168). The narrator's word for her is βασίλεια, “the princess” (6.115; see [[basileus]]).
