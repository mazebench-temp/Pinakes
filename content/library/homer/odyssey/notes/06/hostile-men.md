summary: Odysseus wishes Nausicaa a marriage of one mind, “many sorrows to their enemies, / and joy to their well-wishers” (6.184–185); and she asks her handmaids, “Surely you do not think that he is one of the hostile men?” (6.200).
grc: [δυσμενέεσσι, "δυσμενέων φάσθʼ ἔμμεναι ἀνδρῶν"]
---

The antithesis of 6.184–185, sorrow to enemies and joy to friends, is Hector's in reverse, when he calls Paris “a joy to your enemies” (*Iliad* 3.51; [[husbands-and-wives]]). Nausicaa answers her own question: no man will come to the Phaeacians' land bringing combat, for they are very dear to the immortals (6.200–203; [[phaeacians]]).
