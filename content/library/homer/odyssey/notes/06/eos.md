**Dawn wakes the girl.** In the dream Athena bids Nausicaa go to the washing “when dawn appears”, and urge her father “in the early dawn” to make ready the mules and the wagon (6.31, 6.36; see [[dream]] and [[athena]]). The goddess has hardly gone back to Olympus when the day comes: “And at once Dawn of the fair throne came, and she woke her, / well-robed Nausicaa; and at once she marveled at the dream” (6.48–49; see [[nausicaa]] and [[of-the-fair-throne]]). ἐύθρονος is given to Dawn once in the Iliad, in the night of the Trojan watch-fires, when the horses stood beside the chariots “and waited for Dawn of the fair throne” (*Iliad* 8.564–565), and five times in the Odyssey; the verse comes again, nearly as here, when Telemachus' ship comes to land on Ithaca, αἶψα γὰρ Ἠὼς ἦλθεν ἐΰθρονος (15.495). The day she opens is the thirty-second of the poem; it ends at sunset at the grove of Athena (6.321; see [[helios]]).
