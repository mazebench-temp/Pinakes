---
summary: Nausicaa's speech to her handmaids begins “Hear me, white-armed handmaids, so that I may tell you something” (6.239); Odysseus' prayer begins “Hear me, child of aegis-bearing Zeus, Atrytone” (6.324) and ends “So he spoke in prayer, and Pallas Athena heard him” (6.328).
grc: ["κλῦτέ μευ"]
---

κλῦτέ μευ is a scholium's reading; the manuscripts have κλῦτέ μοι (see the translation's note on 6.239). 6.324 is Penelope's verse (4.762) and 6.328 the close of Nestor's prayer (3.385); this time the goddess who hears does not yet appear (6.329–331; [[prayer-type-scene]]).
