summary: Odysseus' prayer in the grove of Athena at the end of the book (6.323–327), which she hears but does not yet answer openly; and the god of the gossip Nausicaa imagines, come to her "at her prayer, much prayed for" (6.280).
refs: [6.325-327]
---

The book ends with a prayer. At sunset, in the grove sacred to Athena, Odysseus "at once then … prayed to the daughter of great Zeus" (6.321–323; see [[the-grove-of-athena]] and [[athena]]). It calls on her with the verse of Penelope's prayer, "Hear me, child of aegis-bearing Zeus, Atrytone" (6.324 = 4.762; see [[hear-me]] and [[atrytone]]); it gives her a reason, which is also a reproach, "listen to me now at least, since before you never listened / to me when I was being wrecked" (6.325–326); and it asks, "Grant that I come to the Phaeacians as one welcomed and pitied" (6.327), in the words of Priam's prayer before he goes to Achilles (*Iliad* 24.309; see [[welcomed-and-pitied]] and [[pity]]). "So he spoke in prayer, and Pallas Athena heard him" (6.328 = 3.385), but she did not yet appear to him face to face, for she respected her father's brother (6.329–330; see [[poseidon]] and [[seeing-the-gods]]).

Earlier, the gossip Nausicaa imagines has a god come down from the sky "at her prayer, much prayed for" (6.280; εὐξαμένῃ, πολυάρητος; see [[the-talk-of-the-people]]).
