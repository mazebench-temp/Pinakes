---
summary: Athena “made the thick hair fall, like the hyacinth flower” (6.231).
grc: ["οὔλας ἧκε κόμας"]
en: [thick hair]
---

Merry takes οὖλαι κόμαι as the thick hair of a man in his vigor, and finds the likeness to the hyacinth in the clustering flowers with curling petals, not in the color (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 6.231; see the translation's note on 6.231). The verse returns when Athena makes him beautiful after his bath at home (23.158; [[hyacinth]], [[gold-poured-around-silver]]).
