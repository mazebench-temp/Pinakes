---
summary: The river that took Odysseus in becomes the place of the washing. Nausicaa drives the clothes to its troughs, the girls play beside it, the ball falls into one of its eddies, and Odysseus washes the brine from his body in its stream, “where there is shelter from the wind” (6.59, 6.85–117, 6.210–225).
# The river of the washing, unnamed: rivers claims ποταμός; the eddy of the ball (6.116) and the dark water (6.91).
refs: [6.59, 6.85-97, 6.116, 6.210, 6.216, 6.224, 6.317]
---

**The washing.** Nausicaa asks her father for the wagon to take the clothes “to the river to wash them” (6.59), and the girls drive there: “And when they came to the beautiful stream of the river, / where the washing troughs were, unfailing, and much fair water / welled up and flowed through, to clean even what is very dirty” (6.85–87; see [[washing-troughs]] and [[nausicaa]]). The troughs, Merry thinks, were tanks dug at the side of the river, open to it above and below, so that its water was always passing in and out of them: in ὑπεκπρόρεεν it wells up from beneath, passes on, and flows out again (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 6.86). The mules are turned loose to graze “along the eddying river” (6.89; see [[eddying]] and [[mules]]), the girls carry the clothes “into the dark water” and tread them in the pits (6.91–92), and after spreading them on the shore of the sea they eat “beside the banks of the river” (6.94–97; see [[shore]]). The ball that wakes Odysseus falls into “a deep eddy” of the stream (6.116; see [[ball]]).

**The bath.** Nausicaa bids her handmaids bathe the stranger “in the river, where there is shelter from the wind” (6.210), echoing the verse in which the river first received him, “smooth, free of rocks, and there was shelter from the wind” (5.443). The handmaids leave the clothes and the oil and bid him bathe “in the streams of the river” (6.216); Nausicaa had told them to bathe him themselves (Merry on 6.216). He asks them to stand apart and washes the brine from his skin ἐκ ποταμοῦ, “with the river water” (6.224), as men are washed ἐκ τρίποδος, with water from the tripod (10.361; Merry on 6.224; see [[bathing]]). At the end of the day the mules “quickly left the streams of the river” (6.317). The god of the river, who heard Odysseus' prayer the day before (5.445–453), plays no part in this book.
