---
summary: Athena plans “that Odysseus should wake and see the fair-faced girl” (6.113), and he ponders whether to “clasp the knees of the fair-faced girl and beg her” (6.142).
---

The epithet marks the two moments of the meeting: the goddess' design, and the stranger's doubt how to approach the girl she has brought to him ([[thought-of-another-thing]], [[decision-scene]], [[nausicaa]]).
