**His daughter's likeness.** The goddess does not come to Nausicaa as herself. She takes the shape of a girl's friend, “who was of an age with her, and dear to her heart” (6.23), and scolds her as a friend would, offering to go with her to the washing and help (6.25–40; see [[nausicaa]], [[athena]] and [[dream-scene]]). ὁμηλικίη, “of an age”, stands for the person, an age-mate, as when Peisistratus says of Telemachus that he is “of an age with me myself” (3.49; Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 6.23). In τῇ μιν ἐεισαμένη προσέφη γλαυκῶπις Ἀθήνη, “In her likeness gray-eyed Athena spoke to her” (6.24), μιν is the object of προσέφη, not a reflexive with ἐεισαμένη (Merry on 6.24). So Athena came to Telemachus in the likeness of Mentes and of Mentor (1.105, 2.268) and sent Penelope a phantom in the likeness of her sister Iphthime (4.795–798; see [[iphthime]]), and in the next book she meets Odysseus at the city in the likeness of a young Phaeacian girl carrying a pitcher (7.19–20, Greek only). Dymas himself does not appear.
