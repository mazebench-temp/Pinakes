---
summary: The sun marks the day of Book 6: the washed clothes dry “in the rays of the sun” while the girls eat and play (6.98), and “the sun set” as the wagon, the handmaids and Odysseus come to the grove of Athena outside the city (6.321).
---

**The rays of the sun.** When the clothes are washed and spread on the shore, the girls bathe and eat, “and waited for the clothes to dry in the rays of the sun” (6.98; see [[shore]] and [[washing-troughs]]). ἠελίοιο … αὐγῇ, “the rays of the sun”, is the sunlight of the living, as the newborn Eudorus, brought into the light, “saw the rays of the sun” (*Iliad* 16.188). μένον τερσήμεναι, “waited for the clothes to dry”, has the turn of the suitors who “waited for evening to come” (μένον δʼ ἐπὶ ἕσπερον ἐλθεῖν, 1.422; W. W. Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 6.98). The day of the washing is the whole of Book 6 after the dream, from dawn (6.48; see [[eos]]) to sunset.

**Sunset.** “And the sun set, and they came to the famous grove, / sacred to Athena, where brilliant Odysseus sat down” (6.321–322; see [[the-grove-of-athena]]). δύσετό τʼ ἠέλιος, “and the sun set”, joined to the next clause by καί, is said again when the Phaeacians' gifts are brought to Odysseus at evening, δύσετό τʼ ἠέλιος, καὶ τῷ κλυτὰ δῶρα παρῆεν (8.417), and is the first half of the full verse of nightfall, δύσετό τʼ ἠέλιος σκιόωντό τε πᾶσαι ἀγυιαί (2.388, 3.487, 3.497; see [[sunset-lines]]). Nausicaa had told Odysseus to wait in the grove until she and her handmaids had reached her father's house (6.295–299); he sits down and prays to Athena as the light goes (6.322–328; see [[prayer]]), and goes into the city at the beginning of the next book.
