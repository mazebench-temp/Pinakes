summary: When the ball is thrown, the narrator calls Nausicaa βασίλεια, “the princess”: “So then the princess flung the ball to one of her handmaids” (6.115).
en: [princess]
---

σφαῖραν ἔπειτʼ ἔρριψε μετʼ ἀμφίπολον βασίλεια (6.115): βασίλεια is elsewhere “queen”, of Penelope, “the much-wooed queen” (4.770), and the translation renders it “princess” for the king's unmarried daughter (see its table of key words). The word gives her rank at the moment of the throw that brings the stranger out of the bushes (6.115–117; see [[ball]] and [[nausicaa]]). Her father goes out that morning to join “the famous kings” at the council (6.54–55; see [[kings]] and [[boule]]), and Odysseus addresses her not as βασίλεια but with the gods' title, ἄνασσα (6.149, 6.175; see [[anax]]).
