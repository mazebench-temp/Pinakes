**At the hearth.** When Nausicaa goes through the house to tell her dream, “Her mother was sitting at the hearth with her handmaids, / turning the sea-purple yarn” (6.52–53; see [[hearth]] and [[handmaids]]). ἠλάκατα is the yarn spun off from the distaff, and ἁλιπόρφυρα is commonly taken as dyed with the purple of the sea, perhaps the famous Phoenician dye of the murex (W. W. Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 6.53; see [[distaff]] and [[purple]]). It is the queen's industry that Athena's taunt sets against her daughter's idleness, “how did your mother bear you so slack?” (6.25; Merry on 6.25). She makes ready the outing: “and her mother put in a box food to satisfy the heart, / of every kind, and put in meats, and poured wine / in a goatskin”, and gave her olive oil in a golden flask for the bath (6.76–80; see [[oil-flask]]).

**The knees to clasp.** Nausicaa's instructions to Odysseus end with her mother:

{{quote:c52f3f76-3743-5ac1-b854-7043f05a51ee}}

She is seated, not standing, with her chair against the pillar, as the herald sets the singer's chair against a tall pillar at the feast of Book 8 (8.65–66); αὐτῇ, “against hers”, is her seat, and the king's chair stands somewhat nearer the door (Merry on 6.307–308; see the translation's note on 6.307–309 and [[throne]]). The stranger is to go right through the hall to the upper end, where the king and queen will be found sitting (Merry on 6.303), and to pass the king by. The last three verses, which promise him his homecoming if she is friendly, are wanting in several manuscripts and are generally rejected by modern editors as adding nothing new; their place is 7.75–77, where Athena says them (see the translation's note on 6.313–315). In the next book Athena too sends him to the queen first, and he clasps her knees (7.53–77, 7.139–145; see [[supplication]]). Merry explains that a stranger was regarded as the suppliant of the person with whom he first established relations, so that Arete can later say of him, “he is my guest” (on 7.53, citing 11.338).
