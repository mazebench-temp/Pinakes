summary: Athena tells Nausicaa that “the best men in the land are wooing you, / of all the Phaeacians” (6.34–35); Nausicaa tells the stranger that at her father's house he will come to know “all the Phaeacians who are the best” (6.257); and Odysseus says there is nothing “stronger or better” than one mind in a house (6.182).
# ἀριστῆες (6.34; again 15.28), ἄρειον "better" (6.182; again 23.114, 23.286).
grc: [ἀριστῆες, ἄρειον]
---

The best of the Phaeacians are the frame of Nausicaa's day. Athena's dream names them as her suitors, ἀριστῆες κατὰ δῆμον, “the best men in the land”, of her own people (6.34–35; see [[marriage]] and [[genee]]); the gossip she fears calls them “many and noble” and has her slight them for a stranger (6.283–284; see [[the-talk-of-the-people]]); and she promises the stranger that in her father's house he will come to know “all the Phaeacians who are the best” (6.255–257). For εἰδησέμεν, “you will come to know”, Merry compares Alcinous' boast that the stranger will learn for himself how far his ships and young men are the best at throwing up the sea with the oar (7.327–328, a plain rendering; W. W. Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 6.257). The comparative belongs to the blessing of marriage: nothing is κρεῖσσον καὶ ἄρειον, “stronger or better”, than a man and a woman of one mind (6.182; see [[nothing-better-than-one-mind-in-a-house]]).
