---
summary: Woken by the girls' cry, Odysseus asks the three verses, “Ah me, to the land of what mortals have I come this time?” (6.119–121), and wonders whether he hears nymphs or men (6.122–126).
---

The question is the first thing he says among the Phaeacians, and the book answers it: the girl who stands her ground tells him the name of her people (6.194–197), and tells her handmaids, in his hearing, that the Phaeacians are very dear to the immortals and that strangers and beggars are from Zeus (6.199–208; [[strangers-and-beggars-are-from-zeus]], [[phaeacians]]). For the doubt between nymphs and men, and the verses suspected after it, see the translation's note on 6.122–125 ([[with-human-voice]], [[nymphs]]).
