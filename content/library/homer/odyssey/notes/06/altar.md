# βωμῷ (6.162), first met in the Odyssey here (the Iliad's, 8.249, is in its Book 8 note).
grc: [βωμῷ]
---

“Once in Delos, beside the altar of Apollo, such a thing / I saw, a young sapling of a palm tree springing up” (6.162–163; see [[delos]], [[apollo]], [[palm]] and [[the-palm-on-delos]]). The altar is the only one of the book. The visit to Delos would belong to the voyage from Aulis to Troy, “on that journey on which I was to have evil sorrows” (6.164–165), and the scholia refer the palm to the first palm, by which Leto gave birth to her children (see the translation's note on 6.162–163). The only other βωμῷ in Homer is the altar of Zeus by which the eagle drops the fawn in the Iliad (*Iliad* 8.249).
