summary: The book of a girl whose marriage is near. Athena tells Nausicaa in a dream, “your marriage is near”, and the best of the Phaeacians are wooing her (6.27–35); she is ashamed to name it to her father (6.66). Odysseus calls most blessed the man who will lead her home, and wishes her a husband and a home (6.158–159, 6.180–185); she wishes for such a husband herself (6.244–245), and fears the talk of a girl who mingles with men before she comes openly to her marriage (6.288).
# γάμος (6.27; again 1.226, 18.272), ὀπυίοντες "married" (6.63), the brothers.

---

**The dream.** The goddess who sends Nausicaa to the washing gives her a bride's reason: “your marriage is near, when you must wear fine clothes yourself / and provide others for those who will lead you home” (6.27–28). τὰ δέ answers καλὰ μέν, as if καλὰ δέ had been written: fine clothes for herself, and others to give (W. W. Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 6.27; see the translation's note on 6.27–28). ἄγωνται, “will lead you home”, may refer to the family into which she marries, or rather, Merry thinks, to the procession in which the bridegroom and his friends brought the bride to her new home by torchlight, to whose splendor she could add by giving fine clothes to those who took part (on 6.28). He cites the weddings on the shield of Achilles, where they led the brides from their chambers through the city under blazing torches (*Iliad* 18.491–493), and the city on the Hesiodic shield of Heracles, where men bring a bride to her husband on a well-wheeled wagon, with the bridal song loud and the light of torches far off (Hesiod, *Shield* 272–275). The marriage is the reason for the whole day: “since you will not be a maiden much longer; / for already the best men in the land are wooing you, / of all the Phaeacians, where you yourself have your birth” (6.33–35). Merry takes ὅθι with δῆμον: Athena reminds her that her suitors are the noblest of her own people (on 6.35; see [[genee]]).

**A blooming marriage.** Nausicaa asks her father for the wagon without a word of it, “for she was ashamed to name her blooming marriage / to her dear father” (6.66–67; see [[aidos]] and [[alcinous]]). θαλερός is an epithet of young men in the flower of their age, and may be transferred to γάμος, “marriage in her maiden-prime”, or be a fixed epithet in the sense of “fruitful” (Merry on 6.66). The house has its married and its unmarried: of her five brothers “two married, and three young unmarried men in their bloom” (ὀπυίοντες, ἠίθεοι θαλέθοντες, 6.62–63; see [[kasignetos]]), and she is the only daughter of the house (Merry on 6.65), “a maiden unwed” (παρθένος ἀδμής, 6.109, 6.228; see [[unbroken]]).

**Marriage in a stranger's mouth.** Odysseus' plea turns on her marriage. Three times blessed are her parents and brothers, “But he in turn is the most blessed in his heart, beyond all others, / who prevails with his bride-price and leads you to his home” (6.158–159; see [[bride-price]] and [[three-times-blessed]]), and he ends wishing her “a husband and a home” and a noble oneness of mind (6.180–185; see [[nothing-better-than-one-mind-in-a-house]] and [[husbands-and-wives]]). When Athena has made him beautiful she says to her handmaids what she would not say to her father, “Would that such a man might be called my husband, / living here, and that it might please him to stay here” (6.244–245; see [[posis]]).

**Openly.** The marriage must be made in the sight of the people. The gossip she fears would make the stranger her husband, or a god come at her prayer, and she would be indignant herself with a girl who, against the will of her father and mother, “mingled with men before she came openly to her marriage” (ἀμφάδιον γάμον ἐλθεῖν, 6.276–288; see [[the-talk-of-the-people]] and [[nemesis]]). ἀμφάδιος is “openly”, as the adverb ἀμφαδίην (*Iliad* 7.196; see the translation's table of key words). Alcinous will wish that a man like this might have his daughter and be called his son-in-law, staying in Scheria, with a house and possessions from him, but no one will keep him against his will (7.311–315; see [[in-laws]]).
