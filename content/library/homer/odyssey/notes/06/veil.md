summary: When the washing is spread to dry and the meal is over, the girls throw off their veils to play at ball (6.100).
---

“they played with a ball, throwing off their veils” (6.100; see [[ball]] and [[handmaids]]). The κρήδεμνον is the head-cloth that a woman of the house draws before her cheeks when she goes out among men, as Penelope does when she comes down to the suitors (1.334; see the Book 1 note). By the river, far from the city and among girls alone, it is put aside for the game. In the Iliad Hecuba and Andromache cast off their veils in grief when Hector is killed (*Iliad* 22.405–406, 22.468–472; see the entry); here the veils come off in play.
