---
summary: Odysseus ponders “whether he should clasp the knees of the fair-faced girl and beg her, / or, as he was, with soothing words, standing apart, / beg her” (6.142–144), and chooses words: “And as he pondered, this seemed to him the better way” (6.145).
en: [this seemed to him]
passages:
  - 6.141-148 | Odysseus chooses to speak from a distance
---

The alternatives are a double question, ἢ … ἦ (6.142–143); the verse of the choice closes it, as at the river in Book 5 (5.474); and the reason follows: “lest the girl be angry in her heart with him if he clasped her knees” (6.147). Athenocles suspected 6.144 for its repeated λίσσοιτο and because the only question is whether to clasp her knees or speak from a distance (see the translation's note on 6.144).
