---
summary: Nausicaa's brothers want fresh clothes "when they go to the dance" (6.64–65); Odysseus imagines her family watching her "going into the dance" (6.157); and by the river she begins the song and dance (6.101).
---

The dance belongs to the young of Alcinous' house. Nausicaa's three unmarried brothers "always want to have freshly washed clothes / when they go to the dance" (6.64–65), one of the reasons she gives her father for the washing (see [[nausicaa]] and [[clothing]]). Odysseus' praise of her imagines her parents and brothers glad "as they watch such a scion going into the dance" (6.157; see [[three-times-blessed]]).

By the river, after the meal, "among them white-armed Nausicaa began the song and dance" (6.101). The scholia take μολπή here as the game itself; Merry sees no reason to doubt that a measured chant and a dance went with it, the throwing and catching keeping time, as ball play goes with the dance of the Phaeacian youths at 8.371 and following (*Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 6.101; see [[ball]]).
