---
summary: At the river the handmaids lay a mantle and a tunic beside Odysseus for clothing, and he puts them on after his bath (6.214, 6.228).
refs: [6.228]
---

“and beside him they laid a mantle and a tunic for clothing” (6.214; see [[cloak]] and [[handmaids]]), clothes from the washing (see [[washing-troughs]]). The pair is the dress of a bathed guest, as when Polycaste bathes Telemachus at Pylos (3.467; see [[bathing]]), and the tunic goes on first, under the mantle (see the Book 5 note). Odysseus puts them on when he has washed off the brine and anointed himself, “the clothes that the maiden unwed had given him” (6.227–228; see [[nausicaa]]). Arete will know them for her own work when he comes to her hall (7.234–235, a plain rendering).
