---
summary: The doors of Nausicaa's chamber are “shining” (6.19), she brings out “the shining clothing” to wash (6.74), and she lashes the mules “with the shining whip” (6.316).
grc: [φαειναί, φαεινήν, φαεινῇ]
---

Merry sets the shining clothing beside the glossy clothes that lie uncared for, both fixed epithets that no contradiction disturbs (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 6.26; [[glossy]]); and he takes φαεινῇ of the whip as alluding to its leather or its handle, polished or colored, comparing the glossy reins (Merry on 6.316; 6.81). The Iliad's drivers have the shining whip too (*Iliad* 10.500, 23.384; [[chariot-journey]]).
