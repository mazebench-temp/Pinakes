---
summary: Odysseus asks Nausicaa whether she is “one of the gods who hold the wide sky” (6.150), and after his bath she says of him, “now he is like the gods who hold the wide sky” (6.243).
---

The verse-end τοὶ οὐρανὸν εὐρὺν ἔχουσιν passes from his mouth to hers: each takes the other for one like the gods. Telemachus says the same of his father when Athena has changed him, first taking him for a god and then setting the old man of a moment before against the godlike man of now, as Nausicaa does (16.183, 16.199–200; 6.242–243; [[divine-epiphany]], [[like-the-immortals]]).
