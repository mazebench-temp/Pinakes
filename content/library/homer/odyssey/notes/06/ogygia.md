# poets, and this note's forms apply only within the Odyssey.

In his supplication Odysseus tells the girl only as much of his story as she needs: “Yesterday, on the twentieth day, I escaped from the wine-dark sea; / and all that time the wave and the swift storm winds were carrying me / from the island of Ogygia” (6.170–172; see [[the-shipwreck-of-odysseus]] and [[wine-dark]]). χθιζός, “yesterday”, has the force of the adverb χθές, and τόφρα is the whole space of twenty days (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 6.170, 6.171). The count is the one Zeus gave when he sent Hermes to Calypso: on a raft, on the twentieth day, he would reach Scheria (5.33–34; see [[calypso]], [[raft]] and [[scheria]]). He names the island but not the nymph, and says nothing of the seven years he spent there; that story waits for the queen's question in the next book, “Who are you among men, and from where? Who gave you these clothes?”, which he answers with “There is an island, Ogygia, lying far off in the sea” (7.237–266; see [[arete-queen-of-the-phaeacians]]).
