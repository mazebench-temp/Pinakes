---
summary: In Nausicaa's description of the city, “the curving ships are drawn up along the road” (6.264).
grc: [ἀμφιέλισσαι]
---

Merry takes νῆες ὁδὸν … εἰρύαται as “are drawn up along the road,” not “guard the road,” since guard ships seem un-Homeric (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 6.264; see the translation's note on 6.262–265). Of the Phaeacians' ships the book has three epithets in eight verses: curving, black and balanced (6.264, 6.268, 6.271; [[the-city-of-the-phaeacians]], [[black-ships]], [[balanced-ships]]).
