---
summary: As the girls make ready to go home, “then the gray-eyed goddess Athena thought of another thing, / that Odysseus should wake and see the fair-faced girl” (6.112–113); and after Odysseus has eaten, “white-armed Nausicaa thought of another thing” (6.251).
grc: ["λευκώλενος ἄλλʼ ἐνόησεν"]
---

The goddess' new thought brings about the meeting, and the ball falls into the eddy (6.112–117); Merry takes ἔπειτα, “So then” (6.115), as the first step in carrying out her intention (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 6.115). The girl's new thought, without αὖτε, starts the journey to the city (6.251–255). See [[fair-faced]], [[white-armed]] and [[gray-eyed]].
