---
summary: Nausicaa sleeps “like the immortals in stature and in looks” (6.16); the Phaeacians are “very dear to the immortals” (6.203); and Alcinous drinks his wine “like an immortal” (6.309).
---

The likenesses run through the book: the girl like the immortals, the stranger like the gods after his bath (6.243), the king on his chair like an immortal; and the people, as Nausicaa says, are dear to the gods ([[like-the-immortals]], [[phaeacians]]).
