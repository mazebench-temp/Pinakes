# ὑβρισταί "arrogant" (6.120 = 9.175 = 13.201; 24.282).
grc: [ὑβρισταί]
en: [arrogant]
---

ὑβριστής is the man of [[hybris|ὕβρις]], and the question sets him against the man who is kind to strangers and fears the gods (6.119–121; see [[xenia]] and [[themis]]). Odysseus asks it on every shore he does not know: of the Cyclopes (9.175–176), of Ithaca when he wakes there (13.200–202), and here (see [[to-the-land-of-what-mortals]]). Laertes will give the answer for Ithaca under the suitors, that arrogant and reckless men hold it (24.282, a plain rendering; see [[the-suitors]]). The first answer on Scheria comes from Nausicaa, who receives him by the rule of Zeus (6.187–210), but the book has its arrogant men too: the overweening Cyclopes, who plundered the Phaeacians in Hypereia and drove them to their new home (6.4–6; see [[cyclopes]] and [[overweening]]), and the overbearing Phaeacians of the town whose talk Nausicaa fears (6.273–274; see [[overbearing]] and [[the-talk-of-the-people]]).
