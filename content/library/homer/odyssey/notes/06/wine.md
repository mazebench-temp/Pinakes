**Wine for the river.** Nausicaa's mother puts food and meats in a box, “and poured wine / in a goatskin” (6.77–78; see [[provisions]] and [[feast]]). The goatskin is the vessel of the wine brought from Troy for the oaths of the duel in the Iliad, “wine that cheers the heart, the fruit of the earth, / in a goatskin” (*Iliad* 3.246–247; see [[oath]]), and Odysseus carries the dark wine of Maron to the Cyclops' cave in a goatskin (9.196–197, a plain rendering). The handmaids give the stranger food and drink when he has bathed (6.246–248).

**The king's wine.** At the hearth “my father's chair leans against hers, / on which he sits and drinks his wine like an immortal” (6.308–309; see [[throne]] and [[alcinous]]); W. W. Merry compares the gods seated beside Zeus while Hebe pours their nectar (*Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 6.309; *Iliad* 4.1–4). The verb οἰνοποτάζω is found again when Telemachus seats the disguised Odysseus in the hall to drink his wine among the men (20.262, a plain rendering), and in the Iliad of the boasts that Aeneas made to the Trojan kings over his wine, which Apollo throws back at him in the likeness of Lycaon (*Iliad* 20.83–85).
