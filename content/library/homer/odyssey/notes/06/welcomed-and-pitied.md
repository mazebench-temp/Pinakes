---
summary: The last verse of the prayer that ends the book, in which Odysseus asks Athena that he may come to the Phaeacians as one welcomed and pitied.
refs: [6.327]
---

The verse ends the last speech of the book. Odysseus has been told where to wait and what to do: to sit in the grove until Nausicaa has reached her father's house, then to go through the hall, pass by the king and throw his arms around the knees of the queen, “so that you may see the day of your homecoming” (6.295–315; see [[supplication]] and [[arete-queen-of-the-phaeacians]]). He asks of the goddess the two things a stranger needs among people he does not know, and he asks them of the one who “never listened” while Poseidon was wrecking him (6.325–326). Athena hears, but the book ends on her reserve, not on an answer: she does not yet appear to him, out of respect for her father's brother (6.328–331; see [[athena]]). The answer is given in the next book, when Alcinous raises him from the ashes of the hearth (7.167–171, a plain rendering). Aristotle quotes the verse as “well said” for the two aims of a speaker's proem (*Rhetoric* 1415b25–28; see [[aristotle]] and [[prooimion]]).
