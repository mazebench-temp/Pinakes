---
summary: Athena rushes like a breath of wind to Nausicaa's bed and stands above her head, in the likeness of a friend, to speak to her in a dream (6.20–23).
---

“And she rushed like a breath of wind to the girl's bed, / and stood above her head and spoke to her” (6.20–21; see [[athena]], [[dream-scene]] and [[nausicaa]]). δέμνια, always plural in Homer, is properly the bedstead (W. W. Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 4.297; see the Book 4 note). The girl sleeps in her chamber with two handmaids by the doorposts (6.15–19; see [[house]] and [[door]]). Odysseus has slept the same night on his bed of leaves under the olives (5.482–493, 6.1–2; see the Book 5 note).
