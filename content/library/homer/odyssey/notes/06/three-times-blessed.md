# τρὶς μάκαρες (5.306, of the dead Danaans; 6.154, 6.155); μακάρτατος (6.158; again 11.483, Odysseus to Achilles).
grc: ["τρὶς μάκαρες", μακάρτατος]
en: [three times blessed, the most blessed]

**The felicitation.** Odysseus has asked whether the girl is a goddess and likened her to Artemis (6.149–152; see [[artemis]]); if she is mortal, he goes on, her family is blessed in her, and the blessing rises by degrees, from her parents and her brothers, whose hearts are always warmed with gladness as they watch her go into the dance, to the husband who will lead her home, “the most blessed in his heart, beyond all others” (6.153–159; see [[bride-price]] and [[marriage]]). Merry notes the plural of the abstract noun in ἐυφροσύνῃσιν, “with gladness”, and the genitive λευσσόντων after the dative σφισι, a construction on its way to the absolute genitive (W. W. Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 6.156, 6.157). Priam praises the young man who meets him on the way to Achilles in the same way, admirable in build and looks, and come of blessed parents (*Iliad* 24.376–377), not knowing that he is Hermes.

**From the dead to the living.** Three days before, in the storm, the same words were his cry for the dead: “Three times blessed are the Danaans, and four times, who perished then / in wide Troy” (5.306–307). Now the felicitation that envied the dead their fame praises the living for their happiness, and the man who envied the dead asks the living for a rag to throw around him (6.178–179; see [[supplication]]). He will give the superlative once more, to Achilles in the underworld: no man before or after was more blessed than he (11.482–483). μάκαρ of men is “blessed”, as of a rich man whom old age overtakes among his possessions (1.217; see the translation's table of key words, and [[olbos]] for ὄλβιος, which the shade of Agamemnon uses of Achilles, 24.36).
