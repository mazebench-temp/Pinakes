---
summary: The first of the things that the wagon is to carry to the washing are ζῶστρα, “sashes” (6.38), a word found only here in Homer.
---

The wagon is to carry “the sashes and the robes and the glossy blankets” (6.38; see [[wagon]], [[robe]], [[blankets]] and [[washing-troughs]]). ζῶστρον is formed from ζώννυμι, “gird”, as ζώνη, the woman's girdle, and ζωστήρ, the warrior's belt, are (see the entry and [[belt]]): a thing to gird oneself with. The translation renders it “sashes” and keeps it apart from ζώνη, “girdle”, the golden girdle of Calypso in the book before (5.231; see the translation's row ζῶστρα). The poem does not say whose they are; the washing is the household's, the clothes of the king and of his sons as well as the girl's own (6.57–65).
