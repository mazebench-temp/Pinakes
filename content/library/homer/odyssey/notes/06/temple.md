# νηούς (6.10), the only instance of the form in Homer; νηός "temple" is easily confused with νηός "of a ship" (6.278).
grc: [νηοὺς]
en: [temples]
---

“and he drove a wall around the city, and built houses, / and made temples of the gods, and divided the fields” (6.9–10; see [[nausithous]], [[the-city-of-the-phaeacians]] and [[house]]). The temples are among the first works of the founder, with the wall, the houses and the allotment of the land (see the translation's row ἐδάσσατʼ ἀρούρας). The one sacred place of the city that Nausicaa names is the precinct of Poseidon at the place of assembly (6.266; see [[poseidon]]). In the later books Odysseus' companions, when they mean to kill the cattle of the Sun, promise to build him a rich temple in Ithaca if they come home (12.345–347, a plain rendering; see [[the-cattle-of-helios]]).
