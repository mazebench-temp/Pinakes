---
summary: The companions killed at Ismarus are left “on the plain” where they fell, and the ships do not sail on until each has been called three times (9.64–66). The six whom the Cyclops eats leave nothing to bury (9.292–293).
refs: [9.64-66, 9.293]
---

The dead of the voyage cannot be given what the dead are owed. At Ismarus the survivors flee with six men of each ship lying “on the plain, cut down by the Cicones” (9.60–66; see [[cicones]]); the one rite left to them is the call, three times to each of the dead, before the ships go on (see [[the-call-to-the-dead]]). The companions eaten in the cave are consumed entirely: the Cyclops ate “like a mountain-bred lion, and left nothing, / entrails and flesh and marrowy bones” (9.292–293; see [[the-hungry-lion]] and [[polyphemus-the-cyclops]]), the fate the Iliad's heroes fear for their bodies, to be eaten by dogs and birds (*Iliad* 1.4–5; see the entry). At the ship the companions who waited lament them, and Odysseus forbids the weeping (9.467–470; see [[lament]] and [[tears]]). In Book 12 the voyage will pause for a funeral, when the dead Elpenor is burned and given a mound with his oar on it (12.8–15).
