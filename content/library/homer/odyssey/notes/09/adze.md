---
summary: In the simile of the blinding a smith dips “a great axe or an adze” in cold water to temper it (9.391–393).
---

The adze is named for the second and last time in Homer in the simile of the smith: “And as when a smith dips a great axe or an adze / in cold water, and it hisses loudly, / to temper it; for this in turn is the strength of iron” (9.391–393; see [[the-smith-tempering-an-axe]], [[axe]] and [[iron]]). The two tools are the pair that Calypso gave Odysseus for building his raft (5.234–237; see the Book 5 note); here they come from the smith's forge. The participle ἰάχοντα agrees with πέλεκυν only, and ἠὲ σκέπαρνον is thrown in as if in parenthesis (W. W. Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 9.392).
