---
summary: The island of goats stretches out beyond the harbor of the Cyclopes' land (9.116), and it has a harbor of its own, “a harbor with good anchorage, where there is no need of a cable”, with a spring of bright water at its head (9.136–141), where the fleet lands in the dark and waits while Odysseus goes to the Cyclops (9.142–151, 9.543–546).
---

“In it is a harbor with good anchorage, where there is no need of a cable, / neither to cast out mooring-stones nor to make fast stern-cables, / but men may run their ships ashore and wait a while, until the sailors' / hearts urge them on, and the breezes blow fair” (9.136–139; see [[the-island-of-goats]], [[mooring-stones]] and [[stern-cables]]). λιμὴν ἐύορμος, “a harbor with good anchorage”, is the harbor of Menelaus' Pharos too (4.358; see the translation's table of key words). πεῖσμα, “a cable”, is here the mooring tackle in general, which the verse divides into the cable from the bows to the anchor-stones and the stern-cables between the stern and the shore (W. W. Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 9.136), and after the negative οὐ χρεώ the affirmative is understood: all that is needful is to run ashore and wait (Merry on 9.138). “And at the head of the harbor flows bright water, / a spring from under a cave; and around it poplars grow” (9.140–141; see [[trees]]): ἐπὶ κρατός is “at the head of the harbor” (Merry on 9.140), the words with which the harbor of Phorcys on Ithaca is described, where ships ride without a mooring rope and a long-leaved olive stands at the head (13.96–102; see [[the-harbor-of-phorcys]]).

The harbor serves the story twice. The ships run ashore there in the dark, on a moonless night, without seeing the island or the waves (9.142–151; see [[moon]]), and it is there that the other well-benched ships wait, with the companions sitting around them in mourning, when Odysseus comes back from the cave (9.543–546; see [[shore]]).
