---
summary: Two offerings: in the cave, “There we kindled a fire and made an offering” (9.231), of the cheeses; and on the shore of the island the ram, given to Odysseus alone: “to Zeus, lord of the dark clouds, son of Cronus, who rules over all, / I sacrificed him and burned the thigh-pieces; but he took no heed of the offering” (9.552–553).
refs: [9.231, 9.550-555]
---

The first is an offering of firstfruits, ἐθύσαμεν in the sense Merry gives θύειν, not the slaughter of a victim (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 9.231; see the Odyssey translation's key words, θύω). The second is told in a verse and a half, the victim, the burning of the thighs and the god's refusal, which Merry explains by the prayer of Polyphemus, already heard and to be answered (Merry on 9.553; [[sacrifice]], [[thigh-pieces]], [[lord-of-dark-clouds]], [[ram]]).
