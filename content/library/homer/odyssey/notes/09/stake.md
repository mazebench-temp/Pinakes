**From club to stake.** Left alone in the cave, Odysseus plans: “there lay beside the pen a great club of the Cyclops, / of green olive wood; he had cut it to carry with him / when it was dry” (9.319–321; see [[mace]], [[olive]] and [[fold]]). ἔκταμεν is the verb of felling a tree; Eustathius reports that “the more exact” wrote ἔκσπασε, “tore up”, since a Cyclops would pull up the tree by its roots (W. W. Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 9.320 and his critical note). The club is as long and thick as “the mast of a black ship of twenty oars, / a broad freight ship” (9.322–323; see [[the-mast-of-twenty-oars]], [[mast]] and [[ships]]). Merry doubts that any limb of an olive could be found long and straight enough for such a staff, but notes that the conjecture “of pine” would bring a worse difficulty, since fresh pine blazes instead of charring in the fire (on 9.322). Then:

{{quote:ad2ccd36-7045-51f5-9225-65295955b9f7}}

ὄργυια, the “fathom”, is the span of the outstretched arms (Merry on 9.325). The men do the rougher work of making the wood smooth and tapering it, and Odysseus shapes the point: ἐθόωσα is “I sharpened it” (Merry on 9.327; see the translation's note on 9.325–327), and the point is hardened in the blazing fire (see [[fire]] and [[like-a-fire-hardened-stake]]). Four companions are chosen by lot to “lift the stake with me / and grind it into his eye, when sweet sleep came upon him”, and the lot falls on those Odysseus would have chosen himself (9.331–335; see [[lots]]). μοχλός is the stake from here on, the word that the translation keeps apart from the raft's “levers” (see the translation's row ῥόπαλον, μοχλός).

**The blinding.** When the Cyclops has drunk the wine and fallen asleep, “I drove the stake under the deep ashes / until it grew hot” (9.375–376), and when “the stake of olive wood was just about to catch fire, / green though it was, and glowed terribly”, Odysseus brings it from the fire (9.378–380): the stake was not consumed, Merry explains, but glowed red-hot (on 9.379). The companions thrust it into the eye and Odysseus, “throwing my weight on it from above, / twirled it”, as a shipwright bores a ship's timber with a drill while his mates below keep it spinning with a strap (9.382–388). The drill belongs to the simile only, Merry warns: no such strap was fixed to the stake; the work and the men's attitudes resembled those of the shipwright and his mates (on 9.383; see the translation's notes on 9.383 and 9.384, [[the-shipwrights-drill]] and [[augers]]). “The blood flowed round the stake, hot as it was”: θερμὸν ἐόντα is said of the stake, whose end was so hot that it hissed in the blood of the eyeball (Merry on 9.388; see the translation's note on 9.387–388), and “his eye hissed around the stake of olive wood” as a smith's axe hisses in cold water (9.391–394; see [[the-smith-tempering-an-axe]], [[axe]] and [[iron]]). The Cyclops “wrenched the stake / out of his eye, drenched with much blood” and flung it from him (9.396–398; see [[eyes]] and [[polyphemus-the-cyclops]]).
