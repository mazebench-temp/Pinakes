# λοιβήν (9.349), the noun of λείβω, only here in the Odyssey.
grc: [λοιβήν]
en: [as a libation]
---

The wine is offered to the Cyclops as a libation is poured to a god: “Cyclops, here, drink wine, since you have eaten human flesh, / so that you may know what kind of drink this was that our ship / held hidden. I was bringing it to you as a libation (σοὶ δʼ αὖ λοιβὴν φέρον), in the hope that you would pity me / and send me home” (9.347–350; see [[wine]], [[pity]] and [[pompe]]). Eustathius called the phrase flattery, since Odysseus offers the wine not as a gift to a host but as a libation to a god; others took it of the libation he would have poured when entertained at the Cyclops' table, but the emphatic σοί, “to you”, settles it for the first: the grosser the flattery the deeper the irony, and it was not too strong for a savage who had set himself above the blessed gods (W. W. Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 9.349; 9.275–276; see [[theos]]). The Cyclops drinks it off three times “in his folly” (9.361).
