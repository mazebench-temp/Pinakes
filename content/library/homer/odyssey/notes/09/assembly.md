---
summary: The Cyclopes have “no assemblies that bring counsel, and no ordinances” (9.112); on the island of goats Odysseus calls an assembly of his own men before he sets out to make trial of them (9.171).
---

Assembly marks the difference between Odysseus' men and the Cyclopes. The Cyclopes have “no assemblies that bring counsel” (ἀγοραὶ βουληφόροι, 9.112; see [[boule]] and [[themis]]), and each gives the law to his own household. Odysseus, at dawn on the island of goats, “called an assembly and spoke among them all” (ἀγορὴν θέμενος, 9.171; again 10.188), and tells the rest of his men to wait while he goes with his own ship to learn what the people across the water are (9.172–176; see [[peira]] and [[xenia]]). When the other Cyclopes come to Polyphemus' cave at his cry, they stand round it, ask what ails him, and go away again (9.399–413; see [[cyclopes]] and [[nobody]]).
