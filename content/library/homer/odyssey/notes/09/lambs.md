**The pens.** In the empty cave “the pens were crowded / with lambs and kids; each kind was penned / apart, the firstlings by themselves, the later ones by themselves, / and the newborn by themselves” (9.219–222; see [[goats]] and [[penned-like-lambs]]). The young are kept in three divisions by age: πρόγονοι, the firstlings; μέτασσαι, the later lambs, “intermediate”, from μετά, as ἔπισσος is formed from ἐπί and περισσός from περί; and ἕρσαι, the newly born, a word that is elsewhere the dew (W. W. Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 9.221; see the translation's table of key words). The companions' first wish is to take some of the cheeses, and then “quickly to drive the kids and lambs to the swift ship / out of the pens, and to sail over the salt water” (9.224–227); Odysseus would not listen, and stayed to see the Cyclops and his guest-gifts (9.228–229; see [[odysseus]]).

**At the milking.** The ewes and goats are milked with their young beside them: the Cyclops “sat down and milked the sheep and the bleating goats, / all in good order, and put her young one under each” (9.244–245 = 9.341–342; 9.309; see [[sheep]]). ἔμβρυον, “her young one”, is found only in this verse.
