# "χολώσατο κηρόθι" (9.480; again 22.224), the adverb of κῆρ.
grc: ["χολώσατο κηρόθι"]
en: [in his heart]
---

The κῆρ laughs and rages in the same scene. Odysseus' heart laughs at the trick: “my dear heart laughed within me, / how my name and my flawless plan had deceived him” (ἐμὸν δʼ ἐγέλασσε φίλον κῆρ, 9.413–414; see [[nobody]] and [[metis]]). The Cyclops' heart wants vengeance: if only the ram could tell where Nobody is skulking, “Then his brains would be dashed against the ground, … / and my heart would find relief / from the evils that Nobody, a nobody, gave me” (9.458–460; see [[poine]]). And at the taunt from the ship “he grew all the more angry in his heart” (χολώσατο κηρόθι μᾶλλον, 9.480; see [[cholos]]), the half-verse of the river's anger at Achilles in the Iliad (*Iliad* 21.136).
