summary: Maron, son of Euanthes, “the priest of Apollo”, lived in the god's grove at Ismarus; the Achaeans spared him “with his child and his wife / in reverence”, and he gave Odysseus the wine that blinded the Cyclops (9.196–211).
# ἱρεύς (9.198), the Odyssey's spelling; the entry's ἱερεὺς does not take it.
grc: [ἱρεύς]
---

The book's priest is spared in a sacked city. Maron, son of Euanthes, was “the priest of Apollo, who used to stand guard over Ismarus” (9.197–198): the guardian is the god, and the pluperfect ἀμφιβεβήκει looks back to the time before Ismarus fell, since a god could not resist the necessity of fate and would desert a city when its doom had gone forth (W. W. Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 9.198; see [[apollo]] and [[ismarus]]). The priest lived “in the grove full of trees / of Phoebus Apollo” (9.200–201; see [[full-of-trees]]), and the raiders protected him with his child and his wife “in reverence” (9.199–200; see [[aidos]]), as the Achaeans at Troy had wished to respect Apollo's priest Chryses (*Iliad* 1.22–23). His gifts in return were seven talents of gold, a silver mixing bowl, and twelve jars of the unmixed wine (9.201–211; see [[maron]] and [[doron]]).
