# μαντοσύνῃ "prophecy" (9.509).
en: [prophecy]
---

The Cyclopes, who have no assemblies and no ordinances, had a seer. Telemus “excelled in prophecy (μαντοσύνῃ) / and grew old among the Cyclopes as their seer” (μαντευόμενος, 9.509–510), and told Polyphemus that he would lose his sight at the hands of Odysseus (9.511–512; see [[telemus]] and [[thesphaton]]). Merry notes that μαντεία was a regular profession, the seer being reckoned among the public servants (δημιοεργοί) with the singer, the healer and the carpenter (17.383–385), and that a seer could read the present and foretell the future from birds, from dreams, or perhaps from the smoke of sacrifice (W. W. Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 9.510; *Iliad* 1.62–69). Κυκλώπεσσιν may go with μαντευόμενος, “as a seer for the Cyclopes”, but its place in the verse suggests the local sense, that he lived and grew old among them (Merry on 9.510). The prophecy is fulfilled at the moment the Cyclops hears the name (see [[prophecy]] and [[nobody]]).
