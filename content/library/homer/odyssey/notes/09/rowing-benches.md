# The thwarts, "ὑπὸ ζυγὰ" (9.99; later 13.21), as a phrase (it applies in the Odyssey only), since ζυγόν is also the
# yoke of a chariot or of oxen and the crossbar of a lyre.

en: [thwarts]

**The verse of boarding.** “And they went on board at once and sat down at the rowing benches, / and sitting in order they struck the gray sea with their oars” closes the departure from the land of the Lotus-eaters, the voyage of Odysseus' own ship to the Cyclopes, the escape from the Cyclops and the departure from the island (9.103–104, 9.179–180, 9.471–472, 9.563–564; see [[oars]]). Before it the captain bids the men go on board and loose the stern-cables (9.177–178, 9.561–562; see [[stern-cables]]).

**Under the thwarts.** The scouts who ate the lotus will not come back: “These I brought back to the ships by force, weeping, / and in the hollow ships I dragged them under the thwarts and bound them” (9.98–99; see [[the-lotus-eaters]], [[lotus-eaters]] and [[lotus]]). νηυσὶν ἔνι answers ἐπὶ νῆας: Odysseus brought them to the ships, and when he had got them there he made them fast under the thwarts (W. W. Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 9.98–99). The translation keeps the ζυγά, the cross-beams of the hull, apart from the κληΐδες, the rowing benches (see the translation's row ζυγά, of a ship); whether the two were the same is part of the dispute over κληΐδες (see the entry and the Book 8 note).
