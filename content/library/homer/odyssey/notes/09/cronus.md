---
summary: Cronus is named in Book 9 only in his son's patronymic, when Odysseus sacrifices the ram “to Zeus, lord of the dark clouds, son of Cronus, who rules over all” (9.552).
---

The ram that carried Odysseus out of the Cyclops' cave is sacrificed on the shore of the island “to Zeus, lord of the dark clouds, son of Cronus, who rules over all” (9.552; see [[zeus]], [[son-of-cronus]] and [[lord-of-dark-clouds]]). The verse comes again when Alcinous sacrifices an ox to Zeus before the Phaeacians take Odysseus home (13.25). κελαινεφής, “of the dark clouds”, is said of the son of Cronus in the Iliad's first book (*Iliad* 1.397; see the translation's formula row), and ὃς πᾶσιν ἀνάσσει, “who rules over all”, is found in Homer only in these two verses: the son holds the rule that his father lost (see the general article). The son of Cronus takes no heed of the offering (9.553–555; see [[zeus]]).
