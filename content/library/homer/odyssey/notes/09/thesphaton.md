---
summary: When Odysseus shouts his name, the Cyclops groans: “Ah, truly the oracles spoken long ago have come home to me” (9.507). The seer Telemus had told him “that I would lose my sight at the hands of Odysseus” (9.508–512).
---

The name fulfills a prophecy:

{{quote:8c4ae89f-da6d-5afe-962a-4cb6ea3bf90e}}

The seer is Telemus (see [[telemus]], [[divination]] and [[prophecy]]). ἱκάνει is “are come home to me”, as a doom “comes” to a man (W. W. Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 9.507). Alcinous will say the same verse when Poseidon turns the Phaeacian ship to stone, remembering his father's prophecy (13.172; see the entry). Euripides' Cyclops, hearing the name, says “Alas, the old oracle is being fulfilled”, and adds that it foretold Odysseus' punishment too, long tossing on the sea (*Cyclops* 696–700; Merry on 9.507).
