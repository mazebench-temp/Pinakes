# ἀοιδοῦ (9.3, 9.7) is claimed by the entry.

**The singer at the feast.** “Truly it is a fine thing to listen to a singer / such as this one is, like the gods in his voice” (9.3–4): the singer is Demodocus, praised in the verses that Telemachus spoke of Phemius (1.370–371; see [[demodocus]], [[phemius]] and [[like-a-god-in-his-voice]]). **Fulfillment.** τέλος, “fulfillment” (9.5), is the “realisation” or “consummation”, the highest perfection of a thing rather than its end, W. W. Merry explains; not yet the “chief good” of later philosophy, for, as a scholium says, Odysseus calls the pleasure of a banquet its τέλος, not the pleasure of the whole of life (*Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 9.5; see the translation's key words and its note on 9.5). The singer is part of a whole picture of the feast: the banqueters “sitting in order”, the tables laden “with bread and meat”, the wine-pourer drawing wine from the mixing bowl and pouring it into the cups (9.8–10; see [[feast]], [[mixing-bowl]] and [[cup]]). τοῦτό τι … κάλλιστον, “something like the finest thing” (9.11): τι softens the superlative, as in σχεδόν τι (Merry on 9.11).

**The censure.** Plato censured the passage for its sensual tone, Merry notes, while Eustathius answers rightly that Odysseus is not propounding a philosophy, only chiming in with what his host had said of the feast and the lyre (8.248; Merry on 9.5): in the *Republic* Socrates would not have the wisest of men say that nothing seems to him finer than tables full of bread and meat, with the wine-pourer drawing wine from the mixing bowl, since it does not help a young man to self-control (*Republic* 390a–b, quoting 9.8–10; see [[plato-zeus-deceived]]). The four verses 9.5–8 bear obeli in one manuscript, which Merry takes for an objection to the sentiment rather than an athetesis, and Eratosthenes read “when gladness holds, evil being away” at 9.6 (Merry's critical notes on 9.5–8 and 9.6; see the translation's notes on 9.5 and 9.6).
