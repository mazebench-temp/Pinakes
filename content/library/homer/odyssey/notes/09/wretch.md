---
summary: “until we had called three times on each of our wretched companions / who had died on the plain, cut down by the Cicones” (9.65–66).
grc: ["δειλῶν ἑτάρων"]
---

δειλός here is pity for the dead, not the Iliad's “cowardly” ([[deos]]). Merry renders “each one of our poor comrades,” and reports the scholium that it was an ancient custom to call three times on the souls of those who died in a foreign land (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 9.64, 9.65; see the translation's note on 9.65; [[lament]], [[the-raid-on-the-cicones]]).
