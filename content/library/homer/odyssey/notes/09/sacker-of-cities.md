---
summary: Odysseus' second taunt, “say that Odysseus, sacker of cities, blinded you” (9.504), and the Cyclops' prayer, “grant that Odysseus, sacker of cities, may not reach home” (9.530).
---

The form is πτολιπόρθιος, found only in these two verses: the title Odysseus gives himself, and the Cyclops turns it against him with the father and the home he has just been told (9.504–505, 9.530–531; see the translation's note on 9.531). Aristotle quotes 9.504 for the anger that wants the wrongdoer to know by whose hand he was punished (*Rhetoric* 1380b22–24; [[say-that-odysseus-blinded-you]], [[odysseus]]).
