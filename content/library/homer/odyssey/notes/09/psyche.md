summary: The ψυχή is the life a man stakes: pirates wander the sea “staking their lives” (9.255), Odysseus plans his escape “as one does where life is at stake” (9.423), and he wishes he could make the Cyclops “bereft of soul and life / and send you into the house of Hades” (9.523–524).
# ψυχῆς (9.423, 9.523; again 21.154, 21.171).
grc: [ψυχῆς]
en: [lives, soul]
---

In Book 9 the ψυχή is what is risked and what is taken. The Cyclops' question, in the words of Nestor's at Pylos, asks whether the strangers wander the sea “like pirates, who wander / staking their lives and bringing evil to foreigners” (ψυχὰς παρθέμενοι, 9.254–255 = 3.73–74; see [[piracy]]). In the cave Odysseus weaves every kind of trick and plan “as one does where life is at stake” (ὥς τε περὶ ψυχῆς, 9.423), “in a matter of life and death”, as when Achilles and Hector run round the walls of Troy “for the life of Hector” (*Iliad* 22.161; W. W. Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 9.423; see [[metis]]). From the ship he answers the Cyclops' boast that his father will heal him: “If only I could make you bereft of soul and life / and send you into the house of Hades” (ψυχῆς τε καὶ αἰῶνος … εὖνιν, 9.523–524; see [[hades]] and the translation's table of key words). The life that leaves the body goes down to Hades as a shade, the ψυχή of the entry, and in two books more Odysseus will meet the shades there (see [[the-tale-to-alcinous]]).
