---
summary: The Cicones fight “beside the swift ships” (9.54); at the land of the Lotus-eaters the companions take their meal “beside the swift ships” (9.86), and Odysseus orders the rest “to make haste and go on board the swift ships” (9.101); in the cave the companions want to drive the kids and lambs “to the swift ship” (9.226).
grc: ["νηῶν ἐπιβαινέμεν ὠκειάων"]
---

9.54 is the verse of the battle by the river on the shield of Achilles, with the ships for the river's banks (*Iliad* 18.533; Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 9.54), and ὠκειάων at 9.101 is the Iliad's adjective of ships in Hector's boast, νηῶν ἐπιβησέμεν ὠκειάων (*Iliad* 8.197; [[ships]], [[the-raid-on-the-cicones]]).
