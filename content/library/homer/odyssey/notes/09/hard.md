summary: σχέτλιος four times: “seeing the cruel deeds” of the Cyclops (9.295); Odysseus' reproach, “Hard man, how should anyone else come to you hereafter” (9.351), and his taunt, “hard man, since you did not shrink from eating strangers” (9.478); and the companions' plea to Odysseus himself, “Hard man, why do you want to provoke a savage man?” (9.494).
en: [cruel]
---

The translation keeps “cruel” for deeds and “Hard man” for the vocative, as the Iliad's “Hard son of Peleus” (*Iliad* 16.203; see the Odyssey translation's key words, σχέτλιος). The word Odysseus uses of the Cyclops his own men use of him, when he will not stop taunting (9.492–499; [[say-that-odysseus-blinded-you]], [[polyphemus-the-cyclops]]).
