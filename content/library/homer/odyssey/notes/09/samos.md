---
summary: As Same, the island is one of those that Odysseus names around Ithaca, “Dulichium and Same and wooded Zacynthus” (9.24).
---

Odysseus names the islands around Ithaca, “Dulichium and Same and wooded Zacynthus” (9.24; see [[ithaca]], [[dulichium]] and [[zacynthus]]), the verse with which Telemachus named the islands of the suitors (1.246). The name was disputed in antiquity: Apollodorus wrote Σάμος here and at 1.246, Aristarchus Σάμη (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, critical note on 9.24; see the translation's note on 9.22, 9.24 and [[aristarchus]]). Strabo, who reports Apollodorus' wish to read Σάμῳ τε at 1.246, holds that the poet, counting the islands, called the island Same that he had called Samos before (*Geography* 10.2.10; see [[strabo]]). In Merry's account of the islands Same is Cephallenia, separated from Ithaca by a narrow strait (Appendix on Ithaca).
