**A city sacked.** The wind from Ilios brings Odysseus to the Cicones, “to Ismarus. There I sacked the city and destroyed the men themselves; / and from the city we took their wives and many possessions / and divided them” (9.39–42; see [[the-raid-on-the-cicones]], [[ismarus]] and [[division-of-spoils]]). It is the raid of the Iliad's heroes, a town sacked on the way home, and the Cicones were allies of Troy, which Merry thinks accounts for the burning of their city by a Greek hero (W. W. Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 9.39; *Iliad* 2.846; see [[cicones]]). The raid ends badly: the men stay to drink and slaughter on the shore, the Cicones of the inland come in the morning, and six men of each ship are killed (9.43–61; see [[nepios]] and [[hetairos]]). To the Cyclops Odysseus names Agamemnon by the greatest of sacks, “so great a city did he sack” (9.265; see [[troy]] and [[sacker-of-cities]]).

**Flocks driven off.** The other Cyclopes' first guess at Polyphemus' cry is a cattle raid: “Surely no one among mortals is driving off your flocks against your will?” (9.405; see [[nobody]]). Their guess comes true at dawn. Odysseus and his men, loosed from the rams, drive the Cyclops' flocks to the ship, “often turning them about” (πολλὰ περιτροπέοντες ἐλαύνομεν, 9.464–465), which Merry would rather take as “driving them in” into a compact flock to take them down to the ship (on 9.465; see the translation's note on 9.465); on the island the flocks are divided like any spoil (9.548–551; see [[sheep]]).
