# θαυμάζοντες (9.153) and "θαῦμʼ ἐτέτυκτο" (9.190).
grc: [θαυμάζοντες, "θαῦμʼ ἐτέτυκτο"]
en: [marveling, wonder]
---

Two wonders frame the approach to the Cyclops. The island of goats, untouched by men, with its meadows, its harbor and its spring, is a sight to marvel at: “we roamed over the island itself, marveling at it” (νῆσον θαυμάζοντες, 9.153; see [[the-island-of-goats]]). Its neighbor is a wonder of another kind: “For he was made a huge wonder, and was not like / a man who eats bread, but like a wooded peak / of high mountains” (θαῦμʼ ἐτέτυκτο πελώριον, 9.190–192; see [[polyphemus-the-cyclops]] and [[like-a-wooded-peak]]). The words that precede it, “alone, far off; and he did not go among the others, / but lived apart”, are so many repetitions to stress the monster's isolated, unsociable life (W. W. Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 9.188).
