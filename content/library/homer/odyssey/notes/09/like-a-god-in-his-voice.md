---
summary: Odysseus begins with praise of the singer: “truly it is a fine thing to listen to a singer / such as this one is, like the gods in his voice” (9.3–4).
---

9.4 is Telemachus' verse about Phemius, and 9.3 the one before it with a new beginning, ἦ τοι μέν for ἔστω, ἐπεί (1.370–371; Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 9.3–4). Here the singer is Demodocus, who is not named in the book, and the praise leads into the praise of the feast (9.5–11; [[singers]], [[demodocus]], [[feast-type-scene]]).
