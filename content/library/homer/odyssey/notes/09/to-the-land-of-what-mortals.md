summary: Odysseus to his companions before he crosses to the land of the Cyclopes: “but I, in my own ship with my companions, / will go and make trial of these men, who they are, / whether they are arrogant and savage and unjust, / or kind to strangers, with a mind that fears the gods” (9.173–176).
en: [arrogant and savage and unjust, with a mind that fears the gods]
---

The two verses of his question on Scheria (6.120–121) become the purpose of a voyage. The tale has already given the answer, “overbearing and lawless” (9.106), and the Cyclops gives it again: “You are a fool, stranger, or you have come from far away” (9.273; see the Odyssey translation's key words, ὑβριστής; [[peira]], [[xenia]], [[overbearing]]).
