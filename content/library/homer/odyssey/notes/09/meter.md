---
summary: The false name is given in the words of a true one: “Nobody is my name; Nobody they call me, / my mother and my father and all my other companions” (9.366–367).
---

Mother and father are the name-givers: Alcinous asked for the name “by which they called you there, your mother and your father” (8.550; see [[no-one-is-nameless]]), and Odysseus answers the Cyclops that his mother and his father call him Nobody (9.366–367; see [[nobody]] and [[pater]]). His real mother is dead, as he will learn in Book 11 when he meets her shade.
