summary: At the land of the Lotus-eaters Odysseus sends two men and a herald “to go and find out / what men they might be who eat bread upon the earth” (9.88–90); the scouts eat the lotus and no longer wish “to bring back word” (9.95). At the land of the Cyclopes he goes himself, to “make trial of these men, who they are” (9.174).

**Scouts who do not come back.** At the land of the Lotus-eaters the men take their meal by the ships, and then Odysseus sends scouts ahead: “then I sent some of my companions to go and find out / what men they might be who eat bread upon the earth, / choosing two men, and sending a herald with them as the third” (9.88–90; see [[heralds]] and [[who-eat-the-fruit-of-the-field]]). The herald gives the party the look of a formal embassy, and Ameis rejected verse 90 for that reason, taking it for an interpolation from Book 10 (10.102), since such a reconnaissance is not elsewhere an embassy and verses 94–95 imply more than three men; in the oldest manuscripts it stands before 89 (W. W. Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, his critical note on 9.89–90; see the translation's note on 9.89–90). The scouts never report: whoever ate the lotus “no longer wished to bring back word (ἀπαγγεῖλαι) or to return” (9.95; see [[the-lotus-eaters]] and [[nostos]]).

**Going himself.** At the island of goats the crews judge that the land of the Cyclopes is near by the smoke they see and the voices they hear (9.166–167; Merry on 9.167), and the next day Odysseus does his own scouting, with his own ship, to “make trial of these men, who they are” (9.174; see [[peira]]). In Book 10, on Circe's island, he will climb a lookout himself before sending men to the house (10.146–152).
