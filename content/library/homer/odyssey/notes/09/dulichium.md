---
summary: Dulichium is the first of the islands that Odysseus names around Ithaca, “Dulichium and Same and wooded Zacynthus” (9.24).
---

Describing his home, Odysseus says that around Ithaca “many islands / lie, very near to one another, / Dulichium and Same and wooded Zacynthus” (9.22–24; see [[ithaca]], [[samos]] and [[zacynthus]]). The verse is Telemachus' list of the islands of the suitors, in the nominative instead of the dative (1.246; see the Book 1 note). Merry, setting this description beside the map, finds that the site of Dulichium is unknown: Strabo would identify it with one of the Echinades, which the silt of the Achelous was joining to the mainland, and others have taken it for the southeastern part of Cephallenia, the other part being Same (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, Appendix on Ithaca; Strabo, *Geography* 10.2.19; see [[echinae]]).
