# βούλευσα (9.299), βούλευον (9.420; again 11.229). ἀγοραὶ βουληφόροι (9.112) is man-of-counsel's.
grc: [βούλευσα, βούλευον]
en: [planned]
---

**Counsel and its absence.** The Cyclopes have “no assemblies that bring counsel, and no ordinances” (ἀγοραὶ βουληφόροι, 9.112; see [[assembly]] and [[themis]]). In the Iliad βουληφόρος is always said of a man, the leader who gives counsel (*Iliad* 1.144, 2.24); only here is it said of assemblies (see [[man-of-counsel]]). Odysseus, by contrast, calls an assembly on the island of goats before he sets out (9.171; see [[assembly]]). The Lotus-eaters' guests have a will of a different kind: “they wished to remain there among the Lotus-eaters” (βούλοντο, 9.96), “would rather remain” (W. W. Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 9.96; see [[nostos]]).

**Plans in the cave.** Odysseus' first plan is a warrior's: “Then I planned in my proud heart / to go up close, drawing the sharp sword from beside my thigh, / and stab him in the chest”, “but a second thought held me back”, since no one could then move the stone from the door (βούλευσα, 9.299–305; see [[thymos]] and [[sword]]). The two plans that succeed are introduced by the same verse, “And this plan seemed to me in my heart the best” (ἀρίστη φαίνετο βουλή, 9.318, 9.424; see [[aristos]]): the olive club cut to a stake (9.319–335), and, when the Cyclops sits in the doorway, the rams (9.420–435), after “I planned how all might turn out best by far” (βούλευον, 9.420; see [[metis]]).
