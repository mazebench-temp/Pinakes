---
summary: The blinded Cyclops asks his ram to tell him “where he is skulking from my might” (9.457).
---

The Cyclops speaks of his own μένος in his lament to the ram: “If only you could be of one mind with me and could find a voice / to tell where he is skulking from my might!” (ἐμὸν μένος ἠλασκάζει, 9.456–457; see [[ram]] and [[nothing-better-than-one-mind-in-a-house]]). ἠλασκάζει, used in the Iliad intransitively of wandering, is here “dodging”, Merry finding that the two meanings meet in that notion (W. W. Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 9.457). The might is real, and useless without sight (see [[polyphemus-the-cyclops]]).
