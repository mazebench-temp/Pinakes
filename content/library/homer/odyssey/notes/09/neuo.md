summary: Two silent signs in the escape: Odysseus forbids his companions to weep with a sign of his brows (9.468), and bids them row by nodding his head, since no one dared speak within the Cyclops' range (9.490).
# νεῦον (9.468), κατανεύων (9.490), each only here in Homer.
grc: [νεῦον, κατανεύων]
en: [signed no with my brows, nodding with my head]
---

The flight from the cave is made in signs. At the ship the companions begin to lament the dead, “But I would not let them—I signed no with my brows to each— / weep” (ἀνὰ δʼ ὀφρύσι νεῦον, 9.468–469): ἀνανεύειν is to refuse by throwing back the head, the opposite of κατανεύειν, the nod of consent, and here the refusal is made with the brows (W. W. Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 9.468; see [[tears]] and [[lament]]). When the first rock has driven the ship back to the shore, Odysseus pushes off and urges his men to row, “nodding with my head” (κρατὶ κατανεύων, 9.490), for no one dared to utter a sound while they were still within range of the Cyclops' missiles (Merry on 9.490; see [[silence]]).
