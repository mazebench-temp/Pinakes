---
summary: On the island off the land of the Cyclopes, “the nymphs, the daughters of aegis-bearing Zeus”, rouse the goats that live in the mountains, so that Odysseus' companions may have their meal (9.154–155).
---

**The nymphs of the island.** The morning after the landing, “we roamed over the island itself, marveling at it. / And the nymphs, the daughters of aegis-bearing Zeus, roused / the goats that lived in the mountains, so that my companions might have their meal” (9.153–155; see [[the-island-of-goats]], [[child-of-aegis-bearing-zeus]], [[aegis-bearing]], [[mountain-dwelling]] and [[goats]]). κοῦραι Διὸς αἰγιόχοιο is their title in both poems (6.105; *Iliad* 6.420; see the Book 6 note). The island has no men: “unsown and unplowed all its days / it is empty of men, and feeds bleating goats” (9.123–124). Its powers are gods: “some god guided us” into its harbor through the dark night (9.142), the nymphs drive the goats out to the hunters, and “at once the god gave us game to satisfy the heart” (9.158; see [[theos]]). At the head of its harbor “flows bright water, / a spring from under a cave; and around it poplars grow” (9.140–141), a place of the kind where the nymphs of the Odyssey are found and honored: the cave of the Naiads at the harbor of Ithaca (13.103–112; see [[the-cave-of-the-naiads]] and [[the-harbor-of-phorcys]]), and the spring of the town of Ithaca, with its grove of poplars and the altar of the nymphs, where every traveler made offerings (17.205–211; see the general article).

**A nymph's son.** The Cyclops himself is the son of a nymph: Thoosa bore him to Poseidon, “having lain with Poseidon in the hollow caves” (1.73; see [[thoosa]], [[polyphemus-the-cyclops]] and [[hollow-caves]]). Book 9 does not name her.
