summary: Apollo is the god of Ismarus in Odysseus' tale. The sweet wine that Odysseus carries to the Cyclops' cave was a gift of Maron, son of Euanthes, “the priest of Apollo, who used to stand guard over Ismarus”, whom Odysseus and his men had spared with his child and his wife “in reverence”, since he lived in the grove full of trees of Phoebus Apollo.
passages:
  - 9.196-205 | Maron, priest of Apollo, and his wine
---

**The priest of Apollo.** When Odysseus goes up to the cave with twelve companions, he carries “a goatskin of dark wine, / sweet wine, which Maron, son of Euanthes, had given me, / the priest of Apollo, who used to stand guard over Ismarus, / because we had protected him with his child and his wife / in reverence; for he lived in the grove full of trees / of Phoebus Apollo” (9.196–201; see [[maron]], [[euanthes]], [[ismarus]] and [[phoebus]]). ἀμφιβεβήκει, “used to stand guard over”, is the verb of Chryses' prayers to the god “who stand guard over Chryse” (*Iliad* 1.37, 1.451; see [[chryses]] and [[chryse]]). Merry takes it of the god, and the pluperfect of the time before Ismarus fell: a god could not resist fate, and would desert a city when the sentence of its doom had gone forth (W. W. Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 9.198, comparing Aeschylus, *Seven against Thebes* 218, and Virgil, *Aeneid* 2.351; see the translation's note on 9.198). ἁζόμενοι, “in reverence”, is the reverence that Chryses asked of the Achaeans when he begged them to release his daughter, “in reverence for the son of Zeus, far-shooting Apollo” (*Iliad* 1.21). Agamemnon refused, and the god sent the plague (see [[wrath-of-apollo]]). At Ismarus the men who sacked the city spared the priest and his household, and the priest gave gifts: seven talents of gold, a mixing bowl all of silver, and twelve jars of the wine, “sweet unmixed wine, a divine drink” (9.201–205; see [[gold-and-silver]], [[mixing-bowl]], [[two-handled-jar]] and [[wine]]). The wine of Apollo's priest is the wine with which Odysseus makes the Cyclops drunk before the blinding (9.345–374).

**The grove.** δενδρήεις, “full of trees”, is said in Homer only of this grove and of Calypso's island (1.51; see [[full-of-trees]] and [[ogygia]]). The Odyssey has one other grove of Apollo, on Ithaca, where the Achaeans gather for a hecatomb “beneath the shady grove of far-shooting Apollo” (20.276–278, a plain rendering), and Athena has hers on the road to the Phaeacian city (6.291–292; see [[the-grove-of-athena]]).
