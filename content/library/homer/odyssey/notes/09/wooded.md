---
summary: Three things are wooded in the tale: Zacynthus among the islands around Ithaca, “Dulichium and Same and wooded Zacynthus” (9.24); the island off the harbor of the Cyclopes, “a wooded one” (9.118); and the peak that the Cyclops is like, “a wooded peak / of high mountains” (9.191–192).
---

9.24 is 1.246 in the nominative, with the feminine ὑλήεσσα, where Book 1 has ὑλήεντι Ζακύνθῳ, the adjective used with two endings; Merry takes the choice of form as merely metrical (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 9.24).
