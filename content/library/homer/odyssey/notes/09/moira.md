summary: At Ismarus the men are “of dread fate” (9.53), and the survivors “escaped death and fate” (9.61). The Cyclops milks his flocks “all in good order” (κατὰ μοῖραν, 9.245, 9.309, 9.342), and Odysseus tells him that he has “not acted fair and right” (9.352); the Cyclops' curse allows that it may be Odysseus' fate to reach home (9.532).
# αἰνομόροισιν (9.53; again 24.169). μόρον, μοῖραν and μοῖρʼ are claimed by the entry and the earlier notes.
grc: [αἰνομόροισιν]
en: [men of dread fate]
---

**Death and fate.** The men who died at Ismarus were ἡμῖν αἰνομόροισιν, “men of dread fate” (9.53; see [[aisa]]), and Odysseus says of the survivors, “the rest of us escaped death and fate” (θάνατόν τε μόρον τε, 9.61; see [[thanatos]]): μόρος, the doom that is a man's share, beside death itself.

**In good order.** κατὰ μοῖραν, “in due portion”, describes the Cyclops at his own work: three times he milks “the sheep and the bleating goats, / all in good order, and put her young one under each” (9.244–245, 9.308–309, 9.341–342), the phrase of the Phaeacian crew who make the ship ready “all in good order” (8.54; see [[sheep]] and [[goats]]). Odysseus turns the phrase against him over the wine: “how should anyone else come to you hereafter / of the many men there are, since you have not acted fair and right?” (οὐ κατὰ μοῖραν ἔρεξας, 9.351–352). ἀνθρώπων πολέων, “of the multitudes of men”: however many they are, such a welcome will keep them all away (W. W. Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 9.352; see [[xenia]]). The herdsman who does everything in order with his flocks does nothing in order with his guests.

**If it is his fate.** The Cyclops' prayer allows for what is fixed: “But if it is his fate to see his friends and reach / his well-built house and his native land, / let him come late and in evil plight” (εἴ οἱ μοῖρʼ ἐστί, 9.532–534; see [[curses]] and [[nostos]]). Poseidon hears him (9.536), and as Zeus tells the gods at the beginning of the poem, he does not kill Odysseus, but drives him wandering from his native land (1.74–75; see [[poseidon]]).
