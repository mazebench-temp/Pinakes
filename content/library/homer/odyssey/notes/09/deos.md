summary: Fear runs through the tale: the crews lower the torn sails “fearing destruction” (9.72); in the cave the companions shrink back “in terror” from the Cyclops and his voice (9.236, 9.257, 9.396); Odysseus encourages them so that no one may “draw back from me in fear” (9.377); and the Cyclops will neither fear nor avoid the gods (9.274).
# δείσαντες (9.72, 9.236, 9.396), δεισάντων (9.257), ὑποδείσας (9.377); none of them in Odyssey 1–8.
grc: [δείσαντες, δεισάντων, ὑποδείσας]
en: [fearing, in terror, in fear]
---

**The men's fear.** In the storm after Ismarus the sails are torn in three and four pieces, “and we lowered them into the ships, fearing destruction” (9.72; see [[sails]]). In the cave the verb marks each of the Cyclops' terrors: when he throws down his load of wood, “we in terror shrank back into the inner part of the cave” (9.236); when he speaks, “our dear heart was broken, / in terror of his heavy voice and of the monster himself” (9.256–257), where ἡμῖν … δεισάντων passes from the dative to the genitive (W. W. Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 9.256; see [[etor]]); and when he groans at the blinding, “we in terror shrank back” (9.396). Odysseus' encouragement at the stake is meant to keep anyone from drawing back “in fear” (ὑποδείσας, 9.377; see [[tharsos]]).

**The Cyclops' fearlessness.** The one who will not fear is the Cyclops: “You are a fool, stranger, … / you who bid me either fear or avoid the gods; / for the Cyclopes care nothing for aegis-bearing Zeus” (δειδίμεν, 9.273–275; see [[theos]] and [[aidos]]).
