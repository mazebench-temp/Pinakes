# σκύλακας (9.289), the young of the dog. σκύλαξ comes only in the Odyssey: σκύλακος (12.86), σκυλάκεσσι (20.14).
grc: [σκύλακας]
en: [puppies]
---

“and seizing two together he dashed them to the ground like puppies, / and their brains flowed out on the ground and soaked the earth” (9.289–290; see [[like-puppies]] and [[polyphemus-the-cyclops]]). σκύλαξ, the young dog, is found in Homer only in the Odyssey: here; in Circe's account of Scylla, whose voice is no louder than a newborn puppy's (12.86); and in the simile of Odysseus' heart, which barks within him at the maids as a bitch standing over her tender puppies barks at a man she does not know (20.13–16; plain renderings). The Cyclops of the tale keeps no dogs; Euripides' Cyclops is away hunting with dogs on Etna when Odysseus comes (*Cyclops* 130).
