summary: In the house of Maron, priest of Apollo at Ismarus, no one of the slaves or the handmaids knew of the strong wine he gave Odysseus, but only he himself, his wife and one housekeeper (9.205–207).
# ἀμφιπόλων (9.206) and ταμίη (9.207) are claimed by the entry; δμώων (9.206), "of the slaves", is the genitive of
# the masculine δμώς and stays unclaimed.
---

Maron kept his wine a secret within his own house: “and no one / of the slaves or the handmaids in the house knew of it, / but only he himself and his dear wife and one housekeeper” (9.205–207; see [[maron]] and [[wine]]). ἀμφίπολοι, W. W. Merry notes, are always women servants in Homer: they spin and weave, attend at meals, pouring water over the diners' hands, do the general work of the house, and accompany its lady or her daughter; the corresponding men servants are the θεράποντες (*Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 9.206). The ταμίη, the housekeeper who keeps the stores (1.139, 2.345), is the one servant trusted with the wine.
