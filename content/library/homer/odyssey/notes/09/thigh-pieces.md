---
summary: On the shore of the island Odysseus sacrifices to Zeus the ram under which he escaped and burns the thigh-pieces, but Zeus takes no heed of the offering (9.550–555).
---

The companions give Odysseus the ram as a special gift when the flocks are divided, “and on the shore / to Zeus, lord of the dark clouds, son of Cronus, who rules over all, / I sacrificed him and burned the thigh-pieces; but he took no heed of the offering, / but was pondering how they might all perish, / my well-benched ships and my trusty companions” (9.550–555; see [[ram]], [[zeus]], [[sacrifice]], [[lord-of-dark-clouds]] and [[son-of-cronus]]). The ram is the particular one by which he had escaped (W. W. Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 9.550). Zeus refused to notice the offering, Merry explains, because the prayer of Polyphemus had been heard and would be answered (on 9.553; see the translation's note on 9.553, [[curses]] and [[polyphemus-the-cyclops]]).
