---
summary: The Cyclops asks the strangers in his cave the question Nestor asked at Pylos: “Is it on some business, or do you wander at random / over the sea, like pirates, who wander / staking their lives and bringing evil to foreigners?” (9.253–255). The tale has begun with a raid on the Cicones (9.39–42).
---

The question is asked here by a host who will not feed his guests, and before the meal rather than after it, as Nestor asked it (9.252–255 = 3.71–74; see [[strangers-who-are-you]], [[prexis]] and [[xenia]]). The text of Book 9 prints ἦ τι where 3.72 has ἤ τι, and a comma after ληιστῆρες that 3.73 lacks; a scholium reports the variant μηχανόωνται, “contrive”, for ἀλόωνται, “wander” (W. W. Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, his critical note on 9.254; see the translation's note on 9.252–255). Odysseus does not answer the charge of piracy; he names his men as Achaeans driven off course on their way home from Troy, “the people of Agamemnon” (9.259–266; see [[laos]]). The tale has already shown what such men do on a foreign shore: the first stop was Ismarus, “There I sacked the city and destroyed the men themselves” (9.40; see [[raiding]] and [[the-raid-on-the-cicones]]). Thucydides took the question for evidence that raiding by sea brought no disgrace in early Greece (see [[thucydides-and-the-pirates]]).
