**Held back.** After his name and his home, Odysseus names the goddesses who kept him from it: “Truly Calypso, brilliant among goddesses, held me back there / in her hollow caves, longing for me to be her husband; / and in the same way Circe kept me in her halls, / the wily one of Aeaea, longing for me to be her husband; / but they never persuaded the heart in my breast” (9.29–33; see [[circe]], [[brilliant-among-goddesses]] and [[hollow-caves]]). The first two verses are the poem's first words of her, “the lady nymph Calypso, brilliant among goddesses, held back / in her hollow caves, longing for him to be her husband” (1.14–15), turned into his own voice (see [[calypsos-cave]]). αὐτόθι, “there”, is defined by ἐν σπέσσι γλαφυροῖσι, “in her hollow caves” (W. W. Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 9.29, comparing *Iliad* 9.617). The second verse is wanting in most manuscripts and is not quoted by Eustathius: probably taken over from 1.15, it was rejected by Wolf, and most modern editors follow him. Köchly, as Merry reports, quotes Sengebusch's conclusion that an ancient athetesis ran from 9.29 to 9.36, so that 9.28 was followed by 9.37 (Merry's critical note on 9.30; see the translation's note on 9.29–36). The last verse is the one Odysseus spoke of Calypso alone in his story to Arete, ἔπειθεν, “she never persuaded” (7.258; see the Book 7 note), now with the plural ἔπειθον, of both goddesses.

**The last first.** He names the last of his hosts first. In the tale Circe's island comes in Book 10, and Calypso's only at the end of Book 12, where Odysseus breaks off, since he has told Alcinous and Arete the day before how the gods brought him to Ogygia (12.447–453; see [[ogygia]] and the Book 7 note).
