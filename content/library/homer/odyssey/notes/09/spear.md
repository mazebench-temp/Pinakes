**At Ismarus.** The Cicones of the inland “set their battle in array and fought beside the swift ships, / and they struck at one another with bronze-fitted spears” (9.54–55; see [[cicones]] and [[the-raid-on-the-cicones]]), the verses of the battle on the Shield of Achilles, where the fighters meet “beside the river's banks” (*Iliad* 18.533–534; see the translation's note on 9.54).

**The javelins of the hunt.** On the island the men take from the ships their “curved bows and long-socketed javelins” (9.156; see [[bow]] and [[goats]]). The αἰγανέη is a light javelin, the one the Myrmidons and the suitors throw for sport (*Iliad* 2.774; 4.626). δολιχαύλους, “long-socketed”, is formed from αὐλός, the metal collar by which the spearhead was fixed to the shaft, where the brain of a fallen man spurts “along the spear's socket” (*Iliad* 17.297); the epithet, W. W. Merry explains, means only that the spear was long, since the socket is common to all spears (*Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 9.156).
