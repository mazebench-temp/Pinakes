---
summary: The Cyclops, hearing Odysseus' name: “Ah, truly the oracles spoken long ago have come home to me” (9.507).
---

Merry renders ἱκάνει “are come home to me” (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 9.507). Alcinous speaks the whole verse when the prophecy of his father Nausithous comes true (13.172; see the Odyssey translation's formulas; [[thesphaton]], [[telemus]]).

## Outside Homer

Merry compares the words of the Cyclops in Euripides when he hears Odysseus' name: “Alas, an old oracle is being fulfilled: it said that I would have my sight blinded by you on your way home from Troy” (Euripides, *Cyclops* 696–698, a plain rendering; Merry on 9.507).
