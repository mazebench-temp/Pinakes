Homer's word for the Cyclops' club is ῥόπαλον, a staff or cudgel, not the κορύνη of war (see the entry). Odysseus finds it in the cave: “there lay beside the pen a great club of the Cyclops, / of green olive wood; he had cut it to carry with him / when it was dry. As we looked at it, we likened it / to the mast of a black ship of twenty oars, / a broad freight ship, which crosses the great gulf; / so great was it in length, so great in thickness to look upon” (9.319–324; see [[the-mast-of-twenty-oars]], [[olive]], [[fold]] and [[mast]]). The Cyclops had cut the tree to season it before carrying it as his staff. γάρ only introduces the main sentence, which begins with τοῦ, “from it”, at 325 (W. W. Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 9.319; see the translation's note on 9.319–322); ὅσσον θʼ ἱστόν is a brachylogy for “as great as a mast”, a comparison that Milton imitated, Merry notes, for Satan's spear (on 9.322). From the club Odysseus cuts a fathom's length and makes the stake (9.325–328; see [[stake]]).
