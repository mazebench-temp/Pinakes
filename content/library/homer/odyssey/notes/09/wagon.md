---
summary: Two and twenty good four-wheeled wagons could not have heaved the Cyclops' door-stone from the ground (9.241–242).
---

The Cyclops closes his cave with a great door-stone, “a heavy one; two and twenty wagons, / good four-wheeled ones, could not have heaved it from the ground” (9.241–242; see [[door-stone]]). W. W. Merry takes the verse for a hyperbolic parody of the Iliad's stone that not even two men “could easily heave … from the ground onto a wagon” (*Iliad* 12.447–448), comic in its exact number and in its verb, since ὀχλίσσειαν hardly describes the work of a wagon (*Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 9.241). Most modern editions print Barnes' τεσσαράκυκλοι for τετράκυκλοι, which would need its ε and α lengthened; the form τετράκυκλον of the Iliad has them short (*Iliad* 24.324; Merry's critical note; see the translation's note on 9.241–242).
