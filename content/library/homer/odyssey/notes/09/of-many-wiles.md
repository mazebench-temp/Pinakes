---
summary: “Answering him, Odysseus of many wiles said” (9.1): the verse that begins the tale to the Phaeacians.
---

The epithet is built on μῆτις, the word of the plan on which the book plays: “my flawless plan” (9.414) and “every kind of trick and plan” (9.422), against the Cyclopes' μή τις, “no one” (9.405–410; [[metis]], [[nobody]], [[blameless]]). Merry compares with the epithet the words with which Odysseus introduces his lie to the Cyclops, εἰδότα πολλά, “for I knew much,” that is, “with my knowledge of the world” (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 9.281; [[odysseus]], [[answering-him]]).
