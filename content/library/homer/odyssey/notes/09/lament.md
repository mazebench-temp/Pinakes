summary: Alcinous' question makes Odysseus “groan and mourn still more” (9.13). The companions at the ship lament the men the Cyclops ate (9.467), but Odysseus forbids them to weep (9.468–470); those who waited on the island of goats “sat mourning” for the men who had gone (9.545).
# γοῶντες (9.467). ὀδυρόμενος, ὀδυρόμενοι (9.13, 9.545) are taken by an earlier note; "mourning" is 9.545.

en: [mourning]

**To tell is to mourn.** Odysseus begins: “But your heart is set on my groaning sorrows, / to ask about them, so that I may groan and mourn still more” (ὀδυρόμενος στεναχίζω, 9.12–13; see [[algos]]); to tell them is to renew the memory of them (W. W. Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 9.13).

**Mourning on the voyage.** The dead of Ismarus are called three times each before the ships leave (9.64–66; see [[the-call-to-the-dead]]). When the men come back from the cave, “we were a welcome sight to our dear companions, / we who had escaped death; but for the others they groaned and lamented” (στενάχοντο γοῶντες, 9.466–467), as the Achaeans and the Myrmidons lamented Patroclus all night (ἀνεστενάχοντο γοῶντες, *Iliad* 18.315, 18.355). Odysseus stops it, with a sign of his brows, and hurries them to the oars (9.468–470; see [[tears]] and [[neuo]]). At the island of goats the other crews “sat mourning, always waiting for us” (ἥατʼ ὀδυρόμενοι, 9.545; see [[the-island-of-goats]] and [[hetairos]]).
