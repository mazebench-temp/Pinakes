---
summary: Tasting Maron's wine, the Cyclops calls it “a branch of ambrosia and nectar” (9.359).
---

The Cyclops knows the wine of his own vines, “but this is a branch of ambrosia and nectar” (9.359; see [[wine]], [[nectar]] and [[polyphemus-the-cyclops]]). ἀπορρώξ is properly a piece broken off, Homer's word for the stream that is a “branch” of the Styx (W. W. Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 9.359; *Iliad* 2.755; see the translation's row ἀπορρώξ): the wine is an offshoot of the food and drink of the gods. In this book the gods' food is only a likeness; νύκτα διʼ ἀμβροσίην, “through the ambrosial night” (9.404), is the night's epithet (see [[ambrosial]]).
