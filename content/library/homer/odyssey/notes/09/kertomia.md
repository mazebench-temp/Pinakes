---
summary: When the ship is as far from the shore as a man can make himself heard with a shout, Odysseus speaks to the Cyclops “with cutting words”: it was no weakling's companions he meant to eat, and Zeus and the other gods have punished him (9.473–479). The Cyclops answers with a rock, and the second taunt gives him Odysseus' name (9.480–505).
---

**The taunts.** The escape is complete, and Odysseus spoils it with words. “But when I was as far away as a man can make himself heard with a shout, / then I spoke to the Cyclops with cutting words” (κερτομίοισι, 9.473–474): the adjective stands as a noun, as when Hera speaks to Zeus “with cutting words” in *Iliad* 1, and as μειλιχίοισιν, “with soothing words”, can stand alone (W. W. Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 9.474; *Iliad* 1.539; see [[soothing-words]]). The first taunt turns the Cyclops' scorn back on him: “Cyclops, it was not a weakling's companions after all that you were / to eat in your hollow cave with your mighty strength” (9.475–476), where the negative goes with ἀνάλκιδος, “He was no weakling whose comrades thou wast minded to eat” (Merry on 9.475; see [[alke]]); and it ends with a verdict on his crime against guests (9.477–479; see [[xenia]] and [[poine]]). The Cyclops answers with the peak of a mountain, which nearly drives the ship back to shore (9.480–486).

**The second taunt.** The companions beg him not to provoke “a savage man” again (9.492–499; see [[hetairos]]), but he does not listen, and answers “with resentful heart” (9.500–501; see [[kotos]] and [[thymos]]), this time with his name: “say that Odysseus, sacker of cities, blinded you” (9.502–505; see [[say-that-odysseus-blinded-you]] and [[nobody]]). The name brings the second rock and the curse (9.526–542; see [[curses]]).
