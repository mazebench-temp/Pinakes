# ἀκαχήμενοι (9.62, 9.105, 9.565; again 10.77, 10.133), the participle of ἀκαχίζω, “grieve”.
grc: [ἀκαχήμενοι]
en: [grieving]
---

ἀκαχήμενοι ἦτορ, “grieving at heart”, is the grief of the survivors as they put out from each shore: after Ismarus, where six men of each ship were killed (9.60–62), after the land of the Lotus-eaters, where no one was lost but the men had been dragged from the lotus in tears (9.98–105), and after the Cyclops' cave (9.565). At Ismarus and at the cave the next verse names its cause, “glad to have escaped death, though we had lost our dear companions” (9.63, 9.566; see [[from-there-we-sailed-on]], [[etor]] and [[hetairos]]). The verse returns twice in Book 10, after Aeolus and after the Laestrygonians (10.77, 10.133).
