summary: Maron, the priest of Apollo at Ismarus, gave Odysseus “splendid gifts” for sparing his household: seven talents of gold, a mixing bowl all of silver, and twelve jars of sweet unmixed wine (9.196–211). In the cave Odysseus asks the Cyclops for a guest-gift “or otherwise / … some present” (9.267–268).
# δωτίνην (9.268; again 11.352).
grc: [δωτίνην]
en: [some present]
---

**Maron's gifts.** The wine that blinds the Cyclops was a gift. The Achaeans had protected Maron, the priest of Apollo, with his child and his wife, “in reverence”, “and he gave me splendid gifts: / he gave me seven talents of well-made gold, / and he gave me a mixing bowl all of silver, and then / he drew off wine in two-handled jars, twelve in all” (ἀγλαὰ δῶρα, 9.199–205; see [[maron]], [[aidos]] and [[gold-and-silver]]). The talents are a definite weight of gold, not coined money (W. W. Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 9.202; see [[talent]]). The gift is a return for protection, and the wine, which no one in the house knew of but Maron, his wife and one housekeeper (9.205–207), is the gift that the tale turns to use (see [[wine]]).

**Some present.** Odysseus' plea asks the Cyclops for “a guest-gift, or otherwise / … some present, as is the custom with strangers” (δωτίνην, 9.267–268): ξεινήιον πορεῖν is the most general expression for hospitality, δωτίνη a more particular gift (Merry on 9.267; see [[guest-gifts]] and [[xenia]]).
