**The dairy.** In the empty cave “all the vessels were swimming with whey, / the pails and the bowls, well made, into which he milked” (9.222–223; see [[cheese]] for the racks and the quotation of the whole scene). The general word ἄγγεα, W. W. Merry explains, is divided into the σκαφίδες, “scoops”, from σκάπτω, and the γαυλοί, probably “round bowls” (*Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 9.222; see the translation's row ταρσοί, σηκοί, ὀρός, γαυλοί, σκαφίδες). ναῖον is “were swimming”, “ran over”, Aristarchus' reading (see the translation's note on 9.222).

**The milking.** Each evening and each morning the Cyclops milks the ewes and the she-goats in order, and gives each her young to suck:

{{quote:7799faa6-f179-5999-ab5f-0e1f73f0f9e2}}

The verses of the milking return on the evening of the blinding, and in part on the morning between (9.308–309, 9.341–342). ἔμβρυον is the young after birth, not the later “embryo” (Merry on 9.245). His supper, after he has eaten two of the companions, is washed down with milk: “eating human flesh and drinking unmixed milk on top of it” (9.297). ἐπὶ … πίνων is “drinking on the top of it”, “to wash it down”, as Euripides' Cyclops boasts that he drinks off a jar of milk after his meat, and ἄκρητον, “unmixed”, shows, Merry thinks, that the common custom was to dilute milk with water, or perhaps to curdle it and drink only the whey (on 9.297; Euripides, *Cyclops* 325–327; see the translation's row ἀκηράσιος and [[wine]]).

**The unmilked ewes.** On the morning of the escape the rams rush out to the pasture, “and the females bleated unmilked around the pens, / for their udders were bursting” (9.439–440; see [[fold]] and [[sheep]]), while their master, worn down by pain, feels along the backs of all the sheep as they stand (9.440–442; see [[ram]] and [[polyphemus-the-cyclops]]).
