summary: Five dawns in the verse “And when early-born Dawn appeared, the rosy-fingered” (9.152, 9.170, 9.307, 9.437, 9.560), three of them after a night of waiting, “There we fell asleep and waited for brilliant Dawn” (9.151) and “So then, groaning, we waited for brilliant Dawn” (9.306, 9.436); and the end of the storm, “But when Dawn of the lovely tresses brought the third day” (9.76).
grc: ["Ἠῶ δῖαν"]
en: [waited for]
---

The dawns count the days of the tale: on the island of goats, the hunt (9.152) and the crossing to the land of the Cyclopes (9.170); in the cave, the morning after the first meal and the morning of the escape (9.307, 9.437), each after a night of waiting, so that the verse of waiting and the verse of dawn follow one another (9.306–307, 9.436–437); and on the island again, the departure (9.560). Ἠῶ δῖαν is the Iliad's phrase for the dawn that a man waits for, “There the old man lay down and waited for brilliant Dawn” (*Iliad* 9.662), and 9.151 is said again on Circe's island (12.7; see the Odyssey translation's formulas). 9.76 is the verse of Odysseus' third day in the sea before Scheria (5.390; [[with-lovely-tresses]], [[early-born]], [[rosy-fingered]], [[brilliant]], [[sunset-lines]]).
