summary: After the rout at Ismarus the ships flee, but do not sail on until each of the companions killed on the plain has been called three times (9.64–66).

passages:
  - 9.59-66 | The dead of Ismarus
---

**For all that.** οὐδʼ ἄρα is “yet, for all that”: the ships are in flight, and still, in Merry's rendering, “I let not my ships sail on, till we had thrice called to each one of our poor comrades” (W. W. Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 9.64). δειλῶν, “wretched”, is the word of pity for the dead (see [[wretch]]). The dead were six from each ship, seventy-two from the twelve ships of 9.159: Crates took the figure for no more than an average, and Zoilus, “the scourge of Homer”, was offended at so suspicious a symmetry (Merry on 9.60; see [[hetairos]]). The call is the last thing done at Ismarus: in the next verse Zeus rouses the North Wind against the ships (9.67; see [[boreas]]).
