summary: Odysseus' first plan, to stab the sleeping Cyclops, and the second thought that holds him back, “For there we too would have perished by sheer destruction” (9.299–305); and two resolutions in the verse of the best plan, “And this plan seemed to me in my heart the best” (9.318, 9.424): the stake and the rams.

The first decision has the two courses of the pattern without the formula: the planned blow, and ἕτερος δέ με θυμὸς ἔρυκεν, “but a second thought held me back” (9.302), with its reason, the stone that no one could move from the doorway (9.303–305). The second and third end in the Iliad's verse of decision, with μοι for the οἱ of Zeus and of Hera (*Iliad* 2.5, 14.161), after “brooding evil deep within” (9.316) and “I wove every kind of trick and plan” (9.422; [[pondering-two-ways]], [[boule]], [[metis]], [[steep]]).
