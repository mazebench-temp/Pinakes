# στῆθος (9.301; again 14.351 and after).
grc: [στῆθος]
en: [breast]
---

The breast holds the heart, and the heart was not persuaded: “but they never persuaded the heart in my breast” (θυμὸν ἐνὶ στήθεσσιν, 9.33; see [[thymos]] and [[peitho]]). The chest is also where a sword goes in: Odysseus planned “to go up close, drawing the sharp sword from beside my thigh, / and stab him in the chest, where the midriff holds the liver, / feeling for the place with my hand” (πρὸς στῆθος, 9.300–302), ἐπιμασσάμενος being better taken of feeling for the mortal spot in the dark, where the breastbone ended, than of fingering the sword (W. W. Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 9.302; see [[phrenes]]). A second thought held him back (9.302–305; see [[boule]]).
