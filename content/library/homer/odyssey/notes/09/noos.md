---
summary: The test Odysseus sets the Cyclopes is whether they are savage “or kind to strangers, with a mind that fears the gods” (9.176); at the cave door, feeling the backs of his rams, the blind Cyclops “did not notice” the men bound beneath them (9.442–443).
---

νόος is first the mind's bent: the people Odysseus hopes to find are those whose νόος is θεουδής, “a mind that fears the gods”, the half-verse that joins piety to the welcome of strangers whenever he lands (9.176 = 6.121; see [[xenia]] and [[theos]]). The Cyclops answers the question by boasting that the Cyclopes care nothing for the gods (9.275–276). The verb is perception: blinded, he feels along the backs of all his sheep as they stand, “but the fool did not notice / how the men were bound under the chests of the woolly sheep” (τὸ δὲ νήπιος οὐκ ἐνόησεν, 9.442–443; see [[nepios]]). The man who boasted of his strength cannot see, and does not think to feel beneath.
