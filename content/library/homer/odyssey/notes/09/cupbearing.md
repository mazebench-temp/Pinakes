# οἰνοχόος (9.10; again 18.418, 21.263).
grc: [οἰνοχόος]
en: [the wine-pourer]
---

The cupbearer belongs to Odysseus' picture of the finest thing: the banqueters sitting in order, the tables laden, “and drawing wine from the mixing bowl / the wine-pourer carries it round and pours it into the cups” (9.8–10; see [[feast]], [[mixing-bowl]] and [[cup]]). Plato's Socrates quotes these verses as unfit for the young to hear, since they make “the wisest man” say that this seems to him the finest thing of all (*Republic* 390a–b; see [[plato]]). The rest of the book's wine is drawn off for other uses: from Ismarus into two-handled jars (9.164–165), by Maron into twelve jars (9.204; see [[two-handled-jar]]), and by Odysseus himself into the Cyclops' ivy-wood bowl, three times (9.346–361; see [[wine]]).
