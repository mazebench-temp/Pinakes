summary: At Ismarus the wives and possessions of the city are divided “so that for my part no one might go away cheated of his fair share” (9.41–42), and the Cyclops' flocks on the island in the same words (9.548–549). Of the goats of the island nine fall by lot to each ship, and ten are chosen out for Odysseus alone (9.159–160); of the flocks the companions give him the ram “as a special gift” (9.550–551).
# δασσάμεθʼ (9.42, 9.549), "δέκʼ ἔξελον" (9.160; bare ἔξελον would also take the prize chosen for Alcinous at 7.10),
# "δόσαν ἔξοχα" (9.551).
grc: [δασσάμεθʼ, "δέκʼ ἔξελον", "δόσαν ἔξοχα"]
en: [divided them, they chose out ten, as a special gift]
---

**A fair share.** The spoil of Ismarus is divided at once, “so that for my part no one might go away cheated of his fair share” (9.41–42), and the flocks taken from the Cyclops are shared on the island in the same verse (9.548–549). μή τίς μοι is “that no one, as far as I could help it”, μοι having the force of the later ἐμοῦ γʼ ἕνεκα (W. W. Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 9.42; see the translation's note on 9.42). The verse-end is Nestor's, from his tale of the Elean spoil, which his father gave to the people to divide “so that no one might go away cheated of his fair share” (*Iliad* 11.704–705; see [[raiding]]).

**The leader's portion.** The leader's share is chosen for him by the men. The goats of the island of goats are allotted, nine to each ship, “but for me alone they chose out ten” (9.159–160; see [[lots]]): ἔξελον is the third person plural, the men choosing for him, as the sons of the Achaeans chose Briseis out for Achilles as his prize (*Iliad* 16.56; Merry on 9.160; see [[geras]]). On the island after the escape it is the ram: “But the ram my well-greaved companions gave to me alone, / as a special gift when the flocks were divided” (μήλων δαιομένων δόσαν ἔξοχα, 9.550–551), the ram that had carried him out of the cave, which he sacrifices to Zeus (Merry on 9.550; see [[ram]] and [[sacrifice]]).
