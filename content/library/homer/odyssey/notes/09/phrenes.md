---
summary: The φρένες are both the mind and an organ: Odysseus thinks to stab the Cyclops “where the midriff holds the liver” (9.301); the wine comes “around the Cyclops' wits” (9.362), and he speaks of the man who “overpowered my wits with wine” (9.454); Odysseus under the ram is “thinking shrewd thoughts” (9.445).
# "πυκινὰ φρονέοντι" (9.445); bare φρονέοντι would also take 5.474 and 6.145.
grc: ["πυκινὰ φρονέοντι"]
en: [midriff, wits, thinking]
---

**The organ.** In the cave Odysseus means to stab the Cyclops, as he lies among his flocks, “in the chest, where the midriff holds the liver” (ὅθι φρένες ἧπαρ ἔχουσι, 9.301), the φρένες of the body, as where Patroclus' spear strikes Sarpedon, “where the midriff closes around the throbbing heart” (*Iliad* 16.481; see the translation's table of key words and [[stethos]]). Merry renders “where the midriff enfolds the liver”, a sufficiently accurate account of the liver lying in the hollow of the diaphragm (W. W. Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 9.301).

**The wits.** The wine does its work on the mind: “when the wine had come around the Cyclops' wits” (περὶ φρένας ἤλυθεν, 9.362), where Κύκλωπα is the object of the compound verb and φρένας defines it more nearly, as when the cry came around Nestor's mind (*Iliad* 10.139; Merry on 9.362; see [[wine]]). The Cyclops later blames the same thing: Nobody blinded him “when he had overpowered my wits with wine” (9.454). Odysseus' wits stay with him: he lies under the ram “thinking shrewd thoughts” (πυκινὰ φρονέοντι, 9.445; see [[metis]]), and the Cyclops, for his part, thought him so foolish “in his mind” as to walk out among the sheep (9.419; see [[nepios]]).
