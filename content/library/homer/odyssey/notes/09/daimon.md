---
summary: As the companions stand round the glowing stake, “a god breathed great daring into us” (9.381).
---

δαίμων here is the unnamed divine power that a man feels at work in himself: “a god breathed great daring into us” (θάρσος ἐνέπνευσεν μέγα δαίμων, 9.381; see [[tharsos]]). Odysseus' tale names other such powers without names: “some god guided us” through the dark night to the island of goats (καί τις θεὸς ἡγεμόνευεν, 9.142), “the god gave us game to satisfy the heart” (9.158), and the Cyclops drove all his flocks into the cave, “either because he suspected something, or because a god so bade him” (9.339; see [[theos]], [[divine-intervention]] and [[some-things-you-will-think-of-yourself]]).
