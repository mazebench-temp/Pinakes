---
summary: In the simile of the blinding the smith dips an axe or an adze in cold water to temper it, “for this in turn is the strength of iron” (9.391–393).
---

The book's one κράτος is a metal's. The Cyclops' eye hisses round the stake “as when a smith dips a great axe or an adze / in cold water, and it hisses loudly, / to temper it; for this in turn is the strength of iron” (τὸ γὰρ αὖτε σιδήρου γε κράτος ἐστίν, 9.391–393; see [[the-smith-tempering-an-axe]] and [[iron]]). The emphatic antithesis implies that the tempering which so strengthens iron destroys the Cyclops' eye (W. W. Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 9.393). The strength of the Cyclops himself is another word, his “mighty strength” (κρατερῆφι βίηφι, 9.476; see [[krateros]] and [[kertomia]]).
