---
summary: The Cyclops tests Odysseus with a question about his ship, and Odysseus answers with a lie, that Poseidon wrecked it (9.279–286); over the wine he gives a false name, Nobody (9.364–367), which deceives the other Cyclopes (9.408–414); and from the ship he tells the truth, his name, his father's and his home's (9.502–505).
refs: [9.281-286, 9.364-367, 9.408, 9.414, 9.502-505]
---

**The test and the lie.** The Cyclops asks where the strangers' ship is moored, “testing me, but he did not escape my notice, for I knew much” (9.279–281; see [[peira]]), and Odysseus answers “with crafty words”: “My ship Poseidon the earth-shaker shattered, / dashing it against the rocks at the borders of your land” (9.282–284; see [[dolos]]). The lie names the god who will turn out to be the Cyclops' father (9.519, 9.529; see [[poseidon]]).

**The false name.** Over the wine he tells the Cyclops, with soothing words, “Nobody is my name” (9.363–367), a lie that is true in its words, since it is the pronoun “no one”. When the blinded Cyclops says that “Nobody” is killing him, he tells the truth and is not believed, and Odysseus' heart laughs “how my name and my flawless plan had deceived him” (ἐξαπάτησεν, 9.408–414; see [[nobody]], [[apate]] and [[metis]]).

**The truth.** At the end he gives up the disguise: “say that Odysseus, sacker of cities, blinded you, / the son of Laertes, who has his home in Ithaca” (9.504–505), and the truth fulfills an old prophecy and arms a curse (9.507–536; see [[thesphaton]] and [[curses]]).
