# κλήρῳ (9.331), "οἱ δʼ ἔλαχον" (9.334), λάγχανον (9.160; again 14.233).
grc: [κλήρῳ, "οἱ δʼ ἔλαχον", λάγχανον]
en: [cast lots, the lot fell, fell by lot]
---

**Shares by lot.** The hunt on the island of goats yields a share for each ship: “Twelve ships followed me, and to each / nine goats fell by lot; but for me alone they chose out ten” (9.159–160). λάγχανον is the one place in Homer where λαγχάνω means “fall by lot”, with the goats as its subject; one manuscript reads αἶγας, which would give the usual construction, the men getting nine goats by lot (W. W. Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 9.160 and his critical note; see the translation's note on 9.159–160). Odysseus' ten are not allotted but chosen out for him (see [[division-of-spoils]]).

**The lot for the stake.** In the cave the lot chooses the men for the danger:

{{quote:dec6142b-efed-5a48-aef4-e1a2583af999}}

πεπαλάσθαι is Aristarchus' reading, as in Nestor's “Now cast lots, all of you in turn” (κλήρῳ νῦν πεπάλασθε, *Iliad* 7.171): the shaking belongs properly to the pebbles in the helmet and is transferred to the men who shake them (Merry on 9.331 and his critical note; see the translation's note on 9.331). καί stresses ἤθελον: they were the men he would have liked to choose even without a lot, and ἐλέγμην is “I counted myself in” (Merry on 9.334, 9.335). So in the Iliad the lot that leapt from the helmet was the one “that they themselves wanted”, Ajax's (ὃν ἄρʼ ἤθελον αὐτοί, *Iliad* 7.182); here it falls on the men the leader would have picked (see [[hetairos]] and [[tharsos]]).
