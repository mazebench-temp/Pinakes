---
summary: “Hear me, Poseidon, dark-haired earth-holder” (9.528), and at the end of the prayer, “So he spoke in prayer, and the dark-haired god heard him” (9.536).
---

At 9.536 the epithet stands alone for the god's name, as in the Iliad (*Iliad* 20.144), in the verse with which Athena hears Nestor and Odysseus (3.385, 6.328; see the Odyssey translation's formulas; [[hear-me]], [[earth-holder]], [[poseidon]]).
