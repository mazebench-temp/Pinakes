---
summary: The harbor of the island off the Cyclopes' land needs no cable, no mooring-stones and no stern-cables (9.136–137). Twice Odysseus bids his men go on board and loose the stern-cables (9.177–178, 9.561–562).
# πείσματος, "cable" (9.136), is caught here (it applies in the Odyssey only); the Book 6 note has πείσματα.
grc: [πείσματος]
en: [cable]
---

The island has “a harbor with good anchorage, where there is no need of a cable, / neither to cast out mooring-stones nor to make fast stern-cables, / but men may run their ships ashore and wait a while, until the sailors' / hearts urge them on, and the breezes blow fair” (9.136–139; see [[mooring-stones]], [[the-island-of-goats]] and the translation's row ἐύορμος). πεῖσμα is properly the cable from ship to shore, generally the same as the πρυμνήσια, but here it stands for the mooring tackle as a whole, divided into the cable from the bows to the mooring-stones and the stern-cables between the stern and the shore (W. W. Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 9.136; see the translation's note on 9.136–138). The verse of departure, “to go on board themselves and loose the stern-cables”, sends Odysseus' own ship to the Cyclopes and the whole fleet away from the island (9.178, 9.562; see [[rowing-benches]]).
