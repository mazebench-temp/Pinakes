# πανάργυρον, "all of silver" (9.203; later 24.275, the same gift in Odysseus' tale to Laertes), first met here (it
# applies in the Odyssey only); χρυσοῦ (9.202) is in the Book 4 note.
grc: [πανάργυρον]
en: [all of silver]
---

“And he gave me splendid gifts: / he gave me seven talents of well-made gold, / and he gave me a mixing bowl all of silver” (9.201–203; see [[maron]], [[talent]], [[mixing-bowl]] and [[well-made]]). The gold is weighed, not counted: τάλαντα are a definite weight of gold, not coined money (W. W. Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 9.202; see the translation's note on 9.202). Maron's gifts are his thanks for the protection of himself, his child and his wife at the sack of Ismarus (9.198–201; see [[guest-gifts]] and [[ismarus]]), and the gold and the silver are told before the wine, the gift that will matter (9.203–205; see [[wine]]). In his false tale to Laertes Odysseus lists the same gold and the same bowl among the gifts he once gave a guest (24.274–275, Greek only).
