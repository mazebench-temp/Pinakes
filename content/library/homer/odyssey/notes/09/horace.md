---
summary: In the Ars Poetica Horace counts the Cyclops among the striking wonders that Homer keeps for later in the Odyssey, after a modest beginning.
---

- **The wonders.** Homer does not open with a boast but means to bring light out of smoke, “so that he may then bring out his striking wonders, Antiphates and Scylla, and with the Cyclops Charybdis” (*speciosa dehinc miracula promat, / Antiphaten Scyllamque et cum Cyclope Charybdim*, *Ars Poetica* 143–145, a plain rendering). The Cyclops is the first of the four in the order of the tale, the wonder of this book: [[horace-on-the-opening-of-the-odyssey]], [[in-medias-res]] and [[polyphemus-the-cyclops]].
