---
summary: The Cyclops eats two of Odysseus' companions “like a mountain-bred lion”, leaving nothing, “entrails and flesh and marrowy bones” (9.292–293).
---

“and he ate like a mountain-bred lion, and left nothing, / entrails and flesh and marrowy bones” (9.292–293; see [[the-devouring-lion]], [[mountain-bred]] and [[polyphemus-the-cyclops]]). It is the one lion of the book. In the Iliad the mountain-bred lion is a warrior, Sarpedon at the Achaean wall and Menelaus stripping the armor of Euphorbus (*Iliad* 12.299, 17.59–69), and in Book 6 it was Odysseus himself, driven by hunger among the girls (6.130–136; see [[the-hungry-lion]]); here it is a man-eater at his meal, and the prey are men.
