---
summary: The maxim closes the speech in which Odysseus at last gives the Phaeacians his name and his land (9.19–36): Ithaca is rugged but a good nurse of young men, the goddesses who held him back never persuaded his heart, and nothing is sweeter than one's native land and parents. Then he turns to the tale of his homecoming (9.37–38).
refs: [9.27, 9.35-36]
---

**Before the tale.** Alcinous asked the stranger for his name, his land and his city (8.550–556; see [[no-one-is-nameless]]). The answer gives all three in order: the name and the fame, “I am Odysseus, son of Laertes” (9.19–20); the island, clear-seen Ithaca with its mountain Neritum and the islands around it, low and farthest out toward the darkness, “rugged, but a good nurse of young men” (9.21–27; see [[ithaca]] and [[rugged]]); and the love of it, “and I, for my part, / can see nothing else sweeter than one's own land” (9.27–28). Calypso and Circe follow as the proof, both “longing for me to be her husband” (9.29–32; see [[calypso]], [[circe]] and [[posis]]), “but they never persuaded the heart in my breast” (9.33; see [[peitho]]); and the maxim draws the lesson from them (9.34–36). The next verse begins the story the Phaeacians asked for: “But come, let me tell you also of my homecoming of many sorrows” (9.37; see [[nostos]] and [[the-tale-to-alcinous]]).

**The text.** Verse 30, which repeats 1.15, is wanting in most manuscripts and is not quoted by Eustathius; Wolf rejected it, and most editors followed him. Verses 34–36 are marked with an obelus in the Ambrosian manuscript Q and in one or two others, a judgment that Nitzsch and Bekker accepted; Sengebusch, as Köchly quotes him, held it certain that the athetesis ran from 29 to 36, so that 28 was followed directly by 37. Merry finds 35–36 at least suspicious, since τοκήων ends both 34 and 36, and the addition of γίγνεται, with the clause that follows, mars the gnomic form of 34 (W. W. Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, his critical note on 9.30, 9.34–36; see the translation's note on 9.29–36). The text prints all the lines, and the English keeps them.
