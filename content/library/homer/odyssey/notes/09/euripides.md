---
summary: Euripides' satyr play Cyclops stages this book's adventure under Etna, keeping Maron's wine, the ivy cup, the name Nobody, the drill simile and the oracle, and Merry cites it again and again for the book's details. A messenger in the Alcestis describes Heracles drinking from an ivy cup until the wine comes round him, as it comes round the Cyclops' wits.
---

- **The Cyclops.** Silenus and the satyrs, cast on Etna by an east wind when they were already near Malea, serve the Cyclops Polyphemus; Odysseus comes with Maron's wine, gives his name as Nobody, blinds the Cyclops with a stake of olive wood twirled like a shipwright's drill, and names himself at the end, when the Cyclops recognizes an old oracle (*Cyclops* 1–709). Merry sets the play beside 9.107, 9.197, 9.219, 9.234, 9.288, 9.297, 9.326, 9.346 and 9.507, and finds the Cyclops' cooking “a later refinement, not found in the Homeric story” (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 9.288): [[euripides-cyclops]], [[polyphemus-the-cyclops]] and [[satyr-play]].
- **The wine comes round.** For “when the wine had come around the Cyclops' wits” (9.362), Merry cites the servant's account of Heracles at Admetus' house: taking an ivy cup in his hands, he drinks the unmixed wine of the dark grape “until the flame of the wine came round him and warmed him” (*Alcestis* 756–759, a plain rendering; Merry on 9.362): [[wine]] and [[cup]].
