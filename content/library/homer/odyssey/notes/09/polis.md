# "ἐκ πόλιος" (9.41; again 24.205); bare πόλιος would also take 6.262 and 8.524.

**The sacked city.** The tale begins with a sack: “There I sacked the city and destroyed the men themselves” (πόλιν ἔπραθον, ὤλεσα δʼ αὐτούς, 9.40), where αὐτούς is the people set against the place (W. W. Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 9.40); “and from the city we took their wives and many possessions / and divided them” (9.41–42; see [[raiding]], [[captive-women]] and [[division-of-spoils]]). The Cicones were allies of Troy, which Merry thinks accounts for the burning of their city by a Greek (on 9.39; *Iliad* 2.846; see [[cicones]] and [[ismarus]]). The wine of the next days had been drawn off “when we took the sacred citadel of the Cicones” (9.165; see [[wine]] and [[hieros]]). Odysseus' boast to the Cyclops names the greater sack: Agamemnon, “so great a city did he sack” (9.265; see [[agamemnon]] and [[troy]]), and the epithet he gives himself, “sacker of cities”, is the name the Cyclops will curse (9.504, 9.530; see [[sacker-of-cities]]).

**A people without cities.** The Cyclopes live in caves on the mountain peaks (9.113–114), and have no ships “going to the cities of men, as men often / cross the sea in ships to visit one another” (ἄστεʼ ἐπʼ ἀνθρώπων, 9.128–129; see [[cyclopes]] and [[the-city-and-the-household]]).
