---
summary: Words are the book's weapons and its pleas: the companions beg Odysseus “with words” (9.224), he answers the Cyclops “with crafty words” (9.282) and lulls him “with soothing words” (9.363), encourages his men “with words” (9.376), and the companions try to hold him back “with soothing words” (9.493); the Cyclopes answer Polyphemus with “winged words” (9.409).
---

ἔπεα are the ordinary words of speech, and the book's phrases name what they do. The companions beg “at first with words” (9.224; see [[supplication]]); Odysseus answers the Cyclops' first question “with words” (9.258), his question about the ship “with crafty words” (δολίοις ἐπέεσσι, 9.282; see [[dolos]]), and, when the wine has done its work, speaks to him “with soothing words” (ἔπεσσι … μειλιχίοισι, 9.363; see [[soothing-words]]) to give him the false name (9.364–367; see [[nobody]]). The same soothing words are the companions' when they try to stop him taunting the Cyclops (9.492–493), and his own taunts are “cutting words” (9.474; see [[kertomia]]). The Cyclopes answer Polyphemus' cry with the formula of a reply, “And they answered him and spoke winged words” (9.409; see [[winged-words]]).
