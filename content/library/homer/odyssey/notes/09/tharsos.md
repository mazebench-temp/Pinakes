# θάρσυνον (9.377) is claimed by the entry's stem; the English "encouraged" falls on 9.376.
en: [encouraged]
refs: [9.376]
---

The blinding needs courage, and the book gives it two sources. Odysseus' own words come first: he drove the stake under the ashes until it grew hot, “and with words I encouraged all my companions, / so that no one might draw back from me in fear” (ἔπεσσι … θάρσυνον, 9.376–377; see [[epos]] and [[deos]]). Then, as the green olive wood was about to catch fire and glowed terribly, and the men stood round him, “a god breathed great daring into us” (θάρσος ἐνέπνευσεν μέγα δαίμων, 9.381; see [[daimon]] and [[divine-intervention]]). The four the lot had chosen take the stake and drive it into the eye (9.331–335, 9.382–383; see [[lots]]). θάρσος in Homer is nearly always a god's gift (see the entry); here the god has no name.
