---
summary: Odysseus names himself by his father, “I am Odysseus, son of Laertes” (9.19), and so again to the Cyclops, “the son of Laertes, who has his home in Ithaca” (9.505); the Cyclops repeats the verse in his prayer to Poseidon (9.531).
# Λαερτιάδης (9.19) and Λαέρτεω (9.505, 9.531) are claimed by the entry.
---

The patronymic opens the name that the king asked for: εἴμʼ Ὀδυσεὺς Λαερτιάδης, “I am Odysseus, son of Laertes” (9.19; see [[son-of-laertes]] and [[odysseus]]). At the end of the Cyclops' story Odysseus names himself again in the same way: his second taunt names him in full, by his epithet, his father and his home: “say that Odysseus, sacker of cities, blinded you, / the son of Laertes, who has his home in Ithaca” (9.504–505; see [[say-that-odysseus-blinded-you]]), and the Cyclops turns the two verses into his prayer that Odysseus may not reach home (9.530–531; see [[polyphemus-the-cyclops]] and [[curses]]). Since Wolf nearly all editors have bracketed 9.531, which is wanting in twelve manuscripts and in the text of Eustathius (W. W. Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, critical note on 9.531; see the translation's note on 9.531).
