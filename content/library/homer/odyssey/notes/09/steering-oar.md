---
summary: After the storm “the wind and the helmsmen steered the ships” (9.78). Each of the two rocks that the Cyclops hurls at Odysseus' ship “failed to reach the tip of the steering oar”, the first falling in front of the ship and the second behind it (9.481–483, 9.539–540).
---

When the masts are set up again, “the wind and the helmsmen steered the ships” (9.78; see [[sails]]). The steering oar is named in the verse told of both of the Cyclops' throws: the first rock falls “in front of the dark-prowed ship, / by a little, and it failed to reach the tip of the steering oar” (9.482–483), and the second “behind the dark-prowed ship, / by a little, and it failed to reach the tip of the steering oar” (9.539–540; see [[polyphemus-the-cyclops]], [[dark-prowed]] and [[ships]]). οἰήιον (from οἴαξ) is properly the handle that turns the paddle or πηδάλιον, and W. W. Merry renders the second verse “but he failed to reach the end of the steering-paddle” (*Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 9.539–540; see the translation's row οἰήιον, πλημυρίς, κοντός). The verse suits the second throw, Merry argues, but “cannot be read here” at the first: a stone that fell in front of the ship would not go near the steering oar, since the stern faced the shore; probably the rock passed clean over the ship and fell before her bows (on 9.483). Eustathius reports that the verse bore the asterisk with the obelus, and editors since Wolf bracket or delete it; the text prints it, and the English keeps it, taking τυτθόν with προπάροιθε (see the translation's note on 9.483).
