---
summary: αἰπὺς ὄλεθρος, “sheer destruction,” twice: “but I with these escaped sheer destruction” (9.286), and “For there we too would have perished by sheer destruction” (9.303).
---

The first is Odysseus' lie, the second the truth that held his sword back: with the Cyclops dead, no one could move the stone from the doorway (9.299–305). Merry takes καὶ ἄμμες as “we as well as he,” assassins and victim together (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 9.303; [[decision-scene]], [[door-stone]]).
