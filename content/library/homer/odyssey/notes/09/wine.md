summary: The wine of Ismarus, drawn off into jars when the city was taken, is drunk on the shore and on the island of goats (9.45, 9.161–165). Maron's sweet unmixed wine, “a divine drink”, which was drunk one cup to twenty of water, goes in a goatskin to the Cyclops' cave (9.196–215); Odysseus gives it to him three times, the Cyclops calls it “a branch of ambrosia and nectar”, and he falls asleep, sodden with wine (9.345–374).

**The wine of Ismarus.** After the sack of the Cicones' city the men will not flee: “there much wine was drunk, and many sheep / they slaughtered beside the shore” (9.45–46; see [[the-raid-on-the-cicones]] and [[ismarus]]). On the island off the Cyclopes' land they feast all day “on untold meat and sweet wine; / for the red wine was not yet spent from the ships, / but there was still some in them; for we had each drawn off much in two-handled jars / when we took the sacred citadel of the Cicones” (9.161–165; see [[two-handled-jar]], [[red]] and [[the-island-of-goats]]). The text prints a stop after Κικόνων, which the English does not follow (see the translation's note on 9.165).

**Maron's wine.** For the cave Odysseus takes “a goatskin of dark wine, / sweet wine, which Maron, son of Euanthes, had given me” (9.196–197; see [[maron]] and [[dark]]), the priest of Apollo whom they had spared at Ismarus. Among his gifts were twelve two-handled jars of “sweet unmixed wine, a divine drink”, which none of the household knew of but Maron, his wife and one housekeeper (9.204–207; see [[divine]] and the translation's row ἀκηράσιος):

In the earliest times, W. W. Merry notes, the wine was poured into the water, not, as later, the water into the wine; ἓν δέπας ἐμπλήσας is “one full cup”, poured into twenty measures (*Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 9.209; see [[mixing-bowl]], [[cup]] and the translation's note on 9.209). The wine of Maroneia, Merry adds, kept something of its fame in Pliny's time, when it was drunk eight measures of water to one (on 9.209, citing *Natural History* 14.6). “With this I filled a great skin and carried it” (9.212): ἀσκός is the wineskin, the goatskin in which the wine of the oath is carried from Troy and Nausicaa's wine is packed for the river (*Iliad* 3.247; 6.78; see [[provisions]]).

**The wine and the Cyclops.** Odysseus comes close with “an ivy-wood bowl of dark wine” and offers it as a libation, so that the Cyclops may pity him (9.345–350; see [[cup]] and [[libation]]). “He took it and drank it off; and he was terribly pleased / drinking the sweet drink” (9.353–354), and he asks for more, and for the stranger's name: “for the grain-giving earth bears for the Cyclopes too / wine of great clusters, and the rain of Zeus makes it grow for them; / but this is a branch of ambrosia and nectar” (9.355–359). The Cyclopes, Merry paraphrases, know what good wine is, but this is something quite out of the way (on 9.357): their own grows unsown (9.110–111; see [[vine]] and [[cyclopes]]). ἀπορρώξ is properly a piece broken off, Homer's word for a stream that is a “branch” of the Styx (Merry on 9.359; *Iliad* 2.755; 10.514; see [[ambrosia]], [[nectar]] and the translation's row ἀπορρώξ). “Three times I brought it and gave it to him, and three times he drank it off in his folly” (9.361), and when “the wine had come around the Cyclops' wits” (9.362) Odysseus tells him that his name is Nobody (see [[nobody]]). The Cyclops falls on his back, “and from his throat rushed wine / and gobbets of human flesh; and he belched, sodden with wine” (9.373–374): οἰνοβαρείων is the Odyssey's form of the Iliad's insult οἰνοβαρές, “wine-sodden” (*Iliad* 1.225; see [[wine-sodden]]). In the Cyclops' own account of his blinding the wine is to blame: a bad man blinded him “when he had overpowered my wits with wine” (9.454), one small and feeble “when he had overpowered me with wine” (9.515–516; see [[polyphemus-the-cyclops]]).
