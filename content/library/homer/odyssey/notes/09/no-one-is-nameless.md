---
summary: Odysseus answers Alcinous' question at the beginning of his tale: “Now first I will tell you my name … I am Odysseus, son of Laertes” (9.16–20). In the tale he gives the Cyclops a false name in the words of the question: “Nobody they call me, / my mother and my father and all my other companions” (9.366–367).
refs: [9.16, 9.19, 9.366-367]
---

**The name given.** Alcinous asked for the name “by which they called you there, your mother and your father” (8.550), and at verse 16 of the tale the stranger complies (W. W. Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 9.1): “Now first I will tell you my name, so that you too / may know it” (9.16–17), so that he may be their guest-friend when he is far away (9.17–18; see [[xenia]]):

{{quote:b7f8b7f2-9af6-5dd6-9e88-c49f9d432bba}}

The name comes with the father's and with the fame (see [[odysseus]] and [[kleos]]). The fame reaches the height that the lay of his quarrel with Achilles had reached at the beginning of Book 8 (8.74; see [[oime]]). The land and the city come next, Ithaca and its neighbors (9.21–28; see [[ithaca]] and [[nothing-sweeter-than-ones-own-land]]).

**The name withheld.** In the tale he gives the Cyclops another name, and gives it in the words of Alcinous' question: “Nobody is my name; Nobody they call me, / my mother and my father and all my other companions” (Οὖτις ἐμοί γʼ ὄνομα· Οὖτιν δέ με κικλήσκουσι / μήτηρ ἠδὲ πατὴρ ἠδʼ ἄλλοι πάντες ἑταῖροι, 9.366–367; see [[nobody]]). Mother, father and the others are those whom Alcinous named, κάλεον μήτηρ τε πατήρ τε, / ἄλλοι θʼ (8.550–551); the maxim's claim that no one at all is nameless is answered with a name that means no one. The blinded Cyclops speaks of “the evils that Nobody, a nobody, gave me” (οὐτιδανὸς … Οὖτις, 9.460), and the true name comes at the end, shouted from the ship (9.502–505; see [[say-that-odysseus-blinded-you]]).
