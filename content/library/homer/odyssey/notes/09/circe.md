# Unnamed: "the wily one of Aeaea, longing for me to be her husband" (9.32), where Αἰαίη, "of Aeaea", is her epithet.
refs: [9.32]
---

**The wily one of Aeaea.** Calypso held him back, Odysseus tells the Phaeacians, “and in the same way Circe kept me in her halls, / the wily one of Aeaea, longing for me to be her husband” (9.31–32; see [[calypso]]). ὡς δʼ αὔτως, “and in the same way”, is the regular collocation in Homer for the later ὡσαύτως δέ (W. W. Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 9.31). Αἰαίη, “of Aeaea”, is the name of her island too (10.135; Merry on 9.32; see the translation's note on 9.32 and [[aeaea]]). δολόεσσα, “wily”, is the epithet that Odysseus gave Calypso in his story to Arete (7.245; see [[wily]] and the translation's key-word row on δολόεις). Sengebusch, whom Köchly quotes, held that an ancient athetesis ran from 9.29 to 9.36, which would take these verses with it (Merry's critical note on 9.30; see [[calypso]]).

**Her story.** In Book 10 Odysseus stays a full year with her. When his companions call on him to remember his native land (10.469–474), he begs her to keep her promise to send him home, and she answers at once that they must not stay any longer in her house against their will, but must first go to the house of Hades to consult Teiresias (10.483–495; see [[hades]]).
