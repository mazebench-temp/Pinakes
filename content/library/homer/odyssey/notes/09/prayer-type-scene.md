summary: The Cyclops' prayer to his father: the gesture, “prayed, stretching out his hands to the starry sky” (9.527); the invocation and the claim, “Hear me, Poseidon, dark-haired earth-holder, / if truly I am yours, and you claim to be my father” (9.528–529); the request, that Odysseus may not reach home, or, if he must, that he may come late and in evil plight (9.530–535); and the answer, “So he spoke in prayer, and the dark-haired god heard him” (9.536).

Every part of the pattern is here, and the claim on the god is kinship, not past sacrifice. 9.527 is the verse of Nestor's prayer at the ships (*Iliad* 15.371), and the prayer is heard in the verse of Nestor's and Odysseus' prayers to Athena (3.385, 6.328). What it asks is a curse, and Teiresias' prophecy will repeat its words (11.114–115; see the Odyssey translation's formulas; [[curses]], [[hear-me]], [[poseidon]]). Earlier in the book the companions lift their hands to Zeus as their friends are eaten (9.294), and the Cyclopes tell Polyphemus, “you must pray to your father, lord Poseidon” (9.412).
