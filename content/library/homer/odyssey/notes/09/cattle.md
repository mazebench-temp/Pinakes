summary: The cattle of Book 9 are the “shambling cattle with curving horns” that Odysseus' men slaughter on the shore of Ismarus with the sheep, when they should have fled (9.45–46), and the oxen of the plowman's day, by which the hour of the battle is told (9.58). The Cyclops keeps sheep and goats, not cattle.
# βουλυτόνδε (9.58), "toward the time for unyoking oxen", as the Iliad's βουλυτὸν δέ (16.779, its Book 16 note).

**At Ismarus.** Odysseus bade his men flee, and they would not obey: “But there much wine was drunk, and many sheep / they slaughtered beside the shore, and shambling cattle with curving horns” (9.45–46; see [[sheep]], [[shambling]], [[with-curving-horns]] and [[the-raid-on-the-cicones]]). The subject of ἔσφαζον is the companions who would not obey (W. W. Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 9.46). The verse-end εἰλίποδας ἕλικας βοῦς is the formula of the suitors' slaughter of Odysseus' herds (1.92) and of the feast of Phoenix's kinsmen (*Iliad* 9.466; see the Book 1 note).

**The time for unyoking oxen.** The battle with the Cicones turns in the afternoon: “But when the sun moved on toward the time for unyoking oxen, / then at last the Cicones overpowered the Achaeans and turned them back” (9.58–59; see [[helios]] and [[cicones]]). The hour is the plowman's, as in the verse with which the fight over Cebriones turns in the Iliad (*Iliad* 16.779; see the translation's note on 9.58).

**No cattle in the cave.** The Cyclops' flocks are sheep and goats (9.184; see [[goats]]). Euripides gave his Cyclops cows as well: Silenus offers Odysseus cheese and cow's milk (*Cyclops* 136), as W. W. Merry notes (on 9.234), and the Cyclops asks whether the milk in his bowls is of sheep or of cows or mixed (*Cyclops* 218).
