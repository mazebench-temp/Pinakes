---
summary: The last of the islands that Odysseus names around Ithaca is “wooded Zacynthus” (9.24).
---

Odysseus names the islands around Ithaca, “Dulichium and Same and wooded Zacynthus” (9.24; see [[ithaca]], [[dulichium]] and [[samos]]), Telemachus' verse in the nominative (1.246). Here the adjective has its feminine form, ὑλήεσσα Ζάκυνθος, where 1.246 has ὑλήεντι Ζακύνθῳ, not ὑληέσσῃ: like ἡμαθόεις and ἀμπελόεις, ὑλήεις sometimes serves for the feminine with the masculine ending, probably for the meter (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 9.24; see [[wooded]]). The same epithet is given to the peak of a mountain in the book, when the Cyclops is likened to “a wooded peak / of high mountains” (9.191–192), and to the island of goats, ὑλήεσσʼ, “a wooded one” (9.118; see [[the-island-of-goats]]).
