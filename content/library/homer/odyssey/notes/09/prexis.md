# "ἅλα πρήσσοντες" (9.491), the verb of the noun, of making one's way over the sea.
grc: ["ἅλα πρήσσοντες"]
en: [gone over]
---

The question that Nestor asked his guests after the meal the Cyclops asks before it: “Strangers, who are you? From where do you sail the watery ways? / Is it on some business (κατὰ πρῆξιν), or do you wander at random / over the sea, like pirates” (9.252–254 = 3.71–73; see [[strangers-who-are-you]], [[piracy]] and [[xenia]]). πρῆξις is the business a voyage is made for, trade or an errand, against the aimless wandering of the raider (see the entry and the Book 3 note). The verb returns of the voyage itself: ἅλα πρήσσειν, “to make one's way over the sea”, is like κέλευθον πρήσσειν (W. W. Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 9.491). “But when we had gone over the sea twice as far, / then I spoke to the Cyclops” (9.491–492), a distance that should have put him out of hearing (Merry on 9.491; see [[kertomia]]).
