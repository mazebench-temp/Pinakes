---
summary: The shores of Book 9 are the beach of Ismarus, where the men slaughter sheep and cattle when they should have fled (9.46), and the beach of the island of goats, where the ships run aground in the dark and the men step out “where the sea breaks upon the shore”, sleep and feast, and where Odysseus sacrifices the ram (9.146–151, 9.168–169, 9.543–559). Twice the wave of the Cyclops' rock drives the ship to the shore, first of the mainland and then of the island (9.486, 9.542).
# χέρσον (9.147, 9.486, 9.542; again 6.95), the dry land on which the waves break.
grc: [χέρσον]
---

**Running ashore.** At Ismarus the men slaughter the sheep and cattle of the spoil “beside the shore” (παρὰ θῖνα, 9.46; see [[cattle]] and [[the-raid-on-the-cicones]]). At the island of goats no cable is needed: men may run their ships ashore and wait for a fair wind (9.136–139; see [[harbor]]). The fleet comes in on a moonless night, and no one saw “the long waves rolling toward the shore, / before we ran our well-benched ships ashore” (9.147–148; see [[moon]]); κελσάσῃσι νηυσί, “when the ships were beached”, is a dative where a genitive absolute would be expected (W. W. Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 9.149; see the translation's table of key words). “and we ourselves too stepped out where the sea breaks upon the shore” (9.150 = 9.547) is the verse of the Iliad's landing at Chryse (*Iliad* 1.437), and the men sleep there twice, after the hunt and after the escape (9.168–169 = 9.558–559). Coming back from the cave, “there we came and ran the ship ashore on the sand” (9.546).

**Two shores.** The swell from the first rock drives the ship back “to reach the shore” (9.486), the shore of the mainland where the Cyclops lives; the wave of the second drives it “to reach the shore” of the island (9.542), the same words for the opposite course (Merry on 9.542; see [[sea]]).

**The sacrifice.** The ram that carried Odysseus out of the cave is sacrificed to Zeus “on the shore” (ἐπὶ θινί, 9.551; see [[ram]]).
