---
summary: Odysseus' appeal to the Cyclops: “But we in our turn have reached you and come to your knees, / to see if you will give us a guest-gift” (9.266–267), “we are your suppliants, / and Zeus is the avenger of suppliants and strangers” (9.269–270); and the refusal, “You are a fool, stranger” (9.273).
refs: [9.259-280]
---

The suppliant names his people and his claim, but the knees are reached only in words, and the plea rests on Zeus, “the god of guests” (9.271; see the translation's note on 9.271). The Cyclops refuses with a boast, asks where the ship is moored, and, when Odysseus has answered with a lie, eats two of the men (9.273–293; [[supplication]], [[xenia]], [[good-sir]]).
