# Unnamed: "a singer such as this one is" (9.3-4); ἀοιδοῦ (9.3, 9.7) is claimed by singers.
refs: [9.3-4]
---

The song of the Wooden Horse has just ended, and the king has stopped the lyre because his guest wept at it (8.499–543; see [[odysseus-weeps-at-the-feast]]). Odysseus begins his answer by praising the singer whose songs made him weep: “truly it is a fine thing to listen to a singer / such as this one is, like the gods in his voice” (9.3–4; see [[like-a-god-in-his-voice]]). The two verses are those with which Telemachus bade the suitors stop their shouting, for the sake of Phemius' song (1.369–371; W. W. Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 9.3–4; see [[phemius]]). On the singer at the feast, see [[singers]].
