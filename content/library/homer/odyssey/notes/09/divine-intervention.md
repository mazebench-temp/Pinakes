---
summary: The tale gives the gods their part, mostly unnamed: Zeus raises the storm after Ismarus (9.67–69); “some god guided us” to the island of goats in the dark (9.142); the nymphs rouse the goats and “the god gave us game” (9.154–158); a god may have bidden the Cyclops bring in his rams (9.339); and “a god breathed great daring into us” at the stake (9.381).
refs: [9.67, 9.142, 9.154, 9.158, 9.339, 9.381]
---

Odysseus tells his own story as a man who sees the gods' hands without seeing the gods. Zeus is named for the storm: “But against the ships Zeus the cloud-gatherer roused the North Wind / with a heaven-sent storm” (9.67–68; see [[zeus]] and [[boreas]]). The rest is “some god” or “the god”. The ships come into the harbor of the island of goats on a night so dark that nothing could be seen, “and some god guided us” (9.142): there was not light enough to see, and so they needed the gods' guidance (W. W. Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 9.143; see [[the-island-of-goats]]). In the morning “the nymphs, the daughters of aegis-bearing Zeus”, rouse the mountain goats for the hungry men, and “at once the god gave us game to satisfy the heart” (9.154–158; see [[nymphs]] and [[goats]]). The Cyclops brings all his flocks into the cave on the second evening, “either because he suspected something, or because a god so bade him” (9.339), and so the rams are inside for the escape (see [[some-things-you-will-think-of-yourself]]). At the stake the daring of the men is a god's gift: “a god breathed great daring into us” (9.381; see [[tharsos]] and [[daimon]]). The choice and the plans remain Odysseus' own (see [[metis]] and [[boule]]).
