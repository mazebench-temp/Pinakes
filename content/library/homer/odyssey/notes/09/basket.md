---
summary: The Cyclops curdles half of his evening milk and lays it in woven baskets (9.246–247), the racks on which his cheeses dry (9.219).
---

“And at once he curdled half of the white milk, / gathered it, and laid it in woven baskets” (9.246–247; see [[cheese]] and [[milk]]). πλεκτοῖς ἐν ταλάροισιν is the phrase of the baskets in which the young people of the vintage carry the grapes on the Shield of Achilles (*Iliad* 18.568; see [[woven]] and the entry). W. W. Merry takes the woven baskets for the same things as the ταρσοί, the racks “laden with cheeses” that Odysseus' men find in the empty cave (*Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 9.219; 9.219). In the lemma of the scholia the verse has πονησάμενος μετέθηκεν, with ἀμησάμενος given as a variant (Merry's critical note; see the translation's note on 9.246–247).
