---
summary: Neritum is the first thing Odysseus names on his island: “and on it is a mountain, / Neritum with its quivering leaves, conspicuous” (9.21–22).
---

**The mountain of Ithaca.** “I live in clear-seen Ithaca; and on it is a mountain, / Neritum with its quivering leaves, conspicuous” (9.21–22; see [[ithaca]]). Νήριτον εἰνοσίφυλλον is the verse-end of the Catalogue, where the Cephallenians “held Ithaca and Neritum with its quivering leaves” (*Iliad* 2.632; see [[with-quivering-leaves]] and [[cephallenians]]); Strabo takes the Odyssey's verse to show that the mountain stands in the island (*Geography* 10.2.11; see [[strabo]]). ἀριπρεπές, “conspicuous”, has the sense it has in the Iliad of things that stand out to the eye, a shaggy aegis and a horse in the lead of a race (*Iliad* 15.309, 23.453; see [[preeminent]]): the mountain is seen from afar.

**Neion for Neritum.** According to Eustathius and the scholia, Crates and Philoxenus, the commentator on the Odyssey, preferred the reading Νήιον (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, critical note on 9.22; see the translation's note on 9.22, 9.24). Neion is the island's other named mountain, under which lies the harbor of Rheithron (1.186; see [[neion]] and [[rheithron]]); whether it is another name for Neritum, Strabo says, is not clear (*Geography* 10.2.11). The mountain is named once more, when Athena scatters the mist and shows Odysseus his island: “this is Mount Neritum, clothed in forest” (13.351).
