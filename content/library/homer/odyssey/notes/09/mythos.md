---
summary: “Now first I will tell you my name” (9.16): Odysseus begins his tale; and after his name is known, the Cyclops “groaned and answered me” (9.506).
---

The verb of authoritative telling opens the self-naming: “Now first I will tell you my name, so that you too / may know it” (ὄνομα πρῶτον μυθήσομαι, 9.16–17), complying with Alcinous' request, “Tell me the name” (8.550; W. W. Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 9.1; see [[no-one-is-nameless]] and [[the-tale-to-alcinous]]). The noun comes once, when the Cyclops, hearing the true name, “groaned and answered me” (οἰμώξας ἠμείβετο μύθῳ, 9.506), with the prophecy of Telemus (9.507–516; see [[telemus]] and [[thesphaton]]).
