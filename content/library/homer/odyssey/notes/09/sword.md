---
summary: On the first night in the cave Odysseus means to draw his sword and stab the sleeping Cyclops where the midriff holds the liver, but a second thought holds him back: no one could then move the door-stone (9.299–305).
---

“Then I planned in my proud heart / to go up close, drawing the sharp sword from beside my thigh, / and stab him in the chest, where the midriff holds the liver, / feeling for the place with my hand; but a second thought held me back” (9.299–302; see [[odysseus]] and [[polyphemus-the-cyclops]]). The verse of the drawn sword is the Iliad's, as when Achilles draws his on Agamemnon (*Iliad* 1.190; see the entry), and the verse returns for Odysseus' sword in the books that follow (10.126, 10.294, 10.535; 11.48, Greek only). ὅθι φρένες ἧπαρ ἔχουσι is “where the midriff enfolds the liver” (W. W. Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 9.301; see the translation's row φρένες, of the body). χείρʼ is χειρί, and ἐπιμασσάμενος is better taken as “feeling for” the mortal spot, passing his hand in the dark over the monster's body, than as “fingering” his sword (Merry on 9.302; see the translation's note on 9.301–302). The sword is held back by the stone: “For there we too would have perished by sheer destruction; / for we could not have pushed away from the high doorway / with our hands the heavy stone that he had set against it” (9.303–305; see [[door-stone]]). The weapon that serves in the end is a stake of olive wood (see [[stake]]).
