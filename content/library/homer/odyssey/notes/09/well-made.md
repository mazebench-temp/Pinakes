---
summary: Maron's gifts begin with “seven talents of well-made gold” (9.202), and the Cyclops asks Odysseus “where you moored your well-made ship when you came” (9.279).
---

ἐυεργέα νῆα is the Odyssey's verse-end for a ship (see the Book 8 note); the half-verse of the gold returns when Odysseus, still unknown to his father, tells of the gifts he once gave a guest (24.274). Merry takes the talent as a definite weight, not coined money (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 9.202; [[maron]], [[gold-and-silver]]).
