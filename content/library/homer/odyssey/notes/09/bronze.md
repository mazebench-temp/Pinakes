# χαλκήρεσιν, "bronze-fitted" (9.55 = Iliad 18.534; later 11.40), first met in the Odyssey here (it applies in the
# Odyssey only; the Iliad's is in its notes, and χαλκήρεας, 1.262, is in the Book 1 note).
grc: [χαλκήρεσιν]
en: [bronze-fitted]
---

At Ismarus the armies “struck at one another with bronze-fitted spears” (9.55; see [[bronze-fitted]], [[spear]] and [[the-raid-on-the-cicones]]), the verse of the battle on the Shield of Achilles (*Iliad* 18.534). The smith of the simile of the blinding is a χαλκεύς, “worker in bronze”, though what he tempers is an axe or an adze of iron: “for this in turn is the strength of iron” (9.391–393; see [[the-smith-tempering-an-axe]] and [[iron]]). Aristotle gives the name as an example of the custom of the language: “those who work iron are called bronze-smiths” (*Poetics* 1461a29; see [[aristotle-wine-of-the-gods]]).
