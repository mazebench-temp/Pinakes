---
summary: At the sack of Ismarus Odysseus' men take the wives of the Cicones and many possessions and divide them, so that no one may go away cheated of his fair share (9.40–42).
---

“There I sacked the city and destroyed the men themselves; / and from the city we took their wives and many possessions / and divided them, so that for my part no one might go away cheated of his fair share” (9.40–42; see [[the-raid-on-the-cicones]], [[ismarus]] and [[division-of-spoils]]). μή τίς μοι is “that no one, as far as I could help it”, μοι as the later ἐμοῦ γʼ ἕνεκα (W. W. Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 9.42; see the translation's note on 9.42); the verse returns when the Cyclops' flocks are divided on the island (9.549), after the verse in Nestor's tale in which Neleus gives the spoil taken from the Epeians to the people to divide (*Iliad* 11.703–705; see [[nestor]]).
