summary: The Cyclops' eye hisses round the burning stake as an axe or an adze hisses when a smith dips it in cold water, “for this in turn is the strength of iron” (9.391–394).
# σιδήρου (9.393; later 19.587, 21.97, 21.114, 21.127, 21.328, 24.177, the iron of the axes of the contest), first met
# here in the Odyssey (it applies in the Odyssey only; the Iliad's, 23.834, is in its Book 23 note).
grc: [σιδήρου]
---

The smith of the simile works in iron: “And as when a smith dips a great axe or an adze / in cold water, and it hisses loudly, / to temper it; for this in turn is the strength of iron; / so his eye hissed around the stake of olive wood” (9.391–394; see [[the-smith-tempering-an-axe]], [[axe]], [[adze]] and [[stake]]). φαρμάσσων is “to temper it”, and τὸ γάρ is the tempering: the hot bar answers to the iron, W. W. Merry explains, and the water to the eye, and the emphatic antithesis seems to imply that while iron is greatly strengthened by tempering, the Cyclops' eye was destroyed by the corresponding process (*Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 9.393; see the translation's note on 9.392–393). The dipping, βάπτειν, gave later Greek its word for temper, βαφή: Aristotle says that states that are saved by war and ruined by their empire “lose their temper, like iron, when they are at peace” (*Politics* 7.14, 1334a6–9; Merry on 9.393). As often in the similes, the poet's own world of iron shows through the bronze of the heroes (see the entry and [[bronze]]).
