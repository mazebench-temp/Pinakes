---
summary: In the Cyclops' cave the companions kindle a fire to make an offering (9.231), and the Cyclops kindles his own at evening and at dawn, with wood brought home for light (9.233–234, 9.251, 9.308). Odysseus hardens the point of the stake in the blazing fire and heats it under the ashes until it glows; the blast of the burning eye singes the Cyclops' lids and brows, and the roots of the eye crackle in the fire (9.328, 9.375–390).
---

**The fires of the cave.** While they wait for the owner, the companions kindle a fire, make an offering of the cheeses, and eat (9.231–232; see [[cheese]] and [[sacrifice]]): κήαντες is apparently Aristarchus' reading, κείαντες that of most manuscripts, according to Eustathius (W. W. Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, critical note on 9.231; see the translation's note on 9.231). The Cyclops comes home with “a heavy load / of dry wood, to have light for his supper” (9.233–234): the fuel, Merry thinks, served only for light, like the dry wood set round the three braziers in Odysseus' hall (18.307–308, Greek only), and there is no allusion to cooking, since the Cyclops seems to have eaten cheese and drunk milk (on 9.234; see the translation's row ποτιδόρπιος and [[milk]]). When his tasks are done, “then he kindled the fire, and saw us” (9.251): the men's fire had gone out or been left to smoulder (Merry on 9.251). At dawn he kindles it again before the milking (9.308).

**The fire and the stake.** Odysseus sharpens the stake's point and “at once took it and hardened it in the blazing fire” (9.328; see [[stake]], [[blazing-fire]] and [[like-a-fire-hardened-stake]]). When the Cyclops sleeps, “I drove the stake under the deep ashes / until it grew hot”, and “when the stake of olive wood was just about to catch fire, / green though it was, and glowed terribly, / then I brought it near from the fire” (9.375–380); it was not consumed, Merry explains, but glowed red-hot (on 9.379). In the eye, “the blast singed all his eyelids round about, and his brows, / as the eyeball burned, and its roots crackled in the fire” (9.389–390; see [[eyes]] and [[brows]]); ἀμφί goes with εὗσεν, “round about” (Merry on 9.389; see the translation's note on 9.389).
