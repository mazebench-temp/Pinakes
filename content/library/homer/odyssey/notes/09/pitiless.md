---
summary: Odysseus hopes to be the Phaeacians' guest-friend “when I have escaped the pitiless day” (9.17), and three times the Cyclops answers “with pitiless heart” (9.272, 9.287, 9.368).
---

νηλέι θυμῷ belongs to the Odyssey's verse of an answer, “So I spoke, and at once he answered me with pitiless heart” (9.272 = 9.368; with οὐδέν, “answered me nothing,” 9.287; see the Odyssey translation's formulas and key words, νηλὴς θυμός). The Iliad has the accusative νηλέα θυμόν (*Iliad* 19.229) and Phoenix's νηλεὲς ἦτορ (*Iliad* 9.497; [[answering-him]], [[polyphemus-the-cyclops]]).
