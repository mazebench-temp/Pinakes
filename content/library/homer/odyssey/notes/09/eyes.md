**One eye.** The poem never says that the Cyclops has a single eye, but it speaks of his eye only in the singular, from the plan to “grind it into his eye” (9.333) to his curse (9.525; see [[polyphemus-the-cyclops]]). Hesiod's Cyclopes, the smiths of Zeus' thunderbolt, have “a single eye set in the middle of the forehead”, and were named Cyclopes for that round eye (*Theogony* 142–145; see [[cyclopes]] and the translation's note on 9.106). The first book has already named the wound: Poseidon is angry “because of the Cyclops, whom he blinded of his eye” (1.68–69).

**The blinding.** The men thrust the stake “into his eye”, Odysseus twirls it, and “the blast singed all his eyelids round about, and his brows, / as the eyeball burned, and its roots crackled in the fire”; “so his eye hissed around the stake of olive wood”, and the Cyclops wrenches the stake “out of his eye, drenched with much blood” (9.383–397; see [[stake]], [[fire]], [[brows]] and [[iron]]). The intense heat of the stake's end, W. W. Merry notes, made it hiss in the blood and juices of the eyeball (*Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 9.388).

**The blind man's words.** To his ram: “Surely you miss your master's / eye, which a bad man blinded” (9.452–453; see [[ram]]). To Odysseus, who has told him his name and bidden him say who blinded him if anyone asks about “the unsightly blinding of your eye” (9.502–504; see the translation's row ἀεικέλιος), he answers with the oracle of Telemus, “that I would lose my sight at the hands of Odysseus”, and complains that a small and feeble man “has blinded my eye, when he had overpowered me with wine” (9.507–516; see [[telemus]]). He hopes that Poseidon will heal him, and Odysseus answers that he wishes he could send him to the house of Hades, “as surely as not even the earth-shaker will heal your eye” (9.520–525; see [[poseidon]]).
