---
summary: Thucydides takes the old poets' question to strangers, whether they are pirates, which the Cyclops asks in this book, for evidence that piracy brought no shame in early Greece; Merry cites him for the ancient belief that the Cyclopes lived in Sicily.
---

- **Like pirates.** The Cyclops asks the strangers in his cave whether they wander over the sea like pirates, in the verses of Nestor's question at Pylos (9.252–255 = 3.71–74), the question that Thucydides cites from the old poets as evidence that in early Greece piracy brought no shame: [[thucydides-and-the-pirates]], [[strangers-who-are-you]] and [[piracy]].
- **The Cyclopes in Sicily.** The ancients generally placed the home of the Cyclopes in Sicily, in the neighborhood of Etna, Merry notes, citing Thucydides; for himself he holds that in these books we are in a wonderland that we shall look for in vain on the map (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 9.106, citing Thucydides 6.2): [[cyclopes]] and [[sicily]].
