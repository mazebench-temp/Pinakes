In the morning the Cyclops drives his flocks out of the cave, “easily taking away the great door-stone; and then / he set it back again, as one sets the lid on a quiver” (9.313–314; see [[the-lid-on-a-quiver]], [[door-stone]] and [[polyphemus-the-cyclops]]). The lid is the πῶμα of the quiver, which Pandarus takes off before he shoots (*Iliad* 4.116; see the entry): the stone that twenty-two wagons could not have moved is to the Cyclops what the lid of a quiver is to an archer, and the men are shut in behind it.
