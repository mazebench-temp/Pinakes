The boarding couplet, “And they went on board at once and sat down at the rowing benches, / and sitting in order they struck the gray sea with their oars,” is spoken four times (9.103–104, 9.179–180, 9.471–472, 9.563–564), and the order before it, “to go on board themselves and loose the stern-cables,” twice (9.178; again 9.562; see the Odyssey translation's formulas). The landing verse, “and we ourselves too stepped out where the sea breaks upon the shore” (9.150 = 9.547), is the Iliad's verse of the arrival at Chryse in the first person (*Iliad* 1.437). The harbor of the island needs none of the moorings of the pattern (9.136–138; see the general article): πεῖσμα there is the mooring tackle in general, the cable to the anchor-stones and the stern-cables (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 9.136; [[gray-sea]], [[stern-cables]], [[shore]], [[from-there-we-sailed-on]]).
