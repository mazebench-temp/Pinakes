---
summary: Odysseus sends scouts to learn “what men they might be who eat bread upon the earth” (9.89), and the Cyclops “was not like / a man who eats bread” (9.190–191).
---

Merry takes σῖτον ἔδοντες as marking human beings, as in the Iliad it divides men from gods (*Iliad* 5.341; Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 9.89); the Cyclops is set apart from them by the single word σιτοφάγος (see the Odyssey translation's key words, σιτοφάγος; [[huge]], [[wooded]], [[spies-and-scouts]]).
