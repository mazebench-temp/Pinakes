# The grass, "ἄνθεα ποίης" (9.449), as a phrase (it applies in the Odyssey only), since ποίης is also "of what kind"
# (1.406).
grc: ["ἄνθεα ποίης"]
---

“Before you never used to come behind the sheep, / but you were far the first to graze on the tender flowers of the grass, / with long strides, and the first to reach the streams of the rivers” (9.448–450; see [[ram]], [[tender]] and [[polyphemus-the-cyclops]]). ποίη is Homer's common word for grass and herbage; the ἄγρωστις of the entry is named only once (see the entry). μακρὰ βιβάς, “with long strides”, is the proud bearing of the leader of a flock (W. W. Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 9.450; see the translation's note on 9.448, 9.450).
