---
summary: After the storm the men set up the masts again and hoist the sails (9.77). The Cyclops' club is as long and thick as the mast of a black ship of twenty oars (9.322).
---

After two days ashore, “we set up the masts and hoisted the white sails” (9.77; see [[sails]] and [[ships]]): the masts had been lowered with the sails when the storm drove the ships to land (9.70–73). In the cave the Cyclops' club of green olive wood is likened “to the mast of a black ship of twenty oars, / a broad freight ship, which crosses the great gulf; / so great was it in length, so great in thickness to look upon” (9.322–324; see [[the-mast-of-twenty-oars]], [[mace]] and [[stake]]). ὅσσον θʼ ἱστόν is a brachylogy for “as great as a mast”, and Milton, W. W. Merry notes, imitated the comparison for the spear of Satan (*Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 9.322; see the translation's note on 9.319–322). ἱστόν here is the mast, not the [[loom]], whose entry excepts the line.
