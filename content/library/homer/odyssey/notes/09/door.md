---
summary: The Cyclops' cave has a doorway but no door: he closes it each evening with the door-stone (9.240–243), which no man could push away from the high doorway (9.304–305). Blinded, he takes the stone away and sits in the doorway, groping for the men among the sheep (9.416–418).
---

The doorway of the cave is shut not with doors but with a rock: “so lofty a rock he set against the doorway” (9.243; see [[door-stone]] and [[the-cave-of-polyphemus]]). That is why Odysseus does not kill the sleeping giant: “for we could not have pushed away from the high doorway / with our hands the heavy stone that he had set against it” (9.304–305). θυράων ὑψηλάων is the phrase of the high doors of Nestor's house (3.407), said here of the mouth of a cave; the Book 3 note of [[high-roofed]], which claims the phrase, excepts the line. On the morning of the escape the blind Cyclops “took the stone away from the doorway, / and sat down himself in the doorway, stretching out both his hands, / to see if he could catch anyone going out of the door among the sheep” (9.416–418; see [[polyphemus-the-cyclops]]); the ram goes out of the door last of the flock (9.444, 9.461; see [[ram]]).
