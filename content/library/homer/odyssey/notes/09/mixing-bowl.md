---
summary: The wine-pourer of Odysseus' ideal feast draws the wine from the mixing bowl (9.9). Among Maron's gifts is “a mixing bowl all of silver” (9.203), and when his wine was mixed, one cup to twenty of water, a sweet fragrance rose from the bowl (9.209–210).
---

**The feast.** “Drawing wine from the mixing bowl / the wine-pourer carries it round and pours it into the cups” (9.9–10; see [[cupbearing]] and [[cup]]).

**Maron's bowl.** Maron gave Odysseus seven talents of gold, “and he gave me a mixing bowl all of silver” (9.202–203; see [[maron]], [[gold-and-silver]] and [[talent]]), a gift like the silver mixing bowl of Sidonian work that is the prize for the footrace at Patroclus' games (*Iliad* 23.740–745; see the entry). In his false tale to Laertes Odysseus claims to have given the same gifts to a guest, seven talents of gold and “a mixing bowl all of silver” (24.274–275, a plain rendering). When Maron's wine was mixed, “he would fill one cup and pour it into twenty measures of water, / and a sweet fragrance rose from the mixing bowl, / a wondrous one” (9.209–211; see [[wine]]). In the earliest times, W. W. Merry notes, the wine was poured into the water, not the water into the wine (*Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 9.209; see the translation's note on 9.209).
