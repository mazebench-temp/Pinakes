summary: Odysseus names himself as the man “who for tricks of every kind / am in the thoughts of men” (9.19–20). He answers the Cyclops “with crafty words” (9.282), the Cyclopes ask whether someone is killing Polyphemus “by trickery or by force”, and he answers that Nobody is killing him “by trickery, and not by force” (9.406–408); to escape, Odysseus “wove every kind of trick and plan” (9.422).
# δόλοισιν (9.19) and δολίοις (9.282), each only here in Homer.
grc: [δόλοισιν, δολίοις]
en: [crafty words]
---

**The man of tricks.** The name comes with its fame: “I am Odysseus, son of Laertes, who for tricks of every kind / am in the thoughts of men, and my fame reaches the sky” (9.19–20; see [[odysseus]] and [[kleos]]). πᾶσι goes with δόλοισιν in the sense of παντοίοις, “of every kind”, rather than with ἀνθρώποισι, “to all men”, a question Merry finds settled by Nestor's παντοίοισι δόλοισι of Odysseus (3.121–122; W. W. Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 9.19; see the translation's note on 9.19). The guile that Agamemnon and Socus reproached in the Iliad (*Iliad* 4.339, 11.430; see the entry) is a title of honor in his own mouth.

**Crafty words.** The Cyclops asks where the ship is moored, testing him, and “I answered him back with crafty words” (δολίοις ἐπέεσσι, 9.282): the ship, he says, Poseidon shattered on the rocks (9.283–286; see [[truth-and-deception]] and [[peira]]).

**By trickery, not by force.** The other Cyclopes ask the question that sets guile against strength, “Surely no one is killing you yourself by trickery or by force?” (δόλῳ ἠὲ βίηφιν, 9.406), and Polyphemus answers truly and is misheard, “Friends, Nobody is killing me by trickery, and not by force” (δόλῳ οὐδὲ βίηφιν, 9.408): the ambiguity of the name carries over into οὐδέ, which they take for “neither … nor” (Merry on 9.408; see [[nobody]] and [[cunning-not-strength]]). For the escape Odysseus “wove every kind of trick and plan” (πάντας δὲ δόλους καὶ μῆτιν ὕφαινον, 9.422), and the trick is the rams (9.424–435; see [[metis]]).
