---
summary: The nymphs who rouse the goats are “the daughters of aegis-bearing Zeus” (9.154), and the Cyclops answers Odysseus' appeal to the god of guests: “the Cyclopes care nothing for aegis-bearing Zeus / nor for the blessed gods, since we are far the stronger” (9.275–276).
---

The genitive αἰγιόχου (9.275) stands beside the usual αἰγιόχοιο. Merry finds the boast inconsistent with what the other Cyclopes say of the power of Zeus (9.410–411) and with Polyphemus' own claim to be Poseidon's son, but true to the nature of an impious braggart (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 9.275; see the translation's note on 9.275; [[child-of-aegis-bearing-zeus]], [[blessed-gods]], [[zeus]], [[cyclopes]]).
