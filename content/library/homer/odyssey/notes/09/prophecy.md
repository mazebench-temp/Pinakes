---
summary: When Odysseus shouts his name, the Cyclops recognizes a prophecy fulfilled: the seer Telemus had told him “that all these things would come to pass hereafter, / that I would lose my sight at the hands of Odysseus” (9.507–512). His curse, heard by Poseidon, will be repeated as prophecy by Teiresias (9.528–536).
# θέσφαθʼ (9.507) is claimed by thesphaton; here the lines of the prophecy and its fulfillment.
refs: [9.507-512]
---

**A prophecy fulfilled.** The Cyclops' recognition is the moment of fulfillment that the poem's prophecies wait for: “Ah, truly the oracles spoken long ago have come home to me” (9.507; see [[thesphaton]]). The seer, Telemus son of Eurymus, “told me that all these things would come to pass hereafter” (τελευτήσεσθαι, 9.511), the verb of accomplishment (see the entry), “that I would lose my sight at the hands of Odysseus” (9.512; see [[telemus]] and [[divination]]). χειρῶν ἐξ is “at the hands of”, and this use of ἁμαρτάνω, “lose” one's sight, is not found elsewhere in Homer (W. W. Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 9.512). The Cyclops had misread it, looking for a tall and handsome man (9.513–516; see [[nobody]]). The same verse of recognition will be Alcinous' when Poseidon fulfills his father's prophecy against the Phaeacians (13.172; see the Book 8 note).

**A curse that becomes prophecy.** What the Cyclops asks of Poseidon (9.528–535; see [[curses]]) the poem turns into prophecy: Teiresias in the underworld tells Odysseus that he will come home “late and in evil plight, having lost all his companions, / on someone else's ship”, in the words of the curse (11.114–115; see the translation's table of formulas).
