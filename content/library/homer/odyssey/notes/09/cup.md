summary: In his praise of the feast Odysseus names the wine-pourer who pours the wine into the cups (9.10); Maron's wine was drunk one cup to twenty measures of water (9.209); and in the cave Odysseus offers the Cyclops his wine in an ivy-wood bowl (9.346).
# κισσύβιον, the ivy-wood bowl (9.346), first met here (it applies in the Odyssey only); κισσυβίῳ, the bowl in which
# Eumaeus mixes wine (14.78, 16.52), is to be added with its books.
grc: [κισσύβιον]
en: [ivy-wood bowl]
---

**The cups of the feast.** The finest thing, Odysseus tells Alcinous, is a feast with a singer, when “drawing wine from the mixing bowl / the wine-pourer carries it round and pours it into the cups” (9.9–10; see [[cupbearing]], [[feast]] and [[mixing-bowl]]).

**Maron's cup.** Maron's wine was so strong that “he would fill one cup and pour it into twenty measures of water” (9.209; see [[wine]] and [[maron]]): ἓν δέπας ἐμπλήσας is “one full cup” (W. W. Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 9.209).

**The ivy-wood bowl.** “Then I stood close by the Cyclops and spoke to him, / holding in my hands an ivy-wood bowl of dark wine” (9.345–346; see [[polyphemus-the-cyclops]]). κισσύβιον is named from κισσός, “ivy”: Merry supposes that the bowl was first made of ivy wood and that the word came to be used of any wooden cup or bowl, though the ivy carved round the lip of the κισσύβιον in Theocritus' first Idyll suggests to him rather a cup “decorated with ivy” (on 9.346; see the translation's row κισσύβιον and its note on 9.346). In Euripides' *Cyclops* the giant sets out a σκύφος κισσοῦ, an ivy-wood cup three cubits wide and four deep, beside his bowl of milk, and Odysseus fills a cup with Maron's wine and brings it to him (*Cyclops* 390–391, 411–412; see [[euripides]]). In the later books Eumaeus mixes wine in a κισσύβιον for his guest (14.78, 16.52, Greek only; see [[eumaeus]]).
