# ἔπειθον (9.33; again 19.151), πιθόμην (9.228), πεῖθον (9.500).
grc: [ἔπειθον, πιθόμην, πεῖθον]
en: [persuaded, did not listen]
---

**Not persuaded.** The book's persuasions mostly fail. The goddesses who held Odysseus back “never persuaded the heart in my breast” (9.33), the phrase of Meleager's dearest companions, who could not move him in Phoenix's story (*Iliad* 9.585–587; see [[nothing-sweeter-than-ones-own-land]]). His men at Ismarus, “the great fools, would not obey” (οὐκ ἐπίθοντο, 9.44; see [[nepios]]). He in his turn would not listen to them in the cave: “but I did not listen—it would have been far better—” (9.228), the verse of Pandarus' and Hector's regrets in the Iliad (*Iliad* 5.201, 22.103; see [[kerdos]]). And when they beg him not to provoke the Cyclops again, “So they spoke, but they did not persuade my proud heart” (9.500; see [[thymos]] and [[kertomia]]).

**Trusting the gods.** The perfect is “trust”: the Cyclopes neither plant nor plow, “trusting in the immortal gods” (θεοῖσι πεποιθότες, 9.107). There is no devoutness in it, as the Cyclops' boast that they care nothing for Zeus shows (9.275), but a careless confidence in the bounty of nature (W. W. Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 9.107; see [[cyclopes]] and [[theos]]).
