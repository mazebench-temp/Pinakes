# ἐθύσαμεν (9.231), and the phrases "ῥέξας μηρίʼ ἔκαιον" and "ἐμπάζετο ἱρῶν" (9.553).
grc: [ἐθύσαμεν, "ῥέξας μηρίʼ ἔκαιον", "ἐμπάζετο ἱρῶν"]
en: [made an offering, I sacrificed him, the offering]

**An offering of cheese.** Waiting for the Cyclops in his cave, the companions “kindled a fire and made an offering (ἐθύσαμεν), and we ourselves too / took some of the cheeses and ate” (9.231–232): θύειν is in Homer the offering of firstfruits to the gods, never the slaughter of a victim, and the offering here is of part of the cheeses before they took them for themselves (W. W. Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 9.231; see the translation's table of key words). Merry compares the makeshift sacrifice on Thrinacia, where water serves for wine and leaves for barley (12.356–365; see [[the-cattle-of-helios]]).

**The ram.** On the island the flocks are divided, and the companions give Odysseus the ram that carried him out as his special share (9.548–551; see [[division-of-spoils]] and [[ram]]). He sacrifices it to Zeus, and the god refuses it:

{{quote:99aedea8-f663-5e99-8757-b845d320b9c9}}

The god's portion, the thigh-pieces, is burned, and the god is named with his titles, “lord of the dark clouds, son of Cronus, who rules over all” (see [[zeus]], [[thigh-pieces]] and [[lord-of-dark-clouds]]). Zeus refused to notice the offering, Merry explains, because the prayer of Polyphemus had been heard and would be answered (on 9.553; see [[curses]] and [[reciprocity-with-the-gods]]). The sacrifice is followed by the feast of the shore, “until the sun went down” (9.556–557; see [[feast]]).
