---
summary: The goats of Book 9 are the countless wild goats of the island off the land of the Cyclopes, which the nymphs rouse for the companions' meal and which are shared out by lot, nine to each ship and ten to Odysseus (9.116–124, 9.154–160), and the Cyclops' goats, which he milks with his ewes, while the he-goats stay outside in the fold (9.183–184, 9.237–245).
---

**The island of goats.** The island that lies off the harbor of the Cyclopes is a wooded one, “and on it live countless goats, / wild ones; for no path of men keeps them away, / nor do hunters go into it” (9.118–120). κυνηγέται, “hunters”, occurs only here; Homer's usual words for huntsmen are θηρητῆρες and ἐπακτῆρες (W. W. Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 9.120). No herdsman and no plowman has taken the island, and “it is empty of men, and feeds bleating goats” (9.122–124; see [[bleating]]), because the Cyclopes have no ships to cross to it (9.125–130; see [[cyclopes]]).

**The hunt.** On the first morning, “the nymphs, the daughters of aegis-bearing Zeus, roused / the goats that lived in the mountains, so that my companions might have their meal” (9.154–155; see [[nymphs]] and [[mountain-dwelling]]). In ὀρεσκῴους, “that lived in the mountains”, the first half of the compound keeps the true form of the neuter stem, as in ἐπεσβόλος (Merry on 9.155). The men shoot in three bands with their curved bows and long javelins, and “at once the god gave us game to satisfy the heart” (9.156–158; see [[bow]]). The game is shared like spoil: “Twelve ships followed me, and to each / nine goats fell by lot; but for me alone they chose out ten” (9.159–160), and they feast on it until sunset (9.161–162). λάγχανον is the only place in Homer where λαγχάνω means “fall by lot” (Merry on 9.160; see the translation's note on 9.159–160).

**The Cyclops' goats.** From the island the men hear the voices of the Cyclopes, “and those of the sheep and the goats” (9.167). In the cave of the Cyclops “many / flocks, sheep and goats, used to sleep” (9.183–184): μῆλα are the lesser cattle, sheep and goats together (Merry on 9.184; see [[sheep]]). The pens are crowded “with lambs and kids”, penned by age (9.219–222; see [[lambs]]), and the companions beg Odysseus to drive “the kids and lambs” to the ship (9.226). At evening the Cyclops drives in the flocks he milks and leaves outside “the rams and the he-goats” (ἀρνειούς τε τράγους τε, 9.238–239; see [[ram]]), the one place where Homer names the he-goat; then he “milked the sheep and the bleating goats, / all in good order, and put her young one under each” (9.244–245 = 9.341–342). The escape is made under the rams (9.425–463).

The wine of Maron is carried up to the cave in “a goatskin of dark wine” (αἴγεον ἀσκόν, 9.196; see [[wine]]).
