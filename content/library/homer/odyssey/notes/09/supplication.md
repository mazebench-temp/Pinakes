summary: Odysseus comes to the Cyclops as a suppliant: “we in our turn have reached you and come to your knees, / to see if you will give us a guest-gift … But respect the gods, good sir; we are your suppliants, / and Zeus is the avenger of suppliants and strangers” (9.266–271). The Cyclops answers that the Cyclopes care nothing for Zeus (9.273–278). Before he came, the companions had begged Odysseus to take the cheeses and go (9.224).

**The appeal.** After the Cyclops' question, Odysseus names his people, the Achaeans of Agamemnon, “whose fame is now the greatest under heaven” (9.259–266; see [[agamemnon]] and [[kleos]]), and makes his plea. τὰ σὰ γοῦνα ἱκόμεθα is “we have come to your knees”, the knees the object of the verb of coming, as in Telemachus' words to Nestor and to Menelaus, “That is why I come now to your knees” (3.92 = 4.322), and κιχανόμενοι is “visiting you” (W. W. Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 9.266; see [[gounata]]). He joins the two whom Zeus protects:

{{quote:38118631-ab86-5442-a8fb-ffd32608f480}}

The suppliant and the stranger are named together, as Alcinous names them in his maxim (8.546; see [[as-good-as-a-brother]] and [[xenia]]), and Zeus is the ἐπιτιμήτωρ, “avenger”, of both (see the translation's table of key words). The last verse, which repeats the verse-end of the Phaeacians' libation to Zeus “who attends on revered suppliants” (7.165, 7.181), with strangers for suppliants, Bekker set at the foot of the page, and Köchly found it feeble after the verse before and borrowed from Book 7 (Merry's critical note on 9.271; see the translation's note on 9.271 and [[a-suppliant-is-respected-by-the-gods]]). The Cyclops will hear none of it: “You are a fool, stranger … for the Cyclopes care nothing for aegis-bearing Zeus” (9.273–275; see [[zeus]] and [[polyphemus-the-cyclops]]). The suppliant's god answers in the end, and Odysseus says so from the ship: “therefore Zeus and the other gods have punished you” (9.479; see [[poine]]). When he wakes on Ithaca, thinking himself betrayed, he will call on Zeus “of suppliants” against the Phaeacians (Ζεὺς … ἱκετήσιος, 13.213, a plain rendering).

**Begging.** In the cave the companions “begged me at first with words” (λίσσοντʼ, 9.224) to take some of the cheeses and go: the verb of the suppliant's entreaty, used of men pleading with their leader (see [[hetairos]] and [[epos]]). He would not listen (9.228; see [[peitho]]).
