---
summary: Odysseus sets out to learn whether the Cyclopes are “arrogant and savage and unjust, / or kind to strangers, with a mind that fears the gods” (9.175–176); the tale has already called them “overbearing and lawless” (9.106).
---

The word comes only in the question, the one Odysseus asks on every unknown shore: “whether they are arrogant (ὑβρισταί) and savage and unjust, / or kind to strangers, with a mind that fears the gods” (9.175–176 = 6.120–121; see [[to-the-land-of-what-mortals]], [[themis]] and [[xenia]]). The narrator has given the answer in advance, with a word of the same kind: “the land of the Cyclopes, overbearing and lawless” (ὑπερφιάλων ἀθεμίστων, 9.106; see [[overbearing]]). The deeds that follow, the strangers eaten in the cave and the gods scorned, are never called ὕβρις in the book; Odysseus' verdict names them “evil deeds” (κακὰ ἔργα, 9.477) and the punishment of Zeus and the other gods (9.479; see [[poine]]).
