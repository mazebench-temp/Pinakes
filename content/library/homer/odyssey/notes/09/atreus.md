---
summary: Atreus is named only in his son's patronymic, Ἀτρεΐδεω, “son of Atreus”, of Agamemnon, whose people Odysseus and his men claim to be when they stand before the Cyclops (9.263).
---

λαοὶ δʼ Ἀτρεΐδεω Ἀγαμέμνονος εὐχόμεθʼ εἶναι, “And we claim to be the people of Agamemnon, son of Atreus” (9.263; see [[agamemnon]] and [[son-of-atreus]]). Ἀτρεΐδεω, with the ending -εω, is the genitive that names Agamemnon among the dead of the fourth book, “the son of Atreus” whose companions all perished with him (4.536). The book tells nothing of Atreus himself.
