---
summary: Odysseus names his home: “I live in clear-seen Ithaca; and on it is a mountain, / Neritum with its quivering leaves, conspicuous” (9.21–22).
---

The verses that follow place the island among its neighbors, “But my island itself lies low in the sea, farthest out of all / toward the darkness, while the others lie apart toward the dawn and the sun” (9.25–26), and bear on the sense of the epithet: Merry refers the whole passage to his Appendix on Ithaca (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 9.21; see the translation's notes on 2.167 and 9.21–28).
