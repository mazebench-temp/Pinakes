---
summary: The Cyclopes, “who, trusting in the immortal gods, / neither plant anything with their hands nor plow” (9.107–108).
---

Merry finds no devoutness in θεοῖσι πεποιθότες, as 9.275 shows, but a careless confidence in the bounty of nature (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 9.107; see the translation's note on 9.107; [[cyclopes]], [[aegis-bearing]]).
