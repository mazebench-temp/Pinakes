---
summary: Odysseus' praise of the feast: there is no fulfillment more gracious “than when gladness holds the whole people” (9.6).
---

The δῆμος here is the community gathered at a feast: “For I, for my part, say there is no fulfillment more gracious / than when gladness holds the whole people, / and the banqueters throughout the house listen to a singer” (9.5–7; see [[feast]] and [[singers]]). The text accents κάτα, in tmesis with ἔχῃ, “holds”; Merry prints κατὰ δῆμον and takes ἔχῃ as intransitive, gladness “reigns” throughout the people, every one of them (W. W. Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 9.6; see the translation's note on 9.6). The people are the Phaeacians whom Alcinous rules, “preeminent among all the people” (9.2; see [[laos]]).
