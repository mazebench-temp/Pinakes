---
summary: The Cyclops' eye hisses round the burning stake as a great axe or an adze hisses when a smith dips it in cold water to temper it, “for this in turn is the strength of iron” (9.391–394).
---

“And as when a smith dips a great axe or an adze / in cold water, and it hisses loudly, / to temper it; for this in turn is the strength of iron; / so his eye hissed around the stake of olive wood” (9.391–394; see [[the-smith-tempering-an-axe]], [[adze]], [[iron]], [[stake]] and [[eyes]]). ἰάχοντα, “hissing”, agrees with πέλεκυν only, the adze being thrown in, as it were, in parenthesis (W. W. Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 9.392; see the translation's note on 9.392–393). The axe of the simile is of iron, the metal of tools in the poet's own world, while the heroes fight with bronze (see [[bronze]]); the axe that Calypso gave Odysseus for his raft was bronze (5.234–235).
