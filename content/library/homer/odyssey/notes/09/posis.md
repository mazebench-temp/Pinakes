---
summary: Calypso and Circe each kept Odysseus in her halls, “longing for me to be her husband” (9.30, 9.32).
---

The verse-end is said twice, of the two goddesses: Calypso “held me back there / in her hollow caves, longing for me to be her husband”, and Circe “kept me in her halls, / the wily one of Aeaea, longing for me to be her husband” (λιλαιομένη πόσιν εἶναι, 9.29–32; see [[calypso]] and [[circe]]). The first is the proem's verse of Calypso (1.15), and verse 30, wanting in most manuscripts, is probably borrowed from it (W. W. Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, his critical note on 9.30). Neither persuaded him: nothing is sweeter than one's native land (9.33–36; see [[nothing-sweeter-than-ones-own-land]]).
