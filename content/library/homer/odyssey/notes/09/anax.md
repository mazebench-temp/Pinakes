en: [master, master's]

The title of the gods is given to Poseidon by his son's neighbors, “but you must pray to your father, lord Poseidon” (9.412), and by the narrator as the son prays (9.526; see [[poseidon]] and [[prayer]]); Zeus, to whom Odysseus burns the ram, is “son of Cronus, who rules over all” (9.552, again 13.25; see [[zeus]] and [[son-of-cronus]]). The same word names the Cyclops as master of his flock. At dawn, “their master, worn down / by evil pains, felt along the backs of all the sheep” (ἄναξ, 9.440–441), and he asks the ram that lags behind, “Surely you miss your master's / eye” (ἄνακτος, 9.452–453; see [[ram]] and [[sheep]]). The ruler of no people is lord of his household and his herds (see [[themis]] and [[cyclopes]]).
