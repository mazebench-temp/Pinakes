# τισαίμην (9.317) and τίσατο (9.479), each only here in the Odyssey.
grc: [τισαίμην, τίσατο]
en: [take vengeance, have punished you]
---

The middle of τίνω is “make someone pay, take vengeance”, and the book uses it at both ends of the blinding. In the cave Odysseus was left “brooding evil deep within, / to see whether I might take vengeance, and Athena grant me triumph” (εἴ πως τισαίμην, 9.316–317; see [[athena]] and [[euchomai]]), vengeance for the companions eaten (9.288–311). From the ship he names the avengers: “since you did not shrink from eating strangers / in your own house; therefore Zeus and the other gods have punished you” (τῷ σε Ζεὺς τίσατο, 9.478–479; see [[xenia]] and [[zeus]]). Zeus is the avenger of suppliants and strangers, as Odysseus had told the Cyclops (9.270; see [[supplication]]). When the tale is retold to Penelope, the vengeance is the Cyclops' payment for the companions: “how he exacted vengeance for his mighty companions” (ἀπετίσατο ποινήν, 23.312, a plain rendering).
