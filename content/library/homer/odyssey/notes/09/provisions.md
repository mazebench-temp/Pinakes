---
summary: Odysseus carries provisions in a leather sack, with a great skin of Maron's wine, to the Cyclops' cave, for his proud spirit foreboded that they would meet a savage (9.212–215).
---

“With this I filled a great skin and carried it, and provisions too / in a leather sack; for at once my proud spirit foreboded / that a man would come upon us, clothed in great courage, / a savage, who knew well neither judgments nor ordinances” (9.212–215; see [[wine]], [[polyphemus-the-cyclops]] and [[clothed-in-courage]]). The words of the sack are those of the provisions that Calypso put on board the raft, “and provisions too / in a leather sack” (5.266–267; see the Book 5 note), where the wine and the water were in skins. ὀίσατο is “foreboded”, a presentiment (see the translation's row ὀίομαι), and ἄνδρα is the subject of ἐπελεύσεσθαι, the man who would come upon them, not its object (W. W. Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 9.214; see the translation's note on 9.214). The ships' own stores are named only in passing: at the land of the Lotus-eaters the men draw water and take their meal by the ships (9.85–87), and on the island off the Cyclopes' land the wine from Ismarus “was not yet spent from the ships” (9.163; see [[two-handled-jar]]).
