# κεκοτηότι (9.501; again 19.71, 22.477).
grc: [κεκοτηότι]
en: [resentful]
---

The grudge is Odysseus'. The companions try to hold him back with soothing words, “but they did not persuade my proud heart, / but I answered him back with resentful heart” (κεκοτηότι θυμῷ, 9.500–501; see [[peitho]] and [[thymos]]): ἄψορρον is only “I answered him back”, as at 9.282, and this is his second address to the Cyclops (W. W. Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 9.282, 9.501). What the resentful heart says is the name, “say that Odysseus, sacker of cities, blinded you” (9.502–505; see [[say-that-odysseus-blinded-you]]), the anger that Aristotle found satisfied only when the wrongdoer knows by whom he is punished (*Rhetoric* 1380b22–24; see [[kleos]]).
