# ἐξαπάτησεν (9.414), only here in Homer.
grc: [ἐξαπάτησεν]
en: [deceived]
---

The book's deceit is named once, at its triumph: “So they spoke as they went away, and my dear heart laughed within me, / how my name and my flawless plan had deceived him” (ἐξαπάτησεν, 9.413–414). The subject is double, the name and the plan, ὄνομα and μῆτις, and the pun that joins them is the deceit: the Cyclopes heard the false name as the pronoun “no one” and answered with μή τις, the sound of μῆτις (9.405–410; W. W. Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 9.408; see [[nobody]] and [[metis]]). The one deceived is the Cyclops, whose truthful cry “Nobody is killing me” sent his helpers away (see [[truth-and-deception]]).
