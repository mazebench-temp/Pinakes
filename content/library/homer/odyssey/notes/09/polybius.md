---
summary: Polybius described the lotus shrub of Libya, and Merry quotes the description for the Lotus-eaters' food; as Strabo reports, he also placed the Lotus-eaters at Meninx and reckoned the nine days of deadly winds as a short run.
---

- **The lotus shrub.** The tree is not large, rough and thorny, with green leaves like the buckthorn's; its fruit, at first like white myrtle berries, turns red as it grows and reaches the size of round olives, with a very small stone, and tastes like a fig or a date, but sweeter-smelling (Polybius 12.2, as Merry quotes it on 9.94, a plain rendering; Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886). From the same description Bothe proposed ἀκάνθινον, “thorny”, for ἄνθινον, “flowery”, in “who eat a flowery food” (9.84; Merry on 9.84; see the translation's note on 9.84): [[lotus]] and [[lotus-eaters]].
- **The nine days.** Polybius, as Strabo reports, found what is said of the Lotus-eaters in agreement with the island of Meninx, and took the nine days of “deadly winds” (9.82) for a short run rather than a voyage into the Ocean (Strabo, *Geography* 1.2.17–18): [[the-lotus-eaters]] and [[strabo]].
