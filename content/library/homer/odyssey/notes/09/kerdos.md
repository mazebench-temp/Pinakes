---
summary: In the Cyclops' cave the companions beg Odysseus to take some of the cheeses and the lambs and go; “but I did not listen—it would have been far better—” (9.228).
---

The comparative of κέρδος is the narrator's judgment on his own choice. The companions wanted to take the cheeses, drive the kids and lambs out of the pens and sail away (9.224–227; see [[supplication]]); Odysseus stayed “so that I might see him himself, and whether he would give me guest-gifts” (9.229; see [[guest-gifts]] and [[xenia]]). The parenthesis ἦ τʼ ἂν πολὺ κέρδιον ἦεν, “it would have been far better”, is spoken by the man who knows what followed: six companions eaten (9.288–293, 9.311, 9.344; see [[hetairos]]). The whole verse is a regret of the Iliad: Pandarus says it of the horses he did not bring to Troy, and Hector, waiting for Achilles, of the advice of Polydamas that he would not take (ἀλλʼ ἐγὼ οὐ πιθόμην, *Iliad* 5.201, 22.103). The next verse says the same in other words: “But when he appeared, he was not to be a lovely sight for my companions” (9.230), οὐδʼ ἄρʼ ἔμελλε, “yet, as it fell out, he was not going to be a joy to my comrades” (W. W. Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 9.230). See [[foresight]] and [[peitho]].
