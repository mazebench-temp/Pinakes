---
summary: The harbor of the island off the Cyclopes' land is so sheltered that there is no need to cast out mooring-stones or to make fast stern-cables (9.136–137).
---

“In it is a harbor with good anchorage, where there is no need of a cable, / neither to cast out mooring-stones nor to make fast stern-cables, / but men may run their ships ashore and wait a while” (9.136–138; see [[stern-cables]] and [[the-island-of-goats]]). The cable, W. W. Merry explains, is here the mooring tackle as a whole: the cable from the bows fastened to the εὐναί, and the stern-cables between the stern and the shore (*Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 9.136). After the negative, the affirmative is understood: all that is needful is to run the ships ashore and wait for a wind (Merry on 9.138; see the translation's note on 9.136–138 and its row ἐπικέλλω, κέλλω). The εὐναί are the stones of the Iliad's landing at Chryse, cast out with the same verb (*Iliad* 1.436; see the entry).
