summary: Four times in the book the crews go on board, sit at the benches and strike the gray sea with their oars (9.103–104, 9.179–180, 9.471–472, 9.563–564). In the storm they row to land (9.73); escaping from the Cyclops' land they lay to their oars in silence (9.488–490).
# Forms first met here (they apply in the Odyssey only): προερέσσαμεν, "rowed toward" (9.73; later 13.279; the entry's
# προέρεσσαν is Il. 1.435), ἔρεσσον, "rowed" (9.490; later 11.78, 12.194), and the phrase "ἐμβαλέειν κώπῃς", "lay to
# their oars" (9.489; later 10.129), since κώπη alone is also the hilt of a sword (8.403) and the handle of a key (21.7).

**The verse of rowing.** “And sitting in order they struck the gray sea with their oars” follows each boarding (9.104, 9.180, 9.472, 9.564; see [[rowing-benches]] and [[gray-sea]]), as in Menelaus' tale (4.580).

**In the storm.** With their sails torn, the men lower them “and rowed the ships themselves with all speed toward the land” (9.72–73; see [[sails]]): προερέσσαμεν is, according to Eustathius, Aristarchus' reading (W. W. Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, critical note on 9.73; see the translation's note on 9.73–74).

**The silent rowing.** After the first rock Odysseus pushes off from the shore and “urged on my companions and bade them / lay to their oars, so that we might escape from evil, / nodding with my head; and they bent forward and rowed” (9.488–490; see [[ships]]). ἐμβαλέειν κώπῃς is taken as the Latin *incumbere remis*, “to throw themselves on the oars”, or, as Merry suggests, “to dash into the sea with our oars”; Odysseus nods instead of speaking, for no one dared make a sound within range of the Cyclops (Merry on 9.489, 9.490; see the translation's note on 9.488–491). The κώπη is properly the handle of the oar. The Cyclops' club is the mast of a ship ἐεικόσορος, “of twenty oars”, a word that keeps the root of ἐρέσσω, “row” (9.322; Merry on 9.322; see [[mace]]).
