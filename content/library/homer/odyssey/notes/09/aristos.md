# "ἀρίστη φαίνετο βουλή" (9.318, 9.424) and "πλέονες καὶ ἀρείους" (9.48): the bare forms would also take 1.280, 2.277,

The superlative grades men, plans and beasts alike. The Cicones who come down from the inland are “more numerous and braver too” than those of the coast (9.48; see [[cicones]]). For the venture to the cave Odysseus takes “twelve of my companions, the best” (9.195; see [[hetairos]]). His two plans are announced in the verse of a counselor's resolve, “And this plan seemed to me in my heart the best” (9.318, 9.424), the verse with which, in the Iliad, Zeus resolves to send the dream to Agamemnon, Agamemnon to go to Nestor, and Hera to go to Ida adorned for Zeus (ἥδε δέ οἱ κατὰ θυμὸν ἀρίστη φαίνετο βουλή, *Iliad* 2.5, 10.17, 14.161; see [[boule]]). ὅπως ὄχʼ ἄριστα γένοιτο, “how all might turn out best by far”, uses the neuter plural as an abstract noun (9.420; W. W. Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 9.420), and the same ὄχʼ ἄριστος describes the ram that saves him, “by far the best of all the flock” (9.432; see [[ram]]).
