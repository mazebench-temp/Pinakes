summary: Odysseus leaves the fleet at the island of goats to “make trial of these men, who they are” (9.174); in the cave the Cyclops asks where his ship is moored, “testing me”, and Odysseus, who “knew much”, answers with a lie (9.279–286).
# πειράζων (9.281; πειράζειν again 16.319, 23.114).
grc: [πειραζ*]
---

**The trial of the Cyclopes.** At the assembly on the island of goats Odysseus tells the others to wait while he goes in his own ship “and make trial of these men, who they are, / whether they are arrogant and savage and unjust, / or kind to strangers, with a mind that fears the gods” (9.174–176; see [[xenia]] and [[to-the-land-of-what-mortals]]). The trial is the reason he gives for the whole venture, and the wish to see the man himself keeps him in the cave when his men want to go (9.228–229; see [[kerdos]]).

**The tester tested.** The Cyclops makes a trial of his own. After his answer about the gods he asks, “But tell me where you moored your well-made ship when you came, / whether somewhere at the far edge of the land, or near, so that I may know” (9.279–280), and Odysseus sees through it: “So he spoke, testing me, but he did not escape my notice, for I knew much” (ὣς φάτο πειράζων, 9.281). εἰδότα πολλά is “with my knowledge of the world”, the knowledge that the epithet πολύμητις names (W. W. Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 9.281; see [[metis]]). The answer is a lie, “with crafty words”: Poseidon shattered the ship on the rocks (9.282–286; see [[dolos]] and [[truth-and-deception]]).
