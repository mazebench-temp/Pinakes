# ἀλόχους (9.41), the wives of the Cicones, taken as spoil.
grc: ["ἀλόχους καὶ κτήματα"]
en: [their wives]
---

The raid on the Cicones is told in the Iliad's terms, and its spoil is the Iliad's: “There I sacked the city and destroyed the men themselves; / and from the city we took their wives and many possessions / and divided them, so that for my part no one might go away cheated of his fair share” (9.40–42; see [[raiding]], [[division-of-spoils]] and [[the-raid-on-the-cicones]]). The women are ἄλοχοι, the wedded wives of the men just killed (see [[marriage]]), taken with the goods as the women of Thebe and Lyrnessus were in the Iliad (see the entry). The tale says nothing more of them: the Cicones of the inland come in the morning, and by evening they have driven the Achaeans back to the ships (9.47–61). One household of Ismarus was spared, the priest Maron's, with his child and his wife (9.197–201; see [[maron]] and [[priesthood]]).
