summary: The Achaeans spared Maron, the priest of Apollo, with his child and his wife, “in reverence” (9.199–200). In the cave Odysseus begins his plea, “But respect the gods, good sir” (9.269), and names Zeus “who attends on revered strangers” (9.271); from the ship he tells the Cyclops that he “did not shrink from eating strangers / in your own house” (9.478–479).
# αἰδεῖο (9.269), ἅζεο (9.478); ἁζόμενοι (9.200) is the entry's, αἰδοίοισιν (9.271) the Book 7 note's.

**Reverence for a priest.** At Ismarus the city was sacked and the men destroyed (9.40), but Maron, “the priest of Apollo, who used to stand guard over Ismarus”, was protected “with his child and his wife / in reverence” (ἁζόμενοι, 9.197–200; see [[maron]], [[priesthood]] and [[apollo]]). ἅζομαι is the awe owed to the gods and to what is theirs: the priest lived in the god's own grove (9.200–201). His gifts repaid it, and among them the wine that would blind the Cyclops (9.201–211; see [[doron]]).

**The plea.** Odysseus' appeal to the Cyclops uses the words of Priam's appeal to Achilles, “But respect the gods” (ἀλλʼ αἰδεῖο θεούς, 9.269; *Iliad* 24.503), and grounds them in the protection Zeus gives to suppliants and strangers, “who attends on revered strangers” (9.270–271). αἰδοίοισιν means only that all guests are revered as guests (W. W. Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 9.271; see [[a-suppliant-is-respected-by-the-gods]] and [[supplication]]). The Cyclops answers that he will neither fear nor avoid the gods (9.274–278; see [[deos]]).

**What he did not shrink from.** The last taunt names the respect the Cyclops lacked: “hard man, since you did not shrink from eating strangers / in your own house” (ξείνους οὐχ ἅζεο, 9.478–479; see [[xenia]] and [[hard]]). The verb of the priest's protection is the verb of the host's failure.
