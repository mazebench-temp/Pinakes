---
summary: At Ismarus the Cicones of the inland, who knew how to fight from chariots and on foot, “set their battle in array and fought beside the swift ships” from morning until the sun turned toward evening (9.49–59).
---

The book's one battle is told in the Iliad's words. The Cicones of the inland came, “who knew how to fight with men from chariots, / and on foot too, where there was need” (9.49–50): ἀφʼ ἵππων is from the war chariot, and the sense is that they knew how to fight from the chariot, and how to fight on foot in that part of the battle where one ought to (W. W. Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 9.49–50; see [[cicones]] and the translation's table of key words). “They set their battle in array and fought beside the swift ships” (στησάμενοι δʼ ἐμάχοντο μάχην, 9.54), the verse of the battle on the Shield of Achilles (*Iliad* 18.533; Merry on 9.54; see [[the-shield-of-achilles]]). The day is measured as in the Iliad's battles: they held their ground as long as the sacred day was growing, and gave way when the sun moved on “toward the time for unyoking oxen” (9.56–59), βουλυτόνδε, in Homer only here and in the verse that turns the battle over Cebriones (*Iliad* 16.779; Merry on 9.58; see [[the-raid-on-the-cicones]]).
