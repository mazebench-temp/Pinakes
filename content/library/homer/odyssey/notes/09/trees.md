summary: The trees of Book 9 are the poplars around the spring at the head of the harbor of the island of goats (9.140–141), the laurels that overhang the Cyclops' cave and the tall pines and high-leafed oaks built into his fold (9.182–186), and the grove of Apollo at Ismarus, “full of trees” (9.200).
# The kinds that the book names, claimed here for the Odyssey: αἴγειροι, poplars (9.141; again 10.510), δάφνῃσι, laurels
# (9.183, the only laurel in Homer), πίτυσσιν, pines, and δρυσὶν, oaks (9.186, in the Odyssey only here).

**The poplars of the island.** “And at the head of the harbor flows bright water, / a spring from under a cave; and around it poplars grow” (9.140–141; see [[harbor]] and [[the-island-of-goats]]). ὑπὸ σπείους is “forth from under” the cave (W. W. Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 9.141). In the Odyssey the poplar grows by water: around Calypso's cave, in Athena's grove beside a spring on Scheria, in Persephone's groves by Oceanus and around the fountain of the town of Ithaca (5.64, 6.291–292, 10.510, 17.208; see the Book 5 and Book 6 notes).

**The laurels, the pines and the oaks.** The Cyclops' cave is “high, and overhung with laurels” (9.183; see [[the-cave-of-polyphemus]]); δάφνη, the laurel, is named nowhere else in Homer. Around its mouth a high fold “had been built with stones set deep in the earth / and with tall pines and high-leafed oaks” (9.185–186; see [[fold]] and [[high-leafed]]). Merry pictures the trees as worked into the wall, the stonework brought close up to the standing trunks (on 9.184). The oak and the pine fall together, with the white poplar, in the Iliad's simile of a warrior struck down, the pine one that builders cut on the mountains to be ship timber (*Iliad* 13.389–391 = 16.482–484; see [[the-felled-tree]]), and ὑψίκομος, “high-leafed”, is the oak's epithet there too (*Iliad* 14.398, 23.118). The Cyclops himself is likened to a wooded peak of high mountains (9.190–192; see [[like-a-wooded-peak]]).

**The grove of Apollo.** Maron, the priest of Apollo at Ismarus, lived “in the grove full of trees / of Phoebus Apollo” (9.200–201; see [[maron]], [[apollo]] and [[full-of-trees]]).
