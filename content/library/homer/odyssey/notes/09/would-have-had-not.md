---
summary: “And now I would have reached my native land unharmed; / but as I was rounding Malea, the wave and the current / and the North Wind pushed me back, and drove me off course past Cythera” (9.79–81).
---

The narrator's turn in Odysseus' own mouth: the voyage home is brought to the edge of its end, and the wind turns it into the wanderings ([[malea]], [[cythera]], [[boreas]]).

## Outside Homer

The danger of the cape was proverbial, and Merry cites the proverb that Strabo quotes, Μαλέας δὲ κάμψας ἐπιλάθου τῶν οἴκαδε, “when you have rounded Malea, forget your home” (Strabo, *Geography* 8.6.20, a plain rendering; Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 9.80).
