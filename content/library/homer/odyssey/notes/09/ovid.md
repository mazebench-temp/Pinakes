---
summary: Ovid gives the Cyclops a life before Odysseus came: in the Metamorphoses he laughs at the seer Telemus, who foretells that Ulysses will take his eye, sings his love to Galatea and crushes her lover Acis; and Achaemenides, left behind in the cave, tells a companion of Ulysses what he saw there.
---

- **Telemus.** “Telemus, son of Eurymus, whom no bird had ever deceived”, came to Etna and told the terrible Polyphemus that the one eye he bore in the middle of his forehead Ulysses would take from him; the Cyclops laughed, “Another has taken it already”, meaning Galatea (*Metamorphoses* 13.770–775, plain renderings). Merry cites the lines for Telemus, son of Eurymus (9.509; Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 9.509): [[telemus]] and [[polyphemus-the-cyclops]].
- **Galatea and Acis.** Galatea tells Scylla how the Cyclops loved her, sang his love from a headland over the sea, boasted of the one eye in the middle of his forehead and of his father, who rules the sea, and, finding her with Acis, tore off part of the mountain and crushed him; Acis became a river that keeps his name (*Metamorphoses* 13.738–897): [[galateia]], [[polyphemus-the-cyclops]] and [[cyclops-dithyrambs]].
- **Achaemenides.** On the shore that would later take the name of Aeneas' nurse, Caieta, Macareus of Neritum, a companion of Ulysses, finds Achaemenides, whom they had left among the rocks of Etna, alive, and Achaemenides tells him of the rocks the blinded Cyclops hurled at the ship and of the companions he saw dashed to the ground and eaten, the Cyclops vomiting gobbets of flesh mixed with wine (*Metamorphoses* 14.157–222; compare 9.289–293, 9.373–374, 9.481–486): [[polyphemus-the-cyclops]] and [[neritum]].
