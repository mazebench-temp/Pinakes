---
summary: Maron gives Odysseus seven talents of well-made gold (9.202).
---

“He gave me seven talents of well-made gold, / and he gave me a mixing bowl all of silver” (9.202–203; see [[maron]], [[gold-and-silver]] and [[mixing-bowl]]). τάλαντον, W. W. Merry explains, is in Homer nothing more than a definite weight, as the same word names the scales; values were reckoned by weight and in oxen, and we can form very little idea of the value of gold in Homeric times. Comparing the prizes at Patroclus' games, he guesses that an ox may have been worth two talents of gold, though the value must have varied with the scarcity of oxen or of gold (*Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 9.202; *Iliad* 23.269, 23.750–751; see the translation's note on 9.202 and the entry). In his false tale to Laertes Odysseus claims to have given a guest the same seven talents (24.274, Greek only).
