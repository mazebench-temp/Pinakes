# χολώσατο (9.480; again 22.224).
grc: [χολώσατο]
en: [grew all the more angry]
---

The Cyclops' anger is roused by words. Odysseus taunts him that his evil deeds have caught up with him and that Zeus and the other gods have punished him (9.475–479; see [[kertomia]] and [[poine]]), “and he grew all the more angry in his heart” (χολώσατο κηρόθι μᾶλλον, 9.480), the half-verse of the river's anger at Achilles in the Iliad (*Iliad* 21.136; see [[ker-heart]]). The anger becomes a rock, the peak of a great mountain, thrown so that the backwash nearly carries the ship to shore (9.481–486). Odysseus' own anger answers it “with resentful heart” (9.501; see [[kotos]]), and the Cyclops' last anger is a prayer (9.526–536; see [[curses]]).
