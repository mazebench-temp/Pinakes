summary: Two days on the island end in the same verses, “So all day long, until the sun went down, / we sat feasting on untold meat and sweet wine” and “When the sun went down and darkness came on, / and then we lay down to sleep where the sea breaks upon the shore” (9.161–162 and 9.168–169; again 9.556–559); and at Ismarus the battle turns in the afternoon, “But when the sun moved on toward the time for unyoking oxen” (9.58).
grc: ["δὴ τότε κοιμήθημεν", "ἠέλιος μετενίσσετο βουλυτόνδε"]
en: [lay down to sleep, toward the time for unyoking oxen]
---

9.161 and 9.168 are the Iliad's verses of the feast of the gods and of the night at Chryse (*Iliad* 1.601, 1.475), and the sleep on the shore is the verse of Menelaus' nights on Pharos (4.430, 4.575; see the Odyssey translation's formulas). βουλυτός is “the time for unyoking oxen”: Merry explains that the sun's course is a rise until noon and a descent after it, and that at the end of the day's work the Sun-god unyokes his horses as the plowman his oxen (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 9.58). The Iliad has the same verse at the turn of the battle over Cebriones (*Iliad* 16.779). See [[feast-type-scene]], [[untold]] and [[helios]].
