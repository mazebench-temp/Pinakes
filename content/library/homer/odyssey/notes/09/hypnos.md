summary: Sleep marks the nights of the tale and the turn of the blinding: the crews fall asleep on the island of goats (9.151) and lie down to sleep on the shore (9.169, 9.559); the flocks and their master used to sleep in the cave (9.184, 9.187); the stake is to be driven in “when sweet sleep came upon him” (9.333), and the drunken Cyclops is taken by “sleep / that subdues all” (9.372–373).
# ἀποβρίξαντες (9.151; again 12.7), ἰαύεσκον (9.184), ἐνίαυε (9.187).
grc: [ἀποβρίξαντες, ἰαύεσκον, ἐνίαυε]
en: [fell asleep, used to sleep]
---

**Nights of the voyage.** The crews come to the island of goats in a night so dark that nothing could be seen, run ashore, “There we fell asleep and waited for brilliant Dawn” (ἀποβρίξαντες, 9.151): ἀποβρίζειν is properly to be heavy with sleep (W. W. Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 9.151; see [[the-island-of-goats]]). The day's feast ends with the formula of lying down, “and then we lay down to sleep where the sea breaks upon the shore” (9.169, 9.559; see [[sunset-lines]]).

**Sleep in the cave.** The cave was where “many / flocks, sheep and goats, used to sleep” (ἰαύεσκον, 9.183–184), “were housed at night”, since by day they were out at pasture (Merry on 9.184), and “There a huge man used to sleep” (ἐνίαυε, 9.187; see [[the-cave-of-polyphemus]]). The plan depends on the Cyclops' sleep: the four chosen by lot are to drive the stake into his eye “when sweet sleep came upon him” (9.333; see [[sweet-sleep]]). After the third bowl of wine “he lay with his thick neck bent aside, and sleep / that subdues all took him” (9.372–373; see [[that-subdues-all]] and [[wine]]). After the blinding his neighbors complain that he has made them “sleepless” in the ambrosial night (9.404; see [[sleepless]]), and at the escape the rams on which he slept carry the men out (9.427–431).
