---
summary: Strabo reads this book as geography and history: Ithaca's place among its islands, the proverb of Malea, Polybius' reckoning of the nine days to the Lotus-eaters, the Cyclopes around Etna, the cave that Odysseus reached from the sea, and Plato's Cyclopes as the first stage of human settlement. He also records a statue of a citharode inscribed with the book's verses on the singer.
---

- **Ithaca.** Strabo takes Neritum for a mountain from “I live in clear-seen Ithaca; and on it is a mountain, / Neritum” (9.21–22), and explains “lies low in the sea, farthest out of all / toward the darkness” as lying close to the mainland and farthest toward the north; he also counts these verses among the poet's marks of latitude (*Geography* 10.2.11–12, 1.2.20; 9.21–26): [[ithaca]] and [[neritum]].
- **Malea.** The sea beyond Malea was hard to sail for its contrary winds, “and so they say in a proverb, ‘When you have rounded Malea, forget your home’” (*Geography* 8.6.20, a plain rendering), the danger that Merry sets beside 9.80 (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 9.80): [[malea]].
- **The nine days.** Polybius, as Strabo reports, found what is said of the Lotus-eaters in agreement with the island of Meninx, and took the nine days of “deadly winds” (9.82) for a short run, since deadly winds do not blow straight, rather than a voyage out into the Ocean; Strabo answers that he hides the verses that place the wanderings in the Ocean (*Geography* 1.2.17–18): [[lotus-eaters]] and [[the-lotus-eaters]].
- **The Cyclopes.** Homer took his beginnings from history: the Cyclopes and the Laestrygonians, inhospitable men, were said to live around Etna and Leontini, and he may have brought the one-eyed Cyclopes from the Scythian story of the Arimaspians told by Aristeas; their size belongs to the poet's myth (*Geography* 1.2.9–11). Odysseus says that he came to the land of the Cyclopes because he went from the sea as far as the cave, which he places “at the far edge of the land” (*Geography* 1.2.32; 9.182): [[cyclopes]], [[the-cave-of-polyphemus]] and [[strabo-gold-around-silver]].
- **The first settlement.** Strabo retells Plato's three stages of settlement after the floods, giving the first, on the mountain tops, to the Cyclopes, and quotes 9.109 and 9.112–115; elsewhere the soldiers who campaigned among the Albanians of the Caucasus describe “a sort of Cyclopean life”, a land where “all these things grow unsown and unplowed” (*Geography* 13.1.25, 11.4.3): [[plato-the-cyclopes]].
- **Cyclopean caves.** Next to Nauplia are caves with labyrinths built in them, “which they call Cyclopean”, Κυκλώπεια, the word of the book's title (*Geography* 8.6.2): [[the-title-of-odyssey-9]].
- **The citharode's statue.** At Magnesia on the Maeander a bronze statue of the citharode Anaxenor bore Odysseus' verses on the singer, “truly it is a fine thing to listen to a singer / such as this one is, like the gods in his voice” (9.3–4), with the last letter left off for want of room on the base (*Geography* 14.1.41): [[the-praise-of-the-feast-in-later-writers]] and [[singers]].
