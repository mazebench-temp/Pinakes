---
summary: Odysseus' plea to the Cyclops: “But we in our turn have reached you and come to your knees” (9.266).
---

The suppliant's goal is the knees, and Odysseus names them in words: τὰ σὰ γοῦνα ἱκόμεθα, “we in our turn have reached you and come to your knees” (9.266), the knees the object of the verb of coming, as in Telemachus' “That is why I come now to your knees” to Nestor and to Menelaus (3.92 = 4.322; W. W. Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 9.266; see [[supplication]]). There is no sign that he clasps them: the plea is spoken, and the Cyclops answers it with scorn for the gods (9.273–278; see [[xenia]]).
