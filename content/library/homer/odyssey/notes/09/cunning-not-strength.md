---

**Trickery, not force.** In the cave strength is useless to Odysseus. His first plan is to stab the Cyclops with his sword as he lies among his flocks, but then they would all have perished there, since their hands could not have pushed the stone from the doorway (9.298–305; see [[boule]] and [[door-stone]]). The plan that works is cunning: the stake, the wine, the false name and the rams (see [[metis]] and [[odysseus-and-the-cyclops]]). When the Cyclopes come to the door they ask whether anyone is killing him “by trickery or by force” (δόλῳ ἠὲ βίηφιν, 9.406), and his answer, “Friends, Nobody is killing me by trickery, and not by force” (9.408), sends them away. The Cyclopes hear that no one is killing him at all, while he means that he is being killed by craft and not by force (W. W. Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 9.408; see [[nobody]] and [[dolos]]). Odysseus' heart laughs “how my name and my flawless plan had deceived him” (9.414; see [[apate]]).

**The strong man expected.** The Cyclops learns the lesson too late. He had always expected “some tall and handsome man … clothed in great courage”, and he was blinded by “one who is small and a nobody and feeble”, who overpowered him with wine (9.513–516; see [[alke]] and [[wine]]). Odysseus had already turned the word against him from the ship: “Cyclops, it was not a weakling's companions after all that you were / to eat in your hollow cave with your mighty strength” (κρατερῆφι βίηφι, 9.475–476; see [[kratos]]).
