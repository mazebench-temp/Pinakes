# ὀφρύας (9.389), first met in the Odyssey here (it applies in the Odyssey only); ὀφρύσι (9.468) is the entry's.
grc: [ὀφρύας]
---

When the stake is twirled in the Cyclops' eye, “the blast singed all his eyelids round about, and his brows, / as the eyeball burned” (9.389–390; see [[eyes]] and [[fire]]). Back at the ship the men lament the six who were eaten, “But I would not let them—I signed no with my brows to each— / weep” (9.468–469; see [[lament]]). ἀνα-νεύειν, W. W. Merry explains, is the gesture of refusal, throwing back the head, opposed to κατανεύειν, the nod of assent; it may be used absolutely, with the head, as when Achilles signs to his men not to shoot at Hector (*Iliad* 22.205), or, as here, with the brows, the refusal being shown also in the knitting of the brow (*Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 9.468; see the translation's note on 9.468–470). The words ἀνὰ … ἑκάστῳ are parenthetical, and κλαίειν depends on οὐκ εἴων (Merry on 9.469). In the Iliad the brows nod the other way, when Zeus grants Thetis' prayer (*Iliad* 1.528; see the entry).
