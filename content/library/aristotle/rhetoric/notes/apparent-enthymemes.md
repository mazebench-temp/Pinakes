**Book II.** “Since it is possible for one syllogism to be real and another not to be one but to appear so, there must also be some enthymemes that are real and others that are not enthymemes but appear to be” (1400b34–37). The topics: from diction, stating a conclusion without having reasoned to it (1401a1–13), and homonymy, the mouse and the mysteries, the dog and Pan (1401a13–25; see [[the-dog-of-the-great-goddess]]); combination and division, the argument of Euthydemus, Polycrates' “thirty tyrants”, Theodectes' Orestes (1401a25–1401b3; see [[euthydemus]], [[polycrates]] and [[it-is-just-that-she-die]]); exaggeration (1401b3–9); a sign, “the love of Harmodius and Aristogeiton overthrew the tyrant Hipparchus” (1401b9–14; see [[hipparchus]]); the accidental, Polycrates' mice and Achilles' anger at Tenedos (1401b14–20; see [[tenedos]]); the consequent, the Alexander (1401b20–29; see [[the-alexander]]); the false cause, Demades on Demosthenes' policy (1401b29–34; see [[demades]]); the omission of when and how, Alexander's right to Helen (1401b34–1402a3); and the absolute and the qualified, Agathon's probable improbability and the art of Corax (1402a3–28; see [[the-probable-improbable]] and [[the-art-of-corax]]).
