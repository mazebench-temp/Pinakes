**Book III.** Antisthenes likened lean Cephisodotus to frankincense (1407a10–11). His own metaphors are among those of 3.10: when Chares was eager to submit to his audit for the Olynthian war, Cephisodotus said “that he was trying to submit to his audit while he had the people by the throat” (1411a6–9); urging the Athenians to Euboea, he said they must take provisions and march out, “a decree of Miltiades” (1411a9–11); he called the triremes “painted mills” (1411a23–24); and he urged them to beware of making their “concourses” many (1411a28–29; see [[chares]], [[miltiades]] and [[metaphora]]).
