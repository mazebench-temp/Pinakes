summary: Homer names Nireus three times, and so seems to say much of a man he mentions once.
heading: In the Rhetoric
---

**Book III.** Asyndeta and repetition amplify, making one thing seem many: “This is what Homer too wants to do in ‘Nireus from Syme, Nireus, son of Aglaea, Nireus, the most handsome.’ For a man about whom much is said must be named often; so if he is named often, much seems to be said” (1414a1–5; see [[asyndeton]] and [[nireus]]).
