---
summary: Rhetoric is the counterpart of dialectic, its offshoot and its likeness: both are capacities for furnishing arguments, not sciences of any defined subject.
heading: In the Rhetoric
grc: ["διαλεκτικ*"]
en: [dialectic, dialectical, dialectician]
---

Rhetoric and dialectic “are concerned with such things as it is in a way the common business of all to know, and as belong to no one defined science” (1354a1–3). Both alone among the arts argue opposite conclusions (1355a33–35), and both see the apparent as well as the real argument; a man is a sophist by his choice, a dialectician by his capacity (1355b15–21). Rhetoric is “a part of dialectic and a likeness of it”, for “neither of them is a science of how any defined thing stands, but they are capacities for furnishing arguments” (1356a30–33). Its proofs correspond to dialectic's: the example to induction, the enthymeme to the syllogism (1356a35–b11; see [[paradeigma]], [[enthymema]] and [[syllogismos]]). But the more one makes either a science of a particular subject, the more one destroys its nature (1359b12–16; see [[topos]]).

**Book II.** Enthymemes differ from dialectical syllogisms in not drawing their conclusions from far back (1395b24–26); refutative and demonstrative enthymemes differ as refutation and syllogism do in dialectic (1396b22–25); the fallacy of the absolute and the qualified works “as in eristic arguments” (1402a3–7).
