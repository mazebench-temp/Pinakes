---
summary: Leodamas argued, against Callistratus, that the man who plans a deed is more guilty than the man who does it, and against Chabrias the reverse: a thing can be made greater either way.
heading: In the Rhetoric
grc: [Λεωδάμας]
---

Among the topics of the greater good, the beginning is greater than what is not a beginning, and also the end: so, “as Leodamas, accusing Callistratus, said that the one who planned did more wrong than the one who did the deed, for it would not have been done had he not planned it” (1364a19–21), and “again, accusing Chabrias, that the doer did more wrong than the planner, for it would not have happened had there been no one to do it” (1364a21–23). The point is that the same topic can be turned to either side (see [[megethos]], [[callistratus]] and [[chabrias]]).

**Book II.** Accused by Thrasybulus of having had his name erased from a stele under the Thirty, he answered that the Thirty would have trusted him more if his enmity to the people had stayed inscribed (1400a32–36; see [[thrasybulus]] and [[the-thirty]]).
