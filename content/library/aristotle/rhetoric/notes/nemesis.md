---
summary: Indignation is pain at undeserved good fortune, opposed to pity.
heading: In the Rhetoric
---

**Book II.** Chapter 9: “Opposed to pity above all is what people call being indignant” (1386b8): pain at undeserved good fortune, as pity is pain at undeserved misfortune, and both belong to a good character; “that is why we attribute indignation to the gods as well” (1386b14–15). It is felt at wealth and power, not at virtue, and more at the newly rich (1387a8–24); when a good man gets what does not suit him, and when the inferior contends with the superior, as Hector with Ajax (1387a30–1387b3; see [[aristotle-nemesis]]). Those who are worthy of the greatest goods are prone to it, the slavish and the bad are not (1387b5–15). Envy is different (1386b16–24; see [[phthonos]]).
