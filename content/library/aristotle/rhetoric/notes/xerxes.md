summary: Aristotle's example of a historical example: neither Darius nor Xerxes crossed over to Greece before he had taken Egypt. Lycophron called Xerxes “a monstrous man”, and Isocrates' Panegyricus recalls his bridging of the Hellespont and the canal through Athos.
heading: In the Rhetoric
grc: [Ξέρξην]
---

**Book II.** An example of the kind that tells of past events: one must prepare against the king and not let him take Egypt, “for in the past Darius did not cross over before he had taken Egypt, but having taken it he crossed; and again Xerxes did not set about it before he had taken it, but having taken it he crossed” (1393a33–1393b2; see [[darius]], [[egypt]] and [[paradeigma]]). Aristotle's history is compressed. Egypt had been Persian since Cambyses (Herodotus 3.1–16); what Xerxes took was Egypt in revolt (7.7).

**Book III.** Lycophron's rare word πελώριος, “monstrous”, for Xerxes is frigid (1406a7). Isocrates' antithesis about him, “to sail through the mainland and march through the sea, bridging the Hellespont and digging through Athos” (1410a10–12), is quoted without his name (see [[isocrates]]).
