summary: Shame before the eyes of others: he told the Athenians to picture the Greeks around them, seeing what they voted.
heading: In the Rhetoric
---

**Book II.** People feel more shame when those before whom they feel it are looking on, “as Cydias said in his speech to the assembly about the Samian cleruchy: he asked the Athenians to imagine that, standing around them in a circle, were the Greeks, seeing and not only going to hear of whatever they would vote” (1384b32–35; see [[aischyne]] and [[samos-ionia]]). The image makes the hearers' shame present, which is what the chapter teaches: shame is felt before the eyes of those whose opinion counts (see [[shame-is-in-the-eyes]]).
