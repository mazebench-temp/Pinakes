**Book II.** Three of the topics of enthymemes name him. Leodamas, “when Thrasybulus accused him of having had his name on a stele on the Acropolis, but of having had it cut out under the Thirty”, answered that this was impossible, since the Thirty would have trusted him more if his enmity toward the people had stayed inscribed (1400a32–36; see [[leodamas]]); whether that accuser was the hero of Phyle is not certain. From the name: “Conon called Thrasybulus the bold in counsel” (1400b20; see [[conon]]). Among the apparent enthymemes, from combination: Polycrates said “that he overthrew thirty tyrants; for he combines them” (1401a35–36), counting the Thirty as thirty separate tyrannies (see [[polycrates]] and [[apparent-enthymemes]]).
