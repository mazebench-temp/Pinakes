“The laughable has been treated separately in the books on poetics” (1372a1–2). The surviving *Poetics* defines the laughable briefly (*Poetics* 1449a32–37), but the fuller treatment Aristotle refers to here, and again in Book III (1419b5–6), was in the lost second book (see [[the-lost-book-on-comedy]] and [[geloion]]).

**Book III.** Book III refers to the *Poetics* five times: for the style of poetry, which “has been discussed in the books on poetics” (1404a39); for the kinds of noun, “studied in the books on poetry” (1404b26–28), and the words other than the standard (1404b7); for what metaphor is and how many its kinds (1405a3–6); and for the kinds of the laughable (1419b6; see [[the-lost-book-on-comedy]]). The surviving *Poetics* treats the kinds of word and of metaphor (chapters 21–22); the laughable it defines only in passing.
