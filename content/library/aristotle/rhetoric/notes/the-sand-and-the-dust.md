---
summary: Achilles' refusal is Aristotle's example of hyperbole, the speech of anger.
heading: In the Rhetoric
---

**Book III.** “Hyperboles are adolescent, for they show vehemence. That is why angry people use them most” (1413a30–31), and Achilles' answer to the embassy is the example: “not if he gave me as much as the sand and the dust. And I will not marry the daughter of Agamemnon, son of Atreus, not if she rivaled golden Aphrodite in beauty, and Athena in handiwork” (1413a32–34; *Iliad* 9.385, 388–390). Aristotle joins line 385 to 388 and leaves out what lies between (see [[hyperbole]] and [[achilles]]).
