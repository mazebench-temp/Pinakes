**Book III.** Alcidamas' metaphor for the poem, “a beautiful mirror of human life”, is frigid because too poetic (1406b12–13; see [[alcidamas]]). Book III quotes the Odyssey for Sisyphus' shameless stone (1411b33–34; *Odyssey* 11.598; see [[homers-lifeless-things-alive]]), for the proem “Tell me, Muse, of the man” (1415a16; *Odyssey* 1.1; see [[proem]]), for Odysseus' prayer to come to the Phaeacians “as a friend and one to be pitied” (1415b26–27; *Odyssey* 6.327; see [[prooimion]]), for the tale to Alcinous retold to Penelope (1417a13–15), for the old nurse who covers her face (1417b5; *Odyssey* 19.361; see [[diegesis]]), and for “all that a wise man would” (1418a8; *Odyssey* 4.204; see [[enthymema]]).
