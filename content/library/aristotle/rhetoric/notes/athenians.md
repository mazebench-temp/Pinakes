Socrates' saying on praising Athenians among Athenians shows that praise must suit its audience (1367b8–9; see [[praising-athenians-among-athenians]]), and the Athenians' appeal to the Catalogue of Ships in their dispute over Salamis is the first example of an ancient witness (1375b29–30; see [[solon-and-salamis]]).

**Book II.** Cydias asked them to imagine the Greeks standing round them (1384b32–35; see [[cydias]]); to blame them one may say that they enslaved the Aeginetans and Potidaeans (1396a17–20; see [[potidaea]]); they prospered under the laws of Solon (1398b17–18).

**Book III.** Saying that for the Athenians the empire of the sea was not the beginning of their troubles plays on ἀρχή (1412b5–6; see [[asteion]]); and it is easy to praise Athenians among Athenians, hard among Lacedaemonians (1415b31–32; see [[praising-athenians-among-athenians]]).
