**Book III.** “Frigidities in style arise in four ways” (1405b35). First, double words, as in Lycophron and Gorgias (1405b35–1406a6; see [[lycophron-the-sophist]], [[gorgias]] and [[compound-word]]); second, rare words (1406a6–10; see [[glotta]]); third, epithets “long, or untimely, or crowded”, as in Alcidamas, “That is why the writings of Alcidamas seem frigid” (1406a10–32; see [[alcidamas]] and [[epitheton]]); fourth, metaphors that are laughable, too dignified and tragic, or far-fetched and obscure (1406b5–14; see [[metaphora]]). The poetic manner can be well used, as in Gorgias' rebuke of the swallow (1406b14–19; see [[philomela]]).
