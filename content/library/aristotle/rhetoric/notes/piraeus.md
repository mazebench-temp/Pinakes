**Book II.** Among the apparent enthymemes from combination and division is “the argument of Euthydemus: for instance, knowing that there is a trireme in the Piraeus, since he knows each of the two” (1401a28–29; see [[euthydemus]] and [[apparent-enthymemes]]). The man knows each fact separately, and the sophist combines them.

**Book III.** Two metaphors of chapter 10 name the Piraeus: Peitholaus called Sestus “the meal-tray of the Piraeus” (1411a14–15), and Pericles urged the Athenians to remove Aegina, “the eyesore of the Piraeus” (1411a15–16; see [[peitholaus]] and [[pericles]]).
