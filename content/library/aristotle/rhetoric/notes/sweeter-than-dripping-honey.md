---
summary: Even anger is pleasant, for it hopes for revenge.
heading: In the Rhetoric
grc: ["γλυκίων μέλιτος"]
en: [which is far sweeter than dripping honey]
---

Things that delight when present delight also when we hope for them and remember them, “that is why even being angry is pleasant, as Homer wrote of temper, ‘which is far sweeter than dripping honey’ (for no one is angry with someone on whom it seems impossible to get revenge, ...)” (1370b10–14; see [[thymos]] and [[hedone]]). Aristotle calls the passion θυμός, “temper”, where Homer has χόλος.

**Book II.** In the definition of anger, with the next line: “That is why it has been well said of temper, ‘which, far sweeter than dripping honey, swells in the breasts of men’; for a certain pleasure attends it” (1378b4–7; see [[orge]] and [[aristotle-anger]]).
