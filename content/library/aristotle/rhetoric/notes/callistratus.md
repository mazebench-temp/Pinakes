summary: Prosecuted by Leodamas, Callistratus himself accused his rival Melanopus of cheating the temple-builders of three sacred half-obols: a small theft that proves a great injustice.
heading: In the Rhetoric
grc: [Καλλίστρατος, Καλλιστράτου, Μεσσηνιακῇ]
---

Leodamas, accusing him, argued that the man who plans a deed is more guilty than the man who does it (1364a19–21; see [[leodamas]]). In chapter 14 Callistratus is the prosecutor: a wrong is greater the greater the injustice it comes from, “that is why the smallest can be the greatest, as in the charge Callistratus brought against Melanopus, that he had cheated the temple-builders of three sacred half-obols” (1374b24–27; see [[melanopus]]). Whoever steals three sacred half-obols would do any wrong whatever. Freese notes that the two were rival orators and that nothing more is known of the charge. Book III recalls his speech in the Messenian assembly (1418b10–12).

**Book III.** The first speaker should meet the opposing arguments first if they are many, “as Callistratus did in the Messenian assembly: he first demolished what they would say, and only then made his own case” (1418b10–12; see [[pistis]]).
