“A friend is one who, whatever he thinks good for another, is active in doing it for the other's sake” (1361b36–37); many friends and good friends are parts of happiness (1360b20; see [[eudaimonia]]). “A friend too is among pleasant things; for loving is pleasant (no one is fond of wine who does not enjoy wine), and being loved is pleasant” (1371a17–19; see [[hedone]]). In chapter 12 friends are easy to wrong, and enemies pleasant to wrong (1373a4–5).

**Book II.** Chapter 4: “Let loving be wishing for someone what one thinks good, for his sake and not for one's own, and being disposed, so far as one can, to bring it about” (1380b36–1381a1). A friend is one who loves and is loved in return (1381a1–3). People love those who do them good and those like them who share their pursuits, so long as their living does not come from the same source, “for then it becomes ‘potter against potter’” (1381b15–17; see [[potter-against-potter]]). Enmity and hatred are studied from the opposites; hatred, unlike anger, is felt toward classes and wishes the other not to exist (1382a1–15; see [[orge]]). The young are fond of their friends, the old love as though they will hate (1389a35–1389b2, 1389b23–25; see [[love-as-though-you-will-hate]]).
