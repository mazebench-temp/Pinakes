---
summary: The necessary sign, which alone cannot be refuted; Aristotle connects the word with tekmar, “limit”.
heading: In the Rhetoric
grc: ["τεκμηρι*", "τέκμαρ"]
en: [tekmērion, tekmar]
---

“Of these, the necessary sign is a tekmērion, while the one that is not necessary has no name for the difference” (1357b4–5). People think they bring a tekmērion when they think what they have said cannot be refuted, “for tekmar and limit mean the same in the ancient tongue” (1357b9–10). The example: that a woman has given birth because she has milk (1357b14–16). All three kinds of speech need premises from tekmēria, probabilities and signs (1359a7–8; see [[semeion]] and [[eikos]]).

**Book II.** If it is clear both that a thing is so and that it is a tekmērion, it cannot be solved, “for everything becomes a demonstration, now evident” (1403a10–15; see [[enstasis]]).
