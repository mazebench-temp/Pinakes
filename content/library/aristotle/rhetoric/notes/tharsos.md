**Book II.** Confidence, the Attic θάρρος: “for confidence is the opposite of ⟨fear, and what inspires confidence of⟩ what is fearful, so that it is hope, with an image, of what brings safety as near” (1383a16–18). People are confident when they have often succeeded or escaped, when they have many allies, when they have done no wrong, and when they are angry, “for anger inspires confidence” (1383a19–1383b10; see [[orge]] and [[phobos]]). The young are confident through hope (1389a26–28); those in their prime are neither very confident nor too fearful (1390a29–31).
