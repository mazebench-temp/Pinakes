---
summary: “Alexander was chosen by the goddesses”: a judge preferred by the wise and good.
heading: In the Rhetoric
---

The goddesses chose Alexander to judge their beauty, and so he is among the examples of what the wise and good have preferred (1363a16–19; see [[theseus]] and [[the-abduction-of-helen]]).

**Book II.** If Theseus and the sons of Tyndareus did no wrong, neither did Alexander, and if Hector did no wrong in killing Patroclus, neither did Alexander in killing Achilles (1397b22–25); Isocrates proves him good because the goddesses preferred him (1399a3); a lost defense argued that he was not licentious and that he was great-souled (1398a22–23, 1401b20–23; see [[the-alexander]]); and he took Helen justly, since her father gave her the choice (1401b35–1402a1).

**Book III.** An epideictic proem may advise honoring “those who are neither of good repute nor worthless, but who, though good, are obscure, like Alexander son of Priam” (1414b38–39; see [[prooimion]]).
