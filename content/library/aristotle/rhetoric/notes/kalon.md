For those who praise and blame the end is “the noble and the shameful” (1358b27–28; see [[three-kinds-of-rhetoric]] and [[aischros]]).

{{quote:90dfcd0f-e664-555b-b39c-bdeb8880cdad}}

So virtue is noble, and so are the works of justice and courage, what is done for others or for one's country, honors, victory, and what each people holds in honor (1366b25–1367a33; see [[the-parts-of-virtue]]). Among the greater goods, “the nobler than the less noble; for the noble is either what is pleasant or what is chosen for itself” (1364b27–28). Praise may treat a neighboring quality as the noble one, calling the rash man courageous and the prodigal generous (1367a33–b3).
