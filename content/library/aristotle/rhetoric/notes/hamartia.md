---
summary: Errors are distinguished from wrongs and misfortunes: not unexpected, but not from wickedness.
heading: In the Rhetoric
grc: [ἁμαρτήματα, ἁμαρτία, ἁμαρτεῖν, ἁμαρτάνειν]
en: [error, errors, erred]
---

It is equitable “not to count errors and wrongs as equal, nor errors and misfortunes” (1374b4–6): errors are “those that are not unexpected and do not come from wickedness” (1374b8). People do wrong when they can seem “to have erred rather than done wrong” (1372b18). The doctor's error does less harm than the habit of disobeying authority (1375b22–23). The same distinctions appear in the *Nicomachean Ethics* (5.8; see [[adikia]] and [[epieikes]]).
