summary: Demades blamed Demosthenes' policy for all the evils because the war came after it, Aristotle's example of an apparent enthymeme from a false cause. Demosthenes likened the people to seasick passengers on a ship.
heading: In the Rhetoric
grc: [Δημοσθένης]
---

**Book II.** Among the apparent enthymemes is the one that takes what is not a cause for a cause, because it happened at the same time or after: people take “after this” as “because of this”, “and above all those in political life, as Demades took Demosthenes' policy to be the cause of all the evils, since the war came after it” (1401b31–34; see [[demades]] and [[apparent-enthymemes]]). The war is presumably Philip's, which ended at Chaeronea. A trial “about Demosthenes and those who killed Nicanor” among the topics of enthymemes (1397b7) concerns a Demosthenes who cannot be identified (see [[the-trial-over-nicanor]]).

**Book III.** Among the similes: “what Demosthenes said of the people, that it is like passengers on ships who are seasick” (1407a6–8). Aristotle does not say which Demosthenes he means (see [[eikon]]).
