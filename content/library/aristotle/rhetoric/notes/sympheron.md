summary: The end of the adviser; it is a good, and the means to happiness, so the goods must be studied for deliberative speech.
heading: In the Rhetoric
grc: ["συμφερ*", "ἀσύμφορ*"]
en: [advantageous, advantage, interests]
---

The adviser's end is “the advantageous and the harmful” (1358b21–22), and he brings in justice and nobility only in support of it: advisers will often admit that a policy is unjust, but never that it is disadvantageous (1358b33–37; see [[three-kinds-of-rhetoric]]). “Since the target set before the adviser is the advantageous (for people deliberate not about the end but about the means to the end, and these are what is advantageous in actions, and the advantageous is a good), we should grasp the elements concerning the good and the advantageous in general” (1362a17–21; see [[agathos]] and [[eudaimonia]]). All people are persuaded by what is advantageous, and what preserves a constitution is advantageous to it (1365b24–25; see [[politeia]]).
