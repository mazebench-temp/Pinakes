summary: Happiness is the target of all exhortation; it is defined by the common opinions, and its parts are listed and defined one by one.

---

Every person and every city has a target in what they choose and avoid, “and this, to put it in a word, is happiness and its parts” (1360b6–7). Aristotle's definition gathers the common opinions:

{{quote:01f36af3-d7e1-5d7e-b9e2-c4cfc755b9dd}}

The parts follow: good birth, many friends, good friends, wealth, good children, many children, a good old age, the excellences of the body, reputation, honor, good fortune and virtue, goods within a man and goods outside him (1360b19–30), and each is defined in turn (1360b31–1362a14; see [[time]], [[philos]] and [[tyche]]). Health does not count if it keeps a man from living, as Herodicus' did (1361b3–6; see [[herodicus]]).
