**Book III.** Epithets, like metaphors, must fit (1405a10–11), and they can be drawn from the worse or the better side, as Simonides showed when he wrote of mules (1405b20–28; see [[simonides]]). Poetic epithets in prose are a source of frigidity: “In poetry it is appropriate to say ‘white milk’, but in prose some are rather inappropriate” (1406a12–13), and Alcidamas uses them “not as a seasoning but as the main dish” (1406a18–19; see [[alcidamas]] and [[psychron]]). They also make for bulk (1407b31–32; see [[onkos]]), and suit a speaker carried away by emotion (1408b11–12).
