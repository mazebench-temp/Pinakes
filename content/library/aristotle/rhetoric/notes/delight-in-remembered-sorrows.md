---
summary: Remembered sorrows are pleasant, because not having an evil is also pleasant.
heading: In the Rhetoric
grc: ["ἄλγεσι τέρπεται"]
en: [For a man takes delight even in his sorrows afterward]
---

After the line of Euripides, “and ‘For a man takes delight even in his sorrows afterward, remembering, one who has suffered much and done much.’ The reason is that not having an evil is also pleasant” (1370b5–7; see [[sweet-to-remember-toils]] and [[hedone]]).
