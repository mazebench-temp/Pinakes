All voluntary actions aim at what is or seems good or pleasant, so after the good the pleasant must be studied (1369b18–32; see [[seven-causes-of-action]]).

{{quote:5bb620db-2d5e-5d62-ad39-174945dd2729}}

From the definition follow the pleasant things: the natural and the habitual, what is not forced (see [[every-necessary-thing-is-grievous]]), ease and sleep; everything for which there is appetite (see [[epithymia]]); what is remembered and hoped for, even past toil, anger and mourning (see [[sweet-to-remember-toils]], [[sweeter-than-dripping-honey]] and [[the-longing-for-lament]]); revenge, victory, honor and friendship; change (see [[change-in-all-things-is-sweet]]); learning and wonder, imitations, reversals and narrow escapes (see [[pleasure-of-imitation]]); the kindred and the self (see [[like-to-like]]); ruling, and laughter (1370a1–1372a3).
