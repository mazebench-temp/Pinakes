**Book II.** The young care least for money “because they have not yet experienced want, as Pittacus' saying about Amphiaraus has it” (1389a14–16; see [[amphiaraus]]); the saying itself is lost. In the chapter on objections, his law is a judgment of a famous man set against an enthymeme: to the argument that one should pardon the drunk, since they err in ignorance, the objection is “that Pittacus, then, is not to be praised; for he would not have laid down greater penalties if someone does wrong when drunk” (1402b9–12; see [[enstasis]]).
