summary: People are ashamed of shameful things when they say them, do them or are about to, so the opposites of what shames are noble.
heading: In the Rhetoric
grc: ["θέλω τι εἰπῆν", "αἱ δʼ ἦχες ἐσθλῶν ἵμερον"]
en: [I want to say something, but shame prevents me, If you had a longing for good or noble things]
---

Among noble things are “the opposites of what people are ashamed of; for people are ashamed of shameful things when they say them, when they do them, and when they are about to, as Sappho too has shown in her poem” (1367a7–9). Sappho's answer shows that shame attends what one is about to say, and so marks it as shameful (see [[kalon]] and [[aischros]]).
