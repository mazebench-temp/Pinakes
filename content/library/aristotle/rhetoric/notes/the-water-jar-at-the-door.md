---
summary: The proverb shows the topic that what has cost much toil is a good: to lose it at the door is the worst loss.
heading: In the Rhetoric
grc: ["ἐπὶ θύραις τὴν ὑδρίαν"]
en: [the water jar at the door]
---

“And the proverb about the water jar at the door” (1363a7) follows the lines of Homer on Priam's boast and the shame of staying long (see [[a-thing-to-boast-of-for-priam]] and [[shameful-to-stay-long]]). Toil spent on a thing makes it an end, and so a good; that is why it hurts to lose it at the very end.
