---
summary: What deliberation is about, and the five subjects, with what the adviser must know for each.
heading: In the Rhetoric
refs: [1359a30-1360b3]
---

“Of the things about which all people deliberate, and about which advisers speak in public, the most important are, roughly, five in number: revenue, war and peace, the defense of the country, imports and exports, and legislation” (1359b18–23). On revenue the adviser must know the city's income and expenses; on war, the city's forces and its neighbors', and the outcomes of other wars; on defense, the garrison and its posts; on food, what the land produces and what must be imported; on legislation, the kinds of constitution and how each is preserved or destroyed, for the safety of the city lies in its laws (1359b23–1360a37). Descriptions of the earth help with legislation, and “the histories of those who write about men's actions” with political advice, but “all these things, however, are the work of politics, not of rhetoric” (1360a33–38; see [[historia]]).
