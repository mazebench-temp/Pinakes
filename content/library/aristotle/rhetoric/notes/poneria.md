“For as the proverb says, wickedness needs only a pretext” (1373a3; see [[wickedness-needs-only-a-pretext]]). Errors are acts “that are not unexpected and do not come from wickedness; wrongs, those that are not unexpected and do come from wickedness; for acts done from appetite come from wickedness” (1374b8–10; see [[hamartia]] and [[adikia]]). Eubulus quoted against Chares Plato's remark that admitting to being wicked had grown in the city (1376a10–11; see [[eubulus]]).
