---
summary: “Not yet”: Philocrates would not defend himself before an angry people until they had spent their anger on someone else.
heading: In the Rhetoric
---

**Book II.** Anger is stopped by a revenge taken first on someone else, “that is why Philocrates answered well when someone, the people being angry, said, ‘Why don't you defend yourself?’ ‘Not yet,’ he said. ‘But when?’ ‘When I see someone else slandered’” (1380b8–10; see [[philocrates-not-yet]] and [[praotes]]). Aristotle does not say which Philocrates; the politician of the peace of 346, who had good reason to fear the people's anger, is the usual guess.
