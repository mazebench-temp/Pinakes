**Book III.** “As for interrogation, it is most opportune to use it when the opponent has stated one of two points, so that when one more question is asked the absurdity follows” (1418b40–1419a2). The examples are Pericles and Lampon (1419a2–5; see [[lampon]]), Socrates and Meletus (1419a8–12; see [[meletus]]), Sophocles and Pisander (1419a25–30; see [[pisander-the-oligarch]]) and the Laconian ephor (1419a31–35; see [[laconians]]). Questions put to the opponent are also a way of comparison in the epilogue (1420a3–4; see [[epilogos]]).
