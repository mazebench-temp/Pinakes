# Dionysius at 1405a32 is Dionysius the Brazen, the poet.
except: [1405a32]
---

The example, Aristotle says, is neither from part to whole nor from whole to part but from part to part, like to like, when both fall under the same class and one is better known (1357b26–30):

{{quote:acf847f6-02e6-5517-ac1c-9c1f3d8daa5a}}

The conclusion is not certain, which is why the example persuades only as a kind of induction (see [[paradeigma]], [[pisistratus]] and [[theagenes-of-megara]]). Dionysius' request is the one Diodorus describes (13.95).

**Book II.** He had Antiphon the poet put to death (1385a10–11; see [[antiphon-the-tragic-poet]]); the descendants of Dionysius the elder degenerated into madder characters (1390b28–29; see [[cimon]]); “Dionysius is a thief, for he is wicked” is an invalid sign (1401b12–14; see [[apparent-enthymemes]]), where the name may stand for anyone.
