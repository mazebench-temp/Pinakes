**Book III.** The proems drawn from the speaker and the opponent are “whatever serves to dispel slander or to create it” (1415a28–29): the defendant first, the accuser in the epilogue (1415a29–34; see [[prooimion]]). Chapter 15 lists the topics (1416a4–b15): Iphicrates' reply to Nausicrates (1416a10–12; see [[iphicrates]]), Sophocles' trembling (1416a14–16; see [[sophocles]]), the plea of motive (1416a16–20), Euripides' answer to Hygiaenon (1416a28–35; see [[euripides]]), the tokens in Sophocles' *Teucer* (1416b1–3; see [[hesione]]), and the two constructions of Diomedes' choice of Odysseus (1416b9–15; see [[diomedes]]). The speech that has been slandered beforehand is not accepted, as a man is not (1418b14–16; see [[pistis]]).
