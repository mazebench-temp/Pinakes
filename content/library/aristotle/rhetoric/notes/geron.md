---
summary: A good old age comes slowly and without pain; “Never do good to an old man”.
heading: In the Rhetoric
---

“A good old age is old age that comes slowly and without pain” (1361b26–27), a part of happiness that needs both bodily excellence and fortune (see [[eudaimonia]]). In chapter 15 a proverb is cited as testimony against befriending an old man (1376a3–5; see [[never-do-good-to-an-old-man]]).
