**Book II.** His *Alcmaeon* (1397b2–6; see [[alcmaeon-theodectes]]), *The Law* (1398b6–10, 1399b1–4; see [[the-law-theodectes]]), *Socrates* (1399a8–10; see [[what-sanctuary-has-he-profaned]]), *Ajax* (1399b28–31, 1400a28–29; see [[ajax-plays]]) and *Orestes* (1401a35–1401b2; see [[orestes-theodectes]]).

**Book III.** “The beginnings of periods have been listed nearly in full in the Theodectea” (1410b2–3). The *Theodectea* was a handbook of rhetoric named after Theodectes; whether it was his own work or a work of Aristotle's dedicated to him was already uncertain in antiquity (see [[periodos]]).
