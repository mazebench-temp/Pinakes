**Book II.** People are angry with those who do not perceive their needs, “as Plexippus in Antiphon's play was with Meleager; for not perceiving is a sign of slight” (1379b15–16; see [[plexippus]] and [[oligoria]]). In the chapter on shame: “Antiphon the poet, when he was about to be put to death on the plank by Dionysius, seeing those who were to die with him veiling their faces as they went through the gates, said, ‘Why do you veil your faces? Is it so that none of these may see you tomorrow?’” (1385a9–13; see [[why-do-you-veil-your-faces]] and [[aischyne]]). Among the topics of enthymemes, the lines from his *Meleager* say that the Greeks came “not to kill the beast, but to be witnesses to the valor of Meleager before Greece” (1399b25–28; see [[meleager-antiphon]] and [[witnesses-of-meleagers-valor]]).
