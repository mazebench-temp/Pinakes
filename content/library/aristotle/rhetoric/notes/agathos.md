“Let good be that which is chosen for its own sake, and that for whose sake we choose something else, and what all things aim at” (1362a21–23), and what produces, preserves or follows such things. Agreed goods are happiness, the virtues, health and beauty, wealth, friends, honor, the abilities to speak and to act, natural gifts, the arts and sciences, life, and justice (1362b10–28). Disputed goods are argued from topics: what the enemy dislikes, what has cost toil, what many desire, what even enemies praise, what the wise prefer (1362b29–1363b4; see [[surely-priam-would-rejoice]] and [[a-thing-to-boast-of-for-priam]]). Chapter 7 then gives the topics of the greater good (see [[megethos]]).
