See [[eubulus]] (1376a9–11). The witness of a known man about a general truth is brought to bear on a particular case.

**Book III.** When Chares was eager to submit to his audit for the Olynthian war, Cephisodotus said that he was trying to do it while he had the people by the throat (1411a6–9; see [[cephisodotus]]). Iphicrates said, “The road of my words runs through the midst of Chares' deeds” (1411b2–3; see [[iphicrates]]). And Isocrates, even when giving advice, accuses: “the Lacedaemonians in the Panegyricus and Chares in the Symmachicus” (1418a32), the speech *On the Peace*.
