Each kind has its time, the adviser the future, the litigant the past, the epideictic speaker above all the present, and its end, the advantageous, the just and the noble; the others are brought in only in support (1358b8–29). The sign is what speakers will concede: a litigant may admit that he did harm but never that he did wrong; advisers ignore the injustice of enslaving their neighbors; praise counts it to Achilles' credit that he came to the aid of Patroclus knowing that he must die (1358b29–1359a5; see [[achilles]]).

The rest of the book follows the division. Chapters 4 to 8 give the materials of deliberative speech: its subjects (see [[subjects-of-deliberation]]), happiness, the good, the greater good and the constitutions. Chapter 9 gives those of epideictic, virtue and the noble. Chapters 10 to 15 give those of judicial speech: wrongdoing and its causes, pleasure, the wrongdoer and his victim, law and equity, greater wrongs, and the non-artistic proofs. Of the forms common to all speeches, amplification suits epideictic, the example deliberative, and the enthymeme judicial speech (1368a26–33; see [[auxesis]], [[paradeigma]] and [[enthymema]]).

**Book II.** Each kind of speech has its own end, but all use the common topics: amplification suits epideictic, past fact the courts, the possible and the future deliberation (1391b22–1392a7; see [[koina]]). The topic of incentives and deterrents is common to litigants and advisers (1399b31–1400a4).

**Book III.** Each kind has its style: speaking to the assembly is like shadow-painting, the judicial is more precise, the epideictic is the most suited to writing (1414a8–19; see [[styles-of-writing-and-debate]]). Narration belongs to judicial speech (1414a37–b1; see [[taxis]]), and is rarest in speeches to the assembly, for no one narrates the future (1417b12–13; see [[diegesis]]); deliberative and judicial speeches dispute different things (1417b21–38; see [[pistis]]); and speaking to the assembly is harder than pleading in court, because it concerns the future (1418a21–26; see [[epimenides]]).
