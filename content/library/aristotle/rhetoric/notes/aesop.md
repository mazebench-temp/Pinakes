**Book II.** Of examples that the speaker makes up himself, “one kind is comparison, another fables, such as the Aesopic and Libyan fables” (1393a28–31; see [[paradeigma]] and [[libya]]). Aristotle's example is Aesop's own speech: “Aesop, speaking to the assembly at Samos when a demagogue was on trial for his life”, told the fable of the fox and the ticks (1393b22–1394a1; see [[the-fox-and-the-hedgehog]] and [[samians]]). Fables suit speeches to the assembly, since it is easier to make up a likeness than to find an event that matches (1394a2–8).
