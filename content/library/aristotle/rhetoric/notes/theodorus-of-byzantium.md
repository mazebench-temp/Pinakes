**Book II.** After the topic that accuses or defends “from the mistakes made”, illustrated from Carcinus' *Medea*, Aristotle adds: “This topic of enthymeme, and this kind, is the whole of Theodorus' former art” (1400b15–17; see [[medea-carcinus]] and [[topics-of-enthymemes]]). The Greek, ἡ πρότερον Θεοδώρου τέχνη, may mean an earlier version of his handbook, or the handbook he wrote before the others.

**Book III.** Riddles are pleasant, “and so is what Theodorus calls ‘saying novel things’” (1412a26): the surprise, “not in keeping with” what one expected (1412a27; see [[asteion]]). His jest at Nicon the citharode, θράξει σε, “it will upset you”, makes as if to say Θρᾷξ εἶ σύ, “you are a Thracian” (1412a33–36; see [[nicon]] and [[thracians]]); the jest is usually taken to be this Theodorus', though Aristotle does not say so. And to divide a speech into narration, “supplementary narration and preliminary narration”, “refutation and further refutation”, “as the followers of Theodorus did”, is empty (1414b13–18; see [[taxis]]).
