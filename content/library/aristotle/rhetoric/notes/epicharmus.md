---
summary: Epicharmus' figure of combining and building up makes things seem great.
heading: In the Rhetoric
---

“And combining and building up, as Epicharmus does, both for the same reason as division (for combination shows great superiority) and because it seems to be the origin and cause of great things” (1365a15–18; see [[megethos]] and [[aristotle-cleopatras-plea]]). Epicharmus' comedies were known for such chains, in which each clause grows out of the last; Book III quotes him again, for a false antithesis (1410b4–5).

**Book III.** There are also “false antitheses, like those Epicharmus made: ‘At one time I was in their house, at another I was with them’” (1410b4–5): the clauses are opposed in form, but not in sense (see [[antithesis]]).
