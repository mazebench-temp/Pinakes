“The greatest and most decisive of all things for being able to persuade and to advise well is to grasp all the constitutions and to distinguish the characters, customs and interests of each” (1365b21–24).

{{quote:24de00aa-7b7f-56bb-a6b2-7488202d99c2}}

Since character persuades, the speaker must know the character of each constitution, “for the character of each is bound to be the most persuasive to each” (1366a12–13). The safety of the city is in its laws, so the adviser on legislation must know how constitutions are preserved and destroyed (1360a18–30; see [[subjects-of-deliberation]]).
