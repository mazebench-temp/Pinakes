**Book III.** The first part of speaking good Greek is the connectives: they must answer one another as they naturally come, “as men and egō men require de and ho de”, and not be held far apart (1407a20–30; see [[hellenizein]]). Many connectives make writing hard to read (1407b11–13); speaking with a connective gives bulk, and leaving it out conciseness (1407b37–39; see [[onkos]]); the strung-on style is made one by the connective (1409a24–25; see [[periodos]]); and “the connective makes many things one”, so that leaving it out makes one thing many (1413b32–34; see [[asyndeton]]).
