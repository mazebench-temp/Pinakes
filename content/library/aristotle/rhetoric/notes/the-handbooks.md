summary: The compilers of the Arts teach what lies outside the subject, slander, pity and anger and the parts of a speech, and say nothing of enthymemes.
heading: In the Rhetoric
grc: ["τέχνας τῶν λόγων", "τεχνολογ*"]
en: [Arts of speeches, writers on the art]
---

“As things are, those who compile the Arts of speeches have provided, so to speak, no part of it (for proofs are the only thing within the art, and the rest is accessory); they say nothing about enthymemes, which are the body of proof, but busy themselves for the most part with matters outside the subject” (1354a11–16). Slander, pity and anger aim at the juror, not the subject (see [[pathos]] and [[areopagus]]). They teach what the introduction and the narration should contain, and they neglect speaking in the assembly, though it is nobler, because there appeals outside the subject pay less (1354b16–1355a3). They also leave out of the art the speaker's character (1356a10–13; see [[three-kinds-of-proof]]).

**Book III.** Book III returns to the handbooks in its treatment of arrangement. They divide the speech “laughably” into narration, replies and the like, when narration belongs to judicial speech alone (1414a37–b4), and the followers of Theodorus and Licymnius multiply names (1414b13–18; see [[taxis]], [[theodorus-of-byzantium]] and [[licymnius-of-chios]]); they say that the narration should be quick (1416b30; see [[diegesis]]); and some wrongly put recapitulation in the proem (1419b28–29; see [[epilogos]]).
