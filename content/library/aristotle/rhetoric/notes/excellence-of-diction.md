**Book III.** “Let the excellence of style be defined as being clear (for speech is a kind of sign, and if it does not make its meaning plain it will not do its own work), and neither mean nor above the dignity of the subject, but appropriate” (1404b1–4). Standard words make it clear, the other kinds of word ornament it (1404b5–8; see [[kyrion]] and [[xenikon]]), and the right mixture is “the excellence of rhetorical speech” (1404b35–37). At the end of the treatment of style there is no need to add that style should be pleasant or magnificent, “if the excellence of style has been rightly defined” (1414a19–28; see [[prepon]]).
