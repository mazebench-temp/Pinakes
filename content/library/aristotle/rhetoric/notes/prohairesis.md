summary: The sophist differs from the dialectician by choice; character shows in choice; praise must show that deeds were chosen; wrongdoing lies in the choice.
heading: In the Rhetoric
grc: ["προαιρ*"]
en: [choice, choose, chooses, chosen]
---

“For what makes sophistry is not the capacity but the choice” (1355b17–18). The characters of constitutions are revealed by choice, “and choice is referred to the end” (1366a14–16; see [[politeia]]). Praise must show that a man acted by choice, so coincidences should be treated as chosen (1367b21–26). “Everything done by choice is done knowingly” (1368b11–12), and the wrong lies in the choice, which is why the names of crimes such as outrage and theft signify it (1374a11–13; see [[adikia]]). The equitable man looks “not to the action but to the choice” (1374b13–14; see [[epieikes]]).

**Book III.** The narration shows character by making the choice plain, “the character is of a certain kind by the choice's being of a certain kind, and the choice by its end” (1417a17–19): so the Socratic dialogues have character and mathematical discourses none (1417a19–22; see [[socratic-dialogues]] and [[ethos]]). Demonstration has neither character nor choice (1418a16–17; see [[enthymema]]).
