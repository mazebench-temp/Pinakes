**Book III.** The third of the matters of style, after the facts and their setting out, is delivery, “a matter that has the greatest power but has not yet been taken in hand” (1403b20–22). It came late into tragedy and rhapsody; it lies in the voice, its loudness, its pitch and its rhythm, and those who master it win the prizes, “and just as there the actors now count for more than the poets, so it is in political contests too, because of the depravity of the citizens” (1403b32–35; see [[hypokrites]] and [[glaucon]]). It is thought vulgar, but it must be studied because of the hearer's depravity (1404a1–8). Some have tried to say a little about it, like Thrasymachus (1404a13–15; see [[thrasymachus]]); being good at delivery is a gift of nature (1404a15–16). In chapter 12 the style of debate is the most suited to delivery (1413b9–10), and repetitions and asyndeta, which seem silly without delivery, must be acted, as the actor Philemon did, or one becomes “the man carrying the beam” (1413b17–29; see [[philemon-the-actor]] and [[styles-of-writing-and-debate]]); where delivery counts most, precision is least present (1414a15–17).
