---
summary: The elegies of Solon as an ancient witness: Cleophon used them against Critias to show that his family had long been licentious.
heading: In the Rhetoric
grc: [Σόλωνος, Σόλων]
---

By ancient witnesses Aristotle means “the poets and all other well-known men whose judgments are on record”, and his third example: “and Cleophon used Solon's elegies against Critias, saying that the family had long been licentious; for otherwise Solon would never have written, ‘Tell red-haired Critias, for me, to listen to his father’” (1375b31–34; see [[tell-red-haired-critias]], [[cleophon-the-demagogue]] and [[critias]]). Just before, the Athenians cite Homer about Salamis, the island Solon had urged them to win (see [[solon-and-salamis]]).

**Book II.** The Athenians prospered when they used his laws (1398b17–18; see [[all-men-honor-the-wise]]).
