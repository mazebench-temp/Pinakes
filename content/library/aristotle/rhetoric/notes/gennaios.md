The men who hold out “nobly” under torture are the hardy and dull-witted, so that tortures prove nothing (1377a7).

**Book II.** “To be well-born is a matter of the excellence of the stock, to be true to one's birth is a matter of not falling away from one's nature; which for the most part does not happen to the well-born” (1390b21–23; see [[the-gifts-of-fortune]]). Iphicrates turned the word against a descendant of the tyrant-slayer: the best man is the most nobly born, since Harmodius and Aristogeiton “had nothing of nobility before they did a deed of nobility” (1398a17–20; see [[iphicrates-against-harmodius]]).
