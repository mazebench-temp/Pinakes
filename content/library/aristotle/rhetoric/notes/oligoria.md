**Book II.** “A slight is the actualization of an opinion about what appears worth nothing (for we think that bad things and good ones are worth taking seriously, and so are the things that tend toward them; but whatever is nothing or small we suppose worth nothing), there are three kinds of slight: contempt, spite and outrage” (1378b10–15). The one who despises slights, since people despise what they think worth nothing; spite thwarts another's wishes not to gain but to keep him from gaining; and outrage shames its victim for the pleasure of it (1378b15–29; see [[hybris]]). Dishonor belongs to outrage, which is why Achilles complains that he has been dishonored (1378b29–35; see [[aristotle-anger]]). Forgetting a name is a sign of slight (1379b34–38), and so is not perceiving another's needs (1379b14–16; see [[plexippus]]). People are calm toward those who slight them involuntarily, or who humble themselves (1380a8–26; see [[praotes]]). Shamelessness is “a kind of slight and unconcern” about what brings disrepute (1383b14–15; see [[aischyne]]). In the chapter on emulation: “the opposite of emulation is contempt, and of emulating, despising” (1388b22–23; see [[zelos]]).
