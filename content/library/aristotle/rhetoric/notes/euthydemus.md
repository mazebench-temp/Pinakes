summary: “Euthydemus' argument”, the fallacy of combining what is divided.
heading: In the Rhetoric

**Book II.** Among the apparent enthymemes is the one that combines what is divided or divides what is combined: “This is the argument of Euthydemus: for instance, knowing that there is a trireme in the Piraeus, since he knows each of the two” (1401a24–29; see [[apparent-enthymemes]] and [[piraeus]]).
