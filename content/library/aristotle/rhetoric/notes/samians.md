summary: Aesop's fable of the fox and the hedgehog, told to the Samian assembly to save a demagogue. Pericles likened the Samians to children who take the morsel but cry as they take it.

**Book II.** Aesop, “speaking to the assembly at Samos when a demagogue was on trial for his life”, told the fable of the fox who would not have her sated ticks taken off, and ended, “So you too, men of Samos: this man will do you no more harm (for he is rich)” (1393b23–33; see [[the-fox-and-the-hedgehog]] and [[aesop]]).

**Book III.** One of Pericles' similes (1407a1–3; see [[pericles]] and [[eikon]]).
