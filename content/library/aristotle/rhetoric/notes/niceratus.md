**Book III.** A simile is good when there is a metaphor in it: one may “say that Niceratus is a Philoctetes bitten by Pratys, as Thrasymachus likened him on seeing Niceratus, beaten by Pratys in reciting epic, with his hair still long and unwashed” (1413a7–10). Like Philoctetes on Lemnos, he was left long-haired and squalid after his defeat (see [[thrasymachus]], [[pratys]], [[philoctetes]] and [[eikon]]).
