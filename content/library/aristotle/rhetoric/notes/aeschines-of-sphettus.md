---

**Book III.** Narrate from what is emotional, telling the signs the hearers know: “And as Aeschines said of Cratylus, that he was hissing and shaking his fists; for these things are persuasive, because what the hearers know becomes a token of what they do not know” (1417b1–3; see [[cratylus]] and [[diegesis]]). The scene is probably from one of his dialogues; that the speaker is the Socratic and not the orator is the usual view.
