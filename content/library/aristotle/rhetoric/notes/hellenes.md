grc: ["^Ἑλλην*"]
en: [Greeks, men of Greece]
---

**Book II.** Cydias asked the Athenians to imagine the Greeks standing round them (1384b32–35); the Athenians enslaved the Greeks (1396a17–18); “If a private citizen must care for your good opinion, you must care for that of the Greeks” (1397b28–29; see [[the-opinion-of-the-greeks]]); “freed the Greeks” is one of the heads of many syllogisms (1401a10–11).

**Book III.** “Then the Greeks, darting forth on their feet” (1411b30), from Euripides' *Iphigenia at Aulis*, is an example of activity (see [[euripides]]); and Gorgias' *Olympic Speech* opens, “By many are you worthy to be admired, men of Greece” (1414b31–32; see [[gorgias]]).
