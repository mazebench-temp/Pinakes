summary: Turning a charge back on the accuser works only if the accuser is the likelier wrongdoer, and against Aristides it would look absurd. An epideictic proem may advise honoring the good, as the speaker does in praising Aristides.
heading: In the Rhetoric
grc: [Ἀριστείδην]
---

**Book II.** Iphicrates' retort to Aristophon depends on the opponent's seeming more likely to do wrong; “if not, it would look absurd, ⟨as⟩ if, when Aristides was the accuser, someone else were to say this to cast doubt on the accuser” (1398a8–10; see [[iphicrates-and-aristophon]]).

**Book III.** Epideictic proems come also from advice, “for example, that one should honor the good, which is why the speaker himself praises Aristides” (1414b36–37; see [[prooimion]]).
