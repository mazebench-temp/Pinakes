---
summary: Aristotle's example for the topic that the opposite of what the enemy wants, or rejoices at, is beneficial.
heading: In the Rhetoric
---

Among the topics for disputed goods: “And in general, the opposite of what the enemy wants, or of what they rejoice at, seems beneficial; hence it was said, ‘Surely Priam would rejoice’” (1362b34–35). Nestor's appeal becomes a rule of argument, and Aristotle adds at once that it holds only for the most part, since the same thing can harm both sides (1362b36–38; see [[misfortunes-bring-people-together]] and [[agathos]]).
