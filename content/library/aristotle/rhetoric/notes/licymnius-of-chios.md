**Book III.** Metaphors should come from beautiful words, and “the beauty of a word lies, as Licymnius says, in its sounds or in its meaning” (1405b6–7). He is one of the dithyrambic poets whose works are read rather than performed (1413b13–14; see [[styles-of-writing-and-debate]]). And his *Art* is one of those that divide a speech into needless parts, “naming ‘wafting’, ‘wandering off’ and ‘branches’” (1414b17–18; see [[taxis]]).
