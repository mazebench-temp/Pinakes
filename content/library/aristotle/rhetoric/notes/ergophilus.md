---
summary: The jurors spared him because they had spent their anger the day before on Callisthenes.
heading: In the Rhetoric
---

**Book II.** People become calm “when they have spent their anger on another, as happened in the case of Ergophilus: though they were more exasperated with him than with Callisthenes, they acquitted him because they had condemned Callisthenes to death the day before” (1380b10–13; see [[callisthenes-the-general]] and [[praotes]]). Demosthenes' list of condemned men includes Ergophilus (*On the False Embassy* 180), which may mean that he was fined rather than put to death, or that Aristotle's “acquitted” is loose.
