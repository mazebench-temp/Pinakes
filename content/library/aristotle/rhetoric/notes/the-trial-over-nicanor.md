**Book II.** “And the trial about Demosthenes and those who killed Nicanor: since they were judged to have killed him justly, it seemed that he had died justly” (1397b6–8; see [[topics-of-enthymemes]]). The Demosthenes of this trial cannot be identified with the orator, and Nicanor is unknown; the point of the example is the inference from the doers to the sufferer.
