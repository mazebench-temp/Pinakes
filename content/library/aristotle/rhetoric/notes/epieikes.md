---
summary: Equity is justice that goes beyond the written law, made necessary because laws must speak universally; the equitable man pardons, looks to the lawgiver's intention and to the choice, and prefers arbitration to the court.
heading: In the Rhetoric
grc: ["ἐπιεικ*"]
en: [equity, equitable, decent, decency]
---

The speaker is believed through his character, “for we believe decent people more fully and more readily on all matters in general” (1356a6–7; see [[ethos]]). In chapter 13 unwritten justice includes what the written law leaves out:

{{quote:49a46d67-b3be-5d1d-abc6-eeab3169d35f}}

The equitable pardons human failings, distinguishes misfortunes, errors and wrongs, looks “not to the law but to the lawgiver, and not to the words but to the intention of the lawgiver; and not to the action but to the choice” (1374b11–14), remembers the good received rather than the bad, and prefers arbitration, “for the arbitrator looks to equity, the juror to the law” (1374b20–21; see [[katholou]] and [[prohairesis]]). In chapter 15 a speaker whose case is against the written law appeals to equity, “that equity always remains and never changes” (1375a31–32; see [[non-artistic-proofs]]). People also wrong those from whom they can count on equity (1372b18–19, 1373a18).
