**Book II.** The *Messenian Speech*, probably his, on war and peace (1397a11–13; see [[war-the-cause-of-our-evils]]); his induction that all men honor the wise (1398b10–20; see [[all-men-honor-the-wise]]).

**Book III.** Alcidamas supplies more examples of frigidity than anyone. His double words: “filled with fury in his soul, and his face becoming fire-hued”, zeal and persuasion that are “end-fulfilling”, “azure-hued” the floor of the sea (1406a1–5); his rare words: “a plaything for poetry”, “the reckless folly of nature”, and “whetted with the unmixed anger of his thought” (1406a8–10); and above all his epithets, which he uses “not as a seasoning but as the main dish, so crowded are they, so long and so obvious” (1406a18–20), as in “the moist sweat” and “the festival of the Isthmia” (1406a20–32). His metaphors are too poetic: philosophy “a fortress raised against the law”, and the Odyssey “a beautiful mirror of human life” (1406b11–13; see [[psychron]], [[epitheton]] and [[odyssey]]).
