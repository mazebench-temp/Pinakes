summary: The enthymeme needs few premises: to say that Dorieus has won a contest with a crown for a prize is enough, without adding that the Olympic prize is a crown.
heading: In the Rhetoric
grc: [Δωριεὺς]
---

“For if one of these is well known, it need not even be stated, since the hearer supplies it himself: for instance, that Dorieus has won a contest whose prize is a crown; it is enough to say that he has won at Olympia; that the Olympic prize is a crown need not even be added, for everyone knows it” (1357a17–21). The example shows why the enthymeme is a syllogism with few premises, made for hearers who cannot follow a long chain (see [[enthymema]] and [[olympic-games]]).
