Every art “teaches and persuades about its own subject matter” (1355b27–28), while rhetoric's function “is not to persuade but to see the means of persuasion available in each case” (1355b10–11; see [[pithanon]] and [[rhetorike]]). One must be able to persuade of opposites, not to do both, but to refute the unjust argument (1355a29–33). “For all people are persuaded by what is advantageous” (1365b24; see [[sympheron]]), and some appetites come “from being persuaded” (1370a25; see [[epithymia]]).
