---
summary: What is cherished, the only one of its kind to someone, is a greater good: the eye of a one-eyed man.
heading: In the Rhetoric
en: [cherished]
---

“And what is cherished, whether to some it is the only one and to others one among others. That is why the penalty is not the same if one blinds a one-eyed man and a man with two eyes; for what was cherished has been taken away” (1365b16–19; see [[megethos]]). Homer's “beloved” son is the only son, the sense Aristotle keeps.
