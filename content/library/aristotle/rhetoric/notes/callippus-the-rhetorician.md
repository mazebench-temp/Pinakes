**Book II.** After the topic that exhorts or dissuades from the good and bad consequences of the same thing (education brings envy, but wisdom), Aristotle says: “This topic is the art of Callippus, with the addition of the possible and the other things mentioned” (1399a17–18). The topic that looks to what makes people act and what keeps them from acting “is the whole art both of Pamphilus and of Callippus” (1400a3–4; see [[pamphilus]] and [[topics-of-enthymemes]]). Both are topics of motive and consequence, as might be expected of a handbook for deliberative speech.
