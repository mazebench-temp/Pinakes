Among the means of amplification: “if the incentives and honors were invented and established on his account, and if the first encomium was composed for him, as for Hippolochus, and the setting up of a statue in the marketplace for Harmodius and Aristogeiton” (1368a15–18; see [[auxesis]] and [[hippolochus-of-the-encomium]]). The honor is greater for having been created for him.

**Book II.** Iphicrates argued from the tyrant-slayers that the best man is the most nobly born, for “Harmodius and Aristogeiton had nothing of nobility before they did a deed of nobility”, and that his own deeds were more akin to theirs than his opponent's (1398a17–22; see [[iphicrates-against-harmodius]]); the opponent was a descendant of the same name (1397b30). Their love, which overthrew “the tyrant Hipparchus”, is Aristotle's example of a fallacious sign (1401b10–12; see [[hipparchus]] and [[apparent-enthymemes]]).
