**Book III.** The paean is a rhythm of three to two, between the dactyl's one to one and the iambus' two to one (1409a3–6). It is the only rhythm from which no meter is made (1409a8–9). There are two paeans: the one that begins with a long syllable and ends with three short ones suits the beginning, as in “Delos-born, or Lycia” and “Golden-haired Far-Shooter, son of Zeus”, the openings of paeans to Apollo (1409a12–15; see [[apollo]] and [[lycia]]); the one with three shorts and a long suits the end, as in “After earth and waters, night made the ocean vanish” (1409a16–17), for the short syllable, being incomplete, makes the end seem docked (see [[rhythmos]] and [[simonides]]).
