**Book II.** After the characters, “it remains for us to go through the common topics. For all must make use in their speeches of the topic of the possible and impossible, and try to show, some that something will be, others that it has been. Further, ⟨the topic⟩ of greatness is common to all speeches” (1391b26–31). The possible (1392a8–b14): if one opposite is possible, so is the other; if the harder, so the easier; if the beginning, the end; if a worse man can do it, a better can (see [[euthynus]]); what can come about without art can come about through it (see [[some-things-are-for-art]]). Past fact (1392b15–1393a1): if the less likely has happened, so has the more likely; if a man could and wished, he did it. The future (1393a1–8): what is in one's power and wish will be. Magnitude (1393a9–19) was treated in the deliberative books, and to seek further about greatness absolutely “is idle talk” (1393a16–18; see [[megethos]]).
