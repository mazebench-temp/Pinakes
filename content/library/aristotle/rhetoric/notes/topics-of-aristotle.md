---
summary: Aristotle refers to the Topics for dealing with the many, for the difference between syllogism and induction, and for the division of topics.
heading: In the Rhetoric
grc: [Τοπικοῖς, Τοπικῶν]
en: [Topics]
---

The Topics is cited three times in chapters 1 and 2: for the way to deal with popular audiences, using common notions (1355a28–29); for what syllogism and induction are, to which enthymeme and example correspond (1356b11–13); and for the division of topics, common and special (1358a29–30; see [[topos]]).

**Book II.** The *Topics* is cited for selecting premises on each subject (1396b3–5), for the senses of “rightly” (1398a28–29), for the parts of motion (1399a7–8), and for objections (1402a34–35, 1403a31–33; see [[enstasis]]).

**Book III.** How to answer ambiguous questions and apparent contradictions, and their solutions, “are plain to us from the Topics” (1419a24; see [[erotesis]]).
