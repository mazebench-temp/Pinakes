**Book II.** Aristippus answered his boast with Socrates' example (1398b30–33; see [[aristippus-and-plato]]).

**Book III.** Among the similes is “the one in Plato's Republic, that those who strip the dead are like puppies that bite the stones without touching the thrower” (1406b32–34; *Republic* 469d–e); then the people likened to a shipowner who is strong but a little deaf (1406b35–36; *Republic* 488a–b), and the poets' verses to the young who have bloom without beauty (1406b36–1407a1; *Republic* 601b; see [[eikon]]). Poetic words, which suit emotion, may be used with irony, “as Gorgias did, and as in the Phaedrus” (1408b20), whose speeches on love Socrates delivers in a dithyrambic manner.
