**Book III.** Long periods become a speech in themselves, like a prelude, “so that what Democritus of Chios mocked comes true, jibing at Melanippides, who wrote preludes instead of antistrophes: ‘A man contrives evil for himself who contrives evil for another, and a long prelude is worst for its maker’” (1409b26–29). The jibe parodies Hesiod, who wrote “and an evil plan is worst for its maker” (*Works and Days* 265–266): ἀναβολή, “prelude”, takes the place of βουλή, “plan” (see [[melanippides]] and [[periodos]]).
