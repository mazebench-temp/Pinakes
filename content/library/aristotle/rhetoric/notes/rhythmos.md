**Book III.** Rhythm is one of the three things delivery considers, with magnitude and melody (1403b30–31; see [[delivery]]). Chapter 8 gives the rule: “The form of the style should be neither metrical nor without rhythm” (1408b21–22). Meter seems artificial and makes the hearer wait for the recurrence, as children shout “Cleon!” before the herald has finished (1408b22–26; see [[cleon]]); what has no rhythm is unlimited, and the unlimited is unpleasant; “the number of the form of style is rhythm, of which meters too are segments” (1408b28–29). Of the rhythms, the heroic is dignified and needs a melody not that of speech, the iambus is the speech of the many, the trochee too much like the cordax (1408b32–1409a1; see [[hexameter]], [[iambus]] and [[trochee]]); the paean, used since Thrasymachus, is the only one from which no meter is made, and so it best escapes notice (1409a1–9; see [[paean]] and [[thrasymachus]]). The end of a period should be marked by the rhythm, not by the scribe (1409a19–21), and rhythm is part of what makes style pleasant (1414a28).
