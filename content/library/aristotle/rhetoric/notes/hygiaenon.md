**Book III.** In an exchange suit (ἀντίδοσις), the procedure by which a citizen named for a public service could challenge a richer man to take it on or exchange property, Hygiaenon “accused him of impiety for having written a line urging perjury, ‘My tongue has sworn, but my mind is unsworn’” (1416a30–32; *Hippolytus* 612). Euripides answered that the judgments of the Dionysiac contest should not be brought into the courts (see [[euripides]], [[dramatic-competitions]] and [[diabole]]).
