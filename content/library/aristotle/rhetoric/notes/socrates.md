---
summary: Socrates is Aristotle's stock individual, an example of a sign that is not conclusive, and the author of the saying that it is easy to praise Athenians among Athenians.
heading: In the Rhetoric
grc: ["Σωκρατ*"]
en: [Socrates]
---

No art studies the individual, what is healthy “for Socrates or for Callias”, and rhetoric does not study what is reputable to Socrates or Hippias but to kinds of people (1356b30–35; see [[callias]] and [[hippias-of-elis]]). A sign that is not conclusive: “if someone were to say that it is a sign that the wise are just that Socrates was wise and just” (1357b11–13; see [[semeion]]). And praise must suit its audience, “for as Socrates used to say, it is not hard to praise Athenians among Athenians” (1367b8–9; see [[praising-athenians-among-athenians]]).

**Book II.** He is again a stock individual, as anger is against Callias or Socrates (1382a5–6). His descendants sank into dullness (1390b30–31; see [[cimon]]); comparison is “the Socratic sort” of example (1393b4); his argument on the divine sign (1398a15–17; see [[the-divine-sign-of-socrates]]); his refusal to go to Archelaus (1398a24–26; see [[archelaus]]); Aristippus set his judgment against Plato's boast (1398b30–33; see [[aristippus-and-plato]]); and Theodectes' *Socrates* defends him (1399a8–10; see [[what-sanctuary-has-he-profaned]]).

**Book III.** Socrates said in the funeral speech that it is not hard “to praise Athenians among Athenians, but among Lacedaemonians” (1415b30–32; see [[praising-athenians-among-athenians]]); and his questioning of Meletus, which made him admit that daimones are children of gods, is the model of interrogation (1419a8–12; see [[meletus]] and [[erotesis]]). The Socratic dialogues show character (1417a19–22; see [[socratic-dialogues]]).
