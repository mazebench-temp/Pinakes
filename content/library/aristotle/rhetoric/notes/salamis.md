**Book II.** One praises the Athenians from the sea battle at Salamis (1396a12–13).

**Book III.** “In the funeral speech, that it was fitting that at the tomb of those who died at Salamis Greece should cut her hair, since her freedom was being buried with their virtue” (1411a31–33): a metaphor that sets things before the eyes, with a kind of antithesis (see [[before-the-eyes]]). The words are close to a passage of the funeral speech that goes under Lysias' name (2.60), where they follow the defeat at Aegospotami (405 BCE), not Salamis.
