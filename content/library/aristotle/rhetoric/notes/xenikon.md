**Book III.** Departing from the ordinary makes style seem more dignified, for people feel toward style as toward strangers and fellow citizens, so one should make the language foreign, “for people are admirers of what is far off, and the marvelous is pleasant” (1404b10–12; see [[thaumaston]]). In prose it must be used sparingly, and if one composes well “there will be something unfamiliar, the art can escape notice, and the meaning will be clear” (1404b36). Metaphor has the unfamiliar above all (1405a8–9; see [[metaphora]]); epithets vary the usual and make the style unfamiliar (1406a15; see [[epitheton]]); and the right mixture of the usual and the unfamiliar is part of what makes style pleasant (1414a27–28; see [[excellence-of-diction]]).
