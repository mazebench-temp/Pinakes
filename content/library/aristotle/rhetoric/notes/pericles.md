The greatest part of a great thing is great: “as Pericles, delivering the funeral oration, said that the youth had been taken from the city as if the spring had been taken out of the year” (1365a31–33; see [[the-spring-taken-from-the-year]]).

**Book II.** His descendants sank into dullness (1390b30–31; see [[cimon]]).

**Book III.** Book III gives four more of his sayings. His similes: the Samians “are like children who take the morsel but cry as they take it”, and the Boeotians are like holm oaks, felled by one another (1407a1–6; see [[samians]] and [[boeotians]]). The spring taken out of the year returns as the first of the metaphors by analogy (1411a1–4), and he urged the Athenians to remove Aegina, “the eyesore of the Piraeus” (1411a15–16). In interrogation, he asked Lampon about the rites of the Savior goddess (1419a2–5; see [[lampon]] and [[erotesis]]).
