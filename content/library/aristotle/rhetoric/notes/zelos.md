**Book II.** “If emulation is a kind of pain at the apparent presence of goods that are held in honor and that one might obtain oneself, in those who are like one by nature, not because another has them but because one does not have them oneself (that is why emulation is decent and belongs to the decent, while envying is bad and belongs to the bad; for the one prepares himself through emulation to obtain the goods, the other through envy to keep his neighbor from having them)” (1388a32–37; see [[phthonos]]). Those who think themselves worthy of goods they do not have, the young and the great-souled, are given to emulation, and so are those whose ancestors, kin or city are held in honor (1388b1–10). What is emulated is what is held in honor: the virtues, wealth, beauty, and the powers of generals and orators (1388b10–22). “The opposite of emulation is contempt, and of emulating, despising” (1388b22–23; see [[oligoria]]).
