---
summary: An argument from time: having helped the Thebans against the Phocians, Philip should not be refused passage into Attica.
heading: In the Rhetoric
---

**Book II.** The topic “from considering the time” has two examples: Iphicrates' claim to his statue, and the argument “about the Thebans letting Philip pass through into Attica, that if he had asked before he helped them against the Phocians, they would have promised; it would be absurd, then, if because he let it pass and trusted them, they will not let him through” (1397b32–1398a2; see [[topics-of-enthymemes]] and [[thebans]]). The occasion is probably 339 BCE: after Philip seized Elatea, his envoys asked the Thebans to repay his favors by letting him pass or by joining an invasion of Attica (Demosthenes, *On the Crown* 213), and the Thebans allied themselves with Athens instead.
