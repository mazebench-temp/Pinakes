# Ἀντιγόνης at 1418b32 is Antigone herself.
---

If the written law is against one's case, one may argue that the common law never changes, “hence the words in Sophocles' Antigone; for she argues in her defense that she buried him against Creon's law, but not against the unwritten law” (1375a33–35; see [[not-of-today-or-yesterday]]).

**Book III.** If something is incredible, give the reason, “as Sophocles does; an example is the one from the Antigone, that she cared more for her brother than for a husband or children; for these could come again if they were lost, ‘but with my mother and father gone to Hades, there is no brother who could ever be born’” (1417a29–33; lines 909–912). The guard's first words, “My lord, I will not say that I come in haste” (1415b20; line 223), are a proem that seeks to dispel fear (see [[prooimion]] and [[diegesis]]).
