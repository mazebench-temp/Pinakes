**Book III.** “The foundation of style is speaking good Greek, and this lies in five things” (1407a19–20): connectives (1407a20–30; see [[syndesmos]]); specific words (1407a31–32); avoiding ambiguity, which the seers and Empedocles use to seem to say something (1407a32–b6; see [[croesus]] and [[empedocles]]); the genders, as Protagoras divided them (1407b6–9; see [[gender-of-nouns]] and [[protagoras]]); and number (1407b9–10). Writing should be easy to read and to speak; Heraclitus is hard to punctuate (1407b11–18; see [[heraclitus-of-ephesus]]), and joining to two words one that does not suit both makes for solecism (1407b18–21). Of the two styles a speaker must know, that of debate “is knowing how to speak good Greek” (1413b5–6; see [[styles-of-writing-and-debate]]).
