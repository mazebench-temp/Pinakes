**Book III.** Book III treats λέξις, “style”, after proof and before arrangement, because “it is not enough to have what one should say; one must also say it as one should” (1403b15–16). The poets set style in motion, and the first prose style was poetic, like Gorgias' (1404a20–29; see [[gorgias]]); but the style of prose is different. Chapters 2 to 12 treat its excellence, clarity and appropriateness (see [[excellence-of-diction]] and [[prepon]]), its words, standard, rare, double and metaphorical (see [[kyrion]], [[glotta]], [[compound-word]] and [[metaphora]]), frigidity (see [[psychron]]), the simile (see [[eikon]]), good Greek (see [[hellenizein]]), bulk (see [[onkos]]), rhythm (see [[rhythmos]]), the period (see [[periodos]]), urbane sayings (see [[asteion]]), and the written style and the style of debate (see [[styles-of-writing-and-debate]]). The book's other great division is arrangement (see [[taxis]]). Euripides “was the first to show the way” in composing from ordinary words (1404b25–26; see [[euripides]]). In Book I the word is “wording”: to turn advice into praise one changes the wording (1367b38–1368a8; see [[praise-and-blame]]).
