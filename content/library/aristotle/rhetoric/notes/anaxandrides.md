**Book III.** His iambic line about daughters slow to marry, “My maidens are overdue for their weddings” (1411a19–20), takes ὑπερήμεροι, the word for debtors past the day of payment, and is one of the metaphors by analogy (see [[metaphora]]). His “much-praised line, ‘It is noble to die before doing anything worthy of death’” (1412b17–18) is an urbane saying that uses a word in two senses (see [[asteion]]). And the actor Philemon varied his delivery “in Anaxandrides' Old Men's Madness whenever he said ‘Rhadamanthus and Palamedes’, and with the ‘I’ in the prologue of the Pious” (1413b25–27): repetitions that seem silly in writing but need acting (see [[philemon-the-actor]] and [[delivery]]). The digitized text reads Γεροντομαχίᾳ, “Old Men's Battle”; the title is Γεροντομανία.
