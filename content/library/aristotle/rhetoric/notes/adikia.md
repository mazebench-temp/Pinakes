“Let doing wrong be causing harm voluntarily against the law” (1368b6–7), particular or common (see [[nomos]]). People choose to do wrong from vice and lack of self-control (1368b12–14), and every action has one of seven causes (see [[seven-causes-of-action]]). Chapter 12 shows the state in which people do wrong, when they think they can act unnoticed or unpunished, and whom they wrong; chapter 13 divides wrongs by the law they break and by their victim, one person or the community; chapter 14 gives the marks of a greater wrong. A wrong differs from an error and a misfortune: “wrongs, those that are not unexpected and do come from wickedness” (1374b8–9; see [[epieikes]]).
