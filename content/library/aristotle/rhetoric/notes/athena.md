summary: Athena chose Odysseus, an example of what the wise prefer; Hera's words to her are quoted for the good that costs toil.
heading: In the Rhetoric
---

“As Athena chose Odysseus” (1363a18) opens the list of choices by the wise and good (see [[theseus]]). The words “leave to Priam a thing to boast of” are Hera's to Athena, and Athena's to Odysseus (1363a5–6; see [[a-thing-to-boast-of-for-priam]]).

**Book III.** “Not if she rivaled golden Aphrodite in beauty, and Athena in handiwork” (1413a34): Achilles' refusal of Agamemnon's daughter, a hyperbole (see [[the-sand-and-the-dust]]).
