**Book II.** The topic from induction is illustrated by Alcidamas' argument that all men honor the wise: “The Parians at any rate have honored Archilochus, though he was a slanderer” (1398b11–12; see [[all-men-honor-the-wise]] and [[parians]]).

**Book III.** To avoid the envy of praising oneself and the abuse of blaming others, make someone else speak, “as Archilochus blames: he makes the father speak about his daughter in the iambic poem, ‘Nothing is beyond hope, nothing can be sworn impossible,’ and Charon the carpenter in the iambic poem that begins ‘Not for me the wealth of Gyges’” (1418b27–31; see [[ethos]]). Gyges was the king of Lydia whose gold was proverbial.
