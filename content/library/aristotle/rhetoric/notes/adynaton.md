---
summary: The possible and the impossible are a premise common to all three kinds of speech.
heading: In the Rhetoric
refs: [1359a11-16]
---

“The adviser, the litigant and the epideictic speaker must all have premises about the possible and the impossible, about whether a thing has happened or not, and whether it will be or not” (1359a14–16; see [[topos]]). People do wrong when they think the deed possible for them (1372a5–6); what seems possible and impossible is left for the common topics of Book II (1372a9–11).
