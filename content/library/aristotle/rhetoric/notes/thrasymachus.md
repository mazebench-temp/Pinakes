**Book II.** Among the topics of enthymemes is the one “from the name”, and one of its examples is a joke: “Herodicus said to Thrasymachus, ‘You are always bold in fight’” (1400b20–21), for θρασύμαχος, his name as a common word, means “bold in battle” (see [[herodicus-and-thrasymachus]] and [[topics-of-enthymemes]]).

**Book III.** Delivery has not yet been made an art, though some have tried “to say a little about it, such as Thrasymachus in his Appeals to Pity” (1404a14–15), his book of pitiful commonplaces, the Ἔλεοι. The paean is the rhythm “which speakers have used since Thrasymachus began it, though they could not say what it was” (1409a2–3; see [[rhythmos]]). And among similes that work as metaphors is his likening of Niceratus, beaten by Pratys in a contest of reciting epic, to a Philoctetes bitten by Pratys (1413a7–10; see [[niceratus]] and [[eikon]]).
