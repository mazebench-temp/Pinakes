---
summary: Those whom their enemies do not blame are good, which is why the Corinthians heard Simonides' line as an insult.
heading: In the Rhetoric
grc: ["οὐ μέμφεται τὸ Ἴλιον"]
en: [Ilium does not blame]
---

What even enemies praise is good, “for it is as if everyone already agrees, if even those who have suffered from it do” (1363a11–12), and so the Corinthians took Simonides' line as an insult: not to be blamed by Troy was to have done Troy no harm. The joke is on the topic itself, which can be turned to praise or to blame.
