---
summary: Athena chose Odysseus; his words to the army and the words of Phemius and Eumaeus to him are quoted.
heading: In the Rhetoric
---

Athena's choice of Odysseus shows what the wise prefer (1363a18; see [[athena]]). His line to the army, “It is shameful to stay so long”, shows that what has cost much toil is good (1363a6; see [[shameful-to-stay-long]]). From the Odyssey come Phemius' plea to him, “I am self-taught” (1365a30; see [[i-am-self-taught]]), and Eumaeus' words to him on remembered sorrows (1370b5–6; see [[delight-in-remembered-sorrows]]).

**Book II.** He named himself to the Cyclops, since revenge must be perceived (1380b22–25; see [[say-that-odysseus-blinded-you]]); in Theodectes' *Ajax* Diomedes chooses him as the lesser companion, and he explains to Ajax why, though braver, he does not seem so (1399b29–31, 1400a28–29; see [[ajax-plays]]).

**Book III.** In the proem Odysseus' prayer to come to the Phaeacians “as a friend and one to be pitied” names the two things to aim at (1415b26–28; see [[phaeacians]]). In Sophocles' *Teucer* he argues from kinship that Teucer is Priam's man (1416b1–3; see [[hesione]]), and Diomedes' choice of him can be told in a better or a worse light (1416b12–15; see [[diomedes]] and [[diabole]]).
