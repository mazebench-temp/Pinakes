summary: Virtue is a capacity for providing and preserving goods and for conferring benefits; it is noble, the subject of praise, a part of happiness, and the only good that cannot be misused.
heading: In the Rhetoric
grc: ["ἀρετ*"]
en: [virtue, virtues, excellence, excellences]
---

That rhetoric can be misused is a fault common to all good things “except virtue” (1355b4–5). Virtue is a part of happiness (1360b23), and its full treatment is left for praise (1362a12–14). In chapter 9 virtue and vice, the noble and the shameful, are the targets of praise and blame, and “Virtue is, it seems, a capacity for providing and preserving goods, and a capacity for conferring many great benefits” (1366a36–b1; see [[the-parts-of-virtue]] and [[kalon]]). The excellences of the body are health, beauty, strength, stature and athletic ability (1360b21–22, 1361b3–26; see [[eudaimonia]]).
