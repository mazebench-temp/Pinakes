**Book III.** What is incredible needs a promise of the reason, “as Jocasta in Carcinus' Oedipus keeps promising when the man who is searching for her son questions her; and Sophocles' Haemon” (1417b18–20); Aristotle does not say which of Haemon's speeches he means. And to avoid seeming to speak of oneself, put the words in another's mouth, “as Sophocles makes Haemon speak for Antigone to his father as if others were saying it” (1418b31–32; *Antigone* 688–700; see [[ethos]]).
