**Book III.** “Lycoleon, speaking for Chabrias: ‘not even ashamed before his suppliant, the bronze statue’” (1411b6–7). The statue of Chabrias in the agora, set up after his victory, showed him kneeling with his shield against his knee (Nepos, *Chabrias* 1); at the trial it seems to kneel in supplication for him. The metaphor holds only “for the moment”, but it sets things before the eyes: “while he is in danger the statue is a suppliant, the ‘lifeless thing alive’, the memorial of the city's deeds” (1411b8–10; see [[chabrias]] and [[before-the-eyes]]).
