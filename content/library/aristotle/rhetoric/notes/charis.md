In the courts the jurors, “looking to their own interest and listening to please themselves, they give in to the litigants” (1354b33–34). Among the unwritten matters of justice is “being grateful to a benefactor” (1374a23; see [[nomos]]).

**Book II.** Chapter 7: “Let favor, by which the one who has it is said to be grateful, be a service to one in need, not in return for anything, nor so that the one who serves may get something, but so that the other may” (1385a17–19). It is great when the need is great, or the times critical, or when one is the only or the first to help (1385a19–21), like the man who gave the mat in the Lyceum (1385a25–28; see [[lyceum]]); and one can take it away by showing that the service was for the doer's own sake, by chance, under compulsion, or a repayment (1385a34–1385b10). Gratitude is not owed for good done under compulsion (1397a13–17; see [[gratitude-under-compulsion]]).
