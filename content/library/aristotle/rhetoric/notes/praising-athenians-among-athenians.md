---
summary: Praise must consider before whom it is spoken, and speak of what each audience honors.
heading: In the Rhetoric
grc: ["οὐ χαλεπὸν Ἀθηναίους"]
en: [it is not hard to praise Athenians]
---

“Consider also before whom the praise is given; for as Socrates used to say, it is not hard to praise Athenians among Athenians. One must speak of what is honored among each audience as present, as among Scythians, or Laconians, or philosophers” (1367b7–10; see [[scythians]] and [[laconians]]).

**Book III.** In the epideictic proem the hearer must think himself praised with the subject, “for what Socrates says in the funeral speech is true, that it is not hard to praise Athenians among Athenians, but among Lacedaemonians” (1415b30–32; see [[prooimion]]). Book III names the speech: it is the funeral oration of Plato's *Menexenus* (235d).
