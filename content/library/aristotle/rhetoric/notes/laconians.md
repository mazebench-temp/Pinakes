Good children include daughters of beauty, stature, self-control and industry, “for those whose women are in a bad state, like the Lacedaemonians, are almost half unhappy” (1361a10–11; see [[eudaimonia]]). Praise must speak of what each audience honors, “as among Scythians, or Laconians, or philosophers” (1367b10; see [[scythians]]).

**Book II.** Laconic sayings suit maxims that are not paradoxical but unclear (1394b33–35; see [[gnome]]); it makes no difference whether one speaks about Athenians or Lacedaemonians (1396a23–25); in Alcidamas' induction the Lacedaemonians made Chilon one of their elders and prospered under the laws of Lycurgus (1398b14–18; see [[chilon]] and [[lycurgus-of-sparta]]).

**Book III.** Style shows character when it suits the speaker, “Laconian or Thessalian” (1408a28); Leptines would not see Greece made one-eyed by the loss of the Lacedaemonians (1411a5–6; see [[leptines]]); it is not hard “to praise Athenians among Athenians, but among Lacedaemonians” (1415b31–32; see [[praising-athenians-among-athenians]]); Isocrates accuses “the Lacedaemonians in the Panegyricus” (1418a32); and a Laconian, called to account for his ephorate, gave his reason when asked whether he had not deserved death like his colleagues (1419a31–35; see [[erotesis]]).
