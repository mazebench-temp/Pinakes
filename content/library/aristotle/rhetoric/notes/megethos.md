summary: Greatness is a premise common to all three kinds of speech; the greater good and the greater wrong have their own chapters.
heading: In the Rhetoric
grc: [μέγεθος, μεγέθους, μεγέθει, μεγέθεσι]
en: [greatness, stature, magnitudes]
---

All speakers try to show that something is great or small, so all need premises “about greatness and smallness and the greater and the lesser, both universally and about each thing” (1359a16–23; see [[topos]]). Chapter 7 gives the topics of the greater good (see [[agathos]]), from the exceeding and the exceeded (1363b7–8) to the real and the apparent; chapter 14 those of the greater wrong (see [[adikia]]). Amplification makes things great in praise (see [[auxesis]]). μέγεθος is also stature, an excellence of the body (1360b22, 1361a3).

**Book II.** Greatness and smallness are a common topic, but what is to be said about them was said in the deliberative chapters, and to inquire further about greatness absolutely “is idle talk” (1393a9–19; see [[koina]]).
