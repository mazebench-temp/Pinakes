---
summary: Cleophon attacked Critias with a line of Solon to an ancestor of the same name, as proof that the family had long been licentious.
heading: In the Rhetoric
grc: [Κριτίου, Κριτίαν]
---

“Cleophon used Solon's elegies against Critias, saying that the family had long been licentious; for otherwise Solon would never have written ‘Tell red-haired Critias, for me, to listen to his father’” (1375b31–34). The Critias of the verse is an ancestor, and the argument turns a poet's advice into evidence about a whole family (see [[tell-red-haired-critias]] and [[cleophon-the-demagogue]]).

**Book III.** Well-known deeds need only be recalled, “But if you praise Critias, you need it; for not many know them” (1416b29; see [[diegesis]]). Which Critias is meant is not said.
