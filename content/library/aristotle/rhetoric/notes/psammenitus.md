**Book II.** People do not pity those very close to them, for whom they feel as for themselves, “that is why the son of Amasis, they say, did not weep over his son when he was led to his death, but did weep over his friend begging; for this was pitiable, but that was terrible; for the terrible is different from the pitiable and drives out pity” (1386a18–24; see [[pity]] and [[phobos]]). Aristotle's text names him only as Amasis' son, ὁ Ἀμάσιος; in Herodotus he is Psammenitus (3.14).
