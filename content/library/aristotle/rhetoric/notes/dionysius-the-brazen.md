**Book III.** A metaphor can go wrong in its syllables, if they are not signs of a pleasant sound: “so Dionysius the Brazen calls poetry ‘the scream of Calliope’ in his elegies, because both are sounds” (1405a32–34). The rest of the sentence is corrupt (see [[calliope]] and [[metaphora]]).
