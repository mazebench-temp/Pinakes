---
summary: The lawgiver judges the future and the universal; law must speak universally where the matter holds only for the most part, which is why equity is needed.
heading: In the Rhetoric
---

Laws should decide as much as possible, because “the lawgiver's judgment is not about particulars but about what is future and universal” (1354b4–6). But because the lawgivers “cannot define precisely, but must speak universally where the matter is not universal but holds for the most part” (1374a30–31), the written law sometimes condemns the innocent, and equity corrects it (see [[epieikes]] and [[nomos]]). No art studies the particular, which is infinite and cannot be known (1356b30–33).
