**Book II.** “By fortune I mean good birth, wealth, powers and their opposites, and in general good fortune and bad fortune” (1389a1–2). Good birth (1390b14–31): “good birth is the honor of one's ancestors” (1390b18); “to be well-born is a matter of the excellence of the stock, to be true to one's birth is a matter of not falling away from one's nature” (1390b21–23; see [[gennaios]]); the descendants of Alcibiades and Dionysius, of Cimon, Pericles and Socrates show the decline (1390b27–31; see [[cimon]]). Wealth (1390b32–1391a19): “the character of wealth is the character of a prosperous fool” (1391a13–14), and the newly rich are worse than those rich of old, as in indignation (1387a17–24). Power and good fortune (1391a20–1391b4): the powerful “are not petty wrongdoers but wrongdoers on a grand scale” (1391a28–29); the fortunate “love the gods” (1391b2).
