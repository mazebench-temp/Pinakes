{{quote:84196315-060e-52fe-b967-a64f02edf2c2}}

Then each is defined: chance has an indefinite cause and no purpose; nature an ordered cause within; force acts against the agent's appetite or reasoning; habit is what people do because they have done it often; reasoning acts for what seems advantageous; temper and anger for revenge; appetite for what seems pleasant (1369a32–b18). “So, to sum up, all that people do through themselves is either good or apparently good, or pleasant or apparently pleasant” (1369b18–20), which is why the next chapter treats pleasure (see [[hedone]]).
