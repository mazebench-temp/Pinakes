---
summary: Past troubles are pleasant to remember when what followed was good.
heading: In the Rhetoric
grc: ["ἡδύ τοι σωθέντα"]
en: [It is sweet to remember toils when one is safe]
---

Things remembered are pleasant, “not only those that were pleasant at the time, when they were present, but some that were not, if what came after was noble and good” (1370b1–3), and the line of Euripides comes first, before Homer's lines on remembered sorrows (see [[delight-in-remembered-sorrows]] and [[hedone]]).
