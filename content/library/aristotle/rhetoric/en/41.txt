1403a16	Amplifying and diminishing are not an element of enthymeme;
1403a17	for I call element and topic the same thing; for an element
1403a18	and a topic is that under which many enthymemes fall. Amplifying and
1403a19	diminishing are enthymemes for showing that something is great or small,
1403a20	as there are enthymemes that it is good or bad, or just or unjust,
1403a21	or anything else. All these
1403a22	are what syllogisms and enthymemes are about; so if
1403a23	none of these is a topic of enthymeme, neither is
1403a24	amplifying and diminishing. Nor are enthymemes that solve
1403a25	a kind of enthymeme [different from those that establish]; for it is clear that
1403a26	one solves either by demonstrating or by bringing an objection, and one demonstrates the
1403a27	opposite: for instance, if the other has shown that something happened, this man shows that it did not,
1403a28	and if that it did not happen, this one that it did; so this
1403a29	could not be the difference (for both use the same means;
1403a30	for they bring enthymemes that it is not or that it is); and
1403a31	an objection is not an enthymeme, but, as in
1403a32	the Topics, it is stating some opinion from which it will be clear
1403a33	that the opponent has not reasoned validly or has assumed something false. Since
1403a34	there are three things that must be dealt with concerning speech,
1403a35	about examples, maxims,
1403a36	enthymemes, and in general what concerns thought,
1403b1	where we shall find plenty to say and how we shall solve them, let this be
1403b2	enough; it remains to go through diction and arrangement.
