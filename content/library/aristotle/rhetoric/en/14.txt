1374b24	A wrong is greater the greater the injustice it comes from;
1374b25	that is why the smallest can be the greatest, as in the charge Callistratus brought against Melanopus,
1374b26	that he had cheated the temple builders of three sacred
1374b27	half-obols; with justice it is the opposite. These things
1374b28	follow from what is contained potentially; for one who has stolen three sacred
1374b29	half-obols would do any wrong whatever. Sometimes, then, the greater
1374b30	is judged in this way, sometimes by the harm. And a wrong for which there is no
1374b31	equal punishment, but every one is less. And one for which there is no remedy;
1374b32	for it is hard †and impossible†. And one for which the victim cannot get
1374b33	justice; for it is incurable, since justice and punishment are a kind of remedy.
1374b34	And if the one who suffered and was wronged punished himself severely;
1374b35	for the doer deserves a still greater punishment, as Sophocles,
1374b36	speaking for Euctemon, who had cut his own throat after suffering outrage,
1375a1	said he would not assess the penalty at less than the victim had assessed it for himself.
1375a2	And a wrong that one has done alone, or first, or with few.
1375a3	And to commit the same offense often. And a wrong
1375a4	for which preventives and penalties have been sought and found,
1375a5	as in Argos a man is penalized on whose account a law is made, and those on whose account
1375a6	the prison was built. And the more brutal
1375a7	wrong is greater. And one done with premeditation is more so. And one that
1375a8	hearers fear more than they pity. The rhetorical devices
1375a9	are such as these: that a man has destroyed or violated many things, such as
1375a10	oaths, pledges, assurances, marriage rights; for this is an excess of many
1375a11	wrongs. And doing wrong in the very place where wrongdoers are punished, which is what
1375a12	false witnesses do; for where would they not do wrong,
1375a13	if even in the court? And wrongs that bring the most shame.
1375a14	And if a man wrongs one from whom he has received benefits; for he does more wrong, both because
1375a15	he does harm and because he does not do good. And what goes against the unwritten
1375a16	rules of justice; for it marks a better man to be just without compulsion,
1375a17	and the written rules are binding by necessity, the unwritten
1375a18	are not. In another way, what goes against the written; for one
1375a19	who commits wrongs that are feared and penalized would also commit those that go
1375a20	unpunished. About wrongs, then,
1375a21	greater and lesser, enough has been said.
