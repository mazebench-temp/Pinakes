1392a8	First, then, let us speak about the possible and the impossible. If
1392a9	one of two opposites can be or come to be, the other opposite
1392a10	would seem possible too; for instance, if a man can be cured,
1392a11	he can also fall ill; for the same capacity is of opposites, as opposites.
1392a12	And if one of two like things is possible, so is the other; and if the
1392a13	harder is possible, so is the easier; and if it is possible for something excellent and beautiful
1392a14	to come into being, it is possible for the thing in general; for it is harder
1392a15	for a beautiful house to come into being than a house. And if the beginning of something can
1392a16	come into being, so can the end; for nothing that is impossible comes into being or begins to come into being:
1392a17	for instance, the diagonal's being commensurable could
1392a18	neither begin to come about nor come about. And if the end is possible, so is the beginning;
1392a19	for everything comes into being from a beginning. And if what is later in
1392a20	being or in coming-to-be can come into being, so can what is earlier: for instance,
1392a21	if a man can come into being, so can a child (for that
1392a22	comes first), and if a child, a man (for the child is the beginning).
1392a23	And things that are by nature objects of love or appetite; for no one, for the most part,
1392a24	loves or desires the impossible. And the things of which there are sciences and
1392a25	arts can both be and come into being. And all things whose
1392a26	coming into being has its beginning in what we could compel or
1392a27	persuade; these are those to which we are superior, or whose masters or friends we are.
1392a28	And if the parts are possible, so is the whole; and if the whole is possible,
1392a29	so for the most part are the parts; for if a vamp, a toe-cap and
1392a30	an upper can be made, shoes can
1392a31	be made, and if shoes, then a vamp,
1392a32	a toe-cap and an upper. And if the whole genus
1392b1	is among things that can come into being, so is the species, and if the species, so is
1392b2	the genus: for instance, if a ship can come into being, so can a trireme, and
1392b3	if a trireme, a ship. And if one of two things naturally related to each other is possible,
1392b4	so is the other: for instance, if the double, so the half, and if
1392b5	the half, the double. And if something can come about without art and
1392b6	preparation, all the more can it come about through art
1392b7	and care; whence it has also been said by Agathon,
1392b8	“Indeed, some things are for art to do,
1392b9	while others come to us by necessity and chance.”
1392b10	And if it is possible for those who are worse, weaker and less intelligent,
1392b11	it is more so for their opposites; as
1392b12	Isocrates said it would be strange if Euthynus had learned something
1392b13	and he himself could not discover it. As for the impossible,
1392b14	it is clear that it is to be had from the opposites of what has been said.
1392b15	Whether something has happened must be examined from the following. First, if
1392b16	what is less likely by nature to happen has happened, what is
1392b17	more likely would have happened too. And if what usually happens later has happened, what
1392b18	comes earlier has happened: for instance, if someone has forgotten something, he also once learned it.
1392b19	And if he was able and wished to, he has done it; for all, when
1392b20	they are able and wish to, act; for nothing is in the way. Again, if
1392b21	he wished to and nothing outside prevented him; and if he was able and
1392b22	was angry; and if he was able and desired; for for the most part what
1392b23	people long for, if they are able, they do, the bad through
1392b24	lack of self-control, the decent because they desire decent things. And if
1392b25	he was about to do it, he did it; for it is probable that one who was about to
1392b26	do something did it. And if all the things have happened that naturally come before it or for the sake of
1392b27	it: for instance, if it lightened, it also thundered, and if he attempted it,
1392b28	he also did it. And if the things that naturally happen later, or for the sake of which
1392b29	it happens, have happened, so has what comes earlier and what is for the sake of this:
1392b30	for instance, if it thundered, it also lightened, and if he did it, he attempted it. Of
1392b31	all these, some hold of necessity and some for the most part.
1392b32	As for something's not having happened, it is clear that it comes from the opposites of what has been said.
1393a1	And about what will be, it is clear from the same things; for what is in
1393a2	one's power and wish will be, and what is in one's appetite, anger
1393a3	and calculation together with power; these too, when one is in the impulse to
1393a4	do them or about to, will be; for for the most part what is about to happen
1393a5	happens rather than what is not. And if what naturally comes before has happened:
1393a6	for instance, if it is cloudy, it is probable that it will
1393a7	rain. And if what is for the sake of something has happened, it is probable that this too
1393a8	will happen: for instance, if there is a foundation, there will be a house. About
1393a9	the greatness and smallness of things, and the greater
1393a10	and the lesser, and great and small things in general, it is clear from what
1393a11	we have said before. For in the treatment of deliberative speech we spoke
1393a12	both about the greatness of goods and about the greater
1393a13	and the lesser absolutely; so, since for each kind of speech the
1393a14	end proposed is a good, namely the advantageous, the
1393a15	noble or the just, it is clear that it is through these
1393a16	that all must find their amplifications. To go beyond these
1393a17	and seek further about greatness and superiority absolutely
1393a18	is idle talk; for for practical use particular facts are
1393a19	more decisive than universals.
1393a20	About the possible and the impossible, then, and whether something has happened
1393a21	or not and will be or not, and further about
1393a22	the greatness and smallness of things, let this be enough.
