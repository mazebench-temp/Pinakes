1391a20	Likewise with power, the characters are for the most part
1391a21	clear. Power has some of the same characters as wealth, and some
1391a22	better; those in power are fonder of honor and manlier in character
1391a23	than the rich, because they aim at deeds that their power gives them license
1391a24	to do, and more diligent,
1391a25	because they are in a position of care, being compelled to look after
1391a26	what concerns their power; and dignified rather than heavy;
1391a27	for their rank makes them more dignified, and so they behave with moderation,
1391a28	and dignity is a soft and graceful heaviness; and if
1391a29	they do wrong, they are not petty wrongdoers but wrongdoers on a grand scale.
1391a30	Good fortune has the characters of the parts of what has been mentioned (for to these
1391a31	tend what are thought to be the greatest pieces of good fortune), and besides, in
1391a32	good children and the goods of the body good fortune provides an advantage.
1391b1	People are more arrogant and more thoughtless because of their good fortune,
1391b2	but one excellent character goes with good fortune: they love the gods and
1391b3	stand in a certain relation to the divine, trusting it because of what comes from
1391b4	fortune. About the characters that go with age and fortune, then, enough has been said; for
1391b5	the opposites of those described are clear from the opposites,
1391b6	for instance the character of the poor man, the unfortunate and the powerless.
