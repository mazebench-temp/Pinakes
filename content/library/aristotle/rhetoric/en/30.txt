1390b14	As for the goods that come from fortune, those of them through which
1390b15	people's characters too come to be of certain kinds, let us speak of them
1390b16	next. The character of good birth is that its possessor is fonder
1390b17	of honor; for everyone, when he has something, is used to heaping up more
1390b18	on it, and good birth is the honor of one's ancestors.
1390b19	And it is contemptuous even of those who are like one's own
1390b20	ancestors, because the same things far off are more honored than near at hand,
1390b21	and easier to boast of. To be well-born is a matter of the excellence
1390b22	of the stock, to be true to one's birth is a matter of not falling away from one's
1390b23	nature; which for the most part does not happen to the well-born,
1390b24	but most of them are worthless; for there is a kind of crop in the
1390b25	families of men, as in what the land produces,
1390b26	and sometimes, if the family is good, exceptional men are born in it for some
1390b27	time, and then it gives way again. Gifted families
1390b28	degenerate into more manic characters, like the descendants of Alcibiades
1390b29	and of Dionysius the elder; steady ones into
1390b30	stupidity and sluggishness, like the descendants of
1390b31	Cimon, Pericles and Socrates.
