1406b20	The simile, too, is a metaphor, for the difference is small. When
1406b21	the poet says [of Achilles] “he rushed on like a lion”,
1406b22	it is a simile; when he says “a lion, he rushed on”, it is a metaphor: because
1406b23	both are brave, he transferred the name and called
1406b24	Achilles a lion. The simile is useful in prose too, but seldom,
1406b25	for it is poetic. Similes should be brought in as metaphors are;
1406b26	for they are metaphors, differing in the way described. Examples of
1406b27	similes are the one Androtion made of Idrieus, that he was like
1406b28	puppies let off the chain: they fly at people and bite,
1406b29	and Idrieus, freed from his chains, was savage. And
1406b30	Theodamas likened Archidamus to Euxenus without his
1406b31	geometry; it is by analogy, for Euxenus will be
1406b32	Archidamus with geometry. And the one in Plato's Republic,
1406b33	that those who strip the dead are like puppies
1406b34	that bite the stones without touching the thrower; and
1406b35	the one about the people, that it is like a shipowner who is strong but a little
1406b36	deaf; and the one about the poets' verses, that they are like
1406b37	the young who have bloom without beauty: once the bloom has faded, and once the verses are broken up,
1407a1	they no longer look the same. And Pericles' simile for
1407a2	the Samians, that they are like children
1407a3	who take the morsel but cry as they take it; and his simile
1407a4	for the Boeotians, that they are like holm oaks;
1407a5	for holm oaks are felled by themselves, and the
1407a6	Boeotians by fighting one another. And what Demosthenes
1407a7	said of the people, that it is like passengers on ships
1407a8	who are seasick. And Democrates likened the orators to
1407a9	nurses who swallow the morsel themselves and smear the children's lips
1407a10	with the spittle. And Antisthenes likened lean Cephisodotus
1407a11	to frankincense, because he gives pleasure as he wastes away. All
1407a12	of these can be spoken either as similes or as metaphors,
1407a13	so that it is clear that those which are well liked when spoken as metaphors
1407a14	will also be similes, and that similes are metaphors
1407a15	that need a word more. A metaphor from
1407a16	analogy should always be reciprocal and apply also to the other
1407a17	of the two things of the same genus: if the cup is “the shield of Dionysus”,
1407a18	the shield may fittingly be called “the cup of Ares”.
