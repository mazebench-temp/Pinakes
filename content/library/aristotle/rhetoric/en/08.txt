1365b21	The greatest and most decisive of all things for being able
1365b22	to persuade and to advise well is to grasp all the
1365b23	constitutions and to distinguish the characters, customs and
1365b24	interests of each. For all people are persuaded by what is advantageous,
1365b25	and what preserves the constitution is advantageous. Further, authoritative
1365b26	is the pronouncement of the authority, and the authorities are divided according to
1365b27	the constitutions; for there are as many authorities as there are
1365b28	constitutions. There are four constitutions: democracy, oligarchy,
1365b29	aristocracy, monarchy; so that the authority and
1365b30	what judges would be some part of these, or the whole.
1365b31	Democracy is a constitution in which offices are distributed
1365b32	by lot; oligarchy, one in which those
1365b33	with property qualifications hold them; aristocracy, one in which they are held according to
1365b34	education; and by education I mean the education laid down
1365b35	by the law. For in an aristocracy those who have kept to the customs
1365b36	rule. These must seem to be the best, and from this it has taken its
1365b37	name. Monarchy is, as its name says, the constitution in which one man has authority over all;
1366a1	of monarchies, the one under some order is kingship, the
1366a2	unlimited one tyranny. The end of each constitution
1366a3	must not be overlooked, for people choose what leads to
1366a4	the end. The end of democracy is freedom,
1366a5	of oligarchy wealth, of aristocracy what concerns education
1366a6	and the customs, of tyranny protection. It is clear, then, that we must
1366a7	distinguish the characters, customs and interests that lead to the end of each,
1366a8	since people choose by referring things to it. And since
1366a9	proofs come about not only through demonstrative argument but
1366a10	also through argument that shows character (for we believe the speaker because he
1366a11	seems to be of a certain kind, that is, if he seems good, or well disposed,
1366a12	or both), we should need to know the characters of each constitution;
1366a13	for the character of each is bound to be the most persuasive to each.
1366a14	These will be grasped through the same means; for
1366a15	characters are revealed by choice, and choice
1366a16	is referred to the end. What, then, those who exhort should aim at,
1366a17	as things that will be or are, and from what
1366a18	proofs about the advantageous should be drawn,
1366a19	and further, about the characters and customs of the constitutions,
1366a20	by what means and how we shall be well supplied, so far as
1366a21	was suited to the present occasion, has been said;
1366a22	for these matters are worked out precisely in the Politics.
