1390b32	The characters that go with wealth lie on the surface for all to see;
1390b33	the rich are given to outrage and arrogant, being affected by the possession of
1390b34	wealth (for they are disposed as though they had all good things;
1391a1	wealth is a kind of standard of the worth of other things, so that
1391a2	everything seems to be for sale for it); and they are luxurious and swaggering:
1391a3	luxurious because of their way of life and the display of their
1391a4	prosperity, swaggering and vulgar because everyone is used to
1391a5	dancing attendance on what they love and admire,
1391a6	and because they think that others emulate what they emulate themselves. At the same time
1391a7	it is reasonable that this should happen to them (for many are those who need
1391a8	those who have; whence the saying of Simonides about the wise
1391a9	and the rich, when Hiero's wife asked him whether
1391a10	it was better to become rich or wise: “Rich,” he said;
1391a11	for he said he saw the wise spending their time at the doors
1391a12	of the rich); and they think they are worthy to rule; for they think
1391a13	they have what makes one worthy to rule. In sum, the character
1391a14	of wealth is the character of a prosperous fool. The characters
1391a15	of the newly rich and those rich of old differ in that
1391a16	the newly rich have all the vices more, and in a worse form (for
1391a17	being newly rich is a sort of lack of education in wealth);
1391a18	and the wrongs they commit are not malicious, but some
1391a19	outrageous and others from lack of self-control, such as assault and adultery.
