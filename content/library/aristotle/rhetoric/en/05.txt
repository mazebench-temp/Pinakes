1360b4	Both for each person privately and for all in common there is, roughly, a target
1360b5	at which they aim in what they choose and what they avoid;
1360b6	and this, to put it in a word, is happiness and
1360b7	its parts. So, by way of illustration, let us grasp what
1360b8	happiness is, speaking generally, and of what its parts
1360b9	consist; for it is about happiness, and what contributes to it,
1360b10	and what is opposed to it, that all exhortations and dissuasions
1360b11	are made: what produces it or one of its parts,
1360b12	or makes a part greater instead of smaller, we should do; what
1360b13	destroys or hinders it, or produces its opposites, we should not do.
1360b14	Let happiness, then, be doing well together with virtue, or self-sufficiency
1360b15	of life, or the pleasantest life with security, or abundance
1360b16	of possessions and bodies, with the power to protect
1360b17	and use them; for nearly everyone agrees that happiness
1360b18	is one or more of these. If, then,
1360b19	happiness is something of this kind, its parts must be
1360b20	good birth, many friends, good friends, wealth, good children,
1360b21	many children, a good old age; also the excellences of the body (such as health,
1360b22	beauty, strength, stature, athletic ability), reputation, honor,
1360b23	good fortune, virtue [or else its parts, practical wisdom, courage,
1360b24	justice, self-control]; for in this way a man would be most self-sufficient,
1360b25	if he had both the goods within himself and the goods outside,
1360b26	since there are no others besides these. Within himself are the goods of
1360b27	the soul and of the body; outside, good birth, friends,
1360b28	money and honor; and we think it fitting that he should also have
1360b29	power and fortune, for so his life is most secure. Let us grasp,
1360b30	then, in the same way what each of these is. Good birth,
1360b31	for a nation or a city, is to be sprung from the soil or
1360b32	ancient, and to have had distinguished first leaders,
1360b33	and many descendants distinguished for things
1360b34	that are envied. For an individual, good birth is either on the men's or on the
1360b35	women's side, and legitimacy on both; and, as with a city,
1360b36	that the founders of the family were notable for virtue or wealth or
1360b37	something else that is honored, and that many distinguished people have come from the
1360b38	family, men and women, young and old.
1361a1	Good children and many children are not hard to make out. For the community it means
1361a2	a youth that is numerous and good, good in excellence of body, such as
1361a3	stature, beauty, strength, athletic ability; while self-control and
1361a4	courage are the virtues of soul in a young man. For a household it means that one's own children
1361a5	are many and of this kind, both female and male. The
1361a6	excellence of women in body is beauty and stature, in soul,
1361a7	self-control and industry without servility. Likewise,
1361a8	privately and in common, for men and for women, one must
1361a9	seek that each of these qualities be present; for those whose women
1361a10	are in a bad state, like the Lacedaemonians, are almost
1361a11	half unhappy. The parts of wealth are
1361a12	plenty of coin and land, the possession of estates outstanding in number
1361a13	and size and beauty, and also the possession of furniture,
1361a14	slaves and livestock outstanding in number and
1361a15	beauty, all of these one's own, secure,
1361a16	fit for a free man, and useful. Useful above all are
1361a17	the productive possessions; fit for a free man, those for enjoyment (by productive
1361a18	I mean those that bring income; by those for enjoyment, those from which nothing
1361a19	worth mentioning comes besides their use). The definition of security
1361a20	is to possess things in such a place and such a way that their use
1361a21	is in one's own power; of their being one's own or not, whether it is in one's power
1361a22	to alienate them; by alienation I mean giving and selling. In general,
1361a23	being rich lies in using rather than in possessing;
1361a24	for wealth is the activity of such things, their use.
1361a25	Good reputation is being thought by everyone to be a person of worth,
1361a26	or to have something that everyone desires, or
1361a27	most people, or the good, or the wise. Honor is
1361a28	a sign of a reputation for doing good; those who are rightly honored,
1361a29	and most of all, are those who have done good, though honor is also given
1361a30	to one who is able to do good. A benefaction relates either to safety
1361a31	and whatever causes existence, or to wealth, or to some other
1361a32	good not easily got, either at all, or in that place, or at that time;
1361a33	for many receive honor for what seem small things, but the
1361a34	places and the occasions are the reason. The parts of honor are sacrifices, memorials in
1361a35	verse and without verse, privileges, grants of land, front seats, tombs,
1361a36	statues, maintenance at public expense, barbarian honors such as prostrations
1361a37	and making way, and the gifts that each people prizes. For
1361a38	a gift is both the giving of a possession and a sign of honor; that is why
1361a39	both lovers of money and lovers of honor desire gifts:
1361b1	a gift has what both of them need; it is a possession, which
1361b2	lovers of money desire, and it carries honor, which lovers of honor desire.
1361b3	The excellence of the body is health, and health such that people are free of disease
1361b4	while using their bodies; for many are healthy as
1361b5	Herodicus is said to have been, and no one would call them happy for their health,
1361b6	because they abstain from all, or most, of what human beings do.
1361b7	Beauty differs with each age. A young man's
1361b8	beauty is to have a body fit for exertions, both
1361b9	of running and of strength, while being pleasant to look at;
1361b10	that is why pentathletes are the most beautiful, since they are built for strength
1361b11	and speed together. A man in his prime: fit for the exertions
1361b12	of war, and seeming pleasant to look at but formidable;
1361b13	an old man: equal to the exertions he cannot avoid,
1361b14	and not painful to see, because he has none of the blemishes of old age.
1361b15	Strength is the power to move another as one wishes,
1361b16	and one must move another by pulling or pushing or lifting
1361b17	or pressing down or squeezing, so that the strong man is strong in all
1361b18	or some of these. The excellence of stature is to
1361b19	exceed most people in height, depth and breadth by just so
1361b20	much as not to make one's movements slower because of the
1361b21	excess. Athletic excellence of body is made up of stature
1361b22	and strength and speed (for the swift man too is strong):
1361b23	one who can throw his legs out in a certain way and move them fast
1361b24	and far is a runner; one who can squeeze and hold down, a wrestler;
1361b25	one who can drive another back with a blow, a boxer; one who can do both,
1361b26	a pancratiast; and one who can do all, a pentathlete. A good old age is
1361b27	old age that comes slowly and without pain; for if a man ages quickly
1361b28	he does not have a good old age, nor if slowly but with pain. It comes both
1361b29	from the excellences of the body and from fortune; for one who is not free of disease
1361b30	or strong will not be free of suffering and pain, and
1361b31	he would not last long without good fortune. There is also
1361b32	another capacity for long life, apart from strength and health;
1361b33	for many live long without the excellences of the body; but
1361b34	minute precision about these things is of no use for the present purpose.
1361b35	Many friends and good friends are not hard to make out once a friend is defined:
1361b36	a friend is one who, whatever he thinks good
1361b37	for another, is active in doing it for the other's sake. One who has many
1361b38	such friends has many friends; one whose friends are also decent men
1361b39	has good friends. Good fortune is the coming and the presence of the goods of which fortune is the cause,
1362a1	either all of them, or most, or the greatest.
1362a2	Fortune is the cause of some things that the arts also produce, and of many
1362a3	that owe nothing to art, such as those that nature causes (though fortune can also
1362a4	work against nature): for art is a cause of health, but of beauty
1362a5	and stature nature is. In general, the goods that come from fortune
1362a6	are those that are envied. Fortune is also
1362a7	the cause of goods that defy reckoning: for instance, if the other
1362a8	brothers are ugly and one is handsome, or the others did not see the
1362a9	treasure and one found it, or if the arrow struck the man next to someone
1362a10	and not him, or if he alone did not go to a place he always visited, while the
1362a11	others, going there for once, were killed; for all such things
1362a12	seem to be strokes of good fortune. As for virtue, since
1362a13	its most fitting place is the treatment of praise, we shall
1362a14	define it when we come to speak about praise.
