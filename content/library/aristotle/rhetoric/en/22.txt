1385a16	To whom people are grateful, and for what, and in what state they themselves are,
1385a17	will be clear once we have defined favor. Let favor, by which the
1385a18	one who has it is said to be grateful, be a service to one in need, not in return for anything,
1385a19	nor so that the one who serves may get something, but so that the other may; and it is great
1385a20	if the need is great, or the things are great and hard, or the times
1385a21	critical, or if one is the only one, or the first, or the chief one to serve. Needs are
1385a22	longings, and above all those accompanied by pain at what is not happening.
1385a23	Such are the appetites, for instance love, and those that arise in the
1385a24	body's afflictions and in dangers; for one in danger
1385a25	desires, and so does one in pain. That is why those who stand by people in poverty
1385a26	and exile, even if they do them small services, because of the greatness of the need
1385a27	and the critical time, have done a favor, like the man in the Lyceum who gave the
1385a28	mat. The service, then, must be above all for those same needs,
1385a29	or if not, for equal or greater ones. So, since it is clear to whom
1385a30	and for what a favor is done, and in what state people are, it is clear
1385a31	that one must prepare the case from these: showing that the one party
1385a32	either is or has been in such pain and
1385a33	need, and that the other has rendered such a service in such a need
1385a34	or is rendering it. It is clear too from what sources
1385a35	one can take away the favor and make people out to be ungracious:
1385b1	either by saying that they serve or served for their own sake (and this,
1385b2	we said, was not a favor), or that it happened by chance, or that they
1385b3	were compelled, or that they repaid rather than gave, whether
1385b4	knowingly or not; for in both cases it was in return for something, so that
1385b5	not even so would it be a favor. And one must examine it under all the categories;
1385b6	for a favor is one because it is this thing, or so much, or of such a kind, or
1385b7	at this time, or in this place. A sign is if they did not render a lesser service, and if
1385b8	they rendered the same, or equal or greater services to our enemies; for clearly these too were not
1385b9	for our sake. Or if one knowingly gave what was worthless; for no one admits
1385b10	to needing what is worthless.
