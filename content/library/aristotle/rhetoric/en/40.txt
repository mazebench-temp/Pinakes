1402a29	About enthymemes, then, both real and apparent,
1402a30	enough has been said; next after what has been said we must speak about solution.
1402a31	One can solve an argument either by a counter-syllogism or by bringing an objection.
1402a32	Counter-syllogisms, clearly, can be made from the same topics;
1402a33	for syllogisms are drawn from reputable opinions,
1402a34	and many things that are believed are contrary to each other. Objections
1402a35	are brought, as in the Topics too, in four ways: either from the thing itself,
1402a36	or from the like, or from the opposite, or from previous judgments. By
1402a37	“from the thing itself” I mean, for instance, if the enthymeme were about love, that it is good,
1402b1	the objection would be twofold: either by saying in general that every
1402b2	want is bad, or in particular that there would be no talk of Caunian
1402b3	love if there were not wicked loves too. From the
1402b4	opposite an objection is brought, for instance, if the enthymeme were
1402b5	that the good man does good to all his friends, ⟨by saying⟩
1402b6	that neither does the wicked man do harm to all. From the like: for instance, if
1402b7	the enthymeme were that those who have been ill treated always hate, by saying
1402b8	that neither do those who have been well treated always love. Judgments
1402b9	are those from well-known men: for instance, if someone stated the enthymeme
1402b10	that one should pardon the drunk, since they err
1402b11	in ignorance, the objection is that Pittacus, then, is not to be praised; for he would not
1402b12	have laid down greater penalties if someone does wrong when drunk.
1402b13	Since enthymemes are drawn from four sources, and these four
1402b14	are probability, example, tekmērion and sign, and
1402b15	enthymemes drawn from what is, or seems to be, so for the most part
1402b16	are from probabilities, those through induction from the
1402b17	like, either one or more, when one takes the universal and then reasons
1402b18	to the particulars, are through example, those through what is necessary
1402b19	and ⟨always⟩ so are through a tekmērion, and those through the universal [or]
1402b20	the particular, whether it is so or not, are through signs; and since the
1402b21	probable is not what is always so but what is so for the most part: it is clear that
1402b22	enthymemes of this kind can always be solved by bringing an objection,
1402b23	but the solution is apparent and not always true; for the objector solves it
1402b24	not by showing that it is not probable but that it is not necessary; that is why
1402b25	by this fallacy one can always gain the advantage in defending rather than in accusing;
1402b26	for since the accuser demonstrates through probabilities,
1402b27	and it is not the same to solve an argument by showing that it is not probable or
1402b28	that it is not necessary, and what holds for the most part is always open to objection
1402b29	(for otherwise it would not be probable but always and necessary), the judge
1402b30	thinks, if it is solved in this way, either that it is not probable or that it is not for him to judge it,
1402b31	reasoning falsely, as we said (for he must judge not only from
1402b32	what is necessary, but also from what is probable;
1402b33	for this is judging to the best of one's judgment); it is not enough, then,
1402b34	to solve it by showing that it is not necessary; one must solve it by showing that it is not probable.
1402b35	This will happen if the objection is more a matter of what
1402b36	holds for the most part. It can be so in two ways, either by
1402b37	time or by the facts, and most decisively if by both;
1403a1	for if things are so ⟨in more cases and⟩ more often, this is more
1403a2	probable. Signs, too, and enthymemes stated through a sign can be
1403a3	solved, even if they are true, as was said in the first part;
1403a4	for that every sign is invalid is clear to us from the
1403a5	Analytics. Against arguments from examples the solution is the same
1403a6	as against probabilities; for if we have ⟨one⟩ thing
1403a7	that is not so, it has been solved, because it is not necessary, even if
1403a8	more things, or more often, are otherwise; and if more and
1403a9	more often are so, one must fight it by saying that the present case is not
1403a10	like, or not alike in manner, or has at least some difference. Tekmēria
1403a11	and enthymemes from tekmēria cannot be solved as
1403a12	invalid (this too is clear to us from the Analytics);
1403a13	it remains to show that what is stated is not so. But if
1403a14	it is clear both that it is so and that it is a tekmērion, it then becomes insoluble;
1403a15	for everything becomes a demonstration, now evident.
