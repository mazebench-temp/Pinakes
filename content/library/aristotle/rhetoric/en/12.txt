1372a4	These, then, are the things for the sake of which people do wrong; in what state
1372a5	they do it, and to whom, let us now say. They do wrong themselves when they think
1372a6	that the thing can be done, and done by them, and then
1372a7	that they will go unnoticed when they have done it, or, if noticed, will not pay the penalty, or
1372a8	will pay it, but the penalty will be less than the gain to themselves
1372a9	or to those they care for. What sorts of things seem possible and what
1372a10	impossible will be stated later (for these are common
1372a11	to all kinds of speech). Those who think themselves most able
1372a12	to do wrong with impunity are those able to speak, those practiced in affairs, and
1372a13	those experienced in many lawsuits, and those who have many friends or are
1372a14	rich. They think they can do it most of all if they themselves have the qualities mentioned,
1372a15	and if not, if they have such
1372a16	friends or servants or partners; for through these they can
1372a17	act, and go unnoticed, and not pay the penalty. And if they are friends
1372a18	of those they wrong or of the judges; for friends
1372a19	are off their guard against being wronged, and are reconciled
1372a20	before taking proceedings; and judges favor those they are friends with,
1372a21	and either acquit them altogether or impose small penalties. Likely to go unnoticed are
1372a22	those whose nature is at odds with the charges: the weak in cases of assault,
1372a23	the poor and ugly man in cases of adultery; and things done too much in
1372a24	the open and before everyone's eyes; for no one guards against them, because no one would
1372a25	think of them at all. And crimes so great and of such a kind that no one would think them possible;
1372a26	for these too are unguarded against: everyone guards against the usual wrongs, as
1372a27	against the usual diseases, but against a disease no one has ever
1372a28	had, no one takes precautions. And those who have no enemy or
1372a29	many enemies: the former think they will go unnoticed because no one is on guard,
1372a30	the latter go unnoticed because they would not seem likely to attack
1372a31	people on their guard, and because they have the defense that they would never have attempted it.
1372a32	And those who have means of concealment, in ways or places, or ready means
1372a33	of disposal. And those who, if not unnoticed, can thrust the trial aside or
1372a34	put it off, or corrupt the judges. And those who, if a
1372a35	penalty is imposed, can avoid paying it or put it off for a long time; or if
1372a36	through poverty a man has nothing to lose. And those whose gains
1372a37	are clear or great or near, and the penalties small or unclear or far off.
1372b1	And one for whom there is no punishment equal to the benefit, as seems true of
1372b2	tyranny. And those for whom wrongs bring gains, and the
1372b3	penalties only reproaches. And those for whom, on the contrary, the wrongs
1372b4	bring some praise, as when it happened that one avenged at the same time
1372b5	a father or mother, as with Zeno, while the penalties fall on
1372b6	money or exile or something of the kind; for people do wrong for both reasons
1372b7	and in both states, though not the same people, but people opposite
1372b8	in character. And those who have often gone unnoticed or not been
1372b9	punished, and those who have often failed (for there are some
1372b10	in such matters too, as in war, who are the kind
1372b11	to renew the fight). And those for whom the pleasure is immediate and the pain
1372b12	later, or the gain immediate and the penalty later; for those without self-control
1372b13	are such, and lack of self-control concerns everything people long for. And
1372b14	those for whom, on the contrary, the pain or the penalty is immediate, and the pleasure
1372b15	and the benefit come later and last longer; for the self-controlled
1372b16	and the wiser pursue such things. And those who may be able
1372b17	to seem to have acted by chance or by necessity or by nature or by habit,
1372b18	and in general to have erred rather than done wrong. And those who can
1372b19	count on equity. And all who are in want; and there are two ways of being in want:
1372b20	either of necessities, like the poor, or of superfluities, like
1372b21	the rich. And those with very high reputations and those with very
1372b22	low ones: the former as not likely to be suspected, the latter as no worse
1372b23	off in reputation. These, then, are the states in which people set about doing wrong;
1372b24	and they wrong such people and in such matters: those who have what
1372b25	the wrongdoers themselves lack, whether for necessities, or for superiority, or for enjoyment;
1372b26	and both those far off and those near: from the near, the taking
1372b27	is quick; from the far, revenge is slow, as with those who plunder the
1372b28	Carthaginians. And those who are not cautious or watchful but
1372b29	trusting, for it is easy to escape the notice of them all. And the easygoing,
1372b30	for prosecuting takes a careful man. And the bashful, for they
1372b31	will not fight over gain. And those wronged by many
1372b32	who have not prosecuted, as being, in the words of the proverb,
1372b33	Mysian plunder. And those never wronged before and those often wronged;
1372b34	for both are off guard, the former as if it would never happen, the latter as if it could not
1372b35	happen again. And those who have been slandered or are easily slandered; for such people
1372b36	neither choose to go to court, fearing the judges,
1372b37	nor can they persuade, being hated and envied.
1373a1	And those against whom they have a pretext, because their ancestors or they themselves or their friends
1373a2	have done harm, or meant to, to the wrongdoers themselves, or their ancestors, or those
1373a3	they care for; for as the proverb says, wickedness needs only a pretext.
1373a4	And both enemies and friends: the latter it is easy,
1373a5	the former pleasant, to wrong. And the friendless, and those not clever at speaking
1373a6	or acting; for they either do not try to prosecute, or come to terms,
1373a7	or achieve nothing. And those for whom it does not pay to waste time
1373a8	waiting for a trial or for payment, such as foreigners and those who work their own land;
1373a9	for they settle for little and are easily put off.
1373a10	And those who have done many wrongs, or such wrongs as they now suffer; for it seems
1373a11	almost no wrong when someone is wronged
1373a12	in the way he himself used to wrong others: I mean, for instance, if
1373a13	someone assaults a man who habitually commits outrage. And those who have done
1373a14	harm, or wished to, or wish to, or will; for this has
1373a15	both the pleasant and the noble, and seems almost no
1373a16	wrong. And those whose wronging will please friends, or men they admire, or
1373a17	those they love, or their masters, or in general those on whom their lives depend. And those
1373a18	from whom they can count on equity. And those against whom they have made complaints
1373a19	and with whom they have already broken, as Callippus did in the affair of
1373a20	Dion; for such things too seem almost no wrong.
1373a21	And those who are going to be wronged by others if not by themselves, there being no longer
1373a22	time to deliberate, as Aenesidemus is said
1373a23	to have sent the cottabus prize to Gelon, who had enslaved a city, because he had got there first,
1373a24	since he himself had meant to. And those whom they can wrong
1373a25	and then do many just things, as if easily making amends; as
1373a26	Jason the Thessalian said one must do some wrongs in order to be able
1373a27	to do many just things too. And the wrongs that all or many are in the habit
1373a28	of doing; for they think they will be pardoned. And things
1373a29	easy to hide: such are things quickly consumed, like
1373a30	food, or things easily altered in shape or color or
1373a31	mixture, or things easy to make vanish in many places: such are
1373a32	things easily carried and hidden in small places. And things
1373a33	of which the wrongdoer already had many indistinguishable and similar ones. And
1373a34	wrongs that the victims are ashamed to speak of, such as outrages against
1373a35	the women of their house, or against themselves or their sons. And wrongs
1373a36	for which one who prosecuted would seem litigious: such are small ones
1373a37	and those that are pardonable. In what state, then, people do wrong,
1373a38	and what wrongs, to whom, and why, is roughly this.
