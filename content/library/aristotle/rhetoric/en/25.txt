1387b22	It is clear too at what people feel envy, and whom they envy, and in what state,
1387b23	if envy is a kind of pain at apparent good fortune in the
1387b24	goods mentioned, toward those like oneself, not in order that one may get something oneself, but
1387b25	because of them; for those will feel envy who have some
1387b26	who are like them, or appear to be; I mean like in birth, in
1387b27	kinship, in age, in states, in reputation, in
1387b28	possessions. And those who lack only a little of having everything (that is why
1387b29	those who do great things and the fortunate are envious);
1387b30	for they think everyone is carrying off what is theirs. And those honored
1387b31	exceptionally for something, and above all for wisdom or happiness. And
1387b32	those fond of honor are more envious than those indifferent to it. And those who fancy themselves wise;
1387b33	for they are fond of honor for wisdom. And in general those fond of reputation in something
1387b34	are envious about it. And the small-souled; for everything seems great
1387b35	to them. As for the things at which people feel envy, the goods have been mentioned;
1388a1	for nearly all the deeds or possessions in which people seek reputation and honor,
1388a2	and for which they long for reputation, and all the gifts of fortune,
1388a3	are objects of envy, and above all those that people either long for themselves or
1388a4	think they ought to have, or in the possession of which they are a little superior
1388a5	or a little inferior. It is clear too whom people envy;
1388a6	it has been said at the same time; for people envy those near them in time,
1388a7	place, age and reputation; whence it has been said,
1388a8	“for kinship knows how to envy too.” And those with
1388a9	whom they compete for honor; for they compete with those
1388a10	mentioned, but with those ten thousand years off, or with those
1388a11	yet to be, or the dead, no one does, nor with those at the Pillars of Heracles.
1388a12	Nor with those they think they fall far short of, in their own eyes or in
1388a13	others', nor with those they far surpass; and it is the same toward
1388a14	these and about such things. And since people compete for honor with their rivals
1388a15	and rivals in love and in general those who aim at the same things,
1388a16	they must envy these most of all; that is why
1388a17	it has been said, “potter against potter too.” And those whose possessions
1388a18	or successes are a reproach to them (these too are
1388a19	those near and like them); for clearly it is through their own fault
1388a20	that they do not get the good, so that this pains them and produces
1388a21	envy. And those who have, or have acquired, what was fitting for them,
1388a22	or what they once had acquired; that is why older men envy younger ones, and
1388a23	those who have spent much on something envy those who spent little on it. And those who got something
1388a24	quickly are envied by those who got it with difficulty or not at all. It is clear
1388a25	too at what such people rejoice, and at whom, and in what
1388a26	state; for as they are pained in one state, so in that state they will be pleased at the
1388a27	opposites. So if the hearers themselves are brought into
1388a28	that state, and those who claim to be pitied or to obtain some good
1388a29	are such as described, clearly they will not obtain pity
1388a30	from those who decide.
