1391b7	Since the use of persuasive speeches is directed toward judgment (for about
1391b8	what we know and have judged there is no further need of speech), and there is judgment
1391b9	both if one uses speech to exhort or dissuade a single person,
1391b10	as those do who admonish or persuade (for the single person is no less
1391b11	a judge; for the one who must be persuaded is, simply speaking,
1391b12	the judge), and if one speaks against opponents or against a proposition,
1391b13	alike (for one must use speech and demolish
1391b14	the opposing arguments, against which one makes one's speech as against an opponent),
1391b15	and likewise in epideictic speeches (for the speech is composed for the
1391b16	spectator as for a judge; though in general only he is a judge in the full sense
1391b17	who decides the questions at issue in political contests; for both
1391b18	the points in dispute are examined, how they stand, and the matters on which people deliberate);
1391b19	and since the characters of the constitutions were discussed earlier, in the treatment of
1391b20	deliberative speech—it would be determined how, and
1391b21	by what means, one should make speeches show character. And since
1391b22	for each kind of speech the end was different, and for
1391b23	all of them the opinions and premises have been taken
1391b24	from which people draw their proofs when advising,
1391b25	displaying and disputing, and further those from which it is possible to make
1391b26	speeches show character, and these have been defined, it remains
1391b27	for us to go through the common topics. For all must
1391b28	make use in their speeches of the topic of the possible and impossible, and
1391b29	try to show, some that something will be, others that it has been.
1391b30	Further, ⟨the topic⟩ of greatness is common to all speeches;
1391b31	for all use diminishing and amplifying, whether advising or
1391b32	praising or blaming, accusing or defending.
1392a1	When these have been determined, let us try to speak about enthymemes in general,
1392a2	if we have anything to say, and about examples,
1392a3	so that by adding what remains we may complete our original plan.
1392a4	Of the common topics, amplification is most appropriate to
1392a5	epideictic speeches, as has been said, what has happened
1392a6	to judicial ones (for the judgment is about this),
1392a7	the possible and the future to deliberative ones.
