summary: Tragedy accomplishes “through pity and fear the catharsis of such feelings”; the word appears once more in the treatise, of the rite by which Orestes is saved in the Iphigenia.
grc: [κάθαρσιν, καθάρσεως]
en: [catharsis, purification]
---

The word occurs only twice in the *Poetics*. The first is the last clause of the definition of tragedy:

{{quote:2dd7c34a-e65c-5e12-ad2d-6ff86d3e0014}}

Aristotle does not explain it in what survives, though the *Politics* says he would (see above). The second is ritual. In his outline of Euripides' *Iphigenia among the Taurians* the episodes must belong to the story, “as in the case of Orestes the madness through which he was caught, and the rescue through the purification” (1455b14–15): Iphigenia saves her brother and herself by pretending that Orestes, a matricide, and the image of the goddess he has touched must be cleansed in the sea (see [[iphigenia-among-the-taurians]]). Those who read catharsis as a ritual term point to this passage; those who read it as a medical one point to the *Politics*.
