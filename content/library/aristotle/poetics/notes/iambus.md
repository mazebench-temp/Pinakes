---
summary: The iambic meter was named from lampooning; lampoon gave way to comedy; and iambic poets write about particular persons, where comedy uses general plots.
grc: [ἰάμβιζον, ἰάμβων, ἰαμβικῆς, ἰαμβοποι*]
en: [lampoons, lampooned, iambic poets, iambic form]
---

The iambic meter came into the Margites and poems like it, “which is why it is still called iambic today, because it was in this meter that they lampooned (iambizon) one another”, and so of the old poets “some became poets of heroic verse, others of lampoons” (1448b30–34; see [[iambic-trimeter]] and [[margites]]). When comedy appeared, “some became makers of comedies instead of lampoons” (1449a4), and Crates was the first at Athens to give up “the iambic form” for plots of a general kind (1449b7–8; see [[crates]]). The iambic poets “write about the particular individual”, unlike the comic (1451b14–15). Euclides the elder lampooned the epic lengthenings “in the very diction” (1458b9; see [[euclides-parodies]]).
