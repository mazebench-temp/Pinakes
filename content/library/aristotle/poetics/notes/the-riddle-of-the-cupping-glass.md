---
summary: Aristotle's example of a riddle, diction made wholly of metaphors.
grc: [ἄνδρʼ εἶδον πυρὶ χαλκὸν, ἐπʼ ἀνέρι κολλήσαντα]
en: [I saw a man who with fire had welded bronze, upon a man]
---

Diction made entirely of unusual words will be either a riddle or barbarism: a riddle if it is made of metaphors, barbarism if of rare words (1458a23–26; see [[barbarismos]]). “For this is the form of a riddle: to say things that are real by joining impossibilities”, which the combination of ordinary words cannot do, but the combination of metaphors can (1458a26–30). The *Rhetoric* quotes the same riddle: the operation has no name, but both are a kind of application, and so the poet called the application of the cupping glass “a welding” (*Rhetoric* 3.2, 1405a35–b5). Good riddles, it adds, supply good metaphors, since metaphors themselves speak in riddles.
