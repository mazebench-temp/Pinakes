Whether tragedy is fully developed, “judged both in itself and in relation to the theaters”, is another question (1449a7–9). The double structure seems best “because of the weakness of the audiences; for the poets follow along, composing to suit the wishes of the spectators” (1453a33–35; see [[the-double-plot]]). Carcinus' play failed when the audience was annoyed at what a reader would not have noticed (1455a26–29), and a composite recognition can rest on “a false inference by the audience” (1455a12–13; see [[paralogismos]]). In chapter 26 the less vulgar art is said to be the one addressed to better spectators, epic to decent ones who need no gestures, tragedy to inferior ones; Aristotle answers that the fault lies in the acting (1461b26–1462a5; see [[epic-and-tragedy]]).
