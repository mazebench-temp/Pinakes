The general outline of the *Iphigenia among the Taurians* begins with a sacrifice and ends with another averted: a girl sacrificed vanished from the sight of those who sacrificed her and was settled in a land where it was the custom to sacrifice strangers to the goddess; her brother came, was seized, and was about to be sacrificed when he made himself known (1455b3–12). In Polyidus' version Orestes reflected that his sister had been sacrificed and that it was his lot to be sacrificed too (1455a6–8; see [[the-recognition-of-orestes-and-iphigenia]]).
