---
summary: Aristotle's rule for apparent contradictions, to ask in how many senses a word can be meant.
grc: [τῇ ῥʼ ἔσχετο χάλκεον, ταύτῃ κωλυθῆναι]
en: [there the bronze, spear was stopped]
---

When a word seems to signify a contradiction, one must consider in how many ways it might signify in the passage, here in how many senses the spear could be “stopped” (1461a31–35). The critics, the next lines add, do the opposite of what Glaucon describes: they make an unreasonable assumption, pass judgment on it themselves, and find fault with the poet whenever he contradicts their own notion (1461a35–b3; see [[glaucon]] and [[problems-and-solutions]]).
