---
summary: An irrational scene that epic can carry because it is not seen, and an impossibility justified by the astonishment it produces.
refs: [1460a14-17, 1460b26]
---

Epic has more room than tragedy for the irrational, from which the marvelous chiefly comes, because we do not see the person acting: “the scenes of Hector's pursuit would seem ridiculous on the stage, with the men standing still and not pursuing, and Achilles waving them back; but in epic it goes unnoticed” (1460a11–17; see [[alogon]] and [[thaumaston]]). In chapter 25 the same scene shows when an impossibility is justified: if it achieves the end of the art, making this or another part more astonishing (1460b23–26; see [[adynaton]] and [[ekplexis]]).
