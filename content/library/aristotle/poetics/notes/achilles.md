---
summary: Homer made Achilles good, though he is an example of harshness; his patronymic is Aristotle's example of a lengthened word, and his pursuit of Hector an irrational scene that only epic can carry.
heading: In the Poetics
---

The poet should portray irascible or easygoing characters as decent, “as Homer made Achilles good, though an example of harshness” (1454b11–14; see [[four-aims-of-character]]); the words are corrupt, and many editors read “as Agathon and Homer made Achilles”. Πηλείδου lengthened to Πηληιάδεω, the form of the first line of the Iliad, is the example of a lengthened word (1458a3–4; see [[son-of-peleus]] and [[epektetamenon]]), and the tragedians' Ἀχιλλέως πέρι one of the turns that Ariphrades mocked (1459a1; see [[ariphrades-and-the-tragedians]]). The pursuit of Hector, with Achilles waving his men back, would be ridiculous on the stage (1460a14–17; see [[the-pursuit-of-hector]]), and Aeneas' spear stopped in the gold of his shield is one of the problems of chapter 25 (1461a33–34; see [[the-bronze-spear-stopped]]). The award of his arms heads the list of tragedies from the Little Iliad (1459b5; see [[the-judgment-of-arms]]).
