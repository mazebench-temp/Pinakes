---
summary: Theodectes' version of the story supplies Aristotle's second example of reversal and his example of complication and resolution.
refs: [1452a27-29, 1455b29-32]
---

“In the Lynceus, one man is being led off to be put to death, and Danaus follows to kill him, but it came about, from what had been done, that the second was killed and the first saved” (1452a27–29; see [[peripeteia]]). The same play shows where a complication ends and a resolution begins: the complication is what was done before the play and the seizure of the child, the resolution runs from the accusation of murder to the end (1455b29–32; see [[desis-and-lysis]] and [[lynceus-play]]).
