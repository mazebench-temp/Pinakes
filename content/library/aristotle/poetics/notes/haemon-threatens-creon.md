---
summary: The worst way of handling a terrible deed, to be about to act knowingly and not to act.
refs: [1453b37-39, 1454a1-2]
---

Of the four ways of handling a terrible deed, “to be about to act knowingly and not to do it is the worst: for it is repugnant, and not tragic, for there is no suffering. So no one composes in this way, except rarely, as in the Antigone, where Creon is threatened by Haemon” (1453b37–1454a2). Haemon's attempt on his father fails, and the suffering that follows is his own death, not his father's (see [[haemon-son-of-creon]] and [[pathos]]).
