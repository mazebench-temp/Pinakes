---
summary: A traditional story that cannot be undone, which comedy would turn into a reconciliation.
refs: [1453a37-39, 1453b22-24]
---

The double ending, with opposite fates for the better and the worse, suits comedy rather than tragedy: “there, those who are the bitterest enemies in the story, such as Orestes and Aegisthus, go off at the end as friends, and no one is killed by anyone” (1453a35–39; see [[the-double-plot]]). In tragedy the story stands: “the stories that have been handed down cannot be undone, I mean such as the death of Clytemnestra at the hands of Orestes” (1453b22–24; see [[traditional-stories]]).
