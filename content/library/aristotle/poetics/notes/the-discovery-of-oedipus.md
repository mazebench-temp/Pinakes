---
summary: The model of reversal and recognition together, and of a recognition that arises from the incidents themselves.
refs: [1452a24-26, 1452a32-33, 1453b4-7, 1455a16-18]
---

The messenger is Aristotle's first example of reversal, a change to the opposite of what is being done: “the man who comes to cheer Oedipus and to free him from his fear about his mother does the opposite by revealing who he was” (1452a24–26; see [[peripeteia]]). The finest recognition is the one that comes together with a reversal, “as does the one in the Oedipus” (1452a32–33; see [[anagnorisis]]), and the best of all is the one that arises from the incidents themselves, the astonishment coming about through probable events, as in Sophocles' *Oedipus* (1455a16–18). The story is so well made that one who only hears it told shudders and feels pity (1453b4–7).
