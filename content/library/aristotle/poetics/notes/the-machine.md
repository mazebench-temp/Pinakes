summary: Resolutions should come from the plot itself, not from the machine; the machine is for what lies outside the drama, what happened before or will happen after, which only the gods can see.
grc: [μηχαν*]
en: [machine]
---

{{quote:a4bb4d4c-b1fc-59e2-95a0-9d37c9ab1e2e}}

Medea's escape in the chariot of the Sun ends Euripides' play without arising from it (see [[medea-play]]); the second example is the episode in Iliad 2 where Athena stops the Achaeans from sailing home (see [[the-test-of-the-army]]). Aristotle allows the machine only for what lies “outside the drama”: events before the action that no human can know, or after it, which need prophecy, “for we grant the gods the power to see everything” (1454b2–6).
