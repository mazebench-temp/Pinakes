Among the arts that imitate, dance uses “rhythm by itself, without melody”, for dancers, “through rhythms given shape in movement, imitate characters and feelings and actions” (1447a26–28; see [[mimesis]] and [[rhythmos]]). Its dissimilarities of object are those of the other arts (1448a9–10). Early tragedy used the trochaic tetrameter “because the poetry was satyric and more suited to dancing” (1449a22–23; see [[trochaic-tetrameter]]), and that meter is still “suited to dancing” in chapter 24 (1459b37–1460a1). Not all movement on the stage is to be rejected, “any more than all dancing, but only that of inferior people” (1462a8–10). In the *Poetics* χορός is the tragic or comic [[chorus]], not the dance.
