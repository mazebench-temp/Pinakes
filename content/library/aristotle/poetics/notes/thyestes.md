---
summary: With Oedipus, the type of the man who falls through error, and the head of one of the few houses of tragedy.
heading: In the Poetics
---

The tragic hero is the man between, neither outstanding in virtue nor falling through wickedness, “but through some error, one of those who stand in great repute and good fortune, such as Oedipus and Thyestes” (1453a7–11; see [[the-tragic-hero]] and [[hamartia]]). Aristotle does not say what Thyestes' error was; his adultery with his brother's wife, and his unknowing meal at his brother's table, have both been suggested (see [[the-feast-of-thyestes]]). His house is one of the few on which the finest tragedies are composed (1453a21; see [[the-few-houses]]), and in Carcinus' *Thyestes* the signs by which someone was recognized were stars on the body (1454b22–23; see [[thyestes-play]]).
