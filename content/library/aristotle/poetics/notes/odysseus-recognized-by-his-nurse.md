---
summary: One sign used in two ways: the recognition that comes out of a reversal, and the one made for proof.
refs: [1454b26-30]
---

Signs “can be used better or worse: Odysseus, for example, was recognized through his scar in one way by the nurse and in another by the swineherds. Recognitions made for the sake of proof are less artistic, and so are all such, while those that come out of a reversal, like the one in the Bath, are better” (1454b25–30; see [[semeion]] and [[anagnorisis]]). The nurse's recognition is unplanned and changes the situation; the herdsmen are shown the scar to convince them. The same book of the Odyssey gives Aristotle his example of false inference (1460a25–26; see [[the-bath]] and [[paralogismos]]).
