---
summary: Aristotle's example of metaphor from species to species.
grc: [ἀπὸ ψυχὴν ἀρύσας, τεμὼν ταναήκεϊ χαλκῷ]
en: [drawing off his life]
refs: [1457b13, 1457b15]
---

“From species to species, for example ‘with the bronze drawing off his life’ and ‘cutting with the long-edged bronze’; for here he has said ‘drawing off’ for ‘cutting’ and ‘cutting’ for ‘drawing off’: both are kinds of taking away” (1457b13–16). Each verb is a species of the genus “taking away” (ἀφελεῖν), and each stands for the other. The second phrase ends in the Homeric formula ταναήκεϊ χαλκῷ, “with the long-edged bronze” (see [[long-edged]] and [[metaphora]]).
