---
summary: Spectacle is the first part of tragedy to be named and the last in Aristotle's ranking: it moves the audience, but it belongs to the mask-maker's art more than the poet's.
grc: [ὄψεως, ὄψις, ὄψεων, ὄψεις]
en: [spectacle]
---

Since tragedy is performed by people acting, “of necessity one part of tragedy would be the adornment of spectacle” (1449b31–33). But it ranks last:

{{quote:eeaaafb5-1320-5106-a0af-67cc633a3048}}

The fearful and the pitiable can arise from spectacle, but it is better, and the mark of a better poet, that they arise from the structure of the incidents, so that someone who only hears the story of Oedipus shudders and feels pity (1453b1–7). To produce this through spectacle “is less a matter of art and needs a sponsor's money”, and those who produce through spectacle not the fearful but only the monstrous “have nothing in common with tragedy” (1453b7–10; see [[choregia]]). One of the four kinds of tragedy may have been the tragedy of spectacle, if that is the word lost in the corrupt text of 1456a2, with the *Daughters of Phorcys*, the *Prometheus* and plays set in Hades as examples (see [[four-kinds-of-tragedy]]). Epic lacks song and spectacle (1459b10); tragedy has them, “through which pleasures are produced most vividly” (1462a15–16).
