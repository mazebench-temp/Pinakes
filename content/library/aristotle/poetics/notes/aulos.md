summary: The music of the pipe is one of the arts of imitation, in melody and rhythm; bad pipers whirl about to imitate a discus; and the Iliad's “flutes and pipes” raise a problem solved by metaphor.

“Most of the music of the pipe and of the lyre” is among the imitative arts (1447a14–16), using melody and rhythm only (1447a23–24); playing the pipe can portray better or worse as other arts do (1448a9–10). In chapter 26 vulgar performers “move about a great deal, like bad pipers whirling round if they have to imitate a discus, and dragging the chorus leader about if they are piping the Scylla” (1461b30–32; see [[discus]] and [[scylla-of-timotheus]]). In chapter 25 Agamemnon hears “the din of flutes and pipes” though “all the gods and men slept”, which Aristotle solves by metaphor (1461a16–20; see [[all-the-gods-slept]]).
