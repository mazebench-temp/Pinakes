Sophocles is the same kind of imitator as Homer in that both imitate serious people, and as Aristophanes in that both imitate people acting (1448a25–28). “Three actors and scene painting came with Sophocles” (1449a18–19; see [[the-origins-of-tragedy]]). His *Oedipus* is the model play of the treatise: its reversal (1452a24–26), its recognition joined with reversal (1452a32–33), its terrible deed done in ignorance (1453b29–31), its irrational element kept outside the drama (1454b6–8, 1460a29–30), and its recognition arising from the incidents themselves (1455a16–18); its story, merely heard, makes one shudder (1453b3–7), and its concentrated power would be diluted at the length of the Iliad (1462b1–3; see [[oedipus-the-king]]). His *Tereus* has the contrived recognition by the voice of the shuttle (1454b36–37; see [[tereus-play]]), his *Tyro* the recognition by the little boat (1454b25; see [[tyro-play]]), and his *Electra* the irrational report of the Pythian games (1460a31; see [[electra-play]]). The chorus should share in the action “as in Sophocles” (1456a25–27; see [[chorus]]). And:
