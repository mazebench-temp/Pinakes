---
summary: Aristotle's example of a problem solved by punctuation.
grc: [αἶψα δὲ θνήτʼ ἐφύοντο, ζωρά τε πρὶν κέκρητο]
en: [things grew mortal that before, things unmixed before were mixed]
---

Some problems are solved by division, that is, by punctuation (1461a23–25). Greek was written without spaces or stops, and the sense of ζωρά τε πρὶν κέκρητο turns on where the pause falls. With a pause after πρίν, “things unmixed before were mixed”; with a pause before it, the elements had learned to be immortal and unmixed “before they were mixed”. Aristotle does not say how he would read it.
