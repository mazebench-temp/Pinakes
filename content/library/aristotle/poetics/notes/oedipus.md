---
summary: Oedipus is Aristotle's first example of the man who falls through error, the head of one of the few houses of tragedy, and the hero of the play that supplies the model reversal and recognition.
heading: In the Poetics
en: [Oedipus]
---

Oedipus is named with Thyestes as the man between, “neither outstanding in virtue and justice, nor falls into misfortune through badness and wickedness, but through some error, one of those who stand in great repute and good fortune” (1453a7–12; see [[the-tragic-hero]] and [[hamartia]]), and his house is one of the few on which the finest tragedies are composed (1453a20; see [[the-few-houses]]). One who merely hears “the story of Oedipus” shudders and feels pity (1453b6–7). Sophocles' Oedipus did the terrible deed in ignorance and recognized the relationship later (1453b29–31), and the irrational fact that he does not know how Laius died lies outside the drama (1460a29–30). For the play see [[oedipus-the-king]].
