Speech is one of the three media of imitation (1447a22), and “bare speech alone” is prose, which with verse lacks a common name (1447a28–b9). Tragedy's dialogue is speech: Aeschylus “made speech take the leading role” (1449a17–18). In chapter 6 thought is shown in speeches (1450a6–7, 1450b4–12; see [[dianoia]]), and diction has the same power “in verse and in prose” (1450b14–15). Stories, those already composed and those the poet makes up, should be set out in general terms (1455a34–b2), and “the story of the Odyssey is not long” (1455b17; see [[the-return-of-odysseus]]).
