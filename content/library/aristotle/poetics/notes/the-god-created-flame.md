---
summary: Aristotle's example of an analogy in which one term has no name of its own.
grc: [σπείρων θεοκτίσταν]
en: [sowing the god-created]
refs: [1457b26-28, 1457b30]
---

For some terms of an analogy there is no established word: to scatter seed is “to sow”, but the sun's scattering of its flame has no name. Yet it stands to the sun as sowing to the seed, “which is why it has been said: ‘sowing the god-created flame’” (1457b25–30). The metaphor by analogy (see [[metaphora]] and [[the-cup-of-ares]]) can supply a word the language lacks.
