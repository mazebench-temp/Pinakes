---
summary: Euripides' Menelaus in the Orestes is Aristotle's example of baseness of character that the plot does not need.
---

“An example of baseness of character that is not necessary is Menelaus in the Orestes” (1454a28–29), and criticism of baseness is right when there is no necessity for it, “as in the Orestes that of Menelaus” (1461b19–21). In Euripides' play Menelaus, newly returned from Troy, refuses to help his nephew against the Argives (see [[orestes-play]] and [[four-aims-of-character]]).
