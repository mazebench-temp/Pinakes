summary: Command and prayer, question and answer belong to the art of delivery, not to poetry; the inflections of question and command are “inflections concerning delivery”; and overacting is a fault of acting, not of tragedy.
grc: [ὑποκριτικ*]
en: [delivery, art of delivery, art of acting, concerns delivery]
---

The “forms of utterance” are known by “the art of delivery and whoever has the master art of it”, and no criticism of poetry worth taking seriously can be made from them (1456b8–15; see [[forms-of-utterance]]). Among the inflections of a verb, “Did he walk?” and “Walk!” are those “that concern delivery” (1457a21–23; see [[ptosis]]). The charge that tragedy is vulgar because its performers move about too much “is not against the art of poetry but against the art of acting”, for a rhapsode like Sosistratus or a singer like Mnasitheus can overdo it too (1462a5–8; see [[epic-and-tragedy]]).
