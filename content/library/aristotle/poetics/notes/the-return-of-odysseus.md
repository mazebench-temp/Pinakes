---
summary: Aristotle's outline of the Odyssey, the story stripped of its episodes.
refs: [1455b17-23]
---

“For the story of the Odyssey is not long: a man is away from home for many years, watched closely by Poseidon and alone; and further, things at home are such that his property is being wasted by suitors and his son plotted against; he himself arrives, storm-tossed, and after making himself known to certain people he attacks; he himself is saved, and his enemies he destroys. This is what belongs to it; the rest is episodes” (1455b17–23; see [[epeisodion]] and [[katholou]]). Elsewhere Aristotle calls the Odyssey complex, “for there is recognition throughout”, and a poem of character (1459b15–16), and gives its double ending, opposite fates for the good and the bad, as the type of the plot that pleases weak audiences (1453a30–35; see [[the-double-plot]]).
