summary: Sophocles introduced scene painting; the actors' songs “from the stage” are part of some plays; on the stage tragedy can show only one action at a time; and things invisible on the page fail on the stage.
grc: [σκην*]
en: [stage, scene painting, from the stage, on the stage]
---

“Three actors and scene painting came with Sophocles” (1449a18–19; see [[sophocles]]). Songs from the stage and kommoi are peculiar to some plays (1452b17–18), the kommos being a lament shared by the chorus and those on the stage (1452b24–25; see [[kommos]]). Plays that end in misfortune appear the most tragic “on the stage and in the competitions” (1453a27–28). Carcinus' Amphiaraus failed “on the stage” because of an incongruity a reader would miss (1455a26–29; see [[carcinus]]). Epic can narrate many parts at once, tragedy only “the part on the stage and among the actors” (1459b24–26); and the pursuit of Hector “would seem ridiculous on the stage” (1460a14–16; see [[the-pursuit-of-hector]]).
