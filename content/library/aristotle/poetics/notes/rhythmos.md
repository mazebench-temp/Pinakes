---
summary: Rhythm is a medium of imitation: with melody in music for the pipe and lyre, alone in dance, with speech and melody in tragedy; and the love of rhythm is natural.
grc: [ῥυθμ*]
en: [rhythm, rhythms]
---

All the arts of imitation work “in rhythm and speech and melody” (1447a21–23). Music for the pipe and lyre uses melody and rhythm alone, dancing rhythm alone: dancers “through rhythms given shape in movement, imitate characters and feelings and actions” (1447a23–28; see [[dance]]). Dithyramb, nome, tragedy and comedy use “rhythm and song and meter”, the first two all at once, the others by turns (1447b24–28). Imitation is natural to us, “and so are melody and rhythm (for that meters are sections of rhythms is evident)”, and those most gifted for them gave birth to poetry out of their improvisations (1448b20–24; see [[origins-of-poetry]]). The “embellished language” of tragedy is language with rhythm and melody (1449b28–29).
