summary: The Odyssey is complex and full of character; its story is short and the rest is episodes; it has a double ending; and its recognitions and its irrational landing on Ithaca supply examples.

en: [Odyssey]

Homer did not put into the Odyssey everything that happened to Odysseus, but built it, like the Iliad, around one action (1451a24–29; see [[unity-of-action]]). Its story is short:

{{quote:94abfee7-8b8c-5d3b-8d16-ca2ddc8e943b}}

It is the example of the double structure, “ending in opposite ways for the better and the worse people”, which some put first and Aristotle second (1453a30–33; see [[the-double-plot]]). It is “complex (there is recognition throughout) and full of character” (1459b15–16; see [[simple-and-complex-plots]]), and its recognitions are examples in chapter 16: the scar, used one way by the nurse and another by the swineherds, and the recognition in the Bath (1454b26–30; see [[the-bath]] and [[the-scar-of-odysseus]]); the tale told to Alcinous supplies a recognition through memory (1455a2–3; see [[the-tale-to-alcinous]]). Its irrational landing, Odysseus set ashore asleep on Ithaca, Homer hides with his other virtues (1460a35–b2; see [[odysseus-set-ashore-on-ithaca]]). Several lines are quoted in chapters 21 and 22 (see [[here-my-ship-stands]], [[small-and-of-no-account]] and [[the-unseemly-stool]]).
