---
summary: The rare word is a word used by other people; it lifts diction above the commonplace, suits the heroic meter, makes barbarism if overused, and solves some problems in Homer.
grc: [γλωττ*]
en: [rare word, rare words]
---

The rare word is the second of the eight kinds of noun (1457b1–6; see [[kyrion]] and [[sigynon]]). It belongs to the “unfamiliar” words that make diction dignified and free of the commonplace (1458a21–23; see [[xenikon]]), but diction made entirely of rare words is barbarism (1458a24–26; see [[barbarismos]]). Aeschylus and Euripides wrote the same iambic line, and Euripides' line, with a rare word in place of a standard one, is the beautiful one (1458b19–24; see [[the-ulcer-that-eats-my-foot]]). “Rare words suit heroic verse” (1459a10), which “most readily admits rare words and metaphors” (1459b34–36; see [[hexameter]]).

In chapter 25 rare words solve problems in Homer: the mules of Iliad 1.50 may be “guards”, Dolon's ill looks may be an ugly face in the Cretan sense of εὐειδής, and ζωρότερον may mean “quicker” (1461a9–16; see [[aristotle-mules]], [[aristotle-dolons-face]] and [[stronger-or-quicker]]).
