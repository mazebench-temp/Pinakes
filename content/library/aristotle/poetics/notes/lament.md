summary: The kommos is defined as a lament shared by chorus and actors, and the lament of Odysseus in Timotheus' Scylla is Aristotle's example of the unseemly in character.

en: [lament]
---

“A kommos is a lament shared by the chorus and those on the stage” (1452b24–25; see [[kommos]]). The lament of Odysseus in the *Scylla* is Aristotle's example of character that is “unseemly and inappropriate” (1454a30–31): a hero should not wail like a woman (see [[scylla-of-timotheus]] and [[four-aims-of-character]]).
