summary: Reversal is one of the three parts of the plot, “the change to the opposite in what is being done”; the finest recognition comes with it, as in the Oedipus.

en: [reversal, reversals]
---

Reversals and recognitions are “the most powerful means by which tragedy sways the soul” and parts of the plot (1450a33–35). A complex action is one whose change comes “with recognition or reversal or both” (1452a16–18; see [[simple-and-complex-plots]]). The definition:

{{quote:3327d519-de4f-56ab-b1ef-794b82dc55fc}}

In the *Oedipus* the Corinthian comes to free Oedipus from his fear and reveals that he is not the son of Polybus (see [[the-discovery-of-oedipus]]); in the *Lynceus* the man led off to die is saved and Danaus, who follows to kill him, is killed (see [[lynceus-and-danaus]]). The finest recognition comes together with a reversal, “as does the one in the Oedipus” (1452a32–33; see [[anagnorisis]]), and the recognition in the Bath, which comes out of a reversal, is better than those made for proof (1454b29–30). One of the four kinds of tragedy is the complex, “which is all reversal and recognition” (1455b33–34); epic too needs reversals (1459b11), and in reversals and simple incidents poets aim at what they want “with marvelous skill” (1456a19–21).
