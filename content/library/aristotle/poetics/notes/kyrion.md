---
summary: The standard word is the one a people uses; the same word may be standard for one people and rare for another. It gives clarity but, alone, meanness; Aristotle shows its effect by substituting standard words into lines of verse.
grc: [κύριον, κυρίων, κύρια, κυρίου]
en: [standard, standard word, standard words]
---

“By standard I mean the word that each people uses, and by rare word one that others use; so it is evident that the same word can be both rare and standard, but not for the same people: sigynon is standard for the Cypriots, but a rare word for us” (1457b3–6; see [[sigynon]]). The clearest diction is made of standard words, but it is mean, like the poetry of Cleophon and Sthenelus (1458a18–21; see [[excellence-of-diction]]); the standard word “will give clarity” (1458a34). Chapter 22 demonstrates by substitution: put standard words into the verses of Homer and the effect is lost (1458b15–31; see [[small-and-of-no-account]], [[the-unseemly-stool]] and [[the-shores-roar]]).
