---
summary: Aristotle's proof that a single rare word can lift a line.
grc: [φαγέδαιναν ἥ μου σάρκας, θοινᾶται]
en: [the ulcer that eats the flesh of my foot, feasts on]
---

How much the fitting use of words matters can be seen by substituting the standard words (1458b15–19). “The same iambic line was composed by Aeschylus and by Euripides, but with only one word changed, a rare word in place of a standard, usual one, and the one line seems beautiful, the other trivial” (1458b19–24). The beautiful one is Euripides', with the rare word (see [[glotta]] and [[kyrion]]).
