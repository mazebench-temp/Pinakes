---
summary: “Probability or necessity” governs plot and character; baseness of character that is not necessary is a fault, and criticism is right when the poet uses the irrational without necessity.
grc: [ἀναγκαῖον, ἀναγκαίας, ἐξ ἀνάγκης, οὔτʼ ἀνάγκη]
en: [necessary, necessity, necessarily, by necessity]
---

Necessity is the stronger partner of probability throughout the theory of plot (see [[eikos]]). A beginning is what does not necessarily follow something else, an end what follows something else “either by necessity or as a rule” (1450b27–30; see [[beginning-middle-end]]). Of the wound on Parnassus and the madness at the mustering, “neither, when the one had happened, made it necessary or probable that the other should happen” (1451a26–28; see [[unity-of-action]]). Menelaus in the *Orestes* is “baseness of character that is not necessary” (1454a28–29), and the poet who uses the irrational or wickedness “there being no necessity” is rightly criticized (1461b19–21).
