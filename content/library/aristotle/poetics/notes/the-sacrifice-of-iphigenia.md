---
summary: Euripides' Iphigenia at Aulis is Aristotle's example of inconsistent character, and her vanishing from the altar is the first step in his outline of the Iphigenia among the Taurians.
refs: [1454a31-33, 1455b3-5]
---

An example of inconsistent character is “Iphigenia at Aulis, for the girl who pleads for her life is nothing like the later one” (1454a31–33; see [[ethos]] and [[iphigenia-at-aulis]]). The sacrifice also begins Aristotle's general outline of the *Iphigenia among the Taurians*: “a girl was sacrificed and vanished unseen by those who sacrificed her, and was settled in another land” (1455b3–5; see [[the-recognition-of-orestes-and-iphigenia]]).
