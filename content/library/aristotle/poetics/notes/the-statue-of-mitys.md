---
summary: Aristotle's example of the most marvelous kind of chance, the kind that looks deliberate.
refs: [1452a4-11]
---

Fear and pity arise most when things happen contrary to expectation but because of one another, “since even among chance events the most marvelous seem to be those that look as if they had happened on purpose, as when the statue of Mitys at Argos killed the man responsible for the death of Mitys by falling on him as he looked at it; for such things seem not to happen at random” (1452a1–11; see [[thaumaston]]). The plot should have the same effect by design that the statue had by chance.
