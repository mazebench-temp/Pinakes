---
summary: The god of the cup in Aristotle's model metaphor of analogy.
grc: [Διόνυσον, Διονύσου]
---

“The cup stands to Dionysus as the shield to Ares”, and so the poet may call the cup “the shield of Dionysus” (1457b20–22; see [[the-cup-of-ares]] and [[phiale]]). Aristotle does not name Dionysus in his account of the origins of tragedy and comedy, though the dithyramb and the phallic songs from which he derives them belonged to the god's cult (see [[the-origins-of-tragedy]] and [[the-origins-of-comedy]]).
