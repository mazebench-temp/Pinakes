Aeschylus “reduced the part of the chorus and made speech take the leading role” (1449a16–18; see [[the-origins-of-tragedy]]). A comic chorus was granted by the archon only late (1449b1–2; see [[choregia]]). Chapter 12 defines the sections of a tragedy by the chorus: the prologue comes before its entry, an episode lies between whole choral songs, the exodos after the last; of the choral part, the parodos is its first whole utterance, the stasimon a song without anapests or trochees, and the kommos a lament shared with the actors (1452b16–25; see [[prologue]], [[epeisodion]], [[parodos]], [[stasimon]] and [[kommos]]).
