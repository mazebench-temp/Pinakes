---
summary: The recognition of a deed done in ignorance is astonishing; the best recognition produces astonishment through probable events; and an impossibility is justified if it makes a part more astonishing.
grc: [ἐκπληκ*, ἐκπλήξ*]
en: [astonishing, astonishment, more astonishing]
refs: [1460b26]
---

To act in ignorance and recognize afterwards is better than to act knowingly, “for the repugnant is absent, and the recognition is astonishing” (1453b39–1454a4). The best recognition arises from the incidents themselves, “when the astonishment comes about through probable events” (1455a16–18). In chapter 25 an impossibility in a poem is an error, but it is correct “if in this way it makes this or another part more astonishing”, as with the pursuit of Hector (1460b23–26; see [[the-pursuit-of-hector]]).
