Dithyrambic poetry is named in the first list of imitative arts (1447a14), and with the nome it uses all the media, “rhythm and song and meter”, at once (1447b24–28). Its objects differ as in the other arts: one might portray Cyclopes better or worse, as Timotheus and Philoxenus did (1448a14–16; see [[cyclops-dithyrambs]]). Tragedy arose “from those who led off the dithyramb”, the ἐξάρχοντες (1449a10–11; see [[the-origins-of-tragedy]]). “Double words suit dithyrambs best” (1459a9; see [[compound-word]]).
