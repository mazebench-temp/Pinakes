---
summary: The rush to the ships in Iliad 2, stopped by Athena, is Aristotle's example of a resolution brought about by a god rather than by the plot.
refs: [1454b2]
---

The resolutions of plots should come from the plot itself, “and not, as in the Medea, from the machine, or as with the sailing away in the Iliad” (1454a37–b2; see [[the-machine]] and [[desis-and-lysis]]). When Agamemnon tests the army, the men rush to launch the ships, and the Achaeans would have sailed home against fate had not Hera sent Athena to stop them (2.142–181).
