---
summary: An utter villain's fall satisfies human feeling but is not tragic; Menelaus in the Orestes is baseness without necessity; and the clever villain deceived, like Sisyphus, is tragic.
grc: [πονηρ*]
en: [villainous, baseness]
---

An utterly villainous man falling from good fortune into misfortune might satisfy human feeling, but would bring neither pity nor fear (1453a1–4; see [[philanthropon]] and [[the-tragic-hero]]). Menelaus in the *Orestes* is Aristotle's example, twice, of baseness that the plot does not require (1454a28–29, 1461b21; see [[orestes-play]] and [[menelaus]]). In chapter 18 it is tragic, and satisfies human feeling, when “the clever but villainous man is deceived, like Sisyphus” (1456a21–23; see [[sisyphus]]).
