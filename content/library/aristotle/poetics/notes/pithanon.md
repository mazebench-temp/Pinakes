summary: Historical names are convincing because what happened is possible; the poet in the grip of feeling is most convincing; and convincing impossibilities are to be preferred.
grc: [πιθαν*, ἀπιθαν*]
en: [convincing, unconvincing, most convincing]
refs: [1455a31]
---

“What is possible is convincing”, which is why tragedians keep the names of people who existed (1451b15–19; see [[adynaton]]). Given the same gifts, “those in the grip of feelings are the most convincing” (1455a30–32; see [[the-gifted-or-the-mad]]). “One should prefer probable impossibilities to unconvincing possibilities” (1460a26–27), and “for poetry, a convincing impossibility is preferable to what is unconvincing though possible” (1461b11–12; see [[probable-impossibilities]]).
