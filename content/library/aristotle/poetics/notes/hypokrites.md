“The number of actors was first raised from one to two by Aeschylus … three actors and scene painting came with Sophocles” (1449a15–19; see [[the-origins-of-tragedy]]); who introduced masks, prologues and the number of actors in comedy is unknown (1449b4–5). Good poets make episodic plots “because of the actors”, composing show pieces for competition and stretching the plot beyond its capacity (1451b36–1452a1; see [[epeisodion]]). The power of tragedy “exists even without a performance and actors” (1450b18–19; see [[opsis]]), and in tragedy only the part “on the stage and among the actors” can be imitated at one time (1459b24–26). The chorus should be treated as one of them (1456a25–26; see [[chorus]]).

In chapter 26 the older actors thought their successors overdid it: Mynniscus called Callippides an ape (1461b33–35; see [[mynniscus-and-callippides]]). Aristotle's reply is that the charge “is not against the art of poetry but against the art of acting” (1462a5–6; see [[delivery]]).
