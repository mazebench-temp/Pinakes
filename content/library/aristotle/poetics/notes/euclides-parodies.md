---
summary: Aristotle's example of criticism that mocks a legitimate license.
grc: [Ἐπιχάρην εἶδον, ἐλλέβορον]
en: [I saw Epichares, hellebore]
refs: [1458b10]
---

Lengthenings, curtailments and alterations make diction uncommon by departing from the usual form, and keep it clear by sharing in it. So those are wrong who find fault with this manner and mock the poet, like Euclides the elder, who said composing would be easy if one were allowed to lengthen syllables as far as one liked, and lampooned the practice in the very diction (1458b1–11; see [[euclides-the-elder]]). Aristotle grants half the point: to be seen using the device obtrusively is ridiculous, and moderation is common to all the kinds of word (1458b11–15).
