Nouns are simple or double (compound), and compounds may have several parts (1457a31–1457b1; see [[compound-word]]). Every noun is “either standard, or a rare word, or a metaphor, or an ornament, or coined, or lengthened, or shortened, or altered” (1457b1–3; see [[kyrion]], [[glotta]], [[metaphora]], [[kosmos]], [[pepoiemenon]], [[epektetamenon]], [[hyphairemenon]] and [[exellagmenon]]), and masculine, feminine or in between (1458a8–17; see [[gender-of-nouns]]).

ὄνομα is also the name of a person in a play. Poetry aims at universals “though it attaches names” (1451b10); comedy supplies names after building the plot, while tragedy keeps to historical names, though Agathon's *Antheus* invents them all (1451b11–23; see [[antheus]]); and the poet supplies the names only after outlining the story (1455b12–13).
