summary: Pity and fear are strongest when events happen contrary to expectation but because of one another, for this has most of the marvelous; epic, which is not seen, admits more of the irrational and so of the marvelous.

en: [marvelous, the marvelous, most marvelous, marvelous skill]

The fearful and pitiable arise most “when things happen contrary to expectation but because of one another”:

{{quote:0de467d9-556b-544a-96c3-5c14497eb70e}}

Even chance events seem most marvelous when they look designed, as when the statue of Mitys at Argos fell on his murderer (see [[the-statue-of-mitys]]). In chapter 24 the marvelous is required in tragedy, but more admissible in epic is the irrational, “through which the marvelous most of all comes about, because we do not see the person acting”: Hector's pursuit would be ridiculous on the stage (1460a11–17; see [[the-pursuit-of-hector]] and [[alogon]]). “The marvelous is pleasant: evidence of this is that everyone adds something when telling a story, thinking to please” (1460a17–18).
