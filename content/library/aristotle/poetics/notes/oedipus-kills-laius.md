---
summary: The deed done in ignorance and recognized later; in Sophocles it lies outside the drama, together with the irrational fact that Oedipus never learned how Laius died.
refs: [1453b29-32, 1454b6-8, 1460a27-30]
---

Of the ways a terrible deed can be handled, one is for people to act in ignorance and recognize the relationship afterward, “as Sophocles' Oedipus does; here, it is true, the deed is outside the drama” (1453b29–32). The deed is better done in ignorance and recognized later, for the repugnant is absent and the recognition astonishing (1454a2–4). The play has an irrational element too, but this also lies outside the tragedy (1454b6–8): that Oedipus does not know how Laius died, though he has been king of Thebes for years (1460a27–30; see [[alogon]]).
