---
summary: Decent men must not be shown falling from good fortune to bad, which is repugnant; yet the poet should make his irascible or easygoing characters decent, and epic is said to address decent spectators.
grc: [ἐπιεικεῖς]
en: [decent]
---

The first case ruled out in chapter 13 is the fall of decent men, which is “neither fearful nor pitiable but repugnant” (1452b34–36; see [[the-tragic-hero]]). The word returns in the advice of chapter 15, where the poet, like a good portrait painter, must make irascible or easygoing people “being such, decent” (1454b11–13; see [[painting-and-poetry]]), and in the argument of chapter 26 that epic is addressed to “decent spectators who have no need of gestures” (1462a2–3; see [[epic-and-tragedy]]).
