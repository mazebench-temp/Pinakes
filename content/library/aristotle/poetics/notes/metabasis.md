The adequate magnitude of a plot is the one in which, by probability or necessity, “there is a change to good fortune from misfortune, or from good fortune to misfortune” (1451a11–15; see [[megethos]]). In a simple action the change comes without reversal or recognition, in a complex one with them (1452a14–18; see [[simple-and-complex-plots]]). Chapter 13 judges the directions: decent men must not pass from good fortune to misfortune, nor the wicked from misfortune to good fortune, and the best plot changes “not to good fortune from misfortune but the opposite” (1452b34–1453a15; see [[the-tragic-hero]]). In chapter 18 the complication runs up to the point “from which the change comes”, and the resolution from the beginning of the change to the end (1455b26–29; see [[desis-and-lysis]]).
