---
summary: Virtue and badness distinguish characters, and the tragic hero is not outstanding in virtue; diction too has its excellence.
grc: [ἀρετῇ, ἀρετὴ]
en: [virtue, excellence]
---

It is by badness and virtue that people differ in character (1448a2–4; see [[better-worse-or-like-us]]), and the tragic hero is one “who is neither outstanding in virtue and justice, nor falls into misfortune through badness and wickedness, but through some error” (1453a7–10; see [[the-tragic-hero]]). “The excellence of diction is to be clear and not mean” (1458a18; see [[excellence-of-diction]]).
