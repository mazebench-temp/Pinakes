---
summary: An episode is a section between choral songs, and also an incident that fills out a plot; epic is lengthened by its episodes, and the episodic plot is the worst of the simple plots.
grc: [ἐπεισοδ*]
en: [episode, episodes, episodic, add episodes, diversifying with episodes]
---

The number of episodes was one stage in the growth of tragedy (1449a28–29). As a section, “an episode is a whole part of a tragedy between whole choral songs” (1452b20–21; see [[prologue]]). As a part of a plot, an episode is what the poet adds after setting out the story in general terms; it must belong to the story, as Orestes' madness and his rescue through the purification belong to the *Iphigenia* (1455a34–b16). The episodes of a drama are short, but epic is lengthened by them: “the story of the Odyssey is not long … This is what belongs to it; the rest is episodes” (1455b16–23; see [[the-return-of-odysseus]]). Homer took one part of the war and used many others as episodes, such as the catalogue of ships (1459a35–37; see [[catalogue-of-ships]]), and epic can vary the hearer “with episodes unlike each other” (1459b29–31).
