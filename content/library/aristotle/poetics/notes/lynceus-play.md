---
grc: [Λυγκεῖ]
en: [the Lynceus]
---

The *Lynceus* gives an example of reversal: “one man is being led off to be put to death, and Danaus follows to kill him, but it came about, from what had been done, that the second was killed and the first saved” (1452a27–29; see [[peripeteia]]); and of complication and resolution: “the complication is what was done before, and the seizure of the child, and again their own …; the resolution runs from the accusation of murder to the end” (1455b29–32; see [[desis-and-lysis]]).
