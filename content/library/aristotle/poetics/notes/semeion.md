summary: The first and least artistic kind of recognition is by tokens, congenital or acquired, on the body or outside it; tokens used for proof are inferior to those that come out of a reversal.

{{quote:55e833b0-b46f-57cb-a726-a6253d526f1c}}

Congenital signs are the spearhead birthmark of the Earth-born (see [[the-spearhead-of-the-earth-born]] and [[the-earth-born]]) and the stars that Carcinus gives his characters in the *Thyestes* (see [[thyestes-play]]); acquired ones are on the body, like scars (see [[the-scar-of-odysseus]]), or outside it, like necklaces (see [[necklace]]) or the little boat in Sophocles' *Tyro* (see [[the-boat-of-tyro]]). They can be used better or worse: Odysseus is recognized through his scar in one way by the nurse and in another by the swineherds, and recognitions made for proof are less artistic than those that come from a reversal, like the one in the Bath (1454b25–30; see [[the-bath]]). The best recognitions do without “contrived signs and necklaces” (1455a19–21).
