---
summary: In the Poetics εἶδος is a technical term, a kind or species; only in the quotation about Dolon does it keep its Homeric sense of looks.
grc: [εἴδη, εἰδῶν, εἴδεσι*, εἴδους]
en: [kinds, species, kind]
---

The treatise opens by promising to speak of the art of poetry “and its kinds” (εἰδῶν, 1447a8), and the word recurs for the kinds of recognition, of tragedy and of nouns (1454b19, 1455b32, 1457a31). In the theory of metaphor εἶδος is species, set against γένος, genus (1457b7–16; see [[metaphora]] and [[genee]]). In the Homeric line about Dolon, “who was ill-favored to look at” (εἶδος, 1461a12), Aristotle takes the word to mean the face (see [[dolon]]).
