---
summary: Euripides' use of Aegeus in the Medea is Aristotle's example of an irrational element that the plot does not need.
grc: [Αἰγεῖ]
---

In Euripides' *Medea* the king of Athens happens to pass through Corinth just when Medea needs a refuge, and promises to receive her (*Medea* 663–763). Aristotle names it as the irrational used without necessity: criticism is right “when, there being no necessity, the poet makes use of the irrational, as Euripides does of Aegeus” (1461b19–21; see [[medea-kills-her-children]], [[medea-play]] and [[alogon]]).
