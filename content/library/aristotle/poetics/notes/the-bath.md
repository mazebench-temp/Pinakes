---
grc: [Νίπτροις, Νίπτρων]
en: [the Bath]
---

Recognitions that come out of a reversal, “like the one in the Bath”, are better than those made for proof (1454b29–30; see [[anagnorisis]]), and “the passage from the Bath” is Aristotle's example of the false inference by which Homer taught poets to tell lies as they should (1460a25–26; see [[paralogismos]] and [[homer-taught-lies]]).
