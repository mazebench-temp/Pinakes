---
summary: Aristotle's third example of a shortened word, after Homer's kri and dō.
grc: [μία γίνεται ἀμφοτέρων ὄψ]
en: [one sight (ops) comes from both]
---

A word is shortened “if something has been taken away from it”: κρῖ for κριθή, “barley”, δῶ for δῶμα, “house”, and ὄψ for ὄψις in this half-line (1458a1–5; see [[hyphairemenon]]). The first two are Homer's: the horses that eat “white barley”, κρῖ λευκόν (5.196, 8.564), and the “bronze-floored house” of Zeus, χαλκοβατὲς δῶ (1.426).
