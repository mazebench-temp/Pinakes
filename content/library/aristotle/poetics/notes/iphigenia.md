---
summary: Iphigenia's recognition of her brother is Aristotle's favorite example after the Oedipus; at Aulis she is his example of an inconsistent character.
heading: In the Poetics
en: [Iphigenia]
---

Iphigenia and Orestes recognize each other in two steps, she through her letter and he by another means (1452b5–8; see [[the-recognition-of-orestes-and-iphigenia]]); the sister about to sacrifice her brother and recognizing him in time is the best kind of tragic deed (1454a7); and it is probable that she should wish to send a letter (1455a18–19; see [[epistle]]). Chapter 17 outlines her story without names: “a girl was sacrificed and vanished unseen by those who sacrificed her, and was settled in another land, where it was the custom to sacrifice strangers to the goddess” (1455b3–5; see [[human-sacrifice]]). At Aulis she is inconsistent: “the girl who pleads for her life is nothing like the later one” (1454a31–32; see [[iphigenia-at-aulis]]). Polyidus the sophist proposed that Orestes should reason his way to recognition through her sacrifice (1455a6–8; see [[polyidus]]).
