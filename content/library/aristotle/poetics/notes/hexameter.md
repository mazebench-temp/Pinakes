summary: The heroic meter is the meter of epic, “the most stately and weighty of the meters”, found fitting by experience; we seldom speak in it, and mixing it with others is absurd.

Aristotle calls the hexameter τὸ ἡρωικόν, “the heroic”. Of the old poets “some became poets of heroic verse, others of lampoons” (1448b33–34). We speak iambics often in conversation, but “hexameters seldom, and only when we depart from the tone of speech” (1449a26–28). Chapter 6 opens by setting aside “the art of imitation in hexameters” for later (1449b21). In chapter 22 rare words suit heroic verse, where all the kinds of word are useful (1459a10–11).
