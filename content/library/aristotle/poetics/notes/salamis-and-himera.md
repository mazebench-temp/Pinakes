---
summary: Aristotle's example of events that happen at the same time without tending to one end.
refs: [1459a21-29]
---

Epic compositions should not be like histories, which must set out not one action but one period of time, “all that happened in it to one man or more, each of these events related to the others as chance would have it. For just as the sea battle at Salamis was fought at the same time as the battle with the Carthaginians in Sicily, though they in no way tended to the same end, so too in successive times one thing sometimes happens after another, without any single end coming from them” (1459a21–29; see [[poetry-and-history]] and [[unity-of-action]]).
