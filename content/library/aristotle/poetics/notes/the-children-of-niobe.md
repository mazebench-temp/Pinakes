---
summary: A story too large for one tragedy, unless it is handled as Aeschylus handled it.
refs: [1456a15-19]
---

A tragedy should not be given an epic structure, one with many stories in it. The proof is that all who made the whole Sack of Troy, and not a part of it as Euripides did, “or Niobe, and not as Aeschylus did”, either failed or fared badly in the competition (1456a10–19; see [[niobe-play]]). Aeschylus, it seems, took a single part of the story, the mother's grief after the deaths.
