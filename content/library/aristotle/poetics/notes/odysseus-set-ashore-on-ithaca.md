---
summary: An irrational incident that would be intolerable in a bad poet, hidden by Homer's other excellences.
refs: [1460a35-36, 1460b1-2]
---

“Even the irrational things in the Odyssey about Odysseus' being put ashore would clearly be intolerable, if a bad poet had made them; as it is, the poet conceals the absurdity, sweetening it with his other good qualities” (1460a35–b2; see [[alogon]]). That a man should sleep through being carried from a ship and laid on a beach is the absurdity; the beauty of the voyage and of Odysseus' waking covers it.
