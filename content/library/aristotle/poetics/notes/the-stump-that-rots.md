---
summary: The second of Hippias' solutions by accent.
grc: [τὸ μὲν οὗ καταπύθεται ὄμβρῳ]
en: [part of which rots in the rain]
---

Written without accents, the letters ΟΥ can be read as οὗ, “of which”, or as οὐ, “not”. Critics read οὗ and found the words absurd, a stump “part of which rots in the rain”; Hippias read οὐ, and the stump does not rot (1461a21–23; see [[hippias-of-thasos]]). The *Sophistical Refutations* tells the same story without naming Hippias: the solution is to pronounce the word with a higher pitch (4, 166b3–6). The first of his solutions in the same sentence is [[aristotle-and-hippias]].
