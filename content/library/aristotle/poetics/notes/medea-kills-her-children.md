---
summary: The terrible deed done knowingly, as the old poets made it.
refs: [1453b27-29]
---

A terrible deed may be done “as the old poets made it, with people acting knowingly and in full awareness, as Euripides too made Medea kill her children” (1453b27–29). Aristotle ranks this way below the deed done in ignorance and recognized afterward, and below the deed prevented by a recognition in time (1453b36–1454a9; see [[the-best-tragic-plot]]). The play's ending, Medea's escape in the chariot of the Sun, is his example of a resolution brought about by the machine rather than by the plot (1454b1–2; see [[the-machine]]).
