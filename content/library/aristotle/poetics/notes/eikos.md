summary: “By probability or necessity” is the phrase that runs through the theory of plot: events must follow from one another, characters must say and do what such people would, and poetry tells what could happen.
grc: [εἰκός, εἰκότων, εἰκότα]
en: [probable, probability, probably, probable events, likely]
---

The phrase “by probability or necessity” (κατὰ τὸ εἰκὸς ἢ τὸ ἀναγκαῖον) appears whenever Aristotle describes how a plot must be built. The magnitude of a plot is whatever allows events to follow “one after another by probability or necessity” to a change of fortune (1451a12–14; see [[megethos]]); the poet's task is to tell “what could happen, things possible by probability or necessity” (1451a36–38; see [[poetry-and-history]]); the universal is what a person of a certain sort “will happen to say or do by probability or necessity” (1451b8–9). Episodic plots are the worst because their episodes follow “with neither probability nor necessity” (1451b34–35), and reversal and recognition must follow from what has gone before “either by necessity or by probability” (1452a18–21). In comedy the plot is built through probable events and the names supplied afterwards (1451b11–13). Agathon's saying that “it is probable that many things should happen even contrary to probability” is quoted twice (1456a24–25, 1461b15; see [[the-probable-improbable]]), and probable impossibilities are to be preferred to unconvincing possibilities (1460a26–27; see [[probable-impossibilities]]).
