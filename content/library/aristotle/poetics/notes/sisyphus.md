---
summary: The clever but villainous man deceived, an outcome that is tragic and satisfies human feeling.
---

In reversals poets achieve their aim when “the clever but villainous man is deceived, like Sisyphus, or the brave but unjust man is defeated” (1456a21–23; see [[philanthropon]] and [[the-probable-improbable]]). Aristotle does not say which story he means. Sisyphus, the cleverest of men, was said to have cheated Death himself, and was punished in the end with the stone that rolls back down the hill (*Odyssey* 11.593–600); he was the hero of satyr plays by Aeschylus and Euripides.
