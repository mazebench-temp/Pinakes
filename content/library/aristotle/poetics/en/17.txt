1455a22	In constructing plots and working them out in diction,
1455a23	the poet should put things before his eyes as much as possible; for so,
1455a24	seeing them most vividly, as if he were present at the
1455a25	events themselves, he would find what is fitting and would be least likely to miss
1455a26	contradictions. Evidence of this is the fault found with Carcinus.
1455a27	Amphiaraus was coming back up from the temple, which escaped one who did not see it [the
1455a28	spectator], but on the stage the play failed because the audience
1455a29	was annoyed at it. As far as possible the poet should also work things out
1455a30	with gestures: for, given the same natural gifts, those
1455a31	in the grip of feelings are the most convincing, and the one in distress conveys distress, and
1455a32	the one in anger conveys anger, most truly. That is why the art of poetry belongs to the gifted
1455a33	or to the mad: for of these, the one kind are adaptable, the other
1455a34	carried out of themselves. Stories, both those already composed and
1455b1	those he makes up himself, he should set out in general terms, and only then add episodes
1455b2	and expand them. I mean that the general outline might be seen in this way,
1455b3	for example that of the Iphigenia: a girl was sacrificed and vanished
1455b4	unseen by those who sacrificed her, and was settled in another land, where
1455b5	it was the custom to sacrifice strangers to the goddess; she came to hold this
1455b6	priesthood. Some time later the brother of the
1455b7	priestess happened to come (that the god ordered him [for some reason outside the
1455b8	general outline] to go there, and for what purpose, lies outside the plot);
1455b9	having come and been seized, as he was about to be sacrificed he made himself known, whether as
1455b10	Euripides made it or as Polyidus did, saying, as was probable,
1455b11	that it was not only his sister, then, but he too who was to be
1455b12	sacrificed; and from this comes the rescue. After this, once the names have been supplied,
1455b13	one should add the episodes; but care must be taken that the
1455b14	episodes are proper to the story, as in the case of Orestes the madness through which he was caught, and the
1455b15	rescue through the purification. In dramas the
1455b16	episodes are brief, but the other [epic] is lengthened by them.
1455b17	For the story of the Odyssey is not long: a man is away from home
1455b18	for many years, watched closely by Poseidon
1455b19	and alone; and further, things at home are such that his
1455b20	property is being wasted by suitors and his son
1455b21	plotted against; he himself arrives, storm-tossed, and after making himself known
1455b22	to certain people he attacks; he himself is saved, and his enemies
1455b23	he destroys. This is what belongs to it; the rest is episodes.
