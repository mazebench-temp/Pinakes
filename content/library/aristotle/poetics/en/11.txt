1452a22	Reversal is the change to the opposite
1452a23	in what is being done, as has been said, and this too
1452a24	must come, as we say, by probability or necessity; as in the
1452a25	Oedipus, the man who comes to cheer Oedipus and to free him
1452a26	from his fear about his mother does the opposite by revealing who he was;
1452a27	and in the Lynceus, one man is being led off to be put to death,
1452a28	and Danaus follows to kill him, but it came about, from what
1452a29	had been done, that the second was killed and the first saved. Recognition,
1452a30	as the name itself indicates, is a change from ignorance to knowledge,
1452a31	leading to friendship or to enmity, among those marked out for good fortune or
1452a32	misfortune. The finest recognition is when it comes
1452a33	together with a reversal, as does the one in the Oedipus. There are
1452a34	other recognitions too: for there can be recognition of lifeless and
1452a35	random things †it is as has been said, it happens†, and of whether someone has done
1452a36	something or not. But the one that belongs most
1452a37	to the plot and most to the action is the one we have described:
1452a38	for such a recognition and reversal will bring pity
1452b1	or fear (and tragedy is taken to be an imitation of such actions), since
1452b2	misfortune and good fortune too will turn on such things.
1452b3	Since recognition is recognition of persons, some
1452b4	are of one person by the other only, when it is clear who the other
1452b5	is, while sometimes both must recognize each other:
1452b6	thus Iphigenia was recognized by Orestes
1452b7	from the sending of the letter, but for him to be recognized by
1452b8	Iphigenia another recognition was needed. These, then, are two
1452b9	parts of the plot, reversal
1452b10	and recognition; a third is suffering. Of these, reversal
1452b11	and recognition have been discussed; suffering is an action
1452b12	that destroys or gives pain, such as deaths in full view,
1452b13	and agonies and woundings and all such things.
