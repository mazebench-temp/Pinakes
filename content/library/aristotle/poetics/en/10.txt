1452a12	Of plots, some are simple and some complex; for
1452a13	the actions of which plots are imitations
1452a14	are themselves of these two kinds from the first. I call an action simple when,
1452a15	as it proceeds, continuous and one, as we have defined it, the change comes about without reversal
1452a16	or recognition; and complex,
1452a17	one in which it is with recognition or reversal or both that the
1452a18	change takes place. These must arise from the very structure
1452a19	of the plot, so that they follow from what has gone before
1452a20	either by necessity or by probability; for it makes a great
1452a21	difference whether things happen because of what went before or only after it.
