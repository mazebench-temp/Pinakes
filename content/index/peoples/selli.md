## The name

Σελλοί (Selloi) is found only in this verse. The letters could also be divided ἀμφὶ δέ σʼ Ἑλλοί, “and around you the Helli”, and the name was disputed in antiquity. [[strabo|Strabo]] reports that Pindar called them Ἑλλοί, while others supposed that Homer wrote Σελλοί, and that the ambiguous writing does not allow one to insist on either. Philochorus said that the region around Dodona was once called Hellopia, quoting Hesiod: “There is a land Hellopia, rich in grain and good in pasture, and at its edge Dodona is built”. Apollodorus reported the view that the name came from the marshes (ἕλη) around the sanctuary, but held that Homer wrote Selli, since he also names a river Selleis; Demetrius of Scepsis answered that Homer's Selleis was in Elis, not in Thesprotia (*Geography* 7.7.10; see [[pindar]], [[hesiod]] and [[selleis-ephyra]]; the Apollodorus whom Strabo cites is the grammarian of Athens, not the author of the Library).

## Description

They are the god's ὑποφῆται, “interpreters”, those who speak for him (ὑπό + φημί, “speak”), a word found only here in Homer. Strabo took it to mean that men gave the oracles at Dodona at first, before three old women were appointed, “for he calls them ὑποφῆται, among whom the prophets too might be ranked” (*Geography* 7.7.12; see [[divination]]). They are ἀνιπτόποδες, “with unwashed feet” (ἀ- “not” + νίπτω “wash” + πούς “foot”), and χαμαιεῦναι, “sleeping on the ground” (χαμαί “on the ground” + εὐνή “bed”), both found only here (see [[with-unwashed-feet]]); the Odyssey calls swine χαμαιευνάδες, “that sleep on the ground” (*Odyssey* 10.243, 14.15). Homer does not say why they lived so. Strabo read their way of life as proof that the people around the sanctuary were barbarians (*Geography* 7.7.10), and reports that some called the interpreters τομοῦροι, from Mount Tomarus above the sanctuary, and wanted to read the word in the Odyssey, where Amphinomus would ask the will of Zeus before the suitors kill Telemachus (*Geography* 7.7.11; *Odyssey* 16.402–403).

## Outside Homer

Strabo counts the Selli among the peoples that Homer names in order, “the Dolopians and the Selli around Dodona as far as the Achelous” (*Geography* 1.2.20). [[aristotle|Aristotle]] places ancient Hellas in the same country: the flood of Deucalion's time struck “the land around Dodona and the Achelous”, “for the Selli lived there, and those who were then called Graeci and now Hellenes” (*Meteorology* 1.14, 352a32–b3). The Suda quotes Homer's ἀνιπτόποδες, χαμαιεῦναι in its entry on ἀνίπτοις ποσίν, “with unwashed feet”, which it explains as “unprepared” (Suda α 2477).
