Lampsacus stood on the Asian shore of the Hellespont. Diogenes Laertius tells that Anaxagoras, leaving Athens, lived there until his death and was buried with honor by the city, which set up an altar to Mind and Truth, and that the children were given a holiday each year in the month of his death (2.14–15). See [[anaxagoras]].
