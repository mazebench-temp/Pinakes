Dolon names the Leleges among the allies camped “toward the sea” (10.428–429; see [[trojan-allies]]). They are not in the Trojan Catalogue (see [[trojan-catalogue]]), but later books place them in the southern Troad. Aeneas says that when Achilles sacked Lyrnessus and Pedasus, Athena urged him on “to kill Leleges and Trojans” (20.90–96). Lycaon's mother was a daughter of old Altes, “who rules over the war-loving Leleges, holding steep Pedasus on the Satnioeis”, and Achilles kills both of her sons (21.85–91; see [[lycaon-son-of-priam]], [[pedasus-on-the-satnioeis]] and [[satnioeis]]).

## Outside Homer

Herodotus says that the Carians were called Leleges when they lived in the islands as subjects of Minos (1.171.2; see [[carians]]). Strabo reports that some thought the two peoples one, and that tombs and deserted forts of the Leleges were shown all over Caria (7.7.2, 13.1.59); Homer, he notes, names them separately (13.1.58). Driven from the Troad by Achilles, the Leleges were said to have settled round the later Halicarnassus (13.1.58–59). Strabo also quotes Hesiod's verses on the Leleges as “peoples picked out of the earth” (λεκτοὺς ἐκ γαίης λάους) for Deucalion, and finds there the origin of the name (7.7.2; see [[hesiod]]).
