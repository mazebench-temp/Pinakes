Πυγμαῖοι, from πυγμή ("fist"), a measure of length from the elbow to the knuckles: the "fist-high" men. The Iliad names them once, in the simile that opens Book 3. The Trojans come on with clamor like the cranes that flee the winter and fly to the streams of Oceanus, "bringing slaughter and doom to the Pygmy men" (ἀνδράσι Πυγμαίοισι φόνον καὶ κῆρα φέρουσαι). The battle is the cranes' "evil strife", fought at dawn, or perhaps in the air (ἠέριαι, 3.7). Like the Ethiopians, the Pygmies live at the edge of the world ([[ethiopians]]). See [[cranes]] and [[cranes-and-pygmies]].

## Outside Homer

[[aristotle|Aristotle]] placed them in the marshes above Egypt where the Nile rises, and insisted that the story was no fable: there really is a race of small men there, and their horses are small too (*History of Animals* 8.12, 597a4–9; see [[aristotle-pygmies]]). The battle of the Pygmies and the cranes is painted on the foot of the François Vase, an Athenian vase of about 570 BC.
