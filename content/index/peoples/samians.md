Samos was one of the great cities of Ionia. Under the tyrant Polycrates in the sixth century BCE it built the temple of Hera and the tunnel of Eupalinus that Herodotus admired (3.60). A member of the Athenian alliance, it revolted in 440 BCE and was reduced by Pericles after a siege of nine months (Thucydides 1.115–117). In 365 the Athenian general Timotheus took the island from a Persian garrison (Isocrates, *Antidosis* 111), and Athenian settlers, cleruchs, were sent to hold the land; the Samians recovered it only after Alexander's death.
