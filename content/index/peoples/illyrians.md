The Illyrians were neighbors and enemies of Macedon; Philip II, Aristotle's patron, fought them repeatedly. In the *Poetics* their custom answers a critic of Homer: the spears of Diomedes' companions stand “upright on their butt-spikes”, “for that was the custom then, as it is still now among the Illyrians” (1461a1–4; see [[aristotle-upright-spears]] and [[illyria]]).
