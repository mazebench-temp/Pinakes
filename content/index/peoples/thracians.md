These are the Thracians of the European shore of the Hellespont, the Chersonese and its hinterland. The strait bounds them as seen from Troy: it "encloses" them (ἐντὸς ἐέργει). Other Thracian peoples appear too: the [[Cicones]] (2.846), and, in Book 10, King Rhesus with his newly arrived Thracians and his splendid horses, whom Diomedes and Odysseus kill and steal in the night (10.434–502). Thrace is where the North and West winds come from (9.5), and ships bring Thracian wine to the Achaean camp every day (9.71–72). The singer [[Thamyris]], whom the Muses silenced, was a Thracian (2.595).

## Outside Homer

"The Thracians are the largest people in the world after the Indians," Herodotus says. "If they had one ruler, or were of one mind, they would be invincible" (5.3).
