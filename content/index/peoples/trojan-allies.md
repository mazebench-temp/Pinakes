ἐπίκουρος means "helper, ally", one who comes to the aid of others in war. The Trojans could not hold out alone. Agamemnon admits as much in his speech to the army: the Achaeans outnumber the Trojans of the city more than ten to one, but allies from many cities keep him from sacking Troy.

## Elsewhere in Homer

The allies' place in Troy is uneasy. Sarpedon tells Hector that the allies do the fighting while Priam's sons hang back (5.471–492). Hector tells the allies that he did not summon them for their numbers but to defend the Trojans' wives and children, and that he is wearing out his own people with the gifts and food he gives them (17.220–232). Dolon lists where each people is camped (10.428–434). New allies keep arriving: Rhesus and his Thracians in Book 10, Othryoneus of Cabesus, who came for the hand of Cassandra (13.363–369), and Asteropaeus with his Paeonians only eleven days before his death (21.155–156). After the Iliad the *Aethiopis* brought the Amazon Penthesilea and Memnon with his Ethiopians, and the *Little Iliad* Eurypylus son of Telephus.
