ἐπίκουρος means "helper, ally", one who comes to the aid of others in war. The Trojans could not hold out alone. Agamemnon admits as much in his speech to the army: the Achaeans outnumber the Trojans of the city more than ten to one, but allies from many cities keep him from sacking Troy.

{{quote:809ad908-2b5f-522f-b7a2-1865426b47a4}}

At the end of the book Iris names the allies' difficulty. They are many, and "the tongues of men scattered far and wide are many and various" (2.804), so each leader must command his own people. The Trojans and their allies then form up at [[Batieia]] (2.815), and the Trojan catalogue lists them ([[trojan-catalogue]]). After [[Hector]]'s Trojans and [[Aeneas]]' Dardanians come the other towns of the Troad and the Hellespont, down to the [[Pelasgians]] of Larisa. Then the list runs out from Troy along four lines: west into Europe ([[Thracians]], [[Cicones]], [[Paeonians]]), east along the Black Sea coast ([[Paphlagonians]], [[Halizones]]), inland ([[Mysians]], [[Phrygians]]), and south ([[Maeonians]], [[Carians]], [[Lycians]]). Each line ends with a people who come "from far away" (τηλόθεν or τῆλε: 2.849, 2.857, 2.863, 2.877). Unlike the Achaean catalogue, the Trojan catalogue gives no numbers of ships or men.

## Elsewhere in Homer

Priam himself once went to war as an ally, fighting beside the Phrygians against the Amazons (3.184–189). The allies' place in Troy is uneasy. Sarpedon tells Hector that the allies do the fighting while Priam's sons hang back (5.471–492). Hector tells the allies that he did not summon them for their numbers but to defend the Trojans' wives and children, and that he is wearing out his own people with the gifts and food he gives them (17.220–232). Dolon lists where each people is camped (10.428–434). New allies keep arriving: Rhesus and his Thracians in Book 10, Othryoneus of Cabesus, who came for the hand of Cassandra (13.363–369), and Asteropaeus with his Paeonians only eleven days before his death (21.155–156). After the Iliad the *Aethiopis* brought the Amazon Penthesilea and Memnon with his Ethiopians, and the *Little Iliad* Eurypylus son of Telephus.
