## Etymology

Ἀχαιοί (Achaioi). The name's origin is unknown. Since the 1920s it has been compared with *Ahhiyawa*, a powerful kingdom "across the sea" in Hittite texts of the 14th–13th centuries BCE, which many scholars identify with a Mycenaean Greek state. The identification is widely but not universally accepted.

## Three names

Homer uses three names for the Greek army without distinction of meaning:

- **Achaeans** (Ἀχαιοί), by far the most frequent, in more than forty lines of the Iliad's first book;
- **Argives** (Ἀργεῖοι), after Argos: [[argives]];
- **Danaans** (Δαναοί), after Danaus: [[danaans]].

Meter often decides which name is used. The Greeks called themselves Hellenes only later. In the Iliad, Hellenes are one people in Achilles' corner of Thessaly (2.683–684). The women of the Achaeans have names of their own, Ἀχαιϊάδες and Ἀχαιαί (see [[achaean-women]]).
