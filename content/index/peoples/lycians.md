{{quote:5ea519a8-cd28-560d-8c06-98a8618dbf0c}}

The Lycians come last in the Trojan catalogue and from farthest away. They are the only allies whose name enters the Trojans' own rallying cry, "Trojans and Lycians and Dardanians who fight hand to hand" (8.173 and elsewhere). At home their kings are honored almost as gods, with the best seats, meat and full cups, and a great estate by the Xanthus; Sarpedon says that such honor must be earned in the front line (12.310–321). Their royal family goes back to the Corinthian hero Bellerophon, who came to Lycia with a message meant to kill him, survived every task the king set him, and married the king's daughter (6.168–195).

## Outside Homer

The Lycians called themselves Termilae, Herodotus says, and he reports a tradition that they came from Crete with Sarpedon, the brother of Minos (1.173). Their own inscriptions write the name Trm̃mili. Lycian is an Anatolian language, close to Luwian, known from inscriptions and coins of the fifth and fourth centuries BC. Hittite texts of the Late Bronze Age speak of the Lukka lands and the Lukka people in southwest Anatolia, generally connected with the later Lycians. When the Persians under Harpagus took the city of Xanthus, its men burned their women, children and goods on the acropolis and died fighting (Herodotus 1.176).
