## Outside Homer

Achilles brought fifty ships of Myrmidons, Hellenes and Achaeans from Phthia (Iliad 2.681–685). Later legend explained the name from μύρμηκες, "ants": Zeus turned the ants of Aegina into men for his son Aeacus, Achilles' grandfather (Ovid, *Metamorphoses* 7.614–660).

Strabo thinks it likely that the name belonged to all the men under Achilles and Patroclus, who had followed Peleus when he fled from Aegina, while all the people of Phthia were called Achaeans. In the same passage he takes the Spercheius for the river of Achilles' own country, since Achilles grew his hair for it and Menesthius, one of his captains, was called the son of the Spercheius and of Achilles' sister (*Geography* 9.5.9; see [[strabo]], [[spercheius]] and [[menesthius-son-of-spercheius]]).
