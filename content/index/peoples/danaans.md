## Etymology

Δαναοί (Danaoi). Greek tradition derived the name from Danaus, a king of Argos who came from Egypt with his fifty daughters. Egyptian texts of the 14th century BCE mention a land *Tanaja*, which some have connected with the Danaans. Both explanations are uncertain. Merry reports two Greek derivations: the expression δίψιον Ἄργος, “thirsty Argos”, suggested to K. O. Müller a connection with δανός, “dry”, while others gave the name the contrary sense, as if from δα-, “very”, and να-, “flow” (νάω) (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on *Odyssey* 8.578). The Iliad calls Argos πολυδίψιον, “thirsty” (*Iliad* 4.171; see [[argos]]).
