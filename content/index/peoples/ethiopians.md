## Etymology

Αἰθίοπες (Aithiopes), traditionally αἴθω (burn) + ὤψ (face): "burnt-faced", the people nearest the sun.

## In Book 1

{{quote:9d8e7067-6d84-59d8-b0ca-65fb72d34720}}

ἀμύμονες: "blameless". The Ethiopians are the gods' favorite hosts, honored with their presence at sacrifices. Their remoteness explains why Zeus cannot hear Thetis at once. See [[zeus-among-the-ethiopians]].

## Outside Homer

The Odyssey divides them in two, "some where Hyperion sets, and some where he rises" (1.22–24), and sends Poseidon to feast with them. Memnon, son of Eos, led an Ethiopian army to Troy in the *Aethiopis*, which is named after them. Herodotus later placed them south of Egypt.
