## Etymology

Αἰθίοπες (Aithiopes), traditionally αἴθω (burn) + ὤψ (face): "burnt-faced", the people nearest the sun.

## Descriptions

The Iliad calls them ἀμύμονες, “blameless”, and places them by Oceanus (1.423, 23.205–206). The Odyssey adds that they are ἔσχατοι ἀνδρῶν, “the farthest of men”, and live divided in two, “some where Hyperion sets and some where he rises” (*Odyssey* 1.23–24). In both poems the gods go to them for their hecatombs: Zeus and all the gods to a feast (1.423–424; see [[zeus-among-the-ethiopians]]), Iris to share in their sacrifices (23.205–207), and Poseidon to a hecatomb of bulls and rams (*Odyssey* 1.22–26).

## Outside Homer

Memnon, son of Eos, led an Ethiopian army to Troy in the *Aethiopis*, which is named after them. Herodotus later placed them south of Egypt, and he counts two kinds in Xerxes' army: the Ethiopians above Egypt, with the woolliest hair of all men, and the straight-haired Ethiopians from the sunrise, armed like the Indians (7.69–70).
