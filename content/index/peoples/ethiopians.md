## Etymology

Αἰθίοπες (Aithiopes), traditionally αἴθω (burn) + ὤψ (face): "burnt-faced", the people nearest the sun.

{{notes}}

## Elsewhere in Homer

The Odyssey divides them in two, “some where Hyperion sets, and some where he rises”, and sends Poseidon to feast with them on a hecatomb of bulls and rams (*Odyssey* 1.22–26).

## Outside Homer

Memnon, son of Eos, led an Ethiopian army to Troy in the *Aethiopis*, which is named after them. Herodotus later placed them south of Egypt.
