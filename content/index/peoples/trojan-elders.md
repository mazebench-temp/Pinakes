## Who they are

δημογέροντες = δῆμος ("people") + γέροντες ("old men"): "elders of the people". The word recurs of Ilus, "the ancient elder of the people", whose tomb stands in the plain (11.372). The elders sit "around Priam" (ἀμφὶ Πρίαμον), and the poem names seven: [[Panthous]], [[Thymoetes]], [[Lampus]], [[Clytius]], [[Hicetaon]], [[Ucalegon]] and [[Antenor]]. Lampus, Clytius and Hicetaon are Priam's brothers, sons of [[Laomedon]] (20.237–238). Ucalegon and Antenor are "both wise men" (3.148; see [[wise]]). They are "the leaders of the Trojans" (Τρώων ἡγήτορες, 3.153) as well as old men, the Trojan counterpart of the Achaean [[elders]].

## The cicadas

They are "done with war now because of their age, but excellent speakers" (ἀγορηταὶ ἐσθλοί), like cicadas that sit on a tree in the woods and send out their "lily-soft voice" (3.150–152; see [[like-cicadas]]). The simile compares their voices, though the point of "lily-like" (λειριόεσσα) is disputed. The old men who can no longer fight can still talk, and in Book 2 Iris mocked Priam for his love of endless words (2.796).

## Helen

When Helen comes to the tower they speak "softly" (ἦκα) to one another:

{{quote:3c4d36e9-c3f8-5218-8926-ac9bc6d674e3}}

οὐ νέμεσις, "there is no blame" ([[nemesis]]): no one could blame the two armies for suffering so long for such a woman, for she is "terribly like the immortal goddesses to look on". The next words take it back: let her go home all the same, and not be left a grief to us and to our children. The old men feel her beauty and count its cost in the same breath. Priam, who speaks next, does not blame her at all (3.162–165). See [[no-blame]], [[beauty-and-its-price]] and [[the-view-from-the-wall]].

## Outside Homer

Later readers took the old men's words as the highest praise of Helen's beauty, because they show it through its effect on those least likely to be moved. The painter Zeuxis was said to have written them under his picture of Helen (see [[zeuxis-helen]]), and Lessing made them his example of how poetry shows beauty by its effects (see [[lessing-helen]]).
