## Names and family

Μολίονε is a dual, “the two Moliones”. Homer gives no reason for the name; later writers call their mother Molione (see below). They are also “the two sons of Actor” (Ἀκτορίωνε, 11.750, 23.638; see [[actor-of-elis]]), but in the same breath Nestor calls Poseidon their father, “the wide-ruling earth-shaker” (11.751; see [[poseidon]], [[wide-ruling]] and [[earth-shaker]]).

Homer never names them in the scenes where they act. The names come from the Catalogue, where the Epeian leaders Amphimachus and Thalpius are “sons, one of Cteatus and one of Eurytus, both of Actor's line” (2.621; see [[cteatus]], [[eurytus-of-elis]], [[amphimachus]] and [[thalpius]]). Amphimachus is “son of Cteatus, of Actor's line” (13.185), and when he falls Poseidon is angry “for his grandson” (13.206–207), which agrees with Nestor: Poseidon was the father.

## In Homer

Nestor tells of them twice, and both times they are his opponents.

**The Epeian war.** They arm among the Epeians who come to besiege Thryoessa, “still boys, not yet well skilled in furious courage” (11.709–710; see [[furious]]). After Nestor has killed Mulius and taken fifty chariots, he would have killed them too, “had not their father, the wide-ruling earth-shaker, / saved them from the war, hiding them in thick mist” (11.750–752; see [[would-have-had-not]] and [[nestor-and-the-epeians]]). Other gods save their favorites in the same way: Aphrodite hides Paris in thick mist (ἐκάλυψε δʼ ἄρʼ ἠέρι πολλῇ, 3.381), and Apollo Hector and Agenor (20.444, 21.597); Hephaestus saves the son of his priest, “covering him in night” (5.23). Here, as when Aphrodite carries her son Aeneas out of the fighting (5.311–318), the god who saves is the parent.

**The chariot race at Buprasium.** At the funeral games of Amarynceus, when Nestor won every other contest, “the two sons of Actor” beat him in the chariot race “by their numbers”, for they were twins (δίδυμοι): one held the reins steadily, while the other urged the horses on with the whip (23.638–642; see [[amarynceus]] and [[buprasium]]). It is the only contest Nestor says he lost.

## Outside Homer

A scholium on 11.750 reports that Hesiod made Actor and Molione their parents in name and Poseidon their father in fact, and that Aristarchus, citing Hesiod, took the “twins” of 23.641 to mean not twins like the Dioscuri but a double being, two bodies grown together (Hesiod fr. 9 Evelyn-White; see [[aristarchus]] and [[hesiod]]). [[apollodorus|Apollodorus]] calls Eurytus and Cteatus συμφυεῖς, “grown together”, stronger than any men of their time, sons of Molione and Actor but said to be Poseidon's, and makes Actor a brother of Augeas (*Library* 2.7.2). In his account Augeas made them generals against Heracles; they fell on his army while he was sick and killed many, and Heracles later lay in wait for them at Cleonae and killed them, when the Eleans sent them to the Isthmian festival (2.7.2). Pindar tells the same ambush: Heracles killed “Poseidon's son, blameless Cteatus”, and Eurytus “in the thickets below Cleonae”, because “the overbearing Moliones” had once destroyed his army from Tiryns in the valleys of Elis; then he sacked the city of Augeas (*Olympian* 10.24–38; see [[pindar]]).
