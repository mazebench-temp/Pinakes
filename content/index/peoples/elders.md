## Etymology

γέρων (“old man”), plural γέροντες. The word for the council they form is [[boule|βουλή]], and a man who sits in it is βουληφόρος (“one who sits in counsel”, 2.24).

## Who they are

{{quote:523fef7e-1e66-53ec-8643-dab33e11e5f5}}

When Agamemnon sacrifices before the first battle, the elders are [[Nestor]], [[Idomeneus]], the two Ajaxes ([[ajax]] and [[ajax-son-of-oileus]]), [[Diomedes]] and [[Odysseus]], with [[Menelaus]], who comes without being called. Three of them, Ajax, Idomeneus and Odysseus, are the men Agamemnon named in Book 1 as fit to command the ship to Chryse (1.145), and every one of them leads a contingent in the Catalogue. Nestor is the one Agamemnon “honored most of all the elders” (2.21), and Diomedes is young enough to be Nestor's son (9.57–58). The title goes with rank.

## The council

{{quote:3a8197f9-c1b1-520f-bb75-3ebe58e4fccd}}

The council meets before the assembly and apart from it, beside Nestor's ship. There Agamemnon tells the elders his dream, and his plan to test the army by ordering it home; they are to hold the men back (2.56–75). Only Nestor answers, and the council ends without debate (2.76–86). When the test goes wrong, it is not the council that saves the day but [[Odysseus]], sent by Athena. The crowd that runs for the ships is made up of those “who had not heard the council” (2.143), and Odysseus reminds the kings that “not all of us heard what he said in the council” (2.194). See [[assembly]] and [[the-test-of-the-army]].

## Elsewhere in Homer

The elders drink the “elders' wine” (γερούσιος οἶνος) at the kings' feasts (4.259–260), and Agamemnon feasts them in his hut before the embassy to Achilles (9.89–90). Troy has its elders too. They sit in Priam's assembly with the young men (2.789), and the old men of the city, too old for war but fine speakers, sit on the tower by the Scaean gates (3.146–153). On the Shield of Achilles the elders of a city sit in a sacred circle on polished stones and give judgment, holding heralds' scepters (18.503–505).

## Outside Homer

Sparta kept a council of elders, the γερουσία, into historical times (Plutarch, *Lycurgus* 6).
