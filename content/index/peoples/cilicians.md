Homer names them twice, both times in Andromache's account of her family. Eetion ruled at Thebe, “lord over the Cilician men” (Κιλίκεσσʼ ἄνδρεσσιν ἀνάσσων, 6.397), and Achilles “utterly sacked the well-peopled city of the Cilicians, high-gated Thebe” (6.415–416; see [[eetion]], [[andromache]] and [[sack-of-thebe]]). They have no place in the Trojan Catalogue; their city had fallen before the poem begins (see [[trojan-catalogue]]).

Thebe lay near Adramyttium, at the head of the gulf south of Ida, in what Herodotus still calls “the plain of Thebe” on Xerxes' road to Ilium (7.42). The Cilicia of later times lay far away, on the southeast coast of Asia Minor; whether the two peoples were related is not known.
