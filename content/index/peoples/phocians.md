Their towns are [[Cyparissus]], rocky [[Pytho]], holy [[Crisa]], [[Daulis]] and [[Panopeus]], [[Anemorea]] and [[Hyampolis]], the land along the river [[Cephisus]], and [[Lilaea]] at its springs (2.519–523). Pytho is Delphi, which lay in Phocian territory.

{{quote:e3cfd78c-ed60-5e0d-80d0-a30708f8c2bd}}

They are the only contingent whose place in the line of battle the Catalogue gives: their leaders set the Phocian ranks in order close beside the [[Boeotians]], on the left (2.525–526).

## Elsewhere in Homer

Hector kills two Phocian leaders called Schedius: the son of Perimedes (15.515–516) and the son of Iphitus, "the best of the Phocians", who lived in Panopeus (17.306–311). See [[schedius]].

## Outside Homer

Control of Delphi drew the Phocians into war more than once. In the Third Sacred War (356–346 BC) they seized the sanctuary and paid mercenaries from its treasures until Philip II of Macedon defeated them. Before the Persian invasion they had beaten the Thessalians near Hyampolis (Herodotus 8.27–28).
