The Carians are the only people in the Iliad whose language is singled out: they are [[barbarophonos|βαρβαρόφωνοι]], "of barbarous speech". Their land is the coast opposite Samos: [[Miletus]], the wooded mountain of the [[Phthires]], the streams of the [[Maeander]] and the heights of [[Mycale]]. In historical times this was Ionian Greek country. Homer knows Miletus only as a Carian town. Their leaders are the two sons of [[Nomion]], one of whom went to war wearing gold "like a girl" and was killed by Achilles in the river ([[nastes]], [[the-river-battle-foretold]]).

## Elsewhere in Homer

Dolon places the Carians by the sea, with the Paeonians, Leleges, Caucones and Pelasgians (10.428–429).

## Outside Homer

Herodotus says that the Carians had once lived in the islands as subjects of Minos, under the name of Leleges, and that the Greeks learned from them to put crests on helmets, devices on shields and handles inside them (1.171). When the Athenians purified Delos, Thucydides reports, more than half of the graves they opened proved to be Carian, known by the arms buried with the dead and by the manner of burial (1.8.1). The Ionians who settled Miletus killed the Carian men and married their women (Herodotus 1.146). Carians served abroad as mercenaries, and with Ionians they helped the Egyptian king Psammetichus to his throne (Herodotus 2.152–154). Their language, written in an alphabet of its own from the seventh century BC, was largely deciphered at the end of the twentieth century. It is an Anatolian language, related to Lycian and Luwian. Hittite texts of the Late Bronze Age name a land Karkiša, often identified with Caria.
