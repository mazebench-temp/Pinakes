## The name

Φθῖοι, “Phthians”, are the men of Phthia, Achilles' homeland in southern Thessaly (see [[phthia]]). Homer uses the name only in this scene, three times (13.686, 13.693, 13.699).

## In Homer

They are one of the five peoples who hold Hector off by the ships of Ajax and Protesilaus, where the wall was lowest: “There the Boeotians and the Ionians with trailing tunics, / the Locrians and the Phthians and the shining Epeians, / hardly held him off from the ships” (13.685–687; see [[ionians]] and [[the-battle-at-the-ships]]). Medon commands the men of Philoctetes, from Methone, Thaumacia, Meliboea and Olizon, since Philoctetes lies wounded on Lemnos (2.716–728; see [[medon]] and [[philoctetes]]); Podarces commands the men of his dead brother Protesilaus, from Phylace, Pyrasus, Iton, Antron and Pteleus (2.695–710; see [[podarces]] and [[protesilaus]]). The Catalogue calls neither contingent Phthian. It gives Phthia to Achilles, whose men are those “who held Phthia and Hellas of the lovely women” and were called Myrmidons, Hellenes and Achaeans (2.683–684; see [[myrmidons]] and [[hellenes]]). While Achilles keeps to his hut, they do not fight.

## Outside Homer

Strabo answers the puzzle from this passage. Those under Achilles, Protesilaus and Philoctetes alike are called Phthians, he says, “and the poet is witness to this”: having named Achilles' men in the Catalogue as those “who held Phthia”, in the battle at the ships (ἐν τῇ ἐπὶ ναυσὶ μάχῃ) he keeps them at rest by the ships with Achilles, and has the men of Philoctetes fighting under Medon and those of Protesilaus under Podarces. Strabo quotes 13.685–686, 13.693 and 13.699–700, and adds that the men of Eurypylus, their neighbors, were perhaps called Phthians too (*Geography* 9.5.7; see [[strabo]], [[eurypylus]] and [[title-of-book-13]]). In historical times the Achaeans of Phthiotis, Ἀχαιοὶ οἱ Φθιῶται, lived in southern Thessaly; Herodotus names them among the peoples who gave earth and water to Xerxes (Herodotus 7.132.1; see [[herodotus]]).
