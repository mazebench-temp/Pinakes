αἰχμηταί, "spearmen" ([[spearmen]]). In the Iliad they are named once more, as the people of Mentes, whose likeness Apollo takes (17.73; see [[mentes]]).

In the Odyssey they are the first people Odysseus meets on his way home from Troy. He sacks their city, Ismarus, but the Cicones of the inland, who fight from chariots and on foot, come to its help and kill six men of each of his ships (*Odyssey* 9.39–61; see [[ismarus]] and [[the-raid-on-the-cicones]]); the wine taken from their city, and the wine that Maron, the priest of Apollo there, gave him, later make the Cyclops drunk (*Odyssey* 9.163–165, 9.196–211; see [[maron]]). In the summary of his wanderings that Odysseus gives Penelope they come first (*Odyssey* 23.310). The Cicones thus belong both to Troy's alliance and to the first adventure of the homecoming.

## Outside Homer

Merry places them on the south coast of Thrace, between the rivers Hebrus and Lissus (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on *Odyssey* 9.39). Herodotus says that the coast beside Doriscus, the plain on the Hebrus where Xerxes numbered his army, belonged of old to the Cicones, and that the land on the Lisus farther west, called Briantice in his day, was by right theirs too; and he names them among the Thracian peoples through whose land Xerxes marched (*Histories* 7.59.2, 7.108.3, 7.110; see [[herodotus]]). Strabo names Xanthea, Maroneia and Ismarus as the cities of the Cicones (*Geography* 7 fr. 44; see [[strabo]] and [[ismarus]]), and calls Orpheus “the Cicon”, a wizard who lived at Pimpleia, a village near Dium under Olympus (*Geography* 7 fr. 18).
