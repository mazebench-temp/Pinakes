Ἀμαζόνες ἀντιάνειραι: ἀντιάνειρα = ἀντί ("against, a match for") + ἀνήρ ("man"): "the equals of men", or perhaps "who fight against men" ([[equals-of-men]]). Priam fought against them as an ally of the Phrygians on the Sangarius (3.184–189; see [[priam-and-the-amazons]]).

{{notes}}

## Elsewhere in Homer

The Iliad names them once more, in the same words: Bellerophon killed "the Amazons, the equals of men" as the third of the tasks set him by the king of Lycia (6.186). The poem itself never brings an Amazon to Troy.

## Outside Homer

Ancient readers took Myrine, whose tomb stands in the plain before Troy (2.814), for an Amazon (see [[myrine]]). The *Aethiopis*, the poem that continued the Iliad, began with the arrival of the Amazon Penthesilea, a daughter of Ares, to help the Trojans. Achilles killed her at the height of her valor, and when Thersites mocked him for his supposed love for her, Achilles killed him too ([[proclus|Proclus]]; see [[thersites]]). Heracles' labor for the belt of their queen Hippolyta is told by Apollodorus (*Library* 2.5.9). Herodotus tells how Amazons captured at the Thermodon married young Scythians and became the Sauromatae (4.110–117). Battles of Greeks and Amazons were a favorite subject of Greek sculpture and vase painting.
