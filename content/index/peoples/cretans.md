In the Iliad the Cretans are always Idomeneus' men, and they fight under him and Meriones in the battle by the ships in Book 13.

The Odyssey knows Crete as an island of many peoples and languages, where Achaeans lived beside Eteocretans, Cydonians, Dorians and Pelasgians (*Odyssey* 19.175–177). Odysseus passes himself off as a Cretan in his lying tales (*Odyssey* 13.256–286, 14.199–359, 19.165–202; see [[crete]] and [[the-tale-to-eumaeus]]), and later Greeks gave the Cretans a name for lying. The half-line "Cretans are always liars" appears in Callimachus, who explains it by their claim to show the tomb of Zeus (*Hymn to Zeus* 8). The Letter to Titus quotes the whole line as the saying of "a prophet of their own" (Titus 1:12), traditionally Epimenides of Crete. Cretan archers later served as mercenaries all over the Greek world, and at the funeral games of Patroclus the Cretan Meriones wins the archery (23.850–883).
