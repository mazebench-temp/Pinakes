ἀγκυλότοξοι = ἀγκύλος ("curved, bent") + τόξον ("bow"): "with curved bows", perhaps the recurved bow. Dolon uses the same word of them (10.428). Elsewhere, though, they fight otherwise. Pyraechmes brings Paeonian "chariot-fighters" (ἱπποκορυσταί, 16.287), and Asteropaeus, who arrives late in the war, leads Paeonians "with long spears" (21.155). Asteropaeus, grandson of the river [[Axius]], is the Paeonian the Iliad knows best. He faces Achilles with a spear in each hand, wounds him, and dies in the river (21.139–204).

## Outside Homer

Herodotus tells how Darius saw a Paeonian woman who could carry water, lead a horse and spin all at once, and had the Paeonians of the Strymon moved to Asia (5.12–15). The Paeonians told him that they were colonists of the Teucrians from Troy (5.13). Later they formed a kingdom north of Macedonia, which Philip II brought under Macedonian control.
