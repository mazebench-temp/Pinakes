## In Homer

The Cydonians are named twice. They live by the river where some of Menelaus' ships were driven, on the way home from Troy (*Odyssey* 3.291–292; see [[iardanus-in-crete]] and [[crete]]), and they are one of the peoples among whom, as Odysseus tells Penelope in his Cretan tale, the languages of Crete are mixed: Achaeans, Eteocretans, Cydonians, Dorians and Pelasgians (*Odyssey* 19.175–177; see [[achaeans]], [[dorians]] and [[pelasgians]]). Merry notes that Homer does not name their city (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on *Odyssey* 3.292).

## Outside Homer

Strabo quotes the verses on the peoples of Crete and reports that Staphylus placed the Dorians in the east of the island, the Cydonians in the west and the Eteocretans in the south; the Eteocretans and the Cydonians, he thinks, were probably natives, and the others newcomers (*Geography* 10.4.6). Cydonia was one of the three greatest cities of Crete, with Cnossus and Gortyn, and whichever of the two others it joined had the upper hand (10.4.7, 10.4.11; see [[cnossus]] and [[gortyn]]). It lay by the sea, facing Laconia, about eight hundred stadia from both Cnossus and Gortyn (10.4.13). Herodotus says that Samian exiles founded Cydonia, though they had not sailed for that purpose, prospered there for five years, and built the temples that stood there in his day, until the Aeginetans defeated them at sea and enslaved them (Herodotus 3.59; see [[samians]]). Cydonia is modern Chania.
