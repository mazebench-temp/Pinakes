Achilles' men "were called Myrmidons and Hellenes and Achaeans". The first name is his people's own (see [[myrmidons]]), and the last is the name of the whole army (see [[achaeans]]). Hellenes lies in between: the people of [[Hellas]], one region of his kingdom. Homer calls the army as a whole Achaeans, Argives or Danaans, never Hellenes. The one hint of the later use is Πανέλληνες, "all the Hellenes", which occurs once (2.530; see [[panhellenes]]).

Thucydides drew the historical conclusion. Homer, he says, lived long after the Trojan War, yet he nowhere calls the Greeks as a whole Hellenes, but only Achilles' men from Phthiotis, "who were in fact the first Hellenes" (1.3.3; see [[thucydides-hellenes]]). The Greeks traced the name to Hellen, son of Deucalion, whose sons Dorus, Xuthus and Aeolus were the ancestors of the Greek peoples (Hesiodic *Catalogue of Women*, fr. 9 Merkelbach–West). Thucydides says that Hellen and his sons grew powerful in Phthiotis (1.3.2).
