{{quote:048f277c-3b7a-5164-9bb1-34b00e23fa0a}}

The Catalogue of Ships begins with the Boeotians and gives them more of everything than any other people: five commanders, twenty-nine towns in thirteen lines (2.496–508), and the largest crews in the fleet.

The towns are [[Hyria]] and rocky [[Aulis]]; [[Schoenus]], [[Scolus]] and [[Eteonus]]; [[Thespeia]], [[Graea]] and [[Mycalessus]]; [[Harma]], [[Eilesium]] and [[Erythrae]]; [[Eleon]], [[Hyle]] and [[Peteon]]; [[Ocalea]] and [[Medeon]]; [[Copae]], [[Eutresis]] and [[Thisbe]]; [[Coronea]] and [[Haliartus]]; [[Plataea]] and [[Glisas]]; [[Hypothebae]] and [[Onchestus]]; [[Arne]], [[Midea]], [[Nisa]] and [[Anthedon]]. The order is not a route. It starts on the strait facing Euboea, crosses the region more than once, and ends at Anthedon on the same coast. Thebes itself is missing: the list has only Hypothebae, "Lower Thebes". [[Orchomenus]] and [[Aspledon]], in the northwest, form a contingent of their own (2.511–516).

{{quote:5ff32a2c-3e15-5aca-99e3-15b49977e04f}}

Fifty ships with a hundred and twenty young men in each make six thousand men. Thucydides took these crews and the fifty men in each of Philoctetes' ships (2.719–720) as the largest and the smallest in the fleet (1.10.4). In the line of battle the [[Phocians]] stand on the Boeotians' left (2.526).

## Elsewhere in Homer

The Boeotians fight beside the Ionians, Locrians, Phthians and Epeians when Hector attacks the ships (13.685–686). Three of their five leaders are killed: [[Prothoenor]] (14.449–457), [[Arcesilaus]] (15.329–330) and [[Clonius]] (15.340). [[Peneleos]] and [[Leitus]] are wounded in the fight over Patroclus' body (17.597–604).

## Outside Homer

Thucydides says that the Boeotians were driven out of [[Arne]] in Thessaly by the Thessalians sixty years after the fall of Troy and settled the land "now called Boeotia, formerly Cadmeis"; a part of them had lived there before, and it was these who sailed to Troy (1.12.3). In antiquity the whole Catalogue went by their name, the *Boeotia* (see [[the-boeotia]]). Why they come first is disputed. The simplest answer is that the fleet gathered at Aulis, on Boeotian soil (2.303); others have seen in it a Boeotian origin for the Catalogue itself.
