The Dardanians take their name from Dardanus, son of Zeus, who founded Dardanie on the slopes of Ida "when sacred Ilios had not yet been built in the plain" (20.215–218). [[Aeneas]], of Dardanus' line through Assaracus and Anchises, leads them, with two sons of Antenor. They are the second contingent of the Trojan catalogue, after Hector's Trojans, and they come third in the Trojans' rallying cry: "Trojans and Lycians and Dardanians who fight hand to hand" (8.173 and elsewhere). Priam himself is Δαρδανίδης, "descendant of Dardanus" (3.303), and one of Troy's gates is the Dardanian gate (5.789).

At 2.701 a "Dardanian man" kills Protesilaus, the first of the Achaeans to leap ashore. Here the word seems to mean no more than "Trojan", and the killer is not named. See [[the-death-of-protesilaus]].

## Outside Homer

The town of Dardanus on the Hellespont, south of Abydos, kept the name, and through it the strait is now called the Dardanelles. Herodotus lists it among the towns the Persians took in the Ionian Revolt (5.117).
