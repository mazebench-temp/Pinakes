μεγάθυμοι "great-hearted" ([[great-hearted]]). The Cephallenians are all of [[Odysseus]]' subjects: the men of [[Ithaca]] and Mount [[Neritum]], of [[Crocyleia]] and rugged [[Aegilips]], of [[Zacynthus]] and [[Samos]], and those who held "the mainland" and "the coast across from them" (2.635).

{{quote:9c03507b-9887-5f75-ba10-30dc72cbc764}}

Their twelve ships are "red-painted" (μιλτοπάρῃοι; [[red-painted]]), and twelve is also the number of Odysseus' ships in the Odyssey (Od. 9.159). It is one of the smaller contingents, the same size as the one from Salamis, for one of the greatest leaders.

## Elsewhere in Homer

When Agamemnon reviews the army, "the ranks of the Cephallenians" stand around Odysseus (4.330–331). In the Odyssey Odysseus has herds of cattle "in the land of the Cephallenians" (Od. 20.210), and Laertes, at the end of the poem, remembers leading "the Cephallenians" to take Nericus on the mainland (Od. 24.377–378).

## Outside Homer

The name survived in Cephallenia (Kefalonia), the largest of the Ionian islands, which Homer calls Samos or Same.
