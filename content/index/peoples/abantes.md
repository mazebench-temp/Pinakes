They hold [[Euboea]] and its towns (2.536–539), and they follow [[Elephenor]], son of Chalcodon.

## Descriptions

- μένεα πνείοντες "breathing fury" ([[breathing-fury]]).
- θοοί "swift" ([[swift]]).
- ὄπιθεν κομόωντες "their hair long at the back" = ὄπιθεν (behind) + κομάω (wear long hair). The Achaeans in general are "long-haired" (κάρη κομόωντες; [[long-haired]]); the Abantes let their hair grow only behind.
- αἰχμηταί "spearmen" ([[spearmen]]), "eager with outstretched ash spears to tear the corselets around the chests of their enemies" (2.543–544): they fight at close range with the thrusting spear.
- μεγάθυμοι "great-hearted" ([[great-hearted]]), in the line that names their leader (2.541).

## Outside Homer

Plutarch explains the haircut: the Abantes were close fighters, and they cut their hair short in front so that no enemy could seize it; Theseus, he says, had his hair cut the same way (*Theseus* 5). He quotes Archilochus on the "spear-famed lords of Euboea", who fight with swords, not bows (fr. 3 West). Later writers derived the people's name from a hero Abas.
