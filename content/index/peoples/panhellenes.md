In Homer, Hellas and the Hellenes are a region and a people of southern Thessaly, subjects of Achilles (2.683–684; see [[hellas]] and [[hellenes]]). "Panhellenes and Achaeans" is probably a way of saying "all the Greeks", the northerners and the rest together.

## Outside Homer

Thucydides observed that Homer, though he lived long after the Trojan War, nowhere calls the whole army Hellenes, but only the followers of Achilles from Phthia; he calls the rest Danaans, Argives and Achaeans (1.3.3). Hesiod uses the word of all the Greeks: in winter the sun shines more slowly "on the Panhellenes" (*Works and Days* 528). Because the word looks late, some ancient critics suspected the lines about Ajax.
