The Iliad names Corinth once in the Catalogue, among Agamemnon's towns (2.570), and once more as the home of Euchenor, son of the seer Polyidus, whom Paris kills in Book 13 (13.663–672; see [[euchenor]] and [[polyidus-the-seer]]). Glaucus, the Lycian ally of Troy, traces his family to Sisyphus and Bellerophon, who lived in “Ephyre, in a corner of horse-pasturing Argos” (6.152–211; see [[glaucus]] and [[bellerophon]]), and Ephyre was taken to be the old name of Corinth. Pindar's ode for a Corinthian victor tells how the Corinthians were seen on both sides of the war at Troy (*Olympian* 13.55–62).
