Dolon names the Caucones among the allies camped “toward the sea” (10.428–429; see [[trojan-allies]]). The Trojan Catalogue does not name them (see [[trojan-catalogue]]). When Poseidon lifts Aeneas out of his fight with Achilles, he sets him down “at the farthest edge of the battle, where the Caucones were arming for war” (20.325–329). Homer does not say where they came from. In the Odyssey, Athena in the likeness of Mentor leaves Nestor's house at Pylos to go “to the great-hearted Caucones”, where a debt is owed to her (*Odyssey* 3.366–368). Whether the poems mean one people is not known.

## Outside Homer

Herodotus says that some Ionian cities took as kings Pylian Caucones descended from Codrus (1.147.1), and that the Minyans drove the Caucones from the western Peloponnese (4.148.4). Strabo found Caucones in Triphylia and near Dyme in Elis, thought that Troy's allies came from Paphlagonia, where there were Cauconiatae (8.3.17; see [[paphlagonians]]), and says that in his day the race had died out everywhere (7.7.2, 12.3.9).
