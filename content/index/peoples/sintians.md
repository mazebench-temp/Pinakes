The Sintians are the only mortals in *Iliad* 1 who help a god. The Odyssey names them once more, when Ares tells Aphrodite that Hephaestus has gone “to Lemnos, to the Sintians of savage speech” (*Odyssey* 8.294). ἀγριόφωνοι, “of savage speech”, marks them as speakers of a foreign tongue, like the Carians, βαρβαρόφωνοι, “of barbarous speech” (2.867; see [[barbarophonos]]).

{{notes}}

## Outside Homer

Strabo took them for Thracians. The Sinti, a Thracian people, lived on the island of Lemnos, “hence Homer calls them Sinties” (*Geography* 7 fr. 46). Names change often among barbarians, he says: some of the Thracians were called Sinties, then Sinti, then Saii, among whom Archilochus says that he threw away his shield, and the same people are now called Sapaei; all of them lived around Abdera and the islands near Lemnos (*Geography* 12.3.20; see [[archilochus]]). Some, he reports, said that Samothrace was named after the Saii, Thracians who once lived there, whether they were the Sapaei or the Sinti, “whom the poet calls Sinties”, or others (*Geography* 10.2.17; see [[samothrace]] and [[strabo]]).
