ἡμιόνων γένος ἀγροτεράων, "the breed of wild mules": perhaps wild asses, or mules bred on the open range. The land is marked by its animals, as [[Alybe]] is by its silver (2.857). Mules were valued at Troy; the pair that draw Priam's wagon in the last book were a gift from the Mysians (24.277–278). See [[mules]]. The Greek leaves it open whether Pylaemenes himself or his whole contingent came "from the Eneti". The Eneti are not otherwise known in Paphlagonia, and later geographers found no trace of them there.

## Outside Homer

Livy begins his history with the story that after the fall of Troy Antenor led a band of Eneti, driven out of Paphlagonia after losing their king Pylaemenes at Troy, to the head of the Adriatic, where they became the Veneti (1.1.1–3). Strabo reports this view among others (*Geography*, Books 5 and 12). Herodotus knew Eneti on the Adriatic in Illyria, and says that they auctioned their brides as the Babylonians did (1.196). The Veneti of Italy were famous for their horses. Alcman's *Partheneion* compares a girl to "an Enetic racehorse" (fr. 1.50–51); whether he meant the Adriatic or the Paphlagonian Eneti is disputed.
