summary: The ἀοιδός, the singer who performs to the lyre at feasts and leads the songs of mourning. Homer's singers sing at a wedding on the shield of Achilles and at Menelaus' wedding feast, beside Hector's bier, in Odysseus' house, where Phemius sings to the suitors, and in Agamemnon's, where a singer guards Clytemnestra.

**In the Iliad.** On the shield of Achilles, at the dance, “among them a divine singer sang / to the lyre; and two tumblers among them” (*Iliad* 18.604–605; see [[the-singer-on-the-shield]], [[lyre]] and [[tumblers]]). At Hector's bier in Troy the Trojans “set singers beside him / to lead the dirges, and they the song of groaning / sang in lament, and the women groaned with them” (*Iliad* 24.720–722; see [[lament]]). The Thracian Thamyris, whom the Muses maimed for his boast, is a singer whose art the poem calls ἀοιδή, “singing” (*Iliad* 2.594–600; see [[thamyris]]).

**In the Odyssey.** Phemius sings to the suitors in Odysseus' house: “The renowned singer was singing to them, and they in silence / sat listening” (*Odyssey* 1.325–326; see [[phemius]]). Penelope weeps and speaks “to the divine singer” of the “deeds of men and gods, which singers make famous” (*Odyssey* 1.336–338; see [[kleos]]), and Telemachus defends him: “It is not the singers / who are to blame, but Zeus” (*Odyssey* 1.347–348). Agamemnon left a singer to guard his wife, and Aegisthus took him away to a desert island (*Odyssey* 3.267–271; see [[the-singer-of-clytemnestra]]). At Menelaus' wedding feast in Sparta the verse of the shield returns: “among them a divine singer sang / to the lyre” (*Odyssey* 4.17–18; see [[the-wedding-at-sparta]]). The Phaeacians' singer is Demodocus (see [[demodocus]]).
