Homer has two forms of the name, Καδμεῖοι (4.388, 4.391) and Καδμεΐωνες (4.385), and uses both only of the Thebans who lived before the Trojan War. They are the men feasting in the house of [[Eteocles]] whom Tydeus challenged and beat, alone among many (4.385–390; told again by Athena, 5.800–808); the fifty young men who lay in ambush for him on his way back, under [[Maeon]] and [[Polyphontes]] (4.391–398); and the Thebans whom Mecisteus beat at the funeral games of Oedipus (23.679–680). Their epithet κέντορες ἵππων (“goaders of horses”, 4.391; see [[goaders-of-horses]]) is given also to the Trojans (5.102).

In the Odyssey Oedipus “ruled over the Cadmeians” after he had unknowingly married his mother (Od. 11.271–276). Cadmus himself is named only once in Homer, as the father of Ino (Od. 5.333). See [[thebes]] and [[tydeus]].

## Outside Homer

Herodotus says that the Phoenicians who came to Greece with Cadmus brought the alphabet, and he saw “Cadmeian letters” on tripods in the temple of Ismenian Apollo at Thebes (5.58–59). The citadel of Thebes was called the Cadmea, and a victory that ruins the victors was a “Cadmeian victory” (Herodotus 1.166).
