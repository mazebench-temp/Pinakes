Homer names them once. Phoenix, received by Peleus as a son, lived “at the far edge of Phthia, ruling over the Dolopians” (9.484; see [[the-flight-of-phoenix]] and [[peleus]]). ἐσχατιή is the border land, the outermost part of a country. Strabo took the verse to show that the Dolopians lay at the farthest bounds of Phthia and had the same lord as the Phthians, Peleus (Strabo 9.5.11; see [[phthia]]). Homer brings no Dolopian contingent to Troy. In the Catalogue the men of Achilles' kingdom are “Myrmidons and Hellenes and Achaeans” (2.684), and when Phoenix fights he leads a company of the Myrmidons (16.196; see [[myrmidons]]).

## Outside Homer

Pindar, in lines that Strabo quotes, made Phoenix lead to Troy “a bold throng of Dolopians” armed with slings, and Strabo argues that Homer leaves their presence to be understood (Strabo 9.5.5). In historical times the Dolopes lived in the mountains of southwestern Thessaly, south of Pindus and above the Amphilochians of Acarnania (Strabo 9.5.3, 9.5.12, 10.2.1). They gave earth and water to Xerxes and sent men to his army (Herodotus 7.132, 7.185). Thucydides says that Scyros, the island where Achilles' son was reared, was inhabited by Dolopes when the Athenians took it, enslaved them and settled it themselves (Thucydides 1.98; see [[scyros]]).
