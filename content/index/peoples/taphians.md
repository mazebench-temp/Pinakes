## In Homer

The Taphians are islanders and seafarers. Their lord, as Athena describes him in the likeness of Mentes, sails over the sea to men of another tongue with a cargo of iron, to bring back bronze (*Odyssey* 1.180–184), and their standing epithet, φιλήρετμοι, “lovers of the oar”, is otherwise the Phaeacians' (*Odyssey* 5.386, 8.96, 11.349, 13.36; see [[phaeacians]]).

The rest of the Odyssey shows them at the trade and the robbery of the sea. Eumaeus bought his servant Mesaulius from Taphians with his own goods (*Odyssey* 14.449–452). The Phoenician woman who carried Eumaeus off as a child had herself been snatched from Sidon, as she came in from the fields, by Τάφιοι ληΐστορες ἄνδρες, “Taphian pirates”, and sold to his father's house (*Odyssey* 15.425–429). And Antinous' father Eupeithes once came to Odysseus' house as a fugitive, in fear of the people, who were angry because he had joined Taphian pirates in harrying the Thesprotians, the Ithacans' allies (*Odyssey* 16.424–427; see [[eupeithes]]). The same men are traders and raiders, and the house of their lord is bound to Odysseus' house by guest-friendship from their fathers' time (*Odyssey* 1.187–188). See [[trade]] and [[raiding]].

## Outside Homer

In later legend the Taphians and the Teleboans were the enemies of Amphitryon. In the *Shield of Heracles*, ascribed to Hesiod, Amphitryon may not enter Alcmene's bed until he has avenged her brothers and burned with fire the villages “of the heroic Taphians and Teleboans” (*Shield* 14–19). In Euripides' *Heracles* Megara greets him as the man who once sacked the city of the Taphians (*Heracles* 60), and the chorus remembers the sea-washed town of the Taphians that he sacked to avenge his wife's brothers (1078–1080). Strabo says that the islands of the Taphians were formerly of the Teleboans; Amphitryon, campaigning against them with Cephalus son of Deioneus, an exile from Athens, gave him their rule, but the poet puts them under Mentes and calls them pirates, as all the Teleboans were said to be (*Geography* 10.2.20; compare 10.2.24). The Suda gives Τάφιος both as the name of the people and as “the pirate” (τ 191). See [[amphitryon]] and [[taphos]].
