Hecuba's robes are “richly embroidered, the work of women of the Sidonians” (6.289–290). παμποίκιλος is “worked all over”, from ποικίλος, “many-colored, embroidered”. The relative τάς is feminine: what Alexander brought from Sidon were the women themselves. The finest of their robes, which “shone like a star”, is the one Hecuba takes to Athena (6.293–295; see [[the-robe-for-athena]] and [[robe]]). In the Odyssey the same lines, almost word for word, describe robes that Helen made herself, from which she chooses a gift for Telemachus' bride (Od. 15.104–108).

Their metalwork was as prized. Sidonians “of many skills” (πολυδαίδαλοι) made the silver mixing bowl that Achilles sets as a prize at Patroclus' funeral games, and Phoenician men carried it over the sea (23.740–745). Phaedimus, “king of the Sidonians”, gave Menelaus a silver mixing bowl rimmed with gold, the work of Hephaestus (Od. 4.615–619 = 15.115–119). See [[sidon]] and [[mixing-bowl]].
