λείβω means “pour in drops”. A libation is wine poured out for the gods: before drinking, before a journey, or during a sacrifice. The usual later word, σπένδω, does not occur in Book 1.

At Chryse there are two libations. During the [[sacrifice|sacrifice]], the old priest burns the [[thigh-pieces|thigh-pieces]] and pours sparkling [[wine|wine]] over them (ἐπὶ … λεῖβε, {{ref:Iliad 1.462-463}}). After the feast, the young men fill the mixing bowls and serve everyone, “first pouring drops for the libation into the cups” (ἐπαρξάμενοι δεπάεσσιν, {{ref:Iliad 1.471}}). Each drinker gives a little to the gods before he drinks (see [[cup|cup]]).

Hands must be clean to pour a libation: Hector will not pour one for Zeus with unwashed hands (Iliad 6.266–268; see [[hand-washing|hand-washing]]).

## Outside Homer

A truce or treaty in classical Greek is σπονδαί, “libations”, from the libations that sealed it.
