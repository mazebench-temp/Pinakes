At a feast the wine was mixed in a bowl, drawn off (ἀφύσσω), and carried round to the guests in order, from left to right (ἐνδέξια), toward the lucky side. The young did the serving. At Chryse the young men (κοῦροι) fill the [[mixing-bowl|mixing bowls]] brim-full and serve everyone ({{ref:Iliad 1.470-471}}). Among the gods the cupbearer is young and beautiful: Hebe (Iliad 4.2–3), or Ganymede, carried off to pour wine for Zeus “for the sake of his beauty” (Iliad 20.232–235).

## Outside Homer

[[Plato]] objected to the scene: the guardians of his city must not be lovers of laughter, and Homer's gods overcome by laughter are not to be accepted (*Republic* 3.388e–389a; see [[plato-homeric-laughter]]). The phrase became proverbial as “Homeric laughter” (see [[homeric-laughter-idiom]]).
