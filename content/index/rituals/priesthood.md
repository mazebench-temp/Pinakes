Homer has two words for a priest: ἱερεύς, “the one concerned with sacred things” (ἱερά), and ἀρητήρ, “the one who prays” (from ἀράομαι). A Homeric priest serves one god at one place and is honored by the people, but priests are not a separate class, and kings and heads of households sacrifice for themselves.

[[Chryses]] is Apollo's priest at [[Chryse]]. The poem gives the cause of the wrath in its first lines: Agamemnon had dishonored the priest ({{ref:Iliad 1.11-12}}). Calchas names the cause again ({{ref:Iliad 1.94}}). The priest carries the god's [[fillets|fillets]] on a golden staff, and the army wants to respect him ({{ref:Iliad 1.23}}; see [[time|honor]]).

{{quote:35bd44ba-4ba8-5812-a075-a85aabcb9aee}}

His service gives him a claim on the god: he has roofed Apollo's [[temple|temple]] and burned thigh-pieces for him ({{ref:Iliad 1.39-41}}; see [[prayer|prayer]]). At Chryse he does the priest's part of the [[sacrifice|sacrifice]]: he prays for the Danaans and burns the god's portion ({{ref:Iliad 1.450-463}}). A priest could also be asked for the god's will: Achilles proposes to consult “some seer, or a priest” ({{ref:Iliad 1.62}}; see [[divination|divination]]).

Elsewhere Onetor, priest of Zeus on Ida, “was honored like a god by the people” (Iliad 16.604–605), and the Trojans made Theano priestess of Athena (Iliad 6.300).
