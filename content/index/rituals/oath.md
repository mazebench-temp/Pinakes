To swear (ὄμνυμι) is to call on something greater than oneself as guarantor of one's words, and to accept its punishment if they prove false. The particle μά marks what is sworn by: οὐ μά, “no, by…”, and ναὶ μά, “yes, by…”.

The gods' own great oath is by the water of the Styx (Iliad 15.36–38).

## Outside Homer

[[Hesiod]] makes Oath (Ὅρκος) a child of Strife, who “does most harm to men on earth, whenever anyone knowingly swears falsely” (*Theogony* 231–232).
