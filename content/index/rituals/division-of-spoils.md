The goods and captives taken in a raid were brought back to the camp and divided among the army. The leaders received, besides their share, a prize of honor ([[geras]]) chosen out and given by the army. After the sack of Thebe, Achilles tells Thetis, the Achaeans divided the spoils fairly and “chose out” Chryseis for Agamemnon (see [[sack-of-thebe]] and [[raiding|raiding]]).

{{quote:25d6c03d-1740-57b8-8513-89288ea5b95e}}

When Agamemnon demands a new prize, Achilles explains why he cannot have one at once. There is no “common store” (ξυνήϊα) laid up; everything taken has been divided (δέδασται); and it is not fitting to make the army gather it back (παλίλλογα). He promises repayment three and four times over when Troy falls.

{{quote:14040c8f-8e16-5bb7-99d0-f63203a3fb99}}

Achilles' grievance is the division itself: he does most of the fighting, but “whenever the division (δασμός) comes, the far greater prize is yours” ({{ref:Iliad 1.165-168}}; see [[achilles-raids]]). When Briseis is taken, he blames the whole army: “you who gave her are taking her away” ({{ref:Iliad 1.299}}).

## Outside Homer

After Plataea the Greeks set aside a tenth of the spoils for Apollo at Delphi and gifts for Olympia and the Isthmus, then divided the rest (Herodotus 9.81).
