## The custom

The Homeric warrior fights with the spear and the sword, and also with stones. The usual word is χερμάδιον, a stone of a size to throw; λᾶας, λίθος and πέτρος are stones and rocks of every size. Everyone uses them. The Achaeans pelt Hector with arrows and stones when he steps out between the armies (3.79–80). In the first battle Peirous, leader of the Thracians, crushes Diores' ankle with a jagged stone, “the shameless stone” (4.517–522; see [[shameless-stone]]). The defenders of the Achaean wall hurl stones from its towers, and the missiles fall from both sides as thick as snowflakes (12.154–161). A verse lists the three weapons of a hero's rampage: “with spear and sword and great stones” (ἔγχεΐ τʼ ἄορί τε μεγάλοισί τε χερμαδίοισιν), of Agamemnon and of Hector (11.265, 11.541).

## The great stone

A great stone is a hero's feat, and the poem measures it against the men of its own day. Diomedes' stone is “a huge thing, which two men could not carry, such as mortals are now; but he wielded it easily even alone” (5.303–304), and Aeneas lifts one in the same verses against Achilles (20.285–287). The stone Hector carries to the gate of the Achaean wall is bigger still: two of the best men of the people could not easily have levered it off the ground onto a wagon, “such as mortals are now”, and Zeus made it light for him (12.445–450). Ajax kills Epicles with a stone that a man could not easily hold in both hands even in the prime of youth (12.380–386). The words οἷοι νῦν βροτοί εἰσι, “such as mortals are now”, set the heroes above the listeners, as Nestor sets the men of his youth above the heroes (1.271–272; see [[men-of-today]]).

Stones decide great fights. In their duel Hector and Ajax throw stones after their spears, and Ajax's, much larger and shaped like a millstone, breaks Hector's shield and lays him on his back until Apollo sets him on his feet (7.264–272). Hector breaks Teucer's bowstring with a jagged stone (8.321–329); Ajax fells Hector with one of the stones that propped the ships (14.409–418); Patroclus kills Cebriones, Hector's charioteer, with a stone to the forehead (16.734–741). In the battle of the gods Athena picks up a boundary stone, “which men of former times had set to mark the field”, strikes Ares on the neck and loosens his limbs (21.403–406).
