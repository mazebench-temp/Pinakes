tags: [sacrifice]
summary: The killing of human beings for a god, which Greek legend placed at the edges of time and of the world: Iphigenia at Aulis, Polyxena at the tomb of Achilles, the strangers sacrificed to the goddess of the Taurians.
aliases: [the sacrifice of strangers, Taurian sacrifice]
---

Herodotus says that the Taurians of the Crimea sacrifice shipwrecked sailors and any Greeks they capture to a maiden goddess, whom they identify with Iphigenia, daughter of Agamemnon (4.103). In legend Agamemnon sacrificed his daughter at Aulis, or would have but for Artemis (see [[the-sacrifice-of-iphigenia]]), and the Greeks sacrificed Polyxena at the tomb of Achilles before they sailed (see [[the-fall-of-troy]]). The Iliad's nearest approach is Achilles' killing of twelve young Trojans at the pyre of Patroclus, a deed the poet calls evil (23.175–176). In Euripides' *Iphigenia among the Taurians*, the priestess consecrates the strangers for sacrifice, and the plot turns on her brother's coming as one of them (see [[the-recognition-of-orestes-and-iphigenia]] and [[sacrifice]]).
