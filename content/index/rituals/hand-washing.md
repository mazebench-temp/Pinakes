χερνίπτομαι (χείρ, “hand”, + νίζω, “wash”) is to wash one's hands in the χέρνιψ, the water brought for the purpose. At Chryse, when the hecatomb stands around the altar, the Achaeans wash their hands and take up the [[barley-grains|barley grains]], and then Chryses prays ({{ref:Iliad 1.449}}; see [[sacrifice|sacrifice]] and [[purification|purification]]). Clean hands are the first condition for approaching a god.

{{quote:0aecf4d1-1793-506b-8fd1-2275966a39d9}}

The rule holds throughout Homer. Hector, back from battle, will not pour wine to Zeus with unwashed hands (Iliad 6.266–268). Telemachus washes his hands in the sea before he prays to Athena (Odyssey 2.261), and Priam washes before his libation and prayer (Iliad 24.302–306).

## Outside Homer

[[Hesiod]]: never pour sparkling wine to Zeus or the other gods at dawn with unwashed hands, “for they do not listen, but spit back your prayers” (*Works and Days* 724–726).
