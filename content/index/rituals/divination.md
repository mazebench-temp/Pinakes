The μάντις (“seer”) reads the will of the gods from signs. Achilles names three kinds of expert: “some seer, or a priest, or even an interpreter of dreams (ὀνειροπόλος), for a dream, too, comes from Zeus” ({{ref:Iliad 1.62-63}}; see [[onar]] and [[a-dream-comes-from-zeus]]). [[Calchas]] is an οἰωνοπόλος, a “bird-seer” who reads the flight and cries of [[birds|birds]]. A seer's pronouncement is a θεοπρόπιον, “what the god makes known” (see [[theopropion]]), and his art is μαντοσύνη. For prophecy as a theme, see [[prophecy]].

## Outside Homer

[[Plato]] derives the name of the seer's art, μαντική, from μανία, “madness”, the inspired frenzy of the prophet (*Phaedrus* 244b–c).

In the *Prometheus Bound* Prometheus counts the ways of divination among the arts he gave to mortals: he was the first to judge which dreams must come true, he made known to them chance utterances hard to read (κληδόνας) and signs met on the road, and he marked out exactly the flight of crooked-taloned birds, which are by nature on the right and which on the left, their ways of life and their enmities and friendships; then the signs of the entrails and of the flame of the sacrifice (484–499; see [[prometheus-play]]). Every kind of sign that Homer knows is in the list: the dream, the chance word (*Odyssey* 2.35, 20.100–120), the bird on the right or the left, and the smoke of the sacrifice, which the θυοσκόοι read (*Iliad* 24.221).
