## Meaning

ἀγγελίη is a message, or news: Iris comes to the Trojans “with grievous news” (ἀγγελίῃ, 2.787). It is also the errand of carrying one, and with a verb of going or sending it is an embassy. Menelaus and Odysseus came to Troy “on an embassy about you” (σεῦ ἕνεκʼ ἀγγελίης, 3.206), and the Achaeans before Thebes “sent Tydeus ahead on an embassy” (ἀγγελίην ἐπὶ Τυδῆ στεῖλαν, 4.384). The man who carries it is an ἄγγελος, “messenger”; Achilles greets the heralds as “messengers of Zeus and of men” (1.334). Aristarchus took ἀγγελίης at 3.206 as a masculine noun, “messenger”, and the accusative ἀγγελίην at 4.384 could be read the same way, “sent Tydeus as messenger” (see [[the-embassy-to-troy]]).

## The embassies

**Tydeus at Thebes.** Agamemnon tells it to shame Tydeus' son (4.376–398). Tydeus came to Mycenae with Polynices, “not in war, a guest”, to gather allies against Thebes, but Zeus turned the Mycenaeans back with ill-omened signs. On the march the army reached the Asopus, and from there the Achaeans sent Tydeus ahead. He found the Cadmeians feasting in the house of Eteocles, and “though he was a stranger” (ξεῖνός περ ἐών), alone among many, he challenged them to contests and won them all, for Athena helped him. They set an ambush of fifty young men for his return, and he killed them all but one, Maeon, whom he sent home, “obeying the portents of the gods” (4.384–398). Athena tells the story again to Diomedes: she had bidden Tydeus feast quietly, but he challenged the young Cadmeians (5.800–808). Diomedes, praying to her, says that his father carried “a gentle word” to the Cadmeians and on his way back did terrible deeds with her help (10.285–290). See [[tydeus]], [[cadmeians]], [[contests]] and [[ambush]].

**Menelaus and Odysseus at Troy.** Before the war, the two came to demand Helen. They stayed in Antenor's house as his guests, and spoke in the Trojan assembly (3.205–224). The Trojans refused, and Antimachus urged that Menelaus be killed then and there and not allowed to go back (11.138–142). See [[the-embassy-to-troy]].

**The embassy to Achilles.** In Book 9 Nestor chooses Phoenix, Ajax and Odysseus, with the heralds Odius and Eurybates, to carry Agamemnon's offer to Achilles (9.168–170): an embassy inside the army, to a man who has withdrawn from it.

## The envoy's safety

An envoy went as a guest, under the protection that covered heralds. The two embassies from the time before the fighting both turn on it. Tydeus goes as a messenger and a stranger, finds the Cadmeians at a feast, and is attacked from ambush on his way back. Antimachus would have killed a guest in the Trojan assembly, and Agamemnon kills his two sons for it, reminding them of their father's advice (11.122–147). See [[xenia]] and [[heralds]].

## Outside Homer

In [[apollodorus|Apollodorus]] the Seven send Tydeus from Cithaeron to tell Eteocles to give up the kingdom to Polynices as they had agreed. Eteocles refuses; Tydeus challenges the Thebans one by one and beats them all, and when fifty armed men lie in wait for him on his way back, he kills them all but Maeon (*Library* 3.6.5). Statius made the embassy and the ambush the matter of the second book of his *Thebaid* (see [[statius-tydeus]]).
