---
title: An animal ten thousand stades long
greek: εἰ μυρίων σταδίων εἴη ζῷον
kind: animal
summary: Aristotle's imagined creature, too large to be seen whole: beauty requires a magnitude that can be taken in at a glance, and a plot, like an animal, must have a length that the memory can hold.
aliases: [the animal of ten thousand stades, the huge creature]
---

A beautiful thing made of parts, a living creature or anything else, must have its parts in order and a magnitude that is not arbitrary. A tiny creature cannot be beautiful, since our view of it is blurred in an almost imperceptible moment; nor can a huge one, since it cannot be seen all at once and its unity and wholeness are lost to the viewer, “as if there were a creature ten thousand stades long”, nearly two thousand kilometers. So a plot must have a length that can easily be remembered (see [[megethos]] and [[beginning-middle-end]]).
