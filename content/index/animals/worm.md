σκώληξ is the earthworm. Homer names it only in the death of Harpalion, son of the Paphlagonian king Pylaemenes. Shot through the buttock into the bladder as he retreats from Menelaus, he sits down in the arms of his companions, “breathing out his life, like a worm on the ground he lay stretched out; and the dark blood flowed and soaked the earth” (13.654–655; see [[like-a-worm]] and [[harpalion]]). The worm gives the body lying at its length on the earth, and the helplessness of a young man who had followed his father to Troy and would not come home (13.643–645).
