## Name and descent

Ξάνθος, Xanthus, is “Bay”, from ξανθός, “yellow, fair”. The name is shared by one of Hector's horses (8.185; see [[the-horses-of-hector]]), by a Trojan son of Phaenops (5.152; see [[xanthus-son-of-phaenops]]), by the river of Lycia (2.877; see [[xanthus-of-lycia]]), and by the river of Troy, which the gods call Xanthus and men Scamander (20.74; see [[scamander]]).

He and Balius are the pair that Automedon yokes for Patroclus, “Xanthus and Balius, who flew as fast as the winds, / whom the Harpy Podarge bore to the West Wind / as she grazed in a meadow beside the stream of Oceanus” (16.149–151; see [[the-horses-of-achilles]], [[podarge]] and [[zephyrus]]). They are immortal, “the splendid gifts that the gods gave to Peleus” (16.381 = 16.867; see [[peleus]]), and Achilles drives them as “the horses of his father” (19.399). The poem speaks of the two as one team, often in the dual (16.149, 17.427–444), and names Xanthus without his yokemate only when he speaks and when Achilles answers him (19.405, 19.420).

## Descriptions

πόδας αἰόλος, “with flashing feet” (19.404), occurs only here in Homer. αἰόλος joins quick movement and glancing light; Walter Leaf compares the “flashing of feet” of the Phaeacian dancers, μαρμαρυγαὶ ποδῶν (*Odyssey* 8.265; *Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 19.404; see [[flashing]] and the translation's note on 19.404).

αὐδήεντα δʼ ἔθηκε, “gave him speech” (19.407): Hera makes him αὐδήεις, endowed with a human voice (see [[with-human-voice]]). The Odyssey uses the word of Ino, who was “a mortal with human voice” before she became a goddess of the sea (βροτὸς αὐδήεσσα, *Odyssey* 5.334), and of Circe, “a dread goddess with human voice” (θεὸς αὐδήεσσα, *Odyssey* 10.136; see the translation's note on 19.407). It is a gift for one occasion: when he has spoken, the Erinyes check his voice (19.418; see [[erinyes]] and [[the-horses-voice]]).
