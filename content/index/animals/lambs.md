In the assembly, Achilles proposes to ask a seer why Apollo is angry: whether he blames the Achaeans for a vow or a hecatomb left unpaid, and whether, if he receives “the savor of lambs and unblemished goats”, he will keep the ruin away ({{ref:Iliad 1.65-67}}). The god is imagined as receiving the κνίση, the savor of burning fat (see [[sacrifice|sacrifice]] and [[goats|goats]]).

{{quote:6015a139-9273-549a-b37a-9877f18caf83}}

Lambs are a fitting gift for Apollo: Athena tells Pandarus to vow him a hecatomb of firstborn lambs (Iliad 4.101–102). They are also the victims of oaths, as at the truce in Book 3 (Iliad 3.103–104).
