σφήξ is the wasp. Homer names it only in the plural and only in two similes, and both are about a home defended. Asius complains to Zeus that the two Lapiths will not give way from the gate, like wasps with nimble waists, or bees, that have made their homes beside a rocky road and fight off the hunting men for the sake of their young (12.167–172; see [[wasps-at-the-roadside]] and [[bees]]). The Myrmidons pour out behind Patroclus like wasps by the road (σφήκεσσιν, 16.259), which foolish boys keep teasing, making them a common evil for many; if a traveler disturbs them, they fly out with valiant hearts and fight for their young (16.259–267). Homer's one description of the insect is μέσον αἰόλοι (12.167), literally “quick at the middle”: its narrow, supple waist.
