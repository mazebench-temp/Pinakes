Homeric dogs guard herds and houses, hunt, and eat carrion. They are loyal and they are shameless. To lie unburied, food for dogs and [[birds|birds]], is the worst fate a warrior can fear (see [[cremation|cremation]]). The dog stands for shamelessness ([[anaideia]]): Helen calls herself “dog-faced” (Iliad 3.180; Odyssey 4.145), and Hera calls Artemis a “shameless dog” (Iliad 21.481).

{{notes}}

## Outside Homer

Thucydides observed that in the Athenian plague the dogs, because they lived with people, showed its effects most clearly (2.50). The Cynic philosophers took their name from the dog (κύων) and made its shamelessness a principle.
