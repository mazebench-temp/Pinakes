αἴξ is the goat. Goats graze in flocks under goatherds, and with sheep they make up the μῆλα, the small livestock of the Homeric farm. A rich man counts them among his herds: Odysseus had twelve wide flocks of goats on the mainland and eleven on Ithaca (Odyssey 14.100–104). They are eaten, and they are sacrificed, often together with [[bulls|bulls]] or [[lambs|lambs]] (see [[sacrifice|sacrifice]]). The wild goat is hunted. Pandarus' bow was made from the horns of one he had shot himself (Iliad 4.105–111).
