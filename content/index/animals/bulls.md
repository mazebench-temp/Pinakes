The bull is the grandest victim a Greek could offer, and bulls are especially Poseidon's. The Pylians sacrifice black bulls to him on the shore (*Odyssey* 3.5–8; see [[the-sacrifice-at-pylos]]), and Nestor remembers offering one bull to the river Alpheus and another to Poseidon (*Iliad* 11.727–728). In a simile young men drag a bellowing bull around the Heliconian lord, Poseidon, and the god is glad (*Iliad* 20.403–405; see [[the-bull-of-the-heliconian-lord]]).
