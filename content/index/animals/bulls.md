The bull is the grandest victim a Greek could offer. In Book 1 it is twice paired with the [[goats|goat]]. Chryses reminds Apollo of the thighs “of bulls and goats” he has burned ({{ref:Iliad 1.40-41}}), and after the purification the army offers perfect [[hecatomb|hecatombs]] “of bulls and goats” on the shore ({{ref:Iliad 1.315-316}}; see [[sacrifice|sacrifice]]).

{{quote:855497d1-e22b-5975-bdbc-fbc8e2d79fd5}}

Bulls are especially Poseidon's. The Pylians sacrifice black bulls to him on the shore (Odyssey 3.5–8), and Nestor remembers offering one bull to the river Alpheus and another to Poseidon (Iliad 11.727–728).
