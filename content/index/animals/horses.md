Horses are the most prized animals of the Homeric world. They draw the heroes' chariots and are among the richest spoils and prizes.

{{notes}}

## Elsewhere in Homer

Achilles' own horses, Xanthus and Balius, are immortal, foaled by the Harpy Podarge to the West Wind (Iliad 16.148–151).

## Outside Homer

[[Horace]], telling Maecenas that he would give back all his gifts rather than lose his freedom, borrows the answer of Telemachus to Menelaus: *non est aptus equis Ithace locus*, “Ithaca is no place fit for horses, since it neither stretches out in level courses nor is lavish of much grass; son of Atreus, I will leave you your gifts, as better suited to yourself” (*Epistles* 1.7.40–43, a plain rendering; *Odyssey* 4.601–608).
