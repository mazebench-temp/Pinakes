Horses are the most prized animals of the Homeric world. They draw the heroes' chariots and are among the richest spoils and prizes. Book 1 has no chariots and no battle, and horses appear once, as the kind of property whose theft starts a war. Achilles has no such grievance against the Trojans: they never drove off his [[cattle|cattle]] or his horses, or ravaged his harvest in Phthia ({{ref:Iliad 1.154-156}}; see [[raiding|raiding]]).

{{quote:7fec6979-db86-5dd6-aaeb-02271fe3d072}}

Achilles' own horses, Xanthus and Balius, are immortal, foaled by the Harpy Podarge to the West Wind (Iliad 16.148–151).
