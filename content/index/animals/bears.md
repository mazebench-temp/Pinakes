## In Homer

No simile of Homer's uses the bear, and the animal appears only once, among the works wrought on the golden strap of Heracles' baldric: “bears and wild boars and lions with flashing eyes, / combats and battles and slaughters and killings of men” (*Odyssey* 11.611–612; see [[the-baldric-of-heracles]], [[boar]] and [[lion]]). The Bear in the sky, Ἄρκτος, “which they also call by the name of the Wagon”, turns in its place and watches Orion, and alone has no share in the baths of Oceanus (*Iliad* 18.487–489; *Odyssey* 5.273–275; see [[the-bear]]).

## Outside Homer

The Homeric Hymns know the animal. When Aphrodite comes to Ida, “mother of wild beasts”, gray wolves and lions with flashing eyes, χαροποί τε λέοντες, as on the baldric, bears and swift leopards follow her fawning, and she puts desire in their hearts, so that they lie down two by two in the shadowy glens (*Hymn to Aphrodite* 68–74); on Anchises' bed lie “the skins of bears and of deep-roaring lions / that he himself had killed in the high mountains” (*Hymn to Aphrodite* 159–160). Apollo, tracking his stolen cattle, finds footprints that are not those of a man or a woman, of gray wolves or of bears or of lions (*Hymn to Hermes* 222–223), and Dionysus, captured by pirates, makes a shaggy-necked bear appear in the middle of their ship (*Hymn to Dionysus* 44–46).
