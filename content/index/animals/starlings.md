## In Homer

ὅς τʼ ἐφόβησε κολοιούς τε ψῆράς τε, “which puts jackdaws and starlings to flight” (16.583; see [[like-a-hawk]] and [[jackdaws]]). The swift hawk of the simile is Patroclus, making straight through the champions to avenge Epeigeus, and the small birds are the Lycians and Trojans who scatter before him (16.581–587). The starlings are named once more, in a flock that the poem calls a cloud: τῶν δʼ ὥς τε ψαρῶν νέφος ἔρχεται ἠὲ κολοιῶν, “And as a cloud of starlings or of jackdaws goes”, screaming a cry of death, when they see a falcon coming; so the young men of the Achaeans flee before Aeneas and Hector (17.755–759; see [[a-cloud-of-starlings]] and [[falcon]]). Walter Leaf notes the older form ψαρῶν here beside the Ionic ψῆρας of *Iliad* 16 (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 17.755). See [[hawk]] and [[birds]].
