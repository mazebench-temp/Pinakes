## In Homer

ὅς τʼ ἐφόβησε κολοιούς τε ψῆράς τε, “which puts jackdaws and starlings to flight” (16.583; see [[like-a-hawk]] and [[jackdaws]]). The swift hawk of the simile is Patroclus, making straight through the champions to avenge Epeigeus, and the small birds are the Lycians and Trojans who scatter before him (16.581–587). The starlings are named once more, in a flock that the poem calls a cloud: τῶν δʼ ὥς τε ψαρῶν νέφος ἔρχεται ἠὲ κολοιῶν, “as a cloud of starlings or jackdaws goes”, screaming, when they see the hawk coming; so the young men of the Achaeans fall back before Aeneas and Hector (17.755–759). See [[hawk]] and [[birds]].
