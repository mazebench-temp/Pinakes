summary: The ape or monkey, to the Greeks the comic double of man and the type of the mimic; the old actor Mynniscus called the overacting Callippides an ape.
aliases: [monkey, apes]
---

Barbary apes from Libya, and monkeys brought from further away, were kept as pets and performers. For Aristotle the ape shares the natures of man and of the four-footed beasts (*History of Animals* 2.8, 502a16–18); Heraclitus said that the most beautiful ape is ugly compared with the human race (fr. 82 DK, quoted in Plato, *Hippias Major* 289a). In Semonides' poem on women, the woman made from an ape is the ugliest of all (7.71–82). As an insult the word meant a mimic, a buffoon or a cheat (see [[mynniscus-and-callippides]] and [[most-imitative-of-animals]]).
