κτιδέη, “of κτίς skin”, occurs only in the phrase κτιδέη κυνέη, the cap that Dolon puts on as he sets out to spy on the Achaean ships (10.335) and that Odysseus and Diomedes take from his head when they have killed him (10.458). The animal is named nowhere else in Homer. It is usually taken to be the ἰκτίς of later Greek, a small hunter of the weasel kind, a marten, a polecat or a ferret; the translation calls it ferret (see the translation's note on 10.335, 10.458).

κυνέη, the word for the cap, is formed from κύων, “dog”, and once meant a cap of dogskin (see [[helmet]] and [[dogs]]). Dolon's is made from the skin of another small hunter, and he wears it with the skin of a gray wolf over his shoulders (10.333–335). The two Achaeans who catch him have been fitted out in the same way, Diomedes with a skullcap of bull's hide and Odysseus with a leather helmet faced with a boar's tusks (10.257–265; see [[wolves]], [[the-boars-tusk-helmet]], [[dolon]] and [[the-doloneia]]).
