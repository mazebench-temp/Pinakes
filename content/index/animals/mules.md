οὐρεύς (also ἡμίονος, “half-ass”) is the mule, the draft animal of the Homeric world. Mules pull wagons, haul timber, and plow better than oxen (Iliad 10.351–353; 23.110–122; 24.265–280).

{{notes}}

## Outside Homer

[[Aristotle]] treats the line in which Apollo's arrows strike the mules first (Iliad 1.50) as a problem to be solved: why should the god begin with the mules? He offers a solution by rare word (γλῶττα): “perhaps he does not mean the mules but the guards” (*Poetics* 25, 1461a). The idea rests on οὖρος, “watcher, guardian”, which Homer uses of Nestor, “guardian of the Achaeans” (Iliad 8.80). Most readers since have kept the mules (see [[aristotle-mules]]).
