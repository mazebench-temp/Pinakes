Nestor ends his advice to his son with the turning post: if Antilochus can drive past the others there, no one will catch him or pass him, not even if he drove Arion in pursuit, “the swift horse of Adrastus, who was of the stock of the gods”, or the horses of Laomedon (23.344–348; see [[nestor]], [[antilochus]] and [[turning-post]]). The two are the swiftest horses an old man of the time could name: Arion before the war, from the generation of the fathers, and at Troy the breed that Zeus gave Tros in recompense for Ganymede, which Laomedon owned (5.265–272; see [[laomedon]] and [[ganymede-and-the-horses-of-tros]]). The poem says nothing more of Arion. ἐκ θεόφιν γένος, “of the stock of the gods”, gives him a divine sire, as Achilles' immortal horses were foaled by the Harpy Podarge to the West Wind (16.148–151; see [[the-horses-of-achilles]]) and the mares of Erichthonius bore foals to the North Wind (20.221–229; see [[the-mares-of-erichthonius]]). His master is the Adrastus whom the Catalogue remembers as a former king of Sicyon (2.572), and whose grandson Diomedes wins the race in which Arion is named (14.121; see [[adrastus]] and [[diomedes]]).

Walter Leaf reports that the manuscripts vary between Ἀρίονα and Ἀρείονα, and that the horse was a favorite of the Theban epics, “quite outside the Homeric myth-world” (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 23.346).

## Outside Homer

In the Hesiodic *Shield*, Heracles tells his charioteer Iolaus to wheel “the great horse, dark-maned Arion” this way and that in the fight with Cycnus (μέγαν ἵππον Ἀρίονα κυανοχαίτην, *Shield* 120; see [[heracles]] and [[hesiod]]).

[[Pausanias]] tells the Arcadian story at Thelpusa. When Demeter was wandering in search of her daughter, Poseidon pursued her; she changed herself into a mare and grazed among the mares there, and he saw through it and mated with her in the form of a stallion (Pausanias 8.25.5). She bore him a daughter whose name is not told to the uninitiated, and the horse Areion, and for this reason, the people say, they were the first Arcadians to call Poseidon “Horse” (Pausanias 8.25.7; see [[poseidon]] and [[demeter]]). Pausanias quotes Homer's two verses (23.346–347) and a verse of the *Thebaid*, in which Adrastus fled from Thebes in wretched clothes with dark-maned Areion, and says that they hint at Poseidon as the father; the poet [[antimachus-of-colophon|Antimachus]] made Earth his mother (Pausanias 8.25.8). In Antimachus' verses Adrastus, son of Talaus, son of Cretheus, was the first of the Danaans to drive his famous horses, swift Caerus and Areion of Thelpusa, whom Earth herself sent up beside the grove of Oncean Apollo, a marvel for mortals to see (Pausanias 8.25.9). Heracles, Pausanias adds, asked Oncus for the horse when he made war on Elis, rode him when he took the city, and afterward gave him to Adrastus, and Antimachus called Adrastus the third lord who tamed him (Pausanias 8.25.10).

Propertius gives him a voice: *Adrasti … vocalis Arion*, Adrastus' speaking Arion, the horse that won, in sorrow, at the funeral of Archemorus (Propertius 2.34.37–38).
