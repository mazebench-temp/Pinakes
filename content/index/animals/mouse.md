[[Chryses]] addresses [[Apollo]] as Σμινθεύς ({{ref:Iliad 1.39}}). Homer uses the title only here and does not explain it. Ancient scholars derived it either from σμίνθος, said to be a local word for “mouse”, or from a place in the Troad called Sminthe. If the first is right, the god who sends the plague is invoked as lord of mice. Some modern readers connect the two, since rodents carry plague; Homer says nothing of mice, and his plague is arrows.

## Outside Homer

[[Strabo]] (13.1.48) describes the sanctuary of Apollo Smintheus at Chrysa in the Troad. A mouse lay under the foot of its cult statue, a work of Scopas of Paros, as the sign of the god's name. Strabo also tells how settlers from Crete, bidden by an oracle to stay where “the earth-born” attacked them, stopped near Hamaxitus when field mice came out at night and gnawed the leather of their weapons and gear (see [[smintheus-and-the-mice]]). The sanctuary has been excavated at Gülpınar, in northwest Turkey.
