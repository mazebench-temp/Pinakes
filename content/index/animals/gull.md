# λάρῳ (Od. 5.51), the only form in Homer; not λαρός, "sweet" (λαρὸν, Il. 19.316; Od. 12.283).
grc: [λάρῳ]
en: [gull]

λάρος occurs once in Homer, in the simile of Hermes' flight to Calypso's island (*Odyssey* 5.51–54; see [[the-gull]] and [[hermes]]). The bird is named twice over, the general word and then the kind, λάρῳ ὄρνιθι, “a bird, a gull” (see [[birds]]). Homer says nothing of its look but its thick plumage; what he gives is its way of life: it goes through the bosom of the barren sea “hunting fish”, and wets its feathers in the brine (*Odyssey* 5.52–53). W. W. Merry notes that thick plumage is characteristic of sea birds (*Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 5.53). It is not the adjective λαρός, “sweet”, of a meal (*Iliad* 19.316; *Odyssey* 12.283).

Which bird it was is not certain. Merry thinks that λάρος “may be the gull (*larus*) or the tern (*sterna*), neither of which dives deep”, and notes that in modern Greece the gull is still called γλάρος (on 5.51). He keeps it apart from the αἴθυια, the bird to which Ino is likened when she rises from the sea and plunges back into it (*Odyssey* 5.337, 5.353), which “must be a bird that dives” (on 5.51; see [[like-a-diving-bird]]). The translation renders λάρος “gull” and αἴθυια “diving bird” (see its table of key words).

## Outside Homer

[[Aristotle]], as Merry notes (on 5.51), keeps the gull and the αἴθυια apart by their seasons: “The αἴθυια and the gulls lay their eggs in the rocks by the sea, two or three in number; but the gull in summer, the αἴθυια at the beginning of spring, straight after the solstice, and it sits on them as the other birds do” (*History of Animals* 5.9). He names two gulls. One, “ash-colored”, is among the birds that live by lakes and rivers; the other, “the white gull”, is among the birds of the sea, with the κέπφος, the αἴθυια and the χαραδριός (*History of Animals* 8.3). And among the birds that live from the sea he counts the βρένθος, the gull and the ἅρπη as enemies of one another (*History of Animals* 9.1; see [[harpe]]). Merry also reports that the *Periplus of the Euxine Sea* distinguishes the λάρος from the αἴθυια, and that Pliny says the gulls nest in the rocks in summer (on 5.51).
