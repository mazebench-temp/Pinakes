The Iliad says nothing more of Aethra. But Aethra, daughter of Pittheus, king of Troezen, is famous in later legend as the mother of Theseus. The story, as later writers tell it: Theseus carried off Helen while she was still a girl and left her in his mother's keeping in Attica; while he was away, Helen's brothers Castor and Polydeuces came with an army, took their sister home, and carried off Aethra as a captive. Aethra then served Helen, went with her to Troy, and was found there at the sack by her grandsons, the sons of Theseus. That last episode was told in the *Iliupersis*, according to Proclus' summary. If the line means that Aethra, it is the only allusion in Homer to Theseus' abduction of Helen.

Plutarch, in his *Life of Theseus*, tells the story and cites this line as Homer's testimony that Aethra followed Helen to Troy. But he adds that some reject the verse as spurious (*Theseus* 34).

The case against the line is like the case against the Theseus line of Book 1 (1.265, see [[theseus-line]]): Athenians had an interest in giving their hero, and his family, a place in Homer. The case for it is that the poet gives Helen's companions names, as he often names minor figures, and could have taken them from traditions he knew. The line is in the manuscripts, and Monro and Allen print it. Whether the poet meant Theseus' mother, or only borrowed a name, cannot be decided.
