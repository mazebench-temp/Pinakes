Heraclitus, probably of the first century CE and not the philosopher, begins his *Homeric Problems* with a defense of Homer: "if he meant nothing allegorically, he was impious throughout". His first test case is the plague.

On his reading, Apollo is the sun, and his "arrows" are its rays. The plague that strikes first the animals and then the men is the sickness of a hot, marshy summer, which Homer describes in the language of myth. The nine days, the order of the victims, and the healing at the end all fit a natural epidemic. For Heraclitus, Homer is a natural philosopher speaking in images.
