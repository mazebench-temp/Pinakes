title: Ariphrades mocks the tragedians

summary: Ariphrades made fun of the tragedians for expressions no one would use in conversation, "halls from" for "from halls", "of thee", "and I him", "Achilles about"; Aristotle answers that it is just such departures from the usual that make diction distinguished.

{{quote:b5faa1eb-d2f2-518d-86aa-6cb4c943efa6}}

Ariphrades' examples are the commonplaces of tragic style: a preposition set after its noun, δωμάτων ἄπο for ἀπὸ δωμάτων and Ἀχιλλέως πέρι for περὶ Ἀχιλλέως; the poetic genitive σέθεν, “of thee”, for σοῦ; and νιν, “him”, for αὐτόν (see [[ariphrades]]). He did not know, says Aristotle, that because such expressions are not among the standard words, they make the diction uncommon (see [[kyrion]], [[xenikon]] and [[excellence-of-diction]]). The placing of a preposition after its noun is common in Homer, from whom the tragedians took it.
