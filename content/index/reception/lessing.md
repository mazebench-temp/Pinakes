summary: Gotthold Ephraim Lessing (1729–1781), German dramatist and critic, whose Laocoön (1766) drew the line between painting and poetry and took Homer as the model of poetry that shows actions rather than bodies.
---

Lessing wrote the comedy *Minna von Barnhelm*, the tragedy *Emilia Galotti* and the verse drama *Nathan the Wise*, and his *Hamburg Dramaturgy* was a founding work of German theater criticism. The *Laocoön, or On the Limits of Painting and Poetry* takes its name from the ancient statue of the Trojan priest and his sons strangled by serpents, and compares it with Virgil's account of the same scene in *Aeneid* 2. Its examples of what poetry can do and painting cannot come above all from Homer.
