The first half of the *Aeneid* follows the Odyssey, the wanderings of a hero after the fall of Troy; the second half follows the Iliad, a war fought on the plain before a city. Virgil left it unfinished at his death. His borrowings from Iliad 2 include:

- the poet's ten tongues, multiplied to a hundred: [[ten-tongues-in-virgil]];
- the catalogue of forces, with its invocation of the Muses: [[virgil-catalogue-of-italians]];
- Thersites' insult "Achaean women, Achaean men no longer" (2.235), turned against the Trojans by the Italian Numanus: "O truly Phrygian women, for you are not Phrygian men" (*Aeneid* 9.617): [[virgil-numanus]];
- Rumor, who grows as Homer's Strife does: [[virgil-rumor]];
- Rhesus and Dolon among the pictures at Carthage: [[virgil-rhesus-and-dolon]];
- the truce for burning the dead: [[virgil-truce-for-the-dead]];
- Pandarus and Bitias at the gate of the camp: [[virgil-pandarus-and-bitias]];
- the duel for Lavinia and the broken truce: [[virgil-duel-for-lavinia]];
- Neptune's drives over the sea, after Poseidon's journey to the ships: [[virgil-neptunes-chariot]].
