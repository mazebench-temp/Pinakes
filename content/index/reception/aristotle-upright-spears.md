When Nestor comes to wake Diomedes, he finds him asleep outside his hut among his companions, their shields under their heads, and their spears “were driven upright on their butt-spikes, and far off the bronze / shone like the lightning of father Zeus” (10.152–154; see [[diomedes]] and [[like-lightning]]). The σαυρωτήρ, the spike at the butt end of a spear, is named only here in Homer; elsewhere the butt is the οὐρίαχος (13.443, 16.612; see [[spear]]). Planted on it, the spears stand ready to the hand of a man who wakes in the night.

Chapter 25 of the *Poetics* begins from the poet's freedom. Like a painter, he represents things as they were or are, as men say they are or seem, or as they ought to be, and the critic must judge what he says by that standard. Some things, Aristotle goes on, are “perhaps not better, but so it was”, as with the arms: “‘and their spears upright on the butt-spike’: for so they used to do then, as the Illyrians still do now” (1461a1–4). He does not state the objection. It was to the practice itself, presumably as a careless way to keep weapons in camp. His answer is that the poet is not at fault for describing a custom of the heroic age, and that the custom was real, since a people of his own day kept it. The Illyrians lived on the coast of the Adriatic, beyond Epirus, at the edge of the Greek world.

Walter Leaf inferred from Aristotle's words that the custom was unknown to the Greeks of the classical age (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 10.153). The butt-spike itself was familiar. Herodotus uses the same word of the ten thousand picked Persian foot soldiers who marched with Xerxes: a thousand of them carried golden pomegranates on their spears in place of butt-spikes, and the rest silver ones (ἀντὶ τῶν σαυρωτήρων, *Histories* 7.41.2; see [[herodotus]]).

The same chapter answers three more problems drawn from this book: Dolon's looks, the reckoning of the night, and the flutes heard while “all” slept (see [[aristotle-dolons-face]], [[aristotle-portions-of-the-night]] and [[all-the-gods-slept]]).
