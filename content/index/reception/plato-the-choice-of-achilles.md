**Homer.** Achilles has told his mother that his heart does not bid him live unless Hector dies first by his spear and pays for Patroclus (18.90–93). Thetis answers that his own death will come straight after Hector's, and Achilles accepts it: he failed to defend his companion, he sits idly by the ships, and he will accept his doom whenever Zeus wills it, as even Heracles did not escape his (18.95–121; see [[death-of-achilles-foretold]] and [[achilles]]). In *Iliad* 9 he had told the embassy of his two fates, glory and an early death at Troy or a long life at home without glory (9.410–416; see [[the-two-fates-of-achilles]]). Here he chooses the first.

**The Apology.** Socrates imagines someone asking whether he is not ashamed of a pursuit that has brought him into danger of death. A man of any worth, he answers, should not weigh the chances of living or dying, but only whether what he does is just or unjust, the work of a good man or a bad. Otherwise the demigods who died at Troy would be worthless, above all the son of Thetis, who thought so little of danger beside disgrace that when his mother, a goddess, told him something like this, “My son, if you avenge the killing of your companion Patroclus and kill Hector, you will die yourself, for ‘straight after Hector your own fate is ready’”, he made light of death and danger. Fearing far more to live as a coward and not avenge his friends, he said, “‘May I die at once’, once I have punished the wrongdoer, so that I may not stay here a laughingstock by the curved ships, a burden on the earth” (*Apology* 28b–d). So, Socrates goes on, a man must hold the post where he has stationed himself or been stationed by his commander, and face the danger there, weighing neither death nor anything else against disgrace; he held his post at Potidaea, at Amphipolis and at Delium, and he will not leave the post the god has given him, to live philosophizing (28d–29a; see [[plato]] and [[socrates]]).

**What Plato changes.** Socrates quotes 18.96 almost exactly, and the first words of 18.98; the rest he retells. Homer's Achilles grieves that he was “no light at all to Patroclus” and sits “idly beside the ships, a burden on the earth” (ἐτώσιον ἄχθος ἀρούρης, 18.102–104). Plato's fears to stay by the ships καταγέλαστος, “a laughingstock”, a word not found in Homer, and dies δίκην ἐπιθεὶς τῷ ἀδικοῦντι, “having punished the wrongdoer”, in the language of the law court where Socrates is speaking. In Homer, Hector is to make amends: ἀποτίσῃ, he “pays for” Patroclus (18.93). In the same passage Socrates calls Achilles and the others who fell at Troy ἡμίθεοι, “demigods” (28c), the word Homer uses once, of the men who fell by the Simoeis (12.23; see [[hemitheos]]).

**The Symposium.** In Phaedrus' speech in praise of Love, the gods honor the courage that love gives. They sent Orpheus back from Hades with only a phantom of his wife, but honored Achilles, the son of Thetis, and sent him to the Isles of the Blessed, because, having learned from his mother that he would die if he killed Hector, and that if he did not, he would go home and die an old man, he dared to choose to help his lover Patroclus and avenge him, and not only to die for him but to die after him (*Symposium* 179d–180a). Phaedrus' version joins the choice of *Iliad* 18 to the long life at home of Book 9. Homer's Achilles is not taken to the Isles of the Blessed: Odysseus meets his shade in Hades (*Odyssey* 11.467–540). Phaedrus adds that Aeschylus talks nonsense in making Achilles the lover of Patroclus, since Achilles was the more beautiful, still beardless, and much the younger, “as Homer says” (180a; see [[aeschylus-myrmidons]]).

**Afterward.** Proclus uses Thetis' prophecy, “for straight after Hector your own fate is ready”, to defend Achilles against another of Plato's charges, that he gave his hair to Patroclus (*On the Republic* I 149 Kroll; see [[proclus-the-neoplatonist]]). The orator Aeschines had the lines read out in court (see [[aeschines-on-achilles-and-patroclus]]). See also [[plato-socrates-dream]] and [[the-short-life-of-achilles]].
