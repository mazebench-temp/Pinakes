The Suda calls him an Alexandrian and an epic poet, a pupil of Callimachus, who lived for a time in Rhodes, from which he took his name, and who was head of the Library at Alexandria under the Ptolemies (α 3419). His *Argonautica*, in four books, is the one Greek epic that survives whole from the Hellenistic age. It tells the voyage of the Argo from Iolcus to Colchis for the Golden Fleece and the long way home with Medea, in Homeric hexameters and a Homeric vocabulary used with a scholar's care. Its Jason is a new kind of hero, a leader who often does not know what to do and who wins by persuasion and by Medea's help rather than by strength. Virgil drew on the poem for the *Aeneid*, above all for Dido (see [[virgil]]).

Homer names Jason in the Iliad as the father of Euneus, king of Lemnos (7.468–469), and the Odyssey knows the Argo, “known to all”, which alone passed the Wandering Rocks, on its way home from Aeetes, because Hera loved Jason (*Odyssey* 12.69–72). Apollonius tells the stories behind both lines (see [[jason]] and [[the-lemnian-women]]).
