The line was known in antiquity, and Monro and Allen print it. But most medieval manuscripts omit it, and it matches word for word a line of the Hesiodic *Shield* (182), in a list of Lapiths fighting the Centaurs. Athens had an interest in placing its national hero in Homer. A like charge was brought against a verse of the Odyssey, which Hereas of Megara, as Plutarch reports him, said that the Athenian tyrant Peisistratus had inserted into Homer's Nekyia (*Odyssey* 11.631; see [[pisistratus]] and the note on Book 11 of the Odyssey).

Whether or not it is authentic, the line makes Theseus a companion of Peirithous against the Centaurs, as later tradition consistently did. See [[theseus]].
