The line was known in antiquity, and Monro and Allen print it. But most medieval manuscripts omit it, and it matches word for word a line of the Hesiodic *Shield* (182), in a list of Lapiths fighting the Centaurs. Athens had an interest in placing its national hero in Homer. Plutarch reports a similar charge, that the Athenian tyrant Peisistratus had inserted a line about Theseus into the Odyssey (*Theseus* 20, on Odyssey 11.631).

Whether or not it is authentic, the line makes Theseus a companion of Peirithous against the Centaurs, as later tradition consistently did. See [[theseus]].
