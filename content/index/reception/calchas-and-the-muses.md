The Muses in Hesiod's *Theogony* sing "the things that are, the things to come, and the things that were before" (38). They breathe into Hesiod a divine voice "to celebrate the things to come and the things that were before" (32). The formula names complete knowledge of time, and it belongs to both kinds of inspired speaker, the seer and the singer.

In the Iliad the Muse knows the past and tells it (1.1). The seer knows the causes of the present and reveals them (1.93–100). Both speak with authority that comes from the gods. See [[calchas]], [[muses]] and [[prophecy]].
