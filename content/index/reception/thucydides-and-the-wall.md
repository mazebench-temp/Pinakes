Thucydides wanted to explain why the war at Troy lasted ten years. The cause, he argued, was not a shortage of men but a shortage of money. For lack of supplies the Greeks brought a smaller army, as large as they hoped could live off the country while it fought. When they arrived they won a battle, “and this is clear, for otherwise they would not have built the fortification for their camp”; but even then they did not use their whole strength, and turned to farming the Chersonese and to raiding for want of food (1.11.1). The argument follows his reckoning of the fleet from the Catalogue of Ships (see [[thucydides-and-the-catalogue]]).

The wall is evidence for him, as the Catalogue's numbers are. He does not doubt that it existed; he dates it. An army that has just landed can fortify its camp only if it holds the ground, so the wall implies a victory on the beach. The epic tradition knew such a battle. In the *Cypria*, as [[proclus|Proclus]] summarizes it, the Trojans resisted the landing and killed Protesilaus, until Achilles killed Cycnus and routed them (see [[the-death-of-protesilaus]]), and the Iliad remembers that Protesilaus died as he leapt from his ship, “by far the first of the Achaeans” (2.702).

Homer's wall belongs to another moment. The Achaeans build it in the tenth year, on the advice Nestor gives on the evening of the duel of Hector and Ajax, when “many long-haired Achaeans are dead” (7.328). It is to be “a safeguard for the ships and for ourselves” (7.338), “lest the war of the proud Trojans ever press hard upon us” (7.343): a defense for an army that is losing, not the mark of a victory (see [[nestor]] and [[the-building-of-the-wall]]). Thucydides does not mention the discrepancy. Denys Page argued that the Iliad he knew did not yet tell of the building, and that the passages of Book 7 were added later (*History and the Homeric Iliad*, 1959). Others think that Thucydides reasoned, as he does throughout these chapters, from what an army must have done, and took from Homer only the fact of the wall. See [[the-wall-in-the-tenth-year]] and [[the-achaean-wall]].
