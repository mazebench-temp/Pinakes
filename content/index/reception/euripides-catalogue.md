*Iphigenia at Aulis* was one of Euripides' last plays, staged after his death. It is set at Aulis while the fleet waits for a wind. The chorus are women from Chalcis, across the narrow strait from Aulis, who have come to see the army they have heard so much about. Their entrance song (164–302) is a catalogue in two parts.

First they describe the heroes as they found them: the two Ajaxes; Protesilaus and Palamedes playing at draughts; Diomedes delighting in the discus; Meriones, Odysseus and Nireus. Achilles, in full armor, races on foot along the shore against a four-horse chariot driven by Eumelus. Then they count the ships, beginning with Achilles' fifty, whose sterns carry golden images of Nereids, and going on through the contingents of Argos, Athens, Boeotia, Mycenae, Pylos and others, often naming the emblem each carries.

The song is Homer's Catalogue turned into spectacle. The heroes at their games recall Achilles' men amusing themselves with discus, javelin and bow on the shore at Troy (2.773–775). The race of Achilles against Eumelus' team sets Homer's best man against his best horses (2.763–770). And the scene is Aulis, where, many scholars think, the Catalogue itself began as a muster list: see [[the-boeotia]]. Palamedes, whom Homer never names, comes from the *Cypria*.

The text of the play suffered many later additions, and some editors doubt that the ship catalogue in this song is Euripides' own.
