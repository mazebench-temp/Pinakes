Book 2 opens on a contrast. The others slept, but Zeus lay awake, planning how to honor Achilles.

Chapter 25 of the *Poetics* collects objections to the poets, above all to Homer, and the ways to answer them. One solution is metaphor, and Aristotle's example is this opening, quoted as πάντες μέν ῥα θεοί τε καὶ ἀνέρες... εὗδον παννύχιοι, "all the gods and men slept all night long". He sets beside it a line from the night scene of Book 10, where Agamemnon, awake, looks out over the Trojan plain and marvels at the sound of pipes and flutes (10.11–13). How could all have slept? Aristotle answers that "all" is said by metaphor for "many", since all is a kind of many.

His solution shows that he read πάντες, "all". Our manuscripts have ἄλλοι, "the others", here and in the opening of Book 10 ("the others, the best of all the Achaeans, / slept all night long", 10.1–2), and with that reading the problem does not arise. Aristotle may have quoted from memory, blending two openings that are built alike: everyone sleeps except one who lies awake with cares, Zeus in Book 2 and Agamemnon in Book 10. Or his text may have read πάντες. The Pinakes translation follows the manuscripts: "Now the other gods and the men who fight from chariots slept all night long".
