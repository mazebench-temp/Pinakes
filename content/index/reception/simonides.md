Simonides was born at Iulis on Ceos and lived, the Suda says, to the age of eighty-nine (σ 439). He wrote victory odes, dirges, hymns and elegies, and many epigrams went under his name, some of them certainly not his. He was the uncle of Bacchylides. Ancient tradition made him the inventor of the art of memory: Cicero tells how he identified the guests crushed when a banqueting hall collapsed by remembering where each had sat (*On the Orator* 2.351–354).

A papyrus of his elegies, published in 1992, preserves part of a poem on the battle of Plataea (479 BCE). It sets the Greeks who fought there beside the heroes of Troy, and praises the poet who received the whole truth from the Muses and made the short-lived race of demigods famous among later men (fr. 11 West).
