Ancient editors divided over the genitive ἑῆος. With the rough breathing it is the possessive "own"; with the smooth breathing (ἐῆος), it is from ἐΰς, "noble, good". Aristarchus preferred "good", arguing that the possessive ἑός in Homer is used of the third person, not the second ("your"). The breathing marks were added by editors, so the choice is theirs. Monro and Allen print ἑῆος, and the translation renders "your own son".
