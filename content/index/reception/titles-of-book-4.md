The episodes of the Iliad had names before the poem was divided into twenty-four books (see [[the-boeotia]]). The traditional titles of Book 4 are Ὁρκίων σύγχυσις, “the confounding of the oaths”, for its first part, and Ἀγαμέμνονος ἐπιπώλησις, “Agamemnon's Epipolesis” or review, for the second.

Both come from the text. The first takes up Idomeneus' words to Agamemnon, “the oaths have been confounded by the Trojans” (σύν γʼ ὅρκιʼ ἔχευαν, 4.269–270). The verb is συγχέω, “pour together, confound”, split in two as often in Homer. Plato uses the same noun and verb of Pandarus' shot: “the confounding of the oaths and the truce, which Pandarus confounded” (*Republic* 2.379e; see [[plato-broken-oaths]]). Aelian, listing the old names of episodes that were once sung separately, gives a variant, Ὁρκίων ἀφάνισις (ἀφάνισιν in his list), “the obliteration of the oaths” (*Varia Historia* 13.14).

ἐπιπώλησις is the noun of ἐπιπωλέομαι, “go about among”, the verb the narrator uses twice of Agamemnon on his tour: “he himself, on foot, ranged through the ranks of men” and “So, like a master, he ranged through the ranks of men” (4.231, 4.250). The review runs from 4.220 to 4.421. Agamemnon goes from the Cretans to the two Ajaxes, to Nestor, to Menestheus and Odysseus, and to Diomedes and Sthenelus, praising some and rebuking others (see [[the-review]], [[the-review-of-the-army]] and [[praise-and-blame]]).

The two names divide the book at the healing of Menelaus. The first part tells how the oaths sworn in Book 3 were broken ([[the-oaths-sworn]], [[pandarus-breaks-the-truce]]); the second brings the army to its first battle.
