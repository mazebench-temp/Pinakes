Book 7 of the *Aeneid* ends with a catalogue of the Italian leaders who took up arms against the Trojans, from Mezentius, "scorner of the gods", to Turnus and the warrior maiden Camilla (7.647–817). It opens with an invocation. "Open Helicon now, goddesses, and stir up song," Virgil begins, and asks which kings were roused to war and which armies filled the plains. Then: "For you remember, goddesses, and can recall; to us there barely drifts a faint breath of report" (7.641–646).

These lines translate Homer's claim that the Muses are present and know all things, while "we hear only the report, and know nothing" (2.485–486). Homer's κλέος, "report", becomes Virgil's *fama*, and the report now reaches the poet only as a faint breath. When Virgil lists Aeneas' Etruscan allies in Book 10, he begins with the same line, "Open Helicon now, goddesses" (10.163).

Like Homer's, Virgil's catalogue stands before the first great battle and maps the forces for the reader. It even keeps one of Homer's images. Messapus' men march singing like snow-white swans returning from their feeding grounds, when "the river and the Asian marsh, struck far off, resound" (7.699–705). The model is the simile that comes just before Homer's Catalogue: geese, cranes and long-necked swans in the Asian meadow by the streams of the Cayster, where "the meadow resounds" (2.459–463).
