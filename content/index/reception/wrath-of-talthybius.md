At Sparta there was a shrine of Talthybius, and a family of hereditary heralds, the Talthybiadae, claimed descent from him. When Darius sent heralds to demand earth and water, the Spartans threw them into a well. After that, Herodotus says, the omens at their sacrifices were bad for a long time. The Spartans read this as "the wrath of Talthybius" and sent two nobles to Xerxes to be killed in atonement. Xerxes refused to kill them (7.134–136). Herodotus adds that the wrath fell again, a generation later, on the sons of those two men (7.137).

The story shows two things the Iliad takes for granted. Heralds were sacred, "messengers of Zeus and of men" (1.334). And the μῆνις of a hero, like a god's, fell on a whole community until it was appeased. See [[menis]], [[talthybius]] and [[messengers-of-zeus]].
