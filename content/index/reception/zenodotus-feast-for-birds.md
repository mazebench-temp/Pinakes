The standard text reads οἰωνοῖσί τε πᾶσι, "and to all the birds". Zenodotus' reading, δαῖτα, makes the dead "a feast" for them. It is a grimmer and more pointed phrase, and tragedy has similar images: Aeschylus makes the unburied "a meal for the birds" (*Suppliants* 800–801), and Euripides speaks of a bloody "feast" (δαῖτα) for birds and beasts (*Ion* 504–505).

According to the ancient critics, Aristarchus rejected it because in Homer δαίς is always a feast of men or gods, never the eating of animals. The debate is a clear example of how Alexandrian scholars argued: they tested each reading against Homer's usage elsewhere.

Modern editors, including Monro and Allen, print πᾶσι. See [[zenodotus]] and [[aristarchus]].
