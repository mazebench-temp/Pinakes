Lucian's *True Histories* (second century CE) is a parody of travelers' tales. In its second book the narrator reaches the Isle of the Blessed, where the heroes and poets of the past live on, and questions Homer. Homer tells him that he was really a Babylonian named Tigranes, that he wrote all the lines the Alexandrian editors had rejected as spurious, and that he began the Iliad with "wrath" for no particular reason; it simply came into his head.

Homer was glad to answer, the narrator adds, especially after his trial. Thersites had brought a suit against him for outrage (ὕβρις), for the way he had mocked him in the poem, and Homer won, with Odysseus as his advocate (2.20).

The joke depends on the portrait in Iliad 2, where Homer calls Thersites the ugliest man who came to Troy: bandy-legged, lame, hunch-shouldered, with a pointed head and a scant stubble of hair (2.216–219). Lucian's court treats the description as defamation, and the verdict repeats the Iliad. Odysseus, who silenced Thersites with the scepter, defeats him again, this time in words.
