**Homer.** Phoenix places his youth in Hellas. He would not leave Achilles even to be made young again, “as I was when I first left Hellas of the lovely women, fleeing the quarrels of my father Amyntor, son of Ormenus” (9.446–448), and he fled “through Hellas with its wide dancing floors” to Peleus in Phthia (9.478–479). In the Iliad Hellas is a district of Achilles' realm beside Phthia: the Catalogue's Myrmidons hold “Phthia and Hellas of the lovely women” (2.683), and Achilles speaks of the Achaean women “throughout Hellas and Phthia” (9.395; see [[hellas]] and [[phthia]]). Book 10 gives Amyntor a home elsewhere. The boar's-tusk helmet that Odysseus wears on the night raid was stolen by Autolycus from the house of Amyntor, son of Ormenus, at Eleon (10.266–267), and Eleon is a Boeotian town in the Catalogue (2.500; see [[eleon]] and [[amyntor]]).

**Demetrius' reading.** Strabo takes up the question in his account of Thessaly, at Eurypylus' town of Ormenium, which in his day was called Orminium, a village under Pelion on the Gulf of Pagasae (2.734; see [[ormenium]]). Demetrius of Scepsis, the learned commentator on the Trojan Catalogue, said that Phoenix came from Ormenium and fled from there to Peleus. Ormenus, son of Cercaphus, son of Aeolus, founded the place; his sons were Amyntor and Euaemon, the fathers of Phoenix and of Eurypylus; and since Phoenix had left his home, the succession was kept for Eurypylus. Demetrius therefore wrote “as I was when I first left Ormenium, rich in flocks” (Ὀρμένιον πολύμηλον), in place of “left Hellas of the lovely women”. The Catalogue does make Eurypylus the son of Euaemon (2.736; see [[eurypylus]] and [[euaemon]]), and Ormenium is the first of his towns (2.734). The genealogy that joins the two families is not in Homer.

**Crates' Phoenix.** Crates of Mallos made Phoenix a Phocian, reasoning from the helmet of Book 10. Eleon, he said, was a small town on Parnassus, and Autolycus, who lived on Parnassus, would have robbed his neighbors, as burglars do. Demetrius answered that no Eleon is shown on Parnassus, only Neon, founded after the Trojan war, and that burglars do not rob only their neighbors. Others, Strabo adds, write “out of Heleon” in the verse of Book 10, a town of the Tanagran country, and that reading would make Phoenix's flight “through Hellas” to Phthia stranger still (*Geography* 9.5.18).

**The text.** Strabo says only that Demetrius “writes” (γράφει) the verse so; it is not the reading of our manuscripts, and Monro and Allen print Ἑλλάδα καλλιγύναικα. The reading fits Demetrius' genealogy, and the debate shows the problem the critics saw. Book 9 places Phoenix's home in Hellas, near Phthia, and Book 10 places the house of Amyntor, son of Ormenus, at Eleon (9.448, 10.266), and the poem does not reconcile them. The grandfather's name, Ormenus, matches the name of Eurypylus' town, Ormenium.
