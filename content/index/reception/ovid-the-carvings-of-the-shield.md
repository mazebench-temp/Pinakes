**Homer.** Hephaestus begins the Shield with the earth, the sky and the sea, the sun and the full moon, and the constellations, ending with the Bear, which “alone has no share in the baths of Oceanus”. Then come the two cities, one at peace and one at war, and at the end the river Oceanus around the rim (18.483–490, 18.607–608; see [[the-shield-of-achilles]] and [[alone-without-a-share]]).

**Ovid.** In the thirteenth book of the *Metamorphoses* Ajax and Ulysses argue before the assembled chiefs for the arms of the dead Achilles (see [[the-judgment-of-arms]] and [[ovid-dolon-and-rhesus]]). Ulysses tells how he carried Achilles' body from the field on his own shoulders, and the arms with it (*Metamorphoses* 13.280–285), and then turns the arms against his rival: “Was it for this, then, that the sea-blue mother was so ambitious for her son, that heavenly gifts, the work of so great an art, should be put on by a rough soldier without a mind? For he does not know the carvings of the shield, Oceanus and the lands and the stars with the high sky, the Pleiades and the Hyades and the Bear untouched by the sea, and the opposed cities and the shining sword of Orion. He demands to take arms he does not understand!” (*Metamorphoses* 13.288–295). Some texts read *orbes*, “circles”, for *urbes*, “cities”, and bracket the last verse.

**What Ovid takes.** Ulysses' list runs in Homer's order, and two of its phrases translate Homer's. *Pleiadasque Hyadasque* is Πληϊάδας θʼ Ὑάδας τε (18.486), and *inmunemque aequoris Arcton*, “the Bear untouched by the sea”, is the Bear that “alone has no share in the baths of Oceanus” (18.489). Walter Leaf compares Ovid's phrase in his note on the line (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 18.489). The sword of Orion is Ovid's; Homer has “the strength of Orion” (18.486). Ulysses' argument is a reader's: the arms are a work of art, and only a man who can understand the Shield deserves to wear it. In the Odyssey the judges of the arms were the sons of the Trojans and Pallas Athena, and Odysseus wishes he had not won (*Odyssey* 11.543–548).
