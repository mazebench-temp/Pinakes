Strabo describes the Smintheum near Hamaxitus in the Troad, identified with Homer's Chryse. There the statue of Apollo by Scopas had a mouse at its foot. He tells the foundation story, which he credits to the elegiac poet Callinus. Settlers from Crete, the Teucrians, had been told by an oracle to settle where "the earth-born" attacked them. At Hamaxitus mice came out of the ground at night and gnawed their weapons and harness, so they settled there and named Apollo Smintheus, since σμίνθοι was a word for mice.

Modern readers have been tempted to link a mouse-god with a plague, since rodents spread disease. Homer says nothing of mice, and his plague is arrows. The connection is tantalizing and unprovable.
