In *Republic* 2 Socrates lists the myths that must not be told to the young. It includes "the binding of Hera by her son, and Hephaestus being hurled by his father when he was about to defend his mother being beaten, and all the battles of the gods that Homer composed" (378d). These are banned "whether they are composed with hidden meanings or without". The phrase answers the allegorists, who defended such stories as veiled physics.

The binding of Hera by Hephaestus is not in Homer; it was told elsewhere. The hurling is Iliad 1.590–594.
