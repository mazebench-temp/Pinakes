In the fourth book of the *Aeneid*, after Dido and Aeneas have come together in the cave during the storm, Rumor (*Fama*) goes at once through the great cities of Libya (*Aeneid* 4.173). No evil is swifter, and she gathers strength as she goes (*Aeneid* 4.174–175). Then comes Homer's image: *parva metu primo, mox sese attollit in auras / ingrediturque solo et caput inter nubila condit*, “small at first through fear, she soon rises into the air, and walks upon the ground, and hides her head among the clouds” (*Aeneid* 4.176–177). Homer's Strife is “small when first she rears her head” and then “plants her head against the sky and walks upon the earth” (4.442–443).

Virgil keeps the growth and the body that spans earth and sky, and changes almost everything else. Homer gives Strife a family in a single line: she is “the sister and companion of man-slaying Ares” (4.441). Virgil's Rumor is the last child of Earth, the sister of the giants Coeus and Enceladus (*Aeneid* 4.178–180). Strife throws quarrel among the armies, “increasing the groaning of men” (4.444–445). Rumor's weapon is talk. She is a monster with as many watchful eyes, tongues, mouths and ears as she has feathers; she flies by night and keeps watch by day on roofs and towers, as quick to hold on to what is false as to announce what is true (*Aeneid* 4.181–188). She carries the story of Dido and Aeneas to Iarbas, the African king whom Dido refused, and so sets the tragedy of the book in motion (*Aeneid* 4.189–197).

The image passed on through Virgil. In Chaucer's *House of Fame* the goddess Fame at first seems less than a cubit tall, and then stretches until her feet reach the earth and her head touches heaven (Book 3).
