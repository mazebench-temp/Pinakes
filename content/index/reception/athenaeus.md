His guests talk of food, drink and the table, and quote earlier poets and scholars so freely that the work preserves much that is otherwise lost.

The *Deipnosophistae* survives in fifteen books, some of them only in an abridgment, and the conversation moves from dish to dish and from cup to cup. Homer is quoted throughout, as the first authority on the customs of the table, and with him the grammarians who had commented on him. The eleventh book is a catalogue of drinking cups in alphabetical order.

- Nestor's cup, with the treatise of Asclepiades of Myrlea *On the Nestoris*, the doves read as the Pleiades, and Sosibius' “solution” of Nestor's strength (*Deipnosophistae* 11.487f–494b): [[athenaeus-nestors-cup]].
- Aeschylus' saying that his tragedies were “slices from the great banquets of Homer” (*Deipnosophistae* 8.347e): [[aeschylus]].
