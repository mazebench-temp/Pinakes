Athena takes Ares by the hand and speaks to him in a whole line of his names: Ἆρες Ἄρες βροτολοιγὲ μιαιφόνε τειχεσιπλῆτα, “Ares, Ares, ruin of mortals, blood-stained stormer of walls” (5.31). Apollo speaks the same line to him later in the book (5.455), to send him back into the battle that Athena led him out of. See [[ruin-of-mortals]], [[blood-stained]] and [[stormer-of-walls]].

The name is said twice, and it is not scanned the same way twice. The accents show it. The first Ἆρες has a long α and carries a circumflex; the second, Ἄρες, has a short α and an acute. The verse requires this. A hexameter begins with a long syllable, and the line opens with a dactyl, Ἆ-ρες Ἄ-, one long syllable and two short. Only in the vocative, whose last syllable is short, does the difference show in the accent. In the other forms of the name the accent is an acute whatever the length of the first syllable.

Homer treats the first syllable of the god's name as long or short as the verse needs. It is long where Ἄρης begins a line (5.518, 5.594), and short in the formula ὄζος Ἄρηος, “offshoot of Ares”, at the end of one (2.540). The doubled name at 5.31 puts the two quantities side by side.

The freedom was noticed. Martial wanted to write about Earinus, a favorite of the emperor Domitian, but the name would not fit his meter. The Greek poets, he says, call him “Eiarinos”; but they are Greeks, to whom nothing is denied, and who may properly sound Ἆρες Ἄρες. Romans, who serve stricter Muses, may not be so clever (*Epigrams* 9.11.13–17).
