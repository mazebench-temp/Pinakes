In the *Ars Poetica*, Horace contrasts Homer with poets who begin at the very beginning. Homer does not start the return of Diomedes from the death of Meleager, nor the Trojan War "from the twin egg", the egg of Leda from which Helen was born. He "always hurries to the outcome, and snatches the listener into the middle of things (in medias res), as if they were known".

The proem shows the method. It names the wrath, its cost and its starting point, "from the time when first the two of them stood apart in strife", and the story begins nine years into the war. Everything earlier (the Judgment of Paris, Helen, Aulis, the raids) comes into the poem only in allusions and memories, which the Myths gather by period.
