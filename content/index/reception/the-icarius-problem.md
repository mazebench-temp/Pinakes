In the Odyssey Penelope is “the daughter of Icarius” (*Odyssey* 1.329), and the suitors shrink from going to her father's house to ask him to give her in marriage (*Odyssey* 2.52–54). When Telemachus goes to Lacedaemon for news, Menelaus and Helen entertain him, and the poem never mentions his grandfather (book 4). Later tradition made Icarius a Spartan, the brother of Tyndareus; Pausanias saw near Sparta a statue of Modesty set up, it was said, by Icarius at the place where Penelope, choosing to go with her husband, veiled her face (3.20.10–11). Strabo knew another tradition, that Icarius and his sons ruled in Acarnania (10.2.24; see [[icarius]] and [[telemachus]]).

Critics who assumed a Spartan Icarius found it absurd that Telemachus did not meet him. Aristotle's reply illustrates his rule that critics must not assume a meaning and then condemn the poet for contradicting it: perhaps it is as the Cephallenians say, that Odysseus married among them, and that the man was Icadius, not Icarius; the problem arises through a mistake (see [[icadius]], [[cephallenians]] and [[problems-and-solutions]]).
