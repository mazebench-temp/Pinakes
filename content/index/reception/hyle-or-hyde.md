The last of the six Achaeans killed by Hector and Ares is Oresbius, a rich Boeotian. Hyle is in the Boeotian entry of the Catalogue: “those who held Eleon and Hyle and Peteon” (2.500). It is also the home of Tychius, the best of leather-workers, who made Ajax's great shield of seven oxhides (7.219–223; see [[hyle]] and [[oresbius]]).

Strabo discusses the line in his account of Boeotia (*Geography* 9.2.20). Some, he says, wrote ὅς ῥʼ ἐν Ὕδῃ ναίεσκεν, “who lived in Hyde”. He rejects the reading. Hyde is in Lydia, “under snowy Tmolus, in the rich land of Hyde” (20.385), and this man is a Boeotian, as the next words show: “and near him lived other Boeotians” (5.709–710). The same variant was found in the line on Tychius, “Hyde” for “Hyle” at 7.221, and Strabo answers it with a joke: Ajax did not send to Lydia for his shield.

He also identifies the lake. The river [[Cephisus]] flows into Lake Copais, and some took Homer's Cephisian lake for Copais. Strabo says that Homer means instead the lake called Hylice, after the nearby village of Hylae. Copais is large and lies outside the territory of Thebes; the lake by Hyle is small, filled from Copais through underground channels, and lies between Thebes and Anthedon. The lake still bears the name, as Yliki (see [[cephisian-lake]]).

Strabo notes one more thing about the name. Homer lengthens its first syllable in the Catalogue, “and Hyle” (ἠδʼ Ὕλην), as a poetic license, and keeps it short in Book 5 and in the line on Tychius.

Monro and Allen print Ὕλῃ in both places, and the translation follows them. See [[boeotians]] and [[strabo]].
