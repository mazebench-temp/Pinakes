ἄσβεστος = ἀ- (not) + σβέννυμι (quench, extinguish): unquenchable. The phrase ἄσβεστος γέλως appears in Homer only for the gods: here, as Hephaestus bustles about with the wine, and in the Odyssey when he traps Ares and Aphrodite in his net and calls the gods to see (8.326).

From these scenes European languages took the idiom: French *rire homérique*, German *homerisches Gelächter*, English "Homeric laughter". In both scenes the laughter comes from something the lame god does: once his serving, once the trap he sets.
