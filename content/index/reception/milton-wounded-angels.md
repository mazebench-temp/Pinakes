In the sixth book of *Paradise Lost* (1667) the archangel Raphael tells Adam about the war in heaven. On the first day of battle Michael meets Satan in single combat. Michael's sword, from the armory of God, cuts Satan's sword in two and shears into his right side, and “then Satan first knew pain”. The heavenly substance closes again, not long divisible, but first “a stream of nectarous humor” flows from the gash, “sanguine, such as celestial spirits may bleed”, and stains his bright armor (*Paradise Lost* 6.320–334). His angels carry him back to his chariot, gnashing his teeth for anguish, spite and shame. He soon heals, for spirits that live throughout, vital in every part, cannot die except by annihilation (*Paradise Lost* 6.335–353). Elsewhere on the field Gabriel cleaves Moloch, who had threatened to drag him bound at his chariot wheels, down to the waist, and Moloch, with shattered arms and strange pain, flees “bellowing” (*Paradise Lost* 6.354–362).

The borrowings come from Book 5. Satan's nectarous humor, blood and yet not blood, is Homer's ichor, the “immortal blood” that flows in the blessed gods, who eat no bread and drink no wine (5.339–342; see [[ichor]]). Nectar, the drink of Milton's angels as of Homer's gods, gives it its name. The spirit's quick healing is Ares' at the end of the book. Paeëon's drugs heal him, “for he was not made to be mortal”, as fast as fig juice curdles milk (5.899–904). And Moloch's flight is Ares' own. When Diomedes' spear, driven by Athena, goes into his flank, bronze Ares bellows as loud as nine thousand or ten thousand men in battle, and goes up to Olympus in pain to complain to Zeus (5.855–869).

Milton's line on the wound that closes passed in its turn to Pope, the translator of the Iliad. In *The Rape of the Lock* (1714) the scissors that cut Belinda's lock also cut a sylph in two, “but airy substance soon unites again” (canto 3.151–152).
