## Meaning

The words belong to ποιέω, “make, do”. A ποιητής is a maker, and so a poet (see [[poietes]]); ποίησις is making, and so poetry or the composition of a poem; ποίημα is a thing made, a poem; and ἡ ποιητική, with τέχνη understood, is the art of making, poetry as a craft that can be taught and judged. Homer has none of these words in this sense: his poet is an ἀοιδός, a singer, and what he makes is ἀοιδή, song. The language of making appears in the fifth century. Herodotus speaks of the poets (ποιηταί) said to have lived before Homer and Hesiod (2.53), and in Plato's *Symposium* Diotima observes that ποίησις properly means any making, but that the name has been given to one part only, music and meter, and only those who practice it are called poets (205b–c).

## After Aristotle

The word passed into Latin as *poetica* and into the modern languages as the name of a discipline: Horace's *Ars Poetica*, the Renaissance commentaries on Aristotle, and every later “poetics” take their title from ἡ ποιητική.
