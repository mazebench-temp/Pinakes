## Meaning

ἄριστος is the superlative that goes with ἀγαθός, “good”: “best”, in whatever sense the context requires. It can be the best at a craft, as Calchas is the best of the bird-seers (1.69), but its heroic sense is the best man in war. Its comparative is ἀρείων, “better”. From the same stem come ἀριστεύς, “chief, one of the best men” (1.227), ἀριστεύω, “be the best”, and ἀρετή, “excellence”. The root is uncertain.

To be ἄριστος is the heroic ambition. Glaucus and Achilles were both sent to war by their fathers with the same advice: “always to be best, and to stand above the others” (6.208, 11.784).

## In Book 1

The title ἄριστος Ἀχαιῶν, “best of the Achaeans”, is claimed by two men.

{{quote:1c328d00-13a1-58dc-8462-b480058b2d6b}}

Achilles' first move against Agamemnon, before the quarrel has begun, is to describe him as the man “who now claims (εὔχεται) to be far the best of the Achaeans”. The verb makes the title a boast.

{{quote:c2dd9e17-c98d-53e4-8f6a-9dacda2e7a9c}}

In his oath Achilles claims it for himself. The Achaeans will long for him, and Agamemnon will tear his heart in anger that he paid no honor to the best of the Achaeans. He repeats the claim to Thetis (1.412).

Nestor tries to divide the title. He flatters both men as first among the Danaans in counsel and in battle (1.258). Then he weighs them: Achilles is stronger and the son of a goddess, but Agamemnon is greater, “since he rules over more” (1.280–281). Nestor adds, for good measure, that he once kept company with men better (ἀρείοσιν) than either of them (1.260).

Achilles' charge that Agamemnon never goes on ambush “with the best of the Achaeans” (σὺν ἀριστήεσσιν, 1.227) touches the same nerve. Ambush was where a man's courage showed most plainly (13.276–277).

The Iliad gives its own verdict in the Catalogue of Ships. The best of the men was Telamonian Ajax, “as long as Achilles kept up his wrath; for he was by far the strongest” (2.768–769). Achilles himself, grieving for Patroclus, admits a limit: he is such as no other Achaean in war, but in the assembly others are better (18.105–106).
