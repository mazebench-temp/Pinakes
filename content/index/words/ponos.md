## Meaning

πόνος is toil: hard work, the effort and pain it costs, and the business it consists of. The verb πονέομαι is “toil, labor, be busy at”. The word belongs with πένομαι, “toil, be needy”, and πενίη, “poverty”.

In the Iliad πόνος is above all the work of war, and battle itself can be called by the name: “so from their mingling came the shouting and the toil” (4.456), and the armies “toiled on through the fierce battle” (5.84, 5.627). It weighs most on those who lead. Helenus tells Aeneas and Hector that “on you most of all rests the toil” (6.77), and Hector tells Paris that the Trojans “have much toil for your sake” (6.525). Gods can lay it on men: Zeus accepted Agamemnon's offerings “but made the toil greater, a toil none would envy” (2.420), and when Aeneas comes back healed to his companions, they have no time to question him, for “the other toil did not allow it, the toil that the god of the silver bow had awakened” (5.514–518). Hera calls the gathering of the army against Troy her own “labor”, which Zeus must not make vain (4.26, 4.57). Odysseus, before Troy, admits that “the toil here is enough to make a man go home in weariness” (2.291), and the Trojan Socus taunts him as “much-praised Odysseus, insatiable of tricks and toil” (11.430).

The word also names the work of peace. A sacrifice and a meal are labor, and the formula that closes the preparations says that the men “had ceased from the work and made the feast ready” (1.467 = 2.430 = 7.319; see [[feast-type-scene]]). The Achaeans “toiled” at their wall (7.442; see [[the-achaean-wall]]).

## Outside Homer

Hesiod makes Toil a child of Strife: “but hateful Strife bore painful Toil” (Πόνον ἀλγινόεντα, *Theogony* 226), with Forgetfulness, Famine and Pains. The golden race of men lived “far from toils and misery” (ἄτερ τε πόνων καὶ ὀιζύος, *Works and Days* 113). In Herodotus, Dionysius of Phocaea tells the Ionians before Lade that if they will endure hardship they will have toil for the moment and freedom after (*Histories* 6.11.2; see [[on-the-razors-edge]]).
