## Meaning

ἄποινα (always plural) are the goods given to buy back a person: a captive taken alive, or the body of a dead man. The word is related to ποινή (“blood-price, penalty”), the payment that settles a killing. Latin borrowed ποινή as *poena*, the source of English *pain* and *penal*. The formation of the first syllable of ἄποινα is disputed.

Ransom ends a hostility without more bloodshed. The one who pays gives up wealth; the one who accepts gives up a claim. To accept is also to show [[aidos|αἰδώς]] (“respect”) toward the one who asks. To refuse is to choose further violence.

## Related words

- [[geras|γέρας]] (“prize”): what Chryseis was to Agamemnon.
- [[doron|δῶρον]] (“gift”): the hecatomb is “gifts to the lord” (1.390).
- λύω (“release”) and λύομαι (“win release, ransom”): λυσόμενος (1.13, 1.372), λύσαιτε (1.20), λύσω (1.29), ἀπέλυσε (1.95).
- τίνω (“pay”) and ποινή (“blood-price”).

## Outside Homer

Plato would not have poets say that Achilles was so fond of money that he took gifts from Agamemnon and gave back Hector's body only for a price (*Republic* 3.390e).
