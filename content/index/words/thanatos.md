## Meaning

θάνατος means death, and in Homer Death can also be a person. In Book 16, Sleep and Death, twin brothers, carry Sarpedon's body home to Lycia (16.671–683). The verb θνῄσκω ("die") and the adjective θνητός ("mortal") share the root. The gods are ἀθάνατοι, "deathless", and in the Iliad death is the one thing that divides them from men.
