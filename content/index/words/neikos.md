## Meaning

νεῖκος is a quarrel or dispute, and often the abuse that fills one. The verb νεικέω means “quarrel with, rebuke, scold”. Where [[eris|ἔρις]] is the spirit of contention, νεῖκος is usually the quarrel as it is spoken: two men wrangling, one man rebuking another. On the shield of Achilles, a νεῖκος has arisen in the assembly between two men over the blood-price (ποινή) for a man killed (18.497–498).

## In Book 1

{{quote:54cc5918-8444-5a1e-bc80-cc34a5955d71}}

Zeus fears what Thetis' request will cost him at home. Hera is always quarreling with him among the gods, and says that he helps the Trojans.

{{quote:f38327f1-79e7-5d3c-8f2d-054da67e7803}}

Hephaestus urges his mother to be gracious to Zeus, so that he will not scold her again and upset the feast.

## Related words

- [[eris|ἔρις]] (“strife”).
- [[oneidos|ὄνειδος]] (“reproach”) and [[kertomia|κερτόμια]] (“cutting words”).

## Outside Homer

In Empedocles, Νεῖκος (Strife) and Φιλότης (Love) are the two forces that by turns separate and unite the elements (fragment B17 DK).
