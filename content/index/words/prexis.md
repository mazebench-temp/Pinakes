## Meaning

πρῆξις is the noun of πρήσσω (Attic πράττω), “pass through, accomplish”, the Ionic form of Aristotle's πρᾶξις, “action” (see [[praxis]]). In Homer it is the end a doing reaches. Nestor asks the strangers whether they sail κατὰ πρῆξιν, “on some business”, or μαψιδίως, “at random”, like pirates (*Odyssey* 3.72–73, again 9.253–254; see [[piracy]]). Merry takes the business as a trading voyage, comparing πρηκτῆρες (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 3.72): the Phaeacian Euryalus tells Odysseus that he looks like no athlete but like a captain of sailors who are traders, mindful of his cargo and watchful of his gains (*Odyssey* 8.159–164).

With a negative it is the profit that does not come. Achilles tells Priam, “for there is no profit in chilling lament” (24.524; see [[the-two-jars-of-zeus]]), and Odysseus' companions weep twice on Circe's island, but there was no profit in their weeping (οὐ γάρ τις πρῆξις ἐγίγνετο μυρομένοισιν, *Odyssey* 10.202, 10.568). The adjective ἄπρηκτος is “unaccomplished”: “a war that comes to nothing” (2.121), “fruitless strife” (2.376). The noun of the doer, πρηκτήρ, comes in Phoenix's account of what he taught Achilles, to be “a speaker of words and a doer of deeds” (9.443; see [[speaker-of-words-doer-of-deeds]]).
