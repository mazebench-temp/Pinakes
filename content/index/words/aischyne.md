## Meaning

αἰσχύνη is formed from αἶσχος, “disgrace”, the same root as αἰσχρός, “shameful” (see [[aischros]]). Homer's word for the feeling is αἰδώς, the respect and shame that hold a man to his place (see [[aidos]]); αἰσχύνη is the classical word, and Aristotle uses the two as near synonyms. In the *Nicomachean Ethics* shame is “a kind of fear of disrepute”, felt in the body like fear, with blushing; it is not a virtue, since it is more like an emotion than a state, and it suits youth, which errs and is restrained by it, not age (4.9, 1128b10–21).
