## The knot

The complication often includes events outside the play, and some within it; the resolution is everything from the beginning of the change to the end (see [[metabasis]]). Tragedies are best called the same or different by their plots, that is, by having the same complication and resolution, and “many who tie the knot well untie it badly” (1456a7–10). The resolution must come from the plot itself, not from the machine (1454a37–b2; see [[the-machine]]). The French *dénouement*, “untying”, keeps Aristotle's metaphor.
