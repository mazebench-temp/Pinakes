---
title: Slander
greek: διαβολή
kind: term
tags: [style, emotions, kinds-of-speech]
summary: Διαβολή, slander or prejudice, the charge or suspicion that a speaker must dispel or create; in Book I the handbooks' stock in trade, in Book III the subject of a chapter of topics.
aliases: [diabole, prejudice, calumny]
---

Book I faults the handbooks for dealing in slander, pity and anger, which are aimed at the juror and not about the subject (*Rhetoric* 1.1, 1354a16–18). Book III gives the topics for meeting slander: dispel the suspicion; deny the fact, the harm, its extent, its injustice or its shame; offset the wrong with some good; call it an error, a misfortune or a necessity; give another motive; show that the slanderer or others above suspicion are implicated; appeal to an earlier judgment; or attack slander itself (*Rhetoric* 3.15, 1416a4–b4). The slanderer, for his part, praises something small at length and blames something great briefly (*Rhetoric* 1416b5–8).
