## Meaning

ἀλείτης is an offender: one who has wronged another and so must be punished. It belongs with the verb ἀλιταίνω, “offend against, sin against”, which is used of wrongs against men and gods alike. Achilles says of Agamemnon, “he deceived me and wronged me” (ἤλιτεν, 9.375), and in the last book he fears to transgress the commands of Zeus (ἀλίτωμαι, 24.570). The word's origin is uncertain.

When Menelaus sees Paris step out before the Trojans, he rejoices like a hungry lion that comes upon a carcass, “for he thought he would take vengeance on the wrongdoer” (φάτο γὰρ τίσεσθαι ἀλείτην, 3.28). The noun and the verb of vengeance (τίνομαι; see [[poine]]) share the line. For Menelaus the question of blame has one answer. See [[who-is-to-blame]] and [[lion-on-a-carcass]].
