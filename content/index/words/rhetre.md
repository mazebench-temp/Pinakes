## Meaning

ῥήτρη (Attic ῥήτρα) is what has been said and agreed between two parties: a compact. It belongs to the root of ἐρέω, “I will say”, with ῥῆμα, “word”, and ῥήτωρ, “speaker”. Homer has it only once, when the disguised Odysseus proposes to Eumaeus a compact with the gods as witnesses, a wager on the truth of his news (*Odyssey* 14.393–394).

## Related words

- [[oath]]: the compact has the gods as its witnesses (μάρτυροι, as in *Iliad* 1.338).
- [[euangelion|εὐαγγέλιον]] (“reward for good news”): the reward that the compact restates.
- [[eperopeuo|ἠπεροπεύω]] (“deceive”): the penalty is meant to deter deceivers.

## Outside Homer

The Suda glosses ῥῆτραι as “covenants, speeches, agreements” (συνθῆκαι, λόγοι, ὁμολογίαι), and adds that at Tarentum the word meant laws and decrees, and that “among the Spartans a rhetra is a law of Lycurgus, as laid down from an oracle” (παρὰ Λακεδαιμονίοις ῥήτρα Λυκούργου νόμος, ὡς ἐκ χρησμοῦ τιθέμενος, Suda ρ 154; see [[lycurgus-of-sparta]]).
