Aristotle divides style into the strung-on (εἰρομένη), held together only by connectives, the style “that has no end in itself, unless the matter being told is finished” (*Rhetoric* 3.9, 1409a30–31), and the compact (κατεστραμμένη), the style in periods (*Rhetoric* 1409a24–b1). The period pleases because it is the opposite of the unlimited, and it is easy to learn because it can be counted, like verse. It must be complete in sense, and its clauses neither stunted nor too long. A period is either simple, of one clause, or in clauses, and the style in clauses is divided or opposed (see [[antithesis]]).

## Outside the Rhetoric

Demetrius begins *On Style* with the clause and the period, and quotes Aristotle's definition (*On Style* 1–35). Cicero's names for it are ambitus, circuitus, comprehensio and continuatio (*Orator* 204).
