## Meaning

{{quote:bead43ef-f2ab-581d-a787-282407a09bbc}}

εἰρήνη is peace, the state of not being at war. Its origin is unknown. In the Iliad it occurs only in the phrase ἐπʼ εἰρήνης, "in time of peace", and only of Troy's past. Iris uses it first: Priam loves long talk, "as once in time of peace; but relentless war has come" (2.797). Achilles speaks of the wealth Troy held "before, in time of peace, before the sons of the Achaeans came" (9.401–403). As Achilles chases Hector around the walls, they pass the stone troughs where the Trojan women washed their clothes "before, in time of peace, before the sons of the Achaeans came" (22.153–156). Peace in the Iliad is always remembered, never present. The shield of Achilles shows a city at peace, with weddings and a lawsuit, beside a city at war (18.490–540), but the word does not appear there.

## Related words

- [[polemos|πόλεμος]] ("war"): its opposite, ἀλίαστος, "relentless", in the same line (2.797).

## Outside Homer

The Odyssey ends with Zeus' decree for Ithaca: "let there be wealth and peace in plenty" (24.486). Hesiod makes Eirene one of the Seasons (Horai), daughters of Zeus and Themis, with Eunomia, "good order", and Dike, "justice" (*Theogony* 901–902), and in the just city "peace, the nurse of children, is on the land" (*Works and Days* 228).
