## Meaning

ἔπος is a word, an utterance, something said. It began with a digamma (ϝέπος) and is cognate with Sanskrit *vácas*, “word”, and Latin *vox*, “voice”; the aorist εἶπον, “I said”, comes from the same root. In the plural it is often simply “words, speech”, as in the formula ἔπεα πτερόεντα προσηύδα, “addressed him with winged words” (1.201). It is paired with ἔργον, “deed”: ἢ ἔπει ἢ ἔργῳ, “by word or deed” (1.395, 1.504).

Where [[mythos|μῦθος]] tends to be authoritative speech, a command or a threat, ἔπος is the neutral word for what is said.

## In Book 1

Words are the book's substitute for violence, and the adjectives given to them tell its story.

{{quote:62e76069-16d2-506c-8b1e-af0436f9981d}}

Athena tells Achilles to reproach Agamemnon “with words” (ἔπεσιν) rather than draw his sword. Calchas had asked for protection “with words and with hands” (1.77); now words alone are allowed.

{{quote:c6576198-0380-5ca7-b340-e51b63a7239d}}

{{quote:0fcb2cdd-c007-5dc9-875d-5b04eac781be}}

Achilles addresses Agamemnon again “with harsh words” (ἀταρτηροῖς ἐπέεσσιν), and the two men part “having fought with hostile words” (ἀντιβίοισι μαχεσσαμένω ἐπέεσσιν). The verb is the verb of battle.

On Olympus the words change. Zeus fears Hera's “abusive words” (ὀνειδείοις ἐπέεσσιν, 1.519). Hephaestus advises his mother to approach Zeus “with gentle words” (ἐπέεσσι μαλακοῖσιν, 1.582), and the quarrel ends in laughter.

Other uses belong to prophecy and promise. Agamemnon complains that Calchas has never spoken a good word or brought one to pass (1.108). Achilles says a man must heed the word of the goddesses (1.216). Thetis will carry her son's word to Zeus (1.419). Hera complains that Zeus never tells her a word of what he intends (1.543). Nestor is ἡδυεπής, “sweet of speech” (1.248).
