## Meaning

λοιμός is plague: a pestilence that kills many at once. It is a rare word in Homer. According to the scholia, Zenodotus read it also at 1.97, where our text has λοιγόν. The traditional title of Book 1 is Λοιμός, Μῆνις, “Plague, Wrath”.

## In Book 1

{{quote:4001330b-8bf0-5e31-8dca-2ca592516128}}

On the tenth day Achilles calls the assembly and states the problem: if war and plague together are crushing the Achaeans, they will have to sail home, if they can even escape death. He then proposes that they ask a seer why Apollo is angry.

## Outside Homer

Hesiod names the pair as the punishment Zeus sends on a city of unjust men: famine and plague together (λιμὸν ὁμοῦ καὶ λοιμόν, *Works and Days* 243).

When plague struck Athens in 430 BCE, Thucydides reports, people remembered an old verse, “a Dorian war will come, and plague with it”, and argued whether the verse had said λοιμός, “plague”, or λιμός, “famine” (Thucydides 2.54.2–3).
