## Meaning

ῥήτωρ is a speaker, from the root of εἴρω, ἐρέω (“say”); at Athens the word meant the politician who spoke in the assembly and the courts. Homer has the older ῥητήρ in Phoenix's account of what he was to teach Achilles, to be “a speaker of words and a doer of deeds” (9.443; see [[speaker-of-words-doer-of-deeds]]). The adjective ῥητορική, with τέχνη (“art”) understood, is first attested in Plato's *Gorgias* (448d), where Socrates argues that what passes for the art of speaking is no art but a knack, a kind of flattery (463a–465d); in the *Phaedrus* he allows a true art of speaking founded on knowledge of souls (271a–272b). Aristotle, in a lost dialogue, called Empedocles the inventor of rhetoric and Zeno of Elea the inventor of dialectic (Diogenes Laertius 8.57).

## After Aristotle

The three kinds of speech and the three kinds of proof passed into the Latin handbooks; Quintilian sets out the three kinds of cause with Aristotle as his authority (*Education of the Orator* 3.4). See [[three-kinds-of-rhetoric]] and [[three-kinds-of-proof]].
