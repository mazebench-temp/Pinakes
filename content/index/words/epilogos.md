“The epilogue is composed of four things” (*Rhetoric* 3.19, 1419b10): disposing the hearer, amplifying and depreciating, emotion, and recapitulation. The topics for each have been given in the earlier books. Recapitulation belongs here, not in the proem; one should say that one has done what one promised, then compare one's case with the opponent's, directly, ironically or by questions. The end should be without connectives, “so that it may be an epilogue and not a speech: ‘I have spoken; you have heard; you have the facts; judge’” (*Rhetoric* 1420a7–8).

## Outside the Rhetoric

Cicero's conclusion has three parts, enumeration, indignation and complaint (*De inventione* 1.98), which answer to Aristotle's recapitulation, amplification and emotion.
