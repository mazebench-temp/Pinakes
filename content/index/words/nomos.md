## Meaning

νόμος belongs to νέμω, “distribute, hold, graze”, and means what is customary and accepted, and so a law. Homer does not have it (νομός is “pasture”). In Hesiod Zeus “ordained this law for men”: beasts eat one another, but to men he gave justice (*Works and Days* 276–280). Pindar called custom “king of all” (fr. 169a, quoted by Herodotus 3.38), and Sophocles' Antigone appeals against Creon's decree to the unwritten laws of the gods (*Antigone* 450–457; see [[not-of-today-or-yesterday]]). The *Nicomachean Ethics* divides political justice into the natural and the legal (5.7, 1134b18–1135a5).
