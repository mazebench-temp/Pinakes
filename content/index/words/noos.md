## Meaning

νόος is the mind as it perceives and understands: seeing what a situation is, and so what to do about it. It can also mean the plan or intention that results. The verb νοέω means “notice, perceive, understand”, and also “think of, intend”. Kurt von Fritz (“ΝΟΟΣ and ΝΟΕΙΝ in the Homeric Poems”, 1943) described νοεῖν as the realization of a situation, the moment when one sees what it means.

The etymology is disputed. Douglas Frame (*The Myth of Return in Early Greek Epic*, 1978) connected νόος with νέομαι, “return home”, and so with [[nostos|νόστος]]; the connection is not generally accepted.

## In Book 1

{{quote:3bdf4acd-8bdb-502e-a437-b8bab9a524a0}}

Agamemnon tells Achilles not to “steal with his νόος”, that is, not to outwit him.

{{quote:d126eb2c-254f-599d-a09b-ed02b090dc3a}}

Achilles' charge is a failure of νόος. Agamemnon “does not know how to look (νοῆσαι) both before and after”. See [[before-and-after]] and [[foresight]].

{{quote:7c7c852e-aaab-5004-8720-890e35d8cd98}}

Thetis asks her son not to hide his grief in his νόος, “so that we both may know”.

On Olympus the verb becomes the language of secrecy. Zeus sends Thetis away before Hera notices anything (νοήσῃ, 1.522). Hera complains that he never tells her what he has in mind (ὅττι νοήσῃς, 1.543). Zeus answers that what he wishes to devise (νοῆσαι) apart from the gods is not hers to ask about (1.549). Hephaestus advises his mother, “though she understands this herself” (νοεούσῃ, 1.577), to be gracious to Zeus.

## Outside Homer

For the philosophers νοῦς (the contracted form) became intellect itself. In Anaxagoras it is the Mind that orders all things (fragment B12 DK).
