---
title: Urbanity
greek: τὰ ἀστεῖα
kind: term
tags: [style, figure]
summary: Τὰ ἀστεῖα, “urbane sayings”, the witty and well-liked expressions that give the hearer quick learning: metaphor, antithesis and the vivid, often with a surprise.
aliases: [asteion, urbane sayings, wit, urbanity]
---

Aristotle explains wit by learning: “Learning easily is naturally pleasant to all” (*Rhetoric* 3.10, 1410b10), and the words that teach us something quickly are the most pleasant. Rare words are unknown and standard words familiar; metaphor does it best. Urbane sayings come from metaphor, especially by analogy, from antithesis, and from setting things before the eyes (*Rhetoric* 3.10–11, 1410b6–1413b2); many add a deception, so that the hearer learns by surprise and the soul seems to say, “How true! And I was wrong” (*Rhetoric* 1412a19–22). Jokes from changing a letter, words used in two senses and apophthegms work the same way.
