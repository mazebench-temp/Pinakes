## Meaning

ὄναρ is a dream, and ὄνειρος the more common word for it. Dreams in Homer come from outside the sleeper and can carry a god's message. An ὀνειροπόλος (from ὄνειρος and πολέω, “busy oneself with”) is a man who deals with dreams and interprets them.

## In Book 1

{{quote:787ba520-59b5-56bd-8f29-5e009f6b03ae}}

When the plague has lasted nine days, Achilles lists the people who might explain it: a seer (μάντις), a priest (ἱερεύς), or an interpreter of dreams, “for a dream, too, comes from Zeus” (see [[a-dream-comes-from-zeus]]). The seer Calchas answers. See [[prophecy]] and [[divination]].

The remark about dreams gains an edge in the next book. At the start of Book 2 Zeus does send a dream, to Agamemnon, and it is false (2.1–40).

## Related words

- [[theopropion|θεοπρόπιον]] (“oracle”).
- μάντις (“seer”), ἱερεύς (“priest”).

## Outside Homer

In the *Odyssey* Penelope distinguishes dreams that come through the gate of horn, which are true, from those that come through the gate of ivory, which deceive (*Odyssey* 19.562–567).
