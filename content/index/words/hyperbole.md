summary: Hyperbole, the figure of exaggeration, which Aristotle counts among metaphors and calls adolescent, fit for the angry and unfit for an older man.
aliases: [hyperboles, exaggeration]
---

“Well-liked hyperboles are also metaphors” (*Rhetoric* 3.11, 1413a21–22): a man with a black eye is “a basket of mulberries”. Similes become hyperboles by changing the form (*Rhetoric* 1413a24–29). “Hyperboles are adolescent, for they show vehemence” (*Rhetoric* 1413a30–31), as in Achilles' refusal of Agamemnon's gifts; it is inappropriate for an older man to use them, but the Attic orators use them most (*Rhetoric* 1413b1–2).

## Outside the Rhetoric

Demetrius calls hyperbole the most frigid of all things (*On Style* 124); Quintilian allows it as a virtue when the thing itself goes beyond nature (8.6.67–76).
