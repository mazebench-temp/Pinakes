“A speech has two parts: for one must state the subject it is about, and demonstrate it” (*Rhetoric* 3.13, 1414a31–32). These are the statement (πρόθεσις) and the proof (πίστις). The handbooks divide the speech further, into narration, reply, comparison and recapitulation, but narration belongs only to judicial speech, and the reply and comparison are parts of the proof. At most a speech has four parts: proem, statement, proof and epilogue (*Rhetoric* 1414b7–9). Theodorus' school and Licymnius multiply names without differences (*Rhetoric* 1414b13–18). Chapters 14–19 then treat each part: the proem, slander, narration, proof, interrogation and jest, and the epilogue.

## Outside the Rhetoric

The Latin handbooks keep the fuller division Aristotle rejects: exordium, narration, division, proof, refutation and conclusion (*Rhetorica ad Herennium* 1.4; Cicero, *De inventione* 1.19).
