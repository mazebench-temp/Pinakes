## Meaning

ποθή is longing for what is absent: a lost leader, a dead friend, home. The verb ποθέω means “long for, miss”. In the Catalogue of Ships the men of Protesilaus, killed on landing, and of Philoctetes, left behind on Lemnos, are “not without a leader, though they longed for their leader” (2.703, 2.726).

## In Book 1

{{quote:ee051648-367b-5173-91a0-75535c70c8dd}}

Achilles swears on the scepter that someday a longing for Achilles will come upon all the sons of the Achaeans, and Agamemnon will be powerless to help them as they die at Hector's hands. He speaks of himself by name, as the object of that longing.

{{quote:cf7160a8-260c-5635-8b62-0aa02701d7a1}}

The book's last picture of Achilles turns the longing round. Sitting by his ships, he stays away from assembly and war, and he longs (ποθέεσκε, day after day) for the war cry and for battle. The army will miss him, and he misses the fighting.
