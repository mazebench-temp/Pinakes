## Meaning

λώβη is an outrage: a deliberate injury to a man's honor by word or deed, and the disgrace that it brings. The verb λωβάομαι is “outrage, treat shamefully”, and a λωβητήρ is an outrager or slanderer. The etymology is uncertain.

The word runs through the quarrels of the first books. Achilles, stopping short of the sword, tells Agamemnon that “otherwise, son of Atreus, this insult would be your last” (λωβήσαιο, 1.232), and Thersites repeats the line against the same king (2.242). The army, laughing at Thersites' beating, calls him a “word-slinging slanderer” (λωβητῆρα ἐπεσβόλον, 2.275). Hector wishes his brother had never been born rather than be “a disgrace like this” (λώβην, 3.42): Paris is the outrage in person.

An outrage demands payment. Achilles will not be reconciled until Agamemnon has paid back “all the heart-grieving outrage” (θυμαλγέα λώβην, 9.387). Menelaus reminds the Trojans of “the outrage and shame” with which they outraged him (λώβης τε καὶ αἴσχεος, ἣν ἐμὲ λωβήσασθε, 13.622–623). Agamemnon kills the sons of Antimachus to make them pay for their father's “shameful outrage”, his proposal to kill Menelaus when he came to Troy as an envoy (11.138–142).
