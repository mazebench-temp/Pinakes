## Meaning

δόλος is a trick or a snare, anything contrived to catch or to deceive. In a simile of the Odyssey it is the fisherman's bait, cast down from a long rod as food for the little fishes (*Odyssey* 12.251–252). It is the ambush on the Shield of Achilles, whose victims “did not foresee the trick at all” (18.526), the net that Hephaestus forges to catch Ares and Aphrodite (*Odyssey* 8.276, 8.282, 8.317), and the wooden horse that Odysseus brought into the citadel of Troy (*Odyssey* 8.492–494; see [[apate]]). Apollo draws Achilles away from the city “by a trick” (21.599, 21.604; see [[apollo-deceives-achilles]]), and Odysseus, lifted by Ajax in the wrestling, “did not forget his trickery” (23.725).

It is set against force. Lycurgus killed Areithous “by guile, not by strength at all” (7.142), and the Cyclopes ask Polyphemus whether someone is killing him by guile or by force (δόλῳ ἠὲ βίηφιν), to hear that Nobody is killing him by guile and not by force (*Odyssey* 9.406–408). Athena tells Telemachus to kill the suitors “whether by guile or openly” (*Odyssey* 1.296).

A δόλος is woven. The king of Lycia “wove another cunning trap” for Bellerophon, an ambush of his best men (6.187–190); Odysseus in the Cyclops' cave wove all kinds of tricks and cunning (πάντας δὲ δόλους καὶ μῆτιν ὕφαινον, *Odyssey* 9.422); and the trick of Penelope is a web (*Odyssey* 2.93–110, 19.137–156; see [[the-web-of-penelope]] and [[weaving]]).

The word praises or reproaches according to who uses it. Helen tells Priam that Odysseus “knows all kinds of tricks and shrewd counsels” (3.202), and in the Odyssey he names himself as the man known to all men for his tricks (*Odyssey* 9.19–20). Agamemnon calls him “skilled in evil tricks” (4.339), the Trojan Socus “insatiable of tricks and toil” (11.430), and Athena, smiling, uses the same words of him (δόλων ἆτʼ, *Odyssey* 13.287–293). In the Odyssey the word also names the murder of Agamemnon, by the guile of Aegisthus and of his wife (*Odyssey* 3.235, 4.92, 11.439; see [[the-murder-of-agamemnon]]).
