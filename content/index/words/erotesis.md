---
title: Interrogation
greek: ἐρώτησις
kind: term
tags: [style, proof]
summary: Ἐρώτησις, the questioning of the opponent in a speech, which Aristotle allows when one more question will bring out an absurdity, a contradiction or a sophistical answer.
aliases: [erotesis, interrogation, questioning]
---

Interrogation is opportune, Aristotle says, when the opponent has granted one premise and one more question will expose an absurdity, as when Pericles questioned Lampon; when one premise is evident and the conclusion can be stated, as Socrates did with Meletus; when the opponent can be shown to contradict himself or say something paradoxical; and when he can answer only sophistically (*Rhetoric* 3.18, 1418b39–1419a19). Otherwise it should be avoided, for the hearer cannot follow many questions. In answering, one should draw distinctions, give the solution at once, and when the conclusion is put as a question, give one's reason, as Sophocles did to Pisander and the Laconian ephor to his auditor (*Rhetoric* 1419a20–b2). Book III ends the chapter with the use of jest (see [[geloion]]).
