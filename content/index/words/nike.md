## Meaning

νίκη is victory, and νικάω means “win, be victorious”, and more widely “prevail over, surpass”. The word covers any contest. Nestor surpasses the sons of the Achaeans in the assembly (νικᾷς, 2.370). The singer Thamyris boasted that he would win even if the Muses sang against him, and they maimed him (νικησέμεν, 2.597; see [[thamyris-and-the-muses]]). Hephaestus complains that a quarrel over mortals spoils the gods' feast, “since worse things prevail” (τὰ χερείονα νικᾷ, 1.576; see [[worse-things-prevail]]).

In battle a victory is given by the gods. Menelaus, arming to face Hector, says that “the bounds of victory are held among the immortal gods” (νίκης πείρατʼ, 7.101–102; see [[the-bounds-of-victory]]), and the moment of winning is marked by κῦδος, the glory that a god grants (see [[kydos]]). Victory in Homer is not always easy to see. In Book 3 the duel is fought to decide “whichever of us wins” (3.71), and it ends without a death; Agamemnon claims that victory clearly belongs to Menelaus (3.457), and the gods decide in Book 4 that the Trojans will not honor the claim. See the note on Book 3.

## Related words

- [[kydos|κῦδος]] (“glory”): the radiance of victory, granted by a god.
- κρείσσων (“stronger”): “whichever of us wins and proves the stronger” (3.71, 3.92).
- [[aristos|ἄριστος]] (“best”).
