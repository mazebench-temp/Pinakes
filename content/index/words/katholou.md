## The universal

Poetry speaks rather of universals, history of particulars; a universal is the sort of thing a person of a certain sort will say or do by probability or necessity, and this is what poetry aims at, though it attaches names (see [[poetry-and-history]] and [[eikos]]). The word appears in three other places in the *Poetics*: Crates was the first at Athens to compose stories and plots “of a general kind” (1449b7–9; see [[crates]]); thought is shown where people declare something universal (1450b11–12; see [[dianoia]]); and a poet should set out his story in general terms before he adds the episodes, as Aristotle does for the *Iphigenia* (1455b1–8; see [[the-recognition-of-orestes-and-iphigenia]]). In the *Metaphysics* Aristotle credits Socrates with two things, inductive arguments and universal definitions (M 4, 1078b27–29).
