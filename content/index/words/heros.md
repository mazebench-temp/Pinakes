## Meaning

In Homer ἥρως is a respectful word for a warrior. It carries no hint of divinity. The Iliad calls the men who fought at Troy “demigods” (ἡμίθεοι) only once, looking back from a later age (12.23). In later Greek the heroes are the powerful dead, honored with cult at their tombs; Hesiod's race of heroes, “who are called demigods”, already points that way (*Works and Days* 159–160). The etymology is uncertain.

## In Book 1

{{quote:37ba8e17-2be0-5269-9b77-b0cbb0760472}}

The first heroes of the poem are dead ones, whose souls the wrath sent to Hades.

{{quote:dca84a4c-ccea-54f9-bfe7-9bd4f97d0cae}}

The first living ἥρως is a king in a rage. The title is given to Agamemnon just as he rises, in distress, to answer Calchas.
