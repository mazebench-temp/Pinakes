## Meaning

ἵστωρ is formed from the root of οἶδα, “know”, and means the man who knows: a judge or witness, whose knowledge settles a question. The word comes twice in Homer. On the Shield two men dispute the penalty for a dead man, “and both were eager to win a decision at the hands of an arbiter” (ἐπὶ ἴστορι πεῖραρ ἑλέσθαι, 18.501). Walter Leaf notes that ἐπί, “at the hands of”, has no exact parallel in Homer, though the use is common in later Greek, and takes πεῖραρ as “an issue, an end of the matter” (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 18.501). The other use is at the chariot race in Book 23, where Idomeneus offers Ajax, son of Oileus, a wager on which horses are leading, with Agamemnon to be the ἵστωρ between them (23.485–487).

The arbiter of 18.501 is not named. The scene goes on to the elders, who rise in turn to give judgment, and to the two talents of gold set out for the one who speaks the straightest (18.503–508; see [[the-trial-on-the-shield]]).
