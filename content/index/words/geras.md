## Meaning

γέρας (“prize”, “gift of honor”) is a portion set apart for someone as a mark of rank. At Troy it is chosen from the spoils of a sacked city before the rest is shared out, and it is given by the whole army: δόσαν υἷες Ἀχαιῶν, “the sons of the Achaeans gave” (1.162, 1.276, 1.392). It is property and a public statement of worth at once. To lose it is to lose [[time|τιμή]].

The word reaches beyond booty. The gods receive the libation and the savor of sacrifice as their γέρας (4.48–49). A mound and a gravestone are “the γέρας of the dead” (16.457). The adjective ἀγέραστος means “without a prize”. An old connection with γέρων (“old man”) and γῆρας (“old age”) would make the prize an honor first paid to elders, but the link is uncertain.

## In Book 1

{{quote:6844b909-b971-5ad2-9684-41d8a60770cc}}

Agamemnon's first answer to losing Chryseis is to demand a replacement. It would not be fitting, he says, for him alone of the Argives to be ἀγέραστος. This is not greed alone. A leader without a γέρας is a leader without honor, and everyone can see it.

{{quote:79f9c6eb-b42d-5e38-a106-065f6e467dfa}}

Achilles answers with the practical difficulty. The spoils have been divided, and it would not be fitting to gather them back. He offers instead a promise: threefold and fourfold repayment when Troy is taken (1.127–129).

{{quote:36c2072e-46e4-5381-8bcf-816c37f86de8}}

From here the quarrel is about distribution. Achilles does most of the fighting and goes back to his ships with the lesser γέρας, “something small but dear” (see [[achilles-raids]]). When Agamemnon says he will take Briseis so that Achilles may know how much greater he is (1.185–186), the prize becomes a direct measure of rank between the two men. Nestor reminds Agamemnon that it was the Achaeans who gave her to Achilles (1.276). Achilles makes the same point with bitterness: “you who gave her are taking her away” (1.299).

{{quote:c2acccb0-d262-568d-9dd1-66dc323bd7b7}}

The line returns word for word in Thetis' prayer to Zeus (1.507).

## Related words

- [[time|τιμή]] (“honor”): what the γέρας makes visible.
- [[doron|δῶρον]] (“gift”): Achilles calls the prize a gift (δῶρα, 1.230).
- [[apoina|ἄποινα]] (“ransom”): the price refused for Chryseis.
- δασμός (“division”, 1.166) and δατέομαι (“divide”, 1.125, 1.368): the sharing of spoils. See [[division-of-spoils]].

## Outside Homer

Aristotle quotes 1.356 in the *Rhetoric* (2.2, 1378b) as the voice of a man who has been slighted (see [[aristotle-anger]]).
