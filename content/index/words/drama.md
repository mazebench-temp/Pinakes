## Doing

Drama imitates people acting and doing, where epic narrates: tragedy is an imitation “by people acting and not through narration” (1449b26; see [[modes-of-imitation]] and [[definition-of-tragedy]]). Homer alone of the epic poets made his imitations dramatic, and in the *Margites* he was the first to dramatize the ridiculous (1448b34–38; see [[margites]]). The verb δρᾶν is the tragedians' own: “the doer must suffer” (δράσαντι παθεῖν) is the old saying that the chorus of Aeschylus' *Libation Bearers* sings (313). Aristotle's word for action, πρᾶξις, comes from the Attic πράττειν (see [[praxis]]).
