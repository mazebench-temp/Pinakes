## Meaning

ἡδύς is “sweet”, of honey, wine, sleep and song, and ἡδονή, which Homer does not use, the pleasure itself. Plato's *Philebus* treats pleasure as the restoration of a living being to its natural state (31d–32b); Aristotle's *Rhetoric* uses the same account, while the *Nicomachean Ethics* rejects it, since pleasure is not a process of coming to be but an activity, and completes the activity it goes with (7.12, 1153a7–17; 10.4, 1174b23).
