---

Asyndeta must be acted, Aristotle says, “and not said as one thing with the same character and tone” (*Rhetoric* 3.12, 1413b30–31): “I came, I met him, I begged.” Since the connective makes many things one, taking it out makes one thing many, and so asyndeton amplifies; Homer's triple naming of Nireus works the same way (*Rhetoric* 1413b31–1414a7). The speech should end without connectives, “so that it may be an epilogue and not a speech” (*Rhetoric* 3.19, 1420a7–8).

## Outside the Rhetoric

Demetrius says that asyndeton is fit for debate and delivery, and gives the same reason (*On Style* 193–194).
