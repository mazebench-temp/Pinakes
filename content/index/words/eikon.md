“The simile, too, is a metaphor, for the difference is small” (*Rhetoric* 3.4, 1406b20). When Homer says of Achilles “he rushed on like a lion”, it is a simile; “a lion, he rushed on” would be a metaphor. The simile is useful in prose, but seldom, since it is poetic; it is less pleasant than the metaphor because it is longer and does not say that this is that (*Rhetoric* 3.10, 1410b17–20). The best similes, like the best metaphors, work by analogy (*Rhetoric* 3.11, 1412b34–1413a15). The line of Homer that Aristotle quotes is not in our text; the nearest is “the son of Peleus rushed against him from the other side like a lion” (*Iliad* 20.164).

## Outside the Rhetoric

Quintilian reverses the relation: “on the whole, metaphor is a shorter simile” (8.6.8). For Homer's similes, see [[similes]].
