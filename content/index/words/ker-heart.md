## Meaning

κῆρ (neuter, “heart”) is an old word, related to καρδία (Epic [[kradie|κραδίη]]), Latin *cor* and English *heart*. In Homer it is seldom the organ itself. It is the heart as the place where one feels, and it often stands in the accusative, “at heart” (χωόμενος κῆρ, 1.44), or in the phrase φίλον κῆρ, “one's own heart”.

It must be kept apart from κήρ (feminine), “doom”. The two words have the same letters and differ in accent and gender; see [[ker-doom|κήρ]]. In Book 1 κῆρ “heart” always has the circumflex, while “doom” appears once, as κὴρ (1.228).

## In Book 1

{{quote:202e7666-b1d3-5a69-a8a5-16d262377b6b}}

Apollo comes down from Olympus χωόμενος κῆρ, “angry at heart”: the accusative says where the anger is felt.

{{quote:8abdce84-18c6-5c4b-8248-b5684ab52038}}

Achilles, withdrawn from assembly and war, “wasted away his own heart” (φθινύθεσκε φίλον κῆρ). The verb is iterative: it happened day after day.

{{quote:3b337c12-b3ad-521a-b960-c2c9a3ecb0e7}}

The last two uses frame the second half of the book. Achilles' withdrawal consumes his heart; Hera's submission bends hers. Both have been silenced by a stronger will.
