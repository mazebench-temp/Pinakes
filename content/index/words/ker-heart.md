## Meaning

κῆρ (neuter, “heart”) is an old word, related to καρδία (Epic [[kradie|κραδίη]]), Latin *cor* and English *heart*. In Homer it is seldom the organ itself. It is the heart as the place where one feels, and it often stands in the accusative, “at heart” (χωόμενος κῆρ, 1.44), or in the phrase φίλον κῆρ, “one's own heart”.

It must be kept apart from κήρ (feminine), “doom”. The two words have the same letters and differ in accent and gender; see [[ker-doom|κήρ]]. In Book 1 κῆρ “heart” always has the circumflex, while “doom” appears once, as κὴρ (1.228).
