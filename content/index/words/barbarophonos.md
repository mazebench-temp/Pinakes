## Meaning

{{quote:1341487d-fb70-5bef-8a85-d45b0b12b9e6}}

βαρβαρόφωνος = βάρβαρος + φωνή ("voice, speech"): "whose speech is barbarous", unintelligible to Greek ears. The word occurs only here in Homer. βάρβαρος itself does not occur in Homer at all. It is usually explained as imitative, a doubling of meaningless syllables, "bar-bar", the sound of a language one does not understand, and it is often compared with Sanskrit *barbara-*, "stammering", and Latin *balbus*, "stammering".

In the Iliad the word describes sound, not rank or race. Trojans and Achaeans speak to each other without interpreters, and the poem notices language only among Troy's allies. Iris tells Hector that "the tongues of men scattered far and wide are many and various" (2.804), and the Trojan army sounds like a flock of bleating ewes, "for they had not all one speech or one voice, but their tongues were mixed, and the men were called from many lands" (4.433–438). Why the Carians alone are given the word is not known. They were the non-Greek people whom the Greeks of Ionia knew best: at Miletus, Herodotus says, the first Ionian settlers took Carian wives (1.146).

Thucydides noticed the absence of the plain word. Homer, he says, never speaks of barbarians, because the Greeks had not yet been set apart under one name as their opposite (1.3.3).

## Related words

- ἄλλη δ᾽ ἄλλων γλῶσσα, "one tongue and then another" (2.804): the languages of Troy's allies. See [[trojan-allies]].
- ἀλλόθροος, "of other speech", of foreign peoples in the Odyssey (1.183).
- ἀγριόφωνος, "of savage speech", of the [[Sintians]] of Lemnos (Odyssey 8.294).

## Outside Homer

Strabo discusses the word at length (*Geography* 14.2.28). He takes βάρβαρος to have imitated harsh or thick speech, like βατταρίζειν, "to stammer", and suggests that Homer called the Carians βαρβαρόφωνοι because, living among Greeks, they spoke Greek badly. By Herodotus' time βάρβαροι was the name for all non-Greeks, and his history begins by promising to record the great deeds "of Greeks and of barbarians".
