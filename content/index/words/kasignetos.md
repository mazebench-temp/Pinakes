## Meaning

κασίγνητος is a compound of κάσις, an old word for a brother or a sister that survives in tragedy, and -γνητος, “born”: one born with another. αὐτοκασίγνητος, “own brother”, stresses the shared blood. Helen's brothers Castor and Polydeuces are “my own brothers, whom one mother bore with me” (3.238), and Podarces is the own brother of Protesilaus (2.706). κασιγνήτη is a sister. The later word ἀδελφός, “of the same womb”, appears in Homer in the Epic form ἀδελφεός: Menelaus comes unbidden to his brother's sacrifice, “for he knew in his heart how his brother was laboring” (2.409). γνωτός, “known”, can mean a kinsman or a brother; Helen left her γνωτοί when she followed Paris (3.174).

## Brothers in the Iliad

**The sons of Atreus.** The war is Menelaus' and Agamemnon leads it (see [[war-for-menelaus]] and [[atreidae]]). The elder brother protects the younger. In Book 4 he holds the wounded Menelaus by the hand and mourns him as dead (4.148–182); in Book 7 he takes him by the right hand again and stops him from answering Hector's challenge, since Hector is the better man (7.107–120).

**The sons of Priam.** Priam has many sons, and the poem shows them as brothers. Hector rebukes Paris before the duel (3.38–57), and Paris wears the corselet of his brother Lycaon (3.333). Legitimate sons and bastards fight side by side, and die side by side (4.489–504, 11.101–104; see [[nothos]]). Hector cannot hold back when Achilles kills Polydorus, his youngest brother (20.419–423), and at the end Athena takes the shape of Deiphobus, the brother he loves best, to make him stand and fight (22.226–247).

**Vengeance.** A brother is the first to avenge a brother. Coon, Antenor's eldest son, strikes Agamemnon to avenge his brother Iphidamas, and is killed over his body (11.248–263).

**The gods.** Zeus, Poseidon and Hades are brothers who divided the world by lot (15.187–193). Hera is Zeus' sister as well as his wife, and he addresses her as “his sister and wife” (κασιγνήτην ἄλοχόν τε, 16.432, 18.356); in Book 4 she claims her rank on both counts (4.58–61). Strife is “the sister and companion of man-slaying Ares” (4.441).
