“The proem is the beginning of a speech, as the prologue is in poetry and the prelude in piping” (*Rhetoric* 3.14, 1414b19–20). The epideictic proem is like the prelude: the speaker says whatever he likes and then strikes the keynote. The judicial proem does what the openings of epics and plays do: it gives a sample of the subject, “Sing, goddess, the wrath” (*Rhetoric* 1415a12–16; see [[proem]]). That is its necessary work; the other proems are remedies, drawn from the speaker, the hearer, the subject and the opponent: dispelling or creating slander, making the hearer well disposed, attentive or inattentive (*Rhetoric* 1415a24–b9). They are all for a poor hearer, who listens to what is outside the subject.

## Outside the Rhetoric

Cicero and Quintilian make the exordium's task to render the hearer well disposed, attentive and ready to learn (Cicero, *De inventione* 1.20; Quintilian 4.1.5).
