## Meaning

βουλή is what one wills and plans. It is the noun of βούλομαι, “want, prefer”, and it covers a range from the will of a god to a piece of advice. It can mean a purpose or plan, counsel given in debate, or the council of elders that gives it: in Book 2 Agamemnon first seats a council (βουλή) of the elders by Nestor's ship (2.53). The verb βουλεύω means “plan, deliberate”, and βουληφόρος, “counsel-bearing”, is a title of leaders who advise.

βούλομαι in Homer often means “prefer, choose one thing rather than another”, with ἤ, “rather than”.

## In Book 1

{{quote:1b4c453c-646c-5e94-a341-f61e540648db}}

The proem's first sentence ends with Διὸς δʼ ἐτελείετο βουλή, “and the plan of Zeus was being fulfilled”. Ancient readers disagreed about which plan it meant. Some took it to be the promise to Thetis that this book will make. The scholia quote the opening of the *Cypria*, which ends with the same half-line, for another answer: Zeus planned the war to relieve the Earth of the weight of too many people. See [[plan-of-zeus]].

{{quote:61d54813-940e-56b6-8a6f-4ebf5e4584e1}}

Agamemnon uses the verb three times in a few lines. He much prefers to keep Chryseis at home; he prefers her to his wedded wife (προβέβουλα, a rare perfect); yet he would rather the people be safe than perish. Achilles earlier hoped that Apollo might “be willing” to keep ruin away (1.67).

{{quote:cd0a7418-e678-5cac-9d0c-c83a096c23c2}}

Nestor calls the two quarreling men the first of the Danaans in counsel and in battle. His own claim to be heard rests on βουλή: the great men of his youth “heeded my counsels and obeyed my word” (1.273). When Agamemnon proposes an embassy to Chryse, it is to be led by a man who is βουληφόρος (1.144).

{{quote:eb3cca80-10e6-5bce-9596-0e6ecb355413}}

The book ends with a plan made on Olympus. Zeus and Thetis part “when the two had made their plans” (βουλεύσαντε, 1.531). Hera sees at once that Thetis has been laying plans (βουλάς) with him, and asks which god it was. By the end of Book 1 the plan of Zeus announced in the proem has been set going, and Hera has been told that she will not share it (1.545–550).

## Related words

- [[metis|μῆτις]] (“cunning, counsel”): Zeus is μητίετα, “the counselor”.
- [[noos|νόος]] (“understanding”) and [[mythos|μῦθος]] (“speech”): Zeus' “counsels” at 1.545 are μῦθοι.
- [[themis|θέμιστες]] (“ordinances”): Nestor tells Agamemnon that Zeus gave him the θέμιστες “so that you may take counsel” (βουλεύῃσθα, 9.99).
- See [[plan-of-zeus]] and [[assembly]].

## Outside Homer

The opening lines of the *Cypria*, quoted in the scholia on 1.5, tell how Zeus pitied the Earth, weighed down by the countless tribes of men, and planned the Trojan War to lighten her load; they end, like Iliad 1.5, with “and the plan of Zeus was being fulfilled” (see [[cypria-plan-of-zeus]]).
