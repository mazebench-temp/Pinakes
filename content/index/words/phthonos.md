## Meaning

φθόνος is the grudging of another's good, and in archaic Greek also the gods' grudging of human success. Pindar prays that his victors may escape it, and Herodotus' Artabanus warns Xerxes that the god, being jealous, strikes down what is great (7.10ε). Aristotle separates envy from indignation (νέμεσις), which is pain at undeserved good fortune and belongs to a good character, while envy is pain at the good fortune of equals as such (see [[nemesis]] and [[aristotle-nemesis]]). In the *Nicomachean Ethics* envy, with malice and shamelessness, is one of the emotions whose names already imply badness (2.6, 1107a9–12).
