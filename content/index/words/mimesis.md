## Meaning

μίμησις is the noun of μιμέομαι, “imitate, represent, mimic”, from μῖμος, an actor in a mime (see [[mime]]). μίμημα is the thing produced, an imitation or likeness, and μιμητής the imitator. The word does not occur in Homer. “Representation” is the other common English rendering; the translation keeps “imitation”, so that the family of words stays visible.

## Before and after Aristotle

Plato distinguishes simple narrative from narrative through imitation, in which the poet speaks as if he were someone else, and illustrates it by retelling the opening of the Iliad without speeches (*Republic* 3.392d–394b; see [[plato-chryses-diegesis]]). In Book 10 he goes further: the painter and the poet make imitations at the third remove from the truth, and the poet must be banished from the well-ordered city (595a–608b). Aristotle's account answers him point by point: imitation is natural, it teaches, it gives a pleasure proper to it, and tragedy, through pity and fear, brings about the [[katharsis]] of such feelings.
