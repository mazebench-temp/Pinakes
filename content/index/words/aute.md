## Meaning

ἀϋτή is a shout or cry, especially the cry of men going into battle, and so the battle itself. It belongs with the verb ἀΰω, “shout”. A good fighter in Homer is βοὴν ἀγαθός, “good at the war cry”, as Menelaus often is. Achilles' own cry will turn a battle. When he appears unarmed at the trench after Patroclus' death, he shouts three times and the Trojans panic (18.217–229).

The form ἀϋτήν has the same letters as αὐτήν, “her”, and differs only in its diaeresis and accent.
