## Meaning

πειράω, more often in the middle πειράομαι, means “make trial of”. With the genitive of a person it means to put someone to the test; with an infinitive, to try to do something. It is a verb of finding out: the one who makes the trial does not yet know, and the trial is meant to show what is there. Achilles uses it as a dare in Book 1. Agamemnon may take the girl, but let him try to carry off anything else: “Come, try it, so that these men too may know” (πείρησαι, 1.302). What they would learn is that the king's dark blood would gush out around Achilles' spear (1.303).

Homer uses only the verb. The noun πεῖρα, “trial, attempt”, common in later Greek, does not occur in the Iliad or the Odyssey. The family belongs with Latin *experior* (“try, experience”) and *periculum* (“trial, danger”).

A test can be made with words. Agamemnon will test the army “with words” (ἔπεσιν πειρήσομαι, 2.73) by saying what he does not mean. When Priam questions the disguised Hermes, the god twice answers, “you are testing me, old man” (πειρᾷ ἐμεῖο γεραιέ, 24.390, 24.433). In the Odyssey the hero, home in disguise, tries the people he has come back to before he makes himself known. With his father he announces “I will make trial of my father” (πατρὸς πειρήσομαι, Odyssey 24.216), and chooses to try him first “with mocking words” (κερτομίοις ἐπέεσσιν πειρηθῆναι, 24.240).

The test of Book 2 returns in Book 9. After a day of defeat Agamemnon calls an assembly and repeats, nearly word for word, the opening and the close of the speech with which he tested the army (9.18–28, repeating 2.111–118 and 2.139–141). This time he means it. Diomedes answers that he will oppose him “as is the right, lord, in the assembly” (ἣ θέμις ἐστὶν ἄναξ ἀγορῇ, 9.33). It is the phrase with which Agamemnon had introduced his test as a custom (2.73; see [[themis|θέμις]]).

## Related words

- [[themis|θέμις]] (“what is right, custom”): the test is made ἣ θέμις ἐστί, “as is the custom” (2.73).
- [[apate|ἀπάτη]] (“deceit”) and [[pseudos|ψεῦδος]] (“lie”): a test with words says what the speaker does not mean.
- [[boule|βουλή]] (“council”): the test was planned in the council of elders (2.53–83).
- See [[the-test-of-the-army]].

## Outside Homer

Pindar: “trial is the test of mortals” (διάπειρά τοι βροτῶν ἔλεγχος, *Olympian* 4.18). The same compound, διάπειρα, “trial, testing”, is the name by which the episode of Book 2 is known, the Diapeira.
