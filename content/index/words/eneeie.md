## Meaning

ἐνηής is “gentle, kindly”, said of a friend, and ἐνηείη, found only once, is the quality itself. Homer has the two words six times, and four are of Patroclus. Zeus, seeing Hector put on Achilles' armor, says that he has killed the companion of the best of men, “gentle and strong” (ἐνηέα τε κρατερόν τε, 17.204), and Lycaon, begging Achilles for his life, says the same half-verse of the man Hector killed (21.95–96). Menelaus bids the Ajaxes and Meriones remember Patroclus' gentleness (17.670–672), and after the burning of his body his companions, weeping, gather “the white bones of their gentle companion” (ἑτάροιο ἐνηέος ὀστέα λευκά, 23.252). The other two are of friends as well. Nestor thanks Achilles for remembering him, a kindly friend, with a cup given at the funeral games as a keepsake of Patroclus (23.615–650), and in the Odyssey Odysseus is glad to see a friendly companion in the contest when Athena, in a man's likeness, marks his throw of the discus (*Odyssey* 8.193–200).

The poem shows the quality before it names it. Patroclus cuts the arrow from the wounded Eurypylus' thigh and lays a root on the wound (11.842–848), and stays in his hut cheering him with talk while the battle comes to the wall (15.390–394; see [[healing]]); he comes to Achilles weeping for the Argives (16.2–29; see [[pity]] and [[tears]]); and Briseis, mourning over his body, weeps for him as one who was always kind (μείλιχον αἰεί, 19.300).
