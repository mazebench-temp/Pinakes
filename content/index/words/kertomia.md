## Meaning

κερτόμιος means “taunting, mocking”, and the neuter plural κερτόμια, with or without ἔπεα, “words”, means taunts. The verb κερτομέω is “taunt, mock, tease”. Such words can wound or merely needle; the tone depends on the speaker. The word has been explained as “heart-cutting” (from κέαρ, “heart”, and τέμνω, “cut”), but the etymology is uncertain.

## In Book 1

{{quote:109bdcdf-e4a2-5754-8d9b-96c7164d8134}}

Hera goes straight at Zeus: which god has been laying plans with him, the schemer, this time? It is always dear to him to decide things in secret.

The roles are reversed at the start of Book 4, where Zeus tries to provoke Hera “with cutting words” (κερτομίοις ἐπέεσσι, 4.5–6).
