## Meaning

ἐπιτάρροθος and the shorter ἐπίρροθος both mean “helper, defender”. The second looks like a compound of ἐπί and ῥόθος, “rushing noise”, as if the helper were one who comes at the cry; the longer form is obscure.

The helper is nearly always a god. When Apollo saves Hector, first Diomedes and then Achilles tells him that he will finish him another time, “if there is any god who is a helper to me as well” (11.362–367, 20.449–454). Aeneas says that a god has told him Zeus is the Trojans' “helper in battle” (17.338–339). Poseidon tells Achilles, in danger in the river, that he and Athena are his helpers, with Zeus' consent (21.288–290). When the Achaeans are penned by their ships, the gods who were “helpers of the Danaans in battle” are grieved (12.179–180). In the Odyssey the ghost of a suitor tells Agamemnon in Hades that as Odysseus shot them down “it was clear that some god was their helper” (*Odyssey* 24.178–182).

**Athena and the sons of Tydeus.** The word ties Book 5 to Book 4. Telling the story of Tydeus at Thebes, Agamemnon ends the account of the contests with the Cadmeians: “easily; such a helper was Athena to him” (τοίη οἱ ἐπίρροθος ἦεν Ἀθήνη, 4.390). In Book 5 Athena tells the story herself, and the line comes back in the first person:

{{quote:75e354e1-3051-5fa4-b82e-02e3a7ad75f3}}

A little later she promises the son the same: “do not fear Ares for that, or any other of the immortals; such a helper am I to you” (τοίη τοι ἐγὼν ἐπιτάρροθός εἰμι, 5.827–828). She takes Sthenelus' place in the chariot and drives Diomedes' spear into Ares' belly (5.835–859). What Agamemnon knew by report, the goddess says of herself, and then does. See [[tydeus-at-thebes]], [[athena]] and [[divine-intervention]].

At the funeral games Odysseus prays to Athena in the footrace to come to his feet as “a good helper” (ἀγαθή μοι ἐπίρροθος, 23.770). The next two lines are the ones that follow Diomedes' prayer in Book 5: she heard him, “and she made his limbs light, his feet and his hands above” (23.771–772 = 5.121–122).
