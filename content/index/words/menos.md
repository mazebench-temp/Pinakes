## Meaning

μένος (“might, fury, ardor”) is a surge of energy: the force in a warrior's limbs, the power of a god, the strength of fire, wind or a river. A god can put it into a man, and it can fill him (1.103). The word is cognate with Sanskrit *mánas*, “mind, spirit”, from a root meaning “have in mind, be intent on”. The same root gives μέμονα, “be eager, strive”.

## In Book 1

{{quote:1268706a-4af9-5914-938b-6946cfc73e41}}

Agamemnon rises in distress; his mind is filled with μένος and his eyes blaze like fire.

{{quote:69ff8c26-fc62-5264-8017-b0f0827e17ad}}

Athena tells Achilles that she has come to stop his μένος, “if you will obey”.

{{quote:6672ee30-ef65-5f4f-a1c7-3b8154a4c849}}

Nestor says the same thing to Agamemnon: “check your μένος”. The old man does for the king what the goddess did for Achilles, and fails.
