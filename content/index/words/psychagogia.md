---
title: ψυχαγωγία
greek: ψυχαγωγία
kind: term
tags: [tragic-emotions]
summary: "Leading of the soul", the power of a work to sway and enthrall its audience. Aristotle says that the greatest means by which tragedy sways the soul are parts of the plot, reversals and recognitions, and that spectacle sways the soul but is the least artistic of the parts.
aliases: [psychagogia, sways the soul, soul-leading]
---

A ψυχαγωγός was first one who called up the souls of the dead, and Aeschylus' *Spirit-Raisers* told of Odysseus' consultation of the dead. Plato defines rhetoric as “a leading of souls through words” (*Phaedrus* 261a). In the *Poetics* the word marks the emotional force of tragedy: the greatest means by which it sways the soul are reversals and recognitions (1450a33–35; see [[peripeteia]] and [[anagnorisis]]), and spectacle sways the soul but is the least artistic part and the least proper to poetry (1450b16–20; see [[opsis]]).
