## Meaning

τέκμωρ (Epic for τέκμαρ) is a sign or token, and also a fixed mark: a goal, an end. In Book 9 Achilles tells the embassy that the Achaeans will never find the τέκμωρ of Troy, the end of steep Ilios (9.418–419). The verb τεκμαίρομαι means “fix, ordain”, and later “judge from signs”.

## In Book 1

{{quote:8eeed97b-852d-55ce-8672-0e3e41da4e6e}}

Thetis has asked Zeus to nod or refuse. Zeus answers that he will nod, “so that you may be certain”, for the nod is the greatest τέκμωρ that comes from him among the immortals. What he confirms with it cannot be taken back, cannot deceive, and cannot go unfulfilled (see [[no-word-taken-back]]). The sign is also a binding pledge.

## Related words

- [[neuo|νεύω]] (“nod”).
- [[peitho|πείθω]]: πεποίθῃς, “so that you may be certain” (1.524).
- ὅρκος (“oath”): Achilles' great oath on the scepter (1.233–239) is the mortal counterpart. See [[oath]].

## Outside Homer

In Aristotle's *Rhetoric* τεκμήριον is a sign that proves conclusively, as distinct from a sign that is only probable (1.2, 1357b).
