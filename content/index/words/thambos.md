## Meaning

θάμβος is amazement, and the verb θαμβέω means “be amazed”. The word marks the moment when someone sees more than he expected. It often comes when a god is recognized. Achilles, seized by the hair from behind, turns in amazement and knows Athena at once (1.199–200). Helen, looking at the old woman who has come to call her, sees the neck, the breasts and the flashing eyes of a goddess, and is amazed (3.396–398). Both then speak to the goddess and ask why she has come.

It also marks a spectacle. When Paris and Menelaus stride into the middle, glaring, amazement holds both armies (3.342). In the last book, when Priam appears in Achilles' hut and clasps his knees, Achilles is amazed at the sight of him, and so are his companions (24.483–484).

The etymology is uncertain; the word is usually connected with τέθηπα, “be astounded”.

## Related words

- θαῦμα (“wonder”) and θαυμάζω (“marvel”): Priam and Achilles marvel at each other (24.629–632).
- ἄγαμαι (“marvel at, admire”): Priam marvels at Agamemnon (ἠγάσσατο, 3.181), and the Trojans once marveled at Odysseus' looks (ἀγασσάμεθʼ, 3.224).
