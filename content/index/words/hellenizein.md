Aristotle's five rules are: connectives placed in their natural order and answering one another; specific words rather than general ones; no ambiguity, unless it is wanted; the genders of nouns given correctly; and many, few and one named correctly (*Rhetoric* 3.5, 1407a19–b10). What is written should be easy to read and to speak, as Heraclitus' book is not (*Rhetoric* 1407b11–18). The opposite fault is solecism.

## Outside the Rhetoric

Later lists of the virtues of style put correct language first: Theophrastus' (Cicero, *Orator* 79) and the Stoics', who called it Ἑλληνισμός (Diogenes Laertius 7.59).
