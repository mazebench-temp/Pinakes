## Meaning

θρέπτρα, from τρέφω, “rear, nourish”, is the return a child makes to its parents for its upbringing. Parents feed their children, and grown children feed their parents when they are old: the debt runs both ways across a lifetime.

The word occurs twice in the Iliad, both times in the same sentence and both times of a young man killed in battle: οὐδὲ τοκεῦσι θρέπτρα φίλοις ἀπέδωκε, “he did not repay his dear parents for his rearing”. It is said of Simoeisius, killed by Telamonian Ajax in the first battle of the poem (4.477–479), and of Hippothous, the leader of the Pelasgians (2.840–843), killed by the same Ajax in the fight over the body of Patroclus (17.300–303). Both times the sentence goes on, “and short was his life, brought down by the spear of great-hearted Ajax”. The same word, μινυνθάδιος, “short-lived”, is what Achilles calls himself (1.352; see [[short-lived]]).

The phrase counts a young man's death as a debt left unpaid. It looks past the fighter to the old people at home, who have lost both their son and their support in age. The Iliad's dead are often given such a line, pointing to parents, a wife, a house left unfinished (2.700–701; see [[death-in-battle]]).

## Outside Homer

Hesiod counts the failure to repay it among the evils of the age of iron: men will dishonor their parents as they grow old, and will not give their aged parents a return for their nurture (θρεπτήρια, *Works and Days* 185–188).
