## Meaning

κραδίη (Attic καρδία) is the heart: the organ that pounds in the chest in fear, and the seat of courage, anger and joy. It is cognate with Latin *cor* and English *heart*. The coward's heart leaps and pounds; the brave man's stays steady.

## In Book 1

{{quote:19e7913b-3269-5f8d-841b-23bf303ad812}}

Achilles calls Agamemnon a man “with the eyes of a dog and the heart of a deer”. The dog stands for shamelessness, the deer for timidity. Agamemnon himself uses the image of timid fawns to rebuke his men in Book 4 (4.243).

{{quote:3ee679d6-60cc-5bb8-b034-4413cba90a0b}}

Achilles asks Thetis to go to Zeus “if ever you gladdened the heart of Zeus by word or deed”. The line looks forward to her own prayer, “if ever I helped you by word or deed” (1.503–504).

## Outside Homer

Plato quotes 1.225 as an insult that should not be put before the young (*Republic* 3.389e–390a; see [[plato-achilles-insults]]). Twice he quotes the line in which Odysseus strikes his chest and rebukes his κραδίη (*Odyssey* 20.17): once as an example of endurance (*Republic* 3.390d), and once to show reason restraining the spirited part of the soul (*Republic* 4.441b).
