## Meaning

μισθός is the wage agreed for work done for another, or the reward promised for a task. Hector offers a reward to the man who will spy on the Achaean ships, “His reward will be sure” (10.304; see [[dolon]]), and the scales of the battle hang level like those of “an honest working woman”, who weighs out the wool “so that she may win a meager wage for her children” (12.433–435; see [[the-working-womans-scales]]).

The man who works for a wage is a θής, and the verb θητεύω is “serve for hire”. The Odyssey names the hired men beside the slaves of a household, θῆτές τε δμῶές τε (*Odyssey* 4.644). Eurymachus offers the beggar Odysseus work for hire on a farm at the edge of the land, building walls and planting trees, with a sure wage, food, clothing and sandals (*Odyssey* 18.357–361), and the dead Achilles would rather be a hired man, bound to the land of another who has no great living, than rule over all the dead (*Odyssey* 11.489–491; see [[slavery-and-freedom]]). A wage can also buy a service of another kind: Aegisthus pays his lookout two talents of gold to watch for Agamemnon's return for a year (*Odyssey* 4.524–527).

## Related words

- θητεύω, to serve for hire (21.444; *Odyssey* 11.489, 18.357), and θής, the hired man (*Odyssey* 4.644).
- [[doron|δῶρον]] (“gift”), [[apoina|ἄποινα]] (“ransom”) and [[poine|ποινή]] (“penalty”), the other payments of the poem; [[charis|χάρις]] (“favor”).

## Outside Homer

Hesiod tells his brother, “let the wage stated to a friend be sufficient” (μισθὸς δʼ ἀνδρὶ φίλῳ εἰρημένος ἄρκιος ἔστω), and, when the harvest is stored, to take on a θής who has no house and a work-woman who has no child (*Works and Days* 370, 602–603). Aristotle tells how Simonides, when the victor with the mule team “offered him a small fee”, would not write the ode, and wrote it when he was given enough (*Rhetoric* 3.2, 1405b23–27; see [[simonides]]).
