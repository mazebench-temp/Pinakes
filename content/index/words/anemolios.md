## Meaning

ἀνεμώλιος is formed from ἄνεμος, “wind”: what is windy has no weight and no effect. μεταμώνιος, “carried off by the wind”, is usually explained from the same word. Both describe things that come to nothing, above all words.

To “talk wind” (ἀνεμώλια βάζειν) is to talk idly. Odysseus says it of Agamemnon's rebuke (4.355). In the Odyssey the phantom of Penelope's sister will not say whether Odysseus is alive or dead, since “it is a bad thing to talk empty words” (*Odyssey* 4.837), and Odysseus gives Agamemnon's ghost the same answer about Orestes (*Odyssey* 11.464). μεταμώνια βάζειν is the same reproach (*Odyssey* 18.332). A thing done in vain is μεταμώνιον too: Penelope, as Antinous reports her words, asked the suitors to wait until she had finished Laertes' shroud, “so that my threads may not be wasted” (*Odyssey* 2.98).

The word is also used of weapons. In Book 5 Pandarus, whose arrows have wounded two men and killed neither, swears to break his bow, since it has gone with him “as empty wind” (ἀνεμώλια, 5.215–216). In the battle of the gods Artemis mocks her brother Apollo for carrying a bow that is ἀνεμώλιον when he will not fight (21.474).
