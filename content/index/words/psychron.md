Aristotle names four sources of frigidity in prose: double words, rare words, epithets that are long, untimely or crowded, and inappropriate metaphors (*Rhetoric* 3.3, 1405b35–1406b19). His examples come from the sophists of the generation before him, Lycophron, Gorgias and Alcidamas. What is frigid is what is poetic out of place: it produces the laughable and obscures the meaning.

## Outside the Rhetoric

Demetrius treats the frigid as the fault next to the grand style (*On Style* 114–127), and the author of *On the Sublime* finds it in the straining after novelty (3.4–4.7).
