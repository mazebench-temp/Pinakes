## Meaning

Οὖτις is οὔ τις, “not anyone, no one”, written as one word and used as a name. Odysseus gives it to the Cyclops over the wine, when he is asked his name so that he may be given a guest-gift (*Odyssey* 9.355–367; see [[polyphemus-the-cyclops]] and [[guest-gifts]]). It is declined as a name: its accusative is Οὖτιν, where the pronoun has οὔτινα, and Merry takes the difference to be intended, “because it is used as a proper name” (W. W. Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 9.366).

The trick is in the ambiguity of the name. When the blinded Cyclops shouts that Οὖτις is killing him, his neighbors hear the pronoun, and answer with the form the pronoun takes after a condition, μή τις: “If then nobody is doing you violence”. μή τις has the sound of [[metis|μῆτις]], “plan, cunning”, and Odysseus, telling the story, says that his heart laughed “how my name and my flawless plan had deceived him” (*Odyssey* 9.408–414). Merry finds it difficult not to suppose a pun, “for the Cyclops has been over-reached by the μῆτις of the οὔτις or Οὖτις” (on 9.408). The name is a disguise, and like the beggar's rags on Ithaca it is put off when it has done its work: from the ship Odysseus shouts his real name, with his father's and his home's (*Odyssey* 9.502–505; see [[say-that-odysseus-blinded-you]] and [[odysseus]]).

## Related words

- [[metis|μῆτις]] (“plan, cunning”), heard in the Cyclopes' μή τις, and Odysseus' epithet πολύμητις, “of many wiles” (see [[of-many-wiles]]). Years later, on Ithaca, he reminds his heart of the cave, where it endured “until cunning led you out of the cave, when you thought you would die” (ὄφρα σε μῆτις / ἐξάγαγʼ ἐξ ἄντροιο, *Odyssey* 20.20–21, a plain rendering).
- οὐτιδανός, “a nobody”, the man of no account: Achilles calls the Achaeans whom Agamemnon rules “nobodies” and would himself be called “a coward and a nobody” if he yielded to him in everything (*Iliad* 1.231, 1.293), and Diomedes scorns the arrow “of a weakling, a nobody” (*Iliad* 11.390). The stranger uses it of the guest who quarrels with his host (*Odyssey* 8.209; see [[strife-with-ones-host]]), and the Cyclops twice of the man who blinded him (*Odyssey* 9.460, 9.515; see [[small-and-of-no-account]]).
- [[no-one-is-nameless]]: Alcinous' maxim that parents give a name to everyone, and his question, which the false name answers in the tale.

## Outside Homer

Euripides keeps the name in his satyr play *Cyclops*. Asked by what name he should be called, Odysseus answers “Nobody” (Οὖτιν), and the Cyclops promises to feast on him last of all his companions (*Cyclops* 548–550). Blinded, the Cyclops cries to the satyrs, “Nobody has destroyed me” and “Nobody is blinding my eye”, and they turn the name against him: “Then no one was wronging you”, “Then you are not blind”, and to his question where Nobody is, “Nowhere, Cyclops” (*Cyclops* 672–675).
