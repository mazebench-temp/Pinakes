## Meaning

ἠπεροπεύω means “cheat, beguile”, and ἠπεροπευτής is “a cheat, a deceiver”. In the Iliad the words belong to seduction. Hector calls Paris γυναιμανὲς ἠπεροπευτά, “mad for women, seducer”, in a line he says to him twice (3.39, 13.769). Helen asks Aphrodite why she longs to beguile her (ἠπεροπεύειν, 3.399). Diomedes, having wounded the goddess in the hand, tells her to keep away from war: “is it not enough that you deceive feeble women?” (5.349). The seducer and the goddess of desire are described with one word, and in each case the one beguiled is a woman. The etymology is unknown.
