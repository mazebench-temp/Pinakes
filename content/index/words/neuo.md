## Meaning

νεύω is “nod, bow the head”. κατανεύω, “nod down”, means assent: to grant a request or confirm a promise. ἀνανεύω, “nod up”, means refusal. When Achilles prays for Patroclus in Book 16, Zeus grants one request and refuses the other (ἀνένευσε, 16.250). Latin *numen*, “nod” and so “divine will”, comes from the same root.

## In Book 1

{{quote:b54ab252-f290-579c-9e33-dbd1cbd2de96}}

Zeus has sat in silence after Thetis' plea. She asks for a clear sign either way: nod assent, or refuse, so that she may know.

{{quote:7589640c-96ff-51c5-a34b-314daf332e28}}

Zeus explains before he acts. The nod is the greatest sign he gives among the immortals, and whatever he confirms with it is final (see [[no-word-taken-back]]). Then he nods with his dark brows; his ambrosial hair flows down from his immortal head, and great Olympus shakes.

Hera needs no witness. She guesses at once that Zeus “truly nodded assent” to honor Achilles and destroy many Achaeans (1.558–559).
