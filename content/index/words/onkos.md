---
title: Bulk
greek: ὄγκος
kind: term
tags: [style, diction]
summary: Ὄγκος, the “bulk” or amplitude of style, gained by description instead of names, metaphors, plurals for singulars and the like; its opposite is conciseness.
aliases: [onkos, amplitude, weight]
---

Aristotle's means of bulk are: a description instead of a name (“a plane figure equidistant from the middle” for “circle”); metaphors and epithets; making one into many, as the poets do; giving each noun its own article; speaking with connectives; and describing a thing by what it does not have, as Antimachus does (*Rhetoric* 3.6, 1407b26–1408a9). For conciseness one does the opposite. In the *Poetics* epic has more bulk than tragedy, since its episodes add to the poem's ὄγκος (*Poetics* 1459b28), and the hexameter is the weightiest of meters (*Poetics* 1459b35).
