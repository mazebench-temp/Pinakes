## Meaning

ἄναξ is the older of the two Homeric words for a ruler. It appears in the Mycenaean tablets as *wa-na-ka*, the title of the king at Pylos and Knossos. The initial *w-* (digamma) was lost in later Greek, but it still shapes Homer's meter. In 1.7 (Ἀτρεΐδης τε ἄναξ) and 1.36 (Ἀπόλλωνι ἄνακτι) a vowel stands before ἄναξ without elision, because a consonant once stood there.

In Homer ἄναξ means lord or master, of gods as well as men. Apollo is ἄναξ (1.36, 1.75, 1.390, 1.444), and so is Zeus (1.502, 1.529). Agamemnon's standing title is ἄναξ ἀνδρῶν, “lord of men” (1.7, 1.172, 1.442, 1.506).

The verb ἀνάσσω means “be lord, rule”, over people in the dative or genitive.
