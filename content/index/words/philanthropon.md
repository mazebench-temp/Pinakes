## Meaning

φιλάνθρωπος is “loving mankind”, kind and humane; τὸ φιλάνθρωπον is what appeals to that feeling. In the *Poetics* it is something the audience wants and a plot may give: the wicked man's rise has none of it, the villain's fall has it but no pity or fear (1452b38–1453a4), and the deception of a clever villain or the defeat of an unjust brave man is “tragic and satisfies human feeling” (1456a19–23). Whether this is fellow feeling even for the wicked or the satisfaction of seeing justice done is disputed; the second fits the passages better.
