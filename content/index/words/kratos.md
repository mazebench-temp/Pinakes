## Meaning

κράτος is power that prevails: the upper hand, the superiority of one side over another. The poem has other words for the strength of a body, βίη, σθένος and ἴς. κράτος is what such strength wins, or what a god grants without it. κάρτος is the same word with the ρ moved, as καρτερός stands beside κρατερός (see [[krateros]]). The word is usually connected with κρατύς, “strong”, an epithet of Hermes (κρατὺς ἀργεϊφόντης, 16.181, 24.345; see [[argeiphontes]]). Émile Benveniste argued that κράτος never means bodily strength in Homer, but always superiority, the power to prevail over others in battle or in the assembly (*Le vocabulaire des institutions indo-européennes*, 1969).

Above all it belongs to Zeus, and he gives it. Agamemnon says of him, “for his power is the greatest” (2.118 = 9.25), and the Odyssey calls him the god whose power is greatest in nearly the same words (*Odyssey* 5.4). Thetis asks him to “give strength to the Trojans” until the Achaeans honor her son (1.509; see [[plan-of-zeus]]). He sends Iris to tell Hector that when Agamemnon leaves the battle, “then I will put power in his hands” to kill until he reaches the ships (11.192; Iris repeats it, 11.207), and Diomedes sees that Zeus “wishes to give power to the Trojans rather than to us” (11.319). In Nestor's story of his first war, “Zeus put great power into the hands of the Pylians” (11.753). The gift is often made with ἐγγυαλίζω, “put into the hand”, and what is given is a relation between two sides: Andromache heard “that the Trojans were hard pressed, and the Achaeans had great strength” (6.387). In the contest of the bow in the Odyssey, Odysseus tells the suitors that at dawn the god will give the power to whom he wills (*Odyssey* 21.280).

κράτος is set against other ways of winning and other gifts. Lycurgus killed Areithous “by guile, not by strength at all” (7.142). Diomedes tells Agamemnon that Zeus gave him the scepter, “but courage he did not give you, and that is the greatest power” (9.39; see [[alke]]). Ajax and Achilles drew up their ships at the two ends of the line, “trusting in their manhood and the strength of their hands” (8.226 = 11.9). Among men κράτος is also authority. Calchas fears to anger “a man who rules with great power over all / the Argives” (κρατέει, 1.78–79; see [[kingship-and-authority]]), and in the Odyssey Telemachus sends his mother to her loom, for speech will be the concern of men, and his above all, “for mine is the power in the house” (*Odyssey* 1.356–359); he says it again of the bow (*Odyssey* 21.350–353). Alcinous says the same of his people's escort for Odysseus, “for mine is the power among the people” (*Odyssey* 11.352–353).

## Related words

- [[krateros|κρατερός]] (“mighty”) and καρτερός; κρατέω (“rule, prevail”).
- [[kydos|κῦδος]] (“glory”): the other gift Zeus makes to the side that prevails.
- [[nike|νίκη]] (“victory”).
- [[alke|ἀλκή]] (“courage”), [[menos|μένος]] (“might”), and βίη (“strength”).

## Outside Homer

Hesiod makes Kratos and Bie, “Power” and “Force”, the children of Styx, with Zelos and Nike: they have no house or seat or road apart from Zeus, and sit always beside him (*Theogony* 383–388; see [[styx]]). In Aeschylus' *Prometheus Bound* Kratos and Bia bring Prometheus to the rock where Hephaestus must bind him, and Kratos speaks the play's first lines (*Prometheus Bound* 1–11; see [[aeschylus]]). The word gave later Greek its names for kinds of rule, as δημοκρατία, the rule of the δῆμος (see [[demos]]).
