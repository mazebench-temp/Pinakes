## Meaning

ποιητής is a maker, the agent noun of ποιέω, “make, do”. It became the ordinary word for a poet in the fifth century, replacing Homer's ἀοιδός, “singer” (see [[poietike]]). The older word places the poet in performance, a singer before an audience; the newer one makes him a craftsman of a thing that can be judged as a work.

{{notes}}

## After Aristotle

Latin borrowed the word as *poeta*. Sidney, defending poetry in the 1580s, took the Greek sense literally: the Greeks called him “a poet”, “which name hath, as the most excellent, gone through other languages. It cometh of this word ποιεῖν, which is, to make” (*The Defence of Poesy*).
