## Meaning

Homer has several words for a leader, and they are not titles of rank like [[basileus|βασιλεύς]] (“king”) or [[anax|ἄναξ]] (“lord”). They name the man who commands a body of men: a contingent, a ship, an embassy.

- ἡγεμών, from ἡγέομαι, “go before, lead the way”: the one who goes in front. The Catalogue's leaders are “the leaders and lords of the Danaans” (ἡγεμόνες Δαναῶν καὶ κοίρανοι, 2.487, 2.760), pairing the function with the lordship (see [[koiranos]]).
- ἀρχός, from ἄρχω, “begin, lead”: the one who takes the lead and commands. ἄναρχος is “without a leader”.
- ἡγήτωρ, “leader”, in the address “leaders and rulers (μέδοντες) of the Argives” (2.79).

The verbs go with them: ἡγέομαι and ἡγεμονεύω, “lead”, and ἄρχω, “lead” or “begin”. A leader in the Iliad is defined by those who follow him (ἕπονται): his people, his ships. When he is missing, his men “long for” him (ποθέω; see [[pothe]]).

The family had a long future. ἡγεμών became the Greek word for a commander or a governor, and ἡγεμονία gives English *hegemony*. ἀρχός lies behind the many compounds in -αρχος (ναύαρχος, “admiral”; πολέμαρχος, “war leader”), and ἄναρχος behind *anarchy*.
