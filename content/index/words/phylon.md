## Meaning

φῦλον (plural φῦλα) is a group defined by birth or by kind. It can be a people, or a tribe within one: Hippothous leads “the tribes of the Pelasgians” (φῦλα Πελασγῶν, 2.840), and Hector addresses the Trojan allies as “countless tribes of neighboring allies” (μυρία φῦλα περικτιόνων ἐπικούρων, 17.220). It can be any race or kind of being. Apollo warns Diomedes that the race (φῦλον) of the immortal gods and that of men who walk the earth are never alike (5.441–442). Zeus sends Hera “among the tribes of the gods” (μετὰ φῦλα θεῶν, 15.54), and the captive women of Lesbos whom Agamemnon offers Achilles surpassed “the tribes of women” in beauty (φῦλα γυναικῶν, 9.130). In the Odyssey the Muse loves “the tribe of singers” (φῦλον ἀοιδῶν, Odyssey 8.481). The word comes from φύω, “grow, beget”, as does φυλή, the usual word for a tribe in later Greek. The adverb καταφυλαδόν, “by tribes”, occurs only once in Homer (2.668).

φρήτρη (Attic φρατρία) is a clan or brotherhood, a group of families bound together as kin. It belongs with φράτηρ, “member of a phratry”, the Greek form of the old Indo-European word for “brother” (Latin *frater*, Sanskrit *bhrātar-*, English *brother*). In Greek a brother by blood is ἀδελφός or κασίγνητος, and φράτηρ kept only the social sense. In Homer φρήτρη occurs only in Nestor's advice (2.362–363). Nestor again, in Book 9, calls the man who loves war among his own people ἀφρήτωρ ἀθέμιστος ἀνέστιος, “without clan, without law, without hearth” (9.63).

Nowhere else in the Iliad is the army arrayed by tribes and clans. The Catalogue that follows Nestor's advice arranges it by region and leader. The three divisions of the Rhodians (2.655, 2.668) are often connected with the three tribes of the Dorians, since Rhodes was Dorian in historical times. The Dorians of Crete in the Odyssey are τριχάϊκες (Odyssey 19.177), a word often explained as “in three tribes”, though its meaning is disputed.

## Outside Homer

In the Greek cities of historical times the citizens were divided into tribes (φυλαί) and phratries. Dorian cities had three tribes, the Hylleis, the Pamphyloi and the Dymanes; Herodotus names them when he tells how Cleisthenes of Sicyon renamed the tribes of his city (5.68). Athens had four old Ionian tribes until Cleisthenes of Athens made ten (Herodotus 5.66, 5.69). The phratries kept their place: Cleisthenes let the clans and phratries keep their ancestral rights ([Aristotle], *Constitution of the Athenians* 21.6).
