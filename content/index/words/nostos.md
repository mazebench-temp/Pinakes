## Meaning

νόστος, from νέομαι, “go, return”, is the return home, above all the return of a warrior from war. It is the subject of the Odyssey, and in the Iliad it is always in question. The verb ἀπονοστέω means “return home”, and the adverb οἴκαδε “homeward”.

Achilles' fate is framed as a choice between homecoming and glory. His mother told him that if he stays and fights at Troy his νόστος is lost but his glory will be imperishable; if he goes home his glory is lost but his life will be long (9.410–416).

## Related words

- [[noos|νόος]] (“understanding”): an etymological connection with νέομαι has been proposed, and disputed.
- οἶκος (“house”): Agamemnon means to keep Chryseis in his house in Argos (1.30, 1.113).

## Outside Homer

The *Nostoi*, a lost poem of the epic cycle known from Proclus' summary, told the returns of the Achaean heroes from Troy.
