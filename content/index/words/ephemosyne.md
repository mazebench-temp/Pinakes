## Meaning

ἐφημοσύνη belongs with ἐφίημι, “lay upon, enjoin”, and ἐφετμή, “behest”: it is what one is charged to do, and a charge can be kept or neglected. In the Iliad it is used once. Menelaus sends Antilochus with the news of Patroclus' death to Achilles, and Antilochus, struck speechless and in tears, “even so he did not neglect the command of Menelaus” (17.694–699; see [[antilochus]] and [[messenger-scene]]).

The Odyssey has the word twice. Odysseus, before Scylla, forgets Circe's hard command not to arm against her, and puts on his armor (*Odyssey* 12.226–228), and Eumaeus tells Penelope everything her son bade him say, and when he has delivered the whole charge goes back to his swine (*Odyssey* 16.338–341).
