## Meaning

A νόθος is a son whose mother was not his father's wedded wife but a concubine or a captive. His opposite is γνήσιος, “legitimate”. The origin of the word is unknown. In Homer a bastard is acknowledged by his father and may be brought up in his house, sometimes by his father's wife. [[theano|Theano]], Antenor's wife and a priestess of Athena, reared his bastard son [[pedaeus|Pedaeus]] “with care like her own dear children, to please her husband” (5.70–71). Telamon brought up Teucer in his house “bastard though you were”, Agamemnon reminds him (8.283–284).

Bastards fight in the war, and many die in it. Medon, a bastard son of Oileus, leads Philoctetes' men while their leader lies wounded on Lemnos (2.726–728). Priam has several: Democoon, who came to him from Abydos, “from his swift mares” (4.499–500); Isus, killed in one chariot with his legitimate brother Antiphus, “one a bastard and one born in wedlock” (11.101–104); Doryclus (11.489–490); and Cebriones, Hector's charioteer (16.737–739). Their place is at the side of their brothers, often as drivers and keepers of horses.

The Odyssey knows the same custom. Menelaus' son Megapenthes was born to him by a slave woman (*Odyssey* 4.10–14), and in one of his false tales Odysseus claims to be the son of a Cretan by a bought concubine, honored by his father like the legitimate sons and cheated of his share by them when the father died (*Odyssey* 14.199–210).
