## Meaning

καλός is “beautiful”, of persons, things and places, and so “fine, good of its kind”; its opposite is αἰσχρός, both “ugly” and “shameful” (see [[aischros]]). In Homer it is mostly beauty that is καλός, though a death in battle can be fitting (κατὰ μοῖραν). In Tyrtaeus it is already a word of moral judgment: “for it is a fine thing for a brave man to die, fallen among the front ranks, fighting for his fatherland” (fr. 10.1–2 West). Aristotle's ethics makes τὸ καλόν the end for which the virtuous man acts: the brave man faces danger “for the sake of the noble” (*Nicomachean Ethics* 3.7, 1115b12–13).
