## The word

The Hermus and the Caicus are rivers of Lydia and Mysia that flow into the Aegean near Smyrna and Pergamum; the Xanthus is either the Trojan river, which gods call Xanthus and men Scamander (20.74), or the river of Lycia. The compound joins three significant parts into one name. Aristotle introduces it as an instance of the many-part nouns of the Massaliots (see [[massaliots]]), after which the manuscripts break off; commentators have supposed that the source was a poem in praise of a river, or a prayer, with a phrase like ἐπευξάμενος Διὶ πατρί, but the text gives nothing more (see [[compound-word]]).
