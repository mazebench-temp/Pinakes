## Meaning

δαίδαλα, "intricate designs", is the plural of δαίδαλον, a thing of cunning workmanship. It belongs with the verb δαιδάλλω, "work with art" (πάντοσε δαιδάλλων, "working it intricately all over", 18.479), with δαιδάλεος and πολυδαίδαλος, "intricately worked" (see [[intricately-worked]]), and with the name of Daedalus, who fashioned a dancing floor for Ariadne at Cnossus (18.590–592; see [[daedalus]]). The translation renders it "intricate designs", and "every kind / of intricate work" for the skill of Phereclus (5.60–61).

## Use

The word belongs to the gods' craftsmen and to the work they love. Athena loved Phereclus above all others (5.61; see [[phereclus]]), and she herself set the designs in Hera's robe (14.179). Hephaestus made δαίδαλα πολλά twice: in the nine years he lived with the goddesses who took him in when he fell, brooches and spirals, rosettes and necklaces (18.398–401), and on the Shield (18.482; see [[the-shield-of-achilles]]). When Thetis lays the new armor before Achilles, "all its intricate designs rang" (19.13), and he takes his fill of gazing on them (19.19).
