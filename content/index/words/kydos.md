## Meaning

κῦδος is glory of a particular kind: a visible superiority, the shine of success that a god grants for a time. Zeus gives it to kings and to warriors, and he can take it away. It differs from κλέος, fame, which lives in what people say about a man. Émile Benveniste argued that κῦδος is a talisman of supremacy, granted by a god and effective while it lasts.

The superlative κύδιστος, “most glorious”, is a formal way of addressing a king. The compound κυδιάνειρα, “bringing glory to men” (κῦδος and ἀνήρ), is usually an epithet of battle.

## Outside Homer

English *kudos* comes from this word, by way of nineteenth-century university slang.
