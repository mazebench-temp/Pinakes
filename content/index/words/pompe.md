## Meaning

πομπή is a “sending”, the noun of πέμπω, “send”: the act of sending someone on his way, and the company or the ship that takes him there. πομπός and πομπεύς are the one who escorts, a guide or a convoy. The translations of both poems render them “escort” and “escorts” (see the Odyssey translation's table of key words, πομπός).

**Gods as escorts.** In the Iliad the word belongs to the gods and their errands. Bellerophon went to Lycia “under the blameless escort of the gods” (6.171; see [[bellerophon-in-lycia]]). Zeus has Apollo send the body of Sarpedon home “with swift escorts”, Sleep and Death, the twins (16.671–673, 16.681–683; see [[the-death-of-sarpedon]], [[sleep]] and [[death]]). Zeus promises Priam the slayer of Argus as his escort to Achilles' hut (24.153, 24.182), and Hermes, in the likeness of a young prince (24.347), tells the old man that as his escort he would go even to famous Argos, and then reveals himself: “for my father granted me to you as an escort” (24.437–439, 24.460–461; see [[hermes]] and [[the-ransom-of-hector]]). Deiphobus turns the word into a boast over the body of Idomeneus' victim: Asius will be glad even as he goes to the house of Hades, “since I have given him an escort” (13.414–416; see [[asius]]).

**The host's last gift.** In the Odyssey the escort belongs to hospitality (see [[xenia]]). A host feeds his guest, entertains him and gives him gifts, and sends him on his way when he wishes to go. Menelaus puts it as a rule: one should welcome the stranger who is present and send him off when he wishes (*Odyssey* 15.74, a plain rendering; see [[what-is-due-is-better]]). Nestor offers Telemachus his sons as escorts to Sparta (*Odyssey* 3.324–326), and tells him that he will be no coward “if, young as you are, the gods go with you as your escorts” (*Odyssey* 3.376). The winds are “the escorts / of ships” (*Odyssey* 4.361–362). Odysseus asks for an escort wherever he is received. Aeolus “did not refuse, and prepared the escort” (*Odyssey* 10.17–18, a plain rendering), an oxhide bag in which he bound up the winds (*Odyssey* 10.19–20), and when it fails the crew must row, “since the escort no longer appeared” (*Odyssey* 10.79, a plain rendering), where πομπή is the wind itself, the means of the voyage (W. W. Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 10.79). The blinded Cyclops mocks the custom, calling Odysseus back so that he may give him guest-gifts and urge the earth-shaker to grant him an escort (*Odyssey* 9.517–518; see [[guest-gifts]] and [[polyphemus-the-cyclops]]). Escort and gifts are named together as the host's whole due: “the escort and the gifts of friendship” (πομπὴ καὶ φίλα δῶρα, *Odyssey* 8.545, 13.41; see [[doron]]).

**The Phaeacians.** The Phaeacians are the escorts of all men, πομποὶ ἀπήμονες, whose ships need no helmsmen and come to no harm, and the escorts are what Poseidon begrudges them. Nausithous foretold that the god would strike one of their ships as it came back from an escort, and when Poseidon has turned the ship that took Odysseus home into stone, Alcinous bids his people cease from escorting mortals and sacrifice twelve bulls to the god (*Odyssey* 8.555–571, 13.159–164, 13.172–183; see [[phaeacians]] and [[poseidon]]).

## Related words

- πέμπω (“send”): Calypso “sent me on a raft of many bindings” (πέμπε, *Odyssey* 7.264).
- [[nostos|νόστος]] (“homecoming”): Nausicaa sends Odysseus to her father so that he may “win escort and a homecoming” (πομπῆς καὶ νόστοιο, *Odyssey* 6.290).
- [[xenia]] and [[guest-gifts]]: the escort is the host's last service to his guest.
