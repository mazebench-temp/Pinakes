## Meaning

πομπή is a “sending”, the noun of πέμπω, “send”: the act of sending someone on his way, and the company or the ship that takes him there. πομπός and πομπεύς are the one who escorts, a guide or a convoy. The translations of both poems render them “escort” and “escorts” (see the Odyssey translation's table of key words, πομπός).

**Gods as escorts.** In the Iliad the word belongs to the gods and their errands. Bellerophon went to Lycia “under the blameless escort of the gods” (6.171; see [[bellerophon-in-lycia]]). Zeus has Apollo send the body of Sarpedon home “with swift escorts”, Sleep and Death, the twins (16.671–673, 16.681–683; see [[the-death-of-sarpedon]], [[sleep]] and [[death]]). Zeus promises Priam the slayer of Argus as his escort to Achilles' hut (24.153, 24.182), and Hermes, in the likeness of a young prince (24.347), tells the old man that as his escort he would go even to famous Argos, and then reveals himself: “for my father granted me to you as an escort” (24.437–439, 24.460–461; see [[hermes]] and [[the-ransom-of-hector]]). Deiphobus turns the word into a boast over the body of Idomeneus' victim: Asius will be glad even as he goes to the house of Hades, “since I have given him an escort” (13.414–416; see [[asius]]).

**The host's last gift.** In the Odyssey the escort belongs to hospitality (see [[xenia]]). A host feeds his guest, entertains him and gives him gifts, and sends him on his way when he wishes to go. Menelaus puts it as a rule: one should welcome the stranger who is present and send him off when he wishes (*Odyssey* 15.74, a plain rendering; see [[what-is-due-is-better]]). Nestor offers Telemachus his sons as escorts to Sparta (*Odyssey* 3.324–326), and tells him that he will be no coward “if, young as you are, the gods go with you as your escorts” (*Odyssey* 3.376). The winds are “the escorts / of ships” (*Odyssey* 4.361–362). Odysseus asks for an escort wherever he is received. Aeolus “did not refuse, and prepared the escort” (*Odyssey* 10.17–18, a plain rendering), an oxhide bag in which he bound up the winds (*Odyssey* 10.19–20), and when it fails the crew must row, “since the escort no longer appeared” (*Odyssey* 10.79, a plain rendering), where πομπή is the wind itself, the means of the voyage (W. W. Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 10.79). The blinded Cyclops mocks the custom, calling Odysseus back so that he may give him guest-gifts and urge the earth-shaker to grant him an escort (*Odyssey* 9.517–518; see [[guest-gifts]] and [[polyphemus-the-cyclops]]). Escort and gifts are named together as the host's whole due: “escort and dear gifts” (πομπὴ καὶ φίλα δῶρα, *Odyssey* 8.545, 13.41, a plain rendering; see [[doron]]).

**The Phaeacians.** The Phaeacians' ships need no helmsmen; they know men's minds and cross the sea wrapped in mist and cloud, and no harm can come to them (*Odyssey* 8.555–563). Alcinous calls his people “the harmless escorts of all men” (πομποὶ ἀπήμονές εἰμεν ἁπάντων, *Odyssey* 8.566 = 13.174, a plain rendering). The same words give Poseidon his grievance. Nausithous foretold that the god would one day strike a Phaeacian ship “returning from an escort” and hide the city behind a great mountain (*Odyssey* 8.564–569), and when Poseidon has turned the ship that took Odysseus home into stone (*Odyssey* 13.149–152, 13.159–164), Alcinous bids his people cease from escorting mortals and sacrifice twelve bulls to the god (*Odyssey* 13.172–183; see [[phaeacians]] and [[poseidon]]). Welcker, as Merry reports, saw in the Phaeacians and their ships, which carry Odysseus home in a deep sleep, a Greek form of the northern legend of the ferrymen of the dead; Merry answers that the office seems little suited to “the gay and easy Phaeacians, who are πομποὶ ἀπήμονες” (Merry on 8.556; on 5.34).

## Related words

- πέμπω (“send”): Calypso “sent me on a raft of many bindings” (πέμπε, *Odyssey* 7.264).
- [[nostos|νόστος]] (“homecoming”): Nausicaa sends Odysseus to her father so that he may “win escort and a homecoming” (πομπῆς καὶ νόστοιο, *Odyssey* 6.290).
- [[xenia]] and [[guest-gifts]]: the escort is the host's last service to his guest.
