## Meaning

μῆτις is practical intelligence: the skill to find a way, make a plan, devise a trick. It is the intelligence of the helmsman, the charioteer and the hunter as well as of the counselor. Nestor tells his son Antilochus that it is by μῆτις, not strength, that the woodcutter does better, the helmsman steers his ship through the wind, and one charioteer beats another (23.315–318). The word is often connected with a root meaning “measure”, but this is uncertain.

Marcel Detienne and Jean-Pierre Vernant made μῆτις the subject of a classic study (*Les ruses de l'intelligence: la mètis des Grecs*, 1974; English *Cunning Intelligence in Greek Culture and Society*, 1978).

The word stands in several compounds. Elsewhere in the poem Cronus is ἀγκυλομήτης, “of crooked counsel” (for instance 2.205). Book 1 has three.

## Outside Homer

In Hesiod, Metis is the first wife of Zeus. Warned that she would bear a son who would rule gods and men, Zeus swallowed her (*Theogony* 886–900), and Athena was later born from his head (924–926).
