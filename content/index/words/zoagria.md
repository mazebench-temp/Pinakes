## Meaning

ζῳάγρια is a neuter plural, from ζωός, “alive”, and the root of ἀγρέω, “take”, as in ζωγρέω, “take alive”: “Take me alive, son of Atreus, and accept a worthy ransom”, Adrestus begs Menelaus (ζώγρει, 6.46). Walter Leaf takes the word to have meant first the price paid to a warrior who took a prisoner alive instead of killing him, and its two uses in Homer to be a playful extension of it, which fixed the sense for later Greek (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 18.407).

Hephaestus uses it when Thetis comes to his house. She and Eurynome took him in when he had fallen far by the will of his mother, who wished to hide him away because he was lame, and he forged jewelry in their cave for nine years (18.394–405): “so I must indeed / pay Thetis of the lovely tresses the full price for my life” (18.406–407). The price is the armor of Achilles (see [[poine]], [[fall-of-hephaestus]] and [[thetis]]). In the Odyssey Nausicaa takes leave of Odysseus with the same word: when he is home in his own country, he should remember her, since he owes her first of all the price of his life (*Odyssey* 8.461–462).

## Related words

- ζωγρέω (“take alive”) and ζωός (“alive”).
- [[poine|ποινή]] (“penalty”) and ἄποινα (“ransom”): other payments for a life. ζῳάγρια, like the penalty, is paid with the verb τίνω, “pay” (τίνειν, 18.407).
