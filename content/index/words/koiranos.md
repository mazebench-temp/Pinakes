## Meaning

κοίρανος is a lord: a man who has the right to command others. In the Iliad it names the commanders of the army. The poet asks the Muses who were “the leaders and lords of the Danaans” (ἡγεμόνες Δαναῶν καὶ κοίρανοι, 2.487), and Hector addresses Ajax as κοίρανε λαῶν, “lord of the people” (7.234). The title can also be used in mockery. In the Odyssey, Odysseus props the beaten beggar Irus against the courtyard wall and tells him not to set himself up as “lord of strangers and beggars” (ξείνων καὶ πτωχῶν κοίρανος, Odyssey 18.106).

The verb κοιρανέω means “be lord, give orders”: to move among men exercising command (2.207). The abstract noun πολυκοιρανίη, “the rule of many” (πολύς, “many”, and κοίρανος), occurs in Homer only in Odysseus' maxim “the rule of many is not good; let there be one ruler” (2.204; see [[one-ruler]]).

The word is usually connected with Old Persian *kāra-* (“army, people”) and Gothic *harjis* (“army”), the source of German *Heer*. If that is right, a κοίρανος was first the leader of an army. Beside [[basileus|βασιλεύς]] (“king”) and [[anax|ἄναξ]] (“lord”), it stresses command rather than rank, and Odysseus' maxim sets the two side by side: “one ruler, one king” (εἷς κοίρανος … εἷς βασιλεύς, 2.204–205).

## Related words

- [[basileus|βασιλεύς]] (“king”) and [[anax|ἄναξ]] (“lord”).
- [[hegemon|ἡγεμών]] (“leader”): paired with κοίρανος in the frame of the Catalogue (2.487, 2.760).
- [[laos|λαός]] (“people, army”): κοίρανε λαῶν, “lord of the people” (7.234).
- [[themis|θέμιστες]] (“ordinances”): with the scepter, Zeus' gift to the one ruler (2.206).
- See [[kingship-and-authority]] and [[kings]].

## Outside Homer

Aristotle closes Book Λ of the *Metaphysics* with Odysseus' maxim, applied to the single first principle of the universe (1076a4), and asks in the *Politics* what kind of rule by many Homer meant (4.4, 1292a13–15). See [[aristotle-one-ruler]].
