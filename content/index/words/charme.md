## Meaning

χάρμη is the Homeric word for eagerness to fight, and by extension for the fight itself. It lives in a few phrases. μνήσαντο δὲ χάρμης, “remembered the joy of battle”, is said of an army that turns to fight again after a pause or a rout (4.222, 8.252, 15.380). The Ajaxes, roused by Poseidon, are “rejoicing in the joy of battle that the god had put in their hearts” (χάρμῃ γηθόσυνοι, 13.81–82). Deer have none: the Trojans who once fled like deer in the woods were “without courage, and with no joy of battle in them” (ἀνάλκιδες, οὐδʼ ἔπι χάρμη, 13.101–104).

The word is usually connected with χαίρω, “rejoice”, and with χάρμα, “joy” (3.51): battle as the fighter's delight. The connection has been doubted, and some translators render χάρμη simply as “battle”. The translation keeps the joy.

χάρμη and [[alke|ἀλκή]], “courage”, go together. Both are things a fighter can forget and must remember, and both are what the fawn and the deer lack (4.243–245, 13.101–104). ἀλκή is the strength to stand; χάρμη is the wish to fight.
