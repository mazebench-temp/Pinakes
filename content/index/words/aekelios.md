## Meaning

Thetis reminds Achilles that Zeus has fulfilled his prayer, “that all the sons of the Achaeans should be penned in among the sterns / for want of you, and suffer unseemly deeds” (παθέειν τʼ ἀεκήλια ἔργα, 18.76–77; see [[prayer]] and [[stern]]). Aristarchus derived the word from ἕκηλος, “at ease”, so that ἀεκήλια would be things in which there is no quiet, or from ἀέκητι, “against one's will”, things one would not suffer willingly. Walter Leaf finds neither plausible and holds that the word can hardly be separated from ἀεικέλιος (14.84), “unseemly”, and the phrase ἀεικέα ἔργα, “shameful deeds” (22.395; “shameful tasks”, 24.733), the two adjectives perhaps coming from one older form lengthened differently for the meter (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 18.77). The translation follows him (see its note on 18.77).

The deeds are the defeat at the ships that Achilles asked Thetis to win from Zeus in Book 1, so that Agamemnon might learn his blind folly (1.407–412; see [[ate]]). Fulfilled, the prayer has cost him Patroclus: “but what pleasure have I in them, since my dear companion has perished” (18.80).

## Related words

- ἀεικής (“unseemly, shameful”), as in ἀεικέα λοιγόν, “shameful ruin” (1.97; see [[loigos]]).
- ἕκηλος (“at ease”) and ἀέκητι (“against one's will”), from which Aristarchus derived it.
