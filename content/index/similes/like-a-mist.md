## The simile

καρπαλίμως δʼ ἀνέδυ πολιῆς ἁλὸς ἠΰτʼ ὀμίχλη: “swiftly she rose from the gray sea like a mist”. ἠΰτε means “as, like”, and ὀμίχλη is mist or fog. The rising goddess is compared with sea-mist lifting off the water: swift, silent, and gray on the gray sea ([[gray-sea]]). Nothing is said of her form.

## In context

{{quote:2c55f231-d2cc-5255-8921-d0dfd71377c8}}

Achilles has wept and prayed on the shore, looking out over the sea. His mother hears him in the depths, where she sits beside her father, and one line brings her up to him. She sits before him and strokes him ({{ref:Iliad 1.360-361}}). The image suits a sea-goddess, and a mother's quiet coming to her weeping son (see [[thetis]] and [[divine-epiphany]]).

Twelve days later she rises again and goes up to Olympus ἠερίη ({{ref:Iliad 1.497}}). The word is usually taken as “early in the morning”; some have taken it as “misty”, from ἀήρ, “mist, haze”.

## Outside Book 1

Book 1's three similes are all short. The long similes of the battle books build a whole scene beside the action, and the first of them begins with the same word, ἠΰτε: the Achaeans pour out to the assembly like swarms of bees (Iliad 2.87–93). Mist returns in such a simile. The dust of the advancing armies is like the mist the south wind spreads over the mountain peaks (Iliad 3.10–14).
