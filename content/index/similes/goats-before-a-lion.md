## The simile

{{quote:c2ace520-fde4-5413-8a2c-b81c2a23c0f1}}

οἵ τέ σε πεφρίκασι λέονθʼ ὡς μηκάδες αἶγες: “who shudder at you as bleating goats shudder at a lion”. πεφρίκασι is a perfect of φρίσσω, “bristle, shudder”, the verb of a surface roughened by wind and of hair standing on end. μηκάς, “bleating”, is a stock epithet of goats (23.31; *Odyssey* 9.124, 9.244). λέονθʼ is λέοντα with its last vowel cut off before ὡς. The comparison is a single verse inside a speech, and it makes Diomedes a lion and the Trojans a flock that can only tremble (see [[lion]] and [[goats]]).

The simile tells the truth about the Trojans, and Paris says it in triumph. Since his great day in Book 5 the Trojans have feared Diomedes above all; the seer Helenus said that they never feared Achilles as they fear him (6.96–101; see [[the-aristeia-of-diomedes]]). What Paris wants is relief for them: “then the Trojans too would have had a breathing space from misery” (11.382).

## In context

Paris shot from behind a pillar on the tomb of Ilus while Diomedes was stripping Agastrophus, and the arrow went through the flat of his right foot and stuck in the ground. He leapt from his ambush with a hearty laugh (11.369–379; see [[paris]] and [[the-tomb-of-ilus]]). Diomedes answers without fear and turns the boast on the archer: face to face, in armor, his bow would not help him, the wound is a scratch, he cares no more than if a woman or a witless child had hit him, and the man his own spear hits rots in the dust, with more birds around him than women (11.384–395; see [[like-a-boy-or-a-woman]] and [[bow]]).

## Elsewhere in Homer

Paris himself was the frightened animal in Book 3. When Menelaus saw him and rejoiced like a lion that finds a great carcass, a stag or a wild goat, Paris shrank back into the ranks like a man who meets a serpent (3.21–37; see [[lion-on-a-carcass]] and [[man-who-meets-a-serpent]]). Goats and sheep without a herdsman are the lion's prey when Diomedes falls on the sleeping Thracians (10.485–486; see [[lion-among-the-flocks]]).
