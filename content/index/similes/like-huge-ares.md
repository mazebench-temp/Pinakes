## The simile

{{quote:84a83657-6f10-570a-956c-7f66f042fa45}}

σεύατʼ ἔπειθʼ οἷός τε πελώριος ἔρχεται Ἄρης, / ὅς τʼ εἶσιν πόλεμον δὲ μετʼ ἀνέρας οὕς τε Κρονίων / θυμοβόρου ἔριδος μένεϊ ξυνέηκε μάχεσθαι. / τοῖος ἄρʼ Αἴας ὦρτο πελώριος ἕρκος Ἀχαιῶν. οἷος … τοῖος, “such as … such”, frames the comparison. πελώριος, “huge, monstrous” (from πέλωρ, “monster”; see [[huge]]), is given to the god and then to the man. It is Ajax's own word. Helen named him on the wall with the same half-line, “huge Ajax, the bulwark of the Achaeans” (πελώριος ἕρκος Ἀχαιῶν, 3.229; see [[bulwark-of-the-achaeans]]), and the poem uses it of him again (17.174, 17.360). The simile is built on the epithet: the huge man is like the huge god.

Ares is not pictured fighting. He “goes to war among men”, the men whom Zeus has set to fight “in the fury of heart-devouring strife” (see [[ares]] and [[eris]]). The god is war itself arriving, and Ajax brings it with him. The lines that follow are Ajax's own. He smiles “with grim face” (βλοσυροῖσι προσώπασι): βλοσυρός is the look of the Gorgon on Agamemnon's shield (βλοσυρῶπις, 11.36) and of Hector's eyes in his fury (15.608). He goes “with long strides” (μακρὰ βιβάς), the gait that the Iliad gives him more than any other man (13.809, 15.676, 15.686); Paris strode out so before his own duel (3.22). He brandishes his long-shadowed spear (see [[long-shadowed]]).

## In context

The lot has just leapt from the helmet, and Ajax has rejoiced to see his mark on it (7.182–192; see [[lots]]). While he arms, the army prays to Zeus to give him victory, or, if Zeus loves Hector too, “equal strength and glory to them both” (7.202–205). The arming itself takes one line (7.206; see [[arming-scene]]). The simile does the rest.

It is measured by its effect. “And the Argives rejoiced as they looked at him, but a dread trembling came over the limbs of every Trojan, and Hector's own heart pounded in his breast” (7.214–216). Hector cannot draw back, “since he had made the challenge to battle” (7.217–218). Then Ajax comes near with his shield “like a tower” (7.219; see [[like-a-tower]]). See [[ajax]] and [[the-duel-of-hector-and-ajax]].

## Elsewhere in Homer

The Trojans' trembling is told in the same line when Achilles appears in Book 20 (20.44 = 7.215). They tremble as they see him shining in his armor, “equal to Ares, the bane of mortals” (20.46). Only these two men, Ajax like Ares and Achilles equal to Ares, make the whole Trojan army tremble in those words.

Other warriors are likened to the god. Meriones and Idomeneus go to battle as Ares goes to war with his son Rout (13.298–305), Hector rages “as when Ares, wielder of the spear” rages (15.605–606), and both Hector and Achilles are “the equal of Ares” or of Enyalius (11.295, 20.46, 22.132). In Book 7 Meriones is “the equal of Enyalius, slayer of men” (7.166; see [[enyalius]]).
