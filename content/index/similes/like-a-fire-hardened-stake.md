## The simile

{{quote:bff81bc1-3606-50b4-af10-9013deb98904}}

καὶ τὸ μὲν αὐτοῦ μεῖνʼ ὥς τε σκῶλος πυρίκαυστος / ἐν σάκει Ἀντιλόχοιο, τὸ δʼ ἥμισυ κεῖτʼ ἐπὶ γαίης: “And part of it stayed there like a fire-hardened stake in the shield of Antilochus, and the other half lay on the ground” (13.564–565). A σκῶλος is a pointed stake (not the Boeotian town Σκῶλος of the Catalogue, 2.497), and πυρίκαυστος, “burnt in the fire”, occurs only here: a stake whose point has been hardened by charring. Odysseus makes such a point when he sharpens the olive-wood stake for the Cyclops' eye and hardens it in the blazing fire (*Odyssey* 9.326–328). The stakes set in the ditch of the Achaean wall are called by another word, σκόλοπες (7.441, 12.55–57, 12.63–64).

The point of likeness is the stump of shaft standing stiff out of the shield, with nothing more to do. The cause is a god: ἀμενήνωσεν δέ οἱ αἰχμὴν / κυανοχαῖτα Ποσειδάων βιότοιο μεγήρας, “but his spear-point was made powerless by dark-haired Poseidon, who begrudged him the other's life” (13.562–563; see [[dark-haired]] and [[poseidon]]). The verb ἀμενηνόω, “make powerless” (from μένος, “might”), occurs only here; the adjective ἀμενηνός, “strengthless”, is said of Ares, who would have lived on strengthless among the dead (5.887), and of the dead themselves in the Odyssey (νεκύων ἀμενηνὰ κάρηνα, *Odyssey* 10.521). Spears break against shields twice more in this book, Meriones' on the shield of Deiphobus and Peisander's on the shield of Menelaus, both “at the socket” (13.162, 13.608; see [[spear]]).

## In context

Poseidon guards Antilochus, son of Nestor, among the missiles: Antilochus is never away from the enemy, and his spear never stops moving as he looks for a man to throw at or to rush on (13.554–559; see [[antilochus]]). Adamas, son of Asius, stabs the middle of his shield from close by, and the spear breaks (13.560–565). Adamas draws back among his companions, “avoiding doom”, but Meriones follows and spears him, and he writhes around the spear like an ox bound with ropes (13.566–575; see [[adamas]] and [[the-roped-ox]]).
