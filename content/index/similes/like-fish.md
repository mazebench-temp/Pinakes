## The simile

{{quote:095d13be-7d54-59d7-abcb-4869f2579d83}}

ἰχθῦς δʼ ὣς πείροντες ἀτερπέα δαῖτα φέροντο: “and spearing them like fish they carried them off to a joyless meal” (*Odyssey* 10.124). ὥς follows its noun and takes the accent, as in λύκοι ὥς, “like wolves” (*Iliad* 4.471; see [[like-wolves]]); ἰχθῦς is the accusative plural (see [[fish]]). πείρω is “pierce”, the verb with which meat is spitted for roasting, ἀμφʼ ὀβελοῖσιν ἔπειραν (*Iliad* 1.465), and with which Patroclus' spear goes through Thestor's teeth before he drags him from the chariot like a fish (πεῖρεν, *Iliad* 16.405; see [[the-fisherman]]). Both senses are in the verse, for the Laestrygonians kill to eat. W. W. Merry renders “Harpooning them like fish, they carried them off for a gruesome meal”: the ships have been broken by the stones and the men thrown into the water, where the Laestrygonians spear them as they float, with tridents or other sharpened tools, as Eustathius says (*Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 10.124). δαίς is the word of the shared feast, and ἀτερπής, “joyless”, is said elsewhere of the land of the dead (*Odyssey* 11.94) and of hunger (*Iliad* 19.354); the meal is the men.

The ancient readings vary. Aristophanes read εἴροντες, which a scholion explains as threading them together into strings; Apollonius the Sophist has ἀσπαίροντας, “gasping”, the word of the hooked fish and of the men in Scylla's simile (*Odyssey* 12.254–255), and Eustathius paraphrases that they feasted on them as on gasping fish. Some ancient commentators seem to have taken ἰχθῦς as a nominative, which would account for a variant σπαίροντες and for a scholion that explains πείροντες as “swimming and passing through like fish”. For φέροντο a variant has δαῖτα πένοντο, “prepared a meal” (Merry's critical note on 10.124, whose attributions are partly garbled in the scan; see the translation's note on 10.124).

## In context

The other eleven ships are moored close together inside the harbor, where no wave ever swells; Odysseus alone keeps his black ship outside, making the cables fast to a rock (*Odyssey* 10.87–96; see [[harbor]] and [[odysseus-among-the-laestrygonians]]). His scouts meet the king's daughter at the spring and find her mother “as huge as a mountain peak”, and the king seizes one of them at once and makes ready his meal (*Odyssey* 10.105–116; see [[antiphates-the-laestrygonian]]). At his cry “the mighty Laestrygonians came, one from one place and one from another, / countless, not like men, but like the Giants” (*Odyssey* 10.119–120; see [[laestrygonians]] and [[giants]]). The episode has three likenesses in a few verses: the queen like a mountain peak, the people like the Giants, and their victims like fish. While they kill the men in the deep harbor, Odysseus draws his sword, cuts the cables of his ship and urges his companions to row; “but the others perished there in a body” (*Odyssey* 10.125–132; see [[sword]] and [[stern-cables]]). On Aeaea the survivors weep when they remember “the deeds of Antiphates the Laestrygonian / and of the mighty, great-hearted Cyclops, the eater of men” (*Odyssey* 10.199–200; see [[polyphemus-the-cyclops]]).

## Elsewhere in Homer

Fishing gives Homer other likenesses of killing. Patroclus drags Thestor over the rail of his chariot on the spear as a man on a jutting rock drags a fish out of the sea with line and bronze (*Iliad* 16.406–408; see [[the-fisherman]]); the Trojans in the river cower like small fish fleeing a dolphin, which devours whatever it catches (*Iliad* 21.22–26; see [[the-dolphin-and-the-fishes]]); and Sarpedon warns Hector that the Trojans may be caught “as if in the meshes of an all-catching net” (*Iliad* 5.487; see [[the-net]]). In the later books of the Odyssey Scylla lifts six of Odysseus' companions as an angler on a jutting rock, with a long rod and the horn of an ox, flings a gasping fish ashore, and they gasp as they are carried up to the rocks (*Odyssey* 12.251–255; see [[the-fisherman]]), and the dead suitors lie heaped in the hall like fish that fishermen have drawn out of the gray sea in a net of many meshes onto the beach (*Odyssey* 22.383–389, a plain rendering). The men of the Odyssey fish when hunger forces them (*Odyssey* 4.368–369, 12.330–332); here the men are the catch.
