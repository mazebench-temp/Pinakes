## The simile

Ἱππόλοχος δʼ ἀπόρουσε, τὸν αὖ χαμαὶ ἐξενάριξε / χεῖρας ἀπὸ ξίφεϊ τμήξας ἀπό τʼ αὐχένα κόψας, / ὅλμον δʼ ὣς ἔσσευε κυλίνδεσθαι διʼ ὁμίλου: “Hippolochus leapt down, and him he slew and stripped on the ground, cutting off his arms with the sword and hewing through his neck, and he sent him rolling like a log through the throng” (11.145–147). ὥς placed after its noun is the short form of comparison, “like a log”. ἔσσευε is “sent off, drove”, and κυλίνδεσθαι “to roll”.

ὅλμος occurs nowhere else in the Iliad, and its sense here is not certain: a round stone or a log, a roller. Hesiod's ὅλμος is a mortar, to be cut three feet long from a tree (*Works and Days* 423). Either way it is a solid, rounded piece of wood or stone, and that is what is left of Hippolochus when the arms and head are gone: a trunk that rolls (see the translation's note on 11.147).

## In context

The two sons of Antimachus had lost the reins and begged from their chariot to be taken alive, and Agamemnon refused them because their father had urged the Trojans to kill Menelaus when he came as an envoy (11.122–142; see [[sons-of-antimachus]] and [[the-embassy-to-troy]]). He spears Peisander in the chest from the chariot; Hippolochus leaps down, and is mutilated where he lands (11.143–147; see [[hippolochus-son-of-antimachus]]). The comparison makes the man a thing, and it closes the most savage of Agamemnon's killings (see [[the-aristeia-of-agamemnon]]).

## Elsewhere in Homer

A head is sent through the throng in the same way in Book 13. The Locrian Ajax cuts off Imbrius' head in anger for the death of Amphimachus and sends it spinning like a ball through the throng, until it falls in the dust at Hector's feet (σφαιρηδὸν … διʼ ὁμίλου, 13.202–205). Agamemnon beheads Coon a hundred lines after Hippolochus (11.261).
