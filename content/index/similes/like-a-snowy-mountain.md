## The simile

{{quote:88809c0b-006a-5b01-a540-2335a840d248}}

ἦ ῥα, καὶ ὁρμήθη ὄρεϊ νιφόεντι ἐοικὼς / κεκλήγων, διὰ δὲ Τρώων πέτετʼ ἠδʼ ἐπικούρων: “He spoke, and rushed off like a snowy mountain, shouting, and flew through the Trojans and the allies” (13.754–755). The comparison is half a verse and occurs only here. νιφόεις, “snowy”, is said of high mountains: of the mountains of Thrace (14.227), of Olympus (18.616), of Tmolus (20.385) and of the mountains of Crete (*Odyssey* 19.338). κεκλήγων, “shouting”, is the shrill cry of birds and of men in an onset (12.125, 16.430, 17.756), and πέτετο, “flew”, makes Hector a bird in the next half-verse.

What a snowy mountain shares with Hector is not said (see the translation's note on 13.754). It has been taken of his towering size, of the white gleam of his armor and his helmet's plume, or of the rush of snow sliding down a mountainside. The first two fit a mountain that stands still; the third fits the verb ὁρμήθη, “rushed off”, and the flight that follows. A mountain is the likeness of size in the Odyssey, where the Cyclops is like a wooded peak of high mountains that stands out alone from the others (*Odyssey* 9.190–192).

## In context

Polydamas has warned Hector that the Trojans are scattered among the ships and that “a man insatiable of war” is waiting by them, and has advised him to call the best men together (13.726–747; see [[polydamas]]). “So spoke Polydamas, and his safe counsel pleased Hector”, and he leaps from his chariot and tells Polydamas to hold the best men there while he goes to give orders (13.748–753). The simile carries him off. The Trojans hurry to Polydamas, and Hector goes looking for Deiphobus, Helenus, Adamas and Asius, and finds them dead or wounded (13.756–764). He finds Paris instead (13.765–788; see [[hector]] and [[paris]]).

## Elsewhere in Homer

Snow in the Iliad's similes is a thing of number and of whiteness: words thick as winter snowflakes (3.222; see [[snowflakes-of-winter]]), stones falling like the snow of Zeus (12.278–286; see [[the-great-snowfall]]), helmets and shields pouring out of the ships like snowflakes (19.357–361). Hector's other likenesses in this book are a flame (13.53, 13.688; see [[like-a-flame]]), a rolling boulder (13.137–146; see [[the-rolling-boulder]]) and Ares (13.802).
