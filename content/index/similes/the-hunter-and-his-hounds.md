## The simile

ὡς δʼ ὅτε πού τις θηρητὴρ κύνας ἀργιόδοντας / σεύῃ ἐπʼ ἀγροτέρῳ συῒ καπρίῳ ἠὲ λέοντι, / ὣς ἐπʼ Ἀχαιοῖσιν σεῦε Τρῶας μεγαθύμους / Ἕκτωρ Πριαμίδης βροτολοιγῷ ἶσος Ἄρηϊ: “and as a hunter sets his white-toothed hounds on a wild boar or a lion, so against the Achaeans he set on the great-hearted Trojans, Hector, son of Priam, the equal of Ares, ruin of mortals” (11.292–295). θηρητήρ is “hunter” (θήρ, “wild beast”). ἀργιόδους, “white-toothed”, is elsewhere the epithet of boars, “with white tusks” (9.539; also 10.264, 23.32); here it is given to the dogs. σεύω, “set on, urge”, is used in both halves, σεύῃ … σεῦε, and joins the hunter to Hector.

The roles are clear. Hector is the master of the hunt, the Trojans his hounds, and the Achaeans the quarry, but the quarry is a boar or a lion, the two beasts that turn on their hunters (see [[boar]], [[lion]] and [[dogs]]). The simile gives Hector command, and it keeps the danger of the Achaeans in view.

## In context

Hector has seen Agamemnon leave the field and calls on the Trojans, Lycians and Dardanians: their best man is gone, and Zeus has given Hector a great triumph (11.284–291; see [[hector]] and [[plan-of-zeus]]). The hunter's simile follows, and then Hector himself goes in among the foremost and falls on the battle like a storm wind (11.296–298; see [[the-storm-wind-on-the-sea]]). He kills nine Achaean leaders and then the multitude (11.299–309; see [[the-west-wind-and-the-clouds]]).

## Elsewhere in Homer

In Book 8 Hector was the hound himself, catching at a boar or a lion from behind (8.338–342; see [[the-hound-at-the-heels]]); here he is the hunter who sets the hounds on. Hunting dogs fill the similes of Book 11. Odysseus and Diomedes fall on the hunting dogs like two boars (11.324–326; see [[two-boars]]), and dogs and young men close in on Odysseus as on a boar at bay (11.414–420; see [[the-boar-at-bay]]). In Book 12 the scene is turned on Hector, who wheels among his enemies like a boar or a lion among dogs and hunters (12.41–48), and in the fight for Patroclus' body the Trojans rush like dogs at a wounded boar until it turns on them (17.725–734).
