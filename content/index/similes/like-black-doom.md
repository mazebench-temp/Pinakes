## The simile

οὐ μὲν γὰρ φιλότητί γʼ ἐκεύθανον εἴ τις ἴδοιτο· / ἶσον γάρ σφιν πᾶσιν ἀπήχθετο κηρὶ μελαίνῃ: “for they would not have hidden him out of love, if anyone had seen him; for he was hateful to them all, like black doom”. ἶσον … κηρί is “equally with doom”. κήρ is the doom of death, the fate that takes a man (see [[ker-doom]]), and it is often “black” (μέλαινα). ἀπήχθετο, “was hateful”, is the verb of lasting enmity: it is used of men hated by all the gods, like Lycurgus and Bellerophon (6.140, 6.200), and of Troy itself, hateful to Hera, Poseidon and Athena “because of Alexander's blind folly” (24.27–28).

The comparison measures hatred by the most hateful thing a man can name. φιλότης (“love, friendship”) in the line before is the word of the truce, “making friendship and swearing a trusty truce” (3.73, 3.94), and of the bed that Paris has just gone to (3.441, 3.445; see [[philotes]]).

## In context

{{quote:f33d8b09-db86-5ab5-9ca6-26eb273ec9dc}}

Menelaus is searching the throng “like a wild beast” (3.449; see [[like-a-wild-beast]]). The narrator explains why the search fails: no one could show him Alexander, and it was not because anyone was protecting him. Three times in the book Alexander escapes doom. He draws back from Menelaus “avoiding doom” (κῆρʼ ἀλεείνων, 3.32); the spear cuts his tunic and “he swerved aside and avoided black doom” (ἀλεύατο κῆρα μέλαιναν, 3.360); and Aphrodite snatches him from Menelaus' hands (3.380). Now the word comes back to describe him. To his own people, the man who has escaped black doom is black doom.

The hatred has been heard before. Hector told him that if the Trojans were not such cowards he would already have “put on a tunic of stone” (3.56–57). The old men at the gates wanted Helen sent home, “and not be left here to be a grief to us and to our children after us” (3.160). In Book 6 Hector wishes the earth would open for his brother (6.281–285), and in Book 7 the herald Idaeus, reporting that Paris will give back the treasure but not Helen, adds, “I wish he had perished before that!” (7.390).

## Elsewhere in Homer

Penelope uses the same image of the suitor Antinous: “Antinous most of all is like black doom” (Odyssey 17.500). Achilles measures hatred by death in another form, when he tells the embassy that the man who hides one thing in his heart and says another is as hateful to him “as the gates of Hades” (9.312–313).
