## The comparison

Ἕκτωρ δʼ ἀμφιπεριστρώφα καλλίτριχας ἵππους / Γοργοῦς ὄμματʼ ἔχων ἠδὲ βροτολοιγοῦ Ἄρηος (8.348–349). ἀμφιπεριστρωφάω, “wheel round and about”, occurs only here: Hector drives his horses this way and that. ὄμματʼ ἔχων is “having the eyes”: there is no word of comparison, no “like” or “as when”, and the figure says that Hector has the Gorgon's eyes and Ares' eyes. ἠδέ joins the two, “and”; the translation's “or” offers them as alternatives, as English prefers (see the translation's note on 8.349).

The Gorgon is a face and a gaze. Homer shows her only as a head, on Athena's aegis, “dreadful and terrible” (5.741–742), and on Agamemnon's shield, where “the grim-faced Gorgon” is set as a crown, “glaring terribly”, with Terror and Rout around her (11.36–37; see [[gorgon]] and [[aegis]]). Ares is the god of the battle's slaughter, βροτολοιγός, “ruin of mortals” (see [[ares]] and [[ruin-of-mortals]]). The two together make Hector's look the look of terror and of killing. The Achaeans, who have just halted at their ships and lifted their hands to the gods (8.345–347), are the ones who must face it.

## In Book 8

The line ends Hector's advance of the afternoon, after the simile of the hound at the boar's heels (8.338–342; see [[the-hound-at-the-heels]]), and it sets him beside the god with whom the day has already matched him: Hector hemmed the Achaeans in, “the equal of swift Ares” (8.215; see [[hector]]). It is the last picture of him before the poem goes up to Olympus, where Hera pities the Achaeans and Athena wishes him dead (8.350–359).

## Elsewhere in Homer

A hero's fury shows in his eyes. Agamemnon's eyes are “like blazing fire” when he rises to answer Calchas (1.104; see [[eyes-like-fire]]). When Hector breaks through the gate of the Achaean wall, his eyes blaze with fire (πυρὶ δʼ ὄσσε δεδήει, 12.466); when he storms the ships, he rages like Ares, foam about his mouth, and his eyes flash under grim brows (15.605–608). When Achilles sees the new armor, his eyes flash out terribly from under his lids like a blaze (19.16–17). Hector is often matched with Ares in the battles to come, “the equal of Ares, ruin of mortals” (11.295, 13.802).
