## The simile

ὣς οἳ μὲν μάρναντο δέμας πυρὸς αἰθομένοιο: “so they fought on like blazing fire” (11.596). δέμας is “body, build”, and in the accusative it serves as a word of comparison, “in the likeness of”, as in δέμας ἀθανάτοισιν ὁμοῖος, “like the immortals in build” (*Odyssey* 3.468). πυρὸς αἰθομένοιο is “of blazing fire”. The comparison builds no scene. It gives the fighting the heat, the spreading and the endlessness of fire.

The verse is a formula of transition. Its μέν looks forward to a δέ, and the narrative leaves the fighters to go on while it turns elsewhere: “So they fought on like blazing fire; and the Neleian mares carried Nestor out of the war” (11.596–597). The story moves from the battle to Nestor's chariot and Achilles watching it from his ship (see [[achilles-withdraws]]).

## In context

The verse closes the battle of the afternoon in Book 11. Zeus has put Ajax to flight, Eurypylus has come to his side and been shot by Paris, and the Achaeans have formed up with their shields sloped against their shoulders to receive Ajax, who turns and stands when he reaches them (11.575–595; see [[eurypylus]] and [[ajax]]). The fire is left burning while the book turns to Patroclus' errand and Nestor's long speech. The fighting is not seen again in this book; when the story comes back to it at the start of Book 12, it has reached the Achaean ditch and wall (12.1–9).

## Elsewhere in Homer

The same verse turns the story three times more. It closes a fight on the left of the battle before the narrative turns to Hector, who did not know of it (13.673–674); it closes the fighting around the body of Patroclus, in a variant that goes on, “and you would not have said that the sun was still safe, or the moon” (17.366–367); and it opens Book 18, as Antilochus runs to bring Achilles the news (18.1–2). Fire is a common likeness for fighting men in the Iliad: the Trojans follow Hector like flame or a storm wind (φλογὶ ἶσοι … ἠὲ θυέλλῃ, 13.39–40), and Hector rages like a deadly fire in the mountains (15.605–606; see [[forest-fire]]).
