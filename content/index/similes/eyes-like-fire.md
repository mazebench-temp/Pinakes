## The simile

ὄσσε δέ οἱ πυρὶ λαμπετόωντι ἐΐκτην: “and his eyes were like blazing fire”. ὄσσε is a dual, “the two eyes”. πυρί is the dative of πῦρ, “fire”, and λαμπετόωντι, “blazing”, is from an epic verb related to λάμπω, “shine”. ἐΐκτην is a dual pluperfect of ἔοικα, “be like”. The eyes are compared with a burning fire: they shine with anger.

## In context

{{quote:e7897fe9-de29-5f2b-9e34-091af596a409}}

Calchas has just named Agamemnon as the cause of the plague. The king rises “in distress” (ἀχνύμενος). His mind, “dark on every side” (φρένες ἀμφιμέλαιναι), is filled with fury ([[menos]]), and his eyes blaze: darkness within, fire in the eyes. The next line carries the look into speech. He addresses Calchas κάκʼ ὀσσόμενος, “with menace in his look”, a verb from the same root as ὄσσε. His first words are “Prophet of evils” ([[prophet-of-evils]]). See [[eyes]] and [[phrenes]].

When Athena appears to Achilles, “her eyes blazed terribly” ({{ref:Iliad 1.200}}). The Greek does not say whose eyes; some readers take them to be Achilles', on the model of this line ([[whose-eyes-blaze]]).

## Outside Book 1

The same lines, from “in distress” to the blazing eyes, describe Antinous, the leader of the suitors, when he learns that Telemachus has sailed (Odyssey 4.661–662). When Achilles arms at last to avenge Patroclus, his eyes “shone like the blaze of fire” (Iliad 19.365–366).
