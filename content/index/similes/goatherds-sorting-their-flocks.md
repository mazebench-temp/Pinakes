## The simile

τοὺς δʼ ὥς τʼ αἰπόλια πλατέʼ αἰγῶν αἰπόλοι ἄνδρες / ῥεῖα διακρίνωσιν ἐπεί κε νομῷ μιγέωσιν, / ὣς τοὺς ἡγεμόνες διεκόσμεον ἔνθα καὶ ἔνθα / ὑσμίνην δʼ ἰέναι: “and as goatherds sort out the wide flocks of goats easily, when they have mingled in the pasture, so the leaders marshaled them, this way and that, to go into battle”. αἰπόλια are herds of goats ([[goats]]), πλατέα “wide, spreading”, and αἰπόλοι ἄνδρες “goatherd men”. ῥεῖα is “easily”, διακρίνωσιν “sort out, separate”, νομῷ “in the pasture”. διεκόσμεον, from διακοσμέω, is “set in order, marshal”.

The vehicle is a herdsman who easily picks his own animals out of a mixed herd. The tenor is the ἡγεμόνες (“leaders”) setting the mass of men in order, each over his own contingent. The point of likeness is ease and knowledge. A goatherd knows his goats, and a leader knows his men.

## In context

{{quote:1c54ff70-6f00-53a3-a21b-65bc66b1162d}}

After the similes of number ([[leaves-and-flowers]], [[flies-around-the-milk-pails]]), order comes to the multitude. Nestor had told Agamemnon to “separate the men by tribes and by clans” (κρῖνʼ ἄνδρας κατὰ φῦλα κατὰ φρήτρας), so that each would fight among his own and the brave and the cowardly would be known (2.362–368; [[phylon]]). The kings carry out the advice: they “rushed about marshaling them” (θῦνον κρίνοντες, 2.446), and here the leaders divide the men (διεκόσμεον) as goatherds divide their goats (διακρίνωσιν). The only other use of διακοσμέω in the book is in Agamemnon's fantasy of counting: “we Achaeans ranged in companies of ten” (διακοσμηθεῖμεν, 2.126; [[numbers-and-counting]]).

The ἡγεμόνες of the simile are the men whom the poet asks the Muses to name, “who were the leaders and lords of the Danaans” (2.487), and the Catalogue lists them contingent by contingent ([[catalogue-of-ships]]). The herdsman suits the Homeric language of rule, in which a king is ποιμὴν λαῶν, “shepherd of the people” ([[shepherd-of-the-people]]). The sentence then runs straight on to the one leader above the rest, “and among them was powerful Agamemnon” ([[like-zeus-ares-and-poseidon]]).
