## The simile

{{quote:92095ec8-5305-5acb-b609-ea0fe10cf139}}

ἐκ δὲ τὼ ἀΐξαντε πυλάων πρόσθε μαχέσθην / ἀγροτέροισι σύεσσιν ἐοικότε: “the two darted out and fought in front of the gates, like two wild boars” (12.145–146). The comparison is in the dual, the number for a pair: ἀΐξαντε, μαχέσθην, ἐοικότε, and in the simile τώ, δοχμώ, ἀΐσσοντε and ἄγνυτον. σῦς ἀγρότερος is the wild boar (see [[boar]]). The boars ἀνδρῶν ἠδὲ κυνῶν δέχαται κολοσυρτὸν ἰόντα, “await the din of men and dogs coming on” (12.147): κολοσυρτός, the noisy rush of a hunting party, occurs only here and in the boar simile of Idomeneus (13.472). δοχμώ τʼ ἀΐσσοντε περὶ σφίσιν ἄγνυτον ὕλην / πρυμνὴν ἐκτάμνοντες: “and darting sideways they break the wood around them, cutting it off at the root” (12.148–149); δοχμώ, “slantwise”, is found only here. ὑπαὶ δέ τε κόμπος ὀδόντων / γίγνεται, “and a gnashing of tusks goes on”, is the half-line of Odysseus' boar at bay (11.417), and it goes on εἰς ὅ κέ τίς τε βαλὼν ἐκ θυμὸν ἕληται, “until someone hits them and takes their life” (12.149–150).

The tenor turns the sound of the tusks into the sound of bronze: ὣς τῶν κόμπει χαλκὸς ἐπὶ στήθεσσι φαεινὸς / ἄντην βαλλομένων, “so rang the shining bronze on the chests of these two as they were hit face on” (12.151–152). κομπέω, “ring, clash”, is made from κόμπος and occurs only here. They fought very fiercely, “trusting in the men above them and in their own strength” (12.152–153), the defenders on the towers, whose stones fall like snow in the next simile (see [[a-snow-of-stones]]).

The point of likeness is ferocity at bay. The boars do not run from the hunt: they wait for it, turn on it, and the wood breaks around them. The simile ends with the boars' death, but the Lapiths do not die.

## In context

The oaks of the first simile stood still in front of the gates (12.131–136; see [[oaks-on-the-mountains]]). For a while, the narrator goes back to tell, the two stayed inside, rousing the Achaeans to fight for the ships; when they saw the Trojans rushing at the wall, and shouting and rout rose among the Danaans, they darted out (12.141–145). The boars are their stand outside the gate. Asius' complaint follows (12.162–172; see [[wasps-at-the-roadside]]), and then the killing: Polypoetes kills Damasus, Pylon and Ormenus, and Leonteus Hippomachus, Antiphates, Menon, Iamenus and Orestes (12.182–194; see [[polypoetes]], [[leonteus]] and [[lapiths]]).

## Elsewhere in Homer

The boar at bay is the Iliad's image for a defender who holds his ground (see [[like-a-boar]]). Odysseus, cut off, is a boar that comes out of a thicket whetting its tusk while dogs and young men close in around it (11.414–420; see [[the-boar-at-bay]]). Idomeneus waits for Aeneas like a boar in the mountains that awaits the κολοσυρτός of a crowd of men coming at it in a lonely place, its back bristling and its eyes shining with fire, and whets its tusks to fight off dogs and men (13.470–477). Ajax goes through the champions like a boar that scatters dogs and young men in the mountains (17.281–285).

Two boars, also in the dual, are Odysseus and Diomedes in Book 11, but they attack: they fall in high spirits on the hunting dogs, and the hunted become the hunters (11.324–327; see [[two-boars]]). The Lapiths' boars wait for the hunt to come to them.
