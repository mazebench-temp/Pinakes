## The simile

{{quote:13f08b55-ff45-5103-9c12-ffa3bb952127}}

Τρῶες δὲ φλογὶ ἶσοι ἀολλέες ἠὲ θυέλλῃ / Ἕκτορι Πριαμίδῃ ἄμοτον μεμαῶτες ἕποντο: “And the Trojans, like a flame or a storm wind, in a body, raging without end, followed Hector, son of Priam” (13.39–40). φλόξ is the flame, and ἶσος “equal to”; the usual form of the phrase is φλογὶ εἴκελος, with εἴκελος, the epic form of ἴκελος, “like”. The comparison is a half-verse and builds no scene. It gives the heat, the speed and the spread of fire, and here the force of the wind as well, θύελλα, the storm wind (see [[the-storm-wind-on-the-sea]]). The Trojans come on ἄβρομοι αὐΐαχοι, “roaring and crying aloud” (13.41; see the translation's note).

The phrase belongs to Hector. Poseidon, in Calchas' likeness, fears most the place “where that frenzied man leads them, like a flame, Hector, who claims to be the son of mighty Zeus” (13.53–54; see [[frenzied]]). Where he holds on at the gate he broke, by the ships of Ajax and Protesilaus, the Boeotians, Ionians, Locrians, Phthians and Epeians “could not push away from them brilliant Hector, like a flame” (13.679–688; see [[hector]]). Once it is given to an Achaean: the Trojans see Idomeneus φλογὶ εἴκελον ἀλκήν, “like a flame in his courage”, with his attendant, and all come at him (13.330–332; see [[idomeneus]]). ἀλκήν limits the likeness, as in Idomeneus' other likeness, “like a boar in courage” (συῒ εἴκελος ἀλκήν, 4.253; see [[like-a-boar]] and [[alke]]).

The flame is the Trojans' aim as well as their likeness. Hector has promised fire for the ships since Book 8 (8.180–182) and called for it as he broke the gate (12.440–441), and Menelaus says in this book that the Trojans are eager “to throw deadly fire” among them (13.628–629).

## Elsewhere in Homer

The half-verse returns three times for Hector: shouting shrilly as he comes for Patroclus' body, “like the unquenchable flame of Hephaestus” (φλογὶ εἴκελος Ἡφαίστοιο / ἀσβέστῳ, 17.88–89), pursuing the body again “like a flame in his courage” (18.154), and going out against Achilles after the death of Polydorus, brandishing his spear (20.423). Fire is a likeness for fighting men in fuller similes too: fighters battle “like blazing fire” (11.596, 13.673; see [[like-blazing-fire]]), the Achaean bronze flashes like a forest fire (2.455–458) and Agamemnon falls on the Trojans like one (11.155–159; see [[forest-fire]]), and Hector rages like Ares or a deadly fire in the mountains (15.605–606). Hector says of Achilles, “even if his hands are like fire” (εἰ πυρὶ χεῖρας ἔοικε, 20.371–372).
