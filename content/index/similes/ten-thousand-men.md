## The simile

{{quote:a278f8c2-33a9-5c7a-bb7d-1d9f60297189}}

ὃ δʼ ἔβραχε χάλκεος Ἄρης / ὅσσόν τʼ ἐννεάχιλοι ἐπίαχον ἢ δεκάχιλοι / ἀνέρες ἐν πολέμῳ ἔριδα ξυνάγοντες Ἄρηος: “and bronze Ares bellowed as loud as nine thousand or ten thousand men shout in war as they join in the strife of Ares”. βράχω is a word for a crashing, clanging noise. It is the ring of bronze armor on a man who leaps down from his chariot (4.420), the groan of a chariot axle under a goddess' weight (5.838), the scream of a wounded horse (16.468), and the ringing of the wide earth when the gods clash (21.387). Here it is a god's roar of pain. ἐννεάχιλοι and δεκάχιλοι, “nine thousand” and “ten thousand”, occur only here and in the lines of Book 14 that repeat this one. ἐπίαχον is “shout (as they charge)”, and ἔριδα ξυνάγοντες Ἄρηος, “joining the strife of Ares”, is a phrase for battle, with the god's own name in the measure of his voice.

The point of likeness is loudness, and it is measured by number. Two large numbers side by side (“nine or ten thousand”) give the noise of a whole army, and the army is shouting at the onset of battle, which is what Ares is. The effect follows: τοὺς δʼ ἄρʼ ὑπὸ τρόμος εἷλεν Ἀχαιούς τε Τρῶάς τε / δείσαντας, “and trembling seized them, Achaeans and Trojans alike, in their fear” (5.862–863). Both armies hear it.

## In context

Athena, wearing the helmet of Hades so that Ares cannot see her, has taken the reins of Diomedes' chariot. She catches Ares' spear and pushes it away, and drives Diomedes' spear into the lowest part of his flank, “where he girded on his band”. The spear tears the fair skin, and Diomedes draws it out again (5.835–859; see [[helmet-of-hades]] and [[diomedes-wounds-ares]]).

Shortly before, Hera shouted to the Argives in the likeness of Stentor, “who could shout as loud as fifty other men” (5.785–786; see [[stentor]]). The two comparisons measure two voices. The loudest of mortals has the voice of fifty men; the wounded god has the voice of an army. After the bellow Ares goes up into the sky like a dark mist (5.864–867; see [[dark-mist-from-the-clouds]]).

## Elsewhere in Homer

The same lines are used of Poseidon in Book 14, when he rouses the Achaeans at the ships: he shouted “as loud as nine thousand or ten thousand men shout in war as they join in the strife of Ares; such a voice did the lord, the earth-shaker, send out from his chest”, and put great strength into every Achaean's heart (14.147–152). The god's shout gives one army courage; the war god's cry of pain makes both armies tremble. The great mortal shout of the poem is Achilles' at the trench, clear as a trumpet when enemies surround a city, which three times throws the Trojans into confusion (18.217–229).
