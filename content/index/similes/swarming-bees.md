## The simile

{{quote:ba4da8de-746b-5d9d-99e3-258d5b8b58ce}}

ἠΰτε ἔθνεα εἶσι μελισσάων ἁδινάων: “as the tribes of thronging bees go out”. ἔθνεα are “tribes, bands”, a word for any body of creatures that moves together. μελισσάων is “bees” (see [[bees]]), and ἁδινάων “close-packed, thronging”. They come “from a hollow rock” (πέτρης ἐκ γλαφυρῆς), “always coming on anew” (αἰεὶ νέον ἐρχομενάων), and fly βοτρυδόν, “in clusters”, like bunches of grapes (βότρυς), over the flowers of spring. “Some are on the wing here in crowds, and some there” (αἳ μέν … αἳ δέ).

The bees are the army. “So the many tribes of men (ὣς τῶν ἔθνεα πολλά), from the ships and huts, marched in companies along the front of the deep shore, crowd after crowd (ἰλαδόν), to the assembly.” The same word, ἔθνεα, serves for both, and ἰλαδόν, “in bands”, answers βοτρυδόν. The point of likeness is number without end and movement in groups: swarm after swarm keeps coming, and each goes its own way.

## In context

Nestor leads the kings out of the council, and the people stream after them (2.84–86). As they go, Rumor, “the messenger of Zeus”, blazes among them (see [[ossa]]). The place of assembly is soon in uproar; the earth groans as they sit, and nine heralds shout to quiet them (2.93–99).

Book 1 has three similes of half a line or a line (see [[like-night]], [[like-a-mist]], [[eyes-like-fire]]). This is the first that builds a whole scene of its own, and it opens the book in the Iliad richest in similes. The army comes to the assembly as a swarm, is stirred by Agamemnon's speech like the sea and like a field of grain (see [[waves-of-the-icarian-sea]] and [[wind-in-the-wheat]]), and runs for the ships. When it marches out at last to battle, the similes return in a cluster: fire, birds, leaves, flies, goatherds (2.455–483). The word ἔθνεα comes back with them, for the birds (2.459), the men (2.464) and the flies (2.469); see [[birds-on-the-asian-meadow]] and [[flies-around-the-milk-pails]].

The picture is of spring and plenty, bees seeking flowers. The men are going to an assembly that will almost send them home, and then to war. Some readers also hear in “some here, some there” the disorder that breaks out a few lines later.

## Elsewhere in Homer

Bees return in the battle books, as defenders of their home: the Lapiths at the gate stand firm like wasps or bees that will not leave their hollow house (12.167–172). The Myrmidons pour out like wasps that boys have stirred up by the road (16.259–265).

## Outside Homer

Virgil gave the image to his own crowds: the Carthaginians building their city like bees in early summer (*Aeneid* 1.430–436), and the souls waiting by the river Lethe like bees in a meadow among the flowers (*Aeneid* 6.707–709).
