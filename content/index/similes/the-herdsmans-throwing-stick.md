## The simile

{{quote:24cdbd7c-3341-5349-8cd9-585d8afac8c2}}

ὅσσόν τίς τʼ ἔρριψε καλαύροπα βουκόλος ἀνήρ, / ἣ δέ θʼ ἑλισσομένη πέτεται διὰ βοῦς ἀγελαίας: “as far as a herdsman hurls his throwing stick, / and it flies spinning through the cattle of the herd” (23.845–846). The καλαῦροψ occurs only here in Homer. Walter Leaf takes the second part of the word from ῥέπω, “incline”, and reports Curtius' view, after Hoffmann, that the first part is κάλος, “cord”, so that the stick would have had a loop of cord to help the throw, as the T scholia describe it, with a loop at its lower end through which the hand was put (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 23.845). It goes spinning, ἑλισσομένη, through the herd. βόες ἀγελαῖαι are the cows of the herd at pasture, about which the calves frisk when the cows come home to the yard in a simile of the Odyssey (*Odyssey* 10.410–414).

The tenor: τόσσον παντὸς ἀγῶνος ὑπέρβαλε, “so far did he overshoot the whole gathering” (23.847). The sense of ἀγῶνος is not certain. Leaf thinks it can hardly mean beyond all the spectators, and takes it as beyond all the competitors, or beyond the ground marked out for the contest (on 23.847; see the translation's note on 23.847). The simile measures a throw by a throw. Ajax had just overshot the marks of all the others, the pegs set in the ground at each man's cast (23.842–843); Polypoetes overshoots Ajax by as much as a herdsman can throw his stick, and the measure itself spins through the air like the lump.

The likeness fits the prize. Achilles has said that the winner may live far from any city, but the lump of iron will last him five years, and “for not for lack of iron will his shepherd / or his plowman go to the city” (23.834–835; see [[iron]]). The man who wins the farmer's iron throws it as far as a farmer's man throws his stick.

## In context

The lump, rough as it was cast, had belonged to Eetion, Andromache's father, who used to throw it; Achilles killed him and brought it away in his ships with the rest of his possessions (23.826–829; see [[eetion]]). Four men rise: Polypoetes, Leonteus, Telamonian Ajax and Epeius. Epeius whirls it and throws, and the Achaeans laugh; Leonteus throws second, and Ajax third, overshooting the marks of all; then Polypoetes takes it (23.836–846; see [[polypoetes]], [[leonteus]], [[ajax]] and [[epeius]]). After the simile, “they shouted”, and his companions carry the king's prize to the hollow ships (23.847–849; see [[the-funeral-games-of-patroclus]]).

## Elsewhere in Homer

The Iliad measures its distances by throws: the cast of a spear or a javelin, the range of a discus, the throw of a stone (3.12, 15.358–359, 16.589–590, 23.431–432; see [[the-cast-of-a-spear]]). Herdsmen work in other similes: they bind a struggling ox with twisted ropes in the mountains and lead it along against its will (13.571–572), and a beast that has killed a dog or a herdsman among the cattle runs away before the men gather (15.586–588; see [[the-beast-that-has-done-harm]]). In the Odyssey, at the games of the Phaeacians, Odysseus takes up a discus larger than theirs, and the stone flies past the marks of all (ὑπέρπτατο σήματα πάντων, *Odyssey* 8.186–193), the half-verse that Ajax's throw echoes here; Leaf reports that 23.843 was athetized in antiquity as borrowed from that passage (on 23.843).
