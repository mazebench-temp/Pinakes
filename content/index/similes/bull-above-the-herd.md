## The simile

ἠΰτε βοῦς ἀγέληφι μέγʼ ἔξοχος ἔπλετο πάντων / ταῦρος· ὃ γάρ τε βόεσσι μεταπρέπει ἀγρομένῃσι: “as an ox in a herd stands out far above all the rest, a bull, for he is conspicuous among the gathered cattle”. βοῦς is any head of cattle ([[cattle]]), and ταῦρος, held back to the start of the next line, makes it a bull ([[bulls]]). ἀγέληφι is “in the herd”, from ἀγέλη (“herd”) with the old case ending -φι. ἔξοχος (“standing out”) and μεταπρέπει (“is conspicuous among”) are words of prominence.

The tenor repeats them: τοῖον ἄρʼ Ἀτρεΐδην θῆκε Ζεὺς ἤματι κείνῳ / ἐκπρεπέʼ ἐν πολλοῖσι καὶ ἔξοχον ἡρώεσσιν, “such did Zeus make the son of Atreus on that day, conspicuous among many and preeminent among heroes”. ἐκπρεπέα answers μεταπρέπει, and ἔξοχον answers ἔξοχος. The point of likeness is size and prominence: the bull, the leader of the herd, is seen above all the rest.

## In context

{{quote:771472fc-8d29-5183-8bff-5bf79aedab0b}}

The simile completes the comparison of Agamemnon with three gods ([[like-zeus-ares-and-poseidon]]) and closes the run that began with the gleam of bronze ([[forest-fire]]). The next line calls on the Muses ([[invocation-of-the-muses]]), and the Catalogue follows ([[catalogue-of-ships]]). There he stands out again in the same terms: “he stood out among all the heroes, because he was the best, and led by far the most men” (πᾶσιν δὲ μετέπρεπεν ἡρώεσσιν, 2.579–580).

His prominence is Zeus' doing, “on that day” (ἤματι κείνῳ). The same words ended a line of the narrator's judgment on the Dream: Agamemnon thought he would take the city of Priam “that very day” (ἤματι κείνῳ, 2.37), “the fool” ([[nepios|νήπιος]]; [[agamemnons-dream]]). Some readers see irony in the echo. Zeus gives the king splendor on the day he has deceived him, soon after refusing his prayer to take Troy before sunset (2.419–420).

## Elsewhere in Homer

Bull and herd return at the death of Sarpedon, the leader of the Lycians, who falls to Patroclus like a tawny bull that a lion kills among the herd (ἀγέληφι … βόεσσι, Iliad 16.487–491).
