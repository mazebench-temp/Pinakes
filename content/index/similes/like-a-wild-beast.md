## The simile

Ἀτρεΐδης δʼ ἀνʼ ὅμιλον ἐφοίτα θηρὶ ἐοικὼς / εἴ που ἐσαθρήσειεν Ἀλέξανδρον θεοειδέα: “but the son of Atreus ranged through the throng like a wild beast, to see if he could catch sight of godlike Alexander”. θήρ is a wild beast, most often a beast of prey; in the Iliad's similes it is usually the lion. ἐφοίτα is “went to and fro, ranged”, and ἐσαθρήσειεν, “catch sight of”, occurs only here. The comparison is a half-line and gives no picture beyond the word. The point is the movement of a hunting animal that has lost its quarry and casts about for it.

## In context

{{quote:a6e20a1a-fa63-5a11-940c-f785a1203826}}

The line follows directly on the bed. “So the two of them lay down together on the corded bed; but the son of Atreus ranged through the throng like a wild beast” (3.448–449). The two lines put the lovers inside the house and the husband outside it, still hunting the man who is in bed with his wife.

The simile closes the ring that opened with the lion. When Menelaus first saw Alexander, he rejoiced like a hungry lion that comes upon a great carcass (3.23–28; see [[lion-on-a-carcass]]). He has since held his enemy in his hands, and Aphrodite has taken him away in a mist (3.369–382; see [[aphrodite-rescues-paris]]). Now the beast is still hungry and the carcass is gone. No Trojan can show him Alexander, and none would hide him, “for he was hateful to them all, like black doom” (3.454; see [[like-black-doom]]). Agamemnon then claims the victory for his brother (3.455–461; see [[the-duel-of-paris-and-menelaus]]).

## Elsewhere in Homer

The same phrase describes Telamonian Ajax when Zeus puts fear into him: he retreats toward the throng “like a wild beast”, looking about him and turning back step by step, and a lion simile follows (11.544–555). Antilochus runs from Hector “like a beast that has done some harm”, one that has killed a dog or a herdsman and flees before the men gather (15.586–588). The opening half-line, Ἀτρεΐδης δʼ ἀνʼ ὅμιλον ἐφοίτα (“the son of Atreus ranged through the throng”), is used again of Agamemnon, going through his army with many commands (5.528).
