## The simile

{{quote:3866987f-5f06-57f4-8067-7c170d159de6}}

ἀργύρῳ ἀντομένη μόλιβος ὣς ἐτράπετʼ αἰχμή: “meeting the silver, the point was bent like lead”. μόλιβος, “lead”, occurs only here in Homer. ἀντομένη is “meeting, encountering”, and ἐτράπετο, “was turned”, is the word used three lines before for Agamemnon's own spear, which “turned aside” and missed (11.233). The point of likeness is softness. A spear point of bronze bends on the silver of the belt as if it were made of lead, the soft metal that gives way.

The belt is ζωστὴρ παναίολος, “the gleaming belt” (11.236), worked with silver. Iphidamas thrust low, below the corselet, and leaned his whole weight on the spear, “trusting in his heavy hand” (11.234–235), and the metal held (see [[belt]]).

## In context

Iphidamas, son of Antenor, is the first man to come against Agamemnon after the invocation of the Muses (11.218–231; see [[iphidamas]]). After the bent spear the exchange is quickly over. Agamemnon catches the spear in his hand, drags it toward him “eager as a lion”, wrenches it from Iphidamas' grip and strikes him on the neck with his sword (11.238–240; see [[like-a-lion]]). The bending of the point is the turn of the duel, as a spear point bent back on a shield is elsewhere (3.348, 7.259).

## Elsewhere in Homer

Pandarus' arrow did what Iphidamas' spear could not. Athena guided it to where the golden clasps of Menelaus' belt held and the corselet overlapped, and it went through the belt, the corselet and the guard beneath (4.132–138; see [[mother-and-fly]]). Lead appears once more in the Iliad as a lead weight, μολύβδαινα: Iris plunges into the sea like a lead sinker set on a piece of ox horn, which goes down bringing death to the fish (24.80–82).
