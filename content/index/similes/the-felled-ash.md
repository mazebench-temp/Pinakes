## The simile

{{quote:f8b2d322-1da2-5a27-880b-52296d51f810}}

ὃ δʼ αὖτʼ ἔπεσεν μελίη ὣς / ἥ τʼ ὄρεος κορυφῇ ἕκαθεν περιφαινομένοιο / χαλκῷ ταμνομένη τέρενα χθονὶ φύλλα πελάσσῃ: “and he fell like an ash tree that on the peak of a mountain seen from afar on every side is cut by the bronze and brings its tender leaves to the ground” (13.178–180). μελίη is the ash tree itself. It is also the wood of the Homeric spear: Achilles' spear is the “Pelian ash” that Chiron cut on the peak of Pelion (16.143–144 = 19.390–391), and the Locrians of this book have no “ash spears” (μείλινα δοῦρα, 13.715; see [[spear]]). The tree that falls to the bronze is the tree spears are made of. περιφαινόμενος is “seen on every side, conspicuous”, of the mountain whose peak carries the tree; the word returns in the Odyssey for an open place (*Odyssey* 5.476). τέρην, “tender”, is the softness of young leaves, and of young flesh (see [[tender]]): the Trojans cannot “graze the tender flesh” of Antilochus (τέρενα χρόα, 13.553).

The tenor is short: ὣς πέσεν, ἀμφὶ δέ οἱ βράχε τεύχεα ποικίλα χαλκῷ, “so he fell, and around him his armor rang, inlaid with bronze” (13.181). The crash of the tree becomes the ring of armor. The point of likeness is height brought down. The tree stands out on its peak, “seen from afar”, as Imbrius “stood out among the Trojans” (μετέπρεπε δὲ Τρώεσσι, 13.175), and its tender leaves come down with it.

## In context

Imbrius is the first man killed in the fight that follows Hector's charge. His short life is told before he dies: son of Mentor, “rich in horses”, he lived in Pedaeum before the Achaeans came and married Medesicaste, a bastard daughter of Priam; when the ships came he returned to Ilios, “and stood out among the Trojans, and lived beside Priam; and Priam honored him as he honored his children” (13.171–176; see [[imbrius]] and [[medesicaste]]). Teucer stabs him below the ear (13.177–178; see [[teucer]]). When Teucer rushes to strip him, Hector's spear misses Teucer and kills Amphimachus, Poseidon's grandson (13.182–187). The two Ajaxes carry off Imbrius like two lions with a goat (13.197–202; see [[two-lions-and-a-goat]]), and the son of Oileus cuts off his head and sends it flying through the throng to Hector's feet (13.202–205; see [[like-a-ball]]).

## Elsewhere in Homer

Warriors fall like trees throughout the Iliad: Simoeisius like a poplar felled by a chariot-maker (4.482–487; see [[the-poplar]]), the sons of Diocles like tall fir trees (5.559–560; see [[fir-trees]]), Asius and Sarpedon like an oak, a white poplar or a pine felled for ship-timber (13.389–391, 16.482–484; see [[the-felled-tree]]), Hector like an oak uprooted by the lightning of Zeus (14.414–417), and Euphorbus like a young olive tree torn up by a gale (17.53–58). In the battle over Cebriones' body the winds shake the oak, the ash and the cornel of a mountain forest, and the branches crash against one another (16.765–769).
