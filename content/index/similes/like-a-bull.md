## The simile

{{quote:4b49e0dc-c977-5b51-9649-bf8a332006e9}}

μεμυκώς is the perfect participle of μυκάομαι, the lowing or bellowing of cattle. It is said of the bull that two lions drag from the herd on the Shield, “lowing loudly” (μακρὰ μεμυκώς, 18.580), and the cattle hurry to pasture with lowing (μυκηθμῷ, 18.575); the gates of heaven groan with the same verb as they open (5.749), and the gates of the Achaean wall as Hector's stone smashes them (12.460). The river's voice in his rage is the voice of a bull. ταῦρος is the bull as a victim of the gods (see [[bulls]]), and in the comparison of *Iliad* 20 the dying Hippodamas bellows “as when a bull / bellows as he is dragged around the Heliconian lord” (20.403–404; see [[the-bull-of-the-heliconian-lord]]).

The half-verse is a likeness of sound, but it carries an old picture of rivers. Walter Leaf notes that it “probably explains the common personification of rivers in the form of bulls” (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 21.237), and he connects with the same figure the bulls that the Trojans sacrifice to this river (on 21.131; see [[rivers]]). Nestor's Pylians too give a bull to the river Alpheus (11.728; see [[alpheus]]).

## In context

Achilles has leapt from the bank into the river, and the river rushes on him with his swell, stirs up all his streams, and thrusts out the dead whom Achilles killed in them, “bellowing like a bull”; the living he hides in his eddies (21.233–239; see [[scamander]] and [[achilles-and-the-river]]). Only here, in the first moment of his attack, is the river likened to a beast. When he speaks again, it is to his brother the Simoeis (21.308–323; see [[simoeis]]).

## Elsewhere in Homer

The *Odyssey* has the comparison once, for a sound: when Penelope unbolts the storeroom where Odysseus' bow is kept, the fine doors bellow like a bull grazing in a meadow (ἀνέβραχεν ἠΰτε ταῦρος, *Odyssey* 21.46–50).

## Outside Homer

Later poets gave rivers the form of bulls. In [[euripides|Euripides]]' *Ion*, Ion, cursing Creusa, calls on “the bull-shaped face of father Cephisus”, the river of Attica from whom her line descends (ὦ ταυρόμορφον ὄμμα Κηφισοῦ πατρός, *Ion* 1261), and in the *Orestes* the Phrygian slave sings of “bull-headed” Oceanus, who circles the earth in his arms (ταυρόκρανος, *Orestes* 1376–1379; see [[oceanus]]).
