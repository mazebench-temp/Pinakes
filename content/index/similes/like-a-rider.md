## The simile

αὐτὰρ Ὀδυσσεὺς / ἀμφʼ ἑνὶ δούρατι βαῖνε, κέληθʼ ὡς ἵππον ἐλαύνων: “But Odysseus / bestrode a single beam, as though riding a horse” (*Odyssey* 5.370–371). κέλης is a riding horse, a horse ridden and not driven in a chariot, and occurs only here in Homer; ἐλαύνω is the verb of driving horses and chariots, used here of riding. ἀμφʼ ἑνὶ δούρατι βαίνω is to go astride the beam, one leg on either side. W. W. Merry renders “he bestrode a single plank, as one mounted on a riding-horse” (*Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 5.371).

The comparison follows the simile of the heap of husks, in which the wave scatters the long beams of the raft as the wind scatters dry husks (*Odyssey* 5.368–370; see [[the-heap-of-husks]]). Of all those beams Odysseus keeps one, and sits on it as a man sits on a horse. The likeness is of the posture, and perhaps of the motion, the beam rising and falling under him on the swell.

**Riding in Homer.** Merry notes that there is only one other place in Homer where the art of riding is alluded to, the simile of Ajax striding from deck to deck of the ships like a man skilled in riding, who leaps from one to another of four horses as they run (*Iliad* 15.679–684; see [[the-rider-of-four-horses]]). Even there, he says, it is not an ordinary rider but the feats of an acrobat that are described. Diomedes and Odysseus ride the horses of Rhesus back to the ships (*Iliad* 10.513–514), but that, as the scholia say, was necessity; and the horses of a later simile of the Odyssey draw a chariot (*Odyssey* 13.81–83). Merry sets beside these the mounted men outside the city of the wedding on Hesiod's shield (on 5.371): “others, before the city, mounted on horses' backs, sped along”, νῶθʼ ἵππων ἐπιβάντες ἐθύνεον (*Shield of Heracles* 285–286). Homer's heroes go to battle in chariots (see [[horses]] and [[chariot]]).

## In context

Ino has given Odysseus her veil and told him to leave the raft and swim for the land of the Phaeacians (*Odyssey* 5.343–350; see [[like-a-diving-bird]] and [[leucothea]]); he resolved to stay on the raft as long as its beams held (*Odyssey* 5.356–364). Now Poseidon's great wave breaks it, and he does as the goddess bade: astride the beam, he “stripped off the garments that brilliant Calypso had given him”, stretched the veil under his chest, and “plunged headlong into the sea, stretching out both his hands, / eager to swim” (*Odyssey* 5.372–375; see [[veil]]). The garments had weighed him down when the first wave threw him from the raft (*Odyssey* 5.321). Poseidon sees him, shakes his head, taunts him, and drives off to Aegae (*Odyssey* 5.375–381; see [[poseidon]] and [[aegae]]).
