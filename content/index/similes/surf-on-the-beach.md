## The simile

ἠχῇ, ὡς ὅτε κῦμα πολυφλοίσβοιο θαλάσσης / αἰγιαλῷ μεγάλῳ βρέμεται, σμαραγεῖ δέ τε πόντος: “with a roar, as when a wave of the loud-roaring sea crashes on the long beach, and the sea resounds”. ἠχή is “noise, roar”. πολύφλοισβος (“loud-roaring”) is a standing epithet of the sea ([[loud-roaring-sea]]). αἰγιαλός is a long, open beach. βρέμεται (“roars, crashes”) and σμαραγεῖ (“resounds”) are verbs of loud noise.

The vehicle is a wave breaking on a long shore; the tenor, the army pouring back from the ships and huts to the place of assembly. The point of likeness is sound. Three words of noise, ἠχῇ, βρέμεται and σμαραγεῖ, fill two lines, and the crowd comes back like a single wave.

## In context

{{quote:b93532aa-44ee-5de3-80c6-bc828482d157}}

Athena has sent [[Odysseus]] through the camp, and he has taken Agamemnon's scepter. He has checked the kings with gentle words and struck the men of the people with the scepter: “let there be one ruler” (2.182–206; see [[one-ruler]]). Now the rush of 2.149–154 runs in reverse. The half-line νεῶν ἄπο καὶ κλισιάων (“from the ships and huts”) is the one used of the first march to the assembly (2.91; [[swarming-bees]]), and the sea comes back with the men. The assembly that was stirred like the waves of the Icarian sea ([[waves-of-the-icarian-sea]]) breaks on the shore like a wave, and settles. “The others sat down and were held in their places”; only [[Thersites]] keeps up his brawling (2.211–212).

The words σμαραγεῖ δέ τε (“and … resounds”) return in the simile of the birds on the Asian meadow, where “the meadow resounds” (σμαραγεῖ δέ τε λειμών, 2.463; [[birds-on-the-asian-meadow]]). The third sea simile of the assembly gives the shout that greets Agamemnon's call to battle ([[wave-on-the-headland]]).

## Elsewhere in Homer

In Book 4 the Danaan ranks move to battle like the waves that the West Wind stirs up (κινήσαντος, “stirring”) and drives in, one after another, onto an echoing beach: they rear up out at sea, then break roaring on the land (Iliad 4.422–428; see [[waves-on-the-shore]]). There the picture is the rhythm of an advance; here it is the noise of a return.
