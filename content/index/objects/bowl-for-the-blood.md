## The word

ἀμνίον occurs once in Homer (*Odyssey* 3.444), and the translation renders it “the bowl for the blood”. W. W. Merry calls it the vessel for the blood and thinks the word probably connected with αἷμα, “blood”; Curtius suggested a connection with ἀμίς, “pot” (*Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 3.444). The sacrifices of the Iliad have no such vessel: their throats are cut in the one verse that tells the killing (*Iliad* 1.459, 2.422; see [[sacrifice-type-scene]]).

## Outside Homer

In the messenger's account of Aegisthus' sacrifice in Euripides' *Electra*, the slaves carry the σφαγεῖον, the vessel for the blood of the slaughter (from σφάζω, “cut the throat”), with the baskets (*Electra* 800; see [[the-sacrifice-to-athena]]).
