ἐρετμόν (“oar”), ἐρέτης (“oarsman”) and ἐρέσσω (“row”) share a root with Latin *rēmus* and English *row*. Homeric ships sailed with a fair wind and rowed without one or in harbor, and the same men did both.

The oar is the rower's emblem: Elpenor asks for his oar to be fixed on his mound, and Teiresias sends Odysseus inland with an oar until a man who has never seen the sea takes it for a winnowing fan (*Odyssey* 11.77–78, 11.121–129; see [[tomb]] and [[winnowing-fan]]).
