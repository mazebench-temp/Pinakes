κύπειρον is galingale, a sedge of the genus later called *Cyperus*, which grows in wet ground. Homer names it twice, each time with the lotus.

In the Iliad it grows by the Xanthus with the lotus and the rushes, and it burns when Hephaestus turns his fire on the river:

{{quote:ac3d0b83-6290-5fac-861a-ed50a5daeb5e}}

See [[lotus]], [[rushes]], [[scamander]] and [[fire]]. Walter Leaf reports Barker Webb's observation, by way of Schliemann, that all the trees and plants named in these verses are still found in the plain of Troy, so far as they can be identified (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 21.350).

In the *Odyssey* it is fodder. Menelaus offers Telemachus horses, and Telemachus declines them, since Ithaca has no meadows; Menelaus rules a broad plain, ᾧ ἔνι μὲν λωτὸς πολύς, ἐν δὲ κύπειρον, “in which there is much lotus, and galingale”, and wheat and spelt and barley (*Odyssey* 4.601–604; see [[menelaus]]).

## Outside Homer

In the *Homeric Hymn to Hermes* the cattle that the newborn god has stolen from Apollo are driven into their stall as they crop the lotus and the dewy galingale, λωτὸν ἐρεπτομένας ἠδʼ ἑρσήεντα κύπειρον (*Homeric Hymn to Hermes* 105–107; see [[hermes]]). The phrase λωτὸν ἐρεπτόμενοι is used in the Iliad of Achilles' idle horses (2.776).
