## Description

A χηλός has a lid (πῶμα, 16.221) and stands in a hut, a storeroom or a ship. Achilles' chest is “fine and intricately worked” (καλῆς δαιδαλέης), and his mother had put it on his ship herself, “filling it well with tunics / and cloaks to keep off the wind and woolly rugs” (16.222–224; see [[thetis]], [[tunic]] and [[cloak]]): a mother's packing for a son going far from home (see [[mothers-and-sons]]). In it he keeps his own cup, from which no other man drank the wine and from which he poured libations to no god but Zeus. Before he sends Patroclus out he takes the cup from the chest, cleanses it with sulfur and water, prays to Zeus of Dodona, and puts it back in the chest (16.225–232, 16.253–254; see [[cup]] and [[libation]]).

## In the Odyssey

Chests keep the wealth of a house. In Odysseus' storeroom, beside the gold, the bronze and the jars of old wine, lies “clothing in chests” (*Odyssey* 2.337–342), and Penelope climbs to the storeroom where the chests stand, with fragrant garments in them, to take down the bow (*Odyssey* 21.51–53). Among the Phaeacians Arete brings out a fine chest for the stranger and puts in it the clothing and gold the Phaeacians gave him, and Odysseus ties the lid fast with an intricate knot that Circe taught him (*Odyssey* 8.424–448); the chest goes with him to the ship (*Odyssey* 13.10–12, 13.68). Alcinous adds a gold cup, so that Odysseus will remember him whenever he pours libations to Zeus and the other gods (*Odyssey* 8.430–432): a cup kept for libations, like the cup in the chest of Thetis.
