## In Homer

**The τάλαρος.** On the shield of Achilles girls and young men carry the grapes of the vintage “in woven baskets” (*Iliad* 18.567–568; see [[the-shield-of-achilles]]), and the Cyclops sets his curdled milk in woven baskets (*Odyssey* 9.247, a plain rendering). Helen's τάλαρος is a lady's work basket, of silver with a golden rim and set on wheels, full of finely spun yarn, with her golden distaff lying across it (*Odyssey* 4.125–135; see [[distaff]] and [[weaving]]). W. W. Merry takes the word to mean properly what “holds” the work, and ὑπόκυκλος, with Apollonius and Hesychius against Eustathius, to mean “with wheels underneath”, so that the basket could be moved about easily, like the tripods on golden wheels that Hephaestus made (*Iliad* 18.375–377; *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 4.131; see [[tripod]]).

**The κάνεον.** At a meal the bread is set out in baskets: Patroclus and Automedon serve it “in beautiful baskets” in Achilles' hut (*Iliad* 9.216–217, 24.625–626), and in Odysseus' hall slave women heap bread beside the suitors in baskets (*Odyssey* 1.147; see [[feast-type-scene]]). Hecamede sets before Nestor and Machaon a bronze basket with an onion in it (*Iliad* 11.630; see [[hecamede]]). The barley grains for a rite are carried in one: Aretus brings them in a basket to Nestor's sacrifice (*Odyssey* 3.441–442), and Penelope puts them in a basket when she prays to Athena (*Odyssey* 4.761; see [[barley-grains]]).
