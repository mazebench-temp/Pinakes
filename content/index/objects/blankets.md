## The word

ῥῆγος, plural ῥήγεα, is a blanket or rug of fine stuff, often dyed purple (πορφύρεα) or “shining” (σιγαλόεντα). The Iliad names it twice, both times in a bed made for a guest. At Patroclus' bidding the companions and the slave women spread Phoenix's bed with “fleeces and a rug and the fine nap of linen” (*Iliad* 9.658–661), and at Achilles' they make beds for Priam and his herald: they are to “set beds beneath the colonnade, and throw fine purple / blankets on them, and spread rugs over them, / and put woolly cloaks on top for them to wear” (*Iliad* 24.643–646; see [[bed]] and [[cloak]]). The Odyssey repeats these verses when Helen and Arete have beds made for their guests (*Odyssey* 4.296–299, 7.335–338).

W. W. Merry describes the order of the bedding. The ῥήγεα were costly blankets, fine, thick and soft; on a bed a fleece lay under them, and over them, for the sleeper to lie on, a linen sheet or the still finer woolen coverlet (τάπης), while the cloak (χλαῖνα) was laid over the sleeper as a counterpane. Blankets were laid on chairs too (*Odyssey* 10.352; Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 3.349).

## Elsewhere in Homer

Blankets are part of the house's wealth and of a guest's welcome. Nausicaa takes the “shining blankets” of the palace to the river with the other clothes to be washed (*Odyssey* 6.37–38). The Phaeacians spread a blanket and linen on the deck of their ship for Odysseus to sleep on, and lift him ashore on Ithaca still asleep in them (*Odyssey* 13.73–75, 13.116–119). To lack them is a sign of grief or of a part played: Laertes, who keeps to his farm in the country, has no bed and no cloaks or shining blankets, and sleeps in winter among the slaves in the ashes by the fire (*Odyssey* 11.187–191), and the disguised Odysseus refuses the bed that Penelope offers him with the words “cloaks and shining blankets have been hateful to me” since he left Crete (*Odyssey* 19.317–318, 19.336–340; see also 20.139–142). The renderings of the later books are plain renderings, not the translation.
