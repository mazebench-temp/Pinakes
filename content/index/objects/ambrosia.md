Ambrosia itself does not appear in Book 1. There is only the adjective ἀμβρόσιος, at the moment Zeus nods to Thetis: “the ambrosial locks of the lord flowed down from his immortal head” (see [[hair|hair]] and [[brows|brows]]).

{{quote:640348fc-7fe1-56c7-a683-ce6bf661080e}}

ἀμβρόσιος means “immortal, belonging to the gods” (ἀ-, “not”, + βροτός, “mortal”). It is the same formation as Sanskrit *amṛta*, the drink of immortality. Homer uses the adjective of many divine things: the gods' hair, robes and sandals, and even night and sleep. The noun ἀμβροσίη is the gods' food, which they eat as they drink [[nectar|nectar]] (Odyssey 5.93). It is also a divine ointment: Hera cleanses her body with ambrosia (Iliad 14.170–171), and Thetis drips ambrosia and nectar into the nostrils of Patroclus' corpse to keep it from decay (Iliad 19.38–39).
