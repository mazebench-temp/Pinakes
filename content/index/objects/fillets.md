The στέμμα (from στέφω, “put around, wreathe”) is a band or wreath, probably of wool, that marks a person or thing as belonging to a god.

[[Chryses]] comes to ransom his daughter holding “the fillets of far-shooting Apollo upon a golden scepter”: the bands hang from his [[scepter|scepter]] (ἀνά, “upon”). They are Apollo's, not the priest's, and they claim for the old man the respect owed to the god. Agamemnon sees the claim and rejects it: the scepter and the fillet “may not protect you”. The lines describing the fillets return when [[Achilles]] retells the story ({{ref:Iliad 1.373-374}}).

## Outside Homer

Later suppliants carried a branch wound with wool. In Sophocles' *Oedipus the King*, Oedipus asks the Thebans gathered at his altars why they sit “wreathed with suppliant branches” (ἱκτηρίοις κλάδοισιν ἐξεστεμμένοι, line 3), with a verb from the same root (see [[supplication|supplication]]).
