Book 1 has two words for hair: κόμη, and χαίτη, the long flowing hair that is also a horse's mane. The epithet ἠΰκομος, “of the lovely hair”, is given to Leto ({{ref:Iliad 1.36}}).

At the height of the quarrel, as Achilles draws his [[sword|sword]], Athena stands behind him and seizes him by his fair hair (ξανθῆς κόμης), seen by him alone. A goddess checks the hero from behind, by the hair (see [[athena-restrains-achilles]]).

{{quote:602192bc-858b-55bf-822c-e34fcec2eef9}}

When Zeus grants Thetis' prayer, “the ambrosial locks of the lord flowed down from his immortal head”, and Olympus shakes (see [[ambrosia|ambrosia]] and [[brows|brows]]).

{{quote:640348fc-7fe1-56c7-a683-ce6bf661080e}}

Achilles' fair hair returns at Patroclus' funeral, when he cuts the lock he had grown for the river Spercheius and puts it in his dead friend's hands (Iliad 23.141–153).
