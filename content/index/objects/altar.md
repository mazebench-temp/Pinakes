βωμός first means a raised base: Poseidon sets Zeus's chariot on a stand (βωμοί) at Iliad 8.441. As an altar it is the built base on which offerings are burned, in the open air before a god's house or in a sacred grove.

In Book 1 the altar is Apollo's at Chryse. Odysseus leads [[Chryseis]] to it and places her in her father's arms, so that the girl is returned in the god's presence ({{ref:Iliad 1.440-441}}). Then the hecatomb is set in order around “the well-built altar” (ἐΰδμητον περὶ βωμόν), and the [[sacrifice|sacrifice]] begins ({{ref:Iliad 1.448}}; see [[return-of-chryseis]]).

## Outside Homer

Some altars grew from the ash of past sacrifices. Pausanias describes the great ash altar of Zeus at Olympia (5.13.8–11).
