κλισίη (from κλίνω, “lean, lie down”) is a place to lie down: in the camp before Troy, a leader's quarters. It is often translated “tent”, but it is a solid building. Achilles' hut in *Iliad* 24 is built of fir beams and roofed with thatch, with a fenced courtyard and a door-bar that took three men to work (Iliad 24.448–456).

In the Odyssey the κλισίη is also a herdsman's dwelling: the swineherd Eumaeus has a hut in his farmstead on Ithaca, and there he feeds and lodges the disguised Odysseus (*Odyssey* 14.45–51; see [[eumaeus]] and [[fold]]). Once the word is a chair (*Odyssey* 4.123; see [[throne]]).
