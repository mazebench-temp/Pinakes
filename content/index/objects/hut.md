κλισίη (from κλίνω, “lean, lie down”) is a place to lie down: in the camp before Troy, a leader's quarters. It is often translated “tent”, but it is a solid building. Achilles' hut in Book 24 is built of fir beams and roofed with thatch, with a fenced courtyard and a door-bar that took three men to work (Iliad 24.448–456).

The huts stand by the [[ships|ships]], and “huts and ships” together make the camp ({{ref:Iliad 1.306}}, {{ref:Iliad 1.328}}, {{ref:Iliad 1.487}}). The hut is also where the prize lives. Agamemnon threatens to come to Achilles' hut and take Briseis himself ({{ref:Iliad 1.185}}), then sends his [[heralds|heralds]] instead ({{ref:Iliad 1.322}}). They find Achilles sitting beside his hut and his black ship ({{ref:Iliad 1.329}}), and Patroclus leads Briseis out ({{ref:Iliad 1.346}}; see [[taking-of-briseis]]). Achilles tells Thetis how the heralds led her away “from my hut” ({{ref:Iliad 1.391}}).
