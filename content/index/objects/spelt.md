# ζειαί (Odyssey) and ὀλύραι (Iliad): Herodotus 2.36.2 makes them one grain.
grc: [ζειάς, ζειαί, ὀλύρας]
en: [spelt]

ζειά, usually in the plural ζειαί, is spelt or emmer, a kind of wheat. The Odyssey names it twice, in one book: the attendants of Menelaus throw spelt before the guests' horses and mix white barley with it, and spelt grows with wheat and barley on Menelaus' plain (*Odyssey* 4.41, 4.604). The Iliad has another word, ὄλυρα, in the verse of horses at their fodder: Pandarus' pairs stand in Lycaon's halls “munching white barley and spelt”, and the Trojan horses wait by their chariots for the dawn, munching the same (5.195–196, 8.564–565; see [[horses]] and [[barley]]). The translation renders both words “spelt”.

The grain is in the epithet of the plowland, ζείδωρος ἄρουρα, “the grain-giving earth”, from ζειά and δῶρον (see [[grain-giving]]).

## Outside Homer

Herodotus makes the two words one grain. Other men live on wheat and barley, he says, but the Egyptians think it the greatest disgrace to live on them and make their food from ὄλυρα, τὰς ζειὰς μετεξέτεροι καλέουσι, “which some call ζειαί” (*Histories* 2.36.2). They bake their bread from it, loaves that they call κυλλήστις, and drink a wine made from barley, since they have no vines (*Histories* 2.77.4; see [[wheat]] and [[herodotus]]).
