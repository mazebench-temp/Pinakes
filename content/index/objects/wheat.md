πυρός is wheat, the grain, usually in the plural, πυροί. Homer names it with barley (κριθαί; see [[barley]]): reapers drive their swaths “through a rich man's field / of wheat or barley” (11.68–69), and in the Odyssey the earth bears the two together, unsown in the land of the Cyclopes and in the land of a just king (*Odyssey* 9.108–111, 19.111–113, plain renderings). A land rich in wheat is πολύπυρος (see [[rich-in-wheat]]).

Wheat is food for men. In Odysseus' house twelve women grind at the mills, making barley meal and wheat meal, “the marrow of men”, and one of them is still grinding when the others have finished their wheat and gone to sleep (*Odyssey* 20.105–110, a plain rendering; see [[barley-meal]]). It is also fodder. Hector reminds his horses that Andromache set “honey-sweet wheat” before them, and mixed wine for them, before she served her husband (8.185–190), and the horses of Diomedes stand at the manger “eating honey-sweet wheat” (10.568–569; see [[horses]]). In Penelope's dream her twenty geese eat wheat from the water in the house, until an eagle from the mountain breaks their necks (*Odyssey* 19.535–540, a plain rendering).

## Outside Homer

Herodotus says that other men live on wheat and barley, but that for an Egyptian to live on them is the greatest disgrace: the Egyptians make their food from ὄλυρα, which some call ζειά (*Histories* 2.36.2; see [[spelt]] and [[herodotus]]).
