## In Homer

When Hecuba has brought the wine, Priam “urged the housekeeper, a handmaid, / to pour pure water over his hands; and she stood beside him, / the handmaid, holding a basin and a pitcher in her hands” (24.302–304; see [[handmaids]], [[hand-washing]] and [[libation]]). Then he washes, takes the cup from his wife and prays (24.305–307; see [[cup]] and [[prayer]]). The pitcher, πρόχοος, is named from προχέω, “pour forth”; the basin catches the water as it runs off the hands. The water is ἀκήρατον, “pure”: Walter Leaf takes the word in its usual sense, “inviolate, undefiled”, and not as “unmixed”, a sense that came from confusion with ἄκρητος (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 24.303).

In the Odyssey the same service has its own verses, said whenever a guest sits down to eat: “And a handmaid brought water for the hands in a pitcher and poured it, / a fine golden one, over a silver basin, / for them to wash” (*Odyssey* 1.136–138, and often; see [[water-for-the-hands]]). There the basin is a λέβης (see [[cauldron]]), and the water itself is χέρνιψ; at Nestor's sacrifice his son brings it “in a flowered basin” (*Odyssey* 3.440). The pitcher serves wine as well: when Eurymachus hurls a footstool at Odysseus, it strikes the wine-pourer's right hand, and the pitcher falls ringing to the ground (*Odyssey* 18.396–397).

## The word χέρνιβον

χέρνιβον, the basin, is found nowhere else in Greek. Leaf quotes the scholia: the verse was athetized because the poet elsewhere calls the vessel that receives the water λέβης and the water poured over the hands χέρνιψ, and some marked it only as a word said once. Apart from Aristarchus' remark that “we” call the vessel so, there is no other evidence for the word, though χερνιβεῖον occurs rarely. Bentley conjectured χέρνιβά τʼ, “water and pitcher”, but the sense needs a basin and a pitcher; the Massaliote edition seems to have read χέρνιβα ἀμφίπολος ταμίη μετὰ χερσὶν ἔχουσα, “a handmaid, the housekeeper, holding water for the hands”, more in keeping with usage (on 24.304; see the translation's note on 24.304).
