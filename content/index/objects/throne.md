The θρόνος is a high seat of honor, often with a footstool (θρῆνυς; see [[footstool]]). The gods sit on thrones, and their furniture is golden, made by [[Hephaestus]]: Hera is χρυσόθρονος, “of the golden throne” (Iliad 1.611; see [[gold-and-silver]]), and she promises Sleep a golden throne of Hephaestus' making, with a footstool (Iliad 14.238–241). When Zeus comes home, the gods rise from their seats to meet him, and he sits down on his throne (Iliad 1.533–536; see [[rising-for-zeus|rising for Zeus]]).

Among men the θρόνος is the chair of state and the seat of an honored guest, set higher than the κλισμός, a chair with a sloping back. Achilles seats the envoys on chairs with purple rugs (Iliad 9.200), and in the Odyssey the guest is given a θρόνος with a footstool while his host takes a κλισμός beside him (Odyssey 1.130–132). In the Odyssey both words are rendered “chair”, and the pair κατὰ κλισμούς τε θρόνους τε is “on the chairs and the seats” (see the translation's row κλισμός, θρόνος).
