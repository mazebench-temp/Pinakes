The χιτών (“tunic”) was the garment worn next to the skin. A man got into it like a shirt (ἐνδύνω, “put on”) and wore a [[cloak]] over it; in war it went under the [[corselet]]. Its epithets praise the cloth: soft (μαλακός), fine (καλός), shining (σιγαλόεις), well-spun (ἐΰννητος). The word is usually derived from a Semitic word for linen (compare Hebrew *kuttonet*, “tunic”), and the Homeric tunic was probably of linen. The Ionic form is κιθών.

The word reaches beyond cloth. The Achaeans are χαλκοχίτωνες, men “with tunics of bronze”, that is, in bronze armor (see [[bronze-armored]]). And Hector tells Paris that if the Trojans were not so timid he would long ago have “put on a tunic of stone” (λάϊνον ἕσσο χιτῶνα, 3.57): he would have been stoned to death for the harm he has done.

## Elsewhere in Homer

When Agamemnon cannot sleep in Book 10, he dresses in the order of Book 2: tunic, sandals, and over them the skin of a great tawny lion in place of a cloak (10.21–24). Nestor, woken by him, puts on tunic and sandals and pins on a crimson cloak (10.131–134). Athena, arming for war, lets her own embroidered robe fall on her father's floor and puts on the tunic of Zeus (5.733–737).

Tunics are gifts and ransom. Priam's ransom for Hector includes twelve tunics, with robes, cloaks and rugs (24.229–231). Achilles' men leave two mantles and a well-spun tunic out of it, and in them Hector's washed and anointed body is wrapped to be carried home (24.580–588).

In the Odyssey, the disguised Odysseus proves to Penelope that he has seen her husband by describing his clothes: a purple woolen cloak with a golden brooch, and a tunic that shone like the skin of a dried onion (*Odyssey* 19.225–235).

## Outside Homer

Herodotus (5.87–88) tells how the Athenians made their women give up their Dorian dress, fastened with long pins, for the linen tunic (κιθών) of the Ionians, which needs none. The women had used their pins to stab to death the only man to come home from a disastrous expedition against Aegina. The dress called Ionian, Herodotus adds, was in origin Carian.
