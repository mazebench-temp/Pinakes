---
title: The voice of the shuttle
greek: ἡ τῆς κερκίδος φωνή
kind: object
summary: Sophocles' phrase in the Tereus for the woven robe on which Philomela, her tongue cut out, told the story of her rape; the web speaks for her. Aristotle counts it a recognition contrived by the poet.
aliases: [the shuttle's voice]
---

The shuttle (κερκίς) is the rod that carries the weft through the warp on the upright loom (see [[loom]] and [[weaving]]); in the Iliad Andromache drops hers when she hears the wailing for Hector (22.448). Philomela, mute, wove her story into a robe and sent it to her sister Procne (see [[procne-and-philomela]] and [[tereus-play]]). The critic Geoffrey Hartman took the phrase as the title of an essay on the language of literature (1969), and from him it passed to much modern writing on poetic speech.
