The φαρέτρη is the case for arrows, carried on the shoulder. Apollo comes down from Olympus “with his bow on his shoulders and his close-covered quiver” (ἀμφηρεφέα φαρέτρην), and as he moves the [[arrows|arrows]] clatter inside it. ἀμφηρεφής, “covered all round”, means a quiver with a lid. Pandarus takes the lid (πῶμα) off his quiver before he shoots (Iliad 4.116), and the Cyclops closes his cave with its door-stone “as one puts the lid on a quiver” (Odyssey 9.314).
