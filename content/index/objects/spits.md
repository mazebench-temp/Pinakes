After the god's portion has burned and the entrails have been tasted, the rest of the meat is cut up, threaded on spits, roasted with care and drawn off ({{ref:Iliad 1.465-466}}). The meat of the victims becomes the [[feast|feast]] (see [[sacrifice|sacrifice]]).

{{notes}}

## Outside Homer

Bundles of iron spits were dedicated in early Greek sanctuaries, such as the Heraion at Argos. The coin names *obol* (ὀβολός, a form of ὀβελός) and *drachma* (“handful”, of six obols) were explained in antiquity as memories of a time when spits served as money (Plutarch, *Lysander* 17).
