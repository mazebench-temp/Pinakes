## In Homer

Grinding is the work of slave women, and in Homer the mill belongs to the great houses. In the house of Alcinous, of the fifty slave women, “some grind the yellow grain at the mills, / and others weave at the looms and turn the yarn / as they sit” (*Odyssey* 7.104–106; see [[the-house-of-alcinous]], [[handmaids]] and [[loom]]). In the house of Odysseus twelve women toil at the mills, making barley meal and wheat flour, “the marrow of men”; on the morning of the day of the bow the weakest of them, still grinding when the others have finished their wheat and gone to sleep, hears Zeus thunder from a cloudless sky, stops her mill and prays that the suitors may take their last meal that day, for they have loosened her knees with heartbreaking toil as she made them barley meal (*Odyssey* 20.105–119, a plain rendering; see [[barley-meal]] and [[the-suitors]]). The meal for Telemachus' voyage is “the meal of mill-ground barley”, μυληφάτου ἀλφίτου ἀκτῆς (*Odyssey* 2.355; see [[provisions]]); μυληφάτος is “mill-crushed”, from the root of φένω, “slay” (W. W. Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 2.355).

The Iliad names no mill, only its stone: the stone with which Ajax breaks Hector's shield is “like a millstone”, μυλοειδέϊ πέτρῳ (*Iliad* 7.270; see [[like-a-millstone]]), and in the battle at the wall the helmets and the bossed shields ring as men are struck μυλάκεσσι, “with stones like millstones” (*Iliad* 12.161).

## Outside Homer

Plutarch, asking why the priest of Jupiter at Rome might not touch meal or leaven, suggested that grinding destroys the nature of the grain, and that the poet therefore called barley meal μυλήφατος by a metaphor, as slain in the mill (*Roman Questions* 109, as Merry quotes it on *Odyssey* 2.355). The Suda explains μύλη as the lower part of the mill, “for the upper is called the ass”, ὄνος (μ 1408). Among the metaphors that Aristotle collects in the *Rhetoric* is the saying of Cephisodotus, who called the triremes “painted mills” (*Rhetoric* 1411a23–24; see [[cephisodotus]] and [[metaphora]]).
