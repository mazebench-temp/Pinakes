ἱστός (from ἵστημι, “set up”) is “the upright”: the mast of a ship, and also the upright [[loom|loom]], which is what the same word means at {{ref:Iliad 1.31}}. A Homeric ship stepped and unstepped its mast. At Chryse the crew lower the mast by the [[forestays|forestays]] into the [[mast-crutch|mast-crutch]] and row in; leaving, they set it up again and spread the [[sails|sails]].

In the Odyssey the mast is of fir, set up in its box amidships and made fast with the forestays (Odyssey 2.424–425).
