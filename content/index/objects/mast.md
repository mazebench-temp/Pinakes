ἱστός (from ἵστημι, “set up”) is “the upright”: the mast of a ship, and also the upright [[loom|loom]], which is what the same word means at {{ref:Iliad 1.31}}. A Homeric ship stepped and unstepped its mast: coming into harbor, the crew lowered it by the forestays into its crutch and rowed in, and leaving, they set it up again and spread the sails (1.433–435, 1.480–481). In the Odyssey the mast is of fir, set up in its box amidships and made fast with the forestays (Odyssey 2.424–425).
