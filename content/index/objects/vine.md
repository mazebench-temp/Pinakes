ἡμερίς is a vine, and Homer names it so only once. The word is the feminine of ἥμερος, “tame”, and W. W. Merry explains that it means only a vine “brought into cultivation from the wild state”; he notes that Theophrastus gives the same name to an oak that bears sweet acorns (*Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 5.69). The translation renders it “garden vine”. The ordinary word for the vine in Homer is ἄμπελος, and the vine-growing towns of the Catalogue are ἀμπελόεις, “vine-rich” (see [[vine-rich]] and [[rich-in-grapes]]).

## In Homer

On Calypso's island the vine grows over the cave itself. Around the cave there is a wood of alder, poplar and fragrant cypress, where the long-winged birds roost (*Odyssey* 5.63–67; see [[trees]] and [[birds]]), “And there, stretched about the hollow cave, / was a garden vine in its prime, and it flourished with clusters of grapes” (*Odyssey* 5.68–69). Merry marks the new feature: the trees stand around the cave, but the vine climbs upon it and hangs over its edge, a different kind of growth, and τετάνυστο is “trailed” (on 5.68). Four springs flow there in a row, turned this way and that, and soft meadows of violets and parsley bloom all around, so that even an immortal would gaze at the sight (*Odyssey* 5.70–74; see [[violet]], [[parsley]] and [[calypsos-cave]]). The garden vine is the one cultivated thing among the wild growth of the island, and Hermes, coming to the cave with Zeus' command, stands and gazes at it all (*Odyssey* 5.75–76; see [[hermes]]).

The vineyard on the Shield of Achilles is a vineyard of gold heavy with clusters of black grapes, gathered in baskets by girls and young men while a boy sings the Linus song (*Iliad* 18.561–572; see [[the-vintage]]). In the Odyssey vines grow unsown and unplowed in the land of the Cyclopes, bearing wine of great clusters, and the rain of Zeus makes them grow (*Odyssey* 9.108–111, a plain rendering); in the orchard of Alcinous cluster grows upon cluster, and some grapes are drying in the sun while others are gathered and trodden (*Odyssey* 7.121–126, a plain rendering); and in Laertes' orchard no vine is without care (*Odyssey* 24.245–247, a plain rendering; see [[olive]]).
