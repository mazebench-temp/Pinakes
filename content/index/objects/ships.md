νηῦς (Attic ναῦς) is the ordinary word for a ship. The ships have standing epithets: swift (θοός), hollow (κοῖλος), black (μέλας, probably from the pitch on their hulls), curved (κορωνίς), balanced (ἐΐση), swift-sailing (ὠκύπορος) and seafaring (ποντοπόρος).

{{notes}}

## Outside Homer

Thucydides (1.10.4) used the Catalogue of Ships to estimate the size of the expedition: Homer, he says, makes the fleet twelve hundred ships, with a hundred and twenty men in each Boeotian ship and fifty in each of Philoctetes'.
