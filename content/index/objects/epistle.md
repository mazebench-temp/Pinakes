title: Letter (epistle)
greek: ἐπιστολή
kind: object
summary: The written letter that Iphigenia, among the Taurians, wants to send to Argos, and which reveals her to her brother Orestes; for Aristotle the model of a recognition that arises from the incidents themselves. Homer knows writing only once, in the folded tablet that Bellerophon carries.
aliases: [letters, Iphigenia's letter]
---

Euripides makes a letter the hinge of his plot. Iphigenia offers to free one of the two strangers if he will carry it to Argos, and reads it aloud in case it should be lost, so that Orestes hears his own name (*Iphigenia among the Taurians* 725–797; see [[the-recognition-of-orestes-and-iphigenia]]). The one piece of writing in Homer is the folded tablet with “grim signs” that Proetus gives Bellerophon to carry to his father-in-law in Lycia, asking that the bearer be killed (6.167–170; see [[bellerophons-letter]], [[writing]] and [[bellerophon-in-lycia]]).
