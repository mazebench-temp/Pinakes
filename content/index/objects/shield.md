Homer has two words for the shield, ἀσπίς and σάκος, neither with a certain etymology. They are not strictly distinguished: Thetis asks Hephaestus for an ἀσπίς for her son (18.458), and he makes a σάκος (18.478). But σάκος is the usual word for the greatest shields. Ajax carries a σάκος “like a tower”, of seven oxhides with an eighth layer of bronze, the work of Tychius, the best of leather-workers, who lived at [[hyle|Hyle]] (7.219–223). An ἀσπίς is often “equal on every side” (πάντοσʼ ἐΐση, for example 11.61), that is, round, and “bossed” (ὀμφαλόεσσα, for example 4.448).

The shield hung from a strap (τελαμών) that crossed the chest and went over the shoulder. The largest covered a man from head to foot, and are called ἀμφιβρότη, “man-covering” (2.389, 11.32). As Hector walks back to the city, the dark leather rim of his bossed shield knocks against his ankles and his neck (6.117–118).

Archaeology offers parallels for both kinds. Tall “tower” shields and shields shaped like a figure eight, made of hide on a frame, appear in early Mycenaean art, of the sixteenth and fifteenth centuries BC; smaller round shields became common later. Whether the poems keep memories of different ages side by side, or describe the arms of a single period, is disputed.

## Elsewhere in Homer

In the arming scenes the shield is taken up after the sword, in the same words for Paris, Patroclus and Achilles: “and then the great and heavy shield” (αὐτὰρ ἔπειτα σάκος μέγα τε στιβαρόν τε, 3.335, 16.136, 19.373). Agamemnon's is an ἀσπίς, “the furious shield, man-covering, intricately worked” (11.32). The shield Hephaestus makes for Achilles, with the earth, the sky and the sea, two cities and the work of the seasons on it, is described over some 130 lines (18.478–608).

## Outside Homer

Archilochus boasts that a Thracian now glories in the shield he left behind by a bush, and that he will get another no worse (fragment 5 West). The *Shield of Heracles*, ascribed to Hesiod, describes the hero's shield in the manner of Iliad 18 (lines 139–320).
