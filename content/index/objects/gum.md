---
title: Gum
greek: κόμμι
kind: object
summary: Gum, the resin of the acacia, a word borrowed from Egyptian; for Aristotle one of the only three Greek nouns that end in iota.
aliases: [kommi, gum arabic]
---

Herodotus says that the Egyptian embalmers wrapped the body in linen bandages smeared with gum, “which the Egyptians generally use instead of glue” (2.86). The word came from Egypt with the substance, and passed through Latin *gummi* into the modern languages. Aristotle lists κόμμι with μέλι and πέπερι as the only nouns ending in iota (1458a15–16; see [[honey]], [[pepper]] and [[gender-of-nouns]]).
