The aegis (αἰγίς) belongs to [[Zeus]], whose standing epithet αἰγίοχος (αἰγίς + ἔχω, “holding the aegis”) is named from it (see [[aegis-bearing]]). The name is usually taken from αἴξ, “goat”, as if the aegis were a goatskin; others connect it with καταιγίς, “squall”, and see a storm-cloud. Homer never says what it is made of.

{{notes}}

## Elsewhere in Homer

The smith [[Hephaestus]] gave it to Zeus to carry “to put men to flight” (Iliad 15.308–310). Rout, Strife, Courage and Onslaught are set on it with the Gorgon's head (Iliad 5.738–742). Zeus shakes it to raise a storm and send panic among men (Iliad 17.593–596), and Athena and Apollo carry it into battle.

## Outside Homer

Herodotus (4.189) thought that the Greeks took the dress and aegis of Athena's statues from the Libyan women, who wore goatskins (αἰγέαι) fringed with tassels and dyed red. In Archaic and Classical art Athena wears the aegis as a scaly cape edged with snakes, often with the Gorgon's head at its center.
