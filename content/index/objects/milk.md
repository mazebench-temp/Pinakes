## The word

γάλα, genitive γάλακτος, is milk; γλάγος is an older form, used by the Iliad in the formula of the spring “when the milk wets the pails” (2.471, 16.643). The standing phrase is γάλα λευκόν, “white milk” (4.434, 5.902).

## In Homer

The Iliad's milk belongs to its similes of the countryside. The Trojans clamor like the countless ewes of a rich man “that stand in their thousands while they are milked of their white milk, / bleating ceaselessly as they hear the voices of their lambs” (4.433–435; see [[sheep-in-the-fold]]). The Achaean army is as many as the flies that buzz around a shepherd's steading “in the springtime, when the milk wets the pails”, and the fighters throng around the body of Sarpedon as flies around “the buckets brimming with milk” (2.469–471, 16.641–643; see [[flies-around-the-milk-pails]]). Paeëon heals the wound of Ares as quickly as fig juice, stirred in, curdles white milk (5.902–904; see [[fig-juice]] and [[cheese]]). When Zeus turns his eyes away from Troy, he looks on the land of the “proud mare-milkers / who live on milk”, the γλακτοφάγοι (13.4–6; see [[abii]] and [[strabo-on-the-mare-milkers]]).

In the Odyssey Menelaus tells of Libya, where “neither master nor shepherd has any lack / of cheese or meat or sweet milk, / but the flocks always yield unfailing milk to drink” (*Odyssey* 4.87–89), and the root of moly that Hermes gives Odysseus is black, its flower “like milk” (*Odyssey* 10.304).

## Outside Homer

Aristotle takes milk twice for his examples. If someone says that a woman has given birth because she has milk, the sign is necessary: of signs this alone is a τεκμήριον, “for it alone, if it is true, cannot be refuted” (*Rhetoric* 1357b14–17; see [[tekmerion]]). And of epithets, that in poetry it is appropriate to say “white milk”, while in prose some epithets are rather inappropriate (*Rhetoric* 1406a11–13). In Euripides' *Cyclops* the Cyclopes live on milk, cheeses and the flesh of sheep, and Silenus has cow's milk to offer (*Cyclops* 121–122, 136); the Cyclops boasts that he drinks off a jar of milk on top of his meat, and before his meal of two companions he fills a mixing bowl with milk (*Cyclops* 325–327, 388–389; see [[euripides]]).
