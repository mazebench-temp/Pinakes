κνημίς is named from κνήμη, “shin”: the greaves go “around the shins” (περὶ κνήμῃσιν). They come first in every arming scene, in a pair of verses that recur: “First he put the greaves around his shins, fine ones, fitted with silver ankle-pieces” (κνημῖδας μὲν πρῶτα περὶ κνήμῃσιν ἔθηκε / καλάς, ἀργυρέοισιν ἐπισφυρίοις ἀραρυίας, 3.330–331; see [[arming-scene]]). The ἐπισφύρια (from σφυρόν, “ankle”) were fittings at the ankle, clasps or guards; their exact form is not known.

The Achaeans are ἐϋκνήμιδες, “well-greaved” ([[well-greaved]]), and Book 3 uses the epithet six times.

## Elsewhere in Homer

Hephaestus makes Achilles' new greaves of pliant tin (ἑανοῦ κασσιτέροιο, 18.613). Old Laertes, working in his orchard, wears greaves of stitched oxhide against the thorns (Odyssey 24.228–229).

## Outside Homer

Bronze greaves were worn by warriors of the Late Bronze Age, and again, shaped to the leg, by the hoplites of the Archaic and Classical periods.
