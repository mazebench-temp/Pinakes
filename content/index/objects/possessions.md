κτῆμα, from κτάομαι, “acquire”, is a thing gained and owned; κτήματα are a man's goods, above all the treasure kept in his house.

Paris did not take Helen alone. Book 3 names her possessions with her in nearly every statement of the stakes; only Iris, speaking to Helen, leaves them out (3.138). The two men are to fight “for Helen and all her possessions” (ἀμφʼ Ἑλένῃ καὶ κτήμασι πᾶσι, 3.70, 3.91), and the winner is to “take all the possessions and the woman, and lead them home” (3.72, 3.93). The oath repeats the pair (3.282, 3.285), and Agamemnon ends the book by demanding “Argive Helen and the possessions with her” (3.458). See [[single-combat]] and [[oath]].

## Elsewhere in Homer

After the duel of Book 7 Antenor proposes to give back Helen and the possessions. Paris will not give up Helen, but offers to return what he brought from Argos and to add more of his own, and the Achaeans refuse (7.345–411). Menelaus reproaches the Trojans for carrying off his wedded wife and many possessions (13.626–627), and Hector, waiting for Achilles, thinks of offering Helen and all that Alexander brought to Troy in his ships (22.111–118).

## Outside Homer

The *Cypria*, in the summary of [[proclus]], told how Paris and Helen put most of the possessions aboard and sailed away by night. In Herodotus' version the king of Egypt kept both Helen and the stolen goods for Menelaus (2.114–115).
