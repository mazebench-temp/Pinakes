## The word

κληΐς is a thing that shuts or holds. Of a door it is the key, as when Hecuba is to “open with the key the doors of the sacred house” (6.89), or the bolt that fastens the gate of the Achaean wall (12.456; see [[door]]); of the body it is the collarbone (5.146, 22.324). On a ship the plural names the rowers' places. Achilles' ships each carried “fifty men, companions, at the rowing benches” (16.170), and in the Odyssey the crew sit down at them when they go on board, and from them they row: Odysseus bids his men strike the surf of the sea with their oars, κληίδεσσιν ἐφήμενοι, “sitting at the benches” (*Odyssey* 12.214–215). But when the Phaeacian youths have launched the ship that is to take Odysseus home, they are told to bind the oars well ἐπὶ κληῖσιν and go ashore (*Odyssey* 8.34–38), a use that suits the pins to which oars were tied rather than the benches. Which is meant is disputed, as it is for πολυκλήϊς, “many-benched” (see [[many-benched]]). The translation renders the word “rowing benches”.

## The verse of boarding

The Odyssey gives the crew's boarding its own verse, οἱ δʼ αἶψʼ εἴσβαινον καὶ ἐπὶ κληῖσι καθῖζον, “and quickly they went on board and sat down at the benches” (*Odyssey* 9.103, 9.179, 9.471, 9.563, 11.638, 12.146, 15.549; with αἶψα δʼ ἄρʼ, 15.221). Before it the captain bids the men go on board and loose the [[stern-cables]] (*Odyssey* 9.178, 9.562, 11.637, 12.145, 15.548), and when the ship is to be rowed it is followed by ἑξῆς δʼ ἑζόμενοι πολιὴν ἅλα τύπτον ἐρετμοῖς, “and sitting in order they struck the gray sea with their oars” (*Odyssey* 4.580, 9.104, 9.180, 9.472, 9.564, 12.147; see [[oars]]). The renderings of the Odyssey's later books are plain renderings, not the translation.
