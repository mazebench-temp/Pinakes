## The word

φάρμακον is any herb, salve or potion that changes the body or the mind, and Homer tells which kind by its epithet. The healer's drugs are “soothing” and “pain-killing” (*Iliad* 4.218, 5.401; see [[healing]], [[soothing-drugs]] and [[pain-killing]]), and Agamede “knew all the drugs that the wide earth nourishes” (*Iliad* 11.741; see [[agamede]]). A serpent waiting at its hole has “fed on evil drugs” (*Iliad* 22.94; see [[the-serpent-at-its-hole]]). In the Odyssey Odysseus once sought “a man-slaying drug” for his arrows (*Odyssey* 1.261; see [[odysseus-seeks-poison]]), and the suitors fear that Telemachus may bring “drugs that destroy life” from Ephyra and put them in the mixing bowl (*Odyssey* 2.329–330). Helen's “cunning drugs” came from Egypt, where the earth bears “many good and many grim, growing intermixed” (*Odyssey* 4.227–230; see [[nepenthe]]). Circe's are “evil drugs” and “grim drugs”, Hermes' is “this good drug”, and the drug that made the companions swine is “the ruinous drug” (*Odyssey* 10.213, 10.236, 10.287, 10.394).

## Related words

πολυφάρμακος, “with many drugs”, “skilled in many drugs”, is said of the healers of the camp and of Circe (*Iliad* 16.28; *Odyssey* 10.276; see [[with-many-drugs]]). φαρμάσσω is to temper iron, as the smith of the simile of the blinding does when he plunges an axe into cold water (*Odyssey* 9.393; see [[the-smith-tempering-an-axe]]).

## Outside Homer

In Ovid's *Metamorphoses* Macareus tells how Circe had roasted barley mixed with honey, strong wine and curdled milk, and added juices to lie hidden under the sweetness (*Metamorphoses* 14.273–276; see [[kykeon]]); when she gives the companions back their shape, they are sprinkled with the juices of a better herb and struck on the head with her wand reversed, while she sings words contrary to her first ones (*Metamorphoses* 14.299–301; see [[wand]]).
