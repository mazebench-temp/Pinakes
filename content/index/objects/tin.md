Tin came from far away, by sea routes from the west; later Greeks placed its source in the Tin Islands, the Cassiterides, of which Herodotus says he knows nothing (3.115). Homer uses it as an ornament. Agamemnon's breastplate has ten bands of dark kyanos, twelve of gold and twenty of tin (11.24–25), and his shield twenty bosses of tin (11.34; see [[the-aristeia-of-agamemnon]] and [[kyanos]]). Achilles' new shield has two layers of tin (20.271; see [[the-bronze-spear-stopped]]) and his greaves are of pliant tin (18.613); a greave “of newly wrought tin” (21.592) is one of the problems of the *Poetics* (see [[aristotle-wine-of-the-gods]], [[greaves]] and [[bronze]]).
