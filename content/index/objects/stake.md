## The word

μοχλός is a bar or pole of wood used to move a weight. Odysseus launches his finished raft “with levers” (μοχλοῖσιν, *Odyssey* 5.261; see [[raft]]), and the Trojans storming the Achaean wall “pried at the jutting buttresses” with them (στήλας … ἐμόχλεον, 12.259; see [[the-achaean-wall]]). In the Cyclops' cave the word names the stake that Odysseus shapes from the giant's club (see [[mace]]). The translation renders it “stake” there and “levers” for the raft (see the translation's row ῥόπαλον, μοχλός).

## Outside Homer

In Euripides' *Cyclops* the club becomes a branch. Odysseus tells the chorus of satyrs that there is “a branch of olive” in the Cyclops' house, which he will sharpen at the point with his sword, put into the fire, and, when he sees it burning, thrust hot into the middle of the Cyclops' eye (*Cyclops* 455–459); he will twirl it as a shipwright works a drill with a double strap, the Odyssey's own simile (*Cyclops* 460–462; *Odyssey* 9.384–386), and the play calls it a δαλός, a “brand” (*Cyclops* 471–472, 593, 630; see [[euripides]]). W. W. Merry thought the Homeric picture spoiled by the substitution of a branch for the club (*Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 9.326).
