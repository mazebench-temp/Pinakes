The σκῆπτρον is a wooden staff carried as a sign of standing: by priests, by kings, by elders sitting in judgment, by heralds, and by whoever has the floor in an assembly. The word belongs with σκήπτομαι (“lean on”), and a σκῆπτρον can still be a plain walking stick: Athena gives one to Odysseus with his beggar's disguise (Odyssey 13.437).

## In Book 1

**The priest's staff.** [[Chryses]] comes to the ships holding the [[fillets|fillets]] of Apollo “upon a golden scepter”. The woolen bands hang from the staff, and its gold marks it as the god's (see [[gold-and-silver]]). Together they declare him Apollo's priest and put his plea under Apollo's protection. Agamemnon's threat is aimed at exactly that protection. Achilles repeats the description when he tells the story to [[Thetis]] ({{ref:Iliad 1.373-374}}).

**The judges' staff.** In the assembly [[Achilles]] swears by the scepter in his hand, and describes it as he swears. It was cut in the mountains and stripped by the bronze of leaves and bark, and it will never sprout again. Now the Achaeans carry it when they give judgment (δικασπόλοι), “those who guard the ordinances that come from Zeus” (see [[themis]]). The dead wood is the guarantee of the [[oath|oath]]: as surely as it will never grow leaves again, the Achaeans will one day long for Achilles (see [[longing-for-achilles]]). Then he throws it to the ground, “studded with golden nails”, and sits down.

{{quote:4b6b4160-bde4-5709-8bec-4e9b312f7fd2}}

The staff that stands for the community's justice is thrown at its feet. Telemachus makes the same gesture, in the same half-line, when he gives up on the assembly of Ithaca (Odyssey 2.80).

**The king's staff.** [[Nestor]] answers that no ordinary share of honor falls to a σκηπτοῦχος βασιλεύς, a “scepter-bearing king, to whom Zeus has given glory”. The staff Achilles has just thrown down stands for an authority that comes from [[Zeus]], and that is what he refuses to grant [[Agamemnon]] (see [[basileus]] and [[kingship-and-authority]]).

{{quote:56007caf-8888-557e-8716-089111f36532}}

Agamemnon's own scepter does not appear in Book 1. Its history is told at Iliad 2.100–108: [[Hephaestus]] made it and gave it to Zeus, and it passed from Zeus to Hermes, to Pelops, to [[Atreus]], to Thyestes, and last to Agamemnon. Achilles swears by a stripped branch; Agamemnon rules with the work of a god.

## Elsewhere in Homer

A herald puts the scepter into the hand of the man who is about to speak (Iliad 23.567–568; Odyssey 2.37–38). The elders who judge a case on the Shield of Achilles hold heralds' scepters as they speak (Iliad 18.505). Hector swears an oath to Dolon on a scepter (Iliad 10.321–331). Odysseus beats Thersites with Agamemnon's (Iliad 2.265–266).

## Outside Homer

[[Aristotle]], describing the kings of the heroic age, says that they judged cases, some under oath and some not, and that their oath was the raising of the scepter (*Politics* 3.14, 1285b). Pausanias (9.40.11–12) reports that the people of Chaeronea honored above all gods a staff they took to be the scepter Homer says Hephaestus made for Zeus. They called it “the Spear”, kept it in no temple but in the house of that year's priest, and offered sacrifices to it every day.
