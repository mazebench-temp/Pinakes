When the ship returns from Chryse, the crew draw it up on the land, high on the sands, and set long props beneath it ({{ref:Iliad 1.485-486}}). The ἕρματα are timbers or stones set under the hull to keep a beached ship upright and off the sand. When the army rushes for home in Book 2, the men pull the props from under the ships (Iliad 2.154). The word can mean any support: Sarpedon was a “prop” (ἕρμα) of Troy (Iliad 16.549).
