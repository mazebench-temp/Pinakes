In Homer μάχαιρα is a knife, not a weapon of war; later Greek used the word for a cutting sword. Agamemnon's knife “always hung beside the great sheath of his sword” (ἥ οἱ πὰρ ξίφεος μέγα κουλεόν αἰὲν ἄωρτο):

{{quote:51665e87-cf2d-5fef-8846-1aa289798cdf}}

At the oath of Book 3 he cuts hair from the lambs' heads with it, and then their throats, “with the pitiless bronze” (3.292; see [[oath]], [[sheath]] and [[pitiless-bronze]]). The same two verses bring out the knife when he swears over a boar in Book 19 (19.252–253).

## Elsewhere in Homer

Patroclus uses a knife to cut the arrow from Eurypylus' thigh (11.844). On the shield of Achilles the young men who dance wear golden knives hanging from silver belts (18.597–598).
