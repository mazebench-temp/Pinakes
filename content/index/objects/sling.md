## The word

σφενδόνη occurs only once in Homer, and it explains the words before it: Agenor binds Helenus' hand “with well-twisted sheep's wool, / a sling” (ἐϋστρεφεῖ οἰὸς ἀώτῳ / σφενδόνῃ, 13.599–600). The sling is a band of twisted wool. ἄωτος is the finest part of wool or flax, the fleece of a sheep (Od. 1.443, 9.434) or “the fine nap of linen” on the bed spread for Phoenix (9.661). ἐϋστρεφής, “well-twisted”, is said elsewhere of a bowstring (15.463), a rope (Od. 10.167, 14.346), withies (Od. 9.427) and the sheep-gut string of a lyre (Od. 21.408). The same three words describe the Locrians' slings (13.716), where the word σφενδόνη is not used and the wool alone stands for the weapon. See [[sheep]].

## In Homer

**A bandage.** Menelaus' spear has gone through Helenus' hand into his bow, and Helenus draws back with the ash spear trailing from it (13.593–597). Agenor draws the spear out and binds the hand:

{{quote:efe93ee4-f19a-5629-930e-1451f80a9893}}

The sling is not used as a weapon here. A θεράπων, an attendant, carries it for “the shepherd of the people” (13.600), as Pandion carries Teucer's bow (12.372); the words do not make clear whether the lord is Agenor or Helenus (see [[helenus]], [[agenor]] and [[healing]]).

**The Locrians.** Where Hector presses hardest, by the ships of Ajax and Protesilaus, the Locrians do not keep with their leader, the son of Oileus, in the close fight:

{{quote:58fd12c7-b858-579e-a165-062a7ef588b6}}

They are without the arms of the close fight, the bronze helmet, the round shield and the ash spear, and they fight from behind the men who have them, shooting “thick and fast”, so that the Trojans remember nothing of the joy of battle (see [[locrians]], [[ajax-son-of-oileus]] and [[bow]]). So Teucer shot from under the shield of Ajax in Book 8 (8.266–272; see [[the-shield-of-ajax]]). The Catalogue gives their leader a linen corselet (λινοθώρηξ, 2.529), armor as light as theirs. Nowhere else in the poems is a sling used; elsewhere stones are thrown by hand (see [[stone-throwing]]).

## Outside Homer

Strabo, following Ephorus, tells how the Aetolians under Oxylus came back to Elis with the sons of Heracles and the two armies agreed to a single combat. The Epeian Degmenus came out light-armed with a bow, sure of beating a man in armor from a distance; the Aetolian Pyraechmes, seeing the trick, came with a sling and a bag of stones. The sling, newly invented by the Aetolians, carried farther than the bow, and Degmenus fell (*Geography* 8.3.33). Strabo also quotes Pindar on Phoenix, “who led a bold throng of Dolopians to sling, a help to the horse-taming Danaans with their missiles”, and argues that Homer's silence about Phoenix's men must be filled in from such poets (9.5.5). The Balearic islanders, Strabo says, are reputed the best slingers. They went into battle with three slings around the head, for long, middle and short shots, and were trained from childhood: a child was given his bread only when he had hit it with the sling (3.5.1; see [[strabo]]).
