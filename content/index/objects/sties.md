## The word

συφεός, the pen of the σῦς, “swine”, is a word of the Odyssey alone. Circe's companions are also kept πυκινοὺς κευθμῶνας, in “close-packed sties” (*Odyssey* 10.283), with κευθμών, “hiding place”, the word of the lairs out of which the sea beasts frolic beneath Poseidon as he drives over the waves (*Iliad* 13.27–28) and of the recesses of the cave of the nymphs (*Odyssey* 13.367; see [[the-cave-of-the-naiads]]). The translation keeps “sties” for both words (see its row ῥάβδος, συφεοί). In his farmstead on Ithaca the swineherd Eumaeus built twelve sties within the fold (*Odyssey* 14.13–16; see [[eumaeus]] and [[fold]]).
