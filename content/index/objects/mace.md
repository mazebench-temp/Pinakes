## Meaning

κορύνη is a club or mace, a staff with a heavy head. The word has been connected with κόρυς, “helmet”, and κορυφή, “crown, top”, as a stick with a knob at its end, but the connection is not certain. κορυνήτης, “mace-man”, is formed from it as αἰχμητής, “spearman”, is formed from αἰχμή, “spear-point” (see [[spearmen]]). Both words occur in Homer only in Book 7: κορυνήτης twice, as the byname of Areithous (7.9, 7.138), and κορύνη twice, in the story of his death (7.141, 7.143).

Homer's other word for a club, ῥόπαλον, is a staff or cudgel, not a weapon of war. Boys break their sticks on a stubborn donkey in a simile for Ajax (11.558–562), the Cyclops keeps a great club of green olive wood in his cave (Od. 9.319–320), and in the underworld the hunter Orion holds a club of solid bronze (Od. 11.572–575).

## In Homer

Nestor tells of the armor that Ereuthalion wore when he challenged the Pylians, and of the man who first owned it:

{{quote:42b799cc-0c27-59e7-8f10-82dd159a81e6}}

The byname was a distinction, used by “both men and lovely-girdled women”, and no other fighter in the poems carries a mace (see [[areithous]]). The weapon that made his name was his undoing. Lycurgus killed him “by guile, not by strength at all”, in a narrow road where there was no room to swing the mace, and ran him through with a spear before he could use it (7.142–145; see [[lycurgus-of-arcadia]] and [[spear]]). The mace is of iron (σιδηρείῃ, 7.141, 7.144), which is as unusual as the weapon itself, since the heroes fight with bronze (see [[iron]]). Lycurgus stripped the armor, the gift of Ares, and wore it, and in his old age gave it to Ereuthalion, whom the young Nestor killed (7.146–156; see [[nestor-and-ereuthalion]] and [[ereuthalion]]). The armor passed from hand to hand, and the mace is not heard of again.

The byname marks Areithous again as the father of Menesthius, whom Paris kills in the first lines of the book: “the son of lord Areithous, Menesthius, who lived in Arne, whom the mace-man Areithous fathered” (7.8–10; see [[menesthius]]).

## Outside Homer

Theseus, on his way from Troezen to Athens, killed at Epidaurus a son of Hephaestus named Periphetes, who was called Corynetes, “the club-man”, from the club he carried. Apollodorus says the club was of iron, and that Theseus took it and carried it himself (*Epitome* 1.1); Plutarch says he was so pleased with it that he made it his weapon, as Heracles wore the lion's skin (*Theseus* 8). When Peisistratus asked the Athenians for a bodyguard, they gave him not spearmen but men with wooden clubs, κορυνηφόροι, “club-bearers” (Herodotus 1.59).
