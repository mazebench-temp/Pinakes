## In the Odyssey

The scar is the oldest proof of who Odysseus is. Eurycleia finds it as she washes his feet, and the poem stops to tell how he got it, hunting a boar on Parnassus with the sons of Autolycus (*Odyssey* 19.390–475; see [[the-boar-hunt-on-parnassus]] and [[odysseus-recognized-by-his-nurse]]). He shows it to the swineherd and the cowherd before he fights the suitors (*Odyssey* 21.217–221), Eurycleia tells Penelope of it (*Odyssey* 23.73–77), and he shows it to his father Laertes (*Odyssey* 24.331–335; see [[odysseus]] and [[eurycleia]]).

Erich Auerbach opened *Mimesis* (1946) with an essay on this scene, “Odysseus' Scar”, setting Homer's leisurely digression, which brings everything into the foreground, against the fraught silences of the binding of Isaac in Genesis.
