Nestor's voice flowed from his tongue “sweeter than honey” (1.249; see [[sweeter-than-honey]]), and Hecamede sets before Nestor and the wounded Machaon an onion, pale honey and barley meal beside the drink she mixes for them (11.630–631; see [[kykeon]]). Honey mixed with milk was the first libation poured for the dead (*Odyssey* 10.519, 11.27). In the *Poetics* μέλι appears with κόμμι, “gum”, and πέπερι, “pepper”, as the only three nouns that end in iota (1458a15–16; see [[gender-of-nouns]], [[gum]] and [[pepper]]).
