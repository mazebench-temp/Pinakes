summary: The shallow, handleless bowl from which the Greeks poured libations, often with a raised boss at the center; the "cup" of Dionysus in Aristotle's model metaphor of analogy, which calls a shield "the cup of Ares".
aliases: [libation bowl, the cup of Dionysus]
---

The phiale was the vessel of libation, held flat on the palm and tipped so that the wine ran out; vase painters show gods and mortals holding it over an altar (see [[libation]]). In the Iliad a golden φιάλη holds the bones of Patroclus (23.243, 23.253), and one never set on the fire is a prize at his funeral games (23.270). Its round shape and central boss make the comparison with a round shield natural (see [[the-cup-of-ares]] and [[shield]]).
