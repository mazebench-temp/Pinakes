ἱστίον is formed from ἱστός, the [[mast|mast]]: the thing that belongs to the mast. A Homeric ship carries a single square sail, but the plural ἱστία is used for it as freely as the singular. At Chryse the crew furl the sails and stow them in the ship before they row in ({{ref:Iliad 1.433}}). For the voyage back, far-working Apollo sends them a following wind; they set up the mast, spread the white sails, and the wind fills the sail.
