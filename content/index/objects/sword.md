Homer has three words for the sword, used without clear distinction: ξίφος, φάσγανον and ἄορ. It hangs at the thigh from a strap over the shoulder, and a sword “studded with silver” (ξίφος ἀργυρόηλον) is a standing phrase (Iliad 2.45). κώπη is any handle, of a sword, an oar or a key (Odyssey 21.7).
