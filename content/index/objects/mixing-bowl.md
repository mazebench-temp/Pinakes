The κρητήρ (from κεράννυμι, “mix”) is the large bowl in which wine was mixed with water before being ladled into cups. Greeks did not usually drink their wine unmixed.

At Chryse, when the meal is over, the young men fill the mixing bowls “brim-full with drink” and serve it round, with the first drops poured for the [[libation|libation]] ({{ref:Iliad 1.470-471}}). The line is a formula for the start of drinking; it recurs at Iliad 9.175. The gods mix their drink the same way: [[Hephaestus]] serves them, drawing [[nectar|nectar]] from the mixing bowl ({{ref:Iliad 1.598}}).

{{quote:e3c5fd14-83e7-5656-b694-d7fb66d0aa41}}

A fine mixing bowl was a treasure: the prize for the footrace at Patroclus' games is a silver one of Sidonian work (Iliad 23.740–745).

## Outside Homer

The krater is one of the chief shapes of Greek pottery. The François Vase, a large Athenian black-figure krater of about 570 BCE painted by Kleitias, is among the most famous.
