## The words

αὐλή is an open space within a wall: the court of a house (see [[house]]) and the walled yard of a steading, where the flocks are shut in at night. The translation calls the herdsman's αὐλή a “fold” and keeps “court” for a palace's (see the translation's row αὐλή, of a herdsman). The fold's wall is its defense. In a simile of the Iliad a lion, grazed by a shepherd “as it leapt over the wall of the fold”, scatters the undefended sheep and “in his fury leaps back out of the high-walled fold” (5.136–142; see [[wounded-lion]]), and the Trojans clamor like the countless ewes of a rich man “in the fold”, bleating at milking time as they hear their lambs (4.433–435; see [[sheep-in-the-fold]] and [[milk]]).

The σηκοί are the pens within the fold, in which the young are kept apart from their mothers. The farmstead, σταθμός, is the herdsman's whole station, with its fold, its huts and its pens: the pasture on the Shield of Achilles has “farmsteads and roofed huts and pens” (18.587–589; see [[the-sheep-pasture]] and [[hut]]). The same word is the doorpost of a house (see [[door]]).

## Elsewhere in the Odyssey

The words of the Cyclops' fold, περὶ δʼ αὐλὴ / ὑψηλὴ δέδμητο, “around it a high fold had been built” (*Odyssey* 9.184–185), return for the fold of the swineherd's farmstead on Ithaca, which Eumaeus built himself while his master was away (*Odyssey* 14.5–10; see [[eumaeus]] and [[sties]]). And in a simile of Odysseus' companions crowding round him on Circe's island, the calves of a farm frisk about the cows coming home from pasture, and the pens no longer hold them (*Odyssey* 10.410–414).
