## The word

ἤϊα, a neuter plural, is food taken on a journey. The Iliad has the word once, of deer that are the prey of jackals and leopards and wolves (13.103), and the Odyssey uses it of a ship's stores. Telemachus' provisions are wine and barley meal, the wine in two-handled jars and the meal in bags of leather, δοροί, a word found only here in Homer, or “thick skins” (δέρμασιν ἐν πυκινοῖσιν, *Odyssey* 2.289–291, 2.354; see [[two-handled-jar]] and [[barley-meal]]). A wineskin, ἀσκός, is another thing (*Odyssey* 9.196; *Iliad* 3.247).

## Elsewhere in the Odyssey

On a long voyage the provisions give out. Menelaus, held for twenty days on Pharos without a wind, would have seen all his provisions spent, and the strength of his men, if Eidothea had not pitied him (*Odyssey* 4.360–366); on Thrinacia, when all the provisions of the ship were gone, Odysseus' companions roamed hunting fish and birds with bent hooks, and in the end, at Eurylochus' urging, turned to the cattle of the Sun (*Odyssey* 12.327–332, 12.339–343; see [[the-cattle-of-helios]]). Hosts and goddesses put food on board: Calypso stows a skin of wine and a skin of water on Odysseus' raft, and ᾖα in a bag (*Odyssey* 5.265–267; see [[calypso]]), and Odysseus carries ᾖα in a bag, with a skin of Maron's wine, to the cave of the Cyclops (*Odyssey* 9.212–213). The renderings of these books are plain renderings, not the translation.
