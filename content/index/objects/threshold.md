βηλός is a rare word for a threshold, used in Homer of the threshold of the gods. [[Hephaestus]] tells his mother why he dares not stand against Zeus: once, when he tried to defend her, Zeus seized him by the foot and hurled him “from the divine threshold”, and he fell all day and landed on Lemnos at sunset (see [[fall-of-hephaestus]]).

{{quote:7fe8d317-d2cf-5ea1-baea-2cdf0dfce120}}

Zeus uses the same phrase in Book 15, recalling how he would seize any god who tried to help Hera and hurl him “from the threshold” (τεταγὼν ἀπὸ βηλοῦ) until he reached the earth half dead (Iliad 15.22–24). [[Plato]] banned the story from his ideal city (see [[plato-hephaestus-hurled]]).
