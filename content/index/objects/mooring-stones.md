εὐναί, literally “beds” or “resting places”, are heavy stones on ropes, dropped to hold a moored ship. Homer has no anchor with arms; the word ἄγκυρα is later. At Chryse the crew cast out the mooring-stones and make fast the [[stern-cables|stern-cables]] ({{ref:Iliad 1.436}}): stones off the bow, ropes from the stern to the land. In the harbor of the island off the land of the Cyclopes there is no need for either, “to cast out mooring-stones or tie stern-cables” (Odyssey 9.136–137).

## Outside Homer

Stone anchors pierced for a rope have been found in large numbers at Bronze Age harbors and sanctuaries around the eastern Mediterranean, some of them dedicated to the gods.
