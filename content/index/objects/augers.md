## The word

τέρετρον, like Latin *terebra*, “borer”, comes from the root of τετραίνω, “bore through”, the verb that follows it in the raft's building (*Odyssey* 5.247). W. W. Merry takes the τέρετρα for small borers or gimlets that can be worked with one hand, as distinct from the τρύπανον, the larger drill of the Cyclops simile, which one man guides from above while others spin it with a strap (*Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 5.246; *Odyssey* 9.384–386). The translation renders them “augers”.

## In Homer

Homer names the tool twice, both times in Odysseus' carpentry. While he hews the timbers of his raft, Calypso brings him augers, and “he bored them all and fitted them to one another” (*Odyssey* 5.246–247; see [[the-building-of-the-raft]] and [[raft]]). Building his bed in Ithaca around the trunk of an olive, he smoothed the trunk with the bronze, made it straight to the chalk line, shaped it into a bedpost and “bored it all with an auger” (τέτρηνα δὲ πάντα τερέτρῳ, *Odyssey* 23.195–198, a plain rendering), the verb of the raft (τέτρηνεν δʼ ἄρα πάντα, *Odyssey* 5.247; see [[odysseus]] and [[bed]]).
