πυρή (from πῦρ, “fire”) is the pile of wood on which a body is burned. In Book 1 the pyres are the visible toll of Apollo's [[arrows|arrows]]: “the pyres of the dead burned on, thick and constant” ({{ref:Iliad 1.52}}). The line gives no names and no funerals, only fires burning always (αἰεί) and crowded together (θαμειαί), day after day for nine days (see [[cremation|cremation]]).

{{quote:2370c7e0-aad2-5a38-82fa-6000b23c58d4}}

The great pyre of the poem is Patroclus', a hundred feet each way (Iliad 23.164).
