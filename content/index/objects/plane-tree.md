πλατάνιστος is the Homeric form of the name; later Greek usually says πλάτανος. It is generally derived from πλατύς, “broad”, for the tree's broad leaves or spreading crown. The oriental plane (*Platanus orientalis*) grows wild in the eastern Mediterranean and western Asia, most often beside springs and streams, and gives deep shade. Homer's tree stands in just such a place, over a spring at [[aulis|Aulis]] where the army sacrificed before sailing for Troy (2.305–307). Homer names the tree only in this story (2.307, 2.310).

## Outside Homer

Pausanias saw a temple of Artemis at Aulis, and says that the people kept in it what was left of the wood of the plane tree Homer mentions in the Iliad (9.19.7). Herodotus tells how Xerxes, marching on Greece, came upon a plane tree in Lydia so beautiful that he adorned it with gold and left one of his Immortals to guard it (7.31). The best-known plane tree in Greek literature grows on the bank of the Ilissus outside Athens, where Socrates and Phaedrus sit down in its shade to talk (Plato, *Phaedrus* 229a–b, 230b).
