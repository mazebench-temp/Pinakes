## The word

τυρός is cheese, the curdled milk of the flocks. Homer does not tell how cheese is made beyond the Cyclops' curdling of half his milk (*Odyssey* 9.246–247); the Iliad knows the curdling of milk by fig juice, which thickens it “very quickly … as a man stirs it” (5.902–903; see [[fig-juice]] and [[milk]]).

## In Homer

Cheese is the food of the flocks' country and a part of mixed drinks. In Nestor's hut Hecamede makes a mixture for him and the wounded Machaon “with Pramnian wine, and on it she grated goat's cheese / with a bronze grater, and sprinkled white barley meal over it” (11.638–640; see [[kykeon]], [[hecamede]] and [[pramnian]]). Menelaus tells of Libya, where the lambs are horned at once and the ewes bear three times a year, so that “neither master nor shepherd has any lack / of cheese or meat or sweet milk” (*Odyssey* 4.85–89). Circe stirs cheese, barley meal and pale honey into Pramnian wine for Odysseus' companions, with her drugs mixed in (*Odyssey* 10.234–236; see [[circe]]), as Hecamede's drink had cheese and barley meal in Pramnian wine. In Penelope's story of the daughters of Pandareus, Aphrodite tended the orphaned girls “with cheese and sweet honey and pleasant wine” (*Odyssey* 20.68–69, a plain rendering).

## Outside Homer

In Euripides' *Cyclops* Silenus tells Odysseus that the Cyclopes live “on milk and cheeses and the flesh of sheep” (*Cyclops* 121–122), and trades him cheese curdled with fig juice for his wine (*Cyclops* 136–139, 188–192); coming home, the Cyclops asks whether his vessels of rush are full of cheeses (*Cyclops* 208–209; see [[euripides]]).
