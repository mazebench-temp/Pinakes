## In Homer

Homer's word is ὅρμος, a chain that passed round the neck and hung down on the breast (D. B. Monro, *Homer's Odyssey, Books XIII–XXIV*, 1901, on *Odyssey* 15.460). Hephaestus made necklaces among the jewelry he forged in Thetis' cave (*Iliad* 18.400–401; see [[brooch]]). The two necklaces of the Odyssey are of gold strung with amber: the one that a Phoenician trader brings to the house of Eumaeus' father, where the women handle it while he gives the nurse the sign to flee with the child (*Odyssey* 15.458–464; see [[the-kidnapping-of-eumaeus]], [[the-phoenician-nurse]] and [[amber]]), and the one that the suitor Eurymachus gives Penelope, “like the sun” (*Odyssey* 18.295–296, a plain rendering; see [[eurymachus]]). A ὅρμος is also an anchorage (*Iliad* 1.435; *Odyssey* 15.497; see [[harbor]]).

## Outside Homer

Tokens left with an exposed child, necklaces, rings and the like, are the stock means by which the child is later recognized, in tragedy and above all in New Comedy, whose plots often turn on them (Menander's *Arbitration*, Plautus' *Rope*). The necklace of legend was the one that Hephaestus made for Harmonia when she married Cadmus; Polynices bribed Eriphyle with it to send her husband to Thebes, and it brought ruin on all who owned it (Apollodorus 3.4.2, 3.6.2; see [[alcmaeon-kills-eriphyle]] and [[cadmus]]).
