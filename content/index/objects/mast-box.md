## The word

μεσόδμη is formed from μέσος, “middle”, and the root of δέμω, “build”, the root of δόμος, “house”: “the thing built in the middle”. Homer uses it twice of a ship and twice of a house. On the ship it is the hollow box in which the mast stands: “The mast of fir, within the hollow mast-box, / they raised and set up, and made it fast with forestays” (*Odyssey* 2.424–425, again at 15.289–290; see [[mast]] and [[forestays]]). How it was built the poem does not say. The Iliad names the other end of the work, the crutch in which the lowered mast is laid when the ship comes into harbor (1.434; see [[mast-crutch]]).

In the house the plural μεσόδμαι stands with the walls, the beams of fir and the pillars of the hall. Telemachus sees them shine as if with blazing fire when Athena goes before him with a golden lamp (*Odyssey* 19.33–40), and Theoclymenus, in his vision of the suitors' death, sees the walls and the μεσόδμαι spattered with blood (*Odyssey* 20.351–357; see [[house]]). The renderings of Books 19 and 20 of the Odyssey are plain renderings, not the translation.
