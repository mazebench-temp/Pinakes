## The words

ἕρματα are “earrings” in two places in Homer, here and in the Odyssey (*Odyssey* 18.297), and elsewhere the props that hold a beached ship upright (1.486, 2.154; see [[props]] and the translation's note on 14.182–183). The verse that describes them is shared by both passages, τρίγληνα μορόεντα, rendered “with three drops, clustered like mulberries”. τρίγληνος is formed from “three” and γλήνη, the eyeball, which Peneleos' spear drives out of Ilioneus' head at 14.494; here the eyes are three beads or drops hanging from each earring. μορόεις is uncertain; it is usually explained from μόρον, “mulberry”, for the clustered drops. The ears are ἐΰτρητοι λοβοί, “well-pierced lobes”, and λοβός occurs only here in Homer.

## In Homer

Hera puts them on after her robe, its golden pins and her girdle, and before the veil and the sandals: “and in her well-pierced ears she put earrings / with three drops, clustered like mulberries; and much grace shone from them” (14.182–183; see [[the-adornment-of-a-goddess]], [[girdle]] and [[charis]]).

In the Odyssey the same ornaments are a suitor's gift. When Penelope reminds the suitors that wooers bring gifts, Antinous gives her a great embroidered robe with twelve golden brooches, Eurymachus a golden necklace strung with amber, “and earrings for Eurydamas two attendants brought, / with three drops, clustered like mulberries; and much grace shone from them”, and Peisander a necklace (*Odyssey* 18.290–301; see [[brooch]]). The robe with golden pins and the earrings that Hera puts on to win Zeus are the gifts with which men try to win a queen.

## Outside Homer

In the sixth Homeric Hymn the Hours dress Aphrodite when she comes ashore at Cyprus, and put “in her pierced ears / flowers of orichalc and of precious gold” (*Hymn* 6.8–9; see [[aphrodite]]). Proclus takes Hera's earrings and sandals as images of the first and the last of the powers that flow from the goddess, the highest and those about her feet (*On the Republic* I 137–138 Kroll; see [[proclus-the-neoplatonist]]).
