## Description

σάκος is Homer's word for the greatest shields (see [[shield]]). Ajax's is χάλκεον ἑπταβόειον, “bronze, of seven oxhides” (7.220): seven layers of hide “of well-fed bulls”, and an eighth, of bronze, hammered on over them (ἐπὶ δʼ ὄγδοον ἤλασε χαλκόν, 7.223). ἑπταβόειος = ἑπτά (“seven”) + βόειος (“of oxhide”), and Homer gives it to no other shield (7.220, 7.222, 7.245, 7.266, 11.545; see [[seven-oxhide]]). ἐλαύνω, “drive”, is the verb for beating out metal. Sarpedon's round shield is made in the same way, of bronze that the smith hammered out, with oxhides stitched close inside (12.294–297). Ajax's is αἰόλον, “flashing” (7.222), and δεινόν, “dread” (7.245, 7.266), and its maker is the leather-worker [[Tychius]] of [[Hyle]]. The only other shield whose layers Homer counts is the one Hephaestus makes for Achilles, of five (18.481; 20.269–272).

ἠΰτε πύργον, “like a tower”: the shield is a wall for one man. πύργος is the tower of a city wall (see [[the-tower-of-ilios]] and [[like-a-tower]]), and the man who carries this one is “the bulwark of the Achaeans” (7.211; see [[bulwark-of-the-achaeans]]).

## In the duel

Hector's spear strikes “the outermost bronze, which was the eighth layer upon it”, cuts through six layers, and stops in the seventh hide (7.245–248). At close quarters his spear point does not break through, and is bent back (7.258–259). His stone strikes the shield “in the middle, on the boss” (μέσσον ἐπομφάλιον, 7.267), and the bronze rings. ἐπομφάλιον, from ὀμφαλός, the central boss (see [[bossed]]), occurs only here. Hector's own shield, “balanced on every side”, is pierced by Ajax's spear and broken by his stone (7.250–251, 7.270; see [[the-duel-of-hector-and-ajax]]).

## Elsewhere in Homer

Teucer shoots from behind it and ducks back to Ajax “like a child under its mother” (8.266–272; see [[teucer]]). Hector's charioteer knows Ajax by “the broad shield around his shoulders” (11.526–527), and when Zeus puts fear in him, Ajax swings the shield behind him and retreats (11.544–547). His companions take it from him when he tires (13.709–711). Hector's spear strikes where two straps cross on Ajax's chest, the shield's and his silver-studded sword's, and they save him (14.402–406); the poem does not say whether this is the sword Hector gave him (7.303). At the ships his left shoulder tires from holding the flashing shield steady (16.106–107). The verse of 7.219 returns when he comes to rescue Odysseus (11.485) and when he stands over Patroclus, covering the body with the broad shield (17.128–137). Achilles, with no armor of his own, knows of none that he could put on “except the shield of Ajax, son of Telamon” (18.192–193).

## Outside Homer

In Sophocles' *Ajax* the hero gives the shield to his son Eurysaces, “Broad-shield”: he is to take “the thing you are named for … the unbreakable shield of seven oxhides” (574–576; see [[sophocles-tecmessa]]).

Tall body shields of hide on a frame, some rectangular like a tower and some shaped like a figure eight, appear in the art of the early Mycenaean age: on an inlaid dagger from the shaft graves at Mycenae, and in the miniature fresco from Akrotiri on Thera. Many scholars have taken Ajax's shield as a memory of these, kept in the formulas of the tradition, like [[the-boars-tusk-helmet|the helmet of boar's tusks]] that Meriones lends Odysseus (10.261–265). But its bronze facing and its boss (7.223, 7.267) belong rather to later, round shields, and the shield of the poem may join features of different ages (see [[shield]]).
