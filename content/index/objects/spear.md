The spear, thrown or thrust, is the main weapon of the Homeric warrior; the sword is for close work. δόρυ first meant “tree, wood”. It is related to δρῦς (“oak”) and to English *tree*, and Homer still uses it of timber: the “timbers” of the ships have rotted (δοῦρα, Iliad 2.135).

In Book 1 the spear appears once. Achilles will not fight for the girl, but if Agamemnon tries to take anything else from beside his ship, “at once your dark blood will gush out around my spear”.

{{quote:24c2617b-4b08-5d6a-9175-25dc5fb62f60}}

A warrior is also named from his spear-point (αἰχμή): the Trojans are “spearmen” (αἰχμηταί, {{ref:Iliad 1.152}}), and Agamemnon grants that the gods made Achilles a spearman ({{ref:Iliad 1.290}}). Achilles' own spear is the ash from Mount Pelion that no other Achaean can wield, given to his father by the centaur Chiron (Iliad 16.140–144).
