## The gifts

**Foretold.** When Zeus sends Hermes to Calypso, he foretells the Phaeacians' welcome: they will send Odysseus home in a ship, “giving him bronze and gold in plenty, and clothing, / a great store, more than Odysseus would ever have carried off from Troy, / if he had come home free of harm and won his share of the spoil” (*Odyssey* 5.38–40; see [[zeus]] and [[phaeacians]]). Poseidon's complaint to Zeus that the Phaeacians have given them repeats the verses (*Odyssey* 13.135–138, where 137–138 = 5.39–40; see [[poseidon]]).

**Given on Scheria.** After the games Alcinous proposes a guest-gift, “as is fitting”: each of the twelve kings who rule the land, and he himself the thirteenth, is to bring a freshly washed mantle, a tunic and a talent of gold, and Euryalus is to make amends for his taunt “with words / and with a gift” (*Odyssey* 8.389–397; see [[alcinous]], [[kings]], [[cloak]], [[tunic]], [[talent]], [[euryalus-the-phaeacian]] and [[guest-gifts]]). Euryalus gives his sword, all of bronze, with a hilt of silver and a sheath of new-sawn ivory (*Odyssey* 8.401–405; see [[sword]]). At sunset the heralds bring the gifts to the house; Alcinous adds his own golden goblet, so that the stranger may remember him whenever he pours libations at home, and Arete lays the clothing and the gold in a chest with a mantle and a tunic of her own, which Odysseus ties with a knot that Circe taught him (*Odyssey* 8.417–448; see [[cup]], [[chest]] and [[arete-queen-of-the-phaeacians]]).

**Increased.** When Odysseus breaks off his tale among the dead, Arete asks the Phaeacians not to cut short their gifts to one in such need, and Alcinous bids the stranger stay a day, “until I complete / the whole present”; Odysseus answers that he would stay a year for such gifts, since it would be better “to come to my dear native land with a fuller hand” (*Odyssey* 11.338–361; see [[possessions]] and [[xenia]]). The addition comes at the opening of *Odyssey* 13, a great tripod and a cauldron from each lord (see [[tripod]], [[cauldron]] and the Book 13 note).

**Carried home and hidden.** The gifts go to Ithaca on the ship that carries Odysseus asleep; the sailors leave them by the olive at the harbor of Phorcys, Odysseus counts them when he wakes, and Athena hides them in the cave of the nymphs (*Odyssey* 13.117–124, 13.215–219, 13.361–371; see [[odysseus-set-ashore-on-ithaca]], [[olive]], [[the-harbor-of-phorcys]] and [[the-cave-of-the-naiads]]).

## Later in the Odyssey

Odysseus tells Telemachus how the Phaeacians “carried me asleep in a swift ship over the sea / and set me down in Ithaca, and gave me splendid gifts, / bronze and gold in plenty, and woven clothing. / And these lie in caves by the will of the gods” (*Odyssey* 16.229–232, a plain rendering; see [[telemachus]] and [[the-cave-of-the-naiads]]). At the end, telling Penelope his wanderings, he comes to the Phaeacians, who sent him home in a ship, “giving him bronze and gold in plenty, and clothing” (*Odyssey* 23.338–341, a plain rendering; see [[penelope]]): the verse of Zeus' promise in *Odyssey* 5.

## Outside Homer

Hyginus' summary of the Odyssey keeps the gifts: Alcinous received him generously as a guest, “honored him with gifts”, *donisque decoratum*, and sent him home to Ithaca; and then it adds a second shipwreck, “by the anger of Mercury”, *ira Mercurii*, which Homer does not tell (Hyginus, *Fabulae* 125; see [[hermes]]).
