ὀθόνη is fine linen, and in the plural linen cloths or garments. When Iris has filled her with longing for her former husband and her home, Helen goes out to the wall: “and at once she veiled herself in white linen (ἀργεννῇσι καλυψαμένη ὀθόνῃσιν) and hurried from her chamber, letting her tender tears fall, not alone”, for two handmaids go with her (3.141–143). A woman of her rank went out covered and attended. At the end of the book she goes back the same way, wrapped in her robe, in silence (3.419–420; see [[robe]]).

## Elsewhere in Homer

The girls who dance on the shield of Achilles wear linen (ὀθόναι, 18.595). Penelope comes down among the suitors holding her shining veil (κρήδεμνα) before her cheeks (Odyssey 1.334). When Hector is killed, his mother throws off her veil (καλύπτρη, 22.405–406), and Andromache casts from her head the veil that golden Aphrodite gave her on the day Hector took her from her father's house (22.468–472).
