## In Homer

{{quote:7a3681ef-ed27-5f34-81b0-bf4552019aa2}}

Zeus has told Hermes to bring Priam to the ships unseen (24.334–338; see [[hermes]]), and the god arms himself for the errand with his sandals and his wand (see [[sandals]]). ῥάβδος is a rod or staff; in the Iliad the word is found elsewhere only of the golden wires that hold the hides of Sarpedon's shield (12.297). The wand's power is the one Hermes uses on this journey: at the Achaean wall he “poured sleep over” the sentries (24.445; see [[night-watch]]), and before dawn he wakes Priam in Achilles' porch and leads him out of the camp (24.682–691). Walter Leaf, noting that the six verses of the sandals and the wand recur in the Odyssey when Hermes goes to Calypso (*Odyssey* 5.43–49), finds the wand more in place here, “for in 445 it is actually used to lull the sentinels to sleep”, and calls it the origin of the later κηρύκειον, the herald's staff (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 24.339).

In the Odyssey the wand is golden. Hermes is χρυσόρραπις, “of the golden wand” (*Odyssey* 5.87, 10.277, 10.331), and he drives the souls of the suitors down to Hades with it, holding in his hands “a fine golden wand, with which he bewitches the eyes of men” (*Odyssey* 24.1–5). Circe strikes Odysseus' companions with her wand and pens them as swine (*Odyssey* 10.238, 10.293, 10.319, 10.389), and Athena touches Odysseus with hers to make him an old beggar and to restore him (*Odyssey* 13.429, 16.172, 16.456).

## Outside Homer

In the *Homeric Hymn to Hermes*, sealing his friendship with the young god, Apollo promises him “a very beautiful wand of wealth and prosperity, / golden, with three leaves”, which will keep him from harm (*Homeric Hymn to Hermes* 524–532; see [[apollo]]). The herald's staff was a sign of peace and of news: at Mycale, on the day of the battle of Plataea, a rumor of the victory flew through the Greek army as it advanced, and a herald's staff, κηρυκήιον, was seen lying at the edge of the surf (Herodotus 9.100.1; see [[herodotus]] and [[heralds]]).
