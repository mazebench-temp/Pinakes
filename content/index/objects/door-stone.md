## The word

θυρεός, from θύρη, “door”, is a stone set against a doorway like a door; in this verse it is scanned as two syllables, and in later Greek the word names a shield (W. W. Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 9.240). The verse that brings it in is a formula of the book: αὐτὰρ ἔπειτʼ ἐπέθηκε θυρεὸν μέγαν ὑψόσʼ ἀείρας, “Then he lifted high and set in place a great door-stone” (*Odyssey* 9.240 = 9.340; see the translation's row θυρεός and [[door]]).
