## The instrument

The aulos had a double reed, like the modern oboe, and the player usually played two pipes at once, held in place by a mouth-band. Its music was associated with Dionysus and with strong emotion, and Plato would have banished it from his city in favor of the lyre (*Republic* 3.399d–e). In the Iliad, Agamemnon, lying awake in the night, looks out at the Trojan plain and hears “the sound of flutes and pipes and the din of men” (10.13), and on the shield of Achilles pipes and lyres play at a wedding (18.495).
