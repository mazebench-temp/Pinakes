The οὐλοχύται are whole barley grains (οὐλαί) for scattering (χέω, “pour”). At Chryse, when the victims stand around the [[altar|altar]], the Achaeans wash their hands and take up the barley ({{ref:Iliad 1.449}}). Chryses prays, and when the prayer is over they throw the grains forward (προβάλοντο) and the killing begins ({{ref:Iliad 1.458-459}}; see [[sacrifice|sacrifice]]). Water and barley open the rite: at Pylos, Nestor begins with the hand-water and the barley (Odyssey 3.445), brought in a bowl and a basket (Odyssey 3.440–441).

## Outside Homer

In later sacrifice the barley was carried in a basket (κανοῦν), and the basket-bearer (κανηφόρος) was an honored role for girls in Athenian processions. A chorus of women in Aristophanes' *Lysistrata* recalls, “I was a basket-bearer, a pretty girl” (646).
