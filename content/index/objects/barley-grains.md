The οὐλοχύται are barley grains (οὐλαί) for scattering (χέω, “pour”), whole or, as W. W. Merry takes the οὐλαί, coarsely ground (*Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on *Odyssey* 3.441). Water and barley open the rite, at Chryse and at Pylos (*Iliad* 1.449; *Odyssey* 3.440–445).

{{notes}}

## Outside Homer

In later sacrifice the barley was carried in a basket (κανοῦν), and the basket-bearer (κανηφόρος) was an honored role for girls in Athenian processions. A chorus of women in Aristophanes' *Lysistrata* recalls, “I was a basket-bearer, a pretty girl” (646).
