## In Homer

When Thetis comes to ask for armor for her son, Hephaestus rises from his anvil, “He set his bellows away from the fire, and all the tools / he toiled with he gathered into a silver chest” (18.412–413; see [[hephaestus]] and [[the-shield-of-achilles]]). It is a smith's tool box, and being a god's it is of silver (see [[gold-and-silver]]).

The other is the last vessel of the poem. When Hector's pyre has been quenched with wine, his brothers and companions gather his white bones, weeping, “And they took the bones and put them in a golden chest, / covering them with soft purple robes, / and quickly laid it in a hollow trench, and above it / they spread great stones, close-set” (24.795–798), and over it they heap the mound (24.799–801; see [[the-funeral-of-hector]], [[tomb]], [[robe]] and [[purple]]). Walter Leaf takes the chest to answer to the golden bowl in which the bones of Patroclus were gathered and laid in the hut (23.243–254), and compares the κάπετος, “trench”, with the trench around the vineyard on the Shield (18.564) and with Sophocles' *Ajax*, κοίλην κάπετον χερσὶ ταχύνετε, “hasten the hollow trench with your hands” (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 24.795, citing *Ajax* 1165 and 1403; see the translation's note on 24.795 and [[the-golden-urn]]). Patroclus' bones were to wait in the hut for those of Achilles (23.243–248); Hector's are buried at once in his own land.

The poem has two other words for chests. The clothes chest is χηλός, like the chest that Thetis packed for her son (16.221–224; see [[chest]]), and Priam takes the ransom for Hector from his φωριαμοί, “chests”, a word found elsewhere in Homer only of the chests by which Helen stands in Sparta (24.228; *Odyssey* 15.104; see [[apoina]]).

## Outside Homer

In Herodotus the λάρναξ is a chest for treasure: to deceive Polycrates of Samos, the Persian Oroetes filled eight chests with stones almost to the brim, laid gold on top, tied them up and showed them to Polycrates' secretary as his wealth (Herodotus 3.123.2; see [[herodotus]]).
