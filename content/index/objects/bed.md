## The words

λέχος is a bed, especially the marriage bed. ἄλοχος, “wife”, is literally “she who shares the bed” (ἀ-, “together”, + λέχος; see [[marriage|marriage]]). The plural λέχεα is a bed, or the bier on which the dead are laid (Iliad 18.233, 24.589). λέκτρον is another word for the bed (Odyssey 1.437), and εὐνή is the place where one lies down, the marriage bed of the phrase εὐνῇ μίσγεσθαι, “lay with her in bed” (*Odyssey* 1.433), and also an animal's lair. The bedstead is the δέμνια, and on it a guest's bed is made with blankets, rugs and woolly cloaks (Iliad 24.643–646; Odyssey 4.297–299, 7.336–338). The bed of Paris and Helen in the Iliad and Telemachus' bed in the Odyssey are τρητὰ λέχεα, “the corded bed” (Iliad 3.448; Odyssey 1.440; see [[corded]]).

## In the Odyssey

The recognition of husband and wife turns on a bed. Penelope tests the stranger by telling Eurycleia to make up the bed outside the chamber that Odysseus built; he answers in anger that no man could move it, for he built it himself round the trunk of an olive tree that grew inside the courtyard, and by that sign she knows him (Odyssey 23.177–206; see [[penelope]]).
