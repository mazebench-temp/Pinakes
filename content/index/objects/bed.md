λέχος is a bed, especially the marriage bed. ἄλοχος, “wife”, is literally “she who shares the bed” (ἀ-, “together”, + λέχος; see [[marriage|marriage]]).

The bed opens and closes the book. Agamemnon refuses to free [[Chryseis]]: she will grow old in his house “as she walks to and fro at the [[loom|loom]] and comes to share my bed” (ἐμὸν λέχος ἀντιόωσαν, literally “meeting my bed”).

{{quote:d96703ad-b70e-5b6f-8a55-4f75a90f0111}}

At the end of the book, after the quarrel and the feast on Olympus, [[Zeus]] goes up to the bed where he always sleeps, and [[Hera]], with whom he has just been quarreling, lies beside him.

{{quote:95b0933f-f50b-5a86-8ad9-f21867ef468d}}

The book moves from a captive's bed, part of the prize men quarrel over, to the marriage bed of the gods, where their quarrel is laid to rest (see [[quarrel-on-olympus]]).
