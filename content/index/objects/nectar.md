In Homer nectar is the gods' drink, as [[ambrosia|ambrosia]] is their food. It is red (Odyssey 5.93), and it is mixed in a bowl and served in cups like wine. The name is often explained as “overcoming death” (νεκ-, “death”, and a root meaning “pass beyond”), but the derivation is uncertain.

Book 1 serves it once. After the quarrel of Zeus and Hera, [[Hephaestus]] goes round from left to right and pours for the other gods, drawing sweet nectar from the [[mixing-bowl|mixing bowl]] ({{ref:Iliad 1.597-598}}). The verb, οἰνοχόει, is literally “poured wine” (see [[cupbearing|cupbearing]]). At the start of Book 4, Hebe pours the nectar and the gods pledge one another in golden cups (Iliad 4.2–4).
