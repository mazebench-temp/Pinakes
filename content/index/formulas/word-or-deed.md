## The pattern

Speech and action together make up everything a person can do for someone else. The ideal hero, as Phoenix puts it in Book 9, is "a speaker of words and a doer of deeds" (9.443).

## In Book 1

{{quote:be8e0e4b-cf4a-596b-8d22-d5c00b909155}}

Calchas asks Achilles to protect him "with words and with your hands".

{{quote:8f239b4b-a8a5-51e4-a382-ca7f0d1529f5}}

Achilles asks whether Thetis ever helped Zeus "by word or deed".

{{quote:822e916e-fe51-5d5a-9dc6-ae0164abcf9c}}

Thetis uses the same pairing to Zeus himself: ἢ ἔπει ἢ ἔργῳ. The phrase belongs to the language of reciprocity: favors done in speech or act are what a suppliant can claim in return. See [[reciprocity-with-the-gods]] and [[words-and-violence]].
