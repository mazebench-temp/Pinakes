## The pattern

ἦμος (“when”) + ἠριγένεια ([[early-born]]) + φάνη (“appeared”) + ῥοδοδάκτυλος ([[rosy-fingered]]) + Ἠώς (Dawn, [[eos]]). The next verse usually begins with “then” (τῆμος, καὶ τότε) and the first action of the new day. The line is at home in the Odyssey, where it opens day after day. In the Iliad the whole line occurs only here and at the dawn of Hector's funeral in the last book (Il. 24.788).

Another dawn formula counts days: ἀλλ’ ὅτε δή ῥ’ ἐκ τοῖο δυωδεκάτη γένετ’ ἠώς, “but when the twelfth dawn after that had come” ({{ref:Iliad 1.493}}). The same verse opens a second twelve-day wait at the start of Book 24 (Il. 24.31).

## In Book 1

{{quote:7b1bb166-c3fc-5e06-adb2-cdefa98fc70a}}

The formula divides the night at Chryse from the voyage home, and it pairs with the sunset line two verses before ([[sunset-lines]]). The book keeps careful count of its days: nine days of plague ({{ref:Iliad 1.53}}), the tenth for the assembly ({{ref:Iliad 1.54}}), and twelve days until the gods return from the Ethiopians ({{ref:Iliad 1.425}}).
