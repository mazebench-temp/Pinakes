## The pattern

ἄγχι (“near”) + σχών (aorist participle of ἔχω, “hold”) + κεφαλήν (“head”): the speaker leans his head close to his neighbor's, ἵνα μὴ πευθοίαθʼ οἱ ἄλλοι, “so that the others might not hear”. The verse comes each time at a meal, among people who must not overhear: Telemachus speaks to Athena, in the likeness of Mentes, while the suitors feast (*Odyssey* 1.156–157); to Peisistratus at Menelaus' table (*Odyssey* 4.69–70); and Eumaeus speaks to him among the suitors before he goes back to his swine (*Odyssey* 17.591–592). See [[telemachus]].
