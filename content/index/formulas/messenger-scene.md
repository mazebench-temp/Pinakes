## The pattern

The sender gives the message in direct speech and sends the messengers off. They go (βάτην), arrive (ἱκέσθην), and find the one they seek, often in the middle of something (τὸν δʼ εὗρον …). The messenger delivers the message, often in the sender's exact words. The addressee answers, and the messengers go back.

{{notes}}

## Elsewhere in Homer

The embassy of Book 9 arrives in the same words: “they came to the huts and ships of the Myrmidons, and found him” delighting his heart with the lyre (Iliad 9.185–186). Its walk along the shore is told in dual verbs (9.182), though the envoys are more than two. The duals are a much-discussed puzzle.
