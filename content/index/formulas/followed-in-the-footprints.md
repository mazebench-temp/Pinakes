## The pattern

ὣς ἄρα φωνήσασʼ ἡγήσατο Παλλὰς Ἀθήνη / καρπαλίμως· ὁ δʼ ἔπειτα μετʼ ἴχνια βαῖνε θεοῖο, “So she spoke, and Pallas Athena led the way / swiftly; and he followed in the footprints of the goddess” (*Odyssey* 2.405–406). The goddess speaks and leads; the mortal walks in her tracks (ἴχνια, “footprints”). The couplet is used four times: Athena, in the likeness of Mentor, leads Telemachus to the ship in Ithaca and to the gathering of the Pylians (*Odyssey* 2.405–406, 3.29–30); Calypso, with δῖα θεάων, “brilliant among goddesses,” in the place of Pallas Athena, leads Odysseus to her cave after promising to help him home (*Odyssey* 5.192–194; [[brilliant-among-goddesses]]); and Athena, in the likeness of a young girl, leads Odysseus through the town of the Phaeacians to the palace of Alcinous (*Odyssey* 7.37–38). The first half of the first verse, ὣς ἄρα φωνήσας ἡγήσατο, is used of men too, as of Telemachus leading his crew a few verses later (*Odyssey* 2.413; [[so-he-spoke]]). See [[athena]] and [[pallas]].
