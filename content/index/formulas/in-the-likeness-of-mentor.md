## The pattern

Μέντορι εἰδομένη, “making herself like Mentor,” the participle of εἴδομαι with the dative of the one she resembles ([[eidos]]), + ἠμὲν δέμας ἠδὲ καὶ αὐδήν, “both in form and in voice,” two accusatives of respect. The verse says that the likeness is complete: body and voice are those of the man to whom Odysseus entrusted his house when he sailed ([[mentor]]). Athena's other disguises are told in other words: as the Taphian Mentes (*Odyssey* 1.105), as Telemachus himself (*Odyssey* 2.383), and as a young girl carrying a pitcher in the town of the Phaeacians (*Odyssey* 7.19–20).

It is used five times. In *Odyssey* 2 it brings the goddess to Telemachus on the shore after his prayer, and at night to call him to the ship (*Odyssey* 2.268, 2.401). In Book 22 she comes in the same verse into the hall where Odysseus is fighting the suitors (*Odyssey* 22.205–206), and in Book 24 to the fight with the suitors' kinsmen, and again in the last verse of the poem, when she has set oaths between the two sides (*Odyssey* 24.502–503, 24.546–548). See [[athena]], [[divine-epiphany]] and [[divine-intervention]].
