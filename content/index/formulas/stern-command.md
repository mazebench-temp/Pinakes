## The pattern

κρατερόν (“strong, harsh”) + μῦθον (“word, command”) + ἐπιτέλλω (“enjoin, lay upon,” here split in tmesis as ἐπὶ … ἔτελλε): “and he laid a harsh word upon him.” μῦθος in Homer is often an authoritative utterance: an order, a threat, a formal speech. The half-line follows a verb of sending away and says how it was done.

The formula is Agamemnon's, and it marks the two acts that cause the book's two disasters: the dismissal of the priest, which brings the plague, and the dispatch of the heralds to take Briseis, which brings Achilles' withdrawal ([[messenger-scene]]). The whole verse ἀλλὰ κακῶς ἀφίει, κρατερὸν δ’ ἐπὶ μῦθον ἔτελλε returns word for word in Achilles' account to Thetis ({{ref:Iliad 1.379}}; [[retelling]]), where it stands for the whole of Agamemnon's threat.
