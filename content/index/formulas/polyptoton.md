## The pattern

πολύπτωτον (“in many cases”) is the rhetoricians' term for repeating a word in different cases or forms. It binds clauses together, and the repeated word carries the emphasis.

## In Book 1

{{quote:4b566c6a-fe52-52b8-8002-35217903558e}}

Nestor repeats κάρτιστος (“mightiest”) three times in two verses, twice in the nominative and once in the dative: κάρτιστοι δὴ κεῖνοι … / κάρτιστοι μὲν ἔσαν καὶ καρτίστοις ἐμάχοντο. Since the first two stand at the head of consecutive lines, the figure is also [[anaphora]]. The heroes were the mightiest of men and they fought the mightiest of foes; the repetition makes their greatness mutual ([[catalogue-of-lapiths]]).

Agamemnon's complaint about Achilles repeats πᾶς four times in three verses, in the genitive and in two forms of the dative: περὶ πάντων, πάντων, πάντεσσι, πᾶσι ({{ref:Iliad 1.287-289}}). The word “all” comes to stand for Achilles' ambition as Agamemnon sees it.
