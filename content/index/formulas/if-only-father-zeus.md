## The pattern

αἲ γάρ, “if only”, the particle of a wish, with the optative, and three gods named in the vocative: “father Zeus and Athena and Apollo”. The line has the invocation of a prayer, but what follows is a wish, not a request, and no god is asked to act (see [[prayer]]). The three gods are the greatest powers a speaker can call on, the father and the two children closest to him, and the wishes they introduce are large: that a lost strength or a lost friend might return, or that the impossible might happen.

## In Homer

- Agamemnon wishes that he had ten counselors like Nestor, for then Priam's city would soon fall (2.371–374), and, in nearly the same words, that all the army had the spirit of the two Ajaxes (4.288–291). See [[prayer-type-scene]].
- Nestor wishes that he were young, as when he fought Ereuthalion (7.132–133; see [[nestor-and-ereuthalion]]).
- Achilles wishes that every Trojan and every Argive might die, and he and Patroclus alone take the sacred battlements of Troy (16.97–100).
- In the Odyssey Menelaus wishes twice that Odysseus might come among the suitors as he was when he wrestled on Lesbos (Odyssey 4.341, 17.132), Alcinous that such a man as Odysseus might marry his daughter (Odyssey 7.311), Telemachus that the suitors might hang their heads beaten in the palace (Odyssey 18.235), and old Laertes that he were as he was when he took Nericus (Odyssey 24.376).

Most of the wishes look back to a strength that is gone or forward to what will not be. Agamemnon's two wishes are the ones that praise men who are present.
