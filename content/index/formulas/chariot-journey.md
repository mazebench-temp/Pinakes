## The pattern

1. **Yoking.** The horses are led under the yoke, and the chariot is made ready; for a god, the horses, the chariot and the driver's gear are described. Zeus and Poseidon yoke their bronze-hoofed horses with golden manes, put on gold, and take a golden whip, in nearly the same four verses (8.41–44, 13.23–26).
2. **The drive.** The driver takes the reins and the whip: μάστιξεν δʼ ἐλάαν, τὼ δʼ οὐκ ἀέκοντε πετέσθην, “and whipped them to a run, and the pair flew on not unwilling” (5.366, 8.45, 22.400; Odyssey 3.484, 3.494), or with ἵππους, “the horses”, in place of ἐλάαν (5.768, 10.530, 11.519). οὐκ ἀέκοντε is an understatement, “not unwilling”, for “gladly” (see [[litotes]]).
3. **The way.** Mortal horses cross the plain; the gods' horses fly “between earth and the starry sky” (5.769 = 8.46), or over the sea without wetting the axle (13.27–30).
4. **Arrival.** “There he halted the horses” (ἔνθʼ ἵππους ἔστησε), “unyoked them from the chariot” (λύσας ἐξ ὀχέων), and then set ambrosial fodder before them (5.368–369, 13.34–35) or poured a thick mist around them (5.775–776, 8.49–50).

Mortals drive in the same steps: Priam yokes his horses and drives with Antenor to the oath (3.259–263), and in the Odyssey Telemachus and Nestor's son Peisistratus drive from Pylos to Lacedaemon in two days, whipping on their horses in the verse of the gods' journeys (Odyssey 3.475–497).
