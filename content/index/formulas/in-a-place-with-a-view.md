## The pattern

περίσκεπτος = περί (“around”) + the root of σκέπτομαι (“look about”): a place from which one looks round, or which is seen from all round. Merry takes it of a site “with a clear view round it,” not necessarily on high ground, and finds the explanation in the next verse of the passage on Eumaeus' courtyard, which is περίδρομος, “that can be run round”; he compares ἐν περιφαινομένῳ (*Odyssey* 5.476) and ἐν καθαρῷ, “in a clear space” (*Iliad* 8.491), and rejects Döderlein's “well-sheltered,” as if the word came from σκεπάζω, “cover” (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 1.425).

The half-verse ends the line four times, all in the Odyssey, always of a building: Telemachus' high chamber in the courtyard (*Odyssey* 1.425–426), Circe's house of polished stone in the glens, twice, in the narrative and in Eurylochus' report (*Odyssey* 10.210–211, 10.252–253), and the courtyard, built high, of the swineherd Eumaeus (*Odyssey* 14.5–7).
