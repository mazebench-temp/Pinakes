## The pattern

δαίνυντ’, οὐδέ τι θυμὸς ἐδεύετο δαιτὸς ἐΐσης = “they feasted, and no heart lacked an equal feast.” δαίς (“feast, portion”) is from δαίομαι (“divide, apportion”): a feast is a division into shares. ἐΐση (“equal, fair”; see [[balanced-ships]]) says that each received his due portion. The equal feast is the community's picture of justice in the distribution of goods. Zeus uses the phrase of the sacrifices Troy has always offered him: “my altar never lacked an equal share of the feast” (Il. 4.48, 24.69).
