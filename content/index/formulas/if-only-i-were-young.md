## The pattern

εἴθε, “if only,” the particle of a wish, + ὥς, “so, like that,” + ἡβώοιμι, the optative of ἡβάω, “be in the bloom of youth” (ἥβη; see [[hebe]]), + βίη δέ μοι ἔμπεδος εἴη, “and my strength were steadfast.” The ὥς looks to the time of the old strength, which the next verse names, “as when” (ὡς ὁπότε, ὡς ὅτε), and the story of a deed follows.

Nestor speaks the verse three times in the Iliad, twice after saying that his strength is not what it was (11.668–669, 23.627–628). It closes his story of the duel with Ereuthalion, which began with a wish of another form, “If only, father Zeus and Athena and Apollo, / I were young” (7.132–133, 7.157; see [[if-only-father-zeus]] and [[nestor-and-ereuthalion]]); it opens his story of the cattle raid on the Eleans, told to Patroclus (11.670–672; see [[nestor-and-the-epeians]]); and it opens his story of the funeral games of Amarynceus, told to Achilles at the games of Patroclus, with βίη τέ μοι for βίη δέ μοι (23.629–630; see [[the-funeral-games-of-amarynceus]]). At the review Agamemnon wishes the old man the second half of it: “so your knees might follow, and your strength be steadfast” (4.313–314; see [[old-age-and-memory]] and [[nestor]]).
