## The pattern

αὐτὰρ ἐπεί (“but when”) + πόσιος καὶ ἐδητύος (“of drink and of food”) + ἐξ ἔρον ἕντο (“they had sent away the desire”: ἐξίημι in tmesis, with ἔρος, “desire,” a form of the word ἔρως). It is one of the commonest lines in Homer. It closes the eating whenever a meal is described, and it moves the story on to what follows: drinking, talk, song, or action.

## In Book 1

{{quote:67088b24-5e40-5999-96a9-458152a4aae0}}

At Chryse the line follows the feast after the sacrifice and leads into the drinking and the paean. The young men fill the mixing bowls, serve everyone, and first pour drops for the gods ([[feast-type-scene]], [[sacrifice-type-scene]]). It comes right after [[equal-feast]], and the two lines form the standard close of a sacrificial meal; the same pair returns at Agamemnon's sacrifice in Book 2.
