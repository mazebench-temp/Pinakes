## The pattern

The five verses run in a fixed order, the washing first and the food after: a handmaid brings water for the hands (χέρνιψ) and pours it from a golden pitcher over a silver basin, “for them to wash”; she draws up a polished table; the revered housekeeper (ταμίη) brings bread and sets it before the guests; and she adds many dainties, “giving freely of what she had” (*Odyssey* 1.136–140). Merry notes the manner of the washing, the guest holding his hands over the basin while water is poured on them, and takes the dainties for what the housekeeper has by her for a guest who comes unexpected, perhaps what was left from an earlier meal (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 1.138 and 1.140).

The block is found six times, always in the Odyssey and always when a guest has just been seated: Athena in the likeness of Mentes at Odysseus' house (*Odyssey* 1.136–140), Telemachus and Peisistratus at Menelaus' house in Sparta, on their arrival and again before they leave (*Odyssey* 4.52–56, 15.135–139), Odysseus at the house of Alcinous and in the house of Circe (*Odyssey* 7.172–176, 10.368–372), and the seer Theoclymenus at Telemachus' house (*Odyssey* 17.91–95). The bread verse is used once alone (*Odyssey* 17.259). In the Iliad the service has no fixed verses; a handmaid holds basin and pitcher for Priam before his libation (24.302–304; [[basin-and-pitcher]]).
