## The pattern

μέγα (“greatly”) + ὀχθήσας, aorist participle of ὀχθέω (“be vexed, troubled”). It takes the place of ἀπαμειβόμενος in the reply formula ([[answering-him]]): τὴν δὲ μέγ’ ὀχθήσας προσέφη νεφεληγερέτα Ζεύς. The same whole line introduces Zeus' reply to Hera in Book 4 (Il. 4.30).

## In Book 1

{{quote:d8681153-b344-5ce5-9178-cbe3fdcaddc3}}

Zeus has sat a long time in silence, and Thetis has clung to his knees and asked again ([[supplication-type-scene]]). His trouble is not about her request but about Hera. The verb returns fifty lines later for the whole company of the gods: ὄχθησαν δ’ ἀνὰ δῶμα Διὸς θεοὶ Οὐρανίωνες, “and throughout the house of Zeus the heavenly gods were troubled” ([[heavenly-gods]], {{ref:Iliad 1.570}}). What troubled Zeus alone now troubles all of Olympus.
