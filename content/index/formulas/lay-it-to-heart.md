## The pattern

ἄλλο δέ τοι ἐρέω (“I will tell you something else”) + σὺ δ’ ἐνὶ φρεσὶ βάλλεο σῇσι (“and you, put it in your mind”): the speaker marks what follows as important and aims it directly at the listener. The line recurs many times in Homer. Its most memorable use is Patroclus' last words to Hector, a prophecy of Hector's own death (Il. 16.851).

## In Book 1

{{quote:38074ae3-5eff-5a65-a0ed-6a64f8a1e9cb}}

Achilles uses it to turn from refusal to threat. He will not fight with his hands for the girl, since those who gave her are taking her back. But let Agamemnon try to take anything else from beside his ship, and his dark blood will flow around Achilles' spear ({{ref:Iliad 1.301-303}}). The formula marks the line Achilles draws: the girl he gives up, nothing more.
