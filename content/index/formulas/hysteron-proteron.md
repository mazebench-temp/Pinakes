## The pattern

ὕστερον πρότερον (“the later first”) is the name for reversing the natural order of events, so that the more important or more recent comes first. Homer does it with pairs of verbs, as here, and on a larger scale in the order of answers to questions: in the Odyssey, Odysseus' mother answers his questions in the reverse of the order in which he asked them (Od. 11.170–203).

## In Book 1

{{quote:dd9be6f9-e43e-59b3-9093-e57b2633665e}}

τράφεν ἠδ’ ἐγένοντο says that the men of Nestor's generation were raised and born with him. The emphasis falls on the upbringing they shared, and birth comes second. Unless ἐγένοντο is taken in a weaker sense (“lived, came of age”), the natural order is reversed. The translation restores it: “who had been born and raised with him.”

## Outside Homer

Cicero knew the figure as Homeric. He opens a letter to Atticus by promising to answer his questions ὕστερον πρότερον, Ὁμηρικῶς, “the later first, in Homer's way” (*Letters to Atticus* 1.16.1).
