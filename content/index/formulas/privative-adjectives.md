## The pattern

The prefix ἀ- (ἀν- before a vowel), the “alpha privative,” negates the word it forms, like English “un-.” Homer likes to set two or three such words together, often without a conjunction, so that each negation strengthens the next. The classic example is Nestor's description of the man who loves civil strife: ἀφρήτωρ ἀθέμιστος ἀνέστιος, “without clan, without law, without hearth” (Il. 9.63).
