## The pattern

ἔπος τ’ ἔφατ’ (“and spoke a word”) + ἔκ τ’ ὀνόμαζε (“and called out by name,” ἐξονομάζω in tmesis): the speaker addresses the other by name, a sign of closeness and urgency. With a gesture before it, it makes a whole line: χειρί τέ μιν κατέρεξεν ἔπος τ’ ἔφατ’ ἔκ τ’ ὀνόμαζε, “she stroked him with her hand, and spoke to him, and called him by name.” That line recurs for Dione comforting Aphrodite (Il. 5.372), for Hector with Andromache (Il. 6.485), and for Thetis with Achilles again in the last book (Il. 24.127).

## In Book 1

{{quote:23fccda7-011b-5bdb-b43b-c86635aef55a}}

Thetis sits before her weeping son, strokes him, and speaks. The gesture and the naming together make this the one scene of tenderness in the book. The same line brings her to him again in Book 24, near the end of his story. Her words, “speak out, do not hide it,” are themselves a formula of grief ([[speak-out]]).
