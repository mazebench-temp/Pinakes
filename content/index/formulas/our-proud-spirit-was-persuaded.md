## The pattern

The first half of the verse closes the speech, ὣς ἔφατʼ, ὣς ἔφαν, ὣς ἐφάμην ([[so-he-spoke]]), and says whose spirit it was: ἡμῖν δʼ αὖτʼ, “our,” αὐτὰρ ἐμοί γʼ, “my,” τοῖσιν δʼ, “their.” The second half does not change: ἐπεπείθετο, the imperfect of ἐπιπείθομαι, “let oneself be persuaded, obey” ([[peitho]]), and θυμὸς ἀγήνωρ, the “proud spirit” that in the Iliad drives a man to dare ([[proud]], [[thymos]]). Here the proud spirit yields (see the Odyssey translation's formulas).

## Use

The verse belongs to the Odyssey alone, and every time it is spoken by someone telling a story of his own:

- **Penelope's web.** Antinous tells the assembly how the suitors let Penelope weave the shroud for Laertes: “So she spoke, and our proud spirit was persuaded” (*Odyssey* 2.103). Penelope, who did the persuading, tells the same story to the stranger, ὣς ἐφάμην, τοῖσιν δʼ (*Odyssey* 19.148), and the soul of Amphimedon tells it once more among the dead (*Odyssey* 24.138; [[the-web-of-penelope]]).
- **Circe's island.** In *Odyssey* 10 the verse marks four turns of the stay on Aeaea: Odysseus obeys Circe's bidding to fetch his companions from the ship (*Odyssey* 10.406); they all yield to her invitation to eat and take heart again (*Odyssey* 10.466); after a year Odysseus yields to his companions, who remind him of home (*Odyssey* 10.475); and they yield to him when he wakes them for the voyage (*Odyssey* 10.550). Circe persuades them once more when they come back from the house of Hades (*Odyssey* 12.28).
- **Thrinacia.** Odysseus persuades his companions, for a time, to keep their hands from the cattle of the Sun (*Odyssey* 12.324; [[the-cattle-of-helios]]).
