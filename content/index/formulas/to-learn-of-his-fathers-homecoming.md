## The pattern

νόστον, “homecoming” ([[nostos]]), + πευσόμενος, the future participle of πυνθάνομαι, “learn by asking, hear of,” which gives the purpose, + πατρός, “of his father,” with δὴν οἰχομένοιο, “who has long been gone,” or φίλου, ἤν που ἀκούσῃ, “dear, if he may hear of it somewhere,” to finish the verse. Athena gives it first, in the council of the gods and then to Telemachus himself, the second time without νόστον, “and go to learn of your father, who has long been gone” (*Odyssey* 1.94, 1.281). Telemachus repeats it to the assembly, in his prayer and to Eurycleia (*Odyssey* 2.215, 2.264, 2.360), and gives it to Theoclymenus at Pylos as the reason for his voyage (*Odyssey* 15.270). Athena tells Odysseus the same of his son with κλέος in the place of the father: he went “to learn of your fame” (*Odyssey* 13.415; [[kleos]]). See [[the-journey-of-telemachus]] and [[telemachus]].
