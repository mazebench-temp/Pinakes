## The pattern

Three participles of εἰμί (“be”), each with the article: τὰ ἐόντα (“what is”), τὰ ἐσσόμενα (“what will be”), πρὸ ἐόντα (“what was before”). The order, present, future, past, is not chronological. Together the three express complete knowledge, as a polar expression expresses a whole.

{{notes}}

## Outside Homer

Hesiod's Muses sing “the things that are, the things that will be, and the things that were before” in the same verse (*Theogony* 38), and they breathe into the poet a divine voice to celebrate “the things that will be and the things that were before” (32).
