## The pattern

Three participles of εἰμί (“be”), each with the article: τὰ ἐόντα (“what is”), τὰ ἐσσόμενα (“what will be”), πρὸ ἐόντα (“what was before”). The order, present, future, past, is not chronological. Together the three express complete knowledge, as a polar expression expresses a whole.

## In Book 1

{{quote:3bb2e669-e8d6-58dc-927f-2480e171a854}}

The line gives Calchas his authority before he speaks ([[best-of-bird-seers]], [[with-good-intent]]). Its subject is time, and the scene depends on it: Calchas explains the past (the dishonoring of Chryses) to account for the present (the plague) and to prescribe the future (the return of Chryseis and a hecatomb).

The seer's knowledge is set against the king's blindness. Achilles says that Agamemnon “does not know how to look both before and after” ([[before-and-after]], {{ref:Iliad 1.343}}).

## Outside Homer

Hesiod's Muses sing “the things that are, the things that will be, and the things that were before” in the same verse (*Theogony* 38), and they breathe into the poet a divine voice to celebrate “the things that will be and the things that were before” (32).
