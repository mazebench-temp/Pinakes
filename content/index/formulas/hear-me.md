## The pattern

κλῦθι, aorist imperative of κλύω (“hear”), + μευ, the epic form of μου (“me”): “hear me.” The god's name or epithet follows in the vocative, then usually a relative clause naming his places or powers ([[prayer-type-scene]]). Homeric prayers open this way elsewhere; Diomedes' prayer to Athena begins κλῦθί μευ, αἰγιόχοιο Διὸς τέκος (Il. 5.115).

The close repeats the verb in the narrative: ὣς ἔφατ’ εὐχόμενος, τοῦ δ’ ἔκλυε …, “so he spoke in prayer, and (the god) heard him.” The same frame can report a refusal instead: when the Trojan women pray to Athena, “Pallas Athena shook her head” (ἀνένευε, Il. 6.311).
