## The pattern

κλῦθι, aorist imperative of κλύω (“hear”), + μευ, the epic form of μου (“me”): “hear me.” The god's name or epithet follows in the vocative, then usually a relative clause naming his places or powers ([[prayer-type-scene]]). Homeric prayers open this way elsewhere; Diomedes' prayer to Athena begins κλῦθί μευ, αἰγιόχοιο Διὸς τέκος (Il. 5.115).

The close repeats the verb in the narrative: ὣς ἔφατ’ εὐχόμενος, τοῦ δ’ ἔκλυε …, “so he spoke in prayer, and (the god) heard him.” The same frame can report a refusal instead: when the Trojan women pray to Athena, “Pallas Athena shook her head” (ἀνένευε, Il. 6.311).

## In Book 1

{{quote:cb648778-c4a3-5b32-959e-f8651f753f3b}}

Both of Chryses' prayers open with the same two lines and close with the same line ({{ref:Iliad 1.451-452}}, {{ref:Iliad 1.457}}), so that the whole story of the plague is enclosed by the formula ([[ring-composition]]). The first prayer asks for the Danaans to be punished, the second for their relief, and the god hears both ([[god-of-the-silver-bow]]).

The same close marks Achilles' appeal to his mother: ὣς φάτο δάκρυ χέων, τοῦ δ’ ἔκλυε πότνια μήτηρ, “so he spoke, shedding tears, and his lady mother heard him” ({{ref:Iliad 1.357}}). The son's appeal to Thetis is framed as a prayer, and it is heard.
