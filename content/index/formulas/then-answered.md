## The pattern

τὸν (τὴν) δ’ ἠμείβετ’ ἔπειτα = “and to him (her) then answered”: ἠμείβετο, imperfect of ἀμείβομαι (“answer, reply”), + ἔπειτα (“then”). The phrase fills the verse up to the caesura in the middle of the third foot, and the second half of the line takes the speaker's name in a phrase of the right length. Because the -α of ἔπειτα stands before a word that once began with a consonant, it is not elided before ἄναξ (see [[lord-of-men]]).

Book 1 fills the slot with five different speakers: ποδάρκης δῖος Ἀχιλλεύς ([[podarkes]], 121), ἄναξ ἀνδρῶν Ἀγαμέμνων ([[lord-of-men]], 172), Θέτις κατὰ δάκρυ χέουσα (“Thetis, letting her tears fall,” 413; [[shedding-tears]]), πατὴρ ἀνδρῶν τε θεῶν τε ([[father-of-men-and-gods]], 544), and βοῶπις πότνια Ἥρη ([[ox-eyed]], 551). The companion frame for replies is [[answering-him]].

Two of the speakers are the antagonists of the assembly and three belong to the divine story; the frame serves both alike. At 413 the slot is filled not by a noun-epithet formula but by a name and a participle of grief, a sign that the system bends to the moment.

A variant replaces ἔπειτα with ὑποβλήδην (“interrupting”): τὸν δ’ ἄρ’ ὑποβλήδην ἠμείβετο δῖος Ἀχιλλεύς, “breaking in on him, brilliant Achilles answered” ({{ref:Iliad 1.292}}). Achilles cuts Agamemnon off before he has finished, and the formula records the discourtesy.
