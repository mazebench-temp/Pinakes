## The pattern

τὸν (or τὴν) δ’ ἀπαμειβόμενος προσέφη = “and to him (her), answering, he said”: ἀπαμείβομαι (“reply”) + προσέφη (“spoke to”). The phrase fills the verse up to the caesura in the fourth foot. The rest is filled by a noun-epithet formula for the speaker, of just the right length, such as πόδας ὠκὺς Ἀχιλλεύς ([[swift-footed]]), κρείων Ἀγαμέμνων ([[powerful]]), or νεφεληγερέτα Ζεύς ([[cloud-gatherer]]).

The pronoun changes with the addressee, and the participle can change with the speaker's mood, keeping the frame: τὸν δ’ ἄρ’ ὑπόδρα ἰδὼν προσέφη ([[looking-darkly]]), τὴν δὲ μέγ’ ὀχθήσας προσέφη ([[deeply-troubled]]), τὴν δὲ βαρὺ στενάχων προσέφη ([[groaning-heavily]]). A related opening is τὸν δ’ αὖτε προσέειπε, “then … spoke to him in turn” ({{ref:Iliad 1.206}}). The other common frame of reply is [[then-answered]].
