## The pattern

τὸν (or τὴν) δ’ ἀπαμειβόμενος προσέφη = “and to him (her), answering, he said”: ἀπαμείβομαι (“reply”) + προσέφη (“spoke to”). The phrase fills the verse up to the caesura in the fourth foot. The rest is filled by a noun-epithet formula for the speaker, of just the right length. Book 1 shows three: πόδας ὠκὺς Ἀχιλλεύς ([[swift-footed]]), κρείων Ἀγαμέμνων ([[powerful]]), and νεφεληγερέτα Ζεύς ([[cloud-gatherer]]).

The pronoun changes with the addressee, and the participle can change with the speaker's mood, keeping the frame: τὸν δ’ ἄρ’ ὑπόδρα ἰδὼν προσέφη ([[looking-darkly]]), τὴν δὲ μέγ’ ὀχθήσας προσέφη ([[deeply-troubled]]), τὴν δὲ βαρὺ στενάχων προσέφη ([[groaning-heavily]]). A related opening is τὸν δ’ αὖτε προσέειπε, “then … spoke to him in turn” ({{ref:Iliad 1.206}}). The other common frame of reply is [[then-answered]].

The same line brings in Achilles in the assembly and Zeus on Olympus; only the name at the end changes. The quarrel among men and the quarrel among gods are told in the same language. In the assembly the formula alternates between the two antagonists, Achilles (84, 215) and Agamemnon (130, 285).
