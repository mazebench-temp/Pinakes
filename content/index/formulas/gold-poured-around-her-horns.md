## The pattern

The vow follows the request of a prayer. σοὶ δʼ αὖ ἐγὼ ῥέξω, “and I in turn will sacrifice to you,” marks the exchange, the god's favor for the victim ([[reciprocity-with-the-gods]]), and the victim is named with three epithets, βοῦν ἦνιν εὐρυμέτωπον, / ἀδμήτην, “a yearling heifer, broad of brow, / unbroken” ([[yearling]], [[broad-browed]], [[unbroken]]). The third verse promises the costliest form of the offering: τήν τοι ἐγὼ ῥέξω χρυσὸν κέρασιν περιχεύας. περιχεύας is “spreading,” of gold foil laid around the horns, not of molten metal (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 3.384 and 3.437; see the Odyssey's translation note on 3.384, 3.437).

{{quote:22403eed-4545-5df6-a313-cfcc580aa9d1}}

The verses are spoken twice, both times to Athena, and both prayers are answered in nearly the same verse, ὣς ἔφατʼ εὐχόμενος, τοῦ δʼ ἔκλυε Παλλὰς Ἀθήνη, in the Iliad with the plural, for Odysseus has prayed too (*Iliad* 10.295, *Odyssey* 3.385; [[hear-me]]). Diomedes vows them as he and Odysseus set out by night among the Trojans, and the Iliad does not tell the fulfillment (*Iliad* 10.283–295). Nestor vows them at Pylos when he has seen the goddess leave in the likeness of a sea eagle (*Odyssey* 3.380–385), and the vow is kept the next morning: the goldsmith Laerces spreads the gold on the horns, “so that the goddess might rejoice to see the offering” (*Odyssey* 3.425–438; [[the-sacrifice-to-athena]], [[laerces-the-goldsmith]]). The Trojan women's vow in *Iliad* 6 is another formula, twelve yearling cows “never touched by the goad,” and Athena refuses it (*Iliad* 6.93–94, 6.308–311; [[never-touched-by-the-goad]]).
