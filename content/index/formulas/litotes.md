## The pattern

λιτότης (“plainness, simplicity”) is the rhetoricians' term for affirming something by denying its opposite. The negative statement often says more than a direct one would: “not glad” can mean “bitterly displeased.”

## In Book 1

{{quote:221d5bfe-ef2e-59c2-bc22-f7efe4a25033}}

The narrator's first word on Agamemnon's anger is a litotes: “this did not please the heart of Agamemnon, son of Atreus.” What follows shows how much the understatement held back: a harsh dismissal and a threat ([[stern-command]]). The line returns in Achilles' retelling ({{ref:Iliad 1.378}}).

{{quote:403e9390-3318-5084-8398-734b0c2ff40f}}

The heralds find Achilles by his ship, and “he was not glad to see them.” The understatement lets the scene stay quiet; Achilles' feelings come out only in his courteous speech to them ([[messenger-scene]]).

Other cases in the book:

- Achilles “did not disobey the word of Athena” (οὐδ’ ἀπίθησε, {{ref:Iliad 1.220-221}}): full obedience, reluctantly given.
- Thetis “did not forget the requests of her son” (οὐ λήθετ’, {{ref:Iliad 1.495-496}}): she remembered and went up to Olympus at dawn.
- Hera “was not unaware” that Thetis had been making plans with Zeus (οὐδέ μιν … ἠγνοίησεν, {{ref:Iliad 1.536-538}}): she knew exactly, and said so at once.
