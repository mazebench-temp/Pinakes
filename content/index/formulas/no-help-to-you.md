## The pattern

χραισμέω means “help, ward off, be of use.” Book 1 builds two patterns on it:

- μή νύ τοι οὐ χραίσμῃ …, “lest … be of no help to you”: a threat that the protection someone counts on will fail;
- τότε δ’ οὔ τι δυνήσεαι (δυνήσομαι) ἀχνύμενός περ / χραισμεῖν, “then, for all your (my) grief, you (I) will be powerless to help”: a prediction of helpless sorrow, running over the end of the verse.

## In Book 1

Agamemnon tells the priest that his scepter and the god's fillet may not protect him. Zeus tells his wife that all the gods on Olympus may be no help to her. The king of men and the king of gods threaten in the same construction.

{{quote:2aaccd36-6875-5b46-8557-6e36049144fe}}

{{quote:82b871c9-1a78-5cd3-8211-70a87cdec135}}

The second pair is closer still. Achilles tells Agamemnon that when the Achaeans are dying at Hector's hands, he will be powerless to help them, for all his grief. Hephaestus tells his mother that if Zeus strikes her, he will be powerless to help her, for all his grief. Almost the same words bind the human and the divine quarrels together, as do [[the-worse-for-you]] and [[a-sorry-business]] ([[ring-composition]]).
