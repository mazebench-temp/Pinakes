## The pattern

A full supplication has four parts.

1. **Approach.** The suppliant comes to the one who can help and sits or kneels before him.
2. **Gesture.** The suppliant takes hold of him: clasps his knees (λάβε γούνων) and often touches his chin (ὑπʼ ἀνθερεῶνος). Contact turns a request into a ritual claim, which the gods watch over.
3. **Appeal.** The speech states a claim, often a past service (“if ever I helped you”), and then the request. The verb is λίσσομαι, “beg, entreat”.
4. **Answer.** The one supplicated must accept or refuse.

What the gestures mean is debated. On one view the knees hold a man's strength, and to clasp them is to hold him fast. The chin belongs to the head that nods or refuses. Thetis holds Zeus by the chin and asks him to “nod your assent” (κατάνευσον, {{ref:Iliad 1.514}}); he answers with a nod ({{ref:Iliad 1.528}}). See [[supplication]] and [[gounata]].

## Outside Book 1

Athena recalls the scene: Thetis “kissed his knees and took his chin in her hand” (Iliad 8.370–372). In the last book Priam, another father who comes with a ransom, clasps Achilles' knees and kisses his hands, and is heard (Iliad 24.477–479; see [[book-1-and-book-24]]).
