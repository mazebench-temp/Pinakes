## The pattern

ἐπανάληψις, “taking up again,” is a later rhetorical term for the resumption of a word or phrase. Walter Leaf uses it for the repetition of a whole phrase across the verse-end, and notes that Homer has it only three times (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 20.371):

- Hector, answering Achilles' threats: “I will go against him, even if his hands are like fire, / if his hands are like fire, and his might like gleaming iron” (20.371–372).
- Hector, waiting for Achilles before the walls, knows that he cannot talk with him now as a girl and a young man talk together, παρθένος ἠΐθεός τε / παρθένος ἠΐθεός τʼ ὀαρίζετον ἀλλήλοιιν (22.127–128).
- Nestor, remembering the funeral games for Amarynceus, tells how the twin sons of Actor beat him in the chariot race: one held the reins steady, held them steady, and the other plied the whip (ἔμπεδον ἡνιόχευεν, / ἔμπεδον ἡνιόχευʼ, 23.629–642; [[the-moliones]]).

Twice it is Hector who speaks it, and both times of Achilles (see [[hands-like-fire]]). Compare [[anaphora]], where successive verses begin with the same word.
