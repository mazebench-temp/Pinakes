## The pattern

ἐξαύδα (“speak out,” imperative of ἐξαυδάω) + μὴ κεῦθε νόῳ (“do not hide it in your mind”) + ἵνα εἴδομεν ἄμφω (“so that we may both know”). The line follows a question about the listener's grief: τέκνον, τί κλαίεις; τί δέ σε φρένας ἵκετο πένθος; (“child, why do you weep? What grief has come upon your heart?”).

## In Book 1

{{quote:19791026-55d9-51c8-8a09-d60adef79847}}

The two lines return in Book 18, when Thetis comes to Achilles again, after the death of Patroclus (Il. 18.73–74). In Book 16 Achilles himself speaks the second line to Patroclus, who stands weeping before him for the Achaeans (Il. 16.19). The formula joins three scenes of grief: the loss of Briseis, the ruin of the army, and the loss of Patroclus. See also [[called-him-by-name]] and [[groaning-heavily]].
