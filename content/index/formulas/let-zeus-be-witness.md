## The pattern

ἴστω, “let him know,” the imperative of οἶδα, + νῦν, “now,” + Ζεύς: the god is told to know what is sworn, and so to witness it and to punish a false oath. The verse names Zeus and then the other witnesses, and the terms follow in the next verse, with μή and the indicative, “that … not,” or with ἦ μέν and the future, “truly … will” (see [[oath-formulas]] and [[oath]]).

In the Iliad Zeus is named with his titles. Agamemnon grants the Trojans their truce for the dead with “As for the oaths, let Zeus be witness, the loud-thundering husband of Hera” (7.411; see [[loud-thundering]]), and Hector swears in nearly the same verse that Dolon shall have the horses of Achilles: “Now let Zeus himself be witness, the loud-thundering husband of Hera, / that no other man shall ride behind those horses” (10.329–330). Agamemnon swears that he never touched Briseis with “Now let Zeus be witness first, highest and best of the gods, / and Earth and the Sun and the Erinyes” (19.258–259; see [[most-high]] and [[erinyes]]).

In the Odyssey the oath is sworn four times, three of them by the beggar who is Odysseus himself: to Eumaeus (*Odyssey* 14.158–162), to Penelope (*Odyssey* 19.303–307) and to the cowherd Philoetius (*Odyssey* 20.230–234); the fourth is the seer [[theoclymenus|Theoclymenus]]' to Penelope (*Odyssey* 17.155–161). After Zeus come the witnesses of the place where the oath is sworn, “the table of hospitality, / and the hearth of noble Odysseus, to which I have come” (*Odyssey* 14.158–159, 17.155–156, 20.230–231; see [[table]] and [[hearth]]); to Penelope the beggar swears in the Iliad's verse, “highest and best of the gods,” with the hearth alone after it (*Odyssey* 19.303–304). Each time what is sworn is that Odysseus will come home, or is already home.

The same opening calls other witnesses: Earth, the wide Heaven and the water of Styx in Hera's oath to Zeus, whose three verses Calypso swears to Odysseus (*Iliad* 15.36–38; *Odyssey* 5.184–186; see [[styx]]).
