## The pattern

ἐμεῦ ζῶντος (“while I live”) + καὶ ἐπὶ χθονὶ δερκομένοιο (“and look upon the earth”), a genitive absolute. To see is to be alive; the dead do not see the light. The phrase makes a promise last as long as the speaker's life. In the Odyssey the suitor Eurymachus uses the same words in a false promise to Penelope (Od. 16.439).

## In Book 1

{{quote:b8f26eda-901c-5136-874d-ed2bc09fb41a}}

Achilles stakes his life on the seer's safety, and the protection extends to the king himself: “not even if you mean Agamemnon” ([[oath-formulas]]). The oath is kept. Agamemnon abuses Calchas ([[prophet-of-evils]]) but does not touch him, and he takes his revenge on Achilles instead.
