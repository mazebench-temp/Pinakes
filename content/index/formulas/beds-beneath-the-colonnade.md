## The pattern

The host gives the order, and the verses carry it out in a fixed sequence: the slave women are to set beds (δέμνια) beneath the colonnade (αἴθουσα), throw fine purple blankets (ῥήγεα) on them, spread rugs (τάπητες) over them and put woolly cloaks (χλαῖναι οὖλαι) on top for the guests to wear; and they go out from the hall with torches in their hands and spread the beds. The four verses are found three times: Achilles orders them for Priam and his herald (*Iliad* 24.643–648), Helen for Telemachus and Peisistratus at Sparta (*Odyssey* 4.296–301), and Arete for Odysseus (*Odyssey* 7.335–340). δέμνια, always plural, properly means the bedstead, but θέμεναι, “set,” shows that something portable is meant (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 4.297).

The scene ends with the sleepers in their places: the guests in the porch or under the colonnade, the master in the inner part of the house with a woman beside him, Achilles with Briseis, Menelaus with Helen, Alcinous with Arete (*Iliad* 24.673–676; *Odyssey* 4.302–305, 7.344–347). At Pylos, without the verses of the bedding, Telemachus sleeps “on a corded bed beneath the loud-resounding colonnade” and Nestor in the inner part of the high house beside his wife (*Odyssey* 3.397–403; [[corded]], [[loud-resounding]]).
