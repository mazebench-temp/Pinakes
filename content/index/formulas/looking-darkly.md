## The pattern

ὑπόδρα (“from beneath,” an adverb related to δέρκομαι, “look”) + ἰδών (“having looked”): a glance from under lowered brows, a look of anger or menace. It belongs to the reply formula τὸν δ’ ἄρ’ ὑπόδρα ἰδὼν προσέφη + the speaker ([[answering-him]]). It introduces a rebuke, usually from a speaker who feels that the other has overstepped. J. P. Holoka argued that it marks a response to a breach of status or decorum (*Transactions of the American Philological Association* 113, 1983).

## In Book 1

{{quote:61b9514d-f8f4-5b43-bf55-774d7be97a2c}}

Agamemnon has just threatened to take the prize of Achilles, Ajax, or Odysseus, and has named Achilles “most terrible of all men” ([[most-terrible-of-men]]). The glare introduces the first of Achilles' outbursts: “Ah, you clothed in shamelessness, with your mind set on gain” ([[clothed-in-shamelessness]], [[mind-set-on-gain]]).

Agamemnon has already looked at Calchas in the same spirit, κάκ’ ὀσσόμενος, “with evil in his eyes” ({{ref:Iliad 1.105}}), just after the narrator compared his eyes to blazing fire ([[eyes-like-fire]], {{ref:Iliad 1.104}}). The quarrel is carried by the eyes before it is carried by words.
