## Meaning

βλοσυρός means “grim, fierce, terrible to look at”; its origin is unknown. Homer uses it twice, and the compound βλοσυρῶπις once, always of the face. Ajax goes out to fight “smiling with grim face” (βλοσυροῖσι προσώπασι, 7.212). On Agamemnon's shield the Gorgon is βλοσυρῶπις, “grim-faced,” “glaring terribly,” with Terror and Rout around her (11.36–37; [[gorgon]]). And when Hector drives on the ships, possessed like Ares, his eyes blaze “beneath his grim brows,” and foam is at his mouth (15.605–609). The word belongs to the look that frightens an enemy. Compare [[grim]] (λυγρός), which is grievous rather than fierce.

## Use

{{quote:208c2d3f-5e6d-5f51-b9a2-67b6eace53e3}}

The smile and the grim face come together in one verse. The smile is not warmth: Ajax is confident, and it is the Trojans who tremble and Hector whose heart pounds (7.214–216). The narrator has just compared him to Ares going to war (7.208–210; [[like-huge-ares]], [[huge]]), and the grim face is the war god's. Hector's own face will be “grim” when he is the one who comes on like Ares (15.605–609). See [[ajax]] and [[the-duel-of-hector-and-ajax]].
