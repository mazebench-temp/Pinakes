## Meaning

ἐϋκνήμις = εὖ (“well”) + κνημίς (“greave,” the guard for the shin, κνήμη): “with fine greaves.” Greaves protected the lower leg below the shield. Bronze greaves are known from the Mycenaean period on. The formula ἐϋκνήμιδες Ἀχαιοί is among the commonest in the Iliad. It fills the second half of the verse after the caesura in the middle of the third foot, the same place as ἄναξ ἀνδρῶν Ἀγαμέμνων ([[lord-of-men]]).

## Use

{{quote:461a8d7d-c8c7-58cc-bf43-3c08950d372b}}

The priest addresses the whole army with its formal epithet and wishes them what they most want: to sack Priam's city and to come safely home. The courtesy is part of his plea, and the army responds to it: all the other Achaeans shout their assent ({{ref:Iliad 1.22-23}}). Only Agamemnon refuses.
