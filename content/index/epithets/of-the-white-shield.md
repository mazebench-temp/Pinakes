## Meaning

λευκός (“white, bright”) + ἀσπίς (“shield”): “with the white shield.” Homer has the word once, and does not say what made the shield white (see the translation's table of key words).

## Use

{{quote:bba0b963-8200-58fb-b541-8f30fe88ef89}}

Hector's spear has glanced off Achilles' shield, and he has no other. He calls aloud to Deiphobus, whom Athena has impersonated, and asks him for a long spear, “but he was nowhere near him” (22.291–295; [[long-spears]], [[ash-spear]]). The epithet of the brother's shield is the last word Hector gives him before he understands: “for I thought that the hero Deiphobus was beside me; / but he is within the wall, and Athena has deceived me” (22.298–299; [[truth-and-deception]]). See [[deiphobus]] and [[hector]].

## Outside Homer

In tragedy the word belongs to the Argives who came against Thebes with Polynices. The chorus of Aeschylus' *Seven against Thebes* cries out that ὁ λεύκασπις … λαός, the white-shielded host, is rushing on the city (*Seven against Thebes* 89–90), and the messenger of Euripides' *Phoenician Women* tells how the defenders saw from the towers λεύκασπιν … Ἀργείων στρατόν, the white-shielded army of the Argives, leave Teumessus (*Phoenician Women* 1099–1100).
