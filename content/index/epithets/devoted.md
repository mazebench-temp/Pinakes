## Meaning

κεδνός means “careful, trusty, dear,” of persons who can be relied on and are cherished; it is usually connected with κήδομαι, “care for, be troubled for,” though the derivation is uncertain. With the participle of οἶδα it forms the phrase κεδνὰ ἰδυῖα, literally “knowing careful things,” of a woman whose thoughts are loyal, which the translation renders “true-hearted” (see the Odyssey translation's key words).

## Use

The Iliad has it three times: Meleager's companions, “the most loyal and dearest to him of all,” beg him to fight (9.584–586); Menelaus boasts that Hyperenor, whom he killed, did not go home on his own feet to gladden his dear wife and his “devoted parents” (17.24–28); and Andromache, lamenting Hector, says that he kept safe the city's “devoted wives and little children” (24.729–730). The Odyssey gives it to a wife (*Odyssey* 1.432, 22.223), a mother (*Odyssey* 10.8), the dearest of Odysseus' companions (*Odyssey* 10.224–225), and the lord whom Eumaeus mourns (*Odyssey* 14.170). A verse of Penelope's entrances sets “a devoted handmaid” on either side of her (*Odyssey* 1.335, 18.211, 21.66). κεδνὰ ἰδυῖα is Eurycleia (*Odyssey* 1.428; in Odysseus' words, *Odyssey* 19.346) and Penelope (*Odyssey* 20.57, 23.182, 23.232).
