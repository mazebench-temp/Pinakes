## Meaning

δυσκέλαδος = δυσ- (“bad, hard”) + κέλαδος (“din, clamor”; [[clamorous]]): with an evil noise. It is formed like δυσηχής, the epithet of war and death, with δυσ- and a word for sound ([[grim-sounding]]). In Homer φόβος is not the feeling of fear but panic flight, the rout of an army ([[phobos]]), and the rout is heard as well as seen: as the Trojans flee from the ships, their “shouting and rout” come from the ships and fill all the roads (16.366, 16.373–374).

{{notes}}

## Outside Homer

In Hesiod's age of iron Envy will go with all wretched men, “ill-sounding, delighting in evil, hateful-faced” (ζῆλος … δυσκέλαδος κακόχαρτος, *Works and Days* 195–196).
