## Meaning

κυανῶπις = κύανος, κυάνεος (“dark blue, dark”; see [[kyanos]]) + ὤψ (“eye, face”): “dark-eyed,” or “dark of face.” It is formed like [[ox-eyed]] (βοῶπις), [[gray-eyed]] (γλαυκῶπις) and [[bright-eyed]] (ἑλικῶπις), and belongs with Homer's other dark compounds, [[dark-haired]], [[dark-prowed]] and the [[dark-brows]] of Zeus. Homer has it once, in Circe's account of the Wanderers: “On the one side are overhanging rocks, and against them / roars the great wave of dark-eyed Amphitrite” (*Odyssey* 12.59–60). Amphitrite is also [[loud-moaning]] in the same book (*Odyssey* 12.97). See [[amphitrite]] and [[the-wanderers]].

## Outside Homer

The Hesiodic poems give the word to maidens: to Electra among the Pleiads, “lovely Taygete and dark-eyed Electra” (*Astronomy* fr. 1 Evelyn-White, a plain rendering), and to Themistonoe, the daughter of Ceyx (*Shield* 356).
