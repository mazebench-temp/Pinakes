## Meaning

οἰνοβαρής = οἶνος (“wine”) + βαρύς (“heavy”): “weighed down with wine, drunk.” The related participle οἰνοβαρείων (“heavy with wine”) describes the drunken Cyclops in the Odyssey (Od. 9.374).

## Use

{{quote:84c191ed-85e7-5552-858c-4aafa937606a}}

After Athena has told him to fight with words instead of the sword ({{ref:Iliad 1.211}}), Achilles opens with three insults in one verse: a drunkard, with a dog's eyes and a deer's heart ([[dog-eyes-deer-heart]]). Nothing in the narrative supports the charge of drunkenness. It belongs to the language of abuse, and it prepares the charge that follows: Agamemnon has never dared to arm for battle or to lie in ambush with the best of the Achaeans.

## Outside Homer

Plato's Socrates quotes this line in the *Republic* (3.389e–390a) as an example of insolence by a private man toward his ruler, the kind of verse the young guardians of his city should not hear.
