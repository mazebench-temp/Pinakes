## Meaning

ἀταρβής = ἀ- (“not”) + τάρβος (“fear, alarm”), the noun of ταρβέω, “be afraid”: “without fear.” Homer has it once, of Rout, the son of Ares (13.299). The longer ἀτάρβητος is also found once in Homer: Paris tells Hector that his heart is always unyielding (ἀτειρής; [[unyielding]]), like an axe, “so fearless is the mind in your breast” (3.60–63; [[heart-like-an-axe]]).

## Use

{{quote:9a76f33d-c7a5-5802-aa31-48ece0fe2785}}

“And as Ares, ruin of mortals, goes into war, and Rout, his dear son, strong and fearless, follows with him, who routs even a warrior of enduring heart” (13.298–300; [[ares-and-rout]], [[rout]], [[ares]], [[stout-hearted]]). The god of rout is himself without fear, and the next verse says what he does to others: ἐφόβησε, he puts to flight even the man of enduring heart. Idomeneus and Meriones go into the war like the two gods (13.304–305; [[idomeneus]], [[meriones]]).

## Outside Homer

In the Hesiodic *Shield of Heracles* Iolaus tells Heracles that Cycnus will frighten neither “the fearless son of Zeus” nor the son of Iphicles, Iolaus himself (ἀτάρβητον Διὸς υἱόν, *Shield* 109–112).
