## Meaning

οἰοπόλος is usually explained from οἶος, “alone,” and -πόλος, “ranging, frequented” (as in ἱππόπολος, [[horse-herding]]): a place where one goes alone, “lonely.” Homer uses it of wild places: χώρῳ ἐν οἰοπόλῳ, “in a lonely place,” in two similes, of the boar that awaits the hunters (13.473) and of the olive sapling that a gust of wind uproots, the likeness of Euphorbus' death (17.53–58); a lonely farmstead in the mountains whose fire sailors see from the sea (σταθμῷ ἐν οἰοπόλῳ, 19.375–377); the lonely mountains of Sipylus, where Niobe, turned to stone, broods over her sorrows (24.614–617); and the lonely mountains where Orion killed the beasts that he still hunts in Hades (Od. 11.572–575). The Hymn to Hermes gives the word to the god himself (Ἑρμῆς τʼ οἰοπόλος, *Hymn to Hermes* 314); there, of the god of herdsmen, it may come instead from ὄϊς, “sheep,” and mean “shepherd.”

## Use

{{quote:2930d636-c74e-5068-9322-3a90ff084757}}

Idomeneus “stood his ground like a boar in the mountains, trusting in its courage, which awaits a great din of men coming on in a lonely place, and bristles up along its back” (13.471–473; [[idomeneus]], [[the-boar-at-bay]], [[boar]]). The lonely place is where the beast faces the crowd of hunters by itself, as Idomeneus, “alone” (οἴῳ, 13.481), calls to his companions against Aeneas and his men. See [[aeneas]].
