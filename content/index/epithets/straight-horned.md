## Meaning

ὀρθόκραιρος = ὀρθός (“upright, straight”) + a word for the horns of the head, from the root of κέρας (“horn”): “with upright horns.” Homer uses it only in the genitive plural at the end of the verse, of two things. Of cattle: the oxen eaten on Lemnos (8.231), the herd of “straight-horned cattle” that Hephaestus makes of gold and tin on the shield of Achilles (18.573), and the cattle of the Sun, in Eurylochus' speech before the men kill them (Od. 12.348). Of ships: νεῶν ὀρθοκραιράων, the ships before which Antilochus finds Achilles with the news of Patroclus' death, and before which Achilles sits mourning him (18.3, 19.344). The ships' high curved ends stand up like a pair of horns ([[ships]], [[curved-ships]]).

## Use

Agamemnon reminds the Achaeans of their boasts on Lemnos, made “eating much meat of straight-horned cattle and drinking mixing bowls brim-full of wine” (8.231–232). The two verses describe a feast of the heroic kind, meat and wine in plenty, and the boasts that followed it ([[empty-boasting]], [[brim-full]], [[feast]]). See [[cattle]] and [[lemnos]].
