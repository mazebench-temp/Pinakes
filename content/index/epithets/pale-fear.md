## Meaning

χλωρός is the color of young shoots and of honey (μέλι χλωρόν, 11.631): pale green, pale yellow. Of a face it is the pallor of fear. Dolon, caught by Odysseus and Diomedes, stands “pale with fear” (χλωρὸς ὑπαὶ δείους, 10.376), and the Trojans are routed “pale with fear” (15.4). In χλωρὸν δέος the color of the face is given to the fear itself ([[deos]]).

The phrase is a formula, nine times in Homer, and δέος is always what “seizes” men (αἱρεῖ, εἷλεν, ᾕρει). Three times it is in the Iliad: here; the next day, when Zeus thunders from Ida and hurls his lightning among the Achaeans (8.75–77); and in a simile, of the dogs and herdsmen who shout at a lion from far off and do not dare come near (17.65–67). In the Odyssey it seizes Odysseus at the crowds of the dead (Od. 11.43, 11.633), his men at the sight of Charybdis (Od. 12.243), the suitors when he reveals himself (Od. 22.42), and the people of Ithaca when they hear that a god fought beside him and when Athena herself shouts to them to stop the fighting (Od. 24.450, 24.533).

## Use

The book ends with the Achaeans feasting all night on the wine from Lemnos, and the Trojans in their city:

{{quote:f233a103-34a1-5742-8956-6efeb72d8ce7}}

The Achaeans have built their wall without hecatombs (7.450), and Zeus has given Poseidon leave to destroy it (7.459–463). Now “all night long Zeus the counselor devised evil for them, thundering terribly” (7.478–479; [[counselor]], [[plan-of-zeus]]). In their fear the feasters pour their wine on the ground, and no one dares to drink before he has poured a libation to Zeus ([[libation]]). The formula returns the next day, at noon of the battle of Book 8, when Zeus thunders from Ida and all the Achaeans are seized by pale fear (8.68–77). See [[zeus]].
