## Meaning

τειχιόεις = τεῖχος (“wall”) + the suffix -όεις (“full of, having”): “walled, fortified.” The translation renders it “of the great walls.” It occurs only twice in Homer, both times at the end of the verse and in the same form, Τίρυνθά τε τειχιόεσσαν and Γόρτυνά τε τειχιόεσσαν. Compare [[well-walled]] (εὐτείχεος), the epithet of Troy.

## Use

{{quote:e217e833-d088-5825-94e3-20890dcc153d}}

The Mycenaean citadel of Tiryns still stands, enclosed by walls of huge, roughly worked blocks. Later Greeks thought no mortal could have moved such stones and credited them to the Cyclopes; Pausanias, describing the Argolid, calls the walls their work. The epithet matches what can still be seen. Gortyn in Crete receives the same half-line in the Cretan entry ({{ref:Iliad 2.646}}).
