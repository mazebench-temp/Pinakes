## Meaning

ἀργύφεος, with its shorter form ἄργυφος, belongs to the family of ἄργυρος, “silver,” and of ἀργεννός and ἀργής, “white, shining” ([[white]]). Homer uses it of the cave in the depths of the sea where Thetis lives among her sisters, “the silver-white cave” (18.50), of a sheep that Achilles slaughters for Priam (ὄϊν ἄργυφον, 24.621), of flocks (ἄργυφα μῆλα, *Odyssey* 10.85), and of the great robe, φᾶρος ἀργύφεον, that Calypso and Circe put on (*Odyssey* 5.230, 10.543).

## Outside Homer

Athena girds Pandora and dresses her “in a silver-white garment” (ἀργυφέῃ ἐσθῆτι, Hesiod, *Theogony* 573–574). In the Homeric Hymns Iambe spreads a silver-white fleece over the seat she sets for Demeter (*Homeric Hymn to Demeter* 195–196), and the Hours adorn Aphrodite's neck and silver-white breasts with golden necklaces (*Homeric Hymn* 6.10–11).
