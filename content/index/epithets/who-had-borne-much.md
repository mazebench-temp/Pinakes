## Meaning

πολύτλητος = πολύς (“much”) + -τλητος, a verbal adjective from the root of τλῆναι (“bear, endure”): “who has borne much.” The same root gives πολύτλας, “much-enduring,” the standing epithet of Odysseus, and πολυτλήμων, of a heart that holds firm (see [[much-enduring]]), and τλήμων and the verse-end τετληότι θυμῷ, “with enduring heart” (see [[enduring]]). Homer has the word once, of the old men in the throng of the dead that gathers to Odysseus' pit, between the brides and young unmarried men and the maidens “with hearts new to sorrow” (*Odyssey* 11.38–39; see [[new-to-sorrow]] and [[atalos]]). The translation renders it “who had borne much” (see the Odyssey translation's key words).
