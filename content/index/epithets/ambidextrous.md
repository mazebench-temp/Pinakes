## Meaning

περί (“around, exceedingly”) + δεξιός (“on the right hand”). Walter Leaf takes the compound to mean “ambidextrous,” since the more natural ἀμφιδέξιος does not fit the hexameter; the scholia's other explanation, περὶ δεξιός, “very skilful,” he finds less pointed, and Homer never uses δεξιός of cleverness (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 21.163). The translation renders it “for he could use either hand.”

Homer has the word once. Leaf reports that some ancient texts added a verse after the Paeonians' entry in the Catalogue to name Asteropaeus there, “and the son of Pelegon, ambidextrous Asteropaeus” (Πηλεγόνος θʼ υἱὸς περιδέξιος Ἀστεροπαῖος), since he is missing from it (on 21.154; see the translation's note on 21.154–158).

## Use

{{quote:70e1543f-0017-5662-a2bd-7ebebf5cf75b}}

Asteropaeus throws first, with both spears at once (δούρασιν ἀμφίς), and the verb is held back while the sentence divides between the two hands (Leaf on 21.162). One spear strikes Achilles' shield and is stopped by the gold, the gift of the god, in the verse of Aeneas' spear in Book 20 (21.165 = 20.268); the other grazes the forearm of his right arm and draws blood (21.166–167; see the translation's notes on 21.162–163 and 21.166–167, and [[dark-blood]]), and, passing over him, sticks in the ground, “longing to glut itself on flesh” (21.168). See [[asteropaeus]], [[the-shield-of-achilles]] and [[spear]].
