## Meaning

καυστειρή is formed from καίω (“burn, kindle”): “burning, scorching,” the battle that blazes like fire. Homer has it only in the phrase μάχης καυστείρης, in a verse that occurs twice (4.342, Il. 12.316).

## Use

{{quote:7e48bc0b-a377-5c41-9e37-b742e3bf7c3f}}

Agamemnon reproaches Menestheus and Odysseus with the logic of the feast. Those who are first to hear his call to the elders' feast, and drink the honey-sweet wine as long as they like, should be first to meet the blazing battle ([[feast]], [[honey-sweet]]). In Book 12 Sarpedon states the same principle to Glaucus without reproach: the Lycians honor the two of them with a seat of honor, meat and full cups, and so they must stand among the foremost and meet the blazing battle (Il. 12.310–316). The two lines about the blazing battle are the same in both speeches (4.341–342, Il. 12.315–316): a formula for the duty of the front rank ([[the-review]]).
