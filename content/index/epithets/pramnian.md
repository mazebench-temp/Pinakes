## Meaning

Πράμνειος, “Pramnian,” is used only of wine, twice in Homer, both times in the half-verse οἴνῳ Πραμνείῳ, “with Pramnian wine,” and both times in a κυκεών, a mixed drink: in the drink that Hecamede makes, with grated goat's cheese and white barley meal (11.638–640; [[kykeon]]), and in Circe's potion, in which she stirs cheese, barley meal and pale honey into Pramnian wine and adds her baneful drugs (Od. 10.234–236). What the name means was already uncertain in antiquity: the explanations reported derive it from a place of that name, variously located, or take it as the name of a strong, dark kind of wine. See [[wine]].

## Use

{{quote:01fc14e5-7b88-5219-81ec-bb858fb3edba}}

The drink is mixed in Nestor's great cup and given to the two men as they rest from the battle; Machaon has an arrow wound in the shoulder (11.505–507). In the poem it refreshes them: they drive off their thirst and turn to talk (11.642–643; [[parching]]). Patroclus finds them so (11.644). See [[hecamede]], [[like-the-goddesses]] and [[machaon]].

## Outside Homer

Plato twice takes up the drink, as a matter for medicine. In the *Ion* Socrates quotes the verses on the Pramnian wine, the grated cheese and the onion (11.639–640, with 11.630) and asks whether a doctor or a rhapsode is the better judge of whether Homer is right; Ion answers, the doctor (*Ion* 538b–c). In the *Republic* he says that the sons of Asclepius at Troy did not blame the woman who gave the wounded Eurypylus Pramnian wine with barley meal and grated cheese sprinkled on it, though in Homer the drink is made for Machaon (*Republic* 3.405e–406a; [[plato-asclepiads]]).
