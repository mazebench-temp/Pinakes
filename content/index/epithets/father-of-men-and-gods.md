## Meaning

πατήρ (“father”) + ἀνδρῶν τε θεῶν τε (“of men and of gods”). Zeus is not the father of all gods and men in a literal sense: many gods are his brothers, sisters, or elders. The title expresses authority, the father as head of a household, extended to the world. It goes with the ancient habit of calling the sky god “father” ([[father-zeus]]).

## Use

{{quote:b62f9301-a60c-55a8-9ec8-07cbd10b91bd}}

The title introduces Zeus' answer to Hera's jealous question, and the answer is a father's: some counsels are not for her to know, though she is his wife. Moments before, all the gods rose from their seats to meet “their father” (σφοῦ πατρὸς ἐναντίον, {{ref:Iliad 1.533-535}}). The formula fills the second half of the verse after the whole-line frame τὴν δ’ ἠμείβετ’ ἔπειτα ([[then-answered]]).
