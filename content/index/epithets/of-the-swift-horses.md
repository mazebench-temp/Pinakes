## Meaning

ταχύπωλος = ταχύς (“swift”) + πῶλος (“foal,” and in poetry a horse): “with swift horses.” It belongs to the Danaans, above all in the genitive Δαναῶν ταχυπώλων at the end of the verse, a formula for the Achaean army as a force of chariots. Once it is given to the Myrmidons, when Achilles addresses them at the funeral of Patroclus (Il. 23.6). Compare the Trojans' [[tamer-of-horses]] and the Phrygians' [[with-quick-horses]].

## Use

The formula comes twice in the review. Agamemnon encourages “those of the Danaans of the swift horses whom he saw eager” (4.232), and he greets Idomeneus with the highest praise:

The horses of the formula stand idle. Agamemnon has left his own horses and chariot with his attendant Eurymedon and goes through the ranks on foot (4.226–231; [[eurymedon]]). In Book 8 Hector turns the same words of praise into a taunt. As Diomedes retreats before the thunder of Zeus, Hector tells him that the Danaans of the swift horses honored him above others with a seat of honor, meat and full cups, and now will scorn him (Il. 8.161–163). Honor at the feast is the measure the review appeals to ([[feast]], [[the-review]]).
