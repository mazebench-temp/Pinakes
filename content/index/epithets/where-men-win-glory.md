## Meaning

κυδιάνειρα = κῦδος (“glory”) + ἀνήρ (“man”): “bringing glory to men.” In the Iliad it is regularly an epithet of battle, as in the formula μάχην ἀνὰ κυδιάνειραν, “through the battle where men win glory.” Book 1 gives it, unusually, to the ἀγορή.

## Use

{{quote:3af06dba-39b8-5468-bf47-04a98b27563d}}

The narrator names two arenas of glory, the assembly and the war, and Achilles absents himself from both. Giving the battle epithet to the assembly puts the two on a level: the place of speech is also a place of honor, as the quarrel has shown. Achilles has already won and lost honor there, and he wastes away his heart in staying apart ([[assembly-type-scene]]).
