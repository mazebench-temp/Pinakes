## Meaning

χυτός (“poured, heaped”) is the verbal adjective of χέω (“pour, heap up”), the verb of the grave mound: Achilles “heaped a mound” over Eetion (ἐπὶ σῆμʼ ἔχεεν, 6.419; [[tomb]]). χυτὴ γαῖα is the earth heaped over the dead. Homer uses the phrase four times. Hector wishes it for himself (Il. 6.464). Diomedes says of his father Tydeus, “whom the heaped earth covers at Thebes” (Il. 14.114; [[tydeus]]). The Achaeans heap earth over the pyre of Patroclus (Il. 23.255–257; [[cremation]]). And Nestor tells Telemachus that if Menelaus had found Aegisthus alive, no one would have heaped earth over him even in death (Od. 3.255–258).

## Use

Hector ends his foresight of Andromache's slavery with a wish:

{{quote:c3133ce8-811d-5c82-a3bb-19e3c8031ebb}}

He has pictured her carried off by an Achaean, weaving at another woman's bidding in Argos, and a passer-by pointing her out as the wife of Hector (6.454–463; [[captive-women]]). He would rather be under the grave mound than hear her cries. Earlier in the book he wished the earth would gape for Paris (6.281–282; [[may-the-earth-gape]]); now he wishes it heaped over himself.
