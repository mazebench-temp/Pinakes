## Meaning

The verse ἀρητὸν δὲ τοκεῦσι γόον καὶ πένθος ἔθηκας is spoken twice: by Euphorbus to Menelaus, of the parents of the brother Menelaus killed (17.37), and by Andromache to the dead Hector, of his own parents (24.741). The translation renders the printed ἀρητόν as “accursed” (see the translation's note on 17.37). Walter Leaf reports that interpreters in both places divide between ἀρητόν and ἄρρητον, “unspeakable,” a word the Odyssey has in the sense “unspoken” (*Odyssey* 14.466). He argues that ἀρητός should mean “prayed for,” like πολυάρητος (*Odyssey* 6.280, 19.404), and that the simple ἀρή and ἀράομαι mean “prayer, pray,” not “curse” (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 17.37). The exclusion keeps the name Ἄρητος, Aretus (17.535), out of the pattern.
