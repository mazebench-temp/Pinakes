## Meaning

κινυρός is “wailing, plaintive.” Homer has it only once, of the mother cow in the first simile of the fight for Patroclus' body (17.5), and the translation renders it with an adverb, “lowing plaintively” (see the translation's note on 17.4–5). Walter Leaf accepts the gloss of Eustathius, οἰκτρόφωνος διὰ τὸ πολὺ τῆς στοργῆς, “piteous-voiced, from the greatness of her love,” and notes that the word does not recur in Homer except in Zenodotus' reading κινυρίζων at 9.612 (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 17.5).
