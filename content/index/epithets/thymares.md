## Meaning

θυμαρής = θυμός (“heart, spirit”; [[thymos]]) + the root of ἀραρίσκω (“fit, suit”): “suiting the heart,” beloved. Homer uses it twice in the phrase ἄλοχον θυμαρέα. Achilles calls Briseis “the wife dear to my heart” (9.336), and the Odyssey uses the phrase of Penelope when Odysseus at last holds her and weeps, “holding the wife dear to his heart, who knew what was right” (Od. 23.232). Of things it is what suits a man: the staff that Eumaeus gives the disguised Odysseus is “to his liking” (Od. 17.199), and Circe's servant mixes his bath water to suit him (θυμῆρες, Od. 10.362). Compare the phrase of Athena's affection for Diomedes, “dear to my heart” (ἐμῷ κεχαρισμένε θυμῷ, [[dear-to-my-heart]]).

## Use

{{quote:1b69306b-0186-52ec-a5b8-423edd5528d5}}

Achilles calls the captive taken from him his ἄλοχος, his wife, and “dear to my heart”: “from me alone of the Achaeans he has taken mine, and he keeps the wife dear to my heart; let him lie beside her and take his pleasure.” He goes on to set his love for her beside the Atreidae's for Helen: any man who is good and sensible “loves his own and cares for her, as I too loved her from my heart, though she was won by my spear” (9.340–343; [[briseis]], [[captive-women]], [[wedded]]). The only other wife Homer calls by this word is Penelope. See [[achilles]].
