## Meaning

πολυκαγκής = πολύς (“very”) + the root of κάγκανος (“dry,” of firewood: the dry sticks under a boiling cauldron, ξύλα κάγκανα, 21.362–364, and around the braziers in Odysseus' hall, Od. 18.308): “very dry, parching.” It occurs only here, of δίψα, “thirst.” See [[kykeon]].

## Use

{{quote:264e4fce-7f98-535f-952d-64b413157c8f}}

When the two men have drunk and driven off their thirst, they take pleasure in talk with each other, and Patroclus appears at the door, “a man equal to the gods” (11.644; [[equal-to-the-gods]]). The rest of the scene is talk: Patroclus refuses to sit, and Nestor answers him with the longest speech of the book (11.648–803). See [[nestor]], [[machaon]] and [[patroclus]].
