## Meaning

καμπύλος, from κάμπτω (“bend”), means “bent, curved.” It is an epithet of the bow, in the plural τόξα, which Homer uses of a single bow, and of the rounded wheels of a chariot (καμπύλα κύκλα, Il. 5.722).

## Use

{{quote:553b8de8-3760-5a18-a4d2-79fd88e6b536}}

Paris steps out as champion with a leopard's skin on his shoulders ([[leopard-skin]]), a bow and a sword, and two spears ([[tipped-with-bronze]]). The bow marks him as an archer. In later books he wounds Diomedes with an arrow (Il. 11.369–378), and the dying Hector foretells that Paris and Apollo will kill Achilles at the Scaean gates (Il. 22.359–360). For the formal duel he arms instead as a spearman (3.338; [[strong-spear]]). See [[bow]].
