## Meaning

αἰγίλιψ is usually explained as αἴξ (“goat”) + λείπω (“leave”): a rock that even goats leave, too steep for them, and so “sheer.” The derivation is old and uncertain, but the sense of the passages is clear. Homer uses the word three times, always of a rock, αἰγίλιπος πέτρης: the rock from which the spring of dark water pours (9.15 = 16.4; [[of-dark-water]], [[dnopheros]]), and the high rock from which a hawk launches itself to chase a bird across the plain, in the simile of Poseidon leaving the two Ajaxes (13.62–65). The same word is the name of a place in Odysseus' realm, Aegilips, “the rugged” (Αἰγίλιπα τρηχεῖαν, 2.633; [[aegilips]]). Compare [[steep]] (αἰπύς) and [[rocky]] (πετρήεις).

## Use

{{quote:fdbf2d94-5382-55d5-bfcb-835ec9315033}}

The spring runs down a face of rock that nothing can climb ([[the-spring-of-dark-water]]). The simile gives Agamemnon's tears the steady, helpless fall of water down a cliff, and the same two verses describe Patroclus' tears in Book 16 (16.3–4). See [[agamemnon]].
