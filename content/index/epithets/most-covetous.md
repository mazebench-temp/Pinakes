## Meaning

φιλοκτέανος = φίλος (“fond of”) + κτέανα (“possessions,” from κτάομαι, “acquire”): “loving possessions.” The superlative is a rare word, not found elsewhere in Homer.

## Use

{{quote:a798e9d4-2b7f-5fbe-a255-c51201e3f688}}

The insult takes the place in the verse where the honorific ἄναξ ἀνδρῶν Ἀγάμεμνον would stand (see [[most-glorious]]). It names the grievance behind the whole quarrel. Achilles' argument is practical: there is no common store of prizes, since everything taken from the cities has been divided. But the vocative makes it personal, and he returns to the charge at length later in the speech: Agamemnon always takes the larger share although Achilles does most of the fighting ({{ref:Iliad 1.163-168}}).

The later insults develop the same accusation of greed: [[mind-set-on-gain]] (κερδαλεόφρων) and [[people-devouring-king]] (δημοβόρος βασιλεύς).
