## Meaning

ὠμοφάγος = ὠμός (“raw”) + φαγεῖν (“eat”): “eating raw flesh.” Homer uses it of lions in the half-verse λείουσιν ἐοικότες ὠμοφάγοισιν, “like lions that eat raw flesh” (5.782; Il. 7.256, 15.592), of jackals (Il. 11.479), and of wolves (Il. 16.157). See [[lion]]. ὠμηστής = ὠμός (“raw”) + the root of ἔδω (“eat”) has the same sense: the birds and the dogs that will tear the dead are ὠμησταί (Il. 11.454, 22.67), and the last book gives the word to fish and, once, to a man, Achilles (Il. 24.82, 24.207).

## Use

{{quote:efd1a3f9-066a-5f10-b85c-5e20a06837df}}

Hera and Athena find the best of the Achaeans crowded around Diomedes, like lions or wild boars at bay ([[lions-and-boars]], [[boar]]). The two verses of the comparison (5.782–783) are used again when Ajax and Hector fall on each other in their duel (Il. 7.256–257). Hera then shouts in the likeness of Stentor to shame the Argives ([[bronze-voiced]]).
