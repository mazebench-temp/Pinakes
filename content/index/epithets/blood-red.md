## Meaning

δαφοινός is usually explained as an intensive prefix δα- (as in ζα-, “very”) + φοινός (“red,” the color of blood, from the family of φόνος, “slaughter,” and φοῖνιξ, “crimson”): “deep red”; of a hide it may be a tawny red. Homer uses it four times, three of them of beasts or their hides: the serpent that appears at Aulis, “blood-red on its back” (2.308; [[the-portent-at-aulis]]); the lion's skin that Agamemnon puts on at night (10.23); and the jackals in a simile, which gather round a wounded stag as the Trojans gather round Odysseus (δαφοινοὶ θῶες, 11.474). The fourth is the lengthened form δαφοινεός: on the shield of Achilles Ker, the goddess of death, wears a cloak “red with the blood of men” (δαφοινεὸν αἵματι φωτῶν, 18.538; [[ker-doom]]). Compare [[crimson]] (φοινικόεις), from the same family, [[tawny]] (αἴθων), the lion's color in the next verse, and [[red]] (ἐρυθρός).

## Use

{{quote:ceaca53d-2045-55e2-8913-121f3aff77dc}}

Sleepless, Agamemnon dresses to go to Nestor, and his cloak is the skin of a lion: “blood-red” in one verse, “a great tawny lion, reaching to his feet” in the next ([[tawny]], [[reaching-to-the-feet]], [[lion-skin]]). The skin is the hide of the lion's color, not stained with blood, but the word that colors it is the word of the serpent of Aulis and, in its longer form, of the bloody cloak of Ker. When Diomedes puts on the same skin later in the night the second verse is repeated and δαφοινός is not (10.177–178). See [[agamemnon]].

## Outside Homer

The verse of the goddess's cloak is repeated in the Hesiodic *Shield of Heracles* (159), where the Keres too are δαφοιναί, “blood-red” (250).
