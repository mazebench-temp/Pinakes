## Meaning

κρυόεις is formed from κρύος (“icy cold, frost”): “icy, chilling.” Homer uses it twice, each time of a power of battle: Onslaught on the aegis (5.740), and Rout at the beginning of Book 9, when “the Achaeans were held by heaven-sent Panic, companion of chilling Rout” (Il. 9.1–2; [[rout]]). The related κρυερός describes rout (κρυεροῖο φόβοιο, Il. 13.48) and lament (κρυεροῖο γόοιο, Il. 24.524). The cold is the cold of fear. The same image is in ῥιγέω (“shudder”), from ῥῖγος (“cold”): Diomedes tells Aphrodite she will “shudder at war” (5.351), and Diomedes himself shudders when he sees Ares beside Hector (5.596).

## Use

The epithet comes in the description of the aegis: “and on it is Strife, and on it Courage, and on it chilling Onslaught” (5.740; [[tasseled]], [[aegis]]). Of the powers set on the aegis, Rout, Strife, Courage and Onslaught, only Onslaught is given an epithet ([[onslaught]], [[strife]], [[courage]]). The translation keeps the word “chilling” for Ares' complaint to Zeus that the gods “are forever enduring the most chilling things” from one another (ῥίγιστα, 5.873).
