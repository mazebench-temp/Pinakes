## Meaning

ἀγανός means “gentle, mild, kindly.” It is used of words, of gifts (Il. 9.113), and of the “gentle arrows” of Apollo and Artemis that bring sudden, painless death (Il. 24.759). With ἔπεα (“words”; [[epos]]) it forms the phrase ἀγανοῖς ἐπέεσσι, “with gentle words.” The related noun ἀγανοφροσύνη (“gentleness of mind”) is Helen's word for Hector's kindness to her in her lament over him (Il. 24.772), where she praises “your gentleness and your gentle words.”

## Use

The phrase comes three times in Book 2, in a command given twice and then carried out. Hera tells Athena to go through the army and “hold back every man with your gentle words” ({{ref:Iliad 2.164}}); Athena gives the same command to Odysseus ({{ref:Iliad 2.180}}). Odysseus obeys, but only with the kings:
