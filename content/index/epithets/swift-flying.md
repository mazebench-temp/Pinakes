## Meaning

ὠκυπέτης = ὠκύς (“swift”; [[swift-footed]]) + the root of πέτομαι (“fly”): “flying swiftly.” The horses of the gods fly: when they are whipped on, they “flew on not unwilling” (πετέσθην, 8.45, as of Hera's horses at 5.768), and one stride of the horses of Hera covers as much haze as a man can see from a lookout over the sea (5.770–772; [[misty]]). The same root gives the birds πετεηνά (“winged ones,” 8.247; [[surest-of-birds]]).

The adjective occurs only in the dual ὠκυπέτα, in one verse: ὠκυπέτα χρυσέῃσιν ἐθείρῃσιν κομόωντε, “swift-flying, with flowing manes of gold.” It describes Zeus' team as he drives to Ida (8.42) and Poseidon's as he drives from Aegae to the ships (13.24), in passages that are almost the same ([[bronze-footed]]). The manes of gold go with the god's armor of gold and his golden whip in the verses that follow (8.43–44, 13.25–26).

## Use

Zeus yokes the horses after he has forbidden the gods to fight and answered Athena (8.38–44). They carry him from Olympus to Ida “between earth and the starry sky” (8.46; [[starry]]), in the verse that described the flight of Hera's horses in Book 5 (5.769). Later in the day Hera and Athena set out from Olympus in their own chariot, and Zeus sends Iris to turn them back at its gates (8.389–437; [[chariot-journey]], [[storm-footed]]).
