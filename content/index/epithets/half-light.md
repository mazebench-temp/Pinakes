## Meaning

ἀμφιλύκη occurs only here in Homer, in the phrase ἀμφιλύκη νύξ: the half-dark before dawn, when the night still holds. ἀμφι- means “on both sides,” and the second part is usually connected with a root for light, seen in Latin *lux* and in the later Greek λυκόφως, “twilight”: a night that is light and dark at once (see the translation's note on 7.433). The same root has been proposed for Apollo's title [[lycian-born]] (Λυκηγενής), which the translation renders “the Lycian-born.”

## Use

The truce day has ended with the burning of the dead (7.421–432). The next day begins before it is light:

The time is marked with a pair of clauses, ἦμος … τῆμος, “when … then”: when it was not yet dawn, then a chosen band of the Achaeans was astir around the pyre. The Achaeans heap a single tomb over it and build the wall, its towers and gates, and the ditch with its stakes, all on Nestor's plan (7.336–343; [[the-achaean-wall]], [[tomb]]). The work begun in the half-light is finished at sunset (7.465), and the night that follows is filled with Zeus' thunder (7.476–482). See [[dawn-line]].
