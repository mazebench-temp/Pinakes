## Meaning

πυκινός (also πυκνός) means “close, dense, compact”: the ranks of both armies sit “close-packed” on the plain while Hector speaks (7.61; πυκναί). Of the mind it means “shrewd, well-considered”: Agamemnon calls the elders together and “laid out a shrewd plan” (2.55; πυκινὴν ἀρτύνετο βουλήν). πυκινὸν ἔπος is a word with nothing loose in it ([[epos]]). Homer uses the phrase four times, and each time it is a word that is meant to change a mind or to stay with the hearer. Menoetius tells Patroclus to speak “a shrewd word” to Achilles, who will listen for his good (11.786–789); Zeus asks for Thetis to be called so that he may tell her “a shrewd word” and Achilles may accept Priam's gifts and give back Hector's body (24.74–76); and Andromache laments that Hector, dying away from her, spoke to her no shrewd word that she could remember night and day (24.742–745).

Paris' offer, the possessions without Helen, is the message the herald must carry. The shrewd word is Priam's addition: a request that the fighting stop until both sides have burned their dead. Agamemnon refuses the offer and grants the request (7.405–411; [[the-truce-for-the-dead]], [[priam]], [[idaeus]]). See [[counselor-equal-to-the-gods]].
