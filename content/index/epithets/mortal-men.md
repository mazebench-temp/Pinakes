## Meaning

θνητός (“mortal,” from θνῄσκω, “die”) is the defining quality of human beings, as ἀθάνατος (“deathless”) is of gods ([[immortals]]). The pairing of θνητοὶ ἄνθρωποι with θεοί expresses the whole world, divine and human.

## Use

{{quote:00d5c5d5-c6f8-5053-8860-90f87f23fea3}}

Achilles calls the heralds to witness “before the blessed gods and before mortal men” ([[blessed-gods]]), and before Agamemnon himself. The polar pair makes the whole world his audience. Hephaestus uses θνητοί alone for the same contrast when he protests that Zeus and Hera are quarreling “for the sake of mortals” ({{ref:Iliad 1.574}}). See also [[meropes]].
