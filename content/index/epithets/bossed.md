## Meaning

ὀμφαλόεις is formed from ὀμφαλός (“navel,” and so the raised center of a round thing) with -όεις (“having”): “with a boss.” It is a standing epithet of the round shield, ἀσπὶς ὀμφαλόεσσα (for example Il. 6.118, 13.264), and once it describes a yoke with a knob (Il. 24.269). The noun is used of the body in Book 4 as well: Peirous stabs Diores “beside the navel” (παρʼ ὀμφαλόν, 4.525). See [[shield]], [[balanced-on-every-side]].

## Use

In the first clash of the armies “the bossed shields pressed against one another” (4.448–449; [[armored-in-bronze]]). The verses are repeated when the armies meet again in Book 8 (Il. 8.62–63).
