## Meaning

ἁμαρτοεπής = the root of ἁμαρτάνω (“miss the mark, err”) + ἔπος (“word”; [[epos]]): “missing the mark in words, blundering in speech.” Homer has it only here. Its near twin is ἀφαμαρτοεπής, “missing the point,” which Antenor denies of Menelaus when he remembers the embassy to Troy: Menelaus spoke briefly but very clearly, “since he was not a man of many words nor one who missed the point” (3.214–215; [[the-embassy-to-troy]]). Compare Thersites, ἀμετροεπής, “unmeasured in speech” ([[of-unmeasured-words]]).

## Use

{{quote:a1854acf-024b-5bb2-81a5-b3653daffc41}}

Ajax has told Hector that the day is near when he will pray, in flight, for horses swifter than hawks, and as he speaks an eagle flies by on the right, and the Achaeans shout, emboldened by the omen (13.809–823; [[high-flying]], [[divination]]). Hector answers: “Ajax, blundering in speech, you braggart, what a thing you have said!” (13.824; [[braggart]], [[what-a-word]]). His own speech then claims more than any man can: he wishes he were the son of Zeus and Hera, and promises that Ajax will glut the dogs and birds of the Trojans (13.825–832; [[son-of-zeus]], [[lily-like]]). See [[ajax]] and [[hector]].
