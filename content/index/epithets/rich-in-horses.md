## Meaning

πολύϊππος = πολύς (“much, many”) + ἵππος (“horse”): “with many horses,” rich in them. Homer has it only here. It is one of the compounds that measure wealth in herds, like πολύρρηνες and πολυβοῦται, “rich in flocks” and “rich in cattle,” of the men of the seven towns that Agamemnon offers Achilles (9.154; [[rich-in-flocks]], [[rich-in-cattle]]). The Troad's wealth in horses is old: Erichthonius, the wealthiest of mortal men, had three thousand mares grazing in the marsh (20.219–222).

## Use

{{quote:f8b2d322-1da2-5a27-880b-52296d51f810}}

“And Teucer, son of Telamon, was the first to kill his man, Imbrius the spearman, son of Mentor, rich in horses” (13.170–171; [[teucer]], [[imbrius]], [[mentor-father-of-imbrius]], [[spearmen]]). The father's wealth opens the son's short life: Imbrius lived at Pedaeum until the Achaeans came, married a bastard daughter of Priam, and then lived beside Priam, who honored him as he honored his children (13.172–176; [[pedaeum]], [[medesicaste]]).
