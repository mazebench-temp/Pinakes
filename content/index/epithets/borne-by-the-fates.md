## Meaning

κηρεσσιφόρητος = κήρεσσι, the dative plural of κήρ (“fate, doom”; [[ker-doom]]) + φορητός (“carried,” from φορέω, “carry”): “carried by the fates.” Its first part is a case form, as in ὀρεσίτροφος, “mountain-bred,” the epithet of a lion (12.299). Hector explains the word himself in the next verse: they are dogs “whom the fates bring here on the black ships” (8.528; [[black-ships]]). The word occurs only here.

The κῆρες are the dooms of death that wait for every man. Zeus weighed two of them at noon of this day, and the fates of the Achaeans sank to the earth (8.69–74; [[that-lays-men-low]], [[the-scales-of-zeus]]).

## Use

{{quote:daa722f4-5b85-553e-b9b8-028e0e7f72b1}}

Near the end of his night speech Hector states a hope that is also a prayer ([[euchomai]]). He reads the day as the scales read it: the Achaeans' fates have brought them to Troy, and he will drive them off. Teucer had called Hector “this mad dog” (8.299; [[mad-dog]]); Hector calls the Achaeans dogs ([[dogs]]). Zeus has already said that Hector will not stop until Achilles is roused at the ships over the dead Patroclus (8.473–476; [[plan-of-zeus]]). In Book 22 Zeus weighs Hector's own κήρ against Achilles', and it is Hector's that sinks (22.209–213). See [[hector]].
