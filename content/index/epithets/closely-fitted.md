## Meaning

πυκινῶς (“closely, tightly”) + ἀραρυῖαι, the perfect participle of ἀραρίσκω (“fit, join”): “closely fitted.” It is a verse-end formula for doors (θύραι) and for the planks or leaves of a door (σανίδες): of the doors of Phoenix's chamber, which he breaks to escape (9.475); of the gates of Troy, which Priam orders opened for the fleeing Trojans and closed again behind them (21.535); and in the Odyssey of the doors of Odysseus' storeroom (Od. 2.344), of the doors of the hall that the women are told to lock before the contest of the bow (Od. 21.236, 21.382), of the storeroom door that Telemachus left ajar, so that the suitors were armed (Od. 22.154–156), of the door that the suitors' spears strike (Od. 22.258, 22.275), and of the doors of the bedroom that Odysseus built around his olive-tree bed (Od. 23.192–194). Compare [[well-joined]] (κολλητός), used with it in that last verse, and the other words of the joiner's craft, [[eupektos]] and [[polished]].

## Use

{{quote:148d2d4a-fd79-598f-8982-05e2f18d49c7}}

On the tenth night Phoenix “broke the closely fitted doors of the chamber” and leapt over the fence of the court (9.475–476; [[well-fenced]]). The formula for doors that hold is used for the doors he breaks. See [[the-flight-of-phoenix]].
