## Meaning

τειχεσιπλήτης is usually explained as τείχεσι, dative plural of τεῖχος (“wall”), + a noun from the root of πελάζω, πλῆτο (“draw near, come up to”): “he who comes up against walls,” the attacker of cities. The form of the second element is not certain. The word occurs only in the vocative, τειχεσιπλῆτα, at the end of one verse, spoken twice in Book 5. It is the siege among the god's three names in that line, beside the killing of [[ruin-of-mortals]] and the blood of [[blood-stained]]. Compare [[sacker-of-cities]] (πτολίπορθος), which Book 5 gives to Enyo, the goddess who goes into battle with Ares (5.333, 5.592; [[enyo]]).

## Use

Athena begins with this line when she persuades Ares to leave the mortals to fight it out (5.31), and Apollo begins with it when he asks him to go into the battle and drag Diomedes out of it (5.455). The whole verse is discussed under [[ruin-of-mortals]]. It is the only line in Homer that names Ares by the walls of cities. In Book 5 the wall is near: Ares himself, in the likeness of a Thracian leader, asks the sons of Priam whether they will wait until the Achaeans are fighting “around the well-built gates” (5.466; [[well-built]]).
