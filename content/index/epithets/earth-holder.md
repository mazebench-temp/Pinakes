## Meaning

γαιήοχος = γαῖα (“earth”) + ἔχω (“hold”): “who holds the earth,” either the god who holds it fast or the god who embraces it, as the sea surrounds the land; some ancient readers took the second part from ὀχέομαι (“ride”), “who rides over the earth.” The sense is not certain. It belongs to Poseidon alone. Most often it stands before his other title in the line-end formula γαιήοχος ἐννοσίγαιος, “the earth-holder, the shaker of the earth” (9.183; 13.43, 13.59, 13.677, 14.355, 15.222, 23.584; Od. 11.241; [[shaker-of-the-earth]], [[earth-shaker]]), or with his name, Ποσειδάων γαιήοχος (20.34; Od. 1.68, 8.322). In address it goes with κυανοχαῖτα, “dark-haired,” in the words of Iris to Poseidon (15.174, 15.201) and in the prayer of the blinded Cyclops to his father (Od. 9.528). The god who shakes the earth also holds it: Poseidon threatens it and he keeps it steady.

## Use

{{quote:f7d7c6f1-39fe-5e85-b6e7-34c67304dd62}}

This is the epithet's first appearance in the poem. The envoys walk along the shore of the loud-roaring sea ([[loud-roaring-sea]]) and pray earnestly to the earth-holder, the shaker of the earth, that they may easily win over the great mind of Achilles. The poem does not say why they pray to Poseidon; they are walking beside his sea. The prayer is not granted. The duals of the passage, “the two went,” “praying,” are one of the puzzles of the book ([[the-embassy-to-achilles]]). Achilles names the same god in his reply, when he speaks of a fair voyage home granted by “the famous shaker of the earth” (9.362). See [[poseidon]].
