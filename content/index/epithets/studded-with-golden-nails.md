## Meaning

χρύσειος (“golden”) + ἧλος (“nail, stud”) + πεπαρμένον, perfect participle of πείρω (“pierce”): “pierced with golden studs.” Gold studs decorated the wooden staff, as silver studs decorate a Homeric sword hilt (ξίφος ἀργυρόηλον, “silver-studded sword”).

## Use

{{quote:945a4d36-6ed2-5c95-9678-9440f63029ba}}

Achilles has just described the scepter as a piece of wood, cut in the mountains and stripped by the bronze of leaves and bark, never to grow again ({{ref:Iliad 1.234-237}}). As he throws it down, the narrator shows its other side: gold. The staff is dead wood and precious emblem at once, carried by those who give judgment in the name of Zeus ({{ref:Iliad 1.237-239}}; [[oath-formulas]]). In Book 2 Agamemnon's own scepter is given a pedigree reaching back to Hephaestus and Zeus (Il. 2.100–108). See also [[scepter-bearing]] and [[golden-scepter]].
