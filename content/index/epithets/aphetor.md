## Meaning

ἀφήτωρ is an agent noun, found only here in Homer. It is usually derived from ἀφίημι (“send off, let fly”), the verb of shooting arrows: “the one who lets fly,” the Archer, a title like Apollo's other names as bowman, [[far-shooter]] and [[hekatos]]. Some ancient readers derived it instead from φημί (“speak”), “the speaker of oracles,” a sense that would suit Pytho, the seat of his oracle; others, Strabo reports, took ἀφήτωρ to mean “treasure” and “the threshold of the ἀφήτωρ” to be a treasure-chamber under the ground (Strabo 9.3.8; [[strabo]]). The derivation from ἀφίημι is the commoner view, and the translation renders it “the Archer.”

## Use

{{quote:e4d28042-22ef-5a3a-9158-1631190540d1}}

Achilles names the two richest places he knows: Troy as it was in peace, and the treasure of Apollo at Pytho, “all that is shut within the stone threshold of the Archer, Phoebus Apollo, in rocky Pytho” ([[pytho]], [[rocky]], [[phoebus]]). Neither is worth his life. Strabo cites these verses as proof that the temple at Delphi was once very rich (Strabo 9.3.8). In the Odyssey the same stone threshold at Pytho is the one Agamemnon crossed to consult the oracle, which foretold him a quarrel of the best of the Achaeans, in Demodocus' song of the quarrel of Odysseus and Achilles (Od. 8.75–82). See [[apollo]].
