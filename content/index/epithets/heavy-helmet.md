## Meaning

βριαρός (“heavy, strong”) is from the root of βρίθω (“be heavy, weigh down”) and βριθύς (“heavy”), as in the spear βριθὺ μέγα στιβαρόν, “heavy, huge, stout” ([[heavy-spear]]). In Homer it describes only the helmet, κόρυς, seven times in the Iliad: Agastrophus' helmet, which Diomedes strips (11.375); the helmets split with the heads inside them by stones in the fighting of Book 16, when Patroclus kills Erylaus and Hector kills Epeigeus (16.411–413, 16.577–579); the helmet that Hephaestus makes for Achilles (18.611) and that Achilles puts on (19.380–381); Aeneas' helmet as he comes against Achilles (20.162); and Hector's, which he thinks of laying down with his shield to go and bargain with Achilles (22.111–112). The name of the hundred-handed Briareus (1.403; [[briareus]]) is usually connected with the same root. See [[helmet]].

## Use

{{quote:686b2fcd-3374-55c3-b904-4741fcb5df14}}

Diomedes has killed Agastrophus, who fought on foot with his horses held far off (11.338–342), and is taking his armor: the gleaming corselet from his chest, the shield from his shoulders and the heavy helmet ([[panaiolos]], [[stripping-the-dead]]). While his hands are busy with the spoils, Paris draws his bow from behind the pillar on the tomb of Ilus and shoots him through the foot ([[man-made]]). See [[agastrophus]], [[diomedes]] and [[paris]].
