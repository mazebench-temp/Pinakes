## Meaning

κυάνεος is the adjective of κύανος, a dark blue material, probably blue glass paste used for inlay; the word appears in Linear B as *ku-wa-no*. In Homer κυάνεος means “dark, blue-black.” It describes storm clouds, the hair of Hector, and here the brows of Zeus. ὀφρύς is the eyebrow.

## Use

{{quote:640348fc-7fe1-56c7-a683-ce6bf661080e}}

The nod is Zeus' strongest pledge. He has just said that nothing he confirms with a nod of his head can be taken back, prove false, or go unfulfilled ({{ref:Iliad 1.524-527}}). The three lines move from the brows to the hair ([[ambrosial-locks]]) to the whole mountain, which shakes. The first of them recurs in Book 17, when Zeus nods again, over Hector (Il. 17.209).

## Outside Homer

Strabo (*Geography* 8.3.30) tells that Phidias, asked on what model he would make his statue of Zeus at Olympia, answered by quoting these three lines.
