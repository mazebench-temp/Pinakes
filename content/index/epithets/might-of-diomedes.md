## Meaning

βίη (“force, might”) + Διομήδεος (“of Diomedes”): “the might of Diomedes,” that is, Diomedes himself in his strength. Epic names a hero in this way with βίη and a genitive or an adjective, as in [[might-of-heracles]] (βίη Ἡρακληείη), [[might-of-eteocles]] and [[might-of-priam]]. For Diomedes the phrase occurs only here. The translation renders it “mighty Diomedes.”

## Use

{{quote:efd1a3f9-066a-5f10-b85c-5e20a06837df}}

Hera and Athena come to the place where the best of the Achaeans are standing around Diomedes like beasts at bay ([[eating-raw-flesh]], [[lions-and-boars]]). The periphrasis names him by his strength at a moment when he is resting from his wound (5.794–798). In the same book Heracles is named by his might (5.638), and Dione tells how Heracles shot Hera and Hades (5.392–404; [[heracles-wounds-hera-and-hades]]). Diomedes has already wounded Aphrodite, and he is about to wound Ares: the only mortals in Book 5 who wound gods are the two it names by their might ([[gods-wounded-by-mortals]], [[tamer-of-horses]]).
