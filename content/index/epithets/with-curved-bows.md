## Meaning

ἀγκυλότοξος = ἀγκύλος (“bent, curved”) + τόξον (“bow”): “with curved bows.” The same ἀγκύλος describes the bow itself, ἀγκύλα τόξα (5.209; [[curved-bow]]), and a chariot. Homer uses the compound twice, both times of the Paeonians from the river Axius in the north: “Pyraechmes led the Paeonians with their curved bows” (2.848), and in Dolon's account of the Trojan camp (10.428). Elsewhere in the Iliad the Paeonians have other epithets: Pyraechmes leads them as men “who fight from chariots” (16.287; [[chariot-fighters]]), and Asteropaeus, who has been at Troy only eleven days when he faces Achilles, leads them as “long-speared” (δολιχεγχέας, 21.154–156). Compare [[arrow-fighters]] (ἰόμωροι) and [[skilled-with-the-bow]].

## Use

{{quote:7f7baf55-76e2-5b17-904e-9964c19283fd}}

Odysseus asks Dolon how the Trojans and their allies sleep, and Dolon names the allies by their places in the camp, each people with its epithet: toward the sea the Carians, the Paeonians with their curved bows, the Leleges, the Caucones and the brilliant Pelasgians; toward Thymbre the Lycians, the proud Mysians, the Phrygians who fight from horses and the Maeonians who fight from chariots ([[brilliant]], [[proud]], [[horse-fighting]], [[chariot-fighters]], [[trojan-allies]]). The list does not follow the Trojan Catalogue, which has neither the Leleges nor the Caucones (2.816–877). The Paeonians keep the epithet the Catalogue gave them. See [[paeonians]] and [[dolon]].
