## Meaning

μέροψ occurs in Homer only in the plural and only in formulas: μέροπες ἄνθρωποι, μερόπεσσι βροτοῖσι. Its meaning is unknown. The ancient explanation most often repeated analyzes it as μείρομαι (“divide”) + ὄψ (“voice”): “dividing the voice,” that is, speaking articulately, as animals do not. Modern scholars find this doubtful, and none of the other proposals has won general agreement. The translation gives the sense of the context, “mortal.”

The same word is the name of a bird, the bee-eater, and of a man: Merops of Percote, a Trojan ally (Il. 2.831).

## Use

{{quote:dd9be6f9-e43e-59b3-9093-e57b2633665e}}

Nestor has outlived two generations of men and now rules the third. The formula sets his long life against the ordinary span of human generations. Book 1 has other words for humankind as opposed to the gods: [[mortal-men]] (θνητοὶ ἄνθρωποι) and [[upon-the-earth]] (ἐπιχθόνιοι).
