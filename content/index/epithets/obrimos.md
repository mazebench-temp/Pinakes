## Meaning

ὄβριμος means “heavy, mighty,” and belongs with βρίθω (“be heavy”) and βριθύς (“heavy”). Of weapons it is the “heavy spear” ([[heavy-spear]]). Of persons it describes Ares (5.845; Il. 13.444, 13.521, 15.112, 16.613, 17.529) and Hector (Il. 8.473, 10.200, 11.347, 14.44), and once Achilles, whom his horse Xanthus addresses as ὄβριμʼ Ἀχιλλεῦ (Il. 19.408). Two compounds are built on it. Athena is ὀβριμοπάτρη, “daughter of the mighty father” ([[daughter-of-a-mighty-father]]), and Dione calls Heracles ὀβριμοεργός, a “doer of violent deeds” ([[doer-of-violent-deeds]]).

The translation renders ὄβριμος “mighty” of persons and “heavy” of the spear. “Mighty” also renders ἴφθιμος ([[mighty]]) and κρατερός ([[krateros]]).

The epithet of weight and force comes at the moment when force is no help to the god. He cannot see the goddess who will drive Diomedes' spear into his flank (5.855–857; [[helmet-of-hades]]). Book 5 names him by his power in each of his reverses: “insatiable of war” as he bellows with the wound ([[insatiable-of-war]]), and “ruin of mortals” in the last line, when the goddesses have stopped his slaughter ([[ruin-of-mortals]]).
