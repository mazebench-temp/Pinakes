## Meaning

ἐγχέσπαλος = ἔγχος (“spear”) + πάλλω (“shake, brandish”): “brandishing the spear.” It occurs three times in the Iliad: of the Trojan allies here, of the Trojan Polydamas (Il. 14.449), and of Ares himself in a simile (Il. 15.605). Compare σακέσπαλος, “wielder of the shield,” an epithet of Tydeus (Il. 5.126; [[wielder-of-the-shield]]), and [[fighters-with-the-spear]] (ἐγχεσίμωρος).

## Use

Agamemnon's test speech explains why Troy has not fallen. The Achaeans outnumber the Trojans ten to one, but the Trojans are helped by others:

{{quote:809ad908-2b5f-522f-b7a2-1865426b47a4}}

This is the poem's first mention of the Trojan allies, the ἐπίκουροι. The epithet gives them the one quality that counts in Agamemnon's reckoning: they are fighters. They will be named at the end of the book, people by people, in the Trojan Catalogue ([[trojan-catalogue]]), and Iris' warning to Hector turns on the variety of their languages ({{ref:Iliad 2.803-806}}).
