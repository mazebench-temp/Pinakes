## Meaning

βροτολοιγός = βροτός (“mortal”) + λοιγός (“ruin, destruction”; [[loigos]]): “ruin of mortals.” λοιγός is death on a large scale, the ruin that Apollo's plague brings on the army in Book 1. Homer gives the compound to Ares alone, thirteen times, five of them in Book 5. Outside Book 5 it serves mostly in a comparison: a hero at the height of his fury is “equal to Ares, ruin of mortals” (βροτολοιγῷ ἶσος Ἄρηϊ): Hector (Il. 11.295, 13.802), Leonteus (Il. 12.130), Achilles (Il. 20.46), and the Phaeacian Euryalus (Od. 8.115). The god's other epithets in Book 5 describe him from other sides: [[blood-stained]], [[stormer-of-walls]], [[furious]], [[baneful]], [[insatiable-of-war]], [[bronze-ares]] and [[obrimos]].

## Use

{{quote:7bcddba5-17e4-5578-b537-1a6e8999873f}}

Athena opens her persuasion with a line of three epithets, all of them names for what Ares does to men ([[blood-stained]], [[stormer-of-walls]]). The name is doubled, and the two halves do not scan alike: in Ἆρες Ἄρες the first α is long and the second short, as the accents show. Apollo speaks the same line when he sends Ares back into the battle (5.455). The words that led the god out of the fighting bring him back to it.

The narrator uses the epithet three more times, at the turns of Ares' day. He keeps the Trojans at their toil with Apollo and Strife (5.517–518; [[strife]]). He sees Diomedes coming at him in Athena's chariot (5.846), and is wounded a few lines later. And the book ends with the goddesses returning to Olympus, “having stopped Ares, ruin of mortals, from his slaughter of men” (5.909). The epithet that names his power over mortals is the last thing said of him after a mortal has wounded him ([[ares]], [[diomedes]]).

## Outside Homer

In the Hesiodic *Shield of Heracles* the same epithet names Ares when Athena advises Heracles to wait for him and strike where his body is exposed (*Shield* 333–337), and when Heracles does so (425). There too Athena turns the god's spear aside, and the hero wounds him in the thigh (453–462).
