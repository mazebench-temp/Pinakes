## Meaning

πολύμνηστος = πολύς (“much, many”) + μνάομαι (“woo”), the verb of the suitors, μνηστῆρες ([[the-suitors]]): “wooed by many”.

## Use

It is said twice of Penelope, in the same opening, by men who are wrong about her. The suitors, hearing her cry as she prays, say “Surely a marriage for us the much-wooed queen / is preparing” (*Odyssey* 4.770–771); and at the end of the poem those outside the house, hearing the music of the dance that Odysseus has ordered, say in the same opening that someone has married the much-wooed queen, who could not keep her husband's house until he came (*Odyssey* 23.148–151). Between them Eumaeus uses it of another woman, the wife that a kind master gives a good servant, with a house and a plot of land (*Odyssey* 14.62–64). See [[penelope]] and [[tis-speech]].
