## Meaning

κρατερῶνυξ = κρατερός (“strong”; [[krateros]]) + ὄνυξ (“claw, nail, hoof”): “with strong hooves,” or of beasts of prey “with strong claws.” The poems use it of three kinds of animal. Horses are strong-hoofed (5.329; Il. 16.724, 16.732; Od. 21.30), and so are mules (Il. 24.277; Od. 6.253); so too, strong-clawed, are the wolves and lions that fawn on Odysseus' men at Circe's house (Od. 10.218). The same ὄνυξ is in [[single-hoofed]] (μῶνυξ).

## Use

Sthenelus has given Aeneas' horses to Deipylus. Then he mounts his own chariot, takes the glossy reins, “and at once drove the strong-hoofed horses after the son of Tydeus” (5.328–329; [[glossy]], [[sthenelus]]). The team is his own, the one he drives for Diomedes, which he held away from the din a moment before (5.321–322; [[single-hoofed]]). Diomedes, on foot, has gone after Aphrodite ([[diomedes-wounds-aphrodite]]).
