## Meaning

ἕρκος is a fence or enclosing wall, such as the wall around a courtyard or a vineyard, and so anything that keeps harm out. It is related to ἔργω ("shut in, keep out"). The same noun gives the phrase ἕρκος ὀδόντων, "the fence of the teeth" (see [[what-a-word]]).

## Use

Nestor ends his appeal to Agamemnon by asking him to let go of his anger against Achilles:

{{quote:6672ee30-ef65-5f4f-a1c7-3b8154a4c849}}

The compliment is also an argument: the army cannot do without Achilles. Agamemnon does not listen, and the books that follow show what it means for the Achaeans to fight without their wall.

Elsewhere in the Iliad, "bulwark of the Achaeans" (ἕρκος Ἀχαιῶν) is a title of Telamonian [[ajax|Ajax]] (3.229, 6.5), the man who holds the line while Achilles is away.
