## Meaning

ἱππηλάτα = ἵππος (“horse”) + ἐλαύνω (“drive”): “driver of horses,” the man who drives a chariot team. The nominative in -α is an old epic formation, like ἱππότα in the title of Nestor ([[gerenian-horseman]]) and νεφεληγερέτα of Zeus ([[cloud-gatherer]]). Everywhere else in Homer it stands in the phrase γέρων ἱππηλάτα, “old horse-driving”: Peleus (Il. 7.125, 9.438), Oeneus (Il. 9.581), Phoenix (Il. 9.432) and, in the Odyssey, Nestor (Od. 3.436). Tydeus alone has it without the old man's word. Compare [[driver-of-horses]] (πλήξιππος), [[tamer-of-horses]] (ἱππόδαμος) and [[goaders-of-horses]].

## Use

{{quote:c242d099-8d29-56bd-80a8-d476a09a59da}}

Agamemnon's story gives Tydeus two horseman's titles: “son of wise-hearted Tydeus, tamer of horses” (4.370) and “horse-driving Tydeus” here. Both praise the chariot-fighter, and the son who hears them is standing idle among his horses and his chariot (4.365–366; [[well-joined]]). Elsewhere the title belongs to old men. Here it is given to a father in his prime, whom Agamemnon remembers in order to shame his son ([[tydeus]], [[tydeus-at-thebes]], [[contests]]). It is also the title of Tydeus' own father Oeneus, “old horse-driving” in Phoenix's story in Book 9 (Il. 9.581).
