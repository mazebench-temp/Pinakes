## Meaning

δέελος is an epic form of δῆλος (“clear, plain to see”). Homer uses it only once, at 10.466. The compound εὐδείελος, the Odyssey's epithet of Ithaca, is usually taken from the same root, “clear-seen, conspicuous” (Od. 2.167, 9.21, 13.234). The mark is a σῆμα, the word for a sign of any kind, which elsewhere in the book is the mound of Ilus (10.415; [[the-tomb-of-ilus]]).

## Use

{{quote:7faf4cf8-9291-530a-981c-2aa4d4f8918e}}

Odysseus sets Dolon's spoils on a tamarisk and puts “a clear mark” by them, reeds and luxuriant tamarisk branches gathered together, so that they would not miss the place when they came back through the swift black night ([[flourishing]], [[swift-night]], [[of-the-spoil]]). On the way back the mark serves: they come to the place where they killed Hector's spy, and Diomedes jumps down for the spoils (10.526–529; [[bloody-spoils]]). See [[odysseus]] and [[dolon]].
