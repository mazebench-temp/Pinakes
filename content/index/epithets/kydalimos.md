## Meaning

κυδάλιμος is formed from κῦδος (“glory, the radiance of success”; [[kydos]]): “glorious.” It praises heroes and peoples, and Menelaus above all, in the line-end formula Μενελάου κυδαλίμοιο, which recurs in both poems. It can also describe the proud heart: in Book 10 Agamemnon lies awake and “his glorious heart groaned” (κυδάλιμον κῆρ, Il. 10.16). The translation renders it “glorious,” as it does [[glorious]] (ἐρικυδής), from the same noun. Compare [[most-glorious]] (κύδιστος) and [[exultant]] (ὑπερκύδας).

## Use

The first two uses in Book 4 come from those who wish Menelaus dead or fear that he will die. Athena, as Laodocus, promises Pandarus “thanks and glory from all the Trojans” (χάριν καὶ κῦδος, 4.95) if he will shoot at glorious Menelaus:

{{quote:62046cc7-8f34-5a58-853b-51bbda20bdcd}}

Pandarus would win glory by killing the glorious man. Agamemnon, fearing his brother's death, imagines a Trojan “leaping on the tomb of glorious Menelaus” and mocking him (4.176–177; [[overweening]]). The third use is the narrator's: when Sthenelus answers Agamemnon's taunt about the fathers, he is “the son of glorious Capaneus” (4.403; [[capaneus]]).
