## Meaning

αἰολόπωλος = αἰόλος (“quick-moving; glittering, many-colored”) + πῶλος (“foal, horse”): “with quick horses,” or “with dappled horses.” αἰόλος joins the ideas of rapid movement and changing light, as in Hector's [[of-the-gleaming-helmet]] (κορυθαίολος). The word is rare. The Phrygians are horsemen elsewhere in the Iliad too: in Dolon's account of the Trojan allies they are Φρύγες ἱππόμαχοι, “Phrygians who fight from horses” (Il. 10.431).

## Use

{{quote:6e7db609-0616-5bc0-8eb4-67136c9689a8}}

Priam blesses Agamemnon for the size of his army and compares it with the greatest host he ever saw: the Phrygians encamped along the Sangarius, under Otreus and Mygdon, when he fought beside them as an ally against the Amazons ([[priam-and-the-amazons]]). The horses belong to the picture of a great army on the march. Even so, Priam says, they were fewer than the Achaeans. See [[phrygia]], [[otreus]], [[mygdon]], [[sangarius]].
