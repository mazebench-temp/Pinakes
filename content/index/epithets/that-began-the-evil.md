## Meaning

ἀρχέκακος = ἄρχω (“begin”) + κακόν (“evil”): “beginning evil.” The word occurs only here, of the ships in which Alexander sailed for Sparta and brought back Helen. The next words unfold the compound: the ships “became an evil (κακόν) for all the Trojans and for himself.” See [[ships]] and [[balanced-ships]].

## Use

{{quote:9906a6ab-fb26-5b59-8bab-d82d9ba5c3f5}}

The short obituary of Phereclus holds the cause of the war. The craftsman whom Athena loved built the ships that began it, and he dies in it at the hand of Meriones ([[phereclus]], [[meriones]], [[paris]]). “Himself” in 5.64 may be Phereclus or Alexander; the Greek allows both, and Homer does not say what the oracles were. The epithet reaches back from a single death in the battle to the voyage that brought all the deaths of the war ([[the-war-in-small]]).

## Outside Homer

Herodotus says of the twenty ships that Athens sent to help the Ionians in their revolt against Persia that “these ships were the beginning of evils for Greeks and barbarians” (αὗται δὲ αἱ νέες ἀρχὴ κακῶν ἐγένοντο, 5.97.3), a phrase often compared with this line. His history begins with the causes of the enmity between Greeks and barbarians, and the Persian account he reports there includes Alexander's theft of Helen (1.1–4).
