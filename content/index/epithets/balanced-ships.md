## Meaning

ἐΐσος is the epic form of ἴσος (“equal”), with an extra initial ε-: “even, equal on both sides.” Of ships it means well-proportioned, trim. The same adjective describes the “equal feast” in which each gets his share ([[equal-feast]], δαιτὸς ἐΐσης, {{ref:Iliad 1.468}}), and a shield that is balanced all round (ἀσπὶς πάντοσ’ ἐΐση).

## Use

{{quote:a0de976c-697b-50b8-9570-f22802864ac2}}

The assembly breaks up, and Achilles goes back to his huts and his balanced ships. The epithet is ornamental, but after a quarrel about unequal shares the word for equality has an edge. Achilles has just complained that he never receives a prize equal to Agamemnon's (οὐ μὲν σοί ποτε ἶσον ἔχω γέρας, {{ref:Iliad 1.163}}), and Agamemnon has said he will take Briseis so that no one else will dare to speak to him as an equal (ἶσον ἐμοὶ φάσθαι, {{ref:Iliad 1.187}}).
