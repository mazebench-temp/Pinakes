## Meaning

αἰολομίτρης = αἰόλος (“quick-moving, flashing”) + μίτρη (“band”), the guard worn below the belt and corselet ([[band]]). What the μίτρη looked like is disputed. The word occurs only here. It is built like [[of-the-flashing-corselet]] (αἰολοθώρηξ) and [[with-linen-corselet]] (λινοθώρηξ), epithets that name a warrior by a piece of his armor. The same μίτρη guards Ares, who is wounded low in the flank “where he girded on his band” (5.857).

## Use

{{quote:831d0f24-6154-5401-9042-14f15b6b8b65}}

Hector and Ares kill six men, and the narrator names them in a list that begins with a question, “Then whom first, whom last did they slay?” (5.703–707; [[whom-first-whom-last]]). Oresbius is the last of the list and the only one given more than a name and an epithet: his town Hyle on the Cephisian lake, his care for his wealth, and his Boeotian neighbors ([[oresbius]], [[hyle]], [[boeotians]]). The flashing band is his mark in battle, the lake and the rich land his life at home. Such short obituaries are the war seen in small ([[the-war-in-small]], [[androktasia]]).
