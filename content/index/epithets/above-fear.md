## Meaning

The word stands only at 17.330, in the accusative, ὑπερδέα δῆμον. Walter Leaf reports three explanations (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 17.330): Eustathius takes it as “superior to fear,” from δέος (“fear”); the lexicon of Apollonius as “excessively deficient,” that is, much inferior in strength, from δέω (“lack”); and Döderlein as “excessively timid.” Leaf calls all three “virtually impossible” and adopts the conjecture ὑπὲρ Δία, “beyond Zeus,” which answers ὑπὲρ θεόν, “in defiance of a god,” three verses before (17.327). The translation follows the printed word in Eustathius' sense, “above fear,” which agrees with the trust in numbers in the same verse (see the translation's note on 17.330).
