## Meaning

χαλκόπους = χαλκός (“bronze”; [[bronze]]) + πούς (“foot”): “bronze-footed,” with hooves of bronze. The things of the gods are made of bronze, gold and silver. Hera's chariot has bronze wheels on an iron axle, with rims of imperishable gold, tires of bronze and hubs of silver (5.722–726), the house of Zeus is “bronze-floored” (1.426; [[bronze-floored]]), and the threshold of Tartarus is bronze (8.15; [[tartarus]]).

The word occurs only in the dual, χαλκόποδʼ ἵππω, at the start of two chariot journeys of the gods. Zeus yokes his horses to drive to Ida (8.41–44), and Poseidon yokes his at Aegae to drive to the Achaean ships (13.23–26). The four verses of each passage are almost the same, and the second verse describes both teams as “swift-flying, with flowing manes of gold” (8.42, 13.24; [[swift-flying]]).

## Use

{{quote:9ba0e158-ac29-5d16-880d-1769ff07713e}}

Zeus has forbidden the gods to fight and has answered Athena kindly (8.1–40). He arms in gold, as Poseidon will in Book 13, and the horses carry him “between earth and the starry sky” to Gargarus on Ida, where he unyokes them and pours mist around them (8.45–50; [[starry]], [[gargarus]], [[chariot-journey]]). From there he watches the city and the ships, and holds the scales of the day (8.51–74; [[watching-the-war]], [[the-scales-of-zeus]]). At the end of the day he drives them home to Olympus, and Poseidon unyokes them for him (8.438–441; [[well-wheeled]]). See [[horses]] and [[zeus]].
