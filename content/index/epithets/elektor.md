## Meaning

ἠλέκτωρ is a name of the sun, “the beaming one.” Walter Leaf calls it evidently cognate with ἤλεκτρον, the bright amber or electrum of the Odyssey, “but in what sense it would be rash to say” (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 6.513). It occurs twice in the Iliad, both times in the same half-verse of a man shining in armor, τεύχεσι παμφαίνων ὥς τʼ ἠλέκτωρ: alone, as the sun itself, when Paris comes down from Pergamus “gleaming in his armor like the shining sun” (6.513), and as the epithet of Hyperion when Achilles takes his stand on the chariot “gleaming in his armor like shining Hyperion” (19.398; [[like-the-sun]], [[hyperion]], [[helios]]). The *Homeric Hymn to Apollo* has the same pair of words, ἠλέκτωρ Ὑπερίων, when Apollo tells the dead serpent that black Earth and the beaming Hyperion will rot it where it lies (*Homeric Hymn to Apollo* 369).
