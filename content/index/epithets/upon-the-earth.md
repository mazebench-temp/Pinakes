## Meaning

ἐπιχθόνιος = ἐπί (“upon”) + χθών (“earth”): “living on the earth.” Its opposite is ἐπουράνιος, “in heaven,” an epithet of the gods.

Both uses serve Nestor's comparison between past and present. The Lapiths were the mightiest of men ever raised on earth, and no mortal now on earth could fight them. The Iliad measures the decline of men in the same way elsewhere, in the phrase οἷοι νῦν βροτοί εἰσι, “such as mortals are now” (Il. 5.304), used when Diomedes lifts a stone that two men of today could not carry.
