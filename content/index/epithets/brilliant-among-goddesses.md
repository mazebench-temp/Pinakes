## Meaning

δῖα, the feminine of δῖος ([[brilliant]]), + θεάων, the genitive plural of θεά (“goddess”): “brilliant among goddesses.” It is the goddess's counterpart of δῖα γυναικῶν, “brilliant among women,” which the Iliad gives to Alcestis and to Helen (2.714, 3.171). The formula ends the verse. The Iliad has it seven times: of Dione (5.381), Athena (6.305, Il. 18.205), Hera (Il. 14.184), Charis, the wife of Hephaestus (Il. 18.388), and Thetis (Il. 19.6, 24.93). The Odyssey has it more than twenty times, most of them of Calypso and Circe. The praise is general; it names nothing about any one goddess but her rank.

## Use

In its first use Dione, “brilliant among goddesses,” answers her daughter Aphrodite, whom Diomedes has wounded, with stories of gods who suffered at the hands of men (5.381–415; [[dione]], [[gods-wounded-by-mortals]]). In Book 6 the formula closes the first verse of the Trojan women's prayer to the goddess who had set Diomedes on:
