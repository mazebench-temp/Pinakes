## Meaning

μένεα, plural of μένος (“might, fury, spirit”) + πνείοντες (“breathing,” from πνέω): “breathing fury.” μένος is the surge of force and spirit that fills a warrior, often put into him by a god; here it is breathed out, as if it could be felt on the breath. The phrase occurs three times in Homer: of the Abantes, and twice of the Achaeans as they march or stand against the enemy (Il. 3.8, 11.508).

## Use

{{quote:47736121-c8c1-5759-aa0a-6413c90e4751}}

The Abantes are the Catalogue's most vividly described people. Within a few lines they “breathe fury,” they are “swift,” with their “hair long at the back” ([[long-haired]]), and they are “spearmen, eager with outstretched ash spears to tear the corselets around the chests of their enemies” ({{ref:Iliad 2.542-544}}; [[swift]], [[spearmen]]). In Book 3 the whole Achaean army marches out “in silence, breathing fury” (Il. 3.8), against the noisy Trojans.
