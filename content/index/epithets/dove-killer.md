## Meaning

φασσοφόνος = φάσσα (“wood pigeon, dove”) + -φόνος (“killing,” the root of φόνος, “slaughter”): “dove-killer.” Homer has it once, of the hawk (15.238), and φάσσα, the wood pigeon, is not named anywhere else in Homer; the dove of the similes is the πέλεια ([[doves]]). The epithet names the hawk as the hunter of smaller birds, as in the simile of Achilles pursuing Hector like a mountain hawk after a dove (22.139–142; [[hawk]]).

## Outside Homer

In Aristotle's *History of Animals* the φασσοφόνος is one of the kinds of hawk (9.36), and the cymindis is the size of “the hawk called the dove-killer” (9.12), so that Homer's epithet has become the name of a species (see the *Iliad* 15 note on [[aristotle]]).
