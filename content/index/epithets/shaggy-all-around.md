## Meaning

ἀμφιδάσεια is the feminine of ἀμφιδασύς = ἀμφί (“around, on both sides”) + δασύς (“thick, hairy, shaggy”): “shaggy all around.” Homer uses it once, of the aegis (15.309). It may describe the shaggy hide of the aegis itself, or its fringe: the aegis that Athena carries has a hundred tassels of gold (2.448), and its formula calls it “tasseled” (5.738, 15.229; [[tasseled]]). The translation's note on 15.309 leaves both senses open. Compare [[shaggy]] (λαχνήεις).

## Outside Homer

Herodotus thought that the Greeks took the dress and the aegis of Athena's statues from the women of Libya, who wore goatskins fringed with tassels and dyed red; the name αἰγίς, he says, comes from these goatskins, αἰγέαι. He adds that the Libyan goatskins are bare of hair and that their tassels are of leather thongs, not snakes (Herodotus 4.189; [[aegis]]).
