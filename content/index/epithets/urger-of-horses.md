## Meaning

ἱπποκέλευθος = ἵππος (“horse”) + κέλευθος (“path, course”), the word of the “watery ways” of the sea ([[watery-ways]]): one whose course is made with horses, a fighter who goes out in a chariot. How the two parts combine, “traveling with horses” or “urging horses on his course,” is not certain. The translation renders it “urger of horses,” keeping “driver of horses” for πλήξιππος ([[driver-of-horses]]).

The word fills the end of the verse after the vocative Πατρόκλεες, and it is never used of anyone else. Achilles says it when he sends Patroclus out to arm (16.126), the narrator when he turns to speak to Patroclus in his charge at the Lycians (16.584), and Hector when he imagines Achilles' last orders to the friend he has just killed (16.839). The book also calls Patroclus “horseman” in the same place in the verse, Πατρόκλεες ἱππεῦ ([[hippeus]]), and its last line is the immortal horses that the gods gave to Peleus, carrying Automedon away (16.866–867; [[the-horses-of-achilles]], [[automedon]]).
