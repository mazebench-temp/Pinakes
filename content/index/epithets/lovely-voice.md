## Meaning

ὀπί, dative of ὄψ (“voice,” related to Latin *vōx*) + καλῇ (“beautiful”): “with beautiful voice.” The phrase is formulaic for goddesses who sing. In the Odyssey Calypso and Circe sing at their looms ὀπὶ καλῇ (Od. 5.61, 10.221).

## Use

{{quote:a4a16f28-c808-5412-9390-986084543dde}}

The Muses sing in turn (ἀμειβόμεναι, “answering one another”) while Apollo plays the lyre. The god whose arrows brought the plague closes the book as a musician ([[feast-type-scene]]).

The same half-line describes the Muses singing the lament at Achilles' funeral in the Odyssey (Od. 24.60). The Muses who sing at the gods' feast at the end of Iliad 1 will one day mourn the hero whose wrath the book began with.
