## Meaning

ἄσβεστος = ἀ- (“not”) + σβέννυμι (“quench, put out”): “unquenchable,” like a fire that cannot be put out. Homer uses it of fire, of a battle cry ([[unquenchable-cry]]), and of fame (κλέος ἄσβεστον). English “asbestos” comes from the same word.

## Use

{{quote:6616b7bf-55ac-57bf-a00c-f4b390ef615b}}

The gods laugh as Hephaestus bustles through the hall pouring nectar for them, a cupbearer unlike the usual ones; elsewhere in the Iliad it is Hebe who pours the nectar (Il. 4.2–3). The laughter ends the quarrel that Zeus' threats had only silenced ([[feast-type-scene]]).

The same verse describes the gods' laughter at Hephaestus' trap for Ares and Aphrodite in the Odyssey (Od. 8.326). Both times Hephaestus is the cause.

## Outside Homer

“Homeric laughter” (French *rire homérique*) became a proverbial name for loud, unrestrained laughter, from these passages.
