## Meaning

πότνια means “mistress, lady,” a woman with authority. It is one of the oldest words of Greek religion. The Linear B tablets name a goddess *po-ti-ni-ja*, “the Mistress,” and the word is related to πόσις (“husband, master”) and to Sanskrit *pátnī* (“mistress, wife”). In Homer it is used of goddesses, as in πότνια θηρῶν, “mistress of wild beasts,” for Artemis (Il. 21.470), and of mothers in the formula πότνια μήτηρ.

## Use

{{quote:316c4283-a76a-5c64-9cda-75279f631eaf}}

When Achilles weeps on the shore, his lady mother hears him from the depths of the sea. The line-end phrase πότνια μήτηρ gives Thetis a title of respect at the moment she comes to comfort her son.

With Hera the word is part of the formula βοῶπις πότνια Ἥρη ([[ox-eyed]], {{ref:Iliad 1.551}}, {{ref:Iliad 1.568}}), “ox-eyed queen Hera.” In Book 1 it names her at the two turns of her quarrel with Zeus: her challenge and her submission.
