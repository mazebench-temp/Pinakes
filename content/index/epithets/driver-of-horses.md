## Meaning

πλήξιππος = πλήσσω (“strike”) + ἵππος (“horse”): “who strikes the horses,” the driver who urges on his team. Homer gives it four times: to Pelops, to the Athenian leader Menestheus (Il. 4.327), to a Greek named Orestes killed by Hector (Il. 5.705), and to Oileus, a Trojan charioteer killed by Agamemnon (Il. 11.93). Compare [[tamer-of-horses]] (ἱππόδαμος) and [[gerenian-horseman]] (ἱππότα).

## Use

In the history of the scepter Pelops is the first mortal to hold it. Hermes gives it to him, and he passes it to Atreus ({{ref:Iliad 2.104-105}}; [[the-scepter-of-agamemnon]]). Homer tells nothing else about Pelops, and the epithet is the only hint of what later tradition made his story: a chariot race.

## Outside Homer

Pelops won his bride Hippodameia by defeating her father Oenomaus in a chariot race, with horses given by Poseidon. Pindar tells the story in his first Olympian ode. The race was one of the founding myths of the games at Olympia, and it was carved on the east pediment of the temple of Zeus there.
