## Meaning

ἠΰκομος = ἠΰς (“good,” as in ἐΰ-) + κόμη (“hair”): “with beautiful hair.” It is used of goddesses and of women; in the Catalogue of Ships Briseis is ἠΰκομος (Il. 2.689).

## Use

{{quote:b3f8c509-a034-599e-b0f0-fe6f8c49b1d1}}

The line gives the god of the prayer his parentage: “lord Apollo, whom Leto of the lovely hair bore.” It recalls the Iliad's first answer to its first question, “the son of Leto and of Zeus” ({{ref:Iliad 1.9}}; [[son-of-zeus]]). Leto's epithet is conventional praise of beauty. Its point here is that she is Apollo's mother: the priest appeals to the god through his lineage.
