## Meaning

The second half of the compound is φωνή, “voice”; the first is disputed. Walter Leaf reports that Döderlein derived it from ἀείρω, “lift,” as “lifting up the voice,” though the formation is unusual; that from ἀήρ, “mist,” it would mean “misty-voiced,” “from which no good sense can be got”; and that Ahrens conjectured ἱεροφώνων, “strong-voiced” (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 18.505). A herald's voice must carry across an assembly or a battlefield, and the translation's “far-voiced” keeps the word apart from the other epithets of heralds, λιγύφθογγος, “clear-voiced” (2.50; [[clear-voiced]]), and ἠπύτα, “loud-voiced” (7.384; [[loud-voiced]]); see the translation's note on 18.505. See [[heralds]].
