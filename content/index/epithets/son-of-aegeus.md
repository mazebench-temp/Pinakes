## Meaning

Αἰγεΐδης = Αἰγεύς + the patronymic suffix -ίδης: “son of Aegeus.”

## Use

{{quote:f8911627-9361-5cdd-b709-b75841ae189d}}

The whole verse is doubtful: it is missing from many manuscripts and occurs word for word in the Hesiodic *Shield of Heracles* (182), in a list of Lapiths. See [[like-the-immortals]]. The Iliad says nothing more of Aegeus or of Theseus. Later tradition made Aegeus a king of Athens and Theseus the city's great hero, which is why the line has been suspected as an Athenian addition.
