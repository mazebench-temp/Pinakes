## Meaning

κέντωρ is formed from κεντέω (“prick, goad”), the verb of the driver's goad (κέντρον): “one who goads horses.” The phrase κέντορες ἵππων occurs twice in Homer: of the Cadmeians here (4.391), and of the Trojans, when Pandarus calls on the “great-hearted Trojans, goaders of horses” after wounding Diomedes (Il. 5.102). Like [[driver-of-horses]] and [[horse-driving]], it names men by their chariots.

## Use

{{quote:7c0ba908-601b-5172-acf4-16a57a6e9274}}

Beaten by Tydeus in the contests, the Thebans answer with an ambush. Agamemnon's story gives the Cadmeians their horsemen's title just as they turn from games to killing, and it gives their two leaders heroic epithets as well ([[like-the-immortals]], [[steadfast-in-battle]]). The praise raises the victory of Tydeus, who kills all the fifty but one (4.396–398; [[cadmeians]], [[tydeus-at-thebes]]).
