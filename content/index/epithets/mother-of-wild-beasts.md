## Meaning

A land is the “mother” of the creatures it feeds ([[meter]]). Homer calls lands rich in sheep “mother of flocks,” μήτηρ μήλων: Iton in Thessaly (2.696), Phthia (9.479), Thrace (11.222) and Pylos (Od. 15.226). Ida, a mountain of forests and springs ([[of-the-many-springs]]), is the mother of θῆρες, the wild beasts of the mountains that men and dogs hunt in the similes. Artemis, who shoots them, is πότνια θηρῶν, “mistress of wild beasts” (21.470; [[queenly]]).

The phrase occurs in one verse, Ἴδην δʼ ἵκανεν πολυπίδακα μητέρα θηρῶν (8.47), which returns with the verb in the dual for Hera and Sleep (14.283) and in the plural for Iris and Apollo (15.151). Each time a god arrives on the mountain on which Zeus has his seat.

## Use

Zeus drives from Olympus and comes “to Ida of the many springs, mother of wild beasts, to Gargarus, where he has a precinct and a fragrant altar” (8.47–48; [[gargarus]], [[fragrant]]). He unyokes his horses, hides them in mist and sits on the peaks to watch the city and the ships (8.49–52; [[watching-the-war]]). The mountain of the beasts is the god's lookout over the war.
