## Meaning

ὠκύμορος = ὠκύς (“swift”) + μόρος (“fate, death,” from μείρομαι, “receive one's portion”): “whose death comes quickly.” The first element is the adjective of [[swift-footed]] (πόδας ὠκύς): the speed of Achilles' feet and the speed of his death are named with the same word.

In the Odyssey the word describes the suitors, who would all be “swift-fated and bitter in their marriage” if Odysseus came home (Od. 1.266).

The superlative turns lament into argument. Because Achilles' life is short, his honor must come now. This is Achilles' own reasoning in his prayer to her ({{ref:Iliad 1.352-353}}; see [[short-lived]]), and Thetis carries it to Zeus.

Thetis knows more than she says here. Later in the poem Achilles reveals that his mother told him of two fates: a short life with undying glory at Troy, or a long life without it at home (Il. 9.410–416).
