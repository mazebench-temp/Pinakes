## Meaning

ποδηνεκής = πούς, ποδ- (“foot”) + -ηνεκής, the element of “carrying, reaching” in διηνεκής (“stretching through, continuous”) and in δουρηνεκές, “a spear's throw” (10.357): “reaching to the feet.” Homer uses it three times. Twice it closes the verse that describes the lion skin worn on the night of Book 10, first by Agamemnon and then by Diomedes: δέρμα λέοντος / αἴθωνος μεγάλοιο ποδηνεκές, “the skin of a lion, a great tawny lion, reaching to his feet” (10.23–24, 10.177–178; [[tawny]], [[lion-skin]]). The third is a shield. Periphetes of Mycenae, turning back, tripped on the rim of the shield he carried, “which reached to his feet, a wall against javelins,” and Hector killed him as he lay (15.645–651). Compare [[man-covering]] (ἀμφιβρότη), the shield that covers the whole man.

## Use

{{quote:0919a63c-0b9e-5fc6-b3c1-faa59043705a}}

Diomedes, woken by Nestor's heel and sent to rouse swift Ajax and Meges, puts on his cloak, takes his spear and goes (10.175–179; [[swift]]). The two verses are the ones in which Agamemnon dressed at the start of the book (10.23–24), and the skin is the same full-length hide of a great lion; only the color word of Agamemnon's verse, δαφοινόν, is missing ([[blood-red]]). The king and the man who will lead the raid go out into the night in the same dress. See [[diomedes]] and [[agamemnon]].
