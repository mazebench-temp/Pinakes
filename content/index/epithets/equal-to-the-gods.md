## Meaning

ἰσόθεος = ἴσος (“equal”) + θεός (“god”): “equal to a god,” with φώς (“man,” a poetic word). The phrase ἰσόθεος φώς always closes the verse. It occurs fourteen times in Homer, of Patroclus, Telemachus and others. It belongs with the other words that compare a hero to the gods: [[godlike]] (θεοειδής, θεοείκελος) and [[antitheos]] (ἀντίθεος). Such praise does not make the man more than mortal. It measures his beauty or his strength by the gods' standard.

## Use

{{quote:35f5711c-53f2-58a3-9683-9adf696e7386}}

Euryalus is the third and least of the leaders from Argos and Tiryns, after Diomedes and Sthenelus. The formula gives him a hero's line-end in a passage that names him by his father and his grandfather. In Book 23 he appears again, “equal to the gods,” as the only man who dares to box against Epeius (Il. 23.677).
