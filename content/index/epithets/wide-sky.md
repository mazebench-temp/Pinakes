## Meaning

οὐρανός (“sky, heaven”; see [[sky]]) + εὐρύς (“wide, broad”). The wide sky is Zeus' portion of the world: when the three sons of Cronus divided it by lot, “Zeus got the wide sky in the upper air and the clouds” (Il. 15.192). The half-line ἰδὼν εἰς οὐρανὸν εὐρύν, “looking into the wide sky,” describes a man who speaks to Zeus.

## Use

{{quote:be99b5c1-cecb-55a2-8a55-2d2a4bf8b762}}

Menelaus' sword has broken on Paris' helmet. He looks up to the sky and complains to Zeus, whom he had asked for vengeance a few lines before (3.351–354; [[deadly]], [[father-zeus]]). The whole verse is used again when Achilles, trapped by the river, groans and looks to the sky (Il. 21.272). In Book 7 the armies pray “looking into the wide sky” while the lots are shaken for the duel of Hector and Ajax (Il. 7.178, 7.201).
