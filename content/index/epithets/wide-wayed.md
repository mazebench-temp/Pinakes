## Meaning

εὐρύπορος = εὐρύς (“wide”) + πόρος (“passage, way”): the sea of wide ways, open to be crossed in every direction. πόρος is the second part of ὠκύπορος, “swift-going” ([[swift-sailing-ships]]). Homer uses the word only in the verse-end θαλάσσης εὐρυπόροιο: once in the Iliad, in the simile of the Trojans coming over the wall like a wave over a ship's side (15.381), and twice in the Odyssey, of the shore where Menelaus prays at dawn before he takes Proteus, and of the wave that Odysseus' ship reaches when it leaves the stream of Oceanus (*Odyssey* 4.432, 12.2). Compare the sea's [[watery-ways]] and [[broad-back-of-the-sea]]. See [[sea]].
