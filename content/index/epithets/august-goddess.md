## Meaning

πρέσβα is the feminine of πρέσβυς (“old, elder”): “eldest,” and so “honored, august.” It is used of Ate, “the eldest daughter of Zeus” (Il. 19.91), and of Nestor's wife Eurydice, “eldest of the daughters of Clymenus” (Od. 3.452). The verse Ἥρη πρέσβα θεὰ θυγάτηρ μεγάλοιο Κρόνοιο, “Hera, the august goddess, daughter of great Cronus,” occurs four times: twice as Hera harnesses her horses (5.721, Il. 8.383), and twice in address, when Aphrodite and Sleep answer her in the deception of Zeus (θύγατερ, Il. 14.194, 14.243). In Book 4 Hera claims to be the most senior of the gods, both by birth and as the wife of Zeus (4.59–61; [[queenly]]), and πρέσβα may carry the same claim. The translation renders it “august.” Cronus is her father ([[cronus]], [[son-of-cronus]]).

## Use

{{quote:48924456-6d27-502d-91ba-215814bee263}}

The whole verse announces Hera as she does a groom's work. The queen of the gods, the eldest daughter of Cronus, harnesses her own horses ([[with-golden-headbands]]), while Hebe fits the wheels to the chariot (5.722–723; [[hebe]], [[chariot]]). The formal title comes with the practical task, and the chariot that follows is the most richly described in the poem (5.722–732). The verse returns when she and Athena set out again in Book 8 (Il. 8.381–383). That time Zeus sends Iris to turn them back.
