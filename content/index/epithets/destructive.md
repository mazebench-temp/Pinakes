## Meaning

δήϊος means “hostile, destructive.” It belongs with δηϊόω (“cut down, slay”), which Diomedes uses of Trojans and Achaeans “cut down” in battle (4.416–417), and with δηϊοτής (“battle”; [[grim-combat]]). As an epithet it describes fire, above all in the formula πυρὸς δηΐοιο, “consuming fire” ({{ref:Iliad 2.415}}; Il. 6.331, 11.667), and once war (4.281). As a noun the plural means “the enemy”: the Abantes tear the corselets “of their enemies” ({{ref:Iliad 2.544}}), and Tydeus fought “the enemy” far ahead of his companions (4.373).

## Use

{{quote:7717aa52-0413-53c3-924a-c95195ff5b2d}}

In the simile of the goatherd, the battalions around the two Ajaxes are as dark as a storm cloud over the sea when they move “into destructive war” (4.281; [[storm-cloud]], [[zeus-nurtured]]). The epithet that usually belongs to the fire that burns cities and ships is given here to the war that the storm cloud foretells.
