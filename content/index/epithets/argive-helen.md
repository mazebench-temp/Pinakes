## Meaning

Ἀργεῖος (“of Argos, Argive”) + Ἑλένη. Helen came from Sparta, not from the city of Argos. Here Argos has its wide sense, the land of the Achaeans as a whole ([[argos]]), and Ἀργείη means “Greek,” in contrast with Trojan. The accusative Ἀργείην Ἑλένην occurs eight times in the Iliad and once in the Odyssey, and the nominative twice in the Odyssey.

Athena repeats the lines to Odysseus ({{ref:Iliad 2.176-178}}). Helen has not yet appeared in the poem; she enters in Book 3. Here she is named as a prize and as the cause of the war: to leave her in Troy would give Priam and the Trojans something to boast of, and would make the deaths of so many Achaeans worthless. Nestor later names the war's purpose in the same way, to pay the Trojans back “for the struggles and groans for Helen's sake” ({{ref:Iliad 2.356}}).
