## Meaning

ἄφαλος = ἀ- (“not”) + φάλος, the “ridge” of a helmet, the metal band or boss that runs over the crown (3.362; 6.9); ἄλλοφος = ἀ- + λόφος, the “crest” of horsehair (3.337), with the λ doubled after the prefix. Both occur only here (10.258). They describe a helmet by negation, against the battle helmet that is ἀμφίφαλος, “with a ridge on either side,” and τετραφάληρος, “with four plates” ([[with-two-ridges-and-four-plates]]), or ἵππουρις, “with a horsehair crest” ([[horsehair-crested]]). The poet names the kind of cap that has neither, “the kind called a skullcap” (καταῖτυξ, also used only here), and the Suda later glosses that word as “a kind of helmet without a crest” (κ 901; [[helmet]]).

## Use

{{quote:5fdcd4f3-31a4-5510-af52-c501071559f3}}

Diomedes' cap is of bull's hide, “without ridge or crest,” the kind that guards the heads of young men in their prime ([[of-bulls-hide]], [[flourishing]]). A battle helmet is known by its ridge and its nodding crest: Hector's frightens his own child (6.469–470). The cap of the night raid has neither. See [[diomedes]], [[thrasymedes]] and [[the-boars-tusk-helmet]].
