## Meaning

ἄγγελος (“messenger,” the source of English “angel”) + Διός (“of Zeus”) + ἠδὲ καὶ ἀνδρῶν (“and of men too”). Heralds (κήρυκες) were under the protection of Zeus. Their persons were inviolable, and their office carried his authority. The same phrase describes heralds in Book 7 (Il. 7.274).

## Use

{{quote:aa22d460-ff64-53db-833b-a72bb73c538f}}

The heralds are afraid and say nothing ({{ref:Iliad 1.331-332}}). Achilles speaks first, and he speaks with courtesy. The sacred title lets him separate the messengers from the message: they are not to blame, Agamemnon is. See [[messenger-scene]].

The protection of heralds was a real institution of Greek warfare. Herodotus tells how the Spartans, after killing the Persian king's heralds, suffered the wrath of Agamemnon's herald Talthybius (see [[wrath-of-talthybius]]).
