## Meaning

ἔφαλος = ἐπί (“on, by”) + ἅλς (“sea”): “on the sea.” ἀγχίαλος = ἄγχι (“near”) + ἅλς: “near the sea.” The translation renders both “by the sea.” As adjectives both occur in Homer only in the Catalogue, each of two towns. Ἀγχίαλος is also a man's name, fitting for a sailor or a Phaeacian: the Odyssey knows a Phaeacian so called (Od. 8.112) and Anchialus, father of the Taphian seafarer Mentes (Od. 1.180).

## Use

Cerinthus in Euboea is ἔφαλος ({{ref:Iliad 2.538}}), and Helos in Laconia is “the citadel by the sea” (ἔφαλον πτολίεθρον, {{ref:Iliad 2.584}}). Antron in Thessaly is ἀγχίαλος ({{ref:Iliad 2.697}}), and so is Chalcis in Aetolia:

{{quote:e710f956-b1d0-5c89-b66d-c4ee1ce5feef}}

The two words place a town in one stroke. In the Catalogue the fleet is gathered from coasts and islands, and inland peoples like the Arcadians must be lent ships ({{ref:Iliad 2.612-614}}).
