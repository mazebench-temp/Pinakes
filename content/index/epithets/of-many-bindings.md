## Meaning

πολύδεσμος = πολύς (“many”) + δεσμός (“bond, fastening”): “much bound together.” Merry takes σχεδίη, “raft,” as perhaps properly “made of joined planks,” the sense to which the epithet points, and compares the building of the raft, which Odysseus “hammered … together with pegs and fastenings” (γόμφοισιν … καὶ ἁρμονίῃσιν, *Odyssey* 5.248); a scholium explains it in the same way, as fastened with pegs (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 5.33). Others took σχεδίη as a boat hastily made (Merry on 5.33; see the translation's note on 5.33).

## Use

The phrase is used three times, and only of this raft. Zeus decrees that Odysseus shall go home without escort of gods or men, “on a raft of many bindings, suffering woes” (*Odyssey* 5.32–33); in the storm Ino rises from the sea and sits “on the raft of many bindings” to give him her veil (*Odyssey* 5.337–338); and Odysseus tells Arete that Calypso sent him off on it with many gifts (*Odyssey* 7.264). The bindings are what the storm undoes: Odysseus means to stay on the raft “as long as the beams hold together in their fastenings,” and the next wave scatters them (*Odyssey* 5.361–370).
