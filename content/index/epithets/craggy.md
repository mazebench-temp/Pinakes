## Meaning

κραναός means “rocky, rugged, hard.” In Homer it describes Ithaca, here and in the Odyssey (κραναὴν Ἰθάκην, Od. 1.247). The same word is the name of the island Κραναή, where Paris first lay with Helen after taking her from Sparta (3.445; [[cranae]]). Compare the other words for rough places: [[rocky]] (πετρήεις) and [[rugged]] (τρηχύς).

## Use

{{quote:28db2924-e00a-5a24-a5c9-5c8ad4c29c07}}

Helen names Odysseus for Priam: son of Laertes, [[of-many-wiles]], raised on an island “craggy though it is” (κραναῆς περ ἐούσης). The concessive περ sets the poor, rocky island against the man it raised, who knows “all kinds of tricks and shrewd counsels.” Odysseus says the same of his home in the Odyssey: Ithaca is “rugged, but a good nurse of young men” (τρηχεῖʼ, ἀλλʼ ἀγαθὴ κουροτρόφος, Od. 9.27).

## Outside Homer

Herodotus reports that the Athenians, in the days when the Pelasgians held Greece, were called Κραναοί (8.44).
