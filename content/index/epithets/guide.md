## Meaning

The meaning of διάκτορος was already uncertain in antiquity. It is usually connected with διάγω (“lead across, conduct”), which would make Hermes the “guide” who escorts travelers and, in the Odyssey, the souls of the dead. Others take it as “messenger” or “minister,” a sense that suits the god who carries the orders of Zeus. The translation renders it “guide.”

Homer uses it only of Hermes, eighteen times, almost always in the line-end formula διάκτορος ἀργεϊφόντης. It is frequent in Iliad 24, where Hermes guides Priam through the Achaean camp to Achilles' hut (Il. 24.339–445), and in the Odyssey.

## Use

In Book 2 the epithet comes once, in the list of the scepter's owners: Zeus gave it “to the guide, the slayer of Argus” ({{ref:Iliad 2.103}}). Hermes is the link between the gods and the first mortal owner, Pelops. It is fitting that the go-between of the gods should be the one who hands the emblem of kingship to men ([[argeiphontes]], [[the-scepter-of-agamemnon]]).
