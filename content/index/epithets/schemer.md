## Meaning

δολομήτης = δόλος (“trick, snare”) + μῆτις (“counsel, cunning”): “whose counsel is trickery.” The vocative δολομῆτα has the same archaic -α ending as μητίετα ([[counselor]]). The insult turns Zeus' own epithet against him: his μῆτις is only δόλος. In the Odyssey the related δολόμητις (“of crafty counsel”) is the standing epithet of Aegisthus.

## Use

{{quote:f9e5360f-abdd-5ca5-8c3a-ff45c741cb50}}

Hera opens with a question and an insult before she has any proof, and her suspicion is correct. The quarrel on Olympus reproduces the quarrel in the assembly on a small scale: a charge of bad faith, a claim of authority in reply ({{ref:Iliad 1.545-550}}), and a threat of force ({{ref:Iliad 1.565-567}}).
