## Meaning

ἑκατηβελέτης combines ἑκατη- (as in [[hekatebolos]]) with βέλος (“missile, arrow,” from βάλλω) and the agent suffix -έτης: “he who sends his arrows from afar.” The genitive ἑκατηβελέταο keeps the old genitive ending -αο. The word is rare in Homer.

## Use

{{quote:c4406582-af9b-5177-99db-a05fbae8e490}}

Calchas speaks of μῆνιν Ἀπόλλωνος, “the wrath of Apollo,” using for the god the word that the proem used for Achilles ([[menis]]). The long, weighty epithet and the title ἄνακτος (“lord”; see [[lord-of-men]]) fill the second half of the verse with the god's dignity. The seer's caution is audible: he names the god in full before he dares to name the cause.
