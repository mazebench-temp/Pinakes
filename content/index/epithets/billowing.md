## Meaning

κυμαίνω, “swell with waves,” is formed from κῦμα, “wave”; its participle is used of the open sea, πόντος, always in the accusative κυμαίνοντα at the end of the verse. Homer has it six times. In the Iliad it is the sea that Hera crosses (14.229). In the Odyssey it ends the verse of a sea god's departure, ὑπὸ πόντον ἐδύσετο κυμαίνοντα, “plunged beneath the billowing sea,” used of Eidothea, of Proteus and of Poseidon after he has lain with Tyro (*Odyssey* 4.425, 4.570, 11.253), and with ἐς πόντον of Ino (*Odyssey* 5.352); and the billowing sea carries off the drowned Ajax son of Oileus (*Odyssey* 4.510). Compare the other epithets of the sea: [[shining-sea]], [[gray-sea]], [[marmareos]], [[barren-sea]] and [[wine-dark]].

## Use

{{quote:3dbdd621-4576-5d4a-8275-0b3cf5769186}}

From the snowy mountains of the Thracians Hera comes to Athos, “and from Athos she stepped out onto the billowing sea, / and came to Lemnos, the city of divine Thoas” (14.229–230; [[athos]], [[lemnos]], [[divine]], [[niphoeis]]). The verb ἐβήσετο, “stepped,” is the verb of the whole flight, which began with her “stepping on Pieria” (ἐπιβᾶσα, 14.226): the goddess walks over the waves as over the peaks. On her way to Ida with Sleep the two leave the sea at Lectum and go on over the dry land (14.281–285; [[lectum]], [[sleep]]). See [[sea]].
