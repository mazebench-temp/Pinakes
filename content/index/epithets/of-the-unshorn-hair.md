## Meaning

ἀκερσεκόμης = ἀ- (“not”) + the stem of κείρω (“cut, shear”; aorist ἔκερσα) + κόμη (“hair”): “whose hair is not cut.” In Homer hair is cut for the dead. The Achaeans cover Patroclus' body with the locks they shear, and Achilles cuts the hair he had let grow for the river Spercheius and puts it in his dead friend's hands (23.135–136, 23.141–153). The god's hair is never cut.

Homer has the word once, in the list of the gods who go over to the Trojans: “Phoebus of the unshorn hair, and Artemis of the showering arrows” (20.39; [[phoebus]], [[of-the-showering-arrows]]). His common epithets in Homer are those of the archer: [[far-shooter]], [[far-worker]] and [[god-of-the-silver-bow]]. See [[apollo]].

## Outside Homer

In the Hymn to Apollo the newborn god, once he has tasted ambrosia, breaks his golden bands and strides over the earth, “Phoebus of the unshorn hair, the far-shooter,” and all the goddesses marvel (*Homeric Hymn to Apollo* 127–135). Walter Leaf reports that the word is otherwise found only after Homer, in that hymn, in Pindar and in later poets (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 20.39).
