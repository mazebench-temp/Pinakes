## Meaning

ῥηξήνωρ = ῥήγνυμι (“break”) + ἀνήρ (“man”): “one who breaks men,” that is, the ranks of men. ῥήγνυμι is the verb of breaking a battle line: Ajax “was the first to break a battalion of the Trojans” (6.5–6), and Areithous the mace-man “broke the battalions with an iron mace” (ῥήγνυσκε φάλαγγας, 7.141; [[areithous]]). The abstract noun appears once, when the disguised Odysseus claims that Ares and Athena gave him “daring and the breaking of men” (θάρσος … καὶ ῥηξηνορίην, Od. 14.216–217). The compound is also a name: Rhexenor is the brother of Alcinous in the Odyssey (Od. 7.63, 7.146).

As an epithet it belongs to Achilles alone, five times in Homer, always beside his name (Ἀχιλλῆα ῥηξήνορα, Ἀχιλλῆϊ ῥηξήνορι, Ἀχιλλῆος ῥηξήνορος): Il. 7.228, 13.324, 16.146, 16.575; Od. 4.5. Compare his other titles of war, [[lion-hearted]] and [[sacker-of-cities]].

## Use

Ajax, advancing on Hector behind his great shield, tells him what kind of men the Achaeans have:

The two epithets of the absent hero stand side by side at the end of the verse, a breaker of ranks with a lion's heart. καὶ μετʼ Ἀχιλλῆα can mean “next after Achilles,” in rank, or “even without Achilles,” now that he has withdrawn (see the translation's note on 7.228). The same words recur in Book 16, where Automedon is the man whom Patroclus “honored most after Achilles, breaker of men” (τὸν μετʼ Ἀχιλλῆα ῥηξήνορα τῖε μάλιστα, 16.146), and there they can only mean next in rank. Either way Ajax, whom the Catalogue calls the best of the Achaeans “while Achilles kept up his wrath” (2.768–769; [[best-of-the-achaeans]]), names Achilles as the measure of the Achaeans' champions.

The epithet measures Ajax again in Book 13. Idomeneus says that great Telamonian Ajax would yield to no mortal man, “not even to Achilles, breaker of men, in standing fight,” though no one can match Achilles in speed of foot (13.321–325). See [[ajax]] and [[achilles]].
