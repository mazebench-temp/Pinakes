## Meaning

ἰοδόκος = ἰός (“arrow”) + the root of δέχομαι (“receive, hold”): “receiving arrows.” Homer uses it only of the quiver, φαρέτρη, and always run over to the start of the verse after the phrase τόξον … παλίντονον ἠδὲ φαρέτρην, “the back-bent bow and the quiver” ([[back-bent]]): of Teucer's bow and quiver (15.443–444), and in the Odyssey of the bow and quiver of Odysseus, as they lie in the storeroom and as Penelope carries them to the suitors (*Odyssey* 21.11–12, 21.59–60); there the verse goes on to the many groaning arrows inside it. See [[quiver]] and [[bow]].
