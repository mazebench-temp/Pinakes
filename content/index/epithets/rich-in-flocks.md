## Meaning

πολύαρνι = πολύς (“many”) + ἀρήν, genitive ἀρνός (“lamb”): “with many lambs.” It is a dative whose nominative does not occur, and the word is found only here in Homer. πολύμηλος = πολύς + μῆλον (“sheep, goat,” any small flock animal): “rich in flocks.” Homer uses it of men and of a town. In a world where wealth is counted in animals, both words mean “rich.”

## Use

Thyestes is “rich in flocks” in the history of the scepter. Atreus at his death left it to him, and he in turn left it to Agamemnon:

{{quote:21df9459-3371-5238-840d-d6c99e6c8d57}}

The kings of this line are named by pastoral titles: Atreus is “shepherd of the people” ([[shepherd-of-the-people]]), Thyestes is rich in lambs. The passage says nothing of any quarrel between the brothers. The scepter passes peacefully from one to the other and on to the son.

In the Catalogue the epithet marks a town and a family. Orchomenus in Arcadia, an inland land of upland pastures, is “rich in flocks” ({{ref:Iliad 2.605}}). Podarces is the son of Iphiclus, “rich in flocks, the son of Phylacus” ({{ref:Iliad 2.705}}). The Odyssey knows Iphiclus of Phylace for his cattle, which a seer undertook to drive off as the bride-price for Neleus' daughter Pero (Od. 11.287–297).

## Outside Homer

In tragedy the house of Atreus is cursed by the brothers' feud: Atreus served Thyestes his own children at a feast. Aeschylus' *Agamemnon* makes that crime the root of the killing of Agamemnon. Homer's scepter passage knows nothing of it.
