## Meaning

πρεσβυγενής = πρέσβυς (“old, elder”) + the root of γίγνομαι, γένος (“be born, birth”): “eldest by birth.” It occurs only here. The poem marks seniority with other words of the same family: Mulius had Augeas' “eldest daughter,” Agamede (πρεσβυτάτην, 11.739–740; [[agamede]]), and Menoetius reminds Patroclus that he is the elder (πρεσβύτερος), though Achilles is higher in lineage (11.786–787; [[menoetius]]). Compare [[firstborn]] (πρωτόγονος), of lambs.

## Use

{{quote:4f712f7a-6f1e-5839-b8a0-0eedea9e1059}}

Coon sees his brother Iphidamas killed and stripped (11.246–247), stands off to one side with his spear, unseen, and stabs Agamemnon through the forearm. He is dragging his brother's body away by the foot, calling on all the best, when Agamemnon kills him and cuts off his head over Iphidamas: “There the sons of Antenor, at the hands of the king, the son of Atreus, fulfilled their fate” (11.257–263). The eldest son dies defending his brother's body, and the wound he gives is the one that ends Agamemnon's aristeia (11.264–283; [[preeminent]]). See [[coon]], [[iphidamas]], [[antenor]] and [[son-of-antenor]].
