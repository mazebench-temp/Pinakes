## Meaning

ὠκύροος = ὠκύς (“swift”) + ῥέω (“flow”): “swift-flowing.” It occurs twice: here, of an unnamed river in a simile, and of the Celadon, beside which the Pylians fought the Arcadians in Nestor's youth (Il. 7.133). Compare the other epithets of rivers: [[fair-flowing]], [[eddying]], [[silver-eddying]], [[with-high-banks]].

## Use

{{quote:f68b1bcb-43e1-5e04-ae3c-8f4f9d704f5c}}

Diomedes sees Ares at Hector's side and gives ground, like a traveler who comes on a river in flood and turns back ([[man-at-the-river]]). Early in the book Diomedes himself was the river, a winter torrent that sweeps away the dikes (5.87–92; [[river-in-flood]]). Now the god is the river, a force that no man can cross. Diomedes tells the Achaeans to give ground facing the enemy, and not to fight with gods (5.601–606).
