## Meaning

θυμαλγής = θυμός (“heart, spirit”; [[thymos]]) + ἄλγος (“pain, sorrow”; [[algos]]): “paining the heart.” It describes what hurts from within or wounds from without: anger (χόλος θυμαλγής; [[cholos]]); the outrage Agamemnon did to Achilles, which Achilles will not forgive until it is paid back in full (λώβη, Il. 9.387); and, in the Odyssey, words that wound (ἔπος θυμαλγές, Od. 16.69).

## Use

{{quote:c687b0f3-a0ae-5cfd-8a55-a1bda23e67e2}}

Apollo rouses the Trojans with the news of Achilles' absence: “by the ships he broods over his heart-grieving anger” (4.513). This is the only mention of Achilles in Book 4. His wrath, called μῆνις in Book 1 ([[menis]]), is χόλος here, and the verb πέσσω (“digest, brood over”) makes it something he nurses. The same phrase describes Meleager in Phoenix's story in Book 9, lying beside his wife and brooding over his heart-grieving anger (χόλον θυμαλγέα πέσσων, Il. 9.565), and in the same book Odysseus asks Achilles to let his heart-grieving anger go (Il. 9.260). See [[achilles]].
