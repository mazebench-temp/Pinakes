## Meaning

νωθής occurs only here and is usually explained as “slow, sluggish,” the sense of the related νωθρός in later Greek. It describes the donkey (ὄνος, named only here in Homer) who has had his way against the boys driving him past a field and goes in to crop the grain, though they break their cudgels on him.

## Use

{{quote:3bf5b467-19b1-543f-9290-57f16fafee18}}

This is the second of two similes for Ajax's retreat. The first, a lion driven from the cattle yard by dogs and men, gives his unwillingness to go (11.548–557; [[lion-driven-from-the-farmyard]], [[tawny]]); the donkey gives his slowness, under blows that do him no harm, since the boys' “strength is childish” (11.561; [[nepios]]). So the Trojans and their allies follow Ajax, stabbing at his shield, and he gives ground a step at a time (11.563–574; [[gathered-from-many-lands]], [[seven-oxhide]]).
