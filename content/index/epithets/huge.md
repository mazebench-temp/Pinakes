## Meaning

πελώριος is formed from πέλωρ (“monster, prodigy”), and something of the prodigy stays in it: the size is uncanny. Homer gives it to Ajax, to Ares (Il. 7.208), to Hades (Il. 5.395), and to Achilles as he comes on at Hector (Il. 22.92). With Ajax it forms a formula: Αἴας … πελώριος, ἕρκος Ἀχαιῶν, “huge Ajax, the bulwark of the Achaeans” (Il. 3.229, 7.211; [[bulwark-of-the-achaeans]]).

## Use

Priam uses the word first, of a man he does not know: “so that you may name this huge man for me” (3.166). It is Agamemnon, and Priam at once qualifies the size: others are taller by a head, but he has never seen a man so handsome and so stately; he looks like a king (3.168–170). πελώριος here is the impression of a presence as much as of a height.
