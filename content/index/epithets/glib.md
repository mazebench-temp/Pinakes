## Meaning

ἄρτιος (“fitting, ready”) + ἔπος (“word”): one whose words come ready to hand. In Hector's mouth it is reproach, joined to ἐπίκλοπος, “a cheat,” from the root of κλέπτω, “steal” (see the translation's table of key words). Homer has ἀρτιεπής once; ἐπίκλοπος is said three times in the Odyssey, each time of Odysseus: Alcinous does not take him for “a deceiver and a cheat” (*Odyssey* 11.364), Athena calls cunning and a cheat whoever would surpass him in tricks (*Odyssey* 13.291), and a suitor watching him handle the bow takes him for a connoisseur and a thief of bows (*Odyssey* 21.397).

## Use

{{quote:5b913755-a767-5a88-956d-0add7485bb5e}}

Achilles' spear has missed, and Hector, unhurt, turns his enemy's boast against him. Achilles had said that Pallas Athena would bring him down at once by his spear (22.270–271); Hector answers that he did not know his fate from Zeus after all, but spoke to frighten him out of his courage (22.279–282; [[like-the-immortals]]). The taunt is spoken in ignorance: Athena has already given the spear back to Achilles, unseen by Hector (22.276–277; [[truth-and-deception]]). See [[hector]] and [[achilles]].

## Outside Homer

Hesiod calls the Muses ἀρτιέπειαι, “ready of speech,” as they end their address to the shepherd on Helicon, before they give him the staff of laurel and breathe a divine voice into him (*Theogony* 29–32).
