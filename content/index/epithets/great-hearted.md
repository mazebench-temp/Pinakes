## Meaning

μεγάθυμος = μέγας (“great”) + θυμός (“spirit, heart,” the seat of feeling and impulse): “great-spirited.” It is used of peoples and heroes, Trojans as well as Achaeans, and occasionally of gods: Athena is μεγάθυμος in the Odyssey (Od. 8.520).

## Use

Achilles asks how the army could give Agamemnon a new prize:

Agamemnon takes up his exact words, δώσουσι γέρας μεγάθυμοι Ἀχαιοί:

{{quote:299817f5-ffbb-505d-9c4c-20bedcc43961}}

He does not finish the thought: “if they will give me a prize of equal worth —” and the sentence breaks off ([[aposiopesis]]). The epithet flatters the army whose generosity is in question. In Book 1 the Achaeans as a body are the givers of prizes ([[sons-of-the-achaeans]], [[geras]]), and both men appeal to them.
