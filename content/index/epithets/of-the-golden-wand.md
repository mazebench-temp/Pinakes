## Meaning

χρυσόρραπις = χρυσός (“gold”) + ῥαπίς (“rod, wand”): “with a wand of gold.” The Suda explains it so, “having a golden wand,” and glosses ῥαπίς as ῥάβδος, the word for Hermes' wand (Suda χ 580, ρ 46). The wand is the one “with which he bewitches the eyes of men” (*Iliad* 24.343 = *Odyssey* 5.47; [[wand]]). The Iliad does not say what it is made of; in the Odyssey it is golden, a “fine golden” wand, when Hermes leads the souls of the suitors down to Hades (*Odyssey* 24.2–4).

## Use

The epithet is used three times, all on the islands of the two goddesses who keep Odysseus. Calypso greets Hermes with it when he comes to her cave with the command of Zeus (*Odyssey* 5.87). On Circe's island Odysseus, going up alone to her house, is met by “Hermes of the golden wand” in the likeness of a young man, who gives him the herb that will protect him from her drugs (*Odyssey* 10.275–306), and Circe, when her drug has failed, knows him for the man whom “the slayer of Argus of the golden wand” always said would come (*Odyssey* 10.330–331; [[argeiphontes]]). See [[hermes]] and [[guide]].

## Outside Homer

The Homeric Hymns use the epithet more freely. Zeus sends “the slayer of Argus of the golden wand” down to Erebus to bring Persephone back to the light (*Hymn to Demeter* 334–339); Aphrodite, in the tale she tells Anchises, says that he snatched her from the dance of Artemis (*Hymn to Aphrodite* 117–121); and the hymn to Hestia calls him the messenger of the blessed gods, “of the golden wand, giver of good things” (*Hymn* 29.7–8, 29.13). The *Hymn to Hermes* tells how he got the wand: Apollo, swearing friendship with him, promises him “a very beautiful wand of wealth and riches, / golden, three-leaved,” and calls him “my brother of the golden wand” (*Hymn to Hermes* 524–530, 539).
