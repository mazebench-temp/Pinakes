## Meaning

ἀργικέραυνος = ἀργι-, the form of ἀργός (“bright, white,” and “swift”) that begins compounds, + κεραυνός (“thunderbolt”; [[thunderbolt]]): “whose thunderbolt flashes bright.” The same first element is in ἀργιόδους, “white-tusked,” of boars ([[white-tusked]]), and ἀργίπους, “swift-footed,” of dogs (ἀργίποδας κύνας, “the swift dogs”, 24.211). The Iliad uses the epithet three times, always in the vocative ἀργικέραυνε and always in a god's words to Zeus: Hera brings him the news of Eurystheus' birth, “Father Zeus of the gleaming thunderbolt” (19.121); Poseidon asks him why he has called the gods to assembly once more (20.15–16); and Athena answers his pity for Hector, ὦ πάτερ ἀργικέραυνε κελαινεφές (22.177–178). It joins Zeus' other epithets of the storm, [[delighting-in-thunder]] (τερπικέραυνος), [[lord-of-the-lightning]] (ἀστεροπητής) and [[lord-of-dark-clouds]] (κελαινεφής), which follows it in Athena's address. See [[father-zeus]].
