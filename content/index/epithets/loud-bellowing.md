## Meaning

ἐρι- (“very”) + the root of μυκάομαι (“bellow, low”): “loud-bellowing.” Homer gives it only to cattle. In the simile of the threshing floor the barley is husked “beneath the feet of the loud-bellowing bulls” (20.497; [[broad-browed]]). At the funeral games for Patroclus, Ajax son of Oileus slips in the dung of the loud-bellowing oxen that Achilles had killed for the pyre (23.774–777). In the Odyssey Melampus escapes his doom and drives the loud-bellowing cattle of Iphiclus from Phylace to Pylos (*Odyssey* 15.235–236). The verb is used of other loud sounds too: the gates of heaven creak (μύκον, 5.749 = 8.393), and the shield groans when Aeneas' spear strikes it (μύκε, 20.260). See [[cattle]].

## Outside Homer

In the Hymn to Hermes the infant god cuts fifty loud-bellowing cows out of Apollo's herd (*Homeric Hymn to Hermes* 74, 105: βοῦς ἐριμύκους). The Works and Days names the eighth of the month as the day to geld “a boar and a loud-bellowing bull” (*Works and Days* 790–791).
