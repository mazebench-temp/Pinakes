## Meaning

ἐπιβουκόλος = ἐπί + βουκόλος, “cowherd,” the herdsman set over a herd. Merry reports Lehrs' account of Aristarchus' view, that the compound is of the kind of μετάγγελος, “messenger between,” and means the herdsman who is over certain cattle, not, as Nitzsch had it, the overseer of the herdsmen; and he sets βοῶν ἐπιβουκόλος, “cowherd of the cattle,” among Homer's pleonasms, with αἰπόλος αἰγῶν, “goatherd of the goats,” and οἶνον οἰνοχοεῦντες, the wine-pourers pouring wine (*Odyssey* 17.247, 3.472; Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 3.422).

## Use

Nestor orders a herdsman to drive the heifer in from the plain for the sacrifice to Athena (*Odyssey* 3.421–422; [[the-sacrifice-to-athena]]). The same verse-end is the name-formula of Philoetius, the cowherd who stays loyal to Odysseus: twice it brings in his speeches, after the verse of reply (*Odyssey* 20.235, 21.199), and twice it closes the verse in which he kills a suitor, Peisander and Ctesippus (*Odyssey* 22.268, 22.285); his taunt over Ctesippus is closed with a longer form, ἦ ῥα βοῶν ἑλίκων ἐπιβουκόλος, “so spoke the herdsman of the shambling cattle” (*Odyssey* 22.292).
