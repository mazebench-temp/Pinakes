## Meaning

ταύρειος is the adjective of ταῦρος (“bull”): “of a bull,” of things made from its hide. Homer uses it four times. Three times it describes a shield: the shield “balanced on every side, of bull's hide” that stops Meriones' spear, which breaks at the socket, while Deiphobus holds it out from himself in fear (13.160–163; [[balanced-on-every-side]]), and the shield of bull's hide under which Hector, skilled in war, covers his broad shoulders and watches for the whistle of arrows (16.358–361). Once it describes a helmet, the cap that Thrasymedes puts on Diomedes' head (10.257–258). Shields were made of layers of hide, as the shield of Ajax is “of seven oxhides” ([[seven-oxhide]]); compare [[with-the-oxhide-shield]] (ταλαύρινος).

## Use

{{quote:5fdcd4f3-31a4-5510-af52-c501071559f3}}

The cap is described by what it is made of and by what it lacks: it is of bull's hide, without the ridge and the crest of a battle helmet, and it is called a skullcap, the cap that “guards the heads of young men in their prime” ([[without-ridge-or-crest]], [[flourishing]], [[helmet]]). Odysseus' helmet is leather too, set with a boar's tusks (10.261–265; [[the-boars-tusk-helmet]]). Neither of the spies goes out in bronze on his head. See [[diomedes]] and [[thrasymedes]].
