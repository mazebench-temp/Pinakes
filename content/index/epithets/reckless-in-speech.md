## Meaning

ἀκριτόμυθος = ἄκριτος (“undiscerning, without judgment,” from ἀ- “not” + κρίνω “separate, judge”) + μῦθος (“word, speech”): “whose words are undiscerning,” a man who speaks without sorting what should be said from what should not. The same stem gives two other words in Book 2: Iris tells Priam that “endless words” (μῦθοι … ἄκριτοι) are always dear to him ({{ref:Iliad 2.796}}), and the mountain of the Phthires is ἀκριτόφυλλον, “with countless leaves” ({{ref:Iliad 2.868}}). The root sense is “not sorted out, without limit.”

The word occurs twice in Homer. The other use is Penelope's, of dreams: “dreams are hard to understand and confused in their words” (ὄνειροι ἀμήχανοι ἀκριτόμυθοι, Od. 19.560).

The translation gives the same rendering to ἀπτοεπής, which occurs only at 8.209, in Poseidon's answer to Hera. It is usually explained as “undaunted in speech” (ἀ- “not” + πτοέω “scare” + ἔπος “word”): one who says what others would be afraid to say. Thersites' fault is words without judgment; Hera's is words without fear.

## Use

{{quote:20db32a1-d07d-537d-b4d1-6c0df404e7bd}}

Odysseus begins with a concession, “clear-voiced orator though you are” ([[clear-voiced-orator]]), and turns it into a charge. Thersites speaks well and without judgment. The narrator had already called him [[of-unmeasured-words]] (ἀμετροεπής). Odysseus' threat follows, and then the blow with the scepter ({{ref:Iliad 2.265-268}}).

The only other speaker of indiscriminate words in Homer is a dream, and Book 2 is a book about a lying dream ([[agamemnons-dream]]).
