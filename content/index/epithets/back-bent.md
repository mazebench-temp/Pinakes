## Meaning

παλίντονος = πάλιν (“back”) + the root of τείνω (“stretch”): “stretched back, bent back.” It is generally understood of a bow whose arms curve backward against the pull of the string, a strong composite bow like the one a horn-worker made for Pandarus from the horns of a wild goat (4.105–111; [[the-bow-of-pandarus]]); its exact sense is uncertain.

Homer uses it five times. In the Iliad: Teucer's bow here and in Book 15, when Ajax calls him and he comes running, “holding in his hand the back-bent bow and the quiver” (15.440–444); and the bow that Odysseus and Diomedes strip from Dolon (10.458–459). In the Odyssey the same half-verse describes the bow of Odysseus, lying in the storeroom with its quiver of groaning arrows, and in Penelope's hands as she carries it to the suitors (Od. 21.11–12, 21.59–60; [[groaning]]). See [[bow]].

## Use

{{quote:aabfc98f-6cd2-5550-aa96-3210a7c52486}}

When the eagle has heartened the Achaeans, their leaders follow Diomedes across the ditch one after another, and Teucer comes ninth (8.253–266). He fights from behind Ajax's shield, shooting when Ajax moves it aside and running back to him “like a child under its mother” (8.268–272; [[teucer]], [[the-shield-of-ajax]]). The bow's eight arrows each find a man (8.273–277), and its ninth and tenth, aimed at Hector, kill Gorgythion and Archeptolemus (8.300–315; [[long-barbed]]). Hector breaks the string with a stone (8.324–329). See [[arrows]].
