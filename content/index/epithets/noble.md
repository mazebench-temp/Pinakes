## Meaning

ἀγαυός is usually connected with ἄγαμαι (“admire, wonder at”): “admirable, illustrious, noble.” Homer uses it of kings, heroes and peoples, of Tithonus, from whose bed Dawn rises (Il. 11.1), and in the Odyssey of the queen of the dead, ἀγαυὴ Περσεφόνεια (Od. 11.213).

## Use

{{quote:05b59cc6-1e76-553e-98be-f9a9f6c53617}}

The heralds of both armies bring together the victims and the wine, mix the wine in the bowl, and pour water over the hands of the kings. The epithet honors their office. Heralds are the messengers of Zeus and under his protection ([[heralds]], [[messengers-of-zeus]]), and at an oath sacrifice they act for both sides at once: they will also give out the lambs' hair to the best of the Trojans and Achaeans (3.273–274).
