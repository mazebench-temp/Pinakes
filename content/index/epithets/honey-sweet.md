## Meaning

μελίφρων = μέλι (“honey”) + φρήν (“mind, heart”): “honey to the heart,” gladdening the mind. Homer gives it to wine (οἶνον μελίφρονα, as in Il. 6.264, where Hector refuses the cup his mother offers), to grain (Il. 8.188), and once to sleep. It belongs with the other words for sleep in *Iliad* 2: [[sweet-sleep]] (νήδυμος, γλυκύς) and [[ambrosial]] (ἀμβρόσιος).

μελιηδής = μέλι + ἡδύς (“sweet”), “sweet as honey,” is the commoner word: in the Iliad of wine, of wheat, of fruit and of life (μελιηδέα θυμόν, 10.495, 17.17), and in the Odyssey also of the lotus, of the homecoming and of sleep (*Odyssey* 9.94, 11.100, 19.551), and once of the wax with which Odysseus stops his companions' ears (*Odyssey* 12.48).

## Use

The only sleep that Homer calls μελίφρων ends the Dream's message:

The warning not to forget is needless. Agamemnon wakes with the message fixed in his mind and acts on it at once ({{ref:Iliad 2.35-47}}). What he should fear is not forgetting but believing. The sweetness of sleep is the Dream's last kindness to a man it is deceiving.
