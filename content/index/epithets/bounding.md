## Meaning

ἐΰσκαρθμος = εὖ (“well”) + the root of σκαίρω (“skip, bound”), the verb of the young people who follow the singer, skipping, at the vintage on Achilles' shield (ποσὶ σκαίροντες, 18.571–572) and of calves that frisk around their mothers (Od. 10.410–412): “bounding well, nimble.” Homer has it only here. It names the gait of horses that fly over the waves, as the stride of the gods' horses in Book 5 covers as much haze as a man can see from a lookout over the sea (5.770–772; [[swift-flying]]).

## Use

{{quote:9e5e9bae-3cec-5ac8-9da8-2e9e019ef718}}

Poseidon drives over the waves, the sea beasts frolic beneath him and know their lord, the sea parts for joy, “and the horses flew very swiftly, and the bronze axle was not wetted beneath; and the bounding horses carried him to the ships of the Achaeans” (13.29–31; [[poseidon]], [[chariot-journey]], [[bronze-footed]]). The verse ends the drive, and the horses are unyoked in the cave between Tenedos and Imbros (13.32–38). See [[horses]].
