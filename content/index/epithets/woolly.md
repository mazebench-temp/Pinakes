## Meaning

εἰροπόκος = εἶρος (“wool”) + πόκος (“fleece”): “with woolly fleece.” Homer uses it twice, of sheep: here, and of the rams of the Cyclops, under whose chests Odysseus' men were tied as they escaped (Od. 9.443). Compare [[white]] (ἀργεννός), the standing epithet of sheep, and [[fat-sheep]].

## Use

The epithet is in the first simile of Diomedes' wound. Athena has renewed his might, and it seizes him “three times as great”:

{{quote:9cfee6b1-8244-56d1-b556-ed5a9fd89b57}}

The flock that the shepherd fails to defend is soft and helpless, and the wounded lion is roused by the wound rather than stopped by it ([[wounded-lion]], [[sheep]], [[lion]]). The Trojans are the sheep, heaped together in their panic.
