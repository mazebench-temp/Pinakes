## Meaning

ἀμιτροχίτων = ἀ- (“without”) + μίτρη (“band”; [[band]]) + χιτών (“tunic”; [[tunic]]). The μίτρη is the band of metal that a warrior wore under his corselet, low on the body: Pandarus' arrow passes through Menelaus' belt and corselet and through “the band he wore to guard his flesh, a barrier against javelins” (4.137), which Menelaus then names with the kilt as the bronze-smiths' work (4.187), and Diomedes wounds Ares “into the lowest part of his flank, where he girded on his band” (5.857). What exactly the μίτρη was is disputed, and the poem does not say why the Lycians went without it.
