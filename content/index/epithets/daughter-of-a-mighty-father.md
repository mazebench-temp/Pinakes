## Meaning

ὀβριμοπάτρη = ὄβριμος (“heavy, mighty”; [[obrimos]]) + πατήρ (“father”): “she whose father is mighty.” Homer uses it only of Athena: in the verse that describes her spear, ἡρώων, τοῖσίν τε κοτέσσεται ὀβριμοπάτρη (5.747, with οἷσίν; Il. 8.391; Od. 1.101), and in the genitive with gray-eyed, γλαυκώπιδος ὀβριμοπάτρης (Od. 3.135, 24.540; [[gray-eyed]]). It names her by Zeus, who, as Ares complains in Book 5, begot her himself (5.875, 5.880). Compare [[child-of-aegis-bearing-zeus]] and [[daughter-of-zeus]].

## Use

{{quote:0f96594b-5db4-543c-91f3-6c7b0d8ab483}}

The arming of Athena ends with her spear, and the spear with her father. She has put on “the tunic of Zeus the cloud-gatherer” (5.736) and slung round her shoulders the aegis, on which is the Gorgon's head, “the portent of aegis-bearing Zeus” (5.742; [[aegis]], [[arming-scene]]). The last word of the passage names her as her father's daughter. Her resentment, κότος, is a lasting grudge ([[kotos]]), and in Book 5 its object is Ares. The three verses are used again, with one small change, when she arms in Book 8 (Il. 8.389–391), and the last two when she takes up her spear to go to Ithaca at the beginning of the Odyssey (Od. 1.100–101).

## Outside Homer

Hesiod uses γλαυκώπιδος ὀβριμοπάτρης when Athena adorns Pandora (*Theogony* 587).
