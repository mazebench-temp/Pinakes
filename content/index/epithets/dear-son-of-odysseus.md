## Meaning

Ὀδυσσῆος, “of Odysseus,” + φίλος, “dear, one's own,” + υἱός, “son”: a noun-epithet formula that names Telemachus by his father at the end of the verse. φίλος marks what belongs to a man as much as what he loves, as in [[dear-native-land]] ([[philos]]). The formula holds the place that βοὴν ἀγαθὸς Μενέλαος, “Menelaus, good at the war cry,” holds in the same verse when Menelaus rises at Sparta as Telemachus rose in Ithaca (*Odyssey* 2.2, 4.307; [[good-at-the-war-cry]], [[dressing-scene]]).

## Use

It is used nine times, all in the Odyssey and always of Telemachus. Three are in *Odyssey* 2 (*Odyssey* 2.2, 2.35, 2.415). At Pylos he prays after the goddess (*Odyssey* 3.64), and Nestor will not let “the dear son of this man Odysseus” sleep on the deck of his ship (*Odyssey* 3.352). At Sparta he sees Menelaus come in from his bed (*Odyssey* 15.59), and in Eumaeus' hut the swineherd twice promises the beggar clothes when the dear son of Odysseus comes, and Telemachus sits down on the fleece that the swineherd spreads for him (*Odyssey* 14.515, 15.337, 16.46–48). See [[telemachus]] and [[odysseus]].
