## Meaning

εὐτείχεος = εὖ (“well”) + τεῖχος (“wall”): “well-walled.” The walls of Troy were built for King Laomedon by Poseidon, with Apollo's help (Il. 7.452–453, 21.441–457), and they are the city's defense throughout the war.

## Use

{{quote:60b3310f-0ca2-55a0-893f-9710a9554f31}}

The epithet names the obstacle: the city has held out for nine years. Achilles' offer to repay Agamemnon three and four times over depends on something only Zeus can grant. The sack of Troy is spoken of here, as in Chryses' blessing ({{ref:Iliad 1.18-19}}), as the army's shared hope; the book's quarrel puts that hope at risk.
