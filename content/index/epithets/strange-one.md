## Meaning

δαιμόνιος is formed from δαίμων (“divine power”): “under the influence of a δαίμων,” behaving in a way that cannot be explained. In Homer it appears only in the vocative, δαιμόνιε or δαιμονίη, as a form of address: “my strange one,” “you madman.” It can be affectionate or exasperated, and it is used between people who are close. Hector addresses Andromache so (Il. 6.486), Helen addresses Aphrodite (Il. 3.399), and Zeus addresses Hera so again in Book 4 (Il. 4.31).

## Use

{{quote:b5117193-ee65-55eb-8317-001802fd9e8a}}

The word opens Zeus' last answer to Hera. It grants her insight, “you are always suspecting, and I never escape you,” while it dismisses her. The mixture of intimacy and rebuke suits the marriage with which the book ends ([[golden-throned]]).
