## Meaning

ἀγαπητός is the verbal adjective of ἀγαπάω (“greet with affection, cherish, be content with”): “beloved, cherished.” Homer uses it five times, always of a son. Once it describes Astyanax (6.401). Four times it describes Telemachus, the only son of Odysseus and Penelope: when Eurycleia asks him why he, “being an only and beloved son,” wants to go over the wide earth (μοῦνος ἐὼν ἀγαπητός, Od. 2.363–365), when Penelope laments that her beloved son has gone off in a ship without her knowing (Od. 4.727, 4.817), and when Athena tells Zeus that the suitors are eager to kill him on his way home (Od. 5.18). The word belongs to the son on whom a house depends.

Hector's son is named here for the first time, three ways in three verses: Ἑκτορίδης, “son of Hector,” beloved, and then by the two names his father and the people give him ([[astyanax]], [[of-tender-mind]]). The Iliad names no other child of Hector and Andromache. The beloved son is the one for whom Hector prays at the end of the scene (6.476–481; [[fathers-and-sons]]). In her lament over Hector in Book 24 Andromache foresees that the boy will follow her into slavery, or that some Achaean will seize him by the arm and hurl him from the tower (Il. 24.732–738).
