## Meaning

ἀρηΐφιλος = Ἄρης (“Ares,” here as war itself) + φίλος (“dear”): “dear to Ares,” favored by the god of war. Homer uses it twenty-six times. It is Menelaus' standing epithet in the duel of Book 3 (ἀρηΐφιλος Μενέλαος), and it describes the Achaeans as a whole (ἀρηϊφίλων ὑπ’ Ἀχαιῶν). Compare [[dear-to-zeus]] (Διὶ φίλος), [[warlike]] (ἀρήϊος), and [[offshoot-of-ares]].

## Use

{{quote:59741a90-1c33-5381-b0fe-b6be01b9bd7a}}

The Myrmidons wander through the camp, “longing for their leader, dear to Ares,” and do not fight. The epithet names Achilles by the thing he is refusing to do: the man the war god loves sits by his ships in wrath ([[achilles-withdraws]]). The same scene has his people amusing themselves with discus, javelin and bow, and his horses idle beside the chariots ({{ref:Iliad 2.773-777}}).
