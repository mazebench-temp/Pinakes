## Meaning

λινοθώρηξ = λίνον (“flax, linen”) + θώρηξ (“corselet, breastplate”): “with a corselet of linen.” A body armor of many layers of linen glued or quilted together could be stiff and light. It contrasts with the bronze armor implied by χαλκοχίτωνες ([[bronze-armored]]), the commonest description of the Achaeans. The word occurs only twice in Homer, both times in Book 2.

## Use

{{quote:178e4953-dad3-587e-b806-70070e4cf292}}

The Locrian Ajax is introduced by contrast with his namesake: smaller, “not so great as Telamonian Ajax,” and in a linen corselet, but the best of all with the spear. The epithet suits his people. In Book 13 the Locrians are said to fight without helmets, shields, or spears, with bows and slings instead, shooting from behind the heavy-armed men (Il. 13.712–722). On the Trojan side, Amphius of the linen corselet leads the men of Adrasteia and Apaesus with his brother Adrestus ({{ref:Iliad 2.830}}).

## Outside Homer

Linen corselets were still worn and prized in historical times. Herodotus tells of a splendid linen corselet, woven with figures and worked with gold, which the Egyptian king Amasis sent as a gift to the Spartans (3.47).
