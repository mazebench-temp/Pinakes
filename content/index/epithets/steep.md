## Meaning

αἰπύς (feminine αἰπεῖα, neuter αἰπύ) means “steep, sheer, high.” αἰπεινός is a longer form of the same word with the same sense. Both describe mountains and towns set on heights. αἰπύς has a figurative use as well: αἰπὺς ὄλεθρος is “sheer destruction,” a fall with no way back.

The Catalogue also has a town called Αἰπύ, “the steep,” in Nestor's kingdom ({{ref:Iliad 2.592}}; [[aepy]]). That line is the place, not the adjective.

On the Trojan side are “the steep mountain of Tereia” ({{ref:Iliad 2.829}}) and “the steep peaks of Mycale” ({{ref:Iliad 2.869}}). The most striking use is at Troy itself. Before the city stands a steep mound in the plain, which men call Batieia and the gods the tomb of Myrine ({{ref:Iliad 2.811-814}}; [[language-of-gods-and-men]]). There the Trojans and their allies are marshaled for the first battle.
