## Meaning

πύξ (“with the fist,” an adverb, from the family of πυγμή, “fist,” and πυγμαχίη, “boxing”) + ἀγαθός (“good”): “good with the fist,” a boxer. The construction is like βοὴν ἀγαθός, “good at the war cry” ([[good-at-the-war-cry]]). The two brothers are told apart by their skills: Castor is the horseman, “tamer of horses” ([[tamer-of-horses]]), and Polydeuces the boxer. Boxing is one of the contests at the funeral games of Patroclus, won by Epeius (Il. 23.653–699).

## Use

{{quote:70e03702-ee22-558f-a91f-bbf93bc38989}}

Helen looks for her brothers among the Achaeans and cannot find them. She supposes that they stayed at home, or came and will not fight for shame of her (3.239–242). The narrator answers that they are already dead and buried in Lacedaemon ([[life-giving-earth]], 3.243–244). The whole line naming the brothers recurs in the Odyssey, when Odysseus sees their mother Leda among the dead (Od. 11.300). See [[the-death-of-the-dioscuri]].

## Outside Homer

Later poets made Polydeuces' boxing the center of a story. In Apollonius' *Argonautica* he fights and kills Amycus, king of the Bebrycians (2.1–97), and Theocritus tells the same fight in his hymn to the Dioscuri (*Idyll* 22). The Romans called him Pollux.
