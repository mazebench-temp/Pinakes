## Meaning

πτερόεις is formed from πτερόν (“feather, wing”) with -όεις (“having”): “feathered, winged.” It describes arrows, which carry feathers at the notch (4.117; Il. 5.171, 16.773), and the light hide shields that flutter as men run (λαισήϊα πτερόεντα, Il. 5.453, 12.426). It is also the adjective of the formula ἔπεα πτερόεντα, which Book 4 uses seven times. Whether the words of that formula are winged like birds or feathered like arrows is discussed under [[winged-words]]; the translation keeps “winged” for words and “feathered” for arrows.

## Use

Pandarus takes from his quiver an arrow “feathered, never shot before, a source of black pains” (4.117; [[black-pains]], [[bitter]], [[arrows]]). In Book 5 Aeneas asks him where his bow and his feathered arrows are now, when the Achaeans are being driven by Diomedes (Il. 5.171; [[pandarus]]).
