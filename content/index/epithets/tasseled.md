## Meaning

θυσσανόεις is formed from θύσανος (“tassel, fringe”) with the suffix -όεις (“having”): “tasseled.” Homer uses it only of the aegis, always in αἰγίδα θυσσανόεσσαν at the end of the verse (5.738; Il. 15.229, 17.593, 18.204, 21.400). In Book 2, from Athena's aegis “a hundred tassels hang floating, all of gold, all finely woven, and each worth a hundred oxen” (2.448–449). Compare [[aegis-bearing]].

## Use

{{quote:2f8333cd-89e4-5542-83cf-db1ae04bef8c}}

As Athena arms, the epithet comes first of all the things said of the aegis. The fringe of tassels is its edge; within are the powers of battle, Rout as a crown around it and Strife, Courage and Onslaught upon it, and the Gorgon's head ([[rout]], [[strife]], [[courage]], [[onslaught]], [[gorgon]]). In the battle of the gods Ares stabs his spear against the tasseled aegis, which not even the thunderbolt of Zeus can overcome (Il. 21.400–401).
