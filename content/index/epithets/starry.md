## Meaning

ἀστερόεις is formed from ἀστήρ (“star”) with the suffix -όεις (“full of”): “starry.” It is the standing epithet of the sky, οὐρανὸς ἀστερόεις, in all its cases and usually at the end of the verse (for example Il. 5.769, 15.371). It also describes things that shine like the night sky: the “imperishable, starry” house of Hephaestus (Il. 18.370), and the corselet of Achilles that Patroclus puts on (Il. 16.134). Compare [[wide-sky]] (οὐρανὸς εὐρύς).

## Use

{{quote:0400527c-ca17-5edc-9e1a-5a8092362062}}

Zeus measures his love for Troy against the whole inhabited world: of all the cities “under the sun and the starry sky,” sacred Ilios was the most honored in his heart ([[helios]], [[hieros]], [[troy]]). The epithet gives the scale of what he is giving up. A few lines later the starry sky sends a star: Athena comes down to the battlefield “as the son of crooked-counseling Cronus sends a star” (4.75; [[shooting-star]]).
