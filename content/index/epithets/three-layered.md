## Meaning

τρίπτυχος = τρι- (“three”) + πτύξ (“fold, layer”): “of three layers.” It occurs only here. Shields too are built in layers, πτύχες: Ajax's shield has seven layers of oxhide under an eighth of bronze, and Hector's spear goes through six of them (7.245–248; [[seven-oxhide]]); the shield that Hephaestus made for Achilles has five, two of bronze, two of tin inside and one of gold, and Aeneas' spear goes through two (20.268–272). Here the layers of a helmet keep out the spear. The helmet is a τρυφάλεια, and its other epithet, αὐλῶπις, is usually understood as “with a socket for the plume” ([[with-a-socket-for-the-plume]]). See [[helmet]].

## Use

{{quote:e8e09018-28ad-5539-9411-25c9c70ce01b}}

Diomedes' spear hits Hector on the top of the helmet, and “bronze glanced off bronze” (11.351). Hector runs back into the throng and sinks to his knees, and black night covers his eyes; while Diomedes goes after his spear, he comes to himself and drives off in his chariot (11.354–360). Diomedes' taunt names the god behind the helmet: “now again Phoebus Apollo has saved you” (11.363; [[phoebus]], [[apollo]]). See [[hector]] and [[diomedes]].
