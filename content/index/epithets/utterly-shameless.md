## Meaning

ἀναιδής = ἀν- (“not”) + αἰδώς (“shame, respect for others”): “shameless, without regard for anyone.” μέγα, the neuter of μέγας used as an adverb, intensifies it: “greatly shameless.”

## Use

{{quote:5dc1d698-15c2-58e7-9760-af583b5366f8}}

The address comes where Achilles reminds Agamemnon why the army came to Troy at all: to please him, and to win satisfaction for Menelaus and for him. Shamelessness here is ingratitude. Agamemnon accepts the help and returns no honor. A line later comes κυνῶπα, “dog-face” ([[dog-face]]), naming the animal that stood for shamelessness. The same fault opened the speech ([[clothed-in-shamelessness]], {{ref:Iliad 1.149}}).
