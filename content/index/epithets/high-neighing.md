## Meaning

ὑψηχής is usually explained as ὕψι (“on high”) + ἠχή (“sound, noise”): “neighing loudly,” or “with a high-pitched neigh.” It occurs twice. Besides Book 5, the Myrmidons unyoke their high-neighing horses after driving three times round the body of Patroclus (Il. 23.13–27). Compare [[single-hoofed]], [[fine-maned]] and [[with-golden-headbands]].

## Use

{{quote:837f0dd8-6ebf-59c0-8d6d-9d52d06dd509}}

The simile measures the horses of Hera by a man's horizon: at each bound they cover as much air as a man can see from a lookout over the sea ([[the-lookout]], [[wine-dark]]). The epithet adds their sound to their speed.

## Outside Homer

The treatise *On the Sublime* quotes these lines and remarks that the poet measures the horses' leap by the dimensions of the world: if they took two such bounds in a row, there would be no room left for them in the world ([Longinus], *On the Sublime* 9.5; [[longinus]], [[longinus-horses-of-the-gods]]).
