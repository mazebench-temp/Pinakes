## Meaning

ἀψόρροος = ἄψ (“back”) + ῥέω (“flow”): “flowing back.” Oceanus is the river that runs around the whole earth, set by Hephaestus around the outermost rim of the shield of Achilles (18.607–608). Walter Leaf reports that the ancients explained the epithet as flowing back into himself, since he encircles the earth in a circle, and that others have seen in it a vague rumor of the tide (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 18.399). The word stands twice in Homer, both times in the verse-end ἀψορρόου Ὠκεανοῖο: Hephaestus calls Eurynome “daughter of backward-flowing Oceanus” (18.399), and Penelope prays that a storm may snatch her away and cast her down at the outflow of backward-flowing Oceanus (*Odyssey* 20.63–65). ἄψορρος is another word, used of men who “went back” (3.313).

## Outside Homer

Hesiod uses the same verse-end of Styx, “daughter of backward-flowing Oceanus,” the eldest of his daughters (*Theogony* 776–777).
