## Meaning

χαλκοβατής = χαλκός (“bronze”) + βαίνω (“step, walk”): “with a floor of bronze to tread on.” δῶ is a short form of δῶμα (“house”). The gods' houses shine with metal, and in Book 1 each god's house is the work of Hephaestus ({{ref:Iliad 1.606-608}}). The same phrase describes the house of Zeus elsewhere in the Iliad, as when Artemis flees there in tears (Il. 21.505).

## Use

{{quote:d607afd8-ceaf-52b4-ac99-969518e3a3e2}}

Thetis names the house as the goal of her appeal. When she goes up, she finds Zeus not in his house but alone on the highest peak ({{ref:Iliad 1.498-499}}). The house is where the gods gather at the end of the book, rising to meet their father ({{ref:Iliad 1.533-535}}) and falling into dismay at his threats ({{ref:Iliad 1.570}}).
