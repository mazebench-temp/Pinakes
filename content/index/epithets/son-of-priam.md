## Meaning

Πριαμίδης = Πρίαμος + the patronymic suffix -ίδης: “son of Priam.” Priam had fifty sons, nineteen of them by one mother (Il. 24.495–497; [[sons-of-priam]]), and the patronymic can name any of them. Books 2–4 give it to three: Hector, as he leads the Trojans in the Trojan Catalogue ({{ref:Iliad 2.817}}); Paris, when Menelaus' spear strikes his shield ({{ref:Iliad 3.356}}); and Antiphus (4.490). Compare [[son-of-atreus]], which also names more than one brother.

## Use

Two sons of Priam fight in the first battle of the poem. Antiphus, “of the flashing corselet, son of Priam,” throws at Ajax and kills Leucus instead (4.489–491; [[of-the-flashing-corselet]]). Odysseus in his anger then kills “a bastard son of Priam, Democoon” (υἱὸν Πριάμοιο νόθον, 4.499; [[democoon]]). At the beginning of the book Zeus had mocked Hera's hatred of the royal house: she would eat “Priam raw, and the sons of Priam” if she could (4.35).
