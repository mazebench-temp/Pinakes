## Meaning

Beside the noun ἑανός, a woman's or a goddess's fine robe ([[robe]]), Homer has an adjective ἑανός, rendered “soft”: Athena “let her soft robe fall on her father's floor” (πέπλον … ἑανόν, 5.734 = 8.385); Patroclus' body is covered “with a soft linen cloth” (ἑανῷ λιτί, 18.352, and his bones again at 23.254); and Hephaestus makes Achilles “greaves of soft tin” (18.613). Walter Leaf renders it “pliant,” and notes the difficulty of greaves of so soft a metal, which Reichel removed by taking them for guards against the edge of the shield rather than against missiles (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 18.613). Whether the adjective and the noun are one word is disputed. The noun is excepted at 14.178, where Hera puts on her ambrosial robe.
