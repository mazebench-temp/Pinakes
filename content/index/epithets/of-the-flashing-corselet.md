## Meaning

αἰολοθώρηξ = αἰόλος (“quick-moving, glittering”) + θώρηξ (“corselet”; [[corselet]]): “with a corselet that flashes.” Homer gives it to two warriors only: Antiphus, son of Priam (4.489), and Menesthius, a leader of the Myrmidons (Il. 16.173). The same αἰόλος is in Hector's [[of-the-gleaming-helmet]] (κορυθαίολος), in the gleaming belt of Menelaus ([[panaiolos]]), and in the Phrygians' [[with-quick-horses]] (αἰολόπωλος). Compare [[with-linen-corselet]] (λινοθώρηξ) and [[armored-in-bronze]] (χαλκεοθώρηξ).

## Use

{{quote:652651c3-1822-58f1-b46c-5e01536a10c6}}

Antiphus, a son of Priam, enters the poem with this epithet and his patronymic ([[son-of-priam]]). He throws at Ajax, misses, and kills Leucus, the companion of Odysseus, and Odysseus' anger then takes the life of another son of Priam, Democoon (4.494–504; [[leucus]], [[democoon]]). In Book 11 Agamemnon kills Antiphus and his half-brother Isus, whom Achilles had once captured on Ida and let go for ransom (Il. 11.101–121).
