## Meaning

φαεσίμβροτος = φάος (“light”) + βροτοί (“mortals”): “who brings light to mortals.” Walter Leaf counts it among the Odyssean words of the last book (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 24.784).

## Use

In the Iliad it is said once, of the tenth dawn of Hector's funeral: “but when the tenth dawn appeared, bringing light to mortals, / then they carried out bold Hector, shedding tears” (24.785–786; [[dawn-line]], [[the-funeral-of-hector]]). The Odyssey gives it twice to the sun: Circe and her brother are children of the sun who brings light to mortals, and on Circe's island Odysseus does not know where the sun that brings light to mortals goes beneath the earth (*Odyssey* 10.138, 10.190–191). See [[eos]] and [[helios]].
