## Meaning

εὐερκής = εὖ (“well”) + ἕρκος (“fence, enclosing wall”): “well enclosed.” The αὐλή, the courtyard in front of a house, was surrounded by a wall, the ἕρκος (ἑρκίον αὐλῆς, 9.476), with a colonnade (αἴθουσα) along it. Homer uses the word four times: here, in Phoenix's account of his father's house (9.472), and three times in the Odyssey, of the court of Odysseus' house, whose gates are barred as Odysseus takes up the bow and where the bodies of the suitors are laid out (Od. 21.389, 22.449), and of its double doors, so well fenced, Odysseus says, that no man could force them (Od. 17.266–268). Hesiod uses it in his precepts of decent conduct, of the wall of a court (*Works and Days* 730–732). See [[house]].

## Use

{{quote:148d2d4a-fd79-598f-8982-05e2f18d49c7}}

For nine nights Phoenix's kinsmen keep watch on him in turns, with one fire burning “under the colonnade of the well-fenced court” and another in the porch before his chamber. On the tenth night he breaks the doors, leaps over the fence of the court, and escapes unseen by watchmen and slave women ([[closely-fitted]], [[murky]], [[the-flight-of-phoenix]]). The epithet of a strong and well-kept house describes the house that cannot hold him. See [[phoenix]].
