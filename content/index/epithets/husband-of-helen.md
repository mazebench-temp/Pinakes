## Meaning

πόσις (“husband”) + Ἑλένης ἠϋκόμοιο (“of Helen of the lovely hair”; [[lovely-haired]]). The formula names Paris by his relation to the woman over whom the war is fought. It follows his name in the second half of the verse: (δῖος) Ἀλέξανδρος, Ἑλένης πόσις ἠϋκόμοιο. The Iliad uses it when Paris refuses to give Helen back (Il. 7.355) and when he shoots down Achaean heroes with his bow (Il. 11.369, 11.505).

## Use

{{quote:8b86fe54-564c-59c4-8269-af605b8336b8}}

The formula stands at the head of the arming, as Paris prepares to fight for Helen and her possessions (3.70–72). By the terms of the oath the winner will have her (3.281–285); in the narrator's words Paris is already her husband. Menelaus is her “former husband” (πρότερος πόσις, 3.163, 3.429). See [[paris]], [[arming-scene]].
