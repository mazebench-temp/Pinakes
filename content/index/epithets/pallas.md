## Meaning

Two explanations are common. One connects Παλλάς with πάλλω (“brandish, shake”), making Athena the goddess who brandishes the spear. The other connects it with πάλλαξ (“youth”) and παλλακή (“young woman,” later “concubine”), making it mean “maiden,” like her Attic title Parthenos. Neither can be proved. In Homer Παλλάς is a name, fixed in the formulas Παλλὰς Ἀθήνη and Παλλὰς Ἀθηναίη.

At 200 Achilles recognizes the goddess, and her name comes first in the verse, as if in his moment of recognition. At 400 Pallas Athena is one of the three gods who once plotted to bind Zeus, with Hera and Poseidon ([[binding-of-zeus]]). The goddess who restrains Achilles on Hera's orders was, in Achilles' story, a rebel against Zeus.

## Outside Homer

Later mythographers told stories to explain the name: that Athena killed a giant called Pallas and wore his skin, or that as a girl she killed by accident a playmate named Pallas, daughter of Triton (Apollodorus, *Library* 1.6.2 and 3.12.3).
