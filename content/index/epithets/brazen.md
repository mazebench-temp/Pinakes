## Meaning

πολύχαλκος = πολύς (“much”) + χαλκός (“bronze”; [[bronze]]): “with much bronze.” Of men and cities it means wealth: Dolon, son of a herald, is “rich in gold, rich in bronze” (Il. 10.314–315), and so, men used to say, was Priam's city (Il. 18.288–289); in the Odyssey Sidon is “rich in bronze” (Od. 15.425). Of the sky it means “of bronze”: “the brazen sky” (5.504), into which the Sun rises in the morning of Odyssey 3 (Od. 3.1–2). Elsewhere the sky is “bronze” (χάλκεον οὐρανόν, Il. 17.425) and in the Odyssey even “iron” (σιδήρεον οὐρανόν, Od. 15.329, 17.565): a solid, shining vault. Compare [[wide-sky]] and [[starry]]; see [[sky]].

## Use

In the simile the Achaeans turn white with dust as the heaps of chaff turn white on the threshing floor ([[winnowing]]). The dust rises from the horses' hooves into the brazen sky: the plain of battle below, the metal vault above.
