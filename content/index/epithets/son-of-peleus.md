## Meaning

All three words mean “son of Peleus,” formed from Πηλεύς with a patronymic suffix:

- Πηλεΐδης, with the ordinary suffix -ίδης (as in [[son-of-atreus]], Ἀτρεΐδης);
- Πηληϊάδης, with the longer suffix -ιάδης;
- Πηλεΐων, with -ίων (as in [[son-of-cronus]], Κρονίων).

Their different lengths let the poet name Achilles by his father at different points in the verse. In Πηληϊάδεω (1.1) the genitive ending -εω is pronounced as a single syllable. Elsewhere in the Iliad Achilles is also called Αἰακίδης, “grandson of Aeacus,” but not in Book 1. At 1.489 the plain phrase Πηλῆος υἱός, “son of Peleus,” joins his other titles.

## Use

The Iliad names Achilles first by his father:

{{quote:5d895128-2bd2-5af5-9447-d17d47447b4e}}

Forms in Book 1: Πηληϊάδεω (1, 322), Πηλεΐδη (146; spelled Πηλείδη at 277), Πηλεΐδης (223, 245, 306), Πηλεΐωνι (188), Πηλεΐωνα (197), and Πηλῆος υἱός (489).

The patronymic gathers at the crisis of the quarrel. The narrator uses it when grief comes on Achilles and he reaches for his sword, and when Athena seizes him by the hair:

{{quote:63e816fe-216a-579c-b8cf-387822bce1fa}}

It names him again as he renews his abuse (223), as he flings down the scepter (245), and as he leaves the assembly (306). Agamemnon uses it in address when he names Achilles as a possible commander (146), and so does Nestor when he warns him not to strive with a king (277).

One echo stands out. Agamemnon sends his heralds “to the hut of Achilles, son of Peleus,” in the words that ended the first line of the poem, Πηληϊάδεω Ἀχιλῆος ({{ref:Iliad 1.322}}). The name in which the Muse is asked to sing the wrath is the name at which the wrong is done.
