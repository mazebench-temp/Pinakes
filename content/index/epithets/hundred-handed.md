## Meaning

ἑκατόγχειρος = ἑκατόν (“hundred”) + χείρ (“hand”): “with a hundred hands.” The word occurs only here in Homer.

## Use

{{quote:59de0407-4247-5f8c-a189-74cc17fa6a86}}

Achilles tells the story to remind Thetis of the favor Zeus owes her ([[binding-of-zeus]]). When Hera, Poseidon, and Athena wished to bind Zeus, Thetis summoned the hundred-handed one to Olympus, and the gods were afraid of him. His hundred hands are the whole of his description, apart from his two names, one used by gods and one by men ([[language-of-gods-and-men]]). The epithet explains the gods' fear: no one can bind a god who has a hundred-handed guard beside him.

## Outside Homer

Hesiod's *Theogony* makes the Hundred-Handers three brothers, Cottus, Briareus, and Gyges, children of Earth and Sky, each with a hundred arms and fifty heads (147–153). They fight on Zeus' side against the Titans and afterward guard them in Tartarus (617–735).
