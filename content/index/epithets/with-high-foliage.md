## Meaning

ὑψιπέτηλος = ὕψι (“on high”) + πέταλον (“leaf”): “with leaves high up,” a tall tree. The first part is the one in [[high-flying]] (ὑψιπέτης) and [[thunders-on-high]] (ὑψιβρεμέτης). Homer uses it three times, always of a tree, δένδρεον: here; of the tall tree into which Proteus turns in Menelaus' grip, after a lion, a serpent, a leopard, a great boar and running water (Od. 4.456–458); and of the trees whose fruit hangs over Tantalus' head, which the wind tosses away whenever he reaches for it (Od. 11.588–592).

## Use

{{quote:74dcae00-14bf-58e4-ae02-4fd12b8175f0}}

Poseidon bewitches Alcathous' eyes and fetters his limbs, “for he could neither escape backward nor swerve aside, but like a pillar or a tree with high foliage, as he stood motionless, he stabbed him in the middle of the chest with his spear, the hero Idomeneus” (13.436–439; [[alcathous]], [[like-a-pillar]], [[idomeneus]]). Both likenesses are of things that cannot move, a pillar set up by men and a tree rooted in the ground, and the tall tree is a man who stands upright and cannot run. See [[poseidon]].
