## Meaning

κεκορυθμένος, perfect participle of κορύσσω (“arm, equip with a helmet,” from κόρυς, “helmet”), + χαλκῷ (“with bronze”): “helmeted with bronze.” Of men it means armed in bronze. Of spears it makes the bronze point the spear's helmet. The formula δοῦρε δύω κεκορυθμένα χαλκῷ, “two spears tipped with bronze,” is used again in the arming of Agamemnon (Il. 11.43).

## Use

Paris challenges the best of the Argives “brandishing two spears tipped with bronze” (3.18), with his bow and sword ([[curved-bow]]). A pair of spears is the gear of a man who throws them and keeps his distance. When he arms for the formal duel he takes a single spear (3.338; [[strong-spear]]). See [[spear]].
