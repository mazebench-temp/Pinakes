## Meaning

φιλοψευδής = φίλος (“dear, fond of”) + ψεῦδος (“lie, falsehood”; [[pseudos]]): “fond of lies.” Compounds in φιλο- name someone by what he loves, as Aphrodite is φιλομμειδής ([[laughter-loving]]). This one occurs only here in Homer, as a reproach. Asius means that Zeus has given the Trojans false hope: the day's success made them sure the Achaeans would no longer hold out (12.125–126; [[black-ships]]). Agamemnon made the same complaint in his own name when he said that Zeus “has devised an evil deceit” (κακὴν ἀπάτην, 2.114 = 9.21; [[apate]]).

## Use

{{quote:c73de222-c52e-5558-b6e6-df3c72f06b95}}

Asius has driven his chariot through the open gate on the left, against Polydamas' advice, and found the two Lapiths standing in front of it like oaks (12.110–136; [[asius]], [[polypoetes]], [[leonteus]]). As the stones fall like snow he groans, strikes his thighs, and blames the god in the address of prayer ([[father-zeus]], [[irresistible-hands]], [[wasps-at-the-roadside]]). The narrator answers him at once: “So he spoke, but he did not persuade the mind of Zeus by saying this; for his heart wished to hold out glory to Hector” (12.173–174). The god's favor is for Hector, not for every Trojan, and Asius' own death has already been foretold (12.113–117; [[of-evil-name]]). See [[zeus]].
