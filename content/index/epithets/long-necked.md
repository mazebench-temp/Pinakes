## Meaning

δουλιχόδειρος = δολιχός (“long”) + δειρή (“neck”): “long-necked.” The first vowel is lengthened (δουλιχ- for δολιχ-) to fit the verse. The word occurs only in one line, χηνῶν ἢ γεράνων ἢ κύκνων δουλιχοδείρων (“geese or cranes or long-necked swans”), used twice (Il. 2.460, 15.692).

## Use

{{quote:d65fb152-00f9-5b63-bd8c-3537efdc1c0b}}

The army pouring onto the Scamandrian plain is compared to flocks of waterbirds on the meadow of the Cayster ([[birds-on-the-asian-meadow]]). Each kind of bird has its own touch; the swans have their long necks. The detail makes the flocks visible, as the army's many peoples will be made visible, one by one, in the Catalogue.
