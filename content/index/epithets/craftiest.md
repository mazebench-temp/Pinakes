## Meaning

κέρδιστος is the superlative that belongs with κέρδος (“gain, profit,” and in the plural “cunning plans, wiles”); the comparative κερδίων means “more profitable, better,” as when Andromache says that it would be “better” for her to die if she loses Hector (κέρδιον, 6.410). In the Odyssey κέρδεα are the wiles that Odysseus and Athena share: he is the best of mortals in counsel and words, and she is famous among the gods for her cunning (μήτι τε … καὶ κέρδεσιν, Od. 13.296–299). The superlative occurs only here in Homer.

## Use

{{quote:08be46f7-65bf-59a7-b947-53f78d61e986}}

Glaucus' genealogy begins with a man whose one quality is craft, and his name is repeated at the start of the next verse, now with his father's. Homer tells nothing more of his cunning. In the Odyssey Odysseus sees him in the underworld, suffering harsh pains as he pushes a huge stone up a hill, only for it to roll back down, and the poem does not say why (Od. 11.593–600; [[sisyphus]]). His grandson Bellerophon wins by other means: the gods' escort and their portents (6.171, 6.183), against a king who plots against him and “wove another cunning trap” (6.187; [[bellerophon]]).
