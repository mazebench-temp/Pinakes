## Meaning

ἀπηνής means “harsh, ungentle, unbending.” It is the opposite of προσηνής (“kindly, gentle”); the second element of both words is obscure. The Iliad uses ἀπηνής also of the temper of Zeus (Il. 15.94).

## Use

{{quote:f2aac3fc-9074-525e-9e02-7cf13d11f2de}}

Achilles calls the heralds to witness before the blessed gods, before mortal men, and before “that harsh king.” The epithet is his own judgment, not a formula, and it is quieter than the abuse of the assembly. The sentence it stands in never reaches its main clause: Achilles does not say what will happen if the Achaeans need him again ([[aposiopesis]]).
