## Meaning

πολυφόρβη (also πουλυφόρβος) = πολύς (“much”) + φορβή (“fodder, food”): “feeding many.” Homer uses it three times, always of the earth: here (9.568) and twice in Hera's deception of Zeus, when she says she is going “to see the ends of the all-nourishing earth,” to Oceanus and Tethys (14.200, 14.301). The Homeric Hymn to Apollo speaks of men “who eat the fruit of the all-nourishing earth” (*Hymn* 3.365), and Hesiod gives the word to Demeter (*Theogony* 912). Compare the other epithets of the earth that feeds: [[bountiful-earth]] (πουλυβότειρα) and [[life-giving-earth]] (φυσίζοος), and [[earth]].

## Use

{{quote:43f8c035-4e67-57b3-b084-9bbef95742a5}}

Althaea, grieving for her brother whom her son has killed, kneels and beats the all-nourishing earth with her hands, calling on Hades and Persephone to give death to Meleager ([[althaea]], [[dread]], [[meleager]]). The powers below the earth were called on through the earth itself: Hera lays hold of the earth with one hand when she swears by Styx, so that the gods below may be witnesses (14.271–279), and in the Homeric Hymn to Apollo she strikes the ground with the flat of her hand as she prays to Earth, Heaven and the Titans beneath the earth (*Hymn* 3.332–340). The earth that feeds all is struck to bring death to her child, and the Erinys hears her out of Erebus ([[who-walks-in-darkness]], [[erebus]]).
