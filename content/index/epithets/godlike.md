## Meaning

θεοείκελος = θεός (“god”) + εἴκελος (“like,” from the root of ἔοικα, “resemble”): “like a god.” Book 1 expresses the same comparison with two other words: [[antitheos]] (ἀντίθεος, “a match for the gods”) and [[like-the-immortals]] (ἐπιείκελος ἀθανάτοισιν).

## Use

It occurs once, at the start of Agamemnon's reply to Achilles' offer of threefold and fourfold repayment:

{{quote:2258dca5-d914-5ae6-85b9-162dfa400052}}

The address is courteous in form, “brave as you are, godlike Achilles,” and hostile in content: Agamemnon accuses Achilles of trying to trick him (κλέπτε νόῳ, literally “do not steal with your mind”). The concessive ἀγαθός περ ἐών (“good though you are”) grants the hero's quality in order to deny his claim. Nestor uses the same concession to Agamemnon ({{ref:Iliad 1.275}}).

The whole line returns in Book 19, where Odysseus uses it to correct Achilles more gently, urging him to let the army eat before battle (Il. 19.155).
