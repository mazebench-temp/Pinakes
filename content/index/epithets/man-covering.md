## Meaning

ἀμφιβρότη = ἀμφί (“around, on both sides”) + βροτός (“man, mortal”): “surrounding the man.” It describes a shield large enough to cover a warrior from neck to ankles. Such shields, tall body shields of oxhide shaped like a figure eight or like a tower, are known from Mycenaean art, and the Iliad remembers them in Ajax's shield “like a tower” (Il. 7.219). The word occurs four times in Homer, always of the shield (ἀσπίς), in the feminine: here, and in Books 11, 12 and 20.

## Use

{{quote:c7b73d87-8a9e-50d6-a223-f7b0d99d5f5b}}

Agamemnon's order to prepare for battle is a picture of the day's labor. The strap that carries the huge shield from the shoulder will be soaked with sweat. The epithet is not ornament here: a shield that covers the whole man is heavy, and the weight is the point of the sentence.
