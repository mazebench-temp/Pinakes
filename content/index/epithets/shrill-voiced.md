## Meaning

λιγύφωνος = λιγύς (“clear, shrill, piercing”) + φωνή (“voice”): “with a shrill, clear voice.” λιγύς is the adjective of the clear-voiced orator and of the whistling wind ([[clear-voiced-orator]], [[shrill]]); the heralds are λιγύφθογγοι, “clear-voiced,” with the other word for sound ([[clear-voiced]]). Homer has λιγύφωνος only once, of the ἅρπη, the bird of prey that Athena resembles: ἅρπῃ ἐϊκυῖα τανυπτέρυγι λιγυφώνῳ, “like a long-winged, shrill-voiced bird of prey” (19.350; [[harpe]], [[long-winged]]). See [[birds]].

{{notes}}

## Outside Homer

Hesiod gives the epithet to the Hesperides, who live at the ends of the earth toward Night (*Theogony* 275, 518), and in the *Homeric Hymn to Hermes* the lyre that Hermes gives to Apollo is his “clear-voiced companion” (λιγύφωνον ἑταίρην, *Homeric Hymn to Hermes* 478).
