## Meaning

ἐϋπλόκαμος = εὖ (“well”) + πλόκαμος (“lock, braid,” from πλέκω, “plait”): “with fine braids.” It is close in sense to [[lovely-haired]] (ἠΰκομος), but names the plaiting of the hair. In the Iliad it describes the Trojan women (6.380, 6.385), Hecamede, the captive woman who serves Nestor (Il. 11.624, 14.6), the Nereid Amatheia (Il. 18.48), and the serving women of Andromache's house (Il. 22.442, 22.449). In the Odyssey it belongs above all to goddesses: Calypso and Circe, Dawn, Demeter and Artemis. There it is often joined with “dread goddess” in one formula, ἐϋπλόκαμος, δεινὴ θεός (Od. 7.41, of Athena; 7.246, 10.136, 12.150), the two epithets that share a single verse in Book 6 ([[dread-goddess]]).

## Use

Hector finds his house empty and asks the slave women where Andromache has gone:

{{quote:283054c1-7782-5ec5-8634-6e5d0aaee810}}

The housekeeper answers in the same words, and denies them: Andromache is not with the women at the temple but on the tower, hurrying to the wall “like a woman out of her mind” (6.382–389; [[handmaids]], [[like-a-woman-out-of-her-mind]]). The Trojan women's prayer has already been refused (6.311). In Book 22 the epithet names Andromache's own serving women, whom she tells to heat a bath for Hector's return, not knowing that he is dead (Il. 22.442–446; [[trojan-women]], [[andromache]]).
