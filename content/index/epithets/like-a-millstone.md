## Meaning

μυλοειδής = μύλη (“mill, millstone”) + εἶδος (“form, look”): “like a millstone.” The word occurs only here in Homer (7.270). A Homeric mill is a hand mill, turned by women. In the Odyssey twelve women toil at the mills in Odysseus' house, and the weakest of them, still grinding when the others have gone to sleep, stops her millstone to pray that the suitors' meal may be their last (Od. 20.105–119). The comparison gives Ajax's stone its size and weight. Compare the stone that Diomedes throws at Aeneas, “a huge thing, which two men could not carry, such as mortals are now” (5.302–304), and the one with which Hector breaks the gate of the Achaean wall (12.445–462; [[stone-throwing]]).

## Use

Hector's stone has rung on Ajax's shield. Ajax answers:

{{quote:15d84a4a-2fbc-5de4-8d18-b7bcb4369086}}

The stone is “far greater” than Hector's (7.268), and the throw is told with Ajax's whole body in it: he whirls it round and leans his “measureless strength” into it. It breaks Hector's shield inward and knocks him flat, and Apollo sets him on his feet (7.270–272; [[apollo]]). The duel of spears has become a duel of stones, and Ajax has the better of it. The two would have closed with swords if the heralds had not parted them (7.273–282). See [[the-duel-of-hector-and-ajax]] and [[ajax]].
