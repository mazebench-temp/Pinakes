## Meaning

μενεπτόλεμος = μένω (“stand fast, stay”) + πτόλεμος (the epic form of πόλεμος, “war”): “who holds his ground in war.” The compound is of the type with a verb stem first, like τερπικέραυνος ([[delighting-in-thunder]]). Homer uses it ten times, of Polypoetes most often, and of Thrasymedes, Diomedes, and others.

## Use

{{quote:ed410868-dbf6-5b5d-a537-54571068e06c}}

Polypoetes the Lapith, son of Peirithous and grandson of Zeus, leads the men of Argissa with Leonteus. Nine lines later the same epithet goes to a whole people, the Perrhaebians who live around wintry Dodona ({{ref:Iliad 2.749-750}}). Polypoetes keeps it later in the poem, in the fighting of Book 6 and in the funeral games of Book 23, where he wins the weight-throwing contest (Il. 6.29, 23.836–849).
