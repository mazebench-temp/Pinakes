## Meaning

βίη (“force, might”) + Αἰνείαο (“of Aeneas”): “the might of Aeneas,” that is, Aeneas himself in his strength, as [[might-of-heracles]] (βίη Ἡρακληείη) names Heracles and [[might-of-priam]] (Πριάμοιο βίη) names Priam. Homer has it once, in the verse that gives Aeneas the rule after the house of Priam: “and now mighty Aeneas shall be lord over the Trojans, / and his children's children, who will be born hereafter” (20.307–308). Poseidon speaks it as he resolves to save Aeneas from Achilles (20.293–308; [[poseidon]]). The periphrasis that named Priam as king when Menelaus called for him to swear the oath (3.105) is given to the man who will reign after his line. See [[aeneas]].
