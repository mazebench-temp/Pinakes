## Meaning

μιλτοπάρῃος = μίλτος (“red ochre, red earth”) + παρειά (“cheek”): “red-cheeked.” The “cheeks” of a ship are the sides of the bow. The word occurs twice in Homer: of Odysseus' ships in the Catalogue, and in the Odyssey of the ships the Cyclopes do not have (Od. 9.125). A similar compound, φοινικοπάρῃος (“purple-cheeked”), describes ships in Tiresias' prophecy of a land where men know nothing of the sea (Od. 11.124). Compare the other color of the fleet, [[black-ships]].

## Use

{{quote:845226fa-e569-5df9-896c-3f9ccacaa345}}

Odysseus' contingent is small, twelve ships, but it alone is given a detail of decoration. The Catalogue gives most contingents only numbers and a standing epithet (black, hollow). The red ships of the man who is “the equal of Zeus in counsel” ([[equal-of-zeus-in-counsel]]) stand out in a list of black ones.

## Outside Homer

Herodotus says that “in the old days all ships were painted with red ochre” (μιλτηλιφέες), when he tells how the Samians came to Siphnos in red ships (3.58).
