## Meaning

ῥοδοδάκτυλος = ῥόδον (“rose”) + δάκτυλος (“finger”): “with rosy fingers.” The image is of the first light spreading across the sky in pink streaks, like the fingers of a hand. In Homer Dawn is a goddess as well as a time of day ([[eos]]).

## Use

{{quote:7b1bb166-c3fc-5e06-adb2-cdefa98fc70a}}

The formula line ἦμος δ’ ἠριγένεια φάνη ῥοδοδάκτυλος Ἠώς ([[dawn-line]]) marks the morning after the sacrifice at Chryse, when the ship sets out for the camp. The line is at home in the Odyssey, which uses it many times to begin a new day. In the Iliad the whole line occurs only here and in the last book, at the dawn of the day on which the Trojans gather for Hector's funeral (Il. 24.788); the epithet alone appears a few more times. The two dawns frame the poem.

The epithet pairs with [[early-born]] (ἠριγένεια), which opens the same verse.

## Outside Homer

Sappho transfers the epithet to the moon, which rises after sunset “rosy-fingered” (βροδοδάκτυλος σελάννα, fr. 96.8).
