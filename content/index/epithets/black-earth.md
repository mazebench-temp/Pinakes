## Meaning

γαῖα (“earth, land”) + μέλαινα (“black, dark,” feminine of μέλας): “the black earth.” The color is that of plowed, fertile soil, and of the ground that covers the dead. The phrase closes the verse. It is used seven times in Homer, several of them of the earth running with blood in battle (Il. 15.715, 20.494). Compare [[grain-giving]], the other epithet of the earth in Book 2, and [[black-ships]] for the same adjective.

## Use

{{quote:18c19717-694e-5eba-ac7d-260fa39432fa}}

The Catalogue names Protesilaus as leader “while he lived,” then turns: “but by now the black earth held him fast.” The epithet of the earth is the whole of his death notice. It is followed by the grief he left behind, a wife with torn cheeks and a house half-built ([[the-death-of-protesilaus]], [[warlike]]).
