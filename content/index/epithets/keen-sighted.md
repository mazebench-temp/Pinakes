## Meaning

ἐΰσκοπος = εὖ (“well”) + σκοπός, the word of watching and of what one watches: the “lookout” who keeps watch ([[spies-and-scouts]]), and the “mark” an archer aims at (σκοπὸν ἄλλον, “another mark,” *Odyssey* 22.6). So the compound is “keen-sighted,” or “aiming well.” Walter Leaf gives both senses, “aiming well (or simply sharp-sighted)” (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 24.24). The translation renders it “keen-sighted.”

## Use

Homer uses the word five times. In the Iliad it comes only in Book 24, twice at the end of the same verse, ἐΰσκοπον ἀργεϊφόντην: the gods “kept urging the keen-sighted slayer of Argus to steal him away” (24.24), and Zeus tells Thetis of it (24.109; [[argeiphontes]]). The Odyssey gives it to Hermes twice: Zeus recalls that the gods sent Hermes, “the keen-sighted slayer of Argus,” to warn Aegisthus (*Odyssey* 1.37–39), and the Phaeacian lords pour their last libation of the night to him (*Odyssey* 7.136–138). Once it is said of Artemis, when the soul of Odysseus' mother tells him that the keen-sighted archer did not come upon her in her halls and kill her “with her gentle arrows” (*Odyssey* 11.197–199; [[of-the-showering-arrows]], [[gentle-words]]).
