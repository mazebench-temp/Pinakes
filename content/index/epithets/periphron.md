## Meaning

περίφρων = περι- (“exceedingly”) + φρήν (“mind”; [[phrenes]]): “very thoughtful, prudent.” In the Iliad it occurs once, in Book 5. In the Odyssey it is Penelope's epithet, περίφρων Πηνελόπεια, used about fifty times, and it is given also to the old nurse Eurycleia (Od. 19.357, 19.491, 20.134, 21.381) and to Queen Arete of the Phaeacians (Od. 11.345). The translation renders it “wise,” the word it uses also for πεπνυμένος ([[wise]]).

## Use

{{quote:80d02e09-656b-55af-beaf-aeb8a48716d9}}

Dione ends her consolation of Aphrodite with a warning for Diomedes. The man who fights the gods does not live long (5.406–409), and she pictures his wife waking the household with her wailing for him ([[aegialeia]], [[adrastus]]). Aegialeia is named once in Homer, with a patronymic, Ἀδρηστίνη, and two epithets of a wife: wise, and ἰφθίμη, “strong” ([[mighty]]). The epithet that the Odyssey gives to the wife who waits for her husband is given here to a wife imagined in mourning ([[husbands-and-wives]]).
