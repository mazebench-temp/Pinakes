## Meaning

πολύδακρυς = πολύς (“much”) + δάκρυ (“tear”): “causing many tears.” It is an epithet of war (πόλεμος πολύδακρυς) and of Ares, whose name stands for war itself. Compare the other epithets of war: [[hateful]], [[grim-sounding]], [[man-destroying]], [[relentless]], and [[tears]].

## Use

Two speakers in Book 3 give war this epithet. Iris, in the likeness of Laodice, tells Helen that the armies “were bringing tearful Ares on one another” before the truce (3.132). Priam, calling Helen to sit beside him, says she is not to blame:

{{quote:71eb3284-916b-501b-a634-01303f7cd625}}

For Priam the war is “the tearful war of the Achaeans,” the war they brought to Troy. Andromache uses the same words in her lament for Hector: even if their son escapes “the tearful war of the Achaeans,” sorrow will follow him (Il. 22.487). See [[aitios]].
