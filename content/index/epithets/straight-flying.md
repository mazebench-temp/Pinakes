## Meaning

ἰθύς (“straight”) + the root of πέτομαι (“fly”): “flying straight.” Walter Leaf explains it by Aeneas' words of Achilles in Book 20, “his weapon flies straight” (ἰθὺ βέλος πέτεται, 20.99), though he finds no complete analogy for the suffix. Zenodotus read ἰθυκτίωνα, said to mean “straight-grained” and derived from κτηδών, the fibre of wood, a derivation Leaf calls impossible (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 21.169; see the translation's note on 21.169).

## Use

{{quote:3bb4e42e-c6db-5da9-ae04-e2dacdcb347d}}

The verse begins as the one in which Achilles answered Aeneas' cast, “Then second in his turn Achilles let fly his long-shadowed spear” (20.273; [[long-shadowed]]), with the new epithet in place of the old. The straight-flying spear misses, and is driven halfway into the bank, where Asteropaeus cannot draw it out (21.171–177; [[ash-spear]], [[three-times-and-a-fourth]]). See [[the-pelian-ash]], [[asteropaeus]] and [[achilles]].
