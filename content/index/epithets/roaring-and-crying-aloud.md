## Meaning

ἄβρομος joins ἀ- to βρόμος, “roar, din,” and αὐΐαχος joins ἀ- to the root of ἰαχή, ἰάχω, “cry, shout.” Both are found only in this verse, and the prefix can be read two ways. Taken as the privative ἀ-, “not,” the words would mean “without roar, without cry.” Taken as an ἀ- of intensity, they mean “loud-roaring, loud-shouting.” The translation takes the second, since the verse follows a likeness to flame and storm wind (13.39), and the Iliad sets the clamor of the advancing Trojans against the silence of the Achaeans (3.2–9, 4.433–438; see the translation's note on 13.41).

## Use

{{quote:13f08b55-ff45-5103-9c12-ffa3bb952127}}

The Trojans come on “like a flame or a storm wind, in a body,” behind Hector, “roaring and crying aloud,” and hope to take the ships and kill all the best of the Achaeans beside them (13.39–42; [[trojans]], [[hector]], [[like-blazing-fire]]). Their noise is part of their threat; the Achaeans answer with the close order of the ranks around the two Ajaxes (13.126–135; [[battle-order]]). At the end of the book both armies shout, and “the roar of both reached the upper air and the rays of Zeus” (13.837). See [[the-battle-at-the-ships]].
