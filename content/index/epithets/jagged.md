## Meaning

ὀκριόεις is formed from ὄκρις (“sharp point, jag”) with -όεις (“full of”): “jagged.” Homer uses it of stones thrown in battle: here, when Hector strikes Teucer with a jagged stone (Il. 8.327), when Patroclus picks up a jagged block of marble (Il. 16.735), and in the Odyssey of the rocks the Cyclops might hurl at the ship (Od. 9.497–499). The heroes of the Iliad throw stones that two men of the present day could not carry (Il. 5.302–304). See [[shameless-stone]].

## Use

Diores, one of the leaders of the Epeians, is hit “by a jagged stone beside the ankle, on the right shin,” thrown by the Thracian Peirous (4.518–520). The narrator names the stone again as it crushes the tendons and the bones: it is the “shameless stone” (4.521–522; [[shameless-stone]]). Peirous runs up and kills him with the spear (4.524–526; [[diores]], [[peirous]]).
