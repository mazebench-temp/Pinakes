## Meaning

θεῷ, “to a god,” + ἐναλίγκιος, “like,” + ἄντην, “face to face”: Merry renders ἄντην “in presence,” literally “if looked at in the face” (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 2.5; see the translation's note on *Odyssey* 2.5). The phrase is built like [[like-a-god-in-his-voice]], θεῷ ἐναλίγκιος αὐδήν, with the adverb in the place of the accusative of respect.

## Use

It ends the verse βῆ δʼ ἴμεν ἐκ θαλάμοιο θεῷ ἐναλίγκιος ἄντην, “and went out of his chamber, like a god to look upon,” the last of the five verses in which a man rises at dawn and dresses: Telemachus on the morning of the assembly (*Odyssey* 2.5) and Menelaus at Sparta on the morning after Telemachus arrives (*Odyssey* 4.310; [[dressing-scene]]). Compare [[godlike]] and [[equal-to-the-gods]].
