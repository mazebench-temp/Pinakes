## Meaning

ἄναξ ἀνδρῶν = ἄναξ (“lord, master”) + ἀνδρῶν, genitive plural of ἀνήρ (“man”): “lord of men.” ἄναξ is one of the oldest Greek words for a ruler. In the Linear B tablets of the Mycenaean palaces it is written *wa-na-ka*, the title of the king. Its lost initial consonant, the digamma (ϝ), still shows in Homer's verse: at {{ref:Iliad 1.7}}, {{ref:Iliad 1.172}}, and {{ref:Iliad 1.506}} the vowel before ἄναξ is not elided (Ἀτρεΐδης τε ἄναξ, ἔπειτα ἄναξ, νῦν γε ἄναξ), because when the formula took shape the word still began with a consonant, ϝάναξ.

Alone, ἄναξ is used of gods as readily as of men. In Book 1 it names Apollo (Ἀπόλλωνι ἄνακτι, “to lord Apollo,” {{ref:Iliad 1.36}}; also 75, 390, 444) and Zeus (Δία Κρονίωνα ἄνακτα, {{ref:Iliad 1.502}}; also 529). The genitive ἀνδρῶν gives Agamemnon's title its human and collective sense: he is lord of the assembled army.

## Use

The formula names Agamemnon at the opening of the poem:

{{quote:2bffc4f0-a5a0-5770-842d-f3576baeeade}}

The line balances the two men, each with his honor. Agamemnon is named by patronymic ([[son-of-atreus]]) and by rank; Achilles by a personal epithet, [[brilliant]]. The quarrel that follows sets rank against worth.

ἄναξ ἀνδρῶν Ἀγαμέμνων fills the second half of the verse after the caesura in the middle of the third foot. It therefore joins the answer formula τὸν δ’ ἠμείβετ’ ἔπειτα ([[then-answered]]) to make a whole line:

{{quote:01a5a532-8836-5311-bf5a-09a62745d193}}

The title is heard twice more, both times in other mouths. Odysseus uses it when he hands Chryseis back to her father as Agamemnon's envoy, giving his master's authority to the act of restitution ({{ref:Iliad 1.442}}). Thetis uses it in her supplication to Zeus, and there the grand title sharpens the complaint: this lord of men has dishonored her son.

{{quote:edc82e54-6027-5f35-bdf2-7a90446bfb7a}}

In the Iliad as a whole the formula belongs overwhelmingly to Agamemnon. It is given only occasionally to others, among them Anchises and Aeneas (Il. 5.268, 5.311) and Eumelus (Il. 23.288). Agamemnon's other titles in Book 1 are [[powerful]] (κρείων) and [[wide-ruling]] (εὐρὺ κρείων); against them stand the insults of Achilles, from [[most-covetous]] to [[people-devouring-king]].
