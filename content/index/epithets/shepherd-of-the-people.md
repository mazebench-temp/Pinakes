## Meaning

ποιμήν (“shepherd”) + λαῶν, genitive plural of λαός (“the people, the host”): the leader who tends his people as a shepherd tends a flock, guiding and protecting them. The image of the king as shepherd is old and widespread in the ancient Near East, where Mesopotamian kings from Hammurabi on call themselves shepherds of their people. In the Iliad the title is used of many leaders, and above all of [[Agamemnon]].

## Use

{{quote:e1f10bd2-9ead-5161-ad2b-daa4ec1de234}}

In Book 1 the title belongs to a hero of the past, not to the present king. The nearest the book comes to it for Agamemnon is its reverse, Achilles' [[people-devouring-king]] (δημοβόρος βασιλεύς), a king who consumes his people instead of tending them. The plague has already shown the people suffering for their leader's act ({{ref:Iliad 1.10}}).
