## Meaning

ἀταλάφρων = ἀταλός (“tender, young, frolicsome”) + φρήν (“mind, heart”): “with the mind of a young child.” The compound occurs only here in Homer. Its first part describes the young elsewhere: the girls and youths who carry the grapes at the vintage on the shield of Achilles, ἀταλὰ φρονέοντες, “with young and carefree hearts” (Il. 18.567), the mares that delight in their young foals (πώλοισιν … ἀταλῇσι, Il. 20.222), and the young girls among the dead whom Odysseus sees in the underworld (παρθενικαί τʼ ἀταλαί, Od. 11.39). The mind it describes knows nothing yet.

## Use

{{quote:915a8bbc-80e3-5a16-9ff0-d4082a5abbf6}}

Three words for the child's youth stand in one verse: he is carried “at her breast” (ἐπὶ κόλπῳ), he is “of tender mind,” and he is νήπιον αὔτως, “an infant, quite small” ([[nepios]]). Then he is named twice, by his father and by the people ([[astyanax]]). The epithet prepares the scene at the end of the meeting. The child does not know his father in his helmet: he shrinks back crying into the nurse's bosom, frightened by the bronze and the nodding plume, and his parents laugh (6.466–471; [[horsehair-crested]]). The grown-ups in the scene know what is coming for Troy; the child of tender mind knows only what he sees.
