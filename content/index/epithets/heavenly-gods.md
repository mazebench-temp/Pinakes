## Meaning

Οὐρανίωνες is formed from οὐρανός (“sky, heaven”) with the suffix -ίων, which also forms patronymics (Κρονίων, [[son-of-cronus]]). It could mean either “the heavenly ones” or “the sons of Ouranos.” In Homer it normally names the Olympian gods. Once, in Book 5, it seems to mean the older gods whom Zeus imprisoned below the earth (Il. 5.898).

## Use

{{quote:ff1253de-7c30-5796-8fdd-aa7854dabfe1}}

The heavenly gods are troubled (ὄχθησαν) throughout the house of Zeus when he threatens Hera. The same verb introduced Zeus' own reply to Thetis: he answered her “deeply troubled” (μέγ’ ὀχθήσας, [[deeply-troubled]], {{ref:Iliad 1.517}}). The trouble has spread from Zeus to the whole household, and it lasts until Hephaestus speaks.
