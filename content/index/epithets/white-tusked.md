## Meaning

ἀργιόδους = ἀργός, ἀργι- (“white, shining”) + ὀδούς (“tooth”): “with white teeth, white-tusked.” Homer uses it of swine, wild and tame: the boar of Calydon (9.539); the boar's tusks set in rows on the leather helmet that Meriones gives Odysseus (ἀργιόδοντος ὑός, 10.263–264); the swine slaughtered for Patroclus' funeral feast, “white-tusked hogs, rich with lard” (23.32); and in the Odyssey the eight swine the Phaeacians kill for their feast (Od. 8.60), the boar's chine that Odysseus sends to Demodocus (Od. 8.476), and Eumaeus' herd (Od. 14.416, 14.423, 14.438, 14.532). Once it describes hunting dogs, in a simile of Hector urging on his men “as a hunter sets his white-toothed hounds on a wild boar or a lion” (11.292–293). The ἀργ- of the word is the root of whiteness and shine in ἀργής (“bright, white”) and ἄργυρος (“silver”; [[god-of-the-silver-bow]]). Compare [[boar]].

## Use

{{quote:369e4e11-90f1-5979-8d4e-cbb57e3c8a08}}

Artemis, angry that Oeneus forgot her in his harvest offerings, sends against him “a wild boar, a lurker in the grass, with white tusks,” which tears up his orchard (9.533–542; [[artemis]], [[oeneus]], [[lurker-in-the-grass]]). The epithet is the ordinary one of any boar, set beside the rare word that marks this one. See [[the-calydonian-boar]].
