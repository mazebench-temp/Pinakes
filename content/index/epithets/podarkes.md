## Meaning

ποδάρκης = πούς, ποδ- (“foot”) + ἀρκέω (“ward off; be strong enough, suffice”): “whose feet are strong,” “sufficient in the feet.” The sense is close to [[swift-footed]] (πόδας ὠκύς), and the translation renders both the same way. The exact force of the second element is not certain, since ἀρκέω means both “to keep off” and “to be enough.” In Homer the word belongs to Achilles alone.

## Use

It occurs once in Book 1, in a whole-line formula of reply:

{{quote:29e9c380-725c-58be-a5eb-f15349dd7651}}

ποδάρκης δῖος Ἀχιλλεύς joins this epithet to [[brilliant]] (δῖος) and fills the second half of the verse, after the caesura in the middle of the third foot. That is the slot held by ἄναξ ἀνδρῶν Ἀγαμέμνων ([[lord-of-men]]), and fifty lines later Agamemnon answers Achilles in the same frame, τὸν δ’ ἠμείβετ’ ἔπειτα ([[then-answered]], {{ref:Iliad 1.172}}). The two antagonists are introduced by formulas of the same shape, one naming the body's speed and the other the king's rank.
