## Meaning

καλλιπάρῃος = καλός (“beautiful”) + παρειά (“cheek”): “with beautiful cheeks.” It is an epithet of women and goddesses; in Book 15 it is given to Themis (Il. 15.87), and in Book 24 to Leto (Il. 24.607).

## Use

In Book 1 it always follows the woman's name at the end of the verse, Χρυσηΐδα καλλιπάρῃον or Βρισηΐδα καλλιπάρῃον: {{ref:Iliad 1.143}}, {{ref:Iliad 1.184}}, {{ref:Iliad 1.310}}, {{ref:Iliad 1.323}}, {{ref:Iliad 1.346}}, {{ref:Iliad 1.369}}. The two names have the same shape, so the same formula fits either woman. The verse treats them as interchangeable in the same way the quarrel does: one girl is sent away and the other is taken in her place.

{{quote:4c2f8dcf-f335-55bd-8092-c1059e3d6740}}

Every occurrence is in the accusative. The women are always the object of a verb: they are set on board (143, 310), led away (184, 323, 346), or chosen out as a prize (369). The epithet praises a beauty that makes them valuable, and the grammar shows who disposes of them.
