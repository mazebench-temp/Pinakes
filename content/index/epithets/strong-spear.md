## Meaning

ἄλκιμος is formed from ἀλκή (“strength, defense, prowess”): “strong, stout, valiant.” It praises warriors and their spirit (“take up a valiant heart,” ἄλκιμον ἦτορ ἕλεσθε, Il. 5.529) and the spear they carry. The verse εἵλετο δʼ ἄλκιμον ἔγχος, “and he took up a strong spear,” ends the arming of a hero.

## Use

“And he took up a strong spear that fitted his grip” (3.338) is the last step of Paris' arming. At the start of the book he came out with a bow, a sword and two spears (3.17–18; [[curved-bow]], [[tipped-with-bronze]]), the gear of a man who throws and shoots. For the formal duel he arms with a single spear, as Menelaus does. At the arming of Patroclus the verse has two spears (ἄλκιμα δοῦρε, Il. 16.139). See [[arming-scene]], [[spear]].
