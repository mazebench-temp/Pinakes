## Meaning

κενεαυχής = κενεός (“empty”) + a root of boasting, the one in the later verb αὐχέω (“boast”): “boasting emptily.” The adjective describes the speakers, and the translation turns it into a phrase: “the empty boasts you uttered on Lemnos.” The boasts themselves are εὐχωλαί (8.229), the word that also means “prayers” ([[euchomai]]).

A boast made over wine is one that the fighting tests. In Book 20 Apollo, in the shape of Lycaon, asks Aeneas where his threats have gone, the ones he made to the Trojan princes over his wine, that he would fight Achilles face to face (20.83–85). Agamemnon's question in Book 8 has the same form: πῇ ἔβαν εὐχωλαί, “Where have our boasts gone?”

## Use

{{quote:c0105804-2e15-5519-86df-98e1bc03061a}}

Agamemnon shouts from Odysseus' ship to the whole camp ([[huge-hulled]]). He begins with the rebuke that Hera used in Book 5, “Shame, Argives, wretched disgraces, admirable in looks!” (8.228 = 5.787; [[best-in-looks]], [[aidos]]). The occasion of the boasts was a feast on Lemnos, of which the poem says nothing more, with much meat of oxen and bowls full of wine ([[straight-horned]], [[brim-full]], [[lemnos]], [[wine]]). The numbers have shrunk from a hundred or two hundred Trojans for each Achaean to less than one: “now we are not worth even one man, Hector” (8.234–235). Then he turns from the army to Zeus and prays ([[father-zeus]]). See [[agamemnon]] and [[praise-and-blame]].
