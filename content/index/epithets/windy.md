## Meaning

ἠνεμόεις = ἄνεμος (“wind”) + the suffix -όεις (“full of”), with the first vowel lengthened for the meter: “windy.” It describes heights exposed to the wind: the fig tree by the walls of Troy, the headlands of the Odyssey, and above all Troy itself, which is “windy Ilios” (Ἴλιον ἠνεμόεσσαν) in seven lines of the Iliad. The city stood on a hill near the mouth of the Hellespont, where the north wind blows hard.

## Use

{{quote:6d674452-71f1-5bba-a497-3f3c4f044159}}

In the Catalogue the epithet goes once to a place in the Peloponnese. Enispe is one of the Arcadian towns, and nothing else is known of it; its site was unknown even in antiquity. Its epithet suits the mountain country of Arcadia, where the men “fight hand to hand” ([[hand-to-hand-fighters]]) and Orchomenus is “rich in flocks” ([[rich-in-flocks]]).
