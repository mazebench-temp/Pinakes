## Meaning

πολύφρων = πολύς (“much”) + φρήν (“mind, wits”; [[phrenes]]): “of many thoughts, of much understanding.” Achilles wishes that anger might perish, “which sets even a man of much wisdom to rage” (18.108; [[sweeter-than-dripping-honey]]), and the Odyssey uses the same turn of verse of wine, which sets even a man of much wisdom to sing and to laugh softly and to dance (ὅς τʼ ἐφέηκε πολύφρονά περ μάλʼ ἀεῖσαι, *Odyssey* 14.463–466). As an epithet it belongs to the god of craft, Ἡφαίστοιο … πολύφρονος (21.367; *Odyssey* 8.297, 8.327), and to Odysseus, in the verse νοστῆσαι Ὀδυσῆα πολύφρονα ὅνδε δόμονδε, “that Odysseus of much wisdom should return to his own house” (*Odyssey* 1.83, 14.424, 20.239, 21.204; with νοστήσειν, *Odyssey* 20.329). Compare [[of-many-wiles]] (πολύμητις).
