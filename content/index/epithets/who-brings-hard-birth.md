## Meaning

μογοστόκος = μόγος (“toil, pain”) + τόκος (“birth, childbearing”): “of the pains of birth,” the goddess who brings on labor and presides over it. Homer gives the epithet only to Eileithyia: here to the Eileithyiai, in the plural, “the daughters of Hera, who have the bitter pangs in their keeping” (11.270–271); at the birth of the Myrmidon leader Eudorus, whom Eileithyia brought into the light (16.186–188); and in Zeus' boast that on that day Eileithyia would bring to light a man who would rule all his neighbors, the boast that Hera turned against Heracles (19.103–104). The verses around the epithet name the pain as well: the “sharp” dart, the “bitter” pangs ([[sharp]], [[bitter]]).

## Use

{{quote:945db8eb-1f57-5d97-bc58-edc10011c89f}}

When the blood stops and the wound dries, sharp pains come over Agamemnon's might, and the poem compares them to the dart that the Eileithyiai send on a woman in labor. The verse that names the pain stands before and after the simile (11.268, 11.272). The king who has just killed the sons of Antenor mounts his chariot and has himself driven to the ships (11.273–283; [[chariot-journey]]). See [[the-woman-in-labor]], [[eileithyia]], [[hera]] and [[agamemnon]].

## Outside Homer

In the Homeric Hymn to Apollo, μογοστόκος Εἰλείθυια is kept on Olympus by Hera while Leto labors nine days and nights on Delos; the other goddesses send Iris to bring her, and as soon as she sets foot on the island Leto gives birth (*Hymn to Apollo* 89–119).
