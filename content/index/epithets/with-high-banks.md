## Meaning

ἠϊόεις occurs only here. It is usually derived from ἠϊών (“shore, bank”) with the suffix -όεις (“having”): “with banks,” and so “with high banks.” The derivation is not certain. The Scamander's other epithets describe its water and its plain: it has a “flowering meadow” ([[flowering]]), and in later books it is the eddying Xanthus, son of Zeus ([[eddying]]).

## Use

Athena has persuaded Ares to leave the mortals to fight it out: “So saying, she led furious Ares out of the battle, and then she sat him down by the high banks of the Scamander” (5.35–36; [[furious]]). The war god is set down on the riverbank, out of the fighting, while the Danaans turn the Trojans back (5.37). Aphrodite finds him there later, sitting on the left of the battle with his spear and horses resting in a mist (5.355–356). The river returns at the end of the book: Hera halts her chariot where the Simoeis and the Scamander join, before the goddesses go on foot to bring Ares down (5.773–777; [[simoeis]]).
