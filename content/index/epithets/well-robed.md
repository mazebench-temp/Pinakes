## Meaning

ἐΰπεπλος = εὖ (“well”) + πέπλος (“robe”; [[robe]]): “with fine robes.” Homer uses it of women: the Achaean women (5.424; Od. 21.160), the wives of Hector's brothers (Il. 6.378, 6.383, 24.769), the maidservant who stands with Andromache on the tower (Il. 6.372), and Nausicaa (Od. 6.49). Compare [[long-robed]] (τανύπεπλος) and [[fair-belted]] (ἐΰζωνος).

## Use

{{quote:aa77859a-d270-5011-98d6-856cb8b793c5}}

Athena turns the wound Diomedes gave Aphrodite into a joke. The goddess, she says, must have been urging some Achaean woman to go off with the Trojans, and while she caressed one of these well-robed women she scratched her hand on a golden brooch. The fine robe and its pin make a spear wound into a lady's scratch, and Zeus smiles (5.426; [[smiling-at-him]], [[aphrodite]]). Aphrodite's own robe, which the Graces made, was no protection against the spear (5.338; [[ambrosial]]).
