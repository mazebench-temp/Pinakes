## Meaning

πλατύς means “broad, wide.” Homer gives the Hellespont four epithets: “broad” (πλατύς, 7.86, 17.432; Od. 24.82), “strongly flowing” (ἀγάρροος, 2.845, 12.30), “full of fish” (ἰχθυόεις, 9.360) and “boundless” (ἀπείρων, 24.545). The strait between the Troad and the Thracian Chersonese is narrow, and “broad” and “boundless” have been explained by taking Homer's Hellespont to include the open sea along the coast of the Troad, on whose beach the Achaeans drew up their ships. The Achaeans in flight run for “the ships and the Hellespont” (15.233, 18.150; compare 23.2). See [[hellespont]].

## Use

Hector imagines the tomb of the man he will kill:

{{quote:c72710c3-0150-5475-b50c-44f0466cc968}}

and the passer-by who will point it out to later generations (7.87–91; [[born-hereafter]], [[tis-speech]]). The mound on the shore is the point of the promise: a tomb by the sea is seen by every sailor who passes, and it keeps the name of the man who killed him.

In the Odyssey the words are used of the tomb of Achilles and Patroclus. Agamemnon's ghost tells Achilles' ghost that the army heaped a great mound over their bones “on a jutting headland, on the broad Hellespont, so that it might be seen far out from the sea by men, those who are now and those who will be hereafter” (Od. 24.80–84). Hector imagines such a tomb for the Achaean he will kill; the one that stands there in the end belongs to the Achaean who killed Hector. See [[tomb]], [[kleos]] and [[hector]].
