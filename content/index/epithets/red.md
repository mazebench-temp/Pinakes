## Meaning

ἐρυθρός means “red.” Homer uses it of three things: once of bronze (9.365), of the nectar of the gods (νέκταρ ἐρυθρόν, 19.38; Od. 5.93), and in the Odyssey of wine, often in the verse-end οἶνον ἐρυθρόν (Od. 5.165, 9.163, 9.208, 12.19, 12.327, 13.69, 16.444). Bronze is usually described by its gleam, χαλκὸς αἴθοψ, “gleaming bronze” ([[gleaming-bronze]]); “red” names the color of the metal, an alloy rich in copper ([[bronze]]). The town Erythrae in Boeotia takes its name from the same word (Ἐρυθράς, 2.499).

## Use

{{quote:59495f86-bb59-59c8-a19b-fd5028e444d2}}

Achilles answers Agamemnon's list of gifts with a list of his own. He left much at home, and he will take home from Troy other gold, red bronze, fair-belted women and gray iron, “all that I won by lot” (9.364–367; [[fair-belted]], [[gray-sea]], [[gold-and-silver]]). The verse sets out the same kinds of wealth that Agamemnon offered, and its point is that Achilles has them already, as his own share of the spoil. The one thing taken from him, his prize, is the thing the gifts cannot replace (9.367–368; [[geras]]).
