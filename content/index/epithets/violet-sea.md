## Meaning

ἰοειδής = ἴον (“violet,” the flower) + εἶδος (“look”): “violet-looking.” Homer uses it only of the πόντος, the open sea: here, in the simile of the storm wind that stirs it up (11.298), and in the Odyssey of the sea that Hermes crosses to Calypso's island (Od. 5.56) and of the sea from which Odysseus' ship will put in at Thrinacia (Od. 11.107). It is one of the sea's colors in Homer, with [[wine-dark]] (οἶνοψ), [[gray-sea]] (πολιός) and the [[shining-sea]] (δῖα). See [[sea]].

## Use

{{quote:2383fe2e-553e-51bf-99d4-72c94162940d}}

Hector falls on the battle as the squall falls on the water and stirs up the violet sea ([[blustering]], [[the-storm-wind-on-the-sea]]). See [[hector]].

## Outside Homer

Hesiod gives the epithet to the sea that is seized by heat during Zeus' battle with Typhoeus (*Theogony* 844), and to the spring around which the Muses dance on Helicon (*Theogony* 3).
