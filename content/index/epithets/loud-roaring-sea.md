## Meaning

πολύφλοισβος = πολύς (“much”) + φλοῖσβος (“roar, din,” a word that imitates the sound it names): “loud-roaring.” It belongs to the formula παρὰ θῖνα πολυφλοίσβοιο θαλάσσης, “along the shore of the loud-roaring sea,” which fills the second half of the verse.

## Use

{{quote:14b3cea1-ebf0-5761-a62a-a5308699ebd9}}

The line is famous for its sound. The priest goes ἀκέων, “in silence,” and the sea roars. The noise of the waves fills the silence of a man who cannot answer a king, and it is there by the sea, alone, that he prays. Achilles too withdraws to the shore to pray when he is wronged ({{ref:Iliad 1.348-351}}; [[gray-sea]]).

Elsewhere in the Iliad men walk along the shore of the loud-roaring sea at moments of strain: Odysseus and Ajax on their way to Achilles in Book 9 (Il. 9.182), and Achilles himself, lying groaning by the water after Patroclus' death (Il. 23.59).
