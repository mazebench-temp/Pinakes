## Meaning

ἀρήϊος is the adjective of Ἄρης: “of Ares, warlike.” Homer uses it of warriors, especially Menelaus (ἀρήϊος Μενέλαος), and of armor (ἀρήϊα τεύχεα, “war gear”). The god's name stands for war itself, so the adjective means “made for war.” Compare [[dear-to-ares]] (ἀρηΐφιλος), [[offshoot-of-ares]], and [[equal-of-ares]].

## Use

{{quote:18c19717-694e-5eba-ac7d-260fa39432fa}}

The Catalogue lists the men Protesilaus led “while he lived.” The epithet names what he was, and the following lines say what became of him: the first Achaean to leap ashore at Troy, he was killed by a Dardanian, leaving a wife in mourning and a house half-built ([[the-death-of-protesilaus]]). The epithet returns a few lines later, when the Catalogue contrasts him with his younger brother Podarces, who now leads the men: “the other was both older and braver, the warlike hero Protesilaus” ({{ref:Iliad 2.707-708}}).
