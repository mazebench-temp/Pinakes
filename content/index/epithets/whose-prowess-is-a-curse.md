## Meaning

αἰναρέτης = αἰνός (“dread, terrible”; [[most-dread]]) + ἀρετή (“excellence, prowess”; [[arete]]): one whose excellence is a dread thing. The compound is found only here, in the vocative, at the head of the verse. Patroclus explains it at once: the prowess is a curse because it helps no one, “What good will anyone have of you, even one born hereafter, / if you do not ward off shameful ruin from the Argives?” (16.31–32; [[born-hereafter]]).
