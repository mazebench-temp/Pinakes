## Meaning

νεοπενθής = νέος (“new”) + πένθος (“grief, sorrow”; see [[penthos]]): “with fresh grief.” Homer has it once, in the phrase νεοπενθέα θυμὸν ἔχουσαι, “with hearts new to sorrow,” of the tender maidens in the throng of the dead (*Odyssey* 11.39; see [[atalos]] and [[who-had-borne-much]]). Merry takes it as “a heart whose sorrow was just fresh,” and sets beside it νεοπαθής in Aeschylus (*Eumenides* 514) and Hesiod's νεοκηδέι θυμῷ; he finds the same sense in the scholion, “ever keeping their grief fresh, or having newly suffered,” and prefers it to the other explanation, “grieving in their youth”; the word, he says, shows the maidens with all the signs of a new grief, the loosened hair and the streaming eyes (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 11.39).

## Outside Homer

Hesiod's compound of the same kind is in his praise of the singer: even if a man has grief in a heart newly troubled (πένθος ἔχων νεοκηδέι θυμῷ) and wastes away in anguish, when a singer sings the glorious deeds of earlier men and the gods, he forgets his sorrow at once (*Theogony* 98–103).
