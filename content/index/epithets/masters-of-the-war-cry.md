## Meaning

μήστωρ is formed from μήδομαι (“devise, plan”): “deviser, master” + ἀϋτή (“shout, war cry,” and so battle): “masters of the war cry.” The noun makes several formulas. μήστωρες ἀϋτῆς is used of Meriones and Antilochus (Il. 13.93, 13.479), and of Patroclus and Hector fighting over the body of Cebriones (Il. 16.759–760). Diomedes and Hector are μήστωρ φόβοιο, “master of rout” (Il. 6.97, 12.39), and Priam is θεόφιν μήστωρ ἀτάλαντος, “a counselor equal to the gods” (Il. 7.366). Compare [[good-at-the-war-cry]] (βοὴν ἀγαθός).

## Use

{{quote:a7018e75-6081-5f66-b393-fd416e2516df}}

The Athenians, masters of the war cry, and the Cephallenians beside them are standing still, because “their people had not yet heard the war cry” (4.331). The same noun ends both verses. Their leader Menestheus was praised in the Catalogue as the best of all men at marshaling horses and shield-bearing men ({{ref:Iliad 2.552-554}}), and here he waits with Odysseus for another column to attack first. Agamemnon rebukes them both (4.336–348; [[menestheus]], [[athenians]], [[driver-of-horses]]).
