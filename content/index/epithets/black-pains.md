## Meaning

ὀδύνη (“pain,” especially the pain of a wound) with μέλας (“black, dark”; compare [[black-doom]] and [[dark-blood]]): “black pains,” pains that darken the mind. The genitive μελαινάων ὀδυνάων ends the verse in a formula of healing, of the drugs that stop or cure the black pains (4.191; Il. 15.394, of the drugs Patroclus spreads on the wound of Eurypylus).

## Use

The phrase frames the wounding of Menelaus. The arrow Pandarus takes from his quiver is “a source of black pains” (μελαινέων ἕρμʼ ὀδυνάων, 4.117); ἕρμα is a prop or foundation, and the exact sense of the phrase is debated. When Agamemnon learns that the wound is not deadly, he promises that “a healer will examine the wound and lay on it drugs that will stop the black pains” (4.190–191). The pain the arrow brings and the pain the healer will end are named in the same words ([[healing]], [[soothing-drugs]], [[bitter]]).
