## Meaning

ἀκρόκομος = ἄκρος (“topmost”) + κόμη (“hair”): “with hair at the top,” that is, with the hair gathered in a knot on the crown of the head. It occurs only here in Homer (4.533). Homer marks several peoples by their hair: the Achaeans are [[long-haired]] (κάρη κομόωντες), and the Abantes wear their hair “long at the back” (ὄπιθεν κομόωντες, {{ref:Iliad 2.542}}). The Thracians' topknot marks them as strangers in the same way.

## Use

{{quote:11f8b239-70df-50b8-9e67-53609aa28116}}

Thoas has killed Peirous, the Thracian leader who killed Diores, but he cannot strip his armor. The Thracians close around their leader's body with their long spears and push Thoas back, “big and mighty and noble as he was” (4.534; [[noble]], [[mighty]]). The two leaders lie side by side in the dust (4.536–538; [[thracians]], [[peirous]], [[thoas]]).
