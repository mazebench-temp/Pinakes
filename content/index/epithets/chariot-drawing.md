## Meaning

ἐρυσάρματες = ἐρύω (“draw, drag”) + ἅρμα (“chariot”): horses that draw the chariot. It is found only in the plural. The Iliad has it twice, both times at the ditch before the Achaean wall: of the Trojan horses driven over it on the road that Apollo makes (15.354), and of the horses that break the poles of their masters' chariots in it when the Trojans flee from Patroclus (16.370–371; [[the-achaean-wall]]). See [[horses]] and [[chariot]].

## Outside Homer

In the Hesiodic *Shield of Heracles*, Cycnus will not listen to Heracles' warning and hold back his chariot-drawing horses, ἐχέμεν ἐρυσάρματας ἵππους (*Shield* 368–369).
