## Meaning

ὑπέρθυμος = ὑπέρ (“over, beyond”) + θυμός (“spirit, heart”): “of exceeding spirit.” It is praise, not blame. Homer uses it twenty-seven times: of heroes such as Diomedes, Heracles and Achilles, and above all of the Trojans as a people, Τρῶες ὑπέρθυμοι. It belongs with [[great-hearted]] (μεγάθυμος, μεγαλήτωρ), a more common word of the same kind.

## Use

{{quote:ed410868-dbf6-5b5d-a537-54571068e06c}}

The Lapith entry of the Catalogue gives its leaders their fathers and grandfathers. Leonteus is the son of high-hearted Coronus and grandson of Caeneus, the Lapith hero who fought the Centaurs ([[caeneus]], [[catalogue-of-lapiths]]). The epithet carries the praise of the older generation down to the son at Troy. In Book 12 Leonteus and Polypoetes are themselves “high-hearted sons of Lapith spearmen” (Il. 12.128).
