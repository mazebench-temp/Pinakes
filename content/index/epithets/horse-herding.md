## Meaning

ἱππόπολος = ἵππος (“horse”) + -πόλος, from πέλομαι, πολέω (“be busy about, tend”), as in αἰπόλος, “goatherd”: “busied with horses, horse-herding.” Homer has it twice, both times in the genitive Θρῃκῶν ἱπποπόλων at the same place in the verse: Zeus looks away from Troy to the land of the horse-herding Thracians (13.4), and Hera, flying from Olympus toward Lemnos, rushes over the snowy mountains of the horse-herding Thracians (14.225–228). Compare [[horse-pasturing]] (ἱππόβοτος), the epithet of Argos, and [[tamer-of-horses]], of the Trojans.

## Use

{{quote:faaba23f-3a21-5855-9503-2f01e20555a0}}

The Thracians stand first in the list of northern peoples on whom Zeus turns his eyes, and their epithet names them by their herds, as the next peoples are named by what they drink (13.4–6; [[thracians]], [[thrace]], [[noble]], [[abii]]). In the night raid of Book 10 Dolon calls the horses of the Thracian king Rhesus the finest he has seen, “whiter than snow, and like the winds in running” (10.436–437; [[rhesus]]).
