## Meaning

κολλητός is the verbal adjective of κολλάω (“glue, join”), from κόλλα (“glue”): “glued, joined together.” It praises the joinery of chariots, whose bodies and wheels were made of fitted and bent wood; the poplar simile of Book 4 shows a chariot-maker cutting a tree to bend into a wheel rim (4.485–486; [[the-poplar]]). The same word describes doors and door planks (Il. 9.583; Od. 21.137). Compare [[polished]] (ἐΰξοος), another word for the woodworker's finish.

## Use

Agamemnon finds Diomedes “standing among his horses and his well-joined chariot” (4.366), with Sthenelus beside him: ready, but not moving. The rebuke follows (4.370–400). The same verse describes Hector in Book 11, standing among his horses and chariots when Iris brings him the message of Zeus (Il. 11.197–198). When Diomedes answers Agamemnon, it is by leaping down from the chariot in his armor (4.419; [[stout-hearted]], [[chariot]], [[diomedes]]).
