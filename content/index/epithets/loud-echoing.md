## Meaning

πολυηχής = πολύς (“much”) + ἠχή (“sound, echo”): “much-sounding, resounding.” Homer uses it twice: of the beach where the waves break (4.422), and in the Odyssey of the nightingale, who pours out her many-voiced song (Od. 19.521). Compare the epithets of the sea's noise, [[loud-roaring-sea]] (πολύφλοισβος) and [[echoing-sea]] (ἠχήεις).

## Use

{{quote:9dc3b891-9cbc-5594-a730-092f3089db1b}}

The simile of the waves opens the battle. The shore resounds as the swell breaks and roars, and the Danaans move to war rank upon rank. Then the sound drops away: the Achaeans march in silence, in fear of their commanders, while the Trojans clamor like sheep in the fold (4.427–438; [[waves-on-the-shore]], [[sheep-in-the-fold]], [[silence]]). The loud-echoing shore belongs to the picture, not to the army it describes.
