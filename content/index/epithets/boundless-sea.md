## Meaning

ἀπείρων = ἀ- (“not”) + πεῖραρ, πέρας (“end, limit”): “boundless.” Homer uses it of the sea and of the earth (ἐπ’ ἀπείρονα γαῖαν, “over the boundless earth”).

## Use

{{quote:9b088558-95a9-5caf-b1d2-e74583663031}}

Achilles sits by the shore of the gray sea ([[gray-sea]]) and looks out ἐπ’ ἀπείρονα πόντον, “over the boundless deep,” toward the depths where his mother lives. The limitless sea frames a man whose life, he says in the next lines, is short ([[short-lived]]).

## Outside Homer

As a noun, τὸ ἄπειρον, “the boundless,” became the first principle of the philosopher Anaximander in the sixth century BCE (DK 12 B1).
