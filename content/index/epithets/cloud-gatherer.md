## Meaning

νεφεληγερέτα = νεφέλη (“cloud”) + ἀγείρω (“gather”) + an agent suffix: “he who gathers the clouds.” It describes the weather god who masses the storm, and it belongs with Zeus' other sky epithets in Book 1: [[lord-of-dark-clouds]] (κελαινεφής), [[thunders-on-high]] (ὑψιβρεμέτης), [[delighting-in-thunder]] (τερπικέραυνος), and [[lord-of-the-lightning]] (ἀστεροπητής). Zeus' own name goes back to an Indo-European word for the bright sky.

The ending is archaic. Nominatives in short -τα, such as νεφεληγερέτα, μητίετα ([[counselor]]), and εὐρύοπα ([[far-seeing]]), stand where ordinary Greek has -της. They are usually explained as old vocatives that came to be used as nominatives in fixed phrases. The form itself is a sign of the formula's age.

## Use

In Book 1 the formula νεφεληγερέτα Ζεύς appears three times, all in the scene on Olympus, and always at the end of a verse after προσέφη (“said to”):

{{quote:9abf2cef-3c98-55b7-8869-bbb700642c82}}

The phrase has exactly the metrical shape of πόδας ὠκὺς Ἀχιλλεύς ([[swift-footed]]) and fills the same place in the line. So the speech formulas that introduce Achilles in the assembly introduce Zeus on Olympus. Compare τὸν δ’ ἀπαμειβόμενος προσέφη πόδας ὠκὺς Ἀχιλλεύς ({{ref:Iliad 1.84}}) with Zeus' answer to Hera:

{{quote:f0c21c6f-63c7-58a8-bfe1-b30fc23d6bd6}}

The divine scene is built from the same parts as the human one ([[answering-him]]).

The first occurrence turns the formula against itself:

{{quote:efe6afba-f0a8-55bd-917c-c018bdbe5f2f}}

The words τὴν δ’ … προσέφη lead the listener to expect a speech, and the negative οὔ τι cancels it: “the cloud-gatherer said nothing to her.” The silence is the most dramatic moment of the supplication ([[supplication-type-scene]]). Thetis must cling to his knees and ask a second time before Zeus replies, and his final answer is a nod that shakes Olympus ({{ref:Iliad 1.528-530}}).

The epithet names the god of storms, but in Book 1 Zeus raises no storm. His power shows in a movement of his brows ([[dark-brows]]), and the thunder is left to his epithets.
