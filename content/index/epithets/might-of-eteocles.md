## Meaning

βίη (“force, might”) + Ἐτεοκλήειος (“of Eteocles,” an adjective formed from the name): “the Eteoclean might,” that is, mighty Eteocles. It occurs only here in Homer. The periphrasis is of the kind used for Heracles, βίη Ἡρακληείη ([[might-of-heracles]]), for Iphiclus in the Odyssey (βίη Ἰφικληείη, Od. 11.290), and in another form for Priam, Πριάμοιο βίη ([[might-of-priam]]).

## Use

{{quote:fd0cb92d-4c10-5b20-9600-cb8a389a9e77}}

Tydeus comes to Thebes as an envoy and finds the Cadmeians “feasting in the house of mighty Eteocles” (4.385–386). The grand periphrasis names the host of the feast at which Tydeus, a stranger and alone, challenges the Thebans to contests and beats them all ([[eteocles]], [[tydeus-at-thebes]], [[contests]]).
