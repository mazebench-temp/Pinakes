## Meaning

πιδήεις is formed from πῖδαξ (“spring,” as in the little spring over which a lion and a boar fight, 16.823–825) with the suffix -ήεις, “full of”: “rich in springs.” It occurs only here. Ida's usual epithet has the same sense: πολυπῖδαξ, “of the many springs” ([[of-the-many-springs]]), eight times in the Iliad, three of them in the verse-end πολυπίδακα μητέρα θηρῶν, “Ida of the many springs, mother of wild beasts” (8.47; [[mother-of-wild-beasts]]). The rivers of the Troad flow “from the mountains of Ida” to the sea (ἀπʼ Ἰδαίων ὀρέων, 12.19–22), the Scamander and the Simoeis among them.

## Use

{{quote:c6c1ae0c-17d6-5c7d-9f54-56662c199210}}

Agamemnon has driven the Trojans to the Scaean gates (11.163–180) when Zeus comes down from heaven and sits on the peaks of Ida with the lightning in his hands. From there he sends Iris to Hector with the message that makes Agamemnon's wounding the turn of the day (11.186–194; [[message-repeated]], [[plan-of-zeus]]). He watched the battle from the peaks of Ida in Book 8 as well (8.47–52), and from Ida he stretches this battle evenly (11.336–337; [[watching-the-war]]). See [[ida]], [[zeus]] and [[iris]].
