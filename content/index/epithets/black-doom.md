## Meaning

κήρ (“doom, the fate of death”; see [[ker-doom]]) + μέλαινα (“black”). Death is black in Homer: “the fates of black death” lead the sons of Merops to war ({{ref:Iliad 2.834}}). The accusative κῆρα μέλαιναν closes the verse, most often after a verb of escaping or warding off: black doom is what a man avoids, if he can. The adjective is the same as in [[black-ships]] and [[black-earth]].

## Use

The Trojan Catalogue uses the formula of the augur Ennomus: “his birds did not save him from black doom” ({{ref:Iliad 2.859}}). In Book 3 it belongs to Paris, who escapes death twice and is compared to it once. At the sight of Menelaus he draws back into the crowd, “avoiding doom” (κῆρʼ ἀλεείνων, 3.32). The same verse ends Ajax's throw at Hector in Book 7 (Il. 7.254). At the end of Book 3, when Menelaus searches the field for him, none of the Trojans would have hidden Paris, “for he was hateful to them all, like black doom” (3.454). The man who has twice escaped death is as hateful as death to his own people ([[like-black-doom]]).
