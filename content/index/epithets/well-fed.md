## Meaning

ζατρεφής = ζα- (an intensive prefix, “very”) + the root of τρέφω (“rear, feed, fatten”): “well-fed, fattened.” Homer uses it four times, always of animals: the bulls of Ajax's shield (7.223), and in the Odyssey the seals that Proteus counts on the shore of Pharos, with Menelaus and his men hidden among them (Od. 4.450–453), the boars of Eumaeus, of which he must send the best to the suitors every day (Od. 14.19), and the goats of which a goatherd does the same (Od. 14.106). The animals are the best of their kind, and in the Odyssey they are the wealth that the suitors consume. See [[bulls]] and [[cattle]].

## Use

{{quote:0ca0ab0a-cc2d-50d1-9f9d-b507ed33c3f0}}

The hides of the shield come from well-fed bulls, animals in their prime, and Tychius layered seven of them under the bronze. It is these layers that stop Hector's spear (7.245–248; [[seven-oxhide]], [[tychius]]).
