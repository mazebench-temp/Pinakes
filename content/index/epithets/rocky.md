## Meaning

πετρήεις = πέτρη (“rock”) + the suffix -ήεις (“full of”): “rocky.” Homer uses it five times, three of them in the Catalogue. Pytho, the sanctuary of Apollo at Delphi under the cliffs of Parnassus, is rocky again in Book 9, where Achilles speaks of the treasure behind its stone threshold (Il. 9.405). In the Odyssey it describes the island Asteris, where the suitors lie in wait for Telemachus (Od. 4.844).

The Catalogue has another word that the translation also renders “rocky”: Ithome in Thessaly is κλωμακόεσσα ({{ref:Iliad 2.729}}), “full of stony steps,” a word found only there.

## Use

Each of the Catalogue's three rocky places is named in a list, and the epithet gives each a single feature. Aulis, from which the fleet sailed ({{ref:Iliad 2.303-304}}), opens the list of Boeotian towns ({{ref:Iliad 2.496}}); Calydon closes the Aetolian list ({{ref:Iliad 2.640}}).
