## Meaning

πρωτόγονος = πρῶτος (“first”) + the root of γίγνομαι (“be born”): “firstborn,” the first young of the flock, the choicest offering. In Homer it occurs only in one verse, the vow of an archer to Apollo: ἀρνῶν πρωτογόνων ῥέξειν κλειτὴν ἑκατόμβην, a vow to offer “a glorious hecatomb of firstborn lambs” (4.102, 4.120; Il. 23.864, 23.873). See [[lambs]], [[hecatomb]], [[famous]].

## Use

{{quote:62046cc7-8f34-5a58-853b-51bbda20bdcd}}

Athena tells Pandarus to vow the firstborn lambs when he prays to Apollo, and he makes the vow as he draws (4.119–121; [[famed-for-his-bow]], [[lycian-born]]). In the archery contest of the funeral games the same verse separates two archers: Teucer shoots without the vow and misses the bird, and Meriones makes it and hits (Il. 23.859–883). Pandarus never comes home to Zeleia to pay his vow ([[pandarus]]).
