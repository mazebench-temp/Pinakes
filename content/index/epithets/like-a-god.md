## Meaning

δαίμονι, dative of δαίμων (“divine power, god”; [[daimon]]), + ἶσος (“equal”): “equal to a god.” The formula closes the verse, usually after ἐπέσσυτο, “he rushed on.” Homer gives it to three men: Diomedes (5.438, 5.459, 5.884), Patroclus (Il. 16.705, 16.786) and Achilles (Il. 20.447, 20.493, 21.18, 21.227). It is not the praise of beauty or strength in [[godlike]] and [[antitheos]]. It describes a man whose onslaught goes beyond human measure, and four times it stands in the verse “but when for the fourth time he rushed on like a god,” after which a god turns the hero back or strikes him down (5.438; Il. 16.705, 16.786, 20.447; see [[three-times-and-a-fourth]]).

## Use

{{quote:c6f743f1-bf92-5222-9e94-25279a5e608e}}

Diomedes knows that Apollo is protecting Aeneas and attacks all the same. At the fourth assault Apollo shouts at him and draws the line the formula has crossed: never alike is the race of gods and of men ([[far-worker]]). The phrase then passes to the gods. Apollo uses it when he asks Ares to drag Diomedes out of the battle: “First he came close to Cypris and wounded her hand at the wrist, and then he rushed at me myself, like a god” (5.458–459). Ares repeats the two verses to Zeus, when he has himself been wounded (5.883–884). What the narrator said of the hero has become the gods' complaint against him ([[diomedes]], [[gods-wounded-by-mortals]]).
