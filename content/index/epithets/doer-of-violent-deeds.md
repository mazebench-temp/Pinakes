## Meaning

ὀβριμοεργός = ὄβριμος (“heavy, mighty”; [[obrimos]]) + ἔργον (“deed, work”): “doer of mighty deeds,” and in its two uses “of violent deeds.” Both are in reproach. Dione uses it of Heracles, and Priam, begging the Trojans to let him go alone to the Achaean ships, calls Achilles “this reckless man, doer of violent deeds” (Il. 22.418).

## Use

{{quote:3f215c34-dcb1-5b22-a7e3-b8c18354bcb9}}

Dione's second and third examples of gods who have suffered at mortal hands are both the work of one man, the son of Amphitryon and of Zeus. He shot Hera in the right breast with a three-barbed arrow, and he shot Hades, who went up to Olympus to be healed by Paeëon ([[heracles-wounds-hera-and-hades]], [[paeeon]], [[pain-killing]]). Then Dione passes judgment on him: σχέτλιος (“hard, cruel”), a doer of violent deeds, “who cared nothing for the evil he did, who with his bow brought sorrow on the gods who hold Olympus” (5.403–404; [[heracles]]). The line that follows turns the judgment on Diomedes, whom Athena has set on Aphrodite (5.405): the mortal who wounds a god does not live long (5.406–409; [[mythological-paradigm]]).
