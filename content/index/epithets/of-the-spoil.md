## Meaning

ληΐτις is formed from ληΐς (“booty, plunder,” above all cattle and goods taken in a raid; [[raiding]]) with the feminine suffix -ιτις: “she of the spoil.” Homer uses it only once, at 10.460. Athena has a related title in the Iliad and the Odyssey, ἀγελείη, which the translation renders “driver of spoil” ([[driver-of-spoil]]). Both make her the goddess to whom the winner of spoil owes thanks. The title comes as Odysseus lifts the cap, the wolfskin, the bow and the spear of the dead Dolon to her, and in his prayer he calls her first of all the immortals on Olympus (10.462–463).

## Use

{{quote:1e661555-4f48-54ad-92fb-d7ede870b515}}

Odysseus' prayer pays for what has been won and asks for more: “once again guide us to the horses and the beds of the Thracian men” ([[prayer]], [[stripping-the-dead]]). He sets the spoils on a tamarisk and marks the place (10.465–468; [[swift-night]]). The prayer is answered: the two come soon to the company of the Thracian men, and Athena breathes might into Diomedes (10.469–470, 10.482; [[athena]]). On the way back Diomedes takes the spoils up again, and at the ships Odysseus lays them in the stern of the ship “until they could make ready an offering to Athena” (10.527–529, 10.570–571; [[bloody-spoils]], [[dedication-of-spoils]]). The offering itself is not told. See [[odysseus]] and [[dolon]].
