## Meaning

πολύαινος = πολύς (“much”) + αἶνος. The second word means both “praise” and “tale, story” (often a story with a point), so the compound is “much-praised” or “of many tales,” that is, a man much talked of, or a man of many words and stratagems. The translation renders it “much-praised.” Homer uses it only of Odysseus and only in address, four times: in the same verse, by Agamemnon when the envoys return from Achilles (9.673) and by Nestor when Odysseus and Diomedes return from the night raid (10.543–544); by the Trojan Socus, who wounds him and calls him “much-praised Odysseus, insatiable of tricks and toil” (11.430); and by the Sirens, who greet him with the whole verse of Agamemnon's praise (Od. 12.184). Compare the epithets of Odysseus' intelligence, [[of-many-devices]], [[of-many-wiles]], [[schemer]], and of his endurance, [[much-enduring]].

## Use

{{quote:feb57755-2cd4-5809-a9d4-93a016cd9ebb}}

When the envoys come back, the sons of the Achaeans pledge them with golden cups, and Agamemnon asks Odysseus first: “Come, tell me, much-praised Odysseus, great glory of the Achaeans” ([[great-glory-of-the-achaeans]]). The king addresses the envoy with full honors before he hears that his gifts have been refused. The two returns of the night are told alike: “and first Agamemnon, lord of men, asked” (9.672) and “first the Gerenian horseman Nestor asked” (10.543), and the whole verse greets Odysseus again when he brings back the horses of Rhesus (10.544). The Sirens use it to tempt him (Od. 12.184). See [[odysseus]] and [[the-embassy-to-achilles]].
