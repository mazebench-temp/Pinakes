## Meaning

χαλκοκορυστής = χαλκός (“bronze”; [[bronze]]) + a noun from κορύσσω (“arm, equip,” from κόρυς, “helmet”): “armed in bronze.” Homer uses it nine times, all in the Iliad: eight times of Hector (5.699; Il. 6.398, 13.720, 15.221, 15.458, 16.358, 16.536, 16.654), usually in Ἕκτορι χαλκοκορυστῇ at the end of the verse, and once of Sarpedon at his birth, “godlike Sarpedon, armed in bronze” (Il. 6.199). Compare the other epithets of bronze armor: [[bronze-armored]] (χαλκοχίτωνες), [[armored-in-bronze]] (χαλκεοθώρηξ), and κεκορυθμένος αἴθοπι χαλκῷ, “armored in gleaming bronze” ([[gleaming-bronze]]).

## Use

{{quote:5114d78d-73dd-52b3-89e6-99cc75a0fa4b}}

The man and the god advance together, and both are named by their bronze: Hector “armed in bronze” and, five lines later, “bronze Ares” ([[bronze-ares]]). The Argives neither run nor charge; they give ground slowly, facing the enemy, as Diomedes told them to do when he saw the god beside Hector (5.605–606). The narrator's question follows, “whom first, whom last did they slay,” and a list of six men killed by the two together (5.703–710; [[androktasia]], [[hector]]).
