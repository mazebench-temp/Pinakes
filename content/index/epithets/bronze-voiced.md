## Meaning

χαλκεόφωνος = χάλκεος (“of bronze”; [[bronze]]) + φωνή (“voice”): “with a voice of bronze,” loud and unbreakable as metal. The word occurs only here. Bronze is Homer's image for a voice that does not fail. Invoking the Muses before the Catalogue, the poet says that he could not name the multitude even with ten tongues and ten mouths, “a voice that could not break, and a heart of bronze within me” (2.489–490; [[ten-tongues]]), and when Achilles shouts from the trench the Trojans hear “the bronze voice” of the grandson of Aeacus (Il. 18.222).

## Use

{{quote:b13e1d57-f506-5a7b-99ad-1d303bca14d0}}

Hera takes the likeness of a man whose voice is his whole description: Stentor appears only here in Homer ([[stentor]]). His shout is measured in men, as Ares' bellow is measured in thousands (5.859–861; [[ten-thousand-men]]). The name has passed into English in “stentorian.”
