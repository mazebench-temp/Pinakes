## Meaning

σῶκος occurs once in Homer, in the pairing of the gods for battle: “against Leto stood strong Hermes, the great helper” (20.72; [[great-helper]], [[leto]]). As a name it belongs to the Trojan [[socus]] whom Odysseus kills (Σῶκος, 11.428), and the exclusion keeps that man out. Its sense is a guess. Walter Leaf reports that the rare verb σωκεῖν, which the tragedians use, seems to mean “be strong,” and adds that this is uncertain (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 20.72; see the translation's note on 20.72).

The same verse has the contracted name Ἑρμῆς, which Leaf notes is otherwise found only in the Odyssey, four times; it is there in the forms Ἑρμῆς, Ἑρμῆν and Ἑρμῇ (*Odyssey* 5.54, 8.334, 14.435, 24.1). Everywhere else in the Iliad the god is Ἑρμείας. See [[hermes]].
