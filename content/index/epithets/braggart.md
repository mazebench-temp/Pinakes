## Meaning

βουγάϊος is of uncertain sense. It is explained as βου-, “big” (from βοῦς, “ox”), and γαίω, “exult, pride oneself”: a big boaster, a braggart; or as a “big ox,” a lout (see the translation's note on 13.824). Homer uses it twice, both times in the vocative, of a man whose words, in the speaker's view, are bigger than he is. Hector calls Ajax so (13.824), and in the Odyssey Antinous turns on the beggar Irus when he trembles before the old man he has threatened: may you never have been born, βουγάϊε, if you tremble so at this old man (Od. 18.79–81).

## Use

“Ajax, blundering in speech, you braggart, what a thing you have said!” (13.824; [[ajax]], [[hector]], [[blundering-in-speech]]). The two insults open Hector's answer to Ajax's prophecy of his flight (13.810–820), and the speech that follows is itself a boast: Ajax, if he dares to stand up to Hector's long spear, will be killed among the Achaeans and glut the dogs and birds of the Trojans (13.828–832).
