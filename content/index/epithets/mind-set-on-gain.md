## Meaning

κερδαλεόφρων = κερδαλέος (“gainful, crafty,” from κέρδος, “gain, profit, advantage”) + φρήν (“mind”). κέρδος in Homer means both material profit and the cleverness that secures an advantage, so κερδαλεόφρων can mean “greedy” or “crafty.” In Book 4 Agamemnon uses the same word of Odysseus, together with “skilled in evil tricks” (Il. 4.339); there the sense is “cunning.”

## Use

{{quote:58cc5ecc-f147-564f-b1b0-dda005cc456d}}

Coming after [[most-covetous]] (φιλοκτεανώτατε) and joined here to [[clothed-in-shamelessness]], the word leans toward greed: Agamemnon thinks only of his profit. The other sense is also in play. Agamemnon has just accused Achilles of trying to trick him (κλέπτε νόῳ, {{ref:Iliad 1.132}}), and Achilles turns the charge of calculation back on him.
