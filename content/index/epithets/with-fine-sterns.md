## Meaning

εὔπρυμνος = εὖ (“well”) + πρύμνη (“stern”): “with a fine stern.” The word is found only here in the Homeric poems (4.248). The ships at Troy were drawn up on the beach with their sterns toward the land, and in the fight at the ships the sterns are where the enemy comes: Hector takes hold of the stern of Protesilaus' ship and calls for fire (Il. 15.704–718). The epithet joins the many that Homer gives the ships: [[black-ships]], [[hollow-ships]], [[swift-ships]], [[curved-ships]], [[balanced-ships]], [[well-benched]].

## Use

{{quote:9c4a72cb-b02a-54fb-b64e-615ea9e0f48b}}

Agamemnon's rebuke ends with a picture of the worst that could happen: the Trojans at the ships, and the army waiting to see whether Zeus will hold his hand over it ([[son-of-cronus]], [[gray-sea]]). The picture comes true in the middle books of the poem, when Hector reaches the ships and the first of them is set on fire (Il. 16.112–124).
