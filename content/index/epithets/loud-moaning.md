## Meaning

ἀγάστονος = ἀγα- (“very,” as in ἀγακλυτός, “very famous”; [[famous]]) + the root of στένω, στόνος (“groan, moan”): “loudly moaning,” the sound of the sea. Homer has it once, in Circe's account of Scylla's fishing: “if anywhere she may catch a greater / sea monster, of which loud-moaning Amphitrite feeds countless numbers” (*Odyssey* 12.96–97; see the translation's key words, ἀγάστονος). Amphitrite has another epithet found only in the same book, [[dark-eyed]] (κυανῶπις, *Odyssey* 12.60); compare the sea's [[loud-roaring-sea]] (πολύφλοισβος). See [[amphitrite]] and [[scylla]].

## Outside Homer

In the *Hymn to Apollo* the goddesses who are with Leto on Delos through her nine days of labor are “Dione and Rhea, / Ichnaean Themis and loud-moaning Amphitrite” (*Hymn to Apollo* 93–94, a plain rendering).
