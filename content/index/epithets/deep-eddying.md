## Meaning

βαθύς (“deep”) + δίνη (“eddy, whirlpool”): “with deep eddies.” The longer βαθυδινήεις is formed like [[eddying]] (δινήεις). The Iliad has the word only in the battle of the gods and the battle of the river. It is first the Scamander, who stands against Hephaestus as “the great deep-eddying river, / whom the gods call Xanthus, and men Scamander” (20.73–74; [[language-of-gods-and-men]]), and who fights Achilles in Book 21 (21.15, 21.212, 21.228, 21.329, 21.603); once it is the Axius, who lay with Periboea and fathered Pelegon, Asteropaeus' father (21.141–143). In the Odyssey it is Oceanus, at whose bank Odysseus is to beach his ship before he goes down to the dank house of Hades (*Odyssey* 10.508–512; [[dank]]). See [[scamander]], [[rivers]] and [[oceanus]].

## Outside Homer

Hesiod gives the epithet to Oceanus, “deep-eddying Oceanus,” whom Earth bore after lying with Heaven (*Theogony* 133), and beside whom the heroes live in the Isles of the Blessed (*Works and Days* 171), and to the Eridanus (*Theogony* 338). The Hymn to Hermes gives it to the Alpheus (*Homeric Hymn to Hermes* 139), and the first Hymn to Dionysus has the longer form of the same river (*Homeric Hymn* 1.3).
