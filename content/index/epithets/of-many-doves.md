## Meaning

πολυτρήρων = πολύς (“many”) + τρήρων (“timid; a dove”). τρήρων, from the root of τρέω (“tremble, flee in fear”), is “the timid one”: Homer uses it as an adjective of doves (τρήρωνα πέλειαν, “a timid dove”) and as a noun for the bird itself. The compound means a place where many doves nest, probably among cliffs.

It occurs only twice in Homer, and both times in the same position in the verse: πολυτρήρωνά τε Θίσβην ({{ref:Iliad 2.502}}) and πολυτρήρωνά τε Μέσσην ({{ref:Iliad 2.582}}).

## Use

{{quote:52a9b1ca-0180-58c2-867f-21c1e0a710f2}}

The two uses are eighty lines apart, one in Boeotia and one in Laconia. The repeated half-line shows the Catalogue's method: a stock of place epithets that can be fitted to any town whose name has the right shape.
