## Meaning

δημοβόρος = δῆμος (“the people, the community,” and the common land and goods that belong to it) + -βόρος (“devouring,” from the root of βιβρώσκω, “eat,” and βορά, “food”): “who eats up the people's substance.” The word is found only here in Homer. βασιλεύς is the king ([[basileus]]).

## Use

{{quote:2384adb6-bc16-5f50-a692-7e283a0ae955}}

The insult sums up the argument of the speech. Agamemnon strips the gifts from anyone who speaks against him, and only a people of nobodies (οὐτιδανοῖσιν) would put up with such a king; otherwise this outrage would be his last. The phrase stands at the head of the verse without a verb, an exclamation.

It is the sharpest of Achilles' charges because it attacks the king's function. A king should protect his people, as the image of the [[shepherd-of-the-people]] (ποιμὴν λαῶν) implies. Achilles says that this one devours them.

## Outside Homer

Hesiod makes the same complaint against the kings of his own day, whom he calls δωροφάγοι, “gift-devouring” (*Works and Days* 39, 264).
