## Meaning

ἅλς (“the sea, salt water”) + the root of μύρομαι (“weep,” properly of flowing tears) and the suffix -ήεις: “flowing, murmuring into the sea.” Walter Leaf notes that the word occurs only here and in the Odyssey, where it has a more special and fitting sense: there it is the mouth of a river, where it murmurs against the brine (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 21.190). In the Odyssey it is the river of Scheria, at whose mouth Odysseus comes ashore and into which he throws back Ino's veil (*Odyssey* 5.459–460).

## Use

{{quote:aa74292d-384d-5f9f-b604-97d143f1b828}}

Achilles' boast over the dead grandson of a river sets all rivers below Zeus, and their descendants below his (21.184–199; [[zeus-is-mightier-than-rivers]], [[broad-flowing]]). τώ, “So,” may also be taken as a relative, “by so much,” and Leaf discusses both readings (on 21.190; see the translation's note on 21.184–191).
