## Meaning

πᾶν (“all”) + the root ὀπ- of seeing (ὄψομαι, “I shall see,” ὄψις, “sight”): “seen by all, in full sight.” Walter Leaf calls it a strange word, apparently meaning “visible to all,” in contrast with the goddess who was unseen; Antimachus read ὑπονόσφιον, “stealthy” or “secretly,” and the variant has been found in a papyrus; Bentley conjectured πανίψιον (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 21.397; see the translation's note on 21.397).

## Use

{{quote:22c2d59a-76a5-5375-9b6b-a6a5efe4bff4}}

Ares recalls *Iliad* 5. There Athena put on the helmet of Hades “so that mighty Ares should not see her” (5.845), and when Diomedes thrust with his spear, “Pallas Athena drove it home” into Ares' flank (5.856–857; [[helmet-of-hades]], [[diomedes-wounds-ares]]). The spear was in full sight, and the hand that drove it was not: the epithet carries Ares' grievance. See [[ares]], [[athena]] and [[spear]].
