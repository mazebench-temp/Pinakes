## Meaning

κούρη (“girl, daughter”) + Ἰκαρίοιο, the genitive of Ἰκάριος: “the daughter of Icarius” ([[icarius]]). The formula names Penelope by the house she was born in. It always stands at the head of the verse, and her own epithet and name most often follow it: κούρη Ἰκαρίοιο, περίφρων Πηνελόπεια ([[periphron]]). The verse changes its case with her part in the sentence, and keeps its shape.

## Use

It is used thirteen times, all in the Odyssey. The whole verse is in the nominative four times (*Odyssey* 1.329, 11.446, 19.375, 20.388), in the vocative four times, as the address to her (κούρη Ἰκαρίοιο, περίφρον Πηνελόπεια, *Odyssey* 16.435, 18.245, 18.285, 21.321), and in the dative three times (κούρῃ Ἰκαρίοιο, περίφρονι Πηνελοπείῃ, *Odyssey* 17.562, 18.159, 21.2). Twice the phrase stands without her name, and something else completes the verse (*Odyssey* 4.840, 18.188).
