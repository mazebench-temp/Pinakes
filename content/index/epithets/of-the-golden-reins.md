## Meaning

χρυσήνιος = χρυσός (“gold”) + ἡνία (“reins”): “with reins of gold.” The gods drive chariots with golden gear: Hera's horses wear golden headbands (5.720; [[with-golden-headbands]]), and Zeus drives a pair with golden manes and wears gold himself (Il. 8.41–44). Homer uses the word twice: of Artemis here, and of Ares, who “kept no blind watch” and saw Hephaestus leave his house (Od. 8.285). Artemis' standing epithet is [[of-the-showering-arrows]] (ἰοχέαιρα), and the Odyssey also calls her “of the golden throne” (χρυσόθρονος, Od. 5.123).

## Use

Glaucus ends the story of his grandfather Bellerophon with the gods' hatred and the deaths of two of his three children:

{{quote:296fd6e7-87eb-5117-9a97-cecc0cf42708}}

The daughter is Laodameia, whom Zeus loved and who bore him Sarpedon (6.198–199; [[laodameia]], [[sarpedon]]). Why Artemis was angry with her Glaucus does not say, as he does not say why Bellerophon came to be hated by all the gods (6.200). In Homer the sudden death of women is Artemis' work, done with her arrows: in the same book she strikes down Andromache's mother in her father's halls (6.428; [[of-the-showering-arrows]]). Here the golden reins, not the bow, give her her title ([[artemis]], [[bellerophon]]).
