## Meaning

ἄσπετος = ἀ- (“not”) + the root of ἐννέπω and ἔσπετε (“tell”): “not to be told,” “beyond words.” It describes size or amount: a “vast forest” in flames (ἄσπετον ὕλην, {{ref:Iliad 2.455}}), the boundless upper air that breaks open (ἄσπετος αἰθήρ, Il. 8.558), and glory. A word of the same kind opens Book 3: the cranes flee “the winter and its endless rain” (ἀθέσφατον ὄμβρον, 3.4), rain beyond even a god's telling.

## Use

{{quote:eb7e0e9c-9189-59c5-8946-0c6e893b428c}}

Menelaus has Paris by the helmet strap, and the glory the duel was to decide is in his hands. The goddess takes it away in a line. The same verse describes Hector dragging the body of Patroclus in Book 18, until Iris comes from Olympus (Il. 18.165). See [[kydos]], [[divine-rescue]].
