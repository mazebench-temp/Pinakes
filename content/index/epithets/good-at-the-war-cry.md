## Meaning

βοήν (“shout, battle cry,” an accusative of respect) + ἀγαθός (“good”): “good at the war cry.” The construction is the same as in πόδας ὠκύς ([[swift-footed]]). βοή can also mean the din of battle itself, so the phrase may once have meant simply “good in battle.” A commander's voice matters in the Iliad: Achilles' shout alone, from the trench, routs the Trojans in Book 18.

The formula βοὴν ἀγαθὸς Μενέλαος / Διομήδης closes the verse. It occurs fifty-one times in Homer, nearly always of these two men; it is given now and then to Ajax, to Hector, and once to a son of Priam, Polites (Il. 24.250).

The war cry is the formula's content; its occasion here is a brother's understanding. In the Catalogue the formula marks both men as leaders. Diomedes commands the men of Argos and Tiryns ({{ref:Iliad 2.563}}, {{ref:Iliad 2.567}}), and Menelaus the Lacedaemonians, who arm apart from the rest ({{ref:Iliad 2.586-587}}).
