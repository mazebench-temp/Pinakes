## Meaning

ἀμβρόσιος is the adjective of ἀμβροσίη, the gods' food ([[ambrosia]]). Both come from ἄμβροτος, “immortal”: ἀ- (“not”) + a form of βροτός (“mortal”), from the same root as Latin *mors* (“death”). Sanskrit has the same compound, *amṛ́ta-*, “immortal,” also the name of the drink of the gods. So ἀμβρόσιος means “immortal, belonging to the gods,” and hence “divinely fine.”

Homer gives it to the gods' own things: Zeus' hair ([[ambrosial-locks]], {{ref:Iliad 1.529}}), Hera's robe, the sandals of Hermes. It is also a standing epithet of night, νὺξ ἀμβροσίη, and once of sleep. Night and sleep are ambrosial because they come from the gods and bring the rest that restores. The same idea makes sleep “sweet” ([[sweet-sleep]]) and “honey-sweet” ([[honey-sweet]]).
