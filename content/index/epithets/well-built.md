## Meaning

ἐϋκτίμενος = εὖ (“well”) + κτίμενος, a participle of κτίζω (“found, build, settle”): “well-founded.” It describes a place that has been built and settled well: a city, an island such as Lemnos or Lesbos, a house. ἐΰκτιτος, from the same root with the adjective suffix -τος, has the same meaning and occurs only once in Homer, of the Messenian town Aepy ({{ref:Iliad 2.592}}).

The formula ἐϋκτίμενον πτολίεθρον fills the second half of the verse. Homer uses it of Troy (Il. 4.33, 21.433), of Pylos (Od. 3.4), and above all in the Catalogue. The translation renders another word “well-built” too: the hall in which Tlepolemus grew up is εὔπηκτος, “well-joined” ({{ref:Iliad 2.661}}).

## Use

In the Catalogue of Ships the formula names four cities in the same position at the end of the verse: Medeon ({{ref:Iliad 2.501}}) and Hypothebae ({{ref:Iliad 2.505}}) in Boeotia, Athens ({{ref:Iliad 2.546}}), and Mycenae:

Other cases fit elsewhere in the line: “well-built Cleonae” in the next verse, and “well-built Iolcus” in Thessaly ({{ref:Iliad 2.712}}). The epithet is general praise and says nothing particular about any of these places. It gives the list a rhythm of repeated endings, like the formulas that count the ships ([[catalogue-of-ships]]).
