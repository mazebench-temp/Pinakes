## Meaning

αἱματόεις = αἷμα (“blood”) + the suffix -όεις (“full of”): “covered with blood, bloody.” It describes what blood has stained: the welt that Odysseus' scepter raises on Thersites' back (2.267), the arm that Eurypylus cuts from Hypsenor (5.82), the dust in which dying men clutch at the ground (13.393 = 16.486), and armor stripped from the dead, as Menelaus strips Peisander's (13.640) and Achilles Hector's (22.368–369). From there it passes to war: πόλεμος αἱματόεις, “bloody war” (9.650; 19.313), and to time, “bloody days” (9.326). The phrase βρότον αἱματόεντα, which the translation renders “the clotted blood” (7.425), belongs to [[clotted-blood]]. Compare [[blood-stained]] (μιαιφόνος, of Ares) and [[bloody-spoils]] (ἔναρα βροτόεντα).

## Use

{{quote:93a75901-ffcf-5360-a6e7-858d43d11309}}

Achilles' account of his service sets the days against the nights: “many sleepless nights,” and “bloody days” spent fighting with men for the sake of their wives (9.325–327; [[sleepless]]). At the end of his last answer, to Ajax, he names war itself by the same word: he will not think of “bloody war” until Hector reaches the ships of the Myrmidons (9.650–653). The epithet runs through both refusals, of service in the past and of fighting now. See [[achilles]] and [[polemos]].
