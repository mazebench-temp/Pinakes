## Meaning

φιλοπτόλεμος = φίλος (“loving”) + πτόλεμος, the old form of πόλεμος (“war”). Homer uses it only of peoples, in the plural and beside their name: in the Iliad of the Myrmidons (16.65, 23.129), the Trojans (16.90, 16.835, 17.194), the Achaeans (17.224), the Argives (19.269), the Danaans (20.351), the Leleges (21.86), and the companions of Achilles (23.5). The love of war is not a reproach; it is praise of a fighting people, and Trojans and Achaeans have it alike. Compare [[warlike]] (ἀρήϊος) and [[dear-to-ares]] (ἀρηΐφιλος).
