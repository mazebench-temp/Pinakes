## Meaning

τηλύγετος has been explained as τῆλε (“far”) + γίγνομαι (“be born”): “born late,” far on in the parents' life, and so especially loved. Others take it simply as “darling, tender,” or “only.” The sense is disputed. Homer uses it of children on whom a house's hope rests: Orestes, Agamemnon's son at home (Il. 9.143, 9.285); the only son of a rich father, to whom Phoenix compares the love Peleus showed him (Il. 9.481–482); and Menelaus' son Megapenthes (Od. 4.11).

## Use

{{quote:151715da-6d6d-5e1c-bb19-a172e05f5179}}

Helen wishes she had died before following Paris, leaving her bridal chamber, her kinsmen, her daughter and the friends of her youth ([[lovely]]). The daughter is not named in the Iliad. The Odyssey names her Hermione and says she was Helen's only child (Od. 4.12–14).
