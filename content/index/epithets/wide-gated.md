## Meaning

εὐρύς (“wide”) + πύλη (“gate”): “with wide gates.” Homer's Hades has gates: the soul of Patroclus asks for burial “so that I may pass the gates of Hades” (23.71), and Achilles hates like the gates of Hades the man who hides one thing in his mind and says another (9.312–313). Walter Leaf notes that εὐρυπυλές is an irregular form for εὐρύπυλον (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 23.74).

## Use

{{quote:41fc21c1-e808-56f9-8b14-ef841ce970ad}}

Leaf finds it hard to see how a soul that has not passed the gates can wander “through the wide-gated house of Hades,” and takes the phrase, here and in the Odyssey, to mean only the threshold of Hades' realm (on 23.74; see the translation's note on 23.71–74). In the Odyssey the souls that Odysseus sees sit and stand κατʼ εὐρυπυλὲς Ἄϊδος δῶ (*Odyssey* 11.571). See [[hades]], [[psyche]] and [[the-ghost-of-patroclus]].
