## Meaning

πολύς, “much, many,” + φήμη, “speech, voice, a word of omen,” from φημί, “say”: “of many voices.” The same word is the name of the Cyclops, Πολύφημος, and of a Lapith of Nestor's youth (*Odyssey* 1.70; *Iliad* 1.264; [[polyphemus-the-cyclops]]); and the singer Phemius has a name from φήμη ([[phemius]]).

## Use

In the assembly of *Odyssey* 2 the eagles of Zeus wheel over “the middle of the assembly of many voices” (*Odyssey* 2.150). In Book 22 Odysseus tells the herald Medon to go out of the hall into the courtyard, “you and the singer of many voices,” while he finishes his work (*Odyssey* 22.375–377), where the epithet may also be rendered “of many songs” ([[phemius]]).
