## Meaning

ῥινός (“hide,” and so a shield of hide) + the root τορ- (“pierce,” as in ἔτορε, “pierced,” 11.236, and ἀντετόρησεν, 5.337): “piercer of hides.” ῥινοί are the oxhide shields of the armies' clash, “they dashed together their shields of hide” (σύν ῥʼ ἔβαλον ῥινούς, 4.447; [[the-armies-clash]], [[shield]]). The translation renders the epithet “the piercer of shields.”

## Use

{{quote:f465ea2e-f951-5a2f-ac3c-ad332be9e756}}

The epithet stands at the head of the verse, in enjambment, at the moment Ares begins the gods' fight (21.391–392). The piercer of shields strikes the one shield he cannot pierce: his spear hits the aegis, “which not even the thunderbolt of Zeus overpowers” (21.400–401; [[tasseled]], [[aegis]]), and Athena fells him with a stone (21.403–406). See [[ares]], [[athena]] and [[the-battle-of-the-gods]].

## Outside Homer

Hesiod gives Ares the same epithet in the Theogony: to Ares, piercer of shields, Cytherea bore Phobos and Deimos, who drive the close ranks of men in confusion in chilling war with Ares, sacker of cities, and Harmonia (*Theogony* 933–937; [[rout]], [[terror]], [[sacker-of-cities]]).
