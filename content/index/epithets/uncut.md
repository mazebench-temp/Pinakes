## Meaning

ἄξυλος = ἀ- (“not”) + ξύλον (“wood, timber”). As an adjective it occurs only here, of ὕλη (“woodland”), and is usually explained as woodland in which no timber has been cut, so that it stands dense and full of fuel; the translation renders it “thick, uncut woodland.” The same word is the name of Axylus, son of Teuthras, a rich and hospitable man of Arisbe whom Diomedes kills (6.12–19; [[axylus]]). Compare [[wooded]] (ὑλήεις), and [[aidelos]], the “destroying” fire of the same verse.

## Use

{{quote:028f7b73-87f4-5406-9f30-af5047fe53ee}}

The simile pictures Agamemnon's pursuit after he has killed the sons of Antimachus: the whirling wind carries the fire everywhere, and the thickets fall uprooted (11.155–157), as the heads of the fleeing Trojans fall before him, and their horses rattle empty chariots along the bridges of war (11.158–162). A wood that has never been cut feeds the fire. See [[forest-fire]], [[agamemnon]] and [[the-aristeia-of-agamemnon]].
