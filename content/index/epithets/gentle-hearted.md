## Meaning

ἀγανός (“gentle, mild”; [[gentle-words]]) + φρήν (“mind”; [[phrenes]]): “gentle of mind.” Homer has the adjective once, beside [[sweet-tempered]] (γλυκύθυμος), as Achilles kills Tros, son of Alastor, who is clasping his knees: “for he was no sweet-tempered man, nor gentle-hearted, / but in a great fury” (20.467–468; [[supplication]], [[achilles]]). The noun ἀγανοφροσύνη, “gentleness,” comes twice. Helen, lamenting Hector, remembers how he held back those who reproached her “with your gentleness and your gentle words” (24.771–772; [[helen]], [[hector]]), and in the Odyssey Anticleia tells her son that longing for him and for his gentleness took her life (*Odyssey* 11.202–203).
