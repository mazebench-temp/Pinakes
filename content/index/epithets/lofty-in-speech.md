## Meaning

ὑψαγόρης = ὕψι, “high, aloft,” + ἀγορεύω, “speak,” the verb of the assembly (ἀγορή; [[assembly]]): “speaking high,” a big talker. Homer uses it only of Telemachus and only in Antinous' mouth.

## Use

Antinous first gives Telemachus the word after his first speech to the suitors: “surely the gods themselves are teaching you / to be a man of lofty speech and to speak boldly” (*Odyssey* 1.384–385; [[biting-their-lips]]). Then it becomes an address, Τηλέμαχʼ ὑψαγόρη, μένος ἄσχετε, “Telemachus, lofty in speech, uncontrollable in your fury,” with which Antinous answers him in the assembly and invites him to the feast (*Odyssey* 2.85, 2.303), and which he uses again when Telemachus rebukes him for grudging the beggar a gift (*Odyssey* 17.406). ἄσχετος, “uncontrollable,” and its lengthened form ἀάσχετος are words of the Iliad too, as when Zeus tells Ares, “You have your mother's fury, uncontrollable and unyielding” (*Iliad* 5.892). See [[antinous]] and [[telemachus]].
