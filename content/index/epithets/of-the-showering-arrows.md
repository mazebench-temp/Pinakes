## Meaning

ἰοχέαιρα is usually derived from ἰός (“arrow”; [[arrows]]) + χέω (“pour, shower”): “she who showers arrows.” Another explanation connects the second part with χαίρω (“rejoice”): “delighting in arrows.” Homer uses the epithet twelve times, always of Artemis, and often in the line-end formula Ἄρτεμις ἰοχέαιρα. She is Apollo's sister, “Artemis of the showering arrows, sister of the Far-Shooter” (Il. 20.71; [[hekatos]]), and like him she kills from a distance. Her arrows are the sudden death of women: she shot Andromache's mother in her father's halls (Il. 6.428) and the daughters of Niobe (Il. 24.606). In the Underworld Odysseus asks his mother whether a long illness killed her, or Artemis with her gentle arrows (Od. 11.171–173), and she answers that it was neither, but longing for him (Od. 11.197–203).

## Use

Artemis first appears in the Iliad in Book 5, and the epithet comes with her twice. Neither time does she shoot. The first is a death in the fighting:

{{quote:e0b367d4-9840-5e11-b54d-9f48746be089}}

Scamandrius had learned from Artemis herself “to shoot all the wild things that the forest nurtures in the mountains” (5.52), and it did not save him. The word for his skill, ἑκηβολίαι (“far-shooting”), belongs to the family of Apollo's archer epithet ([[far-shooter]]). The goddess's pupil is killed with a spear in the back as he runs (5.55–58; [[scamandrius]]).

The second use makes her a healer. Apollo sets the wounded Aeneas in his temple on Pergamus, and there “Leto and Artemis of the showering arrows healed him in the great inner shrine and gave him glory” (5.447–448; [[pergamus]], [[leto]], [[healing]]). Mother and daughter tend the man whom Diomedes has crushed with a stone, while outside the armies fight over his phantom ([[the-phantom-of-aeneas]]).

## Outside Homer

Hesiod gives Artemis the same epithet (*Theogony* 14, 918). The Homeric Hymn to Artemis opens with it, and in the next verse calls her the own sister of Apollo of the golden sword (Hymn 27.2–3; [[of-the-golden-sword]]).
