## Meaning

ζάθεος = ζα- (an intensive prefix, “very,” related to δια-) + θεός (“god”): “very divine, most holy.” Homer uses it of places sacred to gods, such as Cilla here and Crisa in the Catalogue of Ships (Κρῖσάν τε ζαθέην, Il. 2.520).

## Use

{{quote:ec1ecef1-1b98-58bd-9c87-04d45b73c864}}

The epithet belongs to the two-line invocation that opens both of Chryses' prayers ([[hear-me]]), naming Apollo's local seats: Chryse, Cilla, and Tenedos. Only Cilla receives an epithet. The Iliad says nothing more about it. See [[cilla]], and for the other words for holiness in Book 1, [[egatheos]] and [[hieros]].
