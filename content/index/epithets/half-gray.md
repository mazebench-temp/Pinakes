## Meaning

μεσαιπόλιος = μέσος (“middle, half”) + πολιός (“gray,” of old men's hair and of the sea; [[gray-sea]]): “half gray,” a man whose hair is graying, no longer young and not yet old. Homer has it only here. Compare the old men of Troy, gray at the temples, whom Hector sets on the walls (8.518; [[gray-templed]]).

## Use

“Then, though he was half gray, calling on the Danaans, Idomeneus leapt among the Trojans and roused rout among them” (13.361–362; [[idomeneus]], [[old-age-and-memory]]). The concessive περ, “though,” sets his age against the leap, and the book returns to it. Facing Aeneas, he fears the younger man: “and he has the flower of youth, which is the greatest power. For if we were of the same age, with the spirit we have now, soon either he would carry off the great mastery, or I would” (13.484–486; [[aeneas]], [[kratos]]). When he has killed Oenomaus, “his legs were no longer steady when he rushed,” and he goes back step by step (13.512–516; [[pitiless]]). In Book 23 the son of Oileus taunts him that he is not the youngest among the Argives, and that his eyes are not the sharpest (23.476–477). Beside him fights Meriones, his younger attendant ([[meriones]]).
