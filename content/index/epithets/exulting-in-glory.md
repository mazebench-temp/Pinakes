## Meaning

κύδεϊ, dative of κῦδος (“glory, the radiance of victory”) + γαίων, participle of γαίω (“rejoice, exult”): “exulting in his glory.” The phrase belongs to the language of divine triumph. Zeus sits on Ida κύδεϊ γαίων (Il. 8.51), and Ares, healed of his wound, sits beside Zeus κύδεϊ γαίων at the end of Book 5 (Il. 5.906).

## Use

{{quote:14121ed4-4e16-5fe7-b7ef-252ad9b91c9b}}

The Hundred-Hander ([[hundred-handed]]) takes his seat beside Zeus with a god's pride, and the other gods shrink back. The line is nearly the one that ends Book 5, where it is Ares who sits beside the son of Cronus. Here the monster occupies the place of honor, and his presence alone restores Zeus' power.
