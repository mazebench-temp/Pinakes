## Meaning

ἅλς (“the salt sea”) + -ύδνη, an element of uncertain sense. Walter Leaf reports Hesychius' gloss ὕδναι, “offspring,” which he takes for a mere attempt to explain the word, and himself refers -υδν- to the stem of ὕδωρ, “water,” so that the word means “daughter of the salt water,” the sense of descent lying only in the feminine ending (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 20.207; see the translation's note on 20.207). He reports too that the Alexandrians accepted the word, since Callimachus gives a Nereid the name Ὑδατοσύδνη.

Homer has it twice. In the Iliad it is Thetis, when Aeneas tells Achilles what men say of his parents: “and that your mother is Thetis of the lovely tresses, daughter of the sea” (20.207; [[of-the-lovely-tresses]]). In the Odyssey the seals that sleep in a crowd around Proteus, come up out of the gray sea, are “the brood of the lovely daughter of the sea” (*Odyssey* 4.403–405). Thetis' usual title names her father, [[daughter-of-the-old-man]] (θυγάτηρ ἁλίοιο γέροντος). See [[thetis]] and [[nereids]].
