## Meaning

ὄζος (“branch, shoot”) + Ἄρηος (“of Ares”): “a shoot of Ares.” The image is of a plant: the warrior is a branch sprung from the god of war. It is praise of a man's fighting quality, not a claim of descent. When the Catalogue means real sons of Ares, it says so: Ascalaphus and Ialmenus are υἷες Ἄρηος, whom Astyoche bore to the god ({{ref:Iliad 2.512-515}}).

The formula closes the verse. It occurs ten times in the Iliad, always of lesser men, never of the great heroes.

## Use

Book 2 has half of all its uses, five men on both sides. Elephenor leads the Abantes:

Licymnius, whom Tlepolemus killed, is an “offshoot of Ares” as he grows old ({{ref:Iliad 2.663}}); Podarces commands the men of the dead Protesilaus ({{ref:Iliad 2.704}}); Leonteus is Polypoetes' fellow commander ({{ref:Iliad 2.745}}); and on the Trojan side Pylaeus leads the Pelasgians with his brother ({{ref:Iliad 2.842}}). The epithet is one of the Catalogue's ways of giving a minor leader a warrior's dignity in a single phrase.
