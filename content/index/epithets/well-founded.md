## Meaning

ἐϋσταθής = εὖ (“well”) + the root of ἵστημι (“stand”): “standing well, firmly built.” The phrase ἐϋσταθέος μεγάροιο, “of the well-founded hall,” occurs once in the Iliad, of the hall of Hephaestus, around whose wall his tripods are to stand (18.374); Walter Leaf calls it elsewhere a purely Odyssean phrase (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 18.374). In the Odyssey it is the hall of Odysseus, where the suitors feast and die (*Odyssey* 20.258, 22.120, 22.127, 22.257, 22.274, 22.441, 22.458), and once the bedchamber that Odysseus built himself (ἐϋσταθέος θαλάμου, *Odyssey* 23.178). Compare [[well-built]] (ἐϋκτίμενος) and [[house]].
