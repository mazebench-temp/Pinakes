## Meaning

ἀργυροδίνης = ἄργυρος (“silver”) + δίνη (“eddy, whirlpool”): “with silvery eddies,” a clear river whose swirls gleam. It occurs three times in Homer: of the Peneius here, and of the Xanthus in Book 21, when the river fights Achilles (Il. 21.8, 21.130). Compare [[eddying]] (δινήεις).

## Use

{{quote:0e56549d-e799-5d97-a5b1-dd703513a223}}

The Catalogue pauses for a natural wonder. The Titaressus flows into the silver-eddying Peneius but does not mix with it; it runs on top “like olive oil” ([[like-olive-oil]]), because it is a branch of the Styx, the river of the gods' oath ([[styx]]). The bright epithet of the Peneius sets off the dark water that will not mingle with it.
