## Meaning

Walter Leaf derives ἐρύγμηλος from a noun ἐρυγμός, “roaring,” with the suffix -ηλος of ἕκηλος, and notes that the word occurs only here (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 18.580). It belongs with ἐρεύγομαι, used of the sea that “belches out” against the shore (17.265), and with the aorist ἤρυγε, “bellowed,” of a bull dragged to Poseidon at Helice, to which Achilles' victim Hippodamas is compared (20.403–405). On the shield the bull bellows as the lions drag it off: “had seized a bellowing bull; and he, lowing loudly, / was being dragged off” (18.580–581; [[the-herd-and-the-lions]]). See [[bulls]].
