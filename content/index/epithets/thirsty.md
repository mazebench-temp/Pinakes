## Meaning

πολυδίψιος = πολύς (“much”) + δίψα (“thirst”): “very thirsty, parched.” Homer uses it nowhere else, and only of Argos (4.171). The usual epithet of Argos makes it a land of pasture, [[horse-pasturing]] (ἱππόβοτος); this one describes a dry country. The rivers of the Argive plain, the Inachus among them, are torrents that run dry in summer, and Greek myth explained the drought by Hera's claim to the land (see Outside Homer).

## Use

{{quote:719ed7ea-d038-510b-a1f2-232977859441}}

Agamemnon's lament over his wounded brother turns to what would follow his death. The army would remember home ([[dear-native-land]]), the war would be given up, and Helen would be left to the Trojans as a thing to boast of ([[argive-helen]]). He pictures himself arriving “in utter disgrace” (ἐλέγχιστος) in a parched land. The Argos of the epithet is his own kingdom ([[argos]], [[agamemnon]]). In Book 2 he proposed the voyage home to test the army; here he fears it in earnest.

## Outside Homer

In the story told by Pausanias, Inachus and the other rivers of the Argolid judged that the land belonged to Hera and not to Poseidon, and Poseidon in anger took away their water; for that reason, he says, they have no water except after rain (2.15.5). Apollodorus tells that when Danaus came to Argos the land was waterless for the same reason, and that Poseidon showed Danaus' daughter Amymone the springs of Lerna (2.1.4).
