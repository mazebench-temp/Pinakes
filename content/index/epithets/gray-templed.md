## Meaning

πολιοκρόταφος = πολιός (“gray”) + κρόταφος (“temple” of the head): “gray at the temples.” πολιός is the gray of the sea ([[gray-sea]]) and of an old man's hair: Priam, begging Hector to come inside the walls, pictures dogs defiling the gray head and gray beard of an old man killed, and tears out his gray hair (22.74–78). κρόταφος is the temple, through which Odysseus' spear passes when he kills Democoon (4.501–502). The compound occurs only here.

## Use

Hector orders that “boys in their first youth and gray-templed old men are to spend the night around the city on the god-built towers” (8.518–519; [[in-first-youth]], [[god-built]]). The old men of Troy are those who sat with Priam on the wall at the Scaean gates, “done with war now because of their age, but excellent speakers” (3.150–151; [[trojan-elders]]). Now they guard the walls themselves, while the army camps on the plain (8.489–522; [[elders]], [[geron]]). See [[the-walls-of-troy]].
