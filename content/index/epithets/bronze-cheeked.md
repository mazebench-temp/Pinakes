## Meaning

χαλκοπάρῃος = χαλκός (“bronze”) + παρειά (“cheek”): “bronze-cheeked,” a helmet with cheek-pieces of bronze that guard the sides of the face. Homer uses it four times, always in the genitive after διά, “through,” at the end of the verse, and each time the helmet does not hold. Three times it is κυνέης διὰ χαλκοπαρῄου: Polypoetes' spear kills Damasus (12.183), Ajax kills Hippothous at close quarters in the fight over Patroclus (17.293–294), and Achilles stabs Demoleon, son of Antenor, at the temple (20.395–397). The fourth is κόρυθος διὰ χαλκοπαρῄου, when Laertes kills Eupeithes at the end of the Odyssey (Od. 24.523–524). κυνέη, once a cap of dog-skin (κύων), is a helmet of any make ([[helmet]]); compare [[four-ridged]] and [[horsehair-crested]].
