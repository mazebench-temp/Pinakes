## Meaning

ἁγνός is “pure, holy,” of what is clean of pollution and belongs to the gods. The Odyssey has it five times, always in the feminine. Three times it is the epithet of the virgin goddess Artemis: “chaste Artemis of the golden throne,” who killed Orion in Ortygia (*Odyssey* 5.123; see [[artemis]] and [[golden-throned]]), and twice in Penelope's words, when she prays that chaste Artemis would give her a soft death, and when she tells how chaste Artemis gave the daughters of Pandareus their tallness (*Odyssey* 18.202, 20.71, plain renderings). Once it is said of Persephone, queen of the dead (*Odyssey* 11.386; see [[persephone]]), and once of the feast of the god that is kept throughout the land on the day of the contest of the bow (*Odyssey* 21.258–259, a plain rendering). The translation renders it “chaste” of Artemis, as in its key words (ἁγνός), and “holy” of Persephone (see the translation's note on 11.385–386).

## Outside Homer

The Homeric *Hymn to Demeter* gives the word to both goddesses of its story: Iambe's jests turn the mood of the holy lady, Demeter herself (*Hymn to Demeter* 202–204); Zeus sends Hermes to Erebus to bring holy Persephone out of the misty darkness into the light (ἁγνὴν Περσεφόνειαν ὑπὸ ζόφου ἠερόεντος, *Hymn to Demeter* 334–339; see [[misty]]); and Hecate embraces the holy daughter of Demeter on her return (κόρην Δημήτερος ἁγνήν, *Hymn to Demeter* 438–439).
