## Meaning

χαλκεοθώρηξ = χάλκεος (“of bronze”) + θώρηξ (“corselet”; [[corselet]]): “with bronze corselets.” It is close in sense to the Achaeans' standing epithet χαλκοχίτωνες ([[bronze-armored]]), but it belongs to neither side. Homer has it only in the passage on the clash of two armies, which is used twice in the same words (4.446–451, Il. 8.60–65). Compare [[with-linen-corselet]] (λινοθώρηξ) and [[of-the-flashing-corselet]] (αἰολοθώρηξ).

## Use

{{quote:20721753-adff-5b3f-84b5-52cc3ce37f4e}}

The first general engagement of the poem is told in these lines, and the men are named by their armor. The verse runs from the hide of the shields to the bronze of the corselets to the bosses ([[bossed]]). The armor does not save them. The passage ends with men “killing and being killed, and the earth ran with blood” (4.451).
