## Meaning

The second part of ἀκαλαρρείτης is ῥέω (“flow”). The first is obscure. It is usually connected with words for quiet and softness, such as ἀκέων (“in silence”) and ἦκα (“softly, gently”), which gives “gently flowing.” The ending -της makes an agent noun used as an adjective, as in βαθυρρείτης, “deep-flowing,” another epithet of Oceanus (21.195).

Homer uses the word twice, both times in the verse ἐξ ἀκαλαρρείταο βαθυρρόου Ὠκεανοῖο, “out of gently flowing, deep-streaming Oceanus,” with the sun rising (7.422; Od. 19.434). Oceanus is the river that circles the earth, from which “all rivers and all the sea and all springs and deep wells” flow (21.195–197), and the gods' origin (14.201, 14.302). The heavenly bodies rise from it and sink into it: the star of late summer “shines out bright when it has bathed in Oceanus” (5.5–6), the light of the sun falls into it at nightfall (8.485), and Dawn rises from its streams (19.1–2). Its other epithets are [[deep-streaming]] (βαθύρροος), in the same verse, and ἀψόρροος, “flowing back on itself” (18.399; Od. 20.65).

## Use

The day of the truce begins with the sun:

{{quote:88ccc4c6-1bc4-57dc-b184-f1835f43bdaf}}

Two epithets of calm and depth introduce a day of burial. Both armies go out to find their dead, wash away the blood, weep, and burn them (7.423–432; [[the-truce-for-the-dead]], [[cremation]]). The sun comes up out of the quiet river at the edge of the world over a plain where it is hard to tell one dead man from another (7.424).

In the Odyssey the same two verses bring the sun up on the morning of the boar hunt on Parnassus, where the young Odysseus got the scar by which his old nurse knows him (Od. 19.428–434). See [[oceanus]], [[helios]] and [[dawn-line]].
