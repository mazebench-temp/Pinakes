## Meaning

ἐπηρεφής = ἐπί (“over”) + the root of ἐρέφω (“roof, cover over”): “roofing over, overhanging,” of a bank or a cliff that leans out over what is below it. Homer uses it three times: of the banks of the Achaean ditch (12.54), and in the Odyssey of the cliffs from which Odysseus' ship gladly escaped to the open sea while the Laestrygonians destroyed the rest of his fleet (Od. 10.131–132), and of the rocks against which, Circe tells him, the great wave of Amphitrite roars (Od. 12.59–60). See [[the-achaean-wall]].

## Use

{{quote:0e835e97-13e1-5699-a641-e75100381002}}

The narrator explains why Hector's horses will not cross. The ditch is wide, “not to be leapt across from close at hand, nor crossed with ease; for overhanging banks stood all along it on both sides,” and stakes were fixed on top (12.53–56; [[sharp]], [[sons-of-the-achaeans]]). There a horse drawing a chariot could not easily go in (12.58–59; [[well-wheeled]], [[swift-footed]]), and on Polydamas' advice the Trojans leave their chariots at the ditch and cross on foot (12.75–87; [[dug]]).
