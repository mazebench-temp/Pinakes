## Meaning

ἄϋπνος = ἀ- (“not”) + ὕπνος (“sleep”; [[hypnos]]): “without sleep.” Homer uses it five times. Achilles has spent “many sleepless nights” at war (9.325), and the same half-verse is spoken by Odysseus in the Odyssey, disguised as a beggar, when he tells Penelope that he will lie as he has lain before, through sleepless nights (Od. 19.340). The blinded Cyclops is called out of his cave by his neighbors, who ask why he makes them sleepless (Od. 9.404); the Laestrygonians' land is one where a man who did without sleep could earn two wages, since the paths of night and day are close (Od. 10.84–86); and Penelope says that men cannot always be sleepless (Od. 19.591). ## Use

{{quote:93a75901-ffcf-5360-a6e7-858d43d11309}}

Achilles compares himself to a bird that brings every morsel to her unfledged chicks while it goes hard with her herself, and says, “so I too spent many sleepless nights, and passed through bloody days in battle” ([[the-bird-and-her-chicks]], [[bloody]], [[birds]]). The nights without sleep belong with the bird's hunger: labor for others that brings the laborer nothing. The book in which he says it is itself a night, in which others keep the watch and Achilles sleeps (9.663). See [[achilles]].
