## Meaning

ἀνδροφόνος = ἀνήρ, ἀνδρ- (“man”) + -φόνος (“slaying,” from the root of φόνος, “killing”): “killer of men.” It is Hector's regular epithet in the genitive formula Ἕκτορος ἀνδροφόνοιο. It is also used of Ares, and of the hands of Achilles: when Priam kisses them in Book 24, they are the “terrible, man-slaying hands” that had killed many of his sons (Il. 24.478–479).

## Use

{{quote:d0e8ae10-5bdf-5ab2-8730-80ee386211eb}}

This is the first time Hector is named in the Iliad, and he is named as a killer of Achaeans. The line belongs to the oath Achilles swears on the scepter ([[oath-formulas]]): the sons of the Achaeans will long for him when they fall in numbers before man-slaying Hector, and Agamemnon will be powerless to help ([[no-help-to-you]]). The epithet announces the action of the middle books, in which Achilles' absence lets Hector drive the Achaeans back to their ships.
