## Meaning

πανομφαῖος = πᾶς (“all”) + ὀμφή (“voice,” always a divine voice in Homer): Zeus as the source of every voice of omen. When Agamemnon wakes from the dream that Zeus sent him, “the divine voice was poured around him” (θείη … ὀμφή, 2.41); Hera wants Achilles to learn from the voice of the gods that they are with him (20.129); and in the Odyssey men act “following a god's voice” (Od. 3.215, 16.96). The god who sends dreams, thunder and birds is the god of all such voices ([[divination]], [[teras]]). The word occurs only here in Homer.

## Use

{{quote:408eeff6-8fd8-5722-997f-a90eca3dab07}}

The epithet stands in the verse that answers Agamemnon's prayer. He has just reminded Zeus that he never passed his altar on the voyage to Troy without burning the fat and thighs of oxen (8.238–241; [[perikalles]], [[many-benched]]), and the sign comes down at an altar of Zeus in the camp, where “the Achaeans used to sacrifice” (ῥέζεσκον, a past habit) to Zeus as lord of omens. The fawn falls beside it, and the sign is read at once: “when they saw that the bird had come from Zeus” (8.251), they turn on the Trojans.
