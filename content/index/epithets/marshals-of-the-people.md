## Meaning

κοσμήτωρ (“marshal, one who orders”) is formed from κοσμέω (“set in order, draw up troops”), from κόσμος (“order, arrangement”). κοσμήτορε is the dual: the two brothers together. λαῶν is the genitive plural of λαός, “the people, the host.”

## Use

{{quote:b43b348c-9f54-531e-ae99-412c9ed8942b}}

The phrase belongs to the narrator's account of Chryses' plea, and it returns word for word in Achilles' retelling ({{ref:Iliad 1.375}}; see [[retelling]]). It singles out the brothers as responsible for the army's order, and so for the decision about the ransom. The dual noun Ἀτρεΐδα δύω, “the two sons of Atreus” ([[son-of-atreus]]), and the dual epithet present Agamemnon and [[Menelaus]] as a pair. Chryses begs both, but only Agamemnon answers.
