## Meaning

ἀμφί (“around”) + χυτός (“poured, heaped,” from χέω, “pour”): “poured around, heaped up.” Walter Leaf takes it of heaped-up earth (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 20.145), and compares χυτὴ γαῖα, the earth heaped over the dead: Hector's wish that the heaped earth may cover him before he hears Andromache's cry (6.464), the heaped earth that covers Tydeus at Thebes (14.114), and the mound piled over Patroclus' bones (23.256).

Homer has the word only of the wall to which Poseidon leads the gods of the Achaean side: “to the heaped-up wall of divine Heracles, / the high wall, which the Trojans and Pallas Athena / had made for him, so that he might slip away and escape the sea monster” (20.145–147; [[divine]], [[sea-beasts]]). See [[heracles]].
