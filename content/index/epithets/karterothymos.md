## Meaning

καρτερόθυμος = καρτερός, κρατερός (“strong”; [[krateros]]) + θυμός (“spirit, heart”; [[thymos]]): “strong of spirit.” It describes heroes and peoples in both poems: Diomedes (5.277); Achilles, whom Zeus meant to honor together with his mother Thetis (Il. 13.350); the Mysians (Il. 14.512); and in the Odyssey Heracles (Od. 21.25). The translation renders it “stout-hearted,” the word it uses also for ταλασίφρων ([[stout-hearted]]).

## Use

{{quote:420c1a66-9710-5951-b6ac-9e7ccb8d7ce4}}

Pandarus addresses his enemy with two epithets, a noble father and the patronymic, and then admits that his arrow has failed ([[wise-hearted]], [[noble]], [[son-of-tydeus]], [[swift-shaft]]). The formal address is the challenger's before the spear-cast. A few lines later Pandarus is dead (5.290–296).

## Outside Homer

Hesiod gives the same epithet to Chrysaor, father of Geryon (*Theogony* 979; [[of-the-golden-sword]]).
