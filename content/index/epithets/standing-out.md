## Meaning

μεταπρεπής is the adjective of μεταπρέπω, “be conspicuous among,” from μετά (“among”) and πρέπω (“be clearly seen”), and like the verb it takes a dative of those among whom one stands out. The verb describes the bull that “is conspicuous among the gathered cattle,” the image of Agamemnon in the Catalogue (2.480–483), and Hector's boast, “with the spear I myself / stand out among the war-loving Trojans” (16.834–835). The adjective occurs once, in the line that describes the house of Hephaestus, “imperishable, starry, standing out among the immortals” (18.370; [[imperishable]], [[starry]]).
