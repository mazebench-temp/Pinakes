## Meaning

φθισήνωρ = φθίω, φθίνω (“waste away, destroy”) + ἀνήρ, stem ἀνερ- (“man”): “destroying men.” The compound is of the type with a verb stem first, like μενεπτόλεμος ([[steadfast-in-battle]]). It occurs five times in the Iliad, always in the phrase πόλεμον φθισήνορα. The same verb gives [[imperishable]] (ἄφθιτος, “not wasting”).

## Use

{{quote:954203b1-5b84-5d49-87e2-68a2b934c60d}}

Merops of Percote, a seer, knew what war would bring and tried to keep his sons from it. They did not obey, and the fates of black death led them on ([[the-sons-of-merops]]). The epithet states the father's knowledge: war destroys men. Both sons are killed by Diomedes in Book 11, where the same four lines are repeated (Il. 11.329–332).
