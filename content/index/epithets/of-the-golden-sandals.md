## Meaning

χρυσοπέδιλος = χρυσός (“gold”) + πέδιλον (“sandal”): “with golden sandals.” The sandals of the gods are golden: Hermes and Athena bind on “fine sandals, / ambrosial, golden,” that carry them over sea and land (*Iliad* 24.340–341; *Odyssey* 1.96–97; see [[sandals]]). Homer has the compound once, of Hera, in the verse on Heracles' wife among the gods, Hebe, “the daughter of great Zeus and of Hera of the golden sandals” (*Odyssey* 11.604; see [[hera]] and [[hebe]]).

## Outside Homer

Hesiod has the same verse in the same story: the son of Alcmene, when he had finished his labors, made Hebe, daughter of great Zeus and of Hera of the golden sandals, his revered wife on snowy Olympus (*Theogony* 950–953). He gives Hera the epithet again among the children of Rhea, Hestia, Demeter and “Hera of the golden sandals” (Ἥρην χρυσοπέδιλον, *Theogony* 453–454), and the Muses at the beginning of the poem sing of “Argive Hera, who walks in golden sandals” (*Theogony* 11–12).
