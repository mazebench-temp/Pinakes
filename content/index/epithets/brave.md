## Meaning

ἠΰς and ἐΰς are two forms of an old adjective meaning “good, brave, noble.” The neuter εὖ became the adverb “well,” and ἐΰ- is the first part of many compounds, such as [[well-built]] (ἐϋκτίμενος) and [[well-benched]] (ἐΰσσελμος). As an adjective it survives only in a few formulas.

ἠΰς τε μέγας τε (“brave and tall”) describes a man by his quality and his size. It is used of Tlepolemus here, of Achaeans whom Priam asks Helen to name from the wall (Il. 3.167, 3.226), and of a few others. ἐῢς πάϊς Ἀγχίσαο (“the brave son of Anchises”) is used three times of Aeneas.

## Use

{{quote:acf6a974-5183-50c1-ba9e-292c02b75dd3}}

The translation reverses the order: “tall and brave.” Tlepolemus, son of Heracles, leads nine ships from Rhodes. The formula praises him before the story of his exile ([[the-flight-of-tlepolemus]]). In the Trojan Catalogue Aeneas, “the brave son of Anchises,” leads the Dardanians ({{ref:Iliad 2.819}}).
