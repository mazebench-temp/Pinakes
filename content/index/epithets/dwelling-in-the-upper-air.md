## Meaning

αἰθέρι, dative of αἰθήρ (“the bright upper air”) + ναίων (“dwelling,” from ναίω): “who lives in the upper air.” In Homer the αἰθήρ is the clear, shining region of the sky above the lower air (ἀήρ), where clouds and mist hang. When the sons of Cronus divided the world, Zeus received “the broad sky, in the upper air and the clouds” (Il. 15.192). The phrase occurs three times in Homer (Il. 2.412, 4.166; Od. 15.523), always of Zeus.

## Use

{{quote:e7ee5ec4-5e3e-5deb-8295-b58b728f5a85}}

Agamemnon's prayer before battle begins with a crowd of titles: “most glorious” and “greatest” ([[most-glorious]]), “lord of the dark clouds” ([[lord-of-dark-clouds]]), and “who dwell in the upper air.” The prayer is for the fall of Priam's hall before sunset. The titles name the god's height and his power over the sky, and the answer comes from that height. Zeus accepts the sacrifice but will not grant the prayer, and increases the toil instead ({{ref:Iliad 2.419-420}}; [[prayer-type-scene]]).
