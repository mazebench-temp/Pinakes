## Meaning

ἐρίηρος is usually explained as ἐρι- (an intensive prefix, “very”) + a stem related to ἀραρίσκω (“fit, join”): “closely joined, devoted.” The etymology is not certain. The word describes a man's companions (ἐρίηρες ἑταῖροι; [[hetairos]]) and a comrade's loyalty: Idomeneus promises Agamemnon to be his “trusty companion” (ἐρίηρος ἑταῖρος, Il. 4.266). In the Odyssey it is also an epithet of the singer (ἐρίηρον ἀοιδόν, Od. 1.346).

## Use

Book 3 uses the epithet twice, at the two ends of Helen's story. Hector asks Paris whether it was as such a coward that he crossed the sea, “gathering your trusty companions,” and brought back a lovely woman from a distant land (3.46–49). At the end of the duel it is Menelaus' men who are trusty:
