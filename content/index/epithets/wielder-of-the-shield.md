## Meaning

σακέσπαλος = σάκος (“shield,” the great shield) + πάλλω (“brandish, wield”): “who wields the shield.” It is formed like ἐγχέσπαλος, “spear-brandishing” ([[spear-brandishing]]). The word occurs only here in Homer. See [[shield]].

## Use

{{quote:17e53094-a7f4-5f9c-816d-41dcfb01e371}}

Athena puts the father's might into the son and names the father with two epithets, “the horseman,” ἱππότα, as the older heroes are called ([[gerenian-horseman]]), and the wielder of the shield ([[menos]], [[tydeus]]). In the same speech she takes the mist from Diomedes' eyes ([[achlys]]). At the end of the book she compares father and son again, this time to the son's disadvantage: “Tydeus has begotten a son little like himself” (5.800; [[tydeus-at-thebes]], [[son-of-tydeus]]).
