## Meaning

Πυλοιγενής = Πύλος (“Pylos”) + -γενής (“born,” from γίγνομαι): “born at Pylos.” The first part looks like an old locative, Πύλοι (“at Pylos”), of the kind seen in οἴκοι (“at home”). The word occurs twice in Homer: here of Nestor, and in Book 23 of his horses, which are “of Pylian breed” (Πυλοιγενέες … ἵπποι, Il. 23.303).

## Use

{{quote:3a8197f9-c1b1-520f-bb75-3ebe58e4fccd}}

Agamemnon holds the council of elders at Nestor's ship, and the narrator names Nestor by his birthplace and his rank. The line honors the oldest of the elders in the moment before he speaks. Twenty lines later he rises as “king of sandy Pylos” ([[sandy]], {{ref:Iliad 2.77}}). Book 1 had called his home “sacred Pylos” ([[egatheos]], {{ref:Iliad 1.252}}). Nestor's authority rests on the city he rules and on the generations he has outlived there.
