## Meaning

εὔπωλος = εὖ (“well”) + πῶλος (“foal,” in poetry a horse): “with good horses.” Homer uses it only of Ilios, and only in the phrase Ἴλιον εἰς εὔπωλον at the end of the verse, “to Ilios of the fine horses” (5.551; Il. 16.576; Od. 2.18, 11.169, 14.71). The phrase names Troy as the goal of a voyage: the place to which a man has gone, or from which he has not come back. Troy's horses are famous in the Iliad. The Trojans are “horse-taming” ([[tamer-of-horses]]); Aeneas' horses are of the breed that Zeus gave to Tros (5.265–272; [[master-of-rout]]); and Heracles sacked the city for the horses of Laomedon (5.640; [[heracles-sacks-troy]]).

## Use

{{quote:a8ac00c9-cd1e-5152-8526-580c1450ba04}}

The twin sons of Diocles came from Pherae in Messenia to fight for the sons of Atreus, and Aeneas kills them both (5.541–560; [[crethon]], [[orsilochus-son-of-diocles]]). The epithet of the city comes in the verse that brings them there, two lines before their death. In Book 16 the same half-verse brings the Myrmidon Epeigeus to Troy, “to fight the Trojans,” just before Hector kills him with a stone (Il. 16.570–580).
