## Meaning

σκηπτοῦχος = σκῆπτρον (“scepter, staff”) + -οχος (“holding,” from ἔχω): “scepter-holding.” The scepter is the emblem of the right to rule and to judge, and the phrase σκηπτοῦχοι βασιλῆες describes the kings in council.

## Use

{{quote:c3ff783c-d2ea-53cc-b7c0-95acf19e5543}}

Nestor's argument is about rank. Achilles is stronger and a goddess' son, but Agamemnon is the greater, since he rules over more, and a scepter-bearing king has a share of honor from Zeus. Nestor speaks just after Achilles has flung the scepter to the ground ({{ref:Iliad 1.245}}; [[studded-with-golden-nails]]). The old man restores to the emblem the meaning Achilles has just thrown away.
