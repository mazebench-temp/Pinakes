## Meaning

ἐυῶπις = εὖ (“well”) + ὤψ (“eye, face, look”): “with a fair face,” or “with fair eyes.” The second part is the one in [[gray-eyed]] (γλαυκῶπις), [[ox-eyed]] (βοῶπις) and [[bright-eyed]] (ἑλικῶπις), epithets of goddesses and women, and in [[wine-dark]] (οἶνοψ). The translation renders it “fair-faced” (see the Odyssey translation's key words).

## Use

Both uses are the verse-end ἐυώπιδα κούρην, “the fair-faced girl,” and both belong to Odysseus' meeting with Nausicaa: Athena plans that he should wake “and see the fair-faced girl” (*Odyssey* 6.113), and he ponders whether to “clasp the knees of the fair-faced girl” (*Odyssey* 6.142). See [[nausicaa]] and [[supplication]].

## Outside Homer

The *Homeric Hymn to Demeter* has the same verse-end of Persephone: Demeter will not let the earth bring forth its fruit until she sees with her eyes her fair-faced daughter, ἑὴν εὐώπιδα κούρην (*Hymn to Demeter* 332–333). The seventh *Homeric Hymn* greets Dionysus as the child of fair-faced Semele, τέκος Σεμέλης εὐώπιδος (Hymn 7.58).
