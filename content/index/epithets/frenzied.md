## Meaning

λυσσώδης is formed from λύσσα, “rage, frenzy,” with the suffix -ώδης, “like, full of”: “full of frenzy.” λύσσα is the battle-madness that the Iliad finds in Hector and in Achilles. Odysseus tells Achilles that “a mighty frenzy has entered into” Hector (9.239), who rages “in his deadly frenzy” (9.305), and in Book 21 λύσσα holds Achilles' heart as he pursues the Trojans to the city (21.542–543). Teucer calls Hector “this mad dog,” κύνα λυσσητῆρα (8.299; [[mad-dog]]). λυσσώδης occurs only here.

## Use

{{quote:c04733e3-e0b5-5c93-a301-c5a80f95e2c6}}

Poseidon, in the likeness of Calchas, does not fear the Trojans elsewhere, “but here I am most dreadfully afraid that we may suffer some harm, where that frenzied man leads them, like a flame, Hector, who claims to be the son of mighty Zeus” (13.52–54; [[hector]], [[son-of-zeus]], [[eristhenes]]). ὅ γʼ ὁ λυσσώδης, with the pronoun and the article, points at the man: “that frenzied one.” The god sees Hector as the Achaean envoys did in Book 9, when Odysseus spoke of his λύσσα (9.237–239, 9.304–306). The flame is the Trojans' likeness a few lines before (13.39; [[like-blazing-fire]]). See [[poseidon]].
