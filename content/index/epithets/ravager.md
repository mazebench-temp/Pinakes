## Meaning

σίντης is formed from σίνομαι (“harm, ravage, plunder”): “one who does harm, a ravager.” It is a noun used as an epithet of beasts of prey, three times in the Iliad: the lion that a god brings upon the jackals (11.480–481); the wolves that fall on lambs and kids and snatch them from the flock (16.352–353); and the lion to which Achilles is compared as he comes against Aeneas, a ravager whom the men of a whole district gather to kill (20.164–166). Compare [[ravening]] (μαλερός), [[eating-raw-flesh]] and [[lurker-in-the-grass]], other names for beasts in the similes.
