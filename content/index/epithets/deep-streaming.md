## Meaning

βαθύρροος = βαθύς (“deep”) + ῥόος (“stream, current,” from ῥέω, “flow”): “with a deep current.” Homer uses it five times. Four times it describes Oceanus, always in the genitive βαθυρρόου Ὠκεανοῖο at the end of the verse: in the sunrise verse that also calls him [[gently-flowing]] (7.422; Od. 19.434), in Hera's lie that she is going to “the house of deep-streaming Oceanus” (14.311), and when Odysseus' ship, sailing to the dead, reaches “the limits of deep-streaming Oceanus” (Od. 11.13). Once it describes a river of the Troad: in Book 21 the routed Trojans are driven into the Xanthus, “deep-streaming, silver-eddying” (21.8; [[silver-eddying]], [[scamander]]).

A related form, βαθυρρείτης, “deep-flowing,” names Oceanus in Achilles' boast over Asteropaeus: not even the great strength of Oceanus, from which all rivers and all the sea flow, can match Zeus, and he too fears Zeus' thunderbolt (21.190–199).
