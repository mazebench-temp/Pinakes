## Meaning

καλλιγύναιξ = καλός (“beautiful”) + γυνή, stem γυναικ- (“woman”): “of the beautiful women.” It praises a land by its women. Homer uses it five times: of Hellas (Il. 2.683, 9.447), of Achaea in the phrase Ἄργος ἐς ἱππόβοτον καὶ Ἀχαιΐδα καλλιγύναικα (“to horse-pasturing Argos and Achaea of the lovely women,” Il. 3.75, 3.258; [[horse-pasturing]]), and of Sparta (Od. 13.412).

## Use

{{quote:273f86a4-b3cf-5f3b-9d04-2dc7f1ea0aec}}

In the Iliad Hellas is a region in the south of Thessaly, part of Achilles' realm beside Phthia. Its people are one of three names given to the Myrmidons' contingent: “Myrmidons and Hellenes and Achaeans” ([[hellenes]]). In Book 9 Phoenix recalls leaving “Hellas of the lovely women” when he fled his father (Il. 9.447). Only much later did Hellas and the Hellenes become names for all Greece and all Greeks ([[panhellenes]]).
