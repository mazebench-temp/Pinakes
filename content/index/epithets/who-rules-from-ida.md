## Meaning

Ἴδηθεν (“from Ida,” with the suffix -θεν of the place from which) + μεδέων, participle of μέδω (“rule, watch over”; compare μέδων, “ruler”): “ruling from Ida.” Mount Ida rises above the Trojan plain. Zeus has a precinct and an altar on its peak Gargarus, and from there he watches the city and the ships (Il. 8.47–52; [[ida]]). The title makes the god of the wide sky the lord of the landscape in which the armies fight.

## Use

{{quote:21e4284e-c1b5-5925-be78-6308bf3433a8}}

The full line opens Agamemnon's oath (3.276), and the people of both armies repeat it when the lots are shaken for the duel (3.320). Achaeans and Trojans alike call on the lord of Ida: “this is what a man would say, of Achaeans and Trojans alike” (3.319). The line returns in the armies' prayer before the duel of Hector and Ajax (Il. 7.202) and in Priam's prayer before he goes to Achilles (Il. 24.308). See [[father-zeus]] and [[most-glorious]], the other parts of the address.
