## Meaning

μειλίχιος (“gentle, soothing”) is formed from μείλιχος and belongs with μειλίσσω (“soothe, appease”). With words it makes the formulas μειλιχίοισι προσηύδα, “spoke … with soothing words,” as when Diomedes answers Glaucus (Il. 6.214), and ἔπεσσί τε μειλιχίοισι, as in Nestor's advice to win over Achilles “with kindly gifts and with soothing words” (Il. 9.112–113). Compare [[gentle-words]] (ἀγανοῖς ἐπέεσσιν) and [[winged-words]].

## Use

Agamemnon's review alternates two kinds of speech. The men who hold back he rebukes “with angry words” (4.241). Idomeneus, arming his Cretans, he addresses “with soothing words” (4.256), and he honors him above all the Danaans ([[of-the-swift-horses]]). The two Ajaxes do the same in Book 12, rebuking some men with soothing words and others with harsh ones (Il. 12.267–268). In Book 10 Diomedes remembers that his father Tydeus carried a soothing word (μειλίχιον μῦθον) to the Cadmeians (Il. 10.288), on the embassy that Agamemnon describes in Book 4 (4.384; [[embassy]], [[tydeus-at-thebes]]).
