## Meaning

τανύπεπλος = τανυ- (“long, stretched,” from τανύω, “stretch”) + πέπλος (“robe,” a woman's long outer garment): “with a long, trailing robe.” A robe that reaches the ground is a mark of rank and grace. The women of Troy are ἑλκεσίπεπλοι, “with trailing robes” (Il. 6.442, 22.105). Homer gives τανύπεπλος to Helen, to Thetis (Il. 18.385, 18.424), and to a few women in the Odyssey.

## Use

{{quote:e12d7b62-92de-51f2-86ac-41170cbc3c37}}

Helen answers Priam's question about Ajax with two epithets, the robe and [[brilliant]] (δῖα γυναικῶν). The same pair marks her in the Odyssey, when she sits beside Menelaus in Sparta (Od. 4.305). On the wall the narrator's epithets praise her: [[white-armed]] (3.121), brilliant among women (3.171, 3.228), child of Zeus ([[daughter-of-zeus]], 3.199), of the long robes. Her own word for herself, in the same scene, is “dog-faced” ([[dog-face]], 3.180).
