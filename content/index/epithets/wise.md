## Meaning

πεπνυμένος is the participle of the perfect πέπνυμαι, “have breath, have one's wits,” from the family of πνέω (“breathe”) and πινυτός (“prudent”). The perfect names a lasting state: a man who has his mind about him. The Odyssey shows the verb's full sense among the dead, where Persephone has granted Tiresias alone “to have his wits” (οἴῳ πεπνῦσθαι), while the rest flit about as shadows (Od. 10.494–495).

In the Odyssey it is the standing epithet of Telemachus, in the line τὸν δʼ αὖ Τηλέμαχος πεπνυμένος ἀντίον ηὔδα, which has the same shape as the line that introduces Antenor's answer here (3.203). In the Iliad it marks the Trojan counselors whose advice is refused: Antenor, who proposes giving Helen back (Il. 7.347–353), and Polydamas, who urges Hector to withdraw into the city (Il. 18.249).
