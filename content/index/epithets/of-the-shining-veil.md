## Meaning

λιπαροκρήδεμνος = λιπαρός (“sleek, glossy, shining,” the word of oil, λίπα) + κρήδεμνον (“headdress, veil”; [[veil]]): “with a shining headdress.” The κρήδεμνον is the veil that a woman of rank wears over her hair when she goes out, and a goddess's veil gleams: Hera covers herself with one, “a fine one, new-made, and it was white like the sun” (14.184–185). The compound occurs once in Homer, of [[charis-wife-of-hephaestus|Charis]], who comes out to meet Thetis at the house of Hephaestus (18.382).

{{notes}}

## Outside Homer

The Homeric Hymn to Demeter gives the epithet to Hecate, who heard Persephone cry out from her cave (*Homeric Hymn to Demeter* 24–25) and comes near to greet her on her return (438), and to Rhea, whom Zeus sends to bring Demeter back to the gods (459).
