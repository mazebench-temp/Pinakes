## Meaning

πολυπενθής = πολύς (“much”) + πένθος (“grief, mourning”): “full of grief.” Homer uses it three times: of the halcyon here (9.563); of the beggar whom Eumaeus addresses as “old man of many sorrows,” not knowing that he is his master (Od. 14.386); and of Penelope's own heart, when she asks the old nurse why she mocks her “much-grieving heart” with the news that Odysseus is home (Od. 23.15). πένθος is the grief of mourning, the “unbearable grief” that strikes the Achaean leaders at the start of Book 9 (πένθεϊ ἀτλήτῳ, 9.3).

## Use

{{quote:d844dee2-9468-56b5-8a4f-1fafbb8fc92f}}

Cleopatra's parents called her Alcyone, “because her mother, having the lot of the halcyon bird of many sorrows, wept that the far-working Phoebus Apollo had snatched her away” ([[cleopatra]], [[marpessa]], [[far-worker]]). The bird's name, ἀλκυών, gave the girl hers, and its epithet gives the reason: the halcyon was a bird of lament, and Marpessa, taken from her husband by a god, had its lot.
