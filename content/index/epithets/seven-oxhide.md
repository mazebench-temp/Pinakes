## Meaning

ἑπταβόειος = ἑπτά (“seven”) + βόειος (“of oxhide,” from βοῦς, “ox”): “of seven oxhides.” A Homeric shield was built of layers of hide on a frame and often faced with bronze, and the hide could stand for the shield itself. Fighters hack at “the oxhide shields around one another's chests” (5.452; ἀμφὶ στήθεσσι βοείας), the Trojans around Asius hold up their “dry oxhides” as they charge the wall (βόας αὔας, 12.137–139), and Hector speaks of wielding “the dry oxhide” (βῶν ἀζαλέην, 7.238–239; [[with-the-oxhide-shield]]).

Homer uses the word five times, always of Ajax's shield: four times in Book 7 (7.220, 7.222, 7.245, 7.266), and once in Book 11, when Zeus rouses fear in Ajax and he slings “his shield of seven oxhides” behind him as he gives ground (11.544–546). The shield has other descriptions of its own: it is “like a tower” (ἠΰτε πύργον, 7.219, 11.485, 17.128; [[like-a-tower]]), “flashing” (αἰόλον, 7.222, 16.107; [[flashing]]), “bronze” (χάλκεον, 7.220), and “dread” (δεινόν, 7.245, 7.266). See [[the-shield-of-ajax]] and [[shield]].

## Use

{{quote:0ca0ab0a-cc2d-50d1-9f9d-b507ed33c3f0}}

The epithet is given twice in three verses, and the layers are counted: seven hides of well-fed bulls, and an eighth layer of bronze hammered on top by Tychius, the best of leather-workers (7.220–223; [[tychius]], [[hyle]], [[well-fed]]). The duel then tests them one by one. Hector's spear strikes the outermost bronze, cuts through six hides and stops in the seventh (7.245–248; [[unyielding]]); his stone strikes the boss, “and the bronze rang all around” (7.266–267). Hector's own shield, which the narrator calls an ἀσπίς, the round shield, where Ajax's is a σάκος, the great body shield, is pierced by Ajax's spear and broken inward by his stone (7.249–254, 7.268–272; [[balanced-on-every-side]], [[like-a-millstone]]). The count of layers explains the result. See [[the-duel-of-hector-and-ajax]] and [[ajax]].

## Outside Homer

The shield became Ajax's emblem. His son was called Eurysaces, “Broad Shield,” and in Sophocles' *Ajax* the hero, before his death, leaves the boy the unbreakable shield of seven hides, to be kept while the rest of his armor is buried with him. In Ovid the contest for the arms of Achilles begins when “Ajax, master of the sevenfold shield,” rises to speak (*clipei dominus septemplicis Aiax*, *Metamorphoses* 13.2; [[ovid]]). See [[sophocles]].
