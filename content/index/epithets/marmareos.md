## Meaning

μαρμάρεος belongs with μαρμαίρω, “flash, sparkle” ([[marmairon]]), and with μάρμαρος, which in Homer is a gleaming boulder, not yet marble (12.380; see the translation's note there): “flashing, gleaming.” Homer uses it three times, always in the accusative μαρμαρέην: of the sea here; of the aegis that Zeus takes up on Ida when he turns the battle against the Achaeans, covering the mountain in cloud, lightening and thundering (17.593–596; [[aegis-bearing]]); and of the triple rim that Hephaestus puts around Achilles' shield (18.479–480; [[shield]]). Of the sea it names the glitter of the surface, as [[shining-sea]] (δῖα) and [[gray-sea]] (πολιή) name its light and its color.

## Use

{{quote:512fa886-cdaa-54fa-a0dc-d7a78c459d19}}

Sleep asks Hera for the gods' greatest oath, by the water of Styx, with one hand on the bountiful earth and the other on the gleaming sea, so that the gods below, who are around Cronus, may be witnesses (14.271–274; [[oath]], [[inviolable]], [[bountiful-earth]], [[cronus]], [[titans]]). Earth and sea are the world above those gods, and the epithets name it as the living world: the earth that feeds and the sea that shines. See [[sleep]] and [[hera]].

## Outside Homer

In Hesiod, at the edge of the world, where the sources and limits of earth, Tartarus, sea and sky lie in order, there are μαρμάρεαι πύλαι, “gleaming gates,” with a threshold of bronze (*Theogony* 807–812; [[tartarus]]).
