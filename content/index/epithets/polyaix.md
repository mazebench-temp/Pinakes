## Meaning

πολύς (“much”) + the root of ἀΐσσω (“dart, rush”): “much-rushing, impetuous.” The translation renders it “furious,” the word it also uses for θοῦρις ([[furious]]), and once “of much charging.” Achilles uses it in his quarrel with Agamemnon: “The greater part of the furious fighting / my hands carry through,” but the greater prize goes to Agamemnon (1.165–166; [[quarrel-of-achilles-and-agamemnon]]). Athena, taunting Diomedes, asks whether “the weariness of much charging has sunk into your limbs” (5.811). Poseidon's throw carries Aeneas “to the far edge of the furious war” (20.328). In the Odyssey the sons of Aloeus threaten to raise the din of furious war against the gods on Olympus (*Odyssey* 11.313–314). See [[polemos]].
