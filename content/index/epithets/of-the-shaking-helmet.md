## Meaning

κόρυς (“helmet”) + the root of ἀΐσσω (“dart, rush”): the warrior whose helmet darts and shakes as he moves. The translation renders it “of the shaking helmet” and keeps it apart from κορυθαίολος, “of the gleaming helmet,” Hector's standing epithet, whose second part is αἰόλος, “quick-moving, flashing” (see its table of key words; [[of-the-gleaming-helmet]], [[flashing]]). Homer has the word once.

## Use

{{quote:b8cc1d24-1b92-5d8e-981b-2627d4da6eec}}

Hector has finished his monologue when Achilles comes near, and the narrator gives him the measure of the war god himself, “the equal of Enyalius, the warrior of the shaking helmet” (22.132; [[equal-of-ares]], [[enyalius]]). He brandishes the Pelian ash over his right shoulder, and his bronze shines like fire or the rising sun (22.133–135; [[the-pelian-ash]], [[blazing-fire]]). At the sight Hector trembles and runs (22.136–137). When Achilles charges in the duel, his own helmet moves as the god's: he “nodded with his shining helmet / with its four ridges; and around it waved the fair plumes / of gold” (22.314–316; [[four-ridged]], [[helmet]]). See [[achilles]].
