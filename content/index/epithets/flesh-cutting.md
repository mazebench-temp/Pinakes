## Meaning

ταμεσίχρως = the root of τάμνω (“cut”) + χρώς (“skin, flesh”): “cutting the flesh.” The compound has its verb first, like τερπικέραυνος ([[delighting-in-thunder]]). Homer uses it three times: of bronze here and in the armed duel of the funeral games (Il. 23.803), and of the long spears of a battle line (Il. 13.339–340). Compare [[pitiless-bronze]] and [[sharp]].

## Use

{{quote:c687b0f3-a0ae-5cfd-8a55-a1bda23e67e2}}

Apollo's call turns on one word. The Argives' flesh (χρώς) is “not stone or iron,” and the bronze is flesh-cutting (ταμεσίχρως): the epithet completes the thought of the line before it (4.510–511). The poem has already shown it. The arrow grazed the outermost flesh of Menelaus (4.139), and in the first battle Echepolus and Elephenor have fallen to the bronze (4.457–469). Apollo ends with the fact that gives the Trojans their chance: Achilles is not fighting ([[heart-grieving]]).
