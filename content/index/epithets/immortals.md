## Meaning

ἀθάνατος = ἀ- (“not”) + θάνατος (“death”): “deathless.” Used as a noun, οἱ ἀθάνατοι, it is the gods' name for what they are. Its opposites are θνητός ([[mortal-men]]) and βροτός (“mortal”); its near-synonym is ἄμβροτος, from which comes [[ambrosial-locks|ambrosial]].

## Use

Forms: ἀθανάτοισιν or ἀθανάτοισι (265, 398, 503, 520, 525), ἀθανάτοιο (530).

{{quote:0e4f37c1-0777-51b8-aa86-7ee8c2a47bbb}}

The word is at home in the gods' talk about themselves. Thetis alone “among the immortals” warded off ruin from Zeus (398); she reminds him of her help “among the immortals” (503); Zeus complains that Hera quarrels with him “among the immortal gods” (520) and calls his nod the greatest sign “among the immortals” (525). After the nod his hair streams “from his immortal head” (530).

The human perspective stays in view through Achilles, who is [[short-lived]] and [[swift-fated]]. In Nestor's speech the word measures mortal praise: Theseus was “like the immortals” ([[like-the-immortals]], 265).
