## Meaning

ταλασίφρων = the root of τλῆναι (“endure, dare”), as in τάλας, + φρήν (“mind, heart”): “of enduring mind, steadfast.” In the Odyssey it is one of Odysseus' standing epithets, in the genitive formula Ὀδυσσῆος ταλασίφρονος. The Iliad has it twice: of Odysseus, when Menelaus hears his cry for help in Book 11 (Il. 11.466), and in Book 4 of no one in particular. It belongs with πολύτλας (“much-enduring”), another title of Odysseus. Compare [[of-many-wiles]] and [[of-many-devices]].

## Use

{{quote:827520e1-cbef-5a88-9132-e8891080fd88}}

Diomedes has kept silent under Agamemnon's rebuke (4.401–402) and silenced Sthenelus (4.411–418). His answer is the sound of his armor. The narrator measures it by an imagined witness: even a man of enduring heart would have been afraid. The epithet that the Odyssey keeps for its patient hero is given here to an unnamed onlooker, and makes the fear general ([[diomedes]], [[silence]], [[well-joined]]).
