## Meaning

ἀμφίφαλος = ἀμφί (“on both sides”) + φάλος, a part of the helmet: the ridge on which Menelaus' sword shattered (κόρυθος φάλον, 3.362). τετραφάληρος = τετρα- (“four”) + φάλαρα, metal plates or bosses on a helmet. What exactly the φάλος and the φάλαρα were, ridges, horns or bosses, is not known, and the translation's “two ridges and four plates” is one reading of the words. The whole verse κρατὶ δʼ ἐπʼ ἀμφίφαλον κυνέην θέτο τετραφάληρον describes two helmets in two armings, Athena's (5.743) and Agamemnon's (Il. 11.41). The shorter τετράφαλος describes the helmet crushed by Ajax's stone (Il. 12.384) and the helmet of Achilles, with its waving crest, as he faces Hector (Il. 22.314–315). See [[helmet]] and [[arming-scene]].

## Use

{{quote:44fc7188-f42c-5697-ab35-ffc3aeddb7a6}}

The helmet comes after the aegis in the arming of Athena ([[tasseled]], [[aegis]]). It is golden, and “fitted with the foot soldiers of a hundred cities”: figures of soldiers worked on it, or, in an ancient explanation, a helmet large enough to cover them. The two technical epithets of the first verse describe a warrior's helmet, and the second verse makes it a god's. The verse is used again for the helmet of Agamemnon, whose arming begins his own great day in Book 11 (Il. 11.41; [[the-aristeia]]).
