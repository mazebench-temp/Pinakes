## Meaning

ἀντιάνειρα = ἀντι- (“against; matching”) + ἀνήρ, stem ἀνερ- (“man”), with a feminine ending. The prefix allows two readings, “a match for men” or “hostile to men,” and both have their defenders. The translation takes the first. The formation is the same as in βωτιάνειρα ([[nurse-of-men]]) and κυδιάνειρα ([[where-men-win-glory]]), and the first element as in ἀντίθεος ([[antitheos]]). Homer uses the word only of the Amazons (Il. 3.189, 6.186).

## Use

{{quote:43d336c4-6b97-525f-9ada-81032819edd0}}

Priam remembers his youth, when he went as an ally to the Phrygians on the Sangarius and fought beside them against the Amazons ([[priam-and-the-amazons]]). He had never seen so many men, and yet the Achaean host below the wall is greater. The Amazons appear once more in the Iliad, in Glaucus' story of Bellerophon, who killed them (Il. 6.186).

## Outside Homer

In the *Aethiopis*, as Proclus summarizes it, the Amazon Penthesileia came to help the Trojans after Hector's death and was killed by Achilles.
