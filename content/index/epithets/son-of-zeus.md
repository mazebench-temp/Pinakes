## Meaning

Διός, genitive of Ζεύς + υἱός (“son”). Parentage works as a title: to be Zeus' son is to share in his authority.

## Use

{{quote:09e67889-fbde-5357-aa03-0c6ed4018307}}

The narrator's question is answered with a genealogy instead of a name: “the son of Leto and of Zeus.” Apollo's name follows only five lines later ({{ref:Iliad 1.14}}).

{{quote:94354377-6c3d-516e-8c15-4b0c0a64ef6c}}

Chryses ends his plea with the same parentage. To respect the priest is to revere the son of Zeus, so Agamemnon's refusal is an offense against Zeus' own family. Leto receives her epithet in the same episode, as Chryses begins to pray ([[lovely-haired]], {{ref:Iliad 1.36}}).
