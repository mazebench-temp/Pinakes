## Meaning

παρθενοπίπης = παρθένος (“girl, maiden”) + the root of ὀπιπεύω (“peer at, ogle”): “one who leers at girls.” It occurs only here, at the end of a verse made of insults. τοξότα, “archer,” is a reproach in itself: the bowman strikes from a distance and from cover, and Diomedes goes on to say that Paris' bow would not help him if he made trial of him face to face in armor (11.386–387; [[bow]]). λωβητήρ, “insulter,” is the man who does outrage (λώβη; [[lobe]]): the army calls Thersites a “word-slinging slanderer” (λωβητῆρα ἐπεσβόλον, 2.275), and Priam drives the Trojans from his portico as λωβητῆρες ἐλεγχέες, “disgraceful wretches” (24.239). κέρᾳ ἀγλαέ is “glorying in your bow,” κέρας being the bow, made of horn like Pandarus' bow of ibex horn (4.105–111); an explanation reported in antiquity takes κέρας as hair dressed in a horn-like lock, “proud of your curl,” which would suit the insult that follows. Compare Hector's insults of Paris, “Evil Paris, best in looks, mad for women, seducer” (3.39; [[evil-paris]], [[best-in-looks]]).

## Use

{{quote:a6ffc63c-4238-5917-b674-f548de2070f9}}

Paris has hit Diomedes in the flat of the right foot from behind the pillar on the tomb of Ilus, and has boasted that the Trojans shudder at him as goats shudder at a lion (11.369–383; [[bleating]]). Diomedes answers “unafraid” (11.384): the wound is a scratch, the shaft of a weakling is blunt, and when his own spear strikes a man, the man's wife tears her cheeks and the birds gather round him ([[torn-in-mourning]]). Then Odysseus stands in front of him while he draws the arrow out, and the pain sends him to the ships (11.396–400). He is next seen among the wounded kings who come up from the ships to meet Nestor (14.27–29). See [[paris]] and [[diomedes]].
