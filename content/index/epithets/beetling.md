## Meaning

ὀφρύς (“brow”) with the adjectival suffix -όεις: “with a brow,” set on the brow of a height. Homer uses ὀφρύς of a hill once, when the gods who favor the Trojans sit “on the brows of Callicolone” (20.151; [[callicolone]]). The translation renders the adjective “beetling.” Homer has it once.

## Use

{{quote:1cff4163-7ecb-513e-9a83-b73759fef637}}

The wailing for Hector fills the city, and the narrator compares it with the cry of a city on fire: “And it was most like as if all / beetling Ilios were smoldering with fire from top to bottom” (22.410–411). The burning is only a likeness, but it looks ahead to the fall of the city that Hector's death makes certain (see [[the-burning-city]] and [[sack-of-troy-foretold]]). See [[troy]].

{{notes}}

## Outside Homer

The oracle that Herodotus reports about the birth of Cypselus, tyrant of Corinth, speaks to the Corinthians “who dwell around fair Peirene and beetling Corinth,” ὀφρυόεντα Κόρινθον (Herodotus 5.92β.3).
