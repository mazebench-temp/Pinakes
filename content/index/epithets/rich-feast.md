## Meaning

θάλεια is formed from θάλλω (“bloom, flourish”), the root of θαλερός ([[flourishing]]) and of θάλος (“shoot”): “flourishing, abundant.” With δαίς (“feast, portion,” [[feast]]) it names a feast of plenty. Homer uses the phrase four times. Here the Achaeans set out a rich feast when the wine comes from Lemnos (7.475). In the Odyssey Nestor, preparing a sacrifice, says that Athena came to him manifestly “to the rich feast of the god” (Od. 3.420); the Phaeacian singer Demodocus sings of the quarrel of Odysseus and Achilles “at a rich feast of the gods” (Od. 8.76); and Alcinous calls the lyre “the companion of the rich feast” (Od. 8.99).

## Use

The day of the wall ends with supper and the arrival of ships from Lemnos:

{{quote:81427023-1f12-5ac5-beff-c7992af3f2b0}}

The Achaeans buy the wine with bronze and iron, hides, cattle and slaves, and then “they set out a rich feast” (7.472–475; [[wine]], [[trade]]). The feast lasts all night, in the camp and in the city, and all night Zeus thunders over it (7.476–482; [[pale-fear]]). The epithet of plenty stands at the end of a day of labor and at the start of a night of foreboding. See [[euneus]] and [[lemnos]].
