## Meaning

κεραός is formed from κέρας (“horn”): “horned,” of a stag “antlered.” The phrase ἔλαφον κεραόν belongs to the language of the hunt ([[deer]]).

## Use

{{quote:a4e1ce0a-6707-58b9-985d-215c02ba8603}}

In the first simile of the duel Menelaus, catching sight of Paris, rejoices like a hungry lion that comes upon the carcass of an antlered stag or a wild goat and devours it, though dogs and young men rush at him ([[lion-on-a-carcass]]). The half-verse ἢ ἔλαφον κεραὸν ἢ ἄγριον αἶγα returns in a simile of hunters and dogs in Book 15 (Il. 15.271).
