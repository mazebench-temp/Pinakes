## Meaning

ἱερή, “sacred” ([[hieros]]), + ἴς, “strength, force,” + the genitive of the name: “the sacred strength of Telemachus,” for Telemachus himself. The Odyssey names Alcinous in the same way, ἱερὸν μένος Ἀλκινόοιο, “the sacred might of Alcinous” (*Odyssey* 7.167, 8.2, 8.4, 8.385, 8.421, 13.20, 13.24), and once Antinous (*Odyssey* 18.34). The *Iliad* has such periphrases with βίη, as for Heracles ([[might-of-heracles]]), and uses ἴς of the strength of a river and of the wind (21.356, 17.739). ἱερός in these phrases is one of the uses of the word that are hard to explain as “holy” (see [[hieros]]); the translation keeps “sacred.”

## Use

It always ends the verse, after a verb: five times after μετέειφʼ, “spoke among them,” when Telemachus speaks to his crew on the shore of Ithaca (*Odyssey* 2.409), to the beggar before his fight with Irus and to the suitors at the end of a day's feasting (*Odyssey* 18.60, 18.405), and twice in the contest of the bow (*Odyssey* 21.101, 21.130); once when he smiles at his father and avoids the swineherd's eye (*Odyssey* 16.476), and once when he hears the singer Phemius beg for his life (*Odyssey* 22.354). See [[telemachus]].

## Outside Homer

The critic Longinus, as Proclus reports, judged a periphrasis of Plato's no different from this one and from “the might of Heracles” (see [[longinus-on-the-sacred-strength-of-telemachus]]).
