## Meaning

καταχθόνιος = κατά (“down, beneath”) + χθών (“earth”): “beneath the earth.” With Ζεύς it makes Hades the Zeus of the world below, the king of the dead as Zeus is king of the gods above. Homer's Hades is the brother of Zeus and Poseidon, who drew the murky darkness as his share when the three divided the world (15.187–193), and he rules with Persephone. The phrase occurs only in Phoenix's speech (9.457); elsewhere Hades is named, or called “Hades the gatekeeper” ([[gatekeeper]]), “implacable and untamable” ([[implacable]], [[untamable]]). Compare Althaea's prayer in the same speech, “calling on Hades and dread Persephone” (9.569; [[dread]]).

## Use

{{quote:21febacc-4a4d-5963-ad7e-e7e355e1b4c4}}

Amyntor curses his son Phoenix, calling on the hateful Erinyes that he may never set on his knees a son born of him, and the gods fulfill the curse, “Zeus of the underworld and dread Persephone” ([[amyntor]], [[erinyes]], [[hateful]]). A father's curse is heard by the powers below, as a mother's is in the story of Meleager (9.566–572). Phoenix, childless, made Achilles his son instead (9.492–495; [[phoenix]], [[the-flight-of-phoenix]]).

## Outside Homer

Hesiod tells the farmer to pray, when he begins to plow, “to Zeus of the earth and to holy Demeter” (Διὶ χθονίῳ Δημήτερί θʼ ἁγνῇ, *Works and Days* 465–466); whether that Zeus is the god of the underworld or Zeus himself in his care for the soil is disputed. In the *Theogony* the halls of Hades and Persephone are “the echoing halls of the god below the earth” (θεοῦ χθονίου, *Theogony* 767–768). In Aeschylus' *Suppliants* Danaus says that a wrongdoer does not escape his guilt even in Hades, where “another Zeus among the dead” passes the final judgment (*Suppliants* 228–231).
