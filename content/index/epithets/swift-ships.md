## Meaning

θοός means “quick, nimble.” It describes ships, warriors, and night (θοὴ νύξ). In one Odyssean phrase, νῆσοι θοαί, it may mean instead “pointed,” and some have looked for that sense elsewhere; of ships, “swift” is the natural reading.

## Use

Occurrences: {{ref:Iliad 1.12}}, {{ref:Iliad 1.300}}, {{ref:Iliad 1.308}}, {{ref:Iliad 1.371}}, {{ref:Iliad 1.389}}.

{{quote:6bfc2cbf-c233-5a44-937b-2ed31ce496ff}}

The phrase θοὰς ἐπὶ νῆας Ἀχαιῶν (12, repeated in Achilles' retelling at 371) sets the scene for the whole book: the camp by the beached ships, where Chryses comes, where the assembly meets, and where Achilles sits. At 308 and 389 the adjective describes the single ship that takes Chryseis home. At 300 Achilles joins it to another epithet: “my swift black ship” ([[black-ships]]).

The swiftness of the beached fleet is always a possibility more than a fact. The ships are the means of going home, and Achilles threatens to use them ({{ref:Iliad 1.169-170}}; [[curved-ships]]).
