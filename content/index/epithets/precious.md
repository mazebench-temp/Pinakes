## Meaning

ἐρίτιμος = ἐρι- (an intensive prefix, “very,” as in [[deep-soiled]], ἐριβῶλαξ) + τιμή (“honor, worth”; [[time]]): “of great worth.” Homer uses it four times: twice of the aegis, which Athena holds as she goes through the army, “highly prized, ageless and immortal” (2.447), and which Apollo holds when he breaks down the Achaean wall (15.361; [[aegis]]); and twice of gold, in the same verse of Agamemnon's offer and Odysseus' repetition of it (9.126, 9.268). The Homeric Hymn to Apollo uses it of the tripods in the god's shrine at Pytho (*Hymn* 3.443).

## Use

{{quote:f456ba10-2d88-516b-93b0-966232bfb778}}

τιμή is both a thing's worth and a man's honor, and Agamemnon's gifts are meant to pay for the second with the first. A man who owned the prizes his horses have won would not be “lacking in precious gold” (9.126). Achilles answers that no amount of it could buy him, not ten or twenty times as much, not as much as the sand and the dust (9.379–387; [[honor-and-dishonor]]).
