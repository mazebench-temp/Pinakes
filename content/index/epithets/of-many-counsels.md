## Meaning

πολύβουλος = πολύς (“much, many”) + βουλή (“counsel, plan”; [[boule]]): “of many counsels.” Homer uses it twice, both times of Athena and both times in the verse after the same formula of instruction, ἄλλο δέ τοι ἐρέω, σὺ δʼ ἐνὶ φρεσὶ βάλλεο σῇσιν, “And I will tell you something else, and you lay it to heart” ([[lay-it-to-heart]]). Here Diomedes speaks it to Sthenelus (5.259–260). In the Odyssey Odysseus speaks it to Telemachus, “whenever Athena of many counsels puts it in my mind” to strike at the suitors (Od. 16.281–283). The goddess of counsel shares the language of her favorite, πολύμητις Ὀδυσσεύς ([[of-many-wiles]]).

## Use

{{quote:46fdb7b7-b210-5128-ac7b-43632df2a903}}

Diomedes plans for the capture of Aeneas' horses before he has fought for them, and he counts on the goddess for the glory. The phrase κῦδος ὀρέξῃ (“hold out glory”) is used elsewhere in Book 5 of Zeus: Athena herself proposes to leave the Trojans and Achaeans to fight it out and see “to which side father Zeus will hold out glory” (5.33), and Aeneas fears that Zeus may hold out glory to Diomedes again (5.225; [[kydos]]). Diomedes expects it from Athena. His plan is carried out: while Aphrodite carries Aeneas from the field, Sthenelus remembers his instructions and drives the horses to the ships (5.319–327; [[sthenelus]], [[horses]]).
