## Meaning

κλυτότοξος = κλυτός (“famous,” from the root of κλέος; [[kleos]]) + τόξον (“bow”; [[bow]]): “famed for his bow.” It is formed like κλυτοτέχνης, the title of Hephaestus ([[famed-for-his-craft]]), and it names Apollo by his weapon, as ἀργυρότοξος does ([[god-of-the-silver-bow]]). Homer gives it to Apollo alone: in Pandarus' prayer (4.101, 4.119), when Zeus sends for Iris and Apollo in Book 15 (Il. 15.55), and twice in the Odyssey, when Penelope wishes that Apollo famed for his bow would strike down Antinous (Od. 17.494), and when the suitors plan to sacrifice to him before they try the bow of Odysseus (Od. 21.265–268).

## Use

{{quote:62046cc7-8f34-5a58-853b-51bbda20bdcd}}

The archer is told to pray to the archer god, and the prayer and its vow are repeated in the narrative as he draws (4.119–121; [[lycian-born]]). The vow is the archer's proper act. At the funeral games of Patroclus the same line decides the archery contest: Teucer shoots without promising Apollo “a glorious hecatomb of firstborn lambs” and misses the dove, and Meriones makes the vow and hits it (Il. 23.859–883). Pandarus' prayer does not bring him what he wants. Athena turns his arrow so that it only grazes Menelaus (4.127–140; [[driver-of-spoil]]).
