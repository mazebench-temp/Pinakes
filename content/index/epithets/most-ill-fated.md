## Meaning

δυσάμμορος = δυσ- (“ill-,” the prefix of misfortune) + ἄμμορος (“without a share,” and so “ill-fated,” from ἀ- and μόρος, “lot, fate”; [[moira]]): “most ill-fated.” The simple ἄμμορος is Andromache's word for herself, “me, ill-fated” (6.408), and Helen's in her lament for Hector (24.773). The compound occurs four times, all in laments for the dead: Achilles addresses the dead Patroclus as δυσάμμορε (19.315); Priam wishes that Hector had died in his arms, so that “his mother, most ill-fated, who bore him,” and he himself might have had their fill of weeping (22.426–428); and Andromache twice speaks of the child “whom you and I begot, both most ill-fated” (22.485, 24.727). See [[lament]].
