## Meaning

Λαερτιάδης = Λαέρτης + the patronymic suffix -ιάδης: “son of Laertes.” Laertes is still alive in the Odyssey, an old man working his farm on Ithaca. In the Iliad he is named only through his son.

The vocative belongs to a whole-line address: διογενὲς Λαερτιάδη, πολυμήχαν’ Ὀδυσσεῦ, “son of Laertes, sprung from Zeus, Odysseus of many devices” ([[sprung-from-zeus]], [[of-many-devices]]). It occurs twenty-one times in Homer, seven in the Iliad and fourteen in the Odyssey, and it is spoken by gods and men alike when they address Odysseus with full formality.

## Use

{{quote:2120afa7-03c3-5a09-b697-ecb38d557968}}

Athena uses the full address when she comes to Odysseus as the army flees. The formal naming, father, divine descent, and his own quality of resource, sets the man she speaks to against the picture she paints of him: a man tumbling into his ship to run home.
