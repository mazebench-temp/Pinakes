## Meaning

χρυσάωρ = χρυσός (“gold”) + ἄορ (“sword”): “with a golden sword.” The Iliad gives it to Apollo twice: here, and in Book 15, when he comes to Hector, still dazed from Ajax's stone, and names himself to him as the helper Zeus has sent, “Phoebus Apollo of the golden sword” (Il. 15.254–257). In the Iliad Apollo is never seen with a sword. His weapon is the bow ([[god-of-the-silver-bow]], [[far-shooter]]), and in Book 15 he carries the aegis of Zeus. The things of the gods are golden ([[gold-and-silver]]).

## Use

{{quote:d796b866-0068-524a-9309-9bec6ba19361}}

The epithet comes as Apollo's orders take effect. He has seen that Athena, the Danaans' helper, has left the field, and it is he who has told Ares to wake the spirit of the Trojans ([[ares]], [[phoebus]]). The god of the golden sword directs the god of war, while he himself sends Aeneas back healed from his shrine and puts might into his breast (5.512–513; [[menos]], [[aeneas]]).

## Outside Homer

Hesiod gives the epithet to Apollo, whom Leto bore on the seventh day of the month (*Works and Days* 770–771). In the *Theogony* it is a name: Chrysaor sprang with the horse Pegasus from Medusa when Perseus cut off her head, and was so called because he held a golden sword (*Theogony* 280–283). The Homeric Hymns use it of Apollo (Hymn to Apollo 123, 395) and of Demeter (Hymn to Demeter 4), and an oracle quoted by Herodotus of Artemis (Herodotus 8.77).
