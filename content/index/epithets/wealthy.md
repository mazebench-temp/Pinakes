## Meaning

ἀφνειός is formed from ἄφενος (“wealth, abundance”), a word of uncertain origin: “rich, wealthy.” Homer uses it of men rich in goods, often with a genitive of what they have (ἀφνειὸς βιότοιο, “rich in livelihood”), of houses, and of cities.

## Use

{{quote:7a238aed-ed1f-56ca-844e-541687b93c49}}

Corinth, in Agamemnon's realm, is the only city in the Catalogue called simply wealthy. The Trojan Catalogue gives the same word to a people: the Trojans of Zeleia, under Mount Ida, are “wealthy men who drink the dark water of the Aesepus” ({{ref:Iliad 2.824-826}}). Their leader is Pandarus, the archer whose bow Apollo gave him ([[the-bow-of-pandarus]]).

## Outside Homer

Thucydides uses the epithet as evidence. Corinth, he says, was a trading place from early times, commanding the traffic over the Isthmus, and so grew rich, “as the old poets show, for they called the place wealthy” (1.13.5).
