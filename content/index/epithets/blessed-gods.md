## Meaning

μάκαρ means “blessed, happy,” the state of those who are free from care and death. It is the gods' condition above all. In Book 24 Achilles tells Priam that the gods have spun a life of grief for wretched mortals, while they themselves are without care (Il. 24.525–526).

## Use

The three occurrences span the range of the gods in Book 1. They are witnesses: Achilles calls the heralds to witness “before the blessed gods and before mortal men” ({{ref:Iliad 1.339}}; [[mortal-men]]). They are frightened: in Achilles' story, “the blessed gods shrank in fear” of Briareus ({{ref:Iliad 1.406}}). And they laugh:

The last use is the most characteristic. The laughter that ends the quarrel on Olympus is the laughter of beings who can afford to laugh ([[unquenchable-laughter]]), while below them the plan agreed with Thetis will bring death to many.
