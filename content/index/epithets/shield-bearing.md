## Meaning

ἀσπιστής and ἀσπιδιώτης are both formed from ἀσπίς (“shield”; [[shield]]) with agent suffixes: “a man with a shield.” Like [[spearmen]] (αἰχμητής), they name warriors by their equipment. The Catalogue uses the second when it praises Menestheus of Athens as the best of all men “at marshaling horses and shield-bearing men” ({{ref:Iliad 2.553-554}}). The first is used in the genitive plural ἀσπιστάων, of ranks (στίχες) and of peoples: the Paphlagonians (Il. 5.577), and the Lycians of Sarpedon and Glaucus, each “leader of the shield-bearing Lycians” (Il. 16.490, 16.593).

## Use

{{quote:b02ce3d1-91a0-5730-b3e8-72823bc556af}}

The same two lines describe how Athena finds Pandarus and how Talthybius finds Machaon (4.90–91, 4.201–202): each man stands surrounded by “the strong ranks of shield-bearing men” who followed him from home, from the Aesepus and from horse-pasturing Tricca ([[horse-pasturing]], [[krateros]]). The archer who wounds Menelaus and the healer who treats him are found in the same words. While the Achaeans are busy around the wounded man, “the ranks of the shield-bearing Trojans came on” (4.221), a verse that returns in Book 11 (Il. 11.412). See [[pandarus]], [[machaon]].
