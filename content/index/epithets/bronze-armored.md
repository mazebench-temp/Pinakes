## Meaning

χαλκοχίτων = χαλκός (“bronze”) + χιτών (“tunic”): “with a tunic of bronze,” that is, armored in bronze. The “bronze tunic” is probably the corslet (θώρηξ). A full bronze body-armor of the Mycenaean age has been found at Dendra in the Argolid. The Trojans too are called χαλκοχίτωνες elsewhere in the Iliad (for example Il. 5.180).

Achilles' account repeats the narrator's lines almost word for word ([[retelling]]), but his sentence begins differently. The narrator's verse opened with Ἀτρεΐδης, carried over from the line before: Ἀτρεΐδης· ὃ γὰρ ἦλθε θοὰς ἐπὶ νῆας Ἀχαιῶν ({{ref:Iliad 1.12}}). Achilles' verse starts at ἦλθε, and the epithet χαλκοχιτώνων fills out the line at the end. A small instance of how formulas expand and contract to fit the verse.
