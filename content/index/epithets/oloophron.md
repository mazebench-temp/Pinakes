## Meaning

ὀλοόφρων = ὀλοός (“destructive, deadly”; [[deadly]]) + φρήν (“mind”; [[phrenes]]): “with a destructive mind,” bent on harm. The translation renders it “deadly.” In the Iliad it is said of beasts: the water snake whose bite left Philoctetes in agony on Lemnos (2.721–723; [[philoctetes]], [[water-snake]]), the lion in the simile of Hector's onset (15.630; [[lion]]), and the boar, whose spirit is the greatest of all beasts, in Menelaus' words to Euphorbus (17.19–22; [[boar]]). In the Odyssey it is given to Atlas, the father of Calypso, to Aeetes, the brother of Circe, and to Minos (*Odyssey* 1.52, 10.137, 11.322). Compare ὀλοιῇσι φρεσί, the “ruinous mind” of Agamemnon in Achilles' words (1.342; [[deadly]]).
