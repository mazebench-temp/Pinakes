## Meaning

κελαδεινός is from κέλαδος, “din, clamor.” After the hunt of the Calydonian boar it was Artemis who “raised around it a great din and shouting / over the boar's head and its shaggy hide” (9.547–548), and the related participle κελάδων is said of a roaring river (18.576, 21.16) and of the West Wind (*Odyssey* 2.421). Of Artemis the epithet names the goddess of the noisy hunt. In the Iliad it is joined twice with [[of-the-golden-distaff]] (χρυσηλάκατος), in the story of Eudorus' birth (16.183) and when Artemis takes her stand against Hera in the battle of the gods (20.70); once it stands alone for her name, when she comes weeping to Zeus after Hera has beaten her (21.511). In the masculine it is the epithet of the West Wind, whom Achilles calls with the North Wind to kindle Patroclus' pyre (23.208; [[zephyrus]]). See [[artemis]].

## Outside Homer

In the *Homeric Hymn to Aphrodite* Artemis “of the golden distaff, the clamorous” loves the bow, the hunt in the mountains, the lyre, the dance and piercing cries (*Hymn to Aphrodite* 16–20). The *Hymn to Hermes* gives the adjective to the echoing glens through which the newborn god drives Apollo's cattle (*Hymn to Hermes* 95).
