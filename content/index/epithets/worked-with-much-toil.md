## Meaning

πολύκμητος = πολύς (“much”) + the root of κάμνω (“toil, labor,” aorist κάμον, “made with labor”): “wrought with much labor.” It describes iron in a whole verse of treasure, χαλκός τε χρυσός τε πολύκμητός τε σίδηρος, “bronze and gold and iron worked with much toil” (Il. 6.48, 10.379, 11.133; Od. 14.324, 21.10). In Homer iron is the harder metal, used for tools and kept as treasure, while weapons are mostly of bronze ([[iron]], [[bronze]]). Once the adjective describes a well-built chamber, on whose threshold Penelope sits in her grief (Od. 4.718).

## Use

Adrastus lists the treasures that lie in his father's house: “bronze and gold and iron worked with much toil” (6.48), from which his father would pay a boundless ransom if he heard that his son was alive at the ships ([[boundless-ransom]], [[adrastus-the-trojan]]). The same verse is spoken by Dolon (Il. 10.379) and by the sons of Antimachus (Il. 11.133), and all three captives are killed. In the Odyssey it lists what lies in the storeroom of Odysseus' house, where Penelope goes to fetch his bow (Od. 21.8–12), and, in a lying tale, the treasure that Odysseus was said to have gathered (Od. 14.323–324). In the Iliad the verse always names treasure far away in a father's house, offered for a life that is not spared.
