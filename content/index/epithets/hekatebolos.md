## Meaning

ἑκατηβόλος has the same second element as ἑκηβόλος, βάλλω (“shoot”), with a longer first element ἑκατη-. Its relation to ἑκάς (“far”) and to the short name ἕκατος ([[hekatos]]) is uncertain. The sense is the same, “the far-shooting god,” and the longer form gives the poet a phrase of another length.

## Use

{{quote:252569cd-2894-5f75-85e4-39354e3f3148}}

In his retelling to Thetis, Achilles introduces Chryses as ἱερεὺς ἑκατηβόλου Ἀπόλλωνος, “priest of Apollo who shoots from afar.” The narrator had called him ἀρητήρ (“one who prays,” {{ref:Iliad 1.11}}). Three lines later Achilles uses the shorter form in a verse he repeats word for word from the narrator (ἑκηβόλου Ἀπόλλωνος, {{ref:Iliad 1.373}} = {{ref:Iliad 1.14}}). The two forms side by side show how an epithet varies to fit the verse ([[retelling]]).
