## Meaning

ἀνθεμόεις = ἄνθεμον (“flower,” from the root of ἄνθος) + the suffix -όεις (“full of”): “flowery.” Homer uses it six times: of meadows, in the Iliad the meadow of the Scamander and in the Odyssey the Sirens' meadow (Od. 12.159); of the Thessalian town Pyrasus; and of a cauldron and mixing bowls “with flowers” worked on them (Il. 23.885; Od. 3.440, 24.275).

## Use

{{quote:2522049d-cd1a-5c03-85ae-944d6fc34c5c}}

The army pours out onto the plain and stands in the flowering meadow of the Scamander, “numberless, as the leaves and flowers that grow in their season.” The epithet leads straight into the simile ([[leaves-and-flowers]]): the flowers of the meadow become an image of the men standing among them. Many of those men will die on this plain.

In the Catalogue the epithet is given to Pyrasus in Thessaly, “the precinct of Demeter” ({{ref:Iliad 2.695-696}}).
