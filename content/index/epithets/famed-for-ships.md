## Meaning

ναυσικλειτός = ναῦς (“ship”) + κλειτός (“famous”; [[famous]]): “famous for ships.” The Odyssey has a twin compound with κλυτός, ναυσίκλυτος, “famed for ships,” always of seafaring peoples: the Phaeacians (*Odyssey* 7.39, 8.191, 8.369, 13.166, 16.227) and the Phoenicians (*Odyssey* 15.415). Compare [[lovers-of-the-oar]] (φιλήρετμος), another epithet of the Phaeacians.

## Use

ναυσικλειτός is used once, at the beginning of Book 6 of the Odyssey: Athena comes to Nausicaa's bed “in the likeness of the daughter of Dymas, famed for ships” (*Odyssey* 6.22). See [[dymas-the-phaeacian]] and [[phaeacians]].

## Outside Homer

The *Homeric Hymn to Apollo* gives the epithet to Euboea, among the lands to which Leto came in her labor, ναυσικλειτή τʼ Εὔβοια (*Hymn to Apollo* 31), and again when Apollo crosses to it in search of a site for his temple, ναυσικλειτῆς Εὐβοίης (*Hymn to Apollo* 219).
