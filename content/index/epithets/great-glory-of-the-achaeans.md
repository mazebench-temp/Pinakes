## Meaning

μέγα (“great”) + κῦδος (“glory,” the radiance of success that a god gives; [[kydos]]) + Ἀχαιῶν (“of the Achaeans”): a man who is himself the glory of his people. The phrase closes a verse of address and is used of two men only. Nestor hears it in the verse ὦ Νέστορ Νηληϊάδη, μέγα κῦδος Ἀχαιῶν, “Nestor, son of Neleus, great glory of the Achaeans,” from Agamemnon (10.87, 14.42), Odysseus (10.555), Idomeneus (11.511) and, in the Odyssey, from Telemachus (Od. 3.79, 3.202; [[son-of-neleus]]). Odysseus hears it in the verse with [[much-praised]], from Agamemnon (9.673), from Nestor (10.544) and from the Sirens (Od. 12.184). Both men are honored for counsel and speech more than for strength.

## Use

{{quote:feb57755-2cd4-5809-a9d4-93a016cd9ebb}}

Agamemnon gives Odysseus this title as he asks whether Achilles will defend the ships or still refuses (9.673–675). It is the first time in the poem that anyone is called “great glory of the Achaeans.” The glory of the Achaeans has gone to ask Achilles for his help, and the answer he brings is no.
