## Meaning

ἐρεβεννός is formed from Ἔρεβος, the darkness under the earth through which the dead go down to Hades (8.368; [[erebus]]): “dark as Erebus.” In the Iliad it describes night and clouds. Night: the night that covers the eyes of a man who dies, in a formula of death in battle (5.659, 13.580, 22.466; [[darkness-covered-his-eyes]]); the night in which Idomeneus longs to wrap a Trojan, that is, to kill him (13.424–425); the night that falls on the battle in Book 8; and the tenth night, on which Phoenix escaped from his father's house, in a half-verse that repeats the one here (9.474). Clouds: the murky mist out of the clouds that Diomedes sees as Ares goes up to heaven (5.864–867), and the murky clouds through which an eagle swoops down to the plain (22.308–309).

## Use

At sunset “to the Achaeans welcome, thrice prayed for, came the murky night” (8.488; [[thrice-prayed-for]], [[black-night]]). The darkness of the dead is the darkness of the night that saves the living army. See [[war-and-night]].

## Outside Homer

Hesiod gives the epithet to Night as a goddess: “murky Night” bears her children without a father (*Theogony* 213), and “the dread house of murky Night” stands wrapped in dark clouds, before the place where the son of Iapetus holds up the wide sky and where Night and Day greet each other as they cross the bronze threshold (*Theogony* 744–750; [[iapetus]]).
