## Meaning

παμφανόων is a participle of παμφαίνω (“shine brightly”), an intensive form of φαίνω (“shine”) with a first syllable taken as πᾶν (“all”): “gleaming all over.” Book 5 has the verb in its first simile, of the star of late summer that “shines out bright” when it has bathed in Oceanus (λαμπρὸν παμφαίνῃσι, 5.6; [[the-autumn-star]]). The participle describes bronze armor (τεύχεα, ἔντεα: 5.295; Il. 9.596, 15.120, 18.144), spears (5.619), a chariot (Il. 8.320), the polished walls of a hall (Il. 8.435, 13.261), cauldrons (Il. 23.613), and fire (Il. 18.206, 21.349). In Book 2 it is the gleam of the army's bronze that goes up to the sky (2.458; [[forest-fire]]). In 5.295 it is joined with αἰόλος (“quick-moving, flashing”), the adjective in [[of-the-gleaming-helmet]] (κορυθαίολος) and [[panaiolos]].

## Use

Book 5 uses it twice, both times of arms around a dead man. Pandarus falls from his chariot:

{{quote:9d0e8c0a-8372-5c3b-9a29-30e42e534ae8}}

The formula of the fall ([[fell-with-a-thud]]) is extended by the brightness of the armor that clatters on him. The horses shy, and he dies ([[swift-footed]], [[psyche]]). When Ajax kills Amphius and runs up to strip him, “the Trojans showered spears on him, sharp and gleaming; and his shield caught many” (5.618–619; [[sharp]], [[amphius-son-of-selagus]]).
