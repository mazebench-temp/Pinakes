## Meaning

λαιψηρός means “light, nimble, quick.” In the Iliad it describes the knees and feet of a man who runs: the formula λαιψηρὰ (πόδας καὶ) γούνατʼ ἐνώμα is used of Dolon, who “plied his nimble knees” to flee (10.358), of Hector, who runs back to battle like a horse loosed from its stall (15.263–269), of Achilles speeding toward the city like a racehorse (22.21–24), and of Hector fleeing before him under the wall of Troy (22.143–144). A god “roused might and nimble knees” in Aeneas (20.92–93) and in Hector (22.203–204). It describes the swift paths of the winds (14.17, 15.620), the arrows of Apollo by which Thetis foretold that Achilles would die (21.277–278), and Achilles himself, whom the river overtakes “nimble though he is” (21.263–264). Compare [[swift]], [[swift-footed]] and [[podarkes]].

## Use

{{quote:e4da7539-1c8d-546e-949a-29c4020eaa53}}

Dolon stops when he hears the two men running behind him, thinking they are Trojans sent to call him back; when they are a spear's throw away, he knows them for enemies and runs (10.354–359; [[dolon]]). The half-verse λαιψηρὰ δὲ γούνατʼ ἐνώμα returns word for word when Hector, terrified at the sight of Achilles, runs from him beneath the wall (22.143–144). Dolon, who was swift-footed (10.316; [[swift-footed]]), is run down by Diomedes and Odysseus, as Hector will be by Achilles. See [[diomedes]].
