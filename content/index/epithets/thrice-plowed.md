## Meaning

τρίπολος = τρι- (“three times”) + πολέω (“turn,” of the soil): “turned three times.” On the shield of Achilles Hephaestus sets “a soft fallow field, rich plowland, / broad, thrice plowed” (18.541–542; [[the-plowing]]). Walter Leaf takes the scene, after Ridgeway, for the plowing of the common field, begun by the whole community on the same day; the field is a fallow because the land was tilled on a two-field system, and τρίπολον probably means that it was plowed three times before being sown (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 18.541). In the Odyssey Calypso recalls how Demeter lay with Iasion “in a thrice-plowed fallow” (νειῷ ἔνι τριπόλῳ), and Zeus struck him down with his thunderbolt (*Odyssey* 5.125–128).

## Outside Homer

Hesiod tells the same story in the same words: Demeter bore Plutus after lying with Iasion in a thrice-plowed fallow in the rich land of Crete (*Theogony* 969–971).
