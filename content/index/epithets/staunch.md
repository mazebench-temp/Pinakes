## Meaning

μενεδήϊος = μένω (“stand fast, await”) + δήϊος (“enemy”): “standing fast against the enemy.” It is formed like μενεπτόλεμος and μενεχάρμης, “steadfast in battle” ([[steadfast-in-battle]]), with the enemy in place of war or battle. Homer has it twice, both times in the nominative.

## Use

The two uses face each other across two books. At the ditch Hector, rejecting Polydamas' reading of the eagle, tells him that he need not fear to die at the ships, “for your heart is not staunch against the enemy, nor eager for battle” (12.246–247; [[polydamas]], [[hector]], [[the-eagle-and-the-serpent]]). At the ships Idomeneus answers the man he takes for Thoas: “But, Thoas, since even before this you were staunch against the enemy, and you urge on another man too, wherever you see him holding back, so now do not give up, and call on every man” (13.228–230; [[idomeneus]], [[thoas]]). The praise is true of the Aetolian, and it is spoken to Poseidon, who has taken his voice (13.215–216; [[poseidon]]).
