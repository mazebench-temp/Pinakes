## Meaning

ἀγάρροος = ἀγα- (“very,” as in ἀγακλειτός, [[famous]]) + ῥόος (“stream, current”): “strongly flowing,” for the current that runs through the strait from the Propontis to the Aegean. It is one of four epithets of the Hellespont, beside “broad” (πλατύς; [[broad-hellespont]]), “fish-filled” (ἰχθυόεις, 9.360) and “boundless” (ἀπείρων, 24.545). Homer uses it twice. In the Trojan Catalogue the Thracians are “all those whom the strong-flowing Hellespont encloses” (2.844–845; [[thracians]]), the strait seen from Troy as their boundary. See [[hellespont]].

## Use

{{quote:a4fae4eb-149e-53ed-9dfc-4eaecf4e9666}}

When the Achaeans have gone home, Poseidon carries the foundations of the wall out on the waves “and made all smooth beside the strong-flowing Hellespont” (12.30; [[shaker-of-the-earth]], [[the-achaean-wall]]). The strait by which the Achaeans beached their ships is the place where the wall leaves no trace ([[fame-and-the-tomb]]).
