## Meaning

ἀμαιμάκετος is an obscure word. It is usually understood as “irresistible, not to be fought against,” and has been connected with μαιμάω (“quiver with eagerness, rage”), but its formation is uncertain. Homer uses it three times. Twice it describes the Chimaera: here, and in Book 16, where two companions of Sarpedon are sons of Amisodarus, “who reared the irresistible Chimaera, a bane to many men” (Il. 16.328–329). Once in the Odyssey it describes the mast of a ship, where the sense must be something like “huge, mighty” (Od. 14.311).

## Use

The first task that the king of Lycia sets Bellerophon is to kill her:

The epithet is followed by the monster's description: of divine race and not of men, a lion in front, a serpent behind and a she-goat in the middle, breathing fire ([[chimaera]], [[blazing-fire]], [[lion]], [[serpent]], [[goats]]). What cannot be withstood is killed in a single verse: “And he killed her, obeying the portents of the gods” (6.183; [[bellerophon]]). The irresistible creature is overcome because the gods are on the hero's side, until they turn against him (6.200).
