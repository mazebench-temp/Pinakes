## Meaning

ἐννοσίγαιος = ἔνοσις (“shaking”) + γαῖα (“earth”): “shaker of the earth.” The double ν lengthens the first syllable for the verse. It means the same as ἐνοσίχθων ([[earth-shaker]]), and the two fill different places in the line. ἐννοσίγαιος occurs twenty-six times in Homer, twenty of them in the Iliad. It stands alone as a name for the god, as when “the shaker of the earth himself, holding his trident in his hands,” destroys the Achaean wall (12.27), or with another title: κλυτὸς ἐννοσίγαιος, “the famous shaker of the earth” (for example 8.440, 14.510), and γαιήοχος ἐννοσίγαιος, “the earth-holder, the shaker of the earth” (for example 9.183, 13.43). By the earth-holder, shaker of the earth, Menelaus makes Antilochus swear that he did not foul his chariot on purpose (23.584–585).

The vocative ἐννοσίγαιʼ opens several speeches to Poseidon (7.455; 20.20, 20.310, 21.462), and the address ὢ πόποι, ἐννοσίγαιʼ εὐρυσθενές (“Ah, shaker of the earth, wide in strength”) opens three speeches: Zeus' here, Hera's when she tries to rouse him to help the Achaeans against Zeus' will (8.201–204), and Zeus' again in the Odyssey, where the whole verse is the same as here (Od. 13.140). See [[wide-in-strength]].

## Use

Poseidon has complained that the Achaeans have built their wall without offering the gods hecatombs, and that its fame will outlast the wall he and Apollo built for Laomedon (7.446–453; [[earth-shaker]]). Zeus answers:

{{quote:83653725-580e-5895-a91a-4f274572ffb7}}

The greeting grants his rank before it answers his fear: only a weaker god could be afraid of this device, and his fame will reach as far as the dawn spreads (7.456–458; [[kleos]]). Then Zeus gives him the wall to destroy when the Achaeans have gone home. The Odyssey uses the same greeting in the same situation. Poseidon complains that he will no longer be honored among the gods, since mortals do not honor him, and Zeus answers with this verse and lets him punish the Phaeacians who carried Odysseus home (Od. 13.125–145). Both times the shaker of the earth is soothed with the promise that his honor is safe and with leave to take it back from mortals himself. See [[poseidon]], [[zeus]] and [[the-achaean-wall]].
