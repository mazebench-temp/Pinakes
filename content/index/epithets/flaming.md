## Meaning

φλόγεος is formed from φλόξ (“flame”): “flaming, blazing.” Homer uses it only in the verse ἐς δʼ ὄχεα φλόγεα ποσὶ βήσετο, λάζετο δʼ ἔγχος, “and she stepped onto the flaming chariot, and took up her spear,” said of Athena twice (5.745; Il. 8.389). The chariot is the one Hera harnessed and Hebe put together: wheels of bronze with rims of gold, hubs of silver, a car strung with gold and silver straps, a silver pole and a golden yoke (5.720–732). Its metals flash like fire. See [[chariot]].

## Use

The epithet comes at the moment when Athena, armed, joins Hera in the chariot (5.745; [[august-goddess]], [[with-two-ridges-and-four-plates]]). The next verses describe her spear ([[heavy-spear]], [[daughter-of-a-mighty-father]]). Later she leaves this chariot and takes Sthenelus' place in Diomedes' chariot, whose oaken axle groans under the weight of a goddess and the best of men (5.835–839).
