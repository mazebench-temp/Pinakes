## Meaning

θεόφιν (“to the gods,” an old case form in -φι) + μήστωρ (“deviser, counselor,” from μήδομαι, “devise, plan”) + ἀτάλαντος (“of equal weight,” from τάλαντον, “scale”): “a counselor who weighs as much as the gods.” The image of the scales is the one in [[equal-of-zeus-in-counsel]] (Διὶ μῆτιν ἀτάλαντος) and [[equal-of-ares]] (ἀτάλαντος Ἄρηϊ). μήστωρ appears in two other formulas, [[master-of-rout]] (μήστωρ φόβοιο) and [[masters-of-the-war-cry]] (μήστωρες ἀϋτῆς).

The formula closes the verse five times in Homer: of Priam here (7.366); of Peirithous, whom Ixion's wife bore to Zeus, in Zeus' list of the women he has loved (14.317–318); twice of Patroclus, by Automedon, who says that no one could handle Achilles' immortal horses as Patroclus did while he lived (17.475–478), and by Nestor in the Odyssey, naming the dead who lie at Troy (Od. 3.109–111); and of Nestor's father Neleus, who used to sit on the polished stones before his doors and had gone down to Hades (Od. 3.406–410). Three of the five uses name men who are already dead.

## Use

The Trojan assembly has heard Antenor's advice to give Helen back and Paris' refusal. Then the king rises:

The two titles in 7.366 give Priam his lineage and his wisdom ([[dardanus]]). His counsel is practical. He does not take up Antenor's proposal or overrule his son; he tells the Trojans to take their supper and keep watch, and to send Idaeus at dawn with Paris' offer and a request for a truce to burn the dead (7.370–378). The truce is granted and the offer refused (7.398–411). See [[priam]], [[antenor]], [[paris]] and [[the-truce-for-the-dead]].
