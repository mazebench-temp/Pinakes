## Meaning

ἠπιόδωρος = ἤπιος (“gentle, kindly”) + δῶρον (“gift”): “giving with a kindly hand, bountiful.” The compound occurs only here in Homer. Its first part is the word for a gentle father, as Telemachus remembers his, who “was gentle as a father” to his people (πατὴρ δʼ ὣς ἤπιος ἦεν, Od. 2.47), and for the healer's “soothing drugs,” ἤπια φάρμακα (4.218; [[soothing-drugs]]).

## Use

Hecuba enters the Iliad with this epithet:

{{quote:7d252193-6f88-5d8f-8c22-418b5aa8bdff}}

The word is borne out at once. She offers to bring him honey-sweet wine, so that he may pour a libation to Zeus and drink and be the better for it (6.258–262; [[honey-sweet]], [[libation]]). Hector refuses the gift: wine would unnerve him, and he will not pour to Zeus with hands spattered with blood (6.264–268). He sends her instead to give a gift of her own, the finest of her robes, to Athena, and she does (6.269–295; [[robe]]). The generous mother's gift to her son is refused by her son, and her gift to the goddess is refused by the goddess (6.311). See [[hecuba]] and [[mothers-and-sons]].
