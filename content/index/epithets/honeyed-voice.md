## Meaning

μελίγηρυς = μέλι (“honey”) + γῆρυς (“voice, speech”): “honey-voiced.” Homer has it once, in the Sirens' song: “For no one has ever yet rowed past here in his black ship / before he has heard the honeyed voice from our lips” (*Odyssey* 12.186–187). Their other words for the song are λιγυρὴ ἀοιδή, “clear song” (*Odyssey* 12.44, 12.183; see [[shrill]]), and ὄψ κάλλιμος, “beautiful voice” (*Odyssey* 12.192). Compare [[honey-sweet]] (μελιηδής), the word of the wax that stops the companions' ears against them (*Odyssey* 12.48); [[lovely-voice]] (ὀπὶ καλῇ), the singing of goddesses; and the voice of Nestor, “sweeter than honey” (*Iliad* 1.249; see [[sweet-of-speech]]). See [[sirens]].

## Outside Homer

In the *Hymn to Apollo* the Cretans follow the god to Pytho singing the paean, “such paeans as the Cretans sing, in whose breasts / the Muse, a goddess, has put honey-voiced song” (*Hymn to Apollo* 518–519, a plain rendering); in the *Hymn to Pan* the god's piping is matched only by the bird that pours out its lament in spring, “honey-voiced song” (*Hymn to Pan* 16–18). Cicero renders the Sirens' verses “For no one has ever sailed past these blue waters / without first stopping, caught by the sweetness of the voices” (*vocum dulcedine captus*, *De Finibus* 5.49).
