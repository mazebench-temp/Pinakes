## Meaning

λειριόεις = λείριον (“lily”) + the suffix -όεις: “lily-like.” Homer uses it twice, and the point of the comparison is not the same in both. Of skin it is taken as white and tender: Hector threatens that his spear will tear Ajax's χρόα λειριόεντα, his “lily-white skin” (13.830). Of a voice it is harder to say: the old men on the Trojan wall, like cicadas on a tree, “send out their lily-soft voice” (3.151–152; [[like-cicadas]], [[trojan-elders]]), where the likeness may be of delicacy, of clearness, or of the pleasure the voice gives. The lily itself is among the flowers that Persephone says she was gathering when Hades seized her (*Hymn to Demeter* 427).

## Use

{{quote:a1854acf-024b-5bb2-81a5-b3653daffc41}}

Hector ends his answer to Ajax with a threat: “and you will be killed among them, if you dare to stand up to my long spear, which will tear your lily-white skin; and you will glut the dogs and the birds of the Trojans with your fat and your flesh” (13.829–832; [[hector]], [[ajax]], [[dogs]]). The epithet of tender skin, given to the bulwark of the Achaeans ([[bulwark-of-the-achaeans]]), belongs to the contempt with which the speech began (13.824; [[blundering-in-speech]], [[braggart]]).

## Outside Homer

Hesiod uses the word of the Muses: the halls of Zeus laugh as their lily voice spreads (θεᾶν ὀπὶ λειριοέσσῃ, *Theogony* 40–42).
