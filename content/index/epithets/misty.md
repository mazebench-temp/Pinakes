## Meaning

ἠεροειδής is formed from ἀήρ, epic ἠήρ, stem ἠερ- (“mist, haze,” not the clear “air” of later Greek) + -ειδής (“like, looking”): “misty, hazy.” In the Odyssey it is the standing epithet of the sea, in ἐπʼ ἠεροειδέα πόντον, “over the misty sea,” and ἐν ἠεροειδέι πόντῳ (for example Od. 2.263, 3.294, 5.164), and it describes caves and a rock seen in shadow (Od. 12.80, 12.233, 13.103). The Iliad has it twice: of the sea in the funeral games (Il. 23.744), and here. The same ἀήρ is the mist in which Ares rests his spear and horses (ἠέρι, 5.356) and the mist Hera pours round her horses (5.776). See [[sea]].

ἠερόεις, from the same stem with the suffix -όεις (“full of”), is the misty dark of the world below and of the west. It describes Tartarus (8.13), and the ζόφος ἠερόεις, “misty gloom,” that Hades received as his share of the world (15.191), into which the dead go down (21.56, 23.51), and toward which, on the left, fly the birds that Hector will not heed (12.238–240). Hesiod's Tartarus is ἠερόεις as well (*Theogony* 119, 682, 721).

## Use

{{quote:837f0dd8-6ebf-59c0-8d6d-9d52d06dd509}}

The neuter stands alone as a noun: “as far as a man sees with his eyes into the haze.” The haze is where sea and sky meet at the limit of sight, and the next verse names the sea itself, “wine-dark” ([[wine-dark]], [[the-lookout]]). The measure of the gods' stride is the farthest a man can see ([[high-neighing]]).
