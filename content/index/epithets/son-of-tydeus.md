## Meaning

Τυδέος (“of Tydeus”) + υἱός (“son”), and the patronymic Τυδεΐδης: “son of Tydeus.” Diomedes is named by his father more often than almost any other hero: Τυδεΐδης occurs about seventy times in Homer, and Τυδέος υἱός nearly thirty. The father's name carries a story. Tydeus fought at Thebes among the Seven, and Agamemnon and Athena both hold his deeds up to his son as a standard (Il. 4.370–400, 5.800–813).

## Use

{{quote:523fef7e-1e66-53ec-8643-dab33e11e5f5}}

Diomedes' first appearance in the Iliad is as a patronymic, fifth in the list of elders whom Agamemnon calls to the sacrifice before battle. He is named by his own name for the first time in the Catalogue, where he leads the men of Argos and Tiryns ({{ref:Iliad 2.563}}; [[good-at-the-war-cry]]).
