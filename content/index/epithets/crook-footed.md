## Meaning

κυλλοποδίων is formed from κυλλός (“crooked, bent”) and πούς (“foot”): “the crook-footed one.” Walter Leaf takes it for a pet form of a *κύλλοπος, “little crook-foot,” meaning the same as ἀμφιγυήεις (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 18.371; see [[lame-in-both-legs]]). In each of its three places in Homer, all in the Iliad, it stands alone as a name: at the end of the verse that tells how Hephaestus built his own house of bronze (18.371); in the account of the layers of Achilles' shield, five of which the crook-footed god had driven into it (20.270); and in the vocative κυλλοπόδιον, when Hera calls on her son to fight the river Xanthus (21.331). The Iliad does not hide the god's lameness: he limps (χωλεύων, 18.411, 18.417), and his mother wished to hide him away because he was lame (18.396–397; see [[fall-of-hephaestus]]).
