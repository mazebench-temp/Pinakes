## Meaning

Ἀντηνορίδης = Ἀντήνωρ + the patronymic suffix -ίδης: “son of Antenor.” Antenor, one of the elders on the wall with Priam, has many sons in the Iliad ([[antenor]]). The patronymic names Helicaon, whose wife Laodice Iris impersonates ({{ref:Iliad 3.122-123}}; [[helicaon]]), and Laodocus (4.87). Another son, Agenor, fights in the first battle of Book 4 (4.467; [[agenor]]).

## Use

{{quote:0ee45413-03c4-59eb-b88c-aa1354ba2845}}

Athena goes among the Trojans in the likeness of a son of Antenor to persuade Pandarus. The goddess borrows the face of a son of the elder who had received Menelaus and Odysseus in his house when they came to Troy as envoys ({{ref:Iliad 3.205-207}}; [[laodocus]], [[spearmen]]).
