## Meaning

ἠεροφοῖτις = ἀήρ, ἠέρος (“mist, haze,” in Homer the thick air that hides, not the clear air) + φοιτάω (“go to and fro”): “she who goes about in the mist,” unseen. Homer uses it twice, both times of the Erinys: here, as she hears Althaea's curse “out of Erebus” (9.571–572), and in Agamemnon's apology in Book 19, where he blames “Zeus and Fate and the Erinys who walks in darkness” for the blind folly that made him take Briseis (19.86–89; [[ate]]). The mist is the haze in which gods move and hide what is theirs, as Ares rests his spear and horses in mist (5.356; [[misty]]), and the darkness of Erebus from which the Erinys comes ([[erebus]], [[murky]]). See [[erinyes]].

## Use

{{quote:43f8c035-4e67-57b3-b084-9bbef95742a5}}

Althaea prays to Hades and Persephone for her son's death, “and the Erinys who walks in darkness heard her out of Erebus, she of the implacable heart” (9.571–572; [[implacable]], [[dread]]). The verse gives the avenger two epithets, one for her way of moving unseen and one for her heart. Homer does not tell how the curse was fulfilled ([[the-death-of-meleager]]). In Book 19 the same epithet comes back in a speech about blind folly, and Agamemnon names the Erinys among the powers that blinded him (19.87).
