## Meaning

ἐρι- (“very”) + δοῦπος (“thud, din”): “loud-sounding, echoing.” It is a different word from [[loud-thundering]] (ἐρίγδουπος), the epithet of Zeus, and the translation keeps the two apart (see its table of epithets). Of shores it is the noise of the surf: Athena shouts “standing now beside the ditch they had dug, outside the wall, / and now on the loud-resounding shores” (20.49–50; [[dug]]). Walter Leaf notes that ἀκταί are always the shores of the sea (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 20.50). Elsewhere it is the echoing colonnade of a house. Priam drives out of the gateway and the echoing colonnade on his way to Achilles (24.323), and in the Odyssey travelers drive out the same way (*Odyssey* 3.493, 15.146, 15.191), guests sleep on beds set under it (*Odyssey* 3.399, 7.345), and the herdsmen tether their beasts beneath it (*Odyssey* 20.176, 20.189). Once it is the roar where two rivers meet, on the way to the dead (*Odyssey* 10.515). See [[sea]].
