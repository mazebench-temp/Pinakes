## Meaning

ὑψιπέτης = ὕψι (“on high”) + the root of πέτομαι (“fly”): “flying high.” The first element is the one of [[thunders-on-high]] (ὑψιβρεμέτης). The word is always the eagle's, at the start of the verse in αἰετὸς ὑψιπέτης, and always of an eagle that is an omen: the eagle on the left at the Achaean ditch, in the narrator's account and in Polydamas' reading of it (12.201, 12.219); the eagle on the right that flies over as Ajax foretells Hector's flight, at which the Achaeans shout (13.821–823); and in the Odyssey the eagle on the left with a dove in its talons, which turns the suitors from their plan to kill Telemachus (Od. 20.242–246). The lengthened ὑψιπετήεις describes the eagle in two similes of a warrior swooping on his enemy: Hector rushing at Achilles, like an eagle that dives through dark clouds on a lamb or a hare (22.306–310), and Odysseus at the suitors' kinsmen (Od. 24.537–538). See [[eagle]] and [[divination]].

## Use

{{quote:98754e30-088a-5ea6-84a3-4f9a590cffd2}}

The bird comes on the left, the side of ill omen, as the Trojans are eager to cross the ditch ([[the-eagle-and-the-serpent]], [[blood-red]], [[huge]]). Polydamas repeats the verse when he reads the sign (12.219), and Hector answers that he does not care whether birds fly to the right or the left (12.237–240; [[long-winged]], [[misty]]). The next eagle in the poem, also “flying high,” comes on the right for the Achaeans (13.821–823). See [[polydamas]] and [[hector]].
