## Meaning

ἰδυίῃσι, the feminine dative plural of the participle εἰδώς (“knowing”) + πραπίδεσσι, dative plural of πραπίδες, an old word for the midriff and hence the mind: “with a knowing mind,” with skill. The phrase belongs to Hephaestus' craft. It is used when he makes the shield of Achilles (Il. 18.482), and the whole verse recurs of the halls he built for Zeus (Il. 20.12).

## Use

{{quote:93d20fd6-fcb4-5f43-b7e4-1d0389626619}}

The last picture of the gods in Book 1 is a picture of order: each goes to the house that the craftsman god has made. The formula for his skill completes the rehabilitation of Hephaestus, who began the scene as a peacemaker, went on as a figure of fun ([[unquenchable-laughter]]), and ends as the builder of Olympus.
