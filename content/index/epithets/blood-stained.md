## Meaning

μιαιφόνος = μιαίνω (“stain, defile”) + φόνος (“slaughter,” and the blood of the slain): “stained with slaughter.” μιαίνω is the verb of Menelaus' wound, when his thighs “were stained with blood” (4.146). Homer uses the adjective only of Ares: twice in the vocative line that begins Ἆρες Ἄρες βροτολοιγέ (5.31, 5.455; see [[ruin-of-mortals]]), once as he strips a man he has killed (5.844), and once in the battle of the gods, when he stabs at Athena's aegis with his long spear (Il. 21.402).

## Use

In the vocative line the word is part of a list of names for the god's work, spoken first by Athena to lead him out of the battle and then by Apollo to send him back (5.31, 5.455). The third use shows the work itself. Ares has killed Periphas, “by far the best of the Aetolians,” and stands over him:

{{quote:756627de-f70b-5e11-9dea-ed8bbc272fe4}}

The narrator gives him the epithet at the only moment in Book 5 when he is seen stripping a man he has killed ([[periphas]]). Athena, invisible in the helmet of Hades, is already beside Diomedes ([[helmet-of-hades]]), and a few lines later the god who is stained with other men's blood is wounded himself (5.855–859). In Book 21 he recalls that wound as he stabs at the aegis: Athena drove the spear at him herself “and tore through my fair skin” (Il. 21.396–398).
