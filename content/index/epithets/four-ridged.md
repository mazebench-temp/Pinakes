## Meaning

τετράφαλος = τετρα- (“four”) + φάλος, the ridge that ran over the crown of the helmet, on which the crest was set and on which Menelaus' sword shattered (κόρυθος φάλον, 3.362): “with four ridges.” How the four ridges were arranged is not known. It is a different word from τετραφάληρος, “with four plates,” in the arming verse of Athena and Agamemnon (5.743, 11.41; [[with-two-ridges-and-four-plates]]); a helmet with no ridge at all is ἄφαλος ([[without-ridge-or-crest]]). Homer uses τετράφαλος twice: here, and of Achilles' shining helmet with its golden plumes as he rushes at Hector (22.314–316). See [[helmet]].

## Use

{{quote:5607d46e-b39a-5ebe-8aa6-793d3996545a}}

The boulder is too big for any man of today to hold easily in both hands, and it crushes the four-ridged helmet and the bones of the head together; Epicles falls from the tower like a diver ([[epicles]], [[jagged]], [[men-of-today]], [[like-a-diver]]). The ridges that turned a sword are nothing against the stone. See [[ajax]].
