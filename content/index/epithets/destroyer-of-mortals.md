## Meaning

φθισίμβροτος = the stem of φθίνω, φθίω (“waste away, perish,” and “destroy”) + βροτός (“mortal”), with the μ before β that also appears in ἄμβροτος, “immortal”: “destroying mortals.” The first part is that of φθισήνωρ, “wasting men,” an epithet of war ([[man-destroying]]), and the second that of βροτολοιγός ([[ruin-of-mortals]]). Homer uses it twice: of battle here, and in the Odyssey of the aegis that Athena holds up from the roof of the hall, at which the suitors' minds are terrified (Od. 22.297–298; [[aegis]]).

## Use

{{quote:ddc81568-ff71-507f-9f69-909397431671}}

“And the battle, destroyer of mortals, bristled with the spears, long and flesh-cutting, that they held” (13.339–340; [[the-dust-storm]], [[flesh-cutting]], [[polemos]]). The battle itself is the killer, and it bristles (ἔφριξεν) with spears as the battalions of Book 4 and Book 7 bristled with shields and spears (4.282, 7.62). The next verses are of eyes blinded by the gleam of bronze (13.340–343; [[phaeinos]]), and the narrator adds that he would be very bold of heart who could rejoice at the sight of the toil and not grieve (13.343–344).
