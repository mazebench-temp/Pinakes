## Meaning

ὠκύπτερος = ὠκύς (“swift”; [[swift-footed]]) + πτερόν (“wing, feather”): “swift-winged.” Homer has it only here, of the ἴρηξ, the hawk. The same speed is given to the hawk in other words: Apollo goes down from Ida like a swift hawk, the slayer of doves, the swiftest of winged things (15.237–238), and Achilles chases Hector as a falcon, the nimblest of winged things, swoops after a dove (22.139–140). Compare [[swift-flying]] (ὠκυπέτης), the epithet of Poseidon's horses a few lines before (13.24).

## Use

{{quote:ec907289-460a-59da-ab3c-461b3406b456}}

“And he himself sped off as a swift-winged hawk flies, that rises from a sheer and towering rock, and darts across the plain to chase some other bird; so from them darted Poseidon the earth-shaker” (13.62–65; [[the-hawk-from-the-rock]], [[hawk]], [[sheer]], [[poseidon]]). The god who came in the likeness of a seer goes away like a bird of prey, and the son of Oileus knows him by his going (13.66–72; [[seeing-the-gods]]).
