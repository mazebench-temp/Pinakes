## Meaning

ἐνοσίχθων = ἔνοσις (“shaking,” as in εἰνοσίφυλλος, “with quivering leaves”; [[with-quivering-leaves]]) + χθών (“earth”): “shaker of the earth.” Its twin, [[shaker-of-the-earth]] (ἐννοσίγαιος), says the same with γαῖα for χθών. Poseidon is the god of the earthquake as well as of the sea. When the gods go to war in Book 20, Zeus thunders from above while Poseidon shakes the boundless earth and the peaks of the mountains, and Hades leaps from his throne in fear that “Poseidon the earth-shaker” will split the ground open above him (20.56–65).

The word is Poseidon's most frequent title: twenty-three times in the Iliad and eighteen in the Odyssey, usually in the line-end formula Ποσειδάων ἐνοσίχθων, and also as κρείων ἐνοσίχθων, “the powerful earth-shaker” (for example 8.208, 13.10), and once εὐρὺ κρείων ἐνοσίχθων, “the wide-ruling earth-shaker” (11.751; compare [[wide-ruling]], [[powerful]]). A Linear B tablet from Knossos names a god e-ne-si-da-o-ne, usually read as “Earth-shaker” and taken as a title of Poseidon, so the idea may be older than the epic formulas.

## Use

Poseidon speaks for the first time in the Iliad in Book 7. He has been named only three times before: among the gods who tried to bind Zeus (1.400), in a comparison for Agamemnon's chest (2.479), and in the name of his grove at Onchestus (2.506). Now, as the gods sit beside Zeus and watch the Achaeans build their wall, “Poseidon the earth-shaker began to speak” (7.445):

Zeus answers him as “shaker of the earth, wide in strength” (7.455; [[shaker-of-the-earth]], [[wide-in-strength]]) and gives him leave to destroy the wall when the Achaeans have sailed home. The destruction is told in Book 12, where “the shaker of the earth himself, trident in hand,” leads the rivers against the wall and sweeps its foundations into the sea (12.17–33). The god named for shaking the ground is the one who levels the wall. See [[poseidon]], [[the-achaean-wall]] and [[the-walls-of-troy]].
