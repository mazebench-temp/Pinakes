## Meaning

πολύκλητος = πολύς (“many”) + κλητός (“called, summoned,” from καλέω, “call”): “called from many places.” Homer uses it twice, both times of the Trojans' allies: here, and in Book 10, where Dolon tells Odysseus that the allies sleep and leave the watch to the Trojans, since they have no children or wives nearby (Il. 10.420–422). Compare the allies' other epithet, [[famous]] (κλειτοὶ ἐπίκουροι).

## Use

{{quote:8e90d374-4124-5071-8bdb-c4ae78bdeca9}}

The Achaeans advance in silence; the Trojans clamor like sheep in a rich man's fold ([[sheep-in-the-fold]]). The narrator explains the noise by the army's origins: the men came from many lands and spoke many tongues. Iris had said as much in Book 2, telling Hector to let each leader command his own countrymen, since the allies' tongues are many ({{ref:Iliad 2.803-806}}; [[trojan-allies]]). The Achaean army is one people moving in silence, the Trojan army many peoples in a din ([[silence]]).
