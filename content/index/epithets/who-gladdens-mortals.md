## Meaning

τερψίμβροτος = τέρπω (“delight, gladden”) + βροτός (“mortal”): “who delights mortals,” a compound of the same kind as φαεσίμβροτος, “bringing light to mortals,” the Sun's other epithet of this shape in the Odyssey ([[bringing-light-to-mortals]]); a scholion records φαεσιμβρότου as a variant in the first of the two verses (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, critical note on 12.269). Homer has it only in the verse νῆσον ἀλεύασθαι τερψιμβρότου Ἠελίοιο, “to avoid the island of the Sun, who gladdens mortals,” in Odysseus' account of the warnings of Teiresias and Circe (*Odyssey* 12.269, 12.274). See [[helios]], [[who-sees-all-things]] and [[the-cattle-of-helios]].

## Outside Homer

In the *Hymn to Apollo* the Cretan ship that the god is carrying to Pytho sails past Malea and along the Laconian coast to “the place of the Sun who gladdens mortals, / Taenarum, where the deep-fleeced sheep of the lord Sun / always graze” (*Hymn to Apollo* 409–413, a plain rendering). The epithet and the flocks of the Sun are found together there, as on Thrinacia.
