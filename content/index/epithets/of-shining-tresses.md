## Meaning

λιπαροπλόκαμος = λιπαρός (“shining, sleek,” properly with oil) + πλόκαμος (“braid, tress”): “with shining braids.” Walter Leaf takes it to refer to an abundant use of unguents on the hair (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 19.126). The word occurs once in Homer, in Agamemnon's story of Ate, whom Zeus seizes “by her head of shining tresses” and hurls from the starry sky (19.126–131). It belongs with the other compounds for a goddess's or a woman's hair, [[with-lovely-tresses]] (ἐϋπλόκαμος), [[of-the-lovely-tresses]] (καλλιπλόκαμος) and [[lovely-haired]] (ἠΰκομος), and its first half is that of [[of-the-shining-veil]] (λιπαροκρήδεμνος), the epithet of Charis (18.382). See [[ate]] and [[hair]].
