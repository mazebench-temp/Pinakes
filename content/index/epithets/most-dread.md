## Meaning

αἰνότατος is the superlative of αἰνός (“dread, terrible, grim”); Κρονίδη is the vocative of the patronymic ([[son-of-cronus]]). The address mixes awe and reproach: “most terrible son of Cronus.”

## Use

{{quote:2f576cd1-4a14-53e1-8edd-e22ce4e5601c}}

The whole verse, αἰνότατε Κρονίδη, ποῖον τὸν μῦθον ἔειπες (“most dread son of Cronus, what a thing you have said!”), is a formula of protest ([[what-a-word]]). In the Iliad it is Hera's. She uses it again when Zeus proposes to let Troy stand (Il. 4.25) and when he thinks of saving his son Sarpedon from death (Il. 16.440). Here it answers his refusal to share his counsels, and it leads her to name Thetis.
