## Meaning

λιπαρός is formed from λίπα (“with oil”) and λίπος (“fat”): “oiled, glistening, sleek,” and so “rich, comfortable.” It describes the body anointed with oil after bathing: the formula ποσσὶ δʼ ὑπὸ λιπαροῖσιν ἐδήσατο καλὰ πέδιλα, “and under his shining feet he bound his fine sandals,” dresses Agamemnon on the morning of the Dream (2.44) and is used again of Agamemnon and Nestor on the night of Book 10, of Hera as she dresses to deceive Zeus, and of Telemachus (10.22, 10.132; 14.186; Od. 2.4). It describes shining headbands and veils (κρήδεμνα, Od. 1.334; καλύπτρη, 22.406), and the sleek old age of a man who dies in comfort, as Teiresias foretells for Odysseus and as Odysseus himself used to pray (Od. 11.134–137, 19.367–368). In Book 9 it describes θέμιστες, the ordinances or dues that the people of the seven towns will pay to Achilles (9.156 = 9.298), a phrase whose sense is disputed: “rich dues” to their ruler, or ordinances carried out in prosperity ([[themis]]). The translation renders it “shining” of feet and “rich” of the dues. Compare [[glossy]] (σιγαλόεις).

## Use

{{quote:b970364a-82bd-549e-8950-b490cd76cf30}}

The men of the seven towns, rich in flocks and cattle, “will honor him with gifts like a god and under his scepter will bring his ordinances to rich fulfillment” ([[rich-in-flocks]], [[rich-in-cattle]]). The epithet of oil and well-being describes what a ruler receives from a prosperous people. See [[the-seven-cities]].
