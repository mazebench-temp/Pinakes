## Meaning

βίη (“force, might”) + Πριάμοιο (“of Priam”): “the might of Priam,” that is, Priam himself in his power. Epic names heroes in this way with βίη and a genitive or an adjective: [[might-of-heracles]] (βίη Ἡρακληείη), and in the Odyssey βίη Ἰφικληείη for Iphiclus (Od. 11.290). The periphrasis honors a man by his strength. Of old Priam it honors his authority.

## Use

{{quote:f054d837-abd8-59e4-ab3a-b67b9633b0e2}}

Menelaus asks for Priam to swear the oath himself, because his sons are overbearing and not to be trusted, and because an old man looks both before and after ([[before-and-after]]). The might he asks for is the king's word, which binds the city. The book also names Priam by his ancestors: the herald Idaeus summons him as “son of Laomedon” (3.250; [[laomedon]]), and he speaks to both armies as “Priam, descendant of Dardanus” (3.303; [[dardanus]]). He drives down to the plain for the oath, and goes back to Troy, unable to watch his son fight (3.259–313). See [[oath]], [[the-oaths-sworn]].
