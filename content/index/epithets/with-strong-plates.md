## Meaning

κραταιγύαλος = κραταιός (“strong, mighty,” as in μοῖρα κραταιή, [[mighty-fate]]) + γύαλον (“hollow, curved plate”), the plate of the corselet: “with strong plates.” Walter Leaf renders it “with solid plates,” the corselet being made of a solid breastplate and backplate (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 19.361). The γύαλον is where a weapon strikes the corselet: Pandarus' arrow hits Diomedes “on the plate of his corselet” (5.98–99), and Idomeneus and Ajax kill their men with a spear that “broke the plate of his corselet” (13.506–507, 17.313–314); Meges' corselet is “fitted with plates” (15.529–530). The compound occurs only once (19.361). See [[corselet]].
