## Meaning

αἰνός (“dread, terrible, grim”) + δηϊοτής (“battle, slaughter,” from δήϊος, “hostile”): “grim combat.” The phrase ἐν αἰνῇ δηϊοτῆτι closes the verse. Compare [[most-dread]] (αἰνότατε), and the epithets of war in Book 2: [[grim-sounding]], [[relentless]], [[man-destroying]].

## Use

Paris challenges “all the best of the Argives to fight him face to face in grim combat” (ἀντίβιον μαχέσασθαι ἐν αἰνῇ δηϊοτῆτι, 3.19–20). The whole verse returns in Book 7, when Apollo proposes that Hector challenge one of the Danaans to single combat, and again when Helenus tells Hector to challenge the best of the Achaeans (Il. 7.38–40, 7.49–51). It belongs to the language of the formal duel ([[single-combat]]).
