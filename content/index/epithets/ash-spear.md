## Meaning

μείλινος is the adjective of μελίη (“ash tree,” and a spear made of ash): “of ash wood.” Spear shafts were made of ash, and μελίη alone can mean the spear, like Achilles' Pelian ash, which Chiron cut on Mount Pelion and gave to Peleus (Il. 16.143–144, 19.390–391). Homer uses the adjective thirteen times, always of spears: μείλινον ἔγχος at the end of the verse, and δόρυ μείλινον. Priam's epithet [[with-good-ash-spear]] (ἐϋμμελίης) is built on the same μελίη. See [[spear]].

## Use

{{quote:7b8205dc-9953-54e9-b121-1866d5ba6a91}}

Book 5 uses the phrase three times, all of one weapon, the spear of Tlepolemus. He raises “his ash spear” (5.655) and wounds Sarpedon in the thigh, as Sarpedon kills him. Sarpedon's companions carry him from the field with the spear dragging from the wound, and no one thinks “to draw the ash spear from his thigh, so that he could walk” (5.666). Under the oak of Zeus, Pelagon pushes “the ash spear” through and out, and Sarpedon faints and revives (5.694–698; [[pelagon-of-lycia]], [[the-oak-of-zeus]], [[psyche]]). The epithet follows the spear from the hand that throws it to the wound it leaves ([[tlepolemus-and-sarpedon]]).
