## Meaning

ἐΰτμητος = εὖ (“well”) + the verbal adjective of τέμνω (“cut”): “well cut,” of leather cut into straps. Homer uses it five times, always of leather. Twice it describes the τελαμών, the strap from which a sword hangs at the shoulder, in the same verse: σὺν κολεῷ τε φέρων καὶ ἐϋτμήτῳ τελαμῶνι, “bringing it with its sheath and its well-cut strap” (7.304, 23.825). The other three are thongs (ἱμάντες): Odysseus and Diomedes tie up the horses of Rhesus with them (10.567), Achilles binds the hands of the twelve Trojan youths he takes alive for Patroclus' pyre (21.30), and Diomedes gives Euryalus boxing thongs of oxhide for his bout with Epeius (23.683–684). See [[sword]] and [[belt]].

## Use

Hector ends the duel with an exchange of gifts:

{{quote:55017447-7604-5bda-a664-2dcf193d8463}}

Hector gives a sword complete with its sheath and baldric, and Ajax a belt ([[silver-studded]], [[phaeinos]], [[guest-gifts]]). The verse of the sword's strap returns at the funeral games of Patroclus, at the end of another armed combat that is stopped before it is decided. Ajax and Diomedes fight in armor for the arms of Sarpedon; the Achaeans, fearing for Ajax, part them and tell them to share the prizes; and Achilles gives Diomedes the silver-studded Thracian sword that he took from Asteropaeus, “with its sheath and its well-cut strap” (23.798–825). Both times a duel of champions ends without a victor and with the gift of a sword. In Sophocles' *Ajax* the hero kills himself with the sword that Hector gave him ([[sophocles]]).
