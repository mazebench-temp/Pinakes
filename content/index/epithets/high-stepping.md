## Meaning

ἀερσίπους is a compound of ἀείρω (“lift”) and πούς (“foot”): “lifting the feet,” the high action of a spirited horse. It is formed like ἑλκεσίπεπλος (“trailing the robe”), with a verbal first element ending in -σι-.

## Use

{{quote:cf15a120-7fe3-5dd6-a9d9-e140c6c62134}}

When the lot has fallen, the two armies sit down in their ranks beside their horses and their armor. The line pictures the war at rest: the high-stepping horses are still, and the “inlaid armor” lies on the ground ([[inlaid]]), where the men laid it for the truce (3.113–115). Only Paris and Menelaus now arm (3.328–339).
