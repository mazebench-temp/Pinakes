## Meaning

θεοί (“gods”) + αἰέν (“always”) + ἐόντες (“being”): “the gods who always are.” The phrase defines the gods by what mortals lack. Elsewhere Homer says the same with θεοὶ αἰειγενέται, “the gods who live forever.” Book 1 sets it against its words for human brevity: [[short-lived]], [[swift-fated]], [[mortal-men]].

In Agamemnon's mouth the formula gives weight to a sneer: the everlasting gods made Achilles a warrior ([[spearmen]]), not a man with the right to insult his king. In the narrative it marks the gods' return from the Ethiopians on the twelfth day, the moment Thetis has been waiting for ({{ref:Iliad 1.425}}).
