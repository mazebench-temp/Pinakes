## Meaning

χρυσόθρονος = χρυσός (“gold”) + θρόνος (“seat, throne”): “with a golden throne.” Homer uses it of Hera, of Artemis (Il. 9.533; *Odyssey* 5.123), and in the Odyssey above all of Dawn, in the verse-end αὐτίκα δὲ χρυσόθρονος ἤλυθεν Ἠώς, “and at once Dawn of the golden throne came” (*Odyssey* 10.541, 12.142, 15.56, 20.91).

Some scholars have connected the second element instead with θρόνα, “flowers, embroidered patterns” (as in Il. 22.441, where Andromache weaves flowers into a cloth), which would give “with golden embroidery.” The question has been argued mostly over Sappho's epithet for Aphrodite, ποικιλόθρονος (fr. 1.1). In Homer the sense “throne” is the natural one.

## Use

{{quote:49297f91-aefc-5130-b8c7-85c7156cec1b}}

The book ends on Hera's epithet as she lies beside Zeus. After the quarrel, the threat, and the laughter, the last words give her back her dignity and restore the marriage. The golden throne recalls the scene a little earlier, when Zeus sat down on his throne and the gods rose to meet him ({{ref:Iliad 1.533-536}}).
