## Meaning

ἐριβρεμέτης = ἐρι- (“very, loud,” the intensive prefix of ἐρίγδουπος, [[loud-thundering]]) + the root of βρέμω (“roar, resound,” of the sea breaking on the shore, 2.210, 4.425): “who roars loud,” the god of thunder. Homer has it only here. It is the twin of ὑψιβρεμέτης, “who thunders on high” ([[thunders-on-high]]), with the same second part, and the translation's table of epithets sets the three words for the thunder of Zeus side by side.

## Use

{{quote:358333ad-21bc-5def-b8bf-b445dd469eb2}}

Menelaus, standing over Peisander, joins the thunderer to the protector of guests: the Trojans did not fear “the harsh wrath of Zeus who thunders loud, god of guests, who will one day destroy your steep city” (13.624–625; [[xenia]], [[menis]], [[steep]]). The wrong is Paris': they carried off Menelaus' wife after they had been welcomed by her (13.626–627; [[paris]], [[helen]]). In Book 3 Menelaus prayed to Zeus before the duel to let him punish Paris, so that men hereafter would shudder to wrong a host who had offered them friendship (3.351–354). The wrath he names here is the wrath of that Zeus, and the epithet gives it the sound of thunder. See [[zeus]] and [[menelaus]].
