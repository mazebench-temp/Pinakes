## Meaning

κορυστής is formed from κορύσσω (“to put on a helmet, to arm”), the verb of κόρυς (“helmet”; [[helmet]]): “a helmeted man, an armed warrior.” The phrase ἄνδρα κορυστήν ends the verse in a formula for the first kill: Antilochus “was the first to kill a helmeted man of the Trojans” (4.457), Diomedes is “far the first” to do so in Book 8 (Il. 8.256), and Meriones in Book 16 (Il. 16.603). The two Ajaxes are called κορυστά, “helmeted,” in the dual (Il. 13.201).

## Use

{{quote:8dab0342-96bd-5384-b5b4-db48a3e65915}}

Book 4 has prepared the word. The verb κορύσσω describes the Ajaxes arming (4.274), the wave that “rears its head” at sea before it breaks on the shore (κορύσσεται, 4.424), and Strife, who “is small when first she rears her head” (4.442; [[strife]]). The helmeting of the wave and of Strife comes before the first helmeted man is killed. The spear strikes Echepolus on “the ridge of his horsehair-crested helmet” and pierces the bone (4.459–461; [[horsehair-crested]], [[echepolus]], [[antilochus]]).
