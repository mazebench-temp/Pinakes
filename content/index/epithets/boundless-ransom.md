## Meaning

ἀπερείσιος means “boundless, countless.” It is usually connected with ἀπείρων (“boundless”; [[boundless-sea]]) and with πεῖραρ (“end, limit”). With ἄποινα (“ransom”; [[apoina]]) it forms a line-end formula, ἀπερείσιʼ ἄποινα, which Homer uses eleven times, all in the Iliad. Twice the adjective describes instead the gifts that a suitor gives for his bride, ἀπερείσια ἕδνα (Il. 16.178; Od. 19.529).

The formula follows the poem's great ransoms. Chryses comes to the ships “bringing a boundless ransom” for his daughter (1.13, 1.372). Agamemnon offers one to Achilles in Book 9 and pays it in Book 19 (Il. 9.120, 19.138). Priam loads a boundless ransom for Hector's body, offers it, and it is accepted (Il. 24.276, 24.502, 24.579).

The same three verses of treasure and ransom are spoken again by Dolon (Il. 10.378–381) and by the two sons of Antimachus (Il. 11.131–135), and none of the three is ransomed: Agamemnon kills Adrastus (6.53–65), Diomedes kills Dolon (Il. 10.454–457), and Agamemnon the sons of Antimachus (Il. 11.143–147). See [[adrastus-the-trojan]], [[worked-with-much-toil]] and [[supplication]].

The second is a ransom accepted. Andromache tells how Achilles brought her mother to his camp with the rest of the spoil of Thebe, “and then he set her free, taking a boundless ransom” (6.426–427; [[sack-of-thebe]]). The Achilles of Andromache's memory takes ransoms and spares; the Achaeans of Book 6 refuse them ([[apoina]]).
