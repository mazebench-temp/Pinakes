## Meaning

ὀλέθριος is the adjective of ὄλεθρος (“destruction, death,” as in λυγρὸς ὄλεθρος, “grim destruction”; [[grim]]): “of destruction, deadly.” Homer uses it only in this phrase, twice, both times of death in war: Briseis' three brothers “all met their day of destruction” (19.294), and Xanthus tells Achilles that “the day of your destruction is near” (19.409). It belongs to a family of “days” that stand for a man's death or a people's fall: the evil day (κακὸν ἦμαρ, [[evil-day]]), the pitiless day (νηλεὲς ἦμαρ, [[pitiless]]), and the fated day (αἴσιμον ἦμαρ, 8.72, 22.212; μόρσιμον ἦμαρ, 15.613). The verb of 19.294, ἐπισπεῖν, “meet,” is used of the fated day when Achilles tells Lycaon that he spared Trojans before Patroclus met his (21.100).
