## Meaning

ἀνδρόκμητος = ἀνήρ (“man”) + the root of κάμνω (“toil, make with toil”): “wrought by men.” It occurs only here. κάμνω is the verb of hard work and of craftsmanship, as in πολύκμητος, “worked with much toil,” of iron ([[worked-with-much-toil]]). The towers of Troy are θεόδμητοι, “god-built” (8.519; [[god-built]]); the grave mound is the work of men. Compare [[heaped-earth]] (χυτὴ γαῖα), the earth heaped over a grave.

## Use

{{quote:ecac8ff6-a878-5baf-b2ce-165e27bbb5d6}}

The mound of Ilus, son of Tros and grandfather of Priam, stands in the middle of the plain (10.415, 11.166). Paris leans against the pillar set on it and, while Diomedes is stripping Agastrophus, shoots him from behind it as from an ambush (11.373–379; [[heavy-helmet]]). See [[the-tomb-of-ilus]], [[ilus]], [[paris]] and [[tomb]].
