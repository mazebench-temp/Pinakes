## Meaning

ἀναιδείη (Attic ἀναίδεια) = ἀν- (“not”) + αἰδώς (“shame, respect for others”): “shamelessness.” ἐπιειμένε is the vocative of the perfect participle of ἐπιέννυμι (“put on clothing”): “you who wear.” A quality of character becomes a garment, as warriors elsewhere in the Iliad are ἐπιειμένοι ἀλκήν, “clothed in … courage” (Il. 7.164).

## Use

{{quote:58cc5ecc-f147-564f-b1b0-dda005cc456d}}

αἰδώς is what the Achaeans wanted to show the priest (αἰδεῖσθαί θ’ ἱερῆα, “to respect the priest,” {{ref:Iliad 1.23}}) and what Agamemnon refused him. Achilles' first words of open abuse name that fault, and he repeats it nine lines later with ὦ μέγ’ ἀναιδές ([[utterly-shameless]]). He uses the same phrase of Agamemnon again in Book 9, αἰὲν ἀναιδείην ἐπιειμένος, “clothed forever in shamelessness” (Il. 9.372).
