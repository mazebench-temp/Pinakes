## Meaning

δμήτειρα is a feminine agent noun from δάμνημι, δαμάζω (“tame, subdue, overcome”), the verb of [[tamer-of-horses]] (ἱππόδαμος): “she who tames.” With θεῶν … καὶ ἀνδρῶν, set on either side of the verb, it gives Night power over both races, gods and men. The word is found only here in Homer. *Iliad* 14 names other powers over the same whole world: Hera greets Sleep as “lord of all gods and of all men” (14.233; [[sleep]]), and she asks Aphrodite for the love and longing with which she overcomes, with the same verb, “all the immortals and mortal men” (δαμνᾷ, 14.198–199; [[mortal-men]], [[philotes]]). In Book 24 sleep itself is πανδαμάτωρ, the sleep “that subdues all” (24.5; [[hypnos]]).

## Use

{{quote:482d4510-28b0-5de5-bd01-af8beba663de}}

Sleep tells Hera why he fears to lull Zeus again. When he did it once before, Zeus woke, flung the gods about his house and looked for him above all, and would have thrown him out of the upper air into the sea; but Sleep fled to Night, and Zeus stopped, “for he shrank from doing what would displease swift Night” (14.256–261; [[swift-night]], [[zeus]], [[hera-drives-heracles-to-cos]]). The title explains the story: the goddess who overcomes gods as well as men is the one power before whom even Zeus in his anger holds back. See [[night]] and [[the-deception-of-zeus]].

## Outside Homer

Hesiod gives Night no such title, but in the Theogony Sleep and Death are among her children (*Theogony* 211–212), and she carries Sleep in her arms, κασίγνητον Θανάτοιο, “the brother of Death,” in the words of Il. 14.231 (*Theogony* 756–757). In Hesiod's genealogy Sleep's refuge is his mother.
