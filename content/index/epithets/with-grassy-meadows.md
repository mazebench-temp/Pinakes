## Meaning

λεχεποίη = λέχος (“bed”) + ποίη (“grass”): “with beds of grass,” a place of deep meadows. Homer gives it to Pteleus, one of the towns of Protesilaus ({{ref:Iliad 2.697}}; [[pteleus-thessaly]]), and to the Asopus (4.383). The Homeric Hymns give it to two more places in Boeotia, Teumessus and Onchestus (*Hymn to Apollo* 224, *Hymn to Hermes* 88). Compare [[flowering]] (ἀνθεμόεις) and [[horse-pasturing]] (ἱππόβοτος).

## Use

{{quote:ce81cdb6-7e13-5f3f-a7d7-49c6ff754cfb}}

Both Homeric uses put the epithet at the end of the verse, after the name of the place. In the Catalogue it is one of the words that give each town its line ({{ref:Iliad 2.695-697}}). In Book 4 it follows [[deep-in-rushes]] in the description of the Asopus, where the Seven halted on the way to Thebes (4.383).
