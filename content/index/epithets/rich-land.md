## Meaning

πίων (“fat, rich”) + δῆμος (“land, country,” and the people who live in it; [[demos]]): “a rich land.” The same adjective is the fat of victims and sheep ([[fat-sheep]], [[thigh-pieces]]), and the noun δημός, “fat,” differs from δῆμος only by its accent (πίονι δημῷ, “in rich fat,” *Odyssey* 17.241); the pattern of this entry keeps the accent of the land.

The Iliad first gives the phrase to the Boeotians who lived beside Oresbius of Hyle by the Cephisian lake, “holding a very rich land” (5.708–710). In Book 16 it is Lycia: Zeus thinks of setting Sarpedon down alive “in the rich land of Lycia,” Glaucus prays to Apollo as the god “who are somewhere in the rich land of Lycia” or at Troy, and Sleep and Death carry Sarpedon's body home “in the rich land of wide Lycia” (16.436–437, 16.514–515, 16.671–673, 16.683; [[lycia]], [[wide-lycia]]). In Book 20 it is Hyde, under snowy Tmolus, where a Naiad bore Iphition (20.383–385; [[niphoeis]]). The Odyssey uses it of the Phaeacians' land (*Odyssey* 13.322), of the Thesprotians' (*Odyssey* 17.526, 19.271), and of Ithaca (*Odyssey* 14.329, 19.399). See [[deep-soiled]] and [[wealthy]].
