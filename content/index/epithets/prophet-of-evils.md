## Meaning

μάντι, vocative of μάντις (“seer, prophet,” related to μαίνομαι, “be in a frenzy”), + κακῶν, genitive plural of κακός (“bad”): “seer of evils.” The seer's usual epithet is honorific. Fourteen lines earlier the narrator called Calchas μάντις ἀμύμων, “the noble seer” ({{ref:Iliad 1.92}}; see [[blameless]]). Agamemnon keeps the noun and replaces the praise with a charge.

## Use

{{quote:d7f30265-b9f4-5dfd-8aec-00755feef565}}

Agamemnon says that Calchas has never told him anything good, and he inverts the seer's art: it is always dear to his heart to prophesy evil. The Iliad does not say which earlier prophecies he means.

Later readers heard an allusion to Aulis, where Calchas demanded the sacrifice of Agamemnon's daughter Iphigenia before the fleet could sail. That story was told in the Cypria (known from Proclus' summary) and in the parodos of Aeschylus' *Agamemnon*, but Homer never mentions it. In the Iliad's own account of Aulis, in Book 2, Calchas' prophecy is a favorable one: Troy will fall in the tenth year.
