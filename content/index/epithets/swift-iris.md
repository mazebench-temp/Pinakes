## Meaning

ταχύς (“swift”) is the plain word for speed ([[swift]]). With the vocative Ἶρι it ends the first half of a verse that opens four commands of Zeus: βάσκʼ ἴθι, Ἶρι ταχεῖα, “Go, swift Iris.” He sends her to turn back Hera and Athena (8.399); to tell Hector to keep out of the fighting until Agamemnon is wounded (11.186); to order Poseidon to leave the battle (15.158); and, from Olympus, to tell Priam to ransom Hector's body (24.144). Each time she carries the message in Zeus' own words ([[message-repeated]], [[messenger-scene]]). Iris' other epithets are of speed and flight as well ([[wind-footed]], [[storm-footed]], [[golden-winged]], [[swift-footed]]).

## Use

Zeus sees Hera and Athena driving out from Olympus and sends Iris after them: “Go, swift Iris, turn them back, and do not let them come face to face with me” (8.399–400). His threat follows (8.401–408). Iris repeats it to the goddesses at the gates of Olympus, changing only the persons (8.416–422 = 8.402–408), and ends with two verses of her own (8.423–424; [[shameless-dog]]).
