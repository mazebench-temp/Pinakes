## Meaning

τέκος (“child,” from τίκτω, “bear”) + Διός (“of Zeus”) + αἰγιόχοιο (“aegis-bearing”; see [[aegis-bearing]]). The phrase is a formula for Athena; Diomedes uses it in prayer to her (Il. 5.115). Athena was born of Zeus alone. Homer does not tell the story of her birth from his head, but Ares complains in Book 5 that Zeus himself begot her (Il. 5.880).

## Use

{{quote:7b11a50a-7ee4-594c-ae7b-5c8eda34c4b9}}

Achilles' greeting is not reverent: “Why have you come now?” He assumes she has come to see Agamemnon's arrogance and predicts that the king will pay with his life. The title answers his question in part, since she comes with her father's authority. In fact Hera has sent her ([[white-armed]], {{ref:Iliad 1.208}}), and she comes not to watch but to stop him.
