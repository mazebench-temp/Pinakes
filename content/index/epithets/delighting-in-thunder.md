## Meaning

τερπικέραυνος = τέρπω (“delight”) + κεραυνός (“thunderbolt”): “who takes delight in the thunderbolt.” The compound is of an old type in which a verbal stem is followed by the word it governs, like τερψίμβροτος, “who gladdens mortals,” an epithet of the Sun in the Odyssey.

## Use

{{quote:1a74728f-7bc4-5370-8ff9-9d6f977b4aa5}}

Thetis names Zeus by his thunderbolt as she resolves to carry her son's request to snowy Olympus ([[snowy-olympus]]). The epithet recalls the power she will ask him to use against the Achaeans. In the dative, Διὶ τερπικεραύνῳ closes the verse.
