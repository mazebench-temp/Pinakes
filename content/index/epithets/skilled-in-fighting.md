## Meaning

εἰδώς, participle of οἶδα (“know”): in Homer to know a skill is to have it, as in [[skilled-with-the-bow]] (τόξων εὖ εἰδώς). μάχης εὖ εἰδότε πάσης is in the dual, “the two, well skilled in every kind of fighting,” and it always describes a pair of brothers: Archelochus and Acamas, the sons of Antenor ({{ref:Iliad 2.823}}, and again at Il. 12.100), Phegeus and Idaeus, the sons of Dares (5.11), and the twins Crethon and Orsilochus, the sons of Diocles (5.549). εἰδότε χάρμης, “who knew the joy of battle” (χάρμη, the zest for fighting; [[charme]]), is also a dual, of Menesthes and Anchialus, two men in one chariot (5.608).

## Use

In Book 5 the praise of their skill comes just before the brothers are killed. Diomedes kills Phegeus, and Idaeus escapes only because Hephaestus hides him in darkness (5.9–24; [[phegeus]], [[idaeus-son-of-dares]], [[dares]]). Aeneas kills the twin sons of Diocles, who fall like tall fir trees (5.541–560; [[crethon]], [[orsilochus-son-of-diocles]], [[fir-trees]]). Hector kills Menesthes and Anchialus (5.608–609; [[menesthes]], [[anchialus]]). The formula makes each pair worthy victims, and it notes their skill at the moment it fails them ([[androktasia]], [[the-war-in-small]]).
