## Meaning

πενταέτηρος = πέντε (“five”) + ἔτος (“year”): “five years old,” an animal in its full strength. Homer uses it four times, always of a victim that is killed to feed a company: Agamemnon's ox for Zeus before the first battle, “a fat one, five years old” (2.402–403); his ox here; the fat pig that Eumaeus kills for his disguised master (Od. 14.419); and the ox that the sons of Autolycus kill for the young Odysseus before the hunt on Parnassus (Od. 19.420). That ox, like Agamemnon's here, is “a male, five years old” (ἄρσενα πενταέτηρον), and the verse that follows is the same in both: “they flayed it and dressed it, and jointed it all” (7.316 = Od. 19.421). Compare [[never-touched-by-the-goad]], the yearlings promised to Athena. See [[sacrifice]] and [[cattle]].
