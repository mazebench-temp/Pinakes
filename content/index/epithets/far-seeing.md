## Meaning

The first element is εὐρύς (“wide”). The second is either ὤψ (“eye”), giving “far-seeing,” or ὄψ (“voice”), giving “wide-voiced,” that is, far-thundering. Both suit Zeus, who watches from Olympus and speaks in thunder, and Homer gives no way to decide. The translation chooses “far-seeing.”

Like μητίετα ([[counselor]]), the word has an archaic form in short -α that serves for more than one case. Here it is accusative, εὐρύοπα Κρονίδην; elsewhere it is nominative, εὐρύοπα Ζεύς.

## Use

{{quote:28f2c288-39b9-5881-bf50-e873d7f58495}}

Zeus sits apart from the other gods on the topmost peak of [[many-ridged-olympus|many-ridged Olympus]]. The god who sees far is here seen alone, and the setting gives Thetis the privacy her supplication needs ([[supplication-type-scene]]). The patronymic Κρονίδην follows the epithet ([[son-of-cronus]]).
