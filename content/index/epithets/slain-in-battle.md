## Meaning

ἀρηΐφατος = Ἄρης, the god of war, standing for war itself ([[ares]]), + -φατος, from the root of φόνος (“slaughter”) that is in πέφαται, “is slain” (19.27): “slain by Ares,” killed in battle. The Iliad has it twice, both times in the verse αἵ ῥά τε φῶτας ἀρηϊφάτους κατέδουσιν, “which feed on men slain in battle”: of the flies that Thetis will keep from the body of Patroclus (19.31), and of the worms that have not touched the body of Hector, as Hermes tells Priam (24.414–415). The gods keep both bodies whole. In the Odyssey the ghosts that gather at Odysseus' sacrifice include ἄνδρες ἀρηίφατοι, men slain in battle, still in their bloodstained armor (*Odyssey* 11.41).
