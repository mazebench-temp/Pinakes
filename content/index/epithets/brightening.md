## Meaning

ἀργεστής is formed from the root ἀργ- (“bright, white”), the root of ἄργυρος (“silver”), of [[white]] (ἀργής) and of [[swift-dogs]] (ἀργός). It is taken as “clearing, brightening,” of a wind that clears the sky, or as “white,” of the white clouds or squalls that the wind brings; the translation renders it “brightening.” In Homer it is given only to Νότος, the South Wind, in the verse-end ἀργεστᾶο Νότοιο, twice: here, and when Hera tells Hephaestus that she will raise a hard storm of the West Wind and the South from the sea to carry his fire against the Trojans (21.334–336).

## Use

{{quote:a0ccc06e-c191-5412-a540-ebc43793c267}}

After the nine leaders, Hector kills the multitude, as the West Wind batters the clouds of the South Wind with a deep squall, and the waves roll and the spray flies ([[far-wandering]]). The clouds belong to one wind and the blow to the other. See [[the-west-wind-and-the-clouds]], [[notus]], [[zephyrus]] and [[hector]].

## Outside Homer

In Hesiod the epithet belongs to the West Wind: Dawn bears to Astraeus the winds, “brightening Zephyrus, and Boreas swift in his course, and Notus” (*Theogony* 378–380), and the winds that come from the gods, a great boon to mortals, are Notus, Boreas and “brightening Zephyrus” (*Theogony* 869–871).
