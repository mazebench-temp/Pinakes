## Meaning

οὖθαρ (“udder”) + ἀρούρης (“of the plowland,” the tilled field; [[who-eat-the-fruit-of-the-field]]): the richest part of the land, the place from which it gives most, as an udder gives milk. The word οὖθαρ occurs nowhere else in Homer; its plural is used literally of the ewes whose udders are bursting in the Cyclops' cave (οὔθατα, Od. 9.440). The phrase is a metaphor, rare in Homer's epithets, and in the Iliad it is used only of Argos, in a verse that Agamemnon speaks and Odysseus repeats (9.141 = 9.283). Whether Argos here means Agamemnon's own realm or the Peloponnese at large is not certain ([[argos]]).

The Homeric Hymn to Demeter uses the same phrase of the Rharian plain near Eleusis, “once the life-giving udder of the plowland” (φερέσβιον οὖθαρ ἀρούρης), which lay bare and leafless while Demeter hid the grain in the earth (*Hymn* 2.450–453).

## Use

{{quote:0d2d9ec7-889d-517f-add5-43e1dd532565}}

Agamemnon's second offer looks beyond Troy. If they come home to Achaean Argos, “the udder of the plowland,” Achilles will be his son-in-law and will be honored like his own son Orestes (9.141–143; [[orestes]]). The epithet sets the richest land in Greece behind the offer of a daughter and seven towns. Achilles has his own answer: Peleus will find him a wife in Hellas and Phthia, and he will enjoy what his father has (9.393–400; [[hellas]], [[phthia]]). See [[the-gifts-of-agamemnon]].
