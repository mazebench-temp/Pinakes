## Meaning

πιστός, “trusted, faithful,” belongs to the family of πείθω, “persuade,” in the middle “trust, obey” ([[peitho]]); with ἑταῖρος, “companion” ([[hetairos]]), it names the comrade on whom a man relies. The formula stands at the verse-end, seven times in the Iliad: of Stichius, the companion of Menestheus, and of Lycophron, who lived in Ajax's house (15.331, 15.437); of [[Alcimedon]], whom Automedon calls to his side (17.500); of Podes, Hector's companion, whom Menelaus kills (17.589–590); and three times of Patroclus, the trusted companion of Achilles, whose body and armor are fought over and mourned (17.557, 18.235, 18.460). In the Odyssey it names Peiraeus, the companion to whom Telemachus entrusts his guest (*Odyssey* 15.539–543). Compare [[trusty]] (ἐρίηρος).
