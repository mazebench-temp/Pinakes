## Meaning

δολιχός (“long”) + ἔγχος (“spear”; [[spear]]). The phrase stands in the formula δολίχʼ ἔγχεα χερσὶν ἔχοντες, “holding long spears in their hands,” used of the Thracians here (4.533) and of the young men who march out with the captains of the watch in Book 9 (Il. 9.86). In Book 7 Hector and Ajax pull their long spears free after their first throws (Il. 7.255). The same adjective is the first half of [[long-shadowed]] (δολιχόσκιος).

## Use

{{quote:11f8b239-70df-50b8-9e67-53609aa28116}}

The Thracians close around the body of their leader Peirous with their long spears and push Thoas away (4.532–535; [[topknotted]], [[thracians]]). The long spear keeps an enemy at a distance, and here it saves a body from being stripped.
