## Meaning

δυσπέμφελος = δυσ- (“hard, ill”) + a second part whose origin is unknown. Of the sea it means “rough, stormy,” hard to sail. In Homer it occurs only once, as a predicate of the sea in Patroclus' mockery of Cebriones' fall: “If he were ever on the fish-filled sea, / this man would feed many, diving for oysters, / leaping from a ship, even if the sea were rough” (16.746–748; [[fish-filled]], [[cebriones]]). Compare [[stormy]] (δυσαής), of the winds.

## Outside Homer

Hesiod has the word three times. Hecate gives a great catch to those “who work the gray-green, stormy (sea)” (*Theogony* 440; [[gray-green-sea]]); the farmer whom desire for “stormy seafaring” seizes must wait for the right season (*Works and Days* 618); and in a man it is churlishness: one should not be δυσπέμφελος at a feast shared by many guests (*Works and Days* 722–723).
