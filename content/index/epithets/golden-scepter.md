## Meaning

χρύσεος (“golden”) + σκῆπτρον (“staff, scepter”). The scepter is the staff of someone with the right to speak: kings, heralds, priests, and seers carry it. On Chryses' staff are wound the στέμματα, the woolen fillets of Apollo, which mark him as the god's servant and as a suppliant.

## Use

{{quote:682a615e-3fb4-5eb9-9356-990aedcd8948}}

The line recurs word for word in Achilles' retelling ({{ref:Iliad 1.374}}; [[retelling]]). Agamemnon's answer mocks the insignia: “the scepter and the fillet of the god may not protect you” ({{ref:Iliad 1.28}}; [[no-help-to-you]]). The gold of the staff echoes the names Chryses and Chryse, which sound like χρυσός (“gold”), but it gives the priest no protection against a king. The contrast with the other scepter of Book 1, the one Achilles swears by and flings down, is pointed ([[studded-with-golden-nails]]).
