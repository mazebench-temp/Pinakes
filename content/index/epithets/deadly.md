## Meaning

ὀλοός (also ὀλοιός) is formed from ὄλλυμι (“destroy”): “destructive, deadly, baneful.” The participle οὐλόμενος, the first epithet of the Iliad (“that ruinous wrath,” {{ref:Iliad 1.2}}), comes from the same verb. Homer uses ὀλοός of war and battle and of fate: the dying Patroclus says that “deadly fate” killed him (μοῖρʼ ὀλοή, Il. 16.849). Achilles says that Agamemnon “rages with a ruinous mind” (ὀλοιῇσι φρεσί, {{ref:Iliad 1.342}}). Compare [[baneful]] (οὖλος) and [[hateful]].

## Use

Iris tells Helen that the armies, “longing for deadly war,” were bringing tearful Ares on one another before the truce (3.132–133; [[tearful]]). After his sword breaks, Menelaus turns the word on Zeus:

{{quote:29b73f9e-837c-549b-9a51-284d2c2b9adc}}

He has prayed for vengeance (3.351–354), and the god has not granted it. The complaint is a reproach, not a denial of the god. The cowherd Philoetius speaks the same verse in the Odyssey, wondering at the sufferings Zeus sends to good men (Od. 20.201). See [[wide-sky]], [[father-zeus]].
