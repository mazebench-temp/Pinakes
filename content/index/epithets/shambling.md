## Meaning

εἰλίπους is usually explained as “rolling the feet” (εἰλέω, “roll, turn,” + πούς, “foot”), the heavy, swaying walk of oxen; the derivation is not certain. Homer uses it only of cattle, ten times, often in the line-end formula εἰλίποδας ἕλικας βοῦς (Il. 9.466, 21.448, 23.166; Od. 1.92, 9.46), “shambling cattle with twisted horns.” The herds of cattle and flocks of sheep are the wealth of a house, and so the prey of raiders ([[raiding]]).

## Use

Andromache tells what Achilles did at Thebe:

{{quote:2ab282c3-c19f-5c9b-a3ba-c96b517a38f7}}

Her brothers were killed out among the herds, the king's sons tending the king's cattle and sheep ([[white]], [[sack-of-thebe]]). Achilles found other sons of Troy's great houses in the same place. He once bound two sons of Priam as they tended sheep on the slopes of Ida, and set them free for ransom (Il. 11.104–106), and he chased Aeneas away from his cattle on Ida when he sacked Lyrnessus and Pedasus (Il. 20.89–92). In Book 21 Poseidon reminds Apollo that he once herded Laomedon's shambling cattle in the glens of wooded Ida (Il. 21.448–449; [[wooded]]).
