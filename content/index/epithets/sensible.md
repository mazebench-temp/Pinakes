## Meaning

ἐχέφρων = ἔχω (“have, hold”) + φρένες (“mind, wits”; see [[phrenes]]): one who keeps his wits, “sensible”. The translation keeps it apart from περίφρων, “prudent”, the commoner epithet of Penelope ([[periphron]]), and from πεπνυμένος, “wise” ([[wise]]).

## Use

Achilles uses it once in the Iliad, when he answers Agamemnon's embassy: “For any man who is good and sensible / loves his own and cares for her” (*Iliad* 9.341–342; [[husbands-and-wives]]). In the Odyssey it is Penelope's: ἐχέφρων Πηνελόπεια at the end of the verse (*Odyssey* 4.111, 17.390, 24.294), and in its other cases (*Odyssey* 13.406, 16.130, 16.458, 24.198). Athena gives it once to Odysseus, praising him as quick-witted and sensible (*Odyssey* 13.332). The name of Nestor's son Echephron is the same word ([[echephron]]).
