## Meaning

γλήνη is the pupil of the eye. In its other Homeric uses it is the eye itself: Peneleos' spear drives the eyeball out of Ilioneus' socket (14.493–494), and the Cyclops' eye hisses round the burning stake (Od. 9.389–390). The plural γλήνεα are bright trinkets, kept with Priam's treasures (24.191–192). In later Greek κόρη, “girl,” is also the pupil of the eye, from the small figure seen reflected in it, and γλήνη seems to have had the same double sense: the pupil, and a doll or puppet. Only here is it a term of abuse, and the translation renders it “wretched doll.” κακός (“bad, cowardly”) is the word Diomedes was afraid of: Nestor has just told him that even if Hector calls him “a coward and a weakling” (κακὸν καὶ ἀνάλκιδα), the Trojans will not believe it (8.153–156; [[kertomia]]).

## Use

{{quote:b237aad7-3e41-5baa-b04e-3451e50d490c}}

Diomedes had foreseen this: when Nestor told him to flee, he answered that Hector would one day say among the Trojans, “The son of Tydeus fled before me to the ships” (8.148–149). Now Hector says worse. The honors of the feast ([[honor-and-dishonor]]) are taken away, the warrior is “no better than a woman,” and the man who meant to climb Troy's towers and carry off its women is called a doll. Three times Diomedes thinks of turning to fight him, and three times Zeus thunders from Ida (8.167–171; [[pondering-two-ways]], [[that-turns-the-battle]]). In the night assembly Hector boasts again that tomorrow Diomedes will lie wounded among the foremost (8.532–538). See [[hector]], [[diomedes]] and [[praise-and-blame]].
