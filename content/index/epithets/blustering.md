## Meaning

ὑπεραής = ὑπέρ (“over, beyond”) + the root of ἄημι (“blow”): “blowing beyond measure.” It occurs only here, of ἄελλα, the storm wind or squall that swoops down on the sea. Hector fights “like a storm wind” (ἶσος ἀέλλῃ) again at the Achaean wall (12.40). The same squall gives Iris an epithet, ἀελλόπος, “storm-footed” ([[storm-footed]]); compare [[stormy]] (δυσαής). In the next simile the winds of the west and the south batter the clouds and the sea ([[brightening]], [[far-wandering]]).

## Use

{{quote:2383fe2e-553e-51bf-99d4-72c94162940d}}

Zeus has given Hector his day, and he strides among the foremost “in high spirits” (11.296). The comparison with the storm on the sea follows the comparison with a hunter (11.292–295; [[wild]]) and leads into the list of the nine Achaean leaders he kills (11.299–304; [[whom-first-whom-last]]). See [[the-storm-wind-on-the-sea]], [[hector]] and [[violet-sea]].
