## Meaning

εὐρύχορος = εὐρύς (“wide”) + χορός (“dancing place, dance”): “with wide dancing floors,” a city with open spaces where the people gather for dances and festivals. The χορός was first the dancing ground and only then the dance. Some scholars have preferred a connection with χῶρος (“place”), “spacious,” but the vowel does not match, and the dancing floor is the usual explanation.

Homer uses it ten times, of Hellas (Il. 9.478), of Sicyon (Il. 23.299), and in the Odyssey of Lacedaemon, Elis, Iolcus, Thebes and other places. Compare [[wide-streeted]] (εὐρυάγυια).

## Use

{{quote:f3871a1d-5d6c-5492-a097-eb7f197db208}}

In the Catalogue the epithet is one of several that relieve a long list of Boeotian names: “Eteonus of the many ridges,” “Mycalessus with its wide dancing floors,” then [[well-built]] Medeon and “Thisbe of the many doves” ([[of-many-doves]]).
