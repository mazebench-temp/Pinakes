## Meaning

ἀργός combines two senses that seem distinct to us: “bright, white, shining” (from the root of ἄργυρος, “silver”; compare [[god-of-the-silver-bow]]) and “swift.” The link may be the flash of rapid movement. Dogs are probably called “swift” here. The formula κύνες ἀργοί is common in Homer, and Odysseus' old dog in the Odyssey is named Argos (Od. 17.292).

## Use

{{quote:cb895faf-dff7-5d33-929a-919af62d32d2}}

The god's arrows strike the animals first and then the men, as sickness in a camp might. The dogs are the same animals that the proem imagined feeding on the unburied dead ({{ref:Iliad 1.4-5}}); here they are the first to die.
