## Meaning

ἠϋγένειος = ἠΰς (“good,” here “abundant”) + γένειον (“chin, beard”): “with a fine beard,” of the lion's mane about its jaws. Homer gives it only to lions, in the verse-end λὶς ἠϋγένειος of three similes of the Iliad: the lion that appears in the path of the hunters and turns them back (15.275); the lion driven from the farmyard by dogs and men, to which Menelaus is compared as he leaves the body of Patroclus, turning back again and again (17.108–110); and the lion whose cubs a hunter has stolen, to which Achilles is compared as he groans over Patroclus (18.317–319). In the Odyssey Proteus, seized by Menelaus, becomes first λέων ἠϋγένειος, “a bearded lion” (*Odyssey* 4.456). See [[lion]].

## Outside Homer

The *Homeric Hymn to Pan* uses the word of the newborn god himself: his nurse sprang up and fled when she saw his harsh, bearded face (*Hymn to Pan* 37–39).
