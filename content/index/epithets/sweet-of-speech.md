## Meaning

ἡδυεπής = ἡδύς (“sweet, pleasant”) + ἔπος (“word”): “sweet in words.” It is a rare word in Homer. Hesiod gives the feminine ἡδυέπεια to the Muses (*Theogony* 965, 1021).

## Use

{{quote:24b70433-3226-52fc-81b1-78beba817c48}}

The epithet opens a three-part portrait of Nestor as a speaker: sweet of speech, the [[clear-voiced-orator]] of the Pylians, and a voice that flowed “sweeter than honey” ([[sweeter-than-honey]]). The emphasis on the pleasure of his words comes just after Achilles has flung down the scepter and Agamemnon sits nursing his wrath. It prepares a speech meant to calm, and its failure is part of the design of the book: the sweetest speaker in the army cannot reconcile the two men ({{ref:Iliad 1.285-303}}).
