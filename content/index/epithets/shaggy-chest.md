## Meaning

λάσιος means “hairy, shaggy, thickly grown,” of animals' coats and of thickets as well as of men. στῆθος (“chest, breast”) is where Homeric heroes feel and think: the heart (ἦτορ, κῆρ) and spirit (θυμός) live there. The Iliad also speaks of a “shaggy heart,” λάσιον κῆρ, in warriors (Il. 2.851, 16.554); a hairy chest seems to mark strength and spirit.

## Use

{{quote:c516dea4-2d1e-59ff-9ef7-e09a032e1069}}

The physical detail comes at the moment of decision. Within the warrior's shaggy chest the heart is divided between drawing the sword and checking his anger ([[pondering-two-ways]], [[decision-scene]]). The word places the deliberation in the body: the question of whether to kill is felt in the chest before it reaches the hand.
