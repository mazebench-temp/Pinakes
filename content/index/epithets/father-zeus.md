## Meaning

Ζεῦ, vocative of Ζεύς + πάτερ, vocative of πατήρ (“father”). The combination is very old. It corresponds exactly to Latin *Iuppiter*, originally the vocative *Iou-pater*, and to Sanskrit *Dyauṣ pitā*, “Father Sky.” The name Ζεύς itself goes back to the Indo-European word for the bright sky.

## Use

{{quote:d7244f28-e947-5002-a343-51566e31a6dc}}

Thetis addresses Zeus in the formal manner of prayer: the vocative, the claim of past service (“if ever I helped you, by word or deed”), and the request, “fulfill for me this wish” ([[fulfill-this-wish]], [[supplication-type-scene]]). The address is one of respect, not kinship; Thetis is not Zeus' daughter. The one who is his son, Hephaestus, calls him “our dear father” when he urges Hera to please him ({{ref:Iliad 1.578}}).
