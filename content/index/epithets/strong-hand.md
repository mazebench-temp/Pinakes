## Meaning

παχύς means “thick, stout,” and of the hand “strong,” the hand of a man who can grip and lift. The dative χειρὶ παχείῃ closes the verse eighteen times in Homer, thirteen of them in the Iliad. It is the hand that takes hold: Menelaus is left with the empty helmet of Paris in his strong hand (3.376); a man struck down leans on the ground with it (Aeneas, 5.309; Hector, 11.355); Agamemnon holds his great purple cloak in it to rally the army (8.221); Poseidon carries in it a dread sword like lightning (14.385). In the Odyssey Odysseus breaks off a leafy branch with it to cover himself before Nausicaa (Od. 6.128), and it takes up the sword against the suitors (Od. 22.326).

The verse and the next are used once more, of a goddess. In the battle of the gods Ares thrusts his spear against Athena's aegis, and she gives ground and “took up in her strong hand a stone that lay on the plain, black and jagged and great,” a boundary stone set by men of old, and strikes Ares down with it (21.403–406; [[rugged]]). Hector's stone rings on Ajax's shield; Ajax answers with a greater one, “like a millstone,” that breaks Hector's shield inward and knocks him flat (7.268–272; [[like-a-millstone]], [[stone-throwing]]).
