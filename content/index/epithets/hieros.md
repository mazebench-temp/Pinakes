## Meaning

ἱερός marks what belongs to the gods: sacred places, rites, and victims. It also has uses that are hard to explain as “holy”: a “holy” fish (Il. 16.407), the “holy might” of a king (ἱερὸν μένος), a “holy” army. For these many scholars compare Sanskrit *iṣirá-* (“vigorous, lively”), and suggest that an old sense such as “strong, filled with divine power” lies behind the Greek word.

## Use

The hecatomb is ἱερή four times, and the formula ἱερὴν ἑκατόμβην ends the verse each time. Calchas demands a holy hecatomb for Chryse ({{ref:Iliad 1.99}}); Odysseus brings it ({{ref:Iliad 1.431}}); he announces it to Chryses ({{ref:Iliad 1.443}}); and the young men set it around the altar:

The formula follows the offering from demand to delivery ([[sacrifice-type-scene]]).

The fifth use is darker. Achilles begins his account of the quarrel with the sack of a holy city:

Thebe was the city of Eetion, Andromache's father, whom Achilles killed (Il. 6.414–416). Its holiness did not protect it, and from its spoils came Chryseis.
