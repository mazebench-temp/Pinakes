## Meaning

ταλα-, the root of τλῆναι (“endure”), + ἔργον (“work”): “enduring work.” Homer gives it only to mules. In the Odyssey the mules are foals at the side of mares in Elis, ἡμίονοι ταλαεργοὶ / ἀδμῆτες, “unbroken” (*Odyssey* 4.636–637, 21.23), as the boxer's mule is unbroken in the Iliad ([[unbroken]]).

## Use

For the boxing Achilles “led in a hard-working mule and tethered it in the gathering, / six years old, unbroken, the hardest to tame,” and set a two-handled cup for the loser (23.654–656). The winner is to “lead the hard-working mule and go back to his hut” (23.662), and Epeius at once “laid hold of the hard-working mule” and claimed it before anyone had stood up against him (23.666–669). He wins it with a single blow (23.689–699; [[epeius]]). See [[mules]] and [[contest-scene]].
