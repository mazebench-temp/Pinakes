## Meaning

εὔτυκτος = εὖ (“well”) + τυκτός, verbal adjective of τεύχω (“make, fashion”): “well-made.” It praises the craftsman's work.

## Use

The epithet belongs to the helmet verse of the arming scene: κρατὶ δʼ ἐπʼ ἰφθίμῳ κυνέην εὔτυκτον ἔθηκεν, “and on his mighty head he set a well-made helmet” (3.336; [[mighty]]). The same verse is used for Patroclus (Il. 16.137) and for other heroes as they arm ([[arming-scene]]). In Book 3 the well-made helmet is at the center of the duel's end: Menelaus' sword breaks on it, and he drags Paris by it until its strap gives way (3.361–376; [[horsehair-crested]]).
