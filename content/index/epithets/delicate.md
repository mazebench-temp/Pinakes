## Meaning

λεπταλέος is a lengthened form of λεπτός, “fine, thin, delicate,” the word of the fine linen the girls of the dance wear on the same shield (λεπτὰς ὀθόνας, 18.595). Homer uses it once: the boy at the vintage “played lovely music, and to it he sang the beautiful Linus song / in a delicate voice” (18.570–571; [[the-linus-song]], [[the-vintage]]). Walter Leaf reports that Zenodotus read λίνος, the string of the lyre, which sang in unison with his treble voice, and that Aristarchus' explanation, a kind of song, is now generally accepted (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 18.570).
