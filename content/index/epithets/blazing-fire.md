## Meaning

αἰθόμενος is the participle of αἴθομαι (“burn, blaze”), from the root of αἴθω (“kindle”), of αἰθήρ (“the bright upper air”) and of αἶθοψ (“fiery-looking,” [[gleaming-bronze]]). With πῦρ (“fire”) it forms a formula, most often in the genitive πυρὸς αἰθομένοιο at the end of the verse. The Iliad uses it of the watch fires on the plain (Il. 8.563), of the fire that threatens the ships (Il. 16.81, 16.293), of the sacrifice on which Peleus poured wine (αἰθομένοις ἱεροῖσι, Il. 11.775), and in the line that ends a battle scene, “so they fought like blazing fire” (δέμας πυρὸς αἰθομένοιο, Il. 11.596, 13.673, 18.1). The Odyssey adds blazing torches (αἰθόμεναι δαΐδες, Od. 1.428). The translation renders the phrase “blazing fire,” as it renders φαεινὸν πῦρ at 5.215 ([[phaeinos]]); [[blazing]] (καυστειρή) is another word, used of battle.

## Use

{{quote:1b9cc57b-d835-5c1d-9333-f9aa99b25cec}}

The Chimaera breathes out “the terrible might of blazing fire” (δεινὸν … πυρὸς μένος αἰθομένοιο). πυρὸς μένος, “the might of fire,” is used elsewhere of the funeral fires of Patroclus (Il. 23.177, 23.238) and of Hector (Il. 24.792). Warriors in Homer come on “breathing fury” (μένεα πνείοντες, 2.536, 3.8; [[breathing-fury]]); no other creature breathes fire ([[chimaera]], [[irresistible]], [[bellerophon]]).
