## Meaning

κρατερός (also καρτερός) is formed from the root of κράτος (“strength, power, mastery”): “strong, mighty,” and of things “hard, harsh.” It is an epithet of warriors and gods. Diomedes has it in the line-end formula κρατερὸς Διομήδης, which Book 4 is the first to use (4.401, 4.411) and which recurs often in later books; Diores is “mighty” in the Catalogue ({{ref:Iliad 2.622}}), and so is Ares ({{ref:Iliad 2.515}}). A good fighter is κρατερὸς αἰχμητής, “a strong spearman,” in Helen's praise of Agamemnon ({{ref:Iliad 3.179}}; [[a-good-king-and-a-strong-spearman]]). Battle is κρατερὴ ὑσμίνη, “fierce battle” ({{ref:Iliad 2.40}}). Of things it means “harsh”: the “bitter pains” of Philoctetes (κρατέρʼ ἄλγεα, {{ref:Iliad 2.721}}), and the harsh word of a king, which has its own entry ([[stern-command]]).

## Use

Book 4 uses the word in seven lines. Athena finds Pandarus “noble and strong,” standing among “the strong ranks” of his men (4.89–90), in the words that later find Machaon among his (4.201; [[shield-bearing]]); the likeness she has taken is that of Laodocus, “a strong spearman” (4.87; [[spearmen]]).
