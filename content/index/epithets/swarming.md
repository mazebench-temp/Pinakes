## Meaning

ἁδινός means “thick, close-packed, crowded,” and then of sound “loud, vehement, frequent.” Homer uses it of flocks (μῆλ’ ἁδινά, Od. 1.92), of the heart beating fast within the chest, and often of lament: the adverb ἁδινά means “deeply, without stopping,” as in Achilles' groaning for Patroclus (Il. 23.225). Its origin is uncertain. The genitive plural ἁδινάων describes bees and flies in Book 2, and in the Odyssey the “thronging” or “loud-voiced” Sirens (Od. 23.326).

## Use

Two similes of Book 2 open with the same pair of words, ἔθνεα (“tribes”) and ἁδινάων. The army goes out to the assembly like bees:

{{quote:ba4da8de-746b-5d9d-99e3-258d5b8b58ce}}

and it stands on the plain before battle, numberless as flies:

{{quote:c963462c-35d5-5e17-8180-96456dd17579}}

In both the epithet gives the density of the crowd, the many becoming a single mass ([[swarming-bees]], [[flies-around-the-milk-pails]]). The first swarm streams toward an assembly that will end in flight; the second is eager for battle.
