## Meaning

ἐφοράω (“look upon, oversee”) + ἐπακούω (“hear, give ear to”): the Sun sees and hears everything beneath him. The relative clause serves as an epithet. The Odyssey uses the same words of Helios, whose cattle Odysseus' men must not touch (Od. 11.109, 12.323). The Sun's sight recurs in epic stories: he sees Ares and Aphrodite together and tells Hephaestus (Od. 8.270–271), and Hera is sure that not even the Sun, “whose light is the sharpest to see with,” could see her with Zeus through the golden cloud (Il. 14.344–345).

## Use

{{quote:21e4284e-c1b5-5925-be78-6308bf3433a8}}

Agamemnon calls on Zeus of Ida ([[who-rules-from-ida]]), on the Sun, on the rivers and the earth, and on the powers below who punish perjurers. The epithet says why the Sun is a witness: no false oath can be hidden from him. Menelaus had asked for a lamb for Earth and one for the Sun (3.103–104). In Book 19 Agamemnon swears again by Zeus, Earth, the Sun and the Erinyes (Il. 19.258–260). See [[helios]], [[oath]], [[the-oaths-sworn]].
