## Meaning

ἀμπελόεις = ἄμπελος (“vine”) + the suffix -όεις (“full of”): “full of vines.” Homer gives it to Epidaurus in the Argolid ({{ref:Iliad 2.561}}), to Phrygia ({{ref:Iliad 3.184}}), and to Pedasus, one of the seven towns Agamemnon offers Achilles (Il. 9.152, repeated at 9.294). Compare [[rich-in-grapes]] (πολυστάφυλος), the Catalogue's other epithet of wine country.

## Use

In the Catalogue the epithet marks Epidaurus among the towns of Diomedes' contingent ({{ref:Iliad 2.561}}). In Book 3 it opens Priam's memory of his youth: “Once before I went to vine-rich Phrygia” (3.184). The fertile valley of the Sangarius is the scene of the great Phrygian army that fought the Amazons ([[priam-and-the-amazons]]), and the praise of the land prepares the praise of its host ([[with-quick-horses]]).
