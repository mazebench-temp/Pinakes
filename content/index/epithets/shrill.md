## Meaning

λιγυρός is formed from the same root as λιγύς (“clear, shrill”), the adjective of Nestor as a “clear-voiced orator” ([[clear-voiced-orator]]) and of the heralds' voices ([[clear-voiced]]): “clear-sounding, piercing.” Homer uses it of the blast of the wind, in the phrase πνοιῇ ὕπο λιγυρῇ (5.526; Il. 13.590, 23.215), of a whip (Il. 11.532), of the bird whose likeness Sleep takes on a tree on Ida (Il. 14.290), and of the Sirens' song (Od. 12.44, 12.183). The sound is high and clear, whether it pleases or threatens.

## Use

In the simile of the clouds on the mountains, the winds scatter the shadowy clouds “with their shrill blasts when they blow” (5.525–526; [[clouds-on-the-mountains]], [[violent]]). The Danaans are like the clouds before the winds wake, and the shrill sound of the wind is what their stillness is measured against.
