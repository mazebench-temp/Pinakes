## Meaning

Διί, dative of Ζεύς + φίλος (“dear, one's own”): “dear to Zeus.” In Homer φίλος marks what belongs to someone and is cherished. To be dear to Zeus is to enjoy his favor and protection. The phrase is used of many heroes.

Twelve lines later Achilles passes the phrase on to the god whose anger is in question, in the oath by which he guarantees the seer's safety ([[oath-formulas]]):

{{quote:48ed61c8-1227-5b43-9ff4-390fee881051}}

The repetition ties the three together. Calchas speaks for Apollo, Apollo is Zeus' son ([[son-of-zeus]]), and Achilles, dear to Zeus, swears by the god dear to Zeus. The phrase that praised the hero now guarantees the prophet.
