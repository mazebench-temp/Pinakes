## Meaning

εὐρυμέτωπος = εὐρύς (“broad, wide”) + μέτωπον (“forehead, brow”): “broad of brow,” the wide forehead between the horns of an ox. Homer uses it only of cattle. In the Iliad it describes the heifer of Diomedes' vow (10.292) and, in a simile, the bulls that a farmer yokes to tread out barley on the threshing floor, as Achilles' horses trample the dead (20.495–499). In the Odyssey it is the same heifer in Nestor's vow (Od. 3.382), the cattle of Iphiclus that Neleus demanded as the bride-price for his daughter (Od. 11.289), the cattle of the Sun on Thrinacia (Od. 12.262, 12.355), and the herd that Philoetius keeps for Odysseus (Od. 20.212). Compare the other epithets of cattle, [[with-curving-horns]] (ἕλιξ), [[straight-horned]] and [[of-the-fields]].

## Use

The vow describes the victim with three epithets in two verses: a yearling, broad of brow, unbroken ([[yearling]], [[unbroken]]). The broad brow marks a strong, well-made beast, a fitting gift for the goddess. The vow repeats Nestor's at Pylos verse for verse (Od. 3.382–384). See [[cattle]], [[sacrifice]] and [[diomedes]].
