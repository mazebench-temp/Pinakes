## Meaning

φαίδιμος comes from the root φα- (“shine”), as in φάος (“light”) and φαεινός ([[phaeinos]]): “shining, bright,” and so “glorious.” It is one of Hector's standing epithets, in the line-end formula φαίδιμος Ἕκτωρ, beside [[of-the-gleaming-helmet]] and [[man-slaying]]. It is given to other heroes too, as when Teiresias addresses Odysseus as φαίδιμʼ Ὀδυσσεῦ, “shining Odysseus” (Od. 11.100), and to the body: the “shining limbs” (φαίδιμα γυῖα) of warriors struck down in battle (Il. 6.27).

## Use

Hector appears in Book 4 once, and in retreat. When Odysseus kills Democoon, “the champions and shining Hector gave ground” (4.505), and the Argives press forward until Apollo rouses the Trojans from Pergamus ([[pergamus]], [[apollo]]). The whole verse is used twice more, in the fighting over the bodies of Sarpedon and of Patroclus (Il. 16.588, 17.316). In the first battle of the poem the formula of radiance names Hector at a moment when he yields ([[hector]]).
