## Meaning

χάλκεος is the adjective of χαλκός (“bronze”; [[bronze]]), the metal of Homeric weapons and armor. The formula χάλκεος Ἄρης closes the verse. It names the god by the stuff of battle, as his name can stand for battle itself ([[ares]]). Outside Book 5 it describes him twice as a power working through men and things. In one of Nestor's stories, Lycurgus strips Areithous of the armor that “bronze Ares had given him” (Il. 7.146), and Glaucus reports that Sarpedon lies dead: “bronze Ares has brought him down under the spear of Patroclus” (Il. 16.543).

## Use

In Book 5 the formula belongs to the god in person, and all three uses fall in the last quarter of the book. The first comes in the narrator's question as Hector and Ares advance together: “Then whom first, whom last did they slay, Hector, the son of Priam, and bronze Ares?” (5.703–704). A list of six Achaeans follows ([[androktasia]]). The other two frame the moment of his wound:

Bronze meets bronze: Ares lunged over the yoke “with his bronze spear,” and Diomedes drove at him “with his bronze spear,” which Athena pushed home (5.851–857). The god of bronze bellows like an army (the simile of [[ten-thousand-men]]), and he rises to the sky like the dark mist that comes out of the clouds before a storm ([[dark-mist-from-the-clouds]]). In the battle of the gods he reminds Athena of this wound (Il. 21.396–398).
