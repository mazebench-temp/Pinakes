## Meaning

καλλίρροος = καλός (“beautiful”) + ῥόος (“stream, flow,” from ῥέω, “flow”): “fair-flowing.” The Iliad uses it three times: of the Titaressus here, of the rivers of Ida, which Poseidon and Apollo turned against the Achaean wall and then sent back to their beds (Il. 12.33), and of the two springs of the Scamander, one warm and one cold, past which Achilles chases Hector (Il. 22.147). The Odyssey gives it to the river at whose mouth Odysseus comes ashore in Scheria (*Odyssey* 5.441) and to the spring outside the city of Ithaca from which the citizens draw water (*Odyssey* 17.206), and has also καλλιρέεθρος (ῥέεθρον, “stream”), of the spring Artacia in the land of the Laestrygonians (*Odyssey* 10.107) and of [[chalcis-in-elis|Chalcis]], past which Telemachus sails from Pylos (*Odyssey* 15.295). Compare the other river epithets of *Iliad* 2, [[silver-eddying]] and [[eddying]].

## Use

{{quote:0e56549d-e799-5d97-a5b1-dd703513a223}}

The Titaressus pours its fair-flowing water into the Peneius, but the two do not mix: the Titaressus runs on top “like olive oil,” because it is a branch of the Styx ([[like-olive-oil]], [[styx]]). The fair water of the one stream is the water of the gods' oath.
