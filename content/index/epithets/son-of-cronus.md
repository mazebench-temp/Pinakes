## Meaning

Κρονίδης = Κρόνος + the patronymic suffix -ίδης; Κρονίων = Κρόνος + -ίων (compare Πηλεΐων, [[son-of-peleus]]): “son of Cronus.” In the Iliad Zeus is the son of Cronus and Rhea. He and his brothers Poseidon and Hades divided the world by lot (Il. 15.187–193), and Cronus lies imprisoned beneath the earth and sea (Il. 14.203–204).

## Use

Forms in Book 1: Κρονίωνι (397, 405), Κρονίδην (498), Κρονίωνα (502, 539), Κρονίων (528), Κρονίδη (552).

{{quote:50530b1f-a0ce-5515-b415-0081670c5444}}

The patronymic gathers where Zeus' power is in question. In Achilles' story of the binding it names the god who needed rescue and beside whom the Hundred-Hander sat ({{ref:Iliad 1.396-406}}; [[binding-of-zeus]]). In Thetis' supplication it names the god she approaches, as εὐρύοπα Κρονίδην ([[far-seeing]], 498) and as Δία Κρονίωνα ἄνακτα (“lord Zeus, son of Cronus,” 502). It is the son of Cronus who nods and shakes Olympus (528). Hera uses it twice when she challenges him: the narrator's line introducing her taunt (539) and her own address αἰνότατε Κρονίδη ([[most-dread]], 552).
