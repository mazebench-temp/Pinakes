## Meaning

ἐπιείκελος = ἐπί + εἴκελος (“like,” from the root of ἔοικα, “resemble”) + ἀθανάτοισιν, dative plural of ἀθάνατος (“immortal”; see [[immortals]]): “like the immortals.” It is a formula of praise for heroes, of a piece with [[godlike]] and [[antitheos]].

## Use

{{quote:16abb1ea-4013-5722-a08f-13cb06250cbe}}

The verse naming Theseus, son of Aegeus ([[son-of-aegeus]]), is doubtful. It is absent from many manuscripts, and it is identical with a line of the Hesiodic *Shield of Heracles* (182), in a list of Lapiths fighting Centaurs. Editors have long suspected that it was added here, perhaps to give Athens a hero in Nestor's story. If it is genuine, it is the only mention of Theseus in the Iliad.
