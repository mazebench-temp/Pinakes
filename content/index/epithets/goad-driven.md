## Meaning

κεντρηνεκής joins κέντρον (“goad”) to a form of the root ἐνεκ- (the aorist of φέρω, ἐνεγκεῖν, “carry”): “carried along by the goad,” horses driven hard. Homer uses it only in one verse, of Hera's horses, here and when she drives out again in Book 8 (5.752 = Il. 8.396). The same κέντρον is in [[goaders-of-horses]] (κέντορες ἵππων).

## Use

Hera touches the horses with the whip, the gates of heaven open of themselves, and “through them they drove the horses, spurred on by the goad” (5.748–752; [[hours]]). On the second stage of the journey, from Olympus to Troy, she whips them again, and the pair fly “not unwilling” between earth and the starry sky (5.768–769; [[with-golden-headbands]], [[high-neighing]]).
