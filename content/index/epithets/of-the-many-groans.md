## Meaning

πολύστονος = πολύς (“much, many”) + στόνος (“groaning,” from στένω, “groan”): “of many groans.” It can name what causes groaning or one who groans. Homer uses it four times. Odysseus, bringing Chryseis back to her father, says that the Achaeans have come to appease Apollo, “who has now sent grievous sorrows upon the Argives” (πολύστονα κήδεα, 1.442–445); Strife rejoices at the battle (11.73); an arrow of Teucer's strikes Cleitus, the charioteer of Polydamas, in the back of the neck (15.445–451); and in the Odyssey the disguised Odysseus begs Penelope not to ask about his family and his country, since memory makes him πολύστονος, a man of many groans (Od. 19.115–118). Compare [[groaning]] (στονόεις) and [[ugly-groaning]] (στόνος ἀεικής).

## Use

{{quote:cd06a2d2-8f77-53c4-82d3-cc1812347e24}}

Zeus sent Strife to the Achaean ships at dawn, “holding in her hands a portent of war” (11.3–4), and her shout put strength into every man (11.10–14). When the two armies meet, cutting each other down like reapers (11.67–71), she watches and rejoices, the only god in the field; the others sit in their houses on Olympus and blame Zeus (11.75–79; [[olympians]]). The epithet gives her the groans of the men she looks on. See [[strife]] and [[eris]].
