## Meaning

ἱππόβοτος = ἵππος (“horse”) + βόσκω (“feed, pasture”): “horse-feeding,” a land of meadows where horses are raised. The Argive plain was good horse country. The epithet belongs chiefly to Argos, in the formula Ἄργεος ἱπποβότοιο (“of horse-pasturing Argos”); it is also given to Tricca in Thessaly (Il. 4.202) and in the Odyssey to Elis (Od. 21.347). Telemachus uses the contrast when he declines Menelaus' gift of horses: Ithaca has no room to drive horses, and is “goat-pasturing” (αἰγίβοτος), dearer to him than a horse-pasturing land (Od. 4.601–608).

## Use

{{quote:6829645b-0fe0-5f11-82d9-60928be5bbb3}}

Odysseus reminds Agamemnon of the army's promise, made as they set out from horse-pasturing Argos, not to go home until Troy was sacked. The epithet recalls the home they left. Argos here has its wide sense, the land of the Achaeans ([[argos]]). The same half-line, ἀπ’ Ἄργεος ἱπποβότοιο, returns in Book 19, when Achilles says he had hoped that he alone would die at Troy, “far from horse-pasturing Argos” (Il. 19.329).
