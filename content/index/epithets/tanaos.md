## Meaning

ταναός is from the root of τανύω (“stretch”), as are ταναήκης, “long-edged” ([[long-edged]]), and τανύφλοιος, “smooth-barked” ([[smooth-barked]]): stretched out, long and slender. The translation renders it “long,” the word it also uses for μακρός and δολιχός ([[long-spears]]).

Homer has it once, of the αἰγανέη, the light javelin that men threw for sport, as the Myrmidons did on the shore while Achilles kept them from the war, “throwing the discus and the javelin” (2.774). In *Iliad* 16 the throw of such a javelin is a measure of distance: “and as far as the rush of a long javelin carries, / which a man throws, trying his strength, either in a contest / or in war, under the attack of enemies who destroy the spirit, / so far the Trojans gave ground” (16.589–592; [[that-destroys-the-spirit]]). See [[spear]].

## Outside Homer

In the *Homeric Hymn to Demeter* the Rarian plain, bare while Demeter hid the seed, is soon to wave with long ears of grain, ταναοῖσι … ἀσταχύεσσιν (*Hymn to Demeter* 450–456).
