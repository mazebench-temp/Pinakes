## Meaning

Τελαμώνιος is an adjective formed from the name of Ajax's father, Telamon: “of Telamon, Telamon's son.” It works like a patronymic, and Homer also uses the patronymic proper, Τελαμωνιάδης. The formula Τελαμώνιος Αἴας occurs twenty-one times in the Iliad. There were two heroes named Ajax, and the epithet keeps the greater one apart from Ajax, son of Oileus ([[swift]]); when both fight together, the dual Αἴαντε names the pair. Teucer, Ajax's half-brother, is also called Telamon's son. Compare [[son-of-neleus]] (Νηλήϊος υἱός), another adjective used as a patronymic.

## Use

{{quote:7a36300d-342c-59c6-9f8f-a6b668b1f744}}

Book 2 uses the formula twice, both times to measure the Locrian against the greater Ajax or the greater Ajax against Achilles. The Locrian Ajax is “the lesser Ajax, not so great as Telamonian Ajax” ({{ref:Iliad 2.528}}). And when the Muse is asked who was the best of the Achaeans, the answer is Telamonian Ajax, “while Achilles kept up his wrath” ([[best-of-the-achaeans]]).
