## Meaning

στρατός (“army,” and the encampment where it lives) + εὐρύς (“wide, broad”): the host spread out along the shore. The phrase describes the army as a place, and it is used with prepositions of movement: κατά or ἀνά (“throughout”), μετά (“to”).

## Use

The formula occurs at {{ref:Iliad 1.229}}, {{ref:Iliad 1.384}}, {{ref:Iliad 1.478}}, and {{ref:Iliad 1.484}}. In Achilles' speeches it measures the reach of a wrong. Throughout the wide army, he says, Agamemnon strips gifts from anyone who speaks against him (229); and in his account to Thetis, the arrows of the god ranged throughout the wide army (384).
