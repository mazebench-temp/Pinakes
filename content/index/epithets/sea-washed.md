## Meaning

ἀμφίρυτος = ἀμφί (“around”) + ῥυτός (“flowing,” from ῥέω, “flow”): “flowed around,” of land with the sea running on every side. The translation renders it “sea-washed,” and keeps “sea-girt” for ἀμφίαλος, the epithet of Ithaca ([[sea-girt]]). Merry lists ἀμφιρύτη among the compound adjectives that Homer gives a feminine ending of their own (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 1.420).

## Use

The word is found four times, always in the dative and always at the head of the verse. Three times it stands in the phrase νήσῳ ἐν ἀμφιρύτῃ, “on a sea-washed island”: twice of Ogygia, where Calypso keeps Odysseus (*Odyssey* 1.50, 1.198), and once of the island of the Sun, where Eurylochus begs Odysseus to let the weary crew land and make their supper (*Odyssey* 12.279–283). The fourth time the island is Dia, where Artemis killed Ariadne (*Odyssey* 11.324–325).
