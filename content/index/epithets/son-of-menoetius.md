## Meaning

Μενοιτιάδης = Μενοίτιος + the patronymic suffix -ιάδης: “son of Menoetius.” The longer suffix gives the name a shape that fits the verse; compare [[son-of-peleus]] (Πηληϊάδης).

## Use

{{quote:5cdba278-5db4-5190-9a57-d52a9d409ef8}}

Patroclus enters the Iliad without his own name, as the son of Menoetius who walks with Achilles back to the huts after the assembly. He is named thirty lines later, when Achilles tells him to bring out Briseis ({{ref:Iliad 1.337}}), and the narrator adds that he obeyed “his dear companion” ({{ref:Iliad 1.345}}). The patronymic remains one of his commonest names through the poem.
