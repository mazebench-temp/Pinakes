## Meaning

θεόδμητος = θεός (“god”) + the root of δέμω (“build”): “built by gods.” In Troy's case the word is literal. Poseidon says that he and Apollo built the wall of Troy for Laomedon, and fears that men will forget it for the Achaeans' wall (7.452–453; [[the-achaean-wall]]), and in Book 21 he reminds Apollo how they served Laomedon for a year, he building the wall and Apollo herding the king's cattle (21.441–457; [[poseidon-and-apollo-serve-laomedon]]). The word is found only here.

## Use

{{quote:edfcf01c-a053-5056-8301-1d77396c2099}}

Hector, camped on the plain, sets the boys and the old men to keep watch on the towers through the night ([[in-first-youth]], [[gray-templed]]). In the morning he had taunted Diomedes that he would not climb “our towers” (8.164–165), and his night orders guard the same towers from an ambush while the army is away (8.521–522). The Achaeans shelter behind a wall they built themselves, without the hecatombs that Poseidon says the gods were owed (7.436–441, 7.450; [[the-achaean-wall]]). See [[the-walls-of-troy]] and [[troy]].
