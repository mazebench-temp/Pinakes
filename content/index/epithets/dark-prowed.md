## Meaning

κυανόπρῳρος = κυάνεος (“dark, blue-black,” from κύανος, a dark blue inlay; see [[dark-brows]]) + πρῷρα (“prow”): “with a dark prow.” It names the dark of the hull, as [[black-ships]] does. In the Iliad it stands three times at the verse-end, in the genitive νηὸς (νεὸς) κυανοπρῴροιο: when Hector makes for a ship (15.693), and twice in the archery contest at the funeral of Patroclus, where the mast of a dark-prowed ship is set up as the target, with a dove tied to it (23.852, 23.878). The Odyssey uses it often of the ships of Odysseus, at the Cyclops' island, at the harbor of the Laestrygonians and before Scylla (*Odyssey* 9.482, 10.127, 12.100), and once in the longer form κυανοπρῳρείους, of the five ships of Menelaus driven to Egypt (*Odyssey* 3.299).

## Outside Homer

In the *Homeric Hymn to Apollo* the Cretan sailors, terrified by the god who has leapt onto their ship as a dolphin, do not dare to lower the sail of the dark-prowed ship, νηὸς κυανοπρώροιο, and it runs on before the South Wind (*Hymn to Apollo* 400–408). See [[ships]].
