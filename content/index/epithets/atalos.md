## Meaning

ἀταλός means “tender, young, delicate,” and is related to ἀτάλλω, “frolic,” the verb of the sea beasts that play beneath Poseidon's chariot (13.27). Homer uses the adjective of the young. Erichthonius' mares, three thousand of them, graze in the marshland, “glorying in their tender foals” (20.221–222; [[horses]]); in the Odyssey the shades that gather at Odysseus' trench include “tender girls with grief still new in their hearts” (*Odyssey* 11.39). With φρονέω, “think,” it makes the compound ἀταλάφρων, “of tender mind,” of Astyanax in his nurse's arms (6.400), and the phrase ἀταλὰ φρονέοντες, “with light hearts,” of the girls and young men at the vintage on the shield of Achilles (18.567); see [[of-tender-mind]]. The translation also uses “tender” for τέρην and ἁπαλός ([[tender]]).
