## Meaning

εὐρύ, the neuter of εὐρύς (“wide”) used as an adverb, + κρείων (“ruling, lord”; see [[powerful]]): “ruling widely.” The phrase measures the extent of a king's power. Homer also gives it to Poseidon.

## Use

The whole formula Ἀτρεΐδης εὐρὺ κρείων Ἀγαμέμνων names Agamemnon three times in Book 1, and each time his power is doing harm. At 102 he rises in anger to answer Calchas:

{{quote:ec02c80c-2742-5bdf-90d1-35ff07928185}}

The other two uses are Achilles'. In his complaint to Thetis he gives Agamemnon the full title in the very sentence that accuses him:

{{quote:6955fd04-b89b-5432-ab9d-4d635f1003a5}}

At 411 he asks that Zeus make this same wide-ruling king learn his blind folly (ἄτη) in paying no [[time|honor]] to the best of the Achaeans, when the army is penned against its ships ({{ref:Iliad 1.409-412}}). In Achilles' mouth the grand title carries bitterness: the wider the rule, the greater the wrong.
