## Meaning

χαλκοβαρής = χαλκός (“bronze”) + βαρύς (“heavy”): “weighted with bronze.” Homer uses it seven times, always of arms. In the Iliad it describes the helmet of the Trojan Oileus (11.96); the arrow that goes astray when Zeus snaps Teucer's bowstring (15.463–465); and Achilles' ash spear, which goes through Hector's neck but does not cut his windpipe, so that he can still answer (22.326–329). In the Odyssey it describes the spear Neoptolemus grips in the wooden horse (Od. 11.530–532), Odysseus' arrow, which passes through every axe (Od. 21.421–423), and twice the suitors' spears that stick in the wall (Od. 22.259, 22.276). Compare [[heavy-spear]] (ὄβριμον ἔγχος) and [[bronze-fitted]] (χαλκήρης).

The helmet of 11.96 is a στεφάνη, a kind of helmet perhaps named for its rim (7.12, 10.30; [[helmet]]).

## Use

{{quote:5a748409-eaf8-5840-a122-71774758137d}}

Agamemnon's first kills on the day of his aristeia are Bianor, “shepherd of the people,” and his companion Oileus, “driver of horses,” who has leapt down from the chariot to face him ([[shepherd-of-the-people]], [[driver-of-horses]]). The helmet is named for its weight at the moment it fails: the spear goes through the bronze and the bone. Agamemnon strips both men of their tunics and leaves them “with their chests shining bare” (11.99–100). See [[agamemnon]], [[bianor]], [[oileus-the-trojan]] and [[the-aristeia-of-agamemnon]].
