## Meaning

Οἰνεΐδης = Οἰνεύς + the patronymic suffix -ίδης (as in [[son-of-atreus]], Ἀτρεΐδης): “son of Oeneus.” Oeneus was king of Calydon in Aetolia, the father of Meleager and of Tydeus (9.533–549; 14.115–118), and Diomedes, the son of Tydeus, names him as his grandfather when he tells his descent (14.113–125). Homer uses the patronymic twice, both times in the genitive Οἰνεΐδαο and both times in a phrase that names Diomedes through his father. Athena taunts Diomedes in Book 5 that he is no true “offspring of Tydeus, the wise-hearted son of Oeneus” (5.812–813; [[wise-hearted]]). In Book 10 Diomedes himself is Οἰνεΐδαο πάϊς, “the son of Oeneus' son” (10.497). Compare [[son-of-tydeus]], his own patronymic, and [[grandson-of-aeacus]], a name from the grandfather.

## Use

{{quote:bf31520c-0daa-5b09-9018-98f86bbca9a0}}

Diomedes reaches the king of the Thracians, the thirteenth man he kills, and takes his life as he lies gasping, “for an evil dream stood over his head that night, the son of Oeneus' son, by the design of Athena” ([[onar]], [[rhesus]], [[honey-sweet]]). The phrase names Diomedes by his father and his father's father at the moment he stands over the sleeper like a dream. The verses can also be read of a real dream, by Athena's design, in which Rhesus saw him; the poem does not say which is meant ([[the-evil-dream]]). See [[diomedes]], [[tydeus]] and [[oeneus]].
