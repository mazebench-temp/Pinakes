## Meaning

λιγύφθογγος = λιγύς (“clear, high, piercing”) + φθόγγος (“voice, sound”): “clear-voiced.” λιγύς describes the sound that carries: the lyre, the wind, the Muse's song, the orator Nestor ([[clear-voiced-orator]]). A herald's voice has to reach the whole army. Homer uses the compound only of heralds, always in the same dative phrase, κηρύκεσσι λιγυφθόγγοισι, five times in all ({{ref:Iliad 2.50}}, {{ref:Iliad 2.442}}; Il. 9.10, 23.39; Od. 2.6).

After the sacrifice, on Nestor's advice, he calls it to war. The lines are the same except for one word, πόλεμον (“war”) for ἀγορήν (“assembly”) ({{ref:Iliad 2.442-444}}). Between the two summonses lie the failed test, the flight to the ships, and Odysseus' recovery of the army. The heralds' clear voices frame the day's first battle of words and announce the battle of arms. See also [[long-haired]].
