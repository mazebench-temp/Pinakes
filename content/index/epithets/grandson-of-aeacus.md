## Meaning

Αἰακίδης = Αἰακός + the patronymic suffix -ίδης: “son or descendant of Aeacus.” A Greek patronymic can name a grandfather as well as a father. Αἰακίδης is used of Peleus, Aeacus' son (Il. 18.433), and of Achilles, his grandson. Achilles traces the line himself: Zeus begot Aeacus, Aeacus begot Peleus (Il. 21.187–189). The name joins Achilles to Zeus, as [[sprung-from-zeus]] does. Compare [[son-of-peleus]], his patronymic in the narrower sense.

## Use

{{quote:81a9365c-5c70-5d61-918e-9d23ebee7634}}

The patronymic appears twice in the last lines of the Trojan Catalogue, in a repeated verse: Ennomus the augur and one of the Carian leaders were “brought down at the hands of the swift-footed grandson of Aeacus in the river” ({{ref:Iliad 2.860}}, {{ref:Iliad 2.874}}). The phrase ποδώκεος Αἰακίδαο ([[swift-footed]]) is a line-end formula used ten times in Homer. The Catalogue ends by looking ahead to Achilles' return to battle and the slaughter in the Scamander in Book 21 ([[the-river-battle-foretold]]).
