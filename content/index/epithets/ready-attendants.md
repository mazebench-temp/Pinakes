## Meaning

ὀτρηρός (“quick, ready, busy”) is related to ὀτρύνω (“urge on”). A θεράπων is a companion in service, a squire or attendant of rank rather than a slave; Patroclus is Achilles' θεράπων. Both words are in the dual, for the pair of heralds.

## Use

{{quote:ed0cdb8f-3cb4-59e0-b3b4-a89d73960de1}}

The epithet describes their office, not their feelings. When they go, they go unwillingly (ἀέκοντε, {{ref:Iliad 1.327}}), and before Achilles they stand in fear and silence ({{ref:Iliad 1.331-332}}). The Odyssey pairs the same words, κήρυκες and ὀτρηροὶ θεράποντες, for the servants who wait on the suitors (Od. 1.109). See [[messenger-scene]].
