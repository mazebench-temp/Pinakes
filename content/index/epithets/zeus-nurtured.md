## Meaning

διοτρεφής = Διός (“of Zeus”) + τρέφω (“rear, nourish”): “reared by Zeus.” Kings hold their office from Zeus. Odysseus says so in Book 2: “Great is the spirit of kings whom Zeus has nurtured; a king's honor comes from Zeus, and Zeus the counselor loves him” (Il. 2.196–197). Compare [[sprung-from-zeus]] (διογενής) and [[scepter-bearing]] (σκηπτοῦχος).

## Use

{{quote:14a2cd16-1b2c-5cd6-8a37-e69db0aee75b}}

Agamemnon frames his insult in the language of royal dignity: of all the Zeus-nurtured kings, Achilles is the most hateful to him. The epithet admits that Achilles is a king in Zeus' care even as Agamemnon disowns him, and two lines later he adds that if Achilles is strong, it was a god who gave him that ({{ref:Iliad 1.178}}). Only a line before, Agamemnon claimed Zeus' favor for himself ([[counselor]], {{ref:Iliad 1.175}}).
