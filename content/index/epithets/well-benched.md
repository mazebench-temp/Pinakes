## Meaning

ἐΰσσελμος = εὖ (“well”) + σέλμα, plural σέλματα, the planking of a ship: its benches or its decking. Which is meant is uncertain, and the translation takes “benches.” Homer uses the adjective of ships twenty-nine times. Compare [[many-benched]].

## Use

The genitive formula νηὸς ἐϋσσέλμοιο μελαίνης (“the black, well-benched ship”; [[black-ships]]) links two scenes. When the army rushes for the ships, Odysseus stands still: “he had not laid a hand on his black, well-benched ship” ({{ref:Iliad 2.170}}). Later Nestor turns the same gesture into a threat:

{{quote:c406ac00-9cee-5bc4-88fd-cbcd58d1afe0}}

Odysseus does not touch his ship (ἅπτετ’, {{ref:Iliad 2.171}}); Nestor tells any man who wants to go home to touch his (ἁπτέσθω), so that he may die first. The same words mark loyalty and desertion.

In the Catalogue the epithet describes the ships Agamemnon lent the Arcadians, an inland people “who cared nothing for the works of the sea” ({{ref:Iliad 2.612-614}}; [[wine-dark]]).
