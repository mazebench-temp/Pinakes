## Meaning

φιλήρετμος = φίλος (“dear, loving”) + ἐρετμόν (“oar”): “fond of the oar,” of a people who live by rowing and sailing. The Iliad does not have it.

## Use

The word is used eight times, always in the dative plural and always at the same place in the verse, after the name of the people. Twice it is the Taphians: Athena, in the likeness of Mentes, names herself with the verse “and I am lord over the Taphians, lovers of the oar” (Ταφίοισι φιληρέτμοισιν ἀνάσσω, *Odyssey* 1.181), and Telemachus repeats it to Eurymachus (*Odyssey* 1.419). Six times it is the Phaeacians (*Odyssey* 5.386, 8.96, 8.386, 8.535, 11.349, 13.36). Their king has the same verse-end as Mentes when he grants the queen's word about the gifts for Odysseus: it shall be so, “as surely as I live and am lord over the Phaeacians, lovers of the oar” (Φαιήκεσσι φιληρέτμοισιν ἀνάσσω, *Odyssey* 11.348–349). Four of the six are one verse, αἶψα δὲ Φαιήκεσσι φιληρέτμοισι μετηύδα, “and at once he spoke among the Phaeacians, lovers of the oar” (*Odyssey* 8.96, 8.386, 8.535, 13.36).
