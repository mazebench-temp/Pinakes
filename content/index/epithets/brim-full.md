## Meaning

ἐπιστεφής goes with ἐπιστέφομαι, the verb of the feast in which “the young men filled the mixing bowls brim-full with drink” (κρητῆρας ἐπεστέψαντο ποτοῖο, 1.470; [[cupbearing]], [[feast-type-scene]]). Both come from στέφω (“crown, put around”): the wine stands at the rim of the bowl. The adjective occurs twice in Homer, both times of bowls of wine at the end of the verse: on Lemnos (8.232), and when Telemachus' crew, ready to sail from Ithaca by night, set up bowls brim-full of wine and pour libations to the gods (Od. 2.430–432). See [[mixing-bowl]] and [[wine]].

## Use

Agamemnon recalls the feast on Lemnos at which the Achaeans boasted: they were “eating much meat of straight-horned cattle and drinking mixing bowls brim-full of wine” (8.231–232). The full bowl is the measure of the feast and of the boasts it produced, which the battle has emptied ([[empty-boasting]], [[straight-horned]], [[lemnos]]). Lemnos is the island from which the wine that the Achaeans drank at the end of Book 7 came (7.467–475; [[euneus]]).
