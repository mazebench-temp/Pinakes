## Meaning

γλυκύς (“sweet”) + θυμός (“heart, temper”; [[thymos]]): “of sweet temper, kindly.” Homer has it once, with its companion [[gentle-hearted]] (ἀγανόφρων), as Tros, son of Alastor, clasps Achilles' knees: “for he was no sweet-tempered man, nor gentle-hearted, / but in a great fury” (20.467–468; [[supplication]], [[achilles]]). Walter Leaf thinks it a strange compound, since γλυκύς is used of things that give pleasure, song and sleep, and never of the mind itself; he finds the poet's judgment of Achilles alien to the epic manner and suspects 463–468 (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 20.463; see the translation's note on 20.463–468).
