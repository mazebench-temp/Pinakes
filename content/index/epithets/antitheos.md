## Meaning

ἀντίθεος = ἀντί (“over against, matching”) + θεός (“god”): “a match for the gods, godlike.” It is a general term of praise for heroes of the past and present, and in the Odyssey even for the suitors. It expresses the same idea as [[godlike]] (θεοείκελος) and [[like-the-immortals]] (ἐπιείκελος ἀθανάτοισιν), with different words.

## Use

{{quote:868930aa-7f17-5c23-b159-5d1b2bbbb2da}}

The Polyphemus of Nestor's list is a Lapith, one of the heroes who fought the Centaurs ([[catalogue-of-lapiths]]), not the Cyclops of the Odyssey. Yet the Odyssey uses the same phrase for the Cyclops, ἀντίθεον Πολύφημον, at the end of the verse as here (Od. 1.70). The noun-epithet formula was made for the name, whoever bears it: a clear case of an epithet that describes nothing about the particular man.
