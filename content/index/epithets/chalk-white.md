## Meaning

ἀργινόεις comes from the root ἀργ- (“white, bright,” as in ἄργυρος, “silver,” and ἀργός, “shining”) with the suffix -όεις: “white, gleaming.” It probably describes towns seen from the sea, on or under cliffs of white stone. It occurs only twice in Homer, both times in the Catalogue and in the same form and place in the verse.

## Use

{{quote:48248bc9-f36a-5050-a76f-1920c2fbb93a}}

Lycastus is one of the towns of Idomeneus in Crete. A few lines later Cameirus, one of the three cities of Rhodes, has the same epithet: “Lindus and Ialysus and chalk-white Cameirus” ({{ref:Iliad 2.656}}). The Catalogue's islands are seen as a sailor would see them, as coasts and landmarks.
