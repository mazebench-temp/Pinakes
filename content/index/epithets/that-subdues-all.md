## Meaning

πανδαμάτωρ = πᾶς (“all”) + δαμάτωρ, an agent noun from δάμνημι (“tame, subdue”), the verb of [[tamer-of-horses]]: “the tamer of all.” Night has a word of the same family in *Iliad* 14, δμήτειρα, “tamer of gods and men” ([[tamer-of-gods-and-men]]).

## Use

Both uses end the same two-verse phrase, ὕπνος / ᾕρει πανδαμάτωρ, “sleep that subdues all took him.” In the Iliad it is denied: at the beginning of the last book the others take their fill of sweet sleep, “but Achilles / wept, remembering his dear companion, and sleep / that subdues all did not take him” (24.3–5; [[sweet-sleep]], [[hypnos]]). In the Odyssey it takes the Cyclops when he has drunk Odysseus' wine (*Odyssey* 9.371–373). See [[achilles]].
