## Meaning

ἦνις (accusative ἦνιν, plural ἤνις) was explained by the ancient glossators as “a year old,” and the translation follows them; the sense is not certain. Homer uses it five times, always of cattle vowed to Athena. Three times it is the twelve cows that Hecuba and the Trojan women promise her, “yearlings, never touched by the goad,” in a verse spoken by Helenus, by Hector and by Theano (ἤνις ἠκέστας, 6.93–94, 6.274–275, 6.308–309; [[never-touched-by-the-goad]]). Twice it is the heifer of a vow repeated word for word in the Iliad and the Odyssey, a yearling, broad of brow, unbroken, with gold poured around her horns: Diomedes' vow on the night of the raid (10.292–294) and Nestor's at Pylos (Od. 3.382–384). Each time the victim is valued because it has never worked ([[unbroken]], [[broad-browed]]). The Trojan women's prayer is refused (6.311); the other two are heard, and each ends with the same half-verse, “and Pallas Athena heard” (10.295; Od. 3.385).

## Use

{{quote:22403eed-4545-5df6-a313-cfcc580aa9d1}}

Diomedes ends his prayer with the vow ([[prayer]], [[sacrifice]], [[athena]]). He has asked Athena to go with him as she went with his father Tydeus to Thebes (10.284–291; [[tydeus-at-thebes]]); the yearling, unbroken heifer with gilded horns is what he promises in return. The vow is heard (10.295), and its fulfillment is not told. See [[diomedes]] and [[cattle]].
