## Meaning

Ὀλύμπια (“Olympian,” the neuter plural adjective of Ὄλυμπος) + δώματα (“houses”) + ἔχοντες (“having, holding”): “who hold Olympian homes.” At the end of Book 1 the narrator shows those homes: each god has a house built by Hephaestus ({{ref:Iliad 1.606-608}}; [[with-cunning-skill]]).

## Use

{{quote:461a8d7d-c8c7-58cc-bf43-3c08950d372b}}

Chryses begins his plea with a wish in the name of all the gods of Olympus: may they grant the Achaeans the sack of Troy and a safe return. The courteous formula puts the gods on the Achaeans' side just before the priest asks for his daughter, and it makes Agamemnon's refusal a slight to them all.
