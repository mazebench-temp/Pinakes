## Meaning

Ὀλύμπιος is the adjective of Ὄλυμπος, the mountain home of the gods ([[olympus]]): “Olympian, dwelling on Olympus.” In the plural it names the gods as a body, as in “the other Olympians” (Ὀλύμπιοι ἄλλοι, {{ref:Iliad 1.399}}; see [[olympians]]). In the singular it belongs to Zeus, the master of the mountain. Book 1 also has the collective formula Ὀλύμπια δώματ’ ἔχοντες ([[homes-on-olympus]]).

## Use

Six times in Book 1. With other titles: Ὀλύμπιος … Ζεὺς ὑψιβρεμέτης (Achilles, {{ref:Iliad 1.353-354}}; see [[thunders-on-high]]), Ὀλύμπιε μητίετα Ζεῦ (Thetis, {{ref:Iliad 1.508}}; see [[counselor]]), and Ὀλύμπιος ἀστεροπητής ([[lord-of-the-lightning]], 580, 609).

Alone, as a name for Zeus, it comes in Hephaestus' speech to his mother:

In a few lines Hephaestus names “the Olympian” three times: the god who could blast them all from their seats (580), the god who will be gracious if approached gently (583), and the god it is hard to stand against, who once hurled Hephaestus from heaven (589; [[fall-of-hephaestus]]). The title lets him speak of his father's power without naming the father, whom he calls “dear father Zeus” only when urging his mother to please him ({{ref:Iliad 1.578}}).
