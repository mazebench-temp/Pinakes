## Meaning

In later Greek φοῖβος is an adjective meaning “bright, pure,” and ancient writers explained Apollo's name that way. Whether that was its original sense is unknown. In Homer the word is only a name, and it almost always stands before Ἀπόλλων in the formula Φοῖβος Ἀπόλλων, which closes the verse.

## Use

Φοῖβος Ἀπόλλων ends the verse five times in Book 1 ({{ref:Iliad 1.43}}, {{ref:Iliad 1.64}}, {{ref:Iliad 1.72}}, {{ref:Iliad 1.182}}, {{ref:Iliad 1.457}}), and the occasions mark the god's part in the story. He hears the prayer that brings the plague (43); he is angry (64); he gave Calchas his prophecy (72); he takes Chryseis from Agamemnon (182); he hears the prayer that ends it (457).

{{quote:cbb72cac-69b1-51c8-8820-9f7767d966b6}}

Once the name stands alone, in Odysseus' speech to Chryses: “to offer a holy hecatomb to Phoebus” ({{ref:Iliad 1.443}}).

## Outside Homer

In Hesiod's *Theogony* Phoebe (Φοίβη) is a Titaness (136) and the mother of Leto (404–406), and so Apollo's grandmother. Aeschylus makes Phoebe give the oracle at Delphi to Phoebus as a birthday gift, and Phoebus take his name from her (*Eumenides* 7–8).
