## Meaning

κυνὸς ὄμματ’ ἔχων = “having the eyes of a dog” (κύων, “dog”; ὄμμα, “eye”); κραδίην δ’ ἐλάφοιο = “and the heart of a deer” (κραδίη, the epic form of καρδία, “heart”; ἔλαφος, “deer”). Each animal stands for a vice. The dog is shameless (compare [[dog-face]]). The deer is the Iliad's image of panic: Agamemnon himself rebukes hesitant troops for standing dazed “like fawns” (Il. 4.243).

## Use

{{quote:84c191ed-85e7-5552-858c-4aafa937606a}}

The two halves set face against heart: bold in looks, craven inside. The lines that follow explain the second half. Agamemnon never arms for battle with the people or goes on an ambush with the best men, the hardest test of courage; “to you that looks like death.” The insult follows [[wine-sodden]] in the same verse.

## Outside Homer

Plato quotes the line in the *Republic* (3.389e) among verses unfit for the young (see [[wine-sodden]]).
