## Meaning

ἐναλίγκιος (“like, resembling”) + αὐδήν (“in voice,” an accusative of respect, like πόδας in πόδας ὠκύς, [[swift-footed]]): “like a god in his voice.” The half-verse closes the line. In the Iliad it is said once, of Talthybius, as he stands with the boar beside Agamemnon at the oath (19.250). In the Odyssey the plural θεοῖς ἐναλίγκιος αὐδήν praises the singer, in the same verse both times: Telemachus asks the suitors to stop their noise, since it is a fine thing to hear a singer like Phemius, like the gods in voice (*Odyssey* 1.368–371), and Odysseus begins his tale to the Phaeacians with the same praise of Demodocus (*Odyssey* 9.3–4). The herald, whose voice must carry through an army, is praised as the singer is. Compare the other epithets of heralds, [[clear-voiced]] (λιγύφθογγος), [[loud-voiced]] (ἠπύτα) and [[far-voiced]] (ἠερόφωνος), and [[with-human-voice]] (αὐδήεις). See [[heralds]] and [[talthybius]].
