## Meaning

κύδιστος is a superlative formed from κῦδος, the glory that a god grants or that victory brings. Ἀτρεΐδη κύδιστε is a formula of respectful address. It opens a whole line elsewhere in the Iliad, Ἀτρεΐδη κύδιστε, ἄναξ ἀνδρῶν Ἀγάμεμνον (for example Il. 2.434, spoken by Nestor), and Zeus himself is invoked as Ζεῦ κύδιστε μέγιστε (Il. 2.412).

## Use

{{quote:2860e5c5-2a8f-5daf-907b-41f24f69f7b8}}

Achilles begins with the honorific the occasion requires. The expected continuation of the verse is ἄναξ ἀνδρῶν Ἀγάμεμνον ([[lord-of-men]]), which would fill the second half of the line. In its place, filling the same part of the verse, he puts φιλοκτεανώτατε πάντων, “most covetous of all men” ([[most-covetous]]). Two superlatives stand side by side in the vocative, one of glory and one of greed, and the second takes the place of the king's title.
