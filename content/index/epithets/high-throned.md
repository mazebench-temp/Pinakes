## Meaning

ὑψίζυγος = ὕψι (“on high”) + ζυγόν (the yoke of a team, and the cross-bench of a ship): “with a high bench,” that is, sitting high above others, ruling on high. The picture behind the word is uncertain, and it is usually rendered “high-throned.” Homer gives it only to Zeus, four times in the Iliad, always with his name or his patronymic: Ζεὺς … Κρονίδης ὑψίζυγος here (4.166), Κρονίδης ὑψίζυγος (Il. 7.69, 18.185), and Ζεὺς … πατὴρ … ὑψίζυγος (Il. 11.544).

## Use

{{quote:0175c964-c192-573a-8b86-5ca3d321cc86}}

Agamemnon's prophecy of the fall of Troy piles up the titles of Zeus: son of Cronus, throned on high, dwelling in the upper air ([[son-of-cronus]], [[dwelling-in-the-upper-air]]). The god who sits above all will shake his dark aegis over the Trojans “in resentment at this deceit” ([[aegis]], [[apate]]). Agamemnon is sure that the oaths will be fulfilled, even if late (4.160–162). In Book 7 Hector gives the opposite verdict with the same title: the son of Cronus, throned on high, “did not fulfill our oaths” (Il. 7.69).

## Outside Homer

Hesiod uses the phrase Κρονίδης ὑψίζυγος, αἰθέρι ναίων, the same words as Il. 4.166, of the Zeus who set the elder and better Strife in the roots of the earth (*Works and Days* 17–19; [[strife]]).
