## Meaning

αἰθρηγενής = αἴθρη (“clear sky, bright weather”) + the root of γίγνομαι (“be born”): “born of the clear sky.” The North Wind blows from a clear sky and brings the cold. In the Iliad the epithet stands twice in the same verse, ψυχρὴ (ψυχραί) ὑπὸ ῥιπῆς αἰθρηγενέος Βορέαο, of snow driven by the North Wind: in the simile of Iris' flight from Ida (15.170–172), and in the simile of the helmets and shields pouring from the ships like snowflakes when the Achaeans arm in Book 19 (19.357–359). In the Odyssey the North Wind is αἰθρηγενέτης, of the same sense, among the four winds that fall together on Odysseus' raft (*Odyssey* 5.295–296). See [[boreas]].
