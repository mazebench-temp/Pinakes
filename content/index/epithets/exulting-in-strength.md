## Meaning

σθένος is strength, the force of a man, a god or a beast. The verb βλεμεαίνω occurs only with σθένεϊ, “in his strength,” and its sense, to exult or rage in one's might, comes from these passages; its origin is uncertain.

The phrase ends the verse six times in the Iliad. Twice it is Hector's: here, and in Book 9, when Odysseus tells Achilles that Hector, “exulting greatly in his strength,” rages terribly, trusting in Zeus (9.237–239). Once it is Hephaestus', as the gods go down to the battle among themselves (20.36). Three times it describes beasts in similes: a boar or a lion that wheels about among the hunters and their dogs (12.41–42), the spirit of a boar in its breast (17.20–22), and a lion that stands over its cubs when hunters meet him in the wood (17.133–136).

## Use

Teucer has been carried wounded to the ships, and Zeus again gives the Trojans their might ([[olympian]], [[menos]]). Hector goes among the foremost, and the simile that follows turns the formula's usual picture around. Elsewhere the boar or lion is the one exulting in its strength while the dogs and hunters close in; here Hector is the hound that snaps at the haunches of a boar or lion from behind, “always killing the hindmost” (8.338–342; [[dogs]], [[boar]], [[lion]]). The Achaeans flee across the ditch and the stakes to the ships (8.343–347). See [[hector]].
