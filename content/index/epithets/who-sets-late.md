## Meaning

ὀψέ (“late”) + δύων, participle of δύω (“sink, set”): “setting late.” Merry gives two explanations. Boötes may be said to set late because the brilliance of his light keeps him visible long after dawn; or, as Sir G. C. Lewis suggested, “slow-setting” refers to the length of his setting, since the constellation goes down nearly upright and slowly, while it rises lying flat and comes quickly into view; Merry compares Catullus 66.67–68 (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 5.272). The participle stands once more in Homer, of the evening: δείελος ὀψὲ δύων, “the evening, late in its setting” (*Iliad* 21.232).

## Use

The epithet comes in the verses of Odysseus' night watch on the raft: sleep does not fall on his eyelids as he watches the Pleiades, Boötes, and the Bear, which Calypso bade him keep on his left hand (*Odyssey* 5.270–277; [[pleiades]], [[the-bear]]). Merry, taking the season to be about the autumn equinox and the course eastward, reckons that near midnight Boötes would be setting in the northwest while the Pleiades rose in the southeast, with Orion following an hour or two later (Merry on 5.273; [[orion]]). See [[bootes]].
