## Meaning

υἷες (“sons”) + Ἀχαιῶν (“of the Achaeans”): a periphrasis for the Achaeans themselves, like κοῦροι Ἀχαιῶν, “the young men of the Achaeans” ({{ref:Iliad 1.473}}). It fills the last two feet of the verse.

## Use

The formula occurs six times in Book 1: {{ref:Iliad 1.162}}, {{ref:Iliad 1.237}}, {{ref:Iliad 1.240}}, {{ref:Iliad 1.276}}, {{ref:Iliad 1.368}}, {{ref:Iliad 1.392}}. Four of them concern the distribution of prizes. The sons of the Achaeans gave Achilles his prize (162, 392), first gave Briseis to him (276, in Nestor's words), and divided the spoils of Thebe (368).

{{quote:96b00c28-bc8e-5c85-be9f-b70c2a30082a}}

The repetition supports a point Achilles insists on: his prize ([[geras]]) came from the army, not from Agamemnon, so Agamemnon has no right to take it. Nestor concedes the point even while urging Achilles to yield.

The other two occurrences belong to Achilles' oath on the scepter. The sons of the Achaeans carry the scepter when they give judgment (237), and the sons of the Achaeans will one day long for Achilles (240, in the accusative, υἷας Ἀχαιῶν). The army that gives honor is the army that will feel the lack of him.
