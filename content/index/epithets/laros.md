## Meaning

λαρός means “pleasant to the taste, sweet,” a word of eating and drinking. Achilles, mourning Patroclus, remembers how he used to set a pleasant meal before him in the hut (19.315–316). In the Odyssey a dinner is λαρόν (*Odyssey* 12.283, 14.408), and the wine that Telemachus asks of his nurse is the sweetest after the one she keeps for Odysseus, λαρώτατος (*Odyssey* 2.349–350). In Book 17 the word is given to the fly's taste for human blood (17.572).
