The Rhodius is the fourth of the rivers that bring the Achaean wall to nothing (12.20; see [[the-achaean-wall]]). The name is accented Ῥοδίος; the adjective “Rhodian” is Ῥόδιος (see the translation's note on 12.19–22).

## Outside Homer

Hesiod names it among the rivers that Tethys bore to Ocean (*Theogony* 341; see [[hesiod]]). [[strabo|Strabo]] says that it reaches the sea between [[Abydos]] and Dardanus, opposite the Cynossema, the “Dog's Tomb” on the Thracian Chersonese, “which they say is the tomb of Hecuba”; others, he adds, say that it flows into the Aesepus, and “it too is one of the rivers named by the poet” (13.1.28; see [[hecuba]] and [[aesepus]]). Reporting Demetrius of Scepsis, he says that it flows from Cleandria and Gordus, sixty stades from the tree called the Fair Pine, into the Aenius (13.1.44).

An ancient note says that the Rhodius was later called the Dardanus and flows from Cleandria, sixty stades from the Pine (scholium T on 12.20).
