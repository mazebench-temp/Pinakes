## Etymology

Κηφισίς is an adjective made from Κηφισός: “the lake of the Cephisus”, the river of Phocis and Boeotia (see [[cephisus]]). The phrase λίμνῃ κεκλιμένος Κηφισίδι, “lying against the Cephisian lake”, is found only here; the translation renders it “on the shore of the Cephisian lake” (5.709).

## Which lake

The Cephisus flows into Lake Copais, the great lake of northern Boeotia, and some ancient readers took the Cephisian lake to be Copais. Strabo disagreed. Homer, he says, does not mean Copais, “as some think”, but the lake called Hylice after the nearby village of Hyle: a small lake between Thebes and Anthedon, filled from Copais through underground channels (9.2.20; see [[hyle]]). On Strabo's own account the river's water reaches both lakes, so the name fits either, and the poem does not settle the question. The land around it is “very rich” (μάλα πίονα δῆμον, 5.710), and the Boeotians who lived there are named with Oresbius (see [[oresbius]] and [[boeotians]]).
