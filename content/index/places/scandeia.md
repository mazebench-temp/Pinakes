Homer names it once, in the history of the helmet that Meriones gives Odysseus for the night raid: Autolycus stole the helmet from Amyntor's house at Eleon, “and at Scandeia he gave it to Amphidamas of Cythera” (10.268; see [[the-boars-tusk-helmet]], [[autolycus]] and [[amphidamas]]). Σκάνδειαν is an accusative of place without a preposition, “at Scandeia”, as the translation takes it, or “to Scandeia”. The poem does not say where the place was. Since the man who received the helmet was “of Cythera”, the later identification with the harbor of Cythera fits the verse.

## Outside Homer

When the Athenians attacked Cythera in 424 BC, ten ships and two thousand Milesian hoplites took “the city on the sea called Scandeia”, and after the surrender the Athenians held “Scandeia, the town at the harbor” (Thucydides 4.54.1, 4.54.4; see [[thucydides]] and [[cythera]]). Pausanias calls Scandeia the port of Cythera, with the city about ten stades inland (Pausanias 3.23.1).
