The Maeonians were “born beneath Tmolus” (ὑπὸ Τμώλῳ γεγαῶτας, 2.866; see [[maeonians]]). Homer names the mountain once more, as the birthplace of Iphition, whom Achilles kills, “beneath snowy Tmolus, in the rich land of Hyde” (20.385; see [[iphition]], [[hyde]] and [[niphoeis]]). In the same passage Achilles places Iphition's birthplace by the Gygaean lake and the rivers Hyllus and Hermus (20.390–392; see [[gygaean-lake]], [[hyllus]] and [[hermus]]).

## Outside Homer

The range, the modern Boz Dağ, rises south of Sardis. Herodotus tells how the Pactolus, bringing gold dust down from Tmolus, flowed through the middle of the market place of Sardis into the Hermus (5.101.2; see [[herodotus]]), and [[Strabo]] that the wealth of Croesus and his ancestors was said to have come from it, though the gold dust had given out by his day; from a lookout of white marble on the summit, built by the Persians, the plains around could be seen (*Geography* 13.4.5). Pliny the Elder says that Sardis was founded on the side of Tmolus, which was earlier called Timolus (*Natural History* 5.110). Tmolus was also a mountain of Dionysus: the chorus of Euripides' *Bacchae* are Lydian women who have followed the god from there (*Bacchae* 55, 64–65; see [[euripides]]).
