εὐρὺ ῥέων, "broad-flowing". The second line takes up the river's name again to praise it. The Trojan catalogue repeats a name at the start of the next line three times: here, with Asius (2.837–838), and with Nastes (2.870–871). In Book 21 the Paeonian Asteropaeus tells Achilles that his family comes from the Axius, who fathered his grandfather Pelegon, and he repeats the praise (21.141–143, 21.157–160). Some ancient critics, who knew the Axius as a muddy river, read the line differently: the clear water of a spring called Aea spreads over the Axius (Strabo, *Geography*, fragments of Book 7). The Axius is the modern Vardar, which rises in North Macedonia and enters the Thermaic Gulf west of Thessaloniki as the Axios.
