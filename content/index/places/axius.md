## Descriptions

εὐρὺ ῥέων, “broad-flowing”, in the verse-end ἀπʼ Ἀξιοῦ εὐρὺ ῥέοντος, “from the broad-flowing Axius” (2.849 = 16.288), which Asteropaeus takes up for his lineage (21.157; see [[broad-flowing]]). εὐρυρέεθρος, “of the broad streams”, is found only of the Axius, when the narrator names it as Asteropaeus' grandfather (21.141), and the same verses call it ποταμὸς βαθυδίνης, “the deep-eddying river” (21.143; see [[deep-eddying]]). The Catalogue praises its water, “whose water is the fairest that spreads over the earth” (2.850), and Asteropaeus repeats the praise, “who sends the fairest water over the earth” (21.158).

## Outside Homer

The praise of the water troubled ancient readers, since the Axius is a muddy river. [[Strabo]] explains it by a spring called Aea near Amydon, which pours its very clear water into the Axius, and reports that some rewrote the verse of the Catalogue so that the fairest water was the spring's, spreading over the Axius, not the river's spreading over the earth; he thinks the received reading faulty (*Geography* 7, fr. 21, fr. 23; see [[amydon]]). He says that the Paeonians once held the country about the Axius, which was called Amphaxitis after it (7, fr. 11), and that the river flows out of Paeonia into Macedonia through a narrow pass and reaches the sea between Chalastra and Therme (7, fr. 4, fr. 20). In Aeschylus' *Persians* the army of Xerxes, retreating from Greece, comes through the land of the Macedonians “to the ford of the Axius” (492–493; see [[aeschylus]]), and in Euripides' *Bacchae* the chorus sings that Dionysus will cross “the swift-flowing Axius” leading his whirling Maenads (568–570; see [[euripides]]). The Axius is the modern Vardar, which rises in North Macedonia and enters the Thermaic Gulf west of Thessaloniki as the Axios.
