## In Homer

Neion is named as the landmark of a harbor: “in the harbor of Rheithron, under wooded Neion” (*Odyssey* 1.186; see [[rheithron]]). Telemachus tells Nestor that he and his companions have come ἐξ Ἰθάκης ὑπονηίου, “from Ithaca under Neion” (*Odyssey* 3.81). Ithaca's mountain in the Catalogue and in Odysseus' own description is Neritum, “with its quivering leaves” (2.632; *Odyssey* 9.21–22; see [[neritum]] and [[ithaca]]).

{{notes}}

## Outside Homer

Strabo reads Telemachus' verse as ἐξ Ἰθάκης ὑπὸ Νηίου, with the preposition apart, and says that it is unclear whether the poet means Neion to be the same as Neritum, or another mountain or place (*Geography* 10.2.11).
