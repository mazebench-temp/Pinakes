## In Homer

{{quote:6fe13aea-71c8-5d38-89ce-9d0171ec9f1c}}

Scyros enters the poem in the last lines of the embassy. Patroclus sleeps beside Iphis, “whom brilliant Achilles gave him / when he took steep Scyros, the citadel of Enyeus” (9.667–668; see [[iphis]], [[enyeus]] and [[patroclus]]). αἰπεῖα, “steep”, is the epithet of hill towns and mountains (see [[steep]]). Scyros is one of the cities that Achilles sacked, like Lesbos (9.129) and Eetion's Thebe (9.188; see [[achilles-raids]]).

The Iliad names Scyros once more, as the place where Achilles' son is growing up. Mourning Patroclus, Achilles says that nothing worse could happen to him, not even news of his father's death, “or of the one who is being reared for me in Scyros, my dear son, if godlike Neoptolemus still lives” (19.326–327). He had hoped that Patroclus would bring the boy home from Scyros to Phthia and show him his house and his possessions (19.328–333). In the Odyssey Odysseus tells the shade of Achilles that he himself brought Neoptolemus from Scyros in his ship to join the Achaeans (Od. 11.506–509).

How the two fit together the poem does not say: a city that Achilles sacked, and the place where his son was reared. It may be the same island, taken before the war or on the way to Troy, or another town of the same name; the poem gives no hint of when Achilles was there, or who Neoptolemus' mother was.

## Outside Homer

The *Homeric Hymn to Apollo* names Scyros among the places Leto passed in her wanderings (3.35). Later tradition filled the gap. In Proclus' summary of the *Cypria*, Achilles is driven to Scyros by a storm on the way back from Mysia and marries Deidameia, daughter of Lycomedes. In another version Thetis, knowing that her son would die at Troy, disguised him as a girl and hid him among the daughters of Lycomedes; he fathered Pyrrhus, later called Neoptolemus, on Deidameia, and Odysseus found him out (Apollodorus, *Library* 3.13.8). Euripides wrote a *Scyrians*, now lost; in one fragment Lycomedes is told that his daughter is ill and in danger (fr. 682 Nauck). Lycomedes of Scyros is not the Achaean Lycomedes, son of Creon (9.84; see [[lycomedes]]).

Strabo says that Scyros was famous above all for the bond between Lycomedes and Achilles and for the birth and rearing of Neoptolemus there, and also for its goats and its quarries of veined marble (9.5.16). Thucydides records that the Athenians, in the first years after the Persian Wars, enslaved the Dolopians who held the island and settled it themselves (1.98.2; see [[dolopians]]), and Plutarch tells how Cimon found there the bones of Theseus, said to have been killed on Scyros by Lycomedes, and brought them to Athens (*Cimon* 8; *Theseus* 35–36).
