Homer uses the name for more than one place. Ephyra "in a corner of Argos" is the home of Sisyphus ([[ephyra-in-argos]]), and was taken in antiquity for the old name of Corinth (6.152–153). The corselet of [[Meges]] came, like Astyocheia, "from Ephyra, from the river Selleis", a gift from a guest-friend to his father Phyleus (15.529–534). In the Odyssey, Odysseus sails to Ephyra to get poison for his arrows (1.259–262).

Strabo argued that the Ephyra of Astyocheia lay in Elis, where there was a river Selleis (*Geography* 8.3.5). Apollodorus puts it in Thesprotia, in Epirus: Heracles took the city and fathered Tlepolemus on Astyoche, the daughter of its king Phylas (2.7.6). Modern opinion is still divided. See also [[selleis-ephyra]].
