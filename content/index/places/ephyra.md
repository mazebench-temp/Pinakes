Homer uses the name for more than one place. Ephyra "in a corner of Argos" is the home of Sisyphus ([[ephyra-in-argos]]), and was taken in antiquity for the old name of Corinth (6.152–153). The corselet of [[Meges]] came, like Astyocheia, "from Ephyra, from the river Selleis", a gift from a guest-friend to his father Phyleus (15.529–534). In the Odyssey, Odysseus sails to Ephyra to get poison for his arrows, and the suitors fear that Telemachus will go to Ephyra, “rich land”, for poisons to put in their wine (*Odyssey* 1.259–262, 2.328–330; see [[odysseus-seeks-poison]]). The poems do not say that the Ephyra of the drugs is Astyocheia's, but ancient readers placed both in the same two regions.

## Outside Homer

Strabo argued that the Ephyra of Astyocheia lay in Elis, where there was a river Selleis, a hundred and twenty stades from the city of Elis on the road to Lasion; from it, he says, came the man-slaying drugs of the Odyssey, and he adds that Nestor makes the eldest daughter of Augeas, king of the Epeians, a woman who knew drugs (*Geography* 8.3.5; *Iliad* 11.739–741). He also knew a Thesprotian Ephyra, the later Cichyrus, above the gulf into which the Acheron flows (7.7.5). Apollodorus puts Astyocheia's Ephyra in Thesprotia, in Epirus: Heracles took the city and fathered Tlepolemus on Astyoche, the daughter of its king Phylas (2.7.6). Modern opinion is still divided.
