Copae stood on a low promontory on the northern shore of the lake, at the modern village of Kastro (formerly Topolia). Lake Copais, which took its name from the town, was a shallow basin fed by the Cephisus; it was drained in the 1880s.

## Outside Homer

The lake's eels were a delicacy; the Boeotian in Aristophanes brings them to Athens (*Acharnians* 880–894). In the Mycenaean period the lake was controlled by dikes and canals, and a great fortress stood at Gla, on an island in it. The Catalogue does not name Gla.
