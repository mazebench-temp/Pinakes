Homer uses the singular Πλάταια; the later city was Πλαταιαί. It lay in the south of Boeotia, on the lower slopes of Cithaeron, looking north over the valley of the Asopus.

## Outside Homer

Plataea sided with Athens against Thebes from the late sixth century BC, and its men were the only Greeks who fought beside the Athenians at Marathon (Herodotus 6.108, 6.111). In 479 BC the Greeks defeated the Persian army of Mardonius near the city (Herodotus 9). In the Peloponnesian War the Spartans and Thebans besieged it, took it and razed it (Thucydides 2.71–78, 3.20–24, 3.52–68).
