ζαθέη "holy" ([[zatheos]]; ζα-, "very", + θεῖος, "divine"). Crisa stood on a spur of Parnassus below Delphi. In the Homeric Hymn to Apollo it lies "beneath snowy Parnassus", and the Cretan sailors whom Apollo makes his priests land there (269, 282, 438–445).

## Outside Homer

A Mycenaean fortified settlement on the spur near the modern village of Chrisso is usually identified with it. In the early sixth century BC the town that controlled the approach to Delphi from the sea was destroyed in the First Sacred War; ancient writers confused Crisa with Cirrha, its harbor, and which of the two was destroyed is still debated.
