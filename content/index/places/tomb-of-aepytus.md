Αἰπύτιος is the adjective of the name Aepytus: "the tomb of Aepytus". The Catalogue sets the [[Arcadians]] "beneath the steep mountain of [[Cyllene]], beside the tomb of Aepytus". Homer says nothing of Aepytus himself; for the poet and his audience the mound was a landmark.

## Outside Homer

In Arcadian legend Aepytus, son of Elatus, was a king killed by a snake while hunting. Pausanias saw his tomb near Cyllene, a mound of earth with a ring of stones around its base, and says he examined it with interest because Homer had mentioned it (8.16.2–3).
