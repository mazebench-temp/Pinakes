## Descriptions

ἠμαθόεις "sandy" ([[sandy]]), from ἄμαθος, "sand", is its standing epithet: Nestor was "king of sandy Pylos" (2.77). It is also ἠγάθεος, "very holy" ([[egatheos]], 1.252). Its people are the [[Pylians]], and its king is [[Nestor]], son of [[Neleus]], "the Pylos-born" (2.54).

## Outside Homer

Ancient scholars disputed which Pylos was Nestor's. Three places bore the name, in Messenia, in Triphylia and in Elis, and a proverb ran "there is a Pylos before Pylos" (Aristophanes, *Knights* 1059). Strabo argued at length for the Pylos in Triphylia, because Homer puts Nestor's kingdom beside the Alpheus (Geography 8.3); see [[strabo-and-the-catalogue]]. Early in the twentieth century Wilhelm Dörpfeld looked for Nestor's palace in Triphylia and excavated tholos tombs at Kakovatos.

In 1939 Carl Blegen and Konstantinos Kourouniotis found a Mycenaean palace at Ano Englianos in Messenia, now known as the Palace of Nestor. Its archive of Linear B tablets, among the first used to decipher the script in 1952, names the place pu-ro, Pylos, and most scholars now take it for Homer's Pylos. The palace burned around 1200 BC.
