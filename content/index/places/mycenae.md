Mycenae is "the well-built citadel" (ἐϋκτίμενον πτολίεθρον; [[well-built]]), first of the twelve places of Agamemnon's realm: [[Corinth]], [[Cleonae]], [[Orneae]], [[Araethyrea]], [[Sicyon]], [[Hyperesia]], [[Gonoessa]], [[Pellene]], [[Aegium]], [[Aegialus]] and [[Helice]] (2.569–575). The kingdom runs north from Mycenae to the Isthmus and west along the southern shore of the Gulf of Corinth, the coast later called Achaea. The city of [[Argos]] and the towns of the Argolid are Diomedes'.

{{quote:4d6d85aa-2bbf-5c97-bfe8-855eff55cfda}}

With a hundred ships, "by far the most and the best men", Agamemnon leads the largest contingent, and he stands out among all the heroes "because he was the best". How this realm fits the scepter's "all Argos" (2.108), and whether it reflects the power of Mycenae in the late Bronze Age, is an old question (see [[catalogue-and-mycenae]]).

## Descriptions

The Iliad also calls Mycenae "rich in gold" (πολύχρυσος, 7.180, 11.46) and "wide-streeted" (εὐρυάγυια, 4.52; [[wide-streeted]]). Hera names it with Argos and Sparta as the three cities she loves most (4.51–52). Homer uses both the plural Μυκῆναι and the singular Μυκήνη; in the Odyssey Mycene is also a heroine of old (Od. 2.120).

## Outside Homer

Pausanias saw the walls, "the work of the Cyclopes", the gate with the lions over it, the underground treasuries of Atreus and his sons, and the graves said to be those of Atreus, Agamemnon and their company (2.16.5–7). Argos destroyed the town in the 460s BC (Diodorus 11.65). Heinrich Schliemann excavated there in 1876 and found the shaft graves of Grave Circle A, with gold masks, one of which is still called the mask of Agamemnon; the graves are of the sixteenth century BC, centuries older than any date proposed for a Trojan War. The Lion Gate and the great tholos known as the Treasury of Atreus belong to the fourteenth and thirteenth centuries BC. The palace was destroyed around 1200 BC.
