The Caresus is the third of the rivers that bring the Achaean wall to nothing (12.20; see [[the-achaean-wall]]). Hesiod's list of the rivers that Tethys bore to Ocean has the seven others, but not the Caresus (*Theogony* 337–345; see [[hesiod]]).

## Outside Homer

[[strabo|Strabo]], reporting Demetrius of Scepsis, says that the Caresus flows from Malus, a place between Palaescepsis and Achaeium, and runs into the [[Aesepus]]. A hill country of many villages, Caresene, was said to take its name from the river, and the town of the same name had been destroyed (13.1.44). Further on he passes “a deserted Caresus” and the river's valley, smaller than the valley of the Aesepus (13.1.45). Homer counts it among the rivers that flow “from the mountains of Ida to the sea” (12.19); Demetrius' Caresus reaches the sea only through the Aesepus.

An ancient note says that the Caresus was later called the Pidys, and that it flows from Malus into the Aesepus (scholium T on 12.20); another reports that Tyrannion accented the name Καρησός, as the people of Cyzicus pronounced it, while Aristarchus kept Κάρησος (scholium A).
