Parrhasia is the last of the Arcadian names, in the southwest of the land, and the only one that belongs to a district rather than a town. It belongs to the [[Arcadians]].

## Outside Homer

Mount Lycaeum and its altar of Zeus Lycaeus lay in Parrhasia; Plato alludes to the tale that whoever tasted human flesh at the sacrifice there became a wolf (*Republic* 8, 565d). In 371 BC the Parrhasians were among the communities joined in the new city of Megalopolis. Latin poets use "Parrhasian" to mean Arcadian.
