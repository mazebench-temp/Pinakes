Later in the Iliad, Coeranus, Meriones' charioteer, is a man who followed him "from well-built Lyctus" (17.611–612). In Hesiod, Rhea is sent "to Lyctus, to the rich land of Crete" to bear Zeus, and Gaia hides the child in a cave nearby (*Theogony* 477–484). The name probably appears in the Linear B tablets from Knossos as ru-ki-to. In classical times Lyttos was a Dorian city of the inland hills, which claimed to be a colony of Sparta (Aristotle, *Politics* 2.10, 1271b28).
