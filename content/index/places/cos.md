Κῶν Εὐρυπύλοιο πόλιν: "Cos, the city of Eurypylus", island and city under one name. Of [[eurypylus-of-cos|Eurypylus]] Homer says nothing more.

Cos is also the island to which Hera's storm drove [[Heracles]] when he sailed home from sacking Troy, as Sleep tells it in *Iliad* 14 and Zeus in Book 15 (14.249–256, 15.18–30; see [[hera-drives-heracles-to-cos]]).

{{notes}}

## Outside Homer

Later writers added that Heracles fought and killed Eurypylus there, and fathered [[Thessalus]] on Eurypylus' daughter Chalciope (Apollodorus 2.7.1, 2.7.8). In that genealogy the Coan leaders, Thessalus' sons, descend from both the island's king and its conqueror.

In historical times Cos was a Dorian island, one of the Dorian Hexapolis (Herodotus 1.144). Thucydides calls it "Cos Meropis" (8.41.2), after the Meropes, its legendary first people. It was the home of Hippocrates, and its sanctuary of Asclepius was one of the great healing centers of the Greek world.
