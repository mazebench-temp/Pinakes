Ἑπτάπορος = ἑπτά (“seven”) + πόρος (“ford, crossing”): the river of seven fords. It is the second of the rivers that bring the Achaean wall to nothing (12.20; see [[the-achaean-wall]]).

## Outside Homer

Hesiod names it among the rivers that Tethys bore to Ocean (*Theogony* 341; see [[hesiod]]). [[strabo|Strabo]], reporting Demetrius of Scepsis, says that it was also called Polyporus, “Many Fords”, and that the road from the Fair Pine, a great tree north of Adramyttium, to the village of Melaenae and the Asclepieum founded by Lysimachus crossed it seven times (13.1.44). Of the Tarsius near Zeleia, crossed twenty times on a single road, he writes that it is “like the Heptaporus, which the poet names” (13.1.10; see [[zeleia]]). Where it ran is not known.
