Ὑποθῆβαι = ὑπό (under, below) + Θῆβαι (Thebes): "Lower Thebes", or "the town below Thebes". Like Medeon, it is "the well-built citadel" ([[well-built]]).

The Catalogue's silence about Thebes stands out, because the city of Cadmus is famous elsewhere in the poem. In Book 4 Agamemnon recalls Tydeus' embassy to Thebes, and Sthenelus boasts that he and Diomedes, sons of the Seven, took "seven-gated Thebes" where their fathers had failed (4.376–410). Ancient readers explained Hypothebae as the lower town of Thebes, inhabited after the Epigoni had destroyed the citadel, the Cadmea; others took it for a separate small town.

## Outside Homer

Excavations under modern Thebes have found a Mycenaean palace and several hundred Linear B tablets, so the site was a center of power in the Bronze Age. Whether "Lower Thebes" reflects a Thebes reduced after a destruction, or something else, is disputed.
