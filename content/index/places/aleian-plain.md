The name plays on the verb beside it: κὰπ πεδίον τὸ Ἀλήϊον οἶος ἀλᾶτο, “he wandered alone over the Aleian plain”, as if it were the plain of wandering (ἀλάομαι, “wander”). Whether Homer had a real place in mind cannot be known. ἀλήϊος, “landless” (9.125, 9.267), is another word. ὃν θυμὸν κατέδων, “eating his heart out”, is the grief that makes a man refuse food and company, as Achilles does in his mourning (“eating your heart”, σὴν ἔδεαι κραδίην, 24.129). See [[bellerophon]].

## Outside Homer

Herodotus names the Aleian plain in Cilicia as the place where the Persian army gathered in 490 BC before it sailed for Greece (6.95.1), and Strabo puts it above the coast of eastern Cilicia, near Mallus, where Alexander's cavalry crossed it on the way to Issus (14.5.17); others, he says, showed an Aleian plain in the Troad too (14.5.21). [Aristotle] quotes 6.200–202 as the picture of a melancholic, who “sought out the wilderness” (*Problems* 30.1, 953a), and Cicero translated the lines (*Tusculan Disputations* 3.63).
