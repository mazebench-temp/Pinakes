Homer's Miletus is Carian. Nothing in the Iliad hints at the Ionian city it later became, and the Catalogue of Ships gives Crete a Miletus of its own ([[miletus-crete]], 2.647). In the Carian entry it is the only town, named first, before the mountain, the river and the headland that frame its country. Later writers connected the two Miletuses by a colony. Ephorus, as Strabo reports him, said that the first Miletus in Asia was founded by Cretans whom Sarpedon brought from Cretan Miletus (Strabo 14.1.6). Another version made the founder a youth named Miletus, a son of Apollo, who fled from Minos in Crete (Apollodorus 3.1.2).

## Outside Homer

The Ionian settlers who took the city killed the Carian men and married their women (Herodotus 1.146). Their founder was remembered as Neleus, son of the Athenian king Codrus (Herodotus 9.97). Miletus became the leading city of Ionia and founded many colonies on the Hellespont and the Black Sea, among them Abydos and Cyzicus. Thales, Anaximander and Anaximenes, the first Greek philosophers, were Milesians, and so was the geographer Hecataeus. The oracle of Apollo at Didyma lay in its territory. The Persians sacked the city in 494 BC, at the end of the Ionian Revolt (Herodotus 6.18–21). Excavation has found a Mycenaean settlement on the site, and Hittite texts of the thirteenth century BC are generally thought to name it as Millawanda, a town under the influence of the Ahhiyawa.
