## In Homer

“And they went past Cruni and fair-flowing Chalcis” (*Odyssey* 15.295; see [[cruni]] and [[the-journey-of-telemachus]]). The verse is in no manuscript of the Odyssey; it is known from Strabo and, with another ending, from the Homeric *Hymn to Apollo* (see the translation's note on *Odyssey* 15.295–300). καλλιρέεθρος, “fair-flowing”, is said in Homer only of this Chalcis and of the spring Artacia, from which the Laestrygonians carried their water (*Odyssey* 10.107–108; see [[artacia]]): the epithet makes it a stream, as Strabo's river Chalcis is.

Homer has two other places of the name, both in the Catalogue of Ships: Chalcis in Euboea, a town of the Abantes, and “Chalcis by the sea”, a town of the Aetolians (*Iliad* 2.537, 2.640; see [[chalcis]] and [[chalcis-aetolia]]). The bird that the gods call χαλκίς is another word (*Iliad* 14.291).

## Outside Homer

Strabo, going down the coast of Triphylia south of the Alpheus, names “another river, Chalcis, and a spring, Cruni, and a settlement, Chalcis”, and after them the Samicum, the sanctuary of Samian Poseidon (*Geography* 8.3.13; see [[strabo]] and [[cruni]]). He counts Chalcis, Cruni and Pheae among the “obscure rivers, or rather brooks” of the voyage (*Geography* 8.3.27; see [[pheia]]). In his list of places that share the name of the Euboean Chalcis he sets this one beside the Aetolian: “and in what is now Elis”, quoting the verse with another ending, βὰν δὲ παρὰ Κρουνοὺς καὶ Χαλκίδα πετρήεσσαν, “past Cruni and rocky Chalcis”, of Telemachus' companions going home from Nestor (*Geography* 10.1.9). In the *Hymn to Apollo* the verse ends καὶ παρὰ Δύμην, “and past Dyme” (*Hymn to Apollo* 425).
