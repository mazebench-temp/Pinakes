Sicyon lay on a plateau above the coast of the Gulf of Corinth, west of Corinth. The Catalogue gives it a single note of history: it was the kingdom of [[Adrastus]] "at first" (πρῶτα), before he ruled at Argos, or formerly. It belongs to [[Agamemnon]]'s realm.

## Outside Homer

Herodotus tells that Adrastus had a hero shrine in the market place of Sicyon, and that the Sicyonians honored his sufferings with tragic choruses. Cleisthenes, tyrant of Sicyon in the early sixth century BC and at war with Argos, tried to drive the Argive hero out, gave the choruses to Dionysus, and stopped the rhapsodes from competing in Homer's poems, "because the Argives and Argos are celebrated in them almost everywhere" (5.67). Hesiod's Mecone, where gods and men parted at Prometheus' sacrifice (*Theogony* 535–536), was later taken to be the old name of Sicyon.
