The name is the feminine of ἱρός, the Ionic form of ἱερός, “holy”, the word in “sacred Ilios” (Ἴλιον ἱρήν; see [[hieros]]). ποιήεις, “grassy”, from ποίη, “grass”, is its epithet in both lists (9.150, 9.292; see [[grassy]] and [[the-seven-cities]]).

## Outside Homer

Strabo reports two sites: Hire was shown on the mountain near Megalopolis in Arcadia, on the road to Andania, or it was the place then called Mesola, reaching down to the gulf between Taygetus and Messenia (8.4.5; see [[strabo]]). The mountain recalls Eira, the stronghold where, in Pausanias' account, the Messenians held out against Sparta for eleven years (4.17.10). Pausanias himself was told that Abia, a town on the coast of Messenia, had long ago been called Ire and was one of the seven cities; it took its new name from Abia, the nurse of Glenus son of Heracles, who had settled there and founded a sanctuary of Heracles (4.30.1).
