## In Homer

Κυλλήνιον, “of Cyllene”, is said of Otus, “companion of the son of Phyleus, and a leader of the great-hearted Epeians”, whom Polydamas kills at the ships (15.518–519; see [[otus-of-cyllene]], [[meges]] and [[epeians]]). The Iliad names no town of Cyllene. Its only other Cyllene is the mountain of Arcadia, “the steep mountain of Cyllene” (2.603; see [[cyllene]]), and the adjective could in itself point to either; the poem does not say.

## Outside Homer

Strabo, going west along the coast of Elis from Cape Araxus, comes to Cyllene, the port of the Eleans, a hundred and twenty stades below the city of Elis. “Homer too mentions this Cyllene”, he says, quoting Ὦτον Κυλλήνιον ἀρχὸν Ἐπειῶν, “for he would not have made a man from the Arcadian mountain leader of the Epeians.” In his day it was a modest village, which had an ivory statue of Asclepius by Colotes, a wonder to see (*Geography* 8.3.4; see [[strabo]] and [[elis]]). Between Cyllene and Cape Chelonatas, he goes on, the Peneius and the Selleis flow into the sea, and on the Selleis stood an Ephyra, which he takes for the Ephyra of the Iliad (*Geography* 8.3.5; see [[selleis-ephyra]] and [[ephyra]]). The Epeians who lived on Dulichium and the Echinades under Meges had come from Elis, which is why, he says, Otus of Cyllene is called the companion of the son of Phyleus (*Geography* 10.2.14; see [[dulichium]]).
