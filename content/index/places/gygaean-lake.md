The lake "bore" Mesthles and Antiphus to Talaemenes: their mother was the nymph of the lake. Rivers and their nymphs are often parents and ancestors in the Iliad. The Paeonian Asteropaeus, for one, is the grandson of the river Axius (21.141–143). Homer names the lake once more, in Achilles' boast over Iphition, whose birthplace and father's domain lay by it, between the Hyllus and the Hermus (20.390–392; see [[iphition]], [[hyllus]] and [[hermus]]).

## Outside Homer

Herodotus places "the lake called Gygaean" beside the tomb of Alyattes near Sardis (1.93). [[Strabo]] puts the lake “named by the poet” forty stadia from Sardis, and says that it was later called Coloe, with a temple of Artemis Coloene of great sanctity, where baskets were said to dance at the festivals, a tale he doubts (*Geography* 13.4.5); the tombs of the kings lie around it, and some say the lake was dug by hand to take the floods of the rivers (*Geography* 13.4.7). Pliny the Elder calls Sardis “famous for the Gygaean lake” (*Natural History* 5.110). The name recalls Gyges, the first king of the Mermnad dynasty of Lydia (Herodotus 1.8–14); whether the lake was named after the king or the king's family after the lake is not known. It is the modern Marmara Gölü.
