"The young men of the Achaeans who held Aegina and Mases" (κοῦροι Ἀχαιῶν) close the list of [[Diomedes]]' contingent. Homer never links the island with Aeacus, the grandfather of Achilles and Ajax.

## Outside Homer

In later myth the nymph Aegina bore Aeacus to Zeus on the island, and Pindar's odes for Aeginetan victors celebrate Aeacus' descendants, Peleus and Telamon, Achilles and Ajax, as heroes of Aegina. The pediments of the temple of Aphaia (about 500–480 BC) show the two sacks of Troy, by Heracles with Telamon and by the Achaeans of the Iliad. Aegina was a rival of Athens, which drove out its people in 431 BC (Thucydides 2.27).
