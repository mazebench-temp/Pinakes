Panopeus lay in eastern Phocis near Chaeronea, on the road from Boeotia to Delphi. Homer names the town twice more. [[Schedius]] son of Iphitus, "the best of the Phocians", lived in "famous Panopeus" (17.306–308), and the giant Tityus assaulted Leto on her way to Pytho "through Panopeus of the fair dancing places" (καλλίχορος, Od. 11.580–581). Epeius, who built the Wooden Horse, is "son of Panopeus" (23.665), a man who bears the town's name.

## Outside Homer

Pausanias doubted that it deserved the name of city: it had "no government offices, no gymnasium, no theater, no marketplace, no water running into a fountain" (10.4.1). He was shown there the clay from which Prometheus was said to have made the human race (10.4.4).
