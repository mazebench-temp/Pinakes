Panopeus lay in eastern Phocis near Chaeronea, on the road from Boeotia to Delphi. Homer names the town twice more. [[Schedius]] son of Iphitus, "the best of the Phocians", lived in "famous Panopeus" (17.306–308), and the giant Tityus assaulted Leto on her way to Pytho "through Panopeus of the fair dancing places" (καλλίχορος, Od. 11.580–581). Epeius, the boxer of Patroclus' games and the builder of the wooden horse, is "the son of Panopeus" (23.665), a man who bears the town's name (see [[epeius]] and [[panopeus-father-of-epeius]]).

## Outside Homer

Strabo identifies it with the Phanoteus of his own day, bordering on the country around Lebadea, the home of Epeius, and says that the story of Tityus is told there (*Geography* 9.3.14; see [[strabo]]). Pausanias doubted that it deserved the name of city: it had "no government offices, no gymnasium, no theater, no marketplace, no water running into a fountain" (10.4.1). Its people told him that the town was named after the father of Epeius, and that they were not Phocians by origin but Phlegyans who had fled from the land of Orchomenus (10.4.1). He was shown there the clay from which Prometheus was said to have made the human race (10.4.4).
