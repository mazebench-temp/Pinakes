Μαιάνδρου ῥοαί, "the streams of the Maeander". This is its only appearance in Homer; Hesiod names it among the rivers born to Oceanus and Tethys (*Theogony* 339). Its lower course winds so much that its name became a word for any winding line, and the Greek key pattern is still called a meander. Ovid compares Daedalus' labyrinth to the river, which "plays in the Phrygian fields" and runs back on itself (*Metamorphoses* 8.162–166). Its silt has since filled the gulf on which Miletus stood, leaving the city far inland. The modern river is the Büyük Menderes.
