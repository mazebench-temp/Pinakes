Only the accent tells the town from the common noun φυλακή ("watch, guard"). Protesilaus' wife stayed behind at Phylace "with her cheeks torn in mourning", and his house was left half-finished (2.700–701). [[Medon]], the bastard son of Oileus, lived there in exile after killing a kinsman (13.694–697).

In the Odyssey the seer Melampus tried to drive off the cattle of Iphiclus from Phylace, to win a bride for his brother, and was held prisoner there for a year (11.287–297, 15.225–238). [[Phylacus]], the father of Iphiclus, shares his name with the town.
