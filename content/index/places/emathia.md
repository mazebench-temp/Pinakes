## In Homer

{{quote:3dbdd621-4576-5d4a-8275-0b3cf5769186}}

Homer names Emathia once, in Hera's flight from Olympus: “stepping on Pieria and lovely Emathia / she sped over the snowy mountains of the horse-herding Thracians” (14.226–227; see [[pieria]], [[thracians]] and [[olympus]]). ἐρατεινή, “lovely”, is a common epithet of lands and towns, as of Lacedaemon (3.443; see [[lovely]]). The names follow Hera's course from Olympus north and east to Athos (see [[athos]]).

## Outside Homer

Strabo gives Hera's route as an example of the order Homer keeps in naming places, from Olympus to Pieria, Emathia, the Thracian mountains and Athos (*Geography* 1.2.20; see [[strabo]]). He also reports that what is now Macedonia was formerly called Emathia, that it took its later name from Macedon, an ancient ruler, and that there was also a city Emathia by the sea (7, fr. 11).
