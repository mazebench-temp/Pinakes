## In Homer

The poem never names the city. Nausithous founded it when he brought the Phaeacians from Hypereia to Scheria: “he drove a wall around the city, and built houses, / and made temples of the gods, and divided the fields” (*Odyssey* 6.9–10; see [[nausithous]], [[hypereia-of-the-phaeacians]] and [[the-migration-of-the-phaeacians]]). Nausicaa describes it to Odysseus before they reach it: a high rampart, a fine harbor on either side, a narrow way in, the curving ships drawn up along the road, each man with a slip of his own, and the place of assembly around the precinct of Poseidon, built of hauled stones set deep in the earth; there the men tend the tackle of their ships and taper their oars (*Odyssey* 6.262–269; see [[harbor]], [[ships]] and [[assembly]]). The houses of the Phaeacians are not built like the house of Alcinous, which a child could show the way to (*Odyssey* 6.298–303; see [[alcinous]] and [[house]]).

Odysseus walks through the city hidden in a mist that Athena pours around him, and marvels at the harbors and the balanced ships, the meeting places of the heroes themselves, and the long walls, high and fitted with stakes, a wonder to see (*Odyssey* 7.14–17, 7.39–45, plain renderings; see [[athena]]). The next morning Alcinous leads the Phaeacians to their place of assembly, “which was built for them beside the ships”, and they sit on polished stones (*Odyssey* 8.4–7, a plain rendering).

The city lives under a threat. Nausithous used to say that Poseidon would one day wreck a Phaeacian ship on its way back from an escort and cover their city round with a great mountain (*Odyssey* 8.564–569). When the god has turned the ship that carried Odysseus home to stone, in sight of the city, the Phaeacians make ready twelve chosen bulls for him in the hope that he will pity them and not hide the city behind the mountain, and the poem leaves their leaders praying around his altar (*Odyssey* 13.159–187; see [[poseidon]]). For the later identification of Scheria and its city with Corcyra, see [[scheria]].
