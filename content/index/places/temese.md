## Outside Homer

Strabo puts Temese, “which people now call Tempsa”, first among the cities of Bruttium after Laus: a foundation of the Ausonians, and later of the Aetolians who came with Thoas. Near it, he says, stood a hero's shrine, shaded by wild olives, of Polites, one of Odysseus' companions, whom the natives killed by treachery; he grew so wrathful that the people around paid him tribute at an oracle's bidding, and “the hero in Temese” became a proverb, until the boxer Euthymus fought him and freed them. The poet, Strabo reports, is said to mean this Temese and not Tamassus in Cyprus, “for it is said both ways”, and copper workings, abandoned in his day, were shown nearby (*Geography* 6.1.5). Of Cyprus he says that the copper mines at Tamassus are plentiful (14.6.5). The Odyssey names a Polites, ὄρχαμος ἀνδρῶν, “a leader of men”, the dearest and most trusted of Odysseus' companions, at Circe's house (*Odyssey* 10.224–225).

Merry prefers Tamassus, in the middle of Cyprus, famous for its copper mines, and notes that the Latin word for copper, *cuprum*, is *aes Cyprium*, “Cyprian bronze” (*Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on *Odyssey* 1.184; see [[cyprus]]).
