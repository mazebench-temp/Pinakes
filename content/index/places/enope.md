Enope is named second of the seven towns, between Cardamyle and Hire (9.150; see [[the-seven-cities]]). The name has the letters of the common noun ἐνοπή, “cry, din of battle”, which is why its pattern counts only the capitalized form.

## Outside Homer

Strabo reports three identifications: Pellana, a place near Cardamyle, or Gerenia (8.4.5; see [[strabo]]). Pausanias chose Gerenia, a town of Messenians in the league of the Free Laconians, “called Enope in the poems of Homer”. There, he says, some held that Nestor was raised, and others that he took refuge there when Heracles was taking Pylos; the town showed the tomb and sanctuary of Machaon (3.26.8–9). Strabo too reports that Nestor was called “the Gerenian” after Gerena near Pherae, “because he was saved there” (Strabo 8.4.4; see [[gerenian-horseman]] and [[nestor]]). On Pausanias' identification, the town that gave Nestor his title is among those offered in his presence.
