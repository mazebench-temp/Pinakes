## The name

κρουνός is a spring, or the stream that gushes from it. In the Iliad κρουνοί are the “great springs” that feed the winter torrents of a simile (4.454) and the two springs of the Scamander by the walls of Troy (22.147, 22.208; see [[the-springs-of-the-scamander]]). As the name of a place it is found only in the verse of Telemachus' voyage.

## In Homer

“And they went past Cruni and fair-flowing Chalcis” (*Odyssey* 15.295): the ship has left Pylos with the wind that Athena sent, the sun sets as it runs north along the coast, and it makes for Pheae and passes Elis (*Odyssey* 15.292–298; see [[chalcis-in-elis]], [[pheia]], [[elis]] and [[the-journey-of-telemachus]]). The verse is in no manuscript of the Odyssey. It is known from Strabo, who quotes it first of four verses on the voyage, and from the Homeric *Hymn to Apollo*, which has it with another ending; Murray prints it in Strabo's order, and D. B. Monro, who held it genuine, placed it after the verse of Pheae (*Homer's Odyssey, Books XIII–XXIV*, 1901, on *Odyssey* 15.295–298; see the translation's note on *Odyssey* 15.295–300).

## Outside Homer

Strabo, going down the coast south of the Alpheus, names “another river, Chalcis, and a spring, Cruni, and a settlement, Chalcis”, and after them the Samicum, the sanctuary of Samian Poseidon in Triphylia (*Geography* 8.3.13; see [[strabo]] and [[chalcis-in-elis]]). He uses the voyage to argue that Nestor's Pylos was the Triphylian one. The places of the verses lie south of Elis, “first Pheae, then Chalcis, then Cruni, then Triphylian Pylos and the Samicum”: a ship sailing north from an Elean Pylos would have left them all behind and passed Elis before sunset, not after, and one sailing from Messenian Pylos would have had a long voyage, past the Neda, the Acidon and the Alpheus, rather than past Cruni, Chalcis and Pheae, “names of obscure rivers, or rather of brooks” (*Geography* 8.3.26–27; see [[pylos]] and [[strabo-on-nestors-pylos]]). In the *Hymn to Apollo* the god drives a Cretan ship up the same coast, past Pylos, and βῆ δὲ παρὰ Κρουνοὺς καὶ Χαλκίδα καὶ παρὰ Δύμην, “she went past Cruni and Chalcis and past Dyme”, and past shining Elis; as she made for Pherae, the mountain of Ithaca came into view, and Dulichium, Same and Zacynthus (*Hymn to Apollo* 422–429; see [[apollo]]). The verse is the Odyssey's with another ending.
