Δάρδανιαι πύλαι, "the Dardanian gates", from the adjective Δαρδάνιος, "of Dardanus" (see [[dardanus]] and [[dardanians]]). The phrase occurs three times in the Iliad, always in the genitive, πυλάων Δαρδανιάων, at the end of the line.

{{notes}}

## Elsewhere in Homer

While Achilles fought, Hera says, the Trojans "never even came out before the Dardanian gates" (5.789). In Book 22 the name returns twice. As Achilles chases Hector around the walls, Hector keeps rushing toward the Dardanian gates, to come under the walls where the Trojans could shield him with their missiles (22.194–196), and after his death Priam, mad with grief, is held back from going out through the Dardanian gates to the Achaeans (22.412–413). In the same book Hector waits for Achilles "in front of Ilios and the Scaean gates" (22.6), and he dies foretelling that Achilles will be killed "at the Scaean gates" (22.359–360).

Because both names are used of the same scenes, many readers have taken the Dardanian gates to be the Scaean gates under another name; others think of a second gate facing the plain. Homer does not say.
