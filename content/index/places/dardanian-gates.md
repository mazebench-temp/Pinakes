Δαρδάνιαι πύλαι, "the Dardanian gates", from the adjective Δαρδάνιος, "of Dardanus" (see [[dardanus]] and [[dardanians]]). The phrase occurs three times in the Iliad, always in the genitive, πυλάων Δαρδανιάων, at the end of the line.

{{notes}}

## Elsewhere in Homer

While Achilles fought, Hera says, the Trojans "never even came out before the Dardanian gates" (5.789). The name returns twice in Book 22, in the chase around the walls and in Priam's grief (22.194, 22.413), in the book that also names the Scaean gates at its beginning and in Hector's last words (22.6, 22.360).

Because both names are used of the same scenes, many readers have taken the Dardanian gates to be the Scaean gates under another name; others think of a second gate facing the plain. Homer does not say.
