Ὕδης ἐν πίονι δήμῳ, “in the rich land of Hyde” (20.385): δῆμος is the land itself, as in “the rich land of Lycia” (16.437; see [[rich-land]] and [[demos]]). Homer names it once, as the birthplace of Iphition, son of Otrynteus, whom Achilles kills first when he returns to the battle (see [[iphition]] and [[otrynteus]]). The verse sets it under Tmolus, the mountain of the Maeonians, and in the same speech Achilles places Iphition's birthplace by the Gygaean lake, between the Hyllus and the Hermus (20.390–392; see [[tmolus]], [[gygaean-lake]], [[hyllus]], [[hermus]] and [[maeonians]]). The poem does not say whether Hyde was a town or a district. Walter Leaf reports a variant Ὕλης, “of Hyle”, which he calls evidently wrong (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 20.385); the opposite error, Hyde for the Boeotian Hyle, was found in some texts of 5.708 and 7.221 (see [[hyle]] and [[hyle-or-hyde]]).

## Outside Homer

[[Strabo]] reports that some added the verse “beneath snowy Tmolus, in the rich land of Hyde” as a fourth line to the Catalogue's entry for the Maeonians (2.864–866), and objects that no Hyde is found among the Lydians. Others, he says, made Tychius, the leather-worker who made Ajax's shield, a man of Hyde by reading Ὕδῃ ἔνι at 7.221, adding that the place was wooded and struck by lightning and that the Arimi were there, and they added “in a wooded country, in the rich land of Hyde” to the verse “among the Arimi, where they say Typhoeus has his bed” (2.783); some call Sardis Hyde, and others its acropolis (*Geography* 13.4.6; see [[tychius]], [[arimi]] and [[typhoeus]]). In his account of Boeotia he rejects the reading Ὕδῃ at 5.708, since Hyde lies in Lydia, “beneath snowy Tmolus” (*Geography* 9.2.20). Pliny the Elder, describing Sardis on the side of Mount Tmolus, writes that the Maeonians called the city itself Hyde, and that it was famous for the Gygaean lake (*Natural History* 5.110). Leaf notes that some identified Hyde with the later Sardis, and that Strabo is incredulous (on 20.385).
