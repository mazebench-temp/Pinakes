Μινύειος "Minyan", the adjective of the Minyans (Μινύαι), tells this Orchomenus apart from [[orchomenus-arcadia|Orchomenus in Arcadia]] (2.605). It is not counted among the towns of the [[Boeotians]]: with [[Aspledon]] it has its own leaders, [[Ascalaphus]] and [[Ialmenus]], and thirty ships (2.511–516).

Homer knows it as rich. Achilles will not be moved by gifts, “not all that comes in to Orchomenus, nor all that comes to Thebes in Egypt” (9.381–382; see [[thebes-in-egypt]]). In the Odyssey, Amphion son of Iasus "once ruled with might in Minyan Orchomenus" (Od. 11.281–284).

## Outside Homer

The Minyans took their name from a king Minyas, and the Argonauts too were called Minyans. In legend Orchomenus was the rival of Thebes: Heracles freed the Thebans from the tribute they paid to its king Erginus (Apollodorus 2.4.11). Pindar's fourteenth Olympian celebrates the Graces who were worshipped there.

Heinrich Schliemann excavated at Orchomenus in 1880–1881 and cleared the great tholos tomb known as the Treasury of Minyas. Pausanias complained that Greek writers described the pyramids of Egypt but not this treasury or the walls of Tiryns, which were no less wonderful (9.36.5; he describes the tomb at 9.38.2). The gray pottery of the Middle Bronze Age that Schliemann found there is still called Minyan ware. A Mycenaean palace stood on the site, and the draining of Lake Copais by dikes and canals, with the fortress of Gla on the lake, is usually connected with the city's power in the thirteenth century BC.
