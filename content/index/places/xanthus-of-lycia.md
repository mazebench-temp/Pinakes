δινήεις, "eddying" ([[eddying]]), is the last word of the Trojan catalogue. The name means "yellow, tawny" (ξανθός). The Lycian kings hold a great estate "by the banks of the Xanthus" (12.313), and Bellerophon's journey ends when he reaches "Lycia and the flowing Xanthus" (6.172). The river of Troy has the same name among the gods: "whom the gods call Xanthus, and men Scamander" (20.74; [[language-of-gods-and-men]], [[scamander]]). The Lycian city of Xanthus stood on its banks. The river is the modern Eşen Çayı. One of Achilles' immortal horses has the same name, the horse that speaks to him and foretells his death (19.404–417; see [[xanthus-the-horse]]).
