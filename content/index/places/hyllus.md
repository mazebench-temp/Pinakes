Ὕλλῳ ἐπʼ ἰχθυόεντι, “by the fish-filled Hyllus” (20.392). Homer names the river once, in Achilles' boast over Iphition, whose father's domain lay by the Gygaean lake between the Hyllus and the Hermus (see [[iphition]], [[otrynteus]], [[gygaean-lake]] and [[hermus]]). ἰχθυόεις, “fish-filled”, is said elsewhere in Homer of the sea and once of the Hellespont (9.360); the Hyllus is the only river that has it (see [[fish-filled]]). Walter Leaf calls the Hyllus a feeder of the great Lydian Hermus (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 20.392).

## Outside Homer

Herodotus describes the plain before Sardis, where Croesus met Cyrus, as large and bare, with rivers flowing through it, among them the Hyllus, which rush together into the greatest of them, the Hermus (1.80.1; see [[herodotus]] and [[croesus]]). [[Strabo]] says that the Hyllus, “now called the Phrygius”, flows into the Hermus with the Pactolus (*Geography* 13.4.5). Pliny the Elder names the Phryx and the Hyllus as two different rivers among the many that the Hermus gathers (*Natural History* 5.119). Strabo also uses the river in his argument that the poet's silence proves nothing: Homer names the Hermus and the Hyllus but not the Meles, which flows past Smyrna, the city most called his homeland, nor the Pactolus, which runs into the same stream from Tmolus, a mountain he does name (*Geography* 12.3.27).
