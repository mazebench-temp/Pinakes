πετρήεσσα "rocky" ([[rocky]]). The epithet returns in *Iliad* 9, where Achilles says that not “all that is shut within the stone threshold of the Archer, Phoebus Apollo, in rocky Pytho” would buy back a man's life (9.404–405). The name Delphi does not occur in Homer.

The Odyssey knows the oracle. Agamemnon consulted Apollo “in sacred Pytho”, crossing the same stone threshold, and was told of a quarrel among the best of the Achaeans (*Odyssey* 8.79–82; see [[egatheos]]). The giant Tityus assaulted Leto as she went to Pytho “through Panopeus of the fair dancing places” (*Odyssey* 11.580–581, a plain rendering).

## Outside Homer

The Homeric *Hymn to Apollo* derives the name from the rotting (πύθεσθαι) of the she-dragon that Apollo killed there (*Hymn* 3.363–374), and tells how Trophonius and Agamedes laid the stone threshold of his temple (*Hymn* 3.294–299). The site was inhabited in the Mycenaean period; the sanctuary grew into the great oracle of the Greek world from the eighth century BC.
