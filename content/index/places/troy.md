## Etymology

Two names: Ἴλιος (Ilios, originally Ϝίλιος, Wilios), from which the Iliad takes its title, and Τροίη (Troiē), which can mean the city or the land around it. Hittite texts mention a city *Wilusa* and a land *Truwisa* in western Anatolia. Many scholars think these are Ilios and Troy, though the identification is not certain.

## The walls

Troy is “well-walled” and “of the fine towers” ([[well-walled]], [[of-the-fine-towers]]). Poseidon built its wall for King Laomedon: in *Iliad* 7 he says that he and Apollo built it together (7.452–453), and in Book 21 that he built it while Apollo herded Laomedon's cattle (21.446–449). See [[the-walls-of-troy]], [[the-tower-of-ilios]] and [[poseidon-and-apollo-serve-laomedon]].

## Outside Homer

The site at Hisarlık in northwest Turkey has been identified with Troy since Heinrich Schliemann dug there from 1870. Its many layers run from the Early Bronze Age to the Roman period; Troy VI and VIIa, of the late Bronze Age, are the usual candidates for Homer's city.
