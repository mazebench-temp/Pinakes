Homer's Athena goes “to Marathon and broad-streeted Athens” (*Odyssey* 7.80). The battle of 490 BCE, in which the Athenians and Plataeans defeated the Persian landing, became the proudest memory of Athens; Aeschylus' epitaph names only Marathon (see [[aeschylus]]). In the *Poetics* it occurs in one of Euclides' mock verses, “I saw Epichares walking to Marathon”, which scans only if the poet lengthens syllables at will (1458b9–10; see [[euclides-parodies]]).
