The name is αἰπεῖα, the feminine of αἰπύς (“steep, high”), with the accent drawn back; Achilles took “steep Scyros” (Σκῦρον … αἰπεῖαν, 9.668) with the same letters (see [[steep]]). Its epithet in the list is καλή, “fair” (9.152; see [[the-seven-cities]]). Nestor's kingdom in the Catalogue includes a town called Aepy (2.592), whose name also means “steep” (see [[aepy]]).

## Outside Homer

Strabo says that Aepeia “is now called Thuria”, which stands on a high hill, “and hence its name”; others, he reports, took Aepeia for Methone (Strabo 8.4.5; see [[strabo]]). Pausanias heard that Corone, on the western shore of the Messenian Gulf, had the old name Aepeia, and was renamed when the Thebans brought the Messenians back to the Peloponnese, after the founder's home town, Coroneia in Boeotia (4.34.5). For Pausanias Thuria was Antheia (see [[antheia]]).
