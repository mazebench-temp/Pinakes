ἀργυροδίνης = ἄργυρος ("silver") + δίνη ("eddy"): "silver-eddying" (see [[silver-eddying]]). The [[Magnetes]] lived "around the Peneius and Pelion" (2.757).

The Peneius drains the whole Thessalian plain and leaves it by the narrow gorge of Tempe. Herodotus tells how Xerxes looked at the gorge and heard that Thessaly had once been a lake, until Poseidon, or an earthquake, opened the way for the river (7.128–130). In myth the river god Peneus was the father of Daphne, who was turned into a laurel to escape Apollo (Ovid, *Metamorphoses* 1.452–567).
