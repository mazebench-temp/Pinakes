# Ψυρίης (Od. 3.171), the only occurrence in Homer.

## The name

Homer has the genitive only, νήσου ἔπι Ψυρίης, “by the island of Psyria” (*Odyssey* 3.171). Merry takes Ψυρίη for the oldest name of the island, unless Ψυρίης is the genitive of an adjective agreeing with νήσου, and compares the change from Ψυρίη to Strabo's τὰ Ψύρα with that from Συρίη (*Odyssey* 15.403) to Σύρα (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on *Odyssey* 3.169). See [[chios]] and [[mimas]].

## Outside Homer

Strabo, going round Chios, comes after the harbor towns of the island to the cape called Melaena, “opposite which lies Psyra, an island fifty stadia from the cape, high, with a city of the same name; the circuit of the island is forty stadia” (*Geography* 14.1.35). Merry gives its modern name, Psara, and the reports of travelers that it lies about twenty miles from Chios (on *Odyssey* 3.169).
