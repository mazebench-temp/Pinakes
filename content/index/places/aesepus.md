ὕδωρ μέλαν, "dark water", as deep, clear water looks in Homer. The Aesepus is one of the rivers "that flow from Ida to the sea" which Poseidon and Apollo later turn against the Achaean wall (12.17–22), and Pandarus' men followed him "from the streams of Aesepus" (4.91). The name is also a man's: Aesepus and Pedasus, twin sons of the nymph Abarbarea, are killed by Euryalus in Book 6 (6.21–28). The river is the modern Gönen Çayı.
