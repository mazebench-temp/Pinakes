ὕδωρ μέλαν, "dark water", as deep, clear water looks in Homer. The Aesepus is one of the rivers “that flow from the mountains of Ida to the sea” which Poseidon and Apollo later turn against the Achaean wall (12.17–22). The name is also a man's: Aesepus and Pedasus, twin sons of the nymph Abarbarea, are killed by Euryalus in Book 6 (6.21–28). The river is the modern Gönen Çayı.

## Outside Homer

Hesiod names the Aesepus among the rivers that Tethys bore to Ocean, in the verse "Granicus and Aesepus and divine Simoeis" (Theogony 342). Strabo, following Demetrius of Scepsis, says that it rises close to the Scamander and the Granicus on Cotylus, a height of Ida above Scepsis, and runs north to the Propontis, its mouth the farthest of the three from its source (Geography 13.1.43); Zeleia lies about eighty stades from the sea where it empties (13.1.10).
