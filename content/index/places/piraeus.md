Themistocles persuaded the Athenians to build walls around the Piraeus, judging its three natural harbors better than the old open roadstead of Phalerum (Thucydides 1.93). The Long Walls joined it to the city in the middle of the fifth century (1.107–108), and in the war with Sparta the people of Attica crowded inside them (2.17). In 403 BCE Thrasybulus and the exiles held the Piraeus against the Thirty (Xenophon, *Hellenica* 2.4.10–22; see [[thrasybulus]]).
