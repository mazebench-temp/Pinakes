κολώνη, "mound, hillock"; αἰπεῖα, "steep" ([[steep]]); περίδρομος, "with a way round it", standing free in the plain. The hill has two names ([[language-of-gods-and-men]]). The human name may come from βάτος, "bramble", "Bramble Hill", but that is a guess. The gods' name says what the hill is, a grave mound ([[myrine]]). Like the tomb of Aesyetes, where Polites keeps watch (2.793), and [[the-tomb-of-ilus|the tomb of Ilus]] in the middle of the plain (10.415, 11.166), it is one of the old burial mounds that serve the Trojans as landmarks.

## Outside Homer

Later mythographers give the name Bateia to a daughter of Teucer, the first king of the Troad, who married Dardanus (Apollodorus 3.12.1). Whether the hill was thought to be her grave is not recorded. Its site has not been identified.
