## The name

Θρῄκη (Attic Θρᾴκη) is the land; Θρῄκηθεν, “from Thrace”, and Θρῄκηνδε, “to Thrace”, are its adverbs, and its people are the Θρήϊκες (see [[thracians]]). For Homer it lies north and northwest of Troy across the sea, a country of horsemen and snowy mountains: Hera, flying from Olympus to Lemnos, crosses “the snowy mountains of the horse-rearing Thracians” and comes down to the sea at Athos (14.225–230). [[samothrace|Samothrace]], from which Poseidon watches the battle, is “wooded Samos, / the Thracian” (13.12–13), and the sea between is the Thracian sea (23.230).

## In the Iliad

{{quote:63cd51ff-19a6-5536-9421-f310d712a415}}

Book 9 names Thrace twice. In the simile that opens it, the grief of the Achaeans is a sea stirred by two winds at once, “the North Wind and the West Wind, which blow from Thrace” (9.4–7; see [[two-winds-from-thrace]], [[boreas]] and [[zephyrus]]). The two winds have their home there: after fanning the pyre of Patroclus they go back “over the Thracian sea” (23.229–230), and seen from the coast of the Troad both winds come from Thrace (see the translation's note on 9.4–7). And Nestor tells Agamemnon that his huts are full of wine, “which the ships of the Achaeans bring you day by day from Thrace over the wide sea” (9.71–72; see [[wine]] and [[agamemnon]]), so that he can feast the elders (see [[feast]]). The Odyssey knows Thracian wine too: the strong wine with which Odysseus makes the Cyclops drunk was a gift of Maron, priest of Apollo at Ismarus, the town of the Cicones on the Thracian coast (Od. 9.39–40, 9.196–211; see [[cicones]]).

Thrace is also the land of Ares. In a later simile Ares and his son Rout arm themselves “from Thrace” to go against the Ephyri or the Phlegyans (13.298–303), and in the Odyssey Ares flies off to Thrace when Hephaestus frees him from the net (Od. 8.360–361; see [[ares]]). Helenus kills Deipyrus with “a great Thracian sword” (13.576–577; see [[deipyrus]]), and the Thracian sword that Achilles took from Asteropaeus is a prize at Patroclus' games (23.807–808). Antenor's son Iphidamas was reared in Thrace, which is given the epithets of Phthia, “deep-soiled … mother of flocks” (11.221–223; 9.479; see [[deep-soiled]] and [[mother-of-flocks]]), and the Thracian Rhigmus came from there to fight for Troy (20.484–485).
