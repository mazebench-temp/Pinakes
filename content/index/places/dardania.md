Δαρδανίη is the place of [[Dardanus]], named after its founder as the [[Dardanians]] are named after him. Homer names it once, when Aeneas recites his lineage to Achilles (20.213–241; see [[genealogy-in-battle]]). The verses put it at the beginning of the line and before the city: Dardanus founded Dardania “since sacred Ilios was not yet / built in the plain”, and its people “still dwelt on the slopes of Ida of the many springs” (20.216–218; see [[troy]] and [[ida]]). The poem says nothing more of it, neither where it stood nor whether it was a town or a country.

## Outside Homer

[[plato|Plato]]'s Athenian quotes the three verses as Homer's witness to the growth of settlements after the great flood, from the slopes of the mountains to a city in the plain: Ilion, he says, was settled out of the heights into a great and fine plain (*Laws* 3.681e–682b). [[Strabo]], retelling the argument, gives the settlement on the foothills to Dardanus and the city in the plain to [[ilus|Ilus]], whom tradition made the founder of Ilium (*Geography* 13.1.25). He thinks it likely that Dardania stood in the country under Aeneas, along the foothills of Ida, though in his day not even a trace of a city was left there (13.1.24; see [[dardanians]] and [[plato-the-slopes-of-ida]]). In [[apollodorus|Apollodorus]] Dardanus comes from Samothrace to the Troad, receives part of the land and a daughter from the king Teucer, and builds a city, Dardanus; when Teucer dies he calls the whole country Dardania (*Library* 3.12.1).
