τειχιόεσσα "walled, of the great walls" ([[walled]]), from τεῖχος, "wall"; Homer gives the same epithet to Gortyn in Crete (2.646). Tiryns stands on a low ridge of rock in the plain of [[Argos]], near the sea. It belongs to [[Diomedes]]' contingent (2.559–568).

## Outside Homer

Tiryns was the city of Heracles' family; Hesiod has Heracles drive the cattle of Geryon "to holy Tiryns" (*Theogony* 292). Pausanias thought its walls the work of the Cyclopes, built of unworked stones so large that a pair of mules could not move even the smallest (2.25.8), and he complained that Greek writers described the pyramids of Egypt but not these walls (9.36.5). Heinrich Schliemann and Wilhelm Dörpfeld excavated the palace in 1884–1885. The citadel as it stands, with galleries roofed by corbeled vaults inside the thickness of its walls, dates mainly from the thirteenth century BC.
