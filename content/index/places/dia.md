## In Homer

Dia is named once, as the place of Ariadne's death. Theseus was bringing her from Crete “to the hill of sacred Athens, / but he had no joy of her; for before that Artemis slew her / in sea-washed Dia, on the testimony of Dionysus” (*Odyssey* 11.323–325; see [[ariadne]], [[theseus]], [[artemis]] and [[dionysus]]). ἀμφίρυτος, “sea-washed”, literally “flowed around”, is an epithet of islands (see [[sea-washed]]).

## Where it lies

W. W. Merry takes Homer's Dia for “the little island, now Standia, just off Heracleion, on the north coast of Crete”, which Theseus would pass in sailing for Athens (*Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on *Odyssey* 11.322; see [[crete]] and [[athens]]). Strabo knows an island of that name, Dia “near the Heracleium of Cnossus”, opposite which he sets Thera (*Geography* 10.5.1; see [[cnossus]] and [[strabo]]). The story of the Odyssey, Merry observes, differs from the common account, in which Theseus deserts Ariadne in Dia, that is, Naxos, and Dionysus takes her as his wife (on *Odyssey* 11.322). Hesiod already makes her the wife of Dionysus, whom Zeus made deathless and ageless for him (*Theogony* 947–949; see [[hesiod]]).
