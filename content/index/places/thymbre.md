πρὸς Θύμβρης, “toward Thymbre”, is the second of the two sides of the allies' camp in Dolon's account; the other is πρὸς ἁλός, “toward the sea” (see [[trojan-allies]]). The name occurs only here in Homer, and the poem does not say where Thymbre lay. A Trojan whom Diomedes kills in Book 11 is called Thymbraeus (Θυμβραῖος, 11.320), a name formed from the place.

## Outside Homer

Strabo cites the verse to place the Iliad's Ilium at the “village of the Ilians”, the older site, rather than at the Ilium of his day: near it lay the plain of Thymbra, with the river Thymbrius flowing through it into the Scamander at the temple of Thymbraean Apollo, fifty stadia from the later city (13.1.35; see [[strabo]] and [[scamander]]). Apollo has the title in poetry. In the *Rhesus* the chorus prays to “Thymbraean” Apollo (224), and Hector says that Odysseus is always found lurking in ambush by the Thymbraean altar near the city (507–509; see [[euripides-rhesus]]). At Delos, Virgil's Aeneas prays to Apollo as *Thymbraeus* for a city of his own (*Aeneid* 3.85; see [[virgil]] and [[apollo]]).
