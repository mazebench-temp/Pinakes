ἐσχατόωσα "at the land's end" is the participle of ἐσχατάω, "be at the edge", from ἔσχατος, "farthest". Anthedon lay on the coast of the Euboean Gulf at the northern edge of Boeotia, and it is the last of the twenty-nine Boeotian towns. The same word describes Myrsinus "at the border" of Elis (2.616).

## Outside Homer

Anthedon was a town of fishermen and sailors, and in legend the home of Glaucus, the fisherman who ate a magic herb and became a god of the sea. The moles of its harbor can still be seen under the water.
