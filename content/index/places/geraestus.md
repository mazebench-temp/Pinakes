## In Homer

Homer names Geraestus once, in Nestor's account of his voyage home from Troy: it is the landfall of the crossing from Lesbos “across the middle of the open sea to Euboea”, and the place of a sacrifice to Poseidon (*Odyssey* 3.174–179; see [[euboea]], [[poseidon]] and [[the-returns-of-the-achaeans]]).

## Outside Homer

Strabo notes that Geraestus is not named in the Catalogue of Ships, though the poet mentions it elsewhere, in this verse, and that the verse shows how well the place lies for those who cross from Asia to Attica, since it is near Sunium; it has the most notable temple of Poseidon in those parts, and a settlement worth mentioning (*Geography* 10.1.7; see [[sunium]]). Euboea, he says, runs about twelve hundred stadia from Cenaeum, by Thermopylae, to Geraestus, which with Petalia lies toward Sunium (10.1.2). Herodotus tells how the Persian fleet sent two hundred ships round Euboea, outside Sciathus, “by Caphereus and round Geraestus to the Euripus”, to cut off the Greeks at Artemisium (Herodotus 8.7.1). In Euripides' *Cyclops* Odysseus reminds Polyphemus, the son of Poseidon, that the Greeks kept his father's temples safe: the harbor of Taenarum, the recesses of the cape of Malea, the rock of Sunium “and the refuges of Geraestus” (*Cyclops* 290–295; see [[malea]]).
