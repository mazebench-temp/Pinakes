## The name

ῥεῖθρον is a stream or the bed of a river; Homer has the word in the plural, ῥέεθρα, of the streams of rivers (2.461, 21.218). The harbor is “the Stream”. Merry takes the name to describe a creek that served as a harbor, well within the coast line, where a ship might lie beside the fields (*Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on *Odyssey* 1.185).

## In Homer

The Odyssey names one other harbor on Ithaca, the harbor of Phorcys, the old man of the sea, between two headlands that keep out the swell, with an olive tree at its head and the cave of the nymphs nearby (*Odyssey* 13.96–112); there the Phaeacians set Odysseus ashore asleep, with their gifts (*Odyssey* 13.113–124). The ship that brought Telemachus' companions home from Pylos is drawn up on land inside “the deep harbor”, λιμένος πολυβενθέος (*Odyssey* 16.322–325). See [[ithaca]] and [[neion]].
