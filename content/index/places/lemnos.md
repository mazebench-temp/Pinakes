## Elsewhere in Homer

Lemnos is the island of Hephaestus. When Zeus threw him from Olympus he fell there, and the Sintians took him in (1.590–594); in the Odyssey it is “by far the dearest to him of all lands”, and when he has laid his snare for Ares and Aphrodite he makes as if to go there, to the Sintians (*Odyssey* 8.283–284, 8.294; see [[sintians]]). The Achaeans put in at Lemnos on their way to Troy: they left the wounded Philoctetes there (2.721–723), and Agamemnon reminds them of the boasts they made there over their wine (8.229–234). Hera goes to Lemnos, “the city of divine Thoas”, to find Sleep (14.229–231; see [[thoas-of-lemnos]] and [[sleep]]). From Lemnos Jason's son Euneus sends wine to the Achaean camp (7.467–471; see [[euneus]]), and Achilles sold Lycaon there, and other sons of Priam (21.40–46, 21.58, 21.78–79; 24.751–753).

## Outside Homer

Its main city was later called Hephaestia. A scholium on Apollonius, as Merry reports it, calls the island δίπολις, “of two cities”, for Myrina and Hephaestia, and Merry notes that its soil is volcanic and that it had a burning mountain (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on *Odyssey* 8.283). The Argonauts' visit to the island's husbandless women was a famous episode (Apollonius, *Argonautica* 1.609–909; see [[the-lemnian-women]] and [[jason]]).
