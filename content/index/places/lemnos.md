## Elsewhere in Homer

Lemnos is the island of Hephaestus. When Zeus threw him from Olympus he fell there, and the Sintians took him in (1.590–594); the Odyssey calls it "by far the dearest to him of all lands" (Od. 8.283–284) and brings him back there among the Sintians (Od. 8.294). The Achaeans put in at Lemnos on their way to Troy: they left the wounded Philoctetes there (2.721–723), and Agamemnon reminds them of the boasts they made there over their wine (8.229–234). Hera goes to Lemnos, "the city of godlike Thoas", to find Sleep (14.229–231). From Lemnos Jason's son Euneus sends wine to the Achaean camp (7.467–471; see [[euneus]]), and Achilles sold Lycaon there, and other sons of Priam (21.40–46, 21.58, 21.78–79; 24.751–753).

## Outside Homer

Its main city was later called Hephaestia. The Argonauts' visit to the island's husbandless women was a famous episode (Apollonius, *Argonautica* 1.609–909; see [[the-lemnian-women]] and [[jason]]).
