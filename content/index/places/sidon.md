Σιδονίηθεν, “from Sidon” (6.291), is formed from Σιδονίη, “the land of Sidon”. The Iliad names the place only here: the women whose robes Hecuba keeps came from it with Alexander, on the voyage that brought Helen to Troy (see [[sidonians]], [[paris]] and [[the-abduction-of-helen]]). It does not say why he went there. The Odyssey has “well-inhabited Sidonie” (Od. 13.285) and “Sidon rich in bronze” (Σιδῶνος πολυχάλκου), home of the Phoenician woman who carried off the child Eumaeus (Od. 15.425). Homer never names Tyre.

## Outside Homer

In the *Cypria*, as [[proclus|Proclus]] summarizes it, a storm sent by Hera drove Alexander to Sidon, and he took the city. [[Herodotus]] quotes 6.289–292 to show that Homer knew of the lovers' wanderings, since Sidon is Phoenician, the Phoenicians live in Syria, and Syria borders on Egypt; the *Cypria*, which brought them from Sparta to Troy in three days, cannot then be Homer's (Herodotus 2.116–117; see [[helen-in-egypt]]). In [[apollodorus|Apollodorus]] the storm forces them into Sidon, and Alexander lingers in Phoenicia and Cyprus for fear of pursuit (*Epitome* 3.4). Sidon is modern Saida, in Lebanon.
