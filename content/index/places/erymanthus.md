## In Homer

Homer names Erymanthus once, as one of the mountains over which Artemis goes hunting: “And as Artemis of the showering arrows goes over the mountains, / along towering Taygetus or Erymanthus, / delighting in boars and swift deer” (*Odyssey* 6.102–104; see [[artemis]], [[taygetus]] and [[artemis-among-her-nymphs]]). The poems do not tell the story of the boar of Erymanthus, but Merry finds the boars of the simile, κάπροι, peculiarly fitting for that reason (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on *Odyssey* 6.102; see [[boar]]).

## Outside Homer

Merry describes Erymanthus as “a lofty range between Arcadia, Achaia, and Elis” (on *Odyssey* 6.102; see [[arcadia]] and [[elis]]); his Achaia is the later name of the coast along the Gulf of Corinth, Homer's Aegialus (see [[aegialus]]). The Suda calls it “the name of a mountain”, and adds the Erymanthian boar, with the story that the Dryopes, a lawless people near Pytho, refused Heracles food when he was carrying the boar, and that he moved them from their land (Suda ε 3104, κ 349; see [[heracles]]). The boar was one of the labors: Cicero's Heracles, in his Latin version of the hero's lament from Sophocles' *Trachiniae*, asks whether it was this right hand that laid low “the ravaging Erymanthian beast” (*Tusculan Disputations* 2.20–22), and Cicero asks elsewhere whether Hercules was angry when he fought the Erymanthian boar or the Nemean lion (*Tusculan Disputations* 4.50; see [[cicero]]).

Strabo knows Erymanthus as a river too, which flows into the Alpheus with the Ladon; of the rivers that fall into the Alpheus, those who know the country told him, it is the one that bounds Arcadia (*Geography* 8.3.12, 8.3.32; see [[strabo]] and [[alpheus]]).
