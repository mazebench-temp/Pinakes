# Βουδείῳ (16.572) occurs only here in Homer.
grc: [Βουδείῳ]
---

ὅς ῥʼ ἐν Βουδείῳ εὖ ναιομένῳ ἤνασσε / τὸ πρίν, “who had been lord in well-peopled Budeum / before” (16.572–573; see [[epeigeus]]). εὖ ναιόμενος, “well-peopled”, is the poem's common praise of a town: Ilios is “the well-peopled citadel” (εὖ ναιόμενον πτολίεθρον, 2.133, 9.402), and Agamemnon offers Achilles “seven well-peopled citadels” (9.149; see [[well-peopled]] and [[the-seven-cities]]). The town is the measure of what Epeigeus lost: he had ruled it, and after killing his cousin he fought at Troy as one of the Myrmidons of Achilles (16.570–576; see [[exile-and-refuge]] and [[myrmidons]]). The poem does not say where Budeum lay, or who ruled it after him.
