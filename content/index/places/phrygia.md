Book 3 names Phrygia twice. Priam remembers going there in his youth, to "vine-rich Phrygia" (Φρυγίην ἀμπελόεσσαν; see [[vine-rich]]), where he fought as an ally beside the Phrygians of [[Otreus]] and [[Mygdon]] on the Sangarius on the day the [[Amazons]] came (3.184–189; see [[priam-and-the-amazons]]). Helen, refusing Aphrodite, asks whether the goddess will lead her on to some city "of Phrygia or of lovely Maeonia" (3.401; see [[maeonia]]).

{{notes}}

## Elsewhere in Homer

Hecuba's brother Asius, son of Dymas, lived "in Phrygia by the streams of the Sangarius" (16.717–719). Hector names the same pair of countries as Helen when he says that Troy's treasures have been sold off "to Phrygia and lovely Maeonia" to pay for the war (18.288–292). Achilles marks the limits of Priam's old kingdom by Lesbos, "Phrygia above" and the Hellespont (24.544–545).

## Outside Homer

In the *Homeric Hymn to Aphrodite*, the goddess, disguised as a mortal girl, tells Anchises that her father is Otreus, "who rules all Phrygia with its fine walls" (111–112). The later kingdom of Phrygia, with its capital at Gordion, is described under [[phrygians]].
