## In Homer

Nausicaa tells Odysseus of the grove when she gives him his directions, and he reaches it at sunset (*Odyssey* 6.291–296, 6.321–322; see [[nausicaa]]). It is a grove of poplars sacred to Athena, near the road, with a spring flowing in it and a meadow around; Alcinous' domain and his flourishing orchard are there, at the distance at which a man can make himself heard with a shout from the city (see [[alcinous]] and [[the-city-of-the-phaeacians]]). Odysseus sits in it and prays to Athena to let him come to the Phaeacians welcomed and pitied, and she hears him, though she does not yet appear to him, out of respect for her father's brother (*Odyssey* 6.321–331; see [[athena]] and [[poseidon]]). He is still there, praying, when the next book begins, and the mules carry Nausicaa on to the city (*Odyssey* 7.1–2).

The Odyssey has one other grove of poplars by a spring outside a city: on Ithaca, near the town, a fountain built by Ithacus, Neritus and Polyctor, where the townsmen draw water, with a round grove of water-nourished poplars, cold water falling from the rock, and above it an altar of the nymphs, at which all wayfarers sacrifice (*Odyssey* 17.205–211, a plain rendering; see [[nymphs]] and [[ithaca]]).

## Outside Homer

Merry calls Alcinous' τέμενος “the royal park”, and notes that the memory of such a domain was kept in Corcyra in the time of the Peloponnesian War, when Thucydides speaks of cutting stakes from the precinct of Zeus and that of Alcinous (Thucydides 3.70, as Merry cites it; Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on *Odyssey* 6.293; see [[thucydides]] and [[scheria]]).
