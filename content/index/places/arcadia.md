The Arcadian entry places the land by its landmarks: "beneath the steep mountain of [[Cyllene]], beside the [[tomb-of-aepytus|tomb of Aepytus]], where men fight hand to hand" (ἀνέρες ἀγχιμαχηταί; [[hand-to-hand-fighters]]). Its towns follow: [[Pheneus]], [[orchomenus-arcadia|Orchomenus]] "rich in flocks", [[Rhipe]], [[Stratia]], "windy" [[Enispe]], [[Tegea]], "lovely" [[Mantinea]], [[Stymphalus]] and [[Parrhasia]] (2.605–608). The list covers the northeast of Arcadia, around Cyllene, the eastern plains of Tegea and Mantinea, and the southwest.

{{quote:4dfff20e-2a16-553c-b456-a32d9b85d1a6}}

The [[Arcadians]] are the only people of the Catalogue without ships of their own. Agamemnon gave them sixty, "since they cared nothing for the works of the sea". Thucydides cites the passage as proof of Agamemnon's naval power (1.9.4; see [[thucydides-and-the-catalogue]]).

## Elsewhere in Homer

In one of his tales of his youth Nestor fights "the Arcadians, fighters with the spear" (ἐγχεσίμωροι; [[fighters-with-the-spear]]), by the river Celadon, and kills their champion Ereuthalion (7.133–156).

## Outside Homer

The Arcadian dialect of historical times is closely related to the Greek of Cyprus, and both preserve features of the Greek of the Mycenaean tablets; the kinship is usually explained by settlement from the Peloponnese in the late Bronze Age. Legend expressed it differently: [[Agapenor]], the Arcadian leader at Troy, was driven to Cyprus by a storm on the way home and founded Paphos (Pausanias 8.5.2). Arcadia was the land of Pan and of Hermes, who was born on Cyllene. Virgil's *Eclogues* made it the landscape of pastoral poetry (*Eclogue* 10).
