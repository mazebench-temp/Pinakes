Idomeneus kills “Phaestus, son of the Maeonian Borus, who had come from deep-soiled Tarne” (5.43–44). ἐριβῶλαξ, “deep-soiled”, literally “with great clods” (ἐρι- “very” + βῶλος “clod”), is a standing epithet of fertile country: Phthia (1.155), Larisa (2.841) and Troy itself (3.74). See [[deep-soiled]]. The phrase ὃς ἐκ … ἐριβώλακος εἰληλούθει, “who had come from deep-soiled …”, is a formula for a man from a distant land. Apisaon, “who had come from deep-soiled Paeonia” (17.350), and the Thracian Rhigmus (20.485) are introduced with it.

The poem says nothing more of Tarne. Since Borus was a Maeonian, it was presumably in Maeonia, the country under Mount Tmolus that was later called Lydia (2.864–866; see [[maeonians]] and [[maeonia]]).
