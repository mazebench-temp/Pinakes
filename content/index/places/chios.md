## Descriptions

παιπαλόεσσα, “rocky” (*Odyssey* 3.170), is the rugged look of a coast or a height: the Iliad gives it to Imbros (13.33, 24.78) and to a mountain path (17.743), and the Odyssey to Ithaca, Samos and a lookout (*Odyssey* 4.671, 10.97, 11.480). Merry derives the word from a reduplicated form of πάλλω and takes it of the rugged lines of upheaved rock on the Chian coast (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on *Odyssey* 3.170).

## Outside Homer

Simonides, or a poet quoted under his name, called Homer “the man of Chios” (see [[the-man-of-chios]]), and the *Hymn to Apollo* ends with the singer's description of himself as a blind man who lives in rocky Chios, whose songs will be the best hereafter (*Hymn to Apollo* 172–173); the same hymn calls Chios the brightest of the islands that lie in the sea (*Hymn to Apollo* 38). Pindar speaks of the Homeridae, “the sons of Homer” (*Nemean* 2.1–2), and later writers place them on Chios: Strabo says that the Chians claim Homer and point to the Homeridae as their great evidence, quoting Pindar (*Geography* 14.1.35).
