πέφνε γὰρ Ὀθρυονῆα Καβησόθεν ἔνδον ἐόντα, “for he killed Othryoneus, from Cabesus, who was within the city” (13.363). Καβησόθεν is the name with the ending -θεν, “from”, as Ἀρίσβηθεν is “from Arisbe” (2.838; see [[arisbe]]). It tells where Othryoneus came from when he joined the Trojans at the report of the war to win Cassandra (13.363–369; see [[othryoneus]] and [[cassandra]]).

Homer names the place nowhere else and gives no hint of where it was. [[Strabo]], who quotes the line, does not place it (*Geography* 13.1.40), and the Suda says only that Cabesus was a city (κ 7).
