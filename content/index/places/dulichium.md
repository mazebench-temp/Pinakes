The name looks like the adjective δολιχός, "long". In the Catalogue Dulichium and the holy [[Echinae]] are "islands that lie across the sea, opposite Elis", and they belong not to Odysseus but to [[Meges]], son of Phyleus (2.625–630). Phyleus had gone to live there after quarreling with his father (2.629; see [[phyleus-and-augeas]]).

## Elsewhere in Homer

In the Odyssey Dulichium is named with Same and wooded Zacynthus as one of the islands around Ithaca (Od. 1.246, 9.24). It is "rich in wheat and grassy" (Od. 16.396), and it sends the largest group of Penelope's suitors, fifty-two young men (Od. 16.247).

## Outside Homer

No island was called Dulichium in historical times, and its identity is one of the old puzzles of Homeric geography. Ancient geographers took it for one of the Echinades at the mouth of the Achelous; others have proposed Cephallenia or part of it. Wilhelm Dörpfeld, who took Leucas for Homer's Ithaca, identified Dulichium with Cephallenia. See [[Ithaca]].
