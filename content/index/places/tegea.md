Tegea lay in the high plain of southeastern Arcadia, on the road from Argos to Sparta. It is one of the towns of the [[Arcadians]].

## Outside Homer

Herodotus tells how the Spartans, long beaten by the Tegeans, won only after they had found and carried home the bones of Orestes buried at Tegea (1.66–68). At Plataea the Tegeans claimed the place of honor on the left wing (Herodotus 9.26–28). Its temple of Athena Alea was rebuilt in the fourth century BC by the sculptor Scopas, and its pediment showed the hunt of the Calydonian boar (Pausanias 8.45.4–7).
