The name is the common noun ἕλος, "marsh". ἔφαλον πτολίεθρον "the citadel by the sea" ([[by-the-sea]]). Helos lay in the marshy plain near the mouth of the Eurotas, on the Laconian Gulf, and belongs to Menelaus' [[Lacedaemon]]. Another Helos belongs to Nestor (2.594; [[helos-pylos]]).

## Outside Homer

An ancient etymology derived the name of the helots, the serfs of Sparta, from the people of Helos, said to have been enslaved when the Spartans conquered it. Modern scholars more often connect the word with ἑλεῖν, "to capture".
