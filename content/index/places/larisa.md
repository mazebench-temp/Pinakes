ἐριβῶλαξ, "deep-soiled" ([[deep-soiled]]). When Hippothous dies over the body of Patroclus, the narrator says that he fell "far from deep-soiled Larisa" (17.301). Larisa was a common place-name. The best known is Larisa in Thessaly, and there were others in the Troad, near Hamaxitus, and in Aeolis, near Cyme. Strabo argued that Homer's must be the one near Cyme, since the Troad Larisa was too close to Troy for a man to die "far from" it (*Geography*, Book 13). The name is probably pre-Greek; it has been taken to mean "citadel", but that is uncertain.
