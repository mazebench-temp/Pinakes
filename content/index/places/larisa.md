ἐριβῶλαξ, "deep-soiled" ([[deep-soiled]]). The Pelasgians' leader Hippothous dies over the body of Patroclus "far from deep-soiled Larisa" (17.301). Larisa was a common place-name. The best known is Larisa in Thessaly, and there were others in the Troad, near Hamaxitus, and in Aeolis, near Cyme. Strabo argued that Homer's must be the one near Cyme: of the three Larisas in the region, the one by Hamaxitus is in full sight of Ilium, about two hundred stadia away, so that Hippothous could not plausibly be said to fall in the fight over Patroclus "far from" it, while the one near Cyme is about a thousand stadia away, and the third, a village in the Caystrian plain near Ephesus, one would rather place among the Maeonians (*Geography* 13.3.2). The name is probably pre-Greek; it has been taken to mean "citadel", but that is uncertain.
