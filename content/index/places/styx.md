ὅρκου γὰρ δεινοῦ Στυγὸς ὕδατος: "the water of Styx, the dread oath". The river and the oath are one: the genitive ὅρκου, in apposition, names what Styx is for the gods. Στύξ is connected with στυγέω ("hate, abhor"): it is the "hateful" river.

## Elsewhere in Homer

The gods' great oath names it. Hera swears to Zeus by "the down-flowing water of Styx, which is the greatest and most dread oath for the blessed gods" (15.36–38), and Calypso swears the same oath to Odysseus (*Odyssey* 5.185–186). Hypnos makes Hera swear by "the inviolable water of Styx" (14.271). Athena says that without her Heracles would never have escaped "the sheer streams of the water of Styx" when he went down for the dog of Hades (8.369). In the Odyssey the Cocytus is "a branch of the water of Styx" (10.514), in the same words as here.

## Outside Homer

In Hesiod, Styx is the eldest daughter of Oceanus and a goddess. Her children, Zelos, Nike, Kratos and Bia (Rivalry, Victory, Power and Force), were the first to join Zeus against the Titans, and in return he made her the great oath of the gods (*Theogony* 383–403). A god who swears falsely by her water lies breathless for a year and is shut out of the gods' councils and feasts for nine more (775–806). The Greeks also knew a real Styx, a waterfall in the mountains of northern Arcadia near Nonacris, whose water was thought deadly; Herodotus tells how Cleomenes of Sparta tried to make the Arcadians swear by it (6.74).
