The Catalogue describes the place rather than a town. Onchestus is holy (ἱερός; [[hieros]]) and a splendid grove (ἀγλαὸν ἄλσος; [[splendid]]) of Poseidon. Ποσιδήϊος is the adjective of Poseidon's name, "Poseidon's", and ἄλσος is a sacred grove. It lay on the low ridge between the basin of Lake Copais and the plain of Thebes.

## Outside Homer

The Homeric Hymn to Apollo describes a rite there. A young colt, freed of its driver, rattles an empty chariot through the grove; if the chariot crashes among the trees, the owners take the horses home and leave the chariot to the god (229–238). In the Hymn to Hermes an old man tending his vineyard at Onchestus sees the infant god driving off Apollo's cattle (87–93, 186–211). In later times the sanctuary was a meeting place of the Boeotians.
