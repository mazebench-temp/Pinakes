# Od. 4.798 is Eumelus' Pherae in Thessaly (pherae)
except: [Od. 4.798]
---

“Whose father lived in well-built Pherae, wealthy in substance” (5.543–544): ἐϋκτιμένῃ ἐνὶ Φηρῇ, with a standing epithet of towns (see [[well-built]]). The Iliad here has the singular, Φηρή; elsewhere Homer uses the plural, Φηραί. It is not the Thessalian Pherae of Admetus and Eumelus (Φεραί, 2.711; see [[pherae]]).

The Odyssey places it between Pylos and Sparta. Telemachus and Peisistratus leave Pylos, drive all day, and come at sunset to Pherae and the house of Diocles; the next day they drive on to Lacedaemon (*Odyssey* 3.484–497, 4.1). They stop at Pherae again on the way home (*Odyssey* 15.182–188). Among the seven towns that Agamemnon offers Achilles, “all near the sea, at the edge of sandy Pylos”, is “holy Pherae” (Φηρὰς ζαθέας, 9.151–153, 9.293–295), probably the same town. It is usually placed at Kalamata, at the head of the Messenian Gulf, where the later town of Pharae (Φαραί) stood.

## Outside Homer

Strabo, going round the Messenian Gulf, names Pherae after Cardamyle, bordering on Thuria and Gerena, with the Nedon flowing into the sea near it (*Geography* 8.4.4). He uses the Odyssey's verse, “they came to Pherae, to the house of Diocles, / son of Ortilochus” (*Odyssey* 3.488–489 = 15.186–187), to show that the house of Ortilochus, where Odysseus met Iphitus in Messene (*Odyssey* 21.15–16), was at Pherae, and so that Messenia was part of Menelaus' Lacedaemon (*Geography* 8.5.8; see [[strabo]], [[ortilochus]] and [[lacedaemon]]). The people of the Messenian town, he says, are called Φαραιᾶται, and those of Phara in Achaea Φαρεῖς (*Geography* 8.7.5).
