καλλιγύναικα, "of the lovely women" (see [[of-the-lovely-women]]). The Iliad always places Hellas close to Phthia. Achilles says there are many Achaean women "throughout Hellas and Phthia" (9.395). Phoenix left "Hellas of the lovely women" after quarreling with his father, and fled “through Hellas with its wide dancing floors” to Peleus in Phthia (9.447, 9.478). Bathycles, a rich Myrmidon killed in Book 16, "lived in Hellas" (16.595). The Odyssey keeps the pair: the shade of Achilles asks whether Peleus is still honored among the Myrmidons, or slighted throughout Hellas and Phthia now that he is old (ἀνʼ Ἑλλάδα τε Φθίην τε, *Odyssey* 11.494–497).

The Odyssey's phrase καθʼ Ἑλλάδα καὶ μέσον Ἄργος, “through Hellas and the heart of Argos” (*Odyssey* 1.344, 4.726, 4.816; with ἀνά, *Odyssey* 15.80), already reaches toward the whole of Greece. Later Ἑλλάς became the land of all the [[Hellenes]], wherever they lived: in Hesiod the Achaeans gather their host at Aulis “from holy Hellas” for Troy (*Works and Days* 651–653). Thucydides noticed that in Homer the Hellenes are only Achilles' men (1.3.3; see [[thucydides-hellenes]]).
