καλλιγύναικα, "of the lovely women" (see [[of-the-lovely-women]]). The Iliad always places Hellas close to Phthia. Achilles says there are many Achaean women "throughout Hellas and Phthia" (9.395). Phoenix left "Hellas of the lovely women" after quarreling with his father, and fled “through Hellas with its wide dancing floors” to Peleus in Phthia (9.447, 9.478). Bathycles, a rich Myrmidon killed in Book 16, "lived in Hellas" (16.595).

The Odyssey's phrase καθʼ Ἑλλάδα καὶ μέσον Ἄργος, "through Hellas and the midst of Argos" (1.344, 4.726), already reaches toward the whole of Greece. Later Ἑλλάς became the land of all the [[Hellenes]], wherever they lived. Thucydides noticed that in Homer the Hellenes are only Achilles' men (1.3.3; see [[thucydides-hellenes]]).
