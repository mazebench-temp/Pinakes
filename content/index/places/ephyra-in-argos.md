ἔστι πόλις Ἐφύρη μυχῷ Ἄργεος ἱπποβότοιο: Glaucus begins his lineage with a place, in the storyteller's way of opening a description, “there is a city …” (compare ἔστι δέ τις Θρυόεσσα πόλις, 11.711, and 2.811). μυχός is an inner corner or recess. Argos here is the Peloponnese or the Argolid (see [[argos]] and [[horse-pasturing]]); the Odyssey uses the same words of Aegisthus, at his ease “in a corner of horse-pasturing Argos” while the others fought at Troy (Od. 3.263). His ancestors, Glaucus says at the end, “were by far the best in Ephyra and in wide Lycia” (6.209–210): the family has two homes, the city it came from and the kingdom it won.

Homer names Corinth separately, as a town of Agamemnon's realm (2.570) and the home of Euchenor (13.664), and never says that Ephyra is Corinth. The identification is later. Apollodorus says that Sisyphus founded “Ephyra, now called Corinth” (*Library* 1.9.3), Pausanias that the Corinthian poet Eumelus made Ephyra, a daughter of Oceanus, the first to live in the land (2.1.1), and Strabo counts “the Corinthian” among the several Ephyras (8.3.5). The Ephyra on the river Selleis, from which Heracles carried off Astyocheia, is another place (2.659, 15.531; see [[ephyra]]). See [[sisyphus]], [[bellerophon]] and [[corinth]].
