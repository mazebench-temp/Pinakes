κοίλη "hollow": the Eurotas valley lies deep between the ranges of Taygetus and Parnon. κητώεσσα is uncertain in meaning; it is usually taken as "full of ravines" or "of the many clefts", and the translation renders it "with its many ravines". An ancient variant, καιετάεσσα, was explained from καιετοί, a Laconian word for clefts opened in the ground by earthquakes. Strabo reports both readings: κητώεσσα was taken either from κήτη, “sea-beasts”, or as “great”, which he found the more plausible, and καιετάεσσα either as “full of calamint” or from the καιετοί, from which the Lacedaemonians' prison, a cave, was called the Kaietas; Laconia, he adds, is much shaken by earthquakes (*Geography* 8.5.7; see [[strabo]]). The whole line recurs at the opening of *Odyssey* 4, when Telemachus arrives at Menelaus' palace (*Odyssey* 4.1).

The towns of the land are [[Pharis]], [[Sparta]], [[Messe]], [[Bryseae]], [[augeiae-laconia|Augeiae]], [[Amyclae]], [[Helos]], [[Laas]] and [[Oetylus]] (2.582–585). [[Menelaus]] leads them with sixty ships. His men arm apart from his brother's, and he goes among them "trusting in his own eagerness", longing "to pay them back for the struggles and groans for Helen's sake" (2.586–590).

## Elsewhere in Homer

Lacedaemon is Helen's homeland. In the Odyssey Nestor sends Telemachus there, “to shining Lacedaemon, where fair-haired Menelaus is” (*Odyssey* 3.326), and he visits Menelaus and Helen there (*Odyssey* 4).
