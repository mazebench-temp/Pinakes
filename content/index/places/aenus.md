Πείρως Ἰμβρασίδης ὃς ἄρʼ Αἰνόθεν εἰληλούθει, “Peirous son of Imbrasus, who had come from Aenus” (4.520). Αἰνόθεν = Αἶνος + -θεν (“from”). It is the only mention of the town in Homer. It places the Thracian leader on the Aegean coast of Thrace, northwest of the Hellespont (see [[thracians]] and [[peirous]]).

## Outside Homer

In historical times Aenus was a Greek city. Herodotus calls it Aeolian: Xerxes' army, marching west from the Chersonese to Doriscus, passed “Aenus, an Aeolian city, and the lake Stentoris” (Herodotus 7.58.3), and the Hebrus reaches the sea “by the city of Aenus” (Herodotus 4.90.2). The site is the modern Enez, in the European part of Turkey.
