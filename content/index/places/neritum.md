εἰνοσίφυλλος "with quivering leaves" ([[with-quivering-leaves]]). In the Catalogue the Cephallenians "held Ithaca and Neritum", as if the mountain were a town. The Odyssey names it twice as Ithaca's landmark: Odysseus' island has on it “a mountain, / Neritum with its quivering leaves, conspicuous” (*Odyssey* 9.21–22), and when Athena reveals Ithaca to him, she points to it, “this is Neritum, the mountain clothed in forest” (*Odyssey* 13.351, a plain rendering). Strabo takes the Odyssey's verse to show that the mountain stands in the island, whether or not the Catalogue's Ithaca is the town (*Geography* 10.2.11; see [[strabo]]); some ancient critics read Neion, the other mountain of Ithaca, in its place (see [[neion]]). It is usually identified with Mount Niritos in the north of Ithaki.
