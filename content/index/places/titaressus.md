ἱμερτός, "lovely", literally "longed for" (from ἵμερος, "desire"). καλλίρροον ὕδωρ, "fair-flowing water".

The Catalogue stops here for a marvel. The Titaressus pours into the Peneius, "yet it does not mingle with the silver-eddying Peneius, but flows on top of it like olive oil" (see [[like-olive-oil]]). There is a natural fact behind it: where two rivers of different color and weight meet, their waters can run side by side for some distance before they mix. The explanation is mythical. The Titaressus is ἀπορρώξ, "a piece broken off, a branch", of the water of Styx, the river of the underworld by which the gods swear, and water so holy will not mix with common water. The Odyssey uses the same words of the Cocytus, a river of Hades, "which is a branch of the water of Styx" (10.513–514).

The river is usually identified with the modern Titarisios, a tributary that joins the Peneius in northern Thessaly. Lucan retells the marvel in his description of Thessaly (*Pharsalia* 6.375–380; see [[like-olive-oil]]).
