Salamis is the home of Telamonian [[Ajax]], son of [[Telamon]]; in Book 7 he says that he was born and raised there (7.198–199). Its entry is the shortest in the Catalogue: no towns, no father's name, no epithet, and only twelve ships for one of the two greatest Achaean fighters.

The second line says that Ajax stationed his ships "where the battalions of the [[Athenians]] stood". The rest of the poem does not bear this out. Ajax's ships are drawn up at one end of the camp and Achilles' at the other (8.222–226, 11.5–9), and when Agamemnon reviews the army the Athenians stand beside Odysseus (4.327–329).

## Outside Homer

In the sixth century BC Athens and Megara fought over Salamis. Solon urged the Athenians to take it in an elegy called *Salamis* (Plutarch, *Solon* 8), and in the dispute both sides appealed to Homer. Ancient critics said that Solon or Peisistratus had added the second line to support the Athenian claim (Plutarch, *Solon* 10; Aristotle, *Rhetoric* 1.15, 1375b30; Strabo 9.1.10); see [[solon-and-salamis]]. In 480 BC the Greek fleet defeated the Persians in the strait between the island and the mainland (Herodotus 8.40–96; Aeschylus, *Persians*). According to legend, Ajax's brother Teucer, banished by their father, founded a new Salamis on Cyprus (Horace, *Odes* 1.7.21–32). A Mycenaean settlement at Kanakia, on the island's southwest coast, has been excavated since 2000; its connection with Ajax's family is a hypothesis.
