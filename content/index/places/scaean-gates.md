## Etymology

Σκαιαὶ πύλαι, "the Scaean gates": the word for a city gate, with its two leaves, is plural in Greek. The name is usually connected with σκαιός, "left", and so perhaps "western", but why the gate was called so is not known.

{{notes}}

## Elsewhere in Homer

It is the gate of the Iliad's great Trojan scenes. In Book 3 Helen goes there to join the elders on the tower above it, and Priam drives out through it to the oath sacrifice (3.145–153, 3.263). Hector comes back to the city by the Scaean gates and the oak tree, and there Andromache meets him (6.237, 6.392–394). Achilles says that while he fought, Hector never ventured beyond the Scaean gates and the oak (9.352–355). Hector waits for Achilles before them (22.5–6), and the dying Hector foretells that Paris and Apollo will kill Achilles "at the Scaean gates" (22.359–360).
