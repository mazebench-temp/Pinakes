Homer uses the name Argos for several places: Diomedes' city, Agamemnon's realm, the Peloponnese, or all of Greece (see [[argos]]). Ancient scholars explained Ἄργος as an old word for a plain. The adjective marks this one off. The Greeks thought of the Pelasgians as the people who held Greece before them. In Homer they are allies of Troy from Larisa (2.840–843; see [[pelasgians]]) and one of the peoples of Crete (*Odyssey* 19.177), and Achilles prays to "Zeus, lord of Dodona, Pelasgian" (16.233).

What exactly Pelasgian Argos was is not clear. Ancient readers disagreed whether it was a town or a whole district, and most took it for the Thessalian plain or part of it. In historical times one of the four districts of Thessaly was called Pelasgiotis.
