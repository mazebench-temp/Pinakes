The Rhesus heads the list of the rivers that bring the Achaean wall to nothing (12.20; see [[the-achaean-wall]] and [[the-building-of-the-wall]]). It has the name of the Thracian king whom Diomedes kills in his sleep in Book 10, but the poem does not connect the two (see [[rhesus]]).

## Outside Homer

Hesiod names the Rhesus among the rivers that Tethys bore to Ocean (*Theogony* 340; see [[hesiod]]). [[strabo|Strabo]], reporting Demetrius of Scepsis, writes that “the Rhesus is now called the Rhoeites, unless the one that flows into the Granicus is the Rhesus” (13.1.44; see [[granicus]]). Where it ran is not known.

An ancient note gives the same later name, the Rhoeites, and says that the river flows north from the Fair Pine, 180 stades from Adramyttium (scholium T on 12.20).
