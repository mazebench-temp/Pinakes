## The name

Ἄργος is neuter. In Homer it has several ranges: a city, the city of Diomedes (2.559); the kingdom of the house of Atreus, "all Argos" (2.108); the Peloponnese, "Achaean Argos" (Ἄργος Ἀχαιικόν, 9.141), and in the Odyssey's phrase for all Greece, “through Hellas and the heart of Argos” (καθʼ Ἑλλάδα καὶ μέσον Ἄργος, *Odyssey* 1.344; see [[hellas]]); and the homeland of the whole army, to which the Achaeans hope to return (2.115). A region of Thessaly in Achilles' kingdom is "Pelasgian Argos" (2.681; see [[pelasgian-argos]]). From Argos come the [[Argives]], one of Homer's three names for the army. Its epithets are "horse-pasturing" (ἱππόβοτον; [[horse-pasturing]]) and, once, "very thirsty" (πολυδίψιον, 4.171). It is one of the three cities Hera loves most, with Sparta and Mycenae (4.51–52). The origin of the name is unknown.

## Outside Homer

The city lies at the foot of two hills, the Larisa and the Aspis, near the head of the Argolic Gulf. The great sanctuary of Hera, the Heraion, stood across the plain toward Mycenae. No Mycenaean palace has been found at Argos; in that age the chief centers of the plain were Mycenae and Tiryns. In historical times Argos was the leading city of the plain, and it destroyed Mycenae in the 460s BC (Diodorus 11.65). Cleisthenes, tyrant of Sicyon and an enemy of Argos, stopped the rhapsodes from competing in Homer's poems at Sicyon, "because the Argives and Argos are celebrated in them almost everywhere" (Herodotus 5.67).
