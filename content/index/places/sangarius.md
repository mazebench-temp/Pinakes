In Priam's memory the Phrygians of [[Otreus]] and [[Mygdon]] were "encamped along the banks of the Sangarius" (παρʼ ὄχθας Σαγγαρίοιο) on the day the Amazons came (3.187; see [[priam-and-the-amazons]]). The river returns in Book 16, when Apollo takes the likeness of Asius, Hecuba's brother, son of Dymas, "who lived in Phrygia by the streams of the Sangarius" (16.717–719).

## Outside Homer

Hesiod names "great Sangarius" among the rivers born to Oceanus and Tethys (*Theogony* 337–345). In some genealogies Hecuba herself is the daughter of the river Sangarius (Apollodorus, *Library* 3.12.5). The river, the modern Sakarya, rises in central Anatolia and flows north into the Black Sea.
