Κελάδων is κελάδων, “roaring”, the participle of κελάδω (“resound, roar”), made into a name: “the Roarer”. The participle describes a river on the shield of Achilles, πὰρ ποταμὸν κελάδοντα, “beside a roaring river” (18.576). ὠκύροος, “swift-flowing” (7.133; see [[swift-flowing]]), is given in the Iliad to one other river, the one before which a traveler stands helpless in a simile (5.597–599).

The Celadon is the first of the three landmarks by which Nestor places the battle, with the walls of Pheia and the streams of the Iardanus (see [[pheia]], [[iardanus]] and [[nestor-and-ereuthalion]]). Pheia lay on the coast of Elis, but the river itself is not named again. [[strabo|Strabo]] reports that some readers wanted Ἀκίδοντι for Κελάδοντι, putting the battle on the Acidon near Lepreum, closer to Arcadia (8.3.21). Pausanias lists a Celadus among the Arcadian streams that feed the Alpheus (8.38.9), a similar name, but nothing ties it to Nestor's river.
