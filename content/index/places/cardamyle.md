Cardamyle heads the list of the “seven well-peopled citadels” that “all lie near the sea, on the borders of sandy Pylos” (9.149–153; see [[the-seven-cities]] and [[the-gifts-of-agamemnon]]). Homer names it nowhere else and gives it no epithet.

## Outside Homer

Strabo and Pausanias agree on its place, as they do not for most of the seven. Strabo sets it “on a strong rock” on the coast between Leuctrum and Pherae, on the sea itself (8.4.4–5; see [[strabo]]). Pausanias, who notes that Homer named it among Agamemnon's promised gifts, puts it eight stades from the sea and sixty from Leuctra; Augustus had cut it off from Messenia and given it to the Spartans. Near the beach was a precinct of the daughters of Nereus, who are said to have come up out of the sea there to see Pyrrhus, son of Achilles, on his way to Sparta to marry Hermione (3.26.7). The village of Kardamyli in the Mani keeps the name.
