Hyperesia lay on a height above the southern shore of the Gulf of Corinth, in the coastal land later called Achaea. It belongs to [[Agamemnon]]'s realm.

The Odyssey names it once, in the genealogy of the seer Theoclymenus: his father Polypheides, whom Apollo made the best of seers after Amphiaraus died, “moved away to Hyperesia, angry with his father, / and living there he prophesied for all mortals” (*Odyssey* 15.252–255; see [[polypheides]] and [[theoclymenus]]).

{{notes}}

## Outside Homer

Strabo, at the end of his account of Sicyon, says that “Hyperesia and the cities which the poet names next”, and the Aegialus as far as Dyme and the borders of Elis, belonged in his day to the Achaeans (*Geography* 8.6.25; see [[strabo]]). Pausanias identifies it with Aegeira, one of the twelve cities of Achaea, which he says Homer called Hyperesia (7.26.1–2).
