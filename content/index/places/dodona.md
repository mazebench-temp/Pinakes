δυσχείμερον = δυσ- ("bad") + χεῖμα ("winter, storm"): "wintry, with harsh winters". Dodona lies in a high valley of Epirus.

## Elsewhere in Homer

Achilles prays to the Zeus of Dodona, “Pelasgian”, around whom the Selli dwell, before he sends Patroclus out to fight (16.233–235; see [[selli]] and [[pelasgians]]). In the Odyssey, Odysseus is said to have gone to Dodona “to hear the will of Zeus from the god's high-leafed oak” (*Odyssey* 14.327–328, 19.296–297).

## Outside Homer

Herodotus calls Dodona the oldest oracle in Greece and gives two stories of its founding. The priests of Egyptian Thebes told him that Phoenicians had carried off two priestesses and sold one into Libya and the other into Greece; the priestesses of Dodona said that a black dove flew from Thebes, settled on an oak and told them in a human voice to found an oracle of Zeus (2.52–57). The god was consulted at his oak, and excavations have recovered thousands of small lead tablets on which visitors wrote their questions to Zeus and Dione.

[[strabo|Strabo]] reports that the oracle had almost failed in his time. Following Ephorus he calls it a foundation of the Pelasgians, and quotes Achilles' address to the “Pelasgian” Zeus with a verse of Hesiod, “Dodona and the oak, the seat of the Pelasgians” (*Geography* 7.7.10; see [[hesiod]]). The sanctuary lay under Mount Tomarus; at first men gave the oracles, and later three old women, when Dione was made Zeus' partner in the temple (7.7.11–12; see [[dione]]).
