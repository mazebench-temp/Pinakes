Homer names Placus only in the story of Andromache's family, and always as “wooded Placus” (Πλάκῳ ὑληέσσῃ; see [[wooded]]). Her father Eetion “lived beneath wooded Placus”, and her mother “was queen beneath wooded Placus” (6.396, 6.425); in her lament for Hector she says that she was born at Thebe under wooded Placus (22.479). The city takes its name from the mountain: Θήβη Ὑποπλακίη, “Thebe under Placus” (6.397), which sets it apart from the other cities called Thebes (see [[thebe]] and [[thebes]]).

The poem says nothing more of the mountain. It was presumably one of the heights above the plain of Adramyttium, where Thebe lay (see [[cilicians]]); which one is not known. Sappho, telling of Hector's wedding, brings Andromache “from holy Thebe and ever-flowing Placia” (fr. 44; see [[sappho]]): in her song Placia is a stream or a spring.
