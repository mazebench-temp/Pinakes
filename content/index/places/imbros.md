## Descriptions

παιπαλόεσσα, “rocky” (13.33, 24.78), is the epithet of the mountain of Samothrace from which Poseidon comes down (13.17), and in the Odyssey of Ithaca, Samos and Chios (Od. 11.480, 4.671, 3.170). The Homeric Hymn to Apollo calls the island “well-built” (Ἴμβρος τʼ εὐκτιμένη, 3.36).

## In Homer

{{quote:9179eccb-7cb2-5395-8e7f-b92cc2883eb6}}

The cave lies “in the depths of the deep sea” (βαθείης βένθεσι λίμνης, 13.32), in the words used a few verses before of Poseidon's golden house at Aegae (13.21; see [[aegae]]). Tenedos lies off the Troad, south of the mouth of the Hellespont, and Imbros to the northwest, off the Thracian Chersonese, so that the cave is in the open sea before the Achaean camp (see [[tenedos]] and [[hellespont]]). The same waters hold Thetis: Iris, sent to fetch her, plunges into the sea between Samos and Imbros and finds her in a hollow cave among the goddesses of the sea (24.77–84; see [[thetis]]).

Elsewhere the poem names Imbros beside its neighbors. Hera and Sleep, wrapped in mist, leave Lemnos and Imbros behind and come to Ida at Lectum (14.281–284). Eetion of Imbros (Ἴμβριος Ἠετίων), a guest-friend, ransomed Priam's son Lycaon from Lemnos, where Achilles had sold him, and sent him to Arisbe (21.40–44; see [[lycaon-son-of-priam]] and [[arisbe]]); he is not Andromache's father (see [[eetion]]). And Hecuba laments that Achilles sold her other sons across the sea, to Samos, Imbros and Lemnos (24.751–753; see [[samothrace]] and [[lemnos]]).

The Trojan Imbrius, whom Teucer kills (13.170–181), had his home at Pedaeum before the war, but his name is the adjective Ἴμβριος, “of Imbros” (see [[imbrius]] and [[pedaeum]]).

## Outside Homer

Herodotus says that Lemnos and Imbros were still inhabited by Pelasgians when the Persian Otanes took them with ships from Lesbos (Herodotus 5.26; see [[pelasgians]]), and that Miltiades, pursued by the Phoenicians, escaped to Imbros with four ships and sailed from there to Athens (6.41.2–4). Strabo says that the Cabeiri were honored above all in Imbros and Lemnos (*Geography* 10.3.21). The island is the modern Gökçeada, in Turkey.
