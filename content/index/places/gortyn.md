τειχιόεσσαν, "walled" (see [[walled]]): Homer gives the same epithet to Tiryns (2.559). In the Odyssey, some of Menelaus' ships are wrecked on “a smooth rock, steep to the sea, / at the far edge of Gortyn” (*Odyssey* 3.293–294; see [[phaestus]]).

## Outside Homer

Strabo ranks Gortyn second only to Cnossus in power: the two together held the rest of the island subject, and when they quarreled they divided it. Gortyn lies on a plain; it may once have been walled, as Homer says, but later lost its wall down to the foundations, and stayed unwalled. It is ninety stadia from the Libyan sea at Lebena, its trading port, and has another port, Matalum, a hundred and thirty stadia away; the river Lethaeus flows through the whole city (*Geography* 10.4.11; see [[cnossus]]). Gortyn became the chief city of Crete, and under Rome the capital of the province of Crete and Cyrene. It is best known for the Gortyn law code, a long inscription of the fifth century BC cut into a stone wall, with rules on marriage, inheritance, property and slaves.
