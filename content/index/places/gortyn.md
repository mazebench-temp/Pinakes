τειχιόεσσαν, "walled" (see [[walled]]): Homer gives the same epithet to Tiryns (2.559). In the Odyssey, some of Menelaus' ships are wrecked on a sheer rock "at the edge of Gortyn" (3.293–294).

Gortyn became the chief city of Crete, and under Rome the capital of the province of Crete and Cyrene. It is best known for the Gortyn law code, a long inscription of the fifth century BC cut into a stone wall, with rules on marriage, inheritance, property and slaves.
