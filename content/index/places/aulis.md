Aulis is named twice in Book 2. After the army has been turned back from its rush to the ships, Odysseus reminds it of the day, "yesterday, it seems, or the day before", when the ships gathered at Aulis "bringing evils for Priam and the Trojans" (2.303–304). As they sacrificed at the altars around a spring, under a plane tree, a serpent darted from beneath an altar, ate eight sparrow chicks and their mother, and was turned to stone; Calchas read it as nine years of war and Troy taken in the tenth (2.305–330; see [[the-portent-at-aulis]]). Nestor adds the lightning Zeus flashed on the right as they embarked (2.350–353; see [[the-departure-from-aulis]]).

In the Catalogue it is "rocky Aulis" (Αὐλίδα πετρήεσσαν; [[rocky]]), a town of the [[Boeotians]] named beside [[Hyria]] (2.496). That the fleet gathered on Boeotian soil is the simplest explanation of why the Boeotians come first.

Homer says nothing of Iphigenia. The only sacrifices he sets at Aulis are the hecatombs offered under the plane tree (2.305–307).

## Outside Homer

Hesiod says the only time he went to sea was the crossing from Aulis to Euboea, "where once the Achaeans, waiting out the winter, gathered a great army from holy Greece to Troy of the fair women" (*Works and Days* 650–653). The *Cypria*, as summarized by [[proclus]], brought the fleet to Aulis twice. At the first gathering came the portent of the serpent and the sparrows. At the second, Agamemnon offended Artemis by killing a deer, the goddess sent storms that kept the fleet from sailing, and Iphigenia was brought to be sacrificed; Artemis snatched her away to the land of the Taurians and put a deer on the altar in her place. Aeschylus (*Agamemnon* 104–247) and Euripides (*Iphigenia at Aulis*) put the sacrifice at the center; see [[aeschylus-aulis]] and [[euripides-catalogue]].

In 396 BC the Spartan king Agesilaus, setting out for Asia, went to Aulis to sacrifice as Agamemnon had done, and the Boeotians broke up the rite (Xenophon, *Hellenica* 3.4.3–4). Pausanias saw a temple of Artemis there (9.19). The remains of a temple of Artemis have been excavated near the modern village of Avlida.
