## In Homer

{{quote:3dbdd621-4576-5d4a-8275-0b3cf5769186}}

Pieria is the first land Hera touches when she darts down from the peak of Olympus: “stepping on Pieria and lovely Emathia”, she speeds over the snowy mountains of the Thracians to Athos, and from Athos over the sea to Lemnos (14.225–230; see [[olympus]], [[emathia]], [[thracians]], [[athos]] and [[lemnos]]). Hermes takes the same road in the Odyssey, in nearly the same words: “Stepping on Pieria, he fell from the upper air upon the sea; / then he hurried over the wave like a bird, a gull” on his way to Calypso's island (Πιερίην δʼ ἐπιβὰς ἐξ αἰθέρος ἔμπεσε πόντῳ, *Odyssey* 5.50–51). For both, Pieria is the step between the gods' mountain and the sea.

## Outside Homer

The Homeric *Hymn to Apollo* begins the god's search for a seat for his oracle with the same step: “To Pieria first you came down from Olympus” (*Hymn to Apollo* 216), and in the *Hymn to Hermes* the immortal cattle of the gods have their stalls in “the shadowy mountains of Pieria”, from which Hermes drives off fifty of them (*Hymn to Hermes* 70–74; see [[hermes]]). Pieria was the home of the Muses. Hesiod says that Mnemosyne bore them in Pieria to the son of Cronus (*Theogony* 53–54), and calls on them as “Muses from Pieria” at the start of the *Works and Days* (1; see [[hesiod]] and [[muses]]). Strabo explains that Pieria, Olympus, Pimpla and Leibethrum were once Thracian places, held in his day by the Macedonians, and that the Thracians who settled Boeotia dedicated Helicon to the Muses (*Geography* 10.3.17); the Pieres, a Thracian people, held Pieria and the country around Olympus until the Argeadae, the Macedonian kings, became masters of it (7, fr. 11). He gives the name Pieria to the coast north of the Haliacmon, where Pydna lay (7, fr. 22; see [[strabo]]). Xerxes' army waited many days in Pieria while a third of it cleared a road over the mountains into Perrhaebia (Herodotus 7.131; see [[herodotus]]).
