δῖα, "shining" ([[brilliant]]). Asius' horses brought him "from Arisbe, from the river Selleis" ([[selleis-arisbe]]). Arisbe was also the home of Axylus son of Teuthras, "a friend to men", who kept open house by the road and whom Diomedes kills in Book 6 (6.12–19). Lycaon, a son of Priam whom Achilles had sold to Lemnos, was ransomed by Eetion of Imbros and sent to "shining Arisbe", and made his way home from there (21.40–44). Later mythographers give the name to Priam's first wife, the daughter of Merops (see [[hyrtacus]]).
