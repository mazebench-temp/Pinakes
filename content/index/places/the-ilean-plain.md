## The name

πεδίον Ἰλήϊον, “the Ilean plain”, is found only in Agenor's debate with his own heart, as he waits alone for Achilles before the city (see [[agenor]] and [[pondering-two-ways]]). The adjective has the form of those in -ήϊος made from nouns in -εύς, as βασιλήϊος (τέμενος βασιλήϊον, “a king's domain”, 18.550) is made from βασιλεύς, and Walter Leaf takes it for the adjective of an unknown name, Ἰλεύς (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 21.558). It is commonly referred to Ilus or to Ilios. The B scholia explain it as the plain by the tomb of Ilus, the mound in the middle of the plain (10.415, 11.166–167; see [[the-tomb-of-ilus]] and [[ilus]]); others make it the plain of Ilios (see [[troy]]); Crates read Ἰδήϊον, “of Ida” (Leaf on 21.558; see the translation's note on 21.558).

Leaf finds none of these possible. The form will not come from Ilus or Ilios, and no plain at Troy appears in the poems, he observes, but the one between the city and the ships, the Trojan plain (ἐς πεδίον τὸ Τρωϊκόν, 10.11), also called the plain of the Scamander (ἐς πεδίον … Σκαμάνδριον, 2.465). The adjective of Ida is Ἰδαῖος, and there is no plain near Troy that could be called the plain of Ida, which is, he says, twenty miles away across hilly country. He concludes that the verse is corrupt, and prefers above all the emendation of van Leeuwen and da Costa, φεύγω πρὸς πεδίον λεῖον, ὄφρʼ ἄγκεʼ ἵκωμαι, “flee toward the level plain, until I reach the glades”: ἄγκεʼ, “glades”, was misread as ἄν κε, and the meter was mended by turning λεῖον into Ἰλήϊον (on 21.558). The level plain is the ground on which Achilles marks out the turning-post far off for the chariot race (ἐν λείῳ πεδίῳ, 23.359). Leaf takes the glades for the natural refuge of a fugitive, and the two words, glades and spurs, stand together in the Odyssey, where a deer that has laid her fawns in a lion's lair goes grazing over the spurs and the grassy glades (κνημοὺς ἐξερέῃσι καὶ ἄγκεα ποιήεντα, *Odyssey* 4.335–338), and in a simile of the next book a hound chases a fawn through the glades and the valleys (22.189–190). With the emendation, the τε after Ἴδης in the next verse has something to join: the glades, and the spurs of Ida (Leaf on 21.558). Leaf's Agenor would turn aside from the slope below the city into the plain of the Scamander southwest of it and follow it up to the foothills of Ida.

## In the poem

Agenor's plan is to let the others be driven in confusion by Achilles while he runs the other way, away from the wall and over the plain, to hide in the brushwood on Ida's slopes until evening (21.556–561; see [[ida]]). He rejects it: Achilles would see him leaving the city “toward the plain” and catch him with his swift feet (πεδίον δέ, 21.563–564). The flight he imagines is the one Apollo makes in his likeness, over “the wheat-bearing plain” and along the Scamander, with Achilles behind him (21.602–603; see [[apollo-deceives-achilles]]).
