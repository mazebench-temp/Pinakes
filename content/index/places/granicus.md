Γρήνικος is the Ionic form of the name; later Greek has Γράνικος. It is named nowhere else in Homer. With the Aesepus it begins the second verse of the list of rivers that bring the Achaean wall to nothing (12.21; see [[aesepus]] and [[the-achaean-wall]]).

## Outside Homer

Hesiod has the same pair in the same order: “the Granicus and Aesepus and divine Simoeis” (Γρήνικόν τε καὶ Αἴσηπον θεῖόν τε Σιμοῦντα, *Theogony* 342; see [[hesiod]]). [[strabo|Strabo]], reporting Demetrius of Scepsis, says that it rises on Cotylus, a height of Ida, close to the springs of the Scamander and the Aesepus, and runs north to the Propontis (13.1.43). It flows between the Aesepus and Priapus, mostly through the plain of Adrasteia, and on that plain, Strabo says, Alexander defeated the satraps of Darius (13.1.11). The river is the modern Biga Çayı.

An ancient note on the line names it as the river where Alexander defeated the satraps of Darius (scholium T on 12.21).
