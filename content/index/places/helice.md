εὐρεῖα "broad". Helice lay on the coastal plain of Achaea, east of Aegium, and closes the list of [[Agamemnon]]'s towns.

## Elsewhere in Homer

Helice was a center of the cult of Poseidon. Hera reminds him that the Achaeans bring him gifts at Helice and Aegae, “many and pleasing” (8.203–204), and the dying Hippodamas bellows “as when a bull / bellows as he is dragged around the Heliconian lord / by young men dragging him” (20.403–405; see [[the-bull-of-the-heliconian-lord]]). The god's title Ἑλικώνιος is usually taken to come from the town (see [[heliconian-poseidon]] and [[helice-or-the-panionia]]).

## Outside Homer

In 373 BC an earthquake and a wave from the sea destroyed Helice in a single night and covered the site (Strabo 8.7.2; Pausanias 7.24.6). The Ionians' common sanctuary at Mycale was dedicated to Poseidon Heliconius (Herodotus 1.148). The remains of the town lie buried in the coastal plain, where excavations have been searching for them since the 1990s.
