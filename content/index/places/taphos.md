## Outside Homer

Strabo says that the islands of the Taphians, formerly of the Teleboans, among them Taphos, “now called Taphias”, lay near the Echinades off Acarnania but under other leaders, the Taphians and the Teleboans (*Geography* 10.2.20; see [[echinae]]). He rejects the view of those who made Taphos the same as Cephallenia and the Cephallenians Taphians and Teleboans, since in Homer the Cephallenians are subjects of Odysseus and Laertes, and Taphos is Mentes' (10.2.14; see [[cephallenians]] and [[samos]]). Merry places the islands of the Taphians off the coast of Leucas, and takes the largest of them, Meganisi, for Taphos (*Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on *Odyssey* 1.105).
