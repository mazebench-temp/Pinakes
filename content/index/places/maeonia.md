In Book 3 Helen asks Aphrodite whether she will lead her on to one of the well-peopled cities "of Phrygia or of lovely Maeonia" (Μῃονίης ἐρατεινῆς; see [[lovely]]), if the goddess has a favorite there too (3.400–402). The two inland countries stand for the rich kingdoms beyond Troy. Hector names the same pair when he says that Troy's treasures have been sold off "to Phrygia and lovely Maeonia" (18.291–292; see [[phrygia]]). The people and their land under Tmolus are described under [[maeonians]].
