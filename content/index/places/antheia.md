Antheia follows “holy Pherae” in the list (9.151; see [[the-seven-cities]] and [[pherae-in-messenia]]). Its epithet βαθύλειμος, “of the deep meadows” (βαθύς “deep” + λειμών “meadow”; see [[of-the-deep-meadows]]), suits a name that recalls ἄνθος, “flower”.

## Outside Homer

Strabo reports that some identified Antheia with Thuria, and others with Asine, “of all the Messenian cities the most fitly called of the deep meadows”, with Corone beside it on the sea (Strabo 8.4.5; see [[strabo]]). Pausanias was told that the city of the Thuriatae, eighty stades inland from Pharae, had been called Antheia in Homer's poems; Augustus had given Thuria to Sparta, and its people had moved down from the old town on the height to the plain, leaving the ruins of a wall above (4.31.1–2). Strabo himself gives Thuria to Aepeia (see [[aepeia]]).
