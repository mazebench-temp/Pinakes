ὄρος αἰπύ "the steep mountain" ([[steep]]). Cyllene, the modern Kyllini, is the second-highest mountain of the Peloponnese, rising to about 2,370 meters over northeastern Arcadia. The Catalogue uses it to mark where the [[Arcadians]] live.

## Elsewhere in Homer

Hermes is "Cyllenian" (Κυλλήνιος) when he leads the souls of the suitors to the underworld (Od. 24.1). Otus "of Cyllene", a leader of the Epeians killed by Polydamas (15.518–519), came, as Strabo argues, from Cyllene, the harbor town of Elis, since Homer would not make a man from the Arcadian mountain a leader of the Epeians (*Geography* 8.3.4; see [[otus-of-cyllene]] and [[cyllene-elis]]).

## Outside Homer

The Homeric Hymn to Hermes begins with his birth to Maia in a cave on Cyllene (1–7).
