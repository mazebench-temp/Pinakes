Homer's form of the name is Καΰστριος (Kaüstrios); later Greek has Κάϋστρος (Kaÿstros). It is the modern Küçük Menderes (“Little Maeander”), which flows west through Lydia to the sea at Ephesus.

{{quote:5d15a4b5-97df-5737-bac4-42c23c749d89}}

The Achaeans pour out onto the plain like the flocks that settle, with loud cries, on the [[asian-meadow|Asian meadow]] by the streams of the Cayster. See [[birds-on-the-asian-meadow]], [[geese]], [[cranes]] and [[swans]].

## Outside Homer

Virgil remembers the simile when he describes the birds that search “the Asian meadows around the sweet pools of the Cayster” (*Georgics* 1.383–384).
