Chalcis stands where [[Euboea]] comes nearest to the mainland, across the Euripus from [[Aulis]]. Later Greeks connected its name with χαλκός, "bronze". Another Chalcis, "by the sea", belongs to the Aetolians (2.640; [[chalcis-aetolia]]), and the Odyssey has a third, “fair-flowing Chalcis”, a stream on the coast of Elis that Telemachus passes on his way home from Pylos (*Odyssey* 15.295; [[chalcis-in-elis]]). Strabo names the three together (*Geography* 10.1.9).

## Outside Homer

With Eretria it led the Greek settlements of the eighth century BC in the west and the north: Pithecusae and Cumae in Italy, Naxos and other towns in Sicily, and so many towns in the north Aegean that the peninsula there was called Chalcidice. Hesiod won his tripod at the funeral games of Amphidamas at Chalcis (*Works and Days* 654–657).
