Pedasus closes the list of seven towns “near the sea, on the borders of sandy Pylos” (9.152–153; see [[the-seven-cities]]). ἀμπελόεις, “vine-rich”, is its epithet in both lists (9.152, 9.294), as it is of Epidaurus in the Catalogue and of Phrygia in Priam's memory (2.561, 3.184; see [[vine-rich]]).

The Iliad has other bearers of the name: the steep town on the Satnioeis in the Troad, home of Elatus and sacked by Achilles (6.35, 20.92; see [[pedasus-on-the-satnioeis]]), Pedasus son of Bucolion, a Trojan killed by Euryalus (6.21; see [[pedasus-son-of-bucolion]]), and Achilles' trace horse (16.152).

## Outside Homer

Strabo writes that Methone is said to be the town the poet calls Pedasus, one of the seven Agamemnon promised to Achilles (8.4.3); some, he adds, gave the name to Corone instead (8.4.5; see [[strabo]]). Pausanias was told the same of Mothone: it was called Pedasus before the army gathered for Troy and during the war, and its people said it was renamed after Mothone, a daughter of Oeneus by a concubine, though Pausanias thought the name came from the rock Mothon that forms the harbor (4.35.1). Methoni, at the southwestern tip of the Peloponnese, keeps the name.
