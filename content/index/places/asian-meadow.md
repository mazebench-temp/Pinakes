The text printed here, Ἀσίω, has been taken in two ways. Read as a genitive, it is “the meadow of Asias”, a local hero. Written Ἀσίῳ, it is an adjective with λειμῶνι, “in the Asian meadow”, which the translation follows. Either way the meadow lay along the lower [[Cayster]], near Ephesus. See [[birds-on-the-asian-meadow]].

The name is usually connected with Assuwa, a league of western Anatolian lands named in Hittite texts of the fifteenth century BCE, and with Asia, which Greeks first used of this region and then of the whole continent. The derivation is widely accepted but not proved.

## Outside Homer

Herodotus reports that the Lydians derived the name Asia from Asies, a son of Cotys and grandson of Manes, after whom a tribe at Sardis was called Asias (4.45).
