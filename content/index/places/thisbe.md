πολυτρήρων "of the many doves" ([[of-many-doves]]) = πολύς (many) + τρήρων (timid, the word for the rock dove). The same epithet is given to Messe in Lacedaemon (2.582). Thisbe lay in southwestern Boeotia, between Helicon and the Gulf of Corinth, where the modern village of Thisvi keeps its name. The Thisbe of Ovid's tale of Pyramus and Thisbe is a Babylonian girl and has nothing to do with it.
