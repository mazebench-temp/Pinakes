Ἰαρδάνου ἀμφὶ ῥέεθρα, “about the streams of the Iardanus” (7.135), is the last of the three landmarks by which Nestor places the battle of his youth, after the Celadon and the walls of Pheia (see [[celadon]], [[pheia]] and [[nestor-and-ereuthalion]]). The same half-verse names a river of Crete in the Odyssey, where the Cydonians lived and where some of Menelaus' ships were driven on their way home (Od. 3.291–292). The name has been compared with that of the Jordan (Hebrew *Yarden*), but the connection is uncertain.

## Outside Homer

[[strabo|Strabo]] applies Nestor's verse to a small stream near cape Pheia (8.3.12). He also reports a meadow and a tomb of Iardanus, shown near the Anigrus in Triphylia (8.3.20), and a reading that moved the battle to Chaa near Lepreum, on the river Acidon, beside “the tomb of Iardanus” (8.3.21; see [[celadon]]). Pausanias heard from an Ephesian that the Acidas, a stream that joins the Anigrus, was once called the Iardanus, but found nothing to support it (5.5.9). He also names Pheia and the Iardanus in an ancient reading of a battle scene on the Chest of Cypselus (5.18.6).
