## The name

Ἀπείρη is known only from the two words of *Odyssey* 7 that name it: Ἀπειραίη, an adjective, “from Apeire”, and Ἀπείρηθεν, “from Apeire”, with the ending -θεν of motion from a place. The scholia, as Merry reports them, explain Ἀπειραίη as “come from far away” or “of the mainland” (ἄποθεν παροῦσα, ἢ Ἠπειρωτική), and the Suda gives the same two glosses (α 3130). Merry himself takes the word, like Hypereia, the old home of the Phaeacians, for a purely mythical name, a land “of which the limits are unknown” (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on *Odyssey* 7.8; see [[hypereia-of-the-phaeacians]]).

## In Homer

Nausicaa's old chamber-woman Eurymedusa came from there: the curving ships had once brought her from Apeire, and she had been picked out as a prize for Alcinous (*Odyssey* 7.8–11; see [[eurymedusa]], [[alcinous]] and [[geras]]). Merry takes her for one of a number of captives (on *Odyssey* 7.10); the poem does not say whose ships brought her, or how she was taken.
