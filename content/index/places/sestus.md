Sestus is the only place across the strait in Asius' realm: the Hellespont here joins more than it divides. The town stood on the Chersonese at the narrowest crossing, facing Abydos. Xerxes' bridges reached the European shore between Sestus and Madytus (Herodotus 7.33), and the Athenian siege of Sestus in 479–478 BC is the last story Herodotus tells (9.114–121). The poet Musaeus tells of Hero, priestess of Aphrodite at Sestus, and Leander of Abydos, who swam to her every night until he drowned in a storm; Ovid wrote their letters (*Heroides* 18–19).
