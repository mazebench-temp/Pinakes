Amyclae lay in the Eurotas valley a few miles south of Sparta. It belongs to Menelaus' [[Lacedaemon]].

## Outside Homer

Its sanctuary, the Amyclaeum, held a colossal archaic image of Apollo on a throne carved by Bathycles of Magnesia, described at length by Pausanias (3.18–19), and the grave of Hyacinthus, the youth Apollo loved and killed by accident. The Spartans celebrated the Hyacinthia there every summer; in 479 BC the festival delayed their march against the Persians (Herodotus 9.7). Offerings at the sanctuary go back to the Mycenaean period.
