## The name

Ἀρέθουσα is the name of several springs in the Greek world. Homer has it only here, of a spring on Ithaca, κρήνῃ Ἀρεθούσῃ, “the spring Arethusa” (*Odyssey* 13.408). The best known was the spring on Ortygia, the island of Syracuse, which the story made the outlet of the river Alpheus, flowing under the sea from the Peloponnese (Strabo, *Geography* 6.2.4; see [[ortygia]] and [[alpheus]]); the Suda describes it as a spring in Sicily into which the Alpheus falls, “as if in love with such a spring” (Suda α 3821). Another was at Chalcis in Euboea, whose springs, Strabo says, were stopped up by an earthquake and broke out many days later through another mouth (*Geography* 1.3.16), and an oracle that he quotes praises “the men who drink the water of sacred Arethusa”, meaning the Chalcidians (*Geography* 10.1.13; see [[strabo]] and [[chalcis]]).

## In Homer

Athena sends Odysseus first to his swineherd, whom he will find beside his swine, “by the Rock of Corax and at the spring Arethusa, / eating acorns to satisfy their hearts and the dark water / drinking” (*Odyssey* 13.408–410; see [[eumaeus]], [[the-rock-of-corax]] and [[boar]]). The spring is not named again.

## Outside Homer

Eustathius, explaining the verse, tells that the spring and the rock were named from a mother and her son: Corax, the son of a local woman named Arethusa, was carried over a cliff while hunting hares on Ithaca and died, and his mother, coming to the spring in her grief, died there. He adds that springs of the name were also said to be at Smyrna, at Chalcis in Euboea and at Syracuse, the last the most famous (Eustathius' note is among the witnesses printed with Aristotle fr. 489 Gigon; paraphrased from an English translation). Sir William Gell, identifying the places of the poem on the modern island in 1807, found that “the fountain Arethusa was still flowing” (W. W. Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, Appendix on Ithaca, p. 559).
