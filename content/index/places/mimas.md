## Descriptions

ἠνεμόεις, “windy” (see [[windy]]), is the epithet that the Iliad gives to Ilios (3.305 and often) and to the Arcadian town of Enispe (2.606). The *Hymn to Apollo* calls the mountain παιπαλόεις, “rocky”, the word the Odyssey uses of Chios in the verse before (*Hymn to Apollo* 39; *Odyssey* 3.170; see [[chios]]). Merry describes Mimas as a mountain chain that runs through the peninsula from north to south (*Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on *Odyssey* 3.169).

## Outside Homer

In the *Hymn to Apollo* Mimas is among the lands that Leto came to in her labor, looking for a home for her son, none of which dared to receive him; it is named after Lesbos and Chios, “and rugged Mimas, and the high peaks of Corycus” (*Hymn to Apollo* 37–48; see [[lesbos]]). Strabo, going along the Ionian coast, places it between Erythrae and Hypocremnus: “Mimas is a high mountain, full of game and of trees” (*Geography* 14.1.33). He also tells of the Erythraeans “who live on Mimas”, who worshipped Heracles as the killer of the worms that ate their vines (*Geography* 13.1.64).
