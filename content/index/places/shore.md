## The words

θίς is the strand at the water's edge, most often in the phrases παρὰ θῖνα θαλάσσης, “along the shore of the sea”, and θῖνʼ ἔφʼ ἁλὸς πολιῆς, “on the shore of the gray sea”. ἠϊών is the beach as a whole, “the long mouth of the whole shore” that the Achaean ships fill from headland to headland (14.35–36), and ἀκτή the shore where the land meets the sea (20.50, 23.125).

## In Homer

The beach is the edge of the camp. The ships are drawn up on it “on the shore of the gray sea” (13.682, 14.31), so many that they had to be hauled up in rows (14.33–36; see [[ships]]), and the army marches along it to the assembly (2.92; see [[assembly]]). It is also where men go to be alone. In *Iliad* 1 Chryses, sent away by Agamemnon, walks in silence along the shore and prays to Apollo, and Achilles, when Briseis has been taken, sits apart on the shore of the gray sea and calls on his mother (1.34–36, 1.348–351). After Patroclus' death Achilles walks the same shore to call the army to assembly (19.40–41), and sleeps on it among the Myrmidons on the night the soul of Patroclus comes to him (23.59–65).
