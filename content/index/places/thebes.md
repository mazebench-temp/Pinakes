## Name

Homer calls the city both Θήβη (4.378, 4.406) and Θῆβαι (5.804, 6.223). The name has no Greek etymology. Two other cities bear it: [[thebe|Thebe]] under Placus in the Troad, the city of Eetion that Achilles sacked (1.366), and Egyptian Thebes, a city of a hundred gates, where “the most treasures lie stored in the houses” (9.381–384); the Odyssey says the same of it (Od. 4.126–127). Boeotian Thebes is “seven-gated” (ἑπτάπυλος, 4.406; Od. 11.263; see [[seven-gated]]).

## In Homer

The Iliad knows Thebes from the two wars fought against it before Troy. Tydeus came to Mycenae with Polynices to raise allies when “they were then making war against the sacred walls of Thebes”, went ahead as envoy, beat the [[Cadmeians]] at their feast, and killed the fifty who lay in wait for him (4.376–398; 5.800–808; 10.284–290). The attackers perished “through their own recklessness”, and Tydeus lies buried there (4.409; 14.114; see [[tydeus]] and [[capaneus]]). In the next generation their sons took the city with a smaller army “under a stronger wall”, trusting in the portents of the gods (4.406–408; see [[sthenelus]] and [[diomedes]]). See [[the-seven-against-thebes]], [[the-epigonoi]] and [[tydeus-at-thebes]].

The poems know other Theban stories. Zeus loved Alcmene and Semele there, and Heracles was born there (14.323–325, 19.98–99); Oedipus died there and had funeral games (23.679–680). The Odyssey adds that Amphion and Zethus, sons of Zeus and Antiope, founded Thebes and walled it (Od. 11.260–265); that Oedipus ruled the Cadmeians there after he had married his mother unknowing (Od. 11.271–280); that Amphiaraus died at Thebes “because of a woman's gifts” (Od. 15.247); and that the seer Teiresias was a Theban (Od. 10.492).

The Catalogue of Ships does not name Thebes. Its list of Boeotian towns has only Hypothebae (2.505), whose name means “Lower Thebes” (see [[hypothebae]] and [[boeotians]]), and ancient readers explained the silence by the sack of the city in the war of the sons.

## Outside Homer

Hesiod names the two wars of the heroes together. Some died “at seven-gated Thebes, in the land of Cadmus”, fighting for the flocks of Oedipus, and others at Troy, for the sake of Helen of the lovely hair (*Works and Days* 161–165). The Theban wars were the subject of lost epics, the *Thebaid* and the *Epigonoi*, and of many tragedies. Excavations under the modern town have found the Mycenaean palace on the citadel, the Cadmea, and Linear B tablets.
