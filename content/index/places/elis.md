In Homer Elis is a land, not a city. The Catalogue names [[Buprasium]] and "shining Elis" (Ἤλιδα δῖαν; [[brilliant]]), and marks out its extent by four points, "as much of it as [[Hyrmine]] and [[Myrsinus]] at the border and the [[olenian-rock|Olenian Rock]] and [[Alesium]] enclose within them" (2.615–617). Its people are the [[Epeians]], and they come in forty ships under four leaders, ten ships to each (2.618–624). The islands of [[Dulichium]] and the [[Echinae]] lie "across the sea, opposite Elis" (2.626).

## Elsewhere in Homer

Nestor's longest tale of his youth is a war between Pylos and Elis. He drove off cattle from Elis in a raid, the Epeians came south to besiege Thryoessa on the Alpheus, and the Pylians drove them back as far as Buprasium (11.670–761). In that tale the people are called both Epeians and Eleans (11.671, 11.688). Their king then was [[Augeas]] (11.701). In the Odyssey Elis is "where the Epeians rule" (Od. 13.275, 15.298), and it is the land where Noemon keeps his horses (Od. 4.634–637).

## Outside Homer

Olympia and its games lay in Elean territory, and the Eleans presided over them. The city of Elis was formed by a union of villages in 471 BC.
