Σκάμανδρος (Skamandros). The name is probably not Greek, and its origin is unknown. In Book 2 only its adjective appears, Σκαμάνδριος (“of the Scamander”), for the plain and the meadow where the Achaean army forms up.

{{quote:e12e0045-9aef-572a-916e-cfa1a84266a3}}

The Achaeans pour out of the camp “onto the plain of the Scamander” and stand “numberless” in its flowering meadow. The simile of the birds on the [[asian-meadow|Asian meadow]] (2.459–465) turns into the real meadow by the river of Troy. See [[birds-on-the-asian-meadow]] and [[leaves-and-flowers]].

## Elsewhere in Homer

The river has two names: “the gods call it Xanthus, and men Scamander” (20.74; see [[language-of-gods-and-men]]). It is a god with its own priest (5.77–78), and in Book 21 it rises against Achilles and nearly drowns him (21.211–382). Hector calls his son Scamandrius after the river, though the Trojans call the boy Astyanax (6.402–403). The Xanthus of Lycia is a different river (2.877; see [[xanthus-of-lycia]]).

## Outside Homer

The river is the modern Karamenderes, which crosses the plain west of Hisarlık to reach the sea near the mouth of the Dardanelles.
