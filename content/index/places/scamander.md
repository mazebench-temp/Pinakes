Σκάμανδρος (Skamandros). The name is probably not Greek, and its origin is unknown. The river has two names: “the gods call it Xanthus, and men Scamander” (20.74; see [[language-of-gods-and-men]]). Its adjective Σκαμάνδριος (“of the Scamander”) names the plain and the meadow where the Achaean army forms up, and men are named after it: the hunter Scamandrius (see [[scamandrius]]) and Hector's son Astyanax, whom Hector calls Scamandrius though the Trojans call him Astyanax (6.402–403).

The Scamander is a god as well as a river. It has a priest and cult among the Trojans, who sacrifice bulls to it and throw live horses into its eddies (21.130–132), and in Book 21 it rises against Achilles and nearly drowns him (21.211–382). The Simoeis joins it on the plain (see [[simoeis]]). The Xanthus of Lycia is a different river (2.877; see [[xanthus-of-lycia]]).

## Outside Homer

The river is the modern Karamenderes, which crosses the plain west of Hisarlık to reach the sea near the mouth of the Dardanelles.
