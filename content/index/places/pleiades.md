## In Homer

{{quote:28b36646-3452-5c0b-bcd0-801e7ec51533}}

The Pleiades are named twice in Homer, each time at the head of a short list of stars. On the Shield they come first of the constellations with which the sky is crowned (18.485–486; see [[sky]]). In the Odyssey Odysseus, sailing on his raft from Calypso's island, never lets sleep fall on his eyelids, watching the Pleiades and Boötes, which sets late, and the Bear, which Calypso told him to keep on his left hand (*Odyssey* 5.270–277; see [[the-bear]]). The lines on the Bear are the same in both poems.

The name is unexplained. Walter Leaf reports the usual explanation, “the sailing stars”, since their rising in May marks the time when voyages become safe, but thinks it possible that they are “the flight of doves”, as if πελειάδες, fleeing like the Bear before the hunter Orion; this, he adds, may be a later interpretation, and the name may rather be connected with πλεῖος, “full”, as if it meant “the crowd” (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 18.486; see [[orion]]). Doves are πέλειαι and πελειάδες in Homer (11.634; see [[doves]]).

## Outside Homer

Hesiod's farmer works by them. “When the Pleiades, born of Atlas, are rising, begin the harvest; the plowing, when they are setting”; they are hidden for forty days and nights, and appear again as the year comes round, when the iron is first sharpened (*Works and Days* 383–387). Their setting with the Hyades and Orion is the time to plow (614–617), and when they flee the mighty strength of Orion and fall into the misty sea, the winds rage and ships must stay on land (618–623). The verse of the Shield is almost Hesiod's: Πληϊάδας θʼ Ὑάδας τε τό τε σθένος Ὠρίωνος (18.486), Πληιάδες θʼ Ὑάδες τε τό τε σθένος Ὠαρίωνος (*Works and Days* 615). The *Astronomy* ascribed to Hesiod, as later writers quote it, calls them Πελειάδες, “Doves”, and names the seven, Taygete, Electra, Alcyone, Asterope, Celaeno, Maia and Merope, whom “glorious Atlas begot” (*Astronomy* fr. 1 Evelyn-White).

Asclepiades of Myrlea, as Athenaeus reports him, read the doves on Nestor's cup as the Pleiades, and gathered poets who called them doves (see [[athenaeus-nestors-cup]] and [[the-cup-of-nestor]]).
