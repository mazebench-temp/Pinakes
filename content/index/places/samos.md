The Cephallenians "lived around Samos" (ἀμφενέμοντο). This is the large island west of Ithaca, later called Cephallenia, whose name preserves that of the [[Cephallenians]].

{{notes}}

## Elsewhere in Homer

In the Odyssey it is Same (Σάμη) as well as Samos. Penelope's suitors lie in wait for Telemachus "in the strait between Ithaca and rugged Samos" (*Odyssey* 4.671, 4.845), and twenty-four of the suitors come from Same (*Odyssey* 16.249).

## Outside Homer

A city called Same stood on the east coast of Cephallenia, facing Ithaca. Strabo says that the island had four cities, and that one of them was called both Samos and Same, sharing its name with the island; Homer, he argues, means the island when he names it with Dulichium and Zacynthus (*Geography* 10.2.10), and in his own day the city was gone, though traces of it were shown in the middle of the strait toward Ithaca (10.2.13). The island is not Samos off the coast of Ionia, nor the "Thracian Samos" of the Iliad, which is [[samothrace|Samothrace]] (13.12–13).
