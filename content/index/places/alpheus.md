In the Catalogue the Alpheus is named only for its ford at [[Thryum]], on the northern border of Nestor's kingdom.

{{notes}}

## Elsewhere in Homer

The river is also a god. In Nestor's tale of his youth the Pylians camp by the Alpheus, and Nestor sacrifices a bull to the river and another to Poseidon (11.712, 11.726–728).

## Outside Homer

Olympia lay on its banks. In legend Heracles turned the Alpheus and the Peneius through the stables of Augeas to clean them in a single day (Apollodorus 2.5.5). The river god loved the nymph Arethusa and followed her under the sea to Syracuse, where she rose as a spring (Ovid, *Metamorphoses* 5.572–641; Pindar, *Nemean* 1.1).
