ἀφνειός "wealthy" ([[wealthy]]). Thucydides explains the epithet: the Corinthians had always had a trading place on the Isthmus, through which the Greeks inside and outside the Peloponnese passed, and so "the old poets" called the city wealthy (1.13.5). Corinth belongs to [[Agamemnon]]'s realm (2.569–580).

## Elsewhere in Homer

Euchenor, son of the seer Polyidus, "lived at Corinth"; knowing that he must die either of illness at home or at Troy, he chose Troy, and Paris killed him (13.663–672). The Ephyra of Bellerophon, "in a corner of horse-pasturing Argos" (6.152), was later identified with Corinth, which claimed Bellerophon as its hero (Pindar, *Olympian* 13).

## Outside Homer

Corinth was ruled by the Bacchiad clan and then by the tyrants Cypselus and Periander; it founded Syracuse and Corcyra and held the Isthmian Games. In the seventh century BC its painted pottery was carried all over the Mediterranean, and a paved slipway, the diolkos, let ships be hauled across the Isthmus. The city lay below the citadel of Acrocorinth, with its temple of Apollo of the sixth century BC in the lower town. The Romans sacked it in 146 BC, and Julius Caesar refounded it as a Roman colony in 44 BC. Paul wrote two of his letters to its church.
