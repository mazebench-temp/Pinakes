## In Homer

The poem describes the island once, at the end of *Odyssey* 4, as the suitors' ship sets out: “a rocky island in the middle of the sea, / between Ithaca and rocky Samos, / Asteris, not large”, with “harbors in it where ships can lie, / with an entrance on either side” (*Odyssey* 4.844–847; see [[ithaca]], [[samos]] and [[the-suitors]]). The ambush is laid “in the strait between Ithaca and rocky Samos” (*Odyssey* 4.671, 15.29). Athena warns Telemachus of it and bids him keep his ship far from the islands and sail by night (*Odyssey* 15.27–35), and he escapes it; Antinous later tells how their lookouts sat by day on the windy heights and their ship waited at sea through the nights (*Odyssey* 16.364–370, plain renderings; see [[telemachus]] and [[antinous]]).

## Outside Homer

Strabo calls it Asteria and places it between Ithaca and Cephallenia. Demetrius of Scepsis, he reports, said that it no longer was what the poet describes, with its harbors, but Apollodorus that it still was, and that it had a small town, Alalcomenae, on its very isthmus (*Geography* 10.2.16; see [[strabo]]). Elsewhere he counts it among the places that have changed: “Asteria too has altered, which the poet calls Asteris … but now it has not even a good anchorage” (*Geography* 1.3.18). Merry rejects the view of modern geographers that it is the rock of Daskalio, and refers to his appendix on Ithaca (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on *Odyssey* 4.846; see the translation's note on *Odyssey* 4.846).
