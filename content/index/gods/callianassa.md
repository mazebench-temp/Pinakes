## Etymology

The name joins καλλι-, “beautiful”, and ἄνασσα, “lady, queen”, the feminine of ἄναξ, “lord”, which Homer uses of Demeter, “Demeter of the lovely tresses, the queen” (14.326), and when Odysseus addresses Nausicaa (*Odyssey* 6.149): “the beautiful queen”. Ianassa, in the next verse, has the same ending (18.47; see [[ianassa]]).

## In Homer

She is named once in Homer, in the catalogue of the Nereids who gather around Thetis in the depths of the sea when Thetis hears Achilles groan for Patroclus (18.37–49; see [[nereids]]).

## Outside Homer

Neither Hesiod nor Apollodorus names a Nereid Callianassa; both have a Lysianassa (Λυσιάνασσα, *Theogony* 258; *Library* 1.2.7).
