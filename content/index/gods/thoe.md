## Etymology

Θόη is the feminine of θοός, “swift”, the epithet of ships and of the night: θοῇ παρὰ νηῒ μελαίνῃ, “beside my swift black ship” (1.300), θοὴν διὰ νύκτα μέλαιναν, “through the swift black night” (10.394; see [[swift-ships]]). The same word ends the names of Cymothoe and Amphithoe in the next two verses (18.41–42; see [[cymothoe]] and [[amphithoe]]).

## In Homer

She is named once in Homer, in the catalogue of the Nereids who gather around Thetis in the depths of the sea when Thetis hears Achilles groan for Patroclus (18.37–49; see [[nereids]]).

## Outside Homer

Hesiod names Thoe among the daughters of Nereus and Doris, between Speio and Halie, as the Iliad does (*Theogony* 245; see [[speio]] and [[halia]]). Apollodorus does not list her, though he has a Nausithoe and a Hippothoe (*Library* 1.2.7).
