## Etymology

The name seems to join πᾶν, “all”, and ὀπ-, “see” (ὄψομαι, “I shall see”): “she who sees all”. Panopeus (Πανοπεύς), the town in Phocis, is a different name (2.520; see [[panopeus]]).

## In Homer

She is named once in Homer, in the catalogue of the Nereids who gather around Thetis in the depths of the sea when Thetis hears Achilles groan for Patroclus (18.37–49; see [[nereids]]).

## Outside Homer

Hesiod has a Panopeia (Πανόπεια) in the verse that matches 18.45 (*Theogony* 250; see [[doris-the-nereid|Doris]] and [[galateia]]), and Apollodorus a Panope (*Library* 1.2.7). Virgil's *Panopea virgo*, the maiden Panopea, rides with Thetis and Melite on the left of Neptune's chariot (*Aeneid* 5.825), and hears, with the chorus of the Nereids and of Phorcus, the prayer of Cloanthus in the boat race (*Aeneid* 5.240; see [[melite]]).
