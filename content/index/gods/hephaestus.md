## Etymology

Ἥφαιστος (Hēphaistos). The name has no accepted Greek etymology and may be pre-Greek. It could stand for fire itself: in Iliad 2.426 the flesh of a sacrifice is roasted "over Hephaestus", that is, over the fire.

## Family

{{quote:36fe9441-9dad-57e7-a5ef-66e9c650a197}}

Son of [[Hera]]. He takes her side, and her smile is his reward (1.595–596).

{{quote:19887302-b550-54eb-b56b-13aa4aae9c5e}}

Son of [[Zeus]]. Here he calls Zeus πατήρ, "father". In Hesiod, by contrast, Hera bears him without a father (*Theogony* 927–929).

## Descriptions

{{quote:fff14d70-614d-52ce-8914-a0fc7622d3c6}}

κλυτοτέχνης = κλυτός (famous) + τέχνη (craft): famed for his craft

{{quote:8f9bb6f1-e051-520e-a2bf-b2793eca4dde}}

περικλυτὸς ἀμφιγυήεις. ἀμφιγυήεις is from ἀμφί (on both sides) + γυῖον (limb). Traditionally it means "lame in both legs"; others take it as "strong in both arms". See [[lame-in-both-legs]].

## Outside Homer

Hephaestus tells a second story of his fall in Iliad 18.395–405. There Hera threw him out because he was lame, and Thetis and Eurynome caught him and kept him hidden for nine years. Lemnos, where he landed in Book 1, is "dearest to him of all lands" in the Odyssey (8.283–284). His wife is Charis in the Iliad (18.382–383) and Aphrodite in the Odyssey's song of Ares and Aphrodite (8.266–366).

Plato's Socrates would ban the story of Hephaestus hurled by his father from the ideal city (*Republic* 2.378d). See [[plato-hephaestus-hurled]].
