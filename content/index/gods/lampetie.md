## Etymology

Λαμπετίη (Lampetiē) belongs with λάμπω, “shine”, and λαμπετάω, “blaze”, the verb of the Iliad's “and his eyes were like blazing fire” (*Iliad* 1.104). W. W. Merry calls the two sisters “Bright” and “Shining”, and their mother “Younger”, “fit wife and daughters for the Sun” (*Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on *Odyssey* 12.132; see [[phaethusa]] and [[neaera]]). The colts that draw Dawn have names of the same kind, Lampus and Phaethon (*Odyssey* 23.246; see [[eos]]).

## Family

{{quote:6a6315fa-2e18-5d9e-9d8c-c9f2bdc04613}}

Daughter of the Sun, [[helios|Helios]], called here “Hyperion the Sun” (see [[hyperion]]), and of [[neaera|Neaera]]; sister of [[phaethusa|Phaethusa]]. The two are θεαί, “goddesses”, and νύμφαι ἐυπλόκαμοι, “nymphs of the lovely tresses” (see [[nymphs]] and [[with-lovely-tresses]]), and their mother settled them on Thrinacia, “to dwell far off”, as herders of their father's flocks and herds (see [[thrinacia]] and [[the-cattle-of-helios]]).

## Descriptions

τανύπεπλος, “of the long robes” (*Odyssey* 12.375), = τανύω (“stretch”) + πέπλος (“robe”): the epithet of Helen (*Iliad* 3.228; *Odyssey* 4.305, 15.171) and of Thetis (*Iliad* 18.385, 18.424), given in the Odyssey to [[ctimene|Ctimene]] too (*Odyssey* 15.363; see [[long-robed]]). Among the goddesses of the Odyssey it is hers alone.

## Outside Homer

Ovid makes Lampetie and Phaethusa two of the Heliades, the daughters of the Sun who mourned their brother Phaethon at his tomb until they were turned into trees. Phaethusa, the eldest, found her feet grown stiff, and *candida Lampetie*, “fair Lampetie”, trying to come to her, was held fast by a sudden root; their tears, dripping from the new branches, hardened in the sun into amber (*Metamorphoses* 2.340–366; see [[phaethusa]] and [[ovid]]).
