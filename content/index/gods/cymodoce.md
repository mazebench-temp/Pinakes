## Etymology

The name seems to join κῦμα, “wave”, as in κῦμα θαλάσσης, “the wave of the sea” (18.66, 18.145), and δοκ-, from δέχομαι, “receive”: “she who receives the waves”.

## In Homer

She is named once in Homer, in the catalogue of the Nereids who gather around Thetis in the depths of the sea when Thetis hears Achilles groan for Patroclus (18.37–49; see [[nereids]]).

## Outside Homer

Hesiod names her among the daughters of Nereus and Doris and gives her a task: with Cymatolege and Amphitrite of the lovely ankles she easily calms the waves on the misty sea and the blasts of the raging winds (*Theogony* 252–254). Apollodorus does not list her (*Library* 1.2.7). Virgil names her in the same verse twice, among the sea goddesses who ride with Neptune and among the nymphs of Cyrene (*Aeneid* 5.826; *Georgics* 4.338).
