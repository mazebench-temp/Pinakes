## Etymology

Νέαιρα (Neaira) seems to be formed from νέος, “young, new”: W. W. Merry renders it “Younger”, and calls the nymphs “Bright” and “Shining” and their mother “Younger”, “fit wife and daughters for the Sun” (*Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on *Odyssey* 12.132; see [[phaethusa]] and [[lampetie]]). She is δῖα, “brilliant” (see [[brilliant]]), and πότνια μήτηρ, “their lady mother” (see [[queenly]]). In later Greek the name was borne by women of another kind: the Suda lists a *Neaera* among the comedies of Timocles, adding that “Neaera is the name of a courtesan” (Suda τ 624), and it cites Demosthenes' speech *Against Neaera* (Suda α 3475; see [[demosthenes]]).

## Family

{{quote:6a6315fa-2e18-5d9e-9d8c-c9f2bdc04613}}

Mother of [[phaethusa|Phaethusa]] and [[lampetie|Lampetie]] by the Sun, [[helios|Helios]], whom the verse calls “Hyperion the Sun” (see [[hyperion]]). She reared the two and settled them on Thrinacia to guard their father's sheep and cattle. The poem gives her no father and no other story. Two manuscripts add after *Odyssey* 12.133, in the margin, a verse that makes her “own sister of Thetis of the shining tresses”, αὐτοκασιγνήτη Θέτιδος λιπαροπλοκάμοιο, and so one of the Nereids (Merry's critical note on *Odyssey* 12.133; see [[thetis]] and [[nereids]]). Homer's only other λιπαροπλόκαμος is Ate, whom Zeus seized “by her head of shining tresses” (*Iliad* 19.126).
