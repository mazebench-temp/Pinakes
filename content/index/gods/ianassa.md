## Etymology

The second part of the name is ἄνασσα, “lady, queen”. Callianassa, in the verse before, has the same ending (18.46; see [[callianassa]]), and Ianeira, named beside her, the same uncertain first part (18.47; see [[ianeira]]).

## In Homer

She is named once in Homer, in the catalogue of the Nereids who gather around Thetis in the depths of the sea when Thetis hears Achilles groan for Patroclus (18.37–49; see [[nereids]]).

## Outside Homer

Neither Hesiod nor Apollodorus names a Nereid Ianassa (*Theogony* 240–264; *Library* 1.2.7).
