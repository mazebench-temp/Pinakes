## Etymology

The name may come from μέλι, “honey”, as in μέλιτος γλυκίων, “sweeter than honey” (1.249): “the honey-sweet”. That is a guess.

## In Homer

She is named once in Homer, in the catalogue of the Nereids who gather around Thetis in the depths of the sea when Thetis hears Achilles groan for Patroclus (18.37–49; see [[nereids]]).

## Outside Homer

Hesiod names “graceful Melite” (Μελίτη χαρίεσσα) among the daughters of Nereus and Doris (*Theogony* 247), and Apollodorus lists her (*Library* 1.2.7). Another Melite is among the companions with whom Persephone was gathering flowers in the meadow when Hades carried her off (*Hymn to Demeter* 417–424; see [[persephone]]). Virgil names Melite with Thetis and the maiden Panopea among the sea goddesses who ride on the left of Neptune's chariot (*Aeneid* 5.825; see [[panope]]).
