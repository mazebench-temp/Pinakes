## Family

The Odyssey gives him children. By [[perse|Perse]], a daughter of [[oceanus|Oceanus]], he is the father of [[circe|Circe]] and [[aeetes|Aeetes]] (*Odyssey* 10.138–139), and by [[neaera|Neaera]] of the nymphs [[phaethusa|Phaethusa]] and [[lampetie|Lampetie]], who herd his flocks on Thrinacia (*Odyssey* 12.131–136). Hesiod makes him a son of Hyperion and Theia (*Theogony* 371–374); in the Odyssey Hyperion is his own name, “Hyperion the Sun” (*Odyssey* 1.8).

## Elsewhere in Homer

In the Odyssey he is again the god "who sees all things and hears all things" (*Odyssey* 11.109, 12.323), and his cattle on Thrinacia are the ruin of Odysseus' crew (*Odyssey* 12; see [[the-cattle-of-helios]]).

## Outside Homer

The Homeric *Hymn to Helios* names his mother Euryphaessa, the sister whom Hyperion married (see [[hyperion]]), and tells how he drives his chariot through the sky, shining for mortals and for the immortal gods, until he sends it down to Oceanus (*Hymn* 31.2–16). In the *Hymn to Demeter* he is “watchman of gods and men”: Demeter asks him, since he looks down with his rays over all the earth and sea, who has carried off her daughter, and he tells her that Zeus gave her to Hades (*Hymn to Demeter* 62–87; see [[persephone]]). His flocks graze elsewhere too: in the *Hymn to Apollo* the thick-fleeced sheep of lord Helios always graze at Taenarum in Laconia, the country “of the Sun, who gladdens mortals” (*Hymn to Apollo* 410–413), and Herodotus tells of sheep sacred to the Sun at Apollonia on the Ionian Gulf, guarded by night in a cave by citizens chosen each for a year (Herodotus 9.93; see [[herodotus]]).
