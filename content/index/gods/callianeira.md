## Etymology

The name joins καλλι-, “beautiful”, and -άνειρα, from ἀνήρ, “man”, the ending of the name of Castianeira, the mother of Gorgythion (8.305; see [[castianeira]]), and of two epithets: κυδιάνειρα, “where men win glory”, said of battle and of the assembly (4.225, 1.490), and ἀντιάνειρα, “the equals of men”, said of the Amazons (3.189). Ianeira, three verses later, has the same ending (18.47; see [[ianeira]]).

## In Homer

She is named once in Homer, in the catalogue of the Nereids who gather around Thetis in the depths of the sea when Thetis hears Achilles groan for Patroclus (18.37–49; see [[nereids]]).

## Outside Homer

Neither Hesiod nor Apollodorus names a Nereid Callianeira (*Theogony* 240–264; *Library* 1.2.7).
