## In Book 1

{{quote:7eb1dc64-998f-5fdc-808f-db76dbafbe72}}

Thetis sits "in the depths of the sea beside her aged father" when Achilles calls her.

{{quote:6247e670-56c3-5601-b4ca-1b43ec4fd64c}}

θυγάτηρ ἁλίοιο γέροντος: "daughter of the Old Man of the Sea". The phrase is repeated at 1.556, both times in the quarrel between Zeus and Hera.

## Outside Homer

In the Iliad the old man of the sea is Thetis' father, and so Nereus; the Nereids are named as a group at 18.38–49. The Odyssey gives the same title to other sea gods, Proteus (4.365) and Phorcys (13.96). Hesiod calls Nereus "the old man" because he is truthful and gentle (*Theogony* 233–236).
