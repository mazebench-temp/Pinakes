## Outside Homer

In the Iliad the old man of the sea is Thetis' father, and so Nereus, whose daughters, the Nereids, are named as a group in Book 18 (18.38–49). The Odyssey gives the same title to other sea gods, Proteus (*Odyssey* 4.365, 4.384) and Phorcys (*Odyssey* 13.96; see [[phorcys-the-sea-god]]), and calls the Nereids who mourn Achilles “the daughters of the old man of the sea” (*Odyssey* 24.58). Hesiod makes Nereus the eldest son of Pontus, truthful and free of falsehood, and says that men call him the old man because he is unerring and gentle and does not forget what is right (*Theogony* 233–236).

Pausanias found at Gythium in Laconia a sea god whom the people called the Old Man, and identified him with Nereus; they had the name from Homer, from Thetis' words to the Nereids, “to see the Old Man of the Sea and our father's house” (Pausanias 3.21.9, quoting 18.140–141).
