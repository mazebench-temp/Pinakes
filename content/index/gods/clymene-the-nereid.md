## Etymology

κλύμενος is an old participle of κλύω, “hear”: “heard of, famous”.

## In Homer

She is named once in Homer, in the catalogue of the Nereids who gather around Thetis in the depths of the sea when Thetis hears Achilles groan for Patroclus (18.37–49; see [[nereids]]). The Iliad has another Clymene, the ox-eyed handmaid who walks with Aethra behind Helen to the Scaean gates (3.144; see [[clymene]]), and Odysseus sees a third among the famous women of the past in the underworld, named with a Maera as here: Μαῖράν τε Κλυμένην τε (*Odyssey* 11.326; see [[maera]]).

## Outside Homer

Neither Hesiod nor Apollodorus has a Nereid Clymene (*Theogony* 240–264; *Library* 1.2.7). Hesiod's Clymene is a daughter of Oceanus and Tethys (*Theogony* 351), whom Iapetus married (*Theogony* 507–508; see [[iapetus]]). Virgil gives the name to one of the nymphs in the chamber of Cyrene, who tells the others of the vain care of Vulcan and the tricks of Mars (*Georgics* 4.345–346).
