## Etymology

Δημήτηρ (Dēmētēr). The second part is μήτηρ (“mother”). The first is uncertain; the old explanation “Earth Mother”, with δᾶ for γῆ (“earth”), is possible but not proved.

## In the Iliad

{{quote:34177f6f-a2e7-5721-a03c-be8b13c94142}}

Pyrasus, among the towns of Protesilaus in Thessaly, is Demeter's τέμενος (“precinct”), land set apart for the goddess. A τέμενος (from τέμνω, “cut”) is land cut off from common use and given to a god or a king. This is the first time the Iliad names her. See [[pyrasus]].

## Elsewhere in Homer

Bread is “the grain of Demeter” (Δημήτερος ἀκτή, Iliad 13.322, 21.76), and in a simile fair-haired Demeter herself separates grain from chaff on the threshing floor (5.499–502). Zeus names her among the goddesses he has loved (14.326). In the Odyssey she lies with Iasion in a thrice-plowed field, and Zeus kills him with a thunderbolt (5.125–128). Homer never brings her to Olympus or into the action of either poem.

## Outside Homer

Hesiod makes her a daughter of Cronus and Rhea (*Theogony* 453–454), and the mother of Persephone by Zeus (*Theogony* 912–914). The *Homeric Hymn to Demeter* tells how Hades carried off Persephone, how Demeter searched for her and came to Eleusis, and how she founded her mysteries there.
