## Etymology

Δεῖμος is formed from the root of δείδω (“fear”), like δέος and δεῖμα (“fear, fright”) and δεινός (“dread”). The translation renders him “Terror”, and his companion Φόβος “Rout” (see [[rout]]).

## In Homer

{{quote:c99a1945-6921-5957-8d4d-c000d5479a6a}}

Terror is named three times in the Iliad, always with Rout. At the first clash of the armies the two are listed among the powers that drive the men on, after Ares, who rouses the Trojans, and Athena, who rouses the Achaeans (4.439–440). The sentence does not say which side they drive. On the shield of Agamemnon they surround the Gorgon's face (11.36–37), and when Ares learns that his son Ascalaphus has been killed, he tells Terror and Rout to yoke his horses (15.119–120). Terror never acts alone.

## Outside Homer

Hesiod makes Phobos and Deimos the children of Ares and Aphrodite, “terrible, who rout the close ranks of men in chilling war with Ares, sacker of cities” (*Theogony* 933–936). The smaller and outer of the two moons of Mars, discovered by Asaph Hall in 1877, bears his name.
