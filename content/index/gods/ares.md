## Etymology

Ἄρης (Arēs). The origin of the name is unknown. It has been connected with ἀρή (“ruin, bane”), and in epic it is often less a person than a word for battle itself, as when the Achaeans go to their meal “so that we may join in the clash of Ares”, literally “bring Ares together” (ξυνάγωμεν Ἄρηα, 2.381), or pray to escape “the toil of Ares” (μῶλον Ἄρηος, 2.401).

Ἐνυάλιος (Enyalius) is another name for him in Homer (2.651). A god *e-nu-wa-ri-jo* already appears on a Linear B tablet from Knossos (KN V 52), in a list of gods that does not name Ares.

## Family

Son of [[Zeus]] and [[Hera]]: Zeus says so when he rebukes him (5.889–898).

{{quote:3cdf4809-6d2b-59aa-9574-c2935f3d6b58}}

Father of Ascalaphus and Ialmenus, the leaders from Aspledon and Orchomenus, by Astyoche. See [[ascalaphus]], [[ialmenus]] and [[the-birth-of-ascalaphus-and-ialmenus]]. When Ascalaphus is killed, Ares does not know it at first (13.518–525), and when he learns of it he wants to avenge him against Zeus' command (15.110–142).

## Descriptions

{{quote:301f2cec-8d25-55d0-8d65-6578c864b1b2}}

θεράποντες Ἄρηος, “attendants of Ares”: the warriors as the servants of war. Agamemnon uses it to address the army.

ὄζος Ἄρηος, “offshoot of Ares”: a warrior as a sprig of the war god (ὄζος, “branch, shoot”; 2.540, 2.663, 2.704, 2.745, 2.842). See [[offshoot-of-ares]].

ἀτάλαντος Ἄρηϊ, “the equal of Ares” (2.627), and ἀτάλαντος Ἐνυαλίῳ, “the equal of Enyalius” (2.651). See [[equal-of-ares]] and [[enyalius]].

Other phrases give him the qualities of war: “the hateful work of Ares” (στυγερῷ … Ἄρηϊ, 2.385), “sharp Ares” (ὀξὺν Ἄρηα, 2.440), and “the panic of Ares” (φόβον Ἄρηος, 2.767) that Eumelus' mares carry. Agamemnon among his men is “in waist like Ares” (2.479; see [[like-zeus-ares-and-poseidon]]).

## Mythology

Ares takes the Trojan side in the Iliad. Diomedes wounds him with Athena's help, and he goes up to Olympus roaring, where Zeus calls him the most hateful of the gods (5.855–898). In the battle of the gods Athena fells him with a stone (21.391–414). His companions in battle are Deimos and Phobos, “Terror” and “Rout” (4.440, 15.119; see [[terror]] and [[rout]]). In the Odyssey the singer Demodocus tells how Hephaestus caught him in bed with Aphrodite (8.266–366).

## Outside Homer

Hesiod names him among the children of Zeus and Hera (*Theogony* 921–923), and makes him the father of Phobos, Deimos and Harmonia by Aphrodite (*Theogony* 933–937). The Romans identified him with Mars.
