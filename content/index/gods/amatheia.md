## Etymology

The name seems to be formed from ἄμαθος, “sand”, as in ἀμάθοιο βαθείης, “deep sand” (5.587): “she of the sands”. Hesiod has a Nereid Psamathe, named from the other word for sand, ψάμαθος (*Theogony* 260).

## In Homer

She is named once in Homer, in the catalogue of the Nereids who gather around Thetis in the depths of the sea when Thetis hears Achilles groan for Patroclus (18.37–49; see [[nereids]]). She is the last of the named Nereids, and one of three in the catalogue with an epithet: ἐϋπλόκαμος, “of the lovely tresses” (see [[with-lovely-tresses]], [[halia]] and [[galateia]]).

## Outside Homer

Neither Hesiod nor Apollodorus has an Amatheia; both have Psamathe (*Theogony* 260; *Library* 1.2.7), who bore Phocus to Aeacus (*Theogony* 1003–1005).
