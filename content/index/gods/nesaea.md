## Etymology

The name seems to be formed from νῆσος, “island”: “she of the islands”.

## In Homer

She is named once in Homer, in the catalogue of the Nereids who gather around Thetis in the depths of the sea when Thetis hears Achilles groan for Patroclus (18.37–49; see [[nereids]]).

## Outside Homer

Hesiod names her among the daughters of Nereus and Doris, in a verse with Actaea: Νησαίη τε καὶ Ἀκταίη καὶ Πρωτομέδεια (*Theogony* 249). In the Iliad Actaea follows in the next verse (18.41; see [[actaea]]). Apollodorus lists her too (*Library* 1.2.7). Virgil calls her *Nisaee* among the sea goddesses who ride with Neptune and *Nesaee* among the nymphs of Cyrene (*Aeneid* 5.826; *Georgics* 4.338).
