## Etymology

The name seems to join ἀμφί, “around”, and νέμω, νέμομαι, “dwell in, range over”: perhaps “she who dwells round about”. The masculine form, Amphinomus, is the name of one of Penelope's suitors (*Odyssey* 16.351, 16.394).

## In Homer

She is named once in Homer, in the catalogue of the Nereids who gather around Thetis in the depths of the sea when Thetis hears Achilles groan for Patroclus (18.37–49; see [[nereids]]).

## Outside Homer

Neither Hesiod nor Apollodorus names a Nereid Amphinome (*Theogony* 240–264; *Library* 1.2.7).
