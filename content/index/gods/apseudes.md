## Etymology

Ἀψευδής is the adjective “without falsehood, truthful”, from ἀ-, “not”, and ψεῦδος, “lie” (2.81). Homer has the adjective only as her name.

## In Homer

She is named once in Homer, in the catalogue of the Nereids who gather around Thetis in the depths of the sea when Thetis hears Achilles groan for Patroclus (18.37–49; see [[nereids]]). In the same verse (18.46) she is paired with Nemertes, whose name means “unerring”: Νημερτής τε καὶ Ἀψευδής (see [[nemertes]]).

## Outside Homer

Neither Hesiod nor Apollodorus has a Nereid Apseudes (*Theogony* 240–264; *Library* 1.2.7), but Hesiod gives the two qualities to their father: Nereus is ἀψευδέα καὶ ἀληθέα, without falsehood and true, and he is called the old man because he is νημερτής, unerring (*Theogony* 233–235; see [[old-man-of-the-sea]]). When the infant Hermes denies to Zeus that he stole Apollo's cattle, he joins the two ideas in one verse, νημερτής τε γάρ εἰμι καὶ οὐκ οἶδα ψεύδεσθαι, for I am truthful and do not know how to lie, and Zeus laughs at the skill of his denial (*Hymn to Hermes* 369, 389–390).
