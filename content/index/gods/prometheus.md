Hesiod tells the story twice: the trick of the sacrifice, in which Zeus chose the bones wrapped in fat, the theft of fire in a fennel stalk, Prometheus' binding, and the making of Pandora as the price of fire (*Theogony* 507–616; *Works and Days* 47–105). In Aeschylus' *Prometheus Bound* he is a benefactor who gave mortals all the arts (see [[prometheus-play]] and [[the-punishment-of-prometheus]]).
