## Etymology

The name may come from δῶρον, “gift”: “the bountiful”. That is a guess.

## In Homer

She is named once in Homer, in the catalogue of the Nereids who gather around Thetis in the depths of the sea when Thetis hears Achilles groan for Patroclus (18.37–49; see [[nereids]]). Her verse is close to one in the *Theogony*: Δωρὶς καὶ Πανόπη καὶ ἀγακλειτὴ Γαλάτεια (18.45), Δωρὶς καὶ Πανόπεια καὶ εὐειδὴς Γαλάτεια (*Theogony* 250; see [[panope]] and [[galateia]]).

## Outside Homer

Hesiod has two goddesses of the name: Doris, a daughter of Oceanus and Tethys (*Theogony* 350), who bore the fifty Nereids to Nereus (*Theogony* 240–242), and a Nereid Doris, one of her daughters (*Theogony* 250). Apollodorus calls the mother of the Nereids Doris but has no Nereid of that name (*Library* 1.2.7). In Ovid the Nereid Galatea calls her mother “sea-blue Doris” (*caerula Doris*, *Metamorphoses* 13.742).
