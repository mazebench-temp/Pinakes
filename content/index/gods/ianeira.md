## Etymology

The second part of the name is ἀνήρ, “man”; the first is uncertain. Callianeira, three verses before, has the same ending (18.44; see [[callianeira]]), and Ianassa, named beside her, the same first part (18.47; see [[ianassa]]).

## In Homer

She is named once in Homer, in the catalogue of the Nereids who gather around Thetis in the depths of the sea when Thetis hears Achilles groan for Patroclus (18.37–49; see [[nereids]]).

## Outside Homer

Hesiod has no Nereid Ianeira but an Oceanid, a daughter of Oceanus and Tethys (*Theogony* 356). Apollodorus lists Ianeira among the Nereids (*Library* 1.2.7). Another Ianeira is among the companions with whom Persephone was gathering flowers when Hades carried her off (*Hymn to Demeter* 417–424; see [[persephone]]).
