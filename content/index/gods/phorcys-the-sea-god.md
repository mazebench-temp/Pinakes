## Name and descriptions

Φόρκυς (Phorkys), genitive Φόρκυνος. Homer names him three times, always in the genitive. In Book 1 of the Odyssey he is ἁλὸς ἀτρυγέτοιο μέδων, “who rules over the barren sea” (*Odyssey* 1.72): μέδων is “ruler, lord”, the word of the Achaean leaders in the formula ἡγήτορες ἠδὲ μέδοντες, “leaders and rulers” (2.79), and ἀτρύγετος, “barren”, is an epithet of the sea (see [[barren-sea]]). In Book 13 the harbor of Ithaca where the Phaeacians set Odysseus ashore is “the harbor of Phorcys, the old man of the sea” (Φόρκυνος … λιμήν, ἁλίοιο γέροντος, *Odyssey* 13.96, 13.345). The Iliad gives that title to the father of the Nereids, and the Odyssey to Proteus as well (see [[old-man-of-the-sea]]).

## Family

Father of the nymph [[Thoosa]], who bore the Cyclops Polyphemus to [[Poseidon]] (*Odyssey* 1.71–73). Homer names no wife for him.

## Outside Homer

[[hesiod|Hesiod]] makes Phorcys a son of Pontus, the Sea, and of Earth, and a brother of Nereus, Thaumas, Ceto and Eurybia (*Theogony* 233–239). By his sister Ceto he fathered the Graeae, “gray from birth”, Pemphredo and Enyo, and the Gorgons who live beyond Oceanus (*Theogony* 270–276; see [[gorgon]]), and last a dread serpent that guards the golden apples in the hidden places of the dark earth (*Theogony* 333–336). Aeschylus' lost *Daughters of Phorcys* was about the Graeae (see [[daughters-of-phorcys]] and [[perseus-and-the-graeae]]). He is not the Phrygian Phorcys of the Iliad (see [[phorcys]]).
