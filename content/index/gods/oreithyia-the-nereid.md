## Etymology

The name seems to join ὄρος, “mountain”, and θύω, “rush, storm”: “she who storms over the mountains”. That is a guess.

## In Homer

She is named once in Homer, in the catalogue of the Nereids who gather around Thetis in the depths of the sea when Thetis hears Achilles groan for Patroclus (18.37–49; see [[nereids]]).

## Outside Homer

Neither Hesiod nor Apollodorus names a Nereid Oreithyia (*Theogony* 240–264; *Library* 1.2.7). The famous Oreithyia is an Athenian, the daughter of Erechtheus. Herodotus tells that Boreas, the north wind, had her as his wife, so that when the Persian fleet lay off the coast, the Athenians, at anchor at Chalcis in Euboea, sacrificed and called on Boreas, their son-in-law, and on Oreithyia to help them and destroy the barbarians' ships; and when they went home they founded a shrine of Boreas by the Ilissus (Herodotus 7.189; see [[boreas]] and [[erechtheus]]). In Plato's *Phaedrus*, Phaedrus asks Socrates whether it was here, from the Ilissus, that Boreas is said to have carried her off; a little farther down, Socrates answers, where there is an altar of Boreas (*Phaedrus* 229b–c; see [[plato]]).
