## Etymology

ὥρα is the season, the right time of year: flies swarm around the milk pails “in the springtime” (ὥρῃ ἐν εἰαρινῇ, 2.471). The Ὧραι are the seasons as goddesses. The translation calls them “the Hours”, the English form of the Latin *Horae*.

## In Homer

{{quote:1cbcd113-faa2-5d6b-a385-007d73729c20}}

The gates open “of themselves” (αὐτόμαται), like the tripods of Hephaestus that roll to the gods' assembly on their own (αὐτόματοι, 18.376), and they groan (μύκον) as they open. The Hours keep them, “to whom great heaven and Olympus are entrusted, both to throw open the thick cloud and to close it” (5.750–751). The gate is a cloud, and its keepers are the goddesses of the seasons. See [[olympus]] and [[sky]].

The same lines return when Hera and Athena drive out again in Book 8 (8.393–395 = 5.749–751). When Zeus turns them back, the Hours unyoke their horses, tie them at ambrosial mangers and lean the chariot against the shining wall (8.433–435). Elsewhere in Homer ὧραι are simply the seasons of the year, as in the Odyssey's “when the seasons came round” (Od. 10.469).

## Outside Homer

Hesiod makes the Hours daughters of Zeus and Themis: Eunomia, Dike and Eirene, Good Order, Justice and Peace (*Theogony* 901–903). The Hours with lovely hair crown Pandora with spring flowers (*Works and Days* 75), and in a fragment of the *Cypria* they help the Graces make Aphrodite's clothes (see [[graces]]).
