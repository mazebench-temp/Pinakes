## Etymology

Ἁλίη is the feminine of ἅλιος, “of the sea” (from ἅλς, “salt, sea”), the word in the title of her father, the Old Man of the Sea, γέρων ἅλιος (18.141), and in Thetis' “sisters of the sea”, ἁλίῃσι κασιγνήτῃσι (18.139).

## In Homer

She is named once in Homer, in the catalogue of the Nereids who gather around Thetis in the depths of the sea when Thetis hears Achilles groan for Patroclus (18.37–49; see [[nereids]]). She is the only one of the thirty-three to be called βοῶπις, “ox-eyed”, the standing epithet of Hera, which the Iliad also gives to Helen's handmaid Clymene and to Phylomedusa, the mother of Menesthius (3.144, 7.10; see [[ox-eyed]], [[clymene]] and [[phylomedusa]]). Her verse ends Ἁλίη τε βοῶπις, as 3.144 ends Κλυμένη τε βοῶπις.

## Outside Homer

Hesiod names her Halie, “lovely” (ἐρόεσσα), among the daughters of Nereus and Doris (*Theogony* 245), and Apollodorus lists her (*Library* 1.2.7).
