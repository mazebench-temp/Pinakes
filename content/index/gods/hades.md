## Etymology

Ἀΐδης (Aïdēs), dative Ἄϊδι. Ancient and modern readers have taken it as "the unseen one" (ἀ- "not" + ἰδεῖν "see"). The derivation is attractive but uncertain.

## In Book 1

{{quote:5fbdb8ad-4245-5590-ad70-17444ee00287}}

Ἄϊδι προΐαψεν: "hurled down to Hades". In Homer "Hades" is always the god; the realm is his "house" (δόμος Ἀΐδαο). The dative here means "to Hades", into his keeping. The proem contrasts the ψυχαί (souls) that go below with αὐτούς ("the men themselves"), the bodies left for dogs and birds. For Homer the self is the body. See [[psyche]].

## Outside Homer

Hades is the brother of Zeus and Poseidon, and the dead are his share when the world is divided (Iliad 15.187–193). The Odyssey's eleventh book takes Odysseus to the edge of his realm.
