## Etymology

Πέρση (Persē), Hesiod's Περσηίς (Perseïs). The name has no agreed etymology. It is not Περσεφόνεια, the queen of the dead (see [[persephone]]), nor Περσεύς, the son of Danae (see [[perseus]]).

## Family

Daughter of [[oceanus|Oceanus]], and by the Sun, [[helios|Helios]], the mother of [[circe|Circe]] and of [[aeetes|Aeetes]] (*Odyssey* 10.138–139). The Odyssey gives her no other story.

{{notes}}

## Outside Homer

Hesiod names Perseis among the daughters whom Tethys bore to Oceanus (*Theogony* 356; see [[tethys]]), and makes her the mother of Circe and king Aeetes by the tireless Sun, calling her “the famous daughter of Oceanus” (956–957; see [[hesiod]]). Cicero's Cotta gives the same pedigree for Circe, Pasiphae and Aeetes, “born of Perseis, daughter of Oceanus, with the Sun for their father” (*On the Nature of the Gods* 3.48; see [[cicero]]). W. W. Merry sees in her descent from Oceanus perhaps the first hint of a connection between Circe and the West (*Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on *Odyssey* 10.135).
