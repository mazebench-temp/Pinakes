## Etymology

Νημερτής is the adjective νημερτής, “unerring, true”, from νη-, “not”, and ἁμαρτάνω, “miss”. Thetis asks Zeus, νημερτὲς μὲν δή μοι ὑπόσχεο, “Promise me truly” (1.514). In the Odyssey it is the epithet of Proteus, the unerring old man of the sea, γέρων ἅλιος νημερτής (*Odyssey* 4.349, 4.384, 4.401, 4.542).

## In Homer

She is named once in Homer, in the catalogue of the Nereids who gather around Thetis in the depths of the sea when Thetis hears Achilles groan for Patroclus (18.37–49; see [[nereids]]). In the same verse (18.46) she is paired with Apseudes, whose name means “without falsehood”: Νημερτής τε καὶ Ἀψευδής (see [[apseudes]]).

## Outside Homer

Hesiod ends his list of the daughters of Nereus with Nemertes, ἣ πατρὸς ἔχει νόον ἀθανάτοιο, who has the mind of her immortal father (*Theogony* 262); Nereus himself, he says, is called the old man because he is νημερτής, unerring, and gentle (*Theogony* 233–236; see [[old-man-of-the-sea]]). Apollodorus does not list her (*Library* 1.2.7).
