## Etymology

Θάλεια is the adjective θάλεια, “rich, abundant”, the word of δαῖτα θάλειαν, “a rich feast” (7.475), related to θάλλω, “bloom, flourish”: “the blooming one”.

## In Homer

She is named once in Homer, in the catalogue of the Nereids who gather around Thetis in the depths of the sea when Thetis hears Achilles groan for Patroclus (18.37–49; see [[nereids]]).

## Outside Homer

Neither Hesiod nor Apollodorus has a Nereid Thaleia (*Theogony* 240–264; *Library* 1.2.7). Hesiod gives the name to one of the nine Muses (Θάλεια, *Theogony* 77; see [[muses]]) and calls one of the three Graces Thalia (Θαλίη, *Theogony* 909; see [[graces]]). Virgil names a Thalia twice in a verse of four names from the Iliad's catalogue, once among the sea goddesses who ride with Neptune and once among the nymphs of Cyrene (*Aeneid* 5.826; *Georgics* 4.338; see [[nesaea]], [[speio]] and [[cymodoce]]).
