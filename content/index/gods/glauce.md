## Etymology

Γλαύκη is the feminine of γλαυκός, “gleaming, gray-green”, the color of the sea. Homer has the adjective once, when Patroclus tells Achilles that Peleus and Thetis were not his parents: “the gray-green sea bore you” (16.34; see [[gray-green-sea]]). Its compound γλαυκῶπις, “gray-eyed”, is the epithet of Athena ([[gray-eyed]]).

## In Homer

She is named once in Homer, in the catalogue of the Nereids who gather around Thetis in the depths of the sea when Thetis hears Achilles groan for Patroclus (18.37–49; see [[nereids]]). She heads the list.

## Outside Homer

Hesiod names Glauce among the fifty daughters of Nereus and Doris, in the verse that also names Thetis: Εὐδώρη τε Θέτις τε Γαλήνη τε Γλαύκη τε (*Theogony* 244). Apollodorus has no Glauce, but a Glauconome (Γλαυκονόμη), as Hesiod has too (*Library* 1.2.7; *Theogony* 256).
