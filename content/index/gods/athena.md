## Etymology

Ἀθήνη (Athēnē), also Ἀθηναίη (1.200, 1.221). Her name and the city's are clearly linked, but which came first is unknown; the name may be pre-Greek. Παλλάς (Pallas), her regular title, is of uncertain meaning, explained in antiquity from πάλλω ("brandish") and from a girl or a giant named Pallas.

## In Book 1

Athena's single scene is the first divine epiphany in the poem. Achilles is pondering whether to kill Agamemnon, with his sword half drawn:

{{quote:107d25e0-78cc-5551-87d0-029244305196}}

She is sent by [[Hera]] and seen by Achilles alone. She seizes him by the hair from behind, and he knows her at once by her eyes. She does not forbid the quarrel, only the sword:

{{quote:cedfa88f-f711-5c61-bcc8-4a6861fd5e26}}

Achilles obeys, "for that is better", and she returns to Olympus (1.216–222). She promises him three times the gifts for this outrage. That promise is fulfilled in Book 9, when Agamemnon offers amends, and again in Book 19. See [[triple-gifts-foretold]].

She is also one of the three gods who, Achilles says, once plotted to bind Zeus (1.400). See [[binding-of-zeus]].

## Outside Homer

Hesiod tells how Zeus swallowed Metis and Athena was born from his head (*Theogony* 886–900, 924–926). At Athens she was Polias, the goddess of the city, and the Parthenon housed Phidias' gold-and-ivory statue of her.
