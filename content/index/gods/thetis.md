## Etymology

Θέτις (Thetis). The name is unexplained. Ancient guesses tied it to τίθημι ("set, establish"), which made her a goddess of order; modern scholars treat it as uncertain.

## Mythology

Achilles reminds her of her greatest service to Zeus. When Hera, Poseidon and Athena plotted to bind him, she alone came to help, bringing the hundred-handed [[Briareus]]:

{{quote:c8aac059-d039-50c0-8ca3-df658cf392c4}}

See [[binding-of-zeus]].

Homer knows her as a rescuer of gods. Besides Zeus, she took Dionysus to her bosom when Lycurgus drove him into the sea (6.130–137; see [[lycurgus-and-dionysus]]), and with Eurynome she took in Hephaestus when he had fallen from heaven by his mother's will, and kept him hidden for nine years (18.394–405; see [[eurynome]] and [[fall-of-hephaestus]]).

Of all the goddesses of the sea, Zeus gave her alone to a mortal man, and she endured the bed of Peleus “much against my will” (18.432–434). Hera says that she herself reared Thetis and gave her to Peleus for his wife, and that all the gods came to the wedding (24.59–63). See [[thetis-in-the-house-of-peleus]].

## Outside Homer

Hesiod names Thetis among the fifty daughters of Nereus and Doris (*Theogony* 240–264). Pindar gives the reason for her marriage to a mortal: Themis foretold that her son would be greater than his father, so Zeus and Poseidon gave up their courtship and married her to Peleus (*Isthmian* 8.26–48).
