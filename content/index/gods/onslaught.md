ἐν δὲ κρυόεσσα Ἰωκή, “and on it chilling Onslaught” (5.740), among the powers of Athena's [[aegis]] (see [[rout]], [[strife]], [[courage]] and [[gorgon]]). Homer makes her a person only here. κρυόεις, “chilling”, is from κρύος, “icy cold”: the cold of fear, as in the “chilling rout” (φόβου κρυόεντος) whose companion is Panic (9.2).

ἰωκή is rare as a common word. It comes twice more in Homer. The Achaeans, standing like clouds on the mountains, “feared neither the strength of the Trojans nor their onslaughts” (ἰωκάς, 5.521), and Achilles, from the stern of his ship, watches “the steep toil and the tearful onslaught” (ἰῶκα, 11.601). The word has been connected with διώκω, “pursue”, but the connection is not certain. The translation renders it “onslaught” throughout.
