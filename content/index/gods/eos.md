## Etymology

Ἠώς (Ēōs) is also the common word for the dawn as a time of day, and for the quarter of the sky where she rises (12.239). W. W. Merry gives the Aeolic form αὔως and takes the name to be the same word as Latin *Aurora* (from *aus-osa*) and the Sanskrit name of the dawn, all from a root *us*, “to give heat, or light” (*Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 2.1, after Curtius).

## Family

In Homer she rises each morning from the bed of her husband [[tithonus|Tithonus]] (11.1–2 = *Odyssey* 5.1–2).

## Outside Homer

Eos is a daughter of Hyperion and Theia (Hesiod, *Theogony* 371–374).
