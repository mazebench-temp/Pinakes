## Etymology

Ἀγαυή is the feminine of ἀγαυός, “noble, illustrious”, an epithet of kings and heroes, as of “noble Laomedon” (5.649, 6.23) and “noble Tithonus” (11.1). In the Odyssey the feminine is the epithet of Persephone, ἀγαυὴ Περσεφόνεια (*Odyssey* 11.213, 11.226, 11.635).

## In Homer

She is named once in Homer, in the catalogue of the Nereids who gather around Thetis in the depths of the sea when Thetis hears Achilles groan for Patroclus (18.37–49; see [[nereids]]).

## Outside Homer

Hesiod names her among the daughters of Nereus and Doris (*Theogony* 247), and Apollodorus lists her as Agaue (Ἀγαύη, *Library* 1.2.7). The better-known Agave is a daughter of Cadmus and the mother of Pentheus, who in Euripides' *Bacchae* leads the women in tearing her son apart and then finds his head in her hands (*Bacchae* 507, 1114–1136, 1280–1284; see [[pentheus]] and [[cadmus]]).
