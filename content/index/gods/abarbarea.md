νύμφη νηῒς, “the nymph … a Naiad” (6.21–22). A νηΐς is a nymph of springs and running water; the word is usually connected with νάω, “flow”. The Iliad tells of two other Naiads who bore sons to mortal men, and both sons die in battle: the mother of Satnius, who bore him to Enops as he tended cattle by the Satnioeis (14.443–445), and the mother of Iphition, who bore him to Otrynteus under snowy Tmolus (20.383–385). The Odyssey knows a cave on Ithaca sacred to “the nymphs who are called Naiads” (Od. 13.103–104, 13.347–350). See [[nymphs]].

She lay with Bucolion “in love and bed” (φιλότητι καὶ εὐνῇ, 6.25; see [[philotes]]) while he was tending his sheep, and bore him twins. Her name occurs only here, and its origin is unknown.
