ἐν δʼ Ἔρις, ἐν δʼ Ἀλκή, ἐν δὲ κρυόεσσα Ἰωκή, “and on it is Strife, and on it Courage, and on it chilling Onslaught” (5.740). Homer makes Courage a person only here, among the powers of Athena's [[aegis]] (see [[rout]], [[strife]], [[onslaught]] and [[gorgon]]).

ἀλκή belongs with ἀλέξω, “ward off”, as does ἄλκαρ, “defense” (5.644). The translation keeps it “courage” and βίη “strength” (see [[alke]]). The word runs through Book 5 before the goddess appears. Agamemnon's exhortation ends with it: “when men flee, there rises neither fame nor any courage” (5.532). And just before Athena puts on the aegis, Hera calls her to arms: “let us two also think of our furious courage” (θούριδος ἀλκῆς, 5.718; see [[furious]]). The courage the goddesses summon is then carried into battle on Athena's shoulders.
