## Etymology

The name is formed from ἀκτή, “shore, headland”: “she of the shore”. Later in the scene the Nereids “went up onto the shore” at Troy, ἀκτὴν εἰσανέβαινον (18.68).

## In Homer

She is named once in Homer, in the catalogue of the Nereids who gather around Thetis in the depths of the sea when Thetis hears Achilles groan for Patroclus (18.37–49; see [[nereids]]).

## Outside Homer

Hesiod names her with Nesaea in one verse (*Theogony* 249; see [[nesaea]]), and Apollodorus lists her (*Library* 1.2.7).
