## Etymology

Ἀπόλλων (Apollōn). The origin of the name is unknown. It has been connected with the Doric form Ἀπέλλων and with ἀπέλλαι, the name of assemblies at Sparta and Delphi, but no explanation has won general agreement.

Φοῖβος (Phoebus), his commonest other name, is usually taken to mean "bright" or "pure"; later Greek used φοῖβος as an ordinary adjective in that sense.

## Descriptions

No god in Book 1 has more titles. Most of them are about distance: Apollo strikes from far off, unseen.

{{quote:180e443d-8020-5d72-84c5-dc1c76ef8b43}}

ἑκηβόλος = ἑκάς (far off) + βάλλω (throw, shoot): [[far-shooter|far-shooting]]. Used alone as a name for him, "the Far-Shooter" (1.96, 1.110).

{{quote:2cd6bf6a-a6c5-5702-9bae-afaafddc4f1b}}

ἑκατηβελέτης = ἑκάς + βέλος (missile): who shoots from afar

{{quote:a62a02f9-d4cc-5944-9cbe-1b3cf04293a7}}

ἑκάεργος = ἑκάς + ἔργον (work): the Far-Worker, one who works his will from far away. Another explanation derives it from εἴργω, "keep away", making him the god who wards off evil; the sense is disputed.

{{quote:8056fbf6-35ca-5016-a86a-90087e870edc}}

ἀργυρότοξος = ἄργυρος (silver) + τόξον (bow): god of the silver bow. Σμινθεύς (Smintheus) is his cult title at Chryse, explained in antiquity from σμίνθος, "mouse"; see [[smintheus-and-the-mice]].

## Outside Homer

The *Homeric Hymn to Apollo* tells of his birth on Delos and the founding of his oracle at Delphi. His arrows are also the ordinary Homeric explanation for sudden death in men, as Artemis' are for women (Odyssey 3.279–281, 11.172–173).

The allegorist Heraclitus read the plague of Book 1 as a natural event, a pestilence brought by the heat of the sun, which he took Apollo to be. See [[allegory-of-apollo]].
