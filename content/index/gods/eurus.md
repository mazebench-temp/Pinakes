Εὖρος (Euros), the east or southeast wind. The origin of the name is uncertain.

{{quote:56df7537-849d-5a7b-bd20-a451bb3fd6af}}

It rushes down with [[Notus]] “from the clouds of father Zeus” and heaps up the waves of the [[Icarian Sea]]. See [[waves-of-the-icarian-sea]].

## Elsewhere in Homer

Eurus and Notus strive against each other in a mountain forest in the simile of Iliad 16.765–769. On Thrinacia no wind blows for a month but these two (Odyssey 12.325–326), and in the storm that wrecks Odysseus' raft all four winds rush together (Odyssey 5.295–296).

## Outside Homer

Hesiod's list of the good winds born to Eos and Astraeus names Zephyrus, Boreas and Notus, but not Eurus (*Theogony* 378–380).
