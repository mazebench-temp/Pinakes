## Etymology

Δεξαμένη is the aorist participle of δέχομαι, “receive”: “she who has received”. Its compound is the verb for the welcome of the sea goddesses in the Iliad: Thetis “took him to her bosom”, the frightened Dionysus (ὑπεδέξατο κόλπῳ, 6.136), and with Eurynome she took in Hephaestus when he fell (18.398; see [[dionysus]], [[eurynome]] and [[hephaestus]]).

## In Homer

She is named once in Homer, in the catalogue of the Nereids who gather around Thetis in the depths of the sea when Thetis hears Achilles groan for Patroclus (18.37–49; see [[nereids]]).

## Outside Homer

Neither Hesiod nor Apollodorus names a Nereid Dexamene (*Theogony* 240–264; *Library* 1.2.7).
