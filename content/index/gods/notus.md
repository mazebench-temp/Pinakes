Νότος (Notos), the south wind, probably related to νοτίς (“moisture”): the wind that brings rain and mist. The winds are gods in Homer, but the similes use them as weather.

{{quote:56df7537-849d-5a7b-bd20-a451bb3fd6af}}

With [[Eurus]], the East Wind, it heaps up the long waves of the [[Icarian Sea]], and the assembly heaves like them when Agamemnon tells the army to go home. See [[waves-of-the-icarian-sea]].

{{quote:de247400-f545-5cc1-a3a2-7b0f2978d266}}

When Agamemnon has finished his speech, the Argives shout like a wave that the South Wind drives against a headland. See [[wave-on-the-headland]].

## Elsewhere in Homer

Notus spreads mist on the mountain peaks (Iliad 3.10–11). With Eurus it pins Odysseus' ship on Thrinacia for a month (Odyssey 12.325–326), and the four winds strike his raft together (Odyssey 5.295–296).

## Outside Homer

Hesiod makes Notus, with Boreas and Zephyrus, a son of Eos and Astraeus (*Theogony* 378–380). The Romans called the south wind Auster.
