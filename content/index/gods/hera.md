## Etymology

Ἥρη (Hērē; Attic Ἥρα). The etymology is unknown. Links with ἥρως ("hero") and ὥρα ("season, the right time") have been proposed since antiquity, but none is secure.

## Family

{{quote:b23a2feb-2dca-5af6-8310-222370ec4f6f}}

Wife of [[Zeus]]. Homer calls her his ἄλοχος, "bedfellow, wife".

Mother of [[Hephaestus]], who calls her "my dear mother"

## Descriptions

λευκώλενος = λευκός (white) + ὠλένη (arm): [[white-armed]]. Her usual epithet, and a mark of a woman who does not work in the sun.

{{quote:974bb287-0bce-593c-bf7f-4bf5719c4944}}

βοῶπις πότνια = βοῦς (ox, cow) + ὤψ (eye, face), with πότνια (queen, lady): [[ox-eyed]], with the large calm eyes of a cow, a term of beauty

{{quote:a1a8c026-dc9e-5fcf-b27d-6ab921c3cb41}}

χρυσόθρονος = χρυσός (gold) + θρόνος (throne): of the golden throne. The book's last word.

## In Book 1

Hera acts first on earth and then in heaven. On the tenth day of the plague it is she who puts the assembly into Achilles' mind:

{{quote:d4414450-e9d5-5349-b7e3-778ccddc0b1b}}

When Achilles draws his sword on Agamemnon, she sends Athena, "loving both men alike in her heart and caring for them both" (1.196, 1.209). She wants neither leader of the Achaeans dead.

On Olympus she sees through Zeus at once. She has noticed Thetis at his knees and guesses the price:

{{quote:fb131b1f-c22d-5f03-9bf4-60667e2f4a9f}}

Zeus answers with a threat, and "ox-eyed queen Hera was afraid" (1.568). Her son Hephaestus reconciles them with a cup and a story, and she smiles (1.595–596).

Achilles names her among the three gods who once tried to bind Zeus (1.399–400). See [[binding-of-zeus]].

## Outside Homer

Hera is a daughter of Cronus and Rhea (Hesiod, *Theogony* 453–454). In the Iliad she names Argos, Sparta and Mycenae as the cities dearest to her (4.51–52). Her hatred of Troy is traced to the Judgment of Paris, which Homer mentions only once, late in the poem (24.25–30).
