## Etymology

The name seems to come from σπέος, “cave”, whose epic genitive is σπείους (*Odyssey* 5.68): the Nereids live in a cave in the depths of the sea, “the silver-white cave” (18.50).

## In Homer

She is named once in Homer, in the catalogue of the Nereids who gather around Thetis in the depths of the sea when Thetis hears Achilles groan for Patroclus (18.37–49; see [[nereids]]).

## Outside Homer

Hesiod names Speio, Thoe and Halie in the same order as the Iliad, in a verse that begins and ends differently: Κυμοθόη Σπειώ τε Θόη θʼ Ἀλίη τʼ ἐρόεσσα, “Cymothoe, Speio, Thoe and lovely Halie” (*Theogony* 245), against Νησαίη Σπειώ τε Θόη θʼ Ἁλίη τε βοῶπις (18.40; see [[thoe]] and [[halia]]). Apollodorus lists her (*Library* 1.2.7). Virgil names Spio among the sea goddesses who ride with Neptune and among the nymphs of Cyrene (*Aeneid* 5.826; *Georgics* 4.338).
