“Cadmus' daughter, fair-ankled Ino, Leucothea, who was once a mortal of human speech, but now in the depths of the sea has her share of honor from the gods” (*Odyssey* 5.333–335). She saw Odysseus on his raft in the storm and gave him her immortal veil to tie under his chest (5.333–353). The double nature, a woman who died and a goddess, made her cult a puzzle, which Xenophanes put to the people of Elea.
