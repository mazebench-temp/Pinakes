## Etymology

The name seems to be formed from δίδωμι, “give”, like δωτίνη, “gift”: the people of the seven towns that Agamemnon offers Achilles will honor him “with gifts like a god”, δωτίνῃσι θεὸν ὣς (9.155). Doto is “the giver”.

## In Homer

She is named once in Homer, in the catalogue of the Nereids who gather around Thetis in the depths of the sea when Thetis hears Achilles groan for Patroclus (18.37–49; see [[nereids]]). She opens a verse that the *Theogony* repeats word for word: Δωτώ τε Πρωτώ τε Φέρουσά τε Δυναμένη τε (18.43 = *Theogony* 248; see [[proto]], [[pherusa]] and [[dynamene]]).

## Outside Homer

Hesiod names her among the daughters of Nereus and Doris in the same verse (*Theogony* 248), and Apollodorus lists her (*Library* 1.2.7).
