## The name

Homer has the genitive Ῥείας (14.203) and the nominative Ῥέα (15.187); Hesiod writes Ῥείη. Plato's Socrates, who sees flux in the oldest names, takes Rhea and Cronus both for names of streams (*Cratylus* 402b).

## In Homer

{{quote:763594fd-a1dd-5dc3-8e8c-5361a1bbc3be}}

Hera says that Oceanus and Tethys reared her in their halls, “taking me from Rhea, when far-seeing Zeus put Cronus / down beneath the earth and the barren sea” (14.203–204; see [[tethys]], [[oceanus]] and [[fall-of-cronus]]). The poem implies that Rhea is her mother, as Hera is the “daughter of great Cronus” (14.194, 14.243; see [[hera]] and [[cronus]]). In Book 15 Poseidon says that he, Zeus and Hades are three brothers, “born of Cronus, whom Rhea bore”, who divided the world by lot (15.187–193; see [[poseidon]] and [[hades]]). Rhea does nothing herself in Homer.

## Outside Homer

Hesiod names Rhea among the Titans, the children of Earth and Heaven (*Theogony* 135). Yielding to Cronus, she bore Hestia, Demeter, Hera, Hades, Poseidon and Zeus, and Cronus swallowed each as it was born, having learned that a son would overthrow him; she grieved without end (453–467). On the advice of Earth and Heaven she bore Zeus in Crete and gave Cronus a stone wrapped in swaddling clothes to swallow in his place (468–491; see [[hesiod]]).
