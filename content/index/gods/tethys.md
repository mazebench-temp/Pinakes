## Etymology

Τηθύς (Tēthys). The name has no clear Greek etymology. Plato's Socrates takes it apart into “strained through” and “filtered”, as the name of a spring (*Cratylus* 402c–d). Tethys is not Thetis, the Nereid mother of Achilles, though the names look alike.

## Family

{{quote:763594fd-a1dd-5dc3-8e8c-5361a1bbc3be}}

Wife of [[Oceanus]], and with him the foster parent of Hera, who was given to them by Rhea when Zeus put Cronus beneath the earth (14.202–204; see [[hera]], [[rhea]] and [[cronus]]). Oceanus is “the origin of the gods” (θεῶν γένεσιν) and she is μήτηρ, “mother”: the pair stand at the beginning of the gods' line. For a long time, Hera says, “they have kept away from each other, / from the bed and from love, since anger has fallen upon their hearts” (14.206–207).

## In Homer

Tethys is named twice, in the same verse, both times in the story Hera tells to deceive. To Aphrodite, Hera says that she is going to the limits of the earth to see Oceanus and mother Tethys and to end their long quarrel, and she asks for love and longing to win them back to each other (14.198–210). To Zeus on Gargarus she tells the same story, word for word, and adds that her horses wait at the foot of Ida to carry her (14.301–311). The quarrel is her pretext for the journey (see [[the-deception-of-zeus]]).

## Outside Homer

Hesiod names Tethys among the Titans, the children of Earth and Heaven (*Theogony* 136). She bore to Oceanus the rivers, among them the rivers of the Troad, the Scamander and the Simois, the [[aesepus|Aesepus]], the [[granicus|Granicus]], the [[rhesus-river|Rhesus]] and the [[heptaporus|Heptaporus]], and three thousand daughters, the Oceanids (337–370; see [[hesiod]] and [[scamander]]). Plato's Socrates reads the verse “Oceanus, the origin of the gods, and mother Tethys” as Homer's teaching that all things are the offspring of flow and motion (*Theaetetus* 152e, 180c–d), and cites with it an Orphic verse in which Oceanus married his sister Tethys (*Cratylus* 402b–c; see [[plato]]). Aristotle reports that some thought the earliest theologians held Thales' view, “for they made Oceanus and Tethys the parents of becoming” (*Metaphysics* 1.3, 983b27–31; see [[aristotle]]).
