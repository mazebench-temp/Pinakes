## Etymology

The name seems to join κῦμα, “wave”, and θοός, “swift”: “swift on the wave”. Thoe, “swift”, is named in the verse before, and Amphithoe in the verse after (18.40, 18.42; see [[thoe]] and [[amphithoe]]).

## In Homer

She is named once in Homer, in the catalogue of the Nereids who gather around Thetis in the depths of the sea when Thetis hears Achilles groan for Patroclus (18.37–49; see [[nereids]]).

## Outside Homer

Hesiod names her first in the verse that goes on with Speio, Thoe and Halie (*Theogony* 245), and Apollodorus' list of the Nereids begins with her (*Library* 1.2.7). In Virgil, Cymothoe and Triton push the ships of Aeneas off the rock while Neptune calms Juno's storm (*Aeneid* 1.144–145; see [[virgil-neptunes-chariot]]).
