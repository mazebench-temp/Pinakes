## Etymology

Ποσειδάων (Poseidaōn), Attic Ποσειδῶν. The name is old: it appears on the Linear B tablets from Pylos as *po-se-da-o*. The first part is often read as πόσις, "lord, husband", and the second is disputed. One common guess is Δα-, "earth", which would make him "lord of the earth".

{{notes}}

## Elsewhere in Homer

Poseidon is a son of Cronus and Rhea (see [[rhea]]). When the sons of Cronus drew lots, he won the sea, Zeus the sky and Hades the dead (15.187–193; see [[the-lots-of-the-three-brothers]]). He favors the Achaeans throughout the Iliad and pursues Odysseus through the Odyssey.
