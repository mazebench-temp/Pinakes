## Etymology

Ποσειδάων (Poseidaōn), Attic Ποσειδῶν. The name is old: it appears on the Linear B tablets from Pylos as *po-se-da-o*. The first part is often read as πόσις, "lord, husband", and the second is disputed. One common guess is Δα-, "earth", which would make him "lord of the earth".

## In Book 1

{{quote:7ae841a3-358c-5b8c-a123-0b4d0f224872}}

Achilles names him with [[Hera]] and [[Athena]] among the Olympians who wanted to put Zeus in bonds. See [[binding-of-zeus]]. Some ancient commentators on 1.404 took [[Briareus]]' father, whom the hundred-hander surpasses in strength, to be Poseidon himself.

## Outside Homer

Poseidon is a son of Cronus and Rhea. When the sons of Cronus drew lots, he won the sea, Zeus the sky and Hades the dead (Iliad 15.187–193). He favors the Achaeans throughout the Iliad and pursues Odysseus through the Odyssey.
