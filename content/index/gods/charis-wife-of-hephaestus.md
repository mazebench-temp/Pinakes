## The name

Χάρις is the common word χάρις, “grace, charm, favor”, from the root of χαίρω, “rejoice” (see [[charis]]), made a person, as the Graces, the Χάριτες, are the same charm as a company of goddesses (see [[graces]]). Only the editor's capital tells the goddess from the word, as with Strife and Sleep (see [[strife]] and [[sleep]]). Walter Leaf calls her marriage to the craftsman god “a more transparent allegory than we find elsewhere in Homer”, and quotes the scholiast's reason, that grace must attend on craft (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 18.382; see the translation's note on 18.382).

## Family

{{quote:b616b71b-065c-5da4-a405-bb5e5310319e}}

Wife of [[Hephaestus]]: ὤπυιε, “had married” (see [[marriage]]). The Iliad names no parents for her. In the song of Demodocus in the Odyssey the wife of Hephaestus is Aphrodite, whom he catches in bed with Ares (*Odyssey* 8.266–366; see [[aphrodite]]).

## Descriptions

λιπαροκρήδεμνος = λιπαρός (“shining”) + κρήδεμνον (“headdress, veil”): “of the shining veil”, in Homer said of her alone (18.382; see [[of-the-shining-veil]]). καλή, “the lovely one”, stands at the head of the next verse (18.383). δῖα θεάων, “brilliant among goddesses”, ends the verse in which she leads Thetis in (18.388; see [[brilliant-among-goddesses]]).

## Outside Homer

Hesiod gives Hephaestus one of the Graces for his wife: Aglaea, “the youngest of the Graces”, whom “the famous god, lame in both legs” made his blooming wife (*Theogony* 945–946; see [[hesiod]]). Pausanias, writing of the Graces of Orchomenus, says that Homer mentions the Graces too, and says that one of them is the wife of Hephaestus, giving her the name Charis (Pausanias 9.35.4; see [[pasithea]]).

Diogenes Laertius tells that Plato, when he was about to compete with a tragedy, heard Socrates in front of the theater of Dionysus and burned his poems, saying “Ἥφαιστε, πρόμολʼ ὧδε· Πλάτων νύ τι σεῖο χατίζει”: her call to her husband, with Plato's name for Thetis' (Diogenes Laertius 3.5; see [[plato]]). Walter Leaf reports the story (on 18.392).
