## Etymology

The name seems to join ἀμφί, “around, on both sides”, and θοός, “swift” (see [[thoe]]). The name of Cymothoe, in the verse before (18.41), is formed in the same way, with κῦμα, “wave”, in place of ἀμφί (see [[cymothoe]]).

## In Homer

She is named once in Homer, in the catalogue of the Nereids who gather around Thetis in the depths of the sea when Thetis hears Achilles groan for Patroclus (18.37–49; see [[nereids]]).

## Outside Homer

Neither Hesiod nor Apollodorus names her. Both have a Hippothoe (Ἱπποθόη, *Theogony* 251; *Library* 1.2.7), and Apollodorus a Nausithoe.
