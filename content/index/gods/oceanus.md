## Etymology

Ὠκεανός (Ōkeanos). The etymology is unknown and probably not Greek. Our word "ocean" comes from it.

## Family

Husband of [[tethys|Tethys]], “mother Tethys”, with whom he reared Hera in his halls (14.201–204; see [[hera]]). His daughter in the Iliad is [[eurynome|Eurynome]], who with Thetis took in Hephaestus when he fell from heaven (18.398–399; see [[hephaestus]]).

## In Homer

Oceanus is a river, ποταμός (14.245, 18.607), that runs around the edge of the world. On Achilles' shield it runs around the rim (18.607–608). The sun rises out of it and sets in it (7.421–423, 8.485), and the Ethiopians, whom the gods visit, live beside it (1.423–424). It alone of the rivers does not come to the assembly of the gods in Book 20 (20.7). Achilles calls it the source of all rivers, of the whole sea and of all springs and wells, but no match for Zeus (21.193–199), and Hera and Sleep call it the origin of the gods and of all (14.201, 14.246).

## Outside Homer

Hesiod names Oceanus first among the children of Earth and Heaven, the Titans (*Theogony* 133). With Tethys he fathers the rivers and three thousand daughters, the Oceanids (337–370; see [[tethys]]), the eldest of whom is Styx (776–777; see [[styx]]). Plato's Socrates took the verse of 14.201 as Homer's teaching that all things are born of flow and motion (*Theaetetus* 152e; see [[plato]]), and Aristotle reports that some made the earliest theologians forerunners of Thales, since they made Oceanus and Tethys the parents of becoming (*Metaphysics* 1.3, 983b27–31; see [[aristotle]]).
