## Etymology

Μοῦσα (Mousa), plural Μοῦσαι. The etymology is uncertain. It is often connected with the root *men-* "mind, remember", which would make the Muses goddesses of memory, fitting their mother Mnemosyne ("Memory") in Hesiod.

## Outside Homer

The half-line of 1.604, ἀμειβόμεναι ὀπὶ καλῇ, returns once more in Homer. At Achilles' funeral "all nine Muses, answering one another with lovely voices, sang the lament" (Odyssey 24.60–61). The voices that sing at the gods' feast in Book 1 will sing for Achilles when he dies.

Hesiod names the nine Muses, daughters of Zeus and Mnemosyne (*Theogony* 53–79). He says they sing "the things that are, the things to come, and the things that were before" (*Theogony* 38), the same line Homer uses of the seer [[Calchas]] (1.70). See [[calchas-and-the-muses]].
