## Etymology

The name is probably formed from γάλα, “milk”, as in γάλα λευκόν, “white milk” (4.434): “the milk-white”.

## In Homer

She is named once in Homer, in the catalogue of the Nereids who gather around Thetis in the depths of the sea when Thetis hears Achilles groan for Patroclus (18.37–49; see [[nereids]]). She is one of three in the catalogue with an epithet, with Halia and Amatheia: ἀγακλειτή, “famous” (see [[halia]], [[amatheia]] and [[famous]]). The Iliad gives the word otherwise to men, to Capaneus, to the Trojans' allies, to Thrasymelus and to the gatekeepers of Troy (2.564, 12.101, 16.463, 21.530); she is the only woman or goddess in Homer to be called ἀγακλειτή.

## Outside Homer

Hesiod names “fair Galateia” (εὐειδὴς Γαλάτεια) among the daughters of Nereus and Doris (*Theogony* 250), and Apollodorus lists her (*Library* 1.2.7). In later poetry she is the sea nymph whom the Cyclops Polyphemus loved (see [[polyphemus-the-cyclops]] and [[cyclops-dithyrambs]]). In Ovid she tells the story herself to Scylla: she, “whose father is Nereus, whom sea-blue Doris bore”, loved the young Acis; the Cyclops, finding them together, buried Acis under a mass torn from the mountain, and she made him a river (*Metamorphoses* 13.738–897; see [[ovid]]).
