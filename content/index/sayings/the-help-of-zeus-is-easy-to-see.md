**The sign.** Teucer has shot down Cleitus, Polydamas' charioteer, and takes another arrow for Hector; he would have ended Hector's fighting at the ships, “But he did not escape the shrewd mind of Zeus, who was guarding / Hector”: the new-twisted string breaks as he draws, the arrow goes astray and the bow falls from his hand (15.458–465; see [[teucer]] and [[bow]]). Both sides see it. Teucer says that a god is cutting short their plans of battle (δαίμων, 15.467–470; see [[daimon]]), and Ajax that “a god has confounded them, begrudging the Danaans” (15.473). When Hector shears off Ajax's spear-point in the next book, Ajax himself knows it as the work of the gods in the words Teucer used here: Zeus was cutting short their plans of battle (πάγχυ μάχης ἐπὶ μήδεα κεῖρε, 16.119–121). **Easily known.** ἀρίγνωτος, “easy to recognize”, is a word of plain sight; in the Odyssey it is said of Artemis, who stands out among her nymphs (see [[seeing-the-gods]]). ἀλκή is the strength that wards off (see [[alke]]), and Zeus' ἀλκή is the protection he gives or withholds: in Book 8 Nestor asked Diomedes, “Do you not see that no help from Zeus goes with you?” (8.140). Hector's maxim has two sides, “the higher glory” that Zeus puts into some men's hands (κῦδος ὑπέρτερον ἐγγυαλίξῃ, 15.491; see [[kydos]]) and the might he diminishes in others, “as now he diminishes the might of the Argives, and helps us” (15.493; see [[menos]]). What a god does in secret, a broken string makes visible to both armies.

**What Hector does not know.** The narrator confirms the sign: Zeus was guarding Hector (15.461–462). But he also tells what Hector cannot see. Zeus wishes to give Hector glory only until a ship burns, to fulfil Thetis' prayer, and then he will turn the battle back from the ships; Hector is short-lived, and Athena is already rousing his fated day (15.592–614; see [[plan-of-zeus]] and [[hector]]). Hector reads the help of Zeus as easily as he says, and takes it for more than it is. In Book 17 Ajax reads the same kind of sign from the other side: “even one who is an utter fool” can see that father Zeus is helping the Trojans, whose every missile strikes home while those of the Achaeans fall to the ground in vain (17.629–633; see [[even-a-fool-can-see]]).

**The exhortation.** The maxim is the center of the speech that follows it. From the sign Hector draws the order to fight at the ships in a body, and the consolation that whoever dies dies for his native land (15.494–499; see [[it-is-not-unseemly-to-die-for-ones-country]] and [[exhortation]]).
