## The phrase

A poet who sees the action as if he were present at it will find what is fitting and will be least likely to miss contradictions. Carcinus failed to: his Amphiaraus came back up from the temple, a slip that escaped the reader, but on the stage the audience was annoyed and the play failed (see [[carcinus]] and [[amphiaraus]]). In the *Rhetoric* metaphors “set things before the eyes” when they signify things in activity, and Aristotle's examples are Homer's, who gives life to lifeless things: the arrow “eager to fly into the throng” (4.126), the spears that stood fixed in the ground, “longing to glut themselves on flesh” (11.574), the shameless stone of Sisyphus (*Rhetoric* 3.11, 1411b24–1412a3; see [[metaphora]]).
