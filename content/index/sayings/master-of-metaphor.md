The sentence closes Aristotle's account of diction (see [[metaphora]]). It makes metaphor a gift of nature (εὐφυΐα), like the poet's gift in chapter 17 (see [[the-gifted-or-the-mad]]), and a kind of insight: τὸ ὅμοιον θεωρεῖν, “to see likeness”, the same verb Aristotle uses for philosophical contemplation. The *Rhetoric* says that metaphor should be drawn from things related but not obvious, “as in philosophy too it is the mark of a sharp mind to see the likeness even in things far apart” (*Rhetoric* 3.11, 1412a12–13).
