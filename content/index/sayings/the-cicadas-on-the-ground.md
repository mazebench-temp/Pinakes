{{quote:7d52e88a-e035-5751-bfa2-8e3fc0097513}}

Cicadas sing in trees. If an enemy invaded and cut down the orchards and olives, they would sing from the ground. Aristotle gives the saying as an example of the riddling and Laconic sayings that suit maxims about what is not paradoxical but unclear (*Rhetoric* 2.21, 1394b33–1395a2), and returns to it in the third book among the riddles that are a kind of metaphor (3.11, 1412a22–23). See [[stesichorus]], [[locri]] and [[gnome]].
