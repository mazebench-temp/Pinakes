{{quote:836e20ea-a0df-5a3c-b96c-dacb08847e3a}}

The Dream greets Agamemnon with a reproach and a maxim. A βουληφόρος ἀνήρ, “a man of counsel”, is a leader who sits in council and decides for others (see [[man-of-counsel]] and [[boule]]). The people (λαοί) have been entrusted to him, and he has “so much to care for”. Such a man should not sleep παννύχιος, “all night long”.

The maxim turns back on the opening of the book. The other gods and men “slept all night long” (εὗδον παννύχιοι, 2.2), but sleep did not hold Zeus, who was pondering how to honor Achilles and destroy many Achaeans (2.1–4; see [[all-the-gods-slept]]). The one who lies awake with cares is the god, and his care is the sleeper's ruin. The rebuke is true, and it opens a lie: it wins Agamemnon's trust for the false promise that follows (see [[agamemnons-dream]] and [[pseudos]]). He repeats it word for word when he tells the council his dream (2.60–62; see [[message-repeated]]).

In Book 10, after a day of defeat, the pattern is reversed. The other chiefs of the Achaeans sleep all night, but sweet sleep does not hold Agamemnon, who lies awake with many cares (10.1–4). The king now keeps the watch that the Dream demanded of him. See [[hypnos]].
