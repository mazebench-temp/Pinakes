## The figure

The analogy has four terms: cup : Dionysus :: shield : Ares. The poet may say the fourth for the second (calling the cup a shield) and then add the term to which the replaced word belongs (“of Dionysus”), which keeps the transfer intelligible. The variant at 1457b30–32 names the shield a cup and denies it what belongs to cups: “not of Ares but wineless”. The shallow wine bowl (see [[phiale]]) and the round shield (see [[shield]]) share a shape, and the comparison of the warrior's shield to the drinker's bowl was a favorite of later poets and rhetoricians; the *Rhetoric* returns to it (3.4, 1407a15–18).
