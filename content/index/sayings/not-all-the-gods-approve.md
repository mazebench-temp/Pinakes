Zeus has proposed that the war end with Menelaus' victory. Hera protests that he would waste her labor and the sweat of her horses, and ends with a dare: “Do it; but not all of us other gods approve.” The line gives Zeus leave and warns him at once. He can do as he likes, since he is the strongest; the other gods will remember that he did it against them.

The line comes twice more in the Iliad, both times when Zeus thinks of saving a mortal he loves from his fate. Hera says it when Zeus wonders whether to snatch Sarpedon from death (16.443), and Athena when he pities Hector running around the walls (22.181). Each time Zeus gives way. To Athena he answers much as he answered Hera in Book 4, “Do as you wish” (ἕρξον ὅπως ἐθέλεις, 4.37; ἕρξον ὅπῃ δή τοι νόος ἔπλετο, 22.185). The warning works: the gods' approval, which Zeus does not need, is what he will not do without. See [[zeus]], [[hera]] and [[the-gods-mirror-the-mortals]].
