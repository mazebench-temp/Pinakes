summary: A priestess would not let her son speak in the assembly: if he said what was just, men would hate him, and if what was unjust, the gods. The answer turns the dilemma round: if he says what is just, the gods will love him, and if unjust, men.

{{quote:b9e89f2f-c4c0-5034-81ff-a9658b82ef54}}

Aristotle's topic is the dilemma in which good and bad follow each of two opposites, and the counter-dilemma that answers it with the opposite pairs. It is the same, he says, as the saying about buying the marsh and the salt, and he calls it βλαίσωσις, a word for being bow-legged, here the bandying of a dilemma back on its maker (*Rhetoric* 2.23, 1399a19–29). See [[topics-of-enthymemes]].
