Simonides spent his last years at Hieron's court and had a name for loving money. Aristotle tells the story to show that the rich are flattered, since many need them (*Rhetoric* 2.16, 1391a6–12). Plato's Socrates knows the saying that the wise go to the doors of the rich and calls its author a liar: the sick must go to the doctor's door, and those who need ruling to the man who can rule (*Republic* 6, 489b–c). See [[simonides]], [[hieron]] and [[the-gifts-of-fortune]].
