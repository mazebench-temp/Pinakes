The honey of speech became a commonplace. Hesiod says the Muses pour sweet dew on the tongue of the king they favor, "and from his mouth his words flow sweet" (*Theogony* 81–84). Cicero quoted Nestor's honey as proof that old age keeps its eloquence; see [[cicero-nestor-honey]]. In Book 1 the sweetness does not work, and both men ignore the advice. See [[nestor]].
