Diomedes has gone back to the ships with Paris' arrow in his foot, and “rout had seized” the other Argives; Odysseus is alone, with the Trojans coming on (11.399–402). He speaks to his own heart, and the speech has two parts. First he weighs two evils, as a man does who is torn two ways: “It is a great evil if I flee, fearing the multitude; but it is worse if I am caught alone” (11.404–406; see [[pondering-two-ways]] and [[deeply-troubled]]). Then he stops the weighing with a verse that the poem gives to every such monologue, “But why does my heart debate these things with me?” (11.407; also 17.97, 21.562, 22.122, 22.385), and puts a rule in its place.

The rule sets two kinds of men side by side. κακοί, “the bad”, are the cowards, the men who leave the war; the other is the man who would be best, ὃς δέ κʼ ἀριστεύῃσι, and for him there is only one course, to stand “strongly” (κρατερῶς; see [[aristos]] and [[always-to-be-the-best]]). The last half-verse admits what the course costs: “whether he is struck or strikes another”. Sarpedon will tell Glaucus the same in other words, “let us go, and either hold out triumph to another, or another to us” (12.328), and Nestor will call to the Achaeans at the ships “to stand strongly, and not turn to flight” (ἑστάμεναι κρατερῶς, 15.666). In Odysseus' mouth the rule is not addressed to an army. No one hears it.

It holds. Odysseus stands, the Trojans close around him like dogs and young men around a boar, and he kills or wounds five of them before Socus wounds him (11.411–438; see [[like-a-boar]]). Wounded, he gives ground backward and shouts for help (11.461), and the rule is not broken: it was made against flight, and a man who calls his companions is still fighting. Of the four men in the poem who argue with their heart over whether to stand or yield, Menelaus gives way to Hector over the body of Patroclus (17.91–108), and Agenor and Hector stand against Achilles (21.553–570, 22.98–130); Odysseus, the first, is the one whose answer is a maxim (see [[retreat]] and [[odysseus]]).
