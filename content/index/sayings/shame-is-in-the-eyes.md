{{quote:75b9d04e-ed78-5be3-98c1-7c56f6199397}}

In Euripides a character says that “shame is born in the eyes” (*Cresphontes*, fr. 457), and the thought is old: Homer's warriors are shamed by being seen (see [[aidos]] and [[eyes]]). Aristotle uses the proverb to explain why people feel more shame before those who will always be with them and those who pay attention to them: in both cases they are under the eyes of others (*Rhetoric* 2.6, 1384a33–36). Cydias' image of the Greeks standing around the Athenian assembly is the same idea put to work (see [[cydias]]). See [[aischyne]].
