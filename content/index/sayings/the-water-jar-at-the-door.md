---
title: “The water jar at the door”
greek: τὸ ἐπὶ θύραις τὴν ὑδρίαν
kind: saying
tags: [the-good]
summary: A proverb of labor lost at the last moment, the water jar broken at the door after it has been carried all the way from the spring; Aristotle cites it for the topic that what has cost much toil is a good.
aliases: [the pitcher at the door, to break the pitcher at the door]
---

Aristotle gives only the words “the water jar at the door”. Freese supplies the verb, “to break the pitcher at the door”, explains the proverb as lost labor, and compares the French *faire naufrage au port*, to be shipwrecked in port, and the English “there's many a slip 'twixt the cup and the lip”.
