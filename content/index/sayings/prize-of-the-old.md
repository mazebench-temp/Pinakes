{{quote:e11a5ff3-2647-523f-8a92-c6c75fdc877b}}

Nestor answers Agamemnon's wish that he were young again. He cannot fight as he fought when he killed Ereuthalion, but he will be among the horsemen and give them orders “with counsel and with words (βουλῇ καὶ μύθοισι); for that is the prize of the old”. The spears will be wielded by the younger men, who trust in their strength (4.322–325).

A [[geras|γέρας]] is a prize of honor, the share set apart for a man as a mark of his standing, and in Book 1 it was a captive woman. Nestor's γέρας is a function: the right to be heard. The line sets γέρας beside γέρων, “old man”, as if the prize belonged to the old by name; whether the two words are related is uncertain (see [[geron]]).

The half-line is a formula. Achilles uses it in Book 9 when he sends the envoys back with his answer, “for that is the prize of the old” (9.421–422). With one word changed it belongs to the dead: a mound and a gravestone, “for that is the prize of the dead” (τὸ γὰρ γέρας ἐστὶ θανόντων), is the burial Hera proposes for Sarpedon and Zeus orders for him (16.457, 16.675), and the Odyssey uses the line for the laments over the dead and the closing of their eyes (*Odyssey* 24.190, 24.296). Old age and death each have their due, and the formula names both. See [[old-age-and-memory]], [[all-things-at-once]] and [[nestor]].
