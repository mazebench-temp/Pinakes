## The definition

The definition looks trivial and is not. A beginning is not just the first thing told but what “does not itself necessarily follow something else”, while something else follows it; an end follows something else, necessarily or as a rule, and has nothing after it; a middle follows and is followed (1450b27–31). The terms are causal: they describe a sequence in which each part is required by what comes before it. So “well-constructed plots must neither begin wherever it happens nor end wherever it happens” (1450b32–34). The same words return in the chapter on epic, whose plots must be about one action “with a beginning, middle parts and an end, so that, like one whole living creature, it may produce its proper pleasure” (1459a18–21; see [[unity-of-action]]).
