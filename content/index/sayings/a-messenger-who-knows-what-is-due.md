**The messenger's part.** Zeus sent Iris with the verse with which he once sent her against Hera and Athena, “Go, swift Iris” (15.158 = 8.399), and with a warning: she is to tell Poseidon everything, “and do not be a false messenger” (μὴ δὲ ψευδάγγελος εἶναι, 15.159; the compound only here in Homer). She keeps to his words (15.174–183; see [[message-repeated]]). When Poseidon refuses, she does more than her message. She asks whether she is really to carry so harsh an answer, or whether he will turn it, and gives him two maxims, “The hearts of good men can be turned” and “You know how the Erinyes always follow the elder” (15.201–204; see [[the-hearts-of-good-men-can-be-turned]] and [[the-erinyes-follow-the-elder]]). Poseidon praises the word as “fair and right” (κατὰ μοῖραν, 15.206; see [[moira]]), and the messenger who spoke it.

**Knowing what is due.** αἴσιμα are things in their due measure, what is right and fitting (see [[aisa]]); the narrator says Agamemnon turned his brother's mind “urging what was due” (αἴσιμα παρειπών, 6.62, 7.121). To “know” such things is Homer's way of naming a disposition rather than information. In the Odyssey the swineherd Eumaeus, carving the meat, “knew what was right in his mind” (περὶ γὰρ φρεσὶν αἴσιμα ᾔδη, *Odyssey* 14.433), and Mentor and Athena wish that no king should be kind and gentle or know what is right, since Odysseus' people have forgotten him (*Odyssey* 2.230–234 = 5.8–12). A messenger who knows what is due is one with a sense of fitness: she carries the words given her, and knows when to soften what she carries back.

**Other messengers.** The contrast is with Iris' other errand to a rebel. In *Iliad* 8 she delivered Zeus' threat to Hera and Athena word for word and then added an insult of her own to Athena, “you shameless dog” (8.413–424; see [[aidos]]). Here what she adds is counsel. Poseidon calls her ἄγγελος, and the narrator introduces her as the go-between of the gods, μετάγγελος (15.144; see [[iris]] and [[messenger-scene]]); among men the word is used of Tydeus sent to Thebes (5.804) and of Patroclus going back to Achilles with Nestor's word (11.652; see [[embassy]]).
