---
title: The most imitative of animals
greek: μιμητικώτατόν ἐστι
kind: saying
tags: [imitation]
summary: Aristotle's claim that imitation is innate in human beings from childhood, and that man differs from the other animals in being the most imitative of them and in learning his first lessons through imitation.
aliases: [man the most imitative animal]
---

{{quote:4623b112-54b9-5bd1-af5d-9af883e556dd}}

The saying makes imitation a mark of human nature and the beginning of learning; Aristotle draws from it the first of the two natural causes of poetry (see [[origins-of-poetry]] and [[mimesis]]). Democritus had made the opposite point, that human beings are the pupils of animals, learning song from the swan and the nightingale by imitation (fr. 154 DK). Aristotle's word for the ape, the animal that imitates, served as an insult for an actor who overdid it (see [[mynniscus-and-callippides]]).
