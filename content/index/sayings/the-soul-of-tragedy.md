## The argument

Of the six parts of tragedy, the most important is the structure of the incidents, for tragedy is an imitation not of people but of action and life (see [[six-parts-of-tragedy]], [[mythos]] and [[praxis]]). People do not act in order to display their characters; they take on characters for the sake of the action. There could be no tragedy without action, but there could be one without character, and most of the moderns write such tragedies. A row of fine speeches will not do the work of tragedy, while a play with a plot will, and the greatest means by which tragedy sways the soul, reversals and recognitions, are parts of the plot; beginners master diction and character before they can construct incidents (1450a15–38). Character, thought, diction, song and spectacle follow in that order (see [[ethos]] and [[dianoia]]).

Modern readers, for whom a tragedy meant its great characters, often reversed the order, and the saying remains the classic statement of the opposite view.
