{{quote:23f3728d-ba36-5748-9c8d-cbee148ea8be}}

Agamemnon sends Talthybius for Machaon, to see Menelaus, “whom someone well skilled with the bow has hit with an arrow, one of the Trojans or Lycians”. The herald finds Machaon among his men and gives the message in the same words (4.204–207). The line divides in two equal halves joined by μέν and δέ, “for him … for us”. What is [[kleos|κλέος]] for one side is [[penthos|πένθος]] for the other: the same event, told by the enemy as fame and mourned by the Achaeans as grief.

The archer is “someone” (τις). Agamemnon does not know his name, and the fame he grants him is anonymous. It is the fame Athena promised Pandarus as thanks and glory from the Trojans (4.95), and it comes to him even though the shot broke a truce. See [[pandarus]] and [[charis]].

Diomedes weighs the same balance at the end of the review, for the king himself: “glory will go with him if the Achaeans … take sacred Ilios, and great grief will be his if the Achaeans are cut down” (4.415–417). See [[kydos]].

## Outside Homer

Virgil, retelling the broken truce in the last book of the *Aeneid*, denies his archer both: no one knew who shot the arrow that wounded Aeneas, and no one boasted of it (see [[virgil-duel-for-lavinia]]).
