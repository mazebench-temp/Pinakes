---
title: “A fool, who kills the father and leaves the sons”

kind: saying
tags: [law, epic-cycle]
summary: A verse that ancient tradition gave to Stasinus' Cypria, quoted as a proverb: whoever kills a father must not spare the sons, who will take revenge. Aristotle quotes it twice in the Rhetoric.
aliases: [a fool who slays the father and leaves the children]
source: Stasinus, Cypria (according to Clement of Alexandria, Stromata 6.2.19)
of: [cypria]
---

Clement of Alexandria quotes the verse as Stasinus' and says that Herodotus borrowed it (*Stromata* 6.2.19); in Herodotus Cyrus says that he has acted like a man who killed the father and spared the sons (1.155.1). Its place in the *Cypria* is lost (see [[cypria]]). In the *Rhetoric*'s second book Aristotle quotes it, with “children” for “sons”, as a common maxim to use for urging the destruction of an enemy's innocent children (2.21, 1395a16–18; see [[gnome]]).
