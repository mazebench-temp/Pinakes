Hector has told Andromache that no man escapes his fate (6.486–489; see [[no-man-escapes-his-fate]]). Now he sends her home. The work he gives her is the work of the house, the loom (ἱστός) and the distaff (ἠλακάτη), and the direction of the handmaids (see [[weaving]] and [[handmaids]]). The war is given to the men, “all the men who were born in Ilios, and mine most of all”. μέλω, “be a care, be the concern of”, divides the labor of the city: the men will see to the fighting, the women to the house. The line answers Andromache's advice to post the army by the fig tree, which Hector passed over (6.433–439; see [[andromaches-advice]]), and her plea that he stay (6.431). The strategy of the war is not hers to plan. See [[hector-and-andromache]] and [[women-and-war]].

**The same verses in the Odyssey.** Telemachus twice sends Penelope from the hall in these verses, with one word changed. When she asks the singer Phemius to stop his song of the Achaeans' return, he tells her to go to her loom, “and speech will be the concern of men (μῦθος δʼ ἄνδρεσσι μελήσει), all of them, and mine most of all; for mine is the power in the house” (*Odyssey* 1.356–359). When she asks that the beggar be allowed to try the bow, he says the same, with “the bow” (τόξον) in place of speech (*Odyssey* 21.350–353). Alcinous uses the half-line of Odysseus' escort home, “the escort will be the concern of men” (*Odyssey* 11.352–353). In Homer the formula names a man's sphere, and what goes into it depends on the scene: war in Troy, speech and the bow in Ithaca.

**Gods and men.** The division can also run between gods and mortals. Before the battle of the gods, Poseidon proposes that the gods sit apart on a lookout, “and war will be the concern of men” (πόλεμος δʼ ἄνδρεσσι μελήσει, 20.136–137). Zeus had drawn a line among the gods with the same verb when he sent Aphrodite from the war: “all these things will be the business of swift Ares and Athena” (5.428–430; see [[the-works-of-war]]).

## Outside Homer

Aristophanes' Lysistrata quotes the line as her husband's answer whenever she asked about the war, and turns it round when the women take the Acropolis: “war will be the concern of women” (πόλεμος δὲ γυναιξὶ μελήσει, *Lysistrata* 520, 538).
