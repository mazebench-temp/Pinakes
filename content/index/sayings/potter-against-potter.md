Hesiod distinguishes two kinds of Strife. The good one rouses even the idle to work, when he sees his neighbor getting rich, “and potter is jealous of potter, and craftsman of craftsman, and beggar envies beggar, and singer singer” (*Works and Days* 11–26). For Hesiod the rivalry is useful; in Aristotle it explains why people do not love those who live by the same trade, and why they envy their competitors most (*Rhetoric* 2.4, 1381b15–17; 2.10, 1388a14–17). In the *Nicomachean Ethics* the proverb is the view of those who think that friendship is between unlikes: “they say that all such people are potters to one another” (*Nicomachean Ethics* 8.1, 1155a35–b1).
