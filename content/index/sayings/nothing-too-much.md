Plato's Socrates says that the Seven Sages met at Delphi and dedicated to Apollo the first fruits of their wisdom, the sayings “Know yourself” and “Nothing too much”, which everyone repeats (*Protagoras* 343a–b; see [[know-yourself]]). Aristotle twice treats it as common property. The young break it, for “they do everything too much” (*Rhetoric* 2.12, 1389b2–5); and because it is so well known, a speaker can show his character by contradicting it: “Nor do I like ‘Nothing too much’; for the bad, at least, one should hate too much” (*Rhetoric* 2.21, 1395a32–33). See [[chilon]], [[gnome]] and [[pytho]].
