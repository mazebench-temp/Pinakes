{{quote:5138b2fe-7b43-5b65-8872-95dd551a8a6f}}

Athena has just mocked Aphrodite before Zeus: she must have been urging some Achaean woman to run off with the Trojans she loves, and scratched her slender hand on a golden brooch as she caressed her (5.418–425). Zeus smiles (5.426), calls Aphrodite to him and gives her this answer, gentle and final. δέδοται, “are given”, is the language of allotment, the word in which the gods' provinces are assigned and a man's gifts are said to come from a god (see [[gifts-of-the-gods]] and [[strength-is-a-gift]]). Aphrodite's province is ἔργα γάμοιο, “the works of marriage”, a phrase found only here. War belongs to Ares and Athena, the two gods of battle whom the rest of the book sets against each other (see [[ares]] and [[athena]]). Diomedes has told her the same thing more roughly: “Keep away, daughter of Zeus, from war and combat” (5.348). The mortal, the rival and the father agree about where she belongs.

**Among men.** The division of labor between the gods is the one the poem draws between men and women. Nestor tells the Achaeans in assembly that they talk like children, “foolish little ones, who care nothing for the works of war” (2.337–338), and Hector tells Ajax not to try him like a feeble boy, or “a woman, who does not know the works of war” (7.235–236). In the next book Hector sends Andromache back to her loom and distaff with the verb Zeus uses here: “war will be the concern of men” (πόλεμος δʼ ἄνδρεσσι μελήσει, 6.490–493). In the Odyssey Telemachus sends his mother back to her loom in the same verses, with speech and then the bow in the place of war (*Odyssey* 1.356–359, 21.350–353). Polydamas tells Hector that a god gives the works of war to one man, to another dancing, to another the lyre and song, and to another a good mind (13.726–734). What Zeus says of the gods, the poem says of every man and woman: each has a portion, and does well to keep to it. See [[husbands-and-wives]] and [[the-gods-mirror-the-mortals]].

Aphrodite does not keep to it for long. In the battle of the gods she leads the wounded Ares out of the fighting, and Athena knocks her down with a blow to the chest (21.416–426).
