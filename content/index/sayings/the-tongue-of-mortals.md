Aeneas has told his lineage and said that prowess is Zeus' to give (20.213–243; see [[zeus-increases-and-diminishes]]). Now he would stop talking “like children” in the middle of the battle (20.244–245). Insults prove nothing: both men have more of them than a ship could carry (see [[oneidos]]), and the tongue can say anything at all.

**The tongue.** στρεπτός is “turnable”, and Walter Leaf renders it “voluble”, capable of turning easily this way or that, and so of uttering words of every sort (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 20.248). The adjective is said elsewhere of the gods, whom offerings and prayer can turn (9.497; see [[even-the-gods-can-be-turned]]), and of the hearts of good men (15.203; see [[the-hearts-of-good-men-can-be-turned]]); here it is said of speech itself.

**The pasture.** Everywhere else in Homer νομός is a pasture, of flocks or horses (2.475, 6.511, 18.587). Leaf takes it here as the field of words, the pasture-ground in which they find their food: the range of insult is very wide. The scholia explained it as the portioning out of words, abundant on either side, which comes to the same; and νόμος, “law”, is not an epic word at all (Leaf on 20.249; see the translation's note on 20.249). Leaf also reports Fäsi's comparison with the winged words that fly from man to man (see [[winged-words]]).

The next verse turns the picture into a rule of exchange: “Whatever kind of word you speak, such will you hear back” (20.250; see [[such-will-you-hear-back]] and [[words-and-violence]]).

## Outside Homer

Hesiod uses the phrase of a man who begs too often from his neighbors: he will talk much in vain, “and useless will be the pasture of your words” (ἀχρεῖος δʼ ἔσται ἐπέων νομός, *Works and Days* 403), which Leaf thinks does not explain the Homeric phrase (on 20.249). Leaf also compares the Hymn to Apollo, which he quotes as νομοὶ … ᾠδῆς; Evelyn-White's text reads νόμοι … ἀοιδοῖς (*Homeric Hymn to Apollo* 20). Hesiod's counsel about the tongue is the opposite of Aeneas' account of it: “the best treasure among men is a sparing tongue” (*Works and Days* 719–720; see [[hesiod]]).
