**The occasion.** Ajax's spear, thrown at Polydamas, has killed Archelochus, son of Antenor. As the Boeotian Promachus drags the body by the feet, Archelochus' brother Acamas, standing over it, stabs him, and boasts to the Argives that “the toil and the misery” will not be the Trojans' alone, and that he has killed Promachus so that the penalty for his brother may not go unpaid (14.463–484; see [[acamas]], [[promachus]], [[archelochus]] and [[poine]]). Then the boast becomes a maxim with τις, “a man”: anyone would pray for what Acamas has just done.

**The words.** γνωτός, “known”, is a kinsman, often a brother (see [[kasignetos]]). He is left ἐνὶ μεγάροισιν, “in the halls”, at home, to outlive the man who prays for him. ἀλκτήρ is “defender”, from the root of ἀλκή and ἀλέξω, “ward off” (see [[alke]]), and ἀρή, “disaster”, is the ruin that falls on a house (see the translation's note on 12.334). A man's protection against his own death is that someone of his blood will survive to pay it back. Achilles uses the same words of his failure: Patroclus perished far from his country, and needed him “to be a defender against disaster” (ἀρῆς ἀλκτῆρα γενέσθαι, 18.98–100).

**The other rule.** In Book 9 Ajax gave the custom of peace in the same form, a maxim with τις: “a man accepts from the killer of his brother / the penalty, or for his own son who is dead; / and the killer stays there among his people, having paid much” (9.632–634; see [[the-embassy-to-achilles]]). Acamas gives the custom of war, in which the penalty is taken in blood and at once, and from whoever is nearest: Promachus did not kill Archelochus. In the Odyssey the same reckoning works in both directions. Odysseus reminds Telemachus that a man who has killed even one man in the land, one with few avengers left behind him, goes into exile, and they have killed the best young men of Ithaca (*Odyssey* 23.118–122); and Eupeithes tells the people that it would be a disgrace for later men to hear of it if they did not punish the killers of their sons and brothers (*Odyssey* 24.433–436).
