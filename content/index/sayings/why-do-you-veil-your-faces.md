tags: [shame, emotions]
summary: Antiphon the tragic poet, on his way to execution with others, mocked them for hiding their faces from the crowd: was it so that none of the onlookers would see them tomorrow?

{{quote:eb713d7a-a808-50a5-a975-97da51fccc2d}}

The jest makes Aristotle's point about shame: it is felt before people one will meet again, and those about to die will not meet the onlookers tomorrow (*Rhetoric* 2.6, 1385a7–13). Antiphon was put to death by Dionysius I of Syracuse (see [[antiphon-the-tragic-poet]] and [[dionysius-of-syracuse]]). The word ἀποτυμπανίζεσθαι means execution on the plank, the τύμπανον, on which condemned men were fastened to die. See [[aischyne]].
