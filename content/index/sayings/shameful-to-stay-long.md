{{quote:117dd526-3442-5a8b-bb75-ee019e5d8b9d}}

The line is the hinge of Odysseus' speech to the assembly. He has granted the men everything. A man away from his wife for a single month chafes beside his ship when the winter storms keep him penned in, and this is the ninth year; “so I do not blame the Achaeans” (2.291–297; see [[nemesis]]). Then, “all the same” (ἀλλὰ καὶ ἔμπης), comes the maxim. It is αἰσχρόν, “shameful” (see [[aischros]]), both to stay long and to go home κενεόν, “empty”, with nothing to show. The two halves are bound by τε … τε: the length of the stay is what makes an empty return disgraceful. Nine years of waiting can be redeemed only by the spoils of Troy.

Agamemnon had used the same argument in the test, while telling the army to flee: it is “a shameful thing, even for men to come to hear of”, that so great an army should fight in vain (2.119–122). Odysseus makes it an argument for staying. He follows it with the proof that they will not stay in vain, the portent at Aulis (2.299–332; see [[the-portent-at-aulis]]).

In the Odyssey the same man says the same thing of himself. Among the Phaeacians he would stay a whole year, if it meant coming home with a fuller hand, for he would be more respected and more loved by all who saw him return to Ithaca (Odyssey 11.355–361). See [[nostos]].
