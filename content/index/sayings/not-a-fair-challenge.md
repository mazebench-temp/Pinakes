---
title: “Not a fair challenge, from the impious to the pious”
greek: οὐκ ἴση πρόκλησις αὕτη τἀσεβεῖ πρὸς εὐσεβῆ
kind: saying
tags: [law, oath, philosopher]
summary: Xenophanes' saying that for an impious man to challenge a pious one to an oath is unfair, like a strong man challenging a weak one to strike or be struck; Aristotle shows how to use it and how to turn it around.
source: Xenophanes (Aristotle, Rhetoric 1.15, 1377a19–20)
of: [xenophanes, oath]
---

The saying is known only from the *Rhetoric* (see [[xenophanes]]). The impious man will swear falsely without fear, the pious will not, so an oath tendered by the one to the other favors the liar.
