---
title: “Mysian plunder”
greek: Μυσῶν λεία
kind: saying
tags: [wrongdoing]
summary: A proverb for people or goods that anyone may plunder with impunity; Aristotle uses it of those who have been wronged often and never prosecuted.
aliases: [Mysian booty, the Mysians' plunder]
source: Proverb (Aristotle, Rhetoric 1.12, 1372b33; Demosthenes 18.72)
of: [mysians]
---

Demosthenes uses the proverb in *On the Crown*, of a Greece left open to Philip's plunder (18.72). The ancient lexicographers explain it from a time when the Mysians' neighbors raided their land unopposed, while their king Telephus was away (see [[mysians]]).
