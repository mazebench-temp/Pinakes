{{quote:bcc71c87-7747-557f-b9fb-3a85c07a78b0}}

The saying was written at the entrance of the temple at Delphi. Plato's Socrates makes it his own task (*Phaedrus* 229e–230a), and in the *Charmides* Critias explains it as the god's greeting to those who enter, meaning “be self-controlled” (164d–165a). Aristotle uses it as a common maxim that an emotional speaker may turn round: “if someone in anger were to say that it is false that one should know oneself: this man at any rate, if he had known himself, would never have thought himself fit to be general” (*Rhetoric* 2.21, 1395a23–26).
