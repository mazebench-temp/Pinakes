{{quote:94c7776e-1021-51d6-a7f2-c88a13f973a7}}

Diogenes Laertius gives the precept to Bias, with the reason that most men are bad (1.87). In Cicero's dialogue on friendship Scipio calls it the most hostile saying there is to friendship and refuses to believe that one of the Seven Sages said it (*On Friendship* 59). For Aristotle it is the wisdom of age: the old, deceived often, neither love nor hate strongly (*Rhetoric* 2.13, 1389b22–25). In the chapter on maxims a speaker shows a better character by reversing it, saying that one should not, as they say, love as though one will hate, but rather hate as though one will love, or by rejecting it outright: “the true friend should love as though he will love always” (*Rhetoric* 2.21, 1395a26–32).
