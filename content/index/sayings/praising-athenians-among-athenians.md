In Plato's *Menexenus* Socrates mocks the funeral speeches, which praise the city and the dead so grandly that every hearer feels himself greater: “if one had to praise Athenians among Peloponnesians, or Peloponnesians among Athenians, one would need a good speaker to persuade and win approval; but when one competes before the very people one is praising, it is no great matter to seem to speak well” (235d). He then recites a funeral speech he says he learned from Aspasia. In the *Rhetoric*'s third book Aristotle gives the saying its full form: it is not hard to praise Athenians among Athenians, “but among Lacedaemonians” (*Rhetoric* 3.14, 1415b32).
