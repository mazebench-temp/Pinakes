title: Plutarch
greek: Πλούταρχος

tags: [scholarship, philosophy]
summary: Greek biographer and philosopher from Chaeronea in Boeotia (c. 46–c. 120 CE), author of the Parallel Lives and the essays of the Moralia, who quotes Homer constantly.
---

Plutarch was a Platonist and for many years a priest at Delphi. His *Lives* preserve stories of Homer's text being used, or altered, for political ends. In the *Life of Solon* he tells how Athens was said to have won Salamis with a line added to the Catalogue of Ships ([[solon-and-salamis]]). In the *Life of Theseus* he reports the similar charge that Peisistratus inserted a line about Theseus into the Odyssey ([[theseus-line]]). In the *Life of Tiberius Gracchus* he tells how Scipio Africanus, hearing at Numantia that Tiberius had been killed, recited Athena's verse on Aegisthus, “so may any other perish too who did such things” ([[scipio-and-tiberius-gracchus]]). Among the *Moralia*, the essay *How the Young Man Should Study Poetry* is a guide to reading Homer with a critical eye. In *On Isis and Osiris* he reports Heraclitus' complaint that Homer, praying that strife might perish, was cursing the birth of all things ([[heraclitus-and-achilles-wish]]).
