summary: Greek lyric poet from Thebes (c. 518–after 446 BCE), whose victory odes for winners at the great games set each victory beside a myth of the heroes. He tells the old stories freely, and refuses those he thinks unworthy of the gods.
---

Four books of Pindar's victory odes survive, for winners at the Olympian, Pythian, Nemean and Isthmian games, with fragments of his other poetry. An ode moves from the victor to a myth, often of the heroes of Thebes and Troy, and back again. Pindar corrects the myths as he tells them. In his first Olympian ode he will not call any of the blessed gods a glutton, and he rejects the story that they ate the flesh of Pelops (*Olympian* 1.52).

- Heracles fights Poseidon, Apollo and Hades at Pylos, a story Pindar breaks off as blasphemous (*Olympian* 9.29–41): [[pylos-or-the-gate]].
- One race of men and gods, divided by power (*Nemean* 6.1–7): [[pindar-one-race]].
- Athena made Diomedes an immortal god (*Nemean* 10.7): [[birds-of-diomedes]].
- Antilochus gives his life for his father Nestor (*Pythian* 6.28–42): [[antilochus]].
