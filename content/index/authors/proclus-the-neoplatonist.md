summary: The Neoplatonist philosopher Proclus (412–485 CE), head of the Platonic school at Athens, whose commentaries on Plato quote Homer often and read the poems' gods as figures of divine order. Some take him for the author of the Chrestomathy that summarizes the Epic Cycle.
aliases: [Proclus, Proclus Diadochus, Proclus Lycius]

Proclus was born at Constantinople to a family from Xanthus in Lycia, studied at Alexandria and then at Athens, and succeeded his teacher Syrianus as head of the Platonic school there; his surname Diadochus, "successor", names that office. His commentaries on Plato's *Timaeus*, *Republic*, *Parmenides* and *First Alcibiades* survive, with excerpts from one on the *Cratylus*. In the commentary on the *Republic* he defends Homer against Plato's own criticisms, taking the poet's stories of the gods as symbols.

Whether he wrote the *Chrestomathy*, the handbook whose summaries of the lost poems of the Epic Cycle survive, is disputed (see [[proclus]]).

- The golden chain of beings, from Zeus' golden rope: [[homers-golden-chain]].
- Zeus' rank above Poseidon, from "but Zeus was born first and knew more" (13.355): [[poseidon-rallies-the-achaeans]] and the *Iliad* 13 note.
- The union of Zeus and Hera on Ida, defended against Plato as theology (*On the Republic* I 132–140 Kroll): [[plato-zeus-deceived]] and the *Iliad* 14 note.
- Hera hung with two anvils at her feet, the air above the two heaviest elements (*On the Cratylus* 170), the three Zeuses of the division by lot (*On the Cratylus* 148) and the eternal lots of the gods (*On the Timaeus* I 136, 141 Diehl): [[hera-hung-from-the-sky]], [[the-lots-of-the-three-brothers]] and the *Iliad* 15 note.
- The soul of Patroclus loath to leave the body, and Zeus' lament for Sarpedon, against Plato's censures (*On the Republic* I 118–126 Kroll), and ἀΐσθων at 16.468 (*On the Timaeus* III 331 Diehl): [[plato-the-terrors-of-hades]], [[plato-laments-of-gods-and-heroes]] and the *Iliad* 16 note.
- Achilles' dragging of Hector around the tomb of Patroclus, defended against Plato as a Thessalian custom and as the penalty for Hector's design on the body of Patroclus (*On the Republic* I 150–151 Kroll): [[the-dragging-of-hector-defended]].
- Athena likened to Mentor, a god come down to the lowest of its orders, against Plato's censure of gods who change their shape (*On the Republic* I 112–114 Kroll): [[athena-as-mentor-in-later-writers]].
- Proteus an angelic intellect full of the forms of all things, Eidothea a daemonic soul joined to him and the seals rational souls, against Plato's ban on lies about Proteus (*On the Republic* I 112–113 Kroll): [[platos-proteus]].
- The nutritive power of the body, which “puts together again what it has undone, like Penelope's web” (*On the Timaeus* III 332 Diehl): [[penelopes-web-in-later-writers]].
- Longinus on a periphrasis of Plato's, likened to “the sacred strength of Telemachus” (*On the Timaeus* I 68 Diehl): [[longinus-on-the-sacred-strength-of-telemachus]].
