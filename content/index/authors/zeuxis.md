## Life and work

Pliny tells that Zeuxis painted grapes so lifelike that birds flew at them, and was himself deceived by his rival Parrhasius' painted curtain (*Natural History* 35.65–66). For his Helen at Croton he chose five girls and combined the best features of each (Cicero, *On Invention* 2.1–3; see [[zeuxis-helen]]).

## In the Poetics

The tragedies of most of the moderns are without character, “just as among painters Zeuxis stands to Polygnotus: for Polygnotus is a good painter of character, while the painting of Zeuxis has no character at all” (1450a25–29; see [[polygnotus]] and [[ethos]]). In chapter 25 he is the example of the ideal: if it is impossible that people should be as Zeuxis painted them, “it is better so: for the model should surpass the reality” (1461b12–13; see [[problems-and-solutions]] and [[painting-and-poetry]]).
