Astydamas, of a family descended from Aeschylus' sister, was among the most successful tragedians of the fourth century; he won his first victory in 372 BCE, and after the success of his *Parthenopaeus* in 340 the Athenians voted him a bronze statue in the theater. In the *Poetics* his *Alcmaeon* is an example of a terrible deed done in ignorance within the play itself: “within the tragedy itself there is Astydamas' Alcmaeon” (1453b32–33; see [[alcmaeon-play]] and [[alcmaeon]]).
