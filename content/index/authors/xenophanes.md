## Life and work

Xenophanes left Ionia for the West and wandered the Greek world reciting his poems for sixty-seven years, by his own account. “Homer and Hesiod have attributed to the gods everything that is a shame and reproach among men, stealing and adultery and deceiving each other” (fr. 11 DK); and if oxen and horses had hands, they would draw the gods like oxen and horses (fr. 15 DK). He taught one god, greatest among gods and men, unlike mortals in body or thought (fr. 23 DK).

## In the Poetics

A poet criticized for untruth may answer “that this is what people say, as with stories about the gods; for perhaps it is neither better to tell them so nor true, but, as may well be, as Xenophanes thought; but at any rate people say so” (1460b35–1461a1; see [[problems-and-solutions]]).
