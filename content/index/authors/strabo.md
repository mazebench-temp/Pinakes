title: Strabo
greek: Στράβων
kind: author
tags: [scholarship]
summary: Greek geographer (c. 64 BCE–c. 24 CE) who treated Homer as the first geographer. His Geography follows the Catalogue of Ships region by region, and preserves anecdotes such as Phidias and the nod of Zeus.
---

Strabo's *Geography* opens by claiming Homer as the founder of geography, and it discusses Homeric places at length, above all the Troad (Book 13). For Iliad 1 he supplies [[phidias-zeus]] (8.3.30) and [[smintheus-and-the-mice]] (13.1.48), and he locates [[chryse]] and [[cilla]]. He argues for Nestor's Pylos in Triphylia ([[strabo-on-nestors-pylos]]), and from Book 3 of the Odyssey places the Pylians' sacrifice at the sanctuary of Samian Poseidon and proves from the messenger sent to Telemachus' ship that the city lay inland (8.3.16, 8.3.26). On the far peoples at the start of Iliad 13 he weighs Posidonius, Apollodorus and Eratosthenes ([[strabo-on-the-mare-milkers]], 7.3.2–10), and he finds that Homer's Cassandra is still a maiden, untouched by the Locrian Ajax ([[strabo-on-cassandra]], 13.1.40). He reads the shield of Achilles as geography: the Bear that alone never bathes in Oceanus stands for the arctic circle, and Oceanus on the rim shows the earth surrounded by Ocean (1.1.6–7; see [[alone-without-a-share]] and [[title-of-book-18]]).
