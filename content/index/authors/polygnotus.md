## Life and work

Polygnotus worked at Athens, where he painted in the Stoa Poikile, and at Delphi, where his two great paintings in the clubhouse of the Cnidians showed Troy after its capture and Odysseus' visit to the underworld; Pausanias describes them figure by figure (10.25–31). Aristotle advises in the *Politics* that the young should look at the works not of Pauson but of Polygnotus, “and of any other painter or sculptor who portrays character” (8.5, 1340a35–38).

## In the Poetics

Polygnotus portrayed people better than they are, Pauson worse, Dionysius like us (1448a5–6; see [[better-worse-or-like-us]], [[pauson]] and [[dionysius-of-colophon]]). He is the model of character in painting: “Polygnotus is a good painter of character, while the painting of Zeuxis has no character at all” (1450a26–29; see [[zeuxis]], [[ethos]] and [[painting-and-poetry]]).
