---

Chaucer read no Greek, and knew the Trojan War through Latin, French and Italian writers. *Troilus and Criseyde* (1380s) is a free retelling of Boccaccio's *Filostrato*, which he never names; he claims instead to follow an author called Lollius, whom no one has identified. At the end he sends his “little book” to kiss the steps where “Virgile, Ovide, Omer, Lucan, and Stace” walk (*Troilus and Criseyde* 5.1791–1792).

In the last book Cassandra explains a dream of Troilus by retelling the story of the Seven against Thebes, following Statius' *Thebaid*. The boar of the dream, she says, is Diomede, the son of Tydeus, who has won Criseyde from him.
