The first half of the *Aeneid* follows the Odyssey, the wanderings of a hero after the fall of Troy; the second half follows the Iliad, a war fought on the plain before a city. Virgil left it unfinished at his death. His borrowings from Homer include:

- the poet's ten tongues, multiplied to a hundred: [[ten-tongues-in-virgil]];
- the catalogue of forces, with its invocation of the Muses: [[virgil-catalogue-of-italians]];
- Thersites' insult "Achaean women, Achaean men no longer" (2.235), turned against the Trojans by the Italian Numanus: "O truly Phrygian women, for you are not Phrygian men" (*Aeneid* 9.617): [[virgil-numanus]];
- Rumor, who grows as Homer's Strife does: [[virgil-rumor]];
- Rhesus and Dolon among the pictures at Carthage: [[virgil-rhesus-and-dolon]];
- the truce for burning the dead: [[virgil-truce-for-the-dead]];
- Pandarus and Bitias at the gate of the camp: [[virgil-pandarus-and-bitias]];
- the duel for Lavinia and the broken truce: [[virgil-duel-for-lavinia]];
- Neptune's drives over the sea, after Poseidon's journey to the ships: [[virgil-neptunes-chariot]];
- Juno's promise of a nymph to Aeolus for a storm, after Hera's promise of a Grace to Sleep: [[virgil-juno-and-aeolus]];
- Jupiter, who could not save his son Sarpedon, consoling Hercules for Pallas: [[jupiter-and-sarpedon]];
- Aethon, the war-horse that weeps in Pallas' funeral procession, as the horses of Achilles weep for Patroclus: [[virgils-weeping-horse]];
- Venus asking Vulcan for arms for her son, as Thetis asked Hephaestus, and the shield of Aeneas with the history of Rome: [[virgil-shield-of-aeneas]];
- Mercury sent to Carthage with Jupiter's command, binding on the golden sandals and taking the wand, as Hermes when Zeus sends him to Calypso: [[virgils-mercury]];
- the storm that opens the *Aeneid*, with Aeneas' cry that those who died beneath the walls of Troy were three and four times blessed, as Odysseus cries on his raft: [[virgils-storm]];
- the helmsman Palinurus, who watches the stars, is thrown overboard with the tiller and swims to Italy, as Odysseus swims to Scheria: [[virgils-palinurus]];
- in the fourth book of the *Georgics*, the capture of Proteus among his seals, retold for the shepherd Aristaeus and his lost bees: [[virgils-proteus]].
