## The poet

The Greeks told many stories about Homer: that he was blind, that he came from Smyrna or Chios (seven cities claimed him), that he lost a riddle contest with fishermen. None has any historical value. The Iliad and the Odyssey were probably composed in Ionia in the eighth or early seventh century BCE, at the end of a long tradition of oral poetry. Whether one poet made both, and how writing entered the process, is the "Homeric Question", debated since F. A. Wolf's *Prolegomena* (1795).

## The poem's voice

The poet speaks in his own voice only through the Muse: "Sing, goddess" (1.1). He names no author, gives no date, and addresses no patron. See [[muses]] and [[in-medias-res]].

## Oral poetry

The recurring epithets and formulas that fill Book 1 ("swift-footed Achilles", "rosy-fingered Dawn", "in answer to him said…") were explained by Milman Parry in the 1920s and 1930s as the tools of oral composition. They are ready-made phrases that fit the hexameter and let a singer compose in performance. See [[winged-words]] and the index of [[swift-footed|epithets]].
