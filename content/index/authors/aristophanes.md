## Life and work

Aristophanes' comedies mock the politicians, poets and philosophers of wartime Athens: Cleon in the *Knights*, Socrates in the *Clouds*, Euripides in the *Thesmophoriazusae* and the *Frogs*, where Dionysus goes down to Hades to bring back a tragic poet and judges a contest between Aeschylus and Euripides (see [[euripides]] and [[aeschylus]]). His last plays, the *Women at the Assembly* and the *Wealth*, show the turn toward Middle Comedy.

## In the Poetics

“In one respect Sophocles would be the same kind of imitator as Homer, since both imitate serious people, and in another the same as Aristophanes, since both imitate people acting and doing” (1448a25–28; see [[modes-of-imitation]]). Several men Aristotle names are figures in his comedies: Sthenelus, Ariphrades, and Philoxenus, whose *Cyclops* the *Wealth* parodies (see [[sthenelus-the-poet]], [[ariphrades]] and [[philoxenus]]).
