---

Diodorus names a Polyidus among the famous dithyrambic poets of about 398 BCE, with Philoxenus, Timotheus and Telestes, and says he was also a painter (14.46.6). Aristotle twice mentions his treatment of the Iphigenia story, a recognition by reasoning: “that of Polyidus the sophist about Iphigenia: for it was probable, he said, that Orestes would reason that his sister had been sacrificed and that it was his lot to be sacrificed too” (1455a6–8), and, in the outline of chapter 17, Orestes is recognized “whether as Euripides made it or as Polyidus did” (1455b9–12; see [[the-recognition-of-orestes-and-iphigenia]]). Whether Polyidus wrote a play, a dithyramb, or offered the idea in a critical discussion is not known.
