Antiphon wrote tragedies at Syracuse in the time of Dionysius I (tyrant 405–367 BCE), who wrote tragedies himself. Of his plays almost nothing survives beyond Aristotle's references to a *Meleager* (see [[meleager-antiphon]]). Dionysius had him put to death. The *Lives of the Ten Orators* that go under Plutarch's name confuse him with the Athenian orator Antiphon of Rhamnus and tell how, when the guests at the tyrant's table disputed which bronze was finest, he named the bronze of the statues of Harmodius and Aristogeiton (*Moralia* 833); Philostratus tells the same story (*Lives of the Sophists* 1.15). See [[dionysius-of-syracuse]].
