## Life and work

Protagoras taught for money across the Greek world and was a friend of Pericles; Plato's *Protagoras* and *Theaetetus* argue with him. He was said to have been the first to divide speech into wish, question, answer and command (Diogenes Laertius 9.53–54), and to have distinguished the genders of nouns (Aristotle, *Rhetoric* 3.5, 1407b6–8; see [[gender-of-nouns]]). In Plato's *Protagoras* he says that the greatest part of a man's education is to be skilled about poems, able to judge what is said rightly and what not (338e–339a).

## In the Poetics

Aristotle dismisses his criticism of the Iliad's first line as belonging to another art: “who would suppose it a fault, what Protagoras criticizes, that thinking to pray, Homer gives an order when he says ‘Sing, goddess, the wrath’?” (1456b15–18; see [[protagoras-on-the-first-line]] and [[forms-of-utterance]]).
