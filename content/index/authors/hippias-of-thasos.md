Hippias is not the sophist of Elis but an otherwise unknown Thasian scholar. Aristotle cites his solutions twice. By a change of accent he read δίδομεν (“we grant”) as διδόμεν (an infinitive used as an imperative) in the dream's message to Agamemnon, so that Zeus does not lie, and read οὐ (“not”) as οὗ (“of which”) in Nestor's description of the turning post, “part of which rots in the rain” (1461a21–23; *Sophistical Refutations* 4, 166b1–8; see [[aristotle-and-hippias]], [[the-stump-that-rots]] and [[thasos]]).
