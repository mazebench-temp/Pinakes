Hippias is not the sophist of Elis but an otherwise unknown Thasian scholar. Aristotle cites his solutions twice. By a change of accent he read δίδομεν (“we grant”) as διδόμεν (an infinitive used as an imperative) in the dream's message to Agamemnon, so that Zeus does not lie, and solved a difficulty in Nestor's description of the turning post, “part of which rots in the rain”, by the accent of ΟΥ, which can be read οὐ, “not”, or οὗ, “of which”; which reading was the problem and which the solution is disputed (1461a21–23; *Sophistical Refutations* 4, 166b1–8; see [[aristotle-and-hippias]], [[the-stump-that-rots]] and [[thasos]]).
