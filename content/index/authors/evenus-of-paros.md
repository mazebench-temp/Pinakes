In Plato's *Apology* Socrates says that Evenus of Paros teaches virtue for five minae (20b); in the *Phaedo* he sends him word that a philosopher should be ready to die (60d–61c); and in the *Phaedrus* he credits him with inventing covert allusion and indirect praise (267a). Aristotle names him when he quotes the line “every necessary thing is by nature grievous” in the *Metaphysics* (Δ 5, 1015a28–29), and quotes his couplet that long practice becomes nature in the end in the *Nicomachean Ethics* (7.10, 1152a31–33).
