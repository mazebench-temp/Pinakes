title: Gorgias
greek: Γοργίας
kind: author
tags: [rhetoric]
summary: Sophist and teacher of rhetoric from Leontini in Sicily (c. 485–c. 380 BCE). His Encomium of Helen argues that she was not to blame for going to Troy.
---

Gorgias came to Athens as an envoy of Leontini in 427 BCE and made his name with a new prose style, full of antitheses, balanced clauses and rhyming endings. He was said to have lived past a hundred. Plato named a dialogue after him.

Two of his showpiece speeches take their subjects from the Trojan War: the *Encomium of Helen* ([[gorgias-helen]]) and the *Defense of Palamedes*, in which Palamedes answers Odysseus' charge of treason. Homer never names Palamedes; his death was told in the *Cypria*, and his trial in tragedy.
