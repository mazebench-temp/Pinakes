Polybius was a leader of the Achaean League. After the Roman victory over Macedon at Pydna he was taken to Rome in 167 BCE among a thousand Achaean hostages, and there he became the close friend of the young Scipio Aemilianus. His *Histories*, in forty books, told chiefly the years 220–146 BCE; the first five books survive complete, the rest in excerpts and quotations.

He held that history should be written by men of affairs who had seen things for themselves, and he took his model from Homer. The opening lines of the Odyssey, on the man who “saw the cities of many men and knew their mind”, describe for him what a historian should be (*Histories* 12.27.10–11).
