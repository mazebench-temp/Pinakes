Demosthenes son of Demosthenes, of the deme Paeania, began as a writer of speeches for the courts and became the chief advocate of resistance to Philip II of Macedon, in the *Philippics* and the *Olynthiacs*. After the defeat at Chaeronea (338 BCE) the Athenians chose him to speak over the dead (*On the Crown* 285), and in 330 he defended his whole career against Aeschines in the speech *On the Crown*. Condemned in the affair of Harpalus, he went into exile; after the failure of the revolt against Macedon that followed Alexander's death he took poison on the island of Calauria in 322, rather than fall into the hands of Antipater's men (Plutarch, *Demosthenes* 29–30).
