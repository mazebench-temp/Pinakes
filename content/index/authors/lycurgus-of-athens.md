summary: Athenian orator and statesman (c. 390–324 BCE). His one surviving speech, Against Leocrates, prosecutes a man who fled Athens after the defeat at Chaeronea, and calls Euripides, Homer and Tyrtaeus as witnesses of what a citizen owes his country. He is not the Spartan lawgiver of the same name.

Of Lycurgus' speeches only *Against Leocrates* survives entire. Leocrates, he charges, deserted the city after the battle of Chaeronea (338 BCE). When the people had voted to bring the women and children in from the country within the walls, and the generals to assign the Athenians and the other residents to guard duty, Leocrates packed up his property and carried it down with his slaves to a boat, and toward evening slipped out through the postern gate with the courtesan Eirenis and sailed away, leaving his part of the walls unguarded (*Against Leocrates* 16–17).

To show the jurors how their ancestors thought of their country, Lycurgus quotes the poets at length: Euripides' *Erechtheus*, in which Praxithea, the king's wife, gives her daughter to be killed so that the city may win its war (98–101), Homer (102–104), and the elegies of Tyrtaeus, whom the Spartans, he says, took from Athens as their general (105–107).

- Hector's exhortation at the ships, and the law that Homer alone of the poets should be recited at every Great Panathenaea (*Against Leocrates* 102–104): [[lycurgus-quotes-hector]], [[the-robe-and-the-panathenaea]] and [[rhapsody]].
