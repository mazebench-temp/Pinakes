tags: [sophist, rhetoric]
summary: Alcidamas of Elaea, a sophist and orator of the fourth century BCE, pupil of Gorgias and rival of Isocrates, whose lost Messenian Speech declared that God has left all men free.
---

Alcidamas' surviving speech, *On Those Who Write Written Speeches, or On the Sophists*, argues that the power to speak on the spur of the moment matters more than writing out speeches at leisure, a criticism aimed at Isocrates; an *Odysseus*, accusing Palamedes, also goes under his name. A papyrus has shown that his *Museum* contained a version of the *Contest of Homer and Hesiod*.

Aristotle quotes him several times. In the second book of the *Rhetoric* Alcidamas argued “that all men honor the wise” (*Rhetoric* 2.23, 1398b11; see [[all-men-honor-the-wise]]), and an argument from the *Messenian Speech*, probably his, illustrates the topic from opposites (*Rhetoric* 2.23, 1397a11–13; see [[war-the-cause-of-our-evils]]). In the third his compound words and piled-up epithets are the chief examples of frigid style: “filled with fury in his soul, and his face becoming fire-hued” (*Rhetoric* 3.3, 1406a1–2; see [[psychron]]).
