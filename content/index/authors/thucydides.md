summary: Athenian historian of the Peloponnesian War (c. 460–c. 400 BCE). In the opening chapters of his history he used Homer as evidence for early Greece, and the Catalogue of Ships for the size of the army at Troy.
---

Thucydides commanded an Athenian squadron in 424 BCE, was exiled after the loss of Amphipolis, and wrote the history of the war between Athens and Sparta that began in 431; it breaks off in 411. Its first chapters, often called the "Archaeology" (1.2–19), argue that the wars of the past were smaller than his own. Homer is his chief witness, used critically: a poet likely to exaggerate, but evidence all the same.

- On the Catalogue's numbers and Agamemnon's power: [[thucydides-and-the-catalogue]].
- On the name of the Hellenes and the absence of "barbarians" in Homer: [[thucydides-hellenes]].
- On why the Catalogue's Boeotians could sail from Boeotia: [[the-boeotia]].

Elsewhere he cites the Homeric *Hymn to Apollo*, which he took to be Homer's, as evidence for the ancient festival on Delos (3.104).
