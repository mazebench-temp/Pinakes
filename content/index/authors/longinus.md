The treatise survives incomplete, with several pages lost. Its manuscript title names “Dionysius Longinus”, and its table of contents “Dionysius or Longinus”. It was long credited to Cassius Longinus, the Greek critic of the third century CE, but most scholars now date it to the first century CE and leave the author unnamed. Boileau's French translation (1674) made it one of the most admired works of criticism in Europe.

Its author holds that sublimity comes first of all from greatness of mind, “the echo of a great mind” (*On the Sublime* 9.2), and he finds it above all in Homer. He compares the Iliad, full of action and conflict, the work of Homer's prime, with the Odyssey, the work of his old age, which he likens to the setting sun, still great but without the same force (9.11–15).
