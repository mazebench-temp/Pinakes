summary: Lyric poet of Mytilene on Lesbos (born c. 620 BCE), Sappho's contemporary, who sang of war, exile, wine and his city's tyrants; the Rhetoric gives him the first words of an exchange with Sappho.
---

Alcaeus took part in the civil strife of Mytilene, against the tyrants Melanchrus and Myrsilus and then against his former ally Pittacus, whom the city chose as its elected ruler. Aristotle quotes his complaint that the people set up “the low-born Pittacus as tyrant of the spiritless, ill-fated city” (*Politics* 3.14, 1285a37–b1). Herodotus tells how, in the war with Athens for Sigeum, Alcaeus fled from the battle and the Athenians hung up his shield in the temple of Athena there, and how he told the story in a poem to his friend Melanippus (5.95; see [[sigeum]]). The four-line stanza named after him is the meter of the exchange the *Rhetoric* quotes.
