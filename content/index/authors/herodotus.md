summary: Greek historian from Halicarnassus (c. 484–c. 425 BCE), whose Histories of the wars between the Greeks and the Persians quote Homer as evidence and argue with him. He believed that Helen spent the Trojan War in Egypt.
---

Herodotus opens his work with the account, which he gives to Persian learned men, that the quarrel between Europe and Asia began with a series of stolen women, Io, Europa, Medea and Helen; then he sets these stories aside for what he knows himself (1.1–5). He dates Homer and Hesiod no more than four hundred years before his own time (2.53). He knew the Iliad's episodes by their old titles, citing lines of our Book 6 as coming from "the Aristeia of Diomedes", and he judged that the *Cypria* was not Homer's work (2.116–117).
