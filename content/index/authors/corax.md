# Od. 13.408 names the Rock of Corax, a place in Ithaca, not the rhetorician: the-rock-of-corax claims the line.
except: [Od. 13.408]
---

Cicero reports from Aristotle's lost collection of the arts that after the tyrants were expelled from Sicily, when property was claimed back in the courts, Corax and Tisias first wrote down an art and precepts of speaking (*Brutus* 46). In Plato's *Phaedrus* the argument of the weak man and the strong is given to Tisias (273a–c). See [[the-art-of-corax]] and [[the-handbooks]].

The same name belongs to a place in the Odyssey, the Rock of Corax in Ithaca, by the spring Arethusa, where the swine that Eumaeus keeps for Odysseus feed (*Odyssey* 13.407–410): see [[the-rock-of-corax]] and [[arethusa]]. The Suda gives the bird and the man under one headword: κόραξ is “a kind of bird”, and also the rhetor, “the inventor of rhetoric” (Suda κ 2066, plain renderings).
