summary: Corax of Syracuse, named with his pupil Tisias as the first to write an art of rhetoric, in the fifth century BCE; his art was built on the argument from probability. His name means “crow”.
---

Cicero reports from Aristotle's lost collection of the arts that after the tyrants were expelled from Sicily, when property was claimed back in the courts, Corax and Tisias first wrote down an art and precepts of speaking (*Brutus* 46). In Plato's *Phaedrus* the argument of the weak man and the strong is given to Tisias (273a–c). See [[the-art-of-corax]] and [[the-handbooks]].
