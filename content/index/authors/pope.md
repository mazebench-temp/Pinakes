Pope's Iliad appeared in six volumes between 1715 and 1720, and his Odyssey, made with two collaborators, in 1725–1726. He turned Homer's verses into heroic couplets. His version was much admired, and later attacked, by Wordsworth for its false picture of nature and by Matthew Arnold, in his lectures *On Translating Homer* (1861), for a style that fails Homer's plainness. His first lines are compared with other translators' in [[first-line-in-english]], and a line of his *Rape of the Lock* is traced to Milton's wounded angels in [[milton-wounded-angels]].
