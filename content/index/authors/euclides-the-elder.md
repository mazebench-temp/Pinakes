Euclides is known only from this passage. He mocked the poets for lengthening words at will, “saying that composing would be easy if one were allowed to lengthen syllables as far as one liked”, and lampooned the practice “in the very diction” with lines that scan as verse only if short syllables are lengthened (1458b5–11; see [[euclides-parodies]] and [[epektetamenon]]). Aristotle grants the point against obtrusive use, not against use: moderation is common to all the kinds of word.
