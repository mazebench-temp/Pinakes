Xenophon gives him a conversation with Socrates on self-control and freedom, in which he chooses a life that is neither ruling nor ruled (*Memorabilia* 2.1). Diogenes Laertius collects the stories of his life at the court of Dionysius of Syracuse and his sayings about pleasure (2.65–104). Aristotle names him in the *Metaphysics* as one of the sophists who mocked mathematics for taking no account of the good (3.2, 996a32–b1). See [[socrates]] and [[plato]].
