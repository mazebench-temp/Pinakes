Arnold gave the lectures as Professor of Poetry at Oxford and published them in 1861. He judged the English translators by Homer's four qualities: for want of the first, rapidity, Cowper failed; for want of the second, plainness and directness of style, Pope; for want of the third, plainness and directness of ideas, Chapman; and for want of the fourth, nobleness, Francis Newman failed “more conspicuously than any of them”. Newman answered him in *Homeric Translation in Theory and Practice*, and Arnold replied in *On Translating Homer: Last Words* (1862), where he also found Tennyson's blank verse too subtle and elaborate for Homer.
