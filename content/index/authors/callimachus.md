summary: Poet and scholar from Cyrene (third century BCE), who worked at Alexandria under the Ptolemies. His Pinakes, the tables of those distinguished in every branch of learning and of their writings, gave this project its name. His Hymn to Zeus denies a tale that the Iliad tells, the division of the world by lot.

The Suda calls Callimachus the son of Battus, of Cyrene, a grammarian, who wrote poems in every meter and very many works in prose, more than eight hundred books in all, and who lived under Ptolemy Philadelphus and on into the reign of Ptolemy Euergetes. Among the works it lists are the *Pinakes*, “Tables of those who were distinguished in every branch of learning, and of what they wrote”, in a hundred and twenty books (Suda κ 227). The same notice makes Apollonius, the author of the *Argonautica*, his enemy; elsewhere the Suda calls Apollonius his pupil (α 3419; see [[apollonius]]). Zenodotus worked in the same Library (see [[zenodotus]]).

His hymns retell the births and the honors of the gods, and at times correct the older stories.

- The lots of the sons of Cronus denied, and Zeus made king by his own deeds and might (*Hymn to Zeus* 55–67): [[callimachus-against-the-lots]].
- “Cretans are always liars”, for they show a tomb of Zeus, who does not die (*Hymn to Zeus* 8–9): [[cretans]].
- The child Artemis, on her father's knees, asks Zeus for lasting maidenhood (*Hymn to Artemis* 4–6): [[artemis]].
