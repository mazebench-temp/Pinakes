In the first book of Plato's *Republic* Thrasymachus breaks into the conversation and maintains that justice is “nothing other than the advantage of the stronger” (338c). In the *Phaedrus* Socrates gives the prize for speeches that draw tears over old age and poverty to “the might of the Chalcedonian” (267c–d). Aristotle names him among those who developed rhetoric after its first inventors: Tisias came after them, Thrasymachus after Tisias, Theodorus after him (*Sophistical Refutations* 34, 183b31–32; see [[theodorus-of-byzantium]]).
