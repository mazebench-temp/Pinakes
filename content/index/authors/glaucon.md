Plato's rhapsode Ion claims to speak about Homer better than Metrodorus of Lampsacus, Stesimbrotus of Thasos or Glaucon (*Ion* 530c–d), and the *Rhetoric* mentions a Glaucon of Teos who wrote on delivery (*Rhetoric* 3.1, 1403b26); they may be the same man. In the *Poetics* Aristotle approves his description of bad critics: “some people make an unreasonable assumption and, having passed judgment themselves, argue from it, and, as though the poet had said whatever they think, find fault if it contradicts their own notion” (1461b1–3), as in the Icarius problem (see [[the-icarius-problem]] and [[problems-and-solutions]]).
