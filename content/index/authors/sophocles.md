summary: Athenian tragic poet (c. 496–406 BCE), of whose many plays seven survive. Ancient critics thought him the most Homeric of the tragedians, and his Ajax gives Ajax's captive wife Tecmessa the plea of Andromache.
---

The Suda credits him with 123 plays and twenty-four victories (σ 815). Seven tragedies survive whole, *Ajax*, *Antigone*, *Women of Trachis*, *Oedipus the King*, *Electra*, *Philoctetes* and *Oedipus at Colonus*, with much of a satyr play, the *Trackers*, recovered on papyrus. Many of his plays, lost and surviving, took their stories from the war at Troy and its aftermath. The Academic philosopher Polemon called Homer an epic Sophocles, and Sophocles a tragic Homer (Diogenes Laertius 4.20).
