Cleophon is probably the Athenian tragic poet of that name listed in the Suda. The *Rhetoric* gives an example of his style, the grand address “O lady fig” for a humble thing (3.7, 1408a15–16). In the *Poetics* he imitates people “like us”, between Homer, who imitates better, and the parodists, who imitate worse (1448a11–14; see [[better-worse-or-like-us]]), and his poetry and Sthenelus' are examples of diction that is clear but mean (1458a18–21; see [[excellence-of-diction]] and [[sthenelus-the-poet]]).
