summary: Boeotian poet of about 700 BCE, author of the Theogony and the Works and Days. His genealogy of the gods is the fullest early account of the myths the Iliad alludes to.
---

Hesiod's *Theogony* tells the origin of the gods, from Chaos to the rule of Zeus, including the Titanomachy and the Hundred-Handers who appear in the story of [[binding-of-zeus|the binding of Zeus]]. Pinakes cites him under "Outside Homer" throughout.

He shares with Homer the same epic language, and occasionally the same lines. Iliad 1.70, on the seer's knowledge of past, present and future, is used by Hesiod of the Muses (*Theogony* 38); see [[calchas-and-the-muses]]. The line naming Theseus, Iliad 1.265, is also line 182 of the Hesiodic *Shield of Heracles*; see [[theseus-line]]. The *Shield*, whose shield has the plan of the shield of Achilles, also has four verses of Iliad 18, on Strife, Tumult and Doom in the battle (*Shield* 156–159 = Iliad 18.535–538); see [[shield-of-heracles]].

The *Catalogue of Women* gave Telemachus and Nestor's youngest daughter Polycaste a son, Persepolis (fr. 12 Evelyn-White; see [[polycaste]]), and told how Nestor alone of the sons of Neleus escaped Heracles, as a guest among the Gerenians (fr. 11; see [[gerenian-horseman]]). In the *Theogony* Cottus promises Zeus his help “with unbending mind and thoughtful counsel”, the words in which Nestor tells of his counsels with Odysseus at Troy (*Theogony* 661; *Odyssey* 3.128).
