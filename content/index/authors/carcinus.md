## Life and work

Carcinus the younger, son of Xenocles and grandson of the Carcinus mocked by Aristophanes, spent time at the court of Dionysius II of Syracuse; ancient sources credit him with many plays and eleven victories. Aristotle quotes his *Medea* in the *Rhetoric* (2.23, 1400b9–15) and his *Alope* in the *Ethics* (7.7, 1150b10).

## In the Poetics

Among congenital signs of recognition are “stars such as Carcinus uses in his Thyestes” (1454b22–23; see [[thyestes-play]] and [[semeion]]). And “the fault found with Carcinus” shows why a poet must put things before his eyes: his Amphiaraus was coming back up from the temple, which escaped one who did not see it, “but on the stage the play failed because the audience was annoyed at it” (1455a26–29; see [[before-the-eyes]] and [[amphiaraus]]).
