---
title: Solon
greek: Σόλων
kind: author
tags: [poet, statesman, athenian]
summary: Athenian lawgiver and poet (archon 594/3 BCE), whose laws the Athenians claimed to live by and whose elegies were quoted as evidence in their quarrels.
---

As archon Solon canceled debts and freed those enslaved for debt, divided the citizens by property into four classes, and gave Athens a code of laws. His own poems defend the settlement, and the *Constitution of the Athenians* quotes them at length (5–12). Plutarch tells how he roused the Athenians to fight Megara for Salamis with an elegy that began “I have come as a herald from lovely Salamis” (*Solon* 8), and how the Catalogue of Ships was said to have helped him win the island (see [[solon-and-salamis]]). Herodotus makes him the wise man who tells Croesus to call no one happy until he is dead (1.29–33). In the *Rhetoric*'s second book the Athenians prospered by using Solon's laws, in Alcidamas' induction that all men honor the wise (2.23, 1398b17–18; see [[all-men-honor-the-wise]]).
