Philoxenus was taken prisoner as a boy, lived at Athens and later at the court of Dionysius I of Syracuse. The story was told that the tyrant sent him to the stone quarries for criticizing his tragedies, and that Philoxenus took revenge in his *Cyclops* (or *Galatea*), making Dionysius the Cyclops and himself Odysseus (Athenaeus 1.6e–7a). Aristophanes parodies the poem in the *Wealth* (290–321). In the *Poetics* Philoxenus and Timotheus portray Cyclopes, one better and one worse (1448a15; see [[cyclops-dithyrambs]] and [[timotheus]]).
