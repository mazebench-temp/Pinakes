## Life and work

Timotheus composed dithyrambs and citharodic nomes and was celebrated, and attacked, for his innovations: more strings on the lyre, mimetic music, and bold diction. Some 250 lines of his *Persians*, a nome on the battle of Salamis, survive on a papyrus of the fourth century BCE (see [[nome]]). Aristotle says in the *Metaphysics* that without Timotheus we would lack much of our lyric poetry, but without Phrynis there would have been no Timotheus (α 1, 993b15–16).

## In the Poetics

Timotheus and Philoxenus portrayed Cyclopes differently, the one better and the other worse, in dithyramb or nome (1448a14–16; see [[cyclops-dithyrambs]]). “The lament of Odysseus in the Scylla” is Aristotle's example of character that is unseemly and inappropriate (1454a30–31), and bad pipers drag the chorus leader about “if they are piping the Scylla” (1461b31–32); the *Scylla* is generally taken to be Timotheus' dithyramb (see [[scylla-of-timotheus]]).
