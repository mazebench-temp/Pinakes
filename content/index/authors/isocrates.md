summary: Athenian orator and teacher of rhetoric (436–338 BCE). His Helen praises her as the cause of the first war in which the Greeks united against Asia.
---

Isocrates wrote his speeches to be read rather than delivered, and his school at Athens was the rival of Plato's Academy. His *Helen* opens by faulting an earlier writer on Helen, generally taken to be Gorgias, for composing a defense of her instead of a praise (14–15). He then praises her for the men who desired her, beginning with Theseus; for the war fought over her, in which the Greeks first acted together against the barbarians; and for her power, which she showed when she blinded Stesichorus for slandering her and gave him back his sight when he recanted (64).
