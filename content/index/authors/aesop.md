Herodotus calls him “Aesop the fable-maker”, a slave, like the courtesan Rhodopis, of Iadmon of Samos, and says that the Delphians killed him and later paid blood-money for him to Iadmon's grandson (2.134). In prison Plato's Socrates turned into verse the fables of Aesop that he knew (*Phaedo* 60c–61b). No book of his survives; the collections that bear his name were made later.
