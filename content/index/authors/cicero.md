title: Cicero
kind: author
tags: [rhetoric]
summary: Roman orator and statesman (106–43 BCE), who quoted and translated Homer: Nestor's honeyed speech in On Old Age and the portent at Aulis in On Divination; in the Brutus he takes the singers of the Odyssey for poets before Homer and judges the Latin Odyssey of Livius Andronicus, and elsewhere he likens dialectic to Penelope unweaving her web and wishes for Homer's Minerva disguised as Mentor.
---

See [[cicero-nestor-honey]] and, for Jupiter complaining that he could not save his son Sarpedon from death against fate (*On Divination* 2.25), [[jupiter-and-sarpedon]].

On the Odyssey: the songs of the Phaeacians' and the suitors' feasts as proof of poets before Homer, and the Latin Odyssey “like some work of Daedalus” (*Brutus* 71–73; see [[phemius]] and [[odusia]]); Scipio's verse on the death of Tiberius Gracchus (*Pro Milone* 8; see [[scipio-and-tiberius-gracchus]]); dialectic, “like Penelope unweaving her web”, taking away in the end what came before (*Lucullus* 95; see [[penelopes-web-in-later-writers]]); and, before facing Caesar, the wish for “that Minerva of Homer's, disguised as Mentor” (*Letters to Atticus* 9.8.2; see [[athena-as-mentor-in-later-writers]]).
