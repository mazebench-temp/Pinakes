Crates began as an actor in the plays of Cratinus and won his first victory about 450 BCE. Aristophanes remembers him for his refined wit, which drew the audience's anger and its applause (*Knights* 537–540). In the *Poetics* he marks the point at which Athenian comedy turned from personal abuse to general plots: “of the poets at Athens, Crates was the first to give up the iambic form and compose stories and plots of a general kind” (1449b7–8; see [[iambus]], [[katholou]] and [[the-origins-of-comedy]]).
