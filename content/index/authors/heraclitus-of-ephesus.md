Heraclitus wrote a single book in short, riddling sentences, and later writers called him “the obscure”. Its opening, on the logos that is always and that men fail to understand, is quoted by Aristotle, who complains that it cannot be punctuated, since one cannot tell whether “always” goes with what precedes or what follows. He is not [[heraclitus-allegorist]], the later interpreter of Homer.
