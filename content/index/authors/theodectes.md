## Life and work

Theodectes came from Phaselis in Lycia, studied rhetoric with Isocrates, and associated with Plato and Aristotle; he won eight victories in fifty tragedies, and his tomb stood on the Sacred Way to Eleusis (Pausanias 1.37.4). Aristotle quotes him often in the *Rhetoric*, from his *Ajax*, his *Alcmaeon*, his *Orestes* and other works.

## In the Poetics

His *Lynceus* gives the example of reversal, the man led off to death saved and Danaus, his pursuer, killed (1452a27–29), and of the division between complication and resolution (1455b29–32; see [[lynceus-play]] and [[desis-and-lysis]]). His *Tydeus* has a recognition by reasoning: the hero, “having come to find his son, is himself to die” (1455a8–10; see [[tydeus-play]]).
