summary: Jean Racine (1639–1699), French tragic poet, author of Andromaque, Iphigénie and Phèdre, who took many of his subjects from Greek myth. His Andromaque makes Hector's widow the heroine of a tragedy of love and fidelity at the court of Pyrrhus.
---

Racine was educated at Port-Royal, where he learned Greek, and he read Homer and the tragedians in the original. His tragedies, in rhymed alexandrines, keep the unities of time, place and action. Four of them take their stories from Greek myth: *La Thébaïde* (1664), *Andromaque* (1667), *Iphigénie* (1674) and *Phèdre* (1677). In their prefaces he names his ancient sources, above all Euripides, and explains where he departed from them.
