summary: Sextus Propertius (c. 50–c. 15 BCE), Roman elegiac poet, author of four books of elegies, many of them on his love for Cynthia. His dead Cynthia comes to him in a dream as the soul of Patroclus comes to Achilles, and he names Adrastus' speaking horse Arion among the themes of Theban epic.
aliases: [Sextus Propertius]

In the elegy of his fourth book in which she comes back from the dead, Cynthia herself says that she reigned long in his books (*Elegies* 4.7.50).

- The ghost of Cynthia, who stands over his bed, reproaches him for forgetting her and for her poor funeral, foretells that her bones will soon be mixed with his, and slips from his embrace (*Elegies* 4.7): [[propertius-the-ghost-of-cynthia]].
- “Adrastus' speaking Arion”, the horse that won, in sorrow, at the funeral of Archemorus, among the themes of epic that, he tells his friend Lynceus, will be of no help to a lover (*Elegies* 2.34.25–40): [[arion-the-horse]].
- Penelope, who put off marriage “with a false Minerva, unweaving by a trick of the night what was woven by day”, set against an inconstant mistress (*Elegies* 2.9.3–8, 19): [[penelopes-web-in-later-writers]].
