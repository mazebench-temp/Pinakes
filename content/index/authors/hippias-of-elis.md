title: Hippias of Elis
greek: Ἱππίας

tags: [sophist]
summary: Sophist and polymath of Elis (late fifth century BCE), the speaker of Plato's two Hippias dialogues; probably the Hippias whom Aristotle sets beside Socrates as an example of an individual.
---

Hippias served his city as an envoy, lectured at Olympia on any subject his hearers chose, and boasted that he had made everything he wore, down to his ring and his shoes (Plato, *Hippias Minor* 368b–d). He compiled a list of Olympic victors (Plutarch, *Numa* 1.4) and lectured on Homer: in the *Hippias Minor* he holds that Achilles is truer and better than Odysseus. In Xenophon's *Memorabilia* he discusses with Socrates the unwritten laws, observed in every land and laid down, they agree, by the gods (4.4.19–25). He is a different man from the critic of the *Poetics* (see [[hippias-of-thasos]]).
