“The written style and the style of debate are not the same” (*Rhetoric* 3.12, 1413b4): the written style is the most precise, the style of debate the most suited to delivery, whether it shows character or emotion. Poets good to read are carried about in people's hands, while the speeches of writers seem thin in debate and those of orators amateurish in the hand. Repetition and asyndeta, rejected in writing, are right in debate. Of the kinds, speaking to the assembly is like shadow-painting, where precision is wasted; the judicial style is more precise; the epideictic is the most suited to writing (*Rhetoric* 1414a8–19).

## Outside the Rhetoric

Demetrius contrasts the written style with the style suited to acting in the same terms (*On Style* 193–194). See [[three-kinds-of-rhetoric]] and [[delivery]].
