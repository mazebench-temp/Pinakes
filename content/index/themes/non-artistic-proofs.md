## The five proofs

In an Athenian trial the litigants brought into court the laws they relied on, the depositions of witnesses, written contracts, the results of torture or a challenge to torture, and oaths sworn or offered. Evidence from slaves was admitted only from questioning under torture, and a litigant might challenge his opponent to hand over slaves for it; the speaker of Antiphon's speech against his stepmother makes the refusal of such a challenge an argument (Antiphon 1.6–13). Aristotle calls these proofs ἄτεχνοι, “non-artistic”, because they are not made by the speaker's art, but he gives the art of using them (see [[pistis]]).
