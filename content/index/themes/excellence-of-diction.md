## The argument

Diction of standard words is clear but mean, like the poetry of [[Cleophon]] and [[sthenelus-the-poet|Sthenelus]]; diction of unfamiliar words is dignified, but used throughout it becomes a riddle or barbarism (1458a18–31; see [[kyrion]], [[xenikon]], [[ainigma]] and [[barbarismos]]). So diction must be a blend: rare words, metaphors and ornaments lift it, the standard word keeps it clear, and lengthenings and alterations do both at once (1458a31–b5). Euclides the elder mocked such forms, but obtrusive use is the fault, not use: “moderation is common to all the kinds” (1458b5–15; see [[euclides-parodies]]). Substituting standard words into the verses of Aeschylus and Homer shows the difference (1458b15–31), and Ariphrades was wrong to laugh at the tragedians' unusual turns (1458b31–1459a4; see [[ariphrades-and-the-tragedians]]). The kinds of word suit different genres: compounds the dithyramb, rare words heroic verse, metaphors iambics (1459a8–14), and above all a gift for metaphor (see [[master-of-metaphor]]).
