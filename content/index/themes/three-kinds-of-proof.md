## The division

Of the artistic proofs, the ones the speaker builds (see [[pistis]]), some lie in the character of the speaker, some in putting the hearer into a frame of mind, and some in the speech itself, by showing or seeming to show. The speaker persuades by character when the speech makes him seem worthy of belief; by emotion, when the hearers are led into it, for people do not judge alike when they are pained and when they are glad; and by argument, when he shows what is true or seems so (see [[ethos]], [[pathos]] and [[logos]]). The second book of the *Rhetoric* treats the emotions one by one and the characters of hearers by age and fortune; the first and the second treat the arguments.

{{notes}}

## After Aristotle

The triad, under its Greek names ēthos, pathos and logos, is the best known of Aristotle's doctrines. Cicero's orator has three tasks, to prove, to win over and to move (*On the Orator* 2.115), a Roman version of the same division.
