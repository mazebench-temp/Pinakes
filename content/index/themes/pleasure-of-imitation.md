## Learning and inference

The pleasure is in recognition: we see the likeness and infer what it is. If one has never seen the original, the pleasure will come not from the imitation as such but from the workmanship or the color. Aristotle makes the same argument in the *Rhetoric*: painting, sculpture, poetry and everything well imitated give pleasure even when the original is not pleasant, for one enjoys not the thing but the inference that “this is that”, and so one learns something (1.11, 1371b4–10). In the *Parts of Animals* he uses it to defend the study of humble creatures: if we enjoy their images for the art of the painter or sculptor, we should enjoy still more the study of the animals themselves and their causes (1.5, 645a7–15). The pleasure proper to tragedy, which comes from pity and fear through imitation, is a special case (see [[tragic-pleasure]] and [[mimesis]]).
