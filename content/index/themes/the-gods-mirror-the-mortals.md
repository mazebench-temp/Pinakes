The gods of the Iliad live as the kings of the poem live, only more easily. Olympus has its king, its assemblies (4.1–4, 8.1–3, 20.4–6), its quarrels, its messengers and its feasts, and the gods speak to one another in the formulas of human speech. More than once the poet tells a story among men and then among the gods, and builds the second to recall the first. The parallels show how closely the gods resemble men. The differences show what the gods can afford: their quarrels end in laughter, while among men a quarrel is paid for in lives.
