## The principle

The thought runs through the *Poetics*. Tragedy keeps to names of people who existed because the possible is convincing, and what has happened is evidently possible (1451b15–19); but the poet's task is to tell what could happen by probability or necessity (see [[eikos]] and [[poetry-and-history]]). In chapter 24 Aristotle puts it as a rule, “one should prefer probable impossibilities to unconvincing possibilities” (1460a26–27), and in chapter 25 as a defense against critics: “for poetry, a convincing impossibility is preferable to what is unconvincing though possible” (1461b11–12; see [[adynaton]], [[pithanon]] and [[problems-and-solutions]]). Coleridge's “willing suspension of disbelief for the moment, which constitutes poetic faith” (*Biographia Literaria*, chapter 14) names the same consent from the audience's side.
