## Meaning

In Homer φόβος is not the feeling but the flight it causes, panic and rout: φόβονδε is “to flight”, and Phobos is a god, the son of Ares (see [[rout]]). Homer's word for the feeling is δέος. By the fifth century φόβος is fear in general. Aristotle defines it in the *Rhetoric* as “a pain or disturbance arising from the imagination of a future evil that is destructive or painful” (2.5, 1382a21–22), and notes that we fear for others what we would pity in them if it happened (1382b26–27).
