The Iliad's action takes about fifty days, and Book 1 spends twenty-one of them. It does so with a few counted intervals:

| Days | Lines | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| 1–9 | 1–52 | Chryses is refused; Apollo's arrows fall for nine days |
| 10 | 53–476 | The assembly and quarrel; Briseis taken; Thetis comes to Achilles; Odysseus reaches Chryse and sacrifices |
| 11 | 477–487 | The ship sails back to the camp |
| 11–20 | 488–492 | Achilles sits idle by the ships |
| 21 | 493–611 | The gods return; Thetis supplicates Zeus; the quarrel and feast on Olympus |

{{quote:4e0b98c1-d4cc-5be5-87fa-b6206dd5949f}}

The plague is counted in days, nine and then "on the tenth". The same pattern, nine days and then something on the tenth, recurs in Homer for long, even stretches of time.

{{quote:3e5fbf5a-0fc2-5673-a6f6-98884d29224e}}

Thetis' "twelfth day" sets the second interval. The narrator bridges it in five lines of repeated action (1.488–492), then picks up the gods' return on the twelfth dawn. Day 10 alone takes more than two-thirds of the book: Homer slows down where the choices are made. See [[eos]] and [[achilles-withdraws]].
