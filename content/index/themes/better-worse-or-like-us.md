## The division

People differ in character by virtue and badness, and so those who imitate people in action imitate them as better than we are, worse, or like us. Aristotle's examples come from painting and from every kind of poetry: Polygnotus, Pauson and Dionysius among painters; Homer, Cleophon, Hegemon of Thasos and Nicochares among poets; the Cyclopes of Timotheus and Philoxenus among dithyrambs. Tragedy and comedy are set apart by this difference, comedy aiming to imitate people worse, tragedy better, than those of today (see [[polygnotus]], [[pauson]], [[dionysius-of-colophon]], [[cleophon]], [[hegemon-of-thasos]], [[nicochares]], [[cyclops-dithyrambs]] and [[spoudaios]]). Northrop Frye built his theory of fictional modes on this passage, classing works by the hero's power of action, greater than ours, less, or roughly the same (*Anatomy of Criticism*, 1957, first essay).
