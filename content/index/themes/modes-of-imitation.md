## The division

Imitations differ by their media, their objects and their manner (see [[mimesis]]). In the same medium and of the same objects one may imitate by narrating, either becoming someone else, as Homer does, or remaining oneself without change, or by presenting all the people imitated as acting and doing. So Sophocles is the same kind of imitator as Homer in one respect, since both imitate serious people, and the same as Aristophanes in another, since both imitate people acting, which is why, some say, such works are called dramas (see [[drama]], [[epic-and-tragedy]] and [[the-poet-should-say-little]]). Plato's *Republic* draws the same three-way division, between plain narrative, imitation, and the mixture of the two that Homer uses (3, 392d–394c).
