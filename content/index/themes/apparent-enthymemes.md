## The fallacies

As there are syllogisms that only appear to be syllogisms, so there are apparent enthymemes, which are not enthymemes at all (2.24). Aristotle's list follows the fallacies of his *Sophistical Refutations*: from diction, by putting a conclusion in conclusive form or by homonymy; from combining what is divided or dividing what is combined; from exaggeration; from a sign; from the accidental; from the consequent; from taking what is not a cause as the cause; from the omission of when and how; and from confusing what is so absolutely with what is so in some respect, the art of Corax, “making the weaker argument the stronger” (see [[the-art-of-corax]] and [[protagoras]]). See [[enthymema]], [[topics-of-enthymemes]] and [[syllogismos]].
