Homer marks silence with the adverb ἀκέων, "in silence", and with what people do not say.

{{quote:d71b641b-af05-5bb7-b9af-c0e7d9688bbf}}

The old man obeys and walks off in silence beside the loud-roaring sea. The silence and the roar set each other off, and the next sound is his prayer.

{{quote:018f0906-012a-5410-bfe0-8434494d3bb1}}

The heralds stand "in fear and awe of the king" and say nothing. Achilles has to speak first.

{{quote:4a75cc92-2850-5123-86d1-6cad9620996b}}

Zeus' silence is the longest in the book. It is the pause before the decision on which the poem turns. Thetis holds on through it.

{{quote:e28d11c0-1bf6-5c02-8e19-57675fb003b0}}

Zeus orders Hera to "sit in silence", and she does, "bending her heart to obedience". On Olympus, silence is what defeat looks like. See [[words-and-violence]] and [[obedience-and-persuasion]].
