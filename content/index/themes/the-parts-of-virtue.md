## The virtues

Greek ethics before Aristotle knew a canon of four, wisdom, courage, self-control and justice, which Plato's *Republic* assigns to the parts of the soul and the classes of the city (4, 427e–434d). Aristotle's ethics multiplies them: the *Nicomachean Ethics* treats the virtues of character one by one, courage, self-control, generosity, magnificence, greatness of soul, gentleness and others (Books 3–4), justice in Book 5, and the virtues of thought, practical wisdom and wisdom among them, in Book 6. The *Rhetoric*'s list is shorter and defined for the use of speakers, by what each virtue does and, for three of them, by the law (see [[arete]] and [[kalon]]).
