## The distinction

The difference lies in the actions imitated, which are “of these two kinds from the first” (1452a12–14). Reversal and recognition must arise “from the very structure of the plot”, so that they follow from what has gone before by necessity or probability, “for it makes a great difference whether things happen because of what went before or only after it” (1452a18–21; see [[peripeteia]], [[anagnorisis]] and [[eikos]]). Of simple plots the episodic are the worst (1451b33–35; see [[epeisodion]]).

The finest tragedy is complex (1452b31–32; see [[the-best-tragic-plot]]), and the complex tragedy, “all reversal and recognition”, is the first of the four kinds (1455b32–34; see [[four-kinds-of-tragedy]]). Epic has the same kinds, simple or complex, of character or of suffering: “the Iliad simple and full of suffering, the Odyssey complex (there is recognition throughout) and full of character” (1459b8–16; see [[iliad]] and [[odyssey]]).
