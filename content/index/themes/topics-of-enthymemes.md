## The list

After the special topics drawn from the subjects of each kind of speech and the common topics, Aristotle lists the general topics of enthymemes, “elements” from which arguments on any subject can be made (2.23). They are, in order: from opposites; from like inflections; from correlatives; from the more and the less; from the time; from turning what was said against the speaker; from definition; from the number of senses; from division; from induction; from a previous judgment; from the parts; from consequences; from two opposite courses; from what is praised openly and what in secret; from analogy; from the same consequence; from changed choice; from the purpose; from incentives and deterrents; from the incredible; from inconsistency; from the cause of a false suspicion; from the cause; from a better course; from opposites done; from mistakes; from the name. The refutative are more admired than the demonstrative, and both are applauded most when the hearer foresees them, but not from far off. The next chapter lists the topics of apparent enthymemes (see [[apparent-enthymemes]]). See [[topos]], [[enthymema]] and [[topics-of-aristotle]].
