## The argument

Why should anyone enjoy watching suffering? Aristotle's answer begins in chapter 4: we enjoy accurate images even of things painful to see, because we learn from them (1448b10–19; see [[pleasure-of-imitation]]). Tragedy's pleasure is “the pleasure that comes from pity and fear through imitation”, and so the poet must build it into the incidents (1453b10–14). The pleasure of a happy ending for the good and an unhappy one for the bad “is not the pleasure that comes from tragedy; it belongs rather to comedy” (1453a35–36; see [[the-double-plot]]). Epic, too, should produce “its proper pleasure”, like one whole living creature (1459a20–21), and tragedy is superior to epic because it achieves the end of the art better, producing “not any chance pleasure but the one we have described” (1462b12–15; see [[epic-and-tragedy]]). How the pleasure is related to [[katharsis]] Aristotle does not say in what survives.
