At first the poets ran through any stories that came to hand; now the finest tragedies are about a few houses, those in which someone happened “to suffer or to do terrible things” (1453a17–22). Chapter 14 gives the reason. The plots that produce pity and fear set terrible deeds among kin, and the poets, searching “not by art but by chance”, found such deeds in a few families and are forced to return to them (1454a9–13).

The six names stand for six sagas: [[Alcmaeon]], who killed his mother Eriphyle; [[Oedipus]], who killed his father and married his mother; [[orestes|Orestes]], who killed his mother Clytemnestra; [[Meleager]], killed by his mother Althaea; [[Thyestes]], who ate his own children at his brother's table; and [[Telephus]], who killed his mother's brothers and was wounded and healed by Achilles. Surviving tragedy bears him out: the house of Atreus and the house of Laius between them supply most of the extant plays.
