## The pattern

Book 1 opens with a father:

{{quote:269356fa-d33a-518f-8c99-240a0367bcb1}}

Agamemnon refuses him and threatens him, and the whole book follows from that refusal. Book 24 closes with another father. Priam, king of Troy, comes by night to Achilles' hut to ransom the body of his son Hector. He brings a boundless ransom and kneels to kiss the hands that killed his son. Achilles accepts, and grieves with him, for his own father and for Patroclus.

The correspondences run through both books:

| Book 1 | Book 24 |
|---|---|
| Chryses, an old man, ransoms a daughter | Priam, an old man, ransoms a son |
| Agamemnon refuses: "Old man, let me not find you beside the hollow ships" (1.26) | Achilles accepts, though he warns Priam against provoking him (24.560–570) |
| Thetis goes up to Olympus to ask Zeus for honor for her son | Thetis is summoned to Olympus to tell her son to give back the body |
| Zeus nods, and the Achaeans' suffering begins | Zeus decides, and the Trojans get their dead |
| Nine days of plague, then an assembly | Nine days of mourning, then a funeral |

The poem begins in a quarrel over a refused ransom and ends in the one ransom the poem allows to succeed. The wrath opened with a man who would not accept gifts for a captive. It ends when Achilles accepts gifts for a corpse.

## Outside Book 1

The ransom of Hector fills Book 24; Priam's plea is 24.486–506. The nine days of mourning and the funeral close the poem (24.664–804). See [[ring-composition]] for the same shaping on a smaller scale.
