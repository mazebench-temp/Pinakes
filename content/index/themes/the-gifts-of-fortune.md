## Fortune and character

Book I of the *Rhetoric* lists good birth, wealth, friends and good fortune among the parts of happiness (1.5, 1360b19–30; see [[eudaimonia]]). Book II asks what characters these goods produce. Good birth makes men fonder of honor and contemptuous even of those like their ancestors; but the well-born are seldom true to their birth, and families degenerate, the gifted into madness and the steady into dullness (2.15). Wealth makes men insolent and arrogant, as if they had all goods, luxurious and swaggering; Simonides' answer to Hieron's wife shows why (2.16; see [[rich-or-wise]]). Power makes men fonder of honor and more serious, dignified rather than heavy; good fortune makes them arrogant and thoughtless, but also god-loving (2.17). See [[tyche]], [[gennaios]] and [[the-characters-of-the-ages]].
