## The division

The six parts follow from the definition (see [[definition-of-tragedy]]). Because tragedy is enacted, it has spectacle; because it imitates in language and music, it has diction and song; because it imitates an action done by people of a certain character and thought, it has plot, character and thought:

{{quote:73a1f879-0220-528b-a181-463b64cabc68}}

The parts are ranked in chapter 6, and the ranking is the argument of the treatise. First is [[mythos|plot]], “the first principle and, as it were, the soul of tragedy” (1450a38–39); second [[ethos|character]]; third [[dianoia|thought]]; fourth [[lexis|diction]]; of the rest, [[melopoiia|song]] is the greatest of the embellishments, and [[opsis|spectacle]] the least artistic (1450a15–b20). Chapters 7–18 treat plot and character, chapter 19 thought, chapters 20–22 diction.

These are parts “as constituent kinds” (ὡς εἴδεσι), distinct from the quantitative sections into which a performance divides, prologue, episode, exodos and choral songs (1452b14–27; see [[prologue]] and [[epeisodion]]). Epic has the same parts, except song and spectacle (1459b9–12; see [[epic-and-tragedy]]).
