## The handbooks

Tradition made Corax and Tisias of Syracuse the first writers of an art of speaking, in the fifth century, and Plato's *Phaedrus* reviews their successors, Theodorus, Evenus, Tisias, Gorgias, Prodicus, Hippias, Polus, Licymnius, Protagoras and Thrasymachus, with their parts of a speech, their figures and their appeals to pity and anger (266d–267d; see [[evenus-of-paros]] and [[gorgias]]). Aristotle collected the early handbooks in a lost *Collection of Arts*, which Cicero says replaced them, so clear and brief was its account (*On Invention* 2.6).
