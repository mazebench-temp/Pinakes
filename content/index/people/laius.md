summary: King of Thebes, son of Labdacus, killed at a crossroads by his son Oedipus, who did not know him. That Oedipus never learned how Laius died is Aristotle's example of an irrational element kept outside the drama.
aliases: [Laios, Laius of Thebes]
---

Warned by Apollo that his son would kill him, Laius had the infant Oedipus exposed; years later, traveling to Delphi, he quarreled with a stranger at the place where the roads from Delphi and Daulis meet and was killed with all but one of his attendants (Sophocles, *Oedipus the King* 711–813; see [[oedipus-kills-laius]]). Aeschylus wrote a *Laius*, the first play of his Theban trilogy.
