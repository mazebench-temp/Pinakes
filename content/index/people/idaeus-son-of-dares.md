When his brother Phegeus falls, Idaeus leaves “the beautiful chariot” and runs. He “did not dare to stand over his slain brother” (5.21): περιβῆναι is to bestride a fallen comrade and defend the body, as Aeneas bestrides Pandarus later in the book (5.299). The narrator says that he would not have escaped either, and gives the reason he did: Hephaestus “saved him, covering him in night”, for the sake of his father, the god's priest (5.23–24; see [[dares]]). It is the first of the book's rescues by a god. Aphrodite and then Apollo will carry Aeneas out of the fighting (5.311–318, 5.344–346; see [[divine-rescue]]). Diomedes drives off the horses (5.25–26).

His name means “of Ida”, the mountain above Troy (see [[ida]]). Priam's herald has the same name (see [[idaeus]]).
