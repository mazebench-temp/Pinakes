**His death.** Otus is the last of three leaders killed in three verses, when Hector and Ajax have each urged on their men: Hector kills Schedius, son of Perimedes, Ajax kills Laodamas, son of Antenor, and Polydamas kills Otus (15.515–519; see [[schedius-son-of-perimedes]], [[laodamas]] and [[polydamas]]). ἐξενάριξε, “slew and stripped” (see [[stripping-the-dead]]). Meges sees it and springs at Polydamas, who ducks aside, “for Apollo / would not let the son of Panthous be overpowered among the champions” (15.520–522; see [[meges]] and [[apollo]]); Meges' spear takes Croesmus instead (15.523; see [[croesmus]]).

**Companion of the son of Phyleus.** Φυλεΐδεω ἕταρον, μεγαθύμων ἀρχὸν Ἐπειῶν, “companion of the son of Phyleus, and a leader of the great-hearted Epeians” (15.519; see [[phyleus]] and [[epeians]]); the leader is Otus, not Meges (see the translation's note on 15.519). Meges leads the men of Dulichium and the Echinae in the Catalogue (2.625–630; see [[dulichium]]), but his father Phyleus came from Elis, and where Hector broke in Meges is named among the leaders of the Epeians (13.691–692). Otus is one of those Epeians, and the first man Meges tries to avenge.

**Cyllene.** Κυλλήνιον, “of Cyllene” (15.518). The Iliad's other Cyllene is the Arcadian mountain, “the steep mountain of Cyllene” (2.603; see [[cyllene]]); a town of the name is not mentioned, and the poem does not say which place it means (see [[cyllene-elis]]).

**The name.** Homer's other Otus is the giant son of Aloeus, who with his brother Ephialtes bound Ares in a bronze jar (5.385–391; *Odyssey* 11.305–320; see [[otus]]). The poem makes no connection between them.

## Outside Homer

Strabo, describing the coast of Elis, says that Homer means Cyllene, the port of the Eleans, “for he would not have made a man from the Arcadian mountain leader of the Epeians” (*Geography* 8.3.4; see [[cyllene-elis]] and [[strabo]]). In another book he explains the companionship from the same history: Dulichium and the other Echinades were under Meges, and the Epeians who lived there had come from Elis, “which is why” the poet calls Otus of Cyllene the companion of the son of Phyleus and a leader of the Epeians (*Geography* 10.2.14; see [[dulichium]]).
