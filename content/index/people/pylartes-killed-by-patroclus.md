ἠδὲ Πυλάρτην, “and Pylartes” (16.696), ends the list of nine men whom Patroclus kills when the narrator asks him “whom first, whom last” (16.692–697; see [[adrastus-killed-by-patroclus]], [[whom-first-whom-last]] and [[patroclus]]). “These he killed; and the others each turned their minds to flight” (16.697).

The verse ends as the verse does in which Telamonian Ajax wounds a Pylartes in Book 11: “and wounded Lysander, and Pyrasus, and Pylartes” (11.491; see [[pylartes]] and [[ajax]]). οὖτα, “wounded”, does not say whether that Pylartes died, and the poem does not say whether he is the man whom Patroclus kills here; the index keeps them apart. The name is the word πυλάρτης, “gatekeeper”, Hades' title (8.367; see [[gatekeeper]]).
