## Etymology

Νέστωρ (Nestōr). Often connected with νέομαι, "return home safely", so that the name would mean something like "he who brings home". The derivation is plausible but not certain.

## Descriptions

{{quote:04e146fb-f34a-51ee-a642-191bb5855dde}}

ἡδυεπής = ἡδύς (sweet) + ἔπος (word): sweet of speech. λιγύς: clear-voiced, piercing, used of the Muses and of singers. Πυλίων ἀγορητής: the speaker of the Pylians. His voice flows "sweeter than honey" (μέλιτος γλυκίων).

{{quote:b2e867e1-8f01-58be-b69f-dcd8a8de3710}}

Two generations of his contemporaries have died, and he rules among the third. His age is his authority.

## In Book 1

Nestor's speech is the model of Homeric counsel. It has three movements: a warning (the Trojans would rejoice), an example from the past (the Lapiths listened to me), and advice to each side in turn.

## Outside Homer

Nestor is the son of Neleus (Iliad 11.682–761). Heracles sacked Pylos and killed his eleven brothers (11.690–693). His long tales of youth in the Iliad (7.132–157, 11.670–761, 23.629–642) are gathered under the period [[nestor]] in the Myths. In Odyssey 3 he receives Telemachus at Pylos. Cicero quoted his honeyed speech as proof that old age need not silence a man (*On Old Age* 31); see [[cicero-nestor-honey]]. The Mycenaean palace excavated at Pylos by Carl Blegen from 1939 is known as the Palace of Nestor.
