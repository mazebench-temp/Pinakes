ἀντίθεον Πολυφήτην, “godlike Polyphetes” (13.791), is the only man of the six in this list to be given an epithet (see [[antitheos]]). The list names the men around Cebriones and Polydamas, where Hector and Paris go to the thickest of the fighting (see [[hector]], [[cebriones]] and [[polydamas]]); whether Polyphetes is one of those who had come from Ascania “as relief” the morning before is not clear (13.793–794; see [[ascanius-the-mysian]]). He does nothing in the poem and is not named again.
