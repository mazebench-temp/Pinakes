μαντοσύναι, "the arts of prophecy" (plural of μαντοσύνη). Merops knew them "above all men" and still could not keep his sons at home. Other sons of seers die at Troy. The Corinthian seer Polyidus told his son Euchenor that he would die of a painful sickness at home or be killed by the Trojans at the ships; Euchenor chose Troy (13.663–672). Diomedes kills the sons of the old dream-reader Eurydamas, "but the old man read no dreams for them when they set out" (5.148–151). See [[the-sons-of-merops]] and [[prophecy]].

Later mythographers make Merops the father of Arisbe, Priam's first wife, and the teacher of his grandson Aesacus in the interpretation of dreams (Apollodorus 3.12.5). The name is the same as the old epithet of mortals, μέροπες ἄνθρωποι ([[meropes]]).
