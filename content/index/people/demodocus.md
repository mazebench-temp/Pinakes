“The Muse loved him greatly, and gave him both good and evil: she took away his eyes, but gave him sweet song” (*Odyssey* 8.63–64). He sings of the quarrel of Odysseus and Achilles, of Ares and Aphrodite, and, at Odysseus' request, of the Wooden Horse, and Odysseus weeps (*Odyssey* 8.62–82, 8.266–366, 8.487–531). Aristotle calls him simply “the lyre player” (1455a2–3; see [[the-tale-to-alcinous]] and [[odysseus-weeps-at-the-feast]]).
