Athena sends Odysseus to him first when she has changed him into a beggar (*Odyssey* 13.404–411), and the poem names him when Odysseus comes to his hut (*Odyssey* 14.55). He tells his story in *Odyssey* 15.403–484: he was the son of a king of Syrie, stolen by a Phoenician nurse. Before the contest of the bow Odysseus shows him and the cowherd Philoetius his scar to prove who he is (*Odyssey* 21.205–225). Aristotle: Odysseus “was recognized through his scar in one way by the nurse and in another by the swineherds”, and recognitions made for the sake of proof are less artistic (*Poetics* 1454b26–29; see [[the-scar-of-odysseus]]). In his *Constitution of the Ithacans* Aristotle told that Telemachus freed Eumaeus' people and settled them among the citizens, and that the family of the Koliadai descended from Eumaeus, as the Boukoloi from the cowherd Philoetius (fr. 511 Gigon, from Plutarch's *Greek Questions*; see [[aristotle]]).
