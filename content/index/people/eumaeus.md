Eumaeus tells his story in Odyssey 15 (403–484): he was the son of a king of Syrie, stolen by a Phoenician nurse. Before the contest of the bow Odysseus shows him and the cowherd Philoetius his scar to prove who he is (*Odyssey* 21.205–225). Aristotle: Odysseus “was recognized through his scar in one way by the nurse and in another by the swineherds”, and recognitions made for the sake of proof are less artistic (1454b26–29; see [[the-scar-of-odysseus]]).
