Πάλμύν τʼ Ἀσκάνιόν τε Μόρυν θʼ υἷʼ Ἱπποτίωνος, “Palmys and Ascanius and Morys, son of Hippotion” (13.792): the three names before the relative clause that brings them from Ascania ἀμοιβοί, “as relief”, on the morning before, the morning of the day of Book 8 (13.793–794; see [[ascanius-the-mysian]], [[morys]] and [[ascania]]). They stand with Cebriones and Polydamas where the fighting is thickest when Hector and Paris come (see [[hector]] and [[polydamas]]). Strabo, who quotes these verses, takes the Ascania they came from as the Mysian Ascania near Nicaea (*Geography* 12.4.5; see [[mysians]]). Palmys does nothing in the poem and is not named again.
