Ἰδαῖος means "of Ida", the mountain of the Troad ([[ida]]). He is one of the heralds who carry the offerings for the oath through the city: the two lambs, the wine in a goatskin, and, in his own hands, the shining mixing bowl and the golden cups (3.245–248; see [[heralds]] and [[mixing-bowl]]). He stands beside Priam, calls him by his father's name, "Rise, son of Laomedon", and gives him the terms of the duel (3.250–258). Hector had sent two heralds to the city (3.116); the poem names only Idaeus.

{{notes}}

## Elsewhere in Homer

In Book 7 he parts Hector and Ajax at nightfall and carries Paris' offer and Priam's request for a truce to the Achaean ships (7.273–282, 7.372–416). In Book 24 he drives the mule wagon with Hector's ransom and goes with Priam by night to Achilles' hut (24.325, 24.470–471). A different Idaeus, son of Dares, the priest of Hephaestus, is saved from Diomedes by the god (5.9–24).
