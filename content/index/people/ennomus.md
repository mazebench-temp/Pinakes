{{quote:81a9365c-5c70-5d61-918e-9d23ebee7634}}

οἰωνιστής, "augur", from οἰωνός, "bird of omen" ([[birds]], [[divination]]): a reader of the flight and cries of birds. The irony is plain. The man who read the future in birds could not see his own. The narrator adds such a death to the list of leaders only here and for the Carian in gold (2.872–875); the sons of the seer Merops, a few lines earlier, are another case of foreknowledge that saved no one (2.831–834). Ennomus is still alive in Book 17, where Hector calls on him by name (17.218). The river fighting of Book 21 does not mention him. An Ennomus killed by Odysseus in Book 11 would seem to be another man. See [[the-river-battle-foretold]].
