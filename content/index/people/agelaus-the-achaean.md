Ἀγέλαος joins ἄγω, “lead”, and λαός, “people”: “leader of the people”, a fit name for one of “the leaders of the Danaans” (11.304). He ends the second verse of the list, after Opheltius (11.302; see [[opheltius-the-achaean]]), and Homer tells nothing more of him (see [[hector]] and [[whom-first-whom-last]]).

The name belongs to two other men. The Trojan Agelaus, son of Phradmon, was the first man Diomedes killed when the Achaeans came out again after the eagle of Zeus (8.257; see [[agelaus]]), and in the Odyssey Agelaus, son of Damastor, is one of the suitors (Od. 20.321, 22.212).
