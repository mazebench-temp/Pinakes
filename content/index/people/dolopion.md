Hypsenor is the “son of high-hearted Dolopion, who had been made priest of the Scamander and was honored by the people like a god” (5.77–78). ὑπέρθυμος, “high-hearted”, is a common epithet of warriors (see [[high-hearted]]). ἀρητήρ, literally “one who prays”, from ἀράομαι (“pray”), is the word used of Chryses, the priest of Apollo (1.11, 1.94; see [[chryses]] and [[priesthood]]). The river has a priest because it is a god. Achilles later tells the Trojans that the river to which they sacrifice many bulls, and into whose eddies they throw live horses, will not save them (21.130–132). See [[scamander]].

θεὸς δʼ ὣς τίετο δήμῳ, “and was honored by the people like a god”, is a formula of rank. It is said of kings, Agamemnon (10.33) and Thoas (13.218), of Aeneas (11.58), and of another priest, Onetor, priest of Zeus of Ida, whose son Laogonus Meriones kills (16.603–605). Book 5 has two priests whose sons are struck down: Dares, priest of Hephaestus, whose god saves one of his two sons (5.9–24; see [[dares]]), and Dolopion, whose son no god saves. See [[hypsenor]] and [[bereaved-parents]].
