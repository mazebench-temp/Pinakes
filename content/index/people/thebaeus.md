“Eniopeus, son of high-hearted Thebaeus” (8.120): the poem gives him one verse and an epithet, ὑπέρθυμος, “high-hearted” (see [[high-hearted]]), and tells nothing else about him. His son drives for Hector and is killed by Diomedes' spear (8.118–123; see [[eniopeus]]).

Θηβαῖος is also the word for “Theban”. In the Odyssey Teiresias is Θηβαῖος, “the Theban” (Od. 10.492, 11.90). Here it is a man's name, as the translation takes it, and the poem does not connect him with Thebes, either the Boeotian city or Eetion's Thebe in the Troad (see [[thebes]] and [[thebe]]).
