Ἀλκιμέδων υἱὸς Λαέρκεος Αἱμονίδαο, “Alcimedon, son of Laerces, son of Haemon” (17.467): Alcimedon sees Automedon fighting alone from the chariot of Achilles' horses, and the narrator names him with his father and his grandfather (see [[alcimedon]] and [[automedon]]). The genitive Αἱμονίδαο agrees with Λαέρκεος, so the second patronymic belongs to the father: Laerces is the son of Haemon (see [[laerces]]). Phoenix, who led the fourth company of the Myrmidons as Alcimedon led the fifth (16.196–197), names his own father in the same way, “Amyntor, son of Ormenus” (Ἀμύντορος Ὀρμενίδαο, 9.448; see [[phoenix]] and [[amyntor]]). Nothing else is told of him.

Homer's other two men of the name are the Pylian Haemon, one of Nestor's captains (4.296; see [[haemon]]), and the Theban whose son Maeon Tydeus spared (4.394; see [[haemon-of-thebes]]). Sophocles' Haemon, the son of Creon, belongs to the legend of Thebes (see [[haemon-son-of-creon]]).
