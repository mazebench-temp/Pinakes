Αἴολος Ἱπποτάδης, “Aeolus, son of Hippotes” (*Odyssey* 10.2): Hippotes is named only in the patronymic of the keeper of the winds, and the poem says nothing else of him (see [[aeolus]]). The name is the word ἱππότης, “horseman”, which in the Iliad and the Odyssey is the title of old Nestor, Γερήνιος ἱππότα Νέστωρ, “the Gerenian horseman Nestor” (*Iliad* 2.336 and often; see [[nestor]]). W. W. Merry takes the patronymic, like the name Aeolus, to describe the rapid movement of the wind, from ἵππος, “horse” (*Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on *Odyssey* 10.1).

{{notes}}

## Outside Homer

Ovid keeps the patronymic: Macareus tells of “Aeolus, son of Hippotes” (*Aeolon Hippotaden*), who holds the winds in prison (*Metamorphoses* 14.224), and Alcyone, warning her husband Ceyx, calls her father “your father-in-law, the son of Hippotes” (*socer Hippotades*, *Metamorphoses* 11.431; see [[ovid]]). Merry gives Aeolus' mother as Melanippe, without naming his source (Merry on *Odyssey* 10.1).
