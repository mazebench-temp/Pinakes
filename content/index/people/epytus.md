**A herald's name.** Apollo rouses Aeneas “in form like Periphas, / the herald, son of Epytus” (17.323–324; see [[periphas-son-of-epytus]] and [[aeneas]]). ἠπύτα, “loud-voiced”, is the epithet of the herald Idaeus (7.384; see [[idaeus]] and [[heralds]]), from the verb ἠπύω (14.399; *Odyssey* 9.399), “cry out, call”, and Walter Leaf takes Ἠπυτίδης as a name formed from the herald's profession, like the craftsmen's names Ἁρμονίδης (5.60) and Τεκτονίδης (*Odyssey* 8.114) (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 17.324; see the translation's note on 17.324 and [[harmon]]). The nominative Ἤπυτος is not found in Homer; it is inferred from the patronymic, and the poem says nothing more of him.
