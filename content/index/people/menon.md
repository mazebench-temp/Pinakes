αὐτὰρ ἔπειτα Μένωνα καὶ Ἰαμενὸν καὶ Ὀρέστην, “and then Menon and Iamenus and Orestes” (12.193): Leonteus kills the three, “all of them, one after another” (12.194), after Hippomachus and Antiphates (see [[leonteus]], [[iamenus]] and [[orestes-the-trojan]]). Iamenus and Orestes came on with Asius (12.139); Menon has no other mention, and the poem gives him no wound.

The name looks like the participle μένων, “staying, standing one's ground”, a word found everywhere in the poem.
