## The name

Ἁλιθέρσης joins ἅλς, “sea” (ἁλι- in compounds, as ἁλιπόρφυρος, “sea-purple”, *Odyssey* 6.53), to θέρσος, the Aeolic form of θάρσος, “boldness”, which is also in the names of Thersites and Thersilochus (see [[thersites]] and [[thersilochus]]): “bold on the sea”. The name says nothing of his art.

## Family

He is the son of Mastor, Μαστορίδης (*Odyssey* 2.158, 24.452), of whom nothing else is known (see [[mastor-father-of-halitherses]]). The Iliad's Mastor, the father of Lycophron, is another man (see [[mastor]]).

## Descriptions

- γέρων ἥρως, “the old hero” (*Odyssey* 2.157 = 24.451). Aegyptius, the first speaker in the assembly, is likewise “the hero Aegyptius”, “who was bent with age” (*Odyssey* 2.15–16; see [[aegyptius]], [[heros]] and [[geron]]).
- ὁ γὰρ οἶος ὁμηλικίην ἐκέκαστο / ὄρνιθας γνῶναι καὶ ἐναίσιμα μυθήσασθαι, “for he alone surpassed those of his age / in knowing birds and in speaking words of fate” (*Odyssey* 2.158–159; see [[birds]] and [[divination]]). The Iliad uses the verb of Hippodameia, who “surpassed all the girls of her age / in beauty and handiwork and wits” (*Iliad* 13.431–432; see [[hippodameia-daughter-of-anchises]]), and introduces its two seers, Calchas and Helenus, as “by far the best of the bird-seers” (*Iliad* 1.69, 6.76; see [[best-of-bird-seers]]).
- ὁ γὰρ οἶος ὅρα πρόσσω καὶ ὀπίσσω, “for he alone saw before and after” (*Odyssey* 24.452), the verse that the Iliad gives to Polydamas in the Trojan assembly (*Iliad* 18.250; see [[before-and-after]] and [[polydamas]]).
- ἐὺ φρονέων, “with good intent” (*Odyssey* 2.160; ἐϋφρονέων, *Odyssey* 24.453), in the verse that brings in Calchas before he explains the plague (*Iliad* 1.73), Polydamas after “before and after” (*Iliad* 18.253) and Mentor in the same assembly of Ithaca (*Odyssey* 2.228; see [[with-good-intent]] and [[calchas]]).
- πατρώιοι ἑταῖροι, “his father's companions”, said of Mentor and Halitherses by Leiocritus (*Odyssey* 2.253–254) and, with Antiphus, by the narrator (*Odyssey* 17.68–69; see [[mentor]] and [[hetairos]]).

## Later in the Odyssey

When Telemachus comes back from Pylos and goes out to the place of assembly, he keeps away from the throng of the suitors and sits where Mentor, Antiphus and Halitherses sit, “who were his father's companions from of old”, and they question him about everything (*Odyssey* 17.67–70, a plain rendering; the second verse is *Odyssey* 2.254, with ἦσαν for εἰσιν).

In the last book, when the kinsmen of the dead suitors gather in the place of assembly, the herald Medon tells them that a god stood beside Odysseus, in all things like Mentor (*Odyssey* 24.443–449). Then Halitherses rises, with the verse that brought him in before and the verse of Polydamas (*Odyssey* 24.451 = 2.157; *Odyssey* 24.452 = *Iliad* 18.250, with Μαστορίδης for Πανθοΐδης), and tells them that these things happened through their own wickedness, since they would not obey him, nor Mentor, and stop their sons from their folly; let them not go out, lest someone find an evil of his own making (*Odyssey* 24.451–462, plain renderings). More than half of them spring up with a great shout and follow Eupeithes, the father of Antinous, to arms (*Odyssey* 24.463–471; see [[eupeithes]]), and Eupeithes falls to the spear of old Laertes (*Odyssey* 24.520–525; see [[laertes]]). Halitherses is not heard of again.
