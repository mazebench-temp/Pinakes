## Family

Son of Phronius: Φρονίοιο Νοήμονα φαίδιμον υἱόν, “Noemon, the shining son of Phronius” (*Odyssey* 2.386; see [[phaidimos]]). Book 4 names him υἱὸς Φρονίοιο Νοήμων, “Noemon, son of Phronius”, both times he speaks (*Odyssey* 4.630, 4.648). Nothing more is told of his father (see [[phronius]]).

## The name

Νοήμων is the adjective νοήμων, “discerning” (*Odyssey* 2.282), and Φρόνιος is formed from the root of φρονέω, “think”, as φρόνις, “wisdom”, is (*Odyssey* 3.244, 4.258): both names are words of the mind. The Iliad has two men of the name, the Lycian whom Odysseus kills (5.678; see [[noemon-of-lycia]]) and a companion of Antilochus (23.612; see [[noemon-companion-of-antilochus]]).

## Later in the Odyssey

In Book 4 Noemon comes up to Antinous and Eurymachus and asks whether they know when Telemachus will come back from Pylos: he has gone off with Noemon's ship, and Noemon needs it to cross to Elis, where he keeps twelve mares with unbroken mules, one of which he would drive off and break in (*Odyssey* 4.630–637). The suitors are astonished, for they had not thought that Telemachus had gone to Pylos (*Odyssey* 4.638–640). To Antinous' questions Noemon answers that he gave the ship willingly, for what could anyone do when such a man, with cares in his heart, asked for it; that the young men who went with him are the best in the land after themselves; and that he saw Mentor go on board as their leader, or a god in all things like him. But he wonders at it, for he saw noble Mentor in Ithaca the day before at dawn, though Mentor had then gone on board for Pylos (*Odyssey* 4.641–656; see [[mentor]] and [[athena]]). Then he goes home to his father's house, and Antinous, furious, asks for “a swift ship and twenty companions” to lie in wait for Telemachus on his way back, in the words of Telemachus' own request (*Odyssey* 4.657–672; 4.669 = 2.212; see [[the-journey-of-telemachus]]). The renderings of Book 4 are plain renderings, not the translation.
