Homer never names Cadmus in the Iliad, but he calls the Thebans Καδμεῖοι, “Cadmeians” (4.385, 4.388; see [[cadmeians]]), and the Odyssey names Ino Leucothea “the daughter of Cadmus” (*Odyssey* 5.333). Sent by his father to find Europa, whom Zeus had carried off, Cadmus was told at Delphi to follow a cow and to found a city where she lay down. There he killed the serpent of Ares that guarded the spring and, on Athena's advice, sowed its teeth (Apollodorus 3.4.1–2; see [[cadmus-and-the-sown-men]] and [[the-earth-born]]). The gods came to his wedding with Harmonia, daughter of Ares and Aphrodite (Pindar, *Pythian* 3.86–95). His daughters were Semele, the mother of Dionysus, Ino, Agave, the mother of Pentheus, and Autonoe, the mother of Actaeon. At the end of his life he and Harmonia went to the Illyrians and were turned into serpents (Apollodorus 3.5.4; Euripides, *Bacchae* 1330–1339; see [[illyrians]]).
