Homer never names Cadmus in the Iliad, but he calls the Thebans Καδμεῖοι, “Cadmeians” (4.385, 4.388; see [[cadmeians]]), and the Odyssey names [[leucothea|Ino Leucothea]] “the daughter of Cadmus” (*Odyssey* 5.333). Sent by his father to find [[europa|Europa]], whom Zeus had carried off, Cadmus was told at Delphi to follow a cow and to found a city where she lay down. There he killed the serpent of Ares that guarded the spring and, on Athena's advice, sowed its teeth (Apollodorus 3.4.1–2; see [[cadmus-and-the-sown-men]] and [[the-earth-born]]). The gods came to his wedding with Harmonia, daughter of Ares and Aphrodite (Pindar, *Pythian* 3.86–95). His daughters were [[semele|Semele]], the mother of Dionysus, Ino, Agave, the mother of Pentheus, and Autonoe, the mother of Actaeon. [[hesiod|Hesiod]] names the children Harmonia bore him in Thebes: Ino, Semele, fair-cheeked Agave, Autonoe, whom Aristaeus married, and a son, Polydorus (*Theogony* 975–978). At the end of his life he and Harmonia went to the Illyrians and were turned into serpents (Apollodorus 3.5.4; Euripides, *Bacchae* 1330–1339; see [[illyrians]]).
