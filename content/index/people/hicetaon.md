He is the only one of the elders given a warrior's epithet, ὄζος Ἄρηος, "offshoot of Ares" ([[offshoot-of-ares]]), though they are all "done with war now because of their age" (3.150). The epithet belongs to the line: the same verse names him with Lampus and Clytius among the sons of Laomedon in Aeneas' genealogy (3.147 = 20.238). See [[trojan-elders]].

His son Melanippus had herded cattle at Percote, but when the Achaean ships came he returned to Troy and lived with Priam, who honored him like his own children. In Book 15 Hector rebukes him for letting the Achaeans strip their cousin Dolops, son of Lampus, and Antilochus kills him (15.545–578).
