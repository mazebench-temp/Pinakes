The Iliad names him once, when Helen looks from the wall for her two brothers and cannot see them:

{{quote:70e03702-ee22-558f-a91f-bbf93bc38989}}

Helen thinks that shame at her disgrace keeps them out of the fighting. The narrator knows that they are dead and buried in Lacedaemon.

## Family

He is Helen's brother by the same mother (3.238), and the brother of [[Castor]]. The Odyssey names the mother and the father: Leda, the wife of Tyndareus, bore both sons to Tyndareus (*Odyssey* 11.298–300; see [[leda]] and [[tyndareus]]). Homer never calls Polydeuces a son of Zeus. Later writers do. Pindar makes Polydeuces the son of Zeus and Castor the son of Tyndareus (*Nemean* 10.80–82), and Apollodorus says that Zeus fathered Polydeuces and Helen, and Tyndareus Castor (3.10.7).

## Descriptions

πὺξ ἀγαθός = πύξ (“with the fist”) + ἀγαθός (“good”): “good with his fists”, a boxer (see [[good-with-his-fists]]). The Odyssey names him with the same words (*Odyssey* 11.300 = 3.237). W. W. Merry takes the name Πολυδεύκης as “very comely” or “seemly”, connecting its second part with ἀδευκής, which he renders “unseemly” (*Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on *Odyssey* 11.300 and 4.489).

## Mythology

In the Iliad he is dead and buried at home (3.243–244), and nothing is said of a life after death. In the Odyssey, the later poem, the earth holds both brothers “alive”: they live and are dead on alternate days, and have honor equal to the gods' (*Odyssey* 11.301–304). See [[castor]].

## Outside Homer

Pindar tells the story at the end of *Nemean* 10. Idas kills Castor, and Polydeuces, weeping, asks Zeus to let him die with his brother. Zeus gives him the choice: to live on Olympus forever, or to share everything with Castor, half the time under the earth and half in the golden houses of heaven. He chooses his brother (*Nemean* 10.55–90). The *Cypria* already told of the fight with Idas and Lynceus and of the immortality on alternate days ([[proclus|Proclus]]' summary; see [[the-death-of-the-dioscuri]]). The Homeric Hymns call both brothers sons of Zeus born to Leda under Taygetus (*Hymn* 17.1–4, 33.1–6; see [[leda]]).

As a boxer he beat Amycus, king of the Bebryces, when the Argonauts landed in his country (Apollonius, *Argonautica* 2.1–97; Theocritus, *Idyll* 22). The Latin form of his name is Pollux.
