Σχεδίον Περιμήδεος υἱὸν, “Schedius, son of Perimedes” (15.515): Perimedes is named only as the father of the Phocian leader whom Hector kills after his exhortation at the ships (see [[schedius-son-of-perimedes]], [[phocians]] and [[hector]]). The Phocian leaders of the Catalogue are sons of another father, Iphitus (2.517–518; see [[iphitus]] and [[schedius]]). In the Odyssey a Perimedes is one of Odysseus' companions: with Eurylochus he holds the victims when Odysseus calls up the dead, and with Eurylochus he binds Odysseus tighter to the mast as the ship passes the Sirens (*Odyssey* 11.23–24, 12.195–196). He is another man. The name joins περι-, “exceedingly”, and μήδεα, “counsels”.
