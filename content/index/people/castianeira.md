**A mother named at her son's death.** When Teucer's arrow, aimed at Hector, kills Gorgythion, the narrator turns to his mother: “whom his mother, a bride brought from Aesyme, had borne, lovely Castianeira, like the goddesses in form” (8.304–305; see [[gorgythion]], [[aesyme]] and [[the-poppy]]). She is named nowhere else in Homer. ὀπυιομένη, “a bride”, is a participle of ὀπυίω, the verb of taking a wife (13.379, 14.268, 16.178).

**Priam's wives.** Hecuba was not Priam's only wife. Laothoe, daughter of Altes, king of the Leleges at Pedasus, bore him Lycaon and Polydorus (21.84–91, 22.46–51; see [[lycaon-son-of-priam]]), and of his fifty sons, Priam says, nineteen were “from one womb” and the rest were borne to him “by women in my halls” (24.495–497; see [[hecuba]] and [[sons-of-priam]]). ἐϊκυῖα θεῇσι, “like the goddesses”, is said also of Hecamede, Nestor's captive from Tenedos (11.638), and of Briseis as she laments Patroclus (19.286). The ending of her name, -άνειρα, is from ἀνήρ, “man”, as in ἀντιάνειρα, “the equals of men”, said of the Amazons (3.189).
