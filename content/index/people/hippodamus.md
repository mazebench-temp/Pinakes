While Diomedes kills the two sons of Merops of Percote, Odysseus kills Hippodamus and Hypeirochus (11.328–335; see [[the-sons-of-merops]] and [[odysseus]]). The two Trojans share one verse and one verb, ἐξενάριξεν, “slew and stripped” (see [[stripping-the-dead]]).

Ἱππόδαμος, from ἵππος, “horse”, and δαμάζω, “tame”, is the word that the poem gives to the Trojans as a people, “the horse-taming Trojans” (Τρώων ἱπποδάμων, 11.568), and to Hector in the last line of the Iliad (24.804; see [[tamer-of-horses]]). Here it is a man's name, and the poem does not use it again. Achilles kills a Trojan Hippodamas, a different name, in Book 20 (20.401).
