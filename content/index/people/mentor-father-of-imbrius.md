Ἴμβριον αἰχμητὴν πολυΐππου Μέντορος υἱόν, “Imbrius the spearman, son of Mentor, rich in horses” (13.171). The poem says nothing more of him. πολύϊππος, “rich in horses”, occurs only here in Homer (see [[rich-in-horses]]). Horses were the wealth of the Trojan kings: Erichthonius, son of Dardanus, “the richest of mortal men”, had three thousand mares grazing in the marsh (20.219–222). His son lived at Pedaeum and married a bastard daughter of Priam, and returned to Ilios when the war began (13.172–176; see [[imbrius]], [[pedaeum]] and [[medesicaste]]).

The Mentor of the Odyssey, Odysseus' Ithacan companion and son of Alcimus, in whose likeness Athena guides Telemachus, is another man (Od. 2.225–227, 2.267–268, 22.235).
