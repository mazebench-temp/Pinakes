**Stopped at the knee.** Demuchus is the second man Achilles kills after Hector's escape, after Dryops (20.455–456; see [[dryops]] and [[achilles]]). Δημοῦχον δὲ Φιλητορίδην ἠΰν τε μέγαν τε, “and Demuchus, son of Philetor, tall and brave” (20.457; see [[philetor]]): the half-verse of praise is said of Tlepolemus, son of Heracles, when fate rouses him against Sarpedon, and of the Thracian Acamas, whom Ajax kills (5.628, 6.7–8; see [[tlepolemus]] and [[acamas-of-thrace]]). Achilles “hit on the knee with his spear and stopped him” (20.458). κὰγ γόνυ, “on the knee”, is, as Walter Leaf notes, the only place in Homer where κατά is cut short before γ (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 20.458; see [[gounata]]). Then he took his life with his great sword (20.459; see [[sword]]): the spear stops the man and the sword ends him, as with Deucalion a few verses later, whose arm the spear pierces before the sword takes his head (20.478–482; see [[deucalion-the-trojan]]).

**The name.** Δημοῦχος seems to join δῆμος, “people”, and ἔχω, “hold”.
