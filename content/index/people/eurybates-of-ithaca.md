The name means “wide-striding” (εὐρύς, “wide” + βαίνω, “walk”), a good name for a herald. The same name belongs to one of Agamemnon's heralds in Book 1 (1.320). The poem marks this one as “Eurybates of Ithaca, who attended him”.

## Elsewhere in Homer

In the Odyssey, Odysseus, disguised as a Cretan stranger, describes his herald to Penelope as a proof that he has seen her husband: round-shouldered, dark-skinned and curly-haired, named Eurybates, and honored by Odysseus above his other companions because they thought alike (19.244–248). A herald Eurybates goes with Odius on the embassy to Achilles (Iliad 9.170); the poem does not say which of the two he is.
