Orestes is the second of the five men named around Asius when his company rushes the gate on the left of the ships (12.139; see [[asius]] and [[the-gates-of-the-wall]]). Leonteus kills him with Menon and Iamenus, the last of his five victims (12.193–194; see [[leonteus]], [[menon]] and [[iamenus]]). The two verses that name him end alike, καὶ Ἰαμενὸν καὶ Ὀρέστην, “and Iamenus and Orestes” (12.139, 12.193).

Homer has two other men of the name: [[orestes-the-achaean|Orestes, driver of horses]], killed by Hector and Ares (5.705), and [[orestes|Orestes, son of Agamemnon]], named in the Iliad among his father's children (9.142, 9.284) and in the Odyssey the son who avenged his father. The name is usually derived from ὄρος, “mountain”.
