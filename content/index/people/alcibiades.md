Alcibiades led Athens into the Sicilian expedition in 415 BCE, was recalled to stand trial for sacrilege, fled to Sparta, then to the Persians, returned in triumph to Athens in 407, and was murdered in Phrygia in 404. Thucydides and Plutarch tell his story; in Plato's *Symposium* he arrives drunk at Agathon's party and praises Socrates (see [[agathon]]). Aristotle uses him as the type of the particular that history records: “a particular is what Alcibiades did or what he suffered” (1451b10–11; see [[poetry-and-history]] and [[katholou]]).
