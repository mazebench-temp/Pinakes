Φυλακίδης = Φύλακος + -ίδης ("son of"). He shares his name with the family's town, [[Phylace]]. In the Odyssey the seer Melampus lay bound for a year “in the halls of Phylacus” (*Odyssey* 15.231; see [[melampus]]). With his son Iphiclus and his grandsons Protesilaus and Podarces, the Catalogue gives the house of Phylace three generations in three lines (2.704–706).

{{notes}}

## Outside Homer

In the Hesiodic *Catalogue of Women* Phylacus is a son of Deion, and his wife Clymene, daughter of Minyas, bears him Iphiclus, “a swift-footed boy” (fr. 84 Evelyn-White); among the suitors of Helen are Podarces, “son of Iphiclus, Phylacus' son”, and Protesilaus, both from Phylace (fr. 68 Evelyn-White; see [[hesiod]], [[iphiclus]], [[podarces]] and [[protesilaus]]). In a fragment of the *Melampodia*, the poem named after Melampus, “the seer”, Μάντης, takes the ox by its tether, Iphiclus feels its back, and Phylacus walks behind, “holding a cup in one hand and raising a scepter in the other”, and speaks among the servants (fr. 6 Evelyn-White; see [[melampus]]).
