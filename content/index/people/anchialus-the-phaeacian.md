## The name

His name is the adjective ἀγχίαλος, “near the sea” (ἄγχι “near” + ἅλς “sea”), which the Catalogue gives to coastal towns, “Chalcis by the sea” and “Antron by the sea” (*Iliad* 2.640, 2.697; see [[by-the-sea]]). W. W. Merry counts the Phaeacian names among those “taken from circumstances of seafaring life” (*Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on *Odyssey* 8.111).

Homer has two other men of the name: an Achaean whom Hector kills in one chariot with Menesthes (*Iliad* 5.608–609; see [[anchialus]]), and the father of Mentes, the Taphian whose likeness Athena takes to visit Telemachus (*Odyssey* 1.105, 1.180; see [[anchialus-father-of-mentes]]).
