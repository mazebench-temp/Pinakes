summary: Tyrant of Gela and then of Syracuse (d. 478 BCE), who defeated the Carthaginians at Himera in 480; he refused to help the Greeks against Xerxes unless he had the command.
---

Gelon, a cavalry commander under Hippocrates of Gela, took power there after Hippocrates' death and in 485 BCE made himself master of Syracuse. He enlarged the city by moving to it the people of Camarina and the wealthy of Megara Hyblaea and of Euboea in Sicily; the common people of those two cities he sold into slavery (Herodotus 7.153–156). When the Greek envoys came to ask his help against Xerxes, he offered two hundred triremes and a great army if he could lead them, and on their refusal sent them home to say that Greece had lost the spring out of its year (7.157–162; see [[the-spring-taken-from-the-year]]). In the same year he and Theron of Acragas destroyed a Carthaginian army at Himera, by Sicilian tradition on the day of Salamis (7.165–167; see [[salamis-and-himera]]).
