οὐδʼ ὅτε Φοίνικος κούρης τηλεκλειτοῖο, “nor when I loved the daughter of far-famed Phoenix, / who bore me Minos and godlike Rhadamanthys” (14.321–322). He is named only as a father, in Zeus' list of his loves (see [[the-loves-of-zeus]], [[europa]], [[minos]] and [[rhadamanthus]]). τηλεκλειτός, “far-famed”, is the epithet of the Trojans' allies (6.111, 12.108; see [[famous]]), and in the Odyssey of Penelope's father, in a verse-end of the same shape: Ἰκαρίου κούρη τηλεκλειτοῖο, “daughter of far-famed Icarius” (*Odyssey* 19.546). The name is the word for a Phoenician, and for the red-purple dye (see [[phoenix]] and [[purple]]). He is not Phoenix, son of Amyntor, Achilles' old tutor.

## Outside Homer

The Hesiodic *Catalogue of Women* called him “illustrious Phoenix”, father of Europa, whom Zeus carried over the sea to Crete (fr. 19a Evelyn-White), and a scholium on the Iliad tells her story as “Europa, the daughter of Phoenix” (fr. 19). In the commoner version Europa is the daughter of Agenor, who had settled in Phoenicia, and Phoenix one of her brothers, with Cadmus and Cilix. [[apollodorus|Apollodorus]] gives both: Agenor sent his sons to look for their lost sister, and Phoenix, failing to find her, settled in Phoenicia, though “some say that Europa was a daughter not of Agenor but of Phoenix” (*Library* 3.1.1; see [[cadmus]]). Walter Leaf thought that the legend probably kept a memory of the mingling of Greek and Phoenician in Crete (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 14.321).
