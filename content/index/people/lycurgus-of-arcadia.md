Nestor names him in passing, as one of the owners of the armor that Ereuthalion wore (7.136–150; see [[ereuthalion]] and [[nestor-and-ereuthalion]]). Homer does not say where he ruled. He had halls (μέγαρα, 7.148) and an attendant (θεράπων, 7.149), and the attendant was the champion of the Arcadians, so he was presumably an Arcadian lord.

**By guile.** He killed Areithous δόλῳ, οὔ τι κράτεΐ γε, “by guile, not by strength at all” (7.142). The guile was the place and the moment: “in a narrow road”, where the iron mace could not save its owner, he struck first (ὑποφθάς) and ran him through with the spear (7.143–145; see [[areithous]] and [[mace]]). The same “narrow road”, στεινωπῷ ἐν ὁδῷ, is where Nestor's son Antilochus forces his way past Menelaus in the chariot race (23.415–427). In the duel of Book 7, Hector says that he will not strike Ajax “by spying for a chance in secret, but openly” (7.243; see [[single-combat]]).

**The armor.** He stripped the armor that Ares had given Areithous, “wore it afterward into the toil of Ares”, and when he “grew old in his halls” gave it to Ereuthalion (7.146–149). Armor and heirlooms in Homer keep the history of their owners: the helmet of boar's tusks that Meriones lends Odysseus was stolen by Autolycus and passed by gift to Amphidamas, to Molus and to Molus' son (10.266–271).

The name is that of the Thracian king who drove off Dionysus (6.130; see [[lycurgus]]), in the epic form of the later Λυκοῦργος. It seems to join λύκος (“wolf”) with the root of ἔργον (“work”); what it meant is not clear.

## Outside Homer

Pausanias makes him Lycurgus son of Aleus, who succeeded his father as king of the Arcadians and is remembered for killing Areithous by treachery (8.4.8–10). His son Ancaeus sailed with Jason and was killed by the Calydonian boar; Lycurgus outlived both his sons and reached a great age (8.4.10–8.5.1), and his grandson Agapenor led the Arcadians to Troy (8.5.2; see [[ancaeus]] and [[agapenor]]). The people of Lepreum in Triphylia told Pausanias that their city had once had his grave (5.5.5). [[apollodorus|Apollodorus]] also makes him a son of Aleus, and gives him four sons, Ancaeus, Epochus, Amphidamas and Iasus, the father of Atalanta (*Library* 3.9.1–2).
