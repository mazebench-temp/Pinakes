ξανθός "fair-haired" ([[fair-haired]]), an epithet he shares with Achilles and Menelaus. The Catalogue names him last among the dead of the house of [[Oeneus]], and his death is the reason why [[Thoas]] leads the [[Aetolians]] (2.641–643). Homer does not say how he died (see [[the-death-of-meleager]]).

## Elsewhere in Homer

In Book 9 Phoenix tells Achilles the story of his anger, as a warning to take the gifts while they are offered (9.527–599; see [[the-wrath-of-meleager]] and [[mythological-paradigm]]).

## Outside Homer

Later poets made the hunt a gathering of heroes, with the huntress Atalanta, to whom Meleager gave the boar's hide (Apollodorus 1.8.2–3; Ovid, *Metamorphoses* 8.260–546). Bacchylides has his shade tell Heracles in the underworld how he died (*Ode* 5). Swinburne's *Atalanta in Calydon* (1865) retold the story in English verse drama.
