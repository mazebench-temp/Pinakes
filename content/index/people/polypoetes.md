μενεπτόλεμος, "steadfast in battle" (see [[steadfast-in-battle]]). The Catalogue dates his birth by a famous day, "when he took vengeance on the shaggy beasts" (see [[lapiths-drive-out-the-centaurs]]). He is the son of one of the heroes Nestor named in Book 1 (1.263), and a grandson of Zeus.

In Book 12 he and Leonteus, "the high-hearted sons of the Lapith spearmen", stand before the gate of the Achaean wall like two oaks on a mountain that endure wind and rain, and hold off Asius and his men (12.127–194). At the funeral games of Patroclus he wins the contest of throwing a lump of iron (23.826–849).

After the war, according to Proclus' summary of the *Nostoi*, Polypoetes and Leonteus went overland with Calchas to Colophon, and there they buried the seer Teiresias.
