γονὴν Ἀρκεισιάδαο, “the offspring of the son of Arceisius” (*Odyssey* 4.755): Eurycleia, comforting Penelope, does not think that the line of Laertes is altogether hateful to the gods (see [[laertes]] and [[eurycleia]]). The poem names the grandfather again when Eumaeus tells the disguised Odysseus that the suitors lie in wait for Telemachus, so that the race of godlike Arceisius may perish nameless from Ithaca (*Odyssey* 14.179–182), and when Telemachus tells the stranger how the son of Cronus made the family single, “Arceisius begot Laertes as his only son, / and his father begot Odysseus as his only son; and Odysseus / left me, his only son, in the halls” (*Odyssey* 16.117–120, a plain rendering; see [[odysseus]] and [[telemachus]]). In the last book Odysseus says that his guest claimed Λαέρτην Ἀρκεισιάδην, “Laertes, son of Arceisius”, for his father (*Odyssey* 24.270), and Athena addresses Laertes ὦ Ἀρκεισιάδη, “son of Arceisius, dearest by far of all my companions” (*Odyssey* 24.517).
