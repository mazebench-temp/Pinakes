“The son of Creon, brilliant Lycomedes” closes the list of the seven captains (9.84; see [[night-watch]], [[creon]] and [[brilliant]]). The name joins λύκος (“wolf”) and the -μήδης of Diomedes and Thrasymedes (μήδεα, “counsels”). Homer does not say where he came from or whom he led.

## Elsewhere in Homer

At the wall in Book 12, when Telamonian Ajax goes to help Menestheus, he leaves the other Ajax “and mighty Lycomedes” to hold their ground and urge on the Danaans (12.364–369; see [[ajax-son-of-oileus]]). In Book 17 Aeneas kills Leocritus, son of Arisbas, “the noble companion of Lycomedes”; Lycomedes, “dear to Ares”, pities him and strikes down Apisaon, son of Hippasus, a Paeonian who was the best fighter after Asteropaeus (17.344–351; see [[dear-to-ares]]). In Book 19 he is one of the young men whom Odysseus takes to Agamemnon's hut to bring out the gifts, with the sons of Nestor, Meges, Thoas, Meriones and Melanippus (19.238–248), and there he is called Κρειοντιάδης, “son of Creon” (19.240). Two of the captains who kept watch on the night of the embassy, Meriones and Lycomedes, carry out the gifts that were offered that night (see [[the-gifts-of-agamemnon]] and [[meriones]]).
