Ascalaphus and Ialmenus share the command of thirty ships from [[Aspledon]] and Minyan [[Orchomenus]]. The Catalogue gives them a birth story instead of a list of deeds: their mother [[Astyoche]], "a modest maiden", went up to the upper room of her father's house, and [[Ares]] lay with her in secret (see [[the-birth-of-ascalaphus-and-ialmenus]]).

The name is also that of a bird, perhaps a kind of owl.

## Elsewhere in Homer

The brothers are among the captains of the night watch (9.82). Idomeneus calls Ascalaphus to his side (13.478), and soon afterward Deiphobus, throwing at Idomeneus, misses him and strikes Ascalaphus through the shoulder (13.516–520). The poet turns at once to Ares, who "did not yet know that his son had fallen", sitting on Olympus under golden clouds, held back by the will of Zeus (13.521–525). When Hera tells him, Ares strikes his thighs and sets out to avenge his son against Zeus' orders, until Athena stops him (15.110–142).

## Outside Homer

Apollodorus counts both brothers among the Argonauts and among the suitors of Helen (1.9.16, 3.10.8). A different Ascalaphus, son of Acheron, was turned into an owl for telling that Persephone had eaten in the underworld (Ovid, *Metamorphoses* 5.533–550).
