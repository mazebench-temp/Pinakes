## The name

Ναυσίθοος joins ναῦς, “ship”, and θοός, “swift”. The founder of a seafaring people bears a name of swift ships, as his granddaughter's begins with the ship (see [[nausicaa]] and [[phaeacians]]).

## Family

Poseidon begot him on Periboea, “most beautiful in form of women”, the youngest daughter of Eurymedon, who once was king over the Giants and destroyed his reckless people and perished himself (*Odyssey* 7.56–61; see [[poseidon]], [[periboea-mother-of-nausithous]] and [[eurymedon-king-of-the-giants]]). He ruled among the Phaeacians and had two sons, Rhexenor and Alcinous. Apollo struck down Rhexenor in his hall, newly married and without a son, and he left one daughter, Arete, whom Alcinous made his wife (*Odyssey* 7.62–66; see [[rhexenor]], [[alcinous]] and [[arete-queen-of-the-phaeacians]]). So Nausicaa descends from him on both sides, his granddaughter through her father and his great-granddaughter through her mother (see [[nausicaa]]). Alcinous calls him “my father” (*Odyssey* 8.564–565, 13.172–173).

## Descriptions

- θεοειδής, “godlike” (*Odyssey* 6.7; see [[godlike]]).
- μεγάθυμος, “great-hearted” (*Odyssey* 7.62; see [[great-hearted]]).
- ὁ γέρων, “the old man”, in his son's memory of him (*Odyssey* 8.570, 13.178).

## Later in the Odyssey

The prophecy of his that Alcinous tells the stranger in *Odyssey* 8, that Poseidon would one day shatter a Phaeacian ship coming home from an escort and cover their city round about with a great mountain, comes true in *Odyssey* 13: when the ship that carried Odysseus home is turned to stone in sight of the city, the king recognizes his father's words, spoken long ago, and repeats them (*Odyssey* 13.172–178, a plain rendering). For the migration and the prophecy, see [[the-migration-of-the-phaeacians]].

## Outside Homer

Hesiod's Nausithous is another man: at the end of the *Theogony* Calypso, “brilliant among goddesses”, bears Odysseus two sons, Nausithous and Nausinous (*Theogony* 1017–1018; see [[hesiod]] and [[calypso]]).
