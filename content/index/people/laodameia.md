The name is formed like Hippodameia, from λαός (“people”) and the stem of δάμνημι (“tame, master”). She is the only daughter of Bellerophon, and through her Glaucus and Sarpedon, the two leaders of the Lycians, are cousins (see [[glaucus]] and [[sarpedon]]). μητίετα Ζεύς, “Zeus the counselor” (see [[counselor]]), lay with her, and she bore “godlike Sarpedon, armed in bronze”. Zeus' own list of his loves, mortal and divine, does not name her (14.315–328).

Her death is told in one line: “and Artemis of the golden reins killed his daughter in her anger” (6.205; see [[artemis]] and [[of-the-golden-reins]]). Artemis brings sudden death to women (6.428; Od. 11.172–173), but χολωσαμένη, “in her anger”, makes this a punishment, like the death of Niobe's daughters, whom Artemis killed because Niobe had set herself beside Leto (24.605–607). What Laodameia had done the poem does not say. See [[bellerophon]] and [[isander]].

## Outside Homer

Herodotus makes Sarpedon a son of Europa and a brother of Minos, who led settlers from Crete to Lycia (1.173.2), and so do the mythographers (Apollodorus 3.1.1–2). In the Iliad, the daughter of Phoenix, whom later writers call Europa, is the mother of Minos and Rhadamanthys only (14.321–322).
