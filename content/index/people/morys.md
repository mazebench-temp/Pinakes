Μόρυν θʼ υἷʼ Ἱπποτίωνος, “and Morys, son of Hippotion” (13.792), is the last name in the list of men around Cebriones and Polydamas, where Hector and Paris go to the thickest of the fighting (see [[hector]], [[cebriones]] and [[polydamas]]). With Palmys and Ascanius he had come from Ascania “as relief” on the morning before (13.793–794; see [[palmys]], [[ascanius-the-mysian]] and [[ascania]]). In the verses as Strabo quotes them, a line that is not in our text follows his name, Μυσῶν ἀγχεμάχων ἡγήτορα, “leader of the Mysians who fight hand to hand”. Standing there, it would most naturally describe Morys, but Strabo's argument makes Ascanius the Mysian leader (*Geography* 12.4.5; see [[mysians]]). In the rout of Book 14, when Poseidon has turned the battle, Meriones kills “Morys and Hippotion” (14.514; see [[meriones]] and [[hippotion]]). The poem does not say whether that Hippotion is his father.
