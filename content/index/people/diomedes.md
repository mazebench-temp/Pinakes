## Etymology

Διομήδης (Diomēdēs), usually analyzed as Διός (“of Zeus”) + μήδομαι (“plan, take thought”): “he who has the counsel of Zeus” or “cared for by Zeus”. The analysis is plausible but not certain.

## Family

{{quote:523fef7e-1e66-53ec-8643-dab33e11e5f5}}

Τυδέος υἱός, “the son of Tydeus”: the poem often names him by his father, and later calls him Τυδεΐδης (“son of Tydeus”). His father [[Tydeus]] was a son of Oeneus of Calydon who settled at Argos and married a daughter of Adrastus (14.113–125). His wife Aegialeia was also a daughter of Adrastus (5.412–415).

## Descriptions

βοὴν ἀγαθός (“good at the war cry”, 2.563, 2.567) is his standing epithet, which he shares with [[Menelaus]]. See [[good-at-the-war-cry]].

## Mythology

Before Troy he and Sthenelus took seven-gated Thebes, where their fathers had failed (4.404–410). At Troy he is the youngest of the kings: Nestor says he could be his youngest son (9.57–58). With Athena at his side he has the first great run of fighting in the poem, and he wounds both Aphrodite and Ares (5.330–351, 5.855–863). He exchanges armor with the Lycian Glaucus instead of fighting him (6.119–236), goes on the night raid with Odysseus (Book 10; see [[the-doloneia]] and [[odysseus-and-diomedes]]), and is wounded in the foot by an arrow of Paris (11.369–400). In the Odyssey Nestor reports that he reached Argos safely on the fourth day after leaving Troy (3.180–182).

## Outside Homer

In the *Little Iliad*, as [[proclus|Proclus]] summarizes it, he and Odysseus carry the Palladium out of Troy. Pindar says that Athena made him a god (*Nemean* 10.7). Italian tradition made him the founder of Argyripa in Apulia, and in the *Aeneid* he refuses the Latins' request to fight Aeneas (Virgil, *Aeneid* 11.243–295).
