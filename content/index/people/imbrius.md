**A short life.** Imbrius is the first man to die after Poseidon has rallied the Achaeans (see [[the-battle-at-the-ships]] and [[teucer]]), and the narrator gives him a life in four verses. He lived at Pedaeum (see [[pedaeum]]) “before the sons of the Achaeans came” (13.172), the phrase in which Achilles and the narrator recall Troy in the time of peace (9.403, 22.156), and he married Medesicaste, a bastard daughter of Priam (see [[medesicaste]] and [[nothos]]). When the Achaean ships came, he returned to Ilios, and Priam honored him as one of his own children. The three verses of his return are told again of Melanippus, son of Hicetaon, who had herded cattle at Percote (13.174–176 = 15.549–551; see [[percote]]). His father is “Mentor, rich in horses” (13.171; see [[mentor-father-of-imbrius]]).

**His fall.** He falls like an ash felled on a mountain peak (see [[the-felled-ash]]), and the half-verse of his armor ringing is the one that ends the falls of Alcmaon and of Hector under Ajax's stone (13.181; 12.396, 14.420; see [[inlaid]]). As Teucer rushes to strip him, Hector's spear misses Teucer and kills Amphimachus, and Ajax drives Hector back from both bodies (13.182–194; see [[amphimachus]] and [[hector]]).

**His head.** The two Ajaxes carry him off like two lions holding a goat high above the ground in their jaws, and strip his armor. The son of Oileus, angry for Amphimachus, cuts his head from his tender neck and sends it spinning through the throng, σφαιρηδόν, “like a ball”, a word found only here, to fall in the dust at Hector's feet (13.197–205; see [[the-two-ajaxes]], [[ajax-son-of-oileus]] and [[two-lions-and-a-goat]]).

**The name.** Ἴμβριος means “of Imbros”, the island off the Thracian Chersonese (see [[imbros]]); in Book 21 Eetion of Imbros is Ἴμβριος Ἠετίων (21.43). The poem does not say that Imbrius came from the island.
