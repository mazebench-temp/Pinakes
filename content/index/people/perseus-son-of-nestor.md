He bears the name of the hero of Argos, the son of Zeus and Danae, “preeminent among all men” (*Iliad* 14.319–320; see [[perseus]]), but nothing joins the two. He is named twice, among his brothers on the polished stones before Nestor's doors and at the sacrifice, where his task is to hold the vessel for the victim's blood (*Odyssey* 3.412–415, 3.444).

{{notes}}

## Outside Homer

Apollodorus names Perseus first of the seven sons of Nestor by Anaxibia, daughter of Cratieus (*Library* 1.9.9).
