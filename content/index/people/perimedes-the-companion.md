## The name

Περιμήδης joins περι-, “exceedingly”, and μήδεα, “counsels”. It is also the name of the father of Schedius, the Phocian leader whom Hector kills at the ships (*Iliad* 15.515; see [[perimedes]]), another man.

{{notes}}

## Elsewhere in Homer

The two companions are named together again when the ship passes the Sirens. Odysseus, bound to the mast, bids his men with his brows to set him free, “And at once Perimedes and Eurylochus stood up / and bound me with more bonds and pressed me tighter” (*Odyssey* 12.195–196, a plain rendering; see [[eurylochus]]).
