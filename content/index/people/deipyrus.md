Deipyrus is the sixth of the seven captains, named last in the verse “and around Meriones and Aphareus and Deipyrus” (9.83; see [[night-watch]], [[meriones]] and [[aphareus]]). Homer tells nothing of his family or his country. The name joins δήϊος (“hostile, destructive”) and πῦρ (“fire”), as Deiphobus joins it with φόβος (“rout”).

## Elsewhere in Homer

In Book 13 he is named twice in lists of fighters called to the defense of the ships. Poseidon goes among the ranks and comes first “to Teucer and Leitus”, “and to the hero Peneleos, and Thoas and Deipyrus, / and Meriones and Antilochus” (13.91–93), and Idomeneus, facing Aeneas, calls on “Ascalaphus and Aphareus and Deipyrus / and Meriones and Antilochus” (13.478–479; see [[aphareus]] and [[ascalaphus]]). Soon after, Helenus strikes him on the temple with “a great Thracian sword” and knocks off his helmet; it falls and rolls among the feet of the fighters, an Achaean picks it up, and dark night covers his eyes (13.576–580; see [[helenus]] and [[thrace]]).
