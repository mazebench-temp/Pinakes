## Etymology

Βρισηΐς (Brisēïs): "daughter of Briseus", the same type of name as Chryseis. Homer does not give her a personal name.

{{quote:1258be00-b64f-5332-92cc-3cfd81b31a44}}

ἀέκουσα, "unwilling". It is a single word, but Homer rarely gives captives any feelings at all. Achilles calls her "the girl whom the sons of the Achaeans gave me" (1.392), and he tells Agamemnon he will not fight for her with his hands, "since you who gave her are taking her away" (1.299).

Thetis calls her "the fair-belted woman" (1.429) as she leaves Achilles angry over her.

## Outside Homer

In Book 19 Briseis is returned and laments over Patroclus. She tells how Achilles killed her husband and three brothers when he sacked Lyrnessus, and how Patroclus promised to make her Achilles' wife (19.282–300; see also 2.688–694). Her name, merged with Chryseis', gave the Middle Ages its Cressida. See [[from-briseis-to-cressida]].
