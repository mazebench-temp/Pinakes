{{quote:d41be128-2a41-5121-b838-14ee3cdabd18}}

Sthenelus seizes the horses of Aeneas, as Diomedes told him to, and hands them to Deipylus, “his dear companion, whom above all the men of his age he honored, because his mind was in tune with his own” (5.325–326). ὅτι οἱ φρεσὶν ἄρτια ᾔδη, literally “because he knew fitting things in his mind”, is said in the Odyssey of Eurybates, whom Odysseus honored above his other companions (Od. 19.247–248). Deipylus takes the horses to the ships and is not named again. They are the horses of the breed Zeus gave to Tros (5.265–273; see [[tros]]), and Diomedes drives them in the chariot race at the funeral games of Patroclus (23.290–292). See [[sthenelus]] and [[hetairos]].
