In Book 4 Nestor names him in a single line, as the measure of the strength that old age has taken from him (4.319). δῖος (“brilliant”; [[brilliant]]) is a standing epithet, and Nestor gives it to the man he killed.

The story comes in Book 7, when no Achaean will take up Hector's challenge and Nestor shames them with his youth (7.132–156). The Pylians and the Arcadians were fighting by the river Celadon, under the walls of Pheia. Ereuthalion came forward as the Arcadians' champion in the armor of Areithous, the Mace-man, whom Lycurgus had killed by a trick; Ares had given Areithous the armor, and Lycurgus in his old age gave it to Ereuthalion, his squire (θεράπων). The best of the Pylians trembled at his challenge. Nestor, the youngest of them, fought him, and Athena gave him the victory. He remembers Ereuthalion as the tallest and strongest man he ever killed, sprawled far this way and that (7.153–156). The tale brings nine men to their feet to answer Hector (7.161–169). See [[nestor-and-ereuthalion]] and [[arcadians]].

The name is usually connected with ἐρεύθω (“make red”), but its meaning is not certain.
