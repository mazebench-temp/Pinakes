He is named three ways in twenty-five verses: in the patronymic of Charops, Ἱππασίδης, “son of Hippasus” (11.426); in Socus' challenge, which promises Odysseus a boast over “both sons of Hippasus” (Ἱππασίδῃσι, 11.431); and in Odysseus' boast over Socus, “son of wise-hearted Hippasus, tamer of horses” (11.450; see [[wise-hearted]] and [[tamer-of-horses]]). The boast turns to the parents: “your father and your lady mother / will not close your eyes in death” (11.452–453). In a single encounter one of his sons is wounded and the other killed, as Antenor loses Iphidamas and Coon one after the other earlier in the book (see [[antenor]] and [[bereaved-parents]]).

The name is made from ἵππος, “horse”, and it suits the epithet. Two more men called Hippasus appear only in their sons' patronymics, in the same verse-pattern: Hypsenor, an Achaean killed by Deiphobus (13.411–412), and the Paeonian Apisaon, killed by Lycomedes (17.348–349; see [[apisaon]]).
