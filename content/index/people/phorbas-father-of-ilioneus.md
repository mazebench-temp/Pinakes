**Rich in flocks.** πολύμηλος, “rich in flocks”, is said in the Catalogue of Arcadian Orchomenus and of Iphiclus, son of Phylacus (2.605, 2.705; see [[rich-in-flocks]]). The name is usually connected with φορβή, “fodder, pasture”. Hermes “loved” him and “granted him wealth”, ἐφίλει καὶ κτῆσιν ὄπασσε (14.491), the half-verse with which Eumaeus speaks of the master who would have loved him and given him property (*Odyssey* 14.62). In the Odyssey Hermes is the “giver of good things” (δῶτορ ἑάων, *Odyssey* 8.335), and he gave Autolycus his gifts because Autolycus burned thigh pieces of lambs and kids to him (*Odyssey* 19.395–398; see [[hermes]] and [[autolycus]]).

**The only son.** For all his wealth he had one son: “to him the mother bore Ilioneus, an only child” (14.492). Peneleos kills the son with a spear through the eye, holds up the head, and tells the Trojans to bid Ilioneus' “dear father … and his mother” wail for him in their halls (14.493–502; see [[ilioneus]], [[peneleos]] and [[bereaved-parents]]).

## Outside Homer

[[Hesiod]] gives the increase of the herds to Hecate “with Hermes” (σὺν Ἑρμῇ): she swells the herds of cattle, the flocks of goats and the flocks of sheep from few to many, or makes them fewer (*Theogony* 444–447). Near the end of the *Homeric Hymn to Hermes*, Apollo gives him the cattle, horses and mules to tend, and he is to rule over all flocks (567–571).
