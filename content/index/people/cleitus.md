**Polydamas' charioteer.** He is “the companion of Polydamas, the noble son of Panthous” (15.446; see [[polydamas]] and [[panthous]]), and he holds the reins, driving the horses where the battalions are most confused, “to do a favor to Hector and the Trojans” (15.449). The couplet that begins there and ends “eager though they were” is said in Book 17 of the Pelasgian Hippothous, who drags Patroclus' body by the foot until Ajax kills him (15.449–450 = 17.291–292; see [[hippothous]]). The name is the adjective κλειτός, “famous”.

**His death.** Called by Ajax, Teucer comes with his bow and shoots him in the neck from behind (15.436–451; see [[teucer]]): “he fell from the chariot, and the horses shied back” (15.452), the verse in which Hector lost two charioteers in Book 8, Eniopeus to Diomedes and Archeptolemus to Teucer himself (8.122, 8.314; see [[eniopeus]] and [[archeptolemus]]). Polydamas is the first to reach the runaway horses and gives them to Astynous, son of Protiaon (15.453–457; see [[astynous-son-of-protiaon]]).

Homer's other Cleitus is a son of Mantius, of the line of Melampus, whom Dawn of the golden throne carried off for his beauty, to live among the immortals (*Odyssey* 15.249–251).
