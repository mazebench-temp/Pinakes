ἀλλʼ ἔβαλʼ Ἱππασίδην Ὑψήνορα ποιμένα λαῶν, “but struck Hypsenor, son of Hippasus, shepherd of the people” (13.411). The patronymic is all that the poem tells of him. His son, an Achaean leader and a companion of Antilochus, is struck by a spear that Deiphobus meant for Idomeneus (13.402–423; see [[hypsenor-son-of-hippasus]]).

The same verse pattern names two other sons of men called Hippasus, each father known only through his son: the Trojan Charops, brother of Socus (11.426; see [[hippasus-father-of-socus]]), and the Paeonian Apisaon (17.348). The Hippasus of Book 11 is a Trojan, “tamer of horses” (11.450), and nothing joins him to the father of an Achaean. The name is made from ἵππος, “horse”.
