Βώρῳ Περιήρεος υἷι, “to Borus, son of Perieres” (16.177): Perieres is named only to name his son, the man whose son Menesthius was called, though the river Spercheius had begotten him (see [[borus-son-of-perieres]], [[polydora]] and [[the-birth-of-menesthius]]). Homer tells nothing else of him.

## Outside Homer

Later genealogy knows a famous Perieres. The Hesiodic *Catalogue of Women* counts “high-spirited Perieres” among the sons of Aeolus (fr. 4 Evelyn-White; see [[aeolus-father-of-sisyphus]]). In Apollodorus that Perieres takes Messene and marries Gorgophone, daughter of Perseus, who bears him Aphareus, Leucippus, Tyndareus and Icarius; but many, Apollodorus adds, call him a son of Cynortas, not of Aeolus (*Library* 1.9.5, 3.10.3, where the marriage is credited to Stesichorus). Apollodorus also makes Borus, Polydora's husband, a son of Perieres (*Library* 3.13.1) without saying which Perieres he means, and the Iliad gives no hint that its Perieres is the son of Aeolus.
