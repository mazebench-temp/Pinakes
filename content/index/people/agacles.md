υἱὸς Ἀγακλῆος μεγαθύμου δῖος Ἐπειγεύς, “the son of great-hearted Agacles, brilliant Epeigeus” (16.571; see [[epeigeus]] and [[great-hearted]]). Agacles is known only as a father. His son had been lord in Budeum, and left it after killing a cousin (16.572–574; see [[budeum]]); whether Agacles had ruled there before him the poem does not say.

**The name.** Ἀγακλῆς joins ἀγα-, “very”, and κλέος, “fame”: the man's name is the adjective ἀγακλεής, “very famous”, which the same book gives to Priam, “Cebriones, the bastard son of famous Priam” (16.738), and which is used of Menelaus at the funeral games (23.529). In the phrase ἀγακλῆος Πριάμοιο the adjective has the very letters of the name.
