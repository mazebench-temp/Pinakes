Κτήσιος Ὀρμενίδης, ἐπιείκελος ἀθανάτοισιν, “Ctesius, son of Ormenus, like the immortals” (*Odyssey* 15.414): Ormenus is named only in the patronymic of Eumaeus' father, the king of Syrie, when the swineherd tells the disguised Odysseus his own story, and the poem says nothing else of him (see [[ctesius]], [[eumaeus]], [[syrie]] and [[the-kidnapping-of-eumaeus]]). The name is the participle ὄρμενος, “starting up, rushing on”, of ὄρνυμι, “rouse”. Homer gives it to Amyntor's father, also in a patronymic, “my father Amyntor, son of Ormenus” (*Iliad* 9.448; see [[ormenus-father-of-amyntor]]), and to two Trojans killed at Troy (*Iliad* 8.274, 12.187; see [[ormenus]] and [[ormenus-killed-by-polypoetes]]).
