Dion brought Plato to Syracuse in the hope of making the young Dionysius II a philosopher-king. Exiled, he lived at Athens among Plato's friends, returned in 357 BCE with a small force, and took Syracuse (Plutarch, *Dion* 22–29). His rule grew harsh, and his friend [[callippus]] organized his murder (54–57). The Platonic *Seventh Letter* defends his aims. His father-in-law was [[dionysius-of-syracuse]].
