Θῶνος παράκοιτις / Αἰγυπτίη, “the wife of Thon, / a woman of Egypt” (*Odyssey* 4.228–229): the poem names him only as the husband of the woman who gave Helen her drugs (see [[polydamna]], [[nepenthe]] and [[egypt]]).

{{notes}}

## Outside Homer

Herodotus heard from the priests of Egypt that Paris, carrying Helen off from Sparta, was driven by winds to what is now called the Canopic mouth of the Nile, where his servants took refuge at the temple of Heracles and denounced him to the priests and to the warden of that mouth, “whose name was Thonis” (Herodotus 2.113.1–3); Thonis sent word to Proteus at Memphis, then seized Paris and his ships and brought him to the king with Helen and the treasure (Herodotus 2.114.1–2.115.1). A little later Herodotus quotes the verses on Polydamna, “the wife of Thon”, as a sign that Homer knew the story (Herodotus 2.116.4; see [[herodotus]], [[helen-in-egypt]] and [[paris]]). Strabo, describing the coast between Pharos and the Canopic mouth, says that there was once a city Thonis there, named for the king who received Menelaus and Helen as guests, and quotes the same verse (*Geography* 17.1.16; see [[strabo]]).
