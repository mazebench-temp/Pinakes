Maeon shares the command of the ambush with [[Polyphontes]], son of [[Autophonus]]. ἐπιείκελος ἀθανάτοισιν (“like the immortals”; [[like-the-immortals]]) is high praise for a man who appears in two lines. The poem does not say what the portents were, or why the gods wanted him spared. Of the fifty he is the one man Tydeus sends home (4.397–398). See [[tydeus]] and [[cadmeians]].

## Outside Homer

Later writers gave the survivor a sequel. Apollodorus tells the ambush in the same way: Tydeus killed all the fifty except Maeon (3.6.5). In the Theban story that Pausanias heard, Maeon buried Tydeus, who lay under three unhewn stones by the road from Thebes to Chalcis, and the Thebans cited the Iliad's line on Tydeus' grave as proof (Pausanias 9.18.1–2; Il. 14.114). In Statius' *Thebaid* he carries the news of the ambush back to Thebes, denounces Eteocles, and kills himself (books 2–3).
