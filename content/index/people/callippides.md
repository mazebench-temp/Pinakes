Xenophon makes Callippides “the actor who is so proud that he can make many people weep as they sit and watch” (*Symposium* 3.11), and Plutarch tells how he tried in vain to win the notice of the Spartan king Agesilaus (*Agesilaus* 21.4–5). In the *Poetics* he is the example of overacting: Mynniscus called him an ape (1461b34–35), and he was faulted for imitating women who are not free, as others are now (1462a8–10; see [[mynniscus-and-callippides]] and [[epic-and-tragedy]]).
