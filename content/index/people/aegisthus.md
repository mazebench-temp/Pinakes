In the Odyssey Zeus himself cites Aegisthus as the man who brought ruin on himself beyond his share: warned by Hermes, he courted Agamemnon's wife and killed him, and Orestes took vengeance (*Odyssey* 1.29–43; 3.303–310; 4.512–537; see [[clytemnestra-and-the-return]]). Aristotle uses him to show that the happy ending belongs to comedy: “there, those who are the bitterest enemies in the story, such as Orestes and Aegisthus, go off at the end as friends, and nobody is killed by anybody” (1453a36–39; see [[the-double-plot]]).
