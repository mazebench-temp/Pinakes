Orus stands between Aesymnus and Hipponous in the last verse of the list, Αἴσυμνόν τʼ Ὦρόν τε καὶ Ἱππόνοον μενεχάρμην, “and Aesymnus and Orus and Hipponous, steadfast in battle” (11.303; see [[aesymnus]] and [[hipponous]]). His is the shortest name of the nine, and Homer gives him nothing but the name: no father, no people and no wound. He is one of “the leaders of the Danaans” only in the narrator's summing up (11.304), and with that the battle turns from the leaders to the multitude (11.304–309; see [[hector]] and [[whom-first-whom-last]]).
