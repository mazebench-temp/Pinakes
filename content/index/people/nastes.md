The Carian entry names Nastes three times: first alone, as leader (2.867), then with his brother, in both orders (2.870–871). The man "who came to the war wearing gold, like a girl" is not named. The relative pronoun ὅς could refer to either brother, and the Greek does not settle it. Nastes, named first and most often, is the likelier; Amphimachus is the nearer name. The narrator calls him νήπιος, "the fool" ([[nepios]], [[like-a-girl]]). His gold did not keep off destruction: Achilles killed him "in the river" and took the gold himself. The river fighting of Book 21 does not name him. See [[the-river-battle-foretold]].
