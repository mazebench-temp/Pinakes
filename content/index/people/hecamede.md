## In Homer

**A prize for counsel.** Nestor “won” her (ἄρετο) from Tenedos when Achilles sacked the island, the only time the Iliad names that raid (11.625; see [[tenedos]] and [[achilles-raids]]). She is “the daughter of great-hearted Arsinous” (11.626; see [[arsinous]]). The Achaeans “picked” her “out for him” (ἔξελον, 11.627), the word for a prize set apart for a leader before the spoil is divided: so they chose Chryseis for Agamemnon (1.369) and Briseis for Achilles (16.56 = 18.444; see [[geras]] and [[division-of-spoils]]). In Nestor's story Mulius was “the best of them in fighting” (ὃς ἀριστεύεσκε μάχεσθαι, 11.746); Nestor was given his prize “because he was the best of all in counsel” (βουλῇ ἀριστεύεσκεν ἁπάντων, 11.627; see [[boule]] and [[aristos]]). In Book 4 he had called counsel and words “the prize of the old” (4.323). The Odyssey has a close parallel: the Phaeacians picked out the captive Eurymedusa for Alcinous, “because he ruled all the Phaeacians” (*Odyssey* 7.8–11).

**The drink.** “Hecamede of the lovely tresses” (ἐϋπλόκαμος, 11.624; see [[with-lovely-tresses]]) receives Nestor and Machaon when they come in from the battle. She sets out a table with feet of dark blue enamel, an onion, honey and barley meal, and Nestor's great cup (11.628–637; see [[table]] and [[the-cup-of-nestor]]), and makes the mixed drink:

{{quote:01fc14e5-7b88-5219-81ec-bb858fb3edba}}

The κυκεών is Pramnian wine with grated goat's cheese and barley stirred into it, like the potion Circe gives Odysseus' men (*Odyssey* 10.234–236; see [[kykeon]], [[wine]] and [[barley-grains]]). The poem calls her “the woman, like the goddesses” (γυνὴ ἐϊκυῖα θεῇσιν, 11.638), as it calls Briseis when she laments Patroclus (19.286).

**Book 14.** When the cry of the battle at the wall reaches Nestor, still drinking, he tells Machaon to sit and drink “until Hecamede of the lovely tresses / heats a warm bath and washes off the bloody gore” (14.5–7; see [[bathing]]). It is her last appearance.

## Outside Homer

In Plato's *Ion* Socrates asks, “when Homer says that Hecamede, Nestor's concubine (ἡ Νέστορος παλλακή), gives the wounded Machaon a mixed drink”, whether a doctor or a rhapsode should judge if Homer is right; Ion answers, a doctor (538b–c). Plato's quotation joins the cheese of 11.639–640 to the onion of 11.630, and gives the drink to Machaon alone; in Homer both men drink. In the *Republic* the same drink is given to the wounded Eurypylus (3.405e–406a), whom Patroclus treats in Homer (11.828–848; see [[plato-asclepiads]]). Homer does not call her a concubine; his word παλλακίς is used of the concubine of Phoenix's father (9.449, 9.452).
