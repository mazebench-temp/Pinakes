## Family

{{quote:82c79dab-d187-55c0-95bf-4b05e30db21e}}

Her father is Amphion, son of Iasus, king of Minyan Orchomenus in Boeotia, not the Theban Amphion of the same catalogue (see [[amphion-son-of-iasus]], [[orchomenus]] and [[amphion-of-thebes]]); her husband Neleus, son of Poseidon and Tyro (see [[neleus]] and [[tyro]]). Her sons are Nestor, the old king of the Iliad and of *Odyssey* 3, Chromius and Periclymenus, and her daughter is “strong Pero, a marvel to mortals” (*Odyssey* 11.287; see [[nestor]], [[chromius-son-of-neleus]], [[periclymenus]] and [[pero]]). Nestor tells in the Iliad that Neleus had twelve sons and that he alone was left when Heracles came to Pylos (*Iliad* 11.690–693; see [[heracles-sacks-pylos]]), so that the poem knows more sons than Chloris' three.

## Descriptions

περικαλλής, “beautiful” (*Odyssey* 11.281; see [[perikalles]]), and Neleus married her “for her beauty” (*Odyssey* 11.282). ὁπλοτάτη κούρη, “the youngest daughter” (*Odyssey* 11.283), as Polycaste is “the youngest daughter of Nestor, son of Neleus” (*Odyssey* 3.465; see [[polycaste]]). Her name is the adjective χλωρός, “pale, fresh”, made a woman's name.

## Outside Homer

Strabo, explaining the name of the Minyeios, the river of Nestor's country, offers among other origins “those who came with Chloris, the mother of Nestor, from Minyan Orchomenus” (*Geography* 8.3.19; see [[minyeios]] and [[strabo]]). W. W. Merry reports a later form of the story that made her the daughter of the Theban Amphion by Niobe, the one child whom the gods spared (*Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on *Odyssey* 11.283; see [[niobe]]).
