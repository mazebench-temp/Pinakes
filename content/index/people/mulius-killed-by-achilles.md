“And he went up to Mulius and stabbed him / with his spear in the ear; and at once through the other ear went / the bronze point” (20.472–474): Mulius is the sixth man Achilles kills after Apollo has saved Hector, after Tros, son of Alastor, and before Echeclus, son of Agenor (20.455–477; see [[tros-son-of-alastor]], [[echeclus-son-of-agenor]] and [[achilles]]). He is given no father and no country. παραστάς, “going up to him”, makes it a thrust at close quarters, not a throw. Walter Leaf thinks the contracted form οὖς, “ear”, suspicious in Homer, and reports conjectures that would restore the longer form (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 20.473).

**Other men of the name.** Homer has three others: the Epeian spearman, son-in-law of Augeas, who was the first man Nestor killed in the battle by the Alpheus (11.737–746; see [[mulius]]); a Trojan whom Patroclus kills when he pursues the Trojans toward the city (16.696; see [[mulius-killed-by-patroclus]]); and a herald from Dulichium, who mixes the wine for the suitors (*Odyssey* 18.423–424).
