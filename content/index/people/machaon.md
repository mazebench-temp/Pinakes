The name seems to be formed from μάχη ("battle"). Machaon has two scenes in the Iliad: he heals Menelaus in Book 4, and he is wounded himself in Book 11.

{{notes}}

## Elsewhere in Homer

In Book 11 Paris hits him in the right shoulder with an arrow, and Idomeneus urges Nestor to carry him from the field, since “a healer is a man worth many others” (11.504–520). Achilles, watching from his ship, sends [[Patroclus]] to find out who the wounded man is, and the narrator says of this errand, “this was the beginning of evil for him” (11.597–615).

## Outside Homer

In the *Little Iliad* he heals Philoctetes, and its poet also said that Eurypylus, the son of Telephus, killed him (Pausanias 3.26.9). Machaon had a sanctuary at Gerenia in Messenia, where his bones were said to lie.
