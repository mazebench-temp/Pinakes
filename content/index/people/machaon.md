The name seems to be formed from μάχη ("battle"). Machaon has two scenes in the Iliad. When Pandarus' arrow wounds Menelaus, Talthybius fetches him; he draws out the arrow, sucks out the blood and spreads on the wound the drugs Chiron gave his father (4.192–219). In Book 11 Paris hits him in the right shoulder with an arrow, and Idomeneus urges Nestor to carry him from the field, since "a healer is worth many other men" (11.504–520). Achilles, watching from his ship, sends [[Patroclus]] to find out who the wounded man is, and the narrator comments that this errand "was the beginning of evil" for Patroclus (11.597–615).

In the *Little Iliad* he heals Philoctetes, and its poet also said that Eurypylus, the son of Telephus, killed him (Pausanias 3.26.9). Machaon had a sanctuary at Gerenia in Messenia, where his bones were said to lie.
