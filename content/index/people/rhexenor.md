## The name

His name is the epithet ῥηξήνωρ, “breaker of men”, from ῥήγνυμι, “break”, and ἀνήρ, “man”, which Homer gives as an epithet only to Achilles (see [[breaker-of-men]]). The Suda glosses the plural ῥηξήνορες as ἀνδρεῖοι, “brave” (Suda ρ 138).

## Family

Poseidon begot Nausithous on Periboea, and Nausithous begot Rhexenor and Alcinous (*Odyssey* 7.56–63; see [[poseidon]], [[periboea-mother-of-nausithous]] and [[nausithous]]). Rhexenor died young: Apollo, god of the silver bow, struck him down in his hall, newly married and without a son, and he left one only child, a daughter, Arete, whom Alcinous made his wife (*Odyssey* 7.64–66; see [[apollo]], [[alcinous]] and [[arete-queen-of-the-phaeacians]]). So Alcinous married his niece, and Odysseus greets her by her father's name, “Arete, daughter of godlike Rhexenor” (*Odyssey* 7.146; see [[antitheos]]). W. W. Merry draws the family tree in this order: Poseidon and Periboea; their son Nausithous; his sons Rhexenor and Alcinous; and Rhexenor's daughter Arete, so that Alcinous is “uncle as well as husband of Arete” (*Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on *Odyssey* 7.54).

## Outside Homer

Hesiod, as a scholium on *Odyssey* 7.54 reports, made Arete the sister of Alcinous (*Catalogue of Women* fr. 49 Evelyn-White; see [[hesiod]] and [[arete-queen-of-the-phaeacians]]).
