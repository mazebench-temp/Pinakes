Μερμερίδης = Μέρμερος + the patronymic suffix -ίδης, “son of Mermerus”. Odysseus came back “from Ephyra, from Ilus, son of Mermerus” (*Odyssey* 1.259; see [[ilus-son-of-mermerus]] and [[odysseus-seeks-poison]]), and nothing else is said of the father. The name is the adjective μέρμερος, “grim, baneful”, which the Iliad uses of the deeds of war, μέρμερα ἔργα (see [[grim-deeds]]), and it is also the name of a Trojan whom Antilochus kills and strips (*Iliad* 14.513; see [[mermerus]]).

## Outside Homer

In later genealogy Mermerus is a son of Jason and Medea. In the *Library* of Apollodorus, Medea, abandoned by Jason at Corinth, kills Mermerus and Pheres, the children she had by him (*Library* 1.9.28; see [[apollodorus]] and [[medea-kills-her-children]]). Pausanias reports that the epic *Naupactia* made Jason move from Iolcus to Corcyra after the death of Pelias, and told that Mermerus, the elder of his children, was killed by a lioness while hunting on the mainland opposite (Pausanias 2.3.9; see [[pausanias]]). Merry reports a scholion that made Ilus son of Mermerus a great-grandson of Jason and Medea (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 1.259; see [[medea]]). Homer says nothing of the line.
