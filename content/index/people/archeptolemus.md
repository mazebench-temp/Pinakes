When Diomedes' spear kills Eniopeus, Hector leaves the body and takes Archeptolemus, “the bold son of Iphitus”, as his driver (8.126–129; see [[eniopeus]] and [[hector]]). θρασύς, “bold”, is the word the poem has just used of Hector himself, “a bold charioteer” (8.89), and it marks Archeptolemus at his death too (8.312; see [[bold]]).

Teucer, shooting from under Ajax's shield, sends two arrows “straight at Hector”. The first kills Gorgythion, a son of Priam; the second, “for Apollo made it swerve”, strikes Archeptolemus in the chest (8.300–313; see [[teucer]], [[gorgythion]] and [[apollo]]). He falls, and Hector grieves, in the verses used for Eniopeus (8.314–317 = 8.122–125). This time Hector gives the reins to his brother Cebriones (8.318–319; see [[cebriones]]).

The name is ἄρχω, “lead, begin”, and πτόλεμος, “war”: “leader in war”. The poem names him nowhere else.
