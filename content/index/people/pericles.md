summary: Athenian statesman and general (c. 495–429 BCE), the first man of Athens for thirty years, whose funeral speech gave Aristotle the image of the city's lost youth as the spring taken out of the year.

Pericles son of Xanthippus led Athens from the 450s until he died in the plague of 429 BCE. The buildings of the Acropolis, the Samian War (440–439) and the strategy of the first years of the Peloponnesian War were his. Thucydides gives him three speeches, among them the funeral speech for the dead of the war's first year (2.35–46), and judges that under him Athens was “in name a democracy, but in fact the rule of the first man” (2.65.9).

The *Rhetoric* keeps several of his sayings: the Samians are like children who take the morsel and cry (3.4, 1407a1–3), Aegina must be removed as the eyesore of the Piraeus (3.10, 1411a15–16), and a question he put to the seer Lampon about the rites (3.18, 1419a2–5).
