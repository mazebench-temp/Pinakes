When the Trojans flee toward the city, Patroclus cuts off the foremost battalions and hems them back toward the ships; “between / the ships and the river and the high wall / he rushed among them and killed them, and exacted the penalty for many” (16.396–398; see [[patroclus]]). Pronous is the first: “There first he struck Pronous with his shining spear / in the chest, where it was bared beside the shield, and loosened his limbs; / he fell with a thud” (16.399–401; see [[loosened-his-limbs]] and [[fell-with-a-thud]]). The second verse was said of Thoas, whom Menelaus killed in the first rout from the ships (16.312; see [[thoas-killed-by-menelaus]]). Thestor, Erylaus and nine more follow (16.401–418; see [[thestor-son-of-enops]], [[erylaus]] and [[erymas-killed-by-patroclus]]).

Walter Leaf took the wall of 16.397 to be the wall of Troy, not the Achaean wall, and reports that almost all editors had rejected the verse as a gloss because they took it for the Achaean wall (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 16.397).

The name is the adjective πρόνοος, “foreseeing”, from πρό, “before”, and νόος, “mind”. Pronous is named only here.
