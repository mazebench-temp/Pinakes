He is named only in Odysseus' list of seven Lycians (5.677–678; see [[lycians]] and [[odysseus]]). The name is the adjective ἅλιος, “of the sea”, as in ἁλίοιο γέροντος, “the Old Man of the Sea” (1.538; see [[old-man-of-the-sea]]). The same letters make another word, ἅλιος, “vain, fruitless”: “the shaft did not fly from his hand in vain” (οὐχ ἅλιον βέλος, 5.18). Only the capital tells the name from either. In the Odyssey a son of Alcinous, king of the Phaeacians, has the same name (Od. 8.119, 8.370).

## Outside Homer

Ovid's Ulysses names all seven Lycians among the men he has killed, in Homer's order (*Metamorphoses* 13; see [[ovid]]).
