Αἴας δʼ αὖ Φόρκυνα δαΐφρονα Φαίνοπος υἱόν, “And Ajax in turn, as Phorcys, the wise-hearted son of Phaenops, / stood over Hippothous, struck him in the middle of the belly” (17.312–313): the father is named at his son's death, and nothing more is told of him (see [[phorcys]], [[hippothous]] and [[wise-hearted]]). The Catalogue names Phorcys with godlike Ascanius as the leader of the Phrygians, but gives him no father (2.862–863); Walter Leaf takes the man of *Iliad* 17 to be the Phrygian leader (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 17.312).

He cannot be the old Trojan Phaenops of *Iliad* 5, whose two sons, Xanthus and Thoon, Diomedes killed, and who had no other son to leave his wealth to (5.152–158; see [[phaenops]]). Nor is he Phaenops, son of Asius, of Abydos, the dearest of Hector's guest-friends, whose likeness Apollo takes later in the book (17.582–584; see [[phaenops-son-of-asius]]).
