ἡνίοχον θεράποντα, “the charioteer, his attendant” (8.119): Eniopeus drives Hector's horses, as Mydon drove for Pylaemenes (5.580; see [[mydon]]). Diomedes' spear, thrown at Hector, kills him instead, the first man Diomedes kills in the book (see [[diomedes]] and [[nestor]]). The two verses of his fall return when Teucer's arrow, also meant for Hector, kills the next driver, Archeptolemus (8.122–123 = 8.314–315; see [[archeptolemus]] and [[menos]]), and so do the verses of Hector's grief (8.124–125 = 8.316–317). Hector leaves him “to lie there” and finds a new driver at once (8.126–129; see [[hector]]).

The name looks formed from ἡνία, “reins”, as in ἡνίοχος, “charioteer”: a driver's name. The poem names him nowhere else.
