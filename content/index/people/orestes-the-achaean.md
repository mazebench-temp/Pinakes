πλήξιππον Ὀρέστην, “Orestes, driver of horses” (5.705). πλήξιππος = πλήσσω (“strike”) + ἵππος (“horse”): “lashing the horses”, a charioteer's epithet, used of Pelops (2.104) and of Menestheus (4.327; see [[driver-of-horses]]). He is the second of the six killed by Hector and Ares, who are not told apart (see [[hector]] and [[ares]]). The name is usually derived from ὄρος (“mountain”).

Homer has two other men of the name. Orestes, son of Agamemnon, is named in the Iliad only among his father's children (9.142, 9.284); in the Odyssey he is the son who avenged his father by killing Aegisthus (Od. 1.29–30, 1.298–300). A Trojan Orestes, one of Asius' men, is killed by Leonteus at the Achaean wall (12.139, 12.193).
