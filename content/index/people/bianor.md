At the hour of the woodcutter's meal the Danaans break the Trojan battalions, and Agamemnon “sprang in first” (11.84–92). Bianor is the first man he kills in the day of his aristeia (see [[the-aristeia-of-agamemnon]] and [[agamemnon]]). He is ποιμὴν λαῶν, “shepherd of the people”, a leader's title (see [[shepherd-of-the-people]]), and αὐτόν, “him first”, sets the leader before his companion, who had leapt down from the chariot to face Agamemnon. Only the companion's death is told. Agamemnon leaves the two of them lying “with their chests shining bare, for he had stripped off their tunics” (11.99–100; see [[stripping-the-dead]]), and goes on to Isus and Antiphus, two more men in one chariot (11.101–109).

The name seems to join βίη, “might”, to ἀνήρ, “man”. The poem names him nowhere else.
