{{quote:5a748409-eaf8-5840-a122-71774758137d}}

ἑταῖρον Ὀϊλῆα πλήξιππον, “his companion, Oileus, driver of horses” (11.93). πλήξιππος, “striking horses”, from πλήσσω and ἵππος, is said of the man who drives with the whip: of Pelops (2.104), Menestheus (4.327) and Orestes the Achaean (5.705; see [[driver-of-horses]]). The epithet would suit Bianor's charioteer, but the poem does not say which of the two held the reins. Oileus leaps down and stands to face Agamemnon, and as he comes on, Agamemnon stabs him in the forehead: the helmet, “heavy with bronze”, “did not hold the spear”, and the brain was spattered inside (11.94–98; see [[helmet]] and [[heavy-with-bronze]]). The last verse and a half return for two more men whose helmets fail them, Damasus (12.185–186) and Demoleon (20.399–400). Agamemnon strips the two and leaves them lying (11.99–100; see [[stripping-the-dead]]).

He shares his name with the father of the Locrian Ajax (2.527, 2.727–728; see [[oileus]] and [[ajax-son-of-oileus]]). The poem says nothing to connect them.
