καὶ Μελάνιππον, “and Melanippus”, closes the list of the eight men whom Teucer brings down with his arrows while Ajax covers him with his shield (8.273–277; see [[teucer]]). Nothing is told of him but his name and his death.

The name joins μέλας, “black”, and ἵππος, “horse”, like many Greek names made with ἵππος. Three more men bear it in the Iliad. Melanippus, son of Hicetaon, who herded cattle at Percote until the Achaeans came and then lived in Priam's house, honored like one of his sons, is rebuked by Hector and killed by Antilochus (15.546–583; see [[hicetaon]], [[percote]] and [[antilochus]]). Patroclus kills a Trojan Melanippus in his last charge (16.695), and an Achaean Melanippus is among the young men whom Odysseus takes to fetch Agamemnon's gifts for Achilles (19.238–241).

## Outside Homer

The best-known Melanippus of later legend is the Theban son of Astacus, who killed Mecisteus and Tydeus in the war of the Seven. Cleisthenes, tyrant of Sicyon, brought his cult from Thebes to spite the Argive hero Adrastus, Mecisteus' brother and Tydeus' father-in-law, and gave him the sacrifices and festivals that had been Adrastus' (Herodotus 5.67.2–4; see [[tydeus]], [[mecisteus]] and [[adrastus]]).
