ἦ ῥα, καὶ Ἀντιλόχοιο Νοήμονι δῶκεν ἑταίρῳ / ἵππον ἄγειν, “He spoke, and to Noemon, the companion of Antilochus, he gave / the mare to lead away” (23.612–613; see [[menelaus]] and [[antilochus]]). He is named only here. The mare is the second prize of the chariot race, which Antilochus has yielded to Menelaus rather than swear that he won it fairly; Menelaus gives her back, “though she is mine”, and takes for himself the third prize, the cauldron (23.609–613; see [[amends-and-reconciliation]] and [[cauldron]]). Antilochus does not take her from Menelaus' hand himself, as Diomedes left the first prizes to Sthenelus, who gave them to his companions to lead away and to carry (23.511–513; see [[sthenelus]]). When Antilochus ran to Achilles with the news of Patroclus' death, he left his armor with another companion, Laodocus, who was wheeling his horses close by (17.698–699; see [[laodocus-companion-of-antilochus]]).

The name is the adjective νοήμων, “thoughtful”, from νοέω, “perceive, think” (see [[noos]]). Two other men bear it: the Lycian whom Odysseus kills (5.678; see [[noemon-of-lycia]]), and in the Odyssey Noemon, son of Phronius, who lends Telemachus a ship (Od. 2.386–387, 4.630–656).
