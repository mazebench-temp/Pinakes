λαοὺς Ὀτρῆος καὶ Μυγδόνος ἀντιθέοιο, "the people of Otreus and godlike Mygdon" (3.186). ἀντίθεος = ἀντί ("equal to, a match for") + θεός ("god"): "godlike" ([[antitheos]]). Homer names him only here, beside [[Otreus]]. See [[phrygia]], [[sangarius]] and [[priam-and-the-amazons]].

## Outside Homer

Virgil gives him a son, the young Coroebus, "son of Mygdon" (*Mygdonides*), who came to Troy for love of Cassandra and died on the night of the sack (*Aeneid* 2.341–346, 2.424–426). Pausanias, describing the painting of the sack by Polygnotus at Delphi, also calls Coroebus the son of Mygdon (10.27.1).
