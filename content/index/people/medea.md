Hesiod knows her as the daughter of Aeetes, whom Jason led away from Colchis (*Theogony* 992–1002), and Pindar's fourth *Pythian* tells how she helped him yoke the fire-breathing bulls. An older Corinthian tradition said that the Corinthians killed her children (Pausanias 2.3.6–7); Euripides made her kill them herself (see [[medea-play]], [[medea-kills-her-children]] and [[jason]]). In the *Poetics* she is the type of the deed done in full knowledge, as the old poets made it, “as Euripides too made Medea kill her children” (1453b27–29), and the *Medea* is criticized for its ending by the machine (1454a37–b1; see [[the-machine]]).
