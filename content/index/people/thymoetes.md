Homer names him only here, second among the elders who sit with Priam at the Scaean gates (3.146; see [[trojan-elders]]).

## Outside Homer

Virgil gives him a part in the fall of Troy. When the Trojans find the wooden horse on the shore, "Thymoetes is the first to urge that it be brought within the walls and set on the citadel, whether by treachery or because the fates of Troy were already tending that way" (*Aeneid* 2.32–34).
