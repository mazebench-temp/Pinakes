εἰδὼς πυγμαχίης υἱὸς Πανοπῆος Ἐπειός, “skilled in boxing, the son of Panopeus, Epeius” (23.665): Homer says nothing more of him (see [[epeius]]). The name is that of a town of the Phocians, which the Catalogue lists with Crisa and Daulis (2.520) and where Schedius, the best of the Phocians, had his home (17.306–308; see [[panopeus]] and [[schedius]]); Homer does not say that the father came from it.

## Outside Homer

Pausanias reports that the epic poet Asius made Panopeus and Crisus sons of Phocus, the son of Aeacus, and Epeius the son of Panopeus (*Description of Greece* 2.29.2–4; see [[pausanias]]). The people of Panopeus said that their town took its name from the father of Epeius (10.4.1). A verse of the Hesiodic *Catalogue of Women* names a daughter of a Panopeus, Aegle, whom Theseus loved (Πανοπηΐδος Αἴγλης, Hesiod fr. 76 Evelyn-White, quoted by Plutarch, *Theseus* 20, and Athenaeus 13.557a; see [[theseus]]).
