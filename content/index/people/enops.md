Ἠνοπίδην … Ἤνοπι βουκολέοντι, “the son of Enops … to Enops as he tended his cattle” (14.444–445): Homer names him only as the father of Satnius, whom the son of Oileus kills, and says only that a Naiad bore him the son by the banks of the Satnioeis (see [[satnius]], [[nymphs]] and [[satnioeis]]). βουκολέων, “tending cattle”, is said also of Anchises, to whom Aphrodite bore Aeneas (5.313; see [[anchises]]), and Bucolion was shepherding his sheep when the Naiad Abarbarea lay with him (6.21–26; see [[bucolion]]).

Homer has two other men of the name. Thestor, “son of Enops”, a Trojan, is Patroclus' second victim in his charge (16.401–410; see [[thestor-son-of-enops]]); the poem does not say whether his father is the herdsman of the Satnioeis (see [[enops-father-of-thestor]]). Clytomedes, “son of Enops”, was beaten by Nestor at boxing in the funeral games of Amarynceus (23.634; see [[clytomedes]] and [[enops-father-of-clytomedes]]). ἦνοψ is also an adjective of bronze, “gleaming” (ἤνοπι χαλκῷ, 16.408, 18.349).

## Outside Homer

[[Strabo]] quotes the lines twice with Οἰνοπίδην … Οἴνοπι, “son of Oenops … to Oenops” (*Geography* 13.1.50, 13.3.1), the name of the father of Helenus, whom Hector kills (5.707; see [[oenops]]).
