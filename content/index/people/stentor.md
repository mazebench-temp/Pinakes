χαλκεόφωνος, “with the voice of bronze” (5.785): χαλκός (“bronze”) and φωνή (“voice”). Homer names Stentor only here and tells nothing more of him. Gods usually take the likeness of someone their listeners know, so Stentor was presumably a man of the Achaean army. Hera needs his voice, not his person: what she shouts is “Shame, Argives” (5.787; see [[hera]] and [[divine-epiphany]]). The name looks like an agent noun from στένω, “groan, roar”: “the Roarer”.

## Outside Homer

Aristotle, asking how large a city can be, wonders who could be its herald if its people were too many to hear him, “unless he had the voice of a Stentor” (*Politics* 7.4, 1326b). English has used “stentorian” of a loud voice since the seventeenth century.
