## The name

Κλυτόνηος seems to join κλυτός, “famous”, and νηῦς, “ship”, the two words of the epithet of his people, ναυσίκλυτοι, “famed for ships”: Φαίηκες δολιχήρετμοι, ναυσίκλυτοι ἄνδρες, “the Phaeacians of the long oars, men famed for ships” (*Odyssey* 8.191, 8.369; see [[famed-for-ships]] and [[phaeacians]]). Almost all Phaeacian names, W. W. Merry observes, are taken from seafaring life; of the sons of Alcinous he excepts only Laodamas (*Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on *Odyssey* 8.111).

## Family

He is one of the three sons of Alcinous who stand up for the games, “Laodamas and Halius and godlike Clytoneus” (*Odyssey* 8.118–119; see [[alcinous]], [[laodamas-son-of-alcinous]] and [[halius-son-of-alcinous]]). The king has five sons, two married and three unmarried (*Odyssey* 6.62–63), and the three who compete are probably the unmarried ones (Merry on *Odyssey* 8.204). Nausicaa is their sister (see [[nausicaa]]).

## Descriptions

- ἀντίθεος, “godlike” (*Odyssey* 8.119; see [[antitheos]]).
- ἀμύμων, “noble” (*Odyssey* 8.123; see [[blameless]]).
