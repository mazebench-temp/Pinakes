Dorieus belonged to the family of Diagoras of Rhodes, the boxer celebrated in Pindar's seventh *Olympian*. Thucydides dates the revolt of Mytilene by “the Olympiad in which Dorieus the Rhodian won for the second time” (3.8.1), that of 428 BCE. Pausanias, describing the family's statues at Olympia, credits him with three successive Olympic victories in the pancration, eight at the Isthmus and seven at Nemea; he fought for Sparta with his own ships in the Ionian War, was captured by the Athenians and brought to Athens, and was let go unharmed when the assembly saw him (6.7.1–5). Xenophon shows him bringing fourteen ships from Rhodes into the Hellespont (*Hellenica* 1.1.2).
