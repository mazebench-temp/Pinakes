Echemmon and Chromius are “two sons of Priam, descendant of Dardanus”, riding in one chariot (5.159–160), and the last of Diomedes' victims before Aeneas goes to find Pandarus. Diomedes brings them down “as a lion leaps among cattle and breaks the neck of a heifer or a cow” (5.161–162; see [[lion-among-cattle]]), strips their armor and sends their horses to the ships (5.163–165). They are the second and third sons of Priam to die in the poem, after the bastard Democoon (4.499; see [[democoon]] and [[sons-of-priam]]). Neither is named again.
