## Descriptions

- δῖα, “brilliant” (*Odyssey* 3.266), which Merry takes as “queenly”, of rank or birth only (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 3.266; see [[brilliant]]).
- στυγερή, “hateful”: she is Orestes' “hateful mother” (*Odyssey* 3.310).
- δολόμητις, “scheming” (*Odyssey* 11.422), the epithet of Aegisthus (see [[schemer]]); in the same speech Agamemnon's shade calls her οὐλομένη, “accursed” (*Odyssey* 11.410).

{{notes}}

## Elsewhere in Homer

In the Iliad she is named only in the first book, when Agamemnon says he prefers his captive Chryseis to her (1.113–114). The Odyssey calls her the daughter of Tyndareus (*Odyssey* 24.199) and tells again and again how, with Aegisthus, she brought about Agamemnon's death on his return (*Odyssey* 3.262–275, 4.512–537, 11.405–434), a warning set against Penelope's faithfulness. See [[the-murder-of-agamemnon]] and [[clytemnestra-and-the-return]].

## Outside Homer

Aeschylus' *Agamemnon* gives her a motive the Iliad does not mention: the sacrifice of her daughter Iphigenia.
