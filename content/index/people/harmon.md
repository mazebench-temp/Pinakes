“And Meriones slew Phereclus, son of the builder, the son of Harmon” (5.59–60): Φέρεκλον … τέκτονος υἱὸν Ἁρμονίδεω. Ἁρμονίδεω is the epic genitive of Ἁρμονίδης, and it goes with τέκτονος, “the builder”: the builder is Harmon's son, and Phereclus is Harmon's grandson. Some take Τέκτων as a proper name, so that Phereclus is “son of Tecton, son of Harmon”. The nominative Ἅρμων is not found in Homer; it is inferred from the patronymic.

The name belongs with ἁρμονίη, “joint, fastening”, the joints with which Odysseus fastens his raft (Od. 5.248, 5.361), and with ἁρμόζω, “fit together”. A joiner, a builder, and a son who built the ships of Alexander: the family's names and its trade go together. See [[phereclus]].
