**Hector's companion.** The verse that brings him in, ἔσκε δʼ ἐνὶ Τρώεσσι, “There was among the Trojans”, stands, as Walter Leaf notes, for the usual ἦν δέ τις ἐν (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 17.575), the words that introduce the priest Dares and the spy Dolon (5.9, 10.314; see [[dares]] and [[dolon]]). ἀφνειός τʼ ἀγαθός τε, “rich and brave”, is said also of Euchenor, the seer's son from Corinth, before Paris kills him (13.663–664; see [[euchenor]], [[wealthy]] and [[brave]]). Hector honored him most “of all the people”, δήμου, which Leaf takes as the community rather than the commons (on 17.577), because he was his ἑταῖρος … φίλος εἰλαπιναστής, “his dear companion at the feast” (17.577; see [[hetairos]] and [[feast]]). The noun is found only here in Homer; Leaf renders it “boon-companion”, from εἰλαπίνη, “feast”, and adds that Zeus was worshipped in Cyprus under the title εἰλαπιναστής (on 17.577).

**His death.** Athena, in the likeness of Phoenix, has told Menelaus that it will be a shame and a reproach to him if “the trusted companion of noble Achilles” is torn by dogs under the wall of the Trojans, and she puts in his breast “the daring of a fly” (17.553–573; see [[athena]] and [[phoenix]]). He goes to stand over Patroclus and throws his spear, and it is Podes who falls: Menelaus strikes him “on the belt / as he darted into flight, and drove the bronze straight through” (17.578–579; see [[belt]] and [[menelaus]]), and “he fell with a thud” (17.580; see [[fell-with-a-thud]]). Then Menelaus drags the body out from among the Trojans into the crowd of his companions (17.581).

**The taunt.** Apollo, in the likeness of Phaenops, son of Asius, the dearest of Hector's guest-friends, rebukes him for shrinking back before Menelaus, “who before now at least / was a soft spearman”, and ends with the dead man: “and he has killed your trusted companion, / brave among the champions, Podes, son of Eetion” (17.582–590; see [[apollo]], [[phaenops-son-of-asius]] and [[trusted-companion]]). “Brave among the champions” is said also of Echepolus, the first man killed in the Iliad (4.458; see [[echepolus]]). “So he spoke, and a black cloud of grief covered him, / and he went through the champions, armored in gleaming bronze” (17.591–592; see [[hector]] and [[achos]]). Athena had called Patroclus “the trusted companion of noble Achilles” a few verses before (17.557), and the verse of Hector's grief for his companion returns when Antilochus brings Achilles the news that Patroclus is dead (ἄχεος νεφέλη ἐκάλυψε μέλαινα, 18.22).

**His father.** He can hardly be a son of Eetion, the father of Andromache, whose seven sons Achilles killed on a single day (6.421–423; see [[eetion-father-of-podes]] and [[eetion]]).
