Aretaon has one epithet, δῖος, “brilliant” (see [[brilliant]]), and no father or home. He dies in a half-line with no verb of its own: Τεῦκρος δʼ Ἀρετάονα δῖον, “and Teucer slew brilliant Aretaon” (6.31), takes ἐξενάριξεν (“slew and stripped”) from Odysseus' kill in the line before. His killer comes into the poem in the same half-line (see [[teucer]]). The name occurs only here.
