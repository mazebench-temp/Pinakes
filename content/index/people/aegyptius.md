## The name

Αἰγύπτιος is the adjective “Egyptian”, which the Odyssey gives to Proteus, the Old Man of the Sea, Πρωτεὺς Αἰγύπτιος, “Proteus the Egyptian” (*Odyssey* 4.385, a plain rendering; see [[egypt]]). The poem does not say why an Ithacan elder bears it.

## Family

He had four sons (*Odyssey* 2.17–22). Antiphus the spearman sailed with Odysseus to Troy, and the Cyclops killed him in his cave (see [[antiphus-son-of-aegyptius]] and [[polyphemus-the-cyclops]]); Eurynomus keeps company with the suitors (see [[eurynomus]] and [[the-suitors]]); the other two, who are not named, work their father's fields.

## Descriptions

- ἥρως, “hero” (*Odyssey* 2.15), as old Laertes (*Odyssey* 1.189, 2.99) and the old bird-reader Halitherses (*Odyssey* 2.157) are called (see [[heros]]).
- γήραϊ κυφός, “bent with age” (*Odyssey* 2.16). κυφός, “bent, stooping”, occurs only here in Homer.
- μυρία ᾔδη, “knew countless things” (*Odyssey* 2.16; see [[geron]]).
