καὶ δῖον Ἀγαυόν, “and brilliant Agauus” (24.251): the last of the nine sons whom Priam rebukes as he makes ready to ransom Hector (24.248–264; see [[sons-of-priam]] and [[priam]]). Both words are also common epithets, δῖος, “brilliant”, and ἀγαυός, “noble” (see [[brilliant]] and [[noble]]), and the phrase could be read either way, “brilliant Agauus” or “noble Dius”. The text prints Ἀγαυός as the name, as δῖος stands before the name of Agathon two verses above (24.249; see [[agathon-son-of-priam]] and the translation's note on 24.251).

## Outside Homer

Walter Leaf reports that the scholia point out that, in the absence of other notice, one cannot say whether Δῖος or Ἀγαυός is the proper name, and that Pherecydes mentioned a Dius as a son of Priam (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 24.250).
