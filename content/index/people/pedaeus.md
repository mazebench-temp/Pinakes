**A bastard son.** ὅς ῥα νόθος μὲν ἔην, “who was a bastard” (5.70): a son born to Antenor by a woman who was not his wife (see [[nothos]]). The wife herself brought him up. πύκα, “closely, with care”, and ἶσα φίλοισι τέκεσσι, “like her own dear children”, describe the upbringing, and χαριζομένη πόσεϊ ᾧ, “to please her husband”, gives the reason: she raised him as a favor (χάρις) to Antenor. Telamon too raised his bastard son Teucer in his own house (8.283–284), and in a tale that Odysseus tells in the Odyssey a Cretan says that his father honored him, the son of a bought concubine, equally with his legitimate sons (Od. 14.202–204). Theano is named here for the first time. In Book 6 she opens the temple of Athena as the goddess's priestess (6.298–300). See [[theano]] and [[antenor]].

**The wound.** Meges comes close and strikes him κεφαλῆς κατὰ ἰνίον, at the back of the head where it meets the neck; ἰνίον is found only here. The point passes through the teeth and cuts under the tongue, and Pedaeus falls in the dust “and bit the cold bronze with his teeth” (5.75), a verse found only here. The dead man's jaws close on the spearhead that has passed through them. See [[meges]] and [[androktasia]].

**The name.** Πήδαιον is also the name of a town: Imbrius lived in Pedaeum before the Achaeans came (13.172).
