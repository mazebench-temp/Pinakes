The Lycian Glaucus kills him, the first man the poem shows Glaucus killing (see [[glaucus]]). He is named with his patronymic, Δεξιάδην, “the son of Dexius” (7.15; see [[dexius]]). The spear catches him ἵππων ἐπιάλμενον, “as he was leaping up behind his swift horses”, that is, onto his chariot; ἐπιάλμενον occurs only here. So Idomeneus killed Phaestus in Book 5, stabbing him “on the right shoulder as he was mounting his chariot” (5.46; see [[phaestus-son-of-borus]]). “He fell from the chariot to the ground, and his limbs were loosened” (7.16): the last half-line ended the death of Eioneus four lines before (7.12; see [[eioneus]] and [[loosened-his-limbs]]).

The name joins ἶφι (“with might”) and νόος (“mind”).
