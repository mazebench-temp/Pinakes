## In Book 1

{{quote:4630ecf7-c829-5e61-b61f-793096803b5a}}

Πηληϊάδεω: "of the son of Peleus". The patronymic is the second name in the poem, after the goddess.

{{quote:a873fb78-7fbb-55e9-a4d4-a7d50034233e}}

σεο πατρὸς ἐνὶ μεγάροισιν: "in my father's halls". This is where Achilles grew up and where he heard his mother tell the story of Zeus' binding. See [[thetis-in-the-house-of-peleus]].

## Outside Homer

Peleus is the son of Aeacus, a son of Zeus (Iliad 21.189). The gods attended his wedding to Thetis (24.59–63), and in the *Cypria* the wedding feast was the scene of Eris' golden apple, which led to the Judgment of Paris. When Achilles sailed for Troy, Peleus sent Phoenix with him (9.438–443). In Book 24 Achilles weeps for him with Priam, an old father who will not see his son again.
