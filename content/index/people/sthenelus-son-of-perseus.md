Σθένελος, from σθένος (“strength”), is also the name of Diomedes' companion, the son of Capaneus (see [[sthenelus]]). Homer names this Sthenelus twice, both times in Agamemnon's story of the birth of Heracles and both times with his father's name: Hera came to Argos, where she knew “the strong wife of Sthenelus, son of Perseus” (Σθενέλου Περσηϊάδαο, 19.116), and told Zeus that a man was born to rule the Argives, “Eurystheus, child of Sthenelus the son of Perseus” (19.123; see [[eurystheus]] and [[the-birth-of-heracles]]). Περσηϊάδης, “son of Perseus”, occurs nowhere else in Homer.

## Outside Homer

In [[apollodorus|Apollodorus]] he is one of the five sons whom Andromeda bore to Perseus in Mycenae, with Alcaeus, Heleus, Mestor and Electryon, the father of Alcmene. By Nicippe, daughter of Pelops, he had two daughters, Alcyone and Medusa, and then a son, Eurystheus, who also reigned over Mycenae (*Library* 2.4.5; see [[perseus]], [[alcmene]] and [[pelops]]). When Amphitryon killed Electryon by accident, Sthenelus took it as a pretext to banish him from all Argos, seized the throne of Mycenae and Tiryns himself, and entrusted Midea to Atreus and Thyestes, the sons of Pelops (*Library* 2.4.6; see [[amphitryon]], [[mycenae]], [[tiryns]], [[atreus]] and [[thyestes]]). Walter Leaf notes that the mythographers give several names for his wife (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 19.116); Frazer, in his note on Apollodorus 2.4.5, cites a scholium on Iliad 19.119 for the name Antibia and Tzetzes (*Chiliades* 2.172, 2.192) for Archippe.
