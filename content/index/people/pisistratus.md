Pisistratus had made his name in the war with Megara when, Herodotus says, he wounded himself and his mules, drove into the agora, and told the Athenians that his enemies had tried to kill him. He asked for a guard. The assembly gave him a band of club-bearers, and with them he seized the Acropolis (Herodotus 1.59). He was twice driven out and twice came back, once with a tall woman named Phye dressed as Athena riding beside him in a chariot (1.60–64), and he ruled until his death in 528/7 ([Aristotle], *Constitution of the Athenians* 17.1). His sons Hippias and Hipparchus succeeded him; the murder of Hipparchus made [[harmodius]] and [[aristogeiton]] the city's heroes (Thucydides 6.54–59).

Strabo reports that some charged him, others Solon, with inserting the line that stations Ajax's ships beside the Athenians (see [[solon-and-salamis]]).

Herodotus says that the Peisistratids were by descent Pylians and Neleids, and that Hippocrates named his son Peisistratus after Nestor's son of that name (Herodotus 5.65.3–4; see [[peisistratus]]).
