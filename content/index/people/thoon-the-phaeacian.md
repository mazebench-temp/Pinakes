## The name

Θόων seems to be formed from θοός, “swift”, the commonest epithet of ships (see [[swift-ships]]). W. W. Merry counts the Phaeacian names among those “taken from circumstances of seafaring life” (*Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on *Odyssey* 8.111).

The Iliad gives the name to Trojans: the son of Phaenops whom Diomedes kills (*Iliad* 5.152; see [[thoon-son-of-phaenops]]), the man Odysseus kills when the Trojans close around him (*Iliad* 11.422; see [[thoon]]), and a companion of Asius at the Achaean wall (*Iliad* 12.140; see [[thoon-companion-of-asius]]), perhaps the Thoon whom Antilochus kills in the next battle (*Iliad* 13.545).
