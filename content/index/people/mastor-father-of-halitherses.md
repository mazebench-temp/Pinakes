Μαστορίδης = Μάστωρ + the patronymic suffix -ίδης, “son of Mastor”. The Odyssey names the father only in the patronymic, at the head of the second verse of the two that bring Halitherses into an assembly, “the old hero Halitherses, / son of Mastor” (*Odyssey* 2.157–158, 24.451–452; see [[halitherses]]). Nothing else is said of him.

The Iliad has a Mastor too, the father of Lycophron of Cythera, the attendant of Ajax, whom Ajax calls Μαστορίδης, “the son of Mastor”, when he is killed (*Iliad* 15.430–438; see [[mastor]] and [[lycophron]]). Nothing joins the two.
