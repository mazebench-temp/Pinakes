Κάστωρ Ὑλακίδης, “Castor, son of Hylax” (*Odyssey* 14.204): the father whom the disguised Odysseus gives himself when Eumaeus asks who he is and where he comes from (see [[the-tale-to-eumaeus]], [[odysseus]] and [[eumaeus]]). He is named only in this tale. In it he is a rich man of wide Crete, honored by his people, with many lawful sons by his wife and one more, the teller, by a bought concubine, whom he honored equally with the true-born (*Odyssey* 14.199–206; see [[crete]], [[cretans]], [[concubines]] and [[nothos]]). When Eumaeus passes the story on to Telemachus he keeps the Cretan line but not the father's name: “He claims that his line is from wide Crete” (*Odyssey* 16.62, a plain rendering). In his other false tales Odysseus has other fathers: to Penelope he is Aethon, younger son of Deucalion and brother of Idomeneus (*Odyssey* 19.180–184), and to Laertes the son of Apheidas (*Odyssey* 24.305).

## The name

Κάστωρ is the name of Helen's brother, “Castor, tamer of horses” (*Iliad* 3.237 = *Odyssey* 11.300; see [[castor]]), who in Homer has only the accusative Κάστορα; the nominative is found only here. The patronymic names a father who is mentioned nowhere else (see [[hylax]]).

## Descriptions

- ἀνέρος ἀφνειοῖο πάϊς, “the son of a rich man” (*Odyssey* 14.200; see [[wealthy]]).
- θεὸς ὣς τίετο δήμῳ, “was honored … by the people like a god” (*Odyssey* 14.205), the verse-end that the Iliad gives to Dolopion, Agamemnon, Aeneas, Thoas and Onetor (*Iliad* 5.78, 10.33, 11.58, 13.218, 16.605; see [[honored-like-a-god]]).
- ὄλβῳ τε πλούτῳ τε, “for his prosperity and wealth” (*Odyssey* 14.206), the words in which the Iliad praises Bathycles among the Myrmidons and Peleus (*Iliad* 16.596, 24.536; see [[olbos]]).
