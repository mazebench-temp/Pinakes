Electra is not named in Homer; the lyric poet Xanthus said that she was first called Laodice, one of Agamemnon's daughters in the Iliad (Aelian, *Varia Historia* 4.26; see [[laodice-daughter-of-agamemnon]]). Aeschylus' *Libation Bearers*, Sophocles' *Electra* and Euripides' *Electra* all tell her recognition of Orestes and his revenge (see [[electra-recognizes-orestes]], [[electra-play]] and [[libation-bearers]]). In the *Poetics* her reasoning is the example of recognition from inference: “someone like her has come; no one is like her but Orestes; so he has come” (1455a4–6).
