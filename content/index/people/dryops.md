**After Hector.** Apollo has snatched Hector away in a thick mist, and Achilles, after striking the mist three times, has shouted after him, “Now I will go after the others, whomever I can catch” (20.443–454; see [[hector]] and [[achilles]]). ὣς εἰπὼν, “So saying”, he stabs Dryops, the first of the ten men he kills in the next thirty-five verses (20.455–489; see [[androktasia]]). He is given no father and no country, and he dies in a verse and a half: the javelin in the middle of the neck, and ἤριπε δὲ προπάροιθε ποδῶν, “he fell before his feet” (20.456). The same words, προπάροιθε ποδῶν, have just been said of Hector's spear, turned back by Athena's breath, which “fell right there before his feet” (20.441). “And he left him there” (20.456), and went on to Demuchus (see [[demuchus]]).

**The name.** Δρύοψ seems to be formed on δρῦς, “oak”.
