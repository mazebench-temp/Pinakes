Left alone, Odysseus has argued with his own heart and chosen to stand, and the Trojans close around him like dogs and young men around a boar (11.401–420; see [[odysseus]] and [[the-boar-at-bay]]). Deiopites is the first he strikes, “on the shoulder from above, leaping at him with his sharp spear” (11.421). οὔτασεν, “wounded”, does not say whether he died. Thoon and Ennomus are slain and stripped, Chersidamas falls in the dust, and “these he left” gathers them all (11.422–426; see [[thoon]], [[ennomus-the-trojan]] and [[chersidamas]]). ἀμύμων, “noble”, is his only epithet (see [[blameless]]). He is named nowhere else.
