θεοειδής, "godlike" ([[godlike]]). His name matches his homeland, Ascania. In Book 13 an Ascanius from "deep-soiled Ascania" is among allies who had come as relief only the morning before (13.790–794); whether he is the same man is unclear. In Roman legend Ascanius, also called Iulus, is the son of Aeneas and the ancestor of the Julian family (Virgil, *Aeneid* 1.267–268). Homer's Phrygian has nothing to do with him.
