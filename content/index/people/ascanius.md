θεοειδής, "godlike" ([[godlike]]). His name matches his homeland, Ascania. In Book 13 an Ascanius from "deep-soiled Ascania" is among men who had come as relief only the morning before (13.790–794). Homer does not say whether he is the same man; Strabo makes him another, a leader of the Mysians from a nearer Ascania (*Geography* 12.4.5; see [[ascanius-the-mysian]]). In Roman legend Ascanius, also called Iulus, is the son of Aeneas and the ancestor of the Julian family (Virgil, *Aeneid* 1.267–268). Homer's Phrygian has nothing to do with him.
