He dies in half a verse, after Thoon: “and then he slew and stripped Thoon and Ennomus” (11.422; see [[thoon]], [[odysseus]] and [[stripping-the-dead]]). The Catalogue's Ennomus, the augur of the Mysians whose birds did not save him from Achilles in the river (2.858–861), is another man, for Hector calls on him by name in Book 17 (17.218; see [[ennomus]]).
