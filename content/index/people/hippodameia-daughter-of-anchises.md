**The eldest daughter.** Her story is told within the sentence of her husband's death: the narrator names Alcathous, turns aside for six verses to his wife, and comes back to him only to have Poseidon beat him down at Idomeneus' hands (13.427–435; see [[alcathous]], [[idomeneus]] and [[poseidon]]). She is πρεσβυτάτη θυγατρῶν, “the eldest of his daughters”, loved by “her father and lady mother”; the mother is not named. Her gifts, κάλλος, ἔργα and φρένες, are nearly those by which Agamemnon measured Chryseis against his wife Clytemnestra: form and stature, wits and handiwork (1.115; see [[chryseis]]). ἐκέκαστο ὁμηλικίην, “she surpassed all the girls of her age”, is said in the Odyssey of the old seer Halitherses among the men of his age (Od. 2.158).

**Her husband.** “And so / the best man in wide Troy had married her” (13.432–433): ὤριστος, “the best”, is the narrator's word for Alcathous, who leads a company against the wall with Paris and Agenor (12.93). As her husband he was Aeneas' brother-in-law, γαμβρός, and Deiphobus reminds Aeneas that Alcathous reared him in his house when he was small (13.463–466; see [[aeneas]] and [[anchises]]).

**The name.** Ἱπποδάμεια is “tamer of horses” (ἵππος + δάμνημι), a name for a daughter of a horse-breeding house (see [[tamer-of-horses]]); Anchises stole the strain of Tros' horses for his own mares (5.265–272). The Lapith wife of Peirithous has the same name (2.742; see [[hippodameia]]), and so does one of Penelope's handmaids in the Odyssey (Od. 18.182).
