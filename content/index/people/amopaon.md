καὶ Πολυαιμονίδην Ἀμοπάονα, “and Amopaon, son of Polyaemon” (8.276). In a list of bare names the patronymic gives one man a father, and with it half a verse (8.273–277; see [[teucer]] and [[polyaemon]]). Nothing else is told of him.
