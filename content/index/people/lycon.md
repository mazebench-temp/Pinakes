**A duel in the rout.** The seventh of the nine killings at the ships is the only one with a fight in it (16.306–351; see [[areilycus-killed-by-patroclus]]). “Peneleos and Lycon ran together” (16.335; see [[peneleos]]); both throw and miss, in vain (μέλεον), and “the two ran together again with swords” (16.337; see [[sword]]). Lycon strikes first, on the ridge of Peneleos' horsehair-crested helmet, and his sword shatters at the socket that holds the crest (16.338–339; see [[helmet]] and [[horsehair-crested]]), as Menelaus' sword shattered on the ridge of Paris' helmet in their duel (3.361–363). Peneleos strikes him on the neck below the ear, “and the whole sword sank in, and only the skin / held, and the head hung to one side, and his limbs were loosened” (16.340–341; see [[loosened-his-limbs]]). In *Iliad* 14 Peneleos had struck off the head of Ilioneus with his sword, helmet and all (14.496–498; see [[ilioneus]]).

**The name.** Λύκων is formed from λύκος, “wolf”, and has the same letters as λύκων, the genitive plural of λύκος, as in the simile of the deer that are the prey “of jackals and leopards and wolves” (13.103; see [[wolves]]). He is named only here.
