**Hector's guest-friend.** Of all Hector's ξεῖνοι, his guest-friends, Phaenops was the dearest, ξείνων φίλτατος (17.584; see [[xenia]]), and he had his home at Abydos, on the Hellespont (Ἀβυδόθι, 17.584; see [[abydos]]). The poem says no more of him; he appears only as the shape Apollo takes. A little earlier Glaucus has reproached Hector for leaving Sarpedon, “at once your guest-friend and your companion”, to the Argives (17.150; see [[glaucus]]). Apollo chooses his likenesses in this book for their weight with the man he rouses: for Aeneas the old herald of his father's house, for Hector this friend (17.322–325; see [[periphas-son-of-epytus]] and [[apollo]]), as in *Iliad* 16 he came to Hector as his uncle Asius (16.715–720).

**The taunt.** As Phaenops the god asks which of the Achaeans will still fear Hector, now that he has shrunk back before Menelaus, “who before now at least / was a soft spearman”, and tells him that Menelaus has killed Podes, his trusted companion (17.586–590; see [[hector]], [[menelaus]] and [[podes]]). “So he spoke, and a black cloud of grief covered him” (17.591).

**Which Asius.** The Iliad knows two men named Asius. Asius, son of Hyrtacus, led the men of Percote, Practius, Sestus, Abydos and Arisbe, and his horses carried him “from Arisbe” (2.835–839); Idomeneus killed him at the ships, and Meriones his son Adamas, also called Ἀσιάδης, “son of Asius” (13.384–393, 13.560–575; see [[asius]] and [[adamas]]). Asius, son of Dymas, was Hecuba's brother, Hector's uncle on his mother's side, of a family that lived in Phrygia by the Sangarius (16.717–719; see [[asius-son-of-dymas]]). Abydos, Phaenops' home, was one of the towns of the son of Hyrtacus, and that makes him the likelier father; and the narrator, who called Hecuba's brother Hector's uncle, calls Phaenops a guest-friend, not a kinsman. But the poem does not say. Two other men in the Iliad are called Phaenops, the father of Xanthus and Thoon (5.152–158) and the father of Phorcys (17.312; see [[phaenops]] and [[phaenops-father-of-phorcys]]).
