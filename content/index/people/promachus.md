{{quote:Iliad 14.476-485 | Acamas kills Promachus}}

**His death.** Ajax's spear, thrown at Polydamas, has killed Archelochus, son of Antenor (14.459–468; see [[archelochus]] and [[ajax]]). Promachus tries to drag the body away by the feet, and Archelochus' brother Acamas, standing over it, stabs him (14.476–477; see [[acamas]], [[antenor]] and [[fighting-over-the-body]]). The poem calls him “Promachus the Boeotian” (Πρόμαχον Βοιώτιον, 14.476), as it will call Peneleos, their leader, “the Boeotian” (17.597; see [[boeotians]]). He is not among the five Boeotian leaders of the Catalogue (2.494–495).

**Two boasts.** Acamas boasts over him “terribly … shouting aloud”, in the verse with which Polydamas has just boasted over Prothoenor (14.478, 14.453; see [[prothoenor]] and [[polydamas]]). The Argives will not have the toil and misery to themselves: “Consider how your Promachus sleeps, overcome / by my spear, so that the penalty for my brother / may not long go unpaid” (14.482–484; see [[poine]] and [[kasignetos]]). The kill pays for his brother. Then, as after Polydamas' boast, “grief came upon the Argives at his boasting”, and one man is stirred “most of all” (14.486–487 = 14.458–459). This time it is Peneleos, the leader of the Boeotians. He kills Ilioneus, cuts off his head, holds it up to the Trojans, and answers Acamas through the dead men's families: Ilioneus' father and mother are to wail for him in their halls, “for neither will the wife of Promachus, son of Alegenor, / be glad at her dear husband's coming home” when the Achaeans sail back from Troy (14.501–505; see [[peneleos]], [[ilioneus]] and [[bereaved-parents]]).

**The name.** πρόμαχος is the common word for a man who fights in front; the πρόμαχοι are the men the translation calls “champions” (ἐνὶ προμάχοις, “among the champions”, 4.253). As a name it suits a fighter, as Ἀρχέλοχος, “leader of the company”, suits the man over whose body he dies (see [[archelochus]]).
