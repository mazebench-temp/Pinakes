**The third company.** Peisander leads the third of the five ranks of the Myrmidons (16.168–172, 16.193; see [[myrmidons]]). He is ἀρήϊος, “warlike”, as Eudorus is in the verse that introduces the second company (16.179; see [[warlike]] and [[eudorus]]), and Μαιμαλίδης, “son of Maemalus” (16.194; see [[maemalus]]). His praise is a ranking: he “stood out among all the Myrmidons / at fighting with the spear, after the companion of the son of Peleus” (16.194–195). The companion is Patroclus, Πηλεΐωνος ἑταῖρος, whom Achilles is sending out in his own armor at the head of them all (see [[patroclus]] and [[hetairos]]): the verse measures Peisander against the man he will follow into the battle. He does nothing more in the poem and is not named again.

**Others of the name.** The name, from πείθω, “persuade”, and ἀνήρ, “man”, is borne by three other men in Homer: the son of Antimachus whom Agamemnon kills (11.122–147; see [[peisander-son-of-antimachus]]), the Trojan whom Menelaus kills with his sword (13.601–642; see [[peisander-killed-by-menelaus]]), and one of Penelope's suitors (*Odyssey* 18.299, 22.243, 22.268).
