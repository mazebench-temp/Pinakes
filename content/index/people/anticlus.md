summary: One of the Achaeans hidden in the wooden horse, the only one who wanted to answer when Helen called the chieftains by name in their wives' voices, until Odysseus pressed his hands upon his mouth (*Odyssey* 4.285–289). Aristarchus rejected the five verses that name him, since the Iliad never mentions him.
aliases: [Antiklos]
# Ἄντικλος (Od. 4.286) is his only mention in Homer; the form occurs nowhere in the Iliad or in Aristotle.
grc: [Ἄντικλος]
---

He is named once, in Menelaus' story of the night in the wooden horse, and is otherwise unknown to Homer: the Iliad does not count him among the Achaeans at Troy (see [[sinon-and-the-wooden-horse]] and [[odysseus]]).

{{notes}}

## Outside Homer

A scholium says that Anticlus came “from the Cycle”, and Merry suggests that his story may have come from the cyclic *Sack of Troy* (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 4.285; see [[sack-of-troy]]).
