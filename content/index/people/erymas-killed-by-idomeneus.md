The last of the nine killings at the ships is the most exact in its anatomy (16.306–351; see [[areilycus-killed-by-patroclus]] and [[androktasia]]). Idomeneus stabs Erymas “in the mouth with the pitiless bronze” (16.345; see [[idomeneus]] and [[pitiless-bronze]]), and the spear passes “straight through / below the brain, and split the white bones” (16.346–347). His teeth are shaken out and his eyes fill with blood, “and he spurted it up through his mouth and nostrils / as he gaped; and the black cloud of death enfolded him” (16.349–350). The black cloud of death is named only here in the Iliad. In the Odyssey Menelaus says that nothing would have parted him and Odysseus until it enfolded them (θανάτοιο μέλαν νέφος, Od. 4.178–180).

**Two men named Erymas.** Patroclus kills another Erymas seventy verses later, the first of nine men named in a row (16.415; see [[erymas-killed-by-patroclus]]). Walter Leaf remarks that the poem usually avoids repeating a name at so short an interval, even a minor one (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 16.411).
