Apollodorus tells that Polyphontes killed Cresphontes and two of his sons and took Merope as his wife; the third son, Aepytus, raised by his grandfather in Arcadia, returned and killed Polyphontes (2.8.5). Hyginus gives the version of the play, with Merope's axe raised over her sleeping son (*Fabulae* 137; see [[merope-and-her-son]] and [[cresphontes-play]]). Aristotle: “best of all is the last, as in the Cresphontes, where Merope is about to kill her son, and does not kill him, but recognizes him” (1454a4–7).
