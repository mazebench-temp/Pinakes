Socrates questioned his fellow citizens about justice and virtue and claimed to know only that he knew nothing; he was tried and put to death in 399 BCE. He is known from the dialogues of Plato and Xenophon and from Aristophanes' *Clouds*. In Plato's *Republic* Socrates banishes the imitative poets from the ideal city, and in the *Ion* he argues that the poet composes not by art but by divine inspiration (see [[plato]]). Aristotle names him only in the adjective: “the Socratic dialogues” (1447b11; see [[socratic-dialogues]]).
