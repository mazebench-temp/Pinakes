## Homer and the myth

The Odyssey tells the story in a few lines of its catalogue of heroines: his mother, Epicaste, married her own son unknowing, and he married her when he had slain his father; the gods made it known at once, and she hanged herself, while he went on ruling the Cadmeians in Thebes, suffering the sorrows that the Erinyes of a mother bring to pass (*Odyssey* 11.271–280; see [[jocasta]] and [[erinyes]]). Homer says nothing of the oracle, the exposure, the Sphinx or the blinding. The Iliad mentions the games held at Thebes “when Oedipus had fallen”, for his funeral (23.679–680). The fuller story is that of Sophocles: Laius, warned that his son would kill him, exposed the baby on Cithaeron with his ankles pinned; a shepherd gave him to the king of Corinth; grown, Oedipus fled Corinth to escape the oracle, killed Laius at a crossroads, solved the riddle of the Sphinx, and married the widowed queen; when the truth came out, Jocasta hanged herself and Oedipus blinded himself (see [[oedipus-the-king]], [[oedipus-kills-laius]] and [[the-discovery-of-oedipus]]). His sons Eteocles and Polynices killed each other at Thebes (see [[the-seven-against-thebes]]).
