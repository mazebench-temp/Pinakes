## Homer and the myth

The Odyssey tells the story in a few lines: Odysseus saw in the underworld “the mother of Oedipus, fair Epicaste, who did a monstrous thing in the ignorance of her mind, marrying her own son; he killed his father and married her, and the gods soon made it known to men”; she hanged herself, and he ruled on in Thebes in pain (*Odyssey* 11.271–280). The Iliad mentions the funeral games of “fallen Oedipus” at Thebes (23.679). The fuller story is that of Sophocles: Laius, warned that his son would kill him, exposed the baby on Cithaeron with his ankles pinned; a shepherd gave him to the king of Corinth; grown, Oedipus fled Corinth to escape the oracle, killed Laius at a crossroads, solved the riddle of the Sphinx, and married the widowed queen; when the truth came out, Jocasta hanged herself and Oedipus blinded himself (see [[oedipus-the-king]], [[oedipus-kills-laius]] and [[the-discovery-of-oedipus]]). His sons Eteocles and Polynices killed each other at Thebes (see [[the-seven-against-thebes]]).
