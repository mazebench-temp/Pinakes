## The name

Πεισίστρατος joins πείθω, “persuade”, and στρατός, “army”: “he who wins over the host”, a name of the same shape as Πεισήνωρ, “who wins over men” (see [[peisenor-the-herald]]).

## Family

He is a son of Nestor, Νεστορίδης Πεισίστρατος, “Peisistratus, son of Nestor” (*Odyssey* 3.36, 3.482), and the brother of Thrasymedes, beside whom he seats the strangers (*Odyssey* 3.39; see [[thrasymedes]]). Of the six sons at home he is the only one still unmarried, a young man in his father's halls (*Odyssey* 3.401), and he is of an age with Telemachus (*Odyssey* 3.49). He never saw his brother Antilochus, who died at Troy (*Odyssey* 4.199–202; see [[antilochus]]).

## Descriptions

- ὄρχαμος ἀνδρῶν, “leader of men” (*Odyssey* 3.400, 3.454, 3.482; see [[leader-of-men]]).
- ἐυμμελίης, “of the good ash spear” (*Odyssey* 3.400; see [[with-good-ash-spear]]).
- ἥρως, “the hero” (*Odyssey* 3.415, 15.131; see [[heros]]).

## Elsewhere in Homer

**At Sparta.** The two young men stand with their horses in the forecourt of Menelaus, “the hero Telemachus and the splendid son of Nestor” (*Odyssey* 4.20–22). Telemachus leans close to him and whispers his wonder at the bronze and gold of the palace (*Odyssey* 4.69–75). When Menelaus sees the likeness of Odysseus in the guest, Peisistratus answers for him: he is truly the son of that man, but he is modest and ashamed to speak rashly in Menelaus' presence, and the Gerenian horseman Nestor sent Peisistratus with him as his escort (*Odyssey* 4.155–167). At the memory of the dead he weeps for his own brother, whom the splendid son of shining Dawn killed, and says that he never met him or saw him (*Odyssey* 4.186–202); Menelaus answers that he has spoken as a wise man would, as befits the son of such a father (*Odyssey* 4.203–211; see [[nestor]]).

**The return.** In *Odyssey* 15 Athena finds the two asleep in Menelaus' forecourt; Telemachus wakes him with a touch of his foot, and he answers that they cannot drive through the dark night and should wait for the gifts of their host (*Odyssey* 15.4–8, 15.44–55). They drive back by the same road and spend the night again at Pherae with Diocles, in the verses of the outward journey (*Odyssey* 3.486–490, 15.184–188; see [[diocles]]). At Pylos Telemachus asks him not to take him to the house, “lest the old man keep me in his house against my will”, and Peisistratus drives him to the ship, stows Menelaus' gifts in the stern, and bids him embark before he himself reaches home and tells the old man (*Odyssey* 15.193–216). The renderings of books other than the third here are plain renderings, not the translation.

## Outside Homer

Apollodorus names him among the seven sons of Nestor by Anaxibia, daughter of Cratieus (*Library* 1.9.9). Herodotus says that the Peisistratids of Athens were by descent “Pylians and Neleids”, and that Hippocrates named his son Peisistratus, the future tyrant (see [[pisistratus]]), after Nestor's son (Herodotus 5.65.3–4; see [[herodotus]]).
