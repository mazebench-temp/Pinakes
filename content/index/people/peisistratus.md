## The name

Πεισίστρατος joins πείθω, “persuade”, and στρατός, “army”: “he who wins over the host”, a name of the same shape as Πεισήνωρ, “who wins over men” (see [[peisenor-the-herald]]).

## Family

He is a son of Nestor, Νεστορίδης Πεισίστρατος, “Peisistratus, son of Nestor” (*Odyssey* 3.36, 3.482), and the brother of Thrasymedes, beside whom he seats the strangers (*Odyssey* 3.39; see [[thrasymedes]]). Of the six sons at home he is the only one still unmarried, a young man in his father's halls (*Odyssey* 3.401), and he is of an age with Telemachus (*Odyssey* 3.49). He never saw his brother Antilochus, who died at Troy (*Odyssey* 4.199–202; see [[antilochus]]).

## Descriptions

- ὄρχαμος ἀνδρῶν, “leader of men” (*Odyssey* 3.400, 3.454, 3.482; see [[leader-of-men]]).
- ἐυμμελίης, “of the good ash spear” (*Odyssey* 3.400; see [[with-good-ash-spear]]).
- ἥρως, “the hero” (*Odyssey* 3.415, 15.131; see [[heros]]).

## Elsewhere in Homer

**At Sparta.** The two young men stand with their horses in the doorway of Menelaus' house, “the hero Telemachus and the splendid son of Nestor” (*Odyssey* 4.20–22). Telemachus leans close to him and whispers his wonder at the bronze and gold of the palace (*Odyssey* 4.69–75). When Menelaus sees the likeness of Odysseus in the guest, Peisistratus answers for him: he is truly the son of that man, but he is modest and ashamed to speak rashly in Menelaus' presence, and the Gerenian horseman Nestor sent Peisistratus with him as his escort (*Odyssey* 4.155–167). At the memory of the dead he weeps for his own brother, whom the splendid son of shining Dawn killed, and says that he never met him or saw him (*Odyssey* 4.186–202); Menelaus answers that he has spoken as a wise man would, as befits the son of such a father (*Odyssey* 4.203–211; see [[nestor]]).

**The return.** He drives Telemachus home from Sparta by the same road, with the gifts of Menelaus, and at Pylos, at his friend's request, leaves him at his ship instead of taking him to Nestor's house (*Odyssey* 15.1–216; see [[the-journey-of-telemachus]]).

## Outside Homer

Apollodorus names him among the seven sons of Nestor by Anaxibia, daughter of Cratieus (*Library* 1.9.9). Herodotus says that the Peisistratids of Athens were by descent “Pylians and Neleids”, and that Hippocrates named his son Peisistratus, the future tyrant (see [[pisistratus]]), after Nestor's son (Herodotus 5.65.3–4; see [[herodotus]]).
