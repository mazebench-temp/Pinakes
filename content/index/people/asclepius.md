## In Homer

ἰητῆρʼ ἀγαθώ, "good healers" (2.732), is said of the sons, and healing is the family's craft. Asclepius himself appears only as a father. Machaon is “the son of Asclepius, the noble healer” (4.194, 11.518), and he heals with “soothing drugs, which Chiron once gave to his father in friendship” (4.218–219). [[Chiron]], “the most just of the Centaurs”, also taught Achilles medicine (11.831–832). Homer never makes Asclepius a god, and never names his parents.

## Outside Homer

Later poets gave him a divine father. In the Homeric *Hymn to Asclepius* and in Pindar he is the son of Apollo by Coronis, daughter of Phlegyas. In Pindar's version Coronis was unfaithful, and Apollo's sister Artemis killed her, but Apollo snatched the child from his mother's body on the pyre and gave him to Chiron to raise. Asclepius became so skilled that he brought a man back from the dead for gold, and Zeus struck them both down with the thunderbolt (*Pythian* 3.8–58). In Euripides' *Alcestis* this is why Apollo, in revenge, killed the Cyclopes and was sent to serve [[Admetus]] (1–9).

From the fifth century BC Asclepius was worshipped as a god all over the Greek world. His great sanctuaries were at Epidaurus; at Cos, where the Hippocratic physicians called themselves his descendants; at Pergamum; and at [[Tricca]], which claimed the oldest. His cult came to Athens in 420/19 BC, and the dying Socrates asks Crito to pay the cock "we owe to Asclepius" (Plato, *Phaedo* 118a). Early in the third century BC the Romans brought him to Rome as Aesculapius.
