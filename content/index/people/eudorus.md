**The second company.** Eudorus leads the second of the five ranks into which Achilles divides the Myrmidons (16.168–172, 16.179; see [[myrmidons]]). He is ἀρήϊος, “warlike”, the epithet of Peisander in the next entry of the catalogue (16.193; see [[warlike]]), and he is “surpassingly swift at running, and a fighter” (πέρι μὲν θείειν ταχὺν ἠδὲ μαχητήν, 16.186). Speed of foot and courage in battle are the praise of a young fighter: Menelaus tells Antilochus that no Achaean is younger than he, “nor swifter of foot, nor as valiant as you in fighting” (15.569–570; see [[antilochus]]). Eudorus takes no further part in the poem and is not named again.

**A maiden's son.** παρθένιος, “a maiden's son” (16.180), occurs only here in Homer: the child of an unmarried girl. Hermes saw Polymele among the girls dancing in the chorus of Artemis, went up to her room and lay with her in secret, and she bore a son; Eileithyia brought him into the light; Echecles, son of Actor, then married her, and the boy was reared by her father, the old man Phylas, “loving him as if he were his own son” (16.180–192; see [[polymele]], [[phylas]], [[echecles]], [[eileithyia]] and [[the-birth-of-eudorus]]). Bucolion, the eldest son of Laomedon, was born of another hidden union, σκότιος, “in secret” (6.24; see [[bucolion]]).

**The name.** Εὔδωρος joins εὖ, “well”, and δῶρον, “gift”. Hermes “gave” Polymele her son (πόρεν, 16.185), and in the Odyssey he is addressed as δῶτορ ἐάων, “giver of good things” (*Odyssey* 8.335; see [[hermes]]). The poem does not comment on the name.

## Outside Homer

Walter Leaf, who thinks the catalogue of the Myrmidon leaders a later addition, notes that Menesthius, Eudorus and Peisander, for all the ceremony with which they are introduced, are never named again (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 16.168), and counts Eudorus, with Menesthius, among the few sons of gods at Troy whom Hera could mean when she warns Zeus that “many are fighting around the great city of Priam, / sons of immortals” (16.448–449; on 16.445; see [[the-death-of-sarpedon]]).
