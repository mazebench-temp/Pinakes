He is named with Antenor, the two of them "both wise men" (πεπνυμένω ἄμφω, 3.148; see [[wise]] and [[trojan-elders]]). The name looks like οὐκ ἀλέγων, "not caring, heedless", an odd name for a man of good sense. Homer names him only here.

## Outside Homer

Aeneas, telling how Troy fell, sees the fire reach the house next to his: "already Ucalegon next door is burning" (*iam proximus ardet / Ucalegon*, *Aeneid* 2.311–312). Juvenal borrowed the name for the neighbor in a Roman tenement who is already calling for water and carrying out his few belongings while the fire climbs toward the attic (*Satires* 3.198–201).
