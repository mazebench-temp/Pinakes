Λυκάων recalls λύκος, "wolf". In Book 3 he is present only through his corselet (θώρηξ). Paris came out as an archer in a leopard's skin (3.17), and for the duel he arms as a spearman, in a corselet borrowed from his brother, and "fitted it to himself" (ἥρμοσε δʼ αὐτῷ). Menelaus' spear goes through it and cuts the tunic beneath, and Paris escapes death by swerving (3.357–360). See [[corselet]] and [[arming-scene]].

## Elsewhere in Homer

His own story is told in Book 21. Achilles had once caught him by night in Priam's orchard, cutting fig branches for the rails of a chariot, and sold him in Lemnos. A guest-friend ransomed him and he escaped home, and on the twelfth day after his return he met Achilles at the river. He clasped Achilles' knees and begged for his life, and Achilles killed him (21.34–135). His mother was Laothoe, daughter of Altes, king of the Leleges (21.84–91, 22.46–48). Apollo takes his likeness to rouse Aeneas against Achilles (20.81–82). The silver mixing bowl that Achilles sets out as a prize at Patroclus' games had been paid by Euneus of Lemnos as the price of Lycaon (23.740–747).
