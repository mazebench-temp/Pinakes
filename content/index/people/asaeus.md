Asaeus heads the list with which the narrator answers his own question, “Then whom first, whom last did he slay, / Hector, son of Priam, when Zeus gave him glory?” (11.299–300; see [[whom-first-whom-last]]). Zeus has just given Hector the power he promised for the moment when Agamemnon was wounded and left the battle (11.191–194; see [[hector]] and [[plan-of-zeus]]). Nine names fill three verses, and then the multitude falls, like clouds and spray under the blast of the West Wind (11.304–309).

None of the nine men appears anywhere else in Homer, though four of the names belong to other men too (see [[autonous]], [[dolops]], [[opheltius-the-achaean]] and [[agelaus-the-achaean]]), and none is in the Catalogue of Ships. ἡγεμόνες, “leaders”, need not command a whole contingent: the Myrmidons have five leaders under Achilles (16.171; see [[hegemon]]).
