Αἰολίδης, “son of Aeolus”, stands beside Sisyphus' name as a title (see [[sisyphus]]). The Odyssey gives the same patronymic to Cretheus, the husband of Tyro (Od. 11.237), whom later genealogy makes another son of this Aeolus. The name looks like the adjective αἰόλος, “quick-moving, shifting”. Homer's other Aeolus, the son of Hippotes who keeps the winds on his floating island and gives Odysseus the skin of the winds, is a different figure (*Odyssey* 10.1–76; see [[aeolus]]).

## Outside Homer

In the Hesiodic *Catalogue of Women* Hellen's sons are Dorus, Xuthus and Aeolus, and the sons of Aeolus, “kings who dispensed justice”, are Cretheus, Athamas, Sisyphus, Salmoneus and Perieres (fr. 4 Evelyn-White). [[apollodorus|Apollodorus]] says that he ruled the country around Thessaly and called its people Aeolians after himself (*Library* 1.7.3).
