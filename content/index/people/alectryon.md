υἱὸν Ἀλεκτρυόνος μεγαθύμου, “the son of great-hearted Alectryon” (17.602): Alectryon is named only to name his son. Leitus is one of the five leaders of the Boeotians in the Catalogue, where he has no father (2.494; see [[leitus]] and [[boeotians]]); the patronymic comes fifteen books later, in the verse in which Hector stabs him on the wrist and puts him out of the battle (17.601–604; see [[hector]]). μεγάθυμος, “great-hearted”, is the title the same book gives to the fathers of Schedius and Antilochus, Iphitus and Nestor (17.306, 17.653; see [[great-hearted]]). Homer tells nothing else of him.

The name is the word that later Greek uses for the cock, ἀλεκτρυών. The bird does not appear in Homer.

## Outside Homer

Apollodorus calls Leitus' father Alector, Ἀλέκτωρ, in his lists of the Argonauts and of the suitors of Helen: Λήιτος Ἀλέκτορος, “Leitus, son of Alector” (*Library* 1.9.16, 3.10.8; see [[apollodorus]]). The Odyssey's Alector is another man, whose daughter Menelaus brought from Sparta as a wife for his son Megapenthes (*Odyssey* 4.10–12).
