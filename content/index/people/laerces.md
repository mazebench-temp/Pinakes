Λαέρκεος υἱὸς ἀμύμων, “the noble son of Laerces” (16.197): Laerces is named to name his son Alcimedon, the leader of the fifth company of the Myrmidons (see [[alcimedon]] and [[myrmidons]]). He is himself a son of Haemon, the third man of that name in the Iliad, after Nestor's captain and the father of the Theban Maeon (17.467; 4.296, 4.394; see [[haemon-father-of-laerces]] and [[haemon]]). Homer tells nothing else of him.

The Odyssey has another Laerces, the goldsmith of Pylos whom Nestor sends for to gild the horns of the heifer he sacrifices to Athena (*Odyssey* 3.425–438).
