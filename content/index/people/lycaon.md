Pandarus is regularly "the son of Lycaon". In Book 5 he tells Aeneas how "old Lycaon the spearman" urged him, as he left home, to take his horses and chariots to Troy. He did not listen: he left the horses in their stalls, to spare them short rations in a city under siege, and came on foot with his bow (5.192–205). The name recalls λύκος, "wolf". A different Lycaon, a son of Priam, is killed by Achilles in the river in Book 21 (21.34–135).
