“Whom then did noble Teucer kill first of the Trojans? Orsilochus first, and Ormenus and Ophelestes” (8.273–274). He heads the list of eight men, otherwise unknown, whom Teucer brings down with his arrows while Ajax covers him with his shield (8.266–277; see [[teucer]] and [[ajax]]). Nothing is told of him but his name and his death.

The name, which joins λόχος, “armed band, ambush”, to a verb of rousing, is borne by an Achaean twin whom Aeneas kills (5.541–560; see [[orsilochus-son-of-diocles]]), and in one of his lying tales Odysseus claims to have killed Orsilochus, a swift-footed son of Idomeneus, in Crete (Od. 13.259–261).
