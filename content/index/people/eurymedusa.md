## Descriptions

- γρῆυς Ἀπειραίη, “an old woman from Apeire” (*Odyssey* 7.8; see [[apeire]]). The scholia explain Ἀπειραίη as ἄποθεν παροῦσα, “come from afar”, or Ἠπειρωτική, “from the mainland” or “from Epirus”, but W. W. Merry takes Apeire, like Hypereia, the old home of the Phaeacians, for a land of legend, one “of which the limits are unknown” (*Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on *Odyssey* 7.8; see [[hypereia-of-the-phaeacians]]).
- θαλαμηπόλος, “chamber-woman” (*Odyssey* 7.8), the woman who keeps her lady's θάλαμος, “chamber”. Homer has the word once more, of Eurynome, Penelope's chamber-woman, who lights Odysseus and Penelope to their bed with a torch (*Odyssey* 23.293–294).

## A prize for the king

She was not born in the house. “Whom once the curving ships had brought from Apeire; / and they had picked her out as a prize for Alcinous, because over all / the Phaeacians he was lord, and the people listened to him as to a god” (*Odyssey* 7.9–11; see [[curving]]). γέρας ἔξελον, “picked out as a prize”, is the phrase of the Iliad for a woman chosen for a leader before the spoil is shared, as the Achaeans picked out Briseis for Achilles (*Iliad* 16.56 = 18.444) and Hecamede for Nestor, “because he was the best of all in counsel” (*Iliad* 11.626–627; see [[geras]], [[hecamede]] and [[division-of-spoils]]). αὐτήν is emphatic, “her”, from among all the other captives (Merry on *Odyssey* 7.10). Hecamede was chosen for her master's counsel, Eurymedusa for her master's rule.
