## Etymology

Ἀχιλλεύς (Achilleus), also spelled with one λ (Ἀχιλεύς) when the meter needs a short syllable (1.199, 1.74). The name's origin is unknown. A much-discussed modern proposal derives it from ἄχος (grief) and λαός (the people or the fighting men): "he whose people have grief". That is exactly what the proem says his wrath brought the Achaeans.

## The wrath

The poem's first word, μῆνις, belongs to Achilles, although in Book 1 it is used first of Apollo (1.75) and its verb of Agamemnon (1.247). Achilles' anger is a response to dishonor (ἀτιμία). What has been taken from him is his γέρας, the prize that makes his worth visible. See [[menis]], [[time]], [[geras]] and [[honor-and-dishonor]].

## Mythology

Achilles' past in Book 1 is war: the sack of Thebe (1.366–369) and the raiding of many Trojan towns (1.163–168). See [[sack-of-thebe]] and [[achilles-raids]]. His future is a short life, which Thetis knows:

## Elsewhere in Homer

Later books of the Iliad tell more. Phoenix raised him (9.485–495) and Chiron taught him medicine (11.831–832). His mother has told him of two fates, a long life at home without fame or a short one at Troy whose fame will be imperishable (9.410–416; see [[the-two-fates-of-achilles]]). He dies at the Scaean gates, killed by Paris and Apollo, as the dying Hector foretells (22.359–360).

## Outside Homer

The *Aethiopis* told of his death and of Thetis carrying him to the White Island. Alexander the Great sacrificed at his tomb at Troy (Arrian, *Anabasis* 1.12.1).
