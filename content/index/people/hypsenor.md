**The priest's son.** “Brilliant Hypsenor” (Ὑψήνορα δῖον) is the son of Dolopion, “who had been made priest of the Scamander and was honored by the people like a god” (5.77–78; see [[dolopion]]). He is the second priest's son to die in the book. Hephaestus saved one of the two sons of his priest Dares (5.9–24; see [[dares]]), but no god saves Hypsenor.

**The sentence.** The narrator names the killer and the victim (5.76), turns aside to the victim's father (5.77–78), and then begins again: τὸν μὲν ἄρʼ Εὐρύπυλος, Εὐαίμονος ἀγλαὸς υἱός, “him Eurypylus, the splendid son of Euaemon” (5.79). The translation keeps the broken construction.

**The sword.** Eurypylus runs after him as he flees, darts at him with his sword (φάσγανον), strikes the shoulder and shears off the arm; “the bloody arm fell to the plain” (5.82). Then comes, for the first time in the poem, the verse “and his eyes were seized by purple death and mighty fate” (5.82–83). It is used twice more, each time after a sword stroke (16.334, 20.477; see [[purple-death]]). See [[eurypylus]] and [[androktasia]].

**The name.** Ὑψήνωρ seems to join ὕψι, “on high”, with the -ήνωρ of names like Agenor and Elephenor, from ἀνήρ, “man”. Another Hypsenor, son of Hippasus, is an Achaean whom Deiphobus kills in Book 13 with a spear meant for Idomeneus (13.402–412).
