He dies with his companion in the chariot, and nothing more is told of either (see [[menesthes]]). His name is the adjective ἀγχίαλος, “near the sea” (ἄγχι “near” + ἅλς “sea”), which the Catalogue gives to coastal towns: “Chalcis by the sea” (2.640) and “Antron by the sea” (2.697; see [[by-the-sea]]). Here the text prints it as a name.

Two men in the Odyssey have the name: the father of Mentes, the Taphian king in whose likeness Athena visits Telemachus (Od. 1.180–181), and a young Phaeacian at the games (Od. 8.112).
