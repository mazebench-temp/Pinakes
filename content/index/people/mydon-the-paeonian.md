**The second of seven.** When Asteropaeus, “their best man”, has fallen, the Paeonians flee along the river, and Achilles goes after them and kills seven: Thersilochus, Mydon, Astypylus, Mnesus, Thrasius, Aenius and Ophelestes (21.205–210; see [[asteropaeus]], [[paeonians]] and [[achilles]]). Μύδωνά τε, “and Mydon”, is the second name. The seven are given no father, no wound and no word; the list is cut off by the river, which speaks to Achilles before he can kill more (21.211–213; see [[would-have-had-not]] and [[scamander]]).

**Another Mydon.** The name has been heard once before. Mydon, son of Atymnius, the charioteer of the Paphlagonian Pylaemenes, was struck on the elbow by Antilochus' stone, dropped the reins, and fell headfirst from the chariot into deep sand (5.580–589; see [[mydon]] and [[antilochus]]). The poem does not connect the Paeonian with him.
