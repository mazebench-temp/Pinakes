He and his brother are caught together in one chariot, beg together, and are refused together, because their father had urged the Trojans to kill Menelaus when he came as an envoy (11.126–142; see [[sons-of-antimachus]] and [[antimachus]]). When Peisander falls, Hippolochus leaps down, and Agamemnon kills him on the ground:

{{quote:5b62016e-d79e-5a4d-91cd-606e8d85c3d3}}

ὅλμος, found only here in the Iliad, is a round stone or log, a roller: the trunk, without arms or head, rolls through the crowd like a cylinder (see the translation's note on 11.147 and [[like-a-log]]). Agamemnon will cut off Coon's head too (11.261; see [[coon]]). The narrator then turns to the rout, foot soldiers killing foot soldiers and horsemen horsemen (11.148–154). μενεχάρμης, “steadfast in battle” (11.122), is given also to the Achaean Hipponous whom Hector kills later in the book (11.303).

The name joins ἵππος, “horse”, to λόχος, “ambush, company”. The Lycian Hippolochus, Bellerophon's son, is another man (6.197, 6.206; see [[hippolochus]]).
