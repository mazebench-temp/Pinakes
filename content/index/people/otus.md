The first of Dione's examples of gods who suffered at the hands of men (see [[dione]] and [[the-aloadae-bind-ares]]). The Iliad names Otus only here, with his brother, and calls the pair “the sons of Aloeus” (παῖδες Ἀλωῆος, 5.386; see [[aloeus]]); later writers called them the Aloadae. It does not say why they bound Ares. Another Otus, of Cyllene, a companion of Meges and a leader of the Epeians, is killed by Polydamas at 15.518–519.

## Elsewhere in Homer

In the underworld Odysseus sees their mother, Iphimedeia, the wife of Aloeus, who said that she had lain with Poseidon. Her two sons were the tallest men the earth ever reared, and the most beautiful after Orion: at nine years old they were nine cubits across and nine fathoms tall. They threatened the gods with war and meant to pile Ossa on Olympus and Pelion on Ossa to climb the sky, but Apollo killed them before their beards grew (Od. 11.305–320).

## Outside Homer

In [[apollodorus|Apollodorus]] they also woo goddesses, Ephialtes Hera and Otus Artemis, and they bind Ares, whom Hermes steals away. Artemis kills them on Naxos: she leaps between them as a deer, and aiming at the deer they spear each other (*Library* 1.7.4). In Virgil the Sibyl tells Aeneas that she saw the twin sons of Aloeus in Tartarus (*Aeneid* 6.582–584).
