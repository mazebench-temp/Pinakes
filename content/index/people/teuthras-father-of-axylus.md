Τευθρανίδης = Τεύθρας + the patronymic suffix -ίδης: “son of Teuthras”. The narrator names the father as he names the victim, in the first line of the killing (6.12–13; see [[androktasia]]), and says nothing more of him. The same name belongs to an Achaean, “godlike Teuthras”, whom Hector and Ares kill in Book 5 (5.705; see [[teuthras]]).
