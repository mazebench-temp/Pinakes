## Family

Her father was “great-hearted Eurymedon, / who once was king over the high-hearted Giants. / But he destroyed his reckless people, and perished himself” (*Odyssey* 7.58–60; see [[eurymedon-king-of-the-giants]] and [[giants]]). Poseidon lay with her and begot Nausithous, who was lord among the Phaeacians (*Odyssey* 7.61–62; see [[poseidon]] and [[nausithous]]), so that she is the grandmother of Alcinous and of his brother Rhexenor, and both the king and the queen, Rhexenor's daughter, descend from her (*Odyssey* 7.63–66; see [[alcinous]], [[rhexenor]] and [[arete-queen-of-the-phaeacians]]).

## Descriptions

- γυναικῶν εἶδος ἀρίστη, “most beautiful in form of women” (*Odyssey* 7.57), the praise the Iliad gives to the loveliest of a house's daughters, as to Alcestis, “most beautiful in form of the daughters of Pelias” (*Iliad* 2.715; see [[best-in-looks]]).
- ὁπλοτάτη θυγάτηρ, “the youngest daughter” (*Odyssey* 7.58), as Polycaste is “the youngest daughter of Nestor” (*Odyssey* 3.465; see [[polycaste]]).

The Iliad's Periboea is another woman, the eldest of the daughters of Acessamenus, who bore Pelegon to the river Axius (*Iliad* 21.141–143; see [[periboea]]). The two verses begin with the same words, γείνατο καὶ Περίβοια, with the god and the woman together the subject of the verb: “and him the Axius of the broad streams / begot, and Periboea” (*Iliad* 21.141–142), as “Nausithous first was born to Poseidon the earth-shaker / and Periboea” (*Odyssey* 7.56–57). The one is the eldest daughter and the other the youngest, and each bears a god's son who heads a line of kings and heroes.
