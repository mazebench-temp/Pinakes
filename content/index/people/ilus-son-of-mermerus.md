## Family

Son of Mermerus: ἐξ Ἐφύρης ἀνιόντα παρʼ Ἴλου Μερμερίδαο, “on his way back from Ephyra, from Ilus, son of Mermerus” (*Odyssey* 1.259). The patronymic is all that Homer tells of the father (see [[mermerus-father-of-ilus]]). He is not the Trojan Ilus, son of Tros, whose grave mound stands in the plain of Troy (see [[ilus]] and the translation's note on 1.259).

## Ephyra

Homer names his town, Ephyra, but not his people, and which Ephyra is meant was disputed (see [[ephyra]]). Merry (*Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 1.259) reports Nitzsch's case for the town in Thesprotia, later called Cichyrus: Odysseus touched at Taphos on his way back (ἀνιόντα), and Taphos lies on the straight line from Thesprotia to Ithaca; but, Merry objects, a ship rounding the Leucadian promontory would avoid Taphos altogether. He sets beside it the case for the old town on the river Selleis in Elis. If the Taphian islands are placed among the Echinades, Taphos would lie between that Ephyra and Ithaca; Agamede, the daughter of Augeas, king of Elis, “knew all the drugs that the wide earth nourishes” (*Iliad* 11.740–741); and the suitors, who fear that Telemachus will fetch “life-destroying drugs” from Ephyra, name it with Pylos and Sparta, as if all three were in the Peloponnese (*Odyssey* 2.326–330). Strabo had already placed the Ephyra “from which come the man-slaying drugs” in Elis, citing both passages of the Odyssey and Agamede (*Geography* 8.3.5; see [[strabo]], [[agamede]] and [[elis]]). The renderings of the second book are plain renderings, not the translation.

## Outside Homer

Merry reports a scholion, resting on the authority of Apollodorus, that made Ilus son of Mermerus a great-grandson of Jason and Medea and a king of Thesprotia, and a story in Eustathius that had Medea live for a while in Elis; he takes each to have been invented, or taken up, by the partisans of one Ephyra or the other (on 1.259; see [[medea]] and [[mermerus-father-of-ilus]]).
