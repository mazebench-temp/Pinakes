## Family

Antiope, the daughter of Asopus, “claimed that she had slept even in the arms of Zeus, / and she bore two sons, Amphion and Zethus” (*Odyssey* 11.261–262; see [[antiope]], [[zeus]] and [[zethus]]). The two are named together and act together: “who first founded the seat of seven-gated Thebes / and fenced it with towers” (*Odyssey* 11.263–264; see [[thebes]]).

Homer has two other men of the name: Amphion, son of Iasus, king of Minyan Orchomenus and grandfather of Nestor (*Odyssey* 11.283–284; see [[amphion-son-of-iasus]]), and Amphion, a leader of the Epeians at the ships (*Iliad* 13.692; see [[amphion-the-epeian]]).

## Outside Homer

**The walls and the lyre.** Palaephatus reports that “Hesiod and others” told how Zethus and Amphion walled Thebes with the lyre (Palaephatus 42, printed as fr. 96 Evelyn-White among the fragments of the *Catalogue of Women*; see [[hesiod]] and [[lyre]]). Euripides' Antigone, looking out from the walls, asks whether the gates are fast in “the stone works of Amphion's wall” (*Phoenician Women* 114–116), and his Amphitryon tells the old Cadmeian story that Lycus ruled the city before “the two of the white horses, Amphion and Zethus, the offspring of Zeus”, became its lords (*Heracles* 26–30; see [[euripides]]). In his lost *Antiope* Amphion was the musician and his brother the man of action: Plato's Callicles casts himself as Zethus rebuking Socrates as Amphion for neglecting the business of life, and Socrates regrets that he could not pay him back “with Amphion's speech for Zethus'” (*Gorgias* 485e–486c, 506b; see [[plato]]).

**The founding of Thebes.** W. W. Merry notes that the Odyssey's account, in which the brothers are the first builders and fortifiers of Thebes, is older than the common story of its foundation by Cadmus. The scholia reconcile the two by having the city sacked after the brothers' death, so that Cadmus had to found it anew; Apollodorus puts Cadmus first and has the brothers build the lower city at the foot of the Cadmeia (*Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on *Odyssey* 11.260; see [[cadmus]]). Strabo reports that they lived at Eutresis, a village of the Thespians, before they ruled Thebes (*Geography* 9.2.28; see [[strabo]]).

**Niobe.** Later legend made him the husband of Niobe: Strabo says that Pelops gave his sister Niobe to Amphion (*Geography* 8.4.4), and Hesiod gave the couple ten sons and ten daughters, as later writers report (fr. 23 Evelyn-White; see [[niobe]] and [[the-children-of-niobe]]). Merry adds that a later form of the story identified him with Amphion son of Iasus, making Chloris the daughter of Amphion and Niobe and the one child whom the gods spared, and he quotes Grote's remark that the logographers here, against their habit, blended two persons whom the Odyssey keeps distinct (on *Odyssey* 11.260, 11.283; see [[chloris]]).
