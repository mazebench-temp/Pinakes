καὶ ἀντίθεον Λυκοφόντην, “and godlike Lycophontes” (8.275), is the only one of Teucer's eight victims given an epithet (8.273–277; see [[teucer]] and [[godlike]]).

The name joins λύκος, “wolf”, to -φόντης, “slayer”, the ending of ἀνδρειφόντης, “slayer of men”, which is said of Enyalius a few lines before (8.264; see [[enyalius]]), and of the names Bellerophontes and Polyphontes (6.155, 4.395): “wolf-slayer”.
