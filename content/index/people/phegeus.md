{{quote:24641446-719a-5759-a044-823c927020c5}}

He and his brother Idaeus are “well skilled in every kind of fighting” (5.11), the line that is given later in the book to the twin sons of Diocles, who also die together (5.549). The brothers drive at Diomedes, who is on foot (5.12–13). Phegeus throws first and misses over the left shoulder; Diomedes' spear strikes him “on the chest between the nipples” (μεταμάζιον, a word found only here in Homer) and throws him from the chariot (5.15–19). Idaeus does not stand over the body, and Hephaestus hides him in night (5.20–24; see [[idaeus-son-of-dares]]). Diomedes drives off their horses and gives them to his companions to take to the ships (5.25–26).

He is the first of the book's many victims whose father is named and left to grieve (see [[dares]] and [[androktasia]]). The name recalls φηγός, “oak”, the tree of Zeus under which Sarpedon is laid later in the book (5.693; see [[the-oak-of-zeus]]).
