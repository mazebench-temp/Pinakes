Κλυτομήδεα Ἤνοπος υἱόν, “Clytomedes, son of Enops” (23.634), is all that the poem tells of him (see [[clytomedes]] and [[the-funeral-games-of-amarynceus]]). His son boxed against the young Nestor at Buprasium; Nestor does not say whether he was an Epeian, a Pylian or an Aetolian (23.632–633).

Homer has two other men of the name, both Trojans: the herdsman Enops, to whom a Naiad bore Satnius by the river Satnioeis (14.444–445; see [[enops]]), and the father of Thestor, whom Patroclus kills (16.401; see [[enops-father-of-thestor]]). The name has the form of the adjective ἦνοψ, “gleaming”, said of bronze (16.408, 18.349).
