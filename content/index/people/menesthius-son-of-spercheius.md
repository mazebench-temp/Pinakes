**The first company.** When Achilles arms the Myrmidons and sends them out with Patroclus, the narrator counts them: fifty ships, fifty men in each, and five leaders, each over a rank (στιχός). Menesthius leads the first (16.168–173; see [[myrmidons]]). He is αἰολοθώρηξ, “of the flashing corselet”, an epithet that Homer gives to one other warrior only, Antiphus, son of Priam (4.489; see [[of-the-flashing-corselet]]). He does nothing more in the poem and is not named again.

**Two fathers.** The six verses give him a birth instead of deeds. His mother Polydora, a daughter of Peleus, bore him to the river Spercheius, “a woman who lay with a god”, but “by name”, ἐπίκλησιν, he was the son of Borus, son of Perieres, who married her openly after giving a boundless bride-price (16.175–178; see [[spercheius]], [[polydora]], [[borus-son-of-perieres]] and [[the-birth-of-menesthius]]). Eudorus, who leads the second company, has a god for a father as well, Hermes, and a mortal house that reared him (16.179–192; see [[eudorus]]). If the Peleus of 16.175 is Achilles' father, Menesthius is Achilles' nephew, the son of his sister, but the poem draws no such connection, and Achilles never speaks of him.

**Two of the name.** Μενέσθιος, like Menestheus, looks formed from μένος, “might”. The other Menesthius of the Iliad is an Achaean of Arne in Boeotia, son of Areithous the mace-man and of Phylomedusa, whom Paris kills in Book 7 (7.8–10; see [[menesthius]] and [[areithous]]).

## Outside Homer

Strabo, arguing that the Spercheius ran through Achilles' kingdom, cites Menesthius, “one of his captains”, who “is called the son of the Spercheius and of Achilles' sister” (*Geography* 9.5.9). Walter Leaf reports a scholium on 16.175 which says that some held this Peleus to be only a namesake of Achilles' father, since the kinship is not mentioned, and that Zenodotus read the mother's name as Cleodora (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 16.175). Apollodorus makes Polydora the daughter of Peleus by Antigone, daughter of Eurytion, whom Peleus married when he fled to Phthia, and the wife of Borus, son of Perieres (*Library* 3.13.1; see [[polydora]]).
