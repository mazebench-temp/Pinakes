summary: Athenian politician (d. 404 BCE), a lyre-maker by trade, the leading man of the people after 410, who twice persuaded Athens to refuse peace with Sparta and was put to death in the last months of the war.
aliases: [Cleophon the lyre-maker]

The *Constitution of the Athenians* names him as the leader of the people after the fall of the Four Hundred, “Cleophon the lyre-maker”, who introduced the two-obol payment (28.3), and blames him for the rejection of Sparta's offer of peace after Arginusae (34.1). He was put to death in 404 BCE on a charge brought under the oligarchs' pressure (Lysias 13.12, 30.10–13). Comedy mocked him as a foreigner: in Aristophanes' *Frogs* a Thracian swallow chatters on his lips (674–685), and a play of Plato the comic poet was named after him (see [[plato-the-comic-poet]]). He is not the tragic poet Cleophon of the *Poetics* (see [[cleophon]]).
