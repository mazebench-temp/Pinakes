Itymoneus is the first man Nestor names in the story of his war with the Epeians, and the first he kills in it. The quarrel arose “over a cattle raid” (ἀμφὶ βοηλασίῃ, 11.672), and Nestor was “driving off cattle in reprisal” (ῥύσιʼ ἐλαυνόμενος, 11.674), taking by force what the Epeians owed (see [[raiding]] and [[nestor-and-the-epeians]]). Itymoneus stood “among the foremost”, “defending his cows” (ᾗσι βόεσσιν, 11.674), and fell to a javelin from Nestor's hand; “the country people around him fled in fear” (11.675–676). He has cattle of his own and country people around him, and he is named nowhere else.

His patronymic, Ὑπειροχίδης, “son of Hypeirochus”, gives his father the name of a Trojan whom Odysseus kills earlier in the book (11.335; see [[hypeirochus]]), but nothing joins the two. The name means “excelling”, the word of Peleus' charge to Achilles, “to excel above the others” (ὑπείροχον ἔμμεναι ἄλλων, 11.784).
