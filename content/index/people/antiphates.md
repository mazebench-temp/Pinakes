After spearing Hippomachus, Leonteus draws his sharp sword from its sheath, darts through the throng and strikes Antiphates “hand to hand”, and he is flung on his back on the ground (12.190–192; see [[leonteus]], [[hippomachus]] and [[sword]]). ὃ δʼ ὕπτιος οὔδει ἐρείσθη, “and he was flung on his back on the ground”, is the half-verse of the falls of Areithous (7.145) and of Peisander, son of Antimachus (11.144). He is the second of Leonteus' five victims, and the poem names him nowhere else.

The Odyssey has two other men of the name: Antiphates, king of the Laestrygonians, who seizes one of Odysseus' men for his meal (Od. 10.106–132), and Antiphates, son of Melampus, grandfather of the seer Amphiaraus (Od. 15.242–244).
