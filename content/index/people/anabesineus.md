## The name

The name seems to be made from ἀναβαίνω, “go up, go on board”, and νηῦς, “ship”. ἀναβαίνω is the verb of embarking: the men who take Chryseis home “embarked and sailed over the watery ways” (οἳ μὲν ἔπειτʼ ἀναβάντες ἐπέπλεον ὑγρὰ κέλευθα, *Iliad* 1.312). W. W. Merry counts the Phaeacian names among those “taken from circumstances of seafaring life” (*Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on *Odyssey* 8.111); the word for ship also ends the names of Acroneus and Polyneus (see [[acroneus]] and [[polyneus]]).
