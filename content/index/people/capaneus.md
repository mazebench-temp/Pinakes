ἀγακλειτός "famous, glorious" = ἀγα- (very) + κλειτός (renowned). Homer names him only as the father of [[Sthenelus]] (2.564, 4.367, 5.109, 5.319). In Book 4 Sthenelus says that the fathers who attacked Thebes “perished through their own recklessness” (4.409), while their sons took the city. Homer does not tell how Capaneus died.

## Outside Homer

Aeschylus sets him at the Electran gate of Thebes, boasting that he will sack the city whether the gods will or no (*Seven against Thebes* 422–451; see [[aeschylus-seven]]). In Euripides a thunderbolt of Zeus strikes him at the top of his scaling ladder (*Phoenician Women* 1172–1186), and his wife Evadne throws herself onto his pyre (*Suppliants* 980–1071). Dante places him among the blasphemers, still defiant under the rain of fire (*Inferno* 14.43–72).
