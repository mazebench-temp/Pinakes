**Named once.** Ἀκεσσαμενοῖο θυγατρῶν / πρεσβυτάτη, “of the daughters of Acessamenus / the eldest” (21.142–143): Acessamenus is named only to place Periboea, the mother of Pelegon, among his daughters, in the narrator's account of the lineage of Asteropaeus (see [[periboea]], [[pelegon]], [[axius]] and [[asteropaeus]]). The poem says nothing of his country or his rank, and Asteropaeus, telling his lineage to Achilles, does not name him (21.157–160). Walter Leaf notes that the name does not recur in Homer (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 21.141).

**The name.** Ἀκεσσαμενός seems to be formed from ἀκέομαι, “heal, mend”, whose aorist participle it resembles (see [[healing]]).
