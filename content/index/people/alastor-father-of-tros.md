Τρῶα δʼ Ἀλαστορίδην, “And Tros, son of Alastor” (20.463): Alastor is named only in his son's patronymic, at the start of the one supplication of Achilles' rampage (20.463–472; see [[tros-son-of-alastor]], [[supplication]] and [[achilles]]). The poem says nothing else of him.

The Iliad has three other men of the name: one of the five captains around whom Nestor marshals the Pylians (4.295; see [[alastor]]), a Lycian whom Odysseus kills (5.677; see [[alastor-of-lycia]]), and “brilliant Alastor”, who with Mecisteus carries the wounded Teucer, and later Hypsenor, to the ships (8.333, 13.422; see [[alastor-who-carries-teucer]]). On the name, ἀλάστωρ, later the avenging spirit of tragedy, see [[alastor]].
