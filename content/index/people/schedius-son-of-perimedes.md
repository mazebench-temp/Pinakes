ἔνθʼ Ἕκτωρ μὲν ἕλε Σχεδίον Περιμήδεος υἱὸν / ἀρχὸν Φωκήων, “Then Hector killed Schedius, son of Perimedes, / leader of the Phocians” (15.515–516; see [[hector]], [[perimedes]] and [[phocians]]). Hector and Ajax have each urged on their men, Hector telling the Trojans that a man who dies for his native land leaves his wife and children safe, and Ajax that it is better to perish once and for all than to be slowly drained away by worse men (15.486–513; see [[exhortation]], [[it-is-not-unseemly-to-die-for-ones-country]] and [[better-to-perish-once-and-for-all]]). Three leaders then fall in three verses: Hector kills Schedius, Ajax kills Laodamas, son of Antenor, “leader of the foot soldiers”, and Polydamas kills Otus of Cyllene, a leader of the Epeians (15.515–519; see [[ajax]], [[laodamas]], [[polydamas]] and [[otus-of-cyllene]]). The poem tells nothing more of Schedius than his father and his people.

**Two of the name.** The Catalogue gives the Phocians two leaders, Schedius and Epistrophus, sons of great-hearted Iphitus (2.517–518; see [[schedius]], [[epistrophus]] and [[iphitus]]). In *Iliad* 17 Hector kills Schedius, son of great-hearted Iphitus (Σχεδίον μεγαθύμου Ἰφίτου υἱὸν), far the best of the Phocians, who lived in famous Panopeus (17.306–311; see [[panopeus]]). So Hector kills a Phocian leader called Schedius twice, and each has his own father. The poem says nothing to bring the two together. The index keeps them apart, as the patronymics do.
