**A family of builders.** τέκτονος υἱὸν Ἁρμονίδεω: Phereclus is the son of a τέκτων, a builder or worker in wood, who is himself the son of Harmon. Some take Τέκτων as a name, “son of Tecton, son of Harmon”. The names suit the craft: τέκτων is “builder”, and Harmon's name belongs with ἁρμονίη, “joint, fastening”, the word for the joints with which Odysseus fastens his raft (Od. 5.248, 5.361). Φέρεκλος may be “bringer of fame” (φέρω + κλέος). See [[harmon]].

He “knew how to make with his hands every kind of intricate work” (δαίδαλα πάντα), “for Pallas Athena loved him above all others” (5.60–61). Athena is the patron of craftsmen. A simile of Book 15 has a carpenter “who knows all his craft well by the promptings of Athena” (15.411–412), and in the Odyssey Hephaestus and Athena teach the goldsmith his art (Od. 6.232–234). Troy had such men: Alexander built his own house with “the men who were then the best builders in deep-soiled Troy” (6.314–315).

**The ships.** He “built for Alexander the balanced ships that began the evil” (5.62–63). ἀρχέκακος, “beginning the evil”, is found only here, and in Book 22 Hector remembers the ships that brought Helen as “the beginning of the quarrel” (νείκεος ἀρχή, 22.116). See [[balanced-ships]] and [[paris]]. The ships “became an evil for all the Trojans and for himself, since he knew nothing of the oracles of the gods” (5.63–64). Whether “he” is Phereclus or Alexander is not certain, and Homer does not say what the oracles were. In Proclus' summary of the *Cypria*, Alexander builds his ships at Aphrodite's suggestion, and Helenus and Cassandra foretell what will come of the voyage.

**The death.** Meriones catches him in pursuit and strikes him in the right buttock; the point goes “under the bone and into the bladder”, and he falls to his knees with a groan, and “death enfolded him” (5.65–68). The builder of the ships that began the war dies running from it. Meriones gives the same wound, in the same words, to Harpalion, son of the Paphlagonian king Pylaemenes, in Book 13 (13.650–652; see [[meriones]] and [[pylaemenes]]).
