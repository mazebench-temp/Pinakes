Homer names him twice in Book 2, both times as "Eurytus of Oechalia" (Εὐρύτου Οἰχαλιῆος). Thamyris was coming from his house when the Muses met him (2.596). The Thessalian contingent of Podalirius and Machaon includes "Oechalia, the city of Eurytus" (2.730). On where Oechalia lay, see [[Oechalia]].

## Elsewhere in Homer

Odysseus, boasting of his archery among the Phaeacians, says he would not compete with the men of old, with Heracles or Eurytus of Oechalia, who challenged even the gods with the bow. Eurytus died young, "for Apollo in anger killed him, because he challenged him to shoot" (Od. 8.223–228). Eurytus' bow passed to his son Iphitus, who gave it to Odysseus in Messene; Heracles later killed Iphitus while he was his guest. It is with this bow that Odysseus kills the suitors (Od. 21.13–41).

## Outside Homer

In the lost epic *The Capture of Oechalia*, attributed to Creophylus of Samos, Heracles sacked Oechalia when Eurytus refused him his daughter Iole (Callimachus, *Epigram* 6 Pfeiffer). Sophocles' *Women of Trachis* tells the end of that story (see also Apollodorus 2.6.1–2, 2.7.7). A different Eurytus, one of the twin sons of Actor, is the father of the Epeian leader Thalpius (2.621; [[eurytus-of-elis]]).
