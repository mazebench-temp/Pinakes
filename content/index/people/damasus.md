{{quote:04e81115-9d6c-5a59-b445-1b4070fb3827}}

When the Lapiths join the fight at the gate, Polypoetes strikes Damasus through his bronze-cheeked helmet; the helmet does not stop the point, which breaks the bone, and the brain inside is spattered (12.182–186; see [[polypoetes]], [[helmet]] and [[the-gates-of-the-wall]]). The death is told in formulas. Achilles kills Demoleon, son of Antenor, in nearly the same verses (20.397–400), and the last of them, “and he beat him down for all his eagerness”, ended the death of Bianor's companion Oileus at Agamemnon's hands (11.98; see [[oileus-the-trojan]]).

The name looks like a form of δαμάζω, “tame, subdue”, the verb of that last verse (δάμασσε, 12.186). He is the first of the three men Polypoetes kills; Pylon and Ormenus follow in the next verse (see [[pylon]] and [[ormenus-killed-by-polypoetes]]).
