## The name

Three men of the name fight in the Iliad: the Heraclid who leads the ships of Cos (*Iliad* 2.678; see [[antiphus]]), the Maeonian (*Iliad* 2.864; see [[antiphus-of-maeonia]]) and a son of Priam whom Agamemnon kills (*Iliad* 4.489, 11.101–109; see [[antiphus-son-of-priam]]). The Odyssey has another besides this one, an Ithacan who sits with Mentor and Halitherses, the old companions of Odysseus (see [[halitherses]]), in the place of assembly when Telemachus has come back from Pylos (*Odyssey* 17.61–70; see [[mentor]]).

## Family

His father is the old hero Aegyptius. Of his three brothers one, Eurynomus, keeps company with the suitors, and two work their father's fields (*Odyssey* 2.15–22; see [[aegyptius]] and [[eurynomus]]).

## Descriptions

αἰχμητής, “spearman” (*Odyssey* 2.19), the Iliad's word for a fighting man (see [[spearmen]]).

## Elsewhere in Homer

In Odysseus' own story of the Cyclops' cave, the giant seizes two of his companions at a time, dashes them against the ground like puppies and makes his meal of them, for supper, for breakfast and for supper again, six men in all (*Odyssey* 9.287–293, 9.311, 9.344, a plain rendering; see [[polyphemus-the-cyclops]]). Odysseus does not name them there; the second book names one of them, in the verses that bring in his father.
