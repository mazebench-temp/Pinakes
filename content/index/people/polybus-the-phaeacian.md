## The name

W. W. Merry counts Polybus among the few Phaeacian names, with Alcinous, Arete, Laodamas and Rhexenor, that are not “taken from circumstances of seafaring life” (*Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on *Odyssey* 8.111).

The poems give the name to several men, and nothing joins them: a son of Antenor (*Iliad* 11.59; see [[polybus]]), the father of the suitor Eurymachus (*Odyssey* 1.399; see [[polybus-father-of-eurymachus]]), a rich man of Egyptian Thebes (*Odyssey* 4.126; see [[polybus-of-thebes]]), and a suitor whom the swineherd strikes down (*Odyssey* 22.243, 22.284). Three of them are δαΐφρων, “wise-hearted”: this one, the father of Eurymachus (*Odyssey* 15.519) and the suitor (*Odyssey* 22.243; see [[wise-hearted]]).
