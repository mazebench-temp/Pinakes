The origin of the name Τυδεύς (Tydeus) is unknown. In Book 2 he is named only in his son's title, “the son of Tydeus” (2.406). Later books often call Diomedes simply Τυδεΐδης, “son of Tydeus”.

{{notes}}

## Elsewhere in Homer

His son is measured against him again and again. Agamemnon taunts Diomedes with the story of Tydeus' embassy to Thebes, where he challenged the Cadmeians to contests and beat them all, and killed all but one of the fifty men who lay in ambush for him on his way home (4.370–400). Athena tells Diomedes that Tydeus “was small in build, but he was a fighter” (5.801). Diomedes tells his own descent: Tydeus, son of [[oeneus|Oeneus]], left Calydon, settled at Argos and married a daughter of Adrastus, and now “the heaped earth covers him at Thebes” (14.113–125).

## Outside Homer

Tydeus is one of the Seven who attacked Thebes with Adrastus and Polynices; Aeschylus describes his shield in the *Seven against Thebes* (see [[aeschylus-seven]]; Statius tells his whole story in the *Thebaid*: [[statius-tydeus]]). A later story says that Athena meant to make him immortal, but when she found the dying Tydeus eating the brains of his enemy Melanippus, she turned away in disgust (Apollodorus, *Library* 3.6.8).
