Τληπόλεμόν τε Δαμαστορίδην, “Tlepolemus, son of Damastor” (16.416), is the only mention of him in the Iliad. His son is one of the nine men whom Patroclus kills one after another between the ships, the river and the wall (16.415–418; see [[tlepolemus-son-of-damastor]] and [[erymas-killed-by-patroclus]]).

In the Odyssey Δαμαστορίδης is the patronymic of Agelaus, one of the suitors (Od. 20.321, 22.212, 22.241), whom Odysseus stabs in the hall (Od. 22.292–293); his father is another Damastor. The name seems to be formed from δάμνημι, “subdue”.
