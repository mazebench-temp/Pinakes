He is named in one tale only, the first of the false stories that Odysseus tells on Ithaca, to Athena, whom he takes for a young herdsman (see the note on *Odyssey* 13, [[the-first-cretan-tale]], [[odysseus]] and [[athena]]). In the later Cretan tales, to Eumaeus and to Penelope, Idomeneus has a part again, but his son is not named (*Odyssey* 14.237–239, 19.181–184).

## The name

Ὀρσίλοχος is a name of the Iliad, which gives it to two men who die in battle: the son of Diocles of Pherae, the twin of Crethon, whom Aeneas kills (*Iliad* 5.541–560; see [[orsilochus-son-of-diocles]], where the name is discussed), and a Trojan, the first of the eight men whom Teucer shoots from behind the shield of Ajax (*Iliad* 8.274; see [[orsilochus-killed-by-teucer]]). The Odyssey's Orsilochus is a Cretan, and the only son of Idomeneus whom Homer names (see [[idomeneus]] and [[crete]]).

## Descriptions

- πόδας ὠκύς, “swift-footed” (*Odyssey* 13.260), the Iliad's formula for Achilles (*Iliad* 1.58 and often; see [[swift-footed]]). The accusative πόδας ὠκύν is found only here in Homer.
- A runner: “who in wide Crete / outran toiling men with his swift feet” (*Odyssey* 13.260–261), ἀλφησταί, “toiling men”, as at *Odyssey* 1.349 and 6.8 (see [[toiling-men]]).
