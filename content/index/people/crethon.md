**Twins.** Crethon and Orsilochus are always named together, and the Greek speaks of them in the dual: διδυμάονε παῖδε, “twin sons” (5.548), and τὼ μὲν ἄρʼ ἡβήσαντε, “these two, grown to manhood” (5.550). They are Aeneas' first kills after Apollo sends him back healed. The narrator calls them “two of the best men of the Danaans” (5.541) and traces their family back to the river Alpheus (5.543–549; see [[diocles]]).

**For the sons of Atreus.** They came “seeking satisfaction for the sons of Atreus, Agamemnon and Menelaus” (τιμὴν … ἀρνυμένω, 5.552–553). These are the words in which Achilles described the whole expedition in the quarrel of Book 1 (τιμὴν ἀρνύμενοι Μενελάῳ σοί τε, 1.159; see [[war-for-menelaus]]). Menelaus, for whom they came, sees them fall and pities them (5.561; see [[menelaus]]). “And there the end of death covered them both” (5.553). τέλος θανάτοιο, “the end of death”, covers Sarpedon, Patroclus and Hector at their deaths (16.502, 16.855, 22.361).

**Two lions.** Their fall is given two similes. They are like two lions raised by their mother in the thickets of a deep forest, which carry off men's cattle and sheep until they are killed at men's hands with the sharp bronze (5.554–558; see [[two-lions]]), and they fall “like tall fir trees” (5.560; see [[fir-trees]]). Raised together, they die together. Menelaus and Antilochus drag the bodies back to the army and lay “the two poor men” in the arms of their companions (5.573–574).
