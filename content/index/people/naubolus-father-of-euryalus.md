Ναυβολίδης, “the son of Naubolus” (*Odyssey* 8.116), is all that Homer says of him: the patronymic of Euryalus, who rises for the games “the equal of Ares, ruin of mortals, / the son of Naubolus, who was the best in looks and form / of all the Phaeacians after noble Laodamas” (*Odyssey* 8.115–117; see [[euryalus-the-phaeacian]]). The common reading Ναυβολίδης θʼ would make him the father of another competitor, named only by the patronymic; the text omits θʼ (see the Odyssey translation's note on 8.116).

The name is one of the Phaeacian names taken from seafaring life, and its etymology, W. W. Merry suggests, would seem to be βολίς, a plummet for sounding (*Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on *Odyssey* 8.111; see [[phaeacians]]). The Iliad's [[naubolus|Naubolus]] is a Phocian, the father of Iphitus and grandfather of Schedius and Epistrophus, known in the same way only through a patronymic, Ναυβολίδαο (*Iliad* 2.518; see [[iphitus]]).
