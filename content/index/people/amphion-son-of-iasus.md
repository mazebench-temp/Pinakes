## Family

Chloris, Neleus' queen, was “the youngest daughter of Amphion, son of Iasus, / who once ruled with might in Minyan Orchomenus” (*Odyssey* 11.283–284; see [[chloris]], [[neleus]] and [[orchomenus]]). Through her he is the grandfather of Nestor, Chromius, Periclymenus and Pero (see [[nestor]]). His father Iasus is known only from the patronymic; the Iliad's Iasus, a leader of the Athenians, and the father of Dmetor in Odysseus' tale in *Odyssey* 17 are other men (see [[iasus]]).

The name is shared by two other men in Homer: Amphion of Thebes, son of Antiope, named twenty lines earlier in the same catalogue (*Odyssey* 11.262; see [[amphion-of-thebes]]), and Amphion the Epeian, who holds the ships against Hector (*Iliad* 13.692; see [[amphion-the-epeian]]).

## Descriptions

ὅς ποτʼ ἐν Ὀρχομενῷ Μινυείῳ ἶφι ἄνασσεν, “who once ruled with might in Minyan Orchomenus” (*Odyssey* 11.284; see [[orchomenus]]). ἶφι, “with might”, an old instrumental of ἴς, “strength”, goes with ἀνάσσειν as in Chryses' prayer to Apollo, who “rules Tenedos with might” (*Iliad* 1.38), and in Hector's prayer for his son (*Iliad* 6.478); the same words are used of Dmetor, son of Iasus, “who ruled with might in Cyprus” (*Odyssey* 17.443, a plain rendering).

## Outside Homer

W. W. Merry holds him distinct from the Theban Amphion, and reports that a later form of the story identified the two, so that Chloris became the daughter of Amphion and Niobe and the one child whom the gods spared; he quotes Grote's remark that the logographers, who usually multiplied persons of the same name to smooth their chronology, here blended into one two men whom the Odyssey keeps apart (*Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on *Odyssey* 11.260, 11.283; see [[niobe]]).
