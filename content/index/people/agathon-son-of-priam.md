νεικείων Ἕλενόν τε Πάριν τʼ Ἀγάθωνά τε δῖον, “rebuking Helenus and Paris and brilliant Agathon” (24.249). He is the third of the nine sons whom Priam rebukes as “liars and dancers” while he sends them for the mule wagon, and the poem says nothing more of him (24.248–264; see [[sons-of-priam]] and [[priam]]). δῖος, “brilliant”, is given in the same list to Agauus (24.251; see [[brilliant]] and [[agauus]]). The name is formed from ἀγαθός, “good, brave” (see [[agathos]]).

The Athenian tragic poet of the fifth century bore the same name (see [[agathon]]).

## Outside Homer

Apollodorus counts Agathon among the sons whom Priam had by women other than Hecuba (*Library* 3.12.5; see [[apollodorus]]).
