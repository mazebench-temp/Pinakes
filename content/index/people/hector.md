## Etymology

Ἕκτωρ (Hektōr), from ἔχω, "hold": "the holder, the one who keeps safe", apt for the defender of Troy.

## In Book 1

{{quote:32bf3f32-ab12-5dda-bb11-10b5d2fe4f77}}

ἀνδροφόνος = ἀνήρ (man) + φόνος (slaughter): man-slaying. Hector's first appearance in the poem is as a threat foretold by his future killer. See [[longing-for-achilles]].

## Outside Homer

Hector leads the Trojans through the Iliad. He kills Patroclus in Book 16, is killed by Achilles in Book 22, and is buried in Book 24, with whose last line the poem ends: "So they buried Hector, tamer of horses."
