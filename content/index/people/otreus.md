λαοὺς Ὀτρῆος, "the people of Otreus" (3.186): the Phrygians are named by their kings. Homer names him only here, beside [[Mygdon]]. See [[phrygia]], [[sangarius]] and [[priam-and-the-amazons]].

## Outside Homer

In the *Homeric Hymn to Aphrodite*, the goddess, disguised as a mortal girl, tells Anchises that she is the daughter of "Otreus, famous in name, who rules all Phrygia with its fine walls" (111–112). [[apollodorus|Apollodorus]] names Placia, a daughter of Otreus, as one of the women said to have been the wife of Laomedon, and so the mother of Priam (*Library* 3.12.3).
