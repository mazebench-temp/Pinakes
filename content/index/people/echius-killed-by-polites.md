Μηκιστῆ δʼ ἕλε Πουλυδάμας, Ἐχίον δὲ Πολίτης / πρώτῃ ἐν ὑσμίνῃ, “And Polydamas killed Mecisteus, and Polites Echius, / in the front line of battle” (15.339–340; see [[polites]], [[polydamas]] and [[mecisteus-son-of-echius]]). He is one of the Achaeans named among the dead when Apollo has shaken the aegis and the battle scatters (15.326–342; see [[aegis]]). It is the only man Polites kills in the Iliad. Priam's son, who kept watch for the Trojans from the tomb of Aesyetes, “trusting in his speed of foot”, and who led his wounded brother Deiphobus out of the fighting (2.791–794, 13.533–539), is named here once among the killers.

**Father and son?** Mecisteus, killed in the same verse, is “Mecisteus, son of Echius” when he carries the wounded Teucer and Hypsenor to the ships (8.333, 13.422; see [[echius]]). Whether the Echius whom Polites kills is that father, dying beside his son, or another man of the name, the poem does not say (see the translation's note on 15.339). The index keeps the two apart. Patroclus kills a third Echius, on the Trojan side, in his charge (16.416; see [[echius-killed-by-patroclus]]).
