Μόρυν θʼ υἷʼ Ἱπποτίωνος, “and Morys, son of Hippotion” (13.792): the father is named to distinguish the son, one of the men who had come from Ascania as relief the morning before (13.792–794; see [[morys]] and [[ascanius-the-mysian]]). In the next book Meriones kills “Morys and Hippotion” together (Μόρυν τε καὶ Ἱπποτίωνα, 14.514). If the second is Morys' father, father and son came to the war together and died together; the poem does not say. The name is formed from ἵππος, “horse”.
