Hector has rallied the Trojans with Ares and Enyo beside him, and Diomedes has told the Achaeans to give ground (5.590–606). The first men Hector kills in the poem are these two, told in two verses: two men εἰδότε χάρμης, “who knew the joy of battle” (see [[charme]]), riding together “in one chariot” (5.608–609). The same words, εἰν ἑνὶ δίφρῳ, told how Diomedes caught two sons of Priam, Echemmon and Chromius, earlier in the book (5.160; see [[echemmon]]). Nothing else is said of Menesthes and Anchialus: no father, no home. Telamonian Ajax pities them, and his answer is a spear: he kills Amphius of Paesus (5.610–617; see [[ajax]] and [[amphius-son-of-selagus]]).

He is not Menestheus (Μενεσθεύς), the leader of the Athenians (see [[menestheus]]).
