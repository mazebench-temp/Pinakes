πολύσκαρθμος = πολύς ("much") + σκαίρω ("skip, bound"): "much-bounding", nimble. The word occurs only here. Because horses are praised with a related word, εὔσκαρθμος, "well-bounding" (13.31), ancient readers inferred that Myrine was a horsewoman and an Amazon (Strabo, *Geography*, Book 12). The Iliad knows the Amazons as women "a match for men" (ἀντιάνειραι), whom Priam fought in his youth in Phrygia (3.189) and Bellerophon in Lycia (6.186). Towns in Aeolis and on Lemnos were called Myrina, and later writers connected them with the Amazon. The poem itself gives only her grave and the gods' name for it ([[language-of-gods-and-men]]).
