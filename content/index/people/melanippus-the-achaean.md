καὶ Μελάνιππον, “and Melanippus”, ends the list of the men whom Odysseus takes with him to fetch Agamemnon's gifts: the sons of Nestor, Meges, Thoas, Meriones, Lycomedes and Melanippus (19.238–240; see [[odysseus]] and [[the-gifts-of-agamemnon]]). Agamemnon told Odysseus to choose κούρητας ἀριστῆας Παναχαιῶν, “young men, the best of all the Achaeans” (19.193), and “the other young men of the Achaeans carried the gifts” with Odysseus (19.248), so Melanippus is counted among the young men, with leaders of contingents and captains of the watch (see [[kouretes]], [[meges]], [[thoas]], [[meriones]] and [[lycomedes]]). Homer tells nothing else of him: not his father, his country, or whom he led.

The same two words end the verses in which Teucer shoots a Trojan Melanippus and Patroclus kills another (8.276, 16.695; see [[melanippus]] and [[melanippus-killed-by-patroclus]]), and the fourth man of the name is Melanippus, son of Hicetaon, Hector's cousin, whom Antilochus kills (15.546–583; see [[melanippus-son-of-hicetaon]]). He is the only Achaean of the four. The name joins μέλας, “black”, and ἵππος, “horse”.
