Δρύαντος υἱός, “the son of Dryas”, names Lycurgus by his father at the start of Diomedes' story, as heroes are named in the poem (see [[lycurgus]]). Nothing else is said of him. The name is formed from δρῦς, “oak”. The Lapith Dryas, “shepherd of the people”, whom Nestor names among the heroes of his youth (1.263), is another man (see [[dryas]]).

Later poets kept the name in the family. In Sophocles Lycurgus is “the son of Dryas, king of the Edonians” (*Antigone* 955–956), and in Apollodorus Lycurgus in his madness kills his own son, also called Dryas (*Library* 3.5.1).
