## The name

Πεισήνωρ is formed from πείθω, “persuade”, and ἀνήρ, “man”, like the names of the two other men who bear it in Homer: a Trojan, the father of Cleitus, Polydamas' charioteer (*Iliad* 15.445; see [[peisenor]]), and the father of Ops, whom the Odyssey names only in Eurycleia's lineage, “Eurycleia, daughter of Ops, son of Peisenor” (*Odyssey* 1.429; see [[peisenor-father-of-ops]]). Nothing joins the three.

## Descriptions

- κῆρυξ … πεπνυμένα μήδεα εἰδώς, “the herald … who knew wise counsels” (*Odyssey* 2.38). The verse is the Iliad's for the Trojan herald Idaeus, who parts Hector and Ajax at nightfall, κῆρυξ Ἰδαῖος πεπνυμένα μήδεα εἰδώς, “by the herald Idaeus, who knew wise counsels” (*Iliad* 7.278; see [[idaeus]] and [[wise]]), with one name put for the other. The two verses are the only places in Homer where the phrase occurs.
