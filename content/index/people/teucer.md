## Etymology

Τεῦκρος (Teukros). The name has no clear Greek etymology.

## Family

Agamemnon tells his birth when he cheers him on in Book 8: “Teucer, dear heart, son of Telamon, lord of the people, shoot on like this, and you may become a light to the Danaans and to your father Telamon, who reared you when you were small and cared for you in his own house, bastard though you were” (8.281–284). His mother was not Telamon's wife, and the Iliad does not name her (see [[nothos]]). Ajax is his κασίγνητος καὶ ὄπατρος, “brother by the same father” (12.371; see [[kasignetos]]). Some scholars have argued that the dual Αἴαντε, “the two Ajaxes”, once meant Ajax and Teucer; in the Iliad as we have it the pair is Ajax and the son of Oileus, and the theory is unproven (see [[ajax-son-of-oileus]]).

## Descriptions

- τόξων ἐῢ εἰδώς, “well skilled with the bow” (12.350 = 12.363; see [[skilled-with-the-bow]]). Idomeneus calls him ἄριστος Ἀχαιῶν τοξοσύνῃ, “the best of the Achaeans at archery”, and brave in the close fight too (13.313–314).
- ἀμύμων, “noble” (8.273, 8.292; see [[blameless]]), and βίη Τεύκροιο ἄνακτος, “the might of lord Teucer” (23.859), a hero named by his strength, like βίη Ἡρακληείη, “mighty Heracles”, literally the might of Heracles (11.690).
- His bow is παλίντονα τόξα, “his back-bent bow” (8.266, 15.443), the bow “that Phoebus Apollo gave you”, as Ajax says (15.441), like Pandarus' (2.827; see [[the-bow-of-pandarus]]).

## Elsewhere in Homer

**Behind the shield.** In Book 8 he shoots from under the shield of Ajax, “like a child under its mother”, until Hector fells him with a stone (8.266–334).

**At the wall and the ships.** He wounds Glaucus in the arm at the Achaean wall (12.387–391, 16.508–512). In Book 15 he draws on Hector, but Zeus snaps the string, and at Ajax's word he puts the bow away and takes up shield and spear (15.458–483).

**The contest.** At Patroclus' funeral games he shoots first in the archery but makes no vow to Apollo, and Apollo begrudges him the bird: his arrow cuts the cord that tethers the dove, and she flies free. Meriones, who makes the vow, wins the first prize (23.859–883; see [[meriones]]).

Three times a god turns his bow: Apollo at 8.311, Zeus at 15.461–465, Apollo again at 23.865. The best archer of the Achaeans shoots with Apollo's gift and against Apollo's will. He does not appear in the Odyssey.

## Outside Homer

Later writers made his mother Hesione, daughter of Laomedon, whom Heracles gave Telamon as his prize when he sacked Troy (Apollodorus, *Library* 2.6.4, 3.12.7; see [[telamon]] and [[heracles-sacks-troy]]). Teucer was then Priam's nephew, fighting against his mother's city. In Sophocles' *Ajax* he defends his dead brother's burial against Menelaus and Agamemnon, who taunts him as the son of a captive woman. In the prologue of Euripides' *Helen* he meets Helen in Egypt: Telamon has banished him for coming home without Ajax, and Apollo has told him to found a new Salamis on Cyprus. Horace gives him a speech to his companions as they flee, *nil desperandum Teucro duce et auspice Teucro* (*Odes* 1.7.21–32), and Virgil's Dido remembers him coming to Sidon as an exile (*Aeneid* 1.619–626).

A different Teucer, son of the river Scamander, was said to have been the first king of the Troad (Apollodorus, *Library* 3.12.1). Virgil's Trojans are *Teucri*, after an ancestor Teucer whom he brings to the Troad from Crete (*Aeneid* 3.104–110).
