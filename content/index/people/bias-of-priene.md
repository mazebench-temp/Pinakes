title: Bias of Priene
greek: Βίας

summary: One of the Seven Sages, of Priene in Ionia (sixth century BCE), to whom the precept “love as though you will one day hate” was ascribed.
---

Herodotus tells that Bias, or by another account Pittacus, stopped Croesus from building ships to attack the islanders (1.27), and that after the Persian conquest Bias advised the Ionians to sail together to Sardinia and found one city there (1.170). Plato names him among the Seven Sages (*Protagoras* 343a). The precept that one should love as though one will hate is his in Diogenes Laertius (1.87); in Cicero's dialogue on friendship Scipio calls it the most hostile saying to friendship there is, and will not believe that a wise man said it (*On Friendship* 59).
