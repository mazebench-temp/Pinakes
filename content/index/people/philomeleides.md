## The name

Φιλομηλεΐδης has the form of a patronymic, like Πηλεΐδης, “son of Peleus”, and Τυδεΐδης, “son of Tydeus”, but Homer gives him no other name and names no father. Nothing connects him with Philomela, the daughter of Pandion who was changed into a bird (see [[philomela]]).

{{notes}}

## Outside Homer

Eustathius, as Merry reports him, says that “the more accurate” writers made Philomeleides a king of Lesbos who used to challenge passers-by to wrestle, and did the same to the Achaeans when they put in there (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 4.343; see [[odysseus-wrestles-philomeleides]] and [[lesbos]]).
