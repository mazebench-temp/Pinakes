Aphareus is named between Meriones and Deipyrus, the fourth, fifth and sixth of the seven captains, each of whom leads a hundred young men to sit between the ditch and the wall (9.83; see [[night-watch]], [[meriones]] and [[deipyrus]]). Homer gives him no contingent and no home.

## Elsewhere in Homer

The poem brings him back with his fellow captains. When Aeneas comes at Idomeneus in Book 13, Idomeneus calls on “Ascalaphus and Aphareus and Deipyrus, and Meriones and Antilochus” (13.477–479; see [[ascalaphus]]). Ascalaphus, Aphareus and Deipyrus are all killed in the next hundred lines (13.518–520, 13.541–544, 13.576–580). Aeneas springs on Aphareus, “son of Caletor”, as he turns toward him, and strikes him in the throat; his head sinks to one side, and the shield and helmet fall with him (13.541–544; see [[aeneas]]). Καλητορίδης, “son of Caletor”, is all the poem tells of his father. The Trojan Caletor, son of Clytius, killed by Ajax at the ships (15.419–421), is another man.
