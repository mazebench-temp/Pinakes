Πείραιε Κλυτίδη, “Peiraeus, son of Clytius” (*Odyssey* 15.540): Telemachus calls his companion by his father's name when he asks him to take the seer Theoclymenus into his house (see [[peiraeus-son-of-clytius]] and [[theoclymenus]]). The poem names the father once more, when Telemachus' companions bring the ship to the city and carry the gifts of Menelaus “to the house of Clytius” (*Odyssey* 16.327, a plain rendering), where Peiraeus keeps them for Telemachus (*Odyssey* 17.74–83). Nothing else is told of him.

The name is formed from κλυτός, “famous” (see [[famous]]). Homer has two other men of the name: the Trojan elder, son of Laomedon and father of Caletor (*Iliad* 3.147, 15.419; see [[clytius]]), and the father of the Achaean Dolops, whom Hector kills (Κλυτίδην, *Iliad* 11.302; see [[dolops]]).
