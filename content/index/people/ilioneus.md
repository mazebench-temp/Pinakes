**The only son.** The narrator gives him a father before his death: Phorbas, “rich in flocks, whom most / of the Trojans Hermes loved, and granted him wealth” (14.490–491; see [[phorbas-father-of-ilioneus]], [[hermes]] and [[rich-in-flocks]]). “And to him the mother bore Ilioneus, an only child” (14.492). The name is formed from Ἴλιος, the city (see [[troy]]).

**His death.** Acamas has boasted over Promachus, and Peneleos rushes at him; Acamas does not wait, and Peneleos stabs Ilioneus instead (14.486–489; see [[acamas]] and [[peneleos]]). The spear goes in under the brow, drives out the eyeball, and passes through the back of the head (14.493–495). γλήνη, the “eyeball”, is the word with which Hector called Diomedes a “wretched doll” (8.164; see [[wretched-doll]]). He sits down stretching out both his hands, and Peneleos draws his sharp sword, in the verses with which Achilles kills Lycaon in *Iliad* 21 (14.495–496 ≈ 21.115–116; see [[lycaon-son-of-priam]]). The sword cuts through the middle of the neck, and the head falls, helmet and all, with the spear still in the eye (14.496–499). Heads were cut off before: Agamemnon hewed through the neck of Hippolochus and “sent him rolling like a log through the throng” (11.146–147), and the son of Oileus sent Imbrius' head “flying like a ball through the throng” to fall at Hector's feet (13.202–205; see [[hippolochus-son-of-antimachus]] and [[imbrius]]). Peneleos lifts this one up, “like a poppy head”, for the Trojans to see (14.499–500; see [[the-poppy]]).

**The boast.** “Trojans, tell noble Ilioneus' dear father for me / and his mother, to wail for him in their halls” (14.501–502; see [[bereaved-parents]]): the wife of Promachus, Peneleos says, will not be glad at her husband's coming home either (14.503–505; see [[promachus]]). Trembling seizes the Trojans, “and each man looked around for a way to escape sheer destruction” (14.506–507), the verse that tells the Trojans' panic when Patroclus appears in Achilles' armor (16.283), and the suitors' when Odysseus names himself (*Odyssey* 22.43; see [[trojans]]).
