## Etymology

Ἀγαμέμνων (Agamemnōn). Usually analyzed as ἀγα- (very, exceedingly) + μέμνων, from μένω (stand fast) or μέδομαι (rule): "very steadfast" or "ruling mightily". The analysis is plausible but not certain.

## Family

{{quote:90fba18f-532e-5cd7-977c-9bf5c6530598}}

Son of [[Atreus]]. Ἀτρεΐδης (son of Atreus) is his most frequent name in Book 1, often standing alone.

{{quote:63f57839-c317-508c-b63c-d19548ac2ff2}}

With [[Menelaus]], one of the two sons of Atreus, κοσμήτορε λαῶν, "the two marshals of the people", a dual

{{quote:366c4114-1eba-5d86-849b-07ee9cb4e4dd}}

Husband of [[Clytemnestra]], whom he rates below a captive in public. The audience knew what she would do when he came home. See [[clytemnestra-and-the-return]].

## Descriptions

{{quote:8e546bc4-545d-5cab-abfb-179d3e301c5c}}

ἄναξ ἀνδρῶν = ἄναξ (lord) + ἀνήρ (man): [[lord-of-men]], his formulaic title as supreme commander

{{quote:51624906-2521-5194-abb1-424e82334a22}}

εὐρὺ κρείων = εὐρύ (widely) + κρείων (ruling): wide-ruling, the formula for his office

{{quote:21e7a914-2b5f-550e-9038-e136d580e253}}

κύδιστε, φιλοκτεανώτατε: "most glorious" and "most covetous" in one breath. φιλοκτέανος = φίλος (loving) + κτέανον (possession).

{{quote:08040c89-a364-58ec-839b-7d64eaa76706}}

The insults gather into a portrait: drunk, cowardly, greedy, a "people-devouring king" (δημοβόρος βασιλεύς) who rules over nobodies.

## Outside Homer

The Odyssey tells of his murder by Aegisthus and Clytemnestra on his return (1.35–43, 4.512–537, 11.405–434). Aeschylus made it the subject of the *Agamemnon* (458 BCE). The sacrifice of his daughter Iphigenia at Aulis was told in the *Cypria* and is unknown to Homer. The gold "Mask of Agamemnon" that Schliemann found at Mycenae in 1876 is several centuries older than any Trojan War.
