summary: The lawgiver to whom the Spartans traced their constitution and way of life; whether he was a historical man was doubted already in antiquity. He is not Homer's Lycurgus, the Thracian king who attacked Dionysus.

Herodotus says that the Spartans brought their order from Crete under Lycurgus, the guardian of the young king Leobotes (1.65). Plutarch begins his life of him by saying that nothing can be said about him beyond dispute (*Lycurgus* 1). Aristotle discusses the Spartan constitution in the *Politics* (2.9). See [[laconians]] and [[lycurgus]].
