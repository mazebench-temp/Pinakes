καὶ βάλεν Ἱππασίδην Ἀπισάονα ποιμένα λαῶν, “and hit Apisaon, son of Hippasus, shepherd of the people” (17.348): the patronymic is all that the poem tells of him. His son “had come from deep-soiled Paeonia” and was the best of its fighters after Asteropaeus (17.350–351; see [[apisaon-son-of-hippasus]] and [[paeonians]]), so the father too was presumably a Paeonian.

The verse is built like the one that names Hypsenor, son of Hippasus, whom Deiphobus strikes (13.411), and each of Homer's three men called Hippasus is named only as a father: the Trojan father of Charops and Socus, “wise-hearted” and a “tamer of horses” (11.426–450; see [[hippasus-father-of-socus]]), the father of the Achaean Hypsenor (13.411; see [[hippasus-father-of-hypsenor]]), and this Paeonian. Nothing joins them. The name is made from ἵππος, “horse”.
