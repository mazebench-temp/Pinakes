The leaders of the Lycians are climbing onto the battlements of Menestheus' tower “like a dark storm” (12.375–376; see [[lycians]] and [[like-a-black-storm]]), and Ajax, just come with Teucer, opens the fighting with this kill (see [[ajax]] and [[sarpedon]]). The stone lay inside the wall, “uppermost beside the battlement” (12.381; see [[battlements]]), and the narrator measures it against men “such as mortals are now” (12.383; see [[men-of-today]] and [[stone-throwing]]), as he will measure Hector's stone at the gate (12.447–449). It crushes the τετράφαλος κυνέη, the “four-ridged helmet” (12.384; see [[helmet]]).

The verses of his fall return twice. Patroclus kills Hector's charioteer Cebriones with “a jagged boulder” (μάρμαρον ὀκριόεντα), and he falls from the chariot like a diver, “and his spirit left his bones” (16.735–743); Patroclus then mocks the dive (16.745–750; see [[cebriones]] and [[jagged]]). In the Odyssey the falling mast smashes all the bones of the helmsman's head together, and he falls from the deck like a diver (Od. 12.411–414). See [[like-a-diver]].

Ἐπικλῆς, contracted from Ἐπικλέης, is one of the names built on κλέος, “fame” (see [[kleos]]), like that of Diocles (Διοκλῆος, 5.542).
