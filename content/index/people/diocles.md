**The house.** Aeneas' first kills after his return to the battle are two men from the Peloponnese, and the narrator traces their descent back three generations, to a river. Their father “lived in well-built Pherae, wealthy in substance” (5.543–544; see [[pherae-in-messenia]]). His father was Ortilochus, and Ortilochus was a son of the river Alpheus (see [[ortilochus]] and [[alpheus]]). The twins “followed with the Argives to Ilios of the fine horses, seeking satisfaction for the sons of Atreus” (5.551–552), and they died together (see [[crethon]]).

**A father at home.** Book 5 counts the cost of the killing to the fathers at home. Dares, priest of Hephaestus, keeps one son of two (5.9–24), and the old dream-reader Eurydamas and old Phaenops lose two sons each (5.148–158; see [[dares]] and [[phaenops]]). All three are Trojans. Diocles is the Achaean counterpart: like Phaenops he is rich, and like him he loses two sons at once. See [[bereaved-parents]] and [[fathers-and-sons]].

**In the Odyssey.** He is alive in the Odyssey, and he is a host. Telemachus and Peisistratus, driving from Pylos to Sparta, come at sunset to Pherae, “to the house of Diocles, son of Ortilochus, whom the Alpheus begot”; they spend the night there, and he gives them the gifts due to guests (Od. 3.488–490). They stop with him again on the way home (Od. 15.186–188). The Odyssey says nothing of his sons.
