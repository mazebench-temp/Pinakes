## The name

Her name is the name of a town at the southern end of the Argolic peninsula, which the Catalogue gives to Diomedes' men (*Iliad* 2.560; see [[hermione]]).

## Family

The Odyssey makes her Helen's only child: “for to Helen the gods brought no more offspring to light, / once she had first borne a lovely child, / Hermione, who had the form of golden Aphrodite” (*Odyssey* 4.12–14). She is the daughter Helen left behind when she followed Paris, “my darling daughter” in the Iliad (*Iliad* 3.175), and “my child” in Helen's remorse at Sparta (*Odyssey* 4.263). Megapenthes, Menelaus' son by a slave woman, is her half-brother (*Odyssey* 4.10–12; see [[megapenthes]]). Her husband is the son of Achilles, unnamed in Book 4 of the Odyssey (*Odyssey* 4.5–9; see [[neoptolemus]]).

## Descriptions

- παῖδʼ ἐρατεινήν, “a lovely child” (*Odyssey* 4.13).
- ἣ εἶδος ἔχε χρυσέης Ἀφροδίτης, “who had the form of golden Aphrodite” (*Odyssey* 4.14; see [[aphrodite]]).

## Outside Homer

The Hesiodic *Catalogue of Women* gives Helen a son as well: “she bore Hermione to Menelaus, famed for the spear; / her youngest child was Nicostratus, a shoot of Ares” (fr. 70 Evelyn-White, from a scholium on Sophocles, *Electra* 539; a plain rendering; see [[hesiod]]); a papyrus of the same poem has Helen bear “lovely-ankled Hermione in the halls, / unexpectedly” (fr. 68, part B, Evelyn-White, from the Berlin papyri).

The scholium on *Odyssey* 4.4 and Eustathius (p. 1479) cite Sophocles' lost *Hermione* for the story that Tyndareus gave her to Orestes while Menelaus was still at Troy, and that she was afterward taken from him and given to Neoptolemus by the promise made at Troy (as Merry reports them, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 4.11; see [[sophocles]] and [[orestes]]). In Euripides' *Andromache* (see [[euripides]] and [[andromache]]) she is the childless wife of Neoptolemus at Phthia, who came from Sparta with rich gifts from her father and blames the drugs of the captive Andromache for her barrenness (147–158); Orestes says that Menelaus had given her to him before he invaded Troy, then promised her to Neoptolemus if he sacked the city (966–981), and he takes her away to her father while he contrives Neoptolemus' death at Delphi (984–1008). In the *Orestes* Apollo declares that Orestes is fated to marry her, and that Neoptolemus, who thinks to marry her, never will (1653–1657). In the *Helen*, set in Egypt when Menelaus comes there from Troy, Helen grieves that her daughter grows gray unmarried (282–283, 688–690), a passage Merry cites to show that Hermione was past childhood when her mother fled (on 4.11).
