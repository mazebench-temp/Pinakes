Sarpedon has just seen Glaucus leave the wall, wounded in the arm by Teucer's arrow (12.387–392; see [[glaucus]] and [[sarpedon]]), and his answer to grief is a kill: “but even so he did not forget the joy of battle” (12.393; see [[achos]] and [[charme]]). He draws the spear out, and Alcmaon follows it and falls headlong; “around him his armor rang, inlaid with bronze” (12.396), the half-verse of the falls of Imbrius (13.181) and of Hector under Ajax's stone (14.420; see [[inlaid]]). Then Sarpedon pulls down the battlement (12.397–399; see [[battlements]]).

Θεστορίδης, “son of Thestor”, is also the patronymic of the seer Calchas (1.69; see [[calchas]] and [[thestor]]). Whether Alcmaon's father is the father of Calchas the poem does not say; another Thestor, son of Enops, is killed by Patroclus (16.401–410). The name Ἀλκμάων belongs, like Alcmene's (14.323), to the family of ἀλκή, “strength” (see [[alke]]); the Alcmaeon of the Odyssey, son of Amphiaraus, is another man (Od. 15.248).
