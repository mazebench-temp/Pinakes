## Etymology

Τληπόλεμος = τλῆναι ("to endure") + πόλεμος ("war"): "he who endures war", a fitting name for a warrior.

## Family

{{quote:7204b3d1-57f6-547e-89a1-99d35a8e7a64}}

Son of [[Heracles]] by [[Astyocheia]], whom Heracles carried off from [[Ephyra]] on the river [[selleis-ephyra|Selleis]]. Ἡρακλεΐδης, "son of Heracles" (2.653), is his patronymic. βίῃ Ἡρακληείῃ, "to the might of Heracles", is the epic way of naming the hero by his strength (see [[might-of-heracles]]). The next contingent in the Catalogue is also led by descendants of Heracles, his grandsons [[Pheidippus]] and [[Antiphus]] (2.678–679).

## Descriptions

ἠΰς τε μέγας τε, "tall and brave" (2.653): ἠΰς ("good, noble") and μέγας ("big"). The same words introduce him again in Book 5 (5.628). δουρὶ κλυτός, "famed for his spear" (2.657; see [[famed-for-his-spear]]).

## In the Iliad

The Catalogue gives him a whole history: the killing of Licymnius, the flight, and the settling of Rhodes (2.661–670; see [[the-flight-of-tlepolemus]]).

He appears once more. In Book 5 "mighty fate" drives him against [[Sarpedon]], and the narrator marks the meeting of "the son and the grandson of Zeus the cloud-gatherer" (5.628–631). Tlepolemus taunts Sarpedon that he is no true son of Zeus beside Heracles, who once sacked Troy with only six ships (5.633–646). They throw at the same moment. Sarpedon's spear goes through his neck and kills him; his own spear pierces Sarpedon's thigh (5.655–662).

## Outside Homer

Pindar's seventh *Olympian*, for the Rhodian boxer Diagoras (464 BC), makes Tlepolemus the founder of Rhodes. It tells that he killed Licymnius at Tiryns in anger, striking him with a staff of olive wood, and that Apollo's oracle sent him to the island (*Olympian* 7.27–33). Pindar calls his mother Astydameia, not Astyocheia (7.24). The Rhodians honored him "as a god", with sacrifices and games (7.77–80). In Apollodorus the killing is an accident: Licymnius ran between Tlepolemus and a servant he was beating (2.8.2).
