Agrius is the first named of the three sons of Portheus who “lived in Pleuron and steep Calydon” (14.116–117; see [[portheus]], [[pleuron]] and [[calydon]]). Diomedes names him only to come to the third son, his grandfather Oeneus, who “in prowess … stood out among them” and stayed in Aetolia when Tydeus left for Argos (14.117–119; see [[oeneus]], [[tydeus]] and [[diomedes]]). The poem says nothing more of him.

The name is the adjective ἄγριος, “wild, savage”, which Homer uses of a boar, of anger and of a heart: the “wild boar” that Artemis sent against Calydon in Oeneus' time (9.539), the “savage anger” that gripped Hera (4.23), the “savage” heart of Achilles (9.629).

## Outside Homer

Strabo, discussing the Curetes of Pleuron, reports a division of Aetolia in which Oeneus held the country around Calydon, and the sons of Porthaon, “those around Agrius”, held a part of the land of Pleuron (*Geography* 10.3.6; see [[strabo]] and [[curetes]]).
