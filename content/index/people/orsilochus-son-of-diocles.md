He dies with his twin brother, and the story is theirs together: they came to Troy for the sons of Atreus, and Aeneas killed them both (5.541–560; see [[crethon]] and [[diocles]]).

**The name.** The grandson's name, Ὀρσίλοχος, is close to the grandfather's, Ὀρτίλοχος (5.546–547), and the two are probably forms of one name, joining λόχος, “armed band, ambush”, to a verb of rousing (ὄρνυμι, aorist ὦρσα): “rouser of the band”. The Odyssey spells the grandfather's name the same way as the Iliad (Od. 3.489, 15.187, 21.16).

The name was not rare. Teucer kills a Trojan Orsilochus (8.274), and in one of his lying tales Odysseus claims to have killed Orsilochus, a swift-footed son of Idomeneus, in Crete (Od. 13.259–261).
