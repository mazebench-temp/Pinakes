He is the fourth man Odysseus strikes in his stand alone, the last before the brothers Charops and Socus (11.420–427; see [[odysseus]], [[charops]] and [[socus]]). He leaps down from his chariot to fight, and the spear takes him κατὰ πρότμησιν, a word found only here, usually explained as the navel or the narrowing of the waist (11.423–424; see the translation's note on 11.424). ὃ δʼ ἐν κονίῃσι πεσὼν ἕλε γαῖαν ἀγοστῷ, “and he fell in the dust and clutched the earth in his palm” (11.425), is a death-verse that returns four times in later books (13.508, 13.520, 14.452, 17.315; see [[death-in-battle]]).

The name seems to join χείρ, “hand”, to the stem of δάμνημι, “subdue”, like Iphidamas and Polydamas (see [[iphidamas]] and [[polydamas]]). He is named nowhere else.
