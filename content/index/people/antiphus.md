Several men in Homer have this name. Another Antiphus, a son of Talaemenes, leads the Maeonians in the Trojan Catalogue (2.864; see [[antiphus-of-maeonia]]), and Agamemnon kills Antiphus, a son of Priam, in *Iliad* 11 (11.101–121). The Odyssey has two more: [[antiphus-son-of-aegyptius|Antiphus, son of Aegyptius]], the spearman whom the Cyclops ate (*Odyssey* 2.17–20), and an old companion of Odysseus in Ithaca (*Odyssey* 17.68). This one, a Heraclid from Cos, is named only here.
