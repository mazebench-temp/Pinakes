πρῶτ' ἐμβασίλευεν: "was king at first", or "formerly". The Catalogue ties him to [[Sicyon]]; Homer never calls him king of Argos.

## Elsewhere in Homer

Tydeus married one of Adrastus' daughters, so that Diomedes is his grandson (14.121). His horse Arion, "the swift horse of Adrastus, of divine race", was the fastest ever known (23.346–347). Two Trojans have almost the same name: Adrestus, son of Merops, who leads the men of Adrasteia (2.830; [[adrestus]]), and the Adrestus whom Menelaus takes alive and Agamemnon kills (6.37–65).

## Outside Homer

In the epic of the Theban war, and in tragedy after it, Adrastus is king of Argos, father-in-law of Tydeus and Polynices, and leader of the Seven. He alone survived the disaster, carried from the field by Arion, and in the next generation he went with the sons of the Seven, the Epigoni, when they took Thebes. Pindar says that he founded games at Sicyon while he ruled there, having fled from Amphiaraus at Argos (*Nemean* 9.9–14). At Sicyon he had a hero shrine and tragic choruses until the tyrant Cleisthenes drove his cult out (Herodotus 5.67).
