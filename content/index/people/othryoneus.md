**The suitor.** Othryoneus is one of the Iliad's latecomers. Καβησόθεν, “from Cabesus”, names a place found nowhere else in Homer (see [[cabesus]]). μετὰ κλέος, “at the report of the war”, is how Iphidamas came to Troy from his bride in Thrace, “at the report of the Achaeans” (11.227; see [[iphidamas]] and [[trojan-allies]]). He asked for Cassandra ἀνάεδνον, “without bride-price”, the word of Agamemnon's offer to Achilles of any daughter he liked (9.146, 9.288), and offered a deed in place of gifts (see [[cassandra]] and [[bride-price]]). The praise of her beauty, θυγατρῶν εἶδος ἀρίστην, is given also to her sister Laodice (3.124, 6.252; see [[laodice]] and [[best-in-looks]]). Priam's promise, ὑπό τʼ ἔσχετο καὶ κατένευσε, is made in the words of the promises of Zeus that Agamemnon thought deceitful and Hector trusted (2.112, 9.19, 12.236; see [[priam]]), and Othryoneus fought “trusting in the promises” (13.369).

**His death.** Idomeneus, half gray as he is (see [[half-gray]]), leaps among the Trojans, and his spear catches Othryoneus “as he came striding high”: the bronze corselet does not hold, and the spear stands in the middle of his belly (13.361–373; see [[idomeneus]] and [[corselet]]). The two verses of the wound are used again a moment later of Asius' charioteer (13.371–372 = 13.397–398). Idomeneus answers the bargain with another:

{{quote:c11cf530-7db5-5f74-82a6-4ee0e576c89c}}

The Achaeans offer what Priam promised, a king's fairest daughter, for the same service turned the other way. ἐεδνωταί, “givers of bride-gifts”, occurs only here (see the translation's note on 13.382 and [[laodice-daughter-of-agamemnon]]). The taunt dwells on promising, ὑπέστης, ὑπέσχετο, ὑποσχόμενοι (13.375–377); Idomeneus himself once pledged his loyalty to Agamemnon with ὑπέστην καὶ κατένευσα, “as I promised at first and nodded my assent” (4.267). He drags the body by the foot through the battle, and Asius comes on foot to defend it (13.383–384; see [[asius]]).

**Afterward.** Rebuking Paris, Hector asks for him by name after the sons of Priam and of Asius: “And where is Othryoneus?” (13.772; see [[hector]]). Paris answers that the companions he asks about have been killed (13.780).

## Outside Homer

[[Strabo]] quotes the lines on Othryoneus' suit (13.363–366) to show that Homer knew Cassandra as a maiden at this time and knew nothing of her violation at the sack (*Geography* 13.1.40; see [[strabo-on-cassandra]] and [[cassandra]]).
