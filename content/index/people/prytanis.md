He closes Odysseus' list of seven Lycians (5.677–678; see [[lycians]] and [[odysseus]]). The name is a word for a ruler, πρύτανις, “lord, chief”, which Homer uses only here and only as a name. In later Greek it was a title: at Athens the prytaneis were the members of the council who presided over it in turn, and the Prytaneion was the hall where the city kept its hearth. The origin of the word is unknown.

## Outside Homer

Ovid's Ulysses names all seven Lycians among the men he has killed, in Homer's order (*Metamorphoses* 13; see [[ovid]]).
