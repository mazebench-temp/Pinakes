νόθος υἱός, "bastard son": his mother [[Rhene]] was not Oileus' wife. Book 13 tells more. Medon lived at [[Phylace]], far from his homeland, because he had killed a kinsman of his stepmother Eriopis (γνωτός, which can mean a brother), the wife of Oileus; he fights beside [[Podarces]] at the head of the Phthians (13.693–697). Aeneas kills him in Book 15, and the lines about his exile are repeated (15.332–336).

His story is a common one in the Iliad. A man who has killed a kinsman flees and finds a new home under a new lord, as [[Tlepolemus]] did (2.661–667) and as Patroclus did at the house of Peleus (23.85–90).
