νόθος υἱός, "bastard son": his mother [[Rhene]] was not Oileus' wife. *Iliad* 13 tells more. Medon lived at [[Phylace]], far from his homeland, because he had killed a kinsman of his stepmother Eriopis (γνωτός, which can mean a brother), the wife of Oileus; he fights beside [[Podarces]] at the head of the Phthians (13.693–697). Aeneas kills him in Book 15, and the lines about his exile are repeated (15.332–336). The Medon whom Hector urges on in Book 17 is an ally of the Trojans (17.216; see [[medon-ally-of-the-trojans]]).

His story is a common one in the Iliad. A man who has killed a kinsman flees and finds a new home under a new lord, as [[Tlepolemus]] did (2.661–667) and as Patroclus did at the house of Peleus (23.85–90). The Medon of the Odyssey is another man, the herald of Odysseus' house (see [[medon-the-herald]]).
