## The name

The Iliad gives the same name to an Achaean, the companion of Lycomedes, whom Aeneas kills in the fight over Patroclus (*Iliad* 17.344–345; see [[leiocritus]], where the name is discussed). The suitor is another man.

## Family

He is named with his father, Εὐηνορίδης Λειώκριτος, “Leiocritus, son of Euenor” (*Odyssey* 2.242), and the patronymic returns when he dies (*Odyssey* 22.294; see [[euenor]]).

## Elsewhere in Homer

In the slaughter of the suitors Telemachus stabs him with his spear in the middle of the flank and drives the bronze clean through, and he falls on his face and strikes the ground with his whole forehead (*Odyssey* 22.294–296, a plain rendering; see [[telemachus]] and [[the-suitors]]).
