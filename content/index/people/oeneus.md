μεγαλήτωρ "great-hearted" ([[great-hearted]]). The name comes from οἶνος, "wine". The Catalogue names him only to say that his line has ended: "the sons of great-hearted Oeneus were no more, nor was he himself still living, and fair-haired Meleager was dead" (2.641–642). So the [[Aetolians]] follow Thoas.

## Elsewhere in Homer

In Phoenix's story, Oeneus forgot to offer the first fruits of the harvest to Artemis, and she sent the wild boar that ravaged Calydon (9.533–549; see [[the-wrath-of-meleager]]). He once entertained Bellerophon for twenty days and exchanged gifts with him, a belt of purple and a golden cup, which is why Diomedes and Glaucus are guest-friends (6.215–221). Diomedes gives his genealogy: Portheus had three sons, Agrius, Melas and Oeneus, who lived in Pleuron and steep Calydon, and Oeneus, the best of them, was the father of Tydeus (14.115–118).

## Outside Homer

Later tradition made Oeneus the first man to receive the vine from Dionysus (Apollodorus 1.8.1). His wife was Althaea, mother of Meleager and of Deianeira, the wife of Heracles. In his old age the sons of his brother Agrius deposed him, and Diomedes avenged him (Apollodorus 1.8.6).
