The name means "swift with horses" (ἵππος, "horse" + θοός, "swift"). He is named twice, first as leader of the Pelasgians and then with his brother. In Book 17 he has tied a strap around the ankle of Patroclus' body and is dragging it toward the Trojans when Ajax's spear drives through his helmet. He falls on the body "far from deep-soiled Larisa", and "he did not repay his dear parents for his rearing" (17.288–303). A son of Priam of the same name appears in Book 24 (24.251).
