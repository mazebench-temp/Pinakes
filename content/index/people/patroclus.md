## Etymology

Πάτροκλος (Patroklos), also Πατροκλέης (vocative Πατρόκλεες, 1.337) = πατήρ (father) + κλέος (glory): "glory of the fathers".

## In Book 1

{{quote:d78ebeff-4c18-532f-b838-4fb27dffd8fd}}

Μενοιτιάδης = Μενοίτιος + -ιάδης: "son of Menoetius". He is named first by his father, before we are told who he is.

{{quote:a268189f-d04d-5fba-b38a-63135575496a}}

διογενές: "sprung from Zeus", an honorific. Achilles gives him the order, and he does not answer.

{{quote:f2cad32a-c404-5821-9596-d2679c4f7b03}}

"He obeyed his dear companion" (φίλῳ ἑταίρῳ). In this book he carries out an order without speaking. His role grows until his death in Book 16, which brings Achilles back to the war.

## Outside Homer

Patroclus came to Peleus' house as a boy after killing another boy over a game of dice (Iliad 23.84–90). He fights in Achilles' armor, is killed by Hector (16.818–857), and Achilles' grief for him turns his wrath against the Trojans.
