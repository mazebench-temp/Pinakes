He is the second of the four Trojans whom Ajax wounds after killing Doryclus, Priam's bastard son: “and wounded Lysander, and Pyrasus, and Pylartes” (11.491; see [[doryclus]], [[pandocus]], [[pyrasus-the-trojan]], [[pylartes]] and [[ajax]]). The poem does not say whether he died. Then Ajax sweeps across the plain like a winter torrent carrying oaks and pines down to the sea (11.492–497). The name seems to join λύω, “loose, release”, to ἀνήρ, “man”; no one else bears it in Homer.
