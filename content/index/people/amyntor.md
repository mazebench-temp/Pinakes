Ἀμύντωρ is the common noun ἀμύντωρ, “defender”, from ἀμύνω, “ward off”, a verb that runs through Phoenix's plea to Achilles (9.435, 9.495, 9.518, 9.602). Homer tells of him only through his son. He loved a fair-haired concubine and dishonored his wife; when his son, at his mother's urging, lay with the girl, he called on the Erinyes and cursed him to have no son, and the gods below fulfilled the curse (9.449–457; see [[the-flight-of-phoenix]], [[concubines]], [[curses]] and [[erinyes]]). He was rich: while his kinsmen kept Phoenix at home, “much wine was drunk from the old man's jars” (9.469).

In Book 10 the helmet set with boar's tusks that Meriones lends Odysseus for the night raid has a history. Autolycus broke into Amyntor's house at Eleon and stole it, and it passed by gift to Amphidamas of Cythera, to Molus, and to Molus' son Meriones (10.260–271; see [[the-boars-tusk-helmet]], [[helmet]] and [[meriones]]). Eleon is a Boeotian town in the Catalogue (2.500; see [[eleon]]), but in Book 9 Phoenix leaves Hellas and flees through it to Phthia (9.447, 9.478; see [[hellas]]).

## Outside Homer

In later versions Amyntor blinds his son. Euripides' lost *Phoenix* showed Phoenix blind, and in Apollodorus the concubine, Phthia, falsely accuses him of seducing her (*Library* 3.13.8; see [[phoenix]]). Ancient scholars disputed where he lived. Demetrius of Scepsis placed him at Ormenium in Thessaly and made his brother Euaemon the father of Eurypylus; Crates put Eleon on Parnassus, near the home of Autolycus; others read “from Heleon”, a town of Tanagra (Strabo 9.5.18; see [[hellas-or-ormenium]], [[ormenium]] and [[euaemon]]).
