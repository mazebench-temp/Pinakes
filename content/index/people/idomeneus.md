## Family

He is Δευκαλίδης, “son of Deucalion” (12.117, 13.307, 17.608; see [[deucalion]]), and over the body of Alcathous he gives the whole line: Zeus fathered Minos, Minos Deucalion, and Deucalion him, “a lord over many men in wide Crete” (13.449–453; see [[minos]] and [[genealogy-in-battle]]). In the Odyssey, Odysseus, posing as a Cretan, makes Minos his grandfather and Idomeneus his elder brother, “older and better” (Od. 19.178–184). His companion and second in command is Meriones, son of Molus (2.650–651, 13.249; see [[molus]]).

## Descriptions

- δουρὶ κλυτός or δουρικλυτός, “famed for his spear”, from the Catalogue on (2.645, 2.650; 5.45; 13.210; see [[famed-for-his-spear]]); Κρητῶν ἀγός, “leader of the Cretans” (4.265; 13.221; 23.450), and Κρητῶν βουληφόρος, “counselor of the Cretans” (13.219, 13.255; see [[cretans]]).
- ἄναξ, “lord” (2.405, 10.112, 15.301); ἀρήϊος, “warlike” (11.501; see [[warlike]]); δαΐφρων, “wise-hearted” (4.252; see [[wise-hearted]]); μεσαιπόλιος, “half gray” (13.361), only here in Homer.

## In the Iliad

**One of the elders.** He leads the Cretans of “Crete of the hundred cities” in eighty ships, a fleet exceeded only by Agamemnon's and Nestor's (2.645–652; see [[crete]]). Agamemnon names him with Ajax and Odysseus as a man of counsel fit to command the ship to Chryse (1.144–145), calls him second, after Nestor, to the sacrifice of the elders (2.404–405), and honors him above all the Danaans, with a cup that “stands full, as mine does” (4.257–263; see [[elders]]). Helen picks him out unasked from the wall and remembers that Menelaus often entertained him (3.230–233; see [[xenia]]). After the reconciliation he is one of the six who stay with Achilles (19.309–312).

**In battle.** He volunteers to face Hector (7.165) and follows Diomedes across the ditch (8.263), though he ran when Zeus thundered (8.78); beside Nestor by the Scamander he sends the old man to carry the wounded Machaon to the ships (11.501, 11.510–516). His great day is the battle at the ships, when Poseidon rouses him and he kills Othryoneus, Asius and Alcathous before he gives ground to the younger Aeneas (13.210–520; see [[the-battle-at-the-ships]]). Over Patroclus' body he comes on foot against Hector, until Meriones' charioteer Coeranus, driving up to rescue him, is killed, and he flees to the ships (17.605–625).

**Age.** He is no longer young: half gray, afraid of Aeneas' youth, slow on his feet (13.361, 13.481–486, 13.512–515). At the funeral games the son of Oileus mocks him as neither the youngest of the Argives nor the sharpest-eyed, when he is the first to make out the leading horses; Achilles stops the quarrel, and Diomedes, whom Idomeneus named, comes in first (23.448–513; see [[ajax-son-of-oileus]]).

## In the Odyssey

Nestor tells Telemachus that Idomeneus brought home to Crete all his companions who had escaped the war, and the sea took none (Od. 3.191–192). Crete is Idomeneus' country in Odysseus' false tales: he killed Orsilochus, Idomeneus' son (the only son Homer names), who tried to rob him of his Trojan spoils because he had not served under Idomeneus at Troy (Od. 13.259–270); the people made him and Idomeneus lead the ships to Ilios (Od. 14.237–239); and he is Aethon, Idomeneus' younger brother, who entertained Odysseus in Crete after Idomeneus had sailed (Od. 19.178–193).

## Outside Homer

In the Hesiodic *Catalogue of Women* he is a suitor of Helen, “Deucalion's son, offspring of far-famed Minos”, who sent no one to woo for him but crossed the sea himself to the house of Tyndareus (fr. 68 Evelyn-White); the fragment calls him μέγα σθένος Ἰδομενῆος, “the great might of Idomeneus”, with the name partly restored, in the Iliad's phrase (13.248). Apollodorus names Idomeneus, Crete and a bastard son, Molus, as Deucalion's children (3.3.1). Herodotus reports the people of Praesus as saying that the Trojan war came in the third generation after Minos died, that the Cretans were not the worst of Menelaus' helpers, and that on their return famine and plague fell on them and their flocks (7.171.1–2). In the *Aeneid* the Trojans hear that Idomeneus has been driven from his father's kingdom and has left Crete (3.121–123), and Helenus tells Aeneas that Lyctius Idomeneus, Idomeneus “of Lyctus”, has occupied the Sallentine plains of Italy with his soldiers (3.400–401); Lyctus is one of his towns in the Catalogue (2.647).
