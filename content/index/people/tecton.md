## The name

τέκτων is a builder, a worker in wood, the maker of houses and ships: the Cyclopes have no shipwrights, ἄνδρες νηῶν … τέκτονες, to build them ships (*Odyssey* 9.125–127, a plain rendering). W. W. Merry counts the Phaeacian names among those “taken from circumstances of seafaring life” (*Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on *Odyssey* 8.111).

The verse that names him has a likeness in the Iliad, where the same word stands in a chain of father and son: Meriones slew “Phereclus, son of the builder, / the son of Harmon”, Φέρεκλον … τέκτονος υἱὸν / Ἁρμονίδεω (*Iliad* 5.59–60), and Phereclus himself built the ships of Alexander (*Iliad* 5.62–63; see [[phereclus]]). There the translation takes τέκτων as the father's trade, though some take it as a name, “son of Tecton, son of Harmon” (see [[harmon]]); here the patronymic Τεκτονίδαο makes it a name.
