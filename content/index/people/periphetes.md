Τεῦκρος δὲ Προθόωνά τʼ ἐνήρατο καὶ Περιφήτην, “and Teucer slew Prothoon and Periphetes” (14.515; see [[teucer]] and [[prothoon]]). He is named only here.

Homer's other Periphetes is an Achaean of Mycenae, the son of Copreus, who carried the messages of Eurystheus to Heracles. The son was far better than the father in every excellence, in speed and in fighting, and in mind among the first of the Mycenaeans; he trips on the rim of his own shield and is killed by Hector (15.638–652; see [[eurystheus]] and [[heracles]]).
