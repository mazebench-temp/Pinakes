He is the first of Bellerophon's three children (6.197). When the gods turn against his father, Ares kills him and Artemis kills his sister. The name seems to be formed from ἴσος (“equal”) and ἀνήρ (“man”). His killer is “Ares, insatiable of war” (see [[insatiable-of-war]]), and he dies fighting the people his father had fought for the king of Lycia (6.184; see [[solymi]]). Whether the god himself killed him, or his name stands for war, as it often does, the poem leaves open (see [[ares]]). The sons of his brother [[Hippolochus]] and his sister [[Laodameia]], Glaucus and Sarpedon, lead the Lycians at Troy. See [[bellerophon]].

Strabo twice quotes the line with Πείσανδρον, “Peisander”, for Ἴσανδρον, and says that his tomb lay near Termessus in Pisidia, beside the palisade of Bellerophon (12.8.5; 13.4.16).
