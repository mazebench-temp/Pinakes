Φηρητιάδης = Φέρης + -ιάδης: "descendant of Pheres". It is used here of his grandson Eumelus, as Αἰακίδης, "descendant of Aeacus", is used of Achilles (2.860). In the Odyssey, Pheres is a son of Tyro and Cretheus, a brother of Aeson and Amythaon and a half-brother of Pelias and Neleus (*Odyssey* 11.258–259; see [[tyro]], [[cretheus]], [[aeson]], [[amythaon]], [[pelias]] and [[neleus]]), so that Eumelus descends from Tyro through both his father and his mother, Alcestis, the daughter of Pelias. His name goes with that of [[Pherae]], and later writers made him its founder. In Euripides' *Alcestis* he refuses to die for his son, and father and son quarrel bitterly over Alcestis' body (614–733).
