“And around the son of Creon, brilliant Lycomedes” (9.84) is the last line of the list of captains who lead the sentries out on the night of the embassy (see [[lycomedes]] and [[night-watch]]). The poem says nothing else of the father. In Book 19 the son is called Κρειοντιάδης, “son of Creon”, among the young men who carry Agamemnon's gifts into the assembly (19.240).

The name is the word κρείων, “ruler, lord”, which Homer uses as a title: “powerful Agamemnon” (κρείων Ἀγαμέμνων, 9.62, 9.368; see [[powerful]]), and Zeus is “highest of lords” (ὕπατε κρειόντων, 8.31). Other men bore it. Among the heroines Odysseus sees in the underworld is Megara, “daughter of high-hearted Creon”, the wife of Heracles (Od. 11.269–270). In tragedy that Creon, son of Menoeceus, is lord of Thebes, where Heracles married his daughter (Euripides, *Heracles* 8–12), the brother of Jocasta (Euripides, *Phoenician Women* 10–11), and the king who has the body of Polynices cast out unburied (*Phoenician Women* 1627–1633; Sophocles, *Antigone*; see [[sophocles]] and [[euripides]]).
