πὺξ μὲν ἐνίκησα Κλυτομήδεα Ἤνοπος υἱόν, “In boxing I defeated Clytomedes, son of Enops” (23.634): the first of the four victories that Nestor claims at the games of Amarynceus, and the only thing Homer tells of him (see [[the-funeral-games-of-amarynceus]], [[nestor]] and [[enops-father-of-clytomedes]]). Nestor names the peoples of his rivals, Epeians, Pylians and Aetolians (23.632–633), but not which of them Clytomedes was.

The name joins κλυτός, “famous”, and μήδεα, “counsels”: “famous for counsel”.

The boxing at Buprasium is the first contest in Nestor's list, as it is the first of the contests Achilles names when he gives the old man his prize without a contest: “for you will not fight with fists” (23.621). At Patroclus' games the boxing is won by Epeius (23.653–699; see [[epeius]]).
