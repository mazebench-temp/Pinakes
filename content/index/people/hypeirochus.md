He dies in a single verse, the sixth man whom Diomedes and Odysseus kill in their stand against Hector's charge, after Thymbraeus, Molion, the two sons of Merops and Hippodamus; Agastrophus follows (11.320–342; see [[odysseus-and-diomedes]] and [[the-sons-of-merops]]). The name is the adjective ὑπείροχος, “excelling”, the word of the charge that Hippolochus gave Glaucus and Peleus gave Achilles, “always to be the best and to excel above the others” (6.208, 11.784; see [[always-to-be-the-best]]). In Nestor's story, later in the book, the Elean Itymoneus is “the brave son of Hypeirochus” (11.672–673; see [[itymoneus]]), another man of the same name.
