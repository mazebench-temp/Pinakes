**The mother of Pelegon.** Periboea is named once, in the three verses with which the narrator introduces Asteropaeus when Achilles leaps at him in the river: the Axius begot Pelegon, “and Periboea, of the daughters of Acessamenus / the eldest; for with her the deep-eddying river lay” (21.142–143; see [[pelegon]], [[axius]], [[acessamenus]], [[asteropaeus]] and [[deep-eddying]]). When Asteropaeus tells his lineage himself he names only the river and his father (21.157–160; see [[genealogy-in-battle]]). A river god's union with a mortal woman founds a family elsewhere in the Iliad too: Polydora, the daughter of Peleus, bore Menesthius to the Spercheius, “a woman who lay with a god” (16.175–176; see [[polydora]] and [[spercheius]]).

**The Periboea of the Odyssey.** Walter Leaf notes that neither Pelegon nor Acessamenus recurs in Homer, but that the Odyssey tells of another Periboea honored with a like union with a god (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 21.141; see the translation's note on 21.141–143). The two verses begin with the same words. Athena tells Odysseus that the line of Alcinous goes back to Nausithous, whom Poseidon begot, γείνατο καὶ Περίβοια, “and Periboea bore”, the best of women in beauty and the youngest daughter of Eurymedon, king of the Giants; Poseidon lay with her (ἐμίγη, the verb of 21.143) and begot Nausithous, who ruled among the Phaeacians (*Odyssey* 7.54–62). The one Periboea is the eldest of her father's daughters and the other the youngest, and each bears a god's son who heads a line of kings and heroes.
