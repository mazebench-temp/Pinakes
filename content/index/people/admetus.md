Homer names Admetus only as Eumelus' father. The name is the adjective ἄδμητος, "untamed" (ἀ- "not" + δάμνημι "tame").

Later legend is richer. Apollo, made to serve a mortal for a year after he killed the Cyclopes, kept Admetus' herds (Euripides, *Alcestis* 1–9; Apollodorus 3.10.4). With Apollo's help Admetus won Alcestis from her father Pelias, who had promised her to the man who could yoke a lion and a boar to a chariot (Apollodorus 1.9.15). When his fated day came, the gods allowed another to die for him; his parents refused, and Alcestis went in his place. In Euripides' play Heracles wrestles with Death at her tomb and brings her back. Admetus was also counted among the Argonauts (Apollonius, *Argonautica* 1.49–50).
