**Nine names.** After Pronous, Thestor and Erylaus, whose deaths are told one by one, Patroclus kills nine men whose names fill three verses, with no blow told (see [[patroclus]], [[pronous]], [[thestor-son-of-enops]] and [[erylaus]]). Erymas heads the list: αὐτὰρ ἔπειτʼ Ἐρύμαντα καὶ Ἀμφοτερὸν καὶ Ἐπάλτην, “Then Erymas and Amphoterus and Epaltes” (16.415). Then come Tlepolemus, son of Damastor, Echius, Pyris, Ipheus, Euippus, and Polymelus, son of Argeas (see [[amphoterus]], [[epaltes]], [[tlepolemus-son-of-damastor]], [[echius-killed-by-patroclus]], [[pyris]], [[ipheus]], [[euippus]] and [[polymelus]]), and one verse closes the list: “all of them, one after another, he brought down to the bountiful earth” (16.418; see [[bountiful-earth]]). The same verse closes the list of Teucer's eight victims in Book 8 and that of Leonteus' victims in Book 12, where it follows a verse of the same shape: αὐτὰρ ἔπειτα Μένωνα καὶ Ἰαμενὸν καὶ Ὀρέστην, “and then Menon and Iamenus and Orestes” (8.273–277, 12.193–194; see [[androktasia]] and [[leonteus]]).

Then Sarpedon sees “his companions, who wear their tunics without the band, / brought down at the hands of Patroclus”, and leaps from his chariot to meet him (16.419–427; see [[sarpedon]] and [[lycians]]).

**Nine.** Hector's victims in Book 11 are nine men named in three verses (11.301–303), and so are Patroclus' in his pursuit of the Trojans toward the city (16.694–696; see [[whom-first-whom-last]]). The Achaean leaders have killed nine men one by one at the ships (16.306–351), and in Patroclus' last charge “three times he killed nine men” (16.785).

**Two men named Erymas.** Idomeneus has killed another Erymas seventy verses before (16.345; see [[erymas-killed-by-idomeneus]]). Walter Leaf, who thought this passage mechanical, remarks that the poem usually avoids repeating a name at so short an interval, even a minor one (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 16.411).
