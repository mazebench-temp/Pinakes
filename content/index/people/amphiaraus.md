The Odyssey calls him dear to Zeus and Apollo, a man who “did not reach the threshold of old age, but died at Thebes because of a woman's gifts” (*Odyssey* 15.244–247). As the Seven fled, Zeus split the earth with his thunderbolt and swallowed him with his chariot and horses (Pindar, *Nemean* 9.24–27; see [[the-seven-against-thebes]]); his oracle and healing sanctuary stood at Oropus. In the *Poetics* Carcinus' play failed because “Amphiaraus was coming back up from the temple”, an incongruity the poet missed by not visualizing the scene (1455a26–29; see [[carcinus]] and [[before-the-eyes]]).
