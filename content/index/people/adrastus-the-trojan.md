{{quote:498cd375-59c1-5a73-8a62-a863211e30e5}}

The rout undoes him. His horses bolt across the plain, catch the chariot in a tamarisk branch and snap the pole, and run on to the city without him; he is thrown out beside the wheel, face down in the dust (6.38–43). μυρίκινος, “of tamarisk”, occurs only here; the tamarisks of the Iliad grow on the plain and along the Scamander (10.466–467, 21.18, 21.350).

{{quote:a09c4a8d-5d7c-5156-ad44-7bdf2a0389fd}}

He clasps Menelaus' knees and offers what a captive can offer: ζώγρει, “take me alive”, and a ransom from his father's store of bronze, gold and iron (see [[supplication]], [[gounata]] and [[apoina]]). The same words serve Dolon (10.378–381) and the sons of Antimachus, pleading with Agamemnon (11.131–135), and none of them is spared. Of the men they beg, only Menelaus is moved, and Agamemnon forbids it and kills Adrastus himself (6.51–65; see [[menelaus]] and [[agamemnon]]). As Menelaus pushes him away, the narrator calls him “the hero Adrastus” (ἥρωʼ Ἄδρηστον, 6.63; see [[heros]]). His father is “wealthy” but unnamed (6.47), and Adrastus himself occurs only here.
