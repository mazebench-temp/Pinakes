# Ναυβολίδης at Od. 8.116 is the Phaeacian Euryalus, son of another Naubolus (naubolus-father-of-euryalus).
except: [Od. 8.116]

{{quote:479a7019-4767-5bc5-9911-f7d06cbb7533}}

Ναυβολίδης: "son of Naubolus". The name seems to join ναῦς, "ship", and βάλλω, "throw, launch". Through him the Catalogue traces the Phocian leaders back three generations: Naubolus, Iphitus, and his sons Schedius and Epistrophus. The Odyssey's Naubolus is another man, [[naubolus-father-of-euryalus|a Phaeacian]] known, like this one, only through a patronymic: Ναυβολίδης, “the son of Naubolus”, is the young Euryalus who taunts Odysseus at the games (*Odyssey* 8.116; see [[euryalus-the-phaeacian]]).
