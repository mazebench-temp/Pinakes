Οἰνόμαόν τε, “and Oenomaus” (5.706): he is named without an epithet, a father or a home, in the list of those killed by Hector and Ares, who are not told apart (see [[hector]] and [[ares]]). The name is usually explained as οἶνος (“wine”) + μαίομαι (“strive after”).

A Trojan Oenomaus attacks the Achaean wall with Asius (12.140) and is killed by Idomeneus with a spear through the belly (13.506–508). The best-known bearer of the name, Oenomaus king of Pisa, whose daughter Hippodameia Pelops won in a chariot race, does not appear in Homer.
