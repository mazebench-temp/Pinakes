Menelaus' blow takes a verse and a half: “warlike Menelaus stabbed Thoas / in the chest, where it was bared beside the shield, and loosened his limbs” (16.311–312; see [[menelaus]] and [[loosened-his-limbs]]). Thoas is the second of the nine men whom the Achaean leaders kill one by one when the Trojans are driven from the ships (16.306–351; see [[areilycus-killed-by-patroclus]]). The second verse returns whole for Pronous, the first man Patroclus kills when he pens the Trojans between the ships, the river and the wall (16.400 = 16.312; see [[pronous]]).

Homer's other men of the name are the Aetolian Thoas, son of Andraemon, a leader of the Achaeans (2.638; see [[thoas]]), and the king of Lemnos, whom later writers make the father of Hypsipyle (14.230; see [[thoas-of-lemnos]]). Θόας is formed from θοός, “swift”.
