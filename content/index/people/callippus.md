Plutarch tells how Callippus, Dion's friend and host at Athens, who had shared his initiation into the Mysteries, plotted against him in Syracuse and had him killed in his own house by soldiers from Zacynthus (*Dion* 54–57). He held the city for some thirteen months before he was driven out, and was killed, it was said, with the same dagger that had killed Dion (58). He is not the Callippus whose rhetorical handbook the *Rhetoric*'s second book cites (2.23, 1399a17–18, 1400a3–4; see [[callippus-the-rhetorician]]).
