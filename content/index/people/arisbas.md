# Ἀρίσβαντος (17.345) occurs only here in Homer; Ἀρίσβην and Ἀρίσβηθεν (2.836, 2.838 …) are the town Arisbe.

υἱὸν Ἀρίσβαντος Λυκομήδεος ἐσθλὸν ἑταῖρον, “the son of Arisbas, the brave companion of Lycomedes” (17.345): the father is named at his son's death, and nothing more is told of him (see [[leiocritus]] and [[lycomedes]]).

The name is formed like that of Arisbe, the town near the Hellespont from which Asius came to Troy (2.836–838; see [[arisbe]]), but the poem does not connect them, and Arisbas' son fights on the Achaean side.
