Ἀντηνορίδης = Ἀντήνωρ + -ίδης ("son of"): "son of Antenor". The poem names him twice by his father before it gives his own name, and calls him κρείων, "powerful" ([[powerful]]). His marriage joins the house of [[Antenor]], the elder who will propose giving Helen back (7.347–353), to the house of [[Priam]]. He appears nowhere else in Homer.

## Outside Homer

Pausanias describes the painting of the fall of Troy by Polygnotus at Delphi, in which Laodice stands among the Trojan women. He argues that she was not taken captive, because of the Achaeans' old ties of hospitality with Antenor's house, and he cites Lesches' *Little Iliad* for the story that Helicaon was wounded in the fighting on the night of the sack, and that Odysseus recognized him and brought him out of the battle alive (Pausanias 10.26.7–8).
