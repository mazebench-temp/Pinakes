## The name

Μέντωρ has the agent suffix -τωρ of Ἕκτωρ and Νέστωρ; its root is probably that of μένος, “spirit”, and μέμονα, “be eager, have in mind”. The Iliad gives the name to a Trojan, “Mentor, rich in horses”, the father of Imbrius (*Iliad* 13.171; see [[mentor-father-of-imbrius]]), who is another man. Athena takes the likeness of two men of almost the same name: in the first book of the Odyssey of Mentes, lord of the Taphians, and from the second of Mentor (see [[mentes-the-taphian]]).

## Family

Son of Alcimus: Μέντωρ Ἀλκιμίδης, “Mentor, son of Alcimus” (*Odyssey* 22.235), the only mention of his father. Odysseus speaks to him as to a man of his own age, ὁμηλικίην δέ μοί ἐσσι, “and you are of my age” (*Odyssey* 22.209), though it is the goddess in his likeness whom he addresses.

## Descriptions

- ἑταῖρος, “companion” (*Odyssey* 2.225; see [[hetairos]]): with [[halitherses|Halitherses]], and once with another Antiphus, one of the companions of Odysseus “from of old” (*Odyssey* 2.254, 17.68–69).
- ποιμὴν λαῶν, “shepherd of the people” (*Odyssey* 24.456; see [[shepherd-of-the-people]]), in Halitherses' mouth.
- δῖος, “brilliant” (*Odyssey* 4.655; see [[brilliant]]), in the mouth of Noemon, who lent Telemachus his ship.

The renderings of books other than the second here are plain renderings, not the translation.

## Elsewhere in Homer

**At Pylos.** The goddess is still Mentor when the ship comes in to Pylos, and Telemachus calls her by his name, “Mentor, how then shall I go?” (*Odyssey* 3.22, 3.240), until she leaves in the likeness of a sea eagle (*Odyssey* 3.371–373; see [[the-journey-of-telemachus]]). In Ithaca Noemon is puzzled: he saw Mentor go on board as the leader of the crew, “or a god”, and yet he has since seen brilliant Mentor here in Ithaca (*Odyssey* 4.653–656).

**In the hall.** On the day of the slaughter Athena comes to Odysseus in Mentor's likeness. Odysseus calls on “Mentor” to remember his dear companion and ward off ruin, and the suitor Agelaus threatens him with death; the goddess rebukes Odysseus for his faint heart and bids him watch how Mentor, son of Alcimus, repays kindness, then flies up to the roof-beam like a swallow, and Agelaus tells the suitors that Mentor has gone, after empty boasts (*Odyssey* 22.205–240, 22.249).

**The end of the poem.** Medon the herald tells the Ithacans that a god “in all things like Mentor” stood beside Odysseus (*Odyssey* 24.445–446), and Halitherses reminds them that they would not listen to him or to Mentor and stop their sons (*Odyssey* 24.454–457; see [[the-assembly-of-the-ithacans]]). Athena comes once more in his likeness to the fight with the suitors' kin, and the poem's last verse is the formula Μέντορι εἰδομένη ἠμὲν δέμας ἠδὲ καὶ αὐδήν, “in the likeness of Mentor, both in form and in voice”, as she sets oaths between the two sides (*Odyssey* 24.502–504, 24.546–548; see [[athena]]).
