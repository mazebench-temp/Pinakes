Οἰνόμαόν τε, “and Oenomaus” (12.140), ends the list of the five men named around Asius when his company rushes the gate on the left of the ships (see [[asius]] and [[the-gates-of-the-wall]]). Book 12 gives him nothing more.

In Book 13 Idomeneus hits an Oenomaus in the middle of the belly and breaks the hollow of his corselet, and the bronze lets out his bowels; he falls in the dust and clutches the earth with his palm. Idomeneus pulls his spear from the body but cannot strip the armor, for the missiles press him hard (13.506–511; see [[idomeneus]]). The poem does not say that this is Asius' companion. If it is, every man named with Asius dies before the end of Book 13 (see [[thoon-companion-of-asius]]).

Homer has another Oenomaus, an Achaean killed by Hector and Ares (5.706; see [[oenomaus]]). The king of Pisa, whose daughter Hippodameia Pelops won in a chariot race, does not appear in Homer. The name is usually explained as οἶνος, “wine”, and μαίομαι, “strive after”.
