υἱός τʼ Αὐτοφόνοιο μενεπτόλεμος Πολυφόντης, “the son of Autophonus, Polyphontes, steadfast in battle” (4.395; see [[steadfast-in-battle]]). Πολυφόντης = πολύς (“much”) + the root of φόνος (“killing”): “much-slaying”. His father's name is made from the same root (see [[autophonus]]). Both names suit men who lie in wait to kill, and they may have been made for the story. Tydeus killed him with all the rest of the fifty but Maeon (4.396–397; see [[tydeus]]).

## Outside Homer

A Theban Polyphontes appears again in Aeschylus' *Seven against Thebes*, where Eteocles posts him at the gate that Capaneus attacks (422–451).
