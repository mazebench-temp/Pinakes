αὐτὰρ ὑπέρθυμον Πολυφείδεα μάντιν Ἀπόλλων / θῆκε βροτῶν ὄχʼ ἄριστον, ἐπεὶ θάνεν Ἀμφιάραος, “But Apollo made high-hearted Polypheides a seer, / by far the best of mortals, after Amphiaraus died” (*Odyssey* 15.252–253; see [[apollo]], [[amphiaraus]] and [[divination]]). He is the son of Mantius and the brother of Cleitus, whom Dawn carried off (*Odyssey* 15.249–251; see [[mantius]] and [[cleitus-son-of-mantius]]); he moved away to Hyperesia, angry with his father, and lived there prophesying for all mortals (*Odyssey* 15.254–255; see [[hyperesia]]); and his son is the seer Theoclymenus (*Odyssey* 15.256; see [[theoclymenus]]). The poem tells nothing else of him, and no later author in the library names him.

## Descriptions

- ὑπέρθυμος, “high-hearted” (*Odyssey* 15.252; see [[high-hearted]]).
- βροτῶν ὄχʼ ἄριστον, “by far the best of mortals”, as a seer: the words in which the Iliad calls Calchas “by far the best of bird-seers”, οἰωνοπόλων ὄχʼ ἄριστος (*Iliad* 1.69; see [[best-of-bird-seers]] and [[calchas]]).
