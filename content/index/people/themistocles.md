summary: Athenian statesman and general (c. 524–459 BCE), who built the fleet, read the Delphic oracle's “wooden wall” as the ships, and won the battle of Salamis.
---

In 483/2 BCE Themistocles persuaded the Athenians to spend the new silver of Laurium on warships (Herodotus 7.144). When Delphi told them, as Xerxes came, that Zeus would grant Athena a wooden wall that alone would not be taken, and ended “divine Salamis, you will destroy the children of women”, the professional interpreters of oracles advised them to leave Attica for good; Themistocles argued that the wall was the fleet, and that an oracle against the Athenians would have called Salamis “cruel”, not “divine” (7.140–143; see [[the-wooden-wall]]). The battle in the straits followed (8.40–96; see [[salamis-and-himera]]). He was later ostracized and condemned for treason, and ended his life in the service of the Persian king, as governor of Magnesia (Thucydides 1.135–138).
