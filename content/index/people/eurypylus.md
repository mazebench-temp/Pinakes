Εὐρύπυλος = εὐρύς ("wide") + πύλη ("gate"). ἀγλαὸς υἱός, "splendid son" (see [[splendid]]). He is not [[eurypylus-of-cos|Eurypylus of Cos]], whose city is named at 2.677.

He is one of the most active Achaeans after the great chiefs. In Book 11, as he strips a Trojan he has killed, Paris hits him in the thigh with an arrow (11.575–584). Patroclus, running back from Nestor's hut, meets him limping from the fighting, and stays to cut out the arrow and dress the wound with a bitter root (11.806–848). Patroclus is still sitting with him when the Trojans storm the wall, and only then hurries back to Achilles (15.390–405). Later epic gave the same name to Eurypylus, son of Telephus, who came to help the Trojans after Hector's death.
