Κισσηῒς ἄλοχος Ἀντήνορος ἱπποδάμοιο, “daughter of Cisseus, the wife of Antenor, tamer of horses” (6.299). Κισσηΐς is a feminine patronymic, formed like the names Briseis and Chryseis, “daughter of Briseus” and “daughter of Chryses” (see [[briseis]] and [[chryseis]]).

He is named once more, as Κισσῆς, in Book 11. Iphidamas, a son of Antenor, “was reared in deep-soiled Thrace”. Cisseus, “his mother's father, who fathered fair-cheeked Theano”, reared him in his house, and when he came of age kept him there and gave him his own daughter, the boy's aunt (11.221–226). The line is the one used of the Lycian king who kept Bellerophon and gave him his daughter (6.192 = 11.226). Iphidamas left his bride to sail for Troy, and Agamemnon killed him (11.227–247; see [[theano]] and [[antenor]]).

## Outside Homer

Euripides makes Cisseus the father of Hecuba (*Hecuba* 3), whose father in the Iliad is Dymas (16.718). [[apollodorus|Apollodorus]] gives three fathers: Dymas, or, as some say, Cisseus, or the river Sangarius and Metope (*Library* 3.12.5). Virgil calls Hecuba *Cisseis*, “daughter of Cisseus” (*Aeneid* 7.320), and names a Thracian Cisseus who once gave Anchises an embossed mixing bowl (*Aeneid* 5.536–538).
