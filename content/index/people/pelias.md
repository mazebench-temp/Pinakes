The Iliad names him only as the father of Alcestis. The Odyssey tells of his birth: Tyro, the daughter of Salmoneus, bore Pelias and Neleus to Poseidon, and Pelias "lived in broad Iolcus, rich in flocks" (11.235–257). Neleus, Nestor's father, was his brother, and [[Pheres]], the grandfather of Eumelus, his half-brother (11.258–259).

In later legend Pelias held the throne of Iolcus and sent his nephew Jason to fetch the Golden Fleece (Pindar, *Pythian* 4). When Jason came back, Medea tricked Pelias' daughters into cutting up their father and boiling him, on the promise that he would be made young again (Apollodorus 1.9.27; Ovid, *Metamorphoses* 7.297–349). His funeral games were a famous subject of early poetry and art.
