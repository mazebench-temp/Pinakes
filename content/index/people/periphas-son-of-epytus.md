**The herald of Anchises.** Periphas had grown old as a herald “beside Aeneas' old father” (17.324; see [[anchises]] and [[heralds]]), and φίλα φρεσὶ μήδεα εἰδώς, “a man whose mind was kindly toward him” (17.325): Walter Leaf explains the phrase as “an attached retainer of the family” (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 17.325). The English adds the name of Aeneas, since after “son of Epytus” the pronoun would seem to point to Epytus (see the translation's note on 17.324). His father's name is the herald's calling: Ἠπυτίδης, “son of Epytus”, is made from ἠπύτα, “loud-voiced”, the epithet of the herald Idaeus (7.384), as Leaf observes (on 17.324; see [[epytus]] and [[idaeus]]).

**The likeness.** Ajax has killed Hippothous and Phorcys, and the Trojans would have been driven back into Ilios had not Apollo urged on Aeneas in the shape of this old servant of his house (17.319–326; see [[apollo]] and [[aeneas]]). Through Periphas' mouth the god rebukes him: other men have defended their cities even in defiance of a god, trusting in their own strength and numbers, but the Trojans tremble and do not fight, though Zeus wills victory for them (17.327–332). Aeneas sees through the likeness: “Aeneas knew Apollo who shoots from afar / as he looked him in the face” (17.333–334).

The other Periphas of the Iliad, “huge Periphas, / by far the best of the Aetolians”, son of Ochesius, is the man whom Ares kills and strips in Book 5 (5.842–847; see [[periphas]]).
