δουρὶ δʼ ὑπειρέβαλον Φυλῆά τε καὶ Πολύδωρον, “and with the spear I outthrew Phyleus and Polydorus” (23.637): the last of the four victories that Nestor claims at the games of Amarynceus, and all that Homer tells of him (see [[the-funeral-games-of-amarynceus]], [[nestor]] and [[phyleus]]). Nestor does not say whether he was an Epeian, a Pylian or an Aetolian, the three peoples he names (23.632–633).

The name is the adjective πολύδωρος, “of many gifts”, which Homer uses of wives, “richly dowered” (6.394, 22.88; see [[richly-dowered]]). The Iliad's other Polydorus is Priam's youngest son, who “surpassed them all in speed of foot” and whom Achilles kills (20.407–418; see [[polydorus-son-of-priam]]).
