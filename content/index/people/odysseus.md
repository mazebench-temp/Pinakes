In the Iliad Odysseus is one of the Achaean leaders at Troy, a speaker and counselor who leads the embassy to Achilles in Book 9. The Odyssey tells how he comes home to Ithaca in the twentieth year after he sailed (*Odyssey* 16.205–206), and how he kills the suitors of his wife (see [[the-return-of-odysseus]] and [[odyssey]]).

## Etymology

Ὀδυσσεύς (Odysseus), also Ὀδυσεύς. The Odyssey explains it through his grandfather [[autolycus|Autolycus]]. Having been angered (ὀδυσσάμενος) by many people, he named the baby "Odysseus", the man of wrath or the man hated (*Odyssey* 19.406–409). Athena's question to Zeus in the council of the gods, τί νύ οἱ τόσον ὠδύσαο, Ζεῦ; “Why are you so angry with him, Zeus?” (*Odyssey* 1.62), plays on the same verb: Merry renders the name, by this etymology, “a man of wrath”, one who suffers the wrath of the gods or deals wrath to his enemies (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 1.62). Scholars regard the name itself as probably pre-Greek.

## Family

He is the son of Laertes, whose name he carries in his patronymic, Λαερτιάδης (2.173; see [[laertes]] and [[son-of-laertes]]), and of Anticleia, the daughter of Autolycus (*Odyssey* 11.84–85); the husband of Penelope, “the daughter of Icarius” (*Odyssey* 1.329; see [[penelope]]); and the father of Telemachus, who was a baby when he left for Troy (2.260, 4.354; *Odyssey* 11.448–449; see [[telemachus]]). Telemachus describes the family as a line of only sons: Arceisius had only Laertes, Laertes only Odysseus, and Odysseus only Telemachus (*Odyssey* 16.117–120).

## Descriptions

{{quote:d944d9f6-2f85-5c6f-ad35-ce332af87ea9}}

πολύμητις = πολύς (much) + μῆτις (cunning, counsel): of many wiles. It is his standing epithet in both poems, and in the first book of the Iliad it is used of him twice, both times when he takes charge (1.311, 1.440; see [[of-many-wiles]]).

The Odyssey's first verse calls him πολύτροπος, “of many turns”, a word that Homer uses only of him, here and once more, when Circe knows him (*Odyssey* 1.1, 10.330; see [[of-many-turns]] and the Odyssey translation's note on 1.1).

## Outside Homer

The Latin form of his name, Ulixes, gave English "Ulysses".
