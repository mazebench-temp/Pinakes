## Etymology

Ὀδυσσεύς (Odysseus), also Ὀδυσεύς. The Odyssey explains it through his grandfather Autolycus. Having been angered (ὀδυσσάμενος) by many people, he named the baby "Odysseus", the man of wrath or the man hated (19.406–409). Scholars regard the name itself as probably pre-Greek.

## Descriptions

{{quote:d944d9f6-2f85-5c6f-ad35-ce332af87ea9}}

πολύμητις = πολύς (much) + μῆτις (cunning, counsel): of many wiles. It is his standing epithet, and in Book 1 it is used of him twice, both times when he takes charge (1.311, 1.440).

## Outside Homer

He is the hero of the Odyssey, which tells of his ten years' wandering after Troy. In the Iliad he leads the embassy to Achilles in Book 9. The Latin form of his name, Ulixes, gave English "Ulysses".
