## Descriptions

ἀτάλαντος Ἐνυαλίῳ ἀνδρειφόντῃ: "the equal of Enyalius, slayer of men". ἀτάλαντος is "equal in weight" (ἀ- "one and the same" + τάλαντον "balance"), and Enyalius is a name of the war god (see [[enyalius]] and [[equal-of-ares]]). The whole half-line returns when Meriones is listed among the chiefs in later books (for example 7.166).

## In the Iliad

Meriones is Idomeneus' θεράπων ("squire, companion") and his partner in battle. His father is Molus (13.249). He lends Odysseus a bow, a sword and the famous helmet of boar's tusks for the night raid (10.260–271), fights beside Idomeneus by the ships in Book 13, and at the funeral games of Patroclus wins the archery, shooting down the dove after Teucer's arrow has cut its cord (23.850–883).
