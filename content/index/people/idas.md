Ἴδεω (9.558) is the Ionic genitive of Ἴδας, scanned here in two syllables; the name is not that of Ida, the mountain (Ἴδη; see [[ida]]). Homer names him once, as the father of Meleager's wife, and gives him a verse and a half of praise: he “was the strongest of men on earth / in those days” (9.558–559; see [[cleopatra]]). Nestor praises the Lapiths of his youth in nearly the same words, “the mightiest of all men ever raised on earth” (κάρτιστοι … ἐπιχθονίων τράφεν ἀνδρῶν, 1.266); in both paradigms the men of old were stronger than the men of now (see [[mythological-paradigm]]). His one deed is told in a clause: he “took up his bow against the lord / Phoebus Apollo” when the god carried off his bride (9.559–564; see [[marpessa]] and [[idas-and-apollo]]). Eurytus, who challenged Apollo with the bow, died young (*Odyssey* 8.223–228), but Phoenix says nothing of what became of Idas, only that Marpessa bore his daughter (see [[who-fights-the-gods]]).

## Outside Homer

Later writers give him a family and a country. He was the elder and braver son of Aphareus, king of Messenia, and the brother of Lynceus, whose sight could pierce the trunk of an oak (Pausanias 4.2.4, 4.2.7). This Aphareus is not the Achaean captain of the watch (9.83; see [[aphareus]]). [[apollodorus|Apollodorus]] tells how he carried off Marpessa and how Zeus let her choose between him and Apollo (*Library* 1.7.8–9), and lists him with Lynceus among the hunters of the Calydonian boar and the Argonauts (*Library* 1.8.2, 1.9.16). He died in a quarrel with Castor and Polydeuces over cattle. In the *Cypria*, as [[proclus|Proclus]] summarizes it, Idas killed Castor and Polydeuces killed Lynceus and Idas; in Apollodorus Zeus strikes Idas with a thunderbolt (*Library* 3.11.2; see [[the-death-of-the-dioscuri]], [[castor]] and [[polydeuces]]).
