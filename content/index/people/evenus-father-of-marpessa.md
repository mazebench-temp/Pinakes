Εὐηνίνη, “daughter of Evenus” (9.557), is a woman's patronymic, formed like Ἀδρηστίνη, “daughter of Adrastus” (5.412), and it is all that Homer tells of him (see [[marpessa]]). Euenus, son of Selepus and father of Mynes and Epistrophus at Lyrnessus, bears the same Greek name (2.693; see [[euenus]]); the index keeps the two apart by spelling.

## Outside Homer

In [[apollodorus|Apollodorus]] he is a son of Ares and a brother of Thestius, the father of Althaea (*Library* 1.7.7, 1.7.10). When Idas carried off Marpessa in a winged chariot, Evenus pursued them as far as the river Lycormas; unable to overtake them, he killed his horses and threw himself into the river, which is called Evenus after him (*Library* 1.7.8; see [[idas-and-apollo]]). Strabo says that the Aetolian river, which runs past Calydon, was once called Lycormas (10.2.5; see [[calydon]]).
