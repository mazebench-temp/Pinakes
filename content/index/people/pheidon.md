ἔνθα με Θεσπρωτῶν βασιλεὺς ἐκομίσσατο Φείδων, “There the king of the Thesprotians, Pheidon, took care of me” (*Odyssey* 14.316): the king who takes in the castaway of the false tale that Odysseus tells Eumaeus (see [[the-tale-to-eumaeus]], [[thesprotians]] and [[odysseus]]). His son finds the stranger on the shore and clothes him; the king tells him that Odysseus has been his guest, shows him the treasure Odysseus has gathered, says that Odysseus has gone to Dodona to hear the will of Zeus from the oak, and sends the stranger on a Thesprotian ship bound for Dulichium, to King Acastus (*Odyssey* 14.316–337; see [[dodona]], [[dulichium]] and [[acastus-king-of-dulichium]]). Odysseus tells Penelope the same story in nearly the same verses (*Odyssey* 19.287–299), beginning “So the king of the Thesprotians, Pheidon, told me” (*Odyssey* 19.287, a plain rendering). The poems name him nowhere else.

## The name

Φείδων is formed from φείδομαι, “spare”, like φειδώ, “sparing”, which Eumaeus has just used of the suitors, who devour his master's possessions “and there is no sparing” (*Odyssey* 14.92; see [[the-suitors]]). The king of the tale keeps his guest ἀπριάτην, “without price” (*Odyssey* 14.317; see [[the-tale-to-eumaeus]]), the word that in the Iliad describes Chryseis, to be given back “unbought, unransomed” (*Iliad* 1.99; see [[chryseis]]). Historical Greeks bore the name: Herodotus tells of Pheidon, tyrant of Argos, “who made the measures for the Peloponnesians”, drove out the Eleans who ran the games at Olympia and held them himself (*Histories* 6.127.3; see [[herodotus]]). The king of the [[thesprotians|Thesprotians]] has nothing to do with him.
