## The name

Κτιμένη has the participle of κτίζω, “found, build”, which is in the epithet ἐυκτίμενος, “well-built”, of houses, cities and islands (see [[well-built]]); the poem does not explain it.

## Family

οὕνεκά μʼ αὐτὴ θρέψεν ἅμα Κτιμένῃ τανυπέπλῳ, / θυγατέρʼ ἰφθίμῃ, τὴν ὁπλοτάτην τέκε παίδων, “because she herself reared me together with Ctimene of the long robes, / her strong daughter, whom she bore the youngest of her children” (*Odyssey* 15.363–364): she is the daughter of Anticleia, Odysseus' mother, and so Odysseus' sister (see [[anticleia]], [[odysseus]] and [[laertes]]). She is the only sister of Odysseus whom Homer names. When Telemachus calls his house a line of single sons, Arceisius begetting “a single son, Laertes”, and Laertes “a single son, Odysseus”, he counts sons (*Odyssey* 16.117–120, a plain rendering; see [[arceisius]]). The swineherd Eumaeus, a bought slave of the house, was reared with her, and when she married into Same he was given new clothes and sent to the farm (*Odyssey* 15.365–370; see [[eumaeus]]).

## Descriptions

- τανύπεπλος, “of the long robes” (*Odyssey* 15.363), the epithet of Helen (*Iliad* 3.228; *Odyssey* 15.171) and of Thetis (*Iliad* 18.385; see [[long-robed]]).
- ἰφθίμη, “strong” (*Odyssey* 15.364), the word of strong women (see [[mighty]]).

## Outside Homer

Strabo reports that Apollodorus took the city on the island to be called Same from the count of the suitors, “from Same there are twenty-four men” (*Odyssey* 16.249), and “from the account of Ctimene”, τὴν μὲν ἔπειτα Σάμηνδʼ ἔδοσαν, “they gave her in marriage to Same” (*Odyssey* 15.367); Strabo grants that the poet does not set out these places clearly (*Geography* 10.2.10; see [[samos]] and [[strabo]]). W. W. Merry notes that a later story made her the wife of Eurylochus, whom Odysseus calls his kinsman by marriage (*Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on *Odyssey* 10.441; see [[eurylochus]] and [[in-laws]]).
