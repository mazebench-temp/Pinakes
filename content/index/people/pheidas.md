Hector has broken in where the wall was built lowest, and five peoples can hardly hold him off from the ships (13.679–688; see [[the-battle-at-the-ships]]). Then the poet names their leaders, and the Athenians come first:

{{quote:5dda8018-71dc-5b5d-9c20-0d89b8bcf3ec}}

Pheidas is named first of the three who follow Menestheus, with Stichius and Bias (see [[menestheus]], [[stichius]], [[bias-the-athenian]] and [[athenians]]). Homer gives him no father, no home and no deed, and does not name him again. The name, like that of Pheidippus, a leader from Cos (2.678; see [[pheidippus]]), seems to come from φείδομαι, “spare”.
