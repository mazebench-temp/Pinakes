## Etymology

Εὐχήνωρ joins the stem of εὔχομαι, “pray, vow, boast” (see [[euchomai]]), to -ήνωρ, from ἀνήρ, “man”, the ending of Agenor and Hypsenor.

## In Homer

**The choice.** Euchenor is named only here, “rich and brave” (13.664); ἀφνειός, “rich”, is the Catalogue's word for his city, “wealthy Corinth” (2.570; see [[corinth]]). His father often told him that he would die “of a grievous sickness in his own halls, or be beaten down by the Trojans among the ships of the Achaeans” (13.667–668; see [[polyidus-the-seer]] and [[nousos]]). He chose the ships, and so escaped both the sickness and “the grievous fine of the Achaeans” (13.669) laid on a man who stayed at home. θωή, “fine”, occurs elsewhere in Homer only in the suitors' threat to the seer Halitherses (Od. 2.192). A rich man could buy his way out: Echepolus of Sicyon, another of Agamemnon's towns (2.572), gave Agamemnon a mare so that he need not follow him to Ilios (23.296–299; see [[agamemnon]]).

**Like Achilles.** The two fates have the shape of Achilles' choice, fame and a short life at Troy or a long life at home (9.410–416; see [[the-two-fates-of-achilles]]). Euchenor's is a choice between two deaths, and he takes the one without disgrace. Other seers' sons die at Troy against their fathers' will or beyond their foresight (see [[merops]] and [[eurydamas]]); Euchenor went knowing.

**The arrow.** Paris “was very angry at heart over his killing; for he had been his guest-friend among the many Paphlagonians” (13.660–661): Meriones has just shot Harpalion (see [[harpalion]], [[meriones]] and [[xenia]]). Paris' arrow, told in the words of Meriones', “a bronze-fitted arrow” (13.650, 13.662), strikes Euchenor “under the jaw and the ear”, and “hateful darkness seized him” (13.671–672); the same two verses end the Trojan Laogonus at Meriones' hands (16.606–607). No penalty was paid for Harpalion (ποινή, 13.659; see [[poine]]). Paris takes a life for him, the life of a man who had come to Troy rather than pay a fine.

## Outside Homer

At Megara, Pausanias was told that an image of Dionysus Dasyllius had been dedicated by Euchenor, son of Coeranus, son of Polyidus: a grandson of the seer, not Homer's son (1.43.5; see [[polyidus-the-seer]]).
