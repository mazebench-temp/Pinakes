**Who he was.** ἠΐθεος θαλερός, “the young man in his prime”. An ἠΐθεος is a young man not yet married; the word is paired with παρθένοι (“maidens”) for the young people who dance together on the shield of Achilles (18.593). θαλερός, from θάλλω (“bloom”), is “flourishing, in his prime”. The poem gives him a father, [[Anthemion]], a mother who is not named, and grandparents with flocks on [[Ida]]. His mother had gone up the mountain with her parents “to watch over the flocks”, and on the way down she bore him beside the banks of the [[Simoeis]]; “therefore they called him Simoeisius”, the boy of the Simoeis. Other Trojans take their names from rivers. Hector calls his son Scamandrius (6.402), and Satnius, whom a Naiad bore to Enops as he herded cattle “by the banks of the Satnioeis”, falls in Book 14 to the other Ajax, the son of Oileus (14.442–445).

**The unpaid debt.** οὐδὲ τοκεῦσι θρέπτρα φίλοις ἀπέδωκε, “but he did not repay his dear parents for his rearing”. θρέπτρα, from τρέφω (“rear, feed”), is what a child owes for being raised: care for his parents when they are old (see [[threptra]]). The word occurs only here and in the same lines about Hippothous, whom the same Ajax kills over the body of Patroclus (17.301–303; see [[hippothous]]). μινυνθάδιος (“short”, of a life) is the word Achilles uses of himself to Thetis: “you bore me to be short-lived” (1.352; see [[short-lived]] and [[the-short-life-of-achilles]]). The notice is told from the parents' side, by what the son did not live to do for them.

**The death.** Ajax strikes him in the chest beside the right nipple “as he came on in front”, and the spear goes straight through the shoulder (4.480–482). He falls in the dust like a poplar:

{{quote:824228e3-c914-5fc0-abfe-4fb950636b04}}

The simile answers the life. The tree grew by water, “in the low wet ground of a great marsh”, as he was born by a river. It is “smooth” (λείη), a straight trunk with branches only at the top. A chariot-maker cuts it down with “the gleaming iron” (αἴθωνι σιδήρῳ) to bend it into the rim (ἴτυς) of a wheel, and it lies drying “by the banks of the river” (ποταμοῖο παρʼ ὄχθας, 4.487), the words that told his birth, “beside the banks of the Simoeis” (παρʼ ὄχθῃσιν Σιμόεντος, 4.475). He was born on a river bank, and he lies like a felled tree on one. The poplar is cut to make a chariot, a thing of war. See [[the-poplar]].

**After.** Ajax strips him of his armor (4.488–489). Antiphus, a son of Priam, throws at Ajax, misses, and kills Leucus, a companion of Odysseus, who is dragging the body away, most likely Simoeisius' own (4.489–493; see [[antiphus-son-of-priam]] and [[leucus]]).

**The first obituary.** Simoeisius is the first of the Iliad's short obituaries, in which a man is given, as he dies, a few lines of life: where he was born, who his parents were, the marriage or the old age he will not have. Gorgythion, a son of Priam, whose beautiful mother came from Aesyme, bows his head like a poppy in a garden (8.302–308). Iphidamas, son of Antenor, dies “far from his wife, whom he had wooed”, from whom he had seen no joy (11.241–243). See [[the-first-battle]] and [[androktasia]].
