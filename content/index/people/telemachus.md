## Etymology

Τηλέμαχος = τῆλε (“far off”) + μάχη (“battle”): “fighting far away”. The name may refer to the war his father was fighting far from home; the explanation is plausible, not certain.

## Family

He is the only son of an only son. Arceisius had only Laertes, Laertes only Odysseus, and Odysseus only Telemachus (*Odyssey* 16.117–120; see [[laertes]]). He was a baby at the breast when his father left for Troy (*Odyssey* 11.448–449), and Menelaus and Helen remember him as the son whom Odysseus “left newly born in his house” (*Odyssey* 4.112, 4.144). The Iliad names him twice, both times in Odysseus' mouth, as “the father of Telemachus” (2.260, 4.354). Other heroes are named by their fathers; Odysseus names himself by his son.

## Descriptions

πεπνυμένος, “wise”, is his epithet in the Odyssey, above all in the verse that brings in his answers, τὴν (τὸν) δʼ αὖ Τηλέμαχος πεπνυμένος ἀντίον ηὔδα, “And wise Telemachus answered her face to face” (*Odyssey* 1.213 and often, with “him” at 1.388 and 1.412; see [[wise]]). He is also θεοειδής, “godlike” (*Odyssey* 1.113; see [[godlike]]).

## Elsewhere in Homer

The first four books of the Odyssey follow him: the assembly of Ithaca (Book 2; see [[the-assembly-of-the-ithacans]]) and his journey to Pylos and Sparta for news of his father (Books 2–4; see [[the-journey-of-telemachus]]). He comes home in Book 15, escaping the suitors' ambush. Father and son meet in Eumaeus' hut (*Odyssey* 16.154–320) and kill the suitors together (Book 22).

## Outside Homer

In the *Cypria*, as [[proclus|Proclus]] summarizes it, Odysseus pretended to be mad to avoid the war, and Palamedes exposed him by seizing the infant Telemachus. In the *Telegony*, after Odysseus' death Telemachus marries Circe, and Telegonus, Odysseus' son by Circe, marries Penelope. In the Hesiodic *Catalogue of Women* Nestor's youngest daughter, Polycaste, who bathed him at Pylos, bears him a son, Persepolis (fr. 12 Evelyn-White; see [[polycaste]]).

For Athena at his side in the likeness of Mentor in later writers, and Fénelon's *Télémaque*, see [[athena-as-mentor-in-later-writers]].
