Λαοδίκη joins λαός (“people”) and δίκη (“right, justice”). It is also the name of the most beautiful of Priam's daughters, whose likeness Iris takes to call Helen to the wall (3.121–124, 6.252; see [[laodice]]).

She is named second of the three daughters in Agamemnon's offer and in Odysseus' repetition of it (9.145, 9.287; see [[chrysothemis]], [[iphianassa]] and [[the-gifts-of-agamemnon]]). Achilles may take whichever he likes “without bride-price”, with a dowry greater than any father ever gave (9.146–148), but he refuses to marry a daughter of Agamemnon at all (9.388–392). The Iliad speaks of Agamemnon's daughters once more, when Idomeneus mocks the dead Othryoneus, who had come to Troy for Cassandra: the Achaeans too would have given him the most beautiful of the daughters of the son of Atreus, brought from Argos, if he had helped them sack Ilios (13.374–382).

## Outside Homer

Homer never names Electra or Iphigenia. Later poets kept the number of Agamemnon's daughters but not all the names: in Euripides' *Orestes* Electra counts “Chrysothemis and Iphigenia and I, Electra” (*Orestes* 22–23; see [[euripides]]). No surviving tragedy names Laodice.
