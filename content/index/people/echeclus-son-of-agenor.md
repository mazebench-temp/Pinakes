**The sword.** Echeclus, “son of Agenor”, is the seventh man Achilles kills after Apollo has saved Hector, after Mulius (20.472–474; see [[mulius-killed-by-achilles]], [[agenor-father-of-echeclus]] and [[achilles]]). Achilles brings his sword down μέσσην κὰκ κεφαλήν, “full on the head”, where his spear struck Iphition, the first man he killed (20.387, 20.475; see [[iphition]] and [[hilted]]). The last two verses are those in which Ajax, son of Oileus, killed Cleobulus with his hilted sword after taking him alive (20.476–477 = 16.333–334; see [[cleobulus]]), and the last of them closed the death of Hypsenor at the hands of Eurypylus (5.83; see [[hypsenor]]): “and the whole sword grew warm with blood; and his eyes / were seized by purple death and mighty fate” (see [[purple-death]] and [[mighty-fate]]). Walter Leaf notes the repetition (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 20.475).

**His father.** Agenor is also the name of Antenor's son, one of the leading Trojans, whom Achilles will meet before the walls in the next book (21.544–598; see [[agenor]]). The poem does not say whether Echeclus' father is that Agenor, and the index keeps them apart (see [[agenor-father-of-echeclus]]).

**The name.** Homer's other Echeclus is a Trojan whom Patroclus kills, the third of the nine in the list that answers the narrator's question “whom first, whom last” (16.692–694; see [[echeclus]] and [[whom-first-whom-last]]). The name joins ἔχω, “hold”, and κλέος, “fame”.
