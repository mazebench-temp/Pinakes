φεύγοντʼ, “as he fled” (6.36), is the one thing told of him. He is killed in flight, as the Trojans give way all along the line; a few lines later the horses of Adrastus bolt toward the city, “where the others too were fleeing in terror” (6.41). The name belongs also to the father of Iphiclus, whom the Catalogue names in a patronymic (Φυλακίδαο, 2.705; see [[phylacus]]). This Phylacus occurs only here.
