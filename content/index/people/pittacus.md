Aristotle calls Pittacus an elected tyrant, an aisymnete: the Mytilenaeans chose him to rule against the exiles led by Antimenides and the poet Alcaeus, who abused him in his songs (*Politics* 3.14, 1285a35–b1). He is the author of the law that a man who did wrong when drunk should pay a greater penalty than one who did it sober, because drunken men do more wrong (*Politics* 2.12, 1274b18–23; *Nicomachean Ethics* 3.5, 1113b30–33). Plato counts him among the Seven Sages (*Protagoras* 343a). See [[alcaeus]].
