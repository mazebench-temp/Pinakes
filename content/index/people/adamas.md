**At the wall.** Ἀσιάδην τʼ Ἀδάμαντα, “and Adamas, son of Asius” (12.140), is the third of the five men named around his father when Asius drives at the gate on the left of the ships (see [[asius]] and [[the-gates-of-the-wall]]). The patronymic counts for both father and son.

**His death.** At the ships he stabs the middle of Antilochus' shield, but Poseidon, “begrudging him the life” of Nestor's son, makes the point fail: half the spear sticks in the shield “like a stake burnt in the fire”, and half lies on the ground. As he draws back among his companions, Meriones follows and spears him between the genitals and the navel, “where Ares is most painful for wretched mortals”; he writhes around the spear like an ox that herdsmen bind with ropes in the mountains, until Meriones pulls it out and darkness covers his eyes (13.560–575; see [[antilochus]], [[meriones]] and [[poseidon]]). When Hector goes looking among the foremost for Deiphobus, Helenus, Adamas and Asius, some of them lie dead by the ships and the others are wounded, and he asks Paris where they are (13.758–773).

The name is ἀδάμας, from ἀ-, “not”, and δαμάζω, “tame, subdue”: “the untamed”. As a common noun the word is “adamant”, the hard gray metal of Hesiod (*Theogony* 161). Homer has no other Adamas.
