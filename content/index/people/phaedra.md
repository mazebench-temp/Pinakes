## The name

Φαίδρη is the feminine of φαιδρός, “bright, beaming”, a word of the family of φαίδιμος, “shining”, Homer's epithet of heroes (see [[phaidimos]]).

## In Homer

Homer names her once, in the verse that brings three women of Attic legend together: “And I saw Phaedra and Procris and beautiful Ariadne” (*Odyssey* 11.321; see [[procris]] and [[ariadne]]). He says nothing of her family or her story. W. W. Merry, who thinks the passage a later interpolation, gives the usual account: Phaedra was the daughter of the Cretan Minos and the wife of Theseus after the death of Hippolyte, and her tragic fate was caused by her guilty passion for her stepson Hippolytus (*Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on *Odyssey* 11.321; see [[minos]] and [[theseus]]).

## Outside Homer

Her story is the plot of Euripides' *Hippolytus*. In the prologue Aphrodite tells how Hippolytus, the son of Theseus and the Amazon, scorns her and honors Artemis alone, and how, when he came from the house of Pittheus to Athens for the mysteries, “his father's noble wife, Phaedra, / saw him, and her heart was seized / by a terrible love, by my design” (*Hippolytus* 10–28); she founded a temple of Cypris on the rock of Pallas, looking toward Troezen, and when Theseus went into a year's exile at Troezen she wasted away there in silence, and she must die, Aphrodite says, though with her good name, so that Hippolytus may be punished (*Hippolytus* 29–50; see [[euripides]]). Confessing her love to her nurse, she remembers the women of her family: her mother, and the love she had for the Bull, and her sister, “bride of Dionysus”, and herself, “the third, how wretchedly I perish” (*Hippolytus* 337–341; see [[ariadne]]). She hangs herself in the house (*Hippolytus* 776–779, 802). In Virgil's underworld she is among the women whom cruel love has wasted, in the Fields of Mourning: *his Phaedram Procrinque locis maestamque Eriphylen … cernit*, “here he sees Phaedra and Procris and sorrowful Eriphyle” (*Aeneid* 6.440–447; see [[virgil]], [[procris]] and [[eriphyle]]).
