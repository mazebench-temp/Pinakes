**Out of his wits.** Thestor is the second man Patroclus kills when he pens the Trojans between the ships, the river and the wall (16.394–418; see [[pronous]]), and he has no fight left in him: “he sat crouching in the well-polished chariot; / for he was struck out of his wits, and out of his hands / the reins had slipped” (16.402–404). The same words, ἐκ … πλήγη φρένας, are said of Asius' charioteer, who “was struck out of the wits he had before” and did not dare to turn his horses before Antilochus speared him (13.394–397; see [[asius]]).

**On the spear.** Patroclus comes up and stabs him “on the right jaw, and drove it through his teeth” (16.405), then drags him by the spear over the rail of the chariot, “as when a man / sitting on a jutting rock drags a sacred fish / out of the sea onto the shore with line and gleaming bronze” (16.406–408; see [[the-fisherman]] and [[patroclus]]): “so he dragged him gaping from the chariot on his shining spear, / and threw him down on his face; and as he fell his spirit left him” (16.409–410). The gleaming bronze of the hook is ἤνοπι χαλκῷ: the adjective ἦνοψ has the letters of his father's name, Ἤνοπος (16.401; see [[enops-father-of-thestor]]).

**Other men named Thestor.** Homer's other Thestors are known only as fathers: of the seer Calchas (Θεστορίδης, 1.69; see [[thestor]] and [[calchas]]), and of Alcmaon, whom Sarpedon kills at the Achaean wall (12.394; see [[alcmaon]]).
