summary: Croesus, the last king of Lydia (c. 560–546 BCE), proverbial for his wealth, who attacked Persia on the strength of an oracle and lost his kingdom.
---

Herodotus tells his story: his talk with Solon about happiness (1.30–33; see [[solon]]), the oracle that if he made war on the Persians he would destroy a great empire (1.53), his defeat by Cyrus, and the god's answer to his reproach, that he should have asked whose empire was meant (1.91). Aristotle gives the oracle as a hexameter: Κροῖσος Ἅλυν διαβὰς μεγάλην ἀρχὴν καταλύσει, “Croesus, crossing the Halys, will destroy a great empire.” The Halys was the river between Lydia and the Persian empire.
