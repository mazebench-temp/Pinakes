κρατερός τʼ Ἐφιάλτης, “mighty Ephialtes” (5.385), is named with his brother at the head of Dione's examples of gods who suffered at the hands of men (see [[dione]]). The Iliad tells nothing of him apart from Otus; their story is told under [[otus]] and [[the-aloadae-bind-ares]]. The Odyssey calls him τηλεκλειτός, “far-famed” (Od. 11.308).

The name has been explained from ἐφάλλομαι, “leap upon”. In later Greek ἐφιάλτης is also the word for a nightmare, and it still is in Greek today.
