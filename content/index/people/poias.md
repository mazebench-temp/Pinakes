Ποιάντιος, “of Poias”, is a patronymic adjective, not a noun in -ίδης. Merry counts the form among Homer's Aeolisms, quoting the rule that the Aeolic dialect avoided patronymics in -ίδης and -ίων and used possessive adjectives in -ιος instead, as the Romans used their family names, and that Homer mostly joins them with υἱός, as in Τελαμώνιος Αἴας, “Telamonian Ajax” (*Iliad* 2.528), and Σθένελος Καπανήϊος υἱός, “Sthenelus, the son of Capaneus” (*Iliad* 4.367; Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 3.190; see [[telamonian]]). Homer says nothing else of Poias. The later legends of the father and the son, and of the bow of Heracles, are told under [[philoctetes]].
