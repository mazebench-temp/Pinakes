Homer names him only in the history of the helmet that Meriones gives Odysseus for the night raid. Autolycus stole it from Amyntor's house at Eleon, “and at Scandeia he gave it to Amphidamas of Cythera; / and Amphidamas gave it to Molus to be a guest-gift” (10.268–269; see [[the-boars-tusk-helmet]], [[autolycus]] and [[scandeia]]). “Of Cythera” is all the poem says of him. A ξεινήϊον, a guest-gift, is a gift of guest-friendship, so he and Molus were guest-friends (see [[guest-gifts]] and [[molus]]). The name joins ἀμφί, “on both sides”, and δαμάω, “tame”, like Iphidamas.

## Others of the name

Patroclus killed the son of another Amphidamas as a boy, not meaning to, in a quarrel over knucklebones, and so was brought from Opus to Peleus' house (23.85–88; see [[patroclus]] and [[opus]]). Nothing joins that man to the man of Cythera but the name. Hesiod crossed to Chalcis for the funeral games of “wise-hearted Amphidamas” and won a tripod with a song (*Works and Days* 654–657; see [[hesiod]]).
