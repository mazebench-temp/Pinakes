υἱὸν δʼ Ἀντιμάχοιο, “the son of Antimachus”, comes before the name, and the name a verse later: Leonteus, “offshoot of Ares”, hits Hippomachus with his spear “at the belt” (κατὰ ζωστῆρα, 12.188–189; see [[leonteus]], [[offshoot-of-ares]] and [[belt]]). Telamonian Ajax hits Amphius there (5.615), and Menelaus Podes (17.578). He is the first of Leonteus' five victims at the gate.

The poem names one other Antimachus, the Trojan who took Alexander's gold and would not let Helen be given back, and whose sons Peisander and Hippolochus Agamemnon killed in Book 11 (11.122–147; see [[antimachus]] and [[sons-of-antimachus]]). If Hippomachus is his son, he is the third to die at Achaean hands; the poem does not make the connection.

The name means “horse-fighter”, ἵππος and μάχομαι: the Phrygians are ἱππόμαχοι, “who fight from horses” (10.431).
