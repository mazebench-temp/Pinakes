## Etymology

Μενέλαος (Menelaos) = μένος (might) or μένω (stand fast) + λαός (the people, the host): "who withstands the host" or "whose might is the people's".

## In Book 1

{{quote:11a34307-3361-54f4-928a-c633d70082b8}}

τιμὴν ἀρνύμενοι Μενελάῳ: "to win satisfaction for Menelaus". Achilles reduces the war to a private debt: the Trojans owe Menelaus τιμή, compensation, and Achilles fights to collect it, though he has no quarrel of his own. Helen is not named in Book 1. See [[war-for-menelaus]].

He is also one of the two sons of Atreus whom Chryses begs "most of all" (1.16, 1.375).

## Outside Homer

Paris' abduction of Helen from Menelaus' house was told in the *Cypria*. Menelaus fights Paris in a duel in Iliad 3. In the Odyssey he is home at Sparta with Helen after years of wandering by way of Egypt (Book 4).
