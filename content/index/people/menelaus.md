## Etymology

Μενέλαος (Menelaos) = μένος (might) or μένω (stand fast) + λαός (the people, the host): "who withstands the host" or "whose might is the people's".

## Mythology

In the Odyssey he sailed from Troy with Nestor, lost his helmsman at Sunium, and was driven by a storm off Malea, losing ships on Crete and coming to Egypt with five (see [[the-wanderings-of-menelaus]] and [[phrontis-son-of-onetor]]). He wandered for years among men of another tongue, gathering wealth; the gods held him in Egypt until he offered them hecatombs, and there Proteus told him the fates of Locrian Ajax, of Agamemnon and of Odysseus, and that he himself would not die in Argos but be sent to the Elysian plain, because he had Helen and was the son-in-law of Zeus. He came home in the eighth year, the last of the Achaeans (*Odyssey* 1.286; 3.276–312; 4.81–92, 4.351–586; see [[the-returns-of-the-achaeans]] and [[egypt]]); at Sparta he and Helen receive Telemachus in the fourth book.

## Outside Homer

Paris' abduction of Helen from Menelaus' house was told in the *Cypria*.
