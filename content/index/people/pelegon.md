**A river's son.** Pelegon has no part in the story except as a link in his son's lineage, and the poem gives it twice. The narrator gives it when Achilles leaps at Asteropaeus in the Scamander: the Axius “of the broad streams” begot Pelegon on Periboea, the eldest of the daughters of Acessamenus, “for with her the deep-eddying river lay” (21.141–143; see [[axius]], [[periboea]], [[acessamenus]] and [[deep-eddying]]). Asteropaeus gives it again when Achilles asks who he is, beginning from the river, “who begot Pelegon, famed for his spear; and he, they say, / was my father” (21.159–160; see [[asteropaeus]] and [[genealogy-in-battle]]). κλυτὸν ἔγχεϊ, “famed for his spear”, is all that is said of the man himself (see [[spear]]).

**The pattern of the lineage.** A river at the head of a mortal family is told in the same words elsewhere. Of Diocles of Pherae, whose twin sons Aeneas kills, the narrator says, “and his lineage was from the river / Alpheus, which flows broad through the land of the Pylians, / who begot Ortilochus, lord over many men” (5.544–546; see [[diocles]], [[ortilochus]] and [[alpheus]]): γένος … ἐκ ποταμοῖο Ἀλφειοῦ, ὅς τʼ εὐρὺ ῥέει … ὃς τέκετʼ Ὀρτίλοχον, as Asteropaeus says ἐμοὶ γενεὴ ἐξ Ἀξιοῦ εὐρὺ ῥέοντος … ὃς τέκε Πηλεγόνα (21.157–159). The Myrmidon Menesthius was the son of the river Spercheius: Polydora, the daughter of Peleus, bore him to the river, “a woman who lay with a god” (16.173–176; see [[menesthius-son-of-spercheius]], [[spercheius]] and [[polydora]]). Over Asteropaeus' body Achilles answers that a river's lineage cannot strive with the lineage of Zeus (21.184–191; see [[zeus-is-mightier-than-rivers]]).

**“They say.”** τὸν δʼ ἐμέ φασι / γείνασθαι, “and he, they say, / was my father” (21.159–160): Asteropaeus gives his father as report, as Aeneas, a book earlier, gave Achilles' own descent, “They say that you are the offspring of noble Peleus” (20.206; see [[genee]]). Telemachus, asked whether he is the son of Odysseus, answers that his mother says so, but that no one knows his own begetting (*Odyssey* 1.215–216).

**The name.** Walter Leaf takes Pelegon for a Thracian eponym, since a tribe of Pelagones is heard of in that country (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 21.141; see the translation's note on 21.141–143).

## Outside Homer

[[Strabo]] reports that the Paeonians were once called Pelagonians, and Pelagonia formerly Orestia, so that it is “not unreasonable” that Asteropaeus, one of the leaders who came from Paeonia to Ilios, is called the son of Pelegon (*Geography* 7, fr. 38, fr. 39). He counts Pelagonia in Upper Macedonia, and the Erigon, which runs through it, falls into the Axius (7.7.8; 7, fr. 20).
