## The name

The name is borne by two other men in Homer: the Trojan whom Leonteus kills at the Achaean wall (*Iliad* 12.191; see [[antiphates]]) and a son of Melampus, grandfather of the seer Amphiaraus (*Odyssey* 15.242–243; see [[antiphates-son-of-melampus]]). W. W. Merry reports Fäsi's view that Antiphates and Lamus, whose “steep citadel” the ships reach first (*Odyssey* 10.81), are the double title of the murderous king of the land, the one from the root of φένω, πέφαται, “slay”, the other from λαιμός, “throat”, and with Lamus he compares Lamia, the child-devouring ogress (*Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on *Odyssey* 10.81; see [[telepylus]]). The poem declines the name two ways, the genitive Ἀντιφάταο (*Odyssey* 10.106, 10.199) as from Ἀντιφάτης and the accusative Ἀντιφατῆα (*Odyssey* 10.114) as from Ἀντιφατεύς, and Merry notes that Eustathius quotes the like pair Γηρυόνης and Γηρυονεύς (Merry on *Odyssey* 10.114; see the Odyssey translation's note on 10.114).

## Family

His wife is a giantess, “as huge as a mountain peak” (*Odyssey* 10.113), and his daughter, “the strong daughter of Antiphates the Laestrygonian”, draws water at the spring Artacia outside the city (*Odyssey* 10.105–108; see [[artacia]]). The poem names neither of them.

## Descriptions

- Λαιστρυγών, “the Laestrygonian” (Λαιστρυγόνος Ἀντιφάταο, *Odyssey* 10.106, 10.199; see [[laestrygonians]]).
- κλυτός, “famous” (κλυτὸν Ἀντιφατῆα, *Odyssey* 10.114; see [[famous]]), the epithet of his house, κλυτὰ δώματα, “the glorious house” (*Odyssey* 10.112), as of the house of Aeolus (*Odyssey* 10.60).
- The man who “devised a grim destruction” for Odysseus' men (ὃς δὴ τοῖσιν ἐμήσατο λυγρὸν ὄλεθρον, *Odyssey* 10.115).

## Outside Homer

Ovid tells the story through Macareus, one of the companions: “From there we came to the old city of Lamus the Laestrygonian; Antiphates ruled in that land” (see [[lamus]]). Macareus was sent to him with two others; he and one companion barely found safety in flight, and the third stained the impious mouth of the Laestrygonian with his blood; Antiphates pursued them and roused his people, who hurled stones and beams and sank both men and ships (*Metamorphoses* 14.233–240; see [[ovid]]). Strabo, who believed that Homer drew the wanderings from history, places inhospitable Laestrygonians, with the Cyclopes, in the country about Aetna and Leontini in Sicily (*Geography* 1.2.9; see [[strabo]]).
