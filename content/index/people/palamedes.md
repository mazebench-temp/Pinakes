title: Palamedes

kind: person
tags: [achaean]
summary: Son of Nauplius of Euboea, the cleverest of the Greeks at Troy, credited with inventing letters, numbers and draughts; he exposed Odysseus' feigned madness, and Odysseus later contrived his death.
aliases: [Palamedes son of Nauplius]
---

Homer never mentions him. The Cypria told that he exposed Odysseus' pretended madness, and that Odysseus and Diomedes drowned him while he was fishing (Proclus, summary of the *Cypria*; Pausanias 10.31.2; see [[odysseus-feigns-madness]]). In the tragedians' version Odysseus forged a letter from Priam and hid gold in his tent, and the army stoned him as a traitor; Aeschylus, Sophocles and Euripides each wrote a *Palamedes*. He was remembered as an inventor, of letters, numbers, weights and measures and the game of draughts among other things (Gorgias, *Defense of Palamedes* 30; see [[gorgias]]), and as the type of the wise man unjustly condemned: Socrates, in Plato's *Apology*, looks forward to meeting him among the dead (41b; see [[socrates]]).
