The origin of the name Λαέρτης (Laertes) is uncertain.

{{quote:0462bfbd-d0ab-5278-9d34-e47bbd209fe9}}

Λαερτιάδης = Λαέρτης + -ιάδης (“son of”). The Iliad's first use of it is Athena's address to Odysseus when she sends him to stop the flight to the ships. The whole line, διογενὲς Λαερτιάδη πολυμήχανʼ Ὀδυσσεῦ, is a fixed formula of address, and the Odyssey uses it often. See [[son-of-laertes]].

{{notes}}

## Elsewhere in Homer

In the Odyssey Laertes is the son of Arceisius (*Odyssey* 16.117–120) and the husband of Anticleia (*Odyssey* 11.85). While Odysseus is away he lives apart on his farm with an old servant woman, grieving for his son (*Odyssey* 1.189–193, 11.187–196). Odysseus finds him in his orchard and proves who he is by his scar and by naming the trees his father had given him as a boy (*Odyssey* 24.205–348). Laertes remembers taking Nericus when he ruled the Cephallenians (*Odyssey* 24.377–378), and at the end Athena gives him strength to kill Eupeithes with a spear (*Odyssey* 24.513–525).

## Outside Homer

Tragedy sometimes makes Odysseus a son of Sisyphus rather than of Laertes, a slur on his cunning (Sophocles, *Ajax* 189).
