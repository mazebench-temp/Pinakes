Κάστωρ Ὑλακίδης, “Castor, son of Hylax” (*Odyssey* 14.204): Hylax is named only in the patronymic of the father whom the disguised Odysseus gives himself in his false tale to Eumaeus, and the poem says nothing else of him (see [[castor-son-of-hylax]] and [[the-tale-to-eumaeus]]). The name has the stem of ὑλακτέω, “bark”, the verb of the dogs on the shield of Achilles (*Iliad* 18.586), and of ὑλακόμωροι, “barking”, the word for Eumaeus' dogs when they run at the stranger in the same book (*Odyssey* 14.29; see [[dogs]]). In Latin poetry Hylax is the name of a dog: *et Hylax in limine latrat*, “and Hylax is barking on the threshold” (Virgil, *Eclogues* 8.107).
