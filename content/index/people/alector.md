# Ἀλέκτορος (Od. 4.10) is the only occurrence of the name in Homer, and it is not in the repository's Aristotle.
grc: [Ἀλέκτορος]
---

υἱέι δὲ Σπάρτηθεν Ἀλέκτορος ἤγετο κούρην, “And for his son he was bringing the daughter of Alector of Sparta” (*Odyssey* 4.10). Σπάρτηθεν, “from Sparta”, goes closely with Ἀλέκτορος, not with ἤγετο, since the wedding was being held at Sparta (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 4.10; see the translation's note on *Odyssey* 4.10, and [[sparta]]). The bride is not named, and nothing more is said of her father. A scholium that Merry reports makes Menelaus' attendant Eteoneus the son of Alector, Menelaus' fellow father-in-law, though the poem calls Eteoneus the son of Boethous (Merry on 4.22; see [[eteoneus]] and [[boethous]]).
