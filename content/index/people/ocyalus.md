## The name

ὠκύαλος, “swift-sailing”, from ὠκύς, “swift”, and ἅλς, “sea”, is said of ships: Hector takes hold of the stern of “a fine, swift-sailing ship, which had brought Protesilaus” to Troy (*Iliad* 15.704–705; see [[swift-sailing-ships]]). The Odyssey has the adjective again in later books, of Odysseus' ship as it passes the Sirens and of a Phoenician ship (*Odyssey* 12.182, 15.473). W. W. Merry counts the Phaeacian names among those “taken from circumstances of seafaring life” (*Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on *Odyssey* 8.111).
