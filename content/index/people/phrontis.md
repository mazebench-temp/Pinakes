**The mother of the sons of Panthous.** Euphorbus speaks to Menelaus over the body of Patroclus. Menelaus has killed his brother Hyperenor, widowed his wife “and brought accursed lamentation and grief upon his parents” (17.36–37; see [[hyperenor]]), and Euphorbus would end their lamentation with Menelaus' head and armor, laid in the hands of his father and mother (see [[euphorbus]], [[panthous]] and [[bereaved-parents]]). The parents of Hyperenor are thus Panthous and Phrontis, and she is the mother of both brothers; whether she is also the mother of Polydamas, the third son of Panthous, the poem does not say (see [[polydamas]]). Menelaus kills Euphorbus a moment later (17.43–60). δῖα, “brilliant”, is said of goddesses and of noble women, as of Helen, “brilliant among women” (3.171; see [[brilliant]]).

**The name.** Φρόντις is the noun φροντίς, “thought, care”, which Homer does not otherwise use. The Odyssey gives the name to a man, Menelaus' steersman Phrontis, son of Onetor, the best of men at steering a ship in a storm, whom Apollo killed with his gentle arrows off Sunium on the voyage home (*Odyssey* 3.278–285; see [[menelaus]]).
