## Family

“And Assaracus fathered Capys, and he fathered Anchises, his son” (20.239). Capys is the middle link in the branch of the royal house that runs from Assaracus, a son of Tros, to Aeneas, and Homer tells nothing of him but his place in it (20.231–240; see [[assaracus]], [[anchises]], [[aeneas]] and [[genealogy-in-battle]]).

## Outside Homer

In [[apollodorus|Apollodorus]] Capys is the son of Assaracus by Hieromneme, daughter of the river Simoeis, and marries Themiste, a daughter of Ilus, who bears him Anchises (*Library* 3.12.2). Through his marriage the two branches of the house, which divide in Homer at the sons of Tros, meet again (see [[ilus]]). [[virgil|Virgil]] gives the name to other men: a companion of Aeneas (*Aeneid* 1.183, 2.35), from whom, he says, Capua takes its name (10.145), and a king of Alba Longa among Aeneas' descendants (6.768).

Among the tales of Aeneas after the war Strabo reports one in which he founded Capyae (Καπύας) near Mantinea in Arcadia and named it after Capys (*Geography* 13.1.53; see [[strabo-on-the-rule-of-aeneas]]).
