Homer knows two heroes called Ajax: the great Ajax, son of Telamon, from Salamis, and the lesser, swift Ajax, son of Oileus, from Locris. Book 1 does not say which is meant. Ancient and modern readers usually assume the greater, whom Agamemnon ranks with Odysseus and Achilles as holders of prizes worth taking.

## Outside Homer

Telamonian Ajax holds off Hector at the ships (Iliad 15), and after Achilles' death he loses the contest for his armor to Odysseus and kills himself. The *Little Iliad* told the story, and Sophocles dramatized it. His shade will not speak to Odysseus in the underworld (Odyssey 11.543–564). The Locrian Ajax drags Cassandra from Athena's altar at the sack of Troy.
