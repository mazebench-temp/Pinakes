Αὐγηϊάδης: "son of Augeas", said of [[Agasthenes]], father of [[Polyxeinus]]. The Catalogue also tells, without naming him, how Phyleus "went off to live in Dulichium, angry with his father" (2.629); later tradition made Augeas that father (see [[phyleus-and-augeas]]).

## Elsewhere in Homer

In Nestor's tale of his youth Augeas, "lord of men", is king of the Epeians. He kept the four prize-winning horses and the chariot that Neleus had sent to race for a tripod in Elis, and sent the driver home grieving, one of the wrongs that led to the war between Pylos and the Epeians (11.698–702).

## Outside Homer

Later writers made Augeas a son of Helios, the Sun, and counted him among the Argonauts (Apollonius, *Argonautica* 1.172–175). For his fifth labor Heracles cleaned the dung from Augeas' cattle yards in one day by turning the rivers Alpheus and Peneius through them. Augeas refused the promised tenth of his cattle; his son Phyleus testified for Heracles, and Augeas drove them both out (Apollodorus 2.5.5). Later Heracles returned, killed Augeas and his sons, and gave the kingdom to Phyleus (Apollodorus 2.7.2). Pindar tells that Heracles then founded the Olympic games (*Olympian* 10.24–59). Theocritus' twenty-fifth idyll shows Heracles visiting Augeas' herds with Phyleus as his guide.
