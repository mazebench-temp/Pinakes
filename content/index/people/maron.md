## The name

W. W. Merry takes the name to be cognate with Ismarus, the town of the Cicones, and with Maroneia, the later name of the Ciconian city (*Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on *Odyssey* 9.39; see [[ismarus]]). Herodotus names Maroneia among the Greek cities of the Thracian coast that Xerxes passed, with the lake Ismaris lying between Maroneia and Stryme (Herodotus 7.109.1; see [[herodotus]] and [[cicones]]).

## Family

Μάρων, Ἐυάνθεος υἱός, “Maron, son of Euanthes” (*Odyssey* 9.197; see [[euanthes]]). He lived with his wife and a child in the grove of Apollo at Ismarus, and his house had slaves and handmaids and a housekeeper (*Odyssey* 9.199–207; see [[handmaids]]).

## Outside Homer

In Euripides' satyr play *Cyclops* the wine that Odysseus carries is still Maron's gift, but Maron is “the son of the god”, of Bacchus, and Silenus asks whether he is the one he once reared in his arms (*Cyclops* 141–143; see [[euripides]] and [[polyphemus-the-cyclops]]). A scholium on Apollonius of Rhodes (on 3.996) makes his father Euanthes the son of Dionysus and Ariadne, as Merry reports (Merry on *Odyssey* 9.197). A fragment printed with Strabo's seventh book, citing “the Geographer”, places a hero-shrine of Maron near Maroneia, by the lake whose outflow was called the Odysseion (*Geography* 7 fr. 44a; see [[strabo]]). The wine of Maroneia kept something of its fame: in Pliny's day, Merry notes, it was commonly mixed with eight measures of water to one (Pliny, *Natural History* 14.6, as Merry quotes it; Merry on *Odyssey* 9.209).
