In Sophocles' *Antigone* Haemon argues with his father for Antigone's life (631–765), and the messenger tells how he found her hanged, lunged at Creon with his sword, missed, and killed himself (1226–1243). He is not the Pylian Haemon of the Iliad (see [[haemon]]); the Theban Haemon whose son Maeon fights at Thebes in Iliad 4 may be another figure of the same family (see [[haemon-of-thebes]]). Aristotle's example of the deed about to be done knowingly and not done (1454a1–2; see [[haemon-threatens-creon]]).
