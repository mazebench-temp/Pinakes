## The name

Εὐρύνομος is formed from εὐρύς, “wide”, and the root of νέμω, “deal out, hold”; it is the masculine of Εὐρυνόμη, the name of the daughter of Oceanus who took in Hephaestus when he fell from heaven (*Iliad* 18.398–405; see [[eurynome]]) and of Penelope's housekeeper in later books of the Odyssey (*Odyssey* 17.495 and after).

## Family

He is one of the four sons of Aegyptius. The narrator names him with the brother whom the Cyclops ate and the two who stay on the farms: “one kept company with the suitors, / Eurynomus, and two always kept to their father's fields” (*Odyssey* 2.21–22; see [[aegyptius]] and [[antiphus-son-of-aegyptius]]).

## Elsewhere in Homer

On the day of the slaughter a suitor Eurynomus is one of the six who urge the others on, with Agelaus, son of Damastor, Amphimedon, Demoptolemus, Peisander, son of Polyctor, and wise-hearted Polybus, “for they were by far the best in excellence of the suitors, / of those who still lived and fought for their lives” (*Odyssey* 22.241–245, a plain rendering; see [[the-suitors]]). This is presumably the son of Aegyptius, though the poem does not name his father there. His death is not told, but none of the suitors survives (*Odyssey* 22.381–389).
