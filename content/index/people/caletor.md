**Son of Clytius.** υἷα Κλυτίοιο Καλήτορα, “Caletor, son of Clytius” (15.419): his father sits with Priam among the elders at the Scaean gates and is Priam's brother (3.147, 20.237–238; see [[clytius]] and [[trojan-elders]]), so that Caletor is Hector's first cousin, his ἀνεψιός (15.422). Two more of Laomedon's grandsons die in this book, Dolops, son of Lampus, and Melanippus, son of Hicetaon (15.525–545, 15.568–578; see [[dolops-son-of-lampus]] and [[melanippus-son-of-hicetaon]]). καλήτωρ is also a common noun, “crier”, from καλέω, “call”: Priam's herald is the old man's “crier” (24.577; see [[heralds]]).

**The brand.** Hector and Ajax are fighting over one ship, and Caletor is carrying fire to it when Ajax's spear hits him in the chest: “He fell with a thud, and the brand dropped from his hand” (15.421; see [[ajax]] and [[fell-with-a-thud]]). Hector has vowed to burn the ships (8.180–182), and his cousin dies with the brand in his hand. δαλός, the “brand”, is named elsewhere in the Iliad only by Idomeneus, who said that Hector would not easily burn the ships, “unless the son of Cronus himself / casts a blazing firebrand on the swift ships” (13.319–320; see [[idomeneus]]).

**Over his body.** Hector's call to save the body is the verse of his exhortations, “Trojans and Lycians and Dardanians who fight hand to hand” (15.425; see [[hector]], [[exhortation]] and [[stripping-the-dead]]). Then he throws at Ajax, and the spear misses and kills Ajax's attendant Lycophron (15.429–435; see [[lycophron]]).
