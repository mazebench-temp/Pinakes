ὃ δʼ Ἀγήνορος υἱὸν Ἔχεκλον, “And Echeclus, son of Agenor” (20.474): Agenor is named only as the father of the seventh man Achilles kills after Apollo has saved Hector (see [[echeclus-son-of-agenor]] and [[achilles]]). The poem says nothing else of him.

**One Agenor or two?** The best-known Agenor of the Iliad is the son of Antenor, one of the leading Trojans, who will stand alone against Achilles before the walls in the next book until Apollo carries him away (21.544–611; see [[agenor]] and [[antenor]]). The poem does not say whether he is Echeclus' father, and the index keeps the two apart without ruling out that they are one man, as it does with the Alastor of Nestor's captains and the Alastor who carries Teucer (see [[alastor-who-carries-teucer]]).
