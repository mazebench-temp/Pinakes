The Iliad does not name Memnon. In the Odyssey, when all at Sparta weep for Odysseus, Peisistratus weeps for his brother Antilochus, “whom the splendid son of shining Dawn had killed” (*Odyssey* 4.187–188; see [[antilochus]], [[eos]] and [[peisistratus]]). Among the dead, Odysseus names Eurypylus, son of Telephus, as the handsomest man he saw after “godlike Memnon” (Μέμνονα δῖον, *Odyssey* 11.522, a plain rendering).

## Outside Homer

Hesiod makes him the son of Eos by Tithonus, whom the Iliad makes a brother of Priam (20.237): “And to Tithonus Eos bore Memnon of the bronze helmet, king of the Ethiopians, and lord Emathion” (*Theogony* 984–985, a plain rendering; see [[tithonus]] and [[hesiod]]). His coming to Troy with an army of Ethiopians was the matter of the *Aethiopis* (see [[ethiopians]]). W. W. Merry notes that Achilles killed Memnon to avenge Antilochus, and refers to Pindar, *Pythian* 6.28 and following and *Nemean* 3.59 and following (*Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 4.188).
