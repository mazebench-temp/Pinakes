Homer names him only as a link in the chain: Sisyphus “fathered a son, Glaucus, and Glaucus fathered noble Bellerophon” (6.154–155). The Lycian [[glaucus|Glaucus]] who tells the story carries his great-grandfather's name, as Greek families often named a boy after an ancestor. See [[sisyphus]], [[bellerophon]] and [[ephyra-in-argos]].

## Outside Homer

A papyrus of the Hesiodic *Catalogue of Women* tells how he came with gifts to woo Eurynome, daughter of Nisus, by Athena's counsel, but Zeus refused him children of his own; Eurynome bore Bellerophon to Poseidon in Glaucus' house (fr. 43a Merkelbach–West, fr. 7 Evelyn-White). [[apollodorus|Apollodorus]] makes him the son of Sisyphus and Merope and the father of Bellerophon by Eurymede (*Library* 1.9.3). Later writers identified him with Glaucus of Potniae in Boeotia, whose mares, maddened by Aphrodite, devoured him; Aeschylus wrote a tragedy on his death (*Glaucus of Potniae*, fragments), and Virgil recalls the mares (*Georgics* 3.267–268).
