The Odyssey names Alcmaeon and Amphilochus as the sons of Amphiaraus, at the end of the elder line of the seer Melampus (*Odyssey* 15.248; see [[amphilochus]] and [[melampus]]); Homer tells nothing else of him.

{{notes}}

## Outside Homer

Amphiaraus, sent to his death at Thebes by Eriphyle, who had been bribed with a necklace, charged his sons to avenge him; Alcmaeon led the Epigoni to take Thebes, killed his mother, and was pursued by the Erinyes until he found purification (Apollodorus 3.7.2–7; see [[the-epigonoi]] and [[alcmaeon-kills-eriphyle]]). An epic *Alcmaeonis* told his story, and Sophocles, Euripides, Astydamas and Theodectes wrote tragedies about him. In the *Poetics* he heads the list of the few houses (1453a20; see [[the-few-houses]]), his killing of Eriphyle is a traditional story that cannot be undone (1453b22–24), and Astydamas' *Alcmaeon* has him kill her in ignorance (1453b33; see [[alcmaeon-play]]).

In the Hesiodic *Catalogue of Women* the two sons of Amphiaraus, son of [[oicles|Oicles]], are among the suitors of Helen from Argos (fr. 68 Evelyn-White; see [[hesiod]]). Ephorus, as Strabo reports him, said that after the war of the Epigoni Alcmaeon joined Diomedes in conquering Aetolia and Acarnania, stayed in Acarnania when Diomedes went to Troy, founded Amphilochian Argos and named it after his brother, and that Acarnania itself was named after his son Acarnan (Strabo, *Geography* 7.7.7, 10.2.26; see [[strabo]] and [[amphilochus]]). In the *Nicomachean Ethics* Aristotle finds the reasons that compelled Euripides' Alcmaeon to kill his mother ridiculous, among the acts a man should rather die than be compelled to do (1110a27–29; see [[aristotle]]).
