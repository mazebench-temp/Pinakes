The Odyssey names Alcmaeon and Amphilochus as the sons of Amphiaraus (*Odyssey* 15.248). Amphiaraus, sent to his death at Thebes by Eriphyle, who had been bribed with a necklace, charged his sons to avenge him; Alcmaeon led the Epigoni to take Thebes, killed his mother, and was pursued by the Erinyes until he found purification (Apollodorus 3.7.2–7; see [[the-epigonoi]] and [[alcmaeon-kills-eriphyle]]). An epic *Alcmaeonis* told his story, and Sophocles, Euripides, Astydamas and Theodectes wrote tragedies about him. In the *Poetics* he heads the list of the few houses (1453a20; see [[the-few-houses]]), his killing of Eriphyle is a traditional story that cannot be undone (1453b22–24), and Astydamas' *Alcmaeon* has him kill her in ignorance (1453b33; see [[alcmaeon-play]]).
