ποιμὴν λαῶν, “shepherd of the people”, marks him as a leader (see [[shepherd-of-the-people]]). He is the second of the pair Diomedes kills after his wound, and his death is the more violent. The first man takes a spear; Hypeiron takes the “great sword” on the collarbone, which cuts “the shoulder away from the neck and from the back” (5.146–147). Diomedes leaves the two where they lie and goes after the sons of Eurydamas (5.148; see [[astynous]] and [[abas-son-of-eurydamas]]).

The name seems to be formed on ὑπείρ, the epic form of ὑπέρ (“above”), as in ὑπείροχος (“preeminent”, 6.208).
