Νηλήϊος (“of Neleus”) is an adjective used as a patronymic: Νηληΐῳ υἷι, “the son of Neleus” (2.20), is Nestor, whose likeness the Dream takes when it comes to Agamemnon. See [[dream]].

## Elsewhere in Homer

Nestor tells how Heracles sacked Pylos and killed all of Neleus' twelve sons except himself (Iliad 11.690–693; see [[heracles-sacks-pylos]]), and how Augeas of Elis kept a team of four prize horses that Neleus had sent to race for a tripod, so that Neleus took a great herd of cattle and sheep in return (11.696–705). In the Odyssey Neleus is a son of Poseidon by Tyro, and the brother of Pelias (*Odyssey* 11.235–259; see [[tyro]] and [[pelias]]). He married Chloris, the daughter of Amphion of Orchomenus, for her beauty, with a countless bride-price, and she bore him Nestor, Chromius, Periclymenus and a daughter, Pero, whom he would give only to the man who drove off the cattle of Iphiclus (*Odyssey* 11.281–297; see [[chloris]], [[chromius-son-of-neleus]], [[periclymenus]], [[pero]] and [[iphiclus]]). The seer who won her, Melampus, avenged himself on Neleus, who had held his possessions by force for a year (*Odyssey* 15.229–237; see [[melampus]]). Pylos is “the well-built citadel of Neleus”, and the polished stones before Nestor's doors were his seat before he went down to Hades (*Odyssey* 3.4, 3.406–410).
