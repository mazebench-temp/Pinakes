Νηλήϊος (“of Neleus”) is an adjective used as a patronymic: Νηληΐῳ υἷι, “the son of Neleus” (2.20), is Nestor, whose likeness the Dream takes when it comes to Agamemnon. See [[dream]].

## Elsewhere in Homer

Nestor tells how Heracles sacked Pylos and killed all of Neleus' twelve sons except himself, and how Neleus later seized a team of four prize horses from Augeas of Elis (Iliad 11.690–702). In the Odyssey Neleus is a son of Poseidon by Tyro, and the twin of Pelias (11.235–259). His wife Chloris bore him Nestor, Chromius, Periclymenus and a daughter, Pero (Odyssey 11.281–297).
