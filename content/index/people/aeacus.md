Αἰακίδης = Αἰακός + -ίδης: “descendant of Aeacus”, used of his son Peleus and of his grandson Achilles. It first appears in the Trojan Catalogue, in the foretelling of deaths “at the hands of the swift-footed grandson of Aeacus in the river” (2.860, 2.874). See [[the-river-battle-foretold]].

{{notes}}

## Elsewhere in Homer

Over the body of Asteropaeus, a grandson of the river Axius, Achilles sets out his descent: Zeus begot Aeacus, Aeacus begot Peleus, and Peleus begot Achilles (21.187–189; see [[zeus-is-mightier-than-rivers]]).

## Outside Homer

Aeacus was the son of Zeus and the nymph Aegina, and king of the island named after his mother. Ovid tells how Zeus repopulated the island after a plague by turning ants into men, the Myrmidons (*Metamorphoses* 7). Plato makes him one of the three judges of the dead, with Minos and Rhadamanthys (*Gorgias* 523e–524a), and in the *Apology* Socrates looks forward to meeting below the true judges, Minos, Rhadamanthys, Aeacus and Triptolemus (41a; see [[plato]]).
