ποδωκείῃσι πεποιθώς, "trusting in his speed of foot": a lookout must be able to run back with his news. The goddess borrows the voice of the man whose report the Trojans would expect. His name means "citizen" (πολίτης, from πόλις, "city"), and Iris ends her speech by telling Hector to lead out "his own citizens" (πολιήτας, 2.806).

Later in the Iliad Polites helps his wounded brother Deiphobus out of the fighting (13.533–539), and Priam names him among the sons left to him after Hector's death (24.250). In Virgil, Neoptolemus kills him before his father's eyes at the altar on the night Troy falls (*Aeneid* 2.526–532).
