## Descriptions

- μάντις ἀνὴρ ἠύς τε μέγας τε, “a seer here, a man tall and brave” (*Odyssey* 9.508): the verse-end ἠύς τε μέγας τε is the Iliad's for tall warriors, as when Priam asks Helen who “this Achaean man is, so tall and brave” (*Iliad* 3.167; again 2.653, 3.226, 11.221, 23.664; see [[divination]]).
- ὃς μαντοσύνῃ ἐκέκαστο, “who excelled in prophecy” (*Odyssey* 9.509), and μαντευόμενος κατεγήρα Κυκλώπεσσιν, he “grew old among the Cyclopes as their seer” (*Odyssey* 9.510; see [[prophecy]]).

## Outside Homer

In Ovid, Galatea tells how Telemus, son of Eurymus, “whom no bird had deceived”, came to Etna and warned the Cyclops that Ulysses would take the one eye he had in the middle of his forehead; the Cyclops laughed and told the most foolish of seers that another, Galatea, had taken it already, and so scorned the warning that was true (*Metamorphoses* 13.770–775; see [[ovid]] and [[polyphemus-the-cyclops]]). Theocritus names him too, as W. W. Merry notes (*Idylls* 6.23; *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on *Odyssey* 9.509).
