Πάμμονά τʼ Ἀντίφονόν τε βοὴν ἀγαθόν τε Πολίτην, “and Pammon and Antiphonus and Polites, good at the war cry” (24.250). He heads the second verse of the list of nine sons whom Priam rebukes as he makes ready to ransom Hector, and the poem says nothing more of him (24.248–264; see [[sons-of-priam]], [[antiphonus]] and [[polites]]).

## Outside Homer

Apollodorus names Pammon among the sons whom Hecuba bore to Priam, after Deiphobus and Helenus (*Library* 3.12.5; see [[apollodorus]] and [[hecuba]]).
