Amphimachus and his cousin [[Thalpius]] lead the first division of the Epeians, "sons, one of Cteatus and one of Eurytus, both of Actor's line" (2.621). The name joins ἀμφί, "on both sides", and μάχη, "battle".

## Elsewhere in Homer

Hector, throwing at Teucer, hits Amphimachus in the chest as he enters the fight, and he falls with a crash (13.185–187). Stichius and Menestheus, the Athenian leaders, carry off his body (13.195–196). Then "Poseidon was angered in his heart for his grandson who had fallen" (13.206–207): his father Cteatus was one of the twins whom Nestor calls Poseidon's sons (11.750–752).

Another Amphimachus, son of Nomion, leads the Carians with his brother Nastes (2.870–871; [[amphimachus-of-caria]]).

## Outside Homer

Apollodorus lists him among the suitors of Helen (3.10.8).
