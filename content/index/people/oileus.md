{{quote:178e4953-dad3-587e-b806-70070e4cf292}}

Oileus does not come to Troy; he is named through his sons. His legitimate son leads the [[Locrians]] (2.527).

{{quote:153e56c8-d183-5a21-9e42-b1b2269d7b64}}

His bastard son [[Medon]], "whom Rhene bore to Oileus, sacker of cities" (2.728), takes command of Philoctetes' men after Philoctetes is left behind on Lemnos. Book 13 adds that Medon lived in exile in Phylace, because he had killed a kinsman of his stepmother Eriopis, Oileus' wife (13.694–697).

πτολίπορθος "sacker of cities" ([[sacker-of-cities]]) is an epithet Homer gives chiefly to Achilles and Odysseus. Which cities Oileus sacked is not said.

A Trojan of the same name, the charioteer of Bienor, is killed by Agamemnon (11.93–98).
