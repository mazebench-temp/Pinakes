Arsinous is named once, as the father of [[Hecamede]]: θυγατέρʼ Ἀρσινόου μεγαλήτορος, “the daughter of great-hearted Arsinous” (11.626; see [[great-hearted]]). The poem says no more of him. It does not say whether he ruled on Tenedos, or what became of him when Achilles sacked it (11.625; see [[tenedos]] and [[achilles-raids]]). Briseis and Chryseis are known by names formed from their fathers', Briseus and Chryses (9.132, 1.11); Hecamede has a name of her own, and her father is named only to say whose daughter she was.
