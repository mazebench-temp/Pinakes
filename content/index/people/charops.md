He is the fifth Trojan Odysseus strikes in his stand alone, after Deiopites, Thoon, Ennomus and Chersidamas (11.420–426; see [[odysseus]]). The poem does not say that he dies. His brother speaks as if he were already lost: today, Socus tells Odysseus, “either you will boast over both sons of Hippasus, / having killed two such men and taken their armor, / or struck by my spear you will lose your life” (11.431–433; see [[socus]]). αὐτοκασίγνητος, “own brother” (11.427), is a full brother, as Helen's brothers are, “whom one mother bore with me” (3.238; see [[kasignetos]]). The wound of Charops brings his brother into the fight, as Iphidamas' death brought Coon (11.248–250; see [[coon]]), and the second brother dies too.

The name is the adjective χαροπός, “bright-eyed” or “fierce-eyed”, said of lions on Heracles' baldric (Od. 11.611). Charopus, the father of Nireus, has a different name (2.672).
