τοῖα Διὸς θυγάτηρ ἔχε φάρμακα μητιόεντα, / ἐσθλά, τά οἱ Πολύδαμνα πόρεν, Θῶνος παράκοιτις / Αἰγυπτίη, “Such cunning drugs the daughter of Zeus had, / good ones, which Polydamna had given her, the wife of Thon, / a woman of Egypt” (*Odyssey* 4.227–229; see [[helen]], [[nepenthe]] and [[thon]]). Her gift is one of the Egyptian gifts Helen keeps at Sparta, with the golden distaff and the work basket that Alcandre of Thebes gave her (*Odyssey* 4.130–132; see [[alcandre]]). The land she comes from is the land of drugs, “where the grain-giving earth bears the most / drugs, many good and many grim, growing intermixed”, and every man is a healer (*Odyssey* 4.229–232; see [[egypt]]).

## The name

Aristarchus took Πολύδαμνα for a proper name; another reading made it an adjective of the drugs, πολύδαμνα φάρμακα, drugs “that subdue many” (scholia, in the critical note of Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 4.228), from πολύς, “many”, and δάμνημι, “subdue”.

## Outside Homer

Herodotus quotes the verses that name her (*Odyssey* 4.227–230) as a sign that Homer knew the Egyptian priests' story of Helen's stay in Egypt, though he set it aside as less fitting for epic (Herodotus 2.116.1–4; see [[herodotus]] and [[helen-in-egypt]]). Strabo quotes the verse on “Polydamna, the wife of Thon” where he records an old city Thonis on the coast toward Canopus, named for the king who received Menelaus and Helen (*Geography* 17.1.16; see [[strabo]]).
