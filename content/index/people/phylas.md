Φύλαντος θυγάτηρ, “the daughter of Phylas” (16.181): Phylas is first named as the father of Polymele, the girl whom Hermes loved in the chorus of Artemis (see [[polymele]] and [[the-birth-of-eudorus]]). When she had borne her son and Echecles, son of Actor, had married her and taken her to his own house, the child stayed with her father: τὸν δʼ ὃ γέρων Φύλας εὖ ἔτρεφεν ἠδʼ ἀτίταλλεν / ἀμφαγαπαζόμενος ὡς εἴ θʼ ἑὸν υἱὸν ἐόντα, “and the old man Phylas reared the boy well and cherished him, / loving him as if he were his own son” (16.191–192; see [[eudorus]] and [[echecles]]). τρέφω and ἀτιτάλλω, “rear” and “cherish”, are the pair of verbs with which Hera tells how Oceanus and Tethys nursed her in their halls (14.202 = 14.303; see [[tethys]]), and with which, in the Odyssey, Penelope speaks of the nurse who reared Odysseus (*Odyssey* 19.354).

The care of a child not one's own is a mark of love in the Iliad's stories of upbringing. Peleus received the exile Phoenix and “loved me as a father loves his son” (9.481), and Priam honored his son-in-law Imbrius “as he honored his children” (13.176; see [[phoenix]], [[imbrius]] and [[exile-and-refuge]]). Phylas raises his daughter's child. Walter Leaf suggested that the arrangement may be a distant memory of a kind of marriage in which the children belonged to the mother's family, not the father's (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 16.191).

## Outside Homer

Apollodorus names another Phylas, the king of Ephyra in Thesprotia, whose city Heracles took and whose daughter Astyoche bore him Tlepolemus (*Library* 2.7.6, 2.7.8; see [[astyocheia]] and [[ephyra]]). The Iliad gives Tlepolemus' mother another name, Astyocheia, and does not name her father (2.658).
