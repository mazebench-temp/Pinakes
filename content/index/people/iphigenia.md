## The myth

Homer does not name her. Agamemnon offers Achilles one of his three daughters, Chrysothemis, Laodice and Iphianassa (9.145), and Iphianassa was later taken to be Iphigenia. The *Cypria* told the sacrifice: Artemis, angered by Agamemnon, held the fleet at Aulis until he offered his daughter; she snatched the girl away to the land of the Taurians, put a deer on the altar, and made her immortal (Proclus, *Chrestomathy*). In Aeschylus' *Agamemnon* she is killed, gagged so that she cannot curse her father (205–247), and her death is Clytemnestra's motive. Euripides wrote of her at Aulis and among the Taurians (see [[the-sacrifice-of-iphigenia]], [[iphigenia-at-aulis]] and [[iphigenia-among-the-taurians]]).
