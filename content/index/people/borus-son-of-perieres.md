αὐτὰρ ἐπίκλησιν Βώρῳ Περιήρεος υἷι, / ὅς ῥʼ ἀναφανδὸν ὄπυιε πορὼν ἀπερείσια ἕδνα: “but by name to Borus, son of Perieres, / who married her openly, after giving a boundless bride-price” (16.177–178). Polydora, daughter of Peleus, bore the Myrmidon leader Menesthius to the river Spercheius, but the boy was called the son of Borus, her husband (see [[polydora]], [[menesthius-son-of-spercheius]] and [[the-birth-of-menesthius]]).

ἐπίκλησιν, “by name”, is elsewhere the word for a second name that people use: Areithous was called by the name of “the mace-man” (7.138), the Bear is also called by the name of the Wagon (18.487), the brightest star by the name of the Dog of Orion (22.29), and Hector's son by the name of Astyanax (22.506; see [[areithous]] and [[astyanax]]). Here it marks a father by name, set against the father by birth. The marriage is told in the language of the wooer's gifts: ὄπυιε, “married”, and πορὼν ἀπερείσια ἕδνα, “after giving a boundless bride-price”, the half-verse used in the Odyssey of the suitor who would win Penelope (*Odyssey* 19.529; see [[bride-price]]). ἀναφανδόν, “openly”, is found only here in Homer.

Borus is named once. Homer's other Borus is a Maeonian, the father of Phaestus, whom Idomeneus kills (5.43–44; see [[borus]] and [[phaestus-son-of-borus]]).

## Outside Homer

Apollodorus says only that Polydora, daughter of Peleus, was married by Borus, son of Perieres (*Library* 3.13.1; see [[perieres]]).
