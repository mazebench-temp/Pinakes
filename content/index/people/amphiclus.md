Meges watches him as he rushes in (ἐφορμηθέντα δοκεύσας) and is the quicker of the two: ἔφθη ὀρεξάμενος, he “was first to reach out” (16.314; see [[meges]]). The spear takes Amphiclus at the top of the leg, “where a man's / muscle is thickest”, and “around the point of the spear / the sinews were torn apart; and darkness covered his eyes” (16.314–316; see [[darkness-covered-his-eyes]]). He is the third of the nine men whom the Achaean leaders kill one by one when the Trojans are driven from the ships (16.306–351; see [[areilycus-killed-by-patroclus]]). The same words, ἔφθη ὀρεξάμενος, are said of Thrasymedes a few verses later, when he strikes Maris before Maris can strike Antilochus (16.322; see [[maris]]).

Walter Leaf reports that the scholia doubted whether a wound at the root of the thigh would kill at once, as the verse seems to imply; he observed that the man would soon die if the femoral artery were cut (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 16.316).

The name is made with κλέος, “fame”, as Patroclus' is. Amphiclus is named only here.
