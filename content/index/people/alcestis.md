## Descriptions

δῖα γυναικῶν, "brilliant among women" (see [[brilliant]]). Πελίαο θυγατρῶν εἶδος ἀρίστη: "most beautiful in form of the daughters of Pelias", literally "best in form". Homer names her once, as a mother, and tells nothing of her story. The name may come from ἀλκή ("strength, defense").

## Outside Homer

Her story is first told in Euripides' *Alcestis* (438 BC). Apollo won for Admetus the right to escape his fated death if someone would die in his place. His old parents refused, and Alcestis agreed. On the day of her death Heracles arrives as a guest, learns what has happened, wrestles with Death at her tomb and brings her back. In Plato's *Symposium* Phaedrus makes her the model of a love willing to die for another: the gods so admired her that they sent her soul back from Hades (179b–d). Apollodorus tells how Admetus won her, and says that Persephone sent her back, or that Heracles fought Hades for her (1.9.15). The story has been retold ever since, in Gluck's opera *Alceste* (1767) and in Rilke's poem "Alkestis".
