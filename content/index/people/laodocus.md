{{quote:0ee45413-03c4-59eb-b88c-aa1354ba2845}}

Λαόδοκος is formed from λαός (“people, host”) and the root of δέχομαι (“receive”), as in ξεινοδόκος (“host”). Ἀντηνορίδης, “son of Antenor”, is also the patronymic of his brother [[Helicaon]] (3.123).

He exists in the poem as a face. Athena does not show herself to Pandarus, as she did to Achilles (1.197–200), and nothing suggests that he knows he is listening to a goddess (see [[divine-epiphany]] and [[truth-and-deception]]). The choice has its irony. Antenor rode beside Priam to the oath sacrifice (3.262), and in Book 7 he urges the Trojans to give Helen back because they are fighting with the oaths broken (7.347–353). In Book 3 Iris came to Helen as Laodice, the wife of Helicaon (3.121–124): twice in two books a god borrows a face from Antenor's house.

A different Laodocus, a companion of [[antilochus|Antilochus]], appears in Book 17 (17.699).
