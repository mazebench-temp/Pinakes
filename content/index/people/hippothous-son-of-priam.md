Δηΐφοβόν τε καὶ Ἱππόθοον καὶ δῖον Ἀγαυόν, “and Deiphobus and Hippothous and brilliant Agauus” (24.251). He is named only in the list of nine sons whom Priam rebukes as he makes ready to ransom Hector (24.248–264; see [[sons-of-priam]] and [[priam]]). The name, “swift with horses” (ἵππος, “horse” + θοός, “swift”), is that of the leader of the Pelasgians of Larisa, an ally of Troy, whom Telamonian Ajax kills as he drags the body of Patroclus by the foot (2.840–843, 17.288–303; see [[hippothous]]).

## Outside Homer

Apollodorus names a Hippothous among the sons whom Priam had by women other than Hecuba (*Library* 3.12.5; see [[apollodorus]]).
