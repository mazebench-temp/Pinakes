**The fourth of seven.** When their leader Asteropaeus has fallen, Achilles goes after the Paeonians along the river and kills seven of them, named in two verses (21.205–210; see [[asteropaeus]], [[paeonians]] and [[achilles]]). Μνῆσόν τε, “and Mnesus”, opens the second verse. He is named nowhere else. The seven die without a word or a wound described, and the list ends only because the river, in anger, calls to Achilles from an eddy (21.211–213; see [[scamander]]).

**The name.** Μνῆσος seems to be formed from the root of μιμνῄσκω, “remember”, as are the later names in Μνησι-.
