## In Homer

Achilles names him in the bounds of the land over which Priam was once first in wealth and in sons: “all that Lesbos, the seat of Macar, shuts in, out to sea, / and Phrygia inland, and the boundless Hellespont” (24.544–545; see [[lesbos]], [[phrygia]] and [[hellespont]]). Μάκαρος ἕδος, “the seat of Macar”, names the island by its king; ἕδος is a dwelling place, as when Iris is bidden to “leave the seat of Olympus” (24.144). Homer says nothing more of him.

Walter Leaf takes Macar for a legendary king or hero of Lesbos, after whom the island was said to have been called Makaria. He finds that the tales the mythographers told of him differ so completely that there can have been no real local tradition: the name served as a peg on which to hang theories of colonization in the form of genealogies. He notes that a Makar appears also in Phocis, as the father of Amphissa (citing Pausanias 10.38.4), and finds no ground but a slight likeness of form for the proposal to identify the name with Melkart, the Baal of Tyre (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 24.544).

**The variant.** For Μάκαρος some read μακάρων, “of the blessed”, which Leaf calls no mere blunder: it is the reading of Plutarch and of Dio Chrysostom, and was taken as a compliment to the fortunate island, while other critics saw in it a tribe, the Makares, whose eponym Makar was (on 24.544; see the translation's note on 24.544). Strabo, who quotes the verse to show that Priam ruled the whole coast of the Troad, reads Μάκαρος πόλις, “the city of Macar” (*Geography* 13.1.7).

## Outside Homer

The *Homeric Hymn to Apollo* names the island in the same words among the lands that Leto passed in her wanderings, Λέσβος τʼ ἠγαθέη, Μάκαρος ἕδος Αἰολίωνος, “holy Lesbos, seat of Macar son of Aeolus” (*Homeric Hymn to Apollo* 37). Leaf sets the Hymn's list beside Achilles' verse, for it too begins ὅσσους … ἐντὸς ἔχει, “all whom … holds within” (*Homeric Hymn to Apollo* 30), and he suggests that ὅσσους would be the simpler reading here too (on 24.544).
