## The name

The first part of the name is ἶφι, “by might”, the old instrumental of ἴς, “strength”, which the catalogue uses of Amphion, who “ruled with might in Minyan Orchomenus” (ἶφι ἄνασσεν, *Odyssey* 11.284), and which begins the names Iphianassa and Iphidamas (9.145, 11.221). The second is the stem of μέδω, “rule”, as in μέδων, “ruler”.

## Family

Homer makes her “the wife of Aloeus”, Ἀλωῆος παράκοιτιν, and the mother of two short-lived sons, Otus and Ephialtes, whose father she said was Poseidon (*Odyssey* 11.305–308; see [[aloeus]], [[otus]], [[ephialtes]], [[poseidon]] and [[the-aloadae-threaten-olympus]]). The Iliad calls the two brothers “the sons of Aloeus” and gives them a stepmother, the beautiful Eeriboea, who told Hermes where they had hidden Ares (5.385–391; see [[eeriboea]] and [[the-aloadae-bind-ares]]); it does not name their mother. W. W. Merry gives the later genealogy: she was the daughter of Triops, king of Thessaly, and Aloeus, her uncle and husband, was a son of Poseidon by Canace, daughter of Aeolus (*Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on *Odyssey* 11.305; see [[aeolus-father-of-sisyphus]]).

## Outside Homer

The Hesiodic *Catalogue of Women*, as a scholiast on Apollonius reports it, made the two brothers the children of Aloeus and Iphimedeia in name, but in truth of Poseidon and Iphimedeia, and said that their father founded Halus, a city in Aetolia (fr. 6 Evelyn-White; see [[hesiod]]).
