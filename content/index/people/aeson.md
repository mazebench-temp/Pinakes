## Family

He is the first of the three sons whom Tyro bore to her husband: “And her other sons she, the queen among women, bore to Cretheus, / Aeson and Pheres and Amythaon, who fought from his chariot” (*Odyssey* 11.258–259; see [[tyro]], [[cretheus]], [[pheres]] and [[amythaon]]). Pelias and Neleus, Tyro's sons by Poseidon, were his half-brothers (see [[pelias]] and [[neleus]]). Homer says nothing more of him, and does not call Jason his son.

## Outside Homer

In the *Theogony* Jason is Αἰσονίδης, “the son of Aeson”, who brought Aeetes' daughter home to Iolcus after finishing the many grievous labors that “the great, overbearing king, / violent Pelias, reckless and harsh in deed” had laid on him (*Theogony* 992–999; see [[hesiod]], [[jason]] and [[aeetes]]). A scholion on *Odyssey* 12.69, among the fragments of the *Catalogue of Women*, says that according to Hesiod Jason was born to Aeson and Polymede, and quotes the verses “Aeson, who fathered a son, Jason, shepherd of the people, / whom Chiron reared on wooded Pelion” (fr. 13 Evelyn-White; see [[chiron]]). W. W. Merry tells the later story: after the death of Cretheus, Pelias refused his half-brother Aeson any share in the kingdom and made himself king in Iolcus, sent Aeson's son Jason to Colchis for the golden fleece for fear that he would claim his rights, and murdered Aeson while Jason was away (*Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on *Odyssey* 11.259).
