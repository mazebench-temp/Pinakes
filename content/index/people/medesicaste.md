κούρην δὲ Πριάμοιο νόθην ἔχε, Μηδεσικάστην, “and had as his wife a bastard daughter of Priam, Medesicaste” (13.173). Her husband Imbrius lived at Pedaeum before the war, then came back to Ilios and lived beside Priam, who honored him as he honored his own children; Teucer kills him at the ships (13.171–181; see [[imbrius]] and [[pedaeum]]). Nothing more is said of her.

She is the only bastard daughter of Priam named in Homer. His bastard sons fight beside their brothers, and the poem names them as they die: Democoon (4.499), Isus (11.102), Doryclus (11.490) and Cebriones (16.738; see [[nothos]], [[sons-of-priam]] and [[cebriones]]).

The name joins μήδεα, “counsels”, to the -κάστη of Epicaste, the mother of Oedipus (Od. 11.271), and of Polycaste, Nestor's daughter (Od. 3.464), from κέκασμαι, “excel”: “she who excels in counsel”. The poem uses the verb of another Trojan bride in the same book, Anchises' daughter Hippodameia, who “surpassed all the girls of her age” (ἐκέκαστο, 13.431; see [[hippodameia-daughter-of-anchises]]).
