## In Homer

In the Iliad Zeus names him among the sons of his loves, with his brother Minos: the daughter of far-famed Phoenix “bore me Minos and godlike Rhadamanthys” (14.321–322; see [[minos]], [[europa]] and [[the-loves-of-zeus]]). The Odyssey twice calls him ξανθὸς Ῥαδάμανθυς, “fair-haired Rhadamanthys” (see [[fair-haired]]). Proteus tells Menelaus that he will not die in Argos: the immortals will send him to the Elysian plain at the ends of the earth, where fair-haired Rhadamanthys is and life is easiest for men, with no snow, no great storm and no rain, but the shrill breezes of the West Wind that Oceanus always sends up to cool them (*Odyssey* 4.561–569). Alcinous tells Odysseus that the Phaeacians once carried Rhadamanthys to Euboea, the farthest of lands, to see Tityos, the son of Earth, and came home the same day without toil (*Odyssey* 7.321–326).

## Outside Homer

**The just judge.** The Hesiodic *Catalogue of Women* calls him “just Rhadamanthys” and makes him, with Minos and Sarpedon, a son of Europa (fr. 19, 19a Evelyn-White; see [[hesiod]]). In Aeschylus' *Carians* or *Europa* his mother calls him “the one of my children who is immortal”, though he lives out of her sight (fr. 99 Nauck). [[pindar|Pindar]] says that Rhadamanthys “has prospered, because his lot was the blameless fruit of the mind”, and that he does not delight his heart with deceits (*Pythian* 2.73–74); on the Isle of the Blest the good live under “the straight counsels of Rhadamanthys”, whom the great father, Cronus, the husband of Rhea, keeps beside him as his assessor (*Olympian* 2.75–77).

**Plato.** In the *Laws* the Cretan Clinias says that Minos' brother Rhadamanthys was the most just of men (624b), and the Athenian admires his way of judging: in an age when men believed that the gods plainly existed, and many were sons of gods, as he himself was said to be, he gave the parties an oath on each point in dispute and so settled cases quickly and safely (948b–c). Socrates names him among the true judges in Hades, with Minos, Aeacus and Triptolemus (*Apology* 41a). In the myth of the *Gorgias* Zeus makes three of his sons judges of the dead, Minos and Rhadamanthys from Asia, Aeacus from Europe; Rhadamanthys judges the souls from Asia and sends the wicked to Tartarus (523e–524a, 526b; see [[plato]] and [[aeacus]]).

**Apollodorus.** He gave laws to the islanders, then fled to Boeotia and married Alcmene, the widow of Amphitryon, and since his death he judges in Hades with Minos (*Library* 3.1.2, 2.4.11; see [[alcmene]]).
