Peneleos heads the list of the Boeotian commanders, who bring fifty ships from [[Hyria]], [[Aulis]] and twenty-seven other towns (2.494–510). The Catalogue gives him no father and no epithet.

## Elsewhere in Homer

He is one of the busier Achaean fighters of the middle books. He kills Ilioneus, cuts off his head and holds it up on his spear "like a poppy head" to taunt the Trojans (14.487–505). He kills Lycon in a fight with swords (16.335–341). In the fight over Patroclus' body Polydamas wounds him in the shoulder (17.597–600).

## Outside Homer

Apollodorus counts him among the Argonauts and the suitors of Helen (1.9.16, 3.10.8). Pausanias tells that the Boeotians chose him as their general after their king Thersander was killed in Mysia, and that Eurypylus, son of Telephus, killed him at Troy (9.5.14–15).
