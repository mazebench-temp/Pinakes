## Family

“Eurycleia, daughter of Ops, son of Peisenor” (*Odyssey* 1.429): the verse that is her title gives her a father and a grandfather (see [[ops]] and [[peisenor-father-of-ops]]). Laertes bought her “when she was still in her first youth” for the worth of twenty oxen, and honored her in his halls as he honored his wife, but never lay with her, “for he shunned his wife's anger” (*Odyssey* 1.430–433; see [[laertes]]). She nursed Odysseus at her own breast (*Odyssey* 19.482–483) and reared his son (*Odyssey* 1.435).

## Descriptions

- κεδνὰ ἰδυῖα, “true-hearted” (*Odyssey* 1.428), literally “knowing devoted things”.
- γραίης πυκιμηδέος, “the shrewd old woman” (*Odyssey* 1.438): πυκιμηδής, “of close counsel” (πυκινός + μήδεα), is found only here in Homer; Penelope calls her an old woman “with shrewd counsel in her mind” (πυκινὰ φρεσὶ μήδεʼ ἔχουσα, *Odyssey* 19.353).
- περίφρων Εὐρύκλεια, “prudent Eurycleia” (*Odyssey* 19.357, 19.491, 20.134, 21.381), the epithet of Penelope herself (see [[periphron]]).
- φίλη τροφός, “dear nurse” (*Odyssey* 2.361, 4.742 and often), and μαῖα, the old nurse's name in address (*Odyssey* 2.349, 19.482).

## In the Odyssey

She keeps the storeroom of the house night and day, and she gives Telemachus wine and barley meal for his voyage and swears not to tell his mother until the eleventh or twelfth day (*Odyssey* 2.345–380; see [[the-storeroom-of-odysseus]] and [[provisions]]). When Penelope learns that he has gone, Eurycleia confesses that she knew (*Odyssey* 4.742–757). Penelope bids her wash the stranger's feet; she knows the scar, lets the foot fall into the basin, and Odysseus seizes her by the throat and makes her swear to silence (*Odyssey* 19.353–507; see [[odysseus-recognized-by-his-nurse]], [[the-bath]] and [[the-scar-of-odysseus]]). After the killing of the suitors she would raise the cry of triumph, and Odysseus checks her: “it is not holy to exult over slain men” (*Odyssey* 22.407–412); she tells him that of the fifty slave women in the house twelve have taken to shamelessness (*Odyssey* 22.420–425; see [[handmaids]]), and she goes up to wake Penelope with the news that Odysseus has come home (*Odyssey* 23.1–9).
