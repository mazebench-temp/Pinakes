## The name

The name is the adjective ἅλιος, “of the sea”, as in γέρων ἅλιος νημερτής, “the unerring Old Man of the Sea” (*Odyssey* 4.384, 4.401; see [[old-man-of-the-sea]]); the same letters make ἅλιος, “vain, fruitless”: “the shaft did not fly from his hand in vain” (οὐχ ἅλιον βέλος, *Iliad* 5.18). Only the capital tells the name from either. Almost all Phaeacian names, W. W. Merry observes, are taken from seafaring life; of the sons of Alcinous he excepts only Laodamas (*Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on *Odyssey* 8.111; see [[phaeacians]]).

## Family

He is one of the three sons of Alcinous who stand up for the games, “Laodamas and Halius and godlike Clytoneus” (*Odyssey* 8.118–119; see [[alcinous]], [[laodamas-son-of-alcinous]] and [[clytoneus]]), probably the three unmarried sons of the five (Merry on *Odyssey* 8.204), who, their sister says, “always want to have freshly washed clothes / when they go to the dance” (*Odyssey* 6.62–65; see [[nausicaa]] and [[dance]]).

Homer's other Halius is a [[halius|Lycian]], the fifth of the seven men of Sarpedon's people whom Odysseus kills when Athena turns his heart against the Lycians (*Iliad* 5.677–678; see [[lycians]]).
