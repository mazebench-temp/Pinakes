## Family

Tyro “said she was the wife of Cretheus, son of Aeolus” (*Odyssey* 11.237; see [[tyro]] and [[aeolus-father-of-sisyphus]]), and after the twins she bore to Poseidon, “her other sons she, the queen among women, bore to Cretheus, / Aeson and Pheres and Amythaon, who fought from his chariot” (*Odyssey* 11.258–259; see [[aeson]], [[pheres]] and [[amythaon]]). Pelias and Neleus, Poseidon's sons, were his stepsons (see [[pelias]] and [[neleus]]). Αἰολίδης, “son of Aeolus”, is the title of Sisyphus in the Iliad (*Iliad* 6.154; see [[sisyphus]]), and later genealogy makes Cretheus and Sisyphus brothers.

## Outside Homer

The Hesiodic *Catalogue of Women* names him first of the sons of Aeolus, “Cretheus and Athamas and Sisyphus of shifting counsel, / and unjust Salmoneus and high-hearted Perieres” (fr. 4 Evelyn-White; see [[hesiod]] and [[salmoneus]]), and a scholion on *Odyssey* 12.69, printed among the fragments of the *Catalogue*, tells that Tyro, the daughter of Salmoneus, already had two sons by Poseidon when she married Cretheus, and bore him Aeson, Pheres and Amythaon (fr. 13 Evelyn-White). W. W. Merry calls Cretheus the founder of Iolcus and a brother of Salmoneus, both sons of Aeolus by Laodice, so that Tyro married her uncle (*Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on *Odyssey* 11.235; see [[iolcus]]).
