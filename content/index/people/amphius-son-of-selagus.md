**A rich ally.** He lived in Paesus, πολυκτήμων πολυλήϊος, “rich in possessions, rich in grainland” (5.613), two adjectives found only here. “But his fate led him to be an ally to Priam and his sons” (5.613–614): μοῖρα, a man's portion, is the power that brings him to Troy to die (see [[moira]]). Paesus is the Apaesus of the Catalogue (2.828; see [[apaesus]]).

**Two men named Amphius.** The Catalogue's leaders from Apaesus were the sons of the seer Merops, Adrestus and “Amphius of the linen corselet”, who went to war against their father's warning, “for the fates of black death were leading them on” (2.828–834; see [[amphius]] and [[the-sons-of-merops]]). Book 5's Amphius comes from the same town, bears the same name, and is led to Troy by his fate. The poem does not connect the two.

**The body.** Ajax's spear strikes him at the belt and sticks in his lower belly, and he falls with a thud. Ajax runs up to strip his armor, but the Trojans shower spears on him. He sets his heel on the body and pulls out his spear, but he cannot take the armor, and the Trojans who stand over the body push him back (5.615–626; see [[ajax]]). Ajax takes back only his own spear.
