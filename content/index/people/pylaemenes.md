"The shaggy heart of Pylaemenes" (λάσιον κῆρ) stands for the man himself. A hairy chest marked strength and spirit ([[shaggy-chest]]).

{{notes}}

## Elsewhere in Homer

Book 13 brings him back alive. His son Harpalion, who had followed him to the war, is killed by Meriones, and as the Paphlagonians carry the body back to Troy, “his father went with them, letting tears fall” (13.643–659), though Menelaus killed him in Book 5 (5.576–579).

## Outside Homer

Livy makes the loss of King Pylaemenes at Troy the reason why the Eneti left Paphlagonia and followed Antenor to Italy (1.1.2). See [[eneti]].
