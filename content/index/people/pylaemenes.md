"The shaggy heart of Pylaemenes" (λάσιον κῆρ) stands for the man himself. A hairy chest marked strength and spirit ([[shaggy-chest]]).

In Book 5 Menelaus spears "Pylaemenes, the equal of Ares, leader of the great-hearted Paphlagonian shield-bearers" at the collarbone as he stands in his chariot (5.576–579). In Book 13 his son Harpalion is killed by Meriones, and as the Paphlagonians carry the body back to Troy, "his father went with them, shedding tears" (13.643–659). The contradiction was noticed in antiquity. It may be a slip of the poet or a second man of the same name; the poem does not say.

## Outside Homer

Livy makes the loss of King Pylaemenes at Troy the reason why the Eneti left Paphlagonia and followed Antenor to Italy (1.1.2). See [[eneti]].
