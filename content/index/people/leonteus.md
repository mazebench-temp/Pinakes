The name is formed from λέων ("lion"). ὄζος Ἄρηος, "offshoot of Ares" (see [[offshoot-of-ares]]). His grandfather Caeneus was one of the Lapiths whom Nestor named among the heroes of his youth (1.264), as was Peirithous, the father of his fellow leader.

Leonteus fights beside Polypoetes at the gate of the Achaean wall in Book 12 (12.127–194), and he is one of the four who throw the lump of iron at the funeral games of Patroclus (23.836–840). After the war he went with Calchas and Polypoetes to Colophon (Proclus' summary of the *Nostoi*).
