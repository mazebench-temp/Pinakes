ἱππότα "horseman", the title of Nestor's formula ([[gerenian-horseman]]), and Διῒ φίλος "dear to Zeus" ([[dear-to-zeus]]). The Catalogue gives him a line of story: he "once went off to live in Dulichium, angry with his father" (πατρὶ χολωθείς, 2.629). Homer does not name the father; later tradition made him [[Augeas]], king of the Epeians (see [[phyleus-and-augeas]]).

## Elsewhere in Homer

Nestor recalls that he outthrew Phyleus with the spear at the funeral games of Amarynceus (23.637). Phyleus brought home a corselet from Ephyra on the river Selleis, the gift of his guest-friend Euphetes, and it later saves his son's life (15.529–534). Meges is often simply "the son of Phyleus" (10.110, 15.519, 15.528).
