## The name

ἀμφίαλος, from ἀμφί, “around”, and ἅλς, “sea”, is the epithet of Ithaca, “sea-girt Ithaca” (*Odyssey* 1.386, 1.395, 1.401; see [[sea-girt]]). W. W. Merry counts the Phaeacian names among those “taken from circumstances of seafaring life” (*Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on *Odyssey* 8.111).

## Family

Of the twelve young men named first in the list of those who rise for the games, he alone is given a father, and a grandfather with him: Ἀμφίαλός θʼ, υἱὸς Πολυνήου Τεκτονίδαο, “and Amphialus, son of Polyneus, the son of Tecton” (*Odyssey* 8.114; see [[polyneus]] and [[tecton]]). Read as words, the three names go from the builder (τέκτων) to many ships (πολύς, νηῦς) to the sea around (ἀμφίαλος).
