πέμπτης δʼ Ἀλκιμέδων Λαέρκεος υἱὸς ἀμύμων, “and the fifth by Alcimedon, the noble son of Laerces” (16.197): Alcimedon is the last of the five leaders of the Myrmidons, named after the old horseman Phoenix (16.196; see [[myrmidons]], [[phoenix]] and [[laerces]]). Unlike Menesthius, Eudorus and Peisander, he returns in the next book, as the charioteer of Achilles' horses.

Ἀλκιμέδων joins ἀλκή, “courage, defense” (see [[alke]]), and μέδων, “ruler”, as Automedon's name joins αὐτός and μέδων. In Books 19 and 24 Automedon's fellow attendant is Ἄλκιμος, Alcimus (19.392; 24.474, 24.574), and the commentators make the two one man. Walter Leaf calls Ἄλκιμος “the familiar short form of Ἀλκιμέδων” (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 19.392), and gives the pair as the type of such a shortening, as Πάτροκλε (16.11) stands beside Πατρόκλεες (16.7) in Achilles' words to Patroclus (on 16.11); ancient readers cited the same names (see below). The poem itself never says so. It gives Alcimedon a father and a command, and names Alcimus only beside Automedon; but the two hold the same place, at the horses of Achilles (17.479–483, 19.392–394; see [[the-horses-of-achilles]]).

## Outside Homer

Strabo reports that those who take Homer's Messe (2.582) for Messene, shortened, cite other shortenings from the poet, κρῖ, δῶ and μάψ, and “the hero Automedon and Alcimus” (24.474), Ἄλκιμος “instead of Ἀλκιμέδων” (*Geography* 8.5.3; see [[strabo]]).
