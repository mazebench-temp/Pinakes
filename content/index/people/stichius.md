He is named three times. When Hector has killed the Epeian Amphimachus and Ajax has driven him back from the body, “Amphimachus, then, Stichius and brilliant Menestheus, / the leaders of the Athenians, carried back to the army of the Achaeans” (13.195–196; see [[amphimachus]] and [[menestheus]]). Where Hector breaks in and the wall is lowest, he is one of the three who follow Menestheus at the head of “the picked men of the Athenians” (13.689–691):

{{quote:5dda8018-71dc-5b5d-9c20-0d89b8bcf3ec}}

In Book 15 Hector kills him with Arcesilaus, a leader of the Boeotians; he is “the trusted companion of great-hearted Menestheus” (15.329–331; see [[hector]] and [[arcesilaus]]). Homer gives him no father and no home. The name may come from στίχες, “ranks”, the lines of an army (πυκινὰς στίχας, “the close ranks”, 13.680).
