**Two brothers in one chariot.** Laogonus and his brother Dardanus are the third and fourth of the men Achilles kills after Apollo has saved Hector (20.455–462; see [[dardanus-son-of-bias]], [[bias-father-of-laogonus]] and [[achilles]]). Achilles rushes on them both and thrusts them from their chariot, “hitting the one with his spear, and striking the other with his sword at close quarters” (20.462; see [[chariot]], [[spear]] and [[sword]]). The verse does not say which brother died by which weapon. Its words are those of Apollo's warning to Hector a little before, to keep out of Achilles' way “lest he hit you with a spear, or strike you with his sword at close quarters” (20.378; see [[hector]] and [[apollo]]): what Hector was spared, the sons of Bias suffer. Brothers in one chariot often die together in the Iliad. Diomedes took two sons of Priam, Echemmon and Chromius, “who were in one chariot”, and brought them both down from it (5.159–165; see [[chromius-son-of-priam]]), and, fighting beside Odysseus, he took the chariot of the two sons of Merops of Percote, who had gone to war against their father's will, and killed them both (11.328–334; see [[merops]]).

**The name.** He shares the name of Laogonus, son of Onetor, the priest of Zeus of Ida, whom Meriones killed in the fight for Sarpedon's body (16.603–607; see [[laogonus]]). Λαόγονος joins λαός, “people”, and γόνος, “offspring”.
