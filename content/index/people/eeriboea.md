μητρυιὴ περικαλλὴς Ἠερίβοια, “their stepmother, the beautiful Eeriboea” (5.389). A μητρυιή is a father's wife who is not one's mother: she was the wife of [[Aloeus]], and the Odyssey names another woman, Iphimedeia, as the brothers' mother (Od. 11.305). Without her Ares “would have perished there” (5.388). She brought word to Hermes, and he stole the god away when the cruel bonds were already overpowering him (5.389–391; see [[the-aloadae-bind-ares]] and [[hermes]]). Homer does not say why she betrayed her stepsons, and he tells nothing more of her.

Names ending in -βοια speak of wealth in cattle (βοῦς, “ox”), like Περίβοια (21.142, Od. 7.57); the first part of hers is obscure.
