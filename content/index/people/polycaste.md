## Family

She is Νέστορος ὁπλοτάτη θυγάτηρ Νηληϊάδαο, “the youngest daughter of Nestor, son of Neleus” (*Odyssey* 3.465; see [[nestor]] and [[son-of-neleus]]). Homer names no other daughter of Nestor, though his daughters raise the cry with the women of the house at the sacrifice (*Odyssey* 3.450–451).

## Descriptions

καλή, “lovely” (*Odyssey* 3.464).

## Outside Homer

**The mother of Persepolis.** The Hesiodic *Catalogue of Women* made her the mother of a son by Telemachus: “And to Telemachus well-girdled Polycaste, / the youngest daughter of Nestor, son of Neleus, / bore Persepolis, joined with him through golden Aphrodite” (fr. 12 Evelyn-White, from Eustathius, p. 1796.39; a plain rendering; see [[hesiod]]). The second verse is the Odyssey's verse, with κούρη for θυγάτηρ (*Odyssey* 3.465). Apollodorus names her with Peisidice as the two daughters of Nestor by Anaxibia, daughter of Cratieus (*Library* 1.9.9). The Suda's life of Homer records that some made the poet himself the son of Telemachus and Polycaste (Suda ο 251; see [[homer]]). Strabo's Polycaste, daughter of Lygaeus and mother of Penelope, is another woman (*Geography* 10.2.24).
