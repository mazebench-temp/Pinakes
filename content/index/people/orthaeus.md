Ὀρθαῖόν τε, “and Orthaeus” (13.791), is the second of six names in the list of men around Cebriones and Polydamas, where Hector and Paris go to the thickest of the fighting (see [[hector]], [[cebriones]] and [[polydamas]]). Whether he is one of the men who had come from Ascania “as relief” the morning before is not clear (13.793–794; see [[ascanius-the-mysian]]). The poem gives him nothing to do, and he is not named again. The name looks like a formation from ὀρθός, “upright”.
