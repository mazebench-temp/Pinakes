## Family

He is the last of Tyro's three sons by Cretheus: “Aeson and Pheres and Amythaon, who fought from his chariot” (*Odyssey* 11.259; see [[tyro]], [[cretheus]], [[aeson]] and [[pheres]]), half-brother of Pelias and Neleus (see [[pelias]] and [[neleus]]). Homer gives him no wife or children. The seer of the same catalogue, who drove off the cattle of Iphiclus for Neleus' daughter, is named in *Odyssey* 15 as Melampus, and later writers call Melampus the son of Amythaon (see [[melampus]] and [[pero]]).

## Descriptions

ἱππιοχάρμης, “who fought from his chariot” (*Odyssey* 11.259; see [[hippiocharmes]]): χάρμη is the joy of battle, and W. W. Merry renders the compound “fighting from the war chariot” (*Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on *Odyssey* 11.259). The Hesiodic *Catalogue* gives the same epithet to Aeolus, the father of Cretheus (fr. 4 Evelyn-White; see [[aeolus-father-of-sisyphus]]).

## Outside Homer

Herodotus calls Melampus “the son of Amytheon”, the seer who taught the Greeks the name of Dionysus and his rites (Herodotus 2.49.1; see [[herodotus]]), and Merry makes Amythaon the father of Bias and Melampus by Idomene (on *Odyssey* 11.259). A Hesiodic verse quoted in the Suda divides the gifts of the Olympian among three houses: “strength to the sons of Aeacus, / wit to the sons of Amythaon, and wealth to the sons of Atreus” (fr. 8 Evelyn-White, among the unplaced fragments; see [[hesiod]]).
