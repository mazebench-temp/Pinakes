## The name

Merry takes Τιτυός for a reduplicated form from a root τυ-, “swell”, “grow big”, seen in Latin *tumor* and *tuber* (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on *Odyssey* 11.576). Γαιήιον υἱόν, “the son of Earth” (*Odyssey* 7.324), names his mother in an adjective, as Ποιάντιον υἱόν names Philoctetes the son of Poias (*Odyssey* 3.190; Merry on *Odyssey* 7.324; see [[earth]]); in the underworld he is Γαίης ἐρικυδέος υἱόν, “the son of glorious Earth” (*Odyssey* 11.576).

## In Homer

Homer names him twice, both times in the Odyssey. Alcinous measures the speed of his ships by a voyage his people once made, when they carried fair-haired Rhadamanthys to Euboea “to visit Tityus, the son of Earth”, and came back home the same day (*Odyssey* 7.321–326; see [[the-voyage-of-rhadamanthus]], [[rhadamanthus]], [[euboea]] and [[phaeacians]]). Among the dead Odysseus sees him punished for the violence he did to Leto, the glorious wife of Zeus, as she went to Pytho through Panopeus (*Odyssey* 11.576–581; see [[leto]], [[pytho]] and [[panopeus]]). The poem does not say how the giant whom the Phaeacians visited in Euboea came to be in Hades.

## Outside Homer

**His mother and his death.** A half-verse of the Hesiodic *Catalogue of Women*, preserved by Herodian, calls him Εὐλαρίδην Τιτυόν, “Tityus, son of Elara” (fr. 25 Evelyn-White; see [[hesiod]]). Merry reports that in Pindar Artemis kills him (*Pythian* 4.90), that Pausanias joins Apollo with her (Pausanias 3.18.15; see [[pausanias]]), and that Hyginus has Zeus strike him down with the thunderbolt (*Fabulae* 55; Merry on *Odyssey* 11.581). The Suda tells the story briefly: in love with Leto, he caught hold of her veil, and her children Apollo and Artemis shot him down; he is punished in Hades with arrows fixed in his liver (Suda τ 696; see [[apollo]] and [[artemis]]).

**Panopeus and Euboea.** Strabo says that the stories of Tityus are told at Panopeus, and quotes Homer's verse on the Phaeacians who brought Rhadamanthys to Euboea “to see Tityus, son of Earth”; in Euboea, he adds, a cave called Elarium, named after Elara, Tityus' mother, is shown, with a hero-shrine of Tityus, and certain honors are paid to him there (*Geography* 9.3.14; see [[strabo]]). Ephorus, as Strabo reports and mocks him, turned the myth into history: Apollo, going from Athens to Delphi by the road on which the Athenians still send their procession to Pytho, came to Panopeus and destroyed Tityus, who held the place, “a violent and lawless man” (*Geography* 9.3.12).

**Plato.** Socrates in the *Gorgias* names Tityus with Tantalus and Sisyphus as the kings and men of power whom Homer shows punished for all time in Hades, while no poet has shown a wicked commoner like Thersites held in such punishments (*Gorgias* 525d–e; see [[plato]], [[sisyphus]], [[tantalus]] and [[thersites]]).

**Virgil and Lucretius.** Virgil's Tityos is stretched over nine *iugera*, with a single vulture at his liver (*Aeneid* 6.595–600; see [[virgil-the-punished-in-tartarus]]); Lucretius makes him the lover torn by anguish and desire (*On the Nature of Things* 3.984–994; see [[lucretius-the-punishments-of-acheron]]).
