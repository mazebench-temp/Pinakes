**After the rally.** Apollo, in the likeness of the herald Periphas, has shamed Aeneas for trembling while Zeus wills victory for the Trojans, and Aeneas has called on Hector and the leaders to go straight at the Danaans (17.319–341; see [[apollo]] and [[aeneas]]). He leaps out far in front of the champions, the Trojans rally and stand facing the Achaeans, and his spear finds Leiocritus (17.342–345). He is the first man to die when the Trojans turn, and one of the three Achaeans whom the book names among the dead, with Schedius, killed by Hector's spear (17.306–311; see [[schedius]]), and Coeranus, Meriones' charioteer (17.610–619). The narrator says that the Danaans lost far fewer men than the Trojans, “for they always remembered / to ward off sheer slaughter from one another in the throng” (17.364–365).

**Pity and revenge.** “And Lycomedes, dear to Ares, pitied him as he fell” (17.346; see [[lycomedes]], [[dear-to-ares]] and [[pity]]). Lycomedes comes up close and kills the Paeonian Apisaon, son of Hippasus, and Asteropaeus pities Apisaon in the same words (17.346–352; see [[apisaon-son-of-hippasus]] and [[asteropaeus]]). Aeneas had killed the twin sons of Diocles in Book 5, and then too a man “dear to Ares”, Menelaus, pitied them as they fell (5.559–561; see [[crethon]] and [[menelaus]]).

**The name.** Walter Leaf calls the name “Odyssean”, and reports Nauck's view that it should be Ληόκριτος, from ληός, an Ionic form of λαός, “people” (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 17.344). The second half is κριτός, “chosen”, from κρίνω, “pick out”, as in κριτὸς … λαὸς Ἀχαιῶν, “a chosen band of the Achaeans” (7.434).

In the Odyssey Leiocritus, son of Euenor, is one of the suitors: he answers Mentor in the assembly of Ithaca, and Telemachus stabs him with his spear in the hall (*Odyssey* 2.242–256, 22.294–296).
