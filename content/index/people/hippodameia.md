κλυτὸς Ἱπποδάμεια, "famous Hippodameia": the adjective keeps its masculine ending with a woman's name, as Homer sometimes lets adjectives in -ος serve for both genders. The name means "tamer of horses" (ἵππος + δάμνημι), like the epithet ἱππόδαμος (see [[tamer-of-horses]]).

Homer says only that she bore Polypoetes on the day Peirithous took vengeance on the Centaurs. Later writers made that day her wedding: the Centaurs, invited to the feast, got drunk and tried to carry off the bride and the other women, and the Lapiths drove them out (Ovid, *Metamorphoses* 12.210–535). She is not the Hippodameia whom Pelops won in a chariot race.
