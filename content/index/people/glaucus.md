## Etymology

γλαυκός means "gleaming, gray-blue", the color of the sea; Athena is γλαυκῶπις ([[gray-eyed]]). Several men of myth bear the name. This Glaucus shares it with his great-grandfather, Glaucus of Corinth, son of Sisyphus and father of Bellerophon (6.153–155).

## Descriptions

{{quote:5ea519a8-cd28-560d-8c06-98a8618dbf0c}}

Γλαῦκος ἀμύμων, "noble Glaucus": ἀμύμων is a general term of praise ([[blameless]]). He comes second to [[Sarpedon]], the king, here as throughout the poem.

{{notes}}

## Elsewhere in Homer

With his cousin [[Sarpedon]] he leads the Lycians in the Trojan catalogue (2.876–877). At the Achaean wall Sarpedon tells him why the two of them are honored in Lycia and must fight in the front rank (12.310–328), and Teucer's arrow wounds him as he climbs the wall (12.387–391). Dying, Sarpedon calls on him to defend his body; Glaucus, still wounded, prays to Apollo, who heals him, and he rallies the Lycians and kills Bathycles (16.492–531, 16.593–599). When the Trojans leave Sarpedon's body to the Achaeans, he rebukes Hector bitterly (17.140–168).

## Outside Homer

The exchange became proverbial. In Plato's *Symposium*, Socrates tells Alcibiades that he is trying to get the better of the bargain, "gold for bronze" (χρύσεα χαλκείων, 218e).
