## The name

The name is the adjective ἀγχίαλος, “near the sea” (ἄγχι “near” + ἅλς “sea”), which the Iliad gives to coastal towns, Chalcis and Antron (*Iliad* 2.640, 2.697; see [[by-the-sea]]). Homer has two other men of the name: an Achaean whom Hector kills in one chariot with Menesthes (*Iliad* 5.609; see [[anchialus]]), and a young Phaeacian who rises for the games (*Odyssey* 8.112).

## Family

Father of Mentes, lord of the Taphians (*Odyssey* 1.180–181, 1.418–419; see [[mentes-the-taphian]] and [[taphians]]). He was a friend of Odysseus: Mentes first saw Odysseus in his father's house, and the father, “for he loved him terribly”, gave him the drug that Ilus son of Mermerus would not give (*Odyssey* 1.257–264; see [[odysseus-seeks-poison]] and [[ilus-son-of-mermerus]]).
