The name joins ἵππος, “horse”, and νόος, “mind”. Hipponous closes the list and is the only one of the nine who is given an epithet: Ἱππόνοον μενεχάρμην, “Hipponous, steadfast in battle” (11.303; see [[steadfast-in-battle]]). Earlier in the book the same word ends the verse of another man with a horse name, Hippolochus, son of Antimachus, whom Agamemnon kills (Ἱππόλοχον μενεχάρμην, 11.122; see [[hippolochus-son-of-antimachus]]). After the nine leaders Hector kills the multitude, beating down their heads as the West Wind batters the clouds (11.304–309; see [[hector]] and [[whom-first-whom-last]]).
