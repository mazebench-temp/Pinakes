Λυκόφρονα Μάστορος υἱὸν, “Lycophron, son of Mastor” (15.430), and Μαστορίδης, “the son of Mastor”, as Ajax calls him when he tells Teucer that their companion has been killed (15.438; see [[lycophron]], [[ajax]] and [[teucer]]). Mastor is known only as a father. His son came from Cythera and fled to Ajax after killing a man there (15.431–432; see [[cythera]] and [[exile-and-refuge]]); whether Mastor lived on Cythera the poem does not say.

In the Odyssey the patronymic Μαστορίδης is given to Halitherses, the old Ithacan who alone of his age knew how to read birds, and who “alone saw before and after” (*Odyssey* 2.157–159, 24.451–452). His father is another Mastor.
