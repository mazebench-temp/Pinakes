Tychius is named only here. σκυτοτόμος = σκῦτος (“hide, leather”) + τέμνω (“cut”): a leather-cutter, a word found only here in Homer and later the common word for a shoemaker. σκυτοτόμων ὄχʼ ἄριστος gives a craftsman the praise of Calchas and Helenus, “by far the best of the bird-seers” (1.69, 6.76). The name may speak of the craft: τεύχων, “making”, stands beside it, as the shipbuilder Phereclus is “son of the builder” (5.59; see [[phereclus]]). See [[the-shield-of-ajax]].

## Outside Homer

Some ancient texts read Ὕδῃ, “in Hyde”, and made him a Lydian; Strabo rejects the reading (9.2.20, 13.4.6; see [[hyle-or-hyde]]). Pliny the Elder's list of inventors credits “Tychius the Boeotian” with shoemaking (*Natural History*, Book 7). The *Life of Homer* ascribed to Herodotus makes him a leather-worker of Neon Teichos, near Cyme, who took in the wandering poet, and says that Homer repaid him with these verses.
