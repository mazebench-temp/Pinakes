The name joins ἵππος (“horse”) and λόχος (“ambush, band of armed men”). The Iliad knows him only as a father: Glaucus is “the son of Hippolochus” (Ἱππολόχοιο πάϊς, 6.119; also 6.144, 7.13, 12.309, 12.387, 17.140). He is one of the three children of Bellerophon and the king of Lycia's daughter (6.197), and the only one of them of whom nothing bad is told. His brother [[Isander]] and his sister [[Laodameia]] were killed by gods, and his sister's son is [[Sarpedon]].

His charge to his son, αἰὲν ἀριστεύειν καὶ ὑπείροχον ἔμμεναι ἄλλων, is the one Peleus gave Achilles, in the same words (11.784; see [[always-to-be-the-best]]), and he adds the claim of the family: “not to shame the race of my fathers” (6.209; see [[fathers-and-sons]]). See [[glaucus]] and [[bellerophon]].
