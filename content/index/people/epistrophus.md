Epistrophus is named only in the Catalogue. The adjective ἐπίστροφος means "turning toward, going about among": Telemachus says that his father "had many dealings with men" (ἐπίστροφος ἀνθρώπων, Od. 1.177).

Two other men in Book 2 bear the name: Epistrophus, son of Euenus, whom Achilles killed when he sacked Lyrnessus (2.692; [[epistrophus-of-lyrnessus]]), and Epistrophus, who leads the Halizones with Odius (2.856; [[epistrophus-of-the-halizones]]).

## Outside Homer

Apollodorus lists him with his brother among the suitors of Helen (3.10.8).
