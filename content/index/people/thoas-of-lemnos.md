## In Homer

{{quote:3dbdd621-4576-5d4a-8275-0b3cf5769186}}

Lemnos is πόλιν θείοιο Θόαντος, “the city of divine Thoas” (14.230): the island is named by its king, as Troy is “the city of lord Priam” (2.373; see [[lemnos]] and [[divine]]). There Hera finds Sleep (14.231; see [[sleep]]). He appears once more, in the past: the silver mixing bowl that Achilles sets out as a prize at the funeral games was the work of Sidonian craftsmen, and Phoenician traders “set it out in the harbors, and gave it as a gift to Thoas” (23.743–745); Euneus, son of Jason, later gave it to Patroclus as the price of Lycaon (23.746–747; see [[euneus]], [[mixing-bowl]] and [[sidonians]]). Homer does not say how the bowl passed from Thoas to Euneus. Euneus' mother is Hypsipyle (7.469), whom later writers make Thoas' daughter (see [[hypsipyle]]).

## Outside Homer

Later writers tell of Thoas in the story of the Lemnian women (see [[the-lemnian-women]]). When the women killed the men of the island, Hypsipyle alone spared her father: in Apollonius she sets him adrift on the sea in a hollow chest, and fishermen bring him to the island of Oenoe, later called Sicinus after his son by the nymph Oenoe (*Argonautica* 1.620–626; see [[apollonius]]), and in Apollodorus she saves him by hiding him (*Library* 1.9.17; see [[apollodorus]]). Herodotus follows another version, in which the women killed their husbands “together with Thoas” (Herodotus 6.138.4; see [[herodotus]]).
