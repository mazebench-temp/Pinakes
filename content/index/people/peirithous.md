## Family

Peirithous is a son of Zeus. The Catalogue calls him the son “whom immortal Zeus begot” (2.741), and Zeus himself names his mother among his loves, “the wife of Ixion” (14.317–318; see [[ixion]] and [[the-loves-of-zeus]]). His wife Hippodameia bore him Polypoetes, who leads the Lapiths to Troy, “on the day when he took vengeance on the shaggy beasts” (2.740–744; see [[hippodameia]], [[polypoetes]] and [[lapiths-drive-out-the-centaurs]]).

## Elsewhere in Homer

The Odyssey places the quarrel of the Centaurs and men in his house: the Centaur Eurytion, maddened by wine, did evil “in the hall of great-hearted Peirithous”, and the heroes dragged him out and cut off his ears and nose (*Odyssey* 21.295–304; see [[centaurs]]). In the underworld Odysseus would have stayed to see Theseus and Peirithous, “glorious children of the gods”, had the dead not crowded around him (*Odyssey* 11.630–635; see [[theseus]]).

## Outside Homer

Theseus and Peirithous agreed to marry daughters of Zeus. Theseus, with Peirithous' help, carried off Helen, and to win Persephone for Peirithous the two went down to Hades, where Hades seated them on the Chair of Forgetfulness; Heracles brought Theseus back, but Peirithous remained bound there forever (Apollodorus, *Epitome* 1.23–24; see [[helen-and-theseus]] and [[persephone]]).
