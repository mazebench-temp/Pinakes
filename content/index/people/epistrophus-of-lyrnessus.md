He is named only here, and his death, like his brother's, is told in a single line to show what the sack of Lyrnessus cost. The name comes from ἐπιστρέφω, "turn around". Two other men in Book 2 bear the same name: [[epistrophus|Epistrophus]] the Phocian leader (2.517), and [[epistrophus-of-the-halizones|Epistrophus]], leader of the Halizones, an ally of Troy (2.856).
