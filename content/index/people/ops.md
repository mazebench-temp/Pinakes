Εὐρύκλειʼ, Ὦπος θυγάτηρ Πεισηνορίδαο, “Eurycleia, daughter of Ops, son of Peisenor” (*Odyssey* 1.429). Ops is named only to name his daughter, in a verse that gives the old servant a father and a grandfather, a lineage of the kind the poem gives its heroes (see [[eurycleia]] and [[peisenor-father-of-ops]]). The poem says nothing else of him. The verse is her title: it returns when Telemachus finds her keeping the storeroom, night and day, and asks her for wine and barley meal for his voyage (*Odyssey* 2.345–355), and when she sets the slave women to make the hall ready on the morning of the suitors' last feast (*Odyssey* 20.147–156).
