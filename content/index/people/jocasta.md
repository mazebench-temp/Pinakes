---
title: Jocasta
greek: Ἰοκάστη
kind: person
tags: [theban, labdacids, woman]
summary: Queen of Thebes, wife of Laius and then, unknowing, of her son Oedipus, to whom later poets give four children, Eteocles, Polynices, Antigone and Ismene; Homer calls her Epicaste.
aliases: [Iocasta, Epicaste, Iokaste]
---

The Odyssey names her Epicaste. Odysseus saw her among the dead, “who did a monstrous thing in the ignorance of her mind, marrying her own son”; she hanged herself from a high beam and left Oedipus the many sorrows that a mother's Furies bring (*Odyssey* 11.271–280). In Sophocles' *Oedipus the King* she tries to calm Oedipus by showing that oracles fail, understands the truth before he does, and hangs herself (see [[the-discovery-of-oedipus]] and [[oedipus-the-king]]). In Euripides' *Phoenician Women* she lives on to try to reconcile her sons, and kills herself over their bodies. Aristotle does not name her, but her part belongs to what he calls the finest recognition.
