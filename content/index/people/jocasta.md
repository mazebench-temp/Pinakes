## The name

The Odyssey calls her Ἐπικάστη, “fair Epicaste” (*Odyssey* 11.271); Ἰοκάστη is the tragedians' name. W. W. Merry derives Epicaste from the root of κέκασμαι, “excel”, as meaning “brightness” (*Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on *Odyssey* 11.271).

## Family

In the Odyssey she is the mother and then, unknowing, the wife of Oedipus, who married her when he had slain his father; when the gods made it known, she hanged herself (*Odyssey* 11.271–279; see [[oedipus]]). Homer names neither her father nor her first husband, and gives her no children by her son. The tragedians make her the wife of Laius, the daughter of Menoeceus and the sister of Creon (see [[laius]] and [[creon-of-thebes]]).

## Outside Homer

In Sophocles' *Oedipus the King* she tries to calm Oedipus by showing that oracles fail, understands the truth before he does, and hangs herself (see [[the-discovery-of-oedipus]] and [[oedipus-the-king]]). In Euripides' *Phoenician Women* she is “the daughter of Menoeceus” and the sister of Creon (*Phoenician Women* 10–12; see [[creon-of-thebes]]), lives on to try to reconcile her sons, and kills herself over their bodies. Aristotle does not name her, but her part belongs to what he calls the finest recognition.
