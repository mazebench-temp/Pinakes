Panthous is named first among the elders who sit "around Priam" at the Scaean gates (3.146; see [[trojan-elders]]).

## Family

The Iliad knows him through his sons. Polydamas, Πανθοΐδης, "son of Panthous", is the shrewdest of the Trojan counselors, who "alone saw both before and after" (18.249–250), the gift that Menelaus gives to old men (3.109; see [[before-and-after]]). Euphorbus is the first to wound Patroclus (16.806–815). Menelaus kills him, after reminding him that he had killed his brother Hyperenor too (17.9–60, 14.516–519). Euphorbus had hoped to put Menelaus' head and armor "in the hands of Panthous and noble Phrontis", his father and mother (17.39–40). The old man who watches Menelaus fight Paris in Book 3 will lose two sons to him.

## Outside Homer

Virgil makes him "Panthus, son of Othrys, priest of the citadel and of Phoebus", who escapes the Greeks on the night of the sack carrying the sacred objects and his small grandson, and falls in the fighting: "neither your great piety nor the fillet of Apollo protected you as you fell, Panthus" (*Aeneid* 2.318–321, 2.429–430). His son had a stranger afterlife. Pythagoras claimed to have been Euphorbus in an earlier life (Ovid, *Metamorphoses* 15.160–164).
