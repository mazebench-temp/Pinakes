## Etymology

Χρυσηΐς (Chrysēïs): the -ίς ending marks a daughter or a woman "of" a place or father, so the name means "daughter of Chryses" or "the girl from Chryse". Homer never gives her another name.

## In Book 1

She does not speak. Agamemnon describes her as the prize he would take home to Argos, to work at the loom and share his bed (1.29–31). Later he says he prefers her to his wedded wife:

{{quote:d0132f3e-b126-51dc-83e9-5a78abb5a30a}}

Calchas calls her "the bright-eyed girl" (ἑλικῶπις κούρη, 1.98). She was captured when Achilles sacked Thebe and chosen for Agamemnon when the spoils were divided (1.366–369).

## Outside Homer

In medieval retellings of the Trojan War, her name merged with Briseis': Boccaccio's Criseida, Chaucer's Criseyde and Shakespeare's Cressida all descend from the pair. See [[from-briseis-to-cressida]].
