He is the last name in the list of the Achaeans who die when Apollo has shaken the aegis in their faces and they flee (15.318–342; see [[apollo]] and [[aegis]]), and the only one whose wound is told. The others die in a verb, ἔπεφνε, ἐξενάριξεν, ἕλε, “killed”, “slew and stripped” (15.329, 15.332, 15.339–340; see [[androktasia]]). Deiochus is struck “at the base of the shoulder from behind”, and the bronze goes straight through, as when Idomeneus drove his spear through the throat of Asius (διὰ πρὸ δὲ χαλκὸν ἔλασσεν, 15.342 = 13.388).

He is caught φεύγοντʼ ἐν προμάχοισι, “as he fled among the champions”: a man who had fought in front, struck as he turned. So Agamemnon killed Odius, leader of the Halizones, in the first rout of the poem, “for as the man was the first to turn, he planted his spear in his back” (5.40; see [[odius]]). Agamemnon's exhortation in that battle gave the reason, and Ajax repeats it later in this book: “when men feel shame, more are saved than are killed; / but when men flee, there rises neither fame nor any courage” (5.531–532 = 15.563–564; see [[shame-saves-more]]).

Paris has no other part in the book (see [[paris]]). Δηΐοχος is named nowhere else in Homer, and the poem gives him no father and no home.
