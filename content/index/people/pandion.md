The poem gives him no father, no home and no title. Carrying another man's bow, he does an attendant's work (see [[teucer]], [[curved-bow]] and [[bow]]). Teucer has the bow in his hands again as soon as they reach the tower, and shoots Glaucus in the arm (12.387–389).

## Outside Homer

In Athenian legend Pandion is the name of a king of Athens, the father of Aegeus (Euripides, *Medea* 665, 1385) and of Lycus, from whom, Herodotus says, the Lycians took their name (1.173.3, 7.92). Homer does not connect the bow-bearer with Athens.
