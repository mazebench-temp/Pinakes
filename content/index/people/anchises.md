## Family

{{quote:9ad11811-e0ed-5d96-b078-d49251675067}}

In the Iliad Anchises is known through his son, his daughter and his horses. He is a great-grandson of [[tros|Tros]], as Priam is, from another branch of the royal house (20.230–240). Aphrodite bore Aeneas to him "as he tended his cattle" (5.313), and Aeneas names his parents with pride when he faces Achilles: "I claim to be the son of great-hearted Anchises, and my mother is Aphrodite" (20.208–209). His daughter Hippodameia married Alcathous (13.428–433).

## Elsewhere in Homer

His horses came from the divine breed that Zeus gave Tros in payment for [[ganymede|Ganymede]]. "Lord of men Anchises" stole the strain by putting his mares to Laomedon's stallions without Laomedon's knowledge, and gave two of the foals to Aeneas (5.265–272). Diomedes knows their pedigree and has them driven off when Aeneas falls (5.319–327).

A different Anchises is the father of Echepolus of Sicyon, who gave Agamemnon a mare so that he need not go to Troy (23.296–299).

## Outside Homer

The *Hymn to Aphrodite* tells how the goddess came to him on Ida and warned him never to boast of her (see [[aphrodite-and-anchises]]). Later writers say he did, and was struck by Zeus' thunderbolt; in Virgil he says that Zeus "blasted me with the wind of his thunderbolt and touched me with fire" (*Aeneid* 2.647–649). Aeneas carries him out of burning Troy, and he dies in Sicily before the Trojans reach Italy (*Aeneid* 3.707–715).
