Θέστορα Ἤνοπος υἱόν, “Thestor, son of Enops” (16.401), is all that the poem tells of him. His son is the man whom Patroclus drags from his chariot on the spear like a fish on a line (16.401–410; see [[thestor-son-of-enops]] and [[the-fisherman]]).

Homer has two other men of the name. Enops the herdsman, to whom a Naiad nymph bore Satnius by the river Satnioeis, is named when the son of Oileus kills Satnius in Book 14 (14.442–445; see [[enops]] and [[satnius]]); whether he is also Thestor's father, so that Thestor and Satnius were brothers, the poem does not say. The third is the father of Clytomedes, whom Nestor beat at boxing in the funeral games of Amarynceus (23.634).

The name has the form of the adjective ἦνοψ, “gleaming”, said of bronze, and the adjective is used in the simile of his son's death: the angler draws up his fish “with line and gleaming bronze” (λίνῳ καὶ ἤνοπι χαλκῷ, 16.408).
