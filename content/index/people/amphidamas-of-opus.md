Homer names him once, in the speech of the ghost of Patroclus. The soul asks that their bones lie together, as the two were reared together in Peleus' house, and recalls how Patroclus came there: Menoetius brought him from Opus, “still a little boy”, because he had killed the son of Amphidamas (23.83–90; see [[the-ghost-of-patroclus]], [[patroclus]] and [[menoetius]]). The poem names neither the son nor his country, but since the killing took Patroclus away from Opus, the boy was presumably killed there (see [[opus]]).

**The killing.** ἀνδροκτασίης ὕπο λυγρῆς, “because of a grievous killing” (23.86), uses the word that elsewhere names the slaughter of the battlefield (see [[androktasia]]). Patroclus was νήπιος, “a foolish child”, and he killed οὐκ ἐθέλων, “not meaning to”, χολωθείς, “in anger”, over ἀστράγαλοι, “knucklebones” (23.88; see [[nepios]] and [[cholos]]). The game is named only here in Homer (Walter Leaf, *Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 23.88); elsewhere ἀστράγαλος is a vertebra of the neck (14.466; see the translation's note on 23.86–90). Leaf takes the story as an instance of exile for an unintended killing, where the usual Homeric remedy seems to have been a blood price, and observes that we do not know whether Amphidamas was akin to Patroclus (on 23.87; 9.632–636; see [[exile-and-refuge]] and [[poine]]). He suggests that the story is told to reconcile two other passages: Patroclus living in Peleus' house when the army gathered (11.765–790), and Achilles' promise to bring him home to Opus (18.324–327).

## Outside Homer

Apollodorus gives the boy a name: at Opus, in a quarrel during a game of knucklebones, Patroclus killed Clitonymus, son of Amphidamas (παῖδα Κλειτώνυμον τὸν Ἀμφιδάμαντος), and fled with his father to Peleus, with whom he lived (*Library* 3.13.8; see [[apollodorus]]). Strabo, who also takes from Homer that Patroclus was from Opus and fled to Peleus after an involuntary killing, says that the boy he killed is called Aeanes, after whom a precinct called the Aeaneum and a spring called Aeanis are shown; in Strabo's account Menoetius stayed behind in his native land (*Geography* 9.4.2; see [[strabo]]).
