## Family

“And Megara, the daughter of high-hearted Creon, / whom the son of Amphitryon, ever unyielding in his might, had as wife” (*Odyssey* 11.269–270; see [[creon-of-thebes]], [[heracles]] and [[amphitryon]]). The poem gives her no children and no story; she follows Heracles' mother Alcmene in the catalogue of heroines (see [[alcmene]]). Her name is that of the city of the Isthmus, which Homer does not name (see [[megara]]).

## Outside Homer

Euripides' *Heracles* is her tragedy. Amphitryon tells in the prologue that “Creon, the son of Menoeceus”, lord of Thebes, was the father of Megara, whose wedding all the Cadmeians celebrated with songs and the pipe when Heracles brought her home, and that Lycus has now killed Creon and taken the city (*Heracles* 8–12, 31–34). Heracles returns from Hades in time to save her and their sons from Lycus, but Hera sends madness on him, and he kills the children and their mother: she tries to shield the last child behind a locked door, and he breaks it down and kills wife and child with one arrow (996–1000), “having killed his children and his wife” (1014; see [[euripides]]). W. W. Merry gives the same story of her marriage and death: Creon gave her to Heracles for his victory over Erginus, king of Orchomenus, who had laid Thebes under tribute, and Heracles, maddened by Hera, killed her and their children; he adds that this Creon is not the brother-in-law of Oedipus (*Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on *Odyssey* 11.269). Euripides gives Megara's father the name and father that he gives Jocasta's brother, “Creon, my brother by the same mother”, the children of Menoeceus (*Phoenician Women* 10–11; see [[jocasta]]).
