**Born by the river.** Σάτνιος is formed from the name of the river Σατνιόεις, by whose banks he was born (see [[satnioeis]]), as Simoeisius, born on the banks of the Simoeis, was named for it (4.473–477; see [[simoeisius]]). His mother was “a noble Naiad nymph” and his father Enops tended cattle (14.444–445; see [[nymphs]] and [[enops]]). The nymphs of the Troad bear sons to herdsmen: the Naiad Abarbarea bore twins to Bucolion, who lay with her while he was tending his sheep (6.21–26; see [[abarbarea]] and [[bucolion]]), and Aphrodite bore Aeneas “to Anchises as he tended his cattle” (5.313; see [[aeneas]]). A Naiad bore Iphition too, to Otrynteus beneath snowy Tmolus, in nearly the same words (νύμφη τέκε νηῒς, 14.444, 20.384).

**His death.** He is the first man to die when the Argives, seeing Hector carried away, spring on the Trojans (14.440–443; see [[hector]]). The son of Oileus, “by far the first”, leaps on him and stabs him, and the stab is told twice, the second time with its place, “in the flank, and he fell back” (14.443, 14.446–447; see [[ajax-son-of-oileus]]), the words in which Agamemnon killed Adrastus (14.447 ≈ 6.64; see [[adrastus-the-trojan]]). Trojans and Danaans join battle around him, and Polydamas, coming to his defense, kills Prothoenor (14.447–452; see [[polydamas]], [[prothoenor]] and [[fighting-over-the-body]]).

## Outside Homer

[[Strabo]] quotes the three verses on his birth twice, reading Οἰνοπίδην … Οἴνοπι, “son of Oenops … to Oenops”, for Ἠνοπίδην … Ἤνοπι. Once they show that the poet names the river Satnioeis, on which Pedasus stood (*Geography* 13.1.50); once, with Dolon's list of the allies camped toward the sea, they show that the Leleges, whom the Catalogue leaves out, were present at the fighting (13.3.1; see [[leleges]] and [[pedasus-on-the-satnioeis]]). Strabo thus takes Satnius for one of the Leleges.
