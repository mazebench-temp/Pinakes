## The name

W. W. Merry takes the name Μαῖρα to mean “brilliant” (*Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on *Odyssey* 11.326). The Iliad gives the same name to a Nereid, one of the sisters of Thetis who come to her when she hears Achilles groan for Patroclus (18.48; see [[maera]]); nothing connects the two.

## In Homer

Her name opens the last verse of the catalogue of heroines: “And I saw Maera and Clymene and hateful Eriphyle” (*Odyssey* 11.326; see [[clymene-the-heroine]] and [[eriphyle]]). With these three names, Merry says, the story returns to Boeotian legend after the Attic names of Phaedra, Procris and Ariadne (on *Odyssey* 11.326).

## Outside Homer

Merry quotes the scholia for the story of Pherecydes: Maera was the daughter of Proetus and Anteia, most beautiful of women; Zeus fell in love with her, came to her and secretly seduced her, and she bore a son named Locrus, who founded Thebes with Amphion and Zethus. She was a nymph of Artemis, Merry adds, and the goddess killed her for her affair with Zeus (on *Odyssey* 11.326; see [[proetus]], [[anteia]], [[zeus]] and [[artemis]]). Proetus and Anteia are the king and queen of Argos in the Iliad's story of Bellerophon (6.157–165).
