τῇ δʼ ἄρʼ ἅμʼ Ἀδρήστη κλισίην εὔτυκτον ἔθηκεν, “And with her came Adreste and set for her a well-made chair” (*Odyssey* 4.123; see [[helen]] and [[handmaids]]). She is the first of the three women who attend Helen when she comes from her chamber, with Alcippe, who brings a rug of soft wool, and Phylo, who brings the silver basket (*Odyssey* 4.124–125; see [[alcippe]] and [[phylo]]). Her name is the feminine of Ἄδρηστος, the name of the king of Sicyon in the Catalogue and of several Trojans in the Iliad (*Iliad* 2.572, 2.830, 6.37; see [[adrastus]], [[adrestus]] and [[adrastus-the-trojan]]). Some ancient readers made no name of it at all, reading δρήστη, “a serving woman”, as if θεράπαινα, “handmaid” (scholia, in the critical note of Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 4.123), a word of the family of δρηστῆρες, the “serving men” who attend the suitors (*Odyssey* 16.248).
