{{quote:49bab69d-4f8d-5216-b8fd-34dd331dd0f7}}

τὴν μὲν Ἐχεκλῆος κρατερὸν μένος Ἀκτορίδαο / ἠγάγετο πρὸς δώματʼ, ἐπεὶ πόρε μυρία ἕδνα: “the strong might of Echecles, son of Actor, took her / to his house, after giving a countless bride-price” (16.189–190). When Polymele had borne Eudorus to Hermes, Echecles married her, and the boy stayed behind to be reared by her father Phylas (see [[polymele]], [[eudorus]], [[phylas]] and [[the-birth-of-eudorus]]).

κρατερὸν μένος, “the strong might”, with a man's name in the genitive, stands for the man himself, as when Apollo proposes to rouse “the strong might of Hector, tamer of horses” (7.38; see [[menos]]). ἠγάγετο, the middle, “led home for himself”, is the verb of taking a wife: Achilles wishes that Peleus had “taken a mortal wife” (ἀγαγέσθαι, 18.87), and in the Odyssey the same half-verse, ἠγάγετο πρὸς δώματα, tells how Melampus brought Neleus' daughter home as a wife for his brother (*Odyssey* 15.237–238; see [[marriage]]). ἐπεὶ πόρε μυρία ἕδνα, “after giving a countless bride-price”, is the formula of a marriage made, said also of Hector, who led Andromache from the house of Eetion (22.472), and of Neleus, who married Chloris for her beauty (*Odyssey* 11.282; see [[bride-price]]). The poem tells nothing else of Echecles. The name joins ἔχω, “hold”, and κλέος, “glory”.

The Trojan Echeclus, whom Patroclus kills in the same book (Ἔχεκλον, 16.694), bears a different name.
