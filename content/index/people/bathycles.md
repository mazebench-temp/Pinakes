**The rich man.** After Patroclus kills Sthenelaus, the Trojans give ground “as far as the rush of a long javelin carries” (16.586–592; see [[the-cast-of-a-spear]]), and Glaucus, “leader of the shield-bearing Lycians”, is the first to turn (see [[glaucus]] and [[shield-bearing]]). The man he kills is given a verse and a half of his own: he “had his home in Hellas” (see [[hellas]]) and “stood out among the Myrmidons for prosperity and wealth” (16.595–596; see [[myrmidons]]). ὄλβῳ τε πλούτῳ τε, “in prosperity and wealth”, is the pair of words with which Achilles will praise his father Peleus to Priam: the gods gave Peleus splendid gifts from his birth, for he surpassed all men “in prosperity and wealth”, and ruled the Myrmidons (24.534–536; see [[peleus]]). μετέπρεπε Μυρμιδόνεσσι, “stood out among the Myrmidons”, is said in the same book of Peisander, the best spearman among them after Patroclus (16.194; see [[peisander-son-of-maemalus]]). The Iliad often marks a dying man by his wealth: Oresbius of Hyle was “much intent on his wealth” (5.708), Axylus of Arisbe was “wealthy in substance” (6.14), and Euchenor of Corinth was “rich and brave” (13.664; see [[oresbius]], [[axylus]] and [[euchenor]]).

**His death.** Glaucus turns “suddenly, as he was overtaking him in pursuit”: the pursuer is struck by the man he is chasing. “He fell with a thud” (16.599; see [[fell-with-a-thud]]), and the two armies answer the death together: “thick grief seized the Achaeans / that a brave man had fallen; but the Trojans rejoiced greatly, / and came and stood around him in a body” (16.599–601). Then Meriones kills Laogonus, son of Onetor, and the fight goes on (16.603–607; see [[meriones]] and [[laogonus]]). Bathycles is named only here.

**The name.** Βαθυκλῆς joins βαθύς, “deep”, and κλέος, “fame”.
