## Family

Chloris bore Neleus “splendid children, / Nestor and Chromius and proud Periclymenus”, and besides them a daughter, Pero (*Odyssey* 11.285–287; see [[chloris]], [[neleus]], [[nestor]], [[periclymenus]] and [[pero]]). Nothing else is told of him. Nestor says in the Iliad that Neleus had twelve sons and that he alone was left of them when Heracles came to Pylos and killed the best of its men (*Iliad* 11.690–693; see [[heracles-sacks-pylos]]), so that Chromius, if he is one of the twelve, fell then.

He is not the Pylian Chromius whom Nestor sets among his five captains in *Iliad* 4 (*Iliad* 4.295; see [[chromius]]): by Nestor's own account his brothers were long dead by then. The Iliad has three more men of the name, a son of Priam, a Lycian and a Trojan killed by Teucer (see [[chromius-son-of-priam]], [[chromius-of-lycia]] and [[chromius-killed-by-teucer]]).

## Outside Homer

The Hesiodic *Catalogue of Women* told how Heracles “killed the noble sons of steadfast Neleus, / eleven of them; the twelfth, the Gerenian horseman Nestor, / happened to be a guest among the horse-taming Gerenians” (fr. 11 Evelyn-White; see [[hesiod]]), and W. W. Merry repeats that all of Neleus' twelve sons but Nestor were killed by Heracles (*Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on *Odyssey* 11.285).
