“ἐκ μὲν Σιδῶνος πολυχάλκου εὔχομαι εἶναι, / κούρη δʼ εἴμʼ Ἀρύβαντος ἐγὼ ῥυδὸν ἀφνειοῖο”, “‘I claim to be from Sidon, rich in bronze, / and I am the daughter of Arybas, whose wealth came in a flood’” (*Odyssey* 15.425–426; see [[sidon]] and [[brazen]]): the Phoenician slave woman in the house of Eumaeus' father names him when the trader who has lain with her asks who she is; Taphian pirates had seized her as she came from the fields and sold her to Ctesius (*Odyssey* 15.427–429; see [[the-phoenician-nurse]], [[taphians]], [[piracy]] and [[ctesius]]). The traders promise to take her home, to the house of her father and mother, “for truly they are still alive and are reputed rich” (*Odyssey* 15.431–433). The poem tells nothing else of him.

## The name

D. B. Monro: “ῥυδόν ‘in a stream’; ‘one to whom riches came in a flood’—an appropriate word for a trader's wealth. It sounds like a piece of mercantile argot. Ἀρύβας seems to be a Phoenician name—perhaps Hasdrubal” (*Homer's Odyssey, Books XIII–XXIV*, 1901, on *Odyssey* 15.426; see the Odyssey translation's note on 15.426). A Greek bore the name in history: the Suda's Arybas is “the son of Alcetas, and king of the Molossians” (Suda α 4058).

## Descriptions

- ῥυδὸν ἀφνειός, “whose wealth came in a flood” (*Odyssey* 15.426; see [[wealthy]]): ῥυδόν is found only here in Homer.
