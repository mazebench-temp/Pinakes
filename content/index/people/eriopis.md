She is named only in the story of her stepson:

{{quote:d5190763-e1f7-5a8c-88e8-c4139123a167}}

Medon, the son of Oileus by Rhene (2.727–728; see [[rhene]]), killed γνωτὸν μητρυιῆς Ἐριώπιδος, “a kinsman of his stepmother Eriopis” (13.697), and lived in exile at [[Phylace]] (see [[medon]] and [[exile-and-refuge]]). γνωτός is a kinsman, often a brother (17.35, 22.234); which of her kin he was is not said. μητρυιή, “stepmother”, is used elsewhere in the Iliad only of the beautiful Eeriboea, stepmother of the sons of Aloeus, who had imprisoned Ares; she told Hermes, and he stole Ares away (5.385–391; see [[eeriboea]]). ἣν ἔχʼ Ὀϊλεύς, “whom Oileus had as wife” (13.697): Eriopis was his wife, as Rhene, Medon's mother, was not (see [[oileus]] and [[nothos]]). Whether she bore the lesser Ajax the poem does not say; it names no mother for him (see [[ajax-son-of-oileus]]). The four verses are told again when Aeneas kills Medon (15.332–336).

The name joins ἐρι-, “very” (as in ἐριβῶλαξ, “deep-soiled”), and -ῶπις, “eyed” (as in βοῶπις, “ox-eyed”): “large-eyed”.
