Homer names him only in his son's patronymic, Ἀμύντορος Ὀρμενίδαο, “my father Amyntor, son of Ormenus” (9.448), and again when the helmet of boar's tusks is said to have been stolen from Amyntor's house at Eleon (10.266; see [[amyntor]]). The name is the participle ὄρμενος, “starting up, rushing on”, and other men bear it: the Trojan shot by Teucer (8.274; see [[ormenus]]), another Trojan killed by Polypoetes (12.187), and the father of Ctesius, who was the father of the swineherd Eumaeus (Od. 15.414).

## Outside Homer

Demetrius of Scepsis made this Ormenus a son of Cercaphus son of Aeolus and the founder of Ormenium, a village under Mount Pelion, and gave him two sons, Amyntor and Euaemon, so that Phoenix and Eurypylus were cousins (Strabo 9.5.18; see [[hellas-or-ormenium]], [[ormenium]], [[euaemon]] and [[eurypylus]]).
