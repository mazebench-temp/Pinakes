βίης Ἐτεοκληείης, literally “the Eteoclean might”, names the man by his strength, as βίη Ἡρακληείη names Heracles ([[might-of-heracles]]) and Πριάμοιο βίη names Priam (3.105; [[might-of-priam]]). The adjectival form is old: a Linear B tablet from Pylos has the same adjective formed from the same name, e-te-wo-ke-re-we-i-jo. Ἐτεοκλῆς = ἐτεός (“true”) + κλέος (“fame”): “true fame”.

Homer makes him the lord of the house where the [[Cadmeians]] feast and a lone stranger beats them at every contest, and nothing more. He does not call him the son of Oedipus or the brother of [[Polynices]], and he does not say how he died. See [[tydeus]] and [[thebes]].

## Outside Homer

In the lost *Thebaid* Oedipus cursed his sons to share their inheritance only in war (Athenaeus 11.465e; see [[polynices]]). In tragedy Eteocles keeps the throne from his exiled brother and defends Thebes against the Seven. In Aeschylus' *Seven against Thebes* he posts a Theban champion against each of the attackers and goes himself to the seventh gate to meet Polynices, and the brothers kill each other. Euripides' *Phoenician Women* and Statius' *Thebaid* tell the same end. See [[the-seven-against-thebes]].
