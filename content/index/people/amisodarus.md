**The man who reared the Chimaera.** The narrator gives the two dead brothers a father, and the father a single deed: υἷες ἀκοντισταὶ Ἀμισωδάρου, ὅς ῥα Χίμαιραν / θρέψεν ἀμαιμακέτην πολέσιν κακὸν ἀνθρώποισιν, “the spearmen sons of Amisodarus, who reared the Chimaera, / the irresistible, an evil to many men” (16.328–329; see [[chimaera]] and [[irresistible]]). θρέψεν, “reared”, is the verb of rearing children and beasts, as Apollo reared the mares of Eumelus in Pereia (θρέψʼ, 2.766). The Chimaera is the monster “of divine race, not of men” that Bellerophon killed at the bidding of the king of Lycia (6.179–183; see [[bellerophon-in-lycia]] and [[bellerophon]]); Book 6 does not say who reared it. The sons of Amisodarus fought at Troy among the companions of Bellerophon's grandson Sarpedon, and the sons of Nestor killed them both (16.317–327; see [[sarpedon]], [[atymnius-son-of-amisodarus]] and [[maris]]).

**The name and the verses.** Walter Leaf observes that Amisodarus is not named in Book 6, where the Chimaera seems to be a wild beast rather than a creature reared by a man. He reports that the name has been recognized as genuinely non-Greek, formed like those of Pixodarus of Caria (Herodotus 5.118.2) and of Pandarus, and thought it probably connected with the town of Amisus in Pontus; he also notes that Payne Knight rejected 16.326–329 (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 16.326–329).

## Outside Homer

Apollodorus repeats, citing Homer, that Amisodarus reared the Chimaera, and adds from Hesiod that it was born of Typhon and Echidna (*Library* 2.3.1; see [[apollodorus]]). Plutarch tells a version without the monster among his stories of brave women. Amisodarus, whom the Lycians call Isaras, came from the Lycian colony near Zeleia with pirate ships; their captain, Chimarrhus, a brutal warrior, sailed in a ship with a lion for its figurehead and a serpent at its stern and did the Lycians much harm, until Bellerophon pursued him on Pegasus and killed him (*Bravery of Women* 9; see [[plutarch]]). Leaf points to the passage, *Moralia* 247F, for “the rationalizing history” of Amisodarus (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 16.326). Zeleia, under Ida, is the town of Pandarus, whose homeland the Iliad also calls Lycia (2.824–827, 5.105, 5.173; see [[zeleia]] and [[pandarus]]).
