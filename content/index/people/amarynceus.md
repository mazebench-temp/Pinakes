Ἀμαρυγκεΐδης: "son of Amarynceus". Homer names him in the patronymic of his son Diores, one of the four leaders of the Epeians (2.622, 4.517; see [[diores]]), and once for himself, in Nestor's memory of his funeral, when the Epeians buried him at [[Buprasium]] and his sons set out prizes; there the young Nestor won every contest but the chariot race (23.629–645; see [[the-funeral-games-of-amarynceus]]).

## Outside Homer

Pausanias tells the Elean legend. Augeas, preparing to resist Heracles, made friends with the sons of Actor and with Amarynceus, a good soldier, whose father Pyttius had come from Thessaly to Elis, and gave him a share in the rule of Elis (*Description of Greece* 5.1.10–11; see [[pausanias]], [[augeas]] and [[the-moliones]]). Neither Amarynceus nor his son Diores, Pausanias adds, was a mere private man, as Homer's Catalogue shows by giving Diores ten of the forty Elean ships (5.3.4). The Hesiodic *Catalogue of Women* knew a Hippostratus, “son of Amarynceus, a shoot of Ares” (Ἀμαρυγκείδης Ἱππόστρατος, ὄζος Ἄρηος, Hesiod fr. 50 Evelyn-White), who, as Apollodorus reports Hesiod, seduced Periboea, the daughter of Hipponous of Olenus, so that her father sent her away to Oeneus with orders to kill her (fr. 51, from Apollodorus 1.8.4; see [[hesiod]] and [[oeneus]]).
