πελώριος, “huge”, is said of Ajax (3.229), of Ares himself (7.208) and of Hades (5.395). Periphas is “by far the best of the Aetolians, the splendid son of Ochesius” (5.843), and he is the only man the Iliad shows Ares killing by himself. The god is stripping his armor, like any warrior, when Athena drives Diomedes' chariot at him. She puts on the helmet of Hades so that Ares will not see her, and Ares leaves the body lying “where he had first killed him and taken away his life” and goes straight for Diomedes (5.844–849; see [[helmet-of-hades]] and [[diomedes-wounds-ares]]). The dead man is an Aetolian, of the people of Oeneus and of Tydeus, and Diomedes, Tydeus' son, wounds his killer (see [[aetolians]] and [[tydeus]]).

The herald Periphas, son of Epytus, whose likeness Apollo takes to rouse Aeneas in Book 17, is another man (17.322–325).
