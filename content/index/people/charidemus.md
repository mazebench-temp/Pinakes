Charidemus served Athens, Olynthus and the Thracian kings by turns in the 360s and 350s BCE. Aristocrates proposed that anyone who killed him should be liable to seizure anywhere in the allied cities, and Demosthenes' speech against the decree recounts his career (23). When Alexander, after destroying Thebes in 335, demanded his surrender, he fled to Persia, where Darius III had him put to death. See [[strabax]].
