## The name

Δολίος is the adjective δόλιος, “crafty”, from δόλος, “trick” (see [[dolos]]), made a name; the same letters stand in the lion simile at the end of Book 4 of the Odyssey, δόλιον περὶ κύκλον, “their crafty circle” (*Odyssey* 4.792).

## Later in the Odyssey

The goatherd Melanthius, who insults Odysseus on the road to the town, is υἱὸς Δολίοιο, “the son of Dolius” (*Odyssey* 17.212; again 22.159), and the faithless handmaid Melantho is the daughter “whom Dolius begot, but Penelope reared” (*Odyssey* 18.322), plain renderings. In the last book a Dolius is the old man of Laertes' farm: Odysseus does not find him in the great orchard, for he has gone with his sons to gather stones for a wall (*Odyssey* 24.222–225); he comes in from the fields with his sons, called by their mother, an old Sicilian woman, who reared them and tended the old man (*Odyssey* 24.386–390), and with his six sons and old Laertes he puts on armor to stand beside Odysseus against the suitors' kinsmen (*Odyssey* 24.497–499). Merry holds that the father of Melanthius and Melantho and the gardener of Laertes can hardly be the same character (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 4.735).
