Δόλων Εὐμήδεος υἱὸς / κήρυκος θείοιο, “Dolon, son of Eumedes / the divine herald” (10.314–315): the father is named when the son volunteers to spy for Hector, and again in the verse that brings in Dolon's answers to Odysseus (10.412, 10.426; see [[dolon]]). θεῖος, “divine”, is given to heralds, the messengers of Zeus (4.192; see [[heralds]] and [[divine]]). The house is rich: Dolon says that his father would pay a boundless ransom for him (10.378–381).

The name joins εὖ, “well”, and μήδεα, “counsels”: “good at counsel”, as a herald should be; Priam's herald Idaeus “knew wise counsels” (πεπνυμένα μήδεα εἰδώς, 7.278; see [[idaeus]]). His son goes out “in his thoughtlessness” (10.350).

## Outside Homer

In the *Aeneid* Dolon's son is named Eumedes, “bearing his grandfather's name”, and Turnus kills him (*Aeneid* 12.346–361; see [[virgil-rhesus-and-dolon]]).
