Φείδιππος = φείδομαι ("spare") + ἵππος ("horse"): "sparer of horses". Names with ἵππος were fashionable in aristocratic families, and Aristophanes jokes about them: Strepsiades' wife wants a horse in her son's name, he wants his father's name Pheidonides, and they settle on Pheidippides (*Clouds* 60–67). Pheidippus is named only here in the Iliad.
