**The son of Lampus.** Λαμπετίδης, “son of Lampus”, is found only here, and the narrator explains it at once: “the son of Lampus, the strongest son that Lampus, / son of Laomedon, fathered” (15.526–527). Lampus sits among the elders on the wall and is Priam's brother (3.147, 20.237–238; see [[lampus]] and [[laomedon]]), so that Dolops is Hector's first cousin, as are Caletor and Melanippus, who also die at the ships (15.419–421, 15.575–578; see [[caletor]] and [[melanippus-son-of-hicetaon]]). He is twice called skilled, αἰχμῆς ἐῢ εἰδὼς, “well skilled with the spear” (15.525), and εὖ εἰδότα θούριδος ἀλκῆς, “well skilled in furious courage” (15.527). His name is the word for a Dolopian, one of the people at the edge of Phthia whom Peleus gave Phoenix to rule (9.484; see [[dolopians]]).

**Against Meges.** He springs at Meges, who is stripping Croesmus, and stabs the middle of his shield from close by, but the corselet that Meges' father Phyleus brought from Ephyra keeps the spear off (15.523–534; see [[meges]], [[phyleus]] and [[croesmus]]). Meges' spear strikes the crown of his helmet and breaks off the horsehair plume, which falls in the dust, “bright with fresh purple” (15.535–538; see [[helmet]]). While they fight, Menelaus comes to Meges' defense, “unseen, and hit him in the shoulder from behind; / and the point sped through his chest in its fury” (15.541–542; see [[menelaus]]), and the two Achaeans hurry to strip his armor (15.544–545).

**Fought over.** Hector, seeing the Achaeans “busy about the armor of Dolops” (15.555), calls on his kinsmen and rebukes Melanippus, son of Hicetaon, whose cousin has been killed (15.545–558; see [[hector]] and [[stripping-the-dead]]). Melanippus follows him into the fight, and Antilochus kills him (15.559–578).

## Outside Homer

[[Aristotle]] quotes the verse of his death among the lines in which Homer makes lifeless things alive: in “the spear point sped through his chest, raging” the spear point rages like a living thing, and raging is activity (*Rhetoric* 3.11, 1411b31–1412a4; see [[homers-lifeless-things-alive]]).
