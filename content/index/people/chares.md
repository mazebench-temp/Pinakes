---
title: Chares
greek: Χάρης
kind: person
tags: [statesman, warrior, athenian]
summary: Athenian general (c. 400–after 324 BCE), often in command and often prosecuted, who led Athenian forces in the Social War and, with Lysicles, at Chaeronea.
---

Chares held commands for four decades: in the Social War against Athens' rebellious allies (357–355 BCE; Diodorus 16.21–22), against Philip in the north and at Olynthus, and at Chaeronea in 338, where he and Lysicles commanded the Athenians (16.85). In the *Rhetoric*'s third book Cephisodotus attacks him when he is eager to submit his accounts for the Olynthian war (*Rhetoric* 3.10, 1411a6–9), Iphicrates says that the road of his words runs through the midst of Chares' deeds (3.10, 1411b2–3), and Isocrates, advising the city, accuses him in the speech *On the Peace*, which Aristotle calls the *Symmachicus* (3.17, 1418a31–32).
