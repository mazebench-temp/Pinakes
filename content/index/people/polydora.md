Πηλῆος θυγάτηρ καλὴ Πολυδώρη, “the daughter of Peleus, fair Polydora” (16.175), is named once, as the mother of the first of the five Myrmidon leaders. She bore him to the river Spercheius, γυνὴ θεῷ εὐνηθεῖσα, “a woman who lay with a god” (16.176; see [[spercheius]] and [[menesthius-son-of-spercheius]]). Then Borus, son of Perieres, married her ἀναφανδόν, “openly”, a word found only here in Homer, “after giving a boundless bride-price” (πορὼν ἀπερείσια ἕδνα, 16.178), and the son went by his name (see [[borus-son-of-perieres]] and [[bride-price]]). The god's union is told in half a verse; the man's marriage is public and paid for. The next birth in the catalogue is told in the same order: Polymele bears Eudorus to Hermes, and then Echecles takes her to his house “after giving a countless bride-price” (16.179–190; see [[polymele]] and [[the-birth-of-eudorus]]).

The name is the adjective πολύδωρος, “of many gifts”, which Homer gives to wives, Andromache (6.394, 22.88) and Penelope (*Odyssey* 24.294; see [[richly-dowered]]). Whether the gifts are a dowry or the bride-price paid for a girl is disputed; Polydora was married with “a boundless bride-price”.

If her father is Achilles' father, she is Achilles' sister, and the poem does not say so. Achilles himself, in *Iliad* 24, speaks of Peleus as a man who had no line of sons in his halls to rule after him, only one son, doomed to an untimely death (24.538–540; see [[peleus]]); he says nothing of a daughter.

## Outside Homer

A scholium on 16.175 says that Hesiod and the others called Peleus' daughter Polydora (Hesiod, fr. 60 Evelyn-White); Walter Leaf reports from the same scholium that Zenodotus read her name as Cleodora (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 16.175). In Apollodorus Peleus, fleeing to Phthia, is purified by Eurytion, son of Actor, and receives his daughter Antigone and a third of the land; “and a daughter Polydora was born to him, whom Borus, son of Perieres, married” (*Library* 3.13.1). Strabo calls Menesthius the son “of Achilles' sister” (*Geography* 9.5.9). Another Polydora, the wife of Protesilaus in the *Cypria*, is a different woman (Pausanias 4.2.7; see [[protesilaus]]).
