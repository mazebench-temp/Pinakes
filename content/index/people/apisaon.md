The Trojans and their allies are following Ajax as he gives ground, stabbing at his shield, when Eurypylus comes to stand beside him (11.563–577; see [[ajax]] and [[eurypylus]]). His spear takes Apisaon ἧπαρ ὑπὸ πραπίδων, “in the liver beneath the midriff”, and loosens his knees (11.578–579). As Eurypylus strips the armor from his shoulders, Paris draws his bow and shoots him in the right thigh, and the reed breaks off in the wound (11.580–584; see [[paris]] and [[the-wounded-chiefs]]). As with Agastrophus, the Achaean is hit while he is bent over the Trojan he has killed (11.373–377; see [[agastrophus]] and [[stripping-the-dead]]).

The two verses of his death return twice with other names: for Hypsenor, son of Hippasus, whom Deiphobus kills (13.411–412), and for another Apisaon, a Paeonian, also son of Hippasus, whom Lycomedes kills (17.348–349). Only the patronymic tells this Apisaon from that one. Phausius, his father, is named only here.
