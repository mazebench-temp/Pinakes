Οἰνοπίδης, “son of Oenops” (5.707), is all that Homer says of him. The name is the adjective οἶνοψ, “wine-colored, wine-dark”, which Homer uses of the sea (οἴνοπα πόντον, “the wine-dark sea”, 5.771; see [[wine-dark]]) and of a yoke of oxen (13.703). In the Odyssey another Oenops is the father of Leodes, the suitors' soothsayer (Od. 21.144).
