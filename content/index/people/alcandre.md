Ἀλκάνδρη, Πολύβοιο δάμαρ, ὃς ἔναιʼ ἐνὶ Θήβῃς / Αἰγυπτίῃς, “Alcandre, the wife of Polybus, who lived in Thebes / in Egypt” (*Odyssey* 4.126–127; see [[polybus-of-thebes]] and [[thebes-in-egypt]]). While her husband gave Menelaus two silver bathtubs, two tripods and ten talents of gold, she gave Helen gifts of her own: “she granted her a golden distaff and a basket on wheels / of silver, and its rims were finished with gold” (*Odyssey* 4.130–132; see [[helen]], [[gold-and-silver]] and [[guest-gifts]]). The name is the feminine of Ἄλκανδρος, from ἀλκή, “might”, and ἀνήρ, “man”, the name of a Lycian whom Odysseus kills in the Iliad (*Iliad* 5.678; see [[alcandrus]]). With [[Polydamna]], the wife of Thon, who gave Helen her drug, she is one of the women of Egypt whose gifts Helen keeps at Sparta (*Odyssey* 4.228).
