Alastor is the second of Nestor's five captains, with [[Pelagon]], [[Chromius]], [[Haemon]] and [[Bias]] (4.295–296). The name is a word. ἀλάστωρ is usually derived from ἀ- (“not”) and the root of λανθάνομαι (“forget”): “one who does not forget”. In tragedy it is the avenging spirit that pursues a crime, as in Clytemnestra's “ancient bitter avenger of Atreus” (Aeschylus, *Agamemnon* 1501).

Homer has several men of the name. The same pair of names, Alastor and Chromius, returns among the Lycians whom Odysseus kills (5.677). A “brilliant Alastor” who, with Mecisteus son of Echius, carries wounded men back to the ships (8.333, 13.422) may be the Pylian, but the poem does not say so (see [[alastor-who-carries-teucer]] and [[mecisteus-son-of-echius]]). Tros, whom Achilles kills, is the son of another Alastor (20.463).
