Εὐηνορίδης, “son of Euenor” (*Odyssey* 2.242, and Εὐηνορίδην when his son dies, 22.294): Euenor is named only in the patronymic of the suitor Leiocritus, and the poem says nothing else of him (see [[leiocritus-son-of-euenor]]). The name is formed from εὖ, “well”, and ἀνήρ, “man”. It is not the Iliad's Euenus (Εὔηνος), the father of Mynes and Epistrophus (*Iliad* 2.693; see [[euenus]]).
