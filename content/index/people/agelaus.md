**The first kill of the rally.** When the eagle of Zeus has put heart into the Achaeans, no one drives across the ditch ahead of Diomedes, and the first man he kills is Agelaus (8.247–257; see [[diomedes]] and [[the-achaean-wall]]). ἕλεν ἄνδρα κορυστήν, “he killed a helmeted man”, ends the verse of Antilochus' first kill in the first battle (4.457; see [[helmeted]]). Agelaus has turned to flee, and he dies of the wound that Diomedes warned Odysseus of earlier in the day: “Do not let someone plant a spear in your back as you flee” (8.95). The verses of the thrust are used for other men struck from behind (8.258–259 ≈ 5.40–41, 11.447–448), and his fall is told in the verse of Pandarus' death at Diomedes' hands (8.260 = 5.294; see [[androktasia]]).

**The name.** Ἀγέλαος joins ἄγω, “lead”, and λαός, “people”: “leader of the people”. Hector kills an Achaean Agelaus in Book 11 (11.302), and in the Odyssey Agelaus, son of Damastor, is one of the suitors (Od. 20.321, 22.212).
