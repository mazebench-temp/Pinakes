Homer names Molus twice, both times as the father of Meriones. In the history of the helmet of boar's tusks, Autolycus stole it from Amyntor's house at Eleon and gave it at Scandeia to Amphidamas of Cythera, “and Amphidamas gave it to Molus to be a guest-gift, and Molus gave it to his son Meriones to wear” (10.266–270; see [[the-boars-tusk-helmet]], [[autolycus]], [[amphidamas]] and [[meriones]]). ξεινήϊον, “guest-gift”, makes Molus the guest-friend of Amphidamas (see [[guest-gifts]] and [[xenia]]). In Book 13 Idomeneus calls his squire Μηριόνη Μόλου υἱέ, “Meriones, son of Molus” (13.249; see [[idomeneus]]). The poem says nothing else of him, not even where he lived; his son leads the Cretans with Idomeneus (2.650–651; see [[cretans]]).

The name is not that of Molion, the attendant of Thymbraeus whom Odysseus kills (Μολίονα, 11.322), or of the two Moliones whom Nestor fought in his youth (Μολίονε, 11.709, 11.750).

## Outside Homer

The mythographer Apollodorus names Idomeneus, Crete and a bastard son, Molus, as the children of Deucalion (3.3.1). Molus would then be Idomeneus' half-brother, and Meriones his nephew. The poem says nothing of this. It names no kinship between the two men; Meriones is Idomeneus' squire (ὀπάων, 7.165) and his good attendant (θεράπων ἐΰς, 13.246; see [[meriones]] and [[idomeneus]]).
