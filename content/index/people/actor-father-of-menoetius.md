Μενοίτιος Ἄκτορος υἱός, “Menoetius, son of Actor” (11.785): the son is named by his father, as Diomedes is Τυδέος υἱός, “son of Tydeus”. Nestor gives Menoetius his father's name as he brings in the charge Menoetius gave Patroclus at Peleus' house (see [[peleus-sends-achilles-to-troy]]). Achilles uses the same phrase when he asks the weeping Patroclus whether some news has come from Phthia: “they say that Menoetius, son of Actor, is still alive” (16.13–14). Homer tells nothing else of him. The name is usually explained as ἄκτωρ, “leader”, from ἄγω, “lead”.

Homer knows several men of the name: [[actor-son-of-azeus|Actor, son of Azeus]], in whose house the sons of Ares were born (2.513), and [[actor-of-elis|Actor of Elis]], father of the twins Cteatus and Eurytus (2.621, 11.750). This Actor is neither.
