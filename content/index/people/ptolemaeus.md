Πτολεμαῖος is formed from πτόλεμος, the epic by-form of πόλεμος (“war”): “warlike”. His own patronymic, Πειραΐδης (“son of [[Peiraeus]]”), follows in the same line, so that a single verse gives three generations of Agamemnon's attendant. Nothing else is said of him, and the name occurs nowhere else in Homer.

## Outside Homer

The name became famous as that of Ptolemy, the Macedonian general who made himself king of Egypt after the death of Alexander, and of the dynasty he founded.
