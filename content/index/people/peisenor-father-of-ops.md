Πεισηνορίδαο, “son of Peisenor” (*Odyssey* 1.429): Peisenor is named only in his son's patronymic, at the end of the verse that gives Eurycleia her lineage (see [[ops]] and [[eurycleia]]). The poem says nothing else of him. The name is formed from πείθω, “persuade”, and ἀνήρ, “man”.

The Iliad has a Trojan of the name, the father of Cleitus, Polydamas' charioteer (*Iliad* 15.445; see [[peisenor]] and [[cleitus]]), and in the Odyssey the herald Peisenor of Ithaca puts the staff into Telemachus' hand in the assembly (*Odyssey* 2.37–38). Nothing joins the three.
