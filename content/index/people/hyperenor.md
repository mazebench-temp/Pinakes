**His death.** The son of Atreus who kills him is Menelaus, as Menelaus himself says in Book 17 (17.24–28; see [[menelaus]]). He stabs him in the flank, “and the bronze let out his bowels / as it tore through” (14.517–518), as Idomeneus' spear let out the bowels of Oenomaus (13.507–508; see [[oenomaus-the-trojan]]). “His soul through the stab wound / rushed out in haste, and darkness covered his eyes” (14.518–519; see [[psyche]] and [[darkness-covered-his-eyes]]). At the deaths of Patroclus and Hector the soul flies from the limbs to Hades (16.856 = 22.362); Hyperenor's goes out through the wound, by the way Euphorbus' blood will flow (κατʼ οὐταμένην ὠτειλήν, 14.518, 17.86). He is ποιμὴν λαῶν, “shepherd of the people” (14.516), the title of kings and leaders, given in this book to Hector too (14.423; see [[shepherd-of-the-people]]).

**His family.** Book 14 does not say whose son he is. In Book 17, when Euphorbus stands over Patroclus' body, Menelaus warns him that the sons of Panthous are too proud: Hyperenor, “tamer of horses”, did not enjoy his youth when he scorned Menelaus as the worst warrior among the Danaans and stood up to him, and he did not go home on his own feet to gladden his wife and his parents (17.23–28). Euphorbus answers that Menelaus will pay for his brother, whose wife he widowed in a new bridal chamber, leaving his parents grief and lamentation (17.34–37). He is thus a brother of Polydamas, who kills Prothoenor earlier in the book (14.449–457; see [[polydamas]] and [[panthous]]).
