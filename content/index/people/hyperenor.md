**His death.** The son of Atreus who kills him is Menelaus, as Menelaus himself says in *Iliad* 17 (17.24–28; see [[menelaus]]). He stabs him in the flank, “and the bronze let out his bowels / as it tore through” (14.517–518), as Idomeneus' spear let out the bowels of Oenomaus (13.507–508; see [[oenomaus-the-trojan]]). “His soul through the stab wound / rushed out in haste, and darkness covered his eyes” (14.518–519; see [[psyche]] and [[darkness-covered-his-eyes]]). At the deaths of Patroclus and Hector the soul flies from the limbs to Hades (16.856 = 22.362); Hyperenor's goes out through the wound, by the way Euphorbus' blood will flow (κατʼ οὐταμένην ὠτειλήν, 14.518, 17.86). He is ποιμὴν λαῶν, “shepherd of the people” (14.516), the title of kings and leaders, given in Book 14 to Hector too (14.423; see [[shepherd-of-the-people]]).

**His family.** *Iliad* 14 does not say whose son he is. Book 17 does: Menelaus names him among the proud sons of Panthous (17.23–24), and Euphorbus calls him his brother (γνωτὸν ἐμόν, 17.35; see [[euphorbus]] and [[panthous]]). He is thus a brother also of Polydamas, who kills Prothoenor earlier in Book 14 (14.449–457; see [[polydamas]]). He had a wife, newly married (17.28, 17.36), and both his parents were living (17.28, 17.37); Menelaus kills Euphorbus too over the body of Patroclus (17.43–60).
