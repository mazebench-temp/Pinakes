υἷε Βίαντος, “the two sons of Bias” (20.460): Bias is named only in this patronymic phrase, in the list of the men Achilles kills after Apollo has saved Hector (20.455–489; see [[laogonus-son-of-bias]], [[dardanus-son-of-bias]] and [[achilles]]). The poem says nothing else of him.

The name, formed from βίη, “might, force”, is borne by two other men in the Iliad: one of the five captains around whom Nestor marshals the Pylians (4.296; see [[bias]]), and an Athenian who follows Menestheus at the ships (13.691; see [[bias-the-athenian]]). The sage Bias of Priene, whose precept Aristotle cites, is another (*Rhetoric* 1389b24; see [[bias-of-priene]]).
