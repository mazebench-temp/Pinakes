# Θερσίλοχον (17.216; 21.209, a Paeonian killed by Achilles, perhaps the same man).
grc: [Θερσίλοχον]
passages:
  - 17.215-219 | Hector urges on the leaders of the allies
---

**One of ten.** Hector, in Achilles' armor, urges on “Mesthles and Glaucus and Medon and Thersilochus” and six more leaders of the allies (17.216–218; see [[hector]] and [[trojan-allies]]). Walter Leaf notes that these names are mostly taken from the Catalogue (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 17.216); Thersilochus is not in it, and the poem does not say whom he led.

The name returns in Book 21, at the head of the list of Paeonians whom Achilles kills beside the river after Asteropaeus, Θερσίλοχόν τε Μύδωνά τε Ἀστύπυλόν τε (21.209; see [[paeonians]] and [[asteropaeus]]). The poem does not say whether they are one man; in Hector's list the next name after his is that of the Paeonian leader Asteropaeus (17.217).

**The name.** Θερσίλοχος joins θέρσος, the Aeolic form of θάρσος, “boldness”, to λόχος, “ambush”: “bold in the ambush” (see [[thersites]]).
