The name is found twice in Homer, in two lists. Hector, in Achilles' armor, urges on ten leaders of the allies, and Thersilochus is the fourth, just before the Paeonian leader Asteropaeus (17.216–217); and a Thersilochus heads the list of the Paeonians whom Achilles kills by the river when Asteropaeus has fallen (21.209; see [[asteropaeus]] and [[paeonians]]). The poem does not say whether they are one man, and the index takes both lines for the same entry.

## Etymology

Θερσίλοχος joins θέρσος, the Aeolic form of θάρσος, “boldness”, to λόχος, “ambush”: “bold in the ambush” (see [[thersites]]).

## Outside Homer

Virgil takes three names of Hector's list, in Homer's order, for Trojans whom Aeneas sees among the war dead in the underworld, “Glaucumque Medontaque Thersilochumque” (*Aeneid* 6.483), a verse built like Homer's, Γλαῦκόν τε Μέδοντά τε Θερσίλοχόν τε (17.216), with -que for τε; and a Thersilochus is among the men Turnus kills in the last battle (*Aeneid* 12.363; see [[virgil]]).
