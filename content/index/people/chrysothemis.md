**The name.** Χρυσόθεμις joins χρυσός (“gold”) and θέμις (“custom, right”; see [[themis]]): “golden right”.

**In the Iliad.** She is named only in the offer, first of the three daughters who live “in my well-built hall”, and again when Odysseus repeats the list to Achilles (9.144–145, 9.286–287). Whichever of them Achilles chooses he may take to the house of Peleus “without bride-price” (ἀνάεδνον), and Agamemnon will give with her a dowry “such as no man has ever yet given with his daughter” (9.146–148; see [[marriage]] and [[the-gifts-of-agamemnon]]). Achilles will not marry a daughter of Agamemnon, “not if she rivaled golden Aphrodite in beauty and equaled gray-eyed Athena in handiwork” (9.388–391). See [[laodice-daughter-of-agamemnon]] and [[iphianassa]].

## Outside Homer

Later poets kept her when they changed her sisters. In Euripides' *Orestes* Electra names the three daughters as “Chrysothemis and Iphigenia and I, Electra” (*Orestes* 22–23; see [[euripides]]). In Sophocles' *Electra* the chorus names Chrysothemis and Iphianassa as sisters still living in the house (157), and Chrysothemis is on stage, the sister who yields to the rulers and urges Electra to yield too (see [[sophocles]]).
