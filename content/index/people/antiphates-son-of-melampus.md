γείνατο δʼ Ἀντιφάτην καὶ Μάντιον, υἷε κραταιώ, “and begot Antiphates and Mantius, two mighty sons” (*Odyssey* 15.242): Melampus, settled in Argos, married a wife there and had two sons, the heads of the two lines of his house (see [[melampus]] and [[mantius]]). From Antiphates came Oicles, and from Oicles Amphiaraus, who perished at Thebes, and his sons Alcmaeon and Amphilochus (*Odyssey* 15.243–248; see [[oicles]], [[amphiaraus]], [[alcmaeon]] and [[amphilochus]]); from Mantius came the seers Polypheides and Theoclymenus (*Odyssey* 15.249–256; see [[polypheides]] and [[theoclymenus]]). Nothing else is told of him, and the poem does not name his mother. W. W. Merry's table of the house sets him at the head of the elder line, Melampus, Antiphates, Oecles, Amphiaraus (*Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on *Odyssey* 11.281).

Homer gives the name to two other men: the Trojan whom Leonteus kills at the Achaean wall (*Iliad* 12.191; see [[antiphates]]) and the man-eating king of the Laestrygonians (*Odyssey* 10.106–132; see [[antiphates-the-laestrygonian]]).
