## Namesakes

The Iliad gives the name to a leader of the Cicones, whose likeness Apollo takes, and the verse that brings him in ends, as the Odyssey's does, with ἡγήτορι Μέντῃ (*Iliad* 17.73; *Odyssey* 1.105; see [[mentes]] and the translation's note on 1.105). In the Odyssey Athena takes the shape of two men of almost the same name: Mentes in the first book, and from the second on Mentor, the companion of Odysseus (*Odyssey* 2.225), whose likeness she takes “in form and in voice” (Μέντορι εἰδομένη ἠμὲν δέμας ἠδὲ καὶ αὐδήν, *Odyssey* 2.268, 2.401). The renderings of books other than the first are plain renderings, not the translation.

## Family

Son of Anchialus: Μέντης Ἀγχιάλοιο δαΐφρονος εὔχομαι εἶναι / υἱός, “I claim to be Mentes, son of wise-hearted Anchialus” (*Odyssey* 1.180–181; see [[anchialus-father-of-mentes]] and [[wise-hearted]]). His father loved Odysseus, and gave him the drug for his arrows that Ilus of Ephyra had refused (*Odyssey* 1.257–264; see [[odysseus-seeks-poison]]).

## Descriptions

- Ταφίων ἡγήτωρ, “the leader of the Taphians” (*Odyssey* 1.105), and in his own words Ταφίοισι φιληρέτμοισιν ἀνάσσω, “I am lord over the Taphians, lovers of the oar” (*Odyssey* 1.181, again 1.419; see [[taphians]], [[taphos]] and [[anax]]).
- ξεῖνος πατρώιος, a “guest-friend … from our fathers' time” (*Odyssey* 1.175–176, 1.187, 1.417; see [[xenia]]).

## Outside Homer

Strabo quotes Mentes' verses to show that Taphos was his, as the Cephallenians were subjects of Odysseus and Laertes (*Geography* 10.2.14). The poet, he says, puts the Taphians under Mentes and calls them pirates (*Geography* 10.2.20), and he does not say that they ruled the Acarnanians before Laertes and the Cephallenians came, only that they were friends of the Ithacans (*Geography* 10.2.24; see [[strabo]], [[taphos]] and [[taphians]]).
