ἦ καὶ Πείραιον προσεφώνεε, πιστὸν ἑταῖρον, “He spoke, and called to Peiraeus, his trusted companion” (*Odyssey* 15.539; see [[telemachus]] and [[trusted-companion]]): the son of Clytius, one of the companions who sailed with Telemachus to Pylos, and the one who “most of all” obeys him (*Odyssey* 15.540–541; see [[clytius-father-of-peiraeus]]). At the landing in Ithaca Telemachus gives the seer Theoclymenus into his care, and Peiraeus takes the ship on to the city (*Odyssey* 15.539–549; see [[theoclymenus]]).

## Descriptions

- πιστὸς ἑταῖρος, “trusted companion” (*Odyssey* 15.539; see [[trusted-companion]]).
- δουρικλυτός, “famed for his spear” (*Odyssey* 15.544; again 17.71), the Iliad's epithet of Idomeneus, Odysseus, Automedon, Meriones and Achilles (*Iliad* 5.45, 11.396, 16.472, 16.619, 21.233), given in the Odyssey to him alone (see [[famed-for-his-spear]]).

## Elsewhere in Homer

The companions bring the ship to the city and carry Menelaus' gifts “to the house of Clytius” (*Odyssey* 16.325–327, a plain rendering; see [[clytius-father-of-peiraeus]]). Telemachus, back in the city, goes to the place of assembly to fetch the stranger, and Peiraeus, “famed for his spear”, brings him there; he asks Telemachus to send women for the gifts, and Telemachus bids him keep them: if the suitors kill him secretly and divide his father's goods, he would rather Peiraeus had them than any of these, and if he plants slaughter for the suitors, let Peiraeus bring them to his house (*Odyssey* 17.52–84, a plain rendering). When the suitors laugh at the seer's vision, [[theoclymenus|Theoclymenus]] leaves the hall and goes to Peiraeus, “who received him gladly” (*Odyssey* 20.371–372, a plain rendering).

## The name

Homer gives the name to one other man, the grandfather of Agamemnon's attendant Eurymedon, named in the patronymic of his son Ptolemaeus, “son of Peiraeus” (Πειραΐδαο, *Iliad* 4.228; see [[peiraeus]]). The harbor of Athens is Πειραιεύς (see [[piraeus]]).
