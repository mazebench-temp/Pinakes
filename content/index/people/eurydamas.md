He is an ὀνειροπόλος, “interpreter of dreams” (5.149), one of the three kinds of expert Achilles names in the first assembly (1.62–63; see [[onar]] and [[divination]]).

The line about him can be read two ways. τοῖς οὐκ ἐρχομένοις ὃ γέρων ἐκρίνατʼ ὀνείρους (5.150) is rendered “the old man read no dreams for them when they set out”; others join the negative to the participle, “for them, who were not to come home, the old man read dreams”. Either way his art did not save his sons. Nor could Merops of Percote, who “knew the art of prophecy”, keep his sons from the war (2.831–834; see [[the-sons-of-merops]]), and the Corinthian seer Polyidus foretold his own son's death (13.663–670). See [[bereaved-parents]].

Εὐρυδάμας joins εὐρύς (“wide”) and δαμάω (“subdue”). One of Penelope's suitors has the same name (Od. 18.297, 22.283).
