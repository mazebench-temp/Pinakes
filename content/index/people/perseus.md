Zeus, listing his loves, names “Danae of the lovely ankles, daughter of Acrisius, who bore Perseus, the most distinguished of all men” (*Iliad* 14.319–320). Acrisius of Argos, warned that his daughter's son would kill him, shut Danae in a bronze chamber, where Zeus came to her as a shower of gold; he set mother and child adrift in a chest, which came ashore on Seriphos. There King Polydectes sent the youth to fetch the Gorgon's head. With the help of Hermes and Athena, Perseus forced the Graeae, the daughters of Phorcys, to show him the way, took winged sandals, a pouch and the cap of Hades from the nymphs, beheaded Medusa, and on his way home rescued Andromeda from the sea monster. Later he killed Acrisius by accident with a discus (Apollodorus 2.4.1–4; see [[perseus-and-the-graeae]], [[gorgon]] and [[discus]]).
