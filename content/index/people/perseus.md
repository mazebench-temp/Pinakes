Zeus, listing his loves, names “Danae of the lovely ankles, daughter of Acrisius, / who bore Perseus, preeminent among all men” (14.319–320; see [[danae]], [[acrisius]] and [[the-loves-of-zeus]]). It is all that the Iliad tells of him. His line appears once more, in Book 19, where Eurystheus, born early by Hera's contrivance to rule the Argives, is “child of Sthenelus the son of Perseus”, of the blood of Zeus (19.116, 19.123; see [[eurystheus]], [[sthenelus-son-of-perseus]] and [[the-birth-of-heracles]]). The Odyssey's Perseus, a son of Nestor, is another man (*Odyssey* 3.414, 3.444).

## Outside Homer

Acrisius of Argos, warned that his daughter's son would kill him, shut Danae in a bronze chamber, where Zeus came to her as a shower of gold; he set mother and child adrift in a chest, which came ashore on Seriphos. There King Polydectes sent the youth to fetch the Gorgon's head. With the help of Hermes and Athena, Perseus forced the Graeae, the daughters of Phorcys, to show him the way, took winged sandals, a pouch and the cap of Hades from the nymphs, beheaded Medusa, and on his way home rescued Andromeda from the sea monster. Later he killed Acrisius by accident with a discus (Apollodorus 2.4.1–4; see [[perseus-and-the-graeae]], [[gorgon]] and [[discus]]).

The Hesiodic *Shield* shows him in gold on the shield of Heracles, “the son of lovely-haired Danae, the horseman Perseus”, in winged sandals, with the Gorgon's head on his back and the cap of Hades on his head, flying from the Gorgons (*Shield* 216–237). Pindar calls him “the son of Danae, who, we say, was born of gold that flowed of its own accord” (*Pythian* 12.17–18). Herodotus makes his son by Andromeda, Perses, the ancestor from whom the Persians took their name (7.61.3), and says that the Greeks trace the Dorian kings back to him, “Perseus, son of Danae”, but no further, since he had no mortal father (6.53.1–2; see [[herodotus]]). In Apollodorus his sons by Andromeda include Electryon, the father of Alcmene, and Sthenelus, the father of Eurystheus (*Library* 2.4.5; see [[alcmene]]).
