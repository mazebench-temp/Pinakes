# Μήστορα (24.257); the common noun μήστωρ "counselor" has ω in the accusative (μήστωρα, 6.97, 12.39).
grc: [Μήστορα]
relations:
  - father: priam (24.255-257)
---

Μήστορά τʼ ἀντίθεον, “godlike Mestor” (24.257). Priam, rebuking the nine sons who are left, names three of the best whom Ares has destroyed, Mestor, Troilus and Hector (24.255–260; see [[sons-of-priam]], [[troilus]] and [[hector]]). Walter Leaf notes that Mestor appears only here (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 24.257). ἀντίθεος, “godlike”, is an epithet of heroes (see [[antitheos]]). The name is the noun μήστωρ, “counselor, deviser”, which the poem gives to his father, θεόφιν μήστωρ ἀτάλαντος, “a counselor equal to the gods” (7.366; see [[counselor-equal-to-the-gods]]), and to Hector, κρατερὸν μήστωρα φόβοιο, “the mighty master of rout” (12.39; see [[master-of-rout]]).

## Outside Homer

In Apollodorus he is one of the sons whom Priam had by women other than Hecuba (*Library* 3.12.5), and Achilles kills him with the herdsmen on Ida when he drives off the cattle of Aeneas (*Epitome* 3.32; see [[apollodorus]] and [[achilles-raids]]). The Iliad tells that raid without naming him: Aeneas remembers how Achilles came upon his cattle on Ida and drove him to Lyrnessus, which Achilles sacked (20.89–96, 20.187–194; see [[aeneas]]).
