Two of his sons, [[Archelochus]] and [[Acamas]], serve under [[Aeneas]] with the Dardanians. Antenor himself is too old to fight. In Book 3 he sits with Priam and the other elders on the wall, and remembers entertaining Odysseus and Menelaus when they came to Troy as envoys to ask for Helen; he compares the two men as speakers (3.146–148, 3.203–224). In Book 7 he proposes giving back Helen and all her treasure, and Paris refuses (7.347–364). His wife Theano is the priestess of Athena at Troy (6.297–300). Many of his sons appear in the Iliad, and most of them are killed.

## Outside Homer

Later tradition spared him at the sack. Livy says the Achaeans held back from Aeneas and Antenor because of old ties of hospitality and because both had always urged peace and the return of Helen (1.1.1). Antenor then led the Eneti, who had lost their king [[Pylaemenes]] at Troy, to the head of the Adriatic (1.1.2–3). Virgil makes him the founder of Patavium, modern Padua (*Aeneid* 1.242–249).
