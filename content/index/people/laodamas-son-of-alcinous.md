## Descriptions

- ἀγαπήνωρ, “manly” (*Odyssey* 7.170), an epithet of warriors in the Iliad, given in the Odyssey to him alone (see [[manly]]).
- ἀμύμων, “noble” (*Odyssey* 8.117; see [[blameless]]), and ἀγαθὸς πάϊς Ἀλκινόοιο, “the good son of Alcinous” (*Odyssey* 8.130, 8.143).
- The measure of beauty among the young Phaeacians: Euryalus is “the best in looks and form / of all the Phaeacians after noble Laodamas” (*Odyssey* 8.116–117; see [[euryalus-the-phaeacian]]).

He is one of the five sons of Alcinous, two of them married and three still unmarried (*Odyssey* 6.62–63), and he stands up for the games with two of his brothers, Halius and Clytoneus (*Odyssey* 8.118–119): probably the three unmarried ones, W. W. Merry suggests (*Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on *Odyssey* 8.204; see [[alcinous]], [[halius-son-of-alcinous]], [[clytoneus]] and [[nausicaa]]). His name, which joins λαός, “people”, and the stem of δάμνημι, “tame”, is one of the few Phaeacian names not taken from seafaring life, Merry observes, with those of Alcinous, Arete, Polybus and Rhexenor (on *Odyssey* 8.111). He shares his name with a Trojan, the son of Antenor whom Ajax kills at the ships (*Iliad* 15.516–517; see [[laodamas]]).
