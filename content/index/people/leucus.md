Λεῦκον Ὀδυσσέος ἐσθλὸν ἑταῖρον, “Leucus, the brave companion of Odysseus” (4.491). He is a ἑταῖρος ([[hetairos]]), one of the men who follow a leader and fight beside him. He is named only here, with a leader and no more; the name means “white, bright” (λευκός).

He is killed by chance. Antiphus' spear was meant for Ajax, who had just stripped Simoeisius. It hits Leucus in the groin (βουβών) as he drags a body away, most likely Simoeisius' own; he falls over it, and it drops from his hand.

His death brings Odysseus into the fighting. Odysseus goes through the front ranks, throws, and kills Democoon, a bastard son of Priam, with a spear through both temples (4.494–504; see [[democoon]] and [[odysseus]]). The poem says twice that he did it for his companion: he was “very angry at heart over his killing” (τοῦ … ἀποκταμένοιο χολώθη, 4.494), and he struck “in his anger for his companion” (ἑτάροιο χολωσάμενος, 4.501). The pattern of a companion's death and a leader's revenge returns through the battles of the Iliad, and on the largest scale in Achilles' revenge for Patroclus.
