Polyidus and his brother Abas are killed and stripped in one line (5.151); the lines around them are about their father (see [[eurydamas]]). Πολύειδος joins πολύς (“much”) and εἰδέναι (“know”): “knowing much”. It suits the son of a man who read dreams, and it is the name of a seer elsewhere in the Iliad: the Corinthian Polyidus, who told his son Euchenor that he would die of sickness at home or be killed by the Trojans at the ships, and whose son sailed to Troy knowing it (13.663–672).
