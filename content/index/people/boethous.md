Βοηθοΐδη Ἐτεωνεῦ, “Eteoneus, son of Boethous” (*Odyssey* 4.31): Menelaus names his attendant with his father's name when he rebukes him for asking whether to receive the strangers, and the poem names him so again when he comes to serve at Telemachus' departure (*Odyssey* 15.95, 15.140; see [[eteoneus]] and [[menelaus]]). Nothing else is said of the father. The name is the adjective βοηθόος, from βοή, “battle cry”, and θέω, “run”, which the Iliad uses of Aeneas coming on “to help” and of a chariot “swift to the rescue” (*Iliad* 13.477, 17.481).
