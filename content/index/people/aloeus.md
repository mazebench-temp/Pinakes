παῖδες Ἀλωῆος, “the sons of Aloeus” (5.386). Homer names him only through his sons and his wives. Dione calls Eeriboea their μητρυιή, “stepmother” (5.389), so she was his wife but not their mother (see [[eeriboea]]). The Odyssey names their mother, Iphimedeia, “the wife of Aloeus” (Ἀλωῆος παράκοιτιν, Od. 11.305), who claimed that she had lain with Poseidon. After him the brothers were later called the Aloadae. The name looks like a derivative of ἀλωή, “threshing floor” or “orchard” (5.90, 5.499), but no story explains it.

## Outside Homer

[[apollodorus|Apollodorus]] makes Aloeus a son of Poseidon and Canace, daughter of Aeolus, and the husband of Iphimedeia, daughter of Triops. Iphimedeia fell in love with Poseidon and bore him the two giants (*Library* 1.7.4).
