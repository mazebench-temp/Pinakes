## The name

The name is the adjective ἴφθιμος, “mighty”, which Merry refers to ἶφι, “with might” (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 4.365). The poems give it to heroes and to noble women: to the wife of Diomedes (*Iliad* 5.415), to Pero (*Odyssey* 11.287), and twice to Penelope herself, ἰφθίμη βασίλεια and ἰφθίμη παράκοιτις (*Odyssey* 16.332, 23.92). Aristarchus was in doubt whether ἰφθίμη at *Odyssey* 4.797 is an epithet or a proper name (Merry's critical note on *Odyssey* 4.797; see the translation's note on *Odyssey* 4.797).

## Family

Ἰφθίμῃ, κούρῃ μεγαλήτορος Ἰκαρίοιο, / τὴν Εὔμηλος ὄπυιε Φερῇς ἔνι οἰκία ναίων, “Iphthime, daughter of great-hearted Icarius, / whom Eumelus had married, who had his home in Pherae” (*Odyssey* 4.797–798; see [[icarius]] and [[pherae]]). Her husband is the son of Admetus who led eleven ships from Pherae and Iolcus to Troy, and whose mares were the best there (*Iliad* 2.711–715, 2.763–767; see [[eumelus]]). Penelope calls her κασιγνήτη, “sister” (*Odyssey* 4.810; see [[penelope]]). The poem names no other child of Icarius here; Penelope's brothers are mentioned elsewhere (*Odyssey* 15.16–17).
