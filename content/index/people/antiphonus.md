Πάμμονά τʼ Ἀντίφονόν τε, “and Pammon and Antiphonus” (24.250): he is named only in the list of nine sons whom Priam rebukes as he makes ready to ransom Hector (24.248–264; see [[sons-of-priam]] and [[priam]]). The name joins ἀντί, “against, in return”, and φόνος, “slaughter”. Antiphus (Ἄντιφος), the son of Priam whom Agamemnon killed in one chariot with his half-brother Isus, is another man (11.101–109; see [[antiphus-son-of-priam]] and [[isus]]).

## Outside Homer

Apollodorus' list of Priam's sons does not name him; it has an Antiphus among the sons of Hecuba (*Library* 3.12.5; see [[apollodorus]]).
