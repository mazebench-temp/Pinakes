Οἰνοπίδην θʼ Ἕλενον, “and Helenus, son of Oenops” (5.707). He is the only one of the six given a father (see [[oenops]]), and he is named only here. The poem does not say whether Hector or Ares killed him (see [[hector]] and [[ares]]).

The Trojan Helenus, son of Priam and “by far the best of the bird-seers”, comes forward in Book 6 (6.76; see [[best-of-bird-seers]]).
