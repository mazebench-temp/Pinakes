ἀντίθεον θεράποντα, “the godlike attendant” (11.322): a θεράπων is the companion who serves a warrior, drives or holds his horses, and fights at his side, as Patroclus serves Achilles (see [[hetairos]] and [[antitheos]]). Diomedes and Odysseus kill the lord and his man together, as Diomedes killed Axylus and his charioteer (6.12–19; see [[axylus]]). Molion is named nowhere else. His name is the singular of the Moliones, Μολίονε, the twins whom Nestor would have killed in his youth if their father Poseidon had not saved them (11.709–710, 11.750–752), but the poem does not connect them.
