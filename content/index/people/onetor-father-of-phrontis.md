Ὀνητορίδης = Ὀνήτωρ + the patronymic suffix -ίδης, “son of Onetor”. The Odyssey names the father only in the verse that names the son, Φρόντιν Ὀνητορίδην, “Phrontis, son of Onetor” (*Odyssey* 3.282; see [[phrontis-son-of-onetor]]), and says nothing else of him.

The Iliad's Onetor is a Trojan, the priest of Zeus of Ida, whose son Laogonus Meriones kills, “Laogonus, the bold son of Onetor, who was the priest of Zeus / of Ida” (*Iliad* 16.604–605; see [[onetor]] and [[laogonus]]). Nothing joins the two.
