Dionysius rose as a popular speaker during the Carthaginian invasion of Sicily, accusing the generals and the rich, and was made sole general; in 405 BCE he claimed that his life had been attempted and was given a bodyguard, with which he made himself master of Syracuse (Diodorus 13.91–96). The *Politics* names him among the tyrants who rose by accusing the rich (5.5, 1305a). He ruled until 367, fought four wars against Carthage, and wrote tragedies; one won the prize at the Lenaea in Athens shortly before his death (Diodorus 15.74). His brother-in-law [[dion-of-syracuse]] later drove out his son, Dionysius the Younger.
