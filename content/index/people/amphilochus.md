τοῦ δʼ υἱεῖς ἐγένοντʼ Ἀλκμαίων Ἀμφίλοχός τε, “His sons were Alcmaeon and Amphilochus” (*Odyssey* 15.248): Homer names him once, with his brother, at the end of the elder line of Melampus in the pedigree of the seer Theoclymenus, after the verse that tells how their father perished at Thebes “for the sake of gifts to a woman” (*Odyssey* 15.247; see [[amphiaraus]], [[alcmaeon]], [[eriphyle]], [[melampus]] and [[theoclymenus]]). The poem tells nothing else of him.

## Outside Homer

**A suitor of Helen.** In the Hesiodic *Catalogue of Women* the two suitors of Helen from Argos, close at hand, are “the two sons of lord Amphiaraus, son of Oicles” (fr. 68 Evelyn-White, a papyrus text in part restored; see [[hesiod]], [[helen]] and [[oicles]]).

**After Troy.** It was said, Strabo reports, that the seer Calchas came on foot with “Amphilochus, the son of Amphiaraus” to Clarus near Colophon on the way home from Troy, and died of grief there when he met a better seer than himself, Mopsus, the son of Teiresias' daughter Manto; Hesiod told how Calchas asked Mopsus the number of the figs on a wild fig tree, and Mopsus answered rightly (*Geography* 14.1.27; Hesiod, *Melampodia* fr. 1 Evelyn-White; see [[calchas]] and [[strabo]]). Herodotus says that the Pamphylians are descended from those “scattered from Troy with Amphilochus and Calchas”, and that Amphilochus, son of Amphiaraus, founded Posideium, on the border of the Cilicians and the Syrians (Herodotus 7.91.1, 3.91.1; see [[herodotus]]). In Strabo, Amphilochus and Mopsus, back from Troy, founded Mallus in Cilicia; Amphilochus went away to Argos, came back displeased with what he found there, and, shut out of the partnership, fought Mopsus in single combat; both fell, and their tombs were shown near Magarsa by the Pyramus, out of sight of each other. Alexander made offerings to him “because of their kinship from Argos”. Hesiod said that Apollo killed Amphilochus at Soli; others put his death in the Aleian plain, others in Syria (*Geography* 14.5.16–17; Hesiod, *Melampodia* fr. 8 Evelyn-White). Cicero names Amphilochus and Mopsus as kings of the Argives who were also augurs and founded Greek cities on the coast of Cilicia (*On Divination* 1.88; see [[cicero]]).

**Amphilochian Argos.** The Argos of the Amphilochians, next to Ambracia, bore his name. Ephorus, as Strabo reports him, made it a foundation of Alcmaeon, who stayed in Acarnania when Diomedes went to Troy and named the city after his brother; Thucydides made Amphilochus himself the founder, who, displeased with things at Argos when he came back from Troy, went to Acarnania, succeeded to his brother's power and founded the city that bears his name (Strabo, *Geography* 7.7.7, 10.2.26).
