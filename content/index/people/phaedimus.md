## The name

The name is the epithet φαίδιμος, “shining, glorious”, which the poems give to Hector, Ajax and other heroes (see [[phaidimos]]). The scholia say that it is uncertain whether φαίδιμος is a proper name here (the critical note of Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 4.617; see the translation's note on *Odyssey* 4.617); if it is not, the giver of the bowl is a “shining hero, the king of the Sidonians”, and has no name.

## Descriptions

- ἥρως, “the hero” (*Odyssey* 4.617; see [[heros]]).
- Σιδονίων βασιλεύς, “the king of the Sidonians” (*Odyssey* 4.618; see [[sidonians]]).

## Outside Homer

Strabo quotes the five verses (*Odyssey* 4.615–619) to show the fine workmanship of the Sidonians and Menelaus' long stay among them, beside the Iliad's silver bowl that Sidonians wrought and Phoenicians brought over the sea (*Iliad* 23.741–745), and he takes “the work of Hephaestus” as praise in excess, as fine things are called the works of Athena, of the Graces or of the Muses (*Geography* 1.2.33; see [[strabo]]).
