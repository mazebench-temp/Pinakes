**In place of Hector.** Teucer, who has killed eight men from under the shield of Ajax, shoots “straight at Hector, and his heart was eager to hit him” (8.300–301; see [[teucer]]). The arrow misses, in the half-verse that told how Diomedes' spear missed Hector and killed his charioteer Eniopeus (καὶ τοῦ μέν ῥʼ ἀφάμαρτ-, 8.119, 8.302; see [[eniopeus]]), and Gorgythion dies in his brother's place.

**His mother.** The narrator stops to name her: “lovely Castianeira, like the goddesses in form”, whom Priam had married and brought from Aesyme (8.304–305; see [[castianeira]] and [[aesyme]]). He was not Hecuba's son, then, but one of the sons whom, as Priam says, “women in my halls” bore him (24.497; see [[sons-of-priam]]). His epithets are ἀμύμων, “noble”, and ἐΰς, “brave” (8.302–303; see [[blameless]]).

**The poppy.** His head droops to one side, weighed down by his helmet, as a poppy in a garden droops, heavy with its seed and with the showers of spring (8.306–308; see [[the-poppy]]). No other death in Book 8 is given a simile. He is named nowhere else in Homer.
