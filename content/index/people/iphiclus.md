πολυμήλου, "rich in flocks" (see [[rich-in-flocks]]). Φυλακίδης, "son of Phylacus", is his patronymic.

In the Odyssey the seer Melampus tried to drive off the cattle of "mighty Iphiclus" from Phylace, to win Neleus' daughter Pero for his brother. He was held prisoner for a year, and Iphiclus set him free when he had told him the oracles (11.287–297, 15.225–238). Nestor remembers outrunning an Iphiclus at the funeral games of Amarynceus (23.636). In a fragment of the Hesiodic *Catalogue of Women*, Iphiclus runs so lightly over the ears of grain that he does not break them.
