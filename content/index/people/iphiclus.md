πολυμήλου, "rich in flocks" (see [[rich-in-flocks]]). Φυλακίδης, "son of Phylacus", is his patronymic.

{{notes}}

## Elsewhere in Homer

In the Odyssey the seer Melampus tried to drive off the cattle of "mighty Iphiclus" from Phylace, to win Neleus' daughter Pero for his brother. He was held prisoner for a year, and Iphiclus set him free when he had told him the oracles (*Odyssey* 11.287–297, 15.225–238). The Iphiclus whom Nestor remembers outrunning at the funeral games of Amarynceus (23.636) is probably the same man, though Homer does not say so (see the note on *Iliad* 23 and [[the-funeral-games-of-amarynceus]]).

## Outside Homer

A scholium on *Odyssey* 11.326 makes him the son of Phylacus by Clymene, “a swift-footed boy”, who was said to race the winds and to run over the ears of grain, and quotes the story from Hesiod: “he ran over the topmost fruit of the stalks without breaking them” (ἄκρον ἐπʼ ἀνθερίκων καρπὸν θέεν οὐδὲ κατέκλα, Hesiod fr. 84 Evelyn-White, from the *Catalogue of Women*; see [[hesiod]]).
