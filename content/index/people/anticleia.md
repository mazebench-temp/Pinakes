## The name

Ἀντίκλεια joins ἀντι-, “against, matching”, and κλέος, “fame”: the man's name Ἄντικλος is formed in the same way, that of the Achaean in the wooden horse who alone wanted to answer Helen's voice until Odysseus pressed his mouth shut (*Odyssey* 4.285–288).

## Family

Αὐτολύκου θυγάτηρ μεγαλήτορος Ἀντίκλεια, “Anticleia, daughter of great-hearted Autolycus” (*Odyssey* 11.85; see [[autolycus]] and [[great-hearted]]): her father is the grandfather who gave Odysseus his name, and her mother is Amphithea, who embraced the boy when he came to Parnassus (*Odyssey* 19.399–409, 19.416, Greek only). She is the wife of Laertes, whom Autolycus calls his son-in-law when he names her child, γαμβρὸς ἐμὸς θυγάτηρ τε, “my son-in-law and my daughter” (*Odyssey* 19.406, a plain rendering; see [[laertes]]), and the mother of Odysseus (*Odyssey* 11.84–86; see [[odysseus]]) and of a daughter, Ctimene, “whom she bore the youngest of her children” and brought up together with the swineherd Eumaeus until Ctimene was given in marriage to Same (*Odyssey* 15.363–367; see [[ctimene]] and [[eumaeus]]).

## Descriptions

- πότνια μήτηρ, “my lady mother”, in her son's tale (*Odyssey* 11.180, 11.215; see [[queenly]]), and μητρὸς κατατεθνηυίης, “my dead mother” (*Odyssey* 11.84, 11.141, 11.205).
- κεδνὴ ἄλοχος, Laertes' “devoted wife”, whose anger he shunned (*Odyssey* 1.432–433; see [[devoted]]), and κουριδίη ἄλοχος δαΐφρων, his “wise-hearted wedded wife”, in the swineherd's mouth (*Odyssey* 15.356; see [[wedded]] and [[wise-hearted]]).

## Elsewhere in Homer

She died while Odysseus was away. Her shade tells him in the land of the dead that longing for him took her life (*Odyssey* 11.202–203), and the swineherd, who was reared in her house, tells the disguised Odysseus the same: she “died of grief for her glorious son”, and her death brought Laertes to old age before its time (*Odyssey* 15.355–360; see [[laertes]] and [[eumaeus]]).

## Outside Homer

W. W. Merry reports a later story in which she hanged herself when Nauplius falsely reported that Odysseus was dead, referring to Hyginus, *Fabulae* 243 (*Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on *Odyssey* 11.85). Hyginus' own summary of the Odyssey has Ulysses speak in the underworld with his mother Anticlia “about the end of his wandering”, *de fine errationis suae* (*Fabulae* 125), the subject of Teiresias' prophecy in Homer. For the story that Sisyphus lay with her before she married Laertes, see [[autolycus]].
