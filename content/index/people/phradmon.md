Φραδμονίδην Ἀγέλαον, “Agelaus, son of Phradmon” (8.257), is the man Diomedes kills first when the Achaeans come out across the ditch (see [[agelaus]] and [[diomedes]]). Homer says nothing else of the father.

The name is also an adjective, φράδμων, “discerning, shrewd”, from φράζομαι, “consider, perceive”. No man, however discerning (φράδμων περ ἀνήρ), could have recognized Sarpedon's body under the missiles, the blood and the dust (16.638–640), and Agamemnon wishes for ten such counselors (συμφράδμονες) as Nestor among the Achaeans (2.372).
