## Family

“And after her I saw Antiope, the daughter of Asopus, / who claimed that she had slept even in the arms of Zeus, / and she bore two sons, Amphion and Zethus” (*Odyssey* 11.260–262; see [[asopus]], [[zeus]], [[amphion-of-thebes]] and [[zethus]]). Her father is the river of southern Boeotia, which flows between Thebes and Plataea, and her sons are Thebes' first founders, so that W. W. Merry counts her story among the Boeotian legends of the catalogue (*Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on *Odyssey* 11.260; see [[thebes]]).

## Outside Homer

Later writers make her the daughter of Nycteus: Strabo names Hyria, a foundation of “Nycteus, the father of Antiope” (*Geography* 9.2.12; see [[strabo]]). Merry reports the later forms of her legend, with Lycus, Dirce and Nycteus, in which her sons build the lower city of Thebes at the foot of the Cadmeia (on *Odyssey* 11.260). Euripides made her the heroine of his *Antiope*, now lost; what is best remembered of it is the debate of her sons, Zethus the herdsman and Amphion the musician, which Plato's Callicles takes up against Socrates (Plato, *Gorgias* 485e–486c, 506b; see [[plato]] and [[euripides]]).
