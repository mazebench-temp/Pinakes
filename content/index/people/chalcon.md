Βαθυκλῆα μεγάθυμον / Χάλκωνος φίλον υἱόν, “great-hearted Bathycles, / the dear son of Chalcon” (16.594–595; see [[bathycles]]). Chalcon is known only as a father. The relative clause that follows, ὃς Ἑλλάδι οἰκία ναίων / ὄλβῳ τε πλούτῳ τε μετέπρεπε Μυρμιδόνεσσι, “who had his home in Hellas / and stood out among the Myrmidons for prosperity and wealth” (16.595–596), comes after the son's title and is read here of the son, who fights among the Myrmidons at Troy; the Greek would also allow it to describe the father (see [[hellas]] and [[myrmidons]]).

**The name.** Χάλκων is formed from χαλκός, “bronze”.
