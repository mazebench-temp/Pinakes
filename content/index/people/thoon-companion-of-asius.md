Θόωνά τε, “and Thoon” (12.140): the fourth of the five men named around Asius when his company rushes the gate on the left of the ships (see [[asius]] and [[the-gates-of-the-wall]]). Book 12 gives him nothing more.

In Book 13 Antilochus watches a Thoon as he turns away, leaps on him and cuts the whole vein that runs up the back to the neck; he falls on his back in the dust, stretching out both hands to his companions (13.545–549; see [[antilochus]]). The poem does not say that this is Asius' companion, but Asius' other men die in the same fighting: Iamenus and Orestes at the gate (12.193), and Adamas and an Oenomaus at the ships (13.506, 13.567; see [[iamenus]], [[orestes-the-trojan]], [[adamas]] and [[oenomaus-the-trojan]]).

Homer has two other Trojans of the name, the son of Phaenops whom Diomedes kills (5.152; see [[thoon-son-of-phaenops]]) and the man Odysseus kills (11.422; see [[thoon]]), and a Phaeacian Thoon among the young men at the games (Od. 8.113). The name seems to be formed from θοός, “swift”.
