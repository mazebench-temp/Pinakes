**Taken alive.** ζωὸν ἕλε βλαφθέντα κατὰ κλόνον: the son of Oileus “took him alive, caught in the turmoil” (16.331; see [[ajax-son-of-oileus]]). βλάπτω is to hamper or entangle; the word is used of the horses of Adrastus, caught in a tamarisk branch, before Menelaus took him alive too (βλαφθέντε, 6.39; see [[adrastus-the-trojan]]). The capture lasts half a verse: “but there and then / he loosed his strength, striking him on the neck with his hilted sword” (16.331–332; see [[sword]] and [[hilted]]). Adrastus at least had time to clasp his captor's knees and offer a ransom (6.45–50; see [[supplication]]); Cleobulus is given no words. He is the sixth of the nine men whom the Achaean leaders kill one by one when the Trojans are driven from the ships (16.306–351; see [[areilycus-killed-by-patroclus]]).

**The death.** “and the whole sword grew warm with blood; and his eyes / were seized by purple death and mighty fate” (16.333–334; see [[purple-death]]). The two verses return together when Achilles kills Echeclus, son of Agenor, with his sword (20.476–477), and the second ends the death of Hypsenor, whose arm Eurypylus cut off with a sword (5.83; see [[hypsenor]]).

**The name.** Κλεόβουλος joins κλέος, “fame”, and βουλή, “counsel”. Its best-known bearer is Cleobulus of Lindus, whom Plato's Socrates names among the seven wise men (*Protagoras* 343a; see [[plato]]).
