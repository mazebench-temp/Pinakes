Melampus, settled in Argos, “begot Antiphates and Mantius, two mighty sons” (*Odyssey* 15.242; see [[melampus]] and [[antiphates-son-of-melampus]]), and “Mantius begot Polypheides and Cleitus” (*Odyssey* 15.249): Cleitus was carried off by Dawn for his beauty, and Apollo made Polypheides the best of seers after Amphiaraus died; Polypheides moved to Hyperesia, “angry with his father”, and his son is the seer Theoclymenus (*Odyssey* 15.250–256; see [[cleitus-son-of-mantius]], [[polypheides]] and [[theoclymenus]]). The poem tells nothing of Mantius himself, nor why his son was angry with him.

## The name

Μάντιος has the stem of μάντις, “seer”, and of μαντεύομαι, “prophesy”: the name of a son of the seer Melampus and the father of a seer, in a house of seers (*Odyssey* 15.225, 15.252–255; see [[divination]]). μάντιος with a small letter, “of a seer”, is the genitive of the common word, of the seer Polyidus, father of Euchenor (*Iliad* 13.663; see [[polyidus-the-seer]]).
