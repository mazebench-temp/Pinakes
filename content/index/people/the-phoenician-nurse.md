ἔσκε δὲ πατρὸς ἐμοῖο γυνὴ Φοίνισσʼ ἐνὶ οἴκῳ, / καλή τε μεγάλη τε καὶ ἀγλαὰ ἔργα ἰδυῖα, “And there was in my father's house a Phoenician woman, / beautiful and tall and skilled in splendid handiwork” (*Odyssey* 15.417–418; see [[phoenicians]] and [[ctesius]]): the swineherd Eumaeus tells of her in his own story and does not name her. She was the daughter of Arybas, a rich man of Sidon, and Taphian pirates had seized her as she came from the fields and sold her to Eumaeus' father (*Odyssey* 15.425–429; see [[arybas]], [[sidon]], [[taphians]] and [[piracy]]). She was rearing the king's young son, and when Phoenician traders came to the island she lay with one of them and agreed to sail home with them, bringing gold and the child as her passage money (*Odyssey* 15.419–453; see [[eumaeus]]). On the seventh day of the voyage Artemis struck her dead, and the traders threw her body overboard (*Odyssey* 15.477–481; see [[artemis]] and [[the-kidnapping-of-eumaeus]]).

## Descriptions

- καλή τε μεγάλη τε καὶ ἀγλαὰ ἔργα ἰδυῖα, “beautiful and tall and skilled in splendid handiwork” (*Odyssey* 15.418): the words of the woman whose form Athena takes when she makes herself known to Odysseus on Ithaca, and again when she comes to the swineherd's hut (*Odyssey* 13.288–289, 16.157–158; see [[athena]]).
- εὐεργός, “one who does good” (*Odyssey* 15.422), in the verse of “the womenfolk” whose minds love deceives (see [[womenfolk]]).
