Ποδῆς υἱὸς Ἠετίωνος, “Podes, son of Eetion” (17.575), and again in Apollo's taunt to Hector, Ποδῆν υἱὸν Ἠετίωνος (17.590): the poem names him only in his son's name. Podes was rich and brave, and Hector honored him most of all the people as his dear companion at the feast (17.575–577; see [[podes]]).

The poem does not say which Eetion he is. He can hardly be Eetion of Thebe, the father of Andromache, for Andromache tells Hector that “the seven brothers I had in our halls / all went down on a single day into the house of Hades” at the hands of Achilles (6.421–423; see [[eetion]] and [[andromache]]). So Walter Leaf: “This Eëtion can hardly be the father of Andromache, as her brothers are dead” (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 17.575). A third Eetion, a guest-friend from Imbros, ransoms Priam's son Lycaon from Lemnos and sends him to Arisbe (Ἴμβριος Ἠετίων, 21.42–44; see [[eetion-of-imbros]] and [[lycaon-son-of-priam]]).
