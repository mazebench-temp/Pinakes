He is βουληφόρος, “a counselor”, as the men of Hector's council are βουληφόροι (10.414), and Rhesus' ἀνεψιός, “cousin” (see [[rhesus]] and [[thracians]]). Apollo wakes him too late. He sees the empty place “where the swift horses had stood” and the men gasping in the slaughter, groans aloud, and calls his dear companion by name, presumably the dead king (10.519–522; see [[apollo]] and [[the-horses-of-rhesus]]). Then the Trojans rush together in an untold tumult (10.523–525; see [[trojans]]).

The ending of Democoon (4.499) and Deicoon (5.534) has been explained from κοέω, “mark, perceive”; here it is joined to ἵππος, “horse”. The name suits the man who wakes to find the horses gone.

## Outside Homer

The *Rhesus* gives his part to Rhesus' wounded charioteer (*Rhesus* 728–803; see [[euripides-rhesus]]). Later tradition knows other men of the name: a king of Sparta killed with his sons by Heracles (Apollodorus 3.10.5), and an archer in the games of the *Aeneid* (Virgil, *Aeneid* 5.492).
