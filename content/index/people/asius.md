ὄρχαμος ἀνδρῶν, "leader of men" ([[leader-of-men]]). His name comes twice, at the end of one line and the start of the next (Ἄσιος … / Ἄσιος Ὑρτακίδης), and his are the only horses the Trojan catalogue describes: αἴθωνες μεγάλοι, "great and tawny". αἴθων means "blazing, burning", used of the sheen of a coat.

The horses are his undoing. In Book 12, when the Trojans leave their chariots at the ditch, Asius alone refuses, "the fool", and drives at the Achaean wall; the gate is held by two Lapiths, and he fails (12.108–174). The Catalogue's lines about his horses return there (12.96–97). In Book 13 he comes on foot in front of his horses to attack Idomeneus, who spears him in the throat. He falls "as an oak falls, or a poplar, or a tall pine" (13.384–393). A different Asius, son of Dymas and brother of Hecuba, appears at 16.715–719.
