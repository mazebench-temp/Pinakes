Ἄλκανδρος = ἀλκή (“strength, defense”; see [[alke]]) + ἀνήρ (“man”). He is named only in Odysseus' list of seven Lycians (5.677–678; see [[lycians]] and [[odysseus]]). Like most of the seven, he has a name made of ordinary Greek words, and no father, home or wound.

## Outside Homer

Ovid's Ulysses names all seven Lycians among the men he has killed, in Homer's order (*Metamorphoses* 13; see [[ovid]]).
