**Man killed man.** The Trojans have been driven from the burning ship, but they still stand against the Danaans and give way only “under compulsion” (16.305). Then “man killed man, as the battle scattered, / among the leaders” (16.306–307): nine Achaean leaders each kill a man, and the list closes on its own count, “These leaders of the Danaans each killed his man” (16.351; see [[androktasia]]). Patroclus, whose spear has just killed Pyraechmes, the leader of the Paeonians (16.284–292; see [[pyraechmes]]), comes first. He catches Areilycus as he turns (στρεφθέντος) and strikes him on the thigh; the bronze goes straight through and breaks the bone, and he falls face down (16.308–311; see [[patroclus]]). Then Menelaus kills Thoas, Meges Amphiclus, Antilochus Atymnius, Thrasymedes Atymnius' brother Maris, the son of Oileus Cleobulus, Peneleos Lycon, Meriones Acamas and Idomeneus Erymas (16.311–350; see [[thoas-killed-by-menelaus]], [[amphiclus]], [[atymnius-son-of-amisodarus]], [[maris]], [[cleobulus]], [[lycon]], [[acamas]] and [[erymas-killed-by-idomeneus]]).

**The name.** He shares it with the father of Prothoenor, a leader of the Boeotians, whom Polydamas kills at the ships in Book 14 (14.449–451; see [[areilycus]] and [[prothoenor]]). Ἀρηΐλυκος seems to join Ἄρης, “Ares”, and λύκος, “wolf”.
