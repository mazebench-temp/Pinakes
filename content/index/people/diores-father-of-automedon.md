ἦ μὰν Αὐτομέδων Διώρεος ἄλκιμος υἱός, “Truly Automedon, the valiant son of Diores” (17.429): the father is named when the narrator turns to Achilles' horses, weeping apart from the battle, and to their driver, who cannot move them (17.426–440; see [[automedon]] and [[the-horses-of-achilles]]). The patronymic comes again when Automedon answers Alcimedon and gives him the reins (17.474; see [[alcimedon]]). Nothing else is told of him.

The Catalogue's Diores, son of Amarynceus, is one of the four leaders of the Epeians, and he dies in the first battle, his ankle crushed by the stone of the Thracian Peirous (2.622, 4.517–526; see [[diores]], [[epeians]] and [[peirous]]). The poem does not connect him with Automedon.
