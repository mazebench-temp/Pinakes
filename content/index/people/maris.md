**Over his brother's body.** Μάρις δʼ αὐτοσχεδὰ δουρὶ / Ἀντιλόχῳ ἐπόρουσε κασιγνήτοιο χολωθεὶς / στὰς πρόσθεν νέκυος: when Antilochus has killed his brother Atymnius, Maris springs at him with his spear at close quarters, “angered for his brother, / standing in front of the corpse” (16.319–321; see [[antilochus]], [[atymnius-son-of-amisodarus]] and [[fighting-over-the-body]]). But Antilochus' own brother Thrasymedes “was first to reach out, before he could stab, and did not miss” (16.322; see [[thrasymedes]]): the spear takes Maris in the shoulder, tears the upper arm away from the muscles and breaks the bone clean through. “He fell with a thud, and darkness covered his eyes” (16.323–325; see [[fell-with-a-thud]] and [[darkness-covered-his-eyes]]). So the two sons of Amisodarus, “brought down by two brothers”, went down to Erebus together (16.326–327; see [[erebus]] and [[amisodarus]]). The words with which Thrasymedes strikes first, ἔφθη ὀρεξάμενος, were said of Meges a few verses before, when he struck Amphiclus (16.314; see [[amphiclus]]).

**The verses.** Walter Leaf reports that Payne Knight rejected 16.326–329, which hold several unusual expressions: βήτην εἰς Ἔρεβος, “went down to Erebus”, occurs nowhere else, and ἀκοντισταί, “spearmen”, only once more, in the Odyssey (Od. 18.262; *Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 16.326–329).

Maris is named only here.
