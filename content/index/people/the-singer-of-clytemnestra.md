## In Homer

He is a singer, ἀοιδὸς ἀνήρ (*Odyssey* 3.267), with the word for Phemius, the singer of Odysseus' house, and for Demodocus among the Phaeacians (see [[phemius]] and [[demodocus]]), and he is not named. Nestor tells only that Agamemnon, as he left for Troy, gave him many charges to guard his wife, and that Aegisthus did not kill him but left him on a desert island for the birds (*Odyssey* 3.267–271; see [[birds]]). In the other house that the poem shows in its king's absence, Odysseus' singer Phemius sings for the suitors under compulsion, and is spared at Telemachus' word when Odysseus kills them (*Odyssey* 1.154, 22.330–377).

## Outside Homer

Strabo, defending poetry as an education against Eratosthenes, cites him: Homer called singers σωφρονισταί, “teachers of self-control”, like the guardian of Clytemnestra, to whom the son of Atreus gave many charges as he left for Troy, and Aegisthus did not prevail over her until he had taken the singer to a desert island and left him there (*Geography* 1.2.3, quoting *Odyssey* 3.267–268, 3.270–272; see [[strabo]]). The Suda says the same under ἀοιδός: the race of singers was temperate of old and had the disposition of philosophers, for Agamemnon left this one with Clytemnestra as a σωφρονιστήρ, who first recounted the virtues of women and so roused in her an ambition for nobility, and then, by giving her pleasant pastime, turned her mind from base thoughts; so Aegisthus did not corrupt her until he had killed the singer, where Homer has him left alive on the island (Suda α 4402; see [[phemius]]). Merry quotes nearly the same words from Athenaeus (1.14b), by way of Eustathius, who also cites one Timolaus as saying that the singer was a brother of Phemius, and that Phemius went with Penelope to Ithaca as her guardian; Merry answers, after Hayman, that Odysseus set no one to guard Penelope, and that Mentor's charge was the house and its goods (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 3.267; *Odyssey* 2.225–227; see [[mentor]] and [[athenaeus]]).

Proclus makes him the third of Homer's singers, after the inspired Demodocus and the knowing Phemius: an imitative singer with right opinion, whose songs of self-control kept the queen from wrongdoing as long as she had his company (*On the Republic* I 194 Kroll, quoting *Odyssey* 3.267–268; see [[proclus-the-neoplatonist]], [[demodocus]] and [[phemius]]).
