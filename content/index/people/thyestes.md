# Θυέστ* takes Θυέστῃ and Θυέστʼ (Il. 2.106-107), and Θυέστης and the patronymic Θυεστιάδης (Od. 4.517-518).

The origin of the name Θυέστης (Thyestes) is unknown.

{{quote:778cced3-e225-5437-8786-4ee584223e25}}

πολύαρνι = πολύς (“many”) + ἀρήν (“lamb”): “rich in flocks”, a king whose wealth is in sheep. See [[rich-in-flocks]].

In the Iliad he is named only here. Atreus at his death left the scepter to Thyestes, and Thyestes left it to Agamemnon. The lines describe an orderly succession and say nothing of any quarrel between Atreus and Thyestes. See [[the-scepter-of-agamemnon]].

## Family

The Odyssey names his son: Θυεστιάδης Αἴγισθος, “Aegisthus, son of Thyestes” (*Odyssey* 4.518; see [[aegisthus]]). Homer does not call Thyestes the brother of Atreus, and does not say how his son came to be the enemy of Atreus' son.

{{notes}}

## Outside Homer

Tragedy made the house of Atreus a house of crimes. In Aeschylus' *Agamemnon*, Aegisthus tells how Atreus drove Thyestes out, then welcomed him back and served him the flesh of his own children (1583–1602). Seneca's *Thyestes* dramatizes the feast. Later versions add a golden lamb, the sign of kingship, which Thyestes won through his affair with Atreus' wife Aerope (the chorus of Euripides' *Electra*, 699–746).
