## The name

Πολίτης is the common noun πολίτης, “citizen”, from πόλις, “city”, and the name of a son of Priam in the Iliad, the swift lookout whose voice Iris takes and who leads his wounded brother Deiphobus out of the battle (*Iliad* 2.791–794, 13.533–539; see [[polites]]).

## Descriptions

- ὄρχαμος ἀνδρῶν, “leader of men” (*Odyssey* 10.224; see [[leader-of-men]]).
- ὅς μοι κήδιστος ἑτάρων ἦν κεδνότατός τε, “who was the nearest and dearest to me of my companions” (*Odyssey* 10.225). W. W. Merry calls the pairing a play of sound, renders it “nearest and dearest”, and compares Ajax's words to Achilles in the embassy, κήδιστοί τʼ ἔμεναι καὶ φίλτατοι, “to be the nearest and dearest to you” (*Iliad* 9.642; *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on *Odyssey* 10.225; see the Odyssey translation's key words).

## Outside Homer

Strabo, describing Temese in Bruttium, says that near it stood a hero-shrine of Polites, “one of the companions of Odysseus”, shaded by wild olives: treacherously killed by the natives, he became so wrathful that the people around paid him tribute at an oracle's bidding, and men said of disagreeable people that “the hero in Temese” was upon them, until the boxer Euthymus, when the Epizephyrian Locrians had taken the city, went down to fight him, beat him and freed the people from the tribute (*Geography* 6.1.5; see [[temese]] and [[strabo]]). Ovid has Macareus name “faithful Polites” among the men whom the lot sent to Circe's house (*fidumque Politen*, *Metamorphoses* 14.251; see [[ovid]] and [[eurylochus]]).
