## The two phrases

Aristotle names no author; modern editors print both phrases among the fragments of Empedocles (see [[empedocles]]). The first, “with the bronze drawing off his life”, is usually taken of the sacrificial knife, which Empedocles condemned: souls pass through the bodies of animals, and a father who sacrifices may slaughter his own son changed in form (fr. 137 DK). The second is identified with a line about purification that Theon of Smyrna quotes in another form, “cutting from five springs with unwearying bronze”: water drawn from five springs in a bronze vessel (fr. 143 DK).
