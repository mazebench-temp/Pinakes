The Doric forms θνατά, θνατόν point to a Sicilian poet, and the line has been counted among the sayings of Epicharmus (see [[epicharmus]]). Pindar puts the same thought in a phrase, “mortal things befit mortals” (*Isthmian* 5.16). In the *Nicomachean Ethics* Aristotle rejects the advice of those who tell man to think human things: one should, as far as possible, make oneself immortal (10.7, 1177b31–34).
