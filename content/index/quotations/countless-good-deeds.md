## In the Iliad

When Thersites has railed at Agamemnon and Odysseus has silenced him with the scepter, the troubled army laughs, and one man says to his neighbor: “Ah, truly Odysseus has done countless good deeds, leading the way in good counsel and marshaling the battle; but now this is by far the best thing he has done among the Argives, to stop this word-slinging slanderer from haranguing us” (2.272–275; see [[odysseus-and-thersites]] and [[thersites]]). Homer uses μυρίος, “numberless”, of any great number: the proem's “countless sorrows” are μυρία ἄλγεα (1.2).
