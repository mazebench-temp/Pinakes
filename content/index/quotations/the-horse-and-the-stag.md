The fable is Aristotle's example of the invented example, the λόγος, beside the comparison (see [[paradeigma]]). A horse had a meadow to himself; a stag came and spoiled the pasture; the horse asked a man to help him take revenge, and the man agreed, if the horse would take a bit and let him mount with javelins; and so the horse became the man's slave. The Himeraeans have already given Phalaris the bit by making him general with full powers; a bodyguard would let him mount (*Rhetoric* 2.20, 1393b10–22). Horace tells the fable of a stag and a horse in the same shape, to show what is lost by a man who will not be content with little (*Epistles* 1.10.34–41). See [[stesichorus]], [[phalaris]] and [[himera]].
