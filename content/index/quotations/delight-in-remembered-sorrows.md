## In the Odyssey

In his hut the swineherd Eumaeus, asked how he came to Ithaca, offers the stranger, who is Odysseus, a story for a story: “let us two, drinking and eating in the hut, take delight in each other's grievous sorrows, remembering them; for afterward a man takes delight even in sorrows, one who has suffered very much and wandered much” (*Odyssey* 15.398–401). Then he tells how he was a king's son, stolen by Phoenician traders and sold to Laertes (see [[eumaeus]]).

## Aristotle's text

Our text ends “one who has suffered very much and wandered much” (ὅς τις δὴ μάλα πολλὰ πάθῃ καὶ πόλλʼ ἐπαληθῇ). Aristotle has “remembering, one who has suffered much and done much” (μνημένος, ὅστις πολλὰ πάθῃ καὶ πολλὰ ἐόργῃ), which makes memory, his subject, part of the verse.
