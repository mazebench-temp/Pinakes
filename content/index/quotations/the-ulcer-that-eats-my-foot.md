## The two lines

Aeschylus and Euripides each wrote a *Philoctetes*, and so did Sophocles, whose play survives; Dio Chrysostom compares the three in his fifty-second oration (see [[philoctetes-plays]]). The hero, bitten by a water snake on the way to Troy, was left on Lemnos with a festering wound in his foot (2.721–725; see [[philoctetes]] and [[philoctetes-on-lemnos]]). Aeschylus made him speak of “the ulcer (φαγέδαινα) that eats the flesh of my foot”; Euripides put θοινᾶται, “feasts on”, a word of banquets, in place of ἐσθίει, “eats”.
