Aristotle's second example of a fable is told as Aesop's own speech in the Samian assembly, when a demagogue was on trial for his life. The fox, swept into a ravine while crossing a river, lay there covered with dog-ticks; a passing hedgehog took pity and offered to pull them off, but she refused: these were full and drew little blood, and others would come hungry and drink the rest. “So you too, men of Samos: this man will do you no more harm (for he is rich); but if you put him to death, others will come who are poor” (*Rhetoric* 2.20, 1393b22–1394a1).
