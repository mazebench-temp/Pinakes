## In Sophocles

Creon has forbidden the burial of Polynices, and Antigone, caught burying him, answers him: it was not Zeus who made this proclamation, nor Justice who dwells with the gods below, and she did not think that his decrees, a mortal's, could override the unwritten and unfailing laws of the gods. “For these live not now or yesterday but forever, and no one knows from when they appeared. For these I was not going, for fear of any man's will, to pay the penalty among the gods” (*Antigone* 450–460; see [[antigone-play]], [[antigone]] and [[creon-of-thebes]]). Aristotle's text has “this lives” (ζῇ τοῦτο) at 1373b12, where ours has “these”; at 1375b1–2 he gives the lines in a shortened form.
