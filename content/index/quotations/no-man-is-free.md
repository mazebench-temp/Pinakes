Agamemnon has said that he pities Hecuba but dares not help her against Polymestor for fear of the army. She answers that no mortal is free: a slave either of money or of fortune, or forced by the crowd or by the laws to act against his own judgment (*Hecuba* 864–867). Euripides wrote “of mortals”, θνητῶν; Aristotle quotes “of men”, ἀνδρῶν. On its own the first line is a maxim; “together with what follows it is an enthymeme” (*Rhetoric* 2.21, 1394b3–6). See [[gnome]], [[hecuba]] and [[eleutheros]].
