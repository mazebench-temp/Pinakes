## The line

The verse survives only here, and what Simonides meant by it is not known. Corinthians had fought on both sides at Troy. Euchenor, son of the Corinthian seer Polyidus, came with the Achaeans and was killed by Paris (13.663–672; see [[euchenor]]); Glaucus, who led the Lycians for Troy, traced his family to Bellerophon of Ephyre, which was taken to be the old name of Corinth (6.152–211; see [[glaucus]] and [[bellerophon]]). Pindar's ode for a Corinthian victor recalls Corinthians on both sides of the war (*Olympian* 13.55–62; see [[corinthians]]).
