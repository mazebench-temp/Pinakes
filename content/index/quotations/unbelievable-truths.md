Euripides' *Thyestes* is lost. The argument suits a speaker who must make an improbable story believed, and it turns the rhetoric of probability against itself: if the probable can be false, the improbable can be true (compare [[the-probable-improbable]]). See [[topics-of-enthymemes]] and [[euripides]].
