## In the Odyssey

Polyphemus, blinded, remembers the prophecy that he would lose his sight at the hands of Odysseus: “I always expected some tall and handsome man to come here, clothed in great strength; but now one who is small and of no account and feeble has blinded my eye, when he had overcome me with wine” (*Odyssey* 9.507–516; see [[polyphemus]] and [[cyclopes]]). Our text ends the line with ἄκικυς, “feeble”; Aristotle's had ἀεικής, “unseemly”.
