Helen has blamed Aphrodite for her flight with Paris. Hecuba answers that it was her own mind, seeing Paris' beauty, that became Aphrodite: “all follies are Aphrodite for mortals, and rightly does the goddess's name begin with folly” (*Trojan Women* 988–990). Aristotle cites the line with Chaeremon's on Pentheus as examples of the topic from the name (*Rhetoric* 2.23, 1400b23–25). See [[aphrodite]], [[helen]], [[hecuba]] and [[topics-of-enthymemes]].
