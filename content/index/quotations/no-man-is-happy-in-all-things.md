The *Stheneboea*, now lost, opened with Bellerophon's speech: “there is no man who is happy in all things”, for one is well born and poor, another rich and low-born. In the play Stheneboea, the wife of Proetus, falls in love with him, is rejected, and accuses him to her husband (see [[bellerophon]]). Aristotle quotes the line beside a maxim from the *Hecuba* (*Rhetoric* 2.21, 1394b1–6; see [[no-man-is-free]]). See [[gnome]] and [[euripides]].
