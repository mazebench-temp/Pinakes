## In the Odyssey

When Odysseus has killed the suitors, the bard Phemius, who sang for them under compulsion, clasps his knees and begs for his life: he is self-taught, and the god has planted all kinds of songs in his mind; he will sing for Odysseus as for a god (*Odyssey* 22.344–353). Telemachus speaks for him, and Odysseus spares him (22.354–377; see [[odysseus]]). The claim joins the poet's own skill to the gift of the god.
