## The lines

In Empedocles' cosmic cycle Love and Strife by turns unite and divide the four roots, fire, air, earth and water. In this passage Love has reached the center of the whirl, and the elements, which are immortal, mingle into the mortal forms of living things: “at once things grew mortal that before had learned to be immortal, and things unmixed before were mixed” (fr. 35.14–15 DK; see [[empedocles]]). Simplicius, who preserves the whole passage, has a different text of the second line. The word ζωρός, “pure, unmixed”, is the one that Aristotle has just discussed in Achilles' order to “mix the wine stronger” (see [[stronger-or-quicker]]).
