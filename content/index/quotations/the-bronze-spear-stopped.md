## In the Iliad

Aeneas' spear strikes the shield that Hephaestus made for Achilles, but the gold, the god's gift, held it back: “it drove through two layers, but there were still three, since the lame god had forged five layers, two of bronze, two of tin on the inside, and one of gold, and there the ashen spear was stopped” (*Iliad* 20.267–272; see [[aeneas]], [[achilles]] and [[shield]]). If the gold lay between the others, how could the spear reach it through two layers and be stopped there? Aristarchus removed the difficulty by rejecting lines 269–272 as an addition. Our text has “ashen”, μείλινον, where Aristotle has “bronze”, χάλκεον.
