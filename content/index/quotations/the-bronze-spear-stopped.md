## In the Iliad

Aeneas' spear strikes the shield that Hephaestus made for Achilles, but the gold, “the gifts of the god”, held it back: “but it drove through two layers, and there were still three, / since the crook-footed god had beaten out five layers, / two of bronze, and two of tin inside, / and one of gold, and in that the ash spear was stopped” (*Iliad* 20.268–272; see [[aeneas]], [[achilles]] and [[shield]]). If the gold lay between the others, how could the spear reach it through two layers and be stopped there? Aristarchus removed the difficulty by rejecting lines 269–272 as an addition. Our text has “ash”, μείλινον, where Aristotle has “bronze”, χάλκεον (see the-bronze-spear-stopped in the Poetics notes and the translation's note on 20.272).
