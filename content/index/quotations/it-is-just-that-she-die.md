Orestes' defense takes the two just rules separately: a husband-killer should die, and a son should avenge his father. Put together, says Aristotle, they may no longer be just; for a son to kill his mother is another thing. The argument also omits by whom the punishment should be done (*Rhetoric* 2.24, 1401a35–1401b3). See [[orestes-theodectes]], [[orestes]], [[clytemnestra]] and [[apparent-enthymemes]].
