Alcmaeon killed his mother Eriphyle, who had sent his father Amphiaraus to his death at Thebes for the bribe of a necklace. In Theodectes' scene his wife Alphesiboea asks whether no mortal hated his mother, and Alcmaeon answers with a distinction: she deserved to die, but he was not the one to kill her. Aristotle takes it as an example of the warning that goes with the topic from correlatives: the sufferer may have deserved to suffer, and the doer still not have deserved to do it (*Rhetoric* 2.23, 1397a28–1397b6).
