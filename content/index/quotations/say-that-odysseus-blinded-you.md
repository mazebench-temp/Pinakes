Aristotle reads the scene as the poet's insight into anger: “anger is against the individual”, and revenge is not complete unless the victim knows both by whom and for what he suffers (*Rhetoric* 2.3, 1380b20–25). See [[odysseus]], [[polyphemus-the-cyclops]] and [[aristotle-anger]].

## In the Odyssey

Odysseus has escaped from the cave under the ram, and as his ship pulls away he taunts the Cyclops twice, against his companions' pleading. The second time he names himself, with his title, his father and his home (*Odyssey* 9.502–505). The boast costs him dear, for with the name Polyphemus can call down his father Poseidon's curse, and he calls it down in the words of the boast (*Odyssey* 9.528–536; see [[poseidon]] and [[curses]]).
