Odysseus has escaped from the cave under the ram, and as his ship pulls away he taunts the Cyclops twice, against his companions' pleading. The second time he names himself: “Cyclops, if any mortal man asks you about the shameful blinding of your eye, say that Odysseus, sacker of cities, blinded you, the son of Laertes, whose home is in Ithaca” (*Odyssey* 9.502–505). The boast costs him dear, for with the name Polyphemus can call down his father Poseidon's curse (9.528–535).

Aristotle reads the scene as the poet's insight into anger: “anger is against the individual”, and revenge is not complete unless the victim knows both by whom and for what he suffers (*Rhetoric* 2.3, 1380b20–25). See [[odysseus]], [[polyphemus-the-cyclops]] and [[aristotle-anger]].
