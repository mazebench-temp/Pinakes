Hippolytus, accused by Theseus, begins his defense by apologizing for his lack of skill: he is clumsy at speaking before a crowd, wiser among a few of his own age, and this is fitting, “for those who are of no account among the wise are more musical in speaking before a crowd” (*Hippolytus* 986–989). Aristotle gives the reason: the educated speak of what is common and universal, the uneducated of what they know and what is near at hand. Enthymemes should therefore not draw their conclusions from far back, nor take in every step (*Rhetoric* 2.22, 1395b24–31). See [[enthymema]] and [[euripides]].
