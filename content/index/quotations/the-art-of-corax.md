{{quote:7fba2f11-fa74-5677-8dcf-c41ee3cb7a55}}

The second half of the argument is the trick: what is probable becomes improbable because it is probable, since the strong man would have known that he would be suspected. Aristotle calls this making the weaker argument the stronger, and says that people were justly offended at Protagoras' profession of teaching it (*Rhetoric* 2.24, 1402a17–28; see [[protagoras]]). Plato's *Phaedrus* gives the same argument to Tisias (273a–c). See [[corax]], [[eikos]] and [[apparent-enthymemes]].
