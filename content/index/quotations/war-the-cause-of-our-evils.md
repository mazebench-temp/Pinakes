The first of the twenty-eight topics of demonstrative enthymemes is from opposites: one sees whether the opposite belongs to the opposite. Aristotle's examples are a plain one of his own, that self-control is good because licentiousness is harmful, and the argument “in the Messenian Speech” (*Rhetoric* 2.23, 1397a7–13). Book I quotes Alcidamas' *Messenian Speech* by name (1.13, 1373b18), and this is probably the same speech, spoken on behalf of the Messenians freed from Sparta (see [[messenian-speech]] and [[alcidamas]]). See [[topics-of-enthymemes]].
