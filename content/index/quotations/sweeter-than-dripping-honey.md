## In the Iliad

When Thetis comes to him after Patroclus' death, Achilles wishes that strife might perish from among gods and men, “and anger, which drives even a man of good sense to rage, and which, far sweeter than dripping honey, swells like smoke in the breasts of men” (18.107–110); so Agamemnon angered him, but he will let the past be past and go to find Hector. Homer's word is χόλος (see [[cholos]] and [[achilles]]).

## In Aristotle

The second book of the *Rhetoric* quotes the lines again, with the next line, in the definition of anger: anger is accompanied by a pleasure that comes from the hope of revenge (2.2, 1378b1–9; see [[aristotle-anger]]).
