## In the Iliad

When Thetis comes to him after Patroclus' death, Achilles wishes that strife might perish from among gods and men, “and anger, which sets even a man of much wisdom to rage, / and which, far sweeter than dripping honey, / swells in the breasts of men like smoke” (18.107–110); so Agamemnon angered him, but he will let the past be past and go to find Hector. Homer's word is χόλος (see [[cholos]] and [[achilles]]).

## In Plato

The *Philebus* quotes the verses, without naming the poet, for the pleasures mixed in with the pains of the soul, anger first among them (47d–48a; see [[plato-the-pleasure-of-anger]]). On honey as Homer's measure of sweetness see [[sweeter-than-honey]].

## In Aristotle

The second book of the *Rhetoric* quotes the lines again, with the next line, in the definition of anger: anger is accompanied by a pleasure that comes from the hope of revenge (2.2, 1378b1–9; see [[aristotle-anger]]).
