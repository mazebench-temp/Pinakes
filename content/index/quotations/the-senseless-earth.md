On the twelfth day after Hector's death, with Achilles still dragging the body round Patroclus' tomb, Apollo rebukes the gods for allowing it: Achilles has lost pity and shame, and “in his rage he defiles the senseless earth” (Iliad 24.33–54). Hector's body has become earth, deaf and without feeling, and the outrage no longer reaches him. Zeus then sends Thetis to tell her son to accept a ransom.

Aristotle takes the verse into his account of calming: people are not angry with the dead, “since they have suffered the utmost and will feel no pain or perception, which is what the angry aim at” (*Rhetoric* 2.3, 1380b25–28). The poet who gives the line to Apollo is “wishing to stop Achilles' anger against the dead man”. See [[praotes]], [[achilles]] and [[hector]].
