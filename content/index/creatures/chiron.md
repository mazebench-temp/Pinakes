## Etymology

Χείρων is usually connected with χείρ (“hand”), as if for the skilled hand of the healer, but the etymology is not certain.

## In Homer

The Iliad names him in four lines, one of them repeated, and each time he gives something: drugs, a spear, his knowledge. In Book 4 [[Machaon]] spreads on Menelaus' wound the drugs his father had from him:

{{quote:0e1ce1ad-1f84-5cd3-90c5-caf768b12e7a}}

φίλα φρονέων, “with friendly thoughts”: the translation's “in friendship”. The drugs are ἤπια φάρμακα, “soothing drugs”, and the knowledge of them passes from the Centaur to Asclepius and from Asclepius to his son (see [[healing]] and [[chiron-and-asclepius]]). In Book 11 the wounded Eurypylus asks Patroclus for the drugs he learned from Achilles, “whom Chiron taught, the most just of the Centaurs” (ὃν Χείρων ἐδίδαξε δικαιότατος Κενταύρων, 11.831–832). Achilles' spear is the Pelian ash “which Chiron gave to his dear father from the peak of Pelion, to be the death of heroes” (16.143–144 = 19.390–391). Both times he gives to a father: πατρί in 4.219, πατρὶ φίλῳ in 16.143.

Homer never describes his body, and in Book 4 does not even call him a Centaur. δικαιότατος (“most just”) sets him apart from the wild “beasts” whom the Lapiths fought in Nestor's youth (1.262–268, 2.742–744; see [[centaurs]]). The Iliad does not say that he raised Achilles: in Book 9 Phoenix tells how he reared the child himself in Peleus' house (9.485–495). The Odyssey does not name him.

## Outside Homer

[[hesiod|Hesiod]] calls him Φιλλυρίδης, “son of Philyra”, and has him rear Medeius, the son of Jason and Medea, in the mountains (*Theogony* 1001–1002). For Pindar he is the son of Cronus and Philyra (*Pythian* 3.1–4), who raised Jason in his cave and taught Asclepius “the gentle-handed art of drugs” (*Nemean* 3.53–55). The boy Achilles, living in the house of Philyra, killed lions and boars and brought them to the Centaur (*Nemean* 3.43–52). In *Pythian* 3 Apollo gives him the infant Asclepius to be taught to heal men's diseases (3.45–46), and Pindar wishes that Chiron still lived in his cave, so that he might send a healer to the sick Hieron (3.63–67). A lost poem ascribed to Hesiod, the *Precepts of Chiron*, gave the Centaur's teaching to the young Achilles.

[[apollodorus|Apollodorus]] makes him the son of Cronus and Philyra, a Centaur “of double form” (*Library* 1.2.4). Heracles, fighting the other Centaurs, wounded him by accident with an arrow poisoned by the Hydra; the wound would not heal, and since he was immortal he could not die, until Prometheus took his immortality on himself (2.5.4). At the wedding of Peleus and Thetis he gave Peleus the ash spear (3.13.5), and he raised Achilles on the flesh of lions and boars and the marrow of bears (3.13.6). He also restored the sight of [[Phoenix]], whom his father Amyntor had blinded, so that Peleus could make him king of the Dolopians (3.13.8).
