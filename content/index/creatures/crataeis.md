## The name

Κράταιις is “the Mighty One”, from κραταιός, “mighty” (see [[kratos]]), formed as ἡμερίς, “the garden vine” (*Odyssey* 5.69), is formed from ἥμερος, “tame” (W. W. Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on *Odyssey* 11.597). Merry calls her a personification of the wild forces of nature (on *Odyssey* 12.124). The scholia preferred to write the word here as κραταιίς, an adverb, “mightily”, so that Odysseus is to “mightily entreat” Scylla's mother, who is then left without a name (Merry's critical note and note on *Odyssey* 12.124).

## The mighty weight

The same word, as a common noun, undoes the labor of Sisyphus in the land of the dead: whenever he was about to heave his stone over the top of the hill, “then its mighty weight would turn it back” (ἀποστρέψασκε κραταιίς, *Odyssey* 11.597; see [[sisyphus]]). Aristarchus took κραταιίς there for an adverb too. Merry sets that aside, and takes it either for a power in the stone itself, as we should say “the force of gravity”, which is how a scholion explains it, ἡ κραταιὰ δύναμις ὅ ἐστι τὸ βάρος, “the mighty power, that is, the weight”, or for a “Power” or “Mighty One” outside the stone, like the δαίμων that dries up Tantalus' water (*Odyssey* 11.587), and he refers to the name of Scylla's mother (on *Odyssey* 11.597; see the translation's note on *Odyssey* 11.596–597). The κραταιίς of Sisyphus' stone has no entry of its own.

## Family

She is the mother of [[scylla|Scylla]], “who bore her to be a bane to mortals” (*Odyssey* 12.125), and, in Circe's words, the one power that can stop her daughter from rushing out again (*Odyssey* 12.126). Homer names no father for Scylla.

## Outside Homer

Ovid keeps the name: in his story of Galatea and the Cyclops the Nereid tells her grief to Scylla, “the daughter of Crataeis” (*Crataeide natam*, *Metamorphoses* 13.749; see [[ovid]] and [[scylla-and-charybdis-in-later-writers]]). Other writers gave Scylla other parents: the *Great Eoiae* ascribed to Hesiod made her the daughter of Phorbas and Hecate (fr. 13 Evelyn-White; see [[hesiod]]), and Merry reports a legend that made her the daughter of Hecate by Phorcys or by Triton (on *Odyssey* 12.124).
