## Name

Ἅρπυια, plural Ἅρπυιαι, is from the root of ἁρπάζω, “snatch”: the Harpies are the Snatchers. The Suda explains them as “snatching spirits” (ἁρπακτικαὶ δαίμονες, Suda α 4009).

## In Homer

The Iliad names one Harpy, Podarge, who bore Achilles' horses to the West Wind “as she grazed in a meadow beside the stream of Oceanus” (16.150–151; see [[podarge]], [[zephyrus]] and [[the-horses-of-achilles]]). Her foals “flew as fast as the winds” (16.149).

In the Odyssey the harpies are the winds that carry a man off so that no one knows his end. Telemachus says that if his father had died at Troy, the Achaeans would have made him a tomb, and he would have won great fame for his son too: “But now the Harpies have snatched him away without report” (*Odyssey* 1.241), and the swineherd Eumaeus says the same verses (*Odyssey* 14.369–371; see [[fame-and-the-tomb]]). Penelope, wishing for death, prays that a storm wind may carry her off, as the storm winds carried off the daughters of Pandareus: while Aphrodite went to Olympus to ask Zeus for their marriage, “the harpies snatched the girls away and gave them to the hateful Erinyes to serve” (*Odyssey* 20.61–78; see [[erinyes]]). The harpies are the storm winds, θύελλαι: Penelope says that storm winds have snatched Telemachus away “without report”, in the words used of the harpies (*Odyssey* 4.727–728), and in her story the storm winds that carry off the daughters of Pandareus are called harpies at the end of it (*Odyssey* 20.63, 20.66, 20.77).

## Outside Homer

[[hesiod|Hesiod]] makes the Harpies daughters of Thaumas and Electra, the daughter of Oceanus, and sisters of Iris: the Harpies “of the lovely hair”, Aello and Ocypete, who follow the blasts of the winds and the birds on their swift wings (*Theogony* 265–269; see [[iris]]). Their names are “Storm-wind” (ἄελλα) and “Swift-flier”. In Aeschylus' *Eumenides* the Pythia, seeing the sleeping Furies, compares them with creatures she once saw in a painting, carrying off the meal of Phineus, though these are wingless (*Eumenides* 50–52; see [[aeschylus]]).
