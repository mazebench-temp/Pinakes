## Etymology

Βριάρεως (Briareōs), from βριαρός, "strong, sturdy" (compare βρίθω, "be heavy"): "the Mighty One". Αἰγαίων (Aigaiōn), the name men use, is usually connected with the sea, the Aegean (Αἰγαῖον πέλαγος). ἑκατόγχειρος = ἑκατόν (hundred) + χείρ (hand).

## Outside Homer

In Hesiod, Briareos is one of the three Hundred-Handers (Cottus, Briareos and Gyes), sons of Uranus and Gaia, each with fifty heads and a hundred arms (*Theogony* 147–153). Uranus imprisoned them under the earth. Zeus freed them, and they won the war against the Titans for him (617–735). Afterwards they guarded the Titans in Tartarus. Poseidon gave Briareos his daughter Cymopolea as wife (817–819).

The story in Iliad 1 thus repeats the pattern of the Titanomachy: Zeus' rule is saved by the monstrous strength of the Hundred-Handers.
