## The evidence

Chapter 6 opens: “Of the art of imitation in hexameters, and of comedy, we shall speak later” (1449b21–22). Epic is treated in chapters 23–26, but comedy is not, and the *Rhetoric* twice refers to a discussion of the laughable “in the Poetics” that the surviving book does not contain (1.11, 1372a1–2; 3.18, 1419b5–6). The catalogue of Aristotle's works in Diogenes Laertius lists the *Poetics* in two books (5.24). A short Byzantine summary of a theory of comedy, the *Tractatus Coislinianus*, has been thought to derive from it, but its origin is uncertain. The surviving text breaks off in the middle of a sentence (1462b19).

In Umberto Eco's novel *The Name of the Rose* (1980) the last copy of the book is hidden in a monastery library, and the monks who read it die of poisoned pages.
