title: The Alexander
greek: Ἀλέξανδρος

tags: [rhetoric, lost-work]
summary: A lost defense of Paris (Alexander), perhaps a sophist's speech, from which Aristotle takes two arguments: Paris was not licentious, since he was content with one woman, and he was great-souled, since he lived apart on Ida.
aliases: [Alexander]
---

Aristotle names no author. Paradoxical defenses of the guilty of myth were a sophistic genre: Gorgias wrote one for Helen and Isocrates another, and Isocrates' *Helen* defends Paris' choice of her (41–48). See [[paris]], [[helen]] and [[polycrates]].
