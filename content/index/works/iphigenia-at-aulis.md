summary: Euripides' last tragedy, staged after his death (405 BCE): Agamemnon lures his daughter to Aulis to be sacrificed so that the fleet can sail. Aristotle's example of an inconsistent character, the girl who first begs for her life and then offers it.
aliases: [Iphigenia at Aulis, Iphigeneia in Aulis, IA]

Iphigenia arrives believing she is to marry Achilles, learns the truth, and pleads with her father for her life (1211–1252); then, as the army demands her death, she resolves to die willingly for Greece (1368–1401). The ending was reworked after Euripides' death (see [[iphigenia]], [[the-sacrifice-of-iphigenia]] and [[euripides-catalogue]]).
