## The poem

Margites (“madman”, from μάργος) was a fool who “knew many things, but knew them all badly”; he did not know whether his father or his mother had borne him, and did not dare to sleep with his wife. Only a few lines survive, of hexameters irregularly mixed with iambic trimeters. Plato quotes the line about knowing many things badly (*Alcibiades II* 147b–c).

## In the Poetics

The Margites is the earliest poem of the trivial kind that can be named, ascribed to Homer, and the iambic meter came into it “as was fitting” (1448b28–32; see [[iambus]] and [[iambic-trimeter]]). As Homer was the poet of serious things, “so too the form of comedy he was the first to point out, dramatizing not invective but the ridiculous; for the Margites is analogous: as the Iliad and the Odyssey are to tragedies, so is this poem to comedies” (1448b36–1449a2; see [[comedy]] and [[homer]]).
