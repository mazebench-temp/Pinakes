Aristotle's published works, unlike the treatises that survive, were dialogues written for a general public; *On Poets* was one of them. Its fragments include stories about Homer's birth and death, and the judgment that Empedocles was “Homeric and skilled in diction” (Diogenes Laertius 8.57; see [[empedocles]]). The *Poetics* ends its chapter on character by referring to “my published works” for the perceptions that necessarily attend the art of poetry (1454b15–18), and most scholars think the reference is to this dialogue.
