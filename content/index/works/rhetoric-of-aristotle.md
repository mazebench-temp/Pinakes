The *Rhetoric* defines rhetoric as the power of seeing the available means of persuasion in each case (1.2, 1355b25–26), by character, by emotion, and by argument; its second book analyzes the emotions, among them anger, pity and fear (see [[aristotle-anger]] and [[pity]]), and its third treats style and arrangement, with many examples from Homer and the tragedians. In the *Poetics*, “what concerns thought may be left to my discussions of rhetoric, for it belongs more properly to that inquiry” (1456a34–36; see [[dianoia]]).
