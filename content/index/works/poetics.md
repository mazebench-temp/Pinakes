## The work

The *Poetics* is a set of lecture notes rather than a finished book: it is compressed, sometimes obscure, and has lacunae. It was probably composed during Aristotle's second stay at Athens, when he taught at the Lyceum (335–323 BCE), though parts may be earlier. Its 26 chapters treat imitation and its kinds (1–3), the origins of poetry and of tragedy and comedy (4–5), tragedy (6–22), with its definition, its parts, the plot, character, thought and diction, epic (23–24), the problems critics raise against poets and their solutions (25), and the comparison of epic and tragedy (26). The promised account of comedy belonged to a second book, now lost (see [[the-lost-book-on-comedy]]).

The text depends on two medieval manuscripts, Parisinus 1741 (tenth century) and Riccardianus 46 (fourteenth century), on a Latin translation made by William of Moerbeke in 1278, and on an Arabic translation made from a Syriac version in the tenth century. It had little influence in antiquity; the Arabic tradition, through Averroes' commentary, knew it in the Middle Ages, but it became central to European literary theory only after the Aldine edition of 1508 and the commentaries of Robortello (1548) and Castelvetro (1570).

This library contains the Greek text of Kassel's edition and a new line-for-line translation; see [[aristotle]] and the index entries linked from each chapter.
