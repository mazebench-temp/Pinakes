summary: The ancient name for Odyssey 19, in which the old nurse Eurycleia, washing the disguised Odysseus' feet, recognizes him by his scar. Aristotle praises the recognition and uses the book's false inference as a model of telling lies.
aliases: [Niptra, Washing, The Washing, Odyssey 19, foot-washing]

In Odyssey 19 Penelope orders the stranger's feet to be washed; Eurycleia touches the scar from the boar's tusk and knows her master, and Odysseus seizes her throat to silence her (*Odyssey* 19.386–490; see [[odysseus-recognized-by-his-nurse]] and [[the-scar-of-odysseus]]). Earlier in the book the stranger convinces Penelope by describing Odysseus' clothes and brooch (*Odyssey* 19.220–250).
