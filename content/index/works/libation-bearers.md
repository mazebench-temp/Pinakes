summary: The second play of Aeschylus' Oresteia (458 BCE): Orestes returns and kills his mother Clytemnestra and Aegisthus. Electra's recognition of her brother, by reasoning from a lock of hair, is Aristotle's example of recognition by inference.
aliases: [Choephoroi, Choephori, Libation Bearers, the Libation Bearers]
---

Electra, sent to pour libations at Agamemnon's tomb, finds a lock of hair like her own and footprints like hers, and reasons that Orestes has come; he steps forward and proves himself by a woven cloth (164–245). Brother and sister lament with the chorus in the great kommos, and Orestes kills Aegisthus and his mother (see [[electra-recognizes-orestes]], [[orestes-kills-clytemnestra]] and [[kommos]]).
