## The poem

The Odyssey tells how Odysseus, ten years after the fall of Troy, reaches home from Calypso's island by way of the Phaeacians, to whom he tells his wanderings, and how with his son Telemachus and two loyal servants he kills the suitors who have been wasting his house and wooing his wife Penelope (see [[the-return-of-odysseus]]). Like the Iliad it is in hexameters and divided into twenty-four books, each named by a letter of the alphabet; the first is α. This library contains its first two books in Greek and English.

## Outside Homer

Aristotle gives the story of the Odyssey in a few lines, the rest being episodes, and praises Homer for building it, like the Iliad, around one action (*Poetics* 1451a22–29, 1455b16–23; see [[aristotle-the-story-of-the-odyssey]]). Alcidamas called it “a beautiful mirror of human life”, a metaphor that Aristotle finds too poetic to persuade (*Rhetoric* 1406b11–14; see [[alcidamas]]). Aelian, writing that the ancients sang Homer's poems in separate parts, names for the Odyssey “the events at Pylos”, “the events at Lacedaemon”, “the cave of Calypso”, “the raft”, “the tales to Alcinous”, the “Cyclopeia”, the “Nekyia”, “the events with Circe”, the “Washing”, “the killing of the suitors”, “the events in the country” and “the events at Laertes' farm” (*Historical Miscellany* 13.14; see [[aelian]]). His list begins at Pylos, and has no part for the first two books, the council of the gods, Athena's visit to Telemachus and the assembly of the Ithacans.

The poem was put into Latin by Livius Andronicus (see [[odusia]]), and Horace twice turned its opening lines into Latin, as the model of a modest beginning and as the example of Ulysses' virtue and wisdom (see [[horace-on-the-opening-of-the-odyssey]] and [[in-medias-res]]). Ovid gave Penelope a letter to the absent Ulysses (see [[ovid-penelopes-letter]]), and Fénelon carried on the journey of Telemachus in French prose, with Minerva in the form of Mentor as his guide (see [[athena-as-mentor-in-later-writers]]).
