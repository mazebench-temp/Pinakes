Cresphontes, king of Messenia, was murdered by Polyphontes, who took his wife Merope; their youngest son, saved and raised abroad, returned in disguise claiming to have killed himself, and Merope, believing him the killer, was about to strike him when an old servant recognized him. Plutarch describes the audience rising in terror at the moment (*On the Eating of Flesh* 998e). Papyri preserve parts of the play (see [[merope]], [[cresphontes]] and [[merope-and-her-son]]).
