summary: Euripides' play (c. 414–412 BCE) in which Iphigenia, rescued from sacrifice at Aulis and made priestess of Artemis among the Taurians, is about to sacrifice a stranger who proves to be her brother Orestes. Aristotle praises its recognitions and outlines its plot.

Orestes and Pylades come to the land of the Taurians to steal the image of Artemis, are captured, and are brought to Iphigenia for sacrifice. She offers to spare one of them if he will carry a letter to her brother in Argos; the letter reveals her to Orestes, and he proves himself to her by recalling things from their home. She deceives the king, pretending that the stranger, a matricide, and the image he has touched must be purified in the sea, and they escape with Athena's help (see [[iphigenia]] and [[the-recognition-of-orestes-and-iphigenia]]).
