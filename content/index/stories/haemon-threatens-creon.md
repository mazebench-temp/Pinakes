## The story

After the brothers Eteocles and Polynices had killed each other at Thebes, Creon, the new ruler, forbade the burial of Polynices, who had brought an army against his own city. Antigone buried her brother and was condemned to be shut up alive in a rock-cut tomb. Haemon, Creon's son and her betrothed, pleaded with his father and left in anger. When Creon, warned by the seer Tiresias, came to free her, he found that she had hanged herself, and Haemon clinging to her body. Haemon spat in his father's face, drew his two-edged sword and lunged at him; Creon fled, the blow missed, and Haemon, angry with himself, drove the sword into his own side and died embracing her (Sophocles, *Antigone* 1192–1243; see [[antigone-play]]). Creon's wife Eurydice killed herself when she heard the news.
