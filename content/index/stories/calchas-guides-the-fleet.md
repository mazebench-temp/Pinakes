## The story

{{quote:826b195b-6f5c-551d-9e2c-0d1f5b52dc7f}}

Two lines of backstory establish Calchas' authority before he speaks. He has served the expedition from the start, and his skill comes from the very god whose anger he must now explain.

## In the poem

The detail makes a quiet irony. Apollo gave Calchas his prophecy, and Apollo is now killing the Achaeans. Calchas' knowledge of "the things that are, the things to come, and the things that were before" (1.70) is what makes him dangerous to a king.

## Outside Homer

The *Cypria* told of the gathering at Aulis, where Calchas interpreted the omen of the snake and the sparrows (recalled in Iliad 2.299–332). In the *Cypria* he also declared that Agamemnon must sacrifice Iphigenia to Artemis before the fleet could sail. See [[calchas-evil-prophecies]].
