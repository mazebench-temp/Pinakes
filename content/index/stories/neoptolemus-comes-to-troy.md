## The story

Achilles speaks of “my dear son, who is being reared for me in Scyros” (*Iliad* 19.326–327). In the Little Iliad, Odysseus brought Neoptolemus from Scyros and gave him his father's arms, and Achilles appeared to him (Proclus, summary of the *Little Iliad*). Odysseus tells the shade of Achilles how the boy proved himself: in council he spoke first and never missed the mark, surpassed only by Nestor and Odysseus; in battle he killed many, Eurypylus above all; and in the wooden horse, where other leaders wept and trembled, he never paled (*Odyssey* 11.506–537; see [[neoptolemus]] and [[scyros]]).
