## The story

Circe warned Odysseus of the strait. On one side lives Scylla, a monster with twelve feet and six long necks, each with a head and three rows of teeth, who takes a man in each mouth from every ship that passes; on the other Charybdis, who sucks down the sea three times a day. Better to lose six men than all, she told him (*Odyssey* 12.73–126; see [[circe]]). Odysseus armed himself, forgetting her advice not to fight, and while the crew watched Charybdis, Scylla took six of the strongest from the ship. They cried out to him by name for the last time, and she devoured them at her door, shrieking and stretching out their hands to him: “that was the most pitiable thing my eyes saw of all I suffered, searching out the paths of the sea” (*Odyssey* 12.222–259; see [[scylla]]).
