## The story

In Aeschylus' *Libation Bearers* Orestes, back from exile with Pylades, lays a lock of his hair on his father's tomb, and the two hide as Electra comes with libations sent by Clytemnestra. She finds the lock, like her own hair, and footprints that match her own, and reasons that Orestes must have sent it, or come himself; Orestes steps forward and proves himself by the weaving that her own hands made for him (*Libation Bearers* 164–234; see [[libation-bearers]], [[electra]] and [[pylades]]). Euripides' *Electra* mocks these tokens: an old servant urges the same signs on Electra, and she dismisses them one by one, until he recognizes Orestes by a scar on his brow (*Electra* 509–581). In Sophocles' *Electra*, Electra mourns over an urn that she believes holds her brother's ashes before he makes himself known (1098–1231; see [[electra-play]]).
