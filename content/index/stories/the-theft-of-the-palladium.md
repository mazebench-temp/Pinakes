## The story

The Palladium was a wooden image of Pallas, three cubits high, with its feet together, a spear raised in its right hand and a distaff and spindle in its left, which Zeus had thrown down from heaven to Ilus when he founded the city (Apollodorus 3.12.3). Helenus, captured by Odysseus, revealed that Troy could not be taken while it remained inside the walls (Apollodorus, *Epitome* 5.10; see [[helenus]]). The Little Iliad told how Odysseus and Diomedes carried it off (Proclus, summary of the *Little Iliad*; see [[odysseus]] and [[diomedes]]). The chorus of Sophocles' *Laconian Women* seem to have been Helen's Spartan handmaids, and the play to have told of the theft (see [[laconian-women]]). The image of Athena on the citadel in the Iliad, on whose knees Theano lays the Trojan women's robe, is a seated statue (6.297–311; see [[the-robe-for-athena]]).
