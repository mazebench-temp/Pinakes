Half a line gives a whole childhood: the goddess in the mortal household, telling stories of Olympus, and the boy listening. By the time of the Iliad she lives in the sea with her father (1.358).

{{notes}}

## Elsewhere in Homer

The marriage was the gods' doing. Achilles wishes that his mother had stayed among the immortal goddesses of the sea and that Peleus had married a mortal wife (18.86–87); Thetis tells Hephaestus that Zeus subdued her, alone of the sea-goddesses, to a mortal man, and that she endured his bed much against her will (18.432–434); and Hera says that she herself reared Thetis and gave her to Peleus, and that all the gods came to the wedding (24.59–63). On that day, Achilles says, the gods gave Peleus the armor that Achilles brought to Troy (18.84–85; see [[the-armor-of-peleus]]).

## Outside Homer

Apollodorus tells the wedding. Zeus and Poseidon had contended for Thetis, but held back when Themis prophesied that her son would be mightier than his father; some said that Zeus gave her to a mortal in anger because she would not lie with him, having been reared by Hera. Chiron advised Peleus to seize her and hold her fast while she changed her shape, and Peleus held her as she became fire, water and a beast, until she took her own form again. They were married on Pelion, where the gods feasted and sang, and Chiron gave Peleus an ash spear and Poseidon the immortal horses Balius and Xanthus (*Library* 3.13.5; see [[apollodorus]], [[the-pelian-ash]] and [[the-horses-of-achilles]]). A later story tells why she left: she tried to make the infant Achilles immortal by fire, Peleus cried out when he saw it, and she went back to the sea (Apollodorus, *Library* 3.13.6).
