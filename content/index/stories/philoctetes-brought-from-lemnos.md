## The story

The Iliad already looks forward to it: Philoctetes lies on Lemnos in agony from the wound of a water snake, “but soon they were to remember, the Argives beside their ships, lord Philoctetes” (2.721–725; see [[philoctetes-on-lemnos]]). In the Little Iliad, Odysseus captured the Trojan seer Helenus, who prophesied how Troy would be taken; Diomedes brought Philoctetes from Lemnos, Machaon healed him, and he killed Paris in single combat (Proclus, summary of the *Little Iliad*). Each of the three tragedians wrote a *Philoctetes*. In Sophocles' play, the only one that survives (409 BCE), Odysseus brings the young Neoptolemus to trick the hero out of his bow, Neoptolemus repents, and only Heracles, appearing from the gods, persuades Philoctetes to go to Troy (see [[philoctetes-plays]] and [[philoctetes]]).
