## The story

{{quote:786662ac-4809-529e-9743-b75e65236fa6}}

The proem dates the whole poem from this quarrel. It unfolds in the assembly in stages:

1. **The demand** (1.101–120). Agamemnon will give up Chryseis if he is given a prize at once.
2. **The first exchange** (1.121–147). Achilles says there is nothing left to divide. Agamemnon threatens to take Achilles', Ajax's or Odysseus' prize.
3. **The break** (1.148–187). Achilles says he fought only for Agamemnon's honor and will go home. Agamemnon tells him to go, and says he will take Briseis.
4. **The sword** (1.188–222). Athena stops Achilles from killing him.
5. **The oath** (1.223–246). Achilles swears on the scepter and flings it down.
6. **Nestor** (1.247–303). The old man tries to reconcile them and fails.

{{quote:70188a4f-f10d-539a-a2db-e57ebbb7090d}}

## In the poem

Both men are right by their own measure. Agamemnon cannot be the one Achaean without a prize: "that would not be fitting" (1.119). Achilles cannot accept having his prize taken by a man who fights less. The quarrel is about τιμή, honor, and its visible token, the γέρας, the prize.
