## The story

The Cypria told that Agamemnon, hunting at Aulis, shot a deer and boasted that he surpassed Artemis. The angry goddess held the fleet back with storms, and Calchas declared that Iphigenia must be sacrificed to her. They sent for her as if to marry Achilles, and were about to sacrifice her when Artemis snatched her away to the Taurians, put a deer on the altar in her place, and made her immortal (Proclus, summary of the *Cypria*; see [[aulis]] and [[artemis]]). In Aeschylus the sacrifice is carried out, the girl gagged so that she cannot curse her father, and it is the first cause of Clytemnestra's hatred (*Agamemnon* 184–247). Euripides' *Iphigenia at Aulis* shows her pleading for her life and then offering it willingly for Greece (see [[iphigenia-at-aulis]] and [[iphigenia]]). Homer does not tell the story; the Iliad names Agamemnon's daughters as Chrysothemis, Laodice and Iphianassa (9.145), and Agamemnon's complaint that Calchas has never prophesied anything good for him (1.106–108) has been read as a memory of it (see [[calchas-evil-prophecies]]).
