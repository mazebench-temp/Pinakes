## The story

Odysseus saw her among the shades. Tyro, daughter of Salmoneus and wife of Cretheus, loved the river Enipeus, and Poseidon, taking his likeness, lay with her at its mouth while a dark wave arched over them like a mountain. She bore Pelias and Neleus, who became kings of Iolcus and of Pylos (*Odyssey* 11.235–257; see [[pelias]] and [[neleus]]). Later writers tell how she exposed the twins. A horse-herder found them, and a mare kicked one in the face and left a livid mark, from which he was named Pelias. Grown, the brothers learned who their mother was and killed Sidero, the stepmother who had ill-treated her, at the altar of Hera (Apollodorus 1.9.8; see [[tyro]]). Sophocles wrote two plays named *Tyro*, in which the sons were recognized through the little boat in which their mother had set them adrift (see [[tyro-play]] and [[the-boat-of-tyro]]).
