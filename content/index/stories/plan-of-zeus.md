## The story

{{quote:86460625-f50f-58a7-990b-6330c318d18b}}

Διὸς δ' ἐτελείετο βουλή: "and the plan of Zeus was being fulfilled". The imperfect ἐτελείετο means "was being brought to completion": the plan unfolds through all the suffering the proem lists.

## Two readings

**The plan inside the poem.** Zeus' plan is the promise he makes to Thetis at 1.524–530, to honor Achilles by giving the Trojans the upper hand. Everything the proem lists (the souls sent to Hades, the bodies left for dogs) is the working out of that nod. This is the reading most modern commentators prefer. The words "from the time when first the two of them stood apart in strife" (1.6) fit it well.

**The plan before the poem.** An ancient scholion on this line quotes the opening of the *Cypria*, the epic that told how the war began. Earth was weighed down by the numberless tribes of men, and Zeus took pity on her. He planned the Trojan War so that the load of men might be lightened by death, "and the plan of Zeus was fulfilled". On this reading the phrase points beyond the Iliad to a cosmic design of which Achilles' wrath is one part.

Homer does not settle it. The phrase opens onto both scales, the quarrel and the whole war.

## Outside Homer

The *Cypria* fragment is known from the scholia (Cypria fr. 1 in modern editions). The idea of the war as a relief for an overcrowded earth reappears in Euripides (*Helen* 36–41; *Orestes* 1639–1642). See [[cypria-plan-of-zeus]].
