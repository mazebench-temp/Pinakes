## The story

Achilles tells the story to Priam to persuade him to eat: even Niobe of the lovely hair thought of food, though her twelve children, six daughters and six sons, had died in her halls. She had compared herself with Leto, who had borne only two; and those two, Apollo with his silver bow and Artemis with her arrows, killed them all. For nine days they lay in their blood, since Zeus had turned the people to stone, and on the tenth the gods buried them. Niobe herself, now a stone among the crags of Sipylus, still broods on the sorrows the gods sent her (*Iliad* 24.602–617; see [[niobe]]). Later writers make her a daughter of Tantalus and the wife of Amphion, one of the two brothers who walled Thebes, and give her seven sons and seven daughters (Apollodorus 3.5.6).

Aeschylus' *Niobe* began with the mother seated at her children's tomb, veiled and silent, a silence that Aristophanes mocks in the *Frogs* (911–920; see [[niobe-play]]).
