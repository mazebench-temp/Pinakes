## The story

Polyphontes, another descendant of Heracles, killed Cresphontes and two of his sons, took the kingdom and forced the widow, Merope, to marry him. Her youngest son, saved as an infant and brought up in Aetolia, came back as a young man and presented himself to Polyphontes as the killer of Cresphontes' son, claiming the reward. Merope, believing him her son's murderer, came at night with an axe to kill him as he slept, and was stopped by the old servant who had carried the child away and now recognized him; mother and son then killed Polyphontes at a sacrifice (Hyginus, *Fabulae* 137; see [[merope]] and [[cresphontes]]). In Euripides' *Cresphontes* the son bears his father's name; Apollodorus calls him Aepytus (2.8.5), Hyginus Telephontes. Plutarch describes the terror of the audience as Merope raised the axe (*On the Eating of Flesh* 2.5, 998e; see [[cresphontes-play]]).
