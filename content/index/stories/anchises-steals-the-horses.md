## The story

{{quote:cf5e5c11-2895-5b03-b124-a1d45373dffb}}

The theft is a breeder's theft. Anchises did not take Laomedon's horses. He “stole a strain” (τῆς γενεῆς ἔκλεψεν) from the breed, “putting his mares to them” (ὑποσχὼν θήλεας ἵππους), and he did it “without Laomedon's knowledge” (λάθρῃ Λαομέδοντος). The six foals were born “in his halls”, and he reared four himself “at the manger” (ἐπὶ φάτνῃ). The other two he gave to Aeneas, and Diomedes calls them μήστωρε φόβοιο, “masters of rout” (5.272; see [[master-of-rout]]). The breed was the one that Zeus gave Tros for his son Ganymede (5.265–267; see [[ganymede-and-the-horses-of-tros]]).

Anchises and Laomedon are kinsmen. Both are descended from Tros, Laomedon through Ilus and Anchises through Assaracus, and Laomedon is the older by a generation (20.230–240; see [[anchises]] and [[laomedon]]). The theft took place in Laomedon's reign, before the war, and it is told only here.

## In the poem

Diomedes tells it to Sthenelus as a reason to seize Aeneas' horses (5.259–273), and Sthenelus does so when Aeneas falls (5.319–327). The Achaeans know the pedigree of their enemies' horses as they know the pedigree of their enemies. The story also gives a picture of the house of Troy before the war. Laomedon, who cheated gods and heroes of what he owed them (21.441–457, 5.649–651; see [[heracles-sacks-troy]]), was himself cheated by his own kinsman.

Aeneas' horses pass from the Trojan royal house to Diomedes, as Aeneas himself nearly passes from life. His mother saves him; his horses are taken. Diomedes keeps them for the rest of the poem and wins the chariot race with them at the funeral games of Patroclus (23.290–292, 23.499–513).
