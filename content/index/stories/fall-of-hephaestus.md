## The story

{{quote:b2b207ac-383e-519a-93c3-e8c20c7e4877}}

ῥῖψε ποδὸς τεταγών: "seizing me by the foot, he hurled me". βηλὸς θεσπέσιος: "the divine threshold" of Olympus. The fall lasts a whole day, measured by the sun.

## In the poem

Like Nestor's story, it is a paradigm, told to make someone yield. Hephaestus tells Hera not to provoke Zeus, because he could not protect her: he tried once, and this is what happened. It works. Hera smiles, the gods laugh, and the feast resumes. The same kind of story that failed with Achilles and Agamemnon succeeds on Olympus.

The story also explains Hephaestus himself: his lameness is left unstated, but his fall and his bond with Lemnos are told.

## Outside Homer

In Iliad 18.395–405 Hephaestus tells a different fall. Hera threw him out because he was born lame, and Thetis and Eurynome caught him and kept him hidden for nine years in a cave by Oceanus, where he made jewelry. Plato's Socrates would censor the Book 1 version: "Hephaestus being hurled by his father when he was about to defend his mother being beaten" (*Republic* 2.378d). See [[plato-hephaestus-hurled]]. A later story, well known from vase painting, told how Dionysus brought him back to Olympus drunk, riding a donkey.
