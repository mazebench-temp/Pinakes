## The story

Amphiaraus, a seer, foresaw that the expedition of the Seven would end in disaster and refused to join it. But he had sworn to let his wife Eriphyle settle any dispute between him and Adrastus, and Polynices bribed her with the necklace of Harmonia; she sent her husband to Thebes, where the earth swallowed him with his chariot (Apollodorus 3.6.2–8; see [[the-seven-against-thebes]] and [[amphiaraus]]). The Odyssey remembers “hateful Eriphyle, who took precious gold for her own husband” (*Odyssey* 11.326–327), and the seer who “died at Thebes because of a woman's gifts” (*Odyssey* 15.247). Amphiaraus had charged his sons to avenge him. Alcmaeon led the Epigoni against Thebes (see [[the-epigonoi]]), and afterward, on an oracle of Apollo, killed his mother. Pursued by her Furies, he wandered until he settled where the oracle sent him, on land that the sun had not yet seen when he killed his mother, the new ground laid down by the silt of the Achelous (Apollodorus 3.7.5; Thucydides 2.102.5–6; see [[alcmaeon]] and [[eriphyle]]).

Euripides wrote two plays on Alcmaeon. In the *Nicomachean Ethics* Aristotle calls absurd the reasons that “compelled” Euripides' Alcmaeon to kill his mother (3.1, 1110a27–29).
