## The story

Auge, daughter of Aleus, king of Tegea in Arcadia, bore a son to Heracles, and the child, exposed on Mount Parthenion, was suckled by a doe and named Telephus. Grown up, he killed his mother's brothers (Hyginus, *Fabulae* 244), and on the advice of Delphi went to Mysia, where his mother had been given in marriage to King Teuthras; Teuthras adopted him and made him his heir (Apollodorus 2.7.4, 3.9.1; see [[telephus]], [[tegea]], [[mysia]] and [[teuthras]]). One who had shed a kinsman's blood was polluted, and might not speak to others until he had been purified, and so tragedy showed Telephus making the long journey from Tegea to Mysia in silence (see [[mysians-play]] and [[purification]]). Years later, when the Greeks sailing for Troy landed in Mysia by mistake, he was wounded by Achilles, and was healed at last by the rust of the spear that had wounded him (Proclus, summary of the *Cypria*; Apollodorus, *Epitome* 3.17–20).
