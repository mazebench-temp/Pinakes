## The story

{{quote:c6d87825-e770-5354-9ebc-05a5190bc82d}}

ᾧ καὶ τόξον Ἀπόλλων αὐτὸς ἔδωκεν, "to whom Apollo himself had given his bow". καί ("even") and αὐτός ("himself") give weight to the gift. Apollo is the archer god, and the bow is his weapon ([[apollo]], [[bow]]).

Book 4 tells a different story of the bow. Pandarus made it himself from the horns of a wild goat that he had shot from ambush; the horns were sixteen palms long, and a horn-worker smoothed and joined them and tipped them with gold (4.105–111). The two accounts can be reconciled if Apollo's gift is the skill to use the bow rather than the weapon itself. Apollo gives the bow in that sense to others too: Ajax asks Teucer where his bow is, "which Phoebus Apollo gave you" (15.440–441).

## In the poem

The Catalogue names Pandarus with his bow because the bow is his part in the story. In Book 4 Athena persuades him to shoot Menelaus during the truce. He prays to "Apollo, the Lycian-born, famed for his bow", vows him a hecatomb of firstborn lambs, and wounds Menelaus, breaking the oaths (4.93–147). In Book 5 the bow fails him. He has hit two of the best Achaeans, Menelaus and Diomedes, and only roused them. If he ever gets home, he says, someone may cut off his head if he does not break the bow and throw it into the fire (5.206–216). Diomedes kills him before he can (5.290–296). See [[pandarus]].
