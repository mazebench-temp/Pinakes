## The story

Hesiod names two Graeae, Pemphredo and Enyo, daughters of Phorcys and Ceto, gray-haired from birth (*Theogony* 270–273). Aeschylus describes three, “old maidens, swan-shaped, with one eye between them and a single tooth, whom neither the sun looks upon with its rays nor the moon by night” (*Prometheus Bound* 794–797). Perseus, sent to fetch the head of Medusa, came to them first. He took the eye and the tooth as they passed them from one to another, and gave them back only when they had shown him the way to the nymphs who kept the winged sandals, the pouch and the cap of Hades (Apollodorus 2.4.2; see [[perseus]] and [[phorcys-the-sea-god]]). In Aeschylus' version the sisters were the Gorgons' guards, and Perseus threw their eye into Lake Tritonis ([Eratosthenes], *Catasterisms* 22; see [[daughters-of-phorcys]]).
