## The story

{{quote:92894c35-1783-5479-be06-28d0b03ac23f}}

[[Erechtheus]] had no human parents. The earth bore him, and [[Athena]], daughter of Zeus, raised him and gave him a home in her own temple at [[Athens]]. There the [[Athenians]] honor him with sacrifices of bulls and rams every year.

## In the poem

This is how the Catalogue introduces Athens. It defines the city by its first king, its goddess and its cult, and names no other place in Attica. It is also one of the most local details in the whole Catalogue, a yearly rite of a named people, and that has made the passage a favorite piece of evidence for Athenian interest in the text (see [[solon-and-salamis]]).

The lines bind hero and goddess closely. Athena raises the child and the temple is hers, but the pronoun in 2.550 leaves it open whether the yearly victims are for him or for her. In the Odyssey her house at Athens is "the strong house of Erechtheus" (Od. 7.80–81).

## Outside Homer

Later Athenians told the story of Erichthonius. Hephaestus pursued Athena, his seed fell on the earth, and the earth bore a child, whom Athena took up, hid in a chest and entrusted to the daughters of Cecrops, who opened it against her orders (Euripides, *Ion* 20–26, 267–274; Apollodorus 3.14.6). Athenian vase painters of the fifth century BC show Earth rising from the ground to hand the child to Athena.
