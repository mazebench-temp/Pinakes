## The story

The Odyssey begins in the tenth year after the fall of Troy. Odysseus, the only survivor of his crew, is held on Calypso's island; on Ithaca more than a hundred suitors court Penelope and devour his wealth, and Telemachus, whom they plot to kill, goes to Pylos and Sparta for news of his father (*Odyssey* 1–4). Released at the gods' command, Odysseus is shipwrecked by Poseidon, who hates him for blinding his son the Cyclops, and reaches the Phaeacians, to whom he tells his wanderings (5–12; see [[odysseus-weeps-at-the-feast]]). They carry him home asleep (13; see [[odysseus-set-ashore-on-ithaca]]). Disguised by Athena as an old beggar, he reveals himself to Telemachus and to a few servants, is recognized by his old nurse (see [[odysseus-recognized-by-his-nurse]]), strings his bow in the contest for Penelope's hand, and with his son and two herdsmen kills the suitors (13–22). Penelope tests him by the secret of their bed; he goes out to his father Laertes, and Athena makes peace between him and the suitors' kin (23–24; see [[odyssey]], [[odysseus]] and [[the-suitors]]).
