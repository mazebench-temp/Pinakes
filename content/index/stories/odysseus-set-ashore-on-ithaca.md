## The story

The Phaeacian ship, swifter than a falcon, carries Odysseus through the night, while he sleeps a sweet sleep, unwaking, most like death (*Odyssey* 13.79–80). At dawn it runs ashore at the harbor of Phorcys on Ithaca. The sailors lift him out with his bedding, still asleep, lay him on the sand with the Phaeacians' gifts beside him, and sail for home, where Poseidon turns their ship to stone (*Odyssey* 13.96–187). Odysseus wakes not knowing where he is, until Athena, disguised as a young shepherd, tells him he is on Ithaca (*Odyssey* 13.187–360; see [[ithaca]]).
