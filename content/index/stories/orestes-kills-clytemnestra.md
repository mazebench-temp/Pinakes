## The story

The Odyssey holds Orestes up as a model son. Zeus himself recalls how Aegisthus married Agamemnon's wife and killed him on his return, though Hermes had warned him that Orestes would take vengeance when he came of age (*Odyssey* 1.29–43). In the eighth year Orestes came back from Athens and killed his father's murderer, and held a funeral feast for the Argives over his hateful mother and the coward Aegisthus (*Odyssey* 3.303–310; see [[the-murder-of-agamemnon]]). Homer leaves the manner of Clytemnestra's death unsaid. The tragedians made it the center of the story: in Aeschylus' *Libation Bearers* Orestes kills his mother at Apollo's command, after she has bared her breast to him, and is driven off by her Furies (see [[libation-bearers]]); Sophocles' *Electra* ends with the killings, and Euripides' *Electra* and *Orestes* show their horror and aftermath (see [[orestes]], [[clytemnestra]], [[electra-play]] and [[orestes-play]]).
