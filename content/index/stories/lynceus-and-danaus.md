## The story

Danaus and Aegyptus were brothers, descendants of Io. Danaus had fifty daughters and Aegyptus fifty sons. Fearing his brother, Danaus fled with his daughters from Egypt to Argos, the land of their ancestress, where the king received them as suppliants (Aeschylus, *Suppliants*). When the sons of Aegyptus followed and claimed the girls in marriage, Danaus gave each daughter a dagger, and on the wedding night they killed their husbands, all but Hypermnestra, who spared Lynceus. Danaus kept her a prisoner (Apollodorus 2.1.5), and in one account brought her to trial before the Argives, who acquitted her (Pausanias 2.19.6). Lynceus ruled Argos after Danaus, and from him and Hypermnestra descended Acrisius, Danae and Perseus (Apollodorus 2.2.1; see [[lynceus]], [[danaus]] and [[perseus]]).
