## The story

**Telepylus.** With no wind to carry them, the men rowed for six days, night and day alike, and on the seventh came “to the steep citadel of Lamus, / Telepylus of the Laestrygonians, where herdsman hails herdsman / as he drives his flock in, and the other answers as he drives his herd out”. There a man who took no sleep could have earned two wages, one by herding cattle and the other by pasturing sheep, “for the paths of night and of day are close together” (*Odyssey* 10.80–86; see [[odysseus-and-aeolus]], [[telepylus]], [[lamus]] and [[laestrygonians]]).

**The harbor.** The harbor was closed by cliffs, and the other captains took their ships inside; Odysseus did not:

{{quote:59e4a1aa-0844-564d-a2f7-5cdd951682f6}}

He climbed a rugged lookout. No works of oxen or of men were to be seen, only smoke rising from the land (*Odyssey* 10.97–99; see [[harbor]]).

**The scouts.** He sent two men and a herald to find out who lived there (*Odyssey* 10.100–102; see [[spies-and-scouts]] and [[heralds]]). They took a smooth road by which wagons brought wood down to the city from the mountains, and before the city they met a girl drawing water at the spring Artacia, “the strong daughter of Antiphates the Laestrygonian” (*Odyssey* 10.103–108; see [[artacia]]). They asked her who was king, and she pointed out the high-roofed house of her father. There they found his wife, “as huge as a mountain peak, and they shuddered at her” (*Odyssey* 10.109–113). She called from the place of assembly “famous Antiphates, / her husband, who devised a grim destruction for them”: at once he seized one of the companions and made ready his meal, and the other two fled to the ships (*Odyssey* 10.114–117; see [[antiphates-the-laestrygonian]]).

**The ships destroyed.** Antiphates raised a cry through the city:

{{quote:997324a9-fb5d-5b32-aad1-b66eb8b9c191}}

While they were killing the men inside the harbor, Odysseus drew his sword, cut the cables of his ship, and bade his companions lay to their oars, and they tossed up the sea, fearing destruction: “Gladly out to the open sea, away from the overhanging rocks, fled / my ship; but the others perished there in a body” (*Odyssey* 10.125–132; see [[giants]], [[like-fish]], [[stern-cables]] and [[sword]]). “From there we sailed on, grieving at heart, / glad to have escaped death, though we had lost our dear companions” (*Odyssey* 10.133–134; see [[from-there-we-sailed-on]]), and they came to the island of Aeaea (see [[odysseus-and-circe]]).

## In the poem

**One ship.** The adventure destroys the fleet. Twelve ships followed Odysseus from Troy (*Odyssey* 9.159); eleven perish here with their crews, and the rest of the tale is the story of a single ship. After the Cyclops' curse, Zeus “took no heed of the offering, / but was pondering how they might all perish, / my well-benched ships and my trusty companions” (*Odyssey* 9.553–555; see [[odysseus-and-the-cyclops]] and [[the-return-of-odysseus]]); the ships perish here, and the last ship with its crew after Thrinacia (see [[the-shipwreck-of-odysseus]]). The teller gives no reason for keeping his ship outside, and tells it in two verses, before the scouts set out (*Odyssey* 10.95–96); it is the one thing that saves any of the fleet. In the summary that he gives Penelope the adventure is the loss alone: he came to Telepylus of the Laestrygonians, who destroyed his ships and his well-greaved companions, all of them, and Odysseus alone escaped in his black ship (*Odyssey* 23.318–320, a plain rendering).

**The scouts.** The scouts are sent in the verses that sent them out among the Lotus-eaters (*Odyssey* 10.100–102 = 9.88–90; see [[the-lotus-eaters]]), and the outcome is turned around in the same words: the Lotus-eaters “devised no destruction for our companions” (οὐδʼ … μήδονθʼ ἑτάροισιν ὄλεθρον, *Odyssey* 9.92), Antiphates “devised a grim destruction for them” (ἐμήσατο λυγρὸν ὄλεθρον, *Odyssey* 10.115). A girl who meets strangers on their way to the city and shows them her father's house is a scene the poem knows from the other side: Nausicaa sends Odysseus to her father's house, and Athena meets him as a young girl carrying a water jar and leads him there (*Odyssey* 6.255–257, 7.19–20; see [[the-meeting-of-odysseus-and-nausicaa]] and [[odysseus-at-the-house-of-alcinous]]). Merry compares the daughters of Celeus, who meet Demeter as they come for water and take her to their father's house (*Homeric Hymn to Demeter* 105–106; W. W. Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 10.105). Here the king of the house makes a meal of his guest (*Odyssey* 10.116).

**Like the Cyclopes.** On Aeaea the companions weep, remembering “the deeds of Antiphates the Laestrygonian / and of the mighty, great-hearted Cyclops, the eater of men” (*Odyssey* 10.199–200). The two peoples are alike in eating men and in their size, and the Laestrygonians are “not like men, but like the Giants” (*Odyssey* 10.120); Alcinous names the Cyclopes and “the savage tribes of the Giants” together, as peoples near to the gods, like his own (*Odyssey* 7.205–206; see [[cyclopes]] and [[giants]]). But the Laestrygonians have a city, a road for wagons, a king and a place of assembly, while the Cyclopes “have no assemblies that bring counsel, and no ordinances” (*Odyssey* 9.112): as having a king and an assembly, Merry observes, they are more advanced in the externals of civilization (on 10.114). The Cyclops shut his guests in his cave and ate them two at a time; the Laestrygonians come down on the harbor in countless numbers and carry the men off, speared like fish, to a joyless meal (*Odyssey* 10.119–124).

## Outside Homer

Strabo reports that inhospitable peoples called Cyclopes and Laestrygonians were said to have held the country around Aetna and Leontini in Sicily (*Geography* 1.2.9; see [[strabo]]). Among the Romans the city was placed at Formiae on the coast of Latium: Cicero, writing from his villa there, calls the town “this Telepylus of the Laestrygonians”, Τηλέπυλον … Λαιστρυγονίην (*Letters to Atticus* 2.13.2; see [[cicero]]). For these and for later tellings of the story, see [[laestrygonians]], [[telepylus]] and [[lamus]].
