## The story

Odysseus tells it in the underworld, where the shade of Ajax stands apart from him, still angry over the judgment by the ships for the arms of Achilles, which “his lady mother set as a prize, and the sons of the Trojans and Pallas Athena judged”. “I wish I had not won in such a contest,” says Odysseus; Ajax does not answer, and goes off into the darkness (*Odyssey* 11.543–564). The Aethiopis ended with the quarrel over the arms, and the Little Iliad began with the judgment: Odysseus won by the will of Athena, and Ajax went mad, destroyed the Achaeans' plunder and killed himself (Proclus, summaries of the *Aethiopis* and the *Little Iliad*; see [[ajax]]). Aeschylus' *Judgment of Arms* and Sophocles' *Ajax*, which shows the hero's madness, his recovery and his death on his own sword, both came from this story (see [[the-judgment-of-arms-play]] and [[ajax-plays]]).
