## The story

Odysseus tells it among the dead, where the soul of Ajax stands apart from him, still angry over the victory that Odysseus won at the ships for the armor of Achilles: “his lady mother had set it as a prize, / and the sons of the Trojans were the judges, and Pallas Athena”. “Would that I had never been the victor for such a prize!” says Odysseus; Ajax answers nothing, and goes off into Erebus (*Odyssey* 11.543–564). The Aethiopis ended with the quarrel over the arms, and the Little Iliad began with the judgment: Odysseus won by the will of Athena, and Ajax went mad, destroyed the Achaeans' plunder and killed himself (Proclus, summaries of the *Aethiopis* and the *Little Iliad*; see [[ajax]]). Aeschylus' *Judgment of Arms* and Sophocles' *Ajax*, which shows the hero's madness, his recovery and his death on his own sword, both came from this story (see [[the-judgment-of-arms-play]] and [[ajax-plays]]).
