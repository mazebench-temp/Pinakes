## The story

{{quote:bf92e6b6-e0b8-5d95-88ff-c1ae38a348a6}}

[[Phyleus]], a prince of the Epeians of Elis, quarreled with his father and "went off to live in Dulichium" (ἀπενάσσατο, "moved away"). His son [[Meges]] rules there, and leads the islanders to Troy.

## In the poem

The line explains an anomaly. [[Dulichium]] and the [[Echinae]] lie in the Ionian Sea, next to the islands of Odysseus, yet their ruler is an Epeian by descent. At Troy Meges fights beside the Epeians (13.691–693). The Catalogue's story reconciles the two: the dynasty came from Elis, driven out by a family quarrel. Homer gives the quarrel in three words, πατρὶ χολωθείς, "angry with his father", and does not name the father.

## Outside Homer

Later mythographers tell the whole story. Augeas, king of Elis, promised Heracles a tenth of his cattle if he cleaned the dung from his yards in a single day. Heracles turned the Alpheus and the Peneius through them and did it, but Augeas refused to pay, saying the labor had been done at Eurystheus' command. When the dispute came to judgment, his son Phyleus testified for Heracles, and Augeas, in a rage, banished them both before the vote; Phyleus went to Dulichium (Apollodorus 2.5.5). Heracles later came back, killed Augeas and his sons, and recalled Phyleus to give him the kingdom of Elis (Apollodorus 2.7.2).
