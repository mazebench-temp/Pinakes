## The story

**The tools and the trees.** At dawn Odysseus puts on a cloak and a tunic, and the nymph her silver-white mantle, her golden girdle and her head covering (*Odyssey* 5.228–232; see [[cloak]], [[tunic]], [[girdle]] and [[veil]]). Then she plans his escort:

{{quote:ce5552ec-146d-51ca-8a26-dfd0fc948862}}

The πέλεκυς is the axe for felling, ἄρμενον ἐν παλάμῃσι, “that fitted his hands”, as the potter's wheel “fits his hands” (*Iliad* 18.600); “sharpened on both sides” means a double blade (W. W. Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 5.234, 5.235; see [[axe]]). The σκέπαρνον is the adze, for squaring and smoothing (Merry on 5.237; see [[adze]]). Calypso goes first on the way, ὁδοῖο being a local genitive, “she was his guide” (Merry on 5.237). The trees are alder, poplar and fir, “dry long since”: trees dead but still standing, which might serve in an emergency in place of properly seasoned wood (Merry on 5.240; see [[trees]]).

**The hull.** Calypso goes back to her house, and Odysseus works alone:

{{quote:bb431fe4-9a7a-5d44-96d7-d3426ec4fa71}}

πελέκκησεν, “hewed them”, is the work of the axe after the felling, the lopping of the limbs, and the work of the adze begins with ξέσσε, “smoothed them” (Merry on 5.244), straight to a line of chalk or ochre snapped along the wood (Merry on 5.245; see [[the-chalk-line]]). τέρετρα are small borers or gimlets used with one hand (Merry on 5.246; see [[augers]]). Odysseus bores every piece so that the hole in the upper piece matches the one in the lower, fits them in place, and “hammers the boat together with pegs and clamps” (Merry on 5.247); the grammarian Aristophanes held that the two verses say the same thing (the scholia, in Merry's critical note). The pegs are wooden trenails, and the fastenings perhaps a simple mortise and tenon (Merry on 5.248; see [[raft]] and [[harmonia]]). The breadth is a ship's: the broad freight ship is built for cargo, broad in the beam, not for speed (Merry, Appendix I §4; see [[ships]]).

**The deck, the mast and the sails.**

{{quote:71785625-0000-5b55-9f9e-a72895160ab9}}

Merry translates the first two verses “And he worked away, setting up the decks by fitting them to the close-set ribs, and he finished off with the long side-planking”, and the translation follows him (Merry on 5.252; see the translation's note on *Odyssey* 5.252–253 and [[deck]]). The mast carries a yard (ἐπίκριον), the steering oar is a single paddle at the stern, and the sails are set with braces, halyards and sheets (*Odyssey* 5.254–260; Merry, Appendix I §§14, 16, 17; see [[mast]], [[steering-oar]] and [[sails]]). The fence of osier withes is a bulwark of wattle all round, against the waves; the wood that he heaps upon it the scholia took for ballast, but ἐπιχέω means “pour over”, not “pour in”, and it is strange that the ballast should go in before the raft is dragged down to the sea, adding to the labor, unless the verses put the later event first (Merry, Appendix I §7). With μοχλοί a lever lifts and pushes rather than drags, and Merry thinks the word may mean “rollers” (on 5.261). “It was the end of the fourth day when all was now accomplished for him”, Merry renders the last verse, taking καί as joining the two clauses (on 5.262).

**The fifth day.** On the fifth day Calypso bathes him, clothes him in fragrant garments, puts on board a skin of dark wine, a great skin of water and provisions in a leather sack, with many meats, and sends a fair wind, harmless and warm (*Odyssey* 5.263–268; see [[bathing]], [[wine]] and [[provisions]]). Glad of the wind, he spreads his sails (*Odyssey* 5.269).

## In the poem

**Alone.** Zeus decreed that Odysseus should go home “with the escort neither of gods nor of mortal men”, on a raft (*Odyssey* 5.31–34; see [[zeus]] and [[raft]]), and the building makes the decree good. He has no ships with oars and no companions, as Athena and Calypso both say (*Odyssey* 5.16–17, 5.141–142), and the goddess gives him tools, cloth, food and a wind, but the work is his. Merry would not press the story for impossibilities, a man working single-handed, without a shipwright's tools, with a vessel ready for sea in four days: it comes in the middle of a marvelous tale, and the poet may elaborate as he goes, so that the details at the end are out of keeping with the simplicity of the beginning (Merry, Appendix I §1).

**Calypso's part.** The goddess who held him against his will now speeds him (*Odyssey* 5.14–15, 5.161). She walks ahead to the far edge of the island and shows him the trees, goes home, and twice comes back “meanwhile” (τόφρα), with the augers and with the cloth (*Odyssey* 5.237–246, 5.258; see [[calypso]]). Odysseus had suspected some other plan in her command, and built only after her oath (*Odyssey* 5.173–191; see [[oath]]).

**The craftsman.** The skills of the raft are the skills of the bed in Ithaca. Odysseus tells Penelope how he cut back the olive that grew in the court, trimmed the trunk with the bronze “well and skillfully”, made it straight to the chalk line, and bored it all with an auger, and from it built the bed (*Odyssey* 23.195–199, a plain rendering; see [[augers]]). The verse of the chalk line is used too of the carpenters who made the doorpost of the hall and the threshold of the storeroom (*Odyssey* 17.340–341, 21.43–44). The freight ship that measures the raft measures the Cyclops' club as well, as long and as thick as the mast of a black ship of twenty oars, a broad freight ship (*Odyssey* 9.322–323).

**Days of work.** The building fills the four days after Hermes' visit, from the dawn of *Odyssey* 5.228 to the fourth day of 5.262, and Odysseus sails on the fifth (*Odyssey* 5.263). In his account to Arete he says only that Calypso sent him “on a raft of many bindings” (*Odyssey* 7.264, a plain rendering), not that he built it.

**The title.** Aelian, listing the names by which the ancients sang parts of the Odyssey on their own, gives “the cave of Calypso, and the events about the raft” (*Historical Miscellany* 13.14, as [[the-title-of-odyssey-3]] gives the list; see [[the-title-of-odyssey-5]] and [[calypsos-cave]]).
