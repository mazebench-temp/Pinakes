## The story

The name κωμῳδία was usually taken to mean the song of the κῶμος, the band of revelers in the god's procession. At the Rural Dionysia of Attica a phallus was carried in procession with a song to Phales, the god's companion, as Aristophanes shows in the *Acharnians* (237–279; see [[phallic-songs]]). The Megarians claimed comedy, both those of Megara near Athens and those of Megara in Sicily, where Epicharmus composed comedies with plots in the early fifth century (see [[megarians]] and [[epicharmus]]). At Athens a contest for comedy was added to the City Dionysia, by the usual reckoning in 486 BCE; Chionides and Magnes were among the first victors, and Crates, Cratinus, Eupolis and Aristophanes followed (see [[chionides]], [[magnes]], [[crates]] and [[aristophanes]]).
