## The story

Tragedy was performed at Athens at the festival of Dionysus, by a chorus and actors in masks, and the Greeks themselves disagreed about its beginnings. Herodotus tells of “tragic choruses” at Sicyon in honor of the hero Adrastus, which the tyrant Cleisthenes transferred to Dionysus (5.67), and says that Arion of Methymna was the first to compose a dithyramb, give it a name and produce it, at Corinth (1.23; see [[dithyramb]]). Later tradition named Thespis as the first tragic poet and dated his first production at Athens to the 530s BCE (the Parian Marble); an Aristotelian tradition, reported by Themistius, credited him with adding the prologue and the set speech to the songs of the chorus (*Orations* 26, 316d). Aeschylus' first production was in 499 BCE, and his surviving plays, from the *Persians* (472) to the *Oresteia* (458), use two actors and then three; the third is credited to Sophocles (see [[aeschylus]] and [[sophocles]]). The Dorians of the Peloponnese claimed tragedy for themselves (see [[dorians]]).
