## The story

{{quote:0410b1b9-b02d-54d2-84d5-100927d57bd6}}

[[Polydora]], the daughter of [[Peleus]], lay with a god, the river [[Spercheius]], and bore him a son, [[menesthius-son-of-spercheius|Menesthius]]. But [[borus-son-of-perieres|Borus]], son of Perieres, married her openly, after giving a boundless bride-price, and the boy was called by his name. He grew up to lead the first of the five companies of the Myrmidons at Troy.

## In the poem

The story is the first of the short genealogies with which the narrator introduces the five leaders of the Myrmidons as they go out to battle with Patroclus (16.168–197; see [[myrmidons]]). It is told in six verses, and the second leader has a story of the same shape: Eudorus, too, is a god's son, born to a girl who was afterward married by a mortal (16.179–192; see [[the-birth-of-eudorus]]). The two stories have the same order, the god's union first and then the man's marriage with its gifts, and in both the child is brought up as a mortal's son. ἐπίκλησιν, “by name” (16.177), marks the division: by birth Menesthius is the son of the river, by name the son of Borus.

The verses set the birth inside Achilles' family. If Peleus is Achilles' father, Polydora is Achilles' sister and Menesthius his nephew, and the river is the one to which Peleus vowed Achilles' hair, the god of his home country (23.141–151; see [[spercheius]]). The poem says nothing of either connection. A river god founds a mortal line elsewhere in the Iliad: the Alpheus is the grandfather of Diocles (5.544–547), the Axius of Asteropaeus (21.157–159; see [[alpheus]] and [[axius]]).

Later in the same book Hera warns Zeus that if he saves Sarpedon, his own son, other gods will wish to save theirs, “for many are fighting around the great city of Priam, / sons of immortals” (16.448–449; see [[the-death-of-sarpedon]]). The catalogue of the Myrmidons has just named two of them.

## Outside Homer

A scholium on 16.175 says that Hesiod and the others called Peleus' daughter Polydora (Hesiod, fr. 60 Evelyn-White), and Walter Leaf reports from it that Zenodotus called her Cleodora (*Commentary on the Iliad*, 1900, on 16.175). Apollodorus names her mother, Antigone, daughter of Eurytion, whom Peleus married when he fled to Phthia, and her husband, “Borus, son of Perieres” (*Library* 3.13.1). Strabo cites Menesthius, “son of the Spercheius and of Achilles' sister”, as proof that the river ran through Achilles' kingdom (*Geography* 9.5.9).
