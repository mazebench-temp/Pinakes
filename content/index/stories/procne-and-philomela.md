## The story

Pandion, king of Athens, gave his daughter Procne in marriage to Tereus, a Thracian who had helped him in a war. Tereus seduced her sister Philomela, cut out her tongue so that she could not tell, and hid her away; but Philomela wove the story into a robe and sent it to her sister. Procne killed her son Itys, cooked him and served him to Tereus, and the sisters fled. When Tereus pursued them with an axe, the gods turned Procne into a nightingale, Philomela into a swallow and Tereus into a hoopoe (Apollodorus 3.14.8; see [[tereus]]); Latin poets make Philomela the nightingale (Ovid, *Metamorphoses* 6.424–674). The Odyssey has an older form of the story, in which the nightingale is the daughter of Pandareus, who killed her son Itylus in her folly and mourns him in her song (*Odyssey* 19.518–523). Sophocles' *Tereus* made the tale famous at Athens (see [[tereus-play]] and [[the-voice-of-the-shuttle]]).
