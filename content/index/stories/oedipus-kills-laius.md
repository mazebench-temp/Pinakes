## The story

An oracle warned Laius, king of Thebes, that he would die at the hands of a son. When a son was born, he pinned the baby's ankles together and had him exposed on Mount Cithaeron; but the herdsman gave the child to a shepherd from Corinth, and the childless King Polybus raised him as his own (Sophocles, *Oedipus the King* 711–725, 1022–1045; see [[oedipus-the-king]]). Grown up, and taunted that he was not Polybus' son, Oedipus went to Delphi and heard that he would kill his father and marry his mother. He resolved never to return to Corinth. At the place where the roads from Delphi and Daulis meet, an old man in a carriage, with a herald and attendants, tried to drive him off the road; in anger Oedipus killed him and all his attendants but one, who escaped (*Oedipus the King* 779–813). The old man was Laius, on his way to Delphi (see [[laius]] and [[oedipus]]). The Odyssey says only that Oedipus killed his father and married his mother (*Odyssey* 11.271–274).
