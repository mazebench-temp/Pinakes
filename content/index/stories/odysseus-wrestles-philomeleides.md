## The story

Homer tells it in three verses, inside a wish of Menelaus:

{{quote:433cb27a-1645-5c36-b446-9219162ca416}}

Telemachus repeats the whole speech to his mother when he is home again, and the wish with it (*Odyssey* 17.124–137). ἐξ ἔριδος, “out of strife”, Merry renders “in a match” or “after a challenge”, comparing Agamemnon's warning to Menelaus in the Iliad, “and do not wish, out of strife, to fight a man better than you” (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 4.343; *Iliad* 7.111). The poem does not say when the Achaeans were at Lesbos. Achilles took the island during the war (*Iliad* 9.128–130), and on the voyage home Nestor and Diomedes were at Lesbos, choosing their course, when Menelaus overtook them (*Odyssey* 3.168–172; see [[lesbos]] and [[the-returns-of-the-achaeans]]).

Odysseus' one bout in the Iliad is with Telamonian Ajax at the funeral games for Patroclus: after two falls in which both men go down together, Achilles stops them, “victory is for both” (*Iliad* 23.700–739; see [[the-funeral-games-of-patroclus]] and [[odysseus]]).

## Outside Homer

Eustathius, as Merry reports him, gives the account of “the more accurate” writers: Philomeleides was a king of Lesbos who used to challenge passers-by to wrestle, and he challenged the Achaeans too when they put in there (Merry on 4.343; see [[philomeleides]]).
