## The story

This is the plot of Euripides' *Iphigenia among the Taurians*. Artemis had carried Iphigenia from the altar at Aulis to the land of the Taurians on the Black Sea, where as priestess of the goddess she must consecrate for sacrifice every Greek stranger who comes ashore (see [[the-sacrifice-of-iphigenia]] and [[human-sacrifice]]). Orestes, still pursued by some of the Furies after his trial at Athens, is told by Apollo to bring the goddess' image from the Taurians to Attica. Seized on the beach after a fit of madness, he and Pylades are brought to the priestess. She offers to spare one of them if he will carry a letter to Argos, and reads it aloud in case it should be lost, so that Orestes learns who she is; he proves himself to her by what he knows of their home, the weaving of the quarrel of Atreus and Thyestes and the ancient spear of Pelops hidden in her room. To escape, she tells the king that the strangers and the image must be purified in the sea, and they sail away with the image; Athena, appearing at the end, settles the goddess' cult in Attica (see [[iphigenia-among-the-taurians]], [[iphigenia]], [[orestes]] and [[pylades]]).
