## The story

The Iliad foresees it in Priam's plea to Hector, his sons killed, his daughters dragged away, the little children dashed to the ground, and himself torn by his own dogs at his door (22.59–76), and in Andromache's fear that some Achaean will hurl Astyanax from the wall (24.734–738). Demodocus sings how the Greeks poured from the horse and sacked the city, and how Odysseus went with Menelaus to the house of Deiphobus (*Odyssey* 8.514–520). The Sack of Troy told the rest. Neoptolemus killed Priam, who had fled to the altar of Zeus of the courtyard; Menelaus found Helen and led her to the ships, after killing Deiphobus; Ajax son of Oileus dragged Cassandra away from the image of Athena; Odysseus killed Astyanax, and Neoptolemus took Andromache as his prize; the Greeks sacrificed Polyxena at the tomb of Achilles, and Athena planned their destruction at sea (Proclus, summary of the *Sack of Troy*; see [[sack-of-troy]], [[priam]] and [[astyanax]]). Euripides' *Trojan Women* (415 BCE) shows the captive women on the morning after (see [[trojan-women-play]] and [[hecuba]]).
