## The story

Homer does not tell it. Later writers tell it in several versions, which agree on the outline. [[Theseus]] of Athens, with his friend Peirithous, carried off Helen from Sparta while she was still a girl, and left her in the keeping of his mother [[Aethra]] at Aphidnae in Attica. While he was away, her brothers [[Castor]] and [[Polydeuces]] invaded Attica with an army. They were shown where she was hidden, took Aphidnae, brought their sister home, and led away Theseus' mother as a captive. Aethra became Helen's servant and went with her to Troy, and at the sack of the city her grandsons, the sons of Theseus, found her and took her home.

In later legend Aethra, daughter of Pittheus, king of Troezen, is the mother of Theseus (see [[pittheus]]). If the Iliad's Aethra is that Aethra, the line assumes the whole story: the first abduction of Helen, the rescue by her brothers, the captive queen serving the woman she once guarded. The poem says nothing more of her, and nowhere mentions the story. Its Theseus appears only in Nestor's list of the mighty men of old (1.265), a line that is itself suspected (see [[theseus-line]]), and the Odyssey names him only in the book of the dead, as the man who was taking Ariadne from Crete to Athens, and with Peirithous among the heroes of old whom Odysseus would have liked to see (Odyssey 11.321–325, 11.631).

Ancient readers already disagreed about the line. Plutarch cites it as Homer's evidence that Aethra went to Troy with Helen, and reports that others rejected it as spurious (*Theseus* 34). The suspicion is that Athenians put their hero's family into Homer, as Peisistratus was said to have put Theseus into the Odyssey (Plutarch, *Theseus* 20); the defense is that the line is in the manuscripts and that the poet names minor figures from traditions he knew. It cannot be decided (see [[aethra-line]]).

Helen's own words touch the same past only at a distance. Looking for Castor and Polydeuces, she guesses that they stay out of the battle for shame at her disgrace (3.239–242). In the story they had once come to her rescue; now they are dead (see [[the-death-of-the-dioscuri]]).

## Outside Homer

Herodotus tells how the sons of Tyndareus invaded Attica to recover Helen and did not know where she was hidden, until the men of Decelea, angry at the outrage of Theseus, led them to Aphidnae; the Spartans honored the Deceleans for it ever after (9.73). Plutarch tells the whole story in his *Life of Theseus*, with its variants (31–34), and Apollodorus gives it in outline (3.10.7). The *Iliupersis*, as [[proclus|Proclus]] summarizes it, told how Demophon and Acamas, the sons of Theseus, found Aethra at the sack of Troy and took her with them.
