## The story

The voyage is known only from what Athena tells Telemachus in the likeness of Mentes, lord of the Taphians, as the memory of the first time Mentes saw Odysseus:

{{quote:38c1f63e-604b-56b3-a3fd-cbbfafec7be9}}

Odysseus was then a guest in the house of Mentes' father, Anchialus, on his way back from Ephyra, where he had gone on his swift ship to ask Ilus, son of Mermerus, for a drug to smear on his bronze-fitted arrows. Ilus would not give it, for fear of the gods; Anchialus, who loved Odysseus, did (see [[mentes-the-taphian]], [[anchialus-father-of-mentes]] and [[ilus-son-of-mermerus]]). It happened before the war: Mentes says that he and Odysseus used to meet often “before he embarked for Troy”, and that he has not seen him since (*Odyssey* 1.209–212). The poem does not say what became of the drug. Which Ephyra was meant, the Thesprotian or the Elean, was disputed in antiquity (see [[ephyra]] and the translation's note on 1.259).

φάρμακον ἀνδροφόνον, “a man-slaying drug” (*Odyssey* 1.261). ἀνδροφόνος is the Iliad's word for Hector, “man-slaying Hector”, and for the hands of Achilles (*Iliad* 1.242, 24.479; see [[man-slaying]]); here alone in Homer it is said of a drug, and here alone in the Odyssey at all. The Iliad's φάρμακα are the healer's “soothing drugs” spread on wounds (*Iliad* 4.218, 11.515, 11.830; see [[healing]] and [[soothing-drugs]]), and its one harmful drug is the food of the snake that waits for a man at its hole, “having fed on evil drugs” (*Iliad* 22.94). No arrow in the Iliad is said to be poisoned (see [[arrows]]).

## In the poem

The memory serves a wish. Athena wishes that Odysseus would come now and stand at the doors with helmet, shield and two spears, as he was when Mentes first saw him, and engage the suitors: “they would all be swift-fated, and bitter would their wedding be” (*Odyssey* 1.255–266; see [[the-suitors]]). In the next book the suitors think of the same place: Telemachus, one of them jeers, may go to Ephyra, “that rich plowland”, to bring back “drugs that destroy life”, put them in the mixing bowl and destroy them all (*Odyssey* 2.328–330; see [[mixing-bowl]]). When Odysseus turns on the suitors he begins with the bow, and his first arrow, aimed at Antinous, is called “bitter” (πικρὸν ὀϊστόν), but nothing is said of poison (*Odyssey* 22.1–21; see [[bow]]). The renderings of books other than the first two are plain renderings, not the translation.

## Outside Homer

Strabo placed the Ephyra of the drugs in Elis, on the river Selleis: from it, he says, came “the man-slaying drugs”, as both passages of the Odyssey show, and Nestor makes Agamede, the daughter of Augeas, king of the Epeians, a woman skilled in drugs (*Geography* 8.3.5; *Iliad* 11.740–741; see [[strabo]] and [[agamede]]). Merry finds that the Odyssey, which deals less with open fighting than with the shifts of hunters and of pirates such as the Taphians, alludes to poisoned arrows in words that show a strong feeling against them as barbarous or impious; he compares Virgil's Amycus, who poisoned arrows to kill wild beasts, and the arrow dipped in the Hydra's blood with which Heracles shot Nessus (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 1.263). Virgil's Amycus is “the destroyer of wild beasts, than whom no other was more skilled to smear weapons and arm iron with poison” (*Aeneid* 9.771–773; see [[virgil]]), and in Apollodorus Heracles, having killed the Hydra, slits its body and dips his arrows in the gall (*Library* 2.5.2; see [[apollodorus]] and [[heracles]]).
