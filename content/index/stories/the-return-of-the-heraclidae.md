## The story

Thucydides dates it eighty years after the fall of Troy, when the Dorians with the Heraclidae took possession of the Peloponnese (1.12.3). The legend made it the return of an exiled royal house. After several failed attempts, the descendants of Heracles in the fifth generation, Temenus, Cresphontes and Aristodemus, crossed the gulf from Naupactus; Aristodemus died there, and when they had conquered the land, Temenus, Cresphontes and the sons of Aristodemus drew lots for its three kingdoms by throwing their tokens into a jar of water. Cresphontes, who wanted Messenia, threw in a clod of earth, which dissolved, so that the other two lots came out first and Messenia fell to him (Apollodorus 2.8.2–4; see [[cresphontes]], [[dorians]] and [[peloponnese]]). The story of his murder and of his son's revenge is the subject of Euripides' *Cresphontes* (see [[merope-and-her-son]]).
