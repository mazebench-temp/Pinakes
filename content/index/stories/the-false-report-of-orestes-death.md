## The story

In Sophocles' *Electra* the old servant who had saved the child Orestes comes to the palace as a messenger from Phocis and tells Clytemnestra that Orestes is dead. He had won every contest at the Pythian games at Delphi, but in the chariot race, on the last lap, he struck the turning post, was thrown and dragged to his death (*Electra* 680–763). Clytemnestra is relieved, Electra in despair, and the deception lets Orestes enter the house with the urn that is supposed to hold his ashes (see [[electra-play]] and [[pythian-games]]). In Aeschylus' *Libation Bearers* Orestes himself, disguised as a stranger from Phocis, brings the false news (674–690).
