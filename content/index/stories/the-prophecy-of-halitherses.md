## The story

Telemachus has ended his speech to the assembly with a call on the gods, “if ever Zeus will grant that deeds of requital be done” (*Odyssey* 2.143–145; see [[the-assembly-of-the-ithacans]]), and Zeus sends two eagles. Over the middle of the assembly they wheel, foresee destruction, tear each other with their talons and dart away on the right across the city (*Odyssey* 2.146–154; see [[the-omen-of-the-eagles]] and [[eagle]]). The people are amazed and ponder what is to be fulfilled. Then the old hero Halitherses, son of Mastor, who alone surpassed those of his age “in knowing birds and in speaking words of fate”, speaks to the men of Ithaca, and above all to the suitors (*Odyssey* 2.155–162; see [[halitherses]], [[ithacans]] and [[divination]]):

{{quote:54b47e15-5893-5c97-b754-b80b6a348f23}}

He speaks from experience, for he has foretold the return once before, when the fleet sailed for Troy:

{{quote:46b49703-3fb1-582b-8d6d-6f3cae86eb93}}

Eurymachus, son of Polybus, answers for the suitors. Let the old man prophesy to his own children; not all birds are birds of fate; Odysseus has perished far away, and the suitors will lay a fine on the seer if he stirs Telemachus up; “nor do we heed the oracle that you, old sir, / pronounce, never to be fulfilled” (*Odyssey* 2.177–207; see [[eurymachus]] and [[many-birds-not-all-of-fate]]). When Leiocritus dissolves the assembly, he leaves Telemachus' journey to “Mentor and Halitherses”, “who are his father's companions from of old” (*Odyssey* 2.253–254; see [[mentor]] and [[leiocritus-son-of-euenor]]).

## In the poem

**A seer's word.** In the first book of the Odyssey Athena, in the likeness of Mentes, foretold the return “though I am no seer and have no clear knowledge of birds”: Odysseus would not be away much longer from his dear native land (*Odyssey* 1.202–203; see [[athena-visits-telemachus]] and [[the-return-of-odysseus]]). The second book gives the same news the authority of a seer, who reads it from a sign of Zeus before the whole people. Its language is that of prophecy and fulfillment (see [[prophecy]]). The people ponder what “was to be fulfilled” (τελέεσθαι, *Odyssey* 2.156); Halitherses does “not prophesy untried, but with sure knowledge”, for “all has been fulfilled” as he told Odysseus (τελευτηθῆναι, *Odyssey* 2.170–171), and “now it is all being fulfilled” (τελεῖται, *Odyssey* 2.176). To Eurymachus the oracle is ἀκράαντον, “never to be fulfilled” (*Odyssey* 2.202), and he is the second of the suitors to die, by Odysseus' arrow (*Odyssey* 22.79–88).

**The prophecy at the departure.** The earlier prophecy is known only from this speech, and its terms are the poem's own words for the homecoming. ὀλέσαντʼ ἄπο πάντας ἑταίρους, “losing all his companions” (*Odyssey* 2.174), is the curse of the blinded Cyclops, that Odysseus should come home late and in misery, ὀλέσας ἄπο πάντας ἑταίρους, “having lost all his companions” (*Odyssey* 9.534; see [[polyphemus-the-cyclops]]), the warning of Teiresias and of Circe (*Odyssey* 11.114, 12.141), and Athena's word when he is home, that she always knew he would return having lost all his companions (*Odyssey* 13.339–340). ἄγνωστον πάντεσσιν, “unknown to all” (*Odyssey* 2.175), is what Athena makes him on the shore of Ithaca: “I will make you unknown to all mortals” (ἄγνωστον τεύξω πάντεσσι βροτοῖσι, *Odyssey* 13.397; also 13.191; see [[odysseus-set-ashore-on-ithaca]]). The twentieth year is his own reckoning when he makes himself known: “I have come in the twentieth year to my native land”, he tells his son, his nurse, his herdsmen and his father (ἤλυθον εἰκοστῷ ἔτεϊ ἐς πατρίδα γαῖαν, *Odyssey* 16.206, 19.484, 21.208, 24.322). The dog Argus dies when he has seen Odysseus ἐεικοστῷ ἐνιαυτῷ, “in the twentieth year”, the words of the prophecy (*Odyssey* 17.326–327), and Telemachus and then Odysseus reproach Penelope with the same verses: no other woman would hold back from a husband who came home in the twentieth year (*Odyssey* 23.100–102, 23.168–170). The twenty years are the ten of the war, in whose tenth year Troy was to fall (*Iliad* 2.328–329), and the ten that Merry counts from the sack of Troy to the return (Merry, *Commentary on the Odyssey*, 1886, on 1.16).

**Like Calchas at Aulis.** τὰ δὲ δὴ νῦν πάντα τελεῖται, “and now it is all being fulfilled” (*Odyssey* 2.176), is the half-verse, with or without δέ, that closes a remembered prophecy elsewhere in Homer (*Iliad* 2.330, 14.48; *Odyssey* 5.302, 13.178, 18.271; see [[prophecy]]). The nearest of them is Calchas' reading of the portent at Aulis, which Odysseus, in the Iliad, ends with it: “So he spoke then; and now it is all being fulfilled” (*Iliad* 2.330; see [[the-portent-at-aulis]] and [[calchas]]). Each seer spoke when the fleet sailed for Troy, and each prophecy is recalled at the time of its fulfillment: Calchas' city taken in the tenth year of the war, Halitherses' homecoming in the twentieth year of the absence.

**Already near.** Halitherses says that Odysseus “is already somewhere / near” (*Odyssey* 2.164–165). When he speaks, the poem has left Odysseus on Calypso's island (*Odyssey* 1.13–15, 1.48–59), and he reaches Ithaca only in the thirteenth book (see [[odysseus-set-ashore-on-ithaca]]). The slaughter he plants “for all of them” (*Odyssey* 2.165–166) comes in the twenty-second (see [[the-suitors]]).

**The last assembly.** The evil foretold “for many others too”, the people of Ithaca (*Odyssey* 2.166–167), comes when the kinsmen of the dead suitors gather in assembly to avenge them. Halitherses rises again, brought in by the verses of the first assembly, and reminds them that he warned them: they would not obey him or Mentor and stop their sons, who wasted the possessions and dishonored the wife of the best of men, and said that he would come home no more (*Odyssey* 24.451–462), as Mentor complained in the first assembly (*Odyssey* 2.238). More than half of them follow Eupeithes to arms, and Eupeithes falls before Athena stops the fight (*Odyssey* 24.463–532; see [[halitherses]] and [[eupeithes]]). The renderings of books other than the first two are plain renderings, not the translation.
