## The story

Odysseus, washed up on Scheria, has been received by Alcinous, king of the Phaeacians, without giving his name. At the feast the blind bard Demodocus sings of the quarrel of Odysseus and Achilles, and Odysseus draws his cloak over his head and weeps; only Alcinous notices, and he turns the company to games (*Odyssey* 8.62–103). In the evening Odysseus himself asks for the song of the wooden horse, and weeps again, as a woman weeps who falls upon the body of her husband; Alcinous stops the bard and asks the stranger his name, his country and why he grieves at the fate of the Argives (*Odyssey* 8.485–586). “I am Odysseus, son of Laertes,” he answers, and tells the story of his wanderings (*Odyssey* 9.19–20; see [[demodocus]], [[alcinous]] and [[the-tale-to-alcinous]]).
