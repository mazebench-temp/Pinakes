## The story

{{quote:51db06d9-5600-5864-ac0b-2980eb809ac2}}

The Iliad tells this war only here, inside Nestor's story of his war with the Epeians (see [[nestor-and-the-epeians]]). Heracles “had come and ill-used us” (ἐλθὼν … ἐκάκωσε), picking up the Pylians' complaint that “we in Pylos were few and had been ill-used” (κεκακωμένοι, 11.689); he is named by his might, βίη Ἡρακληείη (11.690; see [[might-of-heracles]] and [[heracles]]). It happened “in the years before” Nestor's war, and “all our best were killed”. Of the twelve sons of “noble Neleus” Nestor alone was left (11.691–693; see [[neleus]] and [[nestor]]). Neleus himself survived: in the story that follows he is glad of his son's plunder and hides his horses (11.683, 11.717–718). Homer gives no cause for the attack and does not say how Nestor escaped. Dione's story that Heracles shot Hades “in Pylos among the dead” (5.397) may belong to the same war (see [[pylos-or-the-gate]] and [[heracles-wounds-hera-and-hades]]).

## In the poem

These verses explain what follows. “Haughty because of this, the bronze-armored Epeians / treated us with outrage and devised reckless deeds” (11.694–695; see [[epeians]]). Pylos was weak and was owed much, and Neleus had one son left, still untried in war (11.717–719); it is he who drives off the Epeians' cattle in the raid that begins the tale (11.670–684). The survivor of his own generation, who rules “among the third” (1.250–252), was already the last of his brothers. The Odyssey names two of them, Chromius and Periclymenus, sons of Neleus and Chloris (*Odyssey* 11.281–286).

## Outside Homer

The Hesiodic *Catalogue* told the war at length. Heracles killed the eleven noble sons of Neleus, while the twelfth, “the Gerenian horseman Nestor”, happened to be a guest among the horse-taming Gerenians, so that “Nestor alone escaped”, at Gerenus (fr. 11 Evelyn-White, quoted by Stephanus of Byzantium under Gerenia; see [[gerenian-horseman]] and [[hesiod]]). Periclymenus, who could change his shape, settled on the boss of the yoke of Heracles' horses to fight him, and Heracles shot him when Athena pointed him out (fr. 10, from a scholium on Apollonius, *Argonautica* 1.156). [[apollodorus|Apollodorus]] puts the war after Heracles' capture of Elis. Heracles took Pylos, killed Periclymenus, the bravest of Neleus' sons, who changed his shape as he fought, and killed Neleus and all his sons except Nestor, who was young and was being brought up among the Gerenians; in the battle he wounded Hades, who was helping the Pylians (*Library* 2.7.3). That sequence leaves no room for Nestor's war against Augeas, whom Apollodorus' Heracles has already killed (2.7.2). Hades is one of several gods later said to have fought for the Pylians (see [[pylos-or-the-gate]]). Strabo, retelling Nestor's story, says that Heracles had wiped out all the youth of Pylos (*Geography* 8.3.28), and argues that the Pylos he sacked cannot have been the Elean one, since Elis would have been sacked with it (8.3.29; see [[strabo-on-nestors-pylos]]).
