## The story

Oedipus answered the riddle of the Sphinx, freed Thebes from her, and was given the kingdom and the hand of the widowed queen. Years later a plague struck the city, and the oracle declared that the killer of Laius must be driven out. Oedipus vowed to find him. The seer Tiresias named Oedipus himself, and Oedipus accused him and Creon of a plot; Jocasta, to calm him, told him of the oracle given to Laius and of his death at a crossroads, and Oedipus began to fear (Sophocles, *Oedipus the King* 698–862). Then a messenger came from Corinth with news that Polybus was dead and that the Corinthians wanted Oedipus as their king. When Oedipus said he still feared to marry his mother, the messenger, to free him from that fear, told him that Polybus and Merope were not his parents: he himself had received the infant from a shepherd of Laius on Cithaeron (924–1085). Jocasta understood and went inside. The shepherd, brought in under threat, confessed that the child was Laius' own, handed to him by Jocasta to be killed (1110–1185). Jocasta hanged herself, and Oedipus put out his eyes with the pins of her brooches (1237–1285; see [[jocasta]], [[oedipus]] and [[oedipus-the-king]]).
