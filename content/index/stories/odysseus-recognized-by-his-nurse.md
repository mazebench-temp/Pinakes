## The story

Penelope tells her maids to wash the stranger's feet, and he asks for an old woman instead. Eurycleia, who nursed him as a baby, says that no stranger has ever come who was so like Odysseus in body, voice and feet. Odysseus turns away from the firelight, afraid that she will see the scar. As she runs her hands over his leg she knows it, lets the foot fall so that the basin rings and tips over, and turns to Penelope; but Athena has turned Penelope's mind elsewhere, and Odysseus grips the old woman by the throat and makes her swear to keep silent (*Odyssey* 19.357–507; see [[eurycleia]] and [[the-scar-of-odysseus]]). The story of the boar hunt is told as her hands touch the scar (see [[the-boar-hunt-on-parnassus]]). Before the slaughter of the suitors Odysseus shows the scar to the swineherd Eumaeus and the cowherd Philoetius to prove who he is (*Odyssey* 21.205–225); later Eurycleia offers it to Penelope as proof (*Odyssey* 23.73–77), and Odysseus shows it to his father (*Odyssey* 24.331–335; see [[eumaeus]]).
