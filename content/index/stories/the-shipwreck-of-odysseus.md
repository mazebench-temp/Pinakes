## The story

**The threat.** When the companions had killed the cattle of the Sun on Thrinacia, Helios demanded that they be punished, and Zeus promised to strike their swift ship with his gleaming thunderbolt and shatter it in the middle of the wine-dark sea (*Odyssey* 12.374–388; see [[the-cattle-of-helios]] and [[helios]]). Odysseus heard this, he says, from Calypso, who had it from Hermes (*Odyssey* 12.389–390). The threat is spoken in the words of the deed: νῆα θοὴν ἀργῆτι κεραυνῷ / τυτθὰ βαλὼν κεάσαιμι μέσῳ ἐνὶ οἴνοπι πόντῳ (*Odyssey* 12.387–388), and Calypso tells the wreck in the same words, νῆα θοὴν ἀργῆτι κεραυνῷ / Ζεὺς ἔλσας ἐκέασσε μέσῳ ἐνὶ οἴνοπι πόντῳ (*Odyssey* 5.131–132; see [[of-the-gleaming-thunderbolt]] and [[thunderbolt]]).

**The wreck.** Odysseus tells it to the Phaeacians (*Odyssey* 12.403–425, plain renderings). When the island was behind them and no land was in sight, Zeus set a dark cloud over the ship; the West Wind came shrieking in a great squall and snapped both forestays, and the mast fell back and crushed the helmsman's skull. Zeus thundered and struck the ship with his thunderbolt; she spun round and was filled with sulfur, and the companions fell overboard and were carried on the waves around her like sea crows, “and the god took away their homecoming” (*Odyssey* 12.419; see [[nostos]]). Odysseus went up and down the ship until the surge tore the sides from the keel and broke off the mast; with the oxhide backstay that hung from the mast he lashed keel and mast together, and sat on them, carried by the deadly winds.

**To Ogygia.** The South Wind carried him back to Charybdis, where he clung to the fig tree “like a bat” until the whirlpool cast up the timbers again (*Odyssey* 12.426–446); for nine days he drifted, and on the tenth night the gods brought him to the island of Ogygia, where Calypso lives (*Odyssey* 12.447–450; see [[ogygia]] and [[calypso]]).

**The other tellings.** To Arete, Odysseus speaks the verses of Calypso's account, with “my ship” for “his ship”, and adds that he clasped the keel in his arms and was carried for nine days (*Odyssey* 7.248–254). Disguised as a stranger, he tells Penelope that Odysseus lost his companions and his ship on the way from Thrinacia, because Zeus and Helios were angry with him, and that the wave cast him on the keel onto the land of the Phaeacians; in that telling Calypso is left out (*Odyssey* 19.273–282). When at last he tells her his own story, Zeus who thunders on high strikes the ship with a smoky thunderbolt, and all the companions perish together (*Odyssey* 23.330–333). The wreck serves a lie as well: the Cretan of Odysseus' false tale to Eumaeus, carried off on a Phoenician ship bound for Libya, loses it when Crete is left behind, in the verses of the true wreck, and clings to the mast for nine days until a wave brings him to Thesprotia (*Odyssey* 14.301–315, after 12.403–406 and 12.415–419).

**In the poem.** The wreck ends the wanderings that Odysseus tells and begins his seven years with Calypso (*Odyssey* 7.259). It is why the poem finds him alone, with “no ships with oars and no companions” (*Odyssey* 4.559 = 5.16), and it fulfills the prophecy of Teiresias, that if the cattle were harmed the ship and the companions would be destroyed, and Odysseus, if he escaped, would come home late and in misery, having lost all his companions, in another man's ship (*Odyssey* 11.112–115; see [[odysseus]] and [[the-return-of-odysseus]]).
