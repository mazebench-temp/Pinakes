## The story

{{quote:6a1b3048-96a6-51f6-be66-100c76396d62}}

[[Thamyris]], a Thracian singer, was on his way from the house of [[Eurytus]] at [[Oechalia]]. He boasted that he would win in singing even if the [[Muses]] themselves, the daughters of Zeus, sang against him. The Muses met him at [[Dorium]]. In their anger they maimed him, took away his song and made him forget how to play the lyre.

## In the poem

The story is told as a note on a place name, the last of Nestor's towns, and it is the only story in the Catalogue about a singer. It comes a hundred lines after the poet's own appeal to the Muses, in which he confesses that he knows only what they tell him, and that he could not name the multitude "unless the Olympian Muses remind me" (2.484–493; see [[invocation-of-the-muses]]). Thamyris is the singer who forgot this and set himself against his source.

The detail that he came "from Eurytus of Oechalia" adds a second offender. Eurytus challenged Apollo to a contest with the bow and was killed for it (Od. 8.223–228). Both are mortals who claimed to match a god in the god's own art, like Niobe, who compared herself to Leto (24.602–609).

## Outside Homer

Later versions filled in what Homer leaves out: the prize Thamyris wanted, his blinding, and his parents (see [[Thamyris]]). Polygnotus painted him blind in the underworld with his broken lyre (Pausanias 10.30.8).
