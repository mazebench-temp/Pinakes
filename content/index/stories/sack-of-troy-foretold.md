## The story

Troy fell in the tenth year of the war. The Achaeans pretended to sail away and left the Wooden Horse behind them, with their best fighters hidden inside it. The Trojans took the horse into the city, and that night the Achaeans came back and burned it.

## In the poem

The first words anyone speaks in the Iliad are a wish for the sack. Chryses comes to buy back his daughter, and he begins with a blessing on the whole army.

{{quote:8fb573bd-1326-5966-ba64-00b8f5ea7fec}}

The blessing is also the price. Chryses offers the gods' favor for the war, a safe voyage home and a splendid ransom, all in exchange for his daughter. Agamemnon refuses, and Apollo's arrows fall on the camp instead.

The sack comes up again when Achilles asks Agamemnon to give the girl back. Every loss in the camp is to be made good out of Troy's wealth, and the whole system of prizes depends on the city's fall (see [[geras]]).

{{quote:60b3310f-0ca2-55a0-893f-9710a9554f31}}

The towns Achilles says he has sacked (1.163–164) are the smaller cities of the Troad, such as [[thebe]], not Troy itself (see [[achilles-raids]]).

The poem never reaches the sack. It ends with Hector's funeral. Its characters look ahead to it all the same. The same two lines come from Agamemnon and then from Hector: "a day will come when sacred Ilios will perish, and Priam, and the people of Priam of the good ash spear" (4.164–165, 6.448–449). At Hector's death the Trojans cry out as if the whole city were already burning (22.410–411). Andromache, mourning over his body, foresees the city's fall and her son's death (24.728–738).

## Outside Homer

The *Odyssey* tells parts of the sack. Demodocus sings of the Wooden Horse and the night of the sack at the court of the Phaeacians (*Odyssey* 8.499–520). Menelaus remembers how Helen walked around the horse calling to the men inside (4.271–289). Odysseus tells the shade of Achilles how bravely his son Neoptolemus waited inside it (11.523–532).

The epic cycle gave the fall of Troy a poem of its own, the *Iliupersis* ("Sack of Ilion") attributed to Arctinus of Miletus. It is lost. It is known from the summary by [[proclus]]:

- The Trojans argue about the horse. Serpents kill Laocoön and one of his sons, and Aeneas and his people withdraw to Mount Ida.
- Sinon lights the signal fire, and the fleet sails back from Tenedos.
- Neoptolemus kills Priam at the altar of Zeus Herkeios. Menelaus kills Deiphobus and takes Helen back to the ships.
- Ajax the son of Oileus drags Cassandra away from Athena's image, pulling the image with her.
- Odysseus kills Hector's son Astyanax. Neoptolemus takes Andromache as his prize, and Polyxena is killed at Achilles' tomb.
- The Achaeans sail away, and Athena plans their destruction at sea. That is where the [[clytemnestra-and-the-return|Nostoi]] begin.
