## The story

{{quote:5c0f98df-257c-5401-8e3b-9f84ce3ece13}}

Homer does not say which prophecies Agamemnon means. The lines imply a history between the two men that the audience is expected to know or imagine.

## In the poem

The outburst shows Agamemnon's temper, and it justifies Calchas' fear: "a king is the stronger when he is angry with a lesser man" (1.80). It also shows what a prophecy is in the poem. The seer states the gods' will, and a king may hate him for it.

## Outside Homer

The obvious candidate is Aulis. In the *Cypria*, and in Aeschylus' *Agamemnon* (184–247), Calchas declared that the winds would not allow the fleet to sail until Agamemnon sacrificed his daughter Iphigenia to Artemis. Ancient commentators on this line made the connection. Homer never mentions the sacrifice, and at Iliad 9.145 Agamemnon lists three living daughters, one of them Iphianassa, whom some ancient writers identified with Iphigenia.
