## The story

Nestor tells Telemachus how it began. Zeus devised a grim homecoming for the Argives, since not all of them were discerning or just, and the wrath of Athena set the two sons of Atreus at strife. They called the army to assembly at sunset, the men heavy with wine; Menelaus urged them to sail at once, Agamemnon to stay and appease Athena with sacrifices, and the army split in two. Nestor, Diomedes and Menelaus sailed, and at Tenedos a second strife sent Odysseus back to Agamemnon (*Odyssey* 3.130–164; see [[agamemnon]], [[menelaus]] and [[the-returns-of-the-achaeans]]). The Sack of Troy ended with the Greeks sailing away and Athena planning their destruction at sea, and the Returns began with the quarrel (Proclus, summaries of the *Sack of Troy* and the *Returns*). What the tragedy called the *Sailing Away* contained is unknown; the sacrifice of Polyxena, which delayed the fleet, and the feigned departure before the wooden horse have both been suggested (see [[the-sailing-away]]).
