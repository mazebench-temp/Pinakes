## The story

Nestor tells Telemachus how it began. Zeus planned a bitter homecoming for the Argives, since not all of them were wise or just, and the anger of Athena set the two sons of Atreus quarreling. They called the army to assembly at sunset, the men heavy with wine; Menelaus urged them to sail at once, Agamemnon to stay and appease Athena with sacrifices, and the army split in two. Nestor, Diomedes and Menelaus sailed, and Odysseus turned back from Tenedos to Agamemnon (*Odyssey* 3.130–164; see [[agamemnon]] and [[menelaus]]). The Sack of Troy ended with the Greeks sailing away and Athena planning their destruction at sea, and the Returns began with the quarrel (Proclus, summaries of the *Sack of Troy* and the *Returns*). What the tragedy called the *Sailing Away* contained is unknown; the sacrifice of Polyxena, which delayed the fleet, and the feigned departure before the wooden horse have both been suggested (see [[the-sailing-away]]).
