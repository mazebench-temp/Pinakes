## The story

Tiresias foretold that death would come to Odysseus “from the sea” (ἐξ ἁλός), a gentle death in sleek old age, with his people prospering around him (*Odyssey* 11.134–137). The Telegony told how it came. Telegonus, the son born to Circe after Odysseus left her, sailed in search of his father, landed on Ithaca and began to plunder it; Odysseus came out to defend his land and was killed by his son, who did not know him. Recognizing his error, Telegonus carried his father's body, with Penelope and Telemachus, to his mother; Circe made them immortal, and Telegonus married Penelope and Telemachus Circe (Proclus, summary of the *Telegony*). His spear was tipped with the spine of a stingray, so that death did come from the sea (Apollodorus, *Epitome* 7.36; see [[telegonus]] and [[circe]]). Sophocles told the story in a tragedy known as *Odysseus Struck by the Spine*, probably the play Aristotle calls *Odysseus Wounded* (see [[odysseus-wounded]]).
