## The story

Homer knows nothing of a feud: the scepter of Zeus passed from Atreus, when he died, to Thyestes “rich in flocks”, and from Thyestes to Agamemnon (2.106–107; see [[the-scepter-of-agamemnon]]). The tragedians tell of a quarrel over the throne of Mycenae. Thyestes seduced Atreus' wife Aerope and with her help took the golden lamb, the token of the kingship; Zeus turned back the course of the sun to show who was the rightful king (Euripides, *Electra* 699–746). Atreus banished his brother, then recalled him with a show of reconciliation and served him a feast of his own sons' flesh, showing him their hands and feet afterward; Thyestes, when he understood, fell back, kicked over the table and cursed the line of Pelops (Aeschylus, *Agamemnon* 1583–1602; see [[atreus]] and [[thyestes]]). His surviving son, Aegisthus, took revenge on Atreus' son Agamemnon (see [[aegisthus]] and [[clytemnestra-and-the-return]]).
