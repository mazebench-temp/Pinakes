## The story

{{quote:701b6af0-4570-56fc-86ed-2e39efc29a71}}

The killing is told in one line and without a motive. The victim is an old man and a close kinsman, "his own father's dear uncle", and the killer acts "at once" (αὐτίκα), as soon as he has grown up. What follows is the usual fate of a homicide in Homer. The dead man's kin threaten revenge, and the killer must leave his country. Tlepolemus does not go alone. He builds ships, gathers "many people" and wanders "suffering pains" (ἄλγεα πάσχων) until he comes to Rhodes.

The end is a foundation story. The settlers "lived there in three groups, by tribes" (τριχθὰ ... καταφυλαδόν), "and were loved by Zeus, who rules over gods and men, and the son of Cronus poured wondrous wealth upon them" (2.668–670).

## In the poem

The digression gives the Rhodian contingent a past, as the Catalogue does for a few other leaders: Thamyris (2.594–600), Protesilaus (2.698–702), Philoctetes (2.721–725) and Peirithous (2.741–744). It is also one of several stories of exile after a killing. Patroclus came to the house of Peleus as a boy after killing a playmate over a game of knucklebones (23.85–90), and [[Medon]], who leads Philoctetes' men, lived in exile at Phylace for killing a kinsman of his stepmother (13.694–697). Tlepolemus is the only one of them who founds a new home for a whole people.

Rhodes was a Dorian island in historical times, and the Heraclid founder, the three cities and the three tribes all fit that later world, where the Dorians were divided into three tribes and their kings claimed descent from Heracles. The Odyssey itself calls the Dorians of Crete τριχάϊκες, which probably means "in three tribes" (19.177). Some scholars take the passage as one of the Catalogue's latest layers. Others note the Mycenaean settlement on Rhodes. The question is open.

## Outside Homer

Pindar's seventh *Olympian* retells the story for a Rhodian victor. Tlepolemus killed Licymnius at Tiryns in a fit of anger, striking him with a staff of olive wood, and went to Delphi, where Apollo told him to sail to the island (*Olympian* 7.27–33). Apollodorus makes the killing an accident: Licymnius ran between Tlepolemus and a servant he was beating (2.8.2).
