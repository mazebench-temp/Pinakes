## The story

Demodocus sings it at the court of Alcinous at Odysseus' own request: the horse that Epeius made with Athena's help, which Odysseus brought into the citadel as a trap, filled with men; the Argives burned their huts and sailed away, and the Trojans, having dragged the horse inside, debated whether to split it open, throw it from the rocks, or let it stand as an offering to the gods; and it was fated that the city should perish once it had taken in the horse (*Odyssey* 8.492–520). Menelaus remembers how Helen walked around it calling the leaders by name in the voices of their wives (*Odyssey* 4.271–289), and Odysseus how Neoptolemus alone never paled inside it (*Odyssey* 11.523–532). In the Little Iliad, Epeius built the horse at Athena's prompting, the Greeks burned their tents and withdrew to Tenedos, and the Trojans took the horse in, pulling down part of their wall; in the Sack of Troy, Sinon, who had entered the city by a pretense, raised the fire signal to the fleet (Proclus, summaries of the *Little Iliad* and the *Sack of Troy*). Virgil gives Sinon his famous lying speech (*Aeneid* 2.57–198; see [[sinon]] and [[sinon-play]]).
