## The story

When Achilles comes at him, Hector, alone outside the Scaean gate, loses his nerve and runs. Achilles pursues him like a hawk after a dove, past the lookout and the wild fig tree, past the two springs where the Trojan women once washed their clothes, three times around the city, as in a dream, where one cannot catch a man who runs and he cannot escape (*Iliad* 22.131–201). Achilles shakes his head at his own men, forbidding them to throw at Hector, so that no one else might win the glory and he come second (22.205–207). Zeus weighs the fates of the two men, and Hector's sinks; Athena, in the likeness of his brother Deiphobus, persuades Hector to stand, and Achilles kills him (22.208–366; see [[achilles]] and [[hector]]).
